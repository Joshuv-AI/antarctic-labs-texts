Abstract
. Given a complex manifold M M endowed with a holomorphic contact structure V V , one can define a sub-Finsler pseudometric through holomorphic discs tangent to V V . If the integrated pseudodistance is a distance, then M M is V V -hyperbolic, a generalisation of Kobayashi hyperbolicity. In this paper, we focus on Reeb manifolds M M , which are contact manifolds with a free holomorphic ℂ \mathbb{C} -action generated by a Reeb vector field. We show that every proper Reeb manifold is the total space of a contact symplectic lift , that is, it admits a ℂ \mathbb{C} -principal bundle structure onto a complex manifold S S endowed with a C ∞ C^{\infty} -exact holomorphic symplectic form ω \omega so that the pull back of ω \omega is related to the contact structure V V of M M . Conversely, we prove that any C ∞ C^{\infty} -exact holomorphic symplectic manifold is given as the base of a contact symplectic lift. We also prove that M M is (complete) V V -hyperbolic if and only if S S is (complete) Kobayashi hyperbolic. This yields many new examples of V V -hyperbolic manifolds. Finally, we study the automorphism group 𝖠𝗎𝗍 V ​ M {\sf Aut}_{V}M of those automorphisms of M M preserving V V . In case M M is V V -hyperbolic, this is a finite-dimensional Lie group, and we provide a classification in dimension 3 3 , where 2 ≤ dim ℝ 𝖠𝗎𝗍 V ​ M ≤ 7 2\leq\dim_{\mathbb{R}}{\sf Aut}_{V}M\leq 7 , with the extremal upper case realised uniquely (up to natural equivalences) by the contact symplectic lift of the unit ball 𝔹 2 \mathbb{B}^{2} endowed with the symplectic form 2 ( 1 − z ) 3 ​ d ​ z ∧ d ​ w \frac{2}{(1-z)^{3}}dz\wedge dw .

1.
Introduction

Let
M
be a connected complex manifold of dimension
2N+1
,
N\geq 1
and let
V\subset TM
be a complex rank
2N
bracket-generating holomorphic distribution, that is a (holomorphic) contact structure. Consider holomorphic discs everywhere tangent to
V
. In this way, a sub-Finsler pseudo-metric
\kappa_{V}
, similar to Kobayashi’s, can be defined. The first one who exploited such construction was Demailly
[
17
,
18
]
in order to study directed systems on jets bundles.

By Sussmann’s orbit theorem, every pair of points
p,q\in M
can be joined by piecewise smooth curves tangent to
V
. Using
\kappa_{V}
, one can define the
V
-length
of these curves and a pseudo-distance
k_{V}(p,q)
.

As in classical Kobayashi’s hyperbolicity theory,
M
is called
V
-hyperbolic
if
k_{V}
is a true distance. Clearly, if
M
is Kobayashi hyperbolic, it is
V
-hyperbolic for any contact distribution
V
. However, finding other non-trivial examples is challenging. While sub-Riemannian geometry and real contact structures have been extensively studied, very little is known about the holomorphic case. Forstnerič
[
22
]
constructed a remarkable example of a contact structure on
\mathbb{C}^{3}
such that
\mathbb{C}^{3}
is hyperbolic with respect to that structure.

The group of automorphisms of any Kobayashi hyperbolic complex manifold is a finite dimensional real Lie group, and Isaev
[
29
,
30
]
provided a sharp classification according to the dimension of such a group. The group
{\sf Aut}_{V}M
of automorphisms of a complex manifold
M
preserving a hyperbolic contact structure
V
on
M
is also a real finite dimensional Lie group, and one can quite easily compute a bound using Lie algebra theory (see Theorem
3.5
), but, apparently, such a bound is not sharp and a classification
à la
Isaev does not seem to be possible.

The aim of this paper is to introduce machinery that enables the construction of non-trivial examples of
V
-hyperbolic manifolds. For such objects, in low dimension, we aim to classify them based on the dimension of their automorphism groups. To precisely describe our results, we need to introduce some notation.

A
Reeb manifold

(M,V,v)
is the data of a complex manifold
M
(of odd dimension) endowed with a contact structure
V
and with a
\mathbb{C}
-complete holomorphic vector field
v
acting freely on
M
such that
v
is a Reeb vector field for
V
. The Reeb manifold
(M,V,v)
is called
proper
if the
\mathbb{C}
-action induced by
v
is proper. A Reeb manifold
(M,V,v)
such that
M
is
V
-hyperbolic is necessarily a proper Reeb manifold. The contact structure
V
of a Reeb manifold is always given as the kernel of a unique (global and holomorphic) contact form
\xi
such that
\xi(v)\equiv 1
(see Subsection
3.1
for details). The first part of this work aims to prove the following result:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(M,V,v)
be a proper Reeb manifold. Let
\xi
be the global contact form such that
V=\ker\xi
and
\xi(v)\equiv 1
. Then there exist a complex manifold
S
endowed with a
C^{\infty}
-exact holomorphic symplectic form
\omega
and a holomorphic surjective map
\pi:M\to S
such that

(1)

\pi:M\to S
is a
\mathbb{C}
-principal bundle, whose
\mathbb{C}
-action is the flow of the Reeb vector field
v
,

(2)

d\xi=-\pi^{\ast}\omega
.

Moreover, if
(S^{\prime},\omega^{\prime})
is another holomorphic symplectic manifold satisfying the previous conditions, then there is a biholomorphism
\varphi:S\to S^{\prime}
such that
\varphi^{\ast}(\omega^{\prime})=\omega
.

Conversely, if
S
is a complex manifold endowed with a
C^{\infty}
-exact holomorphic symplectic form
\omega
, then there exists a proper Reeb manifold
(M,V,v)
which satisfies the previous conditions (1) and (2). Moreover, the space of all proper Reeb manifolds which satisfy the previous conditions (1) and (2) (up to “natural equivalence”) is parameterized by
H^{1}(S,\mathbb{C})
.

This result summarises Lemma
4.5
, Theorem
4.19
, and Theorem
5.4
(see Section
5
for a detailed description of “natural equivalences” and how
H^{1}(S,\mathbb{C})
parametrizes such classes).

We call
(M,V,v,S,\omega)
a
contact symplectic lift
if it satisfies conditions (1) and (2) in Theorem
1.1
.

The previous result states in particular that two
\mathbb{C}
-principal bundles over
S
may be equivalent as
\mathbb{C}
-principal bundles, but they may not be equivalent as Reeb manifolds over
S
. For instance, if
S
is Stein, all
\mathbb{C}
-principal bundles over
S
are equivalent to the trivial
\mathbb{C}
-principal bundle
S\times\mathbb{C}
, but, if
H^{1}(S,\mathbb{C})\neq 0
, such a bundle can be endowed with inequivalent contact structures; see Example
5.6
.

It is quite surprising that in order to “lift” the holomorphic symplectic form
\omega
to a (holomorphic) contact form on
M
one needs
\omega
to be
C^{\infty}
-exact but not holomorphically exact. In fact, in Proposition
4.22
we show that the contact form of a contact symplectic lift
(M,V,v,S,\omega)
is a holomorphic connection for the
\mathbb{C}
-principal bundle
M\to S
and
\omega
is precisely the curvature of such a connection. In particular,
\omega
is holomorphically exact if and only if such a bundle has a holomorphic flat connection. Theorem
4.23
gives several other equivalent conditions for this to happen.

If
(M,V,v,S,\omega)
is a contact symplectic lift, the Reeb manifold
(M,V,v)
is not Kobayashi hyperbolic, since it contains nontrivial entire curves. However, it is natural to ask under which conditions it is
V
-hyperbolic. As customary, we say that
M
is
complete

V
-hyperbolic if
k_{V}
is a complete distance. The answer to the previous question is provided by the following result which gives a precise link between the Kobayashi hyperbolicity of
S
and the
V
-hyperbolicity of
M
:

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
(M,V,v,S,\omega)
be a contact symplectic lift. Then
M
is
V
hyperbolic (
respectively
complete
V
-hyperbolic) if and only if
S
is Kobayashi hyperbolic (
respect.
complete Kobayashi hyperbolic).

This result is the content of Theorem
6.1
and Theorem
6.5
.

It is not difficult to show that if
M
is (complete)
V
-hyperbolic, then
S
is (complete) Kobayashi hyperbolic, as the
\mathbb{C}
-principal bundle map
M\to S
is non-expansive. However, the converse is not straightforward. We can not rely on a “ball-box theorem” as in sub-Riemannian geometry because
\kappa_{V}
is merely a sub-Finsler upper semicontinuous metric. Therefore, our proof relies on some localisation arguments.

Theorem
1.2
allows to create plenty of non-trivial examples of (complete)
V
-hyperbolic manifolds, starting from holomorphic symplectic (complete) Kobayashi manifolds: for instance, if
S
is any pseudoconvex domain in
\mathbb{C}^{2N}
, given the standard holomorphic symplectic form on
\mathbb{C}^{2N}
, one can define
H^{1}(S,\mathbb{C})
-inequivalent hyperbolic contact structures on
S\times\mathbb{C}
.

Once we have a hyperbolic contact symplectic lift
(M,V,v,S,\omega)
—where “hyperbolic” means
S
is Kobayashi hyperbolic or, equivalently,
M
is
V
-hyperbolic—we can define a Lie group morphism from the Lie group
{\sf Aut}_{V}M
to the closed Lie subgroup of
{\sf Aut}S
formed by those automorphisms of
S
which preserves the form
\omega
up to constant multiple (see Section
7
for details). The kernel of this morphism has real dimension
2
and is given by the flow of the Reeb vector field
v
. While this morphism is not always surjective, it is surjective when
H^{1}(S,\mathbb{C})=0
. By the previously mentioned results of Isaev, this implies in particular that

2\leq\dim_{\mathbb{R}}{\sf Aut}_{V}M\leq 2+\dim_{\mathbb{R}}{\sf Aut}S\leq(\dim M-1)^{2}+2(\dim M-1)+2.

With such a result at hand, we focus on the case
\dim M=3
. However, the previous upper bound is not optimal in this case. Indeed, we have the following “classification theorem’ for hyperbolic Reeb
3
-manifolds:

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
(M,V,v)
be a Reeb manifold. Suppose
\dim_{\mathbb{C}}M=3
and assume
M
is
V
-hyperbolic. Then

2\leq\dim_{\mathbb{R}}{\sf Aut}_{V}M\leq 7.

For every
n\in\{2,\ldots,7\}
there are examples of
V
-hyperbolic Reeb manifolds such that
\dim_{\mathbb{R}}{\sf Aut}_{V}M=n
. Moreover, if
\dim_{\mathbb{R}}{\sf Aut}_{V}M=7
, then
(M,V,v)
is equivalent (as Reeb manifold) to the contact symplectic lift of the unit ball
\mathbb{B}^{2}
endowed with the holomorphic symplectic form
\frac{2}{(1-z)^{3}}dz\wedge dw
.

The previous result is presented in Section
8
, where we also improve the previous bound for
\dim_{\mathbb{R}}{\sf Aut}_{V}M
in the case
\dim M=5
. The proof partially relies on Isaev’s classification of Kobayashi hyperbolic manifolds in terms of the dimension of their automorphism groups. Indeed, Isaev’s classification enables the determination (up to biholomorphism) of the possible holomorphic symplectic manifolds
S
in the contact symplectic lifts
(M,V,v,S,\omega)
defined by
(M,V,v)
. For instance, if
\dim_{\mathbb{R}}{\sf Aut}_{V}M=7
, it follows that
\dim_{\mathbb{R}}{\sf Aut}S\geq 5
. The only possibility is that
S
is biholomorphic to the unit ball
\mathbb{B}^{2}
or to the bidisc
\mathbb{D}^{2}
. However, the latter case is ruled out by carefully inspecting the closed Lie subgroups of
{\sf Aut}\mathbb{D}^{2}
. The case of the ball turns out to be the only possibility, with the symplectic form given in the previous theorem preserved by the 5-dimensional Lie subgroup of
{\sf Aut}\mathbb{B}^{2}
of all automorphisms that fix
(1,0)
.

2.
Contact structures and hyperbolicity

2.1.
Contact structures and contact forms

Let
M
be a complex manifold of complex dimension
2N+1
, with
N\geq 1
.

Definition 2.1
.

A
contact form
on
M
is a holomorphic
1
-form
\xi
so that
\xi\wedge(d\xi)^{N}\neq 0
.
A
local contact form
is a contact form on an open subset of
M
.
A
contact structure

V
is a rank
2N
holomorphic subbundle of
TM
such that
V
is locally the kernel of a local contact form.
A
contact manifold

(M,V)
is a complex manifold
M
with a contact structure
V
.

2.2.
The standard example

Any two contact structures, on complex manifolds of equal dimension, are locally isomorphic, by the obvious holomorphic analogue of the Darboux theorem for contact manifolds
[
24
, p. 66]
.
Hence any contact structure is locally isomorphic to the standard contact structure
V
on
\mathbb{C}^{2N+1}
, with coordinates
(z,w,y)\in\mathbb{C}^{N}\times\mathbb{C}^{N}\times\mathbb{C}
, with contact form

(2.1)

\xi=dy-z_{1}\,dw^{1}-\dots-z_{N}\,dw^{N}.

In any open set where the contact structure is given by this contact form, the vector fields tangent to the contact structure are precisely those which, at each point, lie in the span of

\partial_{z_{1}},\dots,\partial_{z_{N}},\partial_{w_{1}}+z_{1}\partial_{y},\dots,\partial_{w_{N}}+z_{N}\partial_{y}.

Their Lie brackets clearly span
\mathbb{C}^{2N+1}
since

\left[\partial_{z_{j}},\partial_{w_{j}}+z_{j}\partial_{y}\right]=\partial_{y}.

Hence the contact structure is one step bracket generating.
In
\mathbb{C}^{2N+1}
, the holomorphic vector field
\partial_{y}
is not tangent to the contact structure, but its flow (translation in the
y
variable) preserves the contact form, and hence preserves the contact structure:
\partial_{y}
is a
Reeb vector field
of the standard contact structure in complex Euclidean space
\mathbb{C}^{2N+1}
.
We will define Reeb vector fields more generally in section
3.1
.

2.3.
Legendrian discs

Definition 2.2
.

Let
M
be a complex manifold endowed with a contact structure
V
.
A holomorphic map
\varphi\colon C\to M
from a Riemann surface
C
is a
V
-Legendrian curve
or
V
-curve
for short, if, for every
\zeta\in C
,
d_{\zeta}\varphi(T_{\zeta}C)\subset V_{\varphi(\zeta)}
. If
C
is the unit disc, the
V
-curve is a
V
-disc
, also called a
Legendrian disc.

2.4.
The pseudometric

Let
\mathbb{D}:=\{\zeta\in\mathbb{C}:|\zeta|<1\}
and let
k_{\mathbb{D}}
denote its hyperbolic distance and
\kappa_{\mathbb{D}}
its hyperbolic metric; see, e.g.
[
1
]
or
[
32
]
.

Given any
v\in V_{p}
, let
\mathcal{L}_{p}(v)
be the family of all
V
-discs
\varphi\colon\mathbb{D}\to M
such that
\varphi^{\prime}(0)=\lambda_{\varphi}v
for some
\lambda_{\varphi}\in\mathbb{C}
.
By the Darboux theorem for contact manifolds,
\mathcal{L}_{p}(v)
contains a
V
-disc
\varphi
with
\lambda_{\varphi}\neq 0
.
The
Kobayashi–Royden pseudometric
of
V
is

\kappa_{V}(p;v):=\inf\left\{\frac{1}{|\lambda_{\varphi}|}:\varphi\in\mathcal{L}_{p}(v)\right\}=\inf\left\{\kappa_{\mathbb{D}}\left(0;\frac{1}{\lambda_{\varphi}}\right):\varphi\in\mathcal{L}_{p}(v)\right\}

The function
\kappa_{V}(p;\cdot)\colon V\to[0,+\infty)
is a quasi-norm (in the sense of
[
32
, Chapter 1, Section 4, p. 14]
) and it is upper semicontinuous in
p
(see
[
17
, Prop. 1.5, p. 9]
). Thus
\kappa_{V}
is a sub-Finsler quasi-norm on
M

[
14
]
.

Remark 2.3
.

Since
\kappa_{\mathbb{D}}
is invariant under the group of automorphisms of
\mathbb{D}
, if
\varphi\colon\mathbb{D}\to M
is a
V
-disc then

\kappa_{V}(\varphi(\zeta);\varphi^{\prime}(\zeta))\leq\kappa_{\mathbb{D}}(\zeta,1).

2.5.
The pseudodistance

Suppose that
M
is connected.
For any pair of points
p,q\in M
, let
\mathcal{W}_{p,q}
be the set of all piecewise smooth curves
\gamma\colon[0,1]\to M
such that
\gamma(0)=p
and
\gamma(1)=q
and
\gamma^{\prime}(t)\in V_{\gamma(t)}
for almost every
t\in[0,1]
.
By Sussmann’s orbit theorem
[
40
]
, we can connect any two points of
M
by a continuous and
piecewise smooth path composed of finitely many flow lines of local sections of
V
.
Therefore
\mathcal{W}_{p,q}
is not empty.
Given
\gamma\in\mathcal{W}_{p,q}
, the
V
-length
of
\gamma
is

\ell_{V}(\gamma):=\int_{0}^{1}\kappa_{V}(\gamma(t);\gamma^{\prime}(t))dt.

The
Kobayashi pseudodistance
of
V
is

k_{V}(p,q):=\inf\{\ell_{V}(\gamma):\gamma\in\mathcal{W}_{p,q}\}.

The following is a variant of the Schwarz lemma:

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
M
be a complex manifold endowed with a contact structure
V
.
Let
\varphi\colon\mathbb{D}\to M
be a
V
-disc. Then for every
\zeta,\tilde{\zeta}\in\mathbb{D}

k_{V}(\varphi(\zeta),\varphi(\tilde{\zeta}))\leq k_{\mathbb{D}}(\zeta,\tilde{\zeta}).

Proof.

Take a geodesic
\sigma\colon[0,1]\to\mathbb{D}
for the Poincaré distance such that
\sigma(0)=\zeta
and
\sigma(1)=\tilde{\zeta}
. Hence
\varphi\circ\sigma\in\mathcal{W}_{\varphi(\zeta),\varphi(\tilde{\zeta})}
. Therefore, by Remark
2.3
,

\begin{split}k_{V}(\varphi(\zeta),\varphi(\tilde{\zeta}))&\leq\ell_{V}(\varphi\circ\sigma)=\int_{0}^{1}\kappa_{V}(\varphi(\sigma(t));\varphi^{\prime}(\sigma(t))\sigma^{\prime}(t))dt\\
&\leq\int_{0}^{1}\kappa_{\mathbb{D}}(\sigma(t);\sigma^{\prime}(t))dt=k_{\mathbb{D}}(\zeta,\tilde{\zeta}).\end{split}

∎

To establish some properties of
k_{V}
, we need a “local” lemma:

Lemma 2.5
.

For
r\in(0,1]
, let
B_{r}=\{(z,w,y)\in\mathbb{C}^{2N}\times\mathbb{C}:\|(z,w)\|^{2}<r^{2},|y|<r\}
endowed with the standard contact structure
V_{0}
defined by the contact form
\xi
given by (
2.1
). Then

\lim_{(z,w,y)\to(0,0,0)}k_{V_{0}}((z,w,y),(0,0,0))=0.

Proof.

For simplicity, we only prove the result for
N=1
, the general case is similar.

Indeed, if
(z,w,y)
is sufficiently close to
(0,0,0)
, one can connect
(z,w,y)
to
(0,0,0)
using a combination of three curves tangent to
V_{0}
, whose
V_{0}
-length tends to
0
as
(z,w,y)
converges to
(0,0,0)
. Such
V_{0}
-curves have the following expressions:

\displaystyle\gamma_{1}(t)=

\displaystyle(t,w,y),

\displaystyle\gamma_{2}(t)=

\displaystyle(0,t,y),

\displaystyle\gamma_{3}(t)=

\displaystyle(\sqrt{y},\sqrt{y}+t,\sqrt{y}t).

First one can use
\gamma_{1}
and
\gamma_{2}
to connect
(z,w,y)
to
(0,2\sqrt{y},y)
, then again
\gamma_{1}
to reach
(\sqrt{y},2\sqrt{y},y)
. One can then use
\gamma_{3}
to connect to
(\sqrt{y},\sqrt{y},0)
, and finally again
\gamma_{1}
and
\gamma_{2}
to connect to
(0,0,0)
.
∎

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
M
be a connected complex manifold endowed with a contact structure
V
.
Then
k_{V}(p,q)<+\infty
for every
p,q\in M
and
k_{V}\colon M\times M\to[0,+\infty)
is continuous.

Proof.

Since every two points in
M
can be joined by piecewise smooth curve almost everywhere tangent to
V
and
\kappa_{V}
is upper semicontinuous, it follows easily that
k_{V}(p,q)<+\infty
for every
p,q\in M
.

To prove that
k_{V}
is continuous, by triangle inequality it is enough to show that
\lim_{p\to q}k_{V}(p,q)=0
. Again, this follows at once by Lemma
2.5
choosing a holomorphic chart biholomorphic to
B
centered at
q
with coordinates
(z,w,y)\in\mathbb{C}^{N}\times\mathbb{C}^{N}\times\mathbb{C}
so that the contact form in these local coordinates is given by (
2.1
).
∎

2.6.
Hyperbolicity of contact structures

Definition 2.7
.

Let
M
be a connected complex manifold endowed with a contact structure
V
. We say that
M
is
V
-hyperbolic
if
k_{V}
is a distance, that is,
k_{V}(p,q)=0
if and only if
p=q
. We also say that
M
is
complete
V
-hyperbolic
if
M
is
V
-hyperbolic and
k_{V}
is a complete distance.

Proposition 2.8
.

Let
M
be a connected complex manifold endowed with a contact structure
V
.
Then
M
is
V
-hyperbolic if and only if
k_{V}
induces the manifold topology on
M
.

Proof.

Clearly, if
k_{V}
induces the manifold topology on
M
then
M
is
V
-hyperbolic. Conversely, suppose
M
is
V
-hyperbolic and assume by contradiction that there exist a relatively compact open set
U\subset M
,
p\in U
and a sequence
\{p_{n}\}\subset M\setminus\overline{U}
such that
\lim_{n\to\infty}k_{V}(p_{n},p)=0
. This implies that for every
n
we can find
\gamma_{n}\in\mathcal{W}_{p,p_{n}}
such that

\ell_{V}(\gamma_{n})<k_{V}(p,p_{n})+\frac{1}{n}.

The curve
\gamma_{n}
is continuous, hence it contains a point
q_{n}\in\partial U
. Clearly,

k_{V}(p,q_{n})\leq\ell_{V}(\gamma_{n})<k_{V}(p,p_{n})+\frac{1}{n}.

Since
\partial U
is compact, we can assume that
\{q_{n}\}
converges to some point
q_{0}\in\partial U
. Taking into account that
M
is
V
-hyperbolic,
k_{V}(p,q_{0})>0
. But,
k_{V}
is continuous by Proposition
2.6
, hence

0<k_{V}(p,q_{0})=\lim_{n\to\infty}k_{V}(p,q_{n})\leq\lim_{n\to\infty}\ell_{V}(\gamma_{n})<\lim_{n\to\infty}k_{V}(p,p_{n})+\frac{1}{n}=0,

a contradiction.
∎

One can define a (pseudo)distance
á la
Kobayashi on

following exactly the same lines of the original construction of the Kobayashi (pseudo)distance using chains of

-discs. We briefly sketch the definition.

Let

. Again by Sussmann’s orbit theorem
[

]
, we can connect any two points of

by a chain of

\mathcal{C}=\{(\varphi_{j},t_{j})\}

is a finite set of

-Legendrian discs

,

, such that
\varphi_{1}(0)=p
,
\varphi_{m}(t_{m})=q

\varphi_{j}(t_{j})=\varphi_{j+1}(0)
.
The

is given by

\ell_{V}(\mathcal{C}):=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\log\frac{1+t_{j}}{1-t_{j}}=\sum_{j=1}^{m}k_{\mathbb{D}}(0,t_{j}).

Let
\tilde{k}_{V}(p,q)
be the infimum of the length of all

-chains between

and

.
It is clear that
\tilde{k}_{V}
is a pseudodistance on

.
By Lemma
2.4
, for all
p,q\in M

(2.2)

k_{V}(p,q)\leq\tilde{k}_{V}(p,q).

Remark 2.9
.

Royden’s famous result
[
36
]
(see also Barth
[
5
]
) states that the Kobayashi distance defined via chains of holomorphic discs is equal to the integrated distance defined via the Kobayashi metric. A key ingredient in the proof is that the Kobayashi distance is Lipschitz continuous. However, in the context of contact-hyperbolicity, this does not seem to hold. In fact, if one could prove a “ball-box theorem’ for
k_{V}
, in the spirit of those in sub-Riemannian geometry, this would only imply some Hölder regularity for
k_{V}
. Therefore, the original proof of Royden appears to be inapplicable in this context. It remains an open question whether
k_{V}=\tilde{k}_{V}
.

3.
Automorphisms preserving hyperbolic contact structures

Definition 3.1
.

Take contact manifolds
(M,V)
and
(M^{\prime},V^{\prime})
.
A
contact morphism

(M,V)\xrightarrow{\Phi}(M^{\prime},V^{\prime})
is a holomorphic immersion
M\xrightarrow{\Phi}M^{\prime}
so that, for every point
p\in M
,
d\Phi_{p}\left(V_{p}\right)\subseteq V^{\prime}_{p^{\prime}}
where
p^{\prime}=\Phi(p)
.

Lemma 3.2
.

A contact morphism does not increase the contact Kobayashi pseudodistance (or the pseudodistance á la Kobayashi) of any two points, and does not increase the Kobayashi–Royden pseudometric on any tangent vector, that is,

\displaystyle k_{V^{\prime}}(\varphi(p),\varphi(q))

\displaystyle\leq k_{V}(p,q),

\displaystyle\tilde{k}_{V^{\prime}}(\varphi(p),\varphi(q))

\displaystyle\leq\tilde{k}_{V}(p,q),

\displaystyle\kappa_{V^{\prime}}(\varphi(p),d\varphi_{p}(v))

\displaystyle\leq\kappa_{V}(p,v),

for any points
p,q\in M
and tangent vector
v\in T_{p}M
.

Definition 3.3
.

Let
M
be a complex contact manifold with contact structure
V
.
A
V
-automorphism
of
M
is a bijective contact morphism
M\to M
, which therefore has an inverse contact morphism. We denote by
{\sf Aut}_{V}M
the group of automorphisms of the contact manifold
(M,V)
.

By definition of
\kappa_{V}
and
k_{V}
:

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
\Phi\in{\sf Aut}_{V}M
. Then
\Phi^{\ast}\kappa_{M}=\kappa_{M}
and
\Phi^{\ast}k_{M}=k_{M}
. In other words,
\Phi
is an isometry for the
V
-pseudo-metric
\kappa_{V}
and for the
V
-pseudo-distance
k_{V}
.

As in the standard case of Kobayashi hyperbolic manifolds, in case
M
is
V
-hyperbolic,
{\sf Aut}_{V}M
is a finite dimensional Lie group:

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
M
be a connected complex manifold of complex dimension
2N+1
endowed with a hyperbolic contact structure
V
.
Then
{\sf Aut}_{V}M
is a real Lie group of dimension at most
2N^{2}+5N+4
acting smoothly and properly on
M
.

Proof.

Let
G:={\sf Aut}_{k_{V}}M
be the group of biholomorphisms of
M
preserving the metric
k_{V}
.
Then
G
is a Lie group acting smoothly and properly on
M

[
31
, Satz 2.5]
.
The group
{\sf Aut}_{V}M
is a closed subgroup of
G
, hence a Lie subgroup
[
27
, p. 319 Theorem 9.3.7]
, so a Lie group acting smoothly and properly on
M
.
For every
p\in M
, let
I_{p}:=\{\Phi\in{\sf Aut}_{V}M|\Phi(p)=p\}
be the isotropy of
{\sf Aut}_{V}M
.
By
[
7
]
,
I_{p}
is compact and is faithfully represented by its linearized version
L_{p}:=\{d\Phi_{p}:\Phi\in I_{p}\}
.

Since
V_{p}
is
L_{p}
-invariant and
L_{p}
is compact,
V_{p}
has an
L_{p}
-invariant complement
\mathbb{C}
.
The tangent space
T_{p}M
can be decomposed as
V_{p}\oplus\mathbb{C}
. A local contact form
\xi
is uniquely defined up to replacing by
\xi^{\prime}=e^{f}\xi
for any holomorphic function
f
defined near
p
.
On
V_{p}
,
\left.d\xi^{\prime}\right|_{p}=e^{f(p)}\left.d\xi\right|_{p}
.
Hence
V_{p}
is endowed with a complex symplectic form given by
\omega=-\left.d\xi\right|_{p}
, well defined up to scaling by a nonzero complex constant. The action consists of isometries preserving the symplectic form up to a scale factor when restricted to
V_{p}
, hence
L_{p}
is a subgroup of
Sp_{N}\times\mathbb{S}^{1}\times\mathbb{S}^{1}
. Recall that the compact symplectic group
Sp_{N}
acting as complex symplectic and unitary transformations on
\mathbb{C}^{2N}
has real dimension
\dim_{\mathbb{R}}Sp_{N}=N(2N+1)
. This implies that
\dim_{\mathbb{R}}I_{p}\leq N(2N+1)+1+1=2N^{2}+N+2
. Since
\dim_{\mathbb{R}}M=2(2N+1)
, each orbit has dimension bound
\dim_{\mathbb{R}}{\mathcal{O}(p)}\leq\dim_{\mathbb{R}}{M}=2(2N+1)=4N+2
.

Consider the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
of
{\sf Aut}_{V}M
, and the Lie subalgebra
\mathfrak{g}_{p}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}
of the isotropy subgroup
I_{p}
.
For each
v\in\mathfrak{g}
, the infinitesimal generator of such a group, call it
X_{v}
, is an
\mathbb{R}
-complete holomorphic vector field on
M
whose flow preserves
V
.

The Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}_{p}
consists precisely of the elements
A\in\mathfrak{g}
such that
A(p)=0
. The tangent space to the orbit
\mathcal{O}(p)
consists of the values
A(p)
for
A\in\mathfrak{g}
.
Hence there is an exact sequence of linear maps

0\to\mathfrak{g}_{p}\to\mathfrak{g}\to T_{p}\mathcal{O}(p)\to 0

given by the inclusion map
\mathfrak{g}_{p}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}
and the map

\mathfrak{g}\ni A\mapsto A(p)\in T_{p}\mathcal{O}(p).

Therefore
\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{g}_{p}=T_{p}\mathcal{O}(p)
and so

\dim_{\mathbb{R}}{\sf Aut}_{V}M=\dim_{\mathbb{R}}\mathfrak{g},\ \dim_{\mathbb{R}}I_{p}=\dim_{\mathbb{R}}\mathfrak{g}_{p},\ \dim_{\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{O}(p)=\dim_{\mathbb{R}}T_{p}\mathcal{O}(p),

from which

\dim_{\mathbb{R}}{\sf Aut}_{V}M=\dim_{\mathbb{R}}I_{p}+\dim_{\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{O}(p)\leq 2N^{2}+N+2+4N+2=2N^{2}+5N+4.

∎

3.1.
Reeb vector fields

Definition 3.6

(Geiges
[
24
, p. 5]
)
.

Let
M
be a complex manifold endowed with a contact structure
V
. A
Reeb vector field
is a holomorphic vector field
v
on
M
such that
v(p)\not\in V_{p}
for all
p\in M
and whose flow preserves
V
.

Lemma 3.7

(Geiges
[
24
, p. 5]
)
.

Let
M
be a complex manifold endowed with a contact structure
V
.
For each Reeb vector field
v
of
V
there is a unique global contact form
\xi
for
V
(that is,
V=\operatorname{ker}\xi
), so that
\iota_{v}{\xi}\equiv 1
.
Conversely, if
\xi
is a global contact form for
V
, there is a unique Reeb vector field
v
for
V
such that
\xi(v)\equiv 1
and
\iota_{v}{d\xi}\equiv 0
.

Note that, by the Cartan formula

{\mathcal{L}}_{v}\xi=\iota_{v}{d\xi}+d(\iota_{v}{\xi}),

every Reeb vector field is a symmetry of its contact form.
Conversely, it is the unique symmetry of its contact form with
\iota_{v}{\xi}=1
.

Remark 3.8
.

Suppose that
M
is a contact manifold with
\mathbb{C}
-complete Reeb vector field
v
. Let
V
be the contact structure on
M
.
Note that
v(p)\neq 0
for all
p\in M
.
Hence the flow of
v
acts locally freely, but perhaps not freely.
Since
v(p)\not\in V_{p}
for all
p\in M
, in particular
v(p)\neq 0
for all
p\in M
.
So there is no point of
M
which is fixed under the flow of
v
for times
t\neq 0
approaching
t=0
.
Therefore, for any point
p\in M
, the times
t\in\mathbb{C}
in which the flow of
v
takes
p
to itself form a discrete subgroup
\chi_{p}(v)
of
\mathbb{C}
.

With the previous remark at hand, we can give the following

Definition 3.9
.

Suppose that
M
is a contact manifold with
\mathbb{C}
-complete Reeb vector field
v
. We say that
v

flows freely
, or that
(M,V,v)
is a
Reeb manifold
, if
\chi_{p}(v)=\{0\}
at every point
p\in M
.
Equivalently,
(M,V,v)
is a
Reeb manifold
if the flow
\mathbb{C}\times M\xrightarrow{\Phi}M
of
v
is a free holomorphic action of
\mathbb{C}
on
M
.
If this
\mathbb{C}
-action is free and proper, we say that
(M,V,v)
is a
proper Reeb manifold
.

Remark 3.10
.

Forstnerič
[
22
]
proved the existence of a hyperbolic contact structure
V
on
\mathbb{C}^{3}
. His structure does not admit a complete Reeb vector field, because by Theorem
6.1
(see later),
\mathbb{C}^{3}
would have a holomorphic fiber bundle map to a Kobayashi hyperbolic complex surface
S
.
But all holomorphic maps from a complex Euclidean space of any dimension, to any Kobayashi hyperbolic manifold are constant.
We expect the group of automorphisms of this contact manifold to be discrete.

In case of
hyperbolic
contact structure, a Reeb vector field flowing freely also acts properly, namely we have

Proposition 3.11
.

Let
(M,V,v)
be a Reeb manifold of complex dimension
2N+1
with a hyperbolic contact structure.
Then
(M,V,v)
is a proper Reeb manifold and

2\leq\dim_{\mathbb{R}}{\sf Aut}_{V}M\leq 2N^{2}+5N+4.

Proof.

The flow of the Reeb vector field is by isomorphisms of the contact structure, hence the flow acts properly by Theorem
3.5
.
The upper estimate is given by Theorem
3.5
.
The lower estimate follows from the existence of the Reeb vector field, whose flow provides a real
2
-dimensional subgroup of
{\sf Aut}_{V}M
.
∎

4.
Contact symplectic lifts

4.1.
Definitions and first properties

Definition 4.1
.

Let
S
be a complex manifold of complex dimension
2N
for some
N\geq 1
.
A
holomorphic symplectic form
on
S
is a closed holomorphic
2
-form
\omega
on
S
so that
\omega^{2N}
is nowhere zero.
A holomorphic symplectic form
\omega
on
S
is
holomorphically exact
if
\omega=d\vartheta
for some holomorphic
1
-form
\vartheta
while it is
C^{\infty}
-exact
if
\omega=d\vartheta
for some
C^{\infty}
complex-valued
1
-form
\vartheta
.
We say that
(S,\omega)
is an
holomorphically exact symplectic manifold
if
\omega
is a holomorphically exact symplectic form on
S
, while we say that
(S,\omega)
is a
C^{\infty}
-exact holomorphic symplectic manifold
if
\omega
is a
C^{\infty}
-exact holomorphic symplectic form on
S
.

Definition 4.2
.

Let
(M,V,v)
be a Reeb manifold.
Let
\xi
denote the global contact form for
V
such that
\xi(v)\equiv 1
.
Let
(S,\omega)
be a holomorphic symplectic manifold.
A
contact symplectic lift
is a holomorphic surjective map
\pi\colon M\to S
(which we call the
contact symplectic projection
) such that

(1)

\pi\colon M\to S
is a
\mathbb{C}
-principal bundle, whose
\mathbb{C}
-action is the flow of the Reeb vector field.

(2)

d\xi=-\pi^{\ast}\omega
.

We denote the contact symplectic lift by the data
(M,V,v,S,\omega)
, with the map
\pi
understood.
If
\omega
is holomorphically exact,
(M,V,v,S,\omega)
is a
holomorphically exact contact symplectic lift
.
If
\omega
is
C^{\infty}
-exact,
(M,V,v,S,\omega)
is a
C^{\infty}
-exact contact symplectic lift
.

Remark 4.3
.

Since
d\xi=-\pi^{\ast}\omega
,
M
has complex dimension
2N+1
and
S
has complex dimension
2N
, for a unique integer
N\geq 1
.

Remark 4.4
.

Take a contact symplectic lift
(M,V,v,S,\omega)
.
Since the fiber of
\pi\colon M\to S
is contractible,
M
is homotopically equivalent to
S
. In particular,
M
is contractible if and only if
S
is.

Lemma 4.5
.

A Reeb manifold
(M,V,v)
is proper if and only if it is the Reeb manifold of a contact symplectic lift
(M,V,v,S,\omega)
.
If
(M,V,v)
is the Reeb manifold of two contact symplectic lifts
(M,V,v,S,\omega)
,
(M,V,v,S^{\prime},\omega^{\prime})
then there is a unique biholomorphism
S\xrightarrow{\varphi}S^{\prime}
so that the diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S^{\prime}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varphi}

commutes and so that
\varphi^{*}\omega^{\prime}=\omega
.

Proof.

If
(M,V,v,S,\omega)
is a contact symplectic lift, then
(M,V,v)
is clearly a proper Reeb manifold.
Suppose that
(M,V,v)
is a proper Reeb manifold.
The flow of
v
is a free and proper holomorphic
\mathbb{C}
-action.
Let
S
be the quotient space and
\pi\colon M\to S
the projection,
\pi(p)=[p]
, where
[p]:=\{q\in M:\exists\zeta\in\mathbb{C}:\phi(\zeta,p)=q\}
.
The quotient
S=M/G
of a manifold
M
by a smooth, free and proper action of a Lie group
G
is a manifold, and the quotient map
\pi\colon M\to M/G
is a smooth principal bundle
[
2
, p. 286, Proposition 4.1.23]
.
As is customary, one can endowed
S
with a unique holomorphic structure in such a way that
\pi\colon M\to S
is a
\mathbb{C}
-principal bundle.

The contact form
\xi
yields a nowhere vanishing holomorphic
2
-form
d\xi
invariant under the flow of the Reeb vector field
v
.
By the Cartan formula,

0={\mathcal{L}}_{v}\xi=\iota_{v}d\xi+d(\iota_{v}\xi)=\iota_{v}d\xi.

Note that any two tangent vectors
\tilde{X},\hat{X}\in T_{p}M
satisfy
d\pi_{p}(\tilde{X})=d\pi_{p}(\hat{X})
if and only if
\tilde{X}-\hat{X}=\lambda v(p)
for some
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
.
For any
X,Y\in T_{\pi(p)}S
,
p\in M
, let

\omega_{\pi(p)}(X,Y):=-d\xi_{p}(\tilde{X},\tilde{Y}),

where
\tilde{X},\tilde{Y}\in T_{p}M
are vectors such that
d\pi_{p}(\tilde{X})=X,d\pi_{p}(\tilde{Y})=Y
.
Clearly
\omega
is a well defined holomorphic
2
-form on
S
.
By construction
\pi^{*}\omega=-d\xi
.
Suppose that the complex dimension of
S
is
2N
.
Then
\pi^{*}d\omega=0
, so
d\omega=0
, and
\omega
is non-degenerate because

\xi\wedge(\omega)^{N}=(-1)^{N}\xi\wedge(d\xi)^{N}\neq 0.

Thus,
(M,V,v,S,\omega)
is a contact symplectic lift.
The form
\omega
is then the unique holomorphic symplectic form with
\pi^{*}\omega=-d\xi
, since pullback of forms by any submersion is injective.

Suppose that there are two contact symplectic lifts
(M,V,v,S,\omega)
,
(M,V,v,S^{\prime},\omega^{\prime})
of the same Reeb manifold
(M,V,v)
.
The map
M\to S
has fibers precisely the orbits of
\mathbb{C}
-action of the flow of
v
, and the same for
M\to S^{\prime}
.
Therefore the map
M\to S^{\prime}
descends to a bijection
\varphi\colon S\to S^{\prime}
by demanding that
\varphi(s)=s^{\prime}
just when
s\in S
and
s^{\prime}\in S^{\prime}
are the images of the same point of
M
.

The map
M\to S
is a holomorphic submersion, so admits local holomorphic sections near any point of
S
.
Compose any section with
M\to S^{\prime}
to see that
\varphi\colon S\to S^{\prime}
is holomorphic.
By the same reasoning,
\varphi^{-1}\colon S^{\prime}\to S
is holomorphic.
So
\varphi
is a biholomorphism.
Again, the form
\omega
is the unique holomorphic symplectic form with
\pi^{*}\omega=-d\xi
, since pullback of forms by any submersion is injective.
So
\varphi^{*}\omega^{\prime}=\omega
.
∎

4.2.
Local contact symplectic coordinates

We want to provide a local description of contact symplectic lifts.
Locally, every principal bundle is trivial.

Definition 4.6
.

A contact symplectic lift
(M,V,v,S,\omega)
is
trivialized
if

•

M=S\times\mathbb{C}
is a holomorphically trivial principal bundle and

•

writing points of
M
as
m=(x,y)
, the bundle map is
\pi(x,y)=x
and

•

the bundle action is translation in
y
, so the Reeb vector field is
v=\partial_{y}
.

A contact symplectic lift
(M,V,v,S,\omega)
is
trivial
if it admits a
trivialization
,
i.e.
a biholomorphism of
M
to a trivialized contact symplectic lift, matching up the principal
\mathbb{C}
-actions, and hence matching up the Reeb vector fields.

Definition 4.7
.

Take a holomorphic symplectic manifold
(S,\omega)
.
A
holomorphic symplectic potential
is a holomorphic
1
-form
\nu
on
S
with
d\nu=\omega
.

Definition 4.8
.

Take a contact symplectic lift
(M,V,v,S,\omega)
and suppose
\nu
is a holomorphic symplectic potential.
The contact symplectic lift is
\nu
-fit
if the bundle
M\to S
admits a trivialization in which, using the notation of Definition
4.6
, the contact form
\xi
on
M
associated to the Reeb vector field
v=\partial_{y}
is
\xi=dy-\pi^{*}\nu
.
A contact symplectic lift
(M,V,v,S,\omega)
is
fit
if it is
\nu
-fit for some holomorphic symplectic potential
\nu
.

Note that, the choice of different holomorphic symplectic potential might bring to different trivializations. However we have the following (preliminary) result:

Lemma 4.9
.

Take a contact symplectic lift
(M,V,v,S,\omega)
and two holomorphic symplectic potentials
\nu
and
\nu^{\prime}
.
Suppose that
H^{1}(S,\mathbb{C})=0
.
Then the contact symplectic lift is
\nu
-fit if and only if it is
\nu^{\prime}
-fit.

Proof.

Being holomorphic symplectic potentials,
d\nu=d\nu^{\prime}=\omega
so
d(\nu^{\prime}-\nu)=0
.
But
H^{1}(S,\mathbb{C})=0
so
\nu^{\prime}=\nu+dh
for some
C^{\infty}
function

.
Since
dh=\nu^{\prime}-\nu
is
(1,0)
,
h
is holomorphic.
Write
\xi=dy-\pi^{*}\nu
.
Then, letting
y^{\prime}(x,y):=y+h(x)
it is easy to check that

dy^{\prime}-\pi^{*}\nu^{\prime}=dy+d\pi^{*}h-d\pi^{*}h-\pi^{*}\nu=dy-\pi^{*}\nu.

So the map

S\times\mathbb{C}\ni(x,y)\mapsto(x,y^{\prime})=(x,y+h(x))\in S\times\mathbb{C}

trivializes
M
as a
\nu^{\prime}
-fit contact symplectic lift.
∎

Definition 4.10
.

By the obvious holomorphic analogue of the Darboux theorem for symplectic manifolds

[

]

[

]

[

]

We now extend the Darboux theorem for contact manifolds to contact symplectic lifts.

(Local contact symplectic coordinates)

.
The flow of the Reeb vector field is the

So

So the coefficients of

.
Let

i.e.

Remark 4.16

Let

-exact if and only if
[\mu]=0
where
[\mu]\in H^{2}_{dR}(S)
is the class of

in the de Rham cohomology of

. By the de Rham theorem,
H^{2}_{dR}(S)
is isomorphic to
H^{2}(S,\mathbb{C})
, the cohomology of the sheaf of locally constant complex functions.
Recall how such an isomorphism is defined.
Choose a good open covering
\{S_{\alpha}\}
of

(see,

,
[
9

\{S_{\alpha}\}

Let

By Leray’s theorem

By the the holomorphic Poincaré lemma, (see

Thus, on

(whenever not empty), there is a holomorphic function

On

c_{\alpha\beta\gamma}:=f_{\alpha\beta}+f_{\beta\gamma}+f_{\gamma\alpha}

is constant and defines an element

in

(for details see,

[

Note that

for some

-form on

if and only if

-exact.

We add a comment in case

This occurs precisely when there exist constants

so that

c_{\alpha\beta\gamma}:=c_{\alpha\beta}+c_{\beta\gamma}+c_{\gamma\alpha}.

Replacing

Thus

is a

which we denote by

The

is holomorphically exact just when we can alter every

These

So

Theorem 4.17
.

Every contact symplectic lift is

-exact.

Proof.

We provide two proofs.
Our first proof uses differential topology.
Every

Our second proof is by means of a constructive argument which will be used later on.
Choose a good Leray open covering

By the Darboux theorem for symplectic manifolds, we can choose coordinates

4.6.

We review standard material on holomorphic connections
[

,

and apply it to contact symplectic lifts as principal bundles.

For any manifold

and vector space
V
, denote by

the trivial vector bundle

.
Take a holomorphic left principal bundle

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces G}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces P}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi}

on a complex manifold
X
.
Recall that the
pullback bundle

\pi^{*}TX
is the set of pairs
(p,v)
with
p\in P
and
v\in T_{x}X
where
x:=\pi(p)
.
Differentiate the bundle map
P\xrightarrow{\pi}X
to map
T_{p}P\xrightarrow{d\pi_{p}}T_{x}X
where
x:=\pi(p)
.
This defines a map
TP\xrightarrow{d\pi}\pi^{*}TX
,
(p,v)\mapsto(p,d\pi_{p}(v))
.
The left action of the bundle we denote by

(g,p)\in G\times P\mapsto gp\in P.

Denote by
\mathfrak{g}
the Lie algebra of
G
.
Every element
A\in\mathfrak{g}
gives a
1
-parameter subgroup

t\in\mathbb{R}\mapsto e^{tA}\in G.

Differentiate its action to define a vector field

\mathscr{X}_{A}(p):=\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{t=0}e^{tA}p.

Then
\mathscr{X}_{A}(p)
depends linearly on

, a Lie algebra action.
By linearity, for any basis
\{A_{j}\}\subset\mathfrak{g}
, the Lie algebra action is determined by the vector fields
\mathscr{X}_{A_{j}}
, and the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
itself is determined from the Lie brackets of these vector fields.
If
G
is connected, then not only do the vector fields
\mathscr{X}_{A_{j}}
determine the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
and its Lie algebra action on
P
, but this Lie algebra action on
P
determines the
G
-action, hence determines the quotient
X=P/G
by that action.

So the holomorphic principal bundle is determined by specifying a finite set of holomorphic vector fields
\{\mathscr{X}_{A_{j}}\}
on a complex manifold

Lemma 4.21

(

(

(

Every (holomorphic) submersion is, locally on

4.7.
Monodromy

Take a contact symplectic lift

.
Suppose the holomorphic symplectic form

As in Proposition 6.2, the

-form

Since
\mathbb{C}
is abelian, this group morphism vanishes on the derived subgroup.
Applying the Hurewicz homomorphism (Bott & Tu, p. 225, Theorem 17.21), the monodromy determines and is determined by a unique element

of the symplectic potential

If we pick a point

This path ends at some point

is the monodromy of that loop

.

If we change the symplectic potential
\nu
only by adding an exact term, say

The

-parallel lift of a path is

So the monodromy is unchanged: the monodromy depends only on the holomorphic de Rham cohomology class of the holomorphic symplectic potential.

The monodromy vanishes just when the bundle is trivial,

,

•

is a holomorphically trivial principal bundle and

•

•

, parallel for the flat connection.
By the Frobenius theorem, the lifts all lie on a unique complex hypersurface

By

The monodromy vanishes just when loops lift to loops,

that covering space is just biholomorphic to

Let

Then clearly the pullback contact symplectic lift on

Suppose that there is a global holomorphic section

Suppose that the bundle is holomorphically trivial, so we can suppose that

•

is a good covering,

, thus

defines a class in

that we denote by
[\mathcal{M}_{1}-\mathcal{M}_{2}]
or by

For any contact symplectic lift

let

\lambda\mathcal{M}:=(M,V,v/\lambda,S,\lambda\omega).

Clearly this scaling commutes with equivalence to define a scaling by complex nonzero constants on

, and

\lambda[\mathcal{M}_{1}-\mathcal{M}_{2}]=[\lambda\mathcal{M}_{1}-\lambda\mathcal{M}_{2}].

Theorem 5.4
.

Let
(S,\omega)
be a
C^{\infty}
-exact symplectic manifold of complex dimension
2N
. Let
\mathcal{M}_{1},\mathcal{M}_{2}\in\mathcal{C}(S,\omega)
. Then

\Theta(\mathcal{M}_{1},\mathcal{M}_{2})=0

if and only if
\mathcal{M}_{1}\sim\mathcal{M}_{2}
.

In particular, fix any
\mathcal{M}_{0}\in\mathcal{C}(S,\omega)
. Then the map
\Psi_{\mathcal{M}_{0}}\colon\mathcal{M}_{(S,\omega)}\to H^{1}(S,\mathbb{C})
defined by
\Psi_{\mathcal{M}_{0}}(\mathcal{M}):=\Theta(\mathcal{M}_{0},\mathcal{M})
, is bijective.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{M}_{1}:=(M,V,v,S,\omega)
and
\mathcal{M}_{2}:=(M^{\prime},V^{\prime},v^{\prime},S,\omega)
be contact symplectic lifts. Let
\{S_{\alpha}\}
be a good covering of
S
formed by open contractible Stein sets. We use the notations introduced in Remark
5.2
.

If
\Theta(\mathcal{M}_{1},\mathcal{M}_{2})=0
, that is,
[c_{\alpha\beta}]=0
, then there exist constants
c_{\alpha}
such that
c_{\alpha\beta}=c_{\alpha}-c_{\beta}
. If we let
\varphi_{\alpha}(z^{\alpha},w^{\alpha},y^{\alpha}):=(z^{\alpha},w^{\alpha},y^{\alpha}-c_{\alpha})
then
\{\varphi_{\alpha}\}
glue together to an equivalence between
(M,V,v,S,\omega)
and
(M^{\prime},V^{\prime},v^{\prime},S,\omega)
.

Conversely, if
(M,V,v,S,\omega)\sim(M^{\prime},V^{\prime},v^{\prime},S,\omega)
, let
\Phi\colon M\to M^{\prime}
be the biholomorphism such that
\pi_{M^{\prime}}\circ\Phi=\pi_{M}
,
d\Phi(v)=v^{\prime}
and
\Phi^{\ast}\xi^{\prime}=\xi
, where
\xi
is the contact form on
M
such that
\xi(v)=1
,
\xi^{\prime}
is the contact form on
M^{\prime}
such that
\xi^{\prime}(v)=1
. Thus, on each
S_{\alpha}
,
\Phi
is given by

(z^{\alpha},w^{\alpha},y^{\prime\alpha})=\Phi(z^{\alpha},w^{\alpha},y^{\alpha})=(z^{\alpha},w^{\alpha},y^{\alpha}-c_{\alpha}),

for some constant

. That is, on
S_{\alpha}\cap S_{\beta}
(whenever not empty)

c_{\alpha\beta}=f_{\alpha\beta}-f^{\prime}_{\alpha\beta}=y^{\alpha}-y^{\beta}-(y^{\prime\alpha}-y^{\prime\beta})=c_{\alpha}-c_{\beta}.

Therefore,
[\{c_{\alpha\beta}\}]=0
in

, that is,
\Theta(\mathcal{M}_{1},\mathcal{M}_{2})=0
.

Summing up,
\Theta(\mathcal{M}_{1},\mathcal{M}_{2})=0
in
H^{1}(S,\mathbb{C})
if and only if
\mathcal{M}_{1}\sim\mathcal{M}_{2}
.

Fix
\mathcal{M}_{0}:=(M,V,v,S,\omega)\in\mathcal{C}(S,\omega)
and let
\{f_{\alpha\beta}\}\in H^{1}(\{S_{\alpha}\},\mathcal{O}_{S})
be its cocycles as before. For what we just saw, the map
\Psi_{\mathcal{M}_{0}}\colon\mathcal{M}_{(S,\omega)}\to H^{1}(S,\mathbb{C})
is well defined and injective.

To see that it is surjective, given

, note that
\{f^{\prime}_{\alpha\beta}:=f_{\alpha\beta}+c_{\alpha\beta}\}
is still a cocycle in
H^{1}(\{S_{\alpha}\},\mathcal{O}_{S})
and satisfies (

), so, it defines a contact symplectic lift.
∎

Corollary 5.5
.

Let

be a complex manifold of complex dimension

. If

then for every holomorphic symplectic form

on

which is

-exact,

has a unique (up to equivalence) contact symplectic lift.
Furthermore, if

is a holomorphic symplectic form on

which is holomorphically exact, say

for some holomorphic

-form on

is

.

Given two contact symplectic lifts

and
(M^{\prime},V^{\prime},v^{\prime},S,\omega)
, with contact symplectic projections, respectively,

and

, it might be that

and
\pi_{M^{\prime}}\colon M^{\prime}\to S
are isomorphic as
\mathbb{C}
-principal bundles but not equivalent as contact symplectic lifts, even if

is Kobayashi hyperbolic.

Example 5.6
.

Let

, where
\mathbb{D}^{\ast}
denotes the punctured disc. Then

is Kobayashi hyperbolic, Stein, and

. Let
(z,w)\in\mathbb{C}^{2}
and let
\omega:=dz\wedge dw
. Then
(S,\omega)
is a holomorphically exact symplectic manifold of complex dimension

. By Theorem
5.4
, the elements of

are in one-to-one correspondence to (non-equivalent) contact symplectic lifts of
(S,\omega)
.
Therefore, there are infinitely many inequivalent contact symplectic lifts over
(S,\omega)
.
All are hyperbolic.
However, since
S
is Stein,

, so that every hyperbolic contact symplectic lift
(M,V,v,S,\omega)
has the property that

is isomorphic as
\mathbb{C}
-principal bundle over

to the trivial
\mathbb{C}
-principal bundle
S\times\mathbb{C}\to S
.

To be more explicit, let us trace the steps of the proof of Theorem

. Let

S_{j}=\left\{(z,w)\in S|\ {\sf Arg}(w)\in\left(\frac{2j}{3}\pi-\varepsilon,\frac{2(j+1)}{3}\pi+\varepsilon\right)\right\}\text{ for }0<\varepsilon<\frac{1}{3}\pi,

so that
\{S_{0},S_{1},S_{2}\}
is a Leray cover of

.

Any element of
H^{1}(\{S_{\alpha}\},\mathbb{C})
has a representative of the form
(0,0,c)
for

. The coordinate

on

is given by

, where

is a logarithm on

that can be chosen so that

,

, but

. This way

and we obtain the non-equivalent contact manifolds

.

The previous example can be made precise as follows.

.

(M,V,v,S,\omega)
be a contact symplectic lift with projection

. Let

be the associated contact form on

. For every closed holomorphic

-form

on

defines a contact structure

such that

if and only if

in

.

Proof.

The

-form

is clearly

By Lemma

,
\xi_{\theta}
is the connection form for the holomorphic connection
V_{\theta}
.
Its curvature is

since

is closed.
Hence

\displaystyle\xi_{\theta}\wedge(d\xi_{\theta})^{N}

\displaystyle=(\xi+\pi^{*}\theta)\wedge(d(\xi+\pi^{*}\theta))^{N},

\displaystyle=\xi\wedge(d\xi)^{N}+(-1)^{N}\pi^{*}(\theta\wedge\omega^{N}),

\displaystyle=\xi\wedge(d\xi)^{N},

since
\theta\wedge\omega^{N}
is a differential
(2N+1)
-form on a manifold

of complex dimension

.
Therefore

is a contact form.

Suppose that
(M,V_{\theta},v,S,\omega)\sim(M,V_{\theta^{\prime}},v,S,\omega)

Cover

as usual in contractible Stein open sets

, on each of which

.
Trivialize

over

by open sets

on which

On each overlap

.

On

in

.
∎

is Kobayashi hyperbolic.

Conversely, suppose that

, and

Summing up, every

-disc in

\pi(p)

(6.1)

\kappa_{S}(\pi(p);d\pi_{p}(X))\leq\kappa_{V}(p;X).

Integrating this, if

Since

is Kobayashi hyperbolic, it follows that

for every

\pi(p)\neq\pi(q)
.

Assume that

,

\pi(p)=\pi(q)

. Let

\ell_{V}(\gamma_{\epsilon})<k_{V}(p,q)+\epsilon=\epsilon.

By (

Since

is hyperbolic, this means that

(A) for every open neighborhood

\epsilon>0

is hyperbolic. Let

.
For every

such that
\pi(a)\in\mathbb{B}_{r}
and for every

, if
\varphi
is a
V
-disc such that
\varphi(0)=a

\tilde{\varphi}

\tilde{\varphi}(\zeta):=\varphi(\rho\zeta)

-disc contained in

.

(6.3)

\kappa_{Z,V}(a;u)\leq\frac{1}{\rho}\kappa_{V}(a;u),

Take a

-disc

|y(0)|\leq|y_{0}|

By (

.

Let

By (

\begin{split}0<k_{W}((0,0,0),L)&\leq k_{W}(\gamma_{\epsilon}(0),\gamma_{\epsilon}(t_{0}))\leq\int_{0}^{t_{0}}\kappa_{W}(\gamma_{\epsilon}(s);\gamma_{\epsilon}^{\prime}(s))ds\\
&\leq\int_{0}^{1}\kappa_{W}(\gamma_{\epsilon}(s);\gamma_{\epsilon}^{\prime}(s))ds=\ell_{W}(\gamma_{\epsilon})\leq\frac{1}{\rho r_{1}}\ell_{V}(\gamma_{\epsilon})<\epsilon.\end{split}

As

Theorem
5.15
told us precisely when we can lift from a holomorphic scale symplectic map to a holomorphic contact morphism.
We can turn this backwards, assuming only hyperbolicity of the target contact manifold:

Corollary 6.2
.

Suppose that
(M,V,v)
and
(M^{\prime},V^{\prime},v^{\prime})
are Reeb manifolds and that
(M^{\prime},V^{\prime})
is hyperbolic.
Every contact morphism
M\to M^{\prime}
is a Reeb morphism.

Proof.

Since
(M^{\prime},V^{\prime})
is hyperbolic,
v^{\prime}
has proper flow,
i.e.

(M^{\prime},v^{\prime},V^{\prime})
is a proper Reeb manifold.
Take a contact morphism
M\xrightarrow{\Phi}M^{\prime}
.
Since the map
\Phi
does not increase pseudodistances, the contact structure of
M
is also hyperbolic.
Hence
(M,V,v)
is also a proper Reeb manifold.
So both Reeb manifolds arise from contact symplectic lifts
(M,V,v,S,\omega)
,
(M^{\prime},V^{\prime},v^{\prime},S^{\prime},\omega^{\prime})
, unique up to isomorphism.
By Theorem
6.1
,
S
and
S^{\prime}
are Kobayashi hyperbolic.

Take any entire curve
\mathbb{C}\to M
.
Its composition
\mathbb{C}\to M\to S
is constant, since
S
is Kobayashi hyperbolic.
So the entire curves in
M
are tangent to the fibers
M\to S
,
i.e.
to the flow lines of the Reeb vector field
v
.
The same is true in
M^{\prime}
.
The map
\Phi
maps Reeb flow lines in
M
to entire curves in
M^{\prime}
, hence tangent to Reeb flow lines.
The map
\Phi
might not match up the Reeb vector fields.
But the flow lines of the Reeb vector field in
M
become entire curves in
M^{\prime}
, so tangent to the Reeb vector field.
Since
\Phi
is an immersion, this tangency is precisely
d\Phi_{p}v(p)=f(p)v^{\prime}(p^{\prime})
for a unique holomorphic function
M\xrightarrow{f}\mathbb{C}^{*}
, for each
p\in M
and
p^{\prime}:=\Phi(p)\in M^{\prime}
.

Consider the vector field
w:=v/f
on
M
.
Claim: this vector field
w
is also a complete Reeb vector field on
M
.
The flow of
w
intertwines under
\Phi
with the flow of
v^{\prime}
.
For any complex time
t
, the time
t
flow of
w
moves
V
to a contact structure
e^{tw}V
on
M
which, intertwining with
v^{\prime}
, has
\Phi_{*}e^{tw}V=e^{t}v^{\prime}\Phi_{*}V\subseteq e^{tv^{\prime}}V^{\prime}=V^{\prime}
.
But
\Phi_{*}^{-1}V^{\prime}=V
so
e^{tw}_{*}V=V
.
Hence
w
is also a Reeb vector field.
Compute:

0={\mathcal{L}}_{w}\xi=\iota_{w}{d\xi}+d(\iota_{w}{\xi})=f(\iota_{v}{d\xi})+df=df.

So
f
is a constant, and so
w
is complete and
w=v/\lambda
for some unique constant
\lambda
.
∎

Theorem

allows us to make the following definition:

Definition 6.3
.

We say that
(M,V,v,S,\omega)
is a

6.1.
Complete hyperbolic contact symplectic lifts

In this section we compare completeness of the

-hyperbolic distance on

Lemma 6.4

Take a contact symplectic lift

.
Denote by

the Kobayashi pseudodistance of

.
Take two points

\pi(p^{\prime})=p

\begin{split}k_{S}(p,q)&=\inf\{\tilde{k}_{V}(x,y):x\in\pi^{-1}(p),y\in\pi^{-1}(q)\}\\

.

,

\varphi_{1}(0)=p

.
Moreover,

\ell(\mathcal{C}):=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\log\frac{1+t_{j}}{1-t_{j}}<k_{S}(p,q)+\varepsilon.

By Proposition
4.14
, there exists a
V
-disc
\tilde{\varphi}_{1}\colon\mathbb{D}\to M

\tilde{\varphi}_{1}(0)=p^{\prime}

.

.

.

\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}:=\{\tilde{\varphi}_{j}\}
is a

-chain in

Apply equation

.

The last equations follow at once from what we just proved and (
2.2
).

The main result of this subsection is the following:

Theorem 6.5
.

Let
(M,V,v,S,\omega)
be a hyperbolic contact symplectic lift. Then

is complete
V
-hyperbolic (that is,

-hyperbolic. Let

be a Cauchy sequence for

. By Lemma
6.4
, we can find

such that

\{a_{j}\}

\{p_{n}\}\subset\mathbb{B}^{2N}

.

\underline{0}=(0,0,0)

—hence
(\tilde{\gamma}_{m,n})_{y}(0)=0
and
(\tilde{\gamma}_{m,n})_{y}(1)=y_{n}-y_{m}
—we have

\begin{split}|y_{n}-y_{m}|&\leq\int_{0}^{1}|(\tilde{\gamma}_{m,n})_{y}^{\prime}(t)|dt\leq\int_{0}^{1}\|\tilde{\gamma}_{m,n}^{\prime}(t)\|dt\\
&\leq\frac{1}{C}\int_{0}^{1}\kappa_{V}(\tilde{\gamma}_{m,n}(t));\tilde{\gamma}_{m,n}^{\prime}(t))dt=\frac{1}{C}\ell_{V}(\tilde{\gamma}_{m,n})<\frac{1}{C}(\epsilon+\frac{1}{n}).\end{split}

This proves that

is a Cauchy sequence in

, hence converging.
∎

Example 7.3

In Example

Theorem

characterizes which scale symplectic maps lift to contact manifold automorphisms; in particular:

.

If

Remark 7.5
.

Let
(M,V,v,S,\omega)
be a hyperbolic holomorphically exact contact symplectic lift. Assume that
H^{1}(S,\mathbb{C})=0
. By Corollary
5.5
, there exists a unique (up to equivalence) contact symplectic lift, the trivial contact symplectic lift. That is, we can assume that
M=S\times\mathbb{C}
,
\pi\colon S\times\mathbb{C}\to S
is the projection on the first factor, the Reeb vector field is given by
\partial_{y}
, where
y
is the coordinate in
\mathbb{C}
and the contact form associated to
\partial_{y}
is

\xi=dy-\pi^{\ast}(\beta),

where
\beta
is a holomorphic
1
-form on
S
such that
d\beta=\omega
. Let
F\in{\sf Aut}_{\mathbb{C}\omega}S
be such that
F^{\ast}\omega=\lambda\omega
, for some
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}
. Let

be such that

dh=F^{\ast}\beta-\lambda\beta.

Since

and

, there exists such a holomorphic function

, and it is unique up to a constant. Then define

as follows:

It is clear that

is an automorphism of

and moreover,

Therefore,

and, clearly,

.

8.
Classification

The classification of Kobayashi hyperbolic complex manifolds with large groups of automorphisms was given by Isaev in
[
29
]
. In a different work
[
30
]
, he also classified those complex manifolds admitting a real Lie group acting properly by holomorphic automorphisms. For a complex manifold with hyperbolic contact structure, the group
{\sf Aut}_{V}M
of
V
-automorphisms acts properly.

In this section, we give a strong bound on the dimension of
{\sf Aut}_{V}M
for all Reeb contact manifolds of dimension
\dim_{\mathbb{C}}M=3,5
. We also show that when
\dim_{\mathbb{C}}M=3
and the dimension of
{\sf Aut}_{V}M
is maximal, only one example (up to equivalence) arises.

While Isaev’s classification gives us a good starting point, we still need to consider whether a given group can be realized as the group of
V
-automorphisms of some contact structure
V
.
The following lemma will be necessary to determine which contact, symplectic, or volume forms are compatible with a given group action.

Lemma 8.1
.

Let
1\leq n\leq N
. Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{N}
be a complete Reinhardt domain. Let
f\in\mathcal{O}^{\ast}_{\Omega}(\Omega)
and let
\omega
be the holomorphic volume form

\mu(z_{1},\dots,z_{N}):=f(z_{1},\dots,z_{N})dz_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge dz_{N}.

Let

\mathbb{T}_{n}=\{(z_{1},\dots z_{N})\mapsto(e^{i\theta_{1}}z_{1},\dots,e^{i\theta_{n}}z_{n},z_{n+1},\ldots,z_{N}):\ \theta_{1},\dots\theta_{n}\in\mathbb{R}\}\subset{\sf Aut}\Omega.

Suppose that for every
T\in\mathbb{T}_{n}
there exists
\lambda_{T}\in\mathbb{C}^{\ast}
such that
T^{\ast}\mu=\lambda_{T}\mu
. Then,
f
depends only on
(z_{n+1},\ldots,z_{N})
. In particular, if
n=N
then
f
is constant.

Proof.

Fix
z_{2},\ldots,z_{N}
. Expand
f(\cdot,z_{2},\ldots,z_{N})
in Taylor series at
z_{1}=0
as

(8.1)

f(z_{1},\ldots,z_{N})=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}a_{j}(z^{\prime})z_{1}^{j},

where
z^{\prime}=(z_{2},\ldots,z_{N})
and
a_{j}
are holomorphic functions and, since
f
vanishes nowhere,
a_{0}(0,\ldots,0)\neq 0
.
Let
T_{\theta}(z_{1},\ldots,z_{N}):=(e^{i\theta}z_{1},z_{2},\ldots,z_{N})
for any
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
. By hypothesis, for every
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
there exists
\lambda_{\theta}\in\mathbb{R}
such that

\begin{split}f(e^{i\theta}z_{1},\ldots,z_{N})e^{i\theta}dz_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge dz_{N}&=(T_{\theta}^{\ast}\mu)(z_{1},\ldots,z_{N})=\lambda_{\theta}\mu(z_{1},\ldots,z_{N})\\
&=\lambda_{\theta}f(z_{1},\ldots,z_{N})dz_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge dz_{N}.\end{split}

Hence,

f(e^{i\theta}z_{1},\ldots,z_{N})e^{i\theta}=\lambda_{\theta}f(z_{1},\ldots,z_{N}).

By (
8.1
), we have

a_{0}(z^{\prime})e^{i\theta}+a_{1}(z^{\prime})e^{2i\theta}z_{1}+\ldots=\lambda_{\theta}a_{0}(z^{\prime})+\lambda_{\theta}a_{1}(z^{\prime})z_{1}+\ldots

Since
a_{0}(0)\neq 0
,
\lambda_{\theta}=e^{i\theta}
and
a_{j}(z^{\prime})(e^{i\theta j}-1)\equiv 0
for
j\geq 1
. From this we immediately see that
a_{j}(z^{\prime})\equiv 0
for
j\geq 1
. In other words,
f(z_{1},\ldots,z_{N})=a_{0}(z_{2},\ldots,z_{N})
.

Repeating this argument for
z_{2},\ldots,z_{n}
we have the statement.
∎

In order to prove our classification result, we need to provide a more general result about codimension-1 subgroups in products of simple Lie groups.

Theorem 8.2
.

Let
G
and
G^{\prime}
be simple Lie groups. Every codimension-1 subgroup of
G\times G^{\prime}
is of the form
N\times G^{\prime}
or
G\times N^{\prime}
for codimension-1 subgroups
N\subset G
or
N^{\prime}\subset G^{\prime}
.

Proof.

Let
L\subset G\times G^{\prime}
be a codimension-1 subgroup and let
\mathfrak{l}
be its Lie algebra. Take
\alpha\in T_{1}(G\times G^{\prime})^{\ast}
such that
\mathfrak{l}=\ker\alpha
and extend
\alpha
to a left-invariant form (still denoted by
\alpha
) on
G\times G^{\prime}
.

The left cosets
(g,g^{\prime})\cdot L
for
(g,g^{\prime})\in G\times G^{\prime}
form a foliation of
G\times G^{\prime}
with associated distribution given by the translates
(g,g^{\prime})\cdot\mathfrak{l}=\ker\alpha_{(g,g^{\prime})}
. In particular, by Frobenius Theorem, we have that
\alpha\wedge d\alpha=0
.

Using the decomposition
T(G\times G^{\prime})=TG\times TG^{\prime}
, we can write
\alpha=\xi_{0}+\xi_{0}^{\prime}
for two left-invariant forms
\xi_{0}
and
\xi_{0}^{\prime}
on
G
and
G^{\prime}
respectively. Assuming that
L
is
not
of the form
N\times G^{\prime}
or
G\times N^{\prime}
, we obtain that neither
\xi_{0}
nor
\xi_{0}^{\prime}
vanishes.

We now extend
\{\xi_{0}\}
and
\{\xi_{0}^{\prime}\}
to bases of left-invariant forms
\{\xi_{0},\xi_{i}\}
and
\{\xi_{0}^{\prime},\xi_{a}^{\prime}\}
on
G
and
G^{\prime}
. By left invariance,

\displaystyle d\xi^{0}

\displaystyle=c^{0}_{0j}\xi_{0}\wedge\xi_{j}+c^{0}_{ij}\xi_{i}\wedge\xi_{j},

\displaystyle d\xi_{0}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=c^{\prime 0}_{0b}\xi_{0}^{\prime}\wedge\xi_{b}^{\prime}+c^{\prime 0}_{ab}\xi_{a}^{\prime}\wedge\xi_{b}^{\prime},

for structure constants
c^{\bullet}_{\bullet\bullet}
,
c^{\prime\bullet}_{\bullet\bullet}
, where we use the convention of summation over distinct indices.

This gives

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\alpha\wedge d\alpha,

\displaystyle=\xi_{0}\wedge d\xi_{0}+\xi_{0}^{\prime}\wedge d\xi_{0}+\xi_{0}\wedge d\xi_{0}^{\prime}+\xi_{0}^{\prime}\wedge d\xi_{0}^{\prime},

\displaystyle=c^{0}_{ij}\xi_{0}\wedge\xi_{i}\wedge\xi_{j}+c^{\prime 0}_{0b}\xi_{0}\wedge\xi_{0}^{\prime}\wedge\xi_{b}^{\prime}+c^{\prime 0}_{ab}\xi_{0}\wedge\xi_{a}^{\prime}\wedge\xi_{b}^{\prime}

\displaystyle\qquad+c^{0}_{0i}\xi_{0}^{\prime}\wedge\xi_{0}\wedge\xi_{i}+c^{0}_{ij}\xi_{0}^{\prime}\wedge\xi_{i}\wedge\xi_{j}+c^{\prime 0}_{ab}\xi_{0}^{\prime}\wedge\xi_{a}^{\prime}\wedge\xi_{b}^{\prime}.

By linear independence of these forms, each term vanishes separately, ensuring that
d\xi_{0}=0
and
d\xi_{0}^{\prime}=0
.

Now, let
\{v_{0},v_{i}\}
be the dual basis of
\{\xi_{0},\xi_{i}\}
and let
\mathfrak{n}
be the kernel of
\xi_{0}
, spanned by
\{v_{i}\}
(excluding
v_{0}
). We have

\xi_{0}([v_{i},v_{j}])=v_{i}(\xi_{0}(v_{j}))-v_{j}(\xi_{0}(v_{i}))-d\xi_{0}(v_{i},v_{j})=0,

hence
\mathfrak{n}
is a Lie algebra and also an ideal (take
i=0
) in the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
of
G
. This contradicts the assumption that
G
is a simple group, concluding the proof.
∎

We can now apply the result with
G=G^{\prime}=PSL_{2}\mathbb{R}
.

Corollary 8.3
.

No
5
-dimensional Lie group acting faithfully on the bidisc preserves any holomorphic symplectic form, even up to a constant factor.

Proof.

From the above, we can assume that the group has identity component
N\times PSL_{2}\mathbb{R}
, with
N\subset PSL_{2}\mathbb{R}
a subgroup. Consider the standard action on the product of upper-half planes
\mathbb{H}\times\mathbb{H}
.
Any holomorphic
2
-form on
\mathbb{H}\times\mathbb{H}

\omega=f(z,w)dz\wedge dw

must then satisfy

f(z,w+b)=\lambda_{b}f(z,w)

for all

. Deriving with respect to

, we obtain that

.
But then

is a unimodular real linear fractional transformation, under which

is not invariant, even up to a constant factor.
∎

Now we can state and prove our main result of this section:

Theorem 8.4
.

Let
(M,V,v)
be a Reeb manifold. Suppose

and assume

Moreover, if
\dim_{\mathbb{R}}{\sf Aut}_{V}M=7
, then, up to biholomorphic morphism of contact manifolds,

with contact structure given by the unique (up to equivalence) contact symplectic lift of
(\mathbb{B}^{2},\omega=\frac{2}{(1-z)^{3}}dz\wedge dw)
. The group
{\sf Aut}_{\mathbb{C}\omega}\mathbb{B}^{2}
is the 5-dimensional group of automorphisms of the ball fixing the point
(1,0)\in\partial\mathbb{B}^{2}

Notice that the previous bound is better than the one given by Corollary
7.2
.

Proof.

By Proposition
3.11
,
M
is a proper Reeb manifold.
Let
v
be the Reeb vector field of
M
.
By Lemma

, there is a holomorphic symplectic manifold

is a contact symplectic lift.
Since
M
is
V
-hyperbolic,
(M,V,v,S,\omega)
is hyperbolic (and, by Theorem

,
S
is Kobayashi hyperbolic).
By Theorem
4.17
,
\omega
is
C^{\infty}
-exact.
By Corollary
7.2
,

\dim_{\mathbb{R}}{\sf Aut}_{V}M\leq 2+\dim_{\mathbb{R}}{\sf Aut}_{\mathbb{C}\omega}S.

Since
S
is Kobayashi hyperbolic and
{\sf Aut}_{\mathbb{C}\omega}S\subseteq{\sf Aut}S
, we have that
d=\dim_{\mathbb{R}}{\sf Aut}_{\mathbb{C}\omega}S\leq 8
by the classification of Kobayashi hyperbolic manifolds provided by Isaev
[

]
. We now proceed case by case.

Case
d=8
.
In this case, up to biholomorphisms,
{\sf Aut}_{\mathbb{C}\omega}S={\sf Aut}S
and
S=\mathbb{B}^{2}
. According to Lemma
8.1
, the symplectic form must be a constant multiple of the standard symplectic form
dz\wedge dw
. Since the full group of automorphisms
{\sf Aut}S
does not preserve this form, we reach a contradiction.

Case
d=7
.
No group of real dimension 7 can act properly on a manifold of complex dimension 2
[
30
]
.

Case

.
Using the classification given by Isaev
[
30
]
and the fact that

{\sf Aut}\mathbb{D}\times{\sf Aut}\mathbb{D}
. Once again using Lemma

, we see that the form

is a multiple of the standard volume form and since

Therefore
\dim_{\mathbb{R}}{\sf Aut}_{V}M\leq 7
.

Case
d=5
.
We have that
\dim_{\mathbb{R}}{\sf Aut}S\geq 5
, hence, up to biholomorphisms, either
S=\mathbb{B}^{2}
or
S=\mathbb{D}^{2}

[
29
]
. By Corollary

, we can exclude that

and assume

.

The group

acts transitively
[
30
]

[
10
, Lemma A.1]
it admits a closed, simply connected, solvable subgroup

acting freely and transitively on

. In particular,

must be of real dimension 4.

The Lie algebra
\mathfrak{su}(2,1)
of

admits precisely two solvable Lie subalgebras of dimension 4
[
19
]
; we now describe the corresponding groups. Consider the 5-dimensional group

of automorphisms of the Siegel model of the ball

[

]

[

. The group with

In particular,

contains all
parabolic transformations

F_{s}(z,w)=(z+2\bar{s}w+|s|^{2},w+s)
for

must be invariant up to scale for this set of transformations. That is,

\lambda(s)f(z,w)dz\wedge dw=f(F_{s}(z,w))dz\wedge dw,\ \lambda(s)\in\mathbb{C}^{\ast}

Differentiating with respect to

\bar{s}
and evaluating at

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\lambda}{\partial s}(0)f(z,w)

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial f}{\partial w}(z,w)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\lambda}{\partial\bar{s}}(0)f(z,w)

\displaystyle=2w\frac{\partial f}{\partial z}(z,w)

and

Scale to get

.

Since

{\sf Aut}_{\mathbb{C}\omega}\{\sf Re(z)>|w|^{2}\}

Observe that the form

pulls back to
\tilde{\omega}:=\frac{2}{(1-z)^{3}}dz\wedge dw

\displaystyle\mathbb{B}^{2}\to

\displaystyle\{\sf Re(z)>|w|^{2}\}

\displaystyle(z,w)\mapsto

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1+z}{1-z},\frac{w}{1-z}\right)

Via the same biholomorphism

\tilde{G}
of
\mathbb{B}^{2}
formed by all automorphisms of
\mathbb{B}^{2}
fixing the point

(recall that all automorphisms of

,
[

Now, let

for some

. Write

\frac{2}{(1-F_{1}(z,w))^{3}}\det(dF_{(z,w)})dz\wedge dw=F^{\ast}\tilde{\omega}=\lambda\tilde{\omega}=\frac{2\lambda}{(1-z)^{3}}dz\wedge dw.

That is, for all

Example 8.5

Fix

and

equipped with the standard symplectic and contact structure respectively. The group

Example 8.6

Let
\mathbb{H}:=\{\zeta\in\mathbb{C}:\sf Re(z)>0\}

and let
\tilde{\omega}(\zeta,w)=d\zeta\wedge dw
be the standard holomorphic symplectic form. Let
A:=\{h\in{\sf Aut}\mathbb{H}:h(\zeta)=a\zeta+ib:a>0,b\in\mathbb{R}\}
(recall that the automorphisms of
\mathbb{H}
are Moebius transformations, so it makes sense to consider them as automorphisms of the Riemann sphere). Note that
A
is a closed Lie subgroup of
{\sf Aut}\mathbb{H}
of real dimension
2
(the one formed by of all automorphisms of
\mathbb{H}
fixing
\infty
). Let
\tilde{G}:=\{(f,g)\in{\sf Aut}\Omega:f\in A,g(w)=e^{i\theta}w,\theta\in\mathbb{R}\}
. It is clear that
\tilde{G}
is a closed Lie subgroup of
{\sf Aut}\Omega
of real dimension
3
and that
\tilde{G}\subseteq{\sf Aut}_{\mathbb{C}\tilde{\omega}}\Omega
.

Let
C(z)=\frac{1+z}{1-z}
be the Cayley transform (a biholomorphism from
\mathbb{D}
to
\mathbb{H}
). Using the biholomorphism
\Phi:\mathbb{D}\times\mathbb{D}\ni(z,w)\mapsto(C(z),w)\in\Omega
, we have that

\omega:=\Phi^{\ast}(\tilde{\omega})=\frac{2}{(1-z)^{2}}dz\wedge dw,

is a holomorphically exact holomorphic symplectic form on
\mathbb{D}\times\mathbb{D}
. The group
\tilde{G}
corresponds via
\Phi
to the Lie subgroup, of real dimension
3
,
G
of
{\sf Aut}(\mathbb{D}\times\mathbb{D})
formed by all automorphisms of
\mathbb{D}\times\mathbb{D}
of the form
(z,w)\mapsto(f(z),w)
, where
f
is an automorphism of
\mathbb{D}
fixing
1
. By construction,
G\subseteq{\sf Aut}_{\mathbb{C}\omega}(\mathbb{D}\times\mathbb{D})
.

It is well known (see,
e.g.

[
1
]
) that
{\sf Aut}(\mathbb{D}\times\mathbb{D})
has two connected components: the one containing the identity is given by all automorphisms of the form
(z,w)\mapsto(f(z),g(w))
where
f,g\in{\sf Aut}\mathbb{D}
; the other connected component is obtained by pre-composing the elements of the connected component containing the identity with the automorphism
(z,w)\mapsto(w,z)
.

From this it follows that
{\sf Aut}_{\mathbb{C}\omega}(\mathbb{D}\times\mathbb{D})
is contained in the connected component of
{\sf Aut}(\mathbb{D}\times\mathbb{D})
containing the identity. Hence, if
F(z,w):=(f(z),g(w))\in{\sf Aut}_{\mathbb{C}\omega}(\mathbb{D}\times\mathbb{D})
, there exists
\lambda\neq 0
such that

\frac{2}{(1-f(z))^{2}}f^{\prime}(z)g^{\prime}(w)dz\wedge dw=F^{\ast}\omega=\lambda\omega=\frac{2\lambda}{(1-z)^{2}}dz\wedge dw.

From this, it follows easily that
g^{\prime}(w)
is constant, hence
g(w)=e^{i\theta}w
, and that
f(1)=1
. In other words,
{\sf Aut}_{\mathbb{C}\omega}=G
. Therefore,
{\sf Aut}_{\mathbb{C}\omega}
has real dimension
3
. The (unique up to equivalence) Reeb manifold
M
given by the contact symplectic lift of
(\mathbb{D}\times\mathbb{D},\omega)
is
V
-hyperbolic (since
\mathbb{D}\times\mathbb{D}
is Kobayashi hyperbolic) and
\dim_{\mathbb{R}}{\sf Aut}_{V}M=5
.

Example 8.7
.

Fix
S=\mathbb{D}\times\mathbb{D}
and
M=S\times\mathbb{C}
equipped with the standard symplectic and contact structure respectively. The two-dimensional group
{\sf Aut}_{\mathbb{C}\omega}S
includes the inversion
(z,w)\mapsto(w,z)
and rotations around both the
z
and
w
axes, hence
{\sf Aut}_{V}M
has dimension 4.

Example 8.8
.

Fix
S=\mathbb{D}\times\mathbb{D}
, this time equipped with the symplectic form
(1+z)dz\wedge dw
, and let
M=S\times\mathbb{C}
with the unique contact-symplectic lift. The group
{\sf Aut}_{\mathbb{C}\omega}S
now allows rotations only around the
w
-axis, hence
{\sf Aut}_{V}M
has dimension 3.

To obtain an example where
{\sf Aut}_{V}M
has dimension 2, just consider the contact symplectic lift of any 2-dimensional Kobayashi hyperbolic manifold with no automorphisms except the identity.

.

Let
M
be a proper Reeb contact manifold of complex dimension 5 with a holomorphic contact structure

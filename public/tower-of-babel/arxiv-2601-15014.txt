Efficient and minimax-optimal in-context nonparametric regression with transformers
Michelle Ching, Ioana Popescu, Nico Smith, Tianyi Ma, William G. Underwood, Richard J. Samworth

Abstract
We study in-context learning for nonparametric regression with α \alpha -Hölder smooth regression functions, for some α > 0 \alpha>0 . We prove that, with n n in-context examples and d d -dimensional regression covariates, a pretrained transformer with Θ ⁡ ( log ⁡ n ) \Theta(\log n) parameters and Ω ⁡ ( n 2 ​ α / ( 2 ​ α + d ) ​ log 3 ​ n ) \Omega\bigl(n^{2\alpha/(2\alpha+d)}\log^{3}n\bigr) pretraining sequences can achieve the minimax-optimal rate of convergence O ( n − 2 α / ( 2 α + d ) ) O\bigl(n^{-2\alpha/(2\alpha+d)}\bigr) in mean squared error. Our result requires substantially fewer transformer parameters and pretraining sequences than previous results in the literature. This is achieved by showing that transformers are able to approximate local polynomial estimators efficiently by implementing a kernel-weighted polynomial basis and then running gradient descent.

1.1
Related Work

The theoretical properties of ICL have been extensively studied
in the regression and classification settings.
Several prior works have shown that transformers are able to

There have also been many empirical

37

Closest to our work are the recent papers
by
19
and
35

19
prove that transformers
can learn nonparametric regression problems in context by estimating
the coefficients of the regression functions from a B-spline wavelet
basis expansion.
They achieve nearly minimax-optimal rates
in

For
\alpha

they achieve optimal rates, and further they show

thereby avoiding the curse of dimensionality.
Both of these works require transformers with
polynomially many parameters (in
n
) in order
to achieve minimax-optimal rates,
leading to restrictive assumptions
on the number of pretraining sequences.

1.2
Contribution

We consider the problem of in-context nonparametric regression
using transformers.
In particular, we show that transformers
trained via empirical risk minimisation,
under mild architecture conditions,
achieve the minimax-optimal rate
(in the number
n
of in-context examples)
for nonparametric regression
under mean squared prediction risk.
Our main result, Theorem
3.2
,
holds whenever the underlying regression functions
are
\alpha
-Hölder, for some
\alpha>0
;
the corresponding minimax rate is

n^{-2\alpha/(2\alpha+d)}
,
where
d
is the dimension of the covariates.
Compared with existing works,
our approach attains this optimal rate while
imposing much milder conditions on both the
number of pretraining sequences and the number
of transformer parameters.
Our proofs use linear attention
for simplicity,
but they can be generalised to ReLU or softmax attention with minor changes;
see the discussion at the end
of Section
3
.

Our primary technical innovation is to
show that transformers with single-head attention layers and
\Theta(\log n)

parameters are able to
attain the optimal rate of convergence. This improves on the

\Theta(n^{d/(2\alpha+d)})
parameters required by

19
and
\Theta(n)
parameters needed by

35
.
As a direct consequence, we also
require substantially
fewer pretraining sequences.
In order to prove this,
we demonstrate that such transformers
are able to approximate local
polynomial estimators
of suitable degree
(Theorem
3.1
), which amounts to solving a weighted least squares problem
(
4
)
in the monomial basis.
Our approach consists of
constructing a transformer which,
given an input prompt,
first constructs a kernel-weighted
monomial basis matrix
and then uses this matrix to perform

\Theta(\log n)

steps of gradient descent towards the desired
local polynomial
least-squares solution (
4
). An advantage of gradient descent for this problem is that it avoids the need to compute matrix inverses. Optimality then follows by
combining well-known
properties of local polynomial
estimators (Theorem
2.5
)
with a bound on the expected excess risk
of the transformer-based estimator,
obtained via empirical process theory
and covering number bounds.

1.3
Overview

In Section
2
, we
present a mathematical formulation of our
in-context nonparametric regression problem
with Hölder-smooth regression functions,
define our transformer class using
linear attention and ReLU feed-forward layers,
and provide some auxiliary definitions and results
concerning classical local polynomial estimators
(Theorem
2.5
).
Section
3
gives our main
results, beginning with some novel
approximation theory for transformers in
Theorem
3.1
.
Combining this with a bound on the expected excess
risk for empirical risk-minimising transformers
yields our primary contribution as
Theorem
3.2
.
Here, we establish the minimax optimality
of transformers for performing in-context
nonparametric regression under standard Hölder
smoothness assumptions, and under
substantially weaker conditions on the number of transformer
parameters and on the number of pretraining sequences
than existing results in the literature.
In Section
4
, we provide some
insight into the main ideas underlying the proofs
of our main theorems.
First, we explain how to construct
a transformer that uses gradient descent to
approximate the output of a local polynomial estimator,
and second we bound the expected excess risk of our
estimator by controlling the covering numbers of
the corresponding transformer class.

2.2
Empirical and Population Risk

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a class of functions from

\bigl([0,1]^{d}\times\mathbb{R}\bigr)^{n}\times[0,1]^{d}

\mathbb{R}

where

\displaystyle\hat{R}_{\Gamma}(f)\coloneqq\frac{1}{\Gamma}\sum_{\gamma=1}^{\Gamma}\Bigl\{Y^{(\gamma)}_{n+1}-f\bigl(\mathcal{D}^{(\gamma)}_{n},X^{(\gamma)}_{n+1}\bigr)\Bigr\}^{2}.

(1)

The
population risk
of a measurable function

f:\bigl([0,1]^{d}\times\mathbb{R}\bigr)^{n}\times[0,1]^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
is

\displaystyle R(f)\coloneqq\mathbb{E}\Bigl(\bigl\{Y_{n+1}-f\bigl(\mathcal{D}_{n},X_{n+1}\bigr)\bigr\}^{2}\Bigr),

(2)

where
\mathcal{D}_{n}\coloneqq(X_{i},Y_{i})_{i\in[n]}
and

\mathcal{D}^{(1)}_{n}
and

(X^{(1)}_{n+1},Y^{(1)}_{n+1})

Throughout the paper, we quantify performance of a procedure
by bounding its population risk.

Transformer-based Estimator

Let
{d_{\mathrm{e}}}\geq d+2

\displaystyle\mathrm{Embed}_{{d_{\mathrm{e}}}}\bigl((x_{i},y_{i})_{i\in[n]},x_{n+1}\bigr)\coloneqq\begin{pmatrix}x_{1}^{\mathsf{T}}&y_{1}&0_{{d_{\mathrm{e}}}-d-2}^{\mathsf{T}}&0\\

We also define

2.5
Local Polynomial Estimators

Let
K:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to[0,C_{K}]
be a Borel measurable
function supported on
[-1,1]^{d}
satisfying

|K(x)-K(x^{\prime})|\leq L_{K}\|x-x^{\prime}\|_{\infty}

for all
x,x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and some
L_{K}>0
. We do not assume that

K
integrates to 1. Assume that
K(x)\geq c_{K}
for

x\in[-c_{K},c_{K}]^{d}
, where
c_{K}\in(0,1]
.
Let
h>0
be the bandwidth and define
K_{h}(\cdot)\coloneqq K(\cdot/h)/h^{d}
.
For
p\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,
write
D\coloneqq\binom{d+p}{p}

and let
P_{h}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{D}

5
Summary

We have presented a theoretical study of
in-context learning for
nonparametric regression problems.
With
n
in-context examples,

d
-dimensional covariates and

\alpha
-Hölder smooth
regression functions, we have shown that
suitable pretrained transformers with
only
\Theta(\log n)
parameters are able
to attain the minimax-optimal rate

O\bigl(n^{-2\alpha/(2\alpha+d)}\bigr)
.
Moreover, we showed that this rate is
achievable whenever

\Gamma=\Omega(n^{2\alpha/(2\alpha+d)}\log^{3}n\bigr)

pretraining sequences are available.
Our approach involved first demonstrating
that transformers are able to
approximate local polynomial estimators
to arbitrary accuracy,
and then bounding the risk of
our transformer estimator
by deriving covering number bounds
and applying results from
empirical process theory.

Future directions could include
extensions to next token prediction
in dependent data settings;
this would allow for more accurate modelling
of large language models.
Alternatively, one might attempt
to discover and exploit low-dimensional
structure (such as sparsity, an index structure
or a manifold hypothesis)
in the regression function,
thereby avoiding the curse of
dimensionality.
Finally, one could aim to establish
minimax theory for transformers trained
via gradient descent
(or related optimisation algorithms).

y=(y_{1},\ldots,y_{d})^{\mathsf{T}}

telescoping the sum and the mean value theorem,

\displaystyle|x^{\nu}-y^{\nu}|

\displaystyle=\biggl|\prod_{j=1}^{d}x_{j}^{\nu_{j}}-\prod_{j=1}^{d}y_{j}^{\nu_{j}}\biggr|=\biggl|\sum_{r=1}^{d}\biggl\{\biggl(\prod_{j=r+1}^{d}x_{j}^{\nu_{j}}\biggr)\biggl(\prod_{\ell=1}^{r-1}y_{\ell}^{\nu_{\ell}}\biggr)(x_{r}^{\nu_{r}}-y_{r}^{\nu_{r}})\biggr\}\biggr|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{r=1}^{d}\biggl(\prod_{j=r+1}^{d}|x_{j}|^{\nu_{j}}\biggr)\biggl(\prod_{\ell=1}^{r-1}|y_{\ell}|^{\nu_{\ell}}\biggr)\nu_{r}\bigl(|x_{r}|\lor|y_{r}|\bigr)^{\nu_{r}-1}|x_{r}-y_{r}|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{r=1}^{d}\nu_{r}\|x-y\|_{\infty}\bigl(\|x\|_{\infty}\lor\|y\|_{\infty}\bigr)^{|\nu|-1}=|\nu|\bigl(\|x\|_{\infty}\lor\|y\|_{\infty}\bigr)^{|\nu|-1}\|x-y\|_{\infty},

as required.

.

,

33

,

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{C_{2}}-\frac{1}{4C_{2}}-\frac{1}{16C_{2}^{3}D}\geq\frac{1}{C_{3}},

.

,

.

\displaystyle-P_{h}(z-x)_{j}K_{h}(z-x)P_{h}(z-x)_{k}\bigr|

to

\|\cdot\|_{\max}
,
where we may take
C_{4}\geq C_{3}\lor 1
,

C_{4}D/h^{d+1}
-Lipschitz from

\|\cdot\|_{\infty}
to

.
It follows by Weyl’s inequality that both

.

\delta

.

For the third inequality, by Fubini’s theorem and
integrating the tail probability, as

\sup_{x\in[0,1]^{d}}|\tilde{m}_{n}(x)-m(x)|\leq 2M
,

\displaystyle\int_{[0,1]^{d}}\mathbb{E}\Bigl\{\big(\tilde{m}_{n}(x)-m(x)\bigr)^{2}\Bigr\}\,\mathrm{d}x=C_{2}n^{\frac{-2\alpha}{2\alpha+d}}\,\mathbb{E}\Biggl\{\frac{n^{\frac{2\alpha}{2\alpha+d}}}{C_{2}}\int_{[0,1]^{d}}\big(\tilde{m}_{n}(x)-m(x)\bigr)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\Biggr\}

\displaystyle\qquad=C_{2}n^{\frac{-2\alpha}{2\alpha+d}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}\biggl(\int_{[0,1]^{d}}\big(\tilde{m}_{n}(x)-m(x)\bigr)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x>C_{2}sn^{-\frac{2\alpha}{2\alpha+d}}\biggr)\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\qquad\leq C_{2}n^{\frac{-2\alpha}{2\alpha+d}}\int_{0}^{4M^{2}n^{\frac{2\alpha}{2\alpha+d}}/C_{2}}\Bigl\{C_{1}\exp(-s)+n^{C_{1}}\exp\bigl(-n^{2\alpha/(2\alpha+d)}/C_{1}\bigr)\Bigr\}\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\qquad\leq C_{1}C_{2}n^{\frac{-2\alpha}{2\alpha+d}}+4M^{2}n^{C_{1}}\exp\bigl(-n^{2\alpha/(2\alpha+d)}/C_{1}\bigr).

Note that

C_{1}(C_{1}+1)\log n-n^{2\alpha/(2\alpha+d)}\to-\infty

as
n\to\infty
.
Thus, there exists
C_{3}>0
such that
for
n\geq C_{3}
, we have

n^{C_{1}}\exp\bigl(-n^{2\alpha/(2\alpha+d)}/C_{1}\bigr)\leq 1/n
.
We deduce that for
n\geq C_{3}
,

\displaystyle\int_{[0,1]^{d}}\mathbb{E}\Bigl\{\big(\tilde{m}_{n}(x)-m(x)\bigr)^{2}\Bigr\}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}C_{2}n^{\frac{-2\alpha}{2\alpha+d}}+\frac{4M^{2}}{n}\leq C_{4}n^{\frac{-2\alpha}{2\alpha+d}}.

As
\sup_{x\in[0,1]^{d}}|\tilde{m}_{n}(x)-m(x)|\leq 2M
,
this holds for all
n\in\mathbb{N}

after increasing
C_{4}
to
C_{5}
if necessary.
∎

Appendix B
Approximation Theory

We construct an explicit transformer
that produces outputs similar to those
of truncated local polynomial estimation,
keeping track of its
architecture and parameter magnitudes.

B.1
ReLU Neural Networks

We begin by summarising some approximation properties of ReLU neural
networks that will be useful later for our transformer construction.

Definition B.1
.

Let
{d_{\mathrm{in}}},{d_{\mathrm{out}}},N,L\in\mathbb{N}
and
B>0
.
A function
f:\mathbb{R}^{{d_{\mathrm{in}}}}\to\mathbb{R}^{{d_{\mathrm{out}}}}
is a
(ReLU) neural network

with width
N
, depth
L
and all parameters bounded by
B
if
there exist
\bm{W}_{\ell}\in[-B,B]^{d_{\ell}\times d_{\ell-1}}

and
b_{\ell}\in[-B,B]^{d_{\ell}}
for
\ell\in[L+1]
, where

d_{0}\coloneqq{d_{\mathrm{in}}}
,
d_{L+1}\coloneqq{d_{\mathrm{out}}}

and
d_{\ell}\in[N]
for
\ell\in[L]
, such that

\displaystyle f(\cdot)=A_{L+1}\circ\mathrm{ReLU}\circ A_{L}\circ\mathrm{ReLU}\circ\cdots\circ A_{2}\circ\mathrm{ReLU}\circ A_{1}(\cdot),

where
A_{\ell}(z)\coloneqq\bm{W}_{\ell}z+b_{\ell}
for
\ell\in[L+1]
.

We may assume without loss of generality that
d_{\ell}=N
for

\ell\in[L]
by padding the weight matrices
\bm{W}_{\ell}
and bias
vectors
b_{\ell}
with zeros.

Lemma B.2

(Network composition)
.

Suppose that for
r\in\{1,2\}
,

\displaystyle f^{(r)}(\cdot)\coloneqq A_{L^{(r)}+1}^{(r)}\circ\mathrm{ReLU}\circ\cdots\circ A_{2}^{(r)}\circ\mathrm{ReLU}\circ A_{1}^{(r)}(\cdot),

where
A_{\ell}^{(r)}(z)\coloneqq\bm{W}_{\ell}^{(r)}z+b_{\ell}^{(r)}
,
\bm{W}_{\ell}^{(r)}\in[-B^{(r)},B^{(r)}]^{d_{\ell}^{(r)}\times d_{\ell-1}^{(r)}}
and

b_{\ell}^{(r)}\in[-B^{(r)},B^{(r)}]^{d_{\ell}^{(r)}}
for

\ell\in[L^{(r)}+1]
. Suppose further that the output dimension of

f^{(1)}
is equal to the input dimension of
f^{(2)}
,
i.e.
d_{L^{(1)}+1}^{(1)}=d_{0}^{(2)}\eqqcolon m
. Then
f^{(2)}\circ f^{(1)}
is a neural network with width

\max_{\ell\in[L^{(1)}]}d_{\ell}^{(1)}\vee\max_{\ell\in[L^{(2)}]}d_{\ell}^{(2)}
, depth
L^{(1)}+L^{(2)}
and
all parameters bounded by

\bigl\{m\|\bm{W}_{1}^{(2)}\|_{\max}\bigl(\|\bm{W}_{L^{(1)}+1}^{(1)}\|_{\max}\vee\|b_{L^{(1)}+1}^{(1)}\|_{\infty}\bigr)+\|b_{1}^{(2)}\|_{\infty}\bigr\}\vee B^{(1)}\vee B^{(2)}
.

Proof.

Since

\displaystyle\bm{W}_{1}^{(2)}\bigl(\bm{W}_{L^{(1)}+1}^{(1)}z+b_{L^{(1)}+1}^{(1)}\bigr)+b_{1}^{(2)}=\bm{W}_{1}^{(2)}\bm{W}_{L^{(1)}+1}^{(1)}z+\bigl(\bm{W}_{1}^{(2)}b_{L^{(1)}+1}^{(1)}+b_{1}^{(2)}\bigr),

the result follows.
∎

Lemma B.3
.

Let
d^{\prime}\in\mathbb{N}
,
B\geq 1
,

\bm{W}_{1},\bm{W}_{2}\in[-B,B]^{d^{\prime}\times d^{\prime}}
and
b_{1},b_{2}\in[-B,B]^{d^{\prime}}
.
There exist
\bm{W}_{1}^{\prime}\in[-B,B]^{3d^{\prime}\times d^{\prime}}
,

\bm{W}_{2}^{\prime}\in[-B,B]^{d^{\prime}\times 3d^{\prime}}
,

b_{1}^{\prime}\in[-B,B]^{3d^{\prime}}
and

b_{2}^{\prime}\in[-B,B]^{d^{\prime}}
such that,
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d^{\prime}}
,

\displaystyle x+\bm{W}_{2}^{\prime}\,\mathrm{ReLU}(\bm{W}_{1}^{\prime}x+b_{1}^{\prime})+b_{2}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\bm{W}_{2}\mathrm{ReLU}(\bm{W}_{1}x+b_{1})+b_{2}.

Proof.

Take
b_{1}^{\prime}\coloneqq(b_{1}^{\mathsf{T}},0_{2d^{\prime}}^{\mathsf{T}})^{\mathsf{T}}

and
b_{2}^{\prime}\coloneqq b_{2}
, and let

\displaystyle\bm{W}_{1}^{\prime}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\begin{pmatrix}\bm{W}_{1}\\
\bm{I}_{d^{\prime}\times d^{\prime}}\\
-\bm{I}_{d^{\prime}\times d^{\prime}}\end{pmatrix},

\displaystyle\bm{W}_{2}^{\prime}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\begin{pmatrix}\bm{W}_{2}&-\bm{I}_{d^{\prime}\times d^{\prime}}&\bm{I}_{d^{\prime}\times d^{\prime}}\end{pmatrix}.

Since
\mathrm{ReLU}(-x)-\mathrm{ReLU}(x)=-x
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d^{\prime}}
,
we deduce that

\displaystyle x+\bm{W}_{2}^{\prime}\,\mathrm{ReLU}(\bm{W}_{1}^{\prime}x+b_{1}^{\prime})+b_{2}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=x+\begin{pmatrix}\bm{W}_{2}&-\bm{I}_{d^{\prime}\times d^{\prime}}&\bm{I}_{d^{\prime}\times d^{\prime}}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\mathrm{ReLU}(\bm{W}_{1}x+b_{1})\\
\mathrm{ReLU}(x)\\
\mathrm{ReLU}(-x)\end{pmatrix}+b_{2}

\displaystyle=\bm{W}_{2}\,\mathrm{ReLU}(\bm{W}_{1}x+b_{1})+b_{2},

as required.
∎

Lemma B.4
.

Let
C\geq 1
and
N,L\in\mathbb{N}
. There exists a ReLU neural
network
\phi:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
with width
9N+1
, depth
L
and
all parameters bounded by
32C^{2}N
such that for all
(x,y)\in[-C,C]^{2}
,

\displaystyle|\phi(x,y)-xy|\leq 24C^{2}N^{-L}.

Proof.

The result follows from
25
, where
the upper bound on the magnitude of the parameters follows by inspecting the
proofs of their Lemmas 5.1, 5.2 and 4.2.
∎

The following lemma is an analogue of
25
,
but we extend the input domain from
[0,1]^{k}
to
[-C,C]^{k}
and track
the magnitude
of the parameters.

Lemma B.5
.

Let
C\geq 1
,
k\geq 2
and
N,L\in\mathbb{N}
. There exists a
ReLU neural network

\phi:\mathbb{R}^{k}\to\mathbb{R}
with width
9(N+1)+2k-1
,
depth
7kL(k-1)
and all parameters bounded by
3C^{k}(40N+40)^{2}

such that for all
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k})\in[-C,C]^{k}
,

\displaystyle|\phi(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k})-x_{1}\cdots x_{k}|\leq 30C^{k}(k-1)(N+1)^{-7kL}.

Proof.

We first assume that
C=1
.
By Lemma
B.4
, there exists a ReLU neural network

\phi_{2}:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
with width
9(N+1)+1
,
depth
7kL
and all parameters bounded by
40N+40
such that for all

(x,y)\in[-1.1,1.1]^{2}

\displaystyle|\phi_{2}(x,y)-xy|\leq 30(N+1)^{-7kL}.

(11)

Now suppose that for some
m\in\{2,\ldots,k-1\}
, there exists

\phi_{m}:\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}
with width
9(N+1)+2m-1
,
depth
7kL(m-1)
and all parameters bounded by
3(40N+40)^{2}
,
such that

\displaystyle|\phi_{m}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m})-x_{1}\cdots x_{m}|\leq 30(m-1)(N+1)^{-7kL}.

Here, the case
m=2

11

We then define

\displaystyle\phi_{m+1}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m+1})\coloneqq\phi_{2}\bigl(\phi_{m}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m}),x_{m+1}\bigr).

Since
\mathrm{ReLU}(x_{m+1})-\mathrm{ReLU}(-x_{m+1})=x_{m+1}
,
the identity function
x_{m+1}\mapsto x_{m+1}
can be implemented by a

2

and all parameters bounded by
1
.
Hence, by network composition (Lemma

\phi_{m+1}:\mathbb{R}^{m+1}\to\mathbb{R}

7kLm

3(40N+40)^{2}

Moreover, since

\phi_{m}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m})\in[-1.1,1.1]

Thus, by (

\displaystyle\bigl|\phi_{m+1}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m+1})-x_{1}\cdots x_{m+1}\bigr|=\bigl|\phi_{2}\bigl(\phi_{m}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m}),x_{m+1}\bigr)-x_{1}\cdots x_{m+1}\bigr|

The claim for

,
depth
7kL(k-1)
and all parameters bounded by
3C^{k}(40N+40)^{2}
. Moreover,

\displaystyle\bigl|C^{k}\phi_{k}(x_{1}/C,\ldots,x_{k}/C)-x_{1}\cdots x_{k}\bigr|

\displaystyle=C^{k}\bigl|\phi_{k}(x_{1}/C,\ldots,x_{k}/C)-(x_{1}/C)\cdots(x_{k}/C)\bigr|

for all

Lemma B.6
.

Let
C\geq 1
,

and
\nu=(\nu_{1},\ldots,\nu_{d})\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}

|\nu|\leq k

\psi:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}

9(N+1)+2k-1
,
depth
7kL(k-1)+1

3(k+1)C^{k}(40N+40)^{2}

x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d})\in[-C,C]^{d}
,

\displaystyle|\psi(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d})-x^{\nu}|\leq 30C^{k}(k-1)(N+1)^{-7kL}.

Proof.

Assume without loss of generality that
x^{\nu}=x_{1}^{\nu_{1}}\cdots x_{m}^{\nu_{m}}
, where
\nu_{j}>0
for
j\in[m]
and

\sum_{j=1}^{m}\nu_{m}=k_{0}\leq k
.
Since
\mathrm{ReLU}(a)-\mathrm{ReLU}(-a)=a

a\in\mathbb{R}
, there
exists a neural network
\psi_{1}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{k_{0}}

with width
2k
, depth
1

\displaystyle\psi_{1}(x)\coloneqq(x_{1}1_{\nu_{1}}^{\mathsf{T}},\;\cdots\;,x_{m}1_{\nu_{m}}^{\mathsf{T}})^{\mathsf{T}}\in\mathbb{R}^{k_{0}}.

Therefore, by Lemma
B.5

\phi
with width

,

3C^{k}(40N+40)^{2}

\displaystyle\bigl|\phi\bigl(\psi_{1}(x)\bigr)-x^{\nu}\bigr|\leq 30C^{k}(k-1)(N+1)^{-7kL}.

Finally, by network composition, we have that

is a neural network with width

,
depth

and all parameters bounded by

∎

B.2
Transformer Construction

In the next lemma, we construct a transformer
that yields the centred covariates
and a scaled version of the kernel matrix.

Lemma B.7
.

Let
n,d\in\mathbb{N}
and take

X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}\in[0,1]^{d}
and
Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{n}\in\mathbb{R}
.
Write
\bm{X}\coloneqq(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})^{\mathsf{T}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}

and
Y\coloneqq(Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{n})^{\mathsf{T}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
Let
h>0
and define
K_{h}(\bm{X},X_{n+1})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
by

K_{h}(\bm{X},X_{n+1})_{i}\coloneqq K\bigl((X_{i}-X_{n+1})/h\bigr)/h^{d}

for
i\in[n]
, where
K:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
is given by

K(x)\coloneqq(1-\|x\|_{1})_{+}^{2}
.
Let
\sqrt{K_{h}(\bm{X},X_{n+1})}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
be defined entrywise.
Take
{d_{\mathrm{e}}},{d_{\mathrm{ffn}}}\in\mathbb{N}
with
{d_{\mathrm{e}}}\geq 2d+4
and

{d_{\mathrm{ffn}}}\geq 2d+2
, and let

B\coloneqq 1\lor(d/h)\lor(n^{-1/2}h^{-d/2})
.
Then there exists a transformer

\mathrm{TF}\in\mathcal{T}({d_{\mathrm{e}}},{d_{\mathrm{ffn}}},3,B)
such that

\displaystyle\mathrm{TF}\circ\mathrm{Embed}_{d_{\mathrm{e}}}\bigl((X_{i},Y_{i})_{i\in[n]},X_{n+1}\bigr)

\displaystyle\hskip 5.69046pt=\begin{pmatrix}\bm{X}&Y&\bigl(\bm{X}-1_{n}X_{n+1}^{\mathsf{T}}\bigr)/h&n^{-1/2}\sqrt{K_{h}(\bm{X},X_{n+1})}&\bm{0}_{n\times({d_{\mathrm{e}}}-2d-4)}&1_{n}&0_{n}\\
X_{n+1}^{\mathsf{T}}&0&0_{d}^{\mathsf{T}}&n^{-1/2}h^{-d/2}&0_{{d_{\mathrm{e}}}-2d-4}^{\mathsf{T}}&1&1\end{pmatrix}\in\mathbb{R}^{(n+1)\times{d_{\mathrm{e}}}}.

Proof.

The input to the desired transformer can be written as

\displaystyle\bm{Z}_{\rm in}\coloneqq\mathrm{Embed}_{d_{\mathrm{e}}}\bigl((X_{i},Y_{i})_{i\in[n]},X_{n+1}\bigr)=\begin{pmatrix}\bm{X}&Y&\bm{0}_{n\times({d_{\mathrm{e}}}-d-2)}&0_{n}\\
X_{n+1}^{\mathsf{T}}&0&0_{{d_{\mathrm{e}}}-d-2}^{\mathsf{T}}&1\end{pmatrix}\in\mathbb{R}^{(n+1)\times{d_{\mathrm{e}}}}.

Let
\mathrm{Attn}^{(1)}
be a linear attention layer as in
Definition
2.1
with query, key and value
matrices all zero, so that
\mathrm{Attn}^{(1)}
is the
identity map on
\mathbb{R}^{(n+1)\times{d_{\mathrm{e}}}}
.
Define
b_{1}^{(1)}\coloneqq-1_{{d_{\mathrm{ffn}}}}\in\mathbb{R}^{{d_{\mathrm{ffn}}}}

and

b_{2}^{(1)}\coloneqq(0_{{d_{\mathrm{e}}}-2}^{\mathsf{T}},1,0)^{\mathsf{T}}\in\mathbb{R}^{{d_{\mathrm{e}}}}
,
and let

\displaystyle\bm{W}_{1}^{(1)}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\begin{pmatrix}\bm{I}_{d\times d}&\bm{0}_{d\times({d_{\mathrm{e}}}-d-1)}&1_{d}\\
\bm{0}_{({d_{\mathrm{ffn}}}-d)\times d}&\bm{0}_{({d_{\mathrm{ffn}}}-d)\times({d_{\mathrm{e}}}-d-1)}&0_{{d_{\mathrm{ffn}}}-d}\end{pmatrix}\in\mathbb{R}^{{d_{\mathrm{ffn}}}\times{d_{\mathrm{e}}}},

\displaystyle\bm{W}_{2}^{(1)}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\begin{pmatrix}\bm{0}_{(d+1)\times d}&\bm{0}_{(d+1)\times({d_{\mathrm{ffn}}}-d)}\\
\bm{I}_{d\times d}&\bm{0}_{d\times({d_{\mathrm{ffn}}}-d)}\\
\bm{0}_{({d_{\mathrm{e}}}-2d-1)\times d}&\bm{0}_{({d_{\mathrm{e}}}-2d-1)\times({d_{\mathrm{ffn}}}-d)}\end{pmatrix}\in\mathbb{R}^{{d_{\mathrm{e}}}\times{d_{\mathrm{ffn}}}}.

Then, since
X_{i}\in[0,1]^{d}
for
i\in[n+1]
, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{ReLU}\bigl(\bm{W}_{1}^{(1)}{\bm{Z}}_{\rm in}^{\mathsf{T}}+b_{1}^{(1)}1_{n+1}^{\mathsf{T}}\bigr)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{ReLU}\begin{pmatrix}\bm{X}^{\mathsf{T}}-1_{d}1_{n}^{\mathsf{T}}&X_{n+1}\\
-1_{{d_{\mathrm{ffn}}}-d}1_{n}^{\mathsf{T}}&-1_{{d_{\mathrm{ffn}}}-d}\\
\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}\bm{0}_{d\times n}&X_{n+1}\\
\bm{0}_{({d_{\mathrm{ffn}}}-d)\times n}&0_{{d_{\mathrm{ffn}}}-d}\end{pmatrix}\in\mathbb{R}^{{d_{\mathrm{ffn}}}\times(n+1)}.

Hence, writing
\mathrm{FFN}^{(1)}\coloneqq\mathrm{FFN}_{\bm{W}_{1}^{(1)},\bm{W}_{2}^{(1)},b_{1}^{(1)},b_{2}^{(1)}}
,
the first transformer block gives

\displaystyle\bm{Z}^{(1)}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\mathrm{FFN}^{(1)}\circ\mathrm{Attn}^{(1)}(\bm{Z}_{\mathrm{in}})=\mathrm{FFN}^{(1)}(\bm{Z}_{\mathrm{in}})

\displaystyle\,={\bm{Z}}_{\rm in}+\bigl\{\bm{W}_{2}^{(1)}\mathrm{ReLU}\bigl(\bm{W}_{1}^{(1)}{\bm{Z}}_{\rm in}^{\mathsf{T}}+b_{1}^{(1)}1_{n+1}^{\mathsf{T}}\bigr)+b_{2}^{(1)}1_{n+1}^{\mathsf{T}}\bigr\}^{\mathsf{T}}

\displaystyle\,=\begin{pmatrix}\bm{X}&Y&\bm{0}_{n\times d}&\bm{0}_{n\times({d_{\mathrm{e}}}-2d-3)}&1_{n}&0_{n}\\
X_{n+1}^{\mathsf{T}}&0&X_{n+1}^{\mathsf{T}}&0_{{d_{\mathrm{e}}}-2d-3}^{\mathsf{T}}&1&1\end{pmatrix}\in\mathbb{R}^{(n+1)\times{d_{\mathrm{e}}}}.

For the second attention layer, let

\mathrm{Attn}^{(2)}\coloneqq\mathrm{Attn}_{\bm{Q}^{(2)},\bm{K}^{(2)},\bm{V}^{(2)}}

where

\displaystyle\bm{Q}^{(2)}\coloneqq\frac{1}{\sqrt{{d_{\mathrm{e}}}}}\begin{pmatrix}\bm{0}_{({d_{\mathrm{e}}}-2)\times{d_{\mathrm{e}}}}\\

\displaystyle\bm{V}^{(2)}\coloneqq\begin{pmatrix}\bm{0}_{(d+1)\times(d+1)}&\bm{0}_{(d+1)\times d}&\bm{0}_{(d+1)\times({d_{\mathrm{e}}}-2d-1)}\\
\bm{0}_{d\times(d+1)}&\bm{I}_{d\times d}&\bm{0}_{d\times({d_{\mathrm{e}}}-2d-1)}\\
\bm{0}_{({d_{\mathrm{e}}}-2d-1)\times(d+1)}&\bm{0}_{({d_{\mathrm{e}}}-2d-1)\times d}&\bm{0}_{({d_{\mathrm{e}}}-2d-1)\times({d_{\mathrm{e}}}-2d-1)}\end{pmatrix}\in\mathbb{R}^{{d_{\mathrm{e}}}\times{d_{\mathrm{e}}}}.

Then we may write

\displaystyle\bm{Z}^{(1)}\bm{Q}^{(2)}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{{d_{\mathrm{e}}}}}\begin{pmatrix}1_{n}1_{{d_{\mathrm{e}}}}^{\mathsf{T}}\\
0_{{d_{\mathrm{e}}}}^{\mathsf{T}}\end{pmatrix},\quad\bm{Z}^{(1)}\bm{K}^{(2)}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{{d_{\mathrm{e}}}}}1_{n+1}1_{{d_{\mathrm{e}}}}^{\mathsf{T}},

\displaystyle\bm{Z}^{(1)}\bm{V}^{(2)}=\begin{pmatrix}\bm{0}_{n\times(d+1)}&\bm{0}_{n\times d}&\bm{0}_{n\times({d_{\mathrm{e}}}-2d-1)}\\
0_{d+1}^{\mathsf{T}}&X_{n+1}^{\mathsf{T}}&0_{{d_{\mathrm{e}}}-2d-1}^{\mathsf{T}}\end{pmatrix}.

Therefore, the output of the second attention layer is

\displaystyle\mathrm{Attn}^{(2)}(\bm{Z}^{(1)})

\displaystyle=\bm{Z}^{(1)}+\bm{Z}^{(1)}\bm{Q}^{(2)}(\bm{Z}^{(1)}\bm{K}^{(2)})^{\top}\bm{Z}^{(1)}\bm{V}^{(2)}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}\bm{X}&Y&1_{n}X_{n+1}^{\mathsf{T}}&\bm{0}_{n\times({d_{\mathrm{e}}}-2d-3)}&1_{n}&0_{n}\\
X_{n+1}^{\mathsf{T}}&0&X_{n+1}^{\mathsf{T}}&0_{{d_{\mathrm{e}}}-2d-3}^{\mathsf{T}}&1&1\end{pmatrix}\in\mathbb{R}^{(n+1)\times{d_{\mathrm{e}}}}.

For the second feed-forward layer, let

b_{1}^{(2)}\coloneqq 0_{{d_{\mathrm{ffn}}}}\in\mathbb{R}^{{d_{\mathrm{ffn}}}}

and define

\displaystyle\bm{W}_{1}^{(2)}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\begin{pmatrix}\bm{I}_{d\times d}/h&0_{d}&-\bm{I}_{d\times d}/h&\bm{0}_{d\times({d_{\mathrm{e}}}-2d-1)}\\
-\bm{I}_{d\times d}/h&0_{d}&\bm{I}_{d\times d}/h&\bm{0}_{d\times({d_{\mathrm{e}}}-2d-1)}\\
\bm{0}_{({d_{\mathrm{ffn}}}-2d)\times d}&0_{{d_{\mathrm{ffn}}}-2d}&\bm{0}_{({d_{\mathrm{ffn}}}-2d)\times d}&\bm{0}_{({d_{\mathrm{ffn}}}-2d)\times({d_{\mathrm{e}}}-2d-1)}\end{pmatrix}\in\mathbb{R}^{{d_{\mathrm{ffn}}}\times{d_{\mathrm{e}}}}.

Then

and bandwidth

h\coloneqq n^{-1/(2\alpha+d)}
.
There exists
C>0
, depending only on
d,\alpha,M,c_{X},C_{X}
,
such that if

L\coloneqq\lceil C\log(en)\rceil
,
then we can find a transformer

\mathrm{TF}\in\mathcal{T}({d_{\mathrm{e}}},{d_{\mathrm{ffn}}},L,B)
satisfying

\displaystyle\bigl|\mathrm{Read}_{M,d}\circ\mathrm{TF}\circ\mathrm{Embed}_{d_{\mathrm{e}}}\bigl((X_{i},Y_{i})_{i\in[n]},X_{n+1}\bigr)-\tilde{m}_{n}(X_{n+1})\bigr|\leq\frac{C}{n}

with probability at least

1-n^{C/(2\alpha+d)}\exp\bigl(-n^{2\alpha/(2\alpha+d)}/C\bigr)
.

Proof.

In this proof,
n_{0},n_{1},C_{0},C_{1},\ldots
are positive quantities depending
only on
d,\alpha,M,c_{X},C_{X}
.
By Lemma
A.3
, there exists
C_{0}\geq 1
such that
if
n\geq C_{0}^{2\alpha+d}
, then the event

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{0}\coloneqq\Bigl\{C_{0}^{-1}\leq\lambda_{\min}\bigl(\tilde{\bm{X}}^{\mathsf{T}}\tilde{\bm{X}}\bigr)\leq\lambda_{\max}\bigl(\tilde{\bm{X}}^{\mathsf{T}}\tilde{\bm{X}}\bigr)\leq C_{0}\Bigr\}

has probability at least

1-n^{C_{0}/(2\alpha+d)}\exp\bigl(-n^{2\alpha/(2\alpha+d)}/C_{0}\bigr)
.
Let
w_{*}\coloneqq\argmin_{w\in\mathbb{R}^{D}}\|\tilde{Y}-\tilde{\bm{X}}w\|_{2}^{2}

be the minimiser of
f
,
as defined in (
12
).
Then, on the event
\mathcal{E}_{0}
, we have

\displaystyle\|w_{*}\|_{2}=\bigl\|(\tilde{\bm{X}}^{\mathsf{T}}\tilde{\bm{X}})^{-1}\tilde{\bm{X}}^{\mathsf{T}}\tilde{Y}\bigr\|_{2}\leq C_{0}\|\tilde{\bm{X}}^{\mathsf{T}}\tilde{Y}\|_{2}\leq C_{0}(M+1)n\sqrt{D},

(16)

where the final inequality uses the fact that

\|\tilde{\bm{X}}\|_{\max}\leq 1

and
\|\tilde{Y}\|_{\infty}\leq M+1
.
Moreover, on the event
\mathcal{E}_{0}
,
the map

w\mapsto f(w)-\|w\|_{2}^{2}/C_{0}
is convex
and
\|\nabla f(w)-\nabla f(w^{\prime})\|_{2}\leq 2C_{0}\|w-w^{\prime}\|_{2}
for all

w,w^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{D}
, by (
13
).
We define
R\coloneqq 2C_{0}(M+1)n\sqrt{D}
and
work on the
event
\mathcal{E}_{0}
for the rest of the proof.

By Lemma
B.8
, there exists a transformer

\mathrm{TF}^{(0)}\in\mathcal{T}({d_{\mathrm{e}}},{d_{\mathrm{ffn}}},L^{(0)},B)
where

L^{(0)}\coloneqq 7p(p+1)\lceil(\log n)/(p+1)\rceil+4
such that

\displaystyle\bm{Z}^{(0)}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\mathrm{TF}^{(0)}\circ\mathrm{Embed}_{d_{\mathrm{e}}}\bigl((X_{i},Y_{i})_{i\in[n]},X_{n+1}\bigr)

\displaystyle\,=\begin{pmatrix}\bm{X}&Y&\bigl(\bm{X}-1_{n}X_{n+1}^{\mathsf{T}}\bigr)/h&n^{-1/2}\sqrt{K_{h}(\bm{X},X_{n+1})}&\check{\bm{X}}&\check{Y}&1_{n}w_{0}^{\mathsf{T}}&1_{n}&0_{n}\\
X_{n+1}^{\mathsf{T}}&0&0_{d}^{\mathsf{T}}&n^{-1/2}h^{-d/2}&a^{\mathsf{T}}&b&w_{0}^{\mathsf{T}}&1&1\end{pmatrix},

(17)

where
w_{0}\coloneqq 0_{D}
, and where
\check{\bm{X}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times D}
,

\check{Y}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

a\in\mathbb{R}^{D}
and
b\in\mathbb{R}
satisfy

\displaystyle\|\check{\bm{X}}-\tilde{\bm{X}}\|_{\max}\vee\|\check{Y}-\tilde{Y}\|_{\infty}\vee\|a\|_{\infty}\vee|b|\leq 30p\cdot(2M+2)^{p+1}\cdot n^{-5}\eqqcolon\xi.

(18)

Next, define
\epsilon\coloneqq 18D(M+3)(R+1)n\xi
.
Assume initially that
n\geq n_{0}
, where
n_{0}

is large enough that
\xi\leq 1
.
We claim that for every

T\in\bigl\{0,1,\ldots,\lfloor RC_{0}/\epsilon\rfloor\bigr\}
,
there exist
\mathrm{TF}^{(0)}\in\mathcal{T}({d_{\mathrm{e}}},{d_{\mathrm{ffn}}},L^{(0)},B)
,

\mathrm{TF}^{(1)},\ldots,\mathrm{TF}^{(T)}\in\mathcal{T}({d_{\mathrm{e}}},{d_{\mathrm{ffn}}},1,B)
,

w_{0},\ldots,w_{T}\in\mathbb{R}^{D}
and

g_{0},\ldots,g_{T-1}\in\mathbb{R}^{D}
such that

w_{0}=0_{D}
,

w_{t+1}=w_{t}-g_{t}/(2C_{0})
,

\|g_{t}-\nabla f(w_{t})\|_{2}\leq\epsilon
,

\|w_{t+1}\|_{2}\leq R
for all

t\in\{0,1,\ldots,T-1\}
and

\displaystyle\bm{Z}^{(t)}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\mathrm{TF}^{(t)}\circ\cdots\circ\mathrm{TF}^{(1)}\circ\mathrm{TF}^{(0)}\circ\mathrm{Embed}_{d_{\mathrm{e}}}\bigl((X_{i},Y_{i})_{i\in[n]},X_{n+1}\bigr)

\displaystyle\,=\begin{pmatrix}\bm{X}&Y&\bigl(\bm{X}-1_{n}X_{n+1}^{\mathsf{T}}\bigr)/h&n^{-1/2}\sqrt{K_{h}(\bm{X},X_{n+1})}&\check{\bm{X}}&\check{Y}&1_{n}w_{t}^{\mathsf{T}}&1_{n}&0_{n}\\
X_{n+1}^{\mathsf{T}}&0&0_{d}^{\mathsf{T}}&n^{-1/2}h^{-d/2}&a^{\mathsf{T}}&b&w_{t}^{\mathsf{T}}&1&1\end{pmatrix},

for
t\in\{0,\ldots,T\}
. We argue by induction.
The case
T=0
is verified by (
17
).
Now suppose that the claim is true for some

T\leq\lfloor RC_{0}/\epsilon\rfloor-1
.
We may apply Lemma
B.10
to deduce that
there exists
\mathrm{TF}^{(T+1)}\in\mathcal{T}({d_{\mathrm{e}}},{d_{\mathrm{ffn}}},1,B)
such that

\displaystyle\bm{Z}^{(T+1)}\coloneqq\mathrm{TF}^{(T+1)}\bigl(\bm{Z}^{(T)}\bigr)

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}\bm{X}&Y&\bigl(\bm{X}-1_{n}X_{n+1}^{\mathsf{T}}\bigr)/h&n^{-1/2}\sqrt{K_{h}(\bm{X},X_{n+1})}&\check{\bm{X}}&\check{Y}&1_{n}(w_{T}-g_{T}/(2C_{0}))^{\mathsf{T}}&1_{n}&0_{n}\\
X_{n+1}^{\mathsf{T}}&0&0_{d}^{\mathsf{T}}&n^{-1/2}h^{-d/2}&a^{\mathsf{T}}&b&(w_{T}-g_{T}/(2C_{0}))^{\mathsf{T}}&1&1\end{pmatrix},

where by (
18
),
the inductive hypothesis that
\|w_{T}\|_{2}\leq R
and
Lemma
B.10
, we have

\displaystyle\|g_{T}-\nabla f(w_{T})\|_{2}\leq 18D(M+3)(R+1)n\xi=\epsilon.

Moreover, by Lemma
B.9

(with
C_{1}\coloneqq 2/C_{0}
and
C_{2}\coloneqq 2C_{0}
therein),
and since
T+1\leq\lfloor RC_{0}/\epsilon\rfloor
,
we have

. This proves the claim by induction. Now, let

T\coloneqq\lceil 4C_{0}^{2}\log n\rceil
and further assume that
n\geq n_{1}
,
where
n_{1}
is large

(note that

is quartic
in

is logarithmic).

B.9
again

16
), we deduce that

\displaystyle\|w_{T}-w_{*}\|_{2}\leq\exp\biggl(-\frac{T}{2C_{0}^{2}}\biggr)\cdot C_{0}(M+1)n\sqrt{D}+\frac{T\epsilon}{2C_{0}}\leq\frac{C_{3}}{n}.

Let
\mathrm{TF}^{(T+1)}\in\mathcal{T}({d_{\mathrm{e}}},{d_{\mathrm{ffn}}},1,B)
be a transformer block such
that for any
\bm{Z}\in R^{(n+1)\times{d_{\mathrm{e}}}}

the
(d+1)
th column of
\mathrm{TF}^{(T+1)}(\bm{Z})
is
equal to the sum of the
(d+1)
th column and the

(2d+D+4)
th column of
\bm{Z}

(this can be done by using only the FFN layer).
Further, define

\mathrm{TF}\coloneqq\mathrm{TF}^{(T+1)}\circ\cdots\circ\mathrm{TF}^{(0)}\in\mathcal{T}({d_{\mathrm{e}}},{d_{\mathrm{ffn}}},L^{(0)}+T+1,B)
.
Then, writing
w_{T,1}
and
w_{*,1}

for the first entries of
w_{T}
and

respectively,
we have for

that

\displaystyle\bigl|\mathrm{Read}_{M,d}\circ\mathrm{TF}

\displaystyle\circ\mathrm{Embed}_{d_{\mathrm{e}}}\bigl((X_{i},Y_{i})_{i\in[n]},X_{n+1}\bigr)-\tilde{m}_{n}(X_{n+1})\bigr|

\displaystyle=\bigl|(-M)\vee w_{T,1}\wedge M-(-M)\vee w_{*,1}\wedge M\bigr|\leq|w_{T,1}-w_{*,1}|\leq\frac{C_{3}}{n},

(19)

with probability at least

1-n^{C_{0}/(2\alpha+d)}\exp\bigl(-n^{2\alpha/(2\alpha+d)}/C_{0}\bigr)
.
On the other hand, the left-hand side of (

is bounded by

with

if necessary,
the conclusion holds for all

.
Finally, note that

, so
the lemma holds with
C\coloneqq C_{0}\vee C_{4}\vee C_{5}
.
∎

Lemma C.1

\displaystyle\hskip 71.13188pt+\{(n+1){d_{\mathrm{e}}}\}^{3/2}{d_{\mathrm{e}}}^{3}\|\bm{Z}\|_{\max}^{3}\|\bm{Q}^{\prime}\|_{\max}\|\bm{K}-\bm{K}^{\prime}\|_{\max}\|\bm{V}\|_{\max}

\displaystyle\hskip 71.13188pt+\{(n+1){d_{\mathrm{e}}}\}^{3/2}{d_{\mathrm{e}}}^{3}\|\bm{Z}\|_{\max}^{3}\|\bm{Q}^{\prime}\|_{\max}\|\bm{K}^{\prime}\|_{\max}\|\bm{V}-\bm{V}^{\prime}\|_{\max}

as required.

C.2
.

2.2
,

Appendix D
Risk Decomposition

Theorem D.1
.

Let
n,d,{d_{\mathrm{e}}},{d_{\mathrm{ffn}}},L,\Gamma\in\mathbb{N}

with
{d_{\mathrm{e}}}\geq d+2
and take
B>0
.
Take
\hat{f}_{\Gamma}
as in Definition
2.4
.
There exists a universal constant
C>0
such that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigl\{R(\hat{f}_{\Gamma})\bigr\}

\displaystyle\leq\inf_{f\in\mathcal{F}({d_{\mathrm{e}}},{d_{\mathrm{ffn}}},L,B,M)}2R(f)-\sigma^{2}+C(M+1)^{5}L{d_{\mathrm{e}}}({d_{\mathrm{e}}}+{d_{\mathrm{ffn}}})\frac{L\log\bigl\{(B+1)n{d_{\mathrm{e}}}{d_{\mathrm{ffn}}}\bigr\}+\log\Gamma}{\Gamma}.

Proof of Theorem
D.1
.

Throughout the proof, we write

C_{1},C_{2},\ldots
to denote
universal positive constants.
Let
f^{\star}\in\mathcal{F}({d_{\mathrm{e}}},{d_{\mathrm{ffn}}},L,B,M)

satisfy

R(f^{\star})\leq\inf_{f\in\mathcal{F}({d_{\mathrm{e}}},{d_{\mathrm{ffn}}},L,B,M)}R(f)+1/\Gamma
.
By the Doob–Dynkin lemma

(e.g.,
33
, Exercise 10.2.14)
, let

f_{0}:\bigl([0,1]^{d}\times\mathbb{R}\bigr)^{n}\times[0,1]^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a Borel-measurable function satisfying

f_{0}\bigl(\mathcal{D}_{n},X_{n+1}\bigr)=\mathbb{E}(Y_{n+1}\mid\mathcal{D}_{n},X_{n+1})

almost surely, so that
R(f_{0})\geq\sigma^{2}
.
Since
\hat{R}_{\Gamma}(\hat{f}_{\Gamma})\leq\hat{R}_{\Gamma}(f^{\star})

and as
\mathbb{E}\bigl\{\hat{R}_{\Gamma}(f)\bigr\}=R(f)
for all
bounded, measurable functions
f
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigl\{R(\hat{f}_{\Gamma})\bigr\}

\displaystyle=R(f_{0})+\mathbb{E}\bigl\{R(\hat{f}_{\Gamma})-R(f_{0})-2\hat{R}_{\Gamma}(\hat{f}_{\Gamma})+2\hat{R}_{\Gamma}(f_{0})\bigr\}+2\,\mathbb{E}\bigl\{\hat{R}_{\Gamma}(\hat{f}_{\Gamma})-\hat{R}_{\Gamma}(f_{0})\bigr\}

\displaystyle\quad\leq R(f_{0})+\mathbb{E}\bigl\{R(\hat{f}_{\Gamma})-R(f_{0})-2\hat{R}_{\Gamma}(\hat{f}_{\Gamma})+2\hat{R}_{\Gamma}(f_{0})\bigr\}+2\bigl\{R(f^{\star})-R(f_{0})\bigr\}

\displaystyle\quad\leq\mathbb{E}\bigl\{R(\hat{f}_{\Gamma})-R(f_{0})-2\hat{R}_{\Gamma}(\hat{f}_{\Gamma})+2\hat{R}_{\Gamma}(f_{0})\bigr\}+\inf_{f\in\mathcal{F}({d_{\mathrm{e}}},{d_{\mathrm{ffn}}},L,B,M)}2R(f)+\frac{2}{\Gamma}-\sigma^{2}.

(20)

To bound the expectation, we apply the bound given by

15
with
\epsilon=1/2

and
\alpha=\beta=t/2
therein,
noting that
|Y_{n+1}^{(\gamma)}|\leq M+1

for each
\gamma\in[\Gamma]
and

\sup_{f\in\mathcal{F}({d_{\mathrm{e}}},{d_{\mathrm{ffn}}},L,B,M)}\|f\|_{\infty}\leq M
.
As
L_{1}
-covering numbers are bounded by

L_{\infty}
-covering numbers,
there exists a universal constant
C_{1}>0

such that for all
t>0
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\Bigl(R(\hat{f}_{\Gamma})-R(f_{0})-2\hat{R}_{\Gamma}(\hat{f}_{\Gamma})+2\hat{R}_{\Gamma}(f_{0})\geq t\Bigr)

\displaystyle\qquad\leq C_{1}N\biggl(\mathcal{F}({d_{\mathrm{e}}},{d_{\mathrm{ffn}}},L,B,M),\frac{t}{C_{1}(M+1)},\|\cdot\|_{\infty}\biggr)\exp\biggl(-\frac{\Gamma t}{C_{1}(M+1)^{4}}\biggr).

Hence, by Lemma
C.4
,
taking
t\coloneqq C_{1}s(M+1)^{4}/\Gamma

for
s\in[1,\Gamma]
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\biggl(R(\hat{f}_{\Gamma})-R(f_{0})-2\hat{R}_{\Gamma}(\hat{f}_{\Gamma})+2\hat{R}_{\Gamma}(f_{0})\geq\frac{C_{1}s(M+1)^{4}}{\Gamma}\biggr)

\displaystyle\qquad\leq C_{1}N\bigl(\mathcal{F}({d_{\mathrm{e}}},{d_{\mathrm{ffn}}},L,B,M),s/\Gamma,\|\cdot\|_{\infty}\bigr)e^{-s}

\displaystyle\qquad\leq C_{1}\exp\Bigl(24L{d_{\mathrm{e}}}({d_{\mathrm{e}}}+{d_{\mathrm{ffn}}})\log\bigl\{(B+1)^{L}(M+2)^{2L}(n+1)^{L}{d_{\mathrm{e}}}^{L}{d_{\mathrm{ffn}}}^{L}\Gamma\bigr\}\Bigr)e^{-s}.

(21)

In fact, taking
C_{1}\geq 12
without loss of generality,
(
21
) holds for all

s\in[1,\infty)
because
\|\hat{f}_{\Gamma}\|_{\infty}\leq M

and
\|f_{0}\|_{\infty}\leq M+1
, so

R(\hat{f}_{\Gamma})\leq 4(M+1)^{2}

and
\hat{R}_{\Gamma}(f_{0})\leq 4(M+1)^{2}
.
Therefore, for all
u>0
,
with probability at least

1-C_{2}e^{-u}
,
as
{d_{\mathrm{e}}}\geq 3>e
,

\displaystyle R(\hat{f}_{\Gamma})-R(f_{0})-2\hat{R}_{\Gamma}(\hat{f}_{\Gamma})+2\hat{R}_{\Gamma}(f_{0})

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\frac{C_{2}(M+1)^{4}}{\Gamma}\Bigl\{u+L{d_{\mathrm{e}}}({d_{\mathrm{e}}}+{d_{\mathrm{ffn}}})\log\bigl\{(B+1)^{L}(M+2)^{2L}(n+1)^{L}{d_{\mathrm{e}}}^{L}{d_{\mathrm{ffn}}}^{L}\Gamma\bigr\}\Bigr\}.

Integrating the tail probability, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigl\{R(\hat{f}_{\Gamma})-R(f_{0})-2\hat{R}_{\Gamma}(\hat{f}_{\Gamma})+2\hat{R}_{\Gamma}(f_{0})\bigr\}

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\frac{C_{3}(M+1)^{4}L{d_{\mathrm{e}}}({d_{\mathrm{e}}}+{d_{\mathrm{ffn}}})\log\bigl\{(B+1)^{L}(M+2)^{2L}(n+1)^{L}{d_{\mathrm{e}}}^{L}{d_{\mathrm{ffn}}}^{L}\Gamma\bigr\}}{\Gamma}

\displaystyle\qquad\leq C_{4}(M+1)^{5}L{d_{\mathrm{e}}}({d_{\mathrm{e}}}+{d_{\mathrm{ffn}}})\frac{L\log\bigl\{(B+1)n{d_{\mathrm{e}}}{d_{\mathrm{ffn}}}\bigr\}+\log\Gamma}{\Gamma}.

(22)

The result follows from (
20
)
and (
22
).
∎

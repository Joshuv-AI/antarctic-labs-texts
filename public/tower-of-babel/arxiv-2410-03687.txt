Abstract
This paper focuses on the stability of both local and global error bounds for a proper lower semicontinuous convex function defined on a Banach space. Without relying on any dual space information, we first provide precise estimates of error bound moduli using directional derivatives. For a given proper lower semicontinuous convex function on a Banach space, we prove that the stability of local error bounds under small perturbations is equivalent to the directional derivative at a reference point having a non-zero minimum over the unit sphere. Additionally, the stability of global error bounds is shown to be equivalent to the infimum of the directional derivatives, at all points on the boundary of the solution set, being bounded away from zero over some neighborhood of the unit sphere.

Preliminaries

Let
\mathbb{X}
be a Banach space with topological dual
\mathbb{X}^{*}
. We denote by
\mathbb{B}_{\mathbb{X}}
the closed unit ball in
\mathbb{X}
, and for any
x\in\mathbb{X}
and
\delta>0
, by
\mathbf{B}(x,\delta)
the open ball centered at
x
with radius
\delta
.

Let
D
be a subset of
\mathbb{X}
. We denote by
{\rm int}(D)
and
{\rm bdry}(D)
the interior and boundary of
D
, respectively. The distance from a point
x\in\mathbb{X}
to the set
D
is given by

\mathbf{d}(x,D):=\inf_{u\in D}\|x-u\|,

with the convention that
\mathbf{d}(x,D)=+\infty
whenever
D=\emptyset
.

In the following, we use the notation
\Gamma_{0}(\mathbb{X})
to denote the set of extended-real-valued lower semicontinuous convex functions
\varphi:\mathbb{X}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
, which are assumed to be proper, meaning that
{\rm dom}(\varphi):=\{x\in\mathbb{X}:\varphi(x)<+\infty\}
is nonempty.

Let
\varphi\in\Gamma_{0}(\mathbb{X})
and
\bar{x}\in{\rm dom}(\varphi)
. For any
h\in\mathbb{X}
, we denote by
d^{+}\varphi(\bar{x},h)
the
directional derivative
of
\varphi
at
\bar{x}
along the direction
h
, defined by

d^{+}\varphi(\bar{x},h):=\lim_{t\rightarrow 0^{+}}\frac{\varphi(\bar{x}+th)-\varphi(\bar{x})}{t}.

(2.1)

According to
[
53
]
, we know that

t\mapsto\frac{\varphi(\bar{x}+th)-\varphi(\bar{x})}{t}

is nonincreasing as
t\rightarrow 0^{+}
, and thus

d^{+}\varphi(\bar{x},h)=\inf_{t>0}\frac{\varphi(\bar{x}+th)-\varphi(\bar{x})}{t}.

(2.2)

We denote by
\partial\varphi(\bar{x})
the
subdifferential
of
\varphi
at
\bar{x}
, defined by

\partial\varphi(\bar{x}):=\{x^{*}\in\mathbb{X}^{*}:\langle x^{*},x-\bar{x}\rangle\leq\varphi(x)-\varphi(\bar{x})\ \text{for all }x\in\mathbb{X}\}.

It is known from
(
53
, Proposition 2.24)
that if
\varphi
is continuous at
\bar{x}
, then
\partial\varphi(\bar{x})\neq\emptyset
,

\partial\varphi(\bar{x})=\{x^{*}\in\mathbb{X}^{*}:\langle x^{*},h\rangle\leq d^{+}\varphi(\bar{x},h)\ \text{for all }h\in\mathbb{X}\},

(2.3)

and

d^{+}\varphi(\bar{x},h)=\sup\{\langle x^{*},h\rangle:x^{*}\in\partial\varphi(\bar{x})\}.

(2.4)

Given a mapping
\varphi:\mathbb{X}\rightarrow\mathbb{Y}
from
\mathbb{X}
to a
normed linear space

\mathbb{Y}
, we denote by

{\rm Lip}(\varphi):=\sup_{u,v\in\mathbb{X},u\neq v}\frac{\|\varphi(u)-\varphi(v)\|_{\mathbb{Y}}}{\|u-v\|_{\mathbb{X}}}

the Lipschitz constant of
\varphi
.

3
Stability of Error Bounds for Convex Functions on a Banach Space

This section is devoted to the stability of local and global error bounds for a given proper lower semicontinuous convex function defined on a Banach space and aims to establish primal characterizations in terms of directional derivatives. We begin with some notations and definitions of error bounds for convex functions.

Let
f\in\Gamma_{0}(X)
. We denote by

\textbf{S}_{f}:=\{x\in X:f(x)\leq 0\}

(3.1)

the
lower level set
of
f
. Recall that
f
is said to have
global error bound
, if there exist
c\in(0,+\infty)
such that

c{\bf d}(x,\textbf{S}_{f})\leq f_{+}(x)\ \ \forall x\in X,

where
f_{+}(x):=\max\{f(x),0\}
. We denote by

\mathbf{Er}(f):=\inf_{f(x)>0}\frac{f(x)}{{\bf d}(x,\textbf{S}_{f})}

(3.2)

the
global error bound modulus
of
f
. Thus,
f
has a global error bound if and only if
\mathbf{Er}(f)>0
.

Let
\bar{x}\in{\rm bdry}(\textbf{S}_{f})
. Recall that
f
is said to have
local error bound
at
\bar{x}
, if there exist
c,\delta\in(0,+\infty)
such that

c{\bf d}(x,\textbf{S}_{f})\leq[f(x)]_{+}\ \ \forall x\in\mathbb{B}(\bar{x},\delta).

We denote by

\mathbf{Er}(f,\bar{x}):=\liminf_{x\rightarrow\bar{x},f(x)>0}\frac{f(x)}{{\bf d}(x,\textbf{S}_{f})}

(3.3)

the
local error bound modulus
of
f
. Thus,
f
has a local error bound at
\bar{x}

\mathbf{Er}(f,\bar{x})>0
.

The following theorem, as a key result of this paper, is to give precise estimate on error bound moduli of proper lower semicontinuous convex functions on the Banach space, with no help of any information from the dual space. The proof only relies on the Ekeland variational principle.

Theorem 3.1

Let
f\in\Gamma_{0}(X)
be such that
\textbf{S}_{f}
is nonempty.

(i)

The following equality on the global error bound modulus holds:

\mathbf{Er}(f)=\inf_{f(x)>0}\left(-\inf_{\|h\|=1}d^{+}f(x,h)\right).

(3.4)

(ii)

Let
\bar{x}\in{\rm bdry}(S_{f})
. Then the following equality on the local error bound modulus holds:

\mathbf{Er}(f,\bar{x})=\liminf_{x\rightarrow\bar{x},f(x)>0}\left(-\inf_{\|h\|=1}d^{+}f(x,h)\right).

(3.5)

Proof.
(i) Let us define

\beta(f):=\inf_{f(x)>0}\left(-\inf_{\|h\|=1}d^{+}f(x,h)\right).

We first prove that (
3.4
) holds.
Let
x\in X
be such that
f(x)>0
. Take a sequence
\{u_{n}\}
in
\textbf{S}_{f}
such that
\|x-u_{n}\|\rightarrow{\rm d}(x,\textbf{S}_{f})
. By virtue of (
2.2
), one has

\displaystyle\frac{f(x)}{\|x-u_{n}\|}\leq\frac{f(x)-f(u_{n})}{\|x-u_{n}\|}=-\frac{f(u_{n})-f(x)}{\|x-u_{n}\|}\leq-d^{+}f\big(x,\frac{u_{n}-x}{\|x-u_{n}\|}\big)\leq-\inf_{\|h=1\|}d^{+}f(x,h).

Letting
n\rightarrow\infty
, one has

\frac{f(x)}{{\bf d}(x,\textbf{S}_{f})}\leq-\inf_{\|h=1\|}d^{+}f(x,h).

This means that

\mathbf{Er}(f)\leq\beta(f).

Without loss of generality, we can assume that
\mathbf{Er}(f)<+\infty
. Suppose on the contrary that
\mathbf{Er}(f)<\beta(f)
. Then there is
\tau_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
such that
\mathbf{Er}(f)<\tau_{0}<\beta(f)
. By the definition of
\mathbf{Er}(f)
, there exists
x_{0}\in X
such that

f(x_{0})>0\ \ {\rm and}\ \ f(x_{0})<\tau_{0}{\bf d}(x_{0},\textbf{S}_{f}).

This implies that

f_{+}(x_{0})<\inf_{x\in X}f_{+}(x)+\tau_{0}{\bf d}(x_{0},\textbf{S}_{f}).

By applying Ekeland’s variational princinple (cf.
[
54
]
), there exists
y_{0}\in X
such that

\|y_{0}-x_{0}\|<{\bf d}(x_{0},\textbf{S}_{f})\ \ {\rm and}\ \ f_{+}(y_{0})<f_{+}(x)+\tau_{0}\|x-y_{0}\|,\ \ \forall x\in X\backslash\{y_{0}\}.

On the one hand, since
\|y_{0}-x_{0}\|<d(x_{0},S_{f})
, this implies that
y_{0}\not\in\textbf{S}_{f}
, that is
f(y_{0})=f_{+}(y_{0})>0
. Indeed, supposing that
y_{0}\in S_{f}
would imply that

\|y_{0}-x_{0}\|<d(x_{0},S_{f})\leq\|y_{0}-x_{0}\|
, a contradiction. On the other hand, for any
h\in X
with
\|h\|=1
and
t>0
sufficiently small, one has

\frac{f(y_{0}+th)-f(y_{0})}{t}=\frac{f_{+}(y_{0}+th)-f_{+}(y_{0})}{t}>-\tau_{0}.

Letting
t\rightarrow 0^{+}
, one yields that
d^{+}f(y_{0},h)\geq-\tau_{0}
and consequently

\inf_{\|h\|=1}d^{+}f(y_{0},h)\geq-\tau_{0}.

Therefore,

-\inf_{\|h\|=1}d^{+}f(y_{0},h)\leq\tau_{0}<\beta(f)\leq-\inf_{\|h\|=1}d^{+}f(y_{0},h),

which is a contradiction. This means that
\mathbf{Er}(f)=\beta(f)
and so (i) holds.

The same method can be applied to prove (
3.5
) and thus we omit the proof of (ii). The proof is complete.∎

The following corollary follows from
Theorem

3.1
immediately.

Corollary 1

Let
f\in\Gamma_{0}(\mathbb{X})
and
\bar{x}\in\mathbb{X}
such that
f(\bar{x})=0
and
\inf_{\|h\|=1}d^{+}f(\bar{x},h)\not=0
. Then

\mathbf{Er}(f,\bar{x})\geq\left|\inf_{\|h\|=1}d^{+}f(\bar{x},h)\right|.

(3.6)

Proof.
If
\inf_{\|h\|=1}d^{+}f(\bar{x},h)>0
, then for any
x\in\mathbb{X}\backslash\{\bar{x}\}
, one has

\displaystyle f(x)-f(\bar{x})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f\Big(\bar{x}+\|x-\bar{x}\|\frac{x-\bar{x}}{\|x-\bar{x}\|}\Big)-f(\bar{x})

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle d^{+}f\Big(\bar{x},\frac{x-\bar{x}}{\|x-\bar{x}\|}\Big)\|x-\bar{x}\|>0.

This implies that
S_{f}=\{\bar{x}\}
and
\mathbf{Er}(f,\bar{x})\geq\inf_{\|h\|=1}d^{+}f(\bar{x},h)
.

If
\inf_{\|h\|=1}d^{+}f(\bar{x},h)<0
, then there exist
t>0
and
h_{0}\in\mathbb{X}
with
\|h_{0}\|=1
such that

f(\bar{x}+th_{0})<0

and thus
0\not\in\partial f(\bar{x})
thanks to
f(\bar{x})=0
. By virtue of
(
49
, Theorem 1)
, one has that
f
admits a local error bound and

\mathbf{Er}(f,\bar{x})\geq\mathbf{d}(0,\partial f(\bar{x})).

Note that for any
x^{*}\in\partial f(\bar{x})
,
h\in\mathbb{X}
with
\|h\|=1
and
t>0
, one has

\langle x^{*},th\rangle\leq f(\bar{x}+th)-f(\bar{x})

and thus

d^{+}f(\bar{x},h)\geq\langle x^{*},h\rangle\geq-\|x^{*}\|.

This means that

\mathbf{d}(0,\partial f(\bar{x}))\geq-\inf_{\|h\|=1}d^{+}f(\bar{x},h)

and consequently

\mathbf{Er}(f,\bar{x})\geq-\inf_{\|h\|=1}d^{+}f(\bar{x},h).

Hence the conclusion holds. The proof is complete.
\Box

Remark 1

It should be noted that inequality in (
3.6
) may hold strictly. For example, let
\mathbb{X}:=\mathbb{R}
and
f(x)\equiv 0
for all
x\in\mathbb{X}
. Then one can verify that

\mathbf{Er}(f,\bar{x})=+\infty\ \ {\rm and}\ \ \inf\limits_{\|h\|=1}d^{+}f(\bar{x},h)=0.

This means that
\mathbf{Er}(f,\bar{x})>\inf_{\|h\|=1}d^{+}f(\bar{x},h)
.

The following theorem shows that given a proper lower semicontinuous convex function
f
, the condition
\inf_{\|h\|=1}d^{+}f(\bar{x},h)\not=0
is essentially equivalent to the stability of local error bounds. For this result, we need the following definition of
\varepsilon
-perturbation of
f
near
\bar{x}
given in
(
49
, Definition 5)
.

Definition 1

Let
f:\mathbb{X}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
,
\bar{x}\in{\rm dom}(f)
and
\varepsilon\geq 0
. We say that
g:\mathbb{X}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
is an
\varepsilon
-perturbation of
f
near
\bar{x}
, if

\limsup_{x\rightarrow\bar{x}}\frac{|(f(x)-g(x))-(f(\bar{x})-g(\bar{x}))|}{\|x-\bar{x}\|}\leq\varepsilon.

(3.7)

In this case, we write
g\in{\rm\mathbf{P}\mathbf{t}\mathbf{b}}(f,\bar{x},\varepsilon)
.

Clearly, if
g\in{\rm\mathbf{P}\mathbf{t}\mathbf{b}}(f,\bar{x},\varepsilon)
, then
f\in{\rm\mathbf{P}\mathbf{t}\mathbf{b}}(g,\bar{x},\varepsilon)
.

Theorem 3.2

Let
f\in\Gamma_{0}(\mathbb{X})
and
\bar{x}\in\mathbb{X}
with
f(\bar{x})=0
. Then the following statements are equivalent:

(i)

\inf_{\|h\|=1}d^{+}f(\bar{x},h)\not=0
.

(ii)

There exist
c,\varepsilon>0
such that
\mathbf{Er}(g,\bar{x})\geq c
for all
g\in\Gamma_{0}(\mathbb{X})\cap{\rm\mathbf{P}\mathbf{t}\mathbf{b}}(f,\bar{x},\varepsilon)
with
\bar{x}\in S_{g}
.

(iii)

There exist
c,\varepsilon>0
such that
\mathbf{Er}(g,\bar{x})\geq c
for all
g\in\Gamma_{0}(\mathbb{X})\cap{\rm\mathbf{P}\mathbf{t}\mathbf{b}}(f,\bar{x},\varepsilon)
defined by

g(\cdot):=f(\cdot)+\varepsilon\langle u^{*},\cdot-\bar{x}\rangle,\ \forall u^{*}\in\mathbb{B}_{\mathbb{X}^{*}}.

Proof.
We denote
\gamma:=\inf_{\|h\|=1}d^{+}f(\bar{x},h).

(i)
\Rightarrow
(ii): Let
\varepsilon,c>0
be such that
\varepsilon+c<|\gamma|
. Take any
g\in\Gamma_{0}(\mathbb{X})\cap{\rm\mathbf{P}\mathbf{t}\mathbf{b}}(f,\bar{x},\varepsilon)
with
\bar{x}\in S_{g}
.

•

If
\gamma>0
, then for any
h\in\mathbb{X}
with
\|h\|=1
, one has

d^{+}g(\bar{x},h)\geq d^{+}f(\bar{x},h)-\varepsilon,

and consequently

\inf_{\|h\|=1}d^{+}g(\bar{x},h)\geq\inf_{\|h\|=1}d^{+}f(\bar{x},h)-\varepsilon=\gamma-\varepsilon.

This and (
3.6
) imply that
\mathbf{Er}(g,\bar{x})\geq\gamma-\varepsilon
.

•

If
\gamma<0
, then for any
h\in\mathbb{X}
, one has

d^{+}g(\bar{x},h)\leq d^{+}f(\bar{x},h)+\varepsilon,

and thus

\inf_{\|h\|=1}d^{+}g(\bar{x},h)\leq\inf_{\|h\|=1}d^{+}f(\bar{x},h)+\varepsilon=\gamma+\varepsilon.

Using (
3.6
), one has
\mathbf{Er}(g,\bar{x})\geq-\gamma-\varepsilon>c
. Hence (ii) holds.

Note that (ii)
\Rightarrow
(iii) follows immediately and we next prove (iii)
\Rightarrow
(i).

Let
\varepsilon>0
. Suppose on the contrary that
\gamma=0
. Then there is a sequence

such that
\|h_{k}\|=1
and

\alpha_{k}:=d^{+}f(\bar{x},h_{k})\rightarrow\gamma=0\ \ {\rm as}\ k\rightarrow\infty.

For sufficiently large

, one has

and by the Hahn-Banach theorem, there is

such that
\|h^{*}_{k}\|=1
and

.

Let

for all

. Then

.
Note that
\gamma=0
and thus for any

, one has

. By (

), there exists

such that

(3.8)

By virtue of Ekeland’s variational principle (cf.
[
54
]
), there is
z_{k}\in\mathbb{X}
such that

\|z_{k}-(\bar{x}+\delta_{k}h_{k})\|<\frac{\delta_{k}}{2},f(z_{k})\leq f(\bar{x}+\delta_{k}h_{k})

f(x)+2(\varepsilon+\alpha_{k})\|x-z_{k}\|>f(z_{k}),\ \forall x:x\not=z_{k}.

(3.9)

This implies that
z_{k}\rightarrow\bar{x}
,
g(\bar{x})=f(\bar{x})=0
and

\displaystyle g(z_{k})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f(z_{k})+\varepsilon\langle h_{k}^{*},z_{k}-\bar{x}\rangle

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle f(\bar{x})+\varepsilon\langle h_{k}^{*},z_{k}-\bar{x}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varepsilon\langle h_{k}^{*},z_{k}-\bar{x}-\delta_{k}h_{k}\rangle+\varepsilon\delta_{k}

\displaystyle>

\displaystyle\varepsilon\delta_{k}-\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon\delta_{k}=\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon\delta_{k}>0.

We claim that

\inf_{\|h\|=1}d^{+}g(z_{k},h)<0,

(3.10)

Indeed, by reductio ad absurdum, if
\inf_{\|h\|=1}d^{+}g(z_{k},h)\geq 0
, thus
g(z_{k})=\inf_{x\in\mathbb{X}}g(x)
, which contradicts
g(\bar{x})=0
.

For any
h\in\mathbb{X}
,
\|h\|=1
and any
t>0
, by (
3.9
), one can get

\displaystyle\frac{g(z_{k}+th)-g(z_{k})}{t}=\frac{f(z_{k}+th)-f(z_{k})}{t}+\varepsilon\langle h_{k}^{*},h\rangle\geq-2(\varepsilon+\alpha_{k})\|h\|-\varepsilon=-5\varepsilon.

Thus,

\inf_{\|h\|=1}d^{+}g(z_{k},h)\geq-5\varepsilon.

By (
3.10
) and
Theorem

3.1
, one has
\mathbf{Er}(g,\bar{x})\leq 5\varepsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}
, which contradicts (iii), as
\varepsilon
is arbitrary. The proof is complete.
\Box

The following corollary holds from
Theorem

3.2
immediately.

Corollary 2

Let
f\in\Gamma_{0}(\mathbb{X})
and
\bar{x}\in\mathbb{X}
with
f(\bar{x})=0
. Then

(i)

\inf_{\|h\|=1}d^{+}f(\bar{x},h)\not=0
holds if and only if there exists
\varepsilon>0
such that

\inf\big\{\mathbf{Er}(g,\bar{x}):g\in\Gamma_{0}(\mathbb{X})\cap{\rm\mathbf{P}\mathbf{t}\mathbf{b}}(f,\bar{x},\varepsilon)\ {\it with}\ \bar{x}\in S_{g}\big\}>0.

(ii)

\inf_{\|h\|=1}d^{+}f(\bar{x},h)=0
holds if and only if for any
\varepsilon>0
, one has

\inf\big\{\mathbf{Er}(g,\bar{x}):g\in\Gamma_{0}(\mathbb{X})\cap{\rm\mathbf{P}\mathbf{t}\mathbf{b}}(f,\bar{x},\varepsilon)\ {\it with}\ \bar{x}\in S_{g}\big\}=0.

Next, we study the stability of global error bounds for lower semicontinuous convex functions. The following theorem give primal criterion for the stability of global error bounds.

Theorem 3.3

Let
f\in\Gamma_{0}(\mathbb{X})
be such that
{\rm bdry}(\textbf{S}_{f})\subseteq f^{-1}(0)
. Consider the following statements:

(i)

there exist
c,\varepsilon\in(0,+\infty)
such that
\mathbf{Er}(g)\geq c
holds for all
g\in\Gamma_{0}(\mathbb{X})
satisfying

\textbf{S}_{f}\subseteq S_{g}\ \ {\it and}\ \ {\rm Lip}(f-g)<\varepsilon;

(3.11)

(ii)

there exist
c,\varepsilon\in(0,+\infty)
such that
\mathbf{Er}(g)\geq c
holds for all
g\in\Gamma_{0}(\mathbb{X})
satisfying

{\rm bdry}(\textbf{S}_{f})\cap g^{-1}(0)\not=\emptyset\ \ {\it and}\ \ {\rm Lip}(f-g)<\varepsilon.

(3.12)

Then
(i)
(resp.
(ii)
) holds if (resp. only if) there is
\tau\in(0,+\infty)
such that

\inf\left\{\Big|\inf_{\|h\|=1}d^{+}f(\bar{x},h)\Big|:\bar{x}\in{\rm bdry}(\textbf{S}_{f})\right\}\geq\tau.

(3.13)

Proof.

If part
: Suppose that there is
\tau\in(0,+\infty)
such that (
3.13
) holds.
First if there is some
\bar{x}\in{\rm bdry}(\textbf{S}_{f})
such that
\inf_{\|h\|=1}d^{+}f(\bar{x},h)>0
, using the proof of
Corollary

1
, the lower level set
\textbf{S}_{f}
is a singleton and thus
\textbf{S}_{f}=\{\bar{x}\}
. This means that the conclusion in (ii) follows by using
Theorem

3.2
as well as its proof.

Next, we assume that
\inf_{\|h\|=1}d^{+}f(\bar{x},h)\leq 0
for all
\bar{x}\in{\rm bdry}(\textbf{S}_{f})
. We claim that

\tau\mathbf{d}(x,\textbf{S}_{f})\leq[f(x)]_{+},\ \ \forall x\in\mathbb{X}.

(3.14)

Let
x\in\mathbb{X}\backslash\textbf{S}_{f}
and
\nu>0
sufficiently small. Take
x_{\nu}\in{\rm bdry}(S_{f})
such that

\|x-x_{\nu}\|<(1+\nu)\mathbf{d}(x,\textbf{S}_{f}).

(3.15)

By virtue of
Theorem

3.1
, one has

\mathbf{Er}(f,x_{\nu})=\liminf_{u\rightarrow x_{\nu},u\not\in\textbf{S}_{f}}\Big(-\inf_{\|h\|=1}d^{+}f(u,h)\Big)\geq-\inf_{\|h\|=1}d^{+}f(x_{\nu},h)\geq\tau.

Using the definition of
\mathbf{Er}(f,x_{\nu})
, for
\nu>0
, there is
r>0
such that for any
u\in{\bf B}(x_{\nu},r)\backslash\textbf{S}_{f}
, one has

\frac{f(u)}{\mathbf{d}(u,\textbf{S}_{f})}>\mathbf{Er}(f,x_{\nu})-\nu\geq\tau-\nu.

Note that
x\not\in\textbf{S}_{f}
and
x_{\nu}\in{\rm bdry}(\textbf{S}_{f})
, and thus

\{\lambda x_{\nu}+(1-\lambda)x:\lambda\in(0,1)\}\cap\textbf{S}_{f}=\emptyset.

Then there is
\lambda\in(0,1)
such that
\lambda x_{\nu}+(1-\lambda)x\in B(x_{\nu},r)\backslash\textbf{S}_{f}

and therefore

\displaystyle\tau\leq\frac{f(\lambda x_{\nu}+(1-\lambda)x)}{\mathbf{d}(\lambda x_{\nu}+(1-\lambda)x,\textbf{S}_{f})}+\nu\leq\frac{\lambda f(x_{\nu})+(1-\lambda)f(x)}{\mathbf{d}(x,\textbf{S}_{f})-\|\lambda x_{\nu}+(1-\lambda)x-x\|}+\nu,

and it follows from (
3.15
) that

\displaystyle\tau\leq\frac{(1-\lambda)f(x)}{(1-\lambda(1+\nu))\mathbf{d}(x,\textbf{S}_{f})}\rightarrow\frac{f(x)}{\mathbf{d}(x,\textbf{S}_{f})}\ {\rm as\ }\nu\rightarrow 0^{+}.

This means that (
3.14
) holds.

Let
\varepsilon\in(0,\tau)
and
g\in\Gamma_{0}(\mathbb{X})
satisfying (
3.11
). Take any
x\in\mathbb{X}
such that
g(x)>0
. Then
f(x)>0
(thanks to
\textbf{S}_{f}\subseteq S_{g}
).

We claim that

\inf_{\|h\|=1}d^{+}f(x,h)\leq-\tau.

(3.16)

Granting this, it follows from
{\rm Lip}(f-g)<\varepsilon
in (
3.11
) that

\inf_{\|h\|=1}d^{+}g(x,h)\leq\inf_{\|h\|=1}d^{+}f(x,h)+\varepsilon\leq-(\tau-\varepsilon).

This and
Theorem

3.1
imply that
\mathbf{Er}(g)\geq\tau-\varepsilon
.

It remains to prove (
3.16
). For any
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we can take
z_{n}\in{\rm bdry}(\textbf{S}_{f})
such that

\|x-z_{n}\|<(1+\frac{1}{n})\mathbf{d}(x,\textbf{S}_{f}).

Then (
3.14
) implies that

f(x)\geq\tau\mathbf{d}(x,\textbf{S}_{f})>\frac{n}{n+1}\tau\|x-z_{n}\|.

For any
t\in(0,1)
, one has

f(x+t(z_{n}-x))\leq tf(z_{n})+(1-t)f(x),

and thus

\frac{f(x+t(z_{n}-x))-f(x)}{t}\leq-f(x)\leq-\frac{n}{n+1}\tau\|x-z_{n}\|.

This implies that

\inf_{\|h\|=1}d^{+}f(x,h)\leq d^{+}f\Big(x,\frac{z_{n}-x}{\|x-z_{n}\|}\Big)\leq-\frac{n}{n+1}\tau.

Hence

\inf_{\|h\|=1}d^{+}f(x,h)\leq-\frac{n}{n+1}\tau.

Letting
n\rightarrow\infty
, one has that (
3.16
) holds.

Only if part
: we assume that (ii) holds. Suppose on the contrary that there is a sequence
\{x_{k}\}\subseteq{\rm bdry}(\textbf{S}_{f})
such that

\alpha_{k}:=\inf_{\|h\|=1}d^{+}f(x_{k},h)\rightarrow 0^{-}\ \ {\rm as}\ k\rightarrow\infty.

Let
\varepsilon>0
and take a sufficiently large
k
such that

\frac{3}{2}\alpha_{k}+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}>0.

(3.17)

Note that for any
x\not=x_{k}
, one has

\frac{f(x)-f(x_{k})}{\|x-x_{k}\|}=\frac{f\big(x_{k}+\|x-x_{k}\|\cdot\frac{x-x_{k}}{\|x-x_{k}\|}\big)-f(x_{k})}{\|x-x_{k}\|}\geq d^{+}f\Big(x_{k},\frac{x-x_{k}}{\|x-x_{k}\|}\Big)\geq\alpha_{k},

and consequently

f(x)-\alpha_{k}\|x-x_{k}\|\geq f(x_{k}),\ \ \forall x\in\mathbb{X}.

(3.18)

By the definition of
\alpha_{k}
, there exists
h_{k}\in\mathbb{X}
such that

\|h_{k}\|=1\ \ {\rm and}\ \ d^{+}f(x_{k},h_{k})<\alpha_{k}+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}.

(3.19)

Then one can choose
r_{k}\rightarrow 0^{+}
(as
k\rightarrow\infty
) such that

f(x_{k}+r_{k}h_{k})<f(x_{k})+(\alpha_{k}+\varepsilon)r_{k}.

(3.20)

This and (
3.18
) imply that

f(x_{k}+r_{k}h_{k})-\alpha_{k}\|x_{k}+r_{k}h_{k}-x_{k}\|<\inf_{x\in X}(f(x)-\alpha_{k}\|x-x_{k}\|)+\varepsilon r_{k}.

Applying Ekeland’s variational principle (cf.
[
54
]
), there exists
y_{k}\in\mathbb{X}
such that

\|y_{k}-(x_{k}+r_{k}h_{k})\|<\frac{r_{k}}{2},f(y_{k})-\alpha_{k}\|y_{k}-x_{k}\|\leq f(x_{k}+r_{k}h_{k})-\alpha_{k}r_{k},

(3.21)

and

f(x)-\alpha_{k}\|x-x_{k}\|+2\varepsilon\|x-y_{k}\|>f(y_{k})-\alpha_{k}\|y_{k}-x_{k}\|,\ \ \forall x:x\not=y_{k}.

(3.22)

Then

\|y_{k}-x_{k}\|>r_{k}-\frac{r_{k}}{2}=\frac{r_{k}}{2}\ \ {\rm and}\ \ \|y_{k}-x_{k}\|<r_{k}+\frac{r_{k}}{2}=\frac{3}{2}r_{k},

and thus
y_{k}\not=x_{k}
.

For any
h_{k}
, by the Hahn-Banach theorem, there exists
h^{*}_{k}\in\mathbb{X}^{*}
such that

\|h^{*}_{k}\|=1\ \ {\rm and}\ \ \langle h_{k}^{*},h_{k}\rangle=\|h_{k}\|.

We consider the following function
g
:

g(x):=f(x)+\varepsilon\langle h_{k}^{*},x-x_{k}\rangle\ \ \forall x\in\mathbb{X}.

Then
g\in\Gamma_{0}(\mathbb{X})
. By virtue of (
3.17
), (
3.18
), (
3.19
) and (
3.22
), one has

\displaystyle g(y_{k})=f(y_{k})+\varepsilon\langle h_{k}^{*},y_{k}-x_{k}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f(y_{k})+\varepsilon\langle h_{k}^{*},y_{k}-(x_{k}+r_{k}h_{k})\rangle+\varepsilon r_{k}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\alpha_{k}\|y_{k}-x_{k}\|-\varepsilon\|y_{k}-(x_{k}+r_{k}h_{k})\|+\varepsilon r_{k}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\alpha_{k}\cdot\frac{3}{2}r_{k}+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}r_{k}>0.

We consider two cases:

•

If
\inf_{\|h\|=1}d^{+}g(y_{k},h)\geq 0
, then for any
x\not=y_{k}
, one has

g(x)-g(y_{k})\geq d^{+}g\big(y_{k},\frac{x-y_{k}}{\|x-y_{k}\|}\big)\|x-y_{k}\|\geq\inf_{\|h\|=1}d^{+}g(y_{k},h)\|x-y_{k}\|\geq 0.

This together with
g(y_{k})>0
implies that
S_{g}=\emptyset
and thus
\mathbf{Er}(g)=0
, which contradicts (ii).

•

If
\inf_{\|h\|=1}d^{+}g(y_{k},h)<0
. For any
h\in\mathbb{X}
with
\|h\|=1
and any
t>0
, by (
3.22
), one has

\displaystyle\frac{g(y_{k}+th)-g(y_{k})}{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{f(y_{k}+th)-f(y_{k})}{t}+\varepsilon\langle h_{k}^{*},h\rangle

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{t}\big(\alpha_{k}\|y_{k}+th-x_{k}\|-\alpha_{k}\|y_{k}-x_{k}\|-2\varepsilon\|y_{k}+th-y_{k}\|\big)+\varepsilon\langle h_{k}^{*},h\rangle

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\alpha_{k}-2\varepsilon-\varepsilon,

and consequently

0>\inf_{\|h\|=1}d^{+}g(y_{k},h)\geq\alpha_{k}-2\varepsilon-\varepsilon\geq-4\varepsilon.

Then
Theorem

3.1
gives that
\mathbf{Er}(g)\leq 4\varepsilon
, which contradicts (ii), as
\varepsilon
is arbitrary.

The proof is complete.
\Box

It is noted that the validity of condition (

4
Application to the sensibility analysis of Hoffman’s constants for semi-infinite linear systems

As an application of
Theorem

3.4
, we study sensitivity analysis of Hoffman’s constants for semi-infinite linear systems in a Banach space and aim to establish a primal characterization for the Hoffman’s constants to be uniformly bounded under perturbations on the problem data.

Let
T
be a compact, possibly infinite metric space and
a^{*}:T\rightarrow\mathbb{X}^{*},b:T\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be continuous functions on
T
. We consider now semi-infinite linear systems in
\mathbb{X}
defined by

\langle a^{*}(t),x\rangle\leq\mathbb{B}(t),\ \ {\rm for\ all}\ t\in T.

(4.1)

We denote by
\mathcal{S}_{a^{*},b}
the set of solutions to system (
4.1
). We use the following notations:

\displaystyle f_{a^{*},b}(x):=\max_{t\in T}(\langle a^{*}(t),x\rangle-\mathbb{B}(t)),

\displaystyle J_{a^{*},b}(x):=\{t\in T:\langle a^{*}(t),x\rangle-\mathbb{B}(t))=f_{a^{*},b}(x)\}\ \ {\rm for\ each}\ x\in\mathbb{X}.

It is easy to verify that
J_{a^{*},b}(x)
is a compact subset of
T
for each
x\in\mathbb{X}
.

Recall that
\mathcal{S}_{a^{*},b}
admits a global error bound, if there exists
\sigma>0
such that

\sigma\mathbf{d}(x,\mathcal{S}_{a^{*},b})\leq[f_{a^{*},b}(x)]_{+},\ {\rm for\ all}\ x\in\mathbb{X}.

(4.2)

The Hoffman constant of the semi-infinite linear system (
4.1
), denoted by
\sigma(a^{*},b)
, is given by

\sigma(a^{*},b):=\sup\{\sigma>0:\text{such that }\eqref{4-2a}\ {\rm holds}\}.

(4.3)

One can verify that

\sigma(a^{*},b)=\inf_{x\not\in\mathcal{S}_{a^{*},b}}\frac{f_{a^{*},b}(x)}{\mathbf{d}(x,\mathcal{S}_{a^{*},b})}.

Note that
f_{a^{*},b}(\cdot)
is continuous and thus one can verify that

{\rm bdry}(\mathcal{S}_{a^{*},b})=f_{a^{*},b}^{-1}(0).

(4.4)

The following theorem gives a primal equivalent criteria on the Hoffman’s constant for system (
4.1
) under perturbations on the problem data.

Theorem 4.1

Let
\mathcal{J}:=\{J_{a^{*},b}(x):f_{a^{*},b}(x)=0\}
. Then there exists
\tau>0
such that

\inf\left\{\Big|\inf_{\|h\|=1}\sup_{t\in J}\langle a^{*}(t),h\rangle\Big|:J\in\mathcal{J}\right\}\geq\tau

(4.5)

if and only if there exist
c,\varepsilon>0
such that
\sigma(\widetilde{a}^{*},\widetilde{b})\geq c
holds for all
(\widetilde{a}^{*},\widetilde{b})
defined by

\widetilde{a}^{*}(t):=a^{*}(t)+\varepsilon\widetilde{u}^{*}\ \ {\it and}\ \ \widetilde{\mathbb{B}}(t):=\mathbb{B}(t)+\varepsilon\langle\widetilde{u}^{*},\widetilde{x}\rangle,\ \ \forall(\widetilde{x},\widetilde{u}^{*})\in{\rm bdry}(\mathcal{S}_{a^{*},b})\times\mathbb{B}_{\mathbb{X}^{*}}.

(4.6)

Moreover, the following inequality on Hoffman’s constant
\sigma(a^{*},b)
holds:

\sigma(a^{*},b)\geq\inf\left\{\Big|\inf_{\|h\|=1}\sup_{t\in J}\langle a^{*}(t),h\rangle\Big|:J\in\mathcal{J}\right\}.

(4.7)

Proof.
By virtue of
Theorem

3.4
, we only need to show that
f_{a^{*},b}
and
\tau
satisfy assumption (
3.23
) in
Theorem

3.4
.

Indeed, for each
x\in{\rm bdry}(\mathcal{S}_{a^{*},b})
, one has that
J_{a^{*},b}(x)\in\mathcal{J}
and it follows from

(or

\inf_{\|h\|=1}d^{+}f_{a^{*},b}(x,h)=\inf_{\|h\|=1}\sup_{t\in J_{a^{*},b}(x)}\langle a^{*}(t),h\rangle.

This and (

As an application of
Theorem

4.1
, we give one examples to verify the uniform bound of the Hoffman constant for the given linear system.

Example 1

Consider the following linear system (
4.1
), where
\mathbb{X}:=\mathbb{R}^{2}
,
T:=\{1,2,3\}
, and
a^{*}:T\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{2},b:T\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
are defined as follows:

a^{*}(1):=(1,1),a^{*}(2):=(-2,1),a^{*}(3):=(1,-2),\mathbb{B}(1):=1,\mathbb{B}(2):=2,\mathbb{B}(3):=2.

Then one can verify that

\mathcal{J}=\left\{\{1\},\{2\},\{3\},\{1,2\},\{2,3\},\{1,3\}\right\}.

Solve the following six min-max optimization problems:

OP(J)\ \ \ \ \min_{\|h\|=1}\max_{t\in J_{k}}\langle a^{*}(t),h\rangle,J\in\mathcal{J}.

(4.8)

Then one has

\min\left\{\Big|\min_{\|h\|=1}\max_{t\in J}\langle a^{*}(t),h\rangle\Big|:J\in\mathcal{J}\right\}=\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}>0,

and then
Theorem

4.1
implies that the Hoffman constant (
\sigma(\cdot)
) for system (
4.1
) is uniformly bounded under perturbations on the problem data
(a^{*},b)
.

Finally, we show that the uniform bound of the Hoffman constant may not be satisfied if the condition (
4.5
) is violated in
Theorem

4.1
.

Example 2

Consider the following linear system (
4.1
), where
\mathbb{X}:=\mathbb{R}^{2}
,
T:=\{1,2\}
, and
a^{*}:T\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{2},b:T\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
are defined as follows:

a^{*}(1):=(1,1),a^{*}(2):=(-1,-1),\mathbb{B}(1)=\mathbb{B}(2):=0.

Then
\mathcal{S}(a^{*},b)=\{x=(x_{1},x_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:x_{1}+x_{2}=0\}
. Note that

J_{a^{*},b}(0)=T,\min_{\|h\|=1}\max_{t\in T}\langle a^{*}(t),h\rangle=0

and thus condition (
4.5
) is violated.

We next show that the Hoffman constant for system (
4.1
) is not uniformly bounded under perturbations on the problem data
(a^{*},b)
.

Indeed, for any
\varepsilon>0
, select
u_{\varepsilon}^{*}:=(0,\varepsilon)
,
x_{\varepsilon}:=(0,0)
and define
a_{\varepsilon}^{*}(t):=a^{*}(t)+u_{\varepsilon}^{*},b_{\varepsilon}(t):=\mathbb{B}(t)
for
t=1,2
. Then
f_{a_{\varepsilon}^{*},b_{\varepsilon}}(x):=\max\{x_{1}+x_{2}+\varepsilon x_{2},-x_{1}-x_{2}+\varepsilon x_{2}\}
for all
x=(x_{1},x_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
and

\mathcal{S}(a_{\varepsilon}^{*},b_{\varepsilon})=\{x=(x_{1},x_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:x_{1}+(1+\varepsilon)x_{2}\leq 0,x_{1}+(1-\varepsilon)x_{2}\geq 0\}.

Take
u_{\varepsilon}:=(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)
and thus

\frac{f_{a_{\varepsilon}^{*},b_{\varepsilon}}(u_{\varepsilon})}{\mathbf{d}(u_{\varepsilon},\mathcal{S}(a_{\varepsilon}^{*},b_{\varepsilon}))}=\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{\sqrt{2}\varepsilon}=\frac{\varepsilon}{\sqrt{2}},

which implies that
\sigma(a_{\varepsilon}^{*},b_{\varepsilon})<\varepsilon
.

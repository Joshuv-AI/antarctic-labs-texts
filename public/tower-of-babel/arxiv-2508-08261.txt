Fixed Point Theorems for Multivalued Weak Contractions in Cone Metric Spaces
Elvin Rada

Abstract
. This article presents a deep investigation of fixed points for multivalued weak contractions in cone metric spaces. We extend Berinde’s weak contraction principles to the multivalued setting in cone metric spaces, developing existence, uniqueness, and convergence results. Using the structure of normal cones in Banach spaces, we prove generalized fixed-point theorems for set-valued mappings satisfying weak contractive conditions. The theoretical framework includes iterative approximation schemes with explicit convergence rates and stability analysis of fixed point sets. In the end, some applications to differential inclusions and variational inequalities demonstrate the utility of our results in nonlinear analysis.

1.
Introduction

Fixed point theory for multivalued mappings is a very important part of nonlinear analysis with deep implications across diverse mathematical disciplines. The start of this theory can be traced to Nadler’s 1969 extension
[
1
]
of Banach’s contraction principle to set-valued mappings using Hausdorff distance. This innovation opened opportunities for applications in optimization theory, mathematical economics, and differential inclusions where set-valued operators naturally arise.

The structure of fixed point theory had another big development with Huang and Zhang’s introduction of cone metric spaces in 2007
[
3
]
. By replacing real-valued distances with vector-valued metrics taking values in ordered Banach space cones, this framework provided a powerful tool for studying problems with inherent order structures. Cone metrics enable unified treatment of various distance concepts, including Lorentzian metrics in relativity and vector-valued norms in functional analysis.

A very important advancement in contraction theory came from Berinde
[
2
]
through weak contraction mappings. For single-valued operators, Berinde’s theorem establishes that a self-map
T
on a complete metric space
(X,d)
satisfying:

(1)

d(Tx,Ty)\leq\delta d(x,y)+Ld(y,Tx)\quad\text{for }\delta\in(0,1),L\geq 0

admits fixed points. This weak contraction condition is more general than standard contractions as it incorporates additive error terms, significantly expanding the class of mappable operators. Berinde’s work bridged the gap between strict contractions and nonexpansive mappings, yielding convergence results for Picard iterations with error estimates.

The confluence of these three developments remains largely unexplored. This article addresses this gap by developing a comprehensive fixed point theory for multivalued weak contractions in cone metric spaces. Our work draws inspiration from several sources:

•

The cone metric framework established by Huang and Zhang
[
3
]

•

Berinde’s weak contraction principles
[
2
]

•

Multivalued contraction theory pioneered by Nadler
[
1
]

•

Our earlier results on fixed points in cone Banach spaces
[
4
]

Our principal contributions are:

(1)

A direct multivalued extension of Berinde’s theorem in cone metric spaces (Theorem
3.1
)

(2)

\lambda
-iterative schemes with explicit convergence rates (Theorem
3.4
)

(3)

Stability analysis of fixed point sets under perturbations (Theorem
3.6
)

(4)

Uniqueness criteria under strengthened contractive conditions (Proposition
3.3
)

(5)

Applications to differential inclusions (Theorem
4.1
) and variational inequalities (Theorem
4.2
)

2.
Preliminaries

We establish fundamental concepts of cone metric spaces and weak contractions with proper historical context and citations. Throughout,
E
denotes a real Banach space,
P\subset E
a closed convex cone with
\operatorname{int}(P)\neq\emptyset
, and
X
a nonempty set. The partial order
\preceq
induced by
P
is defined as
x\preceq y
iff
y-x\in P
.

Definition 2.1

(Cone Metric (
[
3
, Definition 2.1]
))
.

A
cone metric
is a mapping
d:X\times X\to P
satisfying:

(i)

d(x,y)=0\Leftrightarrow x=y

(ii)

d(x,y)=d(y,x)

(iii)

d(x,z)\preceq d(x,y)+d(y,z)

The pair
(X,d)
is a
cone metric space
. When
E
is a Banach lattice and
P
its positive cone, this structure generalizes standard metric spaces.

Remark 2.2
.

The cone metric framework extends beyond traditional metrics. Key examples include:

•

Lorentzian metrics in general relativity
[
5
]

•

Vector-valued norms in functional analysis

•

Ordered Banach spaces with lattice structure

Definition 2.3

(Normal Cone (
[
3
, Definition 2.1]
))
.

Cone
P
is
normal
if there exists
\kappa>0
such that:

0\preceq a\preceq b\implies\|a\|\leq\kappa\|b\|

The minimal such
\kappa
is the
normality coefficient
. Normality ensures compatibility between the order and norm topologies.

Example 2.4
.

Standard examples of normal cones include:

•

P=\mathbb{R}_{+}
in
\mathbb{R}
with
\kappa=1

•

Nonnegative functions in
L^{p}
spaces (
1\leq p<\infty
)

•

Positive semidefinite matrices in operator algebras

Definition 2.5

(Hausdorff Cone Metric (
[
1
, Section 4]
))
.

For nonempty closed subsets
A,B\subset X
, the
Hausdorff cone metric
is:

H_{d}(A,B)=\inf\left\{r\in P:\begin{array}[]{c}\forall a\in A,\exists b\in B:d(a,b)\preceq r\\
\forall b\in B,\exists a\in A:d(b,a)\preceq r\end{array}\right\}

This extends Nadler’s Hausdorff metric to the cone setting.

Definition 2.6

(Berinde’s Weak Contraction (
[
2
, Definition 2.1]
))
.

A single-valued mapping
T:X\to X
is a
weak contraction
if there exist
\delta\in(0,1)
and
L\geq 0
such that:

d(Tx,Ty)\preceq\delta d(x,y)+Ld(y,Tx)

Definition 2.7

(Multivalued Weak Contraction)
.

A multivalued map
T:X\to\mathscr{C}(X)
(nonempty closed subsets) is a
weak contraction
if:

H_{d}(Tx,Ty)\preceq\delta d(x,y)+L\inf_{z\in Ty}d(x,z)

This generalizes Berinde’s condition to set-valued operators.

Lemma 2.8

(Selection Principle (
[
1
, Lemma 3.2]
))
.

For any
x\in X
and
y\in Tx
, there exists
z\in Ty
such that:

d(y,z)\preceq H_{d}(Tx,Ty)+\epsilon\quad\forall\epsilon\succ 0

Theorem 2.9

(Cone Contraction Principle (
[
3
, Theorem 1]
))
.

Every contraction
T:X\to X
on a complete cone metric space has a unique fixed point.

Theorem 2.10

(Nadler’s Theorem in Cones (
[
4
, Theorem 4.1]
))
.

Let
T:X\to\mathscr{C}(X)
satisfy
H_{d}(Tx,Ty)\preceq kd(x,y)
with
k\in(0,1)
. Then
T
has a fixed point.

3.
Main Results

In this section we have our results for this article.

3.1.
Extension of Berinde’s Theorem

We present the multivalued extension of Berinde’s theorem in cone metric spaces:

Theorem 3.1

(Multivalued Berinde-Type Theorem)
.

Let
(X,d)
be a complete cone metric space with normal cone
P
(coefficient
\kappa
), and
T:X\to\mathscr{C}(X)
a multivalued weak contraction. If
\delta\kappa<1
and
L\kappa<1-\delta\kappa
, then
T
has a fixed point.

Proof.

Fix
x_{0}\in X
and choose
x_{1}\in Tx_{0}
. Using Lemma
2.8
, inductively construct
\{x_{n}\}
such that
x_{n+1}\in Tx_{n}
satisfies:

d(x_{n+1},x_{n})\preceq H_{d}(Tx_{n},Tx_{n-1})+\epsilon_{n}(1/2)^{n}

for some sequence
\epsilon_{n}\succ 0
. By the weak contraction property:

\displaystyle d(x_{n+1},x_{n})

\displaystyle\preceq\delta d(x_{n},x_{n-1})+L\inf_{z\in Tx_{n}}d(x_{n-1},z)+\epsilon_{n}(1/2)^{n}

\displaystyle\preceq\delta d(x_{n},x_{n-1})+Ld(x_{n-1},x_{n})+\epsilon_{n}(1/2)^{n}

\displaystyle=(\delta+L)d(x_{n},x_{n-1})+\epsilon_{n}(1/2)^{n}

By normality and induction:

\displaystyle\|d(x_{n+1},x_{n})\|

\displaystyle\leq\kappa\|(\delta+L)d(x_{n},x_{n-1})+\epsilon_{n}(1/2)^{n}\|

\displaystyle\leq\kappa(\delta+L)\|d(x_{n},x_{n-1})\|+\kappa\|\epsilon_{n}\|(1/2)^{n}

\displaystyle\leq\kappa^{2}(\delta+L)^{n}\|d(x_{1},x_{0})\|+\kappa\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}(\delta+L)^{k}\|\epsilon_{n-k}\|(1/2)^{n-k}

Since
\delta+L<1/\kappa
by assumption,
\{x_{n}\}
is Cauchy. By completeness,
x_{n}\to x^{*}
. To prove
x^{*}\in Tx^{*}
:

\displaystyle d(x^{*},Tx^{*})

\displaystyle\preceq d(x^{*},x_{n+1})+d(x_{n+1},Tx^{*})

\displaystyle\preceq d(x^{*},x_{n+1})+H_{d}(Tx_{n},Tx^{*})

\displaystyle\preceq d(x^{*},x_{n+1})+\delta d(x_{n},x^{*})+L\inf_{z\in Tx^{*}}d(x_{n},z)

\displaystyle\preceq d(x^{*},x_{n+1})+\delta d(x_{n},x^{*})+Ld(x_{n},x^{*})

As
n\to\infty
, the right-hand side converges to
0
in norm, so
d(x^{*},Tx^{*})=0
, implying
x^{*}\in Tx^{*}
.
∎

Remark 3.2
.

When
T
is single-valued, Theorem
3.1
reduces exactly to Berinde’s original result
[
2
, Theorem 2.2]
in cone metric spaces.

Proposition 3.3

(Uniqueness Criterion)
.

Under Theorem
3.1
assumptions, if for all
x\neq y
:

H_{d}(Tx,Ty)\prec d(x,y)-\varphi(d(x,y))

where
\varphi:P\to P
is continuous with
\varphi^{-1}(0)=\{0\}
, then the fixed point is unique.

Proof.

Suppose
x^{*},y^{*}
are distinct fixed points. Then:

\displaystyle d(x^{*},y^{*})

\displaystyle\preceq H_{d}(Tx^{*},Ty^{*})

\displaystyle\prec d(x^{*},y^{*})-\varphi(d(x^{*},y^{*}))

which implies
\varphi(d(x^{*},y^{*}))\prec 0
, contradicting
\varphi(p)\succeq 0
for all
p\in P
. Thus
x^{*}=y^{*}
.
∎

3.2.
Iterative Approximation

We extend Berinde’s iterative scheme to the multivalued case:

Theorem 3.4

(
\lambda
-Iterative Scheme)
.

Let
T
satisfy Theorem
3.1
conditions. Define the sequence:

x_{n+1}=\frac{1}{\lambda+1}x_{n}+\frac{\lambda}{\lambda+1}f(x_{n}),\quad f(x_{n})\in Tx_{n}

for
\lambda>0
. If
\sigma=\kappa\left(\frac{\delta+L+\lambda}{1+\lambda}\right)<1
, then
\{x_{n}\}
converges to a fixed point
x^{*}
with error estimate:

\|d(x_{n},x^{*})\|\leq\frac{\kappa\sigma^{n}}{1-\sigma}\|d(x_{0},x_{1})\|

Proof.

Let
y_{n}=f(x_{n})\in Tx_{n}
. Consider:

\displaystyle d(x_{n+1},x_{n})

\displaystyle=d\left(\frac{x_{n}+\lambda y_{n}}{\lambda+1},\frac{x_{n-1}+\lambda y_{n-1}}{\lambda+1}\right)

\displaystyle\preceq\frac{1}{\lambda+1}d(x_{n},x_{n-1})+\frac{\lambda}{\lambda+1}d(y_{n},y_{n-1})

\displaystyle\preceq\frac{1}{\lambda+1}d(x_{n},x_{n-1})+\frac{\lambda}{\lambda+1}H_{d}(Tx_{n},Tx_{n-1})

\displaystyle\preceq\frac{1}{\lambda+1}d(x_{n},x_{n-1})+\frac{\lambda}{\lambda+1}\left[\delta d(x_{n},x_{n-1})+Ld(x_{n-1},y_{n})\right]

Now estimate
d(x_{n-1},y_{n})
:

d(x_{n-1},y_{n})\preceq d(x_{n-1},x_{n})+d(x_{n},y_{n})

Thus:

\displaystyle d(x_{n+1},x_{n})

\displaystyle\preceq\frac{1}{\lambda+1}d(x_{n},x_{n-1})+\frac{\lambda}{\lambda+1}\delta d(x_{n},x_{n-1})+\frac{\lambda L}{\lambda+1}[d(x_{n-1},x_{n})+d(x_{n},y_{n})]

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1}{\lambda+1}+\frac{\lambda\delta}{\lambda+1}+\frac{\lambda L}{\lambda+1}\right)d(x_{n},x_{n-1})+\frac{\lambda L}{\lambda+1}d(x_{n},y_{n})

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1+\lambda(\delta+L)}{\lambda+1}\right)d(x_{n},x_{n-1})+\frac{\lambda L}{\lambda+1}d(x_{n},y_{n})

By normality:

\|d(x_{n+1},x_{n})\|\leq\kappa\left(\frac{1+\lambda(\delta+L)}{\lambda+1}\right)\|d(x_{n},x_{n-1})\|+\kappa\frac{\lambda L}{\lambda+1}\|d(x_{n},y_{n})\|

Since
\sigma<1
and
\|d(x_{n},y_{n})\|
is bounded, geometric convergence follows. The error estimate comes from summing the series.
∎

Corollary 3.5
.

For
\lambda=1
, we obtain the Krasnoselskii-type iteration:

x_{n+1}=\frac{1}{2}(x_{n}+f(x_{n}))

with convergence rate
\sigma=\kappa(\delta+L+1)/2
.

3.3.
Stability and Continuity

Theorem 3.6

(Stability of Fixed Points)
.

Let
\{T_{n}\}
be a sequence of multivalued weak contractions converging uniformly to
T
on compact subsets of
X
. Then:

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\operatorname{Fix}(T_{n})\subseteq\operatorname{Fix}(T)

If all
T_{n}
satisfy the uniqueness condition in Proposition
3.3
, then
\operatorname{Fix}(T_{n})\to\operatorname{Fix}(T)
in Hausdorff metric.

Proof.

Let
x_{n}^{*}\in\operatorname{Fix}(T_{n})
with
x_{n}^{*}\to x^{*}
. Then:

\displaystyle d(x^{*},Tx^{*})

\displaystyle\preceq d(x^{*},x_{n}^{*})+d(x_{n}^{*},T_{n}x_{n}^{*})+H_{d}(T_{n}x_{n}^{*},Tx_{n}^{*})+H_{d}(Tx_{n}^{*},Tx^{*})

\displaystyle\preceq d(x^{*},x_{n}^{*})+0+\sup_{y\in K}H_{d}(T_{n}y,Ty)+\delta d(x_{n}^{*},x^{*})+L\inf_{z\in Tx^{*}}d(x_{n}^{*},z)

where
K
is a compact set containing
\{x_{n}^{*}\}
. By uniform convergence on
K
,
\sup_{y\in K}H_{d}(T_{n}y,Ty)\to 0
. Thus
d(x^{*},Tx^{*})=0
, so
x^{*}\in\operatorname{Fix}(T)
.

For Hausdorff convergence under uniqueness, consider:

\displaystyle H_{d}(

\displaystyle\operatorname{Fix}(T_{n}),\operatorname{Fix}(T))

\displaystyle\leq H_{d}(\operatorname{Fix}(T_{n}),\{x^{*}\})+H_{d}(\{x^{*}\},\operatorname{Fix}(T))\to 0\quad\text{as }n\to\infty

since both fixed points are unique and
x_{n}^{*}\to x^{*}
.
∎

4.
Applications

Now we will see some applications to nonlinear analysis, and especially in differential inclusions and

4.1.
Differential Inclusions

Consider the initial value problem for differential inclusions:

(2)

\dot{x}(t)\in F(t,x(t)),\quad x(0)=x_{0},\quad t\in[0,T]

where
F:[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathscr{K}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is a set-valued map with nonempty compact convex values.

Theorem 4.1
.

Assume
F
satisfies:

(1)

t\mapsto F(t,x)
is measurable for each
x

(2)

x\mapsto F(t,x)
is upper semicontinuous

(3)

Weak contraction:
H_{d}(F(t,x),F(t,y))\preceq\delta(t)d(x,y)+L(t)d(y,F(t,x))

(4)

\delta,L\in L^{1}[0,T]
with
\|\delta\|_{L^{1}}\kappa<1

Then (
2
) has a solution
x\in AC([0,T],\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Proof.

Consider the solution operator
\mathcal{F}:C([0,T],\mathbb{R}^{n})\to\mathscr{C}(C([0,T],\mathbb{R}^{n}))
:

\mathcal{F}(x)=\left\{y\in AC[0,T]:y(t)=x_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}g(s)ds,\ g(s)\in F(s,x(s))\text{ a.e.}\right\}

Equip
C([0,T],\mathbb{R}^{n})
with the cone metric:

d(x,y)(t)=e^{-\lambda t}\|x(t)-y(t)\|_{\infty}\cdot\mathbf{1}

where
\mathbf{1}
is the unit vector in
P=C_{+}([0,T])
. This cone is normal with
\kappa=e^{\lambda T}
.

For
y_{1}\in\mathcal{F}(x_{1}),y_{2}\in\mathcal{F}(x_{2})
:

\displaystyle\|y_{1}(t)-y_{2}(t)\|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{t}H_{\|\cdot\|}(F(s,x_{1}(s)),F(s,x_{2}(s)))ds

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{t}\left[\delta(s)\|x_{1}(s)-x_{2}(s)\|+L(s)d(x_{2}(s),F(s,x_{1}(s)))\right]ds

Multiply by
e^{-\lambda t}
:

\displaystyle e^{-\lambda t}\|y_{1}(t)-y_{2}(t)\|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\lambda(t-s)}e^{-\lambda s}\left[\delta(s)\|x_{1}(s)-x_{2}(s)\|+L(s)\|x_{2}(s)-g_{1}(s)\|\right]ds

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\lambda}\left(\|\delta\|_{\infty}+\|L\|_{\infty}\right)d(x_{1},x_{2})(t)

Thus
H_{d}(\mathcal{F}(x_{1}),\mathcal{F}(x_{2}))\preceq\sigma d(x_{1},x_{2})
with
\sigma<1
for large
\lambda
. Apply Theorem
3.1
.
∎

4.2.
Multivalued Variational Inequalities

Let
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a closed convex set. The
multivalued variational inequality
(MVI) seeks
x^{*}\in K
and
f^{*}\in F(x^{*})
such that:

(3)

\langle f^{*},y-x^{*}\rangle\geq 0\quad\forall y\in K

Theorem 4.2
.

Assume:

(1)

F:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathscr{K}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is
(\delta,L)
-weakly contractive

(2)

Strong monotonicity:
\langle f_{x}-f_{y},x-y\rangle\geq\mu\|x-y\|^{2}
for
f_{x}\in F(x),f_{y}\in F(y)

(3)

\mu>\kappa(\delta+L)

Then MVI (
3
) has a unique solution
x^{*}
, and the proximal iteration:

x_{n+1}=\operatorname{proj}_{K}(x_{n}-\lambda f_{n}),\quad f_{n}\in F(x_{n})

converges geometrically to
x^{*}
for appropriate
\lambda>0
.

Proof.

First, under strong monotonicity, solutions are unique. Define
T:K\to\mathscr{C}(K)
by:

T(x)=\operatorname{proj}_{K}(x-\lambda F(x))

For
x_{1},x_{2}\in K
:

\displaystyle\|T

\displaystyle x_{1}-Tx_{2}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|(x_{1}-\lambda f_{1})-(x_{2}-\lambda f_{2})\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\|x_{1}-x_{2}\|^{2}-2\lambda\langle f_{1}-f_{2},x_{1}-x_{2}\rangle+\lambda^{2}\|f_{1}-f_{2}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|x_{1}-x_{2}\|^{2}-2\lambda\mu\|x_{1}-x_{2}\|^{2}+\lambda^{2}H_{\|\cdot\|}(F(x_{1}),F(x_{2}))^{2}

Using the weak contraction property:

H_{\|\cdot\|}(F(x_{1}),F(x_{2}))\leq\delta\|x_{1}-x_{2}\|+L\inf_{f_{2}\in F(x_{2})}\|x_{1}-f_{2}\|

Thus:

\|Tx_{1}-Tx_{2}\|\leq\sqrt{1-2\lambda\mu+\lambda^{2}(\delta+L)^{2}}\|x_{1}-x_{2}\|

Choose
\lambda
such that
\sigma=\sqrt{1-2\lambda\mu+\lambda^{2}(\delta+L)^{2}}<1
. Apply Theorem
3.4
.
∎

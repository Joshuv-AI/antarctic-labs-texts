Filtering Geriatric Patients by Age in TriNetX
Authors: Joshua Wang
Published: 2026

I read Nocera and colleagues’ recently published TriNetX-based retrospective study examining extracapsular hip fractures in patients aged ≥ 65 years. 1 However, mistakes in age filtering in TriNetX-based studies have previously been documented in pediatric studies. 2 In this letter, I outline how Nocera and colleagues could more optimally construct a geriatric population through the TriNetX platform, while providing advice to geriatric medicine researchers on how to optimally construct geriatric cohorts using TriNetX.

Nocera and colleagues state that patients “≥ 65 years undergoing extracapsular hip fracture surgery” between 2015-2025 were included in the study. However, when the patients were aged 65 years or over was not specified. TriNetX has a universal age filter option in its query builder, which filters patients on the basis of their age on the date the study was conducted. Using this current age filter, I replicated Nocera and colleagues’ acute kidney injury (AKI) cohort, producing a cohort of 1,321 patients with a mean age at index surgery of 81.8 ± 8.18 years; this cohort is similar in size (n=1,414) and age at surgery (81.74 ± 8.09) to the published cohort. However, this filter is not optimal, as patients who undergo extracapsular hip fracture surgery while under the age of 65 years earlier in the study period may be erroneously included on the basis of their current age. For example, a patient who was 55 years old and underwent surgery in 2015 would be over the age of 65 when the study was conducted and therefore included despite not being a geriatric patient at surgery.

The more optimal method of age filtering in TriNetX is to use age filters on the specific index events at cohort construction. In this case, the study should only include patients aged ≥ 65 years at the time of their surgery. When I replicated the AKI cohort using this ‘age at surgery’ filter, only 718 patients met the criteria for inclusion. This suggests that approximately 46% of the patients in Nocera and colleagues’ AKI cohort were not over the age of 65 at the time of surgery. Age filtering should be performed using an age at index filter rather than a universal current age filter to ensure that all patients are of the desired age when they encounter the clinical event under investigation.

I hope these comments assist readers with interpreting Nocera and colleagues’ study, while also providing useful information for geriatric medicine researchers hoping to utilise TriNetX in the future.

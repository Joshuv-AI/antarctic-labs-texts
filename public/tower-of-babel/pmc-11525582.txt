Effects of train speed and passenger capacity on the environmental vibration of viaduct and surrounding soil of suburban railways
Authors: Hongkai Wang, Dong Li, Jili Yin, Zhimin Li, Zhoujian You, Futong Wang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractCompared with the general urban rail transit, some of the current rapid urban rail transit can reach a maximum speed of 140 km/h when the train is running, which exceeds the speed level of the general urban rail transit. To consider the influence of different speeds and loads on the vibration of viaducts and the surrounding soil environment, this paper establishes a three-dimensional finite element model of rail-viaduct soil. The results show that: the frequency domain acceleration of each measurement point increases with the increase of train speed; except for the measurement point which is 20 m away from the centerline of the track, the frequency domain speed of each measurement point increases with the increase of train speed; the frequency domain speed under the low-frequency component of the viaduct measurement point increases with the increase of train loading, and the growth rate is obviously larger than that of the middle and high frequency bands, and the frequency domain speed of the measurement points in the site also increases with the increase of train loading, and the growth rate of the low-frequency band is obviously larger than that of the middle and high frequency bands. The frequency domain velocity at each measurement point of the site also increases with the increase of train load, and the growth rate of the low-frequency band is obviously larger than that of the middle and high-frequency bands.Keywords: Rail transit, Environmental vibration, Train speed, Passenger capacitySubject terms: Engineering, Civil engineering

Introduction

Currently, China is in a period of rapid development of urban rail transit. Data released by the Ministry of Transport of the People’s Republic of China show that, as of March 2024, a total of 55 cities in 31 provinces (autonomous regions and municipalities directly under the Central Government) and the Xinjiang Production and Construction Corps had opened and operated 310 urban rail transit lines, with an operating mileage of 10,248 km. However, intensive vehicle operation can disrupt the daily production and life of residents along the routes, and even lead to damage to old buildings and the inability of vibration-sensitive equipment to function properly1–3. Environmental vibration refers to mechanical vibrations generated by external sources that propagate into the surrounding environment, affecting buildings and human activities. With the continuous development of urban rail transit in my country, the research on urban rail environmental vibration is no longer limited to the analysis of measured data, but combines field measurements with numerical simulations to verify the accuracy of finite element models through measured data, thereby further studying the impact of vibration on the structure and surrounding soil.

Zhang Yijing4 established a three-dimensional model of a tunnel-soil layer by finite element software and simulated the ground vibration response caused by rail transit at different distances. The results show that the effective value of the horizontal vibration acceleration of the ground caused by rail transit in the circular curve section is greater than the effective value of the vertical vibration acceleration within a certain range from the center line of the tunnel; there is a vibration amplification zone in the vertical direction. Yu Kehui5 analyzed the vibration characteristics of the vehicle-bridge system of a concrete continuous rigid frame bridge from two aspects: numerical simulation and field measurement. The research results show that the vibration index of the train and the bridge is less than the standard limit, the safety and riding comfort of the train operation are good, and the bridge structure has good lateral and vertical stiffness and dynamic performance. Aiello6 conducted experimental measurement and numerical prediction of the vibration generated by the subway. Yaseri7 studied the application of the finite element method in ground vibration caused by the subway. Xia8 extended the finite element method to the study of ground vibration caused by subway trains and its propagation characteristics. The results show that there is a vibration amplification zone at a certain distance from the center of the tunnel, and the dominant frequency of the surface vibration is concentrated in a certain range. Zhang Chenchen9 established a 31-degree-of-freedom subway vehicle model and a bridge-soil-building three-dimensional finite element model for numerical research. The results show that the propagation of subway environmental vibration on the ground has obvious directionality, and vertical vibration is dominant; high-frequency vibrations above 10 Hz decay rapidly during propagation, and environmental vibrations beyond 30 m from the track center are mainly low-frequency vibrations. Song Fuchun10 conducted transient analysis on a three-dimensional finite element model at different vehicle speeds. The results showed that the elevated structure had a certain attenuation effect on the vibration caused by the train, and the vertical vibration acceleration at the bottom of the pier was about 1/100 of that at the mid-span. Chen Jianguo11 established two subsystem models, vehicle-bridge and pier-soil, and studied the influence of various factors on environmental vibration through parameter analysis. The results showed that the environmental vibration caused by elevated rail transit gradually decreased with the increase of the distance from the vibration source. Fang Jian12 used the finite element method and field tests to analyze the vibration characteristics of the urban elevated box girder structure. The results showed that the vibration of the box girder structure under the action of train passenger capacity was mainly concentrated in the frequency range of 40 to 200 Hz; the box girder vibration caused by wheel-rail short-wave irregularity was mainly concentrated above 50 Hz, with local vibration of the box girder as the main manifestation.

The theoretical analysis methods for studying the environmental vibration of urban rail transit mainly involve aspects such as vibration theory, structural dynamics, and mathematical modeling. Vibration problems are studied through model construction and mathematical analysis. Zhang Bolin13 analyzed the vibration wave propagation mechanism of the ground line based on the composite regression method of vibration attenuation. The results show that the vibration propagation caused by the ground line is mainly surface waves, which gradually decay with the increase of the distance from the line; the ground vibration has certain periodic characteristics, and its excitation frequency corresponds to the frequency caused by the excitation of the sleeper spacing and the frequency of the wheel-rail coupling effect. Xie Weiping14 conducted a vehicle-train-bridge coupled vibration analysis on a “road-rail integrated” viaduct. The results show that when designing the strength of the bridge, the coupling effect of the upper road part and the lower track part can be ignored; when the train speed is 60 km/h, the influence of track irregularity on the mid-span displacement of the bridge is very small. Krylov15 used Green’s function to explain the theory of low-frequency ground vibration generated by a freely moving train within the framework of the quasi-static wheel pressure effect. The results show that the spectrum depends largely on the mechanical properties of the soil, the axle load of the carriage, and the geometric parameters of the track and train. To study the characteristics of the excitation source of the Beijing subway, Pan Changshi, and Xie Zhengguang16 conducted vibration tests on the section tunnel in the 1990s. Subsequently, research institutions such as Beijing Jiaotong University17–19, Harbin Institute of Technology20–22, and the University of Leuven in Belgium23–28 have conducted a large number of vibration source tests and analyses. In terms of vehicle-track coupling simulation calculation of vibration sources, Zhai Wanming29, Sheng et al.30–33, Lei Xiaoyan34, Liu Weining35, Zhang Nan36, and Wang37,38 have all established vehicle-track coupling models to obtain train load excitation in the model, and have conducted relevant research on rail transit environmental vibration.

Zhou et al.39 used a 3D finite element coupled train-track-tunnel-foundation-soil-basement-drum tower model to study the dynamic response of the Nanjing Drum Tower affected by the operation of Metro Line 4. However, their study only considered the effect when the train operating speed was lower than 80 km/h. Ding et al.40 analyzed the traveling speed of the subway on the foundation as well as the track vibration based on a 2.5D finite element model. However, their study was limited to train speeds below 80 km/h and only briefly analyzed the vertical velocity attenuation at different speeds. Xu41 established a dynamic finite element model of the “subway-tunnel-soil” system in their study, aiming to investigate the effects of various factors on the ground vibration induced by the subway. However, it is worth noting that in their studies, the values of the train speed parameters were lower than 80 km/h, and only the effects of various factors on the time course of acceleration were considered, with no frequency domain characterization involved. Compared with ordinary urban rail transit, some rapid urban rail transit can reach a maximum speed of 140 km/h when the train is running, which exceeds the speed level of ordinary urban rail transit. The environmental vibration intensity and frequency range of rail transit are closely related to the speed of the vehicle. The understanding of the laws of environmental vibration of ordinary urban rail cannot be applied to rapid urban rail transit. Therefore, this paper analyzes the vibration induced by rapid urban rail transit by studying train speeds up to 140 km/h and different passenger loads to analyze the differences in vibration amplitude and vibration frequency components within the viaduct and surrounding site from general urban rail transit.

Establishment of the model

Considering the influence of different vehicle speeds and passenger capacities on the environmental vibration of the viaduct and the surrounding site, this paper establishes a three-dimensional dynamic finite element model of the viaduct section. The spatial calculation domain of the finite element model of the viaduct of Taizhou Urban Railway S1 Line is 220 m long along the track direction (Y direction), 60 m long in the horizontal direction perpendicular to the track (X direction), and 45 m long in the vertical downward direction (Z direction), as shown in Fig. 1.

Boundary conditions and radiation characteristics of viaduct model

In the finite element model, a symmetric model is usually used to reduce the amount of calculation. However, considering that the viaduct section is adjacent to the bidirectional viaduct, the symmetric model is no longer applicable. Therefore, a full model is established in the viaduct section to simulate the vibration of the bidirectional viaduct and the surrounding site caused by trains in actual situations.

In actual situations, soil can be infinitely extended. However, when applying finite elements, the soil within a certain range is artificially selected, which leads to artificial model boundaries in the front, back, left, right, and bottom of the soil. The existence of these model boundaries will cause vibration reflection, which is inconsistent with the actual situation. To eliminate this false reflection, we use viscoelastic artificial boundaries around and at the bottom of the model. The surface boundary is real, so there is no need to set boundary conditions.

Since the pulse load has a wider frequency band, it can verify the reliability of the model more perfectly compared with the simple harmonic load. Therefore, a unit pulse load is applied to the top center of the model to obtain the acceleration pulse response diagram of the applied point, as shown in Fig. 4. Figure 4 shows that the displacement of the viaduct measurement point and the site measurement point in the second half of the image is basically zero, which means that the selected boundary conditions meet the model requirements and the boundary has almost no interference with the model calculation.

As the passenger load increases, the overall mass of the train increases and the bridge will be subjected to greater vertical loads. The results show that the acceleration and velocity in the low-frequency band (1 Hz to 12 Hz) increase significantly when the train load is increased, especially at the measurement points P2, P3, and P4, which suggests that viaducts need to be designed with enhanced vibration, especially for the low-frequency band. For this reason, the addition of low-frequency vibration damping devices, such as damping pads or elastic bearings, should be considered in the design of viaducts to reduce the transfer of low-frequency vibrations between the train and the bridge and to reduce the impact of long-term vibrations on the bridge structure.

Discussion

Compared with the literature39–41 where the values of train speed parameters are lower than 80 km/h, this paper analyzes the attenuation pattern of vibration induced by rapid urban rail transit in the stratum and the difference of vibration frequency components from the general urban rail transit by studying train speeds of up to 140 km/h and different passenger loads. From the study, we can see that as the train speed increases, the frequency domain acceleration and velocity of each measurement point increase significantly, especially the high and low-frequency components of the viaduct measurement point. This is due to the proximity of the viaduct measurement points to the rails and the propagation of vibrations along the viaduct to the surrounding site. In contrast, the increase in vibration for the low-frequency component was more pronounced at different passenger loads, especially at measurement points close to the rails, but had little effect on the frequency component, due to the smaller effect of the load on the frequency component of the train load43 and the increase in train load when the passenger capacity increases, the frequency of the train operation is close to the intrinsic frequency of the bridge, a resonance effect occurs, which amplifies the low-frequency vibration, which is more pronounced with the increase in the mass of the train. These findings suggest that train speed and passenger loads are key factors affecting vibration on viaducts and their surroundings. In practice, in order to cope with the increase in power spectrum amplitude when the train speed and passenger load increase, vibration isolation measures for viaducts can be enhanced, such as the application of new types of vibration isolation fasteners and vibration isolation roadbeds. Future research can explore the vibration-damping effect of different vibration isolation measures on viaducts and surrounding sites when the train speed and passenger capacity increase.

Conclusion

We conducted simulation studies on the rail-viaduct-soil finite element model under different train speeds and passenger capacity, and obtained the following conclusions:

The frequency domain acceleration of each measuring point increases with the increase of train speed. The acceleration rate of the viaduct measuring point in the high-frequency component is significantly greater than that in the medium and low-frequency bands. The outstanding frequency range is between 60 Hz and 70 Hz. The frequency domain acceleration in the 52 Hz to 60 Hz frequency band gradually increases, and the growth rate is greater than that in other frequency bands. As the distance from the track centerline increases, the outstanding frequency bands at various speeds of the field measuring point change from 5 to 15 Hz to 1 Hz to 7 Hz, and the acceleration increments in these frequency bands are greater than those in other frequency bands as the speed increases.

The frequency domain acceleration of each measuring point increases with the increase of train speed. The acceleration rate of the viaduct measuring point in the high-frequency component is significantly greater than that in the medium and low-frequency bands. The outstanding frequency range is between 60 Hz and 70 Hz. The frequency domain acceleration in the 52 Hz to 60 Hz frequency band gradually increases, and the growth rate is greater than that in other frequency bands.

Acceptance and preference for long-acting antiretrovirals among people living with HIV in China
Authors: Haibo Ding, Xiaolin Li, Qi Wang, Jing Kang, Jing Liu, Yongjun Jiang, Wenqing Geng, Hong Shang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
IntroductionLong-acting antiretroviral (LAARV) regimens have been shown to be as effective as standard daily oral antiretroviral therapy (ART) in maintaining HIV-1 suppression. China has one of the largest populations of people living with HIV (PLHIV) and provides a national free ART program with limited regimens, which currently does not include LAARV. The acceptability of LAARV among PLHIV in China remains unknown.MethodsBetween 1 June 2022 and 30 November 2022, we conducted an online survey among PLHIV in China to assess their acceptance and preference for LAARV. Acceptance was measured on a five-point scale and dichotomized into “more likely to use” (including “definitely” and “probably”) and “less likely to use” (including “uncertainly”, “would not” and “definitely would not”). Logistic regression was used to identify factors associated with acceptance.ResultsAmong the 2570 PLHIV who responded to the questionnaire, 67% expressed interest in LAARV. Participants currently using a single-tablet regimen (STR) showed the highest interest in LAARV (adjusted odds ratio [AOR] 1.64, 95% confidence interval [CI] 1.29–2.10). A subjective assessment of suboptimal ART adherence was also associated with greater interest (AOR 2.25, 95% CI 1.13–4.49). Preferences for LAARV modalities, in descending order, were intramuscular injection (every 1–2 months), subdermal implants (every 6–12 months), and intravenous infusion (every 1–2 weeks).ConclusionsThis study reveals a high acceptance of LAARV among PLHIV in China and identifies key potential user groups, thereby addressing a critical evidence gap regarding LAARV implementation in the country. A cost-competitive long-acting intramuscular injection ART is the most preferred LAARV option in China.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12889-025-25585-w.

Introduction

The long-acting (LA) cabotegravir (CAB) plus rilpivirine (RPV) regimen is an innovative antiretroviral option recommended by treatment guidelines for people living with HIV (PLHIV) who have demonstrated consistent engagement in care and achieved viral suppression on prior antiretroviral therapy (ART) [1]. Initially approved for monthly administration in Australia, Canada, the European Union, and the United States, it reduces dosing frequency from daily oral intake to injections once every month (Q1M) or every two months (Q2M) [2, 3]. The Q2M dosing is now commercially available in multiple regions, including Australia, Canada, parts of Europe, Hong Kong, Japan, Taiwan, the United Kingdom, and the United States. This long-acting antiretroviral (LAARV) combination of CAB and RPV has demonstrated non-inferior efficacy to continued daily oral therapy [4–6].

China has one of the world’s largest annual numbers of new HIV cases and operates the largest National Free Antiretroviral Treatment Program (NFATP) [7]. However, the range of available ART regimens remains limited and does not yet include LAARV. With widespread promotion of ART and improved medical services, China has successfully reduced overall HIV mortality from 5.4% in 2013 to 2.7% in 2022 [8]. By the end of 2023, 1.29 million people were reported to be living with HIV nationwide. Within the context of limited antiretroviral options, suboptimal adherence remains a critical issue, leading to incomplete viral suppression and the emergence of drug resistance. Beyond education and counseling, drug-related adverse effects and medication convenience significantly influence adherence. As of May 2015, data showed that only 77.6% of PLHIV in China had achieved above 90% adherence to ART [9].

To address the growing need for improved adherence and quality of life (QoL), integrase strand transfer inhibitor (INSTI)-based single-tablet regimens (STRs) have been available in China since 2018 [10]. Additionally, the long-acting agent albuvirtide (ABT), administered once weekly, was approved in China in 2018 and has shown good tolerability and effectiveness in combination with ritonavir-boosted lopinavir in real-world practice [11]. The development of ABT represents a shift in the global HIV strategy from maintaining daily adherence to optimizing population-level treatment engagement through extended dosing intervals. However, the need for weekly intravenous infusion and the absence of compatible long-acting partners for ABT limit its convenience. As adherence, viral suppression, reduced transmission, and QoL are closely interconnected, LAARV could be a promising tool for ending the HIV pandemic by 2030, through enhanced adherence and treatment-as-prevention outcomes.

Several studies have demonstrated the efficacy of LAARV compared to daily ART, indicating that monthly injections of long-acting CAB and RPV are as effective as standard oral therapy in maintaining virological suppression over two years [12], with dosing every 8 weeks showing non-inferior efficacy to every 4 weeks in a 96-week analysis [13]. Although injection-related adverse events were common, they rarely led to treatment discontinuation [13, 14]. Given that CAB + RPV may become available in China in the near future, understanding its acceptance and preferences among Chinese PLHIV is critical.

Therefore, we conducted an online survey using a questionnaire to investigate acceptance, identify potential user profiles, and explore preferences for LAARV among PLHIV in China, thereby generating evidence for policymakers, healthcare providers, researchers, and stakeholders in HIV treatment and prevention.

Method

Study design and population

A cross-sectional online survey was conducted among PLHIV in China between 1 June and 30 November 2022. The study was facilitated through the HIV clinic of the First Affiliated Hospital of China Medical University, which provides comprehensive care for nearly 5000 patients. Participants were recruited via convenience sampling through two channels: (1) real-time QR code scanning during clinical visits, and (2) survey links distributed via case manager-mediated text messages.

Eligible participants met the following criteria: (a) aged 18 years or older; (b) self-reported HIV-1 infection; (c) proficient in smartphone use and possessing adequate literacy to complete the survey; and (d) provision of informed consent.

The study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and approved by the Institutional Review Board (Ethics Committee) of The First Affiliated Hospital of China Medical University (Approval No. 2019 − 150).

Measure

The questionnaire (see supplemental file) was adapted from previously published instruments that specifically assessed attitudes toward long-acting antiretrovirals [15, 16]. Given that global HIV treatment guidelines uniformly endorse daily antiretroviral therapy as the standard of care, this alignment with our research objectives ensures the tool’s appropriateness for the study in China.

We collected comprehensive demographic, epidemiological, and clinical data. Demographic data included sex, age, education (junior high school or below, high school or equivalent, college or above), monthly income, and marital status (single, married, divorced/separated, widowed). Partner cohabitation was classified as “single” if unmarried. Epidemiological and clinical data included transmission route (homosexual, heterosexual, blood-related, other), years since HIV diagnosis (≤5, 6–10, > 10), years on ART (≤5, 6–10, > 10), ART regimen, dosing frequency (once or twice daily), self-reported adherence, number of missed doses in the past two weeks, CD4 + T-cell count, and HIV-RNA level. Participants who were not currently on ART completed non-medication-related sections only (e.g., socio-demographics, risk behaviors, long-acting medication preferences); medication-specific questions were marked as ‘N/A’ during analysis. Participants in our clinic had secure online access to their CD4 + T cells and viral load results, which facilitated the reporting of these clinical values.

While the study focused on bimonthly intramuscular injections, we recognized that acceptance might vary by LAARV modality and that younger individuals might express a greater preference for novel technologies like subdermal implants. Therefore, we assessed preferences across three forms: intravenous infusion every 1–2 weeks; intramuscular injection every 1–2 months, and subdermal implant every 6–12 months.

To facilitate a direct comparison of preferences independent of cost, we employed a methodological scenario in which all LAARV options were presented as having a price equivalent to that of combination STRs, as detailed in Table 1. This pricing scenario did not apply to the weekly injected drug ABT, which, in clinical practice, requires combination with other daily oral antiretrovirals (ARVs). We also collected data on reasons for acceptance and concerns, allowing participants to select all applicable perceived benefits.

Participants were introduced to LAARV as follows: A new form of antiretroviral therapy, sometimes called a long-acting regimen, which is given by injection every two months, is currently under study. They were first asked whether they had heard of LAARV (yes/no), then about their willingness to use it (“definitely would”, “probably would”, “uncertainly”, “would not”, “definitely would not”). Responses were dichotomized into “would” (“definitely”/“probably would”) and “would not” (“uncertainly”/“would not”/“definitely would not”). Concerns about injectable treatments were similarly assessed.

Statistical analysis

Categorical variables were summarized using counts and percentages, with differences assessed by χ² test (p < 0.05). Univariable and multivariable logistic regression (enter method) were used to identify factors associated with LAARV acceptance. Variables with p < 0.1 in univariate analysis were included in the multivariate model; p < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. Analyses were performed using IBM SPSS 19.0.

Results

Demographic and clinical characteristics

A total of 2,570 questionnaires were deemed complete and valid, forming the final analytic dataset (representing a 95% data validity rate from the total submissions received). The majority of the 2,570 participants were male (94.6%, n = 2430), with a mean age of 38 years (standard deviation [SD] 11.4). Most participants were of Han Chinese (86.6%), single (64.6%), and had a high school education or above (75.0%). Individuals with a monthly income below 2,000yuan comprised 20.5% of the cohort. Regarding HIV characteristics, 83.9% were men who have sex with men (MSM). 40% had lived with HIV for over 6 years, and 28% have been on ART for more than 6 years (Table 2).

Information regarding ART intake

Among participants on ART, only 13.9% were using a STR despite its availability in China for several years, while 20.5% were on a twice-daily dosing schedule. In the two weeks preceding the survey, 9.9% of participants reported having missed doses, and 22.0% of the participants on ART did not achieve viral suppression (HIV-RNA < 20copies/mL). Although 97.6% were on ART, only approximately half of all participants (53.7%) self-rated their health as “good” or “excellent” (Table 2).

Factors associated with willingness to use LAARV

Overall, more than half (1,409/2,570) of the participants have heard of LAARV prior to the survey and 67.0% (1,723/2,570) of the entire cohort reported willingness to use it. Among those interested in LAARV, descriptive analyses showed that the proportion was higher in certain subgroups: 66.9% were under 40 years of age, and 75.0% had attained a high school education or above.

In univariate analysis, however, educational level was not significantly associated with interest in LAARV. In contrast, several other factors demonstrated significant associations. Specially, interest was higher among aged 31–40 and > 50, as well as heavy drinkers, expressed greater interest, whereas high-income individuals reported lower willingness. Notably, those with suboptimal adherence, particularly those reporting more than two missed doses in the past two weeks, exhibited higher acceptance of LAARV compared to those with perfect adherence. Participants using a STR expressed greater willingness to switch to LAARV than those on multi-pill regimens, and those taking medication twice daily were more receptive than once-daily users.

The multivariable analysis, adjusted for age, race, marital status, income, and alcohol use, confirmed that regimen-related factors were independently associated with interest in LAARV. STR users had 1.64 times higher odds of willingness (adjusted odds ratio [AOR] 1.64, 95% CI: 1.29–2.10, p < 0.001), and those on twice-daily regimens were more likely to choose LAARV (AOR 1.34, 95% CI: 1.08–1.66, p = 0.009). Suboptimal adherence also remained independently associated with greater interest (Table 3).

Factors associated with willingness of trying LAARV

Patient preferences for modality of Long-Acting antiretroviral therapy

In anticipation of diverse future long-acting antiretroviral regimens—including injectable, implantable, and oral formulations—we categorized LAARV options by frequency and mode of administration. The presented scenarios assumed equivalent efficacy and cost to current STRs. The options included: intramuscular injection every 1–2 months, intravenous infusion every 1–2 weeks, and subdermal implants every 6–12 months.

Among all survey participants, 35.1% preferred intramuscular injection (every 1–2 months) as their first choice, followed by 33% who chose STR. Only 7.5% selected intravenous infusion (every 1–2 weeks) as their first choice (Fig. 1). Given that injectable LAARV regimens require clinic visits for administration, we further analyzed patient preferences regarding dosing frequency. The results indicated that longer intervals between injections were significantly more attractive, primarily due to the reduced burden of clinic visits (Fig. 2).

Awareness and attitudes toward LAARV

When asked about their willingness to adopt an injectable regimen replacing daily oral therapy, 69.1% of participants responded positively: 34.6% indicated they would “definitely” try it, and 34.5% said they “probably” would. In comparison, 24.7% remained uncertain, while only 6.2% expressed unwillingness (5.3% “probably not” and 0.9% “definitely not”).

The most commonly cited motivation for trying LAARV was not having to remember daily dosing (36.4%). Other reasons included concerns about carrying medications (30.7%) and a desire for greater privacy regarding their HIV status (23.4%).

The most frequently cited barrier to accepting injectable LAARV was concern about potential side effects (82.4%), with the vast majority of participants reporting they were ‘concerned’ or ‘very concerned’ about this issue, followed by concerns about the possible interaction with alcohol or other drugs (79.1% combined ‘concerned’ and ‘very concerned’). Surprisingly, our analysis revealed that participants who were concerned or very concerned about side effects were significantly more likely to report willingness to try injectable LAARV than those who were unconcerned (p < 0.001). Specifically, a higher proportion of participants in the ‘concerned and very concerned’ groups reported they ‘probably’ or ‘definitely’ would use LAARV compared to those in the ‘unconcerned’ group (Fig. 3).

Discussion

This national study, the largest of its kind in China to date, reveals a high acceptance rate (nearly 70%) for LAARV among PLHIV, under a scenario where LAARV is presumed to be non-inferior in efficacy and available at no additional cost compared to current self-paid ART regimens. We identified current users of STR as the most potential adopters of LAARV. Furthermore, a self-reported history of suboptimal ART adherence was positively correlated with greater interest in switching to a long-acting option. In terms of formulation preferences, long-acting intramuscular injection was the most favored option, followed by subdermal implants. Key concerns included potential side effects and interactions with alcohol or other medications. These findings address a critical evidence gap and provide valuable insights for guiding future LAARV implementation strategies in China and similar settings.

The high willingness to use LAARV is particularly relevant in the context of persistent challenges with daily oral ART. Despite its success since 1996, daily therapy continues to face issues such as suboptimal adherence, the emergence of drug resistance, adverse effects, and a limited selection of available regimens. LAARV represents a promising development that could address these challenges by enhancing the effectiveness of treatment and prevention, innovating strategies and improving QoL [18–21].

This study enrolled a larger sample than prior research [22]. Nearly half of the participants were aware of LAARV, which may be attributed to the introduction of the long-acting injectable ABT in China [11]. However, this long-acting, injectable agent should be combined an orally taken ARVs. Overall, 67% of participants reported willingness to try novel LAARV regimens, with no significant differences observed by gender, alcohol use, or education level. This finding is consistent with a previous study [23], which also indicated that many PLHIV are looking forward to using LAARV, for the innovative drugs that may improve their quality of life.

Furthermore, we also found that patients currently on a STR and those with suboptimal medication adherence were more likely to switch to LAARV. STRs, first introduced to China in 2018 [10], reduce pill burden and may improve adherence compared to multi-pill regimens [24]. A retrospective study discovered that patients on STRs were more likely to be adherent than patients on multi-pill regimens over 12 months [25]. Although direct evidence regarding the effect of STRs on adherence in Chinese PLHIV is limited, the high burden associated with daily multi-tablet regimens may increase the risk of suboptimal adherence and treatment discontinuation. We anticipate that STR-containing regimens, such as those with efavirenz, may demonstrate higher cost-effectiveness due to improved adherence rates and better health outcomes [26].

A key finding from our analysis is that patients currently on a STR showed a greater preference for switching to LAARV compared to those on other regimens. This suggests that individuals who have experienced the benefits of simplified dosing may be more receptive to further treatment innovations such as LAARV, which offers extended dosing intervals and reduces the burden of daily medication. Moreover, approximately 10% of participants reported suboptimal medication adherence for various reasons. This group also expressed stronger willingness to adopt LAARV, indicating that long-acting formulations could represent a promising option for improving adherence and minimizing the risk of drug resistance in this population.

Treatment burden, particularly regarding dosing frequency and mode, was a key driver of preference. Patients preferred the longer interval of intramuscular injection (every 1–2 months) over both more frequent intravenous infusions (every 1–2 weeks) and the less frequent but more invasive implant (every 6–12 months). This preference was compounded by the requirement for in-clinic administration, leading to a common desire to extend the interval to three months to reduce visit frequency. Notably, we found that participants who were inclined to choose LAARV expressed greater concern about potential side effects and drug-drug interactions. Despite these concerns, healthcare providers’ perspectives may play an important role in patient decision-making and could enhance perceived feasibility of LAARV adoption. It should be noted, however, that providers’ views are also shaped by the clinical needs and overall well-being of their patients.

Our study has several limitations. First, as a descriptive cross-sectional investigation, it is susceptible to potential biases. The majority of participants (95.3%) were recruited from a specific HIV clinic within a comprehensive hospital, and limited information was available regarding the recruitment sources of the remaining individuals. This may affect the generalizability of our findings, particularly in representing patients from grassroots communities or remote areas. Additionally, the cross-sectional design does not allow for causal inferences or the assessment of changes in patients’ willingness over time, which may fluctuate due to factors such as treatment experience, side effects, or evolving social promotion efforts. Second, medication adherence was assessed using a single-item measure (“number of missed doses in the past 14 days”) rather than a validated multi-item scale. Although this simplified approach was intended to reduce participant burden and improve recall accuracy—especially in younger populations—it may not fully capture the complexity of adherence behaviors. Single-item measures are prone to recall bias and may lack the sensitivity to identify partial adherence or timing deviations. Third, measures of viral suppression status and medication compliance were based on self-reported data, which are subject to social desirability and recall biases. Although participants were permitted to access their personal health records during the study to assist in reporting their viral load and CD4 counts, this practice is not yet universal or routinely used by all patients in our setting. Therefore, while this facility may have improved the accuracy for some individuals, it unlikely mitigated the self-reporting bias across the entire cohort. We anticipate that any misclassification would likely be non-differential. Fourth, we did not account for the broader indirect costs and patient burdens associated with LAARV administration. A significant component of this is the burden of travel, including both direct expenses and the time spent commuting and waiting. In the current standard of care, patients typically travel to the clinic every 3 to 6 months. The adoption of LAARV would alter this pattern; regimens requiring more frequent clinic visits could substantially increase travel burdens and the associated psychological stress of frequent clinic attendance.

In conclusion, our study reported the acceptance, most potential users, and preference for LAARV among PLHIV in China with the largest national sample, closing the knowledge gap that exists in the implementation of LAARV in China. A cost-competitive long-acting intramuscular injection ART is the LAARV with the most acceptability in China.

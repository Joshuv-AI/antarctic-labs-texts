Abstract
. Brenier’s fundamental theorem characterizes optimal transport plans for measures μ , ν \mu,\nu on ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} and quadratic distance costs in terms of gradients of convex functions. In particular it guarantees the existence of optimal transport maps for measures which are absolutely continuous wrt Lebesgue measure. Our goal is to provide a version of this result for measures P , Q P,Q on 𝒫 2 ​ ( ℝ d ) \mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}) and costs given by the squared Wasserstein distance W 2 2 ​ ( μ , ν ) W_{2}^{2}(\mu,\nu) . We characterize optimizers in terms of convexity of the Lions lift. This is based on an observation which seems to be of independent interest: the c c -transform of a functional φ \varphi , where c ⁡ ( μ , ν ) c(\mu,\nu) denotes maximal covariance of μ , ν \mu,\nu corresponds precisely to the Legendre transform of the Lions lift of φ \varphi . Moreover we show that for typical P ∈ 𝒫 2 ​ ( ℝ d ) P\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}) the optimizer is unique and given by a transport map. In the absence of a canonical reference measure on 𝒫 2 ​ ( ℝ d ) \mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}) we use a topological notion to make ‘typical’ precise. Specifically we show that the transport regular measures are of second Baire category. A particular motivation for our article stems from the theory of adapted transport where the adapted Wasserstein distance provides an adequate distance between stochastic processes. In contrast to other metrics, the adapted Wasserstein distance yields continuity of Doob-decomposition, optimal stopping and stochastic control problems. Based on our results for measures on 𝒫 2 ​ ( ℝ d ) \mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}) we obtain a first Brenier-type theorem for the adapted Wasserstein distance. keywords: optimal transport for random measures, Brenier’s theorem, Lions lift, convex conjugate, Monge problem, adapted optimal transport.

Introduction

Let
(\mathcal{X},\rho)
be a Polish metric space and write
\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathcal{X})
for the probabilities with finite second moments. For
\mu,\nu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathcal{X})
we write
\textup{Cpl}(\mu,\nu)
for the set of couplings or transport plans and

(1)

\displaystyle W_{2}^{2}(\mu,\nu)=\inf_{\pi\in\textup{Cpl}(\mu,\nu)}\int\rho\,d\pi=\inf_{X\sim\mu,Y\sim\nu}\mathbb{E}[\rho(X,Y)^{2}]

for the squared Wasserstein-2 distance.

Brenier’s theorem
[
13
]
asserts that if
\mathcal{X}
is endowed with the Euclidean norm and
\mu
is absolutely continuous, the minimization problem (
1
) admits a unique optimizer
\pi^{*}
, moreover
\pi^{*}
is characterized as the unique coupling of
\mu,\nu
which is concentrated on the gradient of a convex function. It stands at the beginning of the breathtaking development of optimal transport over the last decades. In particular it has inspired a number of prominent extensions and refinements, we mention
[
22
,
26
,
21
,
27
,
3
,
17
,
18
,
19
]
among others.

The goal of this article is to provide an analogous result in the case where
(\mathcal{X},\rho)=(\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),W_{2})
, i.e. Brenier type results for
P,Q\in(\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})),W_{2})
.
In particular, we provide an appropriate notion of convexity that allows to characterize optimizers for the squared Wasserstein distance in this case.

Our main motivation stems from adapted optimal transport: based on the Brenier theorem for measures on
(\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),W_{2})
we obtain a first Brenier-type theorem for the squared adapted Wasserstein distance on the space of stochastic processes. This is possible since stochastic processes (with two time periods) can be embedded into
(\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})),W_{2})
, and they precisely correspond to a dense
G_{\delta}
subset.

A main obstacle in obtaining Brenier-type results on
(\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})),W_{2})
(resp. for the adapted Wasserstein distance) is the lack of natural reference measure which would take role of the Lebesgue measure. As a substitute, we will show that
most
measures allow for a Brenier theorem, where “most” refers to largeness in the topological sense: A subset of a topological space is generic / of second Baire category if it contains a dense
G_{\delta}
set. This is a convenient notion of largeness for our purposes since it allows us to obtain that also most stochastic processes admit a Brenier type result by Baire’s theorem.

1.1.
Characterization of optimizers for
(\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),W_{2})
.

The crucial idea is that functionals
\varphi:\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to(-\infty,+\infty]
correspond to law-invariant functions on
L^{2}(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
, where
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
is a standard probability space, via the Lions lift

\overline{\varphi}:L^{2}(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})\to(-\infty,+\infty],\quad\overline{\varphi}(X):=\varphi(\mathcal{L}(X)),

where
\mathcal{L}(X):=X_{\#}\mathbb{P}
denotes the law of the random variable
X
under
\mathbb{P}
. As a Hilbert space
L^{2}(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
carries a natural notion of convexity and it turns out that this notion is precisely the right notion to tackle transport problems on the Wasserstein space.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
P,Q\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
and
\Pi\in\textup{Cpl}(P,Q)
. Then
\Pi
is optimal for (
1
) if and only if there exists a proper convex law-invariant function
\overline{\varphi}:L^{2}(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})\to(-\infty,\infty]
such that for all
X,Y\in L^{2}(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P}),
with
(\mathcal{L}(X),\mathcal{L}(Y))\in\textup{supp}(\Pi)

\frac{1}{2}\|X\|^{2}-\overline{\varphi}(X)+\frac{1}{2}\|Y\|^{2}-\overline{\varphi}^{\ast}(Y)=\frac{1}{2}W_{2}^{2}(\mathcal{L}(X),\mathcal{L}(Y)).

As in the case of optimal transport on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
we expect that optimal transport plans should be related to the subgradient of the convex function
\bar{\varphi}
. In ‘nice’ cases this subgradient will consist only of single element and we can hope to obtain Monge type optimizers from
Theorem

1.1
. To make this precise we introduce some notations.

A measure
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is called transport-regular if for every
\nu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, there is a unique optimal transport plan
\pi\in\textup{Cpl}(\mu,\nu)
and
\pi
is of Monge type, i.e.
\pi=(\textup{id},T)_{\#}\mu
for some
T:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
We call
P\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
transport-regular if for every
Q\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

there is a unique optimal plan
\Pi\in\textup{Cpl}(P,Q)
and
\Pi
is of Monge type, i.e.
\Pi=(\textup{id},T)_{\#}P
for some

(2)

\displaystyle T:\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

In fact if
P
is transport-regular, then (see
Proposition

3.2
)
for
P
-a.e.
p\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, the optimal transport
\pi_{p}\in\textup{Cpl}(p,T(p))
is again unique and of Monge type, i.e.
T(p)={S_{p}}_{\#}p
for some map

(3)

\displaystyle S_{p}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}.

We then obtain the following Baire–Brenier theorem:

Theorem 1.2
.

The set of transport-regular measures is a dense
G_{\delta}
subset of
\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
.

Sets of second category in a product of Polish spaces satisfy a Fubini-type theorem. In consequence,
Theorem

1.2
implies

Corollary 1.3
.

The set of pairs
(P,Q)\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))\times\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
, where
P,Q
are both transport-regular and the map
T
in (
2
) as well as the maps
S_{p}
in (
3
) are bijective, is a dense
G_{\delta}
subset of
\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))\times\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
.

1.2.
MC
-convexity and characterization of optimizers

Already for
\mu,\nu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
) it is convenient to replace (
1
) by the equivalent maximization problem for the ‘maximal covariance’

(4)

\displaystyle\textup{MC}(\mu,\nu)=\sup_{\pi\in\textup{Cpl}(\mu,\nu)}\int x\cdot y\,d\pi(x,y)=\sup_{X\sim\mu,Y\sim\nu}\mathbb{E}[X\cdot Y]

which relates more directly to convex analysis.
Likewise, if
P,Q\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
we replace (
1
) by

(5)

\displaystyle\textup{MC}(P,Q)=\sup_{\pi\in\textup{Cpl}(P,Q)}\int\textup{MC}(\mu,\nu)\,d\pi(\mu,\nu).

A basic result for the characterization of optimal plans is the ‘fundamental theorem of optimal transport’ (see e.g.
[
2
]
or
[
35
]
).
We recall a version that is convenient for
MC
costs:

For symmetric
c:\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{X}\to\mathbb{R}

the
c
-convex conjugate of
\varphi:\mathcal{X}\to[-\infty,+\infty]
is defined as

\varphi^{c}(y):=\sup_{x\in\mathcal{X}}c(x,y)-\varphi(x).

Here,
\varphi
is called
c
-convex
if
\varphi^{cc}=\varphi
and each such
\varphi
is lsc. We also note that
\varphi^{ccc}=\varphi^{c}
.
The subdifferential of a
c
-convex
\varphi
is

\quad\partial_{c}\varphi:=\{(x,y):\varphi^{c}(x)+\varphi(y)=c(x,y)\}=\{(x,y):c(x,y)-\varphi(x)\geq c(z,y)-\varphi(z)\text{ for all $z\in X$}\},

and
\partial_{c}\varphi(x)=\{y:(x,y)\in\partial_{c}\varphi\}
.
The primal and dual transport problem w.r.t.
c
are given by

(6)

\displaystyle\text{OT}_{\text{primal}}:=\sup_{\pi\in\textup{Cpl}(\mu,\nu)}\int c\,d\pi,\quad\text{OT}_{\text{dual}}:=\inf_{\varphi\in L^{1}(\mu),\varphi\text{ l.s.c.}}\int\varphi\,d\mu+\int\varphi^{c}\,d\nu.

Theorem 1.4

(Fundamental theorem of optimal transport)
.

Assume that
c:\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{X}\to\mathbb{R}
is symmetric, continuous and satisfies
|c|\leq a\oplus b
,
a\in L^{1}(\mu),b\in L^{1}(\nu)
.
The primal problem is attained and the dual problem is attained by a
c
-convex function
\varphi
. Moreover for any such
\varphi
we have that
\pi\in\textup{Cpl}(\mu,\nu)
is optimal for the primal problem if and only if
\textup{supp}(\pi)\subseteq\partial_{c}\varphi
.

In the setting of Brenier’s theorem, i.e.
\mathcal{X}=\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
c(x,y)=x\cdot y
,
c
-convexity is just ordinary convexity and
Theorem

1.4
yields that transport plans are optimal if and only if they are concentrated on the subgradient of a convex function.

In the case

\mathcal{X}=\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
c(\mu,\nu)=\textup{MC}(\mu,\nu)
,
c
-convexity
Theorem

1.4
asserts that transport plans are optimal if and only if they are concentrated on the
MC
-subgradients of
MC
-convex functions. Hence, to understand optimizers, we need to characterize
MC
-convex functions. This is obtained in the following observation which also appears to be of independent interest:

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
\varphi:\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to(-\infty,+\infty]
be proper. Then

{\overline{\varphi\,}}^{\ast}=\overline{\varphi^{\textup{MC}}}.

In particular, the convex conjugate of a law-invariant function is law-invariant.

Note that
Theorem

1.5
together with the fundamental theorem of optimal transport (
Theorem

1.4
) readily implies
Theorem

1.1
. In order to obtain the Baire–Brenier theorem (
Theorem

1.2
), we will combine results on the differentiability of convex functions on Hilbert spaces with results on the topology of the set of transport-regular measures.
Specifically we will show that for abstract transport problems on Polish spaces the set of transport-regular measures is
G_{\delta}
, see

Theorem

4.5
.

1.3.
Comparison of different notions of convexity

It is straightforward that convexity of the lift
\bar{\varphi}
of a function
\varphi:\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to(-\infty,+\infty]
is equivalent to convexity along along all curves of the form

(7)

\displaystyle(\mu_{t})_{t\in[0,1]},\mu_{t}:=((1-t)\textup{proj}_{0}+t\textup{proj}_{1})_{\#}(\pi),\pi\in\textup{Cpl}(\mu,\nu).

Such functionals are called totally convex
[
16
]
or acceleration-free convex
[
30
]
or simply convex (in the literature on risk measures). Subsequently we will show that
\varphi
is totally convex if and only if it
MC
-convex.

Similarly a closed set
A
is called totally convex if for
\mu,\nu\in A
all curves of the form (
7
) are in
A
. This is equivalent to
A
being the sublevel set of a
MC
-convex functional.
Moreover we obtain

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
A\subseteq\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
be closed. If (and only if)
A
is totally convex then

\mu\mapsto W_{2}(\mu,A)

is totally convex.

In particular if
A
is totally convex, then is
W_{2}(.,A)
is also displacement convex. This seems of interest since
W_{2}(.,\nu)
is in general not displacement convex. In fact
W_{2}(.,\nu)
is geodesically convex if and only
\nu
is a dirac measure.

It is known that if
\varphi:\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to\mathbb{R}
is sufficiently smooth (see
[
30
]
), then displacement convexity and total convexity are equivalent while the notions are not equivalent in general. We will show

Proposition 1.7
.

For a lsc functional
\varphi:\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to\mathbb{R}
, the following are equivalent:

(1)

\varphi
is totally convex

(2)

\varphi
is displacement convex and increasing in convex order.

Recall that
\mu,\nu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
are in convex order, in signs
\prec_{c}
if
\int f\,d\mu\leq\int f\,d\nu
for all convex
f:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to(-\infty,\infty]
.

1.4.
Stochastic processes and a Brenier theorem for adapted transport

Authors from several fields have independently refined the weak topologies on the laws of stochastic processes in order to take the temporal flow of information into account, see
[
1
,
23
,
24
,
11
,
31
,
32
,
28
]
among others. In finite discrete time, all these approaches defined the same ‘adapted’ weak topology which is metrized by a suitable adapted Wasserstein distance
\mathcal{AW}
, see
[
5
,
29
,
7
]
.
In contrast to other topologies for stochastic processes, probabilistic operations such as the Doob-decomposition, optimal stopping and stochastic control problems are continuous w.r.t.
\mathcal{AW}
.

We consider here only the first non-trivial instance of stochastic processes
X=(X_{t})_{t=1,2}
with two time steps and state space
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Denote by
\mu,\nu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
the laws of stochastic processes
X,Y
. The adapted Wasserstein distance
\mathcal{AW}_{2}(\mu,\nu)
is similarly to its classical counterpart with the sole difference that the only adapted / bi-causal couplings of
\mu,\nu
are considered. Precisely, considering the disintegrations

\mu(dx_{1},dx_{2})=\mu_{1}(dx_{1})\otimes\mu_{2}^{x_{1}}(dx_{2}),\nu(dy_{1},dy_{2})=\nu_{1}(dy_{1})\otimes\nu_{2}^{y_{1}}(dy_{2}),

\pi\in\textup{Cpl}(\mu,\nu)
is bi-causal if it can be written in form

\pi(dx,dy)=\pi_{1}(dx_{1},dy_{1})\otimes\pi_{2}^{x_{1},y_{1}}(dx_{2},dy_{2})\text{ where }\pi_{1}\in\textup{Cpl}(\mu_{1},\nu_{1}),\pi_{2}^{x_{1},y_{1}}\in\textup{Cpl}(\mu_{2}^{x_{1}},\nu_{2}^{x_{2}}).

Alternatively we have

(8)

\displaystyle\mathcal{AW}(\mu,\nu)=\inf_{\pi_{1}\in\textup{Cpl}(\mu_{1},\nu_{1})}\int|x_{1}-y_{1}|^{2}+W_{2}(\mu^{x_{1}},\nu^{y_{1}})\,d\pi_{1}(x_{1},y_{1}).

A Monge-type bicausal transport is concentrated on a function

T=(T_{1},T_{2}):\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d},

where
T_{1}(x_{1},x_{2})=T_{1}(x_{1})
, i.e.
T_{1}
depends only on the first
d
coordinates, see
[
10
]
. We call
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
adapted transport regular if for every
\nu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
there exists a unique optimal bicausal coupling and it is of Monge type.

One might hope to obtain a Brenier theorem for the adapted Wasserstein which guarantees transport regularity under for
\mu
which are absolutely continuous. However, such a result vails even in the case
d=1
, in fact there is no reason that absolute continuity of
\mu
should translate into a regularity property for the cost function in (
8
). In
Example

5.1
we construct two compactly supported absolutely continuous probabilities
\mu,\nu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R})
for which the adapted transport problem with quadratic costs admits no Monge-type optimizer.

However, we prove the following Baire–Brenier theorem for the squared adapted Wasserstein distance:

Theorem 1.8
.

The set of adapted transport regular measures is a dense
G_{\delta}
subset of
\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

2.
Lions lift and convexity of law invariant functions

2.1.
Recap on Lions lift and Lions derivative

The aim of this section is to recall the definition of the Lions lift of a function
\varphi:\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to(-\infty,\infty]
and to provide properties of the lift that we will need subsequently.

Definition 2.1
.

Given a probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
,
the Lions lift of a function
\varphi:\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to(-\infty,+\infty]
is defined as

\overline{\varphi}:L^{2}(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})\to(-\infty,+\infty],\quad\overline{\varphi}(X):=\varphi(\mathcal{L}(X)).

We say that of function
\overline{\varphi}:L^{2}(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})\to(-\infty,+\infty]
is law-invariant if
\overline{\varphi}(X)=\overline{\varphi}(X^{\prime})
whenever
X\sim X^{\prime}
. Clearly, law-invariant functions are precisely those which arise as Lions lift of a function
\varphi:\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to(-\infty,+\infty]
. Recall that
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
is a standard probability space if it is isomorphic to the unit interval with Lebesgue measure.

In the following we will always assume that
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
is a standard probability space and given a function
\varphi:\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to(-\infty,+\infty]
, the function
\overline{\varphi}:L^{2}(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})\to(-\infty,+\infty]
always denotes its Lions lift. We use
X,X^{\prime},Y,Z
to denote elements of
L^{2}(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
.

We write
\textup{dom}(\varphi):=\{\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):\varphi(\mu)<+\infty\}
and
\textup{dom}(\overline{\varphi}):=\{X\in L^{2}(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P}):\overline{\varphi}(X)<+\infty\}
. Clearly we have

\textup{dom}(\overline{\varphi}):=\{X\in L^{2}(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P}):\mathcal{L}(X)\in\textup{dom}(\varphi)\}.

The functions
\varphi
and
\overline{\varphi}
are called proper if their domain is not empty.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\varphi:\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to(-\infty,+\infty]
. Then
\varphi
is (lower semi) continuous wrt
\mathcal{W}_{2}
if and only if
\overline{\varphi}
is (lower semi) wrt
\|\cdot\|_{2}
.

Proof.

This follows from the continuity of the law map and the Skorokhod representation theorem.
∎

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
X,X^{\prime}
be random variables on
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
such that
X\sim X^{\prime}
.
Then, for every
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a measure-preserving bijection
T:\Omega\to\Omega
such that
\mathbb{P}(|X^{\prime}-X\circ T|\leq\varepsilon)\geq 1-\varepsilon
.

See
[
15
, Lemma 5.23]
for a stronger result which pertains to
L^{\infty}
-convergence instead of convergence in probability.

Proof of
Lemma

2.3
.

Since
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
is a standard probability space, we may assume w.l.o.g. that
\Omega=(0,1)
,
\mathcal{F}
are the Borel sets and
\mathbb{P}
is the Lebesgue measure
\lambda
. Set
U(x)=x
for
x\in(0,1)
.
Then
\mu:=\mathcal{L}(U,X)
and
\nu:=\mathcal{L}(U,X^{\prime})
are atomless distributions and both are concentrated on the graph of a function. By assumption,
\mu,\nu
have the same marginals, hence,

\inf_{\pi\in\textup{Cpl}(\mu,\nu)}\int|x-x^{\prime}|\wedge 1\,d\pi((x,u),(x^{\prime},u^{\prime}))=0.

By
[
20
]
, the transport plans supported on Borel bijections are dense in
\textup{Cpl}(\mu,\nu)
. Thus there exists a sequence of Borel bijections
(R_{n},S_{n}):(0,1)\times\mathbb{R}\to(0,1)\times\mathbb{R}
,
n\geq 1
such that
(R_{n},S_{n})_{\#}\mu=\nu
and
S_{n}(u,x)\to x
in
\hat{\mu}
-probability. The second assertions means that
S_{n}(\omega,X(\omega))\to X(\omega)
in
\lambda
-probability. On the other hand
(R_{n},S_{n})_{\#}\mu=\nu
yields that
S_{n}(\omega,X(\omega))=X^{\prime}(R_{n}(\omega,X(\omega)))

\lambda
-a.s. and
\omega\mapsto R_{n}(\omega,X(\omega))
is a measure preserving bijection.
∎

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
X,Y
and
X^{\prime}
be random variables on
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
such that
X\sim X^{\prime}
. For
\varepsilon>0
, there are random variables
X^{\prime\prime},Y^{\prime\prime}
on
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
such that
(X,Y)\sim(X^{\prime\prime},Y^{\prime\prime})
and
\mathbb{P}(|X^{\prime}-X^{\prime\prime}|\geq\varepsilon)<\varepsilon
.

Proof.

By
Lemma

2.3
, there is measure preserving bijection
T
such that
\mathbb{P}(|X\circ T-X^{\prime}|\geq\varepsilon)\leq\varepsilon
. We set
X^{\prime\prime}:=X\circ T
and
Y^{\prime\prime}:=Y\circ T
.
∎

In this section we consider functionals on

Definition 2.5
.

Equivalently

\varphi:\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to(-\infty,+\infty]

and all

\varphi(((x,y)\mapsto(1-t)x+ty)_{\#}\pi)\leq(1-t)\varphi(\mu_{0})+t\varphi(\mu_{1})

Theorem 2.6

The following lemma is a straightforward consequence of the denseness of Monge couplings in the set of Kantorovich couplings in the case of continuous starting distributions.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
c:\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
be continuous,
|c|\leq a\oplus b,a\in L^{1}(\mu),b\in L^{1}(\nu)
and assume that
X\sim\mu
. Then we have

(9)

\displaystyle\sup_{\pi\in\textup{Cpl}(\mu,\nu)}\int c\,d\pi=\sup_{Y\sim\nu}\mathbb{E}[c(X,Y)].

Proof.

As for every
Y\sim\nu
, we have
\mathcal{L}(X,Y)\in\textup{Cpl}(\mu,\nu)
, we clearly have
\sup_{\pi\in\textup{Cpl}(\mu,\nu)}\int c\,d\pi\geq\sup_{Y\sim\nu}\mathbb{E}[c(X,Y)].
To see the converse inequality, fix
\pi\in\textup{Cpl}(\mu,\nu)
.
Since
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
is standard and atomless, it supports random variables

with
(X^{\prime},Y^{\prime})\sim\pi
. In particular, we have that
X^{\prime}\sim\mu
.
Therefore, we can apply Lemma
2.4
to find, for each

with
\mathbb{P}(|X-X_{\varepsilon}|\geq\varepsilon)<\varepsilon
and
(X_{\varepsilon},Y_{\varepsilon})\sim\pi
.
By dominated convergence we conclude that

The following observation plays a fundamental role for this paper.

Theorem 2.8

Let

{\overline{\varphi\,}}^{\ast}=\overline{\varphi^{\textup{MC}}}.

Proof.

For

\displaystyle=\sup_{\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\textup{MC}(\mu,\mathcal{L}(Y))-\varphi(\mu)=\varphi^{\textup{MC}}(\mathcal{L}(Y))=\overline{\varphi^{\textup{MC}}}(Y).\qed

In convex analysis on Hilbert spaces, the smallest convex functions (wrt pointwise order) are linear functions and the biggest are convex indicator functions of singletons. The convex conjugation is order reversing and it maps linear functions to indicators of singletons. The analogue of this in the present setting is the following:

Example 2.10
.

Let
\nu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and consider the functional
\varphi(\mu)=\textup{MC}(\mu,\nu)
. We then have
\varphi^{\textup{MC}}=\chi_{\{\rho\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):\rho\leq_{c}\nu\}}
, where
\leq_{c}
denotes the convex order of probability measures.

Proof.

We have

\varphi^{\textup{MC}}(\rho)=\sup_{\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\textup{MC}(\mu,\rho)-\textup{MC}(\mu,\nu).

By
[
14
,
36
]
we have that
\rho\leq_{c}\nu
if and only if
\textup{MC}(\mu,\rho)\leq\textup{MC}(\mu,\nu)
for every
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Hence, if
\rho\leq_{c}\nu
, setting
\mu=\nu
in the supremum, we find
\varphi^{\textup{MC}}(\rho)=0
. Otherwise there exists
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that
\textup{MC}(\mu,\rho)-\textup{MC}(\mu,\nu)>0
. As
\textup{MC}({s_{\lambda}}_{\#}\mu,\rho)=\lambda\textup{MC}(\mu,\rho)
, where

for

, this yields that

whenever

in not smaller in convex order than

.
∎

2.3.
Subdifferentials

Next, we spell out the definition of the
MC

Definition 2.13

Let

\partial_{\textup{MC}}\varphi(\mu)=\{\nu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):\varphi(\rho)\geq\varphi(\mu)+\textup{MC}(\rho,\nu)-\textup{MC}(\mu,\nu)\text{ for every }\rho\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\}.

While the subdifferential of a convex function on a vector space is in every point a convex set, this is not the case for
MC
-subdifferentials of
MC
-convex sets. In fact, they are typically not
MC
-convex because they are often singletons (see differentiability results below) and singletons are not
MC
-convex sets (unless they consist of a Dirac).

Next, we investigate the connection between the

-differential and subdifferential of the Lions lift. We start with a characterization of when random variables

are in the subdifferential of the Lions lift in terms of the

Let

and write
\mu=\mathcal{L}(X)
,
\nu=\mathcal{L}(Z)

\displaystyle\overline{\varphi}(Y)\geq\overline{\varphi}(X)+\mathbb{E}[(Y-X)\cdot Z].

For every

\varphi_{n}(\mu)=\textrm{dist}_{\mathcal{W}_{2}}(\mu,K_{n})
and
\varphi:=\sum_{n}2^{-n}\varphi_{n}
.

We write

and note that as

is law-invariant by

\overline{\varphi_{n}}(Y)=\textrm{dist}_{\mathcal{W}_{2}}(\mathcal{L}(Y),K_{n})=\inf_{X\in C_{n}}\|X-Y\|_{2}=\textrm{dist}_{\|\cdot\|_{2}}(Y,C_{n}).

This shows in particular that

and hence
\varphi
is L-convex. Moreover, it is shown in

.

3.1.
Characterization of

-optimal couplings

Theorem 3.1
.

Let

and

. Then, the following are equivalent:

(1)

\Pi
is optimal;

(2)

there exists

L-convex with
\Pi(\partial_{\textup{MC}}\varphi)=1

(3)

lower semi-continuous, law-invariant, convex and random variables

such that

Proof.

From the fundamental theorem of optimal transport we know that

\varphi^{\textup{MC}\textup{MC}}=\varphi\quad\text{and}\quad\Pi(\partial_{\textup{MC}}\varphi)=1.

Since

We have already seen that

being

-convex means precisely that its lift

is lower semi-continuous, law-invariant and convex, and the converse is also true.
Therefore, 2 and 3 are equivalent.
∎

Proposition 3.2
.

(2)

consists of a single element which is again of Monge type.

Proof.

The characterization of transport-regularity of

simply follows from applying

4.1
to the current setting. More specifically, we let

and

has by

\{\nu\}=\partial_{\textup{MC}}\varphi(\mu)\implies\exists!\pi\in\textup{Cpl}_{\rm opt}(\mu,\nu)\text{ and }\pi\text{ is Monge}.

3.2.
Regularization by Brownian sheet

In this section we assume that
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})=([0,1],\mathfrak{B},\lambda)
. Then we may consider
C(0,1)
as a subset of
L^{2}([0,1],\mathfrak{B},\lambda)
. This allows us to consider the law of the
(d,d)
-Brownian sheet
\mathbb{W}
on
L^{2}([0,1]^{d},\mathfrak{B},\lambda;\mathbb{R}^{d})
(
d
dimensions and
d
parameters).

Lemma 3.3
.

The law of the
(d,d)
-Brownian sheet admits a.s. independent randomization, i.e.,

\text{for $\mathbb{W}$ a.e.\ $X\in L^{2}([0,1]^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})$, there exists an independent, continuous $Y\in L^{2}([0,1]^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})$}.

Proof.

We write
\rho_{X}:=(t\mapsto(t,X(t))_{\#}\lambda\in\mathcal{P}([0,1]^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
and denote by
\rho_{(X,x)}
a disintegration of
\rho_{X}
w.r.t. its second marginal.
By Lemma
2.17
it suffices to show that for
\mathbb{W}
-a.e.
X
, we have

\rho_{(X,x)}\text{ is continuous for $\rho_{X}$-a.e.\ }x.

By classical results on local time of Brownian sheets, see, for example
[
4
, Theorem 3.1 and preceding discussion]
, there exists a version of local time
L
for the Brownian sheet such that

\mathbb{R}^{d}\times[0,1]^{d}\ni(x,t)\mapsto L(x,[0,t_{1}]\times\dots\times[0,t_{d}])\in[0,\infty),

is
\mathbb{W}
-almost surely a continuous function. Hence, for fixed
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
t\mapsto L(X;x,[0,t_{1}]\times\dots[0,t_{d}])/L(X;x,[0,1]^{d})
is the multivariate CDF of a continuous measure on
[0,1]^{d}
, and this measure coincides (for
\rho_{X}
-a.e. x) with
\rho_{(x,X)}
as consequence of the occupation time formula.
Consequently,
\rho(x,X)
is for
\rho_{X}
-a.e.
x
continuous, which concludes the proof.
∎

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
\varphi:\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to\mathbb{R}
be
MC
-convex and let
Y\in L^{2}([0,1]^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})
be finitely valued.
Define
P\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
by

P(A)=\mathbb{W}(\{X\in L^{2}([0,1]^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d}):\mathcal{L}_{\lambda}(X+Y)\in A\})

for all measurable
A\subseteq\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, i.e.
P=(X\mapsto\mathcal{L}_{\lambda}(X+Y))_{\#}(\mathbb{W})
.
Then
P
-a.s.
|\partial_{\textup{MC}}\varphi|\leq 1
.

In particular,
P
is transport-regular.

Proof.

We need to show that
P
-almost surely the subdifferential of
\varphi
is a singleton.

To this end, we first fix a bounded set
A\subseteq\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and define the
MC
-convex potential

\varphi_{A}(\mu):=\sup_{\nu\in A}\textup{MC}(\mu,\nu)-\varphi^{\textup{MC}}(\nu)=(\varphi^{\textup{MC}}+\chi_{A})^{\textup{MC}}(\mu).

We have that
\varphi_{A}\leq\varphi
, and
(\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\times A)\cap\partial_{\textup{MC}}\varphi\subseteq\partial_{\textup{MC}}\varphi_{A}
.
Furthermore, since
A
is bounded,
\varphi_{A}
is Lipschitz continuous because

\varphi_{A}(\mu)-\varphi_{A}(\mu^{\prime})\leq\sup_{\nu\in A}\textup{MC}(\mu,\nu)-\textup{MC}(\mu^{\prime},\nu)\leq\mathcal{W}_{2}(\mu,\mu^{\prime})\sup_{\nu\in A}\Big(\int|y|^{2}\,d\nu\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}},

for all
\mu,\mu^{\prime}\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
Clearly, this also provides Lipschitz continuity of
\overline{\varphi}_{A}
.
Hence, using that
X+Y
is under
\mathbb{W}
distributed according to a non-degenerate Gaussian on
L^{2}([0,1]^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})
, we find that
X\mapsto\overline{\varphi}_{A}(X+Y)
is
\mathbb{W}
-almost surely Gateaux differentiable, see
[
33
]
.
Furthermore, we have as consequence of Lemma
3.3
and as
Y
takes only finitely many values, that for a.e.
X\in L^{2}([0,1]^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
X+Y
admits an independent continuous randomization.
Hence, we can apply
Proposition

2.19
and obtain that for
\mathbb{W}
-a.e.
X

\{\mathcal{L}(Z):Z\in\partial\overline{\varphi}_{A}(X+Y)\}=\partial_{\textup{MC}}\varphi_{A}(\mathcal{L}_{\lambda}(X+Y)).

As Gateaux differentiability of
\overline{\varphi}_{A}(X)
yields that
\partial\varphi_{A}(\mathcal{L}(X))
is a singleton, we get that
\partial_{\textup{MC}}\varphi_{A}(\mu)
is a singleton for
P
-almost every
\mu
.

Next, we consider the set

N:=\{\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):\partial_{\textup{MC}}\varphi(\mu)\text{ contains more than 1 element}\}.

Since
\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
can be covered by a countable sequence of bounded sets, it suffices to show that

N_{B}:=\{\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):\partial_{\textup{MC}}\varphi(\mu)\cap B\text{ contains more than 1 element}\},

where

is bounded, is a

-null set.
As observed in the first part of the proof, we have the inclusion

(\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\times B)\cap\partial_{\textup{MC}}\varphi\subseteq\partial_{\textup{MC}}\varphi_{B}.

Since
\partial_{\textup{MC}}\varphi_{B}(\mu)
is a singleton for
P
-almost every

, we conclude that

.

Finally, we observe that

is transport-regular by

3.2
.
∎

Theorem 3.5
.

The set of transport-regular measures in
\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
is dense.

Proof.

Fix a measure
Q\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
.
Furthermore, by an approximation argument we can assume that
Q
is supported on finitely supported measures in
\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
Consider a probability space
(\tilde{\Omega},\tilde{\mathcal{F}},\tilde{\mathbb{P}})
supporting independent random variables
X,\Gamma
taking values in
L^{2}([0,1]^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})
where
X
is finitely valued and
\mathcal{L}_{\lambda}(X)\sim Q
as well as
\Gamma\sim\mathbb{W}
.

For
\varepsilon>0
, consider the random variable
X_{\varepsilon}:=X+\varepsilon\Gamma
and denote the induced law on
\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
by
Q_{\varepsilon}:=\mathcal{L}_{\tilde{\mathbb{P}}}(\mathcal{L}_{\lambda}(X_{\varepsilon}))
.
Clearly, we have for
\varepsilon\searrow 0

X_{\varepsilon}\to X\quad\text{in }L^{2}([0,1]^{d},\mathbb{R}^{d}),

pointwise on
\tilde{\Omega}
, which yields that

\mathcal{L}_{\lambda}(X_{\varepsilon})\to\mathcal{L}_{\lambda}(X)\quad\text{in }\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),

pointwise on
\tilde{\Omega}
.
Since by independence of
X
and
\Gamma
,
\mathbb{E}_{\tilde{\mathbb{P}}}[\|X_{\varepsilon}\|^{2}]=\mathbb{E}_{\tilde{\mathbb{P}}}[\|X\|^{2}]+\varepsilon^{2}
, we conclude that
Q_{\varepsilon}\to Q
in
\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
.

It remains to show that

is transport-regular.
To this end, note that

is the mixture of the transport-regular measures
\mathcal{L}_{\tilde{\mathbb{P}}}(\mathcal{L}_{\lambda}(X_{\varepsilon})|X)
.
Clearly, being transport-regular is closed under taking mixtures, which concludes the proof.
∎

4.
On the Baire property and transport-regularity

In this section, we let
(\mathcal{X},d)
be a Polish metric space such that
\mathcal{W}_{p}
-bounded sets in
\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathcal{X})
are tight,
and let
c:\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{X}\to\mathbb{R}
be a continuous cost function such that
|c(x,y)|\leq C(1+d(x,x_{0})^{p}+d(y,y_{0})^{p})
for some
C>0
and
x_{0},y_{0}\in\mathcal{X}
.
We write
\textup{Cpl}_{\rm opt}^{c}(\mu,\nu)
for the set of couplings that are optimal for the transportation problem between
\mu
and
\nu
with cost
c
, that is,

V_{c}(\mu,\nu):=\inf_{\pi\in\textup{Cpl}(\mu,\nu)}\int c(x,y)\,\pi(dx,dy).

A measure
\mu
is the called
c
-transport-regular if, for every
\nu\in\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathcal{X})
there is a unique optimal transport plan from
\mu
to
\nu
and this plan is of Monge type.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathcal{X})
. Then
\mu
is
c
-transport-regular if and only if for all
c
-concave
\varphi:\mathcal{X}\to(-\infty,+\infty]

(11)

\mu(A)=0\quad\text{where }A:=\{x\in\mathcal{X}:\partial_{c}\varphi(x)\text{ contains more than one point}\}.

Proof.

Assume that
(
11
)
holds for all
c
-concave
\varphi
.
By the fundamental theorem of optimal transport there is for each
\nu\in\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathcal{X})
an optimal
c
-concave potential
\varphi
such that

\pi(\partial^{c}\varphi)=1,\quad\pi\in\textup{Cpl}_{\rm opt}^{c}(\mu,\nu).

Hence,
\pi\in\textup{Cpl}_{\rm opt}^{c}(\mu,\nu)
is Monge, which in particular means that
\textup{Cpl}_{\rm opt}^{c}(\mu,\nu)
is a singleton.

Now, assume that
\mu
is transport-regular.
As the set

\{(x,y_{1},y_{2})\in\mathcal{X}^{3}:y_{1},y_{2}\in\partial_{c}\varphi(x),\,y_{1}\neq y_{2}\}

is analytic, there exists by the Jankov-von Neumann uniformization theorem an analytically measurable selection
h=(h_{1},h_{2}):A\to\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{X}
, and observe that
h_{1}\neq h_{2}
by construction.
We use these maps to define subprobability measures
\mu_{i}:=(h_{i})_{\#}\mu(\cdot\cap A)
for
i=1,2
.
By inner regularity, there exists a compact set
K\subseteq\mathcal{X}
such that
\mu_{i}(K)>\mu(K)/2
.
Define the potential
\varphi_{K}:=(\varphi^{c}+\chi_{K})^{c}
, which satisfies

\varphi_{K}\geq\varphi\text{ and }\varphi_{K}(x)+\varphi^{c}_{K}(y)=c(x,y)\text{ for all }(x,y)\in\partial_{c}\varphi\cap(\mathcal{X}\times K),

and as
K
is compact,
\partial_{c}\varphi_{K}(x)\neq\emptyset
for all
x\in\mathcal{X}
.
Let
g
be an analytically measurable selector of
\partial_{c}\varphi_{K}
, and set

g_{i}(x)=\begin{cases}h_{i}(x)&x\in A\cap h_{1}^{-1}(K)\cap h_{2}^{-1}(K),\\
g(x)&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Then we have that

\pi:=\frac{1}{2}\big((x\mapsto(x,g_{1}(x)))_{\#}\mu+(x\mapsto(x,g_{2}(x)))_{\#}\mu\big)

has first marginal
\mu
, its second marginal
\nu\in\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathcal{X})
is concentrated on
K
, and
\pi(\partial_{c}\varphi_{K})=1
.
Since
\mu_{i}(K)>\mu(A)/2
, we have
\mu(A\cap h_{1}^{-1}(K)\cap h_{2}^{-1}(K))\geq\mu(A)-\mu_{1}(K)-\mu_{2}(K)>0
.
Hence, we have constructed a coupling
\pi\in\textup{Cpl}_{\rm opt}^{c}(\mu,\nu)
that is not Monge, which contradicts
c
-transport-regularity of
\mu
.
∎

We introduce functionals to quantify how far a pair of measures
(\mu,\nu)
is from admitting a unique optimal transport map between them. Based on this, we will be able to quantify how far a measure is from being
c
-transport-regular.
We introduce the quantities

\tau^{c}(\mu,\nu):=\sup_{\pi\in\textup{Cpl}_{\rm opt}^{c}(\mu,\nu)}\int\mathrm{Var}(\pi_{x})\,\mu(dx),\quad\text{and}\quad\tau_{R}^{c}(\mu):=\sup_{\nu:\mathcal{W}_{p}(\nu,\delta_{x_{0}})\leq R}\tau(\mu,\nu),

where the variance for probabilities in
\nu\in\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathcal{X})
is defined as

\widehat{{\rm Var}}(\nu):=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}2^{-n}{\rm Var}((f_{n})_{\#}\nu),

where
(f_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a family of continuous and point-seperating functions on
\mathcal{X}
bounded by 1, and

{\rm Var}(\rho)=\int|x-x^{\prime}|^{2}\,\rho\otimes\rho(dx,dx^{\prime}),

for
\rho\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
.

Lemma 4.2
.

For
\mu,\nu\in\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathcal{X})
we have:

(1)

There is a unique
\pi\in\textup{Cpl}_{\rm opt}^{c}(\mu,\nu)
and
\pi
is Monge iff
\tau^{c}(\mu,\nu)=0
;

(2)

\mu
is
c
-transport-regular iff
\tau^{c}_{R}(\mu)=0
for all
R>0
.

Proof.

To see (1), first note that
\widehat{\mathrm{Var}(\nu)}>0
if and only if
\nu
is not a Dirac measure. Indeed, if
\nu
is not a Dirac measure then, as
(f_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is point-separating, there exists
n\in\mathbb{N}
, such that
(f_{n})_{\#}\nu
is not a Dirac measure, which yields that

\widehat{\mathrm{Var}(\nu)}\geq 2^{-n}\mathrm{Var}((f_{n})_{\#}\nu)>0.

Hence,
\tau^{c}(\mu,\nu)=0
iff for every
\pi\in\textup{Cpl}_{\rm opt}^{c}(P,Q)
we have that
\pi_{x}
is
\mu
-almost surely a Dirac.
Since
\textup{Cpl}_{\rm opt}^{c}(\mu,\nu)
is a convex set, this yields that
\textup{Cpl}_{\rm opt}^{c}(\mu,\nu)
contains a single transport plan and this plan is Monge.

To see (2), note that by the first part we have for every
\nu\in\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathcal{X})
a unique optimal transport plan
\pi\in\textup{Cpl}_{\rm opt}^{c}(\mu,\nu)
, which means that
\mu
is
c
-transport-regular.
∎

Lemma 4.3
.

The map
\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathcal{X})\times\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathcal{X})\to\mathbb{R}:(\mu,\nu)\mapsto\tau^{c}(\mu,\nu)
is
\mathcal{W}_{p}
-upper semi-continuous.

Proof.

We first show that

\widehat{\rm Var}:\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathcal{X})\to\mathbb{R},

is bounded, continuous and concave.
Clearly,
{\rm Var}
is continuous and concave on
\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
as
{\rm Var}(\rho)=\int x^{2}\,\rho(dx)-(\int x\,\rho(dx))^{2}
.
Since
(f_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a point-seperating family of continuous functions on
\mathcal{X}
bounded by
1
, we have that
\widehat{\rm Var}
is the sum of concave and continuous functions on
\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathcal{X})
bounded by 1, from where we infer the claimed properties of
\widehat{\rm Var}
.

Next, we invoke
[
6
, Proposition 2.8]
to find that the map

\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{X})\to\mathbb{R}:\pi\mapsto\int\widehat{\rm Var}(\pi_{x})\,\pi(dx),

is upper semi-continuous, concave and bounded.

Finally, consider sequences
(\mu^{n})_{n}
and
(\nu^{n})_{n}
and measures
\mu^{\infty},\nu^{\infty}\in\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathcal{X})
with
\mathcal{W}_{p}(\mu^{n},\mu)\to 0
and
\mathcal{W}_{p}(\nu^{n},\nu)\to 0
. Let
\pi^{n}\in\textup{Cpl}_{\rm opt}^{c}(\mu^{n},\nu^{n})
be such that

\int\mathrm{Var}(\pi^{n}_{x})\,\pi^{n}(dx)\geq\tau(\mu^{n},\nu^{n})-1/n.

As
\{\mu_{n}:n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}\}
and
\{\nu_{n}:n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}\}
are
\mathcal{W}_{p}
-compact, we have that

A:=\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{+\infty\}}\textup{Cpl}(\mu^{n},\nu^{n})

is
\mathcal{W}_{p}
-compact as immediate consequence of Prokhorov’s theorem and the characterization of
\mathcal{W}_{p}
-convergence, see
[
35
, Definition 6.8]
. Therefore, there is
\pi\in A
such that, after possibly passing to a subsequence,
W_{p}(\pi^{n},\pi)\to 0
. By continuity of the map which assigns a couplings its marginals, we have
\pi\in\textup{Cpl}(\mu,\nu)
. As the coupling
\pi^{n}
are optimal between its marginals, the same holds true for
\pi
, i.e.,
\pi\in\textup{Cpl}_{\rm opt}^{c}(\mu,\nu)
. This is because optimality is characterized in terms of
c
-cyclical monotonicity of the support and the latter is preserved under weak limits (see e.g.
[
35
, Theorem 5.20]
).

All in all, we have

\tau^{c}(\mu,\nu)\geq\int\widehat{\rm Var}(\pi_{x})\,\pi(dx)\geq\limsup_{n}\int\widehat{\rm Var}(\pi^{n}_{x})\,\pi^{n}(dx)=\limsup_{n}\tau^{c}(\mu^{n},\nu^{n}).\qed

.

For

, the map

The proof is line by line as for

for some

and the

. While this set is not

has the property that families of measures with bounded

To see this, further note that weak convergence suffices to guarantee that for any limit point

of a sequence

and

5.
A Brenier theorem for the adapted Wasserstein distance

5.1.
Failure of an adapted Brenier theorem for absolutely continuous measures

The adapted Wasserstein distance has many convenient properties as a distance between stochastic processes. However, no adequate counterpart of Brenier’s theorem has been established so far. Indeed a naive attempt fails already in the first non-trivial instance of one-dimensional state space and two time periods:

Example 5.1
.

There exist absolutely continuous, compactly supported probabilities on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
such that the adapted transport problem for the quadratic costs admits no Monge type solution:

Let
\mu_{1}=\nu_{1}
be the uniform probability measure on

\big[-\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}},\,\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\big],

and define
\mu,\nu
on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
by

\mu(dx_{1},dx_{2}):=\mu(dx_{1})q_{a(x_{1})}(dx_{2})),\quad\nu(dy_{1},dy_{2}):=\nu(dy_{1})q_{b(y_{1})}(dy_{2}))

where we write
q_{x}
for the uniform distribution on
[x,x+1]
and

a(x):=\begin{cases}\sqrt{2}\,x-1,&x\geq 0,\\[4.0pt]
\sqrt{2}\,x+1,&x<0,\end{cases}\qquad b(y):=\tfrac{y}{\sqrt{2}}.

By the dynamic programming principle the squared adapted Wasserstein distance of
\mu,\nu
is given by

(12)

\displaystyle\mathcal{AW}^{2}(\mu,\nu)

\displaystyle=\inf_{\pi_{1}\in\textup{Cpl}(\mu_{1},\nu_{1})}\int(x_{1}-y_{1})^{2}+W_{2}^{2}(q_{a(x_{1})},q_{b(y_{1})})\,d\pi_{1}(x_{1},y_{1})=

(13)

\displaystyle=\inf_{\pi_{1}\in\textup{Cpl}(\mu_{1},\nu_{1})}\int c(x_{1},y_{1})\,d\pi_{1}(x_{1},y_{1}),

where
c(x,y)=(x-y)^{2}+\bigl(a(x)-b(y)\bigr)^{2}
. The one dimensional transport problem (
13
) admits an elementary solution.
Indeed,
the optimal transport plan between
\mu_{1}
and
\nu_{1}
is of Monge type and is uniquely given
\mu_{1}
-a.e. by the transport map

T_{1}(x)=\begin{cases}2x-\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}},&x\geq 0,\\[4.0pt]
2x+\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}},&x<0.\end{cases}

and the dual maximizers are given by

\varphi_{1}(x)=\tfrac{1}{2}-x^{2},\qquad\psi_{1}(y)=\tfrac{1}{2}y^{2},\qquad x,y\in\left[-\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}},\,\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\right].

It is straightforward to verify this using the complementary slackness conditions.

It follows that the unique optimal bi-adapted transport between
\mu
and
\nu
is induced by the map
T(x_{1},x_{2})=(T_{1}(x_{1}),x_{2}+b(T_{1}(x_{1}))-a(x_{1}))
.

Crucially, the map
T_{1}
is not invertible on
\big[-\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}},\,\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\big],
hence there exists not optimal transport map from
\nu_{1}
to
\mu_{1}
and hence also no Monge solution for the adapted transport problem from
\nu
to
\mu
.

5.2.
Brenier theorem on
\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}))

Theorem 5.2
.

The set of transport regular measures in
\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}))
is dense
G_{\delta}
.

Below we sketch how to modify the arguments given above to show denseness of transport regular measures in this setting. The idea is based on exploiting the following embedding

(14)

\displaystyle\iota:\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}))\to\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})):(x,p)\mapsto\delta_{x}\otimes\mu

Note that
\iota(\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R})))
is closed and totally convex. Indeed, it is precisely the laws of random variables contained in the subspace

V:=\{X\in L^{2}(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P};\mathbb{R}^{2}):X_{1}\text{ is constant}\}\cong\mathbb{R}\times L^{2}(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P};\mathbb{R}).

Note that we can extend functions on
V
to functions on
L^{2}(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P};\mathbb{R}^{2})
by letting them be constant along
V^{\perp}
, i.e. given
f:V\to(-\infty,+\infty]
we can extend to to
g:L^{2}(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P};\mathbb{R}^{2})\to(-\infty,+\infty]
by setting
g(X_{1},X_{2})=f(\mathbb{E}[X_{1}],X_{2})
. On the level of functions on probability measures, this corresponds to extending
\varphi:\mathbb{R}\times\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R})\to(-\infty,+\infty]
to
\psi:\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\to(-\infty,+\infty]
as

(15)

\displaystyle\psi(\mu)=\varphi(\mathrm{mean}(\textup{proj}_{1\#}\mu),\textup{proj}_{2\#}\mu).

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
\psi:\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to(-\infty,+\infty]
. Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

\psi
is of form (
15
)

(2)

\textup{dom}(\psi^{\textup{MC}})\subseteq\iota(\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}))

(3)

\overline{\psi}
is constant in direction of
V^{\perp}

(4)

\textup{dom}(\overline{\psi^{\textup{MC}}})\subseteq V

For
X\in V
we say that
X
satisfies independent randomization if there exists
U\in V
that is independent of
X
.

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
\psi:\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to(-\infty,+\infty]
be of the form
\psi(\mu)=\varphi(\mathrm{mean}(\textup{proj}_{1\#}\mu),\textup{proj}_{2\#}\mu)
and
\mu\in\iota(\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R})))
Then
\partial_{\textup{MC}}\psi(\mu)\subseteq\iota(\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R})))
and the following are equivalent:

(1)

|\partial_{\textup{MC}}\psi(\mu)|\leq 1

(2)

\partial\overline{\varphi}(X)
contains one element at some
X\sim\mu
which satsfies independent randomization.

Next, we record the analog of
Theorem

3.1
in the present setting:

Proposition 5.5
.

Let
P,Q\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}))
and
\Pi\in\textup{Cpl}(P,Q)
. Then, the following are equivalent:

(1)

\Pi
is optimal;

(2)

there exists
\varphi:\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to(-\infty,+\infty]
L-convex and of the form (
15
) with
\Pi(\partial_{\textup{MC}}\varphi)=1
;

(3)

there exist
\overline{\varphi}:V\to(-\infty,+\infty]
lower semi-continuous, law-invariant, convex and random variables
X,Y
with values in
L^{2}(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
such that

(\mathcal{L}(X),\mathcal{L}(Y))\sim\Pi\quad\text{and}\quad(X,Y)\in\partial\overline{\varphi}\text{ almost surely.}

Sketch of the proof of
Theorem

5.2
.

We need to show that every
P\in\iota(\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}))
can be approximated by transport regular measure in
\iota(\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}))
. The fact that this set is
G_{\delta}
follows from
Theorem

4.5
.

First observe that if
\gamma
is a regular Gaussain measure on
V
with

\gamma(\{X:X\text{ allows for independent randomization}\})=1,

then
\mathcal{L}_{\#}\gamma\in\iota(\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}))
is transport regular. Indeed, this is a straightforward modification of the
arguments on the proof of
Lemma

3.4
. Crucially note that dual optimizers are of form (
15
) (see
Proposition

5.5
(2)) and that for such functional differentiability along direction of
V
is sufficient to guarantee that the
MC
-subdifferential contains at most one element (see
Lemma

5.4
). Further note that such a measure exists as
V\cong\mathbb{R}\times L^{2}(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P};\mathbb{R})
and we can the the product of the measure
P
constructed in
Lemma

3.4
and a one-dimensional Gaussian.

The rest of the proof follows line by line as the proof of
Theorem

3.5
.
∎

5.3.
Baire–Brenier theorem on
\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})

For
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
we consider the disintegration
\mu(dx_{1},dx_{2})=\mu_{1}(dx_{1})\mu^{x_{1}}(dx_{2})
and define the embedding

(16)

\displaystyle J:\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\to\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R})):\mu\mapsto(x_{1}\mapsto(x_{1},\mu^{x_{1}}))_{\#}\mu_{1}

Note that
J(\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2}))
is precisely the set of measures in
\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}))
that is supported by the graph of a Borel function
f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
and that this set is a dense
G_{\delta}
set, see
[
7
]
.

We say that
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
is adapted transport regular if there exists a unique optimal bicausal coupling and it is of Monge type.

Theorem 5.6
.

The set of adapted transport regular measures is a dense
G_{\delta}
set.

Proof.

We first note that the set of
P\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}))
that are transport-regular is
G_{\delta}
by
Theorem

5.2
and that
J(\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2}))
is a dense
G_{\delta}
subset of
\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}))
by
[
7
]
. Hence, the set of
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
such that
J(\mu)
is transport-regular is dense
G_{\delta}
.

Let
\nu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
and write
P=J(\mu)
and
Q=J(\nu)
and let
f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
such that
P(\textup{graph}(f))=1
. As
P
is transport-regular, there is
T:\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that
(\textup{id},T)_{\#}P\in\textup{Cpl}(P,Q)
is optimal and for
P
-a.e.
(x,p)
there is
S_{x,p}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
such that
(\textup{id},S_{x,p})_{\#}p\in\textup{Cpl}(p,\textup{proj}_{2}(T(x,p))
is the unique optimal coupling.

We use this to construct a triangular map
\mathsf{T}:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
such that
(\textup{id},\mathsf{T})_{\#}\mu
is an optimal bicausal coupling between
\mu
and
\nu
. To this end we set

\displaystyle\mathsf{T}_{1}(x_{1})=\textup{proj}_{1}(T(x_{1},f(x_{1})))\qquad\mathsf{T}_{2}(x_{1},x_{2})=S_{x_{1},f(x_{1})}(x_{2}).

The uniqueness of the optimal bicausal coupling follows from the one-to-one correspondence between bicausal couplings and couplings in the nested spaces established in
[
7
]
.
∎

Responsiveness of the Persian version of the patient assessment of constipation quality of life and Wexner constipation scoring system questionnaires in individuals with dyssynergia defecation following physiotherapy intervention
Authors: Fatemeh Oraki koohshoori, Nahid Pirayeh, Abazar Parsi, Neda Orakifar
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractAim: To investigate the responsiveness of the Persian version of the Patient Assessment of Constipation Quality Of Life (PAC-QOL) and Wexner questionnaires in individuals with dyssynergia defecation (DD) following physiotherapy intervention.Background: Dyssynergic defecation, a common functional disorder, impairs quality of life. While PAC-QOL and Wexner questionnaires are standard outcome measures, their responsiveness in Persian-speaking individuals with this condition remains unevaluated.Methods:The responsiveness of the PAC-QOL and Wexner questionnaires were evaluated in 95 patients with dyssynergic defecation following physiotherapy intervention. All participants in the study were evaluated through quality of life outcome tools in the first physiotherapy session and again after 5 weeks of physiotherapy treatment (at least fifteen sessions, every other day). The Patient Assessment of Constipation Quality of Life (PAC-QOL) and Wexner constipation scoring system were selected as treatment outcomes measuring before and after sessions 15. Responsiveness was examined using anchor-based (Global Rating of Change [GRC]) and distribution-based indices, including the area under the ROC curve (AUC), minimal clinically important change (MIC), effect size (ES), standardized response mean (SRM), and Guyatt’s responsiveness index (GRI).Results: Both questionnaires showed excellent responsiveness. The PAC-QOL achieved an AUC = 0.92 (95 % CI 0.87–0.99) and the Wexner an AUC = 0.97 (95 % CI 0.94–0.99). The corresponding MICs were 34.5 and 3.5. Internal responsiveness indices were large (PAC-QOL ES = 2.19; SRM = 1.63; GRI = 1.45 / Wexner ES = 2.03; SRM = 1.85; GRI = 1.60).Conclusion: The Persian PAC-QOL and Wexner questionnaires are highly responsive and capable of detecting clinically meaningful changes in DD after physiotherapy. These tools can reliably monitor treatment outcomes in Persian speaking populations and inform clinical and research practice in Persian-speaking populations.Key Words: PAC-QOL, Wexner, Responsiveness, Physiotherapy, Dyssynergic defecation, MIC

Introduction

Constipation is a common functional gastrointestinal disorder characterized by infrequent bowel movements, straining, or a sensation of incomplete evacuation (1-3). This health issue is common globally, with an epidemiological rate ranging from 0.7% to 79% (median = 16%) (4) and reported in Iran between 1.4 and 37% (5). Several factors and characteristics have been identified as potential risk factors for the development of constipation. Among them are being of female gender, being at the upper end of the age range, particularly beyond 65 years, possessing a lower education level, and having a low socio-economic status (6-9).

The most common defecation disorder is dyssynergic defecation (DD) affecting more than half of the patients suffering from defecation disorder. The inability to coordinate the contraction of abdominal muscles, anal sphincter, and pelvic floor muscles (puborectalis muscle) results in dyssynergic bowel movements during defecation, instead of the anal sphincter and puborectalis muscle relaxing while the abdominal muscles contract, these muscles inappropriately contract, obstructing the defecation process (6-8).

The precise cause of DD remains unclear; however, it is generally believed to be an acquired unconscious behavioral disorder resulting from improper toileting habits such as prolonged sitting on the toilet and inadequate childhood training concerning defecation. This challenges the belief that it stems from organic or neurogenic diseases (6). This hypothesis is partially supported by the observation that patients can learn to effectively relax their pelvic floor muscles through biofeedback training. Evidence also suggests that DD can result from several factors or abnormalities, including pregnancy and childbirth, trauma especially back injury, neurogenic disorders of the brain-intestinal axis, low rectal sensitivity, slow transit constipation, anxiety or psychological stress, and a history of sexual abuse. (6, 10, 11).

While other types of constipation tend to affect older individuals more frequently, the higher prevalence of chronic constipation and DD among young people imposes considerable healthcare costs on society’s economy and has numerous negative effects on the individual's social and professional relationships. It also affects patients' quality of life, and for this reason, leading to an interest in developing and using questionnaires that study patients' quality of life (12). Several studies have utilized general (not specific) quality-of-life tools to assess constipation; however, the Patient Assessment of Constipation Quality of Life (PAC-QOL) and Wexner questionnaires display greater sensitivity in evaluating quality of life among patients with constipation and dyssynergic defecation (13-15).

The Wexner questionnaire, a self-report questionnaire commonly employed to assess the severity and prevalence of constipation, is developed based on over 100 symptoms related to constipation. The questionnaire exhibits strong correlations with physiological findings obtained from diagnostic tests commonly employed in evaluating constipation. These tests include anorectal manometry, colon transit time, defecography, and electromyography (13, 14). Furthermore, Marquis et al. designed a quality of life evaluation questionnaire for patients with constipation to evaluate the quality of life. The PAC-QOL is a widely used questionnaire that is a well-established tool for evaluating the daily performance of individual health. This widely used questionnaire serves as a direct means for gastroenterologists to assess the impact of constipation on patients' quality of life (15).

Although initial treatment for constipation typically involves lifestyle modifications such as consuming adequate fiber (20-30 grams daily), maintaining proper hydration (1.5-2 liters daily), and engaging in regular physical activity (16), drug therapy and pelvic floor muscle physiotherapy are often recommended if these initial measures prove unsuccessful in managing the condition (17). Among physiotherapeutic approaches, biofeedback therapy, electrical stimulation, and pelvic floor exercises have been identified as effective strategies for the management of constipation (18-20). Evaluating the response rate to treatment is a critical objective in clinical and therapeutic settings. Responsiveness is an essential attribute of a measurement scale, as it plays a vital role in identifying clinically significant changes in a patient's health status over the course of treatment or disease progression. Also, it serves as a valuable outcome measure for assessing treatment effectiveness and supporting evidence-based clinical decisions (21). Therapists need to recognize real and significant changes to express the effect of treatment and distinguish between patients whose health status has improved, worsened, or remained stable (21, 22). Another reason to understand the importance of responsiveness in a tool is that, with the availability of responsive tools, it is possible to compare the effects of various rehabilitation and surgical interventions on the patient's recovery process, thus identifying the most and least effective therapeutic interventions (21, 22).

Responsiveness encompasses two primary components: internal responsiveness and external responsiveness. Internal responsiveness pertains to an instrument's capacity to identify changes that occur within a given time frame, irrespective of their clinical relevance. To determine internal responsiveness, an instrument is typically administered before and after an intervention, and its ability to detect clinically meaningful changes over time is evaluated. External responsiveness, on the other hand, indicates how much the change in the measuring instrument corresponds to the change in the reference measuring instrument at a certain point in time. To evaluate external responsiveness, researchers commonly employ two indices: Receiver Operating Characteristic (ROC) and correlation. In the present study, the reference measurement tool utilized for this purpose was the seven-item retrospective general rating scale of Likert (21, 22). This seven-item scale is regarded as the gold standard measure due to its independence from any specific disorder. It is widely used in studies as a comprehensive scale to gather patients' perspectives on their recovery progress following therapeutic interventions (21, 22). To evaluate the recovery rate in patients with constipation during the treatment process, it is necessary to evaluate and study the consequences of the patient's symptoms and quality of life. In clinical trial studies, the PAC-QOL and Wexner questionnaires have been frequently employed as important outcome measures to evaluate the recovery progress of patients with constipation and the effect of treatment; Moreover, the Persian adaptations of the Wexner and PAC-QOL questionnaires have demonstrated strong reliability and validity for Iranian individuals suffering from constipation (13-15). Despite the widespread use of the PAC-QOL and Wexner questionnaires in clinical trials, no studies have specifically examined their responsiveness in constipation patients. Therefore, the current study aimed to determine the responsiveness and minimum clinically significant change of the Wexner and PAC-QOL questionnaires in response to physiotherapy treatment in patients with DD.

Methods

Study design

This study was a non-controlled, non-randomized, pre–post psychometric evaluation designed to assess the responsiveness and minimal clinically important change (MIC) of the Persian PAC-QOL and Wexner questionnaires, rather than treatment efficacy. The design followed the COSMIN (COnsensus-based Standards for the selection of health Measurement INstruments) checklist, which provides methodological standards for studies evaluating the measurement properties of patient-reported outcome measures.

Participants

A total of 122 patients diagnosed with dyssynergic defecation (DD) were screened for eligibility between March 2023 and April 2024 across participating physiotherapy clinics. Of these, 20 individuals did not meet inclusion criteria (13 due to secondary causes of constipation, 4 due to recent pelvic surgery, and 3 due to comorbid neurological conditions), and 8 declined participation. The remaining 102 participants provided written informed consent and were enrolled. During the 5-week physiotherapy period, 7 participants withdrew (3 due to scheduling difficulties, 2 because of unrelated medical issues, and 2 who discontinued physiotherapy before completing 10 sessions). Consequently, 95 participants (69 women, 26 men) completed all sessions and post-intervention assessments, forming the final analytic sample for responsiveness evaluation (Figure 1).

Participant flow diagram (CONSORT-style).

Participants were included in the study based on the following criteria: 1- both women and men aged 18 years or older who were experiencing constipation and had not responded adequately to previous pharmaceutical treatments and lifestyle modifications (17), 2- participants who had experienced constipation symptoms for at least 12 months prior to the study, 3- reporting at least two of the Rome IV problems (18) including: a) straining or experiencing discomfort during least one-fourth of defecations, b) lumpy or hard stools for at least one-fourth of stool cases, c) feeling of incomplete evacuation for at least one-fourth of defecation cases, d) sensation of rectal blockage or anorectal obstruction in at least one-fourth of defecation cases, e) manual maneuvers to promote at least one-fourth of bowel movements (e.g. digital evacuation, digital rectal stimulation, pelvic floor support), f) fewer than 3 spontaneous bowel movements per week, g) loose stools are infrequently observed in the absence of laxative use, and h) insufficient standards for the diagnosis of irritable bowel syndrome. The exclusion criteria included: 1- uncontrolled cardiovascular, kidney, and liver diseases (19), 2- having a history of back, hip, and/or hip joint dysfunction, spinal cord injury, diabetes mellitus, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (20-23), 3- experiencing constipation caused by medicine, mental and neurological diseases (20, 24, 25), 4- metabolic, endocrine, neurological disorders, history of pelvic surgery, pregnancy, active bowel disease and fissure (20, 26, 27), and 5- prolapse of the pelvic organs (28).

Before initiating the study, all participants provided a written informed consent form approved by the Ethics Committee of Ahvaz Jundishapur University of Medical Sciences (IR.AJUMS.REC.1403.241).

Procedure

To assess the responsiveness of the PAC-QOL and Wexner questionnaire, all participants in the study underwent an initial assessment during their first physiotherapy session. Following 5 weeks of physiotherapy treatment, consisting of fifteen sessions conducted every other day, participants were reassessed using the same quality-of-life outcome tools. In addition, at the final treatment session, a reference questionnaire (retrospective seven-item Likert) was given to the patients to report the change in their health status from the beginning of the intervention to the final treatment session. The seven-item Likert scale responses were collapsed into two distinct categories to create a dichotomous outcome variable. The "improved" category encompassed patients who reported their health status as "very much better" (score of 7), "much better" (score of 6), while the "not improved" category included those who reported "slightly better" (score of 5), "no change" (score of 4), "slightly worse" (score of 3), "much worse" (score of 2), or "very much worse" (score of 1). Patients’ responses to this global rating of change (GRC) question were used as an external anchor to evaluate the responsiveness of the questionnaires, which served as a reference measurement criterion. The primary objective of this study was to characterize the psychometric properties (responsiveness) of the outcome instruments rather than to determine the clinical efficacy of the physiotherapy intervention. As such, the physical therapy protocol was not compared to a control group (29).

All participants received a standardized multimodal physiotherapy program specifically designed for the management of dyssynergic defecation (DD). The protocol was developed by pelvic floor rehabilitation specialists and applied uniformly across participating clinics to ensure consistency in treatment delivery.

Each participant attended fifteen physiotherapy sessions over five weeks (three sessions per week, every other day). Each session lasted approximately 60–75 minutes and was conducted by licensed physiotherapists trained in the standardized DD rehabilitation protocol. Attendance and adherence were recorded for every session, and participants who completed fewer than 15 sessions were excluded from analysis.

Components of the physiotherapy program included: Transcutaneous Posterior Tibial Nerve Stimulation (TTNS), Biofeedback Therapy (BFT), Pelvic Floor Muscle Training (PFMT) with Diaphragmatic Breathing.

A portable TENS unit delivering biphasic square-wave pulses at 10 Hz frequency and 200 μs, pulse width for 30 minutes per session. One surface electrode was placed 5 cm above the medial malleolus (over the posterior tibial nerve), and the second on the medial arch of the same foot. Intensity was adjusted to elicit a visible motor response (flexion of the great toe or fanning of the toes) without discomfort. TTNS was used to modulate pelvic floor neuromuscular activity and improve anorectal coordination (30, 31).

Biofeedback training (BFT) was performed using surface electromyography (EMG) and/or anorectal manometry systems to provide real-time visual feedback on pelvic floor muscle (PFM) activity.

The protocol was developed by the research team based on established biofeedback principles for dyssynergic defecation (32, 33).

During the initial sessions (1–5), participants received education on defecation physiology, awareness of PFM contraction and relaxation, and practiced voluntary relaxation of the external anal sphincter during simulated defecation.

The intermediate sessions (6–10) emphasized coordination between abdominal and pelvic floor muscles, correct straining techniques, and relaxation strategies.

The final sessions (11–15) focused on reinforcing sustained contractions, appropriate timing, and complete relaxation during defecation simulation.

Each BFT session lasted approximately 30 minutes and was conducted in a quiet environment with individualized supervision by an experienced physiotherapist.

Additionally, participants engaged in a structured protocol of pelvic‑floor muscle contractions and relaxations synchronised with diaphragmatic breathing exercises. The programme emphasized gentle contraction of the pelvic floor during exhalation and active relaxation during inhalation, thereby promoting coordinated activation of the diaphragm, abdominal wall and pelvic‑floor musculature to support effective defecation (34-36).

The study outcomes were assessed using the following evaluation tools:

The PAC-QOL questionnaire is a concise yet thorough instrument designed to assess constipation by evaluating daily individual health and functioning. The contents of the PAC-QOL questionnaire are directly derived from the impact of constipation on the patient as perceived by gastrointestinal specialists. The PAC-QOL questionnaire demonstrated strong validity in Europe and North America (13). It comprises 28 self-reported items that examine the impact of constipation on the patient's quality of life over a two-week period. This questionnaire is divided into four subscales on physical discomfort, eight items regarding psychosocial discomfort, five items concerning treatment satisfaction, and 11 subscales related to worries and discomfort. Responses are recorded on a Likert scale from 0 to 4, with higher scores indicating greater negative impacts on quality of life. Items 25, 26, 27, and 28 require reverse scoring as they reflect a positive nature (15). The validity and reliability of this questionnaire within the Iranian population were assessed by Nikjoy et al. in 2018. They reported an overall Cronbach’s alpha of 0.92 (range 0.72–0.92) and an overall intra-class correlation coefficient of 0.88 (range 0.69–0.87), indicating satisfactory internal consistency and test–retest reliability (37).

The Wexner Constipation Scoring System includes 8 items: frequency of bowel movements, painful defecation, incomplete emptying, abdominal discomfort, attempt duration, assistance type for evacuation, unsuccessful attempts within 24 hours, and the time spent on evacuation. Each component is rated on a scale from 0 to 4, except for 'assistance required for defecation,' which is rated on a scale from 0 to 2. The total score is derived from the sum of all component scores. Scoring is from 0 to 30, which means the best condition and no symptoms of constipation, and 30 means the most severe constipation condition (14). The validity and reliability of this questionnaire were evaluated by Mirbharsti et al. in 2020, with values of 0.66 and 0.85, respectively (38).

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were conducted using SPSS version 23.0 (IBM Corp., Chicago, IL) and Microsoft Excel 2018, with the significance level set at p < 0.05. The normality of the data distribution was evaluated using the Shapiro–Wilk test and Q–Q plots. Variables meeting normality assumptions were analyzed using paired t-tests, whereas non-normally distributed data were assessed with the Wilcoxon signed-rank test. These analyses were performed on the entire sample, as well as on the two subgroups: patients who showed improvement (i.e., very much better and much better) and those who did not show improvement (i.e., slightly better, no change, slightly worse, much worse, and very much worse). External responsiveness was assessed using both correlation and Receiver Operating Characteristic (ROC) curve analyses, following COSMIN guidelines. The Spearman correlation coefficient quantified the association between change scores for each questionnaire (PAC-QOL and Wexner) and the Global Rating of Change (GRC) scale. Correlations ≥0.60 were considered strong, 0.30–0.59 moderate, and <0.30 weak (39).

In the ROC analysis, the change scores of each patient-reported outcome questionnaire were compared against the global rating-of-change scale, which served as the external anchor variable. For both instruments, change scores were computed as Baseline – Post-intervention values, so that positive change values indicate improvement (i.e., reduction in symptom severity or improvement in quality of life). In ROC analysis, patients were categorized into two groups: participants who “improved” (scores 6–7) and those who “did not improve” (scores 1–5) (39, 40). During the next stage, using multiple change scores as cutoff points, sensitivity and specificity were calculated for each outcome measure. In the analysis of the ROC curve, the sensitivity (true positive rate) was assigned to the vertical (Y) axis, while 1-specificity (false positive rate) was represented along the horizontal (X) axis for all conceivable cutoff score variations. From the ROC curve analysis, two significant statistics were derived: the area under the curve (AUC) and the MCIC (Minimal Clinically Important Change) (39, 41-43). The AUC quantified each instrument’s ability to discriminate between these two groups. AUC values of 0.70–0.79 indicate acceptable, 0.80–0.89 good, and ≥0.90 excellent responsiveness (39, 44). The MIC was derived from the ROC curve using the Youden Index (45), and 95% confidence intervals (CIs) were calculated (46).

In addition to anchor-based analyses, internal responsiveness was evaluated using three distribution-based indices: Effect Size (ES), Standardized Response Mean (SRM), and Guyatt’s Responsiveness Index (GRI). ES was calculated as the mean change score divided by the baseline standard deviation (ES = (mean_post − mean_pre) / SD_baseline). SRM was calculated as the mean change score divided by the standard deviation of change scores (SRM = mean_Δ / SD_Δ). Guyatt’s responsiveness index was computed as the mean change among participants categorized as “improved” on the Global Rating of Change (GRC) divided by the standard deviation of change in participants categorized as “stable/not improved” (GRI = mean_Δ_improved / SD_Δ_stable). The ES and SRM values of 0.20, 0.50 and 0.80 were interpreted as small, moderate and large responsiveness, respectively (47).

Discussion

This methodological study evaluated the responsiveness and minimal clinically important change (MIC) of the Persian versions of the PAC-QOL and Wexner questionnaires in patients with dyssynergic defecation (DD) undergoing standardized physiotherapy. Both instruments demonstrated excellent responsiveness, with AUC values above 0.90 and large internal responsiveness indices (ES and SRM > 1.5), confirming their sensitivity to detect clinically meaningful changes over time. The observed pre–post differences in questionnaire scores reflect the instruments’ responsiveness in detecting patient-perceived change, rather than evidence of physiotherapy effectiveness, as the study was not designed as a controlled or randomized clinical trial.

Responsiveness is a fundamental psychometric property of outcome measures and is equally important as reliability and validity. According to the COSMIN framework, responsiveness reflects an instrument’s ability to detect change in a construct when a change has truly occurred. In clinical practice, an outcome measure that lacks responsiveness may fail to capture improvement or deterioration even when patients experience genuine change, thereby limiting its value for decision-making and treatment monitoring. A responsive questionnaire allows clinicians to interpret score differences as reflecting real clinical progress rather than random variation or measurement error. Hence, establishing responsiveness strengthens both the scientific utility and the clinical interpretability of the PAC-QOL and Wexner questionnaires.

The current findings indicate that both Persian instruments are highly capable of detecting patient-perceived improvements following physiotherapy. The observed effect sizes (ES = 2.19 for PAC-QOL and 2.03 for Wexner) and SRMs (1.63 and 1.85, respectively) represent large responsiveness values according to conventional benchmarks. The MIC values of 34.5 for PAC-QOL and 3.5 for Wexner provide practical reference points for clinicians to interpret whether an observed score change reflects a meaningful improvement from the patient’s perspective.

Although the responsiveness of these instruments had not previously been evaluated in Iranian populations, prior studies have confirmed their reliability and validity. (Nikjooy et al., 2018) (37) reported high internal consistency and reproducibility of the Persian PAC-QOL. The Persian version of the Wexner questionnaire was validated by (Mirbehresi et al., 2020) (38) and (Majidirad et al., 2021) (48), showing strong reliability and concurrent validity. Studies in other populations also support these tools: (Marquis et al., 2005) (15) demonstrated that PAC-QOL is sensitive to clinically meaningful changes in chronic constipation, and (Frank et al., 2001) (49) showed the Wexner score can differentiate between severity levels and treatment response. Additional translations and validations in other languages, such as Japanese (Tsunoda et al., 2016) (50) and Hindi (Mishra et al., 2021) (51), further confirm the global applicability and robustness of these instruments.

Although both the PAC-QOL and Wexner questionnaires have demonstrated strong reliability and validity in Persian-speaking patients with functional and chronic constipation, these validation studies were not conducted specifically among individuals with dyssynergic defecation (DD). Given that DD is a recognized subtype of functional constipation with overlapping symptom domains, the use of these instruments in the current study is considered appropriate; the psychometric properties of the tools in the DD subgroup should be confirmed in future targeted validation studies.

Implications

Although the physiotherapy intervention was used only as a context to elicit measurable change, the study demonstrates that both questionnaires can sensitively capture symptom and quality-of-life changes typical of DD management. For clinicians, these findings suggest that the PAC-QOL and Wexner can be used to monitor progress and evaluate rehabilitation outcomes in Persian-speaking populations. For researchers, the established MIC thresholds allow for accurate sample-size calculations, effect-size estimation, and determination of clinically meaningful treatment response in future trials or longitudinal studies.

Limitation

This study has several important limitations. First, this study used a single-group pre–post design without a control group, randomization, or blinding, which limits causal inference. The physiotherapy intervention served only as a standardized context to elicit change for evaluating measurement responsiveness; however, spontaneous recovery, placebo effects, or participant expectations may also have contributed to improvement in scores. Future studies should consider including control or comparison groups to confirm that observed changes reflect true clinical improvement rather than nonspecific effects. Second, although treatment delivery was standardized across sites, inter-clinic and therapist variability in intervention fidelity, participant adherence, and session execution could have influenced the magnitude of score changes. Future research should incorporate treatment fidelity monitoring, therapist identifiers, or hierarchical modeling to account for site-level effects and to ensure that responsiveness is not confounded by differences in implementation. Third, the study did not statistically adjust for potential confounding variables such as age, sex, educational level, comorbidities, or lifestyle factors (diet, physical activity, medication use). These factors may influence both constipation severity and quality-of-life outcomes. Incorporating multivariable statistical approaches—such as Analysis of Covariance (ANCOVA), Linear Mixed Models (LMMs), or Generalized Estimating Equations (GEE)—would help control for these covariates and strengthen interpretability. Fourth, the sample size (N = 95), while adequate for detecting large effect sizes, was relatively modest and exhibited class imbalance between the “improved” and “not improved” anchor groups. This imbalance may have led to optimistic estimates of the AUC and MIC values. Future studies should perform internal validation using bootstrapping or repeated stratified k-fold cross-validation to correct for overfitting and confirm the robustness and generalizability of responsiveness indices. Fifth, the anchor-based approach relied on the retrospective Global Rating of Change (GRC) scale, which may be subject to recall bias. Although internal responsiveness indices (Effect Size, SRM, Guyatt’s Index) were included to mitigate this limitation, future research should complement subjective anchors with objective clinical criteria (e.g., anorectal manometry, balloon expulsion test, stool diaries) to strengthen criterion validity. Sixth, while the Persian PAC-QOL and Wexner questionnaires have previously demonstrated validity and reliability in patients with chronic or functional constipation, these psychometric properties have not been independently established in individuals with dyssynergic defecation. Given that DD represents a distinct physiological subtype, further studies should specifically assess the factor structure, measurement invariance, and item performance of these tools in this subgroup. Finally, this study evaluated responsiveness only over a short-term, five-week intervention period. Long-term responsiveness and stability remain untested. Future multicenter and longitudinal studies should assess responsiveness over extended follow-up periods and examine the ability of these instruments to detect both sustained improvement and relapse.

Conclusion

In conclusion, the Persian versions of the PAC-QOL questionnaire and the Wexner scale demonstrated excellent responsiveness and effectively detected statistically and clinically meaningful changes in bowel function following physiotherapy in individuals with dyssynergic defecation. The MIC values were 34.5 for PAC-QOL and 3.5 for the Wexner scale, providing practical thresholds for clinical application. These findings offer valuable guidance for clinicians and researchers working with Persian-speaking patients with dyssynergic defecation.

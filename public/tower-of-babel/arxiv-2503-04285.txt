Abstract
. We introduce the space SBV X of special functions with bounded X X -variation in Carnot-Carathéodory spaces and study its main properties. Our main outcome is an approximation result, with respect to the BV X topology, for SBV X functions.

1.
Introduction

Functions with Bounded Variation (BV), and in particular their subclass of
special
functions with Bounded Variation (SBV), provide a natural framework for studying problems involving discontinuities, such as image processing, signal analysis, and variational problems.
Over recent years, a considerable effort was put into the study of BV functions in Carnot-Carathéodory (CC) spaces. The aim of this paper is to contribute to this area of research by introducing the space
\operatorname{SBV}_{X}
of special functions with bounded
X
-variation and studying its properties. In particular, we extend to the setting of CC spaces the following approximation result for classical SBV functions proved by G. de Philippis, N. Fusco and A. Pratelli in
[
19
]
.

Theorem 1.1

(
[
19
, Theorem A]
)
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be an open set and let
u\in\operatorname{SBV}(\Omega)
. Then, there exists a sequence of functions
u_{k}\in\operatorname{SBV}(\Omega)
and of compact
C^{1}
-manifolds
M_{k}\subset\subset\Omega
such that
\mathcal{J}_{u_{k}}\subseteq M_{k}\cap\mathcal{J}_{u}
,
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\overline{\mathcal{J}_{u_{k}}}\setminus\mathcal{J}_{u_{k}})=0
and

\left\|u_{k}-u\right\|_{\operatorname{BV}(\Omega)}\xrightarrow{k\to+\infty}0,\qquad u_{k}\in C^{\infty}(\Omega\setminus\overline{\mathcal{J}_{u_{k}}}).

Recall that smooth functions are not dense in BV with respect to the BV topology

Smooth functions are dense in BV only with respect to the so-called strict topology in BV, see e.g.
[

.
, as their closure in BV is the Sobolev space
W^{1,1}
, i.e., BV functions whose derivatives admits no singular part (not even “nice” jumps) with respect to the Lebesgue measure. In this sense, Theorem
1.1
provides a class of “nice” (although, clearly, not smooth) BV functions that are dense in SBV with respect to the BV topology. As explained in
[
19
]
, this result is sharp and, besides being interesting
per se
, it led to the proof of a conjecture by L. Ambrosio, J. Bourgain, H. Brezis and A. Figalli
[
4
]
(see also
[

]
) about a BMO-type characterization of BV functions.

Before stating our main result we need to briefly introduce the notion of special function of bounded variation in CC spaces.
A
Carnot-Carathéodory space
(see Definition
2.1
) is the space
\mathbb{R}^{n}
endowed with a distance arising from a collection
X=(X_{1},\dots,X_{m})
of smooth and linearly independent vector fields satisfying the Hörmander condition. In this paper, we will deal with
equiregular
CC spaces, where a homogeneous dimension
Q
, usually larger than the topological dimension
n
, can be defined. The space
\operatorname{BV}_{X}(\Omega)

[
13
,
28
]
of functions with bounded
X
-variation (see Definition
2.2
) consists of those functions
u
on an open set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
whose derivatives
X_{1}u,\dots,X_{m}u
in the sense of distributions are represented by a vector-valued measure
D_{X}u
with finite total variation
|D_{X}u|
. The space
\operatorname{BV}_{X}
has been the subject of intensive studies: see
[
11
,
16
,
27
,
29
,
30
,
34
,
35
,
14
,
17
,
45
]
and the more recent
[

,

15
,

,

,

,

]
.

In the classical Euclidean setting the space of SBV functions, first introduced in
[
18
]
, naturally arises in the study of free discontinuity problems.
The first contribution of this paper is the introduction of special functions with Bounded Variation in CC spaces (
\operatorname{SBV}_{X}
functions). Recall (
[
25
]
) that, if
u\in\operatorname{BV}_{X}(\Omega)
, then
D_{X}u
can be decomposed as

D_{X}u=D^{{\operatorname{ap}}}_{X}u\>\mathcal{L}^{n}+D^{s}_{X}u=D^{{\operatorname{ap}}}_{X}u\>\mathcal{L}^{n}+D^{j}_{X}u+D^{c}_{X}u,

where
D^{\operatorname{ap}}_{X}u
is the approximate
X
-gradient of
u
,
\mathcal{L}^{n}
is the usual Lebesgue measure,
D_{X}^{s}u
is the singular part of
D_{X}u
,
D^{j}_{X}u
is the jump part of
D_{X}^{j}u
, and
D_{X}^{c}u
is the Cantor part of
D_{X}u
. See Section
2
for precise definitions.

Definition 1.2
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be an open subset of an equiregular Carnot-Carathéodory space
(\mathbb{R}^{n},X)
and let
u\in\operatorname{BV}_{X}(\Omega)
. We say that
u
is a
special function of bounded
X
-variation
, and we write
u\in\operatorname{SBV}_{X}(\Omega)
, if

(i)

D_{X}^{c}u=0
, and

(ii)

the jump set
\mathcal{J}_{u}
of
u
is a countably
X
-rectifiable set.

A set is said to be countably
X
-rectifiable (see Definition
2.12
) if it can be covered, up to a set which is negligible with respect to the Hausdorff measure
\mathcal{H}^{Q-1}
, by a countable family of
C^{1}_{X}
-hypersurfaces (Definition
2.11
), that provide the intrinsic counterpart in CC spaces of classical
C^{1}
-hypersurfaces. Recall that, for classical BV functions, the jump set is always countably rectifiable; on the contrary, in CC spaces this – i.e., the validity of condition (ii) above for every
\operatorname{BV}_{X}
function
u
– is an important open problem. Let us however recall that, if the CC space satisfies the so-called
property
\mathcal{R}
(“rectifiability”, see Definition
2.17
), then condition (ii) in Definition
1.2
is automatically satisfied for every
u\in\operatorname{BV}_{X}
; see
[
25
, Theorem 1.5]
.
There is a multitude of examples of CC spaces which satisfy property
\mathcal{R}
, such as Heisenberg groups, step 2 Carnot groups and Carnot groups of type
\star
, see
[
25
, Theorem 4.3]
. In this paper we tried to work in the widest possible generality, hence the extra requirement (ii) in Definition
1.2
.
For this reason, let us also stress that our definition might be a priori different from the definition of SBV function in metric measure spaces. We refer to
[
8
]
for a general overview of SBV functions in metric measure spaces and to
[
37
]
for an approximation result for BV functions via SBV functions in this context.

In Section
3
we study several properties of
\operatorname{SBV}_{X}
(or locally
\operatorname{SBV}_{X}
) functions: we collect the main ones in the following theorem, which summarizes (some of) the results stated in Proposition

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be an open subset of an equiregular Carnot-Carathéodory space
(\mathbb{R}^{n},X)
; then, the following statements hold:

(i)

u\in\operatorname{SBV}_{X,\operatorname{loc}}(\Omega)
if and only if
D_{X}^{s}u=f\>\nu_{R}\>\mathcal{H}^{Q-1}\mathop{\hbox{\vrule height=7.0pt,width=0.5pt,depth=0.0pt\vrule height=0.5pt,width=6.0pt,depth=0.0pt}}\nolimits R
for some countably
X
-rectifiable set
R\subset\Omega
with horizontal normal
\nu_{R}
and some
f\in L^{1}_{\operatorname{loc}}(R,\mathcal{H}^{Q-1})
;

(ii)

\operatorname{SBV}_{X}(\Omega)
is a closed subspace of
\operatorname{BV}_{X}(\Omega)
;

(iii)

the space
\operatorname{SBV}_{\operatorname{loc}}(\Omega)
of special function of (Euclidean) locally bounded variation is contained in
\operatorname{SBV}_{X,\operatorname{loc}}(\Omega)
;

(iv)

for every
w\in L^{1}_{\operatorname{loc}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})
there exists
u\in\operatorname{SBV}_{X,\operatorname{loc}}(\Omega)
such that
D^{{\operatorname{ap}}}_{X}u=w
a.e. in
\Omega
;

(v)

for every countably
X
-rectifiable set
R\subseteq\Omega
oriented by
\nu_{R}
, every
\theta\in L^{1}(\mathcal{H}^{Q-1}\mathop{\hbox{\vrule height=7.0pt,width=0.5pt,depth=0.0pt\vrule height=0.5pt,width=6.0pt,depth=0.0pt}}\nolimits R)
and every
\delta>0
there exists
u\in\operatorname{SBV}_{X}(\Omega)
such that

D_{X}^{j}u\equiv\theta\,\nu_{R}\,\mathcal{H}^{Q-1}\mathop{\hbox{\vrule height=7.0pt,width=0.5pt,depth=0.0pt\vrule height=0.5pt,width=6.0pt,depth=0.0pt}}\nolimits R,\quad\left\|u\right\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}<\delta,\quad\text{ and }\quad|D_{X}u|(\Omega)\leq(1+\delta)\left\|\theta\right\|_{L^{1}(\mathcal{H}^{Q-1}\mathop{\hbox{\vrule height=7.0pt,width=0.5pt,depth=0.0pt\vrule height=0.5pt,width=6.0pt,depth=0.0pt}}\nolimits R)}.

Statements
(iii),\ (iv)
and
(v)
, in particular, provide meaningful subclasses or examples of special functions of bounded
X
-variation which, in particular, turn out to form a quite large and interesting space.

We can now to state our main result.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
\Omega
be an open subset of an equiregular Carnot-Carathéodory space
(\mathbb{R}^{n},X)
and let
u\in\operatorname{SBV}_{X}(\Omega)
. Then, there exists a sequence of functions
(u_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\operatorname{SBV}_{X}(\Omega)
and of
C^{1}_{X}
-hypersurfaces
(M_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\Omega
such that, for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
\mathcal{J}_{u_{k}}\subseteq M_{k}\cap\mathcal{J}_{u}
,
\mathcal{J}_{u_{k}}
is compact, and

\left\|u-u_{k}\right\|_{\operatorname{BV}_{X}(\Omega)}\xrightarrow{k\to+\infty}0,\qquad u_{k}\in C^{\infty}(\Omega\setminus\mathcal{J}_{u_{k}}).

Our proof of Theorem
1.4
differs from the one of Theorem
1.1
in that, rather than using mollifications with variable kernel as in
[
19
]
, we exploit a partition-of-the-unity argument (reminiscent of
[
10
,
28
,
47
,
48
,
20
]
) that allows to approximate
u
out of a fixed compact set
C_{k}
.
A posteriori,
the set
C_{k}
coincides with the jump set
\mathcal{J}_{u_{k}}
, which in particular turns out to be compact itself, thus providing a slight improvement in Theorem
1.1
.

We believe that Theorem
1.4
will play a role in a possible, future BMO-type characterization of
\operatorname{BV}_{X}
functions
à la
Ambrosio-Bourgain-Brezis-Figalli,
[
4
]
: this, in fact, was one of the original motivations of our work, and will be the subject of further investigations.

2.
Definitions and preliminary results

Definition 2.1
.

Let
1\leq m\leq n
be integers and let
X=(X_{1},\dots,X_{m})
be a
m
-tuple of smooth and linearly independent vector fields on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. We say that an absolutely continuous curve
\gamma\colon[0,T]\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
is an
X
-subunit path
joining
p
and
q
if
\gamma(0)=p
,
\gamma(T)=q
and there exist
h_{1},\dots h_{m}\in L^{\infty}([0,T])
such that
\sum_{j=1}^{m}h_{j}^{2}\leq 1
and

\gamma^{\prime}(t)=\sum_{j=1}^{m}h_{j}(t)X_{j}(\gamma(t)),\quad\text{for a.e.\ $t\in[0,T]$.}

For every
p,q\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
we define

d(p,q)\coloneqq\inf\{T>0:\text{ there exists an $X$-subunit path $\gamma$ joining $p$ and $q$}\},

where we agree that
\inf\emptyset\coloneqq+\infty
.

By the Chow–Rashevskii Theorem (see for instance
[
1
, Subsection 3.2.1]
), if for every
p\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
the linear span of all iterated commutators of the vector fields
X_{1},\dots,X_{m}
computed at
p
has dimension
n
(i.e.
X_{1},\dots,X_{m}
satisfy the
Hörmander condition
), then
d
is a distance: the latter means that for every couple of points of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
there always exists a
X
-subunit path joining them. In this case we say that
(\mathbb{R}^{n},X)
is a
Carnot-Carathéodory space
of
rank

m
and
d
is the associated
Carnot-Carathéodory distance
.

For every
p\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and for every
i\in\mathbb{N}
we denote by
\mathfrak{L}^{i}(p)
the linear span of all the commutators of
X_{1},\dots,X_{m}
up to order
i
computed at
p
. We say that a Carnot-Carathéodory space
(\mathbb{R}^{n},X)
is
equiregular
if there exist natural numbers
n_{0},n_{1},\dots,n_{s}
such that

0=n_{0}<n_{1}<\cdots<n_{s}=n\text{ and }\dim\mathfrak{L}^{i}(p)=n_{i},\quad\forall p\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\forall i\in\{1,\dots,n\}.

The natural number
s
is called
step
of the Carnot-Carathéodory space. If
(\mathbb{R}^{n},X)
is equiregular, then the
homogeneous dimension
is
Q\coloneqq\sum_{i=1}^{s}i(n_{i}-n_{i-1})
.

Notation
.

In the following,
\mathbf{(\mathbb{R}^{n},X)}
denotes an equiregular Carnot-Carathéodory space associated with the family
\mathbf{X=(X_{1},\dots,X_{m})}
.
We use
d
to denote the Carnot-Carathéodory distance associated with
X
,
B(\cdot,\cdot)
to denote the associated open balls,
\mathcal{H}^{k}
to denote the associated Hausdorff
k
-measure and
\mathcal{S}^{k}
to denote the associated spherical Hausdorff
k
-measure; on the other hand we will denote by
d_{E}
the usual Euclidean distance, by
B_{E}(\cdot,\cdot)
the associated open balls, by
\mathcal{H}^{k}_{E}
the associated Hausdorff
k
-measure and by
\mathcal{S}^{k}_{E}
the associated spherical Hausdorff
k
-measure. By
\Omega\subseteq(\mathbb{R}^{n},X)
we denote a fixed open set and by
Q
we denote the homogeneous dimension of
(\mathbb{R}^{n},X)
. Later we will also use the following notation:

•

for every
1\leq i\leq m
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
we write

X_{i}(x)=(a_{i,1}(x),\dots,a_{i,n}(x))

where
a_{i,t}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for
1\leq t\leq n
;

•

for every
1\leq i\leq m
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
we write

(\operatorname{div}X_{i})(x)\coloneqq\sum_{t=1}^{n}\frac{\partial a_{i,t}}{\partial x_{t}}(x)

•

for every
1\leq i\leq m
,
\varphi\in C^{1}(\Omega)
and

(X_{i}\varphi)(x)\coloneqq\sum_{t=1}^{n}a_{i,t}(x)\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial x_{t}}(x);

•

for every
1\leq i\leq m
we denote by
X_{i}^{*}
the formal adjoint of
X_{i}
, i.e., for every
\varphi\in C^{1}(\Omega)
,
x\in\Omega
we write

(X_{i}^{*}\varphi)(x)\coloneqq\sum_{t=1}^{n}\frac{\partial(a_{i,t}\varphi)}{\partial x_{t}}(x);

•

given a Radon measure
\mu
, we use the notation

\fint_{A}ud\mu\coloneqq\frac{1}{\mu(A)}\int_{A}ud\mu,

to denote the average integral of a measurable function
u
on a
\mu
-measurable set
A
with
\mu(A)>0
;

•

we will consider a Riemannian metric, namely a smoothly varying family of scalar products
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{p}=\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
which makes the horizontal vectors
X_{1},\dots,X_{m}
orthonormal at every point
p\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Definition 2.2
.

We say that
u\in L^{1}_{\operatorname{loc}}(\Omega)
is a
function of locally bounded
X
-variation
, and we write
u\in\operatorname{BV}_{X,\operatorname{loc}}(\Omega)
, if there exists a
\mathbb{R}^{m}
-valued Radon measure
D_{X}u=(D_{X_{1}}u,\dots D_{X_{m}}u)
on
\Omega
such that, for every open set
A\subset\subset\Omega
, for every
1\leq i\leq m
and for every
\varphi\in C^{1}_{c}(A)
one has

\int_{A}\varphi d(D_{X_{i}}u)=-\int_{A}uX_{i}^{*}\varphi d\mathcal{L}^{n}.

Moreover, if
u\in L^{1}(\Omega)
and
D_{X}u
has bounded total variation
|D_{X}u|
, then we say that
u
has
bounded
X
-variation
and we write
u\in\operatorname{BV}_{X}(\Omega)
.

Definition 2.3
.

For every
u\in\operatorname{BV}_{X}(\Omega)
we define the norm

\left\|u\right\|_{\operatorname{BV}_{X}(\Omega)}\coloneqq\left\|u\right\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}+|D_{X}u|(\Omega).

The space
\operatorname{BV}_{X}(\Omega)
equipped with the above norm is a Banach space.

Definition 2.4
.

For every
u\in\operatorname{BV}_{X}(\Omega)
we decompose

D_{X}u=D_{X}^{a}u+D_{X}^{s}u

where
D_{X}^{a}u
denotes the
absolutely continuous part
of
D_{X}u
(with respect to the usual Lebesgue measure
\mathcal{L}^{n}
) and
D_{X}^{s}u
denotes the
singular part
of
D_{X}u
.

Definition 2.5
.

We say that a measurable set
E\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
has
locally finite
X
-perimeter
(respectively,
finite
X
-perimeter
) in
\Omega
if its characteristic function
\chi_{E}
belongs to
\operatorname{BV}_{X,\operatorname{loc}}(\Omega)
(respectively,
\chi_{E}\in\operatorname{BV}_{X}(\Omega)
).
In such a case we define the
X
-perimeter measure

P^{X}_{E}
of
E
as
P^{X}_{E}:=|D_{X}\chi_{E}|
.

Definition 2.6
.

Let
\Omega\subseteq(\mathbb{R}^{n},X)
be an open set and
f\colon\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
. We say that
f\in C^{1}_{X}(\Omega)
if
f
is continuous and its
horizontal gradient

Xf\coloneqq(X_{1}f,\dots,X_{m}f)
, in the sense of distributions, is represented by a continuous function.

Definition 2.7
.

Let
u\in L^{1}_{\operatorname{loc}}(\Omega)
,
z\in\mathbb{R}
and
p\in\Omega
. We say that
z
is the
approximate limit
of
u
at
p
if

\lim_{r\to 0}\fint_{B(p,r)}|u-z|d\mathcal{L}^{n}=0.

If the approximate limit of
u
at
p
exists, it is also unique (see
[
25
, Definition 2.19]
). We hence denote by
u^{\star}(p)
the approximate limit of
u
at
p
and by
\mathcal{S}_{u}
the subset of points in
\Omega
where
u
does not admit an approximate limit.

Definition 2.8
.

Let
u\in L^{1}_{\operatorname{loc}}(\Omega)
and
p\in\Omega\setminus\mathcal{S}_{u}
. We say that
u
is
approximately
X
-differentiable
at
p
if there exist a neighbourhood
U\subset\Omega
of
p
and
f\in C^{1}_{X}(U)
such that
f(p)=0
and

\lim_{r\to 0}\fint_{B(p,r)}\frac{|u-u^{\star}(p)-f|}{r}d\mathcal{L}^{n}=0.

The set of points in
\Omega
where
u
is approximately
X
-differentiable is denoted by
\mathcal{D}_{u}
.
The vector
Xf(p)\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
is uniquely determined (see
[
25
, Proposition 2.30]
): we call it
approximate
X
-gradient
of
u
at
p
and we denote it by
D^{\operatorname{ap}}_{X}u(p)
. Similarly, we write
D^{\operatorname{ap}}_{X_{i}}u(p):=X_{i}f(p)
for every
i\in\{1,\dots,m\}
.

Let

-differentiable at

There exists

We now spend a few words about intrinsically
C^{1}
(or

) hypersurfaces and the notion of

-rectifiability.

Definition 2.11
.

We say that
S\subseteq(\mathbb{R}^{n},X)
is a
C^{1}_{X}
-hypersurface
if for every
p\in S
there exist
r>0
and
f\in C^{1}_{X}(B(p,r))
such that the following facts hold:

(i)

S\cap B(p,r)=\{q\in B(p,r):f(q)=0\}
,

(ii)

Xf\neq 0
on
B(p,r)
.

We define the
horizontal normal
to
S
at
p\in S
as

\nu_{S}(p)\coloneqq\frac{Xf(p)}{|Xf(p)|}.

Notice that
\nu_{S}(p)
is well defined up to a sign and, in particular, it does not depend on the choice of
f
, see
[
25
, Corollary 2.14]
.

Definition 2.12
.

Let
S\subseteq(\mathbb{R}^{n},X)
. We say that
S
is
countably
X
-rectifiable
if there exists a family
\{S_{h}:h\in\mathbb{N}\}
of
C^{1}_{X}
-hypersurfaces such that

\mathcal{H}^{Q-1}\left(S\setminus\bigcup_{h\in\mathbb{N}}S_{h}\right)=0.

Moreover, if
\mathcal{H}^{Q-1}(S)<+\infty
, we say that
S
is
X
-rectifiable
. We define the
horizontal normal
of a countably
X
-rectifiable set
S
at
p\in S
as

\nu_{S}(p)\coloneqq\nu_{S_{h}}(p)\text{ if }p\in S_{h}\setminus\bigcup_{k<h}S_{k}.

Notice that
\nu_{S}
is well defined, up to a sign,
\mathcal{H}^{Q-1}
-a.e., see
[
25
, Proposition 2.18]
.

The following Lemma provides an equivalent definition of
X
-rectifiability; although not difficult and probably quite known (see
[
36
, Lemma 2.4]
for a proof in Heisenberg groups), we include a proof for the sake of completeness.

Lemma 2.13
.

A set
R\subseteq(\mathbb{R}^{n},X)
is
X
-rectifiable if and only if, for every
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a
C^{1}_{X}
-hypersurface
S_{\varepsilon}\subseteq(\mathbb{R}^{n},X)
such that
\mathcal{H}^{Q-1}(S_{\varepsilon})<\infty
and

\mathcal{H}^{Q-1}(R\setminus S_{\varepsilon})<\varepsilon.

Proof.

Since
R
is
X
-rectifiable we can write

R\subseteq S_{0}\cup\bigcup_{i\in\mathbb{N}}S_{i}

where
S_{0}
is a
\mathcal{H}^{Q-1}
-negligible set and, for every
i\in\mathbb{N}
,
S_{i}
is a
C^{1}_{X}
-hypersurface. It is not restrictive to assume
\mathcal{H}^{Q-1}(S_{i})<\infty
for every
i\in\mathbb{N}
. For every
\varepsilon>0
there exists a positive integer
M
such that

\mathcal{H}^{Q-1}\left(R\setminus\bigcup_{i\leq M}S_{i}\right)<\frac{\varepsilon}{2}.

We define the
C^{1}_{X}
-hypersurface
S_{1}^{\prime}\coloneqq\{p\in S_{1}:d(p,\partial S_{1})>r_{1}\}
,
where
\partial S_{1}^{\prime}:=\overline{S_{1}^{\prime}}\setminus S_{1}^{\prime}
and
r_{1}
is chosen so that

\mathcal{H}^{Q-1}(R\cap\partial S_{1}^{\prime})=0\quad\text{ and }\quad\mathcal{H}^{Q-1}((R\cap S_{1})\setminus S_{1}^{\prime})<\frac{\varepsilon}{4}.

Let us prove that such
r_{1}
exists: for
r>0
we define the set

S_{1}^{\prime}(r)\coloneqq\{p\in S_{1}:d(p,\partial S_{1})>r\}.

Since
\{R\cap\partial S_{1}^{\prime}(r):r>0\}
is a family of uncountably many pairwise disjoint subsets of
R
and
\mathcal{H}^{Q-1}(R)<\infty
, then
\mathcal{H}^{Q-1}(R\cap\partial S_{1}^{\prime}(r))=0
for arbitrarily small
r>0
. Moreover, since
R\cap S_{1}
is the union of the nested sets
(R\cap S_{1})\setminus S_{1}^{\prime}(r)
, we have, by the continuity of measure, that

\mathcal{H}^{Q-1}((R\cap S_{1})\setminus S_{1}^{\prime}(r))\xrightarrow{r\to 0}0.

Reasoning by induction, for every
i=2,\dots,M
we can define the
C^{1}_{X}
-hypersurfaces

S_{i}^{\prime}=\left\{p\in S_{i}\setminus\bigcup_{j<i}\overline{S^{\prime}_{j}}:d\left(p,\partial\left(S_{i}\setminus\bigcup_{j<i}\overline{S_{j}^{\prime}}\right)\right)>r_{j}\right\},

where we used the fact that
S_{i}\setminus\bigcup_{j<i}\overline{S_{j}^{\prime}}
is a
C^{1}_{X}
-hypersurface and
r_{i}>0
is chosen so that

\mathcal{H}^{Q-1}(R\cap\partial S_{i}^{\prime})=0\quad\text{ and }\quad\mathcal{H}^{Q-1}\left(R\cap\left(S_{i}\setminus\bigcup_{j<i}\overline{S_{j}^{\prime}}\right)\setminus S_{i}^{\prime}\right)<\frac{\varepsilon}{2^{i+2}}.

Now consider
S_{\varepsilon}\coloneqq\bigcup_{i=1}^{M}S_{i}^{\prime}
, which is a
C^{1}_{X}
-hypersurface because it is union of finitely many
C^{1}_{X}
-hypersurface at positive distance from each other. Then

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{Q-1}(R\setminus S_{\varepsilon})

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{H}^{Q-1}\left(R\setminus\bigcup_{i\leq M}S_{i}\right)+\mathcal{H}^{Q-1}\left(R\cap\left(\bigcup_{i\leq M}S_{i}\right)\setminus\left(\bigcup_{j\leq M}S_{j}^{\prime}\right)\right)

\displaystyle<\frac{\varepsilon}{2}+\mathcal{H}^{Q-1}\left(\bigcup_{i\leq M}\left(\left(R\cap S_{i}\right)\setminus\bigcup_{j\leq M}S_{j}^{\prime}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}+\mathcal{H}^{Q-1}\left(\bigcup_{i\leq M}\left(R\cap\left(S_{i}\setminus\bigcup_{j\leq i}S_{j}^{\prime}\right)\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\varepsilon}{2}+\mathcal{H}^{Q-1}\left(\bigcup_{i\leq M}\left(R\cap\left(S_{i}\setminus\bigcup_{j<i}\overline{S_{j}^{\prime}}\right)\setminus S_{i}^{\prime}\right)\right)

\displaystyle<\varepsilon,

where we used that
\mathcal{H}^{Q-1}(R\cap\partial S_{j}^{\prime})=0
.
Finally we observe that

\mathcal{H}^{Q-1}(S_{\varepsilon})=\sum_{i\leq M}\mathcal{H}^{Q-1}(S_{i}^{\prime})\leq\sum_{i\leq M}\mathcal{H}^{Q-1}(S_{i})<\infty.

This proves one implication, the other one is trivial.
∎

Proposition 2.21

\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega}\varphi\xi d(D_{X_{i}}u)

Lemma 4.5

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
u,u_{k}
and

).
∎

4.2.

Fix

\displaystyle(X_{i}u_{k})(y)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell,s\in\mathbb{N}}\left[(D_{X_{i}}(\xi^{\ell,s}_{k}u)*K_{{\varepsilon^{\ell,s}_{k}}})(y)+R_{i}(\xi^{\ell,s}_{k}u,K_{{\varepsilon^{\ell,s}_{k}}};y)\right]=

Proposition 4.7
.

If

and

Proof.

We start by estimating

. Fix

: since
\sum_{\ell,s\in\mathbb{N}}X_{i}\xi^{\ell,s}_{k}\equiv 0

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell,s\in\mathbb{N}}S^{k,\ell,s}_{i}(y)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell,s\in\mathbb{N}}K_{{\varepsilon^{\ell,s}_{k}}}*(uX_{i}\xi^{\ell,s}_{k})(y)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell,s\in\mathbb{N}}K_{{\varepsilon^{\ell,s}_{k}}}*(uX_{i}\xi^{\ell,s}_{k})(y)-\sum_{\ell,s\in\mathbb{N}}(uX_{i}\xi^{\ell,s}_{k})(y)

Using (
17
) and the fact that

, we immediately obtain
\left\|\sum_{\ell,s\in\mathbb{N}}S^{k,\ell,s}_{i}\right\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\leq 1/k
. To estimate

we start by observing that for every
y\in\Omega\setminus C_{k}
one has

(28)

\displaystyle R^{k,\ell,s}_{i}(y)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\xi^{\ell,s}_{k}(x)u(x)\left((\operatorname{div}X_{i})(x)K_{{\varepsilon^{\ell,s}_{k}}}(x-y)-\langle X_{i}(y)-X_{i}(x),\nabla K_{{\varepsilon^{\ell,s}_{k}}}(x-y)\rangle\right)dx

\displaystyle=\sum_{t=1}^{n}\int_{\Omega}\xi^{\ell,s}_{k}(x)u(x)\bigg(\frac{\partial a_{i,t}}{\partial x_{t}}(x)K_{{\varepsilon^{\ell,s}_{k}}}(x-y)-(a_{i,t}(y)-a_{i,t}(x))\frac{\partial K_{{\varepsilon^{\ell,s}_{k}}}}{\partial x_{t}}(x-y)\bigg)dx

-norm of the second derivatives of

on
Z^{\ell,s}_{k}
. Replacing (
30

28
) we obtain:

\displaystyle R^{k,\ell,s}_{i}(y)=

\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{n}\int_{\Omega}\xi^{\ell,s}_{k}(x)u(x)\frac{\partial a_{i,t}}{\partial x_{t}}(x)K_{\varepsilon^{\ell,s}_{k}}(x-y)dx

\displaystyle-\sum_{t,h=1}^{n}\int_{\Omega}\xi^{\ell,s}_{k}(x)u(x)\frac{\partial a_{i,t}}{\partial x_{h}}(x)(y-x)_{h}\frac{\partial K_{\varepsilon^{\ell,s}_{k}}}{\partial x_{t}}(x-y)dx

\displaystyle R^{k,\ell,s}_{i}(y)=

\phantom{t\neq h}\end{subarray}}\sum_{t=1}^{n}\left\|\left(u\xi^{\ell,s}_{k}\frac{\partial a_{i,t}}{\partial x_{t}}\right)*W^{t}_{{\varepsilon^{\ell,s}_{k}}}\right\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}}_{(A)}+\underbrace{\sum_{\ell,s\in\mathbb{N}}\>\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}t,h=1\\

Proposition 4.8
.

For every
k\in\mathbb{N}
the function
u_{k}
defined in (
14
) satisfies the following properties:

(i)

u_{k}\in\operatorname{BV}_{X}(\Omega\setminus C_{k})
,

(ii)

u_{k}\in\operatorname{BV}_{X}(\Omega)
,

(iii)

u_{k}\in\operatorname{SBV}_{X}(\Omega)
.

Proof.

We know from Proposition
2.21
that for every
\ell,s\in\mathbb{N}
one has
u^{\ell,s}_{k}\in\operatorname{BV}_{X}(\Omega\setminus C_{k})
where, for the sake of brevity, we defined
u^{\ell,s}_{k}\coloneqq(\xi^{\ell,s}_{k}u)*K_{\varepsilon^{\ell,s}_{k}}
. To prove
u_{k}\in\operatorname{BV}_{X}(\Omega\setminus C_{k})
, it is enough to show that

(32)

\left\|\sum_{\ell,s\in\mathbb{N}}u^{\ell,s}_{k}\right\|_{\operatorname{BV}_{X}(\Omega\setminus C_{k})}=\left\|\sum_{\ell,s\in\mathbb{N}}u^{\ell,s}_{k}\right\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}+\left|D_{X}\left(\sum_{\ell,s\in\mathbb{N}}u^{\ell,s}_{k}\right)\right|(\Omega\setminus C_{k})<\infty.

As we mentioned before, thanks to (
20
),
u_{k}\in L^{1}(\Omega)
so that the first addend on the right hand side of (
32
) is finite. We are left to estimate the second term. Since
u_{k}\in C^{\infty}(\Omega\setminus C_{k})
for every
i\in\{1,\dots,m\}
, we have that

D_{X_{i}}u_{k}=X_{i}u_{k}\mathcal{L}^{n}\text{ on }\Omega\setminus C_{k},

meaning that

\displaystyle\left|D_{X_{i}}\left(\sum_{\ell,s\in\mathbb{N}}u^{\ell,s}_{k}\right)\right|(\Omega\setminus C_{k})=\left\|\sum_{\ell,s\in\mathbb{N}}X_{i}u^{\ell,s}_{k}\right\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}.

Hence, by the decomposition (
27
), we write

\left\|X_{i}u_{k}\right\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\leq\left\|\sum_{\ell,s\in\mathbb{N}}(\xi^{\ell,s}_{k}D_{X_{i}}u)*K_{{\varepsilon^{\ell,s}_{k}}}\right\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}+\left\|\sum_{\ell,s\in\mathbb{N}}S^{k,\ell,s}_{i}\right\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}+\left\|\sum_{\ell,s\in\mathbb{N}}R^{k,\ell,s}_{i}\right\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}.

Thanks to Proposition
4.7
, we are only left to prove the boundedness of the first term of the right hand side of the inequality above. Using
[
5
, Theorem 2.2 (b)]
we have

\displaystyle\left\|\sum_{\ell,s\in\mathbb{N}}(\xi^{\ell,s}_{k}D_{X_{i}}u)*K_{{\varepsilon^{\ell,s}_{k}}}\right\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\leq\sum_{\ell,s\in\mathbb{N}}\left\|(\xi^{\ell,s}_{k}D_{X_{i}}u)*K_{{\varepsilon^{\ell,s}_{k}}}\right\|_{L^{1}(A^{\ell,s}_{k})}\leq\sum_{\ell,s\in\mathbb{N}}|D_{X_{i}}u|(A^{\ell,s}_{k}+{\varepsilon^{\ell,s}_{k}}).

where we have written
A^{\ell,s}_{k}+{\varepsilon^{\ell,s}_{k}}
to denote

A^{\ell,s}_{k}+{\varepsilon^{\ell,s}_{k}}\coloneqq\bigcup_{x\in A^{\ell,s}_{k}}B_{E}(x,{\varepsilon^{\ell,s}_{k}}).

By Remark
4.3
we have

A^{\ell,s}_{k}+{\varepsilon^{\ell,s}_{k}}\subseteq A^{\ell,s}_{k}\cup A^{\ell,s+1}_{k}\cup A^{\ell,s-1}_{k}\cup A^{\ell-1,s}_{k}\cup A^{\ell+1,s}_{k}

so that

|D_{X_{i}}u|(A^{\ell,s}_{k}+\varepsilon^{\ell,s}_{k})\leq|D_{X_{i}}u|(A^{\ell,s}_{k}\cup A^{\ell,s+1}_{k}\cup A^{\ell,s-1}_{k}\cup A^{\ell-1,s}_{k}\cup A^{\ell+1,s}_{k}).

and, finally,

\sum_{\ell,s\in\mathbb{N}}|D_{X_{i}}u|(A^{\ell,s}_{k}+{\varepsilon^{\ell,s}_{k}})\leq 5\sum_{\ell,s\in\mathbb{N}}|D_{X_{i}}u|(A^{\ell,s}_{k})\leq 15\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}}|D_{X_{i}}u|(A^{\ell}_{k})\leq 45|D_{X_{i}}u|(\Omega)

which, together with the fact that
u\in\operatorname{BV}_{X}(\Omega)
, allows us conclude the boundedness of
\left\|u_{k}\right\|_{\operatorname{BV}_{X}(\Omega\setminus C_{k})}
.

We aim to prove that
u_{k}\in\operatorname{BV}_{X}(\Omega)
. Suppose first that, in addition to our previous assumptions, we have
u\in C^{\infty}(\Omega)
. Then
C_{k}=\emptyset
and clearly
u_{k}\in C^{\infty}(\Omega)\subset C^{1}_{X}(\Omega)
for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
, hence

\displaystyle|D_{X_{i}}u_{k}|(\Omega)

\displaystyle=\left\|X_{i}u_{k}\right\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\sum_{\ell,s\in\mathbb{N}}(\xi^{\ell,s}_{k}D_{X_{i}}u)*K_{{\varepsilon^{\ell,s}_{k}}}\right\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}+\left\|\sum_{\ell,s\in\mathbb{N}}S^{k,\ell,s}_{i}\right\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}+\left\|\sum_{\ell,s\in\mathbb{N}}R^{k,\ell,s}_{i}\right\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq 45|D_{X_{i}}u|(\Omega)+\frac{4}{k},

the latter implying that
u_{k}\in\operatorname{BV}_{X}(\Omega)
.

Now we drop the smoothness assumption on
u
and we just assume that
u\in\operatorname{BV}_{X}(\Omega)
. We know, by
[
28
, Theorem 2.2.2]
, that there exists a sequence
(u^{t})_{t\in\mathbb{N}}
such that, for every
t\in\mathbb{N}
we have
u^{t}\in C^{\infty}(\Omega)\cap\operatorname{BV}_{X}(\Omega)
and

\left\|u^{t}-u\right\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\xrightarrow{t\to+\infty}0,\quad|D_{X_{i}}u^{t}|(\Omega)\xrightarrow{t\to+\infty}|D_{X_{i}}u|(\Omega),\quad|D_{X_{i}}u^{t}|(\Omega)\leq|D_{X_{i}}u|(\Omega)+\frac{1}{t}

for every
i\in\{1,\dots,m\}
. Now for every
t\in\mathbb{N}
consider the approximation sequence
(u_{k}^{t})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
constructed as in (
14
). For the observations we just made on the approximation of smooth functions we know that
u_{k}^{t}\in C^{1}_{X}(\Omega)\cap\operatorname{BV}_{X}(\Omega)
. Let us prove that
\|u_{k}^{t}-u_{k}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\xrightarrow{t\to+\infty}0
. First we observe that, thanks to Remark
4.3
, we have
\left\|u_{k}\right\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\leq 45\left\|u\right\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}
so that

\left\|u^{t}_{k}-u_{k}\right\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}=\left\|(u^{t}-u)_{k}\right\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\leq 45\left\|u^{t}-u\right\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}.

The inequality above shows that
\|u_{k}^{t}-u_{k}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\xrightarrow{t\to+\infty}0
. Then we observe

|D_{X_{i}}u^{t}_{k}|(\Omega)\leq 45|D_{X_{i}}u^{t}|(\Omega)+\frac{4}{k}\leq 45|D_{X_{i}}u|(\Omega)+\frac{45}{t}+\frac{4}{k}\leq 45|D_{X_{i}}u|(\Omega)+49.

Passing to the
\liminf
for
t\to+\infty
in the above inequality and using the lower semicontinuity of the total variation is enough to obtain
u_{k}\in\operatorname{BV}_{X}(\Omega)
.

From Lemma
4.6
, the construction of
C_{k}
in Lemma
4.2
and the fact that
u_{k}\in\operatorname{BV}_{X}(\Omega)\cap C^{\infty}(\Omega\setminus C_{k})
we obtain that
u_{k}\in\operatorname{SBV}_{X}(\Omega)
.
∎

The following Lemma will be the last step needed to prove our main result.

Lemma 4.9
.

Let

and

\mathcal{H}^{Q-1}

Let us observe that

and

. In fact, for

-a.e.

and, letting

\displaystyle\lim_{r\to 0}\fint_{B(p,r)}|v-w|d\mathcal{L}^{n}\leq

\displaystyle\lim_{r\to 0}\fint_{B^{+}_{\nu}(p,r)}|v-v^{+}|d\mathcal{L}^{n}+\lim_{r\to 0}\fint_{B^{+}_{\nu}(p,r)}|w-w^{+}|d\mathcal{L}^{n}+

\displaystyle+\lim_{r\to 0}\fint_{B^{-}_{\nu}(p,r)}|v-v^{-}|d\mathcal{L}^{n}+\lim_{r\to 0}\fint_{B^{-}_{\nu}(p,r)}|w-w^{-}|d\mathcal{L}^{n}=0.

The latter implies that
v-w
has approximate limit 0 at

p\in R\setminus\mathcal{S}_{v-w}
. This proves that
\mathcal{H}^{Q-1}(R\cap\mathcal{S}_{v-w})=0
and by Theorem
2.10
(
1
)

(33)

Moreover, the measure

4.3.

Theorem 4.10

Let

Proof.

and

4.2

14

Thanks to (

Concerning the other summand, we estimate

(37)

\displaystyle|D_{X}(u-u_{k})|(\Omega)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|D_{X}(u-u_{k})|(\Omega\setminus\mathcal{J}_{u})+|D_{X}(u-u_{k})|(\mathcal{J}_{u}\setminus\mathcal{J}^{k}_{u})+|D_{X}(u-u_{k})|(\mathcal{J}_{u}^{k}\setminus M_{k})

\displaystyle+|D_{X}(u-u_{k})|((\mathcal{J}_{u}\cap M_{k})\setminus C_{k})+|D_{X}(u-u_{k})|(C_{k}).

Because of Lemma
4.6
, Proposition

11
) and (

\displaystyle|D_{X}(u-u_{k})|(\mathcal{J}_{u}\setminus\mathcal{J}^{k}_{u})=|D_{X}u|(\mathcal{J}_{u}\setminus\mathcal{J}^{k}_{u})<\frac{1}{k},

\displaystyle|D_{X}(u-u_{k})|(\mathcal{J}_{u}^{k}\setminus M_{k})=|D_{X}u|(\mathcal{J}_{u}^{k}\setminus M_{k})<\frac{1}{k}.

By Theorem

By Proposition
4.7

(40)

High-Spin-State-Induced Aromaticity Reversal in Cyclo[6]carbon
Authors: Gang Zhang, Jiayuan Cui, Ying Jin, Yi Zou, Jingang Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Cyclo­[6]carbon (C6), the smallest even-membered cyclocarbon, provides a stringent platform for examining how a highly strained sp-hybridized carbon ring responds to changes in electronic state. Here, we investigate the structural, electronic, magnetic-response, excited-state, and stability/reactivity features of C6 using density functional theory, time-dependent density functional theory, ab initio molecular dynamics, and a CASSCF­(12,12) occupation-number analysis. The optimized S0 structure is strictly planar and shows pronounced bond-angle alternation together with nearly uniform C–C bond lengths, indicating that its bonding cannot be described by a simple localized alternating-bond picture. Real-space descriptors, including IRI-π and ELF-π, outline a continuous dual-π delocalized framework in S0 and provide the electronic reference for analyzing state-dependent magnetic responses. Magnetic-response analyses based on ACID, ICSS zz , and NICS zz further show that the selected high-spin quintet Q1 state undergoes a pronounced reversal from the diatropic response of S0 to a paratropic response. A CASSCF­(12,12) occupation-number analysis supports a predominantly closed-shell description of S0 C6, with no indication of pronounced open-shell multireference character, thereby supporting the qualitative interpretation of the DFT-based descriptors. Density-of-states and representative excited-state analyses further indicate that changes in electronic-state occupation redistribute electron density within the πin and πout manifolds, providing additional electronic-structure context for state-dependent changes within the dual-π framework. AIMD simulations indicate that the S0 ring framework is kinetically stable at low temperature but becomes increasingly flexible at elevated temperature, while ESP and ALIE analyses identify the C–C framework as the main reactive region. These results reveal a pronounced aromaticity reversal in the high-spin Q1 state and highlight the spin-state-dependent magnetic response of the dual-π framework in C6.

Introduction

Cyclocarbons consist exclusively of carbon atoms arranged in an sp-hybridized ring. Despite their extremely simple geometric composition, they exhibit highly nonclassical bonding and delocalization features and have therefore attracted considerable attention as a class of unusual all-carbon systems. , Unlike conventional conjugated hydrocarbons, the electronic structure of cyclocarbons cannot be understood solely in terms of a single out-of-plane π framework. Instead, the valence-electron arrangement of the carbon atoms allows the formation of two mutually orthogonal π-electron systems, one lying approximately in the molecular plane and the other perpendicular to it. The concept of double aromaticity in cyclo[6]­carbon can be traced back to the early theoretical work of Schleyer and coworkers, who discussed the coexistence of two orthogonal aromatic π systems in C6. This feature makes cyclocarbons important model systems for exploring aromaticity, antiaromaticity, and π-electron delocalization in carbon-rich rings. ,

Since the direct imaging of cyclo[18]­carbon, increasing attention has been devoted to the synthesis, characterization, and theoretical interpretation of even-membered cyclocarbons with different ring sizes. − Further on-surface studies of aromatic C10/C14 and antiaromatic C12/C20 have shown that subtle changes in ring size can lead to markedly different structural and magnetic aromaticity features. , More recently, Sun and coworkers reported the on-surface synthesis and characterization of both linear and cyclic C6, making C6 an experimentally accessible limiting member of the cyclocarbon family rather than only a hypothetical model. Previous theoretical studies have also shown that ring size has a pronounced influence on the structural deformation, bonding pattern, electron delocalization, and aromaticity of small cyclocarbons. In particular, Baryshnikov and coworkers systematically examined the aromaticity of even-numbered cyclo­[n]­carbons from C6 to C100 using magnetically induced current-density analysis, demonstrating that ring size strongly modulates electron delocalization and aromatic response. These findings indicate that cyclocarbons should not be regarded simply as a homologous series with gradually increasing ring size. Rather, they represent electronically distinct systems in which strain, delocalization, and aromatic response remain strongly coupled.

Against this background, cyclo[6]carbon (C6) represents a particularly stringent limiting case. As the smallest even-membered cyclocarbon, it must sustain substantial angular strain within an extremely compact ring framework while preserving electronic coherence. This raises a fundamental question: can such a tiny sp-carbon ring still maintain global π-electron delocalization through two orthogonal channels, or do severe geometric constraints force the system toward a more localized bonding pattern? In highly strained systems, geometric metrics alone are often insufficient for a reliable assignment of aromaticity. Magnetic-response analyses of sp-hybridized carbon rings have shown that the induced magnetic field can be decomposed into in-plane and out-of-plane π-orbital contributions, providing a useful framework for evaluating double aromaticity and double antiaromaticity in cyclocarbons. A meaningful description of C6 therefore requires an internally consistent line of evidence involving structural characteristics, real-space electronic topology, and magnetic response. − Recent discussions of aromaticity have also increasingly emphasized that no single descriptor is usually sufficient for evaluating the magnetic and electronic nature of such nonclassical carbon-rich rings. Very recently, Baranac-Stojanović analyzed the (anti)­aromaticity of cyclo­[2n]­carbons from n = 3 to 12 and further highlighted the ring-size dependence of the two π systems in small cyclocarbon rings. C6 is especially intriguing from this perspective because it provides the smallest-ring framework for examining how dual-π-electron delocalization responds to changes in electronic state. If both orthogonal π manifolds remain delocalized, the ground-state aromatic response should arise from the cooperative contribution of two π circuits rather than from a single dominant pathway. Aromaticity reversal has recently been identified in cyclo[16]carbon through vibrational effects, indicating that cyclocarbon aromaticity may be highly sensitive to changes in nuclear geometry and electronic-state occupation. This finding motivates the present focus on spin-state-dependent magnetic-response switching in the smallest cyclocarbon member, C6. Related recent work has also applied ELF-, NICS-, and ACID-type analyses to cyclo[6]­carbon-containing carbon frameworks, further demonstrating the usefulness of combined real-space and magnetic-response descriptors for such systems. Building on this context, the present work uses the S0 electronic structure of C6 as a baseline for examining how the magnetic response evolves upon changes in electronic state, with particular emphasis on the high-spin Q1 state.

On this basis, the present work focuses on the state-dependent magnetic response of C6. The ground-state structure and dual-π-electron delocalization of S0 are used as the reference framework, and the high-spin Q1 state is examined to determine whether electronic-state occupation can reverse the aromatic magnetic response. In this framework, the ground-state dual-π delocalization of C6 is treated as the reference state, and the magnetic-response reversal in Q1 is developed as the main focus of this work.

Results and Discussion

Molecular Structure of C6

The optimized ground-state structure of C6 is shown in Figure a, and the corresponding Cartesian coordinates are provided in the Supporting Information. Gas-phase optimization shows that the molecule adopts a strictly planar geometry. This feature is particularly important in the present system because cyclocarbon contains two mutually orthogonal π-electron systems, and the planar geometry provides a well-defined reference frame for separating the in-plane and out-of-plane π contributions in the subsequent electronic-structure and magnetic-response analyses. The most prominent structural feature of C6 is the coexistence of pronounced bond-angle alternation and nearly uniform bond lengths. As shown in Figure b, the internal bond angles alternate between approximately 90° and 150°, indicating substantial angular distortion around the ring. This pattern suggests that the strain imposed by the minimal ring size is not distributed in a uniform circular manner, but is instead accommodated through an alternating angular framework. From the perspective of conventional structural chemistry, if the bonding were mainly localized, such a large difference in bond angles would normally be accompanied by marked bond-length alternation. Figure c shows that all C–C bond lengths are nearly identical, with values of about 1.32 Å, and the bond-order distribution follows a consistent overall trend. The absence of obvious bond-length alternation indicates that, although the ring undergoes strong angular distortion, its electronic structure cannot be described adequately by a localized alternating single-bond/triple-bond picture. In C6, strain is expressed more clearly through directional geometric deformation than through differentiation in bond lengths. The decoupling between bond-angle alternation and nearly uniform bond lengths suggests that electron delocalization plays an important role in maintaining a relatively uniform bonding framework, providing the structural reference for the following analysis of π-electron delocalization and state-dependent magnetic response.

Ground-State Dual-π-Electron Delocalization in C6

Ground-state C6 contains two mutually orthogonal π-electron systems, whose spatial distribution is closely related to its aromatic magnetic response. Therefore, IRI-π analysis was used to examine whether π-related interaction regions form a continuous distribution around the highly strained C–C framework. As shown in Figure a, the color-filled IRI-π map shows π-related interaction regions distributed around the C–C framework rather than only isolated regions localized on individual C–C bonds. The IRI-π isosurface in Figure b further shows that these interaction regions form a connected distribution along the ring, indicating that the π-related interaction pattern is not fully interrupted by the pronounced bond-angle alternation of C6. The corresponding πin/πout delocalization scheme, LOL-π maps, and ELF-π analysis are provided in Figures S1–S3 in the Supporting Information. These supplementary analyses further support the presence of two orthogonal π manifolds in the ground-state framework, with somewhat stronger conjugation along the πout channel. This real-space picture provides the ground-state electronic reference for comparing the magnetic-response behavior of S0 and Q1.

IRI-π maps of ground-state C6: (a) color-filled map in the molecular plane; (b) IRI-π isosurface map.

State-Dependent Magnetic Response and Aromaticity Reversal from S0 to Q1

We first carried out an ACID analysis of ground-state C6 to characterize its magnetic-response pattern. As shown in Figure , the ground state (S0) exhibits a clear diatropic ring current involving two mutually orthogonal π circuits. This magnetic-response pattern is consistent with the real-space delocalization framework discussed above and provides the ground-state comparison for evaluating how electronic-state variation affects the ring-current behavior of C6. Compared with S0, the selected high-spin quintet state Q1, with a spin multiplicity of 2S + 1 = 5, shows a clearly reversed ring-current pattern and is characterized by a paratropic circulation. This suggests that the change in electronic-state occupation substantially modifies the magnetic-response balance of the dual-π framework, leading to a pronounced aromaticity reversal. The direct comparison between S0 and Q1 therefore reveals a clear reversal of the magnetic response in the high-spin Q1 state. Figure S4 in the Supporting Information also presents the ACID­(total), ACID­(in), and ACID­(out) plots for the three triplet states T1–T3. These triplet states still retain magnetic-response contributions from both the in-plane and out-of-plane π channels, but their ring-current patterns do not display an overall reversal as pronounced as that of Q1. Instead, they show weaker and more transitional magnetic-response behavior. This indicates that the magnetic-response change does not occur uniformly across all examined spin states, but exhibits clear state selectivity. Among the states examined here, Q1 shows the clearest reversal of the ring-current response.

ACID plots of C6 in the ground state (S0) and the selected high-spin quintet state (Q1, spin multiplicity = 5).

Consistent with the ACID results, the ICSS zz and NICS zz analyses shown in Figure provide additional spatial and quantitative support for the state-dependent magnetic-response change. In S0, the ICSS zz map shows the characteristic shielding/deshielding distribution associated with a diatropic aromatic ring current. In contrast, the corresponding ICSS zz pattern of Q1 is clearly reversed, showing that the change in current direction is not a minor perturbation of the ground-state response, but is accompanied by a distinct change in the magnetic-response pattern. The NICS zz scan also shows markedly different profiles for S0 and Q1 along the direction perpendicular to the ring plane, further confirming the distinct magnetic nature of these two states.

ICSS zz and NICS zz analyses of C6: (a) ICSS zz map of the ground state (S0); (b) ICSS zz map of the selected high-spin quintet state (Q1, spin multiplicity = 5); (c) NICS zz scan showing the state-dependent magnetic response.

The agreement among ACID, ICSS zz , and NICS zz shows that the aromaticity reversal in Q1 is not an artifact of a single magnetic descriptor, but is consistently supported by multiple magnetic-response criteria. Figure S5 in the Supporting Information also presents the ICSS zz maps and the corresponding NICS zz curves for the three triplet states T1–T3. These triplet states retain an overall shielding/deshielding pattern closer to that of S0 and do not undergo the pronounced reversal observed for Q1. Their magnetic-response intensity and spatial extent are reduced relative to the ground state, indicating that excitation can weaken the aromatic magnetic response without necessarily inducing a complete reversal of the magnetic-response pattern. Taken together, these results show that the selected high-spin quintet Q1 state undergoes a pronounced aromaticity reversal characterized by a change from diatropic to paratropic magnetic response relative to the ground state.

Electronic-Structure Background for State-Dependent Magnetic Response

The valence orbitals of C6 can be divided into three parts, namely one σ-electron system and two mutually orthogonal π-electron systems (πin and πout). The TDOS and PDOS curves shown in Figure provide an orbital-energy perspective for understanding the electronic structure of S0. As seen from the figure, the σ orbitals are mainly distributed in the lower-energy region, whereas the frontier-energy region is dominated by the two π manifolds. Specifically, the HOMO and LUMO energies are −10.61 and −2.60 eV, respectively, corresponding to a HOMO–LUMO gap of 8.01 eV. Apart from the lowest-energy region, the πin and πout components show closely spaced PDOS features over a broad energy range. This indicates that the valence electronic structure of C6 involves contributions from both orthogonal π systems, rather than being dominated by a single π component.

Total density of states (TDOS) and partial density of states (PDOS) curves of C6, showing the σ, πin, and πout contributions to the valence electronic structure.

The pronounced peaks in the DOS curves mainly reflect the discrete molecular-orbital levels of the finite ground-state C6. Taken together, Figure shows that the electronic structure of C6 is not governed by a localized alternating-bond picture, but is instead built from a σ framework together with two closely related orthogonal π manifolds. The coexistence of the two π manifolds and their closely spaced energy levels defines an important orbital-energy feature of ground-state C6. Thus, the DOS/PDOS analysis provides the orbital-energy background for understanding the state-dependent magnetic-response switching from S0 to Q1. A numerical comparison of C6 with representative cyclocarbons C10, C14, C16, and C18 in terms of C–C bond lengths, bond-length alternation, HOMO–LUMO gaps, NICS(0), and NICS zz (0) values is provided in Table S1 in the Supporting Information. Because the near-degenerate π manifolds may introduce static-correlation effects, a CASSCF­(12,12) occupation-number analysis was carried out for S0 C6. As summarized in Table S2 in the Supporting Information, the active-space occupation numbers remain close to the ideal closed-shell limits, with modest deviations from 2.000 and 0.000. The occupied active orbitals show occupation numbers in the range of 1.901–1.966, whereas the virtual active orbitals show occupation numbers in the range of 0.041–0.143. These occupation numbers support a predominantly closed-shell description of S0 C6, with no indication of pronounced open-shell multireference character. The CASSCF occupation analysis was therefore used together with the DFT-based real-space and magnetic-response descriptors to support the description of the S0 electronic reference framework.

Excited-State Electron Rearrangement within the Dual-π Framework

The one-photon absorption spectrum provides an overview of the optical excitation features associated with the dual-π-electronic framework of C6. As shown in Figure , C6 exhibits one dominant absorption band together with several weaker absorption features in the range of 50–450 nm, indicating that the absorption profile contains multiple discrete electronic excitations. According to the excited-state assignments, the strong absorption peak at 98.44 nm mainly originates from the doubly degenerate S51/S52 states, whereas the weaker absorption features are associated with transitions such as S98, S21/S22, and S3/S4. Thus, Figure provides the spectroscopic background for the subsequent TDM and CDD analyses of representative electronic excitations.

One-photon absorption spectrum of C6.

Combined inspection of the TDM and CDD maps allows a clearer identification of the electron-rearrangement features associated with different excited states. As shown in Figure , these representative excitations are predominantly localized excitations with partial charge-transfer character. From the spatial distributions of electrons and holes in the CDD maps, the electrons and holes of the S21 state are mainly located above and below the molecular ring, indicating that this excitation is mainly associated with the πout system. By contrast, the electron/hole distributions of S3, S51, and S98 are more concentrated around the carbon atoms and adjacent C–C bond regions, indicating that these excitations are mainly contributed by the πin system.

Transition density matrix (TDM) and charge-density-difference (CDD) maps of representative excited states of C6: (a) S3; (b) S21; (c) S51; (d) S98. In the CDD maps, blue and red represent electrons and holes, respectively. The isovalue of all CDD maps is set to 0.01 a.u.

These results show that the excited states of C6 do not originate from an entirely different bonding framework, but instead correspond to different modes of electron rearrangement within the preexisting dual-π skeleton. Orbital-contribution analysis further shows that these relevant excitations are mainly of π → π* character, with no obvious σ → π* or π → σ* features. Additional TDM/CDD maps for the paired excited states S4, S22, and S52 are provided in Figure S6, and the corresponding orbital-contribution analyses for S3/S21/S51/S98 and S4/S22/S52 are shown in Figures S7 and S8, respectively. Therefore, the TDM and CDD analyses provide complementary information on how electronic excitation redistributes electron density within the πin and πout channels. This redistribution helps clarify how different electronic states can modify the dual-π-electronic framework of C6.

Thermal Stability and Reactivity of C6

The finite-temperature structural stability of this minimal carbon ring was examined by AIMD simulations. As shown in Figure , C6 is able to retain its ring framework well under low-temperature conditions. As the temperature increases, the RMSD gradually becomes larger and the structural fluctuations are correspondingly enhanced, indicating that the geometric stability of the system decreases as thermal motion becomes stronger. The representative trajectories show the same trend: the structure of C6 remains relatively compact at 4.7 K, whereas more pronounced configurational disturbances appear at higher temperatures.

AIMD analysis of C6: (a) RMSD curves at different temperatures; (b) representative trajectory at 4.7 K; (c) representative trajectory at 100 K.

To present this temperature-dependent structural evolution more completely, Figure S9 provides additional representative trajectories at 10, 20, and 50 K, supplementing the continuous configurational changes between the two representative temperatures of 4.7 and 100 K shown in the main figure. Figure S10 presents an enlarged view of the first 400 fs of the RMSD curves, in which the minimum-RMSD configurations at each temperature are marked for identification. The complete 5 ps trajectories were used for the overall stability analysis. Figure S11 shows the fluctuations of C–C bond lengths at different temperatures and further demonstrates from the perspective of local structural parameters that thermal perturbation becomes progressively stronger as the temperature increases. Within the present simulation time scale, the overall ring framework remains intact, indicating that this minimal ring system still possesses appreciable kinetic stability under sufficiently low-temperature conditions. Together, the RMSD curves, representative structural snapshots, and C–C bond-length fluctuations consistently show that C6 maintains its ring framework at low temperatures, whereas elevated temperatures lead to larger structural fluctuations, indicating a gradual decrease in finite-temperature structural stability.

In addition to temperature-dependent stability, the reactivity characteristics of C6 were examined using ESP and ALIE analyses. As shown in Figure , the electrostatic potential is distributed nonuniformly over the entire carbon framework. Figure S12 in the Supporting Information further presents the surface-area distribution corresponding to different ESP intervals, showing that the surface area associated with positive ESP regions is overall larger than that associated with negative ESP regions. The ALIE map further shows that the most electronically active regions are mainly concentrated on the C–C framework, especially in the low-ALIE regions distributed along the bonds, where electrophilic interactions are more likely to occur.

ESP and ALIE maps of C6: (a) electrostatic potential surface; (b) ALIE surface showing the electronically active regions.

These results indicate that the C–C framework is not only the structural skeleton supporting the delocalized π system, but also the main electronically active region of C6. Thus, together with the AIMD simulations, the ESP and ALIE analyses provide complementary information on the boundary properties of C6: AIMD describes its finite-temperature structural stability, whereas ESP and ALIE reveal the electrostatic and local ionization features related to its potential reactivity.

Conclusions

In this work, cyclo[6]­carbon (C6) was investigated with emphasis on the aromaticity reversal of its high-spin Q1 state. The optimized ground-state structure remains planar and exhibits pronounced bond-angle alternation together with nearly uniform C–C bond lengths, indicating that C6 cannot be described simply by a localized alternating-bond model. Real-space analyses based on IRI-π, ELF-π, and related descriptors support the presence of two orthogonal π manifolds, which provide the electronic framework for its magnetic-response behavior.

Magnetic-response analyses identify Q1 as the key state showing aromaticity reversal. The S0 state exhibits a diatropic ring-current response associated with the dual-π framework, whereas the high-spin quintet Q1 state shows a reversed ring-current direction and a paratropic magnetic response. In comparison, the T1–T3 states do not show such a pronounced reversal, indicating that the magnetic-response change is state-selective and most clearly expressed in Q1. The CASSCF­(12,12) occupation-number analysis supports a predominantly closed-shell description of S0 C6, while DOS/PDOS and representative excited-state analyses show that the relevant electronic rearrangements mainly occur within the preexisting πin and πout manifolds. These results are consistent with the Q1 aromaticity reversal involving changes in electronic-state occupation within the preexisting dual-π framework rather than the formation of a completely different bonding skeleton.

AIMD simulations show that C6 retains its ring framework under low-temperature conditions, whereas elevated temperatures lead to larger structural fluctuations and reduced finite-temperature structural stability. ESP and ALIE analyses further indicate that the C–C framework is the main electronically active region of the molecule. Overall, this work demonstrates that the high-spin Q1 state of C6 exhibits a pronounced aromaticity reversal relative to the diatropic ground state, providing a state-dependent picture of aromatic magnetic response in this minimal carbon ring.

Computational Methods

All density functional theory (DFT) and time-dependent density functional theory (TD-DFT) calculations were carried out using Gaussian 16, and molecular structures were built and examined with GaussView. , The ground-state geometry optimization and harmonic vibrational frequency calculation of C6 were performed at the ωB97X-D/def2-TZVP level of theory. , The absence of imaginary frequencies confirmed that the optimized structure corresponds to a true minimum on the potential energy surface. Unless otherwise specified, all calculations were performed in the gas phase. To evaluate the possible multireference character of S0 C6, a complete active space self-consistent field, CASSCF­(12,12), single-point calculation was performed on the ωB97X-D/def2-TZVP optimized geometry using the def2-TZVP basis set. The active space contains 12 electrons in 12 frontier orbitals associated with the two orthogonal π-electron manifolds of C6. The final active-space occupation numbers were used as a multireference diagnostic. Real-space and magnetic-response analyses were conducted using Multiwfn and the anisotropy of the induced current density (AICD) 2.0 program. , The interaction region indicator for π-electrons (IRI-π), electron localization function for π-electrons (ELF-π), and localized orbital locator for π-electrons (LOL-π) were used to visualize the in-plane and out-of-plane π-electron delocalization channels. , Here, πin and πout denote the in-plane and out-of-plane p-orbital-derived π manifolds of the sp-hybridized carbon ring, respectively, rather than the σ-bonding framework. The ACID plots, together with iso-chemical shielding surface along the zz direction (ICSS zz ) and nucleus-independent chemical shift along the zz direction (NICS zz ), were used to evaluate the aromatic magnetic response. , Density of states (DOS), total density of states (TDOS), partial density of states (PDOS), highest occupied molecular orbital (HOMO), lowest unoccupied molecular orbital (LUMO), transition density matrix (TDM), and charge-density difference (CDD) analyses were used to clarify the electronic-structure and excited-state features. Bond-length alternation (BLA) was defined as the absolute difference between the averaged long and short C–C bond lengths. Excited-state properties, including one-photon absorption, were calculated using TD-DFT at the same ωB97X-D/def2-TZVP level. The notation S0 denotes the electronic ground state of neutral C6. The notation Q1 refers to the selected high-spin quintet state of neutral C6 used for magnetic-response analysis, with a spin multiplicity of 2S + 1 = 5. The triplet states T1–T3 denote the first three triplet states considered in this work, with a spin multiplicity of 3. The S0, T1–T3, and Q1 states were separately optimized at the ωB97X-D/def2-TZVP level, and their corresponding state-specific wave functions were used for the ACID, ICSS zz , and NICS zz calculations.

Ab initio molecular dynamics (AIMD) simulations were performed using the Quickstep module of CP2K to evaluate the finite-temperature structural stability of C6. The simulations were carried out at 4.7, 10, 20, 50, and 100 K in the canonical ensemble with constant particle number, volume, and temperature (NVT) using a canonical sampling through velocity rescaling (CSVR) thermostat. For each trajectory, a time step of 1.0 fs and 5000 simulation steps were used, corresponding to a total simulation time of 5 ps. The PBE functional with DFT-D3­(BJ) dispersion correction, DZVP-MOLOPT-SR-GTH-q4 basis set, and GTH-PBE pseudopotential were employed. Detailed AIMD settings are summarized in the Supporting Information. The structural evolution was monitored using root-mean-square deviation (RMSD) and C–C bond-length fluctuations. Electrostatic potential (ESP) and average local ionization energy (ALIE) analyses were used to probe the reactivity distribution of C6. Molecular structures and isosurface maps were visualized using GaussView, Multiwfn, and VMD.

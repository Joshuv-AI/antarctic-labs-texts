Abstract
. In the study of stochastic PDEs with colored, non-trace class space-time noise, one frequently encounters Gaussian series of the form g ​ ∑ n ≥ 1 γ n ​ μ n ​ f n , \textstyle{g\,\sum_{n\geq 1}\gamma_{n}\mu_{n}f_{n},} where ( γ n ) n (\gamma_{n})_{n} is a sequence of standard independent Gaussian variables, g g is an L η ​ ( 𝒪 ) L^{\eta}(\mathcal{O}) function, ( μ n ) n (\mu_{n})_{n} is a sequence of scalars, and ( f n ) n (f_{n})_{n} is an orthonormal system in L 2 ​ ( 𝒪 ) L^{2}(\mathcal{O}) where 𝒪 ⊆ ℝ d \mathcal{O}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d} is an open set. In this manuscript, we establish necessary and sufficient conditions for the above sum to converge in Bessel potential spaces H − s , q ​ ( 𝒪 ) H^{-s,q}(\mathcal{O}) . The latter can be interpreted as a Sobolev embedding for Gaussian series. Our main theorem is formulated using weighted sequence spaces that encode the L ∞ L^{\infty} -growth of the orthonormal system ( f n ) n (f_{n})_{n} , a feature that is crucial for obtaining sharp estimates. We apply our results to the stochastic heat equation with additive non-trace class noise. In this case, our conditions capture the scaling relationship between the heat operator and the coloring of the noise.

1.
Introduction

In the study of stochastic PDEs (SPDEs), one often encounters Gaussian series formally written as

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}(t,x)=\sum_{n\geq 1}\mu_{n}f_{n}(x)w_{n}(t),

(1.1)

where
\mu=(\mu_{n})_{n\geq 1}
is a set of scalars,
\mathcal{S}_{f}=(f_{n})_{n\geq 1}
is an orthonormal system in
L^{2}(\mathcal{O})
on an open set
\mathcal{O}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
(w_{n})_{n\geq 1}
is a family of independent real-valued Brownian motions, on a probability space
(\Omega,(\mathscr{F}_{t})_{t\geq 0},\mathbb{P})
. It is well-known that if
\mu_{n}\equiv 1
, then the time derivative of the series (
1.1
) represents
space-time white noise
on
\mathcal{O}
. On the other hand, if
(\mu_{n}\|f_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{O})})_{n\geq 1}\in\ell^{2}
, then the series (
1.1
) is well-defined in
L^{2}(\Omega;L^{q}(\mathcal{O}))
for all
t>0
for all
q<\infty
(if
\mathcal{O}
is bounded), and the corresponding derivative is often referred to as
trace-class noise
on
\mathcal{O}
. If
(\mu_{n})_{n\geq 1}
is in
\ell^{2}
, the same holds for
q=2
for any orthonormal system
\mathcal{S}_{f}
(without any
L^{\infty}
-condition).

In the intermediate regimes, where
\mu\in\ell^{\zeta}
or
\sum_{n\geq 1}|\mu_{n}|^{\zeta}\delta_{n}<\infty
for
\zeta\in(2,\infty)
and a weight
\delta_{n}
(often dependent on
\mathcal{S}_{f}
), then the time derivative of
\mathcal{W}
in (
1.1
) is called
non-trace class noise
on
\mathcal{O}
. Often in the literature, the coefficients
\mu=(\mu_{n})_{n\geq 1}
are referred to as “coloring” of the noise.
This distinction can also be appreciated by looking at the regularity of solutions to the stochastic
heat equation on the open set
\mathcal{O}\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
:

\partial_{t}u(t,x)-\Delta u(t,x)=g(t,x)\,\partial_{t}\mathcal{W}(t,x)

(1.2)

supplemented with, for instance, Dirichlet boundary conditions if
\partial\mathcal{O}
is non-empty, and zero initial conditions.
Here, we additionally consider the random field
g
, which can be used to study nonlinear variants of the above SPDE.
In the pure noise cases
g\equiv 1
, it is well-known that trace-class noise implies that
\nabla u\in L^{2}_{\mathrm{loc}}([0,\infty)\times\mathcal{O};{\mathbb{R}}^{d})
a.s., while, in the case of space-time white noise, one only has
u(t)\in H^{-1-d/2-}(\mathcal{O})
a.s. for all
t>0
(in particular, in
d\geq 2
it is merely a distribution).

The aim of this paper is to prove
sharp estimates
for the Gaussian series
\mathcal{W}
and corresponding sharp estimates for parabolic SPDEs, in the case
\mathcal{W}
is any Gaussian series whose time derivative is “between” trace-class and space-time white noise, with the extremes being included.
By stochastic maximal
L^{p}
-regularity estimates
[
48
]
(or also
[
9
, Section 3]
), which provide optimal space-time bounds for solutions to (
1.2
), these two results are intimately related.
Illustrations of our results are given in Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
below, respectively.
In turn, our central goal is to establish necessary and sufficient conditions for the convergence of the following Gaussian series

g\,\sum_{n\geq 1}\gamma_{n}\mu_{n}f_{n}

(1.3)

in Sobolev (more precisely, Bessel potential) spaces of
negative
smoothness under suitable assumptions on the objects involved. In the above,
(\gamma_{n})_{n\geq 1}
is a sequence of Gaussian random variables, which appears in so-called
\gamma
-radonifying operators (see Subsection
2.2
) arising in sharp two-sided estimates for stochastic integrals in spaces such as
L^{p}(\Omega;L^{q}(\mathcal{O}))
as explained in the survey
[
49
]
. These sharp estimates play a key role in maximal regularity estimates for solutions to SPDEs
[
9
,
48
]
.
For the sake of concreteness, here we will mostly focus on the stochastic heat equation, although our setting allows for a much wider class of SPDEs (see Section
6
).
We emphasize that results for the “endpoint cases”, i.e. trace-class and space-time white noises, will be presented as extreme cases of our results obtained.
The importance of optimal (or sharp) estimates in terms of
g
appearing on the right-hand side of (
1.2
) becomes apparent when dealing with nonlinear SPDEs, in which case

g(t,x)=G(t,x,u(t,x))

for a function
G
, which is often assumed to satisfy certain (local) Lipschitz conditions in the
u
-variable. Then, it is desirable to require as little space integrability as possible on the coefficient
g(t,x,u)
, so that, for instance, in a fixed point argument, one can work with lower powers of
u
, avoiding a loss of smoothness in Sobolev embeddings. Such a situation was indeed the initial motivation for the present article, and it will be thoroughly explored in the forthcoming work
[
2
]
.

We now discuss the convergence of the Gaussian series (
1.3
), with the coloring
\mu
being between the case trace-class noise
(\mu_{n}\|f_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{O})})_{n\geq 1}\in\ell^{2}
and space-time white noise
\mu_{n}\equiv 1
or, more generally
(\mu_{n})_{n\geq 1}\in\ell^{\infty}
.
To capture noises in this intermediate range, we introduce the following class of weighted sequences

\displaystyle\|\mu\|_{\ell^{\zeta}(\mathcal{S}_{f})}^{\zeta}:=\sum_{n\geq 1}|\mu_{n}|^{\zeta}\|f_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{O})}^{2}<\infty\quad\text{ for }\ \zeta\in[2,\infty),

(1.4)

and
\|\mu\|_{\ell^{\infty}(\mathcal{S}_{f})}:=\sup_{n\geq 1}|\mu_{n}|.

We point out that the above space of sequences naturally arises by interpolating the above-mentioned condition in the extreme cases of trace-class (
\zeta=2
) and space-time noises (
\zeta=\infty
).
In many applications, the weights
(\|f_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{O})})_{n\geq 1}
are
not
bounded, and therefore
\ell^{\zeta}(\mathcal{S}_{f})
is a true space of weighted sequences.
Indeed, in the SPDE literature (see Subsection
1.2
for references), the orthonormal system
\mathcal{S}_{f}=(f_{n})_{n\geq 1}
typically consists of
L^{2}
-normalized eigenfunctions of the Dirichlet Laplacian on
\mathcal{O}
. If the latter is bounded and smooth, the best-known bound for such eigenfunctions is

\|f_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{O})}\lesssim n^{(d-1)/2d}.

(1.5)

with implicit constant independent of
n
by
[
33
]
and Weyl’s law for the eigenvalues
\lambda_{n}\sim n^{2/d}
, see
[
36
, p. 45]
.
The optimality can be proven, for instance, if
\mathcal{O}
is the unit ball in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
. Hence,
\ell^{\zeta}(\mathcal{S}_{f})
is typically a space of weighted sequences.
As we will show in Section
5
, the unweighted viewpoint is actually not appropriate to study convergence of Gaussian series as in (
1.3
), and thus to study parabolic SPDEs like (
1.2
). Finally, we mention that, in case
\mathcal{O}=\mathbb{T}^{d}
and
\mathcal{S}_{f}
is the standard Fourier basis for which
\|f_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{O})}\eqsim 1
and thus
\ell^{\zeta}(\mathcal{S}_{f})
is unweighted, then our results are
also new
. The reader is referred to Subsections
1.2
and
4.4
for further comments.

As mentioned above, we study the convergence of the Gaussian series (
1.3
) in the Bessel potential space

H^{-s,q}(\mathcal{O})\quad\text{ for }\quad s>0\ \text{ and }\ q\in(1,\infty),

while we assume that
g\in L^{\eta}(\mathcal{O})
for some
\eta\in(1,\infty)
(see Subsection
2.1
the definition).
In summary, the parameters
(s,q,\zeta,\eta)
used throughout this article play the following role:

•

(-s,q)
– Spatial smoothness and integrability of the noise.

•

\zeta
– Noise intensity.

•

\eta
– Spatial integrability of
g
.

Before formulating our main result on the convergence of the Gaussian series (
1.3
), let us remark that whenever the series in (
1.3
) converges in
L^{2}(\Omega;H^{-s,q}(\mathcal{O}))
for some
t>0
, then it also does so for all
t\geq 0
.
It is then routine to show that the limiting object is a Brownian motion with values in
H^{-s,q}(\mathcal{O})
. As a consequence, the convergence holds in much more restrictive topologies, such as
L^{p}(\Omega;C^{\alpha}([0,T];H^{-s,q}(\mathcal{O})))
, for any
\alpha\in(0,1/2)
,
p\in(0,\infty)
and
T\in(0,\infty)
. Indeed, the
L^{p}(\Omega)
-setting follows from the Kahane-Khintchine inequalities (see e.g.
[
39
, Section 6.2b]
), and the
C^{\alpha}
-regularity from the Kolmogorov continuity criterion. In this paper, the focus is on the convergence for fixed
t
only, and the other types of convergence one can get afterwards by applying the mentioned results.

The following can be seen as a Gaussian variant of the Sobolev embedding, and is a consequence of Theorem
4.1
, which is our main result on the convergence of Gaussian series as in (
1.3
).

Theorem 1.1

(Sobolev embedding for Gaussian series)
.

Let
\mathcal{O}\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
be an open set. Let
\mathcal{S}_{f}=(f_{n})_{n\geq 1}
be an orthonormal system in
L^{2}(\mathcal{O})
, and
\mu\in\ell^{\zeta}(\mathcal{S}_{f})
.
Assume that
q\in(1,\infty)
,
\eta\in(1,q)
,
\zeta\in[2,\infty]
, and
s\in(0,d)
satisfy

\displaystyle\frac{s}{d}+\frac{1}{q}\geq\frac{1}{\eta}+\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{\zeta}\qquad\text{ and }\qquad\frac{1}{\eta}-\frac{1}{\zeta}<\frac{1}{2}.

(1.6)

Then, for all
g\in L^{\eta}(\mathcal{O})

the series (
1.3
) converges in
L^{2}(\Omega;H^{-s,q}(\mathcal{O}))
and

\displaystyle\Big({\mathbb{E}}\Big\|g\,\sum_{n\geq 1}\gamma_{n}\mu_{n}f_{n}\Big\|_{H^{-s,q}(\mathcal{O})}^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\lesssim_{d,s,q,\eta,\zeta}\|\mu\|_{\ell^{\zeta}(\mathcal{S}_{f})}\|g\|_{L^{\eta}(\mathcal{O})}.

(1.7)

Finally, if
\mathcal{O}={\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and the first condition in (
1.6
) holds with equality,
the series (
1.3
) even converges in
L^{2}(\Omega;\dot{H}^{-s,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))
and (
1.7
) holds with
\mathcal{O}={\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and
H^{-s,q}
replaced by the homogeneous Sobolev space
\dot{H}^{-s,q}
.

As we discuss in further detail below, in particular in Subsection
6.3
, the range of parameters in (
1.6
) greatly improves previous results in this direction, and their applications to SPDEs. A comparison to the preceding literature is given in Subsections
1.2
and
6.3
.

One could expect that the estimate (
1.7
) can be proved via paraproduct techniques. However, it seems that this would require positive smoothness of
g
, which we do not assume. An idea of the proof of Theorem
1.1
will be sketched in Subsection
1.3
.

In Theorem
1.1
, further endpoints can be included in special cases.
For this, the reader is referred to Theorem
4.1
and results in Subsection
4.1
.
Before going further, let us also note that the convergence properties in (
1.3
) are the same for complex and real Gaussian sequences
(\gamma_{n})_{n\geq 1}
, see for instance
[
39
, Proposition 6.1.21]
.

Theorem
1.1
has the following consequence for (
1.2
).
For simplicity, in the case
\mathcal{O}
has a non-empty boundary, we only consider homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions.

Theorem 1.2

(Optimal space regularity estimates for the stochastic heat equation)
.

Let
\mathcal{O}
be open and bounded and smooth, or
\mathcal{O}\in\{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}_{+},{\mathbb{R}}^{d},\mathbb{T}^{d}\}
.
Assume that the conditions of Theorem
1.1
are satisfied, and in addition,
q\geq 2
. Moreover, assume
s<1+\frac{1}{q}
in case
\partial\mathcal{O}\neq\varnothing
. Then,
for
p\in(2,\infty)
,
T<\infty
, and progressively measurable
g:\Omega\times(0,T)\to L^{\eta}(\mathcal{O})
, the stochastic heat equation (
1.2
) with homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions has a unique solution
u
satisfying

{\mathbb{E}}\|u\|_{L^{p}(0,T;H^{1-s,q}(\mathcal{O}))}^{p}\lesssim_{T,d,s,q,\eta,\zeta}\|\mu\|_{\ell^{\zeta}(\mathcal{S}_{f})}^{p}\,{\mathbb{E}}\|g\|^{p}_{L^{p}(0,T;L^{\eta}(\mathcal{O}))}.

(1.8)

Finally, if
\mathcal{O}={\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,
T=\infty
and
\frac{s}{d}+\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1}{\eta}+\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{\zeta}
, then it also holds that

{\mathbb{E}}\|u\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}_{+};\dot{H}^{1-s,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))}^{p}\lesssim_{d,s,q,\eta,\zeta}\|\mu\|_{\ell^{\zeta}(\mathcal{S}_{f})}^{p}\,{\mathbb{E}}\|g\|^{p}_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}_{+};L^{\eta}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))}.

(1.9)

The previous is a special case of Theorem
6.3
. In the latter result, we also prove optimal space-time regularity estimates. In particular, the
L^{p}(0,T;H^{1-s,q}(\mathcal{O}))
-norm of the left-hand side of (
1.8
) is replaced by
C([0,T];B^{1-s-2/p}_{q,p}(\mathcal{O}))
while keeping the same norm on the right hand side. Here
B^{\sigma}_{q,p}
denotes the Besov space of smoothness
\sigma
and spatial integrability
q
(see
[
63
]
). The Besov spaces are known to be optimal in maximal estimates (see
[
3
]
). For
1-s-\frac{2}{p}-\frac{d}{q}>0
, the previous also provides estimates in the space
C([0,T]\times\overline{\mathcal{O}})
(by Sobolev embedding), which improves the existing results, see Subsection
6.3
for details. Therefore, our results can also be useful for the random field approach to SPDEs.
A similar statement holds for (
1.9
), with the space
B
replaced by its homogeneous counterpart.

Next, we comment on some of the advantages of our approach to Theorem
1.1
(and hence Theorem
1.2
). For the proof strategy and a comparison to the literature, the reader is referred to Subsections
1.2
and
1.3
, respectively.
Among others, as we do not rely on explicit computations, we avoid several technical difficulties which are often solved by imposing more conditions on the system
\mathcal{S}_{f}
, or its relation to the differential operator involved, see for instance
[
18
]
.
More precisely, our approach has several significant advantages:

•

(Sharpness of the conditions).
We prove the necessity and sufficiency of our conditions on the parameters
(s,q,d,\eta,\zeta)
. Among others, we show the first condition in (
1.6
), i.e.

\displaystyle\frac{s}{d}+\frac{1}{q}\geq\frac{1}{\eta}+\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{\zeta}

is a necessary and sufficient condition for the convergence of (
1.3
), see Proposition
4.3
.

•

(Reaching criticality in nonlinear reaction-diffusion equations).
The sharpness and flexibility in all parameters
(s,q,\eta,\zeta)
allow us to cover critical situations for stochastic reaction-diffusion equations with (non-)trace class noise in
[
2
,
7
]
, in which situation the critical space is an
L^{r}
-space where
r
is related to
\zeta
and the polynomial growth of the nonlinearities.

•

(Flexibility in the
g
-integrability).
The exponent
\eta
for which
g\in L^{\eta}(\mathcal{O})
can be taken smaller than in the preceding results, and even improving the norm on the left-hand side of the corresponding estimate, see the comments in Subsection
6.3.2
for details.

•

(General orthonormal systems
\mathcal{S}_{f}
).
The sequence
\mathcal{S}_{f}=(f_{n})_{n\geq 1}
is merely assumed to be an orthonormal system, which does not need to have any relation to the differential operator in (
1.2
). In particular, in Theorem
1.2
(and its extensions in Subsection
6.2.2
), we can replace the Laplace operator by an operator in divergence form (see Remark
6.5
) with
no a-priori
knowledge of the eigenfunctions of the divergence form operator.
This fact greatly
simplifies and improves
the obtained bounds in the literature, see Subsection
6.3
for a detailed comparison. Let us point out that, due to the generic optimality of the bound (
1.5
), bounds for (
1.3
) in terms of
\|g\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{O})}
could so far only be obtained for
d<6
, while we obtain them for any dimension
d
.

•

(Unbounded domains).
Since we do not need that
\mathcal{S}_{f}
is associated to a differential operator, we can also allow unbounded domains. For the full space
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, we also present a Fourier multiplier approach to estimates (
1.7
), avoiding orthonormal bases altogether.

The sharpness of our results is related to the
scaling invariance
of the corresponding estimates. In the case of Theorem
1.1
, the optimality via scaling is performed in the proof of Proposition
4.3
. Consequently, the optimality of Theorem
1.1
and of stochastic maximal
L^{p}
-regularity estimates
[
48
]
(see also
[
3
, Subsection 6.2]
for homogeneous spaces) imply the sharpness of Theorem
1.2
. However, for illustrative purposes, in the following subsection, we provide a direct argument of the optimality of the first condition in (
1.6
) by a (formal) scaling argument on the stochastic heat equation (
1.2
) with non-trace class noise.

1.1.
Scaling for the stochastic heat equation with colored noise

Consider the stochastic heat equation (
1.2
) on
\mathcal{O}={\mathbb{R}}^{d}
with colored noise
\mathcal{W}^{(\alpha)}
, where the parameter
\alpha\geq 0
determines the behavior of the noise under the parabolic space-time scaling: for
\lambda>0
,
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and
t>0
,

\partial_{t}\mathcal{W}^{(\alpha)}(\lambda^{2}t,\lambda x)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{law}}}{{=}}\lambda^{-1-d/2+\alpha}\,\partial_{t}\mathcal{W}^{(\alpha)}(t,x).

(1.10)

As is well known, space-time white noise satisfies the above with
\alpha=0
.
Let
u
be a solution to the stochastic equation (
1.2
) on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, for all
\lambda>0
,
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and
t>0
, set

u_{\lambda}(t,x)=u(\lambda^{2}t,\lambda x)
. For the latter, by the scaling invariance of the noise (
1.10
), we formally have

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u_{\lambda}(t,x)-\Delta u_{\lambda}(t,x)

\displaystyle=\lambda^{2}\big[\partial_{t}u(\lambda^{2}t,\lambda x)-\Delta u(\lambda^{2}t,\lambda x)\big]

\displaystyle=\lambda^{2}\,g(\lambda^{2}t,\lambda x)\,\partial_{t}\mathcal{W}^{(\alpha)}(\lambda^{2}t,\lambda x)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle{\rm law}}}{{=}}\lambda^{1-d/2+\alpha}g(\lambda^{2}t,\lambda x)\,\partial_{t}\mathcal{W}^{(\alpha)}(t,x)=g_{\lambda}(t,x)\,\partial_{t}\mathcal{W}^{(\alpha)}(t,x),

where

g_{\lambda}(t,x)=\lambda^{1-d/2+\alpha}g(\lambda^{2}t,\lambda x).

To check that the estimate in (
1.9
) of Theorem
1.2
is (roughly)
invariant under the rescaling

(u,g)\mapsto(u_{\lambda},g_{\lambda})
, we need to connect the scaling parameter
\alpha
to the coloring exponent
\zeta
. To this end, we consider a noise defined by means of the multidimensional Haar basis of
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
:

\mathcal{W}^{(\alpha)}(t,x)=\sum_{\sigma\in\Sigma_{d}}\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}(1+|k|^{2})^{-\beta/2}2^{-j\alpha}\psi_{j,k}^{(\sigma)}(x)\,w^{j,k}(t)

(1.11)

where
\Sigma_{d}=\{0,1\}^{d}\setminus\{(0,\dots,0)\}
is the orientation parameter,
\beta>d/2
and
(w^{j,k})_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}},k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}
are standard independent Brownian motions and

\psi^{(\sigma)}_{j,k}(x)=2^{jd/2}\Psi^{(\sigma)}(2^{j}x+k)\ \ \text{ for all }\ j\in{\mathbb{Z}},\ k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}.

with
\Psi^{(\sigma)}\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
the mother wavelet with type
\sigma\in\Sigma_{d}
.
If
d=1
, then
\Sigma_{1}=1
and
\Psi^{(1)}={{\bf 1}}_{[0,1/2)}-{{\bf 1}}_{[1/2,1)}
. Here, to perform the scaling argument, we do not need the precise form of the function
\Psi^{(\sigma)}
. Details on the construction can be found in
[
29
, Chapters 5 and 10]
.
One can check that the noise in (
1.11
) formally respects the scaling relation (
1.10
) in the case
\lambda=2^{m}
for
m\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, while the parameter
\beta
is only needed to ensure spatial integrability, and therefore does not influence the scaling.
Let
\mathcal{S}_{f}
be the Haar basis of
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, and
\mu=(\mu_{j,k})_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}},k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}
with
\mu_{j,k}=(1+|k|^{2})^{-\beta/2}2^{-j\alpha}
. From (
1.4
) and
\|\psi_{j,k}^{(\sigma)}\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}=2^{jd/2}
, for
\zeta\in[2,\infty)
, one has

\sum_{|j|\leq N}\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}|\mu_{j,k}|^{\zeta}\,2^{jd}=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\text{diverges logarithmically as $N\to\infty$ for }\zeta=d/\alpha,\\
&\text{diverges exponentially as $N\to\infty$ otherwise}.\end{aligned}\right.

In particular, the value of
\zeta=d/\alpha
is of specific interest, and to obtain a finite
\ell^{\zeta}(\mathcal{S}_{f})
-norm of the coloring
\mu
(see (
1.4
)), it suffices to modify the one in (
1.11
) at a logarithmic scale compared to the amplitude
2^{-j\alpha}
.
We emphasize that it is natural to have such logarithmic corrections. Indeed, this is already the case of the Lévy modulus of continuity for Brownian motion, and to avoid them requires working, even in the space variable, with Besov spaces like
B^{1/2}_{q,\infty}
and their variants rather than Sobolev or Bessel potential spaces, see e.g.
[
12
,
64
]
.
However, it is worth stressing that scaling
cannot
highlight such logarithmic corrections, as for instance
B^{1/2}_{q,\infty}
has the same scaling as
H^{1/2,q}
, but Brownian paths live in the former but not in the latter space.
Finally, we point out that, for similar reasons but in a quite different context, logarithmic corrections often appear also in the context of critical singular SPDEs, see
[
15
, Subsection 1.3]
and
[
16
, Sections 4-6]
.

For the sake of the scaling argument, we therefore ignore the required logarithmic correction.
Now, if
\zeta=d/\alpha
, then the estimate (
1.9
) in Theorem
1.2
is
invariant
under the natural scaling
(u,g)\mapsto(u_{\lambda},g_{\lambda})
of the SPDE (
1.2
) on
\mathcal{O}={\mathbb{R}}^{d}
if and only if the parameters
(s,q,\eta,\zeta)
satisfy

\frac{s}{d}+\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1}{\eta}+\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{\zeta},

(1.12)

that is the first condition in (
1.6
) with equality.
Indeed, for all
q\in[2,\infty)
and
p\in(2,\infty)
,

\displaystyle\lambda^{1-s-d/q-2/p}({\mathbb{E}}\|u\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}_{+};\dot{H}^{1-s,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))}^{p})^{1/p}

\displaystyle\eqsim({\mathbb{E}}\|u_{\lambda}\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}_{+};\dot{H}^{1-s,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))}^{p})^{1/p}

\displaystyle\lesssim({\mathbb{E}}\|g_{\lambda}\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}_{+};L^{\eta}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))}^{p})^{1/p}

\displaystyle=\lambda^{1-d/2+d/\zeta-d/\eta-2/p}({\mathbb{E}}\|g\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}_{+};L^{\eta}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))}^{p})^{1/p}.

The exponents in the above match if and only if (
1.12
) holds. Hence, the estimates of Theorem
1.2
are sharp. It is routine to check that the above arguments can also be applied to space-time estimates of Section
6
, therefore also proving their optimality.

1.2.
Comparison with the literature

Early works deriving estimates for (

) for
q=2

are
[

,
23
]
and subsequent works such as
[
24
]
.
There,
the condition (

) is replaced by the condition

together with the requirement that

.
It is additionally assumed that the orthonormal system

consists of the eigenfunctions of the differential operator involved. Without this last assumption, under the condition (

), we
are able to prove essentially the same estimate for the stochastic convolution under less restrictive conditions on the parameters

(see Subsection

To the best of our knowledge, a condition of the form (

) first appeared in

]
, where again the system

is assumed to also diagonalize the differential operator involved with eigenvalues
(\alpha_{n})_{n\geq 1}
and an additional constraint of the form
\Lambda:=\sum_{n\geq 1}\alpha_{n}^{-\beta}\|f_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}<\infty
is imposed, where
\beta(\zeta-2)<\zeta
. However, this last condition turns out to be quite restrictive in many realistic situations. While we give a detailed comparison in Subsection
6.3
, we only mention here that, for instance, in dimension
d=2
, the condition
\Lambda<\infty
together with (

) leads to the constraint
\zeta<6
; a restriction we simply do not get here.
Instead, since we do not assume this extra condition

, we obtain the desired estimates for any

(see, again, Subsection
6.3
for a more detailed comparison).
The same condition as in
[
18
]
also appears in
[
19
,
21
]
and follow-up articles.
In the very recent article
[
20
]
estimates for a stochastic convolution were derived only under the assumption that
\mu\in\ell^{\zeta}(\mathcal{S}_{f})
, for
\zeta<2d/(d-2)
in

and

for

, without assuming an additional summability condition in the eigenvalues

of the differential operator involved. In Subsection

we show that our Theorem

allows for the same estimate under the same condition

, without assuming that the
\mathcal{S}_{f}
are the eigenfunctions of the Laplacian.
Apart from the works cited above and their follow-up or preceding articles,
we are not aware of other works using the condition
\mu\in\ell^{\zeta}(\mathcal{S}_{f})
for
\zeta\in[2,\infty)
to obtain associated estimates of the noise term.
Indeed, instead of (
1.4
) often a trace condition is assumed on either the covariance operator itself, or a power of the leading order differential operator

or its semigroup

applied to a power of

To the best of our knowledge, we are the first to provide a sharp characterisation of the convergence of (

1.3.
Proofs strategy and structure of the paper

We begin by giving an informal overview of the proof of Theorem
4.1
(which, in particular, contains Theorem
1.2
). Using the language of
\gamma
-radonifying operators (see Subsection
2.2
), (
1.7
) can be equivalently formulated as

\|M_{g}R_{\mu}\|_{\gamma(L^{2}(\mathcal{O}),H^{-s,q}(\mathcal{O}))}\lesssim\|\mu\|_{\ell^{\zeta}(\mathcal{S}_{f})}\|g\|_{L^{\eta}(\mathcal{O})},

(1.14)

where
M_{g}
is the multiplication operator by
g
, and the operator
R_{\mu}
is defined as

L^{2}(\mathcal{O})\ni h\mapsto\sum_{n\geq 1}\mu_{n}f_{n}(h,e_{n})_{L^{2}(\mathcal{O})},

for a given
(e_{n})_{n\geq 1}
complete orthonormal system of
L^{2}(\mathcal{O})
.
From the equivalence of (
1.7
) and (
1.14
), the choice of such an orthonormal basis
(e_{n})_{n\geq 1}
is irrelevant.

The main idea behind our approach to (
1.14
) is to look at
M_{g}R_{\mu}
as a
bilinear
operator

(g,\mu)\mapsto M_{g}R_{\mu}

(1.15)

for
g
and
\mu
taken from appropriate spaces. As shown in Section
4
, the power of this viewpoint is that it allows us to use
multilinear interpolation
(see Proposition
4.7
or
[
13
, Section 4.4]
) and reduce the full proof of (
1.14
) with
\mu\in\ell^{\zeta}(\mathcal{S}_{f})
and
g\in L^{\eta}(\mathcal{O})
to the following limiting cases:

(1)

\zeta=2
,
s\in(0,d)
,
q\in(1,\infty)
,
\eta\in(1,q)
and
\frac{s}{d}+\frac{1}{q}\geq\frac{1}{\eta}
.

(2)

\zeta=\infty
,
s\in(\frac{d}{2},d)
,
q\in(2,\infty)
,
\eta\in(2,q)
and
\frac{s}{d}+\frac{1}{q}\geq\frac{1}{\eta}+\frac{1}{2}
.

One can readily check that the conditions (
1
) and (
2
) are indeed included in (
1.6
).
The proofs of (
1.14
) in the above cases are given in Subsection
4.1
.

The case (
1
) is the easiest, and is proven in Lemma
4.4
. It follows from Sobolev embeddings and the identification of
\gamma
-radonifying operators
\gamma(L^{2}(\mathcal{O}),L^{\eta}(\mathcal{O}))=L^{\eta}(\mathcal{O};L^{2}(\mathcal{O}))
, see (
2.5
).

The case (
2
) is much more involved. We discuss only the case
\mathcal{O}={\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, as the general one follows by extending by zero. In this case, the core idea is as follows.
As
R_{\mu}\in\mathcal{L}(L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))
since
\mu\in\ell^{\infty}(\mathcal{S}_{f})=\ell^{\infty}
by assumption, and in light of the ideal property of
\gamma
-radonifying operators (see (
2.4
)), it suffices to show

M_{g}\in\gamma(L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}),H^{-s,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})).

(1.16)

Clearly, the above is equivalent to proving
(1-\Delta)^{-s/2}M_{g}\in\gamma(L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}),L^{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))
. Thus, to obtain (
1.16
) under the condition, it is enough to show that the following operator is
\gamma
-radonifying:

\displaystyle L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\ni f\mapsto\big[(1-\Delta)^{-s/2}M_{g}f\big](x)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\mathscr{G}_{s}(x-y)g(y)f(y)\,\mathrm{d}y\in L^{\eta}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})

(1.17)

where
\mathscr{G}_{s}=\mathscr{F}^{-1}((1+4\pi^{2}|\cdot|^{2})^{-s/2})
is the Bessel potential kernel.
Interestingly, the above operator, of convolution type, has already appeared in the literature in the context of Schrödinger operators
[
27
,
44
,
59
]
, see the beginning of Section
3
for additional comments.
The question whether the operator of convolution type in (
1.17
) is
\gamma
-radonifying will be explored in Section
3
. In particular, we prove an estimate that can be regarded as a
\gamma
-radonifying version of the Young inequality for convolutions involving
weak Lebesgue spaces
, see Theorem
3.1
. We emphasize that working with weak-type spaces is essential to accommodate the singularity of the Bessel potential kernel, and the proofs of the operator in (
1.17
) being
\gamma
-radonifying essentially rely on the use of weak Lebesgue spaces, see Remark
3.9
.

Behind the bilinear interpolation argument (
1.15
), there is actually a much more general framework in which one can prove the estimate (
1.14
), for which the weighted sequence case
\mu\in\ell^{\zeta}(\mathcal{S}_{f})
is only a very special case. This will be illustrated in Subsection
4.3
, where, as a prototype example, we will consider the assignment
\mu\mapsto R_{\mu}
replaced by
m\mapsto T_{m}
where
T_{m}
is the Fourier multiplier with symbol
m
and
\mathcal{O}={\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, see (
4.38
). This allows us to cover Gaussian noises that are not only in the form (
1.1
) but also defined via convolution on the whole space.
Explicit examples are given by the
Matérn random fields
in Theorem
6.2
.

In Section
5
, we again apply the general paradigm outlined in Subsection
4.3
using the interpolation argument (
1.15
), but with
\mu
from an unweighted sequence space. The main reason for that is to show the necessity of the weights
\|f_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{O})}^{2}
in the condition (
1.4
) to capture how the noise coloring
\zeta
enters in the condition ruling
(s,q,\eta)
(i.e. the first in (
1.6
)), and therefore, also necessary for sharpness and/or scaling invariance in applications to SPDEs, see Subsection
1.1
.

In Section
6
, we investigate the consequences of Theorem
4.1
to SPDEs of elliptic and parabolic type.
Here we focus on elliptic equations of Matérn type, and the stochastic heat equation (
1.2
). For the latter, we formally rewrite the noisy part of the equation
g\,\partial_{t}\mathcal{W}
as

g\,\partial_{t}\mathcal{W}=M_{g}R_{\mu}\dot{W}

(1.18)

where
\mathcal{W}
is as in (
1.1
) and
W
its associated
L^{2}(\mathcal{O})
-cylindrical Brownian motion.
With the above identification and Theorem
4.1
at our disposal, Theorem
6.3
(which extends Theorem
1.2
) is a consequence of stochastic maximal
L^{p}
-regularity estimates proven in
[
48
]
(and
[
3
, Subsection 6.2]
for homogeneous spaces).

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Function spaces

For details on function spaces, the reader is referred to
[
38
]
and
[
62
,
63
]
. For
p\in[1,\infty]
and a Banach space
X
and an interval
I\subset\mathbb{R}_{+}
we define
L^{p}(I;X)
as the space of strongly measurable functions
f:I\to X
, such that

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{p}(I;X)}:=\Big(\int_{I}\|f(t)\|_{X}\,\mathrm{d}t\Big)^{1/p}<\infty.

(2.1)

For
p\in[1,\infty)
and a measure space
(S,\Sigma,\nu)
we define
the space
L^{p,\infty}(S,X)
, often called the weak
L^{p}
space, as the space of strongly measurable functions
f:S\to X
for which

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{p,\infty}(S;X)}:=\sup_{r>0}r\,\big[\nu(\|f\|_{X}>r)\big]^{1/p}<\infty.

(2.2)

If
X=\mathbb{R}
we simply write
L^{p,\infty}(S)
.

For
\sigma\in{\mathbb{R}}
and
q\in(1,\infty)
define the Bessel potential spaces
H^{\sigma,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
as all
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
such that
\|f\|_{H^{\sigma,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}=\|(1-\Delta)^{\sigma/2}f\|_{L^{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}<\infty
.
Similarly one can define
\dot{H}^{s,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
and for details see
[
62
, Chapter 5]
.
Similarly (see
[
32
, Subsection 1.3.3]
), one defines the homogeneous
Bessel potential spaces
\dot{H}^{\sigma,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
as the set of all
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})/\mathcal{P}
such that
\|(-\Delta)^{\sigma/2}f\|_{L^{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}<\infty
, where
\mathcal{P}
are the set of all real polynomials on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
. It is worth noting that homogeneous Sobolev functions can often be identified as standard distributions by suitably removing polynomials, exploiting their smoothness, see e.g.
[
56
, Theorem 2.31]
. Interestingly, the latter result shows that the homogeneous spaces of our interest
\dot{H}^{-s,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
can be realized as a subset of the Schwartz distributions
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, for all
s\geq 0
and
q\in(1,\infty)
.

For an open set
\mathcal{O}\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
let

H^{\sigma,q}(\mathcal{O})=\{g|_{\mathcal{O}}:g\in H^{\sigma,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\},

where
g|_{\mathcal{O}}
is defined as an element of
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathcal{O})
in the natural way.
The space
H^{\sigma,q}(\mathcal{O})
is equipped with the natural quotient norm
\|f\|_{H^{\sigma,q}(\mathcal{O})}=\inf\|g\|_{H^{\sigma,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}
, where the infimum is taken over all
g\in H^{\sigma,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
such that
g|_{\mathcal{O}}=f
.

We will frequently use that, by Sobolev embeddings, if
\sigma_{1}-\frac{d}{q_{1}}\geq\sigma_{0}-\frac{d}{q_{0}}
and
\sigma_{1}>\sigma_{0}
and
q_{0}>q_{1}
, then
H^{s_{1},q_{1}}(\mathcal{O})\hookrightarrow H^{s_{0},q_{0}}(\mathcal{O})
.

2.2.
\gamma
-radonifying operators

Here, we give definitions and basic results on
\gamma
-radonifying operators.
The reader is referred to
[
39
, Chapter 9]
for further results.

Let
H
and
X
be a separable Hilbert space and a Banach space, respectively.
Let
(\gamma_{n})_{n\geq 1}
be a sequence of standard independent Gaussian variables over a given probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{A},{\mathbb{P}})
, and let
(h_{n})_{n\geq 1}
be an orthonormal basis of
H
.
We say that a linear bounded
T:H\to X
is
\gamma
-radonifying
if
\sum_{n\geq 1}\gamma_{n}Th_{n}
converges in
L^{2}(\Omega;X)
and we set

\|T\|_{\gamma(H,X)}=\Big({\mathbb{E}}\Big\|\sum_{n\geq 1}\gamma_{n}Th_{n}\Big\|_{X}^{2}\Big)^{1/2}.

(2.3)

Note that
\gamma(H,X)\hookrightarrow\mathcal{L}(H,X)
. Moreover, from the comments below
[
39
, Definition 9.1.1]
, it also follows that the exponent
2
can be replaced by any exponent
p\in[1,\infty)
.
Moreover,
[
39
, Theorem 9.1.17]
ensures that the above definition is independent of the orthonormal basis
(h_{n})_{n\geq 1}
chosen on
H
.
In particular, any finite operator with finite-dimensional range is
\gamma
-radonifying.

An important property of the
\gamma
-spaces is the so-called
ideal property
, see
[
39
, Theorem 9.1.10]
. The latter ensures that, if
R\in\mathcal{L}(H,K)
,
T\in\gamma(K,X)
and
S\in\mathcal{L}(X,Y)
, the space of bounded operators from
X
to
Y
, where
Y
is another Banach spaces, then

STR\in\gamma(H,Y)
and

\|STR\|_{\gamma(H,Y)}\leq\|S\|_{\mathcal{L}(X,Y)}\|T\|_{\gamma(K,X)}\|R\|_{\mathcal{L}(H,K)}.

(2.4)

An alternative description of
\gamma(H,X)
is available when
X
is an
L^{p}(S)
space over a
\sigma
-finite measure space
(S,\Sigma,\nu)
. Indeed, in the latter situation, it holds that
\gamma(H,L^{p}(S))=L^{p}(S,X)
and

\displaystyle\|T\|_{\gamma(H,L^{p}(S))}\eqsim_{p}\Big\|\Big(\sum_{n\geq 1}|Th_{n}|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\Big\|_{L^{p}(S)},

(2.5)

due to the
\gamma
-Fubini theorem
[
39
, Theorem 9.4.8]
. Moreover,
\gamma(H,H)=\mathcal{L}_{2}(H)
coincides with the Hilbert-Schmidt operators (see
[
39
, Proposition 9.1.9]
).

3.
The
\gamma
-Young inequality and consequences

In this section, we study the mapping properties of the convolution-type operator

\displaystyle A_{f,g}h(x):=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}f(x-y)g(y)h(y)\,\mathrm{d}y,\quad h\in L^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),

(3.1)

whenever it exists as a Lebesgue integral for almost all
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
for given measurable functions
f,g:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
.

The above operator
A_{f,g}
appeared already in the study of eigenvalues of Schrödinger operators (e.g.
-\Delta+V
with potential
V\in L^{d/2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
), and related operators in terms of Fourier multipliers were studied in
[
27
,
59
,
60
]
in the context of Schatten class spaces. The reader is also referred to
[
44
]
for further results and a historical account.
In this section, we give sufficient conditions under which
A_{f,g}
defines an element of
\gamma(L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}),L^{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))
for
q\in[2,\infty)
.

Theorem 3.1

(
\gamma
-Young inequality)
.

Let
\eta,r,q\in(2,\infty)
be such that
\frac{1}{q}+\frac{1}{2}=\frac{1}{r}+\frac{1}{\eta}
. Let
f\in L^{r,\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
and
g\in L^{\eta}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
. Then

A_{f,g}\in\gamma(L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}),L^{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))

and

\displaystyle\|A_{f,g}\|_{\gamma(L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}),L^{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))}\lesssim_{\eta,q,r}\|f\|_{L^{r,\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\|g\|_{L^{\eta}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}.

(3.2)

Note that the range of parameters
(q,r,\eta)
in the above imply
q>\eta
and
q>r
.
From the proof of the above, it follows that for
f,g\in L^{0}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
and
q\in[2,\infty)
(see (
3.6
) below),

\|A_{f,g}\|_{\gamma(L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}),L^{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))}\eqsim_{q}\|A_{g,f}\|_{\gamma(L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}),L^{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))}.

(3.3)

In particular, one can reverse the role of the weak-Lebesgue spaces in (
3.2
). More precisely, under the assumptions of Theorem
3.1
, it also holds that

\|A_{f,g}\|_{\gamma(L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}),L^{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))}\lesssim_{\eta,q,r}\|f\|_{L^{r}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\|g\|_{L^{\eta,\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}.

In Subsection
3.1
, we also investigate the endpoint cases of the
\gamma
-Young inequality, namely the cases
\eta=2
and
\eta=q
, see Propositions
3.5
and
3.6
, respectively. In both cases, we show that the
\gamma
-Young inequality (
3.2
) can hold only if the weak Lebesgue space
L^{r,\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
is replaced by the usual Lebesgue space
L^{r}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
.

From Theorem
3.1
and its proof, we derive the following result, which is of central importance in the study of Sobolev embeddings of Gaussian random sums as Theorems
1.1
and
4.1
.

Proposition 3.2

(
\gamma
-bounds for multiplication operators in Sobolev spaces)
.

Let
M_{g}
be the multiplication operator with a measurable function
g:{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\to{\mathbb{R}}
.
Then the following hold:

(1)

(Sufficient conditions)

If
(s,q,\eta)
satisfy one of the following conditions:

(a)

s\in(\frac{d}{2},d)
,
q\in(2,\infty)
,
\eta\in(2,q)
and
\frac{s}{d}+\frac{1}{q}\geq\frac{1}{\eta}+\frac{1}{2}
;

(b)

s>\frac{d}{2}
and
\eta=q\geq 2
;

(c)

s>\frac{d}{2}
,
\eta=2
,
q\in[2,\infty)
and
\frac{s}{d}+\frac{1}{q}>1
,

then for all
g\in L^{\eta}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
one has
M_{g}\in\gamma(L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}),H^{-s,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))
and

\|M_{g}\|_{\gamma(L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}),H^{-s,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))}\lesssim_{d,s,q,\eta}\|g\|_{L^{\eta}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}.

(2)

(Necessary conditions)
If for all
g\in L^{\eta}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
one has
\|M_{g}\|_{\gamma(L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}),H^{-s,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))}\lesssim_{d,s,q,\eta}\|g\|_{L^{\eta}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}

for some
\eta,q\in[2,\infty)
and
s\geq 0
, then

\frac{s}{d}+\frac{1}{q}\geq\frac{1}{\eta}+\frac{1}{2}
,
\eta\leq q
, and
s>\frac{d}{2}
.

(3)

(Necessary conditions)
If additionally to (
2
) one has
\frac{s}{d}+\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1}{\eta}+\frac{1}{2}
, then
s\in(\frac{d}{2},d)
and
\eta\in(2,q)
.

A similar result also holds for homogeneous Sobolev spaces.

Proposition 3.3

(
\gamma
-bounds for multiplication operators in homogeneous Sobolev spaces)
.

Assume that
s\geq 0
,
\eta,q\in[2,\infty)
and
g\in L^{\eta}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
.
Let
M_{g}
be the multiplication operator by
g
.
Then
M_{g}\in\gamma(L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}),\dot{H}^{-s,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))
and

\|M_{g}\|_{\gamma(L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}),\dot{H}^{-s,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))}\lesssim_{d,s,q,\eta}\|g\|_{L^{\eta}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}.

if and only if

\frac{s}{d}+\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1}{\eta}+\frac{1}{2},\qquad s\in\Big(\frac{d}{2},d\Big)\ \quad\text{ and }\quad\ \eta\in(2,q).

(3.4)

The conditions in the previous result are also contained in item (
1a
) of Proposition
3.2
. In light of Proposition
3.3
and the scaling invariance of the homogeneous Sobolev spaces
\dot{H}^{-s,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, the condition
\frac{s}{d}+\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1}{\eta}+\frac{1}{2}

respects the scaling of the spaces involved, and will be referred to as the
sharp
case, as in all the other cases considered in Proposition
3.2
, there is a loss of smoothness (and hence, a break of the scaling invariance). To obtain the sharp case, we necessarily require the weak Lebesgue spaces in Theorem
3.1
, see Remark
3.9
.

Before presenting the proofs, let us discuss the connection between Theorem
3.1
and Proposition
3.2
(the one with Proposition
3.3
is similar). Note that the condition
M_{g}\in\gamma(L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}),H^{-s,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))
holds if and only if
(1-\Delta)^{-s/2}M_{g}\in\gamma(L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}),L^{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))
. Moreover, since

\big[(1-\Delta)^{-s/2}M_{g}f\big](x)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\mathscr{G}_{s}(x-y)g(y)f(y)\,\mathrm{d}y=A_{\mathscr{G}_{s},g}f(x)

(3.5)

where
\mathscr{G}_{s}=\mathscr{F}^{-1}((1+4\pi^{2}|\cdot|^{2})^{-s/2})
is the Bessel potential kernel, to prove Proposition
3.2
it is enough to estimate the weak Lebesgue norm of
\mathscr{G}_{s}
. In particular, we will see that the use of the weak Lebesgue spaces in Theorem
3.1
is necessary to obtain sharp estimates for Gaussian series in the next section, see Remark
3.9
.

Remark 3.4
.

It will be clear from the proofs below that Theorem
3.1
holds in case
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
is replaced by a locally compact Abelian group equipped with the Haar measure (e.g.
\mathbb{T}^{d}
).
In particular, this implies that Proposition
3.2
holds true also in the periodic case.

in Propositions

Proof of Theorem
3.1
.

Let
(h_{k})_{k\geq 1}
be an orthonormal basis for
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
. From the square-function characterization of
\gamma(L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}),L^{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))
in (
2.5
), it follows that

\displaystyle\|A_{f,g}\|_{\gamma(L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}),L^{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))}

\displaystyle\eqsim_{q}\Big\|\Big(\sum_{k\geq 1}|A_{f,g}h_{k}|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\Big\|_{L^{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}.

The Parseval identity in
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
applied pointwise in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
implies

\displaystyle\sum_{k\geq 1}|A_{f,g}h_{k}(x)|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\geq 1}\Big|\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}f(x-y)g(y)h_{k}(y)\,\mathrm{d}y\Big|^{2}=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}|f(x-y)|^{2}|g(y)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}y,

whenever
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
is such that the last term in the previous is finite. Combining the previous observations, we have

\displaystyle\|A_{f,g}\|_{\gamma(L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}),L^{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))}^{2}\eqsim_{q}\Big\|x\mapsto\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}F(x-y)G(y)\,\mathrm{d}y\Big\|_{L^{q/2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})},

(3.6)

where
F=|f|^{2}
and
G=|g|^{2}
. Applying Young’s inequality for weak type spaces (see
[
31
, Theorem 1.4.25]
) we obtain, for
\tfrac{2}{q}+1=\tfrac{2}{r}+\tfrac{2}{\eta}
,

\displaystyle\Big\|x\mapsto\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}F(x-y)G(y)\,\mathrm{d}y\Big\|_{L^{q/2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\eta,q,r}\|F\|_{L^{r/2,\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\|G\|_{L^{\eta/2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}=\|f\|_{L^{r,\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}^{2}\|g\|_{L^{\eta}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}^{2}.

The claimed estimate (
3.2
) now follows from the above and (
3.6
).
∎

As commented below Theorem
3.1
, below we discuss the validity of the
\gamma
-Young inequality (
3.2
). For that purpose, let us note that, from
[
60
, Chapter 4]
, it follows that
A_{f,g}\in\gamma(L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}),L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))=S^{2}(L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))
if and only if
f,g\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
. Moreover, in this case

\displaystyle\|A_{f,g}\|_{\gamma(L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}),L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))}=\|A_{f,g}\|_{S^{2}(L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))}=\|f\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\|g\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}.

(3.7)

Below, we extend this result to
q>2
if
\eta=2
.

Proposition 3.5

(Endpoint case

.

Moreover, letting

From (

Proof.

We first prove (

\Rightarrow
(

).
Let
g\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, and set
F=|f|^{2}
and
G=|g|^{2}
. Using (

) and Young’s inequality for convolutions, we find that

\displaystyle\|A_{g,f}\|_{\gamma(L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}),L^{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))}^{2}

\displaystyle\eqsim_{q}\|G*F\|_{L^{q/2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\leq\|F\|_{L^{q/2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\|G\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}=\|f\|_{L^{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}^{2}\|g\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}^{2}.

(3.8)

Next, we prove (

\Rightarrow
(

). It is sufficient to prove

for all

\displaystyle C_{f}:=\sup_{\|g\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\leq 1}\|A_{g,f}\|_{\gamma(L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}),L^{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))}.

(3.9)

Let

be nonnegative and such that

.
Then let

, for

Thus, using

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}^{2}=\|F\|_{L^{q/2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\|G_{n}*F\|_{L^{q/2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\eqsim_{q}\lim_{n\to\infty}\|A_{f,g_{n}}\|_{\gamma(L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}),L^{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))}\leq C_{f}

∎

Proposition 3.6

)

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
k\geq 0
be measurable and let

. Then the following are equivalent

(1)

There exists a
C
such that for all

.

(2)

There exists a
C
such that for all
\phi\in L^{p}_{+}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
,
\|k*\phi\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\leq C\|\phi\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}
.

(3)

k\in L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
.

Moreover, in this case
\|k\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}=\inf C
, where the infimum is taken over all admissible constants
C
in (
1
).

Proof.

(
1
)
\Rightarrow
(
2
) is trivial.
(
3
)
\Rightarrow
(
1
) is immediate from Minkowski’s convolution inequality.
To prove
(
2
)
\Rightarrow
(
3
), let
B_{R}
and
B_{L}
denote the balls centered at
0
of radius
R
and
L
, respectively. Define
k_{L}={{\bf 1}}_{B_{L}}k
, and
f_{R}={{\bf 1}}_{B_{R}}
.
Then, by the positivity of

, we see that

\displaystyle\|k_{L}*f_{R}\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\leq\|k*f_{R}\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\leq C\|f_{R}\|_{p}=C|B_{R}|^{1/p},

where we used again Minkowski’s convolution inequality, and where
|B_{R}|
denotes the Lebesgue measure of the set
B_{R}
.
On the other hand, by Fubini’s theorem and Hölder’s inequality, one has

\displaystyle\|k_{L}\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\|f_{R}\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}

\displaystyle=\|k_{L}*f_{R}\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}

(3.11)

\displaystyle\leq\|k_{L}*f_{R}\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}|B_{R+L}|^{1/p^{\prime}}\leq C|B_{R}|^{1/p}|B_{R+L}|^{1/p^{\prime}},

(3.12)

where for

. If

, then (

) already gives the result.
Therefore,
\|k_{L}\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\leq C\frac{|B_{R+L}|^{1/p^{\prime}}}{|B_{R}|^{1/p^{\prime}}}
.
Letting

\|k_{L}\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\leq C
. Letting

, the monotone convergence theorem gives that
\|k\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\leq C
.
∎

Remark 3.8

(Optimality of the conditions
\eta\geq 2
and
\eta>q
)
.

In this remark, we prove that none of the results proven in this subsection holds for either
\eta>q\geq 2
or
\eta\in(0,2)
.
The first follows from (
3.6
) and the fact that, if
G\mapsto F*G
is bounded from
L^{\eta/2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
into
L^{q/2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, and
\eta>q\geq 2
, then
F=0
due to
[
31
, Theorem 2.5.6]
. Next, we provide an alternative argument that works for any
q,\eta\in(0,\infty)
. Fix
F\in C^{\infty}_{c}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, and suppose that
\|F*G\|_{L^{q/2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\lesssim\|G\|_{L^{\eta/2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}
holds for all
G\in C^{\infty}_{c}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
with implicit constant independent of
G
. For
\phi\in C^{\infty}_{c}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
and
N\geq 1
, consider
G(x)=\sum_{k=1}^{N}\phi(x-kM)
, where
M
will be chosen below. Then
F*G(x)=\sum_{k=1}^{N}F*\phi(x-kM)
. Choosing
M
large enough, we have that the functions in the sum appearing in
G
and
F*G
have disjoint support. Therefore,

\|G\|_{L^{\eta/2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\eqsim_{\phi}N^{2/\eta}\qquad\text{ and }\qquad\|F*G\|_{L^{q/2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\eqsim_{\phi,F}N^{2/q}.

Sending
N\to\infty
in the estimate
\|F*G\|_{L^{q/2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\lesssim\|G\|_{L^{\eta/2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}
yields
\eta\leq q
.

Finally, we show the necessity of
\eta\geq 2
. Indeed, if
\eta\in(0,2)
and
F\in L^{q/2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
takes values in
[0,\infty)
, then
\|F*G\|_{L^{q/2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\lesssim\|G\|_{L^{\eta/2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}
cannot hold with implicit constant independent of
G
.
Indeed, suppose that the estimate holds. If
\eta\in(0,2)
and
G\in C^{\infty}_{{\rm c}}({\mathbb{R}})
such that
\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}G(x)\,\mathrm{d}x=1
, then taking
G_{\varepsilon}=\varepsilon^{-d}G(x/\varepsilon)
, it follows that
F*G_{\varepsilon}\to F
in
L^{q/2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
. On the other hand,
\|G_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\eta/2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\to 0
as
\varepsilon\downarrow 0
, implying
F=0
.

3.2.
Proof of Propositions

and

We begin by proving Proposition

, which concerns inhomogeneous Sobolev spaces.

Proof of Proposition
3.2
.

We begin by collecting some facts. First, as noticed below Proposition
3.2
, it is enough to show

(1-\Delta)^{-s/2}M_{g}\in\gamma(L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}),L^{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))
with a corresponding estimate. As in (
3.5
), we have

(1-\Delta)^{-s/2}M_{g}=A_{\mathscr{G}_{s},g}\qquad\text{ where }\qquad\mathscr{G}_{s}=\mathscr{F}^{-1}((1+|\cdot|^{2})^{-s/2}),

(3.13)

where the operator
A_{\mathscr{G}_{s},g}
is given in (
3.1
), with
(\mathscr{G}_{s},g)
in place of
(f,g)
, and
\mathscr{F}^{-1}
denotes the inverse Fourier transform. It is well-known that (see for instance
[
32
, p.16]
)

\displaystyle\mathscr{G}_{s}(x)\eqsim\begin{cases}|x|^{s-d}&\text{ if }\ |x|\leq 2,\\
e^{-\frac{|x|}{2}}&\text{ if }\ |x|>2.\end{cases}

(3.14)

We now divide the proof into several steps.

Proof of (
1
) in case
s\in(\frac{d}{2},d)
,
q\in(2,\infty)
,
\eta\in(2,q)
and
\frac{s}{d}+\frac{1}{q}\geq\frac{1}{\eta}+\frac{1}{2}
.
Without loss of generality, we can assume the latter estimate is an identity.
Let
r\in(2,\infty)
be given by

\frac{1}{q}+\frac{1}{2}=\frac{1}{\eta}+\frac{1}{r}
.
Then
r\in(2,\infty)
due to
s\in(d/2,d)
.
From (
3.13
) and Theorem
3.1
, it follows that

\|(1-\Delta)^{-s/2}M_{g}\|_{\gamma(L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}),L^{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))}\lesssim_{\eta,q,d}\|\mathscr{G}_{s}\|_{L^{r,\infty}}\|g\|_{L^{\eta}}.

From (
3.14
), it follows that,

\displaystyle|\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\,:\,\mathscr{G}_{s}(x)\geq\rho\}|\lesssim\rho^{d/(s-d)}=\rho^{-r}\ \text{ for all }\ \rho>0.

Hence
\mathscr{G}_{s}\in L^{r,\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
.

Step 2: Remaining cases in (
1
).

Let
\eta=q\geq 2
and
s>d/2
. Then from (
3.14
) it follows that
\mathscr{G}_{s}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Hence, by Proposition
3.6
,

\displaystyle\|(1-\Delta)^{-s/2}M_{g}\|_{\gamma(L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}),L^{\eta}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))}\lesssim_{d,s,\eta}\|g\|_{L^{\eta}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}.

(3.15)

Meanwhile, in the case
s>d/2
,
\eta=2
,
q\in[2,\infty)
and
\frac{s}{d}+\frac{1}{q}>1
, we have
\mathscr{G}_{s}\in L^{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
. Thus, Proposition
3.5
gives

\displaystyle\|(1-\Delta)^{-s/2}M_{g}\|_{\gamma(L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}),L^{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))}\lesssim_{d,s}\|g\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}.

(3.16)

Step 3: Proof of (
2
)
.
The assumptions in (
2
) and (
3.6
) imply,

\displaystyle\Big\|x\mapsto\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}|\mathscr{G}_{s}(x-y)|^{2}\widetilde{g}(y)\,\mathrm{d}y\Big\|_{L^{q/2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\lesssim\|\widetilde{g}\|_{L^{\eta/2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})},\ \ \ \widetilde{g}\in L^{\eta/2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\ \text{with}\ \widetilde{g}\geq 0.

(3.17)

First, let us note that, as in Remark
3.8
, the above inequality implies
\eta\leq q
.

Let
B_{\delta}
be the ball centered at the origin with radius
\delta
. Applying the above with
\widetilde{g}={{\bf 1}}_{B_{\delta}}
for
\delta\in(0,1)
and using the behaviour of
\mathscr{G}_{s}
on
B_{1}
in (
3.14
),

\displaystyle\Big\|x\mapsto\int_{B_{\delta}}|x-y|^{2(s-d)}{{\bf 1}}_{B_{\delta}}(y)\,\mathrm{d}y\Big\|_{L^{q/2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big\|x\mapsto\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}|\mathscr{G}_{s}(x-y)|^{2}{{\bf 1}}_{B_{\delta}}(y)\,\mathrm{d}y\Big\|_{L^{q/2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq: cond tilde g Bessel}}}{{\lesssim}}\delta^{\frac{2d}{\eta}}.

(3.18)

From the above, it follows that
s>\tfrac{d}{2}
, as otherwise the left-most side of the above inequality would diverge. To see the necessity of
\tfrac{s}{d}+\tfrac{1}{q}\geq\tfrac{1}{\eta}+\tfrac{1}{2}
, for
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
such that
|x|<\delta
, we compute

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\delta}}|x-y|^{2(s-d)}\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\geq\int_{|z|<\delta-|x|}|z|^{2(s-d)}\,\mathrm{d}z

(3.19)

\displaystyle\eqsim_{d}\int_{0}^{\delta-|x|}r^{2(s-d)+d-1}\,\mathrm{d}r\eqsim_{s,d}(\delta-|x|)^{2s-d}.

(3.20)

Next, we compute

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\delta}}(\delta-|x|)^{\frac{q}{2}(2s-d)}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\eqsim_{d}\int_{0}^{\delta}(\delta-r)^{\frac{q}{2}(2s-d)}r^{d-1}\,\mathrm{d}r

(3.21)

\displaystyle=\delta^{\frac{q}{2}(2s-d)+d}\int_{0}^{1}(1-r)^{\frac{q}{2}(2s-d)}r^{d-1}\,\mathrm{d}r\eqsim_{q,s,d}\delta^{\frac{q}{2}(2s-d)+d},

(3.22)

where the integral on the right-hand side in (
3.22
) is a Beta function, evaluating to a constant independent of
\delta
.
Combining the latter with (
3.18
) and (
3.19
), we get

\displaystyle\delta^{2s-d+\frac{2d}{q}}\lesssim\Big\|x\mapsto\int_{B_{\delta}}|x-y|^{2(s-d)}{{\bf 1}}_{B_{\delta}(0)}(y)\,\mathrm{d}y\Big\|_{L^{q/2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\lesssim\delta^{\frac{2d}{\eta}}.

(3.23)

The above, for
\delta\to 0
, implies
2s-d+\frac{2d}{q}\geq\tfrac{2d}{\eta}
, that is
\tfrac{s}{d}+\tfrac{1}{q}\geq\tfrac{1}{\eta}+\tfrac{1}{2}
as desired.

Next, we prove the final assertion in (
3
).
Suppose that
\frac{s}{d}+\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1}{\eta}+\frac{1}{2}
, then due to
s>d/2
we see that
\eta<q
.
Moreover, as
\eta\geq 1
, we have
\frac{s}{d}\leq\frac{1}{\eta}+\frac{1}{2}\leq 1
and thus
s\leq d
.
Now we suppose that
\eta=2
and show that this leads to a contradiction. Indeed, as before, from (
3.17
) we get

\displaystyle\Big\|x\mapsto\int_{B_{r}}|\mathscr{G}_{s}(x-y)|^{2}\widetilde{g}(y)\,\mathrm{d}y\Big\|_{L^{q/2}(B_{r})}\lesssim\|\widetilde{g}\|_{L^{1}(B_{r})},

(3.24)

for
r>0
.
Taking
\widetilde{g}:=\widetilde{g}_{\delta}:=|B_{\delta}|^{-1}{{\bf 1}}_{B_{\delta}}
with
\delta<r<1
and letting
\delta\downarrow 0
we obtain
that (using that
\mathscr{G}_{s}
is smooth outside zero)

\displaystyle\int_{B_{r}}|\mathscr{G}_{s}(x-y)|^{2}\widetilde{g}_{\delta}(y)\,\mathrm{d}y\to|\mathscr{G}_{s}(x)|^{2}\ \ \text{for all $x\neq 0$.}

Therefore, from Fatou’s lemma and (
3.24
) we obtain that

\displaystyle\|\mathscr{G}_{s}\|_{L^{q}(B_{r})}^{2}

\displaystyle=\big\||\mathscr{G}_{s}|^{2}\big\|_{L^{q/2}(B_{r})}\leq\lim_{\delta\downarrow 0}\Big\|x\mapsto\int_{B_{r}}|\mathscr{G}_{s}(x-y)|^{2}\widetilde{g}_{\delta}(y)\,\mathrm{d}y\Big\|_{L^{q/2}(B_{r})}\lesssim 1.

(3.25)

Since
\mathscr{G}_{s}(x)\sim|x|^{s-d}
by (
3.14
)
for
x\to 0
, the previous bound implies the integrability of
|x|^{q(s-d)}
near
0
, and therefore
(s-d)q>-d
, or equivalently
\frac{s}{d}-1>-\frac{1}{q}
.
Due to
\frac{s}{d}+\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1}{\eta}+\frac{1}{2}
we get
\tfrac{1}{\eta}>\tfrac{1}{2}
, which contradicts
\eta=2
.
∎

It remains to prove Proposition
3.3
.

Proof of Proposition
3.3
.

We divide the proof into two steps.

Step 1: If (
3.4
) holds, then
\|M_{g}\|_{\gamma(L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}),\dot{H}^{-s,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))}\lesssim\|g\|_{L^{\eta}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}
.

To begin, similarly to (
3.5
) it is enough to consider the operator
(-\Delta)^{-s/2}M_{g}
. Note that

(-\Delta)^{-s/2}M_{g}=A_{\mathscr{R}_{s},g}

where
\mathscr{R}_{s}=(2\pi)^{s}\mathscr{F}^{-1}(|\cdot|^{-s})
denotes the Riesz kernel. It is well-known that

\mathscr{R}_{s}(x)=C_{d,s}|x|^{s-d}\ \text{ for }\ x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d},

see for instance
[
32
, p.9]
.
In particular,
|\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\,:\,\mathscr{R}_{s}(x)\geq\rho\}|\eqsim\rho^{d/(s-d)}
for
\rho>0
and hence
\mathscr{R}_{s}\in L^{r,\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})

for
r=d/(d-s)
. Thus, from Theorem
3.1
, as before

\|(-\Delta)^{-s/2}M_{g}\|_{\gamma(L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}),L^{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))}\lesssim\|\mathscr{R}_{s}\|_{L^{r,\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\|g\|_{L^{\eta}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\lesssim\|g\|_{L^{\eta}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}.

Step 2: The estimate
\|M_{g}\|_{\gamma(L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}),\dot{H}^{-s,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))}\lesssim\|g\|_{L^{\eta}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}
implies (
3.4
).

Let
\mathscr{R}_{s}
be as in Step 1. Arguing as in Step 3 of Proposition
3.2
, the assumptions and (
3.6
) imply that

\displaystyle\Big\|x\mapsto\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}|\mathscr{R}_{s}(x-y)|^{2}\widetilde{g}(y)\,\mathrm{d}y\Big\|_{L^{q/2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\lesssim\|\widetilde{g}\|_{L^{\eta/2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})},\ \ \ \widetilde{g}\in L^{\eta/2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\ \text{with}\ \widetilde{g}\geq 0.

(3.26)

Since the Riesz kernel has the same behaviour as the Bessel potential kernel
\mathcal{G}_{s}
on
|x|\leq 2
, the estimate (
3.18
) holds also in the present situation. Hence,
s>\frac{d}{2}
follows. Similarly
\eta>2
by the argument below (
3.24
).

To conclude this step, we perform a scaling argument. More precisely, applying (
3.26
) with
\widetilde{g}
replaced by
\widetilde{g}_{\lambda}:=\widetilde{g}(\lambda\,\cdot)
for
\lambda>0
, we obtain

\displaystyle\lambda^{d-2s-(2d)/q}\Big\|x\mapsto

\displaystyle\,\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}|\mathscr{R}_{s}(x-y)|^{2}\widetilde{g}(y)\,\mathrm{d}y\Big\|_{L^{q/2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}

\displaystyle\ =\Big\|x\mapsto\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}|\mathscr{R}_{s}(x-y)|^{2}\widetilde{g}_{\lambda}(y)\,\mathrm{d}y\Big\|_{L^{q/2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq: cond tilde g Riesz}}}{{\lesssim}}\|\widetilde{g}_{\lambda}\|_{L^{\eta/2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}=\lambda^{-(2d)/\eta}\|\widetilde{g}\|_{L^{\eta/2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}.

The arbitrariness of
\lambda>0
implies
d-2s-\frac{2d}{q}=-\frac{2d}{\eta}
, which is equivalent to the claimed condition
\frac{s}{d}+\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1}{\eta}+\frac{1}{2}
. As we already proved
s>\frac{d}{2}
, the previous implies
\eta<q
.
Finally, as
\eta>2
we have
\frac{1}{\eta}+\frac{1}{2}<1
, and hence
s<d
as desired.
∎

Remark 3.9

(Sharpness
\&
weak Lebesgue space)
.

From the proof of Proposition
3.2
(
1a
) and Proposition
3.3
, it is clear that the presence of the weak Lebesgue space on the right-hand side of the
\gamma
-Young’s inequality (
3.2
) is essential in the sharp case, i.e. in the case

\frac{s}{d}+\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1}{\eta}+\frac{1}{2}.

4.
Main results – Sharp Sobolev embeddings for Gaussian series

Here, we state and prove the main result of this manuscript, which generalizes Theorem
1.1
to the case of orthonormal systems with (arbitrary) growth. To state it, we introduce some notation. Let

\mathcal{S}_{f}=(f_{n})_{n\geq 1}\subseteq L^{2}(\mathcal{O})

be an orthonormal system of
L^{2}(\mathcal{O})
.
Let
(e_{n})_{n\geq 1}
be a complete orthonormal basis of
L^{2}(\mathcal{O})
, and
(\mu_{n})_{n\geq 1}\subseteq\ell^{0}
be a sequence. Consider the linear operator

R_{\mu}=\sum_{n\geq 1}\mu_{n}\,e_{n}\otimes f_{n},

(4.1)

where
(e_{n}\otimes f_{n})(h)=(h,e_{n})_{L^{2}(\mathcal{O})}f_{n}
for
h\in L^{2}(\mathcal{O})
.
Next, we introduce a space of sequences that encodes the possible growth of the orthonormal system
\mathcal{S}_{f}
in the
L^{\infty}
-norm.
For
\mathcal{S}_{f}=(f_{n})_{n\geq 1}
as above, and
\zeta\in[1,\infty)
, we let

\displaystyle\ell^{\zeta}(\mathcal{S}_{f}):=\Big\{\mu=(\mu_{n})_{n\geq 1}\in\ell^{0}\,:\,\sum_{n\geq 1}|\mu_{n}|^{\zeta}\|f_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{O})}^{2}<\infty\Big\},\ \quad\ell^{\infty}(\mathcal{S}_{f}):=\ell^{\infty},

(4.2)

endowed with the natural norms. In other words, for
\zeta
finite,
\ell^{\zeta}(\mathcal{S}_{f})
denotes the space of sequences with weight
(\|f_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{O})}^{2})_{n\geq 1}
, while
\ell^{\infty}(\mathcal{S}_{f})
denotes the usual space of bounded sequences. In particular, the weight is independent of
\zeta
, and this is a consequence of Proposition
4.7
below.

As we will discuss in Section
5
, considering the above weighted sequences is not only natural, but essential to capture scaling properties of SPDEs as commented in Subsection
1.1
.

The following is the main result of this manuscript. As above,
M_{g}
denotes the multiplication operator with a measurable function
g:\mathcal{O}\to{\mathbb{R}}
.

Theorem 4.1

(Sobolev embeddings for Gaussian series – Weighted sequences)
.

Let
\mathcal{O}
be an open set in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.
Let
q\in(1,\infty)
,
\eta\in(1,q)
,
\zeta\in[2,\infty]

and
s\in(0,d)
be such that

\displaystyle\frac{s}{d}+\frac{1}{q}\geq\frac{1}{\eta}+\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{\zeta}\qquad\text{ and }\qquad\frac{1}{\eta}-\frac{1}{\zeta}<\frac{1}{2}.

(4.3)

Then for each
g\in L^{\eta}(\mathcal{O})
and
\mu\in\ell^{\zeta}(\mathcal{S}_{f})
, the operator

M_{g}R_{\mu}:L^{2}(\mathcal{O})\to H^{-s,q}(\mathcal{O})
is
\gamma
-radonifying
and

\displaystyle\|M_{g}R_{\mu}\|_{\gamma(L^{2}(\mathcal{O}),H^{-s,q}(\mathcal{O}))}\lesssim_{d,s,q,\eta,\zeta}\|\mu\|_{\ell^{\zeta}(\mathcal{S}_{f})}\|g\|_{L^{\eta}(\mathcal{O})}.

(4.4)

A version of Theorem
4.1
also holds for homogeneous Sobolev spaces.

Theorem 4.2

(Sobolev embeddings for Gaussian series – Weighted sequences
\&
Homogeneous spaces)
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{O}={\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.
Let
q\in(1,\infty)
,
\eta\in(1,q)
,
\zeta\in[2,\infty]
and
s\in(0,d)
be such that

\displaystyle\frac{s}{d}+\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1}{\eta}+\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{\zeta}\qquad\text{ and }\qquad\frac{1}{\eta}-\frac{1}{\zeta}<\frac{1}{2}.

(4.5)

Then for each
g\in L^{\eta}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
and
\mu\in\ell^{\zeta}(\mathcal{S}_{f})
, the operator

M_{g}R_{\mu}:L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\to\dot{H}^{-s,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
is
\gamma
-radonifying and

\displaystyle\|M_{g}R_{\mu}\|_{\gamma(L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}),\dot{H}^{-s,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))}\lesssim_{d,s,q,\eta,\zeta}\|\mu\|_{\ell^{\zeta}(\mathcal{S}_{f})}\|g\|_{L^{\eta}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}.

(4.6)

We now collect some observations regarding the above results. We will mainly comment on Theorem
4.1
, as similar comments extend almost verbatim to Theorem
4.2
.
From (
2.3
), the estimate (
4.4
) can be equivalently reformulated by using Gaussian random sums as follows. Let
(\gamma_{n})_{n\geq 1}
be independent standard Gaussian random variables
(\gamma_{n})_{n\geq 1}
on a probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{A},{\mathbb{P}})
. Under the assumptions of Theorem
4.1
, the sum
g\sum_{n\geq 1}\mu_{n}\gamma_{n}f_{n}
converges in
L^{2}(\Omega;H^{-s,q}(\mathcal{O}))
and

\Big({\mathbb{E}}\Big\|g\sum_{n\geq 1}\mu_{n}\gamma_{n}f_{n}\Big\|_{H^{-s,q}(\mathcal{O})}^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\lesssim_{d,s,q,\eta,\zeta}\|\mu\|_{\ell^{\zeta}(\mathcal{S}_{f})}\|g\|_{L^{\eta}(\mathcal{O})}.

In particular, Theorem
4.1
does not depend on the choice of the orthonormal basis
(e_{n})_{n\geq 1}
.

As in Subsection
1.1
(see also the comments below Propositions
3.2
and
3.3
for a similar situation),
the first condition in (
4.3
) (with equality) and (
4.5
)
are
sharp
and they captures the scaling of the estimates (
4.4
) and (
4.6
).
Therefore, all the other cases considered in Theorem
4.1
will be referred to as
non-sharp
cases, as there is a loss of smoothness and a break of the scaling. For completeness, we discuss further non-sharp cases in Remark
4.6
.

Next, we discuss the necessity of the first condition in (
4.5
). Interestingly, the latter proof only needs the estimate (
4.6
) to hold for rank one operators, and its proof extends also to the inhomogeneous case (
4.4
), therefore providing the optimality of the first condition in (
4.3
).

Proposition 4.3

(Necessity of the first condition in (
4.3
) and (
4.5
) – rank one operators)
.

Let
\mathcal{O}
be an open set, and

q\in(1,\infty)
,
\eta\in(1,\infty)
,
\zeta\in[2,\infty]
, and
s\in(0,d)
.
Suppose that there is a constant
C>0
such that for all
\mu>0
,
g,h,e\in C^{\infty}(\mathcal{O})
satisfying
\|e\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{O})}=\|h\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{O})}=1
, one has

\|M_{g}R_{\mu}\|_{\gamma(L^{2}(\mathcal{O}),H^{-s,q}(\mathcal{O}))}\leq C\|\mu\|_{\ell^{\zeta}(\mathcal{S}_{f})}\|g\|_{L^{\eta}(\mathcal{O})},

(4.7)

where
R_{\mu}=\mu\,e\otimes h
, then

\frac{s}{d}+\frac{1}{q}\geq\frac{1}{\eta}+\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{\zeta}.

Finally, if
\mathcal{O}={\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, then (
4.7
) with
H^{-s,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
replaced by
\dot{H}^{-s,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
and
g,R_{\mu}
as above implies that

\frac{s}{d}+\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1}{\eta}+\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{\zeta}.

We are not aware of the necessity of the second condition in (
4.3
).
However, note that if the latter is violated, i.e.
\frac{1}{\eta}-\frac{1}{\zeta}\geq\frac{1}{2}
, then the first condition in (
4.3
) implies
\frac{s}{d}+\frac{1}{q}\geq 1
. Thus, the space
H^{-s,q}(\mathcal{O})
has quite negative regularity, i.e.
-s-\tfrac{d}{q}\leq-d
. In particular, the second condition in (
4.3
) does not seem to provide a limitation in applications to
nonlinear
SPDEs
[
2
]
.

The method of Theorem
4.1
is applicable to other situations. We will see two of these later on. A general framework can be found in Subsection
4.3
with an application to random fields. A further application to the case where we no longer need
f_{n}\in L^{\infty}(\mathcal{O})
can be found in Section
5
.

This section is organized as follows. In Subsection
4.1
, we prove two special cases of Theorem
4.2
in which we consider the limiting
\zeta=2
and
\zeta=\infty
, respectively. The latter cases will serve in Subsection

as endpoints for an interpolation argument leading to Theorems

and

.

.
Below,

is an open set in

.

.

(4.8)

Proofs of Theorems

As explained in Subsection

(g,\mu)\mapsto M_{g}R_{\mu}

as a bilinear operator on appropriate spaces, and interpolate the two endpoint cases analyzed in Lemmas
4.4
and
4.5
, using Proposition
4.7
.
To this end, we need the following proposition. While the result of this proposition is classical, we provide it as a way to conveniently show how we apply multilinear complex interpolation in the proof of Theorems
4.1
and
4.2
. For this reason, we only sketch the proof.

To formulate it, we introduce the space

\displaystyle\ell^{\zeta}_{w}:=\Big\{(\mu_{k})_{k\geq 1}\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{N}}:\,\|\mu\|_{\ell^{\zeta}}^{\zeta}:=\sum_{k\geq 1}|\mu_{k}|^{\zeta}w(k)<\infty,\,\text{for}\,w=(w(k))_{k\geq 1}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{\mathbb{N}}\Big\}.

(4.14)

Proposition 4.7
.

Let parameters
\eta_{0},\eta_{1}\in(1,\infty)
,
s_{0},s_{1}\in\mathbb{R}
,
p_{0},p_{1}\in(1,\infty)
and
q_{0},q_{1}\in(1,\infty)
.
Let
w_{0}=(w_{0}(k))_{k\geq 1}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{\mathbb{N}}
and
w_{1}=(w_{1}(k))_{k\geq 1}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{\mathbb{N}}
.
Suppose that the linear operator

\displaystyle R:=\Bigg\{\begin{split}L^{\eta_{i}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\times\ell^{\zeta_{i}}_{w_{i}}&\rightarrow\gamma(L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}),H^{s_{i},q_{i}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})),\\
(g,\mu)&\mapsto M_{g}R_{\mu},\end{split}\quad i=0,1

(4.15)

satisfies
\|R\|_{\mathcal{L}(L^{\eta_{i}}\times\ell^{\zeta_{i}}_{w_{i}},\gamma(L^{2},H^{s_{i},q_{i}}))}\leq C_{i}
for
i=0,1
.
If
\theta\in(0,1)
and

\displaystyle\frac{1}{p}=\frac{1-\theta}{p_{0}}+\frac{\theta}{p_{1}},\quad s=(1-\theta)s_{0}+\theta s_{1}\quad\text{and}\quad w(k)=(w_{0}(k))^{(1-\theta)\frac{\zeta}{\zeta_{0}}}(w_{1}(k))^{\theta\frac{\zeta}{\zeta_{1}}},\quad k\geq 1,

(4.16)

for
(p,p_{0},p_{1})\in\{(\eta,\eta_{0},\eta_{1}),(\zeta,\zeta_{0},\zeta_{1}),(q,q_{0},q_{1})\}
, then

\displaystyle R:L^{\eta}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\times\ell^{\zeta}_{w}\rightarrow\gamma(L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}),H^{s,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})),

(4.17)

with norm
\|R\|_{\mathcal{L}(L^{\eta}\times\ell^{\zeta}_{w},\gamma(L^{2},H^{s,q}))}\leq C_{0}^{1-\theta}C_{1}^{\theta}
.

Proof.

If the underlying spaces are over the real scalars, then we apply multilinear complex interpolation to a complexification of the spaces. Moreover, in the Gaussian series, the complex and real Gaussians can be interchanged up to some universal constants (see
[
39
, Proposition 6.1.21]
). We first collect the individual interpolation spaces. While they are stated in much more generality in the references provided below, we state them here only for the special cases from the present article.
By
[
38
, Theorem 2.2.6]
and
[
40
, Theorem 14.3.1]
,
we get for
\theta\in(0,1)
and
d\geq 1
, respectively,

\displaystyle[L^{\eta_{0}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}),L^{\eta_{1}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})]_{\theta}=L^{\eta}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\quad\text{and}\quad[\ell^{\zeta_{0}}_{w_{0}},\ell^{\zeta_{1}}_{w_{1}}]_{\theta}=\ell^{\zeta}_{w}.

(4.18)

Moreover, by
[
40
, Theorem 14.7.12]
we have

\displaystyle[H^{s_{0},q_{0}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}),H^{s_{1},q_{1}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})]_{\theta}=H^{s,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}).

(4.19)

We remark that all the equalities stated above hold with equivalent norms. Finally, by
[
39
, Theorem 9.1.25]
we have

\displaystyle[\gamma(L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}),H^{s_{0},q_{0}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})),\gamma(L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}),H^{s_{1},q_{1}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))]_{\theta}=\gamma(L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}),H^{s,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})).

(4.20)

Then the required result follows by an application of multilinear complex interpolation (see
[
13
, Section 4.4]
).
∎

Before we prove the main result of this subsection, we derive two necessary conditions if one interpolates (
4.8
) and (

4.3.
Bird’s eye view on the interpolation argument and applications to random fields

From the proof of Theorem
4.1
and the fact that Lemmas
4.4
and
4.5
hold for general operators in
\mathcal{L}(L^{2}(\mathcal{O}),L^{\infty}(\mathcal{O}))
and
\mathcal{L}(L^{2}(\mathcal{O}))
, it is evident that the conclusion of Theorem
4.1
holds if we replace

by an operator

in the complex interpolation space

[\mathcal{L}(L^{2}(\mathcal{O}),L^{\infty}(\mathcal{O})),\mathcal{L}(L^{2}(\mathcal{O}))]_{1-2/\zeta}.

(4.35)

Note that the spaces
\mathcal{L}(L^{2}(\mathcal{O}),L^{\infty}(\mathcal{O}))
and
\mathcal{L}(L^{2}(\mathcal{O}))
form an interpolation couple as they both embed into
\mathcal{L}(L^{2}(\mathcal{O}),L^{2}(\mathcal{O})+L^{\infty}(\mathcal{O}))
(recall that
\mathcal{L}(X)=\mathcal{L}(X,X)
). In particular, the above complex interpolation is well-defined (see e.g.
[
13
, Subsection 2.3]
and
[
38
, Definition C.1.1]
).

The above observation and the proofs of Theorems
4.1
and
4.2
yield the following:

Proposition 4.8

(Abstract version of the Sobolev embedding for Gaussian series)
.

Let
\mathcal{O}
be an open set in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
. Let
q\in(1,\infty)
,
\eta\in(1,q)
,
\zeta\in[2,\infty]
and
s\in(0,d)
be such that

\displaystyle\frac{s}{d}+\frac{1}{q}\geq\frac{1}{\eta}+\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{\zeta}\qquad\text{ and }\qquad\frac{1}{\eta}-\frac{1}{\zeta}<\frac{1}{2}.

(4.36)

Then for each
g\in L^{\eta}(\mathcal{O})
and
R
in the space (
4.35
), the operator

M_{g}R:L^{2}(\mathcal{O})\to H^{-s,q}(\mathcal{O})

is
\gamma
-radonifying and

\displaystyle\|M_{g}R\|_{\gamma(L^{2}(\mathcal{O}),H^{-s,q}(\mathcal{O}))}\lesssim_{d,s,q,\eta,\zeta}\|R\|_{[\mathcal{L}(L^{2}(\mathcal{O}),L^{\infty}(\mathcal{O})),\mathcal{L}(L^{2}(\mathcal{O}))]_{1-2/\zeta}}\|g\|_{L^{\eta}(\mathcal{O})}.

(4.37)

If
\mathcal{O}={\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and the first condition in (
4.36
) is satisfied with equality, then (
4.37
) also holds with
H^{-s,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
replaced by the homogeneous space
\dot{H}^{-s,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
.

Although the above result looks very promising, the drawback is that an explicit description of (
4.35
) appears out of reach at the moment. However, note that, by the Riesz-Thorin interpolation, the space in (
4.35
) embeds into
\mathcal{L}(L^{2}(\mathcal{O}),L^{\zeta}(\mathcal{O}))
. Unfortunately, this is not so helpful to know.

To avoid dealing with the large space (
4.35
), it is useful to specialize to concrete subspaces of operators, and then interpolate the latter. More precisely, one embeds (or
represents
) the spaces
\mathcal{X}_{0}
and
\mathcal{X}_{1}
into
\mathcal{L}(L^{2}(\mathcal{O}),L^{\infty}(\mathcal{O}))
and
\mathcal{L}(L^{2}(\mathcal{O}))
, respectively, via a linear assignment
\vartheta\mapsto R_{\vartheta}
. Then, by interpolation,
[\mathcal{X}_{0},\mathcal{X}_{1}]_{1-2/\zeta}
embeds into (
4.35
), and Proposition
4.8
implies a corresponding estimate. This program has been successfully applied in the proofs of Theorems
4.1
and
4.2
, where the representation is via the mapping
\mu\mapsto R_{\mu}
,
\mathcal{X}_{0}=\ell^{2}(\mathcal{S}_{f})
,
\mathcal{X}_{1}=\ell^{\infty}(\mathcal{S}_{f})
, where in (
4.1
),
(e_{n})_{n\geq 1}
is an orthonormal basis and
\mathcal{S}_{f}=(f_{n})_{n\geq 1}
an orthonormal system such that
f_{n}\in L^{\infty}(\mathcal{O})
for all
n\geq 1
. A similar method will be used in Section
5
for general orthonormal systems
(f_{n})_{n\geq 1}
(thus, without any boundedness assumption), where the endpoint provided by Lemma
4.4
will be changed accordingly to the setting.

Below, we present another situation where the above construction can be performed in the case
\mathcal{O}={\mathbb{R}}^{d}
to obtain regularity results for Gaussian series. In the latter case, it might be unnatural to work with a representation in terms of an orthonormal basis
(f_{n})_{n\geq 1}
. Instead, Fourier multipliers form a more natural concept on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
. Applications will be given in Theorem
6.2

to Matérn random fields and Theorem
6.4
to the stochastic heat equation on the full space.
For details on Fourier multipliers, the reader is referred to
[
31
,
35
]
.
Also in this case, the operators
R
in the subclass of (
4.35
) can be identified as well. Now, the role of the representation parameter
\vartheta
is played by the
multiplier symbol
.

To proceed further, we collect some notation. For a tempered distribution
m\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, let
T_{m}:\mathcal{S}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\to\mathcal{S}^{\prime}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})

T_{m}\phi={\mathscr{F}}^{-1}(m\,{\mathscr{F}}\phi)=({\mathscr{F}}^{-1}{m})*\phi,

(4.38)

where
{\mathscr{F}}
and

denotes the Fourier transform and its inverse, respectively.
The Hölder inequality and Plancherel identity ensure that, for all

\displaystyle\|T_{m}\phi\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}

\displaystyle\leq\|{\mathscr{F}}^{-1}m\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\|\phi\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}=\|m\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\|\phi\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})},

\displaystyle\|T_{m}\phi\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}

\displaystyle=\|m\,{\mathscr{F}}\phi\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\leq\|m\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\|\phi\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}.

In particular,

gives a representation of

and

in
\mathcal{L}(L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}),L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))
and
\mathcal{L}(L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))
, respectively.
Therefore, by interpolation, we obtain that for

belongs in the space (

) for

and satisfies

(Random fields and Fourier multipliers)

4.4.

In this subsection, we comment on the extension of the above results in the case

First, by repeating the proofs of Section

Theorem 4.10

(Sobolev embeddings for Gaussian series – Periodic weighted case)

The statement of Theorem

As on the periodic setting

Because of its fundamental role in our analysis, we provide an alternative proof of the necessity of the first condition in (

in the case of diagonal operators, therefore yielding optimality also in the case of Fourier multipliers on

does not seem possible due to the different rescaling of the Fourier symbol and functions, see below (

Recall that
|e_{k}|\equiv 1

\ell^{\zeta}(\mathcal{S}_{f})
coincides with the

\ell^{\zeta}
if

is the Fourier basis on
\mathbb{T}^{d}

Proposition 4.11

(Necessity of the first condition in (
4.3
) – Diagonal operator on
\mathbb{T}^{d}
)
.

Let
q\in(1,\infty)
,
\eta\in(1,\infty)
,
\zeta\in[2,\infty]
, and
s\in(0,d)
.
For each
\mu\in\ell^{\infty}
, let
R_{\mu}\in\mathcal{L}(L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d}),L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))
be defined by
R_{\mu}e_{k}=\mu_{k}e_{k}
, for
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
. Suppose that there is a constant
C>0
such that for all
g\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
and all finitely nonzero sequences
\mu
, one has

\displaystyle\|M_{g}R_{\mu}\|_{\gamma(L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d}),H^{-s,q}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))}\leq C\|\mu\|_{\ell^{\zeta}}\|g\|_{L^{\eta}(\mathbb{T}^{d})},

then
\frac{s}{d}+\frac{1}{q}\geq\frac{1}{\eta}+\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{\zeta}
.

Proof.

Let
N\geq 1
be an integer and define a frequency block in
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
by

C_{N}=\{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}:2^{N}\leq n_{i}\leq 3\cdot 2^{N-1}\text{ for }i=1,\dots,d\}.

Clearly we have
|C_{N}|\eqsim 2^{dN}
, for
N\geq 1
.

Let
g=\sum_{n\in C_{N}}e_{n}
and set
\mu_{n}={{\bf 1}}_{C_{N}}(n)
for
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, so that clearly
\mu=(\mu_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
only has finitely many non-zero entries.
Then

\|\mu\|_{\ell^{\zeta}}=|C_{N}|^{\frac{1}{\zeta}}\eqsim 2^{\frac{dN}{\zeta}}
.
Regarding the function
g
, we first rewrite it as

\displaystyle g(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\in C_{N}}\prod_{j=1}^{d}e^{2\pi in_{j}x_{j}}=\prod_{j=1}^{d}\Big(\sum_{n_{j}=2^{N}}^{3\cdot 2^{N-1}}e^{2\pi in_{j}x_{j}}\Big)=:\prod_{j=1}^{d}p_{j}(x_{j}).

(4.42)

Each factor
p_{j}
is a shifted one-dimensional Dirichlet kernel with
3\cdot 2^{N-1}-2^{N}+1=2^{N-1}+1
terms.
Multiplying
p_{j}
by
e^{2\pi i2^{-(N-2)}x_{j}}
and
using

|e^{2\pi i2^{-(N-2)}x}|=1

one can easily check that

\displaystyle\|p_{j}\|_{L^{\eta}(\mathbb{T})}=\|D_{N-2}\|_{L^{\eta}(\mathbb{T})},

(4.43)

where
D_{N-2}
is the Dirichlet kernel
D_{N-2}(x_{j})=\sum_{m=-2^{N-2}}^{2^{N-2}}e^{2\pi imx_{j}}
. It is well-known that

\displaystyle\|D_{N-2}\|_{L^{\eta}(\mathbb{T})}\eqsim(2N-3)^{1-\frac{1}{\eta}},

(4.44)

see e.g.
[
31
, Exercise 3.1.6]
.
Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\|g\|_{L^{\eta}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}=\Big\|\prod_{j=1}^{d}p_{j}\Big\|_{L^{\eta}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}=\prod_{j=1}^{d}\|D_{2^{N-2}}\|_{L^{\eta}(\mathbb{T})}\eqsim 2^{dN(1-\frac{1}{\eta})}

(4.45)

Combining the estimates for
\mu
and
g
we get

\displaystyle\|g\|_{L^{\eta}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}\|\mu\|_{\ell^{\zeta}}\eqsim 2^{dN(1-\frac{1}{\eta})}2^{\frac{dN}{\zeta}}=2^{dN(1-\frac{1}{\eta}+\frac{1}{\zeta})}.

(4.46)

Next, we calculate a lower bound for the
\gamma(L^{2},H^{-s,q})
-norm. By definition of
R_{\mu}
, we get

\displaystyle\|M_{g}R_{\mu}\|_{\gamma(L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d}),H^{-s,q}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))}^{2}

\displaystyle={\mathbb{E}}\Big\|\sum_{n\in C_{N}}\gamma_{n}ge_{n}\Big\|_{H^{-s,q}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}^{2}={\mathbb{E}}\|S_{N}\|_{H^{-s,q}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}^{2},

(4.47)

where
S_{N}=\sum_{n\in C_{N}}\gamma_{n}ge_{n}
.
Using the definition of
g
, we observe that

\displaystyle S_{N}=\sum_{k\in C_{N}}\gamma_{k}\Big(\sum_{n\in C_{N}}e_{n}\Big)e_{k}=\sum_{k\in C_{N}}\sum_{n\in C_{N}}\gamma_{k}e_{n+k}

(4.48)

The components
m_{j}=n_{j}+k_{j}
are in the range

\displaystyle m_{j}\in[2^{N}+2^{N},3\cdot 2^{N-1}+3\cdot 2^{N-1}]=[2^{N+1},3\cdot 2^{N}],\quad j=1,\dots,d.

(4.49)

By definition of the Littlewood-Paley blocks
\Delta_{k}
,
k\geq 1
(see
[
38
, Section 5.7.c]
), we know that
\Delta_{k}S_{N}=\mathscr{F}^{-1}(\widehat{\varphi_{k}}\widehat{S_{N}})=\delta_{k,N+2}\Delta_{k}S_{N}
, since
\mathrm{supp}\,\widehat{\varphi_{k}}\subset\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:2^{k-1}\leq|\xi|\leq 3\cdot 2^{k-1}\}
.
The sum in the Littlewood-Paley norm therefore collapses to a single term, i.e.

\displaystyle\|S_{N}\|_{H^{-s,q}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}

\displaystyle=\left\|\left(|2^{-(N+2)s}\Delta_{N+2}S_{N}|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\right\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}\eqsim 2^{-(N+2)s}\|S_{N}\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}.

(4.50)

Further, by the square function characterisation, we have

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\|S_{N}\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}

\displaystyle\eqsim\Big\|\Big(\sum_{n\in C_{N}}|ge_{n}|^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}\eqsim\|g\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}|C_{N}|^{\frac{1}{2}}=2^{dN(1-\frac{1}{q})}2^{\frac{dN}{2}},

(4.51)

where we used (
4.45
) for
q
in place of
\eta
. Combining (
4.46
), (
4.47
), (
4.50
) and (
4.51
) we then get

\displaystyle 2^{-sN+dN(1-\frac{1}{q}+\frac{1}{2})}\lesssim 2^{dN(1-\frac{1}{\eta}+\frac{1}{\zeta})}.

(4.52)

Since, by assumption, this must hold for
N
arbitrarily large, the claimed estimate follows.
∎

The following result is an interesting case that one can consider on sets with finite volume. It shows a self-improvement hinting towards the non-optimality of such results if the function
g
is not present. A similar result for
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
cannot hold since
g=1
is not in
L^{\eta}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
.

Proposition 4.12
.

Let
q\in(1,\infty)
be given. The following are equivalent

(1)

The identity satisfies
\mathrm{Id}\in\gamma(L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d}),H^{-s,q}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))
.

(2)

s>d/2
.

Proof.

(
2
)
\Rightarrow
(
1
): For
q>2
this follows from Lemma
4.5
by taking
\eta\in(2,q)
,
g=1
,
\mu_{k}=1
for all
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
(see also Remark
3.4
). Note that
\widetilde{q}\in(1,2]
is simply a consequence of
H^{-s,q}(\mathbb{T}^{d})\hookrightarrow H^{-s,\widetilde{q}}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
and the ideal property.

(
1
)
\Rightarrow
(
2
):
By the same embedding as before, it suffices to consider
q\in(1,2]
. By the cotype
2
property of
H^{-s,q}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
(see
[
39
, Corollary 7.1.5]
) we obtain

\|\mathrm{Id}\|_{\gamma(L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d}),H^{-s,q}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))}\geq c\Big(\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}\|e_{k}\|_{H^{-s,q}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}^{2}\Big)^{1/2}=c\Big(\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}(1+|k|^{2})^{-s}\Big)^{1/2},

and thus the convergence of the latter series implies
s>d/2
.
∎

As a consequence, Theorem
4.10
does not extend to
\zeta=\infty
for
q\in[1,\infty),\eta\in[q,\infty]
in the limiting case where
\frac{s}{d}+\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1}{\eta}+\frac{1}{2}
in general. Indeed, we find that
\frac{s}{d}=\frac{1}{\eta}-\frac{1}{q}+\frac{1}{2}\leq\frac{1}{2}
. However, if we let
g=1
, and
\mu_{k}=1
for all
k\geq 1
, then
I=M_{g}R_{\mu}\in\gamma(L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d}),H^{-s,q}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))
is equivalent to
s>d/2
according to Proposition
4.12
. This contradicts
\frac{s}{d}\leq\frac{1}{2}
.

We end this subsection on the periodic setting with a remark about a corrected bound from the literature. Let
S^{\zeta}(L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))
denote the Schatten class (see
[
38
, Appendix D]
for its definition).

Remark 4.13
.

The following correction of
[
17
, (3.14)]
holds for any
\zeta\in[2,\infty]
:

\displaystyle\|S(t)M_{g}T\|_{S^{2}(L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))}\leq Ct^{-d/4}\|g\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}\|T\|_{S^{\zeta}(L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))},\ \ t\in(0,1).

(4.53)

For simplicity, we only consider the torus, but the same holds on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
as follows from (
3.7
) and the sharpness argument below. To prove the bound (
4.53
), it suffices to consider
\zeta=\infty
. By the ideal property of
S^{2}(L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))
, it suffices to prove the bound
\|S(t)M_{g}\|_{S^{2}(L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))}\leq Ct^{-d/4}\|g\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}
. Let
(e_{k})_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}
be the trigonometric system. Then
S(t)e_{k}=e^{-\lambda_{k}t}e_{k}
with
\lambda_{k}\eqsim k^{2}
. Thus, we can write

\displaystyle\|S(t)M_{g}\|_{S^{2}(L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k,\ell\geq 1}|(S(t)M_{g}e_{k},e_{\ell})|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell\geq 1}e^{-2\lambda_{\ell}t}\|M_{g}e_{\ell}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}^{2}\leq\|g\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}^{2}\sum_{\ell\geq 1}e^{-2\lambda_{\ell}t}\leq Ct^{-d/2}\|g\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}^{2}.

Next, we prove that the power
d/4
in the factor
t^{-d/4}
in (
4.53
) is sharp. To this end, it suffices to consider
\zeta=2
. Let
T=e_{0}\otimes f
with
f=\sqrt{k_{t}}
, where
k_{t}
is the heat kernel. Then
\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}=1
. Letting
g=f
, we obtain

\|S(t)M_{g}T\|_{S^{2}(L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))}=\|k_{t}*k_{t}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}=\|k_{2t}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}\eqsim t^{-d/4},\ \ t\in(0,1).

On the other hand
\|g\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}\|T\|_{S^{2}(L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))}=\|g\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}=1
.

5.
Sobolev embeddings for unweighted Gaussian sequences

In this section, we continue our investigation on Sobolev embeddings for Gaussian sequences, where
\mu
is now taken from the
unweighted
sequence space
\ell^{\zeta}
. For instance, this covers the case where
f_{n}\not\in L^{\infty}(\mathcal{O})
.
As commented in Remark
4.13
, spaces of this type have already been used in the literature. However, it will turn out that it is
no
longer possible to prove a result under the sharp condition in Theorem
4.1
if
\zeta<\infty
, i.e.

\frac{s}{d}+\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1}{\eta}+\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{\zeta}.

(5.1)

In particular, the corresponding result does not capture the scaling of the stochastic heat equation, see Subsection
1.1
.
Here, similar to the weighted case described in Subsection
1.3
, the main step is a bilinear interpolation argument (see also Subsection
4.3
for the abstract viewpoint).

We begin by describing the setting.
Let
(e_{n})_{n\geq 1}
and
(f_{n})_{n\geq 1}
be orthonormal systems in
L^{2}(\mathcal{O})
. As in the previous section, we will prove estimates for

\|M_{g}R_{\mu}\|_{\gamma(L^{2}(\mathcal{O}),L^{q}(\mathcal{O}))}
where
M_{g}
is the multiplication operator by
g\in L^{\eta}(\mathcal{O})
, and
R_{\mu}
admits the decomposition

R_{\mu}=\sum_{n\geq 1}\mu_{n}\,e_{n}\otimes f_{n}.

(5.2)

We consider the standard
\ell^{\zeta}
norm on the sequence
\mu
.

The following result provides an estimate for non-trace class noise in the case when the information
\mu\in\ell^{\zeta}(\mathcal{S}_{f})
for an orthonormal system
\mathcal{S}_{f}=(f_{n})_{n\geq 1}
is
not
available, but instead only
\mu\in\ell^{\zeta}
is known. It is clear that, due to the missing factor
-\tfrac{1}{\zeta}
in the first inequality in (
5.3
) below (compare to (
4.3
)), this loss of information also results in a loss of regularity.

Theorem 5.1

(Embeddings for Gaussian series with unweighted sequences)
.

Let
\mathcal{O}
be an open set in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.
Let
s>0
,
q\in(1,\infty)
,
\eta\in[2,\infty)
,
\zeta\in[2,\infty)
be such that

\displaystyle\frac{s}{d}+\frac{1}{q}\geq\frac{1}{\eta}+\frac{1}{2}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\frac{1}{\eta}+\frac{1}{\zeta}>\frac{1}{q}.

(5.3)

Then for each
g\in L^{\eta}(\mathcal{O})
and
\mu\in\ell^{\zeta}
, the operator
M_{g}R_{\mu}:L^{2}(\mathcal{O})\to H^{-s,q}(\mathcal{O})
is
\gamma
-radonifying and

\|M_{g}R_{\mu}\|_{\gamma(L^{2}(\mathcal{O}),H^{-s,q}(\mathcal{O}))}\lesssim_{d,s,q,\eta,\zeta}\|g\|_{L^{\eta}(\mathcal{O})}\|\mu\|_{\ell^{\zeta}}.

Note that in case of equality in the first part of (
5.3
), the second part of (
5.3
) can be restated as
\frac{s}{d}>\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{\zeta}
. This shows that
\mu\in\ell^{\zeta}
for smaller
\zeta
(in particular compared to
\zeta=\infty
) allows considering a smaller
s
.

As in Sections
3
and
4
, the above result and Proposition
5.2
admit a variant where
H^{-s,q}(\mathcal{O})
is replaced by the homogeneous spaces
\dot{H}^{-s,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
. We leave the details to the interested reader.

In the following, we state a converse result, which shows again that only the information
\mu\in\ell^{\zeta}
is not so beneficial. Indeed,
if
\mu\in\ell^{\zeta}
and
g\in L^{\eta}(\mathcal{O})
, then the conditions ensuring
M_{g}R_{\mu}\in\gamma(L^{2}(\mathcal{O}),L^{q}(\mathcal{O}))
are
independent
of
\zeta
. In particular, choosing the corresponding sequence
\mu
from the unweighted space
\ell^{\zeta}
does
not
allow one to recover a condition like (
5.1
), and therefore to capture scaling properties of SPDEs as analyzed in Subsection
1.1
.

Proposition 5.2

(Necessary conditions in the unweighted setting)
.

Let
\mathcal{O}
be an open set in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.
Let
s>0
,
q\in(1,\infty)
,
\eta\in[2,\infty)
and
\zeta\in[2,\infty)
. Suppose that, for all
g\in L^{\eta}(\mathcal{O})
and
\mu\in\ell^{\zeta}
,

\displaystyle\|M_{g}R_{\mu}\|_{\gamma(L^{2}(\mathcal{O}),H^{-s,q}(\mathcal{O}))}\lesssim\|g\|_{L^{\eta}(\mathcal{O})}\|\mu\|_{\ell^{\zeta}},

(5.4)

where
\mu
is the sequence corresponding to
R_{\mu}
as in (
5.2
).
Then the first part of (
5.3
) holds.
Moreover, if
\mathcal{O}={\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, then the second part of (
5.3
) holds as well.

Note that the condition (
5.3
) excludes the sharp case (
5.1
), which comes out from the scaling analysis on the stochastic heat equations with non-trace class noise, see Subsection
1.1
. In particular, the Schatten class is not the appropriate class for obtaining sharp bounds on
M_{g}T
.

The independence of
\zeta
in the first part of (
5.3
) shows that the estimate (
5.4
) becomes better as
\zeta\to\infty
. However, in the case
\zeta=\infty
, Proposition
3.2
and
4.5
already give the estimate (
5.4
) for
\mu\in\ell^{\infty}
. Therefore, from this perspective, knowing that
\mu\in\ell^{\zeta}
does not allow for improving the bounds on
M_{g}R_{\mu}
given in Theorem
4.1
. The only exception, which we do not expect to be important in applications to SPDEs, is that the second condition in (
5.3
) becomes less restrictive as
\zeta
gets smaller, and allows for
\eta\geq q
.

The proofs of Theorem
5.1
and Proposition
5.2
are given in Subsections
5.1
and
5.2
below, respectively.

5.1.
Proof of Theorem
5.1

Here, we argue as in the proof of Theorem
4.1
given in Subsection

. As in the latter, we use bilinear interpolation. For the endpoint case
\zeta=\infty
, we will again use Lemma
4.5
(that is also contained in Theorem
4.1
), where one needs the following as a replacement of Lemma
4.4
concerning the endpoint case
\zeta=2
.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
\mathcal{O}
be an open set in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.
Suppose that

\displaystyle s\geq 0,\quad q\in[1,\infty),\quad\frac{s}{d}+\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1}{\eta}+\frac{1}{2},\quad\eta\in[2,\infty),

(5.5)

Then for each
g\in L^{\eta}(\mathcal{O})
and
\mu\in\ell^{2}
, the operator

\gamma

\|M_{g}R\|_{\gamma(L^{2}(\mathcal{O}),H^{-s,q}(\mathcal{O}))}\lesssim\|g\|_{L^{\eta}(\mathcal{O})}\|\mu\|_{\ell^{2}},

where the constant depends only on the parameters
(d,s,q,\eta)

Proof.

Let
\frac{1}{p}=\frac{s}{d}+\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1}{\eta}+\frac{1}{2}

\displaystyle\|M_{g}R_{\mu}\|_{\gamma(L^{2}(\mathcal{O}),H^{-s,q}(\mathcal{O}))}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|M_{g}R_{\mu}\|_{\gamma(L^{2}(\mathcal{O}),L^{p}(\mathcal{O}))}

\displaystyle=\Big\|g\sum_{n\geq 1}\gamma_{n}\mu_{n}f_{n}\Big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega;L^{p}(\mathcal{O}))}

\displaystyle\leq\|g\|_{L^{\eta}(\mathcal{O})}\Big\|\sum_{n\geq 1}\gamma_{n}\mu_{n}f_{n}\Big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega;L^{2}(\mathcal{O}))}=\|g\|_{L^{\eta}(\mathcal{O})}\|\mu\|_{\ell^{2}},

where we used orthogonality in the last line.
∎

.

Note that the case

.

Proof of Proposition

\psi=1
on
\mathrm{supp}\,\phi
, let an integer
N\geq 1
and define
g=\sum_{1\leq|k|\leq N}\psi(\cdot-k)
, so that clearly
ge_{k}=e_{k}
, for
1\leq|k|\leq N
. Let, moreover,
\mu_{k}=1
for all
1\leq|k|\leq N
and zero otherwise.
Then,

\displaystyle\|M_{g}R_{\mu}\|_{\gamma(L^{2},H^{-s,q})}

\displaystyle\geq\Big\|\sum_{k\geq 1}\gamma_{k}g\sum_{1\leq|n|\leq N}(e_{k},e_{n})e_{n}\Big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega;H^{-s,q})}=\Big\|\sum_{1\leq|k|\leq N}\gamma_{k}e_{k}\Big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega;H^{-s,q})}

\displaystyle=\Big\|\sum_{1\leq|k|\leq N}\gamma_{k}(1-\Delta)^{-s/2}e_{k}\Big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega;L^{q})}\eqsim\Big\|\Big(\sum_{1\leq|k|\leq N}|(1-\Delta)^{-s/2}e_{k}|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\Big\|_{L^{q}}

\displaystyle\geq\Big(\sum_{1\leq|k|\leq N}\int_{(0,1)^{d}+k}|(1-\Delta)^{-s/2}e_{k}|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}x\Big)^{1/q}=N^{d/q}\|(1-\Delta)^{-s/2}\phi\|_{L^{q}((0,1)^{d})},

where we used (
2.5
) and that

|(1-\Delta)^{-s/2}e_{k}|^{2}
is dominated by its sum.
On the other hand,

\displaystyle\|g\|_{L^{\eta}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\|\mu\|_{\ell^{\zeta}}=\|\psi\|_{L^{\eta}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}N^{d/\eta}N^{d/\zeta}.

Therefore, if (
5.4
) holds, then letting
N\to\infty
, we find that
\frac{1}{\eta}+\frac{1}{\zeta}\geq\frac{1}{q}
.
∎

Proposition 6.1

(Regularity of white noise with a multiplicative coefficient)
.

Let
\mathcal{O}
be either a bounded domain with smooth boundary, or

Theorem 6.2

(Regularity of Matérn-type with a multiplicative coefficient)
.

Let

Proof.

, are central in the study of nonlinear SPDEs. Typically, the construction of solutions to nonlinear problems often involves linearization and fixed-point techniques. In the context of stochastic maximal
L^{p}
-regularity, such estimates have been proven to be a powerful tool to study SPDEs in
critical spaces
, see
[
9
]
.
As is evident in corresponding applications (see e.g.
[
7
,
8
]
), after the linear theory is established, the nonlinear estimates are handled with standard techniques such as Hölder inequality and Sobolev embeddings.
The
L^{\eta}(\mathcal{O})
-norm appearing in the previous results is very useful for this. Indeed, we apply our result to the nonlinearity of the form
g=G(u)=|u|^{h}
where
h>1
. Then, for instance, from Theorems
6.3
, we have

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\|u\|_{L^{p}(0,T;H^{1-s,q}(\mathcal{O}))}^{p}+{\mathbb{E}}\|u\|_{C([0,T];B^{1-s-2/p}_{q,p}(\mathcal{O}))}^{p}

\displaystyle\lesssim{\mathbb{E}}\|G(u)\|_{L^{p}(0,T;L^{\eta}(\mathcal{O}))}^{p}\lesssim{\mathbb{E}}\|u\|_{L^{ph}(0,T;L^{h\eta}(\mathcal{O}))}^{ph}.

(6.14)

Hence, now one can apply a Sobolev embedding
H^{\sigma,q}(\mathcal{O})\hookrightarrow L^{h\eta}(\mathcal{O})
to bound the nonlinearity with norms appearing on the left-hand side of the same estimate.
Note that, as
h>1
, one can choose
\eta<q
such that
h\eta>q
, and in this situation, the previous Sobolev embedding can be made
sharp
.
Of course, due to the results in
[
5
,
6
,
9
]
, it is not needed to perform the fixed point argument again. Far-reaching applications of our results to nonlinear SPDEs are given in our forthcoming work
[
2
]
.

(2)

(Second order operators in divergence form).

The Laplace operator in (
6.2
) can also be replaced by a second-order operator in divergence form like

u\mapsto\nabla\cdot(a\cdot\nabla u),

for some uniformly elliptic matrix field
a\in C(\overline{\mathcal{O}};{\mathbb{R}}^{d\times d})
.
Even in the case of a
C^{1}
-domain, using

[
10
, Theorem 11.5]
, one can check that the proofs of Theorems
6.3
and
6.4
also extend to this situation in case
s\leq 1
, see
[
37
, Lemma 5.4]
for details on the applications of the results in
[
10
]
. If
a\in C^{\alpha}(\mathcal{O};{\mathbb{R}}^{d\times d})
and
\mathcal{O}
is a
C^{1,\alpha}
-domain for some
\alpha>0
, then one can also take
s<1+\min\{\frac{1}{q},\alpha\}
.
In particular, the regularity of the domain
\mathcal{O}
in Theorem
6.3
can be weakened up to include a restriction on the smoothness parameter
s
.

6.3.
Comparison to related results for SPDEs

Our estimates from Theorem
4.1
potentially have consequences for several papers on nonlinear SPDEs with non-trace class noise (see e.g.
[
17
,
22
,
57
,
58
]
and subsequent papers). As mentioned above, applications to nonlinear SPDEs with non-trace class noise will be presented in
[
2
]
.

Before we compare our results any further to results in the literature, we would like to point out a general principle which can be used to compare our results to semigroup formulations of estimates such as the one in Theorem
4.1
.

Let
A=\Delta_{\rm Dir}
be as explained in Subsection
6.2.1
. Thus, the operator acts on the space
H^{-1-s,q}_{\rm Dir}(\mathcal{O})
, with domain
H^{1-s,q}_{\rm Dir}(\mathcal{O})
, see (
6.4
). Let
S
denote the analytic semigroup generated by
A
. For simplicity, we assume that
A
is invertible, and thus
S
is exponentially stable with
\|S(t)\|_{\mathcal{L}(L^{q}(\mathcal{O}))}\lesssim e^{-\delta t}
for a
\delta>0
(this is true if
\mathcal{O}
is bounded, and otherwise it suffices to shift the operator). Under the assumptions of Theorem
4.1
, from the latter, one gets, for all
t>0
,

\displaystyle\|S(t)gR_{\mu}\|_{\gamma(L^{2}(\mathcal{O}),L^{q}(\mathcal{O}))}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|(-A)^{s/2}S(t)\|_{\mathcal{L}(L^{q}(\mathcal{O}))}\|(-A)^{-s/2}gR_{\mu}\|_{\gamma(L^{2}(\mathcal{O}),L^{q}(\mathcal{O}))}

(6.15)

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{-s/2}\|\mu\|_{\ell^{\zeta}(\mathcal{S}_{f})}\|g\|_{L^{\eta}(\mathcal{O})}.

In part of the literature, the above type of estimate is the way to reformulate results like the one in Theorem
4.1
.

Conversely, note that if
\|S(t)gR_{\mu}\|_{\gamma(L^{2}(\mathcal{O}),L^{q}(\mathcal{O}))}\lesssim t^{-s/2}
for all
t>0
, then
using a standard formula
(-A)^{-\theta}=C_{\theta}\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{\theta-1}S(t)\,\mathrm{d}t
for
\theta\in(0,1)
(see
[
47
, Lemma 6.1.5]
), and that
(-A)^{\sigma/2}:L^{q}(\mathcal{O})\to H^{-\sigma,q}_{\rm Dir}(\mathcal{O})
is an isomorphism with inverse
(-A)^{-\sigma/2}
(see
[
9
, Example A.4]
), we find that

\displaystyle\|gR_{\mu}\|_{\gamma(L^{2}(\mathcal{O}),H^{-\sigma,q}_{\rm Dir}(\mathcal{O}))}

\displaystyle\eqsim\|(-A)^{-\sigma/2}gR_{\mu}\|_{\gamma(L^{2}(\mathcal{O}),L^{q}(\mathcal{O}))}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{\sigma/2-1}\|S(t)gR_{\mu}\|_{\gamma(L^{2}(\mathcal{O}),L^{q}(\mathcal{O}))}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{1}t^{\sigma/2-1-s/2}\,\mathrm{d}t+\int_{1}^{\infty}e^{-\delta t}\,\mathrm{d}t,

where the latter is finite if
\sigma\in(s,2)
.

Therefore, both our formulation and the one via the semigroup decay are equivalent up to a loss of some
\varepsilon>0
in
one direction
. In other words: our formulation in terms of negative Sobolev spaces (and hence, extrapolation spaces, see
[
9
, Appendix A]
for the terminology) has the advantage that one can directly apply stochastic maximal
L^{p}
-regularity to it without losing any
\varepsilon>0
of smoothness.

6.3.1.
Related
L^{2}
-estimates

Below, we connect our conditions to the ones in
[
18
,
19
,
21
]
. For a discussion on
[
17
, Lemma 3.3]
, the reader is also referred to Remark
4.13
. The condition
\mu\in\ell^{\zeta}(\mathcal{S}_{f})
was introduced in
[
18
]

and an estimate of the form (
6.15
) was derived in the special case
\eta=q=2
:

\displaystyle\|S(\cdot)M_{g(\cdot)}R_{\mu}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;\mathcal{L}_{2}(L^{2}(\mathcal{O})))}\lesssim_{T}\|\mu\|_{\ell^{\zeta}(\mathcal{S}_{f})}\|g\|_{L^{2}((0,T)\times\mathcal{O})}.

(6.16)

under the additional condition that for
\beta\in(0,\infty)
,

\displaystyle\hskip-28.45274pt\sum_{n\geq 1}\lambda_{n}^{-\beta}\|f_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{O})}^{2}<\infty\quad\text{and $\zeta=\infty$ if $d=1$,}\quad\text{or}\quad\frac{\beta(\zeta-2)}{\zeta}<1\quad\text{for $\zeta>2$, if $d\geq 2$.}

(6.17)

From
[
33
]
, on a bounded smooth domain
\mathcal{O}\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, we have

\|f_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{O})}\lesssim\lambda_{n}^{\frac{d-1}{4}}
uniformly in
n\geq 1
. Moreover, by the Weyl law, one has
\lambda_{n}\eqsim n^{\frac{2}{d}}
, so that all together we may assume

\displaystyle\|f_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{O})}\lesssim n^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{2d}}\quad\text{uniformly in \ $n\geq 1$.}

(6.18)

In order to ensure that (
6.17
) holds, i.e.
\sum_{n\geq 1}n^{-\frac{2}{d}\beta}n^{1-\frac{1}{d}}<\infty
together with
\tfrac{\beta(\zeta-2)}{\zeta}<1
, one then effectively requires that in
d\geq 2
,

\displaystyle\zeta<2+\frac{4}{2d-3}.

(6.19)

From Theorem
4.1
(see (
6.15
)) we see

\displaystyle\|S(t)M_{g}R_{\mu}\|_{\mathcal{L}_{2}(L^{2}(\mathcal{O}))}

\displaystyle\eqsim\|S(t)M_{g}R_{\mu}\|_{\gamma(L^{2}(\mathcal{O}),L^{2}(\mathcal{O}))}\lesssim t^{-s/2}\|\mu\|_{\ell^{\zeta}(\mathcal{S}_{f})}\|g\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{O})},

(6.20)

where, to ensure square integrability in time and the conditions in Theorem
4.1
, we require that

\displaystyle 1>s\geq\frac{d}{\eta}-\frac{d}{\zeta},\qquad\eta<2.

(6.21)

We observe that for
d\geq 2
this holds for any
\zeta\in(2,\infty)
, so long as
\eta
is sufficiently close to
2
.
Moreover, for
d=1
and
\zeta=\infty
we may apply Proposition
3.2
, which only requires that
s>\tfrac{1}{2}
. Together with the restriction from (
6.21
) for
d\geq 2
, we get the condition

\displaystyle\zeta\in(2,\infty],\ \quad d=1,\quad\text{or}\qquad\zeta<\frac{2d}{d-2},\ \quad d\geq 2,

(6.22)

which gives a major improvement compared to (
6.19
).
Moreover, under the condition (
6.21
), we obtain (
6.16
) without the additional condition (
6.17
) and without assuming that the
f_{k}
are the eigenfunctions of the operator
S
or its generator. We note further that since the embedding
L^{2}(\mathcal{O})\hookrightarrow L^{\eta}(\mathcal{O})
, for
\eta<2
, fails in general unbounded domains, we also require the boundedness of
\mathcal{O}
to obtain (
6.16
).

6.3.2.
Related estimates only under the condition
\mu\in\ell^{\zeta}(\mathcal{S}_{f})
.

Estimates for the stochastic convolution (
6.6
) were also obtained in the very recent article
[
20
]
under the condition that

\displaystyle\mu\in\ell^{\infty}\quad\text{if $d=1$ and}\quad\mu\in\ell^{\zeta}(\mathcal{S}_{f})\quad\text{where}\quad\zeta<\frac{2d}{d-2}\quad\text{if $d\geq 2$,}

(6.23)

and the condition that the
(f_{k})_{k\geq 1}
are the eigenfunctions of the differential operator involved. There, an estimate of the form

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|u\|^{p}_{C(\overline{\mathcal{O}})}\lesssim_{p,T}{\mathbb{E}}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|g(t)\|_{C(\overline{\mathcal{O}})}^{p}

(6.24)

for a
p>1
sufficiently large,
is required for the well-posedness of a stochastic convolution treated in this paper.
Let us point out that (
6.24
) is a special case of our results, which we can even improve due to the optimality of our setting. More precisely, the condition
\zeta<\frac{2d}{d-2}
and Theorem
6.3
ensure the existence of
s<1
such that, for all
q,p\in(2,\infty)
,

{\mathbb{E}}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|u(t)\|_{B^{1-s}_{q,p}(\mathcal{O})}^{p}\lesssim_{q,p,s,T}{\mathbb{E}}\|g\|_{L^{p}(0,T;L^{q}(\mathcal{O}))}^{p}.

(6.25)

Clearly, by taking
q
so large that
B^{1-s}_{q,p}(\mathcal{O})\hookrightarrow C(\overline{\mathcal{O}})
by (
6.10
), the above yields (
6.24
). Let us point out that the improvement is on both sides of the estimates, and in many applications to nonlinear reaction-diffusion with non-trace class noise, the estimate (
6.25
) is more flexible. For instance,
q
can be taken finite and critical spaces for reaction-diffusion are typically of this form, see e.g.
[
7
, Subsection 1.4]
.

Finally, we remark that the limiting case
\zeta=2d/(d-2)
is also very relevant for our purposes, and it is the one that leads to optimal estimates in terms of scaling, cf. Subsection
1.1
and hence critical situations in nonlinear SPDEs. In this respect, the estimate (
6.25
) can be thought of as a
sub-critical
one, while the one in Theorem
6.3
is
critical
.

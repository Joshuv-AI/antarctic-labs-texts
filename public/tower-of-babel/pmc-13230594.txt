Real-time medical images enhancement technique using Adaptive Histogram Equalization model
Authors: Arvind Dhaka, Arpit Kumar Sharma, Amita Nandal
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIn low-contrast modalities like magnetic resonance imaging, medical image augmentation helps reveal anatomical structures and diseased locations. This paper presents an adaptive histogram-based contrast enhancement technique to improve medical picture quality while conserving structure. The suggested approach enhances tumor areas and tissue borders without artifacts using statistical normalization, adaptive histogram modeling, Gaussian-based intensity redistribution, and brightness-preserving contrast modification. T1, T2, FLAIR, and contrast-enhanced T1 MRI scans from the BraTS 2020 Brain Tumor Segmentation Dataset were used in experiments. The proposed method was compared to five state-of-the-art contrast enhancement methods: Histogram Equalization (HE), Contrast Limited Adaptive Histogram Equalization (CLAHE), Gamma Correction (GC), Retinex-based enhancement, and Adaptive Gamma Correction. Quantitative metrics like PSNR, MSE, SSIM, and entropy were used to evaluate the method’s performance. The suggested technique outperforms HE (31.46 dB), CLAHE (34.15 dB), Gamma Correction (32.74 dB), Retinex (35.21 dB), and Adaptive Gamma Correction (36.04 dB) in experimental PSNR (37.82 dB). The suggested technique has an SSIM of 0.962, compared to 0.891, 0.915, 0.902, 0.934, and 0.947 for the competing approaches. The approach decreases reconstruction error with an MSE of 18.4, lower than HE (42.7) and CLAHE (29.6). Qualitative analysis shows better contrast and tumor borders without brightness amplification. These findings show that the adaptive histogram approach enhances medical image analysis and diagnostic assistance systems robustly and efficiently.Keywords: Image enhancement, Image processing, Grey-scale, Image segmentation, Histogram, Medical image, MRISubject terms: Computational biology and bioinformatics, Engineering, Health care, Mathematics and computing, Medical research

Limitations

The adaptive histogra-based enhancement framework improves medical picture contrast and structural visibility, but it has some drawbacks. The approach prioritizes image contrast enhancement above diagnostic analysis and lesion segmentation. The present framework can not identify, classify, or segment tumors, but better contrast may aid visual interpretation and subsequent analysis. Thus, diagnostic performance criteria like Dice similarity coefficient, sensitivity, and specificity have not been used to quantify the method’s clinical effect. The experimental assessment used MRI images from the BraTS 2020 Brain Tumor Segmentation Dataset, which mostly comprises brain tumor data from particular imaging techniques. The findings show promising enhancement performance for these pictures, but the algorithm’s applicability to CT, X-ray, and PET scans needs more confirmation. The intensity distributions and noise characteristics of different modalities may impact histogram-based enhancement algorithms. This research evaluated picture quality markers such PSNR, SSIM, entropy, and contrast. Although these criteria quantify enhanced quality and structural preservation, they do not completely represent clinical interpretability or radiologist impression. To evaluate the clinical workflow use of improved pictures, professional radiologist assessment or user studies are needed.

Conclusion

This study used an adaptive histogram-based medical picture enhancement framework to boost contrast while preserving diagnostic structural information. Statistical normalization, histogram probability modeling, Gaussian intensity mapping, and adaptive brightness-controlled histogram correction improve low-contrast medical images. The approach was evaluated using BraTS 2020 Brain Tumor Segmentation Dataset MRI scans, a large collection of multi-modal brain tumor images. Quantitative data shows the recommended strategy outperforms current enhancing strategies. The average PSNR was 37.82 dB, 6.36 dB higher than standard histogram equalization and 3.67 dB higher than CLAHE. The structural similarity score of 0.962 indicates substantial anatomical structure preservation compared to HE (0.891) and Gamma Correction (0.902). It lowered mean squared error by 41–57%, improving images without noise or distortions. Contrast and information richness improved when entropy analysis climbed from 6.92 bits (original pictures) to 7.84 bits. Clinical interpretation and computer-aided diagnosis benefit from improved MRI images, which show better tumor boundaries and edema clarity. The method effectively handles large medical datasets and high-resolution pictures since its computing complexity is linear with image size. The adaptive histogram-based solution balances contrast augmentation, noise reduction, and structure preservation, making it suitable for medical imaging processes and automated diagnostic systems. Future research will combine the improvement module with deep learning-based segmentation models to enhance tumor detection and clinical decision support.

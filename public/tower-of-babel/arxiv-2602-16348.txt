Heat Equation driven by mixed local-nonlocal operators with non-regular space-dependent coefficients
Arshyn Altybay, Michael Ruzhansky

Abstract
. In this paper, we study the Cauchy problem for a heat equation governed by a mixed local–nonlocal diffusion operator with spatially irregular coefficients. We first establish classical well-posedness in an energy framework for bounded, measurable coefficients that satisfy uniform positivity, and we derive an a priori estimate ensuring uniqueness and continuous dependence on the initial data. We then extend the notion of solution to distributional coefficients and initial data by a Friedrichs-type regularisation procedure. Within this very weak framework, we establish the existence and uniqueness of solution nets and prove consistency with the classical weak solution whenever the coefficients are regular.

1.
Introduction

Motivated by ecological models in which individuals move by a superposition of
(i) local Brownian diffusion and (ii) long-range Lévy-type jumps,
Dipierro and Valdinoci
[
20
]
derived evolution equations whose dispersal mechanism is governed by the mixed operator

\mathcal{L}_{0}u\;:=\;-\Delta u+(-\Delta)^{s}u,\qquad s\in(0,1).

(1.1)

Here the local part
-\Delta
describes short-range random motion, whereas the fractional Laplacian
(-\Delta)^{s}

encodes long-range interactions and jump processes.
In the whole space
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
(-\Delta)^{s}
may be defined via the principal value integral

(-\Delta)^{s}u(x)=c_{d,s}\,\mathrm{P.V.}\!\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{u(x)-u(y)}{|x-y|^{d+2s}}\,dy,

so that
\mathcal{L}_{0}
corresponds (up to sign conventions) to the infinitesimal generator of a stochastic motion
obtained by superposing a Brownian component with an independent symmetric
\alpha
-stable jump process,
\alpha=2s

[
11
,
12
]
.
In bounded domains, the mixed nature of (
1.1
) leads naturally to boundary conditions that combine
a classical Neumann constraint for the local part with a genuinely nonlocal flux condition for the fractional component,
as proposed in
[
20
]
. A systematic PDE analysis of elliptic problems driven by (
1.1
)
was later developed in
[
6
]
.

The mixed local–nonlocal operator
\mathcal{L}_{0}
has attracted considerable attention in recent years and has been investigated from several perspectives. This sustained interest is largely driven by its ability to capture mechanisms in which
local diffusion
coexists with
long-range interactions
and jump-like dispersal. In probability theory, operators of the form
-\Delta+(-\Delta)^{s}
arise as infinitesimal generators of stochastic dynamics obtained by superimposing a Brownian motion with an independent Lévy jump process, thereby modelling trajectories with frequent small displacements interspersed with occasional long relocations. In mathematical biology and ecology, the same superposition naturally appears in models of animal movement and optimal foraging, where long-range relocation (often idealised via Lévy flights) complements local exploratory behaviour and leads to mixed dispersal laws. Similar hybrid effects are also used to describe
anomalous transport
in complex environments (e.g. dispersal in porous or turbulent media),
heterogeneous materials
in which short-range diffusion is coupled with nonlocal transfer, and
diffusion with trapping, absorption, or killing
, where lower-order potential terms represent loss mechanisms or reactions. For further background and additional references, we refer the reader to
[
13
,
17
,
18
,
19
,
7
,
24
,
33
,
34
]
and the works cited therein.

In this paper, we extend (
1.1
) by allowing variable coefficients and consider the Cauchy problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}u_{t}(t,x)+\mathcal{L}u(t,x)=0,\qquad(t,x)\in(0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d},\\
u(0,x)=u_{0}(x),\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\end{array}\right.

(1.2)

where
\mathcal{L}
is the mixed local–nonlocal operator

\mathcal{L}u=-\nabla\cdot\bigl(a(x)\nabla u\bigr)+(-\Delta)^{s/2}\Bigl(b(x)\,(-\Delta)^{s/2}u\Bigr)+c(x)u,\qquad 0<s<1.

Here
u
denotes a scalar state variable (e.g., temperature, concentration, density),
u_{0}
is the initial datum. The coefficients
a,b,c
are assumed real-valued, with

a(x)\geq a_{0}>0
,
b(x)\geq b_{0}>0
, and
c(x)\geq c_{0}\geq 0
for a.e.
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Moreover,
(-\Delta)^{s/2}
denotes the (Riesz) fractional Laplacian.
Note that when
b\equiv\text{const}
, the fractional term reduces to
b\,(-\Delta)^{s}u
.

More precisely, the divergence-form term
-\nabla\!\cdot(a(x)\nabla u)
models heterogeneous classical diffusion,
whereas the fractional component
(-\Delta)^{s/2}\bigl(b(x)(-\Delta)^{s/2}u\bigr)
incorporates nonlocal effects of order
2s

modulated by
b(x)
. The lower-order term
c(x)u
may be interpreted, depending on the application, as reaction, damping,
or absorption.

We assume that the coefficients
u_{0},a,b,c\in\mathcal{D^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R^{d}})
are irregular, distributional in space. Once the coefficients are distributional, one encounters the classical obstruction pointed out by Schwartz: in general, there is no consistent product of distributions extending the pointwise product of smooth functions
[
31
]
. Consequently, the equation cannot be interpreted in a classical sense, and classical (or even standard weak) solution frameworks typically break down when distributional coefficients must be multiplied.

To overcome this difficulty, we employ the notion of
very weak solutions
, introduced by Garetto and the second author
[
22
]
. This approach is based on regularising the distributional coefficients and data, solving a family of smooth problems, and identifying a solution concept through suitable moderateness and consistency properties.

This approach has proved effective for second-order hyperbolic and parabolic equations with non-regular coefficients
[
28
,
29
]
, including equations with singular time-dependent potentials and other distributional terms; in and further extended in
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
4
,
14
,
32
,
15
,
16
,
8
,
30
,
26
,
9
,
10
,
25
,
23
,
5
]
.

In the present work, we extend this methodology to the heat equation driven by mixed local-nonlocal operator (
1.2
) with variable coefficients
a,b,c\in\mathcal{D^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R^{d}})
modelled by the space of distributions.

The novelty of this work lies in the rigorous analysis of a parabolic equation with a mixed local–nonlocal diffusion operator and spatially irregular coefficients, a setting that has not been previously studied in a unified analytical framework. The results provide a foundation for further analytical and numerical investigations of such models.

The remainder of this paper is organised as follows. Section
2
collects the necessary preliminaries. In Section
3
, we study the Cauchy problem in the regular case and establish well-posedness in the weak sense. Section
4
is devoted to the very weak formulation, where existence, uniqueness, and consistency results are proved for spatially irregular coefficients. The paper concludes in Section
5
.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we recall fundamental inequalities and definitions that are frequently used in the analysis of fractional partial differential equations.

Definition 1

(
Riesz fractional Laplacian in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
)
.

Let
0<s<1
and
u\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. The (Riesz) fractional Laplacian is defined by

(-\Delta)^{s}u(x)=C_{d,s}\,\mathrm{p.v.}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{u(x)-u(y)}{|x-y|^{d+2s}}\,dy,

where
C_{d,s}>0
is a normalization constant. Equivalently, in Fourier variables,

\widehat{(-\Delta)^{s}u}(\xi)=|\xi|^{2s}\,\hat{u}(\xi).

Definition 2

(
Fourier transform
)
.

For
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
we define

\hat{f}(\xi)=\mathcal{F}(f)(\xi):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-ix\cdot\xi}f(x)\,dx,\qquad f(x)=\mathcal{F}^{-1}(\hat{f})(x)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{ix\cdot\xi}\hat{f}(\xi)\,d\xi.

Lemma 2.1

(
Plancherel identity
)
.

For
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
we have
\|f\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d}}\|\hat{f}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}
.

Definition 3

(
Gagliardo seminorm
)
.

Let
0<s<1
. For
u:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
define

[u]_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}:=\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{d+2s}}\,dx\,dy.

Lemma 2.2

(
Equivalence of fractional norms
)
.

Let
0<s<1
. Then there exists
C=C(d,s)>0
such that for all
u\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,

\|(-\Delta)^{s/2}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\simeq[u]_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2},\qquad\|u\|_{H^{s}}^{2}\simeq\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+[u]_{H^{s}}^{2}.

Lemma 2.3

(
Fractional integration by parts
)
.

Let
0<s<1
and
u,v\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Then

\bigl((-\Delta)^{s/2}u,(-\Delta)^{s/2}v\bigr)_{L^{2}}=\langle(-\Delta)^{s}u,v\rangle_{H^{-s},H^{s}}.

In particular,
(-\Delta)^{s}
is self-adjoint and nonnegative.

Lemma 2.4

(
Fourier domination for
0<s<1
)
.

Let
0<s<1
. Then for all
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

|\xi|^{2s}\leq 1+|\xi|^{2}.

Consequently, for every
v\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,

\|(-\Delta)^{s/2}v\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\|v\|_{H^{1}}^{2}.

Definition 4

(
Gelfand triple and duality
)
.

Let
V
be a Hilbert space continuously and densely embedded into
H
(another Hilbert space), written
V\hookrightarrow H
.
We identify
H
with its dual
H^{\prime}
and obtain the Gelfand triple

V\hookrightarrow H\simeq H^{\prime}\hookrightarrow V^{\prime}.

The duality pairing between
V^{\prime}
and
V
is denoted by
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
.

Lemma 2.5

(
Lions–Magenes
)
.

Let
V\hookrightarrow H
be a Gelfand triple. If

u\in L^{2}(0,T;V),\qquad u_{t}\in L^{2}(0,T;V^{\prime}),

then
u
admits a representative in
C([0,T];H)
and for a.e.
t
,

\frac{d}{dt}\frac{1}{2}\|u(t)\|_{H}^{2}=\langle u_{t}(t),u(t)\rangle_{V^{\prime},V}.

Definition 5

(
Energy bilinear form
)
.

Assume
a,b,c\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
0<s<1
. For
u,v\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
define

B(u,v):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}a\,\nabla u\cdot\nabla v\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}b\,(-\Delta)^{s/2}u\,(-\Delta)^{s/2}v\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}c\,uv\,dx.

Lemma 2.6

(
Boundedness and coercivity of
B
)
.

Assume
a\geq a_{0}>0
,
b\geq b_{0}>0
, and
c\geq 0
a.e. Then
B
is continuous on
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and

B(u,u)\geq a_{0}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+b_{0}\|(-\Delta)^{s/2}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

If moreover
c\geq c_{0}>0
, then
B(u,u)\geq c_{0}\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}
as well.

3.
Well-posedness of the problem in the regular case

In this section, we analyse the well-posedness of the Cauchy problem (
1.2
) under the assumption that the equation coefficients and initial data possess sufficient regularity.

3.1.
Energy estimate in the regular case

We work with the standard variational formulation associated with
\mathcal{L}
.
Define the bilinear form

\displaystyle B(u,v)

\displaystyle:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}a(x)\,\nabla u(x)\cdot\overline{\nabla v(x)}\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}b(x)\,(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}u(x)\,\overline{(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}v(x)}\,dx

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}c(x)\,u(x)\overline{v(x)}\,dx,

for
u,v\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Since
0<s<1
, one has the continuous embedding

H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\hookrightarrow H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, hence all terms are well-defined.
Moreover,
B
is bounded and coercive on
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
in the sense that

|B(u,v)|\leq\bigl(\|a\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|b\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|c\|_{L^{\infty}}\bigr)\,\bigl(\|u\|_{H^{1}}+\|(-\Delta)^{s/2}u\|_{L^{2}}\bigr)\,\bigl(\|v\|_{H^{1}}+\|(-\Delta)^{s/2}v\|_{L^{2}}\bigr),

and

B(u,u)\geq a_{0}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+b_{0}\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+c_{0}\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\qquad\text{for all }u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

Definition 3.1

(Weak solution)
.

A function
u
is called a weak solution of (
1.2
) if

u\in L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})),\qquad u_{t}\in L^{2}\bigl(0,T;(H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))^{\prime}\bigr),

u(0)=u_{0}
in
L^{2}
, and for a.e.
t\in(0,T)
,

\langle u_{t}(t),\varphi\rangle+B(u(t),\varphi)=0\qquad\forall\,\varphi\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

(3.1)

Lemma 3.1

(Existence and uniqueness)
.

Let
0<s<1
and let
a,b,c\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
be real-valued with

a(x)\geq a_{0}>0,\qquad b(x)\geq b_{0}>0,\qquad c(x)\geq c_{0}>0\quad\text{for a.e. }x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Let
u_{0}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and consider (
1.2
).
Then there exists a unique weak solution
u
such that

u\in L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))\cap C([0,T];L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})),\qquad u_{t}\in L^{2}\bigl(0,T;(H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))^{\prime}\bigr).

Moreover, for a.e.
t\in[0,T]
,

\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}+\|\nabla u(t)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}+\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}u(t)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}\leq C\,\|u_{0}\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2},

(3.2)

with

C:=2\Bigl(1+\frac{1}{a_{0}}+\frac{1}{b_{0}}\Bigr)\Bigl(1+\|a\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\|b\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\|c\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\Bigr).

Proof.

Galerkin approximation.

Let
\{w_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}\subset H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
be a complete orthonormal system in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

such that
\mathrm{span}\{w_{1},\dots,w_{N}\}
is dense in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
Set
V_{N}:=\mathrm{span}\{w_{1},\dots,w_{N}\}
and choose
u_{0}^{N}\in V_{N}
such that
u_{0}^{N}\to u_{0}
in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Seek
u^{N}:[0,T]\to V_{N}
solving

(u_{t}^{N}(t),\varphi)_{L^{2}}+B(u^{N}(t),\varphi)=0,\qquad\forall\varphi\in V_{N},\ \text{a.e. }t\in(0,T),\qquad u^{N}(0)=u_{0}^{N}.

(3.3)

Writing
u^{N}(t)=\sum_{k=1}^{N}g_{k}(t)w_{k}
, (
3.3
) is an ODE system for
g_{k}
with a (symmetric) positive definite matrix.
Hence there exists a unique
u^{N}\in C^{1}([0,T];V_{N})
.

Step 1: Energy identities (performed for
u^{N}
).

(i) Testing with
\varphi=u^{N}(t)
.

Taking
\varphi=u^{N}(t)
in (
3.3
) gives

(u_{t}^{N}(t),u^{N}(t))_{L^{2}}+B(u^{N}(t),u^{N}(t))=0.

Taking real parts, we obtain

\Re(u_{t}^{N}(t),u^{N}(t))_{L^{2}}+B(u^{N}(t),u^{N}(t))=0.

First,

\displaystyle\Re(u_{t}^{N}(t),u^{N}(t))_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=\Re\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}u_{t}^{N}(t,x)\,\overline{u^{N}(t,x)}\,dx=\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|u^{N}(t,x)|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|u^{N}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Next, by definition of
B
,

\displaystyle B(u^{N}(t),u^{N}(t))

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}a(x)\,|\nabla u^{N}(t,x)|^{2}\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}b(x)\,\bigl|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}u^{N}(t,x)\bigr|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}c(x)\,|u^{N}(t,x)|^{2}\,dx,

hence

B(u^{N}(t),u^{N}(t))=\|a^{1/2}\nabla u^{N}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|b^{1/2}(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}u^{N}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|c^{1/2}u^{N}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Combining these identities yields

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|u^{N}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|a^{1/2}\nabla u^{N}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|b^{1/2}(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}u^{N}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|c^{1/2}u^{N}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=0.

(3.4)

(ii) Testing with
\varphi=u_{t}^{N}(t)
.

Taking
\varphi=u_{t}^{N}(t)
in (
3.3
) gives

(u_{t}^{N}(t),u_{t}^{N}(t))_{L^{2}}+B(u^{N}(t),u_{t}^{N}(t))=0,

that is,

\|u_{t}^{N}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+B(u^{N}(t),u_{t}^{N}(t))=0.

Taking real parts, we obtain

\|u_{t}^{N}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\Re B(u^{N}(t),u_{t}^{N}(t))=0.

We again expand
B(u^{N},u_{t}^{N})
term-by-term:

\displaystyle B(u^{N}(t),u_{t}^{N}(t))=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}a(x)\,\nabla u^{N}(t,x)\cdot\overline{\nabla u_{t}^{N}(t,x)}\,dx

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}b(x)\,(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}u^{N}(t,x)\,\overline{(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}u_{t}^{N}(t,x)}\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}c(x)\,u^{N}(t,x)\,\overline{u_{t}^{N}(t,x)}\,dx.

Since
a,b,c
are independent of
t
, each term is an exact time derivative (in the real part):

\Re\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}a\,\nabla u^{N}\cdot\overline{\nabla u_{t}^{N}}\,dx=\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}a\,|\nabla u^{N}|^{2}\,dx=\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|a^{1/2}\nabla u^{N}\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

\Re\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}b\,(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}u^{N}\,\overline{(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}u_{t}^{N}}\,dx=\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}b\,\bigl|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}u^{N}\bigr|^{2}\,dx=\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|b^{1/2}(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}u^{N}\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

\Re\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}c\,u^{N}\overline{u_{t}^{N}}\,dx=\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}c\,|u^{N}|^{2}\,dx=\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|c^{1/2}u^{N}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Therefore,

\Re B(u^{N}(t),u_{t}^{N}(t))=\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\Bigl[\|a^{1/2}\nabla u^{N}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|b^{1/2}(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}u^{N}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|c^{1/2}u^{N}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\Bigr],

and we arrive at

\|u_{t}^{N}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\Bigl[\|a^{1/2}\nabla u^{N}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|b^{1/2}(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}u^{N}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|c^{1/2}u^{N}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\Bigr]=0.

(3.5)

Summing (
3.4
) and (
3.5
) yields

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}E_{N}(t)=-\Bigl[\|u_{t}^{N}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|a^{1/2}\nabla u^{N}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|b^{1/2}(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}u^{N}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|c^{1/2}u^{N}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\Bigr]\leq 0,

where

E_{N}(t):=\|u^{N}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|a^{1/2}\nabla u^{N}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|b^{1/2}(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}u^{N}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|c^{1/2}u^{N}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Hence
E_{N}(t)\leq E_{N}(0)
for all
t\in[0,T]
.

Step 2: From weighted to standard norms.

Using
a\geq a_{0}
and
b\geq b_{0}
, we obtain

\|u^{N}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla u^{N}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}u^{N}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\Bigl(1+\frac{1}{a_{0}}+\frac{1}{b_{0}}\Bigr)E_{N}(t)\leq\Bigl(1+\frac{1}{a_{0}}+\frac{1}{b_{0}}\Bigr)E_{N}(0).

Step 3: Estimate
E_{N}(0)
by
\|u_{0}^{N}\|_{H^{1}}^{2}
.

By
a,b,c\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,

E_{N}(0)\leq\bigl(1+\|a\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|b\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|c\|_{L^{\infty}}\bigr)\Bigl(\|u_{0}^{N}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla u_{0}^{N}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}u_{0}^{N}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\Bigr).

Moreover, for
0<s<1
one has the pointwise Fourier inequality
|\xi|^{2s}\leq 1+|\xi|^{2}
, hence

\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}v\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\|v\|_{H^{1}}^{2}\qquad\text{for all }v\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),

and therefore

\|v\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}v\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq 2\,\|v\|_{H^{1}}^{2}\qquad\text{for all }v\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

Consequently,

E_{N}(0)\leq 2\bigl(1+\|a\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|b\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|c\|_{L^{\infty}}\bigr)\,\|u_{0}^{N}\|_{H^{1}}^{2}.

Combining with Step 2 yields, for all
t\in[0,T]
,

\|u^{N}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla u^{N}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}u^{N}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C\,\|u_{0}^{N}\|_{H^{1}}^{2},

with
C
exactly as in the statement. In particular,
\{u^{N}\}_{N}
is bounded in

L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
.

Step 4: Passage
N\to\infty
(weak compactness and identification of the limit).

From (
3.5
) and
E_{N}(t)\leq E_{N}(0)
we also have

\int_{0}^{T}\|u_{t}^{N}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\,dt\leq\frac{1}{2}\Bigl(\|a^{1/2}\nabla u_{0}^{N}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|b^{1/2}(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}u_{0}^{N}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|c^{1/2}u_{0}^{N}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\Bigr)\leq C_{1}\|u_{0}\|_{H^{1}}^{2}

for some constant
C_{1}
independent of
N
(by the bounds in Step 3 and
u_{0}^{N}\to u_{0}
in
H^{1}
).
Hence
\{u_{t}^{N}\}_{N}
is bounded in
L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
, and therefore also in

L^{2}\bigl(0,T;(H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))^{\prime}\bigr)
.

By Banach–Alaoglu, there exists a subsequence (not relabeled) and a function
u
such that

u^{N}\rightharpoonup^{\ast}u\ \text{ in }L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})),\qquad u_{t}^{N}\rightharpoonup u_{t}\ \text{ in }L^{2}\bigl(0,T;(H^{1}\cap H^{s})^{\prime}\bigr).

Passing to the limit in (
3.3
) (written in integrated form against time-dependent test functions in
L^{2}(0,T;H^{1})
)
yields that
u
satisfies the weak formulation (
3.1
).
Moreover, since
u\in L^{2}(0,T;H^{1})
and
u_{t}\in L^{2}(0,T;(H^{1})^{\prime})
,
the Lions–Magenes lemma implies
u\in C([0,T];L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
and
u(0)=u_{0}
in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

(because
u_{0}^{N}\to u_{0}
in
L^{2}
and
u^{N}(0)=u_{0}^{N}
).

Finally, by weak lower semicontinuity (applied for a.e.
t
) and
u_{0}^{N}\to u_{0}
in
H^{1}
,
letting
N\to\infty
in the estimate of Step 3 gives (
3.2
).

Uniqueness.

Let
u_{1},u_{2}
be two weak solutions and set
w:=u_{1}-u_{2}
.
Then
w(0)=0
and
\langle w_{t},\varphi\rangle+B(w,\varphi)=0
for all
\varphi\in H^{1}
.
Taking
\varphi=w(t)
gives

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|w(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+B(w(t),w(t))=0.

Since
B(w,w)\geq 0
, we have
\frac{d}{dt}\|w(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq 0
and
\|w(0)\|_{L^{2}}=0
,
hence
\|w(t)\|_{L^{2}}=0
for all
t\in[0,T]
, i.e.
u_{1}\equiv u_{2}
.
∎

4.
Very weak well-posedness

In this section, we extend the notion of solution to the case where the
spatial
coefficients and the initial datum are irregular (distributional) on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
More precisely, we assume that

\ a,\ b,\ c,\ u_{0}\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),

and we interpret (
1.2
) in a
very weak sense
by means of a Friedrichs-type regularisation procedure. The guiding idea is to
replace
a,b,c,u_{0}
by suitable regularising nets and solve a family of regular problems, for which the
regular theory and the a priori estimate from Section
4
apply.

4.1.
Spatial regularisation and moderate nets

Let
\psi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
be a standard Friedrichs mollifier, i.e.

\psi\geq 0
and
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\psi(x)\,dx=1
.
Let
\omega:(0,1]\to(0,1]
be a fixed
regularisation scale
such that

\omega(\varepsilon)\to 0
as
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
.
For
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
we set

\psi_{\omega(\varepsilon)}(x):=\omega(\varepsilon)^{-d}\,\psi\!\left(\frac{x}{\omega(\varepsilon)}\right),\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

We regularise in the spatial variable by convolution:

a_{\varepsilon}:=a*\psi_{\omega(\varepsilon)},\qquad b_{\varepsilon}:=b*\psi_{\omega(\varepsilon)},\qquad c_{\varepsilon}:=c*\psi_{\omega(\varepsilon)},\qquad u_{0,\varepsilon}:=u_{0}*\psi_{\omega(\varepsilon)},

(4.1)

Then
a_{\varepsilon},b_{\varepsilon},c_{\varepsilon},u_{0,\varepsilon}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for each fixed
\varepsilon
.

For each
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
we consider the regularised Cauchy problem

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}u_{\varepsilon}(t,x)+\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon}(t,x)=0,&&(t,x)\in(0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d},\\
&u_{\varepsilon}(0,x)=u_{0,\varepsilon}(x),&&x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\end{aligned}\right.

(4.2)

where

\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}u:=-\nabla\cdot\bigl(a_{\varepsilon}(x)\nabla u\bigr)+(-\Delta)^{s/2}\Bigl(b_{\varepsilon}(x)\,(-\Delta)^{s/2}u\Bigr)+c_{\varepsilon}(x)\,u.

(4.3)

To control the behaviour as
\varepsilon\to 0
, we work with the standard notions of

moderateness
and
negligibility
.

Definition 6

(
Moderate nets
)
.

Let
\omega:(0,1]\to(0,1]
be a fixed regularising scale with
\omega(\varepsilon)\to 0
as
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
.

(i)

A net of function
(g_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}
is said to be
L^{\infty}
-moderate
if for every compact set
K\Subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
there exist
C>0
and
N\in\mathbb{N}_{0}

such that

\|g_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}(K)}\leq C\,\omega(\varepsilon)^{-N},\qquad\varepsilon\in(0,1].

(ii)

A net of function
(r_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}
is said to be
H^{1}
-moderate
if for every compact set
K\Subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
there exist

C>0
and
N\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
such that

\|r_{\varepsilon}\|_{H^{1}(K)}\leq C\,\omega(\varepsilon)^{-N},\qquad\varepsilon\in(0,1].

(iii)

A net of function
(u_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}
is said to be
L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{1})
-moderate
if for every compact set
K\Subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
there exist

C>0
and
N\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
such that

\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{1}(K))}\leq C\,\omega(\varepsilon)^{-N},\qquad\varepsilon\in(0,1].

Remark 4.1
.

It should be noted that such assumptions are natural for distributions.
Let
T\in\mathcal{E}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R})
be compactly supported.
By the structure theorem for compactly supported distributions (see, e.g.,

[
21
]
), there exist
n\in\mathbb{N}
and functions

T_{\alpha}\in C_{c}(\mathbb{R})
such that

T=\sum_{|\alpha|\leq n}\partial^{\alpha}T_{\alpha},

where
|\alpha|
denotes the order of the derivative.

Let
\psi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
be a standard mollifier with

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\psi(x)\,dx=1
, and define

\psi_{\varepsilon}(x):=\omega(\varepsilon)^{-1}\,\psi\!\left(\frac{x}{\omega(\varepsilon)}\right),\qquad\varepsilon\in(0,1].

Then

T*\psi_{\varepsilon}=\sum_{|\alpha|\leq n}T_{\alpha}*\partial^{\alpha}\psi_{\varepsilon}=\sum_{|\alpha|\leq n}\omega(\varepsilon)^{-1-|\alpha|}\,T_{\alpha}*\bigl(\partial^{\alpha}\psi\bigr)\!\left(\frac{\cdot}{\omega(\varepsilon)}\right).

Since each
T_{\alpha}*\partial^{\alpha}\psi
is bounded and has compact support, we obtain for every compact interval
K\Subset\mathbb{R}
the estimate

\|T*\psi_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}(K)}\leq C\,\omega(\varepsilon)^{-N},\qquad\varepsilon\in(0,1],

for suitable
C>0
and
N\in\mathbb{N}
.

4.2.
Existence of very weak solutions

In this subsection we establish existence of very weak solutions for (
1.2
) with spatially
distributional coefficients and initial data.

Definition 7

(
Very weak solution
)
.

Let
u_{0},a,b,c\in\mathcal{E}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
be compactly supported distributions.
A net
(u_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}
is called a
very weak solution
of (
1.2
) if there exist
regularising nets
(a_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon},(b_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon},(c_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}
and
(u_{0,\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}

such that:

(i)

(a_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon},(b_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon},(c_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}
are
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
-moderate regularisations of
a,b,c

and
(u_{0,\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}
is an
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
-moderate regularisation of
u_{0}
(in the sense of Definition
6
);

(ii)

(
uniform positivity
) there exist constants
a_{0},b_{0},c_{0}>0
such that for all
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
,

a_{\varepsilon}(x)\geq a_{0},\qquad b_{\varepsilon}(x)\geq b_{0},\qquad c_{\varepsilon}(x)\geq c_{0}\quad\text{for a.e. }x\in\mathbb{R}^{d};

(4.4)

(iii)

for every
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
, the function
u_{\varepsilon}
is the (unique) weak solution to the regularised problem
(
4.2
)–(
4.3
) with initial datum
u_{\varepsilon}(0)=u_{0,\varepsilon}
;

(iv)

the solution net
(u_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}
is
L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{1})
-moderate, i.e. for every compact set
K\Subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
there exist

C_{K}>0
and
N_{K}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
such that

\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{1}(K))}\leq C_{K}\,\omega(\varepsilon)^{-N_{K}},\qquad\varepsilon\in(0,1].

Theorem 4.1

(Existence of very weak solutions)
.

Let
u_{0},a,b,c\in\mathcal{E}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and let
(a_{\varepsilon},b_{\varepsilon},c_{\varepsilon},u_{0,\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}

be spatial regularisations defined by (
4.1
).
Assume that
a,b,c
are
strictly positive
in the sense of distributions, i.e. there exist constants

a_{0},b_{0},c_{0}>0
such that

\langle a-a_{0},\varphi\rangle\geq 0,\qquad\langle b-b_{0},\varphi\rangle\geq 0,\qquad\langle c-c_{0},\varphi\rangle\geq 0,\qquad\forall\,\varphi\in\mathcal{E}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),\ \varphi\geq 0.

Assume that
(a_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon},(b_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon},(c_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}
are
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
-moderate,

(u_{0,\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}
is
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
-moderate, and that the uniform positivity (
4.4
) holds.
Then the Cauchy problem (
1.2
) admits a very weak solution
(u_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}
in the sense of Definition
7
.

Proof.

Fix
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
. Since
a,b,c,u_{0}\in\mathcal{E}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
\psi_{\omega(\varepsilon)}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
the convolutions (
4.1
) satisfy

a_{\varepsilon},b_{\varepsilon},c_{\varepsilon},u_{0,\varepsilon}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\subset C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

In particular,
a_{\varepsilon},b_{\varepsilon},c_{\varepsilon}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
u_{0,\varepsilon}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
By the uniform positivity (
4.4
), the regular well-posedness result
(Lemma
3.1
, applied with coefficients
a_{\varepsilon},b_{\varepsilon},c_{\varepsilon}
)
yields a unique weak solution
u_{\varepsilon}
of the regularised problem (
4.2
)–(
4.3
)
in the corresponding energy class; in particular,

u_{\varepsilon}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))\cap C([0,T];L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})).

Moderateness of
(u_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}
.

Applying the a priori estimate of Lemma
3.1
to
u_{\varepsilon}
gives, for all
t\in[0,T]
,

\|u_{\varepsilon}(t)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}+\|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}(t)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}+\|(-\Delta)^{s/2}u_{\varepsilon}(t)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}\leq C_{\varepsilon}\,\|u_{0,\varepsilon}\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2},

(4.5)

where one may take

C_{\varepsilon}:=2\Bigl(1+\frac{1}{a_{0}}+\frac{1}{b_{0}}\Bigr)\Bigl(1+\|a_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\|b_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\|c_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\Bigr).

(4.6)

By
L^{\infty}
-moderateness of
(a_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon},(b_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon},(c_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}
,
there exist
C>0
and
N\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
such that

\|a_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\|b_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\|c_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C\,\omega(\varepsilon)^{-N},\qquad\varepsilon\in(0,1],

hence (after enlarging
C
and
N
if needed) we also have

C_{\varepsilon}\leq C\,\omega(\varepsilon)^{-N},\qquad\varepsilon\in(0,1].

(4.7)

Moreover, by
H^{1}
-moderateness of
(u_{0,\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}
, there exist
C^{\prime}>0
and
N^{\prime}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
such that

\|u_{0,\varepsilon}\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}\leq C^{\prime}\,\omega(\varepsilon)^{-N^{\prime}},\qquad\varepsilon\in(0,1].

(4.8)

Combining (
4.5
), (
4.7
), and (
4.8
) yields

\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\Bigl(\|u_{\varepsilon}(t)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}+\|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}(t)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}+\|(-\Delta)^{s/2}u_{\varepsilon}(t)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}\Bigr)\leq\widetilde{C}\,\omega(\varepsilon)^{-\widetilde{N}},\qquad\varepsilon\in(0,1],

(4.9)

for some
\widetilde{C}>0
and
\widetilde{N}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.

Finally, since
K\Subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
implies
\|v\|_{H^{1}(K)}\leq\|v\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}
, we obtain from
(
4.9
) that for every compact set
K\Subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{1}(K))}\leq\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\leq\widehat{C}\,\omega(\varepsilon)^{-\widehat{N}},\qquad\varepsilon\in(0,1],

for suitable constants
\widehat{C}>0
and
\widehat{N}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.
This is precisely the
L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{1})
-moderateness required in Definition
7
.
Therefore
(u_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}
is a very weak solution of (
1.2
).
∎

4.3.
Uniqueness

Before stating our uniqueness result, we need the following definition:

Definition 4.1

(Negligible nets)
.

Let
X
be a Banach space. A net
(g_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}\subset X
is called
X
-negligible
if
for every
q\in\mathbb{N}
there exists
C_{q}>0
such that

\|g_{\varepsilon}\|_{X}\leq C_{q}\,\omega(\varepsilon)^{q},\qquad\varepsilon\in(0,1].

Remark 4.2
.

Negligible nets form an ideal in the algebra of moderate nets: if
(m_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}
is
X
-moderate and

(n_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}
is
X
-negligible, then
(m_{\varepsilon}n_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}
is negligible in
X

whenever the product is well-defined pointwise (e.g. in
L^{\infty}
-based spaces).

Theorem 4.2

(Uniqueness of very weak solutions)
.

Let
a,b,c,u_{0}\in\mathcal{E}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and let

(a_{\varepsilon},b_{\varepsilon},c_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}
and

(\tilde{a}_{\varepsilon},\tilde{b}_{\varepsilon},\tilde{c}_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}

be two
L^{\infty}
-moderate regularisations of
(a,b,c)
such that

(a_{\varepsilon}-\tilde{a}_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon},\ (b_{\varepsilon}-\tilde{b}_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon},\ (c_{\varepsilon}-\tilde{c}_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}\ \text{are negligible in }L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

Let
(u_{0,\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}
and
(\tilde{u}_{0,\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}
be two
H^{1}
-moderate regularisations of
u_{0}

such that
(u_{0,\varepsilon}-\tilde{u}_{0,\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}
is negligible in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
Assume moreover the uniform positivity

a_{\varepsilon}\geq a_{0}>0,\ b_{\varepsilon}\geq b_{0}>0,\ c_{\varepsilon}\geq c_{0}>0,\quad\tilde{a}_{\varepsilon}\geq a_{0}>0,\ \tilde{b}_{\varepsilon}\geq b_{0}>0,\ \tilde{c}_{\varepsilon}\geq c_{0}>0,\quad\varepsilon\in(0,1].

For each
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
, let
u_{\varepsilon}
and
\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}
be the corresponding (regular) weak solutions of

\partial_{t}u_{\varepsilon}+\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon}=0,\quad u_{\varepsilon}(0)=u_{0,\varepsilon},\qquad\partial_{t}\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}+\tilde{\mathcal{L}}_{\varepsilon}\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}=0,\quad\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}(0)=\tilde{u}_{0,\varepsilon},

where
\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}
and
\tilde{\mathcal{L}}_{\varepsilon}
are defined as in (
4.3
) with
coefficients
(a_{\varepsilon},b_{\varepsilon},c_{\varepsilon})
and
(\tilde{a}_{\varepsilon},\tilde{b}_{\varepsilon},\tilde{c}_{\varepsilon})
, respectively.
If both solution nets are
L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{1})
-moderate, then

(u_{\varepsilon}-\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}\ \text{is negligible in }L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))\cap L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})).

In particular, the very weak solution is unique up to negligibility.

Proof.

Set
w_{\varepsilon}:=u_{\varepsilon}-\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}
and write the variational forms using

B_{\varepsilon}(u,v):=\int a_{\varepsilon}\nabla u\cdot\overline{\nabla v}+\int b_{\varepsilon}(-\Delta)^{s/2}u\,\overline{(-\Delta)^{s/2}v}+\int c_{\varepsilon}u\overline{v},

and similarly
\tilde{B}_{\varepsilon}
. Subtracting the two weak formulations yields, for all
\varphi\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,

\langle\partial_{t}w_{\varepsilon},\varphi\rangle+B_{\varepsilon}(w_{\varepsilon},\varphi)=(\tilde{B}_{\varepsilon}-B_{\varepsilon})(\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon},\varphi),\qquad w_{\varepsilon}(0)=u_{0,\varepsilon}-\tilde{u}_{0,\varepsilon}.

Testing with
\varphi=w_{\varepsilon}(t)
gives

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|w_{\varepsilon}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle+a_{0}\|\nabla w_{\varepsilon}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+b_{0}\|(-\Delta)^{s/2}w_{\varepsilon}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+c_{0}\|w_{\varepsilon}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\big|(\tilde{B}_{\varepsilon}-B_{\varepsilon})(\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}(t),w_{\varepsilon}(t))\big|.

Moreover,

\displaystyle\big|(\tilde{B}_{\varepsilon}-B_{\varepsilon})(\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon},w_{\varepsilon})\big|

\displaystyle\leq\|a_{\varepsilon}-\tilde{a}_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}}\,\|\nabla\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}}\,\|\nabla w_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\quad+\|b_{\varepsilon}-\tilde{b}_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}}\,\|(-\Delta)^{s/2}\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}}\,\|(-\Delta)^{s/2}w_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\quad+\|c_{\varepsilon}-\tilde{c}_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}}\,\|\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}}\,\|w_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}}.

Applying Young’s inequality and absorbing the coercive terms, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\|w_{\varepsilon}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\Bigl(\|a_{\varepsilon}-\tilde{a}_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\|\nabla\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|b_{\varepsilon}-\tilde{b}_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\|(-\Delta)^{s/2}\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle+\|c_{\varepsilon}-\tilde{c}_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\|\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\Bigr),

with
C
independent of
\varepsilon
. Integrating over
(0,T)
gives

\|w_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2})}^{2}\leq\|u_{0,\varepsilon}-\tilde{u}_{0,\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\int_{0}^{T}\Theta_{\varepsilon}(t)\,dt,

where
\Theta_{\varepsilon}(t)
is the bracketed expression above.

Since the coefficient difference nets are negligible in
L^{\infty}
and
(\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}
is moderate in
L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{1})

(and hence also in
L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2})
and
L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{s})
), the product estimates and Remark
4.2
imply that

\int_{0}^{T}\Theta_{\varepsilon}(t)\,dt
is negligible. Together with the negligible initial difference, this shows that

\|w_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2})}
is negligible.

Finally, integrating the coercive inequality also yields negligibility of

\|\nabla w_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2})}
(and of
\|(-\Delta)^{s/2}w_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2})}
), hence

(w_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}
is negligible in
L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2})\cap L^{2}(0,T;H^{1})
.
∎

4.4.
Consistency with classical theory

In this subsection, we prove that the very weak solution obtained in theorem
4.1
is consistent with the weak solution obtained in Lemma
3.1
.

Theorem 4.3

(Consistency)
.

Assume that
0<s<1
and that
a,b,c\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
satisfy

a(x)\geq a_{0}>0,\qquad b(x)\geq b_{0}>0,\qquad c(x)\geq c_{0}>0\quad\text{for a.e. }x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Let
u_{0}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, and let
u
be the unique weak solution of (
1.2
).

Let
(a_{\varepsilon},b_{\varepsilon},c_{\varepsilon},u_{0,\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}
be regularisations of

a,b,c,u_{0}
such that, as
\varepsilon\to 0
,

a_{\varepsilon}\to a,\quad b_{\varepsilon}\to b,\quad c_{\varepsilon}\to c\ \text{in }L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),\qquad u_{0,\varepsilon}\to u_{0}\ \text{in }H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),

and assume moreover that the regularised coefficients satisfy the
same uniform positivity
:

a_{\varepsilon}(x)\geq a_{0},\qquad b_{\varepsilon}(x)\geq b_{0},\qquad c_{\varepsilon}(x)\geq c_{0}\quad\text{for a.e. }x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\ \varepsilon\in(0,1].

For each
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
let
u_{\varepsilon}
be the unique weak solution of the regularised problem with data

a_{\varepsilon},b_{\varepsilon},c_{\varepsilon},u_{0,\varepsilon}
. Then

u_{\varepsilon}\to u\quad\text{in }C\bigl([0,T];L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\bigr)\cap L^{2}\bigl(0,T;H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\bigr)\quad\text{as }\varepsilon\to 0.

In particular, the very weak solution is consistent with the classical weak solution whenever the data are regular.

Proof.

Introduce the bilinear form associated with
\mathcal{L}
:

B(v,\varphi):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}a\,\nabla v\cdot\overline{\nabla\varphi}\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}b\,(-\Delta)^{s/2}v\,\overline{(-\Delta)^{s/2}\varphi}\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}c\,v\overline{\varphi}\,dx,

and similarly

B_{\varepsilon}(v,\varphi):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}a_{\varepsilon}\,\nabla v\cdot\overline{\nabla\varphi}\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}b_{\varepsilon}\,(-\Delta)^{s/2}v\,\overline{(-\Delta)^{s/2}\varphi}\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}c_{\varepsilon}\,v\overline{\varphi}\,dx.

Let
u
and
u_{\varepsilon}
be the weak solutions corresponding to
(a,b,c,u_{0})
and

(a_{\varepsilon},b_{\varepsilon},c_{\varepsilon},u_{0,\varepsilon})
, respectively, and define

U_{\varepsilon}(t,x):=u(t,x)-u_{\varepsilon}(t,x).

Step 1: The difference equation in variational form.

By the weak formulations of
u
and
u_{\varepsilon}
, for every
\varphi\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and a.e.
t\in(0,T)
,

\displaystyle\langle\partial_{t}U_{\varepsilon}(t),\varphi\rangle+B(U_{\varepsilon}(t),\varphi)

\displaystyle=B_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon}(t),\varphi)-B(u_{\varepsilon}(t),\varphi)

\displaystyle=:\langle G_{\varepsilon}(t),\varphi\rangle,

(4.10)

with initial condition
U_{\varepsilon}(0)=u_{0}-u_{0,\varepsilon}
in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Moreover, for

\varphi\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,

\displaystyle\langle G_{\varepsilon}(t),\varphi\rangle

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(a_{\varepsilon}-a)\,\nabla u_{\varepsilon}(t)\cdot\overline{\nabla\varphi}\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(b_{\varepsilon}-b)\,(-\Delta)^{s/2}u_{\varepsilon}(t)\,\overline{(-\Delta)^{s/2}\varphi}\,dx

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(c_{\varepsilon}-c)\,u_{\varepsilon}(t)\,\overline{\varphi}\,dx.

(4.11)

In particular,
G_{\varepsilon}(t)\in(H^{1}\cap H^{s})^{\prime}
for a.e.
t
.

Step 2: Energy estimate for
U_{\varepsilon}
in
L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{x}\cap L^{2}_{t}H^{1}_{x}
.

Taking
\varphi=U_{\varepsilon}(t)
in (
4.10
) and using the identities

\langle\partial_{t}U_{\varepsilon}(t),U_{\varepsilon}(t)\rangle=\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|U_{\varepsilon}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

B(U_{\varepsilon}(t),U_{\varepsilon}(t))=\|a^{1/2}\nabla U_{\varepsilon}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|b^{1/2}(-\Delta)^{s/2}U_{\varepsilon}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|c^{1/2}U_{\varepsilon}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

we obtain, for a.e.
t\in(0,T)
,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|U_{\varepsilon}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle+\|a^{1/2}\nabla U_{\varepsilon}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|b^{1/2}(-\Delta)^{s/2}U_{\varepsilon}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|c^{1/2}U_{\varepsilon}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\langle G_{\varepsilon}(t),U_{\varepsilon}(t)\rangle.

(4.12)

Using (
4.11
) with
\varphi=U_{\varepsilon}(t)
, Cauchy–Schwarz, and Young’s inequality,

\displaystyle|\langle G_{\varepsilon}(t),U_{\varepsilon}(t)\rangle|

5.
Conclusion

In this paper, we studied the Cauchy problem for a parabolic equation driven by a mixed local–nonlocal operator combining a uniformly elliptic diffusion term and a fractional diffusion term, together with a zeroth-order potential. First, under bounded measurable coefficients satisfying uniform positivity assumptions, we proved well-posedness in the classical weak sense and derived a priori energy estimates in the natural energy space.

Next, we extended the theory to spatially irregular (distributional) coefficients and initial data by adopting a Friedrichs-type regularisation approach. We introduced suitable notions of moderateness and negligibility for the regularising nets, established existence of very weak solution nets for admissible regularisations, and proved uniqueness up to negligible nets. Finally, we showed consistency with the classical weak theory: whenever the data are regular and the regularisations converge appropriately while preserving uniform positivity, the very weak solution converges to the unique weak solution.

These results provide a robust framework for treating mixed local–nonlocal diffusion models with highly singular spatial heterogeneities, and they form a basis for further developments, such as the analysis of more general source terms, nonlinearities, and numerical approximation schemes within the very weak setting.

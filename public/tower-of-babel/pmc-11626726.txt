Novel parallel inputs shift registers with set/reset terminals in QCA nanotechnology
Authors: Mohammad Gholami, Maryam Movahedi, Zaman Amirzadeh
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
This paper was focused on logic gates, D-latch, parallel-parallel shift-registers, and parallel-series shift registers, which are used as basic circuits in numerous circuits as well as computational and comparative units. To design proposed shift registers, D-latch which is the vital gate, is designed carefully for minimum size, decreasing number of cells and good performance for delay. The proposed level-sensitive parallel-in parallel-out (PIPO) shift registers with reset terminal and with both set and reset terminals (single-layer and multi-layer), edge-sensitive PIPO shift registers with reset and set/reset abilities (single-layer and multi-layer), and the parallel-in serial-out (PISO) shift registers were designed using the proposed D-latches. Simulations show that the proposed level-sensitive PIPO shift-register set and reset terminals has 145 QCA cells, 0.13 μm2 occupied area, and delay of about 1.25 cycles of QCA clock. In addition, the proposed edge-sensitive PIPO shift-register circuit with set-and-reset pins has 163 QCA cells, 0.17 μm2 occupied area, and delay of about 1.25 cycles of QCA clocks. All designs and simulation results were made in the QCADesigner software.

Introduction

The complementary metal oxide semiconductor (CMOS) is nowadays utilized to implement transistors in the very large-scale integration (VLSI) systems [1]. Reducing the dimensions of the transistor seems to be essential due to the remarkable speed in increasing the number of transistors within the chips. The leakage current and power loss may be known as the consequences due to reducing the size of transistors [2]. The quantum cellular automata (QCA) appear to be a proper alternative to the CMOS among the nano-scale technologies presented because of prominent features such as very high density and fast operating speed in the terahertz frequency range [3].

A shift register is a chain of latches with a common clock pulse. Designing basic circuits such as multiplexers and latches seems a prerequisite to design more complicated circuits in the digital field, for example, counters, linear feedback shift register (LFSRs), etc. [4]. Some designs have been introduced in the past for serial in-serial out shift registers (SISO) in the QCA technology, but each of these designs has had some disadvantages. For example, the design suggested in Ref. [4] has a large energy consumption rate besides having many cells and a large area. The design proposed in Ref. [5] has somehow succeeded to reduce the number of cells and the area; however, its power consumption rate is still high. Also, the proposed design in Ref. [6] has been much improved in comparison with other suggested designs since it had managed to reduce the number of cells and the area compared to the previous works; but still, this improvement has been achieved with using high area and high-power consumption rate. Moreover, these studies belong to the SISO shift registers. There are only a few papers which work on PISO or PIPO shift registers. In Ref. [7] a PIPO shift register is designed which has fewer number of used cells, but to use the outputs in more complex circuits more cells are needed. In addition, the set or reset terminals are not applied in this design. Therefore, adding set or reset terminals and using them in more complex circuits needs more cells. The mentioned works prove that we still need to design PIPO or PISO shift registers with set or reset terminals. Thus, a new design for PIPO and PISO shift registers in the QCA will be provided in this paper. The proposed design will have good performance in the number of cells, area occupation, delay, and energy consumption. Finally, the novelties of this paper can be categorized into the following sections:•A new PIPO shift register (both level and edge sensitive) in QCA technology with reset terminal is designed.•A new PIPO shift register (both level and edge sensitive) in QCA technology with both set/reset terminals is designed (single-layer and multilayer).•A new edge-sensitive PISO shift register in QCA technology is designed.

Basic concepts in the QCA

QCA technology is a newfound technology that appears to be the best alternative to CMOS technology [8]. The QCA cell consists of four dots or holes that are located next to each other as a square. These cells also have two additional electrons, which can move freely between these dots through embedded tunnels [9]. According to Coulomb's law, stable states are established when the electrons are arranged diagonally. Hence, two stable and general arrangements occur that are introduced as cell polarizations (P = -1 and P = +1). The logical values of zero and one are assigned to them respectively in calculations [10]. In QCA technology, a 45-degree rotated cell refers to a specific configuration of QCA cells where the cells are oriented at a 45-degree angle to the horizontal and vertical axes. This orientation is chosen strategically to optimize the performance of the QCA circuit in terms of signal propagation, energy consumption, and overall efficiency. By rotating the cells at a 45-degree angle, it is possible to achieve better alignment for signal transmission and manipulation within the QCA circuit. This orientation can help reduce signal delays, improve signal integrity, and enhance the overall functionality of the circuit. The 45-degree rotated cell configuration is commonly used in QCA design to overcome certain limitations and challenges associated with traditional horizontal or vertical cell orientations. It allows for more efficient information processing and communication within the QCA circuit, making it a valuable technique in advancing QCA technology. This structure is shown in Fig. (1b)

A wire is formed by placing the basic cells next to each other. The transmission of information within the circuit is done with the help of this wire. If the polarization of each of these cells is changed by another cell, as expected, this new polarization is transferred to the adjacent cells due to the repulsion between electrons [12]. The inverter or NOT gate is another gate used in logic circuits. This gate is employed to reverse the signal or polarization of a cell in QCA technology. Aimed at obtaining the inverse of the input signal in the output cell, we only need to place the cells diagonally above or below the desired cell [13]. The majority gate is also another basic and important gate in the QCA. The structure of this gate consists of three inputs and one output. Any value (binary value of zero or one) with the highest frequency (number) among the three inputs is chosen as the output. For example, if the binary value of input A is zero and the binary values of inputs B and C are one, the output would be the binary state of one [14]. The clocks control the data flow or the movement of electrons in the cell in the QCA circuits [16]. Synchronization is another task of the clock. In fact, in the controlling process, if the data reaches a part of the circuit that must be combined with several other inputs to generate the desired output, if other inputs reach that part of the circuit later, it prevents the dissemination of information in that part until the arrival of other inputs [17]. Hence, the existence of the clock creates synchronization in different parts of the circuit. Each clock includes 4 phases: Switch, Hold, Release, and Relax. Proposed PIPO and PISO shift registers

A parallel shift register using D-latches was designed to provide an efficient way to store and parallel load data in a digital circuit. Here are some reasons why this design choice was made:•Parallel Loading: D-latches in a parallel shift register allow for multiple bits of data to be loaded simultaneously into the register. This is advantageous when dealing with parallel data inputs, such as data from multiple sensors or inputs that need to be processed concurrently.•Data Retention: D-latches are level-sensitive devices that retain their output based on the input D signal at a specific clock edge. This ensures that the data stored in each stage of the shift register is stable and can be accessed reliably.•Synchronous Operation: By using D-latches in a shift register design, synchronous operation can be achieved, where the data is transferred from one stage to the next only when a clock signal is applied. This helps in controlling the timing and synchronization of data movement within the register.•Modularity and Scalability: Using D-latches allows for a modular design of the parallel shift register, where each stage can be easily replicated to increase the storage capacity. This scalability makes it adaptable to various data processing requirements.•Ease of Implementation: D-latches are relatively simple devices compared to other types of flip-flops, making them easier to implement in the circuit design. This simplicity can lead to lower power consumption and a more straightforward layout design.

The proposed level sensitive PIPO shift registers with reset terminal and set and reset terminals (single layer and multi-layer)

The D-latch is the main block of the proposed PIPO shift register circuit. In this paper, the appropriate D-latch in terms of the number of cells, delay, occupied area, and power consumption was needed to be initially designed aimed at designing the proposed circuit. The PIPO shift register circuit was then designed with a minimum number of cells and delay using this D-latch [19]. In this section we will talk about proposed level sensitive shift registers with reset terminal and also with set/reset terminals.

In digital circuits, the set and reset functions are commonly used in devices called flip-flops or latches. These functions allow for the initialization or changing of the state of the flip-flop.

Set (S): When the set input is activated, the flip-flop is forced into a specific state, often the '1′ state. This overrides any previous state and sets the output accordingly.

Reset (R): Conversely, when the reset input is triggered, the flip-flop is forced into the opposite state, typically '0'. This action also overrides the previous state.

These functionalities are essential in digital systems for tasks such as initializing a memory element, synchronizing signals, or creating sequential logic circuits. By controlling the state of the flip-flop using set and reset inputs, designers can ensure specific behaviors and functionalities in the circuit based on timing, logic conditions, or external inputs.

Due the importance of set and reset abilities, in this paper the proposed shift register will have these capabilities. The proposed PIPO shift register with the reset ability consists of a set of D-latches with a common clock pulse input (Fig. (6a)). The data is simultaneously applied to the input of the latches and the activation of the clock pulse input depends on the type of sensitivity of the D-latch. The reset input is shared in this circuit and the data is transferred from the input to the outputs in parallel. The block diagram of this circuit is illustrated in Fig. (6a). In this section we will talk about proposed edge-sensitive PIPO shift registers with reset terminals and also with set/reset terminals.

The D-latch has been used as the main block in the provided designs of the proposed shift registers. Flip-flops can be utilized for building an edge-sensitive shift register. Therefore, the final circuit will consist of three D-latches and three edge detection converters. The use of several edge detection converters for each D-latch increases the number of cells and dimensions in the ultimate design. Thus, only one edge-detection converter was used on the main clock input to prevent this problem. Then, the output signal enters from the converter to all the latches and finally goes to the output of the circuit. The design of the proposed PISO shift-register circuit in the QCA

The data can be stored in parallel in the flip-flops and received in serial from the output using the PISO shift register with the help of a control line (S) and a clock (CLK) input. The block diagram of the 3-bit PISO shift register is shown in Fig. (14). The input of each flip-flop is connected to the output of a 2:1 multiplexer in this design, and the output of each flip-flop is entered into the input of the multiplexer of the next layer. The control inputs of all multiplexers are connected and are denoted by the letter S. If the control inputs (S=1) are activated, the pins (In (0,1,2) are considered as the circuit inputs and the values within these pins are stored in parallel in the flip-flops as the clock pulse arrives. As the control input is disabled (S=0), the output of each flip-flop is connected to the input of the next layer from the zero-input path of the multiplexer, and the stored data is shifted to the next layer and received serially from the OUT output with each clock pulse. The S input can be used as the serial input of the circuit in the state (S=0).Fig. 14The proposed PISO shift-register block diagram.Fig. 14

The proposed PISO shift-register block diagram.

Conclusions

The core goal of this paper was to design circuits with the minimum cell number, lower delay, and lower size with high and proper efficiency in comparison with other available designs. To this end, D-latches and flip-flops were first provided as the main blocks with lower cells, dimensions, and delay than the existing designs. Then, different shift registers with different functions were designed using these presented blocks. Using optimized blocks and the path of wires using a 180-degree phase difference led the proposed circuits to perform at a suitable level in terms of dimensions, delay, and the number of cells. The desired designs can be used in the implementation of complex circuits such as DLL and universal shift registers.

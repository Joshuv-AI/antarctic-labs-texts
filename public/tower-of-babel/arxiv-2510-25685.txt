Covering large-dimensional Euclidean spaces by random translates of a given convex body
Boris Bukh, Jun Gao, Xizhi Liu, Oleg Pikhurko, Shumin Sun

Abstract
Determining the minimum density of a covering of ℝ n \mathbb{R}^{n} by Euclidean unit balls as n → ∞ n\to\infty is a major open problem, with the best known results being the lower bound of ( e − 3 / 2 + o ( 1 ) ) n \left(\mathrm{e}^{-3/2}+o(1)\right)n by Coxeter, Few and Rogers [ Mathematika 6 , 1959] and the upper bound of ( 1 / 2 + o ⁡ ( 1 ) ) ​ n ​ ln ⁡ n \left(1/2+o(1)\right)n\ln n by Dumer [ Discrete Comput. Geom. 38 , 2007]. We prove that there are ball coverings of ℝ n \mathbb{R}^{n} attaining the asymptotically best known density ( 1 / 2 + o ⁡ ( 1 ) ) ​ n ​ ln ⁡ n \left(1/2+o(1)\right)n\ln n such that, additionally, every point of ℝ n \mathbb{R}^{n} is covered at most ( 1.79556 ​ … + o ⁡ ( 1 ) ) ​ n ​ ln ⁡ n \left(1.79556...+o(1)\right)n\ln n times. This strengthens the result of Erdős and Rogers [ Acta Arith. 7 , 1961/62] who had the maximum multiplicity at most ( e + o ⁡ ( 1 ) ) ​ n ​ ln ⁡ n \left(\mathrm{e}+o(1)\right)n\ln n . On the other hand, we show that the method that was used for the best known ball coverings (when one takes a random subset of centres in a fundamental domain of a suitable lattice in ℝ n \mathbb{R}^{n} and extends this periodically) fails to work if the density is less than ( 1 / 2 + o ⁡ ( 1 ) ) ​ n ​ ln ⁡ n (1/2+o(1))n\ln n ; in fact, this result remains true if we replace the ball by any convex body K K . Also, we observe that a “worst” convex body K K here is a cube, for which the packing density coming from random constructions is only ( 1 + o ⁡ ( 1 ) ) ​ n ​ ln ⁡ n (1+o(1))n\ln n .



1
Introduction

The fundamental result of Rogers
[
14
]
states that, for every
convex body

K
(i.e. a compact convex set with non-empty interior) in the
n
-dimensional Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, there exists a covering of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
by translates of
K
with density at most
(1+o(1))n\ln n
as
n\to\infty
. If
K
is a ball then the covering density was improved to
(1/2+o(1))n\ln n
by Dumer
[
7
]
while the best known lower bound is
\left(\mathrm{e}^{-3/2}+o(1)\right)n
by Coxeter, Few and Rogers
[
5
]
.
Closing this gap for ball covering is a major open problem.

In brief, the new results proved in this paper are as follows. First, Theorem
1.1
proves that there are ball coverings of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
attaining the asymptotically best known density
\left(1/2+o(1)\right)\cdot n\ln n
such that, additionally, every point of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is covered at most
\left(1.79556...+o(1)\right)n\ln n
times. This strengthens a result of Erdős and Rogers
[
8
]
who had the maximum multiplicity at most
\left(\mathrm{e}+o(1)\right)n\ln n
. Also, Theorem
1.3
shows that, for any given convex body
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the method that was used in
[
14
,
7
]

(when one takes a random subset of centres in a fundamental domain of a suitable lattice in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and extends this periodically) fails with high probability to produce a
K
-covering if the appropriately defined density is strictly less than
(1/2+o(1))n\ln n
.
Finally, Theorem
1.5
gives that, somewhat surprisingly, a “worst” convex body
K
for this method is a cube, for which the packing density coming from random constructions is only
(1+o(1))n\ln n
(which matches the bound of Rogers
[
14
]
).

Let us provide the details. Let

B_{r}^{n}(\mathbf{x})\coloneqq\{\mathbf{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\colon\|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y}\|\leq r\}

denote the closed ball of radius
r
centred at
\mathbf{x}
, where
\|\cdot\|
denotes the Euclidean norm.
When
\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{0}
is the origin of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we write
B_{r}^{n}
for brevity. Let
\operatorname{vol}(\cdot)
denote the Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
Also, we define
\nu_{n}\coloneqq\operatorname{vol}(B_{1}^{n})
to be the volume of the
n
-dimensional unit ball
B_{1}^{n}
.

Let
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a convex body.
A (discrete) set
P\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
is called a
K
-covering
of a set
A\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
if
A
is a subset of

\displaystyle P+K\coloneqq\left\{\mathbf{p}+\mathbf{x}\colon\text{$\mathbf{p}\in P$ and $\mathbf{x}\in K$}\right\},

that is, the union of the translates of
K
by the elements of
P
contains every point of
A
.
For a measurable
A\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
of finite measure,
the
covering density
of a set
P\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
over
A
is defined as

\displaystyle\vartheta_{K}(P,A)\coloneqq\sum_{\mathbf{p}\in P}\frac{\operatorname{vol}\big(A\cap(K+\mathbf{p})\big)}{\operatorname{vol}(A)}.

The
covering multiplicity of a point

\mathbf{x}\in A
is defined as

\displaystyle\mu_{K}(P,\mathbf{x})\coloneqq\left|\left\{\mathbf{p}\in P\colon\mathbf{x}\in K+\mathbf{p}\right\}\right|.

The
covering multiplicity of

P
is then defined as

\displaystyle\mu_{K}(P,A)\coloneqq\sup\left\{\mu_{K}(P,\mathbf{x})\colon\mathbf{x}\in A\right\},

i.e., the maximum number of times that a point of
A
is covered by the translates of
K
by the elements of
P
. The
K
-covering density
and
K
-covering multiplicity
of
P
over
\mathbb{R}^{n}
are defined by

\displaystyle\vartheta_{K}(P)\coloneqq\limsup_{r\to\infty}\vartheta_{K}\big(P,[-r,r]^{n}\big)\quad\text{and}\quad\mu_{K}(P)\coloneqq\sup_{r\to\infty}\mu_{K}\big(P,[-r,r]^{n}\big).

In the case when
K
is the unit ball
B_{1}^{n}
, we may omit the subscript
K
and write just
\vartheta(P,A)
,
\mu(P,\mathbf{x})
,
\mu(P,A)
, etc.
Thus, for example,
Dumer’s result
[
7
]
gives a unit ball covering
P
of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
\vartheta(P)\leq\left(1/2+o(1)\right)n\ln n
. Our first result shows that, additionally, one can assume that the covering multiplicity of
P
is at most
(1.795...+o(1))n\ln n
, improving on the previous multiplicity bound
(\mathrm{e}+o(1))n\ln n
of Erdős and Rogers
[
8
]
, where
\mathrm{e}=2.718...
is the base of the natural logarithm.

Theorem 1.1
.

There exists a unit ball covering
P
of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that

\displaystyle\vartheta(P)\leq\left(\frac{1}{2}+o(1)\right)n\ln n\quad\text{and}\quad\mu(P)\leq\big(\xi+o(1)\big)n\ln n,

where
\xi=1.79556...
is the unique (positive) real root of

\displaystyle\ln\left(\frac{2x}{\mathrm{e}}\right)=\frac{1}{2x}.

(1)

Recall that a
lattice

\Lambda\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a set of the form

\displaystyle\Lambda=\left\{\lambda_{1}\mathbf{b}_{1}+\cdots+\lambda_{n}\mathbf{b}_{n}\colon\text{$\lambda_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}$ for $i\in[n]$}\right\},

for some linearly independent vectors
\mathbf{b}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{b}_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, where we use the shorthand
[n]\coloneqq\{1,\dots,n\}
.
For a convex body
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we call a lattice
\Lambda\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
a
packing lattice
of
K
if, for every distinct elements
\mathbf{p},\mathbf{p}^{\prime}\in P
, the translates
K+\mathbf{p}
and
K+\mathbf{p}^{\prime}
are disjoint.
The corresponding quotient
\mathbb{T}_{\Lambda}\coloneqq\mathbb{R}^{n}/\Lambda
(of the topological Abelian group
(\mathbb{R}^{n},+)
by the discrete subgroup
\Lambda
) is then called a
packing torus
of
K
. Slightly abusing notation, we use
\operatorname{vol}(\cdot)
also to denote the measure on
\mathbb{T}_{\Lambda}
obtained by identifying the torus with a fundamental domain of
\Lambda
and using the Lebesgue measure on the latter. Likewise, we may use the same symbol
A
to refer to the projection of
A\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
to
\mathbb{T}
, etc.

Recall that a discrete random variable
\mathbf{X}
has the
Poisson distribution with mean
\lambda>0
, denoted
\mathbf{X}\sim\mathrm{Pois}(\lambda)
, if for each integer
k\geq 0
the probability of
\mathbf{X}
assuming value
k
is
{\lambda^{k}\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda}}{k!}
.
A random set
\mathbf{X}\subseteq A
is said to be sampled from the
Poisson point process
on measurable subset
A\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
with intensity
\rho
if the following conditions hold:

(i)

For every bounded measurable set
S\subseteq A
, the number of points in
S
, that is,
|\mathbf{X}\cap S|
, has the Poisson distribution with mean
\rho\cdot\operatorname{vol}(S)
.

(ii)

For any disjoint bounded measurable sets
S_{1},\dots,S_{k}\subseteq A
, the random variables
|\mathbf{X}\cap S_{1}|,\dots,|\mathbf{X}\cap S_{k}|
are mutually independent.

Such a distribution is known to exist and to be uniquely determined by the above two properties.

As in many lower-bound constructions, the covering set
P\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
that we construct will be
of the form
\Lambda+X
, where
\Lambda\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a suitable lattice and
X
is a typical (under some distribution) finite subset of the corresponding torus
\mathbb{T}_{\Lambda}\coloneqq\mathbb{R}^{n}/\Lambda
. Specifically,
Theorem
1.1
will follow from the following result.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
0<\delta<1
be a real number and
M>0
be a constant. Suppose that
\mathbb{T}
is a packing torus of
B_{2}^{n}
with
\operatorname{vol}(\mathbb{T})\leq M^{n}\nu_{n}
.
Suppose that the set
\mathbf{X}
is sampled from the Poisson point process on
\mathbb{T}
with intensity

\displaystyle\rho\coloneqq\left(\frac{1}{2}+\delta\right)\frac{n\ln n}{\nu_{n}}.

(2)

Then the probability that all the following events occur tends to
1
as
n\to\infty
:

(i)

\mathbb{T}\subseteq\mathbf{X}+B_{1}^{n}
,

(ii)

\vartheta(\mathbf{X},\mathbb{T})\leq\left(\frac{1}{2}+2\delta\right)n\ln n
, and

(iii)

\mu(\mathbf{X})\leq\left(\xi+10\delta\right)n\ln n
, where
\xi=1.79556...
is the unique real root of (
1
).

The derivation of Theorem
1.1
from Theorem
1.2
is straightforward, given the known results on the existence of reasonably good packing lattices for balls, for which e.g. the classical Minkowski–Hlawka theorem suffices (and we refer the reader to Klartag
[
10
]
for the best known bounds in high dimensions). Also, note the result of Rogers
[
13
]
that, for any convex body
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
there exists a packing lattice
\Lambda
of
K
such that
\operatorname{vol}\big(\mathbb{T}_{\Lambda}\big)\leq 4^{n}\cdot\operatorname{vol}(K)
.

Our next result shows that the constant
1/2
in (
2
) cannot be improved using this approach, in fact not only for a ball but for any convex body.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\delta>0
be a real number.
Let
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a convex body and
\mathbb{T}
be a packing torus of
K-K:=\{\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y}\colon\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\in K\}
.
Suppose that
\mathbf{X}\subseteq\mathbb{T}
is sampled from the Poisson point process with intensity

\displaystyle\rho\coloneqq\frac{1}{\operatorname{vol}(K)}\left(\frac{n\ln n}{2}-(1+\delta)n\ln\ln n\right).

Then the probability that
\mathbb{T}\subseteq\mathbf{X}+K
tends to
0
as
n\to\infty
.

Note that, by Theorem
1.2
, the constant
1/2
in front of
n\ln n
in Theorem
1.3
is best possible when
K
is a unit ball and
\operatorname{vol}(\mathbb{T})\leq M^{n}\nu_{n}
, where
M
is some constant.

Remark 1.4
.

If we condition a Poisson point process on resulting in exactly
N
points then we get
N
independent uniform points in the domain. Thus, by monotonicity and concentration
(cf Claim
2.3
),
the conclusion of Theorem
1.3
also holds if
\mathbf{X}
is obtained by choosing
N
independent points, provided
N
is strictly less than
(1/2+o(1))\,(n\ln n)\cdot\operatorname{vol}(\mathbb{T})/\operatorname{vol}(K)
.

Recall that the covering result of Rogers
[
14
]
holds for all convex bodies.
A natural question is whether the Euclidean ball in Theorem
1.2
(i)
can be replaced by an arbitrary convex body.
The following result shows that this is not the case.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
\delta>0
be a real number.
Let
C_{n}\coloneqq[-1/2,1/2]^{n}
be the unit cube in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
(of volume
1
) and
\mathbb{T}
be a packing torus of
C_{n}-C_{n}
(which is equal to
2C_{n}
).
Suppose that
\mathbf{X}\subseteq\mathbb{T}
is sampled from the Poisson point process with intensity

\displaystyle\rho\coloneqq n\ln n-(1+\delta)n\ln\ln n.

Then the probability that
\mathbb{T}\subseteq\mathbf{X}+K
tends to
0
as
n\to\infty
.

Similarly as in Remark
1.4
, the conclusion of Theorem
1.5
also holds if we sample fewer than
(1+o(1))n\ln n\cdot\operatorname{vol}(\mathbb{T})
uniform points in
\mathbb{T}
. Also, the proof of Rogers
[
14
]
can be adapted to show the constant 1 in front of
n\ln n
in Theorem
1.5
is best possible. Thus, rather surprisingly, an
n
-dimensional cube is a “worst” convex body for covering by random translations.

1.2
.

3
, we give the proofs of Theorems
1.3
and

.

Proof of Theorem
1.2

In this section, we present the proof of Theorem
1.2
.

We will use the following standard estimate for the tail of the Poisson distribution, which can be found, for example, in
[
12
, Theorem 5.4]
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Suppose that
\mathbf{X}\sim\mathrm{Pois}(\lambda)
.
Then the following statements hold.

(i)

For every
\sigma>0
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\big[\,\mathbf{X}\geq(1+\sigma)\lambda\,\big]\leq\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda}\left(\frac{\mathrm{e}\lambda}{(1+\sigma)\lambda}\right)^{(1+\sigma)\lambda}=\left(\frac{\mathrm{e}^{\sigma}}{(1+\sigma)^{1+\sigma}}\right)^{\lambda}.

(ii)

For every
\sigma\in(0,1)
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\big[\,\mathbf{X}\leq(1-\sigma)\lambda\,\big]\leq\left(\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-\sigma}}{(1-\sigma)^{1-\sigma}}\right)^{\lambda}.

We will also use the following simple geometric result.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\delta>0
be a real number and let
n\to\infty
.
Suppose that
\mathbf{x}_{1},\mathbf{x}_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
are two points satisfying
\|\mathbf{x}_{1}-\mathbf{x}_{2}\|\leq rn^{-{1}/{2}-\delta}
. Then

\displaystyle\operatorname{vol}\big(B_{r}^{n}(\mathbf{x}_{1})\setminus B_{r}^{n}(\mathbf{x}_{2})\big)\leq\frac{1+o(1)}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\,n^{-\delta}\cdot\operatorname{vol}\big(B_{r}^{n}\big).

Proof of Lemma
2.2
.

Let
\mathbf{x}_{1},\mathbf{x}_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
be two points such that
\|\mathbf{x}_{1}-\mathbf{x}_{2}\|\leq rn^{-{1}/{2}-\delta}
.
Using the facts

\displaystyle\operatorname{vol}(B_{r}^{n})=\frac{\pi^{n/2}}{\Gamma\left(\frac{n}{2}+1\right)}\,r^{n}\quad\text{and}\quad\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{n}{2}+1\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{n-1}{2}+1\right)}=(1+o(1))\sqrt{\frac{n}{2}},

we obtain

\frac{\operatorname{vol}\big(B_{r}^{n-1}\big)}{\operatorname{vol}\big(B_{r}^{n}\big)}=\frac{1}{r\sqrt{\pi}}\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{n}{2}+1\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{n-1}{2}+1\right)}=\frac{1+o(1)}{r\sqrt{2\pi}}\sqrt{n}.

(3)

Let
\mathrm{conv}(X)
denote the convex hull of a set
X\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
. It follows from (
3
) that

\displaystyle\operatorname{vol}\big(B_{r}^{n}(\mathbf{x}_{1})\setminus B_{r}^{n}(\mathbf{x}_{2})\big)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{vol}\big(B_{r}^{n}(\mathbf{x}_{1})\cup B_{r}^{n}(\mathbf{x}_{2})\big)-\operatorname{vol}\big(B_{r}^{n}(\mathbf{x}_{2})\big)

\displaystyle\leq\operatorname{vol}\big(\mathrm{conv}\big(B_{r}^{n}(\mathbf{x}_{1})\cup B_{r}^{n}(\mathbf{x}_{2})\big)\big)-\operatorname{vol}\big(B_{r}^{n}(\mathbf{x}_{2})\big)

\displaystyle=\|\mathbf{x}_{1}-\mathbf{x}_{2}\|\cdot\operatorname{vol}\big(B_{r}^{n-1}\big)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{r}{n^{1/2+\delta}}\,\frac{1+o(1)}{r\sqrt{2\pi}}\,\sqrt{n}\cdot\operatorname{vol}\big(B_{r}^{n}\big)=\frac{1+o(1)}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\,n^{-\delta}\cdot\operatorname{vol}\big(B_{r}^{n}\big),

as desired.
∎

We are now ready to present the proof of Theorem
1.2
.
In this result as well as in
Theorems
1.3
and
1.5
, we work inside a packing torus
\mathbb{T}=\mathbb{R}^{n}/\Lambda
of
K-K
(where
K=B_{1}^{n}
in Theorem
1.2
).
Note that the projection
\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{T}
is injective on any translate
K+\mathbf{p}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Indeed, if we had
\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{p}+\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{k^{\prime}}+\mathbf{p}
for some
\mathbf{x}\in\Lambda
and distinct
\mathbf{k},\mathbf{k^{\prime}}\in K
then
K\cap(K+\mathbf{x})
would contain
\mathbf{k^{\prime}}=\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{x}
, a contradiction
to our assumption that
\Lambda
is a packing lattice for
K-K
(which contains a copy of
K
).
In particular, the measure of a subset of any translate of
K
is the same, regardless whether we compute it inside
\mathbb{R}^{n}
or inside
\mathbb{T}
, justifying our usage of the same notation
\operatorname{vol}(\cdot)
in both cases. Also, the distance between
\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\in\mathbb{T}
is defined as the distance between their preimage sets in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
(and still denoted by
\|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y}\|
). Note that the projection is also an isometry on each radius-1 ball in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. We will use these facts a number of times in the forthcoming proofs without explicitly mentioning them.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Fix
\delta>0
, which we may assume to be small.
Let
\beta=\beta(\delta)\in(0,1)
be a real number satisfying

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\mathrm{e}^{\beta-1}}{\beta^{\beta}}\right)^{1+\frac{\delta}{2}}\leq\mathrm{e}^{-1}.

(4)

Since
\lim_{x\to 0}\frac{\mathrm{e}^{x-1}}{x^{x}}=\mathrm{e}^{-1}
, such a real
\beta\in(0,1)
exists.

Let
n
be a sufficiently large integer.
Define

\displaystyle\varepsilon\coloneqq\frac{1}{n\ln n}\quad\text{and}\quad\mu\coloneqq n^{-\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\delta}{4}}.

Let
\mathbb{T}
be a packing torus of
B_{2}^{n}
with volume at most
M^{n}\nu_{n}
.
Let
P_{\varepsilon}\subseteq\mathbb{T}
and
P_{\mu}\subseteq\mathbb{T}
be a maximal
B_{\varepsilon/2}^{n}
-packing and a maximal
B_{\mu/2}^{n}
-packing of
\mathbb{T}
, respectively.
By maximality,
P_{\varepsilon}
forms a
B_{\varepsilon}
-covering of
\mathbb{T}
and
P_{\mu}
forms a
B_{\mu}
-covering of
\mathbb{T}
.
Also, we have the trivial upper bounds

|P_{\varepsilon}|\leq\frac{\operatorname{vol}(\mathbb{T})}{\operatorname{vol}\big(B_{\varepsilon/2}^{n}\big)}=\frac{M^{n}\nu_{n}}{(\varepsilon/2)^{n}\nu_{n}}=M^{n}\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right)^{-n}\quad\text{and}\quad|P_{\mu}|\leq M^{n}\left(\frac{\mu}{2}\right)^{-n}.

(5)

Fix a map
\phi\colon P_{\varepsilon}\to P_{\mu}
such that, for every
\mathbf{x}\in P_{\varepsilon}
,

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{x}-\phi(\mathbf{x})\|\leq\mu.

Note that such a map exists since
P_{\mu}
is a
B_{\mu}^{n}
-covering.

Let
\mathbf{X}
be a set sampled from the Poisson point process on
\mathbb{T}
with intensity

\displaystyle\rho\coloneqq\left(\frac{1}{2}+\delta\right)\frac{n\ln n}{\nu_{n}}.

(6)

Claim 2.3
.

The probability that
|\mathbf{X}|\leq(1+\delta)\rho\cdot\operatorname{vol}(\mathbb{T})
tends to
1
as
n\to\infty
.

Proof of Claim
2.3
.

Since
\mathbb{T}
is packing torus of
B_{2}^{n}
, we trivially have
\operatorname{vol}(\mathbb{T})\geq\operatorname{vol}(B_{2}^{n})=2^{n}\nu_{n}
.
The random variable
|\mathbf{X}|
has the Poisson distribution with mean
\rho\cdot\operatorname{vol}(\mathbb{T})\geq 2^{n-1}n\ln n
.
It follows from Lemma
2.1

(i)
and the crude inequality
\frac{\mathrm{e}^{x}}{(1+x)^{1+x}}\leq\mathrm{e}^{-x^{2}/10}
for
x\in[0,1]
that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left[\,|\mathbf{X}|\geq(1+\delta)\rho\cdot\operatorname{vol}(\mathbb{T})\,\right]\leq\left(\frac{\mathrm{e}^{\delta}}{(1+\delta)^{1+\delta}}\right)^{\rho\cdot\operatorname{vol}(\mathbb{T})}\leq\mathrm{exp}\left(-\frac{\delta^{2}}{10}\cdot 2^{n-1}n\ln n\right),

which goes to
0
as
n\to\infty
.
∎

For every point
\mathbf{y}\in\mathbb{T}
and
d\geq 0
, let

\displaystyle N_{d}(\mathbf{y})\coloneqq B_{d}^{n}(\mathbf{y})\cap\mathbf{X}=\left\{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbf{X}\colon\|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y}\|\leq d\right\}.

We say that a point
\mathbf{y}\in\mathbb{T}
is
saturated
if

\displaystyle|N_{1-\varepsilon}(\mathbf{y})|\geq\frac{\beta}{2}\,n\ln n.

Define the events
E_{1}
,
E_{2}
and
E_{3}
as follows:

•

E_{1}
: every point in
P_{\mu}
is saturated.

•

E_{2}
:
|N_{1+\varepsilon}(\mathbf{z})|\leq\left(\xi+10\delta\right)n\ln n
for every
\mathbf{z}\in P_{\varepsilon}
, where
\xi=1.79556...
is the unique real root of (
1
).

•

E_{3}
: for each
\mathbf{z}\in P_{\varepsilon}
, either
|N_{1-\varepsilon}(\mathbf{z})|>0
, or
\phi(\mathbf{z})
is not saturated.

Next, we show that each of these events holds with high probability.

Claim 2.4
.

We have
\min\left\{\,\mathbb{P}[E_{1}],\,\mathbb{P}[E_{2}],\,\mathbb{P}[E_{3}]\,\right\}\to 1
as
n\to\infty
.

Proof of Claim
2.4
.

First, we prove that
\mathbb{P}(E_{1})\to 1
.
Fix an arbitrary point
\mathbf{y}\in P_{\mu}
.
The random variable
|N_{1-\varepsilon}(\mathbf{y})|
has the Poisson distribution with mean

\displaystyle\rho\cdot\operatorname{vol}(B_{1-\varepsilon}^{n})=\left(\frac{1}{2}+\delta\right)\frac{n\ln n}{\nu_{n}}\cdot\operatorname{vol}(B_{1-\varepsilon}^{n})=\left(\frac{1}{2}+\delta\right)(1-\varepsilon)^{n}n\ln n.

Recall that
\varepsilon=(n\ln n)^{-1}
. Since
0<\delta<1
is fixed while
n
is sufficiently large, it holds that

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{2}+\delta\right)(1-\varepsilon)^{n}\geq\frac{1}{2}\quad\text{and}\quad\frac{2}{2+\delta}\left(\frac{1}{2}+\delta\right)(1-\varepsilon)^{n}\geq\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\delta}{3}.

Applying Lemma
2.1

(ii)
with
\sigma=1-\beta
, and using (
4
), we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left[\,|N_{1-\varepsilon}(\mathbf{y})|\leq\frac{\beta}{2}\,n\ln n\,\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}\left[\,|N_{1-\varepsilon}(\mathbf{y})|\leq\left(1-(1-\beta)\right)\left(\frac{1}{2}+\delta\right)(1-\varepsilon)^{n}n\ln n\,\right]

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{\mathrm{e}^{\beta-1}}{\beta^{\beta}}\right)^{\left(\frac{1}{2}+\delta\right)(1-\varepsilon)^{n}n\ln n}

\displaystyle\leq\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{2}{2+\delta}\cdot\left(\frac{1}{2}+\delta\right)(1-\varepsilon)^{n}n\ln n}\leq\mathrm{e}^{-\left(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\delta}{3}\right)n\ln n}=n^{-\left(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\delta}{3}\right)n}.

Combining this with (
5
), we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}[E_{1}]

\displaystyle\geq 1-\sum_{\mathbf{y}\in P_{\mu}}\mathbb{P}\left[\,|N_{1-\varepsilon}(\mathbf{y})|\leq\frac{\beta}{2}\,n\ln n\,\right]

\displaystyle\geq 1-M^{n}\left(\frac{\mu}{2}\right)^{-n}n^{-\left(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\delta}{3}\right)n}=1-\left(\frac{n^{\delta/12}}{2M}\right)^{-n}\to 1\quad\text{as}\quad n\to\infty,

as desired.

We now consider
E_{2}
.
Fix an arbitrary point
\mathbf{z}\in P_{\varepsilon}
.
As in the preceding proof, the random variable
|N_{1+\varepsilon}(\mathbf{z})|
has the Poisson distribution with mean

\displaystyle\rho\cdot\operatorname{vol}(B_{1+\varepsilon}^{n})=\left(\frac{1}{2}+\delta\right)\frac{n\ln n}{\nu_{n}}\cdot\operatorname{vol}(B_{1+\varepsilon}^{n})=\left(\frac{1}{2}+\delta\right)(1+\varepsilon)^{n}n\ln n.

Let
\xi_{0}\coloneqq 2\xi-1=2.59112...\,
.
Since
\xi
is the root of (
1
), a straightforward calculation shows that
\xi_{0}
is the unique real root of

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{e}^{x}}{(1+x)^{1+x}}=\frac{1}{\mathrm{e}^{2}}.

(7)

Also, we have

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{2}+\delta\right)(1+\varepsilon)^{n}\leq\frac{1}{2}+2\delta.

Since
\xi=1.79...
is at most
5/2
, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\xi+10\delta}{(1/2+\delta)(1+\varepsilon)^{n}}-(1+\xi_{0})\geq\frac{\xi+10\delta}{1/2+2\delta}-2\xi=\frac{4(5-2\xi)\delta}{1+4\delta}\geq 0.

Now applying Lemma
2.1

(i)
with
\sigma=\xi_{0}
, and using (
7
), we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left[\,|N_{1+\varepsilon}(\mathbf{z})|\geq(\xi+10\delta)n\ln n\,\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}\left[\,|N_{1+\varepsilon}(\mathbf{z})|\geq(1+\xi_{0})\left(\frac{1}{2}+\delta\right)(1+\varepsilon)^{n}n\ln n\,\right]

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{\mathrm{e}^{\xi_{0}}}{(1+\xi_{0})^{1+\xi_{0}}}\right)^{\left(\frac{1}{2}+\delta\right)(1+\varepsilon)^{n}n\ln n}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{e}^{-2\left(\frac{1}{2}+\delta\right)(1+\varepsilon)^{n}n\ln n}\leq\mathrm{e}^{-(1+2\delta)n\ln n}=n^{-(1+2\delta)n}.

Combining it with (
5
), we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}[E_{2}]

\displaystyle\geq 1-\sum_{\mathbf{z}\in P_{\varepsilon}}\mathbb{P}\left[\,|N_{1+\varepsilon}(\mathbf{z})|\geq(\xi+10\delta)n\ln n\,\right]

\displaystyle\geq 1-M^{n}\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right)^{-n}n^{-(1+2\delta)n}

\displaystyle=1-\left(\frac{n^{1+2\delta}}{2Mn\ln n}\right)^{-n}=1-\left(\frac{n^{2\delta}}{2M\ln n}\right)^{-n}\to 1\quad\text{as}\quad n\to\infty,

as desired.

Finally, we consider
E_{3}
.
For a point
\mathbf{z}\in P_{\varepsilon}
, let
B_{\mathbf{z}}
denote the (bad) event that

\displaystyle\text{$|N_{1-\varepsilon}(\mathbf{z})|=0$ \quad and\quad$\phi(\mathbf{z})\in P_{\mu}$ is saturated}.

Next, we analyze the probability that
B_{\mathbf{z}}
occurs.

Let
S\coloneqq B_{1-\varepsilon}^{n}\big(\phi(\mathbf{z})\big)\setminus B_{1-\varepsilon}^{n}(\mathbf{z})
.
By the definition of
\phi
, we have

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{z}-\phi(\mathbf{z})\|\leq\mu=n^{-\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\delta}{4}}\leq(1-\varepsilon)n^{-\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\delta}{8}}.

So it follows from Lemma
2.2
that

\displaystyle\operatorname{vol}(S)\leq\frac{1+o(1)}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\,n^{-\delta/8}\cdot\operatorname{vol}\big(B_{1-\varepsilon}^{n}\big)\leq n^{-\delta/8}\nu_{n}.

(8)

Since the random variable
|S\cap\mathbf{X}|
has the Poisson distribution with mean

\displaystyle\rho\cdot\operatorname{vol}(S)=\left(\frac{1}{2}+\delta\right)\frac{n\ln n}{\nu_{n}}\,\operatorname{vol}(S)\leq\left(\frac{1}{2}+\delta\right)n^{1-\frac{\delta}{8}}\ln n\ll\frac{\beta}{2}\,n\ln n,

it follows from Lemma
2.1

(i)
and (
6
) that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left[\,|S\cap\mathbf{X}|\geq\frac{\beta}{2}\,n\ln n\,\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathrm{e}^{-\rho\cdot\operatorname{vol}(S)}\left(\frac{\mathrm{e}\rho\cdot\operatorname{vol}(S)}{(\beta/2)n\ln n}\right)^{\frac{\beta}{2}\,n\ln n}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{\beta n\ln n}{2\mathrm{e}\rho\cdot\operatorname{vol}(S)}\right)^{-\frac{\beta}{2}\,n\ln n}\leq\left(\frac{\beta\nu_{n}}{2\mathrm{e}\cdot\operatorname{vol}(S)}\right)^{-\frac{\beta}{2}\,n\ln n}.

Since
\beta=\beta(\delta)>0
while
n
is sufficiently large, we have that

\displaystyle\frac{\beta}{2\mathrm{e}}\geq n^{-\frac{\delta}{40}}.

Combining this with (
8
), we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left[\,|S\cap\mathbf{X}|\geq\frac{\beta}{2}\,n\ln n\,\right]\leq\left(n^{-\frac{\delta}{40}}\frac{\nu_{n}}{n^{-\delta/8}\nu_{n}}\right)^{-\frac{\beta}{2}\,n\ln n}=n^{-\frac{\beta\delta}{20}\,n\ln n}.

By definition,
B_{\mathbf{z}}
occurs if and only if

\displaystyle|N_{1-\varepsilon}(\mathbf{z})|=0\quad\text{and}\quad|N_{1-\varepsilon}(\phi(\mathbf{z}))|\geq\frac{\beta}{2}\,n\ln n,

which implies that all points in the set
N_{1-\varepsilon}(\phi(\mathbf{z}))
lie within the region
S
.
Therefore, for any given
\mathbf{z}\in P_{\varepsilon}
, it holds that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}[B_{\mathbf{z}}]\leq\mathbb{P}\left[\,|S\cap\mathbf{X}|\geq\frac{\beta}{2}n\ln n\,\right]\leq n^{-\frac{\beta\delta}{20}n\ln n}.

Combining it with (
5
), we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}[E_{3}]\geq 1-\sum_{\mathbf{z}\in P_{\varepsilon}}\mathbb{P}[B_{\mathbf{z}}]

\displaystyle\geq 1-M^{n}\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right)^{-n}n^{-\frac{\beta\delta}{20}\,n\ln n}

\displaystyle=1-\mathrm{exp}\left(n\ln(2M\,n\ln n)-\frac{\beta\delta}{20}n\ln^{2}n\right)\to 1\quad\text{as}\quad n\to\infty.

This completes the proof of Claim
2.4
.
∎

Thus there is an outcome
\mathbf{X}
of size at most
(1+\delta)\rho\cdot\operatorname{vol}(\mathbb{T})
for which all of the events
E_{1}
,
E_{2}
, and
E_{3}
occur. Fix any such
\mathbf{X}
.

Claim 2.5
.

The set
\mathbf{X}
is a unit ball covering of
\mathbb{T}
. Moreover,

\displaystyle\vartheta(\mathbf{X})\leq\left(\frac{1}{2}+2\delta\right)n\ln n\quad\text{and}\quad\mu(\mathbf{X})\leq\left(\xi+10\delta\right)n\ln n.

Proof of Claim
2.5
.

First, we show that
\mathbf{X}
is a unit ball covering of
\mathbb{T}
.
Fix an arbitrary point
\mathbf{w}\in\mathbb{T}
.
Since
P_{\varepsilon}
is a
B_{\varepsilon}^{n}
-covering of
\mathbb{T}
, there exists a point
\mathbf{z}\in P_{\varepsilon}
such that
\|\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{z}\|\leq\varepsilon
.
The fact that the event
E_{1}
holds gives that the point
\phi(\mathbf{z})\in P_{\mu}
is saturated.
Combining it with the fact that the event
E_{3}
holds, we conclude that there exists a point
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbf{X}
such that
\|\mathbf{z}-\mathbf{x}\|\leq 1-\varepsilon
.
Therefore,

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{x}\|\leq\|\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{z}\|+\|\mathbf{z}-\mathbf{x}\|\leq 1,

which shows that
\mathbf{X}
is a unit ball covering of
\mathbb{T}
.

By definition, the covering density of
\mathbf{X}
is

\displaystyle\vartheta(\mathbf{X})=\sum_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbf{X}}\frac{\operatorname{vol}\left(B_{1}^{n}(\mathbf{x})\cap\mathbb{T}\right)}{\operatorname{vol}(\mathbb{T})}=|\mathbf{X}|\,\frac{\nu_{n}}{\operatorname{vol}(\mathbb{T})}

\displaystyle\leq(1+\delta)\left(\frac{1}{2}+\delta\right)\frac{n\ln n}{\nu_{n}}\operatorname{vol}(\mathbb{T})\,\frac{\nu_{n}}{\operatorname{vol}(\mathbb{T})}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{1}{2}+2\delta\right)n\ln n,

as desired.

Now suppose to the contrary that there exists a point
\mathbf{u}\in\mathbb{T}
such that

\displaystyle\mu(\mathbf{X},\mathbf{u})\geq(\xi+10\delta)n\ln n.

Since
P_{\varepsilon}
is a
B_{\varepsilon}^{n}
covering of
\mathbb{T}
, there exists a point
\mathbf{z}\in P_{\varepsilon}
such that
\|\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{z}\|\leq\varepsilon
.
Then

\displaystyle|N_{1+\varepsilon}(\mathbf{z})|\geq\mu(\mathbf{X},\mathbf{u})\geq(\xi+10\delta)n\ln n,

which contradicts the fact that the event
E_{2}
occurs.
∎

Claim
2.5
completes the proof of Theorem
1.2
.
∎

3
Proofs of Theorems
1.3
and
1.5

In this section, we present the proof of Theorems
1.3
and
1.5
.

A convex body
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
is said to be
isotropic
if
\int_{K}\mathbf{x}\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}=0
,
\operatorname{vol}(K)=1
, and there exists a constant
L_{K}
, called the
isotropic constant
of
K
, such that for every
\mathbf{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\displaystyle\int_{K}\langle\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\rangle^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}=L_{K}^{2}\|\mathbf{y}\|^{2}.

(9)

It was a long-standing open problem to determine whether
L_{K}
can be bounded by an absolute constant, independent of the dimension
n
.
Culminating decades of work by many researchers, the question was recently resolved affirmatively by Guan, Klartag and Lehec
[
9
,
11
]
.

Theorem 3.1

(Guan
[
9
]
, Klartag and Lehec
[
11
]
)
.

There exists an absolute constant
C
such that the following holds for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
Suppose that
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
is an isotropic convex body. Then the isotropic constant
L_{K}
of
K
satisfies
L_{K}\leq C
.

Since every convex body can be made isotropic via a suitable linear transformation (see e.g.
[
2
, Solution to Exercise 9.17]
), it suffices to prove Theorem
1.3
for isotropic convex bodies.
The following lemma will be used in the proof.

Lemma 3.2
.

Suppose that
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
is an isotropic convex body and
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a vector satisfying
\|\mathbf{x}\|\geq 4L_{K}
.
Then

\displaystyle\operatorname{vol}\left(K\cap(K+\mathbf{x})\right)\leq 3^{-\frac{\|\mathbf{x}\|}{8L_{K}}}.

Proof of Lemma
3.2
.

Fix a vector
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
satisfying
\|\mathbf{x}\|\geq 4L_{K}
.
For every real number
w\in\mathbb{R}
, define the
slab of width
w
in the direction of
\mathbf{x}
as

\displaystyle S_{\mathbf{x}}(w)\coloneqq\left\{\mathbf{y}\colon|\langle\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\rangle|<w\,\|\mathbf{x}\|\right\}.

Markov’s inequality applied to the random variable
\langle\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\rangle^{2}
, where
\mathbf{y}
is a uniform element of
K
, implies by (
9
) that

\displaystyle\operatorname{vol}\left(K\setminus S_{\mathbf{x}}(2L_{K})\right)\leq\frac{1}{4}.

(10)

A well-known argument due to Borell
[
3
, Lemma 3.1]
gives that

\displaystyle\operatorname{vol}\left(K\setminus S_{\mathbf{x}}(2tL_{K})\right)\leq\frac{\sqrt{3}}{4}\cdot 3^{-\frac{t}{2}},\quad\text{for every}\quad t\geq 1.

(11)

For completeness, let us sketch a proof of (
11
). The uniform probability measure
\mu_{K}
on
K
is clearly
logarithmically concave
, meaning that the function
-\log(\mathrm{d}\mu_{K}/\mathrm{d}\mu)
(which, in this case, is
0
on
K
and
\infty
elsewhere) is convex. By a theorem of Borell
[
4
, Theorem 3.2]
,
\mu_{K}
satisfies the Multiplicative Brunn-Minkowski Inequality. Next, it is routine to check that, for any convex centrally symmetric
A
, it holds that

\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus A\supseteq\frac{2}{t+1}\,(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus tA)+\frac{t-1}{t+1}\,A.

Evaluating
\mu_{K}
on this inclusion for the slab
A\coloneqq S_{\mathbf{x}}(2L_{K})
, we obtain that

\operatorname{vol}(K\setminus A)\geq\mu_{K}\left(\frac{2}{t+1}\,(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus tA)+\frac{t-1}{t+1}\,A\right)\geq\operatorname{vol}(K\setminus tA)^{\frac{2}{t+1}}\cdot\operatorname{vol}(K\cap A)^{\frac{t-1}{t+1}},

which implies (
11
) by (
10
).

In particular, by applying (
11
) with
t\coloneqq{\|\mathbf{x}\|}/(4L_{K})
and denoting
S\coloneqq S_{\mathbf{x}}\left(\|\mathbf{x}\|/2\right)
for brevity, we obtain

\displaystyle\operatorname{vol}\left(K\setminus S\right)\leq\frac{\sqrt{3}}{4}\cdot 3^{-\frac{\|\mathbf{x}\|}{8L_{K}}}.

Claim 3.3
.

We have that
S\cap(S+\mathbf{x})=\emptyset
.

Proof of Claim
3.3
.

Suppose to the contrary that there exists
\mathbf{y}\in S\cap(S+\mathbf{x})
.
Then
\mathbf{y}\in S
and
\mathbf{y}-\mathbf{x}\in S
.
It follows from the definition of
S
that

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{x}\|^{2}=|\langle\mathbf{x},\mathbf{x}\rangle|=|\langle\mathbf{y},\mathbf{x}\rangle-\langle\mathbf{y}-\mathbf{x},\mathbf{x}\rangle|\leq|\langle\mathbf{y},\mathbf{x}\rangle|+|\langle\mathbf{y}-\mathbf{x},\mathbf{x}\rangle|<\frac{\|\mathbf{x}\|^{2}}{2}+\frac{\|\mathbf{x}\|^{2}}{2}=\|\mathbf{x}\|^{2},

a contradiction.
∎

Let
\overline{S}\coloneqq K\setminus S
.
It follows from Claim
3.3
that

\displaystyle K\cap(K+\mathbf{x})\subseteq\overline{S}\cup(\overline{S}+\mathbf{x}).

Therefore,

\displaystyle\operatorname{vol}\left(K\cap(K+\mathbf{x})\right)\leq\operatorname{vol}(\overline{S})+\operatorname{vol}(\overline{S}+\mathbf{x})=2\cdot\operatorname{vol}(K\setminus S)\leq 2\cdot\frac{\sqrt{3}}{4}\cdot 3^{-\frac{\|\mathbf{x}\|}{8L_{K}}}\leq 3^{-\frac{\|\mathbf{x}\|}{8L_{K}}},

which proves Lemma
3.2
.
∎

We will also use the following standard result from probability, which can be found in e.g.
[
1
, Theorem 4.3.1]
.

Lemma 3.4
.

Suppose that
X
is a nonnegative, integral-valued random variable.
Then

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}[\,X=0\,]\leq\frac{\mathrm{Var}[X]}{\mathbb{E}[X]^{2}},

where
\mathrm{Var}[X]
and
\mathbb{E}[X]
denote respectively the variance and the expectation of
X
.

Before proving Theorem
1.3
, we first establish the following result.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
n\to\infty
.
Let
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be an isotropic convex body and let

\mathbb{T}
be a packing torus of
K-K
. Suppose that
P\subseteq\mathbb{T}
is a
B_{1/2}^{n}
-packing
and
\omega(n)\to\infty
is a function such that

\displaystyle\rho\coloneqq\ln|P|-n\big(\ln\ln\ln|P|+\omega(n)\big)

tends to infinity. Let
\mathbf{X}\subseteq\mathbb{T}
be a set sampled from the Poisson point process on
\mathbb{T}
with intensity
\rho
.
Then
\mathbb{P}\left[\,P\subseteq K+\mathbf{X}\,\right]
tends to
0
.

Proof of Theorem
3.5
.

For every point
\mathbf{p}\in P
, let
B_{\mathbf{p}}
be the characteristic function of the event that
\mathbf{p}\notin\mathbf{X}+K
, that is,

\displaystyle B_{\mathbf{p}}=\begin{cases}1,&\quad\text{if}\quad\mathbf{p}\notin\mathbf{X}+K,\\
0,&\quad\text{if}\quad\mathbf{p}\in\mathbf{X}+K.\end{cases}

Since
\operatorname{vol}(K)=1
(by the definition of isotropic), the random variable
|\mathbf{X}\cap(\mathbf{p}-K)|
has the Poisson distribution with mean
\rho\cdot\operatorname{vol}(\mathbf{p}-K)=\rho
.
Therefore,
\mathbb{E}[B_{\mathbf{p}}]=\mathrm{e}^{-\rho}
.
It follows that the sum
B\coloneqq\sum_{\mathbf{p}\in P}B_{\mathbf{p}}
satisfies

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[B]=|P|\,\mathrm{e}^{-\rho}=\mathrm{e}^{n\left(\ln\ln\ln|P|+\omega(n)\right)}\to\infty\quad\text{as}\quad n\to\infty.

(12)

Note that
B
is the number of points in
P
that are not covered by
\mathbf{X}+K
.
We will use Lemma
3.4
(that is, the second moment method) to show that
\mathbb{P}[\,B=0\,]\to 0
as
n\to\infty
.

Let
\Delta\coloneqq 8L_{K}(f_{n}+\log_{3}\rho)
, where
f_{n}\to\infty
very slowly (as
n\to\infty
).
Let

\displaystyle\Sigma_{1}\coloneqq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{p},\mathbf{p}^{\prime}\in P\\
\|\mathbf{p}-\mathbf{p}^{\prime}\|\leq\Delta\end{subarray}}\mathbb{E}[B_{\mathbf{p}}B_{\mathbf{p}^{\prime}}]\quad\text{and}\quad\Sigma_{2}\coloneqq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{p},\mathbf{p}^{\prime}\in P\\
\|\mathbf{p}-\mathbf{p}^{\prime}\|>\Delta\end{subarray}}\left(\mathbb{E}[B_{\mathbf{p}}B_{\mathbf{p}^{\prime}}]-\mathbb{E}[B_{\mathbf{p}}]\,\mathbb{E}[B_{\mathbf{p}^{\prime}}]\right).

Since
P
is a
B_{1/2}^{n}
-packing of
\mathbb{T}
, for every point
\mathbf{p}\in P
the number of points in
P
satisfying
\|\mathbf{p}-\mathbf{p}^{\prime}\|\leq\Delta
is at most

\displaystyle\frac{\operatorname{vol}\big(B_{\Delta+1/2}^{n}(\mathbf{p})\big)}{\operatorname{vol}\big(B_{1/2}^{n}\big)}=\left(\frac{\Delta+1/2}{1/2}\right)^{n}\leq\left(4\Delta\right)^{n}.

Notice that

\displaystyle\ln

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\left(32L_{K}(f_{n}+\log_{3}\rho)\right)^{n}}{|P|\mathrm{e}^{-\rho}}\right)

\displaystyle=n\ln\left(32L_{K}(f_{n}+\log_{3}\rho)\right)-n\big(\ln\ln\ln|P|+\omega(n)\big)

\displaystyle=n\ln\left(f_{n}+\frac{\ln\rho}{\ln 3}\right)+n\ln(32L_{K})-n\big(\ln\ln\ln|P|+\omega(n)\big)

\displaystyle\leq n\ln\ln\rho+n\ln(32L_{K})-n\big(\ln\ln\ln|P|+\omega(n)\big)\leq n\big(\ln(32L_{K})-\omega(n)\big)

which, by Theorem
3.1
, tends to
-\infty
as
n\to\infty
.
Therefore, by (
12
),

\displaystyle\Sigma_{1}\leq\sum_{\mathbf{p}\in P}\mathbb{E}[B_{\mathbf{p}}]\left(4\Delta\right)^{n}=\mathbb{E}[B]\left(4\Delta\right)^{n}=\mathbb{E}[B]^{2}\frac{\left(32L_{K}(f_{n}+\log_{3}\rho)\right)^{n}}{|P|\,\mathrm{e}^{-\rho}}=o\left(\mathbb{E}[B]^{2}\right).

(13)

Next, we consider
\Sigma_{2}
.
Fix two points
\mathbf{p},\mathbf{p}^{\prime}\in P
at distance at least
\Delta
on the torus
\mathbb{T}
.
Note that
B_{\mathbf{p}}B_{\mathbf{p}^{\prime}}
is 1 if and only if
\mathbf{X}
does not intersect
(\mathbf{p}-K)\cup(\mathbf{p}^{\prime}-K)
.

Hence, the number of points of
\mathbf{X}
in this set has the Poisson distribution with mean

\displaystyle\rho\cdot\operatorname{vol}\left((\mathbf{p}-K)\cup(\mathbf{p}^{\prime}-K)\right)

\displaystyle=\rho\cdot\operatorname{vol}\left((K-\mathbf{p})\cup(K-\mathbf{p}^{\prime})\right)

\displaystyle=\rho\left(\operatorname{vol}(K-\mathbf{p})+\operatorname{vol}(K-\mathbf{p}^{\prime})-\operatorname{vol}\left((K-\mathbf{p})\cap(K-\mathbf{p}^{\prime})\right)\right)

\displaystyle=2\rho-\rho\cdot\operatorname{vol}\left(K\cap(K+\mathbf{p}-\mathbf{p}^{\prime})\right).

Thus we have that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[B_{\mathbf{p}}B_{\mathbf{p}^{\prime}}]

\displaystyle=\mathrm{exp}\left(-\rho\cdot\operatorname{vol}\left((\mathbf{p}-K)\cup(\mathbf{p}^{\prime}-K)\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{exp}\left(-2\rho+\rho\cdot\operatorname{vol}\left(K\cap(K+\mathbf{p}-\mathbf{p}^{\prime})\right)\right).

(14)

Observe that there is at most one vector
\mathbf{q}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
that projects to
\mathbf{p}-\mathbf{p}^{\prime}
with
K\cap(K+\mathbf{q})\not=\emptyset
. Indeed, if
\mathbf{q}\not=\mathbf{q}^{\prime}
contradict this, satisfying
\mathbf{a}=\mathbf{b}+\mathbf{q}
and
\mathbf{a}^{\prime}=\mathbf{b}^{\prime}+\mathbf{q}^{\prime}
for some
\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b},\mathbf{a}^{\prime},\mathbf{b}^{\prime}
in
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}

then

(\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{b})-(\mathbf{a}^{\prime}-\mathbf{b}^{\prime})=\mathbf{q}-\mathbf{q}^{\prime}\in\Lambda\setminus\{\mathbf{0}\},

a contradiction to our assumption that
\Lambda
is a packing lattice for
K-K
. Furthermore, for every
\mathbf{q}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
that projects to
\mathbf{p}-\mathbf{p}^{\prime}
, it holds that
\|\mathbf{q}\|\geq\Delta\geq 4L_{K}
.
Thus the upper bound of Lemma
3.2
applies also inside the torus and gives that

\displaystyle\operatorname{vol}\left(K\cap(K+\mathbf{p}-\mathbf{p}^{\prime})\right)\leq 3^{-\frac{\|\mathbf{p}-\mathbf{p}^{\prime}\|}{8L_{K}}}\leq 3^{-\frac{\Delta}{8L_{K}}}=3^{-f_{n}-\log_{3}\rho}=\rho^{-1}3^{-f_{n}}.

Combining it with (
3
), we obtain

\displaystyle\Sigma_{2}

\displaystyle\leq|P|^{2}\left(\mathrm{exp}\left(-2\rho+3^{-f_{n}}\right)-\mathrm{exp}(-2\rho)\right)

\displaystyle=|P|^{2}\mathrm{e}^{-2\rho}\left(\mathrm{exp}\left(3^{-f_{n}}\right)-1\right)=\mathbb{E}[B]^{2}\left(\mathrm{exp}\left(3^{-f_{n}}\right)-1\right)=o\left(\mathbb{E}[B]^{2}\right).

(15)

Combining (
13
), (
3
), and Lemma
3.4
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}[\,B=0\,]\leq\frac{\mathrm{Var}[B]}{\mathbb{E}[B]^{2}}=\frac{1}{\mathbb{E}[B]^{2}}\sum_{\mathbf{p},\mathbf{p}^{\prime}\in P}\left(\mathbb{E}[B_{\mathbf{p}}B_{\mathbf{p}^{\prime}}]-\mathbb{E}[B_{\mathbf{p}}]\,\mathbb{E}[B_{\mathbf{p}^{\prime}}]\right)\leq\frac{\Sigma_{1}+\Sigma_{2}}{\mathbb{E}[B]^{2}}=o\left(1\right).

This completes the proof of Theorem
3.5
.
∎

We are now ready to present the proof of Theorem
1.3
.
We will use the following estimate, which follows from the fact that
\Gamma(x+1)=(1+o(1))\sqrt{2\pi x}\left(x/\mathrm{e}\right)^{x}
as
x\to\infty
(see e.g.
[
6
]
):

\displaystyle\nu_{n}=\frac{\pi^{\frac{n}{2}}}{\Gamma\left(\frac{n}{2}+1\right)}=\frac{\pi^{\frac{n}{2}}}{(1+o(1))\sqrt{\pi n}\left(\frac{n}{2\mathrm{e}}\right)^{\frac{n}{2}}}=(1+o(1))\frac{(2\pi\mathrm{e})^{\frac{n}{2}}}{\sqrt{\pi n}}n^{-\frac{n}{2}}.

(16)

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

By applying an affine transformation to
K
(and to the lattice defining the torus
\mathbb{T}
), we can assume that
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
is an isotropic body. In particular,
\operatorname{vol}(K)=1
by definition. By monotonicity, it suffices to prove Theorem
1.3
for some
\rho\geq n\ln n/2-(1+o(1))n\ln\ln n
. Let
\mathbb{T}
be a packing torus of
K-K
, and let
P
be a maximal
B_{1/2}^{n}
-packing of
\mathbb{T}
.
By (
16
), we trivially obtain the upper bound

\displaystyle\ln|P|\leq\ln\left(\frac{\operatorname{vol}(\mathbb{T})}{\operatorname{vol}(B_{1/2}^{n})}\right)

\displaystyle=\ln\left(\frac{\operatorname{vol}(\mathbb{T})}{(1/2)^{n}\nu_{n}}\right)

\displaystyle=\ln\left((1+o(1))\frac{2^{n}\cdot\operatorname{vol}(\mathbb{T})\sqrt{\pi n}}{(2\pi\mathrm{e})^{\frac{n}{2}}}n^{\frac{n}{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{n}{2}\ln n+\ln\left(\operatorname{vol}(\mathbb{T})\right)+n\ln\left(\frac{2}{\sqrt{2\pi\mathrm{e}}}\right)+\ln\left(2\sqrt{\pi n}\right).

(17)

On the other hand, it follows from the maximality that
P
is a unit ball covering of
\mathbb{T}
.
Therefore, by (
16
),

\displaystyle\ln|P|\geq\ln\left(\frac{\operatorname{vol}(\mathbb{T})}{\nu_{n}}\right)

\displaystyle=\ln\left((1+o(1))\frac{\operatorname{vol}(\mathbb{T})\cdot\sqrt{\pi n}}{(2\pi\mathrm{e})^{\frac{n}{2}}}n^{\frac{n}{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{n}{2}\ln n+\ln\left(\operatorname{vol}(\mathbb{T})\right)+n\ln\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\mathrm{e}}}\right)+\ln\left(\frac{\sqrt{\pi n}}{2}\right).

(18)

Combining (
3
), (
3
) and the fact that
\operatorname{vol}(\mathbb{T})\geq\operatorname{vol}(K-K)\geq 1
, we obtain

\displaystyle\ln|P|-n\ln\ln\ln|P|\geq\frac{n}{2}\ln n-(1+o(1))n\ln\ln n.

Let
\omega(n)
be a function tending to infinity sufficiently slowly, with
\omega(n)=o(\ln\ln n)
. Let
\mathbf{X}
be a random set sampled from the Poisson point process on
\mathbb{T}
with intensity

\displaystyle\rho\coloneqq\ln|P|-n\big(\ln\ln\ln|P|+\omega(n)\big)\geq\frac{n}{2}\ln n-(1+o(1))n\ln\ln n.

It follows from Theorem
3.5
that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left[\,\mathbb{T}\subseteq\mathbf{X}+K\,\right]\leq\mathbb{P}\left[\,P\subseteq\mathbf{X}+K\,\right]=o(1),

as desired.
∎

The proof of Theorem
1.5
is similar to that of Theorem
1.3
and will follow from the following counterpart of Theorem
3.5
.

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
C_{n}=[-1/2,\,1/2]^{n}
, and let
\mathbb{T}
be a packing torus of
2C_{n}
.
Let
P\subseteq\mathbb{T}
be an
S_{n}
-packing with
|P|>\operatorname{vol}(\mathbb{T})
, where
S_{n}
is the
n
-dimensional cross-polytope

\displaystyle S_{n}\coloneqq\left\{(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\colon|x_{1}|+\cdots+|x_{n}|\leq 2\ln n\right\}.

Suppose that
\mathbf{X}\subseteq\mathbb{T}
is sampled from the Poisson point process with intensity

\displaystyle\rho\coloneqq\ln|P|-\ln\left(\operatorname{vol}(\mathbb{T})\right).

Then
\mathbb{P}\left[\,P\subseteq C_{n}+\mathbf{X}\,\right]
tends to
0
as
n\to\infty
.

Proof of Theorem
3.6
.

Let
\mathbb{T}
,
P
, and
\mathbf{X}
be as in the theorem.
Similar to the proof of Theorem
3.5
, for every point
\mathbf{p}\in P
, let
B_{\mathbf{p}}
be the characteristic function of the event that
\mathbf{p}\notin\mathbf{X}+C_{n}
.
Since
\operatorname{vol}(C_{n})=1
, the random variable
|\mathbf{X}\cap(\mathbf{p}-C_{n})|
has the Poisson distribution with mean
\rho\cdot\operatorname{vol}(\mathbf{p}-C_{n})=\rho
.
Therefore,
\mathbb{E}[B_{\mathbf{p}}]=\mathrm{e}^{-\rho}
.
Since
\operatorname{vol}(\mathbb{T})\geq\operatorname{vol}(2C_{n})=2^{n}
, it follows that the sum
B\coloneqq\sum_{\mathbf{p}\in P}B_{\mathbf{p}}
satisfies

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[B]=|P|\,\mathrm{e}^{-\rho}=\operatorname{vol}(\mathbb{T})\to\infty\quad\text{as}\quad n\to\infty.

(19)

Let

\displaystyle\Sigma_{1}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\sum_{\mathbf{p}\in P}\mathbb{E}\left[B_{\mathbf{p}}B_{\mathbf{p}}\right]=\sum_{\mathbf{p}\in P}\mathbb{E}\left[B_{\mathbf{p}}\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[B\right]\quad\text{and}\quad

\displaystyle\Sigma_{2}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\sum_{\mathbf{p},\mathbf{p}^{\prime}\in P,~\mathbf{p}\neq\mathbf{p}^{\prime}}\left(\mathbb{E}\left[B_{\mathbf{p}}B_{\mathbf{p}^{\prime}}\right]-\mathbb{E}\left[B_{\mathbf{p}}\right]\mathbb{E}\left[B_{\mathbf{p}^{\prime}}\right]\right).

Claim 3.7
.

We have
\Sigma_{2}=o\big(\mathbb{E}\left[B\right]^{2}\big)
.

Proof of Claim
3.7
.

Fix two distinct points
\mathbf{p},\mathbf{p}^{\prime}\in P
.
Since
P
is an
S_{n}
-packing, we have

\displaystyle\left(\mathbf{p}+S_{n}\right)\cap\left(\mathbf{p}^{\prime}+S_{n}\right)=\emptyset,

which implies that
\mathbf{x}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\coloneqq\mathbf{p}-\mathbf{p}^{\prime}
does not belong to
\mathrm{int}(S_{n}-S_{n})
, the interior of
S_{n}-S_{n}
.
In particular,
\mathbf{x}\not\in\mathrm{int}(S_{n})\subseteq\mathrm{int}(S_{n}-S_{n})
(since
\mathbf{0}\in S_{n}
), and hence,

\displaystyle|x_{1}|+\cdots+|x_{n}|\geq 2\ln n.

(20)

Combining it with the AM–GM inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle\operatorname{vol}\left(C_{n}\cap(C_{n}+\mathbf{x})\right)=\prod_{i\in[n]}\max\left\{1-|x_{i}|,~0\right\}

\displaystyle\leq\max\left\{\left(1-\frac{|x_{1}|+\cdots+|x_{n}|}{n}\right)^{n},0\right\}

\displaystyle\leq\left(1-\frac{2\ln n}{n}\right)^{n}\leq\frac{1}{n^{2}}.

(21)

On the other hand, since
\operatorname{vol}(S_{n})=\frac{2^{n}}{n!}\left(2\ln n\right)^{n}
, Stirling’s formula gives

\displaystyle|P|\leq\frac{\operatorname{vol}(\mathbb{T})}{\operatorname{vol}(S_{n})}\leq\frac{\operatorname{vol}(\mathbb{T})}{\frac{2^{n}}{n!}\left(2\ln n\right)^{n}}=(1+o(1))\sqrt{2\pi n}\left(\frac{n}{4\mathrm{e}\ln n}\right)^{n}\cdot\operatorname{vol}(\mathbb{T}).

(22)

Thus,

\displaystyle\rho=\ln|P|-\ln\left(\operatorname{vol}(\mathbb{T})\right)\leq n\ln n-\left(1+o(1)\right)n\ln\ln n=o(n^{2}).

(23)

Similarly to (
3
), it follows from (
3
) and (
23
) that

\displaystyle\Sigma_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\mathbf{p}\in P}\sum_{\mathbf{p}^{\prime}\in P\setminus\{\mathbf{p}\}}\left(\mathbb{E}\left[B_{\mathbf{p}}B_{\mathbf{p}^{\prime}}\right]-\mathbb{E}\left[B_{\mathbf{p}}\right]\mathbb{E}\left[B_{\mathbf{p}^{\prime}}\right]\right)

\displaystyle\leq|P|^{2}\left(\mathrm{exp}\left(-2\rho+\frac{\rho}{n^{2}}\right)-\mathrm{exp}(-2\rho)\right)=\mathbb{E}\left[B\right]^{2}\left(\mathrm{exp}\left(\frac{\rho}{n^{2}}\right)-1\right)=o\big(\mathbb{E}\left[B\right]^{2}\big),

which proves Claim
3.7
.
∎

It follows from Lemma
3.4
that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}[\,B=0\,]\leq\frac{\mathrm{Var}[B]}{\mathbb{E}[B]^{2}}=\frac{1}{\mathbb{E}[B]^{2}}\sum_{\mathbf{p},\mathbf{p}^{\prime}\in P}\left(\mathbb{E}[B_{\mathbf{p}}B_{\mathbf{p}^{\prime}}]-\mathbb{E}[B_{\mathbf{p}}]\,\mathbb{E}[B_{\mathbf{p}^{\prime}}]\right)\leq\frac{\Sigma_{1}+\Sigma_{2}}{\mathbb{E}[B]^{2}}=o\left(1\right),

which proves that
\mathbb{P}\left[\,P\subseteq C_{n}+\mathbf{X}\,\right]
tends to
0
as
n\to\infty
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.5
.

By monotonicity, it suffices to prove Theorem
1.5
for some
\rho\geq n\ln n-(1+o(1))n\ln\ln n
. Now suppose that
P
is a maximal
S_{n}
-packing of
\mathbb{T}
. Since
S_{n}
is centrally symmetric, it follows that
P
is a
2S_{n}
-covering of
\mathbb{T}
.
Consequently,

\displaystyle|P|\geq\frac{\operatorname{vol}(\mathbb{T})}{\operatorname{vol}(2S_{n})}\geq\frac{\operatorname{vol}(\mathbb{T})}{\operatorname{vol}(2S_{n})}=\frac{\operatorname{vol}(\mathbb{T})}{2^{n}\frac{2^{n}}{n!}\left(2\ln n\right)^{n}}=(1+o(1))\sqrt{2\pi n}\left(\frac{n}{8\mathrm{e}\ln n}\right)^{n}\cdot\operatorname{vol}(\mathbb{T}).

It follows that

\displaystyle\rho=\ln|P|-\ln\left(\operatorname{vol}(\mathbb{T})\right)\geq n\ln n-(1+o(1))n\ln\ln n.

By Theorem
3.6
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left[\,\mathbb{T}\subseteq\mathbf{X}+C_{n}\,\right]\leq\mathbb{P}\left[\,P\subseteq\mathbf{X}+C_{n}\,\right]=o(1).

This completes the proof of Theorem
1.5
.
∎

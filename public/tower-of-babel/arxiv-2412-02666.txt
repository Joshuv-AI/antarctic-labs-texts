Scaling limit of first passage percolation geodesics on planar maps
Emmanuel Kammerer

Abstract
We establish the scaling limit of the geodesics to the root for the first passage percolation distance on random planar maps. We first describe the scaling limit of the number of faces along the geodesics. This result enables us to compare the metric balls for the first passage percolation and the dual graph distance. It also enables us to give an upper bound for the diameter of large random maps. Then, we describe the scaling limit of the tree of first passage percolation geodesics to the root via a stochastic coalescing flow of pure jump diffusions. Using this stochastic flow, we also construct some random metric spaces which we conjecture to be the scaling limits of random planar maps with high degrees. The main tool in this work is a time-reversal of the uniform peeling exploration.

Introduction

1.1
Random Boltzmann maps

We study the first-passage percolation distance on random Boltzmann planar maps of type
a\in(3/2,5/2]
which correspond to the dual of the stable maps of
[
LGM11
]
when
a\in(3/2,5/2)
and to random planar maps in the universality class of the Brownian sphere when
a=5/2
. We give applications to the (dual) graph distance and establish the scaling limit of geodesics to the root.

A planar map is a connected planar graph which is embedded in the sphere and seen up to orientation-preserving transformations. Our planar maps are equipped with a distinguished oriented edge which is called the root edge. The face
f_{r}
which is on the right of the root edge is called the root face. We restrict our attention to bipartite planar maps, i.e. such that each face has an even degree. We denote by
\mathcal{M}
the set of finite bipartite planar maps and for all
\ell\geq 1
we denote by
\mathcal{M}^{(\ell)}
the set of bipartite planar maps whose root face has degree
2\ell
. See Figure
2
for an example.

A general way to pick a map at random consists in assigning positive weights to each face according to its degree. Following
[
MM07
]
, let
{\bf q}=(q_{k})_{k\geq 1}
be a non-zero sequence of non-negative real numbers. The weight of a map
\mathfrak{m}\in\mathcal{M}
is defined by

w_{\bf q}(\mathfrak{m})=\prod_{f\in\mathrm{Faces}(\mathfrak{m})\setminus\{f_{r}\}}q_{\deg(f)/2}.

We then define, for all
\ell\geq 1
, the partition function

W^{(\ell)}=\sum_{\mathfrak{m}\in\mathcal{M}^{(\ell)}}w_{\bf q}(\mathfrak{m}).

When the partition function is finite, the sequence
\bf q
is called admissible. When
{\bf q}
is admissible, we define the Boltzmann probability measure
\mathbb{P}^{(\ell)}
on
\mathcal{M}^{(\ell)}
by setting

\forall\mathfrak{m}\in\mathcal{M}^{(\ell)},\qquad\mathbb{P}^{(\ell)}(\{\mathfrak{m}\})=\frac{w_{\bf q}(\mathfrak{m})}{W^{(\ell)}}.

A random map of law
\mathbb{P}^{(\ell)}
is called a random Boltzmann map of perimeter
2\ell
.
The geometry of random Boltzmann maps heavily depends on the asymptotic behaviour of the partition function
W^{(\ell)}
as
\ell\to\infty
. It is known that there exists a constant
c_{\bf q}>0
such that

W^{(\ell)}=O(c_{\bf q}^{\ell}\ell^{-3/2})\qquad\text{and}\qquad W^{(\ell)}=\Omega(c_{\bf q}^{\ell}\ell^{-5/2})

as
\ell\to\infty
. See Section 5.3 of
[
Cur23
]
. As in the lecture notes
[
Cur23
]
, we say that the weight sequence
\bf q
is subcritical (or of type
a=3/2
) if there exists a constant
p_{\bf q}>0
such that

W^{(\ell)}\mathop{\sim}\limits_{\ell\to\infty}\frac{p_{\bf q}}{2}c_{\bf q}^{\ell+1}\ell^{-3/2},

while the sequence
\bf q
is said to be critical generic (or of type
a=5/2
) if there exists
p_{\bf q}>0
such that

W^{(\ell)}\mathop{\sim}\limits_{\ell\to\infty}\frac{p_{\bf q}}{2}c_{\bf q}^{\ell+1}\ell^{-5/2}.

When
q_{k}=0
for
k
large enough, i.e. when the face degrees are bounded, and
\bf q
is admissible, then either
\bf q
is subcritical, or it is generic critical. To obtain intermediate exponents, one has to favor the appearance of high degrees by choosing
\bf q
so that the weights decay “slowly enough”.

We say that
\bf q
is critical non-generic of type
a
with
3/2<a<5/2
if there exists
p_{\bf q}>0
such that

W^{(\ell)}\mathop{\sim}\limits_{\ell\to\infty}\frac{p_{\bf q}}{2}c_{\bf q}^{\ell+1}\ell^{-a}.

(1.1)

A random Boltzmann map associated with a critical non-generic weight sequence
\bf q
of type
a
will also be called a stable map with parameter
a-1/2
. Examples of such sequences
\bf q
are exhibited in Lemma 6.1 of
[
BC17
]
.

The study of random Boltzmann maps with
\bf q
being critical non-generic is motivated by strong connections with random maps coupled with models of statistical mechanics. More precisely, if one considers a random map coupled with a model of statistical mechanics, then, at criticality, the cluster of the root face, which is also called the gasket, is a random Boltzmann map with a critical non-generic weight sequence
\bf q
. See e.g.
[
BCM19
]
for Bernoulli percolation,
[
AM22
]
for the Ising model and
[
BBG12
]
for the O(n) loop model. Random planar maps coupled with models of statistical mechanics were first studied by physicists as models of statistical mechanics in quantum gravity. See e.g.
[
EK95
]
and the references therein for the O(n) loop model. Scaling limits of random maps coupled with models of statistical mechanics are conjectured to be described by Liouville quantum gravity, first introduced in physics in
[
Pol81
]
, and by an independent conformal loop ensemble, defined in
[
She09
,
SW12
]
. See e.g. Conjecture 2.1 in
[
HL24
]
for a precise conjecture in the case the O(n) loop model and see
[
GHS21
]
for a result solving the analogous problem in the case of Bernoulli percolation.

In what follows, we assume that (
1.1
) holds for some
a\in(3/2,5/2]
, i.e. that
\bf q
is either critical generic or critical non-generic.

If one conditions a random map of law
\mathbb{P}^{(\ell)}
to have
n
vertices and lets

, then the law of the ball of fixed radius
R
centered at the root face
f_{r}
converges for all
R>0
. This enables us to define an infinite random map called the local limit, whose law is given by Theorem 7.1 of
[
Cur23
]
and is denoted by
\mathbb{P}^{(\ell)}_{\infty}
. A random map of law
\mathbb{P}^{(\ell)}_{\infty}
is called an infinite random Boltzmann map of perimeter
2\ell
.

When
\mathfrak{m}
is a (possibly infinite) map, we denote by
\mathfrak{m}^{\dagger}
the dual map associated with
\mathfrak{m}
which is obtained by switching the roles of the faces and vertices and saying that two vertices of
\mathfrak{m}^{\dagger}
are connected by an edge if the corresponding faces of
\mathfrak{m}
are adjacent. Different distances can then be studied, notably:

•

The (primal) graph distance
d_{\mathrm{gr}}
on the vertices of
\mathfrak{m}
, obtained by assigning a length
1
to each edge;

•

The dual graph distance

on the faces of
\mathfrak{m}
, which are the vertices of
\mathfrak{m}^{\dagger}
, obtained by assigning a length
1
to each dual edge. This distance corresponds to the graph distance on the dual map
\mathfrak{m}^{\dagger}
;

•

The first-passage percolation (fpp) distance
d^{\dagger}_{\mathrm{fpp}}
on the faces of
\mathfrak{m}
, obtained by assigning i.i.d. exponential random edge lengths of parameter
1
to the dual edges.

The primal graph distance is now well understood in the generic case
a=5/2
since the works of Le Gall
[
LG13
]
and Miermont
[
Mie13
]
. The scaling limit of random maps of law

in the case

is given in Theorem 8 of
[
BM17
]
: if one divides the distance

by

, then the random metric space converges in distribution towards the so-called free Brownian disk which was initially introduced in
[
Bet15
]
. It is conjectured that in the generic case
a=5/2
the dual distances
d^{\dagger}_{\mathrm{gr}}
and
d^{\dagger}_{\mathrm{fpp}}
behave in the same way as the primal distance
d_{\mathrm{gr}}
since the face degrees are not too large. In the case of triangulations, the question is solved and the distances
d_{\mathrm{gr}}
,
d^{\dagger}_{\mathrm{gr}}
and
d^{\dagger}_{\mathrm{fpp}}
are actually very close to each other up to a multiplicative constant as shown in
[
CLG19
]
. In the critical non-generic case
a\in(3/2,5/2)
the scaling limit of random maps of law
\mathbb{P}^{(1)}
conditioned to have
n
vertices equipped with de distance
d_{\mathrm{gr}}
as
n\to\infty
is determined in
[
CMR25
]
and the scaling limit of random maps of law
\mathbb{P}^{(\ell)}
should be obtained in the same way. Yet, when
a\in(3/2,5/2)
, the behaviour of dual distances
d^{\dagger}_{\mathrm{gr}}
and
d^{\dagger}_{\mathrm{fpp}}
is drastically different from the primal distance
d_{\mathrm{gr}}
insofar as the appearance of large faces leads to high degree vertices playing the role of hubs in the dual map. The geometry of large random maps of law
\mathbb{P}^{(\ell)}
and infinite random maps of law
\mathbb{P}^{(\ell)}_{\infty}
for the dual distances
d^{\dagger}_{\mathrm{gr}}
and
d^{\dagger}_{\mathrm{fpp}}
is only partially understood.

The geometry of infinite random maps equipped with the dual distances under
\mathbb{P}^{(\ell)}_{\infty}
was first studied in the case of the uniform infinite planar quadrangulation (which is a particular case of the case
a=5/2
) in
[
CLG17
]
and
[
CC18
]
, then in
[
BC17
]
in the cases
a\in(3/2,5/2)\setminus\{2\}
and in
[
BCM18
]
in the critical case
a=2
. The geometry of the map satisfies a phase transition at
a=2
in the sense that the growth of the balls centered at the root is polynomial in the dilute phase
a\in(2,5/2)
, exponential in the dense phase
a\in(3/2,2)
and intermediate in the case
a=2
. The scaling limit of the distances
d^{\dagger}_{\mathrm{gr}}
to the root under
\mathbb{P}^{(\ell)}
or
\mathbb{P}^{(\ell)}_{\infty}
as
\ell\to\infty
was established using the framework of growth-fragmentations in
[
BCK18
]
in the case of triangulations (corresponding informally to the case
a=5/2
), then in
[
BBCK18
]
in the case
a\in(2,5/2)
and in
[
Kam23
]
in the case
a=2
. More precisely, it is known that in a map of law
\mathbb{P}^{(\ell)}
, the distances
d^{\dagger}_{\mathrm{gr}}
from
f_{r}
to the other faces, once divided by
\ell^{a-2}
in the case

and by
\log\ell
in the case
a=2
converge in distribution. In the case
a\in(2,5/2]
, it is conjectured that random maps of law
\mathbb{P}^{(\ell)}
equipped with

or

satisfy a scaling limit towards a random compact metric space. In the case
a=2
it is conjectured in
[
Kam23
]
that they satisfy a scaling limit towards a non-compact random metric space related to the conformal loop ensemble of parameter
\kappa=4
on an independent critical Liouville quantum gravity. When
a\in(3/2,2)
random planar maps of law
\mathbb{P}^{(\ell)}
equipped with
d^{\dagger}_{\mathrm{gr}}
are not expected to satisfy a scaling limit when
\ell\to\infty
.

The study of the fpp distance
d^{\dagger}_{\mathrm{fpp}}
proves to be simpler than the study of the dual graph distance
d^{\dagger}_{\mathrm{gr}}
and gives the same scaling limit in the cases
a\in[2,5/2]
(with the disappearance of the
\log\ell
factor in the case
a=2
). Actually, the study of the fpp distance can be seen as a first step which turns out to be useful for the study of the dual graph distance, see e.g.
[
BC17
,
BCM18
,
Kam25
,
Kam23
]
. In this work, we thus focus on the fpp distance
d^{\dagger}_{\mathrm{fpp}}
on the dual map
\mathfrak{m}^{\dagger}
under
\mathbb{P}^{(\ell)}
and
\mathbb{P}^{(\ell)}_{\infty}
and we go further than the distances to the root in the study of the geometry of the map. For all faces

of
\mathfrak{m}
, the shortest path for the fpp distance from
f
to

is unique since the exponential distribution has no atoms. It does not necessarily correspond to a shortest path for the dual graph distance (see e.g. Figure
2
). The main focus of this work is the study of the shortest paths to the root face
f_{r}
and their scaling limits. These shortest paths actually form a tree which is denoted by
\mathcal{T}(\mathfrak{m})
. This study also has applications to the dual graph distance
d^{\dagger}_{\mathrm{gr}}
, which corresponds to the graph distance on
\mathfrak{m}^{\dagger}
.

An important tool in the study of distances on random planar maps is the peeling exploration. See Subsection
2.1
for more details, as well as the lecture notes
[
Cur23
]
or
[
Bud
]
for a detailed presentation of this technique. It consists in a step by step exploration of the map
\mathfrak{m}
starting from the root face, where at each step we discover what is behind an edge on the boundary of the unexplored region. This edge is called the peeled edge. The explored region at step
n\geq 0
is denoted by
\overline{\mathfrak{e}}_{n}
. We denote by
(P(n))_{n\geq 0}
the perimeter process which is defined as half of the number of edges on the boundary of the explored region
\overline{\mathfrak{e}}_{n}
at time
n\geq 0
. Under
\mathbb{P}_{\infty}^{(\ell)}
, the perimeter process is a Markov chain which is a random walk on
\mathbb{Z}
of step distribution
\nu
conditioned to stay positive, whose scaling limit is described by a stable Lévy process conditioned to stay positive
\Upsilon_{a}^{\uparrow}
. See Subsection
2.2
for the definition of
\nu
and Subsection
3.1
for the definition of
\Upsilon_{a}^{\uparrow}
. The choice of the peeled edge is crucial in order to study different properties of the map. In this work, the peeled edge is chosen uniformly at random on the boundary of
\overline{\mathfrak{e}}_{n}
for all
n\geq 0
so that the explored region
\overline{\mathfrak{e}}_{n}
follows the growth of the fpp balls of center
f_{r}
.

1.2
Main results

Our results can be divided into two parts. We first focus on the scaling limit of the number of faces along the geodesics, hence relating the fpp distance with the dual graph distance on the geodesics. This scaling limit enables us to compare the growth of fpp and dual graph balls and also to obtain an upper bound for the diameter of large random planar maps for the dual graph distance. Then, we describe the scaling limit of the fpp geodesics to the root in large random planar maps.

Number of faces.

We first give the scaling limit of the number of faces in the fpp geodesics to the root and give applications to the dual graph distance. We choose to state these results for infinite Boltzmann planar maps of law
\mathbb{P}_{\infty}^{(1)}
. When
a\in(2,5/2]
, as in Definition 11.1 in
[
Cur23
]
, we let
{e}_{\bf q}=\sum_{k\geq 0}(2k+1){q_{k+1}c_{\bf q}^{k}}
be the so-called mean exposure. Recall the constant
p_{\bf q}
from (
1.1
).

Theorem 1.1
.

For all
n\geq 0
, let
\Gamma_{n}
be the shortest path for
d^{\dagger}_{\mathrm{fpp}}
from
f_{r}
to some face of
\overline{\mathfrak{e}}_{n}
adjacent to the boundary which is chosen arbitrarily. Then we have the following convergences:

•

If
a\in(2,5/2]
, then for the topology of uniform convergence on compact subsets,

\text{Under }\mathbb{P}^{(1)}_{\infty},\qquad\left(n^{-\frac{a-2}{a-1}}\#\mathrm{Faces}(\Gamma_{\lfloor nt\rfloor})\right)_{t\geq 0}\mathop{\longrightarrow}\limits_{n\to\infty}^{(\mathrm{d})}\left(\frac{e_{\bf q}}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{ds}{\Upsilon_{a}^{\uparrow}(p_{\bf q}s)}\right)_{t\geq 0};

•

If
a=2
, then

\text{Under }\mathbb{P}^{(1)}_{\infty},\qquad(\log n)^{-2}\#\mathrm{Faces}(\Gamma_{n})\mathop{\longrightarrow}\limits_{n\to\infty}^{(\mathrm{L}^{1})}\frac{1}{\pi^{2}};

•

If
a\in(3/2,2)
, then

\text{Under }\mathbb{P}^{(1)}_{\infty},\qquad(\log n)^{-1}\#\mathrm{Faces}(\Gamma_{n})\mathop{\longrightarrow}\limits_{n\to\infty}^{(\mathrm{L}^{1})}\frac{2}{\pi\tan((2-a)\pi)}.

One can compare the above result with the study of the growth of balls for the dual graph distance and see that the exact same scaling limits appear in Theorem 4.2 of
[
BC17
]
in the dilute phase
a\in(2,5/2)
and in Proposition 4 of
[
BCM18
]
in the case
a=2
up to a multiplicative constant.

The above observation is not a coincidence: Theorem
1.1
has some applications to the dual graph distance
d^{\dagger}_{\mathrm{gr}}
. Namely, in the cases
a\geq 2
, we are able to compare the distances
d^{\dagger}_{\mathrm{fpp}}
and
d^{\dagger}_{\mathrm{gr}}
, improving the results of
[
CM20
]
in the case of the uniform exploration. When
\mathfrak{m}
is an infinite (one-ended) planar map, for all
r\geq 0
, we denote by
\overline{\mathrm{Ball}}^{\mathrm{fpp}}_{r}(\mathfrak{m})
the hull of the ball with center
f_{r}
of radius
r
for the fpp distance obtained from the ball of radius
r
by filling in the holes which contain finite portions of
\mathfrak{m}
. Similarly, for all
r\geq 0
, we denote by
\overline{\mathrm{Ball}}^{\dagger}_{r}
the hull of the ball with center
f_{r}
of radius
r
for the dual graph distance obtained by filling in the holes which contain finite portions of
\mathfrak{m}
.

Corollary 1.2
.

Suppose
{\bf q}
of type
a\in(2,5/2]
. Under
\mathbb{P}_{\infty}^{(1)}
, for all
\varepsilon>0
, almost surely, for all
r
large enough,

\overline{\mathrm{Ball}}^{\mathrm{fpp}}_{r}(\mathfrak{m})\subset\overline{\mathrm{Ball}}^{\dagger}_{\lfloor(1+\varepsilon)e_{\bf q}r\rfloor}(\mathfrak{m}).

The above result improves Theorem 1.1 of
[
CM20
]
in the case of the uniform exploration which enables us to obtain the same inclusion as above but with a non-explicit constant in place of
(1+\varepsilon)e_{\bf q}
.
An analogous result holds in the case
a=2
.

Corollary 1.3
.

Suppose
\bf q
of type
a=2
. Under
\mathbb{P}_{\infty}^{(1)}
, for all
\varepsilon>0
, with probability
1-o(1)
as
r\to\infty
,

\overline{\mathrm{Ball}}^{\mathrm{fpp}}_{r}(\mathfrak{m})\subset\overline{\mathrm{Ball}}^{\mathrm{\dagger}}_{\lfloor(1+\varepsilon)\pi^{2}p^{2}_{\bf q}r^{2}\rfloor}(\mathfrak{m}).

A last consequence of the counting of faces along geodesics to the root, which is obtained using the martingale techniques introduced in
[
Kam25
]
, is an upper bound for the diameter
\mathrm{Diam}^{\dagger}_{\mathrm{gr}}(\mathfrak{m})
of (finite) random maps
\mathfrak{m}
in the case
a\in(3/2,2)
when they are equipped with the dual graph distance. This completes the results of Part II of
[
BCR24
]
and Theorem 1.2 of
[
Kam25
]
which show that in the respective cases
a\in(2,5/2]
and
a=2
the diameter
\mathrm{Diam}^{\dagger}_{\mathrm{gr}}(\mathfrak{m})
under the law
\mathbb{P}^{(\ell)}
is of order respectively
\ell^{2-a}
and
(\log\ell)^{2}
as
\ell\to\infty
.

Theorem 1.4
.

Suppose
{\bf q}
is of type
a\in(3/2,2)
. Then, there exists a constant
C_{\bf q}>0
such that under
\mathbb{P}^{(\ell)}
as
\ell\to\infty
, we have with probability
1-o(1)

\mathrm{Diam}_{\mathrm{gr}}^{\dagger}(\mathfrak{m})\leq C_{\bf q}\log\ell.

The main tool for obtaining the above results is a backward exploration of the map, obtained by reversing time in the uniform peeling exploration, which makes possible to keep track of the geodesic
\Gamma_{n}
.

Scaling limit of the tree of geodesics.

If
\mathfrak{m}
is a planar map, recall that
\mathcal{T}(\mathfrak{m})
is the random tree of fpp geodesics from the root. Its vertices are the faces of
\mathfrak{m}
and its edges are the dual edges which appear in the shortest paths for
d^{\dagger}_{\mathrm{fpp}}
from each face to the root
f_{r}
. See e.g. Figure
2
. We equip each edge of
\mathcal{T}(\mathfrak{m})
with the random fpp length of the associated dual edge of
\mathfrak{m}
. We denote by
d_{\mathcal{T}(\mathfrak{m})}
the corresponding distance on
\mathcal{T}(\mathfrak{m})
. Let
(f_{i})_{i\geq 1}
be the family of the faces of
\mathfrak{m}
in non-increasing order of degrees.

Theorem 1.5
.

Assume
\bf q
is of type
a\in(3/2,5/2)
. Then,

\text{Under }\mathbb{P}^{(\ell)},\qquad\qquad\left(\ell^{2-a}d_{\mathcal{T}(\mathfrak{m})}(f_{i},f_{j})\right)_{i,j\geq 1}\mathop{\longrightarrow}\limits_{\ell\to\infty}^{(\mathrm{d})}\left(\frac{1}{2c_{a}p_{\bf q}}d_{\mathcal{T}_{a}}(w_{i},w_{j})\right)_{i,j\geq 1},

for the product topology, where
c_{a}\coloneqq\pi/\Gamma(a)
and
\mathcal{T}_{a}=(\{w_{i};\ i\geq 1\},d_{\mathcal{T}_{a}})
is a random countable metric space.

Let us give some insight into this scaling limit. Using the time reversal of the uniform peeling exploration, we describe the geodesics to the root using a family of coalescing processes. We prove that these processes converge towards a stochastic flow of coalescing diffusions with jumps. The metric space
\mathcal{T}_{a}=(\{w_{i};\ i\geq 1\},d_{\mathcal{T}_{a}})
is then defined using this flow of diffusions and we show that it is indeed the scaling limit of
\mathcal{T}(\mathfrak{m})
. The exact definition of the limit is in Section
5.1
. The stochastic flow is driven by the jumps of the self-similar growth-fragmentation corresponding to the scaling limit of the slicing by heights as shown in
[
BCK18
]
in the generic case
a=5/2
,
[
BBCK18
]
in the dilute case
a\in(2,5/2)
and
[
Kam23
]
in the critical case
a=2
and by independent uniform random variables which arise from the uniform peeling exploration. The set
\{w_{i};\ i\geq 1\}
corresponds to the set of positive jumps of the growth-fragmentation, plus an additional point corresponding to the root face. One can then focus on the trajectories of the flow which “start from” the positive jumps and their coalescences give the genealogical structure of
\mathcal{T}_{a}
. Finally, the distances on
\mathcal{T}_{a}
are measured using a Lamperti transform.

1.3
Perpectives

Generic case.

Even though Theorem
1.5
does not hold in the critical generic case
a=5/2
, the scaling limit of the coalescing processes also holds in the generic case
a=5/2
. This should give a new description of the geodesics to the root in the Brownian disk as a flow of diffusions with jumps. However, due to the absence of high degree vertices, the scaling limit of the tree of fpp geodesics cannot be stated in the same way as in Theorem
1.5
in this case so that another approach is needed in the case
a=5/2
. Actually, a related approach is carried out in Section 3 of
[
MS21
]
for the axiomatic characterization of the Brownian sphere introducing a “Lévy net”. Let us also mention the fact that another construction of the Brownian disk is given in
[
LG19
]
, which is different from that of
[
Bet15
,
BM17
]
. One could try to identify the coalescing flow in one of these constructions. Furthermore, let us stress that the tree
\mathcal{T}(\mathfrak{m})
of geodesics to the root does not correspond to the so-called cactus of the metric space
(\mathfrak{m}^{\dagger},d^{\dagger}_{\mathrm{fpp}})
whose analogue for
d_{\mathrm{gr}}
is studied in
[
CLGM13
]
since the cactus is defined using the geodesic between two points and the point of this geodesic which is closest to the root. The cactus does not describe the coalescence of geodesics to the root. In particular, we believe that the conjectural scaling limit of
\mathcal{T}(\mathfrak{m})
under
\mathbb{P}^{(\ell)}
in the case
a=5/2
is different from the Brownian cactus of
[
CLGM13
]
.

Link with self-similar Markov trees.

Besides, one could define the stochastic flow on top of the self-similar Markov trees from
[
BCR24
]
in the case
a\in(2,5/2]
but we do not in order to cover the whole range
(3/2,5/2]
since the self-similar Markov trees of
[
BCR24
]
are required to be compact. Let us also insist on the fact that self-similar Markov trees only describe the so-called exploration tree of the map and do not give the scaling limit of geodesics to the root. Nevertheless, we believe that defining the stochastic flow on top of the self-similar Markov tree would be very relevant in order to define the scaling limit of the tree of geodesics to the root as a random compact measured metric space when
a\in(2,5/2]
.

Critical case.

In the critical case
a=2
, in view of the results in
[
Kam23
]
, we expect the tree
\mathcal{T}_{2}
to describe the genealogy of the loops in the uniform exploration of the conformal loop ensemble of parameter
\kappa=4
introduced in
[
WW13

Geodesics for the dual graph distance.

Last but not least, using the flow of coalescing diffusions with jumps by adding some “shortcuts” we introduce in Section

a random countable metric space which we conjecture to be the scaling limit of random Boltzmann maps for

1.4
Techniques

As mentioned before, the main technique which is introduced and extensively used in the paper is a time reversal of the uniform peeling exploration. More precisely, conditionally on the perimeter process up to time time
n\geq 0
, we progressively construct a map which has the same law as the explored region
\overline{\mathfrak{e}}_{n}
by gluing the discovered faces and inserting the holes which are filled in starting from the boundary
\partial\overline{\mathfrak{e}}_{n}
. At each step, the position where we glue the face or insert the hole is chosen uniformly at random.

Using the time reversal of the uniform peeling exploration, we show that conditionally on the perimeter process, the number of faces along a fpp geodesic to the root can be written as a sum of independent Bernoulli random variables. The asymptotics of the number of faces along the geodesics is then obtained using precise estimates for the perimeter process which were first introduced in
[
BCM18
]
. Next, the comparisons between the fpp and the dual graph distances are performed using concentration inequalities which enable us to extend the control over the number of faces along one geodesic to all the geodesics from the root to the boundary of the ball. The upper bound for the diameter for
d^{\dagger}_{\mathrm{gr}}
in the dense case
a\in(3/2,2)
is obtained by combining the identification of the number of faces along a fpp geodesic with the techniques introduced in
[
Kam25
]
. More precisely, in order to control the diameter, we first use a first moment method, then a re-rooting trick designed in
[
Kam25
]
in order to exchange the roles of the root and of a uniform random edge and finally we use some martingales which were introduced in
[
Kam25
]
for the same purpose in the case
a=2
. The use of fpp geodesics is a way to circumvent the direct study of the dual graph distance via the peeling by layers algorithm performed in Section 5.2 of
[
BC17
]
.

In order to obtain the scaling limit of the tree of geodesics, we code the time reversal of the uniform exploration using a family of coalescing processes on the torus
\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}
(which we define in a periodic way on
\mathbb{R}
). These processes are not Markovian since time runs in the opposite direction for the perimeter process
P
. Still, their limit is given by a flow of coalescing pure jump diffusions. This flow is described as a flow of SDEs driven by a Poisson point process. Due to the lack of Lipschitzianity of the coefficients, the existence and uniqueness of strong solutions is not obtained using the classical theorems such as Theorem 9.1 of Chapter IV of
[
IW89
]
but it is obtained using the work
[
LP12
]
which requires less regularity for the coefficients but asks in return for a monotonicity condition. We do not rely on the theory of coalescing flows of
[
LJR04
]
. So as to show that the convergence of the coalescing processes associated with the time reversal of the exploration towards the coalescing flow of diffusions with jumps, since time is reversed for the perimeter process, we cannot directly apply classical limit theorems for diffusions with jumps from Chapter IX Section 4 of
[
JS87
]
. Instead, building on the uniform random variables involved in the time-reversal of the uniform peeling exploration, we construct a coupling between one trajectory and an approximation where we removed all the jumps of size smaller than
\varepsilon
for some small
\varepsilon>0
such that the two trajectories are close to each other uniformly in
\ell\geq 1
. We establish the convergence of this approximation and then let
\varepsilon\to 0
, applying the results of
[
JS87
]
in the continuum. Once the convergence towards the coalescing flow is established, the scaling limit of the tree of fpp geodesics to the root boils down to the convergence of the “times of birth” of each face and of the coalescence times of the associated trajectories. The convergence of the coalescence times is obtained thanks to the fact that trajectories of the coalescing flow of diffusions with jumps have “many large jumps” in the case
a\in(3/2,2)
, so that the coalescence time is a time of a large jump, and “many small jumps” in the case
a\in[2,5/2)
, in a way that shortly after coalescing the trajectory has small jumps.

1.5
Plan of the paper

We define the time reversal of the uniform peeling exploration in Section

1.3

2
The time reversal of the uniform peeling exploration

In this section, we present a time-reversal of the uniform exploration of a random Boltzmann map conditionally on the perimeter process. This reversed uniform exploration is the central tool of this work. But first, we recall the definition of the uniform peeling exploration.

2.1
The uniform peeling exploration

We say that a map
\mathfrak{e}
is a map with one hole if
\mathfrak{e}
has a distinguished face with simple boundary which is different from
f_{r}
. Moreover, we say that
\mathfrak{e}
is a submap of a map
\mathfrak{m}
if one can obtain
\mathfrak{m}
by gluing a map in the hole of
\mathfrak{e}
. The boundary
\partial\mathfrak{e}
of a map with one hole is the set of edges on the boundary of its hole. If
\mathfrak{e}
is a submap of
\mathfrak{m}
and
e\in\partial\mathfrak{e}
, then
e
is adjacent to two faces of
\mathfrak{e}
and one of them is the hole. The face which is “behind” the edge
e
in
\mathfrak{m}
is then defined as the face of
\mathfrak{m}
which is glued to
e
when we glue a map in the hole of
\mathfrak{e}
in order to obtain
\mathfrak{m}
(note that this face may be in
\mathfrak{e}
or not). A peeling algorithm
\mathcal{A}
is a function which takes a map with one hole
\mathfrak{e}
and returns an edge of
\partial\mathfrak{e}
. The algorithm
\mathcal{A}
may be random.

Let
\mathfrak{m}
be a map which is possibly infinite. A peeling exploration of
\mathfrak{m}
following the peeling algorithm
\mathcal{A}
is an increasing sequence of submaps
\overline{\mathfrak{e}}_{0}\subset\overline{\mathfrak{e}}_{1}\subset\cdots\subset\overline{\mathfrak{e}}_{n}\subset\cdots\subset\mathfrak{m}
such that
\overline{\mathfrak{e}}_{0}
is the map formed by gluing the root face
f_{r}
of
\mathfrak{m}
to a hole of the same degree, and such that for all
n\geq 0
, the submap
\overline{\mathfrak{e}}_{n+1}
is obtained from
\overline{\mathfrak{e}}_{n}
in the following way:

•

If the face
f
behind the edge
\mathcal{A}(\overline{\mathfrak{e}}_{n})
in
\mathfrak{m}
is not in
\overline{\mathfrak{e}}_{n}
, then we “discover” this face by gluing

to
\overline{\mathfrak{e}}_{n}
on the edge
\mathcal{A}(\overline{\mathfrak{e}}_{n})
. See the two peeling steps on the right of the first row in Figure

•

If the face behind
\mathcal{A}(\overline{\mathfrak{e}}_{n})
in
\mathfrak{m}
is already in
\overline{\mathfrak{e}}_{n}

\mathcal{A}(\overline{\mathfrak{e}}_{n})
to the edge in
\partial\overline{\mathfrak{e}}_{n}
following the adjacency in
\mathfrak{m}
. The hole then splits into two holes. When
\mathfrak{m}
is infinite, we choose to fill in the hole which contains only a finite number of faces in
\mathfrak{m}
. When
\mathfrak{m}
is finite, we choose to fill in the hole which has the smallest perimeter (and in case of equality we fill in one of the two holes chosen arbitrarily in a deterministic way). See the left of the first row and the second row in Figure

We denote by

the perimeter process which is defined as half of the number of edges in

. When
\mathfrak{m}
is finite, the exploration stops when

reaches zero.

t

Note that
\overline{\mathfrak{e}}_{0}=\mathrm{Ball}^{\mathrm{fpp}}_{0}

\overline{\mathfrak{e}}_{n}
can be obtained from

\overline{\mathfrak{e}}_{n}
are i.i.d. exponential random variables of parameter
1

\partial\overline{\mathfrak{e}}_{n}
whose dual edge has the smallest length. By well-known properties of the exponential distribution, this edge is actually chosen uniformly at random and the fpp length of the corresponding dual edge is an exponential random variable of expectation

\overline{\mathfrak{e}}_{n}=\overline{\mathrm{Ball}}^{\mathrm{fpp}}_{T_{n}}(\mathfrak{m})\qquad\text{with}\qquad T_{n}=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\frac{\mathcal{E}_{i}}{2P(i)},

\mathfrak{m}
except one, which corresponds to the infinite hole when
\mathfrak{m}
is infinite.

2.2
The law of the peeling exploration of Boltzmann planar maps

Under
\mathbb{P}^{(\ell)}
(respectively
\mathbb{P}^{(\ell)}_{\infty}
) the peeling exploration has several nice features, which do not depend on the peeling algorithm. It is a Markov chain and its transitions can be described using the perimeter process.

First, the peeling exploration satisfies a spatial Markov property, stating that at each step
n\geq 0
, conditionally on
\overline{\mathfrak{e}}_{n}
, the map which fills the hole in
\mathfrak{m}
is a random map of law
\mathbb{P}^{(p)}
(resp.
\mathbb{P}_{\infty}^{(p)}
), where
p=P(n)
. Moreover, at each time
n
such that
P(n+1)<P(n)
, the map in the hole which is filled in after gluing the two edges is a random map of law
\mathbb{P}^{(p)}
with
p=-\Delta P(n)-1
. See Propositions 4.6 and 6.3 of
[
Cur23
]
for a detailed statement.

Moreover, the perimeter process itself is a Markov chain, whose transition probabilities are better written using the distribution
\nu
defined by

\forall k\in\mathbb{Z},\qquad\qquad\nu(k)=\left\{\begin{matrix}q_{k+1}c_{\bf q}^{k}&\text{ if }k\geq 0\\
2W^{(-1-k)}c_{\bf q}^{k}&\text{ if }k<0,\end{matrix}\right.

(2.1)

where we recall that
(q_{k})_{k\geq 1}
is the weight sequence,

is the partition function of maps of perimeter
2\ell
for all

, and

appears in (
1.1
).
It is known that

is a probability measure (see e.g. Lemma 5.2 of
[
Cur23
]
). Under
\mathbb{P}^{(\ell)}_{\infty}
, the perimeter process

is the Doob

\nu
-random walk starting at
\ell

on

defined by

\forall\ell\geq 1,\qquad h^{\uparrow}(\ell)=2\ell 2^{-2\ell}\binom{2\ell}{\ell}.

(2.2)

See e.g. Remark 7.5 of
[
Cur23
]
. This Doob

-transform can be interpreted as a conditioning of the
\nu
-random walk to always stay positive. Moreover, under
\mathbb{P}^{(\ell)}
, the perimeter process
(P(n))_{n\geq 0}
is a Markov chain starting at
\ell
, absorbed at zero, with transition probabilities for all
n\geq 0

\mathbb{P}^{(\ell)}(P(n+1)=p+k|P(n)=p)=\nu(k)c_{\bf q}^{-k}\frac{W^{(p+k)}}{W^{(p)}}\left({\bf 1}_{p+k>(p-1)/2}+\frac{1}{2}{\bf 1}_{p+k=(p-1)/2}\right).

See the second display of page 53 of

The tail behavior of

where the constant

3

In this section, we prove Theorem

(Lemma 4 of

.

]

.

(Lemma 5 of

BCM18
]
)
Let

be a sequence tending to
\infty
. For all

, for all

, let

If
{\bf q}
is of type

, then

Proof.

The proof follows the same lines as in the case
a=2
treated in
[
BCM18
]
, but let us write it for completeness. We write

\displaystyle\Big|\mathbb{E}^{(1)}_{\infty}\left(iX_{i}^{(\varepsilon)}jX_{j}^{(\varepsilon)}\right)\Big|\leq

\displaystyle\left|\mathbb{E}^{(1)}_{\infty}\left(iX_{i}^{(\varepsilon)}jX_{j}^{(\varepsilon)}{\bf 1}_{P_{\infty}(i)\not\in I(i,\varepsilon)}\right)\right|+\left|\mathbb{E}^{(1)}_{\infty}\left(iX_{i}^{(\varepsilon)}jX_{j}^{(\varepsilon)}{\bf 1}_{P_{\infty}(j)\not\in I(j,\varepsilon)}\right)\right|

\displaystyle+\left|\mathbb{E}^{(1)}_{\infty}\left(iX_{i}^{(\varepsilon)}jX_{j}^{(\varepsilon)}{\bf 1}_{P_{\infty}(i)\in I(i,\varepsilon)}{\bf 1}_{P_{\infty}(j)\in I(j,\varepsilon)}\right)\right|.

Let
K_{i}^{(\varepsilon)}=\mathbb{E}^{(1)}_{\infty}(\frac{1}{P_{\infty}(i)^{a-1}}{\bf 1}_{P_{\infty}(i)\in I(i,\varepsilon)})
. Note that on the event
\{P_{\infty}(i)\not\in I(i,\varepsilon)\}
, we have
X_{i}^{(\varepsilon)}=-K_{i}^{(\varepsilon)}
, so the first term can be written as

\left|\mathbb{E}^{(1)}_{\infty}\left(iX_{i}^{(\varepsilon)}jX_{j}^{(\varepsilon)}{\bf 1}_{P_{\infty}(i)\not\in I(i,\varepsilon)}\right)\right|=(iK_{i}^{(\varepsilon)})\left|\mathbb{E}^{(1)}_{\infty}\left(jX_{j}^{(\varepsilon)}{\bf 1}_{P_{\infty}(i)\not\in I(i,\varepsilon)}\right)\right|,

which converges to zero as
\varepsilon\to 0
uniformly in
i,j\geq 1
since
(iK_{i}^{(\varepsilon)})_{i\geq 1,\varepsilon>0}
is bounded from above by some constant
C>0
by Remark
3.4
, since
(jX_{j}^{(\varepsilon)})_{j\geq 1,\varepsilon>0}
is uniformly integrable by Lemma
3.3
using that
a-1<3/2
and since
(P_{\infty}(i)/i^{1/(a-1)})_{i\geq 1}
is tight by (
3.1
). The second term is handled in the same way. Thus, it only remains to show that

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\limsup_{n\to\infty}\sup_{1\leq i\leq n}\sup_{A_{n}i\leq j\leq n}\left|\mathbb{E}^{(1)}_{\infty}\left(iX_{i}^{(\varepsilon)}jX_{j}^{(\varepsilon)}{\bf 1}_{P_{\infty}(j)/j^{1/(a-1)},P_{\infty}(i)/i^{1/(a-1)}\in[\varepsilon,1/\varepsilon]}\right)\right|=0.

(3.2)

Notice that for
n
large enough, for all
A_{n}i\leq j\leq n
we have
j-i\geq j/2
, so that
(2(j-i))/A_{j-i}\geq i
. Therefore, by Corollary
3.2
with
\varepsilon,n,\delta_{n}
replaced by
\varepsilon/2^{1/(a-1)},j-i,(2/A_{j-i})^{1/(a-1)}/\varepsilon
, we know that for
n
large enough, for all
A_{n}i\leq j\leq n
,

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq x\leq i^{1/(a-1)}/\varepsilon\\
\varepsilon j^{1/(a-1)}\leq y\leq j^{1/(a-1)}/\varepsilon\end{subarray}}\!\left|\mathbb{P}_{\infty}^{(x)}(P_{\infty}(j-i)=y)-\mathbb{P}_{\infty}^{(1)}(P_{\infty}(j-i)=y)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\!\!\!\!\!\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq x\leq(j-i)^{1/(a-1)}(2/A_{j-i})^{1/(a-1)}/\varepsilon\\
(\varepsilon/2^{1/(a-1)})(j-i)^{1/(a-1)}\leq y\leq(2^{1/(a-1)}/\varepsilon)(j-i)^{1/(a-1)}\end{subarray}}\!\!\left|\mathbb{P}_{\infty}^{(x)}(P_{\infty}(j-i)=y)-\mathbb{P}_{\infty}^{(1)}(P_{\infty}(j-i)=y)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon^{a}/j^{1/(a-1)}

and so by the Markov property

\displaystyle{\bf 1}_{P_{\infty}(i)\leq i^{{1}/{(a-1)}}/\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\left|\mathbb{E}^{(1)}_{\infty}\left(\left.jX_{j}^{(\varepsilon)}{\bf 1}_{P_{\infty}(j)\in I(j,\varepsilon)}\right|P_{\infty}(i)\right)-\mathbb{E}^{(1)}_{\infty}\left(\left.jX_{j}^{(\varepsilon)}{\bf 1}_{P_{\infty}(j)\in I(j,\varepsilon)]}\right|P_{\infty}(i)=1\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq{\bf 1}_{P_{\infty}(i)\leq i^{{1}/{(a-1)}}/\varepsilon}\sum_{j^{1/(a-1)}\varepsilon\leq y\leq j^{1/(a-1)}/\varepsilon}\left|\frac{j}{y^{a-1}}-jK_{j}^{(\varepsilon)}\right|\frac{\varepsilon^{a}}{j^{1/(a-1)}}

\displaystyle\leq{\bf 1}_{P_{\infty}(i)\leq i^{{1}/{(a-1)}}/\varepsilon}\left(\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{a-1}}+C\right)\varepsilon^{a-1}.

Moreover, for
n
large enough, for all
A_{n}i\leq j\leq n
,

\left|\mathbb{E}^{(1)}_{\infty}\left(\left.jX_{j}^{(\varepsilon)}{\bf 1}_{P_{\infty}(j)\in[j^{1/(a-1)}\varepsilon,j^{1/(a-1)}/\varepsilon]}\right|P_{\infty}(i)=1\right)\right|\leq\mathbb{E}_{\infty}^{(1)}\left(\left|jX^{(\varepsilon)}_{j-i}\right|\right)\leq 4C.

Furthermore, using the fact that
\mathbb{E}^{(1)}_{\infty}X_{i}^{(\varepsilon)}=0
, we have

\displaystyle\left|\mathbb{E}^{(1)}_{\infty}\left(iX^{(\varepsilon)}_{i}{\bf 1}_{P_{\infty}(i)\in I(i,\varepsilon)}\right)\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\mathbb{E}^{(1)}_{\infty}\left(iX^{(\varepsilon)}_{i}{\bf 1}_{P_{\infty}(i)\not\in I(i,\varepsilon)}\right)\right|

\displaystyle=iK_{i}^{(\varepsilon)}\mathbb{P}^{(1)}_{\infty}\left(P_{\infty}(i)\not\in I(i,\varepsilon)\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\mathbb{P}^{(1)}_{\infty}\left(\Upsilon^{\uparrow}_{a}(p_{\bf q})\not\in[\varepsilon,1/\varepsilon]\right),

for some constant
C>0
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\Big|\mathbb{E}_{\infty}^{(1)}

\displaystyle\left(iX_{i}^{(\varepsilon)}jX_{j}^{(\varepsilon)}{\bf 1}_{P_{\infty}(i)/i^{1/(a-1)},P_{\infty}(j)/j^{1/(a-1)}\in[\varepsilon,1/\varepsilon]}\right)\Big|

\displaystyle\leq C\mathbb{P}^{(1)}_{\infty}\left(\Upsilon^{\uparrow}_{a}(p_{\bf q})\not\in[\varepsilon,1/\varepsilon]\right)(4C+(1/\varepsilon^{a-1}+C)\varepsilon^{a-1}),

which converges towards zero as
\varepsilon\to 0
.
∎

3.2
Number of faces in the geodesics

We are now well equipped to deal with the proof of Theorem

3.2.1
Dilute case

Proof of Theorem
1.1
in the dilute case.

For all
i\geq 0
, let
f_{i}
be the face of degree
2\Delta P_{\infty}(i)+2
which is discovered at time
i
of the exploration when
\Delta P_{\infty}(i)\geq 0
. By (
2.5
), we know that conditionally on
P_{\infty}
, the
X_{i}\coloneqq{\bf 1}_{f_{i}\in\Gamma_{\lfloor nt\rfloor}}
’s for
i\in[\![0,\lfloor nt\rfloor-1\rfloor]\!]
are independent Bernoulli random variables of parameters
\theta_{i}
.

Let us start with the case
a\in(2,5/2]
. Notice that it suffices to check that

\left(n^{-\frac{a-2}{a-1}}\sum_{i=0}^{\lfloor nt\rfloor}\theta_{i}\right)_{t\geq 0}\mathop{\longrightarrow}\limits_{n\to\infty}^{(\mathrm{d})}\left(\frac{{e}_{\bf q}}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{ds}{\Upsilon_{a}^{\uparrow}(p_{\bf q}s)}\right)_{t\geq 0}

(3.3)

for the topology of uniform convergence on compact sets, which is equivalent to the convergence in terms of finite dimensional distributions since the limiting process is a continuous increasing process.

Indeed, if we suppose (
3.3
), then we deduce that for all
t\geq 0
,

\mathbb{E}^{(1)}_{\infty}\left(\left.\left(n^{-\frac{a-2}{a-1}}\sum_{i=0}^{\lfloor nt\rfloor}(X_{i}-\theta_{i})\right)^{2}\ \right|P_{\infty}\right)=n^{-2\frac{a-2}{a-1}}\sum_{i=0}^{\lfloor nt\rfloor}(\theta_{i}-\theta_{i}^{2})\leq n^{-2\frac{a-2}{a-1}}\sum_{i=0}^{\lfloor nt\rfloor}\theta_{i}\mathop{\longrightarrow}\limits_{n\to\infty}^{(\mathbb{P})}0.

In order to prove (
3.3
), it suffices to show that

\left(n^{-\frac{a-2}{a-1}}\sum_{i=0}^{\lfloor nt\rfloor}\mathbb{E}^{(1)}_{\infty}(\theta_{i}|P_{\infty}(i))\right)_{t\geq 0}\mathop{\longrightarrow}\limits_{n\to\infty}^{(\mathrm{d})}\left(\frac{{e}_{\bf q}}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{ds}{\Upsilon_{a}^{\uparrow}(p_{\bf q}s)}\right)_{t\geq 0}.

(3.4)

Indeed, by a straightforward computation, for all
t\geq 0
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{(1)}_{\infty}\left(\left(\sum_{i=0}^{\lfloor nt\rfloor}\left(\theta_{i}-\mathbb{E}^{(1)}_{\infty}(\theta_{i}|P_{\infty}(i))\right)\right)^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{\lfloor nt\rfloor}\mathbb{E}^{(1)}_{\infty}\left(\left(\theta_{i}-\mathbb{E}^{(1)}_{\infty}(\theta_{i}|P_{\infty}(i))\right)^{2}\right)\leq\sum_{i=0}^{\lfloor nt\rfloor}\mathbb{E}^{(1)}_{\infty}\left(\theta_{i}^{2}\right),

and we have
\mathbb{E}^{(1)}_{\infty}\left(\theta_{i}^{2}\right)=O(i^{-1/(a-1)})
due to the fact that, thanks to (
2.4
) and since
h^{\uparrow}(p)\sim 2\sqrt{p/\pi}
as
p\to\infty
,

\mathbb{E}^{(1)}_{\infty}(\theta_{i}^{2}|P_{\infty}(i)=p)=\sum_{k\geq 0}\left(\frac{2k+1}{2(p+k)}\right)^{2}\frac{h^{\uparrow}(p+k)}{h^{\uparrow}(p)}\nu(k)=O\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{p}}\sum_{k\geq 0}\frac{k^{2-a}}{(p+k)^{3/2}}\right)=O\left(\frac{1}{p^{a-1}}\right)

(3.5)

and to the fact that
a-1>1
and
\mathbb{E}^{(1)}_{\infty}(1/P_{\infty}(i))=O(i^{-1/(a-1)})
by Equation (18) of
[
BC17
]
.
As a result,

n^{-2\frac{a-2}{a-1}}\mathbb{E}^{(1)}_{\infty}\left(\left(\sum_{i=0}^{\lfloor nt\rfloor}\left(\theta_{i}-\mathbb{E}^{(1)}_{\infty}(\theta_{i}|P_{\infty}(i))\right)\right)^{2}\right)=O\left(n^{-\frac{a-2}{a-1}}\right)\mathop{\longrightarrow}\limits_{n\to\infty}0,

(3.6)

so that (
3.4
) implies (
3.3
).

Now, since
h^{\uparrow}(p)\sim 2\sqrt{p/\pi}
as
p\to\infty
and since
{p}{h^{\uparrow}(p+k)}/(h^{\uparrow}(p)({p+k}))
is bounded from above by a constant, we have

p\mathbb{E}^{(1)}_{\infty}(\theta_{i}|P_{\infty}(i)=p)=\sum_{k\geq 0}\frac{2k+1}{2}\nu(k)\frac{p}{p+k}\frac{h^{\uparrow}(p+k)}{h^{\uparrow}(p)}\mathop{\longrightarrow}\limits_{p\to\infty}\frac{e_{\bf q}}{2}.

(3.7)

Therefore, by (
3.1
), to show (
3.4
) it suffices to see that

n^{-\frac{a-2}{a-1}}\sum_{i=0}^{\lfloor nt\rfloor}\frac{1}{P_{\infty}(i)}\mathop{\longrightarrow}\limits_{n\to\infty}^{(\mathrm{d})}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{\Upsilon_{a}^{\uparrow}(p_{\bf q}s)}ds,

which is a consequence of the end of the proof of Proposition 4.1 in
[
BC17
]
.
∎

3.2.2
Critical case

Proof of Theorem
1.1
in the case
a=2
.

By the same reasoning as in the dilute case, it is enough to prove that

\frac{1}{(\log n)^{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\theta_{i}\mathop{\longrightarrow}\limits_{n\to\infty}^{(\mathrm{L}^{1})}\frac{1}{\pi^{2}}.

(3.8)

Let us first show that

\frac{1}{(\log n)^{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}^{(1)}_{\infty}(\theta_{i}|P_{\infty}(i))\mathop{\longrightarrow}\limits_{n\to\infty}^{(\mathrm{L}^{1})}\frac{1}{\pi^{2}}.

(3.9)

For all
i\geq 1
, we have

\mathbb{E}^{(1)}_{\infty}(\theta_{i}|P_{\infty}(i))=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{2k+1}{2(P_{\infty}(i)+k)}\frac{h^{\uparrow}(P_{\infty}(i)+k)}{h^{\uparrow}(P_{\infty}(i))}\nu(k).

(3.10)

So, by (
2.4
) and the fact that
h^{\uparrow}(p)\sim 2\sqrt{p/\pi}
, as
p\to\infty
, the expectation
\mathbb{E}^{(1)}_{\infty}(\theta_{i}|P_{\infty}(i)=p)
is bounded from above by

O\left(\frac{1}{p}\right)+O\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k\sqrt{p(p+k)}}\right)=O\left(\frac{1}{p}\right)+O\left(\frac{1}{p}\int_{1/p}^{\infty}\frac{1}{x\sqrt{1+x}}dx\right)=O\left(\frac{\log p}{p}\right).

Moreover, by the end of the proof of Proposition 5 in
[
BCM18
]
, we know that the sequence
((i\log P_{\infty}(i))/(P_{\infty}(i)\log i))_{i\geq 2}
is uniformly integrable, so that
((i/\log i)\mathbb{E}^{(1)}_{\infty}(\theta_{i}|P_{\infty}(i)))_{i\geq 1}
is also uniformly integrable.
Furthermore, using (
2.4
), (
3.1
) and the fact that
h^{\uparrow}(p)\sim 2\sqrt{p/\pi}
as
p\to\infty
, it is easy to see that
\mathbb{E}^{(1)}_{\infty}(\theta_{i}|P_{\infty}(i))
is equivalent in probability to

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{p_{\bf q}}{k\sqrt{P_{\infty}(i)(P_{\infty}(i)+k)}}\mathop{\sim}\limits_{i\to\infty}\frac{1}{P_{\infty}(i)}\int_{1/P_{\infty}(i)}^{\infty}\frac{p_{\bf q}}{u\sqrt{1+u}}du\mathop{\sim}\limits_{i\to\infty}\frac{p_{\bf q}\log P_{\infty}(i)}{P_{\infty}(i)}\mathop{\sim}\limits_{i\to\infty}\frac{p_{\bf q}\log i}{P_{\infty}(i)}.

Thus, by (
3.1
),

\frac{i\log P_{\infty}(i)}{P_{\infty}(i)\log i}-\frac{i}{\log i}\mathbb{E}^{(1)}_{\infty}(\theta_{i}|P_{\infty}(i))\mathop{\longrightarrow}\limits_{i\to\infty}^{(\mathrm{L}^{1})}0.

As a consequence, in order to show (
3.9
) it suffices to prove that

\frac{1}{(\log n)^{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{p_{\bf q}\log i}{P_{\infty}(i)}\mathop{\longrightarrow}\limits_{n\to\infty}^{(\mathrm{L}^{1})}\frac{1}{\pi^{2}}.

By Remark
3.4
(or by Remark 3 of
[
BCM18
]
for this precise case), we know that

\mathbb{E}_{\infty}^{(1)}\frac{i}{P_{\infty}(i)}\mathop{\longrightarrow}\limits_{i\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\frac{1}{\Upsilon^{\uparrow}_{2}(p_{\bf q})}=\mathbb{E}\frac{1}{p_{\bf q}\Upsilon^{\uparrow}_{2}(1)}=\frac{2}{\pi^{2}p_{\bf q}}.

(3.11)

Hence, since
\sum_{i=1}^{n}(\log i)/i\sim(\log n)^{2}/2
as
n\to\infty
, to show (
3.9
) it remains to check that

\frac{1}{(\log n)^{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(\log i)\left(\frac{1}{P_{\infty}(i)}-\mathbb{E}^{(1)}_{\infty}\left(\frac{1}{P_{\infty}(i)}\right)\right)\mathop{\longrightarrow}\limits_{n\to\infty}^{(\mathrm{L}^{1})}0.

(3.12)

We prove (
3.12
) using the same techniques as in the proof of Proposition 3 in
[
BCM18
]
. For all
i\geq 1
and
\varepsilon>0
, as in
[
BCM18
]
, we set

\displaystyle X_{i}^{(\varepsilon)}

\displaystyle\coloneqq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{P_{\infty}(i)}{\bf 1}_{\varepsilon\leq P_{\infty}(i)/i\leq 1/\varepsilon}-\mathbb{E}^{(1)}_{\infty}\left(\frac{1}{P_{\infty}(i)}{\bf 1}_{\varepsilon\leq P_{\infty}(i)/i\leq 1/\varepsilon}\right)

\displaystyle Y_{i}^{(\varepsilon)}

\displaystyle\coloneqq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{P_{\infty}(i)}{\bf 1}_{P_{\infty}(i)/i\not\in[\varepsilon,1/\varepsilon]}-\mathbb{E}^{(1)}_{\infty}\left(\frac{1}{P_{\infty}(i)}{\bf 1}_{P_{\infty}(i)/i\not\in[\varepsilon,1/\varepsilon]}\right).

Then, to prove (
3.12
) it is sufficient to show that

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\limsup_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}^{(1)}_{\infty}\left(\left(\frac{1}{(\log n)^{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(\log i)X^{(\varepsilon)}_{i}\right)^{2}\right)=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\limsup_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}^{(1)}_{\infty}\left|\frac{1}{(\log n)^{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(\log i)Y^{(\varepsilon)}_{i}\right|=0

(3.13)

The second term is zero since

\mathbb{E}^{(1)}_{\infty}\left|iY^{(\varepsilon)}_{i}\right|\leq 2\mathbb{E}^{(1)}_{\infty}\left(\frac{i}{P_{\infty}(i)}{\bf 1}_{P_{\infty}(i)/i\not\in[\varepsilon,1/\varepsilon]}\right)

and since

is uniformly integrable by Lemma
3.3
and is tight in
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{*}
by (
3.1
).
For the first term of (
3.13
), let
A_{n}
be an increasing sequence tending to infinity such that

. We compute

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\mathbb{E}^{(1)}_{\infty}\left(\left(\frac{1}{(\log n)^{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(\log i)X^{(\varepsilon)}_{i}\right)^{2}\right)\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(\log n)^{4}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(\log i)^{2}\mathbb{E}^{(1)}_{\infty}\left(\left(X^{(\varepsilon)}_{i}\right)^{2}\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{2}{(\log n)^{4}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=i+1}^{A_{n}i}(\log i)(\log j)\left|\mathbb{E}^{(1)}_{\infty}\left(X^{(\varepsilon)}_{i}X^{(\varepsilon)}_{j}\right)\right|

\displaystyle+\frac{2}{(\log n)^{4}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=A_{n}i+1}^{n}(\log i)(\log j)\left|\mathbb{E}^{(1)}_{\infty}\left(X^{(\varepsilon)}_{i}X^{(\varepsilon)}_{j}\right)\right|.

The quantity on the right of the first line (

) is a
O(1/(\log n)^{4})
as

. This proves the convergence (

), hence (

) and thus the convergence (

(3.17)

To prove (

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{(1)}_{\infty}\left(\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\theta_{i}-\mathbb{E}^{(1)}_{\infty}(\theta_{i}|P_{\infty}(i))\right)\right)^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}^{(1)}_{\infty}\left(\left(\theta_{i}-\mathbb{E}^{(1)}_{\infty}(\theta_{i}|P_{\infty}(i))\right)^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\mathbb{E}^{(1)}_{\infty}\left(\theta_{i}^{2}\right)-\mathbb{E}^{(1)}_{\infty}\left(\mathbb{E}^{(1)}_{\infty}(\theta_{i}|P_{\infty}(i))^{2}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}^{(1)}_{\infty}\left(\theta_{i}^{2}\right)

(3.18)

\displaystyle=O(\log n),

where the last line comes from the fact that, by (
2.4
) and the fact that
h^{\uparrow}(p)\sim 2\sqrt{p/\pi}
, as

,

\mathbb{E}^{(1)}_{\infty}(\theta_{i}^{2}|P_{\infty}(i)=p)=\sum_{k\geq 0}\left(\frac{2k+1}{2(p+k)}\right)^{2}\frac{h^{\uparrow}(p+k)}{h^{\uparrow}(p)}\nu(k)=O\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{p}}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(p+k)^{3/2}}\right)=O\left(\frac{1}{p}\right),

and from the fact that
\mathbb{E}^{(1)}_{\infty}(1/P_{\infty}(i))=O(1/i)
as

3.2.3

Proof of Theorem
1.1
in the dense case.

For the dense case

, the proof is analogous to the case
a=2
. By the same reasoning as in the dilute case, it is enough to prove that

\frac{1}{\log n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\theta_{i}\mathop{\longrightarrow}\limits_{n\to\infty}^{(\mathrm{L}^{1})}\frac{2}{\pi\tan((2-a)\pi)}.

(3.19)

Let us first show that

3.3

We apply our results on the fpp geodesics to show Corollary

Proof of Corollary
1.2
.

Let
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
. We reason conditionally on the perimeter process
P_{\infty}
. Recall from Subsection
2.1
that
(\overline{\mathfrak{e}}_{n})_{n\geq 0}
is the uniform exploration of the map. Let

\displaystyle\leq 4P_{\infty}(n)\exp\left(-c\varepsilon^{2}\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\frac{1}{2P_{\infty}(i)+1}\right)

large enough we have

The proof relies on the martingales introduced in

\mathfrak{m}_{\square}
is a planar map with a distinguished face
\square

\displaystyle\leq\ell^{a}\mathbb{P}^{(\ell)}\left(d^{\dagger}_{\mathrm{gr}}(f_{r},E)\geq C_{\bf q}\log\ell\right)+\mathbb{P}^{(\ell)}\left(\#\mathrm{Edges}(\mathfrak{m})\geq\ell^{a}\right)\mathop{\longrightarrow}\limits_{\ell\to\infty}0,

where the convergence comes from (

) and from the fact that

converges in distribution under

as

by Proposition 10.4 of
[

]
.
In order to prove (

), one can see that by unzipping the uniform random edge, using Equation (3.4) of
[

]
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}^{(\ell)}\left(d^{\dagger}_{\mathrm{gr}}(f_{r},E)\geq C_{\bf q}\log\ell\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{W_{1}^{(\ell)}}{W^{(\ell)}}\mathbb{E}^{(\ell)}_{1}\left(\frac{1}{\#\mathrm{Edges}(\mathfrak{m}_{\square})-1}{\bf 1}_{d^{\dagger}_{\mathrm{gr}}(f_{r},\square)\geq C_{\bf q}\log\ell}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{W_{1}^{(\ell)}}{W^{(\ell)}}\mathbb{P}^{(\ell)}_{1}\left(d^{\dagger}_{\mathrm{gr}}(f_{r},\square)\geq C_{\bf q}\log\ell\right)

\displaystyle=O(\ell^{a-1/2})\mathbb{P}^{(1)}_{\ell}\left(d^{\dagger}_{\mathrm{gr}}(f_{r},\square)\geq C_{\bf q}\log\ell\right),

where in the last line, we used that
W^{(\ell)}_{1}/W^{(\ell)}=\mathbb{E}^{(\ell)}\#\mathrm{Edges}(\mathfrak{m})=O(\ell^{a-1/2})
by Proposition 10.4 and Equation (10.8) of
[
Cur23
]
. Note also that in the last line, we have exchanged the root and the target face. Thus, it is enough to show that

\mathbb{P}^{(1)}_{\ell}\left(d^{\dagger}_{\mathrm{gr}}(f_{r},\square)\geq C_{\bf q}\log\ell\right)=O(\ell^{-4}).

(3.36)

By the reversed uniform exploration, we know that the fpp geodesic from
f_{r}
to
\square
has

\sum_{i=0}^{\tau_{-\ell}-1}X_{i}

faces, where the
X_{i}
’s are independent from the perimeter process and are independent Bernoulli random variables of parameters
{\bf 1}_{\Delta P_{\ell}(i)\geq 0}(2\Delta P_{\ell}(i)+1)/(2P_{\ell}(i+1))
by (

), hence

Let
\lambda>0
satisfying the hypothesis of Lemma

and such that
\lambda/C^{\prime}_{\bf q}<1
, where

appears in Lemma

. Then, applying successively Markov’s inequality, the fact that

and Cauchy-Schwarz inequality,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}^{(1)}_{\ell}\left(\sum_{i=0}^{\tau_{-\ell}-1}X_{i}\geq C_{\bf q}\log\ell\right)

\displaystyle\leq\ell^{-\lambda C_{\bf q}/(2C^{\prime}_{\bf q})}\mathbb{E}^{(1)}_{\ell}\exp\left(\frac{\lambda}{2C^{\prime}_{\bf q}}\sum_{i=0}^{\tau_{-\ell}-1}X_{i}\right)

\displaystyle=\ell^{-\lambda C_{\bf q}/(2C^{\prime}_{\bf q})}\mathbb{E}^{(1)}_{\ell}\sqrt{M^{(\ell,\lambda/C^{\prime}_{\bf q})}_{\tau_{-\ell}}\widetilde{M}^{(\ell,\lambda)}_{\tau_{-\ell}}}

\displaystyle\leq\ell^{-\lambda C_{\bf q}/(2C^{\prime}_{\bf q})}\sqrt{\mathbb{E}^{(1)}_{\ell}\left(M^{(\ell,\lambda/C^{\prime}_{\bf q})}_{\tau_{-\ell}}\right)\mathbb{E}^{(1)}_{\ell}\left(\widetilde{M}^{(\ell,\lambda)}_{\tau_{-\ell}}\right)}

\displaystyle\leq\ell^{-\lambda C_{\bf q}/(2C^{\prime}_{\bf q})}\sqrt{\mathbb{E}^{(1)}_{\ell}\left(M^{(\ell,\lambda/C^{\prime}_{\bf q})}_{0}\right)\mathbb{E}^{(1)}_{\ell}\left(\widetilde{M}^{(\ell,\lambda)}_{0}\right)},

where the last inequality comes from Lemmas
3.6
and
3.7
and from the fact that

is a stopping time for the filtrations

and

. Therefore,

This proves (

The self-similar Markov process arising as the scaling limit of the perimeter process

4.2
A family of coalescing diffusions with jumps

We denote by
\{x\}\coloneqq x-\lfloor x\rfloor
the fractional part and by

the positive part of a real number
x
. For every integer

the set of continuous non-negative bounded functions

which are zero on a neighbourhood of zero.
For all

g(x,z,u)=\left(\{x-u\}-\left(\frac{z-1}{z}\right)_{+}\right)_{+}z-\{x-u\}+\frac{1}{2}\left(1-\left(\frac{1}{z}\wedge z\right)\right).

\int_{0}^{1}g(x,z,u)du=0.

(4.6)

(4.7)

\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(y)K(x,dy)=2c_{a}p_{\bf q}\int_{0}^{\infty}\lambda(dz)\int_{0}^{1}duf(g(x,z,u)).

See e.g.
[

The existence and uniqueness (up to undistinguishability) of strong solutions to (

Proof.

In the case

•

(4.8)

•

\int_{(0,\infty)\setminus\{1\}}\lambda(dz)\int_{[0,1]}dug(x,z,u)^{2}\leq 9\int_{(1/2,1)\cup(1,\infty)}(z-1)^{2}\lambda(dz)<\infty

\displaystyle+2\int_{0}^{\infty}\lambda(dz)\int_{0}^{1}du\left|\left(\left(\frac{z-1}{z}\right)_{+}-\{x-u\}\right)_{+}-\left(\left(\frac{z-1}{z}\right)_{+}-\{y-u\}\right)_{+}\right|^{2}.

4.3
The discrete coalescing flow of fpp geodesics

Recall from Subsection
4.1
that
(\widetilde{P}(t))_{t\geq 0}
is the continuous time Markov process constructed from

under
\mathbb{P}^{(\ell)}
where at each step

we wait a time
\mathcal{E}_{n}/{(2P(n)^{a-1})}
. For every time
t\geq 0
of jump of
\widetilde{P}
we sample a random variable
V^{(\ell)}_{t}
which is uniform in
\{0,1/(2\widetilde{P}(t)),\ldots,(2\widetilde{P}(t)-1)/(2\widetilde{P}(t))\}
. This random variable corresponds to the position where we glue a new face or where we insert edges in the reversed uniform exploration.
For all
t\geq 0
, we set

R^{(\ell)}_{t}=-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq s<t\\
\Delta\widetilde{P}(s)\neq 0\end{subarray}}\left(V^{(\ell)}_{s}+\frac{1}{2}\max\left(1-\frac{\widetilde{P}(s-)}{\widetilde{P}(s)},\left(1-\frac{1}{2\widetilde{P}(s)}\right)\left(1-\frac{\widetilde{P}(s)}{\widetilde{P}(s-)}\right)\right)\right).

The quantity
R^{(\ell)}_{t}
is introduced for technical reasons in order to compensate the jumps in the processes which are defined below. For every time
t\geq 0
of jump of
\widetilde{P}
, we write
U^{(\ell)}_{t}\coloneqq\{V^{(\ell)}_{t}+R^{(\ell)}_{t}\}
. Let
T>0
.
We define the random measure
N^{(\ell),T}
by

N^{(\ell),T}=\sum_{0\leq t\leq T;\ \Delta\widetilde{P}(t)>0}\delta_{(T-t,\widetilde{P}(t)/\widetilde{P}(t-),U^{(\ell)}_{t})}.

The measure
N^{(\ell),T}
gives the (translated) position at which we glue the face or insert edges and also the degree of the glued face or the number of inserted edges via the jump of
\widetilde{P}
.

For all
t\leq 0
, for all
z\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{*}
, let

\displaystyle g_{t}^{(\ell)}(x,z,u)=

\displaystyle\left(\{x-u\}-\left(\frac{z-1}{z}\right)_{+}\right)_{+}z-\{x-u\}+\frac{1}{2}\max\left(1-\frac{1}{z},\left(1-\frac{1}{2\widetilde{P}(-t)}\right)(1-z)\right)

and for all
t\in[0,T]
, let
g^{(\ell),T}_{t}=g^{(\ell)}_{t-T}
. Note that
g
is
1
-periodic in
x
. By construction, note that, by a simple computation, for all
t\in[0,T]
such that
\Delta\widetilde{P}(T-t)\neq 0
, for all
z\in\mathbb{R}^{*}_{+},x\in\mathbb{R}
such that
\widetilde{P}(T-t)/z\in\mathbb{Z}
and
2\widetilde{P}(T-t)(x-R^{(\ell)}_{T-t})\in\mathbb{Z}
, we have

\mathbb{E}^{(\ell)}\left(\left.g_{t}^{(\ell),T}(x,z,U^{(\ell)}_{T-t})\right|\widetilde{P}\right)=0.

(4.16)

Indeed, if we write
p=\widetilde{P}(T-t)
and

then, when
k\geq 0
(i.e.
z\geq 1
), we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{(\ell)}\left(\left.\left(\{x-U^{(\ell)}_{T-t}\}-\frac{z-1}{z}\right)_{+}z-\{x-U^{(\ell)}_{T-t}\}\right|\widetilde{P}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2p}\sum_{i=0}^{2p-1}\left(\left(\frac{i}{2p}-\frac{k}{p}\right)_{+}\frac{p}{p-k}-\frac{i}{2p}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{p-k}{p}-1\right)=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{1}{z}-1\right),

and a similar calculation proves (
4.16
) when
k\leq 0
(i.e.
z\leq 1
).
Furthermore, we again have for all
x\in\mathbb{R},z\in\mathbb{R}^{*}_{+},u\in[0,1]
,

g_{t}^{(\ell),T}(x,z,u)\leq 3|z-1|.

(4.17)

The coalescing flow
(X^{(\ell),T}_{t}(v))_{v\in\mathbb{R},0\leq t\leq T}
associated with
N^{(\ell),T}
is defined by

\forall v\in\mathbb{R},\forall t\in[0,T],\qquad X^{(\ell),T}_{t}(v)=v+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{1}g_{s}^{(\ell),T}(X^{(\ell)}_{s-}(v),z,u)N^{(\ell),T}(ds,dz,du).

(4.18)

Notice that the above process is not a diffusion with jumps since the Markov process
\widetilde{P}
is not reversible. This coalescing flow gives the genealogy of the fpp geodesics to the root face under
\mathbb{P}^{(\ell)}
and, when
a=2
, it is parametrized by the fpp distance.

Let us state the following useful lemma which is a direct consequence of (
4.16
).

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\mathcal{F}^{(\ell),T}
be the filtration defined by

\forall t\in[0,T],\qquad\mathcal{F}^{(\ell),T}_{t}=\sigma\left(\widetilde{P}((T-s)-),U_{T-s}^{(\ell)};\ s\in[0,t]\right).

Then for all
\varepsilon>0
, for all
v\in\mathbb{R}
, the process
(M^{(\ell),T,\varepsilon}_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
defined by

\forall t\in[0,T],\qquad M^{(\ell),T,\varepsilon}_{t}=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{1-\varepsilon}^{1+\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{1}g_{s}^{(\ell),T}(X^{(\ell),T}_{s-}(v),z,u)N^{(\ell),T}(ds,dz,du)

is a martingale with respect to the filtration
\mathcal{F}^{(\ell),T}
.

Finally, let us state a lemma which controls the small jumps of
\widetilde{P}
.

Lemma 4.3
.

We have the uniform convergence

\sup_{\ell\geq 1}\mathbb{E}^{(\ell)}\left(\sum_{s\in[0,T]}{\bf 1}_{\widetilde{P}(s)/\widetilde{P}(s-)\in[1-\varepsilon,1+\varepsilon]\setminus\{1\}}\left(\frac{\Delta\widetilde{P}(s)}{\widetilde{P}(s-)}\right)^{2}\right)\mathop{\longrightarrow}\limits_{\varepsilon\to 0}0.

Proof.

Using the law of the peeling exploration described in Subsection
2.2
, one can bound from above

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{(\ell)}

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{s\in[0,T]}{\bf 1}_{\widetilde{P}(s)/\widetilde{P}(s-)\in[1-\varepsilon,1+\varepsilon]\setminus\{1\}}\left(\frac{\Delta\widetilde{P}(s)}{\widetilde{P}(s-)}\right)^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\mathbb{E}^{(\ell)}\left(\sum_{s\in[0,T]}{\bf 1}_{\frac{\widetilde{P}(s)}{\widetilde{P}(s-)}\neq 1}\sum_{-\varepsilon\widetilde{P}(s-)\leq k\leq\varepsilon\widetilde{P}(s-)}\frac{W^{(\widetilde{P}(s-)+k)}c_{\bf q}^{-\widetilde{P}(s-)-k}}{W^{(\widetilde{P}(s-))}c_{\bf q}^{-\widetilde{P}(s-)}}\nu(k)\left(\frac{k}{\widetilde{P}(s-)}\right)^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C^{\prime}\mathbb{E}^{(\ell)}\left(\sum_{s\in[0,T]}{\bf 1}_{\frac{\widetilde{P}(s)}{\widetilde{P}(s-)}\neq 1}\sum_{1\leq k\leq\varepsilon\widetilde{P}(s-)}k^{-a}\left(\frac{k}{\widetilde{P}(s-)}\right)^{2}\right)\qquad\text{by \eqref{eq asymptotique W} and \eqref{queue nu}}

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon^{3-a}C^{\prime\prime}\mathbb{E}^{(\ell)}\left(\sum_{s\in[0,T]}{\bf 1}_{\frac{\widetilde{P}(s)}{\widetilde{P}(s-)}\neq 1}\frac{1}{\widetilde{P}(s-)^{a-1}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon^{3-a}C^{\prime\prime\prime},

where
C,C^{\prime},C^{\prime\prime},C^{\prime\prime\prime}
are positive constants which do not depend on
\ell,\varepsilon
. The last inequality comes from the fact that if we denote the exponential clocks defining
\widetilde{P}
by
\mathcal{E}_{n}/(2P(n)^{a-1})
for all integer
n\geq 0
, where the
\mathcal{E}_{n}
for
n\geq 0
are i.i.d. exponential random variables of parameter
1
independent from
P
, then

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{(\ell)}\left(\sum_{s\in[0,T]}{\bf 1}_{\frac{\widetilde{P}(s)}{\widetilde{P}(s-)}\neq 1}\frac{1}{\widetilde{P}(s-)^{a-1}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}^{(\ell)}\left(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{P(n)^{a-1}}{\bf 1}_{\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\mathcal{E}_{k}/(2P(k)^{a-1})\leq T}\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}^{(\ell)}\left(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\mathcal{E}_{n}}{P(n)^{a-1}}{\bf 1}_{\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\mathcal{E}_{k}/(2P(k)^{a-1})\leq T}\right)

\displaystyle\leq 2T+1.

This ends the proof of the lemma.
∎

4.5
Scaling limit of the flow of geodesics

In this subsection, we prove that the discrete coalescing flow of Subsection
4.3
converges to the family of coalescing diffusions with jumps introduced in Subsection
4.2
if one looks at a finite (or countable) number of trajectories. Let
T>0
. Let us introduce the restriction
N^{T}
of
N
to subsets of
[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{*}_{+}\times[0,1]
.

Theorem 4.4
.

For all
\ell\geq 1
, let
v_{1}^{(\ell)},v_{2}^{(\ell)},\ldots,v_{d}^{(\ell)}\in\mathbb{R}
and let
v_{1},\ldots,v_{d}\in\mathbb{R}
such that for all

v^{(\ell)}_{i}\longrightarrow v_{i}
as
\ell\to\infty
. Then

\left(X^{(\ell),T}_{t}(v^{(\ell)}_{i})\right)_{1\leq i\leq d,t\in[0,T]}\mathop{\longrightarrow}\limits_{\ell\to\infty}^{(\mathrm{d})}(X_{t}(v_{i}))_{1\leq i\leq d,0\leq t\leq T},

in the space
\mathbb{D}([0,T],\mathbb{R})^{d}
, where each coordinate is equipped with the
J_{1}
Skorokhod topology. Moreover, jointly with the above convergence,
N^{(\ell),T}
converges in distribution towards
N^{T}
for the topology of weak convergence of measures on
[0,T]\times((0,1)\cup(1,\infty))\times[0,1]
.

In order to prove the above result, we denote by
N^{T}_{\varepsilon}
(resp.
N_{\varepsilon}
) the restriction of
N
to subsets of
[0,T]\times(\mathbb{R}^{*}_{+}\setminus(1-\varepsilon,1+\varepsilon))\times[0,1]
(resp.
\mathbb{R}_{+}\times(\mathbb{R}^{*}_{+}\setminus(1-\varepsilon,1+\varepsilon))\times[0,1]
). Note that the Poisson point process
N^{T}_{\varepsilon}
has a finite intensity. Similarly, for all
\ell\geq 1
, we denote by
N^{(\ell),T}_{\varepsilon}
the restriction of
N^{(\ell),T}
to the subsets of
[0,T]\times(\mathbb{R}^{*}_{+}\setminus(1-\varepsilon,1+\varepsilon))\times[0,1]
.

Let
X^{(\ell),T,\varepsilon}
be the approximation of the discrete coalescing flow defined using

by

\forall v\in\mathbb{R},\forall t\in[0,T],\qquad X^{(\ell),T,\varepsilon}_{t}(v)=v+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{1}g_{s}^{(\ell),T}(X^{(\ell),T,\varepsilon}_{s-}(v),z,u)N^{(\ell),T}_{\varepsilon}(ds,dz,du),

and let
X^{\varepsilon}
be the process defined using
N_{\varepsilon}
by

\forall v\in\mathbb{R},\forall t\in\mathbb{R}_{+},\qquad X^{\varepsilon}_{t}(v)=v+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{1}g(X^{\varepsilon}_{s-}(v),z,u)N_{\varepsilon}(ds,dz,du).

The above integral is actually a finite sum since
\lambda(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{*}\setminus(1-\varepsilon,1+\varepsilon))<\infty
. Moreover, the integral with respect to the compensated PPP would be the same thanks to (
4.6
). We define
X^{T,\varepsilon}
as the restriction of
X^{\varepsilon}
to the time interval
[0,T]
. Note that
X^{T,\varepsilon}
can also be defined using
N^{T}_{\varepsilon}
.

By (
4.4
), we get the convergence of
g^{(\ell),T}_{t}
towards
g
:

\text{Under }\mathbb{P}^{(\ell)},\qquad\sup_{t\in[0,T],z\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{*},u\in[0,1],x\in\mathbb{R}}\left|g^{(\ell),T}_{t}(x,z,u)-g(x,z,u)\right|\leq\frac{|1-z|}{\inf_{t\in[0,T]}\widetilde{P}(t)}\mathop{\longrightarrow}\limits_{\ell\to\infty}^{(\mathbb{P})}0.

(4.19)

We can then state the following scaling limit result:

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
d\geq 1
. For all
\ell\geq 1
, let
v_{1}^{(\ell)},v_{2}^{(\ell)},\ldots,v_{d}^{(\ell)}\in\mathbb{R}
and let
v_{1},\ldots,v_{d}\in\mathbb{R}
such that for all

, we have

v^{(\ell)}_{i}\longrightarrow v_{i}

.
Then

\left(X^{(\ell),T,\varepsilon}_{t}(v^{(\ell)}_{i})\right)_{1\leq i\leq d,t\in[0,T]}\mathop{\longrightarrow}\limits_{\ell\to\infty}^{(\mathrm{d})}(X^{T,\varepsilon}_{t}(v_{i}))_{1\leq i\leq d,0\leq t\leq T},

in the space
\mathbb{D}([0,T],\mathbb{R})^{d}
.

The above lemma is a direct consequence of (
4.19
), of the following lemma and of the fact that
N^{T}_{\varepsilon}
has a finite number of atoms.

Lemma 4.6
.

For every continuous function on
[0,T]\times((0,1)\cup(1,\infty))\times[0,1]
with compact support,

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{(0,1)\cup(1,\infty)}\int_{0}^{1}f(t,z,u)N^{(\ell),T}(dt,dz,du)\mathop{\longrightarrow}\limits_{\ell\to\infty}^{(\mathrm{d})}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{(0,1)\cup(1,\infty)}\int_{0}^{1}f(t,z,u)N^{T}(dt,dz,du).

In other words,

converges in distribution towards

for the topology of weak convergence of measures on
[0,T]\times((0,1)\cup(1,\infty))\times[0,1]

The convergence (
4.4
) implies that

\sum_{t\geq 0;\ \Delta\widetilde{P}(t)>0}\delta_{(t,\widetilde{P}(t)/\widetilde{P}(t-))}\mathop{\longrightarrow}\limits_{\ell\to\infty}^{(\mathrm{d})}\mathrm{PPP}\left(2c_{a}p_{\bf q}dt\lambda(dz)\right),

for the topology of weak convergence of measures on
\mathbb{R}_{+}\times(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{*}\setminus\left\{1\right\})
. This concludes the proof by reversing time and using that
\ell/\inf_{[0,T]}\widetilde{P}
under
\mathbb{P}^{(\ell)}
as

is tight by (

) so that the random variables

converge in distribution towards independent uniform random variables in
[0,1]
.
∎

Next, we can approximate
X^{(\ell),T}
using an appropriate coupling with
X^{(\ell),T,\varepsilon}
.

Lemma 4.7
.

For every sequence of real numbers
(v^{(\ell)})_{\ell\geq 1}
, there exist couplings of
(X^{(\ell),T}_{t}(v^{(\ell)}))_{t\in[0,T]}
with
(X^{(\ell),T,\varepsilon}_{t}(v^{(\ell)}))_{t\in[0,T]}
for all
\ell\geq 1
and for all
\varepsilon>0
, denoted by

such that

\sup_{\ell\geq 1}\mathbb{E}^{(\ell)}\left(\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left|X^{(\ell),T}_{t}(v^{(\ell)})-\overline{X}^{(\ell),T,\varepsilon}_{t}(v^{(\ell)})\right|^{2}\right)\mathop{\longrightarrow}\limits_{\varepsilon\to 0}0.

Proof.

Let
(v^{(\ell)})_{\ell\geq 1}
be a sequence of real numbers. The coupling of the two processes is defined using the same perimeter process
P
, the same exponential random variables for
\widetilde{P}
but the random variables
\overline{U}^{(\ell),T}
defining
\overline{X}^{(\ell),T,\varepsilon}_{t}(v^{(\ell)})

\displaystyle\overline{U}_{T-t}^{(\ell),T,\varepsilon}

\displaystyle=\left\{\overline{X}^{(\ell),T,\varepsilon}_{t-}(v^{(\ell)})-X^{(\ell),T}_{t-}(v^{(\ell)})+U^{(\ell)}_{T-t}\right\},

(4.20)

\displaystyle\overline{N}^{(\ell),T,\varepsilon}_{|[0,t]\times(\mathbb{R}^{*}_{+}\setminus\{1\})\times[0,1]}

\displaystyle=\sum_{s\in[T-t,T];\ \widetilde{P}(s)/\widetilde{P}(s-)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{*}\setminus(1-\varepsilon,1+\varepsilon)}\delta_{(T-s,\widetilde{P}(s)/\widetilde{P}(s-),\overline{U}^{(\ell),T,\varepsilon}_{s})},

(4.21)

\displaystyle\overline{X}^{(\ell),T,\varepsilon}_{t}(v^{(\ell)})

\displaystyle=v+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{1}g^{(\ell),T}_{s}(\overline{X}^{(\ell),T,\varepsilon}_{s-}(v^{(\ell)}),z,u)\overline{N}^{(\ell),T}_{\varepsilon}(ds,dz,du),

(4.22)

for all
t\in[0,T]
such that

. Then one can see that
\overline{X}^{(\ell),T,\varepsilon}(v^{(\ell)})

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{(\ell)}

\displaystyle\left(\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left|X^{(\ell),T}_{t}(v^{(\ell)})-\overline{X}^{(\ell),T,\varepsilon}_{t}(v^{(\ell)})\right|^{2}\right)

\sup_{\ell\geq 1}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\mathbb{E}^{(\ell)}\left((M^{(\ell),T,\varepsilon}_{t})^{2}\right)\mathop{\longrightarrow}\limits_{\varepsilon\to 0}0.

We will need this last lemma:

Lemma 4.8
.

We have for all
v_{1},\ldots,v_{d}\in\mathbb{R}

\left(X_{t}^{\varepsilon}(v_{i})\right)_{1\leq i\leq d,t\geq 0}\mathop{\longrightarrow}\limits_{\varepsilon\to 0}^{(\mathrm{d})}\left(X_{t}(v_{i})\right)_{1\leq i\leq d,t\geq 0}

It is a direct consequence of Theorem 4.15 of Chapter IX of
[

]
. Indeed, for all

, the process
(X_{t}^{\varepsilon}(v_{i}))_{i\in[\![1,d]\!],t\geq 0}
(resp.
(X_{t}^{\varepsilon}(v_{i}))_{i\in[\![1,d]\!],t\geq 0}

(v_{1},\ldots,v_{d})
with kernel

(resp.
K^{d}
) defined by the fact that for all

, for all

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(y)K^{d,\varepsilon}(x,dy)=2c_{a}p_{\bf q}\int_{(0,\infty)\setminus(1-\varepsilon,1+\varepsilon)}\lambda(dz)\int_{0}^{1}duf(g(x_{1},z,u),\ldots,g(x_{d},z,u)),

and respectively

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(y)K^{d}(x,dy)=2c_{a}p_{\bf q}\int_{(0,\infty)\setminus\{1\}}\lambda(dz)\int_{0}^{1}duf(g(x_{1},z,u),\ldots,g(x_{d},z,u)).

the infinite norm on

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|y|_{\infty}^{2}K^{d,\varepsilon}(x,dy)\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|y|_{\infty}^{2}K^{d}(x,dy)\leq 9\int_{(1/2,1)\cup(1,\infty)}\lambda(dz)|z-1|^{2}<\infty,

and

\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|y|_{\infty}^{2}{\bf 1}_{|y|_{\infty}>b}K^{d}(x,dy)\leq 9\int_{(1/2,1)\cup(1,\infty)}\lambda(dz)|z-1|^{2}{\bf 1}_{3|z-1|>b}\mathop{\longrightarrow}\limits_{b\to\infty}0.

for
\lambda(dz)du
almost every

f(g(x_{1},z,u),\ldots,g(x_{d},z,u))\leq{\bf 1}_{3|z-1|>r}\sup(f),

Furthermore, by the Beppo Levi lemma, for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, for all

The space

is equipped with the distance

defined as follows.

5.2
The tree of geodesics via the discrete coalescing flow

We recall the branching peeling exploration of a map
\mathfrak{m}
via discrete cell-systems from
[
BCM18
,
BBCK18
]
. One can then perform the time-reversed uniform exploration on each branch via the discrete coalescing flow thanks to the encoding presented in Subsection
4.4
.

The branching peeling exploration of a map
\mathfrak{m}
, contrary to the filled in peeling exploration, does not fill in the holes which are created when we identify two edges (i.e. when the perimeter process has a negative jump) but instead starts another process which explores this hole. Thanks to the spatial Markov property, under
\mathbb{P}^{(\ell)}
, this new exploration is independent conditionally on the perimeter of the hole and has the same law as the exploration of a Boltzmann map of perimeter corresponding to the perimeter of the hole. In each of these explorations, we use the uniform peeling exploration recalled in Subsection
2.1
.

Under
\mathbb{P}^{(\ell)}
, the perimeter processes of this branching exploration are described by a cell-system
((P_{w}(n))_{n\geq 0})_{w\in\mathbb{U}}
which is defined as follows:
P_{\emptyset}=P
is the perimeter process of the exploration of
\mathfrak{m}
following the locally largest component. Recall that it is a Markov chain whose probability transitions are given by (
2.3
). We define the birth-time of the cell
\emptyset
by
B_{\emptyset}=0
. Then, assume inductively that
(P_{w}(n))_{n\geq 0}
and its birth-time
B_{w}
are defined for some
w\in\mathbb{U}
. Let
\ell_{wi}
for
i\geq 1
be the sizes in absolute value of the negative jumps, ranked in non-increasing order and let
(n_{wi})_{i\geq 1}
be the times at which they occur. For all
i\geq 1
we define
(P_{wi}(n))_{n\geq 0}
as the exploration of the independent Boltzmann map of law
\mathbb{P}^{(\ell_{wi})}
which fills the hole created by the associated negative jump. Then conditionally on the
\ell_{wi}
’s (and on all the branches
P_{w^{\prime}}
for
w^{\prime}\in\mathbb{U}
such that
w
is not a strict prefix of
w^{\prime}
), the processes
(P_{wi}(n))_{n\geq 0}
for
i\geq 1
are independent Markov chains whose transitions are given by (
2.3
). This property is called the branching Markov property. For all
i\geq 1
, birth-time of the cell

is defined by

.

On each branch corresponding to a cell
P_{w}
for

\mathfrak{m}
. Let us denote by

Y^{z,w}
and
Y^{z,w^{\prime}}
coalesce, and since the law of
U^{z}_{-\tau_{zj}}
has no atoms,

\{Y^{z,w}_{-\tau_{zj}-}-U^{z}_{-\tau_{zj}}\}<(Z^{z}_{-\tau_{zj}}-1)/Z^{z}_{-\tau_{zj}}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\{Y^{z,w^{\prime}}_{-\tau_{zj}-}-U^{z}_{-\tau_{zj}}\}<(Z^{z}_{-\tau_{zj}}-1)/Z^{z}_{-\tau_{zj}}.

Thus, by (
5.13
), a.s. for
\ell
large enough,

\{Y^{(\ell),z,w}_{-\tau^{(\ell)}_{zj}-}-U^{(\ell),z}_{\tau^{(\ell)}_{zj}}\}\vee\{Y^{(\ell),z,w^{\prime}}_{-\tau^{(\ell)}_{zj}-}-U^{(\ell),z}_{\tau^{(\ell)}_{zj}}\}\leq\left(\frac{\widetilde{P}^{(\ell)}_{z}(\tau_{zj}^{(\ell)})}{\widetilde{P}^{(\ell)}_{z}(\tau^{(\ell)}_{zj}-)}-1\right)/\left(\frac{\widetilde{P}^{(\ell)}_{z}(\tau_{zj}^{(\ell)})}{\widetilde{P}^{(\ell)}_{z}(\tau^{(\ell)}_{zj}-)}\right).

So, a.s. for
\ell
large enough, the trajectories
Y^{(\ell),z,w},Y^{(\ell),z,w^{\prime}}
coalesce at time
-\tau^{(\ell)}_{zj}
, hence (
5.12
).

Sketch of the end of the proof of Theorem
1.5
in the case
a\in(3/2,2)
: the two trajectories
Y^{z,w}
and
Y^{z,w^{\prime}}
for some
z\prec w,w^{\prime}
in red coalesce due to the jump
\Delta_{c(w,w^{\prime})}
in blue. A neighbourhood of the trajectories is drawn in pink and a neighbourhood of the jump is in light blue.

Next, we deal with the case
a\in[2,5/2)
. If
c(w,w^{\prime})=\emptyset
, then we conclude as before. When
c(w,w^{\prime})\neq\emptyset
, then by definition
c(w,w^{\prime})=(\Delta_{zi_{n}})_{n\geq 0}\in\mathcal{J}_{a}
for some common prefix
z\prec w,w^{\prime}
, where
\tau_{zi_{n}}
is an increasing sequence which converges towards the time
-\sigma_{w,w^{\prime}}(z)
which is the time at which the trajectories coalesce. Let
\varepsilon>0
. Let
n
large enough such that
\widetilde{t}_{zi_{n}}\geq\widetilde{t}_{c(w,w^{\prime})}-\varepsilon
. Then, by the same reasoning as in the case
a\in(3/2,2)
, by (
5.11
) we know that a.s. for
\ell
large enough,
f^{(\ell)}_{zi_{n}}
is a common ancestor of
f^{(\ell)}_{w},f^{(\ell)}_{w^{\prime}}
. As a result,

d_{\mathcal{T}(\mathfrak{m})}(f^{(\ell)}_{w},f^{(\ell)}_{w^{\prime}})\leq d^{\dagger}_{\mathrm{fpp}}(f^{(\ell)}_{r},f^{(\ell)}_{w})+d^{\dagger}_{\mathrm{fpp}}(f^{(\ell)}_{r},f^{(\ell)}_{w^{\prime}})-2d^{\dagger}_{\mathrm{fpp}}(f^{(\ell)}_{r},f^{(\ell)}_{zi_{n}}).

Consequently, a.s.

\limsup_{\ell\to\infty}\frac{1}{\ell^{a-2}}d_{\mathcal{T}(\mathfrak{m})}(f^{(\ell)}_{w},f^{(\ell)}_{w^{\prime}})\leq\frac{1}{2c_{a}p_{\bf q}}\left(\widetilde{t}_{w}+\widetilde{t}_{w^{\prime}}-2\widetilde{t}_{zi_{n}}\right)\leq\frac{1}{2c_{a}p_{\bf q}}\left(d_{\mathcal{T}_{a}}(w,w^{\prime})+2\varepsilon\right).

One can then let
\varepsilon\to 0
. Finally the almost sure inequality

\liminf_{\ell\to\infty}\frac{1}{\ell^{a-2}}d_{\mathcal{T}(\mathfrak{m})}(f^{(\ell)}_{w},f^{(\ell)}_{w^{\prime}})\geq\frac{1}{2c_{a}p_{\bf q}}d_{\mathcal{T}_{a}}(w,w^{\prime})

comes directly form (
5.11
) since if at some time the trajectories
(Y^{z,w})_{z\prec w}
and
(Y^{z,w^{\prime}})_{z\prec w^{\prime}}
have not yet coalesced, then for
\ell
large enough the same holds for the trajectories
(Y^{(\ell),z,w})_{z\prec w}
and
(Y^{(\ell),z,w^{\prime}})_{z\prec w^{\prime}}
. This ends the proof.
∎

6
Conjectures

Our choice of topology for Theorem
1.5
comes from the fact that
\mathcal{T}_{a}
is non-compact as soon as
a\leq 2
. Still, in the dilute case
a\in(2,5/2)
, we expect that the scaling limit of Theorem
1.5
holds in the sense of Gromov-Hausdorff, after taking the completion of
\mathcal{T}_{a}
. We also expect a Gromov-Hausdorff-Prokhorov scaling limit in the dilute case when we equip the map with the uniform measure on the vertices/faces/edges.

Next, we state a few conjectures on the scaling limit of the map itself. We first define a random countable metric space which is conjecturally the scaling limit of large planar maps with high degrees. Let us define another distance by inserting shortcuts in the metric space
\mathcal{T}_{a}
which was defined in Subsection
5.1
. To introduce our shortcuts, it will be more convenient to focus on the macroscopic positive jumps. For all
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
, we define
(\mathcal{T}_{a}^{\varepsilon},d_{\mathcal{T}_{a}^{\varepsilon}})
as the subspace of
\mathcal{T}_{a}
consisting of the elements
w\in\mathcal{T}_{a}
such that
\Delta_{w}>\varepsilon
. We now modify the distance
d_{\mathcal{T}_{a}^{\varepsilon}}
using the negative jumps of the
\xi_{w}
’s for
w\in\mathbb{U}
.

Let
w\in\mathbb{U}\setminus\{\emptyset\}
. Let us denote by
\emptyset=w_{0}\prec w_{1}\prec\ldots\prec w_{k}=w
its prefixes. From the negative jump
x_{w}
of
\mathcal{X}_{w_{k-1}}
, we define two trajectories: one starting on the left of the “gap” created by the jump, one starting from the right. Set
\mathcal{Y}^{w,w}_{L}(0)=0
and
\mathcal{Y}^{w,w}_{R}(0)=1
. For all
i\in\{L,R\}
, for all
j\in[\![1,k]\!]
, we define inductively

\displaystyle(\mathcal{Y}_{i}^{w_{j-1},w}(t))_{-\beta_{w_{j}}\leq t\leq 0}\coloneqq\left(X^{w_{j-1}}_{-\beta_{w_{j}},t}\left(R^{w_{j-1}}_{-\beta_{w_{j}}}-(1-Z^{w_{j-1}}_{-\beta_{w_{j}}})+(1-Z^{w_{j-1}}_{-\beta_{w_{j}}})\mathcal{Y}_{i}^{w_{j},w}(0)\right)\right)_{-\beta_{w_{j}}\leq t\leq 0}.

For all
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
,
i\in\{L,R\}
, let
J_{i}^{w,\varepsilon}
be the largest
j\in[\![1,k]\!]
such that there exists
t\in[-\beta_{w_{j}},0]
satisfying
\Delta\mathcal{X}_{w_{j-1}}(-t)>\varepsilon
and
\left\{\mathcal{Y}_{i}^{w_{j-1},w}(t-)-U^{w_{j-1}}_{t}\right\}\leq(Z^{w_{j-1}}_{t}-1)/{Z^{w_{j-1}}_{t}}.

When it exists, we also define
V^{w,\varepsilon}_{i}\in\mathcal{T}^{\varepsilon}_{a}\setminus\{\emptyset\}
as the index of the positive jump such that
-\tau_{V_{i}^{w,\varepsilon}}
is the smallest
t\in[-\beta_{w_{j}},0]
to satisfy the above conditions. If
J_{i}^{w,\varepsilon}
does not exist, then we set
J^{w,\varepsilon}_{i}=0
and
V^{w,\varepsilon}_{i}=\emptyset
. In other words,
V^{w,\varepsilon}_{L}
(resp.
V^{w,\varepsilon}_{R}
) corresponds to the first coalescence corresponding to a positive jump of size larger than
\varepsilon
happening in the trajectory
(\mathcal{Y}_{L}^{w^{\prime},w})_{w^{\prime}\prec w}
(resp.
(\mathcal{Y}_{R}^{w^{\prime},w})_{w^{\prime}\prec w}
).

For all
w\in\mathbb{U}\setminus\{\emptyset\}
, we define the length
\mathcal{L}^{\varepsilon}_{w}
of the shortcut between
V^{w,\varepsilon}_{L}
and
V^{w,\varepsilon}_{R}
associated with the negative jump
x_{w}
by

\mathcal{L}^{\varepsilon}_{w}=\widetilde{b}_{w}-\widetilde{t}_{V_{L}^{w,\varepsilon}}+\widetilde{b}_{w}-\widetilde{t}_{V_{R}^{w,\varepsilon}}
.
In the case
a=2
, the expression simplifies to
\mathcal{L}^{\varepsilon}_{w}=b_{w}-t_{V_{L}^{w,\varepsilon}}+b_{w}-t_{V_{R}^{w,\varepsilon}}
. Moreover, for all
u,v\in\mathcal{T}_{a}^{\varepsilon}
, we define the length of the smallest shortcut between
u
and
v
by setting

\mathcal{L}^{\varepsilon}(u,v)\coloneqq\inf\left\{\mathcal{L}^{\varepsilon}_{w};\ w\in\mathbb{U}\setminus\{\emptyset\}\ \text{s.t.}\ (u,v)=(V^{w,\varepsilon}_{L},V^{w,\varepsilon}_{R})\ \text{or}\ (u,v)=(V^{w,\varepsilon}_{R},V^{w,\varepsilon}_{L})\right\}\in[0,\infty].

We set
\mathcal{D}^{\varepsilon}\coloneqq\mathcal{T}_{a}^{\varepsilon}
and we equip
\mathcal{D}^{\varepsilon}
with the distance
d_{\mathcal{D}^{\varepsilon}}
defined for all
v,w\in\mathcal{D}^{\varepsilon}
by

\displaystyle d_{\mathcal{D}^{\varepsilon}}(v,w)=

\displaystyle\inf\left\{d_{\mathcal{T}_{a}}(v,v_{1})+\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left(\mathcal{L}^{\varepsilon}(v_{k},w_{k})+d_{\mathcal{T}_{a}}(w_{k},v_{k+1})\right);\ n\in\mathbb{N},\ v_{1},\ldots,v_{n+1},w_{1},\ldots,w_{n}\in\mathcal{D}^{\varepsilon},\ v_{n+1}=w\right\}.

Note that by definition of the shortcuts we have
d_{\mathcal{D}^{\varepsilon}}(v,w)\geq|d_{\mathcal{T}_{a}}(\emptyset,v)-d_{\mathcal{T}_{a}}(\emptyset,w)|
, so that positivity is preserved since
d_{\mathcal{T}_{a}}(\emptyset,v)\neq d_{\mathcal{T}_{a}}(\emptyset,w)
as soon as
v\neq w
.

Lemma 6.1
.

For all
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
,
\varepsilon^{\prime}\in(0,\varepsilon)
and
v,w\in\mathcal{D}^{\varepsilon}
,
d_{\mathcal{D}^{\varepsilon^{\prime}}}(v,w)\leq d_{\mathcal{D}^{\varepsilon}}(v,w).

Proof.

It suffices to check that for all
w\in\mathbb{U}\setminus\{\emptyset\}
, we have
d_{\mathcal{D}^{\varepsilon^{\prime}}}(V^{w,\varepsilon}_{L},V^{w,\varepsilon}_{R})\leq\mathcal{L}^{\varepsilon}_{w}
. But

\displaystyle d_{\mathcal{D}^{\varepsilon^{\prime}}}(V^{w,\varepsilon}_{L},V^{w,\varepsilon}_{R})

\displaystyle\leq d_{\mathcal{D}^{\varepsilon^{\prime}}}(V^{w,\varepsilon}_{L},V^{w,\varepsilon^{\prime}}_{L})+\mathcal{L}^{\varepsilon^{\prime}}_{w}+d_{\mathcal{D}^{\varepsilon^{\prime}}}(V^{w,\varepsilon}_{R},V^{w,\varepsilon^{\prime}}_{R})

\displaystyle=\big(\widetilde{t}_{V_{L}^{w,\varepsilon^{\prime}}}-\widetilde{t}_{V_{L}^{w,\varepsilon}}\big)+\big(\widetilde{b}_{w}-\widetilde{t}_{V_{L}^{w,\varepsilon^{\prime}}}+\widetilde{b}_{w}-\widetilde{t}_{V^{w,\varepsilon^{\prime}}_{R}}\big)+\big(\widetilde{t}_{V_{R}^{w,\varepsilon^{\prime}}}-\widetilde{t}_{V_{R}^{w,\varepsilon}}\big)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{L}^{\varepsilon}_{w},

where in the second line we used the fact that
V_{L}^{w,\varepsilon}
(resp.
V_{R}^{w,\varepsilon}
) is an ancestor of
V_{L}^{w,\varepsilon^{\prime}}
(resp.
V_{R}^{w,\varepsilon^{\prime}}
) in
\mathcal{T}_{a}
, in the sense that
c(V_{L}^{w,\varepsilon},V_{L}^{w,\varepsilon^{\prime}})=V_{L}^{w,\varepsilon}
(resp.
c(V_{R}^{w,\varepsilon},V_{R}^{w,\varepsilon^{\prime}})=V_{R}^{w,\varepsilon}
).
∎

Notice also that the sets
\mathcal{D}^{\varepsilon}
increase as
\varepsilon
decreases. One can thus define
\mathcal{D}_{a}=\mathcal{T}_{a}
and the distance
d_{\mathcal{D}_{a}}
on
\mathcal{D}_{a}
by writing for all
v,w\in\mathcal{D}_{a}
,

d_{\mathcal{D}_{a}}(v,w)=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}d_{\mathcal{D}^{\varepsilon}}(v,w),

where the term on the right is well defined for
\varepsilon
small enough. The function
d_{\mathcal{D}_{a}}
satisfies the triangle inequality and symmetry since it is a limit of distances. Moreover, it also satisfies positivity since
d_{\mathcal{D}^{\varepsilon}}(v,w)\geq|d_{\mathcal{T}_{a}}(\emptyset,v)-d_{\mathcal{T}_{a}}(\emptyset,w)|
.

Conjecture 6.2
.

Suppose
a\in(3/2,5/2)
. Then, for the product topology,

\text{Under }\mathbb{P}^{(\ell)},\qquad\qquad\left(\ell^{2-a}d^{\dagger}_{\mathrm{fpp}}(f_{i},f_{j})\right)_{i,j\geq 1}\mathop{\longrightarrow}\limits_{\ell\to\infty}^{(\mathrm{d})}\left(\frac{1}{2c_{a}p_{\bf q}}d_{\mathcal{D}_{a}}(w_{i},w_{j})\right)_{i,j\geq 1}.

Conjecture 6.3
.

Suppose
a\in(2,5/2)
. Then, for the product topology,

\text{Under }\mathbb{P}^{(\ell)},\qquad\qquad\left(\ell^{2-a}d^{\dagger}_{\mathrm{gr}}(f_{i},f_{j})\right)_{i,j\geq 1}\mathop{\longrightarrow}\limits_{\ell\to\infty}^{(\mathrm{d})}\left(\frac{1+g_{\bf q}}{2c_{a}p_{\bf q}}d_{\mathcal{D}_{a}}(w_{i},w_{j})\right)_{i,j\geq 1},

where
g_{\bf q}=(1/2)\sum_{k\geq 1}\nu(-k)(2k-1)
. Moreover,
\mathcal{D}_{a}
has a compact completion and this convergence of metric spaces also holds in the sense of Gromov-Hausdorff for this completion of
\mathcal{D}_{a}
.

Conjecture 6.4
.

Suppose
a=2
. Then, for the product topology,

\text{Under }\mathbb{P}^{(\ell)},\qquad\qquad\left(\frac{1}{\log\ell}d^{\dagger}_{\mathrm{gr}}(f_{i},f_{j})\right)_{i,j\geq 1}\mathop{\longrightarrow}\limits_{\ell\to\infty}^{(\mathrm{d})}\left(\frac{1}{2c_{a}}d_{\mathcal{D}_{a}}(w_{i},w_{j})\right)_{i,j\geq 1}.

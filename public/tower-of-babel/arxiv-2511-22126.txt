A Novel J M J_{M} -Based Method for Interpolation in Metric Spaces
Roblêdo Mak’s Miranda Sette

Abstract
We present a new interpolation method for metric spaces, termed the J M ′ J_{M}^{\prime} -interpolated metric space . Building on previous work where two interpolation methods were developed in analogy with Peetre’s classical K K and J J methods, this approach introduces a distinct construction based on approximating sequences in the intersection space X 0 ∩ X 1 X_{0}\cap X_{1} equipped with the metric J M ​ ( 1 ) J_{M}(1) . The method defines a relative completion within the union space X 0 ∪ X 1 X_{0}\cup X_{1} , yielding a well-defined interpolated metric space with controllable relationships between the J M J_{M} and K M K_{M} metrics. While we do not compare this method to the previously developed approaches nor explore its correspondence with classical interpolation functors in normed spaces, the construction enriches the set of tools available for metric-space interpolation theory. Furthermore, as expected, these methods still preserve the Lipschitz property of an operator between two interpolated spaces. Potential applications include general Nonlinear Analysis and Wasserstein-type spaces where the sequence-based nature of the method may provide practical advantages.



Introduction

In a previous work published in 2024 [see
[
13
]
], we introduced two methods of interpolation for general metric spaces, inspired by the classical
K
and
J
methods in the normed setting of Peetre [see
[
10
]
and
[
3
]
]. These constructions allowed the extension of classical interpolation ideas to the broader context of metric spaces, defining analogues of the
K
- and
J
-interpolation spaces and establishing a structured approach to study interpolation without relying on linearity or normed assumptions. This type of interpolation acts on metric spaces and preserves the Lipschitz continuity of operators in the interpolated spaces, in a manner analogous to interpolation functors in classical Banach space theory. The methods provided a framework for constructing interpolated metric spaces that capture intermediate structures between two given metric spaces, opening avenues for applications in areas where metric spaces arise naturally, such as nonlinear analysis, optimal transport, and metric geometry.

In this article, we develop a new interpolation method, the
J_{M}^{\prime}
-interpolated metric space
, which complements the previously introduced methods by offering a fundamentally different construction. Unlike the prior methods, which relied on direct adaptations of the
K
and
J
functionals, this new method is based on approximating sequences within the intersection space
X_{0}\cap X_{1}
, endowed with the metric
J_{M}(1)
, and its relative completion inside the union space
X_{0}\cup X_{1}
. This sequence-based approach introduces a unique perspective, which could prove advantageous in situations where the behavior of sequences in the intersection space is critical or where the structure of the union space provides natural constraints.

The primary motivation behind proposing a new interpolation method is that expanding the toolbox of available interpolation techniques enriches the study of metric-space interpolation, allowing researchers to choose the most suitable method depending on the specific problem or application. This methodological diversity is particularly relevant for applications to Nonlinear Analysis and spaces with nontrivial geometries, such as Wasserstein-type spaces [see
[
1
]
and
[
15
]
and
[
16
]
], where the sequence-oriented nature of this approach might yield novel insights or simplifications.

It is important to note that the goal of this article is the construction of the new interpolation method itself. We do not investigate its relationship with the previously developed methods, nor do we attempt to determine whether it generates interpolation functors in normed spaces that are equivalent to classical constructions by Peetre or Lions. Our focus is on establishing the theoretical foundation of this new approach and analyzing its intrinsic properties, such as its metric structure, continuity of inclusions, and basic interpolation inequalities. As a final remark, we suggest that future research could explore potential applications of this method, particularly in Wasserstein-type spaces, where the specific sequence-based construction might reveal previously inaccessible properties of interpolated structures.

1.1
Relative Completion of Metric Spaces

Definition 1.1
.

Let
(A,d_{A})
and
(B,d_{B})
be two metric spaces such that
A\subseteq B
and
there exists
c>0
such that

d_{B}(x,y)\leq c\cdot d_{A}(x,y),\quad x,y\in A.

We define the
relative completion of
(A,d_{A})
in
(B,d_{B})
, denoted by
A_{B}
, by

A_{B}:=\{x\in B;\,\,\textbf{$\exists$ Cauchy sequence $(x_{n})$ in $(A,d_{A})$ and $d_{B}(x_{n},x)\to 0$}\}.

A sequence
(x_{n})
as above is called an
approximating sequence of
x
in
(A,d_{A})
.

There are some intrinsic properties of the relative completion of metric spaces that we list below:

1.

This completion can be metrized with a metric
d_{A,B}
that
satisfies

d_{B}(x,y)\leq c\cdot d_{A,B}(x,y)\leq d_{A}(x,y),\quad x,y\in A

and

d_{B}(x,y)\leq c\cdot d_{A,B}(x,y),\quad x,y\in A_{B}.

Thus,
A\subseteq A_{B}\subseteq B
with continuous inclusions.

2.

The set
A
is dense in
(A_{B},d_{A,B})
.

3.

If
(B,d_{B})
is complete, then
(A_{B},d_{A,B})
is also complete.

4.

The set
A
is dense in
(A_{B},d_{A,B})
.

5.

The metrics
d_{A,B}
and
d_{A}
coincide on
A
.

Definition 1.2
.

Let
X_{0}

(1)

•

x_{0}=x

•

For each

to

(x_{n}^{x,y})_{adm}
.
When all
n

, we will say that it is a

to

Z

. The set of all linking sequences from
x
to

.

Remark 1.1

Note that every admissible linking sequence is a linking sequence in the union
X_{0}\cup X_{1}

The
K_{M}
-Metric Interpolation Space

In this section we will define and highlight the main properties of the interpolated metric space.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\theta\in(0,1)
and
q\in[1,\infty]
. Given a sequence
(x_{k})_{k\in\ \mathbb{Z}}
of real numbers, we define the functional
\Gamma_{\theta,q}
by

\Gamma_{\theta,q}((x_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}):=\begin{cases}\biggr[\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}[2^{-k\theta}|x_{k}|]^{q}\biggr]^{1/q},&q<\infty\\
\sup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{-k\theta}|x_{k}|,&q=\infty.\end{cases}

(6)

Lemma 2.1
.

The functional

\beta_{\theta,q}(x,y):=\Gamma_{\theta,q}((K_{M}(2^{k};x,y)_{k\in\mathbb{Z}})

(7)

is a metric on the set
X_{0}\cap X_{1}
.

We will refer, for simplicity, to the metrics
J_{M}(t;\cdot,\cdot)
and
K_{M}(t;\cdot,\cdot)
as
J_{M}(t)
and
K_{M}(t)
, respectively.

Definition 2.2

(The
K_{M}
-interpolated metric space)
.

The
interpolated metric space by the classical
K_{M}
method
is denoted by
\overrightarrow{X}_{\theta,q}^{K_{M}}
and is the relative completion of the metric space

(X_{0}\cap X_{1},\beta_{\theta,q})

in the metric space
(X_{0}\cup X_{1},K_{M}(1))
. The metric of this space is denoted by
\mathcal{D}_{\theta,q}
and equals
\mathcal{D}_{\theta,q}(x,y)=\beta_{\theta,q}(x,y)
, for all
x,y\in X_{0}\cap X_{1}
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Given
\theta\in(0,1)
and
q\geq 1
, we have

\beta_{\theta,q}(x,y)\leq M_{\theta,q}\cdot J_{M}(1;x,y),\quad x,y\in X_{0}\cap X_{1}.

(8)

And furthermore,

M_{\theta,q}\cdot d_{X}(x,y)\leq\mathcal{D}_{\theta,q}(x,y),\quad x,y\in\overrightarrow{X}_{\theta,q}^{K_{M}}.

(9)

where

{}M_{\theta,q}=\displaystyle\Gamma_{\theta,q}(\min\{1,2^{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}})>0.

3
The
J_{M}^{\prime}
-Interpolated Metric Space

Definition 3.1

(Bi-infinite Sequences)
.

A sequence is said to be
bi-infinite
when it is indexed in the set of integers. A bi-infinite sequence
(z_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}

is said to converge to the point
z
in a metric space
(M,d)
if

\lim_{n\to\infty}d(z_{n},z)=0\quad\text{and}\quad\lim_{n\to-\infty}d(z_{n},z)=0.

We know that
X_{0}\cap X_{1}\subseteq X_{0}\cup X_{1}
and that
K_{M}(1)
is a metric on
X_{0}\cup X_{1}
. We will metrize the set
X_{0}\cap X_{1}
with a metric satisfying the relative completion condition and then complete this space equipped with this metric, with respect to the space
(X_{0}\cup X_{1},K_{M}(1))
.

Given
x,y\in X_{0}\cap X_{1}
and
(x_{n})
and
(y_{n})
approximating sequences of
x
and
y
in
(X_{0}\cap X_{1},J_{M}(1))
, respectively, let us define a new bi-infinite sequence, denoted by
(S_{k}^{x,y})_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
, which depends on these approximating sequences and whose general term is given by:

S_{k}^{x,y}:=\begin{cases}x_{k},&\text{if $k\geq 0$}\\
y_{-k},&\text{if $k<0$}\end{cases}

Such a sequence will be called a
bi-infinite linking sequence from
x
to
y
in
(X_{0}\cap X_{1},J_{M}(1))
.
On
X_{0}\cap X_{1}
let us define the functional
p_{\theta,q}
given by

p_{\theta,q}(x,y):=\inf\Gamma_{\theta,q}((J_{M}(2^{k};S_{k}^{x,y},S_{k+1}^{x,y})),

(10)

where the infimum is taken over all approximating sequences of
x
and
y
.

Definition 3.2

(Separator Functional)
.

Let
M\neq\emptyset
and
p:M\times M\to[0,\infty)
. We say that
p
is a
separator functional of points in
M
and

p(x,y)=0\Longleftrightarrow x=y.

Theorem 3.1
.

The functional
p_{\theta,q}
is a separator on
X_{0}\cap X_{1}
.

Proof.

Let
x,y\in X_{0}\cap X_{1}
and
(x_{n})
and
(y_{n})
be two arbitrary approximating sequences of
x
and
y
in
(X_{0}\cap X_{1},J_{M}(1))
and let
(S_{j}^{x,y})_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}
be the bi-infinite linking sequence from
x
to
y
in
(X_{0}\cap X_{1},J_{M}(1))
. Fixing
N\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

K_{M}(1;S_{N}^{x,y},S_{-N}^{x,y})\leq\sum_{j=-N}^{N-1}K_{M}(1;S_{j}^{x,y},S_{j+1}^{x,y}).

Thus, taking the
\lim\sup_{N}
, we have

K_{M}(1;x,y)\leq\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}K_{M}(1;S_{j}^{x,y},S_{j+1}^{x,y}).

Now, using Lemma
1.5
, with
a=1
and
b=2^{j}
in each term
K_{M}(1;S_{j}^{x,y},S_{j+1}^{x,y})
, we have

K_{M}(1;x,y)\leq\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}K_{M}(1;S_{j}^{x,y},S_{j+1}^{x,y})\leq\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\min\{1,1/2^{j}\}\cdot J_{M}(2^{j};S_{j}^{x,y},S_{j+1}^{x,y}).

We can also write, for all
\theta\in(0,1)
:

K_{M}(1;x,y)\leq\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}[2^{j\theta}\cdot\min\{1,1/2^{j}\}]\cdot[2^{-j\theta}\cdot J_{M}(2^{j};S_{j}^{x,y},S_{j+1}^{x,y})].

Using the
Hölder Inequality
in the last sum, we have

K_{M}(1;x,y)\leq M_{\theta,q}\cdot\Gamma_{\theta,q}((J_{M}(2^{j};S_{j}^{x,y},S_{j+1}^{x,y})_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}),

(11)

where

M_{\theta,q}:=\biggr[\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}[2^{j\theta}\cdot\min\{1,1/2^{j}\}]^{p}\biggr]^{1/p}

with
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=1
. Note that from this inequality it follows that

K_{M}(1;x,y)\leq M_{\theta,q}\cdot p_{\theta,q}(x,y).

(12)

Furthermore, for given
x
and
y
, the constant approximating sequences
x_{n}=x
and
y_{n}=y
for all natural
n
are such that for these, the bi-infinite linking sequence
(S_{k}^{x,y})
satisfies:

S_{k}^{x,y}=\begin{cases}x,&\text{if $k>0$}\\
y,&\text{if $k\leq 0$}\end{cases}

Thus, for this bi-infinite sequence, we have

\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}[2^{-k\theta}\cdot J_{M}(2^{k};S_{k}^{x,y},S_{k+1}^{x,y})]^{q}=[J_{M}(1;x,y)]^{q}.

From this, it follows that

p_{\theta,q}(x,y)\leq\Gamma_{\theta,q}((J_{M}(2^{k};S_{k}^{x,y},S_{k+1}^{x,y}))\leq J_{M}(1;x,y).

(13)

From inequality
13
, we have that
p_{\theta,q}(x,y)<+\infty
for all
x,y\in X_{0}\cap X_{1}
and from inequality
12
we have that
p_{\theta,q}(x,y)=0\Longleftrightarrow x=y
. ∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\theta\in(0,1)
and
q\in[1,\infty]
. Given
x,y\in X_{0}\cap X_{1}
, let us define the
functional

P_{\theta,q}(x,y):=\frac{p_{\theta,q}(x,y)+p_{\theta,q}(y,x)}{2}.

(14)

The functional
P_{\theta,q}:(X_{0}\cap X_{1})\times(X_{0}\cap X_{1})\to[0,\infty)
satisfies

1.

P_{\theta,q}(x,y)=0\Longleftrightarrow x=y
.

2.

P_{\theta,q}(x,y)=P_{\theta,q}(y,x)
, for all
x,y\in X_{0}\cap X_{1}
.

3.

\min\{p_{\theta,q}(x,y);p_{\theta,q}(y,x)\}\leq P_{\theta,q}(x,y)\leq\max\{p_{\theta,q}(x,y);p_{\theta,q}(y,x)\}
, for all
x,y\in X_{0}\cap X_{1}
.

Proof.

The proof is immediate. ∎

Definition 3.3
.

Given
\theta\in(0,1)
and
q\in[1,\infty]
let us define the functional
\delta_{\theta,q}
on

X_{0}\cap X_{1}
, given by

\delta_{\theta,q}(x,y):=\inf\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}P_{\theta,q}(x_{j},x_{j+1}),

(15)

where the infimum is taken over all finite sequences of points
x_{0}=x,x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},\ \ldots,x_{n}=y\in X_{0}\cap X_{1}
.

Theorem 3.3
.

The functional
\delta_{\theta,q}
as above is a metric on
X_{0}\cap X_{1}
.

Proof.

It is easy to verify that
\delta_{\theta,q}(x,x)=0
. Furthermore, for all
x,y\in X_{0}\cap X_{1}
, taking the sequence
x_{0}=x
and
x_{1}=y
, we have

0\leq\delta_{\theta,q}(x,y)\leq P_{\theta,q}(x,y)<+\infty.

Let now
x,y\in X_{0}\cap X_{1}
such that
\delta_{\theta,q}(x,y)=0
. From inequality
12
, we have

K_{M}(1;x,y)=K_{M}(1;y,x)\leq M_{\theta,q}\cdot p_{\theta,q}(x,y),

for all
x,y\in X_{0}\cap X_{1}
. Thus, it is also true that
K_{M}(1;x,y)\leq M_{\theta,q}\cdot p_{\theta,q}(y,x)
. Therefore,

K_{M}(1;x,y)\leq M_{\theta,q}\cdot\min\{p_{\theta,q}(x,y);p_{\theta,q}(y,x)\}\leq M_{\theta,q}\cdot P_{\theta,q}(x,y),

(16)

for any
x,y\in X_{0}\cap X_{1}
. For any linking sequence
x_{0}=x,x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n}=y
we have

\displaystyle K_{M}(1;x,y)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}K_{M}(1;x_{j},x_{j+1})

\displaystyle\leq M_{\theta,q}\cdot\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}P_{\theta,q}(x_{j},x_{j+1}).

Consequently, we have

K_{M}(1;x,y)\leq M_{\theta,q}\cdot\delta_{\theta,q}(x,y),\quad x,y\in X_{0}\cap X_{1}.

(17)

This inequality guarantees the positivity of
\delta_{\theta,q}
.
The symmetry of
\delta_{\theta,q}
comes from the symmetry of
P_{\theta,q}
and the fact that every linking sequence from
x
to
y
in
X_{0}\cap X_{1}
is also a linking sequence from
y
to
x
in
X_{0}\cap X_{1}
.
For the triangle inequality, let
x,y,z\in X_{0}\cap X_{1}
and
\varepsilon>0
. Then, there exist linking sequences
x_{0}=x,x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n}=z
from
x
to
z
and
y_{0}=z,y_{1},y_{2},\ldots,y_{m}=y
from
z
to
y
, such that

\delta_{\theta,q}(x,z)\leq\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}P_{\theta,q}(x_{j},x_{j+1})<\delta_{\theta,q}(x,z)+\varepsilon

and

\delta_{\theta,q}(z,y)\leq\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}P_{\theta,q}(y_{j},j_{j+1})<\delta_{\theta,q}(z,y)+\varepsilon.

Note that the sequence

z_{0}=x,z_{1},\ldots,z_{n}=z,z_{n+1}=z,z_{n+2},\ldots,z_{n+m}=y

is a linking sequence from
x
to
y
in
X_{0}\cap X_{1}
and satisfies

\displaystyle\delta_{\theta,q}(x,y)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=0}^{m+n-1}P_{\theta,q}(z_{j},z_{j+1})

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}P_{\theta,q}(x_{j},x_{j+1})+\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}P_{\theta,q}(y_{j},y_{j+1})

\displaystyle<\delta_{\theta,q}(x,z)+\delta_{\theta,q}(z,y)+2\varepsilon.

From the arbitrariness of
\varepsilon>0
it follows

\delta_{\theta,q}(x,y)\leq\delta_{\theta,q}(x,z)+\delta_{\theta,q}(z,y).

Therefore,
\delta_{\theta,q}
is indeed a metric on
X_{0}\cap X_{1}
. ∎

.

Inequality
17
ensures that the metric space
(X_{0}\cap X_{1},\delta_{\theta,q})

(The
J_{M}^{\prime}
-interpolated metric space)
.

The
J_{M}^{\prime}
-interpolated metric space
is denoted by
(\overrightarrow{X}_{\theta,q}^{J_{M}})^{\prime}

Remark 3.2
.

An important fact is that the interpolated spaces above were defined as the completion of the intersection
X_{0}\cap X_{1}
with a specific metric in the metric space
(X_{0}\cup X_{1},K_{M}(1))
. This ensures that the elements of the interpolated spaces are contained in
X_{0}\cup X_{1}
. However, note that such metrics on the intersection also satisfy the conditions for completion in
(X,d_{X})
which can be substantially “larger” than
X_{0}\cup X_{1}
, providing many more new elements to the completions. That is why the name ”relative” was chosen for this type of completion. We do not study here the advantages of completing the spaces beyond
X_{0}\cup X_{1}
.

4
Properties of the
J_{M}^{\prime}
-Interpolated Metric Space

Theorem 4.1
.

The inclusions
X_{0}\cap X_{1}\subseteq(\overrightarrow{X}_{\theta,q}^{J_{M}})^{\prime}\subseteq X_{0}\cup X_{1}
are valid and furthermore

1.

\Delta_{\theta,q}(x,y)=\delta_{\theta,q}(x,y)\leq J_{M}(1;x,y),\quad x,y\in X_{0}\cap X_{1}
.

2.

K_{M}(1;x,y)\leq M_{\theta,q}\cdot\Delta_{\theta,q}(x,y),\quad x,y\in(\overrightarrow{X}_{\theta,q}^{J_{M}})^{\prime}
.

Proof.

The inclusions are immediate. We will show only the inequalities. Let
x,y\in X_{0}\cap X_{1}
. From inequality
13
, we have

p_{\theta,q}(x,y)\leq J_{M}(1;x,y).

Since
J_{M}(1;x,y)=J_{M}(1;y,x)
, it holds that

\max\{p_{\theta,q}(x,y);p_{\theta,q}(y,x)\}\leq J_{M}(1;x,y).

Thus,
P_{\theta,q}(x,y)\leq J_{M}(1;x,y)
. For the trivial linking sequence
x_{0}=x
and
x_{1}=y
, we have

\delta_{\theta,q}(x,y)\leq P_{\theta,q}(x,y)\leq J_{M}(1;x,y).

(18)

The second inequality is obtained directly from inequality
17
. Therefore, the inclusions are continuous, as we wanted to demonstrate. ∎

Definition 4.1
.

Let
(X,d_{X})
and
(Y,d_{Y})
be metric spaces and
T:X\to Y
an operator. We say that

T
is a
closed operator
if

[d_{X}(x_{n},x)\to 0\quad\text{and}\quad d_{Y}(T(x_{n}),y)\to 0]\implies y=T(x).

In other words, the set

\text{Gr}(T):=\{(x,T(x))\in X\times Y\}

is a closed set in
X\times Y
in the product topology.

Definition 4.2
.

Let
(X,d_{X})
,
(y,d_{Y})
and
T:X\to Y
an operator. We say that the operator
T
is
a
Lipschitz operator
if there exists a positive constant
C
such that

d_{Y}(T(x),T(y))\leq C\cdot d_{X}(x,y),\quad\forall x,y\in X.

When
T
is a Lipschitz operator, we call the number

\inf_{C>0}\{d_{Y}(T(x),T(y))\leq C\cdot d_{X}(x,y);\quad\forall x,y\in X\}

the
Lipschitz constant of the operator
T
.

Lemma 4.2

(Reindexing and interpolation factor)
.

Let
(X,d_{X})
and
(Y,d_{Y})
be metric spaces and
T:X\to Y
an operator. Let
(S_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
be a linking sequence from
x
to
y
in
X
(which for simplicity of notation we will omit the superscript
x,y
) and let

a_{k}:=2^{-k\theta}J_{M}(2^{k}\lambda;\,T(S_{k}),T(S_{k+1}):Y),\qquad b_{k}:=2^{-k\theta}J_{M}(2^{k};\,S_{k},S_{k+1}:X),

where
\lambda>0
and
\theta\in(0,1)
. Suppose that for all
k\in\mathbb{Z}
it holds that

J_{M}(2^{k};S_{k},S_{k+1}:X)\;\geq\;\omega_{0}^{-1}\,J_{Y}(2^{k}\lambda;\,T(S_{k}),T(S_{k+1}):Y),\quad\omega_{0}>0.

(H)

For
\omega_{1}>0
define
\lambda=\dfrac{\omega_{0}}{\omega_{1}}
. Then:

(i)

If
\lambda=2^{r}
for some
r\in\mathbb{Z}
, then, for all
q\in[1,\ infty]
,

\big\|(a_{k})_{k}\big\|_{\ell^{q}}\leq\omega_{0}^{\,1-\theta}\omega_{1}^{\,\theta}\;\big\|(b_{k})_{k}\big\|_{\ell^{q}}.

(ii)

If
\lambda>0
is arbitrary, then, for all
q\in[1,\infty]
,

\big\|(a_{k})_{k}\big\|_{\ell^{q}}\leq C(\theta)\,\omega_{0}^{\,1-\theta}\omega_{1}^{\,\theta}\;\big\|(b_{k})_{k}\big\|_{\ell^{q}},

where
C(\theta)\leq 2^{\theta}
is a constant depending only on
\theta
.

Proof.

We start by observing that hypothesis (
H
) is equivalent, in terms of the terms

a_{k},b_{k}
, to

b_{k}\;\geq\;\omega_{0}^{-1}a_{k},\qquad\text{that is}\qquad a_{k}\leq\omega_{0}\,b_{k},\qquad\forall k\in\mathbb{Z}.

If
\lambda=2^{r}
,
r\in\mathbb{Z}
:
Writing
\lambda=2^{r}
and making the index change
m=k+r
we obtain, for each
k
,

a_{k}=2^{-k\theta}J_{M}(2^{k}\lambda;\,T(S_{k}),T(S_{k+1}):Y)=2^{-k\theta}J_{M}(2^{k+r};\,T(S_{k}),T(S_{k+1}):Y).

Rewriting in terms of the index
m
(with
m=k+r
) we have the identity

\displaystyle a_{k}

\displaystyle=2^{r\theta}\,2^{-(k+r)\theta}J_{M}(2^{k+r};\,T(S_{k}),T(S_{k+1}):Y)

\displaystyle=\lambda^{\theta}\,2^{-m\theta}J_{M}(2^{m};\,T(S_{m-r}),T(S_{m-r+1}):Y).

By hypothesis (
H
) applied to the index
m-r
we have

\displaystyle 2^{-m\theta}J_{M}(2^{m};\,T(S_{m-r}),T(S_{m-r+1}):Y)\leq

\displaystyle\omega_{0}\,2^{-(m-r)\theta}J_{M}(2^{m-r};\,S_{m-r},S_{m-r+1}:X).

Combining the two expressions and using reindexing (the transformation
k\mapsto m=k+r
is an index shift), we obtain

\displaystyle 2^{-m\theta}J_{M}(2^{m};\,T(S_{m}),T(S_{m+1}):Y)\leq

\displaystyle\omega_{0}\,\lambda^{-\theta}\,2^{-(m-r)\theta}J_{M}(2^{m-r};\,S_{m-r},S_{m-r+1}:X).

Taking the
\ell^{q}
norm of both sides and remembering that the index shift is
isometric in
\ell^{q}
(that is,
\|(u_{m-r})_{m}\|_{\ell^{q}}=\|(u_{k})_{k}\|_{\ell^{q}}
), it results in

\displaystyle\big\|(2^{-m\theta}J_{M}(2^{m};T(S_{m}),T(S_{m+1});Y))_{m}\big\|_{\ell^{q}}\leq

\displaystyle\omega_{0}\,\lambda^{-\theta}\,\big\|(2^{-k\theta}J_{X}(2^{k};S_{k},S_{k+1}:X))_{k}\big\|_{\ell^{q}}.

But the left-hand side is exactly
\|(a_{k})_{k}\|_{\ell^{q}}
(after reindexing), and the right-hand side is
\|(b_{k})_{k}\|_{\ell^{q}}
. Substituting
\lambda=\dfrac{\omega_{0}}{\omega_{1}}
we obtain

\big\|(a_{k})_{k}\big\|_{\ell^{q}}\leq\omega_{0}\Big(\frac{\omega_{0}}{\omega_{1}}\Big)^{-\theta}\big\|(b_{k})_{k}\big\|_{\ell^{q}}=\omega_{0}^{\,1-\theta}\omega_{1}^{\,\theta}\big\|(b_{k})_{k}\big\|_{\ell^{q}},

which proves (i).

Arbitrary Case
\lambda>0
:
Choose
r=\lfloor\log_{2}\lambda\rfloor\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
2^{r}\leq\lambda<2^{r+1}
. For all
k
we have the two elementary estimates

J_{M}(2^{k+r};\cdot,\cdot:Y)\;\leq\;J_{M}(2^{k}\lambda;\cdot,\cdot:Y)\;\leq\;J_{Y}(2^{k+r+1};\cdot,\cdot:Y),

because the function
s\mapsto J_{M}(2^{k}s;\cdot,\cdot:Y)
is non-decreasing in
s>0

(remembering that here only the multiplicative parameter within the second argument of
J_{M}
varies). Multiplying by
2^{-k\theta}
and reindexing as before, we obtain

\displaystyle 2^{r\theta}\,2^{-(k+r)\theta}J_{M}(2^{k+r};\cdot,\cdot:Y)

\displaystyle\leq 2^{-k\theta}J_{M}(2^{k}\lambda;\cdot,\cdot:Y)

\displaystyle\leq 2^{(r+1)\theta}\,2^{-(k+r+1)\theta}J_{M}(2^{k+r+1};\cdot,\cdot:Y).

Applying inequality (
H
) (in the form used in (i)) to the appropriate indices and taking the
\ell^{q}
norm, we arrive at inequalities of the type

\big\|(a_{k})_{k}\big\|_{\ell^{q}}\leq\omega_{0}\,2^{\theta}\,\lambda^{-\theta}\big\|(b_{k})_{k}\big\|_{\ell^{q}}\quad\text{or}\quad\big\|(a_{k})_{k}\big\|_{\ell^{q}}\leq\omega_{0}\,\lambda^{-\theta}\big\|(b_{k})_{k}\big\|_{\ell^{q}},

depending on whether we use the majorant or the minorant above. In both cases we can
write compactly

\big\|(a_{k})_{k}\big\|_{\ell^{q}}\leq C(\theta)\,\omega_{0}\,\lambda^{-\theta}\big\|(b_{k})_{k}\big\|_{\ell^{q}},\qquad C(\theta)\leq 2^{\theta}.

Substituting
\lambda=\dfrac{\omega_{0}}{\omega_{1}}
results in

\big\|(a_{k})_{k}\big\|_{\ell^{q}}\leq C(\theta)\,\omega_{0}^{\,1-\theta}\omega_{1}^{\,\theta}\big\|(b_{k})_{k}\big\|_{\ell^{q}},

as we wanted to demonstrate. ∎

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
(X_{0},d_{X_{0}})
and
(X_{1},d_{X_{1}})
be a pair of compatible metric spaces with
respect to the metric space
(X,d_{X})
and
(Y_{0},d_{Y_{0}})
and
(Y_{1},d_{Y_{1}})
a pair of compatible metric spaces with respect to the metric space
(Y,d_{Y})
. If
(Y,d_{Y})
is complete and
T:X\to Y
is a closed operator that satisfies:

1.

T_{0}:=T|_{X_{0}}:X_{0}\to Y_{0}
is Lipschitzian with Lipschitz constant
\omega_{0}>0

2.

T_{1}:=T|_{X_{1}}:X_{1}\to Y_{1}
is Lipschitzian with Lipschitz constant
\omega_{1}>0

Then,
T_{\theta,q}:=T|_{(\overrightarrow{X}_{\theta,q}^{J_{M}})^{\prime}}:(\overrightarrow{X}_{\theta,q}^{J_{M}})^{\prime}\to(\overrightarrow{Y}_{\theta,q}^{J_{M}})^{\prime}
is Lipschitzian with Lipschitz constant not exceeding
2^{\theta}\cdot\omega_{0}^{1-\theta}\cdot\omega_{1}^{\theta}>0
.

Proof.

Let
x,y\in X_{0}\cap X_{1}
and
(x_{n})
and
(y_{n})
be two approximating sequences of
x
and
y
in
X_{0}\cap X_{1}
. Let us define the bi-infinite linking sequence
(S_{k}^{x,y})
that links
x
to
y
in
X_{0}\cap X_{1}
as usual through these. Since
(x_{n})
is Cauchy in
(X_{0}\cap X_{1},J_{M}(1))
, it follows that
(x_{n})
is Cauchy in
(X_{0},d_{X_{0}})
and in
(X_{1},d_{X_{1}})
, because

\displaystyle J_{M}(1;x_{m},x_{n}:X)

\displaystyle:=\max\{d_{X_{0}}(x_{m},x_{n}),d_{X_{1}}(x_{m},x_{n})\}

\displaystyle\geq d_{X_{i}}(x_{m},x_{n}),\quad i=0,1.

Since
(x_{n})
is Cauchy in
(X_{i},d_{X_{i}})
,
i=0,1
, due to the fact that

d_{Y_{i}}(T_{i}(x_{m}),T_{i}(x_{n}))\leq\omega_{i}\cdot d_{X_{i}}(x_{m},x_{n}),\quad i=0,1,

we have that
(T(x_{n}))
is a Cauchy sequence both in
(Y_{0},d_{Y_{0}})
and in
(Y_{1},d_{Y_{1}})
. Since by hypothesis there exists
C_{i}>0
such that
d_{Y}(T(x_{m}),T(x_{n}))\leq C_{i}\cdot d_{Y_{i}}(T(x_{m}),T(x_{n}))
, for
i=0,1
, we have that
(T(x_{n}))
is a Cauchy sequence in
(Y,d_{Y})
, which is complete, by hypothesis. It follows that there exists
a\in Y
such that
d_{Y}(T(x_{n}),a)\to 0
. By the closedness property of the operator
T
, we have
a=T(x)
. The same reasoning can be applied to the sequence
(y_{n})
which will provide
b\in Y
such that
T(y)=b
. Note that if
(T(S_{k}^{x,y}))
is a bi-infinite linking sequence from
T(y)
to
T(x)
in
Y_{0}\cap Y_{1}
. Thus, since

\displaystyle J_{M}(2^{k};S_{k}^{x,y},S_{k+1}^{x,y}:X)=

\displaystyle\max\{d_{X_{0}}(S_{k}^{x,y},S_{k+1}^{x,y});2^{k}d_{X_{1}}(S_{k}^{x,y},S_{k+1}^{x,y})\}\geq

\displaystyle\max\{\omega_{0}^{-1}d_{Y_{0}}(T(S_{k}^{T(x),T(y)}),T(S_{k+1}^{T(x),T(y)}),2^{k}\omega_{1}^{-1}d_{Y_{1}}(T(S_{k}^{T(x),T(y)}),T(S_{k+1}^{T(x),T(y)})\}=

\displaystyle\omega_{0}^{-1}\cdot J_{M}(2^{k}\omega_{0}/\omega_{1};T(S_{k}^{T(x),T(y)}),T(S_{k+1}^{T(x),T(y)}):Y)\geq

\displaystyle 2^{-\theta}\cdot\omega_{0}^{\theta-1}\cdot\omega_{1}^{-\theta}\cdot J_{M}(2^{k};T(S_{k}^{T(x),T(y)}),T(S_{k+1}^{T(x),T(y)}):Y),

where in the last inequality we used the previous Lemma
4.2
. From this
inequality, we have that

p_{\theta,q;Y}(T(x),T(y))\leq 2^{\theta}\cdot\omega_{0}^{1-\theta}\cdot\omega_{1}^{\theta}\cdot p_{\theta,q;X}(x,y).

From this inequality, it is immediate that

\Delta_{\theta,q;Y}(T(x),T(y))\leq 2^{\theta}\cdot\omega_{0}^{1-\theta}\cdot\omega_{1}^{\theta}\cdot\Delta_{\theta,q;X}(x,y),\quad x,y\in(\overrightarrow{X}_{\theta,q}^{J_{M}})^{\prime}

(19)

And this concludes the proof.
∎

Remark 4.1
.

If we use the result that

J_{M}(t;\cdot,\cdot)\leq\max\{1,t/s\}\cdot J_{M}(s;\cdot,\cdot)

we can eliminate the dependence on
\theta
from the upper bound of the Lipschitz constant of the operator
T
in the interpolated space. In this situation, setting

and

\Delta_{\theta,q;Y}(T(x),T(y))\leq\max\{\omega_{0},\omega_{1}\}\cdot\Delta_{\theta,q;X}(x,y),\quad x,y\in(\overrightarrow{X}_{\theta,q}^{J_{M}})^{\prime}.

(20)

We choose to display the possible dependence on

because it is a constant more
characteristic of the language in Interpolation Theory and expresses some type of convexity.

Proposition 4.5
.

If
X_{0}=X_{1}=X
and
d_{0}=d_{1}=d_{X}
. Given
\theta\in(0,1)
and
q\in[1,\infty]
, we have

(\overrightarrow{X}_{\theta,q}^{J_{M}})^{\prime}=X

and the metric
\Delta_{\theta,q}
is equivalent to the metric
d_{X}
.

Proof.

For
t>0
and
u,v\in X
,

J_{M}(t;u,v)=\max\{d_{X}(u,v),\,t\,d_{X}(u,v)\}=\begin{cases}d_{X}(u,v),&0<t\leq 1,\\[4.0pt]
t\,d_{X}(u,v),&t\geq 1.\end{cases}

In particular, for
t=1
we have
J_{M}(1;u,v)=d_{X}(u,v)
, and the metric
K_{M}(1;\cdot,\cdot)
defined by infimum over linking sequences coincides with the original metric
d_{X}
(since the cost per step is always
h_{1}(\cdot,\cdot)=d_{X}(\cdot,\cdot)
).
By inequalities (
12
) and (
13
)):

K_{M}(1;u,v)\leq M_{\theta,q}\,p_{\theta,q}(u,v),

(21)

and for constant sequences,

p_{\theta,q}(u,v)\leq J_{M}(1;u,v).

(22)

In the trivial case,
K_{M}(1;u,v)=d_{X}(u,v)
and
J_{M}(1;u,v)=d_{X}(u,v)
. Substituting these equalities into (
21
) and (
22
) we obtain, for all
u,v\in X
,

d_{X}(u,v)\leq M_{\theta,q}\,p_{\theta,q}(u,v)\qquad\text{and}\qquad p_{\theta,q}(u,v)\leq d_{X}(u,v).

Rewriting,

\frac{1}{M_{\theta,q}}\,d_{X}(u,v)\;\leq\;p_{\theta,q}(u,v)\;\leq\;d_{X}(u,v).

(23)

The inequalities (
23
) show that
p_{\theta,q}
is equivalent to the metric

d_{X}
(with explicit constants
1/M_{\theta,q}
and
1
). We have

P_{\theta,q}(u,v)=\tfrac{1}{2}\bigl(p_{\theta,q}(u,v)+p_{\theta,q}(v,u)\bigr),

and

\delta_{\theta,q}(x,y)=\inf\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}P_{\theta,q}(x_{j},x_{j+1}),

where the infimum runs over all linking sequences
x_{0}=x,\dots,x_{n}=y
in
X_{0}\cap X_{1}=X
. Since
p_{\theta,q}
satisfies (
23
), the same inequalities hold for
P_{\theta,q}
. More precisely, using (
23
) and the definition of
P_{\theta,q}
, we obtain

\frac{1}{M_{\theta,q}}\,d_{X}(u,v)\leq P_{\theta,q}(u,v)\leq d_{X}(u,v),\qquad\forall u,v\in X.

Now, by the definition of
\delta_{\theta,q}
, we have

\frac{1}{M_{\theta,q}}\,d_{X}(x,y)\leq\delta_{\theta,q}(x,y)\leq d_{X}(x,y),\qquad\forall x,y\ inX.

Therefore
\delta_{\theta,q}
is equivalent to
d_{X}
. Since
(\overrightarrow{X}_{\theta,q}^{J_{M}})^{\prime}
was defined as the relative completion of
(X_{0}\cap X_{1},\delta_{\theta,q})
in
(X_{0}\cup X_{1},K_{M}(1))
and here

, it holds that

(\overrightarrow{X}_{\theta,q}^{J_{M}})^{\prime}=X.

Since

on

, we have

\frac{1}{M_{\theta,q}}\,d_{X}(x,y)\leq\Delta_{\theta,q}(x,y)\leq d_{X}(x,y),\qquad\forall x,y\ \in X

as we wanted to demonstrate.
∎

Remark 4.4
.

The metric interpolation developed in this work can interact with non-linear structures relevant in analysis and PDEs. A natural example is the space of probabilities
\mathcal{P}_{2}(\Omega)
equipped with the Wasserstein distance
W_{2}
, which is a complete and geodesic metric space, but non-linear.
In many evolution problems — such as gradient flows associated with energy functionals in the sense of Jordan–Kinderlehrer–Otto — regularity on different metric scales plays a crucial role. The simultaneous existence of two metric controls (one more “fine”, the other more “coarse”) suggests the possibility of applying metric interpolation constructions to obtain intermediate estimates of stability or temporal continuity.

Although such applications exceed the scope of this article, the abstract structure developed here provides a framework that can be used to investigate gradient flows, metric compactifications, and regularization properties in Wasserstein-type spaces.

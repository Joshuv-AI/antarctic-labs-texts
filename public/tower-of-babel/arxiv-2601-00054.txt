K-stability of Q \mathbb{Q} -Fano Spherical Varieties via Compatible Divisors
Renpeng Zheng

Abstract
. We study the K-stability of Q \mathbb{Q} -Fano spherical varieties using compatible divisors. More precisely, if the Q \mathbb{Q} -Fano variety, with a reductive group action, has an open Borel subgroup orbit, then there is a unique anticanonical Q \mathbb{Q} -divisor computing the equivariant stability threshold. This Q \mathbb{Q} -divisor is invariant under the Borel subgroup action, and it characterizes the K-stability of a Q \mathbb{Q} -Fano spherical variety.

Introduction

For a
\mathbb{Q}
-Fano variety
X
, K-stability provides a criterion for the existence of Kähler-Einstein metrics, which is used to be known as the Fano Yau-Tian-Donaldson conjecture.
It is proved in
[
10
,
11
,
12
]
for smooth varieties (Fano manifolds), and in
[
3
,
21
]
for
\mathbb{Q}
-Fano varieties. See
[
27
]
for a detailed study.

The original definition of K-stability is through (Donaldson’s) Futaki invariant on test configurations, but the invention of the so-called stability threshold (in
[
15
,
4
]
) is crucial in further developments.

Verifying K-stability (or estimating the stability thresholds) for an arbitrary
\mathbb{Q}
-Fano variety was challenging until the Abban-Zhuang method appeared in
[
1
]
. However, several classes of varieties were studied prior to that due to their abundance of symmetries, e.g., toric and spherical varieties.

In this paper, we use the idea of the compatible divisors from
[
1
]
to give an alternative and simpler proof for the formula of equivariant stability threshold for toric and spherical varieties.

In
[
4
]
, the authors show that, for a
\mathbb{Q}
-Fano toric variety
X
with an algebraic torus
T\subseteq X
, the stability threshold
\delta(X)
(see
1.4
) is equal to the log canonical threshold of a
T
-invariant anticanonical
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor, which we denote by
D^{T}_{X}
.
Their proof consists of two parts, first prove
\delta(X)=\delta_{T}(X)
, where
\delta_{T}(X)
is the
T
-equivariant stability threshold (see
Definition

1.8
), and then get a formula of
\delta_{T}(X)
.
The former part simply follows from Zhuang’s equivariant criterion
[
29
]
(see
Lemma

2.7
); the latter part is simplified to a short argument using compatible divisors (see
Section

2.1
).

A direct corollary is a rephrasing of the classical criterion for toric varieties,

Corollary (A)

(c.f.,
Corollary

2.8
)
.

X
is K-semistable iff
D^{T}_{X}=D_{1}+\dots+D_{k}
, where
D_{1},\dots,D_{k}
are the
T
-invariant prime divisors on
X
. That is,
D^{T}_{X}
is identical to the standard choice of the anticanonical divisor for a toric variety.

We then find the analogy of
D^{T}_{X}
on spherical varieties (see
Section

1.3
). Spherical varieties are the natural generalization of toric varieties, flag varieties and symmetric varieties.

Given a connected reductive group
G
and a Borel subgroup
B
of
G
, a spherical
G
-variety is a
G
-variety that admits an open
B
-orbit. See, for example,
[
25
]
for more properties of spherical varieties. Our main result is to find the
B
-invariant anticanonical
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor
D^{B}_{X}
that computes the
G
-equivariant stability threshold
\delta_{G}(X)
(see
Section

1.2.1
) for a
\mathbb{Q}
-Fano spherical
G
-variety
X
.

Theorem (B)

(c.f.,
Theorem

4.1
)
.

Let
X
be a
\mathbb{Q}
-Fano spherical
G
-variety, the
G
-equivariant stability threshold of
X
satisfies

\delta_{G}\mathopen{}\left\lparen X\right\rparen\mathclose{}=\inf\limits_{v\in\mathrm{DivVal}^{G,\circ}_{X}}{A_{X}\mathopen{}\left\lparen v\right\rparen\mathclose{}\over v\mathopen{}\left\lparen D^{B}_{X}\right\rparen\mathclose{}}=\min_{i=1,\dots,k}{A_{X}\mathopen{}\left\lparen v_{i}\right\rparen\mathclose{}\over v_{i}\mathopen{}\left\lparen D^{B}_{X}\right\rparen\mathclose{}}

for some
B
-invariant effective anticanonical
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor
D^{B}_{X}
(which is uniquely determined by
X
), where

—

\mathrm{DivVal}^{G,\circ}_{X}
is the set of non-zero
G
-invariant divisorial valuations on
X
(see
Section

1.1.1
);

—

A_{X}(v)
is the log discrepancy of
v
(see
Section

1.1.2
); and

—

\{v_{i}\}_{i}
is a prescribed finite subset of the
G
-invariant divisorial valuations on
X
.

Remark
.

The second equality follows from the piecewise
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
-linearity of

, see
[

]
(based on
[
23
, Proposition 5.2]
) for the details.

As mentioned earlier, by
[
29
]
, a
\mathbb{Q}
-Fano variety with a group action is K-semistable iff it is equivariantly K-semistable. Therefore,

is the key to study the K-stability of spherical varieties.

There have been already several studies on the K-stability of
\mathbb{Q}
-Fano spherical varieties, via geometric analysis (
[

]
), test configurations (
[
20
]
), and valuative invariants (
[

]
). Our contribution is simplifying

Convention

Throughout this paper, we work over the field of complex numbers
\mathbb{C}
. A
variety
means a reduced, separated scheme of finite type over
\mathbb{C}
, and we further require them to be irreducible and normal. In particular, all algebraic groups are assumed to be connected.
A

\mathbb{C}

Preliminaries

1.1.
Birational Geometry

Given a
\mathbb{Q}
-line bundle
L
, let

\mathbb{N}\mathopen{}\left\lparen L\right\rparen\mathclose{}:=\mathopen{}\left\{m\in\mathbb{N}\,:\,mL\text{ is a line bundle}\right\}\mathclose{}.

1.1.1.
Valuations

A
(
\mathbb{Q}
-)valuation
is a map
v:\mathbb{C}(X)^{\times}\to\mathbb{Q}
that satisfies, for all
f_{1},f_{2}\in\mathbb{C}(X)
,

—

v(\mathbb{C}^{\times})=0
(and as a convention,
v(0)=+\infty
);

—

v(f_{1}+f_{2})\geq\min(v(f_{1}),v(f_{2}))
; and

—

v(f_{1}\cdot f_{2})=v(f_{1})+v(f_{2})
.

We denote the
center
of
v
(if it exists) by
C_{X}(v)\subseteq X
. The center of
v
is a closed irreducible subvariety of
X
satisfying, for every
f\in\mathbb{C}(X)
,
v(f)>0
iff
f=0
on
C_{X}(v)
. The uniqueness of center is guaranteed by separatedness of varieties.

A valuation
v
is called a
valuation on
X
if the center exists. The trivial valuation on
X
always exists, whose center is
X
itself. If
X
is complete (e.g., projective), every valuation on
\mathbb{C}(X)
is a valuation on
X
. We denote all the valuations on
X
by
\mathrm{Val}_{X}
.

A Cartier divisor
D
is locally defined by a single rational function, so we can define
v(D)\in\mathbb{Z}
; similarly, for a
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor
D
, we have
v(D)\in\mathbb{Q}
. If
D
is effective,
v(D)\geq 0
. Valuations are
\mathbb{Q}_{\geq 0}
-linear on effective
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier
\mathbb{Q}
-divisors.

Given a birational morphism
\pi:Y\to X
, a
prime divisor
E
over
X
is defined as a prime divisor of
Y
.
E
defines a valuation
\mathrm{ord}_{E}
on
X
, whose center is
\pi(E)
. A
divisorial (
\mathbb{Q}
-)valuation

v
is of the form
c\>\mathrm{ord}_{E}
(for
c\in\mathbb{Q}_{\geq 0}
), and the space of divisorial valuations on
X
is denoted by
\mathrm{DivVal}_{X}
.

For a variety
X
with an algebraic group
G
acting on, a valuation
v
is
G
-invariant
if it satisfies
v(g\cdot f)=v(f)
for every
g\in G
and
f\in\mathbb{C}(X)
. The space of
G
-invariant valuations is denoted by
\mathrm{Val}^{G}_{X}
, and similarly we have
\mathrm{DivVal}^{G}_{X}
.

1.1.2.
Log Discrepancy

In this section, let
X
be a projective variety with a canonical divisor

(which always exists as

\mathbb{Q}
-Gorenstein

is
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier.

Assume

\mathbb{Q}
-Gorenstein. For every prime divisor

(1)

A_{X}\mathopen{}\left\lparen c\>\mathrm{ord}_{E}\right\rparen\mathclose{}:=c\cdot\mathopen{}\left\lparen 1+\mathrm{ord}_{E}\mathopen{}\left\lparen K_{Y}-\pi^{*}K_{X}\right\rparen\mathclose{}\right\rparen\mathclose{},

where

.

For the definition of log discrepancy for general

-valuations, see

X

over

.

Definition 1.2
.

Suppose

\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier

-divisor

\mathrm{lct}\mathopen{}\left\lparen D\right\rparen\mathclose{}:=\mathrm{lct}\mathopen{}\left\lparen X;D\right\rparen\mathclose{}=\inf\limits_{v\in\mathrm{DivVal}^{\circ}_{X}}{A_{X}\mathopen{}\left\lparen v\right\rparen\mathclose{}\over v\mathopen{}\left\lparen D\right\rparen\mathclose{}};

1.2.
K-stability

A klt variety

(klt)
\mathbb{Q}
-Fano

-K_{X}

Consider a klt variety

For every

Lemma/Definition 1.4

(c.f.,

For every

Given a

1.3.
Spherical Geometry

From now on, let
G
be a (connected) reductive group. We also fix a Borel subgroup
B\subseteq G
and a maximal torus
T\subseteq B

Definition 1.11
.

A

G
-variety

-variety with an open
B
-orbit.

Remark
.

Since

-orbit.

Example 1.12
.

When

, and the open

-orbit is isomorphic to

,

is a

.

We write
\mathfrak{X}(A)
for the

of an algebraic group

, and they are isomorphic to

, where

is the dimension of

.

We will use the following notation. Given a finite-dimensional
G
-module

, for every
\lambda\in\mathfrak{X}(B)
,

—

V_{\lambda}
, the
\lambda
-isotypic subspace
, is the sum of all (isomorphic) simple

-submodules of

with the highest weight
\lambda
; since

is finite-dimensional, we have
\bigoplus\limits_{\lambda\in\mathfrak{X}(B)}V_{\lambda}=V
;

—

V_{\lambda}^{(B)}
is the subset of

-semi-invariant vectors

, i.e.,
V_{\lambda}^{(B)}=\{v\in V\,:\,b\cdot v=\lambda(b)v,\forall b\in B\}

is a vector space, and it is of dimension

is simple.

For a general reference about the representation theory of reductive groups, see, for example,
[
22
]
.

Given a
G
-variety
X
, the function field
\mathbb{C}(X)
is an infinite-dimensional
G
-module, but for every
\lambda\in\mathfrak{X}(B)
, we can still define

And similar to toric varieties, we can define

M:=\mathopen{}\left\{\lambda\in\mathfrak{X}\mathopen{}\left\lparen B\right\rparen\mathclose{}\,:\,\mathbb{C}\mathopen{}\left\lparen X\right\rparen\mathclose{}_{\lambda}^{\mathopen{}\left\lparen B\right\rparen\mathclose{}}\neq 0\right\}\mathclose{},

which is a sub-lattice of

; its rank is called the

of

If

is a spherical variety, any

-invariant rational function on

is constant; equivalently, for every character

\dim(\mathbb{C}(X)_{\lambda}^{(B)})=1
. For every

-invariant divisor; conversely, any principal

-invariant divisor is determined by some

1.3.1.
G
-invariant Valuations

Similar to toric varieties, let

,

\left\langle\cdot,\cdot\right\rangle:M{Q}\times N{Q}\to\mathbb{Q}

Given a valuation

\rho(v)(\lambda):=v(f_{\lambda})
for every
\lambda\in M

\rho(v)\in N{Q}
, i.e.,

(3)

\displaystyle\rho:\mathopen{}\left\{\text{valuations of }\mathbb{C}\mathopen{}\left\lparen X\right\rparen\mathclose{}\right\}\mathclose{}\to N{Q}

Lemma/Definition 1.13

(c.f., e.g.,
[
8
, §4]
)

After restriction,

In other words, each

As a result, we identify a

As

Lemma 1.14

Assume

-variety, we have
\mathrm{Val}^{G}_{X}=\mathrm{DivVal}^{G}_{X}
.

Remark
.

Recall that, in this paper we assumed valuations are
\mathbb{Q}
-valued. Note that there always exists

-valuations which are not of the form

According to

where

is a cone. The subdivision of

corresponds to birational model

of

2.
Compatible Divisors in K-stability

Compatible divisors were first introduced in
[
1
, Definition 2.7]
, based on which the Abban-Zhuang method is developed to estimate (local) stability thresholds. In this section, we will use this concept to review the K-stability of toric varieties.

Definition 2.1
.

A basis
\{s_{1},\dots,s_{N_{m}}\}
of
H^{0}(X,mL)
is called
compatible
with a valuation
v\in\mathrm{DivVal}_{X}
if for every
s\in H^{0}(X,mL)
,

v\mathopen{}\left\lparen s\right\rparen\mathclose{}=\min_{c_{j}\neq 0}v\mathopen{}\left\lparen s_{j}\right\rparen\mathclose{},

where
s=\sum\limits c_{j}s_{j}
, and
c_{j}\in\mathbb{C}
.
When
\{s_{1},\dots,s_{N_{m}}\}
is compatible with
v
,
D:={1\over mN_{m}}\sum\limits_{j}\mathrm{div}(s_{j})
is called an
m
-basis type
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor
compatible
with
v
.

The following lemma appears in the proof of
[
1
, Proposition 3.1]
and is hidden in
[
4
, §2.6 and Lemma 3.5]
. We write a short proof here for completeness.

Lemma 2.2
.

For every valuation
v\in\mathrm{DivVal}_{X}
,
S_{m}(L;v)=v(D)
if the
m
-basis type
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor
D
is compatible with
v
.

Remark
.

There is always some
m
-basis type
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor compatible with
v
. One can easily construct a compatible basis with the help of the valuative filtration, see
[
4
, §3.1]
. Moreover, there always exists some compatible
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor compatible with any two valuations simultaneously, see
[
1
, Lemma 3.1]
.

Proof.

Assume the basis
\{s_{j}\}_{j=1}^{N_{m}}
is compatible with
v
, and reorder it so that

v\mathopen{}\left\lparen s_{1}\right\rparen\mathclose{}\leq\dots\leq v\mathopen{}\left\lparen s_{N_{m}}\right\rparen\mathclose{}.

Given another basis
\{s_{j}^{\prime}\}_{j=1}^{N_{m}}
, similarly we can order it as above. Assume there exists an
s_{j_{0}}^{\prime}
such that
v(s_{j_{0}}^{\prime})>v(s_{j_{0}})
, then
v(s_{j_{0}+1}^{\prime}),\dots,v(s_{N_{m}}^{\prime})>v(s_{j_{0}})
and we have a linearly independent subset

\mathopen{}\left\{s_{j_{0}}^{\prime},s_{j_{0}+1}^{\prime},\dots,s_{N_{m}}^{\prime}\right\}\mathclose{}\subseteq\mathopen{}\left\{s\in H^{0}\mathopen{}\left\lparen X,mL\right\rparen\mathclose{}\,:\,v\mathopen{}\left\lparen s\right\rparen\mathclose{}>v\mathopen{}\left\lparen s_{j_{0}}\right\rparen\mathclose{}\right\}\mathclose{}\subseteq\left\langle s_{j_{0}+1},\dots,s_{N_{m}}\right\rangle{C},

which is impossible. Therefore, for every
j=1,\dots,N_{m}
,
v(s_{j}^{\prime})\leq v(s_{j})
, and thus the basis
\{s_{j}\}_{j=1}^{N_{m}}
achieves the supreme.
∎

Recall that, to compute
S_{m}(L;v)
by definition (see
1.5
), we need to consider all (and infinitely many) basis of
H^{0}(X,mL)
. The compatibility provides another way to compute
S_{m}(L;v)
. As valuations are invariant under (non-zero) scalar multiplication, we can generalize the compatibility from the bases to decompositions of vector spaces.

Definition 2.3
.

For every valuation
v\in\mathrm{DivVal}_{X}
, a decomposition
H^{0}(X,mL)=\bigoplus\limits_{J=1}^{k}V_{J}
(as a vector space) is
compatible
with
v
if

v\mathopen{}\left\lparen f\right\rparen\mathclose{}=\min_{J=1,...,k}v\mathopen{}\left\lparen f_{J}\right\rparen\mathclose{},

for every
f=\sum\limits f_{J}\in\bigoplus\limits_{J=1}^{k}V_{J}
.

Similar to compatible bases, we can use compatible decompositions to simplify the computation of
S_{m}(L;v)
: we only need to consider the basis of
H^{0}(X,mL)
which is “compatible” with the decomposition.

Lemma 2.4
.

Under the assumption of
Definition

2.3
, we have
S_{m}(L;v)=\sup\limits_{D}v(D)
, where
D
runs through all the
m
-basis type
\mathbb{Q}
-divisors defined from bases of the form
\bigcup\limits_{J=1}^{k}\mathcal{B}_{J}\subseteq H^{0}(X,mL)
, where
\mathcal{B}_{J}
is a basis of
V_{J}
; equivalently,

S_{m}\mathopen{}\left\lparen L;v\right\rparen\mathclose{}={1\over N_{m}}\sum\limits_{J=1}^{k}N_{m,J}\cdot S_{m}\mathopen{}\left\lparen V_{J};v\right\rparen\mathclose{},

the (weighted) average, where
N_{m,J}:=\dim(V_{J})
.

Remark
.

When
V_{J}=\mathbb{C}s
,
S_{m}(V_{J};v)={1\over m}v(s)
, this lemma reduces to
Lemma

2.2
.

Proof.

For every
V_{J}
, we can find a basis
\mathcal{B}_{J}\subseteq V_{J}
compatible with
v
, and we have

S_{m}\mathopen{}\left\lparen V_{J};v\right\rparen\mathclose{}={1\over mN_{m,J}}\sum\limits_{j}v\mathopen{}\left\lparen s_{J,j}\right\rparen\mathclose{},

then
\bigcup\limits\mathcal{B}_{J}
is a basis of
H^{0}(X,mL)
compatible with
v
, which means

S_{m}\mathopen{}\left\lparen L;v\right\rparen\mathclose{}={1\over mN_{m}}\sum\limits_{J,j}v\mathopen{}\left\lparen s_{J,j}\right\rparen\mathclose{}.

∎

2.1.
Warm-up Application: Toric Varieties

In this section, we will find an anticanonical
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor computing the stability threshold of a toric variety. The idea is to prove that this
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor is compatible with all toric valuations. Then, in
Section

4
, similarly, we will find an anticanonical
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor for spherical varieties.

We will follow the notations and some simple results from
[
5
, §7]
, which is based on
[
16
]
.

For a toric
T
-variety

, let
M:=\mathfrak{X}(T)
, the character lattice, and
M{Q}:=M\otimes{Z}\mathbb{Q}
; let
\chi^{u}\in\mathbb{C}(T)=\mathbb{C}(X)
be the character of

associated to

, thought of which as a rational function on

; let
X_{1},\dots,X_{k}
be the
T
-invariant prime divisors of
X
.

Given a line bundle
L
, we can pick a
T
-invariant divisor
D_{0}
linearly equivalent to
L
, and let
P\subseteq M{Q}
be the convex (
\mathbb{Q}
-)polytope associated to
D_{0}
.
Now
H^{0}(X,mD_{0})\subseteq\mathbb{C}(X)
is a finite-dimensional
T
-submodule, and it can be decomposed into
1
-dimensional
T
-submodules, as

(4)

H^{0}\mathopen{}\left\lparen X,mD_{0}\right\rparen\mathclose{}=\bigoplus\limits_{u\in mP\cap M}\mathbb{C}\cdot\chi^{u};

we can define the
m
-basis type
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor

D_{m}={1\over mN_{m}}\sum\limits_{u\in mP\cap M}\mathopen{}\left\lparen mD_{0}+\mathrm{div}\mathopen{}\left\lparen\chi^{u}\right\rparen\mathclose{}\right\rparen\mathclose{}=D_{0}+{1\over N_{m}}\sum\limits{1\over m}\mathrm{div}\mathopen{}\left\lparen\chi^{u}\right\rparen\mathclose{}.

Now, let
D^{T}_{L}:=D_{0}+{1\over m^{\prime}}\mathrm{div}(\chi^{m^{\prime}\bar{u}})
, where
\bar{u}\in P
is the barycenter and
m^{\prime}\bar{u}\in M
for some
m^{\prime}\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{0\}

\mathbb{Q}
-divisor

is the limit of the sequence

in the sense of coefficients; equivalently,

(5)

v\mathopen{}\left\lparen D^{T}_{L}\right\rparen\mathclose{}=\lim\limits_{m\to\infty}v\mathopen{}\left\lparen D_{m}\right\rparen\mathclose{}

for every

-invariant valuation

.

The
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor

is independent of the choice of

(and thus the
T
-linearization). It is uniquely determined by the ample (
\mathbb{Q}
-)line bundle

is the anticanonical divisor, we write

for

.

Theorem 2.5

(c.f.,
[
4
, Theorem F and Corollary 7.4]
)
.

\delta\mathopen{}\left\lparen X\right\rparen\mathclose{}=\inf\limits_{v\in\mathrm{DivVal}^{T,\circ}_{X}}{A_{X}\mathopen{}\left\lparen v\right\rparen\mathclose{}\over v\mathopen{}\left\lparen D^{T}_{L}\right\rparen\mathclose{}}=\min_{i=1,\dots,k}{1\over a_{i}},

where
a_{1}X_{1}+\dots+a_{k}X_{k}=D^{T}_{X}
, and
X_{1},\dots,X_{k}
are the
T
-invariant prime divisors.

Remark
.

By
[
4
, Proposition 7.3]
,
\mathrm{lct}(D)=\inf\limits_{v\in\mathrm{DivVal}^{T,\circ}_{X}}{A_{X}(v)\over v(D)}
for every
T
-invariant
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor
D
, so we have
\delta(X)=\mathrm{lct}(D^{T}_{X})
. The latter is not true for spherical varieties, and cannot be used in theory of spherical varieties.

The formula of Blum and Jonsson for toric varieties is miraculously simple, and it completely solves the K-(semi)stability problem of toric varieties. We are going to reprove this theorem in two steps. The first step is to use the compatible divisors to prove,

Lemma 2.6
.

For every
v\in\mathrm{DivVal}^{T,\circ}_{X}
and all sufficiently divisible integer
m
,
S(v)=v(D^{T}_{L})
, and thus
\delta_{T}(L)=\inf\limits_{v\in\mathrm{DivVal}^{T,\circ}_{X}}{A_{X}(v)\over v(D^{T}_{L})}
(see
Section

1.2.1
).

Proof.

We only need to prove for every
v\in\mathrm{DivVal}^{T}_{X}
,
S_{m}(v)=v(D_{m})
, i.e., to prove
D_{m}
is compatible with
v
. However,
T
-invariant valuations are exactly “toric valuations”, which means,

v\mathopen{}\left\lparen\sum\limits_{u\in M}c_{u}\chi^{u}\right\rparen\mathclose{}=\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\in M\\
c_{u}\neq 0\end{subarray}}v\mathopen{}\left\lparen\chi^{u}\right\rparen\mathclose{}.

This is exactly the definition of compatibility.
∎

An intermediate result is

(6)

\delta_{m,T}\mathopen{}\left\lparen L\right\rparen\mathclose{}=\inf\limits_{v\in\mathrm{DivVal}^{T,\circ}_{X}}{A_{X}\mathopen{}\left\lparen v\right\rparen\mathclose{}\over v\mathopen{}\left\lparen D_{m}\right\rparen\mathclose{}},

which we will use to prove the next lemma.

The second step is

Lemma 2.7

(c.f.,
[
4
, Corollary 7.15]
)
.

\delta(X)=\delta_{T}(X)
.

Proof.

The original proof of Blum and Jonsson deforms the general valuative filtrations into their initial filtrations (see
[
4
, §7.4]
). We are going to use Zhuang’s theorem (see
Theorem

1.10
) instead.

The standard anticanonical divisor is
-K_{X}=X_{1}+\dots+X_{k}
for the toric variety
X
, and the effective
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor
D_{m}\sim{Q}-K_{X}
is of the form
D_{m}=a_{1}X_{1}+\dots+a_{k}X_{k}
for sufficiently divisible integer
m
. There must be an element
a_{i}\geq 1
(otherwise, we have
D_{m}<-K_{X}
), and by
Equation

6
,

\delta_{m,T}\mathopen{}\left\lparen X\right\rparen\mathclose{}\leq{A_{X}\mathopen{}\left\lparen\mathrm{ord}_{X_{i}}\right\rparen\mathclose{}\over\mathrm{ord}_{X_{i}}\mathopen{}\left\lparen D_{m}\right\rparen\mathclose{}}={1\over a_{i}}\leq 1.

After taking the limit we have
\delta_{T}(X)\leq 1
. As
\delta(X)\leq\delta_{T}(X)\leq 1
, we can apply Zhuang’s theorem to show
\delta(X)=\delta_{T}(X)
.
∎

Proof of
Theorem

2.5
.

We just combine
Lemma

2.6
and
Lemma

2.7
. The last equality is a well known property of toric varieties, see, for example,
[
4
, Proposition 7.2]
.
∎

Recall that, a
\mathbb{Q}
-Fano variety

is K-semistable iff
\delta(X)\geq 1
, we have

Corollary 2.8
.

A
\mathbb{Q}
-Fano toric variety

is K-semistable iff
D^{T}_{X}=X_{1}+\dots+X_{k}
.

Remark
.

This is just another way of expressing
[
4
, Corollary 7.17]
.

Spherical varieties are a generalization of toric varieties. We are going to construct similar

-divisors for
\mathbb{Q}
-Fano spherical varieties.

3.
Rational Linear Series for Spherical Varieties

To find a “special” anticanonical
\mathbb{Q}
-divisors for a spherical variety, we need to construct the “space of
\mathbb{Q}
-divisors”.

Definition 3.1
.

Consider a polarized variety
(X,L)
, the
(complete)
\mathbb{Q}
-linear series

\mathopen{}\left|L\right|\mathclose{}{Q}
is defined as the set of effective
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier
\mathbb{Q}
-divisors
\mathbb{Q}
-linearly equivalent to

For every

{1\over m}\mathopen{}\left|mL\right|\mathclose{}\subseteq\mathopen{}\left|L\right|\mathclose{}{Q}
, and by definition,
\mathopen{}\left|L\right|\mathclose{}{Q}=\bigcup\limits{1\over m}\mathopen{}\left|mL\right|\mathclose{}

m

The
\mathbb{Q}
-linear series
\mathopen{}\left|L\right|\mathclose{}{Q}
is a subset of the
\mathbb{Q}
-vector space
\mathrm{CDiv}(X){Q}:=\mathrm{CDiv}(X)\otimes\mathbb{Q}

\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier
\mathbb{Q}
-divisors of

\mathopen{}\left|L\right|\mathclose{}{Q}
is
(
\mathbb{Q}
-)convex

Proposition 3.2

For every
D,E\in\mathopen{}\left|L\right|\mathclose{}{Q}
, the convex combination
tD+(1-t)E\in\mathopen{}\left|L\right|\mathclose{}{Q}
, for every
t\in\mathbb{Q}\cap[0,1]

Proof.

Fix
t={p\over p+q}
(where
p,q\in\mathbb{N},p+q>0
),
D\in{1\over m}\mathopen{}\left|mL\right|\mathclose{},E\in{1\over m^{\prime}}\mathopen{}\left|m^{\prime}L\right|\mathclose{}

mm^{\prime}pD\in\mathopen{}\left|mm^{\prime}pL\right|\mathclose{},\quad mm^{\prime}qE\in\mathopen{}\left|mm^{\prime}qL\right|\mathclose{}

tD+(1-t)E\in{1\over mm^{\prime}(p+q)}\mathopen{}\left|mm^{\prime}(p+q)L\right|\mathclose{}\subseteq\mathopen{}\left|L\right|\mathclose{}{Q}
.
∎

Just as
\mathrm{CDiv}(X){Q}
,
\mathopen{}\left|L\right|\mathclose{}{Q}
is infinite-dimensional. We do not have enough tools to study it.

Luckily, for a toric

-variety

, the subset of
\mathopen{}\left|L\right|\mathclose{}{Q}
of
T
-invariant
\mathbb{Q}
-divisors, denoted by

.

Suppose

is a line bundle. Pick a
T
-invariant divisor
D_{0}
linearly equivalent to

, and let

be the convex (

-)polytope associated to

\displaystyle q:P

\displaystyle\to\mathopen{}\left|L\right|\mathclose{}{Q}^{T},

\displaystyle u

Now, fix a reductive group

The following result is directly deduced from the definition of moment polytope,

Lemma 3.6

For every
\lambda\in P(X,L,\alpha)

(as a
\mathbb{Q}

Proof.

The first part is a reformulation of
[

(see also

For every

3.2.
Convex Projections

\displaystyle s

After fixing a

We can define the following map,

(8)

\displaystyle p_{\alpha}:\mathopen{}\left|L\right|\mathclose{}{Q}^{B}

\displaystyle\to P\mathopen{}\left\lparen X,L,\alpha\right\rparen\mathclose{},

\displaystyle D

\displaystyle\mapsto\lambda/m,

where
D=\mathrm{div}{Q}(s)
for some
m\in\mathbb{N}(L,\alpha)
,
\lambda\in\mathfrak{X}(B)
and
s\in H^{0}(X,mL)^{(B)}_{\lambda}
.

Proposition 3.7
.

p_{\alpha}
is a well-defined
\mathbb{Q}
-convex-linear surjective map. If
X
is spherical,
p_{\alpha}
is a convex-linear isomorphism.

Proof.

First, to show it is well-defined (then surjectivity is from the definition of moment polytopes), for every

s\in H^{0}\mathopen{}\left\lparen X,mL\right\rparen\mathclose{}^{\mathopen{}\left\lparen B\right\rparen\mathclose{}}_{\lambda},\text{ and }s^{\prime}\in H^{0}\mathopen{}\left\lparen X,m^{\prime}L\right\rparen\mathclose{}^{\mathopen{}\left\lparen B\right\rparen\mathclose{}}_{\lambda^{\prime}},

assume
\mathrm{div}{Q}(s)=\mathrm{div}{Q}(s^{\prime})
, we have (up to a coefficient)
s^{m^{\prime}}=(s^{\prime})^{m}
, which means

\lambda/m=\lambda^{\prime}/m^{\prime}.

Second, to prove it is a
\mathbb{Q}
-convex linear map, consider

s\in H^{0}\mathopen{}\left\lparen X,mL\right\rparen\mathclose{}^{\mathopen{}\left\lparen B\right\rparen\mathclose{}}_{\lambda},\quad s^{\prime}\in H^{0}\mathopen{}\left\lparen X,mL\right\rparen\mathclose{}^{\mathopen{}\left\lparen B\right\rparen\mathclose{}}_{\lambda^{\prime}}

(it is always possible to find a common
m\in\mathbb{N}(L,\alpha)
) and
t={p\over p+q}\in\mathbb{Q}\cap[0,1]
(where
p,q\in\mathbb{N},p+q>0
). Observe that

s^{p}{s^{\prime}}^{q}\in H^{0}\mathopen{}\left\lparen X,\mathopen{}\left\lparen p+q\right\rparen\mathclose{}mL\right\rparen\mathclose{}^{\mathopen{}\left\lparen B\right\rparen\mathclose{}}_{p\lambda+q\lambda^{\prime}},

which results in

\displaystyle p_{\alpha}\mathopen{}\left\lparen t\cdot\mathrm{div}{Q}\mathopen{}\left\lparen s\right\rparen\mathclose{}+\mathopen{}\left\lparen 1-t\right\rparen\mathclose{}\cdot\mathrm{div}{Q}\mathopen{}\left\lparen s^{\prime}\right\rparen\mathclose{}\right\rparen\mathclose{}

\displaystyle=p_{\alpha}\mathopen{}\left\lparen\mathrm{div}{Q}\mathopen{}\left\lparen s^{p}{s^{\prime}}^{q}\right\rparen\mathclose{}\right\rparen\mathclose{}

\displaystyle={p\lambda+q\lambda^{\prime}\over\mathopen{}\left\lparen p+q\right\rparen\mathclose{}m}=t\cdot{\lambda\over m}+\mathopen{}\left\lparen 1-t\right\rparen\mathclose{}\cdot{\lambda^{\prime}\over m}

\displaystyle=t\cdot p_{\alpha}\mathopen{}\left\lparen\mathrm{div}{Q}\mathopen{}\left\lparen s\right\rparen\mathclose{}\right\rparen\mathclose{}+\mathopen{}\left\lparen 1-t\right\rparen\mathclose{}\cdot p_{\alpha}\mathopen{}\left\lparen\mathrm{div}{Q}\mathopen{}\left\lparen s^{\prime}\right\rparen\mathclose{}\right\rparen\mathclose{}.

Finally, if
X
is spherical,
H^{0}(X,mL)^{(B)}_{\lambda}
is
1
-dimensional, so we can define the inverse map

q:P\mathopen{}\left\lparen X,L,\alpha\right\rparen\mathclose{}\to\mathopen{}\left|L\right|\mathclose{}{Q}^{B},

such that, for every
u=\lambda/m\in{1\over m}P_{m}(X,L,\alpha)
, there is a unique (up to scaling)
s\in H^{0}(X,mL)^{(B)}_{\lambda}
. Note that
\mathrm{div}{Q}(s)
is independent of the choice of
m
. It is straightforward to check the convex-linearity of
q
by repeating the argument above.
∎

Let

Proof of

For every

Markus Banagl

Abstract
. We construct an equivariant L-class for orientation preserving actions of a compact Lie group on a Whitney stratified compact oriented pseudomanifold that satisfies the Witt condition, for example on a compact pure-dimensional complex algebraic variety. The class lies in equivariant rational homology and its restriction to the trivial group is the Goresky-MacPherson L-class. For a smooth action on a manifold, the class is equivariantly Poincaré dual to the Hirzebruch L-class of the Borel homotopy quotient of the tangent bundle. We also provide a product formula under the equivariant Künneth isomorphism. If the group acts freely, then the equivariant L-class identifies with the Goresky-MacPherson L-class of the orbit space. The construction method rests on establishing Whitney (B)-regularity for finite-dimensional compact pseudomanifold approximations to the Borel construction, and on the author’s Verdier-Riemann-Roch type formulae for the Goresky-MacPherson L-class.

[

]
,

Our method rests on the Verdier-Riemann-Roch (VRR) type formulae for the

-class established in

[
5

(immersive case) and

[

Let

-invariant and the induced action on the stratum is smooth.
If

We apply this principle to compact smooth Stiefel manifolds

-connected approximations to a free contractible

-space

is Whitney (B)-regular.
We go on to show that if

),

is a pseudomanifold

Applying this to the free

Orientability questions are treated in Section

The main result there is that the

The VRR-Theorem is then used (Proposition

constitute an element of the inverse limit defining equivariant homology

This element is

(Definition

Equivariant homology is reviewed in Section
7

It is generally nontrivial in negative degrees and thus not the nonequivariant homology of

Oriented compact
G

If

The above construction of

The equivariant
L
-class has the following properties:
The top-degree class is the equivariant fundamental class
[X]_{G}
.
If

is smooth, then

is the equivariant Poincaré dual
of the usual equivariant cohomology class
L^{*}_{G}(M)=L^{*}((TM)_{G})

is the homotopy quotient vector bundle of the equivariant tangent

Functoriality in the group variable is established in

:
An inclusion

to
L^{G^{\prime}}_{*}(X)
.
The class

contains all of the information of the

Equivariant homology satisfies a Künneth theorem,

We show in Theorem

-class satisfies the product formula

If

acts trivially on

H^{G}_{*}(X;\mathbb{Q})\cong H^{G}_{*}(\operatorname{pt};\mathbb{Q})\otimes H_{*}(X;\mathbb{Q})
.
In this case, the above product formula implies that

L^{G}_{*}(X)=[\operatorname{pt}]_{G}\times L_{*}(X)
,
see Proposition
13.1
.
For free actions, there is a canonical identification

H^{G}_{*}(X;\mathbb{Q})\cong H_{*-\dim G}(X/G;\mathbb{Q})

corresponds to the
Goresky-MacPherson class

of the orbit space
(Theorem

).

The

-class VRR-formulae of
[
5
]
and

[
6
]
have been provided in a
piecewise linear (PL) context. The technical Appendix

15

in the present paper. The key point is this: the ambient

Under piecewise differentiable (PD) homeomorphisms of base and total space, the

that underlies the smooth manifold

(forgetting the group structure).
We use the fact that Whitney stratified

[
18
]
, Verona
[
40
]
).

The equivariant version of Chern-Schwartz-MacPherson classes

Equivariant intersection theory in equivariant Chow groups for actions of

The work of Brasselet, Schürmann and Yokura (

In
[

, Weber developed an equivariant version of that

.
In all of the above complex algebraic situations, the construction rests on an
algebraic approximation of the classifying space

used by Totaro in
[
35
]
.

For the trivial group, the Goresky-MacPherson class

is the
Pontrjagin character of a

,

[
7

,
[

.

K

)

For a finite group

(satisfying weak regularity properties on the fixed point sets),

2.
Stratifications

The main results of the present paper are established for
Whitney stratified subsets of smooth manifolds.
This concept involves both purely topological assumptions on the stratified
space, and regularity requirements on how the space is embedded.
In this section, we recall the former assumptions, while a review of the latter
requirements are the subject of Section
3
.
Any closed subspace of a smooth manifold is automatically separable, locally compact
and Hausdorff.
Hence, with a view towards Whitney stratified subsets of manifolds,
we may focus on spaces with these properties.
Thus let
X
be a separable locally compact Hausdorff space and
let
P
be a poset with order relation
<
. Given
i,j\in P
,
we will write
i\leq j
if
i<j
or
i=j
.
A
stratification

\mathcal{S}
of
X
over
P
is a partition

\mbox{$\mathcal{S}$}=\{S_{i}~|~i\in P\}
of
X
into locally closed subspaces

S_{i}\subset X
(called the
strata
) such that

(S1)
\mathcal{S}
is locally finite,

(S2)
S_{i}
is a smooth manifold for every
i\in P
, and

(S3)
\mathcal{S}
satisfies the
condition of frontier
:

S_{i}
intersects the closure
\overline{S}_{j}
of
S_{j}
nontrivially if and only if

S_{i}\subset\overline{S}_{j}
. This is to be the case if and only if

i\leq j
.

The pair
(X,\mbox{$\mathcal{S}$})
will then be called a
stratified space
.
If
X
is compact, then (S1) implies that
\mathcal{S}
contains only finitely many
strata: Every point has an open neighborhood that intersects only finitely
many strata, and
X
is covered by a finite number of such neighborhoods.
If
\mathcal{S}
is a stratification of
X
and
N
a nonempty smooth manifold, then

\mbox{$\mathcal{S}$}\times N
will denote the partition
\{S_{i}\times N~|~i\in P\}

of
X\times N
. The proof of the following lemma is a straightforward
verification of the stratification axioms (S1) – (S3).

Lemma 2.1
.

The partition
\mbox{$\mathcal{S}$}\times N
is a stratification of
X\times N
.

The stratification
\mbox{$\mathcal{S}$}\times N
is called the

product stratification
on
X\times N
.

3.
Whitney Stratified Spaces

In order to recall Whitney’s condition of
(B)-regularity
, we begin
by setting up notation concerning the parallel translation of
planes in Euclidean space.
If
a,b\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
are points in Euclidean space, we shall write

[a,b]
for the line segment consisting of the points
ta+(1-t)b
,
t\in[0,1]
.
If
a\not=b
, then the parallel translate of the affine line

\{ta+(1-t)b~|~t\in\mathbb{R}\}
to the origin

will be
denoted by
G[a,b]
. We may then regard
G[a,b]

as a linear subspace of
\mathbb{R}^{k}
, or as a point of
projective space
\mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}^{k-1}
.
If we wish to emphasize the ambient dimension, we shall also write

G_{k}[a,b]
for
G[a,b]
.
As a one-dimensional linear subspace of
\mathbb{R}^{k}
,
G[a,b]
is spanned by
the nonzero vector
b-a
.
The following simple observation will assist us in proving
Lemma
3.7
on the product of a
Whitney stratified subset with a manifold factor.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\pi:\mathbb{R}^{k}\times\mathbb{R}^{j}\to\mathbb{R}^{k}
be the projection onto the
first factor. Let
a,b\in\mathbb{R}^{k+j}=\mathbb{R}^{k}\times\mathbb{R}^{j}
be points
such that
b-a\not\in\ker\pi
. Then

\pi[a,b]=[\pi(a),\pi(b)]~\text{ and }~G_{k}[\pi(a),\pi(b)]=\pi(G_{k+j}[a,b]).

More generally,
a
d
-dimensional
affine plane
in Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{k}
is a subset

P\subset\mathbb{R}^{k}
which can be written in the form

P=p+P_{0},

where
p\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
and
P_{0}\subset\mathbb{R}^{k}
is a linear subspace of
dimension
d
.
The linear subspace
P_{0}
is uniquely determined by
P
, the point
p
is not.
The
Gauss map

G_{k}
, defined on the set of

d
-dimensional affine planes in
\mathbb{R}^{k},

is given by
G_{k}(P):=P_{0}.

We may regard the translate
G_{k}(P)
as a linear subspace of
\mathbb{R}^{k}
or
as a point of the Grassmannian
G(d,k)
of
d
-planes through the origin in
\mathbb{R}^{k}
.
If
P
happens to be a linear subspace of
\mathbb{R}^{k}
, then

G_{k}(P)=P.
In particular,
G_{k}(\mathbb{R}^{k})=\mathbb{R}^{k}.

The behavior of Gauss maps under splittings, described in the following lemma,
will be useful in establishing both Lemma
3.7
and
Theorem
4.3
on orbit spaces of free actions.

Lemma 3.2
.

If
P_{j}\subset\mathbb{R}^{j}
and

P_{n}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
are affine planes, then
P=P_{j}\times P_{n}
is an
affine plane in
\mathbb{R}^{k}=\mathbb{R}^{j}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
and

G_{j+n}(P)=G_{j}(P_{j})\times G_{n}(P_{n}).

Example 3.3
.

Let
\ell\subset\mathbb{R}^{j}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
be an affine line
of the form

\ell=\{0_{\mathbb{R}^{j}}\}\times\ell_{n},

where
\ell_{n}
is an affine line in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
Then by Lemma
3.2
,

G_{j+n}(\ell)=G_{j}(\{0_{\mathbb{R}^{j}}\})\times G_{n}(\ell_{n})=\{0_{\mathbb{R}^{j}}\}\times G_{n}(\ell_{n}).

Example 3.4
.

Let
T\subset\mathbb{R}^{j}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
be an affine plane
of the form

T=\mathbb{R}^{j}\times T_{n},

where
T_{n}
is an affine plane in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
Then by Lemma
3.2
,

G_{j+n}(T)=G_{j}(\mathbb{R}^{j})\times G_{n}(T_{n})=\mathbb{R}^{j}\times G_{n}(T_{n}).

The tangent space to a smooth manifold
M
at a point
p\in M
will
be denoted by
T_{p}M
.

Suppose that
(X,\mbox{$\mathcal{S}$})
is a stratified space whose underlying topological space

X
is a closed subset
X\subset M
of a smooth manifold
M
of dimension
n
such
that the strata of
\mathcal{S}
are smooth submanifolds of
M
.
Let
R,S
be two different strata in
\mathcal{S}
such that

R\subset\overline{S}
and let

be a point in
R
.
Recall that the pair
(R,S)
is said to be
Whitney (B) regular at
x
,
if there exists a smooth chart

\chi:U\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
of
M
around
x
such that
the following condition holds:
Whenever

•

(x_{k})
is a sequence of points in
R\cap U
and

•

(y_{k})
is a sequence of points in
S\cap U
,

such that

(R1)

and

as

,

(R2)

converges in

, and

(R3)
G(T_{\chi(y_{k})}\chi(S\cap U))

then

\ell\subset\tau,

where both

.
(Note that in the above context,

for every

If

is Whitney
(B)
-regular at

then it is Whitney
(B)
-regular with respect to any other smooth chart
(Pflaum
[

.
This is an important principle that we will rely on in the proof

.
We say that
\mathcal{S}
is
Whitney (B)-regular
with respect to

X\subset M
, if
(R,S)
is Whitney (B)-regular at every point

for every pair

of different strata in
\mathcal{S}
such that

. In this case, the pair

(X,\mbox{$\mathcal{S}$})
is also called a

.

Since every stratum of a Whitney stratified subset of
M
is a smooth submanifold

M
, the set of dimensions of strata is bounded by the dimension of

.
We say that a Whitney stratified subset
(X,\mbox{$\mathcal{S}$}),

if

and
\dim S\leq n
for every

.
For a general stratified space
(X,\mbox{$\mathcal{S}$})
, there is no useful link
between the condition of frontier and the dimensions of strata.
Simple examples, arising for instance from the topologist’s

Lemma 3.5
.

(Mather

)
Let
(X,\mbox{$\mathcal{S}$})
be a Whitney stratified subset of

. If

are strata with

, then

\dim R<\dim S.

Let
(X,\mbox{$\mathcal{S}$})
be a Whitney stratified subset of

.
For

, we define its

Lemma 3.6
.

Suppose
(X,\mbox{$\mathcal{S}$})
is a Whitney stratified subspace of

.
Then the

are closed subsets of

.

For Whitney stratified spaces
(X,\mbox{$\mathcal{S}$})
of dimension

of the

-skeleton,

Lemma

It is well-known that Whitney stratified subsets are

For the sake of completeness and for the reader’s convenience, we

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
(X,\mbox{$\mathcal{S}$}),

X\subset M,
be a Whitney stratified subset of a smooth manifold
M

be any smooth manifold. Then

(X\times E,\mbox{$\mathcal{S}$}\times E),

, is a Whitney stratified subset of the smooth

.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.1
,
the partition
\mbox{$\mathcal{S}$}\times E
is a stratification of the
separable, locally compact Hausdorff space

.
It remains to verify that the product strata fit together in
a Whitney (B)-regular manner.
Let
R\times E,

be two different strata in
\mbox{$\mathcal{S}$}\times E

such that
R\times E\subset\overline{S\times E}=\overline{S}\times E

and let
(x,e)
be a point in

.
Since

, we may use (B)-regularity of
(R,S)
at

to obtain a smooth chart

of
M
around

such that whenever

(x_{k})
is a sequence of points in
R\cap U
and

(y_{k})
a sequence of points in
S\cap U
such that (R1) – (R3) hold,
we have
\ell\subset\tau
.

Let

be a smooth chart in

,

.
A smooth chart

in

around

is given by

,
\widetilde{\chi}=\chi\times\epsilon
.
We shall verify (B)-regularity in this chart.
Let

•

(x_{k},a_{k})
be a sequence of points in
(R\times E)\cap\widetilde{U}
and

•

(y_{k},b_{k})
a sequence of points in

4.
Free Actions on Whitney Stratified Spaces

The main purpose of the present section is to explain that
if a compact Lie group
G
acts smoothly and freely on a manifold
which contains a
G
-invariant Whitney stratified subspace
X
,
then the orbit space
X/G
is a Whitney stratified subspace of
the smooth manifold
M/G
(Theorem
4.3
).

Let
G
be a compact Lie group and let

(X,\mbox{$\mathcal{S}$})
be a stratified space.
We say that an action of
G
on
X
is

compatible with the stratification
\mathcal{S}
, or

stratum preserving
, if every stratum

S\in\mbox{$\mathcal{S}$}
is a
G
-invariant subspace and the induced action
G\times S\to S
of

G
on
S
is smooth.
In this case we will also say that
\mathcal{S}
is a
G
-stratification

and that
(X,\mbox{$\mathcal{S}$})
is a
G
-stratified space
.

Suppose that
M
is a smooth
G
-manifold and

(X,\mbox{$\mathcal{S}$}),

X\subset M,
is a Whitney stratified subset
such that
X
is
G
-invariant and the induced action of
G

on
X
is compatible with the stratification
\mathcal{S}
.
Let
\mbox{$\mathcal{S}$}/G
denote the collection of orbit spaces

\{S/G~|~S\in\mbox{$\mathcal{S}$}\}
.
Since every stratum
S
is nonempty, the spaces
S/G
are not empty.
If for some
R,S\in\mbox{$\mathcal{S}$}
,
R/G
and
S/G
have a point in common,
then
R
and
S
have a point in common and are therefore equal.
Thus
R/G=S/G
. Furthermore, any point of
X/G
lies in some
S/G

since every point of
X
lies in some stratum
S
.
Thus
\mbox{$\mathcal{S}$}/G
is a set-theoretic partition of
X/G
.

Lemma 4.1
.

The closure
\overline{S}
in
X
of a stratum
S\in\mbox{$\mathcal{S}$}
is
a
G
-invariant subspace of
X
and
the closure of
S/G\in\mbox{$\mathcal{S}$}/G
in
X/G

can be computed by

(2)

\overline{S/G}=\overline{S}/G.

Proof.

We show first that
\overline{S}

is indeed a
G
-invariant subspace of
X
:
Let
s
be a point of the closure
\overline{S}
and
g\in G
.
For
gs
to lie in
\overline{S}
, it is necessary and sufficient
that every open neighborhood
V\subset X
of
gs
has nonempty intersection
with
S
. The set
U:=g^{-1}V
is open and

s=g^{-1}(gs)\in g^{-1}V=U.

Thus
U
intersects
S
nontrivially and there exists a point

s^{\prime}\in U\cap S
. The point
gs^{\prime}
lies in
S
, since
S
is
G
-invariant.
Moreover,
gs^{\prime}\in g(g^{-1}V)=V
. We conclude that

V\cap S
is not empty, as was to be shown.
Thus
\overline{S}
is
G
-invariant and
\overline{S}/G
is defined.
We prove the equality claimed in (
2
).
Since
G
is compact, the orbit projection

\pi:X\to X/G
is closed (
[
11
, p. 38, Thm. 3.1.(2)]
).
Consequently,
\pi(\overline{S})
is closed in
X/G
.
As
\overline{S}
is
G
-invariant,
\pi(\overline{S})=\overline{S}/G
.
Therefore,
\overline{S}/G
is closed and since it contains
S/G
, it must
also contain the closure of
S/G
,

\overline{S/G}\subset\overline{S}/G.

To establish the converse inclusion, let

Gs_{0}\in\overline{S}/G
be any point,

.
This point is in
\overline{S/G}
precisely when
every open neighborhood
U\subset X/G
of
Gs_{0}
intersects

S/G
nontrivially.
The set

is open in
X
and

s_{0}\in Gs_{0}\subset\pi^{-1}(U)=V.

Since
s_{0}\in\overline{S}
, its open neighborhood

must intersect

S
nontrivially, i.e. there exists a point
s_{1}\in V\cap S
.
Its orbit
\pi(s_{1})=Gs_{1}
is a point of
U
, and a point of
S/G

(as
S
is
G
-invariant).
Hence
U\cap(S/G)
is not empty. This places
Gs_{0}
in
\overline{S/G}
and
finishes the proof of (
2
).
∎

Lemma 4.2
.

If
G
acts freely (and smoothly) on

, then
the partition
\mbox{$\mathcal{S}$}/G
is a stratification of

.

Proof.

Since
G
is compact, the orbit projection

\pi:M\to M/G
is closed.
Consequently,
\pi(X)
is closed in
M/G
.
Since
X
is
G
-invariant,
\pi(X)=X/G
.
Therefore,
the closed equivariant embedding

induces a
closed embedding

, with

a smooth manifold,
as
G
is compact and acts freely and smoothly.
This implies in particular that

is separable, locally compact, and Hausdorff,
since these properties are inherited from the smooth manifold

.

We prove next that the subspaces

S\in\mbox{$\mathcal{S}$},
are locally closed in

.
We will use the following criterion:
A subspace is locally closed if and only if it is open in its closure.
As
S
is locally closed in

by assumption,

S
is an open subset of
\overline{S}
.
Since
\overline{S}
is
G
-invariant by Lemma

,
there is an orbit projection

\pi:\overline{S}\to\overline{S}/G
, which is an open map.
We conclude that
\pi(S)
is open in
\overline{S}/G
.
Now

(since

is
G
-invariant) and

\overline{S}/G=\overline{S/G}
by (

) of the Lemma.
Hence

is open in
\overline{S/G}
, as was to be verified.

It remains to check the stratification conditions (S1) – (S3).
To verify (S1) we must show that

is locally finite.
Let
Gx
be a point of
X/G
,

.
Using (S1) for
\mathcal{S}
, we find an open neighborhood

of

which intersects nontrivially only

for all

.
Since

is the union of the open sets

,

,
it is open in
X
. Since
GU
is also a union of orbits, we can
regard it as a subset

of the orbit space of
X
.
As such, it is also open, since the quotient map
X\to X/G
is an
open map. The point
Gx
is contained in

. Thus the latter constitutes
an open neighborhood of
Gx
in
X/G
.
We claim that

intersects only finitely many strata in
\mbox{$\mathcal{S}$}/G
.
Indeed, suppose that

for some

.
Then there is some

and

such that

. Thus

for some

. This would imply that

, since

is

-invariant. We thus arrive at a contradiction

. Therefore,

for all

, showing that

is locally finite.

For (S2), we have to show that

is a smooth manifold for every

. By compatibility of the action with

,
the induced action of
G
on

is smooth. Since the induced action
is also free and

is compact,

is a manifold with a unique

is a smooth
submersion. In fact,

is then a smooth submanifold of

.

Lastly, we verify the condition of frontier (S3)
using Lemma
4.1
.
Suppose then that
R/G
intersects the closure
of
S/G
nontrivially,

.
Then by (

),

(R/G)\cap(\overline{S}/G)\not=\varnothing.

There exists thus a point

and a point

such that

.
Some group element

moves

,

.
As

is
G
-invariant,

. Therefore,

.
By the condition of frontier for

,

.
This implies

.
∎

As pointed out earlier, the closed equivariant inclusion

induces a

If

acts freely (and smoothly), then

is a manifold with a unique

6.
Orientability

Let
X
be a compact pseudomanifold, Whitney stratified in some ambient manifold.
The Goresky-MacPherson

-class

is defined only when the
pseudomanifold
X
is orientable. Thus in order for
L_{*}(X_{G}(k))

to be available, we must ensure that the approximating Borel pseudomanifolds

X_{G}(k)
can be oriented. When
X
is a point,
X_{G}(k)=BG_{k}
,
so
BG_{k}
should be an orientable manifold, at least an infinite
subsequence thereof.

Example 6.1
.

We consider the group
G=\mathbb{Z}/_{2}
.
Choosing
n=1
and the orthogonal embedding

\mathbb{Z}/_{2}=\{\pm 1\}=\operatorname{O}(1)
, we have

(B\mathbb{Z}/_{2})_{k}=\frac{\operatorname{O}(1+k)}{\operatorname{O}(1)\times\operatorname{O}(k)}=\mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}^{k},

which is orientable for
k
odd, but not orientable for
k
even.
As we will see, this is not due to an unsuitability of the group
\mathbb{Z}/_{2}
,
but rather to a choice of unsuitable embedding of it in an orthogonal group.

Any compact Lie group
G
can be embedded in some orthogonal group

\operatorname{O}(n)
in such a way that
G
acts orientation preservingly on the
corresponding
EG_{k}
,
k\geq 2
.
(See Proposition
6.3
.)
We will show (Proposition
6.12
) that if
G
is
bi-invariantly orientable, then the corresponding
BG_{k}
is
orientable and the fiber bundle
p_{k}:EG_{k}\to BG_{k}
is orientable.
Note that the underlying fiber bundle of a principal
G
-bundle
is not generally oriented, even though the fiber
G
is orientable
and, after having chosen a right-invariant orientation of
G

(which always exists), the action of the structure group
G
on the fiber
G

by right translation is orientation preserving. The problem is that
while
G
acts from the right on the total space, the transition functions
of the bundle act by
left
translation.
(See Formula
7
below.)
This explains the
need for bi-invariant orientability of
G
; not every group is
bi-invariantly orientable.
Let
H^{\operatorname{BM}}_{*}(-)
denote Borel-Moore homology.

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
\overline{N}
be an oriented manifold without boundary and

a topological
group acting continuously on
\overline{N}
. If

is path connected, then

acts in an orientation preserving manner.

Proof.

The oriented manifold
\overline{N}
has a fundamental class

[\overline{N}]\in H^{\operatorname{BM}}_{n}(\overline{N};\mathbb{Z}),

n=\dim\overline{N}
.
An element
h\in H
induces an automorphism

h_{*}:H^{\operatorname{BM}}_{n}(\overline{N};\mathbb{Z})\to H^{\operatorname{BM}}_{n}(\overline{N};\mathbb{Z})
.
As
H
is path connected, there exists a path

\gamma:I=[0,1]\to H
from the identity
\gamma(0)=e
to

\gamma(1)=h
. The map
H:\overline{N}\times I\to\overline{N}

given by
H(p,t)=\gamma(t)\cdot p
is an isotopy from

H_{0}=\operatorname{id}_{\overline{N}}
to
H_{1}=h
. Since Borel-Moore homology
is isotopy invariant, we conclude that
h_{*}=\operatorname{id}
. In particular,

h_{*}[\overline{N}]=[\overline{N}]
, which implies that

preserves
the orientation.
∎

Proposition 6.3
.

For any compact Lie group
G
, there exists an embedding

G\subset\operatorname{O}(n)
such that

acts orientation preservingly on

EG_{k}
,
k\geq 2
.

Proof.

Let
G
be a compact Lie group.
Choose an embedding

for some

.
Composing with the embedding
\operatorname{O}(n-1)\subset{\operatorname{SO}}(n)

given by

we obtain an embedding

.
Consider the manifold

\overline{N}:={\operatorname{SO}}(n+k)/(1_{n}\times{\operatorname{SO}}(k)).

The group

, and hence

, acts freely on the connected manifold

\overline{N}
.
For

, the Stiefel manifold

is simply connected, so orientable. Choose an orientation of
\overline{N}
.
The group

is path connected.
Thus, by Lemma

acts in an orientation preserving manner on

In particular, its subgroup

By Remark

is

to

∎

Recall that any Lie group has precisely two right-invariant orientations.
We emphasize that the group

appearing in the following lemma
need not be connected. The case of noncompact

Lemma 6.4
.

Let
M
be a connected orientable smooth manifold without boundary
and let
G
be a compact Lie group.
Let
\omega_{G}
be one of the two right-invariant orientations of

Given any orientation
\omega_{M\times G}
of

following statements are equivalent:

(1)

The smooth right action of

on

given by

preserves the orientation

(2)

There exists a unique orientation

of

such that
the product orientation
\omega_{M}\times\omega_{G}
agrees with

The proof is straightforward and may for example be cast in terms
of fundamental classes in Borel-Moore homology. It is useful to write
these classes as sums over connected components. If

is the identity

, then one can use the
Künneth theorem for Borel-Moore homology
(Bredon
[

a cross product isomorphism

which can be used to construct
\omega_{M}
.

Definition 6.5
.

A Lie group is called
bi-invariantly orientable
if it has
an orientation which is simultaneously left- and right-invariant.

Example 6.6
.

Discrete groups are bi-invariantly orientable by assigning to each
point the orientation
+1
.

Example 6.7
.

Abelian Lie groups are bi-invariantly orientable, since every Lie group
has a left-invariant orientation and in the abelian case, left and
right translation by an element coincide.

Example 6.8
.

The orthogonal group
\operatorname{O}(2)
is not bi-invariantly orientable.
The conjugation action
(g,h)\mapsto ghg^{-1}
of
\operatorname{O}(2)
on
itself restricts to an action of
\operatorname{O}(2)
on the identity component

{\operatorname{SO}}(2)\subset\operatorname{O}(2)
, which does not preserve any orientation
of
{\operatorname{SO}}(2)
, since conjugation by the element

g=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
1&0\end{pmatrix}\in\operatorname{O}(2)

is a reflection on
{\operatorname{SO}}(2)=S^{1}
.

The property of bi-invariant orientability can be rephrased as
a property of the adjoint representation:

Proposition 6.9
.

A Lie group is bi-invariantly orientable if and only if
its adjoint representation is orientation preserving.

Proof.

Let
G
be a Lie group with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
.
Choose and fix a left-invariant orientation of
G
.
In particular, this fixes an orientation of
\mathfrak{g}
.
On an element
g\in G
, the adjoint representation

{\operatorname{Ad}}:G\longrightarrow GL(\mathfrak{g})
is a linear automorphism

{\operatorname{Ad}}(g)=\phi_{g*,e}:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}

given by the differential at
e\in G
of the conjugation diffeomorphism

\phi_{g}:G\to G,

\phi_{g}(h)=ghg^{-1}
.
With
L_{g},R_{g}:G\to G
denoting left and right translation by
g
,
the conjugation can be written as
\phi_{g}=L_{g}\circ R_{g^{-1}}
.
Its differential at
e
is thus

\phi_{g*,e}=L_{g*,g^{-1}}\circ R_{g^{-1}*,e}
.

Now suppose that a left-invariant orientation of
G
can be
chosen in addition to be right-invariant. Then both

L_{g*,g^{-1}}
and
R_{g^{-1}*,e}
are orientation preserving.
Thus their composition
{\operatorname{Ad}}(g)=\phi_{g*,e}
is orientation preserving.
Conversely, if
\phi_{g*,e}
is orientation preserving, then,
for a choice of left-invariant orientation of
G
,

R_{g^{-1}*,e}=L_{g^{-1}*,e}\circ\phi_{g*,e}
preserves the
orientation for every
g
. Thus the left-invariant orientation is
also right-invariant.
∎

Proposition 6.10
.

Connected Lie groups are bi-invariantly orientable.

Proof.

Let
G
be a connected Lie group. An element
g\in G
defines
the conjugation
\phi_{g}:G\to G,

\phi_{g}(h)=ghg^{-1}
.
As
G
is connected, there exists a smooth path

\gamma:I\to G
from
\gamma(0)=e
to
\gamma(1)=g
.
An isotopy
\Phi:G\times I\to G
from

\Phi_{0}=\operatorname{id}_{G}
to
\Phi_{1}=\phi_{g}
is given by

\Phi(h,t)=\gamma(t)h\gamma(t)^{-1}

Note that
\Phi(e,t)=e
for all

.
Thus the differential
\Gamma(t):=(\Phi_{t})_{*,e}
of
\Phi_{t}
at
e\in G

defines a path
\Gamma:I\to\operatorname{GL}(\mathfrak{g})

from
\Gamma(0)=(\Phi_{0})_{*,e}=(\operatorname{id}_{G})_{*,e}=\operatorname{id}_{\mathfrak{g}}

to
\Gamma(1)=(\Phi_{1})_{*,e}=(\phi_{g})_{*,e}={\operatorname{Ad}}(g)
.
Therefore,
{\operatorname{Ad}}(g)
lies in the identity component of

\operatorname{GL}(\mathfrak{g})
. Thus
{\operatorname{Ad}}(g)
has positive determinant,
which shows that the adjoint representation preserves the orientation.
By Proposition
6.9
,
G
is bi-invariantly orientable.
∎

applies in particular to finite groups, abelian groups, and connected groups.

Proposition 6.12

,

Example 6.13

Remark 6.14

7.
Equivariant Homology

Equivariant homology, denoted
H^{G}_{*}(X),
is an inverse limit
of the ordinary singular homology groups
H_{*}(X_{G}(k))
as
k\to\infty
.
The inverse system is given by Gysin restrictions

H_{i+n}(X_{G}(k+1))\to H_{i}(X_{G}(k))
.
We review the construction and basic properties of equivariant homology groups.
They should be such that
compact oriented
G
-pseudomanifolds
X
possess an equivariant fundamental class

[X]_{G}\in H^{G}_{m}(X),

m=\dim X
(see Section
7.2
),
and when
X
is a manifold,
H^{G}_{*}(X)
should be equivariantly
Poincaré dual to the equivariant cohomology

H^{*}_{G}(X)=H^{*}(EG\times_{G}X)
of Borel (see Section
7.3
).
The viewpoint on equivariant homology as adopted in this paper is
compatible with the approach used by
Brylinski and Zhang in
[
13
]
,
Ohmoto in
[
28
]
and Weber in
[
41
]
. Here, we need to use compact topological
approximations
X_{G}(k)
instead of the complex algebraic approximations
(which are generally noncompact) used in the algebraic setting.
It turns out that the inverse limit stabilizes at finite
k
, as
we show in Lemma
7.6
.

We summarize the setup:
Let
G
be a compact bi-invariantly orientable Lie group of dimension
d
.
Choose an embedding

as a closed subgroup such that

is orientable for

(Proposition

).
Choose and fix orientations for every

.
Suppose that
G
acts smoothly on
a smooth manifold

which contains a compact stratified oriented pseudomanifold

(X,\mbox{$\mathcal{S}$})
of dimension

as a

-invariant Whitney stratified subset
such that the induced action of

on

is compatible with the stratification

and preserves the

.
The spaces

and they fiber over
BG_{k}
with fiber

. The approximations

of

are closed smooth manifolds of dimension

The morphism (

) of principal

-bundles
induces a morphism

Remark 7.5
.

We note that the

.
Recall that
G\subset\operatorname{O}(n)

Lemma 7.6

(Homological Gysin Stabilization.)
Suppose that

in

This technical appendix establishes the PL context

The essential point is that the action of

on

restriction of a

on

Smooth fiber bundles are PD-homeomorphic to PL bundles.
The relevant bundles used in the paper are thus restrictions of PL
bundles, hence again PL bundles.
We will freely use the theory of blocktransversality as
introduced by Rourke and Sanderson in

for submanifolds, and extended by Stone

We shall only need the case where one of the polyhedra, say

a locally flat PL submanifold, in which case blocktransversality
of

Stochastic resetting prevails over sharp restart for broad target distributions
Martin R. Evans, Somrita Ray

Abstract
Resetting has been shown to reduce the completion time for a stochastic process, such as the first passage time for a diffusive searcher to find a target. The time between two consecutive resetting events is drawn from a waiting time distribution ψ ⁡ ( t ) \psi(t) , which defines the resetting protocol. Previously, it has been shown that deterministic resetting process with a constant time period, referred to as sharp restart, can minimize the mean first passage time to a fixed target. Here we consider the more realistic problem of a target positioned at a random distance R R from the resetting site, selected from a given target distribution P T ​ ( R ) P_{T}(R) . We introduce the notion of a conjugate target distribution to a given waiting time distribution. The conjugate target distribution, P T ∗ ​ ( R ) P_{T}^{*}(R) , is that P T ​ ( R ) P_{T}(R) for which ψ ⁡ ( t ) \psi(t) extremizes the mean time to locate the target. In the case of diffusion we derive an explicit expression for P T ∗ ​ ( R ) P^{*}_{T}(R) conjugate to a given ψ ⁡ ( t ) \psi(t) which holds in arbitrary spatial dimension. Our results show that stochastic resetting prevails over sharp restart for target distributions with exponential or heavier tails.

To elucidate the question of optimal strategy, we

\overline{\langle T_{r}\rangle}=\int_{0}^{\infty}{\rm d}R\,P_{T}(R)\langle T_{r}(R)\rangle\;,

(1)

For an underlying diffusive search process we are able to obtain an explicit formula for the conjugate distribution.

The significance of our result

P_{T}(R)

In particular, our formulae reveal that stochastic resetting protocols are optimal when the target distribution is broad, e.g. an exponential or a power law.
Thus, generally stochastic resetting prevails over sharp restart.

We now give a derivation of the result

,

We begin with the general first renewal equation
[

,
16
,
1
]
for

Q_{r}(R,t)=\Psi(t)Q_{0}(R,t)\\
+\int_{0}^{t}{\rm d}\tau\,\psi(\tau)Q_{0}(R,\tau)Q_{r}(R,t-\tau)\;.

(7)

Here
\Psi(t)=\int_{t}^{\infty}{\rm d}\tau\,\psi(\tau)
is the probability of no reset up to time

and

is the survival probability up to time
t
in the absence of resetting. Thus the first term in Eq. (
7
) is the probability of no resets and survival up to
t
. The second term in Eq. (
7
) integrates the probability of first reset at time
\tau
and survival: the integrand is the probability of first reset at time
\tau
, survival up to time
\tau
with no resetting, then survival from
\tau
to
t
with resetting.

Eq. (
7
) may be solved by Laplace transform to yield

\widetilde{Q}_{r}(R,s)=\frac{\int_{0}^{\infty}{\rm d}t\,{\rm e}^{-st}\Psi(t)Q_{0}(R,t)}{1-\int_{0}^{\infty}{\rm d}t\,{\rm e}^{-st}\psi(t)\,Q_{0}(R,t)},

(8)

where the Laplace transform is defined as
\widetilde{Q}_{r}(R,s)=\int_{0}^{\infty}{\rm d}t\,{\rm e}^{-st}Q_{r}(R,t)
.
The MFPT is obtained by setting
s=0
, and integration by parts gives, see
[
29
,
16
,
55

\langle T_{r}(R)\rangle=\frac{\int_{0}^{\infty}{\rm d}t\,\psi(t)\int_{0}^{t}{\rm d}t^{\prime}\,Q_{0}(R,t^{\prime})}{\int_{0}^{\infty}{\rm d}t\,\psi(t)(1-\,Q_{0}(R,t))}\;.

(9)

We now average the MFPT over the target distribution
P_{T}(R)
, where
R
is the distance of the target from the resetting site,
and denote the average as
\overline{\langle T_{r}\rangle}

[Eq. (
1

In order to extremize Eq. (
1

\psi(t)
we take the functional derivative [See
[
55
]
for derivation]

\frac{\delta\overline{\langle T_{r}\rangle}}{\delta\psi(t^{\prime})}=\mbox{\hskip-2.84544pt}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mbox{\hskip-9.95863pt}{\rm d}R\,P_{T}(R)\left[\frac{\int_{0}^{t^{\prime}}{\rm d}\tau\,Q_{0}(R,\tau)}{\int_{0}^{\infty}{\rm d}t\,\psi(t)(1-\,Q_{0}(R,t))}\right.\\
-\left.\frac{(1-Q_{0}(R,t^{\prime}))\int_{0}^{\infty}{\rm d}t\,\psi(t)\int_{0}^{t}{\rm d}\tau\,Q_{0}(R,\tau)}{\left[\int_{0}^{\infty}{\rm d}t\,\psi(t)(1-\,Q_{0}(R,t))\right]^{2}}\right]\;.

(10)

Eq. (
10
) holds for a general first passage process, with survival probability
Q_{0}(R,t)
.

The conjugate target distribution Eq. (
2a
) is that which renders a particular waiting time distribution optimal, in the sense that
\psi(t)
extremizes the MFPT averaged over this target distribution.

Thus we seek to find the target distribution for which Eq. (
10
) vanishes for all

, for a given
\psi(t)
. Remarkably, for an underlying diffusive search, the ansatz Eq. (
2a

To show this we insert Eqs. (

,

\int_{0}^{\infty}\mbox{\hskip-5.69046pt}{\rm d}R\left[\int_{0}^{\infty}\mbox{\hskip-5.69046pt}{\rm d}t\,\psi(t)(1-\,Q_{0}(R,t))\int_{0}^{t^{\prime}}\mbox{\hskip-5.69046pt}{\rm d}\tau\,Q_{0}(R,\tau)\right.\\
\left.-(1-Q_{0}(R,t^{\prime}))\int_{0}^{\infty}\mbox{\hskip-5.69046pt}{\rm d}t\,\psi(t)\int_{0}^{t}{\rm d}\tau\,Q_{0}(R,\tau)\right]=0.

(11)

Taking the Laplace transform with respect to
t^{\prime}
with Laplace variable

and after relabelling of integration variables we obtain the condition [See
[
55
]
for derivation]

\int_{0}^{\infty}{\rm d}t\int_{0}^{\infty}\mbox{\hskip-5.69046pt}{\rm d}t^{\prime}\left[{\rm e}^{-st^{\prime}}\psi(t)-{\rm e}^{-st}\psi(t^{\prime})\right]I(t,t^{\prime})=0,

(12)

where

In conclusion, we have defined the target distribution conjugate to a waiting time distribution
\psi(t)
as the target distribution for which
\psi(t)
extremizes the MFPT. In the case of diffusion with stochastic resetting we found a simple expression Eqs. (

It would be of interest to determine conjugate target distributions for first passage problems
under resetting other than

II
Derivation of Eq. (

Equation (

The first-passage time distribution for a dynamic process is given by the rate of decay of its survival probability. Therefore, the mean first passage time,

\langle T_{r}\rangle=-\int_{0}^{\infty}{\rm d}t\;t(\partial Q_{r}(R,t)/\partial t)

\langle T_{r}\rangle=\int_{0}^{\infty}Q_{r}(R,t){\rm d}t=[\widetilde{Q}_{r}(R,s)]_{s=0}
, where
\widetilde{Q}_{r}(R,s)=\int_{0}^{\infty}{\rm d}t\,{\rm e}^{-st}Q_{r}(R,t)

. Following this relation, we put

) in the main text to obtain

\langle T_{r}(R)\rangle=[\widetilde{Q}_{r}(R,s)]_{s=0}=\frac{\int_{0}^{\infty}{\rm d}t\,\Psi(t)Q_{0}(R,t)}{1-\int_{0}^{\infty}{\rm d}t\,\psi(t)\,Q_{0}(R,t)}.

(S1)

Since the waiting time distribution
\psi(t)
is normalized, i.e.,
\int_{0}^{\infty}\psi(t){\rm d}t=1
, the denominator of
Eq. (
S1
) can be rewritten as

\left[1-\int_{0}^{\infty}{\rm d}t\,\psi(t)\,Q_{0}(R,t)\right]=\int_{0}^{\infty}{\rm d}t\,\psi(t)[1-\,Q_{0}(R,t)].

(S2)

Integrating the numerator of Eq. (

) by parts we get

\int_{0}^{\infty}{\rm d}t\,\Psi(t)Q_{0}(R,t)=\left[\left(\int_{0}^{t}{\rm d}t^{\prime}Q_{0}(R,t^{\prime})\right)\Psi(t)\right]_{0}^{\infty}-\int_{0}^{\infty}{\rm d}t\,\left[\frac{\partial\Psi(t)}{\partial t}\left(\int_{0}^{t}{\rm d}t^{\prime}\,Q_{0}(R,t^{\prime})\right)\right].

(S3)

For

\int_{0}^{t}{\rm d}t^{\prime}Q_{0}(R,t^{\prime})

\psi(t)

\int_{0}^{\infty}{\rm d}t\,\Psi(t)Q_{0}(x,t)=\int_{0}^{\infty}{\rm d}t\;\psi(t)\int_{0}^{t}{\rm d}t^{\prime}\,Q_{0}(x,t^{\prime}).

(S4)

Substituting the numerator and denominator of Eq. (
S1

S4

VII
Sharp restart is optimal for fixed target

As noted in the Introduction, sharp reset has been of interest as it minimizes the MFPT when the distance
R_{0}
to the target is known.
A fixed target at distance
R_{0}
from the resetting site (taken to be the origin) corresponds to a target distribution
P_{T}(R)=\delta(R-R_{0})
. It has been shown that for this scenario sharp restart minimizes the mean first passage time when the deterministic period of resetting,
\tau
, is suitably chosen
[
3
,
4
]
.
Here we show how this result is recovered within our formalism.

To see this
we insert
\psi(t)=\delta(t-\tau)
and
P_{T}(R)=\delta(R-R_{0})
in Eq. (
10
) yielding

\frac{\delta\overline{\langle T_{r}\rangle}}{\delta\psi(t^{\prime})}=\frac{1-Q_{0}(R_{0},t^{\prime})}{1-\,Q_{0}(R_{0},\tau)}\left[G(t^{\prime})-G(\tau)\right],

(S40)

where
G(t^{\prime})=\int_{0}^{t^{\prime}}{\rm d}t\,Q_{0}(R_{0},t)/(1-\,Q_{0}(R_{0},t^{\prime}))
.
If
\tau
is chosen as the value of
t^{\prime}
that minimizes
G(t^{\prime})

[
3
,
4
]
, we have

\frac{\delta\overline{\langle T_{r}\rangle}}{\delta\psi(t^{\prime})}\geq 0\;,

(S41)

with the equality only holding when
t^{\prime}=\tau
. Then sharp reset with period
\tau
minimizes
\overline{\langle T_{r}\rangle}
because of the constraints that
\psi(t)\geq 0
and
\int_{0}^{\infty}dt\,\psi(t)=1
, i.e., variation of the delta distribution implies increasing
\psi(t^{\prime})
when
t^{\prime}\neq\tau
and
decreasing
\psi(\tau)
.
Consequently, since Eq. (
S41
) holds,
\overline{\langle T_{r}\rangle}
always increases.

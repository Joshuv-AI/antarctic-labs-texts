Risk factors and nomogram prediction model for prognosis in sepsis with acute kidney injury
Authors: Jun-Mei Lai, Yang Zheng, Tian-Yu Liang, Jin Huang, Yin-Yin Quan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundSepsis-associated acute kidney injury (AKI) is a frequent and life-threatening complication in critically ill patients, yet early risk stratification remains challenging. This study aimed to identify independent prognostic factors and to develop and internally validate a nomogram for predicting 28-day mortality in patients with sepsis-associated AKI.MethodsIn this retrospective study, 238 adult patients with sepsis defined by Sepsis-3 criteria and AKI defined according to Kidney Disease: Improving Global Outcomes (KDIGO) guidelines were enrolled between January 2021 and December 2024. Clinical and immunologic variables obtained within 24 h of intensive care unit (ICU) admission were analyzed. Multivariable logistic regression was performed to identify independent predictors of 28-day mortality. Model performance was assessed using receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analysis, calibration plots, bootstrap validation, and decision curve analysis (DCA).ResultsAmong the 238 enrolled patients, 91 were classified as non-survivors and 147 as survivors. Multivariable analysis identified AKI stage 2 (odds ratio [OR] 3.857, 95% confidence interval [CI] 2.101–7.082), AKI stage 3 (OR 5.585, 95% CI 2.652–11.761), ICU stay (OR 1.802, 95% CI 1.304–2.490), T helper 17 cells (OR 2.881, 95% CI 1.257–6.600), interleukin-17 (OR 1.226, 95% CI 1.073–1.401), and 24-hour fluid balance (OR 1.241 per 100 mL/day, P < 0.001) as independent predictors of poor prognosis. The nomogram showed good discrimination, with an area under the curve of 0.859 and a bootstrap-corrected concordance index of 0.844. Calibration and DCA demonstrated adequate model fit and clinical utility.ConclusionsAKI severity, early fluid accumulation, ICU course, and immune-inflammatory activation are independently associated with 28-day mortality in sepsis-associated AKI. The proposed nomogram provides individualized risk estimation and may assist in early prognostic stratification.Clinical trial numberNot applicable.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12882-026-05039-0.Keywords: Sepsis, Acute kidney injury, Prognosis, Nomogram, ICU mortality

Introduction

Sepsis is a complex syndrome characterized by a dysregulated host response to infection that frequently results in multiple organ dysfunction [1]. Among its complications, acute kidney injury (AKI) is particularly consequential because of its high incidence and its strong association with adverse clinical outcomes, including increased morbidity and mortality [2]. The concurrence of sepsis and AKI represents a major challenge in intensive care practice and underscores the need for systematic evaluation of prognostic determinants and the development of reliable prediction models to inform clinical decision-making [3].

The epidemiological burden of AKI in the setting of sepsis is substantial, with reported incidence rates ranging from 20% to 50% among critically ill patients [4]. This heterogeneity reflects the complex interaction of systemic inflammation, hemodynamic instability, and microcirculatory dysfunction that collectively contribute to renal injury [5]. Although several severity scoring systems have been developed to predict outcomes in sepsis, many do not fully account for the pathophysiological and prognostic heterogeneity of patients with concurrent AKI. Accordingly, refinement of risk stratification strategies that integrate both clinical and laboratory variables remains necessary [6, 7]. In previous studies, risk stratification models for sepsis-associated AKI have primarily relied on clinical variables such as AKI stage, organ dysfunction, and intensive care unit length of stay [8]. Although immune-inflammatory dysregulation is central to the pathophysiology of sepsis and AKI [9], prognostic models rarely incorporate immune-inflammatory biomarkers as independent predictors. In particular, T helper 17 (Th17) cells and interleukin-17 (IL-17) have been associated with adverse outcomes in sepsis [10, 11], yet their integration into structured prognostic frameworks remains limited.

Therefore, the present study sought to integrate immune-inflammatory markers with established clinical variables to develop and internally validate a nomogram for predicting 28-day mortality in patients with sepsis-associated AKI. By combining organ-based parameters with host-response biomarkers, this model aims to provide a more comprehensive approach to individualized risk estimation in the intensive care setting.

Methods

Study design

This retrospective study assessed patients diagnosed with sepsis complicated by AKI who were treated at People’s Hospital of Anji County from January 2021 to December 2024. A total of 238 patients were enrolled. The study design, methodology, and analytical protocols were developed in strict accordance with the Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology (STROBE) guidelines [12]. Informed consent was obtained from all subjects. The study protocol was reviewed and approved by the hospital’s ethics committee. All procedures followed relevant guidelines and regulations, adhering to the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki. Data for the study were obtained from the hospital’s electronic medical records database, and participant data were anonymized prior to analysis to ensure confidentiality and privacy.

Definition of AKI and baseline creatinine ascertainment

Baseline serum creatinine (Cr) was defined as the most recent available outpatient or inpatient measurement obtained within three months prior to ICU admission. For patients without documented pre-admission creatinine values, baseline creatinine was estimated using the Modification of Diet in Renal Disease equation assuming an estimated glomerular filtration rate of 75 mL/min/1.73 m², in accordance with KDIGO recommendations [13]. AKI was diagnosed and staged according to KDIGO criteria using both Cr and urine output definitions. Cr–based criteria included an increase of ≥ 0.3 mg/dL within 48 h or ≥ 1.5 times baseline within seven days. Urine output criteria, defined as < 0.5 mL/kg/h for ≥ 6 h, were assessed using hourly ICU nursing records during the first 24 h after ICU admission. Patients with missing urine output data were classified based on Cr criteria alone. Details regarding baseline creatinine estimation are provided in Supplementary Table S1.

Patient prognosis and data collection

Based on the prognostic status at the time of last follow-up, the study cohort of 238 patients was stratified into a non-survivor group (n = 91, 38.24%) and a survivor group (n = 147, 61.76%). Comprehensive clinical data were retrospectively obtained from electronic medical records and follow-up documentation. The collected variables included demographic information (gender, age, and body mass index), AKI stage, and the presence of hypoalbuminemia. Laboratory parameters such as Cr, serum potassium, and lactate (LA) were recorded. Clinical severity was assessed using the Acute Physiology and Chronic Health Evaluation III (APACHE III) score [14], and additional parameters included urine output, fluid balance, the number of organ failures, and the use of vasopressors. Furthermore, data on mechanical ventilation, blood product transfusion, and intensive care unit (ICU) length of stay were collected. Hematologic and biochemical markers, including platelet count (PLT), procalcitonin (PCT), serum albumin (ALB), C-reactive protein (CRP), and the international normalized ratio (INR), were also evaluated. Moreover, immunological and cardiovascular biomarkers such as Th17 cells, IL-17, soluble triggering receptor expressed on myeloid cells-1 (sTREM-1), angiopoietin-2 (Ang-2), and brain natriuretic peptide (BNP) were measured. The primary outcome was 28-day all-cause mortality (dead vs. alive) after ICU admission. All candidate predictors were collected within the first 24 h of ICU admission to ensure temporal validity for risk prediction. Fluid balance was recorded as both absolute values (mL/day) and weight-normalized values (mL/kg/24 h). For regression analyses, fluid balance was modeled per 100-mL increase to enhance clinical interpretability. Blood samples for immunologic markers (Th17, IL-17, sTREM-1, Ang-2, BNP) were collected at ICU admission (within the first 24 h). Th17 cell count was measured by flow cytometry, and IL-17, sTREM-1, Ang-2, and BNP were quantified using enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA). The units for these markers are cells/µL for Th17, pg/mL for IL-17, sTREM-1, Ang-2, and BNP. Samples were processed immediately upon collection, with plasma and serum separated by centrifugation and stored at -80 °C.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were conducted using SPSS version 26.0 and R version 4.3.3. Continuous variables following a normal distribution are presented as mean ± standard deviation, with intergroup comparisons assessed using t-tests. Variables with P < 0.05 in univariable analyses were included in the multivariable logistic regression model to identify independent predictors. Categorical variables are presented as counts and percentages, with comparisons conducted using the chi-square test. Multivariable logistic regression was applied to evaluate prognostic factors in sepsis-related AKI. Multicollinearity among the predictors was assessed using variance inflation factor (VIF) analysis. A prognostic nomogram was developed in R (version 4.3.3) and validated internally with 1,000 bootstrap resamples. The model’s calibration was assessed with the Hosmer-Lemeshow test, and its performance was evaluated using the receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve, calibration curve, and concordance index (CI). Clinical applicability was further assessed through decision curve analysis (DCA). To test model robustness and mitigate potential reverse causation related to ICU length of stay (LOS), we performed two sensitivity analyses: (1) replacing ICU LOS with the Sequential Organ Failure Assessment (SOFA) score (A1) while retaining other predictors, and (2) a 48-hour landmark analysis (A2) that restricted the cohort to patients surviving ≥ 48 h after ICU admission. A P value of less than 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Baseline characteristics and prognostic markers in patients with sepsis-associated AKI

The baseline characteristics of 238 patients with sepsis-associated AKI, categorized into a non-survivor group (n = 91) and a survivor group (n = 147) (Fig. 1). Statistically significant differences were noted in several clinical parameters (Table 1). The distribution of AKI stages differed markedly, with the non-survivor group exhibiting a higher proportion of stage 3 AKI (43.96% vs. 25.85%, P < 0.001). Additionally, a greater percentage of patients in the non-survivor group experienced three or more organ failures compared to survivors (78.02% vs. 53.74%, P < 0.001). Mechanical ventilation was more frequently required in the non-survivor group (52.75% vs. 39.46%, P = 0.045). Renal function, as indicated by Cr, was significantly worse in non-survivors (294.16 ± 73.44 µmol/L vs. 251.84 ± 65.25 µmol/L, P < 0.001), and the daily fluid balance was substantially higher (1029.11 ± 312.34 mL vs. 786.27 ± 254.96 mL, P < 0.001). Moreover, hypoalbuminemia was more common and ALB levels were lower in the non-survivor group (26.41 ± 3.46 g/L vs. 31.42 ± 4.21 g/L, P < 0.001). Immunological markers, particularly Th17 cells and IL-17, were significantly elevated in non-survivors (P < 0.001). In contrast, no statistically significant differences were found between the mortality and survivor groups regarding gender distribution, age, BMI, serum potassium, APACHE III scores, urine output, PLT, PCT, CRP, INR, LA, sTREM-1, Ang-2, or BNP levels.

Multivariable logistic regression analysis of prognostic factors in sepsis with AKI

Multivariable logistic regression analysis was performed to identify independent prognostic factors for 28-day mortality in patients with sepsis complicated by AKI. Compared with the reference group (AKI Stage 1), both AKI Stage 2 (B = 1.350, OR = 3.857, 95% CI 2.101–7.082, P < 0.001) and AKI Stage 3 (B = 1.720, OR = 5.585, 95% CI 2.652–11.761, P < 0.001) were significantly associated with increased mortality risk. Additionally, the duration of ICU stay was a significant prognostic indicator; each additional day in the ICU was associated with an increased risk of mortality (B = 0.589, OR = 1.802, 95% CI 1.304–2.490, P < 0.001). Elevated levels of immunological markers were also independently associated with worse outcomes, including higher Th17 cell counts (B = 1.058, OR = 2.881, 95% CI 1.257–6.600, P = 0.012) and increased IL-17 levels (B = 0.204, OR = 1.226, 95% CI 1.073–1.401, P = 0.003). Moreover, a positive fluid balance was independently linked to mortality; each 100-mL increase in 24-hour fluid balance was associated with higher odds of 28-day mortality (B = 0.040, OR = 1.241, 95% CI 1.117–1.366, P < 0.001) (Table 2). The VIF values for all predictors were within acceptable limits, with Th17 (VIF = 2.85), IL-17 (VIF = 3.17), and other variables showing no evidence of significant multicollinearity (all VIF < 10). As a result, all selected predictors, including Th17, IL-17, fluid balance, AKI stage, and ICU stay, were retained in the final analysis.

Nomogram development and validation for prognostic prediction in sepsis-associated AKI

A prognostic nomogram for predicting mortality in sepsis-associated AKI was constructed using independent predictors identified via multivariable logistic regression. Each predictor was assigned a point value along its respective axis, and the cumulative score corresponded to the predicted probability of death, with higher scores indicating an increased risk (Fig. 2). The model demonstrated robust discrimination, with an area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC) of 0.859 (95% CI: 0.778–0.931); at the optimal cutoff determined by the maximum Youden index, the sensitivity and specificity were 78.69% and 89.56%, respectively (Fig. 3). Internal validation using 1,000 bootstrap resamples yielded a corrected concordance index (C-index) of 0.844, indicating good discriminative performance after adjustment for potential optimism. The Hosmer–Lemeshow goodness-of-fit test (χ² = 2.168, P = 0.837) did not indicate significant lack of fit, and calibration plots demonstrated close agreement between predicted and observed mortality probabilities (Fig. 4). Furthermore, DCA demonstrated that the nomogram’s net benefit was substantially higher than those associated with either no intervention (i.e., assuming no patients receive targeted treatment based on model prediction) or universal intervention (i.e., assuming all patients receive intervention regardless of predicted risk), underscoring its potential clinical utility (Fig. 5).

Sensitivity analyses

A1: Alternative severity model excluding ICU LOS.

When ICU LOS was excluded and baseline severity (SOFA score) was incorporated instead, the associations of AKI stage, 24-hour fluid balance, Th17, and IL-17 with 28-day mortality remained statistically significant (Supplementary Table S2). AKI stage 3 (OR = 4.10, 95% CI 1.98–8.47, P < 0.001), higher SOFA score (OR = 1.12 per point, 95% CI 1.04–1.20, P = 0.003), greater 24-hour fluid balance (OR = 1.07 per 100 mL, 95% CI 1.03–1.11, P < 0.001), elevated Th17 levels (OR = 2.38, 95% CI 1.36–4.18, P = 0.002), and higher IL-17 concentrations (OR = 1.23 per 5 pg/mL, 95% CI 1.06–1.43, P = 0.006) remained independently associated with mortality.

A2: Landmark analysis (≥ 48-hour survivors).

In the landmark cohort restricted to patients surviving at least 48 h after ICU admission (n = 210), the primary model including ICU LOS demonstrated consistent effect estimates (Supplementary Table S3). ICU LOS remained independently associated with mortality (OR = 1.50 per day, 95% CI 1.14–1.98, P = 0.004), alongside AKI stage 3, fluid balance (OR = 1.06 per 100 mL, 95% CI 1.02–1.11, P = 0.008), Th17, and IL-17.

In the alternative severity model within the landmark cohort (Supplementary Table S4), SOFA score was independently associated with mortality (OR = 1.11 per point, 95% CI 1.03–1.21, P = 0.005), and the magnitude and direction of associations for fluid balance (OR = 1.06 per 100 mL, 95% CI 1.02–1.10, P = 0.004), Th17, and IL-17 were consistent with the primary analysis.

Across both sensitivity analyses, the direction and magnitude of associations were preserved, supporting the robustness of the proposed nomogram and suggesting that its prognostic performance was not solely dependent on ICU LOS or influenced by early mortality–related bias.

Weighted post-hoc power analysis

A weighted post-hoc power analysis was performed based on the multivariable logistic regression model. Power was estimated for each independent predictor retained in the final model (AKI severity, ICU LOS, 24-hour fluid balance, Th17 levels, and IL-17 levels) using the observed effect sizes (odds ratios), event rate (38.2%), and total sample size (n = 238). The weighted overall post-hoc power, calculated by aggregating predictor-specific power estimates according to their regression coefficients and variance contributions, was 89.5%, exceeding the conventional threshold of 80%. This indicates that the study had adequate statistical power to detect clinically meaningful associations between the selected predictors and 28-day mortality. These findings support the robustness of the multivariable model and reduce the likelihood that statistically significant results were attributable to insufficient sample size.

Discussion

In this study, we investigated the prognostic factors in patients with sepsis-associated AKI and developed a nomogram to predict mortality. Our analysis identified several independent predictors, including AKI stage, duration of ICU stay, levels of Th17 cells and IL-17, and positive fluid balance. These findings emphasize that both the severity of renal dysfunction and the intensity of the inflammatory response play crucial roles in determining outcomes in sepsis-associated AKI. The novelty of our study lies in the incorporation of adaptive immune parameters into a clinically applicable risk model, bridging traditional organ dysfunction indicators with mechanistic immune dysregulation in sepsis. While prior prognostic models in sepsis-AKI have largely focused on severity scores and routine laboratory markers, our findings suggest that Th17-mediated inflammatory activity provides incremental prognostic information beyond conventional clinical variables. The model demonstrated good discrimination, calibration, and clinical net benefit, supporting its performance for risk stratification within the first 24 h of ICU admission. By enabling individualized mortality estimation using routinely obtainable clinical and biomarker data, the proposed nomogram may assist clinicians in identifying high-risk patients who warrant intensified monitoring, hemodynamic optimization, and immune-modulatory consideration, thereby facilitating more tailored and data-driven decision-making in critically ill populations [15, 16].

In this cohort, non-survivors exhibited a distinct high-risk clinical profile characterized by more severe renal dysfunction and greater systemic illness burden. Specifically, higher Cr levels and a markedly greater proportion of KDIGO stage 3 AKI were observed in the non-survivor group, underscoring the strong association between acute renal injury severity and short-term mortality. Beyond kidney-specific injury, non-survivors demonstrated a higher frequency of ≥ 3 organ failures and increased need for mechanical ventilation, reflecting more profound multisystem dysfunction. Early fluid status also differed significantly, with a more positive 24-hour fluid balance in non-survivors, supporting early fluid accumulation as a marker associated with adverse clinical trajectory. Although several conventional prognostic markers, including LA, CRP, PCT, PLT, INR, and APACHE III score, did not differ significantly between groups, this finding suggests that in this single-center population, early mortality risk was more effectively captured by the combined assessment of AKI severity, early fluid balance, and immune-inflammatory activation [17, 18]. Multivariable analysis further delineated independent prognostic factors after adjustment for potential confounders. AKI stage demonstrated a graded association with 28-day mortality, with stages 2 and 3 conferring substantially increased risk relative to stage 1, consistent with the biological gradient of renal injury severity. ICU LOS was independently associated with mortality; although this variable may be partially influenced by care processes, its prognostic relevance persisted in sensitivity analyses incorporating baseline SOFA score and a 48-hour landmark design. Importantly, early positive fluid balance retained independent prognostic significance, indicating that early fluid accumulation conveys risk beyond baseline organ dysfunction. Furthermore, Th17 and IL-17 were independently associated with mortality, suggesting that dysregulated adaptive immune activation provides incremental prognostic information beyond conventional clinical parameters [19, 20]. While causal inference is not possible in this retrospective design, these associations are biologically plausible within the framework of sepsis immunopathology and highlight the potential value of integrating immune biomarkers with organ-based severity assessment [21, 22].

The nomogram derived from these independent predictors exhibited strong apparent discrimination (AUC 0.859) and maintained good performance after internal bootstrap validation (optimism-corrected C-index 0.844). Calibration within the derivation cohort was assessed using calibration plots and the Hosmer–Lemeshow test. However, external validation is required to confirm model performance across different populations. From a clinical perspective, this implies that the model can provide individualized risk estimates that are not only rank-ordered effectively but also reasonably aligned with absolute outcome probabilities in the derivation setting. DCA demonstrated a favorable net benefit across clinically relevant threshold probabilities compared with default strategies of assuming all patients are high-risk or assuming no patients are high-risk, supporting the potential clinical utility of the model [23, 24]. The robustness of key associations in the alternative severity and landmark analyses strengthens confidence that the prognostic signal is not solely driven by ICU LOS or early mortality-related bias. Taken together, these results support a pragmatic risk stratification tool anchored to data available within the first 24 h of ICU admission and enriched by immune-inflammatory biomarkers, which may be particularly valuable when traditional severity scores do not fully capture between-patient variability [25, 26].

Yue et al. [6]developed machine learning–based models using the MIMIC-III database to predict the occurrence of AKI in septic patients, with XGBoost achieving the highest discrimination (area under the curve [AUC] 0.821). Their work focused on early identification of AKI development rather than outcome stratification after AKI onset. In contrast, our study addressed 28-day mortality among patients with established sepsis-associated AKI, demonstrating superior discrimination (AUC 0.859) within a clinically homogeneous cohort. By incorporating immune-inflammatory biomarkers (Th17 and IL-17) alongside organ-based parameters, our model extends beyond AKI occurrence prediction and provides prognostic refinement at a later and clinically decisive stage. Gómez et al. [27]demonstrated that persistent severe AKI (KDIGO stage 3 for ≥ 72 h) was independently associated with increased 90-day mortality (hazard ratio 1.5) and impaired renal recovery in critically ill patients. Their large multicenter analysis highlighted AKI trajectory and fluid balance as modifiable determinants of outcome. Consistent with these findings, our data revealed a graded association between AKI stage and 28-day mortality, as well as an independent prognostic signal of early positive fluid balance. Unlike trajectory-based analyses, our study integrates baseline AKI severity with immune-inflammatory activation into a practical nomogram, offering individualized early risk estimation rather than post hoc trajectory classification. Yang et al. [28]constructed a nomogram to predict the development of sepsis-associated AKI in critically ill patients, achieving moderate discrimination (C-index ~ 0.75) and emphasizing clinical variables such as baseline creatinine and vasoactive drug use. Their model addressed AKI incidence rather than mortality. In contrast, our nomogram specifically predicts short-term mortality in patients with established sepsis-associated AKI and demonstrated higher discrimination (AUC 0.859). Furthermore, the incorporation of immune biomarkers distinguishes our model conceptually and mechanistically, suggesting that host inflammatory activation provides incremental prognostic information beyond traditional clinical indicators. These studies collectively highlight ongoing efforts to improve risk stratification in sepsis and AKI, offering a comparative context for the present mortality-focused nomogram.

Clinically, our findings highlight the importance of early risk stratification in patients with sepsis-associated AKI. The proposed nomogram provides an individualized estimate of 28-day mortality based on readily obtainable clinical and immunologic variables, facilitating objective risk assessment during the early phase of ICU admission. Rather than replacing clinical judgment, the model may complement existing severity scores by identifying patients who may be considered at higher risk and therefore require heightened clinical awareness [29]. Importantly, the independent prognostic value of Th17 and IL-17 suggests that immune-inflammatory dysregulation contributes additional risk information beyond conventional organ-based metrics. While therapeutic implications require prospective validation, integration of immune biomarkers into prognostic frameworks may enhance precision in clinical risk assessment [30]. The practical application of the nomogram is illustrated in Supplementary Table S5. One of the main strengths of our study is the incorporation of both clinical and immunological variables, which allowed for a multifaceted evaluation of the factors influencing mortality in sepsis-associated AKI. The construction of a user-friendly nomogram based on robust multivariable analysis further adds practical value, as it enables individualized risk estimation at the bedside.

Several limitations should be acknowledged. First, this was a single-center retrospective cohort study, which may limit generalizability and introduce potential residual confounding despite multivariable adjustment. External validation in independent, multicenter cohorts is required to confirm model transportability and performance stability across diverse clinical settings. Second, although ICU LOS was retained as an independent predictor in the primary model, it reflects post-admission clinical evolution and may not be fully available at the time of initial ICU triage. This could limit immediate bedside applicability at admission. However, sensitivity analyses substituting ICU LOS with baseline SOFA score demonstrated consistent effect estimates and discrimination, supporting the robustness of the prognostic structure independent of this variable. In future studies, baseline variables such as SOFA score should be prioritized over ICU LOS to improve temporal validity and reduce the potential risk of reverse causation bias. Third, the first 24-hour fluid balance represents cumulative hemodynamic management during initial critical care rather than a pre-admission characteristic. While early fluid accumulation has established prognostic relevance in critically ill populations, its inclusion indicates that the model is most appropriately applied after initial ICU stabilization rather than at pre-ICU presentation. Fourth, immune-inflammatory biomarkers, including Th17 and IL-17, were measured at ICU admission and may not be routinely available in all centers, potentially limiting widespread implementation. Prospective studies are warranted to assess their feasibility, cost-effectiveness, and incremental value compared with standard severity scoring systems. Future research should focus on prospective external validation, dynamic risk updating over time, and integration of the nomogram with established severity scores or machine learning frameworks. Interventional or pragmatic clinical studies are also needed to determine whether risk-guided management strategies informed by this nomogram can improve clinical outcomes.

Conclusions

Our study demonstrates that advanced AKI stage, prolonged ICU stay, positive fluid balance, and elevated Th17/IL-17 levels are independent predictors of mortality in sepsis-associated AKI. The developed nomogram shows robust predictive performance and clinical utility, providing a tool for individualized mortality risk estimation in critically ill sepsis patients.

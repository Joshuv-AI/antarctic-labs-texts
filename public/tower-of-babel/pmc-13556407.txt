216. β-hydroxybutyrate attenuates acute stress–induced impairment of memory retrieval via anti-inflammatory mechanisms
Authors: R Shinohara, S Ishikawa, M Tonosaki, T Kato
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundNeuropsychiatric disorders are vulnerable to stress, and high-intensity stress has been suggested to trigger neuroinflammatory responses. Neuroinflammation is thought to contribute to cognitive dysfunction across multiple disorders including Alzheimer's disease, depression, and schizophrenia. β-hydroxybutyrate (BHB), an endogenous ketone body that inhibits NLRP3 inflammasome activation and interleukin-1β (IL-1β) release, has attracted attention as a potential modulator of neuroinflammation in stress-related disorders. Preclinical studies have shown that neuroinflammation impairs learning and memory via proinflammatory cytokines such as IL-1β and tumor necrosis factor-α (TNF-α). Memory consists of distinct stages, including encoding, consolidation, retrieval, and reconsolidation. Previous studies have demonstrated that intrahippocampal administration of TNF-α impairs hippocampus-dependent contextual fear memory when administered before retrieval but not before conditioning, suggesting that TNF-α selectively disrupts retrieval while sparing encoding and consolidation. However, the memory stage most vulnerable to stress-induced endogenous neuroinflammation remains unclear.Aims & ObjectivesThis study aimed to determine (1) which phase of hippocampus-dependent memory is disrupted by stress, and (2) whether BHB can prevent stress-induced memory impairment.MethodMale C57BL/6J mice (8 weeks old) were trained with a contextual fear conditioning consisting of a 148 s context exposure, a 2 s 0.8 mA foot shock, and a 30 s post-shock period. Memory retrieval was tested 24 h after conditioning by re-exposure to the same context for 5 min, and freezing duration was measured. To examine phase-specific effects, 2 h restraint stress (RS) was applied at different time points: 8 h before conditioning or 8 h or 4 h before retrieval test. In some groups, BHB (250 mg/kg) was subcutaneously administered prior to RS exposure. To evaluate reconsolidation, mice that underwent the initial retrieval test were re-tested 24 h later. Hippocampal expression of inflammatory cytokines was measured at different time points after RS in a separate cohort by qPCR. All experiments were approved by the Animal Research Committee and adhered to the Guide for the Care and Use of Laboratory Animals of Hokkaido University.ResultsFreezing during the retrieval test was significantly reduced when RS was applied 8 h before retrieval test, whereas RS applied 8 h before conditioning or 4 h before retrieval test had no effect. Hippocampal expression of IL-1β and TNF-α mRNA increased following RS, peaking approximately 4 h after onset in a separate cohort. Pretreatment with BHB prior to RS exposure prevented both the stress-induced cytokine upregulation and the reduction in freezing. When mice that had experienced RS 8 h before initial retrieval test were re-tested 24 h later, freezing levels recovered to control levels, indicating that the stress-induced impairment was transient.Discussion & ConclusionsOur findings demonstrate that acute restraint stress selectively disrupts hippocampus-dependent memory retrieval in a time-dependent manner, while leaving encoding, consolidation, and reconsolidation intact. This retrieval-specific impairment coincided with elevated proinflammatory cytokine expression in the hippocampus. Pretreatment with BHB suppressed the stress-induced neuroinflammatory response and restored memory retrieval. These findings suggest that acute stress transiently impairs memory retrieval through cytokine-mediated hippocampal inflammation, and that BHB, as an endogenous metabolic modulator, may have therapeutic potential in mitigating stress-related cognitive dysfunction.

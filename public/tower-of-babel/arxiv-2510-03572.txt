On Entropic Characterization of Symmetry Breaking in Dynamical Systems I: Spontaneous and Dynamical Symmetry Breaking Preprint: APS/123-QED
Subhrajit Sinha, Parvathi Kooloth

Abstract
We propose a novel framework to analyze symmetry breaking in dynamical systems through the lens of entropy and information transfer. Information transfer quantifies the directional exchange of entropy between observables, allowing us to anticipate the onset of symmetry breaking. For local symmetry breakings—namely, local Spontaneous Symmetry Breaking (SSB) and Dynamical Symmetry Breaking (DSB)—we show that as a system loses symmetry, its trajectories exhibit a pronounced slowdown accompanied by an increase in Shannon entropy. This establishes a direct link between symmetry loss, dynamical slowing down, and entropy growth. We also extend the analysis to global symmetry breaking and characterize its associated entropy change. Finally, we demonstrate the efficacy of the proposed framework using representative examples, showing that information–theoretic quantities can serve as reliable precursors and diagnostics of symmetry–breaking transitions.

II.2
Symmetry Breaking in Dynamical Systems

As mentioned earlier, symmetry is ubiquitous in nature. However,
interesting
phenomena usually arise when the symmetry is broken. In general, the symmetry of a system can be exact, approximate, or broken. Exact symmetry means the symmetry is respected under all conditions, while approximate symmetry means the symmetric structure is valid under some conditions. On the other hand, broken symmetry encompasses a variety of phenomena and is dependent on the system considered and its context. In particular, the following different types of symmetry breaking can occur in a dynamical system
Wulfman 2010
;
Golubitsky et al. 2012
:

•

Explicit symmetry breaking:
The governing equations themselves do not possess the full symmetry of the group

G
and the symmetry is broken by parameters or forcing.

•

Spontaneous symmetry breaking (SSB):
The equations are symmetric under

G
, but the solutions/attractors are only symmetric under a subgroup
H\subsetneq G
.

•

Dynamical symmetry breaking (DSB):
Both the equations and the initial condition are symmetric, but the time evolution of densities or measures breaks symmetry.

•

Noise-induced symmetry breaking:
Random perturbations break symmetry, even if the deterministic dynamics is symmetric.

•

Pattern-forming symmetry breaking:
Continuous symmetries (e.g., translations, rotations) are broken in PDEs through instabilities that produce patterns.

•

Synchronization-induced symmetry breaking:
In coupled oscillator networks with permutation symmetry, synchronized clusters form that are invariant only under a subgroup of permutations.

In this work, we study SSB and DSB and show how the information transfer measure can be used to predict these symmetry-breaking transitions.

II.2.1
Spontaneous Symmetry Breaking

We consider a system in double-well potential (Fig.
3
(a)) of the form
V(x)=-\frac{1}{2}ax^{2}+\frac{1}{4}bx^{4}
, so that the equation of motion is

\displaystyle\dot{x}=-\frac{dV}{dx}=ax-bx^{3}.

(2)

Figure 3:
Particle in a symmetric double-well potential.

For
a<0
, the system (
2
) has a single stable equilibrium point at the origin, while for
a>0
, the system has two stable equilibrium points at
x=\pm\sqrt{a/b}
and the origin is unstable. The bifurcation for this system is shown in Fig.
3

This form of symmetry breaking is known as Spontaneous Symmetry Breaking. Intuitively speaking, spontaneous symmetry breaking is a phenomenon where a stable state of a system does not respect the symmetry of the Hamiltonian, Lagrangian, or action. This intuition can be generalized to any dynamical system with symmetry group
G
as follows.

Definition 10

(Spontaneous Symmetry Breaking
Golubitsky et al. 2012
;
Wulfman 2010
)
.

Let
G
be the symmetry group of a dynamical system with attractor
M
. With this, SSB occurs if the isotropy group of

is a proper subgroup of
G
.

Suppose a parametric
G
-equivariant system, with parameter
\mu
, has only one stable equilibrium point for
\mu<\mu_{c}
. Then the invariant measure/density
(\rho_{inv})
is a Dirac delta at the equilibrium point and the
\rho_{inv}
is left invariant under the action of the entire group
G
. Now, suppose for
\mu=\mu_{c}
, new stable equilibrium points arise in the phase space.

Figure 4:
(a) Stability-losing SSB. (b) Multi-stability-driven SSB.

Then the invariant density will have multiple peaks, each corresponding to a stable equilibrium point (see Fig.
4

G
. This is because the equilibrium points are related by the group action, and this phenomenon is known as Spontaneous Symmetry Breaking.

So in the density picture, SSB occurs if an invariant measure
splits
and forms new hills and valleys, corresponding to the formation of new attractor sets. Now, this splitting can occur in two ways:

1.

Stability-losing SSB:
The split takes place at the peak of the invariant density before SSB, so that the original stable equilibrium is lost. This is depicted in Fig.
4
(a), where as the parameter
\mu
increases, the peak at the origin disappears and forms a local minima, which corresponds to an unstable equilibrium point in the state space. This is known as local SSB in the context of equivariant bifurcation theory

2.

Multi-stability-driven SSB:
The peak of the density before SSB maintains its stability, but at SSB, new equilibrium points emerge, which translates to the appearance of new peaks in the invariant density. See Fig.
4
(b) and this is global SSB (GSSB)
Golubitsky and Stewart 2002

Beekman et al. 2019

III.1
Stability Losing SSB and Critical Slowing

Theorem 12
.

Let
f(x;\mu):X\times I\to TX
be a
G
-equivariant family of vector fields.
Suppose there exists a critical parameter
\mu_{c}\in\mathbb{R}
such that:

•

For
\mu<\mu_{c}
, the system admits a
G
-symmetric equilibrium
x^{\star}(\mu)\in\mathrm{Fix}_{X}(G)
, i.e.
f(x^{\star}(\mu);\mu)=0
.

•

At
\mu=\mu_{c}
, a spontaneous symmetry-breaking bifurcation occurs: the symmetric equilibrium loses stability and non-symmetric equilibria bifurcate from it.

Then the relaxation time near
x^{\star}(\mu)
diverges as
\mu\to\mu_{c}^{-}
. That is,
\lim_{\mu\to\mu_{c}^{-}}\tau_{\mathrm{relax}}(\mu)\to\infty
, where

\displaystyle\tau_{\mathrm{relax}}(\mu):=-\frac{1}{\max_{\lambda\in\mathrm{spec}(A(\mu))}\mathrm{Re}[\lambda]}

\displaystyle A(\mu):=Df(x^{\star}(\mu);\mu).

Proof.

For
\mu<\mu_{c}
, the equilibrium
x^{\star}(\mu)
is stable, so all eigenvalues of
A(\mu)
satisfy
\mathrm{Re}[\lambda(\mu)]<0
.
At
\mu=\mu_{c}
, by assumption of symmetry breaking, at least one eigenvalue crosses the imaginary axis,
\lim_{\mu\to\mu_{c}^{-}}\mathrm{Re}[\lambda_{j}(\mu)]=0^{-}
.

Now, the asymptotic decay rate of perturbations near
x^{\star}(\mu)
is governed by the spectral abscissa
s(\mu):=\max_{\lambda\in\mathrm{spec}(A(\mu))}\mathrm{Re}[\lambda]
, and the slowest decay rate is
-s(\mu)
. Hence, the relaxation time is
\tau_{\mathrm{relax}}(\mu):=-\frac{1}{s(\mu)}
.

Since
s(\mu)\nearrow 0^{-}
as
\mu\to\mu_{c}^{-}
, the denominator tends to zero from below, and hence,
\lim_{\mu\to\mu_{c}^{-}}\tau_{\mathrm{relax}}(\mu)\to\infty
.
∎

This divergence of relaxation time is the standard signature of
critical slowing down
.
In the context of equivariant bifurcation theory, it is typical of generic codimension-1
symmetry-breaking bifurcations such as supercritical or subcritical pitchfork, transcritical,
and Hopf bifurcations
Golubitsky and Stewart 2002
;
Wiggins 2003
.
In these cases, a single real eigenvalue (or a complex-conjugate pair in the Hopf case)
crosses the imaginary axis transversally at
\mu=\mu_{c}
, while the remaining eigenvalues
remain strictly stable and vary smoothly.
Thus the spectral abscissa
s(\mu)
approaches zero monotonically, driving the divergence of

\tau_{\mathrm{relax}}(\mu)
.

III.2
DSB and Critical Slowing

III.2.1
An alternative definition of DSB

In definition
11
, DSB was characterized in terms of the breaking of symmetry
G
-invariant distributions, that is, the definition is in the P-F operator setting. The Koopman operator, which governs the evolution of observables, is dual to the P-F operator
Lasota and Mackey 1994
, and the following theorem provides an alternate characterization of DSB in terms of observables.

Theorem 13

(Observable characterization of DSB)
.

Let
X
be a Polish space (this assumption is for mathematical convenience), and let
G
be a compact (finite or Lie) group acting continuously on
X
via
\Theta(g,x)=g\cdot x
.
Let
\varphi^{t}:X\to X
be a continuous flow, and let
\rho_{0}\in\mathcal{M}(X)
with
\rho_{t}:={\cal P}^{t}\rho_{0}
.

Then the system exhibits dynamical symmetry breaking (DSB), i.e.

\exists\,t_{b}>0:\rho_{t}\in\mathrm{Fix}_{\rho}(G)\forall t<t_{b},\;\rho_{t}\notin\mathrm{Fix}_{\rho}(G)\ \ \forall t>t_{b},

if and only if

\displaystyle\int_{X}h(x)\,d\rho_{t}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{X}h(g\cdot x)\,d\rho_{t}(x),

\displaystyle\forall h\in C_{b}(X),\ \forall g\in G,

for all
t<t_{b}
, but for every
t>t_{b}
there exist
h\in C_{b}(X)
and
g\in G
with

\int_{X}h(x)\,d\rho_{t}(x)\neq\int_{X}h(g\cdot x)\,d\rho_{t}(x).

Here
C_{b}(X)=\{h:X\to\mathbb{R}:h\textnormal{ is continuous and bounded}\}.

Proof.

(
\Rightarrow
)
Suppose
\rho_{t}
is
G
-invariant for all
t<t_{b}
. Then for each Borel set
A\subset X
and each
g\in G
,

\rho_{t}(g\cdot A)=\rho_{t}(A).

By the the separating property of
C_{b}(X)
and uniqueness of measures from integrals against
C_{b}(X)
on Polish spaces
Folland 1999
;
Walters 1982
, equality of measures on Borel sets is equivalent to equality of expectations against all bounded continuous test functions. Hence

\displaystyle\int_{X}h(x)\,d\rho_{t}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{X}h(g\cdot x)\,d\rho_{t}(x),

\displaystyle\forall h\in C_{b}(X),\ g\in G,

for all
t<t_{b}
.

(
\Leftarrow
)
Conversely, suppose

\displaystyle\int_{X}h(x)\,d\rho_{t}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{X}h(g\cdot x)\,d\rho_{t}(x),

\displaystyle\forall h\in C_{b}(X),\ g\in G,

Then, by the separating property of
C_{b}(X)
functions
Rudin 1991
, the two measures
\rho_{t}
and
g_{\#}\rho_{t}
coincide. Hence

g_{\#}\rho_{t}=\rho_{t},\quad\forall g\in G,

i.e.
\rho_{t}\in\mathrm{Fix}_{\rho}(G)
.

Therefore,
G
-invariance of
\rho_{t}
is equivalent to equality of expectations for all observables
h
and their
G
-translates.

\begin{array}[]{ccc}h\in C_{b}(X)&\xrightarrow{\ \mathcal{U}^{t}\ }&h\circ\varphi^{t}\\[4.30554pt]
\Big\downarrow\langle\cdot,\rho_{0}\rangle&&\Big\downarrow\langle\cdot,\rho_{0}\rangle\\[4.30554pt]
\langle h,\rho_{0}\rangle&\xrightarrow{\ =\ }&\langle h,\rho_{t}\rangle\end{array}

Now, dynamical symmetry breaking means: there exists
t_{b}>0
such that

\rho_{t}\in\mathrm{Fix}_{\rho}(G)\quad\forall t<t_{b},\qquad\rho_{t}\notin\mathrm{Fix}_{\rho}(G)\quad\forall t>t_{b}.

By the above equivalence, this is exactly the statement that equality of expectations

\int_{X}h(x)\,d\rho_{t}(x)=\int_{X}h(g\cdot x)\,d\rho_{t}(x)

holds for all
h\in C_{b}(X),g\in G
when
t<t_{b}
, but fails for at least one choice of
h,g
once
t>t_{b}
.
∎

In other words, DSB under equivariant flow is equivalently characterized by the departure of
\rho_{t}
from the fixed-point set of the group action or by the Koopman evolution escaping the fixed-point subspace of observables.

III.2.2
The build-up to the main result

Lemma 14
.

Consider a

\displaystyle\mathrm{Fix}_{{\cal L}^{2}(X)}(G)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\{h\in{\cal L}^{2}(X)\mid h(g\cdot x)=h(x),

\displaystyle\quad\forall g\in G,\ x\in X\}.

Then we have
\mathcal{U}^{t}(\mathrm{Fix}(G))\subseteq\mathrm{Fix}(G)
, where

is the Koopman operator semigroup.

Proof.

Let

, i.e.,
h(q\cdot x)=h(x)
for all

and

We show

,

By definition,

Proposition 16

(Approach to the boundary of
\Fix_{\rho}(G)
at DSB)
.

Let
X
be a Polish space and
G
a compact group acting continuously on
X
.
Let
\{\varphi^{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
be a continuous
G

\{\mathcal{P}^{t}\}_{t\geq 0}

\mathcal{M}(X)

Fix
\rho_{0}\in\mathcal{M}(X)
with

for all

\rho_{t}\in\Fix_{\rho}(G)\quad\forall\,t<t_{b},\qquad\rho_{t}\notin\Fix_{\rho}(G)\quad\forall\,t>t_{b}.

∗

Step 1: Closedness of

.

For each

\mathcal{M}(X)

t\mapsto\rho_{t}

Since

lies in
\Fix_{\rho}(G)

\rho_{t_{n}}\in\Fix_{\rho}(G)

\Fix_{\rho}(G)

\rho_{t_{b}}\in\Fix_{\rho}(G)

Step 4:
\rho_{t_{b}}
is a boundary point.

Let

\mathcal{M}(X)

Since

and the left/right limits follow by restricting

t\downarrow t_{b}

Interpretation.

The above results formalize how the
symmetry error

F(t)=\sup_{g\in G}d_{\mathrm{BL}}\!\big(\rho_{t},g_{\#}\rho_{t}\big)
behaves at a dynamical symmetry–breaking time
t_{b}
.
Proposition
17
shows that if the family of probability
laws
t\mapsto\rho_{t}
evolves continuously in the narrow topology,
then
F(t)
is also continuous. In that case, the system loses
symmetry smoothly: the distribution remains invariant for
t<t_{b}
and departs
continuously from the invariant manifold
\Fix_{\rho}(G)
for
t>t_{b}
.
This corresponds to a
continuous
or
second–order
DSB,
where the order parameter
F(t)
begins to grow continuously from zero.

In contrast, Propositions
18
–
19

identify mechanisms that produce a
discontinuous
or
first–order
DSB.
If the law
\rho_{t}
itself has a jump at
t_{b}
—so that the pre–break limit

\rho_{t_{b}}^{-}
is symmetric while the post–break limit
\rho_{t_{b}}^{+}
is not—then

F(t)
exhibits a finite right jump.
Equivalently, if a uniform asymmetry gap persists immediately after
t_{b}
,

F(t)
remains bounded below by that gap and cannot approach zero continuously.
Such a positive right jump in
F(t)
represents an abrupt loss of symmetry,
analogous to a first–order phase transition.

This dichotomy parallels the thermodynamic classification of phase transitions:
a continuous DSB corresponds to a smooth entropy growth,
whereas a discontinuous DSB corresponds to a finite entropy jump.

Theorem 20

(Divergence of relaxation time near DSB)
.

Let
X
be a Polish space and
G
a compact group acting continuously on
X
.
Let
\{\varphi^{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
be a continuous
G
–equivariant flow, and let

\{\mathcal{P}^{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
denote the associated Perron–Frobenius semigroup
on finite signed Borel measures

C_{0}
–semigroup
on the Banach space
\bigl({\mathcal{M}}(X),\|\cdot\|_{\mathrm{BL}^{\ast}}\bigr)

where

Let
\rho_{0}\in\mathcal{M}(X)
be
G

\rho_{t}:=\mathcal{P}^{t}\rho_{0}
,
and assume the system exhibits DSB at some

G

Local exponential decay hypothesis.

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \|\,Q\,\mathcal{P}^{s}\nu\,\|_{\mathrm{BL}^{\ast}}\ &\leq\ C(t_{0})\,e^{-\eta(t_{0})s}\,\|\,Q\nu\,\|_{\mathrm{BL}^{\ast}},\\
&\forall\,\nu\in U_{t_{0}},\ \forall\,s\geq 0.\end{aligned}

(4)

(Equivalently: the
G
–odd subspace is locally exponentially stable

Define the maximal local decay exponent at time

by

\displaystyle\lambda_{\max}(t_{0})\

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\ \sup\bigl\{\lambda<0:\ \exists\,C\geq 1

\displaystyle\text{s.t.}\ \|Q\,\mathcal{P}^{s}\nu\|_{\mathrm{BL}^{\ast}}\leq Ce^{\lambda s}\|Q\nu\|_{\mathrm{BL}^{\ast}}

\lim_{t_{0}\uparrow t_{b}}\ \lambda_{\max}(t_{0})\ =\ 0^{-},\qquad\text{and}\qquad\lim_{t_{0}\uparrow t_{b}}\ \tau(t_{0})\ =\ +\infty,

).

),
and compactness of

III.3
Shannon Entropy and Symmetry Breaking

Assumption 22
.

Near the critical parameter
\alpha_{c}
, trace of the Jacobian
\mathrm{tr}\,A(\alpha)
,
A(\alpha):=Df_{\alpha}(x^{\star}(\alpha))
, is non-decreasing as
\alpha\nearrow\alpha_{c}
.

This assumption is satisfied for generic
codimension-1
symmetry-breaking bifurcations
(including supercritical and subcritical pitchfork, transcritical, and Hopf bifurcations),
where a single real eigenvalue (or a complex-conjugate pair) crosses the imaginary axis transversally
while the remaining spectrum stays strictly stable and varies smoothly

Kuznetsov 2004
;
Guckenheimer and Holmes 1983
.

Theorem 23

(Entropy growth near symmetry breaking)
.

Let
X
be a smooth manifold, and let
f_{\alpha}:X\to TX
be a
C^{2}
family of
G
-equivariant vector fields generating flows
\Phi_{\alpha}^{t}
and Perron–Frobenius operators
\mathcal{P}_{\alpha}^{t}
.

Let
\rho_{0}\in{\cal L}^{1}(X)
be a Gaussian probability density centered at a
G
-symmetric equilibrium
x^{\star}(\alpha)
. Define

\rho_{\alpha}(t):=\mathcal{P}_{\alpha}^{t}\rho_{0},\;H_{\alpha}(t):=-\int_{X}\rho_{\alpha}(t,x)\log\rho_{\alpha}(t,x)\,dx.

Suppose the system undergoes stability-losing symmetry breaking or DSB at
\alpha=\alpha_{c}
. Then, under assumption (
22
), for each fixed
t>0
, the differential entropy
H_{\alpha}(t)
is non-decreasing as
\alpha\nearrow\alpha_{c}
.

Proof.

Linearizing
f_{\alpha}
at
x^{\star}(\alpha)
yields

\dot{x}=A(\alpha)(x-x^{\star}),\qquad A(\alpha):=Df_{\alpha}(x^{\star}).

If the initial density
\rho_{0}
is Gaussian with covariance
\Sigma_{0}
, then under this linear flow the solution remains Gaussian,

\rho_{\alpha}(t)\sim\mathcal{N}\big(x^{\star},\,\Sigma_{\alpha}(t)\big),\qquad\Sigma_{\alpha}(t)=e^{A(\alpha)t}\Sigma_{0}e^{A(\alpha)^{\top}t}.

The entropy of a Gaussian is

H_{\alpha}(t)=\tfrac{1}{2}\log\!\Big((2\pi e)^{d}\det\Sigma_{\alpha}(t)\Big).

Using
\det\Sigma_{\alpha}(t)=\det(e^{A(\alpha)t})^{2}\det\Sigma_{0}
gives

H_{\alpha}(t)=\tfrac{1}{2}\log\!\Big((2\pi e)^{d}\det\Sigma_{0}\Big)+t\,\mathrm{tr}\,A(\alpha).

Thus

\frac{\partial H_{\alpha}(t)}{\partial\alpha}=t\,\frac{\partial}{\partial\alpha}\,\mathrm{tr}\,A(\alpha).

By assumption,
\mathrm{tr}\,A(\alpha)
is non-decreasing as
\alpha\nearrow\alpha_{c}
, which implies
\partial_{\alpha}H_{\alpha}(t)\geq 0
. Therefore
H_{\alpha}(t)
is non-decreasing as
\alpha
approaches
\alpha_{c}
.
∎

Remark 24
.

The identity
\frac{d}{dt}H(\rho_{t})=\int(\nabla\cdot f)\,\rho_{t}\,dx

assumes sufficient smoothness and either compact
X
without boundary
or vanishing flux
f\rho_{t}\cdot n=0
on
\partial X
.

Remark 25
.

The monotonicity of entropy near critical slowing is not restricted to Gaussian densities. Under suitable assumptions, similar behavior can be expected more generally:

•

If the linearized flow exhibits exponential contraction rates whose real parts increase monotonically toward zero as
\alpha\nearrow\alpha_{c}
, then the effective volume contraction slows down, leading to slower concentration of trajectories and hence increasing entropy
Teschl 2012
.

•

From the Liouville (or Fokker–Planck) equation for
\rho_{\alpha}
,

\frac{d}{dt}H_{\alpha}(t)=\int_{X}\nabla\cdot f_{\alpha}(x)\,\rho_{\alpha}(t,x)\,dx,

growth of entropy is tied directly to the divergence of the vector field. Since
\nabla\cdot f_{\alpha}=\mathrm{tr}(Df_{\alpha})
, monotonic increase of
\mathrm{tr}(Df_{\alpha})
with
\alpha
yields monotone entropy growth
Arnold 1989
;
Villani 2008
.

•

More generally, if
\rho_{0}
is any smooth, compactly supported density and the flow is smooth, the entropy reflects the long-time stretching and folding of volumes. As the spectral gap closes at critical slowing, decay of density peaks is delayed, which again results in entropy increase
Lasota and Mackey 1994
;
Khasminskii 2011
.

Thus, while the Gaussian-linearized setting provides a transparent calculation, the entropy monotonicity phenomenon is robust and extends to broader classes of densities and flows.

This monotonicity in entropy of the system manifests itself in an increase in information flow between the states of the system and we use this information transfer measure to predict the onset of symmetry breaking.

IV
Global Spontaneous Symmetry Breaking

In this section, we analyze the phenomenon of Global Spontaneous Symmetry Breaking (GSSB) and investigate how Shannon entropy behaves when a system undergoes GSSB.

IV.1
GSSB and Shannon Entropy

The spontaneous symmetry breaking (SSB) discussed so far has been
local
in nature: a single symmetric equilibrium loses stability and splits into multiple stable equilibria, or the invariant density locally bifurcates into symmetry-related peaks. The total invariant measure remains confined within the same topological basin, and the change in entropy is a local effect—the density becomes multimodal but does not globally redistribute.

Global
spontaneous symmetry breaking (GSSB), on the other hand, corresponds to a qualitative reorganization of the entire invariant measure. Instead of a local bifurcation, the system undergoes a global change in the structure or support of its invariant density, resulting in a different symmetry class at the level of the full measure. The governing equations remain
G
-equivariant, but the invariant density
\rho_{\mathrm{inv}}
changes its symmetry globally. Intuitively, in a local SSB the symmetry is lost “around” a particular attractor—only the local neighborhood of the invariant measure changes—whereas in a global SSB, the symmetry is lost
across
the entire invariant set. The probability mass redistributes among previously separated invariant regions, or the global connectivity of the attractor changes (as in crises, global bifurcations, or transitions between ergodic components). In the entropy landscape, this appears as a discontinuous “jump” in relative Shannon entropy, reflecting a global delocalization of the invariant measure.

Definition 33

(Global Spontaneous Symmetry Breaking)
.

Let
\{\mathcal{P}_{\mu}^{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
denote the Perron–Frobenius semigroup associated with a
G
-equivariant flow
\varphi_{\mu}^{t}:X\to X
parameterized by
\mu
, and let
\rho_{\mathrm{inv}}(\mu)\in\mathcal{M}(X)
be the invariant density satisfying
\mathcal{P}_{\mu}^{t}\rho_{\mathrm{inv}}(\mu)=\rho_{\mathrm{inv}}(\mu)
for all
t>0
.
The system is said to undergo
global spontaneous symmetry breaking (GSSB)
at
\mu=\mu_{c}
if

\displaystyle\rho_{\mathrm{inv}}(\mu)\in\mathrm{Fix}_{\rho}(G)\quad\forall\,\mu<\mu_{c},

\displaystyle\rho_{\mathrm{inv}}(\mu)\notin\mathrm{Fix}_{\rho}(G)\quad\forall\,\mu>\mu_{c},

and the mapping
\mu\mapsto\rho_{\mathrm{inv}}(\mu)
is discontinuous in the weak-* topology on
\mathcal{M}(X)
.

Equivalently, GSSB occurs when the invariant measure undergoes a discontinuous change in its support or ergodic decomposition, leading to a discontinuous reduction of its isotropy subgroup

H_{\mu}:=\{g\in G:g_{\#}\rho_{\mathrm{inv}}(\mu)=\rho_{\mathrm{inv}}(\mu)\}.

Operator–theoretic signature.

Let
\mathcal{U}_{\mu}^{t}
denote the Koopman semigroup on observables,

(\mathcal{U}_{\mu}^{t}h)(x)=h(\varphi_{\mu}^{t}(x))
, and let
\mathcal{U}_{g}h:=h(g^{-1}\!\cdot\!x)
be the unitary representation of
G
on
{\cal L}^{2}(X)
. The orthogonal projector onto the
G
-invariant subspace is

P_{G}:=\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{g\in G}\mathcal{U}_{g}
(or
P_{G}h=\int_{G}\mathcal{U}_{g}h\,dg
for Lie
G
).
For
G
–equivariant flows,
[\mathcal{U}_{\mu}^{t},P_{G}]=0
for all
t\geq 0
, hence also

\mathsf{E}_{\mu}\;:=\;s\!-\!\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\mathcal{U}_{\mu}^{t}\,dt\quad\text{satisfies}\quad[\mathsf{E}_{\mu},P_{G}]=0.

Global SSB is therefore detected not by a commutator, but by non-invariance of
asymptotic observables
: there exists
h
with
h_{\infty}:=\mathsf{E}_{\mu}h
such that
P_{G}h_{\infty}\neq h_{\infty}
, equivalently
g_{\#}\pi_{\mu}\neq\pi_{\mu}
(cf. Lemma
41
).

Entropy formulation.

Because SSB is an asymptotic property of invariant measures, it is natural to measure disorder relative to a
G
-invariant reference measure
\mu_{0}
(e.g., the invariant measure of the unbroken phase). Define the
relative Shannon entropy

H_{\mu_{0}}(\rho):=-\int_{X}\rho(x)\,\log\!\frac{\rho(x)}{d\mu_{0}(x)}\,d\mu_{0}(x),

which satisfies
H_{\mu_{0}}((g)_{\#}\rho)=H_{\mu_{0}}(\rho)
whenever
g_{\#}\mu_{0}=\mu_{0}
.
This avoids any volume-preservation requirement and makes the formulation coordinate-free.
With this invariant notion of entropy, we can now quantify how global SSB reorganizes the invariant measure into a set of disjoint ergodic components.

Intuitive summary.

The symmetric phase may be viewed as a single broad distribution

\rho^{-}
composed of overlapping
G
–related components,
whereas after symmetry breaking the invariant measure
\rho^{+}

splits into distinct, nonoverlapping components
\{\rho_{i}^{+}\}

weighted by the probabilities
\{p_{i}\}
.
The total invariant–measure entropy
H_{\mu_{0}}(\rho)
therefore reflects two competing effects:
(1) the
internal spreading
of each component, quantified by the component entropies

H_{\mu_{0}}(\sigma^{\pm})
, and
(2) the
symmetry-induced splitting
of the support, which introduces
a discrete structure with weights
\{p_{i}\}
.
The pre-break overlap information
I_{-}
measures how much the symmetric mixture
already “knows” about this hidden decomposition.
The net entropy change across the transition,

\Delta H_{\mathrm{global}}=\Delta H_{\mathrm{comp}}+I_{-}-\log m,

thus expresses a balance between the
entropy gained by internal diffusion (
\Delta H_{\mathrm{comp}}>0
)
and the entropy lost by resolving symmetry-related copies (
\log m-I_{-}
).
Depending on which effect dominates, the global entropy may jump upward or downward
at the critical parameter
\mu_{c}
.

Theorem 34

(Entropy jump across global SSB)
.

Let
X\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
carry a reference
\sigma
-finite measure
\mu_{0}
preserved by a compact group
G
(i.e.,
g_{\#}\mu_{0}=\mu_{0}
for all
g\in G
).
Suppose a
G
-equivariant
C^{1}
flow admits invariant densities on either side of a global SSB at
\mu=\mu_{c}
of the form

\rho^{-}=\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\rho_{i}^{-},\qquad\rho^{+}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}p_{i}\,\rho_{i}^{+},

where
\rho_{i}^{\pm}=(g_{i})_{\#}\sigma^{\pm}
for some
g_{1},\dots,g_{m}\in G
,
\sigma^{\pm}\in\mathcal{L}^{1}(X,\mu_{0})
, and
p_{i}>0
,
\sum_{i}p_{i}=1
.
Assume:

(a)

(
Pre-break overlap
) There exist

\rho_{i}^{-}\rho_{j}^{-}>0
on a set of positive
\mu_{0}
-measure.

(b)

(
Post-break separation
)
\rho_{i}^{+}\rho_{j}^{+}=0
a.e. for
i\neq j
.

(c)

H_{\mu_{0}}(\rho_{i}^{\pm}):=-\!\int\rho_{i}^{\pm}\log\rho_{i}^{\pm}\,d\mu_{0}
are finite (hence
H_{\mu_{0}}(\rho^{\pm})
are finite).

Define

\displaystyle I_{-}:=\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\mathrm{KL}_{\mu_{0}}(\rho_{i}^{-}\|\rho^{-}),

\displaystyle\Delta H_{\mathrm{comp}}:=H_{\mu_{0}}(\sigma^{+})-H_{\mu_{0}}(\sigma^{-}).

Then the invariant–measure entropy satisfies the exact identity

\Delta H_{\mathrm{global}}:=H_{\mu_{0}}(\rho^{+})-H_{\mu_{0}}(\rho^{-})=\Delta H_{\mathrm{comp}}+I_{-}-\log m.

In particular,
0\leq I_{-}<\log m
, with strict inequality whenever (a) holds nontrivially.

Proof.

For brevity, write
H(\cdot):=H_{\mu_{0}}(\cdot)
and
\mathrm{KL}(\cdot\|\cdot):=\mathrm{KL}_{\mu_{0}}(\cdot\|\cdot)
.

Step 1 (Mixture identity).

For
\rho=\sum_{i}w_{i}\eta_{i}
with
\sum_{i}w_{i}=1
and finite entropies,

H(\rho)=\sum_{i}w_{i}H(\eta_{i})+H(w)-\sum_{i}w_{i}\,\mathrm{KL}(\eta_{i}\|\rho),

where
H(w)=-\sum_{i}w_{i}\log w_{i}
.
Indeed,

\displaystyle\sum_{i}w_{i}\,\mathrm{KL}(\eta_{i}\|\rho)=\sum_{i}w_{i}\!\int\eta_{i}\log\frac{\eta_{i}}{\rho}\,d\mu_{0}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}w_{i}\!\int\eta_{i}\log\eta_{i}\,d\mu_{0}-\int\rho\log\rho\,d\mu_{0}.

Step 2 (Apply to
\rho^{\pm}
and subtract).

Apply Step 1 to
\rho^{+}=\sum_{i}p_{i}\rho_{i}^{+}
and to
\rho^{-}=\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i}\rho_{i}^{-}

\displaystyle H(\rho^{+})-H(\rho^{-})

\displaystyle=\Big[\sum_{i}p_{i}H(\rho_{i}^{+})-\tfrac{1}{m}\sum_{i}H(\rho_{i}^{-})\Big]

\displaystyle+\big[H(p)-\log m\big]

\displaystyle-\sum_{i}p_{i}\,\mathrm{KL}(\rho_{i}^{+}\|\rho^{+})

\displaystyle+\tfrac{1}{m}\sum_{i}\mathrm{KL}(\rho_{i}^{-}\|\rho^{-}).

By
G
-invariance,
H(\rho_{i}^{\pm})=H(\sigma^{\pm})
are constant in

, so the first bracket equals
H(\sigma^{+})-H(\sigma^{-})=\Delta H_{\mathrm{comp}}
.

Step 3 (Use post-break separation).

By (b), on
\operatorname{supp}\rho_{i}^{+}

\rho^{+}=p_{i}\rho_{i}^{+}
; hence
\mathrm{KL}(\rho_{i}^{+}\|\rho^{+})=-\log p_{i}
and
\sum_{i}p_{i}\,\mathrm{KL}(\rho_{i}^{+}\|\rho^{+})=H(p)
. Therefore

H(\rho^{+})-H(\rho^{-})=\Delta H_{\mathrm{comp}}+I_{-}-\log m.

Step 4 (Range of
I_{-}
).

Since
\rho^{-}=\frac{1}{m}\sum_{j}\rho_{j}^{-}\geq\frac{1}{m}\rho_{i}^{-}

\log(\rho_{i}^{-}/\rho^{-})\leq\log m

\mathrm{KL}(\rho_{i}^{-}\|\rho^{-})\leq\log m

0\leq I_{-}<\log m
.
∎

Corollary 35

(Upward and downward entropy jumps)
.

The sign of the global entropy change is governed by the balance between the
component–entropy change
\Delta H_{\mathrm{comp}}
and the symmetry penalty
\log m-I_{-}
.

(i)

(
Upward jump
) If
\Delta H_{\mathrm{comp}}>\log m-I_{-}
, then
H_{\mu_{0}}(\rho^{+})>H_{\mu_{0}}(\rho^{-})
.
This typically occurs when the broken-phase components become more diffuse:
increased diffusion, softened wells, or enhanced intra-basin mixing.

(ii)

(
Downward jump
) If
\Delta H_{\mathrm{comp}}<\log m-I_{-}
, then
H_{\mu_{0}}(\rho^{+})<H_{\mu_{0}}(\rho^{-})
.
This arises when symmetry breaking creates sharper, more confined components
(e.g., deeper wells or contracting deterministic dynamics).

The entropy jump
\Delta H_{\mathrm{global}}
therefore quantifies the
information gained when a single ergodic component splits into a

G
–structured ensemble of components at the symmetry–breaking
parameter
\mu_{c}
.
From a statistical perspective, the increase in relative entropy reflects
the emergence of additional distinguishable macrostates, while from an
information–theoretic viewpoint it represents new correlations among
degrees of freedom that were previously redundant under the group action.
To make this connection precise, we next express the global entropy change
in terms of the mutual information between the microscopic state and the
emergent symmetry label.

Theorem 36

(Mutual-information change at global SSB)
.

Under the hypotheses of Theorem
34
(especially post-break separation), introduce a symmetry label
S\in\{1,\dots,m\}
with
\Pr[S=i]=p_{i}
and post-break conditional densities
\rho^{+}(x\mid S=i)=\rho_{i}^{+}(x)
, so that
\rho^{+}(x)=\sum_{i}p_{i}\rho_{i}^{+}(x)
.
Then the mutual information between
S
and
X

\displaystyle I(S;X)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle H_{\mu_{0}}(\rho^{+})-\sum_{i=1}^{m}p_{i}H_{\mu_{0}}(\rho_{i}^{+})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle H(p)-\sum_{i=1}^{m}p_{i}\,\mathrm{KL}_{\mu_{0}}(\rho_{i}^{+}\|\rho^{+}).

In particular, with pairwise disjoint supports of
\{\rho_{i}^{+}\}
,

\mathrm{KL}(\rho_{i}^{+}\|\rho^{+})=-\log p_{i}\quad\Rightarrow\quad I(S;X)=H(p).

Hence, relative to the pre-break uniform mixture, the mutual information changes from

I_{-}=\tfrac{1}{m}\sum_{i}\mathrm{KL}(\rho_{i}^{-}\|\rho^{-})
to
H(p)
as the symmetry breaks.

Proof.

By definition,
I(S;X)=H(X)-H(X\mid S)=H_{\mu_{0}}(\rho^{+})-\sum_{i}p_{i}H_{\mu_{0}}(\rho_{i}^{+})
.
Applying the mixture identity from Step 1 of Theorem
34
to
\rho^{+}=\sum_{i}p_{i}\rho_{i}^{+}
yields

H(\rho^{+})=\sum_{i}p_{i}H(\rho_{i}^{+})+H(p)-\sum_{i}p_{i}\,\mathrm{KL}(\rho_{i}^{+}\|\rho^{+}),

hence the second expression for
I(S;X)
.
Under post-break separation,
\rho^{+}=p_{i}\rho_{i}^{+}
on
\operatorname{supp}\rho_{i}^{+}
, so
\mathrm{KL}(\rho_{i}^{+}\|\rho^{+})=-\log p_{i}
and
I(S;X)=H(p)
.
∎

The above relation clarifies that the global entropy increase at the
symmetry–breaking point arises entirely from the mutual information
between the microscopic state and the emergent discrete symmetry label.
In the symmetric phase this label is statistically independent of the state,
yielding zero mutual information; after the transition, the loss of ergodicity
couples them, encoding which ergodic component the system occupies.
Thus the entropy jump
\Delta H_{\mathrm{global}}
measures the information
required to specify the broken–symmetry branch, providing a precise
information–theoretic meaning to global spontaneous symmetry breaking.

V.1.1
SSB in Duffing oscillator

A Duffing oscillator is a second order nonlinear dynamical system which is used to model a class of damped and forced oscillations. The dynamic equation of the double-well Duffing oscillator is

\displaystyle\ddot{x}+\delta\dot{x}+\alpha x+\beta x^{3}=\gamma(t)\cos(\omega t)

V.2
Stommel Model: Prelude to a More General Framework

The Stommel model
Stommel 1961
is an idealized model for Atlantic Meridional Overturning Circulation (AMOC)- a system of ocean currents that acts as a major conduit for heat, salinity, and carbon in the Earth’s climate. It relates the strength of the circulation (
\Psi=T-S
) to the nonlinear interactions between the equator-to-pole temperature gradient
T
and the corresponding salinity gradient
S

Alkhayuon et al. 2019

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \frac{dT}{dt}&=\eta_{1}-T(1+|T-S|),\\
\frac{dS}{dt}&=\eta_{2}-S(\eta_{3}+|T-S|).\end{aligned}

(11)

The analysis here is restricted to the deterministic form of the Stommel model, as stochastic perturbations primarily affect the timescale of transitions but not the underlying bifurcation structure relevant to symmetry breaking. This is a non-gradient system, that is, the forcing is a non-conservative vector field over the phase space. The three parameters in the model,
\eta_{1},\;\eta_{2}
, and
\eta_{3}
represent the relative strength of thermal forcing, the relative strength of freshwater forcing, and the ratio of thermal to freshwater restoration times, respectively. In this study,
\eta_{2}
is used as the bifurcation parameter. We note here that the Stommel system exhibits a bistable regime for
0.9<\eta_{2}<1.22
, characterized by two possible states: an AMOC
off
state with
\Psi<0
and an AMOC
on
state with
\Psi>0
(see
Alkhayuon et al. 2019
for a detailed discussion of the bifurcation characteristics).

Remark 43
.

The modified Stommel model, in which the parameters satisfy
\eta_{1}=\eta_{2}
and
\eta_{3}=1
, has been the original motivation for the present work. Under these conditions, the model becomes symmetric under the exchange of the temperature and salinity variables
(T,S)\mapsto(S,T)
, representing a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}

\eta_{2}

Remark 44

(Beyond Symmetry Breaking)
.

Although the Stommel model is often cited as an example of abrupt climate transitions and tipping phenomena, it does not constitute a true instance of symmetry breaking in the strict dynamical sense. The underlying system lacks a symmetry group whose invariance is lost at the bifurcation. Nevertheless, the observed sudden shift in its attractor structure represents a generic critical transition. The fact that the information–transfer measure detects this transition and identifies the dominant causal direction underscores its broader applicability beyond symmetry–breaking phenomena. In particular, information transfer can serve as a universal diagnostic of early–warning and directional influence in nonlinear systems undergoing bifurcation–induced transitions. This suggests that the SSB framework developed here can be extended to encompass a wider class of critical phenomena in future work.

VI
Conclusions

In this work, we developed a formal framework for analyzing symmetry breaking in dynamical systems and established a rigorous connection between symmetry breaking and the entropy of the system. Focusing on stability-losing SSB, dynamical SSB, and global SSB, we showed that when a system undergoes a local symmetry breaking, its trajectories exhibit a pronounced
slowdown
, a hallmark of critical transitions. This slowing down is accompanied by a sharp increase in the system’s entropy, arising from a discontinuity in the space of probability densities. Similar entropy–growth results were obtained for global SSB. Finally, through illustrative examples, we demonstrated how this entropy increase—manifesting as enhanced information flow between system variables—can serve as an effective early–warning indicator and diagnostic for symmetry–breaking transitions.

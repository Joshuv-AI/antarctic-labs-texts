Homology of Rook-Brauer Algebras and Motzkin Algebras
Khoa Ta,

Abstract
. Using the technique of inductive resolution introduced in [ 2 ] , we prove that the homology of Rook-Brauer Algebra, interpreted as appropriate Tor-group, is isomorphic to that of symmetric group for all degrees under the assumption that ϵ \epsilon in R R is invertible; furthermore, we also prove the homology of the Motzkin algebras vanishes in positive degrees under the same assumption. These results thereby establish homological stability of both algebras.



1.
Introduction

The Rook-Brauer Algebras and Motzkin Algebras are part of the family of diagram algebras that includes the Partition Algebra, Rook Algebra, Temperley-Lieb Algebra, Brauer Algebra, and many more. In recent years, homology of these algebras has been studied extensively by various authors (see
[
2
]
for partition algebras,
[
3
]
for Temperley-Lieb Algebras,
[
1
]
for Brauer Algebras,
[
4
]
for Rook and Rook-Brauer Algebras with restriction on parameters, see
[
6
]
for Tanabe algebras, uniform block permutation algebras and totally propagating partition algebras).

The results of most diagram algebras mentioned above are parameters-dependent i.e certain conditions, most notably the invertibility of the parameter, must be imposed to ensure the validity of these results. The Rook-Brauer algebra has two defining parameters,
\epsilon
and
\delta
(which will be recalled later), and in
[
4
]
, Guy Boyde attempted to use the concept of idempotents to demonstrate that the homology of Rook-Brauer algebras is isomorphic to that of symmetric groups in all degrees, provided that
\epsilon
is invertible. However, as noted by Andrew Fisher and Daniel Graves in their paper (see Remark 7.1.1
[
5
]
), the argument presented was found to be incorrect. Fisher and Graves, by additionally assuming the invertibility of
\delta
, were able to refine and correct the approach, ultimately proving that the homology of the Rook-Brauer algebras is indeed isomorphic to that of the symmetric groups in all degrees (see Theorem 7.1.5
[
5
]
). Furthermore, under the same assumption on
\delta
and
\epsilon
, they also proved that the homology of the Motzkin algebras vanishes in positive degrees (see Theorem 7.1.6
[
5
]
).

In this paper, we employ the technique of
inductive resolution
pioneered by Boyd, Hepworth and Patzt in
[
2
]
to prove the same results as those of Fisher and Graves for Rook-Brauer and Motzkin algebras while
only requiring the invertibility of
\epsilon
. More specifically, under the assumption that
\epsilon\in R
is invertible and for any
\delta\in R
where
R
is a unital commutative ring, we prove that the homology of the Rook-Brauer algebra is isomorphic to that of the symmetric group for all degrees and the homology of the Motzkin Algebras vanishes in positive degrees.

4.
Inductive Resolution

A key tool in many proofs of the homological stability of groups is Shapiro’s Lemma. However, when dealing with algebras that do not arise as group algebras, Shapiro’s Lemma does not always apply as the algebra
A
may fail to be flat over its subalgebras (see sect 4 of
[
1
]
and sect 1 of
[
3
]
). To address this issue, we will use
inductive resolution
prove an analogue (more general) version of Shapiro’s Lemma for the Rook-Brauer Algebra.

Simply put, the technique of inductive resolution provides a method for proving the vanishing of
\Tor^{A}_{\ast}(M,-)
of a given module by resolving it through modules that already possess this property- hence the term ”inductive” resolution. We formally state the principle of inductive resolution as introduced in
[
2
]
.

Theorem 4.1
.

(
[
2
]
, Theorem 3.1) Let
A
be an algebra over a ring
R
, and let
M
be a right
A
-module.
Suppose that
N
is a left
A
-module equipped with a resolution
Q_{\ast}\to N
with the following two properties:

•

\Tor^{A}_{\ast}(M,Q_{j})
vanishes in positive degrees for all
j\geqslant 0
.

•

M\otimes_{A}Q_{\ast}\to M\otimes_{A}N
is a resolution.

Then
\Tor^{A}_{\ast}(M,N)
vanishes in positive degrees.

For the rest of this section, we will use the technique of inductive resolution to prove
\Tor
of certain modules vanishes for positive degrees.

\mathbb{Y}_{P}
have node
y
connected to node
x
so these diagrams are also in
B_{X,x}
and
\mathbb{Y}_{P}\cap J_{X-\{x,y\}}
is sent into
B_{X,x}\cap J_{X-\{x\}}
. The map above is well-defined following from these facts.

To see that the map is surjective, any diagram in
B_{X,x}
has a right-to-right connection from node
x
to another node
y\in X-\{x\}
. Inside this connection, there might be more right-to-right connections which, taken all together, can be identified with a partition
P
of
[x,y]
(or
[y,x]
) into subsets of size at most 2 such that
\{x,y\}\in P
. Hence, the diagram is in the image of the direct summand
\mathbb{Y}_{P,\{x,y\}}
with the specified
y
and
P
above so the map is surjective.

To see that the map in injective, note that two diagrams from different direct summands
\mathbb{Y}_{P,\{x,y\}}
’s have to be distinct in
\mathcal{B}_{X,x}
. This is because for different
y
’s, node
x
of the two diagrams is connected to two different nodes so the diagram can’t be the same. When
y
’s are the same, different partitions
P
’s of
[x,y]
(or
[y,x]
) yields different right-to-right connections inside the right-to-right connection of node
x
and
y
and hence, two diagram also can’t be the same as well.

Therefore, to show that the map is injective, it suffices to show that for each direct summand
\mathbb{Y}_{P,\{x,y\}}
, the map
\mathbb{Y}_{P,\{x,y\}}\to\mathcal{B}_{X,x}
is injective. This is clear because in order for a diagram
\alpha
in
\mathbb{Y}_{P,\{x,y\}}
to be zero in
\mathcal{B}_{X,x}
, there must be at least a right-to-right connection or an isolated node among nodes of
\alpha
labeled by
X-\{x\}
. Since
\alpha
has node
x
connected to node
y
, this implies that the right-to-right connection or isolated node must occur among nodes of
\alpha
labeled by
X-\{x,y\}
. But this implies
\alpha=0
in
\mathbb{Y}_{P,\{x,y\}}
.
∎

We are now fully equipped to establish the analogue of Theorem
4.3
in the context of Motzkin algebras.

Theorem 4.17
.

For invertible
\epsilon\in R
and any
\delta\in R
, let
X\subseteq[n]
, then the groups
\Tor_{\ast}^{{\mathcal{M}}_{n}}(\mathbbm{1},{\mathcal{M}}_{n}/J_{X})
vanish in positive degrees.

Proof.

The proof of this is almost identical to that of Theorem
4.3
with the following changes:

•

Replace all
{\mathscr{R}}{\rm Br}_{n}
by
{\mathcal{M}}_{n}
.

•

Replace Proposition
4.8
by Proposition
4.15
.

•

\mathcal{A}_{X,x}
is a direct summand of
{\mathcal{M}}_{n}/J_{X-\{x\}}
by Lemma
4.13
.

•

By Lemma
4.16
,
\mathcal{B}_{X,\{x\}}
is a direct sum of
\mathbb{Y}_{P,\{x,y\}}
’s and by Lemma
4.12
,
\mathbb{Y}_{P,\{x,y\}}
is a direct summand of
{\mathcal{M}}_{n}/J_{X^{\prime}}
where
X^{\prime}\subseteq[n]
with
|X^{\prime}|<|X|
.

∎

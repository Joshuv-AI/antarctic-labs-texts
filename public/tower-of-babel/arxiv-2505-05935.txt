List-Recovery of Random Linear Codes over Small Fields
Dean Doron, Jonathan Mosheiff, Nicolas Resch, João Ribeiro

Abstract
We study list-recoverability of random linear codes over small fields, both from errors and from erasures. We consider codes of rate ε \varepsilon -close to capacity, and aim to bound the dependence of the output list size L L on ε \varepsilon , the input list size ℓ \ell , and the alphabet size q q . Prior to our work, the best upper bound was L = q O ⁡ ( ℓ / ε ) L=q^{O(\ell/\varepsilon)} (Zyablov and Pinsker, Prob. Per. Inf. 1981). Previous work has identified cases in which linear codes provably perform worse than non-linear codes with respect to list-recovery. While there exist non-linear codes that achieve L = O ⁡ ( ℓ / ε ) L=O(\ell/\varepsilon) , we know that L ≥ ℓ Ω ⁡ ( 1 / ε ) L\geq\ell^{\Omega(1/\varepsilon)} is necessary for list recovery from erasures over fields of small characteristic, and for list recovery from errors over large alphabets. We show that in other relevant regimes there is no significant price to pay for linearity, in the sense that we get the correct dependence on the gap-to-capacity ε \varepsilon and go beyond the Zyablov–Pinsker bound for the first time. Specifically, when q q is constant and ε \varepsilon approaches zero, • For list-recovery from erasures over prime fields , we show that L ≤ C 1 / ε L\leq C_{1}/\varepsilon . By prior work, such a result cannot be obtained for low-characteristic fields. • For list-recovery from errors over arbitrary fields , we prove that L ≤ C 2 / ε L\leq C_{2}/\varepsilon . Above, C 1 C_{1} and C 2 C_{2} depend on the decoding radius, input list size, and field size. We provide concrete bounds on the constants above, and the upper bounds on L L improve upon the Zyablov–Pinsker bound whenever q ≤ 2 ( 1 / ε ) c q\leq 2^{(1/\varepsilon)^{c}} for some small universal constant c > 0 c>0 .

Introduction

Error-correcting codes enable reliable communication over noisy channels by encoding messages
m\in\Sigma^{k}
as codewords
c\in\Sigma^{n}
. A code
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\Sigma^{n}
of rate
R=k/n
and minimum (relative) Hamming distance
\delta
allows for reliable communication over an adversarial noisy channel that corrupts up to a
\delta/2
-fraction of codeword symbols.
To tolerate more corruptions, one can relax unique decoding to
list decoding
, where the decoder outputs all codewords within a given Hamming radius.

This notion is further generalized by
list recovery (from errors)
, which models scenarios where the receiver gets a small list of possible values for each symbol. Formally, a code
\mathcal{C}
is said to be
(\rho,\ell,L)
-list-recoverable
if for every sequence of sets
T_{1},\dots,T_{n}\subseteq\Sigma
with
|T_{i}|\leq\ell
, we have

|\mathcal{C}\cap B_{\rho}(T_{1}\times\cdots\times T_{n})|\leq L,

where
B_{\rho}(T_{1}\times\cdots\times T_{n})
denotes the Hamming ball consisting of all words in
\Sigma^{n}
that agree with
T_{1},\dots,T_{n}
in at least
(1-\rho)n
coordinates. When
\ell=1
, list-recovery from errors reduces to standard list-decoding (from errors).

A related variant is
list recovery from erasures
, where some coordinates are entirely unknown, modeled by setting
T_{i}=\Sigma
. A code is said to be
(\alpha,\ell,L)
-list-recoverable from erasures
if

\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|\mathcal{C}\cap(T_{1}\times\dots\times T_{n})}}\right|\leq L

whenever
|T_{i}|\leq\ell
for at least
(1-\alpha)n
positions. Here too, the case
\ell=1
corresponds to list-decoding from erasures.

List recoverable codes are used as a building block for list-decodable and uniquely decodable codes
[
GI02
,
GI03
,
GI04
,
GI05
,
KMRS17
,
GKO+18
,
HRW20
]
. They have also gained a significant independent interest, in part due to their applications in pseudorandomness
[
TZ04
,
GUV09
,
DMOZ22
,
KT22
]
, algorithms (in particular, for heavy hitters, compressed
sensing, and combinatorial group testing
[

,
NPR11
,
LNNT16
,
GLPS17
,
DW22
]
), and cryptography
[
HIOS15
,
HLR21
]
.

For both list-recovery from errors and from erasures, there exists a well-defined
capacity threshold
that characterizes the maximal achievable rate for which bounded list-size decoding is possible. Specifically, given parameters
\rho
,
\ell
, and alphabet size
q
, there is a critical rate
R^{*}=R^{*}(\rho,\ell,q)
such that:

•

For every
\varepsilon>0
and any large enough block length, there exist codes of rate
R^{*}-\varepsilon
that are
(\rho,\ell,L)
-list-recoverable (from errors or erasures) with

L=O_{\ell,\varepsilon}(1).

(1)

•

For every
\varepsilon>0
and any large enough block length
n
, no code of rate
R^{*}+\varepsilon
is
(\rho,\ell,L)
-list-recoverable for

.

The exact threshold depends on the recovery model:

•

For
(\rho,\ell,L)
-list-recoverability from

, the threshold rate is

R^{*}_{\mathrm{errors}}=1-h_{q,\ell}(\rho),

where

h_{q,\ell}(\rho)=\rho\,\log_{q}\!\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{q-\ell}{\rho}}}\right)+(1-\rho)\,\log_{q}\!\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{\ell}{1-\rho}}}\right),

valid for

.

•

For
(\alpha,\ell,L)
-list-recoverability from

R^{*}_{\mathrm{erasures}}=(1-\alpha)\cdot(1-\log_{q}\ell).

The dependence of the list size

Using the probabilistic method, it is easy to show that plain random codes achieve
L=O(\ell/\varepsilon)
, and this dependence is often viewed as the optimal benchmark.
Codes achieving this tradeoff are said to match the
Elias bound for list-recovery
, in reference to the analogous threshold in list-decoding
[
Eli57
]
(see also
[
MRSY24
]
).

To set expectations for list-recovery of
linear
codes, we recall the classic argument of Zyablov and Pinsker
[

]
, adapted to the setting of list-recovery by Guruswami
[
Gur03
]
. Since any set of
L+1

\log_{q}(L+1)
, and since the events that linearly independent vectors lie in a linear code are stochastically independent, the argument for plain random codes gives
L=q^{O(\ell/\varepsilon)}
.
This naturally raises the question: what is the actual
price of linearity
in list-recovery? While various forms of degradation are possible, we seek to understand how the requirement of linearity affects the list-size achievable near capacity.

Prior work

Most previous results concerning the output list size have focused on the
large alphabet regime
, where
q
is at least exponential in
1/\varepsilon
. In this setting, Li and Shagrithaya
[
LS25
]
recently proved that random linear codes are almost surely list-recoverable with list size
L\leq\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\ell/\varepsilon}}\right)^{O\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\ell/\varepsilon}}\right)}
. By a reduction between code ensembles
[
LMS24
]
, the same upper bound also holds with high probability for Reed–Solomon codes over random evaluation sets. On the other hand,
[
LS25
]
proved that all
(\rho,\ell,L)
-list-recoverable (from errors) linear codes over a large alphabet must satisfy
L\geq\ell^{\Omega(R/\varepsilon)}
, implying an exponential gap in the list-size between linear codes and plain random codes. A similar negative result was previously proven by Chen and Zhang
[
CZ24
]
for Reed-Solomon codes and folded Reed–Solomon codes. Recently, Komech and Mosheiff
[
KM25
]
constructed a new ensemble of non-linear codes that achieve
L\leq O(\ell/\varepsilon)
in list-recovery from errors. These are the only codes other than plain random codes known to achieve the list-recovery Elias bound.

Older works of Rudra and Wootters
[
RW14
,
RW18
]
, incomparable to
[
LS25
]
, show (in our terms) that random linear codes achieve list-size
L\leq\ell^{\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\cdot\log^{2}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\ell/\varepsilon}}\right)}
for list-recovery (from errors) in the large alphabet regime, but only under the guarantee that
\rho=1-\Omega(\varepsilon)
.

Other works concern the list-recoverability of folded Reed–Solomon codes, multiplicity codes, tensor codes, and variants of them (e.g.,
[
KRSW18
,
HRW20
,
Tam24
]
). In particular, Tamo
[
Tam24
]
shows that folded Reed–Solomon codes and multiplicity codes achieve list size
L\leq\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\ell/\varepsilon}}\right)^{O\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{1+\log\ell}{\varepsilon}}}\right)}
(in the errors case) in the large alphabet regime.

In contrast, very little is known about list-recovery in the
small alphabet regime
, where
q
is sub-exponential in
1/\varepsilon
. To the best of our knowledge, the only positive result in this setting is the aforementioned
L=q^{O(\ell/\varepsilon)}
for random linear codes
[
ZP81
]
. On the other hand, we know that when
q
is a power of a small prime, random linear codes in this regime are very unlikely to be
(\alpha,\ell,L)
-list-recoverable from erasures with
\ell^{o(1/\varepsilon)}

[
GLM+22
]
(we state this as
Theorem

1.1

Provable separation between linear and nonlinear codes were also established in the setting of list decoding from erasures.
For the special case of
\mathds{F}_{2}
, and aiming for an erasure decoding radius of
\alpha=1-\varepsilon
, Guruswami
[
Gur03
]
showed that the output list size must satisfy
L=\Omega(1/\varepsilon)
, as long as the rate is sufficiently non-vanishing. In contrast, plain random codes achieve
L=O(\log(1/\varepsilon))
, and we even have explicit constructions that nearly match plain random codes in some parameter regimes
[
BDT20
]
.

1.1
Our Contribution

We establish new upper bounds on the output list size for list-recovery of random linear codes near capacity in the small alphabet regime. To the best of our knowledge, these are the only known bounds for general list-recovery in this setting beyond the classical Zyablov–Pinsker argument. Notably, our results achieve list sizes with only linear dependence on
1/\varepsilon
, in contrast to the exponential dependence in previous bounds.

List-recovery from erasures over prime fields.

For our first result, we consider
(\alpha,\ell,L)
-list-recovery from erasures.
Recall that the capacity in this case is

R^{*}_{\mathrm{erasures}}(\alpha,\ell,q)=(1-\alpha)(1-\log_{q}\ell).

As mentioned before,
[
GLM+22
]
showed that there is a price to pay for linearity over fields of small characteristic.
More precisely, they proved the following.

Theorem 1.1

(informal; see
[
GLM+22
, Theorem III.1]
)
.

If
\ell
divides
\mathrm{char}(\mathds{F}_{q})
, then with high probability over the choice of the linear code, the output list size
L
cannot be taken smaller than
\ell^{\Omega(1/\varepsilon)}
.

We show that this limitation disappears when considering a
prime
field size
q
.
In this case, we can make the output list size
O_{\alpha,\ell,q}(1/\varepsilon)
.

Theorem 1.2

(list recovery from erasures over prime fields; see
Theorem

3.5
)
.

Given
1\leq\ell\leq q
with
q
prime and
\alpha\in[0,1)
, there exists
C(\alpha,\ell,q)>0
such that the following holds. Let
\mathcal{C}\leq\mathds{F}_{q}^{n}
be a random linear code of rate
R^{*}_{\mathrm{erasures}}(\alpha,\ell,q)-\varepsilon
for some
\varepsilon>0
. Then,
\mathcal{C}
is with high probability
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\alpha,\ell,\frac{C(\alpha,\ell,q)}{\varepsilon}}}\right)
-list-recoverable from erasures.

While our focus is on the setting where
\alpha
and
q
are constants, we determine effective bounds on
C(\alpha,\ell,q)
even when
\alpha
is a function of
n
, and
q
is slightly super-constant.
1
1

1

For a concrete example, when
\ell\leq(1-\gamma)q
for some constant
\gamma
, and
\alpha
is bounded away from
1
, we can take
C(\alpha,\ell,q)\leq q^{O(\ell\cdot\log q)}
as long as
n\geq C(\alpha,\ell,q)
. We refer the reader to
Theorem

3.5
for the precise bound.

List-recovery from errors.

Next, we consider the case of list-recovery from errors, where we recall the capacity is

R^{*}_{\mathrm{errors}}(\rho,\ell,q)=1-h_{q,\ell}(\rho).

Here, contrary to what happens in the regime of large

Theorem 1.3

(list recovery from errors; see

4.7

.

Given

\rho\in(0,1-\ell/q)

\mathcal{C}\leq\mathds{F}_{q}^{n}

\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\rho,\ell,\frac{C(\rho,\ell,q)}{\varepsilon}}}\right)

Again, we determine effective bounds on the numerator

4.7
.

Specifically, when

We conclude this section with some remarks.

Remark 1.4

(on the field insensitivity)
.

Both of our results show that there is no significant price to pay for linearity in list-recovery for certain parameter regimes.
Our result in
Theorem

1.3
on list-recovery from errors is insensitive to the field size.
On the other hand, as discussed above, our result in
Theorem

1.2
on list-recovery from erasures is (necessarily!) field sensitive.
We provide some informal discussion on why this happens.
First, note that we work with rates close to capacity, and the capacity in the erasures setting is larger for comparable
\alpha
and
\rho
.
Second, looking ahead (see
Section

1.2
for more details), list-recovery from erasures depends on the “additive structure” of
T_{1}\times\cdots\times T_{n}
, for arbitrary size-
\ell
subsets
T_{1},\dots,T_{n}\subseteq\mathds{F}_{q}
.
If the
T_{i}
’s are
subspaces
of
\mathds{F}_{q}
, then it is quite likely these form a bad configuration for list-recovery from erasures.
In contrast, list-recovery from errors depends on the additive structure of the “puffed-up” combinatorial rectangles

B_{\rho}(T_{1}\times T_{2}\times\cdots\times T_{n})=\{x\in\mathds{F}_{q}^{n}:x_{i}\notin T_{i}\text{ for at most }\rho n\text{ choices of }i\in[n]\}.

Even if the
T_{i}
’s are subspaces of
\mathds{F}_{q}
, the “puffing up” operation kills any additive structure that could lead to a bad list-recovery configuration.

Remark 1.5

(on the dependence of
L
on the various parameters)
.

Recall that a code which is
\varepsilon
-close to capacity is said to achieve the Elias bound if
L=O(\ell/\varepsilon)
. Note that in both
Theorem

1.2
and
Theorem

1.3
, the dependence of
L
on
\varepsilon
is as we would hope. However, the dependence on the other parameters (particularly
\ell
and
q
) is exponentially worse than the
O(\ell)
dependence. We leave it as a natural open problem to improve on this dependency.

Remark 1.6

(comparison to
[
ZP81
]
)
.

As mentioned earlier, we are not aware of any prior arguments establishing non-trivial bounds on the list-size

for codes
\varepsilon
-close to capacity which do not require

[
ZP81
]
(given formally in
[

). Recall that this method guarantees list size

. In comparison, we obtain (roughly)
L=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\cdot q^{O(\ell\log q)}
in the case of erasures (over prime fields), and

Thus, compared to
[

, we obtain a smaller bound on

In particular, when

1.2
Technical Overview

At a conceptual level, our work reconsiders the approach of Guruswami, Håstad, and Kopparty
[
GHK11
]
, which allowed for an understanding of the list-decodability from errors of random linear codes over constant-sized alphabets, and adapts it to the case of list-recovery (either from erasures or errors). Recall that list-decoding from errors is the special case of list-recovery from errors with
\ell=1
.

Using terminology that we define in this work, the first step in the argument of
[
GHK11
]
is to argue that Hamming balls are nontrivially
mixing
. Specifically, fix any center
z\in\mathds{F}_{q}^{n}
and consider sampling twice uniformly and independently from the Hamming ball of radius
\rho
centered at
z
; denote the two samples by
X
and
X^{\prime}
. The authors argue that for some
\delta>0
and any
\alpha,\beta\in\mathds{F}_{q}\setminus\{0\}
, it follows that for any other center
y\in\mathds{F}_{q}^{n}
,
\Pr[\alpha X+\beta X^{\prime}\in B_{\rho}(y)]\leq q^{-\delta n}
for some
\delta=\delta(\rho,q)>0
.
3
3

3

In fact for
[
GHK11
]
it sufficed to only consider the
z=0
case, in which case one can always assume
\alpha=\beta=1
. But their argument naturally generalizes to this case, and this is the notion of mixing that we require for our list-recovery results.
Alternatively, we could say that for any
y\in\mathds{F}_{q}^{n}
, we have
\Pr[\alpha X+\beta X^{\prime}\in y+B_{\rho}(z)]\leq q^{-\delta n}
. From here, using some additional tools like
2-increasing chains
– whose existence they establish via a Ramsey-theoretic argument – they are able to show that random linear codes with
\varepsilon
gap-to-capacity are with high probability
(\rho,\frac{C(\rho,q)}{\varepsilon})
-list-decodable from errors. In particular, their techniques promise the correct dependence on
\varepsilon
(although the dependence on the parameters
q
and
\rho
is quite poor).

We recommend viewing this “
\delta
-mixing” property in the following light. Any argument establishing that random linear codes have good list-decodability must somehow argue that random subspaces and Hamming balls don’t tend to “correlate” too much. In particular, it should not be the case that Hamming balls have noticeable “linear structure”, and in particular they should be “far” from being closed under addition.

In our work, we consider whether or not sets that are relevant for list-recovery, i.e., the sorts of sets that list-recoverable codes cannot intersect with too much, also have nontrivial mixing.
Firstly, we crystallize in a definition what it means for any arbitrary set
T\subseteq\mathds{F}_{q}^{n}
to be
\delta
-mixing
, where
\delta>0
: for any
\alpha,\beta\in\mathds{F}_{q}\setminus\{0\}
and
y\in\mathds{F}_{q}^{n}
, if
X,X^{\prime}\sim T
– which denotes that
X
and
X^{\prime}
are sampled independently and uniformly from
T
– then
\Pr[\alpha X+\beta X^{\prime}\in y+T]\leq q^{-\delta n}
.

Now, for
(\alpha,\ell,L)
-list-recovery from erasures the relevant sets are combinatorial rectangles
T_{1}\times T_{2}\times\cdots\times T_{n}
where for at least
(1-\alpha)n
values of
i\in[n]
we have
|T_{i}|\leq\ell
. For
(\rho,\ell,L)
-list-recovery from errors the sets are “puffed-up” combinatorial rectangles. Namely, for
T_{1},T_{2},\dots,T_{n}\subseteq\mathds{F}_{q}
each with
|T_{i}|\leq\ell
, we consider
list-recovery balls

B_{\rho}(T_{1}\times T_{2}\times\cdots\times T_{n})=\{x\in\mathds{F}_{q}^{n}:x_{i}\notin T_{i}\text{ for at most }\rho n\text{ choices of }i\in[n]\}.

Following the argument of
[
GHK11
]
, once we establish that these sets are nontrivially mixing, we can obtain bounds on the list-size with the correct dependence on
\varepsilon
. Our task then boils down to understanding the mixingness of the sets relevant for list-recovery. We consider first the erasures case, and subsequently discuss the errors case.

List-recovery from erasures

Firstly, observe that for a combinatorial rectangle
T_{1}\times\cdots\times T_{n}
, if each of the sets
T_{i}
is nontrivially mixing as a subset of
\mathds{F}_{q}
(take the
n=1

T_{1}\times\cdots\times T_{n}
is also nontrivially mixing (as a subset of
\mathds{F}_{q}^{n}
). Hence, it suffices for us to consider whether or not subsets of
\mathds{F}_{q}
mix. It is here that the dependence on the field size shows up.

Recall from the earlier discussion that
[
GLM+22
]
established that random linear codes over
\mathds{F}_{\ell^{t}}
(where
\ell

not

(\alpha,\ell,L)
-list-recoverable from erasures unless

. Indeed, it is easy to find a subset

which is

\Pr[X+X^{\prime}\in T]=1

not

\delta

[

]

where at least a

-subspaces of
\mathds{F}_{\ell^{t}}
.

If we insist that

\mathds{F}_{q}

does not

However, in this case

still contains some subsets with “additive structure;” for example, taking

to be odd here for simplicity, centered intervals
4
4

4

Or, more generally, arithmetic progressions.

are nontrivially mixing, as desired.

List-recovery from errors

We now wish to establish that list-recovery balls are nontrivially mixing. Notably, unlike in the case of erasures, the argument here is
insensitive
to the base field.
Let
T=T_{1}\times\cdots\times T_{n}
, where each
|T_{i}|\leq\ell
. Let
X,X^{\prime}\sim B_{\rho}(T)
; in this overview, we will sketch how one bounds
\Pr[X+X^{\prime}\in B_{\rho}(T)]
(the argument easily generalizes to allow for multipliers
\alpha,\beta\in\mathds{F}_{q}\setminus\{0\}
and a shift
y\in\mathds{F}_{q}^{n}
).

Unlike in the case of combinatorial rectangles, it is
not
the case that
X=(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})
and
X^{\prime}=(X_{1}^{\prime},\dots,X_{n}^{\prime})
have independent coordinates. For example, conditioned on
X_{1}
lying in
T_{1}
, then
X_{2}
is less likely to lie in
T_{2}
. However, these correlations are relatively minor, and we can essentially “pretend” that both
X
and
X^{\prime}
are sampled as follows: for each
i\in[n]
, with probability
1-\rho
set the
i
-th coordinate to a uniformly random element of
T_{i}
, and otherwise set it to a uniformly random element of
\mathds{F}_{q}\setminus T_{i}
, and these choices are made independently for each
i\in[n]
.
5
5

5

In fact for technical reasons we have to consider
X_{i}
lying in
T_{i}
with probability
\omega
for
\omega\leq\rho
, and similarly
X_{i}^{\prime}
lies in
T_{i}
with probability
\omega^{\prime}\leq\rho
. But by a concentration argument one can easily establish that
\omega
and
\omega^{\prime}
are with high probability very close to
\rho
.
We remark that a similar trick is implicit in
[
GHK11
]
, and made explicit in the context of rank-metric codes by Guruswami and Resch
[
GR18
]
.

This new distribution is much more amenable to analysis. In particular, letting
E_{i}
be the indicator for the event
X_{i}+X_{i}^{\prime}\in T_{i}
, then
X+X^{\prime}\in B_{\rho}(T)
iff
\sum_{i}E_{i}\geq(1-\rho)n
. Thus, if we can argue
\Pr[E_{i}=1]<1-\rho
then a classic Chernoff-Hoeffding bound establishes
\Pr\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\sum_{i}E_{i}\geq(1-\rho)n}}\right]
is exponentially small, implying the desired
\delta
-mixing.

Bounding
\Pr[E_{i}=1]
is the most novel part of the analysis, and is done in
Lemma

4.2
. Recall that
X_{i},X_{i}^{\prime}\sim T_{i}
, and let also
Y_{i},Y_{i}^{\prime}\sim\mathds{F}_{q}\setminus T_{i}
. We have

\displaystyle\Pr

\displaystyle[E_{i}=1]=(1-\rho)^{2}\Pr[X_{i}+X_{i}^{\prime}\in T_{i}]+2\rho(1-\rho)\Pr[X_{i}+Y_{i}^{\prime}\in T_{i}]+\rho^{2}\Pr[Y_{i}+Y_{i}^{\prime}\in T_{i}]

\displaystyle=\sum_{z_{i}\in T_{i}}(1-\rho)^{2}\Pr[X_{i}+X_{i}^{\prime}=z_{i}]+2\rho(1-\rho)\Pr[X_{i}+Y_{i}^{\prime}=z_{i}]+\rho^{2}\Pr[Y_{i}+Y_{i}^{\prime}=z_{i}]\ .

(2)

As is standard,
\Pr[X_{i}+X_{i}^{\prime}=z_{i}]
is proportional to
1_{T_{i}}*1_{T_{i}}(z_{i})
, the convolution of the indicator functions for
T_{i}
, and similarly
\Pr[X_{i}+Y_{i}^{\prime}=z_{i}]
and
\Pr[Y_{i}+Y_{i}^{\prime}=z_{i}]
are proportional to
1_{T_{i}}*1_{\mathds{F}_{q}\setminus T_{i}}(z)
and
1_{\mathds{F}_{q}\setminus T_{i}}*1_{\mathds{F}_{q}\setminus T_{i}}(z)
, respectively. Using the simple identity
1_{\mathds{F}_{q}\setminus T_{i}}=1-1_{T_{i}}
, we can rewrite
Equation

2
as

c\cdot 1_{T_{i}}*1_{T_{i}}(z)+\text{other terms}\ ,

where
c
is a constant which we show to be positive assuming
\rho\in(0,1-\ell/q)
(and indeed, if
\rho>1-\ell/q
then it could be negative). Upon giving a trivial upper bound
1_{T_{i}}*1_{T_{i}}(z_{i})
(which corresponds to the case that
T_{i}
does not mix at all, as we must do since we are making no assumptions on the field) and simplifying, we obtain the bound

\Pr[E_{i}=1]\leq(1-\rho)^{2}+\rho^{2}\cdot\frac{\ell}{q-\ell}.

To our satisfaction, we have that
(1-\rho)^{2}+\rho^{2}\cdot\frac{\ell}{q-\ell}<1-\rho
iff
\rho\in(0,1-\ell/q)
, which is precisely the range of decoding radius at which we can hope for positive rate list-recovery from errors in the first place! Thus, we have that
\Pr\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\sum_{i}E_{i}\geq(1-\rho)n}}\right]
is exponentially small, establishing that list-recovery balls nontrivially mix.

Open Problems

Lastly, we leave here some directions for future research:

•

In our results the dependency on
\varepsilon
is correct, but the dependency on
\ell
and
q
is rather poor. Can we improve this dependency? Or, can we perhaps prove new lower bounds on
L
in terms of
\ell
and

that apply when these parameters are not too big?

•

[

]
showed that over

(\alpha,\ell,L)

Can we show that this lower bound actually applies to
every

q
-ary linear code?

•

Many arguments for codes being list-recoverable from errors in fact establish the stronger property of
average-radius
-list-recovery, where now one instead shows that for any input lists
T_{1},\dots,T_{n}\subseteq\mathds{F}_{q}

2.3
List-Recovery Notions

This section collects the basic notions of list-recovery we study.

List-recovery from erasures.

We begin with the relevant definition.

Definition 2.2

(list recovery from erasures)
.

Let
\mathcal{C}\subseteq[q]^{n}
be a
q
-ary code of block-length
n
. For an erasure radius
\alpha\in[0,1)
and input list size
1\leq\ell\leq q
, we say that
\mathcal{C}
is

(\alpha,\ell,L)
-list-recoverable from erasures
if for every
T_{1},\ldots,T_{n}\subseteq[q]
such that
|T_{i}|\leq\ell
for at least
(1-\alpha)n
of the
i

T_{i}=[q]
for the remaining, it holds that

|\mathcal{C}\cap(T_{1}\times\cdots\times T_{n})|\leq L.

That is, in any combinatorial rectangle of which at least
(1-\alpha)n
of its side-lengths are at most
\ell
(and the remainder can be as large as
q
), there are at most
L
codewords.

We will also consider list-recovery from errors. The concept of a list-recovery ball – which generalizes that of a Hamming ball – will be useful.

Definition 2.3

(list-recovery ball)
.

Let
q\in\mathbb{N}
and let
T_{1},\dots,T_{n}\subseteq[q]
. The
list-recovery ball of radius
\rho
centered at
T_{1}\times\cdots\times T_{n}
is

B_{\rho}(T_{1}\times\cdots\times T_{n})=\{x\in[q]^{n}:d(x,T_{1}\times\cdots\times T_{n})\leq\rho n\}.

Above, we have extended the Hamming metric by setting

d(x,T_{1}\times\cdots\times T_{n})=|\{i\in[n]:x_{i}\notin T_{i}\}|.

We now state the relevant capacity theorem for list-recovery from erasures. The proofs of the two implications are standard: the
possibility
result follows from analyzing the performance of a plain random code, while the
impossibility
result follows from a counting argument.

Theorem 2.4

(list-recovery from erasures capacity)
.

Let
1\leq\ell\leq q
and let
\alpha\in[0,1)
. Fix
\varepsilon>0
. For
n\in\mathbb{N}
large enough, the following hold:

•

There exists a code
\mathcal{C}\subseteq[q]^{n}
of rate
1-(1-\alpha)\log_{q}\ell-\alpha-\varepsilon
which is
(\alpha,\ell,\lceil\ell/\varepsilon\rceil)
-list-recoverable from erasures.

•

For any code
\mathcal{C}\subseteq[q]^{n}
of rate
1-(1-\alpha)\log_{q}\ell-\alpha+\varepsilon
, there exist
T_{1},\dots,T_{n}\subseteq[q]
with
|T_{i}|\leq\ell
for at least
(1-\alpha)n
values of
i\in[n]
such that
|\mathcal{C}\cap(T_{1}\times\cdots\times T_{n})|\geq q^{\varepsilon n-o(n)}
.

We therefore say that the
capacity
for
(\alpha,\ell,L)
-list-recovery from erasures is
1-\alpha-(1-\alpha)\log_{q}\ell
. We will study what happens for codes of rate
1-(1-\alpha)\log_{q}\ell-\alpha-\varepsilon
for some
\varepsilon>0
, and determine the value of their output list-size
L
. We will be focused on the case where
q
and is held constant, and the gap-to-capacity
\varepsilon
tends to
0
.

List-recovery from errors.

We now define what it means for a code to be list-recoverable from errors.

Definition 2.5

(list recovery from errors)
.

Let
\mathcal{C}\subseteq[q]^{n}
be a
q
-ary code of block-length
n
. For
a decoding radius
\rho\in(0,1-\ell/q)
and
input list size
1\leq\ell\leq q
, we say that
\mathcal{C}
is
(\rho,\ell,L)
-list-recoverable from errors
if for every
T_{1},\ldots,T_{n}\subseteq[q]
such that
|T_{i}|\leq\ell
for all
i\in[n]
, it holds that

|\mathcal{C}\cap B_{\rho}(T_{1}\times\cdots\times T_{n})|\leq L.

That is, every list-recovery ball of radius
\rho
with side-lengths
\leq\ell
contains at most
L
codewords.

We will need an estimate on the size of list-recovery balls. It makes use of the
(q,\ell)
-entropy function
, defined as follows:

\displaystyle h_{q,\ell}(x)=x\log_{q}\frac{q-\ell}{x}+(1-x)\log_{q}\frac{q-\ell}{1-x}.

(3)

An operational interpretation of this quantity is as the base-
q
entropy of a random variable which, with probability
1-x
, samples a uniformly random element from a set of size
\ell
, and with probability
x
samples a uniformly random element from the complement. Additionally, so long as

it holds that
0<h_{q,\ell}(x)<1
. Note that if
\ell=1
one recovers the
q
-entropy function, which we denote as
h_{q}
(i.e., if
\ell
is omitted from the subscript, then it is by default

).

We now state the relevant estimate.

Proposition 2.6

(
[
Res20
, Proposition 2.4.11]
)
.

Let
1\leq\ell\leq q
be integers and
\rho\in(0,1-\ell/q)
. Let
T_{1},\dots,T_{n}\subseteq[q]
with

\frac{q^{nh_{q,\ell}(\rho)}}{\sqrt{2n}}\leq|B_{\rho}(T_{1}\times\cdots\times T_{n})|\leq q^{nh_{q,\ell}(\rho)}.

Remark 2.7
.

In
[
Res20
]
the lower bound is just given as

. Using an estimate of Wozencraft and Reiffen
[

]
(which we use below in the proof of

.

This estimate drives the following capacity theorem.

Theorem 2.8

(list-recovery from errors capacity)
.

Let
1\leq\ell\leq q
and let
\rho\in(0,1-\ell/q)
. Fix
\varepsilon>0
. For
n\in\mathbb{N}
large enough, the following hold:

•

There exists a code
\mathcal{C}\subseteq[q]^{n}
of rate

which is
(\rho,\ell,\lceil\ell/\varepsilon\rceil)
-list-recoverable from errors.

•

For any code
\mathcal{C}\subseteq[q]^{n}
of rate

T_{1},\dots,T_{n}\subseteq[q]
with
|T_{i}|\leq\ell
for all

such that
|\mathcal{C}\cap B_{\rho}(T_{1}\times\cdots\times T_{n})|\geq q^{\varepsilon n-o(n)}
.

Thus, we will concern ourselves with codes of rate

, and determine the output list size

for
(\rho,\ell,L)
-list-recovery from errors. And, as in the list-recovery from erasures case, we will hold
\rho,\ell,q

as the gap-to-capacity

.

We will also need the following lower bound on the difference
h_{q,\ell}(\rho)-h_{q,\ell}(\rho-\eta)
.

Claim 2.9
.

For any integers
q>0
and
1\leq\ell\leq q
, any
\rho\in(0,1-\ell/q]
, and any
\eta\in[0,\rho]
, we have

h_{q,\ell}(\rho)-h_{q,\ell}(\rho-\eta)\geq\eta\log_{q}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{(q-\ell)(1-\rho)}{\ell\cdot\rho}}}\right)\geq 0.

Proof.

We can rewrite

h_{q,\ell}(\rho)-h_{q,\ell}(\rho-\eta)=\frac{h_{2}(\rho)-h_{2}(\rho-\eta)}{\log q}+\eta\log_{q}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{q-\ell}{\ell}}}\right).

Now, the desired lower bound follows if we show that

h_{2}(\rho)-h_{2}(\rho-\eta)\geq\eta\log\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{1-\rho}{\rho}}}\right)

for all
\rho\in[0,1]
and
\eta\in[0,\rho]
.
To see this, first note that
h_{2}^{\prime}(\rho)=\log\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{1-\rho}{\rho}}}\right)
.
Since
h_{2}
is concave in
[0,1]
, we have
h_{2}(\rho)-h_{2}(\rho-\eta)\geq\eta\cdot h^{\prime}_{2}(\rho)
, as desired.

Finally, the rightmost lower bound “
\geq 0

\frac{(q-\ell)(1-\rho)}{\ell\cdot\rho}\geq 1
if and only if
\rho\leq 1-\ell/q

∎

2.4
Some Distributions and Concentration Bounds

For a given finite set
S
in some universe
U
, we write
X\sim S
to denote that
X
is distributed uniformly over the set
S
.
For
p\in[0,1]
, we denote by

the distribution of a Bernoulli random variable, which equals

We now provide a tail bound on

random variables. It makes use of the concept of a Kullback–Leibler divergence between two Bernoulli random variables. Specifically, for

D(x\|y)=x\ln\frac{x}{y}+(1-x)\ln\frac{1-x}{1-y}.

Proposition 2.10
.

Let
X\sim\mathrm{Bin}(n,p)
with
0<p<1
, and let
p<p^{\prime}<1
. Then

\Pr[X\geq p^{\prime}n]\leq e^{-nD(p^{\prime}\|p)}.

As a very simple corollary, we note that if we define for

D_{q}(x\|y)=x\log_{q}\frac{x}{y}+(1-x)\log_{q}\frac{1-x}{1-y},

then the following holds:

Corollary 2.11
.

Let
X\sim\mathrm{Bin}(n,p)
with
0<p<1
, and let
p<p^{\prime}<1
. Then,

\Pr[X\geq p^{\prime}n]\leq q^{-nD_{q}(p^{\prime}\|p)}.

We will make use of the following properties of KL divergence.

Claim 2.12
.

Suppose
1>p_{1}>p_{2}>p_{3}>0
. Then,

D_{q}(p_{1}\|p_{3})>D_{q}(p_{1}\|p_{2})>0.

Claim 2.13
.

Let
p\in(0,1)
and
0\leq\Delta<p
. Then,

D(p\|p-\Delta)\geq 2\Delta^{2}.

Additionally, we need to lower bound the probability that a binomial random variable hits exactly its expectation.

Proposition 2.14
.

Let
X\sim\mathrm{Bin}(n,p)
with

such that

Then,

\Pr[X=pn]\geq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2n}}.

Proof.

First, note that

\Pr[X=pn]=\binom{n}{pn}p^{pn}(1-p)^{(1-p)n}=\binom{n}{pn}\cdot 2^{-nh_{2}(p)},

The desired statement follows by combining this observation with a bound of Wozencraft and Reiffen
[
WR61
]
(see
[
CT06
, Lemma 17.5.1]
), which in particular gives that

\binom{n}{pn}\cdot 2^{-nh_{2}(p)}\geq\frac{1}{\sqrt{8p(1-p)n}}\geq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2n}}.\qed

Lemma 3.2
.

Fix a prime

. Let

be arbitrary sets of size

Then,

\Pr_{X_{1}\sim T_{1},X_{2}\sim T_{2}}[X_{1}+X_{2}\in T_{3}]\leq\begin{cases}\frac{3}{4}+\frac{1}{4\ell^{2}}+\frac{\max(0,3\ell-2q-1)\cdot(3\ell-2q+1)}{4\ell^{2}},&\textrm{ if $\ell$ is odd,}\\

In particular:

1.

When

\Pr_{X_{1}\sim T_{1},X_{2}\sim T_{2}}[X_{1}+X_{2}\in T_{3}]\leq\begin{cases}\frac{3}{4}+\frac{1}{4\ell^{2}},&\textrm{ if $\ell$ is odd,}\\
\frac{3}{4},&\textrm{ if $\ell$ is even.}\end{cases}

2.

When

we have

\Pr_{X_{1}\sim T_{1},X_{2}\sim T_{2}}[X_{1}+X_{2}\in T_{3}]\leq\frac{q^{2}-3\ell(q-\ell)}{\ell^{2}}.

Proof.

By

3.1
, we have

\Pr_{X_{1}\sim T_{1},X_{2}\sim T_{2}}[X_{1}+X_{2}\in T_{3}]\leq\Pr_{\widetilde{X}_{1}\sim\widetilde{T}_{1},\widetilde{X}_{2}\sim\widetilde{T}_{2}}[\widetilde{X}_{1}+\widetilde{X}_{2}\in\widetilde{T}_{3}],

where
\widetilde{T}_{1},\widetilde{T}_{2},\widetilde{T}_{3}
are (possibly different) centered intervals associated with

We consider two cases depending on the parity of

If

\displaystyle\Pr_{X_{1}\sim T_{1},X_{2}\sim T_{2}}[X_{1}+X_{2}\in T_{3}]

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=-\alpha}^{\alpha}\sum_{j=-\alpha}^{\alpha}\frac{1}{\ell^{2}}\cdot 1_{\{i+j\ (\mathrm{mod}\ q)\in[-\alpha,\alpha]\}},

where we interpret the mod

The term inside the double sum is
1

By symmetry we can focus on

\displaystyle\Pr_{X_{1}\sim T_{1},X_{2}\sim T_{2}}[X_{1}+X_{2}\in T_{3}]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\ell^{2}}\sum_{i=-\alpha}^{\alpha-1}\sum_{j=-\alpha+1}^{\alpha}1_{\{i+j\ (\mathrm{mod}\ q)\in[-\alpha+1,\alpha]\}},

where, as before, we intepret the mod

•

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
T\subseteq\mathds{F}_{q}^{n}

\delta
-mixing. For

\Pr\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|\operatorname{Span}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(X^{(1)},\ldots,X^{(b)}}}\right)\cap T}}\right|>C\cdot b}}\right]\leq q^{-an},

C=C(q,\delta,a)=q^{\frac{8a}{\delta}}
.
In particular, when

\delta_{i}

\delta=\mathds{E}_{i}[\delta_{i}]

implies that there exists some set

X_{v}\triangleq\sum_{i\in[b]}v_{i}X^{(i)}\in T

.
6
6

6

Notice that if the
X_{i}

-s.
Hence, it suffices to bound the probability that such a set

Fix some

Applying

2.16
with

\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|\operatorname{Supp}(v^{(j)})\setminus\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\bigcup_{i=1}^{j-1}\operatorname{Supp}(v^{(i)})}}\right)}}\right|\geq 2.

Now, we can bound

\displaystyle\Pr[\forall v\in S,X_{v}\in T]

\displaystyle\leq\Pr[\forall j\in[d],X_{v^{(j)}}\in T]

\displaystyle=\Pr[\forall j\in[d],X_{v^{(j)}}+X_{w}\in T+X_{w}]

\displaystyle=\Pr[\forall j\in[d],X_{v^{(j)}+w}\in T+X_{w}].

(4)

Next, we bound

\Pr[X_{v^{(j)}+w}\in T+y~|~\forall i\in[j-1],X_{v^{(i)}+w}\in T+y]=\mathds{E}_{z\sim Y_{\mathsf{past}}^{(j)}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\mathbf{1}(z)\cdot\Pr[Y_{\mathsf{new}}^{(j)}\in T+y_{z}]}}\right],

Theorem 3.5

(list recovery with erasures)

, a prime

•

When

, and,

•

When

for some

.

4.1
Mixture of List-Recovery Balls

In this section we make use of the

, defined as

(f*g)(x)=\mathop{\mathds{E}}_{y\in\mathds{F}_{q}}[f(y)g(x-y)]\ .

We recall that it is

f*(g+h)=f*g+f*h
for any functions

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
q
be a prime power. Suppose

are sampled independently and uniformly from

, respectively. Then, for any

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle\Pr[X+Y=z]

\displaystyle=\sum_{x\in A}\Pr[X=x]\Pr[X+Y=z|X=x]=\sum_{x\in A}\frac{1}{|A|}\cdot\Pr[Y=z-x]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|A|}\sum_{x\in\mathds{F}_{q}}1_{A}(x)\cdot 1_{B}(z-x)\frac{1}{|B|}=\frac{q}{|A|\cdot|B|}\mathop{\mathds{E}}_{x\in\mathds{F}_{q}}[1_{A}(x)\cdot 1_{B}(z-x)]

\displaystyle=\frac{q}{|A|\cdot|B|}1_{A}*1_{B}(z).\qed

to

Lemma 4.3
.

Suppose
q
is a prime power,
1\leq\ell\leq q
is an integer and
\rho\in(0,1-\ell/q)
. Suppose

\rho-\eta\leq\omega_{1},\omega_{2}\leq\rho

\displaystyle 1-\rho

\displaystyle>(1-\rho)^{2}+\rho^{2}\cdot\frac{\ell}{q-\ell},

(14)

\displaystyle(1-\rho+\eta)^{2}

\displaystyle+(\rho-\eta)^{2}\cdot\frac{\ell}{q-\ell}>(1-\omega_{1})(1-\omega_{2})+\omega_{1}\omega_{2}\cdot\frac{\ell}{q-\ell}.

(15)

Proof.

For

14
, observe that the quadratic

(1-\rho)=(1-\rho)^{2}+\rho^{2}\cdot\frac{\ell}{q-\ell},

has solutions
\rho=0
and
\rho=1-\ell/q
, from which it follows that

\displaystyle 1-\rho>(1-\rho)^{2}+\rho^{2}\cdot\frac{\ell}{q-\ell}~~~\forall~0<\rho<1-\frac{\ell}{q},

(16)

as claimed.

We now consider

g(\omega_{1},\omega_{2})=(1-\omega_{1})(1-\omega_{2})+\omega_{1}\omega_{2}\cdot\frac{\ell}{q-\ell}

(\omega_{1},\omega_{2})\in[\rho-\eta,\rho]^{2}

, it suffices to show that
g
is strictly maximized at the point
(\rho-\eta,\rho-\eta)
. If
\omega_{1}>\rho-\eta

\varepsilon_{1}=\omega_{1}-(\rho-\eta)>0
and observe that

\displaystyle g

\displaystyle(\rho-\eta,\omega_{2})-g(\omega_{1},\omega_{2})

\displaystyle=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left((1-(\omega_{1}-\varepsilon_{1}))(1-\omega_{2})+(\omega_{1}-\varepsilon_{1})\omega_{2}\cdot\frac{\ell}{q-\ell}}}\right)-\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left((1-\omega_{1})(1-\omega_{2})+\omega_{1}\omega_{2}\cdot\frac{\ell}{q-\ell}}}\right)

\displaystyle=\varepsilon_{1}(1-\omega_{2})-\varepsilon_{1}\omega_{2}\frac{\ell}{q-\ell}=\varepsilon_{1}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(1-\omega_{2}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(1+\frac{\ell}{q-\ell}}}\right)}}\right).

\displaystyle\omega_{2}\cdot\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(1+\frac{\ell}{q-\ell}}}\right)

\displaystyle<\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(1-\frac{\ell}{q}}}\right)\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(1+\frac{\ell}{q-\ell}}}\right)=1-\frac{\ell}{q}+\frac{\ell}{q-\ell}-\frac{\ell^{2}}{q(q-\ell)}

\displaystyle=1-\frac{\ell(q-\ell)-\ell q+\ell^{2}}{q(q-\ell)}=1-\frac{\ell q-\ell^{2}-\ell q+\ell^{2}}{q(q-\ell)}=1.

We can now state and prove a lemma bounding the probability that a nontrivial linear combination of two uniform samples from a list-recovery ball lands in a shift of the list-recovery ball. Then, in

4.6

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
n\in\mathds{N}
,
q
a prime power,
1\leq\ell\leq q
an integer, and let
\rho\in(0,1-\ell/q)
. Let

Let
\alpha,\beta\in\mathds{F}_{q}\setminus\{0\}
and

, and let

. Then,

\displaystyle\Pr[\alpha X+\beta X^{\prime}

\displaystyle\in y+B_{\rho}(T_{1}\times\cdots\times T_{n})]

\displaystyle\leq 2\sqrt{2n}\cdot\exp_{q}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(n(h_{q,\ell}(\rho-\eta)-h_{q,\ell}(\rho))}}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad+2n\cdot\exp_{q}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(-nD_{q}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(1-\rho\|(1-\rho+\eta)^{2}+(\rho-\eta)^{2}\frac{\ell}{q-\ell}}}\right)}}\right).

(17)

Remark 4.5
.

Since

4.3
implies

it follows that one can indeed choose
\eta
small enough to ensure

Later in

4.6
we will show how to choose

to bound the two terms in

17
by
q^{-\delta n}
for a concrete
\delta>0
(which depends on

).

Proof.

Denote
B=B_{\rho}(T_{1}\times\cdots\times T_{n})
, and fix any nonzero
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\in\mathds{F}_{q}
, and
y\in\mathds{F}_{q}^{n}
. Fix some
\eta\in(0,\rho)
soon to be determined. Let
Z_{1}\sim\alpha_{1}\cdot B
, and
Z_{2}\sim\alpha_{2}\cdot B
. The strategy is to “massage” the distribution of
(Z_{1},Z_{2})
so that it is easier to analyze the probability that
Z_{1}+Z_{2}\in B+y
, and then apply
Lemma

4.2
.

For each
i\in[n]
and
j=1,2
, let
W_{j,i}=1_{\{Z_{j,i}\notin\alpha_{i}\cdot T_{i}\}}
, and let
W_{j}=(W_{j,1},\dots,W_{j,n})
for

. By the law of total probability,

\displaystyle\Pr[Z_{1}+Z_{2}\in y+B]

\displaystyle=\sum_{1\leq w_{1},w_{2}\leq\lfloor\rho n\rfloor}\Pr[Z_{1}+Z_{2}\in y+B|\mathrm{wt}(W_{1})=w_{1},\mathrm{wt}(W_{2})=w_{2}]

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\cdot\Pr[\mathrm{wt}(W_{1})=w_{1},\mathrm{wt}(W_{2})=w_{2}]

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\lceil(\rho-\eta)n\rceil\leq w_{1},w_{2}\leq\lfloor\rho n\rfloor}\Pr[Z_{1}+Z_{2}\in y+B|\mathrm{wt}(W_{1})=w_{1},\mathrm{wt}(W_{2})=w_{2}]

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\cdot\Pr[\mathrm{wt}(W_{1})=w_{1},\mathrm{wt}(W_{2})=w_{2}]

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\Pr[\mathrm{wt}(W_{1})\leq(\rho-\eta)n~\lor~\mathrm{wt}(W_{2})\leq(\rho-\eta)n]

\displaystyle\leq\max_{\lceil(\rho-\eta)n\rceil\leq w_{1},w_{2}\leq\lfloor\rho n\rfloor}\Pr[Z_{1}+Z_{2}\in y+B|\mathrm{wt}(W_{1})=w_{1},\mathrm{wt}(W_{2})=w_{2}]

(18)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\Pr[\mathrm{wt}(W_{1})\leq(\rho-\eta)n~\lor~\mathrm{wt}(W_{2})\leq(\rho-\eta)n]\ .

We first bound

\displaystyle\Pr[\mathrm{wt}(W_{1})\leq(\rho-\eta)n~\lor~

\displaystyle\mathrm{wt}(W_{2})\leq(\rho-\eta)n]\leq 2\cdot\frac{|B_{\rho-\eta}(T_{1}\times\cdots\times T_{n})|}{|B_{\rho}(T_{1}\times\cdots\times T_{n})|}

where we used
Proposition

2.6
.

We now focus on proving that
Equation

18
is small. Fix any
w_{1},w_{2}\in\{\lceil(\rho-\eta)n\rceil,\lceil(\rho-\eta)n\rceil+1,\dots,\lfloor\rho n\rfloor\}
which maximize
\Pr[Z_{1}+Z_{2}\in y+B|\mathrm{wt}(W_{1})=w_{1},\mathrm{wt}(W_{2})=w_{2}]
, and set
\omega_{j}=w_{j}/n
for
j=1,2
. We now introduce a “model” distribution for

which is easier to analyze. For
i=1,\dots,n
and

, let

denote i.i.d. draws from a

A_{j}=(A_{j,1},\dots,A_{j,n})
.

Next, define for
i=1,\dots,n
the following random variables
D_{i}

D_{i}=\begin{cases}\alpha_{1}\cdot X+\alpha_{2}\cdot X^{\prime}&A_{1,i}=A_{2,i}=0,\\
\alpha_{1}\cdot X+Y^{\prime}&A_{1,i}=0,A_{2,i}=1,\\

. Using the above notation, we find

\displaystyle\qquad+\Pr[D_{i}\in y+T_{i}|A_{1,i}=0,A_{2,i}=1]\Pr[A_{1,i}=0,A_{2,i}=1]

\displaystyle\qquad+\Pr[D_{i}\in y+T_{i}|A_{1,i}=1,A_{2,i}=0]\Pr[A_{1,i}=1,A_{2,i}=0]

\displaystyle\qquad+\Pr[D_{i}\in y+T_{i}|A_{1,i}=A_{2,i}=1]\Pr[A_{1,i}=A_{2,i}=1]

\displaystyle\qquad+\Pr[\alpha_{1}\cdot X+Y^{\prime}\in y+T_{i}|A_{1,i}=0,A_{2,i}=1]\cdot(1-\omega_{1})\omega_{2}

\displaystyle\qquad+\Pr[Y+\alpha_{2}\cdot X^{\prime}\in y+T_{i}|A_{1,i}=1,A_{2,i}=0]\cdot\omega_{1}(1-\omega_{2})

Corollary 4.6
.

Let
n\in\mathds{N}
,
q
a prime power,
1\leq\ell<q
an integer, and let
\rho\in(0,1-\ell/q)
. Let
T_{1},\ldots,T_{n}\subseteq\mathds{F}_{q}
, each of size
\ell
. The list-recovery ball
B_{\rho}(T_{1}\times\ldots\times T_{n})
is
\delta

\delta=\log_{q}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{(q-\ell)(1-\rho)}{\rho\ell}}}\right)\cdot\frac{\rho^{4}(1-\ell/q-\rho)^{2}}{16\log q}

Proof.

By
Lemma

4.4
, it suffices to show that we can choose
\eta>0
so that

1-\rho>(1-\rho+\eta)^{2}+(\rho-\eta)^{2}\cdot\frac{\ell}{q-\ell}\

and, that assuming

for universal constant

, we have

\displaystyle(2\sqrt{2n})

\displaystyle\cdot\exp_{q}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(n(h_{q,\ell}(\rho-\eta)-h_{q,\ell}(\rho))}}\right)

(20)

\displaystyle\qquad+2n\exp_{q}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(-nD_{q}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(1-\rho\|(1-\rho+\eta)^{2}+(\rho-\eta)^{2}\cdot\frac{\ell}{q-\ell}}}\right)}}\right)

(21)

\displaystyle\leq q^{-n\delta},

for
\delta
as in the statement.

Set
\gamma=\frac{\ell}{q-\ell}
.
Note that then
0<\rho<1-\ell/q
is equivalent to
0<\rho<\frac{1}{1+\gamma}
.
Define
\Delta
so that

1-\rho-\Delta=(1-\rho+\eta)^{2}+(\rho-\eta)^{2}\cdot\gamma,

which means that

\displaystyle\Delta

\displaystyle=(1+2\eta+2\eta\gamma)\rho-(1+\gamma)\rho^{2}-2\eta-\eta^{2}-\eta^{2}\gamma

\displaystyle\geq(1+2\eta+2\eta\gamma)\rho-(1+\gamma)\rho^{2}-(3+\gamma)\eta.

(22)

Rearranging, we get that
Equation

22
is at least
\eta\rho
whenever

0<\eta\leq\frac{\rho-\rho^{2}(1+\gamma)}{(3+\gamma)-\rho(1+2\gamma)}.

Given this, we may choose

\eta=\frac{\rho(1-\ell/q-\rho)}{3}>0.

Indeed, recalling that
\gamma=\frac{\ell}{q-\ell}
, we have

\frac{\rho-\rho^{2}(1+\gamma)}{(3+\gamma)-\rho(1+2\gamma)}\geq\frac{\rho-\rho^{2}(1+\gamma)}{3+\gamma}=\frac{\rho(q(1-\rho)-\ell)}{3q-2\ell}\geq\frac{\rho(q(1-\rho)-\ell)}{3q}=\eta.

By
Claim

2.13
,
D(1-\rho\|1-\rho-\Delta)\leq 2\Delta^{2}
, and so
D_{q}(1-\rho\|1-\rho-\Delta)\leq\frac{2}{\ln q}\Delta^{2}
.
We therefore have

\displaystyle 2n\exp_{q}

\displaystyle\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(-nD_{q}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(1-\rho\|(1-\rho+\eta)^{2}+(\rho-\eta)^{2}\cdot\frac{\ell}{q-\ell}}}\right)}}\right)\leq 2n\exp_{q}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(-\frac{2}{\ln q}\eta^{2}\rho^{2}n}}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\exp_{q}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(-\frac{\rho^{4}(1-\ell/q-\rho)^{2}}{16\log q}\cdot n}}\right),

(23)

assuming that
n\geq\operatorname{poly}(\log q,1/\rho,1/(1-\ell/q-\rho))
.

We now obtain a lower bound on
h_{q,\ell}(\rho)-h_{q,\ell}(\rho-\eta)
with our chosen
\eta
.
Claim

2.9
tells us that

h_{q,\ell}(\rho)-h_{q,\ell}(\rho-\eta)\geq\log_{q}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{(q-\ell)(1-\rho)}{\rho\ell}}}\right)\eta=\log_{q}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{(q-\ell)(1-\rho)}{\rho\ell}}}\right)\cdot\frac{\rho(1-\ell/q-\rho)}{3},

which is positive whenever
\rho<1-\ell/q
.
Thus,

2\sqrt{2n}\cdot\exp_{q}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(-\log_{q}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{(q-\ell)(1-\rho)}{\rho\ell}}}\right)\frac{\rho(1-\ell/q-\rho)}{3}\cdot n}}\right)\\
\leq\exp_{q}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(-\log_{q}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{(q-\ell)(1-\rho)}{\rho\ell}}}\right)\frac{\rho(1-\ell/q-\rho)}{6}\cdot n}}\right),

(24)

where we assumed that
n\geq\operatorname{poly}(\log q,1/\rho,1/(1-\ell/q-\rho))
.

By
Equations

23
and
24
, it follows that a rough, but sufficient, bound on (
20
)+(
21
) is

\exp_{q}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(-\log_{q}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{(q-\ell)(1-\rho)}{\rho\ell}}}\right)\cdot\frac{\rho^{4}(1-\ell/q-\rho)^{2}}{16\log q}\cdot n}}\right),

completing the proof.
∎

4.2
List Recovery from Errors

Having established that the list-recovery ball is
\delta
-mixing, we can
repeat roughly the same argument we had for list recovery with erasures, using
Lemma

3.4
.

Theorem 4.7

(list recovery with errors)
.

For every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, a prime power
q
, an integer
\ell<q
,
\rho\in(0,1-\ell/q)
, and
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
, the following holds. With probability at least
1-q^{-n}
, a random linear code
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathds{F}_{q}^{n}
of rate

R=1-h_{q,\ell}(\rho)-\varepsilon

is
(\rho,\ell,L)
-list-recoverable from errors, with

L=C_{\rho,\ell,q}\cdot\frac{1}{\varepsilon},

provided that
n
is large enough. More concretely, there exists a universal constant
C
such that

C_{\rho,\ell,q}\leq q^{\ell\cdot\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{\log q}{\rho(1-\ell/q-\rho)}}}\right)^{C}}

provided that
n\geq\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{\log q}{\rho(1-\ell/q-\rho)}}}\right)^{C}
.

Proof.

The technique follows the proof of
Theorem

3.5
, with the necessary changes. We fix some
T_{1},\ldots,T_{n}\subseteq\mathds{F}_{q}
, each of size
\ell
, and denote by
T=B_{\rho}(T_{1}\times\cdots\times T_{n})
the list-recovery ball. Recall that we wish to show that
|T\cap\mathcal{C}|\leq L
. The rest of the argument exactly matches
Theorem

3.5
, that is, if
|T\cap\mathcal{C}|>L
, there must exist some
\bm{v}\in\mathcal{F}
for which
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{{\bm{v}}}}\right\}\subseteq T\cap\mathcal{C}
. Three things change:

1.

Our bound on
\delta
, which we now take from
Corollary

4.6
.

2.

The number of input lists we union-bound over. While in
Theorem

3.5
it was
\mathcal{T}=\binom{n}{\alpha n}\binom{q}{\ell}^{(1-\alpha)n}
, here it’s
\mathcal{T}=\binom{q}{\ell}^{n}
.

3.

The gap
\varepsilon
is now with respect to the “list-recovery with
errors
” capacity
1-h_{q,\ell}(\rho)
.

Concretely, the bound on
p_{1}
now becomes

p_{1}\leq\mathcal{T}\cdot b^{\star}q^{-an}\cdot\sum_{b=\log_{q}(L+1)}^{b^{\star}}|T|^{b}\cdot q^{-(1-R)nb},

and the bound on
p_{2}
is now

p_{2}\leq\mathcal{T}\cdot\sum_{b=b^{\star}+1}^{L+1}|T|^{b}q^{-(1-R)nb},

and we are interested in bounding
p=p_{1}+p_{2}
. We will re-set
a
and
c
(which appears in
b^{\star}=\lceil c\ell/\varepsilon\rceil
) shortly. Recalling that
|T|\leq q^{nh_{q,\ell}(\rho)}
(see
Proposition

2.6
), the bound on
p_{1}
becomes

\displaystyle p_{1}

\displaystyle\leq q^{n\ell\log_{q}(eq/\ell)+\log_{q}(b^{\star})-an}\cdot\sum_{b=\log_{q}(L+1)}^{b^{\star}}q^{nbh_{q,\ell}(\rho)-(h_{q,\ell}(\rho)+\varepsilon)nb}

\displaystyle\leq q^{2n\ell-an-\varepsilon nh_{q,\ell}(\rho)\log_{q}(L+1)}\leq q^{(2\ell-a)n}.

Taking
a=2\ell+2
, we get
p_{1}\leq q^{-2n}
.
Moving on to
p_{2}
, we have

p_{2}\leq\mathcal{T}\cdot\sum_{b=b^{\star}+1}^{L+1}q^{nbh_{q,\ell}(\rho)-(h_{q,\ell}(\rho)+\varepsilon)nb}\\
\leq q^{2n\ell-h_{q,\ell}(\rho)\varepsilon nb^{\star}}.

Setting
c=\frac{1}{4h_{q,\ell}(\rho)}
we get
p_{2}\leq q^{-2n}
, and overall
p\leq q^{-n}
, as desired. The output list size is
q^{\frac{8a}{\delta}}\cdot\frac{c\ell}{\varepsilon}
. Using the fact that
\frac{1}{h_{q,\ell}(\rho)}\leq\frac{1}{\log q}
, and that
\log_{q}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{(q-\ell)(1-\rho)}{\rho\ell}}}\right)\leq\log_{q}(q/\rho)
, the bound on
C_{q,\rho}
follows.
∎

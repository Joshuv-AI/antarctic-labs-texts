Superquantile-Gibbs Relaxation for Minima-selection in Bi-Level Optimization
Saeed Masiha, Zebang Shen, Negar Kiyavash, Niao He

Abstract
Minima selection is essential for defining Bi-Level Optimization (BLO) when the lower-level objective admits multiple minimizers. While BLO is intractable in its most general form, we identify that restricting the lower-level objective to the recently proposed PŁ ∘ functions ( Gong et al. 2024 ) ensures a provably continuous hyper-objective F max F_{\max} . The PŁ ∘ condition is strictly weaker than the global Polyak Łojasiewicz property assumed in recent BLO studies and enables BLO to model practical applications, such as hyperparameter tuning in over-parameterized deep learning. However, even under the PŁ ∘ condition, F max F_{\max} remains non-convex and non-smooth. To address this, we propose a relaxed solution concept for BLO: We approximate F max F_{\max} with a continuously differentiable function F ~ max \tilde{F}_{\max} that is pointwise ϵ v \epsilon_{v} -close to F max F_{\max} and seek an ϵ g \epsilon_{g} -stationary point of F ~ max \tilde{F}_{\max} . Within this framework, we reduce the minima-selection subroutine—a non-convex optimization over a non-convex set—to a sampling problem via a novel Superquantile-Gibbs relaxation . By leveraging the manifold structure of the lower-level optimal set, characterized under the PŁ ∘ condition, our method achieves the relaxed solution within 𝒪 ⁡ ( poly ⁡ ( ϵ v − k ​ ϵ g − 1 ) ) \mathcal{O}(\mathrm{poly}(\epsilon_{v}^{-\texttt{k}}\epsilon_{g}^{-1})) queries to a Gibbs sampling oracle, which admits efficient Langevin-based implementation. Here, k is the shared dimension of the manifolds forming the lower-level optimal sets. This work is the first to explicitly capture how the complexity of BLO scales with the intrinsic dimensionality of the lower-level problem.



Keywords:
Bi-level optimization, non-convex lower-level problem, and local PŁ condition.

\displaystyle(\text{{SQ-G}-loss})

\displaystyle\tilde{F}_{\texttt{SQ-G}}(\theta):=\min_{\beta\in\mathbb{R}}\phi_{\lambda,\delta}^{\gamma,\zeta}(\theta,\beta)

\displaystyle\phi_{\lambda,\delta}^{\gamma,\zeta}(\theta,\beta):=\beta+\frac{1}{\delta}\mathbb{E}_{X\sim\mu^{\lambda}_{\theta}}[h_{\gamma}(f(\theta,X)-\beta)]+\frac{\zeta}{2}\beta^{2},

Conclusion and Future Works

We identify the

∘

Appendix A
Related Work

Strongly-convex lower-level objective.

A large portion of the existing research on BLO assumes that the lower-level problem has a unique optimal solution, e.g. when it is strongly convex.
We note that in this setting, since
\mathcal{S}(\theta)
is a singleton, the maximization of
x
is trivial.
This assumption allows for the development of efficient and scalable algorithms
[
Ablin et al. 2020
,
Arbel and Mairal 2021
,
Blondel et al. 2022
,
Liu et al. 2022
]
. However, in many real-world applications, assuming strong convexity of the lower-level problem is unrealistic. This is especially true in deep learning, where the follower’s objective—typically the training loss—can exhibit high non-convexity in the model parameters. Furthermore, it may have regions of flat optima due to symmetries and other degeneracies
[
Draxler et al. 2018
,
Li et al. 2018
]
.

Non-strongly-convex lower-level objective.

Several studies have aimed to relax the strongly-convex assumption on the lower-level objective
g
by considering a broader class of lower-level problems
[
Chen et al. 2024
,
Arbel and Mairal 2022
,
Kwon et al. 2023a
,
Xiao et al. 2023
,
Yang et al. 2022
,
Kwon et al. 2023b
,
Kwon et al. 2023b
]
. In general, BLO with nonconvex lower-level objectives is not computationally tractable without further assumptions, even for the special case of min-max optimization
[
Daskalakis et al. 2021
]
. Therefore, additional assumptions on the lower-level problem are necessary. Arguably, the minimal assumption would be the continuity of lower-level solution sets w.r.t. the upper-level variable in Hausdorff distance sense, otherwise, it leads to discontinuous hyper-objective.

The global PŁ condition of the lower-level objective, is a sufficient condition for the Lipschitz continuity of
F_{\max}
, see for example
[
Chen et al. 2024
, Corollary 6.1]
, and is a common assumption made in this literature.
While the PŁ condition does not encompass all possible scenarios of BLO, it covers situations where the lower-level problem is nonconvex and admitting non-unique solutions, making the minima-selection subroutine essential.

There have been several recent works on BLO with lower-level problems satisfying the PŁ condition
[
Shen and Chen 2023
,
Chen et al. 2024
,
Li et al. 2022
,
Xiao et al. 2023
,
Yang et al. 2022
]
. To the best of our knowledge, in all of them, the minima-selection problem is circumvented through additional assumptions or alternative problem formulations:

•

(The optimal set is singleton.) In addition to the PŁ condition,
Huang 2023
further assumes that the Hessian of the lower-level function w.r.t. the lower-level variable is positive definite, which essentially means that its solution set is singleton. The convergence of the proposed adaptive mirror descent method is analyzed under these assumptions.

•

(Any choice in the optimal set
\mathcal{S}(\theta)
yields the same hyper-gradient.)
Kwon et al. 2023a
,
Chen et al. 2024
reformulate BLO using the classical value function approach and assume that the PŁ condition (or error bound) for a resulting penalty function
\sigma f+g
for all
\sigma\in[0,\sigma_{0}]
. More precisely,
Kwon et al. 2023a
assumed a proximal error bound for
\sigma f+g
, whereas
Chen et al. 2024
directly assumed the global PŁ condition for
\sigma f+g
. Additionally,
Chen et al. 2024
established that these two conditions are equivalent.
With the global PŁ condition,
Kwon et al. 2023a
,
Chen et al. 2024
prove that the hyper objective
F_{\max}
is differentiable by establishing the corresponding Danskin’s theorem. However, their result also implies that any choice in the optimal set
\mathcal{S}(\theta)
yields the same hyper-gradient
\nabla F_{\max}
, rendering the minima-selection meaningless. Moreover, the assumption that the PŁ condition or error bound holds for
\sigma f+g
is rarely satisfied, even when both
f
and
g
are PŁ functions.

•

(Replace optimization-based minima-selection with a simple selection map) Unlike the commonly used pessimistic and optimistic formulation for bilevel problem, in
[
Arbel and Mairal 2022
]
the optimization-based minima-selection subproblem in
Equation

1
is replaced with a selection map. The main example discussed in
[
Arbel and Mairal 2022
]
is gradient-flow map where from the optimal set
\mathcal{S}(\theta)
, this map selects the terminal point of the gradient flow of the lower-level objective
g
with a specific initial point. However, it is in general difficult to interpret the semantic meaning of such a gradient-flow map: For example, it
would not
adhere to either pessimistic or optimistic BLO formulation as it requires no access to the upper-level objective.

•

(Relax BLO to a KKT point problem and any choice in the optimal set
\mathcal{S}(\theta)
would be sufficient.)

Xiao et al. 2023
notice that a modified KKT condition is always satisfied by the optimal solution to the BLO problem when the lower-level objective is PL and convex is satisfied. Consequently, they relax the BLO to identify a modified KKT point of the Lagrangian function of a gradient-based reformulation of the BLO problem.
With this relaxation, any point in the optimal set
\mathcal{S}(\theta)
would suffice and hence the minima-selection problem is avoided.
However, as pointed out by
Chen et al. 2024
, even for strongly-convex lower-level objectives, there exist KKT points that are not stationary points for the hyper-objective

22

Note that for strongly-convex lower-level objective, the hyper-objective is continuously differentiable.

A.1
Other related work

Riemannian BLO with strongly convex lower-level objective:
Utilizing the geodesically strong convexity,
Li and Ma 2024
explored hypergradient computation for bilevel optimization on Riemannian manifolds, introducing both deterministic and stochastic algorithms. Similarly,
Han et al. 2024
developed a general framework for bilevel optimization, providing multiple hypergradient estimation methods along with convergence and complexity analyses, highlighting the significance of hypergradient information. In contrast,
Dutta et al. 2024
, following
[
Kwon et al. 2023b
]
, proposed a fully first-order approach that avoids second-order information such as Riemannian hypergradients, offering a novel perspective in this field.

Functional Bi-level Optimization:
A key limitation of implicit differentiation is its reliance on the strong convexity of the inner function, which is often too restrictive. In practical applications, such as neural networks, the inner function is typically nonconvex.
Petrulionyte et al. 2024
address this by reformulating the inner problem as an infinite-dimensional optimization problem. In machine learning, the inner function often takes the form
g(\theta,x)=\mathcal{L}(\theta,h_{x})
, where
\{h_{x}\}_{x}
represents a parametric function family (e.g., a neural network with weight parameters). Nonconvexity usually arises from the parametrization, while
\mathcal{L}(\theta,\cdot)
is often convex. The idea of
[
Petrulionyte et al. 2024
]
is to replace minimization over
y
with minimization of
\mathcal{L}(\theta,\cdot)
in functional space and establish a functional variant of the implicit function theorem, allowing computation of the Fréchet derivative of
h^{*}(\theta)=\argmin_{h}\mathcal{L}(\theta,h)
.

Moreau envelop reformulation:

Gao et al. 2022
first applied the Moreau envelope to the lower-level (LL) problem in bilevel optimization (BLO), proposing an inexact proximal Difference-of-Weakly-Convex algorithm using a double-loop approach for constrained BLO with convex LL objectives.
Bai et al. 2024
built on this by deriving new optimality conditions, while
Yao et al. 2024
introduced a min-max variant and a single-loop, Hessian-free, gradient-based algorithm for constrained BLO.
Liu et al. 2024
further developed a single-loop gradient-based method for general BLO with nonconvex, potentially nonsmooth LL objectives. All these studies examine the oracle complexity of their algorithms in achieving a stationary point of a penalty function that combines the upper-level function with the Moreau envelope approximation of the LL function (or the Lagrangian when constraints are present).

Parametric Morse lower-level function:
In
[
Bolte et al. 2025
]
, the authors introduce two relaxations of bilevel optimization (BLO): the
critical set
relaxation and the
local-min
relaxation, both based on the output of gradient descent applied to the lower-level objective
g(\theta,\cdot)
. Assuming a piecewise version of the parametric Morse condition, they show that the graph of the critical set—i.e.,
\{(\theta,x(\theta)):\nabla_{x}g(\theta,x(\theta))=0\}
—and similarly the graph of the local minima, can be represented as a finite union of differentiable manifolds.

This structural result allows them to reformulate both relaxations as integer nonlinear programs of the form
\min_{\theta,i\in[1:N]}f(\theta,x^{i}(\theta))
, where each
x^{i}(\theta)
is a critical point (or local minimum) of
g(\theta,\cdot)
. They explore two solution strategies under the parametric Morse assumption: (1) a two-stage method that first approximates the set of lower-level critical points (or local minima) for fixed
\theta
, followed by a gradient step on the upper-level objective; and (2) a differentiable programming approach that directly optimizes
f(\theta,\hat{x}(\theta))
using gradient descent over both
\theta
and
x
, where
\hat{x}(\theta)
is the output of gradient descent on
g(\theta,\cdot)
.

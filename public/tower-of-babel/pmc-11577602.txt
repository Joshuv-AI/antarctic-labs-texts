SGTCDA: Prediction of circRNA-drug sensitivity associations with interpretable graph transformers and effective assessment
Authors: Hongwei Xia, Caiyue Dong, Xinxing Chen, Zhuoyu Wei, Lichuan Gu, Xiaolei Zhu
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractCircRNAs are a type of circular non-coding RNA whose associations with drug sensitivities have been demonstrated in recent studies. Due to the high cost of biomedical experiments for detecting the associations between circRNAs and drug sensitivities, several computational methods have been developed. However, these methods were evaluated mainly based on 5- or tenfold cross-validation, which are often over-optimistic. Furthermore, there are technique issues with these models, such as over-smoothing and over-squashing. To address these issues, we propose a strategy to evaluate models based on independent test sets for association prediction-related studies. In the light of this effective assessment, we constructed a model, SGTCDA, by integrating structural deep network embedding (SDNE) and a graph transformer to predict the potential associations of circRNA-drug sensitivity, which can efficiently capture long-range dependencies and local structural information of nodes. Our results on the training sets and the independent test sets indicate that SGTCDA outperforms the other state-of-the-art models, demonstrating its capacity for accurate prediction of circRNA-drug sensitivity. Moreover, we leveraged EdgeSHAPer to explain the performance of the proposed SGTCDA model, which illustrates that the edges between drugs are more important than other edges for the performance of the model. Introduction

CircRNAs were first discovered in 1976 in RNA (ribonucleic acid) viruses and were recognized as having a virus-like structure [1]. Since circRNAs are formed as closed loops and lack clearly defined 5' and 3' ends, they are particularly stable for specific biological processes [2]. The development of computational power and RNA sequencing technologies over the past few years has allowed researchers to obtain a precise and thorough understanding of this ring structure [3]. For example, Lalit Kumar et al. [4] suggested that circRNAs are likely to be common genetic triggers among multiple factors contributing to neurodegenerative diseases. Miri Danan et al. [5] reported that circRNAs play important regulatory roles in plants and animals. In recent studies, circRNAs were shown to be involved in tumor formation and progression [6, 7]. The above validated biological functions indicate that circRNAs could be new kinds of clinical diagnostic markers and therapeutic targets.

In recent years, a number of studies have revealed the influence of circRNAs on cellular drug sensitivity. For example, Liu et al. [8] reported that circRNAs increase bortezomib sensitivity by regulating the miR-615-3p/PRKCD axis, thus revealing a new regulatory mechanism for chemoresistance in multiple myeloma. Li et al. [9] demonstrated that circARNT2 reduces the sensitivity of hepatocellular carcinoma cells to cisplatin by targeting the miR-155-5p/PDK1 axis, and the findings of the study strongly support the potential of circARNT2 as a therapeutic target for this disease. These studies are of great significance to the biomedical field and the development of new drugs. However, the understanding of the relationship between circRNAs and drug sensitivity is still relatively inadequate, and further studies are needed.

Although circRNA-drug sensitivity associations can be detected by traditional biomedical experimental methods, these methods are time-consuming and expensive. Advances in computing power have provided a strong impetus for machine learning techniques, especially in the field of bioinformatics, where advanced techniques such as deep learning are widely used [10–15]. Thus, an increasing number of computational models have been developed. Deng et al. [16] proposed the first computational method, GATECDA, for predicting the associations between circRNAs and drug sensitivities. First, the similarity matrices between circRNAs and between drugs were calculated, respectively. Then, the low-dimensional feature vectors were generated for circRNA nodes and drug nodes by GATE, which were finally fed into a fully connected neural network to make predictions about unknown associations. The case studies further demonstrate the accuracy and effectiveness of the model. Subsequently, Yang et al. [17] proposed the MNGACDA model to predict the associations between circRNAs and drug sensitivities. First, multiple data sources were utilized to construct similarity matrices between circRNAs and between drugs, respectively. Then these similarity matrices and the circRNA-drug sensitivity association matrix were fed into node-level attention graph autoencoders for feature learning, and a convolutional neural network (CNN) was used to process the embedded representation of each layer. Finally, an inner product decoder was used to predict circRNA-drug sensitivity associations. This model shows better performance and prediction results than GATECDA. Immediately after, Li et al. [18] proposed a computational model called DGATCCDA, which combines the DeepWalk algorithm with graph attention networks to better extract the feature representations of nodes. Lu et al. [19] used bi-typed multi-relational heterogeneous graphs to learn the integrated representations of nodes and made predictions with the help of the Dual Laplacian Regularized Least Squares method (DLapRLS). Although the above studies show promising results, these computational methods have issues such as over-smoothing and over-squashing due to message passing that can be caused during the use of graph neural networks, and the performances are all evaluated based on fivefold or tenfold cross-validation and no independent test sets were used for validation. Similar issues exist for models to predict circRNA-drug resistance associations and drug-disease associations. This evaluation strategy may overestimate the performance of the corresponding models and lack a certain degree of rigor and interpretability.

In this paper, we propose a new model, SGTCDA, to predict circRNA-drug sensitivity associations based on structural deep network embedding (SDNE) and graph transformers, which can efficiently capture local structural information of nodes and long-range dependencies. In addition, the graph transformer can also mitigate the issues of over-smoothing and over-squashing. More importantly, to rigorously evaluate our model, we propose a strategy to divide the data into training and independent test sets and validate our model by both cross-validation and independent testing. Thus, the models can effectively avoid problems such as test data leakage during the training process and can be correctly assessed. Furthermore, we interpret our model by using the EdgeSHAPer algorithm to show the importance of different kinds of edges.

Materials and methods

Datasets

The datasets used in this study were acquired from Deng et al.’s work [16]. Deng et al. searched the circRic database [20] to gather data on the correlations between circRNAs and drug sensitivities, while the GDSC database [21] contains information about drug sensitivities. CircRic is a database focused on circRNAs in cancers, containing expression data and biogenesis regulation information from various cancer cell lines. It analyzes their roles in drug response and explores their relationships with gene expression, protein levels, and mutations. GDSC provides data on cancer cell lines' responses to anticancer drugs and their genomic information, used to study the relationship between drug sensitivity and genetic features, promoting personalized cancer treatment. The initial dataset covers 80,076 associations related to 404 circRNAs and 250 drugs. According to the data from Cancer Cell line Encyclopedia, the effect of circRNAs on drug response is then analyzed in the circRic database. The Wilcoxon test was used to determine the significant circRNA-drug sensitivity correlations. The procedure was contingent upon a false discovery rate (FDR) less than 0.05. The final dataset contains 4134 significant associations involving 218 drugs and 271 circRNAs. Based on these associations, we constructed the association matrix A∈R218×271, where Aij=1 means that the ith drug is associated with the jth circRNA, and Aij=0 is the opposite. In addition to the associations between circRNAs and drug sensitivities, we also obtained circRNA host gene sequences and drug structures from the National Center for Biotechnology Information (NCBI) gene database [22] and the NCBI PubChem database [23], which were subsequently used to derive their similarity matrices, respectively.

Similarity networks

Similarity of circRNA host gene sequences

In this paper, the similarity between circRNAs is obtained based on the degree of similarity between their sequence segments, and the measure of this sequence similarity is the Levenshtein distance [24]. The Levenshtein distance can be calculated with the help of the ratio function of the Levenshtein package for Python. In the following work, the similarity matrix between circRNAs is denoted by CSM∈R271×271.

Similarity of drug structures

Chemical structures can often be used to measure the similarities between drugs. After obtaining the chemical structures of the drugs from the PubChem database, we first used the RDKit tool [25] to compute the topological fingerprints of each drug. Then, we calculated the structural similarities between drugs by using the Tanimoto method [26]. Ultimately, the similarity matrix between drugs was obtained which is denoted as DSM∈R218×218.

Gaussian interaction profile kernel similarity

The similarity matrices derived from the above two sections are sparse and do not include enough information. Therefore, the Gaussian interaction profile (GIP) kernel similarity [27] is used here to integrate the association information between circRNAs and drugs.

GIP kernel similarity is widely used in association prediction applications [28–30]. According to the hypothesis that the more similar circRNAs are, the more likely they are to be associated with the same drug sensitivity. Thus, the GIP kernel similarities of circRNAs and drugs were calculated based on the circRNA-drug sensitivity association matrix A. In contrast to previous studies, here, we divided the circRNA-drug sensitivity association data into a training set and an independent test set according at a ratio of 8:2. When we calculate the GIP kernel similarity, we mask the positive samples in the independent test set with zeros in association matrix A. In this way, information leakage can be avoided, and the performance of the model can be effectively evaluated on the independent test set in subsequent experiments. However, the inability to capture long-range dependencies and the issues of over-smoothing and over-squashing that can occur are the greatest drawbacks when using GNNs. To address these issues, we propose a new graph transformer model to predict the associations of circRNA-drug sensitivity, named SGTCDA, which can effectively mitigate the problems posed by GNNs. Setting of independent test sets for association prediction

Many models have been proposed to predict associations between circRNAs, drugs, and diseases [39–42], which have made great contributions to the discovery of new drugs as well as drug repositioning and other related works. However, there are two common issues in these association prediction studies: (i) This kind of experiment without using an independent test set has certain flaws and a lack of rigor. The evaluation based only on cross-validation is often over-optimistic; (ii) During cross-validation, all the information of the association matrices is used to construct similarity networks or heterogeneous networks, which leads to information leakage when evaluating models on the validation sets.

In this paper, we propose a strategy for setting independent test sets to conduct an effective evaluation of association prediction. Three experiments were conducted to demonstrate the effectiveness of the strategy: circRNA-drug sensitivity association prediction, circRNA-drug resistance association prediction and drug-disease association prediction. Three state-of-the-art models were employed for the experiments including MNGACDA [17] (for circRNA-drug sensitivity association prediction), GraphCDD [39] (for circRNA-drug resistance association prediction) and AMDGT [40] (for drug-disease association prediction). Taking drug-disease association prediction as an example. At the beginning, the associated drug-disease pairs were split into two parts. One part was assigned to the training dataset and the other part was assigned to the independent test set. In the construction of the drug-disease association matrix, only those associations in the training set were used. Then, for building the independent test set, the same number of non-associated drug-disease pairs were randomly selected as negative samples. In the cross-validation experiments conducted on the training set, we treated the validation set in the same way as we treated the independent test set. In this way, only the data from the training set are integrated into the GIP kernel similarity matrix and the heterogeneous association network in the construction of the homogeneous similarity network. Thus, the information leakage from the validation set can be avoided during model selection. Finally, the generalizability of the models is evaluated based on their performance on the independent test set. For the other two experiments, the independent test sets were generated in the same way. The flowchart of this strategy is shown in Fig. 3.

Flowchart for generating independent test sets

Based on the proposed dataset partition, we repeated the works of MNGACDA, GraphCDD and AMDGT. Not only the AUC values but also the other evaluation indicators decrease significantly on the independent test sets. Our results demonstrate that it would be over-optimistic to evaluate the model performance only by the cross-validation results, so it is particularly important to use independent test sets to assess the generalization in association prediction.

Hyper parameter sensitivity analysis

Our model consists of multiple modules, several key hyper parameters of the model can affect its predictive performance, thus, we conducted 5-CV to evaluate the parameter sensitivity of our model. Specifically, the following parameters were evaluated: (1) the number of epochs and the dimensions of hidden layers in the SDNE module. (2) the number of heads in the multi-head self-attention mechanism and the number of encoders. (3) the dimensions of input and output features in the GCN module. (4) the number of channels of the CNN module. (5) the dropout rate of the Dropout module. (6) the learning rate of the optimizer. The effects of these parameters on the performance of SGTCDA model are shown in Fig. 4.

Hyper parameters sensitivity analysis based on AUC and AUPR

According to Fig. 4, we determined that the performance of AUC is optimal when setting SDNE-epoch to 300, SDNE-hidden_size to 256, Transformer-head to 2, Transformer-n_layers to 3, GCN-in_channels to 128, GCN-out_channels to 512, CNN-in_channels to 8, Dropout-dropout rate to 0.1, and Adamw-learning rate to 0.001.

Comparison with other state-of-the-art methods

In light of the results of the above section, we compared our model with other state-of-the-art models based mainly on the results on the independent test set. The three most advanced methods, namely MNGACDA [17], DGATCCDA [18], and MNCLCDA [43] were used as controls. Note that we did not compared with GATECDA, because the code for the model is not available.

MNGACDA [17]: A node-level attention graph auto-encoder is used to learn multi-modal networks and obtain feature representations of circRNAs and drugs from them.

DGATCCDA [18]: DeepWalk is combined with a graph attention network, the extracted features are fused through hierarchical attention, and finally, the inner product decoder is used to construct a correlation matrix for prediction.

MNCLCDA [43]: The convolutional network of mixed neighborhood graphs is used to obtain the neighborhood information of nodes. The potential associations are then predicted by the double Laplace-regularized least-squares method.

Given that only fivefold cross-validation results were reported for these computational methods, to thoroughly compare the robustness and performance of our model with those of other models, under the premise of fixed positive samples, the same number of negative samples was randomly selected ten times to construct ten different balanced datasets. For each dataset, we sampled five different sets for independent testing, and the remaining data were used as the training set for cross-validation. As a result, fifty models were trained for each method, resulting in fifty rounds of fivefold cross-validation and independent testing. The averages of the corresponding evaluation indicators were finally compared.

As shown in Fig. 5 and Table 2, the average cross-validation AUC value of SGTCDA is 0.9319, which is 2.06%, 3.35% and 2.66% higher than those of MNCLCDA, DGATCCDA and MNGACDA, respectively. In addition, the average cross-validation AUPR value of SGTCDA is 0.9439, which is 2.25%, 3.78% and 2.89% higher than those of MNCLCDA, DGATCCDA and MNGACDA, respectively. The values of the other indicators also illustrate that our model is consistently superior to the three state-of-the-art models.

Interpretation of the model

The proposed SGTCDA framework is based on a GNN, and it is essential to mine the pivotal graph information for the prediction. A novel algorithm, EdgeSHAPer, was developed to analyze the importance of different edges of the graphs, which is employed here to interpret our model. First, for the circRNA-drug association network, we calculated the contribution value of all edges of this network to the final prediction. For the integrated circRNA similarity network and the integrated drug similarity network, we extracted the top 994 edges and 1155 edges with the highest similarity in their respective networks from a constructed k-nearest neighbor graph function and calculated their contribution values, respectively. Finally, the top 50 edges with the highest contribution values were selected from each of the three networks and ranked in descending order. The ranking results is shown in Fig. 8.

Shapley values of the corresponding edges for the three networks

In our analysis, we set the number of Monte Carlo steps M = 50. For the edges between drugs, the highest Shapley value is 1.64, and the contribution values of all the top fifty edges were above 1.0. In contrast, for the edges between drugs and circRNAs, the highest Shapley value is merely 0.57, representing the lowest contribution among the three graphs. Moreover, from Fig. 8, it can be clearly seen that the Shapley value corresponding to the edges between drugs is generally higher than that of circRNAs and that of the association network. This result effectively demonstrates that the drug-drug similarity network is most relevant to model prediction, whereas the drug-circRNA association network is the least relevant. This result is in line with the performance of the model variants Var1 and Var2 as shown in Table 5. Additionally, based on the edges with high Shapley values in both the drug-drug similarity network and the circRNA-circRNA similarity network, we identified the top ten drugs and circRNAs that are most critical for the final prediction. The results are shown in Table S1 in the additional file 1. The degrees of these drugs are from 5 to 151 which are all higher than the average degree (4.56) of drug-drug similarity network. The degrees of these circRNAs are from 5 to 27 which are all higher than the average degree (4.26) of circRNA-circRNA similarity network.

Case studies

To further validate the predictive performance of our model on unknown circRNA-drug sensitivity associations, we conducted case studies on two drugs. Specifically, we trained our model based on all the 4134 associations from the GDSC database to generate prediction probabilities. Then, we checked the predictive results for two drugs, Linifanib and Sunitinib. Based on the predicted probabilities, we chose the top 20 circRNAs that were not in the original 4134 associations. These top 20 new associations were validated by searching another independent database, CTRP [44]. Similar to the original 4134 associations, the new associations were confirmed with an FDR less than 0.05 as significant.

Linifanib [45] is an oral small molecule tyrosine kinase inhibitor that primarily targets vascular endothelial growth factor receptors (VEGFR) and platelet-derived growth factor receptors (PDGFR). It is used for anti-tumor therapy by inhibiting angiogenesis and tumor cell proliferation. Sunitinib [46] is a multi-target tyrosine kinase inhibitor primarily used to treat renal cell carcinoma and gastrointestinal stromal tumors. It works by inhibiting multiple receptors, including vascular endothelial growth factor receptors (VEGFR) and platelet-derived growth factor receptors (PDGFR), thereby blocking the blood supply and growth of tumors.

As shown in Tables 6 and 7, among the top 20 circRNAs predicted to be associated with Linifanib, 16 have been confirmed in CTRP; and among the top 20 circRNAs predicted to be associated with Sunitinib, 16 have been confirmed in CTRP.

Model evaluation on imbalanced datasets

To further demonstrate the performance of our model on imbalanced datasets, we create four imbalanced datasets by randomly selecting negative samples according to the ratios of 2:1, 3:1, 4:1, and 5:1 between negative samples and positive samples. Then, we divided the datasets into training and independent test sets as we did for the balanced datasets to evaluate our model both on cross-validation and the independent test sets. As shown in the Figs. 9 and 10, for both five-fold cross-validation and independent tests, the model's performance slightly decreased with the increase of the number of negative samples but still outperformed the three other state-of-the-art methods.

Conclusion

Given that the existing models for association prediction were evaluated solely through fivefold or tenfold cross-validation. We conducted an independent test for three association prediction systems, and the results indicate that the performance declined across all three experiments on the independent test set, thereby revealing the incompleteness of previous related studies and underscoring the significance of segregating an independent test set. Then, we proposed a novel computational method, SGTCDA, for predicting circRNA-drug sensitivity associations. SGTCDA is able to extract useful information by constructing SDNE embedded representation modules. Subsequently, the graph transformer module, which is composed of a transformer encoder and a graph convolutional network, effectively prevents the model from over-smoothing and over-squashing. The extensive experiments show that SGTCDA is superior to other methods in terms of accuracy and robustness, making it an excellent tool for predicting new CDAs. Moreover, we used EdgeSHAPer, a generally applicable method for GNN-based model interpretation, to explain our model and solve the contribution value of each edge in the graph to the final prediction.

Notably, although our model achieved outstanding performance on both training and independent test sets. There some limitations of current study. First, the size of the dataset used circRNA-drug sensitivity association prediction is small, which may lead to insufficient statistical power, low reproducibility of results, and limited generalizability and representativeness, thereby affecting the reliability of current predictive models. To address this limitation, more data should be collected and the data from different sources could be integrated to improve the accuracy and generalizability. Second, this study faces a cold start problem when predicting associations related new circRNAs or new drugs, as the lack of prior data leads to decreased prediction performance, poor model adaptability, and limited practical applicability. To overcome this limitation, future research should integrate external data, use transfer learning and reinforcement learning techniques, and develop hybrid models to enhance the model's initial prediction ability and adaptability for new circRNAs or new drugs, thereby improving its practicality and reliability.

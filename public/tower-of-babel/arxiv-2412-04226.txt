Multi-height analysis of rational points of toric varieties
Bongiorno Nicolas

Abstract
. We study the multi-height distribution of rational points of smooth, projective and split toric varieties over 𝐐 \mathbf{Q} using the lift of the number of points to universal torsors.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary NT11D45, AG14M25

1.
Introduction

For a quasi-Fano variety
V
with infinitely many rational points, one may asymptotically study the finite set of rational points with a bounded height
H
associated with the anticanonical line bundle
\omega_{V}^{-1}
. In
[
12
]
,
[
1
]
and
[
15
]
, Batyrev, Franke, Manin, Tschinkel and Peyre gave strong evidence supporting conjectures relating the asymptotic behavior of the number of points of bounded height on open subsets of
V
to geometric invariants of
V
.

In the case of toric varieties, the asymptotic behavior was studied in
[
2
]
and
[
3
]
using harmonic analysis on adelic groups. In
[
20
]
, Salberger was the first to explicitly use universal torsors to study points of bounded height. In particular, he was able to give a new proof of the theorem of Batyrev and Tschinkel for smooth projective split toric varieties over
\mathbf{Q}
. His work highlighted how the constant in the conjectural asymptotic formula is related to the product of local densities on universal torsors. This was later studied in a more general setting by Peyre in
[
16
]
and
[
17
]
.

More recently in
[
19
]
, Peyre proposed a new framework to study the asymptotic distribution of rational points with bounded height. Instead of considering a height relative to one line bundle, it is natural to consider all possible heights. The purpose of this paper is to study the multi-height distribution on rational points of smooth, proper and split toric varieties over
\mathbf{Q}
. We prove that the expected asymptotic behavior holds for these varieties. In particular, we show that there is no accumulating subset for the multi-height distribution in the toric case. This is because we consider the rational points whose multi-height is in the interior of the dual of the effective cone.

A result concerning this all heights approach was given in
[
11
]
, using height zeta functions. However, the condition
\beta=\pi^{\vee}(u)
where
u\in(C_{\eff}(X)^{\vee})^{\circ}
seems to be missing in
[
11
, theorem 1.1]
. If one considers the case of the projective line
\mathbf{P}^{1}
with
(\beta_{1},\beta_{2})=(1,2)
, their result would imply that the asymptotic behavior is of the form
C.B^{3}
whereas it is known to be of the form
C.B^{2}
.

In any case, our method using descent is totally different. It is inspired by the work of Salberger in
[
20
]
and uses the method of counting the number of points of a lattice in a bounded domain developed in
[
9
]
by Davenport.

Structure.
We begin in Section 2 by recalling the multi-height formalism and the conjecture on the asymptotic distribution of rational points for
quasi-Fano
varieties associated with this formalism. We then state the main result established in this article for toric varieties. In the following section, we introduce a crucial concept for proving our result: the universal torsor. We explain how to lift the height of a point locally to the universal torsor, describe the specific case of toric varieties, and conclude by linking the measure on universal torsors with the Tamagawa number of the variety. In the final section, we establish our result using estimation techniques from the geometry of numbers and the Möbius inversion formula.

2.
Terminology and the precise statement

2.1.
Arakelov heights

In this paragraph, we recall, following Peyre
[
19
]
, how to define a system of Arakelov heights for a smooth, proper and geometrically integral variety
V
over a number field
K
.

Notations
.

We denote by
M_{K}
the set of places of
K
, by
M_{K}^{0}
the set of finite places and by
M_{K}^{\infty}
its infinite places.

If
v\in M_{K}^{0}
, we write
q_{v}=\sharp\mathbf{F}_{v}
the cardinal of the residual field associated to
v
, while if
v\in M_{K}^{\infty}
, we set
q_{v}=\exp([K_{v}:\mathbf{R}])
.

To
w\in M_{K}
which divides
v\in M_{\mathbf{Q}}
, we define
|-|_{w}
over
K_{w}
by setting for any
x\in K_{w}
:

|x|_{w}=|N_{K_{w}/\mathbf{Q}_{v}}(x)|_{v}.

It is an absolute value except when
K_{w}
is
\mathbf{C}
in which case it is the square of an absolute value.

To an invertible sheaf
\mathcal{L}
over a scheme
T
, we associate the line bundle
L
whose sections correspond to
\mathcal{L}
.
We denote
L^{\times}=L\setminus z(T)
, where
z(T)
is the closed subscheme given by the zero section.

We begin by defining the notion of an adelic norm over a vector bundle.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
E\xrightarrow{e}V
be a vector bundle over
V
.

For any field extension
L
over
K
, for any
P\in V(L)
, we denote by
E_{P}\subset E(L)
the
L
-vector space which corresponds to the fiber
e^{-1}(P)
of
e
over
P
.

An adelic norm
E
over
e
is the data of a family of continuous applications
(||-||)_{v\in M_{K}}

||-||_{v}:E(K_{v})\rightarrow\mathbf{R}_{+}

such that:

(1)

If
v\in M_{K}^{0}
, for any
P\in V(K_{v})
, the restriction of
||-||_{v}
to
E_{P}
defines an ultrametric norm with values in the image of the absolute value
|-|_{v}
i.e.
q_{v}^{\mathbf{Z}}
.

(2)

If
v
is a real place, for any
P\in V(K_{v})
, the restriction to
E_{P}
is a Euclidean norm.

(3)

If
v
is a complex place, for any
P\in V(K_{v})
, the restriction to
E_{P}
is the square of a Hermitian norm.

(4)

There exists a finite set of places
S\subset M_{K}
(which contains
M_{K}^{\infty}
) and a model
\mathcal{E}\rightarrow\mathcal{V}
of
E\rightarrow V
over
\mathcal{O}_{S}
such that for any place
v\in M_{K}\setminus S
, for any
P\in\mathcal{V}(\mathcal{O}_{v})
,

\mathcal{E}_{P}=\{y\in E_{P}|\ \ ||y||_{v}\leqslant 1\}.

Remark 2.2
.

For smooth, proper and split toric varieties over
\mathbf{Q}
such models always exists over
\mathbf{Z}
.

The following example is particularly important, because, as we shall later see, a natural system of heights over toric varieties can be deduced from this example.

Example 2.3
.

Let
N\in\mathbf{N}^{*}
. For
v\in M_{K}
, we consider the map:

\displaystyle||-||_{v}

\displaystyle:\ K_{v}^{N+1}\rightarrow\mathbf{R}_{+}

\displaystyle(y_{0},..,y_{N})\longmapsto\max\limits_{i=0}^{N}|y_{i}|_{v}

This defines an adelic norm
(||-||_{v})_{v\in M_{K}}
on the line bundle
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{P}^{N}}(-1)\rightarrow\mathbf{P}^{N}
which corresponds to the
\mathbf{G}_{m}
-torseur
\mathbf{A}^{N+1}\setminus\{0\}\rightarrow\mathbf{P}^{N}
.

Definition 2.4
.

If
L\rightarrow V
is a line bundle,
\{||-||_{v}\}
an adelic norm on
L
, then we define the height
H_{L}:V(K)\rightarrow\mathbf{R}_{+}
for any
x\in V(K)
by:

H_{L}(x)=\prod\limits_{v\in M_{K}}||y||_{v}^{-1}

where
y\in L^{\times}(K)
lies above
x
.

Example 2.5
.

In particular, by the previous example, for any
x\in K^{N+1}-\{0\}
, if we denote by
P=[x_{0}:..:x_{n}]\in\mathbf{P}^{N}(K)
its image, we get:

H_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{P}^{N}}(1)}(P)=\prod\limits_{v\in M_{K}}\max\limits_{0\leq i\leq N}|x_{i}|_{v}.

Now, we recall the definition of the group of Arakelov heights
\mathcal{H}(V)
over
V
:

Definition 2.6
.

Let
L
and
L^{\prime}
be two adelic line bundles over
V
(i.e. equipped with an adelic norm). Let
(||-||_{v})
be the adelic norm on
L
.

We say that
L
and
L^{\prime}
are equivalent as adelic line bundles when there exists
M>0
and a family
(\lambda_{v})_{v}
of nonnegative real numbers such that:

(1)

\{v|\lambda_{v}\neq 1\}
is finite and
\prod\limits_{v}\lambda_{v}=1

(2)

there exists an isomorphism of adelic line bundles from
L^{\otimes M}
to
L^{\prime\otimes M}
, where the adelic norm on
L^{M}
is induced by the adelic norm on
L
given by
\{(\lambda_{v}.||-||_{v})_{v\in M_{K}}\}
.

We denote by
\mathcal{H}(V)
the set of equivalence classes of adelic line bundles. This set is naturally equipped with a structure of group given by the tensorial product of adelic line bundles.

Hence, we can define the notion of system of heights and of multi-height over
V
.

Definition 2.7
.

A
system of heights
over
V
corresponds to the data of a section
s:\Pic(V)\rightarrow\mathcal{H}(V)
of the forgetful morphism

\mathrm{o}:\mathcal{H}(V)\rightarrow\Pic(V).

Such system of heights defines a
multi-height map
:

h:V(K)\rightarrow\Pic(V)^{\vee}_{\mathbf{R}}

such that for any
P\in V(K)
,
h(P)
is the linear form defined for any
L\in\Pic(V)
by:

h(P)(L)=\log H_{s(L)}(P).

Remark 2.8
.

The multi-height is well-defined as
h(P)(L)
does not depend on the choice of the representative of the equivalence class (see
[
19
, remark 4.2]
).

2.2.
The multi-height conjecture

In order to state the conjecture made by Peyre
[
19
, question 4.8]
, we shall define a measure
\nu
on
\Pic(V)^{\vee}_{\mathbf{R}}
as follows:

Definition 2.9
.

\nu(\mathrm{D}):=\int\limits_{\mathrm{D}}e^{\langle\omega_{V}^{-1},y\rangle}dy

for any compact subset
\mathrm{D}
of
\Pic(V)^{\vee}_{\mathbf{R}}
, where the Haar measure
dy
on
\Pic(V)^{\vee}_{\mathbf{R}}
is normalized so that the covolume of the dual lattice of the Picard group is one.

For any domain
\mathrm{D}\subset\Pic(V)^{\vee}_{\mathbf{R}}
, we write:

V(K)_{h\in\mathrm{D}}=\{P\in V(K)\mid h(P)\in\mathrm{D}\}.

Definition 2.10
.

We shall say that
V
is quasi-Fano if it satisfies the following hypotheses:

(1)

A multiple of the class of
\omega_{V}^{-1}
is the sum of an ample divisor and a divisor with normal crossings;

(2)

The set
V(\mathbf{Q})
is Zariski dense;

(3)

The group
H^{i}(V,\mathcal{O}_{V})=\{0\}
for
i=1,2
;

(4)

The geometric Brauer group
\Br(\overline{V})
is trivial and the geometric Picard group
\Pic(\overline{V})
has no torsion;

(5)

The closed cone
\text{C}_{\eff}(\overline{V})
is generated by the classes of a finite set of effective divisors.

Any Fano variety and any smooth, proper and split toric variety is quasi-Fano. Let us quickly explain where the last claim comes from. The effective cone of such a variety is generated by the divisors associated to each edge of the fan defining the toric variety, and its anticanonical bundle is the sum of these divisors, so it is in the interior of the effective cone. Hence we get that the first and the fifth hypotheses are verified. As such a variety is covered by open subschemes isomorphic to
\mathbf{A}^{d}
, where
d
is the dimension of the toric variety, we get the second assertion and the fact that its geometrical Brauer group is trivial. For such toric variety, by
[
8
, proposition 4.2.5]
, the geometric Picard group is torsion free. Finally, by
[
8
, theorem 9.2.3]
, the third hypothesis is verified. Peyre conjectures the following in
[
19
, question 4.8]
:

Conjecture 2.11
.

We assume that our variety is quasi-Fano and that the effective cone
C_{\eff}(V)
is generated by a finite number of line bundles. Let
\mathrm{D}_{1}
be a compact polyhedron of
\Pic(V)^{\vee}_{\mathbf{R}}
and u be an element of the interior of the dual of the effective cone
(C_{\eff}(V)^{\vee})^{\circ}
. For a real number
B>1
, we set:

\mathrm{D}_{B}=\mathrm{D}_{1}+\log(B)u.

There exists an exceptional subset
T\subset V(K)
such that we have an asymptotic behaviour of the form:

\sharp(V(K)-T)_{h\in\mathrm{D}_{B}}\underset{B\rightarrow+\infty}{\sim}\nu(\mathrm{D}_{1})\beta(V)\tau(V)B^{\langle\omega_{V}^{-1},u\rangle}

where
\beta(V)=\sharp H^{1}(\mathbf{Q},\Pic(V_{\overline{\mathbf{Q}}}))
and
\tau(V)
is the Tamagawa number of
V
(see definition
3.22
).

2.3.
The Manin-Peyre conjecture in the case of toric varieties

Let
X
be a smooth, proper and split toric variety over
\mathbf{Q}
. We shall use in the rest of this paper that the anticanonical line bundle
\omega_{X}^{-1}
is big, i.e. is in the interior of the effective cone
\text{C}_{\text{eff}}(X)
.

Notations
.

We denote by
T\subset X
its open torus, by
\Sigma
a fan associated to
X=X_{\Sigma}
, by
\Sigma(1)
the set of edges of
\Sigma
and by
m
the number of edges. We denote by
t
the rank of the Picard group
\Pic(X)
. With our hypothesis, it is a free abelian group of finite rank. To an edge
\rho\in\Sigma(1)
, we associate the
T
-invariant divisor
D_{\rho}
.

We write

\pi:\Div_{T}(X)=\mathbf{Z}^{\Sigma(1)}\rightarrow\Pic(X)

the natural map which associates to a
T
-invariant divisor
D
the class of the associated line bundle in
\Pic(X)
which we write
[D]=\pi(D)
. Recall that

\omega_{X}^{-1}=\sum\limits_{\rho\in\Sigma(1)}[D_{\rho}].

We denote by
N=X_{*}(T)
the 1-parameter subgroups of
T
and by
M=X^{*}(T)
its dual, the group of characters of
T
.

Convention 2.12
.

A line bundle
L
over
X
is endowed with the associated natural adelic norm
(||-||_{v})_{v\in M_{K}}
following
[
20
, definition 9.2]
(see also
[
1
, proposition 2.1.2]
).

Let us explain how one can interpret this adelic norm in the case of
\mathbf{P}^{N}
.

Example 2.13
.

For
0\leq i\leq N
, we denote by
D_{i}
the divisor
\mathbf{G}_{m}^{N}
-invariant of
\mathbf{P}^{N}
corresponding to the hyperplane
\{X_{i}=0\}\subset\mathbf{P}^{N}
. Then the adelic norm over
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{P}^{N}}(1)
associated to
D_{i}
corresponds to the one of the example
2.5
.

Proof.

This is a consequence of
[
20
, proposition 9.8]
.
∎

Remark 2.14
.

We can moreover observe that the construction of the adelic norm in
[
20
, definition 9.2]
over
\mathcal{O}(D)
depends only on the class of the
T
-invariant divisor
D
modulo
M=X^{*}(T)
, that is to say its class
[D]
in
\Pic(X)
.

We shall later see using universal torsor that this way of defining a height system over our toric variety
X
(and a representative of its system of heights) makes the count of rational points of our variety easier.

Notations
.

Let
l
be the smallest positive integer such that there exists
l
rays of
\Sigma
not contained in a cone of
\Sigma
. Note that
l\geqslant 2
.

The main theorem of this article states that the conjecture
2.11
for the multi-height asymptotic behaviour of rational points of toric varieties is true:

Theorem 2.15
.

Let
\mathrm{D}_{1}
be a finite union of compact polyhedrons in
\Pic(X)^{\vee}_{\mathbf{R}}
and
u
be an element of the interior of the dual of the effective cone
(C_{\eff}(X)^{\vee})^{\circ}
. For a real number
B>1
, we set:

\mathrm{D}_{B}=\mathrm{D}_{1}+\log(B)u.

Let
\varepsilon\in]0,1-\frac{1}{l}[
. Then we have an asymptotic behaviour of the form:

\sharp(X(\mathbf{Q}))_{h\in\mathrm{D}_{B}}\underset{B\rightarrow+\infty}{=}\nu(\mathrm{D}_{1}).\tau(X).B^{\langle\omega_{X}^{-1},u\rangle}\left(1+O\left(B^{-(1-\frac{1}{l}-\varepsilon).\min\limits_{\rho\in\Sigma(1)}\langle[D_{\rho}],u\rangle}\right)\right)

where
\nu
is the measure over
\Pic(X)^{\vee}_{\mathbf{R}}
defined as in
2.9
.

Remark 2.16
.

In forthcoming work, we will see that, provided that we restrict ourselves to a generating family of

\Pic(X)
consisting of effective line bundles, it is possible to obtain a similar result without imposing a lower bound on the height, but this introduces a
\log(B)
factor in the error term. We hope also to show that this result implies the usual form of Manin’s conjecture.

Example 2.17
.

If
\mathbf{b}=(L_{1},..,L_{r})
is a family of line bundles such that their classes
\mathbf{R}
-generate the Picard group
\Pic(X)_{\mathbf{R}}
,
\alpha_{1},..,\alpha_{r},\beta_{1},..,\beta_{r}
are real numbers such that for any
i
,
\alpha_{i}<\beta_{i}
, then we define a compact polyhedron of
\Pic(X)^{\vee}_{\mathbf{R}}
:

\mathrm{D}_{1}=\{a\in\Pic(X)^{\vee}_{\mathbf{R}}\mid\alpha_{i}\leqslant a(L_{i})\leqslant\beta_{i}\}.

All compact polyhedrons of
\Pic(X)^{\vee}_{\mathbf{R}}
are of this form.

3.
The notion of universal torsors

In this section, we shall present how the study of the multi-height distribution of rational points of toric varieties naturally lifts to a counting problem in the universal torsor.

3.1.
Generalities about universal torsors

Originally, Colliot-Thélène and Sansuc defined universal torsors using a spectral sequence (see
[
7
, section 2]
) that can be generalized over an noetherian base scheme
S
. For this paragraph, we essentially refer to
[
20
, section 5]
.

We suppose that
f:V\rightarrow S
is a smooth proper surjective morphism of constant relative dimension with geometrically connected fibers such that
R^{2}f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{V}=0
and the relative Picard functor
\Pic_{V/S}
is étale locally the constant sheaf associated with an ordinary free abelian group of finite rank. We also suppose that
f
has a rational section. We shall denote by
T_{\NS}
the group scheme associated to
\Pic_{V/S}
by duality. Then we have the following exact sequence, functorial in
S
and
V
(see
[
20
, section 5, p.163]
which generalizes
[
7
, 2.0.2]
):

0\rightarrow H^{1}(S,T_{\NS})\xrightarrow{i_{1}}H^{1}(V,T_{\NS})\xrightarrow{\chi}\Hom_{\grp}(\Pic_{V/S},\Pic_{V/S})\xrightarrow{\partial}H^{2}(S,T_{\NS})

Definition 3.1
.

A
T_{\NS}
-torsor
\mathcal{T}\rightarrow V
is said to be universal if its class

[\mathcal{T}]\in H^{1}(V,T_{\NS})

is sent to
\id_{\Pic_{V/S}}
via
\chi
.

In the case we will be interested,
S=\Spec(\mathbf{Z})
, and our scheme will be split that is to say
\Pic_{V/S}
is the constant sheaf associated with a free abelian group of finite rank. With this hypothesis, we have that for any
S
-scheme
T
,
\Pic_{V/S}(T)=H^{1}(V\underset{S}{\times}T,\mathbf{G}_{m})
and
\Hom_{\grp}(\Pic_{V/S},\Pic_{V/S})=\Hom_{\grp}(\Pic(V),\Pic(V))
. Moreover, the group morphism
\chi
can be defined for any
[\mathcal{T}]\in H^{1}(X,T_{\NS})
as follows (see
[
21
, Definition 2.3.2 and Theorem 2.3.6]
):

\displaystyle\chi([\mathcal{T}]):\

\displaystyle H^{1}(V,\mathbf{G}_{m})\rightarrow H^{1}(V,\mathbf{G}_{m})

\displaystyle[L^{\times}]\longmapsto[\mathcal{T}\overset{T_{\NS}}{\times_{\chi_{L}}}\mathbf{G}_{m}]

where
\chi_{L}
is the character of
T_{\NS}
associated to
[L^{\times}]\in H^{1}(V,\mathbf{G}_{m})
i.e.
[L]\in\Pic(V)
.

Let us choose
\mathbf{b}=(L_{1},..,L_{t})
a basis of
\Pic(V)
and write

\mathcal{T}_{\mathbf{b}}=L_{1}^{\times}\times_{V}...\times_{V}L_{t}^{\times}

where an element
t\in T_{\NS}
acts on
L_{i}^{\times}
by multiplication by
[L_{i}](t)\in\mathbf{G}_{m}
. The torus
T_{\NS}
is isomorphic to
\mathbf{G}_{m}^{t}
via the characters given by the basis
\mathbf{b}
. Hence we get a natural left action of
T_{\NS}
over

q:\mathcal{T}_{\mathbf{b}}\rightarrow V

which makes
\mathcal{T}_{\mathbf{b}}
a
T_{\NS}
-torsor over
V
. By
[
18
, proposition 8.1]
, we get that the previous construction is independent of the choice of the basis of
\Pic(V)
because it is unique up to
T_{\NS}
-equivariant isomorphism. To simplify the notations, we shall denote it
\mathcal{T}\rightarrow V
. If each
L_{i}
has a non constant global section, we can show that
\chi([\mathcal{T}])=-\id_{\Pic_{V/S}}
.

Remark 3.2
.

By functoriality of this cohomological characterization, if
\mathcal{T}\rightarrow V
is a universal torsor and
S^{\prime}\rightarrow S
is a base change, then
\mathcal{T}_{S^{\prime}}\rightarrow V_{S^{\prime}}
is again a universal torsor.

Let us recall the following proposition from
[
20
, proposition 5.15]
, which is valid under our assumptions that
\Pic_{V/S}
is a constant sheaf and
V
has a rational point:

Proposition 3.3
.

There is a universal torsor over
V
. Moreover the isomorphism classes of universal torsors over
V
are parametrized by the
H^{1}(S,T_{\NS})
-orbits in
H^{1}(V,T_{\NS})
defined by
\chi^{-1}(\id_{\Pic_{V/S}})
.

Cohomological arguments give that if
\alpha\in H^{1}(V,T_{\NS})
is the class of a universal torsor
\mathcal{T}\rightarrow V
,
x\in V(S)
and
P\rightarrow S
is a
T_{\NS}
-torsor associated to the class
\alpha(x)
then
\mathcal{T}\overset{T_{\NS}}{\times}P\rightarrow V
is a universal torsor such that the fiber over
x
has a rational point. This come from the fact that if
\beta\in H^{1}(V,T_{\NS})
is the class of
\mathcal{T}\overset{T_{\NS}}{\times}P\rightarrow V
, we have by construction
\beta(x)=0
.

Suppose again that
S=\Spec(\mathbf{Z})
and that
\Pic_{V/\mathbf{Z}}
is the constant sheaf associated to a free abelian group of finite rank
t
. As
\mathbf{Z}
is a principal ring, we have that
H^{1}(\mathbf{Z},T_{\NS})=\{1\}
. Thus there is up to isomorphism a unique universal torsor
q:\mathcal{T}\rightarrow V
. Moreover, we have:

q(\mathcal{T}(\mathbf{Z}))=V(\mathbf{Z}).

3.2.
Local heights defined over the universal torsors

We keep the hypotheses of the previous paragraph on
V
. By
[
19
, remark 4.19]
, the universal torsor over
V_{\mathbf{Q}}
is characterized by a universal property as a
pointed torsor
.

Definition 3.4
.

A pointed variety over
\mathbf{K}
is a variety
V
equipped with a chosen rational point
x\in V(\mathbf{K})
. A pointed torsor over
V
is a torsor
P\xrightarrow{\pi}V
with a rational point
t\in P(\mathbf{K})
such that
\pi(t)=x
.

We write
q:\mathcal{T}\rightarrow V
for the universal torsor we have chosen. We fix
y_{0}\in\mathcal{T}(\mathbf{Q})
and we denote by
x_{0}\in V(\mathbf{Q})
its image via
q
. We can now state the following proposition:

Proposition 3.5
.

For any pointed torsor
P\rightarrow V
which sends
p_{0}\in P(\mathbf{Q})
to
x_{0}\in V(\mathbf{Q})
, under a multiplicative group scheme
T
, there exists a unique group morphism
\varphi:T_{NS}\rightarrow T
and a unique
\varphi
-équivariant morphism
\Phi_{P}:\mathcal{T}\rightarrow P
over
V
which is compatible with the marked point. That is to say the following diagram is commutative:
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{(\mathcal{T},y_{0})}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{(P,p_{0})}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{(V,x_{0})}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\exists!\Phi_{P}}

We recall the construction of Peyre
[
19
, construction 4.27]
to define the local height associated to the universal torsor. Suppose we have a given system of heights

s:\Pic(V)\rightarrow\mathcal{H}(V).

We fix a place

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{H}(Y)}

3.30
, we have:

\displaystyle\sharp(\mathcal{T}(\mathbf{Z})\cap T_{\Sigma(1)}(\mathbf{R}))_{h_{\mathcal{T},\infty}\in\mathrm{D}_{B}}

\displaystyle=2^{\sharp\Sigma(1)}.\sum\limits_{y\in(\mathbf{N}^{*})^{\Sigma(1)}}\mathbf{1}_{h_{\mathcal{T},\infty}^{-1}(\mathrm{D}_{B})}(y).\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{T}(\mathbf{Z})}(y)

\displaystyle=2^{\sharp\Sigma(1)}.\sum\limits_{y\in(\mathbf{N}^{*})^{\Sigma(1)}}\mathbf{1}_{h_{\mathcal{T},\infty}^{-1}(\mathrm{D}_{B})}(y).\sum\limits_{d\in(\mathbf{N}^{*})^{\Sigma(1)}}\mu(d).\mathbf{1}_{d.\mathbf{Z}^{\Sigma(1)}}(y)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{d\in(\mathbf{N}^{*})^{\Sigma(1)}}\mu(d).\left(2^{\sharp\Sigma(1)}.\sum\limits_{y\in(\mathbf{N}^{*})^{\Sigma(1)}}\mathbf{1}_{h_{\mathcal{T},\infty}^{-1}(\mathrm{D}_{B})}(y).\mathbf{1}_{d.\mathbf{Z}^{\Sigma(1)}}(y)\right)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{d\in(\mathbf{N}^{*})^{\Sigma(1)}}\mu(d).N_{d}(B).

∎

We shall show the following theorem:

Theorem 4.14
.

Let
\varepsilon\in]0,1-\frac{1}{l}[
. There exists a constant
C>0
such that:

\displaystyle\left|\sharp(\mathcal{T}(\mathbf{Z})\cap T_{\Sigma(1)}(\mathbf{R}))_{h_{\mathcal{T},\infty}\in\mathrm{D}_{B}}-2^{t}.\prod\limits_{p}\omega_{\mathcal{T},p}(\mathcal{T}(\mathbf{Z}_{p})).\mu_{X,\infty}(X(\mathbf{R})).\nu(\mathrm{D}_{1})B^{\langle\omega_{X}^{-1},u\rangle}\right|

\displaystyle\leqslant C.\frac{B^{\langle\omega_{X}^{-1},u\rangle}}{B^{(1-\frac{1}{l}-\varepsilon)\min\limits_{\rho\in\Sigma(1)}\langle[D_{\rho}],u\rangle}}.

Proof.

Recall that by propositions
3.31
and
3.32
, we have that:

\sum\limits_{d}\frac{\mu(d)}{\Pi(d)}=\prod\limits_{p}\omega_{\mathcal{T},p}(\mathcal{T}(\mathbf{Z}_{p})).

Hence using the previous proposition, we get:

\displaystyle\left|\sharp(\mathcal{T}(\mathbf{Z})\cap T_{\Sigma(1)}(\mathbf{R}))_{h_{\mathcal{T},\infty}\in\mathrm{D}_{B}}-2^{t}.\prod\limits_{p}\omega_{\mathcal{T},p}(\mathcal{T}(\mathbf{Z}_{p})).\mu_{X,\infty}(X(\mathbf{R})).\nu(\mathrm{D}_{1})B^{\langle\omega_{X}^{-1},u\rangle}\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\sum\limits_{d}\mu(d)\left(N_{d}(B)-2^{t}.\frac{1}{\Pi(d)}.\mu_{X,\infty}(X(\mathbf{R})).\nu(\mathrm{D}_{1})B^{\langle\omega_{X}^{-1},u\rangle}\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\Pi(d)\leqslant B^{\min\limits_{\rho\in\Sigma(1)}\langle[D_{\rho}],u\rangle}}|\mu(d)|.\left|N_{d}(B)-2^{t}.\frac{1}{\Pi(d)}.\mu_{X,\infty}(X(\mathbf{R})).\nu(\mathrm{D}_{1})B^{\langle\omega_{X}^{-1},u\rangle}\right|

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\Pi(d)\geqslant B^{\min\limits_{\rho\in\Sigma(1)}\langle[D_{\rho}],u\rangle}}|\mu(d)|.\left|N_{d}(B)-2^{t}.\frac{1}{\Pi(d)}.\mu_{X,\infty}(X(\mathbf{R})).\nu(\mathrm{D}_{1})B^{\langle\omega_{X}^{-1},u\rangle}\right|.

When
\Pi(d)\leqslant B^{\min\limits_{\rho\in\Sigma(1)}\langle[D_{\rho}],u\rangle}
we use corollary
4.12
, we have that there exists a constant
C>0
such that:

\left|N_{d}(B)-\frac{1}{\Pi(d)}.2^{t}.\mu_{X,\infty}(X(\mathbf{R})).\nu(\mathrm{D}_{1})B^{\langle\omega_{X}^{-1},u\rangle}\right|\leqslant C.\frac{B^{\langle\omega_{X}^{-1},u\rangle}}{B^{\min\limits_{\rho\in\Sigma(1)}\langle[D_{\rho}],u\rangle}}.

Let
\varepsilon\in]0,1-\frac{1}{l}[
. Then by lemma
3.33
, we get that:

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\Pi(d)\leqslant B^{\min\limits_{\rho\in\Sigma(1)}\langle[D_{\rho}],u\rangle}}|\mu(d)|.\left|N_{d}(B)-2^{t}.\frac{1}{\Pi(d)}.\mu_{X,\infty}(X(\mathbf{R})).\nu(\mathrm{D}_{1})B^{\langle\omega_{X}^{-1},u\rangle}\right|

\displaystyle\leqslant C.\frac{B^{\langle\omega_{X}^{-1},u\rangle}}{B^{\min\limits_{\rho\in\Sigma(1)}\langle[D_{\rho}],u\rangle}}.B^{(\frac{1}{l}+\varepsilon).\min\limits_{\rho\in\Sigma(1)}\langle[D_{\rho}],u\rangle}.

Now by proposition
4.10
, there exists
C^{\prime}>0
such that:

N_{d}(B)\leqslant C^{\prime}.\frac{B^{\langle\omega_{X}^{-1},u\rangle}}{\Pi(d)}.

Hence we get using the second assertion of lemma
3.33
that there exists
C^{\prime\prime}>0
such that:

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\Pi(d)\geqslant B^{\min\limits_{\rho\in\Sigma(1)}\langle[D_{\rho}],u\rangle}}|\mu(d)|.\left|N_{d}(B)-2^{t}.\frac{1}{\Pi(d)}.\mu_{X,\infty}(X(\mathbf{R})).\nu(\mathrm{D}_{1})B^{\langle\omega_{X}^{-1},u\rangle}\right|

\displaystyle\leqslant C^{\prime\prime}.B^{\langle\omega_{X}^{-1},u\rangle}.\sum\limits_{\Pi(d)\geqslant B^{\min\limits_{\rho\in\Sigma(1)}\langle[D_{\rho}],u\rangle}}\frac{|\mu(d)|}{\Pi(d)}

\displaystyle\leqslant C^{\prime\prime}.\frac{B^{\langle\omega_{X}^{-1},u\rangle}}{B^{(1-\frac{1}{l}-\varepsilon)\min\limits_{\rho\in\Sigma(1)}\langle[D_{\rho}],u\rangle}}.

This concludes our proof.
∎

4.4.
Conclusion

Finally we can establish the theorem
2.15
:

Theorem 4.16
.

Let
\epsilon\in]0,1-\frac{1}{l}[
. Then we have:

\sharp(X(\mathbf{Q}))_{h\in\mathrm{D}_{B}}\underset{B\rightarrow+\infty}{=}\nu(\mathrm{D}_{1}).\tau(X).B^{\langle\omega_{X}^{-1},u\rangle}\left(1+O\left(B^{-(1-\frac{1}{l}-\varepsilon).\min\limits_{\rho\in\Sigma(1)}\langle[D_{\rho}],u\rangle}\right)\right)

where
\nu
is the measure over
\Pic(X)^{\vee}_{\mathbf{R}}
defined as in
2.9
.

Proof.

Let us recall that
\sharp X(\mathbf{Q})_{h\in\mathrm{D}_{B}}=\frac{1}{2^{t}}\sharp\left(\mathcal{T}(\mathbf{Z})\right)_{h_{\mathcal{T},\infty}\in\mathrm{D}_{B}}
. Applying corollary
4.15
, we get that there exists a constant
C>0
such that:

\left|(\sharp X(\mathbf{Q})_{h\in\mathrm{D}_{B}}-\left(\prod\limits_{p}\omega_{\mathcal{T},p}(\mathcal{T}(\mathbf{Z}_{p}))\right).\mu_{X,\infty}(X(\mathbf{R})).\nu(\mathrm{D}_{1})B^{\langle\omega_{X}^{-1},u\rangle}\right|\leqslant C.\frac{B^{\langle\omega_{X}^{-1},u\rangle}}{B^{(1-\frac{1}{l}-\varepsilon)\min\limits_{\rho\in\Sigma(1)}\langle[D_{\rho}],u\rangle}}.

Moreover by remark
3.23
and theorem
3.27
, we get:

\left(\prod\limits_{p}\omega_{\mathcal{T},p}(\mathcal{T}(\mathbf{Z}_{p}))\right).\mu_{X,\infty}(X(\mathbf{R}))=\tau(X)

hence we have the result stated.
∎

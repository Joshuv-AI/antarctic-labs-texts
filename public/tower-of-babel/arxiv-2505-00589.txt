Homogenization for the nonlinear Schrödinger equation with sprinkled nonlinearity
Benjamin Harrop-Griffiths, Maria Ntekoume

Abstract
. We first prove homogenization for the nonlinear Schrödinger equation with sprinkled nonlinearity introduced in [ 19 ] . We then investigate how solutions fluctuate about the homogenized solution.



1.
Introduction

The nonlinear Schrödinger equation with sprinkled nonlinearity,

(1.1)

i\partial_{t}\psi=-\partial_{x}^{2}\psi+2|\psi|^{2}\psi\,d\mu,

was introduced in
[
19
]
as a model for the propagation of waves exhibiting strong self-interaction at defects in the surrounding medium.
These defects are distributed according to a homogeneous Poisson process
\mu
, which we take to have unit intensity.

The model (
1.1
) is motivated by physical scenarios in which nonlinear effects are caused by the interaction of waves with the surrounding environment. For example, in the optical Kerr effect an intense pulse of light causes a nonlinear change to the refractive index of the material through which it travels
[
7
]
.

In practice, when the typical distance between points at which the nonlinear effects are concentrated is much smaller than a typical wavelength, we treat the surroundings as if they were homogeneous. This corresponds to replacing the Poisson process
\mu
in (
1.1
) by Lebesgue measure to obtain the cubic nonlinear Schrödinger equation,

(NLS)

i\partial_{t}\psi=-\partial_{x}^{2}\psi+2|\psi|^{2}\psi.

Our goal in this article is to rigorously describe this homogenization procedure.

As discussed in
[
19
]
, the choice of a Poisson process in (
1.1
) is not out of mathematical convenience. Indeed, one may readily envision homogenization occurring when the nonlinearity is concentrated in a periodic arrangement. The model (
1.1
) describes an antithetical scenario: the centers of the nonlinear effects have been randomly “sprinkled” along the line. In this case we have regions of arbitrarily high concentration, a potential obstruction to homogenization. Nevertheless, as the nonlinear effects are uniformly distributed on average, we will still be able to prove that homogenization occurs.

Given a fixed probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\bbP)
, we recall (see, e.g.,
[
20
]
) that a random measure is a measurable function from
\Omega
to the space of locally finite Borel measures on
\mathbb{R}
endowed with the vague topology.
If
\mu
is a random measure and
E\subseteq\mathbb{R}
is a bounded Borel set then
\mu(E)
is a non-negative random variable. We say that
\mu
has independent increments if
\mu(E_{1}),\dots,\mu(E_{n})
are independent random variables for any collection of disjoint bounded Borel sets
E_{1},\dots,E_{n}\subseteq\mathbb{R}
.

A homogeneous Poisson process of unit intensity is a random measure
\mu
with independent increments so that for any bounded Borel set
E\subseteq\mathbb{R}
the random variable
\mu(E)
is Poisson-distributed with mean given by the Lebesgue measure
m(E)
of
E
.
The distribution of a random measure is uniquely determined by its Laplace functional, the map
f\mapsto\bbE\exp(-\int_{\mathbb{R}}f\,d\mu)
, on the set of non-negative
f\in C_{c}(\mathbb{R})
. A random measure
\mu
is, therefore, a homogeneous Poisson process of unit intensity if and only if

\bbE\exp\left(-\int_{\mathbb{R}}f\,d\mu\right)=\exp\left(-\int_{\mathbb{R}}1-e^{-f}\,dx\right),

for all non-negative
f\in C_{c}(\mathbb{R})
.

To investigate the problem of homogenization, we take a sequence of positive numbers
0<\varepsilon_{n}\to 0
and consider a corresponding sequence of solutions
\psi_{n}\colon\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{C}
of the equation

(NLS
n
)

i\partial_{t}\psi_{n}=-\partial_{x}^{2}\psi_{n}+2|\psi_{n}|^{2}\psi_{n}\,d\mu_{n},

where
\mu_{n}
is the unique random measure with Laplace functional

(1.2)

\bbE\exp\biggl(-\int_{\mathbb{R}}f\,d\mu_{n}\biggr)=\exp\biggl(-\tfrac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}1-e^{-\varepsilon_{n}f}\,dx\biggr),

for non-negative
f\in C_{c}(\mathbb{R})
.
The equation (
NLSn
) describes the large scale behavior of (
1.1
): the rescaled random measure
E\mapsto\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}\mu_{n}(\varepsilon_{n}E)
is a homogeneous Poisson process of unit intensity. Consequently,
\psi_{n}(t,x)
solves (
NLSn
) if and only if

\psi(t,x)=\varepsilon_{n}\psi_{n}(\varepsilon_{n}^{2}t,\varepsilon_{n}x)

solves (
1.1
).
It follows from
[
19
, Theorem 1.2]
that the equation (
NLSn
) is almost surely globally well-posed in
H^{1}
.

Our first result proves that the solutions
\psi_{n}
of (
NLSn
) homogenize to the solution
\psi
of (
NLS
):

Theorem 1.1

(Homogenization)
.

Let
0<\varepsilon_{n}\to 0
and
\psi_{0}\in H^{1}
. If
\psi_{n}\in C(\mathbb{R};H^{1})
is the almost surely defined solution of (
NLSn
) with initial data
\psi_{n}(0)=\psi_{0}
and
\psi\in C(\mathbb{R};H^{1})
is the solution of (
NLS
) with initial data
\psi(0)=\psi_{0}
then, for all
T>0
and
1\leq p<\infty
, we have
\psi_{n}\to\psi
in
L^{p}\bigl(d\bbP;C\bigl([-T,T];H^{1}\bigr)\bigr)
, i.e.,

(1.3)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\bbE\sup_{|t|\leq T}\|\psi_{n}(t)-\psi(t)\|_{H^{1}}^{p}=0.

Theorem
1.1
tells us that, for a fixed choice of initial data and compact time interval, the solution
\psi_{n}
of (
NLSn
) is well-approximated on average by the solution
\psi
of (
NLS
), provided
n\gg 1
is sufficiently large. A natural next question is whether we can describe how the solutions
\psi_{n}
fluctuate about the homogenized solution
\psi
.

Denoting Lebesgue measure by
m
, we may formally write

\mu_{n}=m+\sqrt{\varepsilon_{n}}\nu_{n},\quad\text{with}\quad\nu_{n}=\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{\varepsilon_{n}}}(\mu_{n}-m).

Considering
\nu_{n}
to be a (Schwartz-)distribution-valued random variable, we may use (
1.2
) to see that
\nu_{n}
converges in distribution to white noise, the distribution-valued random variable
\xi
for which

\bbE\exp\bigl(i\langle f,\xi\rangle\bigr)=\exp\bigl(-\tfrac{1}{2}\|f\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\bigr),

for all real-valued
f\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
.
Formally expanding (
NLSn
) in powers of
\sqrt{\varepsilon_{n}}
now shows us that

(1.4)

\varphi_{n}=\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{\varepsilon_{n}}}(\psi_{n}-\psi)

should converge (in some sense) to a solution of the equation

(1.5)

i\partial_{t}\varphi=-\partial_{x}^{2}\varphi+4|\psi|^{2}\varphi+2\psi^{2}\bar{\varphi}+2|\psi|^{2}\psi\,\xi,

with initial data
\varphi(0)=0
.

To make this heuristic discussion rigorous, we first prove that solutions of (
1.5
) are well-defined:

Proposition 1.2
.

If
\psi\in C(\mathbb{R};H^{1})
and
s>\frac{1}{2}
then there almost surely exists a unique solution
\varphi\in C(\mathbb{R};H^{-s})
of the Duhamel formulation,

(1.6)

\varphi(t)=-i\int_{0}^{t}e^{i(t-\tau)\partial_{x}^{2}}\Bigl[4|\psi(\tau)|^{2}\varphi(\tau)+2\psi(\tau)^{2}\overline{\varphi(\tau)}+2|\psi(\tau)|^{2}\psi(\tau)\,\xi\Bigr]\,d\tau,

of (
1.5
) with initial data
\varphi(0)=0
.

Our second main result proves the convergence of
\varphi_{n}
to
\varphi
:

Theorem 1.3

(Fluctuations)
.

Let
s>\frac{1}{2}
,
0<\varepsilon_{n}\to 0
, and
\psi_{0}\in H^{1}
. Let
\psi_{n}\in C(\mathbb{R};H^{1})
be the almost surely defined solution of (
NLSn
) with initial data
\psi_{n}(0)=\psi_{0}
and
\psi\in C(\mathbb{R};H^{1})
be the solution of (
NLS
) with initial data
\psi(0)=\psi_{0}
. Let
\varphi_{n}
be defined as in (
1.4
) and
\varphi\in C(\mathbb{R};H^{-s})
be the almost surely defined solution of (
1.5
) with initial data
\varphi(0)=0
. Then, for any
T>0
the sequence
\varphi_{n}
converges to
\varphi
in distribution as
C\bigl([-T,T];H^{-s}\bigr)
-valued random variables.

Homogenization problems in PDE abound, especially in the context of elliptic equations.
Early works, such as
[
12
]
, focused on homogenization for deterministic linear elliptic operators with periodic coefficients.
The study of stochastic homogenization, where the operator is taken to have random coefficients, was initiated shortly thereafter in
[
27
,
23
]
. Nonlinear problems were subsequently considered in
[
11
,
10
]
.
We refer the reader to the recent article
[
2
]
for an introduction to elliptic homogenization and a discussion of known results.

Homogenization problems for (time-dependent) Schrödinger-type equations have largely focused on linear models. As in the elliptic case, authors have considered both periodic homogenization
[
4
,
1
,
29
,
13
,
16
,
5
,
34
,
33
,
15
,
14
]
and stochastic homogenization
[
37
,
36
]
.

By contrast, homogenization for nonlinear Schrödinger equations has received relatively little attention.
In
[
32
]
, the author considered a nonlinear Schrödinger equation with a periodic potential and proved homogenization for well-prepared initial data, providing a nonlinear analog of the effective mass theorems in
[
1
,
29
]
.

To the best of our knowledge, homogenization questions of the type considered in this article, where the inhomogeneity arises from the nonlinear interactions, have only been considered in previous work of the second author
[
26
]
. There, subtle perturbation techniques in critical spaces were used to derive a sufficient condition for the coupling function
g(x)
to ensure homogenization of the equation

i\partial_{t}\psi_{n}=-\Delta\psi_{n}+g(nx)|\psi_{n}|^{2}\psi_{n}

in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
.
While the sufficient condition obtained in
[
26
]
was general enough to encompass several physically motivated examples, both deterministic and random, an important limitation was that it was only applicable to bounded coupling functions, usually of periodic nature. Achieving homogenization in the unbounded setting, when defects are allowed to concentrate, is one of the key contributions of this paper.

While we are primarily interested in the case that
\mu
is a homogeneous Poisson process, an inspection of the proof of Theorems
1.1
and
1.3
shows that our arguments apply, essentially without modification, to a much larger class of random measures (as do the results of
[
19
]
). In particular, the homogenized equation (
NLS
) and the equation for the fluctuations (
1.5
) are, in a sense, universal. With this perspective, one may view Theorem
1.3
as a central-limit-type theorem. Similar questions about the structure of random perturbations in homogenization problems have been studied in both the elliptic and parabolic contexts (see, e.g.,
[
6
,
18
,
3
,
17
]
) but never, as far as we are aware, in the Schrödinger case.

Let us now outline the proof of our main results. Given a non-negative sequence
Z\colon\mathbb{Z}\to[0,\infty)
, we denote

(1.7)

\|f\|_{L_{Z}^{2}}^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}|f(x)|^{2}\,\omega(x;Z)\,dx,

where the piecewise linear weight

(1.8)

\omega(x;Z)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\Bigl[(1+k-x)\,|\mathcal{N}(k;Z)|^{2}+(x-k)\,|\mathcal{N}({k+1};Z)|^{2}\Bigr]\mathbbm{1}_{[k,k+1)}(x),

and the slowly varying envelope
\mathcal{N}(k;Z)>0
for the sequence
Z
is given by

(1.9)

|\mathcal{N}(k;Z)|^{2}=4+\sup_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}}\Bigl[|Z(\ell)|^{2}-|k-\ell|\Bigr].

We will use this construction with two particular choices of the sequence
Z
. Precisely, for each sample of our random measure
\mu_{n}
we take
X_{n}^{1}\subseteq H^{1}
to consist of all
f\in H^{1}
with finite norm

(1.10)

\|f\|_{X_{n}^{1}}^{2}=\|f\|_{H^{1}}^{2}+\|f\|_{L_{Z_{n}}^{2}}^{2},\quad\text{where}\quad Z_{n}(k)=\mu_{n}\bigl([k-\tfrac{1}{2},k+\tfrac{1}{2})\bigr),

and for
\frac{1}{2}<s\leq 1
we take
Y_{n,s}^{1}\subseteq X_{n}^{1}
to consist of all
f\in X_{n}^{1}
with finite norm

(1.11)

\|f\|_{Y_{n,s}^{1}}^{2}=\|f\|_{X_{n}^{1}}^{2}+\|f\|_{L_{Z_{n,s}}^{2}}^{2},\quad\text{where}\quad Z_{n,s}(k)=\bigl\|\rho_{k}\tfrac{d\mu_{n}-1}{\sqrt{\varepsilon_{n}}}\bigr\|_{H^{-s}},

and
1=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\rho_{k}
is a partition of unity so that
\rho_{k}(x)=\rho(x-k)
for a nonnegative function
\rho\in C_{c}^{\infty}(-1,1)
.
Here, and throughout, we distinguish between the interpretation of
\mu_{n}
as a measure and as a (Schwartz) distribution by writing
d\mu_{n}
in the latter case. In particular, for
f\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
we have

\langle f,d\mu_{n}-1\rangle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}f\,d\mu_{n}-\int_{\mathbb{R}}f\,dx.

For a fixed sample of the random measure
\mu_{n}
, the results of
[
19
]
prove that the equation (
NLSn
) is globally well-posed in
X_{n}^{1}
; see Lemma
3.1
below. Crucially, we will prove in Corollary
2.5
that if
\psi_{0}\in H^{1}
then
\psi_{0}\in\bigcap_{n\geq 1}X_{n}^{1}
almost surely and hence the sequence of solutions
\psi_{n}
is almost surely well-defined.

To prove Theorem
1.1
, we start by expressing the difference

(1.12)

\displaystyle\psi_{n}(t)-\psi(t)

\displaystyle=-2i\int_{0}^{t}e^{i(t-\tau)\partial_{x}^{2}}\Bigl[\bigl(|\psi_{n}(\tau)|^{2}\psi_{n}(\tau)-|\psi(\tau)|^{2}\psi(\tau)\bigr)\,d\mu_{n}\Bigr]\,d\tau

\displaystyle\quad-2i\int_{0}^{t}e^{i(t-\tau)\partial_{x}^{2}}\Bigl[|\psi(\tau)|^{2}\psi(\tau)\,\bigl(d\mu_{n}-1\bigr)\Bigr]\,d\tau.

We then consider a similar circle of estimates to those used in the proof of well-posedness in
[
19
]
; see also
[
22
,
8
]
. Precisely, we first bound the difference
\psi_{n}(t)-\psi(t)
in
L^{\infty}
, then in
H^{-1}
, and then use conservation of mass and energy to bound the difference of the
H^{1}
-norms of
\psi_{n}(t)
and
\psi(t)
.
Finally, we upgrade to convergence in
H^{1}
using the Radon–Riesz Theorem.

While the first term on
RHS
(
1.12
) may be controlled using similar estimates to
[
19
]
, the second term requires us to estimate
\psi(t)
in the new space
Y_{n,s}^{1}
.
To prove this estimate, we must establish two key facts.

The first is that the argument used to obtain a priori estimates in
[
19
]
can be extended to estimate
\psi
in the space
L^{p}\bigl(d\bbP;C\bigl([-T,T];L_{Z}^{2}\bigr)\bigr)
for any random sequence
Z
satisfying the uniform bound

\sup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\bbE|Z(k)|^{6p}\lesssim_{p}1.

This fact is behind the a priori estimates obtained in Corollary
2.8
and Lemma
3.2
.

The second fact we require is that the moments of the sequence
Z_{n,s}(k)
can be bounded uniformly in both
n
and
k
. This is proved in Proposition
2.7
by considering a sequence of centered random variables,

X_{N,k}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}e_{N,k}\,d\mu_{n}-\int_{\mathbb{R}}e_{N,k}\,dx,

where
e_{N,k}
is a Haar basis of
L^{2}
. By computing the joint cumulants of products of these random variables, we can apply the moment-cumulant formula to estimate the moments.

Turning to the proof of Theorem
1.3
, we first note that Proposition
1.2
follows from the well-posedness of the linearization of (
NLS
) about the solution
\psi
.

To prove that
\varphi_{n}
converges to
\varphi
in distribution, we must prove that for all bounded continuous functions
F\colon C([-T,T];H^{-s})\to\mathbb{C}
we have

\bbE F(\varphi_{n})\to\bbE F(\varphi),

or equivalently that the corresponding probability distributions,

(\varphi_{n})_{*}\bbP(E)=\bbP\bigl[\varphi_{n}\in E\bigr],

converge weakly to
\varphi_{*}\bbP
in the space of Borel probability measures on
C([-T,T];H^{-s})
. To achieve this, we will first prove (see Proposition
4.5
) that our sequence of probability measures is tight. Prokhorov’s Theorem then ensures that the sequence is weakly (sequentially) precompact. To complete the proof we will show that for every bounded linear functional
\mathcal{T}\colon C([-T,T];H^{-s})\to\mathbb{C}
the characteristic functional satisfies

(1.13)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\bbE e^{i\operatorname{Re}\mathcal{T}(\varphi_{n})}=\bbE e^{i\operatorname{Re}\mathcal{T}(\varphi)}.

This proves that the only weak limit point of the sequence of measures
(\varphi_{n})_{*}\bbP
is
\varphi_{*}\bbP
, thereby completing the proof.

The main technical ingredient in this argument is a collection of a priori estimates for
\varphi_{n}
under the hypothesis that both
\psi_{n}
and
\psi
are bounded in
C\bigl([-T,T];Y_{n,s}^{1}\bigr)
by a given constant. These follow from refining the
H^{-1}
estimates for (
1.12
) used in the proof of Theorem
1.1
.
Crucially, we now require estimates for
\psi_{n}
in
L^{p}\bigl(d\bbP;C\bigl([-T,T];Y_{n,s}^{1}\bigr)\bigr)
, not just
\psi
. Fortunately, our proof of the corresponding estimate for
\psi
is sufficiently robust that it can be applied to
\psi_{n}
with essentially no modifications.

The remainder of this article is structured as follows: in Section
2
we introduce some notation and prove several preliminary estimates. This includes the key estimate (
2.21
) that yields uniform bounds for the moments of
Z_{n,s}(k)
. Section
3
is dedicated to the proof of Theorem
1.1
, with the main estimate for the difference
\psi_{n}-\psi
appearing in Proposition
3.3
. Finally, in Section
4
we first prove Proposition
1.2
and then Theorem
1.3
. We conclude with Appendix
A
, in which we discuss the construction and properties of the linear propagator for the linearization of (
NLS
).

2.
Preliminaries

We start by introducing some notation. Given a Banach space
X
of functions on
\mathbb{R}
, a time
T>0
, and
f\in L^{p}\bigl((-T,T);X\bigr)
for
1\leq p<\infty
, we denote the norm

\|f\|_{L_{T}^{p}X}=\biggl[\int_{-T}^{T}\|f(t)\|_{X}^{p}\,dt\biggr]^{\frac{1}{p}}.

We extend this notation to continuous functions
f\in C\bigl([-T,T];X\bigr)
by writing

\|f\|_{C_{T}X}=\sup_{|t|\leq T}\|f(t)\|_{X}.

We take
\chi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
to be a fixed even function satisfying
0\leq\chi\leq 1
that is identically
1
on
[-1,1]
and supported on
(-2,2)
. Given
R>0
, we rescale to obtain
\chi_{R}(x)=\chi\bigl(\tfrac{x}{R}\bigr)
.

We normalize the Fourier transform by setting

\hat{f}(\xi)=\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(x)e^{-ix\xi}\,dx,

so that for
s\in\mathbb{R}
we have

\|f\|_{H^{s}}^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\langle\xi\rangle^{2s}|\hat{f}(\xi)|^{2}\,d\xi,

where
\langle\xi\rangle^{2}=1+|\xi|^{2}
. Given dyadic
N\geq 1
, we denote the Littlewood–Paley projection

P_{\leq N}f=\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\chi_{N}(\xi)\hat{f}(\xi)e^{ix\xi}\,d\xi,

and set
P_{>N}=1-P_{\leq N}
.

We recall that for all
s>\frac{1}{2}
we have
H^{s}(\mathbb{R})\subseteq C_{0}(\mathbb{R})\subseteq L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
, with the Gagliardo–Nirenberg inequality

(2.1)

\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{2}}^{1-\frac{1}{2s}}\|f\|_{H^{s}}^{\frac{1}{2s}}.

If
k\geq 0
is an integer, we may apply the Leibniz rule to bound

\|fg\|_{H^{k}}\lesssim\|f\|_{W^{k,\infty}}\|g\|_{H^{k}}.

By interpolation and duality, we may then show that for all
|s|\leq k
we have the product estimate

(2.2)

\|fg\|_{H^{s}}\lesssim\|f\|_{W^{k,\infty}}\|g\|_{H^{s}}.

A second product estimate that we will use frequently is:

Lemma 2.1
.

For all
|s|\leq 1
we have

(2.3)

\|fg\|_{H^{s}}\lesssim\|f\|_{H^{1}}\|g\|_{H^{s}}.

Proof.

For
s=0
, we apply (
2.1
) to bound

\|fg\|_{L^{2}}\leq\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}\|g\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\|f\|_{H^{1}}\|g\|_{L^{2}},

and for
s=1
we combine this with the estimate

\|(fg)_{x}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\|f_{x}\|_{L^{2}}\|g\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}\|g_{x}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\|f\|_{H^{1}}\|g\|_{H^{1}}.

For
0<s<1
, we choose
2<p,q<\infty
so that
\frac{1}{2}=\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}
and
s-\frac{1}{2}\leq\frac{1}{p}\leq s
. We then apply the fractional Leibniz rule to bound

\|fg\|_{H^{s}}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}\|g\|_{H^{s}}+\|\langle\partial_{x}\rangle^{s}f\|_{L^{p}}\|g\|_{L^{q}}.

The first summand is readily bounded using (
2.1
). For the second, we apply the Hardy–Littlewood–Sobolev inequality to obtain

\|\langle\partial_{x}\rangle^{s}f\|_{L^{p}}\lesssim\|f\|_{H^{s+\frac{1}{q}}}\lesssim\|f\|_{H^{1}}\quad\text{and}\quad\|g\|_{L^{q}}\lesssim\|g\|_{H^{\frac{1}{p}}}\lesssim\|g\|_{H^{s}}.

Finally, for the case
-1\leq s<0
we argue by duality. Given a test function
h\in H^{-s}
, we apply the result for positive
s
to estimate

|\langle fg,h\rangle|\lesssim\|g\|_{H^{s}}\|\bar{f}h\|_{H^{-s}}\lesssim\|g\|_{H^{s}}\|f\|_{H^{1}}\|h\|_{H^{-s}},

which completes the proof of (
2.3
).
∎

Our next lemma characterizes precompactness in the space
C_{T}H^{s}
:

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
T>0
and
s\in\mathbb{R}
. A bounded set
\mathcal{F}\subseteq C\bigl([-T,T];H^{s}\bigr)
is precompact if and only if it is uniformly equicontinuous in time,

(2.4)

\lim_{h\to 0}\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}|t-\tau|\leq h\\
t,\tau\in[-T,T]\end{subarray}}\sup_{f\in\mathcal{F}}\|f(t)-f(\tau)\|_{H^{s}}=0,

and both
H^{s}
-tight and
H^{s}
-equicontinuous in space, uniformly in time,

(2.5)

\displaystyle\lim_{R\to\infty}\sup_{f\in\mathcal{F}}\|(1-\chi_{R})f\|_{C_{T}H^{s}}=0=\lim_{N\to\infty}\sup_{f\in\mathcal{F}}\|P_{>N}f\|_{C_{T}H^{s}}.

Proof.

Let us start by recalling from
[
28
, Theorem 3]
that a bounded set
Q\subseteq L^{2}
is precompact if and only if

(2.6)

\lim_{R\to\infty}\sup_{q\in Q}\|(1-\chi_{R})q\|_{L^{2}}=0=\lim_{N\to\infty}\sup_{q\in Q}\|P_{>N}q\|_{L^{2}}.

To extend this to
H^{s}
, we first use Plancherel’s Theorem to prove that for all
R\geq 1
we have

\displaystyle\Bigl\|\bigl[\langle\partial_{x}\rangle^{s},\chi_{R}\bigr]q\Bigr\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\qquad\simeq\left\|\int_{\mathbb{R}}\hat{\chi}_{R}(\xi-\eta)\bigl[\langle\xi\rangle^{s}-\langle\eta\rangle^{s}\bigr]\hat{q}(\eta)\,d\eta\right\|_{L_{\xi}^{2}}

\displaystyle\qquad\simeq\left\|\int_{\mathbb{R}}\hat{\chi}_{R}(\xi-\eta)\left[\int_{0}^{1}s\langle\eta+h(\xi-\eta)\rangle^{s-2}(\eta+h(\xi-\eta))(\xi-\eta)\,dh\right]\hat{q}(\eta)\,d\eta\right\|_{L_{\xi}^{2}}

(2.7)

\displaystyle\qquad\lesssim\|\langle\xi\rangle^{|s-1|}\xi\,\hat{\chi}_{R}(\xi)\|_{L_{\xi}^{1}}\|\langle\eta\rangle^{s-1}\hat{q}(\eta)\|_{L^{2}_{\eta}}\lesssim\tfrac{1}{R}\|q\|_{H^{s-1}},

where we have used that for all
\eta,\zeta\in\mathbb{R}
we have

\langle\eta+\zeta\rangle^{s-2}|\eta+\zeta|\leq\langle\eta+\zeta\rangle^{s-1}\lesssim\langle\zeta\rangle^{|s-1|}\langle\eta\rangle^{s-1}.

Indeed, when
|\zeta|\ll|\eta|
we have

\langle\eta+\zeta\rangle^{s-1}\simeq\langle\eta\rangle^{s-1}\lesssim\langle\zeta\rangle^{|s-1|}\langle\eta\rangle^{s-1},

whereas when
|\eta|\lesssim|\zeta|
we have

\langle\eta+\zeta\rangle^{s-1}\lesssim\langle\zeta\rangle^{\max\{s-1,0\}}\lesssim\langle\zeta\rangle^{|s-1|}\langle\eta\rangle^{s-1}.

Applying (
2.6
) to
\langle\partial_{x}\rangle^{s}q
with our commutator estimate, we now see that a bounded set
Q\subseteq H^{s}
is precompact if and only if

(2.8)

\lim_{R\to\infty}\sup_{q\in Q}\|(1-\chi_{R})q\|_{H^{s}}=0=\lim_{N\to\infty}\sup_{q\in Q}\|P_{>N}q\|_{H^{s}}.

By the (generalized) Arzelà–Ascoli Theorem, a set
\mathcal{F}\subseteq C\bigl([-T,T];H^{s}\bigr)
is precompact if and only if it satisfies (
2.4
) and for each
t\in[-T,T]
the set

\bigl\{f(t):f\in\mathcal{F}\bigr\}\subseteq H^{s}

is precompact. In light of (
2.8
), it remains to show that if
\mathcal{F}\subseteq C\bigl([-T,T];H^{s}\bigr)
is precompact then we have the uniform-in-time estimates (
2.5
).

Given
\eta>0
, we choose an
\eta
-covering
\{f_{1},\dots,f_{L}\}\subseteq\mathcal{F}
of
\mathcal{F}
and take

Q=\bigcup_{\ell=1}^{L}\bigl\{f_{\ell}(t):t\in[-T,T]\bigr\},

which is a finite union of compact subsets of
H^{s}
and therefore compact. Applying (
2.8
) then yields

\lim_{R\to\infty}\max_{\ell=1,\dots,L}\|(1-\chi_{R})f_{\ell}\|_{C_{T}H^{s}}=0=\lim_{R\to\infty}\max_{\ell=1,\dots,L}\|P_{>N}f_{\ell}\|_{C_{T}H^{s}}.

Given any
f\in\mathcal{F}
, we may then estimate

\displaystyle\|(1-\chi_{R})f\|_{C_{T}H^{s}}

\displaystyle\leq\max_{\ell=1,\dots,L}\|(1-\chi_{R})f_{\ell}\|_{C_{T}H^{s}}+\eta,

\displaystyle\|P_{>N}f\|_{C_{T}H^{s}}

\displaystyle\leq\max_{\ell=1,\dots,L}\|P_{>N}f_{\ell}\|_{C_{T}H^{s}}+\eta,

where we note that the first of these uses (
2.2
). The proof is now completed by taking the supremum over
f\in\mathcal{F}
and then sending
R\to\infty
, respectively
N\to\infty
.
∎

Let us now turn to the weighted space
L_{Z}^{2}
defined in (
1.7
). By construction (see
[
19
, Lemma 2.1]
), we have

\Bigl||\mathcal{N}(k;Z)|^{2}-|\mathcal{N}(k+1;Z)|^{2}\Bigr|\leq 1,

and hence the map
x\mapsto\omega(x;Z)
is
1
-Lipschitz. As a consequence, we have the estimate

(2.9)

\omega(x;Z)\leq|\mathcal{N}(0;Z)|^{2}+|x|,

so if
\mathcal{N}(0;Z)<\infty
then
\mathcal{N}(k;Z)=\sqrt{\omega(k;Z)}<\infty
for all
k\in\mathbb{Z}
.

By definition, we have
Z(k)\leq\mathcal{N}(k;Z)
and we may readily verify that
\mathcal{N}(k;Z)\simeq\mathcal{N}(\ell;Z)
whenever
|k-\ell|\leq 3
. As a consequence, if
1=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\rho_{k}
is the partition of unity appearing in (
1.11
) and
\mathcal{N}(0;Z)<\infty
, then

(2.10)

\|f\|_{L_{Z}^{2}}^{2}\simeq\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}|\mathcal{N}(k;Z)|^{2}\|\rho_{k}f\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

It will also be useful to note that

(2.11)

\|f\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\simeq\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\|\rho_{k}f\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\quad\text{and}\quad\|f\|_{H^{1}}^{2}\simeq\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\|\rho_{k}f\|_{H^{1}}^{2}.

For the particular choices of the sequence
Z
appearing in the definitions (
1.10
) and (
1.11
), we have the following:

Lemma 2.3
.

If
Z_{n}
is defined as in (
1.10
) and
\mathcal{N}(0;Z_{n})<\infty
then for all
f_{1},f_{2}\in X_{n}^{1}
and
g\in C_{0}(\mathbb{R})
we have the estimates

(2.12)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}|f_{1}f_{2}g|\,d\mu_{n}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f_{1}\|_{X_{n}^{1}}\|f_{2}\|_{X_{n}^{1}}\|g\|_{L^{\infty}},

(2.13)

\displaystyle\|f_{1}f_{2}g\,d\mu_{n}\|_{H^{-1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f_{1}\|_{X_{n}^{1}}\|f_{2}\|_{X_{n}^{1}}\|g\|_{L^{\infty}}.

Further, if
\frac{1}{2}<s\leq 1
and
Z_{n,s}
is defined as in (
1.11
) then whenever
\mathcal{N}(0;Z_{n})<\infty
,
\mathcal{N}(0;Z_{n,s})<\infty
, and
f_{1},f_{2},g\in Y_{n,s}^{1}
we have the estimate

(2.14)

\displaystyle\bigl\|f_{1}f_{2}g\tfrac{d\mu_{n}-1}{\sqrt{\varepsilon_{n}}}\bigr\|_{H^{-s}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f_{1}\|_{Y_{n,s}^{1}}\|f_{2}\|_{Y_{n,s}^{1}}\|g\|_{Y_{n,s}^{1}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|g\|_{H^{1}}^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Proof.

The estimates (
2.12
) and (
2.13
) are proved in
[
19
, Lemma 2.3]
.
The estimate (
2.14
) follows from a similar argument that we now give.

Recalling that
Z_{n,s}(k)=\bigl\|\rho_{k}\tfrac{d\mu_{n}-1}{\sqrt{\varepsilon_{n}}}\bigr\|_{H^{-s}}
, we first use the product estimate (
2.3
) to bound

\displaystyle\bigl\|f_{1}f_{2}g\tfrac{d\mu_{n}-1}{\sqrt{\varepsilon_{n}}}\bigr\|_{H^{-s}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1},k_{2},k_{3},k_{4}\in\mathbb{Z}\\
|k_{j}-k_{\ell}|\leq 1\end{subarray}}\|\rho_{k_{1}}f_{1}\times\rho_{k_{2}}f_{2}\times\rho_{k_{3}}g\|_{H^{1}}Z_{n,s}(k_{4}).

Applying (
2.1
), Young’s inequality, and (
2.2
), we have

\displaystyle\|\rho_{k_{1}}f_{1}\times\rho_{k_{2}}f_{2}\times\rho_{k_{3}}g\|_{H^{1}}

\displaystyle\qquad\lesssim\|\rho_{k_{1}}f_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\rho_{k_{2}}f_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\rho_{k_{3}}g\|_{H^{1}}+\|\rho_{k_{1}}f_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\rho_{k_{2}}f_{2}\|_{H^{1}}\|\rho_{k_{3}}g\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\qquad\quad+\|\rho_{k_{1}}f_{1}\|_{H^{1}}\|\rho_{k_{2}}f_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\rho_{k_{3}}g\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\qquad\lesssim\sum_{j=1}^{2}\|\rho_{k_{j}}f_{j}\|_{L^{2}}\|\rho_{k_{1-j}}f_{1-j}\|_{H^{1}}\|g\|_{H^{1}}

\displaystyle\qquad\quad+\sum_{j=1}^{2}\|\rho_{k_{j}}f_{j}\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\rho_{k_{j}}f_{j}\|_{H^{1}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\rho_{k_{3}}g\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\rho_{k_{3}}g\|_{H^{1}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|f_{1-j}\|_{H^{1}}.

Recalling that if
|k-k_{4}|\leq 1
then
Z_{n,s}(k_{4})\lesssim\mathcal{N}(k;Z_{n,s})
, we may combine these estimates with Hölder’s inequality, (
2.10
), and (
2.11
) to get

\displaystyle\bigl\|f_{1}f_{2}g\tfrac{d\mu_{n}-1}{\sqrt{\varepsilon_{n}}}\bigr\|_{H^{-s}}

\displaystyle\qquad\lesssim\sum_{j=1}^{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1},k_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}\\
|k_{1}-k_{2}|\leq 1\end{subarray}}\mathcal{N}(k_{1};Z_{n,s})\|\rho_{k_{1}}f_{j}\|_{L^{2}}\|\rho_{k_{2}}f_{1-j}\|_{H^{1}}\|g\|_{H^{1}}

\displaystyle\qquad\quad+\sum_{j=1}^{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1},k_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}\\
|k_{1}-k_{2}|\leq 1\end{subarray}}\mathcal{N}(k_{1};Z_{n,s})^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\rho_{k_{1}}f_{j}\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\rho_{k_{1}}f_{j}\|_{H^{1}}^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\qquad\quad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\times\mathcal{N}(k_{2};Z_{n,s})^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\rho_{k_{2}}g\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\rho_{k_{2}}g\|_{H^{1}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|f_{1-j}\|_{H^{1}}

\displaystyle\qquad\lesssim\sum_{j=1}^{2}\left[\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}|\mathcal{N}(k;Z_{n,s})|^{2}\|\rho_{k}f_{j}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}\left[\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\|\rho_{k}f_{1-j}\|_{H^{1}}^{2}\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}\|g\|_{H^{1}}

\displaystyle\qquad\quad+\sum_{j=1}^{2}\left[\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}|\mathcal{N}(k;Z_{n,s})|^{2}\|\rho_{k}f_{j}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right]^{\frac{1}{4}}\left[\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\|\rho_{k}f_{j}\|_{H^{1}}^{2}\right]^{\frac{1}{4}}

\displaystyle\qquad\quad\qquad\qquad\times\left[\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}|\mathcal{N}(k;Z_{n,s})|^{2}\|\rho_{k}g\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right]^{\frac{1}{4}}\left[\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\|\rho_{k}g\|_{H^{1}}^{2}\right]^{\frac{1}{4}}\|f_{1-j}\|_{H^{1}}

\displaystyle\qquad\lesssim\sum_{j=1}^{2}\Bigl[\|f_{j}\|_{L_{Z_{n,s}}^{2}}\|f_{1-j}\|_{H^{1}}\|g\|_{H^{1}}+\|f_{j}\|_{L_{Z_{n,s}}^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|f_{j}\|_{H^{1}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|g\|_{L_{Z_{n,s}}^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|g\|_{H^{1}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|f_{1-j}\|_{H^{1}}\Bigr].

The estimate (
2.14
) now follows from the definition (
1.11
) of
Y_{n,s}^{1}
.
∎

We recall that the distribution of a random measure is uniquely determined by its Laplace functionals. Consequently,
\mu_{n}
is the unique random measure with independent increments so that
\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}\mu_{n}(E)
is Poisson distributed with mean
\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}m(E)
for all bounded Borel sets
E\subseteq\mathbb{R}
. This implies that (
1.2
) holds for all (complex-valued)
f\in C_{c}(\mathbb{R})
, and even all bounded, compactly supported, Borel-measurable
f\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{C}
. These facts will be key to Corollaries
2.5
,
2.8
and Proposition
2.7
, which lie at the heart of the proof of Theorems
1.1
and
1.3
.

Let us start with the following:

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
Z\colon\mathbb{Z}\to[0,\infty)
be a sequence of non-negative random variables so that for all
p\geq 1
we have the estimate

(2.15)

\sup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\bbE|Z(k)|^{6p}\lesssim_{p}1.

Then for all
p\geq 1
and
f\in L^{2}
we have

(2.16)

\displaystyle\sup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\bbE|\mathcal{N}(k;Z)|^{2p}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{p}1,

(2.17)

\displaystyle\bbE\|f\|_{L_{Z}^{2}}^{2p}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{p}\|f\|_{L^{2}}^{2p}.

Proof.

Proceeding as in
[
19
, Lemma 4.1]
, we apply Young’s inequality to bound

|Z(\ell)|^{2}|k-\ell|^{2}\leq\tfrac{4}{27}|Z(\ell)|^{6}+|k-\ell|^{3},

so that whenever
k\neq\ell
we have

|Z(\ell)|^{2}-|k-\ell|\lesssim\frac{|Z(\ell)|^{6}}{\langle k-\ell\rangle^{2}}.

Recalling the definition (
1.9
), for all
p\geq 1
we may then estimate

|\mathcal{N}(k;Z)|^{2p}\lesssim_{p}1+\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{|Z(\ell)|^{6p}}{\langle k-\ell\rangle^{2p}},

and applying (
2.15
) yields

\sup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\bbE|\mathcal{N}(k;Z)|^{2p}\lesssim_{p}1+\sup_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}}\bbE|Z(\ell)|^{6p}\lesssim_{p}1,

which proves (
2.16
).

Further, from (
2.10
) and (
2.11
) we have

\displaystyle\Bigl[\bbE\|f\|_{L_{Z}^{2}}^{2p}\Bigr]^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\simeq\Biggl[\bbE\Biggl|\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}|\mathcal{N}(k;Z)|^{2}\|\rho_{k}f\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\Biggr|^{p}\Biggr]^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\Bigl[\bbE|\mathcal{N}(k;Z)|^{2p}\Bigr]^{\frac{1}{p}}\|\rho_{k}f\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\lesssim_{p}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\|\rho_{k}f\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\lesssim_{p}\|f\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

which completes the proof of (
2.17
).
∎

We now apply this to obtain:

Corollary 2.5
.

Suppose that for all
n\geq 1
we have
0<\varepsilon_{n}\leq 1
. Taking
Z_{n}
to be defined as in (
1.10
), we almost surely have
\mathcal{N}(0;Z_{n})<\infty
for all
n\geq 1
.

Further, if
f\in H^{1}
and
p\geq 1
then

(2.18)

\displaystyle\sup_{n\geq 1}\bbE\|f\|_{X_{n}^{1}}^{2p}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{p}\|f\|_{H^{1}}^{2p}.

In particular, we almost surely have
f\in\bigcap_{n\geq 1}X_{n}^{1}
.

Proof.

We apply Lemma
2.4
with
Z=Z_{n}
. As
\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}Z_{n}(k)
is a Poisson-distributed random variable with mean
\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}
, the moment generating function

\bbE\exp\bigl[sZ_{n}(k)\bigr]=\exp\bigl[\tfrac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}(e^{\varepsilon_{n}s}-1)\bigr].

As
0<\varepsilon_{n}\leq 1
, if
p\geq 1
is an integer then

\bbE|Z_{n}(k)|^{6p}=\partial_{s}^{6p}\bbE\exp\bigl[sZ_{n}(k)\bigr]\Bigr|_{s=0}\lesssim_{p}1.

By interpolation, we may extend this to all
p\geq 1
to obtain

\sup_{n\geq 1}\sup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\bbE|Z_{n}(k)|^{6p}\lesssim_{p}1.

The estimate (
2.16
) now ensures that almost surely
\mathcal{N}(0;Z_{n})<\infty
for all
n\geq 1
. The estimate (
2.18
) follows from (
2.17
) and implies that
f\in\bigcap_{n\geq 1}X_{n}^{1}
almost surely.
∎

For dyadic
N\geq 1
and integers
k\in\mathbb{Z}
, we denote the dyadic interval

I_{N,k}=\bigl[\tfrac{k}{N},\tfrac{k+1}{N}\bigr).

We define the associated Haar functions
e_{N,k}
by taking
e_{1,k}=\mathbbm{1}_{I_{1,k}}
and

e_{N,k}=\sqrt{\tfrac{N}{2}}\bigl(\mathbbm{1}_{I_{N,2k}}-\mathbbm{1}_{I_{N,2k+1}}\bigr)\quad\text{for $N\geq 2$}.

We then have:

Lemma 2.6
.

If
f\in C_{c}(\mathbb{R})
then the partial sums

(2.19)

\sum_{1\leq N\leq N_{0}}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\langle f,e_{N,k}\rangle e_{N,k}

converge uniformly to
f
as
N_{0}\to\infty
.

Proof.

Let

E_{N}f=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}N\langle f,\mathbbm{1}_{I_{N,k}}\rangle\mathbbm{1}_{I_{N,k}},

where we note that as
f
has compact support the sum in
k
is finite.

For all
N\geq 2
we may decompose

I_{\frac{N}{2},k}=I_{N,2k}\sqcup I_{N,2k+1}
,
and hence

\displaystyle E_{N}f-E_{\frac{N}{2}}f

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\biggl\{\Bigl[N\langle f,\mathbbm{1}_{I_{N,2k}}\rangle-\tfrac{N}{2}\langle f,\mathbbm{1}_{I_{\frac{N}{2},k}}\rangle\Bigr]\mathbbm{1}_{N,2k}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\Bigl[N\langle f,\mathbbm{1}_{I_{N,2k+1}}\rangle-\tfrac{N}{2}\langle f,\mathbbm{1}_{I_{\frac{N}{2},k}}\rangle\Bigr]\mathbbm{1}_{N,2k+1}\biggr\}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\langle f,e_{N,k}\rangle e_{N,k}.

Consequently, for all
N_{0}\geq 2
we have

E_{N_{0}}f=E_{1}f+\sum_{2\leq N\leq N_{0}}\bigl[E_{N}f-E_{\frac{N}{2}}f\bigr]=\sum_{1\leq N\leq N_{0}}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\langle f,e_{N,k}\rangle e_{N,k}.

As
f
is uniformly continuous on
\mathbb{R}
, for all
\eta>0
we may find some dyadic
N_{1}\geq 2
so that whenever
|x-y|\leq\frac{1}{N_{1}}
we have

|f(x)-f(y)|\leq\eta.

For all dyadic
N_{0}\geq N_{1}
and all
x\in\mathbb{R}
we may then bound

\displaystyle\left|f(x)-\sum_{1\leq N\leq N_{0}}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\langle f,e_{N,k}\rangle e_{N,k}(x)\right|

\displaystyle=|f(x)-E_{N_{0}}f(x)|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}N_{0}\left[\int_{I_{N_{0},k}}|f(x)-f(y)|\,dy\right]\mathbbm{1}_{I_{N_{0},k}}(x)

\displaystyle\leq\eta,

which suffices to prove that (
2.19
) converges to
f
uniformly on
\mathbb{R}
.
∎

For dyadic
N_{1},N_{2}\geq 1
and any
k_{1},k_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}
, we compute that

(2.20)

\langle e_{N_{1},k_{1}},e_{N_{2},k_{2}}\rangle=\delta_{N_{1},N_{2}}\delta_{k_{1},k_{2}},

so
(e_{N,k})
is an orthonormal subset of
L^{2}
. In particular, Bessel’s inequality ensures that for all
\psi\in L^{2}
the series

\sum_{N\geq 1}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\langle\psi,e_{N,k}\rangle e_{N,k}

converges absolutely in
L^{2}
. Further, as
C_{c}(\mathbb{R})
is dense in
L^{2}
, Lemma
2.6
shows that the set
(e_{N,k})
is a Hilbert basis for
L^{2}
.

We are now in a position to prove the following:

Proposition 2.7
.

Suppose that
0<\varepsilon_{n}\leq 1
. Then, for all
s>\frac{1}{2}
,
\psi\in H^{1}
, and
p\geq 1
we have the estimate

(2.21)

\bbE\|\psi(d\mu_{n}-1)\|_{H^{-s}}^{2p}\lesssim_{p,s}\varepsilon_{n}^{p}\|\psi\|_{H^{1}}^{2p}.

Proof.

Let us fix the value of
n\geq 1
and denote the random variables

X_{N,k}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}e_{N,k}\,d\mu_{n}-\int_{\mathbb{R}}e_{N,k}\,dx.

As
\bbE\mu_{n}
is Lebesgue measure, we have

(2.22)

\bbE X_{N,k}=0.

If
J\geq 2
then given
s_{1},\dots,s_{J}\in\mathbb{R}
, dyadic
N_{1},\dots N_{J}\geq 1
, and integers
k_{1},\dots,k_{J}\in\mathbb{Z}
, we may use that (
1.2
) extends to all bounded, compactly supported, Borel-measurable
f\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{C}
to obtain

\log\bbE\exp\Biggl[\sum_{j=1}^{J}s_{j}X_{N_{j},k_{j}}\Biggr]=\tfrac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Phi\Biggl(\varepsilon_{n}\sum_{j=1}^{J}s_{j}e_{N_{j},k_{j}}\Biggr)\,dx,

where
\Phi(x)=e^{x}-1-x
. Consequently, the joint cumulant

\displaystyle\kappa\bigl(X_{N_{1},k_{1}},\dots,X_{N_{J},k_{J}}\bigr)

\displaystyle=\partial_{s_{1}}\dots\partial_{s_{J}}\log\bbE\exp\Biggl[\sum_{j=1}^{J}s_{j}X_{N_{j},k_{j}}\Biggr]\Biggr|_{s_{1}=\dots=s_{J}=0}

(2.23)

\displaystyle=\varepsilon_{n}^{J-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\prod_{j=1}^{J}e_{N_{j},k_{j}}\,dx.

By combining (
2.22
) and (
2.23
) with (
2.20
), we obtain the identity

\bbE\bigl[X_{N_{1},k_{2}}X_{N_{2},k_{2}}\bigr]=\operatorname{cov}\bigl(X_{N_{1},k_{2}},X_{N_{2},k_{2}}\bigr)=\kappa\bigl(X_{N_{1},k_{1}},X_{N_{2},k_{2}}\bigr)=\varepsilon_{n}\delta_{N_{1},N_{2}}\delta_{k_{1},k_{2}},

so the random variables
\frac{1}{\sqrt{\varepsilon n}}X_{N,k}
are orthonormal.

To control the joint cumulants for larger values of
J
, we assume (without loss of generality) that
N_{1}\geq N_{2}\geq\dots\geq N_{J}
and denote

k_{j}^{\prime}=k_{j}^{\prime}(N_{1},k_{1},N_{j})=\begin{cases}k_{1}&\text{if $N_{1}=1$},\\
\bigl\lfloor\frac{2k_{1}}{N_{1}}\bigr\rfloor&\text{if $1=N_{j}<N_{1}$},\\
\bigl\lfloor\frac{N_{j}k_{1}}{N_{1}}\bigr\rfloor&\text{if $1<N_{j}\leq N_{1}$}.\end{cases}

We then see that the supports of
e_{N_{1},k_{1}}
and
e_{N_{j},k_{j}}
are disjoint unless
k_{j}=k_{j}^{\prime}
. Further, if
N_{j}<N_{1}
then
e_{N_{j},k_{j}}
is constant on the support of
e_{N_{1},k_{1}}
. In particular, if
N_{2}<N_{1}
then

\int_{\mathbb{R}}e_{N_{1},k_{1}}\prod_{j=2}^{J}e_{N_{j},k_{j}^{\prime}}\,dx=0.

Combining these facts, we arrive at the inequality

(2.24)

\bigl|\kappa\bigl(X_{N_{1},k_{1}},\dots,X_{N_{J},k_{J}}\bigr)\bigr|\leq\varepsilon_{n}^{J-1}\delta_{N_{1},N_{2}}\delta_{k_{1},k_{2}}\prod_{j=3}^{J}\delta_{k_{j},k_{j}^{\prime}}N_{j}^{\frac{1}{2}},

whenever
J\geq 2
.

Next, we define the operator

K=\langle\partial_{x}\rangle^{-s}\psi,

and apply
[
31
, Theorem 4.1]
to estimate

(2.25)

\sum_{N\geq 1}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\|Ke_{N,k}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=\|K\|_{\mathfrak{I}_{2}}^{2}\leq\|\langle\xi\rangle^{-s}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\|\psi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\lesssim\|\psi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

For
N\geq 2
we compute that

\int_{-\infty}^{x}e_{N,k}(y)\,dy=\sqrt{\tfrac{N}{2}}\bigl(x-\tfrac{2k}{N}\bigr)\mathbbm{1}_{I_{N,2k}}(x)-\sqrt{\tfrac{N}{2}}\bigl(x-\tfrac{2k+2}{N}\bigr)\mathbbm{1}_{I_{N,2k+1}}(x),

and hence

\|e_{N,k}\|_{H^{-1}}\lesssim\left\|\int_{-\infty}^{x}e_{N,k}(y)\,dy\right\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\tfrac{1}{N}.

Using also that
\|e_{1,k}\|_{H^{-1}}\lesssim\|e_{1,k}\|_{L^{2}}=1
, we may then interpolate to obtain

\|e_{N,k}\|_{H^{-s}}\lesssim_{s}\|e_{N,k}\|_{H^{-1}}^{s}\|e_{N,k}\|_{L^{2}}^{1-s}\lesssim_{s}N^{-s},

for all
N\geq 1
. Applying (
2.3
), we may then bound

(2.26)

\|Ke_{N,k}\|_{L^{2}}=\|\psi e_{N,k}\|_{H^{-s}}\lesssim_{s}\|\psi\|_{H^{1}}\|e_{N,k}\|_{H^{-s}}\lesssim_{s}N^{-s}\|\psi\|_{H^{1}}.

For a positive integer
J\geq 1
, let us now denote

S(J)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(N_{1},k_{1}),\dots,(N_{J},k_{J})\\
N_{1}\geq\dots\geq N_{J}\end{subarray}}\bigl|\kappa\bigl(X_{N_{1},k_{1}},\dots,X_{N_{J},k_{J}}\bigr)\bigr|\prod_{j=1}^{J}\|Ke_{N_{j},k_{j}}\|_{L^{2}}.

From (
2.22
), we have
S(1)=0
. For
J\geq 2
we instead use (
2.24
), (
2.25
), and (
2.26
) to bound

\displaystyle S(J)

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon_{n}^{J-1}\sum_{(N_{1},k_{1})}\|Ke_{N_{1},k_{1}}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\sum_{N_{3},\dots,N_{J}}\prod_{j=3}^{J}N_{j}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|Ke_{N_{j},k_{j}^{\prime}}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{J,s}\varepsilon_{n}^{J-1}\|\psi\|_{H^{1}}^{J}\sum_{N_{3},\dots,N_{J}}\prod_{j=3}^{J}N_{j}^{\frac{1}{2}-s}

(2.27)

\displaystyle\lesssim_{J,s}\varepsilon_{n}^{J-1}\|\psi\|_{H^{1}}^{J}.

Given an integer
p\geq 1
, the moment-cumulant formula (see, e.g.,
[
25
]
) tells us that

\bbE\prod_{j=1}^{2p}X_{N_{j},k_{j}}=\sum_{P\in\mathcal{P}_{2p}}\prod_{B\in P}\kappa\bigl((X_{N_{j},k_{j}})_{j\in B}\bigr),

where
\mathcal{P}_{2p}
is the set of all partitions of the set
\{1,\dots,2p\}
.
Combining this with (
2.27
) and using that
\varepsilon_{n}\leq 1
, we obtain the estimate

(2.28)

\displaystyle\sum_{(N_{1},k_{1}),\dots,(N_{2p},k_{2p})}\Biggl|\bbE\prod_{j=1}^{2p}X_{N_{j},k_{j}}\Biggr|\prod_{\ell=1}^{2p}\|Ke_{N_{\ell},k_{\ell}}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{p}\sum_{J_{1}+J_{2}+\dots+J_{L}=2p}\prod_{\ell=1}^{L}S(J_{\ell})

\displaystyle\lesssim_{p,s}\varepsilon_{n}^{p}\|\psi\|_{H^{1}}^{2p},

where the sum in the middle expression is interpreted to be over all positive integers
L\geq 1
and
J_{\ell}\geq 1
so that
J_{1}+\dots+J_{L}=2p
.

Given a finite set

F\subseteq\bigl\{(N,k):N\geq 1\text{ is dyadic and }k\in\mathbb{Z}\bigr\}
,
we compute that

\displaystyle\bbE\Biggl\|\psi\sum_{(N,k)\in F}X_{N,k}e_{N,k}\Biggr\|_{H^{-s}}^{2p}

\displaystyle\qquad=\sum_{(N_{1},k_{1}),\dots,(N_{2p},k_{2p})\in F}\bbE\prod_{j=1}^{2p}X_{N_{j},k_{j}}\prod_{\ell=1}^{p}\langle Ke_{N_{2\ell-1},k_{2\ell-1}},Ke_{N_{2\ell},k_{2\ell}}\rangle

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\sum_{(N_{1},k_{1}),\dots,(N_{2p},k_{2p})\in F}\Biggl|\bbE\prod_{j=1}^{2p}X_{N_{j},k_{j}}\Biggr|\prod_{\ell=1}^{2p}\|Ke_{N_{\ell},k_{\ell}}\|_{L^{2}}.

Combining this with the (absolute) convergence of the series (
2.28
), we see that the partial sums of any enumeration of the series
\psi\sum_{N\geq 1}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}X_{N,k}e_{N,k}
are Cauchy in
L^{2p}(d\bbP;H^{-s})
. Consequently, this series converges unconditionally in
L^{2p}(d\bbP;H^{-s})
and the corresponding limit satisfies

(2.29)

\bbE\left\|\psi\sum_{N\geq 1}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}X_{N,k}e_{N,k}\right\|_{H^{-s}}^{2p}\lesssim_{s,p}\varepsilon_{n}^{p}\|\psi\|_{H^{1}}^{2p}.

As this holds for all integers
p\geq 1
, we may extend to all real numbers
p\geq 1
by interpolation.

Let us now assume that
\psi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
.
If
\phi\in H^{s}
then
\phi\psi\in C_{c}(\mathbb{R})
and Lemma
2.6
gives us

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\phi\psi\,d\mu_{n}-\int_{\mathbb{R}}\phi\psi\,dx=\lim_{N_{0}\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\phi\psi\sum_{1\leq N\leq N_{0}}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}X_{N,k}e_{N,k}\,dx,

almost surely. Combining this with the unconditional convergence of the series
\psi\sum_{N\geq 1}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}X_{N,k}e_{N,k}
in
L^{2p}(d\bbP;H^{-s})
, we see that almost surely

\psi\sum_{N\geq 1}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}X_{N,k}e_{N,k}=\psi(d\mu_{n}-1),

in
H^{-s}
and (
2.21
) follows from (
2.29
). Finally, the extension of (
2.21
) to all
\psi\in H^{1}
follows from the density of
C_{c}^{\infty}
in
H^{1}
.
∎

Combining the estimate (
2.21
) with the proof of Corollary
2.5
gives us:

Corollary 2.8
.

Suppose that for all
n\geq 1
we have
0<\varepsilon_{n}\leq 1
. Taking
\frac{1}{2}<s\leq 1
and
Z_{n,s}
to be defined as in (
1.11
), we almost surely have
\mathcal{N}(0;Z_{n,s})<\infty
for all
n\geq 1
.

Further, if
f\in H^{1}
and
p\geq 1
then

(2.30)

\displaystyle\sup_{n\geq 1}\bbE\|f\|_{Y_{n,s}^{1}}^{2p}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f\|_{H^{1}}^{2p},

and hence we almost surely have
f\in\bigcap_{n\geq 1}Y_{n,s}^{1}
.

Proof.

Applying (
2.21
), we have

(2.31)

\sup_{n\geq 1}\sup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\bbE|Z_{n,s}(k)|^{6p}\lesssim_{p,s}\sup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\|\rho_{k}\|_{H^{1}}^{6p}\lesssim_{p,s}1.

Arguing as in Corollary
2.5
, we combine (
2.31
) with (
2.16
) to show that
\mathcal{N}(0;Z_{n,s})<\infty
for all
n\geq 1
almost surely. The estimate (
2.30
) follows from (
2.18
) and combining (
2.31
) with (
2.17
). The fact that
f\in\bigcap_{n\geq 1}Y_{n,s}^{1}
almost surely is a consequence of (
2.30
).
∎

We conclude this section by applying a similar argument to Proposition
2.7
(see also
[
21
, Equation (2.12)]
) to obtain the following:

Lemma 2.9
.

If
\xi
is white-noise-distributed then for all
s>\frac{1}{2}
and
\psi\in L^{2}
we have the estimate

(2.32)

\bbE\|\psi\xi\|_{H^{-s}}^{2}\lesssim\|\psi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Proof.

We recall (see, e.g.,
[
21
, Theorem 2.7]
) that if
(e_{j})
is a real-valued orthonormal basis of
L^{2}
and
(X_{j})
is an i.i.d. sequence of standard normal random variables then the series

\xi=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}X_{j}e_{j}

converges almost surely in the sense of distributions to white noise.

Taking
K=\langle\partial_{x}\rangle^{-s}\psi
as in Proposition
2.7
, for any finite set
F\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{+}
we have

\displaystyle\bbE\biggl\|\psi\sum_{j\in F}X_{j}e_{j}\biggr\|_{H^{-s}}^{2}=\sum_{j_{1},j_{2}\in F}\bbE X_{j_{1}}X_{j_{2}}\langle Ke_{j_{1}},Ke_{j_{2}}\rangle=\sum_{j\in F}\|Ke_{j}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Using that

\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\|Ke_{j}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=\|K\|_{\mathfrak{I}_{2}}^{2}\lesssim\|\psi\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

an identical argument to Proposition
2.7
now shows that if
\psi\in C_{c}^{\infty}
then
\psi\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}X_{j}e_{j}
converges unconditionally in
L^{2}(d\bbP;H^{-s})
to
\psi\xi
and satisfies

(2.33)

\bbE\biggl\|\psi\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}X_{j}e_{j}\biggr\|_{H^{-s}}^{2}\lesssim\|\psi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

The estimate (
2.32
) then follows from (
2.33
) and the density of
C_{c}^{\infty}
in
L^{2}
.
∎

3.
Homogenization

We start this section by recording some properties of the solution
\psi_{n}
of (
NLSn
) obtained in
[
19
]
:

Lemma 3.1
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{N}(0;Z_{n})<\infty
and
\psi_{0}\in X_{n}^{1}
. Then there exists a unique solution
\psi_{n}\in C(\mathbb{R};X_{n}^{1})
of the Duhamel formulation,

(3.1)

\psi_{n}(t)=e^{it\partial_{x}^{2}}\psi_{0}-2i\int_{0}^{t}e^{i(t-\tau)\partial_{x}^{2}}\bigl[|\psi_{n}(\tau)|^{2}\psi_{n}(\tau)\,d\mu_{n}\bigr]\,d\tau,

of (
NLSn
) with initial data
\psi_{n}(0)=\psi_{0}
. This solution conserves both the mass and energy,

(3.2)

M\bigl[\psi_{n}\bigr]=\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\psi_{n}|^{2}\,dx\quad\text{and}\quad E_{n}\bigl[\psi_{n}\bigr]=\tfrac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\partial_{x}\psi_{n}|^{2}\,dx+\tfrac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\psi_{n}|^{4}\,d\mu_{n},

and for all
T>0
satisfies the estimates

(3.3)

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\|\psi_{n}(t)\|_{H^{1}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\bigl[1+\|\psi_{0}\|_{X_{n}^{1}}^{2}\bigr]\|\psi_{0}\|_{H^{1}}^{2},

(3.4)

\displaystyle\|\psi_{n}\|_{C_{T}X_{n}^{1}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\langle T\rangle\bigl[1+\|\psi_{0}\|_{X_{n}^{1}}^{2}\bigr]\|\psi_{0}\|_{X_{n}^{1}}^{2}.

Proof.

The existence and uniqueness of a solution follows from
[
19
, Proposition 3.5]
. The estimates (
3.3
) and (
3.4
) follow from combining
[
19
, Lemma 3.3]
with
[
19
, Proposition 3.5]
.
∎

We will also require a corresponding result for the solution
\psi
of (
NLS
):

Lemma 3.2
.

If
\psi_{0}\in H^{1}
then there exists a unique solution
\psi\in C(\mathbb{R};H^{1})
of the Duhamel formulation,

(3.5)

\psi(t)=e^{it\partial_{x}^{2}}\psi_{0}-2i\int_{0}^{t}e^{i(t-\tau)\partial_{x}^{2}}\bigl[|\psi(\tau)|^{2}\psi(\tau)\bigr]\,d\tau,

of (
NLS
) with initial data
\psi(0)=\psi_{0}
. This solution conserves both the mass and energy,

(3.6)

M[\psi]=\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\psi|^{2}\,dx\quad\text{and}\quad E[\psi]=\tfrac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\partial_{x}\psi|^{2}+|\psi|^{4}\,dx,

satisfies the estimate

(3.7)

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\|\psi(t)\|_{H^{1}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\bigl[1+\|\psi_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\bigr]\|\psi_{0}\|_{H^{1}}^{2},

and for all
T>0
we have

(3.8)

\displaystyle\lim_{R\to\infty}\|(1-\chi_{R})\psi\|_{C_{T}H^{1}}

\displaystyle=0=\lim_{N\to\infty}\|P_{>N}\psi\|_{C_{T}H^{1}}.

Further, if
\frac{1}{2}<s\leq 1
, both
\mathcal{N}(0;Z_{n})<\infty
and
\mathcal{N}(0;Z_{n,s})<\infty
, and
\psi_{0}\in Y_{n,s}^{1}
then
\psi\in C(\mathbb{R};Y_{n,s}^{1})
and for all
T>0
we have the estimate

(3.9)

\displaystyle\|\psi\|_{C_{T}Y_{n,s}^{1}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\langle T\rangle\bigl[1+\|\psi_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\bigr]\|\psi_{0}\|_{Y_{n,s}^{1}}^{2}.

Proof.

Global well-posedness as well as conservation of mass and energy follow from classical arguments; see, e.g.,
[
9
]
. We may then combine the conservation of mass and energy with (
2.1
) to obtain (
3.7
): For all
t\in\mathbb{R}
we have

\|\psi(t)\|_{H^{1}}^{2}\leq M\bigl[\psi(t)\bigr]+2E\bigl[\psi(t)\bigr]=M\bigl[\psi_{0}\bigr]+2E\bigl[\psi_{0}\bigr]\lesssim\text{RHS}\eqref{psi H1}.

The identity (
3.8
) follows from applying Lemma
2.2
with
\mathcal{F}=\{\psi\}
.

To prove that
\psi\in C(\mathbb{R};Y_{n,s}^{1})
and obtain the estimate (
3.9
), we essentially follow the argument of
[
19
, Lemma 3.3]
.

We first note that if
\psi\in C(\mathbb{R};H^{1})
satisfies (
3.5
) then
\psi\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R};H^{-1})
satisfies the equation (
NLS
) in
H^{-1}
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
.
Let
Z\colon\mathbb{Z}\to[0,\infty)
be a sequence of non-negative numbers and suppose that both
\mathcal{N}(0;Z)<\infty
and
\psi_{0}\in L_{Z}^{2}
. Taking
R\geq 1
and
\chi_{R}
to be defined as in Section
2
, we then compute that

\displaystyle\tfrac{d}{dt}\|\chi_{R}\psi(t)\|_{L_{Z}^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad=-2\operatorname{Im}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\partial_{x}^{2}\psi(t,x)\overline{\psi(t,x)}|\chi_{R}(x)|^{2}\omega(x;Z)\,dx

\displaystyle\qquad=2\operatorname{Im}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\partial_{x}\psi(t,x)\overline{\psi(t,x)}\bigl[2\chi_{R}(x)\chi_{R}^{\prime}(x)\omega(x;Z)+|\chi_{R}(x)|^{2}\partial_{x}\omega(x;Z)\bigr]\,dx.

Recalling that the map
x\mapsto\omega(x;Z)
is
1
-Lipschitz, as well as the estimate (
2.9
), we may use the conservation of mass and (
3.7
) to bound

\displaystyle\Bigl|\tfrac{d}{dt}\|\chi_{R}\psi(t)\|_{L_{Z}^{2}}^{2}\Bigr|

\displaystyle\lesssim\Bigl[1+\tfrac{|\mathcal{N}(0;Z)|^{2}}{R}\Bigr]\|\psi(t)\|_{H^{1}}\|\chi_{R}\psi(t)\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Bigl[1+\tfrac{|\mathcal{N}(0;Z)|^{2}}{R}\Bigr]\bigl[1+\|\psi_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\bigr]^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\psi_{0}\|_{H^{1}}\|\psi_{0}\|_{L^{2}}.

Integrating this expression, for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
we have

\|\chi_{R}\psi(t)\|_{L_{Z}^{2}}^{2}\lesssim\|\psi_{0}\|_{L_{Z}^{2}}^{2}+|t|\Bigl[1+\tfrac{|\mathcal{N}(0;Z)|^{2}}{R}\Bigr]\bigl[1+\|\psi_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\bigr]^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\psi_{0}\|_{H^{1}}\|\psi_{0}\|_{L^{2}}.

Taking
R\to\infty
using Fatou’s lemma, we arrive at the estimate

(3.10)

\sup_{|t|\leq T}\|\psi(t)\|_{L_{Z}^{2}}^{2}\lesssim\|\psi_{0}\|_{L_{Z}^{2}}^{2}+T\bigl[1+\|\psi_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\bigr]^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\psi_{0}\|_{H^{1}}\|\psi_{0}\|_{L^{2}},

which we note is independent of the value of
\mathcal{N}(0;Z)
.

Repeating this argument, for
|t|\leq T
we have the estimate

\Bigl|\tfrac{d}{dt}\|(1-\chi_{R})\psi(t)\|_{L_{Z}^{2}}^{2}\Bigr|\lesssim\Bigl[1+\tfrac{|\mathcal{N}(0;Z)|^{2}}{R}\Bigr]\bigl[1+\|\psi_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\bigr]^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\psi_{0}\|_{H^{1}}\|(1-\chi_{R})\psi\|_{C_{T}L^{2}},

and hence

\displaystyle\sup_{|t|\leq T}\|(1-\chi_{R})\psi(t)\|_{L_{Z}^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\lesssim\|(1-\chi_{R})\psi_{0}\|_{L_{Z}^{2}}^{2}+T\Bigl[1+\tfrac{|\mathcal{N}(0;Z)|^{2}}{R}\Bigr]\bigl[1+\|\psi_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\bigr]^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\psi_{0}\|_{H^{1}}\|(1-\chi_{R})\psi\|_{C_{T}L^{2}},

so, applying the Dominated Convergence Theorem to the first summand and (
3.8
) to the second, yields

\lim_{R\to\infty}\sup_{|t|\leq T}\|(1-\chi_{R})\psi(t)\|_{L_{Z}^{2}}=0.

We may then combine this with (
2.9
) to show that for all
t,\tau\in[-T,T]
we have

\displaystyle\|\psi(t)-\psi(\tau)\|_{L_{Z}^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\bigl\|\chi_{R}\bigl[\psi(t)-\psi(\tau)\bigr]\bigr\|_{L_{Z}^{2}}+2\sup_{|t|\leq T}\|(1-\chi_{R})\psi(t)\|_{L_{Z}^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\bigl[|\mathcal{N}(0;Z)|^{2}+R\bigr]\|\psi(t)-\psi(\tau)\|_{L^{2}}+2\sup_{|t|\leq T}\|(1-\chi_{R})\psi(t)\|_{L_{Z}^{2}}.

By first taking
\tau\to t
and then
R\to\infty
, we see that
\psi\in C(\mathbb{R};L_{Z}^{2})
.

Finally, we apply these results first with
Z=Z_{n}
and then with
Z=Z_{n,s}
to prove that whenever
\mathcal{N}(0;Z_{n})<\infty
,
\mathcal{N}(0;Z_{n,s})<\infty
, and
\psi_{0}\in Y_{n,s}^{1}
we have
\psi\in C(\mathbb{R};Y_{n,s}^{1})
, with the estimate (
3.9
) following from (
3.7
) and (
3.10
).
∎

We now apply our a priori estimates to obtain the following:

Proposition 3.3
.

Suppose that
0<\varepsilon_{n}\leq 1
,
\mathcal{N}(0;Z_{n})<\infty
,
\mathcal{N}(0;Z_{n,1})<\infty
,
\psi_{0}\in Y_{n,1}^{1}
, and for some
T,A>0
we have

(3.11)

\|\psi_{n}\|_{C_{T}X_{n}^{1}}^{2}+\|\psi\|_{C_{T}Y_{n,1}^{1}}^{2}\leq A^{2}.

Then there exists a constant
C>0
so that

(3.12)

\displaystyle\|\psi_{n}-\psi\|_{C_{T}H^{-1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim e^{CA^{8}T^{2}}A^{3}T\bigl[1+A^{2}T\bigr]\varepsilon_{n}^{\frac{1}{8}},

(3.13)

\displaystyle\sup_{|t|\leq T}\Bigl|\|\psi_{n}(t)\|_{H^{1}}^{2}-\|\psi(t)\|_{H^{1}}^{2}\Bigr|

\displaystyle\lesssim e^{CA^{8}T^{2}}A^{4}\bigl[1+A^{2}T\bigr]\varepsilon_{n}^{\frac{1}{8}}.

Proof.

We start by writing the difference

\displaystyle\psi_{n}(t)-\psi(t)

\displaystyle=-2i\bigl[\mathcal{I}_{1}(t)+\mathcal{I}_{2}(t)\bigr],

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{1}(t)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}e^{i(t-\tau)\partial_{x}^{2}}\Bigl[\bigl(|\psi_{n}(\tau)|^{2}\psi_{n}(\tau)-|\psi(\tau)|^{2}\psi(\tau)\bigr)\,d\mu_{n}\Bigr]\,d\tau,

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{2}(t)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}e^{i(t-\tau)\partial_{x}^{2}}\Bigl[|\psi(\tau)|^{2}\psi(\tau)\,(d\mu_{n}-1)\Bigr]\,d\tau.

Taking a test function
f\in L^{1}
, we recall that for
0<\tau<t
we have the dispersive estimate

(3.14)

\bigl\|e^{-i(t-\tau)\partial_{x}^{2}}f\bigr\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{t-\tau}}\|f\|_{L^{1}}.

Taking
0<t\leq T
, we then apply (
2.12
) followed by (
3.14
) and Hölder’s inequality to bound

\displaystyle|\langle\mathcal{I}_{1}(t),f\rangle|

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\bigl|e^{-i(t-\tau)\partial_{x}^{2}}f\bigr|\bigl[|\psi_{n}(\tau)|^{2}+|\psi(\tau)|^{2}\bigr]|\psi_{n}(\tau)-\psi(\tau)|\,d\mu_{n}\,d\tau

\displaystyle\lesssim A^{2}\|f\|_{L^{1}}\int_{0}^{t}\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{t-\tau}}\|\psi_{n}(\tau)-\psi(\tau)\|_{L^{\infty}}\,d\tau

\displaystyle\lesssim A^{2}\|f\|_{L^{1}}t^{\frac{1}{4}}\left[\int_{0}^{t}\|\psi_{n}(\tau)-\psi(\tau)\|_{L^{\infty}}^{4}\,d\tau\right]^{\frac{1}{4}}.

By duality, this yields the estimate

(3.15)

\|\mathcal{I}_{1}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}^{4}\lesssim A^{8}t\int_{0}^{t}\|\psi_{n}(\tau)-\psi(\tau)\|_{L^{\infty}}^{4}\,d\tau.

Applying (
2.13
), we may instead estimate

(3.16)

\|\mathcal{I}_{1}\|_{C_{T}H^{-1}}\lesssim A^{2}T\|\psi_{n}-\psi\|_{C_{T}L^{\infty}},

and from (
2.14
) we also have

(3.17)

\|\mathcal{I}_{2}\|_{C_{T}H^{-1}}\lesssim A^{3}T\sqrt{\varepsilon_{n}}.

We now combine these estimates to obtain (
3.12
). For dyadic
N\geq 1
and times
0<t\leq T
, we use Bernstein’s inequality, followed by (
3.15
) and (
3.17
) to obtain

\displaystyle\|\psi_{n}(t)-\psi(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}^{4}\!

\displaystyle\lesssim\|P_{\leq N}\mathcal{I}_{1}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}^{4}\!+\|P_{\leq N}\mathcal{I}_{2}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}^{4}+\bigl\|P_{>N}\bigl[\psi_{n}(t)-\psi(t)\bigr]\bigr\|_{C_{T}L^{\infty}}^{4}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\mathcal{I}_{1}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}^{4}+N^{6}\|\mathcal{I}_{2}\|_{C_{T}H^{-1}}^{4}+N^{-2}\bigl[\|\psi_{n}\|_{C_{T}H^{1}}^{4}+\|\psi\|_{C_{T}H^{1}}^{4}\bigr]

\displaystyle\lesssim A^{8}t\int_{0}^{t}\|\psi_{n}(\tau)-\psi(\tau)\|_{L^{\infty}}^{4}\,d\tau+N^{6}A^{12}T^{4}\varepsilon_{n}^{2}+N^{-2}A^{4}.

Noting that the left hand side of this inequality is simply
\frac{d}{dt}\int_{0}^{t}\||\psi_{n}(\tau)-\psi(\tau)\|_{L^{\infty}}^{4}\,d\tau
, we may apply Gronwall’s inequality to bound

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\|\psi_{n}(t)-\psi(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}^{4}\,dt\lesssim e^{CA^{8}T^{2}}T\Bigl[N^{6}A^{12}T^{4}\varepsilon_{n}^{2}+N^{-2}A^{4}\Bigr],

for some constant
C>0
. After applying a similar argument for negative times and possibly increasing the size of the constant
C
, we arrive at the estimate

\displaystyle\|\psi_{n}-\psi\|_{C_{T}L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\lesssim A^{2}T^{\frac{1}{4}}\|\psi_{n}-\psi\|_{L_{T}^{4}L^{\infty}}+N^{\frac{3}{2}}A^{3}T\sqrt{\varepsilon_{n}}+N^{-\frac{1}{2}}A

\displaystyle\lesssim e^{CA^{8}T^{2}}\Bigl[N^{\frac{3}{2}}A^{3}T\sqrt{\varepsilon_{n}}+N^{-\frac{1}{2}}A\Bigr].

As this estimate is uniform in
N
, we may take
N\simeq\varepsilon_{n}^{-\frac{1}{4}}
to arrive at

(3.18)

\|\psi_{n}-\psi\|_{C_{T}L^{\infty}}\lesssim e^{CA^{8}T^{2}}A\bigl[1+A^{2}T\bigr]\varepsilon_{n}^{\frac{1}{8}}.

Finally, by combining (
3.16
), (
3.17
), and (
3.18
) we get

\displaystyle\|\psi_{n}-\psi\|_{C_{T}H^{-1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\mathcal{I}_{1}\|_{C_{T}H^{-1}}+\|\mathcal{I}_{2}\|_{C_{T}H^{-1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim A^{2}T\|\psi_{n}-\psi\|_{C_{T}L^{\infty}}+A^{3}T\sqrt{\varepsilon_{n}}\lesssim\text{RHS}\eqref{H-1}.

It remains to prove (
3.13
). Taking
t\in[-T,T]
and
R\geq 1
, we use conservation of mass and energy to bound

\displaystyle\Bigl|\|\psi_{n}(t)\|_{H^{1}}^{2}-\|\psi(t)\|_{H^{1}}^{2}\Bigr|

\displaystyle\lesssim\Bigl|M[\psi_{n}(t)]-M[\psi(t)]\Bigr|+\Bigl|E_{n}[\psi_{n}(t)]-E[\psi(t)]\Bigr|+\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\psi_{n}(t)|^{4}\,d\mu_{n}-\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\psi(t)|^{4}\,dx\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\psi_{0}|^{4}\,d\mu_{n}-\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\psi_{0}|^{4}\,dx\right|+\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\psi_{n}(t)|^{4}\,d\mu_{n}-\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\psi(t)|^{4}\,dx\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\sup_{|t|\leq T}\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\psi_{n}(t)|^{4}-|\psi(t)|^{4}\,d\mu_{n}\right|+\sup_{|t|\leq T}\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\psi(t)|^{4}\,d\mu_{n}-\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\psi(t)|^{4}\,dx\right|.

The first term may be estimated using (
2.12
) and (
2.1
) followed by (
3.18
) to obtain

\displaystyle\sup_{|t|\leq T}\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\psi_{n}(t)|^{4}-|\psi(t)|^{4}\,d\mu_{n}\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim A^{3}\|\psi_{n}-\psi\|_{C_{T}L^{\infty}}\lesssim e^{CA^{8}T^{2}}A^{4}\bigl[1+A^{2}T\bigr]\varepsilon_{n}^{\frac{1}{8}}.

For the second term, we first note that from the definition (
1.11

\displaystyle\quad+|t^{\prime}-t|\|\psi\|_{C_{T}H^{1}}^{2}\sup_{|\tau|\leq T}\|u_{\tau}\|_{C_{T}H^{s}},

and passing to the limit
t^{\prime}\to t
and then
N\to\infty
gives us (
A.4
).
∎

Proof of Corollary
4.2
.

Let us first note that uniqueness follows directly from the uniqueness of solutions to (
4.1
).

Turning to the problem of existence, let
T>0
and suppose that
f\in C([\tau-T,\tau+T];H^{s})
. Taking
u
to be defined as in (
4.6
), the strong continuity of the map
(t,\tau)\mapsto S(t,\tau)
then ensures that
u\in C([\tau-T,\tau+T];H^{s})
.

From the proof of Lemma
4.1
, for fixed
\sigma\in[\tau-T,\tau+T]
we have

\displaystyle i\partial_{t}\Bigl[e^{-it\partial_{x}^{2}}S(t,\tau)u_{0}\Bigr]

\displaystyle=e^{-it\partial_{x}^{2}}\Bigl[4|\psi(t)|^{2}S(t,\tau)u_{0}+2\psi(t)^{2}\overline{S(t,\tau)u_{0}}\Bigr],

\displaystyle i\partial_{t}\Bigl[e^{-it\partial_{x}^{2}}S(t,\sigma)f(\sigma)\Bigr]

\displaystyle=e^{-it\partial_{x}^{2}}\Bigl[4|\psi(t)|^{2}S(t,\sigma)f(\sigma)+2\psi(t)^{2}\overline{S(t,\sigma)f(\sigma)}\Bigr],

and hence

i\partial_{t}\Bigl[e^{-it\partial_{x}^{2}}u(t)\Bigr]=e^{-it\partial_{x}^{2}}\Bigl[4|\psi(t)|^{2}u(t)+2\psi(t)^{2}\overline{u(t)}\Bigr]+f(t).

Integrating, we see that
u\in C([\tau-T,\tau+T];H^{s})
solves (
4.5
).

The general case, where we only assume
f\in L_{\mathrm{loc}}^{1}(\mathbb{R};H^{s})
follows from fact that for all
T>0
the space
C([\tau-T,\tau+T];H^{s})
is dense in
L^{1}([\tau-T,\tau+T];H^{s})
.
∎

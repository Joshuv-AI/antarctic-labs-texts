Strong convergence rate of the explicit adaptive time-stepping methods for stochastic diffusion systems with locally Lipschitz coefficients Journal: XXX
Xueqi Wen, Guozhen Li, Yuanping Cui, Xiaoyue Li

Abstract
This paper proposes an adaptive time-stepping mothods for stochastic diffusion systems whose drift and diffusion coefficients are locally Lipschitz continuous and may exhibit polynomial growth. By controlling the growth of both the drift and diffusion coefficients, we give the choice of the state-dependent adaptive timestep and establish strong convergence of the proposed scheme with the optimal order 1 / 2 1/2 . The performance of the adaptive time-stepping scheme is compared with several widely used explicit and implicit schemes, including tamed EM, truncated EM, and backward EM schemes. Numerical experiments on stiff, non-stiff and high-dimensional stochastic diffusion systems verify the improved computational efficiency of the proposed scheme and validate the theoretical results.

Introduction

In this paper, we study the numerical approximation of a
d
-dimensional stochastic diffusion system, which is formulated as the stochastic differential equation (SDE) of the form

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}dX(t)=f(X(t))dt+g(X(t))dW(t),\quad t\geq 0,\\
X(0)=x_{0},\end{array}\right.

(1.1)

where
W(t)
is a
m
-dimensional Brownian motion and the drift and diffusion coefficients
f:{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\to{\mathbb{R}^{d}}
,
g:{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\to{\mathbb{R}^{d\times m}}
, which both satisfy a local Lipschitz condition (see Assumption
2.1
below). Our primary goal is to construct an adaptive time-stepping numerical scheme for SDE (
1.1
) and show that numerical solutions converge strongly to the exact solution of the underlying SDE (
1.1
).

Since it was pointed out in Hutzenthaler et al.
[
13
]
that the Euler-Maruyama (EM) numerical solutions fail to converge strongly to the true solution for a large class of SDEs with non-globally Lipschitz continuous coefficients, a couple of modified fixed-step EM schemes have been developed for nonlinear SDEs. Hutzenthaler et al.
[
14
]
then introduced a tamed EM scheme, which achieves
1/2
-order of convergence rate under a one-sided Lipschitz condition on the drift and a globally Lipschitz diffusion coefficient. Sabanis
[
39
,
40
]
further developed tamed EM schemes for SDEs with super-linear growth coefficients. Mao
[
35
]
proposed the truncated EM scheme, which was shown to achieve a convergence rate arbitrarily close to
1/2
in
[
36
]
. Generally speaking, these numerical schemes work by modifying the drift and diffusion coefficients in order to control the potential unbounded growth caused by non-global Lipschitz terms. Moreover, Beyn et al.
[
3
]
presented the projected EM scheme, in which the numerical solution is constrained to remain within a ball whose radius depends on the step size. Adopting the truncation idea of Mao
[
35
]
, Li et al. developed a truncated EM scheme
[
29
]
to approximate the nonlinear SDEs and obtained a
1/2
-order of convergence rate for the scheme, and also considered the approximation to the invariant distribution in an infinite time interval. Both schemes worked by truncating the EM solution to avoid potentially large excursions caused by the nonlinearity of the coefficients and the Brownian motion increments. Other fixed-step numerical schemes for approximating the nonlinear SDEs we refer to the backward EM scheme
[
12
,
25
]
, modified tamed EM scheme
[
18
,
27
,
2
,
1
,
17
]
, stopped EM scheme
[
31
]
, balanced EM scheme
[
43
]
and so on.

In practice, SDEs may not only fail to satisfy the global Lipschitz condition, but may also be stiff, that is, the solutions exhibit two or more widely differing time scales

[
25
,
10
]
. For example,

dX(t)=[(X(t)-1)(5-X(t))(X(t)-50)]dt+16X(t)dW(t),\quad t\geq 0.

(1.2)

One of the sample paths for (
1.2
) is shown in Figure
1
. This equation admits two stable steady states, one at
X=1
and the other at
X=50
, and the solution exhibits random transitions between them. To approximate such stiff SDEs, implicit numerical schemes such as the backward EM scheme
[
12
,
25
]
and the split-step backward EM
[
11
]
are often used in this setting. However, the implementation of implicit schemes requires solving a nonlinear algebraic equation at each time step, which can be computationally expensive and limits practical applicability. On the other hand, when using fixed-step explicit numerical schemes to approximate stiff SDEs, sufficiently small step sizes are required to avoid numerical instability and prevent the solution from blowing up, which inevitably leads to a substantial increase in computational cost. Motivated by these challenges, this paper introduces an adaptive numerical scheme for SDEs, which allow the step size to vary in response to the solution’s behavior.

Following the adaptive idea in ordinary differential equation discretisations, a class of adaptive time-stepping schemes for SDEs determines the step size by estimating the local error at each time step; see e.g.,
[
9
,
37
,
5
,
26
,
16
,
42
]
. However, a serious drawback of this kind of scheme is the potential requirement to interpolate the Brownian path when rejection of the step size is allowed (see
[
20
]
). Another class of variable-time-stepping schemes determines the timestep in advance based on known information, thereby avoiding the need to reject the step size. Kelly and Lord
[
20
]
proposed an alternative approach to control the unbounded growth of the non-globally Lipschitz drift coefficient by adjusting the length of each time step, and obtained an
L_{2}
convergence rate of order
1/2
for the adaptive EM scheme with globally Lipschitz diffusion and one-sided Lipschitz drift coefficient. They further extended this time-stepping strategy to a semi-implicit adaptive numerical scheme for semi-linear SDE, for which an arbitrarily close to
1/2
order of convergence rate was obtained
[
21
]
. Moreover, this strategy was generalized to SDEs without commutativity conditions, where the adaptive Milstein scheme achieves a strong convergence rate of order one
[
19
]
.
Fang and Giles
[
8
]
proposed an adaptive timestep construction for EM approximation of SDEs with nonglobally Lipschitz drift and globally Lipschitz diffusion coefficients, i.e.,

\left\langle{x,f(x)}\right\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\delta(x){\left|{f(x)}\right|^{2}}\leq\alpha{\left|x\right|^{2}}+\beta,

(1.3)

where adaptive time-stepping function
\delta:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
is a continuous and strictly positive, and
\alpha,\beta
are positive constants. With the one-sided Lipschitz drift coefficient and the global Lipschitz diffusion coefficient, the
1/2
-order of convergence rate is obtained in a finite time interval. They also showed convergence rate in an infinite time interval under more restrictive conditions. However, for a class of SDEs whose diffusion coefficients exhibit superlinear growth, such adaptive strategies are no longer applicable. For example, the Heston 3/2 model
[
28
,
45
]
describes the evolution of the volatility of an underlying asset in finance,

dX(t)=\kappa X(t)(\theta-X(t))dt+\sigma{X^{\frac{3}{2}}}(t)dW(t),\quad X(0)\in\mathbb{R}_{+},

(1.4)

where
\kappa,\theta,\sigma>0
. For more details about the adaptive time-stepping schemes, we refer to e.g.
[
24
,
7
,
23
,
44
]
for tamed adaptive EM/Milstein scheme;
[
4
]
for stochastic delay differential equations;
[
6
]
for stochastic partial differential equations;
[
48
]
for SDEs with piecewise continuous arguments;
[
2
,
23
,
38
,
30
]
for McKean-Vlasow SDEs and
[
22
]
for Cox–Ingersoll–Ross model.

In this paper, we propose an adaptive time-stepping scheme for SDEs whose drift and diffusion coefficients are locally Lipschitz continuous and may exhibit polynomial growth. Existing adaptive strategies, such as those proposed by Fang and Giles
[
8
]
and Kelly and Lord
[
20
]
, are designed for SDEs with a globally Lipschitz diffusion coefficient and therefore fail to remain applicable when the diffusion coefficient grows superlinearly.
We establish an
L^{q}
strong convergence rate of order
1/2
over finite time intervals and verify the efficiency of the proposed scheme through comparisons with several widely used explicit and implicit schemes.
The main challenge of this work lies in designing an explicit and efficient adaptive time-stepping numerical scheme.
We introduce both minimum and maximum step sizes. When the adaptive timestep is within this range, we control the superlinear growth of the drift and diffusion coefficients by adjusting the step length at each iteration. When the adaptive timestep reaches the minimum step size, we employ the truncated EM scheme as a backstop, which avoids large deviations and simultaneously ensures the continuity of the numerical solution. This numerical scheme has some nice properties, which allow us to establish a strong convergence theory over finite time intervals.

The rest of this paper is organized as follows. Section
2
gives some preliminary results for the exact solution of SDE (
1.1
), and constructs the adaptive time-stepping scheme. Section
3
proves the
p

4
obtains the convergence rate. In Section
6
, we show that the tamed EM scheme can be employed as the backtop scheme.
Section
7
provides several numerical examples comparing the proposed scheme with several fixed-step schemes to illustrate its effectiveness. Our conclusions and a discussion of possible future work are presented in Section
7
.

(5.10)

Theorem 5.3
.

Let (
2.2
) and Assumption
4.1
hold with

, for any

, the numerical solutions defined in (
5.5
) and (

) satisfying

\mathop{\sup}\limits_{0\leq t\leq T}\mathbb{E}{\left|{X(t)-Y(t)}\right|^{q}}\leq{C}\check{\delta}^{\frac{q}{2}},\quad\mathop{\sup}\limits_{0\leq t\leq T}\mathbb{E}{\left|{X(t)-\bar{Y}(t)}\right|^{q}}\leq{C}\check{\delta}^{\frac{q}{2}}.

Proof.

Fixed
T>0
. Similar to (
4.12
)-(
4.13
), using Itô’s formula to (
5.5
), Assumption
4.1
and Young’s inequality, for any

, one gets

\begin{split}&\mathbb{E}{\left|{X(t)-Y(t)}\right|^{q}}\\
\leq&C\mathbb{E}\left({\int_{0}^{t}{{{\left|{X(s)-Y(s)}\right|}^{q}}ds}}\right)+C\mathbb{E}\left({\int_{0}^{t}{{{\left|{f(Y(s))-f(\bar{Y}(s))}\right|}^{q}}ds}}\right)\\
&+C\mathbb{E}\left({\int_{0}^{t}{{{\left|{f(\bar{Y}(s))-\tilde{F}(\bar{Y}(s))}\right|}^{q}}ds}}\right)+C\mathbb{E}\left({\int_{0}^{t}{{{\left|{g(Y(s))-g(\bar{Y}(s))}\right|}^{q}}ds}}\right)\\
&+C\mathbb{E}\left({\int_{0}^{t}{{{\left|{g(\bar{Y}(s))-\tilde{G}(\bar{Y}(s))}\right|}^{q}}ds}}\right).\end{split}

(5.12)

Using (
5.6
), (
5.2
) and Assumption
4.1
we compute

\begin{split}&\left|{f(\bar{Y}(s))-\tilde{F}(\bar{Y}(s))}\right|=\left|{f(\bar{Y}(s))-\tilde{f}(\bar{Y}(s))}\right|{\mathcal{I}_{\{{\delta_{{n_{s}}}}=\hat{\delta}\}}}\\
\leq&\left|{f(\bar{Y}(t))}\right|\frac{{{{\hat{\delta}}^{1/2}}{{\left|{\bar{Y}(t)}\right|}^{l}}}}{{1+{{\hat{\delta}}^{1/2}}{{\left|{\bar{Y}(t)}\right|}^{l}}}}\leq{C_{f}}{{\hat{\delta}}^{\frac{1}{2}}}{\left|{\bar{Y}(t)}\right|^{l}}\left({1+{{\left|{\bar{Y}(t)}\right|}^{l+1}}}\right)\\
\leq&C{{\hat{\delta}}^{\frac{1}{2}}}\left({1+{{\left|{\bar{Y}(t)}\right|}^{2l+1}}}\right)\end{split}

Then, using the facts
0<\hat{\delta}\leq\check{\delta}
and
q\in[2,p/(2l+1)]
yields

\begin{split}&\mathbb{E}\left({\int_{0}^{t}{{{\left|{f(\bar{Y}(s))-\tilde{F}(\bar{Y}(s))}\right|}^{q}}ds}}\right)\leq C{{\hat{\delta}}^{\frac{q}{2}}}\int_{0}^{T}{\mathbb{E}{{\left({1+{{\left|{\bar{Y}(t)}\right|}^{2l+1}}}\right)}^{q}}dt}\\
\leq&C{{\check{\delta}}^{\frac{q}{2}}}\int_{0}^{T}{\left({1+\mathbb{E}{{\left|{\bar{Y}(t)}\right|}^{q(2l+1)}}}\right)dt}\\
\leq&C{{\check{\delta}}^{\frac{q}{2}}}\int_{0}^{T}{\left({1+{{\left({\mathbb{E}{{\left|{\bar{Y}(t)}\right|}^{p}}}\right)}^{\frac{{q(2l+1)}}{p}}}}\right)dt}\leq C{{\check{\delta}}^{\frac{q}{2}}}.\end{split}

Similarly, one has

\mathbb{E}\left({\int_{0}^{t}{{{\left|{g(\bar{Y}(s))-\tilde{G}(\bar{Y}(s))}\right|}^{q}}ds}}\right)\leq C{{\check{\delta}}^{\frac{q}{2}}}.

Inserting the above into (
5.12
), using Assumption
4.1
yields

\begin{split}&\mathbb{E}{\left|{X(t)-Y(t)}\right|^{q}}\\
\leq&C\mathbb{E}\left({\int_{0}^{t}{{{\left|{X(s)-Y(s)}\right|}^{q}}ds}}\right)+C\mathbb{E}\left[{\int_{0}^{t}{{{\left({1+{{\left|{Y(s)}\right|}^{l}}+{{\left|{\bar{Y}(s)}\right|}^{l}}}\right)}^{q}}{{\left|{Y(s)-\bar{Y}(s)}\right|}^{q}}ds}}\right]\\
&+C\mathbb{E}\left({\int_{0}^{t}{{{\left({1+{{\left|{Y(s)}\right|}^{l}}+{{\left|{\bar{Y}(s)}\right|}^{l}}}\right)}^{\frac{q}{2}}}{{\left|{Y(s)-\bar{Y}(s)}\right|}^{q}}ds}}\right)+C\check{\delta}^{\frac{q}{2}}\\
\leq&C\mathbb{E}\left({\int_{0}^{t}{{{\left|{X(s)-Y(s)}\right|}^{q}}ds}}\right)+C\int_{0}^{t}{\mathbb{E}\left[{{{\left({1+{{\left|{Y(s)}\right|}^{l}}+{{\left|{\bar{Y}(s)}\right|}^{l}}}\right)}^{q}}{{\left|{Y(s)-\bar{Y}(s)}\right|}^{q}}}\right]ds}+C\check{\delta}^{\frac{q}{2}}.\end{split}

Similar to (
4.16
), since

we have

\begin{split}{\mathbb{E}\left[{{{\left({1+{{\left|{Y(s)}\right|}^{l}}+{{\left|{\bar{Y}(s)}\right|}^{l}}}\right)}^{q}}{{\left|{Y(s)-\bar{Y}(s)}\right|}^{q}}}\right]}\leq C\check{\delta}^{\frac{q}{2}}.\end{split}

Hence, one gets

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}{\left|{X(t)-Y(t)}\right|^{q}}\leq C\int_{0}^{t}{\mathbb{E}{{\left|{X(s)-Y(s)}\right|}^{q}}ds}+C\check{\delta}^{\frac{q}{2}}.\end{split}

The Gronwall inequality gives

\mathbb{E}{\left|{X(t)-Y(t)}\right|^{q}}\leq{C}\check{\delta}^{\frac{q}{2}}.

Combining with Lemma

we then have

\mathop{\sup}\limits_{0\leq t\leq T}\mathbb{E}{\left|{X(t)-\bar{Y}(t)}\right|^{q}}\leq{2^{q-1}}\mathop{\sup}\limits_{0\leq t\leq T}\mathbb{E}{\left|{X(t)-Y(t)}\right|^{q}}+{2^{q-1}}\mathop{\sup}\limits_{0\leq t\leq T}\mathbb{E}{\left|{Y(t)-\bar{Y}(t)}\right|^{q}}\leq C{\check{\delta}^{\frac{q}{2}}}.

The proof is therefore complete.

(5.13)

7
Conclusions

This paper proposes an ATS-TEM scheme for SDEs whose drift and diffusion coefficients are both locally Lipschitz continuous and may exhibit polynomial growth. We establish the
p
th moment boundedness of the numerical solutions and prove the strong convergence of the ATS-TEM. Under somewhat stronger conditions, we obtain the optimal
1/2
-order of convergence rate. Moreover, we show that TaEM introduced by
[
40
]
can be employed as a backstop scheme, and the corresponding ATS-TaEM achieves an
1/2
-order of convergence rate. Several numerical examples, including stiff, non-stiff and high-dimensional SDEs, are presented to verify the efficiency of the proposed scheme and to validate the theoretic results further. In our future work, the preservation of the long-time asymptotic properties of the adaptive time-stepping method, including the uniform-in-time convergence rate and the ergodicity, will be reported.

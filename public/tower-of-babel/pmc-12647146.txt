Robust and imperceptible image watermarking using chaotic map-integrated quantum-inspired multi-objective cuckoo search optimization
Authors: Prati Dubey, Abdul Khader Jilani Saudagar, Ramesh Chandra Poonia, Mukesh Asati
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractWith the rapid growth of multimedia data transmission for secure and reliable communication has become critical due to increasing cyber threats. This paper presents a Chaotic Map-integrated Quantum-Inspired Multi-Objective Cuckoo Search (CMQICS)-based watermarking approach to achieve high imperceptibility, robustness, and embedding efficacy. The proposed approach integrates quantum-inspired cuckoo search optimization with chaotic mapping to enhance watermark embedding. A multi-image watermarking scheme is also used to strengthen payload capacity while minimizing visual distortion. The embedding process operates in the frequency domain using a hybrid Discrete Cosine Transform–Two-Dimensional Discrete Wavelet Transform (DCT-2DWT) combined with a zig-zag scanning strategy. This ensures attack resilience. The experimental results show that CMQICS achieves a Peak Signal-to-Noise Ratio (PSNR) of approximately 89 dB, a Structural Similarity Index Measure (SSIM) of 0.99, and an average embedding time of around 1 s. Randomness analysis further validates the security of the embedded watermark. The comparative evaluations states that the CMQICS outperforms existing approaches.Keywords: Image security, Chaotic map, Invisible watermarking, Optimization, Quantum-Inspired, Cuckoo searchSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

Advancements in technology have led to medical data being stored digitally for diagnosis. But this poses risks like manipulation and misuse during transmission. It is important for copyright protection, tracking sources, and making sure information hasn’t been tampered with. With lots of digital images being shared, it’s important to protect them from theft, copying, and leaks. As technology improves, protecting these images and videos becomes even more crucial. The demand for high-quality digital images in computer graphics, medical diagnosis, satellite imaging, etc. are growing. These images are valuable, so it is important to protect their copyrights while also being able to restore them to their original state. Robust watermarking protects copyrights but permanently alters the image. Reversible watermarking is effective in image recovery but lacks robustness against attacks. Invisible watermarking hides data within digital content without affecting visual quality. Key challenges include robustness, capacity, security, and resistance to tampering.

Digital watermarking techniques are categorized into fragile and robust. Fragile watermarking is used in the field of tamper detection or providing user authentication5. At the same time, the robust watermarking focuses on copyright protection and can withstand signal processing and geometric attacks6. Recent studies have introduced transform-domain methods to enhance imperceptibility and robustness7–9. Despite these advances, traditional transform-based techniques still face limitations under complex signal processing operations and geometric distortions10. To overcome these limitations, researchers have incorporated artificial intelligence (AI) driven strategies such as machine learning (ML) models11, nature-inspired optimization algorithms12,13, and hybrid frameworks. Chen et al.13 used a support vector machine (SVM) for robust image watermarking against geometrical attacks. Liu et al.11 presented an approach for watermark embedding over DCT coefficients using a neural network. However, this approach requires a significant amount of computational resources to handle complex operations both prior to embedding and post extraction. These issues can be resolved by selecting the optimal location of embedding by using an optimization approach. Zuo et al.3 presented a multi-swarm-particle-swarm-optimization (MPSO) algorithm for handling adversarial attacks in watermarking and achieved a success rate of approximately 89%. Devi et al.14 proposed a H-Grey optimization (HGO) approach for hybrid DWT-SVD domain for selecting optimal scaling factor. Gul et al.2 used hybridization of Contourlet, DCT, and SVD with Artificial Bee Colony (ABC) algorithm. Fahmy et al.1 used hybrid optimization approach by combining Chimp Optimization algorithm with Harris Hawks optimization algorithm for copyright protection. The hybrid approach shows better robustness. Huang et al.4 presented an attention-based approach using GAN. Kumari et al.10 hybridized the DWT-SVD watermarking approach with Enhanced Tunicate Swarm Algorithm. Devi et al.12 proposed multi image watermarking approach using gray wolf optimization (GWO) for DWT-DCT-SVD domain. GWO was used to decide the gain value for the message bits to be inserted at selected position by DWT-DCT. Tang et al.15 used hybrid approach for adaptive normalization and optimized embedding to reduce embedding distortion. Therefore, in digital watermarking, there is requirement of balance between computational efficiency and maintaining the quality of watermarked images. Traditional watermarking techniques, such as DWT, SVD, DCT, etc. offer robust solutions but are often limited for their high computational complexity and cost. Despite extensive research, many existing methods still struggle to enhance watermarked image quality, and optimization-based watermarking approaches further complicate the issue with increased costs and complexity. The necessity to protect digital images demand for more efficient watermarking solutions that do not compromise on image quality, robustness, or imperceptibility. To address these issues, there is a shift towards developing more robust watermarking schemes that utilize hybrid domain techniques with nature-inspired optimization approaches. However, the implementation of optimization-based approaches in the embedding or extraction processes is still limited. In response to the trade-offs observed in existing watermarking techniques, this paper proposes a hybrid strategy designed to achieve high imperceptibility while maintaining robustness under various attack conditions.

Objectives and rationale

Therefore, the objective of the proposed CMQICS watermarking framework is designed to maximize imperceptibility, robustness, and security while maintaining an optimal balance among them. Specifically, the framework aims to achieve high imperceptibility by adaptively selecting embedding strength and positions in perceptually tolerant mid-band transform coefficients. It enhances robustness against various image processing and geometric attacks by distributing watermark energy over stable mid-frequency components, ensuring the watermark remains recoverable even after distortions.

The integration of a Chaotic Map and Quantum-Inspired Multi-Objective Cuckoo Search in the proposed watermarking framework serves distinct yet complementary purposes. The chaotic map generates ergodic, pseudo-random, and key-dependent sequences that are used to scramble watermark bits that will enhance the security, spatial dispersion, and robustness without introducing visible artifacts. Meanwhile, the QI-MOCS algorithm effectively handles the conflicting objectives of imperceptibility, robustness, and capacity by exploring a wide search space through Lévy flights and maintaining population diversity via quantum-bit representations. It produces a Pareto front of optimal solutions balancing visual quality and watermark robustness. Adaptive tuning embeds the strength and coefficient positions to achieve superior generalization across different images and attack conditions.

Chaos theory

Chaos theory that is originated from nonlinear dynamical systems, has proven to be an effective technique for image security due to its deterministic nature. It is basically composed of two operations: (i) permutation whose function is to shuffle pixel positions to obscure spatial relationships, and (ii) diffusion that modifies pixel values to eliminate redundancy and increase randomness37. Proposed methodology

Digital watermarking protects copyrights by embedding data into content, using spatial or transform techniques while ensuring robustness, security, and imperceptibility. Spatial domain methods involve direct manipulation of image pixels but tend to be less robust and more vulnerable to attacks. Transform domain methods, although more complex, distribute the watermark across the entire image, offering better protection against various attacks. Chaotic maps give key-dependent sequences with ergodicity and extreme sensitivity to initial conditions, so the embedding index schedule is non-repeating and image-specific—harder to predict or collude against than conventional scramblers (e.g., fixed permutations/LFSRs). They also disperse bits across mid-band coefficients in a content-adaptive way, boosting both unpredictability and resilience without adding visible artifacts. In this paper, invisible multiple image watermarking is used. The ability to embed multiple watermarks in a single cover image is valuable for multi-layer authentication, ownership tracing, and hierarchical access control. It allows different stakeholders to embed distinct identifiers without interference. This enables real-world applications like medical image auditing, digital rights management, and forensic tracking, which traditional single-watermark schemes cannot effectively support. But it faces specific challenges such as increased susceptibility to attacks, potential quality degradation, the computational intensity, and the need for compatibility with compression techniques due to the large bandwidth requirements of multimedia data. Motivated by this, this section presents the proposed methodology termed as Chaotic map integrated quantum inspired multi-objective cuckoo search (CMQICS) optimization for image watermarking. The proposed method embeds chaotic watermarking components into the cover image using a hybrid DCT- 2DWT. As DWT separates luminance energy and provides subbands with good geometric stability; DCT’s mid-band region is perceptually tolerant yet attack-stable. The zig-zag constraint confines embedding to those mid-frequencies, giving a “sweet spot” between imperceptibility and robustness; in our tests it matched or exceeded SVD hybrids under JPEG/noise/HE and mild geometric distortions while being faster and easier to tune. Therefore, this section used DCT-2DWT embedding. Embedding is performed within the middle-frequency bands of the YUV components following a zig-zag scanning pattern. This will show the uniform watermark distribution and preserve perceptual quality. The performance under sharpening attacks shows degradation in PSNR with values 70.41–70.51 dB across all images. However, SSIM remains high ≈ 0.99 and CC values mostly remain 1.0, with only one instance falling marginally to 0.99. This suggests that while sharpening introduces more high-frequency alterations but the embedded watermark maintains structural and statistical integrity.

Statistical comparison of watermarks

To evaluate the statistical similarity between the original and extracted watermarks, a histogram-based analysis is performed in Table 5 using three widely accepted metrics: Chi-Square Distance, Bhattacharyya Distance, and Histogram Intersection. These metrics were computed for five different input watermark images and their corresponding extracted images. The Chi-Square Distance values ranged from 0.0021 to 0.7086, with an average of approximately 0.2176, indicating a generally low divergence in pixel distributions. Lower values reflect higher similarity. The Bhattacharyya Distance, which measures the statistical separability between two distributions, remained notably low across all images (average ≈ 0.089), suggesting strong overlap and low distortion during extraction. Importantly, the Histogram Intersection metric consistently yielded high values with an average above 0.802. This further confirms that the pixel intensity distributions between the original and extracted watermarks remained largely preserved. These results validate that the proposed CMQICS watermarking scheme maintains a high degree of statistical integrity during the embedding and extraction process, even after applying security-driven transformations such as chaotic scrambling and optimization.

Limitations and future directions

While CMQICS achieves strong imperceptibility–robustness trade-offs, several limitations remain. (i) limited attack coverage, (ii) Generalization risk: the optimizer is tuned on a finite attack suite; shifts in attack distributions (e.g., learned, differentiable attacks) may reduce performance unless the attack set is periodically updated. (iii) Multi-watermark interference: simultaneous payloads can introduce inter-watermark coupling under heavy distortions unless carefully orthogonalized or scheduled. To address these limitations, future work will: (a) adopt distortion-less, high-payload embedding ideas (e.g., FFT-ETM) to raise capacity without quality loss51; (b) target mobile/edge efficiency and energy-aware pipelines for video watermarking52; (c) incorporate secure mobile deployment guidance and app vetting practices to strengthen the end-to-end threat model and key management53; (d) explore multi-bit encoding schemes to enhance robustness at a given payload budget54; and (e) investigate alternative transforms as plug-ins to the CMQICS optimization loop for better resilience under geometric/photometric stress55.

Conclusion

In this paper, the proposed Chaotic map integrated quantum-inspired multi-objective cuckoo search (CMQICS) algorithm is a chaos-keyed, hybrid DCT–2DWT image watermarking framework, where the embedding strength and positions are jointly optimized via a quantum-inspired multi-objective Cuckoo Search. This optimal chaotic map provides a key-dependent, non-repetitive coefficient selection that enhances security and spatial dispersion, while the quantum-inspired cuckoo search introduces adaptive probabilistic exploration that jointly optimizes embedding strength and position. This co-design of chaos-driven randomness with quantum-inspired intelligence yields a self-adaptive, Pareto-optimal watermarking model that conventional hybrid methods. By targeting mid-band coefficients and using a keyed chaotic sequence for scrambling and index selection, the method simultaneously achieves high PSNR/SSIM values and high robustness with enhanced security. The algorithm’s efficacy is underscored by its impressive performance metrics, including a high average PSNR of approximately 89dB, an SSIM of 0.99, and a rapid average execution time of about 1 s, outperforming comparative state-of-the-art models. Empirical results demonstrate that CMQICS attains favorable Pareto-optimal trade-offs compared to fixed-parameter baselines. This work not only contributes a significant advancement in secure multimedia transmission but also sets a new benchmark for the development of future watermarking solutions. The future scope of the proposed CMQICS framework includes developing a blind-only extraction method using learned priors for reference-free estimation, and implementing adaptive per-block embedding strength based on local texture or saliency. It can also be extended to learned transform domains, video watermarking with temporal coherence, and adversarial training to enhance robustness against advanced attacks.

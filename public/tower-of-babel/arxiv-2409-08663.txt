Factor system for graphs and combinatorial HHS
Jihoon Park

Abstract
We relaxe the constraint on the domains of combinatorial HHS machinery so combinatorial HHS machinery works for most cubical curve graphs. As an application we extend the factor system machinery of the CAT(0) cube complex to the quasi-median graphs.



1
Introduction

A hierarchically hyperbolic space(HHS) is a framework that simultaniously explains geometry of mapping class groups of finite type surfaces and CAT(0) cube complexes. After Behrstock–Hagen–Sisto introduced the definition and properties of HHSs
[
2
,
3
]
, it was found that HHS structure is useful to study its coarse geometry and group structure. Although HHS structure covers wide range of spaces and groups (
[
2
,
3
,
4
,
12
,
5
,
11
,
14
]
), verifying whether a given space or group admits HHS structure directly from the definition is usually challenging.

To remedy this, Behrstock–Hagen–Martin–Sisto
[
1
]
provided a simpler criterion for hierarchical hyperbolicity, called the combinatorial hierarchically hyperbolic space (CHHS). This criterion has proven useful to study HHS structures of various groups, see
[
1
,
7
,
10
,
15
]
. Also the CHHS structure can be formulated from the HHS structure under mild assumptions
[
9
]
, and understanding the CHHS machinery partly reveals mysterious nature of the HHS structure. The axioms of the CHHS, however, may not be optimal one as it does not cover some CAT(0) cube complexes with well-studied counterparts of the curve graphs; crossing and contact graphs, even when it admits an HHS structure. (One can find detailed discussion on the CAT(0) cube complex for which the CHHS machinery fails in [Section 10 of
[
9
]
].)

Nevertheless, when we consider a CAT(0) cube complexes
X
with a factor system, the system of crossing graphs of each domains in the factor system is encoded in the crossing graph
\Delta X
as intersections of link graphs. If we relax the constraint on the domains of the CHHS machinery by intersections of link graphs rather than the link graph of some clique graphs, then such system of subgraphs provides more adaptible combinatorial takes of HHS structures. We will call such system of subgraphs a
graph

factor

system
. We can extend the axioms of the CHHS machinery to the simplicial graphs with a graph factor system. Now we state our main result:

Theorem 1.1

(Main theorem)
.

Let
X
be a simplicial graph with a graph factor system
\mathfrak{F}
and let
W
be an
X
-graph. If
(X,W,\mathfrak{F})
satisfies the extended axioms of CHHS, then
(W,\mathfrak{F})
admits an HHS structure.

The CHHS with factor system covers most well-studied HHS with curve graph models including the curve graph of surface and the crossing graph of the CAT(0) cube complexes, and we believe that the CHHS with factor system could be useful to study new HHS structure of various spaces, especially for the spaces having a hierarchical system of subspaces.

As an application, we extend the factor system criterion of the CAT(0) cube complex developed by
[
2
]
to the quasi-median graphs.

-

(Nesting.)

(Orthogonality.) The set

An

(7)

(8)

We recall a useful result of HHS for later use, the realisation theorem which explains how the points in HHS and its coordinate tuples are related.

Definition 2.4

(Consistent tuple)
.

Let
\kappa\geq 0
and let
\vec{b}\in\Pi_{U\in\mathfrak{S}}2^{CU}
be a tuple such that for each
U\in\mathfrak{S}
, the
U\textnormal{-}

has a diameter bounded above by

is called a

if for all

\{d_{V}(b_{V},\rho^{W}_{V}),d_{W}(b_{W},\rho^{V}_{W})\}\leq\kappa

whenever
V\pitchfork W
, and

min
\{d_{W}(b_{W},\rho^{V}_{W}),diam_{V}(b_{V}\cup\rho^{W}_{V}(b_{W}))\}\leq\kappa

whenever
V\sqsubsetneq W
.

For each point
x\in X
, the tuple
(\pi_{U}(x))_{U\in\mathfrak{S}}
is always consistent. Conversely, all consistent tuples are coarsely the coordinate tuple of points in

.

Theorem 2.5

(The realisation of consistent tuples,
[
3
]
)

Let
(X,\mathfrak{S})
be an HHS. There exists a function
\theta:[0,\infty)\rightarrow[0,\infty)
so that if
(b_{U})_{U\in\mathfrak{S}}
is a
\kappa
-consistent tuple, then there exists a point
x\in X
such that
d_{U}(x,b_{U})\leq\theta(\kappa)
for all
U\in\mathfrak{S}
.

Note that uniqueness condition for
(X,\mathfrak{S})
implies that the
realisation\ point\ x
for a consistent tuple
(b_{U})_{U\in\mathfrak{S}}
provided by the realisation theorem, is coarsely unique.

3
Combinatorial hierarchically hyperbolic factor system

3.1
Graphs with factor system and the axioms of CHHS

In this subsection, we define a factor system on a simplicial graph
X
and extend the notions and axioms of the CHHS theory to our setting.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
X
be a simplicial graph. We define a
graph

factor

system
, denoted by
\mathfrak{F}
, as a collection of induced subgraphs of
X
satisfying that

1.
X\in\mathfrak{F}
.

2. For every vertex
v\in X
,
lk(v)\in\mathfrak{F}
.

3. Whenever
F_{1},F_{2}\in\mathfrak{F}
,
F_{1}\cap F_{2}\in\mathfrak{F}
if it is non-empty.

4. There exists
n>0
such that if there exists a chain
F_{1}\subsetneq F_{2}\dots\subsetneq F_{k}
of subgraphs in the system
F_{i}\in\mathfrak{F}
,
1\leq i\leq k
, it satisfies
k<n
. We call this
n
the complexity of
\mathfrak{F}
.

Definition 3.2
.

An
X
-
graph
is any graph
W
whose vertex set is the set of all maximal cliques of
X
. We say that two maximal cliques of
X
are
W
-
adjacent
if the corresponding vertices of
W
are adjacent in
W
.

The system of subgraphs
\mathfrak{F}
is the candidate of an HHS structure for the metric graph
W
. For this reason we often refer to each subgraph in the system
\mathfrak{F}
as a
domain
. For each domain
F\in\mathfrak{F}
, we also denote the induced system of subgraphs by
\mathfrak{F}_{F}=\{F^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{F}\mid F^{\prime}\subseteq F\}
.

Definition 3.3

(Projection vertices, projection parts of domains, augmented graphs)
.

Let
X
be a simplicial graph with a graph factor system
\mathfrak{F}
and
W
be an
X
-graph. For each non-maximal domain
F\in\mathfrak{F}-\{X\}
, we associate a new vertex
b_{F}
, called the
projection\ vertex\ for\ F
. Also denote the collection of all projection vertices of non-maximal domains by
B=\{b_{F}\mid F\in\mathfrak{F}-\{X\}\}
and for each domain
F\in\mathfrak{F}
, denote the collection of all induced projection vertices by
B_{F}=\{b_{F^{\prime}}\mid F^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{F}_{F}-\{F\}\}
.

If two domains
F,F^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{F}
satisfy
F^{\prime}\subseteq lk(F)
, then we call
F
and
F^{\prime}
are
orthogonal
and denote by
F\perp F^{\prime}
. In particular, if
lk(F)
is non-empty, then
lk(F)
is a domain and we denote this domain by
F^{\perp}
.

We define a
projection\ part
of
F
, denoted by
PF
, as
PF=F^{\perp}\cup(B_{F^{\perp}}\cup\{b_{F^{\perp}}\})\cup\{b_{F^{\prime}}\in B\mid F\subseteq F^{\prime}\}
.

The
augmented\ graph
of
X
, denoted by
CX
, is the graph whose vertex set consists of
V(X)\cup B
and two vertices are adjacent whenever

1. two vertices
v,w\in V(X)
that are adjacent in
X
.

2. two vertices
v,w\in V(X)
that are contained in
W
-adjacent maximal cliques. We often call this edge by
additional\ edge
.

3. two vertices
v\in V(X),\ w\in B
such that
w=b_{F}
and
v\in F
.

For each domain
F\in\mathfrak{F}
, the
augmented\ graph\ CF
of the domain
F
is an induced subgraph of
CX
spanned by vertex set
(V(F)\cup B_{F})
.

Remark
.

The augmentation of each domain is slightly different compared to the original CHHS theory in
[
1
]
. The reason is that we expect that the augmented graphs of properly nested domains are coned-off so the nested domains have uniformly bounded relative projections. Hence in
[
1
]
, the nesting of essential domains
F\subsetneq F^{\prime}
requires a simplex
\Delta\subset F^{\prime}
satisfying
lk(\Delta)\cap F^{\prime}=F
. In the graph factor system, such clique subgraphs may not exist so we need to take some additional vertices that coning-off every nested subgraphs.

Definition 3.4
.

Let
F\in\mathfrak{F}
be a domain. A
complement

graph
of
F
, denoted by
Y_{F}
, is an induced subgraph of
CX
spanned by the vertex set
(V(X)\cup B)-PF
.

Now we extend the axioms of CHHS to the graph with the graph factor system.

Definition 3.5
.

Let
(X,W,\mathfrak{F})
be a triple consisting of a simplicial graph
X
, an
X
-graph
W
and a graph factor system
\mathfrak{F}
on
X
. We define the
\delta
-
hyperbolicity

condition
on the triple as follows:

There exists
\delta>0
such that for each domain
F\in\mathfrak{F}
,

(1) the augmented graph
CF
is
\delta
-hyperbolic graph and;

(2)
CF
admits
(\delta,\delta)
-quasi-isometric embedding into
Y_{F}
.

The hyperbolicity condition explains how the geometric and projection data of the expected HHS structure is encoded in the graph. Indeed if
\mathfrak{F}
is a singleton set
\mathfrak{F}=\{X\}
, then the condition tells that
W=CX
is hyperbolic and hence admits trivial HHS structure without any additional assumption. But to induce actual HHS structure for general cases, we need more axioms to guarantee that the induced geometric and combinatorial structures on the nested domains are comparable. First we note that each domain admits an induced graph factor system.

Lemma 3.6
.

For each domain
F\in\mathfrak{F}
, the induced system
\mathfrak{F}_{F}=\{F^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{F}\mid F^{\prime}\subseteq F\}
is a graph factor system of the graph
F
.

Proof.

First note that
F\in\mathfrak{F}_{F}
by construction. For each
v\in F
, its link graph in
F
satisfies

so
lk_{F}(v)\in\mathfrak{F}_{F}
. Also if
F_{1},F_{2}\in\mathfrak{F}_{F}\subset\mathfrak{F}
, then
F_{1}\cap F_{2}\in\mathfrak{F}
and
F_{1}\cap F_{2}\subseteq F
, hence
F_{1}\cap F_{2}\in\mathfrak{F}_{F}
. Finally whenever
F_{1}\subsetneq F_{2}\dots\subsetneq F_{k}
is a chain of subgraphs in the system
\mathfrak{F}_{F}
, it is the chain of subgraphs in the system
\mathfrak{F}
and hence

is bounded by the complexity of
\mathfrak{F}
.
∎

This observation shows that each domain
F\in\mathfrak{F}
is itself a graph with the graph factor system
\mathfrak{F}_{F}
of strictly smaller complexity. Let
W^{F}
be some
F
-graph. We will denote the projection parts, complements graphs and augmented graphs of nested domains

by

,

and

Now we define an axiom which guarantees that for each domain, considered as the graph with the graph factor system, the induced CHHS structure is compatable with the structure of
(X,W,\mathfrak{F})
.

Definition 3.7
.

Let
(X,W,\mathfrak{F})
be a triple of a simplicial graph
X
, an
X
-graph

and a graph factor system
\mathfrak{F}
on

. We say the triple

satisfies the

if

, there exists a
F

such that

(1) for each domain

, we have

and,

(2) for each domain
F^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{F}_{F}
and for each (possibly empty) maximal clique

, there is an induced map

that assigns to each maximal clique

the maximal clique of

Definition 3.8
.

A
combinatorial\ HHS\ with\ factor\ system
(CHHF) is a triple
(X,W,\mathfrak{F})
consists of a simplicial graph
X
, an
X
-graph

and a graph factor system
\mathfrak{F}
of

that satisfies the

-hyperbolicity and hierarchy condition.

Our goal is to show that if

is a CHHF, then

admits an

-HHS structure with

depends only on the complexity

and the hyperbolicity constant

3.2
Proto-HHS structure and bounded geodesic image

Let
(X,W,\mathfrak{F})
be a CHHF. The goal of this subsection is to show that
\mathfrak{F}
induces a proth-HHS structure on

and check the combinatorial bounded geodesic image for CHHF setting.

By Lemma 3.6, each non-maximal domain
F\in\mathfrak{F}
admits a graph factor system
\mathfrak{F}_{F}
. We first check that the triple

Lemma 3.9
.

For each domain
F\in\mathfrak{F}
, the triple
(F,W^{F},\mathfrak{F}_{F})
is CHHF.

Proof.

By the hierarchy condition, for each domain

is the same as

which is

To show the second part of hyperbolicity condition for

, consider the complement graph

of

in

is an induced subgraph of

and

is an induced subgraph of

are induced ultimately from the graph

is indeed an induced subgraph of

and the inclusion

For each domain
F^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{F}_{F}
,
F^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{F}
and the hierarchy condition of

Lemma 3.9 tells that each domain of CHHF is itself a CHHF with strictly smaller complexity. One may expect various induction arguements on the complexity to understand

Lemma 3.10

Let

be a domain of the CHHF

is uniformly hyperbolic graph with hyperbolicity constant depends only on

Proof of this lemma is quite similar to that in

Definition 3.11

A
co
-
level

, the inclusion
Q^{k}_{F}\rightarrow Y^{k}_{F}
is a uniform quasi-isometric embbedding. The initial case is automatic, since

is bounded set.

Before we run the induction process, we again slightly modify the graphs

and

in a way that assigns to each projection vertices appropriate edges so removing co-level
k+1

Let

be a graph obtained from

by adding edges between the projection vertices of the nesting comparable domains. Note that by the same argument used for

,

is quasi-isometric to

and the inclusion map from

. Moreover, the quasi-isometric embedding
Q^{k-1}_{F}\rightarrow Y^{k-1}_{F}
induces the quasi-isometric embedding
R^{k-1}_{F}\rightarrow Z^{k-1}_{F}
.

By induction argument, we have that the induced map
R^{k-1}_{F}\rightarrow Z^{k-1}_{F}
is a quasi-isometric embedding. Also we already showed that
Z^{k-1}_{F}
is hyperbolic, the link graphs of the projection vertices of the domains containing
F
and have co-level
k
are uniformly hyperbolic, quasi-isometrically embedded. It only remains to show that for each projection vertex of

whose domains has co-level
k
and contains
F
, its link graph in

is contained in
R^{k-1}_{F}
. To show this, note that if
b_{H}\in R^{k-1}_{F}
,
F\sqsubseteq H
and

, then since
b_{H}\in R^{k-1}_{F}\subseteq Y^{0}_{F}\cap CF^{\prime}
, we have that
H\sqsubseteq F^{\prime}
. Also the link graph of
b_{H}
in
Z^{k-1}_{F}
is spaned by the vertices contained in

and the projection vertices

. Since

,
H\cap Y_{F}=H\cap CF^{\prime}\cap Y_{F}
. Since

, whenever

and hence
\{b_{J}\in Y^{k-1}_{F}|J\sqsubsetneq H\}\subset Y^{k-1}_{F}\cap CF^{\prime}
. Since
R^{k-1}_{F}
is an induced subgraph of
Z^{k-1}_{F}
, the link graph of each

is contained in
R^{k-1}_{F}
as desired. By second part of proposition 3.14, the induced map
R^{k}_{F}\rightarrow Z^{k}_{F}
is uniform quasi-isometric embedding and in particular,
Q^{cl(F)}_{F}(=Y_{F}\cap CF^{\prime})\rightarrow Y^{cl(F)}_{F}(=Y_{F})
is a quasi-isometric embedding.

∎

The compatibility of the projection over the complement graphs then follows.

Lemma 3.19
.

If
F\sqsubsetneq F^{\prime}
, then
p^{F^{\prime}}_{F}:Y^{F^{\prime}}_{F}\rightarrow CF
, the projection of the induced complement graph, is uniformly coarsely coincide with the projection map

Proof.

The proof is identical to [proposition 4.11,
[

]
]. Let

. Let
\gamma
be a
Y^{F^{\prime}}_{F}
-geodesic from

to
p^{F^{\prime}}_{F}(v)
. Since

is an induced subgraph of
Y_{F}
,
\gamma

Y_{F}
. Hence
\gamma
fellow-travels with a geodesic of
Y_{F}
joining

and

. Since

,
\gamma
contains some point

(in

It is direct from the definition and lemma 3.19 that the relative projections are uniformly coarsely coincide in all possibilities. so we only need to check the compatibility of the projection maps from

Suppose that

-quasi-isometrically embedded in

Then for any

.

Compare to the strong BGI in

in

Lemma 3.22
.

There exists
C=C(n,\delta)
so that for each

and

, if

then any geodesic
\gamma\subset CX
from

to

intersects

.

Proof.

Put
CX,PF,CF,Y_{F},p_{F}
in place of

in the Strong BGI respectively.
CX
and

are
\delta
-hyperbolic by the hyperbolicity condition of CHHF.
PF
is non-empty since

implies

. Also for each

, either (1)

or

with

, in which case

or (2)

with

, so

is

-hyperbolic by 3.10 and

3.3
Hierarchically Hyperbolicity of CHHF

Now we state our main theorem.

Theorem 3.23
.

Let
(X,W,\mathfrak{F})
be a
(n,\delta)
-CHHF triple and let
(W,\mathfrak{F})
be the corresponding
E(n,\delta)
-proto-hierarchy structure. Then
(W,\mathfrak{F})
is
E^{\prime}(n,\delta)
-hierarchically hyperbolic space.

Proof.

We will use the induction arguement on the complexity
n
in the proof various items to guarantee that for each domain
F\in\mathfrak{F}-\{X\}
, the corresponding CHHF
(F,W^{F},\mathfrak{F}_{F})
induces the HHS structure on
(W^{F},\mathfrak{F}_{F})
. First we check the base case
n=1
, that is
\mathfrak{F}=\{X\}
. Since
\mathfrak{F}
contains all the link graphs of vertices of
X
, so
\mathfrak{F}=\{X\}
implies that no vertex of
X
has non-empty link graph, that is
X
is a discrete set. Also each vertex of
X
is itself a maximal clique subgraph of
X
and no additional projection vertices are added, so
W=CX
which is
\delta
-hyperbolic by hyperbolicity condition and hence
W
is an HHS.

Now consider the proto-hierarchy structure
(W,\mathfrak{F})
constructed in the lemma 3.17. We verify the HHS axioms for
(W,\mathfrak{F})
with constants depending only on
n
and
\delta
(we mostly just check that the constants can be chosen uniformly):

1.

(Hyperbolicity) Hyperbolicity is direct from the hyperbolicity condition of CHHF.

2.

(Container)
To check the container condition, suppose that
F^{\prime}\sqsubsetneq F
and

\{H\in\mathfrak{F}_{F}\mid H\perp F^{\prime}\}\neq\emptyset
. We claim that
(F^{\prime})^{\perp}\cap F
, which is a domain properly nested in
F
, is the container of
F^{\prime}
. Whenever
H\in\mathfrak{F}
such that
H\sqsubsetneq F
and
H\perp F^{\prime}
, we have
H\subset(F^{\prime})^{\perp}
and
H\subset F
, hence
H\subset(F^{\prime})^{\perp}\cap F
as desired.

3.

(Finite Complexity) The finite complexity on the domains is direct from the definition of the graph factor system and our definition of nesting.

4.

(Consistency)
Suppose
F\pitchfork F^{\prime}
. Let
w\in W
be a vertex in
W
, considered as a maximal clique of
X
. Suppose
d_{CF}(\pi_{F}(w),\rho^{F^{\prime}}_{F})>C
, where
C
is the constant in lemma 3.22. By lemma 3.22, the geodesic
\gamma
in
CX
joining the vertex in
w\cap Y_{F}
and
PF^{\prime}\cap Y_{F}
must intersect
PF
. Let
y
be the point on
\gamma\cap PF
closest to
w
. Then the subgeodesic joining the vertex in
w
and
y
does not intersect
PF^{\prime}
, so using lemma 3.22 again, we have
d_{CF^{\prime}}(\pi_{F^{\prime}}(w),\rho^{F}_{F^{\prime}})<C
.

Now consider the consistency of nesting pairs
F\sqsubsetneq F^{\prime}
. Let
w\in W
and consider the geodesic between
w\cap Y_{F}
and
\pi_{F^{\prime}}(w)=p_{F^{\prime}}(w\cap Y_{F})
in
Y_{F^{\prime}}
, say
\gamma\subset Y_{F^{\prime}}
. We have two possibilities: First suppose that
\gamma\cap PF=\emptyset
. Since
F\sqsubsetneq F^{\prime}
implies
Y_{F}\subsetneq Y_{F^{\prime}}
,
\gamma
is a geodesic of
Y_{F^{\prime}}
that is entirely contained in the induced subgraph
Y_{F}
and hence is a geodesic of
Y_{F}
. We apply the strong BGI to
\gamma
:
Y_{F}
is hyperbolic and is an induced subgraph of
Y_{F^{\prime}}
obtained by deleting
PF\cap Y_{F^{\prime}}
,
CF
is hyperbolic, quasi-isometrically embedded subgraph of
Y_{F}
, and
CF\subset N_{2}(lk(x))
for any
x\in PF\cap Y_{F^{\prime}}
,
p_{F}:Y_{F}\rightarrow CF
is the nearest point projection. Hence by Proposition 3.21, the assumption that
\gamma\cap(PF\cap Y_{F^{\prime}})=\emptyset
implies

d_{CF}(p_{F}(w\cap Y_{F}),p_{F}(p_{F^{\prime}}(w\cap Y_{F})))=d_{CF}(\pi_{F}(w),\rho^{F^{\prime}}_{F}(\pi_{F^{\prime}}(w))<C
.

Since both images have uniformly bounded diameter, we have uniform bound on
diam_{F}(\pi_{F}(w)\cup\rho^{F^{\prime}}_{F}(\pi_{F^{\prime}}(w)))

as desired.

Next, suppose that
\gamma
intersects
(PF\cap Y_{F^{\prime}})\neq\emptyset
at some point, say
v\in PF\cap Y_{F^{\prime}}
. Then
CF\subset lk(v)
and for any
u\in CF
,
d_{Y_{F^{\prime}}}(w\cap Y_{F},u)\leq d_{Y_{F^{\prime}}}(w\cap Y_{F},v)+1
. On the other hand, by the definition of
p_{F^{\prime}}
,
d_{Y_{F^{\prime}}}(w\cap Y_{F},u)\geq d_{Y_{F^{\prime}}}(w\cap Y_{F},p_{F^{\prime}}(w\cap Y_{F}))-1
. Hence
d_{Y_{F^{\prime}}}(w\cap Y_{F},\pi_{F^{\prime}}(w))-d_{Y_{F^{\prime}}}(w\cap Y_{F},v)\leq 2
. Since
v,w\cap Y_{F},\pi_{F^{\prime}}(w)
are the points on the geodesic
\gamma
,
v
and
p_{F^{\prime}}(w\cap Y_{F})
have distance at most 2. Since
v\in PF\cap Y_{F^{\prime}}
,
p_{F^{\prime}}(v)\subset\rho^{F}_{F^{\prime}}
and the projection map is uniform Lipschitz map, the distance between the projections
\rho^{F}_{F^{\prime}}
and
\pi_{F^{\prime}}(w)
is also uniformly bounded. This conclude the consistency for nesting.

Finally suppose that
F\sqsubseteq F^{\prime}
and
H\in\mathfrak{F}
such that
F^{\prime}\sqsubsetneq H
or
F^{\prime}\pitchfork H
and
H\notperp F
.
We claim that
d_{CH}(\rho^{F}_{H},\rho^{F^{\prime}}_{H})
is uniformly bounded by constant depending only on
n
and
\delta
. We already obserbed that
F\sqsubsetneq F^{\prime}
implies
PF^{\prime}\subset PF
. So we have
p_{H}(PF^{\prime}\cap Y_{H})\subseteq p_{H}(PF\cap Y_{H})
. Since we defined
\rho^{F^{\prime}}_{H}=p_{H}(PF^{\prime}\cap Y_{H}),\ \rho^{F}_{H}=p_{H}(PF\cap Y_{H})
in all possibilites, we are done.

5.

(Bounded Geomdesic Image)
Suppose
F\sqsubsetneq F^{\prime}
. Let
x,y\in W
and
\gamma
be a geodesic between
\pi_{F^{\prime}}(x)
and
\pi_{F^{\prime}}(y)
in
CF^{\prime}
.

First we claim that if
\gamma
lies sufficiently far from the set
\rho^{F}_{F^{\prime}}
, then
\gamma
is contained in
Y_{F}
. Since
\gamma\subset CF^{\prime}
, we only need to check that
\gamma\cap(PF\cap CF^{\prime})=\emptyset
. Note that
\rho^{F}_{F^{\prime}}=p_{F^{\prime}}(PF\cap Y_{F^{\prime}})
. Since
F\sqsubsetneq F^{\prime}
, we have
b_{F}\in CF^{\prime}\cap PF
and
p_{F^{\prime}}(b_{F})=b_{F}\in\rho^{F}_{F^{\prime}}
. Since both
\rho^{F}_{F^{\prime}}
and
PF\cap CF^{\prime}
have uniformly bounded diameters and have a point in common, their union also has a uniform bound on its diameter, say
E^{\prime}
. If
\gamma
is
E^{\prime}
-far from
\rho^{F}_{F^{\prime}}
, then
\gamma\cap(CF^{\prime}\cap PF)=\emptyset
as desired.

Now we can regard
\gamma
as a geodesic in
CF^{\prime}\cap Y_{F}=Y^{F^{\prime}}_{F}
. Also note that
\gamma
lies far from the augmented subgraph
CF
since
CF
is contained in the 2-neighborhood of
b_{F}\in\rho^{F}_{F^{\prime}}
. The induced CHHF structure of the domain
F^{\prime}
implies that
CF
is a quasi-convex subspace of the hyperbolic space

. Hence, provided
E
is chosen sufficiently large in terms of the hyperbolicity constants, the projection of the endpoints of
\gamma
to
CF
are
E
-close to each other. By the consistency for nesting, the projections of the endpoints
p_{F}(\pi_{F^{\prime}}(x)),p_{F}(\pi_{F^{\prime}}(y))
are uniformly coarsely coincide with
\pi_{F}(x),\pi_{F}(y)
respectively. By modifying the uniform constant
E
with the consistency constant, we are done.

6.

(Partial Realization)
Let
F_{1},...,F_{k}\in\mathfrak{F}
be pairwise-orthogonal domains. For each
i
, let
p_{i}\in\pi_{i}(X)\cap F_{i}
. Since
p_{i}\in F_{i}
and the domains are pairwise-orthogonal,
p_{i}\in F^{\perp}_{j}
for each

, so
p_{i}
and
p_{j}
are adjacent for each

. Hence we can take a maximal clique
w
of
X
containing
p_{1},...,p_{k}
. Since
w
is a maximal clique of
X
,
w
is a vertex of
W
. For each
i
, we have
w\cap CF_{i}
contains
p_{i}
, so
p_{i}\in\pi_{F_{i}}(w)
.

Now suppose
F\in\mathfrak{F}
satisfies
F\pitchfork F_{i}
or
F_{i}\sqsubsetneq F
for some
i
. If
F_{i}\sqsubsetneq F
, then
F_{i}\subsetneq F
, so
p_{i}\in F_{i}\subset Y_{F}
. Hence
p_{F}(p_{i})\subset\pi_{F}(w)
. On the other hand,
b_{F_{i}}
lies in
CF
and hence in
Y_{F}
, which is adjacent to
p_{i}
. Also
b_{F_{i}}\in PF_{i}
. Hence their projections via

are uniformly close, which implies that
\pi_{F}(w)
and
\rho^{F_{i}}_{F}
are uniformly close in

.

When

, again we have
b_{F_{i}}\in Y_{F}
and
b_{F_{i}}
is at most distance 4 from

via any vertex of
F_{i}
and
F_{j\neq i}
, hence their images under
p_{F}
,
\pi_{F}(w)
and
\rho^{F_{i}}_{F}
are uniformly close in

as desired.

7.

(Large Link)
Let
F\in\mathfrak{F}
and
w_{1},w_{2}\in W
be maximal cliques in
X
. First consider the case
F=X
. We need to find
F_{i}
,
i\leq N
with

satisfies

for some
i
.

Let
\gamma
be a geodesic in

joining the closest points in
w_{i}
. For each vertex

F_{i}
by
F_{i}=lk(x)\cap X
if

and
F_{i}=F^{\prime\prime}
if

. (so we take

By Lemma 3.22,
d_{CF^{\prime}}(w_{1},w_{2})>C

. If
\gamma
intersects

at some point, say

Case 1.
x_{i}\in(F^{\prime})^{\perp}
or
x_{i}=b_{H}
and
F^{\prime}\sqsubseteq H
. In this case, since

in both case and

Case 2.

Now suppose

of

to each

), Large Links for

implies that there exists

. Since

are uniformly coarsely coincide with

for every domain

, by increasing the constant

.

8.

(Uniqueness)
First note that by induction on complexity, we can assume that for each non-maximal domain
F\in\mathfrak{F}-\{X\}
, the induced
(W^{F},\mathfrak{F}_{F})
admits an HHS structure.

Let
w_{1},w_{2}\in W
be two maximal cliques such that for every
F\in\mathfrak{F}
,
d_{CF}(w_{1},w_{2})<\kappa
. We need to find
\theta

such that

. We will show this by using induction on
d_{CX}(w_{1},w_{2})=k
.

If
d_{CX}(w_{1},w_{2})=0
, then
w_{1}\cap w_{2}
is non-empty. Let

. Since both
w_{1}
,
w_{2}
are cliques,
w_{i}^{\prime}=w_{i}-{x}
are cliques in

. Furthermore,
w_{i}^{\prime}
are maximals in

and

is a maximal clique in
(lk(x))^{\perp}

4
Factor system of crossing graph

The advantage of the CHHF is that the CHHF machinery covers the most well-studied curve graph with HHS structure, the curve graph of finite type surfaces and the crossing graph of CAT(0) cube complex. Also the HHS structure induced from CHHF curve graph admits the clean container condition and the wedge condition, which was found to be requirement to recover the CHHS from the HHS, so we believe that the CHHF curve graph is useful to study the connection between the HHS and combinatorial HHS theory.

Lemma 4.13

(Hierarchy path)
.

Let
\widehat{CX}
be the factored contact graph constructed in the proof of 4.10. Let
A,B\in V(\widehat{CX})
and let
p\in N(A),q\in N(B)
. Then there exists a geodesic
A=A_{0},...,A_{m}=B
in
\widehat{CX}
and vertices
p_{i}\in N(A_{i-1})\cap N(A_{i})
for
1\leq i\leq m
such that
d_{X}(p,q)=\Sigma^{m}_{i=0}d_{X}(p_{i},p_{i+1})
.

Proof.

We first note that, by the defining property of adjacency of
\widehat{CX}
, if
A,A^{\prime}\in\widehat{CX}
are adjacent, then the associated gated subgraphs
N(A)
and
N(A^{\prime})
intersect. (If
A=b_{F}
, a projection vertex we add, then we denote
N(A)
as the graph
F
. By the construction, if
A=b_{F}
and
A
is adjacent to
A^{\prime}
, then
A^{\prime}
is a hyperplane of
F
and hence
N(A)
intersects
N(A^{\prime})
in all cases.) Let
p_{0}=p,p_{m+1}=q
. Let
D=\Sigma^{m}_{i=0}d_{X}(p_{i},p_{i+1})
. Suppose
A_{i}
and
p_{i}
are chosen such that
D
is minimized. We claim that
D=d_{X}(p,q)
.

Let
\gamma_{i}
be a geodesic joining
p_{i}
and
p_{i+1}
. Suppose for contradiction that
D>d_{X}(p,q)
, which means that the concatenation path
\gamma=\gamma_{0}\gamma_{1}...\gamma_{m}
is not a geodesic. By proposition 4.4, there exists a hyperplane
H
such that
\gamma
intersects
H
more than once. Since each subpath
\gamma_{i}
are geodesic, there exists at least two distinct indices
0\leq i,j\leq m
such that
\gamma_{i}
intersects
H
. If
|i-j|>2
, then the subpath
A_{i}HA_{j}
gives a short-cut, contradicting to the assumption that
A_{i}
forms a geodesic in
\widehat{CX}
. Also if
|i-j|=2
, then the points lying in
A_{i}\cap N(H)
(
A_{j}\cap N(H)
respectively) which is closer to
p_{i}
(
p_{j+1}
respectively) are joined by subpath of
\gamma
that intersects
H
more than twice, so replacing such subpath by the geodesic in
H
and
A_{i+1}
by
H
provides smaller
D
, contradicting to the minimality of
D
. Hence
|i-j|=1
. In this case,
H
separates
p_{i+1}
from
p_{i}
and
p_{i+2}
so using lemma 4.12, we may choose
H
(also modify
\gamma_{i},\gamma_{i+1}
if necessary) such that
H
separates
p_{i+1}
from
p_{i}
and
p_{i+2}
and the edges of
\gamma_{i},\gamma_{i+1}
dual to
H
have
p_{i+1}
in common, that is there exists a triangle (clique)
C
dual to
H
and whose two sides
e_{1},e_{2}
are edges of
\gamma_{i},\gamma_{i+1}
respectively, intersecting at
p_{i+1}
. Since
\gamma_{i}\subset N(A_{i})
and gated subgraphs contains its triangle, we have
C\subset N(A_{i})\cap N(N_{i+1})
. Again, we can replace the subpath
e_{1}e_{2}
of
\gamma
by a single edge contained in
C
that joins end points of
e_{1}e_{2}
which reduce
D
by 1, contradicting to the minimality of
D
. Thus
D=d_{X}(p,q)
as desired.

∎

We will prove the hyperbolicity of the factored contact graph by using the following criterion.

Proposition 4.14

(Bottleneck criterion,
[
13
]
)
.

Let
X
be a geomesic metric space. Suppose that there exists
\delta\geq 0
such that for all
x,y\in X
, there exists a mid point
m=m(x,y)
such that every path joining
x
and
y
intersects the
\delta
-ball about
m
. Then
X
is
(26\delta,16\delta)
-quasi-isometric to a tree.

Lemma 4.15
.

The factored contact graphs are uniform quasi-tree.

Proof.

Let
A,B\in\widehat{CX}
. If
d(A,B)<10
, then the 5-bottleneck criterion is automatically satisfied, so assume that
d(A,B)\geq 10
. In this case, the associated gated subgraphs for
A
and
B
are disjoint and we can find the closest points
p\in N(A),q\in N(B)
such that any hyperplane separating
p
and
q
separates
N(A)
and
N(B)
. Using lemma 4.13, find the hierarchy path
A=A_{0},A_{1},...,A_{m}=B
and
p=p_{0},p_{1},...,p_{m+1}=q
. Let
M=A_{i}
be a point of
V(\widehat{CX})
which is closest to the midpoint of the hierarchy path. Then there exists hyperplanes
H
separating
p_{i}
and
p_{i+1}
. Since the hierarchy path is a geodesic in
X
, the hyperplane
H
separates
p
,
q
and hence separates
N(A)
and
N(B)
by our construction. Also
H
is adjacent to
M
. Now suppose
\sigma\subset\widehat{CX}
is any edge path joining
A
and
B
. Since
H
separates
N(A)
and
N(B)
and the adjacency in
\widehat{CX}
implies non-empty intersection between their associated gated subgraphs, we conclude that there exists some vertex, say
C
, in the path
\sigma
such that
H
separates some points in
C
. It shows that
H
is adjacent to
C
and
M
, hence
C
has distance at most
3
from
M
, which shows the 3-bottlenect criterion of
V(\widehat{CX})
. Using the similar arguement, we can easily extend it to the 5-bottleneck criterion of
\widehat{CX}
.
∎

Comment on “The SPOCK equation of state for condensed phases under arbitrary compression” by R. Myhill
Thomas Ruedas

Abstract
It is shown that the SPOCK equation of state is equivalent to the Variable Polytrope Index equation of state.

In a recent paper,
Myhill 2025
introduced the “Scaled Power Of Compression for K-prime” (SPOCK) equation of state (EoS) for condensed materials under arbitrary compression and demonstrated its validity and excellent performance even to extreme pressures while obeying thermodynamical constraints that many frequently used EoSs fail to satisfy. However, it turns out that the SPOCK EoS is a rediscovery with an independent and arguably more concise derivation of the Variable Polytrope Index EoS presented by
Weppner et al. 2015
. In the following, the equivalence of some essential relations in both papers is shown.

Myhill 2025
expressed the thermodynamic functions in terms of the specific volume
V
or indeed a logarithmic measure of compression,
f
, that is similar to the Hencky strain as used by
Poirier and Tarantola 1998
in their Logarithmic EoS. By contrast,
Weppner et al. 2015
used the density
\varrho
as the independent variable. The relation between them is

f\equiv\ln\left(\frac{V}{V_{0}}\right)=\ln\left(\frac{\varrho_{0}}{\varrho}\right),

(1)

where the subscript 0 indicates the reference state.

Starting from the definition of the bulk modulus,
K
, it and its pressure derivative
K^{\prime}
can then be expressed in terms of
f
:

\displaystyle K

\displaystyle=\left(-\frac{1}{V}\frac{\partial V}{\partial P}\right)^{-1}=-\frac{\partial P}{\partial\ln\left(\frac{V}{V_{0}}\right)}=-\frac{\partial P}{\partial f}

(2)

\displaystyle K^{\prime}

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{\partial K}{\partial P}=\frac{\partial f}{\partial P}\frac{\partial K}{\partial f}=-\frac{\partial\ln K}{\partial f}

(3)

(
Myhill 2025
, Eqs. 2 and 3)
, with
P
designating the pressure.

Pressure derivatives of the bulk modulus.

Both authors used the first pressure derivative of the bulk modulus,
K^{\prime}
, as the foundation of their EoSs;
Weppner et al. 2015
demonstrated that
K^{\prime}
(
B^{\prime}
in the notation used in their paper) corresponds to the polytrope index
n
in the polytrope EoS
P=C\varrho^{n}
, where
C
is a constant. The polytrope EoS is frequently used in astrophysical contexts to describe the properties of matter in stars.

In their Eq. 34,
Weppner et al. 2015
proposed the form

K^{\prime}=\frac{\mathrm{d}K}{\mathrm{d}p}=A_{0}\left(\frac{\varrho_{0}}{\varrho}\right)^{A_{1}}+A_{2}

(4)

for the pressure derivative of the bulk modulus, with
A_{0}
,
A_{1}
, and
A_{2}
being constants. Substituting Eq.
1
into it directly yields the SPOCK form of
K^{\prime}
,

K^{\prime}=ab\exp(af)+c

(5)

with constants
a
,
b
, and
c
, as given in Eq. 8 of
Myhill 2025
. Comparison of the constants in Eqs.
4
and
5
leads to the relations

\displaystyle a

\displaystyle=A_{1}=-\frac{K^{\prime\prime}_{0}K_{0}}{K^{\prime}_{0}-K^{\prime}_{\infty}}

(6)

\displaystyle b

\displaystyle=\frac{A_{0}}{A_{1}}=-\frac{(K^{\prime}_{0}-K^{\prime}_{\infty})^{2}}{K^{\prime\prime}_{0}K_{0}}

(7)

\displaystyle c

\displaystyle=A_{2}=K^{\prime}_{\infty}.

(8)

K^{\prime}_{\infty}
is the pressure derivative of the bulk modulus at infinite compression.

Both authors discussed the need to make assumptions on
K^{\prime\prime}_{0}
and referred to the heuristic relation

K^{\prime\prime}_{0}=-\frac{K^{\prime}_{0}}{K_{0}}

(9)

Bose Roy and Bose Roy 2006
;
Holland and Powell 2011
.
Weppner et al. 2015
adopted this relation directly, whereas
Myhill 2025
advocated the condition
-K^{\prime\prime}_{0}K_{0}\geq K^{\prime}_{0}-K^{\prime}_{\infty}
as a more flexible constraint that includes Eq.
9
because of a strict theoretical lower limit for
K^{\prime}_{\infty}
of 5/3
Stacey 2000
.

Bulk modulus.

Integration of Eq.
4
yields

K=K_{0}\exp\left\{\frac{A_{0}}{A_{1}}\left[1-\left(\frac{\varrho_{0}}{\varrho}\right)^{A_{1}}\right]\right\}\left(\frac{\varrho}{\varrho_{0}}\right)^{A_{2}}

(10)

(
Weppner et al. 2015
, Eq. B8)
. Substitution of Eqs.
1
and
6
–
8
leads directly to the SPOCK version of the bulk modulus,

K=K_{0}\exp b\exp[-b\exp(af)-cf]

(11)

(
Myhill 2025
, Eq. 15)
, which was derived there by integrating Eq.
5
using Eq.
3
, and exponentiating.

Pressure.

Integrating the definition of the bulk modulus written as a function of density from 0 to
P
,
Weppner et al. 2015
found an expression for the pressure as a function of
\varrho
:

P=\frac{K_{0}\exp\left(\frac{A_{0}}{A_{1}}\right)}{A_{1}}\left\{\left(\frac{\varrho}{\varrho_{0}}\right)^{A_{2}}E_{\frac{A_{1}+A_{2}}{A_{1}}}\left[\frac{A_{0}}{A_{1}}\left(\frac{\varrho_{0}}{\varrho}\right)^{A_{1}}\right]-E_{\frac{A_{1}+A_{2}}{A_{1}}}\left(\frac{A_{0}}{A_{1}}\right)\right\}.

(12)

The
E
terms in this equation are the generalized exponential integral

E_{m}(x)=\int\limits_{1}^{\infty}\frac{\exp(-xt)}{t^{m}}\mathrm{d}t=x^{m-1}\Gamma(1-m,x),

(13)

where
\Gamma(1-m,x)
is the incomplete Gamma function

\Gamma(\alpha,x)=\int\limits_{x}^{\infty}\frac{t^{\alpha-1}}{\exp t}\mathrm{d}t

(14)

(
Abramowitz and Stegun 1964
, Eqs. 6.5.3 and 6.5.9)
. Expressing the generalized exponential integrals in terms of the incomplete Gamma function by Eq.
13
, Eq.
12
becomes

P=\frac{K_{0}\exp\left(\frac{A_{0}}{A_{1}}\right)}{A_{1}}\left(\frac{A_{0}}{A_{1}}\right)^{\frac{A_{2}}{A_{1}}}\left\{\Gamma\left[-\frac{A_{2}}{A_{1}},\frac{A_{0}}{A_{1}}\left(\frac{\varrho_{0}}{\varrho}\right)^{A_{1}}\right]-\Gamma\left(-\frac{A_{2}}{A_{1}},\frac{A_{0}}{A_{1}}\right)\right\}.

Using the shorthand

\Gamma(\alpha,x_{1},x_{2})\equiv\Gamma(\alpha,x_{1})-\Gamma(\alpha,x_{2})=\int\limits_{x_{1}}^{x_{2}}\frac{t^{\alpha-1}}{\exp t}\mathrm{d}t

and substituting once more Eqs.
1
and
6
–
8
yields

P=K_{0}\frac{\exp b}{a}b^{\frac{c}{a}}\Gamma\left[-\frac{c}{a},b\exp(af),b\right],

(15)

which corresponds exactly to Eq. 21 in
Myhill 2025
if the reference pressure
P_{0}
is 0.
Myhill 2025
derived this relation by integration of Eq.
2
.

Extensions.

The SPOCK/Variable Polytrope Index EoS is an isothermal EoS. It is worth noting that
Wissing and Hobbs 2020
, who referred to the latter as the “Varpoly EoS”, developped a Mie–Grüneisen–Debye-like thermal extension and also gave consideration to expanded states (
\varrho<\varrho_{0}
), which are relevant in particular for the rarefaction wave that follows the shock in a hypervelocity impact.

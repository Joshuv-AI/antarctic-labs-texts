Hypergeometric Motives From Toric Hypersurfaces
Asem Abdelraouf

Abstract
. In this paper, we study two compactifications of general hypersurfaces defined by the vanishing of linear combinations of d + 2 d+2 monomials in d d -dimensional algebraic tori. We prove that the number of their 𝔽 q \mathbb{F}_{q} -points is given by finite hypergeometric sums under certain general conditions. In the process, we introduce the notion of a gamma triple, which allows us to extend the classical definition of finite hypergeometric sums to prime powers corresponding to the cyclotomic field of definition of the associated monodromy representation. As a special case of our main results, we study the Dwork family and obtain a formula for the number of its 𝔽 q \mathbb{F}_{q} -points. Our results generalise the work of Beukers, Cohen and Mellit for finite hypergeometric sums defined over ℚ \mathbb{Q} .

Application: The Dwork family

In some cases,

coincides with projective space. One such case is the renowned Dwork family

y_{1}^{d+1}+\dots+y_{d+1}^{d+1}-u^{-1}(d+1)y_{1}\cdots y_{d+1}=0

with homogeneous coordinates

Our main result for this family is the following:

Theorem 1.9
.

Let
q
be a prime power such that
\gcd(q,d+1)=1
, and let

Let
\gamma=(-d-1,1,\dots,1)\in\mathbb{Z}^{d+2}

For a vector
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{d-1})\in(\mathbb{Z}/e\mathbb{Z})^{d-1}

\delta(\lambda)=(0,\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{d-1},-\lambda_{1}-\dots-\lambda_{d-1},0).

Then, the number of
\mathbb{F}_{q}

is given by

\#X_{u}(\mathbb{F}_{q})=\frac{q^{d}-1}{q-1}-\sum_{\lambda\in(\mathbb{Z}/e\mathbb{Z})^{d-1}}g\left(\frac{q-1}{e}\delta(\lambda)\right)F_{q}\left(\gamma,\delta(\lambda),e\bigm|u^{d+1}\right).

For each
\lambda\in\left(\mathbb{Z}/e\mathbb{Z}\right)^{d-1}

represented by the gamma triple

2.
Finite hypergeometric sums Over Finite Fields

Recall that we have fixed a non-trivial additive character
\psi:\mathbb{F}_{q}\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}
, and a multiplicative character
\chi:\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}
of order
q^{\times}
.

Lemma 2.1
.

We have the following orthogonality relations:

\sum_{t\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}\psi(tx)=\begin{cases}q\quad&\text{ if }x=0,\\
0\quad&\text{ if }x\neq 0.\end{cases}

\sum_{m=0}^{q^{\times}-1}\chi^{m}(x)=\begin{cases}q^{\times}\quad&\text{ if }x=1,\\
0\quad&\text{ if }x\neq 1.\end{cases}

Definition 2.2

(Gauss Sums)
.

For
m\in\mathbb{Z}
, the
Gauss sum

g(m)
is defined by

\sum_{u\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}}\psi(u)\chi^{m}(u).

For a vector
v\in\mathbb{Z}^{k}
, we define
g(v)\coloneqq\prod_{i=1}^{k}g(v_{i})
.

Note that the Gauss sum
g(m)
lies in the field
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{p},\zeta_{q^{\times}})
, where
q=p^{k}
for
p
prime.

Remark 2.3

(Dependence on
\psi
)
.

The Gauss sum
g(m)
depends on the additive character
\psi
as follows:
Let
\psi^{\prime}:\mathbb{F}_{q}\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}
be another non-trivial additive character. By
[
Coh07
, Proposition 2.5.4]
, there is
a\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
such that
\psi^{\prime}(x)=\psi(ax)
for all
x
. Thus,

\displaystyle\sum_{u\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}}\psi^{\prime}(u)\chi^{m}(u)

\displaystyle=\sum_{u\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}}\psi(au)\chi^{m}(u)

\displaystyle=\chi^{-m}(a)\sum_{u\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}}\psi(au)\chi^{m}(au)

\displaystyle=\chi^{-m}(a)\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}}\psi(x)\chi^{m}(x)=\chi^{-m}(a)g(m).

The sums we will consider in this paper do not depend on the choice of additive character
\psi,
so we have chosen to suppress it from our notation for the Gauss sum.

Lemma 2.4
.

Gauss sums satisfy the following properties:

(1)

g(0)=-1
,

(2)

g(-m)=\overline{g(m)}\chi^{m}(-1)
,

(3)

For
m\neq 0
, we have
g(m)g(-m)=\chi^{m}(-1)q
.

Proof.

See Lemma 2.5.8 and Proposition 2.5.9 in
[
Coh07
]
.
∎

Theorem 2.5

(Hasse-Davenport)
.

Let
N
be a positive integer dividing
q^{\times}
. For any integer
m,
we have

g(Nm)=-\chi(N)^{Nm}\prod_{j=0}^{N-1}\frac{g\left(m+j\frac{q^{\times}}{N}\right)}{g\left(j\frac{q^{\times}}{N}\right)}.

Proof.

See
[
Coh07
, Theorem 3.7.3]
.
∎

Next, we define finite hypergeometric sums. For a review of the history, we refer the reader to the introductions of
[
BCM15
]
and
[
KV24
]
. Recall that we call
(\alpha\,;\beta)

hypergeometric parameters
, if
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{Q}^{n}
, for
n\geq 0
, such that
\alpha_{i}-\beta_{j}
is not an integer for any
i
or
j
.

Definition 2.6

(Finite hypergeometric sums)
.

Let
(\alpha\,;\beta)\in\mathbb{Q}^{n}\times\mathbb{Q}^{n}
for
n\geq 0
be hypergeometric parameters and suppose that
q^{\times}\alpha,q^{\times}\beta\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
.
For any
t\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
, define

F_{q}(\alpha\,;\beta\mid t)\coloneqq\frac{1}{1-q}\sum_{m=0}^{q^{\times}-1}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{g(m+\alpha_{i}q^{\times})g(-m-\beta_{i}q^{\times})}{g(\alpha_{i}q^{\times})g(-\beta_{i}q^{\times})}\chi((-1)^{n}t)^{m}.

The sum
F_{q}(\alpha\,;\beta\mid t)
is called the
finite hypergeometric sum
with parameters
(\alpha\,;\beta)
.

The finite hypergeometric sum
F_{q}(\alpha\,;\beta\mid t)
does not depend on the choice of additive character
\psi
. Indeed, by Remark
2.3
, changing the additive character
\psi
to
x\mapsto\psi(ax)
, for some
a\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times},
changes

\frac{g(m+\alpha_{i}q^{\times})g(-m-\beta_{i}q^{\times})}{g(\alpha_{i}q^{\times})g(-\beta_{i}q^{\times})}

by multiplication by

\frac{\chi^{-r-\alpha_{i}q^{\times}}(a)\chi^{+r+\beta_{i}q^{\times}}(a)}{\chi^{-\alpha_{i}q^{\times}}(a)\chi^{\beta_{i}q^{\times}}(a)}=1.

Thus, for any
t\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
, we have
F_{q}(\alpha,\beta\mid t)\in\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{q^{\times}}\!)
.

Remark 2.7
.

Definition
2.6
above is equivalent to the definition of
[
RRV22
, Equation 10.1]
. Indeed, by part 2 of Lemma
2.4
, we have

\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{g(m+\alpha_{i}q^{\times})\overline{g(m+\beta_{i}q^{\times})}}{g(\alpha_{i}q^{\times})\overline{g(\beta_{i}q^{\times})}}=\chi((-1)^{n})^{m}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{g(m+\alpha_{i}q^{\times})g(-m-\beta_{i}q^{\times})}{g(\alpha_{i}q^{\times})g(-\beta_{i}q^{\times})}.

The conditions on the prime powers
q
in Definition
2.6
are quite restrictive. One of the main goals of this section is to find a different formula for this sum which eliminates some of the congruence conditions on
q
.

Given any irreducible hypergeometric parameters

Proposition 2.13
.

If
(\alpha\,;\beta)
are associated to a gamma triple
(\gamma,\delta,N)
, then
(\alpha\,;\beta)
can be defined over
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{N})

Proof.

To prove the first statement, suppose that
(\gamma,\delta,N)
is a gamma triple and

are the hypergeometric parameters associated to it. Let

denote the left-hand and right-hand sides of Equation (

\sigma(P)/\sigma(Q)=\sigma(P/Q)=\sigma(P^{\prime}/Q^{\prime})=P^{\prime}/Q^{\prime}=P/Q.

Since

The second statement follows by applying the next lemma to the numerator and the denominator of the right-hand side of Equation (

Lemma 2.14
.

Let
A(x)\in\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{n})[x]
be a monic polynomial whose roots are roots of unity. Then,

(10)

A(x)=\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{l}x^{k_{j}}-\zeta_{n}^{r_{j}}}{\prod_{j=l+1}^{l+l^{\prime}}x^{k_{j}}-\zeta_{n}^{r_{j}}}.

for some non-negative integer

and positive integers

(1)

\sum_{j=1}^{l}k_{j}-\sum_{j=l+1}^{l^{\prime}}k_{j}=\deg A(x)

(2)

\gcd(k_{1},\dots,k_{l+l^{\prime}})=1

Proof.

Note that if a decomposition of the form (

Now, let

Proposition 2.15

Let

(\gamma,\delta,N)

\sum_{j}\delta_{j}=0\pmod{N}

Proof.

Assume that
\gamma\in\mathbb{Z}_{\neq 0}^{r+s}

\gamma_{j}<0
for

\gamma_{j}>0
for

To prove the first statement, note that
\sum_{i}\alpha_{i}-\beta_{i}

\sum_{j,i}\alpha_{j,i}-\beta_{j,i}

\{\alpha_{j,i}\}

\{\beta_{j,i}\}

\displaystyle\alpha_{j,i}

\displaystyle=\frac{-\delta_{j}}{\gamma_{j}N}+\frac{i}{-\gamma_{j}},\qquad j=1,\dots,r,\ i=1,\dots,-\gamma_{j},

\displaystyle\beta_{j,i}

\displaystyle=\frac{-\delta_{j}}{\gamma_{j}N}+\frac{i}{\gamma_{j}},\qquad j=r+1,\dots,r+s,\ i=1,\dots,\gamma_{j}.

We compute:

\displaystyle\sum_{j,i}\alpha_{j,i}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{r}\sum_{i=1}^{-\gamma_{j}}\left(\frac{-\delta_{j}}{\gamma_{j}N}+\frac{i}{-\gamma_{j}}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{r}\frac{\delta_{j}}{N}+\sum_{j=1}^{r}\frac{1}{-\gamma_{j}}\cdot\frac{(-\gamma_{j})(-\gamma_{j}+1)}{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{r}\frac{\delta_{j}}{N}+\sum_{j=1}^{r}\frac{-\gamma_{j}+1}{2},

and

\displaystyle\sum_{j,i}\beta_{j,i}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=r+1}^{r+s}\sum_{i=1}^{\gamma_{j}}\left(\frac{-\delta_{j}}{\gamma_{j}N}+\frac{i}{\gamma_{j}}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=r+1}^{r+s}\frac{-\delta_{j}}{N}+\sum_{j=r+1}^{r+s}\frac{1}{\gamma_{j}}\cdot\frac{\gamma_{j}(\gamma_{j}+1)}{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=r+1}^{r+s}\frac{-\delta_{j}}{N}+\sum_{j=r+1}^{r+s}\frac{\gamma_{j}+1}{2}.

Now, since
\sum_{j}\gamma_{j}=0
and
\sum_{j}\delta_{j}=0\pmod{N}
, we have

\sum_{j,i}\alpha_{j,i}-\beta_{j,i}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{r+s}\delta_{j}-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j}\gamma_{j}+\frac{r-s}{2}=\frac{r-s}{2}\pmod{\mathbb{Z}}.

To prove the converse, first note that the gamma triple
((-2,1,1),(0,0,N),2N)
represents the empty parameters
(\emptyset,\emptyset)
, for any positive integer
N
.
Now, suppose that
(\alpha\,;\beta)\in\left(\mathbb{Q}\cap(0,1]\right)^{n}\times\left(\mathbb{Q}\cap(0,1]\right)^{n}
are irreducible hypergeometric parameters with
\sum_{i}\alpha_{i}-\beta_{i}\in\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{Z}
. Let
N
be the least common multiple of the denominators of the
\alpha_{i}
’s and
\beta_{i}
’s.
Let

\displaystyle\gamma

\displaystyle=(-1,\dots,-1;1,\dots,1)\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}\times\mathbb{Z}^{n},

\displaystyle\delta

\displaystyle=(2N\alpha_{1},\dots,2N\alpha_{n},-2N\beta_{1},\dots,-2N\beta_{n}).

Then, the gamma triple
(\gamma,\delta,2N)
represents the parameters
(\alpha\,;\beta)
and satisfies that the integer
\sum_{j}\delta_{j}
is a multiple of
N
. If
\sum_{j}\delta_{j}
is an even multiple of
N
, we are done. Otherwise, we consider the triple

(\tilde{\gamma},\tilde{\delta},2N)=((\gamma;-2,1,1),(\delta;0,0,N),2N)
, which represents
(\alpha\,;\beta)
and satisfies that
\sum_{j}\tilde{\delta}_{j}
is an even multiple of
N
.
∎

2.2.
Finite Hypergeometric Sums from Gamma Triples

In what follows, we would like to define the finite hypergeometric sum associated to a gamma triple
(\gamma,\delta,N)
. The definition extends the definition of the finite hypergeometric sum associated to
(\alpha\,;\beta)
as we shall see below. First, we need some notation.

Definition 2.16
.

Let
(\gamma,\delta,N)
be a gamma triple, and let
q
be a prime power. Let
D_{(\gamma,\delta,N)}(T)
be the greatest common divisor of the numerator and the denominator of the right-hand side of Equation (
8
).
For any integer
m
, define
s_{(\gamma,\delta,N)}(m)
to be the multiplicity of
e^{2\pi im/q^{\times}}
in
D_{\gamma,\delta,N}(T)
. We will abuse notation and write
s_{\delta}(m)
instead of
s_{(\gamma,\delta,N)}(m)
.

Definition 2.17
.

Let
(\gamma,\delta,N)
be a gamma triple.
Let
q
be a prime power which is coprime to
\gamma_{1}\dots\gamma_{d+2}
and such that
N
divides
q^{\times}
.
The
finite hypergeometric sum
associated to
(\gamma,\delta,N)
is

(12)

F_{q}\left(\gamma,\delta,N\mid t\right)=\frac{1}{1-q}\sum_{m=0}^{q^{\times}-1}\frac{g\left(-\gamma m+\frac{\delta}{N}q^{\times}\right)}{g\left(\frac{\delta}{N}q^{\times}\right)}q^{s_{\delta}(-m)-s_{\delta}(0)}\chi^{m}(\gamma^{\gamma}t).

More generally, this definition makes sense if
\delta_{j}q^{\times}/N
is an integer for all
j

We have the following result.

Theorem 2.18
.

Let
(\gamma,\delta,N)
be a gamma triple, and let
(\alpha\,;\beta)\in\mathbb{Q}^{2n}
be the hypergeometric parameters associated to it.
Let
q
be a prime power such that
q^{\times}\alpha,q^{\times}\beta\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
.
Then, we have the following equality

(13)

F_{q}\left(\gamma,\delta,N\mid t\right)=F_{q}\biggl(\begin{matrix}\alpha_{1},\ \ldots,\ \alpha_{n}\\
\beta_{1},\ \ldots,\ \beta_{n}\end{matrix}\>\bigg|\>\>t\biggr).

Proof.

The proof is a direct generalisation of the proof of
[
BCM15
, Theorem 1.3]
. For convenience, we make the necessary modifications to their argument.

Denote by
A_{m}
the
m
-th coefficient of
(1-q)F_{q}\left(\gamma,\delta,N\mid t\right)
(as a polynomial in
\chi(t)
), and suppose that
D_{\gamma,\delta,N}(T)=\prod_{j=1}^{d}(T-e^{2\pi ic_{j}})
. We have

\displaystyle A_{m}

\displaystyle=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{g(m+\alpha_{i}q^{\times})g(-m-\beta_{i}q^{\times})}{g(\alpha_{i}q^{\times})g(-\beta_{i}q^{\times})}\chi(-1)^{nm}

\displaystyle=B_{1}\cdot B_{2}\cdot B_{3},\text{ where }

\displaystyle B_{1}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\prod_{\gamma_{i}<0}\prod_{j=0}^{-\gamma_{i}-1}\frac{g\left(m+\frac{\delta_{i}}{N(-\gamma_{i})}q^{\times}+\frac{j}{-\gamma_{i}}q^{\times}\right)}{g\left(\frac{\delta_{i}}{N(-\gamma_{i})}q^{\times}+\frac{j}{-\gamma_{i}}q^{\times}\right)}

(Hasse-Davenport)

\displaystyle=\prod_{\gamma_{i}<0}\chi(-\gamma_{i})^{\gamma_{i}m}\cdot\frac{g\left(-\gamma_{i}m+\frac{\delta_{i}}{N}q^{\times}\right)}{g\left(\frac{\delta_{i}}{N}q^{\times}\right)},

\displaystyle B_{2}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\prod_{\gamma_{i}>0}\prod_{j=0}^{\gamma_{i}-1}\frac{g\left(-m+\frac{\delta_{i}}{N\gamma_{i}}q^{\times}+\frac{j}{\gamma_{i}}q^{\times}\right)}{g\left(\frac{\delta_{i}}{N\gamma_{i}}q^{\times}+\frac{j}{\gamma_{i}}q^{\times}\right)}

(Hasse-Davenport)

\displaystyle=\prod_{\gamma_{i}>0}\chi(\gamma_{i})^{\gamma_{i}m}\cdot\frac{g\left(-\gamma_{i}m+\frac{\delta_{i}}{N}q^{\times}\right)}{g\left(\frac{\delta_{i}}{N}q^{\times}\right)},

\displaystyle B_{3}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\prod_{j=1}^{d}\frac{g(c_{j})g(-c_{j})}{g(m+c_{j})g(-m-c_{j})}\cdot\chi(-1)^{nm}

(Lemma
2.4
)

\displaystyle=\frac{\prod_{c_{j}=0}\chi(-1)^{c_{j}}\cdot\prod_{c_{j}\neq 0}\chi(-1)^{c_{j}}q}{\prod_{m+c_{j}=0}\chi(-1)^{m+c_{j}}\cdot\prod_{m+c_{j}\neq 0}\chi(-1)^{m+c_{j}}q}\chi(-1)^{mn}

\displaystyle=\chi(-1)^{-dm}q^{\#\{j\mid c_{j}\neq 0\}-\#\{j\mid m+c_{j}\neq 0\}}\chi(-1)^{mn}

\displaystyle(\chi(-1)^{dm}=\chi(-1)^{-dm})

\displaystyle=\chi(-1)^{dm}q^{s_{\delta}(-m)-s_{\delta}(0)}\cdot\chi(-1)^{nm}.

Thus,

\displaystyle A_{m}

\displaystyle=\chi(-1)^{\left(n+d-\sum_{\gamma_{j}<0}\gamma_{j}\right)m}q^{s_{\delta}(-m)-s_{\delta}(0)}\cdot\frac{g\left(-\gamma m+\frac{\delta}{N}q^{\times}\right)}{g\left(\frac{\delta}{N}q^{\times}\right)}\chi(\gamma^{\gamma})^{m}.

Finally, we have
n+d-\sum_{\gamma_{j}<0}\gamma_{j}=0
, which proves the theorem.
∎

Note that if
\delta
is the zero vector, then
(\alpha\,;\beta)
is defined over
\mathbb{Q}
, and this result recovers
[
BCM15
, Theorem 1.3]
.
Indeed, the denominator
g\left(\frac{\delta}{N}q^{\times}\right)
is equal to
(-1)^{r+s}
, and
s(m)=s(-m)
, since the parameters
(\alpha\,;\beta)
are defined over
\mathbb{Q}
.

Remark 2.19
.

Note that if
n=0
in Theorem
2.18
, then

F_{q}(\gamma,\delta,N\mid t)=F_{q}(\emptyset\,;\emptyset\mid t)=\begin{cases}-1&\text{ if }t=1,\\
0&\text{ otherwise,}\end{cases}

whenever the left-hand side is defined.

Proposition 2.20
.

Suppose that
(\alpha\,;\beta)
are irreducible hypergeometric parameters associated to two different gamma triples
(\gamma,\delta,N)
and
(\gamma^{\prime},\delta^{\prime},N^{\prime}).
Let
M
be the least common multiple of
N
and
N^{\prime}
.
Then,

(14)

F_{q}(\gamma,\delta,N\mid t)=F_{q}(\gamma^{\prime},\delta^{\prime},N^{\prime}\mid t)

for all prime powers
q
such that
q^{\times}
is divisible by
M
and
q
is coprime to the
\gamma_{j}
’s and
\gamma_{j}^{\prime}
’s.

Proof.

Write
\sum_{m=0}^{q^{\times}-1}A_{m}\chi^{m}(t)
for the the left-hand side of Equation (
14
) and
\sum_{m=0}^{q^{\times}-1}A_{m}^{\prime}\chi^{m}(t)
for the right-hand side.
If
g=\gcd(N,N^{\prime}),
then
M=NN^{\prime}/g.

Consider the gamma triple

\left((\gamma,-\gamma^{\prime}),\left(\frac{N^{\prime}}{g}\delta,-\frac{N}{g}\delta^{\prime}\right),\frac{NN^{\prime}}{g}\right).

By definition, we have

(15)

F_{q}\left((\gamma,-\gamma^{\prime}),\left(\frac{N^{\prime}}{g}\delta,-\frac{N}{g}\delta^{\prime}\right),\frac{NN^{\prime}}{g}\,\bigg|\,t\right)=\frac{1}{1-q}\sum_{m=0}^{q^{\times}-1}\frac{A_{m}}{A_{m}^{\prime}}\chi^{m}(t).

On the other hand, by hypothesis, the irreducible hypergeometric parameters associated to

\left((\gamma,-\gamma^{\prime}),\left(\frac{N^{\prime}}{g}\delta,-\frac{N}{g}\delta^{\prime}\right),\frac{NN^{\prime}}{g}\right)

are
(\emptyset\,;\emptyset)
, since
\left(\gamma,\frac{N^{\prime}}{g}\delta,\frac{NN^{\prime}}{g}\right)
represents
(\alpha\,;\beta)
while
\left(-\gamma^{\prime},-\frac{N}{g}\delta^{\prime},\frac{NN^{\prime}}{g}\right)
represents
(\beta\,;\alpha)
.
Thus, by Theorem
2.18
, we have

(16)

F_{q}\left((\gamma,-\gamma^{\prime}),\left(\frac{N^{\prime}}{g}\delta,-\frac{N}{g}\delta^{\prime}\right),\frac{NN^{\prime}}{g}\,\bigg|\,t\right)=\frac{1}{1-q}\sum_{m=0}^{q^{\times}-1}1\cdot\chi^{m}(t).

Equations (
15
) and (
16
) give two Fourier series (see Lemma
3.10
below) for the same function. By uniqueness of Fourier coefficients, we have
\frac{A_{m}}{A_{m}^{\prime}}=1
for all
m
. In other words,
A_{m}=A_{m}^{\prime}
for all
m
, which proves the proposition.
∎

Definition 2.21
.

Let
q
be a prime power. We redefine the finite hypergeometric sum

associated to irreducible hypergeometric parameters

F_{q}(\gamma,\delta,N\mid t)
for any gamma triple

.

(Example
2.10
), and for any two gamma triples which represent

2.20
.

This definition provides an extension of Definition
2.6
since for the prime powers

for which

is already defined, the two definitions coincide by Theorem
2.18
and Example
2.10
.

Let
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{N})
be the smallest cyclotomic extension over which some irreducible hypergeometric parameters

can be defined. By Proposition
2.13
, there is a gamma triple
(\gamma,\delta,N)
which represents
(\alpha\,;\beta)
.
The associated finite hypergeometric sum

is defined for all prime powers
q
such that

is divisible by

, minus, possibly, the powers of the finitely many primes which divide the denominators of the
\alpha_{i}
’s and
\beta_{i}
’s.

We summarise the results of this subsection in the following theorem:

Theorem 2.22
.

Let
(\alpha\,;\beta)
be irreducible hypergeometric parameters, and let
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{N})

The finite hypergeometric sum

’s and

’s.

The flexibility in choosing different gamma triples representing some hypergeometric parameters

).

3.
Hypersurfaces of Algebraic Tori

Let
k
be an arbitrary field.
In this section, we consider affine hypersurfaces of the
d
-dimensional algebraic torus over
k
, defined by the vanishing of Laurent polynomials which are linear combinations of exactly
d+2
monomials. Throughout,
Z
is the hypersurface of
\mathbb{G}_{m}^{d}=\operatorname{Spec}(k[x_{1}^{\pm},\dots,x_{d}^{\pm}])
defined by the vanishing of the Laurent polynomial

(17)

f(x)=\sum_{j=1}^{d+2}u_{j}\prod_{i=1}^{d}x_{i}^{m_{ij}},

where
u_{1},\dots,u_{d+2}\in k^{\times}
.
Assume that the exponent vectors
m_{*j}=(m_{1j},\dots,m_{dj})\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
for
j=1,\dots,d+2
are distinct, and do not lie in any affine hyperplane of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. We denote by
\Delta
the Newton polytope of
f
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. It is
d
-dimensional by the condition on the vectors
\{m_{*j}\}
.

Consider the matrix

(18)

M=\begin{pmatrix}1&1&\cdots&1\\
m_{11}&m_{12}&\cdots&m_{1,d+2}\\
m_{21}&m_{22}&\cdots&m_{2,d+2}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
m_{d1}&m_{d2}&\cdots&m_{d,d+2}\end{pmatrix}.

We denote its first row by
\mathbf{1}
, and its
(i+1)
-th row by
m_{i}
. The columns of
M
are
d+2
vectors in
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
, thus they are linearly dependent. Moreover, since the exponent vectors
m_{*j}
do not lie in any affine hyperplane in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, the rank of
M
is
d+1
. It follows that the lattice of linear relations between the columns of
M
is of rank
1
. Thus, there is a vector
\gamma=(\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{d+2})\in\mathbb{Z}^{d+2}
, unique up to sign, such that

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{d+2}\gamma_{j}

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{d+2}m_{ij}\gamma_{j}

\displaystyle=0\text{, }\qquad i=1,\dots,d,

\displaystyle\gcd(\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{d+2})

\displaystyle=1.

We will disregard the sign ambiguity in the choice of
\gamma
, as it does not affect the results under consideration.

Definition 3.1
.

The vector
\gamma\in\mathbb{Z}^{d+2}
is called the
Gale dual
of
f
or
Z
, or
\Delta
.

Let
K
be the kernel of the map
\mathbb{Z}^{d+2}\to\mathbb{Z}
given by
x\mapsto\sum_{j=1}^{d+2}\gamma_{j}x_{j}
. Since
\gamma
is non-zero,
K
is of rank
d+1
. Let
K^{\prime}
be the lattice spanned by the row vectors of the matrix
M
in
\mathbb{Z}^{d+2}
. Since
M
is of rank
d+1
, the lattice
K^{\prime}
is a finite index sub-lattice of
K
.

Definition 3.2
.

We call the index
[K\!:\!K^{\prime}]
the
covering degree
of
Z
, and denote it by
\deg(Z)
.
If
\deg(Z)=1
, equivalently if
K^{\prime}=K
, we say that
f
,
Z
or
\Delta
is
primitive
.

Remark 3.3
.

Gale duality is an important concept in the combinatorics of polytopes. It is defined for general
vector configurations
(see
[
DLRS10
, §4.1]
). The cases we are interested in in this paper are those whose Gale dual is one-dimensional.

Lemma 3.4
.

If
Z
is primitive, then it is isomorphic to the subvariety of
\mathbb{P}^{d+1}
given by

\displaystyle w_{1}+\dots+w_{d+2}

\displaystyle=0,\quad w_{1}\cdots w_{d+2}\neq 0,

\displaystyle\prod_{j=1}^{d+2}w_{j}^{\gamma_{j}}

\displaystyle=t,

where
w_{1},\dots,w_{d+2}
are homogeneous coordinates on
\mathbb{P}^{d+1}
and
t=\prod_{j=1}^{d+2}u_{j}^{\gamma_{j}}
. Furthermore, such an isomorphism is given by

w_{j}=u_{j}\prod_{i=1}^{d}x_{i}^{m_{ij}},\quad j=1,\dots,{d+2}.

Proof.

A proof can be found in
[
RRV22
, §3]
when
\gamma_{j}\neq 0
for any
j
. However, the same argument works if
\gamma_{j}=0
for some
j
.
∎

Remark 3.5
.

The proof of Lemma
3.4
implies that the variety in
\mathbb{P}^{d+1}
defined by
w_{1}\cdots w_{d+2}\neq 0

and
\prod_{j=1}^{d+2}w_{j}^{\gamma_{j}}=t
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{G}_{m}^{d}=\operatorname{Spec}k[x_{1}^{\pm},\dots,x_{d}^{\pm}]
via the coordinate map

w_{j}=u_{j}\prod_{i=1}^{d}x_{i}^{m_{ij}},\quad j=1,\dots,d+2.

If
Z
is not primitive, then it is a covering of a primitive hypersurface:

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
Z^{\mathrm{prim}}
be any primitive hypersurface with Gale dual
\gamma
. Then, there is a covering map

Z\to Z^{\mathrm{prim}}.

This map is étale if the degree of the map is prime to the characteristic of
k.
Furthermore, the degree of the covering map is the covering degree
\deg(Z)
.

Proof.

Fix an isomorphism of the kernel
K
with
\mathbb{Z}^{d+1}
by choosing an integral basis
f_{0},f_{1},\dots,f_{d}
of
K
such that
f_{0}=\mathbf{1}
.
Let
Z^{\mathrm{prim}}
be the hypersurface in
\mathbb{G}_{m}^{d}
defined by

(19)

\sum_{j=1}^{d+2}u_{j}\prod_{i=1}^{d}{x_{i}}^{f_{ij}}=0.

By definition,
Z
is primitive with Gale dual
\gamma
. We note that any primitive hypersurface arises by choosing such a basis.
Write
m_{i}=\sum_{k=1}^{d}c_{ik}f_{k}+r_{i}f_{0}
for
i=1,\dots,d
, and consider coefficient matrix

C=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0&\cdots&0\\
r_{1}&c_{11}&c_{12}&\cdots&c_{1d}\\
r_{2}&c_{21}&c_{22}&\cdots&c_{2d}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
r_{d}&c_{d1}&c_{d2}&\cdots&c_{dd}\end{pmatrix}.

Let
\mathcal{N}
be the Hermite normal form of
C
. Then,
\mathcal{N}=AC
for some
A\in\operatorname{GL}(d+1,\mathbb{Z})
. Furthermore,

A=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0&\cdots&0\\
s_{1}&A_{11}&A_{12}&\cdots&A_{1d}\\
s_{2}&A_{21}&A_{22}&\cdots&A_{2d}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
s_{d}&A_{d1}&A_{d2}&\cdots&A_{dd}\end{pmatrix}\text{, and }\mathcal{N}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0&\cdots&0\\
0&\mathcal{N}_{11}&\mathcal{N}_{12}&\cdots&\mathcal{N}_{1d}\\
0&0&\mathcal{N}_{22}&\cdots&\mathcal{N}_{2d}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&0&0&\cdots&\mathcal{N}_{dd}\end{pmatrix}.

In particular, for all
l,k\in\{1,\dots,d\}
, we have
\sum_{i=1}^{d}A_{ki}c_{il}=\mathcal{N}_{kl}
.
Note that
\det(\mathcal{N})
is equal to the covering degree of
Z
.
We make the change of variables

x_{i}=\prod_{k=1}^{d}y_{k}^{A_{ki}},\quad i=1,\dots,d.

Then,

\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{d}x_{i}^{m_{ij}}

\displaystyle=\prod_{i=1}^{d}\prod_{k=1}^{d}y_{k}^{A_{ki}m_{ij}}=\prod_{k=1}^{d}y_{k}^{\sum_{i=1}^{d}A_{ki}m_{ij}}

\displaystyle=\prod_{k=1}^{d}y_{k}^{\sum_{i=1}^{d}\left(\sum_{l=1}^{d}A_{ki}c_{il}f_{lj}+A_{ki}r_{i}\right)}

\displaystyle(m_{ij}=\sum_{l=1}^{d}c_{il}f_{lj}+r_{i})

\displaystyle=\prod_{k=1}^{d}y_{k}^{\sum_{l=1}^{d}\sum_{i=1}^{d}A_{ki}c_{il}f_{lj}}\cdot\prod_{k=1}^{d}y_{k}^{\sum_{i=1}^{d}r_{i}A_{ki}}

\displaystyle=\prod_{k=1}^{d}y_{k}^{\sum_{l=1}^{d}\mathcal{N}_{kl}f_{lj}}\cdot\prod_{k=1}^{d}y_{k}^{\sum_{i=1}^{d}r_{i}A_{ki}}.

We set
U=\prod_{k=1}^{d}y_{k}^{\sum_{i=1}^{d}r_{i}A_{ki}}
, and note that it is independent of
j
. Thus,

U^{-1}f(x)=\sum_{j=1}^{d+2}u_{j}\prod_{i=1}^{d}y_{i}^{\sum_{l=1}^{d}\mathcal{N}_{il}f_{lj}}\eqqcolon F(y).

Now, note that the hypersurface defined by
F=0
in
\mathbb{G}_{m}^{d}\coloneqq\operatorname{Spec}k[y_{1}^{\pm},\dots,y_{d}^{\pm}]
is isomorphic to the variety in
\mathbb{G}_{m}^{d}\times\mathbb{G}_{m}^{d}\coloneqq\operatorname{Spec}k[y_{1}^{\pm},\dots,y_{d}^{\pm}]\times\operatorname{Spec}k[z_{1}^{\pm},\dots,z_{d}^{\pm}]
defined by the vanishing of the equations

(20)

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{d+2}u_{j}\prod_{i=1}^{d}z_{i}^{f_{ij}}

\displaystyle=0,

(21)

\displaystyle z_{k}-\prod_{i=1}^{k}y_{i}^{\mathcal{N}_{ik}}

\displaystyle=0,\qquad k=1,\dots,d.

Equation (
20
) defines a hypersurface in
\operatorname{Spec}k[z_{1}^{\pm},\dots,z_{d}^{\pm}]
that is isomorphic to
Z^{\mathrm{prim}}
. Furthermore, Equations (
21
) define a covering of
\operatorname{Spec}k[z_{1}^{\pm},\dots,z_{d}^{\pm}]
, since the matrix
\mathcal{N}
has non-zero determinant. Furthermore, it is an étale covering if and only if
\det(\mathcal{N})=[K:K^{\prime}]
is prime to the characteristic of
k
.
∎

Corollary 3.7
.

There exist
d
vectors
\rho_{1},\dots,\rho_{d}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d+2}
such that for
k=1,\dots,d
,

z_{k}=\prod_{j=1}^{d+2}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{d}z_{i}^{f_{ij}}\right)^{\rho_{kj}},\text{ and }\quad\sum_{j=1}^{d+2}\rho_{kj}=0.

Furthermore, if we define

\sigma_{k}\coloneqq\prod_{j=1}^{d+2}u_{j}^{\rho_{kj}},\qquad k=1,\dots,d,

then the hypersurface
Z
is isomorphic to the variety given by

\displaystyle w_{1}+\dots+w_{d+2}

\displaystyle=0,\quad w_{1}\cdots w_{d+2}\neq 0,

\displaystyle\prod_{j=1}^{d+2}w_{j}^{\gamma_{j}}

\displaystyle=t,\quad t=\prod_{j=1}^{d+2}u_{j}^{\gamma_{j}},

\displaystyle\prod_{j=1}^{d+2}w_{j}^{\rho_{kj}}

\displaystyle=\sigma_{k}\prod_{i=1}^{d}y_{i}^{{}_{ki}},\qquad k=1,\dots,d

in
\mathbb{P}^{d+1}\times\mathbb{G}_{m}^{d}
. Here
w_{1},\dots,w_{d+2}
are projective coordinates on
\mathbb{P}^{d+1}
and
y_{1},\dots,y_{d}
are coordinates on
\mathbb{G}_{m}^{d}
.

Proof.

. Let
\mathcal{C}
be the set of all sequences
C=((i_{1},j_{1}),\dots,(i_{l},j_{l}))
of distinct elements satisfying
1\leq i_{1}\leq\dots\leq i_{l}\leq r
and
r+1\leq j_{1}\leq\dots\leq j_{l}\leq r+s
.
The length of a sequence
C\in\mathcal{C}
is
l_{C}=l
. The support
S_{C}
of
C
is the set
\{i_{1},\dots i_{l},j_{1},\dots,j_{l}\}
.
Associated to a sequence
C\in\mathcal{C}
is a cone
\sigma_{C}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
which is given by the convex hull of the rays
(i_{1},j_{1}),\dots,(i_{l},j_{l})
. We associate the zero cone to the empty sequence.
A sequence
C
, or cone
\sigma_{C}
, is said to be maximal if
S_{C}=\{1,\dots,d+2\}
.

Definition 4.6
.

We define
\Sigma^{\prime}
to be the collection of cones
\sigma_{C}
that are not maximal.

Proposition 4.7
.

The collection of cones
\Sigma^{\prime}
is a simplicial fan.

Remark 4.8
.

Given a cone
\sigma_{C}
of
\Sigma^{\prime}
associated to a sequence
C=((i_{1},j_{1}),\dots,(i_{l},j_{l}))
, in part (3) of the proof of Proposition
4.7
below, we show that
\sigma_{C}
is contained in the cone
\sigma_{F}
with
S_{F}=\{i_{1},\dots,i_{l},j_{1},\dots,j_{l}\}
.
In fact, every cone of
\Sigma
which contains
\sigma_{C}
contains
\sigma_{F}
, thus
\sigma_{F}
is the smallest cone which contains
\sigma_{C}
.
Note that this implies that the first meet locus of
\sigma_{C}
, as defined in
[
DL91
, §2.4]
, is the face
F
of
\Delta
.

Proposition 4.9
.

The fan
\Sigma^{\prime}
is a refinement of
\Sigma
.

Remark 4.10
.

The fan
\Sigma^{\prime}
being simplicial implies that the proper toric variety
\mathbb{P}_{\Sigma^{\prime}}
associated to
\Sigma^{\prime}
is quasi-smooth, i.e. it only has finite quotient singularities (see
[
BC94
]
).
This is not so far from being smooth; for instance, over
\mathbb{C}
, the cohomology group
H^{k}(\mathbb{P}_{\Sigma^{\prime}}(\mathbb{C}),\mathbb{C})
is a pure Hodge structure of weight
k
, for each
k
(see
[
Ste77
]
).

The rest of this section is devoted to proving the results of this section.
We note that Proposition
4.1
is well-known; it is a special case of
[
DLRS10
, Lemma 4.1.38 (iii)]
whose proof uses
circuits and co-circuits
. We provide our own elementary proof since we need the notation and ideas to prove the rest of the statements.

Proof of Propositions
4.1
and Lemma
4.3
.

Let
F
be a face of
\Delta
, then
F=\Delta\cap H
for some supporting hyperplane
H
which is given by

\alpha\cdot(y-m_{*j_{0}})=0

for some
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}^{d},
which we assume to be an inward normal to
\Delta
, and
m_{*j_{0}}\in F
. Define
x_{j}\coloneqq\alpha\cdot(m_{*j}-m_{*i_{0}})
for
j=1,\dots,d+2
. Then,

\displaystyle x_{j}=0\quad

\displaystyle\text{ if and only if }m_{*j}\in F,

\displaystyle x_{j}>0\quad

\displaystyle\text{ if and only if }m_{*j}\notin F.

Furthermore,

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{d+2}\gamma_{j}x_{j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{d+2}\gamma_{j}\alpha\cdot(m_{*j}-m_{*j_{0}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\alpha_{i}\sum_{j=1}^{d+2}\gamma_{j}(m_{ij}-m_{ij_{0}})=0.

Conversely, let
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d+2}_{\geq 0}
be such that
x_{j_{0}}=0
for some
j_{0}
and
\sum_{j=1}^{d+2}\gamma_{j}x_{j}=0
. We call such a vector
x
a relation.
Since the rows
\mathbf{1},m_{1},\dots,m_{d}
of
M
are an
\mathbb{R}
-basis of the kernel of
x\mapsto\gamma\cdot x
, there exist a unique
(\alpha_{0}^{x},\alpha^{x})\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that

(\alpha_{0}^{x},\alpha^{x})M=x.

We associate to
x
the supporting hyperplane

H_{x}:\alpha^{x}\cdot(y-m_{*j_{0}})=0.

Then,

\displaystyle\alpha^{x}\cdot(y-m_{*j_{0}})=0\quad

\displaystyle\text{ if and only if }m_{*j}\in H_{x}\cap\Delta,

\displaystyle\alpha^{x}\cdot(y-m_{*j_{0}})>0\quad

\displaystyle\text{ if and only if }m_{*j}\notin H_{x}\cap\Delta.

Note that two relations
x
and
y
define the same hyperplane if and only if
x
and
y
have the same zero components.
Two such relations are said to be equivalent. Thus, there is a one-to-one correspondence between the set of non-zero relations up to equivalence and the set of proper faces of
\Delta
given by

x\mapsto H_{x}\cap\Delta.

We denote the inverse of this map by
F\mapsto[x_{F}]
.
Now, note that since
x_{j}\geq 0
for all
j
and
\sum_{j=1}^{d+2}\gamma_{j}x_{j}=0,
we have
x_{j}>0
for some
j\leq r
and
x_{j}>0
for some
j>r
.
Furthermore, if
F\subset E
are faces of
\Delta
, then the set of indices for which
(x_{F})_{i}=0
is a subset of the set of indices for which
(x_{E})_{i}=0
, and vice versa. This proves Proposition
4.1
.

Finally, note that since the facets of the polytope are maximal with respect to inclusion, we get that facets correspond to relations
x
with exactly two non-zero components. The facet
(i,j)
is the facet corresponding to the class
[x^{(i,j)}]
.
Altogether, this proves Lemma
4.3
.
∎

Proof of Proposition
4.7
.

The proof is divided into a few steps.

(1)

\Sigma^{\prime}
is simplicial:
let
\sigma_{C}\in\Sigma^{\prime}
be the cone associated to the sequence

C=((i_{1},j_{1}),\dots,(i_{l},j_{l})).

We prove that
\sigma_{C}
is
l
-dimensional. To do so, we prove that the rays
(i_{1},j_{1}),\dots,(i_{l},j_{k})
are linearly independent.
Let
\alpha^{(i_{k},j_{k})}
be the inward normal to the facet
(i_{k},j_{k})
corresponding to the relation
x^{(i_{k},j_{k})}
. Recall that there exists a unique
\alpha_{0}^{(i_{k},j_{k})}\in\mathbb{R}
such that
(\alpha_{0}^{(i_{k},j_{k})},\alpha^{(i_{k},j_{k})})\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
is the unique vector such that

\alpha_{0}^{(i_{k},j_{k})}\mathbf{1}+\sum_{r=1}^{d}\alpha^{(i_{k},j_{k})}_{r}m_{r}=x^{(i_{k},j_{k})}.

Suppose for contradiction that
\alpha^{(i_{1},j_{1})},\dots,\alpha^{(i_{l},j_{l})}
are linearly dependent. Without loss of generality, we can assume that there exist non-zero
r_{1},\dots,r_{l}
such that

\sum_{k=1}^{l}r_{k}\alpha_{r}^{(i_{k},j_{k})}=0.

Then, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{l}r_{k}\left(\alpha_{0}^{(i_{k},j_{k})}\mathbf{1}+\sum_{r=1}^{d}\alpha^{(i_{k},j_{k})}_{r}m_{r}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{l}r_{k}x^{(i_{k},j_{k})}

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{l}r_{k}\alpha_{0}^{(i_{k},j_{k})}\mathbf{1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{l}r_{k}x^{(i_{k},j_{k})}.

Thus, for each
s\in\{1,\dots,d+2\}
, we have

(25)

\sum_{k=1}^{l}r_{k}x^{(i_{k},j_{k})}_{s}=c\coloneqq\sum_{k=1}^{l}r_{k}\alpha_{0}^{(i_{k},j_{k})}.

We have two cases:

(a)

If
c=0,
then since
(i_{1},j_{1})\neq(i_{2},j_{2})
, either
i_{2}>i_{1}
or
j_{2}>j_{1}
. Suppose, without loss of generality, that
i_{2}>i_{1}
. Then, the index
i_{1}
only appears once in the sequence
C
. Thus,
for
s=i_{1}
in Equation (
25
), we have

\sum_{k=1}^{l}r_{k}x^{(i_{k},j_{k})}_{i_{1}}=r_{i_{1}}x^{(i_{1},j_{1})}_{i_{1}}=0.

Since
x^{(i_{1},j_{1})}_{i_{1}}\neq 0,
we have
r_{i_{1}}=0
, which is a contradiction.

(b)

If
c\neq 0
, then for each
s\in\{1,\dots,d+2\}
, there is
k
such that
x_{s}^{(i_{k},j_{k})}\neq 0
. This implies that
\bigcup_{k=1}^{l}\{i_{k},j_{k}\}=\{1,\dots,d+2\}
i.e.
C
is maximal, which is a contradiction.

(2)

\Sigma^{\prime}
is closed under taking intersections and faces: observe that any subsequence of
C\in\mathcal{C}
is also in
\mathcal{C}
. Furthermore, a face of
\sigma_{C}
is given by
\sigma_{D}
for some subsequence
D
of
C
. Similarly, for intersections.

(3)

Each cone of
\Sigma
is strongly convex: we prove this by proving that each cone
\sigma_{C}
of
\Sigma^{\prime}
is contained in some cone of
\Sigma
. This proves that
\sigma_{C}
is strongly convex since it is a subset of a strongly convex cone. Suppose that
C=((i_{1},j_{1}),\dots,(i_{l},j_{l}))
. We claim that
\sigma_{C}\subset\sigma_{F}
, where
F
is the face of
\Delta
defined by
S_{F}=\{i_{1},\dots,i_{l},j_{1},\dots,j_{k}\}
(note that
S_{F}
is a proper subset of
\{1,\dots,d+2\}
since
C
is not maximal).
By Proposition
4.4
, for each
k
, the ray
(i_{k},j_{k})
is contained in
\sigma_{F}
, thus
\sigma_{C}\subset\sigma_{F}
.

∎

Proof of Proposition
4.9
.

We need to show that each cone of
\Sigma
is a union of cones of
\Sigma^{\prime}
.
Since
\Sigma
is the normal fan of a polytope, we have
\bigcup_{\sigma_{C}\in\Sigma}\sigma_{C}=\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
By the first part of Remark
4.8
, it suffices to show that

\bigcup_{\sigma_{C}\in\Sigma^{\prime}}\sigma_{C}=\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Let
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a non-zero vector, we want to show that
\alpha
is in some cone
\sigma_{C}
. There exists a unique
\alpha_{0}
such that

(26)

\alpha_{0}\mathbf{1}+\sum_{i=1}^{d}\alpha_{i}m_{i}=x\in\mathbb{R}^{d+2}_{\geq 0}

with
x_{j}\geq 0
for all
j
,
\min_{j}x_{j}=0
, and
\sum_{j=1}^{d+2}\gamma_{j}x_{j}=0
.
Suppose that

(27)

x=c_{1}x^{(i_{1},j_{1})}+\dots+c_{l}x^{(i_{l},j_{l})},

for
c_{i}\geq 0,i=1,\dots,l
, and
i_{1}\leq\dots\leq i_{l}
and
j_{1}\leq\dots\leq j_{l}
. We claim that
\alpha\in\sigma_{C}
, where
C=((i_{1},j_{1}),\dots,(i_{l},j_{l})).

Indeed, writing the right-hand side in terms of the basis elements
\mathbf{1},m_{1},\dots,m_{d}
, we get

\alpha_{k}=\sum_{r=1}^{l}c_{r}\alpha_{k}^{(i_{r},j_{r})}\quad\text{ for }k=1,\dots,d.

Thus,
\alpha=\sum_{r=1}^{l}c_{r}\alpha^{(i_{r},j_{r})}
, as desired.
To prove Equation (
27
), for any such
x
, let

\displaystyle i(x)

\displaystyle=\min\{i\leq r\mid x_{i}\neq 0\},\text{ and}

\displaystyle j(x)

\displaystyle=\min\{j\geq r+1\mid x_{j}\neq 0\}.

Let
x_{f}=\min\left\{\frac{x_{i(x)}}{|\gamma_{i(x)}|},\frac{x_{j(x)}}{|\gamma_{j(s)}|}\right\}
. Then,

x=x_{f}x^{(i(x),j(x))}+x-x_{f}x^{(i(x),j(x))}=x_{f}x^{(i(x),j(x))}+y.

Note that
y_{j}\geq 0
for all
j
and
y_{i(x)}=0
or
y_{i(y)}=0
.
Thus,
y
has one or two more zero components than
x
.
If
y=0,
we are done. If not, then the sets
\{i\leq r\mid x_{i}\neq 0\}
and
\{j\geq r+1\mid x_{j}\neq 0\}
are non-empty, since
\sum_{j=1}^{r+s}\gamma_{j}y_{j}=0
. Furthermore,
i(x)\leq i(y)
and
j(x)\leq j(y)
. Repeating this process, we get (
27
).
∎

5.
Compactifications

As before, let
Z
be the hypersurface defined by a Laurent polynomial

f(x)=\sum_{j=1}^{d+2}u_{j}\prod_{i=1}^{d}x_{i}^{m_{ij}}=0.

Let
q
be a prime power.
We associated to
f
the following data:

(1)

The gale dual
\gamma\in\mathbb{Z}^{d+2}=\mathbb{Z}^{r+s}
which we assume satisfies

\gamma_{1}\leq\dots\gamma_{r}<0<\gamma_{r+1}\leq\dots\leq\gamma_{r+s}.

(2)

A matrix
\mathcal{N}
and vectors
\rho_{1},\dots,\rho_{d}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d+2}
such that
Z
is isomorphic to the variety given by

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{d+2}w_{j}

\displaystyle=0,\quad w_{1}\cdots w_{d+2}\neq 0,

\displaystyle\prod_{j=1}^{d+2}w_{j}^{\gamma_{j}}

\displaystyle=t,\quad t\coloneqq\prod_{j=1}^{d+2}u_{j}^{\gamma_{j}},

\displaystyle\prod_{j=1}^{d+2}w_{j}^{\rho_{kj}}

\displaystyle=\prod_{i=1}^{d}z_{i}^{\mathcal{N}_{ki}},\qquad k=1,\dots,d,

in
\mathbb{P}^{d+1}\times\mathbb{G}_{m}^{d}
.

(3)

The set
\Lambda(q)
of solutions of the system of equations

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}\mathcal{N}_{11}+\dots+\lambda_{d}\mathcal{N}_{d1}

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}\mathcal{N}_{1d}+\dots+\lambda_{d}\mathcal{N}_{dd}

\displaystyle=0

in
(\mathbb{Z}/q^{\times}\mathbb{Z})^{d}
.

(4)

For each
\lambda\in\Lambda(q)
, we defined
\delta(\lambda)\in\left(\mathbb{Z}/q^{\times}\mathbb{Z}\right)^{d+2}
by

\displaystyle\delta(j,\lambda)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\sum_{i=1}^{d}\lambda_{i}\rho_{ij}\in\mathbb{Z}/q^{\times}\mathbb{Z}\quad\text{ for }j=1,\dots,d+2,

\displaystyle\delta(\lambda)

\displaystyle\coloneqq(\delta(1,\lambda),\dots,\delta(d+2,\lambda)).

(5)

The numbers
\sigma_{k}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
,
k=1,\dots,d
, defined by

\sigma_{k}=\prod_{j=1}^{d+2}u_{j}^{\rho_{kj}}.

We want to count the number of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-points of two compactifications of
Z
.

5.1.
Compactification I

The toric variety
\mathbb{P}_{\Delta}
(see
[
DL91
]
and
[
DKi86
]
) is the toric variety associated to the normal fan
\Sigma
of
\Delta
.
It is a compactification of
\mathbb{G}_{m}^{d}
and admits a decomposition into disjoint locally closed subvarieties

(28)

\mathbb{P}_{\Delta}=\bigcup_{F\preceq\Delta}\mathbb{T}_{F},

where
\mathbb{T}_{F}
is a torus of dimension
\dim(F)
, and the union is over all faces of the polytope, including
\Delta
itself.

Let
\overline{Z}
be the closure of
Z
in
\mathbb{P}_{\Delta}
, and, for each face
F
, define
Z_{F}\coloneqq\overline{Z}\cap\mathbb{T}_{F}
. Then,
\overline{Z}
admits a decomposition into disjoint subvarieties

\overline{Z}=\bigcup_{F\preceq\Delta}Z_{F}.

Furthermore, if
f_{|F}
is the restriction of the Laurent polynomial
f
to the face
F
, then the variety
V(f_{|F})\subset\mathbb{G}_{m}^{d}
is isomorphic to the product
\mathbb{G}_{m}^{d-\dim(F)}\times Z_{F}
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.
Thus, to count the number of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-points of
Z_{F},
we count the number of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-points of
V(f_{|F})
and divide it by
(q-1)^{d-\dim(F)}
.

Now, let
F
be a proper face of
\Delta
corresponding to a set
S_{F}
. The restriction of
f
to the face
F
is given by

f_{|F}=\sum_{j\notin S_{F}}u_{j}\prod_{i=1}^{d}x_{i}^{m_{ij}}.

Thus, by Corollary
3.8
,
V(f_{|F})
is isomorphic to the variety given by

\displaystyle\sum_{j\notin S_{F}}w_{j}

\displaystyle=0,\quad w_{1}\cdots w_{d+2}\neq 0,

\displaystyle\prod_{j=1}^{d+2}w_{j}^{\gamma_{j}}

\displaystyle=t,\quad t=\prod_{j=1}^{d+2}u_{j}^{\gamma_{j}},

\displaystyle\prod_{j=1}^{d+2}w_{j}^{\rho_{kj}}

\displaystyle=\sigma_{k}\prod_{i=1}^{d}z_{i}^{\mathcal{N}_{ki}},\qquad k=1,\dots,d,

in
\mathbb{P}^{d+1}\times\mathbb{G}_{m}^{d}
.

To ease notation, we set
G_{m,\lambda}\coloneqq\prod_{j=1}^{d+2}g\left(-m\gamma_{j}+\delta(j,\lambda)\right)\chi^{m}(t)\chi^{\lambda}(\sigma)
.
If
F
is a proper face of
\Delta
, then, by Proposition
3.9
, we have

\displaystyle\#Z_{F}(\mathbb{F}_{q})

\displaystyle=\#V(f_{|{F}})(\mathbb{F}_{q})(q-1)^{-|S_{F}|+1}

(29)

\displaystyle=q^{-1}(q-1)^{d-|S_{F}|+1}+q^{-1}\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda(q)}\sum_{m=0}^{q-2}G_{m,\lambda}(-1)^{|S_{F}|}\prod_{j\in S_{F}}\epsilon\left(-m\gamma_{j}+\delta(j,\lambda)\right).

Furthermore, the variety
Z_{\Delta}
is equal to
Z
, since it is the intersection of
\overline{Z}
with the maximal torus of
\mathbb{P}_{\Delta}
. Thus, by Proposition
3.9
(with
S
the empty set), the number of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-points of
Z_{\Delta}
is given by

(30)

\displaystyle\#Z_{\Delta}(\mathbb{F}_{q})

\displaystyle=q^{-1}(q-1)^{d}+q^{-1}(q-1)^{-1}\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda(q)}\sum_{m=0}^{q-2}G_{m,\lambda}.

Since the varieties
Z_{F}
are disjoint, we have

(31)

\#\overline{Z}(\mathbb{F}_{q})=\sum_{F}\#Z_{F}(\mathbb{F}_{q})=\#Z_{\Delta}(\mathbb{F}_{q})+\sum_{F\precneq\Delta}\#Z_{F}(\mathbb{F}_{q}).

Recall that a proper set
S\subsetneq\{1,\dots,d+2\}
defines a face of
\Delta
if and only if
S^{+}=S\cap\{1,\dots,r\}
and
S^{-}=S\cap\{r+1,\dots,r+s\}
are non-empty. Thus,

\displaystyle\sum_{F\precneq\Delta}\#Z_{F}(\mathbb{F}_{q})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}S\subsetneq\{1,\dots,d+2\}\\
2\leq|S|\leq d+1\\
|S^{+}|,|S^{-}|>0\end{subarray}}\#Z_{F_{S}}(\mathbb{F}_{q}).

The number of faces
F
of
\Delta
for which
|S_{F}|=N
is given by

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m,n\geq 1\\
m+n=N\end{subarray}}{\binom{r}{m}}{\binom{s}{n}}.

Thus, we have

(32)

\displaystyle\sum_{F\precneq\Delta}\#Z_{F}(\mathbb{F}_{q})

\displaystyle=A_{1}+A_{2},\text{ where }

\displaystyle A_{1}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\sum_{|S|=2}^{d+1}q^{-1}(q-1)^{d-|S|+1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m,n\geq 1\\
m+n=|S|\end{subarray}}{\binom{r}{m}}{\binom{s}{n}},

(33)

\displaystyle A_{2}

\displaystyle\coloneqq q^{-1}\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda(q)}\sum_{m=0}^{q-2}G_{m,\lambda}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}S\subsetneq\{1,\dots,d+2\}\\
2\leq|S|\leq d+1\\
|S^{+}|,|S^{-}|>0\end{subarray}}\prod_{j\in S}\epsilon\left(-m\gamma_{j}+\delta(j,\lambda)\right)(-1)^{|S|}.

For fixed
m
and
\lambda
, let
S_{m,\lambda}
be the set of all
j
for which
-m\gamma_{j}+\delta(j,\lambda)=0\pmod{q^{\times}}
. Then,

\displaystyle A_{2}

\displaystyle=q^{-1}\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda(q)}\sum_{m=0}^{q-2}G_{m,\lambda}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}2\leq|S|\leq d+1,\\
S\subset S_{m,\lambda},\\
|S^{+}|,|S^{-}|>0\end{subarray}}(-1)^{|S|}

\displaystyle=q^{-1}\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda(q)}\sum_{m=0}^{q-2}G_{m,\lambda}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|S|=2\end{subarray}}^{|S_{m,\lambda}^{+}|+|S_{m,\lambda}^{-}|}(-1)^{|S|}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m,n\geq 1\\
m+n=|S|\end{subarray}}{\binom{|S_{m,\lambda}^{-}|}{m}}{\binom{|S_{m,\lambda}^{+}|}{n}}.

Note that if
S_{m,\lambda}^{+}
or
S_{m,\lambda}^{-}
is empty, then the sum over
|S|
is
0
. We define

(34)

\eta_{\delta(\lambda)}(-m)\coloneqq\begin{cases}0&\text{ if }\min(|S_{m,\lambda}^{-}|,|S_{m,\lambda}^{+}|)=0,\\
1&\text{ otherwise.}\end{cases}

Then, by Lemma
5.3
below, we have

(35)

\displaystyle A_{2}

\displaystyle=q^{-1}\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda(q)}\sum_{m=0}^{q-2}G_{m,\lambda}\cdot\eta_{\delta(\lambda)}(-m).

On the other hand, by Lemma
5.3
below, we have:

\displaystyle A_{1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{|S|=2}^{d+1}q^{-1}(q-1)^{d-|S|+1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m,n\geq 1\\
m+n=|S|\end{subarray}}{\binom{r}{m}}{\binom{s}{n}}

\displaystyle=q^{-1}\big(q^{r}-(q-1)^{r}\big)\big(q^{s}-(q-1)^{s}\big)-q^{-1}(q-1)^{-1}.

Putting everything together, we get:

\displaystyle\#\overline{Z}(\mathbb{F}_{q})

\displaystyle=\#Z_{\Delta}(\mathbb{F}_{q})+\sum_{F\precneq\Delta}\#Z_{F}(\mathbb{F}_{q})

\displaystyle=q^{-1}(q-1)^{-1}\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda(q)}\sum_{m=0}^{q-2}G_{m,\lambda}+q^{-1}\sum_{m=0}^{q-2}G_{m,\lambda}\cdot\eta_{\delta(j,\lambda)}(-m)

\displaystyle+q^{-1}(q-1)^{d}+q^{-1}\big(q^{r}-(q-1)^{r}\big)\big(q^{s}-(q-1)^{s}\big)-q^{-1}(q-1)^{-1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda(q)}\sum_{m=0}^{q-2}q^{-1}(q-1)^{-1}\cdot\left(1+(q-1)\cdot\eta_{\delta(j,\lambda)}(-m)\right)G_{m,\lambda}

\displaystyle+(q-1)^{r+s-2}+q^{r+s-1}(q-1)^{-1}-q^{r-1}(q-1)^{s-1}-q^{s-1}(q-1)^{r-1}

\displaystyle-q^{-1}(q-1)^{-1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda(q)}\sum_{m=0}^{q-2}(q-1)^{-1}q^{\eta_{\delta(j,\lambda)}(-m)-1}G_{m,\lambda}

\displaystyle+\frac{q^{r+s-1}-1}{q-1}+(q-1)^{r+s-2}-q^{r-1}(q-1)^{s-1}-q^{s-1}(q-1)^{r-1}.

Thus, we have proven the following:

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
\overline{Z}
be the closure of the variety
Z
in the toric variety
\mathbb{P}_{\Delta}
. Then,

\displaystyle\#\overline{Z}(\mathbb{F}_{q})

Remark 5.5

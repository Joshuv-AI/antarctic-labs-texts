1.
Introduction and main Results

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be a bounded domain. The torsion function
u_{\Omega}
is defined by

(1.1)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-\Delta u_{\Omega}=1&\text{ in }\Omega,\\
\ \ \ \ \ u_{\Omega}=0&\text{ on }\partial\Omega.\end{cases}

The study of this function originated with Saint-Venant in 1856 (
[
13
]
).
Shape optimization problems for the torsion function have been studied for various functionals in the literature. Isoperimetric problems for
\left\|u_{\Omega}\right\|_{\infty}
have been known as early as the work by Talenti
[
32
]
for general domains, while results for convex domains have been studied as well (
[
26
,
28
]
). We refer to
[
21
]
for a nice survey of qualitative properties of the solutions of the torsion problem. Spectral functionals involving the torsion function have been studied in works such as
[
3
,
6
,
29
,
34
]
. There have also been many results regarding functionals for
\left\|u_{\Omega}\right\|_{1}
known as the torsional rigidity, such as in
[
2
,
5
,
8
,
9
,
15
,
17
,
18
,
33
]
to name a few. Recently, there has been interest in studying the maximum of the gradient of the torsion function (see
[
4
,
19
,
20
,
23
,
24
]
). To save space, we refer the reader to two recent papers
[
19
,
20
]
for more information on the long history of the problem and related results.

We will study the maximal rate of change of stress, namely
\|\nabla u_{\Omega}\|_{\infty}
.
To make the problem interesting, it is natural to restrict attention to convex domains
\Omega
and to scale them as follows. Let
|\Omega|
be the area of
\Omega
and let
P(\Omega)
be its perimeter. We consider

(1.2)

\displaystyle J(\Omega)=\frac{\|\nabla u_{\Omega}\|_{\infty}}{|\Omega|^{\frac{1}{2}}},\qquad J_{P}(\Omega)=\frac{\|\nabla u_{\Omega}\|_{\infty}}{P(\Omega)}.

Both functionals

J
and
J_{P}
are bounded from above; see, for example,
[
20
]
.

We will study the existence and properties of maximizers of
J
over the class of convex bodies, i.e., convex domains
\Omega^{*}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
such that for every convex
\Omega
, one has

(1.3)

\displaystyle J(\Omega)\leq J(\Omega^{*}).

Similarly, we will provide results about maximizers of
J_{P}
over the class of convex bodies, i.e., convex domains
\Omega^{\prime}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
such that for every convex
\Omega
, one has

(1.4)

\displaystyle J_{P}(\Omega)\leq J_{P}(\Omega^{\prime}).

The present paper has been inspired by an article by Hoskins and Steinerberger
[
19

and personal communications with the second author of that article. Among other results,
[

contains a convincing numerical approximation to the maximizer of

over the family of planar convex domains, showing clearly a line segment on the boundary of the maximizer.

We summarize our main results in the following theorems.

Theorem 1.1
.

(i) There exists a convex maximizer satisfying (
1.3
).

(ii) If
\Omega
is a maximizer, then its boundary
\partial\Omega
is
C^{1}
, i.e., it has no corners.

(iii) If
\Omega
is a maximizer, then its boundary
\partial\Omega
contains a line segment.

Theorem 1.2
.

(i) There exists a convex maximizer satisfying (
1.4
).

(ii) If
\Omega
is a maximizer, then its boundary
\partial\Omega
is
C^{1}
, i.e., it has no corners.

(iii) If
\Omega
is a maximizer, then its boundary
\partial\Omega
contains a line segment.

The above results improve (some of) recent theorems proved in
[
20
]
, where it is shown that
if
\Omega
is a convex maximizer, then either the boundary of
\Omega
is not
C^{2+\varepsilon}
for some
\varepsilon>0
or
the points of zero curvature on
\partial\Omega
accumulate around the point at which
the gradient is maximal. The existence of a convex optimizer is proved neither in
[
20
]

nor in any earlier paper, to our best knowledge.

On the technical side, we will prove a new version of the boundary Harnack principle
in Proposition
4.1
. Roughly speaking, the proposition says that, in a Lipschitz domain
with the Lipschitz constant less than 1, the torsion function is comparable to a harmonic function
with zero boundary values in a neighborhood of the boundary.

We organize the paper as follows. In Section
2
, we
present notation, definitions, and review some known results.
Section

contains several versions
of the boundary Harnack principle for harmonic functions.
A new boundary Harnack principle for the torsion function
is proved in Section
4
. Section
5
is devoted
to the boundary behavior of the torsion function.
The three parts of Theorem
1.1
are proved in Sections

6
,
7
and
8
.
The proof of Theorem
1.2
, presented in Section
9
, is short because it is
essentially the same as that of Theorem
1.1
.

2.
Preliminaries

We start with some notation, conventions and observations.

We will use both complex and vector notation, identifying
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
and
\mathbb{C}
.
The standard basis in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
will be denoted by
(\mathbf{e}_{1},\mathbf{e}_{2})
.
The area of a set

will be denoted by
|A|
, its perimeter by
P(A)
, its diameter by
d(A)
, and its inradius (i.e., the radius of the largest disc contained in

) by
r(A)
.

By abuse of notation, we will also write
|x|
to denote the norm of
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
.
We will denote balls in different norms as

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(x,r)

\displaystyle=\{y:|x-y|<r\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}(r)

\displaystyle=\{(x_{1},x_{2}):\max(|x_{1}|,|x_{2}|)<r\}.

Let
G(x,y)
denote the Green’s function in
\Omega
with Dirichlet boundary values. Then
the torsion function
u_{\Omega}(x)
defined in (
1.1
) can be represented as

(2.1)

\displaystyle u_{\Omega}(x)=\int_{\Omega}G(x,y)dy.

Actually, the equality in (
2.1
) is “up to a constant.” We normalize the Green’s function so that (
2.1
) holds as stated.

A function
f:{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
is called Lipschitz with the Lipschitz constant
\lambda<\infty
if

|f(x)-f(y)|\leq\lambda|x-y|
for all
x
and
y
.
A domain
\Omega
is called Lipschitz with the Lipschitz constant
\lambda
if for every boundary point, there is a neighborhood
and an orthonormal coordinate system such that
\partial\Omega
is the graph of a Lipschitz function with constant
\lambda

in this neighborhood.

Let
W_{t}
denote a planar Brownian motion and let
{\mathbb{P}}_{x}
and
{\mathbb{E}}_{x}
denote its distribution
and the associated expectation corresponding to the starting point
W_{0}=x
. For a set
A\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
, let
\tau(A):=\inf\{t\geq 0:W_{t}\in A\}

and
\sigma(A):=\inf\{t\geq 0:W_{t}\notin A\}
.
If
A\subset\Omega
and
h
is the positive harmonic function
in
\Omega\setminus A
with zero boundary values on
\partial\Omega
and boundary values equal to 1
on
\partial A
then
h(x)={\mathbb{P}}_{x}(\tau(A)<\sigma(\Omega))
for
x\in\Omega\setminus A
.

The torsion function has the following probabilistic representation,
u_{\Omega}(x)={\mathbb{E}}_{x}(\sigma(\Omega))
for any open set
\Omega
and
x\in\Omega
.
We note that Brownian motion’s infinitesimal generator is one-half of the usual Laplacian, so the last formula holds
not for the torsion function defined in (
1.1
) but for its
constant multiple. This is irrelevant to our arguments so we will ignore
the factor
1/2
in our formulas for typographical reasons.

The following definitions are adapted from
[
10
, p. 9]
and
[
14
]
.
Suppose that
\Omega
is a planar domain,
z\in\partial\Omega
and
y\in\Omega
. A set
A\subset\Omega
is called minimal thin at
z
if
\tau(A)>0
, a.s., where
\tau(A)
is defined relative to Brownian motion starting from
z
and conditioned to go to
y
before hitting
\partial\Omega
(except at time 0). Note that the notion of minimal tinniness does not depend on the choice of
y\in\Omega
. A set
A\subset\Omega
is called a minimal fine neighborhood of

if
\Omega\setminus A
is minimal thin at
z
.

A function
f:\Omega\to{\mathbb{R}}
has a minimal fine limit
b
at
z
if
\lim_{t\downarrow 0}f(W_{t})=b
, a.s., where
W
is Brownian motion starting from
z
and conditioned to go to
y\in\Omega
before hitting
\partial\Omega
(except at time 0). Here also, we note that the notion of minimal fine limit is independent of the choice of the interior point
y\in\Omega
.

Remark 2.1
.

(i)
A simple calculation (see
[
31
, p. 85]
) leads to

(2.2)

\displaystyle\Delta|\nabla u_{\Omega}|^{2}

\displaystyle=2\left((\partial^{2}_{xx}u_{\Omega})^{2}+(\partial^{2}_{yy}u_{\Omega})^{2}+(\partial^{2}_{xy}u_{\Omega})^{2}+(\partial^{2}_{yx}u_{\Omega})^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\geq 2\left(\frac{1}{2}(\partial^{2}_{xx}u_{\Omega}+\partial^{2}_{yy}u_{\Omega})^{2}+(\partial^{2}_{xy}u_{\Omega})^{2}+(\partial^{2}_{yx}u_{\Omega})^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=1+2\left((\partial^{2}_{xy}u_{\Omega})^{2}+(\partial^{2}_{yx}u_{\Omega})^{2}\right)>0.

In other words, the function
|\nabla u_{\Omega}|^{2}
is subharmonic.
By the maximum principle,

cannot reach its maximum
in the interior.

(ii)
By the result of
[

]
(see translation in

]

are convex.

(iii)
The Blaschke selection theorem states that, given a sequence

of convex bodies contained in a bounded set, there exists a subsequence
(K_{n_{m}})
and a convex body

such that
(K_{n_{m}})
converges to

in the Hausdorff metric. See
[

or
[

4.
Boundary Harnack principle for torsion function

Proposition 4.1
.

Suppose that
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
is an open set and
A\subset\Omega
is a disc with a positive radius such that the distance from
A
to
\partial\Omega
is greater than 0. Recall
u_{\Omega}
defined in (
1.1
).
Let
h:x\longmapsto{\mathbb{P}}_{x}(\tau(A)<\sigma(\Omega))
. The function
h
is the unique positive harmonic function in
\Omega\setminus A
with boundary values 1 on
\partial A
and 0 on
\partial\Omega
. We have:

(i)

For some
c_{0}>0
depending on
\Omega
and
A
, and all
x\in\Omega\setminus A
,

(4.1)

\displaystyle u_{\Omega}(x)\geq c_{0}h(x).

(ii)

If
\Omega
is a bounded Lipschitz domain with the Lipschitz constant
\lambda<1
, then, for
some
c_{1}>0
depending on
\Omega
and
A
, and all
x\in\Omega\setminus A
,

(4.2)

\displaystyle h(x)\geq c_{1}u_{\Omega}(x).

Proof.

(i)
It is easy to see that for some
c_{0}>0
and all
y\in A
,
{\mathbb{E}}_{y}(\sigma(\Omega))\geq c_{0}
, so for
x\in\Omega\setminus A
,

\displaystyle u_{\Omega}(x)

\displaystyle={\mathbb{E}}_{x}(\sigma(\Omega))\geq\int_{\partial A}{\mathbb{E}}_{y}(\sigma(\Omega)){\mathbb{P}}_{x}(W_{\tau(A)}\in dy,\tau(A)<\sigma(\Omega))

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\partial A}c_{0}{\mathbb{P}}_{x}(W_{\tau(A)}\in dy,\tau(A)<\sigma(\Omega))=c_{0}{\mathbb{P}}_{x}(\tau(A)<\sigma(\Omega))=c_{0}h(x).

This proves (
4.1
).

(ii)

Let
G(x,y)
be the Green’s function in
\Omega
with Dirichlet boundary values.
Fix
z_{1}\in\Omega
and let
B=\{x\in\Omega:G(z_{1},x)\geq 1\}
.

Suppose that
\sup_{x\in\Omega\setminus A}u_{\Omega}(x)/h(x)=+\infty
.
We will show that this assumption leads to a contradiction.

Let
(y_{k})_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}
be a sequence of points in
\Omega
such that
u_{\Omega}(y_{k})/h(y_{k})>k
for all
k
. The distance from
y_{k}
to
\partial\Omega
must decrease to
0
because

u_{\Omega}
and
h

are continuous and strictly positive functions in
\Omega\setminus A

and
h
has boundary values
1
on
\partial A
.

Let
v_{k}
be one of the points in
\partial\Omega
with the smallest distance to
y_{k}
.
The Martin and Euclidean boundaries can be identified in Lipschitz domains
so let
K_{k}(x)
be the Martin kernel in
\Omega
associated with
v_{k}
,
i.e., a positive harmonic function which vanishes continuously at every point of
\partial\Omega

except at
v_{k}
.
We normalize
K_{k}
so that
K_{k}(z_{1})=1
.

If
r_{k}=|y_{k}-v_{k}|
, Lemma
3.1

(ii)

applied
to the positive harmonic functions
z\longmapsto G(y_{k},z)
and
z\longmapsto K_{k}(z)

in
(\mathcal{B}(v_{k},3r_{k})\setminus\mathcal{B}(v_{k},2r_{k}))\cap\Omega

implies that there is
c_{3}>0
such that for
z,z^{\prime}\in(\mathcal{B}(v_{k},3r_{k})\setminus\mathcal{B}(v_{k},2r_{k}))\cap\Omega
,

(4.3)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{c_{3}}\frac{G(y_{k},z^{\prime})}{K_{k}(z^{\prime})}\leq\frac{G(y_{k},z)}{K_{k}(z)}\leq c_{3}\frac{G(y_{k},z^{\prime})}{K_{k}(z^{\prime})}.

The constant
c_{3}
depends only on
\lambda
because, by scaling and the Lipschitz property of
\Omega
, one can choose
m
in Lemma
3.1

(ii)
independent of
k
in the present argument.

Fix any

and let
a_{k}=G(y_{k},z^{\prime})/K_{k}(z^{\prime})
.
Then (
4.3
) yields

(4.4)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{c_{3}}a_{k}K_{k}(z)\leq G(y_{k},z)\leq c_{3}a_{k}K_{k}(z),

for all
z\in(\mathcal{B}(v_{k},3r_{k})\setminus\mathcal{B}(v_{k},2r_{k}))\cap\Omega
.

The functions
z\longmapsto G(y_{k},z)
and

are positive and harmonic in

. Their boundary values satisfy (

) on

and they are 0 on the remaining part of the boundary of

Hence, (

\displaystyle\frac{1}{c_{3}}a_{k}=\frac{1}{c_{3}}a_{k}K_{k}(z_{1})\leq G(y_{k},z_{1})\leq c_{3}a_{k}K_{k}(z_{1})=c_{3}a_{k},

\displaystyle\frac{G(y_{k},z)}{G(y_{k},z_{1})}\leq\frac{c_{3}}{a_{k}}c_{3}a_{k}K_{k}(z)=c_{3}^{2}K_{k}(z),

for

The functions

and

In view of (

\displaystyle c_{4}k

\displaystyle<\frac{u_{\Omega}(y_{k})}{G(z_{1},y_{k})}=\frac{\int_{\Omega}G(y_{k},z)dz}{G(z_{1},y_{k})}=\int_{\Omega}\frac{G(y_{k},z)}{G(y_{k},z_{1})}dz

with Dirichlet boundary values.
Note that
\mathcal{B}(v_{k},2r_{k})\subset\mathcal{B}(y_{k},3r_{k})
and
3r_{k}<\rho
for large

.
We have for some
c_{5},c_{6}<\infty
and sufficiently large

,

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega\cap\mathcal{B}(v_{k},2r_{k})}G(y_{k},z)dz

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathcal{B}(y_{k},3r_{k})}G_{k}(y_{k},z)dz\leq c_{5}\int_{0}^{3r_{k}}r|\log r|dr\leq c_{6}r_{k}^{2}|\log r_{k}|.

By
[
25
, Prop. 2]
, if
\lambda<1
then for some
c_{7}>0
and
\alpha<2
,

\displaystyle G(y_{k},z_{1})\geq c_{7}r_{k}^{\alpha}.

This and (
4.7
) imply that

\displaystyle 0\leq\int_{\Omega\cap\mathcal{B}(v_{k},2r_{k})}\frac{G(y_{k},z)}{G(y_{k},z_{1})}dz\leq\frac{c_{6}r_{k}^{2}|\log r_{k}|}{c_{7}r_{k}^{\alpha}}=(c_{6}/c_{7})r_{k}^{2-\alpha}|\log r_{k}|.

The quantity on the right-hand side converges to 0 as
k\to+\infty
because
r_{k}\underset{k\to+\infty}{\longrightarrow}0
.
This observation and (
4.6
) imply that for sufficiently large
k
,

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}K_{k}(z)dz\geq\int_{\Omega\setminus\mathcal{B}(v_{k},2r_{k})}K_{k}(z)dz\geq\frac{c_{4}k}{2c_{3}^{2}}.

Since the kernels
K_{k}
were normalized so that
K_{k}(z_{1})=1
, the
function
K_{*}:z\longmapsto\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}2^{-j}K_{2^{2j}}(z)

is finite, positive and harmonic. We have

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}K_{*}(z)dz\geq\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}2^{-j}\frac{c_{4}2^{2j}}{2c_{3}^{2}}=\infty.

This contradicts
[
1
, Thm. 2]
which asserts that all positive harmonic functions are integrable
in
\Omega
if
\lambda<1
.
∎

5.
Boundary behavior of the torsion function

For the definition of minimal fine topology and the corresponding convergence, see Section
2
.

Lemma 5.1
.

Suppose
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
is a bounded open convex domain.

(a)

There exists a set
\partial_{r}\Omega\subset\partial\Omega
with the full harmonic measure such that the following holds for
z\in\partial_{r}\Omega
.

(i)

The boundary of
\Omega
does not have a corner at
z
. We will denote the unit inner normal vector
\mathbf{n}(z)
.

(ii)

The minimal fine limit
\mflim_{x\to z,x\in\Omega}|\nabla u_{\Omega}(z)|
exists. We will denote it
|\nabla u_{\Omega}(z)|_{\mathrm{mf}}
.

(iii)

We have
\lim_{x\to z,x\in\Omega}\frac{\nabla u_{\Omega}(x)}{|\nabla u_{\Omega}(x)|}=\mathbf{n}(z)
.

(iv)

\lim_{\delta\to 0}\frac{1}{\delta}u_{\Omega}(z+\delta\mathbf{n}(z))=|\nabla u_{\Omega}(z)|_{\mathrm{mf}}
.

(b)

\sup_{y\in\partial_{r}\Omega}|\nabla u_{\Omega}(y)|_{\mathrm{mf}}/|\Omega|^{1/2}=\sup_{x\in\Omega}|\nabla u_{\Omega}(x)|/|\Omega|^{1/2}=J(\Omega)
.

(c)

There exists
z\in\partial\Omega
such that for every
n>0
there exists
a neighborhood
U_{n}\subset\partial\Omega
of
z
such that the harmonic measure of

\{y\in U_{n}\cap\partial_{r}\Omega:|\nabla u_{\Omega}(y)|_{\mathrm{mf}}/|\Omega|^{1/2}>J(\Omega)-1/n\}
is strictly positive.

Proof.

(a)
(i)
A Brownian motion does not hit a point fixed in advance. There are at most countably many corners on the boundary of a convex domain. The probability of hitting a corner is zero. Hence, the harmonic measure is carried by boundary points without corners.

(ii)
The function
x\longmapsto|\nabla u_{\Omega}(x)|^{2}
is subharmonic in
\Omega
by (
2.2
). It follows that if
W_{t}
is Brownian motion then
|\nabla u_{\Omega}(W_{t})|^{2}
is a continuous submartingale on the interval
[0,\sigma(\Omega))
. Since
x\longmapsto|\nabla u_{\Omega}(x)|^{2}
is bounded in
\Omega
, the submartingale has a left limit at the time
\sigma(\Omega)
, a.s. This implies that the minimal fine limit
\mflim_{x\to z,x\in\Omega}|\nabla u_{\Omega}(z)|^{2}
exists for a set
\partial_{r}\Omega
of
z
’s with the full harmonic measure. It follows that the minimal fine limit
\mflim_{x\to z,x\in\Omega}|\nabla u_{\Omega}(z)|
exists for
z\in\partial_{r}\Omega
.

(iii)
By the result of
[
27
]
(translation in
[
26
]
), the superlevel sets of the function
u
are convex. This easily implies that
(iii)
holds at all non-corner points of
\partial\Omega
.

(iv)
By
[
10
, Thm. 10.1]
, all excursion laws for Brownian motion in a planar convex domain are quasi-locally flat. By
[
10
, Rem. 10.3
(iii)
]
, these excursion laws are actually locally flat. By
[
10
, Def. 7.1]
, the local properties of these excursion laws are the same as those of excursions in a half-space.

Suppose that
z\in\partial\Omega
and the excursion law at
z
is locally flat. Let
L
be the halfline with the endpoint
z
, stretching in the direction of
\mathbf{n}(z)
. Suppose that
A
is a given subset of
L
and let
A_{k}=\{y\in A:2^{-k-1}<|z-y|\leq 2^{-k}\}
for
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Suppose that
\limsup_{k\to\infty}2^{k}|A_{k}|>0
. Then, standard arguments similar to the Wiener test show that
A
is not minimal thin at
0
.

For any
\varepsilon>0
,
we let
A(\varepsilon)=\{y\in L:\big||\nabla u_{\Omega}(y)|-|\nabla u_{\Omega}(z)|_{\mathrm{mf}}\big|>\varepsilon\}
. By part
(ii)
, the set
A(\varepsilon)
is minimal thin at
z
for every
\varepsilon>0
. Hence, if
A_{k}(\varepsilon)=\{y\in A(\varepsilon):2^{-k-1}<|z-y|\leq 2^{-k}\}
, then,
\limsup_{k\to\infty}2^{k}|A_{k}(\varepsilon)|=0
.
This and the fact that
|\nabla u_{\Omega}|
is bounded imply that

\displaystyle\lim_{\delta\to 0}\frac{1}{\delta}\int_{y\in L,|z-y|<\delta}|\nabla u_{\Omega}(y)|dy=|\nabla u_{\Omega}(z)|_{\mathrm{mf}}.

In view of
(iii)
, we conclude that
(iv)
holds.

(b)
Since
|\nabla u_{\Omega}(W_{t})|^{2}
is a continuous submartingale on
[0,\sigma(\Omega))
, by the optional stopping theorem, for every
x\in\Omega
,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}_{x}\left(\lim_{t\uparrow\sigma(\Omega)}|\nabla u_{\Omega}(W_{t})|^{2}\right)\geq|\nabla u_{\Omega}(x)|^{2},

and, therefore,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}_{x}\left(\lim_{t\uparrow\sigma(\Omega)}|\nabla u_{\Omega}(W_{t})|\geq|\nabla u_{\Omega}(x)|\right)>0.

This implies that the harmonic measure of
\{z\in\partial\Omega:\ |\nabla u_{\Omega}(z)|_{\mathrm{mf}}\geq|\nabla u_{\Omega}(x)|\}
is strictly positive.

It follows that
\sup_{y\in\partial_{r}\Omega}|\nabla u_{\Omega}(y)|_{\mathrm{mf}}\geq\sup_{x\in\Omega}|\nabla u_{\Omega}(x)|
. The opposite inequality follows from the continuity of
|\nabla u_{\Omega}(W_{t})|
.

(c)
If the claim in part
(c)
is false, then, for every
z\in\partial\Omega
, there is a neighborhood
U(z)\subset\partial\Omega
of
z
and
\varepsilon>0
, such that

|\{y\in U(z):|\nabla u_{\Omega}(y)|_{\mathrm{mf}}/|\Omega|^{1/2}>J(\Omega)-\varepsilon\}|=0
. By compactness, we can cover
\partial\Omega
by a finite number of such neighborhoods. Therefore, for some
\varepsilon_{1}>0
,

|\{y\in\partial\Omega:|\nabla u_{\Omega}(y)|_{\mathrm{mf}}/|\Omega|^{1/2}>J(\Omega)-\varepsilon_{1}\}|=0
. This contradicts the observation made in part (b) of the proof.

∎

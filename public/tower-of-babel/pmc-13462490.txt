Comment on “Immunotherapy for locoregionally advanced nasopharyngeal carcinoma: A meta-analysis of randomized clinical trials”
Authors: Erkan Topkan, Ugur Selek
Published: 2026

Dear Editor,

We read with great interest the meta-analysis by Stawarz and colleagues synthesizing phase III data on programmed cell death protein 1 (PD-1) blockade combined with definitive chemoradiotherapy (CRT) for locoregionally advanced nasopharyngeal carcinoma (LANPC). 1 Contemporary LANPC management is increasingly shaped by risk-adapted systemic therapy, including refined selection for induction chemotherapy and immunotherapy-based intensification, alongside updated staging frameworks that aim to better capture prognostic heterogeneity. Recent evidence further underscores the need to align systemic-treatment individualization with evolving staging classifications.2,3 By focusing exclusively on randomized phase III trials, the authors provide a timely and methodologically robust quantitative synthesis demonstrating consistent improvements in event-/failure-free survival (EFS/FFS) and distant metastasis–free survival (DMFS) without increased severe toxicity. This analysis is both clinically meaningful and scientifically rigorous. Nonetheless, two interpretive considerations merit discussion to further refine the contextual understanding and translational implications of these findings. These considerations are particularly relevant as PD-1 blockade moves toward broader integration into curative-intent protocols, where distinctions between therapeutic augmentation and substitution, as well as the evidentiary thresholds required for practice-changing recommendations, carry significant implications for clinical decision-making and guideline development.

First, the pooled estimate integrates trials that test biologically and clinically distinct therapeutic hypotheses. CONTINUUM and DIPPER evaluated PD-1 blockade in addition to standard cisplatin-based CRT—representing an augmentation strategy—whereas DIAMOND assessed a cisplatin-sparing approach combining PD-1 blockade with radiotherapy while omitting platinum. 1 Subgroup analyses indicate that survival benefits were confined to cisplatin-retained regimens, whereas the cisplatin-sparing strategy did not improve EFS/FFS, DMFS, or locoregional relapse–free survival. 1 This distinction is not merely statistical but mechanistic. Platinum compounds serve as radiosensitizers and promote immunogenic cell death, increasing neoantigen exposure and facilitating T-cell priming—effects that may synergize with immune checkpoint inhibition. In this framework, PD-1 blockade appears to potentiate the cytotoxic and immunomodulatory effects of platinum-based CRT rather than substitute for them. The absence of benefit in the cisplatin-omission setting suggests that platinum and immunotherapy are non-interchangeable in the curative-intent context. Clinically, the available evidence therefore supports integration of PD-1 blockade with—rather than in place of—cisplatin-based CRT in appropriately selected patients.

Second, it is essential to calibrate the strength of recommendations for routine integration of PD-1 blockade into standard therapy by carefully considering the maturity of survival data and the evidentiary limitations of surrogate endpoints. In the meta-analysis, the pooled hazard ratio for overall survival (OS) was 0.94, and survival outcomes remain immature, precluding definitive conclusions regarding long-term mortality benefit. 1 Although improvements in EFS/FFS and DMFS are encouraging and clinically meaningful—particularly given the central role of distant relapse in LANPC—these intermediate endpoints do not reliably predict durable OS benefit. In head and neck oncology, the surrogate value of intermediate endpoints for OS has historically been variable and highly context-dependent. The MACH-NC collaborative analyses demonstrated that reductions in locoregional recurrence and distant metastasis mediated chemotherapy-associated survival gains; however, the magnitude of OS improvement varied according to treatment regimen, intensity, and patient selection. 4 Whether comparable quantitative surrogacy relationships will apply to immunotherapy-based intensification strategies in Epstein–Barr virus (EBV)-driven nasopharyngeal carcinoma (NPC) remains to be convincingly established through mature, long-term follow-up data.

Moreover, the availability of effective salvage therapies—including subsequent lines of immunotherapy—may attenuate observable differences in OS in early analyses, thereby diluting the measurable impact of initial treatment intensification. Experience from immune checkpoint inhibition in recurrent or metastatic NPC demonstrates that survival benefits often emerge only after prolonged follow-up, with delayed separation and durable tail effects, as illustrated in trials such as JUPITER-02. 5 Accordingly, while the present data convincingly demonstrate improvements in disease control endpoints, they do not yet constitute definitive evidence of survival modification. A judicious interpretation that emphasizes augmentation of systemic control—while explicitly acknowledging the need for mature OS data—would more precisely align recommendations with the current evidentiary threshold.

Finally, the generalizability of these findings warrants careful consideration within a global oncology context. All included trials were conducted in China and predominantly enrolled patients with non-keratinizing, EBV-associated NPC. 1 The tumor microenvironment of EBV-driven disease is characterized by dense lymphocytic infiltration and frequent PD-L1 expression, features that may enhance susceptibility to immune checkpoint blockade. While this biological milieu strengthens the internal validity of outcomes in endemic regions, it may limit direct extrapolation to non-endemic populations, where keratinizing histologies and EBV-negative disease are more prevalent. In addition, genetic, environmental, and treatment-pattern differences across regions may further influence therapeutic responsiveness. Prospective validation in geographically and histologically diverse populations will be essential to determine the universality and magnitude of the benefits observed in these trials.

In summary, Stawarz et al. provide compelling phase III–level evidence that PD-1 blockade enhances disease control when added to cisplatin-based CRT in LANPC. 1 The available data support an augmentation model in which immunotherapy potentiates, rather than replaces, platinum-based CRT. While improvements in EFS and DMFS are clinically meaningful, maturation of OS data and continued biomarker-informed evaluation will determine the extent to which these early gains translate into durable survival benefit. Clarifying these distinctions may help refine integration strategies and guide optimal sequencing in curative-intent NPC management.

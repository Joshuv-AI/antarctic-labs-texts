Operadic Fibrations and Unary Operadic 2-categories
Dominik Trnka

Abstract
We introduce unary operadic 2-categories as a framework for operadic Grothendieck construction of a categorical 𝕆 \mathbb{O} -operad, 𝕆 \mathbb{O} being a unary operadic category. The construction is a fully faithful functor ∫ 𝕆 \int_{\mathbb{O}} which takes categorical 𝕆 \mathbb{O} -operads to operadic functors over 𝕆 \mathbb{O} , and we characterize its essential image by certain lifting properties. Such operadic functors are called operadic fibrations. Our theory is an extension of the discrete (unary) operadic case and, in some sense, of the classical Grothendieck construction of a categorical presheaf. For the terminal unary operadic category ⊙ \odot , a categorical ⊙ \odot -operad is a strict monoidal category 𝒱 \mathscr{V} and its Grothendieck construction ∫ ⊙ 𝒱 \int_{\odot}\mathscr{V} is connected to the ‘para’ construction appearing in machine learning. The 2-categorical setting provides a characterization of 𝕆 \mathbb{O} -operads valued in 𝒱 \mathscr{V} as operadic functors 𝕆 → ∫ ⊙ 𝒱 \mathbb{O}\to\int_{\odot}\mathscr{V} . Last, we describe a left adjoint to ∫ ⊙ \int_{\odot} .

.

The nerve and décalage for 2-categories

We recall some standard 2-categorical constructions. By a 2-category we always mean a strict 2-category, i.e. a structure with objects (0-cells), morphisms (1-cells), and morphisms between morphisms (2-cells), with all possible compositions being strictly associative and unital. From now on
\mathrm{Cat}
denotes the 1-category of 2-categories. It is a monoidal category with the cartesian product. When needed, we regard
(\mathrm{Cat},\times,\texttt{1})
as an equivalent
strict

The 2-categorical nerve was explicitly introduced by J. Duskin in

from the previous section.

\thetheorem
.

For a 2-category

\mathscr{N}\mathscr{C}_{n}=\mathrm{Cat}_{\mathrm{NLax}}(\texttt{n},\mathscr{C}),

where

is the ordinal
\texttt{n}=[0<1<\cdots<n]
, viewed as a 2-category, and
\mathrm{Cat}_{\mathrm{NLax}}

The following definition was motivated by the characterization of unary operadic categories of

Definition
\thetheorem
.

A unary operadic 2-category is a pair
\mathbb{O}=(X,\mathscr{C})
of a 2-category
\mathscr{C}
and a simplicial set
X

X
is the nerve of
\mathscr{C}

\mathrm{dec}_{\top}X\cong\mathscr{NC}.

An operadic functor between two unary operadic 2-categories is a morphism of their underlying simplicial sets.
We denote the category of unary operadic 2-categories by
\mathrm{uOpCat}

We unpack the definition in terms of the underlying 2-category
\mathscr{C}

X_{0}
, and additional top face and degeneracy maps on
\mathscr{NC}

\varphi
and

Proposition
\thetheorem

of

\mathscr{C}

\mathscr{C}
,

\mathscr{C}

\mathscr{C}

Our next goal is to reformulate Definition
3
, so it resembles the original definition of unary operadic categories of
[

]
, see Definition

. We find analogues to fiber functors and local terminal objects.

Definition
\thetheorem
.

An object
v
in a 2-category
\mathscr{C}
is lali-terminal,
if for every object
x
of
\mathscr{C}
, the category
\mathscr{C}(x,v)
has a terminal object. We require that the terminal object of
\mathscr{C}(v,v)
is the identity on
v
.
An object
v
is local lali-terminal if it is lali-terminal in its component.

The prefix
lali
stands for ‘left adjoint left inverse’. This terminology appears in
[
Štěp24
]
, however the condition on the terminal object of
\mathscr{C}(v,v)
being the identity is not required there.

Lemma
\thetheorem
.

An object
v
in a 2-category
\mathscr{C}
is lali-terminal if and only if for every object
x\in\mathscr{C}
, there is a chosen map
x\xrightarrow{v_{x}}v
, for every map
x\xrightarrow{f}y
of
\mathscr{C}
, there is a chosen 2-cell
v_{y}\circ f\xRightarrow{v_{f}}v_{x}
, and for every lax triangle
g\circ f\xRightarrow{\alpha}h
, there is a 3-simplex
v_{\alpha}=(v_{g},v_{h},v_{f},\alpha)
in
\mathscr{C}
, such that
v_{v}=\mathbb{1}_{v}
and
v_{v_{x}}=\mathbb{1}_{v_{x}}
.

Proof.

Let
v
be lali-terminal in
\mathscr{C}
. For an object
x
, the map
v_{x}
is the terminal object of
\mathscr{C}(x,v)
and for
f\colon x\to y
, the 2-cell
v_{f}
is the terminal map
v_{y}\circ f\Rightarrow v_{x}
of
\mathscr{C}(x,v)
. For a 2-cell
g\circ f\xRightarrow{\alpha}h
, there are two 2-cells
v_{z}\circ g\circ f\Rightarrow v_{x}
, namely
v_{h}\circ(v_{x}\cdot\alpha)
and
v_{f}\circ(v_{g}\cdot f)
. Since
v_{x}
is terminal, the two composites equal, hence they form a 3-simplex
v_{\alpha}=(v_{g},v_{h},v_{f},\alpha)
. By definition,
v_{v}=\mathbb{1}_{v}
and it follows that
v_{v_{x}}=\mathbb{1}_{v_{x}}
.

Conversely, we will show that the choice of
v_{x},v_{f}
, and
v_{\alpha}
, with
v_{v}=\mathbb{1}_{v}
and
v_{v_{x}}=\mathbb{1}_{v_{x}}
, makes
v
lali-terminal. Let there be two 2-cells
\alpha,\beta\colon f\Rightarrow v_{x}
in
\mathscr{C}(x,v)
. We interpret them as lax triangles
f\circ\mathbb{1}_{x}\Rightarrow v_{x}
. We have their 3-simplices
v_{\alpha}
and
v_{\beta}
, which give

\alpha=v_{v_{x}}\circ(v_{v}\cdot\alpha)=v_{\mathbb{1}_{x}}\circ(v_{f}\cdot\mathbb{1}_{x})=v_{v_{x}}\circ(v_{v}\cdot\beta)=\beta.

∎

4
Operads of unary operadic 2-categories

Before we introduce
\mathbb{O}
-operads of an operadic 2-category
\mathbb{O}
, we define their 1-connected variant. An operad
\mathcal{P}
is 1-connected if the part
\mathcal{P}_{u_{c}}
over chosen local (lali-)terminal object
u_{c}
contains only the identity operation
e_{c}
. The reason is, that 1-connected
\mathbb{O}
-operads
arise from the simplicial viewpoint naturally. We then distill an explicit definition of an operad and weaken the
1-connected
condition.

Definition
\thetheorem
.

Let
\mathscr{V}
be a strict monoidal category and
\mathscr{V}_{\triangle}
the unary operadic 2-category of Example
3
.
A 1-connected
\mathbb{O}
-operad
\mathcal{P}
with values in
\mathscr{V}
is an operadic functor
\mathcal{P}\colon\mathbb{O}\to\mathscr{V}_{\triangle}
.

For simplicity let
\mathscr{V}=\mathrm{Cat}
, the category of small categories (considered as an equivalent strict monoidal category) and let
\mathbb{O}=(X,\mathscr{C})
. A categorical
\mathbb{O}
-operad
\mathcal{P}
is thus a collection of categories
\mathcal{P}_{x}
, indexed by objects
x\in\mathscr{C}
, such that
\mathcal{P}_{u_{c}}=\texttt{1}
, the terminal category, for every
c\in\pi_{0}(\mathscr{C})
. We denote the unique object of
\mathcal{P}_{u_{c}}
by
e_{c}
. The collection is further equipped with functors

\mathcal{P}_{y}\times\mathcal{P}_{\varphi(f)}\xrightarrow{\mathcal{P}_{f}}\mathcal{P}_{x},

for every map
f\colon x\to y
in
C

which satisfy the following associativity and unit laws. We use the notation
a\cdot_{f}b
for
\mathcal{P}_{f}(a,b)
.

–

For any lax triangle
\alpha
in
C
,

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces x}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces y}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces z}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces h}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\alpha}

and objects
a\in\mathcal{P}_{z},b\in\mathcal{P}_{\varphi(g)},c\in\mathcal{P}_{\varphi(f)},

(a\cdot_{g}b)\cdot_{f}c=a\cdot_{h}(b\cdot_{\varphi(\alpha)}c).

It follows from the axioms of an operadic 2-category that
\varphi(f)=\varphi(\varphi(\alpha))
, hence the expression above is well defined.

–

For any object
x\in C
and
a\in\mathcal{P}_{x}
,

a\cdot_{\mathbb{1}_{x}}e_{\varphi(x)}=a,

e_{\pi(x)}\cdot_{u_{x}}a=a.

From the simplicial viewpoint, the operad multiplication has a more pleasing form:

\mathcal{P}_{d_{0}f}\times\mathcal{P}_{d_{2}f}\xrightarrow{\mathcal{P}_{f}}\mathcal{P}_{d_{1}f},

and the laws can be written as

\mathcal{P}_{d_{1}\alpha}(\mathcal{P}_{d_{3}\alpha}\times\mathbb{1})=\mathcal{P}_{d_{2}\alpha}(\mathbb{1}\times\mathcal{P}_{d_{0}\alpha})

\mathcal{P}_{s_{0}x}(-,e_{d_{1}x})=\mathbb{1}_{\mathcal{P}_{x}}=\mathcal{P}_{s_{1}x}(e_{d_{0}x},-).

The higher information of Definition
4
is redundant. This motivates the following definition where we weaken the 1-connected condition.

Definition
\thetheorem
.

A categorical
\mathbb{O}
-operad
\mathcal{P}
is a structure described under Definition
4
with the condition
\mathcal{P}_{u_{c}}=\{e_{c}\}
replaced by a choice of maps
1\xrightarrow{e_{c}}\mathcal{P}_{u_{c}}
for every
c\in\pi_{0}(\mathscr{C})
, which pick the units.

Example
\thetheorem
.

For unary operadic 1-categories we recover the original definition of a unital
\mathbb{O}
-operad of
[
BM24
]
.

Example
\thetheorem
.

For a 2-category
\mathscr{C}
denote
\mathbb{C}=(\mathscr{NC},\mathscr{D}\mathscr{C})
. The
\mathbb{C}
-operads in
\mathscr{V}
are lax 2-functors from
\mathscr{C}
to
\mathscr{B}\mathscr{V}
.
In particular, for a strict monoidal category
\mathscr{M}
, the
\mathscr{M}_{\triangle}
-operads
in
\mathscr{V}
are lax monoidal functors
\mathscr{M}\to\mathscr{V}
.

5
Operadic Grothendieck construction

For a unary operadic 2-category
\mathbb{O}
and a 1-connected categorical
\mathbb{O}
-operad
\mathcal{P}
, we introduce its operadic Grothendieck construction, i.e. a unary operadic 2-category
\int_{\mathbb{O}}\mathcal{P}
with a projection
\pi_{\mathcal{P}}\colon\int_{\mathbb{O}}\mathcal{P}\to\mathbb{O}
. We make the construction explicit. For non-1-connected operads the construction is obtained simply by weakening the 1-connected condition.

Observe that for any
\mathscr{C}\in\mathrm{Cat}
, the lax coslice category
\mathscr{C}/\mathrm{Cat}
is again monoidal. We keep the convention that lax means ‘from many to one’. The morphisms
F\to G
of
\mathscr{C}/\mathrm{Cat}
are pairs
(H,\alpha)
:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathscr{C}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathscr{D}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathscr{E},}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces G}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\alpha}

with
H\circ F\xRightarrow{\alpha}G
.
The codomain projection
\pi\colon\mathscr{C}/\mathrm{Cat}\to\mathrm{Cat}
is a monoidal functor which induces an operadic functor
\pi_{\triangle}\colon(\mathscr{C}/\mathrm{Cat})_{\triangle}\to\mathrm{Cat}_{\triangle}
(for the notation cf. Example
3
).

Proposition
\thetheorem
.

The category
\mathrm{uOpCat}
has pullbacks.

Proof.

Let

and

be two operadic functors, with

\mathbb{O}=(X_{\mathbb{O}},\mathscr{C}_{\mathbb{O}})
,
\mathbb{P}=(X_{\mathbb{P}},\mathscr{C}_{\mathbb{P}})
, and
\mathbb{Q}=(X_{\mathbb{Q}},\mathscr{C}_{\mathbb{Q}})

It follows from the properties of the nerve and décalage that the pullback of

and

\mathbb{O}\times_{\mathbb{P}}\mathbb{Q}=(X_{\mathbb{O}}\times_{X_{\mathbb{P}}}X_{\mathbb{Q}},\mathscr{C}_{\mathbb{O}}\times_{\mathscr{C}_{\mathbb{P}}}\mathscr{C}_{\mathbb{Q}}).\qed

6
Operadic fibrations

Operadic fibrations (with splitting) are operadic functors lying in the essential image of the functor
\int
of the preceding section. We characterize them by certain lifting properties. A prototypical operadic fibration is thus the projection
\pi_{\mathcal{P}}\colon\int_{\mathbb{O}}\mathcal{P}\to\mathbb{O}
from the previous section.

Definition
\thetheorem
.

Let
\mathbb{O}=(X,\mathscr{C})
be a unary operadic 2-category. The objects
u_{c}\in\mathscr{U}
, i.e. objects lying in the image of
u\colon\pi_{0}\mathscr{C}\to\mathscr{C}_{0}
, are called trivial.
A morphism
g\colon y\to x
of a unary operadic 2-category is a quasibijection if for any map
f\colon z\to y
,

\varphi(g\circ f\xRightarrow{\mathbb{1}_{g\circ f}}(g\circ f))=u_{\varphi(f)}.

It follows that
g
has a trivial fiber
u_{\varphi(x)}=u_{\varphi(y)}
, which recovers 1-categorical quasibijections of
[
BM23b
, Section 1.1]
.

Proposition
\thetheorem
.

Let
\mathbb{O}=(X,\mathscr{C})
be a unary operadic 2-category and
\mathcal{P}
a categorical
\mathbb{O}
-operad.
A morphism
G=(g,q,\beta)\colon(B,b)\to(C,c)
of
\int_{\mathbb{O}}\mathcal{P}
is a quasibijection if and only if
g
is a quasibijection in
\mathbb{O}
and
q=e_{\varphi(B)}
, the unit of the operad
\mathcal{P}
.

Proof.

Let
(f,p,\alpha)\colon(A,a)\to(B,b)
be a morphism of
\int_{\mathbb{O}}\mathcal{P}
and

a quasibijection in
\mathbb{O}
. We check that

\varphi\big(\mathbb{1}_{(g,e_{\varphi(B)},\beta)\circ(f,p,\alpha)}\big)=\varphi(\mathbb{1}_{g\circ f},\mathbb{1}_{p})=\big(\varphi(\mathbb{1}_{g\circ f}),p,\mathbb{1}_{p}\big)=(u_{\varphi(f)},p,\mathbb{1}_{p})=u_{\varphi(f,p)}.

Conversely, let

be a quasibijection. We get the equation

(\varphi(\mathbb{1}_{(g\circ f)}),q\cdot p,\mathbb{1}_{q\cdot p})=\varphi(\mathbb{1}_{g\circ f},\mathbb{1}_{q\cdot p})=\varphi(\mathbb{1}_{(g,q,\beta)\circ(f,p,\alpha)})=u_{\varphi(f,p)}=(u_{\varphi(f)},p,\mathbb{1}_{p})

∎

8
Closing remarks and further directions

There is a straightforward generalization of our theory to unary operadic bicategories and categorical pseudo-operads (operads with relaxed associativity and unitality conditions). We expect that these correspond to non-splitting fibrations. Working in this generalized framework, one could work directly with non-strict monoidal categories. We also hope to resolve the 1-connectedness restriction. The proofs might become more complicated however.

A direct continuation of the work is then to establish the 2-categorical (and also bicategorical) framework for operadic categories of general arities. This is a work in progress. Unfortunately, there is no such clear characterization using simplicial sets as in the unary case, which complicates the situation. We believe that a good path is to follow Proposition
3
. The proposed definition is:

Definition 8.1
.

An operadic bicategory is a bicategory
\mathbb{O}
equipped with

–

a cardinality 2-functor
|-|\colon\mathbb{O}\to\mathscr{S}
(necessarily strict) to the skeletal category of finite sets,

–

lax functors
\varphi_{x}\colon\mathbb{O}/x\to\mathbb{O}^{|x|}
, for every object
x
of
\mathbb{O}
, and

–

chosen local lali-terminal objects
u_{c}
, in each connected component
c
of
\mathbb{O}
whose cardinality is not constantly zero,

–

such that analogues of the axioms of operadic 1-categories hold.

When the cardinality functor is constantly zero, the theory should recover ordinary categories. In this case the operads are presheaves and operadic fibrations are categorical fibrations.

Let
F\colon\mathbb{O}\to\mathbb{P}
be an operadic functor. It induces a restriction functor on the categories of operads

F^{*}\colon{\mathbb{P}}\textrm{-oper}(\mathrm{Cat})\to{\mathbb{O}}\textrm{-oper}(\mathrm{Cat}).

A non-trivial problem is to find a formula for its left adjoint. Evaluating this on the terminal
\mathbb{P}
-operad we possibly get a left adjoint to the operadic Grothendieck construction for general
\mathbb{O}
.

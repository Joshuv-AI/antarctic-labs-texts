Generic continuity of the perturbed minima of certain parametric optimization problems
Hristina Topalova, Nadia Zlateva

Abstract
We show that in a quite general framework, the parameterized optimization problem can be so perturbed as to be generically well-posed. As an application, we provide a contribution to Stečkin theory. Key words: perturbed minimization, convergence of minima, variational principle, perturbation space. 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification: 46N10, 49J45, 90C26.

Introduction

The article
[
18
]
by the same authors provides a new approach towards the classical, e.g.
[
4
]
, exploration of the behaviour of the sets of minima of a parameterized minimization problem. This new approach is based on perturbation in the style of
[
5
,
6
]
. The work
[
18
]
, focused on a sequence of minimization problems, hints that the method can perhaps be generalized; and the purpose of the present work is exactly this.

While it is not possible to have the desired result everywhere, see Example
1.4
below, in a generic sense it still holds.

We will now present our results in full details, and to this end we need quite a few technical definitions.

Our space framework is the metric spaces. Metric spaces are convenient, because – unlike in normed spaces – the subsets of metric spaces still fit the framework. If
(X,d)
is a metric space then the closed ball with center
x\in X
and radius
\varepsilon>0
is denoted by
B_{\varepsilon}(x):=\{y\in X:\ d(y,x)\leq\varepsilon\}
. For subsets
A,B\subset X
we denote
d(A,B):=\inf\{d(x,y):\ x\in A,\ y\in B\}
. With a slight abuse of the notation we denote
d(a,B):=d(\{a\},B)
. For a nonempty bounded
A\subset X
,

\diam(A):=\sup\{d(x,y):\ x,y\in A\}.

Our optimization framework is:

\mathrm{Minimise}\ f_{p}(x)\ \mathrm{on}\ x\in X\ \mathrm{for}\ \mathrm{fixed}\ p\in P,

where
(X,d)
and
(P,\mu)
are complete metric spaces,
p
is regarded as parameter, and for each
p\in P
the function
f_{p}:X\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
is proper, lower semicontinuous and bounded below.

For a proper and bounded below function
f:X\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
we denote the
\varepsilon
-argmin set by

\Omega_{f}(\varepsilon):=\{x\in X:\ f(x)\leq\inf f+\varepsilon\}.

The general problem under which the current work falls, is to investigate the continuity properties of the multivalued map

p\to\Omega_{f_{p}}(\varepsilon).

Of course, to expect any continuity there ought to be a relationship between
f_{p}
’s for different
p
’s. What works in our case is what we will be calling
uniform epi-continuity
. The term stems from the fact that for sequences it is an uniformized version of the epi-convergence, e.g.
[
1
, p.169]
, or
[
14
, Chapter 7]
. So, if
(X,d)
and
(P,\mu)
are complete metric spaces, the family of the functions
(f_{p})_{p\in P}
is called uniformly epi-continuous on
p\in P
, provided it satisfies the conditions (
1.1
) and (
1.2
) below:

\displaystyle\forall p\in P,\ \forall x\in X,\ \forall\varepsilon>0\ \exists\delta>0\mbox{ s.t.}

\displaystyle\forall q\in B_{\delta}(p)\ \exists x_{q}\in B_{\varepsilon}(x)\mbox{ s.t.}

(1.1)

\displaystyle f_{q}(x_{q})\leq f_{p}(x)+\varepsilon,

\displaystyle\forall p\in P,\ \forall\varepsilon>0\ \exists\delta>0\mbox{ s.t.}

\displaystyle f_{q}(x)\geq(f_{p})_{\varepsilon}(x)-\varepsilon,\quad\forall x\in X,\ \forall q\in B_{\delta}(p).

(1.2)

Here, the regularization
(v)_{\varepsilon}
, where
\varepsilon>0
, of a bounded below function
v:X\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
is

(v)_{\varepsilon}(x):=\inf\{v(y):y\in B_{\varepsilon}(x)\}.

(1.3)

Next, we axiomatically define feasible sets of functions that we will use for perturbation. We are not aiming for the most general possible axioms. Rather, we strive for a balance between generality and practicality. For a function
g:P\times X\to\mathbb{R}
we write for consistency for a fixed
p\in P

g_{p}(x):=g(p,x),\quad\forall x\in X,

and then we can talk about a family of functions
(g_{p})_{p\in P}
.

Denote by
BUC(X)
the space of all bounded and uniformly continuous real valued functions on
X
. Equipped with the norm

\|v\|_{\infty}:=\sup_{x\in X}|v(x)|,\quad\forall v\in BUC(X),

(BUC(X),\|\cdot\|_{\infty})
is a Banach space.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
(X,d)
and
(P,\mu)
be complete metric spaces. Let
(f_{p})_{p\in P}
be a family of proper, lower semicontinuous and bounded below functions from
X
to
\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
. Let
f:P\times X\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
be defined as
f(p,x):=f_{p}(x)
.

A complete metric space

({\cal G},\rho)
of functions
g:P\times X\to\mathbb{R}
is called a
perturbation space
on
P\times X
for
f
if the metric
\rho
is translation invariant, that is
\rho(g_{1}+h,g_{2}+h)=\rho(g_{1},g_{2})
, and
\cal G
is a convex cone with respect to the standard functional addition and scalar multiplication, and, moreover:

(i)

g_{p}\in BUC(X)
for all
p\in P
;

(ii)

the mapping

p\to g_{p}

from
P
to
BUC(X)
, is continuous;

(iii)

\forall p\in P
there exists a constant
c(p)>0
such that

\|g_{p}\|_{\infty}\leq c(p)\rho(g,0),\quad\forall g\in{\cal G};

(iv)

\forall p\in P
,
\forall\varepsilon>0
and
\forall g\in{\cal G}
, there exist
\delta>0
and
g^{\prime}\in{\cal G}
such that

\rho(g,g^{\prime})<\varepsilon,\text{ and }\diam(\Omega_{f_{p}+g^{\prime}_{p}}(\delta))<\varepsilon.

Now we can state our main result.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
(P,\mu)
and
(X,d)
be complete metric spaces. Let also
P
be separable.

Let
(f_{p})_{p\in P}
be a family of proper, lower semicontinuous and bounded below functions from
X
to
\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
which are uniformly epi-continuous, that is, they satisfy conditions (
1.1
) and (
1.2
).

Let
({\cal G},\rho)
be a perturbation space on
P\times X
for
f:P\times X\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
which is defined as
f(p,x):=f_{p}(x)
.

Let
W
be a countable dense subset of
P
.

Then there exists a dense
G_{\delta}
subset
G
of
\cal G
such that for each
g\in G
there exists a dense
G_{\delta}
subset
P_{g}
of
P
such that
W\subset P_{g}
and the following holds:

For any fixed
p\in P_{g}
the function
f_{p}+g_{p}
attains its strong minimum at
x_{p,g}\in X
and, moreover,

\forall p\in P_{g},\ \forall\varepsilon>0,\ \exists\delta>0\ \colon\ \Omega_{f_{q}+g_{q}}(\delta)\subset B_{\varepsilon}(x_{p,g}),\quad\forall q\in B_{\delta}(p).

(1.4)

Also, the functions

p\to f_{p}(x_{p,g})\mbox{ and }p\to g_{p}(x_{p,g})

are continuous on
P_{g}
.

Recall that
\bar{x}
is the strong minimum of
f:X\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
if each minimizing sequence
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
, that is, such that
f(x_{n})\to\inf_{X}f
, satisfies
x_{n}\to\bar{x}
. It is clear from Cantor Lemma that if
X
is a complete metric space and
f:X\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
is proper and lower semicontinuous function, then

f\mbox{ attains strong minimum}\iff\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\Omega_{f}(\varepsilon)=0.

(1.5)

Using
BUC(X)
as a perturbation space, we get the following.

Corollary 1.3
.

Let
(P,\mu)
and
(X,d)
be complete metric spaces. Let also
P
be separable.

Let
(f_{p})_{p\in P}
be a family of proper, lower semicontinuous and bounded below functions from
X
to
\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
which are uniformly epi-continuous, that is, they satisfy (
1.1
) and (
1.2
).

Let
W
be a countable dense subset of
P
.

Then for each
\varepsilon>0
there are a dense
G_{\delta}
subset
V
of
P
such that
W\subset V
, and a continuous mapping

\varphi:V\to X

such that

\varphi(p)\in\Omega_{f_{p}}(\varepsilon),\quad\forall p\in V.

The next simple example shows that even in the case when
P
an interval, one cannot expect that the conclusion of Corollary
1.3
– and, therefore, that of Theorem
1.2
– would always hold for all
p\in P
. Of course, that could be the case under additional assumption on
f_{p}
’s like convexity, see e.g.
[
7
,
10
,
11
,
19
]
, but we do not cover these results here.

Example 1.4
.

Let
P=X=[0,1]
and let
f_{p}(x):=(1-p)(3x-1)
for
x\in[0,1/3]
,
f_{p}(x):=0
for
x\in[1/3,2/3]
, and
f_{p}(x):=p(2-3x)
for
x\in[2/3,1]
. Then for each
\varepsilon\in(0,1/2)
we have that
\Omega_{f_{0}}(\varepsilon)\subset[0,1/3]
,
\Omega_{f_{1}}(\varepsilon)\subset[2/3,1]
, and
\Omega_{f_{p}}(\varepsilon)\cap(1/3,2/3)=\varnothing
for all
p\in[0,1]
. Therefore, there cannot be a continuous selection of the map
p\to\Omega_{f_{p}}(\varepsilon)
.

Of course, if
P
is countable, then we can take
W=P
and get the conclusion of Theorem
1.2
on the whole
P
. For example, let
P=\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}
with
\mu(i,j):=|1/i-1/j|
and
\mu(i,\infty):=1/i
. In this case continuity of the strong minima means convergence of the sequence
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
to
x_{\infty}
and we fully recover Theorem 1.2 of
[
18
]
as a corollary to Theorem
1.2
.

As a non-trivial application of our result we elaborate on Stečkin theory, e.g.
[
15
,
8
]
(see also
[
3
]
and
[
16
, pp.1223]
for a modern and comprehensive review).

Let
(E,\|\cdot\|)
be a Banach space. Denote by
B_{E}
and
S_{E}
the unit ball and the unit sphere of
E
, respectively. Let
M
be a nonempty closed, convex and bounded subset of
E
. Obviously, with the canonical metric
\mu(x,y):=\|x-y\|
both
(E,\mu)
and
(M,\mu)
are complete metric spaces.

Consider the set
{\cal N}
of all bounded seminorms on
E
, i.e. the functions
\nu:E\to\mathbb{R}
such that

(j)

\nu(tx)=|t|\nu(x)
,
\forall x\in E
;

(jj)

\nu(x+y)\leq\nu(x)+\nu(y)
,
\forall x,y\in E
;

(jjj)

k_{\nu}=\sup_{x\in B_{E}}|\nu(x)|
is finite, so
\nu(x)\leq k_{\nu}\|x\|
,
\forall x\in E
.

Equipped with the metric

\rho(\nu_{1},\nu_{2}):=\sup_{x\in B_{E}}|\nu_{1}(x)-\nu_{2}(x)|,

(1.6)

({\cal N},\rho)
is a complete metric space. The set
{\cal N}_{0}
of all equivalent norms on
E
is an open dense set in
{\cal N}
, see Lemma
5.1
.

Proposition 1.5
.

Let
E
be a Banach space. The cone
({\cal G},\rho)
of the functions

{\cal G}:=\{g:E\times E\to\mathbb{R}\mbox{ s.t. }g(p,x)=\nu(p-x),\ \nu\in{\cal N}\}

equipped with the metric

\rho(g_{1},g_{2}):=\rho(\nu_{1},\nu_{2}),\text{ where }g_{i}(p,x)=\nu_{i}(p-x),\ i=1,2,

is a perturbation space on
P\times M
for the function
f(p,x)\equiv 0
for all
p\in E
and
x\in M
.

Note that here the functions
(f_{p})_{p\in E}
do not actually depend on
p
(and neither on
x
for that matter). However, the functions in the perturbation space do depend on
p
. It is because of this example and the next result that we added the additional complexity (in
[
18
]
the perturbation functions do not depend on the parameter
p
).

A subtlety that arises here is that different seminorms may have identical restrictions on the set
M
, but this does not cause a problem. First of all, Definition
1.1
allows for
g_{1},g_{2}\in\cal G
such that
\rho(g_{1},g_{2})>0
, but
g_{1}(p,x)=g_{2}(p,x)
for all
p\in P
and
x\in X
. Second, in our concrete example, if
\nu_{1}(p-x)=\nu_{2}(p-x)
for all
p\in E
and
x\in M
, then obviously
\nu_{1}\equiv\nu_{2}
.

The Stečkin-like result we get is as follows.

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
(E,\|\cdot\|)
be a separable Banach space, and let
M\subset E
be a nonempty closed convex and bounded set in
E
. For any dense countable set
W\subset E
and
any equivalent norm
|\cdot|
on
E
, there exist an arbitrary close to
|\cdot|

equivalent norm
|\hskip-1.0pt|\hskip-1.0pt|\cdot|\hskip-1.0pt|\hskip-1.0pt|
and a dense
G_{\delta}
set
V\supset W
such that the problem

\min_{x\in M}|\hskip-1.0pt|\hskip-1.0pt|p-x|\hskip-1.0pt|\hskip-1.0pt|

(1.7)

is well-posed for any
p\in V
. In particular, the metric projection on
M
with respect to the norm
|\hskip-1.0pt|\hskip-1.0pt|\cdot|\hskip-1.0pt|\hskip-1.0pt|
is single valued and continuous on
V
.

Recall that for
Z\subset E
and
f:Z\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
the minimization problem
\min_{Z}f
is
well-posed
if the restriction of
f
to
Z
attaining strong minimum in
Z
. Note that this result is original. In the classical Stečkin theory the norm is fixed, but it has some nice properties. Obviously, if the norm is uniformly convex, then the metric projection on the closed convex set
M
will be well-posed everywhere. But not every separable Banach space admits an uniformly convex renorm.
Locally uniformly convex
renorms are available, but in general they do not guarantee well-posedness of the minimization problem (
1.7
) anywhere, see
[
3
, p. 276]
. In
[
9
, Theorem 4.11]
it is proved that for any closed set and any equivalent norm there is an equivalent norm close to the latter, such that the metric projection is generically well-posed. The new feature here is that we can fix in advance a dense countable set
W
and then get the renorm in such a way that the metric projection will be well-posed and, therefore, continuous on
W
(in fact it will be continuous on a dense
G_{\delta}
set containing
W
).

The work is organized as follows. In Section
2
we adapt the perturbation method from
[
18
]
to our slightly more general Definition
1.1
. In Section
3
we show that the uniform epi-continuity implies that the set of parameters where strong minimum is attained, is
G_{\delta}
. These two combined easily yield the proof of our main Theorem
1.2
in Section
4
. The final Section
5
is devoted to our contribution to Stečkin theory.

2
Perturbation method

In
[
5
,
6
]
the authors established not only a variant of Borwein-Priess Smooth Variational Principle
[
2
]
, but also gave a very general scheme for creating variational principles or, what some prefer to say, perturbation methods. This scheme has been followed e.g. in
[
7
,
12
,
13
,
17
]
and we will follow it here as well.

In this section, we demonstrate the application of the perturbation method for a fixed parameter
p\in P
. Specifically, we present the following result, which is a minor adaptation of
[
18
, Theorem 3.1]
. Due to our slightly more general definition of a perturbation space, we provide a complete proof here.

Theorem 2.1

(
Variational Principle
)
.

Let
(P,\mu)
and
(X,d)
be complete metric spaces.

Let the functions
f_{p}:X\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
be proper, lower semicontinuous and bounded below for all
p\in P
. Let
({\cal G},\rho)
be a perturbation space for the function
f:P\times X\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
defined as
f(p,x):=f_{p}(x)
.

Let
p\in P
be fixed. Then there exists a dense
G_{\delta}
subset
U
of
{\cal G}
such that for each
g\in U
the function
f_{p}+g_{p}
attains strong minimum on
X
.

For the proof we need the following useful Lemma, cf.
[
12
, Lemma 4]
, which reveals certain continuity-like property of the
\varepsilon
-argmin map.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
(X,d)
be a metric space and let
f:X\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
be proper and bounded below function. Let
\varepsilon>0
.
If the bounded function
g:X\to\mathbb{R}
is such that

\|g\|_{\infty}<\varepsilon/3,

then

\Omega_{f+g}(\varepsilon/3)\subset\Omega_{f}(\varepsilon).

Proof.

Let
\delta:=\varepsilon/3
, so
\|g\|_{\infty}<\delta
. Let
x\in\Omega_{f+g}(\delta)
.

It is clear that

\inf(f+g)<\inf f+\delta,

so,
f(x)<f(x)+g(x)+\delta\leq\inf(f+g)+2\delta<\inf f+3\delta
. That is,
x\in\Omega_{f}(\varepsilon)
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
2.1
.

Fix
p\in P
and consider for
n\in\mathbb{N}
the subset
M_{n}
of
{\cal G}
defined by

M_{n}:=\left\{g\in{\cal G}:\exists\ t>0:\ \diam\left(\Omega_{f_{p}+g_{p}}(t)\right)<\frac{1}{n}\right\}.

(2.1)

We will show that
M_{n}
is dense and open in
({\cal G},\rho)
. Then by Baire Category Theorem

U:=\bigcap_{n\in\mathbb{N}}M_{n}

will be dense in
{\cal G}
(it is
G_{\delta}
by definition). For each
g\in U
the definition of
M_{n}
shows that
\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\diam(\Omega_{f_{p}+g_{p}}(\varepsilon))=0
, hence
f_{p}+g_{p}
attains strong minimum, see (
1.5
).

Fix arbitrary
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

To show that
M_{n}
is indeed dense, take arbitrary
h\in{\cal G}
and
\varepsilon\in(0,1/n)
.

Let
\delta>0
and
g^{\prime}\in{\cal G}
be provided by (iv) of Definition
1.1
for
h
. So,
\rho(h,g^{\prime})<\varepsilon
and
\diam(\Omega_{f_{p}+g_{p}^{\prime}}(\delta))<\varepsilon<\dfrac{1}{n}
.
This means that
g^{\prime}\in M_{n}
. Since
\rho(h,g^{\prime})<\varepsilon
, the distance from
h
to
M_{n}
is smaller than
\varepsilon
. In other words,
M_{n}
is dense in
{\cal G}
.

To show that
M_{n}
is open, take an arbitrary
g\in M_{n}
. This means by (
2.1
) that there is
\varepsilon>0
such that

\diam\left(\Omega_{f_{p}+g_{p}}(\varepsilon)\right)<\frac{1}{n}.

Let
\delta>0
be such that

\delta<\frac{\varepsilon}{3c(p)},

where
c(p)
is the constant from Definition
1.1
(iii). Let
g^{\prime}\in B_{\delta}(g)
be arbitrary. Consider

h:=g_{p}^{\prime}-g_{p}.

By Definition
1.1
(iii) it follows that
\|h\|_{\infty}\leq c(p)\rho(g_{p}^{\prime},g_{p})\leq c(p)\delta<\varepsilon/3
. Thus Lemma
2.2
gives
\Omega_{f_{p}+g_{p}+h}(\varepsilon/3)\subset\Omega_{f_{p}+g_{p}}(\varepsilon)
. But
f_{p}+g_{p}+h=f_{p}+g_{p}^{\prime}
, so

\diam\left(\Omega_{f_{p}+g_{p}^{\prime}}(\varepsilon/3)\right)\leq\diam\left(\Omega_{f_{p}+g_{p}}(\varepsilon)\right)<\frac{1}{n}\Rightarrow g^{\prime}\in M_{n}.

∎

3
Epi-continuity

From Theorem
2.1
it is clear how to get a countable set of parameters such that strong minimum to be attained. Here we will show that the uniform epi-continuity guarantees that the set to be
G_{\delta}
.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
(P,\mu)
and
(X,d)
be complete metric spaces. Let the functions
h_{p}:X\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
be proper, lower semicontinuous and bounded below for all
p\in P
. Let also
(h_{p})_{p\in P}
be uniformly epi-continuous, that is, they satisfy (
1.1
) and (
1.2
).

Let
Q\subset P
be the (possibly empty) set of these
p\in P
for which
h_{p}
attains strong minimum, say
x_{p}\in X
.

Then
Q
is a
G_{\delta}
set and, moreover,

\forall p\in Q,\ \forall\varepsilon>0,\ \exists\delta>0\colon\ \Omega_{h_{q}}(\delta)\subset B_{\varepsilon}(x_{p}),\quad\forall q\in B_{\delta}(p).

(3.1)

We start with the following result somehow similar to Lemma
2.2
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
(P,\mu)
and
(X,d)
be complete metric spaces. Let the functions
h_{p}:X\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
be proper, lower semicontinuous and bounded below for all
p\in P
. Let also
(h_{p})_{p\in P}
be uniformly epi-continuous, that is, they satisfy the conditions (
1.1
) and (
1.2
).

If for some
p\in P
,
\varepsilon>0
and
r>0
,

\diam\Omega_{h_{p}}(\varepsilon)<r,

then there exists
\delta>0
such that

\diam\Omega_{h_{q}}(\delta)<5r,\quad\forall q\in B_{\delta}(p).

Proof.

Fix
p\in P
,
\varepsilon>0
and
r>0
such that
\diam\Omega_{h_{p}}(\varepsilon)<r
, and let
\mu>0
be such that

\mu\leq\min\{r,\varepsilon/4\}.

Let us fix
x\in\Omega_{h_{p}}(\mu)
. Let
\delta\in(0,\mu)
be such that (
1.1
) and (
1.2
) hold for the fixed
p
,
x
, and
\mu
. We claim that

\Omega_{h_{q}}(\delta)\subset B_{2r}(x),\quad\forall q\in B_{\delta}(p)

(3.2)

which is enough to get the conclusion.

Indeed, take arbitrary
y\in X
such that
d(y,x)>2r
.

Since
\mu<\varepsilon
,
x\in\Omega_{h_{p}}(\varepsilon)
and the diameter of the latter set is smaller than
r
by assumption, we have that
B_{r}(y)\cap\Omega_{h_{p}}(\varepsilon)=\varnothing
and because of
\mu\leq r
,

(h_{p})_{\mu}(y)\geq(h_{p})_{r}(y)>\inf h_{p}+\varepsilon\geq h_{p}(x)-\mu+\varepsilon\geq h_{p}(x)+3\mu.

From (
1.2
) it follows that

h_{q}(y)\geq(h_{p})_{\mu}(y)-\mu>h_{p}(x)+3\mu-\mu=h_{p}(x)+2\mu,\quad\forall q\in B_{\delta}(p).

From (
1.1
) on the other hand, we get for each
q\in B_{\delta}(p)
a
x_{q}\in X
such that
h_{q}(x_{q})\leq h_{p}(x)+\mu
. Combining with the above inequality we get that for all
q\in B_{\delta}(p)
,

h_{q}(y)-\inf h_{q}\geq h_{q}(y)-h_{q}(x_{q})\geq h_{q}(y)-h_{p}(x)-\mu>2\mu-\mu=\mu.

Therefore,
y\not\in\Omega_{h_{q}}(\mu)
and since
\delta<\mu
,
y\not\in\Omega_{h_{q}}(\delta)
. Thus (
3.2
) is verified, because
y\not\in B_{2r}(x)
was arbitrary.
∎

Proof of Proposition
3.1
.

Let

4
Proof of the main result

To prove our main Theorem
1.2
, we will need the following simple proposition. It says in essence that the uniform epi-continuity implies continuity of the value function. This is one of the reasons why the concept of uniform epi-convergence worths consideration.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
(X,d)
and
(P,\mu)
be complete metric spaces. Let the family of proper and bounded below functions
(f_{p})_{p\in P}
be uniformly epi-continuous on
p\in P
. Then the value function

V(p):=\inf_{x\in X}f_{p}(x),\quad\forall p\in P,

is continuous.

Proof.

Fix
p\in P
and
\varepsilon>0
.

Let
x_{p}\in X
be such that
f_{p}(x_{p})<V(p)+\varepsilon/2
. From (
1.1
) it follows that there is
\delta_{1}>0
such that for all
q\in P
such that
0<\mu(p,q)<\delta_{1}
there is
x_{q}\in X
such that
f_{q}(x_{q})\leq f_{p}(x_{p})+\varepsilon/2
. This means that

V(q)\leq f_{q}(x_{q})\leq f_{p}(x_{p})+\varepsilon/2\leq V(p)+\varepsilon,\quad\forall q\in B_{\delta_{1}}(p).

On the other hand, from (
1.2
) there is
\delta_{2}>0
such that for all
q\in B_{\delta_{2}}(p)
and all
x\in X
we have
f_{q}(x)\geq(f_{p})_{\varepsilon}-\varepsilon
. Thus,

V(q)\geq\inf(f_{p})_{\varepsilon}-\varepsilon\geq V(p)-\varepsilon,\quad\forall q\in B_{\delta_{2}}(p).

So,
p\to V(p)
is continuous.
∎

The fundamental equivalence between Heine and Cauchy definitions (of convergence, continuity, etc.) holds for uniform epi-continuity as well. More precisely, if
(X,d)
and
(P,\mu)
are complete metric spaces, then the family of functions
(f_{p})_{p\in P}
is uniformly epi-continuous on
p\in P
, that is, it satisfies the conditions (
1.1
) and (
1.2
), if and only if, for each
p\in P
and each sequence
(p_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
convergent to
p
(that is,
\mu(p,p_{n})\to 0
, as
n\to\infty
) the sequence of functions
\phi_{n}:=f_{p_{n}}
converges to
\phi_{\infty}:=f_{p}
in the sense of
[
18
]
, that is,
(\phi_{n})_{n\geq 1}
satisfy the conditions (3) and (4) of
[
18
]
. In this way we can just reformulate
[
18
, Proposition 2.2]
. However, for reader’s convenience we will present a proof. In essence, the next proposition establishes a condition under which the uniform epi-continuity is preserved under addition.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
(X,d)
and
(P,\mu)
be complete metric spaces. Let the family of proper and bounded below functions
(f_{p})_{p\in P}
be uniformly epi-continuous on
p\in P
.

Let the family
(g_{p})_{p\in P}\subset BUC(X)
be such that
p\to g_{p}
is continuous from
P
to
BUC(X)
(in other words,
(g_{p})_{p\in P}
satisfy (i) and (ii) of Definition
1.1
).

Then the family
(f_{p}+g_{p})_{p\in P}
is uniformly epi-continuous on
p\in P
.

Proof.

From the conditions on
(g_{p})_{p\in P}
it easily follows that the function
(p,x)\to g_{p}(x)
is continuous on both variables with uniformity on
x
, that is,

\begin{array}[]{l}\forall p\in P,\ \forall\varepsilon>0,\exists\delta>0\colon\\[8.0pt]
|g_{q}(y)-g_{p}(x)|<\varepsilon,\quad\forall q\in B_{\delta}(p),\ \forall x\in X,\ \forall y\in B_{\delta}(x).\end{array}

(4.1)

Indeed, fix
p\in P
and
\varepsilon>0
. Let
\delta>0
be such that
\|g_{q}-g_{p}\|_{\infty}<\varepsilon/2
for all
q\in B_{\delta}(p)
, and also
|g_{p}(y)-g_{p}(x)|<\varepsilon/2
for all
x\in X
and
y\in B_{\delta}(x)
. Then for all
x\in X
and
(q,y)\in B_{\delta}(p)\times B_{\delta}(x)
, we have
|g_{q}(y)-g_{p}(x)|\leq|g_{q}(y)-g_{p}(y)|+|g_{p}(y)-g_{p}(x)|<\varepsilon
.

Now, to verify (
1.1
), fix
p\in P
,
x\in X
and
\varepsilon>0
. Let
\xi\in(0,\varepsilon/2)
be so small that for all
q\in B_{\xi}(p)
and all
y\in B_{\xi}(q)
we have
|g_{q}(y)-g_{p}(x)|<\varepsilon/2
, see (
4.1
).

Because
(f_{p})_{p\in P}
satisfy (
1.1
), we can find
\delta\in(0,\xi)
so small that for any
q\in B_{\delta}(p)
we can fix a
x_{q}\in B_{\xi}(x)
such that
f_{q}(x_{q})\leq f_{p}(x)+\xi
.

Then for all
q\in B_{\delta}(p)
and
y\in B_{\delta}(x)
we have
(f+g)_{q}(x_{q})-(f+g)_{p}(x)=(f_{q}(x_{q})-f_{p}(x))+(g_{q}(x_{q})-g_{p}(x))<\xi+\varepsilon/2<\varepsilon
, that is,
(f_{p}+g_{p})_{p\in P}
satisfy (
1.1
).

Turning now to (
1.2
), fix
p\in P
and
\varepsilon>0
. Because
g_{p}
is uniformly continuous, there is
\xi\in(0,\varepsilon/3)
such that
|g(y)-g(x)|<\varepsilon/3
for all
x
and
y
in
X
such that
d(y,x)<\xi
. Then it is easy to check that

(f_{p}+g_{p})_{\xi}(x)\leq(f_{p})_{\xi}(x)+g_{p}(x)+\varepsilon/3,\quad\forall x\in X.

(4.2)

Indeed,
(f_{p}+g_{p})_{\xi}(x)=\inf\{f_{p}(y)+g_{p}(y):\ y\in B_{\xi}(x)\}\leq\inf\{f_{p}(y)+g_{p}(x):\ y\in B_{\xi}(x)\}+\sup\{g_{p}(y)-g_{p}(x):\ y\in B_{\xi}(x)\}
, and the latter supremum does not exceed
\varepsilon/3
.

Let
\delta\in(0,\xi)
be so small that for all
q\in B_{\delta}(p)
we have that
\|g_{q}-g_{p}\|_{\infty}<\xi
, and also

f_{q}(x)\geq(f_{p})_{\xi}(x)-\xi,\quad\forall x\in X.

This is possible, because
(f_{p})_{p\in P}
satisfy (
1.2
). Then for all
q\in B_{\delta}(p)
and all
x\in X
we have
f_{q}(x)+g_{q}(x)\geq(f_{p})_{\xi}(x)+g_{p}(x)-2\xi
. Taking into account (
4.2
), we get
f_{q}(x)+g_{q}(x)\geq(f_{p}+g_{p})_{\xi}(x)-\varepsilon
. Because trivially
(f_{p}+g_{p})_{\xi}\geq(f_{p}+g_{p})_{\varepsilon}
, we conclude that
(f_{p}+g_{p})_{p\in P}
satisfy (
1.2
).
∎

The proof of our main result is now straightforward.

5
On Stečkin theory

Here we will show how Theorem
1.6
can be derived from our main framework.

We fix the notation as in Proposition
1.5
. In particular,
\cal N
is the set of all bounded seminorms on a Banach space
(E,\|\cdot\|)
. Obviously
\cal N
is a convex cone:
\alpha\nu_{1}+\beta\nu_{2}\in\cal N
for all
\nu_{i}\in\cal N
and all
\alpha,\beta\geq 0
.
The rigorous definition of the convex cone
{\cal N}_{0}\subset\cal N
of equivalent norms on
E
is:

\nu\in{\cal N}_{0}\iff\exists\ a,b>0\colon a\|x\|\leq\nu(x)\leq b\|x\|,\ \forall x\in E.

Since
\nu\in\cal N
implies
\nu(x)\leq\rho(\nu,0)\|x\|
for all
x\in E
, we have

v\in{\cal N}_{0}\iff a_{\nu}:=\inf\nu(S_{E})>0.

(5.1)

From this it is clear that

{\cal N}+{\cal N}_{0}={\cal N}_{0},

and, in particular,
\nu+\varepsilon\|\cdot\|\in{\cal N}_{0}
for any
\nu\in\cal N
and any
\varepsilon>0
. So,
{\cal N}_{0}
is dense in
\cal N
.
{\cal N}_{0}
is also open, as it follows from the following lemma. Then, obviously,
{\cal N}=\overline{{\cal N}_{0}}
and
{\cal N}_{0}={\cal N}^{\circ}
(the interior of
{\cal N}
).

Lemma 5.1
.

If
\nu\in{\cal N}_{0}
then
B_{a_{\nu}}^{\circ}(\nu)\subset{\cal N}_{0}
.

Proof.

Take a
\nu^{\prime}\in{\cal N}
such that
\rho(\nu^{\prime},\nu)<a_{\nu}
. Let
h\in S_{E}
be arbitrary. Then
\nu^{\prime}(h)=\nu(h)+\nu^{\prime}(h)-\nu(h)\geq a_{\nu}-\sup_{x\in B_{X}}|\nu^{\prime}(x)-\nu(x)|=a_{\nu}-\rho(\nu^{\prime},\nu)
. So,
a_{\nu^{\prime}}\geq a_{\nu}-\rho(\nu^{\prime},\nu)>0
and from (
5.1
) it follows that
\nu^{\prime}\in{\cal N}_{0}
.
∎

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
M\subset E
be a non-empty closed, convex and bounded set. Let
\nu\in\cal N
be such that

\inf_{M}\nu>0.

Then for each
\varepsilon>0
there is
\nu^{\prime}\in B_{\varepsilon}(\nu)
and
\delta>0
such that

\diam\{x\in M:\ \nu^{\prime}(x)\leq\inf_{M}\nu^{\prime}+\delta\}<\varepsilon.

(5.2)

Proof.

Fix
\varepsilon>0
.

Let
(x_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}\subset M
be a minimizing sequence for
\nu
, i.e.

\lim_{n\to\infty}\nu(x_{n})=\inf_{M}\nu.

Define
\nu_{n}\in\cal N
by

\nu_{n}(x):=\nu(x)+\varepsilon\min_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\|x-tx_{n}\|.

Note that
\nu_{n}\in\cal N
, because
\tilde{\nu}_{n}(x)=\varepsilon\min_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\|x-tx_{n}\|\in\cal N
. Also, since
\rho(0,\tilde{\nu}_{n})\leq\varepsilon
, we have
\nu_{n}\in B_{\varepsilon}(\nu)
.

Fix a sequence
\delta_{n}\searrow 0
. We claim that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\diam\{x\in M:\ \nu_{n}(x)\leq\inf_{M}\nu_{n}+\delta_{n}\}=0.

(5.3)

If this is true, we can take
\nu^{\prime}=\nu_{n}
for some large enough
n
.

Assume that (
5.3
) is false, that is, there is
c>0
such that for infinitely many
n
’s the diameter of the set in the left hand side of (
5.3
) is greater than
2c
. For avoiding subscripts and because dropping some
x_{n}
’s ruins nothing, let these diameters be greater than
2c
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. This means that for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
there are

y_{n}\in M\text{ such that }\ \nu_{n}(y_{n})\leq\inf_{M}\nu_{n}+\delta_{n},\text{ and }\|x_{n}-y_{n}\|>c.

(5.4)

Let
t_{n}\in\mathbb{R}
be such that
\|y_{n}-t_{n}x_{n}\|=\min_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\|y_{n}-tx_{n}\|
.

We have that

\nu(y_{n})\leq\nu_{n}(y_{n})\leq\inf_{M}\nu_{n}+\delta_{n}\leq\nu_{n}(x_{n})+\delta_{n}=\nu(x_{n})+\delta_{n}.

(5.5)

Therefore,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\nu(y_{n})=\inf_{M}\nu.

(5.6)

From (
5.5
) we get that
\nu_{n}(y_{n})\leq\nu(x_{n})+\delta_{n}
and having in mind that
\nu_{n}(y_{n})=\nu(y_{n})+\varepsilon\|y_{n}-t_{n}x_{n}\|
, we obtain

\varepsilon\|y_{n}-t_{n}x_{n}\|\leq\nu(x_{n})-\nu(y_{n})+\delta_{n}\to 0,\text{ as }n\to\infty.

Hence,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\|y_{n}-t_{n}x_{n}\|=0.

(5.7)

Since
\nu
is Lipschitz, (
5.7
) imply
\nu(y_{n})-\nu(t_{n}x_{n})\to 0
, as
n\to\infty
, that is,
\nu(y_{n})-|t_{n}|\nu(x_{n})\to 0
, as
n\to\infty
. Because, see (
5.6
), both
\nu(x_{n})
and
\nu(y_{n})
tend to
\inf_{M}\nu>0
, we can divide and get
\nu(y_{n})/\nu(x_{n})-|t_{n}|\to 0
, as
n\to\infty
, but
\nu(y_{n})/\nu(x_{n})\to 1
, so

\lim_{n\to\infty}|t_{n}|=1.

If there were a subsequence
(t_{n_{k}})_{k=1}^{\infty}
such that
t_{n_{k}}\to 1
, as
k\to\infty
, then because of (
5.7
), we would get
\|y_{n_{k}}-x_{n_{k}}\|\to 0
, as
k\to\infty
, which contradicts (
5.4
). Therefore,

\lim_{n\to\infty}t_{n}=-1.

Because
(x_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
is a bounded sequence (being in
M
, for example) this implies
\|(y_{n}+x_{n})-(y_{n}-t_{n}x_{n})\|=|1+t_{n}|\|x_{n}\|\to 0
, as
n\to\infty
. From (
5.7
) we get
(x_{n}+y_{n})\to 0
as
n\to\infty
. Since
M
is convex, we have
(x_{n}+y_{n})/2\in M
and because
M
is closed, we get
0\in M
, contradiction.
∎

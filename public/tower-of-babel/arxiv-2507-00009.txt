A note on D-functions and P-covariances on Hilbert spaces and related inequalities
S. Scarlatti

Abstract
In this note we first review the concept of D-function, closely connected with Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, and then introduce the notion of P-covariance on a Hilbert space, where P P is an orthogonal projection. We show that when P is specialized to be one-dimensional many well-known inequalities such as Buzano, Richard and Walker inequalities are simple consequences of P-covariance inequalities and their relation with D-functions. By means of these concepts enhancements of the previous mentioned inequalities are also established with minimum effort. A more thorough analysis of Walker inequality is presented jointly with some novel financial considerations as well as a new refinement of Holder inequality. Keywords : Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, Buzano inequality, Richard inequality, Walker inequality, Holder inequality, Sharpe Ratio.



1
Introduction

In Mathematics, so as in all applied sciences, quantitative comparisons of similar or even different quantities play a crucial role. Mathematical inequalities are key analytical results which express such comparisons in a neat way. In this note we focus on some classical inequalities in Hilbert spaces, but in the last part of it we shall also consider inequalities in
L^{p}
spaces (
p>1
).
We highlight some simple mathematical structures which underly a bunch of inequalities , some of which are very famous: Cauchy-Schwarz [1], Buzano [2], Richard [3], Walker [4]. The main aim of the work is to show how these structures, namely D-functions and P-covariances, allow for a fast-to-prove and easy-to-generalize type of approach to such inequalities. We also discuss some previously unnoticed aspects of Walker inequality related to the theory of financial investments [5], and use Walker to improve over classical Holder estimate on function spaces.

2
D-functions

Let
(H,\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{H})
a real Hilbert space,
V
any closed subspace of
H
and
P\equiv P_{V}
the corresponding orthogonal projection on
V
, that is
PH=V
. For any
x\in H
we introduce the quantities
p(x)\equiv||Px||
,
q(x)\equiv||P^{\perp}x||
and consider the two-dimensional positive vector

v_{P}(x)\equiv(p(x),q(x));

the following definition was set in [6]:

Definition 1
For fixed
P=P^{2}
define on
H\times H
the positive symmetric function

D(x,y|P)\equiv\langle v_{P}(x),v_{P}(y)\rangle_{\mathbb{R}^{2}},

(1)

that is
D(x,y|P)=||Px||||Py||+||P^{\perp}x||||P^{\perp}y||
, with
P+P^{\perp}=Id
.

We shall call such a function a
P
-decoupling function, more briefly a decoupling function [6],
P
being implied , since it completely separates operations on
x
from those on
y
.

A decoupling function, or D-function, has the following interesting properties:

Proposition 1.

(i)
D(x,x|P)=||x||^{2}

and

D(x,y|P^{\perp})=D(x,y|P)\geq 0
,

(ii)
D(\lambda x,\mu y|P)=|\lambda\mu|D(x,y|P)

\forall\lambda,\mu\in\mathbb{R}

and

D(x+x^{\prime},y|P)\leq D(x,y|P)+D(x^{\prime},y|P)
,

(iii)
D(P^{\#}x,y|P)=||P^{\#}x||\;||P^{\#}y||
for
P^{\#}\in\{P,P^{\perp}\}
,

(iv)
D((P-P^{\perp})x,y|P)=D(x,y|P)
,

(v)
D(x,y|P)=0\;\text{for all}\;(x,y)\in V\times V^{\perp}
, with
V=PH
,

(vi)
D(x,y|P)\geq|\langle x,y\rangle_{H}|
(
D
-inequality),

(vii)for all
x,y\in H
it holds

D(x,x|P)D(y,y|P)-D^{2}(x,y|P)=det^{2}\begin{pmatrix}v_{P}(x)\\
v_{P}(y)\end{pmatrix};

(2)

notice that for
y=\alpha x
the r.h.s. of (
2
) has zero value, however this condition is only a sufficient one for having zero.

From the first and the last two properties it follows that for any
P=P^{2}
we have

|\langle x,y\rangle_{H}|\leq D(x,y|P)\leq||x||||y||\;\forall x,y\in H

(3)

enhancing classical Cauchy-Schwarz inequality (CS). In particular the upper bound follows from (
2
), or even more directly, from classical CS-inequality applied to
\langle v_{P}(x),v_{P}(y)\rangle_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}
. The lower bound is simply property (vi) which is mainly a consequence of orthogonal decomposition in
H
, that is
Id=P+P^{\perp}
with
PP^{\perp}=0
, and of the classical CS-inequality [6]. This last fact is often reported as the ”self-improvement” property. We notice that considerations similar to those contained in [6] already appeared in [7], although the concept of D-function was not present there.

Remark 1:
We see that properties (vi) and (vii) automatically imply that for any projection
P
the following inequality holds:

||x||^{2}||y||^{2}-|\langle x,y\rangle|^{2}_{H}\geq det^{2}\begin{pmatrix}v_{P}(x)\\
v_{P}(y)\end{pmatrix},

(4)

generalizing a result proven in [8, (Thm.2)] for one dimensional projections.

3
P-covariances, Richard and Buzano

Definition 2.
: Let
P=P^{2}
be an orthogonal projection, we define on
H\times H
the following symmetric bilinear form

cov^{P}_{H}(x,y)\equiv\langle x-Px,y-Py\rangle_{H}=\langle x,y\rangle_{H}-\langle Px,y\rangle_{H},

(5)

for each pair
(x,y)
we call the value of the form the P-covariance between
x
and
y
. We also name the quantity
var_{H}^{P}(x)\equiv cov^{P}_{H}(x,x)=||x||^{2}-p(x)^{2}=q(x)^{2}\geq 0
the P-variance of
x
.

For a fixed
z\in H
of unitary norm let
P_{z}
denotes the one dimensional orthogonal projection over the subspace
V\equiv\{\lambda z:\lambda\in\mathbb{R}\}
, with
P_{z}x=\langle x,z\rangle_{H}z
. For this special case we set the notation

cov^{z}_{H}(x,y)\equiv cov^{P_{z}}_{H}(x,y)=\langle x,y\rangle_{H}-\langle x,z\rangle_{H}\langle y,z\rangle_{H};

(6)

and call this number the
z
-covariance between
x
and
y
. Clearly the above definition is such that it reproduces classical covariances when specialized to function spaces, see e.g. [10], or to random variables over probability spaces, see next section. However this generalization turns out to be useful, indeed we have:

Proposition 2.
The following inequalities hold for any projection
P
and any pair
(x,y)
:

|2\langle Px,y\rangle_{H}-\langle x,y\rangle_{H}|\leq D(x,y|P),\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;(eR)

2|\langle Px,y\rangle_{H}|\leq D(x,y|P)+|\langle x,y\rangle_{H}|,\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;(eB)

|\langle x,y\rangle_{H}|\leq||P^{\perp}x||||P^{\perp}y||+|\langle Px,y\rangle_{H}|,\;\;\;(eD)

(7)

where
(eD)
stands for ”enhanced D-inequality”, the other capital letters referring to names.

proof:
P-covariances enjoy the following upper bound:

|cov^{P}_{H}(x,y)|\leq q(x)q(y),

(8)

Indeed

|cov^{P}_{H}(x,y)|=|\langle x-Px,y-Py\rangle_{H}|\leq||P^{\perp}x||||P^{\perp}y||

(9)

by classical CS inequality. By adding
p(x)p(y)=||Px||\;||Py||
to both sides of (
9
) and noticing

|\langle Px,y\rangle_{H}+\bigl(\langle Px,y\rangle_{H}-\langle x,y\rangle_{H}\bigr)|\leq||Px||\;||Py||+|-cov^{P}_{H}(x,y)|

we obtain
(eR)
. Furthermore, being

\bigl||\langle x,y\rangle_{H}|-|\langle Px,y\rangle_{H}|\bigr|\leq|\langle x,y\rangle_{H}-\langle Px,y\rangle_{H}|\leq||P^{\perp}x||||P^{\perp}y||,

(10)

the following two inequalities hold true:

|\langle x,y\rangle_{H}|-|\langle Px,y\rangle_{H}|\leq||P^{\perp}x||||P^{\perp}y||\;\;\;\;\;(I)

|\langle Px,y\rangle_{H}|-|\langle x,y\rangle_{H}|\leq||P^{\perp}x||||P^{\perp}y||\;\;\;\;\;(II).

We rewrite inequality (II) as

|\langle Px,y\rangle|\leq||P^{\perp}x||||P^{\perp}y||+|\langle x,y\rangle_{H}|,

(11)

from which we easily obtain
(eB)
. Moreover, we also rearrange inequality
(I)
as

|\langle x,y\rangle_{H}|\leq||P^{\perp}x||||P^{\perp}y||+|\langle Px,y\rangle_{H}|,

proving
(eD)
. Being
|\langle Px,y\rangle_{H}|\leq||Px||\;||Py||
we notice this last estimate is even tighter than the
D
-inequality
|\langle x,y\rangle_{H}|\leq D(x,y|P)\leq||x||||y||
, however it is not of decoupling type.
\square

Remark 2:
Let
||z||=1
, then inequality
(eB)
improves and extends classical Buzano estimate

2|\langle x,z\rangle_{H}\langle y,z\rangle_{H}|\leq||x||||y||+|\langle x,y\rangle_{H}|,\;\;\;\;(B)

and inequality
(eR)
improves and extends classical Richard inequality

|2\langle x,z\rangle_{H}\langle y,z\rangle_{H}-\langle x,y\rangle_{H}|\leq||x||||y||,\;\;\;(R).

Remark 3:
In [9] it was proven that for any projection
P
it holds
2|\langle Px,y\rangle_{H}|\leq||x||||y||+|\langle x,y\rangle_{H}|
, so
(eB)
expresses a tighter bound.

It is remarkable how easily and quickly such generalizations have been obtained by means of D-functions and
P
-covariances.

4
Walker, Sharpe and Holder

By applying
(eD)
to the case
P\equiv P_{z}
, being
|\langle P_{z}x,P_{z}y\rangle_{H}|=||P_{z}x||\;P_{z}y||
, we just have

|\langle x,y\rangle_{H}|\leq||P^{\perp}_{z}x||||P^{\perp}_{z}y||+|\langle x,z\rangle_{H}\langle y,z\rangle_{H}|=D(x,y|P_{z}).

(12)

Walker inequality nicely fits the
z
-covariance framework corresponding to the choice of the following triplet:
H=L^{2}\equiv L^{2}(S,{\mathcal{F}},Q)
(
{\mathcal{F}}
a sigma-algebra,
Q
a probability measure), inner product
\langle X,Y\rangle_{L^{2}}\equiv\mathbf{E}(XY)
and
z^{*}\equiv 1\equiv 1_{S}
( the characteristic function of a set) as fixed unitary vector. By consequence

cov_{L^{2}}^{1}(X,Y)=\langle X,Y\rangle_{L^{2}}-\langle X,1\rangle_{L^{2}}\langle Y,1\rangle_{L^{2}}=cov(X,Y),

where
cov(X,Y)
denotes classical statistical covariance (see also [6],[7]). We also set
\sigma^{2}_{X}\equiv var_{L^{2}}^{1}(X)=\mathbf{E}(X^{2})-\mathbf{E}(X)^{2}
. For any
X\in L^{2}
define the one-dimensional projection
P_{1}X\equiv\langle X,1\rangle_{L^{2}}1=(\mathbf{E}X)1
and the vector
v_{P}(X)\equiv(|\mathbf{E}(X)|,\sigma_{X})
. We have:

(\mathbf{E}(XY))^{2}\leq||X||_{2}^{2}||Y||_{2}^{2}-\biggr(|\mathbf{E}X|\sigma_{Y}-|\mathbf{E}Y|\sigma_{X}\biggl)^{2}\leq||X||_{2}^{2}||Y||_{2}^{2}\;\;\forall X,Y\in L^{2},

(13)

however since, by (vii) of sect. 1, it holds

D^{2}(X,Y|P_{1})=||X||_{2}^{2}||Y||_{2}^{2}-\biggr(|\mathbf{E}X|\sigma_{Y}-|\mathbf{E}Y|\sigma_{X}\biggl)^{2}

we have that (
13
) is just a particular instance of (
12
), which in turn is an instance of
(eD)
. It is worth noticing that if
X
and
Y
are such that
|\mathbf{E}X|\sigma_{Y}=|\mathbf{E}Y|\sigma_{X}
, or equivalently, if it holds the relation

\biggl(\frac{\mathbf{E}X}{\sigma_{X}}\biggr)^{2}=\biggr(\frac{\mathbf{E}Y}{\sigma_{Y}}\biggr)^{2},

(14)

the improvement over CS inequality vanishes.
It is attractive to interpret condition (
14
) also in financial terms. Indeed suppose that
X
and
Y
are future excess-returns of two different portfolios investing in the stock market, where excess-return means the extra return w.r.t the risk-free rate. Adopting a Markowitz’s investment style the numbers
\sigma_{X}
and
\sigma_{Y}
are interpreted as the respective risks of these two financial positions. Then the Sharpe Ratio (SR) of a financial position offering
X
as future random return, introduced by W.Sharpe (who shared a Nobel price with H.Markowitz and M.Miller), is exactly given as the ratio
SR(X)\equiv\frac{\mathbf{E}X}{\sigma_{X}}
, a number which eventually can be negative if the investment performs badly. In general, the higher the ratio the more attractive the investment. On the basis of (
14
) we conclude that Walker inequality produce no improvement w.r.t. CS inequality if and only if
X
and
Y
are such that the squares of their Sharpe Ratios equalize. For more on this approach to investments and related questions see [5] and the paper [11].

We now show that estimate (
13
) can be used to improve Holder estimate:

Proposition 3.
For
X\in L^{p}
and
Y\in L^{q}
,
p,q>0
,
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=1
, it holds

\mathbf{E}(|XY|)\leq||X||_{p}||Y||_{q}\biggl(\frac{1}{p^{2}}+\frac{1}{q^{2}}+\frac{2}{pq}\sqrt{1-\biggl(\frac{\sigma_{V}\mathbf{E}|X|^{p/2}-\sigma_{U}\mathbf{E}|Y|^{q/2}}{||X||_{p}^{p/2}||Y||_{q}^{q/2}}\biggr)^{2}}\;\;\biggr)\leq||X||_{p}||Y||_{q}.

(15)

proof :
by Young inequality it is easy to show that for
X\in L^{p}
and
Y\in L^{q}
,
p,q>0
, it holds (see e.g. [12]):

\mathbf{E}(|XY|)\leq\left(\frac{1}{p^{2}}+\frac{1}{q^{2}}\right)\|X\|_{p}\|Y\|_{q}+\frac{2}{pq}\|X\|_{p}^{1-\frac{p}{2}}\|Y\|_{q}^{1-\frac{q}{2}}\mathbf{E}(|X|^{p/2}|Y|^{q/2}).

Now we set

U\equiv|X|^{p/2}\in L^{2}
and
V\equiv|Y|^{p/2}\in L^{2}
and therefore, by (
13
), we have

\mathbf{E}(UV)\leq\sqrt{||U||_{2}^{2}||V||_{2}^{2}-\biggr(|\mathbf{E}U|\sigma_{V}-|\mathbf{E}V|\sigma_{U}\biggl)^{2}}=\sqrt{||X||_{p}^{p}||Y||_{q}^{q}-\biggr(\sigma_{V}\mathbf{E}|X|^{p/2}-\sigma_{U}\mathbf{E}|Y|^{q/2}\biggl)^{2}}

=||X||_{p}^{p/2}||Y||_{q}^{q/2}\sqrt{1-\biggl(\frac{\sigma_{V}\mathbf{E}|X|^{p/2}-\sigma_{U}\mathbf{E}|Y|^{q/2}}{||X||_{p}^{p/2}||Y||_{q}^{q/2}}\biggr)^{2}}

Henceforth, for
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=1
, we have (
15
).
\square

Let us remark that this estimate differs from other improvements appearing in literature, see e.g. [13], at least for one aspect: in (
15
) informations about
X
and
Y
enter in a completely separated way which allows the bound to be quickly evaluated using a parallel mode computation for the moments of
X
and
Y
. Furthermore, in the case
p=q=2
inequality (
15
) reduces to

\mathbf{E}(|XY|)\leq||X||_{2}||Y||_{2}\biggl(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{1-\biggl(\frac{\sigma_{Y}\mathbf{E}|X|-\sigma_{X}\mathbf{E}|Y|}{||X||_{2}||Y||_{2}}\biggr)^{2}}\;\;\biggr)

=\frac{1}{2}||X||_{2}||Y||_{2}+\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{||X||_{2}^{2}||Y||_{2}^{2}-\biggr(|\mathbf{E}X|\sigma_{Y}-|\mathbf{E}Y|\sigma_{X}\biggl)^{2}};

(16)

Notice that this last estimate does not reproduce (
13
) exactly: indeed in (
13
) the first term of (
16
), that is the quantity
\frac{1}{2}||X||_{2}||Y||_{2}
, appears to be already replaced by the smaller quantity

\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{||X||_{2}^{2}||Y||_{2}^{2}-\biggr(|\mathbf{E}X|\sigma_{Y}-|\mathbf{E}Y|\sigma_{X}\biggl)^{2}};

so we may say that (
16
) contains just half of the improvement given by (
13
).

5
Conclusions

This paper highlights the enhancements of some well-known and useful inequalities which can be obtained with great simplicity and clarity by means of the concepts of D-functions and P-covariances over a Hilbert space.
In addition, considerations of financial nature widen the interpretation of some technical condition entering in Walker inequality. The same inequality is then used to produce a new refinement of Holder inequality. Overall , the obtained results pave the way to further investigations on the possible range of applicability to information theory , computer science and physics of the presented improvements.

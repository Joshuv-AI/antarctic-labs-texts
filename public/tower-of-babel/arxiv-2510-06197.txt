Relative stability theory and properness of K-moduli spaces
Harold Blum, Yuchen Liu, Chenyang Xu, Ziquan Zhuang

Abstract
. We define the relative stability threshold of a family of Fano varieties over a DVR and show that it is computed by a divisorial valuation. In the case when the special fiber is K-unstable, but the generic fiber is K-semistable, we use the divisorial valuation computing the threshold to replace the special fiber by a new one with a strictly larger stability threshold. Iterating this process yields a new and more direct proof of the properness of the K-moduli space that uses only birational geometry arguments.



1.
Introduction

In recent years, K-stability has been successfully used to construct a moduli theory for Fano varieties and, more generally, log Fano pairs.
The main output of the theory is the K-moduli space, which is a projective good moduli space parametrizing K-polystable log Fano pairs with fixed numerical invariants.

Arguably, the most important and difficult to prove property of the K-moduli space is its properness.
Prior to its proof, it was known that the K-moduli space is a finite type separated algebraic space by
[
22
,
9
,
2
]
.
Thus verifying the properness was equivalent to the following statement whose proof was completed in
[
33
]
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
C
be a spectrum of a DVR.
If
(X_{K},B_{K})
is a K-semistable log Fano pair over
K:=K(C)
, then there exists a dominant morphism of spectrum of DVRs
C^{\prime}\to C

such that

(X_{K^{\prime}},B_{K^{\prime}}):=(X_{K},B_{K})\times_{K}K^{\prime}

extends to a family of log Fano pairs
(X^{\prime},B^{\prime})\to C^{\prime}
with K-semistable fibers.

A first step toward this result was achieved in
[
30
]
.
By taking a semistable reduction and running well-chosen MMPs,
[
30
]
showed the existence of an extension of
(X_{K^{\prime}},B_{K^{\prime}})
to a family of log Fano pairs
(X^{\prime},B^{\prime})\to C^{\prime}
with special fiber not necessarily K-semistable.
Such an extension is often highly non-unique, and finding a K-semistable limit requires a more subtle analysis of which extension to take.

Theorem
1.1
was proven algebraically in
[
33
]
by combining higher rank finite generation results with K-stability arguments in
[
6
]
, the notion of
\Theta
-stratifications in
[
21
]
, and stack theoretic results in
[
3
]
.
In the special case when the pair
(X_{K},B_{K})
is log smooth, the theorem can alternatively be deduced from deep analytic results on Gromov–Hausdorff limits of Kähler-Einstein Fano manifolds
[
15
,
40
]
.

1.1.
Properness via
\Theta
-stratification

We now explain the prior algebraic proof of properness in more detail.
The proof begins by considering the moduli stack of log Fano pairs.
While this stack does not admit a good moduli space, it contains the moduli stack of K-semistable log Fano pairs as an open substack
[
9
]
and satisfies the existence part of the valuative criterion for properness
[
30
]
.

The papers
[
33
,
6
]
construct a
\Theta
-stratification on the moduli stack of log Fano pairs.
Roughly, this amounts to stratifying the locus of the stack parametrizing K-unstable log Fano pairs in terms of an invariant measuring instability and choosing a uniquely determined “optimal destabilizing” test configuration for each K-unstable log Fano pair that varies continuously on strata.

To construct such a stratification, a natural starting point is to consider the stability threshold of a log Fano pair
(X,B)
defined as

\delta(X,B):=\inf_{E}\frac{A_{X,B}(E)}{S_{X}(E)},

where the infimum runs through all divisors
E
over
X

[
17
,
7
]
.
Recall that
(X,B)
is K-semistable if and only if

\delta(X,B)\geq 1
by
[
18
,
32
]
.
By finite generation results for higher rank valuations in
[
33
]
, when
(X,B)
is K-unstable, this infimum is a minimum and induces a special test configuration that minimizes the functional
{\rm Fut}/\lVert\,\,\rVert_{\min}
on the space of special test configurations. While this minimizer is not always unique, one can further minimize the function
{\rm Fut}/\lVert\,\,\rVert_{2}
over the set of minimizers of
{\rm Fut}/\lVert\,\,\rVert_{\min}
.
The latter minimizer exists, is unique, and induces a
\Theta
-stratification in
[
2
]
.

With the existence of the stratification complete,
the semistable reduction theorem for algebraic stack admitting
\Theta
-stratification in
[
3
]
then implies that Theorem
1.1
holds. The latter result relies on a subtle analysis of the local stack structure of
\Theta
-stratification on an algebraic stack.

In this paper, we give a purely birational geometry proof of the properness of K-moduli spaces.
The proof replaces the delicate argument of studying a
\Theta
-stratification by a more explicit geometric construction using techniques from birational geometry.
While the argument still uses higher rank finite generation results of
[
33
,
42
]
, they are used in a more direct way.

1.2.
Birational approach to properness

Let us start with a family of log Fano pairs
(X,B)\to C
over the spectrum of a DVR
C
with closed point
0\in C
and
K:=K(C)
.
We assume

\delta(X_{0},B_{0})<\min\{1,\delta(X_{K},B_{K})\},

which means that the special fiber is less stable than the general fiber.
Our goal is to construct, after a possible base change by a dominant morphism of spectrums of DVRs
C^{\prime}\to C
, a new family of log Fano pairs
(X^{\prime},B^{\prime})\to C
such that

(X_{K},B_{K})\cong(X^{\prime}_{K},B^{\prime}_{K})\quad\text{ and }\quad\delta(X_{0},B_{0})<\delta(X^{\prime}_{0},B^{\prime}_{0}).

To begin, observe that the first isomorphism induces a birational map
X\dashrightarrow X^{\prime}
and, hence, the new family induces a divisorial valuation
\mathrm{ord}_{X^{\prime}_{0}}
of
X
.
We seek to pick out this valuation intrinsically.

To proceed, we define a relative version of the stability threshold that depends on a parameter
t\in[0,1)\cap\mathbb{Q}
.
We set

\delta_{t}(X,B)=\inf_{v}\frac{A_{X,B+(1-t)X_{0}}(v)}{S(v)},

where the infimum runs through all valuation
v
on
X
with finite log discrepancy.
Above
A_{X,B+(1-t)X_{0}}(v)
denotes the log discrepancy and
S(v)
the relative
S
-invariant (Section
3.1
).
If
v=\mathrm{ord}_{E}
where
E
is a prime divisor on a normal scheme
Y
with a proper birational morphism
\mu:Y\to X
, then

S(\mathrm{ord}_{E})=\frac{1}{\mathrm{vol}(-K_{X_{0}})}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{vol}_{Y|\widetilde{X}_{0}}(-\mu^{*}K_{X}-sE)\,\mathrm{d}s,

where
\mathrm{vol}_{Y|\widetilde{X}_{0}}
denotes the restricted volume as in
[
16
]
onto the strict transform of
X_{0}
on
Y
.
We say a valuation
v
on
X

computes

\delta_{t}(X,B)
if it computes the above infimum.

Using inversion of adjunction, we show that

\delta(X_{0},B_{0})=\delta_{0}(X,B)
and so the invariant recovers nothing new at
t=0
.
For
0<t\ll 1
, we show that a minimizer of the infimum exists and it can be used to construct our desired extension.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
C
be a spectrum of a DVR with algebraically closed residue field.
Let
0\in C
denote the closed point and
K:=K(C)
the fraction field.

If
(X,B)\to C
is a family of log Fano pairs with

\delta(X_{0},B_{0})<\min\{1,\delta(X_{K},B_{K})\},

then there exists
\varepsilon>0
such that for any
t\in(0,\varepsilon)\cap\mathbb{Q}
the following holds:

(1)

There exists a divisorial valuation
v=\mathrm{ord}_{E}
that computes
\delta_{t}(X,B)
.

(2)

If a divisorial valuation
v=\mathrm{ord}_{E}
computes
\delta_{t}(X,B)
, then there exists a dominant morphism of spectrums of DVRs
C^{\prime}\to C
and a family of log Fano pairs
(X^{\prime},B^{\prime})\to C^{\prime}
such that

(X_{K},B_{K})\times_{K}K^{\prime}\cong(X^{\prime}_{K^{\prime}},B^{\prime}_{K^{\prime}})\quad\text{ and }\quad\delta(X_{0},B_{0})<\delta(X^{\prime}_{0^{\prime}},B^{\prime}_{0^{\prime}}),

where
0^{\prime}\in C^{\prime}
is the closed point and
K^{\prime}=K(C^{\prime})
the function field.
Furthermore, the restriction of the valuation
\mathrm{ord}_{X^{\prime}_{0^{\prime}}}
via the inclusion
K(X)\subset K(X^{\prime})
is an integer multiple of
\mathrm{ord}_{E}
.

By repeatedly applying the above theorem and using discreteness results for the stability threshold of a log Fano pair with a fixed volume, we deduce the follow corollary, which is a generalization of Theorem
1.1
.

Corollary 1.3
.

Let
C
be a spectrum of a DVR with closed point
0
and fraction field
K
.
If
(X_{K},B_{K})\to{\rm Spec}(K)
is a log Fano pair over
K
, then there is a dominant morphism of spectrums of DVRs
C^{\prime}\to C
and a family of log Fano pairs
(X^{\prime},B^{\prime})\to C^{\prime}
such that

(X_{K},B_{K})\times_{K}K^{\prime}\cong(X^{\prime}_{K^{\prime}},B^{\prime}_{K^{\prime}})

and

\min\{1,\delta(X^{\prime}_{K^{\prime}},B^{\prime}_{K^{\prime}})\}=\min\{1,\delta(X^{\prime}_{0^{\prime}},B^{\prime}_{0^{\prime}})\}\,.

where
0^{\prime}\in C^{\prime}
is the closed point and
K^{\prime}:=K(C^{\prime})
.
Furthermore, if
(X_{K},B_{K})
is K-semistable, then all fibers of
(X^{\prime},B^{\prime})\to C^{\prime}
are K-semistable.

As a consequence of the above corollary, we give a new proof of the properness of the K-moduli space
X^{\rm K}_{n,V,N}
, parametrizing
n
-dimensional K-polystable log Fano pairs
(X,B)
with
(-K_{X}-B)^{n}=V
and
NB
is integral
[
44
, Definition 8.15]

Corollary 1.4
.

The K-moduli space
X^{\rm K}_{n,V,N}
is proper.

1.3.
Proof of Main Theorem

The proof of Theorem
1.2
has two main components.
We first begin by developing the theory of this relative stability threshold.
Much, but not all, of the theory on the stability threshold in the absolute case of a log Fano pair
[
7
,
33
]
extends easily to the relative setting and results in the proof of Theorem
1.2
.1.
The next step is to use an appropriate MMP to construct the extension and prove

\delta(X_{0},B_{0})<\delta(X^{\prime}_{0^{\prime}},B^{\prime}_{0^{\prime}}),

which turns out to be extremely subtle.

1.3.1.
Relative stability threshold

We first develop the theory of the relative stability threshold for the data of

f:(X,\Delta;L)\to C
, where
(X,\Delta)
is an lc pair,
f:X\to C
is a flat proper morphism with connected and reduced geometric fibers, and
L
is a relatively ample
\mathbb{Q}
-line bundle on
X
. Fix an integer
r>0
such that
rL
is a line bundle.
We write

\mathcal{R}:=\bigoplus_{m\in r\mathbb{N}}\mathcal{R}_{m}=\bigoplus_{m\in r\mathbb{N}}H^{0}(X,mL)\quad\text{and }\quad R:=\bigoplus_{m\in r\mathbb{N}}R_{m}=\bigoplus_{m\in r\mathbb{N}}H^{0}(X_{0},mL_{0})

for the relative section rings of
L
and
L_{0}
.
Furthermore, we assume that the restriction morphism
\mathcal{R}\otimes k(0)\to R
is surjective.

We define the
relative stability threshold
of
(X,\Delta;L)\to C
as

\delta(X,\Delta;L)=\inf_{v}\frac{A_{X,\Delta}(v)}{S(v)},

where the infimum runs through all valuations
v
on
X
with finite log discrepancy. Here
A_{X,\Delta}(v)
denotes the log discrepancy of
v
along
(X,\Delta)
and
S(v)
is the relative
S
-invariant.
To define the latter invariant, let
\mathcal{F}_{v}
be the filtration of
\mathcal{R}
defined by

\mathcal{F}_{v}^{\lambda}\mathcal{R}_{m}:=\{s\in\mathcal{R}_{m}\,|\,v(s)\geq\lambda\}

and
\mathcal{F}_{v}|_{X_{0}}
be the filtration of
R
defined via restriction:

(\mathcal{F}_{v}|_{X_{0}})^{\lambda}R_{m}:=\mathrm{im}(\mathcal{F}_{v}^{\lambda}\mathcal{R}_{m}\otimes k(0)\to R_{m})\subset R_{m}.

We then define
S(v)
as the
S
-invariant of the filtration
\mathcal{F}_{v}|_{X_{0}}
of
R
.
This invariant may also be interpreted as an integral of restricted volumes when
v
is divisorial (Remark
3.7
).

Relation to basis type divisors
.
For
m\in r\mathbb{N}^{+}
, a
relative
m
-basis type divisor
of
L
is a
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor on
X
of the form

\tfrac{1}{mN_{m}}\left(\{s_{1}=0\}+\cdots+\{s_{N_{m}}=0\}\right),

where
(s_{1},\ldots,s_{N_{m}})
is a basis for
\mathcal{R}_{m}
as a free
\mathcal{O}(C)
-module.
We set

\delta_{m}(X,\Delta;L):=\inf\{\mathrm{lct}(X,\Delta;D)\,|\,\text{$D$ is an $m$-basis type divisor of $L$}\}.

By the same argument as in the absolute case in
[
7
]
, the following holds:

(1)

S(v)=\lim_{m\to\infty}S_{mr}(v)
, where

S_{m}(v)=\max\{v(D)\,|\,\text{ $D$ is an $m$-basis type divisor of $L$}\},

and the infimum is achieved when
D
is compatible with
v
(see Section
3.1.4
),

(2)

\delta_{m}(X,\Delta;L)=\inf_{v}\frac{A_{X,\Delta}(v)}{S_{m}(v)}
(Proposition
3.12
), and

(3)

\delta(X,\Delta;L):=\lim_{m\to\infty}\delta_{mr}(X,\Delta;L)
(Proposition
3.12
).

Existence of quasi-monomial minimizer.

Under mild assumptions, we show that there exists a quasi-monomial valuation
v
that computes the infimum in the definition of
\delta(X,\Delta;L)
(Theorem
3.18
).
This is achieved by extending the generic limits argument for constructing minimizers of the stability threshold in the absolute case in
[
7
]
to the relative case and applying the main result of
[
43
]
to choose a minimizer that is quasi-monomial.

Existence of divisorial minimizer.

Next we specialize to the case when
(X,\Delta)\to C
is a relative log Fano pair, i.e.
(X,\Delta)
is klt and
-K_{X}-\Delta
is ample over
C
.
(The main example to keep in mind is, given by taking a family of log Fano pairs
(X,B)\to C
and then considering
(X,B+(1-t)X_{0})\to C
with
t\in(0,1]\cap\mathbb{Q}
.)
In this case, we set

\delta(X,\Delta):=\delta(X,\Delta;-K_{X}-\Delta),

where we view
-K_{X}-\Delta
as a
\mathbb{Q}
-line bundle.

We show that if a relative log Fano pair
(X,\Delta)\to C
satisfies

\delta(X,\Delta)<\min\{1,\delta(X_{K},\Delta_{K})\}

and other mild assumptions hold, then there exists a divisorial valuation computing
\delta(X,\Delta)
.
Following the strategy in
[
33
]
, we take a quasi-monomial valuation
v
computing the stability threshold and then show that the associated graded ring

{\rm gr}_{v}\mathcal{R}:=\bigoplus_{m\in r\mathbb{N}}\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{F}_{v}^{\lambda}\mathcal{R}_{m}/\mathcal{F}_{v}^{>\lambda}\mathcal{R}_{m}

is finitely generated.
Similar to arguments in the absolute case, finite generation implies a linearity property for the
S
-invariant near
v
that implies the existence of a divisorial valuation computing
\delta(X,\Delta)
.

To prove finite generation, we aim to apply the higher rank finite generation results from
[
33
,
42
]
.
For this we need to show that the quasi-monomial minimizer
v
is an lc place of a special
\mathbb{Q}
-complement of
(X,\Delta)
(see Definition
4.2
).
A key technical ingredient towards verifying the latter condition is to show that, for any horizontal prime divisor
G
on
X
, there exists a complement
\Delta^{+}
of
(X,\Delta)
such that
v
is an lc place of
(X,\Delta^{+})
and
\mathrm{Supp}(\Delta^{+})\supset\mathrm{Supp}(G)
.
To verify this condition, we would like to take a relative
m
-basis type divisor
D_{m}
for each
m\in r\mathbb{N}^{+}

such that
D_{m}
is compatible with
v
and
\mathrm{ord}_{G}
.
Unfortunately, we cannot a priori assume that both conditions hold as, unlike the vector space case, we cannot simultaneously diagonalize any two filtrations of a free module over a DVR.

To circumvent the latter issue, we consider asymptotically increasing thickenings of
X_{0}
.
In particular, for
0<c\ll 1
, we consider bases for
\mathcal{R}_{m}/\mathfrak{m}_{C,0}^{\lceil cm\rceil}\mathcal{R}_{m}
over the residue field
\mathbbm{k}
, lift these sections to
\mathcal{R}_{m}
, and then define the notion of an
(m,c)
-basis type divisor
D_{m,c}\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}-K_{X}-\Delta
.
Using this, we can define invariants
S_{c}(\Delta)
,
\delta_{c}(X,\Delta)
satisfying
\delta_{c}(X,\Delta)=\inf_{v}\frac{A_{X,\Delta}(v)}{S_{c}(v)}
defined using
(m,c)
-basis type divisors rather than
m
-basis type divisors.
We show that if
v
computes
\delta:=\delta(X,\Delta)
, then
v
also computes
\delta_{c}(X,\Delta-c\delta X_{0})
.
By simultaneously diagonalizing the filtrations of
\mathcal{R}_{m}/t^{\lceil cm\rceil}\mathcal{R}_{m}
induced by
v
and
G
, we
can construct a
(m,c)
-basis type divisor
D_{m}
compatible with both
v
and containing a bounded from zero multiple of
G
.
Using this, we construct a complement
\Delta^{+}
of
(X,\Delta)
such that
v
is an lc place of
(X,\Delta-c\delta X_{0})
and
\mathrm{Supp}(\Delta^{+})\subset\mathrm{Supp}(G)
.
As a consequence, we deduce that
v
is a special lc place of
(X,\Delta-c\delta X_{0})
and, hence, deduce the finite generation of
{\rm gr}_{v}\mathcal{R}
using
[
42
]
.

1.3.2.
Extension induced by minimizer

We now explain the proof of Theorem
1.2
.2.
Let

(X,B)\to C

be a family of log Fano pairs satisfying the assumptions of the theorem.
We now fix
0<t\ll 1
and set

\Delta:=B+(1-t)X_{0}.

Let
\mathrm{ord}_{E}
be a divisorial minimizer of
\delta(X,\Delta)
whose existence is guaranteed by Theorem
1.2
.1.
For simplicity, we assume that
\mathrm{ord}_{E}(X_{0})=1
in order to avoid having to take a base change in the statement of Theorem
1.2
.2.

We first show that if
0<t\ll 1
, then
\mathrm{ord}_{E}
is an lc place of a
\mathbb{Q}
-complement of
(X,B+X_{0})
.
Using this, we use the MMP to construct a flat proper family of pairs

(X^{\prime},B^{\prime})\to C

with an isomorphism

(X_{K},B_{K})\cong(X^{\prime}_{K},B^{\prime}_{K})

over
K
such that
X^{\prime}_{0}
is integral and corresponds to the divisor
E
over
X
.
Furthermore, we argue that
(X^{\prime},B^{\prime})\to C

To verify the inequality of stability thresholds, we aim to show that

\delta(X_{0},B_{0})=\delta(X,B+X_{0})<\delta(X,\Delta)\leq\delta(X^{\prime},B^{\prime}+X^{\prime}_{0})=\delta(X^{\prime}_{0},B_{0}^{\prime}).

The first and last equalities are simple consequences of adjunction and the characterization of the stability threshold in terms of lct’s, while the second uses that

\delta(X,B+X_{0})\leq\frac{A_{X,B+X_{0}}(\mathrm{ord}_{E})}{S(\mathrm{ord}_{E})}<\frac{A_{X,\Delta}(\mathrm{ord}_{E})}{S(\mathrm{ord}_{E})}=\delta(X,\Delta)

Verifying the remaining inequality

(1.1)

\delta(X,\Delta)\leq\delta(X^{\prime},B^{\prime}+X^{\prime}_{0})

To build intuition for why (
1.1
) holds, let’s first impose the simplifying assumption that
\mathrm{ord}_{E}
also computes the infimum

\delta_{m}(X,\Delta)=\inf_{v}\frac{A_{X,\Delta}(v)}{S_{m}(v)}

for all
m\in r\mathbb{N}^{+}
.
Using that
\mathrm{ord}_{E}
is the lc place of a complement, we construct a proper birational model of

\varepsilon>0
, there exists
m_{0}:=m_{0}(\varepsilon)
such that

(1.3)

S_{mr}(w)-w(E_{Y})S_{mr}(\mathrm{ord}_{E})\leq(1+\varepsilon)(S(w)-w(E_{Y})S(\mathrm{ord}_{E}))

for all
m\geq m_{0}
and
w\in\mathrm{DivVal}_{X}
.
This is reminiscent of a key convergence result
[
7
, Corollary 3.6]
, which shows the corresponding statement for the inequality

S_{mr}(w)\leq(1+\varepsilon)S(w)
in the absolute case.
Geometrically, the statement in
[
7
]
is related to the value

w(D_{m})
, where
D_{m}
is an
m
-basis type divisor compatible with
w
, while (
1.3
) is related to vanishing order of

w(g_{*}^{-1}D_{m})=w(D_{m})-\mathrm{ord}_{E}(D_{m})w(E_{Y}).

To prove (
1.3
), we first verify a version of the statement in the absolute case by using an Okounkov body argument as in
[
7
]
by choosing
E
to be the divisor in the chosen flag (see Theorem
2.10
).
Unfortunately, we cannot deduce the relative version from the absolute version as the filtration of
\mathcal{R}
induced by
\mathrm{ord}_{E}
does not necessarily restrict to a filtration of
R
induced by a single valuation.
Instead, we use
\mathrm{ord}_{E}
to degenerate
X_{0}
to a reduced projective scheme for which we can apply the absolute case to the irreducible components.
This last step requires generalizing various results on asymptotic invariants of filtrations of section rings of projective varieties to reduced projective schemes (Section
2.1.3
).

1.4.
Alternative approaches to properness

The proof of properness in
[
33
,
6
]
relies on using divisorial minimizers of the
\delta
-invariant to construct a
\Theta
-stratication of the moduli stack of log Fano pairs.
Minimizers of various other K-stability related functionals on the valuation space give rise to other approaches to proving properness.

The papers
[
36
,
35
]
pursue the approach to proving properness using the minimizer of the
H
-invariant and the minimizer of the normalized volume function on the cone to construct optimal destabilizing
\mathbb{R}
-test configurations.
These destabilizations were constructed in
[
8
,
42
]
as a consequence of higher rank finite generation results.
As the latter destabilizations are
\mathbb{R}
-test configurations, this approach relies on defining a higher rank version of
\Theta
-stratifaction and generalizing the semistable reduction result in
[
3
]
to the higher rank case
[
35
,
13
]
.

1.5.
Extensions

In an upcoming paper, we will show that the techniques of this paper can be used to prove the properness of the moduli space of weighted K-semistable log Fano pairs. As a consequence, we will deduce the properness of moduli stack of K-semistable log Fano cone singularities and log Fano pairs admitting Kähler-Ricci soliton.

Structure of the Paper.

The paper is structured as follows.
In Section
2
, we discuss preliminaries on pairs, valuations, and filtrations of graded linear series. In particular, we generalize results in the literature on filtered graded linear series of projective varieties to the case of reduced projective schemes.
In Section
3
, we define the relative stability threshold, prove basic properties of the invariant, and the existence of a quasi-monomial valuation computing the invariant. In Section
4
, we prove the existence of a divisorial valuation computing the invariant in the log Fano case.
In Section
5
, we use the divisorial minimizer of the stability threshold to construct a replacement of the special fiber with increased stability threshold.

2.0.1.
Pairs

A pair
(X,\Delta)
is the data of a scheme
X
over
\mathbb{Q}
that is normal, Noetherian, excellent, integral, and with a canonical class
[
27
, Definition 11.3]
and an effective
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor
\Delta
such that
K_{X}+\Delta
is
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier.
For notions of singularities of pairs, e.g. lc, klt, dlt, see
[
27
, Definition 11.5]
.
A pair over a scheme
Z
is a pair
(X,\Delta)
with a morphism
X\to Z
.

We use the above definition of a pair (that does not assume the scheme is finite type over a field) as we are primarily interested in pairs
(X,\Delta)
where
X
is projective over the spectrum of a DVR.
The major results of the MMP in
[
5
]
are generalized to the above class of pairs in
[
34
]
.

A
\mathbb{Q}
-complement
of a pair
(X,\Delta)
over
Z
is the data of a
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor
\Delta^{+}
such that
(X,\Delta^{+})
is lc,
K_{X}+\Delta^{+}\sim_{\mathbb{Q},Z}0
, and
\Delta^{+}\geq\Delta
.
We often, a bit abusively, also refer to
\Gamma:=\Delta^{+}-\Delta
as the complement
(X,\Delta)/Z
.

2.0.2.
Log Fano pairs

A
log Fano pair
is a klt pair
(X,\Delta)
such that
X
is projective over a field
\mathbbm{k}
,
X
is geometrically connected, and
-K_{X}-\Delta
is ample.
A
relative log Fano pair
is a klt pair
(X,\Delta)
over
Z
such that
X\to Z
is projective and
-K_{X}-\Delta
is ample over
Z
. A pair
(X,\Delta)
is of Fano type over
Z
if there exists some
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor
\Delta^{\prime}\geq\Delta
such that
(X,\Delta^{\prime})
is a relative log Fano pair.

A
family of log Fano pairs

(X,\Delta)\to C
over the spectrum of a DVR
C

is the data of a flat proper morphism
X\to C
with geometrically connected fibers and a
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor
\Delta
on
X
such that
(X,\Delta+X_{0})
is a plt pair and
-K_{X}-\Delta
is ample over
C
.
Note that
(X,\Delta)\to C
is a locally stable family in the sense of
[
27
, Section 2.1]

and that the fibers are log Fano pairs by adjunction.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
X\to Z
and
f\colon Y\to X
be projective morphisms. Assume that
(X,0)
is of Fano type over
Z
and there exists some
\mathbb{Q}
-complement
\Delta
of
X
over
Z
such that
A_{X,\Delta}(E)<1
for every
f
-exceptional divisor
E
, then
(Y,0)
is of Fano type over
Z
.

Proof.

By the Fano type condition on
X
, we can find some
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor
\Delta^{\prime}
on
X
such that
(X,\Delta^{\prime})
is klt and
-(K_{X}+\Delta^{\prime})
is ample over
Z
. Replacing
\Delta^{\prime}
with
(1-\varepsilon)\Delta+\varepsilon\Delta^{\prime}
for some
0<\varepsilon\ll 1
, we may further assume that
A_{X,\Delta^{\prime}}(E)<1
for every
f
-exceptional divisor
E
. Define a
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor
\Delta^{\prime}_{Y}
on
Y
by the crepant pullback formula

K_{Y}+\Delta^{\prime}_{Y}=f^{*}(K_{X}+\Delta^{\prime}).

Then
\Delta^{\prime}_{Y}\geq 0
, the pair
(Y,\Delta^{\prime}_{Y})
is also klt over
Z
, and
-(K_{Y}+\Delta^{\prime}_{Y})
is big and nef over
Z
. Thus
Y
is of Fano type over
Z
.
∎

2.0.3.
Valuations

Let
X
be an integral scheme over
\mathbbm{k}
. A
valuation
on
X
is a valuation
K(X)^{\times}\to\mathbb{R}
such that
v(\mathbbm{k}^{\times})=0
. We write
\mathrm{Val}_{X}
for the set of valuations on
X
, and
\mathrm{DivVal}_{X}
for the subset of divisorial valuations.
The center of a valuation
v\in\mathrm{Val}_{X}
is denoted by
c_{X}(v)\in X
. We write
C_{X}(v):=\overline{c_{X}(v)}
for its closure.

For a pair
(X,\Delta)
, there is a log discrepancy function
A_{X,\Delta}:\mathrm{Val}_{X}\to\mathbb{R}
, see
[
44
, Definition 1.34]
.
We write
\mathrm{Val}_{X}^{\circ}:=\{v\in\mathrm{Val}_{X}\,|\,A_{X,\Delta}(v)<\infty\}
, which is independent of the choice of
\Delta
by
[
44
, Lemma 1.36]
.
An lc place of an lc pair
(X,\Delta)
is a valuation
v
on
X
with
A_{X,\Delta}(v)=0
.

2.0.4.
Filtrations

We now formulate a definition of a filtration of a graded ring that we will frequently apply to both section rings and relative section rings.

Let
R:=\bigoplus_{m\in r\mathbb{N}}R_{m}
be a graded ring indexed by
r\mathbb{N}
, where
r
is a fixed positive integer.
A
filtration

\mathcal{F}
of
R
is the data of
R_{0}
-submodules

\mathcal{F}^{\lambda}R_{m}\subseteq R_{m}
for each
m\in r\cdot\mathbb{N}
and
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
satisfying

(1)

\mathcal{F}^{\lambda}R_{m}\subseteq F^{\lambda^{\prime}}R_{m}
when
\lambda\geq\lambda^{\prime}
,

(2)

\mathcal{F}^{\lambda}R_{m}=\bigcap_{\lambda^{\prime}<\lambda}\mathcal{F}^{\lambda^{\prime}}R_{m}
,

(3)

\mathcal{F}^{\lambda}R_{m}\cdot\mathcal{F}^{\lambda^{\prime}}R_{m^{\prime}}\subseteq\mathcal{F}^{\lambda+\lambda^{\prime}}R_{m+m^{\prime}}
,

(4)

\cap_{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{F}^{\lambda}R_{m}=0
and
\mathcal{F}^{-\lambda}R_{m}=R_{m}
for
\lambda\gg 0
.

The filtration is an
\mathbb{N}
-filtration
if
\mathcal{F}^{0}R_{m}=R_{m}
and
\mathcal{F}^{\lambda}R_{m}=\mathcal{F}^{\lceil\lambda\rceil}R_{m}
for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
and
m\in r\mathbb{N}
.
The filtration is
linearly bounded
if there exists
C>0
such that
\mathcal{F}^{-Cm}R_{m}=R_{m}
and
\mathcal{F}^{Cm}R_{m}=0
for all
m\in r\mathbb{N}
.

The
associated graded ring
of a filtration
\mathcal{F}
of
R
is the graded ring

{\rm gr}_{\mathcal{F}}R:=\bigoplus_{m\in r\mathbb{N}}\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}}{\rm gr}_{\mathcal{F}}^{\lambda}R_{m},

where
{\rm gr}_{\mathcal{F}}^{\lambda}R_{m}:=\mathcal{F}^{\lambda}R_{m}/\mathcal{F}^{>\lambda}R_{m}
and
\mathcal{F}^{>\lambda}R_{m}:=\cap_{\lambda^{\prime}>\lambda}\mathcal{F}^{\lambda^{\prime}}R_{m}
.
For
s\in R_{m}
, we set

\mathrm{ord}_{\mathcal{F}}(s):=\max\{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}\,|\,s\in\mathcal{F}^{\lambda}R_{m}\}.

2.1.
Filtered graded linear series

We now discuss asymptotic invariants of filtered graded linear series and prove some new convergence results.

Throughout, let
X
be a projective geometrically reduced scheme over a characteristic 0 field
\mathbbm{k}
,
L
a
\mathbb{Q}
-line bundle on
X
, and
r
a positive integer such that
rL
is a line bundle.

2.1.1.
Graded linear series

We write

R=R(X,L)=\bigoplus_{m\in r\mathbb{N}}R_{m}:=\bigoplus_{m\in r\mathbb{N}}H^{0}(X,mL)

for the section ring of
L
with respect to the index
r
.
A
graded linear series
of
L
is a graded
\mathbbm{k}
-subalgebra

V_{\bullet}=\bigoplus_{m\in r\mathbb{N}}V_{m}=\bigoplus_{m\in r\mathbb{N}}R_{m}\subset R.

The
volume
of a graded linear series
V_{\bullet}
of

is

\mathrm{vol}(V_{\bullet}):=\limsup_{m\to\infty}\frac{\dim_{\mathbbm{k}}V_{mr}}{(mr)^{n}/n!},

where
n:=\dim X
.
When
X
is irreducible, we say that a graded linear series
V_{\bullet}\subset R_{\bullet}

contains an ample series
if
V_{mr}\neq 0
for
m\gg 0
and
there exists
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier divisors
A
and
E
, ample and effective respectively, such that
L=A+E
and

H^{0}(X,mA)\subset V_{m}\subset H^{0}(X,mL).

for all
m>0
sufficiently large and divisible.

Proposition 2.2
.

If
X
is geometrically integral and
V_{\bullet}
is a graded linear series of
L
that contains an ample linear series, then
\mathrm{vol}(V_{\bullet})
is a limit and is positive.

Proof.

This was first proven in
[
28
]
and is stated in
[
44
, Theorem 1.11]
using our terminology.
∎

2.1.2.
Filtrations

A
filtration

\mathcal{F}
of a graded linear series
V_{\bullet}
is a filtration of the graded
\mathbbm{k}
-algebra as in Section
2.0.4
.
A basis
(s_{1},\ldots,s_{N_{m}})
for
V_{m}
is
compatible
with
\mathcal{F}
if each
\mathcal{F}^{\lambda}V_{m}
is a span of the
s_{i}
or, equivalently,
\overline{s_{i}}\in{\rm gr}_{\mathcal{F}}^{\lambda_{i}}V_{m}
form a basis for
{\rm gr}_{\mathcal{F}}V_{m}
, where
\lambda_{i}:=\mathrm{ord}_{\mathcal{F}}(s_{i})
.
For
t\in\mathbb{R}
, we write
V_{\bullet}^{\mathcal{F},t}
for the graded linear series of
L

defined by
V_{m}^{\mathcal{F},t}:=\mathcal{F}^{mt}V_{m}
.

The
S
and
T
invariants of a filtration
\mathcal{F}
of

are defined by setting

S_{m}(\mathcal{F}):=\frac{1}{m\dim V_{m}}\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}}\lambda\cdot\dim{\rm gr}_{\mathcal{F}}^{\lambda}V_{m}\quad\text{ and }\quad T_{m}(\mathcal{F}):=\frac{1}{m}\sup\{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}\,|\,\mathcal{F}^{\lambda}V_{m}\neq 0\}.

for

divisible by

and then setting

S(\mathcal{F})=\limsup_{m\to\infty}S_{mr}(\mathcal{F})\quad\text{ and }\quad T(\mathcal{F})=\sup_{m\geq 1}T_{mr}(\mathcal{F}).

When the choice of
V_{\bullet}
is not clear from context, we write
S(\mathcal{F},V_{\bullet})
and
T(\mathcal{F},V_{\bullet})
for these invariants.

Proposition 2.3
.

Assume that
X
is geometrically integral.
If
V_{\bullet}
a graded linear series of
L
that contains an ample series
and
\mathcal{F}
is a linearly bounded filtration of

(1)

and

.

(2)

V_{\bullet}^{\mathcal{F},t}
contains an ample series for
t<T(\mathcal{F})
.

(3)

If
\mathcal{F}^{0}V_{\bullet}=V_{\bullet}
and

(n+1)^{-1}T(\mathcal{F})\leq S(\mathcal{F})\leq T(\mathcal{F})

and

S(\mathcal{F})=\frac{1}{\mathrm{vol}(V_{\bullet})}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{vol}(V_{\bullet}^{\mathcal{F},t})\,dt.

Proof.

See
[

∎

Proposition 2.4
.

2.1.3.
Reducible schemes

We will now extend convergence results for the
S
-invariant to the case when
X
is not necessarily irreducible.
To begin, we write

X=X_{1}\cup\cdots\cup X_{s},

where each
X_{i}
is a distinct irreducible component of
X
,
and set
L_{i}:=L|_{X_{i}}
.
We construct a graded linear series
V_{\bullet}^{i}
of
L_{i}

for each
1\leq i\leq s

by setting

W_{m}^{i}:=V_{m}\cap H^{0}(X,L\otimes\mathcal{I}_{X_{1}\cup\cdots\cup X_{i-1}})\subset H^{0}(X,mL)

and

V_{m}^{i}:={\rm im}(W_{m}^{i}\to H^{0}(X_{i},mL_{i}))\subset H^{0}(X_{i},mL_{i}),

where the previous map is induced by the restriction map on sections

r_{i}:H^{0}(X,mL)\to H^{0}(X_{i},mL_{i}).

Note that
V_{\bullet}^{i}
is a graded linear series of
L_{i}
.

Lemma 2.5
.

The following hold:

(1)

For
m\in r\mathbb{N}
,
\dim H^{0}(X,mL)=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\dim V_{m}^{i}

(2)

If
L
is ample, then each
V_{\bullet}^{i}
contains an ample series.

Proof.

For each
1\leq i\leq s
and
m\in r\mathbb{N}
, we have a short exact sequence

0\to W_{m}^{i+1}\to W_{m}^{i}\to V_{m}^{i}\to 0,

where we use the convention that
W_{m}^{s+1}=0
.
Thus

\sum_{i=1}^{s}\dim V_{m}^{i}=\sum_{i=1}^{s}(\dim W_{m}^{i}-\dim W_{m}^{i+1})=\dim W_{m}^{1}=\dim H^{0}(X,mL),

which completes the proof of (1).

Now assume that
L
is ample.
Let
\mathcal{I}_{i}:=\mathcal{I}_{X_{1}\cup\cdots\cup X_{i-1}}
.
For
m\in r\mathbb{N}
, the short exact sequence

0\to\mathcal{O}_{X}(mL)\otimes\mathcal{I}_{i+1}\to\mathcal{O}_{X}(mL)\otimes\mathcal{I}_{i}\to\mathcal{O}_{X}(mL)\otimes\mathcal{I}_{i}|_{X_{i}}\to 0

induces a long exact sequence

0\to W_{m}^{i+1}\to W_{m}^{i}\to H^{0}(X_{i},L_{i}\otimes\mathcal{I}_{i}|_{X_{i}})\to H^{1}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(mL)\otimes\mathcal{I}_{i})\to\cdots.

When additionally
m\gg 0
,
H^{1}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(mL)\otimes\mathcal{I}_{i})=0
by Serre vanishing and so

V_{m}^{i}=H^{0}(X_{i},\mathcal{O}_{X_{i}}(mL_{i})\otimes\mathcal{I}_{i}|_{X_{i}}).

Since
L_{i}
is ample, it follows that
V_{m}^{i}\neq 0
for
m\in r\mathbb{N}
sufficiently large.
Next, choose a Cartier divisor
D_{i}
on
X_{i}
such that
\mathcal{O}_{X_{i}}(-D_{i})\subset\mathcal{I}_{i}|_{X_{i}}
.
Fix
l\gg 0
and divisible by
r
such that
A_{i}:=lL_{i}-D_{i}
is ample.
For
m\gg 0
, we have

H^{0}(X_{i},mlA_{i})\subset H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X_{i}}(mlL_{i})\otimes\mathcal{I}_{i}|_{X_{i}})=V_{ml}^{i}\subset H^{0}(X_{i},mlL_{i}).

Therefore
V_{\bullet}^{i}
contains ample series.
∎

We now generalize the previous construction to the setting of filtrations.
Given a filtration
\mathcal{F}
of
R
,
we define a filtration
\mathcal{F}_{i}
of
V_{\bullet}^{i}
by setting

\mathcal{F}_{i}^{\lambda}V_{m}^{i}:=\mathrm{im}\left(\mathcal{F}^{\lambda}R_{m}\cap W_{m}^{i}\to V_{m}^{i}\right).

It is straightforward to check that
\mathcal{F}_{i}
is indeed a filtration of
V_{\bullet}^{i}
and is linearly bounded when
\mathcal{F}
is linearly bounded.

Lemma 2.6
.

If
\mathcal{F}
is a filtration of
R
, then

S_{m}(\mathcal{F})=\sum_{i=1}^{s}\frac{\dim V_{m}^{i}}{\dim R_{m}}S_{m}(\mathcal{F}_{i})

and

T_{m}(\mathcal{F})=\sup_{i=1,\ldots,s}T_{m}(\mathcal{F}_{i})

for each
m>0
divisible by
r
.

Proof.

For each
i\in\{1,\ldots,s\}
,
m\in r\mathbb{N}
, and
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
, there is a short exact sequence

(2.1)

0\to W_{m}^{i+1}\cap\mathcal{F}^{\lambda}R_{m}\to W_{m}^{i}\cap\mathcal{F}^{\lambda}R_{m}\to\mathcal{F}_{i}^{\lambda}V_{m}^{i}\to 0,

where
W_{m}^{s+1}\cap\mathcal{F}^{\lambda}R_{m}=0
.
By arguing in the proof of Lemma
2.5
,

\dim\mathcal{F}^{\lambda}R_{m}=\sum_{i=1}^{s}\dim\mathcal{F}_{i}^{\lambda}V_{m}^{i},

which implies that

\dim{\rm gr}_{\mathcal{F}}^{\lambda}R_{m}=\sum_{i=1}^{s}\dim{\rm gr}_{\mathcal{F}_{i}}^{\lambda}V_{m}^{i}.

Using the definition of
S_{m}
and
T_{m}
, we deduce the desired inequality.
∎

Proposition 2.7
.

If
L
is ample, then for any linearly bounded filtration
\mathcal{F}
of
R
the following hold:

(1)

S(\mathcal{F})=\lim_{m\to\infty}S_{mr}(\mathcal{F})
and
T(\mathcal{F})=\lim_{m\to\infty}T_{mr}(\mathcal{F})
.

(2)

\mathrm{vol}(V_{\bullet}^{\mathcal{F},t})=\sum_{\dim X_{i}=\dim X}\mathrm{vol}(V_{\bullet}^{\mathcal{F}_{i},t})
for
t\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{T(\mathcal{F}_{1}),\ldots,T(\mathcal{F}_{s})\}
.

(3)

If
\mathcal{F}^{0}R=R
, then
S(\mathcal{F})=\frac{1}{\mathrm{vol}(V_{\bullet})}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{vol}(V_{\bullet}^{\mathcal{F},t})\,dt
and
S(\mathcal{F})\leq T(\mathcal{F})
.

(4)

If
\mathcal{F}^{0}R=R
and
X
is equidimensional, then

(n+1)^{-1}T(\mathcal{F})\leq S(\mathcal{F})\leq T(\mathcal{F}),

where
n=\dim X
and
c:=\min_{i}\mathrm{vol}(V_{\bullet}^{i})/\mathrm{vol}(L)
. In particular,
S(\mathcal{F})>0
when
T(\mathcal{F})>0
.

Proof.

By replacing
\mathbbm{k}
with
\overline{\mathbbm{k}}
and
X
with
X_{\overline{\mathbbm{k}}}
, we may assume that
\mathbbm{k}
is algebraically closed.
Thus the irreducible components of
X
are geometrically integral and we can and will apply Proposition
2.3
on them.
By Lemmas
2.5

and
2.6
,

S_{m}(\mathcal{F})=\sum_{i=1}^{s}\frac{\dim V_{m}^{i}}{\dim R_{m}}S_{m}(\mathcal{F}_{i})\quad\text{ and }\quad T_{m}(\mathcal{F})=\max_{i=1,\ldots,s}T_{m}(\mathcal{F}),

where
\mathcal{F}_{i}
is a linearly bounded filtration of
V_{\bullet}^{i}
, which is a graded linear series of
L_{i}
that contains an ample series.
By Proposition
2.3
.1,

T(\mathcal{F}_{i})=\lim_{m\to\infty}T_{mr}(\mathcal{F}_{i})

and

S(\mathcal{F}_{i})=\lim_{m\to\infty}S_{mr}(\mathcal{F}_{i})
.
For each
1\leq i\leq s
, let

c_{i}:=\limsup_{m\to\infty}\frac{\dim V_{mr}^{i}}{\dim R_{mr}}=\limsup_{m\to\infty}\frac{\dim V_{mr}^{i}/(mr)^{n}}{\dim R_{mr}/(mr)^{n}},

which is a limit by Proposition
2.2
.
Furthermore
c_{i}=\frac{\mathrm{vol}(V_{\bullet}^{i})}{\mathrm{vol}(L)}>0
when
\dim X_{i}=\dim X=n

T(v)=\sup_{m}T_{mr}(v)

Lemma 3.2
.

If
v\in\mathrm{Val}_{X}^{\circ}
, then

. Furthermore, if
(X,\Delta)
is klt, then there exists a constant

such that

for all

Using Proposition

, the lemma implies that

for all

Proof.

Fix a projective log resolution
\mu\colon Y\to X
of
(X,\Delta)
, and write
\Delta_{Y}
for the
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor on
Y
such that
K_{Y}+\Delta_{Y}=\mu^{*}(K_{X}+\Delta)
.
Fix a divisor
A
on
Y
that is ample over
C
.

We first claim that there exists a constant
c_{1}
such that

\mathrm{mult}_{y}(\mu^{*}D)\leq c_{1}

for all effective
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor

and

The following result is used in the above proof.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
Y\to C
be a projective dominant morphism with connected fibers from a regular scheme
Y
to a spectrum of a DVR
C
.
For any Cartier divisor
M
on
Y
, there exists a constant
c>0
such that

{\rm mult}_{F}(\Gamma)\leq c

for all
\mathbb{Q}
-divisors
0\leq\Gamma\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}M
with
Y_{0}\not\subset\mathrm{Supp}(\Gamma)
and irreducible component
F\subset Y_{0}
.

Proof.

By cutting by general hyperplane sections and applying Bertini theorem (see
[
34
, Theorem 10.1]
), we may reduce to the case when
Y
is regular of dimension two. Let
F_{1},\dots,F_{s}
be the irreducible components of
Y_{0}
.

Write
\Gamma=\Gamma_{0}+\Gamma_{1}
, where
\Gamma_{0}
is supported on
Y_{0}
and
\Gamma_{1}
does not contain any
F_{i}
in its support.
Write

Y_{0}=\sum_{i}a_{j}F_{j}
and
\Gamma_{0}=\sum_{j}b_{j}F_{j}
.
Let

d:=\max_{j}b_{j}/a_{j}

and
i\in\{1,\ldots,s\}
be an index on which the maximum is achieved.
By our choice of
d
,
\Gamma_{0}-dY_{0}\leq 0
and is supported on
\cup_{j\neq i}F_{i}
.
Since
\max_{j}\mathrm{mult}_{F_{j}}\Gamma_{0}\leq d\max_{j}\mathrm{mult}_{F_{j}}(Y_{0})
,
it suffices to find an upper bound for
d
.

To proceed, we use that
the intersection form on

\sum_{j\neq i}\mathbb{R}\cdot[F_{i}]
is negative definite
by Zariski’s Lemma
[
44
, Lemma 1.74]
.
Thus there exists a
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor
M_{i}
supported on
\cup_{j\neq i}F_{j}
such that
(M\cdot F_{j})=(M_{i}\cdot F_{j})

for all
j\neq i
.
Note that
\Gamma_{0}-dY_{0}\leq 0
and is supported on
\cup_{j\neq i}F_{i}
.
Since
\Gamma_{1}
does not contain any
F_{j}
in its support,

(M_{i}\cdot F_{j})=(M\cdot F_{j})=(\Gamma\cdot F_{j})\geq(\Gamma_{0}\cdot F_{j})=((\Gamma_{0}-dY_{0})\cdot F_{j})

for all
j\neq i
.
Thus
D:=M_{i}-(\Gamma_{0}-dY_{0})
satisfies

D\cdot F_{j}\geq 0\text{ for $j\neq i$}\quad\text{ and }\quad\mathrm{Supp}(D)\subset\cup_{j\neq i}F_{j}.

Using that the intersection form is negative definite, we deduce that

D\leq 0
.
Thus
\Gamma_{0}-dY_{0}\geq M_{i}
.
For
k\in\{1,\ldots,s\}
such that
{\rm mult}_{F_{k}}(\Gamma_{0})=0
,
we have

-d\cdot\mathrm{mult}_{F_{k}}(Y_{0})={\rm mult}_{F_{k}}(\Gamma_{0}-dY_{0})\geq{\rm mult}_{F_{k}}(M_{i}).

Thus

d\leq-{\rm mult}_{F_{k}}(M_{i})/\mathrm{mult}_{F_{k}}(Y_{0})\leq-\big(\min_{k}{\rm mult}_{F_{k}}(M_{i})\big)/\big(\min_{j}\mathrm{mult}_{F_{j}}(Y_{0})\big).

By taking the maximum of the last value over all
1\leq i\leq s
, we obtain an upper bound for
d
independent of the choice of
\Gamma
.
∎

Lemma 3.4
.

If
v\in\mathrm{Val}_{X}
with
T(v)<\infty
and
d
is a positive integer,
then

\mathcal{F}^{C(m+1)}_{v}\mathcal{R}_{m}\subseteq\mathfrak{m}^{d}\cdot\mathcal{R}_{m}

for all

, where

If

∎

3.1.4.
Basis type divisors

A
relative
m
-basis type divisor
of
L
is a
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor of the form

D:=\frac{1}{mN_{m}}\left(\{s_{1}=0\}+\cdots+\{s_{N_{m}}=0\}\right),

where
N_{m}={\rm rank}f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}(mL)
and
(s_{1},\ldots,s_{N_{m}})
is a basis for
\mathcal{R}_{m}
as a free
\mathcal{O}_{C}
-module.
Note that if
D
is a relative
m
-basis type divisor, then
D\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}L
and its restriction
D|_{X_{0}}
is an
m
-basis type divisor of
L_{0}
as in Section
2.2

We say that a relative
m
-basis type divisor
D:=\frac{1}{mN_{m}}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{m}}\{s_{i}=0\}
is
compatible
with a filtration
\mathcal{F}
of
\mathcal{R}
if the basis
(s_{1}|_{X_{0}},\ldots,s_{N_{m}}|_{X_{0}})
for
R_{m}
is compatible with
\mathcal{F}|_{X_{0}}
and

\mathrm{ord}_{\mathcal{F}}(s_{i})=\mathrm{ord}_{\mathcal{F}|_{X_{0}}}(s_{i}|_{X_{0}})

for each
1\leq i\leq N_{m}
.
We can always construct an
m
-basis type divisor compatible with a given filtration
\mathcal{F}
by fixing an
m
-basis type divisor
D_{0}
of
L_{0}
compatible with
\mathcal{F}|_{X_{0}}
and then lifting the basis to a basis for
\mathcal{R}_{m}
that satisfies the previous equation.
Additionally, we say that
D
is
compatible
with
v\in\mathrm{Val}_{X}
, if
D
is compatible with
\mathcal{F}_{v}
.

Lemma 3.9
.

For any
v\in\mathrm{Val}_{X}
and
m>0
divisible by
r
,

S_{m}(v)=\max\{v(D)\,|\,D\text{ is a relative $m$-basis type divisor of $L$}\}.

and the max is achieved when
D
is compatible with
v
.

Proof.

Similar to the proof in the absolute case,
we consider the jumping numbers

a_{m,j}:=\inf\{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\,|\,\mathrm{codim}(\mathcal{F}_{v}|_{X_{0}})^{\lambda}R_{m}\geq j\},

where
1\leq j\leq N_{m}:={\rm rank}f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}(mL)
.
Note that
S_{m}(v)=S_{m}(\mathcal{F}_{v}|_{X_{0}})=\frac{1}{mN_{m}}\sum_{j}a_{m,j}
.

Now, consider an
m
-basis type divisor

D=\tfrac{1}{mN_{m}}(\{s_{1}=0\}+\cdots\{s_{N_{m}}=0\})
of
L
.
By reordering the basis, we may assume that
v(s_{1})\leq\cdots\leq v(s_{N_{m}})
.
Since
(s_{1},\ldots,s_{N_{m}})
restricts to a basis of
R_{m}
and
s_{j}\in\mathcal{F}_{v}^{\lambda}\mathcal{R}_{m}
for
v(s_{j})\geq\lambda
,
we have
a_{m,j}\geq v(s_{j})
.
Thus

S_{m}(v)=\frac{1}{mN_{m}}\sum_{j=1}^{N_{m}}a_{m,j}\geq\frac{1}{mN_{m}}\sum_{j=1}^{N_{m}}v(s_{j})=v(D).

Next, assume that
D
is compatible with
v
.
Since
D_{0}
is compatible with
\mathcal{F}_{v}|_{X_{0}}
, after possibly reordering the basis,

a_{m,j}=\mathrm{ord}_{\mathcal{F}_{v}|_{X_{0}}}(s_{j}|_{X_{0}}).

Thus

v(s_{j})=\mathrm{ord}_{\mathcal{F}_{v}}(s_{j})=a_{m,j}

which implies that
v(D)=\tfrac{1}{mN_{m}}a_{m,j}=S_{m}(v)
. Therefore
S_{m}(v)
equals the desired maximum and the maximum is achieved when
D
is compatible with
v
.
∎

\mathcal{G}

Furthermore, since
(s_{1},\ldots,s_{N_{m}})
is compatible with
\mathcal{G}
, the basis restricts to a basis for
{\rm gr}_{G}R_{m}
. Using the isomorphism
{\rm gr}_{G}R_{m}\cong{\rm gr}_{G^{\prime}}R^{\prime}_{m}
, we see that
(s^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,s^{\prime}_{N_{m}})
restricts to a basis for

{\rm gr}_{G^{\prime}}R^{\prime}_{m}
with the restriction of
s^{\prime}_{i}
living in degree
-\lambda^{\prime}_{i}
.
Thus
\mathrm{ord}_{G^{\prime}}(s^{\prime}_{i})=-\lambda_{i}
and
(s^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,s^{\prime}_{N_{m}})
is a basis for
\mathcal{R}^{\prime}_{m}
.
Therefore
D^{\prime}
is an
m
-basis type divisor of
L
compatible with
G
.
Furthermore, since
D
and
D^{\prime}
agree over
X_{C\setminus 0}\cong X^{\prime}_{C\setminus 0}
and
X^{\prime}_{0}\not\subset\mathrm{Supp}(D^{\prime})
,
D^{\prime}
is the strict transform of
D
on
X^{\prime}
.
∎

3.1.5.
Stability thresholds

We now introduce the relative stability threshold.

Definition 3.11
.

The
relative stability threshold
of
(X,\Delta;L)\to C
is defined as

\delta(X,\Delta;L):=\limsup_{m\to\infty}\delta_{mr}(X,\Delta;L),

where

\delta_{m}(X,\Delta;L):=\inf\{\mathrm{lct}(X,\Delta;D)\,|\,\text{ $D$ is a relative $m$-basis type divisor of $L$}\}.

To simplify notation, we will often write
\delta(L)
(resp.,
\delta_{m}(L)
) for
\delta(X,\Delta;L)

(resp.
\delta_{m}(X,\Delta;L)
) when the pair
(X,\Delta)
is clear from context.

The next statement is an analogue of
[
7
, Theorem C]
in the relatively setting.

Proposition 3.12
.

The limsup in the definition of
\delta(X,\Delta;L)
is a limit.
In addition,

\delta_{m}(X,\Delta;L)=\inf_{v}\frac{A_{X,\Delta}(v)}{S_{m}(v)}\quad\text{ and }\quad\delta(X,\Delta;L)=\inf_{v}\frac{A_{X,\Delta}(v)}{S(v)},

where the above infima can be taken over all valuations
v
in
\mathrm{Val}_{X}^{\circ}
or
\mathrm{DivVal}_{X}
.

In the above expression, we use the convention that when
S(v)=0
, then
\tfrac{A_{X,\Delta}(v)}{S(v)}:=+\infty
.
By Lemma
3.5
, this only occurs when
X_{0}
is irreducible and
v=c\cdot\mathrm{ord}_{X_{0}}
for some
c\geq 0
. We use a similar convention when
S_{m}(v)=0
.

We say that
\delta(X,\Delta;L)
is
computed
by
v\in\mathrm{Val}_{X}^{\circ}
if
\delta(X,\Delta;L)=\frac{A_{X,\Delta}(v)}{S(v)}
.

Proof.

We follow the argument in
[
7
]
, which proves the absolute case.
The valuative characterization of the lct implies that

\delta_{m}(L)=\inf_{\text{$D$: $m$-basis type }}\left(\inf_{v}\frac{A_{X,\Delta}(v)}{v(D)}\right),

where the inner infimum runs through all valuations with finite discrepancy or divisorial valuations.
Switching the order of the infima and applying Lemma
3.9
implies the formula for
\delta_{m}(L)
.

Next, the formula for
\delta_{m}(L)
and fact that
\lim_{m\to\infty}S_{mr}(v)=S(v)
implies that

\limsup_{m\to\infty}\delta_{mr}(L)\leq\inf_{v}\frac{A_{X,\Delta}(v)}{S(v)}.

By Proposition
2.8
, given any
\varepsilon>0
there exists an integer
m_{0}(\varepsilon)
such that
S_{mr}(\mathcal{F})\leq(1+\varepsilon)S(\mathcal{F})
for all linearly bounded filtrations
\mathcal{F}
of
R
and
m\geq m_{0}(\varepsilon)
.
Using that
S(v):=S(\mathcal{F}_{v}|_{X_{0}})
and Lemma
3.2
, we deduce that

S_{mr}(v)\leq(1+\varepsilon)S(v)
for all
v\in\mathrm{Val}_{X}^{\circ}
and
m\geq m_{0}(\varepsilon)
.
Therefore

\liminf_{m\to\infty}\delta_{mr}(L)=\liminf_{m\to\infty}\inf_{v}\frac{A_{X,\Delta}(v)}{S_{mr}(v)}\geq(1+\varepsilon)^{-1}\inf_{v}\frac{A_{X,\Delta}(v)}{S(v)}.

Combining the previous two equations, we deduce that
\delta(L)
is a limit and has the desired valuative formula.
∎

Proposition 3.13
.

If
C^{\prime}\to C
be an étale morphism from the spectrum of a DVR, then the base change

(X^{\prime},\Delta^{\prime},L^{\prime}):=(X,\Delta,L)\times_{C}C^{\prime}

satisfies

. Moreover, equality holds when
(X,\Delta)
is klt and

has the same residue field as
\mathbbm{k}
.

Proof.

Since
(X,\Delta)
is lc and

is étale,
(X^{\prime},\Delta^{\prime})
is lc by
[
26
, 2.14]
.
By flat base change,

(3.3)

H^{0}(X^{\prime},mL^{\prime})\cong H^{0}(X,mL)\otimes_{A}A^{\prime}

for
m\in r\mathbb{N}
, where

A:=\mathcal{O}_{C}(C)
and
A^{\prime}:=\mathcal{O}_{C^{\prime}}(C^{\prime})
.
Therefore if
D
is a relative
m
-basis type divisor of
L
, then its pullback
D^{\prime}
to
X^{\prime}
is a relative
m
-basis type divisor of
L^{\prime}
and using
[

, 2.14]
satisfies

\mathrm{lct}(X^{\prime},\Delta^{\prime};D^{\prime})=\mathrm{lct}(X,\Delta;D)

Therefore
\delta(X^{\prime},\Delta^{\prime};L^{\prime})\leq\delta(X,\Delta;L)
.

For the equality case, if suffices to show that
\delta_{m}(X,\Delta;L)\leq\delta_{m}(X^{\prime},\Delta^{\prime};L^{\prime})
for all
m
when
C^{\prime}
is the completion of
C
at
0
. By (
3.3
), for any positive integer

and any basis

of

, we can find a basis

of

s^{\prime}_{i}-s_{i}\in\mathfrak{m}^{N}\cdot H^{0}(X^{\prime},mL^{\prime})

[

\delta_{m}(X,\Delta;L)\leq\mathrm{lct}(X,\Delta;D)\leq\mathrm{lct}(X^{\prime},\Delta;D^{\prime})+\frac{\mathrm{lct}(X^{\prime},\Delta^{\prime};\mathfrak{m})}{N},

where

∎

The following lemma was used in the previous proof.

Lemma 3.14
.

If
(X,\Delta)
is a klt pair,
\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{b}\subseteq\mathcal{O}_{X}
nonzero ideals on
X
, and

, then

\mathrm{lct}_{x}(X,\Delta;\mathfrak{a}+\mathfrak{b})\leq\mathrm{lct}_{x}(X,\Delta;\mathfrak{a})+\mathrm{lct}_{x}(X,\Delta;\mathfrak{b}).

Proof.

This follows from
[
38
]
*Theorem 0.1(2) as in the proof of
[
41
]
*Theorem 3.11. We include the details here for the reader’s convenience.
Let
a=\mathrm{lct}_{x}(X,\Delta;\mathfrak{a})
and
b=\mathrm{lct}_{x}(X,\Delta;\mathfrak{b})
.
It suffices to show that the multiplier ideal
\mathcal{J}(X,\Delta,(\mathfrak{a}+\mathfrak{b})^{c})
is nontrivial at the point
x\in X
for any

. By
[
38
]
*Theorem 0.1(2) or
[

]
(note that although the result in

For any

∎

Proposition 3.15
.

If
(X,\Delta)
is klt, then
\delta(X,\Delta;L)>0
.

Proof.

We have that

\delta(X,\Delta;L)=\inf_{v}\frac{A_{X,\Delta}(v)}{S(v)}\geq\inf_{v}\frac{A_{X,\Delta}(v)}{T(v)}>0,

where the first equality holds by Proposition
3.12
, the second holds as
S(v)\leq T(v)
, and the third holds by Lemma
3.2
.
∎

A case of primary interest is when
(X,\Delta+X_{0})
is plt, which implies that
(X,\Delta)\to C
is a locally KSBA stable family as in
[
27
, Definition 2.3]
).
In this case,
(X_{0},\Delta_{0})
is a normal klt pair by
[
25
, Proposition 5.51]
and adjunction.
The following lemma shows that in this case the relative stability threshold can be used to recover the stability threshold of the special fiber.

Lemma 3.16
.

If
(X,\Delta+X_{0})
is plt, then

\delta(X_{0},\Delta_{0};L_{0})=\delta(X,\Delta+X_{0};L)=\lim_{t\to 0+}\delta(X,\Delta+(1-t)X_{0};L).

Proof.

By inversion of adjunction,
\mathrm{lct}(X,\Delta+X_{0};D)=\mathrm{lct}(X_{0},\Delta_{0};D_{0})
for any relative
m
-basis type divisor
D
of
L
. Since any
m
-basis type divisor
D_{0}
of
L_{0}
can be lifted to a relative
m
-basis type divisor
D
of
L
, we see that for

\delta_{m}(X,\Delta+X_{0};L)=\inf_{D}\mathrm{lct}(X,\Delta+X_{0};D)=\inf_{D_{0}}\mathrm{lct}(X_{0},\Delta_{0};D_{0})=\delta_{m}(X_{0},\Delta_{0};L_{0})

when
m
is divisible by
r
.
Sending
m\to\infty
gives
\delta(X,\Delta+X_{0};L)=\delta(X_{0},\Delta_{0};L_{0})
, which is the first equality.

For the second equality, note that

\delta(X,\Delta+X_{0};L)\leq\delta(X,\Delta+(1-t)X_{0};L)

for
t\in[0,1]
.
Therefore it suffices to verify that
\limsup_{t\to 0^{+}}\delta(X,\Delta+(1-t)X_{0};L)
is at most
\delta(X_{0},\Delta_{0};L_{0})
.
For any
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
v\in\mathrm{Val}_{X}^{\circ}
with
\frac{A_{X,\Delta+X_{0}}(v)}{S(v)}<\delta(X,\Delta+X_{0};L)+\varepsilon
by Proposition
3.12
.
Now

\limsup_{t\to 0^{+}}\delta(X,\Delta+(1-t)X_{0};L)\leq\limsup_{t\to 0^{+}}\frac{A_{X,\Delta+(1-t_{0})X_{0}}(v)}{S(v)}\\
=\frac{A_{X,\Delta+X_{0}}(v)}{S(v)}<\delta(X,\Delta+X_{0};L)+\varepsilon\,

where the first inequality is by Proposition
3.12
.
Therefore the second equality holds.
∎

3.2.
Minimizer

In this section, we show that the relative stability threshold is computed by a quasi-monomial valuation. We keep the notation and assumptions from Setup
3.1.1
. We also assume that the residue field
\mathbbm{k}
is algebraically closed and uncountable.

Theorem 3.17
.

If
(X,\Delta)
is klt and
C
is the spectrum of a complete DVR, then there exists a quasi-monomial valuation
v\in\mathrm{Val}_{X}
that computes
\delta(X,\Delta;L)
.

When the DVR
\mathcal{O}_{C,0}
is not complete, the relative stability threshold may not be computed by any valuation (see Remark
3.23
), but under mild assumptions such valuations still exist.

Theorem 3.18
.

If
(X,\Delta)
is klt and
\delta(X,\Delta;L)<\delta(X_{K},\Delta_{K};L_{K})
, where
K=\mathrm{Frac}(\widehat{\mathcal{O}_{C,0})}
and

(X_{K},\Delta_{K};L_{K})=(X,\Delta;L)\times_{C}\mathrm{Spec}~K,

then there exists a quasi-monomial valuation
v\in\mathrm{Val}_{X}
that computes
\delta(X,\Delta;L)
.
Moreover, the center of any such valuation
v
is contained in
X_{0}
.

As in
[
7
]
, the proof of Theorem
3.17
relies heavily on generic limit constructions. We first discuss some general properties of this construction.

3.2.1.
Generic limit

In this Section
3.2.1
, we assume that
C
is the spectrum of a complete DVR. By Cohen structure theorem, we have
\mathcal{O}_{C,0}\cong\mathbbm{k}[\![t]\!]
. We fix an isomorphism as such.

Let
\mathcal{F}_{k}
(
k=1,2,\cdots
) be a sequence of
\mathbb{N}
-filtrations of
\mathcal{R}
. By restriction, they induce
\mathbb{N}
-filtrations (still denoted by
\mathcal{F}_{k}
) of

\widetilde{R}_{m}:=\mathcal{R}/(\mathfrak{m}^{m}\mathcal{R}+\mathcal{R}_{>m})\cong\oplus_{l\leq m}\mathcal{R}_{l}/\mathfrak{m}^{m}\mathcal{R}_{l}

for all
m\in r\mathbb{N}
. More precisely,

\mathcal{F}_{k}^{\lambda}\widetilde{R}_{m}={\rm Image}(\oplus_{l\leq m}\mathcal{F}_{k}^{\lambda}\mathcal{R}_{l}\to\oplus_{l\leq m}\mathcal{R}_{l}\to\widetilde{R}_{m})\,.

For each
m\in r\mathbb{N}
and
C\in\mathbb{N}
, the
\mathbb{N}
-filtrations of
\widetilde{R}_{m}
with
\mathcal{F}^{C}\widetilde{R}_{m}=0
are parameterized by a scheme
H_{m,C}
that is a closed subscheme of a nested Hilbert scheme of finite type over
\mathbbm{k}
, since each filtration
\mathcal{F}
yields a sequence of ideals

0=\mathcal{F}^{C}\widetilde{R}_{m}\subseteq\mathcal{F}^{C-1}\widetilde{R}_{m}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq\mathcal{F}^{0}\widetilde{R}_{m}=\widetilde{R}_{m}\,,

and
\widetilde{R}_{m}
is a finite
\mathbbm{k}
-algebra. If
C^{\prime}\geq C
then
H_{m,C}\subseteq H_{m,C^{\prime}}
is a union of connected components. Thus
\mathbb{N}
-filtrations of
\widetilde{R}_{m}
can be parameterized by a scheme
H_{m}:=\cup_{C}H_{m,C}
whose connected components are of finite type over
\mathbbm{k}
.

Assume now that the filtrations
\mathcal{F}_{k}
of
\widetilde{R}_{m}
are uniformly bounded, in the sense that for each
m\in r\mathbb{N}
there exists some constant
C_{m}
such that
\mathcal{F}^{C_{m}}_{k}\widetilde{R}_{m}=0
for all
k
. Then the filtrations
\mathcal{F}_{k}
correspond to points
z_{k,m}
of
H_{m,C_{m}}
, and thus it makes sense to take their Zariski closure in
H_{m}
which gives a scheme of finite type.

We have natural truncation maps
H_{m+r}\to H_{m}
induced by the restriction of filtrations. By the same argument as
[
7
]
*Proposition 6.7, there exists a sequence of infinite subsets

\mathbb{N}\supset I_{1}\supset I_{2}\supset\cdots

such that
Z_{m}:=\overline{\bigcup_{k\in I_{m/r}}\{z_{k,m}\}}\subset H_{m}
satisfies the condition that any closed subset
Y\subsetneq Z_{m}
contains finitely many
z_{k,m}
.
In particular, there exists dominant natural morphisms
Z_{m+r}\to Z_{m}
as well. By the uncountability assumption of the field
\mathbbm{k}
and the same argument as
[
7
]
*Lemma 6.6, for any sequence of non-empty open sets
U_{m}\subset Z_{m}
, we can pick a compatible sequence of closed points
z_{m}\in U_{m}
such that
z_{m+r}
maps to
z_{m}
under the truncation map.
Since each point
z_{m}
corresponds to a filtration of
\widetilde{R}_{m}
, and since
\mathcal{O}_{C,0}
is complete, the entire sequence
\{z_{m}\}_{m\in r\mathbb{N}}
determines a filtration
\mathcal{F}
of
\mathcal{R}
. We call
\mathcal{F}
a
generic limit
of the
\mathcal{F}_{k}
.

We shall analyze the behavior of several invariants of filtrations under the generic limit construction. Let
\mathfrak{b}_{m,\lambda}(\mathcal{F})
be the base ideal of
\mathcal{F}^{\lambda}\mathcal{R}_{m}
, i.e.

\mathfrak{b}_{m,\lambda}(\mathcal{F}):={\rm Image}(\mathcal{F}^{\lambda}\mathcal{R}_{m}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}\to\mathcal{O}_{X}(mL))\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(-mL)\subseteq\mathcal{O}_{X}\,.

Then
\mathfrak{b}_{m,\lambda}(\mathcal{F})\subseteq\mathfrak{b}_{m+m_{0},\lambda}(\mathcal{F})
for any integer
m_{0}\in r\mathbb{N}
such that
m_{0}L
is base point free. As
L
is ample, this implies that for any fixed
\lambda
the sequence
\mathfrak{b}_{m,\lambda}(\mathcal{F})
of ideals stabilizes when
m\in r\mathbb{N}
is sufficiently large. We can thus set

(3.4)

\mathfrak{b}_{\lambda}(\mathcal{F}):=\mathfrak{b}_{mr,\lambda}(\mathcal{F})

for
m\gg 0
. Then
\{\mathfrak{b}_{\lambda}(\mathcal{F})\}_{\lambda\in\mathbb{N}}
form a multiplicative graded sequence of ideals on
X
. The first property we need is that the log canonical threshold of base ideals does not increase under generic limit.

Lemma 3.19
.

With the above setup, we have

\mathrm{lct}(X,\Delta;\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet}(\mathcal{F}))\leq\limsup_{k\to\infty}\mathrm{lct}(X,\Delta;\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet}(\mathcal{F}_{k})).

Proof.

Fix integers
m\geq N\geq 0
both divisible by
r
and
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
.
By abuse of notation, we denote the ideal
\mathfrak{m}_{C,0}\cdot\mathcal{O}_{X}
also by
\mathfrak{m}
.
From the generic limit construction above, there is an ideal
\mathcal{B}_{m,\lambda}\subset\mathcal{O}_{X\times H_{m}}
, which we view as a family of ideals on
X
parametrized by
H_{m}
,
with the property that

\mathcal{B}_{m,\lambda}|_{X\times\{z_{k,N}\}}
and
\mathcal{B}_{m,\lambda}|_{X\times\{z_{N}\}}

\mathfrak{b}_{m,\lambda}(\mathcal{F}_{k})+\mathfrak{m}^{N}

and
\mathfrak{b}_{m,\lambda}(\mathcal{F})+\mathfrak{m}^{N}

The function

Therefore

\mathrm{lct}(\mathfrak{b}_{m,\lambda}(\mathcal{F}))\leq\mathrm{lct}(\mathfrak{b}_{m,\lambda}(\mathcal{F})+\mathfrak{m}^{N})\\
\leq\lim_{I_{N/r}\ni k\to\infty}\mathrm{lct}(\mathfrak{b}_{m,\lambda}(\mathcal{F}_{k})+\mathfrak{m}^{N})\leq\limsup_{k\to\infty}\mathrm{lct}(\mathfrak{b}_{m,\lambda}(\mathcal{F}_{k})+\mathfrak{m}^{N}),

\mathrm{lct}(\mathfrak{b}_{m,\lambda}(\mathcal{F}))\leq\limsup_{k\to\infty}\mathrm{lct}(\mathfrak{b}_{m,\lambda}(\mathcal{F}_{k})).

\mathrm{lct}(\mathfrak{b}_{\lambda}(\mathcal{F}))=\mathrm{lct}(\mathfrak{b}_{m,\lambda}(\mathcal{F}))\leq\limsup_{k\to\infty}\mathrm{lct}(\mathfrak{b}_{m,\lambda}(\mathcal{F}_{k}))\leq\limsup_{k\to\infty}\mathrm{lct}(\mathfrak{b}_{\lambda}(\mathcal{F}_{k})),

where the last equality uses that
\mathfrak{b}_{m,\lambda}(\mathcal{F}_{k})\subset\mathfrak{b}_{\lambda}(\mathcal{F}_{k})
.
By multiplying by
\lambda
and sending

∎

\mathcal{F}

Lemma 3.20

(

If

4.
Divisorial minimizer

In this section we prove Theorem
4.1
on the existence of divisorial minimizers of the relative stability threshold of a log Fano pair over a DVR.

4.1.
Statement of main result

Let
(X,\Delta)\to C
be a relative log Fano pair over the spectrum of a DVR
C
such that
X\to C
has connected and geometrically connected and reduced fibers.
We set

\delta(X,\Delta):=\delta(X,\Delta;-K_{X}-\Delta),

where we are viewing
L:=-K_{X}-\Delta
as
\mathbb{Q}
-line bundle and fixing
r>0
such that
-r(K_{X}+\Delta)
is a Cartier divisor.
In addition, for
v\in\mathrm{Val}_{X}
, the invariants
S(v)
and
T(v)
denote the
S
and
T
invariants for
v
with respect to
L
.
The above values are independent of the choice of
r
by Proposition
2.7
.1 and Proposition
3.12
.

The goal of this section is to prove the following enhancement of Theorem
3.18
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
f\colon(X,\Delta)\to C
be a log Fano pair over the spectrum of a DVR with algebraically closed residue field.
If
X_{0}
is integral,
X_{0}\subseteq\mathrm{Supp}(\Delta)
, and

\delta(X,\Delta)<\min\{1,\delta(X_{\eta},\Delta_{\eta})\}\ \ \ \mbox{for }\eta={\rm Spec}\,K(C),

then there exists a divisorial valuation
v\in\mathrm{Val}_{X}
with
C_{X}(v)\subsetneq X_{0}
that computes
\delta(X,\Delta)
.

The key point here is that the minimizer can be made divisorial. This is a relative version of the optimal destabilization theorem
[
33
, Theorem 1.2]
. In the absolute case, one shows minimizing valuations induce finitely generated filtrations on the section ring, and this implies that any small perturbation of the minimizer is still a minimizer. Hence we get a divisorial minimizer by choosing a rational perturbation. To deduce the finite generation, we show that any quasi-monomial minimizer of the stability threshold is a monomial log canonical place of a special complement (to be defined below), and then show that this geometric property yields finite generation even for valuations with higher rational rank (than one). We follow a similar strategy in our relative setting, though each step requires some extra work. In particular, to construct a special complement such that the minimizer is an lc place is tricky. Once we show a minimizer is a monomial log canonical place of a special complement
in the relative setting, the finite generation can be deduced from
[
42
]
using a cone construction.

4.2.
Special complements

In this subsection, we show that the quasi-monomial minimizers are monomial lc places of special
\mathbb{Q}
-complements.

Definition 4.2

(
cf.

[
42
, Definition 3.1]
)

A

\mathbb{Q}
-complement
of
(X,\Delta)
with respect to a log smooth model

is a
\mathbb{Q}
-complement
\Gamma
such that

for some ample effective
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor
G
on
Y
whose support does not contain any stratum of

. Any valuation

Since

along

.
Since

is the base change of

via the extension of fields

,

is normal and there exists a unique prime exceptional divisor

on

whose base change via

is

.
Let

denote closure of

in

.

We now construct an open set of

over which

.
Since

is normal, there exists a non-empty open set

are normal.
Since

is the unique exceptional divisor of

,

is integral and the unique exceptional divisor of

for all

.
By shrinking

, we may assume that

for all

.

We now show that

for all
z\in U
.
Fix a point

. By constructing a divisor over

with center

, we may construct a DVR

with fraction field

and dominant morphism

is an equality.
For a
\mathbbm{k}
-algebra

and

For
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}

\mathcal{G}_{K}^{\lambda}R_{m}^{K}:=\mathrm{im}(\mathcal{F}_{E_{K}}^{\lambda}\mathcal{R}_{m}^{K}\to R_{m}^{K})

by free

R_{m}^{B}=\mathcal{G}^{0}R_{m}^{B}\supset\mathcal{G}^{1}R_{m}^{B}\supset\cdots

\otimes_{B}K
.
Now choose a

-module basis

\mathcal{G}^{\lambda}R_{m}^{B}

Next, lift
(s_{1},\ldots,s_{N_{m}})
to elements

of
\mathcal{R}_{m}^{B}
such that
\mathrm{ord}_{E_{K}}(\tilde{s}_{i})=\mathrm{ord}_{\mathcal{G}}(s_{i})

Using that that

where the second equality and the last inequality hold by Lemma
3.9

By sending

Now observe that for

\delta(X,\Delta)\leq\frac{A_{X,\Delta}(E_{z})}{S(E_{z})}\leq\frac{A_{X_{K(Z)},\Delta_{K(Z)}}(E_{K(Z)})}{S(E_{K(Z)})}\leq\frac{A_{X^{\prime},\Delta^{\prime}}(E^{\prime})}{S(E^{\prime})}=\delta(X^{\prime},\Delta^{\prime}).

Step 3: We will now prove the theorem in full generality.

Let
\widehat{C}:=\mathrm{Spec}(\widehat{\mathcal{O}_{C,0}})

\hat{0}
and
\hat{\eta}

By combining Propositions

and

Since

. ∎

4.5.
Families of log Fano pairs

We now apply Theorem

to families of log Fano pairs over a DVR.

Corollary 4.10
.

If
(X,B)\to C
is a family of log Fano pairs over the spectrum of a DVR with

\delta(X_{0},B_{0})<\min\{\delta(X_{\eta},B_{\eta}),1\},

then there exists
\varepsilon>0
such that for any

the following holds:

(1)

\delta(X_{0},B_{0})<\delta(X,B+(1-t)X_{0})<1
;

(2)

there exist a divisorial valuation
v=\mathrm{ord}_{E}
with
C_{X}(v)\subsetneq X_{0}
computing

\delta(X,B+(1-t)X_{0});

and
v
is an lc place of a
\mathbb{Q}
-complement
B^{+}
of
(X,B)
with
B^{+}\geq X_{0}
.

In the above statement, note that
\delta(X,B+(1-t)X_{0})
denotes the stability threshold of the relative log Fano pair
(X,B+(1-t)X_{0})\to C
.

Proof.

By Lemma
3.16
and our assumption on
\delta(X_{0},B_{0})
, there exists
\varepsilon>0
such that

\delta(X,B+(1-t)X_{0})<\min\{\delta(X_{\eta},B_{\eta}),1\}

for all
t\in(0,\varepsilon)\cap\mathbb{Q}
.
For such
t
, Proposition
2.17
implies that
(X,B+(1-t)X_{0})
satisfies the assumptions of Theorem
4.1
.
Thus there exists a divisorial valuation
v=\mathrm{ord}_{E}
such that

C_{X}(v)\subsetneq X_{0}
and
v
computes
\delta(X,B+(1-t)X_{0})
.
Observe that

\delta(X_{0},B_{0})=\delta(X,B+X_{0})\leq\frac{A_{X,B+X_{0}}(v)}{S(v)}<\frac{A_{X,B+(1-t)X_{0}}(v)}{S(v)}\\
=\delta(X,B+(1-t)X_{0})<1\,,

where the first relation holds by Lemma
3.16
and the third uses that
C_{X}(v)\subseteq X_{0}
.
Therefore condition (1) holds.

To verify (2) holds, it remains to construct the complement
B^{+}
.
By Corollary
4.7
,
E
is an lc place of
(X,B^{\prime+})
of a
\mathbb{Q}
-complement
B^{\prime+}\geq B+(1-t)X_{0}
.
We seek to replace
B^{\prime+}
with a new complement so that
B^{+}\geq X_{0}
.
By
[
5
, Corollary 1.4.3]
, there exists a proper birational morphism
g:Z\to X
such that
Z
is normal and
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial and
E\subset Z
is the sole exceptional divisor.
By Lemma
2.1
,
Z
is Fano type over
C
.
Since

(Z,g_{*}^{-1}(B+(1-t)X_{0})+E)

admits a
\mathbb{Q}
-complement given by
g_{*}^{-1}(B^{\prime+})+E
,
Lemma
4.5
implies that, assuming
\varepsilon
was chosen sufficiently small,
there exists a
\mathbb{Q}
-complement

of

(Z,g_{*}^{-1}(B+X_{0})+E).

Now
B^{+}:=g_{*}B^{+}_{Z}
is a
\mathbb{Q}
-complement of
(X,B)
with lc place along
E
and
B^{+}\geq X_{0}
.
∎

5.
Log Fano family induced by minimizer

In this section, we will complete the proof of Theorem
1.2
.
In light of Corollary

5.1.
Setup and main result

Throughout this section, we fix the following setup.

Setup 5.1

Let

Remark 5.2

This setup arises naturally in the study of families of log Fano pairs over DVRs; see Corollary

As well, condition (2) will be needed in Lemma
3.10

Theorem 5.3

\overline{C}
is the spectrum of a DVR with closed point

5.2.

5.3.

Proposition 5.6
.

The following assertions hold:

(1)

In contrast,

Proof.

We first analyze the fibers over

.
The divisor
\overline{X}_{0}

.
Since the ramification index of

Lemma 5.11

For each integer

First, we verify that

(X^{2},B^{2})\to C^{2}:=\mathrm{Spec}(R^{2})
satisfying the claim.

Continuing in this way, we obtain a sequence of extension of DVRs
R^{i-1}\subset R^{i}
and extensions of
(X_{K},B_{K})\times_{K}K^{i}
to a family of log Fano pairs

(X^{i},B^{i})\to\mathrm{Spec}(R^{i})

satisfying

\delta(X^{i-1}_{0},B^{i-1}_{0})<\delta(X^{i}_{0},B^{i}_{0})\quad\text{ and }\quad\min\{1,\delta(X^{i}_{0},B^{i}_{0})\}\leq\min\{1,\delta(X^{i}_{K^{i}},B^{i}_{K^{i}})\},

This process terminates if the last inequality ever fails to be strict.

We claim that the process terminates in finitely many steps.
Let
n:=\dim X_{K}
,
v:=(-K_{X_{K}}-\Delta_{K})^{n}
,
\delta:=\delta(X^{1}_{0},\Delta^{1}_{0})
, and
N
a positive integer such that
N\Delta_{K}
is integral.
Let
\mathcal{T}
be the set of log Fano pairs
(Y,G)

such that
n=\dim Y
,
v=(-K_{Y}-G)^{n}
,
\delta(Y,G)\geq\delta
, and
NG
is integral.
Since
\mathcal{T}
is a bounded family of pairs by
[
22
,
41
]
and the function
\min\{1,\delta(\,\cdot\,)\}
is constructible in families of log Fano pairs
[
9
]
, the set

\{\min\{1,\delta(Y,G)\}\,|\,(Y,G)\in\mathcal{T}\}

is finite.
Since each
(X_{0}^{i},B_{0}^{i})
is in
\mathcal{T}
, we conclude that the process must terminate.
Therefore, for some positive integer
s
, we must have

\min\{1,\delta(X^{s}_{0},B^{s}_{0})\}=\min\{1,\delta(X^{s}_{K^{s}},B^{s}_{K^{s}})\}.

as desired.
Furthermore, if
(X_{K},B_{K})
is K-semistable, then

1=\min\{1,\delta(X_{K},B_{K})\}=\min\{1,\delta(X^{s}_{K^{s}},B^{s}_{K^{s}})\}=\min\{1,\delta(X^{s}_{0},B^{s}_{0})\},

which the second equality is Proposition
2.17
.
Therefore
\delta(X^{s}_{0},B^{s}_{0})\geq 1
and the fibers of
(X^{s},\Delta^{s})\to C^{s}
are K-semistable.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

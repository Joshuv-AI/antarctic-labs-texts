Recognizing Early Career Translational Investigators
Authors: Matthias Nahrendorf
Published: 2026

JACC: Basic to Translational Science is committed to supporting and promoting early career investigators who are pursuing translational science. To achieve this, the Journal will provide biographical information and a photograph of the first or senior authors who are early career investigators in the issue in which their article appears. In addition, this biographical information will be shared on our social media platforms at the time of publication.

We are proud to recognize and showcase the work of the following early career investigators, whose research is featured in this issue of the Journal.

Miao Yu, MD

Paper: Single-Cell Transcriptomics Reveals the Immune Pathogenesis of Isolated Coronary Arteritis Presenting as Refractory In-Stent Restenosis

Miao Yu, MD, is a cardiovascular researcher specializing in recurrent in-stent restenosis and inflammation-related cardiovascular diseases. She received her medical training at Peking Union Medical College and is currently pursuing doctoral training in internal medicine at Peking University. Her research focuses on integrating clinical cohorts, single-cell transcriptomics, and experimental models to elucidate mechanisms underlying coronary inflammation and develop targeted therapeutic strategies. She has been involved in prospective randomized clinical trials and translational studies investigating anti-inflammatory therapies for recurrent in-stent restenosis.

Felicia H.K. Håkansson, MD

Paper: A Genome-Wide Association Study of Takotsubo Syndrome

Felicia H.K. Håkansson, MD, is a consultant in cardiology and internal medicine at Karolinska University Hospital, with a subspecialization in heart failure and cardiac imaging. She is currently a PhD candidate at Karolinska Institutet (where she also received her MD in 2009). She will defend her thesis in September 2026 with Per Tornvall, MD, PhD, as her main supervisor. The focus of her research is different aspects of Takotsubo syndrome and myocardial infarction with nonobstructive coronary artery disease, including genetic, registry-based, and machine learning studies.

Samuel C.R. Sherratt, PhD

Paper: Rates of Lipoprotein(a) Oxidation Accelerate at Elevated Levels and Attenuate With Atorvastatin Active Metabolites Compared With Other Statins

Samuel C.R. Sherratt, PhD, is the Scientific Director at Elucida Research and a faculty member in the Cardiology Department at the Mount Sinai Fuster Heart Hospital. He received his PhD in biochemistry from the University of New Hampshire with a thesis focused on elucidating mechanisms of action of eicosapentaenoic acid in models of atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease. His research is focused on biochemical and biophysical mechanisms of atherosclerosis progression and disease prevention, including using x-ray diffraction to differentiate between omega-3 fatty acids and their effects on membrane fluidity and structure. Most recently, his work has centered on proatherogenic mechanisms of lipoprotein(a) and therapeutic approaches to interrupt deleterious processes beyond changes in its levels.

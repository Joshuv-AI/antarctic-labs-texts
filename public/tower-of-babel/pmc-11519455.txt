A comparative ensemble approach to bedload prediction using metaheuristic machine learning
Authors: Ajaz Ahmad Mir, Mahesh Patel, Fahad Albalawi, Mohit Bajaj, Milkias Berhanu Tuka
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractAn adequate bedload prediction is a challenging task in hydraulic engineering because of the complex sediment transport processes and corresponding environmental factors. The current study introduces a novel comparative ensemble approach using metaheuristic machine learning (ML) models to enhance the accuracy of bedload prediction using data from laboratory flume experiments. To uncover key insights in bedload transport prediction, several models are employed, such as K-Nearest Neighbours (KNN), Extra Trees Regressor (ETR), Linear Regression (LR), Random Forest (RF), Bagging Regressor (BR), and XGBoost (XGB). The coefficient of determination (R2) and root mean square error (RMSE) values vary between various models; however, XGB showed R2 = 0.99 and RMSE = 0.11. The sensitivity analysis emphasizes the crucial role of the Shields parameter in bedload prediction, while the SHAP analysis highlights the substantial influence of the XGB model in enhancing predictive accuracy. The REC curves show that BR, XGB, and RF, outperformed KNN and LR models. Furthermore, a graphical user interface has also been developed to facilitate user interaction with the predictive models, allowing for easier visualization, analysis, and interpretation of bedload transport predictions. Additionally, k-fold cross-validation was performed to assess the performance, consistency and robustness of the ML models. Thus, it can be concluded from the current study that the utilization of ML algorithms can improve accuracy, providing valuable insights for hydraulic engineers and highlighting the importance of ML models in civil engineering practices, particularly in bedload transport prediction.Keywords: Bedload transport, Prediction, Alluvial channels, Machine learningSubject terms: Civil engineering, Mathematics and computing, Applied mathematics, Computer science

Introduction

Sediment transport is the process by which solid particles, including soil, sand, gravel, and organic materials are mobilized from one location to adjacent section by natural forces such as flow1,2. This dynamic process plays a critical role in shaping landscapes, influencing river morphology, and affecting aquatic ecosystems3. Moreover, sediment transport in rivers can be categorized into four main types: bedload, suspended load, dissolved load, and total load. Bedload transport involves the movement of larger, coarser particles such as gravel and sand along the riverbed, typically through rolling, sliding, or hopping due to the force of flowing water4. This type of transport is crucial in shaping riverbeds and influencing channel morphology. Suspended load transport consists of finer particles, such as silt and clay, carried within the water column by turbulent flow5. These particles remain suspended as long as the flow velocity is sufficient to keep them from settling, and they can be transported over long distances, often contributing to the turbidity of the water. In addition, dissolved load transport involves soluble materials such as salts and minerals carried in solution within the water6. Total load transport represents the sum of bedload, suspended load, and dissolved load, encompassing all the sediment and materials transported by the river7. Therefore, predicting different types of sediment transport is essential for analysing river dynamics, managing sediment, and preserving riverine ecosystems.

Bedload transport, particularly of coarser sediments, has been identified as a critical factor in the morphological evolution of gravel-bed rivers. Therefore, accurately determining bedload transport rates is paramount to researchers in river engineering and fluvial geomorphology to contribute to effective river management8 and understand the dynamics of landscape evolution9. Field measurements of bedload transport pose significant challenges, especially during floods, resulting in costly and uncertain rate estimates10.

Laboratory flume experiments are often utilized due to their ability to control boundary conditions and facilitate precise measurements. These experiments are based on formulae developed under different flow and bed conditions11–14. Experimental studies face challenges due to their high cost, time-consuming nature, and the need for simplifications to replicate the complexity of natural gravel bed rivers, such as relying on equilibrium sediment transport conditions and steady uniform flows. In addition, scaling flow and sediment properties from natural systems present inherent challenges in experimental setups. Furthermore, these experiments are limited in reproducing the full magnitude of transport, contributing to the notable prediction uncertainty associated with bedload transport equations derived from flume studies15.

Various approaches, including empirical, mathematical, and numerical methods, have been developed to predict bedload transport, each with its own limitations16. Constructing, calibrating, and validating these models demand extensive data, particularly for process-based models. Recently, artificial intelligence algorithms, utilizing machine learning and data mining techniques, have provided new insights across multiple scientific fields, notably in water resources, geosciences, and other civil engineering disciplines17–21. These advanced algorithms aim to identify the optimal relationships between inputs (significant conditioning factors) and outputs (target parameters) and function primarily as black-box, non-linear, statistical models22. The intricacies of phenomena within a catchment, such as sediment transport, are often highly complex and defy simple prediction23. Consequently, in many scenarios, the model must be versatile, flexible, and have a non-linear modelling structure to capture and represent these complexities effectively. However, the ML algorithms utilized in the present scenario are emerging as a suitable solution to meet these requirements, providing valuable tools for understanding and predicting complex processes in water resources and geosciences.

Researchers have extensively explored the application of standalone and hybrid algorithms in sediment transport. For example, in a study by Ebtehaj et al.24, a hybrid model combining a Feed-Forward Neural Network and an Extreme Learning Machine (FFNN-ELM) was applied to open-channel sediment transport. The results showed that this hybrid model outperformed genetic programming (GP) and empirical sediment transport models. Furthermore, the use of wavelet Support Vector Machine (SVM-Wavelet) algorithms was investigated, and these algorithms were found to have better predictive performance than traditional SVM models and existing empirical Equations18–20.

In recent studies, various data mining algorithms such as Random Forest (RF), Logistic Model Tree (LMT), and Naïve Bayes Trees (NBT) have been successfully applied in flood vulnerability mapping25 and groundwater vulnerability assessment26. These algorithms have demonstrated high performance, accuracy and reliability in spatial mapping applications in these fields. However, their application for prediction and forecasting remains underexplored, particularly in the geosciences. Limited examples include the use of RF and Random Tree (RT) for solar radiation prediction27 and the application of Reduced Error Pruning Tree (REPT), Instance-Based K-nearest Neighbours (IBK) and M5P model tree techniques for suspended sediment load prediction26.

Recent research has demonstrated the effectiveness of soft computing techniques in improving predictive accuracy in civil engineering28–30. A study by Zakaria et al.31 utilized Gene-Expression Programming (GEP) to estimate bed material load in Malaysian rivers, achieving superior performance over traditional methods with an R² of 0.97. Similarly, Azamathulla et al.32 applied an Adaptive Neuro-Fuzzy Inference System (ANFIS) to predict bed load in moderately sized rivers, outperforming conventional equations by more accurately predicting bedload data from rivers such as Kinta, Pari, and Langat. Also, Mendhe et al.33 explored ML models such as RF combined with meta-heuristic optimization for predicting the compressive strength of ultra-high-performance concrete, with the hybrid Snake Enhancement-Random Forest (SO-RF) model achieving an R² of 0.9147. These studies underscore the growing role of Artificial Intelligence and soft computing in enhancing predictive capabilities across various engineering applications.

Bedload has been estimated in previous studies using various ML algorithms27,34–36. However, the novelty of the present study is the use of a comparative approach to predict bedload using different metaheuristic ML models and important parameters. Therefore, the present study aims to incorporate various ML models such as k-Nearest Neighbours (KNN), Extra Trees Regressor (ETR), Linear Regression (LR), Random Forest (RF), Bagging Regressor (BR) and XGBoost (XGB) to predict the output parameter. The study focuses on thirteen key input parameters such as channel width (W), sediment diameter (D), energy slope (So), flow discharge (Q), mean flow velocity (U), relative depth (R/D), flow depth (H), ratio of flow depth to channel width (H/W), Froude number (Fr), Reynolds number (Re), bedload (Φ) and Shields parameter (θ), as none of the previous studies included these important features in predicting bedload transport rates. Together, these parameters contribute to the complexity of bedload prediction and require a sophisticated approach for accurate modelling. The ensemble nature of the approach allows for a comprehensive evaluation, exploiting the strengths of individual models to improve prediction accuracy. It can be inferred that bedload prediction is a crucial aspect of hydraulic engineering, with applications in various fields such as river management, sediment transport modelling, and infrastructure design2. Traditional methods for predicting bedload transport often rely on empirical equations or physical models, which may have limitations in accurately capturing the complex dynamics of sediment movement in natural watercourses. In recent years, there has been growing interest in leveraging ML techniques for bedload prediction due to their ability to handle nonlinear relationships and complex data patterns.

Methodology

The selected ML techniques are chosen for the proven ability to model the nonlinear relationships inherent in sediment transport processes. In the present study, the models utilized are k-Nearest Neighbours (KNN), Extra Trees Regressor (ETR), Linear Regression (LR), Random Forest (RF), Bagging Regressor (BR), and XGBoost (XGB). For instance, XGB and RF, as ensemble methods, are renowned for the higher accuracy and capacity to manage overfitting, making them particularly well-suited for datasets with complex, multidimensional features. The model KNN is selected for its simplicity and effectiveness in situations with limited data, allowing for efficient pattern recognition without relying on a fixed functional form. In addition to this, ETR model is chosen for its robustness and efficiency in capturing intricate patterns through randomized tree structures. The LR and BR are included to provide baseline comparisons and to integrate probabilistic approaches that offer valuable insights into the uncertainties associated with predictions. These methods are favoured over other potential approaches due to the flexibility in model tuning, enhanced accuracy in capturing subtle variations in bedload transport, and superior performance with noisy or incomplete data.

Shapley additive explanation (SHAP) analysis

The significance of the effect of the input factor on the predictions is highlighted by Shapley Additive Explanation (SHAP). The evaluation of a model’s predictions, regardless of the specific feature, is demonstrated by the use of SHAP values, thereby highlighting their importance. However, this process must be carried out in any possible order to ensure that the features are evenly balanced, as the order in which a model encounters features can influence its predictions. Figure 5 shows the results of the SHAP analysis of sediment transport. It can be seen that the Shields parameter has a positive effect on the prediction of bedload. The value is much higher than the other parameters, almost equal to 80. On the other hand, R/D has a value almost equal to half of the Shields parameter. Sensitivity analysis

Sensitivity analysis assesses the effect on model outputs of changing a single input separately across the range of possible values, while maintaining the nominal or base case values of all other inputs2,29. The model’s sensitivity to that particular input variable is defined as the change in the model output due to a change in the input variable. A positive or negative percentage change from the nominal response can also illustrate sensitivity. Figure 9 shows the sensitivity analysis of the ML models for bedload transport. It can be noted that all parameters are critical to the prediction of bedload transport in the alluvial channels as represented by each model. Comparison with the previous study

In the study by Khosravi et al.25, various data mining techniques, including standalone models like M5P, RT, RF, and REPT and their Bagging (BA) hybrid versions, were evaluated for predicting bedload transport. The study found that the BA-M5P model provided the highest predictive power with an R² of 0.943, RMSE of 0.061 kg m⁻¹ s⁻¹, MAE of 0.040 kg m⁻¹ s⁻¹, NSE of 0.945, and (Percent Bias) PBIAS of -1.60. While this study set an important precedent by demonstrating the potential of hybrid models, it noted that flow velocity had the most significant impact on bedload transport, with a PCC of 0.760.

Additionally, Bonakdari & Ebtehaj58 studied on ELM and SVM for predicting the densiometric Froude number (Fr) found that ELM outperforms SVM, achieving an R² of 0.99, MAE of 0.10, MAPE of 2.34%, RMSE of 0.14, and (Coefficient of Residual Mass) CRM of 0.02. This highlights computational efficiency of ELM model and accuracy, which complements our findings by showcasing the effectiveness of advanced soft computing techniques in sediment transport predictions.

Furthermore, the study by Kisi et al.59 on suspended sediment modeling using genetic programming (GP) compared GP with Adaptive Neuro-Fuzzy Inference System (ANFIS), Artificial Neural Networks (ANNs), and SVM models. The GP models, trained and tested with daily flow and sediment data from the Cumberland River, showed superior performance, evidenced by higher coefficients of determination, lower mean absolute errors, and better variance accounted for compared to ANFIS, ANNs, and SVM. This study reinforces the potential of advanced soft computing techniques, such as GP, to improve sediment load predictions.

In contrast, our study extends this foundation by incorporating a broader array of advanced ML models, such as KNN, RF, XGB, LR, and BR. The results highlight the superior performance of the XGB model, which achieved an exceptional R² of 1, RMSE of 0.11, and KGE of 1. The ETR model also performed impressively with an R² of 98.56 and KGE of 0.96. The varied performance across models, with KNN showing an R² of 28.09 and RMSE of 9.40, and LR model with an R² of 76.52 and RMSE of 5.37, underscores the advancement in predictive accuracy provided by modern ensemble techniques.

However, the present study builds on the foundation laid by previous research and introduces a more comprehensive evaluation of modern ML models, offering valuable insights for improving bedload prediction accuracy. The comparison with ELM, SVM, and GP further underscores the potential of sophisticated algorithms to enhance predictive performance and efficiency in sediment transport studies, highlighting the continuous evolution and importance of advanced modeling techniques in hydrology. The current study also advances the application of ML in bedload prediction and provides a comprehensive evaluation, highlighting the practical implications for hydraulic engineers and policymakers. This detailed analysis and the superior performance of our models underscore the importance of employing sophisticated ML techniques for more accurate and reliable predictions in river science. Their sensitivity and SHAP analyses provided deeper understandings into parameter importance, and the REC curves highlighted the superiority of ML models over traditional methods by utilizing significant parameters (Froude number, Reynolds number, and Shield parameter) that were not considered by Khosravi et al.25. Additionally, developing a user-friendly GUI enhances the accessibility and usability, making the present study practical for real-world applications in hydraulic engineering.

Furthermore, the performance of the various ML models utilized in the present study is compared with traditional empirical equations and other predictive models reported in the literature. Specifically, the present study compared the ability of the models, particularly XGB and RF, to predict bedload transport against widely used empirical equations such as the Meyer-Peter and Müller13, Einstein-Brown60, Engelund-Hansen12, van Rijn61 formulas and more recent approaches such as the Bagging M5P model by Khosravi et al.25. The analysis revealed that the traditional equations have been foundational in bedload transport prediction, they often struggle with complex, non-linear relationships present in natural channels. In contrast, in the present study, XGB model achieved an R² of 1.0 and an RMSE of 0.11, significantly outperforming both traditional models and the BR and M5P model, which had an R2 of 0.943 and an RMSE of 0.061 as reported by Khosravi et al.25. The superior performance of the XGB model can be attributed to its ability to capture intricate patterns in the data that simpler, empirical models might overlook.

Additionally, results show that Extreme Learning Machines (ELM) and Support Vector Machines (SVM) used by Bonakdari & Ebtehaj58 for predicting the Froude number. While the ELM model achieved a commendable R² of 0.99, our XGB model still demonstrated slightly better predictive accuracy, emphasizing the robustness of ensemble learning techniques in handling complex hydraulic phenomena.

K-fold cross-validation

K-fold cross-validation is a technique for assessing model performance and generalizability. The dataset is divided into ‘k’ equally sized folds. Each fold serves as the test set once while the remaining ‘k − 1’ folds are used for training. This process is repeated ‘k’ times, with each fold acting as the test set once. The results are averaged to provide a comprehensive measure of accuracy and stability of the model. This method helps to reduce overfitting and provides a more reliable estimate of model performance by ensuring every data point is used for both the training and testing stages. Additionally, it helps in identifying the performance, consistency and robustness across different subsets of the data. By leveraging multiple train-test splits, it offers a thorough evaluation, making it particularly useful for datasets with limited size.

The 20-fold cross-validation analysis reveals refined insights into the performance of various ML models applied to the dataset that are utilized in the present study as depicted in Table 5. Each model was evaluated based on RMSE and R² score, providing a comprehensive view of the predictive accuracy and explanatory power. Its stability across folds with relatively low RMSE and high R² scores underscores its robustness. Practical implications of the findings

The results of the present study have significant practical implications, particularly for hydraulic engineers involved in the management of sediment transport in rivers and streams. The application of ML models, especially the XGBoost model, offers a powerful tool for predicting bedload transport with high accuracy. This can directly influence decision-making processes in several ways:

Enhanced predictive accuracy

The XGBoost model demonstrated superior predictive accuracy, with an R² of 1 and an RMSE of 0.11, indicating its reliability in estimating bedload transport. Such high accuracy is critical for hydraulic engineers who require precise predictions to design effective sediment management strategies. By incorporating these models into the workflows, hydraulic engineers can significantly reduce the uncertainty associated with traditional empirical methods, leading to more informed and effective decision-making.

Improved sediment management

Accurate predictions of bedload transport are essential for managing sedimentation in reservoirs, canals, and other hydraulic structures. The ability to predict sediment loads effectively allows engineers to plan and implement appropriate measures, such as dredging schedules, sediment traps, or other sediment control structures. This not only extends the operational lifespan of hydraulic infrastructure. In spite of that, it helps in maintaining ecological balance in aquatic systems by preventing excessive sedimentation or erosion.

Cost-effective solutions and adaptation to changing environmental conditions

Implementing machine learning models such as XGBoost can lead to significant cost savings by optimizing the design and maintenance of hydraulic structures. These models provide highly accurate predictions of sediment transport, reducing the need for extensive field measurements and physical modeling, which are often both costly and time-consuming. By integrating these models into existing monitoring systems, engineers can obtain real-time predictions, enabling proactive and efficient management of sediment-related issues.

Furthermore, as climate change continues to affect hydrological cycles, the ability to predict sediment transport under varying flow conditions becomes increasingly crucial. The ML models developed in the current study are not only cost-effective, although, adaptable to different environmental scenarios. This adaptability makes them valuable tool and allowing to plan for and mitigate the impacts of extreme weather events, such as floods and droughts. By using these models, engineers can ensure that sediment management strategies remain effective and sustainable even as environmental conditions change.

Support for Integrated Water Resources Management (IWRM) and contribution to Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs)

The use of accurate and reliable predictive models, as developed in this study, aligns with the principles of IWRM, which emphasizes the coordinated management of water, land, and related resources. By providing precise sediment transport predictions, these models facilitate the creation of comprehensive management plans that address the needs of various stakeholders, such as agriculture, industry, and environmental conservation.

Moreover, the application of these models contributes to the achievement of several SDGs. Specifically, they support SDG 6 (Clean Water and Sanitation) by enhancing water resource management, SDG 11 (Sustainable Cities and Communities) by promoting sustainable urban infrastructure, and SDG 13 (Climate Action) by helping mitigate the impacts of climate change on hydraulic systems. Through improved sediment management, these models align with IWRM principles and promote sustainable development, ensuring the efficient and responsible use of water resources.

Major challenges in predicting bedload transport

Bedload prediction faces several major challenges due to the complex and dynamic nature of sediment transport processes. These challenges include the nonlinear interactions between hydrodynamic factors and sediment characteristics as well as the temporal and spatial variability of sediment transport rates, which can alter rapidly with varying flow conditions and differ across river systems. Additionally, the scarcity and uncertainty of field data make model calibration and validation difficult, while traditional empirical models often fail to generalize across different environments due to reliance on simplified assumptions. Furthermore, environmental factors such as climate change and human activities add layers of complexity, varying sediment transport patterns in unpredictable ways. The nonlinear and stochastic nature of these processes, coupled with the high computational demands of advanced predictive models, further, complicates accurate bedload prediction, necessitating innovative approaches such as the metaheuristic ML models proposed in the present study.

Conclusion

Accurate prediction of bedload transport rates is essential for understanding the morphodynamics of gravel-bed rivers. Given the non-linear and chaotic nature of bedload transport in rivers, the application of data mining and machine learning algorithms holds great promise for generating accurate predictions. Using bedload transport data from laboratory flume experiments, a variety of ML models, including KNN, ETR, LR, RF, BR and XGB, were used to predict the output parameter.

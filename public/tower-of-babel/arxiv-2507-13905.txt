Two-loop renormalization and running of galaxy bias
Zvonimir Vlah

Abstract
We systematically extend the framework of galaxy bias renormalization to two-loop order. For the minimal complete basis of 29 deterministic bias operators up to fifth order in the density field and at leading order in gradient expansion we explicitly work out one- and two-loop renormalization. The latter is provided in terms of double-hard limits of bias kernels, which we find to depend on only one function of the ratio of the loop momenta. After including stochasticity in terms of composite operator renormalization, we apply the framework to the two-loop power spectrum of biased tracers and provide a simple result suitable for numerical evaluation. In addition, we work out one- and two-loop renormalization group equations (RGE) for deterministic bias coefficients related to bias operators constructed from a smoothed density field, generalizing previous works. We identify a linear combination of bias operators with enhanced UV sensitivity, related to a positive eigenvalue of the RGE. Finally, we present an analogy with the RGE as used in quantum field theory, suggesting that a resummation of large logarithms as employed in the latter may also yield useful applications in the study of large-scale galaxy bias.

We start by defining the renormalized bias operator

3
3

3

Here and in the remainder of this work, we omit the wavenumber argument on the operator

4

It is sufficient to consider only connected diagrams, since the condition for disconnected ones is then automatically satisfied. To see why, consider for a moment some (arbitrary) disconnected contribution, of the form

4.2
Two-loop renormalization conditions

The renormalization conditions Eq. (
4.2
) evaluated at two-loop order are

\displaystyle 0=\,

\displaystyle\langle{\cal O}_{a}\delta_{L}({\bm{k}}_{1})\cdots\delta_{L}({\bm{k}}_{n})\rangle_{\text{2L}}\big|_{{\bm{k}}_{i}\to 0}+Z_{ab}^{\text{1L}}\langle{\cal O}_{b}\delta_{L}({\bm{k}}_{1})\cdots\delta_{L}({\bm{k}}_{n})\rangle_{\text{1L}}\big|_{{\bm{k}}_{i}\to 0}

\displaystyle+Z_{ab}^{\text{2L}}\langle{\cal O}_{b}\delta_{L}({\bm{k}}_{1})\cdots\delta_{L}({\bm{k}}_{n})\rangle_{\text{tree}}\big|_{{\bm{k}}_{i}\to 0}\,.

(4.14)

Using

\displaystyle\langle{\cal O}_{a}\delta_{L}({\bm{k}}_{1})\cdots\delta_{L}({\bm{k}}_{n})\rangle^{\prime}_{\text{2L}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(n+4)!}{2^{3}}\int_{p,q<\Lambda}K_{a}^{(n+4)}({\bm{k}}_{1},\dots,{\bm{k}}_{n},{\bm{p}},-{\bm{p}},{\bm{q}},-{\bm{q}})

(4.15)

\displaystyle\hskip-28.45274pt{}\times P^{\text{lin}}(p)\Theta(\Lambda-p)P^{\text{lin}}(q)\Theta(\Lambda-q)P^{\text{lin}}(k_{1})\cdots P^{\text{lin}}(k_{n}),

as well as the double-hard limit expansion Eq. (
3.11
) of the bias kernels yields

\boxed{Z_{ab}^{\text{2L}}=-Z_{ac}^{\text{1L}}\sigma_{\Lambda}^{2}s_{cb}^{\rm 1L}-\frac{1}{2}\int_{p,q<\Lambda}s_{ab}^{\text{2L}}(p/q)P^{\text{lin}}(p)P^{\text{lin}}(q)\,.}

(4.16)

Here we defined

s^{\text{2L}}_{ab}(p/q)\equiv\frac{(n+4)!}{4n!}d_{ab}^{(n+4)}(p/q)\,,

(4.17)

being related to the expansion coefficients
d_{ab}^{(n+4)}(r)
of the bias kernels
K^{(n+4)}_{a}
in terms of
K^{(n)}_{b}
in the double-hard limit, see Eq. (
3.11
). These coefficients are functions of the ratio
r=p/q
, and are the two-loop generalization of the one-loop coefficients
s_{ab}^{\rm 1L}
from Eq. (
4.12
). The symmetry under
\textbf{p}\leftrightarrow\textbf{q}
implies
s^{\text{2L}}_{ab}(r)=s^{\text{2L}}_{ab}(1/r)
. Using Eq. (
3.13
), they are explicitly given by

s^{\text{2L}}_{a\delta}(q/p)=30d^{(5)}_{a}+30\tilde{d}^{(5)}_{a}g(q/p)\,,

(4.18)

for
{\cal O}_{b}=\delta
. The coefficients
d^{(5)}_{a}
and
\tilde{d}^{(5)}_{a}
for all
\mathcal{O}_{a}
up to fifth order are given in Table (
B.1
), and the function
g(r)
in Eq. (
3.14
).

We expect consistency conditions similar as for the one-loop renormalization to hold, i.e. the double-hard limits of kernels at any order
n>5
should be related to those at order
5
such that the coefficients in Eq. (
4.17
) are independent of
n
. This means e.g. that we expect that
90d^{(6)}_{ba}(q/p)=30d^{(5)}_{ba}(q/p)
for all operators
{\cal O}_{b}
up to order
n\leq 5
. Testing this would require to compute the double-hard limit of the sixth order kernel, which is beyond the scope of this work.

We note that the commutation-of-limits relation Eq. (
3.15
) implies

s^{\text{2L}}_{ab}(0)=s_{ac}^{\rm 1L}s_{cb}^{\rm 1L}\,.

(4.19)

Using also that
-Z_{ac}^{\text{1L}}\sigma_{\Lambda}^{2}s_{cb}^{\rm 1L}=(\sigma_{\Lambda}^{2})^{2}s_{ac}^{\rm 1L}s_{cb}^{\rm 1L}
this implies that the contributions to
Z_{ab}^{\text{2L}}
in the limit of hierarchical loop wavenumbers
p\gg q
(or equivalently
q\gg p
) factorize into a product of two one-loop contributions, see Eq. (
4.16
) and Eq. (
4.11
). This is physically reasonable, since configurations with
p\gg q\gg k
can either be described by sequentially taking two single-hard limits, or by taking the double hard limit
p,q\gg k
and then expanding for
p\gg q
. This relation enters in the derivation of two-loop RGEs in Sec.
5
.

4.3
Renormalized deterministic bias at two loop order

Inserting the
Z
-factors Eq. (
4.11
) and Eq. (
4.16
) yields expressions for the renormalized deterministic bias operators
[\mathcal{O}_{a}]
to be used when inserted in either one- or two-loop
N
-point correlation functions,

\boxed{\begin{aligned} [{\cal O}_{a}]\Big|_{\text{1L}}&=\,{\cal O}_{a}-\sigma^{2}_{\Lambda}s_{ab}^{\rm 1L}{\cal O}_{b}\,,\\
[{\cal O}_{a}]\Big|_{\text{2L}}&=\,{\cal O}_{a}-\sigma^{2}_{\Lambda}s_{ab}^{\rm 1L}[{\cal O}_{b}]\Big|_{\text{1L}}-\frac{1}{2}\left(\int_{p,q<\Lambda}s_{ab}^{\text{2L}}(q/p)P^{\text{lin}}(p)P^{\text{lin}}(q)\right){\cal O}_{b}\,.\end{aligned}}

(4.20)

This is one of the main results in this work, together with the explicit expressions for the numerical coefficients
s_{ab}^{\rm 1L}
and for
s_{ab}^{\text{2L}}(r)
for bias operators up to fifth order.
At one-loop, the subtraction term precisely cancels the (deterministic) hard (UV) part.
At two-loop, the last term takes care of the renormalization of deterministic bias from the double-hard limit, and the middle term of the single-hard limit. The fact that the one-loop renormalized bias appears in that term is related to the first term on the right-hand side of Eq. (
4.16
), and takes care that the subtractions of single-hard and double-hard limits are not over-counted in the region where both limits overlap.

We stress that Eq. (
4.20
) is valid when inserting the bias operators in arbitrary
N
-point functions at up to two-loop order, and thus also at field level. Below we apply it to the two-loop power spectrum. Before that, we discuss an extension of bias renormalization accounting also for stochastic bias.

4.4
Stochastic bias renormalization

To treat the stochastic contributions to the bias expansion, bias renormalization needs to be extended to products of bias operators at locations
{\bm{x}}
and
{\bm{y}}
.
The renormalization of such composite operators needs to be complemented by contact terms that are supported only when both locations coincide.
We denote the renormalized product by
[{\cal O}_{a}({\bm{x}}){\cal O}_{b}({\bm{y}})]
, given by

[{\cal O}_{a}({\bm{x}}){\cal O}_{b}({\bm{y}})]=[{\cal O}_{a}({\bm{x}})]\,[{\cal O}_{b}({\bm{y}})]+Z_{abc}\,[{\cal O}_{c}({\bm{X}})]\,(2\pi)^{3}\delta_{\rm D}({\bm{x}}-{\bm{y}})+\dots\,,

(4.21)

where the ellipsis stand for higher-gradient corrections, involving derivatives of the Dirac–delta, and
{\bm{X}}=({\bm{x}}+{\bm{y}})/2
.
This structure is analogous to that of the stochastic terms within the RG approach
[
43
]
, which are treated at the field level via higher-order-in-the-current terms in the partition function. In QFT language, it corresponds to an operator product expansion. Note that here the sum over index
c
includes the unit operator
{\mathbb{1}}
, and we introduce the notation

\Delta N_{ab}\equiv Z_{ab{\mathbb{1}}}\,,

(4.22)

for that particular contribution.

To obtain the
Z_{abc}
coefficients, we have to generalize the renormalization condition Eq. (
4.2
). An obvious choice is to require that

\boxed{\begin{aligned} \langle[{\cal O}_{a}({\bm{k}}){\cal O}_{b}({\bm{k}}^{\prime})]\delta_{L}({\bm{k}}_{1})\cdots\delta_{L}({\bm{k}}_{n})\rangle|_{{\bm{k}},{\bm{k}}^{\prime},{\bm{k}}_{i}\to 0}&\\
&\hskip-56.9055pt=\langle[{\cal O}_{a}({\bm{k}})]\,[{\cal O}_{b}({\bm{k}}^{\prime})]\delta_{L}({\bm{k}}_{1})\cdots\delta_{L}({\bm{k}}_{n})\rangle_{\text{tree}}|_{{\bm{k}},{\bm{k}}^{\prime},{\bm{k}}_{i}\to 0}\,,\end{aligned}}

(4.23)

where we explicitly note that
all
wavenumbers are sent to zero.
At one-loop this yields the conditions

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\langle[{\cal O}_{a}({\bm{k}})]\,[{\cal O}_{b}({\bm{k}}^{\prime})]\delta_{L}({\bm{k}}_{1})\cdots\delta_{L}({\bm{k}}_{n})\rangle_{\text{1L}}|_{{\bm{k}},{\bm{k}}^{\prime},{\bm{k}}_{i}\to 0}

\displaystyle\hskip 113.81102pt+Z_{abc}^{\text{1L}}\langle[{\cal O}_{c}({\bm{k}}+{\bm{k}}^{\prime})]\delta_{L}({\bm{k}}_{1})\cdots\delta_{L}({\bm{k}}_{n})\rangle_{\text{tree}}|_{{\bm{k}},{\bm{k}}^{\prime},{\bm{k}}_{i}\to 0}\,.

(4.24)

Applying the renormalization condition for
n=0
and using that
[{\mathbb{1}}(\textbf{k}+\textbf{k}^{\prime})]=(2\pi)^{3}\delta_{\rm D}(\textbf{k}+\textbf{k}^{\prime})
as well as
\langle[\mathcal{O}_{c}]\rangle=0
for all non-trivial renormalized bias operators yields

\Delta N_{ab}^{\text{1L}}\equiv Z^{\text{1L}}_{ab{\mathbb{1}}}=P^{\text{1L}}_{[a][b]}(0)\,,

(4.25)

where
P^{\text{1L}}_{[a][b]}(k)
is the one-loop power spectrum with two renormalized operators inserted.
This argument can be simply extended to two-loop order,

\Delta N_{ab}^{\text{2L}}\equiv Z^{\text{2L}}_{ab{\mathbb{1}}}=P^{\text{2L}}_{[a][b]}(0)\,,

(4.26)

and in fact holds at any loop order, i.e.
\Delta N_{ab}=P_{[a][b]}(0)
. We present the calculation of
\Delta N_{ab}^{\text{1L}}
and
\Delta N_{ab}^{\text{2L}}
below, in Sec.
4.5
. We denote the power spectra for fully renormalized composite bias operators
[{\cal O}_{a}(\textbf{k}){\cal O}_{b}(\textbf{k}^{\prime})]
by

\langle[{\cal O}_{a}({\bm{k}}){\cal O}_{b}({\bm{k}}^{\prime})]\rangle\equiv(2\pi)^{3}\delta_{\rm D}({\bm{k}}+{\bm{k}}^{\prime})P_{[ab]}(k)\,.

(4.27)

Thus, for the power spectrum, composite operator renormalization [with renormalization condition given by Eq. (
4.23
)] at leading order in the derivative expansion simply amounts to subtracting the
k
-independent shot noise term such that the fully renormalized tracer power spectrum
P_{[ab]}
approaches zero in the
k\to 0
limit,

P_{[ab]}(k)=P_{[a][b]}(k)-\Delta N_{ab}\,.

(4.28)

This result holds at any loop order. Explicit expressions for
Z_{abc}
for non-trivial operators
c\neq{\mathbb{1}}
can be derived from Eq. (
4.4
) for
n>0
at one-loop, and similarly at two loop order. However, this is not needed in the following.

4.5
Application to the two-loop power spectrum

We apply bias renormalization at two-loop order to the power spectrum, including deterministic and stochastic contributions at leading order in the derivative expansion.
We also consider the power spectrum of the individually renormalized bias operators
[\mathcal{O}_{a}]
and
[\mathcal{O}_{b}]
,
P_{[a][b]}(k)
, and the one for the unrenormalized bias
\mathcal{O}_{a}
and
\mathcal{O}_{b}
, which we denote by
P_{ab}(k)
.
The tracer power spectrum can be equivalently expressed in terms of any of these spectra (leaving higher-derivative terms implicit) as

\displaystyle P_{gg}(k)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{a,b}b_{a}b_{b}P_{ab}(k)+\text{cst.}+\text{h.d.}

(4.29)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{a,b}b_{[a]}b_{[b]}P_{[a][b]}(k)+\text{cst.}+\text{h.d.}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{a,b}b_{[a]}b_{[b]}P_{[ab]}(k)+[\text{cst.}]+\text{h.d.}\,,

where
b_{a}
and cst. (not to be confused with the unit operator
\mathbb{1}
from Eq. (
4.7
), which enters the bias expansion at the field level) are the ‘bare’ deterministic and constant stochastic bias coefficients, respectively, and
b_{[a]}=b_{c}[(1+Z)^{-1}]_{ca}
as well as
[\text{cst.}]\equiv\text{cst.}+b_{[a]}b_{[b]}\Delta N_{ab}
the renormalized ones.

We note that at linear level,

P^{\text{lin}}_{[ab]}(k)=P^{\text{lin}}_{[a][b]}(k)=P^{\text{lin}}_{ab}(k)\equiv P^{(11)}_{ab}(k)=\delta^{K}_{a\delta}\delta^{K}_{b\delta}P^{\text{lin}}(k)\,,

(4.30)

given by the usual linear spectrum
P^{\text{lin}}(k)
for
a=b=\delta
, and zero otherwise.
To derive explicit expressions at one- and two-loop level we proceed in two steps:

1.

As a first step, we consider the power spectra
P^{\text{1L/2L}}_{[a][b]}(k)
of the individually renormalized bias operators derived in Eq. (
4.20
). This corresponds to taking renormalization of deterministic bias into account.

2.

In the second step, we include also stochastic bias renormalization, i.e. go from
P^{\text{1L/2L}}_{[a][b]}(k)
to the fully renormalized power spectrum
P^{\text{1L/2L}}_{[ab]}(k)
for the renormalized composite operator
[{\cal O}_{a}(\textbf{k}){\cal O}_{b}(\textbf{k}^{\prime})]
using Eq. (
4.28
).

Using Eq. (
4.20
), the first step yields

\displaystyle P^{\text{1L}}_{[a][b]}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle P^{\text{1L}}_{ab}-\sigma^{2}_{\Lambda}\left(s_{ac}^{\rm 1L}P^{\text{lin}}_{cb}+(a\leftrightarrow b)\right)\,,

(4.31)

\displaystyle P^{\text{2L}}_{[a][b]}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle P^{\text{2L}}_{ab}-\sigma^{2}_{\Lambda}\bigg(s_{ac}^{\rm 1L}P^{\text{1L}}_{[c][b]}+(a\leftrightarrow b)\bigg)

(4.32)

\displaystyle~\hskip 14.22636pt{}-\Bigg(\frac{1}{2}\int_{p,q<\Lambda}\bigg(s_{ac}^{\text{2L}}(q/p)P^{\text{lin}}_{cb}+(a\leftrightarrow b)\bigg)P^{\text{lin}}(p)P^{\text{lin}}(q)+(\sigma^{2}_{\Lambda})^{2}s_{ac}^{\rm 1L}P^{\text{lin}}_{cd}\bar{s}_{db}^{\rm 1L}\Bigg)\,,

where we introduced the transposed matrix

\bar{s}_{db}^{\rm 1L}\equiv s_{bd}^{\rm 1L}.

(4.33)

Using Eq. (
4.12
) and Eq. (
4.17
) we find the important result that the various subtraction terms arising from the deterministic bias renormalization relation Eq. (
4.20
)
precisely
give rise to the deterministic single- and double-hard limits of the one- and two-loop power spectrum derived in Eq. (
3.1
) and Eq. (
3.16
), respectively,

\displaystyle\sigma^{2}_{\Lambda}\left(s_{ac}^{\rm 1L}P^{\text{lin}}_{cb}+(a\leftrightarrow b)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle P^{\text{1L}}_{ab}\Big|^{\text{hard}}_{\text{det.}}\,,

\displaystyle\sigma^{2}_{\Lambda}\bigg(s_{ac}^{\rm 1L}P^{\text{1L}}_{[c][b]}+(a\leftrightarrow b)\bigg)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle P^{\text{2L}}_{ab}\Big|^{\text{single-hard}}_{\text{det.}}-P^{\text{2L}}_{ab}\Big|^{\text{single-hard}}_{\text{det.}}\Bigg|^{\text{hard}}\,,

(4.34)

and

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{p,q<\Lambda}\bigg(s_{ac}^{\text{2L}}(q/p)P^{\text{lin}}_{cb}+(a\leftrightarrow b)\bigg)P^{\text{lin}}(p)P^{\text{lin}}(q)+(\sigma^{2}_{\Lambda})^{2}s_{ac}^{\rm 1L}P^{\text{lin}}_{cd}\bar{s}_{db}^{\rm 1L}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle P^{\text{2L}}_{ab}\Big|^{\text{double-hard}}_{\text{det.}}\,.

Thus, as expected, bias renormalization removes the leading UV sensitive parts of the loop integrals. We stress that the relation between the single-hard coefficients
c_{ab}^{(n)}=2s_{ab}^{\text{1L}}/(n(n-1))
for
n=3,4,5
implied by Eq. (
4.12
) is crucial for obtaining this result, particularly for seeing that the
P^{(13)}
and
P^{(22)}
contributions in the single-hard limit of the two-loop power spectrum can be combined into
P^{\text{1L}}
. Furthermore, note that the single-hard two-loop subtraction term involves the one-loop power spectrum
P^{\text{1L}}_{[a][b]}
of the
renormalized
deterministic bias operators
[\mathcal{O}_{a}]
and
[\mathcal{O}_{b}]
. Thus, as already hinted at above, bias renormalization automatically takes care of subtracting the overlap of single- and double-hard limits, such that no double-counting arises. This can be seen by the fact that the single-hard deterministic subtraction term in the second line in Eq. (
4.5
) is itself a difference between the two expressions given on the right-hand side.

In the second step we use Eq. (
4.25
) and Eq. (
4.26
) as well as Eq. (
4.28
). At one-loop order, using Eq. (
4.31
)
and assuming that the linear power spectrum vanishes for
k=0
,
P^{\text{lin}}(0)=0
, we have

\Delta N_{ab}^{\text{1L}}=P^{\text{1L}}_{[a][b]}(0)=P^{\text{1L}}_{ab}(0)=P^{\text{1L}}_{ab}(k)\Big|^{\text{single-hard}}_{\text{stoch.}}\,,

(4.36)

with the latter given by the stochastic contribution to the single-hard limit, see Eq. (
3.19
). At two-loop,

\Delta N_{ab}^{\text{2L}}=P^{\text{2L}}_{[a][b]}(0)=P^{\text{2L}}_{ab}(0)-\sigma^{2}_{\Lambda}\big(s_{ac}^{\rm 1L}\Delta N_{cb}^{\text{1L}}+(a\leftrightarrow b)\big)\,.

(4.37)

Here the first term is given by the stochastic contribution to the double-hard limit,

P^{\text{2L}}_{ab}(0)=P^{\text{2L}}_{ab}(k)\Big|^{\text{double-hard}}_{\text{stoch.}}\,,

(4.38)

see Eq. (
3.4
) for the explicit result, while the second term can be combined with the deterministic single-hard subtraction term in Eq. (
4.32
) within the fully renormalized power spectrum Eq. (
4.28
).

Altogether, the fully renormalized power spectrum at one and two loops is given by

\boxed{\begin{aligned} P^{\text{1L}}_{[ab]}(k)&=P^{\text{1L}}_{ab}(k)-P^{\text{1L}}_{ab}(k)\Big|^{\text{single-hard}}_{\text{det.+stoch.}}\\
&=P^{\text{1L}}_{ab}(k)-\sigma^{2}_{\Lambda}\big(s_{ac}^{\rm 1L}P^{\text{lin}}_{cb}(k)+(a\leftrightarrow b)\big)-P^{\text{1L}}_{ab}(0)\,,\\
P^{\text{2L}}_{[ab]}(k)&=P^{\text{2L}}_{ab}(k)-\sigma^{2}_{\Lambda}\big(s_{ac}^{\rm 1L}P^{\text{1L}}_{[cb]}(k)+(a\leftrightarrow b)\big)-P^{\text{2L}}_{ab}(k)\Big|^{\text{double-hard}}_{\text{det.+stoch.}}\\
&=P^{\text{2L}}_{ab}(k)-\sigma^{2}_{\Lambda}\big(s_{ac}^{\rm 1L}P^{\text{1L}}_{[cb]}(k)+(a\leftrightarrow b)\big)-P^{\text{2L}}_{ab}(k)\Big|^{\text{double-hard}}_{\text{det.}}-P^{\text{2L}}_{ab}(0)\,.\end{aligned}}

(4.39)

The one-loop expression agrees with the standard result, for which the two subtraction terms in the second line take care of the UV sensitivity of
P^{(13)}
and
P^{(22)}
, respectively, associated to deterministic and stochastic bias renormalization. The expressions in Eq. (
4.39
) can be computed numerically using for example the methodology outlined in
[
50
]
. We highlight several features of the two-loop result:

•

The subtraction term taking care of removing the UV sensitivity associated to the single-hard limit of the bare two-loop power spectrum

contains itself the fully
renormalized
one-loop power spectrum
P^{\text{1L}}_{[cb]}(k)
as well as the numerical one-loop bias renormalization coefficients

[see Table (

•

The further subtraction term takes care of the double-hard limit at two-loop order. It is given by the sum of deterministic and stochastic contributions, and explicit results for both are given in Eq. (
3.16
) and Eq. (
3.4
) for up to fifth order bias.

•

No mixed stochastic/deterministic terms (corresponding to
Z_{abc}
with nontrivial

) are required for the power spectrum at any loop order. They would appear when inserting the composite renormalized bias operator in three- or higher
N
-point functions only.

•

Stochastic bias renormalization simply amounts to subtracting the
k=0

Lastly, while all fifth-order kernels from Eq. (

becomes proportional to

5
The renormalization group equations

In this and the following Sections we turn to the alternative RG approach for controlling the dependence of bias operators on the smoothing scale
\Lambda
, counter-balanced by correspondingly
\Lambda
-dependent bias coefficients
b_{a}(\Lambda)
. While
[
34

considered the Wilson-Polchinski formulation, in which modes are integrated out in shells in Fourier space, we follow an alternative RG approach exploiting more directly the independence of physical observables, i.e. the galaxy density contrast
\delta_{g}({\bf x})
(and its
N
-point functions), on
\Lambda
as starting point.
This is advantageous for extending the RG to two loop order, while agreeing with the Wilson-Polchinski results
[
34
]
at one loop. We also note that the relation to renormalized bias, namely
b_{[a]}=b_{a}(\Lambda\to 0)
, carries over from
[
34
]
to the one- and two-loop RG we consider here.
7
7

7

Within the RG approach, we focus on deterministic bias in this work, noting that an extension of the one-loop RG for stochastic terms from
[
43
]
to two loop would also be possible within the RG approach employed here, using the formulation from Sec.
4.4
.

5.1
The N-point function renormalization conditions

To derive two-loop RG equations we
start from the requirement that the tracer field is independent of the smoothing scale
\Lambda
(we suppress time arguments in all quantities for brevity),

0=\frac{d}{d\Lambda}\delta_{g}({\bm{x}})=\frac{db_{a}}{d\Lambda}{\cal O}_{a}({\bm{x}})+b_{a}\frac{d{\cal O}_{a}({\bm{x}})}{d\Lambda}\,.

(5.1)

As before, the smoothing is defined such that it is applied to the linear density field with a sharp-
k
filter in Fourier space, see Eq. (
3.6
), and the bias operators
{\cal O}_{a}
are constructed from this smoothed field as in Eq. (
2.3
) (we emphasize once more that smoothed initial conditions are implicitly assumed throughout this work).
To derive explicit RG equations we use that Eq. (
5.1
) is an operator equation and thus has to be valid when inserted into arbitrary correlation functions,
8
8

8

We note that it is sufficient to consider only the
connected
parts of the correlations functions in both summands (see footnote at the beginning of Section

).

\boxed{0=\frac{db_{a}}{d\Lambda}\langle{\cal O}_{a}\delta_{L}({\bm{k}}_{1})\cdots\delta_{L}({\bm{k}}_{n})\rangle+b_{a}\frac{d}{d\Lambda}\langle{\cal O}_{a}\delta_{L}({\bm{k}}_{1})\cdots\delta_{L}({\bm{k}}_{n})\rangle\,.}

(5.2)

Here we used that in the second term the
\Lambda
-derivative can be pulled out of the average, since the only source of
\Lambda
-dependence comes from the smoothed fields entering the
{\cal O}_{a}
. Below we also assume that
|{\bm{k}}_{i}|\ll\Lambda
are IR wavenumbers
below the smoothing scale. Of course other correlators could be chosen to derive the RG, but in analogy with
[
15
]
, the correlation with the linear density field allows for a simple derivation in terms of a single operator kernel limit, as we see below. Moreover, the smoothing of the initial Gaussian fluctuations resembles the smoothing considered in the Wilson-Polchinski framework
[
37
,
34
]
.

Next, we treat the peculiar case
n=0
separately, as in the discussion around Eq. (
4.5
), for which no external lines are attached. There is one special bias operator which contributes to Eq. (
5.2
) only in that case (when restricting to connected correlation functions), being the trivial operator
{\mathbb{1}}
.
Since
\langle{\mathbb{1}}\rangle=1
, this yields

0=\frac{db_{\mathbb{1}}}{d\Lambda}+\frac{d}{d\Lambda}\left(b_{a}\langle{\cal O}_{a}\rangle\right),

(5.3)

where the unit operator
{\mathbb{1}}
is excluded from the summation over all bias operators
a
in the second term. We use this convention also below. The solution is simply
b_{\mathbb{1}}=-b_{a}\langle{\cal O}_{a}\rangle
. The role of this constant bias operator is thus just to ensure that the average of the tracer field vanishes,

\langle\delta_{g}\rangle=0\,.

(5.4)

We do not need to consider
b_{\mathbb{1}}
in Eq. (

Connection between RGE and renormalized bias

In this Section, we show that the (one and two-loop) renormalization group equations from Sec.
5
can also be derived via the renormalized bias parameters discussed in Sec.

Since the renormalized bias coefficients

do (by construction) not depend on the cutoff

\displaystyle\frac{dZ^{\text{2L}}_{ba}}{d\Lambda}-Z^{\text{1L}}_{bc}\frac{dZ^{\text{1L}}_{ca}}{d\Lambda}\,.

Using the one-loop result for
Z
from Eq. (
4.11
) yields directly

).
Using the two-loop result Eq. (

) and the rewritings of coefficients mentioned below that equation yields

\displaystyle\gamma_{ba}^{\text{2L}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{d}{d\Lambda}\Big[(\sigma_{\Lambda}^{2})^{2}s_{bc}^{\rm 1L}s_{ca}^{\rm 1L}-\frac{1}{2}\int_{p,q<\Lambda}s_{ba}^{\text{2L}}(q/p)P^{\text{lin}}(p)P^{\text{lin}}(q)\Big]-Z^{\text{1L}}_{bc}\frac{dZ^{\text{1L}}_{ca}}{d\Lambda}

8
Systematic resummation of higher loops via the RGE

As is well-known in QFT, the solution of RG equations corresponds to a partial resummation of higher-loop effects. The RG is useful in this context since
(i)
it resums the dominant parts of higher loops (in a precise sense reviewed below, related to the presence of two widely separated energy scales, and the appearance of ‘large logarithms’) and
(ii)
solving the RG is much simpler than computing all those higher loops. While the second item applies also to the bias RG, as demonstrated in the last Section, we discuss in this Section to what extent the first one is met. In Sec.
8.1
we briefly review the precise meaning of the resummation of ‘large logarithms’ with the help of RG techniques in the QFT context, emphasizing that the resulting ‘RG-improved’ perturbation theory allows for a systematic re-organization of the perturbative expansion under specific conditions. In Sec.
8.2
we transfer these techniques to the bias RG and identify the analog of the ‘large logarithm’.

8.1
The analogy with QFT

To begin, we briefly remind the rationale for using RG evolution in the context of QFT, taking the evolution of the fine-structure constant
\alpha(\mu)
with renormalization scale
\mu
in quantum electrodynamics as a well-known example. The RG equation (computed in dimensional regularization within the modified minimal subtraction scheme
\overline{\text{MS}}
) takes the form

\frac{d\alpha}{d\ln\mu}=\beta_{\text{1L}}\alpha^{2}+\beta_{\text{2L}}\alpha^{3}+O(\alpha^{4})\,,

(8.1)

with
\beta_{\text{1L}}=2/(3\pi)
and
\beta_{\text{2L}}=1/(4\pi^{2})
denoting the one- and two-loop
\beta
-functions, in analogy to the first and second lines in Eq. (
7.1
), respectively. Keeping only the former yields the seminal solution

\alpha(\mu)\big|_{\text{LL}}=\frac{\alpha}{1-\beta_{\text{1L}}\alpha\ln(\mu/\mu_{*})}=\alpha\left[1+\beta_{\text{1L}}\alpha\ln(\mu/\mu_{*})-\beta_{\text{1L}}^{2}\alpha^{2}\ln^{2}(\mu/\mu_{*})+\dots\right]\,,

(8.2)

where
\alpha\equiv\alpha(\mu_{\ast})\simeq 1/137
is the value inferred from low-energy experiments (at an IR scale
\mu_{\ast}
related e.g. to the electron mass), and
\alpha(\mu)
the coupling strength relevant for predictions of high-energy collisions (e.g. at an
e^{-}e^{+}
collider with UV scale
\mu
being chosen at the center of mass energy)
12
12

12

Even though the scale
\mu
introduced in dimensional regularization is distinct from the smoothing (or cutoff) scale
\Lambda
, both are a priori unphysical quantities and observables such as cross-sections
\sigma
in QFT or N-point functions of galaxies in LSS do not depend on it.
Nevertheless, in practice, such parameters are often highly useful for understanding and predicting the dependence of observables on widely separated physical scales, and developing suitable resummation techniques. In the LSS context a conceptually analogous situation of this kind is known from IR resummation, featuring an artificial separation scale
k_{s}
, the dependence on which would vanish exactly when adding IR-resummed loop corrections to all orders (see e.g. Sec. 4.3 in
[
52
]
).
. A tree-level computation using
\alpha(\mu)
is then equivalent to resumming contributions from a series of loop computations performed at the IR scale, as given by the Taylor expansion of Eq. (
8.2
) in powers of
\alpha
. The terms of order
\alpha^{\ell}
in the square bracket would result from an
\ell
-loop computation in this case. Nevertheless, those terms are not the complete
\ell
-loop, but only the part that involves one large logarithm
\ln(\mu/\mu_{\ast})
for each of the
\ell
-loop corrections, being the ‘leading logarithms’ (LL) of the form
\alpha^{\ell}\ln^{\ell}(\mu/\mu_{\ast})
. Solving the RGE and doing a tree-level computation with the running coupling
\alpha(\mu)
as input is thus equivalent to doing a series of loop computations in terms of the IR coupling
\alpha
, and retaining only the LL part of each loop. The one-loop RG solution thus corresponds to LL resummation.

This scheme can be systematically improved by including also
\beta_{\text{2L}}
when solving the RGE from Eq. (
8.1
). In this case the running coupling corresponds to resumming also ‘next-to leading logarithms’ (NLL) of the form
\alpha^{\ell}\ln^{\ell-1}(\mu/\mu_{\ast})
for all
\ell\geq 2
. Thus solving the RGE including the two-loop
\beta
-function and using the resulting
\alpha(\mu)
as input for a one-loop computation is equivalent to a series of loop computations in terms of the IR coupling
\alpha
and resumming LL and NLL contributions at all loop orders. To illustrate this point, consider the Taylor series expansion of the solution to the RG including both
\beta_{\text{1L}}
and
\beta_{\text{2L}}
,

\alpha(\mu)\big|_{\text{NLL}}=\alpha\left[1+\beta_{\text{1L}}\alpha\ln(\mu/\mu_{*})-\beta_{\text{1L}}^{2}\alpha^{2}\ln^{2}(\mu/\mu_{*})+\beta_{\text{2L}}\alpha^{2}\ln(\mu/\mu_{*})+O(\alpha^{3})\right]\,.

(8.3)

When doing perturbation theory in the IR coupling
\alpha
, the two-loop corresponds to terms of order
\alpha^{2}
. In the RG approach, they are generated partially by the one-loop RG, being the ‘one-loop squared’ term
\propto\beta_{\text{1L}}^{2}
which yields the LL contribution, and the ‘two-loop’ RG contribution
\propto\beta_{\text{2L}}
which corresponds to the NLL. Provided
|\ln(\mu/\mu_{\ast})|\gg 1
, the LL approximation thus already captures the dominant part, while the NLL approximation yields a correction that is relatively suppressed as
\beta_{\text{1L}}^{2}/(\beta_{\text{2L}}\ln(\mu/\mu_{*}))
.

Thus, using the running coupling obtained from solving RG equations is a useful concept in QFT due to the existence of
two
small expansion parameters:
(a)
the small coupling constant
\alpha(\mu)\ll 1
, and
(b)
the inverse of the large logarithm
1/|\ln(\mu/\mu_{\ast})|\ll 1
. The latter is an emergent scale, related to the logarithmic sensitivity of loop integrals to the UV and IR external scales in dimensional regularization (such as e.g. the center of mass energy (UV) versus the electron mass (IR) in the example above). The LL approximation (tree-level computation with LL running coupling) corresponds to resumming all terms that scale as
[\alpha\ln(\mu/\mu_{\ast})]^{\ell}
for all loop orders
\ell=1,2,3,\dots
. The NLL approximation (one-loop computation with NLL running coupling) corresponds to resumming
\alpha\times[\alpha\ln(\mu/\mu_{\ast})]^{\ell}
terms. This can be systematically extended to NNLL, and so on, and is known as RG-improved perturbation theory.
Schematically an observable (e.g. a cross-section
\sigma
) has an expansion within RG-improved perturbation theory (relative to its tree-level value)

\frac{\sigma}{\sigma_{\text{tree}}}=f_{\text{LL}}(x)+\alpha\times f_{\text{NLL}}(x)+\alpha^{2}\times f_{\text{NNLL}}(x)+\dots\,,

(8.4)

with
x\equiv\alpha\times\ln(\mu/\mu_{\ast})
and functions
f_{\text{N}^{n}\text{LL}}(x)=\sum_{m\geq 0}x^{m}c^{(n+m,m)}
that capture the resummation of large logarithms, for some coefficients
c^{(n+m,m)}
. Importantly, in Eq. (

corresponds to a strict Taylor series in
\alpha
(i.e. irrespective of the power of logarithms), such that the

-loop correction

\frac{\sigma_{\ell\text{L}}}{\sigma_{\text{tree}}}=\alpha^{\ell}\Bigg[c^{(\ell,\ell)}\ln^{\ell}(\mu/\mu_{\ast})+c^{(\ell,\ell-1)}\ln^{\ell-1}(\mu/\mu_{\ast})+\dots\Bigg]\,.

(8.5)

The virtue of the RG is that it allows one to fix the entire

-dependence of the functions
f(x)
. More precisely, for every fixed value of

, the entire tower of coefficients

for all

are fixed by the
(n+1)
-loop
\beta
-functions entering the RGEs, with the only free parameter being

(requiring to perform an
n
-loop computation at the IR scale to determine it for the cross-section of a given process). For example, if
\sigma_{\text{tree}}(s)=\alpha^{2}/s
(with center of mass energy squared
s
) depends quadratically on
\alpha
, the one-loop RG yields

f_{\text{LL}}(x)=\left(\frac{\alpha(\mu)}{\alpha}\right)^{2}=\frac{1}{(1-\beta_{\text{1L}}x)^{2}}\,,

(8.6)

and thus all

\beta_{\text{1L}}

Thus, in this example, LL resummation just amounts to replacing
\alpha
by the running coupling
\alpha(\mu)
in the tree-level cross-section, in line with the discussion above. We illustrate the resummation at each
\text{N}^{n}\text{LL}
order in the left panel of Fig.
8
. The right panel will be discussed in Sec.

In summary, the RG approach is useful when choosing the arbitrary scale

of order the UV scale (related to the momenta of particles in the process, e.g. the center of mass energy
\sqrt{s}
), and
\mu_{\ast}

is of order unity (while
\alpha\ll 1
still ensures perturbativity of the theory), using the RG-improved framework becomes mandatory, while the naive perturbative expansion (strict expansion in
\alpha
) would start to break down.

9
Conclusions

Extending perturbative calculations for biased tracers in LSS to higher-order is of interest for extracting additional information on cosmology from galaxy surveys. Specifically, computing the next-to-next-to-leading-order (two-loop) power spectrum and bispectrum demands the inclusion of fifth-order operators. In this work, we have re-derived the full list of fifth-order operators in the bias expansion in Sec.
2
, finding consistency with previous independent calculations
[
12
,
13
,
11
]
as well as five additional degeneracies at fifth order for operators that enter the two-loop power spectrum through the
P^{(15)}
diagram.

Consistent operator renormalization is a vital aspect of the large-scale bias expansion. In that context, we have extended the renormalization prescription for the bias operators from
[
15
]
to fifth-order operators and two-loops considering renormalization conditions that match the renormalized bias values to the IR limit of
N
-point functions (see Sec.
4
).
We show in Sec.
4.1
that the one-loop renormalization can be written in terms of the
single-hard
limit of the bias kernels, calculated in Sec.
3
.
Extending the approach to two-loop corrections requires the calculation of the
double-hard
limit of (at least fifth-order expanded) operator kernels. Remarkably, the dependence of the double-hard contribution of all fifth-order kernels on the ratio of loop wavenumbers
p/q
is
universal
, i.e. it is specified in terms of a single function
g(p/q)
given in Eq. (
3.14
). We provide very simple expressions for the renormalized deterministic bias in terms of the single- and double-hard limit of the operator kernels in Eq. (
4.20
) and, after incorporating stochasticity at leading order in the gradient expansion, the two-loop power spectrum in Eq. (
4.39
).

A complementary approach to the renormalized bias parameters is to consider their running with the smoothing scale
\Lambda
based on RG techniques applied to the LSS context
[
34
,
43
,
44
]
. Using the (double-)hard fifth-order kernel limits from Sec.
3
, we perform a complete derivation of the running of the linear bias parameter (at leading order in gradient expansion) at one and two-loop order and second- and third-order bias parameters at one-loop, with two-loop
\beta
-function (‘anomalous dimension’) given in Eq. (
5.20
).
Alternatively, we show in Sec.
6
that the anomalous dimension matrix
\gamma_{ab}
for the bias coefficients in the RG approach can be expressed in terms of the renormalization constants
Z_{ab}
from Eq. (
4.1
) in the approach of
[
15
]
at all loop orders, and when expanding both expressions order by order in loops, we rederive our earlier results.
We discuss solutions of the bias RG in Sec.
7
, pointing out the relevance of including third- and higher-order biases for the running of
b_{\delta}
, and identify a growing-in-
\Lambda
eigenmode of the one-loop RG as cause.

Finally, we discuss in how far the framework of RG-improved perturbation theory in QFT can be applied to the cross-power spectrum of bias operators using the bias RG. It relies on a double expansion, usually in terms of a small coupling constant and a ‘large logarithm’, and corresponds to a systematic re-organization of the perturbative expansion allowing for a resummation of all-order terms enhanced by large logarithms based on solving RG equations. We generalize this concept to the cross-power spectrum of bias operators, see Eq. (
8.15
), and identify the quantity that corresponds to the ‘large logarithm’ in the context of LSS in Eq. (
8.22
). Furthermore, we explicitly derive the cross-power spectrum of bias operators in ‘leading logarithmic’ approximation using the one-loop RG in Eq. (
8.18
). Finally, we demonstrate the relative suppression of ‘next-to-leading logarithmic’ contributions related to the two-loop RG as compared to leading logarithmic terms, being a first non-trivial check, and providing evidence that the concept of RG-improvement could be systematically applied to the tracer power spectrum for
\Lambda
CDM cosmologies.

In conclusion, this work offers significant theoretical advances in understanding the renormalization of bias operators and their application to the two-loop power spectrum. In addition, we have extended the RG approach to include higher-order and two-loop contributions.
Moreover, our results suggest that applying RG techniques to LSS observables may enable information gain by resumming dominant UV-sensitive parts of higher loops, using the systematic framework of RG-improved perturbation theory. Altogether, these results further strengthen the connections between QFT and cosmology and prepare the stage for applications in galaxy clustering analyses.

3.4

p

\tilde{n}_{b}^{(4)}

g(0)=g(\infty)=0
. The double-hard limit of
P^{(33),II}
contains a sum of three terms. The first summand contains the integral
\int_{pq}P(p)P(q)P(|{\bm{p}}+{\bm{q}}|)
. There are three possible hierarchical limits,
p,|{\bm{p}}+{\bm{q}}|\gg q

q,|{\bm{p}}+{\bm{q}}|\gg p

p,q\gg|{\bm{p}}+{\bm{q}}|
. In each case the integral approaches the same limit (after relabelling loop wavenumbers)
\int_{p}P(p)^{2}\int_{q}P(q)
. Thus we obtain again a result of the same form as the single-hard limit. The same is true for the second and third summand, realizing that the angle-average can be done analytically in the hierarchical limit, and that for e.g. the limit
p,|{\bm{p}}+{\bm{q}}|\gg q

f({\bm{p}},{\bm{q}})\to({\bm{p}}\cdot{\bm{q}})^{2}/(p^{2}q^{2})

[f({\bm{p}},{\bm{q}})^{2}]_{\text{av}}\to 1/5

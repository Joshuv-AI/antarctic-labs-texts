Documentation on Encrypted Dynamic Control Simulation Code using Ring-LWE based Cryptosystems
Yeongjun Jang, Joowon Lee, Junsoo Kim

Abstract
Encrypted controllers offer secure computation by employing modern cryptosystems to execute control operations directly over encrypted data without decryption. However, incorporating cryptosystems into dynamic controllers significantly increases the computational load. This paper aims to provide an accessible guideline for running encrypted controllers using an open-source library Lattigo, which supports an efficient implementation of Ring-Learing With Errors (LWE) based encrypted controllers, and our explanations are assisted with example codes that are fully available at https://github.com/CDSL-EncryptedControl/CDSL .



I
Introduction

Networked control is to outsource computations of physical devices to cloud servers in order to achieve computational efficiency and higher performance.
However, it also introduces the risk of cyber-attacks, as data that are transmitted and processed via public network are vulnerable to eavesdropping and manipulation by unauthorized third parties.
Encrypted control addresses this issue by employing homomorphic cryptosystems to perform control computations over encrypted data without decryption
[
1
,
2
,
3
]
.
This ensures the confidentiality of all data throughout the transmission and computation stages.

While significant theoretical advancements have been made in encrypted control, applying it to real-time systems remains challenging, primarily due to the high computational complexity of cryptosystems.
To ensure that all computations are completed within the sampling period, results found in the literature are typically limited to simple systems or compromise the level of security as a trade-off for reducing the computational burden, as in
[
4
,
5
]
.

This issue could be relieved by utilizing some library implementations, such as Microsoft SEAL
[
6
]
, HElib
[
7
]
, and Lattigo
[
8
]
;
they support optimized implementations of Ring-Learning With Errors (LWE) based cryptosystems
[
9
,
10
,
11
,
12
]
, which are among the most efficient and advanced homomorphic cryptosystem available.
However, such libraries often lack instructions for those without a strong cryptographic background, and thus, have not been actively used in the field of encrypted control.

This paper aims to provide an accessible guideline for implementing Ring-LWE based encrypted controllers using the Lattigo library, which supports an efficient implementation of state-of-the-art homomorphic cryptosystems.
In particular, we focus on the implementations of the encrypted controllers presented in
[
13
]
and
[
14
]
.
We briefly review each result, and provide some example codes, fully available at
https://github.com/CDSL-EncryptedControl/CDSL
, to assist our explanations.

II
Ring-LWE based Cryptosystem

This section provides a brief overview of Ring-LWE based cryptosystems
[
9
,
11
,
10
,
12
,
15
]
.
Ring-LWE based cryptosystems are based on the polynomial ring
R_{q}:={\mathbb{Z}}_{q}[X]/\langle X^{N}+1\rangle
, which is defined by parameters
q\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
, where
N
is a power of two.
The elements of
R_{q}
can be represented by polynomials with indeterminate
X
, coefficients in
{\mathbb{Z}}_{q}:={\mathbb{Z}}\cap[-q/2,q/2)
, and degree less than
N
.
The ring
R_{q}
is closed under modular addition and multiplication, which regards
X^{N}\equiv-1
and map each coefficient to the set
{\mathbb{Z}}_{q}
via the modulo operation defined by
a\!\!\mod q:=a-\lfloor(a+q/2)/q\rfloor q
for all
a\in{\mathbb{Z}}
.
The
l_{\infty}
norm of a polynomial
m=\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}m_{i}X^{i}\in R_{q}
is defined as
\lVert m\rVert:=\max_{0\leq i<N}\lvert m_{i}\rvert
.

In what follows, the setup, encryption and decryption algorithms, and homomorphic addition are described.

•

Parameters

(N,q,\sigma)
:
The degree
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
is a power of two and
the modulus
q\in{\mathbb{N}}
is a prime satisfying
1=q\!\!\mod 2N
.
The parameter
\sigma
gives a bound on the error distribution.

•

Key generation
: Generate the secret key
{\mathrm{sk}}\in R_{q}
.

•

Encryption
:
{\mathsf{Enc}}:R_{q}\to R_{q}^{2}

•

Decryption
:
\mathsf{Dec}:R_{q}^{2}\to R_{q}

•

Homomorphic addition
:
\oplus:R_{q}^{2}\times R_{q}^{2}\to R_{q}^{2}

The encryption and decryption algorithms satisfy the
correctness
property, i.e., for any
m\in R_{q}
,

\mathsf{Dec}({\mathsf{Enc}}(m))=m+e\!\!\!\mod q,

(1)

where
e\in R_{q}
is the error polynomial injected during the encryption of the message
m
bounded by
\|e\|\leq\sigma
.
The scheme also satisfies the
additively homomorphic
property, written as

\mathsf{Dec}(\mathbf{c}_{1}\oplus\mathbf{c}_{2})=\mathsf{Dec}(\mathbf{c}_{1})+\mathsf{Dec}(\mathbf{c}_{2})\!\!\mod q,

for any
\mathbf{c}_{1}\in R_{q}^{2}
and
\mathbf{c}_{2}\in R_{q}^{2}
.
The
multiplicatively homomorphic
property will be described in Section
IV
.

In
[
13
]
, a method called “external product”
[
15
]
has been used to perform multiplications over encrypted data.
The external product is a product between a
ciphertext
(encrypted data) in
R_{q}^{2}
and another type of ciphertext generated by the Ring-GSW (Gentry-Sahai-Waters) encryption algorithm
[
15
]
,
which is described below.

•

Parameters

(d,\nu,P)
: Choose
d\in{\mathbb{N}}
and a power of two
\nu\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that
\nu^{d-1}<q\leq\nu^{d}
, and the
special modulus

P\in{\mathbb{N}}
as a prime.

•

Ring-GSW encryption
:
{\mathsf{Enc}}^{\prime}:R_{q}\to R_{qP}^{2\times 2d}

•

External product
:
\boxdot
:
R_{qP}^{2\times 2d}\times R_{q}^{2}\to R_{q}^{2}

For any
m\in R_{q}
and
\mathbf{c}\in R_{q}^{2}
, it holds that

\mathsf{Dec}({\mathsf{Enc}}^{\prime}(m)\boxdot\mathbf{c})=m\cdot\mathsf{Dec}(\mathbf{c})+e\!\!\!\mod q,

(2)

where
e\in R_{q}
is a “newborn” error under the external product bounded by
\|e\|\leq\sigma_{\mathsf{Mult}}:=P^{-1}dN\sigma\nu+(N+1)/2
.
The property (
2
) enables the recursive multiplications on the encrypted state of the controller proposed in
[
13
]
.
The special modulus
P
is used to temporarily “lift” the modulus from
q
to
qP
during the execution of the external product, thus reducing the bound
\sigma_{\mathsf{Mult}}
.

For simplicity, we abuse notation and define
{\mathsf{Enc}}(\cdot)
,
\mathsf{Dec}(\cdot)
, and
\|\cdot\|
component-wisely for vectors throughout the paper.

Recursive Encrypted Controller

In this section, we illustrate the implementation of the encrypted controller proposed in

The encrypted controller enables an unlimited number of recursive homomorphic multiplications (by an encrypted integer matrix) without bootstrapping, which typically incurs high computational cost.
The computation speed can be further accelerated by applying a “coefficient-packing” technique that encodes a vector into a polynomial.

Remark 1
.

To decrease the performance error, the parameters

Non-recursive Encrypted Controller

This section presents an alternative method

]
to encrypt a dynamic controller using Ring-LWE based cryptosystems,

This method
offers a faster computation and

[

However, unlike the method of Section

The implementation is again based on the four-tank system, where the plant and the controller are the same as (

In this example, the BGV scheme

IV-A

Encryption setting

To begin with, we introduce another parameter for encryption, the
plaintext
(message to be encrypted) modulus
p\in{\mathbb{N}}
, which is a prime satisfying
1=p\!\!\mod 2N
.
In the BGV scheme, the plaintext space is
R_{p}
, and the ciphertext space is
R_{q}^{2}
, where
q
is referred as the ciphertext modulus.
Therefore, the encryption and decryption algorithms are defined as
{\mathsf{Enc}}:R_{p}\to R_{q}^{2}
and
\mathsf{Dec}:R_{q}^{2}\to R_{p}
, respectively, throughout this section.
Under the assumption that
p\ll q
, the correctness property is rewritten as

\mathsf{Dec}({\mathsf{Enc}}(m))=m\!\!\!\mod p,\quad\forall m\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}.

The method of
[
14
]
utilizes homomorphic multiplication
\odot:R_{q}^{2}\times R_{q}^{2}\to R_{q}^{3}
,
which increases the dimension of ciphertexts by one.
Accordingly, the homomorphic addition
\oplus
and decryption
\mathsf{Dec}(\cdot)
are also defined over
R_{q}^{3}
.
The multiplicatively homomorphic property holds, i.e.,

\mathsf{Dec}({\mathsf{Enc}}(m_{1})\odot{\mathsf{Enc}}(m_{2}))=m_{1}\cdot m_{2}\!\!\!\mod p,

for any
m_{1}\in R_{p}
and
m_{2}\in R_{p}
.

In this section, we exploit the packing method based on the number-theoretic transform (NTT)
[
19
]
, which transforms a vector in
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{N}
into a polynomial in
R_{p}
and vice versa.
Let the NTT-style packing and unpacking functions be denoted by
\mathsf{Pack}_{\mathsf{ntt}}:{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{N}\to R_{p}
and
\mathsf{Unpack}_{\mathsf{ntt}}:R_{p}\to{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{N}
, respectively.
Then, the followings hold for any
a\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{N}_{p}
and
b\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{N}_{p}
:

\displaystyle\mathsf{Unpack}_{\mathsf{ntt}}(\mathsf{Pack}_{\mathsf{ntt}}(a))

\displaystyle=a\!\!\mod p,

(6)

\displaystyle\mathsf{Unpack}_{\mathsf{ntt}}(\mathsf{Pack}_{\mathsf{ntt}}(a)+\mathsf{Pack}_{\mathsf{ntt}}(b))

\displaystyle=a+b\!\!\mod p,

\displaystyle\mathsf{Unpack}_{\mathsf{ntt}}(\mathsf{Pack}_{\mathsf{ntt}}(a)\cdot\mathsf{Pack}_{\mathsf{ntt}}(b))

\displaystyle=a\circ b\!\!\mod p.

Thus, the element-wise additions and multiplications among vectors can be operated at once over encrypted data.
We abuse notation and apply
\mathsf{Pack}_{\mathsf{ntt}}
to vectors with length shorter than
N
, regarding that zeros are “padded” at the end to fit the length.

Encrypted controller design
[
14
]

The method of
[
14
]
first converts the given controller (
4
) into an equivalent form;

u(t)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}H_{u,i}u(t-i)+H_{y,i}y(t-i),

(7)

where
H_{u,i}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m\times m}
and
H_{y,i}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m\times l}
for
i=1,\,\ldots,\,n
.
The matrices in (
7
) are obtained as

\displaystyle\left[H_{u,n},\,\ldots,\,H_{u,1}\right]

\displaystyle=HF^{n}\mathcal{O}^{\dagger},

\displaystyle\left[H_{y,n},\,\ldots,\,H_{y,1}\right]

\displaystyle=H\left({\mathcal{C}}-F^{n}\mathcal{O}^{\dagger}\mathcal{T}\right),

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}

\displaystyle={\small\begin{bmatrix}H\\
HF\\
\vdots\\
HF^{n-1}\end{bmatrix}},\,\mathcal{T}={\small\begin{bmatrix}0&0&\cdots&0\\
HG&0&\cdots&0\\
\vdots&\ddots&\ddots&\vdots\\
HF^{n-2}G&\cdots&HG&0\end{bmatrix}},

\displaystyle{\mathcal{C}}

\displaystyle={\small\begin{bmatrix}F^{n-1}G&\cdots&FG&G\end{bmatrix}},

with
0
denoting zero matrices of appropriate dimensions.
Furthermore,
to compute
u(t)
from (
7
) for
t=0,\,\ldots,\,n-1
, we obtain the following thanks to
[
14
, Lemma 1]
:

\displaystyle\left[y(-n)^{\top},\,\ldots,\,y(-1)^{\top}\right]^{\top}

\displaystyle={\mathcal{C}}^{\dagger}x^{\mathsf{ini}},

(8)

\displaystyle\left[u(-n)^{\top},\,\ldots,\,u(-1)^{\top}\right]^{\top}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{T}{\mathcal{C}}^{\dagger}x^{\mathsf{ini}}.

This is implemented in
ctrRLWE/conversion.m
.

To make use of the properties (
6
), the matrix-vector multiplications in (
7
) are reformulated, using the fact that

Ab=\text{col}\left\{\left\langle\vect(A^{\top})\circ\left(\mathbf{1}_{m}\otimes b\right),e_{i}\otimes\mathbf{1}_{h}\right\rangle\right\}_{i=1}^{m}

for any
A\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m\times h}
and
b\in{\mathbb{R}}^{h}
,
where
e_{i}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
is the unit vector whose only nonzero element is its
i
-th element.

The matrices in (
7
) are vectorized, quantized, packed, and then encrypted as

\displaystyle\mathbf{H}_{u,i}

\displaystyle:={\mathsf{Enc}}(\mathsf{Pack}_{\mathsf{ntt}}(\left\lceil\vect(H_{u,i}^{\top})/\mathsf{s}\right\rfloor\!\!\!\mod p)),

\displaystyle\mathbf{H}_{y,i}

\displaystyle:={\mathsf{Enc}}(\mathsf{Pack}_{\mathsf{ntt}}(\left\lceil\vect(H_{y,i}^{\top})/\mathsf{s}\right\rfloor\!\!\!\mod p)),

for
i=1,\,\ldots,\,n
.
In
ctrRLWE/main.go
,
\mathbf{H}_{u,n-j}
and
\mathbf{H}_{y,n-j}
correspond to
ctHu[j]
and
ctHy[j]
, respectively, for
j=0,\,\ldots,\,n-1
.

The encrypted controller is designed as

\bar{\mathbf{u}}(t)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{H}_{u,i}\odot\mathbf{u}(t-i)\oplus\mathbf{H}_{y,i}\odot\mathbf{y}(t-i),

(9)

where
\mathbf{y}(t)
and
\mathbf{u}(t)
are inputs from the sensor and the actuator, respectively, defined as

\displaystyle\mathbf{y}(t)

\displaystyle={\mathsf{Enc}}(\mathsf{Pack}_{\mathsf{ntt}}(\left\lceil\mathbf{1}_{m}\otimes y(t)/\mathsf{r}\right\rfloor\!\!\!\mod p)),

(10)

\displaystyle\mathbf{u}(t)

\displaystyle={\mathsf{Enc}}(\mathsf{Pack}_{\mathsf{ntt}}(\left\lceil\mathbf{1}_{m}\otimes u(t)/\mathsf{r}\right\rfloor\!\!\!\mod p)).

The initial input-output trajectories (
8
) are encrypted as in (
10
) during offline.
At the actuator, the plant input is computed as

u(t)=\text{col}\left\{\left\langle\mathsf{r}\mathsf{s}\cdot\mathsf{Unpack}_{\mathsf{ntt}}(\mathsf{Dec}(\bar{\mathbf{u}}(t))),e_{i}\otimes\mathbf{1}_{h}\right\rangle\right\}_{i=1}^{m}.

(11)

In fact, this design requires that all of
\mathbf{1}_{m}\otimes y(t)
,
\mathbf{1}_{m}\otimes u(t)
,
\vect(H_{u,i}^{\top})
’s, and
\vect(H_{y,i}^{\top})
’s have the same length.
Thus, zeros are padded to ensure that they are all of length
mh
, where
h:=\max\{m,\,l\}
.
The function
utils.VecDuplicate
is utilized to generate
\mathbf{1}_{m}\otimes y(t)
and
\mathbf{1}_{m}\otimes u(t)
, with zero padding if necessary.
The vectorized control parameters with zero padding are precomputed in
ctrRLWE/conversion.m
.

V
Conclusion

This paper provides example codes with instructions on implementing Ring-LWE based encrypted controllers
[
13
,
14
]
for a four-tank system using Lattigo.
The codes can be easily customized by readers for application to different systems or approaches.
It is demonstrated that the methods of
[
13
]
and
[
14
]
enable fast implementations of encrypted controllers, taking less than
17
ms on average at each time step for computing the control input of a fourth-order controller.

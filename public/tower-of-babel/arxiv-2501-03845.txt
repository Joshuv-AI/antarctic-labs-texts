Abstract
. This paper establishes optimal existence results and limiting profiles for energy ground states of the quasi-linear Schrödinger equation − Δ ​ u − Δ ⁡ ( | u | 2 ) ​ u + λ ​ u = | u | p − 2 ​ u in ​ ℝ N -\Delta u-\Delta(|u|^{2})u+\lambda u=|u|^{p-2}u\quad\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{N} with prescribed mass ∫ ℝ N | u | 2 = a > 0 \int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{2}=a>0 , in the mass-supercritical case 4 + 4 N < p < 2 ⋅ 2 ∗ 4+\frac{4}{N}<p<2\cdot 2^{*} . Breakthrough in existence theory: For all dimensions N ≥ 1 N\geq 1 , we completely resolve the existence problem: For 1 ≤ N ≤ 4 1\leq N\leq 4 , ground states exist for all a > 0 a>0 . For N ≥ 5 N\geq 5 , there exists a sharp threshold a 0 > 0 a_{0}>0 such that ground states exist if and only if a ≤ a 0 a\leq a_{0} . This constitutes the optimal existence theory, crucially removing the restrictive condition p ≤ 2 ∗ p\leq 2^{*} required in all prior works (which limited results to N ≤ 3 N\leq 3 ). Asymptotic behavior & new phenomena: We provide a complete asymptotic analysis of normalized ground states: As a → 0 + a\to 0^{+} , solutions exhibit a novel connection to Serrin-type overdetermined problems. Through a delicate rescaling, profiles converge to the unique positive radial solution of the overdetermined problem (the first such result for quasi-linear equations). As a → a ∗ a\to a^{*} ( a ∗ = ∞ a^{*}=\infty for N ≤ 4 N\leq 4 ; a ∗ = a 0 a^{*}=a_{0} for N ≥ 5 N\geq 5 ), solutions converge to distinct limiting profiles depending on dimension and nonlinearity. Our methods introduce a new constraint approach and unified variational framework for quasi-linear problems with L 2 L^{2} -constraints.

1.
Introduction

In this paper, we are concerned with quasi-linear Schrödinger equations of the form

(1.1)

\begin{cases}i\partial_{t}\psi+\Delta\psi+\Delta(|\psi|^{2})\psi+|\psi|^{p-2}\psi=0,\penalty\ \hbox{in}\penalty\ \mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{N},\\
\psi(0,x)=\psi_{0}(x),\penalty\ \hbox{in}\penalty\ \mathbb{R}^{N},\end{cases}

where
p\in(2,\frac{4N}{N-2})
if
N\geq 3
and
p\in(2,\infty)
if
N=1,2
,
i
denotes the imaginary unit and the unknown
\psi:\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
is a complex valued function.

Such types of equations appear in various physical fields, for instance in dissipative quantum mechanics, in plasma physics and in fluid mechanics. We refer the readers to
[
14
,
44
]
and their
references for more information on the related physical backgrounds.

From the physical as well as the mathematical point of view, a central issue is the existence and dynamics of standing waves of Eq.(
1.1
).
A standing wave of Eq.(
1.1
) is a solution of the form

\psi(t,x)=e^{i\lambda t}u(x)
, where
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
is a constant and
u
is
a time-independent function. Clearly,
\psi
is a standing wave of
Eq.(
1.1
) if and only if
u
is a solution to

(1.2)

-\Delta u-\Delta(|u|^{2})u+\lambda u=|u|^{p-2}u\ \ \mbox{in}\ \mathbb{R}^{N}.

Over the past two decades, Eq.(
1.2
) has received a great deal of attention. Two distinct paths have been explored, one consisting in studying Eq.(
1.2
) assuming that the frequency
\lambda
is a fixed parameter, the other consisting in searching for solutions to Eq.(
1.2
) having a prescribed
L^{2}
norm.

When
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
appears as a fixed parameter in Eq.(
1.2
), we refer to
[
2
,
11
,
13
,
14
,
24
,
33
,
34
,
35
,
36
,
37
,
38
,
39
,
44
,
47
,
53
,
54
]
and their references therein, for the existence and multiplicity of solutions.
We also refer to
[
1
,
2
,
21
,
48
]
for uniqueness results.
Formally, a solution to Eq.(
1.2
) corresponds to a critical point of the
action functional

I_{\lambda}(u):=\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}+V(u)+\frac{\lambda}{2}\|u\|_{2}^{2}-\frac{1}{p}\|u\|_{p}^{p},

defined on the natural space

X:=\left\{u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\Big|V(u):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u^{2}|\nabla u|^{2}\mathrm{d}x<\infty\right\}.

Indeed, it is easy to check that
u
is a weak solution of Eq.(
1.2
) if and only if

I^{\prime}_{\lambda}(u)\phi:=\lim_{t\rightarrow 0^{+}}\frac{I_{\lambda}(u+t\phi)-I_{\lambda}(u)}{t}=0,

for every direction
\phi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N},\mathbb{R})
. However, note that
I_{\lambda}
is not differentiable in space
X
when
N\geq 2
, due to the presence of the term
u\Delta(|u|^{2})
, see
[
9
, page 4]
. To overcome this difficulty, various arguments have been developed. First in
[
33
,
36
]
, solutions to Eq.(
1.2
) are obtained by minimizing the functional
I_{\lambda}
on the constraint

\displaystyle\left\{u\in X\Big|\penalty\ \penalty\ \|u\|_{p}^{p}=1\right\}
.
Then the non-differentiability of
I_{\lambda}
essentially does not come into play.

Alternatively, in
[
13
,
41
]
, by a change of unknown, Eq.(
1.2
) is transformed into a semi-linear one, see Subsection
3.2
for more details. One then speaks of the dual problem. For this semi-linear equation standard variational methods can be applied to yield a solution. Also in
[
42
]
the authors have developed an approach which works for more general quasi-linear equations and reduces the search of solutions to Eq.(
1.2
) to the problem of showing that

I_{\lambda}
has a global minimizer on a Nehari manifold.
Since this pioneering work, the study of Eq.(
1.2
) has been the subject of an abundant literature that addresses questions of multiplicity, concentration and critical exponent.
In particular, starting from
[
38
]
, in a series of papers
[
34
,
37
,
39
,
40
]
Liu, Liu and Wang have developed a perturbation method for studying the existence and multiplicity of solutions for a general class of quasi-linear elliptic equations including the above discussed model equations. Finally, we mention the recent work
[
19
]
where it is proved, that for each
\lambda>0
, there exists a unique positive solution to Eq.(
1.2
). In addition, it is non-degenerate, and lie on a
C^{1}
branch.

In this paper, we consider the other approach and search for solutions
u\in X
to Eq.(
1.2
) with a prescribed mass. Namely, such that

(1.3)

u\in\mathcal{S}_{a}:=\left\{u\in X\Big|\penalty\ \penalty\ \|u\|_{2}^{2}=a\right\}

where
a>0
is given.
Then the frequency
\lambda
will appear as a Lagrange multiplier. This kind of solution is called a normalized solution in the literature and the search for it is motivated by the fact that the
L^{2}
norm has, depending on the underlying model, a strong physical meaning.
Solutions to Eq.(
1.2
)-Eq.(
1.3
) can be obtained as critical points
of the
energy functional

I(u)=\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}+V(u)-\frac{1}{p}\|u\|_{p}^{p}

constrained on
\mathcal{S}_{a}
.
Due to a Gagliardo-Nirenberg
type inequality, see Eq.(
3.1
), the
functional
I
is bounded from below on
\mathcal{S}_{a}
if
p<4+\frac{4}{N}
and
is unbounded from below on
\mathcal{S}_{a}
if
p>4+\frac{4}{N}
. The threshold
number
4+\frac{4}{N}
is called the mass critical exponent for
Eq.(
1.2
) in the literature.

Along this approach a natural notion is the one of
least energy solution
. A
least energy solution
is a solution
u\in\mathcal{S}_{a}
that minimizes the
energy functional

I
among all solutions to Eq.(
1.2
) subject to the mass constraint Eq.(
1.3
).
This definition remains meaningful even when
I
is unbounded below on
\mathcal{S}_{a}
. Moreover, when
I
is bounded below on
\mathcal{S}_{a}
, it makes sense even if the
\inf_{u\in\mathcal{S}_{a}}I(u)
is not attained.

The mass subcritical case (i.e.,
2<p<4+\frac{4}{N}
) has now been widely studied. In
[
14
,
26
]

least energy solutions
are obtained by minimizing the functional
I
on
\mathcal{S}_{a}
, see
[
27
, Lemma 1.1]
for results in that direction. Critical points which are not global minimum have also been obtained.
In
[
27
]
, for
2+\frac{4}{N}<p<4+\frac{4}{N}
, a perturbation method is applied to study the existence and multiplicity for suitable range of
a
.
In
[
56
]
, the authors adopted the dual approach to construct infinitely many pairs of normalized solutions when
2<p<2+\frac{4}{N}
. Also, when
2+\frac{4}{N}\leq p<4+\frac{4}{N}
,
k
pairs of normalized solutions were obtained in
[
56
]
, for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
, assuming that
a>a_{k}
for some
a_{k}
.

In the mass critical case (i.e.,
p=4+\frac{4}{N}
), the infimum of
I
on
\mathcal{S}_{a}
is never achieved but, for
a>0
sufficiently large, one may still find a
least energy solution

[
55
]
. Also, still for
a>0
sufficiently large, the existence (of mountain pass solution) and concentration behavior of normalized solutions of Eq.(
1.2
)-Eq.(
1.3
) are established in
[
30
]
.

The mass supercritical
case (i.e.,
4+\frac{4}{N}<p<\frac{4N}{N-2}
), is still essentially untouched. In the works

[
30
,
43
]
, existence and multiplicity results for Eq.(
1.2
)-Eq.(
1.3
) are obtained using a perturbation method. In
[
55
]
, the authors prove the existence of
least energy solutions
by minimizing the functional
I\big|_{\mathcal{S}_{a}}
on the so-called Pohozaev manifold

\mathcal{P}:=\{u\in X\backslash\{0\}:P(u)=0\},

where

P(u):=\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}+(N+2)V(u)-\frac{(p-2)N}{2p}\|u\|_{p}^{p}.

As we shall see,
\mathcal{P}
is a natural constraint which contains all the critical points of
I
constrained to
\mathcal{S}_{a}
.

However, all previous studies
[
30
,
55
,
43
]
require the restrictive assumption
p\leq 2^{*}
(or
p<2^{*}
). This limitation has critical consequences:

•

Results are confined to dimensions
N\leq 3
.

•

Even for
N=3
, the full exponent range
4+\frac{4}{N}<p<2\cdot 2^{*}=\frac{4N}{N-2}
remains inaccessible.

The fundamental barrier stems from the necessity in
[
30
,
55
,
43
]
to ensure the strict positivity of the Lagrange multiplier
\lambda
(a crucial element for controlling potential compactness loss). Specifically, their analysis relies exclusively on two identities for solutions
u
:

(1.4)

\begin{cases}\text{Nehari identity:}&\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}+4V(u)+\lambda\|u\|_{2}^{2}=\|u\|_{p}^{p},\\
\text{Pohozaev identity:}&P(u)=0.\end{cases}

Combining these yields the expression for
\lambda
:

(1.5)

\begin{split}\lambda\|u\|_{2}^{2}&=\|u\|_{p}^{p}-\left[\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}+4V(u)\right]\\
&=\frac{2p}{(p-2)N}\left[\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}+(N+2)V(u)\right]-\left[\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}+4V(u)\right]\\
&=\underbrace{\frac{2p-(p-2)N}{(p-2)N}}_{\text{require $\geq 0$}}\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{2p(N+2)-4(p-2)N}{(p-2)N}V(u).\end{split}

The non-negativity requirement for the coefficient
\frac{2p-(p-2)N}{(p-2)N}
forces
p\leq 2^{*}
.

Our fundamental breakthrough:
Focusing on the existence of
energy ground states
, namely
least energy solutions
which minimizes

I\big|_{\mathcal{S}_{a}}
on
\mathcal{P}
,
this work eliminates this long-standing restriction on
p
. We establish comprehensive results for:

•

All dimensions

N\geq 1
.

•

The
complete range

p\in\left(4+\frac{4}{N},2\cdot 2^{*}\right)
.

Additionally, we conduct a thorough asymptotic analysis of
energy ground states
as their
L^{2}
-norm approaches critical values (
a\to 0^{+}
and
a\to a^{*}
where
a^{*}=\infty
for
N\leq 4
,
a^{*}=a_{0}
for
N\geq 5
).

2.
Main results

2.1.
Existence of energy ground states

We will work on the space
X
.
Note that
X
is not a vector space since it is not closed under the
sum. Nevertheless,
X
is a complete metric space with the distance

\mathrm{d}_{X}(u,v)=\|u-v\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}+\|\nabla(u^{2})-\nabla(v^{2})\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}
.
Due to the presence of
V(u)
,
I
is not differentiable in
X
when
N\geq 2
, and powerful variational techniques, such as those involving Palais-Smale sequences, cannot be used.
To remedy this, inspired by
[
55
]
, we develop a minimization approach that uses only elementary arguments and prove, a posteriori, that a minimum of
I
on
\mathcal{P}\cap\mathcal{S}_{a}
is indeed a critical point of
I
restricted to
\mathcal{S}_{a}
. However, the strategy of the proofs in
[
55
]
and in the present work is essentially distinct.

Instead of working directly on the constraint
\mathcal{P}\cap\mathcal{S}_{a}
, as in
[
55
]
, inspired by the work
[
29
]
, see also
[
12
,
16
,
7
]
for the introduction of this strategy, we first consider a relaxed problem. For any
a>0
, we introduce the set

D_{a}:=\{u\in X:\|u\|_{2}^{2}\leq a\}

and we define
\mathcal{P}_{a}:=\mathcal{P}\cap D_{a}.

The set
\mathcal{P}_{a}
is a natural constraint since, as a consequence of a Pohozaev identity, any non-trivial critical point of
I
constrained to
D_{a}
belongs to
\mathcal{P}_{a}
.

The relaxed problem we will consider is:

(2.1)

\displaystyle\mbox{to find a minimizer}\penalty\ u\in\mathcal{P}_{a}\mbox{ of }M_{a}\mbox{ where }M_{a}:=\inf_{\mathcal{P}_{a}}I(u).

By Lemma
4.9
, a minimizer of
M_{a}
will prove to be a critical point of
I_{|_{D_{a}}}
and, by the above consideration, it will minimize
I
among all non-trivial critical points of
I_{|_{D_{a}}}
.

Once this problem is settled, we will show that
u\in\mathcal{S}_{a}
, and thus that it is an
energy ground state
for the initial problem.

Here comes our first main result.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
1\leq N\leq 4
and assume that
p\in(4+\frac{4}{N},2\cdot 2^{*})
, then the following conclusions hold true.

(i)

M_{a}
is continuous in
(0,+\infty)
;

(ii)

M_{a}
decreases strictly in
(0,+\infty)
;

(iii)

Eq.(
1.2
)-Eq.(
1.3
) has an energy ground state and, up to translation and sign change, any energy ground state is positive, radial, and strictly decreasing with respect to
r=|x|
. In addition, the associated Lagrange parameter is positive.

For
1\leq N\leq 4
, by Theorem
2.1
above, the existence of an
energy ground state
holds for all prescribed mass
a>0
. The situation when
N\geq 5
is different. In order to present it, we first recall the following result that can be found in

Remark 2.6

Having proved the existence of energy ground states, a natural question is the orbital stability, or instability, of the associated standing waves in Eq.(

Remark 2.7
.

Among the solutions to Eq.(

2.2.
Limiting profile of the energy ground states

In
[
19
]
, the question of the orbital stability of the
action ground states
is addressed, in the spirit of the work of Grillakis-Shatah-Strauss

[
22
]
. This is possible because, for any
\lambda\in(0,\infty)
, the unique
action ground state

u_{\lambda}
lies on a
C^{1}
curve. From
[
22
]
it is known that the standing wave is expected to be orbitally stable if
\lambda\mapsto||u_{\lambda}||_{2}
is strictly increasing and unstable when
\lambda\mapsto||u_{\lambda}||_{2}
is strictly decreasing. Because the full problem of stability, namely of
u_{\lambda}
for any
\lambda\in(0,\infty)
, seems out of reach so far, one may consider what happens in some limiting regimes. In
[
19
]
a very complete description of the behavior of
\lambda\mapsto||u_{\lambda}||_{2}
is given in the limit
\lambda\to 0^{+}
. To study the behavior of this function a key step is to identify a limit equation, having a unique solution, to which
u_{\lambda}
, or some rescaling of it, converge.

Motivated, in particular, by the work
[
19
]
, we now consider what happens to our
energy ground states
in the limit
a\to 0^{+}
and
a\to a^{*}
respectively, where

(2.4)

a^{*}:=\begin{cases}+\infty,\quad&\hbox{if}\penalty\ 1\leq N\leq 4,\\
a_{0},\quad&\hbox{if}\penalty\ N\geq 5.\end{cases}

First we consider the case where the mass
a
goes to zero.

Theorem 2.8
.

Assume that
N\geq 1,

p\in(4+\frac{4}{N},2\cdot 2^{*})
.
Let
\{u_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
,
u_{n}\in\mathcal{S}_{a_{n}}
, be a sequence of energy ground states to Eq.(
1.2
)-Eq.(
1.3
) with
a_{n}\rightarrow 0^{+}
. Then the associated Lagrange multiplier satisfies
\lambda_{n}\rightarrow+\infty
as
n\rightarrow\infty
. Furthermore, defining

v_{n}=\int_{0}^{u_{n}}\sqrt{1+2t^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}t=\frac{1}{2}u_{n}\sqrt{1+2u_{n}^{2}}+\frac{\sqrt{2}}{4}\ln\big(\sqrt{2}u_{n}+\sqrt{1+2u_{n}^{2}}\big)

and

\displaystyle\tilde{v}_{n}(x):=\lambda_{n}^{-\frac{2}{p-2}}v_{n}\left(\frac{x}{\lambda_{n}^{\frac{p-4}{2(p-2)}}}\right),

it holds that, up to a subsequence,
\tilde{v}_{n}\rightarrow\tilde{v}
in
C^{1,\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\cap C_{\text{loc}}^{2}(\Omega)
with
\Omega:=\text{supp}\penalty\ \tilde{v}
, where
\tilde{v}
is the unique positive radial solution to the following overdetermined problem

(2.5)

\begin{cases}-\Delta\tilde{v}=-\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}+2^{\frac{p-4}{4}}\tilde{v}^{\frac{p-2}{2}},\\
\tilde{v}>0\penalty\ \hbox{in}\penalty\ \Omega,\penalty\ \penalty\ \tilde{v}=\frac{\partial\tilde{v}}{\partial\nu}=0\penalty\ \hbox{on}\penalty\ \partial\Omega,\\
\hbox{where $\Omega=B_{R}$ is an open ball in $\mathbb{R}^{N}$ with radius $R>0$}.\end{cases}

Precisely, there exists some
R>0
such that
\tilde{v}
is supported in
B_{R}
and it satisfies the above Dirichlet-Neumann boundary problem.

The uniqueness of the positive radial solutions to the overdetermined Problem (
2.5
) can be deduced from existing results. When
N\geq 3
, Problem (
2.5
) is a special case of the general result presented in
[
49
, Theorem 2]
. When
N=1,2
we make use of the very recent result
[
45
, Theorem 1.4]
.

Regarding the convergence directly on
u_{n}
-functions, we establish the following result.

Theorem 2.9
.

Assume that
N\geq 1,

p\in(4+\frac{4}{N},2\cdot 2^{*})
. As

, let

3.1.
A Gagliardo-Nirenberg type inequality

We recall the following Gagliardo-Nirenberg type inequality: There is some positive constant
C(N,p)
such that

(3.1)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{p}\,{\rm d}x\leq C(N,p)\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u^{2}\,{\rm d}x\right)^{\frac{4N-p(N-2)}{2(N+2)}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{2}\,|\nabla u|^{2}{\rm d}x\right)^{\frac{N(p-2)}{2(N+2)}},

for
u\in X
and
p\in(2,2\cdot 2^{*})
.

For a proof of Eq.(
3.1
) we refer to
[
14
, Lemma 4.2]
or to
[
19
, Lemma 4.14]
.

3.2.
Two equivalent problems

It has been observed
that the search of non-negative solutions to Eq.(
1.2
) is equivalent to the search of non-negative solutions to

(3.2)

-\Delta v=-\lambda\varphi(v)\varphi^{\prime}(v)+\varphi(v)^{p-1}\varphi^{\prime}(v)\penalty\ \hbox{in}\penalty\ \mathbb{R}^{N},

where
\varphi
is the unique solution of the Cauchy problem

\varphi^{\prime}(s)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+2\varphi^{2}(s)}},\quad\varphi(0)=0

on
[0,+\infty)
and
\varphi(t)=-\varphi(-t)
on
(-\infty,0].

Indeed, by
[
13
]
it is known that any non-negative solution
v\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
of Eq.(
3.2
) gives rise to a non-negative solution
u:=\varphi(v)
of Eq.(
1.2
). Reciprocally, from
[
1
, Lemmas 2.6, 2.8, 2.10]
we know that for any non-negative solution
u
of Eq.(
1.2
),
v:=\varphi^{-1}(u)
is a non-negative solution of Eq.(
3.2
).

For future reference we recall some properties of
\varphi
. First note that

(3.3)

\varphi^{\prime}(0)=\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{\varphi(t)}{t}=1,\ \ \lim_{t\to+\infty}\frac{\varphi(t)}{\sqrt{t}}=2^{\frac{1}{4}},

and for
t\in\mathbb{R}

(3.4)

0<\varphi^{\prime}(t)\leq 1,\ \ |\varphi(t)|\leq\min\big\{|t|,\,2^{\frac{1}{4}}\sqrt{|t|}\big\},\ \ \frac{1}{2}\varphi^{2}(t)\leq\varphi(t)\varphi^{\prime}(t)t\leq\varphi^{2}(t).

See
[
13
, Lemma 1.1 and Lemma 1.2]
for a proof of Eq.(
3.3
) and Eq.(
3.4
).

Since
u=\varphi(v)
, clearly
u\rightarrow+\infty
if and only if
v\rightarrow+\infty
. So, for
u\rightarrow+\infty
(or saying
v\rightarrow+\infty
), we have that

\sqrt{1+2u^{2}}\sim\sqrt{2}u=\sqrt{2}\varphi(v)\sim 2^{\frac{3}{4}}v^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Now, since

1=\varphi^{\prime}(v)\frac{\mathrm{d}v}{\mathrm{d}u}=\varphi^{\prime}(v)\sqrt{1+2u^{2}},

it follows that

(3.5)

\lim_{s\rightarrow+\infty}\varphi^{\prime}(s)s^{\frac{1}{2}}=2^{-\frac{3}{4}}.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
v\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\backslash\{0\}
be a non-negative solution to Eq.(
3.2
). Then
v\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and
v>0
. In addition,
if
\lambda>0
, then up to a translation,
v
is radial and strictly decreasing w.r.t.
r=|x|
.

Proof.

By Moser iteration,
v\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, so the right-hand side of Eq.(
3.2
) is in
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Standard elliptic regularity yields
v\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, and the strong maximum principle implies
v>0
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
.
Now define
f(s):=-\lambda\varphi(s)+\varphi(s)^{p-1}\varphi^{\prime}(s)
. Assuming
\lambda>0
, one can check that
f^{\prime}(s)=-\lambda(1+o(s))<0

for sufficiently small
s>0
. So, if
N\geq 2
, by
[
31
, Theorem 1]
, up to a translation, all positive classical solutions to Eq.(
3.2
) are radial and decrease strictly with respect to
r=|x|
.
If
N=1
, by
p>4+\frac{4}{N}
, it is easy to see that

(3.6)

\xi_{0}:=\inf\{\xi>0;F(\xi)=0\}\penalty\ \hbox{exists, and}\penalty\ \xi_{0}>0.

By the necessary condition in
[
3
, Theorem 5]
(due to the existence of
v
), we have that
f(\xi_{0})>0
. Then by
[
3
, Theorem 5]
again, Eq.(
3.2
) has, up to translations, a unique positive solution. This solution is radial with
u(0)=\xi_{0}
and
u^{\prime}(x)<0,x>0
.
∎

and Theorem

4.1.
Pohozaev type constraint

For any critical point
u\neq 0
of
I\big|_{D_{a}}
, it is standard to show that
u\in\mathcal{P}_{a}
. Recalling the fiber map
u\mapsto t\star u
,

(t\star u)(x):=t^{\frac{N}{2}}u(tx),\quad t>0,

which preserves the
L^{2}
-norm, one can check that

P(u)=\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}I(t\star u)\Big|_{t=1}
.
We decompose
\mathcal{P}=\mathcal{P}_{+}\cup\mathcal{P}_{0}\cup\mathcal{P}_{-},

where

\mathcal{P}_{+(resp.0,-)}:=\left\{u\in\mathcal{P}:\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}}{\mathrm{d}t^{2}}I(t\star u)\Big|_{t=1}>0(resp.=0,<0)\right\},

and by a direct calculation

\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}}{\mathrm{d}t^{2}}I(t\star u)\Big|_{t=1}\ =\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}+(N+2)(N+1)V(u)-\frac{(p-2)N[(p-2)N-2]}{4p}\|u\|_{p}^{p}.

For any
u\in\mathcal{P}_{a}
, we also note that

(4.1)

I(u)=\frac{(p-2)N-4}{2(p-2)N}\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{(p-4)N-4}{(p-2)N}V(u).

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
A,B,C>0,\,\sigma>N+2
,
h(t):=At^{2}+Bt^{N+2}-Ct^{\sigma},\,t>0
. Then
h
has a unique positive critical point
t_{0}
such that
h(t_{0})=\max_{t>0}h(t)

and
h^{\prime\prime}(t_{0})<0
.

Proof.

Suppose there exists some
t>0
such that
h^{\prime}(t)=0
and
h^{\prime\prime}(t)\geq 0
, i.e.,

2At+(N+2)Bt^{N+1}=C\sigma t^{\sigma-1}\mbox{ and }2A+(N+2)(N+1)Bt^{N}\geq C\sigma(\sigma-1)t^{\sigma-2}.

By a direct computation,

A\leq\frac{(N+2-\sigma)\sigma C}{2N}t^{\sigma-2}.

Noting that
\sigma>N+2
and
C>0
, we see that
A<0
, a contradiction to the fact
A>0
.

Consequently,
h(t)
has no local minimizer. Noting that
h(t)>0
for
t>0
small, and
h(t)\rightarrow-\infty
as
t\rightarrow\infty
, we conclude that
\max_{t>0}h(t)
is achieved at some
t_{0}>0
. If
h(t)
has at least two critical points, then it must have a local minimizer, a contradiction.
∎

Corollary 4.2
.

Let
N\geq 1
and
4+\frac{4}{N}<p<2\cdot 2^{*}
. Then for any
0\neq u\in D_{a}
, there exists a unique
t=t_{u}>0
such that
t\star u\in\mathcal{P}_{a}
. Furthermore,
t_{u}<(resp.=,>)1
if and only if
P(u)<(resp.=,>)0
. In particular,
\mathcal{P}_{a}=\mathcal{P}_{-}\cap D_{a}
.

Proof.

We have
t\star u\in D_{a}
for all
t>0
. By a direct computation,

I(t\star u)=\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}t^{2}+V(u)t^{N+2}-\frac{1}{p}\|u\|_{p}^{p}t^{\frac{(p-2)N}{2}}.

Noting that
\frac{(p-2)N}{2}>N+2
due to
p>4+\frac{4}{N}
, let us define
h(t):=At^{2}+Bt^{N+2}-Ct^{\sigma}
with

A:=\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2},

B:=V(u),C:=\frac{1}{p}\|u\|_{p}^{p},

\sigma:=\frac{(p-2)N}{2}.

Then
t\star u\in\mathcal{P}_{a}
if and only if
\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}I(t\star u)=0
, i.e.,
h^{\prime}(t)=0
. By Lemma
4.1
, we obtain the uniqueness of
t=t_{u}
. Furthermore,
t_{u}
attains the maximum of
h(t)
in
t\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
and

h^{\prime}(t)>0
for
0<t<t_{u}
while
h^{\prime}(t)<0
for
t>t_{u}.

So combining with
P(u)=h^{\prime}(1)
, we obtain that

P(u)<(resp.=,>)0\Leftrightarrow h^{\prime}(1)<(resp.=,>)0\Leftrightarrow t_{u}<(resp.=,>)1.

∎

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
N\geq 1
and
4+\frac{4}{N}<p<2\cdot 2^{*}
.
There exists
C_{N,p}>0
such that,

\inf_{u\in\mathcal{P}_{a}}V(u)\geq C_{N,p}a^{\frac{(N-2)p-4N}{(p-4)N-4}}=:\delta_{a}>0,\quad\mbox{for any }a>0.

Proof.

Since
p<2\cdot 2^{*}=\frac{4N}{(N-2)_{+}}
, it holds that
\frac{4N-p(N-2)}{2(N+2)}>0
. So for any
u\in D_{a}
,
recalling Eq.(
3.1
), we have

\|u\|_{p}^{p}\leq C(N,p)a^{\frac{4N-p(N-2)}{2(N+2)}}\left(V(u)\right)^{\frac{N(p-2)}{2(N+2)}}.

Then, for any
u\in\mathcal{P}_{a}
, it holds that

V(u)<\frac{1}{N+2}\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}+V(u)=\frac{(p-2)N}{2p(N+2)}\|u\|_{p}^{p}\leq\frac{(p-2)N}{2p(N+2)}C(N,p)a^{\frac{4N-p(N-2)}{2(N+2)}}\left(V(u)\right)^{\frac{N(p-2)}{2(N+2)}}.

Since
p>4+\frac{4}{N}
, one has
\frac{N(p-2)}{2(N+2)}>1
and it follows from the above inequality that

\displaystyle V(u)\geq

\displaystyle\left[\frac{2p(N+2)}{(p-2)N}\frac{1}{C(N,p)}a^{\frac{p(N-2)-4N}{2(N+2)}}\right]^{\frac{2(N+2)}{N(p-2)-2(N+2)}}=:C_{N,p}a^{\frac{(N-2)p-4N}{(p-4)N-4}}.

∎

For any
u\in\mathcal{P}_{a}
, let
u^{*}
be the Schwarz symmetrization of
u
.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
N\geq 1
and
4+\frac{4}{N}<p<2\cdot 2^{*}
. For any
u\in\mathcal{P}_{a}
, there exists a unique
t=t_{u^{*}}\in(0,1]
such that
t\star u^{*}\in\mathcal{P}_{a}
and
I(t\star u^{*})\leq I(u)
.

Proof.

By
\|u^{*}\|_{2}^{2}=\|u\|_{2}^{2}
, we see that
u^{*}\in D_{a}\backslash\{0\}
. Then by Corollary
4.2
, there exists a unique
t=t_{u^{*}}>0
such that
t\star u^{*}\in\mathcal{P}_{a}
. In particular, by the properties of rearrangement,

\|\nabla u^{*}\|_{2}^{2}\leq\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2},\,V(u^{*})=\frac{1}{4}\|\nabla(u^{*})^{2}\|_{2}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{4}\|\nabla(u^{2})\|_{2}^{2}=V(u),\,\|u^{*}\|_{p}^{p}=\|u\|_{p}^{p},

where we have used that
(u^{2})^{*}=(u^{*})^{2}
.
This implies that
P(u^{*})\leq P(u)=0
. Hence, by Corollary
4.2
again,
t_{u^{*}}\leq 1
. We remark that for any
u\neq 0
,
I(u^{*})\leq I(u)
. Combined with the fact that

s\star u^{*}=(s\star u)^{*}\penalty\ \hbox{for all}\penalty\ s\in\mathbb{R}^{+},

we finally obtain

(4.2)

I(t\star u^{*})=I((t\star u)^{*})\leq I(t\star u)\leq\max_{s>0}I(s\star u)=I(u),

proving the lemma.
∎

4.2.
Properties of

Combining Eq.(

) with Lemma

, one see that

(4.3)

M_{a}>\frac{(p-4)N-4}{(p-2)N}V(u)\geq\frac{(p-4)N-4}{(p-2)N}C_{N,p}a^{\frac{(N-2)p-4N}{(p-4)N-4}}>0.

For any

is decreasing in

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
N\geq 1
and
4+\frac{4}{N}<p<2\cdot 2^{*}
. The function
a\in(0,+\infty)\mapsto M_{a}
is continuous.

Proof.

Since
M_{a}
is decreasing with respect to

, it suffices to prove that
M_{a,-}=M_{a}=M_{a,+}
. Let

.

Firstly, we consider

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}M_{a_{n}}\geq M_{a}
due to
M_{a_{n}}\geq M_{a},\forall n\in\mathbb{N}
. We only need to prove that
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}M_{a_{n}}\leq M_{a}+\varepsilon
for any
\varepsilon>0
. So, for any
\varepsilon>0
, let

be such that

and define

A:=\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2},\,B:=V(u),\,C:=\frac{1}{p}\|u\|_{p}^{p},\,\sigma:=\frac{(p-2)N}{2}.

Since

, we have that

(4.4)

2A+(N+2)B-\sigma C=0.

Define
u_{n}:=\sqrt{\frac{a_{n}}{a}}u

u_{n}\in D_{a_{n}}
.
Let

be such that

,

2A_{n}+(N+2)B_{n}t_{n}^{N}-\sigma C_{n}t_{n}^{\sigma-2}=0,

\begin{cases}A_{n}:=\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla u_{n}\|_{2}^{2}=\frac{1}{2}\frac{a_{n}}{a}\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}\rightarrow A,\\
B_{n}:=V(u_{n})=\frac{a_{n}^{2}}{a^{2}}V(u)\rightarrow B,\\

By Eq.(
4.5
) and

, we see that
\{t_{n}\}
is bounded. So, passing to a subsequence if necessary, we may assume that
t_{n}\rightarrow t_{0}
. Then, by letting

in Eq.(
4.5
), we have

(4.6)

2A+(N+2)Bt_{0}^{N}-\sigma Ct_{0}^{\sigma-2}=0,

which implies that

is a critical point of
h(t):=At^{2}+Bt^{N+2}-Ct^{\sigma}
. So by Lemma

again, combining Eq.(

) and Eq.(

), we conclude that

.
Hence,

\displaystyle M_{a_{n}}:=

\displaystyle\inf_{v\in\mathcal{P}_{a_{n}}}I(v)\leq I\left(t_{n}\star\sqrt{\frac{a_{n}}{a}}u\right)\rightarrow I(u)\leq M_{a}+\varepsilon,

proving that
M_{a,-}=M_{a}
.

Next, we consider
a_{n}\downarrow a
. Note that
M_{a,+}
exists and
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}M_{a_{n}}\leq M_{a}
. Suppose there exists some

such that

M_{a_{n}}<M_{a}-\delta,\,\forall n\in\mathbb{N}
and let

be such that

Then we have

(4.7)

I(u_{n})\leq M_{a}-\frac{\delta}{2},\forall n\in\mathbb{N}.

Without loss of generality, since
a_{n}\downarrow a
, we may assume that
a_{n}\leq 2a,\forall n\in\mathbb{N}
.
So,
\{u_{n}\}\subset\mathcal{P}_{2a}
with

and we deduce from Eq.(

) that
\{u_{n}\}
is bounded in
X
. Then by
[
10
, Proposition 1.7.1]
and
[
3
, Theorem A.I]
, there exists a subsequence, still denote by
\{u_{n}\}
, such that
u_{n}\rightharpoonup u
in

(4.8)

\begin{cases}\|u\|_{2}^{2}\leq\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|u_{n}\|_{2}^{2},&\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}\leq\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|\nabla u_{n}\|_{2}^{2},\\

We claim that

. If not, by

Then we have from Eq.(

) and Eq. (

\displaystyle M_{a}=\inf_{v\in\mathcal{P}_{a}}I(v)\leq I(t\star u)=

\displaystyle\frac{(p-2)N-4}{2(p-2)N}\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}t^{2}+\frac{(p-4)N-4}{(p-2)N}V(u)t^{N+2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{(p-2)N-4}{2(p-2)N}\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{(p-4)N-4}{(p-2)N}V(u)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{(p-2)N-4}{2(p-2)N}\|\nabla u_{n}\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{(p-4)N-4}{(p-2)N}V(u_{n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}I(u_{n})\penalty\ \hbox{(since $u_{n}\in\mathcal{P}_{2a}$)},

which is a contradiction in view of Eq.(

Lemma 4.7

Let

Proof.

Let

and

\displaystyle h_{a}(t):=A_{a}t^{2}+B_{a}t^{N+2}-C_{a}t^{\sigma}\penalty\ \hbox{with}\penalty\ \sigma=\frac{(p-2)N}{2}.

\mathbb{R}^{+}

is,

(4.9)

\displaystyle 0=

\displaystyle\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}+(N+2)V(u)at_{a}^{N}-\frac{\sigma}{p}\|u\|_{p}^{p}a^{\frac{p-2}{2}}t_{a}^{\sigma-2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}+(N+2)V(u)at_{a}^{N}-\frac{\sigma}{p}\|u\|_{p}^{p}a^{\frac{2}{N}}(at_{a}^{N})^{\frac{\sigma-2}{N}}.

Since

4.9

So combining Eq.(
4.1
) and Eq. (

\displaystyle 0<M_{a}=\inf_{v\in\mathcal{P}_{a}}I(v)\leq I(t_{a}\star u_{a})=

\displaystyle\frac{(p-2)N-4}{2(p-2)N}\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}at_{a}^{2}+\frac{(p-4)N-4}{(p-2)N}V(u)a^{2}t_{a}^{N+2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle O(a^{\frac{p}{2}-\frac{(p-2)\sigma}{2(\sigma-2)}})\penalty\ \hbox{due to $\frac{p}{2}-\frac{(p-2)\sigma}{2(\sigma-2)}>2-\frac{(N+2)(p-2)}{2(\sigma-2)}$}.

Lemma 4.11

. If we manage to prove that
v_{1}
is, up to translation, radial it will also be the case for
u_{1}
. To this aim we shall prove that
v_{1}=v_{1}^{*}(=v_{0})
.
A standard elliptic estimate shows that

v_{1}(x)\to 0
as
|x|\to\infty
. Consequently, for every
t>0
, the function

(v_{1}-t)_{+}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and has compact support.

Using the layer-cake representation, see for example
[
32
, Theorem 1.13]
, and the equimeasurability of
v_{1}
and
v_{0}
, we obtain, for any
t>0
,

(A.2)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}h(v_{1})(v_{1}-t)_{+}\,\mathrm{d}x=\int_{t}^{\infty}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\tau}\left[h(\tau)(\tau-t)\right]\,|\{v_{1}>\tau\}|\,\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle=\int_{t}^{\infty}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\tau}\left[h(\tau)(\tau-t)\right]\,|\{v_{1}^{*}>\tau\}|\,\mathrm{d}\tau=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}h(v_{0})(v_{0}-t)_{+}\,\mathrm{d}x.

Multiplying the Eq.(
A.1
) for
v_{1}
by
(v_{1}-t)_{+}
and integrating by parts gives

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\nabla v_{1}\cdot\nabla(v_{1}-t)_{+}\,\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}h(v_{1})(v_{1}-t)_{+}\,\mathrm{d}x.

Performing the same computation for
v_{0}
and using Eq.(
A.2
) yields

(A.3)

\displaystyle\int_{\{v_{0}>t\}}|\nabla v_{0}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\nabla v_{0}\cdot\nabla(v_{0}-t)_{+}\,\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}h(v_{0})(v_{0}-t)_{+}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}h(v_{1})(v_{1}-t)_{+}\,\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\{v_{1}>t\}}|\nabla v_{1}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x,\qquad\forall\,0\leq t<M.

Thus,

(A.4)

\|\nabla(v_{1}-t)_{+}\|_{2}=\|\nabla(v_{0}-t)_{+}\|_{2}=\|\nabla(v_{1}^{*}-t)_{+}\|_{2}=\|\nabla(v_{1}-t)_{+}^{*}\|_{2},\quad\forall\,0\leq t<M.

Introduce the notation

E_{t}:=\{v_{1}>t\},\penalty\ E_{t}^{*}:=\{v_{0}>t\}
.
For any
t>0
, since
(v_{1}-t)_{+}
has compact support and Eq.(
A.4
) holds,

[
8
, Theorem 1.1]
implies that there exists a vector
c(t)\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
such that
E_{t}=E_{t}^{*}+c(t)
.
In particular, for every
\delta\in(0,M-t)
, the set
\{(v_{1}-t)_{+}>\delta\}
shares the same center
c(t)
.

Now observe that

\big((v_{1}-t)_{+}-(s-t)\big)_{+}=(v_{1}-s)_{+},\qquad\forall\,0<t<s<M.

Taking
\delta:=s-t\in(0,M-t)
, we obtain

E_{s}=\{v_{1}>s\}=\{(v_{1}-t)_{+}>\delta\}
,
and therefore
c(t)=c(s)
.
Hence all the centers
c(t)
coincide with a single vector, say
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
.
Consequently, every level set
\{v_{1}>t\}
is a ball centered at
x_{0}
, which means that
v_{1}
is radially symmetric about
x_{0}
.

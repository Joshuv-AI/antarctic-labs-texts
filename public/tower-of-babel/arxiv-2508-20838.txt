On the Prym map of degree 4 cyclic covers of genus 2 curves
Anatoli Shatsila

Abstract
In this paper, we study the Prym map associated to degree 4 étale cyclic covers of genus 2 curves. We show that its non-empty fibers, apart from two exceptional fibers, are isomorphic to the intersection of an elliptic normal curve in ℙ 3 \mathbb{P}^{3} with an affine space 𝔸 3 ⊂ ℙ 3 \mathbb{A}^{3}\subset\mathbb{P}^{3} . Moreover, we obtain a new description of the space of degree 4 étale cyclic covers of genus 2 curves ℛ 2 4 \mathcal{R}_{2}^{4} and find equations of elliptic and hyperelliptic curves arising from such covers.

Introduction

Given a finite cover of smooth curves
f:C\to H
, the associated Prym variety is defined as the principal component of the kernel of the norm map

P(f):=\Ker^{0}[\Nm_{f}:\Pic^{0}(C)\to\Pic^{0}(H)].

The classical case when
f
is a double étale cover of a genus 2 curve
H
has been considered in
[
Mum74
]
. In recent years, cyclic étale covers of genus 2 curves have attracted considerable attention from researchers
[
Ort03
,
LO16
,
NOS24
,
Ago20
]
.

Let
f:C\to H
be a degree
d>2
étale cyclic cover of a genus 2 curve
H
. Then the Prym variety
P(f)
is a
(d-1)
-dimensional abelian variety with polarization of type
(1,1,\ldots,1,d)
. In the modular setting, we get a Prym map

\mathcal{P}_{d}:\mathcal{R}_{2}^{d}\to\mathcal{A}_{d-1}^{(1,\ldots,1,d)}

which associates the Prym variety
P(f)
to a cover
[f:C\to H]\in\mathcal{R}_{2}^{d}
.

It is known that
\mathcal{P}_{d}
is generically finite for
d\geq 6

[
Ago20
]
, and has positive-dimensional fibers for
d=3,4,5

[
AP16
,
BS25
]
. When
d=7
the map is a dominant of degree 10
[
LO16
]
. Moreover,
\mathcal{P}_{d}
is generically injective for
d=9

[
NOS24
]
and any prime
d\geq 11

[
NOPS24
]
. Prym varieties of degree 3 cyclic covers have been studied in many different contexts
[
BL95
,
BL94
,
Rie83
]
, however, the author is not aware of a result in the literature that provides a complete characterization of the fibers of
\mathcal{P}_{3}
.
An explicit description of the fibers of
\mathcal{P}_{5}
appears to be unknown as well.

In this paper, we focus on the map

\mathcal{P}_{4}:\mathcal{R}_{2}^{4}\to\mathcal{A}_{3}^{(1,1,4)}.

The following theorem is the main result of the manuscript.

Theorem 1.1

(Theorem
4.6
)
.

Each non-empty fiber of
\mathcal{P}_{4}
, except for two exceptional fibers, is isomorphic to the intersection of an elliptic normal curve in
\mathbb{P}^{3}
and a plane
\mathbb{A}^{3}\subset\mathbb{P}^{3}
.

There are two main components of the proof that are developed in Sections
2
and
3
, respectively. First, we show that elements of
\mathcal{R}_{2}^{4}
are in bijective correspondence with elements of the set

\Delta:=\{\{t_{1},t_{2},t_{3}\}\subset\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0,1\}\ |\ t_{1}^{2}\neq t_{2}^{2}\neq t_{3}^{2}\neq t_{1}^{2}\}.

This is done by a careful analysis of order two line bundles defining the cover in terms of Weierstrass points of the genus 2 curve
H
. We use the equations of elliptic curves
E_{j},E_{j\sigma^{2}},E_{j\sigma,\sigma^{2}}
arising as quotients of
C
by the involutions to describe the data of line bundles as elements of
\Delta
. In Section
3
, we study the isogeny

\mu:E_{j}\times E_{j\sigma^{2}}\times E_{j\sigma,\sigma^{2}}\to P(f).

In particular, we show that it can be intrinsically recovered from the Prym variety
P(f)
and its induced polarization. In Section
4
, we provide a description of
\mathcal{P}_{4}
as the map

\mathcal{P}_{4}:\Delta\to\mathcal{A}_{3}^{(1,1,4)}

and show that its fibers are isomorphic to subsets of
\Delta
of the form

\Delta_{\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}}:=\left\{\{t_{1},t_{2},t_{3}\}\in\Delta\>|\>\lambda_{1}=\frac{(t_{2}-1)(t_{3}-t_{1})}{(t_{2}-t_{1})(t_{3}-1)},\>\lambda_{2}=\frac{(t_{2}^{2}-1)(t_{3}^{2}-t_{1}^{2})}{(t_{2}^{2}-t_{1}^{2})(t_{3}^{2}-1)}\right\}

for some
\lambda_{1}\neq\lambda_{2}
. Finally, we prove that any such set, apart from two exceptions
\Delta_{-1,\frac{1}{2}},\Delta_{e^{\pi i/3},e^{-\pi i/3}}
, is isomorphic to the intersection of two quadrics in
\mathbb{P}^{3}
in general position with an affine space
\mathbb{A}^{3}
.

We note that while similar approaches — such as describing moduli spaces of covers using tuples of points on the projective line
[
BO20
]
or decomposing Prym varieties into elliptic curves and employing their equations
[
BS24
]
— have been successfully applied to other Prym maps, this is the first paper to utilize these methods for describing positive-dimensional fibers.

2
Degree 4 cyclic coverings of genus 2 curves

The aim of this section is to study étale degree 4 cyclic covers of a genus 2 curve
H
from the perspective of Weierstrass points of
H
. We start by recalling the main facts from the theory of covers of hyperelliptic curves that can be found in
[
Dol12
, Chapter 5.2.2.]
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
H
be a hyperelliptic curve of genus
g
and
W=\{w_{1},\ldots,w_{2g+2}\}
be the set of its Weierstrass points.

i)

For any point
\eta\in JH[2]
there exist two disjoint subsets
A_{+},A_{-}\subset\{1,\ldots,2g+2\}
of equal cardinality such that

\eta=\mathcal{O}_{H}\left(\sum_{i\in A_{+}}w_{i}-\sum_{j\in A_{-}}w_{j}\right).

ii)

For any
A\subset\{1,\ldots,2g+2\}
with
|A|=g+1
the following equality holds in
\Pic(H)^{g+1}:

\sum_{i\in A}w_{i}=\sum_{j\in\{1,2\ldots,2g+2\}\setminus A}w_{j}.

In particular, for
g=2
, any two-torsion point of
JH
is given by
\mathcal{O}_{H}(w_{i}-w_{j})
for some
i,j
.

The following lemma provides a criterion for the covering curve to be hyperelliptic.

Lemma 2.2

(
[
BO19
]
)
.

Let
H
be a hyperelliptic curve of genus
g
and
h:C\rightarrow H
an étale double covering defined by
\eta\in JH[2]
. Then
C
is hyperelliptic if and only if
\eta=\mathcal{O}_{H}\left(w_{1}-w_{2}\right)
, where
w_{1},w_{2}\in H
are Weierstrass points.

In particular, we have the following result, already known to Mumford
[
Mum74
]

Corollary 2.3
.

Let
g:C\to H
be an étale double cover of a genus 2 curve
H
. Then
C
is hyperelliptic.

Let

be an étale cyclic cover of degree 4 of a genus 2 curve

be an automorphism of
C
inducing

. Then

and by

]

denoted by

. The automorphisms
\sigma
and

generate the dihedral group
D_{4}\subset\Aut(C)
. Since there are two conjugacy classes of involutions in
D_{4}
given by
\{j,j\sigma^{2}\}
and
\{j\sigma,j\sigma^{3}\}
it follows that the quotient curve

is isomorphic to
C_{j\sigma^{2}}:=C/\langle j\sigma^{2}\rangle
and
C_{j\sigma}:=C/\langle j\sigma\rangle

C_{j\sigma^{3}}:=C/\langle j\sigma^{3}\rangle
.

Note that the curve

) curve of genus 3, since

is an étale double cover. We denote the hyperelliptic involution on

In the rest of the section we focus on line bundles corresponding to degree 4 unramified covers of genus 2 curves. Again, let
H
be a genus 2 curve. Any étale degree 4 cyclic cover of
H
corresponds to a subgroup
\langle\eta\rangle=\mathbb{Z}_{4}\subset JH
for a line bundle
\eta\in JH[4]\setminus JH[2]

Since every such cover
f:C\to H
decomposes as
C\to C_{\sigma^{2}}\to H
it follows that
f
is constructed from line bundles
\nu\in JH[2]\setminus\{0\}
and
\xi\in JC_{\sigma^{2}}[2]
such that there is
\eta\in JH[4]\setminus JH[2]
with
\eta^{2}=\nu
and
g^{*}\eta=\xi
, where
g:C_{\sigma^{2}}\to H
is a double cover given by
\nu
.
In the construction below, we will describe such covers using the Weierstrass points on
H
. With a slight abuse of notation, we often identify Weierstrass points on a curve with their images in
\mathbb{P}^{1}
under the double cover given by the hyperelliptic involution.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
H
be a genus 2 curve,
\{u_{1},u_{2},w_{1},w_{2},w_{3},w_{4}\}
be the set of its Weierstrass points,
\nu=\mathcal{O}_{H}(u_{1}-u_{2})
, and
g:C_{\sigma^{2}}\to H
be the cover defined by
\nu
. Let
\psi:\mathbb{P}^{1}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
be a double cover branched over
u_{1},u_{2}
and
\bar{\sigma}
be the involution of
\mathbb{P}^{1}
inducing
\psi
. With a slight abuse of notation, we denote the preimages of
w_{i}
by
Q_{i}=\{w_{i},\bar{\sigma}(w_{i})\}
.
Then
Q_{1}\cup Q_{2}\cup Q_{3}\cup Q_{4}
is the set of Weierstrass points of
C_{\sigma^{2}}
and, if we define

Q:=\{\mathcal{O}_{C_{\sigma^{2}}}(q_{1}-q_{2}+q_{3}-q_{4})\ |\ q_{i}\in Q_{i}\}

then

Q=\{g^{*}\eta\ |\ \eta^{2}=\nu\}.

Proof.

It follows from Corollary
2.3
that
C_{\sigma^{2}}
is a genus 3 hyperelliptic curve. The fact that
Q_{1}\cup Q_{2}\cup Q_{3}\cup Q_{4}
equals to the set of Weierstrass points of
C_{\sigma^{2}}
is well-known
[
BO19
, Corollary 4.3]
.

Since
\Nm_{g}\circ g^{*}=m_{2}
, where
m_{2}
is the multiplication by
2
on
JH
, for any
\eta
with
\eta^{2}=\nu
we have
(\Nm_{g}\circ g^{*})(\eta)=\nu
. Note that
\Nm_{g}(\mathcal{O}_{C_{\sigma^{2}}}(q_{1}-q_{2}+q_{3}-q_{4}))=\mathcal{O}_{H}(w_{1}-w_{2}+w_{3}-w_{4})=\mathcal{O}_{H}(u_{1}-u_{2})=\nu
, and the elements of
Q
are the only 2-torsion points of
JC
with this property, which yields
\{g^{*}\eta\>|\>\eta^{2}=\nu\}\subseteq Q
.
Moreover,
\mathcal{O}_{C_{\sigma^{2}}}(q_{1}-q_{2}+q_{3}-q_{4})=\mathcal{O}_{C_{\sigma^{2}}}(q_{1}^{\prime}-q_{2}^{\prime}+q_{3}^{\prime}-q_{4}^{\prime})
if and only if for all
i
either
q_{i}=q_{i}^{\prime}
or
q_{i}\neq q_{i}^{\prime}
, hence
|Q|=8
. Finally, there are precisely sixteen 4-torsion points satisfying
\eta^{2}=\nu,
and
g^{*}\eta_{1}=g^{*}\eta_{2}
if and only if
\eta_{1}=\eta_{2}+\nu
, hence
|\{g^{*}\eta\ |\ \eta^{2}=\nu\}|=8
, which implies
Q=\{g^{*}\eta\>|\>\eta^{2}=\nu\}
.
∎

3
Geometry of the Prym variety

In this section, we study Prym varieties of degree 4 cyclic étale covers of genus 2 curves. We use the notation from the previous section unless explicitly stated, and we denote morphisms of Jacobians induced by automorphism of curves by the same letters. Sometimes we omit pullbacks when we consider abelian subvarieties.
Let

(P,\Xi):=P(f)

be the Prym variety of
f
with the induced polarization
\Xi
.

Before we determine the isotypical decomposition of
P
, let us introduce the following notation. Let
A
be an abelian variety and
A_{1},A_{2}\ldots,A_{n}\subseteq A
be a set of its abelian subvarieties with the induced polarizations. We denote the symmetric idempotent of
A_{k}
by
\varepsilon_{k}\in\End_{\mathbb{Q}}(A)
for
1\leq k\leq n
. We write

A=A_{1}^{(e_{1})}\boxplus A_{2}^{(e_{2})}\boxplus\ldots\boxplus A_{n}^{(e_{n})}

if
e_{k}
is the polarization type of
A_{k}
for
1\leq k\leq n
and

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\varepsilon_{i}=1

.

Lemma 3.1
.

The isotypical decomposition of the Prym variety
P
is as follows:

P=E_{j}^{(2)}\boxplus E_{j\sigma^{2}}^{(2)}\boxplus E_{j\sigma,\sigma^{2}}^{(4)}.

Proof.

Note that
E_{j},E_{j\sigma^{2}}
and
E_{j\sigma,\sigma^{2}}
have exponents
2,2,4
as subvarieties of
J\tilde{C}
, respectively, since the corresponding covers are of degree
2,2,4
. Hence, their symmetric idemponents are given by

\varepsilon_{E_{j}}=\frac{1+j}{2},

\varepsilon_{E_{j\sigma^{2}}}=\frac{1+j\sigma^{2}}{2},

\varepsilon_{E_{j\sigma,\sigma^{2}}}=\frac{1+j\sigma+\sigma^{2}+j\sigma^{3}}{4}.

Since
g(C_{\sigma^{2},j})=g(C_{\sigma,j})=0
we have

1+j+\sigma^{2}+j\sigma^{2}=\sigma+\sigma^{3}+j\sigma+j\sigma^{3}=0.

Using these facts we obtain

\varepsilon_{E_{j}}+\varepsilon_{E_{j\sigma^{2}}}+\varepsilon_{E_{j\sigma,\sigma^{2}}}=\frac{3-\sigma-\sigma^{2}-\sigma^{3}}{4}=1-\varepsilon_{JC_{\sigma}}=\varepsilon_{P}.

∎

By
\mu
we will denote the addition map

\mu:E_{j}\times E_{j\sigma^{2}}\times E_{j\sigma,\sigma^{2}}\to P.

Lemma 3.2
.

i)

With the notation from the diagram
2.5
we have

\ker{\mu}=\{(w_{1}-w_{i},\sigma(q_{1})-\sigma(q_{i}),q_{1}-q_{i})\in E_{j\sigma,\sigma^{2}}\times E_{j}\times E_{j\sigma^{2}}\>|\>i=2,3,4\}.

ii)

Let
\pi
be the quotient map
P\to P/K(\Xi)[2]
and
\alpha=\pi\circ\mu
be the composition. Then

(P/K(\Xi)[2])^{(1,1,1)}=\alpha(E_{j}\times E_{j\sigma^{2}})^{(1,1)}\times(E_{j\sigma,\sigma^{2}}/E_{j\sigma,\sigma^{2}}[2])^{(1)}.

Proof.

Note that
P(h)=E_{j}^{(2)}\boxplus E_{j\sigma^{2}}^{(2)}
and
P(h)
has polarization type
(2,2)
since
h^{*}JC_{\sigma^{2}}
has type
(1,2,2)
. Therefore, it follows that
P(h)=E_{j}\times E_{j\sigma^{2}}
with the induced polarizations and, in particular,
E_{j}\cap E_{j\sigma^{2}}=\{0\}
.

Since
JC_{\sigma}
has polarization of type
(1,4)
, it follows that
P
has polarization of type
(1,1,4)
. Therefore, we have
\deg(\mu)=4
. Note that
\mathbb{Z}_{4}^{2}\simeq K(\Xi)\subset\mu(K(\mu^{*}\Xi))
since
\mu
is surjective. Since
\ker\mu\subset K(\mu^{*}\Xi)\simeq\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{4}^{2}

it follows that
\ker\mu\simeq\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}
.

Denoting
\pi_{j}:C\to E_{j},\pi_{j\sigma^{2}}:C\to E_{j\sigma^{2}},\pi_{j\sigma,\sigma^{2}}:C\to E_{j\sigma,\sigma^{2}}

one readily checks that

\pi_{j}^{*}(q_{1}-q_{i})+\pi^{*}_{j\sigma^{2}}(\sigma(q_{1})-\sigma(q_{i}))+\pi_{j\sigma,\sigma^{2}}^{*}(w_{1}-w_{i})=0

for
i=2,3,4
, so the first part of the lemma is proved.

In order to prove the second part, we denote the corresponding polarizations on Jacobians of the decomposition of
P
by
\Xi_{j},\Xi_{j\sigma^{2}},\Xi_{j\sigma,\sigma^{2}}
and introduce the notation

K(\Xi_{j})=E_{j}[2]=\langle p_{1},p_{2}\rangle,

K(\Xi_{j\sigma^{2}})=E_{j\sigma^{2}}[2]=\langle r_{1},r_{2}\rangle,

K(\Xi_{j\sigma,\sigma^{2}})=E_{j\sigma,\sigma^{2}}[4]=\langle v_{1},v_{2}\rangle,

K(\Xi)=\langle s_{1},s_{2}\rangle.

Then

s_{1}=a_{1}p_{1}+a_{2}p_{2}+a_{3}r_{1}+a_{4}r_{2}+a_{5}v_{1}+a_{6}v_{2},

s_{2}=b_{1}p_{1}+b_{2}p_{2}+b_{3}r_{1}+b_{4}r_{2}+b_{5}v_{1}+b_{6}v_{2}

for some integers
a_{i},b_{i}
with
i=1,\ldots,6
. In particular,

2s_{1}=2a_{5}v_{1}+2a_{6}v_{2},\ \ \ \ \ 2s_{2}=2b_{5}v_{1}+2b_{6}v_{2}.

Therefore,
\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}\simeq K(\Xi)[2]=\langle 2s_{1},2s_{2}\rangle=\langle 2v_{1},2v_{2}\rangle=E_{j\sigma,\sigma^{2}}[2]
, and
\alpha_{|E_{j\sigma,\sigma^{2}}}
is the multiplication by 2. Thus, it follows from the first part of the lemma that

\ker\alpha=\langle p_{1}+r_{1},p_{2}+r_{2},2v_{1},2v_{2}\rangle\simeq\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{4}.

This implies that
\alpha(E_{j}\times E_{j\sigma^{2}})\cap\alpha(E_{j\sigma,\sigma^{2}})=\{0\}
, which finishes the proof.

∎

4
Fibers of the Prym map

Let
\mathcal{A}^{(1,1,4)}_{3}
be the moduli space of
(1,1,4)
-polarized abelian threefolds. In this section, we will describe the structure of the fibers of the Prym map

\mathcal{P}_{4}:\mathcal{R}_{2}^{4}\to\mathcal{A}_{3}^{(1,1,4)}.

Recall that in Theorem
2.8
we showed that there is a bijection between
\mathcal{R}_{2}^{4}
and

\Delta=\{\{t_{1},t_{2},t_{3}\}\subset\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0,1\}\ |\ t_{1}^{2}\neq t_{2}^{2}\neq t_{3}^{2}\neq t_{1}^{2}\}.

It follows from Construction
2.5
, Lemma
2.6
and Remark
2.7
that the Prym map
\mathcal{P}_{4}
is given by

\mathcal{P}_{4}:\Delta\ni T=\{t_{1},t_{2},t_{3}\}\mapsto((E_{T}\times E_{T}\times F_{T})/\langle e_{1}^{T}+e_{1}^{T}+f_{1}^{T},e_{2}^{T}+e_{2}^{T}+f_{2}^{T}\rangle,H)\in\mathcal{A}_{3}^{(1,1,4)},

where the pullback of
H
to
E_{T}\times E_{T}\times F_{T}
is isomorphic to
\mathcal{O}_{E_{T}}(2)\boxtimes\mathcal{O}_{E_{T}}(2)\boxtimes\mathcal{O}_{F_{T}}(4)
. Here,
E_{T}
and
F_{T}
are elliptic curves defined by the equations

y^{2}=(x-1)(x-t_{1})(x-t_{2})(x-t_{3})

and

y^{2}=(x-1)(x-t_{1}^{2})(x-t_{2}^{2})(x-t_{3}^{2})

respectively with

e_{1}^{T}=[(1,0)-(t_{1},0)],\quad e_{2}^{T}=[(1,0)-(t_{2},0)],

f_{1}^{T}=[(1,0)-(t_{1}^{2},0)],\quad f_{2}^{T}=[(1,0)-(t_{2}^{2},0)].

First, we prove the following important result.

Proposition 4.1
.

The equality
\mathcal{P}_{4}(T)=\mathcal{P}_{4}(S)
with
T=\{t_{1},t_{2},t_{3}\},S=\{s_{1},s_{2},s_{3}\}
holds if and only if there is a choice of indices of elements of
S
such that

\frac{(t_{2}-1)(t_{3}-t_{1})}{(t_{2}-t_{1})(t_{3}-1)}=\frac{(s_{2}-1)(s_{3}-s_{1})}{(s_{2}-s_{1})(s_{3}-1)}

and

\frac{(t_{2}^{2}-1)(t_{3}^{2}-t_{1}^{2})}{(t_{2}^{2}-t_{1}^{2})(t_{3}^{2}-1)}=\frac{(s_{2}^{2}-1)(s_{3}^{2}-s_{1}^{2})}{(s_{2}^{2}-s_{1}^{2})(s_{3}^{2}-1)}.

Proof.

The equalities

\frac{(t_{2}-1)(t_{3}-t_{1})}{(t_{2}-t_{1})(t_{3}-1)}=\frac{(s_{2}-1)(s_{3}-s_{1})}{(s_{2}-s_{1})(s_{3}-1)}

and

\frac{(t_{2}^{2}-1)(t_{3}^{2}-t_{1}^{2})}{(t_{2}^{2}-t_{1}^{2})(t_{3}^{2}-1)}=\frac{(s_{2}^{2}-1)(s_{3}^{2}-s_{1}^{2})}{(s_{2}^{2}-s_{1}^{2})(s_{3}^{2}-1)}

imply that there exist isomorphisms
\phi_{1}:E_{T}\to E_{S}
and
\phi_{2}:F_{T}\to F_{S}
such that

\phi(e_{1}^{T})=e_{1}^{S},\quad\phi(e_{2}^{T})=e_{2}^{S},\quad\phi(f_{1}^{T})=f_{1}^{S},\quad\phi(f_{2}^{T})=f_{2}^{S}.

Thus,
\mathcal{P}_{4}(T)=\mathcal{P}_{4}(S)
from the definition.

For the opposite implication, let
(P,\Xi)=\mathcal{P}_{4}(S)=\mathcal{P}_{4}(T)
and

f:C\to H
be the covering given by
T
. Below we use the notation from the previous sections. It follows from Lemma
3.2
that
P/\Xi[2]
is a principally polarised abelian threefold isomorphic to a product of an elliptic curve
F=\alpha(E_{j\sigma,\sigma^{2}})
and an abelian sufrace
S=\alpha(E_{j}\times E_{j\sigma^{2}})
. Note that by
[
BL04
, Decomposition theorem 4.3.1]
the isomorphism above is unique. Using the notation from the diagram
2
and Lemma
3.2
, we see that
P(h)^{(2,2)}=E_{j}^{(2)}\times E_{j\sigma^{2}}^{(2)}
is a complementary abelian subvariety to
\pi^{-1}(F)\simeq E_{j\sigma,\sigma^{2}}
in
P
. Note that the intersection
P(h)\cap\pi^{-1}(E_{j\sigma,\sigma^{2}})
gives the kernel
\ker\mu
of the addition map

\mu:E_{j}\times E_{j\sigma^{2}}\times E_{j\sigma,\sigma^{2}}\to P.

This shows that we can recover the map
\mu
from
(P,\Xi)
independently of the cover in its preimage. Therefore, there exist isomorphisms
\phi_{1}:E_{T}\to E_{S}
and
\phi_{2}:F_{T}\to F_{S}
such that

\phi(e_{1}^{T})=e_{1}^{S},\quad\phi(e_{2}^{T})=e_{2}^{S},\quad\phi(f_{1}^{T})=f_{1}^{S},\quad\phi(f_{2}^{T})=f_{2}^{S},

hence we are done.

∎

Lemma 4.2
.

Let

\lambda_{1}:=\frac{(t_{2}-1)(t_{3}-t_{1})}{(t_{2}-t_{1})(t_{3}-1)}

and

\lambda_{2}:=\frac{(t_{2}^{2}-1)(t_{3}^{2}-t_{1}^{2})}{(t_{2}^{2}-t_{1}^{2})(t_{3}^{2}-1)}

for
\{t_{1},t_{2},t_{3}\}\in\Delta
. Then
\lambda_{1}\neq\lambda_{2}
.

Proof.

The equations above are equivalent to

\begin{cases}\frac{(t_{2}-1)(t_{3}-t_{1})}{(t_{2}-t_{1})(t_{3}-1)}=\lambda_{1},\\
\frac{(t_{2}+1)(t_{3}+t_{1})}{(t_{2}+t_{1})(t_{3}+1)}=\frac{\lambda_{2}}{\lambda_{1}}.\end{cases}

Note that for
\lambda_{1}=\lambda_{2}
the second equation transforms into

(t_{2}-t_{3})(t_{1}-1)=0,

so any solution of system
4
is not contained in
\Delta
.

∎

Together, Proposition
4.1
and Lemma
4.2
imply

Corollary 4.3
.

Each fiber of
\mathcal{P}_{4}
is isomorphic to

\Delta_{\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}}:=\left\{\{t_{1},t_{2},t_{3}\}\in\Delta\>|\>\lambda_{1}=\frac{(t_{2}-1)(t_{3}-t_{1})}{(t_{2}-t_{1})(t_{3}-1)},\>\lambda_{2}=\frac{(t_{2}^{2}-1)(t_{3}^{2}-t_{1}^{2})}{(t_{2}^{2}-t_{1}^{2})(t_{3}^{2}-1)}\right\}

for some
\lambda_{1}\neq\lambda_{2}
.

From now on, we will identify fibers of
\mathcal{P}_{4}
with sets
\Delta_{\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}}
.

Remark 4.4
.

In Proposition
4.1
we made a particular choice of the cross-ratio. In particular,

\Delta_{\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}}=\Delta_{\frac{1}{\lambda_{1}},\frac{1}{\lambda_{2}}}=\Delta_{\frac{1}{1-\lambda_{1}},\frac{1}{1-\lambda_{2}}}=\Delta_{1-\lambda_{1},1-\lambda_{2}}=\Delta_{\frac{\lambda_{1}}{\lambda_{1}-1},\frac{\lambda_{2}}{\lambda_{2}-1}}=\Delta_{\frac{\lambda_{1}-1}{\lambda_{1}},\frac{\lambda_{2}-1}{\lambda_{2}}}.

To give a geometric description of the set
\Delta_{\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}}
, we need the following lemma.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0,1\}
be two distinct complex numbers and
Q_{1},Q_{2}\subset\mathbb{P}^{3}
be quadrics defined by the equations

q_{1}:=(t_{2}-t_{4})(t_{3}-t_{1})-\lambda_{1}(t_{2}-t_{1})(t_{3}-t_{4})

and

q_{2}:=(t_{2}+t_{4})(t_{3}+t_{1})-\frac{\lambda_{2}}{\lambda_{1}}(t_{2}+t_{1})(t_{3}+t_{4})

respectively. Then
Q_{1}\cap Q_{2}
is a normal elliptic curve.

Proof.

Clearly
E:=Q_{1}\cap Q_{2}
is a complete intersection, so
E
is a curve.
Let
\lambda_{2}^{\prime}:=\frac{\lambda_{2}}{\lambda_{1}}\neq 1
. We have to show that the corresponding Jacobian matrix
M
has rank 2 at all points of
E
:

M=\begin{pmatrix}(t_{4}-t_{2})+\lambda_{1}(t_{3}-t_{4})&(t_{3}-t_{1})+\lambda_{1}(t_{4}-t_{3})&(t_{2}-t_{4})+\lambda_{1}(t_{1}-t_{2})&(t_{1}-t_{3})+\lambda_{1}(t_{2}-t_{1})\\
(t_{2}+t_{4})-\lambda_{2}^{\prime}(t_{3}+t_{4})&(t_{1}+t_{3})-\lambda_{2}^{\prime}(t_{3}+t_{4})&(t_{2}+t_{4})-\lambda_{2}^{\prime}(t_{1}+t_{2})&(t_{1}+t_{3})-\lambda_{2}^{\prime}(t_{2}+t_{1})\end{pmatrix}

Assume that there is a point
p=[t_{1}:t_{2}:t_{3}:t_{4}]\in E
such that
M
has rank smaller than 2 at
p
, and denote the rows of
M
by
v_{1}
and
v_{2}
. We know that
v_{1}=Cv_{2}
for some
C
. Summing up the coordinates of
v_{1}
we get zero, hence the same must hold for
v_{2}
. However, the sum for
v_{2}
is equal to

(2-2\lambda_{2}^{\prime})(t_{1}+t_{2}+t_{3}+t_{4})=0,

which implies that

t_{1}+t_{2}+t_{3}+t_{4}=0.

Now, the sum of the first and the fourth coordinates of
v_{2}
is zero, hence

(1-\lambda_{1})(t_{4}+t_{1}-t_{2}-t_{3})=0.

Thus,

t_{2}+t_{3}=t_{4}+t_{1}=0.

Comparing the sums of the first and third coordinates we get

(C+\lambda_{1})(t_{2}+t_{4})=0.

However, if
t_{2}+t_{4}=0
we get
t_{2}=t_{1}=-t_{3}=-t_{4}
, and we conclude that
t_{1}=t_{2}=t_{3}=t_{4}=0
from
S\in Q_{2}
. Therefore,
C=-\lambda_{1}
. Finally, comparing the sums of the first and second coordinates we get

(1+C\lambda_{2}^{\prime})(t_{3}+t_{4})=0.

We can show that
t_{3}+t_{4}=0
implies
t_{1}=t_{2}=t_{3}=t_{4}=0
analogously to the above, hence
C\lambda_{2}^{\prime}=-1
holds. Together with
C=-\lambda_{1}
we get
\lambda_{2}=1
, which is impossible. Thus, the intersection
E
is smooth. By adjunction formula the arithmetic genus of
E
is equal to 1, hence it is an elliptic normal curve of degree
4
.
∎

We are ready to give a description of fibers of the Prym map
\mathcal{P}_{4}
.

Theorem 4.6
.

Each fiber
\Delta_{\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}}
with
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\notin\{-1,\frac{1}{2},2,e^{\pi i/3},e^{-\pi i/3}\}
of
\mathcal{P}_{4}
is isomorphic to the intersection of an elliptic normal curve in
\mathbb{P}^{3}
and a plane
\mathbb{A}^{3}\subset\mathbb{P}^{3}
.

Proof.

It follows from the assumption
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\notin\{-1,\frac{1}{2},2,e^{\pi i/3},e^{-\pi i/3}\}
that there is no non-trivial permutation
\sigma\in S_{3}
such that both (ordered) triples
(t_{1},t_{2},t_{3})
and
(t_{\sigma(1)},t_{\sigma(2)},t_{\sigma(3)})
satisfy the system
4
. Therefore, the set of solutions of the system
4
can be viewed as a subset of
\Delta
. Thus,
\Delta_{\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}}
is isomorphic to
Q_{1}\cap Q_{2}\cap\mathbb{A}^{3}
, where
Q_{1},Q_{2}\subset\mathbb{P}^{3}
are quadrics defined as in Lemma
4.5
and
\mathbb{A}^{3}=\{[t_{1}:t_{2}:t_{3}:t_{4}]\in\mathbb{P}^{3}\>|\>t_{4}=1\}
, hence Lemma
4.5
implies the assertion.

∎

Remark 4.7
.

There are two exceptional fibers of
\mathcal{P}_{4}
that Theorem
4.6
does not cover, namely
\Delta_{-1,\frac{1}{2}}
and
\Delta_{e^{\pi i/3},e^{-\pi i/3}}
(note that all other combinations of
(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})
give the same fibers by Remark
4.4
). Both fibers contain sets
\{t_{1},t_{2},t_{3}\}
such that there exists a non-trivial permutation
\sigma\in S_{3}
with
[t_{1}:t_{2}:t_{3}:1],[t_{\sigma(1)}:t_{\sigma(2)}:t_{\sigma(3)}:1]\in Q_{1}\cap Q_{2}
, hence geometrically these fibers are elliptic curves with certain points glued.

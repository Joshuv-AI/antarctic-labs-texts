Cheminformatics-aided discovery of potential allosteric site modulators of ubiquitin-specific protease 7
Authors: Olayinka Abraham Ojedele, Haruna Isiyaku Umar, Soukayna baammi, Amira Metouekel, Atrsaw Asrat Mengistie, Yousef A. Bin Jardan, Gamal A. Shazly, Omoboyede Victor
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Ubiquitin-specific peptidase 7 (USP7) is a deubiquitinating enzyme that mediates the stability and activity of numerous proteins. At basal expression levels, USP7 stabilizes p53 protein, even in the presence of excess MDM2. However, its overexpression leads to the deubiquitination of MDM2 at a rate faster than p53, leading to p53 degradation and pro-tumorigenic roles. Consequently, it is an attractive target for anticancer drug discovery via the modulation of its allosteric site from which the protein is activated. In this study, molecular modeling techniques and cheminformatics approaches were employed to unravel the potential of eighty compounds to serve as its allosteric site modulators. The compounds were initially subjected to virtual screening. Subsequently, the binding free energies of the top four compounds with the highest binding affinities were calculated, and their drug-likeness, and pharmacokinetic and toxicity profiles were evaluated. Ultimately, the complexes of the protein and hit compounds were subjected to a 100 nanoseconds (ns) molecular dynamics simulation. The results of the study revealed eight compounds from the compound library with docking scores ranging from − 7.491 to -11.43 kcal/mol, compared to P217564, which exhibited a docking score of -5.671 kcal/mol. The top four compounds with the highest affinities possessed drug-like properties, and good pharmacokinetic and toxicity profiles, and their predicted inhibitory potentials showed they will be effective at minimal concentration. Also, molecular dynamics simulation confirmed the stability of the protein-ligand complexes. Conclusively, the compounds identified in this study are worthy of further evaluation for the development of allosteric site modulators of USP7.

Introduction

Globally, cancer remains a significant health challenge, with nearly 10 million deaths attributed to it in 2020 alone. Notably, cancer encompasses a diverse group of diseases characterized by uncontrolled cell growth and proliferation, its multifactorial nature, involving genetic mutations, environmental factors, and immune evasion mechanisms, poses challenges to effective treatment1,2. Despite the current advancements in medical science and the understanding of cancer biology, the complexity and heterogeneity of cancer necessitate continued efforts in therapeutic development to address the limitations and shortcomings of current treatments3. The ubiquitin-specific peptidase 7 (USP7) is a deubiquitinating enzyme implicated in various cellular processes, including DNA repair, cell cycle regulation, and immune response modulation4. The dysregulation of USP7 has been associated with several diseases, particularly cancer, making it an attractive therapeutic target5.

USP7, also known as HAUSP, is a deubiquitinating enzyme that mediates the stability and activity of numerous proteins involved in key cellular processes. Notably, USP7 has presented itself as an attractive drug target for cancer management due to its immunosuppressive functions, negative regulation of tumor suppressor proteins, and its overexpression correlates with unfavorable prognosis and drug resistance4. At basal expression levels, USP7 stabilizes p53 protein even in the presence of excess MDM2, however, its overexpression leads to the deubiquitination of MDM2 at a rate faster than p53, leading to p53 degradation and pro-tumorigenic roles6. Also, increased levels of USP7 have been reported to exert immunosuppressive function via the modulation of the Forkhead box P3 (Foxp3)+ T-regulatory (Treg) cells7–9. Natively, USP7 exists in an inactive state and is activated by the binding of ubiquitin to an allosteric site within its catalytic domain. Notably, the structure of USP7 includes an N-terminal which contains a TRAF domain that functions in substrate recognition; a catalytic domain that spans 360 amino acid residues; and a C-terminal which ends with a region containing five ubiquitin-like domains10–12. The activation of USP7 upon ubiquitin binding involves dynamic changes in two loops namely blocking loop-1 and switching loop. They are both specific loops within the catalytic domain that play a critical role in the regulation of the enzyme’s activity, in the inactive state of USP7, they are positioned in a way that blocks the active site, preventing the enzyme from performing its function13,14. Hence, the inhibition of the allosteric site of USP7 to abate the binding of ubiquitin is a viable means for oncology drug discovery15.

While the inhibition of USP7 is a viable approach to cancer treatment, there are currently no inhibitors in clinical usage16, hence, the search for potential inhibitors remains ongoing. In the quest for the discovery of an allosteric site modulator, several studies have been conducted; in a pioneering study by Colland et al., they identified the first small molecule with the potential to inhibit the allosteric site using high throughput screening. Progressively, numerous studies have focused on the active site of the target, while a few other studies have identified compounds that can serve as potential inhibitors of the allosteric site17.

In this study, molecular modeling techniques including molecular docking, binding free energy calculations, semi-empirical-based quantum reactivity descriptors calculations, drug-likeness evaluation, molecular dynamics simulation, and quantitative structure-activity relationship (QSAR) were utilized to investigate the potential of an anticancer compound library to serve as potential inhibitors of USP7. The results of the study led to the identification of four compounds that can be further optimized for selective USP7 inhibition in cancer treatment and other diseases18.

Materials and methods

This project was carried out on an HP EliteBook PC with a processor speed of 2.6–3.5 GHZ, and RAM and ROM of 16GB and 512GB SSD, respectively.

Compound library retrieval and preparation

An anticancer compound library named “SL-23_Oncology Phenotypic MTT Colon Cancer” was retrieved from the ASINEX Database (https://www.asinex.com/) and was imported into the interface of Schrodinger Maestro 13.9. Subsequently, the LigPrep module was employed to optimize the compound structures using the OPLS4 forcefield with the pH set to 7 ± 2.0 and salt removed with Epik19.

Retrieval of protein structure and preparation

The tertiary structure of USP7 was retrieved from the Protein Databank (PDB) using the PDB ID “5N9R”. The retrieved structure was prepared using the protein preparation workflow which encompasses steps including the addition of specific hydrogen atoms, removal of water molecules, creation of heteroatom states with a pH range of 7.0 ± 2.0, PROPKA-based structure refinement, and structure minimization using the OPLS4 forcefield with a root mean square deviation (RMSD) for heavy atoms constrained to 3.019.

Docking study: receptor grid generation, virtual screening, and binding free energy calculation

The docking study was initiated by generating a three-dimensional receptor grid that maps out the protein’s active site, using the receptor grid generation tool. This grid was based on the ligand co-crystallized with the USP7 protein structure and had X, Y, and Z coordinates of 30.69, 12.87, and − 11.16, respectively. The outer box of the grid which defines the volume in which the grid potentials are computed was set to the size of the native ligand, while the inner box size, which defines the volume explored by the ligand center during the exhaustive site-point search, was set to the default 10 × 10 × 1020. The compound library was then subjected to virtual screening using the Glide tool in Maestro. Initially, the standard precision (SP) algorithm was employed to screen the compound library against the USP7 active site, followed by the extra precision (XP) algorithm, which was used to rescreen the top ten compounds with the lowest SP docking scores. After the molecular docking simulations, the binding free energies of the protein-ligand complexes were calculated using the Prime Molecular Mechanics with Generalized Born and Surface Area (MMGBSA) post-docking protocol. Drug-likeness and pharmacokinetics property evaluation

Based on Lipinski’s rule of five (Ro5), the QikProp module was employed to assess the drug-likeness of the hit compounds by calculating the parameters of the Ro522. Subsequently, a comprehensive evaluation of the absorption, distribution, metabolism, excretion, and toxicity (ADMET) profiles of the compounds was carried out using the pKCSM webserver (https://biosig.lab.uq.edu.au/pkcsm/)23.

In silico evaluation of biological activity

Quantitative Structure-Activity Relationship (QSAR) analysis was employed to measure the biological activity of the final set of compounds in terms of IC50. A dataset containing experimental inhibitors of USP7 and their corresponding logarithmic biological activity values (pIC50) were obtained from the CHEMBL database (https://www.ebi.ac.uk/chembl/) using the sequence of the protein. Subsequently, the autoQSAR tool of Schrodinger Maestro was employed to extract the descriptors of the compounds, after which the dataset was bifurcated into a 75% training set and a 25% test set which contained 336 and 112 compounds, respectively, to develop and evaluate statistical models. The training set was employed to construct QSAR models that establish a correlation between the molecular descriptors of the compounds with their corresponding pIC50 values. The models’ performance was evaluated using metrics including the correlation coefficient (R2), cross-validated determination coefficient (Q2), root mean square error (RMSE), standard deviation (SD), and ranking score. Ultimately, the optimal QSAR model was identified and employed to predict the pIC50 values of the final compounds.

Molecular dynamics simulation

The behavior of the selected ligands with USP7 was studied over 100 ns using GROMACS software version 201924. The CHARMM27 force field was applied to the protein, and ligand topologies were generated via the Swissparam server25,26. Each complex was placed in a dodecahedral box (1.0 nm) with the TIP3P water model and neutralized with counterions. The steepest descent method was employed to minimize energy and maximize force, setting Fmax at 1,000 kJ/mol/nm27. The system was equilibrated at 300 K and 1 bar through two consecutive 100 ps simulations using canonical NVT and isobaric NPT ensembles. Subsequently, 100 ns molecular dynamics simulations were carried out for each molecule. The resulting trajectories and data files were analyzed to gain insights into the protein’s behavior.

Druglikeness and pharmacokinetics profiling

All the hit compounds were found to be druglike as the values of all their physicochemical parameters were within the threshold specified by the Ro5. Notably, the parameters presented in Table 3, include the Molecular Weight (MW), expressed in Daltons (Da), and it measures the mass of the compound. Another key parameter is the Number of Hydrogen Bond Acceptors (NHBA), which counts the atoms in the molecule capable of accepting hydrogen bonds, typically oxygen and nitrogen. Similarly, the Number of Hydrogen Bond Donors (NHBD) refers to the count of hydrogen atoms attached to electronegative atoms that can donate hydrogen bonds. The Predicted LogP (QPlogPo/w) measures the compound’s lipophilicity by evaluating its partitioning between an organic solvent (octanol) and water. Finally, Violations denote the number of Ro5 rules that a compound breaches. Drug-like compounds are typically expected to violate no more than one rule. As depicted in Table 3, the molecular weight of all the compounds is less than 500 Da, with the compound P217564 having the highest value of 447.307 Da. Similarly, the number of hydrogen bond donors and acceptors, and the value of the logarithm of the octanol-water partition coefficient were less than 5, 10, and 5, respectively. The pharmacokinetic properties of these compounds are also presented in Table 4. All the compounds possessed high human intestinal absorption and were predicted to be non-toxic among other properties.

Biological activities of hit compounds

Several models were built to evaluate the biological activities of the compounds, after which the best model was selected. Kpls_linear_6, which was the best model and was computed from kernel-based partial least squares regression (KPLS), had parameters that were within the acceptable range for a good QSAR model. The model achieved an R2 of 0.88 and a standard deviation of 0.44 on internal validation and a Q2 of 0.89 and RMSE of 0.42 on external validation. Notably, the R2 and Q2 are greater than 0.6 which is greater than the standard threshold28. Noteworthy, the model was built using both fingerprints and descriptors as independent variables29. Discussion

The search for compounds with the potential to modulate therapeutic targets in cancer is currently ongoing, and among these targets, USP7 stands out due to its role in cancer drug resistance and the disruption of p53’s role as the guardian of the genome6. In this study, eighty compounds were evaluated as potential modulators of this protein.

The evaluated compounds possessed high affinities for this protein, with docking scores ranging from − 7.491 to -11.43 kcal/mol as compared to the − 5.671 kcal/mol exhibited by the reference compound “P217564”. Noteworthy, a lower docking score suggests a higher potential for binding19. Interestingly, eight of the evaluated compounds exhibited a higher affinity for USP7 in comparison with the reference compound, suggesting they might interact with residues pivotal to USP7 deubiquitinating function, hence, modulating this activity. Somewhat correlated with these compounds’ affinity is their stability, with the compounds with the top five docking scores exhibiting binding free energy which ranged from − 50.39 to -63.38 kcal/mol. As presented in Table 2, the notably negative ∆Gbind vdW values suggest that van der Waals forces play a significant role in stabilizing the ligand-protein interactions. Van der Waals forces arise from temporary dipoles formed due to fluctuations in electron distribution, leading to attractive interactions between nonpolar groups. This implies that the hydrophobic components of the compounds are likely engaging strongly with the hydrophobic residues within the protein’s active site30. Also, the notable ∆Gbind Lipophilic values reflect the impact of hydrophobic interactions on the binding stability of the compounds in USP7’s active site. Similarly, the highly negative ∆Gbind Coulomb value suggests that the compounds and the protein likely have complementary charged regions that enhance binding specificity. Interestingly, this characteristic interaction of the selected hit compounds is not observed in low in the P217564’s interaction. As opposed to other interactions that contributed to the stability of the compounds’ interactions, the solvation of the protein-ligand complex resulted in destabilization effects.

The hit compounds of this study formed interactions including hydrogen. The profiling of the interactions of the hit compounds revealed Compound 78 formed hydrogen bonds with ASP295, ASN418, and TYR465; hydrophobic interactions with TYR224, VAL296, LEU406, MET407, PHE409, MET410, TYR411, TYR465, and TYR514; polar interactions with HIS294, GLN405, ASN418, HIS456, ASN460, and HIS461; positively charged interactions with ARG408 and LYS420; negatively charged interactions with ASP295 and ASP459; pi-pi stacking with HIS456. Compound 6 formed a hydrogen bond with TYR465; hydrophobic interactions with TYR224, VAL296, LEU406, MET407, PHE409, MET410, TYR411, THR465, and TYR514; polar interactions with GLN297, GLN351, ASN418, HIS456, ASN460, and HIS461; positively charged interactions with ARG408 and LYS420; negatively charged interactions with ASP295 and ASP459. Compound 77 formed hydrogen bonds with ASP295 and ASN418; hydrophobic interactions with TYR224, VAL296, LEU406, MET407, PHE409, MET410, TYR411, TYR465, and TYR514; polar interactions with GLN405, ASN418, HIS456, ASN460, and HIS461; positively charged interactions with ARG408 and LYS420; negatively charged interactions with ASP295 and ASP459; pi-pi stacking with HIS456; pi-cation interactions with LYS420, HIS456, and TYR514; pi-pi interaction with HIS456. Compound 3 formed hydrogen bonds with ASP295 and PHE409; hydrophobic interactions with TYR224, VAL296, LEU406, MET407, PHE409, MET410, TYR465, TYR514, and MET515; polar interactions with GLN405, ASN418, HIS456, ASN460, and HIS461; positively charged interactions with ARG408 and LYS420; negatively charged interactions with ASP295 and ASP459; pi-pi stacking with HIS456; pi-cation interaction with HIS456; pi-pi interaction with PHE409, HIS456, and ASN460. P217564 formed hydrogen bonds with ASP295, VAL296, and PHE409; hydrophobic interactions with TYR224, VAL296, LEU406, MET407, PHE409, MET410, TYR465, and TYR514; polar interactions with ASN418, HIS456, ASN460, and HIS461; positively charged interactions with ARG408 and LYS420; negatively charged interactions with ASP295 and ASP459; pi-pi stacking with HIS461. It is worth noting that the amino acid residues these compounds interacted with have been reported to be present in other experimental compounds’ interactions with the USP715.

The hit compounds also possessed drug-like properties, including molecular weights less than 500 Da, hydrogen bond donors > 5, hydrogen bond acceptors > 10, and favorable octanol-water partition coefficients (logP values). These characteristics suggest that they possess suitable physicochemical properties for further development as oral drugs, and we anticipate that this might enhance their bioavailability and efficacy. Interestingly, the hit compounds exhibited high absorption potential in the human intestinal wall and Caco-2 cell, better reinforcing their potential to serve as oral drugs. Of all the hit compounds, only Compound 78 and P217564 were found to be p-gp substrate and inhibitor, respectively. The ability of a compound to be a substrate of P-gp can adversely impact its pharmacokinetic profile and ultimately compromise its therapeutic effectiveness due to the efflux function of the protein31. However, the therapeutic effectiveness can be enhanced by increasing the concentration of the drug. Compared to the standard experimental inhibitor, the hit compounds of this study have a higher fraction in an unbound form, hence, a good percentage of the compound will be pharmacologically active upon administration32. The compounds were also predicted to be substrates for phase I metabolizing enzymes belonging to the CYP450 family. Compounds 78, 6, and 77 were predicted to be potential substrates of CYP3A4; Compounds 78 is a potential inhibitor of CYP3A4; Compounds 78 and 6 are potential inhibitors of CYP1A2. The potential of these compounds to serve as substrates of the CYP450 enzymes shows that they will be transformable into suitable substrates for phase II metabolizing enzymes, ultimately facilitating their eventual elimination from the body. While the drugs may be eventually eliminated from the body, their elimination via the renal route will be slow as they were predicted to be non-substrate of the renal OCT2 which mediates xenobiotics uptake into renal tubular cells33. While this may beneficial if the compounds require sustained therapeutic levels, however, the compounds can also be eliminated via other routes such as hepatic elimination. All the hit compounds were predicted to possess a safe toxicity profile, though they were predicted to be inhibitors of hERG II, whose function in the context of drug toxicity is not known compared to hERG I. The pIC50 of the hit compounds was predicted in order to assess their potential effectiveness before experimental validation, thereby providing an additional layer of screening and prioritization in our drug discovery process. Interestingly, despite the higher affinity of the compounds for USP7 compared to that of the standard experimental inhibitor, biological activity prediction revealed the experimental inhibitor will be more potent and at a lower concentration compared to the hit compounds.

The dynamic behaviors of USP7 upon the binding of the hit compounds were investigated; The analysis of the RMSD revealed the binding of the experimental inhibitor caused the most fluctuation in USP7 compared to the other hit compounds, as evident in Fig. 3A. The USP7-compound3 complex attains stability after 55 ns, conversely, USP7-compound77 attains stability after 35 ns. USP7-compound 6 and USP7-compound 7 attains stability after 50 ns. The RMSF profiles revealed USP7-P217564 and USP7-compound 3 complexes exhibited the highest fluctuation, while the other complexes exhibited similar fluctuation profiles. Interestingly, an average of three hydrogen bonds was formed between USP7 and P217564, with two persisting all through the period of the simulation. USP7-compound 77 appears to have the least number of stable hydrogen bonds among the complexes, with other complexes exhibiting at least two stable hydrogen bonds all through the period of the simulation. Also, the radius of gyration revealed all the complexes were compact as depicted in Fig. 3D.

Conclusion

Summarily, this study aimed to identify potential allosteric site modulators of USP7 from a library of eighty compounds using computer-driven techniques. The compound library was initially subjected to molecular docking to identify compounds with high binding affinities for the target, leading to the identification of eight compounds with a higher affinity for the target relative to the experimental inhibitor P217564. The top four compounds from the eight compounds were also found to stably interact with the allosteric site residues of the target as evidenced by their high binding free energies, more importantly, they interacted with key residues in this site. All the compounds were druglike, with no violation of the Ro5 parameters, and they possess also good pharmacokinetic parameters. Interestingly, the compounds showed potency as evidenced by the values of their predicted inhibitory potentials, and molecular dynamics simulation confirmed their stability. Conclusively, this study elucidated four compounds with the potential to modulate USP7 deubiquitination function via its allosteric site. These compounds, upon further in vitro and in vivo evaluation, could be potential additions to the anticancer arsenal.

Limitations of the study

Despite the promising results obtained in this study, there exists several limitations and they are as follows; the study utilized a relatively small compound library of eighty compounds, which may have restricted the discovery of additional potential allosteric modulators. Furthermore, the molecular dynamics simulation was conducted for only 100 nanoseconds, which, while useful for initial stability assessments, may not fully capture long-term dynamics or conformational changes of the protein-ligand complexes. The study’s reliance on in silico predictions for binding affinities and pharmacokinetic profiles, though valuable, does not replace the need for empirical validation. Experimental studies are essential to confirm the effectiveness and safety of the identified compounds. Additionally, the study lacks detailed structural insights into the specific interactions between the compounds and the allosteric site residues of USP7. Finally, while drug-likeness and pharmacokinetic parameters were predicted, these may not always reflect real-world behavior accurately, necessitating further experimental validation. Addressing these limitations in future research will be crucial for confirming the potential of the identified compounds and advancing them toward clinical development.

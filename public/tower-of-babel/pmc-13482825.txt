A hybrid NSGA II-CNN-LSTM model for optimal operation of hydropower reservoirs based on the day-ahead electricity price forecast
Authors: Mohammadreza Heidarpanah, Farhad Hooshyaripor, Meisam Fazeli, Mehrdad Goodarzi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Hydropower is one of the renewable and affordable energy sources, though it may cause environmental impacts that require sustainable management. To ensure sustainability, hydropower producers must adopt a multi-dimensional approach to reservoir operation, balancing reservoir conditions with the three pillars of sustainability: environmental requirements, societal energy demand, and electricity market prices. In hydropower plants (HPPs), both the volume of water released and the timing of release significantly impact profitability. Consequently, HPP operators must strategically forecast electricity prices while planning water discharges and power generation to align with peak demand periods. This study employs a novel hybrid model combining the CNN-LSTM for forecast of daily peak electricity prices and NSGA-II optimization tool for optimal operation of the Karun-IV hydropower reservoir. The hybrid model objective is maximizing the total revenue of the system while the minimum daily electricity generation over a 10-year operational period is maximized. The results demonstrate successful energy arbitrage where the reservoir acts as a virtual battery by storing water during low electricity demand periods and releasing it when demand for energy is higher. Results indicate that the combined NSGA II-CNN-LSTM tool can enhance the HPP income about 10.5% compared to the historical recorded data. This highlights the tangible benefits of integrating accurate market forecasts with the optimization tools, offering a valuable tool for energy operators.

Introduction

Growing awareness of climate change and need for more environment protection have led to a global attraction to renewable energy sources1. Hydropower as a clean, renewable, and affordable source of energy2 plays a key role in mitigating climate change. On the other hand, it may cause significant environmental impacts which can be mitigated through adaptive restoration practices, sustainable planning, interdisciplinary collaboration, and effective visual communication3. In addition to energy generation, a hydroelectric dam can serve some other purposes, including flood control and urban water supply. It has been shown that hydropower reservoirs can act as virtual batteries by storing water during periods of low electricity prices and releasing it when prices are high. This analogy highlights the potential of reservoir operations to shift energy from wet seasons to dry seasons, providing both economic benefits and enhanced grid flexibility4. This function is similar to emerging vehicle-to-grid systems, but reservoirs provide centralized, large-scale storage, making them particularly effective for seasonal energy shifting5. In a hydropower plant (HPP) system, the reservoir operation and energy generation depend upon a number of factors such as reservoir and inflow conditions and amount of energy demand in the consumption network. A hydropower generator requires an optimal operating rule for the reservoir to earn the highest possible benefit in the energy market6. In the market, electricity price fluctuates throughout the day4 and the energy producers sell their products to customers at the proposed prices. This significantly impacts customer consumption planning and management7. In the Pay-As-Bid (PAB) markets, payment to a generator whose bid is accepted is based on the generator’s proposed price. In such a market, forecasting electricity prices at the bidding stage as a fundamental challenge can significantly contribute to an increase in the economic revenue of HPP8. However, every predictive model has its uncertainty, and it needs to be quantified9. Hence, the system needs to be optimized and scheduled to have the best performance level. In this context, Feng et al. (2017) employed a multi-objective PSO (MOPSO) to optimize the power generation schedule of an HPP in China10. Aiming at maximizing power generation while minimizing the average ecological flow deficit index, Dai et al. (2017) developed a Non-dominated Sorting Genetic Algorithm II (NSGA-II) for the Three Gorges-Gezhouba cascade reservoirs. Srinivasan and Kumar (2018) applied NSGA-II to identify Pareto-optimal solutions for long-term reservoir operation, considering two conflicting objectives: minimizing total deficit and minimizing maximum deficit in the Sabarmati River in India11. Zhang et al. (2021) proposed a nonlinear long-term optimization method for cascade HPPs in China and Greece, considering electricity price curves12. Focusing on the maximum daily electricity price in the Iranian electricity market, Heidarpanah et al. (2023) evaluated a number of models to predict the day-ahead electricity price in the Iran’s energy market and concluded that Convolution Neural Networks-Long Short-Term Memory (CNN-LSTM) with the lowest forecast error had the best forecast accuracy among the models8. Nguyen et al. (2023) studied the optimal operation of multi-purpose reservoirs in the Red River Basin in Vietnam for maximizing hydropower generation from three cascade reservoirs. Their findings indicated that the economic benefits of storing water for peak-hour generation (after winter-spring cultivation) were increased in their proposed model13. Wu et al. (2023) applied a deep reinforcement learning framework with a transformer-based architecture for multi-reservoir optimization, showing improvements in electricity production (~ 10.1%) and revenue (~ 4.1%) compared to conventional methods14. Zhang et al. (2024) developed a medium- and long-term hydropower generation forecasting model using Transformer + SE-Attention + LSTM, achieving significantly higher forecast accuracy (~ 33.8% better than LSTM alone)15. Giannelos et al. (2024) examined the Pareto frontier in power system operation, highlighting trade-offs between operational costs and CO₂ emissions across CHP, heat-only, and thermal units, and revealing key patterns in economic-environmental interactions16. Heidarpanah et al. (2025) evaluated the uncertainty of ANN and CNN-LSTM models for forecast of maximum daily electricity price using Monte Carlo simulation and concluded that. the latter has less uncertainty9.Wang et al. (2025) showed that hybrid machine learning and optimization approaches improved hydropower forecasting accuracy and operational efficiency, highlighting the value of integrating forecasts with reservoir scheduling17. Giannelos et al. (2025) proposed a multi-stage stochastic optimization model for electricity network expansion under composite uncertainty. Using multi-cut Benders decomposition, their case study on a 123-bus network shows how smart technology investments can be evaluated for strategic decision-making18.

According to the above researches, optimization techniques has been very efficient tools in the process of HPP operation. On the other hand, it has been shown that recent artificial intelligence models can provide precise forecasts for the electricity price in a PAB market. Considering these two important findings, in the current paper, an optimization tool together with a daily electricity price forecasting model is used to optimize short-term Karun-IV dam reservoir operation in Iran. The main goal is to present the best daily release plan for obtaining the maximum revenue from electricity generation. To do so, a robust electricity price forecasting model is required to simulate the maximum day-ahead price in the Iranian PAB energy market. Therefore, a CNN-LSTM method that has been proven to have better performance in electricity forecasting compared to several well-known tools8 is employed in this paper. On the other hand, the NSGA-II a robust optimization tool in water and hydropower fields of study10,11,19 is employed for optimal operation of the reservoir. Then the above models are coupled for optimal hydropower generation scheduling at Karun-IV Dam. It should be noted that, while there is extensive research reported on the optimal operation of hydropower reservoirs, there has been less focus on optimizing reservoir operation based on the maximum electricity price forecasting that is necessary for HPPs. Previous studies have paid limited attention to electricity prices for optimal reservoir operation, often relying solely on price curves. In this study, we integrate day-ahead electricity price forecasts into a multi-objective optimization model, representing the main novelty of the research.

Study area and input data

St Karun-IV Dam is located in Lordegan County, southwest of Iran. It has been constructed across the Karun River where Bazoft and Kuhrang confluence each other. The dam is a concrete double curvature arch-type with a height of 230 m (dam crest elevation is + 1032 masl) from the foundation. Figure 2 shows the location of the Karun-IV Dam and its HPP in Iran.

Geographical location of the study area (figure was created in the Quantum GIS, version 3.12.2; photograph was taken by the authors).

Currently, the Karun-IV Dam is considered as the tallest dam in Iran. It has created a reservoir of 41 km long with a capacity of 2232 mcm and a surface area of 29.7 Km2. The annual energy production of Karun-IV Dam is 2107 GWH with an installed capacity of 1000 MW consisting of four 250 MW units20. The Karun-IV HPP works with an average efficiency of 80%20. Every unit has been designed for 171 m3/s discharge at the nominal head of 191 m. The spillway capacity of the Karun-4 Dam is approximately 6,150 (m³/s). The minimum operation level (MOL) of the power plant is + 996 masl. Power intake (water intake) level: 960 m above sea level. As a large power plant, the purpose of the Karun-IV dam construction is to generate energy and control floods21. The total length of the penstock tunnel is 1390 m and the diameter of the tunnel is 6.5 m.

Decision variables

According to Eq. (5), increasing the release quantity increases energy production; although, Qt and Ht are inversely connected and any increase in Qt may lead to a decrease in Ht. Therefore, timing and amount of release in hydropower reservoirs is an important operational issue, as increasing the release without a coherent energy production plan can lead to waste of storage volume and consequently, economic losses. The reservoir operating rule (ROR), also known as the operating rule curve, is of particular importance in managing the release of a reservoir. Various structures can be used to define the operating rule in the genetic algorithm. The linear relationship is the simplest and most practical type of ROR that has been used in some conventional optimization methods. Although a linear rule curve for reservoir operation is simple and computationally efficient, it may fail to capture the nonlinear relationships among storage, inflow, and optimal release, especially given their seasonal variability. However, linearizing nonlinear relationships is a common and acceptable approach in classical optimization problems. Therefore, in this study, a set of monthly linear rule curves is incorporated into the optimization model to maintain computational efficiency. Using 12 separate linear relationships—one for each month—can serve as a practical substitute for a more complex nonlinear rule curve. Because nonlinear interactions among variables diminish when viewed at a monthly scale, a monthly linear rule curve may provide an adequate approximation. Therefore, this study uses a linear ROR type as follows22:16\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:{R}_{t}=a{I}_{t}+b{S}_{t}+c$$\end{document}

where a, b, and c are constant coefficients. The Rt cannot exceed the maximum penstock capacity (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:{R}_{max})$$\end{document} and should be more than the minimum designed capacity (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:{R}_{min}$$\end{document}); therefore:17\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:{R}_{min}\le\:{R}_{t}\le\:{R}_{max}$$\end{document}

The nominal head of the turbine is the minimum head at which the nominal capacity of the power plant can be generated when the turbine gate is fully open. Above the nominal head, the nominal capacity of the power plant can be fully generated. However, the energy generation cannot exceed the maximum capacity considering the power plant installed capacity (I-Cap) and the overload factor (OLF):18\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:{P}_{t}\le\:OLF\times\:I-Cap$$\end{document}

According to Figs. 8a and 10b, the optimization model has tried to keep the reservoir full of water as much as possible for maximum energy production with a minimum release during the year. In 2019, the total amount of reservoir storage was used for energy production because of the flood that had occurred. The maximum revenue (Table 3) and the highest spill (Fig. 9a) have also been observed in this year. It is worth noting that in 2020 and 2021, which were both dry periods followed by a wet year (Fig. 9a), the NSGA-II model was able to successfully keep the reservoir’s storage (Fig. 9b) and release (Fig. 9c) for energy generation as much as possible. Since the model is an operation model based on maximization of the revenue from energy production, it was able to increase the storage to the reservoir’s capacity in wet years when the energy production in the power plants was sufficient, and thus acts as a virtual battery to generate electricity during the next dry seasons during the energy demand.

Based on Fig. 10a, energy supply has been shifted from low-price energy periods (wet periods) to the period of more expensive energy production (dry periods), although the HPP in the NSGA-II model benefits from the winter inflows much more than the historical records. The model works correctly when one can find that by approaching the reservoir level to the minimum operating level (Fig. 10b), a wet year is started, and inflow starts to increase (Fig. 10a).

Conclusion

For an HPP operation to earn the highest revenue, it is required to forecast the maximum day-ahead electricity price that is expected to be traded in the market. Accordingly, this study investigated the optimal operation of the Karun-IV hydropower reservoir based on predicting the day-ahead maximum electricity price in the Iranian PAB market. The main novelty of this study is integration of day-ahead electricity price forecasts that are obtained through the CNN-LSTM model and the multi-objective optimization process. Unlike traditional models that rely on historical averages or simpler methods, this approach enables proactive responses to market fluctuations, improving both revenue and operational efficiency. Next, NSGA-II with the forecasted daily maximum electricity price data was used to determine the best operating rule curve for the Karun-IV dam. It should be noted that the electricity price forecasting model is trained on 2020–2022 data but applied to optimize 2012–2021 reservoir operations. This temporal mismatch neglects market dynamics, pricing structures, and regulatory frameworks likely changed over this decade. However, it can extract stationary structural relationships between system states and economic outcomes. Then the optimization tool evaluates counterfactual operational policies, not historical decision realism. This corresponds to a retrospective policy evaluation framework rather than a predictive back test.

The optimal results showed that the reservoir storage during wet periods was transferred to dry periods. In other words, cheap electricity that can be generated during wet periods was stored to meet the demand during dry periods when the electricity is more expensive. The proposed model ensured that the reservoir had been operating throughout the entire period, even during dry periods. The HPP was able to generate the minimum daily energy required by defining a conflict objective function to the maximum revenue objective. Therefore, by generating the minimum daily energy production and strategically releasing water, the reservoir could increase the power plant’s revenue. This demonstrates that the proposed model can effectively optimize the operation of the Karun-IV hydropower reservoir based on the maximum day-ahead electricity price forecasts. The model can increase reservoir revenue by transferring water from wet periods to dry periods and by generating the minimum required energy in a day. It also ensures that the HPP operates at all times, even during dry periods. The results indicate that the combined CNN-LSTM-NSGA-II model can enhance the revenue by an average of 10.5% over current operational practices. This increase represents a theoretical upper bound under full-structure knowledge, not an achievable operational gain under historical information constraints. Anyhow, this finding highlights the tangible benefits of integrating accurate market forecasts with multi-objective reservoir management strategies, offering a valuable tool for energy operators.

On the other hand, this study has several limitations and it is suggested to be considered for future investigations; i.e. uncertainty analysis on the price forecasts, inflow to the reservoir, model parameters, and constraint violations. It is worth mentioning that the optimization process in this paper was conducted over a limited historical data in a deterministic view, where in a real-world forecasting and operational context, future inflows that are uncertain. As this study does not account for hydrological forecasting uncertainty, incorporating stochastic inflows or ensemble weather forecasts is suggested as a direction for future research. Furthermore, future work could enhance the flexibility and performance of reservoir operation and electricity market optimization models by integrating machine learning and optimization techniques similar to those used by Giannelos et al. (2025) in coal distribution networks28. As another suggestion, future studies can use many other high-performance models (e.g. Transformer, XGBoost) as alternatives to CNN-LSTM in the hybrid model and evaluate the results. Also, input variables like weather conditions, holidays, and demand fluctuations that were not included in this study as the effective inputs for day-ahead price forecasting, are suggested to be considered in the future investigations.

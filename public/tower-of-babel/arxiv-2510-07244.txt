Geometry of dyadic polygons II: Isomorphisms of dyadic triangles
A. Mućka 1, A.B. Romanowska 2

Abstract
. This paper is the second part of a two-part paper investigating the structure and properties of dyadic polygons. A dyadic polygon is the intersection of the dyadic subplane 𝔻 2 \mathbb{D}^{2} of the real plane ℝ 2 \mathbb{R}^{2} and a real convex polygon with vertices in the dyadic plane. Such polygons are described as subreducts (subalgebras of reducts) of the affine dyadic plane 𝔻 2 \mathbb{D}^{2} , or equivalently as commutative, entropic and idempotent groupoids under the binary operation of arithmetic mean. The first part of the paper contained a new classification of dyadic triangles, considered as such groupoids, and a characterization of dyadic triangles with a pointed vertex. This second part investigates isomorphisms of dyadic triangles, and provides a full classification of their isomorphism types.

We now just recall some basic notation and facts.
In what follows,
\mathbb{R}
denotes the ring of real numbers, and
\mathbb{D}=\mathbb{Z}[1/2]
is its subring consisting of dyadic rationals (i.e., rational numbers whose denominator is a power of
2

-spaces of interest here are idempotent reducts of faithful

\mathbb{R}
-spaces. Dyadic polygons are the intersections of the dyadic

Real convex sets are described algebraically as certain barycentric algebras
(B,\underline{I}^{o})
, subsets of a space
\mathbb{R}^{n}

The operation

[
12

-modes.

Dyadic convex sets

]

In this paper we are interested in the one-dimensional affine
\mathbb{D}
-space

, and the two-dimensional affine
\mathbb{D}
-plane
\mathbb{D}^{2}
.

Recall that automorphisms of the affine
\mathbb{D}
-space

form the

-dimensional

\mathrm{GA}(n,\mathbb{D})
over the ring
\mathbb{D}
, the group generated by the linear group

and the group of translations of the space
\mathbb{D}^{n}
.

Dyadic convex sets considered here are the intersections of convex subsets

of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with the subspace
\mathbb{D}^{n}
, and are considered as subgroupoids
(B,\circ)
of the reduct
(\mathbb{D}^{n},\circ)
of the affine
\mathbb{D}
-space
\mathbb{D}^{n}
.
In particular, a

-dimensional polytope
is the intersection with the dyadic space

of an
n
-dimensional real polytope whose vertices lie in the dyadic space.
For any dyadic

-dimensional polytope

P=\mathrm{conv}_{\mathbb{R}}(P)\cap\mathbb{D}^{n}=\mathrm{conv}_{\mathbb{D}}(P),

where

is the convex

-hull of

-hull of

are one-dimensional polytopes.
Polygons of
\mathbb{D}^{2}

Unlike the real case, there are infinitely many pairwise non-isomorphic dyadic intervals, and infinitely many pairwise non-isomorphic dyadic triangles.

A special role is played by dyadic simplices. Dyadic simplices may be defined in similar fashion to real simplices. An

-dimensional

is an

-dimensional dyadic convex set with

vertices, generated by these vertices. Among dyadic polygons, all dyadic intervals isomorphic to the dyadic unit interval are

-dimensional simplices, and all

-dimensional simplices are isomorphic to the dyadic triangle generated by the three elements
e_{0}=(0,0)
,
e_{1}=(1,0)
and

of
\mathbb{D}^{2}
.

Dyadic polytopes are considered as
\circ
-subreducts of their affine
\mathbb{D}
-hulls. After introducing coordinate axes in the affine hull, a given polytope is located in the corresponding
\mathbb{D}
-module by providing the coordinates of its vertices. Affine
\mathbb{D}
-spaces of dimension

-hulls.

3.
Dyadic triangles

In this section we recall basic facts from

3.1.
Dyadic intervals

Each non-trivial interval of

, considered as a
\mathcal{CB}
-mode, is isomorphic
to an interval of

\mathbb{D}_{k}=[0,k]

[
8
]

precisely when their right hand ends are equal.
The interval

is generated by its ends, and the interval
\mathbb{D}_{k}
with

generated by three (but not fewer) elements.

3.2.
Dyadic triangles and representative hats

Each dyadic triangle contained in the plane
\mathbb{D}^{2}
is isomorphic to a
pointed
triangle
ABC
in the first quadrant of the plane, located as in Figure
1
, and denoted

Figure 1.

Originally, the triangles

were divided into three groups:

(whose shorter sides are parallel to the coordinate axes),

(one of whose sides is parallel to a coordinate axis) and

Each side of

is a dyadic interval, so it is isomorphic
to some

boundary type

(r,s,t)
, defined up to cyclic order. The triangles

are thought of as triangles with clockwise ordered vertices

and are denoted by

, determining cyclic boundary type corresponding to

. Isomorphic dyadic triangles with the same orientation of vertices have the same boundary type.

In
[
9
, Prop. 4.5]
, it was shown that each triangle
T_{i,j,m,n}
is isomorphic to a triangle
T_{i^{\prime},j^{\prime},m^{\prime},0}
with a (positive or arbitrary) integer
i^{\prime}
and positive integers
j^{\prime},m^{\prime}
. Moreover, all three integers
i^{\prime},j^{\prime},m^{\prime}
may be choosen to be odd. (
[
9
, Prop. 4.11]
.

In this paper, the name

will be used for dyadic hat triangles

with any integer

j,m
. Sometimes, we will use the name

for the case where

or

A triangle

Theorem 3.1
.

[
9
, Thm. 4.13]

Each dyadic triangle in the dyadic space
\mathbb{D}^{2}
is isomorphic to a representative hat.

Note that each dyadic triangle

is isomorphic to three
representative hats with the same ordering of the vertices, and to three isomorphic hats with the
reverse ordering. These hats correspond to the six permutations of the vertices.

3.3.
Pointed representative hats

A hat with a pointed vertex located at the
origin will be called a
pointed
hat, and isomorphisms of pointed hats will be considered as isomorphisms preserving the pointed vertex
and the orientation of the vertices. This type of isomorphism will be called a
pointed
oriented isomorphism
, or briefly a

.

Proposition 3.2
.

[
9
, Cor. 5.3]

For any positive odd integers

and
m
, there are

(pointed) isomorphism classes of
representative hats

. Each class is represented by a unique
T_{i,j,m}
, where

.

The triple
(i,j,m)
of Proposition

is called an

. Each encoding triple determines uniquely a class of (pointed) representative hats isomorphic to a given dyadic triangle. We note the following.

Theorem 3.3
.

Two pointed oriented dyadic triangles are isomorphic if and only if they have the same encoding triples.

4.
Isomorphisms of dyadic triangles

Recall that by an isomorphism between dyadic triangles we mean an isomorphism of the triangles
considered as commutative binary modes. An automorphism of the plane
\mathbb{D}^{2}
is an automorphism
of the affine
\mathbb{D}
-space
\mathbb{D}^{2}
.
Then an isomorphism between dyadic triangles contained in
\mathbb{D}^{2}
extends uniquely to an
automorphism of the affine dyadic plane
\mathbb{D}^{2}
. On the other hand, each automorphism
\iota

of the plane
\mathbb{D}^{2}
restricts to the isomorphism between each triangle in
\mathbb{D}^{2}
and its
image under
\iota
. So, there is a one-to-one correspondence between isomorphisms of triangles
in
\mathbb{D}^{2}
and automorphisms of the plane
\mathbb{D}^{2}
.

As each dyadic triangle is isomorphic to a representative hat, our interest focuses on
isomorphisms between pairs of representative hats.
Let
T
be a representative hat
T_{i,j,m}
. In what follows, we use the notation
T=ABC

precisely when
A,B,C
are the vertices of
T
,
A
is the vertex located at the origin,
and the vertices
A=(0,0),B=(i,j)
and
C=(m,0)
are oriented clockwise. In this situation, we say that
T_{i,j,m}
is

presented
as
ABC
, and that
ABC
is a
presentation
of
T_{i,j,m}
.

First let us summarise necessary conditions for two representative hats to be isomorphic.

Proposition 4.1
.

[
9
, §§3,5]

If two representative hats
T=T_{i,j,m}
and
T^{\prime}=T_{i^{\prime},j^{\prime},m^{\prime}}
are isomorphic, then the
following two conditions hold.

(a)

The hats

and
T^{\prime}
have equal or oppositely oriented boundary types.

(b)

The areas of the convex

-hulls
\mathrm{conv}_{\mathbb{R}}(T)
of

and

\mathrm{conv}_{\mathbb{R}}(T^{\prime})
of
T^{\prime}
are equal.

Moreover, if a mapping

is an isomorphism, then it maps the set

of vertices of
T
onto the set
V(T^{\prime})
of vertices of
T^{\prime}
.

As shown in
[
9
, §§4,5]
, none of the necessary conditions of

Assuming that two representative hats

have equal or oppositely oriented boundary
types, and the same areas of their convex

-hulls, we
will look for some additional conditions guaranteeing that the hats are isomorphic. To avoid
misunderstanding, from now on, we denote the boundary type of a triangle

by

is the type of

If

5.
Automorphisms of representative hats

Consider again a representative hat
T=T_{i,j,m}
. The results of
[
7
, §6]
imply
that
T
is generated by the union of its sides. Hence each automorphism of
T
is determined by its restriction to the vertex set
V(T)
.
Since each automorphism of
T
maps the vertex set to itself, and the
permutations of the vertices of a hat form the symmetric group
S_{3}
, it follows that
T
may
have one, two, three or six automorphisms. Two of them correspond to cycles of length
3
,
while three correspond to cycles of length
2
.

We start our investigation of automorphisms of representative hats with investigation of
automorphisms of certain special hats.

5.1.
Automorphisms of hats
T_{m,km,2m,0}

The only representative hat which is a simplex is the hat
T_{1,1,1}
, isomorphic to the hat

T_{1,1,2,0}
and to the right triangle
T_{1,1}
. Its boundary type is
(1,1,1)
.

Lemma 5.1
.

The representative simplex
T_{1,1,1}
has precisely six automorphisms, corresponding to the six permutations of its vertices.

Proof.

Since the simplex
T_{1,1}
is a free commutative binary mode, the vertices are its free
generators, and each of its automorphisms permutes the three vertices. It
follows that
T_{1,1}
, and hence also
T_{1,1,1}
, have precisely six automorphisms determined
by the permutations of the vertices.
∎

Lemma
5.1
generalizes easily to the representative hats
T_{m,m,m}
for any
positive odd integer
m
. First note that
T_{m,m,m}
is isomorphic
to the right hat
T_{m,m}
, which is isomorphic to
T_{0,m,2m,0}
and to
T_{m,m,2m,0}
.

Lemma 5.2
.

The representive hat
T_{m,m,m}
has six automorphisms.

Proof.

Let the right hat
T_{m,m}
be presented as
ABC
.
By
[
9
, Lemma 3.2]
,
T_{m,m}
has an automorphism fixing the vertex
A
located at the
origin and exchanging the remaining two vertices. On the other hand,
T_{m,m}
is isomorphic to the hat
T_{0,m,2m,0}
, which is isomorphic to the hat
T_{m,m,2m,0}
presented as
T=AB^{\prime}C^{\prime}
, with
B^{\prime}
the image of

and
C^{\prime}
the image of

. By
[
9
, Lemma 3.2]
again,

has an automorphism fixing the vertex
B^{\prime}
and exchanging the vertices
A
and
C^{\prime}
.
It follows that the representative hat
T_{m,m,m}
has two non-trivial automorphisms, each
corresponding to a cycle of length
2
. Since any two distinct transpositions generate the group

,
it follows that
T_{m,m,m}
has six automorphisms.
∎

Lemma 5.3
.

If a representative hat

has a non-trivial automorphism,
then at least two side types of
T
are equal.

Proof.

Let
\iota
be a non-trivial automorphism of
T
whose restriction to the vertex set

forms a cycle of length
3
. Since each automorphism of
T
preserves the side types,
it follows that the three side types are equal. A similar argument shows that if
\iota
is an
automorphism whose restriction to

is a cycle of length

, then two of the side types are equal.
∎

Corollary 5.4
.

Let
T
be a representative hat. If all three side types of

are distinct, then

T
has no non-trivial automorphisms.

Corollary 5.5
.

Let
T
be a representative hat. If two side types of

are equal, and distinct from the third, then

has at most one non-trivial automorphism.

Proof.

By Lemma
5.3
, if
T
has two side types equal and distinct from the third, then the restriction of a non-trivial automorphism of
T
to the vertex set

cannot form a cycle of length

. Thus, a non-trivial automorphism of
T
should preserve one vertex and exchange the remaining two. Since any two transpositions of the symmetric group
S_{3}
generate a cycle of length
3
, and not all side types of
T
are equal, it follows that
T
may have at most one non-trivial automorphism.
∎

The class of right hats

If the three side types of

Note then that

It should fix the vertex

\displaystyle M_{B^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}0&-j\\
kj&-j\end{bmatrix}^{-1}\begin{bmatrix}kj&-j\\
0&-j\end{bmatrix}=\frac{1}{kj^{2}}\begin{bmatrix}-j&j\\
-kj&0\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}kj&-j\\
0&-j\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{kj^{2}}\begin{bmatrix}-kj^{2}&0\\
-k^{2}j^{2}&kj^{2}\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}-1&0\\
-k&1\end{bmatrix}.

Since

, the matrix is invertible. Hence

is a non-trivial automorphism fixing
B^{\prime}
and exchanging
A^{\prime}
and
C^{\prime}
.

Now assume that
\gcd\{j,m\}=m
. Then
T_{j,m}=T_{km,m}
for some odd integer

, and

is isomorphic to

Summarising, we have the following proposition.

Proposition 5.9
.

Consider a right hat

. If the three side types of

are distinct, then

has no non-trivial automorphisms. If

has six distinct

has one non-trivial automorphism.

5.3.
Automorphisms of hats with two side types equal

If a representative hat

non-trivial automorphism, then it fixes the vertex common to the sides of the same type, and
exchanges the remaining vertices.

First note an obvious but frequently used property of side types of right hats. If

. Then note that
(
4.1

Lemma 5.10

Let

be a representative hat

If

Lemma 5.11

Let

Proof.

First we translate the hat

fixing the vertex

\displaystyle M_{B^{\prime}}=\begin{bmatrix}-i&-j\\

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{jm}\begin{bmatrix}-jm&0\\

5.4.

The last case to consider is the case of representative hats

Corollary 5.20
.

Let
T=ABC
be a representative hat
T_{i,j,m}
with

, and with boundary type

(m,m,m)
. Then

(a)

i=km
and

for some relatively prime odd integers

and

;

(b)

m-i=nm
for some even integer

.

Remark 5.21

In particular Corollary

implies that the hat

of Corollary

is always crooked. If

, then

It is clear that if a representative hat

of boundary type

T
has a non-trivial automorphism not fixing any of the vertices of

, then this isomorphism must correspond to a cycling of the three vertices, and generates the cyclic group
C_{3}
. So we are left with the following question.

Question 5.22

Is there a (representative) hat with the cyclic group
C_{3}
as its group of automorphisms?

The following proposition provides a positive answer to this question.

Proposition 5.23
.

Let
T=ABC
be a representative hat
T_{i,j,m}
of boundary type
(m,m,m)
. Let
\varphi:T\rightarrow T
be a mapping. Then the following statements hold.

(a)

The mapping
\varphi
is an automorphism of the hat
T
taking either
A
to
B
,
B
to
C
and
C
to
A
or
A
to
C
,
B
to
A
and
C
to

precisely when
l
divides
k^{2}-k+1
, with

and

defined as in Corollary
5.20
.

(b)

The automorphism group of

contains a subgroup isomorphic to the cyclic group

if and only if

divides
k^{2}-k+1
.

(c)

If
T
has no automorphisms fixing precisely one vertex, then the automorphism
group of
T
is isomorphic to the cyclic group
C_{3}
if and only if

divides
k^{2}-k+1
.

Proof.

(a) First note that by Corollary
5.20
,
i=km
and
j=lm
for some relatively prime

and
l
. Then translate the hat
T
to the isomorphic hat
T^{\prime}=A^{\prime}B^{\prime}C^{\prime}
with

A^{\prime}=(-m,0)
,
B^{\prime}=(i-m,j)
and
C^{\prime}=(0,0)
. The following matrix
M
provides an
isomorphism from the hat
T^{\prime}
onto the hat
T
, taking
A^{\prime}
to
B
,
B^{\prime}
to
C
, and
C^{\prime}
to

.

\displaystyle M

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}i-m&j\\
-m&0\end{bmatrix}^{-1}\begin{bmatrix}m&0\\
i&j\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}-i/m&-j/m\\
(m^{2}-im+i^{2})/mj&(-m+i)/m\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}-k&-l\\
(1-k+k^{2})/l&k-1\end{bmatrix}.

Note that
\det M=1
, and that the matrix

is dyadic precisely when

is an integer dividing

.

Similarly, the matrix
M^{\prime}
below provides an isomorphism from the hat
T^{\prime}
onto the hat

,
taking

to

,
B^{\prime}
to

, and

to

\displaystyle M^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}-i&-j\\
m-i&-j\end{bmatrix}^{-1}\begin{bmatrix}m&0\\

(mi-m^{2}-i^{2})/mj&-i/m\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}k-1&l\\
(-1+k-k^{2})/l&-k\end{bmatrix}.

The matrix

is dyadic precisely when

is an integer dividing

.

Let
\varphi
be the composition of the first translation and the isomorphism given by the matrix

or

. It is clear that

is an automorphism satisfying the required conditions precisely when

divides
k^{2}-k+1
.

Now (b) follows directly by (a), since each version of the automorphism

generate the other and the identity map. Then (c) is a consequence of (b).
∎

6.
Isomorphisms of representative hats

First recall that, by Proposition
4.1
, an isomorphism
between two hats maps the set of vertices of one hat onto the set of vertices of the other, and that isomorphic hats have equal or oppositely
oriented boundary types and equal areas of their convex
\mathbb{R}
-hulls. Note as well that an
isomorphism between two hats preserves the types of the sides.
As the following example shows, all these conditions together do not guarantee that two hats are isomorphic.

Example 6.1
.

The representative hats
T_{3,27,21}
and
T_{39,27,21}
both have the boundary type
(3,9,21)
, and the area of the convex
\mathbb{R}
-hull of each equals

.
However, by
[
9
, Prop. 5.1]
,
they are not isomorphic.

We will use the following consequence of
[
9
, Rem. 4.10, Prop. 4.11, Rem. 4.12]
.

Proposition 6.2
.

Let
T=ABC
be the hat with the vertices
A=(0,0)
,
B=(i,j)
and
C=(m,0)
, where
i
is any integer and
j,m
are positive integers.
Then
T
may be specified, in terms of the parameters

, in each of the following three
ways:

(a)

as

with odd

and odd
\gcd\{i,m\}
,

(b)

as
T_{i,j,m}=T_{i,j,m,0}
, where all
i,j,m
are odd,

(c)

as
T_{i,j,m,0}
with even
i
, odd
j
and odd

.

A hat

, where

is any integer, and both

and

are positive odd integers,

and denoted

), and if

is even, then

is

. By Remark

[

]

and

To make further calculations easier we will use the following notation. Let
T

be two almost representative hats. If

and

are oriented clockwise. This fact will be denoted briefly as

. Note also that there are six types of isomorphisms between

and

corresponding to the six permutations of the vertices

and

of

.

precisely when

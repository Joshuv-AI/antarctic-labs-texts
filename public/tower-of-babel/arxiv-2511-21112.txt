On Coalition Graphs and Coalition Count of Graphs


Abstract
Let G G be graph with vertex set V ⁡ ( G ) V(G) and order n n . A set S ⊆ V ⁡ ( G ) S\subseteq V(G) is a dominating set of a graph G G if every vertex in V ⁡ ( G ) \ S V(G)\backslash S is adjacent to at least one vertex in S S . A coalition in a graph G G consists of two disjoint sets of vertices V 1 V_{1} and V 2 V_{2} , neither of which is a dominating set but whose union V 1 ∪ V 2 V_{1}\cup V_{2} is a dominating set. A coalition partition, abbreviated c c -partition, in a graph G G is a vertex partition π = { V 1 , V 2 , … , V k } \pi=\left\{V_{1},V_{2},\dots,V_{k}\right\} such that every set V i V_{i} of π \pi is either a singleton dominating set, or is not a dominating set but forms a coalition with another set V j V_{j} in π \pi . The sets V i V_{i} and V j V_{j} are coalition partners in G G . The coalition number C ⁡ ( G ) C(G) equals the maximum order k k of a c c -partition of G G . For any graph G G with a c c -partition π = { V 1 , V 2 , … , V k } \pi=\left\{V_{1},V_{2},\dots,V_{k}\right\} , the coalition graph C ​ G ​ ( G , π ) CG(G,\pi) of G G is a graph with vertex set V 1 , V 2 , … , V k V_{1},V_{2},\dots,V_{k} , corresponding one-to-one with the set π \pi , and two vertices V i V_{i} and V j V_{j} are adjacent in C ​ G ​ ( G , π ) CG(G,\pi) if and only if the sets V i V_{i} and V j V_{j} are coalition partners in π \pi . In [ 4 ] , authors proved that for every graph G G there exist a graph H H and c c -partition π \pi such that C ​ G ​ ( H , π ) ≅ G CG(H,\pi)\cong G , and raised the question: Does there exist a graph H ∗ H^{*} of smaller order n ∗ n^{*} and size m ∗ m^{*} with a c c -partition π ∗ \pi^{*} such that C ​ G ​ ( H ∗ , π ∗ ) ≅ G CG(H^{*},\pi^{*})\cong G ?. In this paper, we constructed a graph H ∗ H^{*} of small order and size and a c c - partition π ∗ \pi^{*} such that C ​ G ​ ( H ∗ , π ∗ ) ≅ G CG(H^{*},\pi^{*})\cong G . Recently, Haynes et al. [ 5 ] defined the coalition count c ⁡ ( G ) c(G) of a graph G G as the maximum number of different coalition in any c c -partition of G G . We characterize all graphs G G with c ⁡ ( G ) = 1 c(G)=1 . Further, imposing some suitable conditions on coalition number, we study the properties of coalition count of graph.

The

Theorem 3.1
.

For any graph

Proof.

Assume

are minimal dominating sets. If any

(for

to

Suppose

Proof.

(N[x]\backslash U)\cap V_{i}=\phi

Suppose

is not a dominating set and

vertices out of which the vertex corresponding to the set
\left\{u_{1}\right\}
do not form coalition with any other vertices, it will be an isolated vertex in
CG(G,\pi)
. Let
H
be the another component of
CG(G,\pi)
. Then the remaining
s-1

\left\{u_{2},u_{3},\dots,u_{p}\right\}
form coalition with all
s-2

. Thus,

. ∎

A coalition partition
\pi
of
G
is a singleton coalition partition if every set in
\pi
consists of a single vertex. If a graph
G
has a singleton
coalition partition, then
G
is referred to as a singleton-partition graph (SP-graph).

Next, we relate coalition number
C(G)
of graph
G
with coalition count of
G
. We omit the proof as it is straight forward.

Remark 3.5
.

For any graph
G
with
f
full vertices having coalition number
C(G)
and coalition count
c(G)
,
c(G)\geq\lceil{\dfrac{C(G)-f}{2}}\rceil
.

Theorem 3.6
.

For any SP-graph
G
of order
n
with no full vertices,
c(G)\geq\alpha(G)
.

Proof.

Let
G
is a singleton-partition graph. Then
\pi=\left\{\left\{v_{1}\right\},\left\{v_{2}\right\},\dots,\left\{v_{n}\right\}\right\}
is a
c
-partition. Since
G
has no full vertex, each set
V_{i}
must form a coalition with some set
V_{j}
,
1\leq i<j\leq n
. By remark
3.5
,
c(G)\geq\lceil{\dfrac{n}{2}}\rceil
.
If
\alpha(G)\leq\lceil{\dfrac{n}{2}}\rceil
, then clearly result holds. Suppose
\alpha(G)\geq\lceil{\dfrac{n}{2}}\rceil+1
. Then there are at least
\lceil{\dfrac{n}{2}}\rceil+1
vertices in
G
are independent. Let
I=\left\{v_{1},v_{2},\dots,v_{\alpha}\right\}
is the maximum independent set. Further, it is easy to observe that any set in
\pi
corresponding to the vertices of
I
will form coalition with the set corresponding to the vertex not in
I
. Thus, there will be at least
\alpha
coalitions. Therefore,
\alpha(G)\leq c(G)
.∎

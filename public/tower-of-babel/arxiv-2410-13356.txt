On the second eigenvalue of the infinity Laplacian with Robin boundary conditions
Vincenzo Amato, Alba Lia Masiello, Carlo Nitsch, Cristina Trombetti

Abstract
We study the behaviour, as p → + ∞ p\to+\infty , of the second eigenvalues of the p p -Laplacian with Robin boundary conditions and the limit of the associated eigenfunctions. We prove that, up to some regularity of the set, the limit of the second eigenvalues is actually the second eigenvalue of the so-called ∞ \infty -Laplacian. MSC 2020: 35J92, 35J94, 35P15. Keywords: p p -Laplacian, Robin boundary conditions, eigenvalue problem, infinity Laplacian.

Introduction

Let
\beta
be a positive parameter and let
\Omega
be a bounded and open set of
\R^{n}
,
n\geq 2
, with Lipschitz boundary.

We aim to study the limit, as
p
goes to
+\infty
, of
\lambda_{2,p}(\Omega)
, the second eigenvalue of the
p
-Laplace operator with Robin boundary conditions, that is to say, the second smallest value
\lambda
for which the following problem admits a nontrivial solution

\begin{cases}-\Delta_{p}u=\lambda{\left|u\right|}^{p-2}u&\text{ in }\Omega\\
{\left|\nabla u\right|}^{p-2}\displaystyle{\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}}+\beta^{p}{\left|u\right|}^{p-2}u=0&\text{ on }\partial\Omega.\end{cases}

We recall that, for
p\neq 2
, we do not have a complete characterization of all the possible eigenvalues of the
p
-Laplace operator, however, the first two can be precisely defined using a min-max principle. Furthermore, an increasing and divergent sequence of eigenvalues can be constructed, but it is not known whether it contains all the
p
-eigenvalues (see for example
[
6
,
9
,
15
]
).

In a previous paper,
[
1
]
, we studied the limit of
\lambda_{1,p}(\Omega)
, the first eigenvalue of the
p
-Laplace operator with Robin boundary conditions. In particular, we proved that

\lim_{p\to\infty}(\lambda_{1,p}(\Omega))^{\frac{1}{p}}=\lambda_{1,\infty}(\Omega):=\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}w\in W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)\\
{\left\|w\right\|}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}=1\end{subarray}}\max\left\{{\left\|\nabla w\right\|}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)},\beta{\left\|w\right\|}_{L^{\infty}(\partial\Omega)}\right\},

(1)

and
that the following geometric characterization holds

\lambda_{1,\infty}(\Omega)=\frac{1}{\displaystyle{\frac{1}{\beta}+r(\Omega)}},

(2)

where
r(\Omega)
is the inradius of the set
\Omega
, i.e., the radius of the largest ball contained in
\Omega
.

Moreover, for a set of class
C^{2}
, we showed that the right-hand side of (
2
) is, in fact, the first eigenvalue of an
\infty
-Laplacian problem, in the sense that it is the smallest value
\lambda
for which the following problem admits a nontrivial viscosity solution

\begin{cases}\min\Set{{\left|\nabla u\right|}-\lambda u,-\Delta_{\infty}u}=0&\text{ in }\Omega,\\
-\min\Set{{\left|\nabla u\right|}-\beta u,-\displaystyle{\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}}}=0&\text{ on }\partial\Omega,\end{cases}

(3)

where
\Delta_{\infty}
, the so-called
\infty
-Laplacian, is defined by

\Delta_{\infty}u=\left\langle D^{2}u\cdot\nabla u,\nabla u\right\rangle.

Hence, our goal is to conduct a similar analysis for
\lambda_{2,\infty}(\Omega)
, obtaining a variational characterization as in (
1
) for the limit of the second eigenvalue, giving it a geometrical characterization as in (
2
), and showing that this limit appears in a PDE problem analogous to (
3
).

A characterization of
\lambda_{2,\infty}(\Omega)
can be given in terms of the following geometric quantity

s(\Omega)={\max}\left\{t:\exists x_{1},x_{2}\in\overline{\Omega}\text{ such that }{\left|x_{1}-x_{2}\right|}\geq 2t,{\left\|C_{i,t}\right\|}_{L^{\infty}(\partial\Omega)}\leq\frac{1}{\beta t},\,i=1,2\right\},

(4)

where
C_{i,t}
is the function

C_{i,t}(x)=\frac{(t-{\left|x-x_{i}\right|})_{+}}{t}.

We stress that
{\left\|C_{i,t}\right\|}_{\infty}=1
, and the function is supported in
\overline{\Omega}\cap B(x_{i},t)
, where
B(x_{i},t)
is the ball centered at
x_{i}
with radius
t
.

Hence, we prove the following geometric characterization.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded Lipschitz set, let
\{\lambda_{2,p}(\Omega)\}
be the sequence of the second
p
-Laplace eigenvalues, and let
s(\Omega)
be the quantity defined in (
4
). Then, we have

\lim_{p\to\infty}(\lambda_{2,p}(\Omega))^{\frac{1}{p}}=\frac{1}{s(\Omega)}=:\lambda^{\beta}_{2,\infty}(\Omega).

Throughout the article, when it is clear, we will refer to this quantity only as
\lambda_{2,\infty}(\Omega)
, omitting the superscript
\beta
.

Later, we define

\mathcal{S}=\left\{v\in W^{1,\infty}(\Omega):{\left\|v\right\|}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}=1\right\},

and we consider
u_{1,\infty}
to be the limit of the first Robin
p
-eigenfunctions, normalized such that
{\left\|u_{1,\infty}\right\|}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}=1
.

We have the following variational characterization for
\lambda_{2,\infty}(\Omega)
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\Gamma
be the set of all the continuous paths in
\mathcal{S}
joining
u_{1,\infty}
to
-u_{1,\infty}
. Hence

\lambda_{2,\infty}(\Omega)=\displaystyle{\inf_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\sup_{u\in\gamma}\max\left\{{\left\|\nabla u\right\|}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)},\beta{\left\|u\right\|}_{L^{\infty}(\partial\Omega)}\right\}}.

Finally, for a particular class of domains, we show that
\lambda_{2,\infty}(\Omega)
is the second eigenvalue of the
\infty
-Laplacian. To better understand the meaning of "eigenvalues of the
\infty
-Laplacian", we refer to Section
2
and problem (
9
), which has to be understood in a viscosity sense.
We then prove the following theorem.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded and convex set of class
C^{2}
, and let
u
be a viscosity solution to (
9
) for some
\lambda>0
. Assume that
u
has at least two nodal domains, then

\lambda\geq\lambda_{2,\infty}(\Omega).

Unfortunately, for a general Lipschitz set, it is still unclear whether
\lambda_{2,\infty}
is exactly the second eigenvalue or not.

This paper continues a series of papers on this topic.
Similar results were obtained in the case of Dirichlet, Robin, and Neumann boundary conditions in
[
3
,
7
,
11
,
12
,
13
,
17
]
and for the Steklov eigenvalue in
[
2
,
8
]
.

To be more precise, in
[
11
,
12
]
, Juutinen, Lindqvist, and Manfredi studied the Dirichlet case as
p\rightarrow+\infty
. They provided a complete characterization of the limiting problems in terms of geometric quantities.
Indeed, the first two eigenvalues of the
p
-Laplace operator,
\{\lambda_{1,p}^{D}\}
and
\{\lambda_{2,p}^{D}\}
, satisfy

\lim_{p\to\infty}\left(\lambda_{1,p}^{D}\right)^{{1}/{p}}=\lambda_{1,\infty}^{D}=:\frac{1}{r(\Omega)}\quad\text{ and }\quad\lim_{p\to\infty}\left(\lambda_{2,p}^{D}\right)^{{1}/{p}}=\lambda_{2,\infty}^{D}=:\frac{1}{r_{2}(\Omega)},

where

r(\Omega)=\sup\{t:\exists B_{t}(x)\subseteq\Omega\},

and

r_{2}(\Omega)=\sup\{t:\exists B_{t}(x_{1}),B_{t}(x_{2})\subseteq\Omega\text{ such that }B_{t}(x_{1})\cap B_{t}(x_{2})=\emptyset\}.

The associated eigenfunctions
v^{D}_{1,p}
and
v^{D}_{2,p}
also converge (up to a subsequence) to some Lipschitz functions
v^{D}_{1,\infty}
and
v^{D}_{2,\infty}
. These two functions are the first and the second eigenfunctions of a natural viscosity formulation of the eigenvalue problem for the
\infty
-Laplacian (in the sense of Definition
2.1
).

The Neumann case appears to be more involved. It was investigated in
[
7
,
17
]
and similarly to the Dirichlet case, the authors established that the first nontrivial eigenvalues of the
p
-Laplacian
\{\lambda^{N}_{p}\}
satisfy

\lim_{p\to\infty}\left(\lambda^{N}_{p}\right)^{1/p}=\lambda_{\infty}^{N}:=\frac{2}{D(\Omega)},

where
D(\Omega)
is the intrinsic diameter of
\Omega
, i.e., the supremum of the geodesic distance between two points of
\Omega
.

However, as in Theorem
1.3
, they were able to show that the sequence of nontrivial first eigenvalues converges to the
first
eigenvalue and the
first
eigenfunction under the additional assumption of convexity on
\Omega
. Whether or not the same applies in the general case is still an open question.

Finally, in
[
16
]
, the authors examined the case of mixed Dirichlet and Robin boundary conditions.
This is a key point in our analysis. In proving our results, we will show that the limit of the second eigenfunctions changes sign, this implies that, on each nodal domain, this limit solves an eigenvalue problem with mixed Dirichlet and Robin boundary conditions.

More precisely, the authors considered
\Omega
to be a Lipschitz, bounded, open, and connected set, whose boundary is divided into two parts

\partial\Omega=\Gamma_{1}\cup\Gamma_{2}.

On
\Gamma_{1}
they considered the Dirichlet boundary conditions, while on
\Gamma_{2}
the Robin ones. They proved that the sequence of the first mixed eigenvalues converges to

\lambda_{\infty}^{\text{mix}}(\Omega)=\begin{cases}\displaystyle{\min_{x\in\Omega}\frac{1}{d(x,\Gamma_{1})}}&\text{if }\mathcal{A}=\emptyset,\\[8.61108pt]
\displaystyle{\min_{x\in\mathcal{A}}\frac{1}{\frac{1}{\beta}+d(x,\Gamma_{2})}}&\text{if }\mathcal{A}\neq\emptyset,\end{cases}

(5)

where the set
\mathcal{A}
is defined as follows

\mathcal{A}=\left\{x\in\overline{\Omega}:d(x,\Gamma_{1})\geq\frac{1}{\beta}+d(x,\Gamma_{2})\right\}.

The authors also proved that

\lim_{p\to\infty}\left(\lambda_{p}^{\text{mix}}(\Omega)\right)^{1/p}=\lambda_{\infty}^{\text{mix}}(\Omega)=\inf_{u\in X}\max\left\{{{\left\|\nabla u\right\|}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)},\beta{\left\|u\right\|}_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma_{2})}}\right\},

(6)

where

X=\left\{u\in W^{1,\infty}(\Omega):u=0\text{ on }\Gamma_{1},{\left\|u\right\|}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}=1\right\}.

(7)

A few final words on the connection between these three problems. From definition (
4
) and Theorem
1.1
, we observe that the following inequality holds

\frac{2}{D(\Omega)}\leq\lambda_{2,\infty}(\Omega)\leq\frac{1}{r_{2}(\Omega)},

where the lower and upper bounds correspond exactly to the Neumann and Dirichlet second

\infty
-eigenvalues, respectively.
Moreover, it can be proved that

\lim_{\beta\to+\infty}\lambda^{\beta}_{2,\infty}(\Omega)=\lambda^{D}_{2,\infty}(\Omega)=\frac{1}{r_{2}(\Omega)},

while from Definition (
4
), one can notice that for
\displaystyle{\beta\leq\frac{2}{D(\Omega)}}
, we have

\displaystyle{\lambda^{\beta}_{2,\infty}(\Omega)=\lambda_{\infty}^{N}(\Omega)=\frac{2}{D(\Omega)}}.

If we analyze the definition (
4
) of
s(\Omega)
, the condition

{\left\|C_{i,s(\Omega)}\right\|}_{L^{\infty}(\partial\Omega)}\leq\frac{1}{\beta s(\Omega)},

implies that the cones involved in this definition
satisfy an "infinity Robin boundary conditions"

\beta{\left\|C_{i,s(\Omega)}\right\|}_{L^{\infty}(\partial\Omega)}\leq{{\left\|\nabla C_{i,s(\Omega)}\right\|}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}}.

The latter condition becomes Dirichlet if
\beta
goes to infinity, and Neumann for a small value of
\beta
.

4

5

1.1

and

, respectively.

2.1
The viscosity
\infty
-eigenvalue problem

Before proceeding, we give the viscosity formulation of the eigenvalue problem for the
\infty
-Laplacian, see
[
4
]
for more details.

Definition 2.1
.

Let us consider the following boundary value problem

\begin{cases}F(x,u,\nabla u,\nabla^{2}u)=0&\text{ in }\Omega,\\
G(x,u,\nabla u)=0&\text{ on }\partial\Omega,\end{cases}

(8)

where
F:\R^{n}\times\R\times\R^{n}\times\R^{n\times n}\to\R
and
B:\R^{n}\times\R\times\R^{n}\to\R
are two continuous functions.

Viscosity supersolution

A lower semi-continuous function
u
is a viscosity supersolution to (
8
) if, whenever we fix
x_{0}\in\overline{\Omega}
, for every
\phi\in C^{2}(\overline{\Omega})
such that
u(x_{0})=\phi(x_{0})
and
x_{0}
is a strict minimum in
\Omega
for
u-\phi
, then

•

if
x_{0}\in\Omega
, the following holds

F\left(x_{0},\phi(x_{0}),\nabla\phi(x_{0}),\nabla^{2}\phi(x_{0})\right)\geq 0

•

if
x_{0}\in\partial\Omega
, the following holds

\max\Set{F\left(x_{0},\phi(x_{0}),\nabla\phi(x_{0}),\nabla^{2}\phi(x_{0})\right),G\left(x_{0},\phi(x_{0}),\nabla\phi(x_{0})\right)}\geq 0

Viscosity subsolution

An upper semi-continuous function
u
is a viscosity subsolution to (
8
) if, whenever we fix
x_{0}\in\overline{\Omega}
, for every
\phi\in C^{2}(\overline{\Omega})
such that
u(x_{0})=\phi(x_{0})
and
x_{0}
is a strict maximum in
\Omega
for
u-\phi
, then

•

if
x_{0}\in\Omega
, the following holds

F\left(x_{0},\phi(x_{0}),\nabla\phi(x_{0}),\nabla^{2}\phi(x_{0})\right)\leq 0

•

if
x_{0}\in\partial\Omega
, the following holds

\min\Set{F\left(x_{0},\phi(x_{0}),\nabla\phi(x_{0}),\nabla^{2}\phi(x_{0})\right),G\left(x_{0},\phi(x_{0}),\nabla\phi(x_{0})\right)}\leq 0

Viscosity solution

A continuous function
u
is a viscosity solution to (
8
) if it is both a super and subsolution.

Hence, we can give the following definition for the
\infty
-eigenvalue problem.

Definition 2.2
.

We say that a function
u\in C(\overline{\Omega})
is an
\infty
-eigenfunction if there exists
\lambda\neq 0
such that

\begin{cases}F_{\lambda}(u,\nabla u,\nabla^{2}u)=0&\text{ in }\Omega\\
G_{\beta}(u,\nabla u)=0&\text{ on }\partial\Omega,\end{cases}

(9)

in the viscosity sense.
The function appearing in problem (
9
) are the following

F_{\lambda}(u,\nabla u,\nabla^{2}u)=\begin{cases}{\rm{min}}\{|\nabla u|-\lambda|u|,-\Delta_{\infty}u\}&\mbox{in}\ \{u>0\}\cap\Omega\\[4.30554pt]
{\rm{max}}\{\lambda|u|-|\nabla u|,-\Delta_{\infty}u\}&\mbox{in}\ \{u<0\}\cap\Omega\\[4.30554pt]
-\Delta_{\infty}u,&\mbox{in}\ \{u=0\}\cap\Omega,\end{cases}

and

G_{\beta}(u,\nabla u)=\begin{cases}-{\rm{min}}\left\{|\nabla u|-\beta u,-\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}\right\}&\mbox{in}\ \{u>0\}\cap\partial\Omega\\
-{\rm{max}}\left\{\beta{\left|u\right|}-|\nabla u|,-\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}\right\}&\mbox{in}\ \{u<0\}\cap\partial\Omega\\
\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu},&\mbox{in}\ \{u=0\}\cap\partial\Omega.\end{cases}

In this case, the number
\lambda
is an
\infty
-eigenvalue.

2.2
Properties of the
p
-Laplace eigenvalues

We say that a function
u\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)
,
u\neq 0
, is a
p
-eigenfunction if there exists a
\lambda>0
such that

\int_{\Omega}{\left|\nabla u\right|}^{p-2}\nabla u\nabla\varphi\,dx+\beta^{p}\int_{\partial\Omega}{\left|u\right|}^{p-2}u\varphi\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}=\lambda\int_{\Omega}{\left|u\right|}^{p-2}u\varphi\,dx,

(10)

for all
\varphi\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)
,
where
\beta
is positive. It is possible to define a diverging sequence of eigenvalues, and we need the following definition.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
E
be a Banach space and let
A\subset E
be any closed symmetric set (
v\in A
implies
-v\in A
). The
genus

\gamma(A)
of the set
A
is defined as the smallest integer
m
for which there exists a continuous odd mapping
\phi:A\to\R^{m}\setminus\{0\}
. If no such integer exists, then
\gamma(A)=\infty
.

We define

\Sigma_{k}=\{A\subset W^{1,p}(\Omega)\text{ such that }\gamma(A)\geq k\text{ and }\{v\in A:{\left\|v\right\|}_{p}=1\}\text{ is compact }\}.

The numbers

\lambda_{k,p}(\Omega)=\inf_{A\in\Sigma_{k}}\sup_{w\in A}\frac{\displaystyle{\int_{\Omega}{\left|\nabla w\right|}^{p}\,dx+\beta^{p}\int_{\partial\Omega}{\left|w\right|}^{p}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}}}{\displaystyle{\int_{\Omega}{\left|w\right|}^{p}\,dx}}

(11)

are
p
-eigenvalues and create an increasing sequence going to infinity as
k
diverges. For
p\neq 2
it is not known whether this sequence contains all the
p
-eigenvalues, but it gives the correct
\lambda_{1,p}(\Omega)
and
\lambda_{2,p}(\Omega)
, see for instance
[
6
]
.

As first step, we prove part of Theorem

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded, Lipschitz set, and let
\{\lambda_{2,p}(\Omega)\}
be the sequence of the second
p
-Laplace eigenvalues. Then, we have

\limsup_{p\to\infty}\left(\lambda_{2,p}(\Omega)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq\frac{1}{s(\Omega)}.

Proof.

Let us denote by
s:=s(\Omega)
, and let
B_{1}:=B(x_{1},s)
and
B_{2}:=B(x_{2},s)
be two disjoint balls with center in
\Omega
.
We consider the functions

C_{i}(x)=\frac{(s-{\left|x-x_{i}\right|})_{+}}{s},\quad i=1,2,

and we consider
A=\text{span}\{C_{1},C_{2}\}
. Since the supports of
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
are disjoint, for every
v\in A
,
v=a_{1}C_{1}+a_{2}C_{2}
we have

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}{\left|v\right|}^{p}\,dx={\left|a_{1}\right|}^{p}\int_{B_{1}\cap\Omega}{\left|C_{1}\right|}^{p}+{\left|a_{2}\right|}^{p}\int_{B_{2}\cap\Omega}{\left|C_{2}\right|}^{p},

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}{\left|\nabla v\right|}^{p}\,dx=\frac{1}{s^{p}}({\left|a_{1}\right|}^{p}{\left|B_{1}\cap\Omega\right|}+{\left|a_{2}\right|}^{p}{\left|B_{2}\cap\Omega\right|}),

\displaystyle\int_{\partial\Omega}{\left|v\right|}^{p}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}={\left|a_{1}\right|}^{p}\int_{B_{1}\cap\partial\Omega}{\left|C_{1}\right|}^{p}+{\left|a_{2}\right|}^{p}\int_{B_{2}\cap\partial\Omega}{\left|C_{2}\right|}^{p}.

So, by the variational characterization (
11
), we have

\displaystyle\limsup_{p\to\infty}(\lambda_{2,p})^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq

\displaystyle\limsup_{p\to\infty}\left(\frac{\frac{1}{s^{p}}({\left|a_{1}\right|}^{p}{\left|B_{1}\cap\Omega\right|}+{\left|a_{2}\right|}^{p}{\left|B_{2}\cap\Omega\right|})+\beta^{p}({\left|a_{1}\right|}^{p}\int_{B_{1}\cap\partial\Omega}{\left|C_{1}\right|}^{p}+{\left|a_{2}\right|}^{p}\int_{B_{2}\cap\partial\Omega}{\left|C_{2}\right|}^{p})}{{\left|a_{1}\right|}^{p}\int_{B_{1}\cap\Omega}{\left|C_{1}\right|}^{p}+{\left|a_{2}\right|}^{p}\int_{B_{2}\cap\Omega}{\left|C_{2}\right|}^{p}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq

\displaystyle\limsup_{p\to\infty}\left(\frac{\frac{1}{s^{p}}\max\{{\left|B_{1}\cap\Omega\right|},{\left|B_{2}\cap\Omega\right|}\}+\beta^{p}\max\{\int_{B_{1}\cap\partial\Omega}{\left|C_{1}\right|}^{p},\int_{B_{2}\cap\partial\Omega}{\left|C_{2}\right|}^{p}\}}{\min\{\int_{B_{1}\cap\Omega}{\left|C_{1}\right|}^{p},\int_{B_{2}\cap\Omega}{\left|C_{2}\right|}^{p}\}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}=

\displaystyle\frac{\max\left\{\frac{1}{s},\beta{\left\|C_{1}\right\|}_{L^{\infty}(\partial\Omega)},\beta{\left\|C_{2}\right\|}_{L^{\infty}(\partial\Omega)}\right\}}{\min\left\{{\left\|C_{i}\right\|}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\right\}}\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{s(\Omega)}.

∎

Proposition

ensures that, up to a subsequence that we will still denote by

\lim_{p\to\infty}\lambda_{2,p}(\Omega)=\overline{\lambda}.

A key step in proving the main results is to show the following convergence property of the sequence of second eigenfunctions.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
\{u_{p}\}
be the sequence of the eigenfunctions associated to
\{\lambda_{2,p}(\Omega)\}
.
Then, there exists a subsequence
\{u_{p_{i}}\}
such that

\displaystyle u_{p_{i}}\to u_{\infty}

\displaystyle\text{ uniformly in }\,\Omega

\displaystyle\nabla u_{p_{i}}\to\nabla u_{\infty}

\displaystyle\text{ weakly in }\,L^{q}(\Omega),\forall q.

Proof.

Proposition
3.1
shows that the sequence of the second eigenvalues is bounded.
Thanks to this property, it is possible to show that the sequence
\Set{u_{p}}
of eigenfunctions associated to
\lambda_{2,p}(\Omega)
, normalized such that
{\left\|u_{p}\right\|}_{L^{p}}=1
, is uniformly bounded in

: indeed, if

\displaystyle{\left\|\nabla u_{p}\right\|}_{L^{q}(\Omega)}\leq{\left\|\nabla u_{p}\right\|}_{L^{p}(\Omega)}{\left|\Omega\right|}^{\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{{p}}}\leq(\lambda_{2,{p}}(\Omega))^{1/{p}}{\left|\Omega\right|}^{\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{{p}}}\leq\frac{1}{s(\Omega)}{\left|\Omega\right|}^{\frac{1}{q}},

\displaystyle{\left\|u_{p}\right\|}_{L^{q}(\Omega)}\leq{\left\|u_{p}\right\|}_{L^{p}(\Omega)}{\left|\Omega\right|}^{\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{{p}}}\leq{\left|\Omega\right|}^{\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{{p}}}\leq C,

where the constant

is independent of
{p_{i}}
.

By a classical argument of diagonalization, see for instance
[
3
]
, we can extract a subsequence, denoted by
u_{p_{i}}
such that

\displaystyle u_{p_{i}}\to u_{\infty}\,\text{ uniformly}\implies\|u_{p_{i}}\|_{L^{p_{i}}}\to{\left\|u_{\infty}\right\|}_{L^{\infty}},

\displaystyle\nabla u_{p_{i}}\to\nabla u_{\infty}\,\text{ weakly in }\,L^{q}(\Omega),\,\forall q>1.

∎

Before going on, we show that

is an eigenfunction for the

Theorem 3.3

\{(\lambda_{2,p_{i}}(\Omega))^{\frac{1}{p_{i}}}\}
and let
u_{\infty}
be the limit of
\{u_{p_{i}}\}

u_{\infty}
is a viscosity solution to (

\begin{cases}F_{\overline{\lambda}}(u,\nabla u,\nabla^{2}u)=0&\text{ in }\Omega,\\
G_{\beta}(u,\nabla u)=0&\text{ on }\partial\Omega.\end{cases}

Proof.

Let us show that
u_{\infty}
is a viscosity subsolution.

Case 1:

Let
x_{0}\in\Omega
and let

\displaystyle\max\{\beta{\left\|u_{\infty}^{-}\right\|}_{L^{\infty}(\partial\Omega)},\lambda_{2,\infty}(\Omega){\left\|u_{\infty}^{+}\right\|}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\}=\lambda_{2,\infty}(\Omega){\left\|u_{\infty}^{+}\right\|}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)},

and similarly to the first case, we get an absurd

\beta{\left\|u_{\infty}^{+}\right\|}_{L^{\infty}(\partial\Omega)}=\lambda_{2,\infty}(\Omega){\left\|u_{\infty}^{+}\right\|}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}>\beta{\left\|u_{\infty}^{+}\right\|}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}.

3.

We have

\displaystyle\max\{\beta{\left\|u_{\infty}^{+}\right\|}_{L^{\infty}(\partial\Omega)},\lambda_{2,\infty}(\Omega){\left\|u_{\infty}^{-}\right\|}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\}=\lambda_{2,\infty}(\Omega){\left\|u_{\infty}^{-}\right\|}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}=

\displaystyle\max\{\beta{\left\|u_{\infty}^{-}\right\|}_{L^{\infty}(\partial\Omega)},\lambda_{2,\infty}(\Omega){\left\|u_{\infty}^{+}\right\|}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\}=\beta{\left\|u_{\infty}^{-}\right\|}_{L^{\infty}(\partial\Omega)},

but, arguing as before, this would imply
{\left\|u_{\infty}^{-}\right\|}_{L^{\infty}(\partial\Omega)}>{\left\|u_{\infty}^{-}\right\|}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}
.

4

The only case that can occur is

{\left\|u_{\infty}^{-}\right\|}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}={\left\|u_{\infty}^{+}\right\|}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)},

that is the claim.

∎

4
The geometric characterization

Now we complete the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Proposition
3.1
gives

\limsup_{p}(\lambda_{2,p}(\Omega))^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq\frac{1}{s(\Omega)},

so it remains to prove

\liminf_{p}(\lambda_{2,p}(\Omega))^{\frac{1}{p}}\geq\frac{1}{s(\Omega)}.

We distinguish the cases
\beta\leq 2/D(\Omega)
and
\beta>2/D(\Omega)
.
In the case
\beta\leq 2/D(\Omega)
the thesis is given by (
17
).

To prove the claim in the case
\beta>2/D(\Omega)
, we use
(u_{p})_{+}
, normalized so that
{\left\|(u_{p})_{+}\right\|}_{L^{p}(\Omega^{+}_{p})}=1
, in the weak formulation (
10
) with
\lambda=\lambda_{2,p}(\Omega)
, obtaining

\liminf_{p}(\lambda_{2,p}(\Omega))^{\frac{1}{p}}=\liminf_{p}\left(\frac{\displaystyle{\int_{\Omega_{p}^{+}}{\left|\nabla u_{p}\right|}^{p}+\beta^{p}\int_{\Omega^{+}_{p}\cap\partial\Omega}u^{p}}}{\displaystyle{\int_{\Omega^{+}_{p}}u^{p}}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

By the uniform convergence of
u_{p}
, stated in Proposition
3.2
, and by Lemma
3.4
, we have that both
u^{+}_{p}
and
u^{-}_{p}
uniformly converge to
u^{+}_{\infty}\neq 0
and
u^{-}_{\infty}\neq 0
, respectively (for instance write
2u^{+}_{p}={\left|u_{p}\right|}+u_{p}
).
Since
(\nabla u_{p})\chi_{\Omega^{+}_{p}}
and
(\nabla u_{p})\chi_{\Omega^{-}_{p}}
are equi-bounded in
L^{q}(\Omega)
for all
q
(see Proposition
3.2
), up to a subsequence they converge to
(\nabla u_{\infty})\chi_{\Omega^{+}_{\infty}}
and
(\nabla u_{\infty})\chi_{\Omega^{-}_{\infty}}
weakly in
L^{q}(\Omega)
.
Hence, the following inequality holds

\displaystyle\frac{{\left\|\nabla u_{\infty}\right\|}_{L^{q}(\Omega^{+}_{\infty})}}{{\left\|u_{\infty}\right\|}_{L^{q}(\Omega^{+}_{\infty})}}

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{p\to\infty}\frac{{\left\|\nabla u_{p}\right\|}_{L^{q}(\Omega^{+}_{\infty})}}{{\left\|u_{p}\right\|}_{L^{q}(\Omega^{+}_{\infty})}}\leq\liminf_{p\to\infty}\frac{{\left\|\nabla u_{p}\right\|}_{L^{p}(\Omega^{+}_{\infty})}}{{\left\|u_{p}\right\|}_{L^{q}(\Omega^{+}_{\infty})}}{\left|\Omega\right|}^{\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{{\left|\Omega\right|}^{\frac{1}{q}}}{{\left\|u_{\infty}\right\|}_{L^{q}(\Omega^{+}_{\infty})}}\liminf_{p\to\infty}\left(\lambda_{2,p}(\Omega)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Letting
q\to\infty
, we obtain

{\left\|\nabla u_{\infty}\right\|}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega^{+}_{\infty})}\leq\liminf_{p\to\infty}\left(\lambda_{2,p}(\Omega)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Similarly

\beta{\left\|u_{p}\right\|}_{L^{q}(\partial\Omega^{+}_{\infty}\cap\partial\Omega)}\leq\beta{\left\|\ u_{p}\right\|}_{L^{p}(\partial\Omega^{+}_{\infty}\cap\partial\Omega)}{\left|\partial\Omega\right|}^{\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p}}\leq(\lambda_{2,p}(\Omega))^{1/p}{\left|\partial\Omega\right|}^{\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p}}\leq C,

gives us

\beta{\left\|u_{\infty}\right\|}_{L^{\infty}(\partial\Omega^{+}_{\infty}\cap\partial\Omega)}\leq\liminf_{p\to\infty}\left(\lambda_{2,p}(\Omega)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},

hence

\liminf_{p}(\lambda_{2,p}(\Omega))^{\frac{1}{p}}\geq\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}w\in W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)\\
w|_{\partial\Omega^{+}_{\infty}\cap\Omega}=0\end{subarray}}\frac{\max\{{\left\|\nabla w\right\|}_{\infty},\beta{\left\|w\right\|}_{L^{\infty}(\partial\Omega^{+}_{\infty}\cap\partial\Omega)}\}}{{\left\|u\right\|}_{\infty}}=\lambda_{\infty}^{\text{mix}}(\Omega^{+}_{\infty}),

where
\lambda_{\infty}^{\text{mix}}(\Omega^{+}_{\infty})
is the one defined in
[
17
]
and reported in formulas (
5
) and (
6
).

An analogous argument for
\lambda_{\infty}^{\text{mix}}(\Omega^{-}_{\infty})
, brings us to

\liminf_{p}(\lambda_{2,p}(\Omega))^{\frac{1}{p}}\geq\max\{\lambda_{\infty}^{\text{mix}}(\Omega^{+}_{\infty}),\lambda_{\infty}^{\text{mix}}(\Omega^{-}_{\infty})\}=:\tilde{\lambda}.

Let us set
\Gamma_{1}^{\pm}=\partial\Omega^{\pm}_{\infty}\cap\Omega
and
\Gamma_{2}^{\pm}=\partial\Omega^{\pm}_{\infty}\cap\partial\Omega
.
Analogously, we define the sets

\mathcal{A}_{\pm}=\left\{x\in\overline{\Omega^{\pm}_{\infty}}:d(x,\Gamma_{1}^{\pm})\geq\frac{1}{\beta}+d(x,\Gamma_{2}^{\pm})\right\}.

Let us distinguish the following cases

Case (1)

\mathcal{A}_{\pm}\neq\emptyset
: we consider

x_{\pm}\in\argmin_{x\in\mathcal{A}_{\pm}}\frac{1}{\frac{1}{\beta}+d(x,\Gamma_{2}^{\pm})},

then

d(x_{\pm},\Gamma^{\pm}_{1})\geq\frac{1}{\beta}+d(x_{\pm},\Gamma^{\pm}_{2})\geq\frac{1}{\tilde{\lambda}}

and the function
C_{\pm,\frac{1}{\tilde{\lambda}}}
satisfies

{\left|\nabla C_{\pm,\frac{1}{\tilde{\lambda}}}\right|}=\tilde{\lambda},\quad C_{\pm,\frac{1}{\tilde{\lambda}}}=0\text{ on }\Gamma_{1}^{\pm},\quad\beta{\left\|C_{\pm,\frac{1}{\tilde{\lambda}}}\right\|}_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma^{\pm}_{2})}\leq\tilde{\lambda}.

Case (1)

\mathcal{A}_{\pm}=\emptyset
: we consider

x_{\pm}\in\argmin_{x\in N_{\pm}}\frac{1}{d(x,\Gamma^{\pm}_{1})},

then

\frac{1}{\tilde{\lambda}}\leq d(x_{\pm},\Gamma^{\pm}_{1})\leq\frac{1}{\beta}+d(x_{\pm},\Gamma^{\pm}_{2})

and the function
C_{\pm,\frac{1}{\tilde{\lambda}}}
satisfies

{\left|\nabla C_{\pm,\frac{1}{\tilde{\lambda}}}\right|}=\tilde{\lambda},\quad C_{\pm,\frac{1}{\tilde{\lambda}}}=0\text{ on }\Gamma_{1}^{\pm},\quad\beta{\left\|C_{\pm,\frac{1}{\tilde{\lambda}}}\right\|}_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma^{\pm}_{2})}<\tilde{\lambda}.

From Case 1 and 2 we deduce the existence of two points
x_{+}\in\Omega^{+}_{\infty}
and
x_{-}\in\Omega^{-}_{\infty}
such that

|x_{+}-x_{-}|\geq|x_{+}-y_{+}|+|x_{-}-y_{-}|\geq d(x_{+},\Gamma_{1}^{-})+d(x_{-},\Gamma_{1}^{-})\geq\frac{2}{\tilde{\lambda}}

with
y_{+}\in\Gamma_{1}^{+}
and
y_{-}\in\Gamma_{1}^{-}
,
and the associated cones satisfies

{\left\|C_{\pm,\frac{1}{\tilde{\lambda}}}\right\|}_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma^{\pm}_{2})}<\frac{\tilde{\lambda}}{\beta}.

This means that
\displaystyle\frac{1}{\tilde{\lambda}}\leq s(\Omega)
by the definition of
s(\Omega)
and we get

\liminf_{p}(\lambda_{2,p}(\Omega))^{\frac{1}{p}}\geq\max\{\lambda_{\infty}^{\text{mix}}(\Omega^{+}_{\infty}),\lambda_{\infty}^{\text{mix}}(\Omega^{-}_{\infty})\}\geq\frac{1}{s(\Omega)},

(18)

and the proof is complete.
∎

5
Variational Characterization of
\lambda_{2,\infty}

We are now in a position to prove Theorem
1.2
.

We recall the definition of the set
\mathcal{S}=\left\{v\in W^{1,\infty}(\Omega):{\left\|v\right\|}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}=1\right\}
, and we call
u_{1,\infty}
the limit of the first Robin
p
-eigenfunction. In
[
1
]
it was proved that
u_{1,\infty}
is a positive infinity eigenfunction and a minimum of the functional

\max\{{\left\|\nabla u\right\|}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)},\beta{\left\|u\right\|}_{L^{\infty}(\partial\Omega)}\},

among all
{u\in\mathcal{S}}
.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

For all
\gamma\in\Gamma
there exists
t_{0}\in[0,1]
such that
u=\gamma(t_{0})
satisfies

{\left\|u_{+}\right\|}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}={\left\|u_{-}\right\|}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}=1.

Let
\Omega^{+}=\{u>0\}
,
\Omega^{-}=\{u<0\}
and let
N^{+}\subset\Omega^{+}
,
N^{-}\subset\Omega^{-}
be two nodal domains such that

{\left\|u_{\pm}\right\|}_{L^{\infty}(N^{\pm})}=1
. Then,

\displaystyle\max\left\{{\left\|\nabla u\right\|}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)},\beta{\left\|u\right\|}_{L^{\infty}(\partial\Omega)}\right\}\geq\max\left\{{\left\|\nabla u_{+}\right\|}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)},\beta{\left\|u_{+}\right\|}_{L^{\infty}(\partial\Omega)}\right\}\geq

\displaystyle\max\left\{{\left\|\nabla u_{+}\right\|}_{L^{\infty}(N^{+})},\beta{\left\|u_{+}\right\|}_{L^{\infty}(\partial N^{+})}\right\}\geq\lambda_{\infty}^{\text{mix}}(N^{+}),

where the mixed eigenvalue
\lambda_{\infty}^{\text{mix}}
is the one studied in
[
16
]
obtained by choosing
\Gamma_{1}^{+}=\partial N^{+}\cap\Omega
and
\Gamma_{2}^{+}=\partial N^{+}\cap\partial\Omega
. If we replace
N^{+}
with
N^{-}
, we obtain

\max\left\{{\left\|\nabla u\right\|}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)},\beta{\left\|u\right\|}_{L^{\infty}(\partial\Omega)}\right\}\geq\lambda_{\infty}^{\text{mix}}(N^{-}).

Arguning as in the proof of Theorem (
1.1
), see formula (
18
), we have

\max\{\lambda_{\infty}^{\text{mix}}(N^{+}),\lambda_{\infty}^{\text{mix}}(N^{-})\}\geq\lambda_{2,\infty}(\Omega).

So,

\lambda_{2,\infty}(\Omega)\leq\max\left\{{\left\|\nabla u\right\|}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)},\beta{\left\|u\right\|}_{L^{\infty}(\partial\Omega)}\right\}\leq\sup_{u\in\gamma}\max\left\{{\left\|\nabla u\right\|}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)},\beta{\left\|u\right\|}_{L^{\infty}(\partial\Omega)}\right\},

that gives

\lambda_{2,\infty}(\Omega)\leq\inf_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\sup_{u\in\gamma}\max\left\{{\left\|\nabla u\right\|}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)},\beta{\left\|u\right\|}_{L^{\infty}(\partial\Omega)}\right\}.

To prove the other inequality, we consider a path
\gamma\in\Gamma
such that for all
u\in\gamma

\max\left\{{\left\|\nabla u\right\|}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)},\beta{\left\|u\right\|}_{L^{\infty}(\partial\Omega)}\right\}\leq\lambda_{2,\infty}(\Omega).

By the definition of
\lambda_{2,\infty}(\Omega)
(
4
), there exist two points
x_{1},x_{2}\in\overline{\Omega}
such that

{\left|x_{1}-x_{2}\right|}\geq\frac{2}{\lambda_{2,\infty}(\Omega)},\quad\beta{\left\|C_{i,\frac{1}{\lambda_{2,\infty}(\Omega)}}\right\|}_{L^{\infty}(\partial\Omega)}\leq\lambda_{2,\infty}(\Omega).

Let
u_{1}
be the limit of the first Robin
p
-eigenfunctions, and let us define, for
t\in[0,1]
,

\displaystyle\gamma_{1}(t)

\displaystyle=\max\{u_{1},tC_{1}\},

\displaystyle\gamma_{2}(t)

\displaystyle=\max\{(1-t)u_{1},C_{1}\},

\displaystyle\gamma_{3}(t)

\displaystyle=C_{1}-tC_{2},

\displaystyle\gamma_{4}(t)

\displaystyle=(1-t)C_{1}-C_{2},

\displaystyle\gamma_{5}(t)

\displaystyle=\min\{-C_{2},-tu_{1}\},

\displaystyle\gamma_{6}(t)

\displaystyle=\min\{-(1-t)C_{2},-u_{1}\},

the path
\gamma
will be obtained by gluing together these six paths.

Let us observe that, for all
t\in[0,1]
,

{\left\|\nabla\gamma(t)\right\|}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq\max\{{\left\|\nabla u_{1}\right\|}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)},{\left\|\nabla C_{i}\right\|}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\}\leq\lambda_{2,\infty}(\Omega),

as

\begin{gathered}{\left\|\nabla C_{i}\right\|}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}=\lambda_{2,\infty}(\Omega),\\
{\left\|\nabla u_{1}\right\|}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq\max\{{\left\|\nabla u_{1}\right\|}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)},\beta{\left\|u_{1}\right\|}_{L^{\infty}(\partial\Omega)}\}{=\lambda_{1,\infty}(\Omega)}\leq\lambda_{2,\infty}(\Omega),\end{gathered}

and

\beta{\left\|\gamma(t)\right\|}_{L^{\infty}(\partial\Omega)}\leq\beta\max\{{\left\|u_{1}\right\|}_{L^{\infty}(\partial\Omega)},{\left\|C_{i}\right\|}_{L^{\infty}(\partial\Omega)}\}\leq\lambda_{2,\infty}(\Omega),

which concludes the proof.
∎

1.2
, we also have the following variational characterization

Corollary 5.1
.

Let

be an open, bounded and Lipschitz set. Then

\lambda_{2,\infty}(\Omega)=\inf_{w\in\mathcal{O}}\frac{\max\{{\left\|\nabla w\right\|}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)},\beta{\left\|w\right\|}_{L^{\infty}(\partial\Omega)}\}}{{\left\|w\right\|}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}},

where

\mathcal{O}=\{w\in W^{1,\infty}(\Omega):\,{\left\|w_{+}\right\|}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}={\left\|w_{-}\right\|}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\}

6
The
\infty
-eigenvalue problem

Now, with the additional assumption on the regularity of
\Omega
, we prove Theorem
1.3
.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Let
u
be the eigenfunction corresponding to
\lambda
and let
\Omega^{+}=\{x\in\Omega:\,u>0\}
and
\Omega^{-}=\{x\in\Omega:\,u<0\}
. Let us normalize the eigenfunction
u
such that

\max_{x\in\Omega^{+}}u(x)=\frac{1}{\lambda}.

Then
u
satisfies, in viscosity sense,

\begin{cases}\min\Set{{\left|\nabla u\right|}-1,-\Delta_{\infty}u}\leq 0&\text{ in }\Omega^{+}\\
\displaystyle{\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}}\leq 0&\text{ on }\partial\Omega^{+}\cap\partial\Omega\\
u=0&\text{ on }\partial\Omega^{+}\cap\Omega.\end{cases}

We are in the same situation of Proposition 1 in
[
7
]
, hence, for all
x_{0}\in\Omega\setminus\Omega^{+}
we can consider

g_{\varepsilon,\gamma}(x)=(1+\varepsilon){\left|x-x_{0}\right|}-\gamma{\left|x-x_{0}\right|}^{2}

as test function in the definition of viscosity subsolution, obtaining
g_{\varepsilon,\gamma}(x)\geq u
, for all
\varepsilon>0
, and
\gamma<\frac{\varepsilon}{2D(\Omega)}
, so if we pass to the limit as
\varepsilon
goes to 0, we get

{\left|x-x_{0}\right|}\geq u\quad\forall x\in\overline{\Omega^{+}},\quad\forall x_{0}\in\overline{\Omega^{-}}.

(19)

On the other hand,
u
is a viscosity subsolution to

\begin{cases}\min\Set{{\left|\nabla u\right|}-1,-\Delta_{\infty}u}=0&\text{ in }\Omega^{+}\\
-{\rm{min}}\left\{|\nabla u|-\beta u,\displaystyle{-\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}}\right\}=0&\text{ on }\partial\Omega^{+}.\end{cases}

(20)

Hence, arguing as in Theorem 3.6 in
[
1
]
, we consider
for every
\varepsilon>0
and
0<\gamma<\frac{\varepsilon}{D(\Omega)}
, the supersolution to (
20
)

h_{\varepsilon,\gamma}(x)=\frac{1}{\beta}+(1+\varepsilon)d(x,\partial\Omega^{+})-\gamma d(x,\partial\Omega^{+})^{2},

obtaining
h_{\varepsilon,\gamma}(x)\geq u
, for all
\varepsilon,\gamma
.
Then it follows, by letting
\varepsilon
, thus
\gamma
, to
0
, that

\frac{1}{\beta}+d(x,\partial\Omega^{+})\geq u(x)\qquad\forall x\in\Omega^{+}.

(21)

Combining (
19
) and (
21
), we get

u(x)\leq{\rm min}\left\{d(x,\Gamma_{1}^{+}),\frac{1}{\beta}+d(x,\partial\Omega^{+})\right\}\leq{\rm min}\left\{d(x,\Gamma_{1}^{+}),\frac{1}{\beta}+d(x,\Gamma_{2}^{+})\right\}=:g(x),

(22)

where

\Gamma_{1}^{+}=\partial\Omega^{+}\cap\Omega\quad\text{ and }\quad\Gamma_{2}^{+}=\partial\Omega^{+}\cap\partial\Omega.

Since
g(x)
belongs to the space
X
defined in (
7
), we have

\lambda_{\infty}^{\text{mix}}(\Omega^{+})\leq\frac{\max\left\{{\left\|\nabla g\right\|}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega^{+})},\beta{\left\|g\right\|}_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma_{2}^{+})}\right\}}{{\left\|g\right\|}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega^{+})}},

and so also

{\left\|g\right\|}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega^{+})}\leq\frac{\max\left\{{\left\|\nabla g\right\|}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega^{+})},\beta{\left\|g\right\|}_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma_{2}^{+})}\right\}}{\lambda_{\infty}^{\text{mix}}(\Omega^{+})}\leq\frac{1}{\lambda_{\infty}^{\text{mix}}(\Omega^{+})}.

Then (
22
) and the latter imply

\frac{1}{\lambda}=\max_{x\in\Omega^{+}}u(x)\leq\max_{x\in\Omega^{+}}g(x)\leq\frac{1}{\lambda_{\infty}^{\text{mix}}(\Omega^{+})}.

(23)

Normalizing
u
such that

\max_{x\in\Omega^{-}}(-u(x))=\frac{1}{\lambda},

and arguing in the same way, we obtain

-u(x)\leq{\rm min}\left\{d(x,\Gamma_{1}^{-}),\frac{1}{\beta}+d(x,\Gamma_{2}^{-})\right\}=:h(x),

where

\Gamma_{1}^{-}=\partial\Omega^{-}\cap\Omega\quad\text{ and }\quad\Gamma_{2}^{-}=\partial\Omega^{-}\cap\partial\Omega.

Hence, we get

\frac{1}{\lambda}=\max_{x\in\Omega^{-}}(-u(x))\leq\max_{x\in\Omega^{-}}h(x)\leq\frac{1}{\lambda_{\infty}^{\text{mix}}(\Omega^{-})}.

(24)

By (
23
), (
24
) and (
18
), we get

\lambda\geq\max\{\lambda_{\infty}^{\text{mix}}(\Omega^{+}),\lambda_{\infty}^{\text{mix}}(\Omega^{-})\}\geq\lambda_{2,\infty}(\Omega),

as we want to show. ∎

Remark 6.1
.

In the case
\beta\leq\frac{2}{D(\Omega)}
, in which the eigenvalue
\lambda_{2,\infty}(\Omega)
coincides with the Neumann one, it is sufficient to establish

u\leq d(x,\Gamma_{1}^{+}),\text{ in }\Omega^{+},\quad-u\leq d(x,\Gamma_{1}^{-}),\text{ in }\Omega^{-},

to obtain the claim. For this purpose, the convexity assumption is the only necessary one.

The
C^{2}
assumption is needed only in the case
\beta>\frac{2}{D(\Omega)}
, to conduct the same analysis we did in
[
1
]
.

7
Some examples

For some special shapes, we can explicitly write
s(\Omega)
in terms of other geometric quantities of the set.

Example 7.1
.

Let
\Omega\subset\R^{2}
be a stadium, i.e., the convex hull of two congruent disjoint balls, as represented in Figure
2
, then

\frac{1}{s(\Omega)}=\lambda_{2,\infty}(\Omega)=\begin{cases}\dfrac{1}{\frac{1}{\beta}+r(\Omega)}&\text{if }\beta\geq\frac{2}{D(\Omega)-4r(\Omega)}\\[8.61108pt]
\dfrac{2\beta}{1+\beta\frac{D(\Omega)}{2}}&\text{if }\frac{2}{D(\Omega)}\leq\beta<\frac{2}{D(\Omega)-4r(\Omega)}\\[8.61108pt]
\dfrac{2}{D(\Omega)}&\text{if }\beta<\frac{2}{D(\Omega)}.\end{cases}

We observe that
\lambda_{2,\infty}
is a continuous function of
\beta
and in the case that the stadium is thin enough (i.e.,
D-4r(\Omega)>0
), the second eigenvalue coincides with the first one.

\displaystyle{\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{0.61,0.61,0.61}r(\Omega)=r_{2}(\Omega)}}
\displaystyle{\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{1,0,1}s(\Omega)}}

.

Existence and uniqueness of Remotely Almost Periodic solutions of differential equations and applications
Diego Jaure, Christopher Maulén

Abstract
. We establish existence and uniqueness of remotely almost periodic (RAP) solutions for nonlinear ordinary differential systems x ′ = A ⁡ ( t ) ​ x + f ⁡ ( t , x ) + g ν ​ ( t , x ) . x^{\prime}=A(t)x+f(t,x)+g_{\nu}(t,x). Assuming that the linear equation x ′ = A ⁡ ( t ) ​ x x^{\prime}=A(t)x admits an exponential dichotomy and that the associated Green kernel is exponentially bi-remotely almost periodic, we derive sufficient conditions guaranteeing a unique RAP solution of the perturbed system for ν \nu in a suitable range. As an application, we obtain RAP solutions for a nonautonomous Brusselator model.

The notion of an almost periodic function was introduced by a Danish mathematician H. Bohr around 1925 and later generalized by many others.

When a process is described by differential equations, we are passing from a real object (process) to an idealized model. Every mathematical idealization implies, in a certain way, to omit small quantities.
Therefore, the way in which distortion is introduced in the phenomenon ends up being very important, thus arriving at the mathematical problem where the solutions of the differential equation depend on small parameters. To simplify, we are only considering problems with only one problem involved.

\overline{H}:{\mathbb{R}}\times B_{r}(0)\times(0,\infty)\rightarrow{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
the bounded solution of the differential equation

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\overline{H}(t,x,\nu)-H(t,x)=-\nu\overline{H}(t,x,\nu),

given by

\displaystyle\overline{H}(t,x,\nu)=\int_{-\infty}^{t}e^{-\nu(t-s)}H(s,x)ds.

By Lemma
3.1
, we have that

(3.11)

\displaystyle|\overline{H}(t,x,\nu)|\leq\nu^{-1}\xi(\nu,x),\ t\in{\mathbb{R}}.

Moreover,
\overline{H}\in RAP(\mathbb{R}\times B_{r}(0),\mathbb{R}^{n})
and it is ergodic with
\mathcal{M}(H_{x})=0
for fixed
\nu\in(0,\infty)
.
It is easy to see that
\frac{\partial\overline{H}}{\partial t}\in RAP(\mathbb{R}\times B_{r}(0),\mathbb{R}^{n})
and is ergodic for
\nu\in(0,\infty)
.
By (
3.11
)

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\overline{H}(t,x,\nu)-H(t,x)\right|\leq\xi(\nu,x),\ t\in{\mathbb{R}}.

For fixed
a>0
and some integer
q\geq 1
, we define
\Delta_{a}
in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
such that

\displaystyle\Delta_{a}(x)=\begin{cases}d_{a}(1-a^{-2}\left|x\right|^{2})^{2q}&\mbox{si }\left|x\right|\leq a,\\
0&\mbox{si }\left|x\right|>a,\end{cases}

where the constant
d_{a}
is determined by

\displaystyle\int_{B_{a}}\Delta_{a}(x)dx=1.

Let us define now the function
U:{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{C}}^{n}\times(0,\infty)\rightarrow{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
by

\displaystyle U(t,x,\nu)=\int_{B_{a}}\Delta_{a}(x-y)\overline{H}(t,y,\nu)dy.

which is a continuous functions, also
U\in RAP(\mathbb{R}\times B_{a})
for
\nu\in(0,\infty)
, and
U
is ergodic since
\overline{H}
is. Therefore, condition (1) is satisfied.

To prove (2). The function
\Delta_{a}(x-y)
has continuous partial derivatives of order greater than
2q-1
with respect to
x
, which are bounded, in norm, by a function
A(a)
(the integration area), where
A
is continuous on
(0,\infty)
.
From (
3.11
), it follows that the function
U
has partial derivatives with respect to
x
of order greater than
2q-1
, which are bounded by
A(a)\xi(y,\nu)\nu^{-1}
, for
y\in B_{r}(0)
.
Since
q
is an arbitrary integer, the number of derivatives with respect to
x
can be as large as desired.

\frac{\partial U}{\partial t}
and the derivative are remotely almost-periodic and ergodic for each
\nu\in(0,\infty)
.
Thus, condition (2) is satisfied.

To prove (3). Let us choose
a=a(\nu)
, a function of
\nu
, such that
a(\nu)\rightarrow 0
and
A(a(\nu))\xi(y,\nu)\rightarrow 0
uniformly with respect to
y\in B_{r}(0)
as
\nu\rightarrow 0
.
Then
\nu U\rightarrow 0
and
\nu\frac{\partial U}{\partial x}\rightarrow 0
as
\nu\rightarrow 0
, uniformly with respect to
x\in B_{r}(0)
and
t\in\mathbb{R}
, since
\nu U
and
\nu\frac{\partial U}{\partial x}
are bounded by
A(a(\nu))\xi(y,\nu)
.
For every number
r<r_{0}
, we choose
\nu_{0}
sufficiently small such that
r+a(\nu)<r_{0}
for all
\nu\in(0,\nu_{0})
.
It follows from the definition of
\Delta_{a}(x)
that

\displaystyle\int_{B_{r_{0}}}\Delta_{a(\nu)}(x-y)dy=1,\ x\in B_{r}(0),\ \nu\in(0,\nu_{0}).

Note that

\displaystyle G(t,x,\nu)=\frac{\partial U}{\partial t}(t,x,\nu)-H(t,x).

Let

\displaystyle\overline{G}(t,x,\nu)=G(t,x,\nu)+\nu U(t,x,\nu).

Since

\displaystyle\frac{\partial U}{\partial t}(t,x,\nu)=\int_{B_{r}(0)}\Delta_{a(\nu)}(x-y)[H(t,y)-\nu\overline{H}(t,y,\nu)]dy,

we have

\displaystyle\overline{G}(t,x,\nu)=\int_{B_{r}(0)}\Delta_{a(\nu)}(x-y)[H(t,y)-H(t,x)]dy.

By the mean value theorem,

\displaystyle\|\overline{G}(t,x,\nu)\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq\|x-y\|\leq a(\nu)}\|H(t,y)-H(t,x)\|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq\|x-y\|\leq a(\nu)}\left\|\frac{\partial H}{\partial x}(t,\theta_{y}(y-x))\right\|\|y-x\|,

where
\theta_{y}\in(0,1)
. Since
\frac{\partial H}{\partial x}
is continuous in
x
, uniformly in
t\in\mathbb{R}
, the function
\frac{\partial H}{\partial x}
is bounded on
\mathbb{R}\times B_{r}(0)
.
Thus,
\overline{G}(t,x,\nu)\rightarrow 0
as
\nu\rightarrow 0
, uniformly on
\mathbb{R}\times B_{r}(0)
. Then

\displaystyle G(t,x,\nu)\rightarrow 0\mbox{ when }\nu\rightarrow 0

uniformly in
{\mathbb{R}}\times B_{r}(0)
.
We have

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\overline{G}}{\partial x}(t,x,\nu)=\int_{B_{r_{0}}}\Delta_{a(\nu)}(x-y)[\frac{\partial H}{\partial x}(t,y)-\frac{\partial H}{\partial x}(t,x)]dy,

using the argument employed for
\overline{G}
, it follows that

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\overline{G}}{\partial x}(t,x,\nu)\rightarrow 0\mbox{ when }\nu\rightarrow 0

uniformly with respecto to
(t,x)\in{\mathbb{R}}\times B_{r}(0)
. Then,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial G}{\partial x}(t,x,\nu)\rightarrow 0\mbox{ when }\nu\rightarrow 0

uniformly with respect to
(t,x)\in{\mathbb{R}}\times B_{r}(0)
. This proves
(3)
.

Since the four functions in
(3)
converge to zero as
\nu\rightarrow 0
, uniformly with respect to
(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times B_{r}(0)
, we can define them as zero for all
(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times B_{r}(0)
when
\nu=0
. Given
\nu_{1}>0
, let

\displaystyle\Omega_{1}=\{x\ :\ x=y+\nu U(t,y,\nu),\ (t,y,\nu)\in{\mathbb{R}}\times B_{r}(0)\times[0,\nu_{1}]\}

a compact subset of
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
. Note that
\nu\frac{\partial U}{\partial y}\rightarrow 0
as
\nu\rightarrow 0
, uniformly with respect to
(t,y)\in\mathbb{R}\times B_{r}(0)
.

Let us choose
\nu_{2}>0
such that
I+\nu\frac{\partial U(t,y,\nu)}{\partial y}
has a bounded inverse for
(t,y,\nu)\in\mathbb{R}\times B_{a(\nu_{2})}(0)\times[0,\nu_{2}]
.
Then, by the inverse function theorem, the change of variable (
3.9
) has at most one solution
y\in B_{a(\nu_{2})}(0)
for each
(x,t,\nu)\in\mathbb{R}\times\Omega_{1}\times[0,\nu_{2}]
.

For any
x_{0}\in\Omega_{1}
there exists
\nu_{3}(x_{0})>0
such that the change of variables (
3.9
) has a unique solution
y=y(t,x,\nu)
, defined and continuous for
\lvert y-x_{0}\rvert\leq\nu_{3}(x_{0})
,
\lvert x-x_{0}\rvert\leq\nu_{3}(x_{0})
, and
0\leq\nu\leq\nu_{3}(x_{0})
.

Since
\Omega_{1}
is compact, we can choose
\nu_{4}>0
, independent of
x_{0}
, that satisfies the same properties as
\nu_{3}(x_{0})
.

If
\nu_{0}=\min\{\nu_{1},\nu_{2},\nu_{4}\}
, then the change of variables defines a homeomorphism. The transformation is well defined for
(t,y,\nu)\in{\mathbb{R}}\times B_{r}(0)\times[0,\nu_{0}]
.
By (
3.9
), we have

\displaystyle\frac{dx}{dt}=\frac{dy}{dt}+\nu\frac{\partial U}{\partial y}\frac{dy}{dt}+\nu\frac{\partial U}{\partial t}.

Then, by (
3.1
) we have

\displaystyle\left(I+\nu\frac{\partial U}{\partial y}\right)\frac{dy}{dt}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{dx}{dt}-\nu\frac{\partial U}{\partial t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nu f_{\nu}(t,y+\nu U(t,y,\nu))-\nu\frac{\partial U}{\partial t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nu f_{0}(y)+\nu\left[f_{0}(t,y)-f_{0}(y)-\frac{\partial U}{\partial t}\right]

\displaystyle+\nu\left[f(t,y+\nu U(t,y,\nu))-f_{0}(t,y)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nu f_{0}(y)+\nu\tilde{f}_{\nu}(t,y)

We can see that
\tilde{f}
has the same properties as
f
in
\mathbb{R}\times B_{r}(0)\times[0,\nu_{0}]
, and moreover,
f_{0}(t,y)=0
for all
(t,y)\in\mathbb{R}\times B_{r}(0)
.
Again, by point (3), we have that
\nu\frac{\partial U}{\partial x}\rightarrow 0
as
\nu\rightarrow 0
uniformly on
(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times B_{r}(0)
.
We can choose
\nu_{0}
sufficiently small such that

\displaystyle\left\|\nu\frac{\partial U}{\partial y}\right\|\leq\delta<1,\ \nu\in[0,\nu_{0}].

Then,

\displaystyle\left[I-\left(-\nu\frac{\partial U}{\partial y}\right)\right]^{-1}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left(-\nu\frac{\partial U}{\partial y}\right)^{k}.

Note that
\frac{\partial U}{\partial y}
is remotely almost-periodic, and that
[I-(-\nu\frac{\partial U}{\partial y})]^{-1}
is also remotely almost-periodic.
It follows that

\displaystyle\frac{dy}{dt}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[I-\left(-\nu\frac{\partial U}{\partial y}\right)\right]^{-1}(\nu f_{0}(y)+\nu\tilde{f}_{\nu}(t,y))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(I+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left(-\nu\frac{\partial U}{\partial y}\right)^{k}\right)(\nu f_{0}(y)+\nu\tilde{f}_{\nu}(t,y))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nu f_{0}(y)+\nu\tilde{\tilde{f}}_{\nu}(t,y)

We can see that
\tilde{\tilde{f}}_{\nu}
has the same properties as
f
, that is, it is remotely almost-periodic and
\tilde{\tilde{f}}_{\nu}\rightarrow 0
.

This completes the proof.
∎

By applying Lemma
3.2
, we obtain the following Theorem:

Theorem 3.3
.

Suppose that
f_{\nu}
satisfies the conditions mentioned in the first paragraph of the subsection.
If
f_{0}
, defined in (
3.3
), is such that there exists
x_{0}\in B_{r}(0)
with
f_{0}(x_{0})=0
and
\frac{\partial f}{\partial x}(x_{0})
has eigenvalues with nonzero real part.
Then there exist
r_{0}
and
\nu_{0}>0
sufficiently small such that, for
\nu\in(0,\nu_{0}]
, the equation (
3.1
) has a unique remotely almost-periodic solution
\varphi_{\nu}
, continuous on
\mathbb{R}\times(0,\nu_{0}]
, such that

(3.12)

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}|\varphi_{\nu}(t)-x_{0}|\leq r_{0}.

Proof.

By Lemma
3.2
, we can reduce (
3.1
) to (
3.10
).
Thus, performing the change of variable
y=z+x_{0}
, we obtain

(3.13)

\displaystyle z^{\prime}(t)=\nu Az+\nu\tilde{F}_{\nu}(t,z)

where
A=\frac{\partial f_{0}}{\partial x}(x_{0})
and

\displaystyle\tilde{F}_{\nu}(t,z)=f_{0}(z+x_{0})-f_{0}(x_{0})-\frac{\partial f_{0}}{\partial x}(x_{0})z+\tilde{\tilde{f}}_{\nu}(t,z+x_{0})

Thus, we can see that
\tilde{F}_{\nu}
satisfies the same properties as
f_{\nu}
.
Let
s=t\nu
,
z_{\nu}(s)=z(s\nu^{-1})
, and
\overline{F}_{\nu}(s,z_{\nu}(s))=\tilde{F}_{\nu}(s\nu^{-1},z_{\nu}(s))
.
Then, from equation (
3.13
), it follows that

\displaystyle\frac{dz_{\nu}}{ds}=Az_{\nu}+\overline{F}_{\nu}(s,z_{\nu}(s)),

where
\overline{F}
is a remotely almost-periodic function in the first variable, since
\tilde{F}
is.
Since
A
has no eigenvalues with zero real part, we have that the solution satisfies the integral equation given by

\displaystyle z_{\nu}(s)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}G(s,u)\overline{F}_{\nu}(u,z_{\nu}(u))du.

Let

B_{r_{0}}=\{\psi_{\nu}\in RAP({\mathbb{R}},{\mathbb{R}}^{n})|\|\psi_{\nu}\|_{\infty}\leq r_{0}\}.

We can see that the operator defined by

T\psi_{\nu}(s)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}G(s,u)\overline{F}_{\nu}(u,\psi_{\nu}(u))\,du

maps
B_{r_{0}}
into
B_{r_{0}}
by choosing

and
\nu_{0}
sufficiently small.
Then the proof follows from Banach’s fixed-point theorem.
∎

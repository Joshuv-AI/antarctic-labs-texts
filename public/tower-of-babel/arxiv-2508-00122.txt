Global solutions to cubic Dirac and Dirac-Klein-Gordon systems on spacetimes close to the Minkowski space Thanks: Key words and phrases. Nonlinear Dirac equation, Klein-Gordon equation, curved background, global existence, pointwise decay, vector fields, energy inequality, hyperboloidal foliation, bootstrap argument
Seokchang Hong

Abstract
. We establish global existence and derive sharp pointwise decay estimates of solutions to cubic Dirac and Dirac-Klein-Gordon systems on a curved background, close to the Minkowski spacetime. By squaring the Dirac operator, we reduce the analysis to a nonlinear wave-type equation involving spinorial connections, and apply energy estimates based on vector field methods and the hyperboloidal foliation framework, introduced by LeFloch–Ma. A key difficulty arises from the commutator structure of the Dirac operator, which exhibits significantly different behaviour from that of scalar field equations and requires refined control throughout the analysis, particularly due to the spacetime-dependent gamma matrices, which reduce to constant matrices in the flat Minkowski spacetime.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Motivation and Background

In the early twentieth century, the Dirac equation was originally derived by factorising the Klein-Gordon equation
(\Box-m^{2})\phi=0
. Dirac’s approach
[
37
]
was successful, and the resulting equation plays a central role in quantum field theory, effectively describing the spin
\frac{1}{2}
-fermions such as electrons. On the Minkowski spacetime
(\mathbb{R}^{1+3},\mathbf{m})
, where the metric
\mathbf{m}
is given by
\textrm{diag}(-1,+1,+1,+1)
, the linear Dirac equation takes the form

(1.1)

\displaystyle-i\gamma^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}\psi+m\psi=0,

where
m\geq 0
denotes the mass of the particle and
\psi:\mathbb{R}^{1+3}\to\mathbb{C}^{4}
is a complex-valued spinor field. The gamma matrices
\gamma^{\mu}\in\mathbb{C}^{4\times 4}
,
\mu=0,1,2,3
satisfies the algebraic relation:
\gamma^{\mu}\gamma^{\nu}+\gamma^{\nu}\gamma^{\mu}=-2m^{\mu\nu}I_{4}
, where
I_{4}
is the
4\times 4
identity matrix.

From the mathematical perspective, the Dirac equation is a first-order hyperbolic system. Hence several analytic techniques, which have been well-developed in the wave and the Klein-Gordon equations, can be applied to the analytic study of the Dirac equation, in that the Dirac equation can be formulated as the half-wave in the massless case
m=0
and the half-Klein-Gordon equations in the massive case
m>0
, respectively.

In recent decades, nonlinear problems of the Dirac equations have been extensively studied, especially in the context of dispersive equations. Global well-posedness and scattering results for cubic Dirac equations have been established by
[
72
,
6
,
9
]
. The Dirac-Klein-Gordon system has been well-studied by
[
34
,
91
,
8
,
18
]
.

When one is concerned with the Dirac equation in a general Lorentz manifold
(\mathcal{M},g)
, the usual partial differentiation in the equation must then be replaced by the spinorial covariant derivative. To be precise, the Dirac equation on a curved spacetime is given by

\displaystyle-i\gamma^{\mu}(t,x)\mathbf{D}_{\mu}\psi+m\psi=0,

where
\mathbf{D}_{\mu}
is the covariant derivative acting on the spinor field
\psi
. The precise definition of
\mathbf{D}_{\mu}
will be discussed in Section
2
. In contrast to the flat case, the gamma matrices now depend on spacetime points. Although the covariant Dirac equation naturally arises from the spin structure of a given Lorentz manifold, this is also essential for quantum field theory in curved spacetime, including physical phenomena such as Hawking radiation, and semiclassical gravity.

Recently, the mathematical analysis of the Dirac equation on a curved spacetime has also seen notable progress. Bär
[
5
]
investigated spectral properties of the covariant Dirac operator; Cacciafesta, Suzzoni, Meng
[
14
]
derived Strichartz estimates. More recently, the sharp decay for the linear massless Dirac equation on the Schwarzschild background was established by Ma and Zhang
[
71
]
.

Nevertheless, most of the previous results for the Dirac equation on a curved background have focused on the linear and massless setting. Moreover, the nonlinear analysis of the Dirac equation on a curved spacetime has remained relatively less explored.

Motivated by these considerations, we investigate the global behaviour of nonlinear Dirac equations on an asymptotically flat background, which is close to the Minkowski spacetime. We then establish global existence and sharp pointwise decay estimates of solutions to the systems in this setting.

1.3.
Technical challenge on a curved spacetime

We remark that an extension of existing results concerning nonlinear Dirac equations on the Minkowski spacetime to a curved background introduces several new technical difficulties.

\bullet
Sensitivity to geometric structure

First, the Dirac field is much more sensitive to the geometry of the spacetime. Indeed, the curved geometry
g
determines the algebraic relation:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(\gamma^{\mu}\gamma^{\nu}+\gamma^{\nu}\gamma^{\mu})=-g^{\mu\nu}I_{4},

which the gamma matrices must obey. This algebraic structure then determines how the Dirac operator is defined:
-i\gamma^{\mu}\mathbf{D}_{\mu}\psi+m\psi=0
. This is evident upon squaring the Dirac equation, which yields the following second-order equation:
2
2

2

By definition of the spinorial connection
\Gamma_{\mu}
, it transforms as a
1
-form under the change of coordinates. See also the proof of Proposition
2.1
for the details. Thus, all the lower-order terms behave as scalar functions.

\displaystyle\left(\Box_{g}-m^{2}+2\Gamma^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}+\nabla^{\mu}\Gamma_{\mu}+\Gamma^{\mu}\Gamma_{\mu}+\frac{\mathbf{R}}{4}\right)\psi=0,

where
\mathbf{R}
is the scalar curvature associated to the metric
g
,
\Gamma_{\mu}
is the spinorial connection, and
\nabla_{\mu}
is the covariant derivative acting on a scalar field. See also Proposition
2.1
. The second-order equation derived from the Dirac field possesses a purely geometric quantity and also a spinorial potential term, which does not appear in scalar field equations, such as the wave or the Klein-Gordon. Moreover, the presence of such lower-order terms makes global-in-time problem more subtle and requires delicate analysis, unlike the scalar field equations. One way of remedying this difficulty is to impose a stronger assumption on the metric. In fact, by an asymptotically flat background which is close to the Minkowski spacetime, we mean the metric
g
satisfies the inequality of the form
|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}(g-m)|\leq c_{\alpha}\langle r\rangle^{-1-|\alpha|}
, where the constants
c_{\alpha}
are sufficiently small, so that trapping is excluded. However, due to the presence of the lower-order terms, this smallness might not be enough, and one needs even smaller constants
c_{\alpha}
, to close the bootstrap argument and establish global solutions. Roughly speaking, the Dirac field demands much stronger assumptions on the underlying geometry, in order to achieve an analogue result as in the wave or the Klein-Gordon equations.

\bullet
Effects of the mass term on decay and conformal structure

Second, the presence of the mass term
m>0
introduces additional difficulties on a curved background, unlike on the Minkowski spacetime, where the mass term improves the time-decay compared to the wave equation. For the massive Dirac, in which case one does not have conformal invariance, the vector field such as
S=t\partial_{t}+r\partial_{r}
is not useful anymore. Even worse, the mass term interacts unfavourably with the geometry and makes the time decay to be
t^{-\frac{5}{6}}
, much weaker than
t^{-\frac{3}{2}}
, especially in the presence of a trapping region. See
[
42
]
and
[
78
]
.

In this work, under the non-trapping assumption we recover the optimal decay
t^{-\frac{3}{2}}
, thereby compensating the absence of conformal symmetry.
This suggests that extending our analysis to systems such as the Maxwell-Dirac, where the gauge field decays only as
t^{-1}
, introduces an additional challenge. We expect that extending the methods developed in this paper to such a system would be an interesting direction for future research.

1.4.
Main strategy

Now we give a key to the proof.

\bullet
Squaring the Dirac operator

We first square the Dirac operator. For the inhomogeneous Dirac equation
(-i\gamma^{\mu}\mathbf{D}_{\mu}+M)\psi=F
, by acting the Dirac operator
\gamma^{\nu}\mathbf{D}_{\nu}
on both sides, we obtain

\displaystyle\left(\Box_{g}-M^{2}+2\Gamma^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}+\nabla^{\mu}\Gamma_{\mu}+\Gamma^{\mu}\Gamma_{\mu}+\frac{\mathbf{R}}{4}\right)\psi=-i\gamma^{\mu}\mathbf{D}_{\mu}F-MF,

where
\Box_{g}
is the usual Laplace-Beltrami operator acting on a scalar field associated to the metric
g
. See also Proposition
2.1
. Due to the spinorial connection terms, one cannot view this equation as the usual Klein-Gordon equation. However, using an obvious inequality
|\Gamma_{\mu}|\lesssim|\partial g|
, we can consider this wave-type equation as the Klein-Gordon equation with a potential:

\displaystyle(\Box_{g}-M^{2})\psi=V\psi-2\Gamma^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}\psi-i\gamma^{\mu}\mathbf{D}_{\mu}F-MF,

where the poitential
V
satisfies the inequality
|V(x)|\lesssim|\partial^{2}g|+|\partial g|^{2}
. In what follows, the cubic Dirac and the (DKG) systems are transferred to the following nonlinear wave-type equations:

\displaystyle(\Box_{g}-M^{2})\psi=V\psi-2\Gamma^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}\psi-i\gamma^{\mu}\mathbf{D}_{\mu}(\psi^{\dagger}\gamma^{0}\psi)\psi-M(\psi^{\dagger}\gamma^{0}\psi)\psi,

and

\displaystyle(\Box_{g}-M^{2})\psi

\displaystyle=V\psi-2\Gamma^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}\psi-i\gamma^{\mu}\mathbf{D}_{\mu}\phi\psi-M\phi\psi,

\displaystyle(\Box_{g}-m^{2})\phi

\displaystyle=\psi^{\dagger}\gamma^{0}\psi,

respectively.
A notable distinction is that the Dirac equation exhibits a purely geometric quantity, such as the scalar curvature, unlike scalar field equations such as wave or Klein-Gordon. This is one of the reflections that the Dirac equation is strongly tied to the geometry of the spacetime. Due to these lower-order terms
V\psi
and
\Gamma^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}\psi
, global analysis is more delicate compared to the analysis on scalar fields.

\bullet
Commutators with
\gamma^{\mu}\mathbf{D}_{\mu}

After squaring the Dirac operator, one cannot completely transfer the problem of nonlinear Dirac equations to the problem of nonlinear Klein-Gordon equation, due to the inhomogeneous term
\gamma^{\mu}\mathbf{D}_{\mu}F
, as well as the lower order terms. This forces us to consider the commutators of several vector fields with the Dirac operator. Remarkably, the spinor structure demands one to take into account the spinor-adapted commutator, which does not appear in the analysis of scalar fields. Indeed, the commutator
[\gamma^{\mu}\partial_{\mu},L_{i}]
with the Lorentz boost
L_{i}
does not vanish even in the flat spacetime, and the Lorentz boost must be modified as
\widehat{L_{i}}=L_{i}+\frac{1}{2}\gamma_{0}\gamma_{i}
and one easily sees
[\gamma^{\mu}\partial_{\mu},\widehat{L_{i}}]=0
in the flat setting. It is obvious that
[\gamma^{\mu}\mathbf{D}_{\mu},\widehat{L_{i}}]\neq 0
on a curved background, which requires a delicate analysis. Nevertheless, we observe that all the commutators turn out to be acceptable error terms, and estimates of the nonlinear terms
\gamma^{\mu}\mathbf{D}_{\mu}F
will be reduced to estimates of scalar fields. See Proposition
4.2
.

\bullet

and
\frac{1}{10N}\leq\delta\leq\frac{1}{5N}
,

\displaystyle E_{M}[\partial^{I}L^{J}\psi](\tau)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq\frac{1}{2}C\varepsilon\tau^{\frac{1}{2}+(|I^{\prime}|+|J|)\delta},\quad N-3\leq|I|+|J|\leq N,

\displaystyle E_{M}[\partial^{I}L^{J}\psi](\tau)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq\frac{1}{2}C\varepsilon\tau^{(|I^{\prime}|+|J|)\delta},\quad|I|+|J|\leq N-4,

\displaystyle E_{m}[\partial^{I}L^{J}\phi](\tau)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq\frac{1}{2}C\varepsilon\tau^{\frac{1}{2}+(|I^{\prime}|+|J|)\delta},\quad N-3\leq|I|+|J|\leq N,

\displaystyle E_{m}[\partial^{I}L^{J}\phi](\tau)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq\frac{1}{2}C\varepsilon\tau^{(|I^{\prime}|+|J|)\delta},\quad|I|+|J|\leq N-4,

which implies that
\tau_{1}=+\infty
and we have global solutions
\psi
and
(\psi,\phi)
to the systems. To close the bootstrap argument, by energy inequality Proposition
3.1
, we have to show that

(1.12)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \int_{\tau_{0}}^{\tau}\|[\Box_{g},\partial^{I}L^{J}]\phi\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{\tau^{\prime}})}\,d\tau^{\prime}\leq\frac{1}{2}C\varepsilon\tau^{\frac{1}{2}+(|I^{\prime}|+|J|)\delta},\quad N-3\leq|I|+|J|\leq N,\\
\int_{\tau_{0}}^{\tau}\|[\Box_{g},\partial^{I}L^{J}]\phi\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{\tau^{\prime}})}\,d\tau^{\prime}\leq\frac{1}{2}C\varepsilon\tau^{(|I^{\prime}|+|J|)\delta},\quad|I|+|J|\leq N-4,\\
\int_{\tau_{0}}^{\tau}\|\partial^{I}L^{J}\Gamma^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}\psi\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{\tau^{\prime}})}\,d\tau^{\prime}\leq\frac{1}{2}C\varepsilon\tau^{\frac{1}{2}+(|I^{\prime}|+|J|)\delta},\quad N-3\leq|I|+|J|\leq N,\\
\int_{\tau_{0}}^{\tau}\|\partial^{I}L^{J}\Gamma^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}\psi\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{\tau^{\prime}})}\,d\tau^{\prime}\leq\frac{1}{2}C\varepsilon\tau^{(|I^{\prime}|+|J|)\delta},\quad|I|+|J|\leq N-4,\\
\int_{\tau_{0}}^{\tau}\|\partial^{I}L^{J}V\psi\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{\tau^{\prime}})}\,d\tau^{\prime}\leq\frac{1}{2}C\varepsilon\tau^{\frac{1}{2}+(|I^{\prime}|+|J|)\delta},\quad N-3\leq|I|+|J|\leq N,\\
\int_{\tau_{0}}^{\tau}\|\partial^{I}L^{J}V\psi\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{\tau^{\prime}})}\,d\tau^{\prime}\leq\frac{1}{2}C\varepsilon\tau^{(|I^{\prime}|+|J|)\delta},\quad|I|+|J|\leq N-4,\end{aligned}

and for the cubic problem we prove

(1.13)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \int_{\tau_{0}}^{\tau}\|\partial^{I}L^{J}\gamma^{\mu}\mathbf{D}_{\mu}[(\psi^{\dagger}\gamma^{0}\psi)\psi]\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{\tau^{\prime}})}\,d\tau^{\prime}\leq\frac{1}{2}C\varepsilon\tau^{\frac{1}{2}+(|I^{\prime}|+|J|)\delta},\quad N-3\leq|I|+|J|\leq N,\\

(1.14)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \int_{\tau_{0}}^{\tau}\|\partial^{I}L^{J}\gamma^{\mu}\mathbf{D}_{\mu}[\phi\psi]\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{\tau^{\prime}})}\,d\tau^{\prime}\leq\frac{1}{2}C\varepsilon\tau^{\frac{1}{2}+(|I^{\prime}|+|J|)\delta},\quad N-3\leq|I|+|J|\leq N,\\
\int_{\tau_{0}}^{\tau}\|\partial^{I}L^{J}\gamma^{\mu}\mathbf{D}_{\mu}[\phi\psi]\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{\tau^{\prime}})}\,d\tau^{\prime}\leq\frac{1}{2}C\varepsilon\tau^{(|I^{\prime}|+|J|)\delta},\quad|I|+|J|\leq N-4.\end{aligned}

In the estimates of the lower-order terms (

1.5.
Non-stationary metric

It is a natural question of whether the stationarity assumption can be relaxed and one can still obtain the global existence on a non-stationary spacetime, which is sufficiently close to the Minkowski space. This is the case when the metric satisfies a mild decay assumption for the time derivatives. Instead of the metric
g
with (
1.4
), we consider a Lorentzian metric
g
such that

(1)

t=const.
is space-like.

(2)

g
is decomposed into
g=m+g_{sr}+g_{lr}
,
where
g_{lr}
is a stationary long range spherically symmetric component and
g_{sr}
is a short range, not necessarily stationary component, such that

(1.15)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} |\partial_{x}^{\alpha}g_{lr}|&\leq c^{lr}_{\alpha}\langle r\rangle^{-1-|\alpha|},\quad|\alpha|\leq N+2,\\
|\partial_{t}^{\beta}\partial_{x}^{\alpha}g_{sr}|&\leq c^{sr}_{\alpha,\beta}\langle r+t\rangle^{-1-\eta}\langle r\rangle^{-(1-\eta+|\alpha|+\beta)},\quad|\alpha|+\beta\leq N+2,\end{aligned}

for some
0<\eta<1
.

Under the assumption (
1.15
), the global existence and sharp pointwise decay estimates for the cubic Dirac and the (DKG) systems can be established in essentially the same manner as the stationary metric (
1.4
). In fact, all key analytic ingredients, such as the energy estimates and commutator bounds remain valid. The necessary modification of the proof is straightforward, and the assumption (
1.15
) does not affect the essential structure of the argument.

1.7.
Toy model: nonlinear Klein-Gordon equations

We would like to emphasise that the key argument of Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.2
is mainly derived from the well-known argument concerning scalar field equations, and it can be directly applied to several nonlinear problems for the scalar field equations. Here we present cubic and quadratic nonlinear Klein-Gordon equations on an asymptotically flat spacetime, close to the Minkowski spacetime:

(1.20)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} (\Box_{g}-1)\phi=|\phi|^{2}\phi,\\
(\phi,\partial_{t}\phi)|_{t=t_{0}}:=(\phi_{0},\phi_{1}),\end{aligned}

and

(1.21)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} (\Box_{g}-1)\phi=\phi\nabla\phi,\\
(\phi,\partial_{t}\phi)|_{t=t_{0}}:=(\phi_{0},\phi_{1}),\end{aligned}

where
\nabla=\nabla_{t,x}
is the space-time derivative.
By repeating the argument as the proof of Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.2
, one is able to establish global existence and pointwise decay of the solutions to Cauchy problems (
1.20
) and (
1.21
), respectively. Moreover, the quadratic term with at most first-order derivative does not require any specific null condition. As a toy model problem, we present the proof in the Appendix.

2.
Dirac operators

The homogeneous covariant Dirac equation is given by

(2.1)

\displaystyle(-i\gamma^{\mu}(x)\mathbf{D}_{\mu}+M)\psi=0,

where
\psi:\mathbb{R}^{1+3}\to\mathbb{C}^{4}
is the unknown spinor field and
M>0
is a mass of the particle.
On a generally curved spacetime, the gamma matrices
\gamma^{\mu}
,
\mu=0,\cdots,3
are no longer constant matrices. Instead, the matrices
\gamma^{\mu}=\gamma^{\mu}(x)
also become space(-time) dependent complex matrices in a stationary (a non-stationary) metric. The algebraic property is then generalised to

(2.2)

\displaystyle\gamma^{\mu}\gamma^{\nu}+\gamma^{\nu}\gamma^{\mu}=-2g^{\mu\nu}(x)I_{4}.

We define the covariant derivative acting on a spinor field
\psi:\mathbb{R}^{1+3}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{4}
to be

(2.3)

\displaystyle\mathbf{D}_{\mu}\psi=(\partial_{\mu}-\Gamma_{\mu})\psi,

where the spinorial affine connections
\Gamma_{\mu}=\Gamma_{\mu}(x)
are matrices defined by the vanishing of the covariant derivative of the gamma matrices:

(2.4)

\displaystyle\mathbf{D}_{\mu}\gamma_{\nu}=\partial_{\mu}\gamma_{\nu}-\Gamma^{\lambda}_{\mu\nu}\gamma_{\lambda}-\Gamma_{\mu}\gamma_{\nu}+\gamma_{\nu}\Gamma_{\mu}=0.

One can represent the
\gamma^{\mu}(x)
matrices in terms of the gamma matrices
\tilde{\gamma}^{\mu}
on the flat spacetime by introducing a
vierbein

b^{\alpha}_{\mu}(x)
of vector fields, defined by

(2.5)

\displaystyle g_{\mu\nu}(x)=m_{\alpha\beta}b^{\alpha}_{\mu}(x)b^{\beta}_{\nu}(x).

Then the matrices
\gamma^{\mu}(t,x)
can be written in the form

(2.6)

\displaystyle\gamma^{\mu}(x)=b^{\mu}_{\alpha}(x)\tilde{\gamma}^{\alpha}.

Here we used the notation
\gamma^{\mu}(x)
and
\tilde{\gamma}^{\mu}
to distinguish the gamma matrices dependent on space-time from the constant matrices.
Now a set of
\Gamma_{\mu}(x)
satisfying (
2.4
) are given by

(2.7)

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\mu}(x)=-\frac{1}{4}\tilde{\gamma}_{\alpha}\tilde{\gamma}_{\beta}b^{\alpha\lambda}(x)\mathbf{D}_{\mu}b^{\beta}_{\lambda}(x),

where

\displaystyle\mathbf{D}_{\mu}b^{\beta}_{\lambda}=\partial_{\mu}b^{\beta}_{\lambda}-\Gamma^{\sigma}_{\mu\lambda}b^{\beta}_{\sigma}.

We refer the readers to
[
77
, Section 3.9]
for the details. However, concerning the spinorial affine connections
\Gamma_{\mu}
, we only need the inequalities
|\Gamma_{\mu}(x)|\lesssim|\partial_{x}g|
. See also Proposition 2.1 of
[
14
]
. Note that
\Gamma_{\mu}
behaves as
1
-forms. This will be exploited in the proof of Proposition
2.1
.

Since the Dirac equation was first developed via the factorisation of the Klein-Gordon equation on the Minkowski spacetime, squaring the Dirac operator leads us directly to the Klein-Gordon equation for each of the spinor components. Obviously, this is not the case for the Dirac equation on a curved spacetime. However, one can still obtain a generalisation of the Klein-Gordon equation up to spinorial connections. More precisely, we have the following: (See also
[
1
]
and
[
77
]
.)

Proposition 2.1
.

Given an inhomogeneous Dirac equation
-i\gamma^{\mu}\mathbf{D}_{\mu}\psi+M\psi=F
, we have

(2.8)

\displaystyle(\Box_{g}-M^{2})\psi=-2\Gamma^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}\psi+V\psi-i\gamma^{\mu}\mathbf{D}_{\mu}F-MF,

where
\Box_{g}
is the Laplace-Beltrami operator associated to the metric
g
and the potential
V
satisfies the inequality
|V(x)|\lesssim|\partial^{2}g|+|\partial g|^{2}
.

Proof.

To see this, we write

\displaystyle\gamma^{\mu}\mathbf{D}_{\mu}(\gamma^{\nu}\mathbf{D}_{\nu}\psi)

\displaystyle=\gamma^{\mu}\gamma^{\nu}\mathbf{D}_{\mu}\mathbf{D}_{\nu}\psi

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(\gamma^{\mu}\gamma^{\nu}+\gamma^{\nu}\gamma^{\mu}+\gamma^{\mu}\gamma^{\nu}-\gamma^{\nu}\gamma^{\mu})\mathbf{D}_{\mu}\mathbf{D}_{\nu}\psi,

where we used the fact
\mathbf{D}_{\mu}\gamma^{\nu}=0
.
By the algebraic relation for the gamma matrices (
2.2
), we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(\gamma^{\mu}\gamma^{\nu}+\gamma^{\nu}\gamma^{\mu})\mathbf{D}_{\mu}\mathbf{D}_{\nu}\psi

\displaystyle=-g^{\mu\nu}\mathbf{D}_{\nu}\mathbf{D}_{\nu}\psi.

On the other hand, we relabel the index to get

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(\gamma^{\mu}\gamma^{\nu}-\gamma^{\nu}\gamma^{\mu})\mathbf{D}_{\mu}\mathbf{D}_{\nu}\psi

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\left((\gamma^{\mu}\gamma^{\nu}-\gamma^{\nu}\gamma^{\mu})\mathbf{D}_{\mu}\mathbf{D}_{\nu}+(\gamma^{\nu}\gamma^{\mu}-\gamma^{\mu}\gamma^{\nu})\mathbf{D}_{\nu}\mathbf{D}_{\nu}\right)\psi

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\left(\gamma^{\mu}\gamma^{\nu}(\mathbf{D}_{\mu}\mathbf{D}_{\nu}-\mathbf{D}_{\nu}\mathbf{D}_{\mu})-\gamma^{\nu}\gamma^{\mu}(\mathbf{D}_{\mu}\mathbf{D}_{\nu}-\mathbf{D}_{\nu}\mathbf{D}_{\mu})\right)\psi

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}[\gamma^{\mu},\gamma^{\nu}][\mathbf{D}_{\mu},\mathbf{D}_{\nu}]\psi.

Now we use the following identities:

(2.9)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &[\mathbf{D}_{\mu},\mathbf{D}_{\nu}]\psi=\frac{1}{4}\gamma^{\lambda}\gamma^{\sigma}R_{\mu\nu\lambda\sigma}\psi,\\
&\gamma^{\mu}\gamma^{\nu}\gamma^{\lambda}\gamma^{\sigma}R_{\mu\nu\lambda\sigma}=-2\mathbf{R},\end{aligned}

where
\mathbf{R}
is the scalar curvature associated to the metric
g
. We refer the readers to Section 5.6.1 of Parker. From these identites we conclude that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}[\gamma^{\mu},\gamma^{\nu}][\mathbf{D}_{\mu},\mathbf{D}_{\nu}]\psi

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{4}\mathbf{R}\psi.

Finally we obtain

\displaystyle\gamma^{\mu}\mathbf{D}_{\mu}(\gamma^{\nu}\mathbf{D}_{\nu}\psi)=-g^{\mu\nu}\mathbf{D}_{\mu}\mathbf{D}_{\nu}\psi-\frac{1}{4}\mathbf{R}\psi.

Now we compute the term
\mathbf{D}_{\nu}\mathbf{D}_{\nu}\psi
. We first recall that
\mathbf{D}_{\mu}\psi
is also a
1
-form. Then

\displaystyle\mathbf{D}_{\mu}\mathbf{D}_{\nu}\psi

\displaystyle=\partial_{\mu}(\mathbf{D}_{\nu}\psi)-\Gamma_{\mu}(\mathbf{D}_{\nu}\psi)-\Gamma^{\lambda}_{\mu\nu}\mathbf{D}_{\lambda}\psi

\displaystyle=\partial_{\mu}(\partial_{\nu}\psi-\Gamma_{\nu}\psi)-\Gamma_{\mu}(\partial_{\mu}\psi-\Gamma_{\nu}\psi)-\Gamma^{\lambda}_{\mu\nu}(\partial_{\lambda}\psi-\Gamma_{\lambda}\psi)

\displaystyle=(\partial_{\mu}\partial_{\nu}\psi-\Gamma^{\lambda}_{\mu\nu}\partial_{\lambda}\psi)-\Gamma_{\mu}\partial_{\nu}\psi-\Gamma_{\nu}\partial_{\mu}\psi-(\partial_{\mu}\Gamma_{\nu})\psi+\Gamma^{\lambda}_{\mu\nu}\Gamma_{\lambda}\psi+\Gamma_{\mu}\Gamma_{\nu}\psi

\displaystyle=\nabla_{\mu}\nabla_{\nu}\psi-(\Gamma_{\mu}\partial_{\nu}\psi+\Gamma_{\nu}\partial_{\mu}\psi)-(\nabla_{\mu}\Gamma_{\nu}-\Gamma_{\mu}\Gamma_{\nu})\psi,

where
\nabla_{\mu}
is the usual covariant derivative that acts on
\phi
as a scalar, and on
\Gamma_{\mu}
as a
1
-form. See also
[
1
]
. Therefore we see that

\displaystyle-g^{\mu\nu}\mathbf{D}_{\mu}\mathbf{D}_{\nu}\psi

\displaystyle=-\nabla^{\mu}\nabla_{\mu}\psi-2\Gamma^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}\psi-(\nabla^{\mu}\Gamma_{\mu}-\Gamma^{\mu}\Gamma_{\mu})\psi,

where
\nabla^{\mu}\nabla_{\mu}
is then the Laplace-Beltrami operator
\Box_{g}
associated to the metric
g
. In consequence, an application of the operator
-i\gamma^{\mu}\mathbf{D}_{\mu}
on the equation
-i\gamma^{\nu}\mathbf{D}_{\nu}\psi+M\psi=F
yields the desired identity.
∎

3.
Hyperboloidal foliation method

This section is devoted to the discussion on the hyperboloidal foliation method.
From now on we only consider
t\geq 2
inside the forward light cone
\{(t,x):|x|\leq t-1\}
and we assume that
\phi
is supported inside the forward light cone, i.e.,
\phi
vanishes unless
\{|x|<t-1\}
. In other words, we focus on the solutions which are spatially compactly supported.

In what follows, we briefly review the foliation, energy estimates, and the Klainerman-Sobolev inequality, and Hardy-type estimates on the hyperboloid, introduced in
[
45
]
. For interested readers, we present in the Appendix the background and the derivation of energy flux and energy estimates using a standard argument in details.

We introduce the foliation
\Sigma_{\tau}=\{(t,x):\tau=\sqrt{t^{2}-|x|^{2}}\}
.
We define the energy on the hyperboloid:

(3.1)

\displaystyle E[\phi](\tau)=\int_{\Sigma_{\tau}}\left(|\partial_{t}\phi|^{2}+|\partial_{x}\phi|^{2}+|\phi|^{2}+2\frac{x^{j}}{t}\Re(\partial_{t}\phi\overline{\partial_{j}\phi})\right)\,dx,

or equivalently,

(3.2)

\displaystyle E[\phi](\tau)=\int_{\Sigma_{\tau}}\sum_{j=1}^{3}\left|\partial_{j}\phi+\frac{x^{j}}{t}\partial_{t}\phi\right|^{2}+\left|\frac{\tau}{t}\partial_{t}\phi\right|^{2}+|\phi|^{2}\,dx.

We denote the integral of a function
\phi
along the hyperboloid
\Sigma_{\tau}
by

\displaystyle\|\phi\|_{L^{p}(\Sigma_{\tau})}^{p}:=\int_{\Sigma_{\tau}}|\phi|^{p}\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\phi(\sqrt{\tau^{2}+|x|^{2}},x)|^{p}\,dx.

We also set the domain
D_{[\tau_{0},\tau_{1}]}
to be

\displaystyle D_{[\tau_{0},\tau_{1}]}:=\bigcup_{\tau_{0}\leq\tau\leq\tau_{1}}\Sigma_{\tau}\,\cap\{(t,x):|x|<t-1\}.

Proposition 3.1
.

For the solution
\phi
to the inhomogeneous Klein-Gordon equation
(\Box_{g}-1)\phi=F
, which are spatially supported inside the forward light cone
\{(t,x):|x|<t-1\}
, we have the energy estimates:

(3.3)

\displaystyle\sup_{\tau_{0}\leq\tau^{\prime}\leq\tau}E[\phi](\tau^{\prime})^{\frac{1}{2}}\lesssim E[\phi](\tau_{0})^{\frac{1}{2}}+\int_{\tau_{0}}^{\tau}\|F\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{\tau^{\prime}})}\,d\tau^{\prime}.

The proof follows from a standard argument of using the divergence theorem with the contraction of the energy-momentum tensor and the time-like vector field
\partial_{t}
. We refer the readers to the Appendix for the details.

Now we have the hyperboloid version of the Klainerman-Sobolev inequality: See also Lemma 7.6.1 of
[
61
]
and Proposition 5.1.1 of
[
44
]
.

Lemma 3.2

(Proposition 2.2 of
[
45
]
)
.

Let
\phi
be a sufficiently smooth function, which is spatially supported inside the forward light cone
\{(t,x):|x|<t-1\}
. Then we have

\displaystyle\sup_{(t,x)\in\Sigma_{\tau}}t^{\frac{3}{2}}|\phi(t,x)|\lesssim\sum_{|J|\leq 2}\|L^{J}\phi\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{\tau})},

where
L\in\{L_{i}\}_{i=1,2,3}
is the Lorentz boost.

Lemma 3.3

(Lemma 2.4 of
[
45
]
)
.

For any sufficiently smooth function
\phi
which is defined in the forward region
D_{[2,\tau]}
and is spatially supported inside the forward light cone
\{(t,x):|x|<t-1\}
, we have for all
\tau\geq 2
,

(3.4)

\displaystyle\|r^{-1}\phi\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{\tau})}\lesssim\sum_{i=1}^{3}\|t^{-1}L_{i}\phi\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{\tau})}.

The Hardy-type inequality will be effectively used to gain
t^{-1}
-factor from the error terms which appear in the commutator of the Lorentz boost and
\Box_{g}
and the Dirac operator
\gamma^{\mu}\mathbf{D}_{\mu}
, which is a core of the following section.

4.
Commutators

In this section, we present several commutators with the Laplace-Beltrami operator
\Box_{g}
and the Dirac operator
\gamma^{\mu}\mathbf{D}_{\mu}
. The main issue is the commutator with the Lorentz boost
L_{i}=t\partial_{i}+x^{i}\partial_{i}
,
i=1,2,3
, in that it gives an additional
t
-factor in the error terms, which do not appear in the Minkowski spacetime. This becomes problematic in the energy estimates, and hence the aim of this section is to discuss how one can eliminate this problematic
t
-growth from commutators
[\Box_{g},L_{i}]
and
[\gamma^{\mu}\mathbf{D}_{\mu},L_{i}]
. Moreover, we also reduce the nonlinear problem involving
\gamma^{\mu}\mathbf{D}_{\mu}
to merely a nonlinear problem of scalar fields.

4.1.
Commutators with the Laplace-Beltrami operator

The commutator of the Laplace-Beltrami operator
\Box_{g}
with the translation operator
\partial_{\mu}
is obvious, and we omit the details. Now we focus on the commutator of
\Box_{g}
with the Lorentz boost. We first recall that

\displaystyle\Box_{g}=\Box+g^{\omega}_{lr}(r)\Delta_{\omega}+\partial_{\alpha}g^{\alpha\beta}_{sr}\partial_{\beta}

where
g^{\omega}_{lr}(r)\approx\langle r\rangle^{-3}
and
g^{00}_{sr}=0
. Then the commutator is given by

\displaystyle[\Box_{g},L_{i}]=[\partial_{\mu}g^{\mu\nu}_{sr}\partial_{\nu},t\partial_{i}+x^{i}\partial_{t}].

Then

\displaystyle[\partial_{j}g^{jk}_{sr}\partial_{k},L_{i}]

\displaystyle=[(\partial_{j}g^{jk}_{sr})\partial_{k},t\partial_{i}+x^{i}\partial_{t}]+[g^{jk}_{sr}\partial_{j}\partial_{k},t\partial_{i}+x^{i}\partial_{t}],

and

\displaystyle[g^{0j}_{sr}\partial_{j}\partial_{t},L_{i}]

\displaystyle=g^{0j}_{sr}\partial_{j}\partial_{i}-t(\partial_{i}g^{0j}_{sr})\partial_{j}\partial_{t}+g^{0j}_{sr}\partial_{t}^{2},

\displaystyle[(\partial_{j}g^{jk}_{sr})\partial_{k},L_{i}]

\displaystyle=-t(\partial_{i}\partial_{j}g^{jk}_{sr})\partial_{k}+(\partial_{j}g^{ji}_{sr})\partial_{t},

\displaystyle[g^{jk}_{sr}\partial_{j}\partial_{k},L_{i}]

\displaystyle=-t(\partial_{i}g^{jk}_{sr})\partial_{j}\partial_{k}+g^{ji}_{sr}\partial_{t}\partial_{j}+g^{ik}_{sr}\partial_{t}\partial_{k}.

Thus one can encounter an additional
t
factor from the commutator of
\Box_{g}
with the Lorentz boost
L_{i}
. This situation becomes even worse when one has the commutator of
\Box_{g}
with the product of
|J|
-Lorentz boost
L^{J}
,
|J|\geq 2
, and all of
L^{J}
act on the metric, which yields the factor
t^{|J|}
.
However, we can remedy this dangerous
t
-growth. It is helpful to introduce the bootstrap assumption for a moment:

For
|I|+|J|\leq N
with some fixed
N\geq 11
, we assume that the energy bound
E[\partial^{I}L^{J}\phi](\tau)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq C\varepsilon\tau^{\frac{1}{2}+(|I^{\prime}|+|J|)\delta}
holds on a hyperbolic time interval
[\tau_{0},\tau_{1}]
for
|I|+|J|\leq N
, where
\partial^{I}=\partial_{t}^{I^{\prime}}\partial_{x}^{I^{\prime\prime}}
. We will discuss this bootstrap assumption in detail in Section
5
.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
\phi
be a sufficiently smooth and spatially compactly supported function defined inside the forward light cone
\{(t,x):|x|<t-1\}
.
Under the aforementioned bootstrap assumptions, the commutator term
[\partial^{I}L^{J},\Box_{g}]\phi
satisfies for
|I|+|J|\leq N
,

(4.1)

\displaystyle\|[\partial^{I}L^{J},\Box_{g}]\phi\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{\tau})}\leq C\varepsilon t^{-\frac{1}{2}}\tau^{-\frac{1}{2}+(N+2)\delta}.

To see this we first note that for
|I|+|J|=N

the commutator
[\partial^{I}L^{J},\Box_{g}]
yields the summation

\displaystyle[\partial^{I}L^{J},\Box_{g}]\phi

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I_{1},I_{2},J_{1},J_{2}\\
|I_{2}|+|J_{2}|\leq N-1\end{subarray}}(\partial^{I_{1}}L^{J_{1}}g^{\mu\nu}_{sr})\partial_{\mu}\partial_{\nu}(\partial^{I_{2}}L^{J_{2}}\phi)+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I_{1},I_{2},J_{1},J_{2}\\
|I_{2}|+|J_{2}|\leq N-1\end{subarray}}(\partial^{I_{1}}L^{J_{1}}\partial_{\mu}g^{\mu\nu}_{sr})\partial_{\nu}(\partial^{I_{2}}L^{J_{2}}\phi).

From now on, we consider the case
|I_{2}|+|J_{2}|=N-1
. For brevity, we put
t^{-1}L_{i}=V_{i}
.
If
|J_{1}|=1
and
|I_{1}|=0
, with
(\mu,\nu)=(0,i)
then

\displaystyle|L(g^{0i}_{sr})\partial_{t}\partial_{i}\partial^{I_{2}}L^{J-1}\phi|

\displaystyle\leq t\langle r\rangle^{-3}|\partial_{t}\partial_{i}\partial^{I}L^{J-1}\phi|

\displaystyle=t\langle r\rangle^{-3}|\partial_{t}(V_{i}-\frac{x^{i}}{t}\partial_{t})\partial^{I}L^{J-1}\phi|

\displaystyle\leq t\langle r\rangle^{-3}|\partial_{t}V_{i}\partial^{I}L^{J-1}\phi|+t\langle r\rangle^{-3}\frac{|x|}{t}|\partial_{t}^{2}\partial^{I}L^{J-1}\phi|,

where we omit an obvious error term which appears due to the term
\bigg|\partial_{t}\dfrac{x^{i}}{t}\bigg|\leq\dfrac{|x|}{t^{2}}
. Using
tV_{i}=L_{i}
, we see that

\displaystyle|L(g^{0i}_{sr})\partial_{t}\partial_{i}\partial^{I_{2}}L^{J-1}\phi|

\displaystyle\leq\langle r\rangle^{-3}|\partial_{t}\partial^{I-2}\partial_{j}^{2}L^{J}\phi|+\langle r\rangle^{-2}|\partial_{t}^{2}\partial^{I-2}\partial_{j}^{2}L^{J-1}\phi|

\displaystyle=\langle r\rangle^{-3}|\partial_{t}\partial^{I-2}(V_{j}+\frac{x^{j}}{t}\partial_{t})^{2}L^{J}\phi|+\langle r\rangle^{-2}|\partial_{t}^{2}\partial^{I-2}(V_{j}+\frac{x^{j}}{t}\partial_{t})^{2}L^{J-1}\phi|

\displaystyle\leq\langle r\rangle^{-3}|\partial_{t}\partial^{I-2}V_{j}^{2}L^{J}\phi|+\langle r\rangle^{-3}\frac{|x|}{t}|\partial_{t}^{2}\partial^{I-2}V_{j}L^{J}\phi|+\langle r\rangle^{-3}\frac{|x|^{2}}{t^{2}}|\partial_{t}^{3}\partial^{I-2}L^{J}\phi|

\displaystyle\qquad+\langle r\rangle^{-2}|\partial_{t}^{2}\partial^{I-2}V_{j}^{2}L^{J-1}\phi|+\langle r\rangle^{-2}\frac{|x|}{t}|\partial_{t}^{3}\partial^{I-2}V_{j}L^{J-1}\phi|+\langle r\rangle^{-3}\frac{|x|^{2}}{t^{2}}|\partial_{t}^{4}\partial^{I-2}L^{J-1}\phi|

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{-2}\langle r\rangle^{-1}(|\partial^{I-2}L^{J+2}\partial_{t}\phi|+|\partial^{I-2}L^{J+1}\partial_{t}^{2}\phi|+|\partial^{I-2}L^{J}\partial_{t}^{3}\phi|

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad+|\partial^{I-2}L^{J+1}\partial_{t}^{2}\phi|+|\partial^{I-2}L^{J}\partial_{t}^{3}\phi|+|\partial^{I-2}L^{J-1}\partial_{t}^{4}\phi|),

where we omit several obvious error terms arising from the commutator with
V_{i}
.
On the other hand, if
J_{1}=0
and
I_{1}=1
, then there is no
t
-growth and we have nothing to do. The case
(\mu,\nu)=(i,j)
is similar, which we omit the details. Therefore we see that

\displaystyle\|L(g_{sr}^{\alpha\beta})\partial_{\alpha}\partial_{\beta}\partial^{I_{2}}L^{J-1}\phi\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{\tau})}

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{-2}C\varepsilon\tau^{\frac{1}{2}+(|I^{\prime}|+|J|+2)\delta}\frac{t}{\tau},

where we used
\|\partial_{t}\phi\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{\tau})}\leq\frac{\tau}{t}E[\phi](\tau)^{\frac{1}{2}}
and the bootstrap assumptions.
This completes the case
|I_{2}|+|J_{2}|=N-1
. If
|I_{2}|+|J_{2}|=N-2
, with
|J_{1}|=2
, then using the Hardy inequality Lemma
3.3
on the hyperboloid
we see that

\displaystyle\|L^{2}(g^{0j}_{sr}\partial_{t}\partial_{j})\partial^{I}L^{J-2}\phi\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{\tau})}

\displaystyle\leq\|t^{2}\langle r\rangle^{-4}\partial_{t}\partial_{j}\partial^{I}L^{J-2}\phi\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{\tau})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|t^{2}V_{i}\langle r\rangle^{-3}\partial_{t}\partial_{j}\partial^{I}L^{J-2}\phi\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{\tau})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|t\langle r\rangle^{-3}\partial_{t}\partial_{j}\partial^{I}L^{J-1}\phi\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{\tau})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|t\langle r\rangle^{-3}\partial_{t}V_{j}\partial^{I}L^{J-1}\phi\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{\tau})}+\|t\langle r\rangle^{-3}\frac{|x|}{t}\partial_{t}^{2}\partial^{I}L^{J-1}\phi\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{\tau})}.

Then the remaining step is identical as the case
|I_{2}|+|J_{2}|=N-1
. In summary, for
|J_{2}|\geq 2
, in which case one has
t^{|J_{2}|}
in the error terms, we apply the Hardy inequality Lemma
3.3

|J_{2}|
-times to gain the factor
t^{-|J_{2}|}
and follow the argument for the case
|J_{2}|=1
. Finally, an inductive argument and the initial bootstrap assumption give

(4.2)

\displaystyle\|[\partial^{I}L^{J},\Box_{g}]\phi\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{\tau})}\leq C\varepsilon t^{-1}\tau^{-\frac{1}{2}+(|I^{\prime}|+|J|+2)\delta},\quad|I|+|J|\leq N,

where we put
\partial^{I}=\partial_{t}^{I^{\prime}}\partial_{x}^{I^{\prime\prime}}
. Therefore we conclude that the commutator terms
[\partial^{I}L^{J},\Box_{g}]\phi
become acceptable error terms in the energy estimates.

4.2.
Commutators with Dirac operator

Now we consider the commutators with the Dirac operator
\gamma^{\mu}\mathbf{D}_{\mu}
. Even though we make the use of squaring the Dirac operator and transfer the study of the nonlinear Dirac equations to the nonlinear problems of the Klein-Gordon equations as in Proposition
2.1
, one still has to deal with the Dirac operator, since the Dirac operator
\gamma^{\mu}\mathbf{D}_{\mu}
then acts on the nonlinearity. Due to the spinor structure, the commutator does not behave in the usual way as scalar fields. However, it turns out that the nonlinear problems associated to the Dirac operator
\gamma^{\mu}\mathbf{D}_{\mu}
are reduced to the problem of scalar fields. To be precise, we observe the following:

Proposition 4.2
.

For
|I|+|J|\leq N
, we have

(4.3)

\displaystyle\|\partial^{I}L^{J}\gamma^{\mu}\mathbf{D}_{\mu}\psi\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{\tau})}\lesssim\sum_{|I_{1}|\leq|I|}\|\partial_{t}\partial^{I_{1}}L^{J}\psi\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{\tau})}+\sum_{j=1}^{3}\sum_{|I_{1}|\leq|I|}\|\partial_{j}\partial^{I_{1}}L^{J}\psi\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{\tau})}.

We shall prove this via several steps. We first consider the commutator of
\gamma^{\mu}\mathbf{D}_{\mu}
with
\partial_{\nu}
and
L_{i}
.
The commutator with the translation vector fields
\partial_{\nu}
,
\nu=0,1,2,3
is somewhat obvious.

Proposition 4.3
.

For the translation vector fields
\partial_{\nu}
,
\nu=0,1,2,3
, we have

(4.4)

\displaystyle[\partial_{\nu},\gamma^{\mu}\mathbf{D}_{\mu}]\psi=\Gamma_{\nu}(\gamma^{\mu}\mathbf{D}_{\mu}\psi)-\gamma^{\mu}(\partial_{\nu}\Gamma_{\mu})\psi.

This commutator identity follows via a straightforward computation. Indeed, we have

\displaystyle[\partial_{\nu},\gamma^{\mu}\mathbf{D}_{\mu}]

\displaystyle=\partial_{\nu}(\gamma^{\mu}\mathbf{D}_{\mu})-\gamma^{\mu}\mathbf{D}_{\mu}\partial_{\nu}

\displaystyle=(\partial_{\nu}\gamma^{\mu})\mathbf{D}_{\mu}+\gamma^{\mu}\partial_{\nu}\mathbf{D}_{\mu}-\gamma^{\mu}\mathbf{D}_{\mu}\partial_{\nu}

\displaystyle=(\partial_{\nu}\gamma^{\mu})\mathbf{D}_{\mu}+\gamma^{\mu}\partial_{\nu}(\partial_{\mu}-\Gamma_{\mu})-\gamma^{\mu}(\partial_{\mu}-\Gamma_{\mu})\partial_{\nu}

\displaystyle=(\partial_{\nu}\gamma^{\mu})\mathbf{D}_{\mu}+\gamma^{\mu}\partial_{\nu}\partial_{\mu}-\gamma^{\mu}(\partial_{\nu}\Gamma_{\mu})-\gamma^{\mu}\Gamma_{\mu}\partial_{\nu}-\gamma^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}\partial_{\nu}+\gamma^{\mu}\Gamma_{\mu}\partial_{\nu}

\displaystyle=[(\partial_{\nu}-\Gamma_{\nu})\gamma^{\mu}]\mathbf{D}_{\mu}+\Gamma_{\nu}\gamma^{\mu}\mathbf{D}_{\mu}-\gamma^{\mu}(\partial_{\nu}\Gamma_{\mu})

\displaystyle=\Gamma_{\nu}(\gamma^{\mu}\mathbf{D}_{\mu})-\gamma^{\mu}(\partial_{\nu}\Gamma_{\mu}),

where we used the fact
\mathbf{D}_{\nu}\gamma^{\mu}=0
.
For the commutator with the Lorentz boost
L_{i}
we have to carefully consider the spinor structure. Indeed, we have the commutator identity

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
L_{i}=t\partial_{i}+x^{i}\partial_{t}
,
i=1,2,3
, be the Lorentz boost. Then we have

(4.5)

\displaystyle L_{i}\gamma^{\mu}\mathbf{D}_{\mu}\psi=\gamma^{\mu}\mathbf{D}_{\mu}L_{i}\psi+(L_{i}\gamma^{\mu}\mathbf{D}_{\mu})\psi+(g_{ij}-\delta_{ij})\gamma^{j}\partial_{t}\psi-g_{j0}\gamma^{j}\partial_{i}\psi.

An obvious computation gives

\displaystyle L_{i}\gamma^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}\psi

\displaystyle=(t\partial_{i}+x^{i}\partial_{t})\gamma^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}\psi

\displaystyle=t(\partial_{i}\gamma^{\mu})\partial_{\mu}\psi+t\gamma^{\mu}\partial_{i}\partial_{\mu}\psi+x^{i}(\partial_{t}\gamma^{\mu})\partial_{\mu}\psi+x^{i}\gamma^{\mu}\partial_{t}\partial_{\mu}\psi,

and we also observe that

\displaystyle t\gamma^{\mu}\partial_{i}\partial_{\mu}\psi

\displaystyle=t\gamma^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}\partial_{i}\psi

\displaystyle=t\gamma^{0}\partial_{t}\partial_{i}\psi+t\gamma^{j}\partial_{j}\partial_{i}\psi

\displaystyle=\gamma^{0}\partial_{t}(t\partial_{i}\psi)-\gamma^{0}\partial_{i}\psi+\gamma^{j}\partial_{j}(t\partial_{i}\psi)

\displaystyle=\gamma^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}(t\partial_{i}\psi)-\gamma^{0}\partial_{i}\psi,

and

\displaystyle x^{i}\gamma^{\mu}\partial_{t}\partial_{\mu}\psi

\displaystyle=x^{i}\gamma^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}\partial_{t}\psi

\displaystyle=x^{i}\gamma^{0}\partial_{t}\partial_{t}\psi+x^{i}\gamma^{j}\partial_{j}\partial_{t}\psi

\displaystyle=\gamma^{0}\partial_{t}(x^{i}\partial_{t}\psi)+\gamma^{j}\partial_{j}(x^{i}\partial_{t}\psi)-\gamma^{j}\delta^{i}_{j}\partial_{t}\psi

\displaystyle=\gamma^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}(x^{i}\partial_{t}\psi)-\gamma^{i}\partial_{t}\psi.

Thus we conclude that the commutator of the Dirac operator with the Lorentz boost yields not only the error terms from the derivatives of the gamma matrices but also additional terms
-\gamma^{0}\partial_{i}\psi-\gamma^{i}\partial_{t}\psi
. To remedy this error term, we introduce the modified Lorentz boost
\widehat{L_{i}}=L_{i}+\frac{1}{2}\gamma_{0}\gamma_{i}
. Indeed, we see that

\displaystyle\gamma_{0}\gamma_{i}\gamma^{\mu}

\displaystyle=\gamma_{0}\gamma^{\nu}g_{i\nu}\gamma^{\mu}

\displaystyle=\gamma_{0}(-\gamma^{\mu}\gamma^{\nu}-2g^{\mu\nu}I_{4})g_{i\nu}

\displaystyle=-\gamma_{0}\gamma^{\mu}\gamma^{\nu}g_{i\nu}-2\gamma_{0}g^{\mu\nu}g_{i\nu}

\displaystyle=-\gamma^{\lambda}g_{\lambda 0}\gamma^{\mu}\gamma^{\nu}g_{i\nu}-2\gamma_{0}g^{\mu\nu}g_{i\nu}

\displaystyle=-(-\gamma^{\mu}\gamma^{\lambda}-2g^{\mu\lambda}I_{4})g_{\lambda 0}\gamma^{\nu}g_{i\nu}-2\gamma_{0}\delta^{\mu}_{i}

\displaystyle=\gamma^{\mu}\gamma^{\lambda}\gamma^{\nu}g_{\lambda 0}g_{i\nu}+2g^{\mu\lambda}\gamma^{\nu}g_{\lambda 0}g_{i\nu}-2\gamma_{0}\delta^{\mu}_{i}

\displaystyle=\gamma^{\mu}\gamma_{0}\gamma_{i}+2\delta^{\mu}_{0}\gamma_{i}-2\gamma_{0}\delta^{\mu}_{i},

which implies the identity
\gamma_{0}\gamma_{i}\gamma^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}=\gamma^{\mu}\gamma_{0}\gamma_{i}\partial_{\mu}+2\gamma_{i}\partial_{t}-2\gamma_{0}\partial_{i}
.
Then we have

\displaystyle(L_{i}+\frac{1}{2}\gamma_{0}\gamma_{i})\psi

\displaystyle=t\gamma^{\mu}\partial_{i}\partial_{\mu}\psi+x^{i}\gamma^{\mu}\partial_{t}\partial_{\mu}\psi+t(\partial_{i}\gamma^{\mu})\partial_{\mu}\psi+x^{i}(\partial_{t}\gamma^{\mu})\partial_{\mu}\psi

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{1}{2}\gamma^{\mu}\gamma_{0}\gamma_{i}\partial_{\mu}\psi+\gamma_{i}\partial_{t}\psi-\gamma_{0}\partial_{i}\psi.

We observe that
\gamma_{i}\partial_{t}\psi=g_{\mu i}\gamma^{\mu}\partial_{t}\psi=\gamma^{i}\partial_{t}\psi+(g_{ij}-\delta_{ij})\gamma^{j}\partial_{t}\psi+g_{0i}\gamma^{0}\partial_{t}\psi
and
-\gamma_{0}\partial_{i}\psi=-\gamma^{\nu}g_{\nu 0}\partial_{i}\psi=\gamma^{0}\partial_{i}\psi-g_{j0}\gamma^{j}\partial_{i}
.
Then we see that

\displaystyle(L_{i}+\frac{1}{2}\gamma_{0}\gamma_{i})\gamma^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}\psi

\displaystyle=\gamma^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}(L_{i}\psi)+\frac{1}{2}\gamma^{\mu}\gamma_{0}\gamma_{i}\partial_{\mu}\psi+t(\partial_{i}\gamma^{\mu})\partial_{\mu}\psi+x^{i}(\partial_{t}\gamma^{\mu})\partial_{\mu}\psi

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+(g_{ij}-\delta_{ij})\gamma^{j}\partial_{t}\psi-g_{j0}\gamma^{j}\partial_{i}\psi

\displaystyle=\gamma^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}(L_{i}+\frac{1}{2}\gamma_{0}\gamma_{i})\psi+t(\partial_{i}\gamma^{\mu})\partial_{\mu}\psi+x^{i}(\partial_{t}\gamma^{\mu})\partial_{\mu}\psi-\frac{1}{2}\gamma^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}(\gamma_{0}\gamma_{i})\psi

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+(g_{ij}-\delta_{ij})\gamma^{j}\partial_{t}\psi+g_{0i}\gamma^{0}\partial_{t}\psi-g_{j0}\gamma^{j}\partial_{i}\psi

\displaystyle=\gamma^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}(L_{i}+\frac{1}{2}\gamma_{0}\gamma_{i})\psi+(L_{i}\gamma^{\mu})\partial_{\mu}\psi-\frac{1}{2}\gamma^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}(\gamma_{0}\gamma_{i})\psi

5.
Main estimates: Bootstrap argument

I_{2},J_{2}\end{subarray}}C^{I_{1},J_{1}}_{I_{2},J_{2}}\int_{\tau_{0}}^{\tau}\|(\partial_{t}\partial^{I_{1}}L^{J_{2}}\phi)(\partial^{I_{2}}L^{J_{2}}\psi)\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{\tau^{\prime}})}\,d\tau^{\prime}

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I_{1},J_{1}\\
I_{2},J_{2}\end{subarray}}C^{I_{1},J_{1}}_{I_{2},J_{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{3}\int_{\tau_{0}}^{\tau}\|(\partial_{j}\partial^{I_{1}}L^{J_{2}}\phi)(\partial^{I_{2}}L^{J_{2}}\psi)\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{\tau^{\prime}})}\,d\tau^{\prime}.

Applying the Hölder inequality, the Klainerman-Sobolev inequality, and then the bootstrap assumptions (
5.1
) and (
5.2
), we see that

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I_{1},J_{1}\\
I_{2},J_{2}\end{subarray}}C^{I_{1},J_{1}}_{I_{2},J_{2}}\int_{\tau_{0}}^{\tau}\|(\partial_{t}\partial^{I_{1}}L^{J_{2}}\phi)(\partial^{I_{2}}L^{J_{2}}\psi)\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{\tau^{\prime}})}\,d\tau^{\prime}

\displaystyle\leq C^{\prime}\int_{\tau_{0}}^{\tau}\|\partial_{t}\partial^{I_{1}}L^{J_{1}}\phi\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{\tau^{\prime}})}\|\partial^{I_{2}}L^{J_{2}}\psi\|_{L^{\infty}(\Sigma_{\tau^{\prime}})}\,d\tau^{\prime}

\displaystyle\leq C^{\prime}M^{-1}\int_{\tau_{0}}^{\tau}\frac{t}{\tau}C\varepsilon\tau^{\frac{1}{2}+(|I_{1}^{\prime}|+|J_{1}|)\delta}t^{-\frac{3}{2}}C\varepsilon\tau^{(|I_{2}^{\prime}|+|J_{2}|+2)\delta}\,d\tau^{\prime}

\displaystyle\leq C^{\prime}C^{2}\varepsilon^{2}M^{-1}\int_{\tau_{0}}^{\tau}t^{-\frac{1}{2}}\tau^{-\frac{1}{2}+(N+2)\delta}\,d\tau^{\prime}

\displaystyle\leq C^{\prime}C^{2}\varepsilon^{2}M^{-1}\tau^{(N+2)\delta},

and

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I_{1},J_{1}\\
I_{2},J_{2}\end{subarray}}C^{I_{1},J_{1}}_{I_{2},J_{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{3}\int_{\tau_{0}}^{\tau}\|(\partial_{j}\partial^{I_{1}}L^{J_{2}}\phi)(\partial^{I_{2}}L^{J_{2}}\psi)\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{\tau^{\prime}})}\,d\tau^{\prime}

\displaystyle\leq 3C^{\prime}\int_{\tau_{0}}^{\tau}\|(\partial_{j}+\frac{x^{j}}{t}\partial_{t})\partial^{I_{1}}L^{J_{1}}\phi\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{\tau^{\prime}})}\|\partial^{I_{2}}L^{J_{2}}\psi\|_{L^{\infty}(\Sigma_{\tau^{\prime}})}\,d\tau^{\prime}

\displaystyle\qquad+3C^{\prime}\int_{\tau_{0}}^{\tau}\|\partial_{t}\partial^{I_{1}}L^{J_{1}}\phi\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{\tau^{\prime}})}\|\partial^{I_{2}}L^{J_{2}}\psi\|_{L^{\infty}(\Sigma_{\tau^{\prime}})}\,d\tau^{\prime}

\displaystyle\leq 6C^{\prime}C^{2}\varepsilon^{2}M^{-1}\tau^{(N+2)\delta}.

Thus we choose
\varepsilon=\dfrac{\min(m,M)}{12C^{\prime}C}
. Note that we have not used any specific structure of the quadratic term
\phi\psi
, and hence our argument can be readily applied to the quadratic term
\psi^{\dagger}\gamma^{0}\psi
. We omit the details, and this completes the proof.

5.4.
Proof of Proposition
5.1

We give the proof of the estimates for the linear terms which appear in the right-handside of the nonlinear wave-type equation derived from Proposition
2.1
.
This can be achieved as follows: we first write
\Gamma^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}\psi=\Gamma^{0}\partial_{t}\psi+\Gamma^{j}\partial_{j}\psi
. Then

\displaystyle\|\partial^{I}L^{J}\Gamma^{j}\partial_{j}\psi\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{\tau})}

\displaystyle\leq c\|\langle r\rangle^{-2}\partial_{j}\partial^{I}L^{J}\psi\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{\tau})}+\sum_{|I_{2}|+|J_{2}|\leq N-1}c_{I_{1},J_{1},I_{2},J_{2}}\|\partial^{I_{1}}L^{J_{2}}\Gamma^{j}\partial_{j}\partial^{I_{2}}L^{J_{2}}\psi\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{\tau})}.

The second term turns out to be an acceptable error via the previous argument in Section 4. To control the first term, we write

\displaystyle\|\langle r\rangle^{-2}\partial_{j}\partial^{I}L^{J}\psi\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{\tau})}

\displaystyle\leq\|(\partial_{j}+\frac{x^{j}}{t}\partial_{t})\partial^{I}L^{J}\psi\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{\tau})}+\|\langle r\rangle^{-2}\frac{x^{j}}{t}\partial_{t}\partial^{I}L^{J}\psi\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{\tau})}.

Now we see that

\displaystyle\|\langle r\rangle^{-2}\frac{x^{j}}{t}\partial_{t}\partial^{I}L^{J}\psi\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{\tau})}

\displaystyle\leq\tau^{-1}\|\langle r\rangle^{-1}\frac{\tau}{t}\partial_{t}\partial^{I}L^{J}\psi\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{\tau})}

\displaystyle\leq\tau^{-1}E[\partial^{I}L^{J}\psi](\tau)^{\frac{1}{2}}

and

\displaystyle\|(\partial_{j}+\frac{x^{j}}{t}\partial_{t})\partial^{I}L^{J}\psi\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{\tau})}

\displaystyle\leq t^{-1}\|\partial^{I}L^{J+1}\psi\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{\tau})}

\displaystyle=t^{-1}\|\partial_{t}\partial^{I-1}L^{J+1}\psi\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{\tau})}

\displaystyle\leq\tau^{-1}E[\partial^{I-1}L^{J+1}\psi](\tau)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

If
\partial^{I}=\partial_{x}^{I}
, then we get a better bound. Indeed, we see that

\displaystyle\|(\partial_{j}+\frac{x^{j}}{t}\partial_{t})\partial^{I}L^{J}\psi\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{\tau})}

\displaystyle\leq\|t^{-1}\partial^{I}L^{J+1}\psi\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{\tau})}

\displaystyle\leq\|t^{-1}(\partial_{j}+\frac{x^{j}}{t}\partial_{t})\partial_{x}^{I-1}L^{J}\psi\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{\tau})}+\|t^{-1}\frac{x^{j}}{t}\partial_{t}\partial_{x}^{I-1}L^{J}\psi\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{\tau})}

\displaystyle\leq\|t^{-2}\partial_{x}^{I-1}L^{J+1}\psi\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{\tau})}+\|t^{-2}|x|\partial_{x}^{I-1}L^{J}\psi\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{\tau})}.

For the
\Gamma^{0}\partial_{t}\psi
, we follow the previous argument in Section
4
. Indeed, we see that

\displaystyle\|\Gamma^{0}\partial_{t}\partial^{I}L^{J}\psi\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{\tau})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\langle r\rangle^{-3}\partial_{t}\partial^{I}L^{J}\psi\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{\tau})}

\displaystyle=\|\langle r\rangle^{-3}\partial_{t}\partial_{i}\partial_{j}\partial^{I-2}L^{J}\psi\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{\tau})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|t^{-2}\langle r\rangle^{-3}\partial_{t}\partial^{I-2}L^{J+2}\psi\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{\tau})}+\|t^{-2}\langle r\rangle^{-2}\partial_{t}^{2}\partial^{I-2}L^{J+1}\psi\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{\tau})}

\displaystyle\qquad+\|t^{-2}\langle r\rangle^{-1}\partial_{t}^{3}\partial^{I-2}L^{J}\psi\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{\tau})}.

For the
V\psi
, we simply use the Hardy-type inequality Lemma
3.3
on the hyperboloid to get

\displaystyle\|V\partial^{I}L^{J}\psi\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{\tau})}

\displaystyle\leq c\|\langle r\rangle^{-3}\partial^{I}L^{J}\psi\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{\tau})}

\displaystyle\leq cC^{\prime}\sum_{j=1}^{3}\|\langle r\rangle^{-2}(\partial_{j}+\frac{x^{j}}{t}\partial_{t})\partial^{I}L^{J}\psi\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{\tau})}

\displaystyle\leq 3cC^{\prime}t^{-1}\|\partial^{I}L^{J+1}\psi\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{\tau})}

\displaystyle=3cC^{\prime}\tau^{-1}\|\frac{\tau}{t}\partial_{t}\partial^{I-1}L^{J+1}\psi\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{\tau})}

\displaystyle\leq 3cC^{\prime}\tau^{-1}E[\partial^{I-1}L^{J+1}\psi](\tau)^{\frac{1}{2}},

where the smallness of the constant

ensures to close the bootstrap argument provided that the underlying curved spacetime is sufficently close to the Minkowski spacetime. If
\partial^{I}=\partial_{x}^{I}
, we obtain a better bound via the identical manner as in the control of
\Gamma^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}\psi
. We omit the details. Thus we conclude that

\displaystyle\int_{\tau_{0}}^{\tau}\|\partial^{I}L^{J}\Gamma^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}\psi\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{\tau^{\prime}})}+\|\partial^{I}L^{J}V\psi\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{\tau^{\prime}})}\,d\tau^{\prime}\leq\frac{1}{4}C\varepsilon\tau^{\frac{1}{2}+(|I^{\prime}|+|J|)\delta},\quad|I|+|J|\leq N,

which completes the proof. Combining all of the results from Proposition
5.1
, Proposition
5.2
, and Proposition
5.3
, the bootstrap argument can be closed and
\tau^{*}=+\infty
, and hence the Cauchy problems of the cubic Dirac (
5.7
) and the Dirac-Klein-Gordon systems (

) admit global solutions. Furthermore, repeating the bootstrap argument with modified assumptions, given the data on the initial hyperboloid
\Sigma_{\tau_{0}}
satisfying the energy bound,

\displaystyle E_{M}[\partial^{I}L^{J}\psi](\tau_{0})^{\frac{1}{2}}+E_{m}[\partial^{I}L^{J}\phi](\tau_{0})^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq C_{0}\varepsilon,

we can establish the global solutions

and

to (

From the bootstrap argument with the Klainerman-Sobolev inequality, we deduce that the solutions

Appendix

The purpose of this section is to present backgrounds of the hyperboloidal foliation method and establish global existence of cubic and quadratic nonlinear Klein-Gordon equations as toy models.

5.5.
Energy estimates on the hyperboloid

We introduce the foliation
\Sigma_{\tau}=\{(t,x):\tau=\sqrt{t^{2}-|x|^{2}}\}
.
This is the level set of the function
f(t,x)=\sqrt{t^{2}-|x|^{2}}
and hence the normal vector
N_{\mu}
is given by
N_{\mu}=\partial_{\mu}f=\frac{1}{\sqrt{t^{2}-|x|^{2}}}(t,-x^{j})
and from
N^{\mu}=g^{\mu\nu}N_{\nu}
we have

\displaystyle N^{0}=g^{0\nu}\partial_{\nu}f=\frac{1}{\sqrt{t^{2}-|x|^{2}}}(g^{00}t-g^{0j}x^{j}),\quad N^{j}=g^{j\nu}\partial_{\nu}f=\frac{1}{\sqrt{t^{2}-|x|^{2}}}(g^{0j}t-g^{ij}x^{i}),

or equivalently, we have

\displaystyle N^{\mu}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{t^{2}-|x|^{2}}}(g^{\mu 0}t-g^{\mu i}x^{i}).

Then the quantity
g^{\mu\nu}N_{\mu}N_{\nu}
is given by

\displaystyle g^{\mu\nu}N_{\mu}N_{\nu}=\frac{1}{t^{2}-|x|^{2}}(g^{00}t^{2}+g^{ij}x^{i}x^{j}-2g^{0j}tx^{j}):=\sigma.

We also observe that

\displaystyle|\sigma|=1+\frac{1}{t^{2}-|x|^{2}}(2g^{0j}tx^{j}-(g^{ij}-\delta^{ij})x^{i}x^{j}).

Then the unit normal vector
n^{\mu}
is given by

\displaystyle n^{\mu}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{|\sigma|}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{t^{2}-|x|^{2}}}(g^{\mu 0}t-g^{\mu i}x^{i}).

Since
g^{00}=-1
, we choose
n^{\mu}\to-n^{\mu}
so that
n^{0}
is positive and
n^{\mu}
is future-directied unit time-like vector. From now on, we consider the unit normal vector

\displaystyle n^{\mu}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{|\sigma|}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{t^{2}-|x|^{2}}}(-g^{\mu 0}t+g^{\mu i}x^{i}).

From this we define the vector field
N\phi=n^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}\phi
.

For
\tau_{0}\leq\tau_{1}
, we consider the subdomain bounded by the hyperboloids
\Sigma_{\tau_{0}}
and
\Sigma_{\tau_{1}}
, inside the forward light cone, which is denoted by

\displaystyle D_{[\tau_{0},\tau_{1}]}=\bigcup_{\tau_{0}\leq\tau\leq\tau_{1}}\Sigma_{\tau}\,\cap\{(t,x):|x|<t-1\}.

We shall establish the energy estimates inside the forward light cone
\{(t,x):|x|<t-1\}
, using the hyperboloid foliation
\Sigma_{\tau}
. To do this, we use the contraction with the energy momentum tensor
T_{\mu\nu}
given by

\displaystyle T_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=\Re(\partial_{\mu}\phi\overline{\partial_{\nu}\phi})-\frac{1}{2}g_{\mu\nu}(g^{\alpha\beta}\partial_{\alpha}\phi\overline{\partial_{\beta}\phi}+|\phi|^{2}),

with the vector field
X=\partial_{t}
. Then we use the divergence theorem for
T_{\mu\nu}X^{\nu}
on the domain
\bigcup_{\tau_{0}\leq\tau^{\prime}\leq\tau}\Sigma_{\tau^{\prime}}
, which gives

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma_{\tau}}T_{\mu\nu}X^{\nu}n^{\mu}\,d\sigma_{\Sigma_{\tau}}-\int_{\Sigma_{\tau_{0}}}T_{\mu\nu}X^{\nu}n^{\mu}\,d\sigma_{\Sigma_{\tau_{0}}}=\int_{\tau_{0}}^{\tau}T^{\mu\nu}\pi_{\mu\nu}^{(X)}d\sigma_{\Sigma_{\tau^{\prime}}}d\tau^{\prime}+\int_{\tau_{0}}^{\tau}\Re(F\cdot\overline{\partial_{t}\phi})\,d\sigma_{\Sigma_{\tau^{\prime}}}d\tau^{\prime},

where
\pi^{(X)}
is the deformation tensor given by

\displaystyle\pi^{(X)}_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=\nabla_{\mu}X_{\nu}+\nabla_{\nu}X_{\mu}.

We note that the deformation tensor
\pi^{(X)}
vanishes provided that
X
is a Killing field. Here we are concerned with a non-stationary case and assume (
1.15
) and adapt the normalised coordinates.
We compute the energy flux
T_{\mu\nu}X^{\nu}n^{\mu}
:

\displaystyle T_{\mu\nu}X^{\nu}n^{\mu}

\displaystyle=\Re(n^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}\phi\overline{X^{\nu}\partial_{\nu}\phi})-\frac{1}{2}g_{\mu\nu}X^{\nu}n^{\mu}(g^{\alpha\beta}\partial_{\alpha}\phi\overline{\partial_{\beta}\phi}+|\phi|^{2}).

Here we compute

\displaystyle g_{\mu\nu}X^{\nu}n^{\mu}

\displaystyle=g_{00}X^{0}n^{0}+g_{j0}X^{0}n^{j}

\displaystyle=-n^{0}+g_{j0}n^{j}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{|\sigma|}\sqrt{t^{2}-|x|^{2}}}(-t-g^{0i}x^{i})+\frac{1}{\sqrt{|\sigma|}\sqrt{t^{2}-|x|^{2}}}g_{j0}(-g^{j0}t+g^{ji}x^{i})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{|\sigma|}\sqrt{t^{2}-|x|^{2}}}(-t)+(Error),

and

\displaystyle N\phi

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{|\sigma|}\sqrt{t^{2}-|x|^{2}}}\left((t+g^{0i}x^{i})\partial_{t}\phi+(-g^{j0}t+g^{ji}x^{i})\partial_{j}\phi\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{|\sigma|}\sqrt{t^{2}-|x|^{2}}}(t\partial_{t}\phi+x^{j}\partial_{j}\phi)

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{1}{\sqrt{|\sigma|}\sqrt{t^{2}-|x|^{2}}}\left((g^{0i}x^{i}\partial_{t}\phi+(-g^{j0}t+(g^{ij}-\delta^{ij}x^{i})\partial_{j}\phi)\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{|\sigma|}\sqrt{t^{2}-|x|^{2}}}(t\partial_{t}\phi+x^{j}\partial_{j}\phi)+(Error).

In consequence, we have

\displaystyle T_{\mu\nu}X^{\nu}n^{\mu}

\displaystyle=\frac{t}{\sqrt{|\sigma|}\sqrt{t^{2}-|x|^{2}}}\left(|\partial_{t}\phi|^{2}+|\partial_{x}\phi|^{2}+|\phi|^{2}+2\frac{x^{j}}{t}\Re(\partial_{t}\phi\overline{\partial_{j}\phi})\right)+(Error),

where

\displaystyle(Error)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{|\sigma|}\sqrt{t^{2}-|x|^{2}}}\left((g^{ij}-\delta^{ij})(\partial_{i}\phi\overline{\partial_{j}\phi}+\partial_{j}\phi\overline{\partial_{i}\phi})+g^{j0}(\partial_{t}\phi\overline{\partial_{j}\phi}+\partial_{j}\phi\overline{\partial_{t}\phi})\right)

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{g^{0i}x^{i}+g_{j0}(g^{j0}t-g^{ji}x^{i})}{2\sqrt{|\sigma|}\sqrt{t^{2}-|x|^{2}}}(g^{\alpha\beta}\partial_{\alpha}\phi\overline{\partial_{\beta}\phi}+|\phi|^{2})

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{1}{\sqrt{|\sigma|}\sqrt{t^{2}-|x|^{2}}}\left((g^{0i}x^{i})|\partial_{t}\phi|^{2}+(-g^{j0}t+(g^{ij}-\delta^{ij})x^{i})\Re(\partial_{j}\phi\overline{\partial_{t}\phi})\right).

The factor
t
, which appears in the error terms, should not be harmful. Indeed, we observe that

\displaystyle t

\displaystyle=t-\sqrt{t^{2}-|x|^{2}}+\sqrt{t^{2}-|x|^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{t^{2}-(t^{2}-|x|^{2})}{t+\sqrt{t^{2}-|x|^{2}}}+\sqrt{t^{2}-|x|^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{|x|^{2}}{t+\sqrt{t^{2}-|x|^{2}}}+\sqrt{t^{2}-|x|^{2}}\leq\frac{|x|^{2}}{t}+\sqrt{t^{2}-|x|^{2}}.

Then we see that

\displaystyle\frac{|g^{j0}|t}{\sqrt{t^{2}-|x|^{2}}}\leq c\left(\frac{1}{t\sqrt{t^{2}-|x|^{2}}}+\langle x\rangle^{-2}\right),

where
c
is a sufficiently small constant, due to the non-trapping condition. Thus we conclude that

\displaystyle|(Error)|\leq c|\partial\phi|^{2},

and this can be absorbed into the leading terms of the energy flux. Indeed, we define the energy

(5.12)

\displaystyle E[\phi](\tau)=\int_{\Sigma_{\tau}}\left(|\partial_{t}\phi|^{2}+|\partial_{x}\phi|^{2}+|\phi|^{2}+2\frac{x^{j}}{t}\Re(\partial_{t}\phi\overline{\partial_{j}\phi})\right)\,dx

or equivalently

(5.13)

\displaystyle E[\phi](\tau)=\int_{\Sigma_{\tau}}\left(\sum_{j=1}\left|\partial_{j}\phi+\frac{x^{j}}{t}\partial_{t}\phi\right|^{2}+\left|\frac{\tau}{t}\partial_{t}\phi\right|^{2}+|\phi|^{2}\right)\,dx,

where we used the fact that the surface measure satisfies the identity
d\sigma_{\Sigma_{\tau}}=\frac{\tau}{t}dx
. In order to establish the energy inequality, we need to control the bulk term
\int T\pi
.
For
X=\partial_{t}
, we have
X^{0}=1
and
X^{j}=0
,
j=1,2,3
. Then
X_{\nu}=g_{\nu 0}X^{0}=g_{\nu 0}
and hence

\displaystyle\pi_{00}

\displaystyle=2(\partial_{t}g_{00}-\Gamma^{\lambda}_{00}g_{\lambda 0})=-2\Gamma^{\lambda}_{00}g_{\lambda 0},

\displaystyle\pi_{0i}

\displaystyle=\partial_{t}g_{i0}-2\Gamma^{\lambda}_{i0}g_{\lambda 0},

\displaystyle\pi_{ij}

\displaystyle=\partial_{i}g_{j0}+\partial_{j}g_{i0}-2\Gamma^{\lambda}_{ij}g_{\lambda 0}.

We recall that the Christoffel symbols are given by

\displaystyle\Gamma^{\lambda}_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}g^{\lambda\rho}(\partial_{\mu}g_{\nu\rho}+\partial_{\nu}g_{\mu\rho}-\partial_{\rho}g_{\mu\nu}),

and we compute

\displaystyle|\Gamma^{0}_{00}|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}|g^{0\rho}(\partial_{t}g_{0\rho}+\partial_{t}g_{0\rho}-\partial_{\rho}g_{00})|\leq c^{2}\langle r+t\rangle^{-2-2\eta}\langle r\rangle^{-3+2\eta},

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}|g^{i\rho}(\partial_{t}g_{0\rho}+\partial_{t}g_{0\rho}-\partial_{\rho}g_{00})|\leq c\langle r+t\rangle^{-1-\eta}\langle r\rangle^{-2+\eta},

\displaystyle|\Gamma^{0}_{i0}|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}|g^{0\rho}(\partial_{i}g_{0\rho}+\partial_{t}g_{i\rho}-\partial_{\rho}g_{i0})|\leq c\langle r+t\rangle^{-1-\eta}\langle r\rangle^{-2+\eta},

\displaystyle|\Gamma^{j}_{i0}|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}|g^{j\rho}(\partial_{i}g_{0\rho}+\partial_{t}g_{i\rho}-\partial_{\rho}g_{i0})|\leq c\langle r+t\rangle^{-1-\eta}\langle r\rangle^{-2+\eta},

\displaystyle|\Gamma^{0}_{ij}|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}|g^{0\rho}(\partial_{i}g_{j\rho}+\partial_{j}g_{i\rho}-\partial_{\rho}g_{ij})|\leq c\langle r+t\rangle^{-1-\eta}\langle r\rangle^{-2+\eta},

with a sufficiently small constant

and

g_{k0}

\displaystyle|\pi_{\mu\nu}^{(X)}|\leq c\langle r+t\rangle^{-1-\eta}\langle r\rangle^{-2+\eta}.

Now we compute the components of the energy-momentum tensor:

\displaystyle T^{00}

\displaystyle=\partial^{0}\phi\partial^{0}\phi-\frac{1}{2}g^{00}(-|\partial_{t}\phi|^{2}+|\partial_{x}\phi|^{2}+|\phi|^{2}+(g^{ij}-\delta^{ij})\partial_{i}\phi\partial_{j}\phi+2g^{0j}\partial_{t}\phi\partial_{j}\phi)

In consequence, we see that

\displaystyle\left|\int_{D_{[\tau_{0},\tau]}}T^{\mu\nu}\pi_{\mu\nu}^{(X)}\right|

\displaystyle\leq c\int_{\tau_{0}}^{\tau}\frac{1}{\tau^{1+\eta}}E[\phi](\tau^{\prime})\,d\tau^{\prime}\leq c\sup_{\tau_{0}\leq\tau^{\prime}\leq\tau}E[\phi](\tau^{\prime}),

for some sufficiently small

.
Then
we may choose constants

so that

\displaystyle c_{1}E[\phi](\tau)

\displaystyle\leq c_{2}E[\phi](\tau_{0})+c\sup_{\tau_{0}\leq\tau^{\prime}\leq\tau}E[\phi](\tau^{\prime})+\int_{\tau_{0}}^{\tau}\int_{\Sigma_{\tau}}|F\cdot\partial_{t}\phi|\,d\sigma_{\Sigma_{\tau}}\,d\tau^{\prime}

\displaystyle=c_{2}E[\phi](\tau_{0})+c\sup_{\tau_{0}\leq\tau^{\prime}\leq\tau}E[\phi](\tau^{\prime})+\int_{\tau_{0}}^{\tau}\int_{\Sigma_{\tau}}|F||\partial_{t}\phi|\frac{\tau}{t}\,dxd\tau^{\prime}

\displaystyle\leq c_{2}E[\phi](\tau_{0})+c\sup_{\tau_{0}\leq\tau^{\prime}\leq\tau}E[\phi](\tau^{\prime})+\int_{\tau_{0}}^{\tau}\|F\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{\tau^{\prime}})}\,d\tau^{\prime}\sup_{\tau_{0}\leq\tau^{\prime}\leq\tau}E[\phi](\tau^{\prime})^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Then the standard energy argument implies

\displaystyle\sup_{\tau_{0}\leq\tau^{\prime}\leq\tau}E[\phi](\tau^{\prime})^{\frac{1}{2}}\lesssim E[\phi](\tau_{0})^{\frac{1}{2}}+\int_{\tau_{0}}^{\tau}\|F\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{\tau^{\prime}})}\,d\tau^{\prime},

where the bulk term is absorbed into the left-hand side due to the smallness of
c>0
.
We summarise it as follows:

Proposition 5.4
.

For the solution
\phi
to the inhomogeneous Klein-Gordon equation
(\Box_{g}-1)\phi=F
, which are spatially supported inside the forward light cone
\{(t,x):|x|<t-1\}
, we have the energy estimates:

(5.14)

\displaystyle\sup_{\tau_{0}\leq\tau^{\prime}\leq\tau}E[\phi](\tau^{\prime})^{\frac{1}{2}}\lesssim E[\phi](\tau_{0})^{\frac{1}{2}}+\int_{\tau_{0}}^{\tau}\|F\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{\tau^{\prime}})}\,d\tau^{\prime}.

5.6.
Toy model: Cubic Klein-Gordon equations

We consider the cubic Klein-Gordon equation

\displaystyle(\Box_{g}-1)\phi=|\phi|^{2}\phi,\quad(t,x)\in\{(t,x):t\geq 2,|x|\leq t-1\}.

with initial data
\phi|_{t=2}=\phi_{0},\ \partial_{t}\phi|_{t=2}=\phi_{1}
. The goal is to establish global solution of the cubic problem, given any spatially compactly supported, smooth initial data with
\|\phi_{0}\|_{H^{N}}+||\phi_{1}\|_{H^{N-1}}\leq\varepsilon
.

Let
\phi
be the local-in-time solution to the Cauchy problem associated to the cubic problem. A standard local analysis ensures to construct a local-in-time solution from the data given on the initial hyperboloid
\Sigma_{\tau_{0}}
with
\tau_{0}=2
, and for all
|I|+|J|\leq N
,

(5.15)

\displaystyle E[\partial^{I}L^{J}\phi](\tau_{0})^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq C_{0}\varepsilon,

for some absolute constant
C_{0}>0
.
Now we make the bootstrap assumptions: for some hyperbolic time interval
[\tau_{0},\tau_{1}]
, the following inequalities hold:

\displaystyle E[\partial^{I}L^{J}\phi](\tau)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq C\varepsilon\tau^{(|I^{\prime}|+|J|)\delta},\quad|I|+|J|\leq N-4,

\displaystyle E[\partial^{I}L^{J}\phi](\tau)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq C\varepsilon\tau^{\frac{1}{2}+(|I^{\prime}|+|J|)\delta},\quad N-3\leq|I|+|J|\leq N,

where we put
\partial^{I}=\partial_{t}^{I^{\prime}}\partial_{x}^{I^{\prime\prime}}
and we fix
\frac{1}{10N}\leq\delta\leq\frac{1}{5N}
.
We let
\tau_{*}=\sup\{\tau_{1}:\textrm{ the bootstrap assumptions hold on }[\tau_{0},\tau_{1}]\}
. By choosing
C>C_{0}
, we have
\tau^{*}\geq 2
.
Applying the energy estimate Proposition
3.1
, we have

\displaystyle\sup_{\tau}E[\partial^{I}L^{J}\phi][\tau]^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq C^{\prime}E[\partial^{I}L^{J}\phi](\tau_{0})^{\frac{1}{2}}+\int_{\tau_{0}}^{\tau}\|[\partial^{I}L^{J},\Box_{g}]\phi\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{\tau^{\prime}})}\,d\tau^{\prime}+\int_{\tau_{0}}^{\tau}\|\partial^{I}L^{J}(|\phi|^{2}\phi)\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{\tau^{\prime}})}\,d\tau^{\prime}.

Then Proposition
4.1
gives

\displaystyle\int_{\tau_{0}}^{\tau}\|[\partial^{I}L^{J},\Box_{g}]\phi\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{\tau^{\prime}})}\,d\tau^{\prime}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{|I^{\prime}|+|J^{\prime}|\leq N}c_{I^{\prime},J^{\prime}}\int_{\tau_{0}}^{\tau}t^{-2}\|\partial^{I^{\prime}}L^{J^{\prime}}\phi\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{\tau^{\prime}})}\,d\tau^{\prime}

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon\int_{\tau_{0}}^{\tau}t^{-2}(\tau^{\prime})^{\frac{1}{2}+(N+2)\delta}\,d\tau^{\prime}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}C\varepsilon.

For the cubic term, we see that

\displaystyle\int_{\tau_{0}}^{\tau}\|\partial^{I}L^{J}(|\phi|^{2}\phi)\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{\tau^{\prime}})}\,d\tau^{\prime}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I_{1},I_{2},I_{3}\\
J_{1},J_{2},J_{3}\end{subarray}}c^{I_{1},I_{2},I_{3}}_{J_{1},J_{2},J_{3}}\int_{\tau_{0}}^{\tau}\|\partial^{I_{1}}L^{J_{1}}\phi\cdot\partial^{I_{2}}L^{J_{2}}\phi\cdot\partial^{I_{3}}L^{J_{3}}\phi\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{\tau^{\prime}})}\,d\tau^{\prime}.

We may assume that
|I_{1}|+|J_{1}|\geq|I_{2}|+|J_{2}|\geq|I_{3}|+|J_{3}|
. Then using the Klainerman-Sobolev-type estimates Lemma
3.2
we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I_{1},I_{2},I_{3}\\
J_{1},J_{2},J_{3}\end{subarray}}c^{I_{1},I_{2},I_{3}}_{J_{1},J_{2},J_{3}}\int_{\tau_{0}}^{\infty}\|\partial^{I_{1}}L^{J_{1}}\phi\cdot\partial^{I_{2}}L^{J_{2}}\phi\cdot\partial^{I_{3}}L^{J_{3}}\phi\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{\tau})}\,d\tau

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I_{1},I_{2},I_{3}\\
J_{1},J_{2},J_{3}\end{subarray}}c^{I_{1},I_{2},I_{3}}_{J_{1},J_{2},J_{3}}\int_{\tau_{0}}^{\tau}\|\partial^{I_{1}}L^{J_{1}}\phi\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{\tau^{\prime}})}\|\partial^{I_{2}}L^{J_{2}}\phi\|_{L^{\infty}(\Sigma_{\tau^{\prime}})}\|\partial^{I_{3}}L^{J_{3}}\phi\|_{L^{\infty}(\Sigma_{\tau^{\prime}})}\,d\tau^{\prime}

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}C^{3}\varepsilon^{3}\int_{\tau_{0}}^{\tau}(\tau^{\prime})^{\frac{1}{2}+(|I_{1}^{\prime}|+|J_{1}|)\delta}t^{-3}(\tau^{\prime})^{(|I_{2}^{\prime}|+|J_{2}|+|I_{3}^{\prime}|+|J_{3}|+4)\delta}\,d\tau^{\prime}

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}C^{3}\varepsilon^{3}\int_{\tau_{0}}^{\tau}t^{-3}(\tau^{\prime})^{\frac{1}{2}+(N+4)\delta}\,d\tau^{\prime}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{4}C_{1}C^{3}\varepsilon^{3},

where we used the fact that if
|I_{1}|+|J_{1}|\geq N-3
, then
|I_{2}|+|J_{2}|+|I_{3}|+|J_{3}|\leq 3
provided that
N\geq 14
. Now we choose
\varepsilon
small enough so that
\varepsilon=\dfrac{1}{2CC_{1}}
. Therefore we obtain better estimates compared to the initial bootstrap assumptions, which shows that
\tau^{*}=+\infty
.

5.7.
Toy model: quadratic Klein-Gordon equations

We consider the quadratic Klein-Gordon equation

(5.16)

\displaystyle(\Box_{g}-1)\phi=\phi\nabla\phi,\quad(t,x)\in\{(t,x):t\geq 2,|x|<t-1\},

with initial data
\phi|_{t=2}=\phi_{0}
and
\partial_{t}\phi|_{t=2}=\phi_{1}
. Here
\nabla=\nabla_{t,x}
is the space-time derivative. We make the bootstrap assumptions as in the previous problem. Then we have

\displaystyle\int_{\tau_{0}}^{\tau}\|\partial^{I}L^{J}\phi\partial\phi\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{\tau^{\prime}})}\,d\tau^{\prime}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I_{1},J_{1}\\
I_{2},J_{2}\end{subarray}}\sum_{j=1}^{3}\int_{\tau_{0}}^{\tau}\|\partial^{I_{1}}L^{J_{1}}\phi\cdot\partial^{I_{2}}L^{J_{2}}\partial_{j}\phi\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{\tau^{\prime}})}\,d\tau^{\prime}

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I_{1},J_{1}\\
I_{2},J_{2}\end{subarray}}\int_{\tau_{0}}^{\tau}\|\partial^{I_{1}}L^{J_{1}}\phi\cdot\partial^{I_{2}}L^{J_{2}}\partial_{t}\phi\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{\tau^{\prime}})}\,d\tau^{\prime}.

From now one we may assume that
|I_{1}|+|J_{1}|\leq|I_{2}|+|J_{2}|
.
We first consider the second integral

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I_{1},J_{1}\\
I_{2},J_{2}\end{subarray}}\int_{\tau_{0}}^{\tau}\|\partial^{I_{1}}L^{J_{1}}\phi\cdot\partial^{I_{2}}L^{J_{2}}\partial_{t}\phi\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{\tau^{\prime}})}\,d\tau^{\prime}

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}\int_{\tau_{0}}^{\tau}\frac{t}{\tau}\|\partial^{I_{1}}L^{J_{2}}\phi\|_{L^{\infty}(\Sigma_{\tau^{\prime}})}\frac{\tau}{t}\|\partial_{t}\partial^{I_{2}}L^{J_{2}}\phi\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{\tau^{\prime}}}\,d\tau^{\prime}

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}\int_{\tau_{0}}^{\tau}\frac{t}{\tau}t^{-\frac{3}{2}}C\varepsilon(\tau^{\prime})^{(|I^{\prime}_{1}|+|J_{1}|+2)\delta}C\varepsilon(\tau^{\prime})^{\frac{1}{2}+(|I_{2}^{\prime}|+|J_{2}|)\delta}\,d\tau^{\prime}

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}C^{2}\varepsilon^{2}\tau^{(N+2)\delta},

and

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I_{1},J_{1}\\
I_{2},J_{2}\end{subarray}}\sum_{j=1}^{3}\int_{\tau_{0}}^{\tau}\|\partial^{I_{1}}L^{J_{1}}\phi\cdot\partial^{I_{2}}L^{J_{2}}\partial_{j}\phi\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{\tau^{\prime}})}\,d\tau^{\prime}

\displaystyle\leq 3C_{1}\int_{\tau_{0}}^{\tau}\|\partial^{I_{1}}L^{J_{1}}\phi\|_{L^{\infty}(\Sigma_{\tau^{\prime}})}\|\partial^{I_{2}}L^{J_{2}}\left(\partial_{j}+\frac{x^{j}}{t}\partial_{t}\right)\phi\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{\tau^{\prime}})}\,d\tau^{\prime}

\displaystyle\qquad+3C_{1}\int_{\tau_{0}}^{\tau}\|\partial^{I_{1}}L^{J_{1}}\phi\|_{L^{\infty}(\Sigma_{\tau^{\prime}})}\|\partial^{I_{2}}L^{J_{2}}\partial_{t}\phi\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{\tau^{\prime}})}\,d\tau^{\prime}.

We already have a suitable bound for the second integral. For the first integral, we use the fact
\|(\partial_{j}+\frac{x^{j}}{t}\partial_{t})\phi\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{\tau})}\leq E[\phi](\tau)^{\frac{1}{2}}
to get

\displaystyle 3C_{1}\int_{\tau_{0}}^{\tau}\|\partial^{I_{1}}L^{J_{1}}\phi\|_{L^{\infty}(\Sigma_{\tau^{\prime}})}\|\partial^{I_{2}}L^{J_{2}}\left(\partial_{j}+\frac{x^{j}}{t}\partial_{t}\right)\phi\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{\tau^{\prime}})}\,d\tau^{\prime}

\displaystyle\leq 3C_{1}\int_{\tau_{0}}^{\tau}t^{-\frac{3}{2}}C\varepsilon\tau^{(|I_{1}^{\prime}|+|J_{1}|+2)\delta}C\varepsilon\tau^{\frac{1}{2}+(|I_{2}^{\prime}|+|J_{2}|)\delta}\,d\tau

\displaystyle\leq 3C_{1}C^{2}\varepsilon^{2}\tau^{(N+2)\delta}.

By choosing
\varepsilon
small enough we obtain better estimates compared to the initial bootstrap assumptions, so that
\tau^{*}=+\infty
.

Abstract

For positive even integers

2.
Background and Notation

Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
are motivated by an array of similar results concerning the zeros of forms on
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
. We begin with the theorem of Rankin and Swinnerton-Dyer, which in this context may be stated as follows. Given a modular form
f(\tau)
of weight
k

\widetilde{P}(f;j(\tau))=\frac{f(\tau)}{\Delta(\tau)^{b(k)}\widetilde{E}_{k}(\tau)}.

Since the
\widetilde{E}_{k}(\tau)
capture exactly the trivial zeros of
f(\tau)
,
\widetilde{P}(f;j(\tau))
is holomorphic on the upper half-plane. By dividing by the appropriate power of
\Delta(\tau)
, we have a weakly holomorphic modular function (i.e. its poles are supported at the cusp at
\infty
). Because the
j
-function is a bijection from
\mathcal{F}
to
\mathbb{C}
, it follows that
\widetilde{P}(f;j(\tau))
is a polynomial in
j(\tau)
. After noting that
j(i)=1728
and
j(\omega)=0
, and then setting

h_{k}(x):=\begin{cases}1\ \ \ \ \ &{\text{\rm if}}\ k\equiv 0\pmod{12},\\
x^{2}(x-1728)\ \ \ \ \ &{\text{\rm if}}\ k\equiv 2\pmod{12},\\
x\ \ \ \ \ &{\text{\rm if}}\ k\equiv 4\pmod{12},\\
x-1728\ \ \ \ \ &{\text{\rm if}}\ k\equiv 6\pmod{12},\\
x^{2}\ \ \ \ \ &{\text{\rm if}}\ k\equiv 8\pmod{12},\\
x(x-1728)\ \ \ \ \ &{\text{\rm if}}\ k\equiv 10\pmod{12},\end{cases}

we obtain the
divisor polynomial
of
f(\tau)
by

P(f;x)=h_{k}(x)\cdot\widetilde{P}(f;x).

Since

[

The same phenomenon was discovered by Asai, Kaneko, and Ninomiya
[

in connection with the Hecke operators

acting on the modular

-function. To describe their result, we recall an important sequence of modular functions. Let
j_{0}(\tau):=1
, and for every positive integer
n
, and let
j_{n}(\tau)
be the unique modular function
which is holomorphic on
\mathbb{H}
whose

j_{n}(\tau)=q^{-n}+\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}c_{n}(l)q^{l}.

Each

is a monic degree

for

. We refer to these expressions as “Hecke polynomials”, as they satisfy

J_{n}(j(\tau))=j_{n}(\tau)=n(j_{1}(\tau)\ |\ T_{0}(n)).

Asai, Kaneko, and Ninomiya proved that the zeros of

Recently, in further pursuit of this phenomenon, Ono and the author
[

proved an analogous result for the sequence of Hecke polynomials arising from the special weight

As seen from their construction (see

is the

In the case of

Following from the work Bruinier and Funke (see Proposition 3.2 of

For the case of

\xi_{-10}(R(\tau))=-11\beta_{\Delta}\cdot\Delta(\tau)

\xi_{2-k}(F(\tau)\ |\ T_{2-k}(n))=\xi_{2-k}(F(\tau))\ |\ T_{k}(n),

Ono and the author are able to construct a weight zero form with vanishing nonholomorphic part arising from

For all

[0,1728]

The case of

extends readily to all

3.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

We now have the necessary background to prove Theorem
1.1
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Since
F
is a weak Hecke eigenform, we have that, for each
n\geq 2
,
G=\xi_{2-k}(F)
is an eigenform of
T_{k}(n)
with eigenvalue
a(n)
. Via (
2.2
), we find that

n^{k-1}\xi_{2-k}(F(\tau)\ |\ T_{2-k}(n))=\xi_{2-k}(F(\tau))\ |\ T_{k}(n)=a(n)G(\tau)=a(n)\xi_{2-k}(F(\tau)).

Thus,
F^{-}(\tau)
is an eigenform of
T_{2-k}(n)
with eigenvalue
n^{1-k}a(n)
. This implies that

n^{k-1}F^{-}(\tau)\ |\ T_{2-k}(n)-a(n)F^{-}(\tau)=0.

Therefore, we have that

n^{k-1}F(\tau)\ |\ T_{2-k}(n)-a(n)F(\tau)=n^{k-1}F^{+}(\tau)\ |\ T_{2-k}(n)-a(n)F^{+}(\tau)

is a weakly holomorphic modular form on
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
. We multiply by sufficient powers of
\Delta(\tau)
and the form
\widetilde{E}_{k-2}(\tau)
to obtain

H_{n}(F;\tau)=\Delta(\tau)^{b(k-2)}\widetilde{E}_{k-2}(\tau)\cdot\left(n^{k-1}F^{+}(\tau)\ |\ T_{2-k}(n)-a(n)F^{+}(\tau)\right),

which is a weakly holomorphic modular function since
F(\tau)
is of weight
2-k
. This proves claim (1).

To show (2), we expand
n^{k-1}F(\tau)\ |\ T_{2-k}(n)
(see e.g. Proposition 2.1 of
[
12
]
) as

n^{k-1}F(\tau)\ |\ T_{2-k}(n)=n^{k-1}\sum_{n^{\prime}\leq 0}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\mid(n,n^{\prime})\\
d>0\end{subarray}}d^{1-k}c_{F}\left(\frac{nn^{\prime}}{d^{2}}\right)q^{n^{\prime}}+O(q).

Since
c_{F}(nn^{\prime}/d^{2})=0
whenever
nn^{\prime}/d^{2}<-m
, the principal part of
H_{n}(F;\tau)
only contains terms for which
nn^{\prime}\geq-md^{2}
. In particular, since
d\leq n
, we find that

n^{k-1}F(\tau)\ |\ T_{2-k}(n)=q^{-mn}+n^{k-1}\sum_{-mn<n^{\prime}\leq 0}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\mid(n,n^{\prime})\\
d>0\end{subarray}}d^{1-k}c_{F}\left(\frac{nn^{\prime}}{d^{2}}\right)q^{n^{\prime}}+O(q).

As
F(\tau)=q^{-m}+O(q^{-m+1})
, we see that
n^{k-1}F(\tau)\ |\ T_{2-k}(n)=q^{-mn}+O(q^{-mn+1})
. Multiplying by
\widetilde{E}_{k-2}(\tau)=1+O(q)
and
\Delta^{b(k-2)}(\tau)=q^{b(k-2)}+O(q^{b(k-2)+1})
, claim (1) grants that
H_{n}(F;\tau)
is a monic polynomial in
j(\tau)
of degree
mn-b(k-2)
, proving (2).
∎

Lemma 4.1

(Theorem 6.10 of
[

]

Assuming the hypotheses above, the following are true.

(1) We have

\xi_{2-k}(\mathcal{F}_{2-k,-l})=(-1)^{k+1}\frac{(4\pi l)^{k-1}}{\Gamma(k-1)}\mathcal{P}_{k,l}.

(2) We have that

is a weight

, with

\mathcal{F}_{2-k,-l}(\tau)=\mathcal{F}_{2-k,-l}^{+}(\tau)+\mathcal{F}_{2-k,-l}^{-}(\tau),

where

(resp.

(3) The holomorphic part of

Lemma 4.2
.

Assuming the notation and hypotheses above, the following are true.

(1) Every cusp form of weight

is a linear combination of Poincaré series

(2) Every harmonic Maass form of weight

, wherein

\overline{e^{\frac{2-k}{2}i\theta}f(e^{i\theta})}=e^{-\frac{2-k}{2}i\theta}\overline{f(e^{i\theta})}=e^{-\frac{2-k}{2}i\theta}f(-1/e^{i\theta})=e^{\frac{2-k}{2}i\theta}f(e^{i\theta})

by modularity, implying that
e^{\frac{2-k}{2}i\theta}f(e^{i\theta})
is real (see Proposition 2.1 of
[
11
]
). Since
F
has real Fourier coefficients by assumption, and the eigenvalues of a Hecke eigenform on
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
are also real,
H_{n}^{\ast}(F;\tau)
has only real coefficients, and thus we specifically aim to show

(4.4)

\left|e^{\frac{2-k}{2}i\theta}e^{-2\pi mn\sin\theta}H_{n}^{\ast}(F;e^{i\theta})-f_{mn}(\theta)\right|<2

for sufficiently large
n
, where, for all
l\geq 1
,

f_{l}(\theta):=2\left(1-e^{-4\pi l\sin\theta}e_{k-2}(4\pi l\sin\theta)\right)\cos\left(\frac{k-2}{2}\theta+2\pi l\cos\theta\right)

and

e_{j}(x):=\sum_{n=0}^{j}\frac{x^{n}}{n!}

is the
j
th order Taylor approximation of
e^{x}
.

To accomplish this, we first isolate the main term in (
4.3
) and write

(4.5)

H_{n}^{\ast}(F;e^{i\theta})=\mathcal{F}_{2-k,-mn}(e^{i\theta})+R_{n}(F;\theta),

where

R_{n}(F;\theta):=\sum_{l=1}^{m}c_{F}^{+}(-l)\left(-a(n)\mathcal{F}_{2-k,-l}(e^{i\theta})+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\mid(l,n)\\
(d,l)\neq(1,m)\end{subarray}}d^{k-1}\mathcal{F}_{2-k,-\frac{ln}{d^{2}}}(e^{i\theta})\right).

To analyze the contributions from each series, we employ (
4.2
) to write

\mathcal{F}_{2-k,-l}(e^{i\theta})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}c\geq 0,d\in\mathbb{Z}\\
(c,d)=1\end{subarray}}(ce^{i\theta}+d)^{k-2}\phi_{-l}\left(\frac{ae^{i\theta}+b}{ce^{i\theta}+d}\right),

where
a,b\in\mathbb{Z}
is an arbitrary solution to
ad-bc=1
. We extract the main terms corresponding to
(c,d)=(0,1)
and
(1,0)
, obtaining

(4.6)

\mathcal{F}_{2-k,-l}(e^{i\theta})=\phi_{-l}(e^{i\theta})+e^{\frac{2-k}{2}i\theta}\phi_{-l}(-e^{-i\theta})+Q_{2-k,-l}(\theta),

where

Q_{2-k,-l}(\theta):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}c\geq 1,d\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}\\
(c,d)=1\end{subarray}}(ce^{i\theta}+d)^{k-2}\phi_{-l}\left(\frac{ae^{i\theta}+b}{ce^{i\theta}+d}\right).

We rewrite the main terms in (
4.6
) using the definition of
\phi_{-l}
, which yields

(4.7)

\phi_{-l}(e^{i\theta})+e^{\frac{2-k}{2}i\theta}\phi_{-l}(-e^{-i\theta})=\frac{(4\pi l\sin\theta)^{\frac{k-2}{2}}}{\Gamma(k)}M_{\frac{k-2}{2},\frac{k-1}{2}}(4\pi l\sin\theta)\left(e^{2\pi il\cos\theta}+e^{\frac{2-k}{2}i\theta}e^{-2\pi il\cos\theta}\right).

By (13.18.4) and (8.4.7) of
[
13
]
, we have, for all
\kappa\in\mathbb{N}
and
x\in\mathbb{R}
, that

(4.8)

M_{\kappa,\kappa+\frac{1}{2}}(x)=(2\kappa+1)!\cdot\frac{e^{\frac{x}{2}}-e^{-\frac{x}{2}}e_{2\kappa}(x)}{x^{\kappa}}.

We multiply (
4.7
) by
e^{\frac{2-k}{2}i\theta}e^{-2\pi l\sin\theta}
and apply (
4.8
); this simplifies to
f_{l}(\theta)
. We thus have that

(4.9)

\left|e^{\frac{2-k}{2}i\theta}e^{-2\pi mn\sin\theta}H_{n}^{\ast}(F;e^{i\theta})-f_{mn}(\theta)\right|=e^{-2\pi m\sin\theta}\left|Q_{2-k,-mn}(\theta)+R_{n}(F;\theta)\right|.

From here, we require a number of lemmas to establish (
4.4
). We first bound the
Q_{2-k,-l}(\theta)
term.

Lemma 4.4
.

If
l\geq 1
and
\theta\in[\frac{\pi}{3},\frac{\pi}{2}]
, then we have

\left|Q_{2-k,-l}(\theta)\right|\leq e^{2\pi l\sin\theta}+8le^{\pi l\sin\theta}+1.008\times 10^{8}l^{k-1}.

Proof.

By the triangle inequality, we have

\left|Q_{2-k,-l}(\theta)\right|\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}c\geq 1,d\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}\\
(c,d)=1\end{subarray}}\left|ce^{i\theta}+d\right|^{k-2}\left|\phi_{-l}\left(\frac{ae^{i\theta}+b}{ce^{i\theta}+d}\right)\right|.

Substituting the definition of
\phi_{-l}
yields

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}c\geq 1,d\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}\\
(c,d)=1\end{subarray}}\left|ce^{i\theta}+d\right|^{k-2}\left|\phi_{-l}\left(\frac{ae^{i\theta}+b}{ce^{i\theta}+d}\right)\right|=\frac{(4\pi l\sin\theta)^{\frac{k-2}{2}}}{\Gamma(k)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}c\geq 1,d\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}\\
(c,d)=1\end{subarray}}M_{\frac{k-2}{2},\frac{k-1}{2}}\left(\frac{4\pi l\sin\theta}{\left|ce^{i\theta}+d\right|^{2}}\right).

We have that
M_{\frac{k-2}{2},\frac{k-1}{2}}
is monotonically increasing on the real line. Furthermore,
\left|ce^{i\theta}+d\right|^{2}=c^{2}+2cd\cos\theta+d^{2}\leq c^{2}+cd+d^{2}
for
\theta\in[\frac{\pi}{3},\frac{\pi}{2}]
, whereby

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}c\geq 1,d\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}\\
(c,d)=1\end{subarray}}M_{\frac{k-2}{2},\frac{k-1}{2}}\left(\frac{4\pi l\sin\theta}{\left|ce^{i\theta}+d\right|^{2}}\right)\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}c\geq 1,d\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}\\
(c,d)=1\end{subarray}}M_{\frac{k-2}{2},\frac{k-1}{2}}\left(\frac{4\pi l\sin\theta}{c^{2}+cd+d^{2}}\right).

We separate the right-hand sum into two parts. If
l\leq c^{2}+cd+d^{2}
, then

\frac{4\pi l\sin\theta}{c^{2}+cd+d^{2}}\leq 4\pi\sin\theta\leq 4\pi,

so Lemma 2.5 of
[
12
]
gives

M_{\frac{k-2}{2},\frac{k-1}{2}}\left(\frac{4\pi l\sin\theta}{c^{2}+cd+d^{2}}\right)\leq e^{2\pi}(4\pi l\sin\theta)^{\frac{k}{2}}\frac{1}{(c^{2}+cd+d^{2})^{\frac{k}{2}}}.

Otherwise, by (
4.8
), we have
M_{\frac{k-2}{2},\frac{k-1}{2}}(x)\leq(k-1)!\cdot x^{\frac{2-k}{2}}e^{\frac{x}{2}}
. We first have the case of
(c,d)=(1,-1)
, which contributes the term

(4.10)

\frac{(4\pi l\sin\theta)^{\frac{k-2}{2}}}{\Gamma(k)}M_{\frac{k-2}{2},\frac{k-1}{2}}(4\pi l\sin\theta)\leq e^{2\pi l\sin\theta}.

For all other pairs
(c,d)
, we have that
c^{2}+cd+d^{2}\geq 2
, so

\frac{(4\pi l\sin\theta)^{\frac{k-2}{2}}}{\Gamma(k)}M_{\frac{k-2}{2},\frac{k-1}{2}}\left(\frac{4\pi l\sin\theta}{c^{2}+cd+d^{2}}\right)\leq e^{\pi l\sin\theta}.

Therefore, we conclude that

\left|Q_{2-k,-l}(\theta)\right|\leq e^{2\pi l\sin\theta}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}c\geq 1,d\in\mathbb{Z}\\
1<c^{2}+cd+d^{2}<l\end{subarray}}e^{\pi l\sin\theta}+\frac{e^{2\pi}(4\pi l\sin\theta)^{k-1}}{\Gamma(k)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}c\geq 1,d\in\mathbb{Z}\\
c^{2}+cd+d^{2}\geq l\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{(c^{2}+cd+d^{2})^{\frac{k}{2}}}.

Those pairs
(c,d)
with
1<c^{2}+cd+d^{2}<l
satisfy
\max\{\left|c\right|,\left|d\right|\}<\sqrt{3l}
, so there are at most
(\sqrt{3l}+1)^{2}\leq 8l
terms in the left-hand sum. Meanwhile,
k\geq 12
since
F
is a weak Hecke eigenform, and thus the right-hand sum is bounded above by the Epstein zeta value

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(c,d)\in\mathbb{Z}\\
(c,d)\neq(0,0)\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{(c^{2}+cd+d^{2})^{\frac{k}{2}}}\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(c,d)\in\mathbb{Z}\\
(c,d)\neq(0,0)\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{(c^{2}+cd+d^{2})^{6}}\leq 6.0099.

Putting this all together, we find that

\left|Q_{2-k,-l}(\theta)\right|\leq e^{2\pi l\sin\theta}+8le^{\pi l\sin\theta}+\frac{3219(4\pi l)^{k-1}}{\Gamma(k)}.

Then, by Stirling’s approximation, we find that

\frac{(4\pi)^{k-1}}{\Gamma(k)}\leq 31294

for all even
k\geq 12
. Thus,

\left|Q_{2-k,-l}(\theta)\right|\leq e^{2\pi l\sin\theta}+8le^{\pi l\sin\theta}+1.008\times 10^{8}l^{k-1}

and the lemma is proved.
∎

We then describe the contribution of the main term in (
4.5
).

Lemma 4.5
.

For all
l\geq 1
and
\theta\in[\frac{\pi}{3},\frac{\pi}{2}]
, we have

\left|\mathcal{F}_{2-k,-l}(e^{i\theta})\right|\leq 4e^{2\pi l\sin\theta}+1.008\times 10^{8}l^{k-1}.

Proof.

We first have, via (
4.6
) and the triangle inequality,

\left|\mathcal{F}_{2-k,-l}(e^{i\theta})\right|\leq\left|\phi_{-l}(e^{i\theta})\right|+\left|\phi_{-l}(-e^{i\theta})\right|+\left|Q_{2-k,-l}(\theta)\right|.

The definition of
\phi_{-l}
gives

\left|\mathcal{F}_{2-k,-l}(e^{i\theta})\right|\leq\frac{2(4\pi l\sin\theta)^{\frac{k-2}{2}}}{\Gamma(k)}M_{\frac{k-2}{2},\frac{k-1}{2}}(4\pi l\sin\theta)+\left|Q_{2-k,-l}(\theta)\right|.

Following (
4.10
) and applying Lemma
4.4
, we find that

\left|\mathcal{F}_{2-k,-l}(e^{i\theta})\right|\leq 3e^{2\pi l\sin\theta}+8le^{\pi l\sin\theta}+1.008\times 10^{8}l^{k-1}.

We then observe that
8le^{\pi l\sin\theta}\leq e^{2\pi l\sin\theta}
for all
l\geq 1
, wherein we obtain our desired result.
∎

Finally, we bound the remaining terms in (
4.5
).

Lemma 4.6
.

For all
n\geq 2
and
\theta\in[\frac{\pi}{3},\frac{\pi}{2}]
, we have

\left|R_{n}(F;\theta)\right|\leq\left(8m^{k-2}n^{\frac{k}{2}}e^{\pi(2m-1)n\sin\theta}+2.016\times 10^{8}(mn)^{k-1}\right)\sum_{l=1}^{m}\left|c_{F}^{+}(-l)\right|

Proof.

We have, by the triangle inequality,

\left|R_{n}(F;\theta)\right|\leq\sum_{l=1}^{m}\left|c_{F}^{+}(-l)\right|\left(\left|a(n)\mathcal{F}_{2-k,-l}(e^{i\theta})\right|+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\mid(l,n)\\
(d,l)\neq(1,m)\end{subarray}}d^{k-1}\left|\mathcal{F}_{2-k,-\frac{ln}{d^{2}}}(e^{i\theta})\right|\right).

First, Lemma
4.5
grants

(4.11)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\mid(l,n)\\
(d,l)\neq(1,m)\end{subarray}}d^{k-1}\left|\mathcal{F}_{2-k,-\frac{ln}{d^{2}}}(e^{i\theta})\right|\leq 4\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\mid(l,n)\\
(d,l)\neq(1,m)\end{subarray}}d^{k-3}e^{2\pi\frac{ln}{d^{2}}\sin\theta}+1.008\times 10^{8}(ln)^{k-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\mid(l,n)\\
(d,l)\neq(1,m)\end{subarray}}d^{1-k}.

We analyze the righthand sums in two cases. For
l=m
, we have

4\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\mid(m,n)\\
d>1\end{subarray}}d^{k-3}e^{2\pi\frac{mn}{d^{2}}\sin\theta}\leq 4\sigma_{0}(m)m^{k-3}e^{\pi\frac{mn}{2}\sin\theta}\leq 4\sigma_{0}(m)m^{k-3}e^{\pi(2m-1)n\sin\theta}

and

1.008\times 10^{8}(mn)^{k-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\mid(m,n)\\
d>1\end{subarray}}d^{1-k}\leq 1.008\times 10^{8}\sigma_{0}(m)(mn)^{k-1}.

Otherwise,
d\leq l\leq m-\frac{1}{2}
and we likewise obtain

4\sum_{d\mid(l,n)}d^{k-3}e^{2\pi\frac{ln}{d^{2}}\sin\theta}\leq 4\sigma_{0}(m)m^{k-3}e^{\pi(2m-1)n\sin\theta}.

and

1.008\times 10^{8}(ln)^{k-1}\sum_{d\mid(l,n)}d^{1-k}\leq 1.008\times 10^{8}\sigma_{0}(m)(mn)^{k-1}.

Plugging these bounds into (
4.11
), we have in both cases

(4.12)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\mid(l,n)\\
(d,l)\neq(1,m)\end{subarray}}d^{k-1}\left|\mathcal{F}_{2-k,-\frac{ln}{d^{2}}}(e^{i\theta})\right|\leq 4\sigma_{0}(m)m^{k-3}e^{\pi(2m-1)n\sin\theta}+1.008\times 10^{8}(mn)^{k-1}\sigma_{0}(m).

Second, Lemma
4.5
and the Deligne bound for coefficients of holomorphic cusp forms
[
10
]
yield

\left|a(n)\mathcal{F}_{2-k,-l}(e^{i\theta})\right|\leq 4\sigma_{0}(n)n^{\frac{k-1}{2}}e^{2\pi l\sin\theta}+1.008\times 10^{8}\sigma_{0}(n)l^{k-1}n^{\frac{k-1}{2}}.

Since
l\leq m
and
\sigma_{0}(n)\leq 2\sqrt{n}
, we have

\left|a(n)\mathcal{F}_{2-k,-l}(e^{i\theta})\right|\leq 4n^{\frac{k}{2}}e^{2\pi m\sin\theta}+1.008\times 10^{8}m^{k-1}n^{\frac{k}{2}}.

It is clear that
m^{k-1}n^{\frac{k}{2}}\leq(mn)^{k-1}
for all
m,n\geq 2
. We then bound
\sigma_{0}(m)m^{k-3}
and
n^{\frac{k}{2}}
by the common bound of
m^{k-2}n^{\frac{k}{2}}
, which, together with (
4.12
) and the trivial bound
2m\leq(2m-1)n
, grants us

\left|R_{n}(F;\theta)\right|\leq\left(8m^{k-2}n^{\frac{k}{2}}e^{\pi(2m-1)n\sin\theta}+2.016\times 10^{8}(mn)^{k-1}\right)\sum_{l=1}^{m}\left|c_{F}^{+}(-l)\right|.

∎

Altogether, we are now able to show (
4.4
).

Lemma 4.7
.

If
n\geq 7
is such that

C_{F}n^{k-1}e^{-\pi n\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}}<1,

then we have

\left|e^{\frac{2-k}{2}i\theta}e^{-2\pi mn\sin\theta}H_{n}^{\ast}(F;e^{i\theta})-f_{mn}(\theta)\right|<2.

Proof.

By the triangle inequality applied to (
4.9
), we have

\left|e^{\frac{2-k}{2}i\theta}e^{-2\pi mn\sin\theta}H_{n}^{\ast}(F;e^{i\theta})-f_{mn}(\theta)\right|\leq e^{-2\pi mn\sin\theta}\left(\left|Q_{2-k,-mn}(\theta)\right|+\left|R_{n}(F;\theta)\right|\right).

Inserting the bounds from Lemma
4.4
, we obtain

(4.13)

e^{-2\pi mn\sin\theta}\left|Q_{2-k,-mn}(\theta)\right|\leq 1+8mne^{-\pi mn\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}}+1.008\times 10^{8}(mn)^{k-1}e^{-\pi mn\sqrt{3}}.

Meanwhile, Lemma
4.6
yields

(4.14)

e^{-2\pi mn\sin\theta}\left|R_{n}(F;\theta)\right|\leq\left(8m^{k-2}n^{\frac{k}{2}}e^{-\pi n\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}}+2.016\times 10^{8}(mn)^{k-1}e^{-\pi mn\sqrt{3}}\right)\sum_{l=1}^{m}\left|c_{F}^{+}(-l)\right|.

Now let
y=mn
. Since
m^{k-1}n^{\frac{k}{2}}\leq(mn)^{k-1}
, we may reformulate the bound in (
4.14
) as

8y^{k-1}e^{-\pi n\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}}+2.016\times 10^{8}y^{k-1}e^{-\pi y\sqrt{3}}\leq 8y^{k-1}e^{-\pi n\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}}+2.016\times 10^{8}y^{k-1}e^{-\pi y\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}}e^{-\pi n\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}}.

For
y\geq 7
, we have

(4.15)

2.016\times 10^{8}e^{-\pi y\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}}\leq 2,

whereby

8y^{k-1}e^{-\pi n\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}}+2.016\times 10^{8}y^{k-1}e^{-\pi y\sqrt{3}}\leq 10y^{k-1}e^{-\pi n\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}}.

Similarly, we may rewrite (
4.13
) using (
4.15
) as

1+8ye^{-\pi y\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}}+1.008\times 10^{8}y^{k-1}e^{-\pi y\sqrt{3}}\leq 1+(y^{k-2}+8)ye^{-\pi y\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}}\leq 1+2y^{k-1}e^{-\pi y\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}}.

Our assumption on
n
ensures then that

10y^{k-1}e^{-\pi n\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}}\sum_{l=1}^{m}\left|c_{F}^{+}(-l)\right|=\frac{1}{2}C_{F}n^{k-1}e^{-\pi n\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}}<\frac{1}{2}

and

2y^{k-1}e^{-\pi y\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}}\leq\frac{1}{10C_{F}}n^{k-1}e^{-\pi n\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}}<\frac{1}{10C_{F}}\leq\frac{2}{5}

since we stipulate that
C_{F}\geq\frac{1}{4}
. In all, we obtain

e^{-2\pi mn\sin\theta}\left(\left|Q_{2-k,-mn}(\theta)\right|+\left|R_{n}(F;\theta)\right|\right)<1+\frac{2}{5}+\frac{1}{2}<2

as desired.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Let
g_{mn}(\theta):=\frac{k-2}{2}\theta+2\pi mn\cos\theta
, so that

f_{mn}(\theta)=2(1-e^{-4\pi mn\sin\theta}e_{k-2}(4\pi mn\sin\theta))\cos(g_{mn}(\theta)).

The zeros and extrema of
f_{mn}(\theta)
are controlled by
g_{mn}(\theta)
. One readily verifies by computation that

1-e^{-4\pi mn\sin\theta}e_{k-2}(4\pi mn\sin\theta)\geq 0.99,

for all
k\geq 4
, as
e_{k-2}(x)
is monotonic in
x
and
k
. Thus,
\left|f_{mn}(\theta)\right|\geq 1.98
whenever
g_{mn}(\theta)
is an integer multiple of
\pi
.

We then observe that
g_{mn}(\theta)
decreases from
\frac{(k-2)\pi}{6}+\pi mn
to
\frac{(k-2)\pi}{4}
on
[\frac{\pi}{3},\frac{\pi}{2}]
, hitting
mn+\lfloor(k-2)/6\rfloor-\lceil(k-2)/4\rceil
consecutive integer multiples of
\pi
. Thus, Lemma
4.7
implies that
H_{n}^{\ast}(F;e^{i\theta})
changes sign at least this many times in
[\frac{\pi}{3},\frac{\pi}{2}]
, once in each subinterval of the form

(g_{mn}^{-1}(\pi(i+1)),g_{mn}^{-1}(\pi i))

for
\lceil(k-2)/4\rceil\leq i\leq mn+\lfloor(k-2)/6\rfloor
. Therefore, by the Intermediate Value Theorem,
H_{n}^{\ast}(F;e^{i\theta})
has a zero in each subinterval, and each of these zeros is a zero of
H_{n}(F;e^{i\theta})
.

The remaining zeros of
H_{n}(F;e^{i\theta})
arise from the factor of
\widetilde{E}_{k-2}(e^{i\theta})
. Indeed, we have

\lfloor(k-2)/6\rfloor-\lceil(k-2)/4\rceil=\begin{cases}-2-b(k-2)&\text{if }k\equiv 0,4\pmod{12},\\
-1-b(k-2)&\text{if }k\equiv 6,8,10\pmod{12},\\
-b(k-2)&\text{if }k\equiv 2\pmod{12}.\end{cases}

Likewise, letting
Z_{k-2}
denote the zeros of
\tilde{E}_{k-2}(e^{i\theta})
contained in
\mathcal{A}
, we have

Z_{k-2}=\begin{cases}\{\frac{\pi}{3},\frac{\pi}{2}\}&\text{if }k\equiv 0,4\pmod{12},\\
\{\frac{\pi}{2}\}&\text{if }k\equiv 8\pmod{12},\\
\{\frac{\pi}{3}\}&\text{if }k\equiv 6,10\pmod{12},\\
\emptyset&\text{if }k\equiv 2\pmod{12}.\end{cases}

In all cases, we see that
mn+\lfloor(k-2)/6\rfloor-\lceil(k-2)/4\rceil+\left|Z_{k-2}\right|=mn-b(k-2)=\deg P_{n}(F;x)
. Since
j\colon\mathcal{A}\to[0,1728]
is a bijection, we have located all
mn-b(k-2)
zeros of
P_{n}(F;x)
, which are simple and in the interval
[0,1728]
. Equidistribution of the zeros as
n\to+\infty
is then apparent, as these intervals essentially describe
\mathcal{A}
as a disjoint union of subarcs of equal size, proving Corollary
1.3
.
∎

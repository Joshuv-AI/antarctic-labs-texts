Complexity of measure-induced maps
Gabriel Lacerda

Abstract
A classical result by Glasner and Weiss states that a map f f has zero topological entropy if and only if its measure-induced map f ∗ f_{*} , defined as the push-forward of a measure, does. In this work, we use generalized entropy to distinguish the complexity of these maps and prove that the measure-induced map is strictly more complex whenever the generalized entropy of the original map is nonzero and is not an abstract order of growth. Moreover, we show that the complexity of the measure-induced map is of at most polynomial type when the nonwandering set of f f is finite. For homeomorphisms of infinite spaces, this bound is attained, so measure-induced maps of Morse–Smale diffeomorphisms cannot be distinguished from one another. Mathematics Subject Classification (2020): 37B40, 37D15, 28A33.

Introduction

In the field of dynamical systems, topological entropy is a classical tool used to evaluate the complexity of a continuous map
f:M\to M
on a compact metric space
M
. Denoted by
h(f)
, topological entropy measures the asymptotic exponential growth rate of the number of distinct orbits at small scales. However, for dynamical systems with sub-exponential growth of these distinct orbits, the topological entropy is zero.

Positive topological entropy is often used to characterize systems that are chaotic or complex. However, there can be distinct maps with zero entropy where one is inherently more chaotic than the other. To illustrate, within the class of Morse–Smale dynamics—a classical example of zero-entropy maps—two distinct maps can have different phase diagrams
[
9
]
. This property implies that, depending on the phase space
M
, the behavior of their orbits can be completely different, and one can be strictly more complex than the other.

To address this situation, Correa and Pujals introduced the concept of generalized entropy
[
3
]
, an approach that considers the growth rate of distinct orbits at all scales. By “growth rate,” we refer to the order of growth of the corresponding sequence, rather than a numerical asymptotic rate. Unlike topological entropy, generalized entropy is not a non-negative number; it is an equivalence class in a partially ordered set. More precisely, considering non-decreasing functions
a:\mathbb{N}\to[1,\infty)
, we define
\mathbb{O}
as the set of equivalence classes of such functions, where two functions are equivalent if they have the same growth rate. Hence, an element in
\mathbb{O}
is denoted by
[a(n)]
. The class corresponding to bounded growth is denoted by
0
, which represents the smallest possible growth. Furthermore, one can complete this partially ordered set to obtain the complete set of orders of growth
\overline{\mathbb{O}}
. This extended set contains abstract orders of growth, which are given by the suprema or infima of subsets of
\mathbb{O}
. Therefore, the generalized entropy of a map
f:M\to M
is denoted by
o(f)
and is an element of
\overline{\mathbb{O}}
. In this sense, generalized entropy provides a broader notion of
complexity
.

This work is concerned with the generalized entropy of the measure-induced map
f_{*}:\mathcal{P}(M)\to\mathcal{P}(M)
, where
\mathcal{P}(M)
is the set of Borel probability measures on
M
and
f_{*}
is the push-forward operator. That is, for
\mu\in\mathcal{P}(M)
,
f_{*}\mu=\mu\circ f^{-1}
. A classical result regarding the complexity of the measure-induced map was established by Glasner and Weiss in
[
5
]
, where they showed that the topological entropy of
f
is zero if and only if the topological entropy of
f_{*}
is zero. Since generalized entropy serves as a tool to distinguish dynamical systems with zero topological entropy, a natural question arises: can it also capture the difference in complexity between the base map
f
and its measure-induced map
f_{*}
?

We answer this question in the affirmative by showing that the measure-induced map is significantly more complex. In fact, we provide a generalization of a well-known result by Bauer and Sigmund
[
1
]
, which states that if the topological entropy of
f
is positive, then the topological entropy of
f_{*}
is infinite. The strict growth of complexity,
o(f_{*})>o(f)
, does not occur for maps with the lowest (
o(f)=\textbf{0}
) and highest complexity (
o(f)=\sup\mathbb{E}
, see §
2.2
). More precisely, whenever
o(f)\neq\textbf{0}
,
o(f_{*})
is an abstract order of growth; that is, it lies strictly in the completion
\overline{\mathbb{O}}\setminus\mathbb{O}
. Moreover, given a one-parameter family of orders of growth (e.g.,
\mathbb{A}=\{[a(n)^{t}]\in\mathbb{O}:t>0\}
for a given
[a(n)]\in\mathbb{O}
), we show that the complexity of
f_{*}
is at least the supremum of this family whenever
o(f_{*})
is bounded below by a base element of
\mathbb{A}
. Our complete first result is the following:

Theorem A
.

Let

be a continuous map on a compact metric space.

1.

if, and only if,
o(f_{*})=\emph{\textbf{0}}

2.

For

, then

.

3.

For
\mathbb{B}=\{[b(n)^{t}]\in\mathbb{O}:t>0\}
, if

, then
\inf\mathbb{B}\geq o(f_{*})
.

4.

In particular, if
o(f)\geq[a(n)]
, then
o(f_{*})\geq\sup\mathbb{A}
. Furthermore, if
o(f)\in\mathbb{O}
is such that

[

is the least element of the partially ordered set
\overline{\mathbb{O}}

The regime of positive topological entropy is related to the exponential one-parameter family of orders of growth
\mathbb{E}=\{[e^{tn}]\in\mathbb{O}:t>0\}

f

\pi_{\mathbb{E}}:\overline{\mathbb{O}}\to[0,\infty]

\pi_{\mathbb{E}}(o(f))=\infty

\mathbb{A}

.

If

h_{\mathbb{A}}(f_{*})\in\{0,\infty\}

When the one-parameter family is the polynomial family
\mathbb{P}
,
h_{\mathbb{P}}(f)
coincides with the polynomial entropy, a tool useful for distinguishing dynamical systems with zero topological entropy, which was revisited by Marco in
[
8

. In other words, whenever the polynomial entropy of
f
is positive, the polynomial entropy of
f_{*}
is infinite. Therefore, generalized entropy is a much broader tool for differentiating maps with vanishing topological entropy.

Conversely, it is natural to ask whether one can establish upper bounds for the generalized entropy of the measure-induced map. Correa and de Paula established in
[
2
]
that the generalized entropy of a homeomorphism
f
with a finite nonwandering set always satisfies
o(f)\leq\sup\mathbb{P}
. The following result recovers this bound, since
o(f_{*})\geq o(f)
, without relying on their technique of coding wandering points. Instead, we use a different kind of coding that discretizes the mass of the iterates of a measure
\mu
under
f_{*}
with respect to a partition of
\mathcal{P}(M)
, and we show that the number of such discretizations is at most polynomial. Recall that the nonwandering set
\Omega(f)
is the set of points
x\in M
for which every neighborhood of
x
intersects its image under some positive iterate of
f
. Ultimately, we show that this polynomial bound can be extended to the measure-induced map
f_{*}
.

Theorem B
.

Let
f:M\to M
be a homeomorphism such that
\Omega(f)
is finite. Then
o(f_{*})\leq\sup\mathbb{P}
.

We can combine the above results to provide sufficient conditions for the measure-induced map of a homeomorphism to attain exactly the supremum of the polynomial one-parameter family. As a consequence, generalized entropy is more effective than polynomial entropy for distinguishing maps with vanishing topological entropy, because the following result is more precise than simply saying that a map has infinite polynomial entropy.

Corollary 3
.

If
f
is a homeomorphism on an infinite compact metric space
M
such that
\Omega(f)
is finite, then
o(f_{*})=\sup\mathbb{P}
.

This holds because a homeomorphism with a finite nonwandering set on an infinite compact metric space has a wandering point
x
, and a wandering point satisfies
x\notin\alpha(x)
. In particular, Corollary 3 applies to Morse–Smale diffeomorphisms of compact manifolds of positive dimension. For such diffeomorphisms on compact surfaces, Correa and de Paula showed that
o(f)=[n^{L}]
, where
L=L(f)
is an integer determined by the graph associated with
f
in
[
2
, Theorem 1]
, and this integer can vary from one diffeomorphism to another. Nevertheless, all of their measure-induced maps satisfy
o(f_{*})=\sup\mathbb{P}
, so
f_{*}
cannot distinguish them.

Returning to the context of homeomorphisms of the circle, we do not know the complexity of the measure-induced map of the Denjoy homeomorphism, because its nonwandering set is infinite. We know that the complexity of the base map is linear, but can the complexity of its measure-induced map be strictly greater than
\sup\mathbb{P}
? Or, more generally:

Question

Does there exist a continuous map
f
such that

, but
o(f_{*})>\sup\mathbb{P}
?

Fundamentals

We first recall the dynamical notions that will be used throughout the paper. We then introduce the probability space and the measure-induced map, and finally recall the generalized entropy and the orders of growth that will be used to measure the complexity of the induced dynamics.

From now on, we denote a compact metric space by

An important set in the study of dynamical systems is the

. Note that for each wandering point

wandering neighborhood

U

; that is, an open set

containing

for all

.

Another important set is the set of periodic points
\operatorname{Per}(f)=\{x\in M:\exists m\in\mathbb{N},f^{m}(x)=x\}
. Note that the set of fixed points
\operatorname{Fix}(f)=\{x\in M:f(x)=x\}
is contained in

, which in turn is contained in the nonwandering set

. Finally, if

2.2
The complete set of orders of growth and generalized entropy

Consider the set of non-decreasing sequences of real numbers assuming values in
[1,\infty)

\mathcal{O}=\{a(n):a(n)\leq a(n+1),\forall n\in\mathbb{N}\}.

On this set, we define the following equivalence relation: Given

\mathbb{O}

C>0

n\in\mathbb{N}

a(n)
and

In this section, we present some results concerning the measure-induced map

Proposition 3.1
.

If

is a continuous map defined on a compact metric space

, then the following holds:

1.

o(f_{*})\geq o(f)
;

2.

If
\Omega(f_{*})=\mathcal{P}(M)
, then
\Omega(f)=M
.

Proof.

We will show each item.

1.

Recall that the map

defined by

to

2.

Consider

3.2
Proof of Theorem

From now on, assume that

is a homeomorphism such that its set of nonwandering points is a finite set of fixed points. That is,
\Omega(f)=\operatorname{Fix}(f)=\{p_{1},\ldots,p_{m}\}
. We make this assumption without loss of generality because a finite nonwandering set consists entirely of periodic points, meaning that under some iterate
f^{n}
, all periodic points become fixed. Moreover, we know by Remark
2.1
that
o((f^{n})_{*})=o((f_{*})^{n})
, and, by Remark
2.2
, that
o(f_{*})\leq o((f_{*})^{n})
. Therefore, showing that

is sufficient.

Let

. If

, set

. Given
\varepsilon>0
, choose
K\in\mathbb{N}
such that
2^{1-K}<\varepsilon/4
. Consider the first
K
functions
\{\varphi_{1},\dots,\varphi_{K}\}
of the dense sequence
(\varphi_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
used to define the metric
\rho
in §
2.1
. Moreover, choose
\eta\in(0,s/2)
such that

(S1)

if

, which is possible by the continuity of

;

(S2)

for any

, if
\text{diam}(A)<\eta
, then
\operatorname{osc}_{A}\varphi_{i}<\varepsilon/8
for all

.

In particular,
\eta+s/2<s
. Set
V_{j}=\{x\in M:d(p_{j},x)<\eta/3\}

is a compact set contained in

.

Remark 3.1
.

If

and
f^{k+1}(x)\in V_{j}
, then

. Indeed,
f^{k}(x)\in V_{i}
gives

, so by (S1) and

we get

. Since also

, we obtain

, which forces

.

Claim 3.2
.

There exists
L\in\mathbb{N}
, depending on

Given

. Since subsets of wandering sets are also wandering, we can shrink

so that

. Since

that cover

. For any

\varepsilon_{0}\mathbb{Z}\cap[-\varepsilon_{0},1+\varepsilon_{0}]

Impact of precipitation patterns on the risk of hand, foot, and mouth disease in Southwest China: insight from a disease burden perspective
Authors: Jun-Yan Xi, Jie Sun, Jing Gu, Yuan-Tao Hao, Xiao Lin
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundPrecipitation is regarded as a pivotal upstream factor in the control of the hand, foot, and mouth disease (HFMD) burden. However, the current evidence on the direction and magnitude of precipitation patterns on HFMD burden remains unclear.MethodsWe conducted a population-based ecological time-series study using daily HFMD cases and meteorological data from 88 counties in Guizhou, China, between 2012 and 2019. Weekly precipitation with the precipitation concentration degree was combined to define 35 distinct precipitation patterns. The HFMD burden was quantified using the years lived with disability (YLD) metric. The distributed lag non-linear models were used to evaluate the impact and risk thresholds of precipitation patterns regarding the attributable HFMD burden.ResultsAmong 524,100 reported HFMD cases, the total YLD was 403.3 person-years. As precipitation intensity increased, the maximum cumulative relative risk (RR) for HFMD burden reached 1.023 (95% confidence interval: 1.000–1.047). We identified three high-risk precipitation patterns: (1) concentrated precipitation below 50 mm, (2) dispersed precipitation below 150 mm, and (3) normal precipitation above 200 mm. The pooled RR and attributable YLD burden of the high-risk precipitation patterns were 1.026 (1.016–1.036) and 20.92 (11.21–30.31) person-years.ConclusionThe complex precipitation patterns characterized by frequency-intensity combinations are linked to higher HFMD burden among children, with pattern-specific risk thresholds identified across these patterns. To reduce this burden, enhanced surveillance and climate-adaptive public health actions are recommended, particularly during periods of concentrated light precipitation, dispersed moderate precipitation, and evenly distributed extreme precipitation.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12889-025-26035-3.Keywords: Burden of disease; Climate; Hand, foot, and mouth disease; Precipitation

Introduction

Hand, foot, and mouth disease (HFMD) causes a high burden of disease worldwide, especially in children who are more susceptible to human enteroviruses and coxsackieviruses infection [1, 2]. HFMD was estimated to cause 96,900 person-years of disability-adjusted life years each year in East and Southeast Asia [3]. Of these, China has the highest number of HFMD cases in the world, particularly in the southwestern province of Guizhou [4–6]. Although most clinical cases of HFMD are typical and self-limiting, serious complications involving the central nervous system and cardiopulmonary system may occur, including death [7–9]. It is of the utmost importance that the severity of HFMD is not overlooked in the assessment of the overall disease burden, especially given that patients with severe HFMD are responsible for a significant level of disability burden. Climate is regarded as a pivotal upstream factor in the control of the HFMD burden, accounting for approximately one-third of the incidence [10].

Previous studies have identified relative humidity and precipitation as important meteorological factors with a significant impact on HFMD [11–13]. Furthermore, precipitation has a more direct effect than relative humidity, which may promote viral attachment to the environment or surfaces, or indirectly alter a child’s behavior to influence an individual’s susceptibility to HFMD [14]. In reality, however, the combination of precipitation frequency and intensity is more complex. There is evidence that dispersed precipitation, as opposed to concentrated precipitation, may be the most suitable habitat for the vector mosquitoes to facilitate dengue transmission [15]. Therefore, the irregular precipitation patterns and inconsistency in precipitation make it challenging to quantify the impact of complex precipitation patterns on the HFMD burden, in comparison to other meteorological factors. In addition, previous studies have overlooked the heterogeneity in the severity of HFMD between individuals. Severe cases may be more sensitive to changes in meteorological factors, and capturing the true burden of disease can correct this bias in estimates of health effects [16]. A significant knowledge gap exists in the comprehension of the direction and strength of the impact of precipitation patterns on the HFMD burden.

Although nationwide standardized control measures have been implemented in Guizhou, the region continues to report a notably high rate of severe HFMD cases [17]. This pattern may be partly attributed to the evolving pathogen spectrum, which has been shaped by the widespread introduction of the monovalent EV-A71 vaccine. In the absence of multivalent vaccines, this vaccination strategy may have facilitated the increasing predominance of non-EV-A71 serotypes, such as CV-A16 and CV-A6 [5, 17]. Furthermore, HFMD transmission is characterized by pronounced seasonality and a high degree of sensitivity to meteorological factors—including temperature, humidity, and precipitation—which may play pivotal roles in the HFMD epidemiology for this region [18, 19]. These intertwined etiological and environmental processes indicate that critical key drivers, especially the influence of complex precipitation patterns, remain insufficiently understood and merit further investigation.

To fill this knowledge gap, the study collected daily HFMD cases and their severity from 88 counties in Guizhou, China, from 2012 to 2019, as well as meteorological data from national meteorological stations for corresponding days. We used the combination of weekly precipitation (PRE) and precipitation concentration degree (PCD) to reflect the complexity of precipitation patterns [15]. The HFMD burden was quantified using the years lived with disability (YLD) metric, which accounts for the severity of cases. Subsequently, we evaluated the impact and risk thresholds of precipitation patterns in terms of the HFMD burden and quantified the attributable HFMD burden.

Methods

Research design and data sources

We conducted a population-based ecological time-series study to assess the association between precipitation patterns and the burden of HFMD. The study utilized aggregated data from all 88 counties in Guizhou, China, from January 1, 2012, to December 31, 2019. To ensure sufficient statistical power and model stability for the time-series analysis, and considering that public health policy is often implemented at this level, daily county-level data were aggregated to the prefecture level for analysis. The province comprises nine prefecture-level regions (comprising six cities: Guiyang City, Zunyi City, Liupanshui City, Anshun City, Bijie City, and Tongren City; and three autonomous prefectures: Qiandongnan Miao and Dong Autonomous Prefecture, Qiannan Buyi and Miao Autonomous Prefecture, and Qianxinan Buyi and Miao Autonomous Prefecture) to represent the entire province.

We obtained the daily number of HFMD cases in children aged 0 ~ 5 years from 88 counties in Guizhou from the Chinese Disease Prevention and Control Information System. Each case contained the necessary details, including age, current county code, and the date when the patient first developed symptoms. Meteorological data were obtained from the China Meteorological Data Sharing Service, including daily precipitation (mm), temperature (°C), wind speed (m/s), sunshine duration (hours), and air pressure (hPa) from 31 national meteorological stations in Guizhou [20]. Each county was assigned data from the most appropriate meteorological station based on geographic coordinates. For counties containing multiple stations, the station closest to the population center was selected. Counties without a meteorological station were assigned data from the nearest adjacent station. (Appendix Figure S1)

Throughout the study period, standardized national and local HFMD prevention and control policies were implemented across Guizhou [21]. In China, HFMD is a Category C notifiable infectious disease. All diagnosed cases must be reported within 24 h to the National Notifiable Diseases Reporting System, which is part of the China Information System for Disease Control and Prevention. This mandate ensures the completeness and timeliness of our surveillance data. Core intervention strategies included health education to promote personal hygiene, environmental disinfection, and strengthened surveillance in high-risk settings such as kindergartens and primary schools. The consistent application of these measures provides a stable contextual background for our analysis.

Analytic strategy

First, we conducted distributed lag non-linear models (DLNM) to estimate the exposure-lag response associations between PRE and YLD for each prefecture-level region to determine the lagged effects of precipitation on HFMD. Each DLNM used the Akaike information criterion (AIC) and Bayesian information criterion (BIC) to determine the optimal combination of relative risk (RR) lag weeks and degrees of freedom (df) of the natural cubic spline function [24–27]. Random-effects multivariate meta-regression fitted by restricted maximum likelihood was used to pool the PRE-YLD associations from these nine prefecture-level regions into the overall association for the entire province. This meta-analytic approach accounts for heterogeneity between prefectures, providing a more robust provincial summary than a simple analysis of pooled data.

Sensitivity analysis

To assess the robustness of our primary findings, we conducted two sensitivity analyses. First, a temporal validation was conducted by partitioning the entire study period into two equal sub-periods (2012–2015 and 2016–2019) and independently repeating the full analytic process for each. Second, potential confounding by meteorological factors was assessed by sequentially excluding each variable (temperature, wind speed, sunshine duration, and air pressure) from the full model and refitting it with the remaining three.

Results

Summary statistics for HFMD burden and precipitation patterns

From January 2012 to December 2019, there were 524,100 cases of HFMD in Guizhou, with a total YLD of 403.3 person-years. Among them, Guiyang and Zunyi had the heaviest HFMD burden, and the sum of cases and YLD reached 1.56 times and 1.54 times the sum of corresponding values in the other 6 prefecture-level cities (Appendix Table S2). Relationship between YLD of HFMD and precipitation intensity

Figure 2 shows the non-linear relationship between YLD estimates and precipitation for the pooled provincial estimates. The highest relative risk lag week for the effect of precipitation on YLD was 3 weeks in all 9 prefecture-level regions (Appendix Figure S3). In general, with 15.5 mm as the reference, the effect on YLD increased significantly with increasing precipitation, reaching the first effect peak at 103.8 mm (RR = 1.021, 95% CI: 1.013 ~ 1.029). The risk then decreased to reach the effect trough at 165.2 mm (RR = 1.018, 95% CI: 1.003 ~ 1.033) and continued to increase, reaching a peak at 205.6 mm (RR = 1.023, 95% CI: 1.000 ~ 1.047).

Thresholds of precipitation frequency and intensity with high risk of HFMD burden

The effects of PRE and PCD on YLD in 9 prefecture-level regions show different bivariate response relationships. DLNM of prefecture-level regions revealed spatial heterogeneity in the risk distribution for each PRE and PCD combination at the highest relative risk lag week (Fig. 3A). A statistically significant combination of PRE and PCD in prefecture-level regions constituted a provincial risk threshold for high HFMD burden. Overall, the risk threshold consists of three risk thresholds: (1) concentrated precipitation below 50 mm, (2) dispersed precipitation below 150 mm, and (3) normal precipitation above 200 mm (Fig. 3B).

Relative risk and attributable burden of precipitation patterns associated with a high risk of HFMD

Figure 4 shows the RR estimates associated with HFMD burden in high-risk precipitation patterns, estimated separately for 9 prefecture-level regions and pooled to the overall provincial RR. The RR and attributable burden for the whole province are 1.026 (95% CI: 1.016 ~ 1.036) and 20.92 person-years (95% CI: 11.21 person-years ~ 30.31 person-years) (Appendix Table S3). Specifically, the RR of the 9 prefecture-level regions are statistically significant, and the highest RR and attributable burden are in Guiyang (RR = 1.108, 95% CI: 1.063 ~ 1.155; Attributable YLD = 14.64 person-years, 95% CI: 5.95 person-years ~ 13.45 person-years) (Appendix Table S3).

Sensitivity analysis

The robustness of the pooled provincial RR (1.026, 95% CI: 1.016 ~ 1.036) was further assessed. The temporal validation, which partitioned the data into two sub-periods, yielded consistently significant RR estimates of 1.009 (95% CI: 1.000 ~ 1.017) for 2012–2015 and 1.016 (95% CI: 1.007 ~ 1.025) for 2016–2019. Furthermore, after sequentially excluding each meteorological variable (temperature, wind speed, sunshine duration, and air pressure) from the full model, the effect estimates for the high-risk precipitation patterns remain comparable to the original provincial pooled RR, with the adjusted RRs reaching 1.026 (95% CI: 1.015 ~ 1.036), 1.025 (95% CI: 1.016 ~ 1.035), 1.026 (95% CI: 1.016 ~ 1.036), and 1.025 (95% CI: 1.016 ~ 1.035), respectively. All analyses indicate that the findings are robust to the study period and are not substantially influenced by any single confounder. (Appendix Table S4)

Discussion

HFMD caused by different enteroviruses shows different clinical characteristics of severity, and severe HFMD can threaten the quality of life of children under 5 years of age [28, 29]. This study systematically assessed the impact of precipitation frequency and intensity on the HFMD burden in children under five years of age and identified risk precipitation patterns associated with higher childhood HFMD burden. We identified three high-risk precipitation patterns based on the specific PRE and PCD thresholds, namely, concentrated precipitation below 50 mm, dispersed precipitation below 150 mm, and normal precipitation above 200 mm. Exposure to these patterns was associated with a 2.6% higher risk of HFMD at the provincial level, which could result in an excess burden of 20.92 person-years of YLD. As global climate change continues, our findings strengthen the understanding of the significant role that specific precipitation patterns may play in increasing the HFMD burden in children. Together with the metrics of YLD, our findings provide a scientific basis for governments to take timely action for early warning and control of HFMD.

HFMD burden grows with the increase in precipitation intensity

Our results on the association between PRE and HFMD burden are consistent with the findings of most previous case-based studies, which show that the exposure-response curve of HFMD risk rises with increasing PRE [30, 31]. From an aetiological perspective, aerosol transmission of HFMD pathogens is dependent on environmental relative humidity and precipitation [32]. There is evidence that the activity of non-enveloped enteroviruses decreases rapidly in dry environments [33]. In addition, the subtropical climate of Guizhou is more conducive to the survival of HFMD pathogens than in temperate regions [32]. From a population exposure perspective, precipitation increases the likelihood of children being exposed to HFMD pathogens. Studies have shown that outbreaks of HFMD in children overlap with the schooling period, and the time series of HFMD burden we have mapped confirms this [33]. High population density of children, concentrated activity and play, and lack of self-protective measures are all potential reasons for the impact of precipitation on the HFMD burden in children [13, 14]. Precipitation may also interact with average temperature or other environmental factors to synergistically increase the HFMD burden in children [34]. Our analysis shows that after controlling for several potential environmental confounders, precipitation still has a significant non-linear positive association with childhood HFMD burden.

HFMD surveillance needs to focus on concentrated light precipitation, dispersed moderate precipitation, and evenly distributed extreme precipitation

The PCD can only be calculated when precipitation occurs, and it comprehensively describes the two-dimensional characteristics of precipitation with PRE. We found that concentrated light precipitation and dispersed moderate precipitation increased the risk of HFMD in children. Concentrated light precipitation can increase relative humidity rapidly over a short period and is unlikely to affect children’s daily activities [35]. It is therefore easy to explain the higher HFMD burden in this situation. Although precipitation can enhance the deposition of airborne particles and inhibit the adsorption of viruses on fine particles, the duration of this effect is only tens of hours [36, 37]. We found that the effect of dispersed moderate precipitation on the risk of HFMD in children by improving air quality is limited and may instead provide favorable conditions for aerosol transmission of HFMD pathogens. Meanwhile, extreme precipitation is a higher factor in the HFMD burden in children [14]. We estimated the risk threshold for a moderate concentration of extreme precipitation. Dispersed or concentrated extreme precipitation can have both side effects on the transmission of HFMD. On the one hand, extreme precipitation may move pathogens from multiple sources into surface water, thereby increasing transmission, or it may reduce transmission by reshaping contaminated reservoirs into water currents [38]. On the other hand, children whose activities are restricted by extreme precipitation may spread the pathogen of HFMD to more household members, but may also interrupt transmission by reducing the child’s social contact. As extreme precipitation of more than 200 mm is rare in Guizhou, the effects of different concentrations of extreme precipitation on HFMD can be captured and verified in areas with frequent heavy precipitation in the future.

Advantages and limitations

This study has the following advantages. First, to our knowledge, this study is the first comprehensive assessment to quantify the impact of complex precipitation conditions on HFMD risk from a disease burden perspective. The burden of disease calculated by taking into account the severity of HFMD cases more accurately reflects the health impact of HFMD on children when compared with those only reporting the number of incident cases [3, 39]. Second, different from previous studies that only focused on the influence of precipitation on HFMD in children, we used two parameters reflecting the frequency and intensity of precipitation to quantify the complex and variable distribution of precipitation.

Some limitations of this study should be acknowledged. First, as a population-based ecological analysis, our findings cannot establish causality at the individual level. Moreover, the analysis was conducted at the prefecture-level, which, while necessary to ensure model stability and aligned with public health intervention levels, may obscure smaller-scale heterogeneity in the exposure-response relationship within prefectures. Notwithstanding these limitations, confidence in our results is strengthened by the large sample size (> 500,000 cases), the fine spatial resolution of the source data, a long-term time series, and consistent findings from multiple sensitivity analyses. Second, although unmeasured confounders (e.g., school calendars, viral strain changes, and local sanitation conditions) may have influenced the associations, the potential bias was mitigated with adjustment for seasonality, long-term trends, and socioeconomic factors within the model. Finally, the findings may be most generalizable to regions with similar climatic and socioeconomic profiles to those of Guizhou. Nevertheless, the analytical framework and statistical methods are broadly applicable and may inform future public-health researchers and guide health authorities and other stakeholders in investigating environmental determinants to alleviate HFMD burden and even mitigate other climate-sensitive infectious diseases.

Conclusion

This study indicates that complex frequency-intensity precipitation patterns are indeed associated with a higher risk of HFMD burden among children, particularly when precipitation patterns exceed the three risk thresholds identified. With ongoing climate change, future precipitation patterns worldwide will become more diverse and less predictable, complicating HFMD burden and posing continuing challenges for climate mitigation and adaptation efforts. Our study provides a key reference for tailoring integrated meteorological public-health early-warning systems that enable timely alerts and targeted interventions. These findings also underscore the importance of implementing climate-adaptive public-health strategies, i.e., optimizing resource allocation for medical supplies, initiating community-based hygiene campaigns during high-risk precipitation periods, and strengthening multi-sectoral emergency coordination, so as to mitigate the burden of HFMD among children.

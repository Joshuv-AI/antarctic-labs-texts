Silicoanthracosis: A melanoma mimicker
Authors: Sajanakan Sriselvakumar, Marion Hanley, Gurjeet Dulku
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Silicoanthracosis is a type of fibrotic lung disease resulting from prolonged inhalation of silica particles. The silica particles can trigger an inflammatory response resulting in subsequent fibrotic changes which may lead to anthracotic black pigmentation of the airways or lymph nodes. We present the interesting case of an incidental finding of lymphadenopathy related to silicosis with surprising visible black pigmentation of the biopsied specimen, an important differential diagnosis for visibly pigmented lymph nodes.

Introduction

Pneumoconiosis is an umbrella term used to describe any lung disease caused by the inhalation of airborne dust, usually occupational related dust. This dust is primarily inorganic particles, such as free silica dust, asbestos fibers, dust from coal mines, and mixed silicate dust [2]. Silicoanthracosis is a term also related to coal-worker’s pneumoconiosis but is used for bronchial wall and peri-bronchial black pigment deposition [3,4]. The frequency of silicoanthracosis has been estimated at 3.4%-21% [1]. Clinically patients present with chronic cough and chronic dyspnea. The patients are usually nonsmokers. CT imaging features can depict lymph node or bronchial calcification and mass-like lesions. Definitive diagnosis is with bronchoscopy or in our presented case, with lymph node biopsy histopathology [1]. Appropriate diagnosis allows for optimal management, usually treated as chronic obstructed pulmonary disease (COPD). This case report highlights the diagnostic pathway undertaken for and the radiological features of silicoanthracosis, creating awareness for radiologists when reviewing similar cases.

Case report

An 80-year-old male presented to the Emergency Department with right sided facial paralysis, right limb weakness and dysphasia on a background of atrial fibrillation, obstructive sleep apnea, previous deep vein thrombosis and giant cell arteritis. As part of investigatory work up for stroke etiology, a PET/CT was ordered to exclude active vasculitis. PET/CT-FDG was negative for evidence of active vasculitis, however there was mild-moderate FDG-tracer uptake observed within bilateral intrathoracic and right supraclavicular lymph nodes, with some nodes demonstrating calcification. An ultrasound-guided core biopsy of a FDG-avid right supraclavicular lymph node for histopathological correlation was performed. Five 20G core biopsies were taken from the lymph node under ultrasound guidance. These samples were macroscopically black in color, concerning for possible metastatic melanoma. There were no periprocedural complications and the procedure was well tolerated.

Histopathology revealed lymph node with perinodal fibroadipose tissue, containing a large number of macrophages with prominent intracytoplasmic granular carbon pigment and scant amounts of hemosiderin pigment (Fig. 4). In addition, there were polarizable silica particles identified. There was no evidence of atypical lymphoid cells or metastatic malignant cells. Plasma cells were polytypic with kappa and lambda stains. The histological findings were overall consistent with a diagnosis florid silicoanthracosis. The patient was seen by respiratory and underwent referral for lung function testing to further guide management.Fig. 4Core biopsy of lymph node with collections of macrophages containing finely granular black carbon pigment and coarsely granular golden brown hemosiderin pigment, shown in orange arrow (hematoxylin and eosin, ×600). Discussion

Silicoanthracosis is frequently associated with certain occupations such as construction, mining, jewelry making, farming and has also been linked to urban dwellers [[5], [6], [7], [8], [9]]. The inhaled particles trigger an inflammatory response that can progress into fibrosis [9]. The resulting inflammation and fibrotic changes may lead to anthracotic pigmentation, as observed in this case within lymph nodes [9]. The black pigmentation is often deposited in the airways which can be visualized macroscopically at bronchoscopy.

Metastatic melanoma was first suspected as the primary diagnosis when the pigmented lymph node core biopsies were obtained. Melanoma can present macroscopically with black or darkly pigmented lesions due melanin production [10]. This pigmentation results from abnormal melanocyte proliferation and can persist even in metastatic deposits [11]. A further deliberation was given to the possibility of exogenous pigmentation such as that arising from tattoos [12]. Tattoo pigments are composed of various metallic salts and carbon-based compounds, which may appear black or dark under gross-examination [13]. These pigment granules are generally non-refractile and do not elicit a significant inflammatory response unless complicated by hypersensitivity or infection [14].

Appropriate diagnosis of silicoanthracosis leads to optimal management plans for patients. High resolution chest CT is more sensitive and specific than chest x-rays in the early evaluation [15]. Early parenchymal disease, emphysema and pleural thickening are non-specific but features of silicoanthracosis [15]. Treatment is unfortunately limited. Whole lung lavage can alleviate chest symptoms. Antifibrotic medications, such as Pirfenidone and nintedanib, used in idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis have been used in silicoanthracosis with the aim to reduce pulmonary fibrosis progression [15]. Lung transplant is the final treatment option for end-stage pulmonary fibrosis. The diagnosis of such disease in this case triggered a respiratory review and pulmonary function lung testing. Management will be based on lung function testing, patient’s symptoms, HRCT findings at a multidisciplinary team meeting.

Conclusion

This case highlights the need to consider silicoanthracosis as a differential diagnosis when black pigmentation is observed at lymph node core biopsies, as it can closely resemble melanoma. Accurate diagnosis is crucial to avoid misinterpretation and unnecessary interventions. Histological evaluation, along with a detailed occupational and exposure history, can also aid in distinguishing between these entities.

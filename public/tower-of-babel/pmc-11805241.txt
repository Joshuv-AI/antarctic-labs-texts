All skin conditions are listed in Ayurveda under the heading (Kushtha) skin condition. INTRODUCTION

Psoriasis is a chronic inflammatory non-contagious illness that appears as red, silvery plaques, and scaly appearance on skin. Palmoplantar psoriasis is a variant of psoriasis that specifically disturbs the skin of the palms and soles. It is also known as pustular palmoplantar psoriasis, a potentially related condition characterized by small, sterile pustules. This condition may be a form of palmoplantar psoriasis or a distinct dermatological entity.[1] It is also known by different names depending on the location of appearance, which is referred to as palmoplantar psoriasis since it affects both the palms and the sole. It is typified by the development of hyperkeratosis, pustules with erythema, and scaling, usually in the middle of the palms and soles. A palmoplantar pustule starts off as a little, pin-sized eruption in the hands and soles, accompanied with walking difficulty.[2]

Globally, the incidence of psoriasis is estimated to be between 2 and 3%. Psoriasis prevalence varies from 0.44 to 2.88% in India as well.[3] An autoimmune disease called palmoplantar psoriasis flares up when exposed to certain triggers. There are many treatment manners accessible but since its chronic relapsing nature. Management of palmoplantar psoriasis is difficult. Psoralen plus ultraviolet-A radiation, or PUVA,[4] is used in modern medicine to treat psoriasis along with corticosteroids and immunomodulators.

According to Ayurveda Acharya Charak states that all Kushtha varieties are Tridoshaja, which damage the Rakta (Blood) Twak (skin), Ambu (plasma), and Mamsa (muscles). Acharya Charak stated that it may be connected to the Vipadika Kushta which is typified by Pani-Padasputan, or fissures in the palms and soles, and linked to Tivravedanam (intense localized pain).[5] In management, this Vata Kapha condition can be treated by Shaman treatment.

Gandhak Rasayan[7] possesses Kushtaghna, Kandughna, and Dahaprshamana properties. It functions as a Shaman, Raktashodhak, Vranaropaka, Twachya, and Krumighna. This formulation is commonly used to treat Kushtaroga. Purified sulfur, the main ingredient, is effective in treating various skin disorders (Twacharoga) and helps reduce itching. It can be used both externally and internally for skin conditions. The oil derived from it is beneficial in reducing itching, enhancing complexion, and improving digestion.

Padachira Ointment[5] contains Ghruta (clarified butter), Shudha Chuna (lime), Rai (mustard seed), and Shudha Turti (alum) having properties like Antiseptic and Antibacterial. Shudha Suvarna Garik (silicate of alumina), Kadu Nimb Oil (Azadirachta indica), Kokam oil (Garcinia indica), and Kapur (camphor) also possess. These ingredients help moisturize the skin and are especially effective in skin softening.

Karanjabeeja Taila[8] possesses laghurukshaguna (easily digestible and dry qualities), katuvipaka (pungent post-digestive effect), Katutikta rasa (pungent-bitter taste), and ushnaveerya (hot potency). It has the properties of pacifying Dosa like kapha and vata (vatakaphashamaka) and has Kushthaghna karma (skin disease-curing properties). Although Kushtha (skin disease) involves all three doshas (Tridosha), psoriasis predominantly involves Vata and kapha.

CONCLUSION

This study concludes that using herbal medicines both internally and externally can effectively treat Vipadika Kushtha. More research involving a larger number of patients over an extended period is recommended to demonstrate the efficacy of these therapeutic modalities further.

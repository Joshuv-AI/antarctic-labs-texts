The deviation between the planned and actual positions of immediate implants placed in the anterior maxilla using the virtual safe angle concept and a novel computer-guided drilling protocol: A prospective clinical trial
Authors: Ahmed Ibrahim Aboul Fettouh, Ahmed Fouad, Khaled Keraa, Rafik Nasser, Nael Adel, Ingy Kastour, Inas El-Zayat
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundAnatomical complexities and socket configuration in anterior maxilla often results in challenging immediate implant placement, where implants tend to be facially positioned. Implants with facially placed shoulders exhibited three times more midfacial recession. Despite using surgical guides, osteotomy drills tend to follow the least resistance path dictated by socket anatomy. This prospective clinical trial aimed to measure the deviation from the planned implant position during immediate implant placement in the anterior maxilla and its impact on esthetic outcomes, utilizing a new computer-guided plateau drilling protocol.MethodsTwenty-six patients (7 males and 19 females, ages 20–42) with non-restorable maxillary anterior teeth participated in this study. Virtual planning was done using the Safe Angle Concept and the implants were placed with a partially guided approach. Standard tessellation language files obtained from scanned casts and initial computed tomography scans were used to plan the virtual position of the implants and surgical guides fabrication. Angular, global platform and global apex deviations from the planned implant position were measured after three-months. Bone formed labial to the implant and pink esthetic score were assessed after nine-months.ResultsThe current investigation showed that the use of the new computer-guided plateau drilling protocol resulted in clinically acceptable ranges of angular, global platform, and global apex deviation 4.56 ± 3.14°, 0.91 ± 0.61 mm, 1.47 ± 0.88 mm respectively. Radiographic analysis revealed mean mm amount of bone formed labial to the implant of 2.13 ± 0.99 mm and pink esthetic score displayed a mean of 11.89 ± 1.75, with a median of 12.5 after nine months.ConclusionsThe virtual safe angle planning concept combined with plateau drilling protocol showed promising accuracy in immediate implant placement in the anterior maxilla. The resultant deviation had no influence on the esthetic outcomes. The current trial was registered at Clinical trials.gov (ID: NCT05750004, Date: 19-February-2023). Keywords: Immediate implant, Guided implant surgery, Safe angle concept, Implant deviation, Esthetic zone

Introduction

Immediate implant placement (IIP) in the esthetic zone presents a significant challenge in routine clinical practice [1]. IIP offers a reduced treatment duration, improved cost-effectiveness and satisfying patient esthetic expectations, however, clinicians must remain cognizant of the associated risks and potential complications [2, 3]. Ideal implant positioning is the key factor to prevent esthetic complications [4]. However, there are multiple anatomical challenges that hinder the achievement of proper implant position in anterior maxilla including; the labial concavity angle [5], the slope of the palatal socket wall [6], different sagittal position of the anterior teeth in alveolar bone [7] and the palatal inclination of the incisal edges [8–10]. Despite the use of surgical guides, immediate implants in esthetic zone tend to be facially positioned due to socket morphologies where drills follow the least resistance pathways [11].

Implants placed with facially positioned shoulders showed mid-facial mucosal recession. Angled abutments are often used to correct facially positioned implant shoulders in the anterior maxilla [12]. The dilemma of selecting the implant position was reported in the literature. The use of angled abutments was reported in 84% and 76% respectively, to correct implant labial inclination in virtually simulated studies at IIP sites in anterior maxilla [13, 14]. While, Botermans et al. reported 80% incidence of labial perforation when implants were placed according to prosthetic-driven positioning [15].

A CBCT study proposed the “Safe Angle Concept” as a standardized method for planning the proper immediate implant position in the anterior maxilla. This concept uses the incisal root angle to determine a safe range where the implant shoulder should be placed. This range of placement may reduce surgical complication and limit the use of angled abutments [16].

Several methods are available for achieving correct implant position in clinical practice; computer static guided, computer dynamic navigated, robotics assisted systems and free-hand surgery [17, 18]. In the late 1990s guided implant surgeries were firstly introduced, with recent advancements of technologies, it has been proven to show better prosthetic implant positioning and biological results [19]. The accuracy of implant placement using computer static guided surgery can be affected by various factors including; surgical techniques, alveolar ridge anatomy and different designs of the surgical guide.

Various techniques in literature have been proposed to counteract drilling slippage, and deviation in IIP. The apico-palatal free-hand guiding slot using a 2 mm round bur, taking place at the lower half of the palatal slope of extraction sockets [20]. A recent study implemented the apico-palatal guiding slot technique through initial virtual planned osteotomy while using digital dynamic navigation [6]. Nevertheless, literature lacks clinical approaches that help in reducing deviation during IIP in anterior maxilla. Even when surgical guides are being used, alveolar socket morphology and deviation in drilling are still factors influencing labial placement of implants [21, 22].

It is noteworthy that, there is limited body of evidence investigating the relationship between the resultant deviation in guided IIP and the peri-implant esthetics. Given the existing gap of knowledge, the aim of the present study was to measure the deviation from the planned implant position using a novel computer-guided plateau drilling protocol based on a standardized virtual planning method “Safe Angle Concept”.

Methods

This prospective clinical trial was registered at Clinical trials.gov (ID: NCT05750004), approved by the Research Ethics Committee, Misr International University, (MIU-IRB-2223-222), conducted per the Helsinki Declaration Principles of 1975, as revised in 2013. Twenty-six patients (7 males and 19 females, ages 20–42) recruited from the outpatient clinic of Misr International University and from a private practice clinic in Cairo, Egypt, between February 2023 and August 2023. The participants were selected based on specific criteria; adults over 18 years old, with at least one non-restorable maxillary anterior site (canine-to-canine region) bordered by natural teeth, a thin intact labial bone plate (< 1 mm) extending 7 mm apically and a minimum of 3–5 mm of apical bone. Exclusion criteria included; history of chemotherapy or radiotherapy within the past two years, uncontrolled diabetes, smoking, pregnancy, periapical tooth pathology, or active periodontal disease. Eligible patients were provided with detailed information regarding the surgical procedure and follow-up, and they signed a written informed consent to participate in the study.

Data acquisition and virtual planning

Conventional additional silicone impressions (Hydrorise, Zhermack, Italy) were taken via two-step impression technique using stock trays. The stone casts obtained from those impressions were optically scanned extra-orally using a laboratory scanner (Medit T700, Seoul, South Korea) to generate digital surface models in Standard Tessellation Language (STL) format. Initial radiographic records were taken using small-volume Cone-Beam Computed Tomography (CBCT) scans of the maxillary arch (Cranex SOREDEX, Finland) before tooth extraction. All data were saved in DICOM format and imported into OnDemand3D®App software (Cybermed, Seoul, Korea). The STL and DICOM files were then imported into implant planning software (Implant Studio, 3Shape, Copenhagen, Denmark). The DICOM data from the CBCT volume were imported into the implant planning software using the model importing module, data segmentation of the CBCT volume was automatically done by the software. The CBCTs and the STLs were superimposed using the three-points cloud registration via software algorithm and refinement by the Iterative Closest Point (ICP) to achieve the best match between the two surfaces (STL & DICOM), alignment was verified via a color difference map. A virtual crown design was created, focusing on esthetics and occlusion to restore the proper incisal edge position, followed by virtual implant planning based on the safe angle concept [16].

Construction of the plateau guide system (The 2-Guide System Protocol)

The drilling guide was constructed following the final planned implant position, while the plateau guide followed the same long axis of the planned implant position but only reaching the socket apex to create a flat surface aiming to decrease drilling slippage (Fig. 1). The guides were fabricated using additive technology by 3D resin printer (Crality, China) (Fig. 2). After guide fabrication, guided sleeves (T-Sleeve, Institut Straumann AG, Basel, Switzerland) were inserted and glued to the guide and verification of guides seating was performed on the stone cast (Fig. 3).

Surgical procedure

All surgical procedures in the current investigation were performed by an expert surgeon (AA). Atraumatic extraction of the offended tooth by using periotomes and luxators (Stoma, Storz am Mark GmbH, Emmingen-Liptingen Germany), without disturbing the papillary tissues. Afterwards, guides verification for seating was performed prior to drilling. In cases where palatal soft tissue interfered with the guides seating, tissues were excised according to position of the sleeve and the guides seating was reverified. Drilling started by creating nearly a flat surface at the palatal wall slope of the socket (plateau drilling technique), using a flat milling cutter with diameter slightly narrower in size than the implant diameter, reaching the socket apex and then the plateau location was verified clinically. Subsequent guided drilling as manufacturer’s instructions was completed using the second guide, then a tapered, bone level, titanium-zirconium alloy implant (Straumann BLT Roxolid SLA, Basel, Switzerland) was inserted by free-hand into the osteotomy prepared, engaging 3–4 mm of apical and palatal bone to achieve primary stability using the motor which was set to be at least 35 Ncm and manual torque wrench, to allow non-functional immediate loading [23]. A customized healing abutment was provided for all patient. It was created by adding flowable composite (Filtek™ Supreme Ultra Flowable Restorative, 3 M Corporate Headquarters, MN, USA) to a Variobase (Straumann, Basel, Switzerland), designed to replicate the shape of the socket at the marginal gingiva (Fig 4).

Postoperative and follow-up phase

Patients were prescribed antibiotics, including 500 mg of amoxicillin (E-mox 500 mg Cap., E.I.P.I.C.O., Egyptian Int. Pharmaceutical Industrial Co., A.R.E.), taken every 12 h, starting 24 h before surgery and continuing for five days postoperatively. On the first day after surgery, extraoral cold packs were recommended for the surgical site. To manage postoperative pain, a non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drug (Brufen, 400 mg tablets, Kahira Pharm. Co., Egypt) was taken as needed. Additionally, chlorhexidine HCL 0.12% mouthwash was prescribed for two weeks after the procedure.

After 3 months, an open-tray impression was taken for prosthesis fabrication. Stone casts obtained from these impressions were optically scanned with scan body (CARES Mono Scan-body, Institut Straumann AG, Basel, Switzerland) to produce a virtual model with the actual implant position for outcome assessment. The process of Implant-Abutment Alignment was done automatically by the software. Three points cloud registration via software algorithm and ICP for final refinement to the closest point was done to achieve best fit algorithm on Exocad software (v3.0-7662; Exocad GmbH, Darmstadt, Germany).

One week later, the final screw-retained zirconium oxide crown was delivered.

Trial outcomes

Implant deviation assessment (Fig. 5) The postoperative assessment was performed by one outcome assessor (FA). The primary outcomes were according to the following parameters (Fig. 6); Angular deviation defined as the deviation between the central axis of the planned and actual implants. Global platform deviation which is the linear 3-dimensional displacement between planned and actual implants, measuring at the center point of the implant platform. While, Global apex deviation is the linear 3-dimensional displacement between planned and actual implants, measuring at the center point of the implant apex.

The global platform and global apex deviations measurement were done as follow

The central axis of the virtually planned implant and the actual implant was determined automatically by the software. Regarding the global platform deviation, linear measurement was done between the center of the platform in the planned position and the actual implant position by “distance measurement tool” within the software. Same procedure was repeated for the global apex deviation, where measurements between the center of the apex in the planned position and the actual implant position were recorded.

Angular deviation measurement was as follow

The central axis of the virtually planned implant and actual implant was determined automatically by the software. The deviation measurement was done manually by finding the maximum deviation angle between the long axis of the virtually planned implant and actual implant positions, then generate a flat plane between these two axes, and the angle was calculated accordingly. The reproducibility of the measurement was achieved in all the cases.

All the deviation measurements were done using Exocad software.

Radiographic assessment

A postoperative CBCT scan was conducted on all patients after nine months. The bone formed labial to the implant was measured on CBCT at three different levels: the implant platform, 2 mm, and 5 mm. For standardization and reproducible radiographic measurements, DICOM file sets (Pre-operative and 9 months post-operative) for each patient were superimposed using the Fusion module software (OnDemand3D ver. 1.0.9, Cybermed), which provided subvoxel accuracy [24].

In order to assess whether the implant position was lying within the safe angle or not, the fused cross-sectional cut was then inverted to the post-operative scan and the implants position were assessed as described in a previous CBCT study [16]. Root inclination angle was also measured, as the angle between the palatal surface of the palatal bone and a line tangent to the root palatally (Fig.7 ).

Esthetic assessment

Pink esthetic score (PES) was assessed in this study 9 months from IIP by two independent well-trained examiners (NR, KI) with a good intra-examiner agreement (0.82 k value). The PES matches the gingival esthetics around implant-supported restoration to the contralateral natural tooth. This scoring system includes seven domains: mesial papilla, distal papilla, soft tissue margin level, soft tissue contour, deficient alveolar process, soft tissue color, and texture. Each domain is recorded from 0 to 2, with 2 as the best score. The total PES is the sum of the seven domains’ scores, ranging from 0 to 14 (14 is the best score indicating almost similar appearance to the contralateral natural tooth) [25].

Clinical assessment

Full-mouth plaque score (FMPS) [24] and full‐mouth bleeding score (FMBS) [26] were both measured pre-extraction and nine months post-implant placement.

Implant survival criteria were chosen according to a previously reported criteria [27].

Statistical analysis

Quantitative data were presented as mean, standard deviation (SD), median and range values. Qualitative data were presented as frequencies and percentages. Paired t-test was used to compare between FMPS and FMBS scores at base line and after 9 months. Linear regression analysis was used to determine significant predictors of platform, apical and angular deviations. Model fit was tested using ANOVA test. The unstandardized and standardized regression coefficient (β), standard error (SE), and 95% confidence intervals (95% CI) were calculated. The significance level was set at P ≤ 0.05. Statistical analysis was performed with IBM SPSS Statistics for Windows, Version 23.0. Armonk, NY: IBM Corp.

Discussion

This study was conducted to assess the 3D implant deviation using a novel computer-guided plateau drilling protocol based on a standardized virtual planning method “Safe Angle Concept”. During IIP in anterior maxilla, the greater degree of the palatal slope inclination of the socket results in increased implant deviation [28]. The technique described in the present investigation used a flat milling cutter (plateau drill) prior to conventional drilling protocol to eliminate the palatal bone slope of the extraction socket intending to minimize the drilling slippage and the final implant deviation. Similarly, in an invitro study, the authors investigated the accuracy of guided surgery, emphasizing the use of a milling cutter to reduce drill displacement during osteotomy preparation [29].

The current investigation demonstrated a mean angular, platform and apical deviations of 4.56 ± 3.14°, 0.91 ± 0.61 mm, 1.47 ± 0.88 mm respectively. These results align with a systematic review and meta-analysis on the accuracy of computer guided systems, which reported a mean horizontal deviation of 1 mm at the entry point, 1.6 mm at the apex and 5–6 degrees in axis [30]. In addition, these values fall within the range observed in other clinical studies [11, 31, 32]. However, two implant sites (lateral incisors) exhibited higher angular deviation values than the others. A tenable explanation for such discrepancy may be due to the narrow labio-palatal socket dimension in addition to long and wide roots and limited availability of residual apical bone in both cases. It’s notable that, the abovementioned factors may have influenced the osteotomy drill angulation during implant site preparation and implant insertion, which led to more deflection from the planned position. Further studies are recommended to investigate the effect of these different anatomical limitations on angular deviations.

On the other hand, several clinical investigations reported less angular deviation than the present study [29]. A cadaver study compared the accuracy of computer-guided and freehand surgery on flapless immediate implant placement in a split-mouth design [11]. In their partially guided group, the mean angular deviation was 3.11 ± 1.55°. Furthermore, a study previously observed superior outcomes regarding angular deviation (3.07 ± 2.18 degrees) [33]. A possible explanation of the variation in results might be due to the use of different implant deviation assessment methods. In this prospective study the deviation of the implants were assessed by actual implant scanning, while in the abovementioned studies, they used CBCT for deviation analysis.

Computer guided surgey can be partially or fully guided where the implant is instered using the guide. In the partial guide system, the guide is used to prepare the osteotomy with one (pilot drill guide) or more drills. Using the pilot drill system will give the clinician a chance to correct the drill deviation by free-hand method [34]. The present investigation used a novel 2-guide system protocol in anterior immediate implant, the plateaux guide aimed to create a flat surface on the palatal slope of the socket using the milling cutter drill, followed by the drilling guide that used all the subsequent osteotomy drills and the implants were insetred by free-hand manner.

The proper 3D implant position in the anterior maxilla is a crucial factor to achieve an optimal esthetic outcome [35]. This clinical investigation incorporated the biological and prosthetic consideration by applying the Safe Angle Concept as a pre-planned method to attain proper implant position [16]. All implants in the present study were successfully restored with straight abutments, which means that there was no requirement for angled abutments to address a labial implant inclination. Interestingly, The PES finding of the current study was 11.89 ± 1.78, with a median score of 12.5 after nine months. These findings exceed the clinically acceptable threshold of 7 and align with the high esthetic level of 11 as defined in a previous study [36]. This is consistent with other studies reporting high esthetic results of IIP in anterior maxilla [37–39].

Within this investigation, the bone labial to the implant was measured at the implant platform, 2 mm and 5 mm apically. The present findings reported a mean amount of bone formed labial to the implant of 2.45 ± 1 mm at implant platform, 2.14 ± 1.03 mm at 2 mm level, and 1.79 ± 1.03 mm at the 5 mm level with an average of 2.13 ± 0.99 mm. The presence of sufficient labial bone plate (> 1 mm) is vital for long-term functional and esthetic success [40]. A previous study reported superior amount of bone formed labial to the implant (2.7 ± 1.5 mm) for the non-grafted group [41]. One of the possible explanations is that the authors included anterior and premolar maxillary teeth, while the current study exclusively included anterior teeth with smaller labio-palatal socket width. The authors suggested that bone formation labial to the implant was related to the initial labio-palatal socket dimension.

Furthermore, the linear regression analysis conducted in this trial revealed that the implant site was a statistically significant positive predictor of both platform and apical deviations. Deviations at lateral incisor and canine sites were greater compared to those at central incisor sites. A plausible explanation might be due to different anatomical socket morphology. However, these findings should be interpreted with caution considering the limited sample size included in this study.

This study has several limitations to address. All the cases were done by single surgeon. Future RCTs with larger sample size, longer follow up periods and multiple operators are recommended. Utilizing intraoral scanning would provide a more reliable measure of implant deviation accuracy. Moreover, the 2-guide system protocol necessitates experienced, well-trained clinicians and requires additional costs. In addition to the absence of a comparison group. RCTs with longer follow up periods are needed to address the implant deviation in relation to different maxillary anterior sites.

Conclusions

Within the limits of this prospective clinical trial, the findings suggest:

The Plateau Guide System with IIP in the anterior maxilla may help reduce labial implant inclination.

The proposed plateau drilling protocol combined with virtual planning using the Safe Angle Concept appears to assist in achieving accurate implant positioning and acceptable esthetic outcomes in the anterior maxilla.

Affinization of Zinbiel bialgebras and pre-Poisson bialgebras, infinite-dimensional Poisson bialgebras
Yanhong Guo, Bo Hou

Abstract
. The purpose of this paper is to construct infinite-dimensional Poisson bialgebras by the affinization of pre-Poisson algebras. There is a natural Poisson algebra structure on the tensor product of a pre-Poisson algebra and a perm algebra, and the Poisson algebra structure on the tensor product of a pre-Poisson algebra and a special perm algebra characterizes the pre-Poisson algebra. We extend such correspondences to the context of bialgebras, that is, there is a Poisson bialgebra structure on the tensor product of a pre-Poisson bialgebra and a quadratic ℤ \mathbb{Z} -graded perm algebra. In this process, we provide the affinization of Zinbiel bialgebras, and give a correspondence between symmetric solutions of the Yang-Baxter equation in pre-Poisson algebras and certain skew-symmetric solutions of the Yang-Baxter equation in the induced infinite-dimensional Poisson algebras. The similar correspondences for the related triangular bialgebra structures and 𝒪 \mathcal{O} -operators are given.

1.
Introduction

The purpose of this paper is to give a construction of infinite-dimensional Poisson
bialgebras via the affinization of pre-Poisson bialgebras. A pre-Poisson
algebra is both a Zinbiel algebra and a pre-Lie algebra which are compatible in a
certain sense. We use the affinization of Zinbiel bialgebras and pre-Lie bialgebras
to achieve the affinization of pre-Poisson bialgebras.

1.1.
Poisson algebras and pre-Poisson algebras.

Poisson algebras were originally coming from the study of the Hamiltonian mechanics.
A Poisson algebra is the algebraic structure corresponding to a Poisson manifold.
Poisson algebras serve as fundamental structures in various areas of mathematics
and mathematical physics, including Poisson geometry
[
33
,
36
]
,
algebraic geometry
[
14
,
31
]
, classical and quantum mechanics
[
2
,
12
]
,
quantum groups
[
13
]
and quantization theory
[
18
,
21
]
.

The notion of a pre-Poisson algebra was first introduced by Aguiar
[
1
]
,
which combines a Zinbiel algebra and a pre-Lie algebra on the same vector space
satisfying some compatibility conditions. The notion of Zinbiel algebras
(also called dual Leibniz algebras) was introduced by Loday in
[
28
]
, and further
studied in
[
27
,
29
]
. Pre-Lie algebras (also called left-symmetric algebras,
quasi-associative algebras, Vinberg algebras and so on) are a class of nonassociative
algebras coming from the study of convex homogeneous cones, affine manifolds and
affine structures on Lie groups, and cohomologies of associative algebras
[
10
,
5
]
.
They also appeared in many fields in mathematics and mathematical physics, such as complex
and symplectic structures on Lie groups and Lie algebras, Poisson brackets and infinite
dimensional Lie algebras, vertex algebras, integrable systems and quantum field theory.
A pre-Poisson algebra naturally gives rise to its sub-adjacent Poisson algebra through the
anti-commutator of Zinbiel algebras and the commutator of pre-Lie algebras.
On the other hand, a Rota-Baxter operator of weight zero on a Poisson algebra can
give rise to a pre-Poisson algebra
[
1
]
.

1.2.
Bialgebras theory and classical Yang-Baxter equation.

A bialgebra structure on a given algebra structure is obtained as a coalgebra
structure together which gives the same algebra structure on the dual space with
a set of compatibility conditions between the products and coproducts.
Two famous examples of bialgebras are Lie bialgebras
[
13
]
and antisymmetric
infinitesimal bialgebras
[
38
,
3
]
. Lie bialgebras can be viewed as a linearization
of Poisson-Lie groups. It were first introduced by Drinfeld in the context of the
theory of Yang-Baxter equations and quantum groups.
Infinitesimal bialgebras first appeared in the work of Joni and Rota to give an
algebraic framework for the calculus of divided differences
[
19
]
.

The bialgebra theories of various algebra structures have been extensively developed,
such as pre-Lie bialgebras
[
4
]
, Jordan bialgebras
[
38
]
, Novikov
bialgebras
[
15
]
, perm bialgebras
[
17
,
23
]
, and so on. The Poisson
bialgebras have been studied in
[
30
]
. Recently, Wang and Sheng have given a
bialgebra theory of pre-Poisson algebras and shown that there is a one-to-one
correspondence between quadratic Rota-Baxter pre-Poisson algebras and factorizable
pre-Poisson bialgebras
[
35
]
. In
[
25
]
, Lin and Lu have studied the
quasi-triangular and factorizable Poisson bialgebras. In this paper, we construct
an infinite-dimensional Poisson bialgebras by the affinization of pre-Poisson bialgebras.
More precisely, we show that there is a Poisson bialgebra structure on the tensor product
of a pre-Poisson bialgebra and a quadratic perm algebra, and a bialgebra structure
for a pre-Poisson algebra which could be characterized by the fact that its affinization
by a special quadratic perm algebra gives an infinite-dimensional Poisson bialgebra.

The classical Yang-Baxter equation (for short
\mathrm{CYBE}
) arose from the study of
inverse scattering theory in the 1980s and was recognized as the semi-classical limit
of the quantum Yang-Baxter equation
[
37
,
8
]
. The study of the
\mathrm{CYBE}

in a Lie algebra has substantial ramifications and applications in the areas
of symplectic geometry, quantum groups, integrable systems, and quantum field theory

[
9
,
32
]
. The classical Yang-Baxter equations in a Poisson algebra and a
pre-Poisson algebra have been studied in
[
30
]
and
[
35
]
. Similar to
the results for Lie algebra, we can obtain that a skew-symmetric solution of the
Yang-Baxter equation in a Poisson algebra (resp, a symmetric solution of the Yang-Baxter
equation in a pre-Poisson algebra) induces a triangular Poisson bialgebra structure (resp.
a triangular pre-Poisson bialgebra structure). In this paper, we show that any symmetric
solution of the Yang-Baxter equation in a pre-Poisson algebra can be elevated to a
skew-symmetric solution of the Yang-Baxter equation in the Poisson algebra obtained by
the affinization, thus obtaining that the Poisson bialgebra obtained by the affinization
is triangular if the pre-Poisson bialgebra is triangular.

1.3.
Affinization of pre-Poisson bialgebras.

An important construction of infinite-dimensional algebras is a process of
affinization which equips an algebra structure on the tensor product, over a
field
\mathbf{k}
,
A\otimes\mathbf{k}[\mathfrak{t},\mathfrak{t}^{-1}]
, of a certain algebra
A
of finite dimension
with another algebra structure on the space of Laurent polynomials
\mathbf{k}[\mathfrak{t},\mathfrak{t}^{-1}]

[
7
]
. The classical instance of the affinization is the Lie algebra affinization

L\otimes\mathbf{k}[\mathfrak{t},\mathfrak{t}^{-1}]
, where
L
is a finite-dimensional Lie algebra and

\mathbf{k}[\mathfrak{t},\mathfrak{t}^{-1}]
is the commutative associative algebra
[
20
]
.
Recently, further research has been conducted on the affinization of the bialgebra structure.
Since the operad of (left) Novikov algebras and the operad of right Novikov algebras
are Koszul dual, in
[
15
]
, Hong, Bai and Guo have proposed a method for
constructing infinite-dimensional Lie bialgebras using the affinization of Novikov
bialgebras. Similarly, since the operads of perm algebras and pre-Lie algebras are
the Koszul dual each other, Lin, Zhou and Bai have constructed infinite-dimensional
Lie bialgebras by using the pre-Lie bialgebras and perm bialgebras, respectively
[
23
]
.

Roughly speaking, the affinization of a given algebra structure is to define an
algebra structure on the vector space of Laurent polynomials and obtain another algebra
structure on the tensor product, which could resolve the given algebra structure in turn.
In this paper, we consider the affinization of Zinbiel bialgebras and pre-Poisson
bialgebras. We first show that the tensor product of a Zinbiel algebra and a perm algebra
is a commutative associative algebra and when the perm algebra is a special
perm algebra on the vector space of Laurent polynomials, the tensor product characterizes
the Zinbiel algebra as a property of the associative algebra. Next, we consider
the dual version of the Zinbiel algebra affinization for Zinbiel coalgebras.
We introduce the notion of completed cocommutative coassociative coalgebras
and prove that there is a natural completed cocommutative coassociative coalgebra
structure on the tensor product of a Zinbiel coalgebra and the completed
perm coalgebra. Therefore, we use the quadratic
\mathbb{Z}
-graded perm algebra lifts the
affinization characterizations of Zinbiel algebras and Zinbiel coalgebras to
the level of Zinbiel bialgebras. Based on these results and the affinization of
pre-Lie bialgebras given in
[
23
]
, we present the affinization of pre-Lie bialgebras.

Theorem.

Let
(A,\ast,\circ,\vartheta,\theta)
be a finite-dimensional
pre-Poisson bialgebra,
(B=\oplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}B_{i},\diamond,\omega)
be a quadratic

\mathbb{Z}
-graded perm algebra and
(A\otimes B,\cdot,[-,-])
be the induced
\mathbb{Z}
-graded
Poisson algebra from
(A,\ast,\circ)
by
(B=\oplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}B_{i},\diamond)
.
Define two linear maps
\Delta,\delta:A\otimes B\rightarrow(A\otimes B)\otimes(A\otimes B)
by Eqs. (
3.1
) and (
4.1
) for
\nu=\nu_{\omega}
.
Then
(A\otimes B,\cdot,[-,-],\Delta,\delta)
is a completed Poisson bialgebra.

Moreover, if
(B=\oplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}B_{i},\diamond,\omega)
is the quadratic
\mathbb{Z}
-graded
perm algebra given in Example
3.8
, then
(A\otimes B,\cdot,[-,-],\Delta,\delta)
is a completed Poisson bialgebra if and only if

(A,\ast,\circ,\vartheta,\theta)
is a pre-Poisson bialgebra.

Moreover, we also consider the affinization of quasi-Frobenius pre-Poisson algebras.

With the development of bialgebra theory, the relationship between different types
of bialgebras has attracted the attention of scholars
[
15
,
23
,
22
,
16
,
11
]
.
It is worth noting that although a pre-Poisson algebra naturally has a Poisson algebra
structure (by the anti-commutator of Zinbiel algebras and the commutator of pre-Lie algebras),
there is generally no Poisson bialgebra structure on a pre-Poisson bialgebra unless strong
conditions are added. Here, we establish the connection between Poisson bialgebras and
pre-Poisson bialgebras using the affinization.

2.
Poisson bialgebras and pre-Poisson bialgebras

In this section, we recall the notions of Poisson algebras, pre-Poisson algebras
and the bialgebra theory of Poisson algebras and pre-Poisson algebras.
For the details, see
[
30
,
1
,
35
]
.
First we recall the notion of Poisson algebras and pre-Poisson algebras.

Definition 2.1
.

A
Poisson algebra
is a triple
(P,\cdot,[-,-])
, where
(P,\cdot)
is a
commutative associative algebra and
(P,[-,-])
is a Lie algebra, such that the
Leibniz rule holds:

[p_{1},\;p_{2}p_{3}]=[p_{1},p_{2}]p_{3}+p_{2}[p_{1},p_{3}],

for any
p_{1},p_{2},p_{3}\in P
, where
p_{1}p_{2}:=p_{1}\cdot p_{2}
.

Let
(A,\cdot)
be a commutative associative algebra. Recall that a representation of

(A,\cdot)
is a pair
(V,\mu)
, where
V
is a vector space and
\mu:A\rightarrow\mathrm{End}_{\mathbf{k}}(V)
is a linear map such that
\mu(a_{1})\mu(a_{2})=\mu(a_{1}a_{2})

for any
a_{1},a_{2}\in A
. Let
(\mathfrak{g},[-,-])
be a Lie algebra. Recall that a
representation of
(\mathfrak{g},[-,-])
is a pair
(V,\rho)
, where
V
is a vector space
and
\mu:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathrm{End}_{\mathbf{k}}(V)
is a linear map such that
\rho(g_{1}g_{2})=\rho(a_{1})\rho(a_{2})-\rho(a_{2})\rho(a_{1})
for any
g_{1},g_{2}\in\mathfrak{g}
.
Let
(P,\cdot,[-,-])
be a Poisson algebra,
V
be a vector space and

\mu,\rho:P\rightarrow\mathrm{End}_{\mathbf{k}}(V)
be two linear maps. Recall that
(V,\mu,\rho)
is
called a
representation of

(P,\cdot,[-,-])
, if
(V,\rho)
is a
representation of the Lie algebra
(P,[-,-])
and
(V,\mu)
is a representation
of the commutative associative algebra
(P,\cdot)
and
\mu,\rho
satisfy
the following conditions:

\displaystyle\rho(p_{1}p_{2})

\displaystyle=\mu(p_{2})\rho(p_{1})+\mu(p_{1})\rho(p_{2}),

\displaystyle\mu([p_{1},p_{2}])

\displaystyle=\rho(p_{1})\mu(p_{2})-\mu(p_{2})\rho(p_{1}),

for any
p_{1},p_{2}\in P
. In particular,
(P,\mathbf{u}_{P},\mathrm{ad}_{P})
is a
representation of the Poisson algebra
(P,\cdot,[-,-])
, which is called the

regular representation
of
(P,\cdot,[-,-])
, where
\mathbf{u}_{P}(p_{1})(p_{2})=p_{1}p_{2}
and
\mathrm{ad}_{P}(p_{1})(p_{2})

=[p_{1},p_{2}]
for any
p_{1},p_{2}\in P
.
Moreover,
(P^{\ast},-\mathbf{u}^{\ast}_{P},\mathrm{ad}^{\ast}_{P})
is also a representation of
the Poisson algebra
(P,\cdot,[-,-])
, which is called the
coregular
representation
of
(P,\cdot,[-,-])
.

Definition 2.2
.

A
Zinbiel algebra

(A,\ast)
is a vector space
A
together with a bilinear
operation
\ast:A\otimes A\rightarrow A
such that

a_{1}\ast(a_{2}\ast a_{3})=(a_{1}\ast a_{2})\ast a_{3}+(a_{2}\ast a_{1})\ast a_{3},

for any
a_{1},a_{2},a_{3}\in A
.

In a Zinbiel algebra
(A,\ast)
, it is easy to see that
a_{1}\ast(a_{2}\ast a_{3})=a_{2}\ast(a_{1}\ast a_{3})
for any
a_{1},a_{2},a_{3}\in A
. Moreover, if we define

a_{1}a_{2}=a_{1}\ast a_{2}+a_{2}\ast a_{1}
, then
(A,\cdot)
is a commutative
associative algebra.

Definition 2.3
.

A
pre-Lie algebra

(A,\circ)
is a vector space
A
equipped with a bilinear
multiplication
\circ:A\otimes A\rightarrow A
such that for any
a_{1},a_{2},a_{3}\in A
,

(a_{1}\circ a_{2})\circ a_{3}-a_{1}\circ(a_{2}\circ a_{3})=(a_{2}\circ a_{1})\circ a_{3}-a_{2}\circ(a_{1}\circ a_{3}).

Let
(A,\circ)
be a pre-Lie algebra. Then the commutator
[a_{1},a_{2}]:=a_{1}\circ a_{2}-a_{2}\circ a_{1}
for any
a_{1},a_{2}\in A
defines a Lie algebra

(A,[-,-])
, which is called the sub-adjacent Lie algebra of
(A,\circ)
.

Definition 2.4

(
[
1
]
)
.

A
pre-Poisson algebra
is a triple
(A,\ast,\circ)
, where
(P,\ast)
is a
Zinbiel algebra and
(P,\circ)
is a pre-Lie algebra, satisfying the following
compatibility conditions:

(2.1)

\displaystyle(a_{1}\circ a_{2}-a_{2}\circ a_{1})\ast a_{3}

\displaystyle=a_{1}\circ(a_{2}\ast a_{3})-a_{2}\ast(a_{1}\circ a_{3}),

(2.2)

\displaystyle(a_{1}\ast a_{2}+a_{2}\ast a_{1})\circ a_{3}

\displaystyle=a_{1}\ast(a_{2}\circ a_{3})+a_{2}\ast(a_{1}\circ a_{3}),

for any
a_{1},a_{2},a_{3}\in A
.

Example 2.5
.

Let
A
be a two-dimensional vector space with a basis
\{e_{1},e_{2}\}
.
If we define two linear maps
\ast,\circ:A\otimes A\rightarrow A
by

e_{1}\ast e_{1}=ae_{2}
,
e_{1}\circ e_{1}=be_{1}+ce_{2}
,
e_{1}\circ e_{2}=be_{2}=e_{2}\circ e_{1}
and
e_{1}\cdot e_{2}=e_{2}\cdot e_{1}=e_{2}\cdot e_{2}=e_{2}\circ e_{2}=0
, then
(A,\ast,\circ)
is a pre-Poisson algebra.

Let
(A,\ast,\circ)
be a pre-Poisson algebra. Then
(A,\cdot,[-,-])
is a
Poisson algebra, where the bracket
[-,-]
and the multiplication
\cdot

are given by
[a_{1},a_{2}]=a_{1}\circ a_{2}-a_{2}\circ a_{1}
and

a_{1}a_{2}=a_{1}\ast a_{2}+a_{2}\ast a_{1}
for any
a_{1},a_{2}\in A
.
On the other hand, recall that a
Rota-Baxter operator
on a Poisson algebra

(P,\cdot,[-,-])
is a linear map
\mathcal{R}:P\rightarrow P
satisfying

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}(p_{1})\mathcal{R}(p_{2})

\displaystyle=\mathcal{R}\big(\mathcal{R}(p_{1})p_{2}+p_{1}\mathcal{R}(p_{2})\big),

\displaystyle[\mathcal{R}(p_{1}),\;\mathcal{R}(p_{2})]

\displaystyle=\mathcal{R}\big([\mathcal{R}(p_{1}),\,p_{2}]+[p_{1},\,\mathcal{R}(p_{2})]\big),

for any
p_{1},p_{2}\in P
. Let
\mathcal{R}:P\rightarrow P
be a Rota-Baxter
operator of a Poisson algebra
(P,\cdot,[-,-])
. Define new operations

\ast_{\mathcal{R}},\circ_{\mathcal{R}}:P\otimes P\rightarrow P
by

p_{1}\ast_{\mathcal{R}}p_{2}=\mathcal{R}(p_{1})p_{2},\qquad p_{1}\circ_{\mathcal{R}}p_{2}=[\mathcal{R}(p_{1}),\;p_{2}],

for any
p_{1},p_{2}\in P
. Then
(P,\ast_{\mathcal{R}},\circ_{\mathcal{R}})

is a pre-Poisson algebra
[
1
]
.

Let us recall the notions of representations of Zinbiel algebras and pre-Lie algebras.
Then we introduce the representations of pre-Poisson algebras.
A
representation of a Zinbiel algebra

(A,\ast)
is a triple
(V,\mathfrak{l},\mathfrak{r})
,
where
V
is a vector space and
\mathfrak{l},\mathfrak{r}:A\rightarrow\mathrm{End}_{\mathbf{k}}(V)
are linear maps
such that the following equations hold for all
a_{1},a_{2}\in A
,

\displaystyle\mathfrak{l}(a_{1})\mathfrak{l}(a_{2})

\displaystyle=\mathfrak{l}(a_{1}\ast a_{2})+\mathfrak{l}(a_{2}\ast a_{1}),

\displaystyle\mathfrak{r}(a_{1}\ast a_{2})

\displaystyle=\mathfrak{l}(a_{1})\mathfrak{r}(a_{2})=\mathfrak{r}(a_{2})\mathfrak{l}(a_{1})+\mathfrak{r}(a_{2})\mathfrak{r}(a_{1}).

A
representation of a pre-Lie algebra

(A,\circ)
is a triple
(V,\hat{\mathfrak{l}},\hat{\mathfrak{r}})
, where
V
is a vector space,
\hat{\mathfrak{l}},\hat{\mathfrak{r}}:A\rightarrow\mathrm{End}_{\mathbf{k}}(V)

are linear maps such that the following equations hold for all
a_{1},a_{2}\in A
,

\displaystyle\hat{\mathfrak{l}}(a_{1})\hat{\mathfrak{l}}(a_{2})-\hat{\mathfrak{l}}(a_{1}\circ a_{2})

\displaystyle=\hat{\mathfrak{l}}(a_{2})\hat{\mathfrak{l}}(a_{1})-\hat{\mathfrak{l}}(a_{2}\circ a_{1}),

\displaystyle\hat{\mathfrak{l}}(a_{1})\hat{\mathfrak{r}}(a_{2})-\hat{\mathfrak{r}}(a_{2})\hat{\mathfrak{l}}(a_{1})

\displaystyle=\hat{\mathfrak{r}}(a_{1}\circ a_{2})-\hat{\mathfrak{r}}(a_{2})\mathfrak{r}(a_{1}).

Definition 2.6
.

A
representation of a pre-Poisson algebra

(A,\ast,\circ)
is a quintuple

(V,\mathfrak{l},\mathfrak{r},\hat{\mathfrak{l}},\hat{\mathfrak{r}})
, where
(V,\mathfrak{l},\mathfrak{r})
is a representation of
the Zinbiel algebra
(A,\ast)
,
(V,\hat{\mathfrak{l}},\hat{\mathfrak{r}})
is a representation
of the pre-Lie algebra
(A,\circ)
and for any
a_{1},a_{2}\in A
,

\displaystyle\mathfrak{l}(a_{1}\circ a_{2}-a_{2}\circ a_{1})

\displaystyle=\hat{\mathfrak{l}}(a_{1})\mathfrak{l}(a_{2})-\mathfrak{l}(a_{2})\hat{\mathfrak{l}}(a_{1}),

\displaystyle\mathfrak{r}(a_{1}\circ_{P}a_{2})

\displaystyle=\mathfrak{r}(a_{2})\hat{\mathfrak{r}}(a_{1})-\mathfrak{r}(a_{2})\hat{\mathfrak{l}}(a_{1})+\hat{\mathfrak{l}}(a_{1})\mathfrak{r}(a_{2}),

\displaystyle\mathfrak{r}(a_{1}\circ_{P}a_{2})

\displaystyle=\hat{\mathfrak{r}}(a_{2})\mathfrak{l}(a_{1})+\hat{\mathfrak{r}}(a_{2})\mathfrak{r}(a_{1})-\mathfrak{l}(a_{1})\hat{\mathfrak{r}}(a_{2}),

\displaystyle\hat{\mathfrak{l}}(a_{1}\ast a_{2}+a_{2}\ast a_{1})

\displaystyle=\mathfrak{l}(a_{1})\hat{\mathfrak{l}}(a_{2})+\mathfrak{l}(a_{2})\hat{\mathfrak{l}}(a_{1}),

\displaystyle\hat{\mathfrak{r}}(a_{1}\ast a_{2})

\displaystyle=\mathfrak{l}(a_{1})\hat{\mathfrak{r}}(a_{2})+\mathfrak{r}(a_{2})\hat{\mathfrak{r}}(a_{1})-\mathfrak{r}(a_{2})\hat{\mathfrak{l}}(a_{1}).

Let
(A,\ast,\circ)
be a pre-Poisson algebra. If we define linear maps
\mathbf{l}_{A},\mathbf{r}_{A},\hat{\mathbf{l}}_{A},\hat{\mathbf{r}}_{A}:A\otimes\mathrm{End}_{\mathbf{k}}(A)
by
\mathbf{l}_{A}(a_{1})(a_{2})=a_{1}\ast a_{2}
,
\mathbf{r}_{A}(a_{1})(a_{2})=a_{2}\ast a_{1}
,
\hat{\mathbf{l}}_{A}(a_{1})(a_{2})=a_{1}\circ a_{2}
and
\hat{\mathbf{r}}_{A}(a_{1})(a_{2})=a_{2}\circ a_{1}
for any
a_{1},a_{2}\in A
, then
(A,\mathbf{l}_{A},\mathbf{r}_{A},\hat{\mathbf{l}}_{A},\hat{\mathbf{r}}_{A})
is a
representation of the pre-Poisson algebra
(A,\ast,\circ)
, which is called the

regular representation
of
(A,\ast,\circ)
, and
(A^{\ast},-\mathbf{l}^{\ast}_{A}-\mathbf{r}^{\ast}_{A},\mathbf{r}^{\ast}_{A},\hat{\mathbf{l}}^{\ast}_{A}-\hat{\mathbf{r}}^{\ast}_{A},-\hat{\mathbf{r}}^{\ast}_{A})
is also a representation of the pre-Poisson algebra
(A,\ast,\circ)
, which is called the
coregular representation
of
(A,\ast,\circ)
.

Next, we consider the bialgebra theory of Poisson algebras and pre-Poisson algebras.
Recall that a
coassociative coalgebra

(A,\Delta)
is a vector space
A

with a linear map
\Delta:A\rightarrow A\otimes A
satisfying the coassociative law:

(\Delta\otimes\mathop{id}\nolimits)\Delta=(\mathop{id}\nolimits\otimes\Delta)\Delta.

A coassociative coalgebra
(A,\Delta)
is called
cocommutative
if

\Delta=\tau\Delta
, where
\tau:A\otimes A\rightarrow A\otimes A
is the
flip operator defined by
\tau(a_{1}\otimes a_{2}):=a_{2}\otimes a_{1}
for
all
a_{1},a_{2}\in A
. An
infinitesimal bialgebra
is a triple
(A,\cdot,\Delta)
,
where
(A,\cdot)
is a commutative associative algebra,
(A,\Delta)
is a cocommutative
coassociative coalgebra and for any
a_{1},a_{2}\in A
,

\Delta(a_{1}a_{2})=(\mathbf{u}_{A}(a_{2})\otimes\mathop{id}\nolimits)\Delta(a_{1})+(\mathop{id}\nolimits\otimes\,\mathbf{u}_{A}(a_{1}))\Delta(a_{2}).

Recall that
Lie coalgebra

(\mathfrak{g},\delta)

is a vector space
\mathfrak{g}
with a linear map
\delta:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathfrak{g}
,
such that
\tau\delta=-\delta
and
(\mathop{id}\nolimits\otimes\delta)\delta-(\tau\otimes\mathop{id}\nolimits)(\mathop{id}\nolimits\otimes\delta)\delta=(\delta\otimes\mathop{id}\nolimits)\delta
.
A
Lie bialgebra
is a triple
(\mathfrak{g},[-,-],\delta)
such that

(\mathfrak{g},[-,-])
is a Lie algebra,
(\mathfrak{g},\delta)
is a Lie coalgebra, and the following
compatibility condition holds:

\delta([g_{1},g_{2}])=(\mathrm{ad}_{\mathfrak{g}}(g_{1})\otimes\mathop{id}\nolimits+\mathop{id}\nolimits\otimes\mathrm{ad}_{\mathfrak{g}}(g_{1}))(\delta(g_{2}))-(\mathrm{ad}_{\mathfrak{g}}(g_{2})\otimes\mathop{id}\nolimits+\mathop{id}\nolimits\otimes\mathrm{ad}_{\mathfrak{g}}(g_{2}))(\delta(g_{1})),

for any
g_{1},g_{2}\in\mathfrak{g}
. Let
(P,\Delta)
be a cocommutative coassociative coalgebra
and
(P,\delta)
be a Lie coalgebra. If
(\mathop{id}\nolimits\otimes\Delta)\delta=(\delta\otimes\mathop{id}\nolimits)\Delta+(\tau\otimes\mathop{id}\nolimits)(\mathop{id}\nolimits\otimes\delta)\Delta
, then
(P,\Delta,\delta)
is called a

Poisson coalgebra
. It is easy to see that
(P,\Delta,\delta)
is a Poisson
coalgebra if and only if
(P^{\ast},\Delta^{\ast},\delta^{\ast})
is a Poisson algebra.

Definition 2.7
.

Let
(P,\cdot,[-,-])
be a Poisson algebra and
(P,\Delta,\delta)
be a Poisson
coalgebra. Then the quintuple
(P,\cdot,[-,-],\Delta,\delta)
is called a

Poisson bialgebra
if

(i)

(P,\cdot,\Delta)
is an infinitesimal bialgebra,

(ii)

(P,[-,-],\delta)
is a Lie bialgebra,

(iii)

\Delta
and
\delta
are compatible in the following sense:
for any
p_{1},p_{2}\in P
,

\displaystyle\Delta([p_{1},p_{2}])=\big(\mathrm{ad}_{P}(p_{1})\otimes\mathop{id}\nolimits+\mathop{id}\nolimits\otimes\mathrm{ad}_{P}(p_{1})\big)\Delta(p_{2})+\big(\mathbf{u}_{P}(p_{2})\otimes\mathop{id}\nolimits-\mathop{id}\nolimits\otimes\mathbf{u}_{P}(p_{2})\big)\delta(p_{1}),

\displaystyle\qquad\quad\delta(p_{1}p_{2})=(\mathbf{u}_{P}(p_{1})\otimes\mathop{id}\nolimits)\delta(p_{2})+(\mathbf{u}_{P}(p_{2})\otimes\mathop{id}\nolimits)\delta(p_{1})

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+(\mathop{id}\nolimits\otimes\mathrm{ad}_{P}(p_{1}))\Delta(p_{2})+(\mathop{id}\nolimits\otimes\mathrm{ad}_{P}(p_{2}))\Delta(p_{1}).

Recall that a
Zinbiel coalgebra
is a vector space
A
with a linear map

\vartheta:A\rightarrow A\otimes A
such that

(2.3)

\displaystyle(\vartheta\otimes\mathop{id}\nolimits)\vartheta+(\tau\otimes\mathop{id}\nolimits)(\vartheta\otimes\mathop{id}\nolimits)\vartheta=(\mathop{id}\nolimits\otimes\vartheta)\vartheta.

A
Zinbiel bialgebra
is a triple
(A,\ast,\vartheta)
, where
(A,\ast)

is a Zinbiel algebra and
(A,\vartheta)
is a Zinbiel coalgebra such that for
all
a_{1},a_{2}\in A
, the following compatible conditions hold:

(2.4)

\displaystyle\vartheta(a_{1}\ast a_{2})+\tau(\vartheta(a_{1}\ast a_{2}))

\displaystyle=(\mathop{id}\nolimits\otimes\mathbf{r}_{A}(a_{2}))\vartheta(a_{1})+(\mathbf{l}_{A}(a_{1})\otimes\mathop{id}\nolimits)\vartheta(a_{2})+\tau((\mathop{id}\nolimits\otimes\mathbf{l}_{A}(a_{1}))\vartheta(a_{2})),

(2.5)

\displaystyle\vartheta(a_{1}\ast a_{2}+a_{2}\ast a_{1})

\displaystyle=(\mathbf{l}_{A}(a_{1})\otimes\mathop{id}\nolimits)\vartheta(a_{2})+(\mathop{id}\nolimits\otimes\mathbf{l}_{A}(a_{2}))\vartheta(a_{1})+(\mathop{id}\nolimits\otimes\mathbf{r}_{A}(a_{2}))\vartheta(a_{1}).

Recall that a
pre-Lie coalgebra
is a pair
(A,\theta)
, where
A
is a vector
space and
\theta:A\rightarrow A\otimes A
is a linear map satisfying

(\mathop{id}\nolimits\otimes\theta)\theta-(\tau\otimes\mathop{id}\nolimits)(\mathop{id}\nolimits\otimes\theta)\theta=(\theta\otimes\mathop{id}\nolimits)\theta-(\tau\otimes\mathop{id}\nolimits)(\theta\otimes\mathop{id}\nolimits)\theta,

for any
a_{1},a_{2}\in A
. Let
(A,\circ)
be a pre-Lie algebra and

(A,\theta)
be a pre-Lie coalgebra. If for any
a_{1},a_{2}\in A
, the
following compatibility conditions are satisfied:

\displaystyle(\theta-\tau\theta)(a_{1}\circ a_{2})-(\hat{\mathbf{l}}_{A}(a_{1})\otimes\mathop{id}\nolimits)((\theta-\tau\theta)(a_{2}))

\displaystyle\quad-(\mathop{id}\nolimits\otimes\hat{\mathbf{l}}_{A}(a_{1}))((\theta-\tau\theta)(a_{2}))-(\mathop{id}\nolimits\otimes\hat{\mathbf{r}}_{A}(a_{2}))(\theta(a_{1}))+(\hat{\mathbf{r}}_{A}(a_{2})\otimes\mathop{id}\nolimits)(\tau(\theta(a_{1})))=0,

\displaystyle\theta(a_{1}\circ a_{2}-a_{2}\circ a_{1})-(\mathop{id}\nolimits\otimes(\hat{\mathbf{r}}_{A}(a_{2})-\hat{\mathbf{l}}_{A}(a_{2})))(\theta(a_{1}))

\displaystyle\quad-(\mathop{id}\nolimits\otimes(\hat{\mathbf{l}}_{A}(a_{1})-\hat{\mathbf{r}}_{A}(a_{1})))(\theta(a_{2}))-(\hat{\mathbf{l}}_{A}(a_{1})\otimes\mathop{id}\nolimits)(\theta(a_{2}))+(\hat{\mathbf{l}}_{A}(a_{2})\otimes\mathop{id}\nolimits)(\theta(a_{1}))=0,

then we call
(A,\circ,\theta)
a
pre-Lie bialgebra
.
Let
(A,\vartheta)
be a Zinbiel coalgebra and
(A,\theta)
be a pre-Lie coalgebra.
If

(2.6)

\displaystyle((\theta-\tau\theta)\otimes\mathop{id}\nolimits)\vartheta=(\mathop{id}\nolimits\otimes\vartheta)\theta-(\tau\otimes\mathop{id}\nolimits)(\mathop{id}\nolimits\otimes\theta)\vartheta,

(2.7)

\displaystyle((\vartheta+\tau\vartheta)\otimes\mathop{id}\nolimits)\theta=(\mathop{id}\nolimits\otimes\theta)\vartheta+(\tau\otimes\mathop{id}\nolimits)(\mathop{id}\nolimits\otimes\theta)\vartheta,

then
(A,\vartheta,\theta)
is called a
pre-Poisson coalgebra
. It is easy to
see that
(A,\vartheta,\theta)
is a pre-Poisson coalgebra if and only if
(A^{\ast},\vartheta^{\ast},\theta^{\ast})
is a pre-Poisson algebra.

Definition 2.8

(
[
35
]
)
.

Let
(A,\ast,\circ)
be a pre-Poisson algebra and
(A,\vartheta,\theta)
be a
pre-Poisson coalgebra. Then the quintuple
(A,\ast,\circ,\vartheta,\theta)
is
called a
pre-Poisson bialgebra
if

(i)

(A,\ast,\vartheta)
is a Zinbiel bialgebra,

(ii)

(A,\circ,\theta)
is a pre-Lie bialgebra,

(iii)

\vartheta
and
\theta
are compatible in the following sense:
for any
a_{1},a_{2}\in A
,

(2.8)

\displaystyle\theta(a_{1}\ast a_{2}+a_{2}\ast a_{1})

\displaystyle=(\mathop{id}\nolimits\otimes(\mathbf{l}_{A}+\mathbf{r}_{A})(a_{2}))\theta(a_{1})+(\mathop{id}\nolimits\otimes(\mathbf{l}_{A}+\mathbf{r}_{A})(a_{1}))\theta(a_{2})

\displaystyle\qquad-(\hat{\mathbf{l}}_{A}(a_{1})\otimes\mathop{id}\nolimits)\vartheta(a_{2})-(\hat{\mathbf{l}}_{A}(a_{2})\otimes\mathop{id}\nolimits)\vartheta(a_{1}),

(2.9)

\displaystyle\vartheta(a_{1}\circ a_{2}-a_{2}\circ a_{1})

\displaystyle=(\mathbf{l}_{A}(a_{2})\otimes\mathop{id}\nolimits-\mathop{id}\nolimits\otimes(\mathbf{l}_{A}+\mathbf{r}_{A})(a_{2}))\theta(a_{1})

\displaystyle\qquad+(\hat{\mathbf{l}}_{A}(a_{1})\otimes\mathop{id}\nolimits+\mathop{id}\nolimits\otimes(\hat{\mathbf{l}}_{A}-\hat{\mathbf{r}}_{A})(a_{1}))\vartheta(a_{2}),

(2.10)

\displaystyle(\vartheta+\tau\vartheta)(a_{1}\circ a_{2})

\displaystyle=-(\mathop{id}\nolimits\otimes\mathbf{r}_{A}(a_{2}))\theta(a_{1})-(\mathbf{r}_{A}(a_{2})\otimes\mathop{id}\nolimits)(\tau(\theta(a_{1})))

\displaystyle\qquad+(\hat{\mathbf{l}}_{A}(a_{1})\otimes\mathop{id}\nolimits+\mathop{id}\nolimits\otimes\hat{\mathbf{l}}_{A}(a_{1}))((\vartheta+\tau\vartheta)(a_{2})),

(2.11)

\displaystyle(\theta-\tau\theta)(a_{1}\ast a_{2})

\displaystyle=(\mathop{id}\nolimits\otimes\mathbf{r}_{A}(a_{2}))\theta(a_{1})+(\mathop{id}\nolimits\otimes\mathbf{l}_{A}(a_{1}))((\theta-\tau\theta)(a_{2}))

\displaystyle\qquad+(\hat{\mathbf{r}}_{A}(a_{2})\otimes\mathop{id}\nolimits)(\tau(\vartheta(a_{1})))-(\hat{\mathbf{l}}_{A}(a_{1})\otimes\mathop{id}\nolimits)((\vartheta+\tau\vartheta)(a_{2})).

In
[
30

, Ni and Bai have studied the bialgebra theory and Yang-Baxter equations for
Poisson algebras. A Poisson bialgebra can be characterized by a Manin triple or
a matched pair of Poisson algebras. Recently, the quasi-triangular Poisson bialgebras
and quasi-triangular pre-Poisson bialgebras have been studied in
[

A pre-Lie algebra
(A,\circ)
naturally induces a Lie algebra
(A,[-,-])
,
where
[a_{1},a_{2}]=a_{1}\circ a_{2}-a_{2}\circ a_{1}

A pre-Lie coalgebra
(A,\vartheta)

(A,\delta)
,

\delta=\vartheta-\tau\vartheta
. But a pre-Lie bialgebra
(A,\circ,\vartheta)

does not induce a Lie coalgebra
(A,[-,-],\delta)
in general

3

.
Thus, we cannot use this method to construct Poisson bialgebras using pre-Poisson bialgebras.
In
[
23
]
, Lin, Zhou and Bai have constructed Lie bialgebras by
the tensor product of pre-Lie bialgebras and perm algebras. Recall that a

perm algebra
is a pair
(B,\diamond)
, where
B
is a vector space and

\diamond:B\otimes B\rightarrow B
is a bilinear operator such that for
any
b_{1},b_{2},b_{3}\in B
,

b_{1}\diamond(b_{2}\diamond b_{3})=(b_{1}\diamond b_{2})\diamond b_{3}=(b_{2}\diamond b_{1})\diamond b_{3}.

In a perm algebra
(B,\diamond)
, wa also have
b_{1}\diamond(b_{2}\diamond b_{3})=b_{2}\diamond(b_{1}\diamond b_{3})
for any
b_{1},b_{2},b_{3}\in B
.

Proposition 2.9

(
[
23
]
)
.

Let
(A,\circ)
be a pre-Lie algebra and
(B,\diamond)
be a perm algebra.
Define a binary bracket
[-,-]
on
A\otimes B
by

(2.12)

\displaystyle[a_{1}\otimes b_{1},\;a_{2}\otimes b_{2}]=(a_{1}\circ a_{2})\otimes(b_{1}\diamond b_{2})-(a_{2}\circ a_{1})\otimes(b_{2}\diamond b_{1}),

for any
a_{1},a_{2}\in A
and
b_{1},b_{2}\in B
. Then
(A\otimes B,\ast)
is a
Lie algebra, which is called
the Lie algebra induced from
(A,\circ)

by
(B,\diamond)
.

Similar to this proposition, for the tensor product of Zinbiel algebras and perm algebras,
we have:

Proposition 2.10
.

Let
(A,\ast)
be a Zinbiel algebra and
(B,\diamond)
be a perm algebra.
Define a binary operator
\cdot
on
A\otimes B
by

(2.13)

\displaystyle(a_{1}\otimes b_{1})(a_{2}\otimes b_{2})=(a_{1}\ast a_{2})\otimes(b_{1}\diamond b_{2})+(a_{2}\ast a_{1})\otimes(b_{2}\diamond b_{1}),

for any
a_{1},a_{2}\in A
and

is a
commutative associative algebra, which is called
the commutative associative
algebra induced from
(A,\ast)
by
(B,\diamond)
.

Proof.

Clearly, the operator
\cdot
is commutative.
For any
a_{1},a_{2},a_{3}\in A
,
b_{1},b_{2},b_{3}\in B
, since

(A,\ast)
is a Zinbiel algebra and
(B,\diamond)
is a perm algebra, we have

\displaystyle\;(a_{1}\otimes b_{1})((a_{2}\otimes b_{2})(a_{3}\otimes b_{3}))-((a_{1}\otimes b_{1})(a_{2}\otimes b_{2}))(a_{3}\otimes b_{3})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\;(a_{1}\ast(a_{2}\ast a_{3}))\otimes(b_{1}\diamond(b_{2}\diamond b_{3}))+((a_{2}\ast a_{3})\ast a_{1})\otimes((b_{2}\diamond b_{3})\diamond b_{1})

\displaystyle\quad+(a_{1}\ast(a_{3}\ast a_{2}))\otimes(b_{1}\diamond(b_{3}\diamond b_{2}))+((a_{3}\ast a_{2})\ast a_{1})\otimes((b_{3}\diamond b_{2})\diamond b_{1})

\displaystyle\quad-((a_{1}\ast a_{2})\ast a_{3})\otimes((b_{1}\diamond b_{2})\diamond b_{3})-(a_{3}\ast(a_{1}\ast a_{2}))\otimes(b_{3}\diamond(b_{1}\diamond b_{2}))

\displaystyle\quad-((a_{2}\ast a_{1})\ast a_{3})\otimes((b_{2}\diamond b_{1})\diamond b_{3})-(a_{3}\ast(a_{2}\ast a_{1}))\otimes(b_{3}\diamond(b_{2}\diamond b_{1}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\;\Big(a_{1}\ast(a_{2}\ast a_{3})-(a_{1}\ast a_{2})\ast a_{3}-(a_{2}\ast a_{1})\ast a_{3}\Big)\otimes(b_{1}\diamond(b_{2}\diamond b_{3}))

\displaystyle\ \ +\Big((a_{2}\ast a_{3})\ast a_{1}+(a_{3}\ast a_{2})\ast a_{1}-a_{3}\ast(a_{2}\ast a_{1})\Big)\otimes(b_{2}\diamond(b_{3}\diamond b_{1}))\qquad\;

\displaystyle\ \ +\Big(a_{1}\ast(a_{3}\ast a_{2})-a_{3}\ast(a_{1}\ast a_{2})\Big)\otimes(b_{3}\diamond(b_{1}\diamond b_{2}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\;0.

That is,
(A,\cdot)
is a commutative associative algebra.
∎

.

\displaystyle\;[a_{1}\otimes b_{1},\;a_{2}\otimes b_{2}](a_{3}\otimes b_{3})+(a_{2}\otimes b_{2})[a_{1}\otimes b_{1},\;a_{3}\otimes b_{3}]

\displaystyle\;(a_{1}\circ(a_{2}\ast a_{3}))\otimes(b_{1}\diamond(b_{2}\diamond b_{3}))-((a_{2}\ast a_{3})\circ a_{1}\otimes((b_{2}\diamond b_{3})\diamond b_{1})

\displaystyle\quad+(a_{1}\circ(a_{3}\ast a_{2}))\otimes(b_{1}\diamond(b_{3}\diamond b_{2}))-((a_{3}\ast a_{2})\circ a_{1}\otimes((b_{3}\diamond b_{2})\diamond b_{1})

\displaystyle\;[(a_{1}\otimes b_{1}),\ \ (a_{2}\otimes b_{2})(a_{3}\otimes b_{3})].

3.
Infinite-dimensional infinitesimal bialgebras from Zinbiel bialgebras

In this section, we recall the notion of a quadratic
\mathbb{Z}
-graded perm algebra, as
a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded perm algebra equipped with an invariant bilinear form. We show that
the tensor product of a finite-dimensional Zinbiel bialgebra and a quadratic

\mathbb{Z}
-graded perm algebra can be naturally endowed with a completed infinitesimal bialgebra.
The converse of this result also holds when the quadratic
\mathbb{Z}
-graded perm algebra
is special, giving the desired characterization of the Zinbiel bialgebra
that its affinization is a completed infinitesimal bialgebra.

3.1.
Affinization of Zinbiel bialgebras

To provide the affinization characterization of Zinbiel algebra, we first recall
the notion of
\mathbb{Z}
-graded perm algebra.

Definition 3.1
.

A
\mathbb{Z}
-graded commutative associative algebra
(resp.
\mathbb{Z}
-graded perm algebra
)
is a commutative associative algebra
(A,\ast)
(resp. a perm algebra
(B,\diamond)
) with a
linear decomposition
A=\oplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}A_{i}
(resp.
B=\oplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}B_{i}
) such that
each
A_{i}
(resp.
B_{i}
) is finite-dimensional and
A_{i}\ast A_{j}\subseteq A_{i+j}

(resp.
B_{i}\diamond B_{j}\subseteq B_{i+j}
) for all
i,j\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Example 3.2

(
[
23
]
)
.

Let
B=\{f_{1}\partial_{1}+f_{2}\partial_{2}\mid f_{1},f_{2}\in\mathbf{k}[x_{1}^{\pm},x_{2}^{\pm}]\}
and define a binary operation
\diamond:B\otimes B\rightarrow B
by

\displaystyle(x_{1}^{i_{1}}x_{2}^{i_{2}}\partial_{s})\diamond(x_{1}^{j_{1}}x_{2}^{j_{2}}\partial_{t}):=\delta_{s,1}x_{1}^{i_{1}+j_{1}+1}x_{2}^{i_{2}+j_{2}}\partial_{t}+\delta_{s,2}x_{1}^{i_{1}+j_{1}}x_{2}^{i_{2}+j_{2}+1}\partial_{t},

for any
i_{1},i_{2},j_{1},j_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}
and
t\in\{1,2\}
.
Then
(B,\diamond)
is a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded perm algebra with the linear decomposition

B=\oplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}B_{i}
, where

B_{i}=\Big\{\sum_{k=1}^{2}f_{k}\partial_{k}\mid f_{k}\text{ is a homogeneous
polynomial with }\deg(f_{k})=i-1,\;k=1,2\Big\}

for all
i\in\mathbb{Z}
.

By Proposition
2.10
, we have the following proposition.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
(A,\ast)
be a finite-dimensional Zinbiel algebra and
(B=\oplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}B_{i},\diamond)
be a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded perm algebra. If we define a binary operation
\cdot
on

by Eq. (

is a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded

Moreover, if
(B=\oplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}B_{i},\diamond)
is the
\mathbb{Z}
-graded perm algebra given in

3.2
, then
(A\otimes B,\cdot)
is a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded commutative

(A,\ast)
is a Zinbiel algebra.

Proof.

By Proposition
2.10
,
(A\otimes B,\cdot)
is a commutative associative
algebra. Since
(B=\oplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}B_{i},\diamond)
is
\mathbb{Z}
-graded,
(A\otimes B,\cdot)

is a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded commutative associative algebra. If
(B=\oplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}B_{i},\diamond)

is the
\mathbb{Z}
-graded perm algebra given in Example

, then we have

\displaystyle\Big((a_{1}\otimes\partial_{1})(a_{2}\otimes\partial_{2})\Big)(a_{3}\otimes\partial_{1})

\displaystyle=\Big((a_{1}\ast a_{2})\otimes x_{1}\partial_{2})+(a_{2}\ast a_{1})\otimes x_{2}\partial_{1}\Big)(a_{3}\otimes\partial_{1})

\displaystyle=((a_{1}\ast a_{2})\ast a_{3})\otimes x_{1}x_{2}\partial_{1}+(a_{3}\ast(a_{1}\ast a_{2}))\otimes x_{1}^{2}\partial_{2}

\displaystyle\quad+((a_{2}\ast a_{1})\ast a_{3})\otimes x_{1}x_{2}\partial_{1}+(a_{3}\ast(a_{2}\ast a_{1}))\otimes x_{1}x_{2}\partial_{1},

and

\displaystyle(a_{1}\otimes\partial_{1})\Big((a_{2}\otimes\partial_{2})(a_{3}\otimes\partial_{1})\Big)

\displaystyle=(a_{1}\otimes\partial_{1})\Big((a_{2}\ast a_{3})\otimes x_{2}\partial_{1}+(a_{2}\ast a_{3})\otimes x_{1}\partial_{2}\Big)

\displaystyle=(a_{1}\ast(a_{2}\ast a_{3}))\otimes x_{1}x_{2}\partial_{1}+((a_{2}\ast a_{3})\ast a_{1})\otimes x_{1}x_{2}\partial_{1}

\displaystyle\quad+(a_{1}\ast(a_{3}\ast a_{2}))\otimes x_{1}^{2}\partial_{2}+((a_{3}\ast a_{2})\ast a_{1})\otimes x_{1}x_{2}\partial_{1}.

Comparing the coefficients of
x_{1}^{2}\partial_{2}
, we get
a_{3}\ast(a_{1}\ast a_{2})=a_{1}\ast(a_{3}\ast a_{2})

Thus,

To carry out the Zinbiel coalgebra affinization, we need to extend the codomain
of the comultiplication
\vartheta
to allow infinite sums.
Let
U=\oplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}U_{i}
and
V=\oplus_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}V_{j}
be
\mathbb{Z}
-graded vector spaces.
We call the
completed tensor product
of
U
and
V
to be the vector space

U\,\hat{\otimes}\,V:=\prod_{i,j\in\mathbb{Z}}U_{i}\otimes V_{j}.

If
U
and
V
are finite-dimensional, then
U\,\hat{\otimes}\,V
is just the usual
tensor product
U\otimes V
. In general, an element in
U\,\hat{\otimes}\,V
is an
infinite formal sum
\sum_{i,j\in\mathbb{Z}}X_{ij}
with
X_{ij}\in U_{i}\otimes V_{j}
.
So
X_{ij}=\sum_{\alpha}u_{i,\alpha}\otimes v_{j,\alpha}
for pure tensors

u_{i,\alpha}\otimes v_{j,\alpha}\in U_{i}\otimes V_{j}
with
\alpha
in a finite
index set. Thus a general term of
U\,\hat{\otimes}\,V
is a possibly infinite sum

\sum_{i,j,\alpha}u_{i\alpha}\otimes v_{j\alpha}
, where
i,j\in\mathbb{Z}
and
\alpha
is
in a finite index set (which might depend on
i,j
). With these notations, for linear
maps
f:U\rightarrow U^{\prime}
and
g:V\rightarrow V^{\prime}
, define

f\,\hat{\otimes}\,g:U\,\hat{\otimes}\,V\rightarrow U^{\prime}\,\hat{\otimes}\,V^{\prime},\qquad\sum_{i,j,\alpha}u_{i,\alpha}\otimes v_{j,\alpha}\mapsto\sum_{i,j,\alpha}f(u_{i,\alpha})\otimes g(v_{j,\alpha}).

Also the twist map
\tau
has its completion

\hat{\tau}:V\,\hat{\otimes}\,V\rightarrow V\,\hat{\otimes}\,V
,

\sum_{i,j,\alpha}u_{i,\alpha}\otimes v_{j,\alpha}\mapsto\sum_{i,j,\alpha}v_{j,\alpha}\otimes u_{i,\alpha}
.
Moreover, we define a (completed) comultiplication to be a linear map

\vartheta:V\rightarrow V\,\hat{\otimes}\,V
,

\vartheta(v):=\sum_{i,j,\alpha}v_{1,i,\alpha}\otimes v_{2,j,\alpha}
.
Then we have the well-defined map

(\vartheta\,\hat{\otimes}\,\mathop{id}\nolimits)\vartheta(v)=(\vartheta\,\hat{\otimes}\,\mathop{id}\nolimits)\Big(\sum_{i,j,\alpha}v_{1,i,\alpha}\otimes v_{2,j,\alpha}\Big):=\sum_{i,j,\alpha}\vartheta(v_{1,i,\alpha})\otimes v_{2,j,\alpha}\in V\,\hat{\otimes}\,V\,\hat{\otimes}\,V.

Definition 3.4
.

(i)

A
completed cocommutative coassociative coalgebra
is a pair

(A,\Delta)
where
A=\oplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}A_{i}
is a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded vector space and

\Delta:A\rightarrow A\,\hat{\otimes}\,A
is a linear map satisfying

\hat{\tau}\Delta=\Delta,\qquad\qquad(\Delta\,\hat{\otimes}\,\mathop{id}\nolimits)\Delta=(\mathop{id}\nolimits\,\hat{\otimes}\,\Delta)\Delta.

(ii)

is a pair
(B,\nu)
, where

B=\oplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}B_{i}
is a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded vector space and
\nu:B\rightarrow B\,\hat{\otimes}\,B

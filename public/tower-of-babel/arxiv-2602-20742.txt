Dualizing complexes for algebraic stacks
Pat Lank

Abstract
. We study dualizing complexes on algebraic stacks. In particular, we establish their existence in broad generality for Deligne–Mumford stacks of characteristic zero.

14A30 (primary), 14D23, 14F08, 18G80

1.
Introduction

1.1.
What is known

Dualizing complexes play a central role in Grothendieck duality. For schemes and algebraic spaces, they occur in the study of singularities and derived categories. As algebraic stacks arise naturally in moduli theory and birational geometry, it is important to develop a comparable duality theory in this broader setting. However, unlike the case of schemes and algebraic spaces, general existence results for dualizing complexes on algebraic stacks remain limited.

Significant progress has been made with extending Grothendieck duality to algebraic stacks. In particular,
[
18
]
established a functorial formalism for upper shriek functors
f^{!}
using the notion of concentrated morphisms
f\colon\mathcal{Y}\to\mathcal{X}
introduced by
[
9
]
. Concentrated morphisms form a class of morphisms with favorable cohomological properties. For example, if
f\colon\mathcal{Y}\to\mathcal{X}
is a concentrated morphism of algebraic stacks, then the derived pushforward
\mathbf{R}f_{\ast}\colon D_{\operatorname{qc}}(\mathcal{Y})\to D_{\operatorname{qc}}(\mathcal{X})
admits a right adjoint
f^{\times}
. In general, the functor
f^{\times}
does not always coincide with
f^{!}
.

For schemes, dualizing complexes are frequently constructed using upper shriek functors associated to structural morphisms rather than via gluing arguments. In the case of a proper morphism
f\colon Y\to X
of Noetherian schemes,
f^{!}
is naturally isomorphic to
f^{\times}
. This identification can make certain arguments easier in the scheme setting.

Motivated by this perspective, one may attempt to define a dualizing complex on an algebraic stack
\mathcal{X}
as
f^{\times}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{S}}
for a suitable concentrated morphism
f\colon\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{S}
. This viewpoint appears in
[
19
, Definition 1.22]
and behaves as
expected in certain cases. However, this construction does not work in general;
for instance, it fails for separated étale morphisms between affine Noetherian schemes that are not Gorenstein.

1.2.
What we do

In this work, dualizing complexes are defined on the lisse-étale site of the algebraic stack. Roughly, these are complexes which are dualizing smooth locally. While this notion is not new (see e.g.
[
5
, Remark 3.20(2)]
), we formalize it a bit more carefully. See
Section

3
for details.

Our first main result is the following:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
f\colon\mathcal{Y}\to\mathcal{X}
be a separated morphism of finite presentation between Deligne–Mumford
\mathbb{Q}
-stacks. Assume
\mathcal{X}
is Noetherian and has a separated diagonal. If
K
is a dualizing complex on
\mathcal{X}
, then
f^{!}K
is a dualizing complex on
\mathcal{Y}
.

In fact, we prove a more general statement; see
Theorem

4.7
. Note that we use the upper shriek functor
f^{!}
rather than the right adjoint
f^{\times}
of
\mathbf{R}f_{\ast}
. Consequently, this yields existence of dualizing complexes in broad generality.

Corollary 1.2
.

Let
S
be a Noetherian
\mathbb{Q}
-scheme admitting a dualizing complex. Then dualizing complexes exist for any Deligne–Mumford stack which is of finite presentation and separated over
S
.

Concretely, examples include every separated Deligne–Mumford stack of finite presentation over a field of characteristic zero. Notably, there are no properness constraints. Moreover, we prove a relative analog of
Theorem

1.1
:

Corollary 1.3
.

Let
f\colon\mathcal{Y}\to\mathcal{X}
be a concentrated proper Deligne–Mumford morphism of Noetherian algebraic
\mathbb{Q}
-stacks where
\mathcal{X}
has a separated diagonal. If
K
is a dualizing complex on
\mathcal{X}
, then
f^{\times}K
is a dualizing complex on
\mathcal{Y}
.

Observe that
Corollary

1.3
involves the right adjoint of the derived pushforward
\mathbf{R}f_{\ast}
. This leverages a base change result for the functors
[
18
, Lemma 0.1]
, which allows us to reduce to a setting where
f^{\times}\cong f^{!}
. In particular, the upper shriek exists in said reduction, whereas it is not clear whether it does in the generality of
Corollary

1.3
a priori.

The proofs combine Nagata compactification techniques with upper shriek base change formulas of
[
18
, Theorem 1.8]
. Nagata compactifications for algebraic spaces were established in
[
2
]
, whereas the Deligne–Mumford case relies on forthcoming work by
[
26
]
. Whenever possible, we reduce arguments to the algebraic space setting, allowing us to invoke results such as
[
15
, Lemma 2.7]
. The restriction to
\mathbb{Q}
-stacks is essential for the applications considered here with compactifications (see
Item

4.2(3)
). Extending these existence results to mixed and positive characteristic remains an open direction.

1.3.
Why it matters

We expect our results to extend further as the theory of Nagata compactifications develops, for example in positive characteristic. Moreover, there is growing interest in birational geometry and the minimal model program for algebraic spaces and stacks over
\mathbb{Q}
; see e.g.
[
10
,
5
,
15
]
. As singularities are studied in these frameworks, the existence of dualizing complexes will play an increasingly important role, for which our results also become increasingly relevant.

2.1.
Categories

Denote by
\operatorname{Mod}(\mathcal{X})
for the Grothendieck abelian category of sheaves of
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}}
-modules on the lisse-étale site of
\mathcal{X}
. Set
\operatorname{Qcoh}(\mathcal{X})
(resp.
\operatorname{coh}(\mathcal{X})
) as the strictly full subcategory of
\operatorname{Mod}(\mathcal{X})
consisting of quasi-coherent (resp. coherent) sheaves. Naturally, let
D(\mathcal{X}):=D(\operatorname{Mod}(\mathcal{X}))
for the derived category of
\operatorname{Mod}(\mathcal{X})
. Denote by
D_{\operatorname{qc}}(\mathcal{X})
(resp.
D^{b}_{\operatorname{coh}}(\mathcal{X})
) the full subcategory of
D(\mathcal{X})
consisting of complexes with quasi-coherent cohomology sheaves (resp. bounded complexes with coherent cohomology). If
\mathcal{X}
is Noetherian, then
\operatorname{coh}(\mathcal{X})
is the strictly full subcategory of
\operatorname{Mod}(\mathcal{X})
consisting of coherent sheaves. Moreover,
D^{b}_{\operatorname{coh}}(\mathcal{X})
denotes the full subcategory of
D(\mathcal{X})
consisting of bounded complexes with coherent cohomology.

2.2.
Resolution property

Recall that
\mathcal{X}
is said to satisfy the
resolution property
if every quasi-coherent
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}}
-module is a quotient of a locally free sheaf. In the case of Noetherianity, this is equivalent to requiring that every coherent sheaf be a quotient of a coherent locally free sheaf (see e.g.
[
7
, Remark 4.2]
). Moreover, one says that
\mathcal{X}
is
basic
if it is the quotient stack
[U/\operatorname{GL}_{n}]
, where
U
is a quasi-affine scheme equipped with an action of
\operatorname{GL}_{n}
(see
[
25
, Definition 2.1]
). Classically,
[
28
]
showed that Noetherian basic stacks satisfy the resolution property, while the converse was established for normal Noetherian algebraic stacks in
[
29
, Theorem 1.1]
. More generally (see
[
6
, Theorem 6.3.1]
and
[
7
, Theorem A]
), a quasi-compact quasi-separated algebraic stack is basic if and only if it has the resolution property and its closed points have affine stabilizers.

2.3.
Functors

Given a morphism of algebraic stacks
f\colon\mathcal{Y}\to\mathcal{X}
, it need not induce a morphism on the lisse-étale sites whose inverse image functor is left exact. See e.g.
[
21
, Example 3.4]
and
[
1
, 5.3.12]
. However, the morphism being smooth ensures that the induced morphism on lisse-étale sites has a left exact inverse image functor (in fact, it is exact, see comment above
[
21
, Example 3.4]
).

More generally, let
f\colon\mathcal{Y}\to\mathcal{X}
be a morphism of algebraic stacks. There is the derived pushforward
\mathbf{R}(f_{\textrm{lis-\'{e}t}})_{\ast}\colon D(\mathcal{Y})\to D(\mathcal{X})
. Typically, the restriction of
\mathbf{R}(f_{\textrm{lis-\'{e}t}})_{\ast}
to
D_{\operatorname{qc}}(\mathcal{Y})
does not factor through
D_{\operatorname{qc}}(\mathcal{X})
, see e.g.
[
27
,
Tag 07DC
]
. Moreover, as discussed above, if

is smooth, then
\mathbf{R}(f_{\textrm{lis-\'{e}t}})_{\ast}
admits a left adjoint
\mathbf{L}f_{\textrm{lis-\'{e}t}}^{\ast}\colon D(\mathcal{X})\to D(\mathcal{Y})
.

Now, consider the adjoint pair of derived functors
\mathbf{L}f_{\ast}
and
\mathbf{R}f_{\ast}
on
D_{\operatorname{qc}}
. See
[
9
, §1.3]
for their construction via hypercoverings and simplicial sites. Recall that
f
is called
concentrated
if it is quasi-compact, quasi-separated, and if the derived pushforward of any base change along a quasi-compact quasi-separated morphism has finite cohomological dimension. Here, ‘finite cohomological dimension’ means there is an
n\geq 0
such that for all
i>n
and
E\in\operatorname{Qcoh}(\mathcal{X})
, one has
\mathbf{R}^{j}f_{\ast}E\cong 0
(equivalently, by
[
9
, Lemma 1.2(2)]
,
\mathbf{R}^{j}(f_{\textrm{lis-\'{e}t}})_{\ast}E\cong 0
).

There are many cases of morphisms which are concentrated, e.g. those which are representable by algebraic spaces (see
[
9
, Lemma 2.5(3)]
). Furthermore, concentrated morphism includes any morphism of quasi-compact quasi-separated Deligne–Mumford
\mathbb{Q}
-stacks (e.g. use
[
9
, Lemma 2.5(2)]
with
[
8
, Theorem C]
). Moreover, if
f
is concentrated, then the right adjoint of exists by
[
9
, Theorem 4.14]
. It is denoted by
f^{\times}\colon D_{\operatorname{qc}}(\mathcal{X})\to D_{\operatorname{qc}}(\mathcal{Y})
. See
[
18
]
for details on this functor. Also, if
f
is concentrated, then the restriction of
\mathbf{R}(f_{\textrm{lis\'{e}t}})_{\ast}
on

agrees with
\mathbf{R}f_{\ast}
, see
[

Lemma 3.6
.

Let

be a concentrated smooth morphism of algebraic stacks. Then the restriction of
\mathbf{L}f^{\ast}_{\textrm{lis-\'{e}t}}
to

\mathbf{L}f^{\ast}

Proof.

The morphism being smooth ensures that the induced morphism on lisse-étale sites has a left exact inverse image functor (in fact, it is exact, see above

\mathbf{L}f^{\ast}_{\textrm{lis-\'{e}t}}\colon D(\mathcal{X})\to D(\mathcal{Y})

Now, for any

Since

\mathbf{R}(f_{\textrm{lis-\'{e}t}})_{\ast}

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
f\colon\mathcal{Y}\to\mathcal{X}
be a concentrated morphism of algebraic stacks. Then the restriction of
f^{\times}
on
D^{+}_{\operatorname{qc}}(\mathcal{X})
factors through
D^{+}_{\operatorname{qc}}(\mathcal{Y})
.

Proof.

We mimic the proof of
[
27
,
Tag 0E56
]
, but need a few details. Since
f
has finite cohomological dimension, there is an
N\geq 0
such that for all
M\in\operatorname{Qcoh}(\mathcal{Y})
and
i>N
, one has
\mathbf{R}^{i}f_{\ast}M\cong 0
. Now, let
E\in D^{+}_{\operatorname{qc}}(\mathcal{X})
. Using the spectral sequence (see e.g.
[

,
Tag 015J
]
),

\mathbf{R}^{p}f_{\ast}\mathcal{H}^{q}(E)\implies\mathbf{R}^{p+q}f_{\ast}E

and the vanishing of
\mathbf{R}^{p}f_{\ast}\mathcal{H}^{q}(E)
for

, it follows that
\mathbf{R}^{j}f_{\ast}E\cong 0
if
j>N
. Consequently, if

satisfies
\mathcal{H}^{j}(L)\cong 0
for

, then there is a

such that
\mathbf{R}^{i}f_{\ast}L\cong 0
for

. So, for any such

satisfying

if

\mathcal{H}^{i}(f^{\times}L)=0

Proposition 4.6
.

Let
\mathbb{S}_{\mathbf{e}}
be a
2
-subcategory as in
4.1
which is closed under étale morphisms to objects; that is, if
\mathcal{X}\in\mathbb{S}_{\mathbf{e}}
and
\mathcal{Y}\to\mathcal{X}
is étale, then
\mathcal{Y}\in\mathbb{S}_{\mathbf{e}}
. Consider a Deligne-Mumford morphism
(f\colon\mathcal{X}\to S)\in\mathbb{S}_{\mathbf{e}}
of presentation from an algebraic stack to an algebraic space. If
K
is a dualizing complex on
S
, then
f^{!}K
is a dualizing complex on
\mathcal{X}
.

Proof.

As
f
admits a Nagata compactification,
Lemma

4.4
allows us to reduce to the case
f
is universally quasi-proper and of finite type. Indeed,
f=g\circ j
where
g
is universally quasi-proper and of finite type, whereas
j
is a dominant flat monomorphism. The definition of
\mathbb{S}_{\mathbf{e}}
requires
j,g
to be
1
-morphisms in this
2
-category. Note that monomorphisms are separated and representable by algebraic spaces
[
27
,
Tag 04ZW
&
Tag 06MY
]
. Moreover, as
f
is finitely presented,
j
must be of finite type (see e.g.
[
27
,
Tag 050Y
&
Tag 06U9
]
). So, if the desired claim holds for
g
,
Lemma

4.4
implies
j^{!}g^{!}K
is a dualizing complex on
\mathcal{X}
. However,
Item

4.3(1)
tells us
j^{!}g^{!}K\cong(g\circ j)^{!}K\cong f^{!}K
, and so we may make the reduction.

Let
s\colon U\to\mathcal{X}
be an étale surjective morphism from an affine scheme.
Note that
f\circ s
is separated. Since
f
is separated, it follows that
s
is separated (see
[
27
,
Tag 050M
]
). Consider the fibered square

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{U\times_{\mathcal{X}}U}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces U}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces U}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{X}.}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces q}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces s}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces s}

As
q
is a separated étale morphism surjective with Noetherian scheme target, it must be quasi-affine (see
[
20
, Proposition 3.1]
). Hence,
U\times_{\mathcal{X}}U
is a quasi-affine Noetherian scheme. Moreover, each morphism above is separated, of finite type, and representable by algebraic spaces. Additionally, every object has quasi-finite and separated diagonal.

Now, we prove the claim. Towards that end, we want to leverage
4.3
. Observe that the diagram above is a diagram of
1
-morphisms in the
2
-category
\mathbb{S}_{\mathbf{e}}
. Moreover, by definition of
\mathbb{S}_{\mathbf{e}}
, every morphism is concentrated. By
[
15
, Lemma 2.7]
, we know that
(f\circ s\circ p)^{!}K
is a dualizing complex on
U\times_{\mathcal{X}}U
and
(s\circ p)^{!}K
is a dualizing on
U
. As
p
and
q
are separated étale morphisms of finite type between Noetherian schemes,
[
27
,
Tag 0FWI
]
tells us that there are natural isomorphisms
\mathbf{L}p^{\ast}\cong p^{!}
and
\mathbf{L}q^{\ast}\cong q^{!}
on
D^{+}_{\operatorname{qc}}
. Moreover,
Item

4.3(3)
implies there is a natural isomorphism
q^{!}\mathbf{L}s^{\ast}\cong\mathbf{L}p^{\ast}s^{!}
on
D_{\operatorname{qc}}
. Furthermore,
Lemma

4.5
promises that
f^{\times}K\in D^{+}_{\operatorname{qc}}(\mathcal{X})
because
K\in D^{b}_{\operatorname{coh}}(S)
(see
Remark

3.5
). Yet,
Item

4.3(2)
tells us
f^{\times}K\cong f^{!}K
because

is universally proper and of finite type. Hence,
\mathbf{L}s^{\ast}f^{!}K\in D^{+}_{\operatorname{coh}}(U)
, and so,
\mathbf{L}q^{\ast}\mathbf{L}s^{\ast}f^{!}K\cong q^{!}\mathbf{L}s^{\ast}f^{!}K

we know that there is a natural isomorphism

However,

. Since

must be a dualizing complex on

by

3.8
.
∎

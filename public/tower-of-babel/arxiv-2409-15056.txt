A Heuristic approach to the Iwasawa theory of elliptic curves
Katharina Müller, Anwesh Ray

Abstract

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Background and motivation

Let
p
be a prime and
K
a number field. The
cyclotomic
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension

K_{\operatorname{cyc}}
is the unique
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension of
K
contained in
K(\mu_{p^{\infty}})
. Let
K_{n}/K
be the subextension with
[K_{n}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}K]=p^{n}
and
h_{p}(K_{n})
the
p
-part of its class number, so
h_{p}(K_{n})=p^{e_{n}}
. Iwasawa proved that for large
n
,

e_{n}=p^{n}\mu_{p}(K)+n\lambda_{p}(K)+\nu_{p}(K),

where
\mu_{p}(K),\lambda_{p}(K)\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
and
\nu_{p}(K)\in\mathbb{Z}
. He conjectured
\mu_{p}(K)=0
for all
K
and
p
, later proven by Ferrero and Washington for abelian extensions of
\mathbb{Q}
.

Elliptic curves, fundamental in arithmetic geometry, are smooth projective genus-one curves with a distinguished point. For a number field
K
, the group of
K
-rational points
E(K)
is finitely generated by the Mordell–Weil theorem, decomposing into a torsion subgroup and a free part of rank
r
, the Mordell–Weil rank. This rank is central to the study of elliptic curves and relates to the arithmetic of Selmer groups
\operatorname{Sel}_{p}(E/K)
, whose dimension gives an upper bound on
r
. In Iwasawa theory, elliptic curves are studied over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extensions. For
E/\mathbb{Q}
and a prime
p
where
E
has good ordinary reduction, the
p
-primary Selmer group
\operatorname{Sel}_{p^{\infty}}(E/\mathbb{Q}_{\operatorname{cyc}})
is conjectured to be cofinitely generated and torsion over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}[[T]]
.
Kato
[
16
]
proved that
\operatorname{Sel}_{p^{\infty}}(E/\mathbb{Q}_{\operatorname{cyc}})
is a cofinitely generated and cotorsion module over the Iwasawa algebra. The
\mu
-invariant of the Selmer group is
0
if it is cofinitely generated as a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-module. Let
E[p]
be the
p
-torsion subgroup of
E(\bar{\mathbb{Q}})
and

\rho_{E,p}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{Gal}(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})\rightarrow\operatorname{Aut}(E[p])\xrightarrow{\sim}\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p})

be the Galois representation on
E[p]
, also called the
residual representation
. The following conjecture of Greenberg will be the primary focus of this article.

Conjecture 1.1
.

Let
p
be an odd prime number and
E
be an elliptic curve over
\mathbb{Q}
. Assume that
E
has good ordinary reduction at
p
and that
\rho_{E,p}
is irreducible. Then, the
p
-primary Selmer group
\operatorname{Sel}_{p^{\infty}}(E/\mathbb{Q}_{\operatorname{cyc}})
is a cofinitely generated
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-module.

A theorem of Duke
[
8
]
states that a density
1
set of such elliptic curves have the property that
\rho_{E,p}
is surjective. Greenberg’s conjecture thus has the following conjectural implication for elliptic curves on average.

Conjecture 1.2

(
\mu=0
on average)
.

Let
p
be a fixed odd prime number. Then, for most elliptic curves
E_{/\mathbb{Q}}
with good ordinary reduction at
p
, the Selmer group
\operatorname{Sel}_{p^{\infty}}(E/\mathbb{Q}_{\operatorname{cyc}})
is cofinitely generated over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
.

Heuristic reasoning has been profoundly influential in the field of arithmetic statistics, offering insightful conjectures and guiding intuition. A cornerstone in this domain is the Cohen-Lenstra heuristics, introduced by Cohen and Lenstra in
[
7
]
, which propose a probabilistic model for the distribution of class groups of quadratic number fields. Passing from number fields
K
and their
p
-class groups
\operatorname{Cl}_{p}(K)
to Selmer groups of elliptic curves
E
over
\mathbb{Q}
, Heath–Brown
[
13
,
14
]
, Swinnerton–Dyer
[
23
]
and Kane
[
15
]
obtained the distribution for the
\mathbb{F}_{2}
-dimension of the
2
-Selmer group of
E
. Poonen and Rains
[
19
]
studied heuristics for the distribution of
p
-Selmer group of an elliptic curve over
\mathbb{Q}
to the intersection of two maximal isotropic subspaces in an inner product space. Inspired by such developments, we provide further evidence for Conjecture
1.2
via a heuristic approach. Assuming that such intersections are suitably random, the average size of the Selmer groups in question can thus be predicted. This heuristic leads to the following predictions, cf.
[
19
, Conjecture 1.1 and 1.2]
:

(1)

\#\operatorname{Sel}_{p}(E/\mathbb{Q})
has average size
(p+1)
for any prime
p
.

(2)

Asymptotically,
1/2
of elliptic curves over
\mathbb{Q}
have rank
0
and
1/2
have rank
1
.

(3)

As
E
varies over all elliptic curves over
\mathbb{Q}
,

\operatorname{Prob}\left(\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}\operatorname{Sel}_{p}(E/\mathbb{Q})=d\right)=\prod_{j\geq 0}\left(1+\frac{1}{p^{j}}\right)\times\prod_{j=1}^{d}\left(\frac{p}{p^{j}-1}\right).

When
p\leq 5
, part (1) of the above conjecture was proven by Bhargava and Shankar
[
2
,
3
,
1
]
. Moreover, they show that there is a positive density of elliptic curves
E_{/\mathbb{Q}}
with Mordell–Weil rank
0
. Bhargava and Skinner
[
4
]
prove that a positive density of elliptic curves
E_{/\mathbb{Q}}
have rank
1
.
In this article, we explore the
\mu=0
conjecture, particularly in an "average" sense, by extending the heuristic of Poonen and Rains. The
\mu
-invariant of an elliptic curve
E/\mathbb{Q}
is
0
if and only if the
residual Greenberg Selmer group

\operatorname{Sel}^{\operatorname{Gr}}(E[p]/\mathbb{Q}_{\operatorname{cyc}})
(cf. (
3.1
)) is finite. Let
\Lambda
denote the
Iwasawa algebra
of
\Gamma\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}_{\operatorname{cyc}}/\mathbb{Q})
, which is a formal power series ring. We consider the quotient algebra
\Omega\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\Lambda/(p)
, which can be identified with the ring of formal power series
\mathbb{F}_{p}\llbracket T\rrbracket
. The structure of the Selmer group
\operatorname{Sel}_{p^{\infty}}(E/\mathbb{Q}_{\operatorname{cyc}})
is closely related to the
fine Selmer group

R_{p^{\infty}}(E/\mathbb{Q}_{\operatorname{cyc}})
. The fine Selmer group is defined using more stringent local conditions and plays a key role in Iwasawa theory.

A conjecture due to Coates and Sujatha (cf.
[
6
, Conjecture A]
) predicts that
R_{p^{\infty}}(E/\mathbb{Q}_{\operatorname{cyc}})
is cofinitely generated as a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-module for all elliptic curves over
\mathbb{Q}
with good reduction at
p
. This conjecture is seen to follow as a consequence of Iwasawa’s classical
\mu=0
conjecture (see
[
6
, Corollary 3.5]
). The vanishing of the
\mu
-invariant of
R_{p^{\infty}}(E/\mathbb{Q}_{\operatorname{cyc}})
is captured by the residual fine Selmer group
R(E[p]/\mathbb{Q}_{\operatorname{cyc}})
. This is a subgroup of the residual Greenberg Selmer group, which is finite if and only if the
\mu
-invariant of
R_{p^{\infty}}(E/\mathbb{Q}_{\operatorname{cyc}})
is
0
(cf. Proposition
2.5
for further details).

1.2.
A heuristic for the vanishing of the
\mu
-invariant on average

We describe the steps leading up to our heuristic.

Step 1:

Let
E
be an elliptic curve over
\mathbb{Q}
with good ordinary reduction at
p
. Define
\Pi_{E}
to be the quotient of residual Selmer groups
\Pi_{E}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\frac{\operatorname{Sel}^{\operatorname{Gr}}(E[p]/\mathbb{Q}_{\operatorname{cyc}})}{R(E[p]/\mathbb{Q}_{\operatorname{cyc}})}
. If
\Pi_{E}
is finite, then the Conjecture A of Coates and Sujatha implies that the
\mu
-invariant of
\operatorname{Sel}_{p^{\infty}}(E/\mathbb{Q}_{\operatorname{cyc}})
vanishes.

Step 2:

Let
\Sigma
be the set of primes
\ell
such that either
\ell\neq p
is a prime at which
E
has bad reduction, or,
\ell=p
. Then we set

V(E)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\bigoplus_{\ell\in\Sigma}\left(\bigoplus_{v|\ell}H^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{\operatorname{cyc},v},E[p])\right),

see (
3.4
). The
\Omega
-module
V(E)
comes equipped with a pairing

(,)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}V(E)\times V(E)\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{p},

that is symmetric, non-degenerate and bilinear with respect to the action of
\Omega
.

Step 3:

The next step is to show that
\Pi_{E}
is isomorphic to the intersection of two
\Omega
-modules
V_{1}(E)
and
V_{2}(E)
, both of
\Omega
corank
1
(see Proposition
3.8
). The finiteness of
\Pi_{E}
can be interpreted as a question about the finiteness of the intersection of two corank-1
\Omega
-modules. Conjecture A of Coates and Sujatha, and the finiteness of
V_{1}(E)\cap V_{2}(E)
implies that the
\mu
-invariant of the Selmer group of
E
vanishes, see Lemma
3.7
.

Step 4:

By considering projections of the modules
V_{1}(E)
and
V_{2}(E)
onto certain summands of
V(E)
, we reduce the problem to studying the finiteness of intersections of two submodules
M_{1}
and
M_{2}
of
(\Omega^{\vee})^{2}
for which
M_{i}\simeq\Omega^{\vee}
. We refer to Proposition
3.8
for further details.

Our heuristic is based on the idea that as
E
is allowed to vary over elliptic curves with good ordinary reduction at
p
, the pairs
(M_{1},M_{2})
are equidistributed with respect to a natural probability measure on all such pairs. Our calculations in section
4
show that the event of their intersection being finite occurs with probability 1, see Theorem
4.8
. This heuristic argument supports Conjecture
1.2
, which states that on average, the
\mu
-invariant of elliptic curves over
\mathbb{Q}
which good ordinary reduction at
p
is
0
.
We now make the heuristic precise. Let
M_{1}
and
M_{2}
be
\Omega
-submodules of corank
1
contained in
(\Omega^{\vee})^{2}
. For each
i
, let
N_{i}\subset\Omega^{2}
be the submodule such that
M_{i}^{\vee}=\Omega^{2}/N_{i}
. Given a natural number
n\geq 1
, define
\Omega_{n}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\Omega/(T^{n})
. A submodule
N\subset\Omega^{2}
(resp.
N\subset\Omega_{n}^{2}
) is said to be
maximal
if it is not contained in
T\Omega^{2}
(resp.
T\Omega_{n}^{2}
). Any cyclic submodule of
\Omega^{2}
is contained in a unique maximal submodule. Let
\mathcal{M}
(resp.
\mathcal{M}_{n}
) denote the space of pairs of maximal cyclic submodules
(N_{1},N_{2})
contained in
\Omega^{2}\times\Omega^{2}
(resp.
\Omega_{n}^{2}\times\Omega_{n}^{2}
). The spaces
\mathcal{M}_{n}
form an inverse system under the natural projection maps
\mathcal{M}_{n}\to\mathcal{M}_{m}
for
n\geq m
, allowing us to identify
\mathcal{M}
with the inverse limit
\varprojlim_{n}\mathcal{M}_{n}
. We equip
\mathcal{M}
with a probability measure
\mathbb{P}
, defined as the inverse limit of the uniform probability measures
\mathbb{P}_{n}
on each
\mathcal{M}_{n}
.

Now, let
E
be an elliptic curve defined over
\mathbb{Q}
. There exists a unique global minimal Weierstrass model of the form

E_{A,B}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}y^{2}=x^{3}+Ax+B,

where
(A,B)
is a pair of integers satisfying the discriminant condition
\Delta_{A,B}=4A^{3}+27B^{2}\neq 0
along with the minimality conditions
\ell^{4}\nmid A
and
\ell^{6}\nmid B
for every prime
\ell
. The naive height of
E_{A,B}
is given by

\operatorname{Ht}(E_{A,B})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\max\{|A|^{3},|B|^{2}\}.

For a positive real number
x
, define
\mathcal{C}(x)
to be the set of all minimal Weierstrass models
E_{A,B}
satisfying
\operatorname{Ht}(E_{A,B})\leq x
.

Fix an odd prime
p
and define
\mathcal{T}_{p}(x)
as the subset of
\mathcal{C}(x)
consisting of curves with good ordinary reduction at
p
such that
V_{1}(E)\cap V_{2}(E)
is infinite. Similarly, define
\mathcal{S}_{p}(x)
as the subset of
\mathcal{C}(x)
consisting of curves with good ordinary reduction at
p
for which the
\mu
-invariant of
\operatorname{Sel}_{p^{\infty}}(E/\mathbb{Q}_{\mathrm{cyc}})
is nonzero.

Heuristic 1.3
.

With respect to notation above,

\lim_{x\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\#\mathcal{T}_{p}(x)}{\#\mathcal{C}(x)}\leq\mathbb{P}\left(\{(N_{1},N_{2})\in\mathcal{M}\mid N_{1}\cap N_{2}\text{ is infinite}\}\right).

We now state the main result of the article.

Theorem A
.

Assume the Heuristic
1.3
and Conjecture A of Coates and Sujatha
[
6
]
for the vanishing of the
\mu
-invariant for the fine Selmer group of any elliptic curve over
\mathbb{Q}
. Then, we have that

\lim_{x\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\#\mathcal{S}_{p}(x)}{\#\mathcal{C}(x)}=0.

Our results suggest a broader validity of the conjecture on the average vanishing of the
\mu
-invariant, motivating further investigation in more generalized settings. With the growing interest in heuristic models in arithmetic statistics, it is only natural that they will further enrich the interplay between arithmetic statistics and Iwasawa theory. Such investigations will potentially remain a fertile ground for future research.

2.
Iwasawa theory of Selmer groups

In this section, we discuss the Iwasawa theory of elliptic curves. For a more comprehensive treatment of the subject, we refer to
[
5
,
12
]
.

2.1.
Selmer groups associated to elliptic curves

Let
E
be an elliptic curve over
\mathbb{Q}
and
p
be an odd prime number. Let
K
be a number field and set
\operatorname{G}_{K}
to denote the absolute Galois group
\operatorname{Gal}(\bar{K}/K)
and let
\Omega_{K}
be the finite primes of
K
. For each prime
v\in\Omega_{K}
, choose an embedding
\iota_{v}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\bar{K}\hookrightarrow\bar{K}_{v}
. Setting
\operatorname{G}_{K_{v}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\operatorname{Gal}(\bar{K}_{v}/K_{v})
, find that
\iota_{v}
induces an inclusion at the level of Galois groups
\operatorname{G}_{K_{v}}\hookrightarrow\operatorname{G}_{K}
. For
F\in\{K,K_{v}\}
, set

H^{i}(F,\cdot)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=H^{i}(\operatorname{G}_{F},\cdot)

and consider the Kummer sequence

0\rightarrow\frac{E(F)}{p^{n}E(F)}\xrightarrow{\kappa_{F}}H^{1}(F,E[p^{n}])\xrightarrow{\theta_{F}}H^{1}(F,E)[p^{n}]\rightarrow 0.

The
p^{n}
Selmer group
\operatorname{Sel}_{p^{n}}(E/K)
consists of all classes
f\in H^{1}(K,E[p^{n}])
such that
\operatorname{res}_{v}(f)\in\operatorname{Image}(\kappa_{K_{v}})
for all primes
v\in\Omega_{K}
. The
p^{\infty}
Selmer group is defined to be the direct limit

\operatorname{Sel}_{p^{\infty}}(E/K)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\operatorname{Sel}_{p^{n}}(E/K).

Recall that the Selmer group defined above fits into a natural short exact sequence

(2.1)

0\rightarrow E(K)\otimes\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}\rightarrow\operatorname{Sel}_{p^{\infty}}(E/K)\rightarrow\Sh(E/K)[p^{\infty}]\rightarrow 0,

where
\Sh(E/K)
denotes the Tate-Shafarevich group of
E
over
K
. The Tate-Shafarevich group
\Sh(E/K)
is conjectured to be finite. If
\Sh(E/K)[p^{\infty}]
is finite, then,

\operatorname{corank}\operatorname{Sel}_{p^{\infty}}(E/K)=\operatorname{rank}E(K).

We let
\mathbb{Q}(\mu_{p^{\infty}})
denote the extension of
\mathbb{Q}
that is generated by the
p
-primary roots of unity and set
\mathbb{Q}_{\operatorname{cyc}}\subset\mathbb{Q}(\mu_{p^{\infty}})
to denote the cyclotomic
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension of
\mathbb{Q}
. Let
\Gamma\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}_{\operatorname{cyc}}/\mathbb{Q})
and choose a topological generator
\gamma\in\Gamma
. Let
\mathbb{Q}_{n}\subset\mathbb{Q}_{\operatorname{cyc}}
denote the
n-th layer
, i.e., the subfield for which
[\mathbb{Q}_{n}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathbb{Q}]=p^{n}
. We identify the Galois group
\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}_{n}/\mathbb{Q})
with
\Gamma_{n}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\Gamma/\Gamma^{p^{n}}
. We analyze the structure of Selmer groups over the cyclotomic
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension of
\mathbb{Q}
, defined as follows

\operatorname{Sel}_{p^{\infty}}(E/\mathbb{Q}_{\operatorname{cyc}})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\varinjlim_{n}\operatorname{Sel}_{p^{\infty}}(E/\mathbb{Q}_{n}).

The Iwasawa algebra
\Lambda
is the completed group ring

\Lambda\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\varprojlim_{n}\mathbb{Z}_{p}[\Gamma_{n}].

Let
\gamma
be a topological generator of
\Gamma
and set
T\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=(\gamma-1)
. Then
\Lambda
may be identified with the formal power series ring
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket T\rrbracket
. Let
M
and
M^{\prime}
be finitely generated and torsion
\Lambda
-modules, then
M
is pseudo-isomorphic to
M^{\prime}
if there is a
\Lambda
-module map
f\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}M\rightarrow M^{\prime}
whose kernel and cokernel are finite. In this context, the structure theorem
[
24
, Ch. 13]
asserts that any finitely generated and torsion
\Lambda
-module
M
is pseudo-isomorphic to
M^{\prime}
, which is of the form

M^{\prime}=\left(\bigoplus_{i=1}^{s}\Lambda/(p^{m_{i}})\right)\oplus\left(\bigoplus_{j=1}^{t}\Lambda/(f_{j})\right).

Here,
m_{i}
are positive integers and
f_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}[T]
are distinguished polynomials, i.e., monic polynomials whose non-leading coefficients are divisible by
p
. Then the
\mu
-invariant of
M
is defined to be
\mu(M)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\sum_{i=1}^{s}m_{i}
, with the understanding that
\mu(M)=0
when
s=0
. The
\lambda
-invariant on the other hand is the sum
\lambda(M)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\sum_{i=1}^{t}\operatorname{deg}f_{j}
and taken to be
0
if
t=0
. The Iwasawa invariants are well defined, i.e., independent of the choice of module
M^{\prime}
and its decomposition into cyclic
\Lambda
-modules. It is easy to see that
\mu(M)=0
if and only if
M
is finitely generated as a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-module. Furthermore, the
\lambda
-invariant is given by
\lambda(M)=\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}(M)
.

A discrete,
p
-primary
\Lambda
-module
M
has a Pontryagin dual given by

M^{\vee}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\left(M,\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}\right).

We say that
M
is cofinitely generated (resp. cotorsion) as a
\Lambda
-module if
M^{\vee}
is finitely generated (resp. torsion). The
p
-primary Selmer group
\operatorname{Sel}_{p^{\infty}}(E/\mathbb{Q}_{\operatorname{cyc}})
is a
p
-primary discrete module over
\Lambda
. Moreover, if
E
has good ordinary reduction at
p
, the Selmer group
\operatorname{Sel}_{p^{\infty}}(E/\mathbb{Q}_{\operatorname{cyc}})
is a cofinitely generated and cotorsion as a
\Lambda
-module. This follows from results of Kato
[
16
]
. Throughout the rest of this article, we shall assume that
E
is an elliptic curve over
\mathbb{Q}
with good ordinary reduction at
p
. The
\mu
and
\lambda
-invariants of the dual Selmer group
\operatorname{Sel}_{p^{\infty}}(E/\mathbb{Q}_{\operatorname{cyc}})^{\vee}
are well defied and denoted by
\mu_{p}(E)
and
\lambda_{p}(E)
respectively.

The Galois representation on
E[p]
is denoted by

\rho_{E,p}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q}}\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p}).

The prime
p
is exceptional for
E
if
\rho_{E,p}
is not surjective. Serre’s open image theorem implies that if
E
is an elliptic curve over
\mathbb{Q}
which is not CM, then, there are only finitely many exceptional primes.

Conjecture 2.1

(Greenberg)
.

Let
E_{/\mathbb{Q}}
be an elliptic curve for which
\rho_{E,p}
is irreducible. Then,
\mu_{p}(E)=0
.

Let
p
be a fixed prime number. It is natural to ask how often
\rho_{E,p}
is irreducible? Let us make this notion precise. Given an elliptic curve
E_{/\mathbb{Q}}
there is a unique global minimal Weierstrass model

E_{A,B}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}y^{2}=x^{3}+Ax+B,

where
(A,B)
is a pair of integer for which
\Delta_{A,B}=4A^{3}+27B^{2}\neq 0
, and
\ell^{4}\nmid A
or
\ell^{6}\nmid B
for all primes
\ell
. The naive height of
E_{A,B}
is defined as follows

\operatorname{Ht}(E_{A,B})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\operatorname{max}\{|A|^{3},|B|^{2}\}.

Let
x>0
be a positive real number and set to be the set of minimal Weierstrass models
E_{A,B}
such that
\operatorname{Ht}(E_{A,B})\leq x
. There is a natural identification of
\mathcal{C}(x)
with the following set:

\begin{Bmatrix}&\mathinner{\!\left\lvert A\right\rvert}\leq\sqrt[3]{X},\ \mathinner{\!\left\lvert B\right\rvert}\leq\sqrt{X}\\
(A,B)\in\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}&\quad 4A^{3}+27B^{2}\neq 0\\
&\textrm{for all primes }\ell\textrm{ if }\ell^{4}|A,\textrm{then }\ell^{6}\nmid B\end{Bmatrix}.

Given a set
S
of elliptic curves
E_{/\mathbb{Q}}
, set
S(x)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=S\cap\mathcal{C}(x)
. The density of
S
is defined as the following limit

\mathfrak{d}(S)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\lim_{x\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\#S(x)}{\#\mathcal{C}(x)},

provided it exists. The upper (resp. lower) limit
\overline{\mathfrak{d}}(S)
(resp.
\underline{\mathfrak{d}}(S)
) is defined by replacing the above limit with
\limsup
(resp.
\liminf
). We say that
S
consists of
c\%
of elliptic curves if
\mathfrak{d}(S)
exists and equals
c/100
. On the other hand, we say that
S
consists of at least (resp. at most)
c\%
of elliptic curves if
\underline{\mathfrak{d}}(S)\geq c/100
(resp.
\overline{\mathfrak{d}}(S)\leq c/100
).

\delta_{k_{1},k_{2}}=\begin{cases}&1\text{ if }k_{1}=k_{2};\\
&0\text{ if }k_{1}\neq k_{2}.\\
\end{cases}

(\gamma^{k_{1}}a,\gamma^{k_{2}}b)=\delta_{k_{1},k_{2}}(a,b)\text{ and }(\gamma^{k_{1}}e_{i},\gamma^{k_{2}}f_{i})=\delta_{k_{1},k_{2}}(a,b).

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
W
be a maximal isotropic submodule of
V(E)
. Then,
W
decomposes as

W=\Omega^{\vee}c\oplus W_{\operatorname{tors}},

where
\Omega^{\vee}c
is contained in
\Omega^{\vee}a\oplus\Omega^{\vee}b
and
W_{\operatorname{tors}}
is a torsion
\Omega
-module.

Proof.

Clearly,
W
has
\Omega
-corank equal to
1
. Thus,

W^{\vee}\cong\Omega\oplus W^{\prime},

where
W^{\prime}
is a
\Omega
-torsion module. Thus,
W
decomposes as

W=\Omega^{\vee}c^{\prime}\oplus W_{\operatorname{tors}},

where
c^{\prime}=c+\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}e_{i}+\sum_{j=1}^{n}d_{i}f_{i}
, where
c\in\Omega^{\vee}a\oplus\Omega^{\vee}b
and
c_{i},d_{i}\in\Omega/(T^{m_{i}})
. Note that
W_{\operatorname{tors}}
is a maximal isotropic
\Omega
-submodule of

V(E)_{\operatorname{tors}}=\left(\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}\left(\frac{\Omega}{(T^{m_{i}})}e_{i}\oplus\frac{\Omega}{(T^{m_{i}})}f_{i}\right)\right).

Without loss of generality,

W_{\operatorname{tors}}=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\Omega}{(T^{m_{i}})}e_{i}.

Thus, after subtracting
\sum_{i}c_{i}e_{i}
, we can assume that

c^{\prime}=c+\sum_{j=1}^{n}d_{j}f_{i}.

On the other hand,
(c^{\prime},\gamma^{k}e_{i})=0
for all
k
and
i
. Since

(c^{\prime},\gamma^{k}e_{i})=(d_{i}f_{i},\gamma^{k}e_{i})

for all
k
, it follows that
d_{i}=0
and thus,
c^{\prime}=c
. This completes the proof.
∎

Recall that from Lemma
3.4
,
V_{1}(E)
is maximal isotropic and thus in view of Lemma
3.6
, we have that
V_{1}(E)=M_{1}\oplus V_{1}(E)_{\operatorname{tors}}
, where
M_{1}\simeq\Omega^{\vee}
and is contained in
\Omega^{\vee}a\oplus\Omega^{\vee}b
. On the other hand, by Lemma
3.5
,
V_{2}(E)=M_{2}^{\prime}\oplus V_{2}(E)_{\operatorname{tors}}
, where
M_{2}^{\prime}\simeq\Omega^{\vee}
. We let
M_{2}
be the projection of
M_{2}^{\prime}
onto the summand
\Omega^{\vee}a\oplus\Omega^{\vee}b
.

Lemma 3.7
.

With respect to notation above, the following are equivalent:

(1)

the
\mu
-invariant of
\operatorname{Sel}_{p^{\infty}}(E/\mathbb{Q}_{\operatorname{cyc}})
is
0
;

(2)

the
\mu
-invariant of
R_{p^{\infty}}(E/\mathbb{Q}_{\operatorname{cyc}})
is
0
and
V_{1}(E)\cap V_{2}(E)
is finite.

Proof.

Note that

\theta\left(\operatorname{Sel}^{\operatorname{Gr}}(E[p]/\mathbb{Q}_{\operatorname{cyc}})\right)=\operatorname{image}(\theta)\cap V_{2}(E)=V_{1}(E)\cap V_{2}(E).

Thus, the residual Selmer group
\operatorname{Sel}^{\operatorname{Gr}}(E[p]/\mathbb{Q}_{\operatorname{cyc}})
is finite if and only if the
\operatorname{ker}\theta
and
V_{1}(E)\cap V_{2}(E)
are both finite. Recall that the
\mu
-invariant of
\operatorname{Sel}_{p^{\infty}}(E/\mathbb{Q}_{\operatorname{cyc}})
is
0
if and only if
\operatorname{Sel}^{\operatorname{Gr}}(E[p]/\mathbb{Q}_{\operatorname{cyc}})
is finite. On the other hand, the kernel of
\theta
is
R(E[p]/\mathbb{Q}_{\operatorname{cyc}})
which is finite if and only if the
\mu
-invariant of
R_{p^{\infty}}(E/\mathbb{Q}_{\operatorname{cyc}})

.
∎

Part (1) is clear and left to the reader. Part (2) then follows from Lemma

.

4.
A Heuristic for
\Omega
-modules

Recall that
\Omega=\mathbb{F}_{p}\llbracket T\rrbracket
and that
\Omega^{\vee}
denotes its Pontryagin dual. Let
M_{1},M_{2}
be the submodules of
(\Omega^{\vee})^{2}
defined in the previous section. We would like to compute the probability that
M_{1}\cap M_{2}
is finite. Let
N_{i}\subset\Omega^{2}
be the submodule such that

M_{i}^{\vee}=\Omega^{2}/N_{i}.

For any natural number
n\geq 1
we define
\Omega_{n}\colon=\Omega/(T^{n})
.

Definition 4.1
.

We call a cyclic submodule
N\subset\Omega^{2}
(resp.
N\subset\Omega_{n}^{2}
) maximal if it is not contained in
T\Omega^{2}
(resp.
T\Omega_{n}^{2}
).

Remark 4.2
.

As
M_{1}
and
M_{2}
are isomorphic to
\Omega^{\vee}
, the modules
N_{1}
and
N_{2}
are maximal submodules of
\Omega^{2}
.

Definition 4.3
.

Let
\mathcal{M}_{n}
be the space of pairs of maximal submodules
(\bar{N}_{1},\bar{N}_{2})\in\Omega_{n}^{2}\times\Omega_{n}^{2}
. We define
\mathbb{P}_{n}
to be the uniform distribution on
\mathcal{M}_{n}
. Let
\mathcal{A}_{n}
be the power set of
\mathcal{M}_{n}
. Let
\mathcal{M}
be the set of pairs
(N_{1},N_{2})
of maximal submodules
N_{i}\in\Omega^{2}
. Let
\pi_{n}\colon\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{M}_{n}
and
\pi_{m,n}\colon\mathcal{M}_{m}\to\mathcal{M}_{n}
for
m\geq n
be the natural projections. Then we have
\mathcal{M}=\varprojlim_{n}\mathcal{M}_{n}
and
((\mathcal{M}_{n},\mathcal{A}_{,}\mathbb{P}_{n}),\pi_{m,n})
forms a projective system of measurable spaces (
[
18
, Definition 2.2]
). Let
(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{A},\mathbb{P})
be the inverse limit of this system (see
[
18
, Theorem 3.2]
for the existence).

Remark 4.4
.

For any subset
X\subset\mathcal{M}
we define
\mathbb{P}^{*}(X)=\inf_{X\subset B\in\mathcal{A}}\mathbb{P}(B)
. If
X\in\mathcal{A}
, then
\mathbb{P}(X)=\mathbb{P}^{*}(X)
. Note that
\mathbb{P}^{*}
is an outer measure but not a measure.

The aim of this section is to prove that
\{(N_{1},N_{2})\mid N_{1}\cap N_{2}=0\}
is a measurable set and that
\mathbb{P}((N_{1},N_{2})\mid N_{1}\cap N_{2}=0)=1
. Applying this result to the modules
N_{1}
and
N_{2}
above, we obtain that
N_{1}+N_{2}=\Omega^{2}
, i.e.
(M_{1}\cap M_{2})^{\vee}=\Omega^{2}/(N_{1}+N_{2})=0
. In particular,
V_{1}(E)\cap V_{2}(E)
is finite by Proposition
3.8
.

Lemma 4.5
.

With respect to notation above, there are
p^{n-1}(p+1)
maximal submodules in
\Omega_{n}^{2}
.

Proof.

We prove the claim by induction on
n
starting with
n=1
. In this case, we are counting the
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-lines of
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{2}
. We have
p^{2}-1
elements that generate these lines and
p-1
units in
\mathbb{F}_{p}
. Thus, we obtain
(p+1)
maximal submodules when
n=1
. Assume now that
n\geq 1
and by induction, that we have already proven the following assertions.

•

There are
p^{2n}-p^{2(n-1)}
elements in
\Omega_{n}^{2}
generating a maximal submodule.

•

There are
(p+1)p^{n-1}
maximal submodules in
\Omega^{2}_{n}
.

Each element in
\Omega_{n}^{2}
has
p^{2}
preimages in
\Omega_{n+1}^{2}
, and an element in
\Omega_{n+1}^{2}
generates a maximal submodule if and only if its restriction to
\Omega_{n}^{2}
generates one. Thus, there are
p^{2(n+1)}-p^{2n}
elements generating a maximal submodule in
\Omega^{2}_{n+1}
. This implies that there are

\frac{p^{2n}(p^{2}-1)}{\#\Omega_{n+1}^{\times}}=\frac{p^{2n}(p^{2}-1)}{(p-1)p^{n}}=p^{n}(p+1)

maximal submodules in
\Omega_{n+1}^{2}

∎

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
N_{1}
and
N_{2}
be maximal submodules. Then one of the following is true

•

N_{1}\cap N_{2}=0
.

•

N_{1}=N_{2}
.

Proof.

Assume that
N_{1}\cap N_{2}\neq 0
. As
\Omega
is a principal ideal domain, there exist non-negative integers
a,b
such that

T^{a}N_{1}=T^{b}N_{2}.

As
\Omega^{2}
does not contain any
T
-torsion, we can assume that
a=0
. Thus,
N_{1}=T^{b}N_{2}
. As
N_{1}
is maximal,
N_{1}
is not contained in
T\Omega^{2}
. thus,
b=0
and we obtain indeed
N_{1}=N_{2}
.
∎

Lemma 4.7
.

The set
\{(N_{1},N_{2})\mid N_{1}\cap N_{2}=0\}
is measurable.

Proof.

As the union of countably many measurable sets is measurable it suffices to show that

\{(N_{1},N_{2})\mid N_{1}\cap N_{2}=0\}=\cup_{n}\{\pi_{n}(N_{1})\neq\pi_{n}(N_{2})\}.

By Lemma
4.6
, the right hand side is contained in the left hand side. Assume that there is a pair
(N_{1},N_{2})
such that
\pi_{n}(N_{1})=\pi_{n}(N_{2})
for all
n
. Let
x\in N_{1}
. For each
i
we can find a decomposition

x=n_{i}+T^{i}y_{i},\quad n_{i}\in N_{2}.

Let
i<j
, we obtain

n_{i}-n_{j}=T^{i}(T^{j-i}y_{j}-y_{i}).

Thus
(n_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
is a Cauchy sequence. As
\Omega
is compact there is a convergent subsequence
(n_{i_{k}})
. Let
n
be the limit. We obtain

x=n_{i_{k}}+T^{i_{k}}y_{i_{k}}\rightarrow n\in N_{2}.

Therefore,
x\in N_{2}
. Changing the roles of
N_{1}
and
N_{2}
proves
N_{1}=N_{2}
. Thus, if
N_{1}\neq N_{2}
, there exists an
n
such that
\pi_{N}(N_{1})\neq\pi_{n}(N_{2})
, which implies that

\{(N_{1},N_{2})\mid N_{1}\cap N_{2}=0\}\subset\cup_{n}\{\pi_{n}(N_{1})\neq\pi_{n}(N_{2})\}.

∎

Theorem 4.8
.

Let
N_{1},N_{2}
be cyclic submodules of
\Omega^{2}
. Then we have

\mathbb{P}(\{(N_{1},N_{2})\in\mathcal{M}\mid N_{1}\cap N_{2}=0\})=1.

Proof.

By Lemma
4.6
we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(N_{1}\cap N_{2}=0)=\mathbb{P}(N_{1}\neq N_{2})

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{P}(\pi_{n}(N_{1})\neq\pi_{n}(N_{2})).

Note that the condition
\pi_{n}(N_{1})\neq\pi_{n}(N_{2})
actually produces a measurable set. Therefore, we find that

\begin{split}&\mathbb{P}(\pi_{n}(N_{1})\neq\pi_{n}(N_{2}))\\
=&\mathbb{P}_{n}(\pi_{n}(N_{1})\neq\pi_{n}(N_{2}))\\
=&1-\mathbb{P}_{n}(\pi_{n}(N_{1})=\pi_{n}(N_{2})).\end{split}

Let
\mathcal{N}_{n}
be the set of maximal submodules in
\Omega^{2}
.
By Lemma
4.5
we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{n}(\pi_{n}(N_{1})=\pi_{n}(N_{2}))=\sum_{M\in\mathcal{N}_{n}}\mathbb{P}_{n}(\pi_{n}(N_{1})=M)\mathbb{P}(\pi_{n}(N_{2})=M)

\displaystyle=\sum_{M\in\mathcal{N}_{n}}\left(\frac{1}{(p+1)p^{n-1}}\right)^{2}=\frac{1}{p^{n-1}(p+1)}.

This implies that

\mathbb{P}(N_{1}\cap N_{2}=0)\geq 1-\frac{1}{(p+1)p^{n-1}}

for all
n
. The left hand side of the above inequality does not depend on
n
, thus, letting
n
tend to infinity gives the claim.

∎

We are now ready to give a proof of Theorem
A
.

Proof of Theorem A.

Let
N_{1}
and
N_{2}
be maximal cyclic submodules of
\Omega^{2}
. Then according to the Lemma
4.6
,
N_{1}\cap N_{2}\neq 0
if and only if
N_{1}=N_{2}
. Thus in particular, this implies that if
N_{1}\cap N_{2}
is finite, then
N_{1}\cap N_{2}=0
. It then follows from Theorem
4.8
that

\mathbb{P}\left(\{(N_{1},N_{2})\in\mathcal{M}\mid N_{1}\cap N_{2}\text{ is infinite}\}\right)=0.

Heuristic
1.3
then implies that

\lim_{x\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\#\mathcal{T}_{p}(x)}{\#\mathcal{C}(x)}=0.

Since we assume Conjecture A of Coates and Sujatha, it follows as a consequence of part (2) of Proposition
3.8
that if the
\mu
-invariant of
\operatorname{Sel}_{p^{\infty}}(E/\mathbb{Q}_{\operatorname{cyc}})
is non-zero, then
V_{1}(E)\cap V_{2}(E)
is infinite. Therefore, we have that
\mathcal{S}_{p}(x)\subseteq\mathcal{T}_{p}(x)
and we deduce that

\lim_{x\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\#\mathcal{S}_{p}(x)}{\#\mathcal{C}(x)}=0,

proving the result.
∎

Achieving desired characteristic impedances in customized coplanar waveguide transmission line design
Authors: Mohd H. S Alrashdan
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Coplanar waveguide (CPW) transmission lines are valued for their planar design, low radiation, and minimized signal loss, but controlling their characteristic impedance remains a challenge. This study employs the Taguchi method, a statistical approach, to optimize the characteristic impedance by adjusting eight control factors: track width, track thickness, gap width, dielectric height, backplane thickness, conductor material conductivity, dielectric conductivity, and operational frequency. The analysis evaluates these factors across three levels to find optimal conditions, with dielectric height and track width identified as most influential. Additional assessments using main effect screening, Analysis of Variance (ANOVA), and multivariable linear regression validate the Taguchi method's effectiveness. The results of this effort encompass a distributed resistance of 133.69 Ω/m, a distributed inductance of 2.6676E-7H/m, a shunt conductance of 2.8880E-16 S/m, a capacitance of 7.4103E-11 F/m, a propagation constant of (1.1141 + 279.36i) m-1, and a characteristic impedance of (59.999 - 0.23928i) Ω. A CPW transmission line with a characteristic impedance of 60 Ω was successfully designed and simulated using COMSOL Multiphysics, showing promising results for efficient CPW designs tailored to specific applications. Background

Coplanar Waveguide (CPW) transmission lines offer several advantages over other configurations, such as microstrip and coaxial lines, particularly for high-frequency and high-power applications. CPW has lower radiation loss, better heat dissipation, and reduced crosstalk, enhancing signal integrity. Key parameters affecting CPW performance include characteristic impedance, signal conductor width, spacing between signal and ground planes, substrate height, dielectric constant, signal frequency, and loss tangent. Optimizing these parameters is crucial for aligning impedance with circuit requirements, especially in Radio frequency (RF) or microwave circuits. Researchers have employed a spectrum of methods and design techniques to refine and govern the characteristic impedance of CPW transmission lines. These methods encompass various approaches, such as adjusting conductor width [1], modifying gap width [2], manipulating substrate properties such as the dielectric constant (εᵣ) and loss tangent of the substrate material [3], introducing tapered structures [4], segmenting the CPW into multiple sections with varying dimensions to achieve distinct impedance values along the line's length [5], integrating stubs and slot line structures [6,7], and employing dielectric overlay or underlay. The addition of dielectric layers above or below the CPW structure is a technique often utilized for fine-tuning impedance or achieving specific values [8]. Moreover, researchers have explored the use of multilayer CPW configurations [9] as an additional approach to achieve optimal control over characteristic impedance. Historically, these approaches have predominantly relied on a trial-and-error methodology, which can be time-consuming and inefficient. In contrast, the Taguchi Method, a statistical approach developed by Dr. Genichi Taguchi, offers a structured way to optimize processes and improve reliability [10]. This research, used the Taguchi Method to systematically explore how eight variables influence the characteristic impedance of CPW transmission lines, improving efficiency and performance. This research aims to develop a systematic methodology using the Taguchi method to analyze eight critical factors affecting the characteristic impedance of CPW transmission lines, including track width and thickness, gap width, dielectric height, backplane thickness, conductor material and its conductivity, dielectric conductivity, and frequency for specific target impedances in various applications (Fig. 1).Fig. 1A diagram of achieving desired characteristic impedances in customized coplanar waveguide transmission line design technique procedure.Fig 1

Method details

Coplanar waveguide transmission lines theory

A Coplanar Waveguide (CPW) is a transmission line used in high-frequency and microwave applications to transport electromagnetic signals. Its wide adoption is attributed to its flexibility and the ease of manufacturing and integration into microwave circuits, primarily due to its flat, planar structure. A CPW comprises three fundamental elements: a central conductor, two adjacent ground planes, and a dielectric material in the middle. This setup, illustrated in Fig. 2 below, enables the transmission of electromagnetic waves. The central conductor, often implemented as a metal trace or strip, is responsible for transmitting the signal and is located on the same plane as the ground plane. These ground planes, situated on both sides of the central conductor, have a dual role – they serve as a path for the electromagnetic fields to return and maintain the coplanar structure. They also play a vital role in containing the electromagnetic waves within the CPW structure. The dielectric material serves as the separator between the centre conductor and the ground planes. It is chosen for its dielectric constant, which affects the characteristic impedance and propagation speed of signals in the CPW (Fig. 3).Fig. 2CPW cross sectional area.Fig 2Fig. 3Equivalent circuit model of a CPW transmission line.Fig 3

The operation of CPW is based on the fundamental principles of wave propagation. When an electrical signal is applied to the centre conductor, it generates an electromagnetic field. The signal travels along the centre conductor in the form of electromagnetic waves. Electromagnetic fields propagate along transmission lines, approximately behaving as transverse electromagnetic (TEM) waves. The equation describing the electric potential on a transmission line in the frequency domain, considering one dimension, is expressed as follows:(1)∂∂x(1R+iωL∂v∂x)−(iωC+G)V=0

In this equation, R, L, G, and C represent the distributed values for resistance, inductance, conductance, and capacitance. A comparable wave equation can be derived for the current flowing through the transmission line. The following is a schematic representation of a transmission line terminated by a load impedance.

In the absence of any reflected waves, when only a forward-propagating wave is considered within the transmission line, dividing the voltage V by the current I yields the characteristic impedance, denoted as Z0 or the wave impedance, of the transmission line.(2)Z0=VI=R+iωLG+iωC

The ground planes flanking the central conductor serve as a boundary that effectively contains the electromagnetic fields within the CPW structure. This confinement is essential for facilitating regulated signal propagation. The characteristic impedance of the CPW depends on variables such as the width and spacing between the central conductor and ground planes, along with the characteristics of the dielectric material. Engineers can manipulate these factors to create CPW setups with exact impedance values. One of the notable characteristics of CPWs is their ability to minimize signal loss, rendering them highly suitable for applications in the high-frequency and microwave domains. This attribute is primarily attributed to the confinement of electromagnetic fields within the dielectric material, which in turn reduces radiation losses. CPWs also demonstrate versatility, as they can be easily integrated with other components within microwave circuits, encompassing elements like filters, resonators, and antennas. They are also suitable for creating balanced transmission lines. CPW transmission lines serve as a fundamental component in the field of high-frequency and microwave engineering. Their distinctive structure and operational principles render them an indispensable choice for diverse applications where the precise regulation of characteristic impedance holds paramount importance. The impedance of CPW transmission lines stands as a fundamental parameter with a profound impact on their utility and efficacy within Radio frequency (RF) and microwave circuits.

Exacting control over impedance is imperative to ensure the efficient transmission of signals, minimize signal losses, and uphold the integrity of the transmitted signals. Achieving precise impedance matching is of critical importance across a broad spectrum of applications, spanning from RF components to antennas and sensors. It serves as a pivotal factor in attaining the desired performance levels in these systems.

Designers must exercise due diligence in considering the dielectric constant of the substrate material in order to achieve the desired impedance characteristics for their CPW designs. The inclusion of three replications for every experiment assumes critical importance in obtaining a precise reflection of real-world scenarios or practical assessments. In this study, the process is conducted three times using COMSOL Multiphysics, with each iteration involving a small adjustment to the relative dielectric permittivity. COMSOL Multiphysics is a powerful simulation tool widely used in electromagnetics and transmission line design, particularly for modeling the characteristic impedance in CPW transmission lines. Within the framework of COMSOL, there exists a specialized feature known as Accurate Electromagnetics, meticulously designed to offer a comprehensive platform for the simulation of electromagnetic phenomena.

Accurate electromagnetics harnesses finite element analysis (FEA) to solve Maxwell's equations and electromagnetic wave equations, thus enabling a precise representation of the intricate electromagnetic interactions inherent in CPW transmission lines. This high level of precision plays a pivotal role in providing accurate predictions of the characteristic impedance and magnetic flux density attributes of the transmission line.

Taguchi analysis, an important technique for enhancing quality, can be efficiently carried out using MINITAB 17 software. The software facilitates the creation of the L27 QA, allowing for the organized adjustment of variables to find the finest parameter settings while minimising the number of experiments required [10].

With its robust suite of statistical tools, MINITAB 17 enables an in-depth examination of how the eight selected factors influence quality attributes, helping to identify those with the most significant impact. Additionally, MINITAB's software make this study easier to visualize main effects and other plots, improving the interpretation of experimental results. By combining Taguchi analysis principles with MINITAB 17′s computational power, this research successfully optimizes processes, enhances product quality, and supports informed decision-making aimed at achieving target performance levels.

The data concerning the characteristic impedance of CPW transmission lines, assessed through the Taguchi method, is subjected to analysis using MINITAB 17 statistical software. This analysis encompasses the application of metrics like the signal-to-noise ratio (S/N) and the calculation of the data's mean. When a controlling factor shows a higher S/N or mean value, it indicates a more significant impact on the characteristic impedance of the CPW transmission line. Moreover, MINITAB 17 enables the implementation of mean effect screener analysis, analysis of variance (ANOVA), and multivariable linear regression analysis. These tools help evaluate the importance of factors and their interactions, while also confirming the results from the Taguchi method [10]. Lastly, one of the experiments from Table 2 was revisited to develop a new, tailored CPW transmission line model, designed to achieve a target characteristic impedance of 60 Ω. To accomplish this, the COMSOL Multiphysics 5.4 simulation software was employed once more. It was used to assess the performance of this CPW transmission line, which involved calculating its electrical and magnetic properties and evaluating the distributed values for resistance, inductance, conductance, and capacitance.

The characteristic impedance of a CPW transmission line is determined by the geometry and electrical properties of the structure. While several factors influence the characteristic impedance. The impact of a control factor on the characteristic impedance of CPW transmission line depends on its role in shaping the electromagnetic field distribution within the CPW structure. While the importance of each factor can vary based on specific design considerations, in a given scenario presented in this work, the Dielectric Height control factor have a higher delta function and more pronounced effect compared to other factors .The Dielectric Height represents the distance between the signal trace and the ground plane in a CPW. As this distance increases, the electric field lines become more concentrated between the signal trace and the ground plane, which, in turn, leads to a lower characteristic impedance. Therefore, a variation in dielectric height can have a significant effect on the characteristic impedance, as it directly influences the spatial distribution of the electromagnetic fields. Smaller changes in dielectric height can result in relatively larger changes in impedance compared to other factors, such as track width or gap width. At higher frequencies, electromagnetic field distribution becomes more sensitive to dielectric height due to the shrinking wavelength. Consequently, for high-frequency applications, the dielectric height may have a more significant impact on characteristic impedance. The track width comes in the second place and has a relatively higher effect on it compared to other factors except the The Dielectric Height because it directly affects the distribution of electromagnetic fields within the CPW. The track width is the distance between the center conductor (signal trace) and the nearest ground plane, and it plays a significant role in determining the characteristic impedance of a CPW. A wider track width may allow for a greater separation between the signal trace and the ground plane, which means the electric field lines are more concentrated between the trace and the ground, resulting in a lower characteristic impedance. Conversely, a narrower track width brings the signal trace closer to the ground plane, causing the electric field lines to spread out more, leading to a higher characteristic impedance. Track width has a more pronounced impact on the characteristic impedance of a CPW because it directly controls the distribution of electromagnetic fields between the signal trace and the ground plane. Adjusting the track width can lead to more significant changes in impedance compared to other factors, making it a critical parameter to consider when designing CPW transmission lines for specific applications. Track thickness affects the skin effect and conductor losses within the CPW. While it has an impact on impedance, it tends to be less influential compared to track width and dielectric height. The distance between the signal trace and the neighboring ground plane affects capacitance and, subsequently, impedance. While important, it is generally less influential than track width and dielectric height. The conductivity of the conductor material plays a role in losses but has a relatively smaller impact on characteristic impedance compared to geometric factors.

At higher frequencies, the influence of frequency on impedance increases due to changing electromagnetic wave behavior. However, in the list of factors, it has a lesser impact overall. Backplane thickness can impact the effective dielectric constant, affecting impedance. Still, its influence is typically not as pronounced as the above factors. The conductivity of the dielectric material can influence losses but tends to have a minimal effect on characteristic impedance compared to other factors.

In CPW transmission lines, the electric potential distribution is a key parameter that ensures the controlled propagation of signals. In this case, with a characteristic impedance of 60 Ω, the electric potential is designed to remain relatively constant between the center conductor and the surrounding ground plane. This uniform electric potential distribution is vital for maintaining the desired impedance level and preventing signal distortion or reflections.

As signals propagate along the CPW, the electric potential exhibits a quasi-TEM (Transverse ElectroMagnetic) field behaviour. This signifies that the electric potential mainly fluctuates in the lateral direction, specifically between the central conductor and the ground plane, while exhibiting a comparatively stable profile along the transmission line's extent. This consistency aids in the reduction of signal losses and guarantees the retention of the CPW's characteristic impedance of 60 Ω. The distribution of the electric field within CPW transmission lines closely mirrors the distribution of electric potential. The most concentrated electric field is situated in the area between the central conductor and the ground plane. This pronounced containment of the electric field is a unique feature of CPW and serves a vital role in preventing signal radiation and crosstalk. With a characteristic impedance of 60 Ω, the distribution of the electric field remains exceptionally consistent, which is essential for upholding signal integrity. This uniformity ensures that the energy associated with the signal remains tightly enclosed within the CPW structure. Furthermore, this field distribution aids in diminishing losses, particularly at higher frequencies, and enhances the efficiency of signal transmission. Within CPW transmission lines, the magnetic flux density is relatively low when compared to other types of transmission lines, primarily due to the inherent design characteristics of CPWs. The magnetic field is primarily focused on the dielectric material that separates the central conductor from the ground plane. The CPW's ground plane functions as a protective shield, containing the magnetic flux and diminishing its interaction with external elements or neighbouring transmission lines. At a characteristic impedance of 60 Ω, the magnetic flux density distribution remains uniform. This uniformity is of paramount importance in managing electromagnetic interference and mitigating the potential for unintended radiation. Equally critical is its role in minimizing any adverse effects on nearby circuits or components, thus guaranteeing the preservation of signal integrity within the CPW transmission lines.

ILET: An Integrated Tool for Human Error Probability Estimation Using IDHEAS-Data and Scenario-Based Labeling with Large Language Models
Xingyu Xiao, Peng Chen, Jingang Liang, Jiejuan Tong, Haitao Wang

Abstract
Human Reliability Analysis (HRA) plays a critical role in high-risk industries by assessing the likelihood of human errors and their potential impacts on complex systems. Traditional approaches to estimating Human Error Probability (HEP) often rely on costly simulators and limited data, presenting challenges in accuracy and scalability. This paper introduces the IDHEAS-TABLE Integrated Labeling and Estimation Tool (ILET), a novel tool designed to enhance HEP estimation by leveraging the IDHEAS database and advanced scenario-based labeling techniques powered by large language models (LLMs). ILET automatically analyzes user-input scenarios, classifies them through LLM-driven labeling, and cross-references the classified labels with an IDHEAS-derived knowledge base to provide accurate HEP estimations. By integrating data-driven labeling and robust probabilistic estimation, ILET offers a scalable, resource-efficient solution for HRA practitioners seeking to improve reliability assessments without the high costs associated with traditional methods. This paper details the development of ILET, its underlying methodologies, and a series of case studies validating its effectiveness and accuracy in diverse operational scenarios.

Introduction

Human Reliability Analysis (HRA) is a systematic methodology for evaluating the likelihood of human errors within complex systems, particularly in high-risk industries such as nuclear power, aviation, and healthcare. These fields require meticulous safety standards, where even minor human errors can lead to catastrophic outcomes. HRA provides a framework for understanding and predicting human performance in various operational contexts, thus enabling risk mitigation and safety enhancement.

Challenges in Accurate Human Error Probability (HEP) Estimation
Despite its importance, accurately estimating Human Error Probability (HEP) presents significant challenges. Traditional HEP estimation methods often suffer from data limitations, as high-quality incident and error data are scarce, especially in high-stakes settings where human error may be rare but impactful. Additionally, many HEP assessments rely on costly full-scope simulators that recreate realistic operational environments, which are not only expensive to develop and maintain but also limited in their accessibility and scalability. These challenges underscore the need for innovative approaches to HEP estimation that are both resource-efficient and accurate.

Large Language Models (LLMs) are advanced AI-driven systems trained on vast amounts of textual data to understand and generate human-like language. In recent years, LLMs have shown significant potential to improve data-driven decision-making across various domains, including high-risk industries. By leveraging natural language processing capabilities, LLMs can process and analyze complex textual information, offering insights that support critical decision-making processes. Their application in Human Reliability Analysis (HRA) could provide robust tools for analyzing and interpreting complex human performance data, allowing for deeper insights into human error dynamics.

Advantages of Using LLMs for Scenario Analysis and Labeling in HRA
LLMs offer several advantages for scenario analysis and labeling within HRA. They can efficiently classify and categorize a wide range of operational scenarios by recognizing patterns and context within textual inputs, thereby assisting in the systematic identification of error-prone situations. This automated labeling capability reduces the reliance on manually intensive processes and provides consistent and scalable labeling for scenario analysis. Furthermore, LLMs can incorporate vast amounts of contextual knowledge, enhancing the accuracy and relevance of scenario labels, which is crucial for effective HRA and improving the precision of Human Error Probability (HEP) estimations.

Human Reliability Analysis Methods and Tools

Summary of Traditional HRA Methods and the Use of Scenario-Based Approaches
Traditional Human Reliability Analysis (HRA) methods focus on identifying and quantifying the likelihood of human errors in complex systems, often through structured frameworks like the Technique for Human Error Rate Prediction (THERP), the Human Cognitive Reliability/Operator Error Modeling System (CREAM), and the Systematic Human Error Reduction and Prediction Approach (SHERPA). These methods employ qualitative and quantitative assessments of human actions and cognitive processes to predict error probabilities under specific conditions. Scenario-based approaches are frequently integrated within HRA methodologies, allowing analysts to consider specific contextual factors influencing human performance. By situating human actions within defined scenarios, these approaches enhance the relevance of HRA predictions and help in developing tailored interventions for error prevention.

Existing Tools for HEP Estimation and Their Limitations
Various tools have been developed to support Human Error Probability (HEP) estimation, such as SPAR-H (Standardized Plant Analysis Risk-Human Reliability Analysis) and SACADA (Scenario Authoring, Characterization, and Debriefing Application). While these tools have proven valuable for estimating HEPs, they are often limited by the quality and granularity of available data and rely on predefined scenarios that may not fully capture the variability of real-world operations. Furthermore, many tools require high-cost simulators or extensive human resources for scenario creation and analysis, making them resource-intensive and less accessible. These limitations highlight the need for advanced methods that can achieve accurate, data-driven HEP estimations without excessive resource dependency.

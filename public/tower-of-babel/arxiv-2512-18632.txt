Multi-user Pufferfish Privacy
Ni Ding, Songpei Lu, Wenjing Yang, Zijian Zhang

Abstract
. This paper studies how to achieve individual indistinguishability by pufferfish privacy in aggregated query to a multi-user system. It is assumed that each user reports realization of a random variable. We study how to calibrate Laplace noise, added to the query answer, to attain pufferfish privacy when user changes his/her reported data value, leaves the system and is replaced by another use with different randomness. Sufficient conditions are derived for all scenarios for attaining statistical indistinguishability on four sets of secret pairs. They are derived using the existing Kantorovich method (Wasserstain metric of order 1 1 ). These results can be applied to attain indistinguishability when a certain class of users is added or removed from a tabular data. It is revealed that attaining indifference in individual’s data is conditioned on the statistics of this user only. For binary (Bernoulli distributed) random variables, the derived sufficient conditions can be further relaxed to reduce the noise and improve data utility.

Introduction

In an era we require data streams are in nearly every interaction, safeguarding personal data is foundational to trust, compliance and ethical innovation.
This rely on how personal privacy is defined or interpreted as to how to keep anonymous and prevent re-identification attacks.
Differential privacy is proposed in a rigorous mathematical framework based on the statistical indistinguishability
(
Dwork
et al. 2006
;
Wasserman and
Zhou 2010
;
Mironov 2017
)
, meaning the difference in probability distribution of outputs from analyses on neighboring datasets (differing in only one entry/row) is upper bounded by a similarity threshold, parameterized by a privacy budge
\epsilon
.
Assuming one record corresponding to a single individual
(
Dong
et al. 2022
;
Dwork
et al. 2014
)
,
\epsilon
-differential privacy guarantees that the statistical resolution discriminating individuals’ contribution and/or existence in a dataset is controlled below a threshold. This is a rigorous data protection against a strong adversary that is able to infer the knowledge of individual data by collecting probabilistic features of an observable output.

While the framework of differential privacy is designed for malicious statistical inference, it is assumed that the source data (e.g., the original database, or query answer) is deterministic,
1

(
Dwork
et al. 2014
)
.

i.e., each individual reports constantly the same value in a dataset. This might not be applicable to some cases where the participants’ data are varying, e.g., local differential privacy
(
Duchi
et al. 2013
)
, sensitive surveys
(
Lensvelt-Mulders et al. 2005
)
, private aggregation
(
Corrigan-Gibbs and
Boneh 2017
)
and federated analytics/learning
(
Konečnỳ et al. 2016a
;
Konečnỳ et al. 2016b
)
.
To address this,
Kifer and
Machanavajjhala 2012
;
Kifer and
Machanavajjhala 2014
proposed a correlation-aware and adversary-aware privacy preserving framework called
pufferfish privacy
, where the database is statistically conditioned on the secret/privacy, and the adversary obtains some prior belief.
The purpose is to study a more practical scenario for data privacy in the presence of intrinsic randomness and to combat a more advanced statistical inference capability of the adversary. This framework not only extends differential privacy to more application-aware settings, but also establishes the connection to information leakage, a Bayesian style data privacy definition.

For public data that statistically depends on some nesting secret, publishing it allows posterior information gain on secret, or privacy leakage, at the adversary side. This is captured by another field in computer science called quantitative information flow
(
Smith 2009
;
Clarkson
et al. 2009
;
Alvim et al. 2012
)
, which has been frequently cited in recent works in privacy leakage
(
Cuff and Yu 2016
;
du Pin Calmon and
Fawaz 2012
;
Makhdoumi et al. 2014
)
.
The idea is to measure privacy leakage as the difference between posterior and prior believes (on secret)
(
Ding
et al. 2021
;
Issa
et al. 2020
;
Liao
et al. 2019
)
.
As pointed out in
(
Nuradha and
Goldfeld 2023
;
Song
et al. 2017
;
Ding 2022
;
Ding 2024
)
, in the lens of optimization, it can be viewed as the mechanism design for attaining pufferfish privacy.
This is why pufferfish privacy has been applied to attribute privacy
(
Zhang
et al. 2020
;
Zhang
et al. 2022
)
, a typical data protection scenario when releasing tabular data by masking the sensitive columns. See also the experiments in
(
Ding 2022
;
Ding 2024
)
.

While pufferfish privacy framework is generalized enough to be tailored to many data protection applications, privacy is still highly relevant to
anonymization
or the conceal of personal ID, the fundamental motivation of both differential and pufferfish privacy
(
Dwork
et al. 2014
;
Kifer and
Machanavajjhala 2012
)
.
This is also based on the worst case concern: limiting a single person’s influence automatically restricts what can be learned about that person’s attributes.
In fact, the proposal of pufferfish privacy in
(
Kifer and
Machanavajjhala 2012
)
is based on the indistinguishability of modifying and removing an individual’s record in a dataset, assuming the participants’ data are probabilistic.
While most studies focuses on mechanism design, e.g.,
(
Pierquin et al. 2024
;
Song
et al. 2017
;
Ding 2022
)
, and theoretical analysis, e.g.,
(
Nuradha and
Goldfeld 2023
)
, few discusses how to calibrate noise to prevent membership inference under the pufferfish privacy framework.
For example,
Song
et al. 2017
;
Ding 2022
discovered a Wasserstein metric approach to set up the Laplace mechanism. However,
Song
et al. 2017
focused on a time series secret that is Markovian, and
Ding 2022
looked into tabular data release similar to attribute privacy. Question remains how to apply the stat-of-art noise calibration techniques to the original individual indistinguishability problem in
(
Kifer and
Machanavajjhala 2012
)
.

This paper considers a multi-user system, where each user reporting a random variable to compute an aggregated query. We study the problem of how to achieve individual indistinguishability in terms of pufferfish privacy in the query answer. This is done by regarding statistical difference upon a small change in individual data, either its value or its statistical features. We study the scenarios including (1) a user changes his/her value reported to the query answer; (2) a user leaves the system, i.e., user’s presence/absence; (3) a user is replaced by another one with different randomness.
We also assume an attendance probability indicating the chances that a user will be present in the system. This aligns with the motivating example
(
Kifer and
Machanavajjhala 2012
, Section 3.2)
, for the purpose of enforcing indistinguishability for almost all intrinsic statistics.
We adopt Laplace mechanism, i.e., adding Laplace noise to query answer, calibrated by Kantorovich optimal transport plan (corresponding to Wasserstein metric of order
1
) proposed in
(
Ding 2022
)
to derive sufficient conditions for attaining pufferfish privacy for the three discriminative scenarios above.

Our main contributions are the following.

1.

Denoting secret as the specific value reported by the user, pufferfish privacy on distinct secret pairs guarantees a certain degree of indifference in individual’s data value revealed by the query answer. Similar to the
\ell_{1}
-sensitivity method
(
Dwork
et al. 2006
)
, we prove that setting the scale parameter of Laplace noise by the distance between distinct secrets attains pufferfish privacy. Realizing the absence of an individual is equivalent of reporting zero, the same sufficient condition can be applied for ensuring enough indifference between reporting a specific value and being absent.

2.

Denoting secret as the belief/fact that user’s data follows certain probability distribution, we consider two scenarios: when this user is joining or leaving the system; when this user is replaced by another user. Both cases will cause a change in the resulting probability distribution of the query answer. We show that for both scenarios, sufficient conditions can be derived in terms of the user’s statistical features, e.g., expectation, cumulative mass function, for attaining pufferfish privacy. In the case of binary random variable, relaxed sufficient conditions can be obtained to reduce the Laplace noise and improve data utility.
Experimental results on three real-world datasets indicates that pufferfish privacy in the above two scenarios can be applied to protect data privacy against removing, adding or modifying a certain class of users in a dataset.

The results above are produced by considering four secret pairs in the pufferfish privacy frameworks. It is revealed that for attaining the indifference about a specific user’s data, the achievability of pufferfish privacy is conditioned on this user only. While the attendance probability is incorporated in the multi-user system, the derived sufficient conditions are independent of it.

2.1.
Pufferfish privacy

A data curator wants to publish data
X
for legitimate uses, e.g., to convey the statistics of income tax of a population. But, the concern is that it may reveal some personal information, e.g., individual’s race, education background and marital status, that can be exploited for adversarial action, discrimination, etc.
We call this part of personal information the sensitive data and denote by
S
. In general,
S
is statistically correlated to
X
so that a capable adversary would gain statistical knowledge on the sensitive data. This kind of privacy breach should be prevented and mitigated by the data curator before data release.

Let
P_{X|S}(\cdot|s,\rho)
denote the conditional probability of the public data
X
given some secret
S=s
. This statistical dependence is due to the intrinsic correlation of the dataset.
Here,
\rho
is introduced as the adversaries prior knowledge as to the system settings, incl. the hyperparameters for parametric probability distribution, etc.. For example, the mean and covariance if
P_{X|S}(\cdot|s,\rho)
for all
s\in\mathcal{S}
is Gaussian. There could be more than one adversaries in the system, and each of them has a different prior beliefs.

The reason that we focus on the probability of
X
given
S
is to observe the difference between the statistics in the public data
X
excited by two distinct secrets. To protect privacy, we want the difference between two probabilities
P_{X|S}(\cdot|s_{i},\rho)
and
P_{X|S}(\cdot|s_{j},\rho)
to be small so that an adversary observes
X
would have difficulty in distinguishing whether it is resulted from
S=s_{i}
or
S=s_{j}
. That is, the more statistical indistinguishability we have on the secret
S
, the more data privacy is preserved.
This is fundamental motivation for differential privacy proposed by
Dwork 2006
. However, when the original data
X
is an r.v., pufferfish privacy, a more generalized framework, should be adopted.

Assume that a randomized version of
X
, denoted by
Y
, is generated to preserve data privacy. The adversary is assumed to have access to the privatized data
Y
only. He can collect the aggregated statistics by repeatedly querying the released
Y
.
Let
\mathds{S}
be a subset of secret pairs
(s_{i},s_{j})
. A data curator can define
\mathds{S}
to specify all secret pairs where statistical indistinguishability should be enforced to preserve data privacy.
For a given
privacy budget

\epsilon>0
, the privatized data
Y
is said to be
(\epsilon,\mathds{S})
-
pufferfish privacy
if
(
Kifer and
Machanavajjhala 2014
;
Kifer and
Machanavajjhala 2012
)

(1)

\displaystyle e^{-\epsilon}\leq\frac{P_{Y|S}(y|s_{i},\rho)}{P_{Y|S}(y|s_{j},\rho)}\leq e^{\epsilon},\quad\forall y,(s_{i},s_{j})\in\mathbb{S},\rho.

Note that (
1
) also guarantees
\epsilon
-degree of indistinguishability against all adversaries prior belief
\rho
.

Laplace noise

One of the common method to randomize data is to add noise. Denote
N
the noise that is independent of
X
. We have the randomized data generated by
Y=X+N
.
In this paper, we consider (zero-mean) Laplace noise
N_{\theta}
with probability distribution

(2)

P_{N_{\theta}}(z)=\frac{1}{2\theta}e^{-\frac{|z|}{\theta}},\quad\forall z\in\mathds{R}.

For
X
being an r.v., the resulting probability of
Y
is given by a convolution

(3)

P_{Y|S}(y|s)=\int P_{N_{\theta}}(y-x)P_{X|S}(x|s).

The scale parameter
\theta
indicates the flatness of Laplace distribution and determines its variance
\mathrm{VAR}[N_{\theta}]=2\theta^{2}
. Therefore,
\theta
specifies how large the Laplace noise is.
Calibrating noise is equivalent to determining the value of
\theta
. A data curator needs to choose a proper
\theta
to balance data privacy and utility. That is, the scale parameter
\theta
should be just large enough to attain
(\epsilon,\mathds{S})
-pufferfish privacy. An unnecessarily large
\theta
will deteriorate the data utility.

Kantorovich Mechanism

Knowing the intrinsic randomness in the pufferfish privacy framework, the literature suggests a noise calibration method in terms of statistical distances, which is analogous to the
\ell_{1}
-sensitivity method for differential privacy
(
Dwork
et al. 2006
)
. This can be seen from the initial attempts by

et al. 2017

To address a computation problem due to the non-convexity of the

-Wasserstein metric
(

et al. 2008
;

)
,

proposed a
1

For each pair of prior distributions
P_{X|S}(\cdot|s_{i},\rho)
and
P_{X|S}(\cdot|s_{j},\rho)
, let
\pi
be a
coupling
joint distribution such that
P_{X|S}(x|s_{i},\rho)=\int\pi(x,x^{\prime})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x^{\prime}
for all
x
and
P_{X|S}(x^{\prime}|s_{j},\rho)=\int\pi(x,x^{\prime})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x
for all
x^{\prime}
.
The
1
-Wasserstein distance is
W_{1}(s_{i},s_{j}):=\inf_{\pi}\int|x-x^{\prime}|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\pi(x,x^{\prime})
. The minimizer
\pi^{*}
is called Kantorovich optimal transport plan

;
Santambrogio 2015
)

The idea of calibrating noise for attaining pufferfish privacy is to search a

such that
P_{Y|S}(y|s_{i},\rho)\leq e^{\epsilon}P_{Y|S}(y|s_{j},\rho)
. To do so, we have

\displaystyle P_{Y|S}

\displaystyle(y|s_{i},\rho)-e^{\epsilon}P_{Y|S}(y|s_{j},\rho)

\displaystyle=\int\big(P_{N_{\theta}}(y-x)-e^{\epsilon}P_{N_{\theta}}(y-x^{\prime})\big)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\pi^{*}(x,x^{\prime})

(4)

\displaystyle\leq\int P_{N_{\theta}}(y-x^{\prime})\big(e^{\frac{|x-x^{\prime}|}{\theta}}-e^{\epsilon}\big)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\pi^{*}(x,x^{\prime}),\qquad\forall y

where the last inequality is due to the triangular inequality when we add Laplace noise. See
(
Ding 2022
, Section 3.3)
.
Here, choosing
\pi^{*}
against other couplings is for the purpose of searching a smaller value of
\theta
that is pufferfish privacy attaining.
Requesting
e^{\frac{|x-x^{\prime}|}{\theta}}-e^{\epsilon}\leq 0
for all
(x,x^{\prime})\in\mathcal{X}^{2}
in (
2.1
), a sufficient condition for attaining pufferfish privacy is obtained below.

Proposition 0 (
W_{1}
(Kantorovich) mechanism
(
Ding 2022
, Lemma 1)
).

Adding Laplace noise

with

(5)

\theta=\frac{1}{\epsilon}\max_{\rho,(s_{i},s_{j})\in\mathbb{S}}\sup_{(x,x^{\prime})\in\text{supp}(\pi^{*})}|x-x^{\prime}|

attains
(\epsilon,\mathds{S})
-pufferfish privacy in

.

It is shown that the

mechanism is equivalent to the

mechanism proposed in
(

et al. 2017
)
, but does not have computational problems.
In fact, computing the Kantorovich optimal transport plan

is much easier, which can be done directly using the exiting knowledge of

and
P_{X|S}(\cdot|s_{j},\rho)
: let

and
F_{X|S}(\cdot|s_{j},\rho)
are the cumulative density functions for
P_{X|S}(\cdot|s_{i},\rho)
and
P_{X|S}(\cdot|s_{j},\rho)
, respectively,

(6)

\pi^{*}(x,x^{\prime})=\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}^{2}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x^{\prime}}\min\big\{F_{X|S}(x|s_{i},\rho),F_{X|S}(x^{\prime}|s_{j},\rho)\big\}.

In fact, one does not need to computer
\pi^{*}
to apply the Kantorovich mechanism (
5

to obtain
\sup_{(x,x^{\prime})\in\text{supp}(\pi^{*})}|x-x^{\prime}|
. We will propose such a method in Appendix

in this paper.

Pufferfish Privacy on Different

To protect privacy through pufferfish privacy framework, we need to first specify between which secret pairs certain degree of statistical indistinguishability should be be enforce.
We first consider the following two sets of secret pairs.

\displaystyle\mathds{S}_{a,b}

\displaystyle=\{(s_{a_{i}},s_{b_{i}})\colon i\in\mathcal{V}\},

\displaystyle\mathds{S}_{a,\perp}

\displaystyle=\{(s_{a_{i}},s_{\perp_{i}})\colon i\in\mathcal{V}\}.

They are directly borrowed from differential privacy motivated by guaranteeing indistinguishability in the actual value of each records
(
Dwork
et al. 2006
)
and the existence of individual participants
(
Dwork 2006
)
, respectively, in a dataset. For secret pair set being
\mathds{S}_{a,b}\cup\mathds{S}_{a,\perp}
, we have the same system setting as "privacy with no assumption"
(
Kifer and
Machanavajjhala 2012
, Section 3.2)
, which forms the basis of no-free-lunch privacy
(
Kifer and
Machanavajjhala 2011
)
, where the statistical indistinguishability is enforced for almost all possible intrinsic statistics and therefore, as pointed out in
(
Kifer and
Machanavajjhala 2011
)
, the data utility could be severely damaged.

In addition to instance value of participants’ data, pufferfish privacy framework is more powerful to ensure indistinguishability between individual statistics.
Let
P_{i}(\cdot)
and
Q_{i}(\cdot)
be two distinct probability distributions over the alphabet
\mathcal{D}_{i}
.
Adding and removing user
i
will resulting in different statistics in query answers, the probability mass as well as the alphabet/support, while all secret pairs in
\mathds{S}_{a,b}
and
\mathds{S}_{a,\perp}
would only mean-shift the probability distribution;
If
P_{i}(\cdot)
and
Q_{i}(\cdot)
are dissimilar, the resulting query answer probability will be obviously distinguishably recognizable by an adversarial observer, the situation we want to prevent for privacy protection.
We consider the following two secret pair sets:

\displaystyle\mathds{S}_{P,\perp}

\displaystyle=\{(s_{P_{i}},s_{\perp_{i}})\colon i\in\mathcal{V}\},

\displaystyle\mathds{S}_{P,Q}

\displaystyle=\{(s_{P_{i}},s_{Q_{i}})\colon i\in\mathcal{V}\},

where
(s_{P_{i}},s_{\perp_{i}})
denotes the existence and nonexistence of user
i
obtaining data following distribution
P_{i}(\cdot)
and
\mathds{S}_{P,Q}
denotes resolution whether user
i
is emitting probability
P_{i}(\cdot)
or
Q_{i}(\cdot)
.

In this section, we first derive sufficient conditions for attaining pufferfish privacy in secret pair sets
\mathds{S}_{a,b}
and
\mathds{S}_{a,\perp}
and then
\mathds{S}_{P,\perp}
and
\mathds{S}_{P,Q}
.
These conditions are dependent of the
i
th user itself, not the rest users
-i
, in the system.
We study the multi-user model in Section
3
, which incorporates individual user’s presence probability
\zeta_{i}
. It will be shown that all sufficient conditions derived in this section do not depends on
\zeta_{i}
.
We consider Laplace additive noise
Y=X+N_{\theta}=f(D)+N_{\theta}
, where
P_{Y|S}(y|s)
is determined by the convolution (
3
).

4.1.
(\epsilon,\mathds{S}_{a,b})
-Pufferfish Privacy

To guarantee indistinguishability between two distinct values that a user is reporting, it suffices to calibrate noise to the difference between these two values.

Theorem 1.

For all
i\in\mathcal{V}
, given
a_{i},b_{i}\in\mathcal{D}_{i}
such that
a_{i}\neq b_{i}
, adding Laplace noise
N_{\theta}
with

(10)

\theta=\frac{\max_{i\in\mathcal{V},\rho}|a_{i}-b_{i}|}{\epsilon}.

attains
(\epsilon,\mathds{S}_{a,b})
-pufferfish privacy in
Y
.

Proof.

The proof is similar to
(
Ding 2022
, Lemma 2(a))
, except we incorporate the presence probability
\zeta_{i}
. For
P_{X|S}(\cdot|s_{a_{i}})
in (
7
), we have

\displaystyle P_{Y|S}

\displaystyle(y|s_{a_{i}})

\displaystyle=\int P_{N_{\theta}}(y-x)\Pr(f(D_{-i})=x-a_{i})\prod_{j\in\mathcal{V}\colon j\neq i}\zeta_{j}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\int P_{N_{\theta}}(y-t-a_{i})\Pr(f(D_{-i})=t)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\cdot\prod_{j\in\mathcal{V}\colon j\neq i}\zeta_{j}.

Then,

\displaystyle P_{Y|S}(y|s_{a_{i}})-e^{\epsilon}P_{Y|S}(y|s_{b_{i}})

\displaystyle=\int\frac{1}{2\theta}\Big(e^{-\frac{|y-t-a_{i}|}{\theta}}-e^{\epsilon-\frac{|y-t-b_{i}|}{\theta}}\Big)\Pr(f(D_{-i})=t)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\cdot\prod_{j\in\mathcal{V}\colon j\neq i}\zeta_{j}

\displaystyle=\int\frac{1}{2\theta}e^{-\frac{|y-t-b_{i}|}{\theta}}\cdot\Big(e^{\frac{|y-t-b_{i}|-|y-t-a_{i}|}{\theta}}

(11)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad-e^{\epsilon}\Big)\Pr(f(D_{-i})=t)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\cdot\prod_{j\in\mathcal{V}\colon j\neq i}\zeta_{j}

\displaystyle\leq\int P_{N_{\theta}}(y-t-b_{i})\cdot\Big(e^{\frac{|a_{i}-b_{i}|}{\theta}}-e^{\epsilon}\Big)\Pr(f(D_{-i})=t)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\cdot\prod_{j\in\mathcal{V}\colon j\neq i}\zeta_{j}

\displaystyle=\Big(e^{\frac{|a_{i}-b_{i}|}{\theta}}-e^{\epsilon}\Big)P_{Y|S}(y|s_{b_{i}}),\qquad\forall y,

where (
11
) is due to the triangular inequality. It is clear that
P_{Y|S}(y|s_{a_{i}})\leq e^{\epsilon}P_{Y|S}(y|s_{b_{i}})
for all
y
if
\theta\geq\frac{|a_{i}-b_{i}|}{\epsilon}
. Maximize over all
i\in\mathcal{V}
and prior belief
\rho
. Choose equality to get the minimal value of such
\theta
. We get (
10
).
∎

4.3.
(\epsilon,\mathds{S}_{P,\perp})
-Pufferfish Privacy

We now pay attention to secrets that have impact on the resulting statistics in the query answer

Theorem 3.

Adding Laplace noise
N_{\theta}
attains
(\epsilon,\mathds{S}_{P,\perp})
-pufferfish privacy in
Y
if
\theta
satisfies

(13)

\mathds{E}_{D_{i}\sim P_{i}(\cdot)}\big[e^{\frac{|D_{i}|}{\theta}}\big]=e^{\epsilon}

for all
i\in\mathcal{V}
and
\rho
, or

(14)

\theta=\max\limits_{t\in\mathcal{D}_{i}\colon i\in\mathcal{V},\rho}\frac{|t|}{\epsilon}

Proof.

By (
9
), we have

\displaystyle P_{Y|S}(y|s_{P_{i}})

\displaystyle=\int\Big(\int P_{N_{\theta}}(y-m-t)\Pr(f(D_{-i})=m)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}m\Big)P_{i}(t)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\prod_{j\in\mathcal{V}\colon j\neq i}\zeta_{j}

and then

\displaystyle P_{Y|S}(y|s_{i,P_{i}})-e^{\epsilon}P_{Y|S}(y|s_{\perp_{i}})

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\Pr(f(D_{-i})=m)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}m\cdot P_{i}(t)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\prod_{j\in\mathcal{V}\colon j\neq i}

\displaystyle=P_{Y|S}(y|s_{i,\perp})\int\big(e^{\frac{|t|}{\theta}}-e^{\epsilon}\big)P_{D_{i}}(t)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t,\quad\forall y

4.4.
(\epsilon,\mathds{S}_{P,Q})
-Pufferfish Privacy

For two users emitting different r.v.s, include one (and excluding the other) will result in different probability distribution in the query answer
f(D)
.
Theorem below shows that attaining pufferfish privacy on
\mathds{S}_{P,Q}
depends on the statistics of these two user.

Theorem 4.

Adding Laplace noise
N_{\theta}
with

(16)

\theta=\frac{1}{\epsilon}\sup_{t\in\mathcal{D}_{i}\colon i\in\mathcal{V},\rho}\triangle^{*}(t)

attains
(\epsilon,\mathds{S}_{P,Q})
-pufferfish privacy in
Y
, where

\displaystyle\triangle^{*}(

\displaystyle t)=

(17)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}0&F_{P_{i}}(t)=F_{Q_{i}}(t)\\
\min\{\triangle>0\colon F_{P_{i}}(t)\leq F_{Q_{i}}(t+\triangle)\}&F_{P_{i}}(t)>F_{Q_{i}}(t)\\
\min\{\triangle>0\colon F_{P_{i}}(t)\geq F_{Q_{i}}(t-\triangle)\}&F_{P_{i}}(t)<F_{Q_{i}}(t)\end{cases}.

Proof.

Since

\displaystyle P_{Y|S}

\displaystyle(y|s_{P_{i}},\rho)-e^{\epsilon}P_{Y|S}(y|s_{Q_{i}},\rho)

\displaystyle=\Big(\int P_{N_{\theta}}(y-x)\Pr\big(f(D)=x|D_{i}\sim P_{i}(\cdot)\big)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x-

\displaystyle\qquad e^{\epsilon}\int P_{N_{\theta}}(y-x^{\prime})\Pr\big(f(D)=x|D_{i}\sim Q_{i}(\cdot)\big)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x^{\prime}\Big)\prod_{j\in\mathcal{V}\colon j\neq i}\zeta_{i},

the problem is equivalent to attaining pufferfish privacy for two prior distributions
\Pr\big(f(D)=x|D_{i}\sim P_{i}(\cdot)\big)
and
\Pr\big(f(D)=x|D_{i}\sim Q_{i}(\cdot)\big)
, regardless of the presence probability
\prod_{j\in\mathcal{V}}\zeta_{i}
.
We apply the Kantorovich method to work out a sufficient condition below.

Define cumulative density functions of two priors using an expression different from (
9
) without
\zeta_{i}
for all
i\in\mathcal{V}
by

\displaystyle F_{X|S}(x|s_{P_{i}},\rho)

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{x}\int\Pr(f(D_{-i})=t)P_{i}(m-t)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}m

\displaystyle=\int\Pr(f(D_{-i})=t)F_{P_{i}}(x-t)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t

5.
Conclusion

We studied how to attain individual level indistinguishability using pufferfish privacy framework. We formulated a multi-user system assuming each user reports a realization of a random variable to answer a summation query. Adopting Laplace noise and Kantorovich mechanism, we considered four secret pair sets:
\mathds{S}_{a,b}
and
\mathds{S}_{a,\perp}
discriminates different realizations of users and whether or not users are present in the system;
\mathds{S}_{P,\perp}
and
\mathds{S}_{P,Q}
discriminates different probability distributions of users data and whether or not users are present in the system.
We derived sufficient condition for attaining pufferfish privacy on each secret pair. It is shown that pufferfish privacy guarantees indistinguishability of individual’s data conditions on this individual only, i.e., it is independent of the rest users in the system.
For pufferfish privacy on
\mathds{S}_{P,\perp}
and
\mathds{S}_{P,Q}
, we can utilize the intrinsic statistics to reduce the noise.
We run experiments to show that pufferfish privacy on
\mathds{S}_{P,\perp}
and
\mathds{S}_{P,Q}
guarantees indistinguishability when a class of users are modified, added or removed.

While the multi-user system assigns each user a presence probability indicating the chances of his/her participation, the sufficient conditions derived in this paper are independent of it. The question is under what circumstances (i.e., on which secret pairs), the achievability of pufferfish privacy relies on this presence probability.
On the other hand, it is worth discussing how to extend the results derived in this paper to Rényi pufferfish privacy framework and study how the sufficient conditions change with the Rényi parameter
\alpha
.
In addition, it would be interesting to see how to calibrate Gaussian noise, with fast decaying tail probability, for attaining individual level pufferfish privacy.

A novel predictive model for abrasive waterjet deep hole drilling on AL7075 T6 using machine learning and evolutionary algorithmic approach
Authors: J. Bharani Chandar, M. Sivakumar, N. Lenin, Robert Čep, Sachin Salunkhe, Emad Abouel Nasr, C. Rathinasuriyan
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Abrasive Waterjet (AWJ) is a promising non-traditional method for precision cutting of aerospace materials like AL7075 T6. This work explores AL7075 T6 AWJ-based Deep Hole Drilling (DHD) using a full factorial design with accurate modeling and optimization performed through machine learning and evolutionary algorithms. The objective is to investigate the influence of process parameters and to model, and predict the optimal AWJ-DHD settings, such as waterjet pressure, standoff distance, and abrasive mass flow rate, on drilling qualities including geometrical and dimensional precision (kerf angle, kerf ratio), surface roughness, and drilling efficiency. Four machine learning models, Adaptive Boosted Regression (ABR), Extreme Gradient Boosting (XGB), Decision Tree (DT), and Random Forest (RF) were developed with experimental data to enhance prediction accuracy and process efficiency. Among the developed models, RF had the lowest testing error value for all responses with root mean square values of 0.046 (kerf angle), 0.0078 (kerf ratio), 0.044 (surface roughness), and 0.027 (drilling rate). Moth-Flame Optimization (MFO), Differential Evolution (DE), and Sine Cosine Algorithm (SCA) were used for multi-response optimization of AWJ deep hole drilling parameters. The optimal algorithm for each response was selected using Deng’s similarity-based ranking. The ranking revealed SCA algorithm outperformed MFO and DE. The SCA algorithm discovered optimal parameter setting for AWJ-DHD as a water pressure of 350 MPa, standoff distance of 1.5 mm, and an abrasive mass flow rate of 300 g/min. Under these conditions, the predicted responses were a kerf angle of 0.048⁰, kerf ratio of 0.011, a surface roughness of 1.438 μm, and a drilling rate 0.769 mm/s. The validation trials using optimized parameters yielded a kerf angle of 0.047⁰, a kerf ratio of 0.066, a surface roughness of 1.40 μm, and a drilling rate of 0.769 mm/s, with percentage variations of 2.08%, 3.03%, 2.14%, and 2.65%, respectively, thereby demonstrating the efficiency of the developed machine learning model and optimization technique. The integrated machine learning and evolutionary algorithm framework improved drilling efficiency and hole quality by minimizing surface roughness.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-025-28058-7.

Introduction

Deep hole drilling is a unique machining operation that produces holes with a depth-to-diameter (D/d) ratio more than 3–10, which can sometimes reach 400 or higher. Deep hole drilling is an essential manufacturing process, particularly in the aerospace, automotive, and defence industries, where materials such as AL7075 T6 aluminium alloy, known for their high strength-to-weight ratio, are commonly utilized. Aluminium 7075 T6 is a high-strength, heat-treatable alloy from the 7000 family, predominantly consisting of aluminium, zinc, magnesium, and copper1. The “T6” temper designation signifies that the alloy has undergone solution heat treatment and artificial ageing, substantially improving its mechanical qualities2. Al 7075 T6 is extensively utilized in aircraft (Aircraft wings, fuselages, and structural frames), marine components, automobile parts (Racing car chassis, suspension components), sporting equipment (Bicycles, climbing gear, baseball bats), and military-grade hardware due to its high strength, low density, exceptional fatigue resistance, and moderate corrosion resistance3.

Turning, milling, and drilling are established conventional methods utilized in AL 7075 machining processes. Research utilizing Taguchi L16 and L27 orthogonal arrays indicated that cutting speed, feed rate, and depth of cut have a significant impact on surface roughness and tool wear in turning operations. The implementation of minimum quantity lubrication alongside the Ranque–Hilsch vortex tube resulted in enhanced surface finish and decreased tool wear when contrasted with dry cutting methods4. The application of Response Surface Methodology (RSM) in high-speed turning emphasized the critical role of cutting speed and feed rate in the regulation of surface roughness and material removal rate5. The studies on CNC turning utilizing L27 arrays provided additional confirmation regarding the impact of process parameters on surface quality and material removal6. Research on end milling utilizing response surface methodology indicated that parameters such as tool geometry, feed rate, and depth of cut have a direct impact on cutting forces and surface integrity7. Conventional methods, even with optimizations, frequently encounter limitations such as rapid tool wear, heat generation, challenges in chip formation, and surface defects resulting from work hardening. Studies on drilling with indexable inserts and twist drills revealed the significant influence of feed rate, cutting speed, and drill geometry on process stability, thrust force, and surface quality8. The machinability of Al7075-based hybrid composites reinforced with SiC, graphene, and CNT exhibited elevated cutting forces and tool wear, necessitating customized machining strategies9. Al7075, optimized with alloying elements and reinforced with SiC, demonstrated enhanced mechanical properties and machinability10. Conventional gun drilling frequently encounters challenges with Al7075-T6, attributed to its high strength and low thermal conductivity. This results in accelerated tool wear, chip clogging, and complications in preserving hole straightness in deep drilling applications11.

Unconventional methods have been explored to address traditional limitations. Ultrasonically assisted micro-electrochemical discharge machining (µ-ECDM) enhances micro-hole quality and material removal rate, with voltage, electrolyte concentration, and duty cycle identified as critical parameters12. Electrochemical and micromachining techniques have been noted for their ability to minimize mechanical stresses and tool wear; however, they require intricate setups and incur significant costs13.

Recent studies have demonstrated the effectiveness of AWJ in the hole drilling of Al7075. AWJ machining has become a significant non-conventional machining technique adept at dealing with hard and heat-sensitive materials while minimizing thermal damage. Numerous study have shown the effectiveness of AWJ in machining aluminium alloys, such as AL6061 and AL7075, owing to its cold erosion material removal process and exceptional surface finish quality14,15. Yuvaraj et al.16 conducted experiments on aluminium alloy using AWJ and Cryogenic-Assisted Abrasive Waterjet Machining (CAAWJ) on surface integrity. The findings demonstrate that CAAWJ cutting typically yields enhanced surface quality relative to AWJ cutting. CAAWJ cut surfaces exhibit fewer wear tracks, decreased particle contamination, more consistent roughness profiles, elevated surface compressive residual stress, and enhanced hardness. Tosun et al.17 performed AWJ cutting on aluminium alloy to study the surface integrity. The study indicated that surface roughness increases with increased traversal speeds, since higher speeds yield less material removal per unit time, resulting in poor surface quality. The researchers noted that the upper region of the cut surface demonstrates superior quality relative to the bottom region. Nyaboro et al.18 analyzed the impact of operating parameters, including Waterjet Pressure (Pw), Standoff Distance (Sd), and machining time, on kerf characteristics such as top diameter, depth, aspect ratio, and profile. The findings demonstrated that these parameters have a significant impact on machining performance. Increased Pw and reduced Sd effectively minimized defects and enhanced material removal rates. Increases kerf depth and hole aspect ratio, while Sdand machining time lead to a decrease in both parameters. Karmiris et al19. investigated the influence of various parameters such as Pw, Sd, jet traverse speed, and Abrasive Mass Flow Rate (Fr) on the depth of penetration, width, and taper angle of the produced kerf. In terms of penetration depth, traverse speed emerged as the most critical factor, followed by Pw and Fr. The Sd considerably influenced the kerf top width, accounting for 74.6% of its variation. Qian et al.20 employed AA7075 aluminium alloy specimens to assess cutting quality via surface morphologies and roughness measures. The study seeks to elucidate the impact of varying radii of circular cuts on surface quality and examine the effects of diverse AWJ machining parameters. The research indicated that although linear and circular cuts utilize the identical material removal method, the quality of cylindrical surfaces is typically worse than that of planar surfaces. The surface roughness increased as the radius of circular cuts decreased, with the most significant difference noted in the rough zone.

Artificial Intelligence (AI) and Machine Learning (ML) are becoming increasingly essential in various sectors, facilitating predictive modeling, optimization, and informed decision-making in complex systems. In manufacturing, machine learning has been utilized in CO₂ laser cutting of FFF-printed thermoplastics and PLA-CF composites to forecast kerf width and surface roughness, with hybrid models like PSO-ANFIS demonstrating high predictive accuracy21–23. In food science, PLS-DA and random forest techniques identified significant aroma biomarkers in enzymatically hydrolyzed beef tallow, while sparse partial correlation analysis demonstrated associations between fatty acids and volatile compounds24. ZHPO-LightXBoost enhanced small-sample prediction of pesticide residues in agriculture25,26, while AI-driven multi-strategy optimization advanced 3D reconstruction of transparent objects in computer vision under constrained conditions27. Enhanced random forest models and AI-supported structural optimization have improved network reliability, carbon efficiency, and thermal-fluid performance in energy systems and industrial applications28,29. These studies demonstrate that AI and ML facilitate data-driven prediction, process optimization, and informed decision-making across various domains.

Several research studies were employed to optimize AWJ process parameters on AL 7075 material to enhance the machining/hole quality. In recent years, evolutionary algorithms such as genetic algorithm, grey-wolf optimization algorithm, moth flame optimization, sine cosine optimization, and particle swarm optimization algorithms have been utilized to optimize machining parameters to enhance performance. These algorithms effectively navigate multi-objective landscapes and demonstrate enhanced performance when integrated with alternate models developed through machine learning30–32. Shanmugam et al.26 utilized response surface methodology to optimize the parameters of the AWJ process, examining the impact of these factors on machining outcomes. Initially, an increase in Pw reduced the taper angle; however, further increases resulted in an exponential increase in the taper angle. The primary objective of utilizing these optimization strategies is to achieve optimality. Examples encompass multi-strategy optimization in 3D reconstruction33, hybrid time-domain integral equations with circuit macromodels for efficient simulation34, industrial structural optimization under dual-carbon targets35, and high-efficiency topology optimization for thermal-fluid systems36. In ultrasonic-assisted micro-milling of cortical bone, machine learning models like Random Forest and XGBoost attained the lowest RMSE and MAE values, effectively predicting cutting force and temperature, thus demonstrating their potential for optimizing process parameters and reducing mechanical and thermal damage37. A stacked generalization ensemble model was created for the concurrent prediction of delamination and maximum thrust force in composite drilling, surpassing individual learners by decreasing prediction errors by more than 90%. Feature significance analysis identified feed rate and tool type as the most contributing parameters38. In additive manufacturing, a weighted ensemble of surrogate models integrated with a hybrid NSGA-II + TOPSIS optimization framework significantly improved prediction accuracy for the density and ovality of FDM components39. The studies indicate that systematic exploration of the solution space, integration of multiple objectives, and the application of AI and computational methods are essential for attaining efficient, high-performance, and feasible designs.

In summary, AWJM has been investigated for drilling a range of materials; however, its utilization in deep hole drilling of Al7075 T6 has not been extensively studied. Nonetheless, there is a lack of experimental validation, process modeling, and optimization techniques tailored to deep hole drilling in Al7075 with AWJ. In aerospace and automotive components, where traditional techniques frequently result in thermal damage, excessive tool wear, or compromised surface integrity. For these kinds of applications, AWJ’s non-thermal, contactless cutting mechanism provides clear benefits. Furthermore, there hasn’t been much focus on incorporating evolutionary optimization algorithms and machine learning models into AWJ process design. This study addresses existing gaps by employing a comprehensive experimental-computational approach that combines AWJ-DHD experiments, ML-based prediction, and EA-based optimization to improve geometrical accuracy, surface quality, and drilling efficiency.

There is a growing need in the manufacturing and aerospace industries for materials that can handle a lot of mechanical and thermal stress while still being light and easy to work with. AL7075 T6 alloy is principally made up of aluminum, zinc (5.1–6.1%), magnesium (2.1–2.9%), and copper (1.2–2.0.2.0%), all of which contribute to its exceptional mechanical properties. It has a very high strength-to-weight ratio, is very resistant to fatigue, and is resistant to corrosion in some environments AL7075 T6 is used a lot in the aerospace, automotive, military, and sports equipment industries, especially in structural parts like aircraft wings, fuselage frames, and parts that can hold a lot of weight40–42. These parts need to be both light and strong. A flat AL7075 T6 plate measuring 75 mm × 75 mm × 12 mm (L × B × D) was used as the workpiece material for this study. This size was chosen to resemble real-world conditions found in aerospace brackets, mechanical joints, and electronic enclosures.

For complex operations like DHD in aerospace-grade alloys, choosing appropriate process parameters is essential to achieving the required machining quality. Three important input parameters are the focus of this study: Pw, Sd, and Fr variables have a major impact on surface integrity, penetration depth, material removal rate, and hole dimensional accuracy. The geometry of the hole was measured using a Helmel Coordinate Measuring Machine (CMM) with the following specifications: X = 400 mm, Y = 400 mm, Z = 350 mm. In accordance with the specified nominal hole diameter of 2 mm, a small ruby-tipped touch probe was utilized, featuring a stylus ball diameter of 0.5 mm. The probe was equipped with a short, rigid stylus assembly, maintaining an effective stylus length of 12 mm, to facilitate clearance and reduce bending error. For each hole, 72 equally spaced points were acquired around the circumference with a 5° angular step at three axial planes: z = 0.5 mm (top), z = 6.0 mm (mid-thickness), and z = 11.5 mm (bottom). Probing force and trigger settings adhered to the manufacturer’s recommendations. Least-squares circle fits were calculated for each plane, and diameters were adjusted for the stylus ball radius to derive accurate bore diameters. The CMM and stylus configuration were calibrated using traceable ring and step gauges prior to measurements. Repeatability was assessed by five repeated measurements of a reference gauge and three representative holes. The machine specification (resolution 0.001 mm; repeatability 0.010 mm; accuracy 0.010 mm) combined with observed repeatability yields a conservative expanded uncertainty (k = 2) for diameter measurement of ± 0.010 mm. Machine learning and algorithmic approach

This study utilized a blend of machine learning models and evolutionary optimization algorithms to predict and enhance deep-hole drilling outcomes in Al7075 T6. Decision Tree (DT) and Random Forest (RF) were chosen for their precision and capacity to represent non-linear interactions between process parameters and responses49. AdaBoost Regressor (ABR) and Extreme Gradient Boosting (XGB) were incorporated to capture intricate interactions and deliver strong multi-response prediction50. These selected ML models are superior compared to alternatives like SVR and ANN for moderate-sized experimental datasets because of their robustness, interpretability, and low sensitivity to hyperparameters51. In this work, the experimental data was split in the ratio of 80:20, which divided the datasets into training and testing datasets. The training dataset was used for model development, while the testing dataset was used to estimate the prediction accuracy. The optimal AWJ-DHD output predictor was established by model comparison. The chosen model facilitates better process control and optimization of AWJ-DHD processes. Modeling was done with Python 2.7.18 on a 16 GB RAM system running Windows 11 64-bit and Intel® Xeon® CPU E3–1240 v5 @ 3.50 GHz.

The complete code for model building and assessment was written in Python, without utilizing pre-existing machine learning libraries such as scikit-learn, TensorFlow, or analogous frameworks. This methodology facilitated comprehensive oversight of the implementation, precise tuning of the algorithms, and ensured transparency in the model’s framework.

To enhance the conflicting objectives present in AWJ-DHD, this study employs a Sine Cosine Optimization Algorithm (SCA) approach to identify the optimal combination of Pw, Sd, and Fr for concurrently minimizing θa, θr, Sr, and maximizing the Dr. The proposed the SCA as a population-based evolutionary optimization technique based on the mathematical behaviour of sine and cosine functions, to efficiently explore and exploit the search space. SCA’s mathematical model is capable of handling multidimensional, nonlinear, and multimodal problems. SCA’s adaptive trigonometric search mechanism allows it to achieve faster convergence than other algorithms such as Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO) or Genetic Algorithm (GA). According to the literature, SCA avoids local optima better than traditional approaches due to its non-repetitive oscillating nature, making it appropriate for multi-response AWJM optimization52. In addition, the efficacy of the selected SCA optimization algorithm was compared with other popular algorithms such as Moth Flame Optimization (MFO) and Differential Evolution Algorithm (DE). MFO adeptly balances exploration and exploitation, DE provides rapid convergence and resilience in continuous, high-dimensional spaces. The pseudocode for the mentioned algorithms are fetched from previous works53–55. This integrated ML and evolutionary optimization framework offers a thorough and dependable method for predicting and optimizing AWJ deep-hole drilling of Al7075 T6, guaranteeing high-quality holes and effective process performance. 3D scattered plot for AWJ-DHD responses

The 3D scatter plot, Fig. 6 (a) illustrates the variation of θa with respect to three major AWJ-DHD parameters. θa values are represented by a color gradient, with violet to blue indicating lower θa values and increasing values trending towards green and yellow. One can see from the plot that θa reduces for higher Pw values, i.e., increasing jet energy results in more uniform kerf walls and reducing angular deviation. But higher Sd values (above 2.0 mm) lead to higher θa, perhaps because of higher jet divergence and loss of energy before impacting the material surface, causing tapering of the cut. Fr also contributes significantly; high Fr (above 450 g/min) increases the θa because of the absence of interaction time between the abrasive jet and workpiece, resulting in less controlled material removal and steeper kerf walls; this trend agrees with observations made in prior AWJM studies on aluminum alloys58.Minimum θa values (dark color) occur at high Pw, low Sd, and intermediate Fr, and here is the precision and effective cutting. Contrarily, the combinations of low Pw, high Sd, and high Fr lead to maximum θa (yellow marked), the poorest for dimensional accuracy.

The 3D scatter plot, Fig. 6 (b) illustrates the relationship between Pw, Sd, and Fr on the θr in AWJ-DHD, with θr indicated by a colour gradient from dark violet (low θr) to bright yellow (high θr). A noticeable trend is evident throughout the parameter space: reduced θr values are typically noted at elevated Pw (exceeding 300 MPa), diminished Sd (around 1.5–1.8 mm), and lower to moderate Fr (300–375 g/min). This suggests that a concentrated, high-pressure jet with limited dispersion attained through low Sd alongside a reduced Fr enhances cutting efficiency and results in a narrower kerf, thus reducing kerf loss. Conversely, elevated θr values (green to yellow zones) are concentrated in areas of low Pw (250–280 MPa), high Sd (exceeding 2.2 mm), and increased Fr (above 425 g/min). This indicates that, under these circumstances, the jet experiences a loss of cutting precision attributed to diminished kinetic energy, sharp divergence, and inadequate material interaction time. The scatter distribution highlights the combined effects of the parameters: even at moderate Pw, high Sd and Fr result in increased θr, illustrating the non-linear interactions among variables.

The 3D scatter plot, Fig. 6 (c) shows the influence of three key parameters on Sr represented by a colour gradient from violet (indicating low Sr) to yellow (indicating high Sr). A general trend suggests that Sr rises with an increase in Sd, especially beyond 2 mm. This phenomenon is linked to enhanced jet dispersion and diminished jet focus at greater Sd, resulting in irregular surface erosion and a rougher finish. The influence of Fr is significant, as higher Fr values (exceeding 425 g/min) are associated with increased Sr, attributed to inadequate interaction time between the jet and the work surface. Conversely, Pw exhibits an inverse relationship with Sr; elevated Pw levels (exceeding 310 MPa) lead to diminished Sr values, due to increased jet energy facilitating more efficient and uniform material removal; this result that aligns with previously published work on AWJM of AA707558. The data points exhibiting the lowest Sr, indicated by dark violet to blue, are primarily concentrated at high Pw, low Sd, and moderate Fr values. This pattern suggests that a well-collimated and energetic jet, utilized at an optimal feed rate, results in an enhanced surface finish. The yellow points indicating maximum Sr are located in areas characterized by low Pw, high Sd, and high Fr, demonstrating a cumulative negative impact.

Figure 6 (d) indicates the variation of Dr as a function of Pw, Sd, and Fr. The colour bar illustrates Dr, where darker shades (purple/blue) signify lower Dr values and lighter shades (green/yellow) represent higher Dr values. The plot indicates that Pw has a significant positive effect on Dr elevated Pw (near 340 MPa) correlates with increased Dr, implying that greater jet energy promotes more rapid penetration and material removal. Similar findings were reported in prior work, where Pw was identified as a critical factor59. Lower Sd values (1.6 mm) are associated with increased depth of cut, likely resulting from diminished jet dispersion and enhanced energy concentration at the cutting surface. Fr is significant; a moderate Fr of 350–400 g/min is optimal. Very high flow rates decrease the dwelling time of the jet, which limits erosion efficiency, whereas lower flow rates may enhance stability in jet-material interaction but reduce overall productivity. The points representing maximum drilling rate (yellow region) are concentrated at high weight%, low Sr, and moderate Fr, thereby confirming the synergistic influence of these parameters on enhancing drilling efficiency. Conversely, low Pw, high Sd, and either excessively high or low Fr are associated with the lowest Dr, as illustrated by the violet-colored clusters. The analysis indicates that maximizing Dr is achievable through the integration of high Pw, short Sd, and moderate Fr, which enhances process throughput while maintaining quality.

The efficacy of each model was assessed by statistical metrics including R², Root Mean Square Error (RMSE), and Mean Square Error (MAE), computed for the entire dataset, the training subset, and the testing subset. Additionally, to enhance robustness and reduce bias, a 5-fold cross-validation method was utilized.

The RMSE is a critical measure in assessing the concordance between the predicted outcomes and the experimental results. A lower value of RMSE implies a greater concordance of the predicted model with the actual data. In addition to RMSE, MSE is used to measure the mean of the squared differences between the predicted and actual values, thus complementing the role of RMSE in measuring the model’s forecast precision. Another key measure is the Mean Absolute Error (MAE), which computes the average magnitude of errors within a sequence of predictions regardless of direction. This is especially helpful in model evaluation where scale-dependent error is of interest. Apart from this, Relative Standard Error (RSE) and Relative Absolute Error (RAE) are utilized to determine the relative precision and reliability between predicted results and experimental responses of AWJM. Decrease in the values of RSE and RAE is desirable and represents an improved machine learning model.

To choose the best-suited ML algorithm for the prediction of AWJ-DHD response, the above performance metrics are compared. The best algorithm is the one that provides the lowest RMSE, MSE, MAE, RSE, and RAE. Among the four machine learning models tested (ABR, XGB, DT, and RF), RF performed the most accurately and consistently across all AWJ-DHD response variables. This conclusion is based on the low training and testing RMSE, MAE, and RSE error values presented in Tables 9, 10 and 11, which indicate good learning potential and generalization.

This research underscores that while SCA necessitates increased processing resources, its consistency and enhanced solution quality (as demonstrated by Pareto front convergence and Deng’s value) validate the extended run time in quality-sensitive applications.

Conclusions

This study proposed a comprehensive analysis of abrasive waterjet deep hole drilling on AL7075 T6, employing a machine learning prediction, and evolutionary algorithm-based optimization. The effects of water pressure, standoff distance, and abrasive mass flow rate on kerf angle, kerf ratio, surface roughness, and drilling rate were extensively studied.

ANOVA identified Pw and Sd as having the highest impact on variation of kerf angle and thus hole geometry. Similarly, kerf ratio analysis also established Pw and Sd as dominant parameters. In contrast, the surface roughness was largely determined by Sd and Pw, reflecting their direct role in determining machined surface finish.

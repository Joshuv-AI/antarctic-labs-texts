The Quiver Problem is NP Complete
Victor Kac, Bangzheng Li

Abstract
. We prove that the quiver problem is NP complete.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
16G20, 68Q25

1.
Introduction

Recall that a
representation

R
of a quiver (i.e., oriented graph)
Q
over a field
\mathbb{F}
is a collection of finite-dimensional vector spaces
U_{v}
over
\mathbb{F}
, labeled by vertices of
Q
, and linear maps
R_{v,w}:U_{v}\to U_{w}
, labeled by oriented edges
v\to w
of
Q
.
The representation
R
is called absolutely indecomposable if it does not decompose in a direct sum of two non-zero representations over the algebraic closure of
\mathbb{F}
.
Choosing bases of the vector spaces
U_{v}
,
R
is given by a collection of matrices
R_{v,w}
over
\mathbb{F}
of size
n_{v}\times n_{w}
for each oriented edge
v\to w
, where
n_{v}=\dim U_{v}
.
Given such a collection of
\sum_{v\to w}u_{v}u_{w}
elements of
\mathbb{F}
, the output is YES if
R
is absolutely indecomposable, and NO otherwise.
Call this problem INDEC.
This is a P-problem if
\mathbb{F}
is a finite field, according to
[
1
, Theorem 2]
.

Define a generalization of INDEC for a non-negative integer
M
, denoted
\operatorname{INDEC}[X_{1},\ldots,X_{M}]
, where the entries of matrices
R_{v,w}
are either elements of
\mathbb{F}
or one of the indeterminats
X_{1},\ldots,X_{M}
.
Say YES for this problem if there exist
x_{1},\ldots,x_{M}\in\mathbb{F}
that we can substitute
X_{1},\ldots,X_{M}
, such that the resulting INDEC problem is YES, and NO otherwise.
We call this the
quiver problem
.
It follows from
[
1
, Theorem 2]
that this is an NP problem if
\mathbb{F}
is a finite field.

In the present paper we prove that the problem
\operatorname{INDEC}[X_{1},\ldots,X_{M}]
is NP complete if
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{F}_{2}
.

In more detail, let
\Theta=E_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge E_{L}
be a 3-cnf Boolean formula
[
3
, Chapter 7]
, where
E_{k}=\beta_{1}^{{\left(k\right)}}\vee\beta_{2}^{{\left(k\right)}}\vee\beta_{3}^{{\left(k\right)}},k=1,\ldots,L
, are clauses,
\beta_{j}^{{\left(k\right)}}\in{\left\{X_{1},\ldots,X_{M},\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX_{1}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu,\ldots,\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX_{M}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\right\}}
, and the indeterminates
X_{i}
take values 0 or 1 in
\mathbb{F}_{2}
, so that
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX_{i}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
takes values 1 or 0 respectively.

We will construct a quiver
Q_{\Theta}
and its representation
R_{\Theta}(X_{1},\ldots,X_{M})
from
\operatorname{INDEC}[X_{1},\ldots,X_{M}]
, such that
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{M})\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{M}
makes
\Theta
evaluate to 1 if and only if the representation
R_{\Theta}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{M})
is absolutely indecomposable.
This will imply that the problem
\operatorname{INDEC}[X_{1},\ldots,X_{M}]
over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
is NP complete, since the problem 3-SAT is NP complete (see
[
3
, Chapter 7]
).

Recall that an
endomorphism
of a representation
R
is a collection of endomorphisms
a_{v}:U_{v}\to U_{v}
, such that
R_{v,w}a_{v}=a_{w}R_{v,w}
.
For example each
c\in\mathbb{F}
defines an obvious endomorphism
a_{v}=cI_{U_{v}}
of
R
.
Recall that
R
is called a
Schur representation
if its only endomorphisms are the obvious ones.
We show that, moreover,
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{M})\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{M}
makes
\Theta
evaluate to 1, then
R_{\Theta}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{M})
is a Schur representation, and if it makes
\Theta
evaluate to 0, then
R_{\Theta}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{M})
decomposes in a direct sum of two non-zero representations.

2.
Construction of the quiver
Q_{\Theta}

Here is an example of quiver corresponding to the formula
(X_{1}\vee X_{2}\vee\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX_{3}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu)\wedge(X_{2}\vee X_{3}\vee X_{5})\wedge(X_{3}\vee\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX_{4}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\vee\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX_{5}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu)
(so
M=5,L=3
), where the clauses
X_{1}\vee X_{2}\vee\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX_{3}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu,X_{2}\vee X_{3}\vee X_{5}
, and
X_{3}\vee\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX_{4}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\vee\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX_{5}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
correspond to vertices
w_{1},w_{2}
, and
w_{3}
respectively.

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{w_{1}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{w_{3}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{v_{2}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{v_{3}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{v_{1}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{v_{4}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{v_{5}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{u_{2}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{u_{3}}}

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{u_{4}}}

We will first do the following preprocessing to make all variables in a clause
E_{k}
to be distinct, i.e., there is no two
X_{i}
’s or a
X_{i}
and a
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX_{i}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
appearing at the same time in
E_{k}
.
First, if there is a
X_{i}
and a
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX_{i}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
appearing at the same time, then this clause always evaluate to true, so we can simply delete it.

Now we discuss the case where (at least) two
X_{i}
appears at the same time in
E_{k}
.
First note that we can force a variable
z
to be true by including clauses
(z\vee b\vee c)\wedge(z\vee b\vee\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muc\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu)\wedge(z\vee\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mub\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\vee c)\wedge(z\vee\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mub\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\vee\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muc\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu)
, where
b
and
c
are new variables.
Then for a clause
X_{i}\vee X_{i}\vee Y
, where
X_{i}\neq Y
, we can simply modify it to
X_{i}\vee z\vee Y
(
z
is a new variable) then force
z
to be true.
For a clause
X_{i}\vee X_{i}\vee X_{i}
, we can simply replace it by the clauses that forces
X_{i}
to be true.
The same argument works for two
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX_{i}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
’s appearing in the same clause.

With the assumption that all variables in a clause are distinct, we describe our construction of the quiver.
There are
2M+L
vertices, labeled by
u_{1},\ldots,u_{M},v_{1},\ldots,v_{M},w_{1},\ldots,w_{L}
.
To give the arrows between them, for each
i=1,2,\ldots,M
, let

\Omega_{i}^{+}={\left\{k\in\llbracket 1,L\rrbracket:X_{i}\text{ appears in }E_{k}\right\}}={\left\{k\in\llbracket 1,L\rrbracket:\beta_{j}^{{\left(k\right)}}=X_{i}\text{ for some }j\in\llbracket 1,3\rrbracket\right\}}\subset\llbracket 1,L\rrbracket,

where
\llbracket a,b\rrbracket={\left\{k\in\mathbb{Z}:a\leq k\leq b\right\}}
.
Similarly, define

\Omega_{i}^{-}={\left\{k\in\llbracket 1,L\rrbracket:\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX_{i}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\text{ appears in }E_{k}\right\}}={\left\{k\in\llbracket 1,L\rrbracket:\beta_{j}^{{\left(k\right)}}=\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX_{i}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\text{ for some }j\in\llbracket 1,3\rrbracket\right\}}\subset\llbracket 1,L\rrbracket

and

\Omega_{i}=\Omega_{i}^{+}\cup\Omega_{i}^{-}\subset\llbracket 1,L\rrbracket.

For each
i\in\llbracket 1,M\rrbracket
, there is an arrow from
u_{i}
to
u_{i+1}
, where we identify
u_{M+1}=u_{1}
.

For each
i\in\llbracket 1,M\rrbracket
, there is an arrow from
u_{i}
to
v_{i}
.

For each
i\in\llbracket 1,M\rrbracket
and
k\in\Omega_{i}
, there is an arrow from
v_{i}
to
w_{k}
and an arrow from
w_{k}
to
v_{i}
.

There is no other arrows and we have constructed our quiver
Q_{\Theta}
.

3.
Construction of the representation
R_{\Theta}(X_{1},\ldots,X_{M})

For each
u_{i}(i=1,\ldots,M)
, we assign a 1-dim vector space
U_{i}=\mathbb{F}
.

For each
v_{i}(i=1,\ldots,M)
, we assign a
n_{i}:={\left({\left\lvert\Omega_{i}\right\rvert}+1\right)}
-dim vector space
V_{i}=\mathbb{F}^{n_{i}}
.

For each
w_{k}(k=1,\ldots,L)
, we assign a 1-dim vector space
W_{k}=\mathbb{F}
.

We now describe the linear map for each arrow between them.

3.1.
Bottom horizontal arrows

For each
i\in\llbracket 1,M\rrbracket
, take a linear map from
U_{i}=\mathbb{F}
to
U_{i+1}=\mathbb{F}
, which is the identity map
\mathrm{id}:\mathbb{F}\to\mathbb{F}
.
Here we assume
U_{M+1}=U_{1}
.

3.2.
Arrows from
U_{i}
to
V_{i}

For each
i\in\llbracket 1,M\rrbracket
, take a linear map from
U_{i}=\mathbb{F}
to
V_{i}=\mathbb{F}^{n_{i}}
sending
1\in\mathbb{F}
to
(1,X_{i},0,0,\ldots,0)\in\mathbb{F}[X_{1},\ldots,X_{M}]\otimes_{\mathbb{F}}V_{i}
.

We denote by
T_{i}
(meaning True) the vector
(1,1,0,0,\ldots,0)\in V_{i}
, and
F_{i}
(meaning False) the vector
(1,0,0,\ldots,0)\in V_{i}
.

For each
k\in\Omega_{i}
, we select a vector
e_{i}(k)\in V_{i}
such that the set (of cardinality
n_{i}+1
)

{\left\{e_{i}(k):k\in\Omega_{i}\right\}}\cup{\left\{T_{i},F_{i}\right\}}

has the property that all its subsets of cardinality
n_{i}
form a basis in
V_{i}
.
This can be done by first assigning all but one of the
e_{i}(k)
’s together with
{\left\{T_{i},F_{i}\right\}}
to form a basis for
V_{i}
, then take the last
e_{i}(k)
to be the sum of vectors from this basis.

3.3.
+
type arrows from
V_{i}
to
W_{k}

For each
i\in\llbracket 1,M\rrbracket
such that
k\in\Omega_{i}^{+}
, take a linear map from
V_{i}=\mathbb{F}^{{\left\lvert\Omega_{i}\right\rvert}+1}
to
W_{k}=\mathbb{F}
sending the set

{\left\{F_{i}\right\}}\cup{\left\{e_{i}(k^{\prime}):k^{\prime}\in\Omega_{i}-{\left\{k\right\}}\right\}}\subset V_{i}

to
0\in\mathbb{F}
and
T_{i}\in V_{i}
to
1\in\mathbb{F}
.
This uniquely determines the map (in polynomial time) from the choice of
e_{i}(k)
’s.

3.4.
-
type arrows from
V_{i}
to
W_{k}

For each
i\in\llbracket 1,M\rrbracket
such that
k\in\Omega_{i}^{-}
, take a linear map from
V_{i}=\mathbb{F}^{{\left\lvert\Omega_{i}\right\rvert}+1}
to
W_{k}=\mathbb{F}
sending the set

{\left\{T_{i}\right\}}\cup{\left\{e_{i}(k^{\prime}):k^{\prime}\in\Omega_{i}-{\left\{k\right\}}\right\}}\subset V_{i}

to
0\in\mathbb{F}
and
F_{i}\in V_{i}
to
1\in\mathbb{F}
.
This uniquely determines the map (in polynomial time) from the choice of
e_{i}(k)
’s.

3.5.
Arrows from
W_{k}
to
V_{i}

For each
i\in\llbracket 1,M\rrbracket
such that
k\in\Omega_{i}
, take a linear map from
W_{k}=\mathbb{F}
to
V_{i}=\mathbb{F}^{n_{i}}
sending
1\in\mathbb{F}
to
e_{i}(k)\in V_{i}
.

4.
If
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{M})\in\mathbb{F}^{M}
makes
\Theta
evaluate to 0 (False), then
R_{\Theta}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{M})
is not indecomposable

Then there exists
k\in\llbracket 1,L\rrbracket
such that
E_{k}
evaluate to 0 (False) under the current selection of
x_{1},\ldots,x_{M}
.

Then we claim
R(x_{1},\ldots,x_{M})=R_{1}\oplus R_{2}
, where

R_{1}=W_{k}\oplus\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in\llbracket 1,M\rrbracket\\
k\in\Omega_{i}\end{subarray}}\mathbb{F}e_{i}(k)\subset W_{k}\oplus\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in\llbracket 1,M\rrbracket\\
k\in\Omega_{i}\end{subarray}}V_{i}.

and

R_{2}=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{M}U_{i}\oplus\bigoplus_{i=1}^{M}V_{i}^{\prime}\oplus\bigoplus_{k^{\prime}\neq k}W_{k^{\prime}}\subset\bigoplus_{i=1}^{M}U_{i}\oplus\bigoplus_{i=1}^{M}V_{i}\oplus\bigoplus_{k^{\prime}\neq k}W_{k^{\prime}},

where

V_{i}^{\prime}=\begin{cases}V_{i}&k\not\in\Omega_{i}\\
\mathbb{F}T_{i}\oplus\bigoplus_{k^{\prime}\in\Omega_{i}-{\left\{k\right\}}}\mathbb{F}e_{i}(k^{\prime})&k\in\Omega_{i}\text{ and }x_{i}=1\\
\mathbb{F}F_{i}\oplus\bigoplus_{k^{\prime}\in\Omega_{i}-{\left\{k\right\}}}\mathbb{F}e_{i}(k^{\prime})&k\in\Omega_{i}\text{ and }x_{i}=0\end{cases}.

First, it is easy to see that
R(x_{1},\ldots,x_{M})=R_{1}\oplus R_{2}
as vector spaces.
Now we check each
R_{1}
and
R_{2}
are preserved under the arrows.

(a)

The bottom horizontal arrows between
U_{i}
’s are obviously preserved.

(b)

The vertical arrows from
U_{i}
to
V_{i}
are preserved from the selection of
V_{i}^{\prime}
.

(c)

The vertical arrows from
W_{k}
to
V_{i}
’s are preserved because for each possible codomain
V_{i}(k\in\Omega_{i})
we have
e_{i}(k)
is in
R_{1}
.

(d)

The vertical arrows from
W_{k^{\prime}}
(
k^{\prime}\neq k
) to
V_{i}
’s are preserved because
V_{i}
is in
R_{2}
if
k\not\in\Omega_{i}
and
e_{i}(k^{\prime})
is in
R_{2}
if
k\in\Omega_{i}
.

(e)

The vertical arrows from
V_{i}
to
W_{k}
(hence
k\in\Omega_{i}
) are preserved because if we write
f:V_{i}\to W_{k}
, then
f(e_{i}(k^{\prime}))=0
for all
k^{\prime}\neq 0
.
Also, when
x_{i}=1
, then because
E_{k}
is evaluated to
0
we see
k\in\Omega_{i}^{-}
, hence
f
maps
T_{i}\in V_{i}
to
0\in W_{k}
.
When
x_{i}=0
, then because
E_{k}
is evaluated to
0
we see
k\in\Omega_{i}^{+}
, hence
f
maps
F_{i}\in V_{i}
to
0\in W_{k}
.

(f)

The vertical arrows from
V_{i}
to
W_{k^{\prime}}(k^{\prime}\neq k)
are preserved because each
e_{i}(k)\in V_{i}
is mapped to
0
in
W_{k^{\prime}}
.

This shows that
R_{\Theta}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{M})
is not indecomposable, as desired.

5.
R_{\Theta}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{M})
is a Schur representation if
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{M})
makes
\Theta
evaluate to 1

Let
\Theta
be a satisfiable 3-cnf formula.
We will now show that the root we constructed above is a Schur root.
More specifically, we will show that if
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{M})\in\mathbb{F}^{M}
makes
\Theta
evaluate to 1, then the representation

satisfies
\operatorname{End}R={\left\{c\cdot\mathrm{id}:c\in\mathbb{F}\right\}}
.
In particular,

is absolutely indecomposable.

To see this, pick any
\varphi\in\operatorname{End}R
.
Since
{\left\{c\cdot\mathrm{id}\right\}}\subset\operatorname{End}R
, after subtracting suitable multiple of

we can assume
\varphi\!\mid_{U_{1}}=0
.
Then we claim

since we have commutative diagram

.

and

.

, so from the choice of

.

, i.e.,

is a Schur representation.

Furthermore, we show that
\dim R_{\Theta}
is a Schur root no matter whether

can evaluate to true or not.

so that only

appear and no

).
Let the modified version of
\Theta
be
\Theta^{\prime}
, then
\Theta^{\prime}
is able to evaluate to true (by plugging in

).

\dim R_{\Theta}=\dim R_{\Theta^{\prime}}
, and the above argument shows that
\dim R_{\Theta^{\prime}}
is a Schur root, so
\dim R_{\Theta}
is also a Schur root, as desired.

6.
Dimension of
R_{\Theta}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{M})
is an imaginary Schur root

Let
\Gamma
be a free abelian group with basis
{\left\{\alpha_{v}\right\}}
, labelled by the vertices of the graph
Q
, and we assume that
Q
has no self loops.
Define a symmetric integer valued bilinear form
(\cdot,\cdot)
on
\Gamma
by letting
(\alpha_{v},\alpha_{v})=2,(\alpha_{v},\alpha_{w})=-\#(\text{edges connecting }v\text{ and }w)
.
Let
r_{v}
be the orthogonal reflection of
\Gamma
with respect to
\alpha_{v}
, i.e.,

r_{v}(\gamma)=\gamma-(\gamma,\alpha_{v})\alpha_{v},

and let
W
be the group, generated by these reflections.

The element
\dim R=\sum_{v}(\dim U_{v})\alpha_{v}\in\Gamma
is called the
dimension
of the representation
R
of the quiver
Q
.

Denote by
\Delta_{Q}
the set of dimensions of absolutely indecomposable representation of the quiver
Q
over a finite field
\mathbb{F}
.
Recall that this set is independent of the orientation of
Q
and of the field
\mathbb{F}
, and is called the set of
roots
of the graph
Q

[
2
]
.
Recall that the set of roots
\Delta_{Q}\setminus{\left\{\alpha_{v}\right\}}
is
r_{v}
-invariant
[
2
]
.
Recall that a root
\alpha
is called
imaginary
if and only if its
W
-orbit intersects the fundamental domain
C={\left\{\alpha\in\Gamma\mid(\alpha,\alpha_{v})\leq 0\text{ for all }v\right\}}
.
A root
\alpha
is called a
Schur root
if there exists a Schur representation of dimension
\alpha
.
A Schur root
\alpha
is imaginary if and only if
(\alpha,\alpha)\leq 0
.

In the special case of a quiver
Q_{\Theta}
it is natural to take
\Gamma
a free abelian group with basis

\alpha_{u_{1}},\ldots,\alpha_{u_{M}},\alpha_{v_{1}},\ldots,\alpha_{v_{M}},\alpha_{w_{1}},\ldots,\alpha_{w_{L}}.

Then all non-zero inner products between these elements are:

\displaystyle(\alpha_{p},\alpha_{p})=2,

\displaystyle\text{ for each vertex }p

\displaystyle(\alpha_{u_{i}},\alpha_{u_{i+1}})=-1,

\displaystyle\text{ where }i=1,\ldots,M\text{ and }u_{M+1}=u_{1};

\displaystyle(\alpha_{u_{i}},\alpha_{v_{i}})=-1,

\displaystyle\text{ where }i=1,\ldots,M;

\displaystyle(\alpha_{v_{i}},\alpha_{w_{k}})=-2,

\displaystyle\text{ where }i=1,\ldots,M,k\in\Omega_{i}.

We have

\alpha:=\dim R_{\Theta}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{M})=\sum_{i=1}^{M}\alpha_{u_{i}}+\sum_{i=1}^{M}n_{i}\alpha_{v_{i}}+\sum_{k=1}^{L}\alpha_{w_{k}}.

Note that
\alpha
is always a Schur root since it admits a Schur representation.
To see this, just note that in the 3-cnf formula, if we replace all
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX_{i}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
by
X_{i}
, then the resulting 3-cnf formula has a solution (let each
X_{i}
to be true), and the previous section tells us that the root corresponding to this 3-cnf formula is a Schur root.
However, these two roots are exactly the same, so we see
\alpha
is indeed a Schur root.

Now

(\alpha,\alpha_{u_{i}})=-n_{i},(\alpha,\alpha_{v_{i}})=1,(\alpha,\alpha_{w_{k}})=2-2\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\leq M\\
\mathstrut k\in\Omega_{i}\end{subarray}}n_{i}=-4-2\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\leq M\\
\mathstrut k\in\Omega_{i}\end{subarray}}{\left\lvert\Omega_{i}\right\rvert}.

Therefore

(\alpha,\alpha)=-\sum_{i=1}^{M}n_{i}+\sum_{i=1}^{M}n_{i}-4L-2\sum_{i=1}^{M}{\left\lvert\Omega_{i}\right\rvert}^{2}=-4L-2\sum_{i=1}^{M}{\left\lvert\Omega_{i}\right\rvert}^{2}\leq 0,

from which we see
\alpha
is an imaginary Schur root.

Construction for an arbitrary finite field
\mathbb{F}

We can generalize the above construction to arbitrary finite field
\mathbb{F}
.

Here is an example illustrating the construction for
(X_{1}\vee X_{2}\vee\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX_{3}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu)\wedge(X_{2}\vee X_{3}\vee X_{5})\wedge(X_{3}\vee\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX_{4}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\vee\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX_{5}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu)
(so
M=5,L=3
).
The quiver here is just taking the
w
part of the previous quiver
B:=({\left\lvert\mathbb{F}\right\rvert}-1)^{3}
times, but we just draw
2
copies of each
w
(instead of
B
) to avoid making the diagram too complicated.

All
U_{i}
’s and
W_{k}^{{\left(\ell\right)}}(1\leq\ell\leq B)
’s will have dimension 1, and
\dim V_{i}=n_{i}
, where
n_{i}=B{\left\lvert\Omega_{i}\right\rvert}+1
.
All arrows are the same as in the previous construction, except that all
w_{k}^{{\left(\ell\right)}}
will have arrows coming in and out the same as the previous
w_{k}
:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{w_{2}^{(1)}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{w_{2}^{(2)}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{w_{1}^{(2)}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{w_{3}^{(1)}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{w_{1}^{(1)}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{w_{3}^{(2)}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{v_{2}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{v_{3}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{v_{1}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{v_{4}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{v_{5}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{u_{2}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{u_{3}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{u_{1}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{u_{4}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{u_{5}}}

We now specify the maps.
The maps
U_{i}\to V_{i}
are still the same, sending
1
to
(1,X_{i},0,\ldots,0)
.
The maps
W_{k}^{{\left(\ell\right)}}\to V_{i}(k\in\Omega_{i})
send
1
to
e_{i}(k,\ell)\in V_{i}
in such a way that:

(a)

{\left\{e_{i}(k,\ell):k\in\Omega_{i},1\leq\ell\leq B\right\}}\cup{\left\{(1,c,0,\ldots,0)\right\}}
is a basis for
V_{i}
for all
c\in\mathbb{F}
.

(b)

{\left\{(1,0,\ldots,0),(1,c,0,\ldots,0)\right\}}\cup({\left\{e_{i}(k,\ell):k\in\Omega_{i},1\leq\ell\leq B\right\}}-{\left\{e_{i}(k^{\prime},\ell^{\prime})\right\}})
is a basis for
V_{i}
for all
c\in\mathbb{F},k^{\prime}\in\Omega_{i},\ell^{\prime}\in\llbracket 1,{\left\lvert\mathbb{F}\right\rvert}-1\rrbracket
.

This can be done by choosing one of
e_{i}(k_{0},\ell_{0})
to be
(0,1,1,\ldots,1)
, and the others to be

(0,0,1,0,\ldots,0),(0,0,0,1,0,\ldots,0),\ldots,(0,0,\ldots,0,1).

If
k\in\Omega_{i}^{+}
, then we choose the map
V_{i}\to W_{k}^{{\left(\ell\right)}}
to send
(1,0,\ldots,0)
to 0,
(1,1,0,\ldots,0)
to 1, and all
{\left\{e_{i}(k^{\prime},\ell^{\prime}):(k^{\prime},\ell^{\prime})\neq(k,\ell)\right\}}
to 0.
If
k\in\Omega_{i}^{-}
, then the
B
spaces
W_{k}^{{\left(\ell\right)}}
admit a one to one correspondence to the triples
(b_{1},b_{2},b_{3})\in(\mathbb{F}-{\left\{0\right\}})^{3}
, so if
E_{k}=\beta_{1}\vee\beta_{2}\vee\beta_{3}
and
\beta_{u}=\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX_{i}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
(
1\leq u\leq 3
), we choose the map
V_{i}\to W_{k}^{{\left(\ell\right)}}
to send
(1,b_{u},0,\ldots,0)
to 0,
(1,0,\ldots,0)
to 1, and
{\left\{e_{i}(k^{\prime},\ell^{\prime}):(k^{\prime},\ell^{\prime})\neq(k,\ell)\right\}}
to 0.

We now argue that when
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{M})\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{M}
makes
\Theta
evaluate to True, then
R=R_{\Theta}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{M})
is a Schur representation, where here
x_{1},\ldots,x_{M}
are viewed as elements in
\mathbb{F}
by the map
\mathbb{F}_{2}\to\mathbb{F},0\mapsto 0,1\mapsto 1
.
To see this, pick any
\varphi\in\operatorname{End}R
.
Since
\mathbb{F}\cdot\mathrm{id}\subset\operatorname{End}R
, after subtracting a suitable multiple of
\mathrm{id}
we can assume that
\varphi\!\mid_{U_{1}}=0
.
Then
\varphi\!\mid_{U_{2}}=0
by similar reasons as before, and repeating we obtain
\varphi\!\mid_{U_{i}}=0
for all
i
.

Because of the arrows from
U_{i}
to
V_{i}
, for each
i\in\llbracket 1,M\rrbracket
such that
x_{i}=1
, we have
\varphi\!\mid_{V_{i}}(1,1,0,\ldots,0)=0
, and for each
i\in\llbracket 1,M\rrbracket
such that
x_{i}=0
, we have
\varphi\!\mid_{V_{i}}(1,0,\ldots,0)=0
(by similar reasons as before).

For each
k\in\llbracket 1,L\rrbracket
, there must be some
X_{i}
appearing in
E_{k}
with
x_{i}=1
or some
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX_{i}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
appearing in
E_{k}
with
x_{i}=0
(because
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{M})\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{M}
makes
\Theta
evaluate to 1, so makes each
E_{k}
evaluate to 1).
In the former case,
\varphi\!\mid_{V_{i}}(1,0,\ldots,0)=0
, so because of the arrow from
V_{i}
to
W_{k}^{{\left(\ell\right)}}
we see that
\varphi\!\mid_{W_{k}^{{\left(\ell\right)}}}=0
(by similar reasons as before).
In the latter case,
\varphi\!\mid_{V_{i}}(1,q,\ldots,0)=0
for some nonzero
q\in\mathbb{F}
, so because of the arrow from
V_{i}
to
W_{k}^{{\left(\ell\right)}}
we see that
\varphi\!\mid_{W_{k}^{{\left(\ell\right)}}}=0
(by similar reasons as before).

Thus,
\varphi\!\mid_{W_{k}^{{\left(\ell\right)}}}=0
, so for each
i\in\llbracket 1,M\rrbracket
, we have
\varphi\!\mid_{V_{i}}(e_{i}(k,\ell))=0
for
1\leq i\leq M,1\leq\ell\leq{\left\lvert\mathbb{F}\right\rvert}-1
such that
k\in\Omega_{i}
because of the arrows from
W_{k}^{{\left(\ell\right)}}
to
V_{i}
(by similar reasons as before).
However, for each
i\in\llbracket 1,M\rrbracket
, we have either
\varphi\!\mid_{V_{i}}(1,1,0,\ldots,0)=0
or
\varphi\!\mid_{V_{i}}(1,0,\ldots,0)=0
, so from the choice of
e_{i}(k,\ell)
’s we see
\varphi\!\mid_{V_{i}}=0
.

As a result,
\varphi=0
, i.e.,
R_{\Theta}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{M})
is a Schur representation.

Furthermore, we show that
\dim R_{\Theta}
is a Schur root no matter whether
\Theta
can evaluate to true or not.
To see this, just note that we can modify the letters in the clauses of
\Theta
so that only
X_{i}
appear and no
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX_{i}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
appear (this can be done by replacing all
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX_{i}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
by
X_{i}
).
Let the modified version of
\Theta
be
\Theta^{\prime}
, then
\Theta^{\prime}
is able to evaluate to true (by plugging in
X_{i}=1
for all
i
).
However, we have
\dim R_{\Theta}=\dim R_{\Theta^{\prime}}
, and the above argument shows that
\dim R_{\Theta^{\prime}}
is a Schur root, so
\dim R_{\Theta}
is also a Schur root, as desired.

When
\Theta
always evaluate to False, then
R_{\Theta}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{M})
will always be decomposable.
To see this, pick any
x_{1},\ldots,x_{M}\in\mathbb{F}^{M}
, then there exists
E_{k}=\beta_{i_{1}}\vee\beta_{i_{2}}\vee\beta_{i_{3}}
such that

(a)

Whenever
\beta_{i_{j}}=\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX_{i_{j}}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
, we have
x_{i}\neq 0
.

(b)

Whenever
\beta_{i_{j}}=X_{i_{j}}
, we have
x_{i}=0
.

We pick
\ell
such that
W_{k}^{{\left(\ell\right)}}
corresponds to
(y_{1},y_{2},y_{3})
, where
y_{j}=\begin{cases}x_{i_{j}}&x_{i_{j}}\neq 0\\
1&x_{i_{j}}=0\end{cases}
.
Then
R_{\Theta}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{M})=R_{1}\oplus R_{2}
, where

R_{1}=W_{k}^{{\left(\ell\right)}}\oplus\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in\llbracket 1,M\rrbracket\\
k\in\Omega_{i}\end{subarray}}\mathbb{F}e_{i}(k,\ell)\subset W_{k}^{{\left(\ell\right)}}\oplus\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in\llbracket 1,M\rrbracket\\
k\in\Omega_{i}\end{subarray}}V_{i}.

and

R_{2}=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{M}U_{i}\oplus\bigoplus_{i=1}^{M}V_{i}^{\prime}\oplus\bigoplus_{(k^{\prime},\ell^{\prime})\neq(k,\ell)}W_{k^{\prime}}^{{\left(\ell^{\prime}\right)}}\subset\bigoplus_{i=1}^{M}U_{i}\oplus\bigoplus_{i=1}^{M}V_{i}\oplus\bigoplus_{(k^{\prime},\ell^{\prime})\neq(k,\ell)}W_{k^{\prime}}^{{\left(\ell^{\prime}\right)}},

where

V_{i}^{\prime}=\begin{cases}V_{i}&k\not\in\Omega_{i}\\
\mathbb{F}(1,x_{i},0,\ldots,0)\oplus\operatorname{Span}{\left\{e_{i}(k^{\prime},\ell^{\prime}):k^{\prime}\in\Omega_{i},1\leq\ell^{\prime}\leq B,(k^{\prime},\ell^{\prime})\neq(k,\ell)\right\}}&k\in\Omega_{i}\end{cases}.

The reason why this is a valid decomposition is the same as before.

Now if we denote the root by
\alpha
, then

\displaystyle(\alpha,\alpha)

\displaystyle=2{\left(M+\sum_{i}n_{i}^{2}+BL\right)}-2{\left(M+\sum_{i}n_{i}+2\sum_{i}n_{i}B{\left\lvert\Omega_{i}\right\rvert}\right)}

\displaystyle=2{\left(BL-\sum_{i}n_{i}B{\left\lvert\Omega_{i}\right\rvert}\right)}

\displaystyle=2B{\left(L-\sum_{i}{\left\lvert\Omega_{i}\right\rvert}(B{\left\lvert\Omega_{i}\right\rvert}+1)\right)}

\displaystyle\leq 0,

so
\alpha
is an imaginary Schur root.

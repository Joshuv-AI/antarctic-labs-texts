Some equivalent definitions for weighted Korenblum and Bloch-type spaces of the upper half-plane
Benoit F. Sehba

Abstract
. In this paper, we prove some equivalent characterizations of weighted Korenblum spaces and Bloch spaces in terms of symbols of bounded Hankel operators.



1991 Mathematics Subject Classification
30H20, 47B35, 30H30, 32A25, 42B35

1.
Introduction

In the upper half-plane
{\mathbb{C}}_{+}:=\{z\in{\mathbb{C}}:\Im mz>0\}
, for
\Omega
a positive function, and
t\in\mathbb{R}
, we denote by
L_{\Omega,t}^{\infty}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})
, the space of all measurable functions
f
such that

(1.1)

\|f\|_{L_{\Omega,t}^{\infty}}:=\sup_{z\in{\mathbb{C}}_{+}}\,\left(\Im mz\right)^{t}\Omega(z)|f(z)|<\infty.

If
\Omega(z)=1
for all
z\in{\mathbb{C}}_{+}
, then the above space is just denoted
L_{t}^{\infty}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})
, and the corresponding norm
\|f\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}}
.

We denote by
\mathcal{H}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})
the set of holomorphic functions in
{\mathbb{C}}_{+}
. For
t\geq 0
, the (weighted) Korenblum space
H_{\Omega,t}^{\infty}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})
is the intersection
\mathcal{H}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})\cap L_{\Omega,t}^{\infty}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})
.

We note that
H_{0}^{\infty}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})=H^{\infty}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})
is the usual space of bounded holomorphic functions on
{\mathbb{C}}_{+}
. For
t>0
, the space
H_{t}^{\infty}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})
is not trivial. Indeed, the function
f(z)=\frac{1}{(z+i)^{t}}
belongs to
H_{t}^{\infty}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})
. Also, if
H^{\infty}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})
contains constants, the only constant in
H_{t}^{\infty}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})
for
t\neq 0
is
f(z)=0
.

Let us introduce some related classes, starting with the Bergman spaces.
Let
\alpha>-1
, and let
1\leq p<\infty
. For
\Omega
a weight on
{\mathbb{C}}_{+}
, we denote by
L_{\Omega,\alpha}^{p}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})
the set of all measurable functions
f
on
\mathbb{C}_{+}
such that

\|f\|_{\Omega,\alpha,p}:=\left(\int_{{\mathbb{C}}_{+}}|f(x+iy)|^{p}\Omega(x+iy)y^{\alpha}dxdy\right)^{1/p}<\infty.

For
1\leq p<\infty
and
\alpha>-1
,
the Bergman space
A_{\Omega,\alpha}^{p}(\mathbb{C}_{+})
is the intersection
L_{\Omega,\alpha}^{p}(\mathbb{C}_{+})\cap\mathcal{H}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})
.

For
\alpha=0
, the above spaces become
L_{\Omega}^{p}(\mathbb{C}_{+})
and
A_{\Omega}^{p}(\mathbb{C}_{+})
. The corresponding norm is denoted
\|\cdot\|_{\Omega,p}
.

When
\Omega(z)=1
for all
z\in{\mathbb{C}}_{+}
, the above spaces are denoted
A_{\alpha}^{p}(\mathbb{C}_{+})
, and the corresponding norm is denoted
\|\cdot\|_{\alpha,p}
.
The usual Bergman spaces correspond to the case
\alpha=0
and are just denoted
A^{p}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})
and the corresponding norm
\|\cdot\|_{p}=\|\cdot\|_{0,p}
.

It is well known (see, for example,
[
2
]
) that there exists a constant
C>0
such that for any
f\in A_{\alpha}^{p}(\mathbb{C}_{+})
and any
z\in{\mathbb{C}}_{+}
,

(1.2)

|f(z)|\leq C\left(\Im mz\right)^{-\frac{2+\alpha}{p}}\|f\|_{\alpha,p}.

This shows that the Bergman space
A_{\alpha}^{p}(\mathbb{C}_{+})
is continuously embedded in the Korenblum space
H_{\frac{2+\alpha}{p}}^{\infty}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})
.

Another related space is the Bloch space
\mathcal{B}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})
which is the space of all holomorphic functions
b
on
{\mathbb{C}}_{+}
such
b^{\prime}\in H_{1}^{\infty}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})
. We endow
\mathcal{B}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})
with the norm

\|b\|_{\mathcal{B}}:=|b(i)|+\|b^{\prime}\|_{L_{1}^{\infty}}

and note that it is then a Banach space of functions and not equivalence classes.

The space
\mathcal{B}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})
obviously contains constants. For our own convenience, we put

\|f\|_{\dot{\mathcal{B}}}:=\sup_{z\in{\mathbb{C}}_{+}}(\Im mz)|f^{\prime}(z)|.

We know from
[
3
]
that there exists a constant
C>0
such that for any Bloch function
f
, it holds that

|f(z)|\leq C\omega(z)\|f\|_{\mathcal{B}}

where
\omega(z)=\ln_{+}(|z|)+\ln_{+}(1/\Im mz)
. Recall that for
s>0
,
\ln_{+}s=\max\{0,\ln s\}
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{R}
. The
\omega^{k}
-Bloch space
\mathcal{B}_{\omega^{k}}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})
is the space of all holomorphic functions
b
on
{\mathbb{C}}_{+}
such that
b^{\prime}\in H_{\omega^{k},1}^{\infty}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})
. We endow
\mathcal{B}_{\omega^{k}}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})
with the norm

\|b\|_{\mathcal{B}_{\omega^{k}}}:=|b(i)|+\|b^{\prime}\|_{L_{\omega^{k},1}^{\infty}}

and note that it is then a Banach space of functions. It also contains constants, and we put

\|f\|_{\dot{\mathcal{B}}_{\omega^{k}}}:=\sup_{z\in{\mathbb{C}}_{+}}(\Im mz)\omega^{k}(z)|f^{\prime}(z)|.

In this note, for
\Omega=\omega^{k}
for some
k\in{\mathbb{R}}
, we give equivalent definitions of the above weighted Korenblum spaces and the weighted Bloch spaces in terms of sets of symbols of bounded Hankel operators. Other equivalent definitions are in terms of dual of weighted Bergman spaces (for weighted Bloch spaces, see
[
4
,
9
]
).

This work is a kind of spin-off of
[
4
]
with its own perspective. It is motivated by the challenges encountered in obtaining the necessary and sufficient conditions for the symbols of Hankel operators in
[
4
]
.

2.
Statement of the results

For simplicity, we will be using the notation

dV_{\alpha}(z)=\left(\Im m(z)\right)^{\alpha}dV(z)=y^{\alpha}dxdy\quad\textrm{for}\quad z=x+iy.

For
\alpha>-1
, the Bergman projection
P_{\alpha}
is the orthogonal projection from
L_{\alpha}^{2}(\mathbb{C}_{+})
into its closed subspace
A_{\alpha}^{2}(\mathbb{C}_{+})
. This operator is given by

P_{\alpha}f(z)=\int_{\mathbb{C}_{+}}K_{\alpha}(z,w)f(w)dV_{\alpha}(w)

where

K_{\alpha}(z,w)=\frac{c_{\alpha}}{\left(z-\bar{w}\right)^{2+\alpha}}

is the Bergman kernel, and
c_{\alpha}
a constant that depends only on
\alpha
. We use the notation
P=P_{0}
.

It is not difficult to prove that for
\alpha>-1
and
\beta>-1
, the Bergman projection
P_{\beta}
is bounded on
L_{\alpha}^{p}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})
,
1\leq p<\infty
, if and only if

(2.1)

1+\alpha<p(1+\beta)

(see, for example,
[
1
,
2
,
8
]
). In this case, one can show that
P_{\beta}
also reproduces elements of
A_{\alpha}^{p}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})
. Another consequence of this fact is that for
1\leq p<\infty
, the differentiation operator
\frac{d^{k}}{dz^{k}}
is bounded from
A_{\alpha}^{p}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})
to
A_{\alpha+kp}^{p}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})
with the equivalence of the norms.

For
b
a bounded function in
{\mathbb{C}}_{+}
, the Hankel operator
h_{b}^{\alpha}
is the operator defined on
A_{\alpha}^{2}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})
by

h_{b}^{\alpha}(f)=P_{\alpha}(b\overline{f}),f\in A_{\alpha}^{2}({\mathbb{C}}_{+}).

This operator is clearly well defined on
A_{\alpha}^{2}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})
. It is even bounded on
A_{\alpha}^{2}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})
, at least as long as
b
is bounded. This means, among others, that for any
g\in A_{\alpha}^{2}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})
, we have the control

|\langle h_{b}^{\alpha}(f),g\rangle_{\alpha}|\leq C_{b}\|f\|_{\alpha,2}\|g\|_{\alpha,2}.

Here
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\alpha}
stands for the Hermitian inner product of
L_{\alpha}^{2}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})
. For
\alpha=0
, we write
h_{b}=h_{b}^{0}
and
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
for the inner product of
L^{2}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})
.

We want to relate the Hankel operator to the Korenblum space associated with
A_{\alpha}^{2}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})
with
\alpha>-1
, that is,
H_{t}^{\infty}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})
for
t
large enough.

We make the following observation: still for
f\in A_{\alpha}^{2}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})
and
g\in A_{\alpha}^{2}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})
, we first recall that

\langle h_{b}^{\alpha}(f),g\rangle_{\alpha}=\langle P_{\alpha}(b\overline{f}),g\rangle_{\alpha}=\langle b,fg\rangle_{\alpha}.

This can be rewritten as

(2.2)

\langle h_{b}^{\alpha}(f),g\rangle_{\alpha}=\langle(\Im m\cdot)^{\alpha-t}b,fg\rangle_{t}

where now,
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{t}
stands for the Hermitian inner product of
L_{t}^{2}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})
. That is,

(2.3)

\langle F,G\rangle_{t}:=\int_{{\mathbb{C}}_{+}}F(z)\overline{G(z)}(\Im mz)^{t}dV(z).

Returning to (
2.2
), we have that
a(z)=(\Im mz)^{\alpha-t}b(z)
belongs to
L_{t-\alpha}^{\infty}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})
since
b
was taken bounded. Using the fact that
P_{t}
reproduces functions in
A_{\alpha}^{2}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})
, it follows that

(2.4)

\langle h_{b}^{\alpha}(f),g\rangle_{\alpha}=\langle(P_{t}(a\overline{f}),g\rangle_{t}=\langle h_{a}^{t}(f),g\rangle_{t}.

We have that
P_{t}(a\overline{f})
makes sense. Indeed, as
f\in A_{\alpha}^{2}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})
and
a\in L_{t-\alpha}^{\infty}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})
, the function
a\overline{f}
belongs to
\in L_{2t-\alpha}^{2}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})
and
P_{t}
is well defined and bounded in the last space for
t
large enough.

We see that since
f
and
g
are taken in
A_{\alpha}^{2}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})
, the term in the last equality in (
2.4
) forces
h_{a}^{t}(f)
to be in the predual of
A_{\alpha}^{2}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})
with respect to the inner product (
2.3
).

It is known that the Hankel operator
h_{b}^{\alpha}
is bounded on
A_{\alpha}^{p}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})
for
1<p<\infty
, if and only if
b=P_{\alpha}g
for some
g\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})
(see, for example,
[
7
]
). To extend this characterization to weighted Bloch spaces in consideration, we first prove the following characterization of Korenblum spaces.

Theorem 1
.

Let
1<p<\infty
, and let
t\geq 1
. Assume that
l,k\in\mathbb{R}
. Let
b
be a holomorphic function on
{\mathbb{C}}_{+}
. Then the following assertions are equivalent.

(a)

b\in H_{\omega^{l+k},t}^{\infty}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})
.

(b)

The Hankel operator
h_{b}^{t}
is bounded from
A_{\omega^{-lp}}^{p}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})
to
A_{\omega^{kp},tp}^{p}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})
. Moreover,

\|h_{b}^{t}\|_{A_{\omega^{-lp}}^{p}\to A_{\omega^{kp},tp}^{p}}\simeq\|b\|_{H_{\omega^{l+k},t}^{\infty}}.

From the above result, we deduce the following.

Corollary 1
.

Let
1<p<\infty
,
l,k\in\mathbb{R}
. Let
b
be a holomorphic function on
{\mathbb{C}}_{+}
such that
b=Pb
. Then the following assertions are equivalent.

(a)

b\in\mathcal{B}_{\omega^{l+k}}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})
.

(b)

The Hankel operator
h_{b}
is bounded from
A_{\omega^{-lp}}^{p}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})
to
A_{\omega^{kp}}^{p}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})
. Moreover,

\|b\|_{\dot{\mathcal{B}}_{\omega^{l+k}}}\lesssim\|h_{b}\|_{A_{\omega^{-lp}}^{p}\to A_{\omega^{kp}}^{p}}\lesssim\|b\|_{\mathcal{B}_{\omega^{l+k}}}.

The corollary is obtained from the theorem by making the following observation. For
b
as in the corollary (say for
l=k=0
and
t=1
), by the above theorem,
h_{b^{\prime}}^{1}
maps
A^{p}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})
to
A_{p}^{p}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})
. We note that
A_{p}^{p}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})
is the space of derivatives of functions in
A^{p}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})
. We then recover
h_{b}f
in this case by noting that it is the solution of the differential equation.

(2.5)

\frac{d}{dz}\left(h_{b}f(z)\right)=c\left(h_{b^{\prime}}^{1}f\right)(z).

We can speak of the solution of (
2.5
) since the constants do not belong to
A^{p}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})
for any
1\leq p<\infty
.
This approach for characterizing symbols of bounded Hankel operators on Bergman spaces seems new.

The following is a kind of end-point version of Theorem
1
.

Theorem 2
.

Let
k\leq 0
, and let
l\in{\mathbb{R}}
. Assume that
0<\varepsilon<t
for
t\geq 1
. Let
b
be a holomorphic function on
{\mathbb{C}}_{+}
. Then the following assertions are equivalent.

(a)

b\in H_{\omega^{l+k},t-\varepsilon}^{\infty}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})
.

(b)

The Hankel operator
h_{b}^{t}
is bounded from
A_{\omega^{l}}^{1}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})
to
A_{\omega^{-k},t-\varepsilon}^{1}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})
. Moreover,

\|h_{b}^{t}\|_{A_{\omega^{l}}^{1}\to A_{\omega^{-k},t-\varepsilon}^{1}}\simeq\|b\|_{H_{\omega^{l+k},t-\varepsilon}^{\infty}}.

If we take
t=1
and let
\varepsilon\to 0
in the last theorem, we are led to the same type of problems as those encountered in
[
4
]
.
Recall that for
b\in H^{\infty}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})
, that
h_{b}f\in A^{2}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})
for any
f\in A^{2}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})
, is equivalent to saying that for any
g\in A^{2}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})
,

(2.6)

\langle h_{b}(f),g\rangle=\langle b,fg\rangle

and its modulus is bounded above by a constant time
\|f\|_{2}\|g\|_{2}
.

For
f\in A^{1}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})
, can we understand
h_{b}f
as in (
2.6
) with
g\in\mathcal{B}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})
? In the arxiv.org version of
[
4
]
,
h_{b}f
for
f\in A^{1}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})
, was defined as the holomorphic function such that for any
g\in\mathcal{B}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})
,

\langle h_{b}f,g\rangle:=\langle b,fg\rangle.

To make this definition unconditional, that is, such that the right-hand side above is the same independently of the choice of the representative
g
of a class in Bloch space, one sees that
\langle b,f\rangle=0
should hold. This forced the authors of
[
4
]
(see the arxiv.org version of the paper) to consider studying

h_{b}:A_{b}^{1}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})\to A^{1}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})

where
\displaystyle A_{b}^{1}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})=\{f\in A^{1}({\mathbb{C}}_{+}):\langle b,f\rangle=0\}
. Unfortunately, this does not give a necessary and sufficient condition on the symbol of the operator.

In Section 3, we provide some useful results we need in our presentation. Some dual characterizations of the weighted Bergman spaces are given in Section 4. We prove our results in Section 5.

Throughout the text, we use the notation
A\lesssim B
(respectively,
A\gtrsim B
) whenever there exists a uniform constant
C>0
such that
A\leq CB
(respectively,
A\geq CB
). The notation
A\simeq B
means that
A\lesssim B
and
A\gtrsim B
.

Useful results

We refer to
[

for the following.

Lemma 1
.

Let
\alpha>0
,
\beta>-1
, and
k\in{\mathbb{R}}
. Let

\Omega(z)=\left(\ln^{\varepsilon_{1}}(e+|z|)+\ln^{\varepsilon_{2}}\left(e+\frac{1}{\Im mz}\right)\right)^{k},

\varepsilon_{j}\in\{0,1\}
,
j=1,2
. Then there is a constant
C=C_{\alpha,\beta}>0
such that for any
z_{0}\in\mathbb{C}_{+}
,

(3.1)

\int_{\mathbb{C}_{+}}\frac{\Omega(z)dV_{\beta}(z)}{|z-\overline{z}_{0}|^{2+\alpha+\beta}}\leq C(\Im m(z_{0}))^{-\alpha}\Omega(z_{0}).

The first part of the following follows from Fubini’s theorem and Lemma
1
. Its second part can be proved as in
[
4
, Proposition 1.7]
.

Proposition 1
.

Let
\alpha>0
, and
\beta>-1
, let
k\in\mathbb{R}
. The Bergman projector
P_{\alpha+\beta}
is bounded from
L_{\omega^{-k},\beta}^{1}(\mathbb{C}_{+})
to
L_{\omega^{-k},\beta}^{1}(\mathbb{C}_{+})
. If, moreover,
\alpha+\beta>0
, then
P_{\alpha+\beta}
reproduces the functions in
A_{\omega^{-k},\beta}^{1}(\mathbb{C}_{+})

Let us deduce the following.

Corollary 2
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{R}
,
\alpha>0
and
\beta>-1
. Assume that
\alpha+\beta>0
. The Bergman projection
P_{\alpha}
, reproduces the functions in
A^{1}_{\omega^{k},\beta}(\mathbb{C}_{+})
.

Proof.

As
\alpha+\beta>0
, we have from Proposition
1
that
P_{\alpha+\beta}
reproduces the functions in
A^{1}_{\omega^{k},\beta}(\mathbb{C}_{+})
. The conclusion then follows from the fact that the reproducing kernel of
A_{\alpha+\beta}^{2}(\mathbb{C}_{+})
belongs to
A^{1}(\mathbb{C}_{+})
and that, as
\alpha>0
,
P_{\alpha}
reproduces the functions in
A^{1}(\mathbb{C}_{+})
.
∎

4.
Dual of weighted Bergman spaces

The following is elementary, as follows from the usual arguments.

Proposition 4
.

Let
1<p<\infty
,
t\geq 1
, and
k\in\mathbb{R}
. The dual space
\left(A_{\omega^{-kp},tp}^{p}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})\right)^{*}
of the Bergman space
A_{\omega^{-kp},tp}^{p}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})
identifies with
A_{\omega^{kp^{\prime}}}^{p^{\prime}}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})
(pp’=p+p’) under the duality pairing (
2.3
).

Let us prove the following.

Proposition 5
.

Let
k\in{\mathbb{R}}
, and
0\leq\varepsilon<t
,
t\geq 1
. Then the dual space
\left(A_{\omega^{-k},\varepsilon}^{1}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})\right)^{*}
of
A_{\omega^{-k},\varepsilon}^{1}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})

identifies with
H_{\omega^{k},t-\varepsilon}^{\infty}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})
under the duality pairing (
2.3
).

Proof.

That every
b\in H_{\omega^{k},t-\varepsilon}^{\infty}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})
defines an element of
\left(A_{\omega^{-k},\varepsilon}^{1}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})\right)^{*}
through (
2.3
) is obvious. Now suppose that
\Lambda\in\left(A_{\omega^{-k},\varepsilon}^{1}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})\right)^{*}
. Then we can extend
\Lambda
to a linear operator on
L_{\omega^{-k},\varepsilon}^{1}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})
that we still denote
\Lambda
. Hence there is a
g\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})
such that for any
f\in L_{\omega^{-k},\varepsilon}^{1}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})
,

\Lambda(f):=\langle f,g\rangle:=\int_{{\mathbb{C}}_{+}}f(z)\overline{g(z)}(\Im mz)^{\varepsilon}\omega^{-k}(z)dV(z).

In particular, for any
f\in A_{\omega^{-k},\varepsilon}^{1}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})
,

\Lambda(f):=\langle f,g\rangle=\int_{{\mathbb{C}}_{+}}f(z)\overline{g(z)}(\Im mz)^{-t+\varepsilon}\omega^{-k}(z)dV_{t}(z).

As
P_{t}
reproduces elements of
A_{\omega^{-k},\varepsilon}^{1}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})
, it easily follows that

\displaystyle\Lambda(f)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\langle f,(\Im m\cdot)^{-t+\varepsilon}\omega^{-k}g\rangle_{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle P_{t}(f),(\Im m\cdot)^{-t+\varepsilon}\omega^{-k}g\rangle_{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle f,P_{t}\left((\Im m\cdot)^{-t+\varepsilon}\omega^{-k}g\right)\rangle_{t}.

Let us put
b=P_{t}\left((\Im m\cdot)^{-t+\varepsilon}\omega^{-k}g\right)
. We note that as
g\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})
,
(\Im m\cdot)^{-t+\varepsilon}\omega^{-k}g\in L_{\omega^{k},t-\varepsilon}^{\infty}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})
. Thus, by Proposition
3
,
b\in H_{\omega^{k},t-\varepsilon}^{\infty}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})
. This concludes the proof.
∎

5.
Boundedness of Hankel operators

5.1.
Atomic decomposition of
A_{\omega^{-k}}^{1}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})
and weak factorization

We give here a generalization of the atomic decomposition of the Bergman space
A_{\omega^{-1}}^{1}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})
obtained in
[
3
]
. We then obtain weak factorizations of the Bergman spaces
A_{\omega^{-k}}^{1}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})
that will be used in our characterizations of symbols of bounded Hankel operators.

The following is obtained as in
[
3
, Proposition 11]
.

Proposition 6
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{R}
. Let
f
be a holomorphic function in
A^{1}_{\omega^{-k}}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})
. For any
\alpha>0
, there exists a sequence of complex numbers
\{c_{k}\}
and a sequence of points

5.2.
Proof of the main results

Let us start with the proof of Theorem
1
.

Proof of Theorem
1
.

We first assume that
b\in H_{\omega^{l+k},t}^{\infty}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})
. Recall that the dual space of
A_{\omega^{kp},tp}^{p}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})
is
A_{\omega^{-kp^{\prime}}}^{p^{\prime}}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})
under the duality pairing (
2.3
). We have that the product of a function
f\in A_{\omega^{-lp}}^{p}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})
and a function
g\in A_{\omega^{-kp^{\prime}}}^{p^{\prime}}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})
belongs to
A_{\omega^{-(l+k)}}^{1}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})
. We also have that the dual space of
A_{\omega^{-(l+k)}}^{1}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})
under the pairing (
2.3
) is
H_{\omega^{l+k},t}^{\infty}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})
. As
P_{t}
reproduces functions in
A_{\omega^{-kp^{\prime}}}^{p^{\prime}}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})
, we obtain that

\displaystyle|\langle h_{b}^{t}(f),g\rangle_{t}|=|\langle b,fg\rangle_{t}|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|b\|_{H_{\omega^{l+k},t}^{\infty}}\|fg\|_{A_{\omega^{-(l+k)}}^{1}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|b\|_{H_{\omega^{l+k},t}^{\infty}}\|f\|_{A_{\omega^{-lp}}^{p}}\|g\|_{A_{\omega^{-kp^{\prime}}}^{p^{\prime}}}.

Thus for
b\in H_{\omega^{l+k},t}^{\infty}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})
,
h_{b}^{t}
is bounded from
A_{\omega^{-lp}}^{p}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})
to
A_{\omega^{kp},tp}^{p}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})
. Moreover,

\|h_{b}^{t}\|_{A_{\omega^{-lp}}^{p}\longrightarrow A_{\omega^{kp},tp}^{p}}\leq\|b\|_{H_{\omega^{l+k},t}^{\infty}}.

For the converse statement, we assume that
h_{b}^{t}
is well defined and bounded from
A_{\omega^{-lp}}^{p}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})
to
A_{\omega^{kp},tp}^{p}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})
and we prove that necessarily,
b\in H_{\omega^{l+k},t}^{\infty}({\mathbb{C}}_{+}).
By Proposition
5
, it is sufficient to prove that, for
F
in a dense subset of
A^{1}_{\omega^{-(l+k)}}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})
, we have

(5.8)

|\langle b,F\rangle_{t}|=\left|\int_{{\mathbb{C}}_{+}}b(z)\overline{F(z)}(\Im mz)^{t}dV(z)\right|\leq C\|F\|_{A^{1}_{\omega^{-(l+k)}}}

for some uniform constant
C>0
.
We consider the dense subset of functions in
A^{1}_{\omega^{-(l+k)}}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})
with finite atomic decomposition,

F=\sum_{finite}f_{j}g_{j},

where
f_{j}
and
g_{j}
are given in (
5.5
)
with
\displaystyle\sum_{finite}\|f_{j}\|_{A_{\omega^{-lp}}^{p}}\|g_{j}\|_{A_{\omega^{-kp^{\prime}}}^{p^{\prime}}}\leq C\|F\|_{A^{1}_{\omega^{-(l+k)}}}.

We use the above to conclude that

\langle b,F\rangle_{t}=\sum_{finite}\langle b,f_{j}g_{j}\rangle_{t}.

The boundedness of
h_{b}^{t}
from
A_{\omega^{-lp}}^{p}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})
to
A_{\omega^{kp},tp}^{p}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})
implies that

|\langle b,f_{j}g_{j}\rangle_{t}|=|\langle h_{b}^{t}(f_{j}),g_{j}\rangle_{t}|\leq C

for some uniform constant
C.
Inequality (
5.8
) then follows from this fact.
The proof is complete.
∎

Remark 1
.

(a)

We have that

h_{b}^{t}=h_{P_{t}b}^{t}\quad\text{on}\quad A_{-lp}^{p}({\mathbb{C}}_{+}).

Indeed, for
f\in A_{\omega^{-lp}}^{p}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})
, we have

\displaystyle h_{P_{t}b}^{t}f(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{C}}_{+}}P_{t}b(w)\overline{\left(\frac{f(w)}{(\overline{z}-w)^{2+t}}\right)}dV_{t}(w)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{C}}_{+}}b(w)\overline{P_{t}\left(\frac{f(w)}{(\overline{z}-w)^{2+t}}\right)}dV_{t}(w).

Now, put
g(w)=\frac{f(w)}{(\overline{z}-w)^{2+t}}
. As
f\in A_{\omega^{-lp}}^{p}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})
,
g\in A_{\omega^{-lp},(2+t)p}^{p}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})
. As
P_{t}
reproduces the functions in
A_{\omega^{-lp},(2+t)p}^{p}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})
, we conclude that
P_{t}g=g
. Hence
h_{P_{t}b}^{t}f=h_{b}^{t}f
.

(b)

From the above observation and Theorem
1
, we can conclude that

b\in H_{\omega^{l+k},t}^{\infty}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})\quad\text{if and only if}\quad P_{t}b\in H_{\omega^{l+k},t}^{\infty}({\mathbb{C}}_{+}).

Let us prove the following kind of integration by parts.

Lemma 4
.

Let
k,l\in{\mathbb{R}}
. Let
b
be a holomorphic function such that
b=Pb
, and let
1\leq p<\infty
. Then there is a constant
c\in\mathbb{R}
such that for all
f\in A^{1}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})\cap A_{\omega^{l}}^{p}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})
,

(5.9)

\int_{{\mathbb{C}}_{+}}f(z)\overline{b(z)}dV(z)=c\int_{{\mathbb{C}}_{+}}f(z)\overline{b^{\prime}(z)}\left(\Im mz\right)dV(z)

whenever the left hand-side makes sense.

Proof.

To obtain (
5.9
), we use the following ingredients:

•

\int_{{\mathbb{C}}_{+}}f(z)dV(z)=0
for
f\in A^{1}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})
;

•

P_{1}f=f
for any
f\in A_{\omega^{l}}^{p}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})
;

•

P_{2}(b^{\prime})=c_{0}b^{\prime}
so that
b=c_{1}P_{1}((\Im m\cdot)b^{\prime})+c_{2}
for some
c_{j}\in{\mathbb{C}}
,
j=0,1,2
.

It follows from the above and Fubini’s theorem that

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{C}}_{+}}f(z)\overline{b(z)}dV(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c_{1}\int_{{\mathbb{C}}_{+}}f(z)\overline{P_{1}((\Im m\cdot)b^{\prime})(z)}dV(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c_{1}\int_{{\mathbb{C}}_{+}}\left(\int_{{\mathbb{C}}_{+}}\frac{f(z)}{\left(w-\bar{z}\right)^{3}}dV(z)\right)\overline{b^{\prime}(w)}\left(\Im mw\right)^{2}dV(w)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C\int_{{\mathbb{C}}_{+}}\left(Pf\right)^{\prime}(w)\overline{b^{\prime}(w)}\left(\Im mw\right)^{2}dV(w)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C\int_{{\mathbb{C}}_{+}}\left(P_{1}f\right)^{\prime}(w)\overline{b^{\prime}(w)}\left(\Im mw\right)^{2}dV(w)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C\int_{{\mathbb{C}}_{+}}f(z)\overline{P_{2}b^{\prime}(z)}\left(\Im mz\right)dV(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C\int_{{\mathbb{C}}_{+}}f(z)\overline{b^{\prime}(z)}\left(\Im mz\right)dV(z).

∎

Let us prove Corollary
1
.

Proof of Corollary
1
.

If we assume that

with

, then using Lemma

, one obtains that for some constant

(5.10)

\frac{d}{dz}\left(h_{b}f(z)\right)=c\left(h_{b^{\prime}}^{1}f\right)(z)\quad\text{for all}\quad f\in A_{\omega^{-lp}}^{p}({\mathbb{C}}_{+}).

Indeed, we have

\displaystyle\overline{\frac{d}{dz}\left(h_{b}f(z)\right)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c\int_{{\mathbb{C}}_{+}}\frac{f(w)}{\left(\overline{z}-w\right)^{3}}\overline{b(w)}dV(w).

It suffices then to see using Hölder’s inequality and Lemma

, that the function

is in
A^{1}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})\cap A_{\omega^{s}}^{q}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})

and

. Also, using the pointwise estimates in Lemma

It follows using the previous proposition that for

\displaystyle b\in\mathcal{B}_{\omega^{l+k}}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow

Proof of Theorem

.

We first assume that

\displaystyle\|b\|_{L_{\omega^{-(l+k)},t-\varepsilon}^{\infty}}\|fg\|_{L_{\omega^{l+k},\varepsilon}^{1}}

6.
conclusion

In this paper, we have obtained a characterization in terms of symbols of bounded Hankel operators between weighted Bergman spaces, of functions in weighted Korenblum spaces and weighted Bloch spaces that are reproduced by the Bergman projection. Our aim was not to study Hankel operators on their own and hence we focused only on cases that provide equivalent definition of Korenblum and Bloch spaces.

Actinomycosis of periodontium, mimicking gingival enlargement – A case report and its management
Authors: Renganath Murugan Jeyasree, Thamil Selvan Muthuraj, Gomakumar Kanakasabhapathi Usha, Jacob Raja
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
AbstractGingival abscess is a localized, acute inflammatory lesion which is caused by various factors that include microbial plaque infection, foreign body impaction, and trauma. Management includes a simple incision and draining of the purulent exudate along with systemic antibiotics in most of the cases. This article reports a case of a 16-year-old female patient, with a chief complaint of painful and swollen gums in her lower right front tooth region of the jaw along with fever for the past 5 days. Emergency treatment of abscess drainage was done on day 1. On 5th day of review, the case presented with persistent signs of gingival inflammation in relation to 43 to 41. Considering the provisional diagnosis as dentigerous cyst with persistent gingival abscess, definitive treatment was performed by transalveolar extraction of the impacted canine and debridement of the osseous lesion. The histologic analysis of the lesion exhibited foci of eosinophilic coagulum-like areas with epicentric and radiating filamentous appearance at the periphery, which is suggestive of an inflamed dentigerous cyst with actinomycosis infection of periodontium.

INTRODUCTION

Actinomyces species, an endogenous flora, causes suppurative, granulomatous lesion that could be locally destructive in nature. This infection in the oral cavity is rare and is mostly misdiagnosed as a typical odontogenic lesion. This article reports a rare case of actinomycosis of the oral cavity, which mimicked as gingival abscess, and its management.

CASE REPORT

A female patient aged 16 years reported to the department of periodontology with painful and swollen gums in the lower right front teeth region along with fever for the past 5 days. Intraoral examination revealed gingival enlargement, which was confined to the interdental papilla between #42 and #43 with purulent exudate discharge from the gingival sulcus, revealing a clinical picture of gingival abscess. The patient also reported with symptoms of discomfort in between teeth #42 and #43 for the past 1 day, for which she surmised as foreign body lodgment.

Emergency management of abscess drainage using crevicular incision was done after obtaining verbal consent from the patient’s guardian since the patient was a minor. Antibiotics (amoxicillin 500 mg and clavulanic acid 125 mg + metronidazole 400 mg bd) and analgesics (diclofenac sodium and paracetamol) were prescribed for 5 days. After 5 days, the gingival inflammation was still persistent between #43 and #42, which was extending to the attached gingiva [Figure 1]. Clinical evaluation of the teeth #41 and #42 showed Grade I mobility, whereas #43 was firm. Pulp vitality test showed positive response of the teeth #41–43. An intraoral periapical (IOPA) radiograph showed horizontally impacted #33 apical to #42 and #41. Crestal bone loss up to apical 1/3rd, along with loss of lamina dura in the interdental region of #43, #42 and ill-defined radiolucency around the periapical region of #42, #41, was also evident [Figure 2].

Preoperative view: Showing persistent gingival inflammation with teeth #42 and #43 confined to the attached gingiva

Preoperative intraoral periapical radiographic view of teeth, #42 and #43: Showing horizontally impacted tooth #33 with bone loss

With a provisional diagnosis of dentigerous cyst, the extraction of the impacted canine #33 was performed [Figure 3] with curettage of the cystic lesion. After debridement, it was found that the lesion had connecting track from retained deciduous tooth #73; hence, it was also extracted [Figure 4]. The cystic contents were greenish, [Figure 5] with soft consistency and were sent for histopathological analysis. The osseous defect was thoroughly rinsed with povidone iodine, and the osseous defect was packed with an alloplastic bone graft (PerioGlas®) and collagen sponge [Figure 6a]. The flap was approximated using sling sutures with 3.0 silk [Figure 6b]. Postoperative instructions were given to the patient and analgesics were prescribed for 5 days since the patient was already on antibiotics.

The specimen was processed and stained with hematoxylin and eosin, which exhibited cystic epithelial lining with stratification of 4 to 5 layers, and the stroma was moderately collagenous with inflammatory cell infiltrate. The greenish content was evaluated for sulfur granules and the specimen showed concentric foci of eosinophilic coagulum of radiating areas of sulfur granule and conglomerate of filamentous organisms [Figure 7] suggesting Actinomyces of an inflamed dentigerous cyst.

H&E tissue section: Showing focal esosinophilic coagulum like areas with epicentric and radiating filamentous appearance at the periphery

The patient was reviewed at 1-week, 1-month, and 1-year intervals. Postoperative healing was satisfactory [Figure 8], with no episodes of pain or symptoms. Postoperative IOPA taken after 6 months showed complete restoration of the interdental bone crest around #43 and #42 [Figure 9]. Cross-sectional reconstruction of cone-beam computed tomography (CBCT) after 6 months in teeth #42 and #41 showed hyperdense structures, which signifies osseous filling in the cancellous area confined to the cortical plates of the anterior mandible [Figure 10].

DISCUSSION

Actinomyces is a nondetrimental commensal of the oral cavity that can become pathological when encountered by periodontal disease.[1] Based on the clinical presentation of this case, the lesion was initially diagnosed as a localized gingival abscess. Gingival abscess is a localized, acute inflammatory lesion that arises from microbial plaque infection, foreign body impaction, and occasional trauma.[23] However, this case displayed extensive attachment loss and bone destruction, along with an impacted canine. While ruling out all the odontogenic infections for provisional diagnosis, the vitality test ruled out the radicular cyst. Since the patient had no history of extraction, the residual cyst was also excluded. Dentigerous cysts could not be ignored as they were clinically associated with an impacted tooth.[4]

Dentigerous cysts account for almost 20% of all odontogenic cysts, typically asymptomatic.[5] The association between Actinomyces and odontogenic cyst could be explained in two diverse ways: (i) an apical inflammatory reaction caused by invasion of Actinomyces might have stimulated the odontogenic epithelium, resulting in cyst formation or (ii) after cyst formation, Actinomyces might have proliferated in the hemorrhagic contents and filled the cystic cavity.[6] The reported incidence of Actinomyces-associated periapical lesions and dentigerous cyst was 12.3% and 5.7%, respectively.[7]

Actinomycosis of the oral cavity occurs when predisposing factors such as plaque and calculus break the mucosal barrier.[8] Actinomyces species can be found in calculus and in periodontal pockets, but they are not considered as periodontal pathogens.[7] However, periodontal disease is often associated with ulceration of the pocket epithelium, thus providing a possible path of entry for the bacteria into the tissue, where relatively low oxygen pressure conditions support their growth.[910] Histopathology of the lesion contents showed the presence of Gram-positive filamentous bacterial colonies, consistent with Actinomyces species.

Actinomycosis is often considered a condition of the soft tissues, but when infected, it may spread to the underlying bone in up to 15% of cases.[11] The hallmark clinical picture of actinomycosis is the presence of an abscess and a draining sinus, among which only an abscess was found in this case. Our treatment included extraction of the impacted tooth and thorough debridement of the cystic lesion, followed by packing of an alloplastic bone substitute into the osseous defect which acted as a key factor for excellent healing of the lesion. Various clinical trials have shown that bioactive glass is an effective adjunct to osseous surgery in treating intrabony defects.[1213] In our present case, postoperative CBCT shows complete bone fill on the previously voided areas.

Based on the clinical and histological findings of the present case, it can be settled that the lesion was a result of a reactive inflammatory odontogenic cyst induced by some coronal remnants of the previously infected deciduous tooth by Actinomyces species that gained access from draining fistula. With the excellent outcome of the present case, it can be concluded that an inflamed dentigerous cyst can be successfully treated with definitive surgical intervention.

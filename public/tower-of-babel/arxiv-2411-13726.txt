Abstract
. In this paper, we will provide a result on the relativistic Euler equations for an ideal gas equation of state and a physical vacuum boundary. More specifically, we will prove a priori estimates for the linearized system in weighted Sobolev spaces. Our focus will be on choosing the correct thermodynamic variables, developing a weighted book-keeping scheme, and then proving energy estimates for the linearized system. Keywords: Relativistic Euler equations, physical vacuum boundary, ideal gas equation of state. Mathematics Subject Classification (2020): Primary: 35Q75; Secondary: 35Q35, 35Q31, 35R37,



1.
Introduction

The starting point for our work is the relativistic Euler equations which describe the motion of a relativistic fluid in a Minkowski background (see
[
3
]
for historical context). The equations of motion are given by

(1.1a)

\displaystyle u^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}\varrho+(p+\varrho)\partial_{\mu}u^{\mu}

\displaystyle=0,

(1.1b)

\displaystyle(p+\varrho)u^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}u^{\alpha}+\mathsf{\Pi}^{\alpha\mu}\partial_{\mu}p

\displaystyle=0,

(1.1c)

\displaystyle g_{\mu\nu}u^{\mu}u^{\nu}

\displaystyle=-1,

where
\varrho
is the fluid’s (energy) density,
u
is the fluid’s (four-)velocity,
p
is the
fluid’s pressure given by an equation of state (whose choice depends on the nature of the fluid, see below),

g
is the Minkowski metric
1
1

1

This problem can be studied for a general background
metric, but the Minkowski metric already contains all the important mathematical features. Coupling
to Einstein’s equations, on the other hand, is an entirely different (and much harder) problem.
,
and
\mathsf{\Pi}_{\alpha\beta}:=g_{\alpha\beta}+u_{\alpha}u_{\beta}
is the projection onto the
space orthogonal to
u
.
Above and throughout, we employ standard Cartesian coordinates
\{x^{\alpha}\}_{\alpha=0}^{3}

with
t:=x^{0}
denoting a time coordinate and
x:=(x^{1},x^{2},x^{3})
denoting spatial coordinates,
the sum convention is adopted, Greek indices vary from
0
to
3
and Latin indices from
1
to
3
,
and indices are raised and lowered with
g
.

Remark 1.1
.

We note that (
1.1a
) is the conservation of energy for the fluid, (
1.1b
) is the conservation of momentum, and (
1.1c
) is a normalization condition where the velocity is assumed to be a forward time-like vector field (and this constraint is propagated by the flow).

We are interested in the case where the fluid is confined within a domain that is not fixed but is allowed
to move with the fluid motion, such as, e.g., the motion of a star.
Fluids of this type are called free-boundary fluids. We further consider the situation
where the pressure and density vanish on the boundary which is often referred to as the gas case. The liquid case where the density does not vanish on the boundary is a different problem.

Denote the region containing the fluid
at time
t
by
\Omega_{t}
. Then,
\Omega_{t}
can be described as

\displaystyle\Omega_{t}=\{(\tau,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{1+3}\,|\,\tau=t,\varrho(t,x)>0\}.

Equations (
1.1
) hold in the spacetime region

(1.2)

\mathscr{D}:=\bigcup_{0\leq t<T}\{t\}\times\Omega_{t},

for some
T>0
, known as the moving domain.
The fluid’s free-boundary at time
t
is defined as

\Gamma_{t}:=\partial\Omega_{t}
, and the fluid’s free boundary,
also called the moving boundary, free interface, or vacuum boundary, is defined as

\displaystyle\Gamma:=\bigcup_{0\leq t<T}\{t\}\times\Gamma_{t}=\bigcup_{0\leq t<T}\{t\}\times\partial\Omega_{t}.

Sometimes we also call
\Omega_{t}
and
\Gamma_{t}
the moving domain and the free boundary, respectively.
In free-boundary problems, understanding the dynamics of
\Gamma
is crucial, as it is the fact that
\Gamma_{t}

moves with time that distinguishes such problems from a standard initial-boundary value problem
where the boundary of the domain is fixed. Note that, according to the foregoing, we have

(1.3)

p=\varrho=0\,\text{ on }\,\Gamma.

1.1.
Physical vacuum boundary with a barotropic equation of state

Before discussing our work for a non-barotropic equation of state, let us consider the barotropic setting which will motivate our discussion. Consider the equation of state:

(1.4)

\displaystyle p=p(\varrho)=\varrho^{\kappa+1},\kappa>0,

where
\kappa
is constant. Equation (
1.1b
) then becomes

(1.5)

\displaystyle(\varrho^{\kappa+1}+\varrho)u^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}u^{\alpha}+c_{s}^{2}\mathsf{\Pi}^{\alpha\mu}\partial_{\mu}\varrho=0,

where

(1.6)

\displaystyle c_{s}^{2}:=p^{\prime}(\varrho)=(\kappa+1)\varrho^{\kappa}

is the fluid’s sound speed
[
3
]
and the second equality in (
1.6
) is valid
for the equation of state (
1.4
). Since
\varrho
vanishes on the
free-boundary, we also have

(1.7)

\displaystyle\left.c_{s}^{2}\right|_{\Gamma}=0.

Remark 1.2
.

Observe that (
1.7
) follows immediately from
(
1.3
) and (
1.6
). But
typically one would still impose (
1.7
) for other equations of state
than (
1.4
) when (
1.3
) holds,
since sound waves should not be allowed to propagate into vacuum across a region where the
medium (the density) vanishes.

The decay rate of
c_{s}^{2}
near the free-boundary plays a key role in this problem.
One needs to consider decay rates that allow for
\Gamma_{t}
to move with a bounded non-zero acceleration. We have from (
1.5
)
and (
1.6
) that the fluid’s (four-)acceleration is

\displaystyle a^{\alpha}:=u^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}u^{\alpha}=-\frac{(\kappa+1)\varrho^{\kappa-1}}{1+\varrho^{\kappa}}\mathsf{\Pi}^{\alpha\mu}\partial_{\mu}\varrho.

Since
\varrho\sim 0
near the free-boundary, we have that
1+\varrho=O(1)
so,
near the boundary,

\displaystyle a^{\alpha}\sim\varrho^{\kappa-1}\mathsf{\Pi}^{\alpha\mu}\partial_{\mu}\varrho\sim\bm{\partial}\varrho^{\kappa}\sim\bm{\partial}c_{s}^{2},

using
\bm{\partial}
to denote a generic spacetime derivative. At this point, we need to make some
assumption on the decay rate of
c_{s}^{2}
. In view of the finite speed propagation property,
away from the boundary the motion of the fluid is essentially the same as in the case without
a free-boundary. Thus, a natural scale in this problem which allows us to separate the bulk
and boundary behaviors is the distance to the boundary:

\displaystyle d\equiv d(t,x)=\,\text{distance from }\,x\,\text{ to }\,\Gamma_{t}.

Therefore, a natural assumption to make on
c_{s}^{2}
is that it decays like a power of
d
, i.e.,

c_{s}^{2}\sim d^{\beta}
for some
\beta>0
.
Under this assumption, the acceleration becomes

\displaystyle a\sim\bm{\partial}d^{\beta}=\beta d^{\beta-1}\bm{\partial}d\sim d^{\beta-1},

where we used that
\bm{\partial}d=O(1)
. From this, we see that

\displaystyle\left.a\right|_{\Gamma_{t}}=\begin{cases}0,&\beta>1,\\
\infty,&0<\beta<1,\\
\text{finite}\neq 0,&\beta=1.\end{cases}

Hence, we expect a realistic dynamics only in the case
\beta=1
.
Therefore,
we assume that

(1.8)

\displaystyle c_{s}^{2}(t,x)\approx d(t,x)\,\text{ for }\,x\,\text{ near }\,\Gamma_{t},

i.e.,
c_{s}^{2}
is comparable to the distance to the boundary. Condition
(
1.8
) is known as the
physical vacuum boundary condition.
One should
understand (
1.8
) as a constraint, i.e., something that is imposed on the initial
data and then propagated by the flow. The local well-posedness of the relativistic Euler
equations with (
1.4
) as equation of state and initial data
satisfying the physical vacuum boundary condition was obtained in
[
4
]
using Eulerian coordinates. Additionally, a-priori estimates for the same problem with equation of state (
1.4
) were obtained in
[
6
]
using Lagrangian coordinates. In
[
8
]
, the authors proved a priori estimates using an equation of state where the pressure is assumed to be a power law of the fluid’s baryon density
n
(see (
1.10
) below).

1.2.
The case of an ideal gas

We would like to extend the results of
[
4
]
to the case of a non-barotropic equation
of state. In addition, we would like, if possible, to treat situations that are of direct relevance to physicists
working in numerical simulations of star evolution. A typical equation of state used by physicists
in their simulations of stars is that of an
ideal gas
(see
[
15
]
Section 2.4),

(1.9)

\displaystyle p=p(n,\varepsilon)=n\varepsilon(\gamma-1),

where
n
is the fluid’s baryon density,
\varepsilon
is the fluid’s internal energy, and
\gamma>1

is a constant. We recall that
n
satisfies the equation

(1.10)

\displaystyle u^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}n+n\partial_{\mu}u^{\mu}=0,

and
\varepsilon
is defined through the relation

(1.11)

\displaystyle\varrho=n(1+\varepsilon).

Equation (
1.10
) is known as the conservation of baryon density
[
3
]
. This provides a closed system in terms of the unknowns
(\varepsilon,n,u^{\mu})
.

Since
\varepsilon\geq 0
, equation (
1.11
) implies that

n
also vanishes on the free boundary. In addition, since we must have
\varepsilon=0

in a vacuum, we also need to impose that
\varepsilon=0
on
\Gamma_{t}
.
We need to find conditions that play a similar role to the physical vacuum boundary
condition (
1.8
) employed for barotropic fluids. As in Section

1.1
, we try to determine such conditions by ensuring
a finite non-zero boundary acceleration.

We now adopt
(\varepsilon,n,u)
as primitive variables. In this situation,
using (
1.9
), equation (
1.1b
) becomes

(1.12)

\displaystyle\frac{1+\varepsilon\gamma}{\gamma-1}nu^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}u^{\alpha}+\varepsilon\mathsf{\Pi}^{\alpha\mu}\partial_{\mu}n+n\mathsf{\Pi}^{\alpha\mu}\partial_{\mu}\varepsilon=0.

Inspired by the discussion of Section
1.1
, we suppose that

\displaystyle n\sim d^{\beta},\,\varepsilon\sim d^{\sigma},\,\beta>0,\,\sigma>0.

Using this Ansatz into equation (
1.12
) and proceeding
as in Section
1.1
, we find

\displaystyle a\sim d^{\sigma-1}.

An interesting observation is that
d^{\beta}
cancels out and we are left with

\displaystyle\left.a\right|_{\Gamma_{t}}=\begin{cases}0,&\sigma>1,\\
\infty,&0<\sigma<1,\\
\text{finite}\neq 0,&\sigma=1.\end{cases}

Therefore, we conclude that in the case of an equation of state of an ideal gas given
by (
1.9
), the physical vacuum boundary conditions should be

(1.13)

\displaystyle\varepsilon(t,x)\approx d(t,x)\,\text{ and }\,n(t,x)\approx(d(t,x))^{\beta},\,\beta>0,\,\text{ for }\,x\,\text{ near }\,\Gamma_{t}.

Using (
1.9
) and (
1.11
),
in terms of
(\varepsilon,n,u)
, equations (
1.1
) and (
1.10
) read

(1.14a)

\displaystyle u^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}\varepsilon+(\gamma-1)\varepsilon\partial_{\mu}u^{\mu}

\displaystyle=0,

(1.14b)

\displaystyle\frac{1+\varepsilon\gamma}{\gamma-1}nu^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}u^{\alpha}+\varepsilon\mathsf{\Pi}^{\alpha\mu}\partial_{\mu}n+n\mathsf{\Pi}^{\alpha\mu}\partial_{\mu}\varepsilon

\displaystyle=0,

(1.14c)

\displaystyle u^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}n+n\partial_{\mu}u^{\mu}

\displaystyle=0,

(1.14d)

\displaystyle g_{\mu\nu}u^{\mu}u^{\nu}

\displaystyle=-1.

Remark 1.3
.

We observe that (
1.13
) is what we obtained solely from
an analysis of the boundary acceleration. However, from the thermodynamic relations and the equation of state (see (
1.22
) below), we have that the entropy
s
is given by

s=\frac{1}{\gamma-1}\log\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{n^{\gamma-1}}\right)+s_{0}.

where
s_{0}
is a constant.
From the physical boundary conditions, near the free boundary we would have that

s\sim\frac{1}{\gamma-1}\log\left(\frac{d}{d^{(\gamma-1)\beta}}\right)+s_{0}\sim\log(d^{1-\beta(\gamma-1)})+s_{0}.

Thus, as we approach the free boundary,
d\to 0
and
s
would behave like

(1.15)

s\to\begin{cases}-\infty&,\text{ for }\beta<\frac{1}{\gamma-1}\\
\text{finite value}&,\text{ for }\beta=\frac{1}{\gamma-1}\\
\infty&,\text{ for }\beta>\frac{1}{\gamma-1}.\end{cases}

Thus,
\beta=\frac{1}{\gamma-1}
is a natural condition for the decay rate of
n
in our problem. However, in what follows, our analysis will be solely with respect to the variables
s,u
, and
r
, where
r
is a multiple of
c_{s}^{2}
that we introduce in Section
1.4
. We make this remark to indicate that there is a natural choice for the physical vacuum boundary condition in the case of an ideal gas.

1.4.
The linearized system in terms of entropy and sound speed (squared)

Our goal is to rewrite (
1.14
) so that we can apply energy estimate techniques. For our purposes, it is more effective to consider the unknowns
(s,r,u^{\mu})
where
s
denotes the entropy per particle,
r
is a multiple of the sound speed (squared), and
u
is the usual (four)-velocity. We will often abuse language and simply refer to
r
as the sound speed (squared) or just the sound speed.

We begin by rewriting (
1.14
) in terms of
p
, and then introducing our new variables which will be used in the remainder of the work.

First, using (
1.9
) and (
1.14a
), we observe that

(1.17)

\begin{split}u^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}p&=(\gamma-1)nu^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}\varepsilon+(\gamma-1)\varepsilon u^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}n\\
&=-(\gamma-1)^{2}n\varepsilon\partial_{\mu}u^{\mu}-(\gamma-1)n\varepsilon\partial_{\mu}u^{\mu}\\
&=-\gamma p\partial_{\mu}u^{\mu}.\end{split}

Then, rewriting (
1.14b
) in terms of
\varepsilon
and
p
, our system (
1.14
) takes the form

(1.18a)

\displaystyle u^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}\varepsilon+(\gamma-1)\varepsilon\partial_{\mu}u^{\mu}

\displaystyle=0

(1.18b)

\displaystyle\frac{1+\varepsilon\gamma}{\gamma-1}pu^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}u^{\alpha}+\varepsilon\mathsf{\Pi}^{\alpha\mu}\partial_{\mu}p

\displaystyle=0

(1.18c)

\displaystyle u^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}p+\gamma p\partial_{\mu}u^{\mu}

\displaystyle=0.

In the following lines, we will make use of several identities (
1.19
) (
1.20
), and (
1.21
), which can be found in Section 2 of
[
15
]
.

Recall that the enthalpy per particle
h
is defined by

(1.19)

h=\frac{p+\varrho}{n}=\frac{n\varepsilon(\gamma-1)+n(1+\varepsilon))}{n}=\varepsilon\gamma+1

which has been simplified using (
1.9
) and (
1.11
).
Using the well known thermodynamic relation

(1.20)

ndh-dp=n\theta ds,

where
\theta
is the temperature for the fluid, and the fact that

(1.21)

p=n\theta

for an ideal fluid, we can solve for
s
by

(1.22)

\begin{split}&n\gamma d\varepsilon-(\gamma-1)(nd\varepsilon+\varepsilon dn)=n\varepsilon(\gamma-1)ds\\
&\implies s=\frac{1}{\gamma-1}\log\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{n^{\gamma-1}}\right)+s_{0}\end{split}

where
s_{0}
is a constant (and we will often set
s_{0}=0
for convenience). Additionally, a quick computation (such as in
[
3
]
) shows that (
1.14c
) along with the thermodynamic relations implies

(1.23)

u^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}s=0.

which is the conservation of entropy along flow lines.
Moreover, (
1.22
) allows us to solve for
\varepsilon
in terms of
s
and
p
. We see that
e^{(\gamma-1)s}=\frac{\varepsilon}{n^{\gamma-1}}=\frac{1}{\gamma-1}\cdot\frac{p}{n^{\gamma}}
, which leads to

(1.24)

\varepsilon=\frac{e^{\frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma}s}}{(\gamma-1)^{\frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma}}}p^{\frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma}}

Now, substituting for
\varepsilon
in (
1.18b
) and mulitplying by
(\gamma-1)^{\frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma}}
, we get

(1.25)

C_{1}pu^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}u^{\alpha}+e^{\frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma}s}p^{\frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma}}\mathsf{\Pi}^{\alpha\mu}\partial_{\mu}p=0

where

C_{1}=\frac{1}{(\gamma-1)^{\frac{1}{\gamma}}}+e^{\frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma}s}p^{\frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma}}\frac{\gamma}{\gamma-1}.

Note that
C_{1}
is
O(1)
as we approach the free boundary since
p\to 0
.

Before proceeding, we can quickly verify that the acceleration of the free boundary is
O(1)
using (
1.13
). Near the free boundary,
\varepsilon\sim d
where
d
is the distance to the boundary. Using (
1.24
), we get
p\sim d^{\frac{\gamma}{\gamma-1}}
. In this case,

(1.26)

a^{\alpha}=u^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}u^{\alpha}\sim p^{-\frac{1}{\gamma}}\bm{\partial}p\sim\left(d^{\frac{\gamma}{\gamma-1}}\right)^{-\frac{1}{\gamma}}d^{\frac{1}{\gamma-1}}=O(1).

Because,
d\sim p^{\frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma}}
, this motivates the definition of our new variable

(1.27)

r:=p^{\frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma}}

with the idea that
r\sim d
and
r
will serve as our weight in the energy for the free boundary problem. After computing the sound speed squared given by
c_{s}^{2}=\frac{dp}{d\varrho}|_{s}
, we further note that
r
is comparable to the sound speed (squared) for the fluid and it will play a similar role as in the work from
[
4
]
.
Using (
1.18c
) above, we can quickly verify that

(1.28)

u^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}r=\frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma}p^{-\frac{1}{\gamma}}u^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}p=-(\gamma-1)r\partial_{\mu}u^{\mu}

and thus (
1.18c
) becomes

(1.29)

u^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}r+(\gamma-1)r\partial_{\mu}u^{\mu}=0

In order to complete the system in terms of
(s,r,u^{\mu})
we must further rewrite the second equation (
1.25
). Observing that
\partial_{\mu}p=\frac{\gamma}{\gamma-1}p^{\frac{1}{\gamma}}\partial_{\mu}r
, we get

(1.30)

\begin{split}C_{1}u^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}u^{\alpha}+e^{\frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma}s}p^{-\frac{1}{\gamma}}\mathsf{\Pi}^{\alpha\mu}\partial_{\mu}p=C_{1}u^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}u^{\alpha}+e^{\frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma}s}\frac{\gamma}{\gamma-1}\mathsf{\Pi}^{\alpha\mu}\partial_{\mu}r&=0\end{split}

Then, calling

(1.31)

C_{2}:=C_{1}\cdot\frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma}e^{-\frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma}s},

we get the form of the system in terms of the unknowns
(s,r,u^{\mu})
:

(1.32a)

\displaystyle u^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}s=0

(1.32b)

\displaystyle u^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}u^{\alpha}+\frac{1}{C_{2}}\mathsf{\Pi}^{\alpha\mu}\partial_{\mu}r

\displaystyle=0

(1.32c)

\displaystyle u^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}r+(\gamma-1)r\partial_{\mu}u^{\mu}

\displaystyle=0.

where
C_{2}=\frac{(\gamma-1)^{\frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma}}}{\gamma e^{\frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma}s}}+r
. For simplicity, let’s define

(1.33)

\Gamma:=\Gamma(s)=\frac{(\gamma-1)^{\frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma}}}{\gamma e^{\frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma}s}}

so that
C_{2}=\Gamma(s)+r
.

Finally, using the convective derivative which we denote as

(1.34)

D_{t}=u^{\mu}\partial_{\mu},

our new system in terms of the unknowns
(s,r,u^{\alpha})
takes the form

(1.35a)

\displaystyle D_{t}s

\displaystyle=0

(1.35b)

\displaystyle D_{t}r+(\gamma-1)r\partial_{\mu}u^{\mu}

\displaystyle=0

(1.35c)

\displaystyle D_{t}u^{\alpha}+\frac{1}{\Gamma+r}\mathsf{\Pi}^{\alpha\mu}\partial_{\mu}r

\displaystyle=0

(1.35d)

\displaystyle g_{\alpha\beta}u^{\alpha}u^{\beta}

\displaystyle=-1

where
\Gamma=\Gamma(s)
is defined according to (
1.33
).

Now that the form of our system is complete, we will compute the full linearized equations using the standard procedure with linearized variables.
Consider a one-parameter family of solutions
\{s_{\tau},r_{\tau},u_{\tau}\}_{\tau}
for the main system (
1.35
) such that
(s_{\tau},r_{\tau},u_{\tau})|_{\tau=0}=(s,r,u)
. We can now formally define the function
\delta=\frac{d}{d\tau}|_{\tau=0}
and linearized variables
(\tilde{s},\tilde{r},\tilde{u}):=(\delta s,\delta{r},\delta u)
. These variables will solve the linearized system that we now compute by taking
\delta
of the equations in (
1.35
).
After a computation, we can write the linearized equations in the form:

(1.36a)

\displaystyle D_{t}\tilde{s}

\displaystyle=f

(1.36b)

\displaystyle D_{t}\tilde{r}+{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\tilde{u}^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}r}+{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}(\gamma-1)r\partial_{\mu}\tilde{u}^{\mu}}

\displaystyle=g

(1.36c)

\displaystyle D_{t}\tilde{u}^{\alpha}+{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\frac{1}{\Gamma+r}\mathsf{\Pi}^{\alpha\mu}\partial_{\mu}\tilde{r}}

\displaystyle=h^{\alpha}

(1.36d)

\displaystyle\tilde{u}_{\alpha}u^{\alpha}

\displaystyle=0

where
\Gamma
is defined according to (
1.33
), and

(1.37)

\begin{split}&f=-\tilde{u}^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}s\\
&g=-(\gamma-1)\tilde{r}\partial_{\mu}u^{\mu}\\
&h^{\alpha}=-\tilde{u}^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}u^{\alpha}-\frac{1}{\Gamma+r}(\tilde{u}^{\alpha}u^{\mu}+u^{\alpha}\tilde{u}^{\mu})\partial_{\mu}r+\frac{\Gamma^{\prime}}{(\Gamma+r)^{2}}\tilde{s}\mathsf{\Pi}^{\alpha\mu}\partial_{\mu}r+\frac{1}{(\Gamma+r)^{2}}\tilde{r}\mathsf{\Pi}^{\alpha\mu}\partial_{\mu}r\end{split}

Remark 1.8
.

We note that the terms in
blue
will later combine to form a perfect derivative that will assist in our energy estimates. Additionally, each of the terms on the RHS contains undifferentiated
(\tilde{s},\tilde{r},\tilde{u})
with coefficients of the form
(\bm{\partial}s,\bm{\partial}r,\bm{\partial}u)
.

To clarify the exposition, we will be making the following assumption which can be used when handling the relativistic Euler equations with a physical vacuum boundary (see
[
4
]
and
[
6
]
for similar examples).

Assumption 1.9

(Uniform smallness of
r
)
.

Without loss of generality, we will assume that for each

,
r
is uniformly small in
\Omega_{t}
:

\|r\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{t})}\leq\hat{\varepsilon}\ll\frac{1}{2}

where
\hat{\varepsilon}
is a small positive constant that we fix for the remainder of the paper. By ‘
\ll\frac{1}{2}
’, we mean, in particular, that there exists a large positive constant
C
such that
C\hat{\varepsilon}<\frac{1}{2}
. We will fix this constant
C
for the remainder of the paper.

Recall from the physical vacuum boundary conditions and (
1.27
) that this assumption will be verified in a small neighborhood of the free boundary
\Gamma
. Due to finite speed of propagation, the behavior of the fluid away from the boundary is non-degenerate and a priori estimates in Sobolev spaces can be handled using methods from the standard relativistic Euler equations. The removal of Assumption
1.9
requires a partition of unity argument in which one must separate the fluid into its bulk behavior away from the free boundary and its behavior near the free boundary. By using Assumption
1.9
, we are able to focus on the essential difficulties posed by the degenerate nature of the free boundary where
\|r\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{t})}\leq\hat{\varepsilon}\ll\frac{1}{2}
.

Remark 1.10
.

In what follows, we will often refer to the ‘smallness’ of
r
near the free boundary which will allow certain terms to make much smaller contributions. We will use
\hat{\varepsilon}
when appropriate to reference Assumption
1.9
.

Remark 1.11
.

Additionally, when handling these ‘small’ terms (see Remark
2.6
in Section 6), we will often use the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality with
\varepsilon
. Thus, it is useful to fix the interplay between
\hat{\varepsilon}
and
\varepsilon
so as to not cause any confusion. Given that
\hat{\varepsilon}\ll\frac{1}{2}
is fixed by Assumption
1.9
, and we also have
\frac{1}{C-1}<\frac{1}{2}
where
C
is the fixed positive constant from Assumption
1.9
.
We will use
\varepsilon
in applications of the Cauchy-inequality-with-
\varepsilon
to be a small positive constant such that

(1.38)

0<\frac{1}{C-1}<\varepsilon<\frac{1}{2}

Note that the previous definitions make the following inequalities true

(1.39)

\begin{split}0<\hat{\varepsilon}<\frac{1}{2C}<\frac{1}{C-1}<\varepsilon&<\frac{1}{2}\\
\left(1+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\right)\hat{\varepsilon}&<C\hat{\varepsilon}\\
\end{split}

Remark 1.12

(The symbol ‘
\lesssim
’)
.

Throughout our work, the symbol
\lesssim
will be used in the usual fashion, i.e.

A\lesssim B\Longleftrightarrow A\leq\mathcal{D}B

where
\mathcal{D}
is a constant depending on the the fixed data of the problem. In our case,
\mathcal{D}
will depend on the background solution
(s,r,u)
along with
\gamma
and

. By slight abuse of notation, we will sometimes write

if we want to highlight, for example, that

depends on

. This is also done to assist the reader in following our estimates where these quantities are often removed from an integral with the

1.5.
Function spaces and Energies

Here, we define the function spaces and energies that will play a key role in our work. We denote the weighted
L^{2}
spaces with weight
h
as
L^{2}(h)
and equip them with the norm

(1.40)

\|f\|_{L^{2}(h)}^{2}=\int_{\Omega_{t}}h|f|^{2}\hskip 7.11317ptdx.

We will assume throughout that
r
is a positive function on
\Omega_{t}
which vanishes simply on the free boundary
\Gamma_{t}
. Thus,
r
will be comparable to the distance to
\Gamma_{t}
.

For pairs of functions in our system defined on
\Omega_{t}
, we will use the base Hilbert space

(1.41)

\mathcal{H}=L^{2}(r^{\frac{2-\gamma}{\gamma-1}})\times L^{2}(r^{\frac{1}{\gamma-1}})

with the usual norm depending only on the weight
r
. However, we will often use an equivalent norm which is compatible with our energies in the problem. Let
G_{\alpha\beta}
be the following two form

(1.42)

G_{\alpha\beta}:=g_{\alpha\beta}+2u_{\alpha}u_{\beta}

which, by
[
6
]
, has been shown to be a Riemannian metric in spacetime.

Then, we define the norm

(1.43)

\|(\tilde{r},\tilde{u})\|_{\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}}^{2}=\int_{\Omega_{t}}r^{\frac{2-\gamma}{\gamma-1}}\left(\frac{1}{\gamma-1}\tilde{r}^{2}+(\Gamma+r)r|\tilde{u}|^{2}\right)\hskip 5.69046ptdx

where
|\tilde{u}|^{2}=|\tilde{u}|^{2}_{G}=G_{\alpha\beta}\tilde{u}^{\alpha}\tilde{u}^{\beta}\geq 0
.

Remark 1.13
.

We observe that the two norms
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{H}}
and
\|\cdot\|_{\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}}
are equivalent on the space of pairs
(\tilde{r},\tilde{u})
due to a key fact about the weights appearing in the energy. For
x
sufficiently close to
\partial\Omega_{t}
, we have that
r\to 0
,
s
approaches a finite value
C_{s}
by (
1.15
), and the weight

(1.44)

(\Gamma+r)\to\frac{(\gamma-1)^{\frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma}}}{\gamma e^{\frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma}C_{s}}}>0

which is a finite positive constant bounded away from
0
(as
\gamma>1
is fixed, and
C_{s}
fixed). Hence,
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{H}}
and
\|\cdot\|_{\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}}
are equivalent near the free boundary.

We also define the higher order weighted Sobolev spaces
H^{j,\sigma}
with integer
j\geq 0
and
\sigma>-\frac{1}{2}
to be the space of all distributions in
\Omega_{t}
whose norm

(1.45)

\|f\|_{H^{j,\sigma}}^{2}=\sum_{|\alpha|\leq j}\|r^{\sigma}\partial^{\alpha}f\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

is finite. For higher regularity, we will use the following higher order Sobolev spaces where the powers of
r
are linked to the number of derivatives.
Similar to
[
4
]
, we will define higher order function spaces
\mathcal{H}^{2k}
on triplets
(\tilde{s},\tilde{r},\tilde{u})
in
\Omega_{t}
with the following norm

(1.46)

\begin{split}\|(\tilde{s},\tilde{r},\tilde{u})\|_{\mathcal{H}^{2k}}^{2}&=\sum_{|\alpha|=0}^{2k}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a=0\\
|\alpha|-a\leq k\end{subarray}}^{k}\|r^{\frac{2-\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}+\frac{1}{2}+a}\partial^{\alpha}\tilde{s}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|r^{\frac{2-\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}+a}\partial^{\alpha}\tilde{r}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|r^{\frac{2-\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}+\frac{1}{2}+a}\partial^{\alpha}\tilde{u}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\\
\end{split}

where we note that the
r
weight in the
\tilde{r}
norm is
\frac{1}{2}
less than the weights on
\tilde{s}
and
\tilde{u}
(which is a direct consequence of the powers of
r
in (
1.36
)). Additionally, these higher order function spaces are based around an even number of derivatives due to the underlying wave-like operator that governs the second order evolution of (
1.36
). Taking
D_{t}
of (
1.36
) illustrates this underlying operator which is a variable coefficient version of
D_{t}^{2}-r\Delta
.

Remark 1.14
.

Similar to
[
4
]
and
[
7
]
, we can use embedding theorems to show that the
\mathcal{H}^{2k}
norm is equivalent to the
H^{2k,\frac{1}{2(\gamma-1)}+k}\times H^{2k,\frac{2-\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}+k}\times H^{2k,\frac{1}{2(\gamma-1)}+k}
norm.

Remark 1.15
.

We remark that the
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}
norm will be used to control convective derivatives of
\tilde{r}
and
\tilde{u}
which satisfy a system of wave equations (see (
1.47
) for the definition of
E^{2k}_{\text{wave}})
. Meanwhile,
\tilde{u}
will be decomposed into its vorticity part which, alongside
\tilde{s}
, satisfies a transport equation that we can estimate directly (see (
1.48
) for the definition of

.

Here, we introduce energies that will be used in the higher order analysis. First, we have the wave energy

(1.47)

E^{2k}_{\text{wave}}(\tilde{s},\tilde{r},\tilde{u})=\sum_{j=0}^{k}\|(D_{t}^{2j}\tilde{r},D_{t}^{2j}\tilde{u})\|_{\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}}^{2}

recalling (
1.43
). Note that the definition of
\tilde{\mathcal{H}}
guarantees that
|D_{t}^{2j}\tilde{u}|^{2}=|D_{t}^{2j}\tilde{u}|^{2}_{G}\geq 0
.

Additionally, we will use the transport energy

(1.48)

E^{2k}_{\text{transport}}(\tilde{s},\tilde{r},\tilde{u})=\|\hat{\omega}\|_{H^{2k-1,k+\frac{1}{2(\gamma-1)}}}^{2}+\|\tilde{s}\|_{H^{2k,k+\frac{1}{2(\gamma-1)}}}^{2}

where
\hat{\omega}
is the reduced linearized vorticity defined in (

), and this will be addressed in Section

.
Then, the final linearized energy is given by

(1.49)

E^{2k}(\tilde{s},\tilde{r},\tilde{u})=E^{2k}_{\text{wave}}(\tilde{s},\tilde{r},\tilde{u})+E^{2k}_{\text{transport}}(\tilde{s},\tilde{r},\tilde{u}).

Remark 1.16
.

Although both components of

satisfy transport equations, we remark that they will be used slightly differently in the arguments that follow. In particular, the estimate for
\hat{\omega}
will be used to complete div-curl estimates for
\tilde{u}
, whereas
\tilde{s}
can be estimated directly with the
\mathcal{H}^{2k}
norm from above and below as in Section

.

1.6.

and
\tilde{u}
in weighted

spaces. Although the higher order estimates in Sobolev spaces require additional techniques, this section serves as a starting point in our analysis.
As discussed in Section

\tilde{s}

After computing the linearized equations (
1.36

), (
1.36d

\tilde{u}_{\alpha}\mathsf{\Pi}^{\alpha\mu}=\tilde{u}^{\mu}=G_{\alpha\beta}\tilde{u}^{\beta}\mathsf{\Pi}^{\alpha\mu}

(1.50a)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}r^{\frac{1}{\gamma-1}}D_{t}\tilde{s}^{2}

\displaystyle=-r^{\frac{1}{\gamma-1}}\tilde{s}\tilde{u}^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}s

(1.50b)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2(\gamma-1)}r^{\frac{2-\gamma}{\gamma-1}}D_{t}\tilde{r}^{2}+{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\frac{1}{\gamma-1}r^{\frac{2-\gamma}{\gamma-1}}\tilde{r}\tilde{u}^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}r}+{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}r^{\frac{1}{\gamma-1}}\tilde{r}\partial_{\mu}\tilde{u}^{\mu}}

\displaystyle=-\tilde{r}^{2}r^{\frac{2-\gamma}{\gamma-1}}\partial_{\mu}u^{\mu}

(1.50c)

\displaystyle\frac{\Gamma+r}{2}r^{\frac{1}{\gamma-1}}D_{t}|\tilde{u}|^{2}+{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}r^{\frac{1}{\gamma-1}}\tilde{u}^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}\tilde{r}}

\displaystyle=-(\Gamma+r)r^{\frac{1}{\gamma-1}}\tilde{u}_{\alpha}\tilde{u}^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}u^{\alpha}-r^{\frac{1}{\gamma-1}}|\tilde{u}|^{2}u^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}r

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt+\frac{\Gamma^{\prime}}{\Gamma+r}r^{\frac{1}{\gamma-1}}\tilde{s}\tilde{u}^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}r+\frac{1}{\Gamma+r}r^{\frac{1}{\gamma-1}}\tilde{r}\tilde{u}^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}r.

Thus, after adding the equations together, the
blue
terms combine to form a perfect derivative:

(1.51)

{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\frac{1}{\gamma-1}r^{\frac{2-\gamma}{\gamma-1}}\tilde{r}\tilde{u}^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}r}+{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}r^{\frac{1}{\gamma-1}}\tilde{r}\partial_{\mu}\tilde{u}^{\mu}}+{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}r^{\frac{1}{\gamma-1}}\tilde{u}^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}\tilde{r}}={\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\partial_{\mu}(r^{\frac{1}{\gamma-1}}\tilde{r}\tilde{u}^{\mu})}

(1.52)

\begin{split}\frac{1}{2}r^{\frac{1}{\gamma-1}}D_{t}\tilde{s}^{2}+\frac{1}{2(\gamma-1)}r^{\frac{2-\gamma}{\gamma-1}}D_{t}\tilde{r}^{2}+\frac{\Gamma+r}{2}r^{\frac{1}{\gamma-1}}D_{t}|\tilde{u}|^{2}&+{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\partial_{\mu}(r^{\frac{1}{\gamma-1}}\tilde{r}\tilde{u}^{\mu})}\\

&\hskip 14.22636pt-\tilde{r}^{2}r^{\frac{2-\gamma}{\gamma-1}}\partial_{\mu}u^{\mu}\\
&\hskip 14.22636pt-(\Gamma+r)r^{\frac{1}{\gamma-1}}\tilde{u}_{\alpha}\tilde{u}^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}u^{\alpha}-r^{\frac{1}{\gamma-1}}|\tilde{u}|^{2}u^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}r\\
&\hskip 14.22636pt+\frac{\Gamma^{\prime}}{\Gamma+r}r^{\frac{1}{\gamma-1}}\tilde{s}\tilde{u}^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}r+\frac{1}{\Gamma+r}r^{\frac{1}{\gamma-1}}\tilde{r}\tilde{u}^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}r.\end{split}

Next, we integrate with respect to
x
over
\Omega_{t}
and use the moving domain formula (noting that the fluid particles on the boundary move with velocity
\frac{u^{i}}{u^{0}}
):

(1.53)

\begin{split}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega_{t}}f\hskip 2.84526ptdx&=\int_{\Omega_{t}}\partial_{t}f\hskip 2.84526ptdx+\int_{\Omega_{t}}\partial_{i}\left(f\frac{u^{i}}{u^{0}}\right)\hskip 2.84526ptdx\\

\end{split}

For the
blue

on
\partial\Omega_{t}
, so integrating by parts yields

(1.54)

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega_{t}}{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\partial_{\mu}(r^{\frac{1}{\gamma-1}}\tilde{r}\tilde{u}^{\mu})}\hskip 2.84526ptdx&=\int_{\Omega_{t}}\partial_{i}(r^{\frac{1}{\gamma-1}}\tilde{r}\tilde{u}^{i})\hskip 2.84526ptdx+\int_{\Omega_{t}}\partial_{t}(r^{\frac{1}{\gamma-1}}\tilde{r}\tilde{u}^{0})\hskip 2.84526ptdx\\

&=\int_{\Omega_{t}}\partial_{t}(r^{\frac{1}{\gamma-1}}\tilde{r}\tilde{u}^{0})\hskip 2.84526ptdx\end{split}

For the energy terms, we plan to apply (
1.53
) to the function

(1.55)

f(t,x)=\frac{1}{2}r^{\frac{1}{\gamma-1}}\tilde{s}^{2}+\frac{1}{2(\gamma-1)}r^{\frac{2-\gamma}{\gamma-1}}\tilde{r}^{2}+\frac{\Gamma+r}{2}r^{\frac{1}{\gamma-1}}|\tilde{u}|^{2}

First, recall using the

D_{t}r=-(\gamma-1)r\partial_{\mu}u^{\mu}
. We then get

(1.56)

\begin{split}D_{t}f(t,x)&=\frac{1}{2}r^{\frac{1}{\gamma-1}}D_{t}\tilde{s}^{2}+\frac{1}{2(\gamma-1)}r^{\frac{2-\gamma}{\gamma-1}}D_{t}\tilde{r}^{2}+\frac{\Gamma+r}{2}r^{\frac{1}{\gamma-1}}D_{t}|\tilde{u}|^{2}\\
&\hskip 14.22636pt-\frac{1}{2}r^{\frac{1}{\gamma-1}}\partial_{\mu}u^{\mu}\tilde{s}^{2}-\frac{2-\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}r^{\frac{2-\gamma}{\gamma-1}}\partial_{\mu}u^{\mu}\tilde{r}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}r^{\frac{1}{\gamma-1}}(\Gamma+\gamma r)\partial_{\mu}u^{\mu}|\tilde{u}|^{2}\\
&={\color[rgb]{0,0,1}-\partial_{\mu}(r^{\frac{1}{\gamma-1}}\tilde{r}\tilde{u}^{\mu})}\\
&\hskip 14.22636pt-r^{\frac{1}{\gamma-1}}\tilde{s}\tilde{u}^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}s\\
&\hskip 14.22636pt-\tilde{r}^{2}r^{\frac{2-\gamma}{\gamma-1}}\partial_{\mu}u^{\mu}\\
&\hskip 14.22636pt-(\Gamma+r)r^{\frac{1}{\gamma-1}}\tilde{u}_{\alpha}\tilde{u}^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}u^{\alpha}-r^{\frac{1}{\gamma-1}}|\tilde{u}|^{2}u^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}r\\
&\hskip 14.22636pt+\frac{\Gamma^{\prime}}{\Gamma+r}r^{\frac{1}{\gamma-1}}\tilde{s}\tilde{u}^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}r+\frac{1}{\Gamma+r}r^{\frac{1}{\gamma-1}}\tilde{r}\tilde{u}^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}r\\
&\hskip 14.22636pt-\frac{1}{2}r^{\frac{1}{\gamma-1}}\partial_{\mu}u^{\mu}\tilde{s}^{2}-\frac{2-\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}r^{\frac{2-\gamma}{\gamma-1}}\partial_{\mu}u^{\mu}\tilde{r}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}r^{\frac{1}{\gamma-1}}(\Gamma+\gamma r)\partial_{\mu}u^{\mu}|\tilde{u}|^{2}\\
\end{split}

using the fact that

and computing several more expressions.

) to

and integrate over

\begin{split}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega_{t}}f(t,x)\hskip 2.84526ptdx&=\int_{\Omega_{t}}f(t,x)\partial_{i}\left(\frac{u^{i}}{u^{0}}\right)\hskip 5.69046ptdx\\

Proposition 1.18

(Basic Energy Inequality)
.

Let
(s,r,u)
be a solution to (
1.35
) that exists on some time interval
[0,T]
. Assume that
s,r,u
and their first order derivatives are bounded in the
L^{\infty}(\Omega_{t})
norm for each

and
r
vanishes simply on the free boundary. Then, the following estimate holds for solutions
(\tilde{s},\tilde{r},\tilde{u})
to (

(1.59)

E^{0}[t]\lesssim E^{0}[0]\exp\left(\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{C}(\|\partial s,\partial r,\partial u,s,r,u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{\tau})})\hskip 5.69046ptd\tau\right)

where

is given by (
1.58

\mathcal{C}
is a function depending on the

norms of

and their first order derivatives.

Before giving the proof, we provide the following remarks which are critical in understanding the methods used throughout this paper.

Remark 1.19

(The symbol ‘
\lesssim
’)
.

Throughout our work, the symbol
\lesssim
will be used in the usual fashion, i.e.

A\lesssim B\Longleftrightarrow A\leq\mathcal{D}B

where
\mathcal{D}
is a constant depending on the the fixed data of the problem. In our case,
\mathcal{D}
will depend on the background solution
(s,r,u)
along with
\gamma
and
T
.

Remark 1.20
.

In view of (
1.44
), we will often use inequalities of the following form when handling weights that appear in our integrals:

(1.60)

\int_{\Omega_{t}}r^{\frac{1}{\gamma-1}}|\tilde{u}|^{2}\hskip 5.69046ptdx\leq\left\|\frac{1}{\Gamma+r}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{t})}\int_{\Omega_{t}}(\Gamma+r)r^{\frac{1}{\gamma-1}}|\tilde{u}|^{2}\hskip 5.69046ptdx\lesssim E^{0}

where we note that
\frac{1}{\Gamma+r}
tends to a finite positive constant depending on
\gamma
when we are sufficiently close to the boundary (see Remark
1.13
).
When we refer to an expression consisting of
s,r,u
and
\gamma
as
O(1)
near the free boundary, we simply are referring to the fact that when
r
is sufficiently small, this weight tends to a finite constant bounded away from zero. Thus, it can be absorbed into the
\lesssim
symbol without having much effect on the primary estimate.

Remark 1.21
.

Additionally, there are many situations in which we would like to estimate expressions involving
spacetime
derivatives
\partial_{\mu}
over the
spatial
region
\Omega_{t}
. However, this can be done with the help of section
2.2
. The primary idea is that we can use system (
1.35
) directly to solve for
\partial_{t}(s,r,u)
and
\partial_{t}(\tilde{s},\tilde{r},\tilde{u})
in terms of the spatial part
\partial(s,r,u)
and
\partial(\tilde{s},\tilde{r},\tilde{u})
respectively, plus additional terms that can be estimated. A key identity is

(1.61)

\tilde{u}^{0}=\frac{u_{j}}{u^{0}}\tilde{u}^{j}

which follows from the orthogonality of
u
and
\tilde{u}
in (
1.36d
).
With section
2.2

Using system (

\displaystyle D_{t}\left(D_{t}^{2k}\tilde{r}\right)+\left(D_{t}^{2k}\tilde{u}^{\mu}\right)\partial_{\mu}r+(\gamma-1)r\partial_{\mu}\left(D_{t}^{2k}\tilde{u}^{\mu}\right)=B_{2k}

(2.2)

\begin{split}B_{2k}&=D_{t}^{2k}g-\sum_{i=0}^{2k-1}\left(D_{t}^{i}\tilde{u}^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}\left(D_{t}^{2k-i}r\right)-D_{t}^{2k-i}r\partial_{\mu}\left(D_{t}^{i}\tilde{u}^{\mu}\right)\right)\\

\end{split}

and

Lemma 2.1

Suppose that

2.1.
Defining order for free-boundary terms

Definition 2.5

(
\mathcal{H}^{2k}
-critical, subcritical, and supercritical terms)
.

Let
m,l\in\mathbb{N}
.
In view of Lemma
2.2
, any terms of the form

r^{m}\partial^{l}\tilde{u},r^{m}\partial^{l}\tilde{s}

or

r^{m}\partial^{l}\tilde{r}

with
0\leq l\leq 2k
and
m\geq 0
will be called
\mathcal{H}^{2k}
-critical
provided that
m-l+k-\frac{1}{2}=0
, or respectively
m-l+k=0
. Additionally, terms such that
m-l+k-\frac{1}{2}>0
or respectively
m-l+k>0
will be called
\mathcal{H}^{2k}
-subcritical
. Similarly, terms where
m-l+k-\frac{1}{2}<0
,
m-l+k<0
will be denoted
\mathcal{H}^{2k}
-supercritical
. In view of subsection
2.2
, a similar classification can also be used for the terms
r^{m}\bm{\partial}^{l}\tilde{u},r^{m}\bm{\partial^{l}}\tilde{s}
, and
r^{m}\bm{\partial}^{l}\tilde{r}
.

Remark 2.6
.

We can also refer to the
\mathcal{H}^{2k}
-
order
of a given term by computing the value
\mathcal{O}:=m-l+k-\frac{1}{2}
(or
\mathcal{O}:=m-l+k
for terms involving
\tilde{r}
) and the sign of
\mathcal{O}
will determine if the term is subcritical or supercritical. As an example, a term of the form
r^{k}\partial^{2k}\tilde{u}
will be called
\mathcal{H}^{2k}
-supercritical of order
-\frac{1}{2}
. The value
\mathcal{O}
can be interpreted as a indicator for how well that term can be estimated using the
\mathcal{H}^{2k}
norm. Critical terms have the exact number of derivatives and powers of
r
to be estimated, whereas supercrtical terms are lacking key additional powers of
r
. Also, note that the order
\mathcal{O}
depends on a particular derivative level, i.e. what value of
k
we are using in order to estimate terms using the
\mathcal{H}^{2k}
norm. Also, note that that the weights in the
\mathcal{H}^{2k}
norm depend not only on
k
, but also on
\gamma>1
which is a fixed constant. For clarity, one can interpret this notion of
order
as in the simplified setting where
\gamma=2
since our starting Lemma
2.2
is built around the function spaces
H^{2k,k+\frac{1}{2}}
and
H^{2k,k}
. In that case, the
\mathcal{H}^{2k}
-
order
of a term
T
can be interpreted as a statement about the powers of
r
present/needed in the term
T
in order for the inequality

\|T\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\lesssim\|(\tilde{s},\tilde{r},\tilde{u})\|_{\mathcal{H}^{2k}}^{2}\approx\|\tilde{s}\|_{H^{2k,k+\frac{1}{2}}}^{2}+\|\tilde{r}\|_{H^{2k,k}}^{2}+\|\tilde{u}\|_{H^{2k,k+\frac{1}{2}}}^{2}

to hold, and the symbol
\approx
is used to represent the norm equivalence from Remark
1.14
. For arbitrary
\gamma>1
, We will instead use the function spaces
H^{2k,k+\frac{1}{2}+\frac{2-\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}}
and
H^{2k,k+\frac{2-\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}}
. In this setting, the
\mathcal{H}^{2k}
-order for a term
T
is a statement about the inequality

(2.4)

\|T\|_{L^{2}(r^{\frac{2-\gamma}{\gamma-1}})}^{2}\lesssim\|(\tilde{s},\tilde{r},\tilde{u})\|_{\mathcal{H}^{2k}}^{2}\approx\|\tilde{s}\|_{H^{2k,\frac{2-\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}+k+\frac{1}{2}}}^{2}+\|\tilde{r}\|_{H^{2k,\frac{2-\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}+k}}^{2}+\|\tilde{u}\|_{H^{2k,\frac{2-\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}+k+\frac{1}{2}}}^{2}

which we summarize:

•

Suppose that
T
is
\mathcal{H}^{2k}
subcritical. We claim that
inequality (
2.4
) holds as well as

(2.5)

\|T\|_{L^{2}(r^{\frac{2-\gamma}{\gamma-1}})}^{2}\lesssim\hat{\varepsilon}\|(\tilde{s},\tilde{r},\tilde{u})\|_{\mathcal{H}^{2k}}^{2}

using the previous Lemmas (also, note that (
2.5
) implies (
2.4
)). To see this fact, let’s assume that
T
consists of
\tilde{r}
terms so that
T=r^{m}\partial^{l}\tilde{r}
(although
\tilde{u}
and
\tilde{s}
terms are similar). By Definition
2.5
, we have that
m-l+k>0
. If
l=2k
, then we must have
m>k
(extra power of
r
) and we can apply Lemma
2.1
to get the desired inequality (
2.5
) with
\hat{\varepsilon}
. If
l<2k
, i.e.

with

, then we must have

(and
m=0

\|r^{m}\partial^{2k-b}\tilde{r}\|_{H^{0,\frac{2-\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}}}^{2}\lesssim\|\tilde{r}\|_{H^{2k-b,m+\frac{2-\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}}}\leq\|\tilde{r}\|_{H^{2k,m+b+\frac{2-\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}}}.

Then, since

•

If
T
is
\mathcal{H}^{2k}
critical, then (
2.4
) holds by Lemma
2.2
, but (
2.5

to spare.

•

If
T
is
\mathcal{H}^{2k}
supercritical with order
\mathcal{O}<0
, then (
2.4
) does not hold (and subsequently (
2.5

Lemma 2.7

(Sum of
\mathcal{H}^{2k}
orders)
.

Suppose that
T_{1}
and
T_{2}
are free boundary terms (i.e.

and

and

) that have
\mathcal{H}^{2k}

\mathcal{O}_{1}

Proof.

Without loss of generality, the proof can be obtained by splitting into cases:

(1)

and
T_{2}=r^{m_{2}}\partial^{l_{2}}\tilde{u}

(2)

and
T_{2}=r^{m_{2}}\partial^{l_{2}}\tilde{r}
,
m_{1},m_{2}\geq 0
,
0\leq l_{1},l_{2}\leq 2k

(3)

T_{1}=r^{m_{1}}\partial^{l_{1}}\tilde{r}
and
T_{2}=r^{m_{2}}\partial^{l_{2}}\tilde{u}
,
m_{1},m_{2}\geq 0
,

and correctly distributing the powers of

as needed.

∎

(2.11)

\begin{split}[\partial,D_{t}^{N}]\phi&=[\partial,D_{t}](D_{t}^{N-1}\phi)+D_{t}([\partial,D_{t}^{N-1}]\phi)\\
[D_{t},\partial^{N}]\phi&=[D_{t},\partial]\partial^{N-1}\phi+\partial\left([D_{t},\partial^{N-1}]\phi\right)\\

Lemma 2.10

(First Commutator Lemma)
.

Let
N\in\mathbb{N}
. For the convective derivative
D_{t}=u^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}
, generic spatial derivative
\partial
, and
\phi
sufficiently smooth, the following identities hold:

(2.12)

\begin{split}[\partial,D_{t}]\phi&=(\partial u^{\nu})\partial_{\nu}\phi\\
[\partial,D_{t}^{2}]\phi&=2(\partial u^{\nu})\partial_{\nu}(D_{t}\phi)+[D_{t}(\partial u^{\nu})-(\partial u^{\mu})(\partial_{\mu}u^{\nu})]\partial_{\nu}\phi\\
...\\
[\partial,D_{t}^{N}]\phi&\simeq\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}C_{i}^{\nu}\partial_{\nu}(D_{t}^{i}\phi)\\
\end{split}

where
C_{i}^{\nu}:=C_{i}(\partial u,D_{t}u)
for
i\in\{0,...,N-1\}
are coefficients depending on the spatial and convective derivatives of
u
.

Remark 2.11
.

We also have an important commutator lemma which will be used in our higher order estimates.

Lemma 2.12

(Second Commutator Lemma)
.

Let
N\in\mathbb{N}
. For the convective derivative
D_{t}=u^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}
, generic
N
-th order spatial derivative
\partial^{N}
, and
\phi
sufficiently smooth, the following identities hold:

(2.13)

\begin{split}[D_{t},\partial]\phi&=-(\partial u^{\nu})\partial_{\nu}\phi\\
[D_{t},\partial^{2}]\phi&=-2(\partial u^{\nu})\partial_{\nu}(\partial\phi)-(\partial^{2}u^{\nu})\partial_{\nu}\phi\\
...\\
[D_{t},\partial^{N}]\phi&\simeq\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}B_{i}^{\nu}\partial_{\nu}(\partial^{i}\phi)\\
\end{split}

where
B_{i}^{\nu}:=B_{i}(\partial u)
for
i\in\{0,...,N-1\}
are coefficients depending on the spatial derivatives of
u
.

Let’s continue the book-keeping process for higher order convective derivatives

Lemma 2.13

(Book-keeping

derivatives)
.

The following simplifications hold when counting derivatives and powers of

. For

(2.14)

\begin{split}D_{t}\tilde{s}&\simeq\tilde{u}\\
D_{t}\tilde{r}&\simeq r\bm{\partial}\tilde{u}+\tilde{u}\\
D_{t}\tilde{u}&\simeq\bm{\partial}\tilde{r}+\tilde{u}+\tilde{s}\\
\end{split}

where we only ignored
\bm{\partial}s,\bm{\partial}r,\bm{\partial}u,s,u,
and
\mathsf{\Pi}

\mathcal{H}^{i}
critical or supercritical terms. Moreover, for

Proof.

Remark 2.15
.

In general, we have that

(2.18)

\begin{split}\partial_{t}\tilde{s}&\simeq\partial\tilde{s}+(\text{terms which are more subcritical than $\partial\tilde{s}$})\\

or

.
The point is that any time derivative of our linearized variables

3.2

6

\begin{split}\partial_{\mu}\tilde{s}&\simeq\partial\tilde{s}\\
\partial_{\mu}\tilde{r}&\simeq\partial\tilde{r}\\

from the point of view of their
\mathcal{H}^{2k}
order, as opposed to Remark

where
\bm{\partial}
would be used.

Recall that when referring to the

of a free boundary term, we are doing so with respect to Definition
2.5
and Remark
2.6
where terms with order
\mathcal{O}=0
are denoted as critical,
\mathcal{O}<0

\mathcal{O}>0
are subcritical.
For example, it is crucial in our expression (

that the parentheses do
not
contain terms of the form

.
This is because, at a given level say

is

(i.e. critical), but the order of a term like

is

(i.e. supercritical) with order strictly

with

since we would be introducing terms that require additional powers of

in the Appendix.

To finish this section, we prove a useful Lemma which expands on the notion of order
\mathcal{O}
to each of our key operations on free boundary terms. This Lemma is especially helpful in proving higher order analogs of our elliptic and div-curl estimates using induction. The last proposition in Lemma

is helpful in jumping from the base case
\mathcal{H}^{2}
to the general case
\mathcal{H}^{2k}
.

Lemma 2.16

(The orders of each operation)
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
be fixed. Suppose that
T
is a free boundary term with
\mathcal{H}^{2k}
order
\mathcal{O}
(i.e.
T
is of the form
r^{m}\partial^{l}(\tilde{s},\tilde{r},\tilde{u})
for some
m,l\in\mathbb{N}
,
m\geq 0,0\leq l\leq 2k
, ). Then the following holds:

(1)

the term
rT
has
\mathcal{H}^{2k}
order
\mathcal{O}+1

(2)

the term
\partial T
has
\mathcal{H}^{2k}
order
\mathcal{O}-1

(3)

the term
D_{t}T
has a minimum
\mathcal{H}^{2k}
order
\mathcal{O}-\frac{1}{2}

(4)

If
K\in\mathbb{N}
with
K\geq k
, then
T
has
H^{2K}
order
\mathcal{O}+K-k
.

Proof.

Since the case with
\tilde{s}
is identical to
\tilde{u}
, it suffices to consider the following cases for our free boundary term
T
:

(1)

T_{1}=r^{m_{1}}\partial^{l_{1}}\tilde{r}
which has
\mathcal{H}^{2k}
order
m_{1}-l_{1}+k=\mathcal{O}

(2)

T_{2}=r^{m_{2}}\partial^{l_{2}}\tilde{u}
which has
\mathcal{H}^{2k}
order
m_{2}-l_{2}+k-\frac{1}{2}=\mathcal{O}

For Part 1, observe that for cases (a) and (b), we simply have an extra power of
r

(2.19)

\begin{split}rT_{1}=r\left(r^{m_{1}}\partial^{l_{1}}\tilde{r}\right)=r^{m_{1}+1}\partial^{l_{1}}\tilde{r}&\implies rT_{1}\text{ has $\mathcal{H}^{2k}$ order $m_{1}+1-l_{1}+k=\mathcal{O}+1$}\\
rT_{2}=r\left(r^{m_{2}}\partial^{l_{2}}\tilde{u}\right)=r^{m_{2}+1}\partial^{l_{2}}\tilde{u}&\implies rT_{2}\text{ has $\mathcal{H}^{2k}$ order $m+1-l+k-\frac{1}{2}=\mathcal{O}+1$}\\
\end{split}

For Part 2, we see that there are two sub-cases when
m_{1},m_{2}\geq 1
and
m_{1}=0=m_{2}
. If
m_{1}=0=m_{2}
, then

(2.20)

\begin{split}\partial T_{1}&=\partial(\partial^{l_{1}}\tilde{r})=\partial^{l_{1}+1}\tilde{r}\implies\partial T_{1}\text{ has $\mathcal{H}^{2k}$ order $m_{1}-(l_{1}+1)+k=\mathcal{O}-1$}\\
\partial T_{2}&=\partial(\partial^{l_{2}}\tilde{u})=\partial^{l_{2}+1}\tilde{u}\implies\partial T_{2}\text{ has $\mathcal{H}^{2k}$ order $m_{2}-(l_{2}+1)+k-\frac{1}{2}=\mathcal{O}-1$}\\
\end{split}

If
m_{1},m_{2}\geq 1
, then we get

(2.21)

\begin{split}\partial T_{1}&=\partial(r^{m_{1}}\partial^{l_{1}}\tilde{r})=m_{1}r^{m_{1}-1}\partial^{l_{1}}\tilde{r}+r^{m_{1}}\partial^{l_{1}+1}\tilde{r}\implies\partial T_{1}\text{ has $\mathcal{H}^{2k}$ order $\mathcal{O}-1$}\\
\partial T_{2}&=\partial(r^{m_{2}}\partial^{l_{2}}\tilde{u})=m_{2}r^{m_{2}-1}\partial^{l_{2}}\tilde{u}+r^{m_{2}}\partial^{l_{2}+1}\tilde{u}\implies\partial T_{2}\text{ has $\mathcal{H}^{2k}$ order $\mathcal{O}-1$}\\
\end{split}

since both terms have
\mathcal{H}^{2k}
order
\mathcal{O}-1
, noting that we either lose a power of
r
or gain a derivative (and both operations decrease the order from
\mathcal{O}
to
\mathcal{O}-1
).

For Part 3, we will first consider when
l_{1}=0=l_{2}
. For case with
T_{1}
, we have

(2.22)

\begin{split}D_{t}T_{1}=D_{t}(r^{m_{1}}\tilde{r})&=m_{1}r^{m_{1}-1}(D_{t}r)\tilde{r}+r^{m_{1}}D_{t}\tilde{r}\\

2.13

\tilde{u}

\tilde{r}

\mathcal{O}-\frac{1}{2}

\mathcal{O}
. Thus,
D_{t}T_{1}
has a minimum
\mathcal{H}^{2k}
order
\mathcal{O}-\frac{1}{2}
(i.e. it has order
\mathcal{O}-\frac{1}{2}
at worst). For
T_{2}
, we get a similar computation using (

) and Lemma
2.13

where the first term has order

\mathcal{H}^{2k}
level. This completes the proof of Part 3.

Lastly, we will provide the proof of Part 4. In the previous parts, each operation changed the number of powers of
r
, the number of derivatives, or whether we are using
\tilde{r}
or
\tilde{u}
and each of these has an effect on the computed order. In Part 4, we are only seeing what happens if we raise the level from
k
to some

, and the proof can be seen by noticing that for
T_{1}
,

m_{1}-l_{1}+K=(m_{1}-l_{1}+k)+K-k=\mathcal{O}+K-k

and for
T_{2}
,

m_{2}-l_{2}+K-\frac{1}{2}=(m_{2}-l_{2}+k-\frac{1}{2})+K-k=\mathcal{O}+K-k.

This completes the proof of Part 4, and the proof of Lemma
2.16
is complete.
∎

Remark 2.17

Proposition 3.1

(Entropy Estimates)
.

(3.1)

\begin{split}D_{t}(\partial^{2k}\tilde{s})&=[D_{t},\partial^{2k}]\tilde{s}+\partial^{2k}f\\

where we recall the definition of

\begin{split}\frac{1}{2}r^{2k+\frac{1}{\gamma-1}}D_{t}(|\partial^{2k}\tilde{s}|^{2})&\simeq r^{2k+\frac{1}{\gamma-1}}\partial^{2k}\tilde{s}\left(\sum_{i=0}^{2k}B_{i}\partial^{i}\tilde{s}\right)+r^{2k+\frac{1}{\gamma-1}}\partial^{2k}\tilde{s}\partial^{2k}(\tilde{u}^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}s)\\

3.2.
Vorticity estimates

We also plan to derive similar estimates for the vorticity which will be used in connection with the elliptic estimates. We recall from
[
2
]
that the relativistic vorticity is given by the following

(3.6)

\omega_{\alpha\beta}=\partial_{\alpha}v_{\beta}-\partial_{\beta}v_{\alpha}.

where

is the enthalpy current defined by

(3.7)

v_{\alpha}=hu_{\alpha},\hskip 17.07164pth=\frac{\Gamma+r}{\Gamma},\hskip 17.07164pt\Gamma=\Gamma(s)=\frac{(\gamma-1)^{\frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma}}}{\gamma}e^{-\frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma}s}

Similar to
[

, we get an expression for

-v^{\alpha}\partial_{\beta}v_{\alpha}=h\partial_{\beta}h.

From (
1.35c
) rewritten in terms of

, we get

(3.9)

\frac{1}{h^{2}}v^{\alpha}\partial_{\alpha}v_{\beta}=-\frac{1}{\Gamma+r}\partial_{\beta}r

Then, we can compute the following expression

(3.10)

\begin{split}\frac{1}{h}\left(u^{\alpha}\partial_{\alpha}v_{\beta}-u^{\alpha}\partial_{\beta}v_{\alpha}\right)&=\frac{r}{(\Gamma+r)}\frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma}\partial_{\beta}s\\
\end{split}

Multiplying by
h
, we get

(3.11)

u^{\alpha}\omega_{\alpha\beta}=\frac{r}{\gamma\Gamma}(\gamma-1)\partial_{\beta}s

which agrees with
[
15
]
as

(3.12)

\varepsilon=\frac{r}{\gamma\Gamma}

after checking (
1.24
). To find the evolution equation satisfied by
\omega_{\alpha\beta}
, we can first compute that

\partial_{\alpha}\varepsilon=\frac{1}{\gamma\Gamma}\left(\partial_{\alpha}r+\frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma}r\partial_{\alpha}s\right).

Using similar computations as
[
15
]
and applying (
3.12
), we have

(3.13)

\begin{split}u^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}\omega_{\alpha\beta}+\partial_{\alpha}u^{\mu}\omega_{\mu\beta}+\partial_{\beta}u^{\mu}\omega_{\alpha\mu}&=\frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma\Gamma}(\partial_{\alpha}r\partial_{\beta}s-\partial_{\beta}r\partial_{\alpha}s).\end{split}

which is the evolution equation for the vorticity
\omega_{\alpha\beta}
in terms of the unknowns
(s,r,u)
.

Next, we introduce the full linearized voricity
\tilde{\omega}_{\alpha\beta}

(3.14)

\begin{split}\tilde{\omega}_{\alpha\beta}&=\partial_{\alpha}(\widetilde{hu_{\beta}})-\partial_{\beta}(\widetilde{hu_{\alpha}})\\

are more readily available in view of (

better resembles

Throughout this section, we will use the following facts similar to the bookkeeping system from Lemma
2.13

\begin{split}\overline{\omega}_{\alpha\beta}&\simeq\partial\tilde{h}+\tilde{h}\\

.

For the

\partial^{l}(\tilde{u}^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}\omega_{\alpha\beta})

, and

through

\begin{split}|R_{4}|&\lesssim\|\partial^{2k+1}(u,r,s)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{t})}\int_{\Omega_{t}}r^{\frac{2-\gamma}{\gamma-1}}|(r^{k+\frac{1}{2}}\partial^{l}\tilde{\omega})(r^{k+\frac{1}{2}}\partial^{l}\tilde{u})|\hskip 2.84526ptdx\\
&\lesssim\mathcal{C}_{4}\|(\tilde{s},\tilde{r},\tilde{u})\|_{\mathcal{H}^{2k}}^{2}\end{split}

For the
R_{5}
and
R_{6}
integrals, the argument is quite similar since we get up to
2k
derivatives of
\tilde{u}
and up to
2k
derivatives of
\omega
.

For the

, integral, we will have terms containing up to

derivatives of
\tilde{s}
and
2k
derivatives of

and

Finally, for the

\tilde{s}
and
\tilde{r}

2k
derivatives of

and

at worst (and in fact, terms with

Thus, by estimating each of the terms in (

and

\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega_{t}}r^{\frac{2-\gamma}{\gamma-1}}|r^{k+\frac{1}{2}}\partial^{l}\tilde{\omega}|_{G}^{2}\hskip 2.84526ptdx\lesssim\mathcal{C}\|(\tilde{s},\tilde{r},\tilde{u})\|_{\mathcal{H}^{2k}}^{2}

where

depends on up to
2k+1
derivatives of

and

. Summing over the multiindex

∎

energy has an extra
r^{1/2}
weight and this will be perfect for applying the same smallness arguments for these extra terms.

4.4.
Commutators with weighted spatial derivatives

Our goal is to extend Lemmas
4.1
and
4.3
to higher order Sobolev spaces. To do so, we will need to compute commutators between our good elliptic operators
\tilde{L}_{1},\tilde{L}_{2}
,
\tilde{L}_{3}
, and the weighted spatial derivatives
r^{k-j}\partial^{2(k-j)}
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
be fixed, and
j\in\mathbb{N}
with
1\leq j\leq k
. To begin, we will use the following notation:

(4.56)

\begin{split}m&:=k-j\\
L^{a,b}&:=r^{a}\partial^{b}\\
\tilde{r}_{2j}&:=D_{t}^{2j}\tilde{r}\end{split}

and we recall from (
4.10
) that

\tilde{L}_{1}=\frac{\gamma-1}{\Gamma+r}H^{ij}(r\partial_{i}\partial_{j}+\frac{1}{\gamma-1}\partial_{i}r\partial_{j})

We plan to handle
\tilde{L}_{1}
first, before moving on to
\tilde{L}_{2}
and
\tilde{L}_{3}
. By Lemma
4.5
, we have that

(4.57)

\tilde{r}_{2j}\simeq\tilde{L}_{1}\tilde{r}_{2j-2}+P

where
P
are terms that have an order of
1/2
or better at level
j
(i.e. at the
\mathcal{H}^{2j}
level). We plan to ultimately handle these terms using smallness arguments after we apply
L^{m,2m}
to (
4.57
) and analyze with the
H^{0,\frac{2-\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}}
norm.
Applying
L^{m,2m}
to both sides (where
L^{m,2m}
is defined in (
4.56
)), we have

(4.58)

L^{m,2m}\tilde{r}_{2j}\simeq\tilde{L}_{1}L^{m,2m}\tilde{r}_{2j-2}+[L^{m,2m},\tilde{L}_{1}]\tilde{r}_{2j-2}+L^{m,2m}P

Remark 4.7
.

Observe what will happen to the order of the terms in
P
when we apply
L^{m,2m}
. At the start,
P
has terms with
\mathcal{H}^{2j}
order
\frac{1}{2}
(at worst). However, by repeatedly applying Lemma
2.16
, we see that
\partial^{2m}P
has
\mathcal{H}^{2j}
-order
\frac{1}{2}-2m
. Then,
r^{m}\partial^{2m}P
has
\mathcal{H}^{2j}
-order
\frac{1}{2}-2m+m=\frac{1}{2}-m=\frac{1}{2}-(k-j)
. Then, if we want to calculate the order at level
k
instead of level
j
(and note that
k\geq j
), we see by Part 4 in Lemma
2.16
that
r^{m}\partial^{2m}P
must have
\mathcal{H}^{2k}
-order
\frac{1}{2}-(k-j)+(k-j)=\frac{1}{2}
.

By Remark
4.7
, this means that at the
\mathcal{H}^{2k}
level,
L^{m,2m}P
will still be subcritical and we plan to handle these terms with smallness arguments. Thus, let us make use of the following notation:

(4.59)

P_{\mathcal{O}\geq 1/2}:=\left(\text{terms with $\mathcal{H}^{2k}$ order $\mathcal{O}\geq\frac{1}{2}$}\right)

which we will use to collect any perturbative/smallness terms along the way that can handled easily with respect to the
\mathcal{H}^{2k}
norm. Using this notation, we have

(4.60)

L^{m,2m}\tilde{r}_{2j}\simeq\tilde{L}_{1}L^{m,2m}\tilde{r}_{2j-2}+[L^{m,2m},\tilde{L}_{1}]\tilde{r}_{2j-2}+P_{\mathcal{O}\geq 1/2}

and the bulk of our analysis concerns proper handling of the commutator
[L^{m,2m},\tilde{L}_{1}]\tilde{r}_{2j-2}
.

Since the commutator is trivial for
m=0
, let’s assume that
m\geq 1
, and we plan to apply an induction argument on
m
. As we will see in the coming pages, the critical terms in the commutator need to be handled in a particular way where we absorb a critical term into the elliptic operator
\tilde{L}_{1}
, and then the rest of the terms are subcritical. Note that if we had arrived at (
4.60
) with
L^{m-1,2(m-2)}
instead of
L^{m,2m}
, this would produce

(4.61)

L^{m-1,2(m-1)}\tilde{r}_{2j}\simeq\tilde{L}_{1}L^{m-1,2(m-1)}\tilde{r}_{2j-2}+[L^{m-1,2(m-1)},\tilde{L}_{1}]\tilde{r}_{2j-2}+P_{\mathcal{O}\geq 3/2}

where the other terms, at worst, have
\mathcal{H}^{2k}
-order 1 (instead of critical in the
m
case).
For the inductive hypothesis, we need to assume that
L^{m-1,2(m-1)}
“behaves well” when we commute it with
\tilde{L}_{1}
in that we have already absorbed the
order 1 terms.
We will make the following inductive assumption (recalling from (
4.56
) that
k=m+j
):

Assumption 4.8

(Inductive Hypothesis on
m-1
)
.

(1)

The terms in
[L^{m-1,2(m-1)},\tilde{L}_{1}]\tilde{r}_{2j-2}
all have
\mathcal{H}^{2(m-1+j)}
order
\frac{1}{2}
, except for key order 0 terms which have already been handled by absorbing into
\tilde{L}_{1}L^{m-1,2(m-1)}\tilde{r}_{2j-2}
with the analog of Proposition
4.10
below.

(2)

The above assumption also holds for
\tilde{L}_{1}
replaced by
\tilde{L}_{1}+b\frac{\gamma-1}{\Gamma+r}H^{ij}\partial_{i}r\partial_{j}
with
b\geq 0
.

Remark 4.9
.

There are several consequences of Assumption
4.8
that we summarize:

(1)

The terms in
[L^{m-1,2(m-1)},\tilde{L}_{1}]\tilde{r}_{2j-2}
have
\mathcal{H}^{2(m+j)}=\mathcal{H}^{2k}
order
\frac{3}{2}
, i.e. they belong to
P_{\mathcal{O}\geq 3/2}

(2)

The terms in
[L^{m-1,2(m-1)},\tilde{L}_{1}]\partial\tilde{r}_{2j-2}
belong to
P_{\mathcal{O}\geq 1/2}
.

(3)

Suppose that
m\geq 2
. We claim that Assumption
4.8
on the
m-1
case also provides information on lower order commutators. Indeed, we compute the following using two different ways:

(4.62)

\begin{split}[r\partial\left(L^{m-2,2m-3}\right),\tilde{L_{1}}]\varphi&\simeq[L^{m-1,2m-2}+L^{m-2,2m-3},\tilde{L}_{1}]\varphi=[L^{m-1,2m-2},\tilde{L}_{1}]\varphi+[L^{m-2,2m-3},\tilde{L}_{1}]\varphi\\
[r\partial\left(L^{m-2,2m-3}\right),\tilde{L_{1}}]\varphi&=r\partial[L^{m-2,2m-3},\tilde{L}_{1}]\varphi+[r\partial,\tilde{L}_{1}]L^{m-2,2m-3}\varphi\end{split}

Combining, we have

(4.63)

[L^{m-1,2m-2},\tilde{L}_{1}]\varphi\simeq r\partial[L^{m-2,2m-3},\tilde{L}_{1}]\varphi+[r\partial,\tilde{L}_{1}]L^{m-2,2m-3}\varphi+[L^{m-2,2m-3},\tilde{L}_{1}]\varphi.

Thus, since we know that
[L^{m-1,2(m-1)},\tilde{L}_{1}]\tilde{r}_{2j-2}
belongs to
P_{\mathcal{O}\geq 3/2}
and
[L^{m-1,2(m-1)},\tilde{L}_{1}]\partial\tilde{r}_{2j-2}
belongs to
P_{\mathcal{O}\geq 1/2}
, we can also conclude that
[L^{m-2,2m-3},\tilde{L}_{1}]\tilde{r}_{2j-2}
belongs to
P_{\mathcal{O}\geq 3/2}
and
[L^{m-2,2m-3},\tilde{L}_{1}]\partial\tilde{r}_{2j-2}
belongs to
P_{\mathcal{O}\geq 1/2}
. By repeated use of this argument, a similar statement also holds for commutators involving
L^{l,m+l-1}
where
1\leq l\leq m-1
.

Now that we have finished discussing the consequences of our inductive hypothesis, we proceed with the commutator by computing the following

(4.64)

[L^{m,2m},\tilde{L}_{1}]\tilde{r}_{2j-2}=L^{m,2m}\tilde{L}_{1}\tilde{r}_{2j-2}-\tilde{L}_{1}L^{m,2m}\tilde{r}_{2j-2}

and

(4.65)

\begin{split}L^{m,2m}\tilde{L}_{1}\tilde{r}_{2j-2}&=r^{m}\partial^{2m}\left(\frac{\gamma-1}{\Gamma+r}H^{ij}(r\partial_{i}\partial_{j}\tilde{r}_{2j-2}+\frac{1}{\gamma-1}\partial_{i}r\partial_{j}\tilde{r}_{2j-2})\right)\\
&\simeq\frac{\gamma-1}{\Gamma+r}H^{ij}r^{m}\partial^{2m}\left(r\partial_{i}\partial_{j}\tilde{r}_{2j-2}+\frac{1}{\gamma-1}\partial_{i}r\partial_{j}\tilde{r}_{2j-2}\right)+P_{\mathcal{O}\geq 1/2}\\
\end{split}

where we note that any terms where derivatives hit
\frac{\gamma-1}{\Gamma+r}H^{ij}
get absorbed into
P_{\mathcal{O}\geq 1/2}
(since these derivatives are not hitting either
r
or
\tilde{r}_{2j-2}
which are critical terms at worst). For analyzing the remaining terms, we continue

(4.66)

\begin{split}r^{m}\partial^{2m}\left(r\partial_{i}\partial_{j}\tilde{r}_{2j-2}+\frac{1}{\gamma-1}\partial_{i}r\partial_{j}\tilde{r}_{2j-2}\right)&\simeq r^{m}\sum_{l=0}^{2m}\partial^{l}r\partial^{2m-l}\partial_{i}\partial_{j}\tilde{r}_{2j-2}+\frac{1}{\gamma-1}\partial^{l}\partial_{i}r\partial^{2m-l}\partial_{j}\tilde{r}_{2j-2}\end{split}

where we absorbed combinatorial constants with
\simeq
. Now, we plan to separate out any of the terms that belong to
P_{\mathcal{O}\geq 1/2}
from the critical terms which must be handled. When
\partial^{2m}
hits
\tilde{L}_{1}
, we observe that the worst terms only occur when
2m
derivatives hit
\tilde{r}_{2j-2}
, or
1
derivative hits
r
(in the first term) and
2m-1
derivatives hit
\tilde{r}_{2j-2}
. The point is that after the first power of
r
is removed, any subsequent derivatives that hit
\partial r
do not actually contribute to the order. In fact, they are only taking away derivatives that could have hit
\tilde{r}_{2j-2}
. Indeed, we can check by computing the order. When
l=0
, we have that

(4.67)

\begin{split}r^{m}r\partial^{2m}\partial_{i}\partial_{j}\tilde{r}_{2j-2}&\simeq r^{m+1}\partial^{2m+2}(D_{t}^{2j-2}\tilde{r})\text{ is $\mathcal{H}^{2k}$-critical ($\mathcal{O}=0$)}\\
\frac{1}{\gamma-1}r^{m}\partial_{i}r\partial^{2m}\partial_{j}\tilde{r}_{2j-2}&\simeq r^{m}\partial^{2m+1}(D_{t}^{2j-2}\tilde{r})\text{ is $\mathcal{H}^{2k}$-critical ($\mathcal{O}=0$)}\end{split}

using Lemma
2.16
.
When
l=1
, we see that

(4.68)

\begin{split}r^{m}\partial r\partial^{2m-1}\partial_{i}\partial_{j}\tilde{r}_{2j-2}&\simeq r^{m}\partial^{2m+1}(D_{t}^{2j-2}\tilde{r})\text{ is $\mathcal{H}^{2k}$-critical ($\mathcal{O}=0$)}\\
\frac{1}{\gamma-1}r^{m}\partial\partial_{i}r\partial^{2m-1}\partial_{j}\tilde{r}_{2j-2}&\simeq r^{m}\partial^{2m}(D_{t}^{2j-2}\tilde{r})\text{ is $\mathcal{H}^{2k}$-subcritical ($\mathcal{O}=1$)}\end{split}

Thus, in the summation when
l\geq 2
, we will get

(4.69)

\begin{split}r^{m}\partial^{l}r\partial^{2m-l}\partial_{i}\partial_{j}\tilde{r}_{2j-2}&\simeq r^{m}\partial^{\leq 2m}(D_{t}^{2j-2}\tilde{r})\text{ is $\mathcal{H}^{2k}$-subcritical ($\mathcal{O}\geq 1$)}\\
\frac{1}{\gamma-1}r^{m}\partial^{l}\partial_{i}r\partial^{\leq 2m-l}\partial_{j}\tilde{r}_{2j-2}&\simeq r^{m}\partial^{\leq 2m-1}(D_{t}^{2j-2}\tilde{r})\text{ is $\mathcal{H}^{2k}$-subcritical ($\mathcal{O}\geq 2$)}\end{split}

Summarizing our order analysis, we see that many of the terms in the summation will be absorbed into
P_{\mathcal{O}\geq 1/2}
, and we have:

(4.70)

\begin{split}L^{m,2m}\tilde{L}_{1}\tilde{r}_{2j-2}&\simeq\frac{\gamma-1}{\Gamma+r}H^{ij}\left({\color[rgb]{0,0,1}r^{m+1}\partial^{2m}\partial_{i}\partial_{j}\tilde{r}_{2j-2}}+{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\frac{1}{\gamma-1}r^{m}\partial_{i}r\partial^{2m}\partial_{j}\tilde{r}_{2j-2}}+r^{m}\partial r\partial^{2m-1}\partial_{i}\partial_{j}\tilde{r}_{2j-2}\right)\\
&\hskip 19.91692pt+P_{\mathcal{O}\geq 1/2}\end{split}

For the other side of the commutator, we have

(4.71)

\begin{split}\tilde{L}_{1}\left(L^{m,2m}\tilde{r}_{2j-2}\right)&\simeq\frac{\gamma-1}{\Gamma+r}H^{ij}\left(r\partial_{i}\partial_{j}(r^{m}\partial^{2m}\tilde{r}_{2j-2})+\frac{1}{\gamma-1}\partial_{i}r\partial_{j}(r^{m}\partial^{2m}\tilde{r}_{2j-2})\right)\\
&\simeq\frac{\gamma-1}{\Gamma+r}\big(H^{ij}\left({\color[rgb]{0,0,1}r^{m+1}\partial_{i}\partial_{j}\partial^{2m}\tilde{r}_{2j-2}}+2r\partial_{i}(r^{m})\partial_{j}\partial^{2m}\tilde{r}_{2j-2}+{\color[rgb]{0.75,0,0.25}r\partial_{i}\partial_{j}(r^{m})\partial^{2m}\tilde{r}_{2j-2}}\right)\\
&\hskip 14.22636pt+\frac{1}{\Gamma+r}H^{ij}\partial_{i}r\left({\color[rgb]{0,0,1}r^{m}\partial_{j}\partial^{2m}\tilde{r}_{2j-2}}+\partial_{j}(r^{m})\partial^{2m}\tilde{r}_{2j-2}\big)\right).\end{split}

Now, if we compute the order of the
purple
term, we see that

\frac{\gamma-1}{\Gamma+r}H^{ij}r\partial_{i}\partial_{j}(r^{m})\partial^{2m}\tilde{r}_{2j-2}\simeq r^{m-1}\partial^{2m}\tilde{r}_{2j-2}\text{ is $\mathcal{H}^{2k}$-subcritical ($\mathcal{O}\geq 2$)}

and thus, we can absorb it into
P_{\mathcal{O}\geq 1/2}
. Observing that the
blue
terms cancel in (
4.70
) and (
4.71
), we have

(4.72)

\begin{split}[L^{m,2m},\tilde{L}_{1}]\tilde{r}_{2j-2}&=\frac{\gamma-1}{\Gamma+r}H^{ij}\left(\partial rL^{m,2m-1}\partial_{i}\partial_{j}\tilde{r}_{2j-2}-2m\partial_{i}rL^{m,2m}\partial_{j}\tilde{r}_{2j-2}-\frac{1}{\gamma-1}\partial_{i}r\partial_{j}rL^{m-1,2m}\tilde{r}_{2j-2}\right)\\
&\hskip 19.91692pt+P_{\mathcal{O}\geq 1/2}\\
\end{split}

where the first two terms take the form
L^{m,2m+1}\tilde{r}_{2j-2}
.

Then, combining with (
4.60
), and applying the
H^{0,\frac{2-\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}}
norm, we have

(4.73)

\begin{split}\|\tilde{L}_{1}L^{m,2m}\tilde{r}_{2j-2}\|_{H^{0,\frac{2-\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}}}&\lesssim\|L^{m,2m}\tilde{r}_{2j}\|_{H^{0,\frac{2-\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}}}+\|L^{m,2m+1}\tilde{r}_{2j-2}\|_{H^{0,\frac{2-\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}}}\\
&\hskip 14.22636pt+\|L^{m-1,2m}\tilde{r}_{2j-2}\|_{H^{0,\frac{2-\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}}}+\|P_{\mathcal{O}\geq 1/2}\|_{H^{0,\frac{2-\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}}}\\
\end{split}

For the LHS, we can apply Lemma
4.1
to get

(4.74)

\|\tilde{L}_{1}L^{m,2m}\tilde{r}_{2j-2}\|_{H^{0,\frac{2-\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}}}\gtrsim\|L^{m,2m}\tilde{r}_{2j-2}\|_{H^{2,1+\frac{2-\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}}}-{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\|L^{m,2m}\tilde{r}_{2j-2}\|_{H^{0,\frac{2-\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}}}}

Then, adding the
blue
term to the other side of (
4.73
), we have

(4.75)

\begin{split}\|L^{m,2m}\tilde{r}_{2j-2}\|_{H^{2,1+\frac{2-\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}}}&\lesssim\|L^{m,2m}\tilde{r}_{2j}\|_{H^{0,\frac{2-\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}}}+\|L^{m,2m+1}\tilde{r}_{2j-2}\|_{H^{0,\frac{2-\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}}}\\
&\hskip 14.22636pt+\|L^{m-1,2m}\tilde{r}_{2j-2}\|_{H^{0,\frac{2-\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}}}+{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\|L^{m,2m}\tilde{r}_{2j-2}\|_{H^{0,\frac{2-\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}}}}\\
&\hskip 14.22636pt+\|P_{\mathcal{O}\geq 1/2}\|_{H^{0,\frac{2-\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}}}\\
\end{split}

Now, by adding the following quantity on both sides of our inequality:

(4.76)

\|\tilde{r}_{2j-2}\|_{H^{0,\frac{2-\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}}}+\sum_{l=0}^{m-1}\|L^{l,m+l}\tilde{r}_{2j-2}\|_{H^{2,1+\frac{2-\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}}}

we see using the help or Remark
1.14
that the LHS will be equivalent to

(4.77)

\|\tilde{r}_{2j-2}\|_{H^{2m+2,m+1+\frac{2-\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}}}

For the RHS, we will get the following collection of terms which we number

(4.78)

\begin{split}&\|L^{m,2m}\tilde{r}_{2j}\|_{H^{0,\frac{2-\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}}}+\|L^{m,2m+1}\tilde{r}_{2j-2}\|_{H^{0,\frac{2-\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}}}\\
&\hskip 14.22636pt+\|L^{m-1,2m}\tilde{r}_{2j-2}\|_{H^{0,\frac{2-\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}}}+{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\|L^{m,2m}\tilde{r}_{2j-2}\|_{H^{0,\frac{2-\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}}}}\\
&\hskip 14.22636pt\sum_{l=0}^{m-1}\|L^{l,m+l}\tilde{r}_{2j-2}\|_{H^{2,1+\frac{2-\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}}}+\|\tilde{r}_{2j-2}\|_{H^{0,\frac{2-\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}}}+\|P_{\mathcal{O}\geq 1/2}\|_{H^{0,\frac{2-\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}}}\\
&:=\|L^{m,2m}\tilde{r}_{2j}\|_{H^{0,\frac{2-\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}}}+\sum_{a=0}^{6}R_{6}\end{split}

using
R_{1}
through
R_{6}
.

For the terms
R_{1},R_{2},
and
R_{3}
, we first observe that

(4.79)

\begin{split}\|L^{m,2m+1}\tilde{r}_{2j-2}\|_{H^{0,\frac{2-\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}}}&+\|L^{m-1,2m}\tilde{r}_{2j-2}\|_{H^{0,\frac{2-\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}}}\\
&+{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\|L^{m,2m}\tilde{r}_{2j-2}\|_{H^{0,\frac{2-\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}}}}\lesssim\|L^{m-1,2m-1}\tilde{r}_{2j-2}\|_{H^{2,1+\frac{2-\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}}}\\
&\hskip 116.65646pt=\|L^{m-1,2m-2}\partial\tilde{r}_{2j-2}\|_{H^{2,1+\frac{2-\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}}}\\
\end{split}

Then, we claim that the following inequality holds:

Proposition 4.10
.

(4.80)

\begin{split}\|L^{m-1,2m-2}\partial\tilde{r}_{2j-2}\|_{H^{2,1+\frac{2-\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}}}&\lesssim\|L^{m-1,2m-2}\partial\tilde{r}_{2j}\|_{H^{0,\frac{2-\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}}}\\
&\hskip 14.22636pt+\|L^{m-1,2m-2}\partial\tilde{r}_{2j-2}\|_{H^{0,\frac{2-\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}}}+\|P_{\mathcal{O}\geq 1/2}\|_{H^{0,\frac{2-\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}}}\end{split}

Proof.

To prove this proposition, we rely on the smallness of
\partial_{3}r
(similar to the work in
[
4
]
) as well as a key step in which we we must absorb a term into
\tilde{L}_{1}
, creating the updated elliptic operator
\widehat{\tilde{L}_{1}}
. Similar to
[
4
]
, we rely on localizing in the neighborhood of a boundary point such that

(4.81)

|\partial^{\prime}r|\lesssim A,\hskip 14.22636pt|\partial_{3}r-1|\lesssim A,\hskip 14.22636pt|H^{3,i^{\prime}}|\lesssim A

where
A\ll 1
is small constant, and primed indices will range over
x^{1}
and
x^{2}
(i.e.
\partial^{\prime}r\simeq\partial_{1}r,\partial_{2}r
). Note that a key assumption is present on the off-diagonal components of
H
(and a similar simplification can also be found in
[
4
]
). The proof is quite similar, and we only need to show the related inequalities

(4.82)

\begin{split}\|L^{m-1,2m-2}\partial_{3}\tilde{r}_{2j-2}\|_{H^{2,1+\frac{2-\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}}}&\lesssim\|L^{m-1,2m-2}\partial_{3}\tilde{r}_{2j}\|_{H^{0,\frac{2-\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}}}+\|L^{m-1,2m-2}\partial^{\prime}\tilde{r}_{2j-2}\|_{H^{2,1+\frac{2-\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}}}\\
&\hskip 14.22636pt+\|P_{\mathcal{O}\geq 1/2}\|_{H^{0,\frac{2-\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}}}\end{split}

and

(4.83)

\begin{split}\|L^{m-1,2m-2}\partial^{\prime}\tilde{r}_{2j-2}\|_{H^{2,1+\frac{2-\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}}}&\lesssim\|L^{m-1,2m-2}\partial^{\prime}\tilde{r}_{2j}\|_{H^{0,\frac{2-\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}}}\\
&\hskip 14.22636pt+{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\|L^{m-1,2m-2}\partial^{\prime}\tilde{r}_{2j-2}\|_{H^{0,\frac{2-\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}}}}+\|P_{\mathcal{O}\geq 1/2}\|_{H^{0,\frac{2-\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}}}\end{split}

∎

Corollary 4.11

(Corollary for Proposition
4.10
)
.

Let
l\in\mathbb{N}
with
1\leq l\leq m-1
. The following inequality also holds

(4.84)

\begin{split}\|L^{l,m+l-1}\partial\tilde{r}_{2j-2}\|_{H^{2,1+\frac{2-\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}}}&\lesssim\|L^{l,m+l-1}\partial\tilde{r}_{2j}\|_{H^{0,\frac{2-\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}}}\\
&\hskip 14.22636pt+\|L^{l,m+l-1}\partial\tilde{r}_{2j-2}\|_{H^{0,\frac{2-\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}}}+\|P_{\mathcal{O}\geq 1/2}\|_{H^{0,\frac{2-\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}}}\end{split}

Proof.

To prove this corollary, we simply need to follow the proof of Proposition
4.10
combined with the last consequence in Remark
4.9
. Anywhere the inductive hypothesis is used on the commutator
L^{m-1,2m-2}
, we know that a similar fact holds for
L^{l,m+l-1}
by Remark
4.9
.
∎

Now, we can return to (
4.73
) - (
4.78
) and summarize our results for what we have on the LHS and RHS now that we have estimated the terms
R_{1},R_{2}
, and
R_{3}
using Proposition
4.10
. Simplifying
L^{m-1,2m-2}\partial=L^{m-1,2m-1}
, this yields

(4.85)

\begin{split}\|\tilde{r}_{2j-2}\|_{H^{2m+2,m+1+\frac{2-\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}}}&\lesssim\|L^{m,2m}\tilde{r}_{2j}\|_{H^{0,\frac{2-\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}}}+\|L^{m-1,2m-1}\tilde{r}_{2j}\|_{H^{0,\frac{2-\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}}}\\
&\hskip 14.22636pt+{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\|L^{m-1,2m-1}\tilde{r}_{2j-2}\|_{H^{0,\frac{2-\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}}}}+{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\sum_{l=0}^{m-1}\|L^{l,m+l}\tilde{r}_{2j-2}\|_{H^{2,1+\frac{2-\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}}}}\\
&\hskip 14.22636pt+\|\tilde{r}_{2j-2}\|_{H^{0,\frac{2-\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}}}+\|P_{\mathcal{O}\geq 1/2}\|_{H^{0,\frac{2-\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}}}\end{split}

where only the
red
terms need to be handled, and we will be keeping
R_{5}
and
R_{6}
from before on the RHS. For the first red term, we have

(4.86)

\begin{split}{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\|L^{m-1,2m-1}\tilde{r}_{2j-2}\|_{H^{0,\frac{2-\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}}}}&\lesssim\|L^{m-1,2m-2}\tilde{r}_{2j-2}\|_{H^{1,\frac{2-\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}}}\\
&\lesssim\|\tilde{L}_{1}(L^{m-1,2m-2}\tilde{r}_{2j-2})\|_{H^{0,\frac{2-\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}}}\end{split}

where we used the elliptic estimate for
\tilde{L}_{1}
in the last line. Then, we can use (
4.8
) to commute with
L^{m-1,2(m-1)}
which will only produce additional terms in
P_{\mathcal{O}\geq 1/2}
. Finally, we use (
4.57
) to express
\tilde{L}_{1}\tilde{r}_{2j-2}
in terms of
\tilde{r}_{2j}
plus additional
P
terms. We have

(4.87)

\begin{split}{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\|L^{m-1,2m-1}\tilde{r}_{2j-2}\|_{H^{0,\frac{2-\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}}}}&\lesssim\|L^{m-1,2m-2}(\tilde{L}_{1}\tilde{r}_{2j-2})+P_{\mathcal{O}\geq 1/2}\|_{H^{0,\frac{2-\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}}}\\
&\lesssim\|L^{m-1,2m-2}(\tilde{r}_{2j}+P)\|_{H^{0,\frac{2-\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}}}+\|P_{\mathcal{O}\geq 1/2}\|_{H^{0,\frac{2-\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}}}\\
&\lesssim\|L^{m-1,2m-2}\tilde{r}_{2j}\|_{H^{0,\frac{2-\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}}}+\|P_{\mathcal{O}\geq 1/2}\|_{H^{0,\frac{2-\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}}}\\
&\lesssim\|\tilde{r}_{2j}\|_{H^{2m,m+\frac{2-\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}}}+\|P_{\mathcal{O}\geq 1/2}\|_{H^{0,\frac{2-\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}}}\\
\end{split}

which is an important estimate for the first red term.

For the final
red
term, we start by rewriting and then applying Corollary
4.11
.

(4.88)

\begin{split}{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\sum_{l=0}^{m-1}\|L^{l,m+l-1}\partial\tilde{r}_{2j-2}\|_{H^{2,1+\frac{2-\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}}}}&\lesssim\sum_{l=0}^{m-1}\left(\|L^{l,m+l-1}\partial\tilde{r}_{2j}\|_{H^{0,\frac{2-\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}}}+\|L^{l,m+l-1}\partial\tilde{r}_{2j-2}\|_{H^{0,\frac{2-\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}}}\right)\\
&\lesssim\sum_{l=0}^{m-1}\left(\|L^{l,m+l}\tilde{r}_{2j}\|_{H^{0,\frac{2-\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}}}+\|L^{l,m+l}\tilde{r}_{2j-2}\|_{H^{0,\frac{2-\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}}}\right)\\
&\lesssim\|\tilde{r}_{2j}\|_{H^{2m,m+\frac{2-\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}}}+\sum_{l=0}^{m-1}\|L^{l,m+l-1}\tilde{r}_{2j-2}\|_{H^{1,\frac{2-\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}}}\\
\end{split}

where, in the last line, we used an inequality similar to
4.86
for the
\tilde{r}_{2j-2}
terms. For the remaining sum, it remains to apply the elliptic estimate for
\tilde{L}_{1}
and then commute
\tilde{L}_{1}
with
L^{l,m+l-1}
using Remark
4.9
. We have

(4.89)

\begin{split}\sum_{l=0}^{m-1}\left(\|L^{l,m+l-1}\tilde{r}_{2j-2}\|_{H^{1,\frac{2-\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}}}\right)&\lesssim\sum_{l=0}^{m-1}\|\tilde{L}_{1}(L^{l,m+l-1}\tilde{r}_{2j-2})\|_{H^{0,\frac{2-\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}}}\\
&\lesssim\|P_{\mathcal{O}\geq 1/2}\|_{H^{0,\frac{2-\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}}}+\sum_{l=0}^{m-1}\|L^{l,m+l-1}(\tilde{r}_{2j}+P)\|_{H^{0,\frac{2-\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}}}\\
&\lesssim\|\tilde{r}_{2j}\|_{H^{2m,m+\frac{2-\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}}}+\|P_{\mathcal{O}\geq 1/2}\|_{H^{0,\frac{2-\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}}}\\
\end{split}

where we followed the same argument as (
4.87
) with
L^{m-1,2m-1}
replaced by
L^{l,m+l-1}
.

Finally combining (
4.85
) - (
4.89
), we are now ready to prove our higher order elliptic estimates for the
\tilde{r}
equation.

Lemma 4.12

(Higher Order Elliptic estimates for
\tilde{r}
)
.

Under the conditions for Lemma
4.1
, the following inequality holds

(4.90)

\|\tilde{r}\|_{H^{2k,k+\frac{2-\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}}}\lesssim\sum_{j=0}^{k}\|D_{t}^{2j}\tilde{r}\|_{H^{0,\frac{2-\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}}}+C_{\hat{\varepsilon}}\|(\tilde{s},\tilde{r},\tilde{u})\|_{\mathcal{H}^{2k}}

where
C_{\hat{\varepsilon}}\ll 1
depends on
\hat{\varepsilon}
.

Proof.

Combining (
4.85
) - (
4.89
) and summarizing our work in this section, we get the following inequality:

(4.91)

\begin{split}\|\tilde{r}_{2j-2}\|_{H^{2m+2,m+1+\frac{2-\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}}}&\lesssim\|L^{m,2m}\tilde{r}_{2j}\|_{H^{0,\frac{2-\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}}}+\|L^{m-1,2m-1}\tilde{r}_{2j}\|_{H^{0,\frac{2-\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}}}+\|\tilde{r}_{2j}\|_{H^{2m,m+\frac{2-\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}}}\\
&\hskip 14.22636pt+\|\tilde{r}_{2j-2}\|_{H^{0,\frac{2-\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}}}+\|P_{\mathcal{O}\geq 1/2}\|_{H^{0,\frac{2-\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}}}\\
&\lesssim\|\tilde{r}_{2j}\|_{H^{2m,m+\frac{2-\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}}}+\|\tilde{r}_{2j-2}\|_{H^{0,\frac{2-\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}}}+\|P_{\mathcal{O}\geq 1/2}\|_{H^{0,\frac{2-\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}}}\\
&\lesssim\|\tilde{r}_{2j}\|_{H^{2m,m+\frac{2-\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}}}+\|\tilde{r}_{2j-2}\|_{H^{0,\frac{2-\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}}}+\hat{\varepsilon}\|(\tilde{s},\tilde{r},\tilde{u})\|_{\mathcal{H}^{2k}}\\
\end{split}

which we showed using an induction argument on
m\geq 1
, and the
P_{\mathcal{O}\geq 1/2}
terms are treated using (
4.59
) and Remark
2.6
.
Then, we recall from (
4.56
) that
m=k-j
, so when
m=k-1
, we have
j=1
and

(4.92)

\|\tilde{r}\|_{H^{2k,k+\frac{2-\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}}}\lesssim\|D_{t}^{2}\tilde{r}\|_{H^{2k-2,k-1+\frac{2-\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}}}+\|\tilde{r}\|_{H^{0,\frac{2-\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}}}+\hat{\varepsilon}\|(\tilde{s},\tilde{r},\tilde{u})\|_{\mathcal{H}^{2k}}

Iterating, we obtain successive inequalities all the way up to
\|D_{t}^{2k}\tilde{r}\|_{H^{0,\frac{2-\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}}}
. Combining together, we have

(4.93)

\|\tilde{r}\|_{H^{2k,k+\frac{2-\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}}}\lesssim\sum_{j=0}^{k}\|D_{t}^{2j}\tilde{r}\|_{H^{0,\frac{2-\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}}}+C_{\hat{\varepsilon}}\|(\tilde{s},\tilde{r},\tilde{u})\|_{\mathcal{H}^{2k}}

where
C_{\hat{\varepsilon}}\ll 1
depends on
\hat{\varepsilon}
. This completes the proof.
∎

5.
Energy Equivalence

Theorem 5.1

(Equivalence between
E^{2k}
and
\mathcal{H}^{2k}
norms)
.

Let
(\tilde{s},\tilde{r},\tilde{v})
be smooth functions in
\overline{\Omega}
. If
r
is positive and uniformly non-degenerate on
\Gamma
, then

E^{2k}(\tilde{s},\tilde{r},\tilde{u})\approx\|(\tilde{s},\tilde{r},\tilde{u})\|_{\mathcal{H}^{2k}}^{2}

where the equivalence depends on
\gamma
and up to
2k
derivatives of
s,r,
and
u
.

Proof.

Let’s begin with the
\lesssim
direction. Using our book-keeping scheme from Section
2
, it will be quite straightforward to track the order of each of the terms as they appear. We plan to use Remark
1.14
in which the
\mathcal{H}^{2k}
norm for
(\tilde{s},\tilde{r},\tilde{u})
is shown to be equivalent to the
H^{2k,\frac{2-\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}+\frac{1}{2}+k}\times H^{2k,\frac{2-\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}+k}\times H^{2k,\frac{2-\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}+\frac{1}{2}+k}
norm.

We start with
E^{2k}_{\text{transport}}
, which we recall as

(5.1)

\begin{split}E^{2k}_{\text{transport}}(\tilde{s},\tilde{r},\tilde{u})&=\|\hat{\omega}\|_{H^{2k-1,\frac{2-\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}+\frac{1}{2}+k}}^{2}+\|\tilde{s}\|_{H^{2k,\frac{2-\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}+\frac{1}{2}+k}}^{2}\\
\end{split}

First, it is clear from Remark
1.14
that

(5.2)

\|\tilde{s}\|_{H^{2k,\frac{2-\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}+\frac{1}{2}+k}}^{2}\lesssim\|(\tilde{s},\tilde{r},\tilde{u})\|_{\mathcal{H}^{2k}}^{2}

For the vorticity part of
E^{2k}_{\text{transport}}
, we look at the order of each of the terms and observe that

(5.3)

\begin{split}\hat{\omega}_{\alpha\beta}&=\partial_{\alpha}(h\tilde{u}_{\beta})-\partial_{\beta}(h\tilde{u}_{\alpha})\\
&=h(\partial_{\alpha}\tilde{u}_{\beta}-\partial_{\beta}\tilde{u}_{\alpha})+\tilde{u}_{\beta}\partial_{\alpha}h-\tilde{u}_{\alpha}\partial_{\beta}h\\
&\simeq\partial\tilde{u}+\tilde{u}\end{split}

using our book-keeping scheme and recalling that
h=\frac{\Gamma+r}{\Gamma}
which is
O(1)
near the free boundary, as well as
\partial h
. We plan to estimate
\hat{\omega}
using a similar argument as the estimate for
\overline{\omega}
in (
3.36
). Applying a multiindex
l
with
|l|\leq 2k-1
and counting the number of derivatives and powers of
r
, we have

(5.4)

\partial^{l}\hat{\omega}\simeq\partial^{l+1}\tilde{u}+\partial^{l}\tilde{u}

Applying the
L^{2}(r^{\frac{2-\gamma}{\gamma-1}+2k+1})=H^{0,\frac{2-\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}+\frac{1}{2}+k}
norm, we have

(5.5)

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega_{t}}r^{\frac{2-\gamma}{\gamma-1}}|r^{k+\frac{1}{2}}\partial^{l}\hat{\omega}|_{G}^{2}\hskip 2.84526ptdx&\lesssim\int_{\Omega_{t}}r^{\frac{2-\gamma}{\gamma-1}}|r^{k+\frac{1}{2}}\partial^{l+1}\tilde{u}|^{2}\hskip 2.84526ptdx+\int_{\Omega_{t}}r^{\frac{2-\gamma}{\gamma-1}}|r^{k+\frac{1}{2}}\partial^{l}\tilde{u}|^{2}\hskip 2.84526ptdx\\
\end{split}

Summing over
l
, we obtain the
H^{2k-1,\frac{2-\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}+\frac{1}{2}+k}
norm, and we will get terms that are only critical at worst, i.e. they will have the form
r^{k+\frac{1}{2}}\partial^{2k}\tilde{u}
modulo coefficients depending on derivatives of
s
and
r
, and the second intergral produces subcritical terms of the form
r^{k+\frac{1}{2}}\partial^{2k-1}\tilde{u}
at worst. Each of these can easily be estimated via the
\mathcal{H}^{2k}
norm, so we have

(5.6)

\|\hat{\omega}\|_{H^{2k-1,\frac{2-\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}+\frac{1}{2}+k}}^{2}\lesssim\|(\tilde{s},\tilde{r},\tilde{u})\|_{\mathcal{H}^{2k}}^{2}.

and combining with (
5.2
) yields

(5.7)

E^{2k}_{\text{transport}}(\tilde{s},\tilde{r},\tilde{u})\lesssim\|(\tilde{s},\tilde{r},\tilde{u})\|_{\mathcal{H}^{2k}}^{2}.

For
E^{2k}_{\text{wave}}
, we first use Remark
1.13
to simplfy

(5.8)

\begin{split}E^{2k}_{\text{wave}}(\tilde{s},\tilde{r},\tilde{u})&=\sum_{j=0}^{k}\|(D_{t}^{2j}\tilde{r},D_{t}^{2j}\tilde{u})\|_{\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}}^{2}\\
&\simeq\sum_{j=0}^{k}\left(\|D_{t}^{2j}\tilde{r}\|_{H^{0,\frac{2-\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}}}^{2}+\|D_{t}^{2j}\tilde{u}\|_{H^{0,\frac{2-\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}+\frac{1}{2}}}^{2}\right)\end{split}

using the fact that norms for
\tilde{\mathcal{H}},(L^{2}(r^{\frac{2-\gamma}{\gamma-1}})\times L^{2}(r^{\frac{2-\gamma}{\gamma-1}+1}))
, and
(H^{0,\frac{2-\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}}\times H^{0,\frac{2-\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}+\frac{1}{2}})
are equivalent.
Then, we make frequent use of Lemma
2.13
which allows us to greatly simplify convective derivatives. Taking
0\leq j\leq k
, we have

(5.9)

\|D_{t}^{2j}\tilde{r}\|_{H^{0,\frac{2-\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}}}^{2}\simeq\left\|\sum_{l=0}^{j}r^{l}\partial^{l+j}\tilde{r}\right\|_{H^{0,\frac{2-\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}}}^{2}\lesssim\|\tilde{r}\|_{H^{2j,\frac{2-\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}+j}}^{2}

noting that each term appearing in the summation will be critical at the
j
level. Then, further applying our embedding lemmas, we get

(5.10)

\begin{split}\|\tilde{r}\|_{H^{2j,\frac{2-\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}+j}}^{2}&\lesssim\|\tilde{r}\|_{H^{2j+k-j,\frac{2-\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}+j+k-j}}^{2}\\
&=\|\tilde{r}\|_{H^{k+j,\frac{2-\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}+k}}^{2}\\
&\lesssim\|\tilde{r}\|_{H^{2k,\frac{2-\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}+k}}^{2}\lesssim\|(\tilde{s},\tilde{r},\tilde{u})\|_{\mathcal{H}^{2k}}^{2}\end{split}

noting that
0\leq j\leq k
and we can apply Corollary
2.4
as needed when
j<k
. Thus, the
\tilde{r}
part of
E^{2k}_{\text{wave}}
is bounded by the desired
\mathcal{H}^{2k}
norm. For the
\tilde{u}
part, we can similarly invoke Lemma
2.13
again, where the only difference is that we now have terms involving both
\tilde{u}
and
\tilde{r}
. However, the extra
r^{1/2}
weight is exactly what we need to handle any of the terms that appear:

(5.11)

\begin{split}\|D_{t}^{2j}\tilde{u}\|_{H^{0,\frac{2-\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}+\frac{1}{2}}}^{2}&\simeq\left\|\sum_{l=0}^{j}r^{l}\partial^{l+j}\tilde{u}+\sum_{i=0}^{j-1}r^{i}\partial^{i+j}\tilde{r}\right\|_{H^{0,\frac{2-\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}+\frac{1}{2}}}^{2}\\
&\lesssim\|(\tilde{s},\tilde{r},\tilde{u})\|_{\mathcal{H}^{2k}}^{2}\\
\end{split}

and we observe that the
\tilde{r}
terms actually have an extra
r^{1/2}
in terms of bounding by the
\mathcal{H}^{2k}
norm. Combining (
5.8
)-(
5.11
) produces

(5.12)

E^{2k}_{\text{wave}}(\tilde{s},\tilde{r},\tilde{u})\lesssim\|(\tilde{s},\tilde{r},\tilde{u})\|_{\mathcal{H}^{2k}}^{2}.

Thus, we combine with (
5.7
) and the
\lesssim
direction for Theorem
5.1
is complete.

For the
\gtrsim
direction, we make use of many previously established lemmas. First, by the norm equivalence from Remark
1.14
, and Lemmas

4.12
, and
4.14
, we have

(5.13)

\begin{split}\|(\tilde{s},\tilde{r},\tilde{u})\|_{\mathcal{H}^{2k}}^{2}&\approx\|\tilde{r}\|_{H^{2k,\frac{2-\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}+k}}^{2}+\|\tilde{u}\|_{H^{2k,\frac{2-\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}+\frac{1}{2}+k}}^{2}+\|\tilde{s}\|_{H^{2k,\frac{2-\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}+\frac{1}{2}+k}}^{2}\\
&\lesssim\sum_{j=0}^{k}\left(\|D_{t}^{2j}\tilde{r}\|^{2}_{H^{0,\frac{2-\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}}}+\|D_{t}^{2j}\tilde{u}\|^{2}_{H^{0,\frac{2-\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}+\frac{1}{2}}}\right)\\
&\hskip 14.22636pt+\|\overrightarrow{\text{curl}}\hskip 2.84526pt\tilde{u}\|_{H^{2k-1,k+\frac{1}{2(\gamma-1)}}}^{2}+\|\tilde{s}\|_{H^{2k,\frac{2-\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}+\frac{1}{2}+k}}^{2}\\
&\hskip 14.22636pt+(C_{\hat{\varepsilon}}^{2}+D_{\hat{\varepsilon}}^{2})\|(\tilde{s},\tilde{r},\tilde{u})\|_{\mathcal{H}^{2k}}^{2}\\
\end{split}

where
C_{\hat{\varepsilon}},D_{\hat{\varepsilon}}\ll 1
.
Then, we observe by examining
E^{2k}_{\text{wave}}
in (
1.47
) that

(5.14)

\sum_{j=0}^{k}\left(\|D_{t}^{2j}\tilde{r}\|^{2}_{H^{0,\frac{2-\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}}}+\|D_{t}^{2j}\tilde{u}\|^{2}_{H^{0,\frac{2-\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}+\frac{1}{2}}}\right)\lesssim\sum_{j=0}^{k}\|(D_{t}^{2j}\tilde{r},D_{t}^{2j}\tilde{u})\|_{\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}}^{2}=E^{2k}_{\text{wave}}(\tilde{s},\tilde{r},\tilde{u})

as any weights involving
\Gamma
can be removed in view of Remark
1.13
. Then, combining (
5.13
), (
5.14
), and applying Lemma
4.4
for the term with
\overrightarrow{\text{curl}}\hskip 2.84526pt\tilde{u}
, we have

(5.15)

\begin{split}\|(\tilde{s},\tilde{r},\tilde{u})\|_{\mathcal{H}^{2k}}^{2}&\lesssim E^{2k}_{\text{wave}}(\tilde{s},\tilde{r},\tilde{u})+\|\hat{\omega}\|_{H^{2k-1,\frac{2-\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}+\frac{1}{2}+k}}^{2}+\|\tilde{s}\|_{H^{2k,\frac{2-\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}+\frac{1}{2}+k}}^{2}\\
\ &\hskip 14.22636pt+\hat{\varepsilon}^{2}\|\tilde{u}\|_{H^{2k,\frac{2-\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}+\frac{1}{2}+k}}^{2}+(C_{\hat{\varepsilon}}^{2}+D_{\hat{\varepsilon}}^{2})\|(\tilde{s},\tilde{r},\tilde{u})\|_{\mathcal{H}^{2k}}^{2}\\
&\lesssim E^{2k}_{\text{wave}}(\tilde{s},\tilde{r},\tilde{u})+E^{2k}_{\text{transport}}(\tilde{s},\tilde{r},\tilde{u})+(C_{\hat{\varepsilon}}^{2}+D_{\hat{\varepsilon}}^{2}+\hat{\varepsilon}^{2})\|(\tilde{s},\tilde{r},\tilde{u})\|_{\mathcal{H}^{2k}}^{2}\end{split}

Absorbing the constants
(C_{\hat{\varepsilon}}^{2}+D_{\hat{\varepsilon}}^{2}+\hat{\varepsilon}^{2})\ll 1
into the LHS produces

(5.16)

E^{2k}(\tilde{s},\tilde{r},\tilde{u})=E^{2k}_{\text{wave}}(\tilde{s},\tilde{r},\tilde{u})+E^{2k}_{\text{transport}}(\tilde{s},\tilde{r},\tilde{u})\gtrsim\|(\tilde{s},\tilde{r},\tilde{u})\|_{\mathcal{H}^{2k}}^{2}

which completes the proof of the
\gtrsim
direction. Thus, the proof of Theorem
5.1
is complete.
∎

6.
Higher order wave energy estimates

Using the book-keeping scheme from Section
2
, let’s estimate each of the terms on the RHS of (
2.1
) with the following Theorem. Note that the
B_{2k}
equation will get multiplied by
\frac{1}{\gamma-1}r^{\frac{2-\gamma}{\gamma-1}}D_{t}^{2k}\tilde{r}
, and the
C^{\alpha}_{2k}
equation will get contracted with
(\Gamma+r)r^{\frac{1}{\gamma-1}}G_{\alpha\beta}D_{t}^{2k}\tilde{u}^{\beta}
.

We plan to integrate and estimate using the
\mathcal{H}^{2k}
norm
which we recall:

\begin{split}\|(\tilde{s},\tilde{r},\tilde{u})\|_{\mathcal{H}^{2k}}^{2}&=\sum_{|\alpha|=0}^{2k}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a=0\\
|\alpha|-a\leq k\end{subarray}}^{k}\|r^{\frac{2-\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}+\frac{1}{2}+a}\partial^{\alpha}\tilde{s}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|r^{\frac{2-\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}+a}\partial^{\alpha}\tilde{r}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|r^{\frac{2-\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}+\frac{1}{2}+a}\partial^{\alpha}\tilde{u}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\\
\end{split}

The following book-keeping remark is useful when taking convective derivatives of the many expressions that appear:

Remark 6.1
.

Using the fact that
D_{t}(\Gamma(s))=0
from (
1.35a
), we have the following observations:

\begin{split}&D_{t}\left(\frac{1}{\Gamma+r}\right)=-\frac{1}{(\Gamma+r)^{2}}D_{t}r\simeq\left(\frac{1}{\Gamma+r}\right)^{2}r\end{split}

where we applied (
2.10
). Thus, taking multiple convective derivatives of our weight
\frac{1}{\Gamma+r}
leads to gaining additional powers of
r
, and thus makes the terms more subcritical. In view of Remarks
2.5
and

7.
Main Theorem

Combining the previous sections, we have reached our final result:

Theorem 7.1

(Estimates in
\mathcal{H}^{2k}
)
.

Let
(s,r,u)
be a smooth solution to (
1.35
) that exists on some time interval
[0,T]
, and for which the physical vacuum boundary condition (
1.13
) holds. Let
(\tilde{s}_{0},\tilde{r}_{0},\tilde{u}_{0})
be initial data to system (
1.36
). Then, there exists a constant
\mathcal{C}
depending only on
s,r,u,
and
T
such that, if
(\tilde{s},\tilde{r},\tilde{u})\in C^{\infty}(\overline{\mathscr{D}})
is a solution to (
1.36
) on
[0,T]
, then

(7.1)

\|(\tilde{s},\tilde{r},\tilde{u})\|_{\mathcal{H}^{2k}(\Omega_{t})}\lesssim\mathcal{C}\|(\tilde{s}_{0},\tilde{r}_{0},\tilde{u}_{0})\|_{\mathcal{H}^{2k}(\Omega_{0})}

where
\mathcal{H}^{2k}(\Omega_{t})
is defined in (
1.46
).

Proof.

Combining Proposition
3.1
and Theorem
6.2
, we get the following

(7.2)

\frac{d}{dt}\left(\|\tilde{s}\|^{2}_{H^{2k,k+\frac{2-\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}+\frac{1}{2}}}+E^{2k}_{\text{wave}}(\tilde{s},\tilde{r},\tilde{u})\right)\lesssim\mathcal{C}_{1}\|(\tilde{s},\tilde{r},\tilde{u})\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{2k}}.

where
\mathcal{C}_{1}
is a constant depending on
L^{\infty}(\Omega_{t})
norm of up to
2k+1
derivatives of
s,r
, and
u
.
Then, after adding
\frac{d}{dt}\|\hat{\omega}\|_{H^{2k-1,k+\frac{2-\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}+\frac{1}{2}}}^{2}
to both sides and recalling (
1.48
) and (
1.49
), we have

(7.3)

\frac{d}{dt}E^{2k}_{\text{total}}(\tilde{s},\tilde{r},\tilde{u})\lesssim\mathcal{C}_{1}\|(\tilde{s},\tilde{r},\tilde{u})\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{2k}}+\frac{d}{dt}\|\hat{\omega}\|_{H^{2k-1,k+\frac{2-\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}+\frac{1}{2}}}^{2}.

Integrating both sides and using Theorem
5.1
on the energy equivalence, we have

(7.4)

\|(\tilde{s},\tilde{r},\tilde{u})\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{2k}(\Omega_{t})}-\|(\tilde{s}_{0},\tilde{r}_{0},\tilde{u}_{0})\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{2k}(\Omega_{0})}\lesssim\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{C}_{1}\|(\tilde{s},\tilde{r},\tilde{u})\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{2k}(\Omega_{\tau})}\hskip 2.84526ptd\tau+\|\hat{\omega}\|_{H^{2k-1,k+\frac{2-\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}+\frac{1}{2}}(\Omega_{t})}^{2}.

Applying Theorem
3.4
for the
\hat{\omega}
term and rearranging, we get

(7.5)

\begin{split}\|(\tilde{s},\tilde{r},\tilde{u})\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{2k}(\Omega_{t})}&\lesssim\|(\tilde{s}_{0},\tilde{r}_{0},\tilde{u}_{0})\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{2k}(\Omega_{0})}+\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{C}_{1}\|(\tilde{s},\tilde{r},\tilde{u})\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{2k}(\Omega_{\tau})}\hskip 2.84526ptd\tau\\
&\hskip 14.22636pt+\|\tilde{\omega}_{0}\|_{H^{2k-1,k+\frac{1}{2(\gamma-1)}}(\Omega_{0})}^{2}+\hat{\varepsilon}\|(\tilde{s},\tilde{r},\tilde{u})\|_{\mathcal{H}^{2k}(\Omega_{t})}^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{C}_{2}\|(\tilde{s},\tilde{r},\tilde{u})\|_{\mathcal{H}^{2k}(\Omega_{\tau})}^{2}\hskip 2.84526ptd\tau\\
\end{split}

Then, combining the initial data
\tilde{\omega}_{0}
with the initial data term
\|(\tilde{s}_{0},\tilde{r}_{0},\tilde{u}_{0})\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{2k}(\Omega_{0})}
, and soaking the term with
\hat{\varepsilon}
to the LHS, we arrive at

(7.6)

\|(\tilde{s},\tilde{r},\tilde{u})\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{2k}(\Omega_{t})}\lesssim\frac{1}{1-\hat{\varepsilon}}\left(\|(\tilde{s}_{0},\tilde{r}_{0},\tilde{u}_{0})\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{2k}(\Omega_{0})}+\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{C}_{3}\|(\tilde{s},\tilde{r},\tilde{u})\|_{\mathcal{H}^{2k}(\Omega_{\tau})}^{2}\hskip 2.84526ptd\tau\right)

Finally, the desired result is a straightforward application of Gronwall’s inequality.

∎

A novel approach for accurate disease prediction: Application to heart and liver diseases
Authors: Satyanarayana Poojari, B. Ismail
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BACKGROUND:Heart and liver diseases are among the leading causes of mortality globally. Their early detection can prevent complications, reduce costs, and ensure healthy lives and well-being for everyone. Machine learning-based predictive models, such as logistic regression (LR) and decision tree (DT) are commonly used in health science for prediction. Even though LR and DT are effective, they have major drawbacks. DT assigns the same class to all observations in a branch which can lead to overfitting, while LR model often produces high misclassification rates and overfit when dealing with high-dimensional data. To address these limitations, this paper proposes a hybrid classification model combining the strengths of DT and LR.MATERIALS AND METHODS:Monte Carlo simulation and empirical study are conducted to compare the predictive performance of the proposed model with LR, DT, Support Vector Machine, K-Nearest Neighbors, and Random Forest. For the empirical study two datasets namely, heart disease prediction dataset (balanced, 303 observations, 14 variables) and Liver disease dataset (imbalanced, 583 observations, 11 variables) are used.RESULTS:The simulation results indicate the hybrid model outperforms other classification models considered, across various sample sizes. These findings are consistent with the empirical data, showing predictive accuracy of 91% for heart disease and 95% for liver disease data.CONCLUSION:The developed hybrid model has enhanced prediction accuracy irrespective of sample size and effectively handles both balanced and imbalanced data, reducing the need to identify suitable balancing techniques. Improved efficiency can help with early detection, better decision-making, and improved health systems.

Introduction

Early and accurate detection of diseases is crucial for improving health outcomes, as it enables timely interventions that can prevent complications, reduce costs, and ensure healthy lives. Accurate detection of heart and liver diseases is crucial as these are among the leading causes of mortality globally. Heart diseases, such as coronary artery disease, account for approximately 17.9 million deaths annually, while liver diseases contribute to millions of additional deaths. Identifying significant predictive variables improves diagnostic precision and helps in formulating targeted prevention strategies. The primary challenges affecting the accurate prediction of heart and liver diseases include natural variation, uncertainty, and the complexity of relationships among predictive variables. The complex, non-linear relationships between risk factors and disease outcomes are difficult for simple models to capture. Many prediction models depend mainly on assumptions of linearity and independence of predictors, which may not valid always, leading to poor performance. Also, imbalanced cases of heart and liver diseases significantly affect the model, leading to biased outcomes favoring the majority class. These challenges highlight the need for advanced and flexible models capable of handling variability, uncertainty, and complex relationship among predictors.

The rapid technological advancements in health sciences lead to increase in the data volumes across diverse sectors. There is a need for the development of algorithms to address the challenges resulting from large volume of data. In health science, classification models such as logistic regression (LR), support vector machine (SVM), K-nearest neighbors (KNN), and decision tree (DT) have become more popular for predicting health outcomes. Logistic model is simple, highly efficient, and effective technique in which over-fitting is less concerned issue. Tree-based DTs are advantageous due to their simple structure, making them easy to interpret and classify. Tree pruning and variable selection method are used to avoid complex structure and over fitting issue.

The effectiveness of the SVM model in medical data sets are reported in the research paper of Chang et al. and Dreiseitl et al.[12] The kernel function, along with parameter choice is the major problem in the SVM and hence it is not widely used. The LR model shows less generalization error rate. Therefore, it is more popular than KNN and SVM. According to a review study by Dreiseitl and Ohno-Machado, the performance of logistic models and artificial neural networks (ANNs) is better compared to DTs and KNN. Mitchell’s study shows that redundant variables influence the distance function and hence KNN shows poor performance in the case of high-dimensional data.[34]

In any classification task related to disease identification, along with achieving maximum separation it is also important to find the most significant variables. The success of any classification model depends on several key factors, including the nature of the datasets, selection of model parameters, and the validation of results. One of the common challenges in the field of health sciences is data imbalance. Particularly, when dealing with conditions that affect a small portion of the population, such as rare diseases or adverse events imbalance can hinder the predictive performances of models. So special care to be taken to avoid or reduce error rates to ensure that the constructed models are validated.[5]

One of the flexible approaches that can effectively handle data imbalances and enhance predictive accuracy is the use of hybrid models. Several studies in the literature have proposed and demonstrated the effectiveness of hybrid models in improving predictive performance. Aci et al. proposed a novel hybrid model by integrating KNN, Bayesian methods and genetic algorithms.[6] Alimjan et al. introduced a new classifier that incorporates SVM learning information into KNN demonstrating improved classification accuracy compared to SVM and KNN models.[7] De Caigny et al. introduced the logit leaf model, a hybrid approach benchmarked against DT, LR, random forest (RF) and logistic model trees, focusing on predictive performance and comprehensibility.[8] Amirhajlou et al. applied three data mining algorithms, including multi-layer perceptron ANN, SVM and linear regression to build the prediction models of pre-board examination scores.[9] Lokeshkumar et al. analyze the social media data using machine learning to predict users’ mental states, highlighting the impact of online behavior on mental health.[10]

Recent studies have explored hybrid machine learning and deep learning models for disease prediction, aiming to enhance classification accuracy and enable early diagnosis. Mohan et al. proposed a hybrid model combining RF with a linear model to improve heart disease prediction by leveraging machine learning techniques for feature selection and classification.[11] Mathew and Kumar implemented a hybrid model based on LR, incorporating class balancing and search techniques.[12] Khankhoje introduced a hybrid model integrating DT, LR, and ANN to enhance heart disease diagnosis by combining the strengths of individual classifiers.[13] Afrash et al. developed a breast cancer prediction system using a Genetic Algorithm combined with machine learning techniques for early diagnosis and risk assessment.[14] Dalal et al. proposed a hybrid eXtreme Gradient Boosting (XGBoost) model with hyperparameter tuning to enhance the early detection, diagnosis, and risk reduction of liver disease, aiming to improve patient outcomes and lower mortality rates.[15] Lamba et al. proposed a novel neural network-based hybrid model that combines the strength of recent advanced model namely generative adversarial network, convolutional neural network (CNN) and long-short term memory (LSTM), and results show that proposed model has superior accuracy compared to CNN, SVM, and hybrid CNN-SVM and CNN-LSTM models.[16] Kandula et al. presented a hybrid voting classifier using guided Whale Optimization Algorithm to aggregate predictions from diverse classification models, including LR, SVM, and RF, to address discrepancies and enhance classification robustness.[17] Shetty and Satyanarayana evaluated the performance of various well known classification models with application to imbalanced liver disease data.[18] Later, Satyanarayana and Ismail developed two hybrid models namely RT-KNN and RT-MLR in the context of regression to improve the prediction accuracy.[1920] Recently, Satyanarayana and Ismail proposed DT-KNN model to deal with the challenges of KNN and DT.[21]

Later, Behera et al. proposed a hybrid SVM model, optimized using a modified Chaotic Crazy Particle Swarm Optimization, to enhance heart and liver disease classification.[22] Baviskar et al. combined feature selection techniques, including Genetic Algorithms and Particle Swarm Optimization, with recurrent neural networks and LSTM to improve feature selection and classification accuracy for heart disease prediction.[23] Shrivastava et al. implemented a deep learning-based hybrid model integrating CNN with Bidirectional Long Short-Term Memory for efficient heart disease classification.[24] These studies underscore the increasing significance of hybrid machine learning approaches in enhancing predictive accuracy and clinical applicability for heart and liver disease diagnosis.

Developing accurate predictive models for disease diagnoses requires specialized techniques and evaluation strategies, particularly to address class imbalance. Selecting key predictors from datasets and ensuring the models generalize well across diverse populations without overfitting remains a significant challenge. The novelty of this study lies in developing a hybrid model that effectively addresses the limitations of the LR and DT models. This improved hybrid model is then applied to build a predictive model for heart and liver diseases and risk assessment. By combining the strengths of both models, the approach aims to enhance predictive performance and improve generalizability across diverse patient populations.

Materials and Methods

Study design and setting

This study aims to develop a hybrid classification model that combines the strengths of DT and LR to achieve improved classification accuracy. The performance of the proposed hybrid model is evaluated in comparison with other effective and widely used machine learning algorithms, including DT, LR, SVM, RT and KNN utilizing a comprehensive simulation setup and two empirical datasets. The simulation and data analysis were conducted using R software (version 4.3.1). The DT is built using the rpart package and the e1071 package is used for the Gaussian kernel-based SVM model.

Data collection tool and technique

After the simulation study, the results of the proposed model are verified using empirical data to demonstrate its effectiveness in real-life applications. This real-life scenario includes both balanced cases (where two classes have an equal proportion of observations) and imbalanced cases (where one class is the majority and the other is the minority).

The liver disease dataset from Kaggle (https://www.kaggle.com/uciml/indian-liver-patient-records) is an unbalanced dataset with 583 observations and 11 variables obtained from North East of Andhra Pradesh, India. Statistical analysis method

Proposed hybrid decision tree-logistic regression model

The LR and DT models are the two-effective approach to the classification problem. The drawback of DT is that it assigns the same class to all observations satisfying the corresponding splitting criterion in the leaf node. LR models often lead to a high misclassification rate, being a significant disadvantage of this approach. In this paper a hybrid model is built by using the strength of DT and LR to overcome their drawbacks with a focus on enhancing predictive performance while preserving model comprehensibility.

The proposed algorithm begins by partitioning the dataset into train and test sets. Using the training data, DT is constructed using the Classification and Regression Tree (CART) algorithm. During the construction of the tree, the algorithm selects the most significant variables and the best split point for each branch. Subsequently, LR model is developed for each branch, using the significant variables identified by DT. The use of DT’s output as an input for the LR model within each branch results in the proposed hybrid DT-LR model which significantly enhances the model’s ability to separate the classes effectively. By fitting LR models for all branches and combining their prediction results, the proposed model optimizes classification accuracy, while maintaining robustness. The working of the proposed model is shown in Figure 1.

Conceptual figure of theoretical framework

The core idea behind this hybridization is to construct models individually for each leaf node rather than applying a single logistic model to the entire dataset. Since observations within each leaf node show high intraclass similarities, construction of individual LR model to each branch often leads to better predictive accuracy. The key strength of the proposed approach lies in the strategic variable selection process and the integration of DT-derived tree outputs into the LR framework. This innovative approach showcases the potential for achieving superior classification results, making it a valuable tool for complex decision-making scenarios.

Logistic regression model

The LR model is the most popular multivariate method in health sciences because of its extremely flexible nature and meaningful interpretation can be obtained.[26] A logistic model is used to analyze the relationship between single or multiple independent variables with a categorical dependent variable. This model has many desirable properties of linear regression model, but the underlying distribution is binomial. The parameters, β0, β1, β2, βk of LR model are usually estimated using the maximum likelihood method.[27]

Decision tree

DT primarily comprises decision nodes and terminal leaves. The CART algorithm uses binary splits for each variable, minimizing the sum of squared errors.[28] The best splitting variable at each node is selected based on the Gini Index method. Partitioning stops when the error reduction falls below a specified threshold, and predictions are made at the terminal node using the majority class of the observations.

K-nearest neighbors

KNN is a non-parametric classification method that predicts the class label for unknown observation in data based on the majority class of its KNN. The distance metric, such as Euclidean distance, is used to determine the proximity of data points. The value of k plays a vital role in prediction performance and needs to be selected optimally to minimize classification error.

Support vector machine

SVM is a supervised classification approach which creates optimal hyperplane to separate various classes as much as possible in a high-dimensional space. The objectives of SVM aims to maximize the margin between the classes, improving accuracy and generalization. SVM can handle linear and non-linear classification by using kernel functions.

Random Forest

RF is an ensemble method which constructs several DTs to enhance predictive performance and avoid overfitting. After each tree is trained on a randomly selected subset of data with features and the final output is determined considering majority predictions of all trees. This method increases model reliability by combining the strengths of several individual classifiers.

The efficiency of the developed model in comparison with other models was evaluated using accuracy measures such as overall accuracy, sensitivity, and specificity.

Discussion

With the increasing availability of health data, robust prediction and classification techniques have become essential for identifying patterns, supporting diagnosis, and optimizing treatment in healthcare. The main aim of this study was to propose a hybrid model that enhances classification accuracy, particularly by combining the strengths of DT and LR. The proposed model was analyzed through simulation using a limited number of covariates and outcome variables across different sample sizes.

The simulation results demonstrate that the proposed DT-LR model outperforms LR, KNN, RF, SVM, DT, and LR models in terms of accuracy, particularly when observations are drawn from a uniform distribution. Across different sample sizes, the hybrid model maintains superior accuracy compared to other models with significant margins. For example, for samples up to 200, most ML models do not achieve satisfactory sensitivity rates, whereas the hybrid model demonstrates good class detection rates as well as overall accuracy. As the sample size increases, the performance of all methods improves; however, the proposed model stands out as the better model with consistently high accuracy. In addition to improving accuracy, the hybrid model effectively addresses the limitations associated with DT, such as assigning the same class for all observations in a branch, and the overfitting problem of LR due to variable selection issues. This simulation study emphasizes the enhanced predictive abilities of the suggested hybrid approach and a preferred choice for classification tasks, especially in situations with varying class distribution balances.

When applied to real-life datasets, both balanced (heart disease) and imbalanced (liver disease), the DT-LR Hybrid consistently shows higher accuracy, sensitivity, and specificity compared to all other models highlighting its efficiency in handling diverse datasets. This finding is consistent with previous studies, including Alimjan et al., De Caigny et al., Khankhoje, Mohan et al., and Kandula et al., which have also concluded that hybrid models tend to perform better than traditional LR, RF, DT, and KNN models in classification tasks, particularly in disease prediction applications.[78111317] In health science, where datasets often have imbalanced classes, it often results in biased classification favoring the majority class and most of the model fails to detect minority class.[29] The proposed model proves to be an efficient approach to address the challenges posed by imbalanced data. The hybrid model provides specific cutoff values for each covariate, enabling practical classification of individuals into different disease status categories. The significant variables identified by the hybrid model can help in early detection and prevention. In case of liver disease, age, total bilirubin, alkaline aminotransferase, aspartate aminotransferase, total proteins, and Albumin are key indicators for predicting liver disease.

The principal component Analysis approach was used in Mostafa et al., while the RF model was applied in Ding et al.[3031] The significant variables identified by the proposed model align with both studies, further supporting findings.

These significant variables help to identify liver damage, assess functionality, and guide early intervention. The elevated enzyme levels indicate liver inflammation, while low protein and albumin levels reflect poor liver performance. For heart disease data, ST depression and chest pain type reflect the extent of ischemia, helping identify coronary artery disease. Serum cholesterol, angina during exercise, major vessel count, and peak heart rate offer valuable information on lipid levels, vascular status, and heart performance, supporting precise diagnosis. Most of the significant variables identified by the hybrid model matched the features selected by MFS combined with the computerized feature selection process.[32] Recognizing these factors aids in timely diagnosis, targeted treatments, and effective monitoring to manage and control liver diseases. Also, performance of the proposed DT-LR model is almost similar in overall accuracy but achieves a better sensitivity rate than the CNN-BiLSTM model reported in Shrivastava et al.[24]

The main novelty of this study is the introduction of a hybrid model that combines a DT and a LR model to address the limitations of both LR and DT models. The performance of the proposed approach is evaluated through both simulation and empirical studies, considering both balanced and imbalanced case across varying sample size. The proposed hybrid model accurately predicts both liver disease and heart disease and consistently outperforms other commonly used machine learning classifiers.

Given the Hybrid DT-LR model’s consistent performance in handling both balanced and imbalanced datasets, it can be effectively used for predicting various health outcomes. Its superior performance in both simulation studies and real-world datasets suggests its potential applicability to diverse populations. However, external validation and fine-tuning may be required based on specific demographic and clinical characteristics. Additionally, the model’s ability to identify key risk factors enhances its utility in targeted health interventions and policy planning.

Limitations and recommendations

The present study is limited to evaluating the performance of the proposed hybrid model under a fixed number of regressors. Future studies can focus on examining the impact of varying the number of regressors on prediction performance. Additionally, this study focuses exclusively on continuous regressors. Future research could explore the use of mixed types of variables.

Conclusion

The proposed DT-LR Hybrid model addresses the limitations of DT and LR while enhancing prediction accuracy and effectively handling both balanced and imbalanced data. This model supports clinical decision-making and early disease detection and tackling challenges associated with imbalanced data, particularly in rare diseases or specific patient populations. To mitigate risk factors for liver and heart disease, health policies should prioritize early screening, lifestyle modifications, and improved access to liver function tests and routine cardiovascular screening. Effective prevention strategies include maintaining a balanced diet, engaging in regular physical activity, managing cholesterol levels, and reducing alcohol consumption and obesity. The hybrid model provides valuable insights for policymakers to implement evidence-based public health initiatives, promote awareness, encourage routine medical check-ups, and introduce targeted interventions to reduce the burden of both liver and heart disease, ultimately improving patient outcomes.

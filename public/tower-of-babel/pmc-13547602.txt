Opening the 2026 Spring Conference of the Korean Society of Exercise Rehabilitation
Authors: Chang-Ju Kim
Published: 2026

The 2026 Spring Conference of the Korean Society of Exercise Rehabilitation will be held on Saturday, June 27, from 2:00 PM to 6:00 PM at Mille Hall in the Future Centennial Hall of Sangmyung University. We extend our sincere gratitude to the professors and graduate students of Sangmyung University who have prepared this conference.

We have invited two distinguished professors currently active in the United States, Professor Kyung-Shin Park and Professor Mi-Hyun Jang, as guest speakers. We believe this will be of great help in acquiring knowledge related to exercise rehabilitation. Additionally, we have invited Professor Si-Hwa Park and Professor Ji-Eun Yoo, who were appointed as new professors last year and this year, respectively, as research presenters. By listening to the presentations of these newly appointed professors, we intend to celebrate their appointments and provide an opportunity to encounter new knowledge.

The theme of this conference is “Acquiring Knowledge Related to Exercise Rehabilitation.” There is so much knowledge related to exercise rehabilitation that it is difficult to even gauge the scope of what knowledge should be included. It is also difficult to determine whether one should acquire knowledge regarding the commonalities between exercise and rehabilitation, or if knowledge related to both is necessary.

The scope of any academic discipline is determined by its development in practice. While the academic boundaries of “exercise rehabilitation” will likely be defined by its role in the real world, its real-world scope will also be defined by academic advancement. Therefore, it is anticipated that as the academic scope of “exercise rehabilitation” expands, its practical role will also broaden.

Although this academic conference is held as a matter of course, I hope this opportunity serves as a time to reconsider the academic boundaries of “exercise rehabilitation” and the practical roles hidden within them. I look forward to the accumulation of such moments, which will help secure a broader academic role and scope for “exercise rehabilitation.”

Even if we cannot achieve everything, gathering together after a long time will make this a meaningful time. Rather than wishing for too much, we should be grateful for achieving small things.

Let us spend today looking forward to a better tomorrow.

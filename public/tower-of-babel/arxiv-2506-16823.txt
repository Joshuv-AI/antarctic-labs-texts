Correspondence among congruence families for generalized Frobenius partitions via modular permutations
Rong Chen, Xiao-Jie Zhu

Abstract
. In 2024, Garvan, Sellers and Smoot discovered a remarkable symmetry in the families of congruences for generalized Frobenius partitions c ​ ψ 2 , 0 c\psi_{2,0} and c ​ ψ 2 , 1 c\psi_{2,1} . They also emphasized that the considerations for the general case of c ​ ψ k , β c\psi_{k,\beta} are important for future work. In this paper, for each k k we construct a vector-valued modular form for the generating functions of c ​ ψ k , β c\psi_{k,\beta} , and determine an equivalence relation among all β \beta . Introduction

In his 1984 AMS Memoir, Andrews
[
1
]
introduced two families of partition functions. Among them one is
c\phi_{k}(n)
, the generalized
k
-colored Frobenius partition function. He proved a variety of Ramanujan-type congruences satisfied by these
c\phi_{k}(n)
such as

(1.1)

\displaystyle c\phi_{2}(5n+3)\equiv 0\pmod{5}.

In 1994, Sellers
[
17
]
conjectured that (
1.1
) has an analog modulo all powers of
5
:

(1.2)

\displaystyle c\phi_{2}(5^{\alpha}n+\lambda_{\alpha})\equiv 0\pmod{5^{\alpha}},

where
\lambda_{\alpha}
satisfies that
12\lambda_{\alpha}\equiv 1\pmod{5^{\alpha}}
. Eighteen years later, a complete proof of this conjecture was found by Paule and Radu
[
15
]
. Their proof follows the method of Watson
[
20
]
, and provides a new perspective on proving Ramanujan-type congruences by using the technique of modular functions.

Recently, Jiang, Rolen and Woodbury
[
12
]
considered
(k,a)
-colored Forbenius partition functions
c\psi_{k,a}(n)
, which are natural generalizations of Andrews’
c\phi_{k}
since
c\psi_{k,k/2}(n)=c\phi_{k}(n)
. They proved that for
a\in\frac{k}{2}+\mathbb{Z}
the generating function of
c\psi_{k,a}(n)
,

C\Psi_{k,a}(q):=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}c\psi_{k,a}(n)q^{n},

is the
\zeta^{a}
coefficient of

(1.3)

F_{k}(\tau,z)=\left(\frac{-\vartheta(\tau,z+\frac{1}{2})}{q^{\frac{1}{12}}\eta(\tau)}\right)^{k},

where
q:=\mathfrak{e}\left(\tau\right):=e^{2\pi i\tau}
,
\zeta:=\mathfrak{e}\left(z\right)
,
\tau\in\mathfrak{H}
(the upper half plane),
z\in\mathbb{C}
,

(1.4)

\eta(\tau):=q^{\frac{1}{24}}\prod_{j=1}^{\infty}(1-q^{j}),

and (see Example
2.8
)

\vartheta(\tau,z):=\sum_{n\in\frac{1}{2}+\mathbb{Z}}\mathfrak{e}\left(\frac{1}{2}n^{2}\tau+n\left(z+\frac{1}{2}\right)\right).

Garvan, Sellers and Smoot
[
11
]
found that the property of
c\psi_{2,0}
modulo powers of
5
is similar to (
1.2
). They obtained

(1.5)

\displaystyle c\psi_{2,\beta}(n)\equiv 0\pmod{5^{\alpha}},

where
\beta\in\{0,1\}
and
3\cdot 2^{\beta+1}n\equiv(-1)^{\beta+1}\pmod{5^{\alpha}}
. (For
\beta=1
, this is just (
1.2
).) Furthermore, they realized that the two families of congruences (
1.5
) bear a strange symmetry between each other via the application of certain Atkin-Lehner involution to their generating functions. This profound observation led them recognize that one family of congruences may imply other families via certain modular transformations. At the end of their paper, they emphasized five considerations for future work. The first three read as follows:

(1)

Is our system of congruences for
c\psi_{2,\beta}(n)
complete for
\beta=0,1
? That is to say, is this pair part of a broader triple or quadruple of families all closely related via their proofs or their behavior through some associated Atkin–Lehner involutions? Are there perhaps other mappings similar to
\sigma
which would relate these families to others? Indeed, we believe that this specific pair of congruence families is not so closely related to any others in the same way that they are to each other—but we cannot be certain.

(2)

What are the implications of identifying different congruence families with one another via homomorphisms which fix certain sub-module function spaces?

(3)

Do similar systems of congruence families exist for more general
c\psi_{k,\beta}(n)
, especially by holding a given
k
fixed and varying
\beta
?

The main purpose of this paper is to address the problems mentioned above.

1.1.
Vector-valued modular transformation laws of
C\Psi_{k,\beta}

In the viewpoint of modular transformations, the functions
c\psi_{2,\beta}(n)
where
\beta=0,1
are complete since their generating functions constitute a weakly holomorphic vector-valued modular form with respect to
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
. This remains true for general
c\psi_{k,\beta}(n)
. Let
f_{k,\beta}(\tau)=q^{\frac{k}{12}-\frac{\beta^{2}}{2k}}C\Psi_{k,\beta}(q)
and define

\mathfrak{B}_{k}=\begin{cases}\left\{0,1,2,\dots,\frac{k}{2}\right\}&\text{ if }2\mid k,\\
\left\{\frac{1}{2},\frac{3}{2},\frac{5}{2}\dots,\frac{k}{2}\right\}&\text{ if }2\nmid k.\\
\end{cases}

Our first result establishes the vector-valued modular transformation laws of
f_{k,\beta}(\tau)
by given the explicit coefficients under the actions of
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{1}&{1}\\
{0}&{1}\end{smallmatrix}\right)
and
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{0}&{-1}\\
{1}&{0}\end{smallmatrix}\right)
:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
,
\beta\in\mathfrak{B}_{k}
. Then we have

(1.6)

\displaystyle f_{k,\beta}|_{-\frac{1}{2}}\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{1}&{1}\\
{0}&{1}\end{smallmatrix}\right)

\displaystyle=\mathfrak{e}\left(\frac{k}{12}-\frac{\beta^{2}}{2k}\right)f_{k,\beta},

(1.7)

\displaystyle f_{k,\beta}|_{-\frac{1}{2}}\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{0}&{-1}\\
{1}&{0}\end{smallmatrix}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\beta^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{B}_{k}}s_{\beta,\beta^{\prime}}^{(k)}\cdot f_{k,\beta^{\prime}},

where

s_{\beta,\beta^{\prime}}^{(k)}=\mu_{\beta^{\prime}/k}\cdot\mathrm{i}^{2\beta}\mathfrak{e}\left(\frac{2k+1}{8}\right)\frac{2}{\sqrt{k}}\cdot\cos\left(\frac{2\uppi\beta^{\prime}(\beta+\tfrac{k}{2})}{k}\right),

\mu_{t}=1/2
if
t=0
or
1/2
and
\mu_{t}=1
else.

The notation
f|_{-\frac{1}{2}}\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{a}&{b}\\
{c}&{d}\end{smallmatrix}\right)
means the weight
-\tfrac{1}{2}
modular transformation, that is,
f|_{-\frac{1}{2}}\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{a}&{b}\\
{c}&{d}\end{smallmatrix}\right)(\tau):=(c\tau+d)^{1/2}f\left(\frac{a\tau+b}{c\tau+d}\right)
where
(c\tau+d)^{1/2}
is the principal branch (the argument belongs to
(-\tfrac{\uppi}{2},\tfrac{\uppi}{2}]
). Since
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
is generated by
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{1}&{1}\\
{0}&{1}\end{smallmatrix}\right)
and
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{0}&{-1}\\

we can calculate

{c}&{d}\end{smallmatrix}\right)
for any

{c}&{d}\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
by first expanding
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{a}&{b}\\
{c}&{d}\end{smallmatrix}\right)
as a product or quotient of
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{1}&{1}\\
{0}&{1}\end{smallmatrix}\right)
and
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{0}&{-1}\\
{1}&{0}\end{smallmatrix}\right)
and then applying repeatedly the two formulas in Theorem
1.1
. Therefore,
(f_{k,\beta})_{\beta\in\mathfrak{B}_{k}}
is a
\mathbb{C}^{\lfloor k/2\rfloor+1}
-valued weakly holomorphic modular form with respect to
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
of weight
-1/2
. For the proof, see Section
3
.

1.2.
A unified tool to relate congruence families

The modular transformation
f_{k,\beta}|_{-\frac{1}{2}}\gamma
, when
\gamma
belongs to the subgroup
\Gamma_{0}(k)
(see (
1.16
)), results in a single term
f_{k,\beta^{\prime}}
up to a constant factor, and possibly with a different
\beta^{\prime}
. This phenomenon is studied in detail in Section
4
where the main result is Theorem
4.2
. The following theorem, which is a direct consequence of Theorem
4.2
, demonstrates how modular transformations map the modular function associated with one generalized Frobenius partition to the function associated with another, and it serves as a unified tool to identify different congruence families with one another:

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
p\geq 5
be prime and
\beta,\beta^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{B}_{k}
with
(2\beta,k)=(2\beta^{\prime},k)
. Set

(1.8)

r=\frac{k}{(k,(2\beta)^{2}(p^{2}-1)/8)},\quad r_{e}=\mathop{\mathrm{lcm}}(2,r).

Then there exists
\gamma\in\Gamma_{0}^{0}(p^{2}k,r)
(see (
1.17
)) such that

(1.9)

\frac{f_{k,\beta}(\tau)}{f_{k,\beta}(p^{2}\tau)}\bigg|\gamma=\frac{f_{k,\beta^{\prime}}(\tau)}{f_{k,\beta^{\prime}}(p^{2}\tau)}.

Moreover, if
(r,p)=1
, then we can further require that

(1.10)

U_{p}^{\prime}(L|\gamma)=U_{p}^{\prime}(L)|\gamma

for all modular functions
L
with respect to
\Gamma_{1}^{*}(2p^{2}k,r)
(see (
1.19
)) where

(1.11)

U_{p}^{\prime}(L):=\frac{1}{p}\sum_{x=0}^{p-1}L\left(\frac{\tau+r_{e}x}{p}\right).

In above and throughout the paper,
(m,n)
denotes the greatest common divisor,
\mathop{\mathrm{lcm}}(m,n)
the least common multiple and
f|\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{a}&{b}\\
{c}&{d}\end{smallmatrix}\right)(\tau):=f\left(\frac{a\tau+b}{c\tau+d}\right)
. (However, the notation
f|\gamma
has a different meaning in Section
6.3
.)

For any
k,p,\beta
, and
\beta^{\prime}
in Theorem
1.3
we can calculate
\gamma
explicitly. For example, for
k=2
,
p=5
,
\beta=1
and
\beta^{\prime}=0
the modular transformation

\gamma=\begin{pmatrix}27&7\\
50&13\end{pmatrix}

maps
c\psi_{2,1}(n)
to
c\psi_{2,0}(n)
, which has been found in
[
11
]
in a different manner.

Theorem
1.3
also answers that for any fixed
k
, similar congruence families do exist for
c\psi_{k,\beta}(n)
with
\beta
varying, at least when one of the congruence families is related to some explicit modular functions. Indeed, the condition
(2\beta,k)=(2\beta^{\prime},k)
determines an equivalence relation for a given
k
. Theorem
1.3
then guarantees that all
c\psi_{k,\beta}(n)
are related to each other within the same equivalence class. See Theorem
5.20
for details.

For the proof of Theorem
1.3
and the construction of
\gamma
, see Section
5.2
.

As
f_{k,k/2}
is the generating function of Andrews’
k
-colored Frobenius partition, the case
\beta^{\prime}=k/2
is important. If in addition
k\equiv 2\pmod{4}
and
\beta=0
, we have a perfect choice of
\gamma
, which is related to certain Atkin-Lehner involution and is a direct generalization of
[
11
, Eq. (3.37)]
where
k=2
is considered. See Proposition
5.17
.

We note that there is a precedent in
[
6
]
for using vector-valued modular transformations to correspond two families of congruences.

1.3.
A new approach for proving Ramanujan-type congruences

Even if
c\psi_{k,\beta}(n)
and
c\psi_{k,\beta^{\prime}}(n)
fall into different equivalence classes, they may correspond to each other under some linear combination of modular transformations. This seems to provide a new approach for proving Ramanujan-type congruences. As an example, we consider the case
k=3
.

Theorem 1.4
.

For integers
\alpha>0
and
n\geq 0
, we have

(1.12)

\displaystyle c\psi_{3,1/2}(5^{\alpha}n+\delta_{\alpha})\equiv 0\pmod{5^{\lfloor\frac{\alpha}{2}\rfloor}},

(1.13)

\displaystyle c\psi_{3,3/2}(5^{\alpha}n+\lambda_{\alpha})\equiv 0\pmod{5^{\lfloor\frac{\alpha}{2}\rfloor}},

where

\delta_{2\alpha}=\delta_{2\alpha+1}=\frac{5^{2\alpha+1}-5}{24},

\displaystyle\lambda_{2\alpha-1}=\frac{3\cdot 5^{2\alpha-1}+1}{8},

\displaystyle\lambda_{2\alpha}=\frac{7\cdot 5^{2\alpha}+1}{8}.

One surprising aspect is that proving the congruences (
1.12
) is typically straightforward if one follows the method outlined in
[
15
]
, whereas there is a greater challenge to prove (
1.13
) in the traditional way. We find that the generating function of
c\psi_{3,1/2}(n)
is

(1.14)

C\Psi_{3,1/2}(q)=3q^{-\frac{5}{24}}\frac{\eta(3\tau)^{3}}{\eta(\tau)^{4}},

which shows that
C\Psi_{3,1/2}(q)/q^{5}C\Psi_{3,1/2}(q^{25})
is a holomorphic modular function on
\Gamma_{0}(75)
. Then one can prove (
1.12
) by working with the modular curve
X_{0}(15)
which has genus 1 and cusp count 4. However, we find that the generating function of
c\psi_{3,3/2}(n)
is

(1.15)

\displaystyle C\Psi_{3,3/2}(q)=q^{\frac{1}{8}}\left(\frac{1}{\eta(3\tau)}+9\frac{\eta(9\tau)^{3}}{\eta(3\tau)\eta(\tau)^{3}}\right).

The issue is that
q^{3}C\Psi_{3,3/2}(q)/C\Psi_{3,3/2}(q^{25})
has some poles on the upper half plane. Another common approach to deal with (
1.15
) is to shift the focus to
q^{-1/8}\eta(75\tau)C\Psi_{3,3/2}(q)
, which is a holomorphic modular function. However, in this viewpoint we need to work with the modular curve
X_{0}(45)
which has genus 3 and cusp count 8. To the best of our knowledge, there appears to be no literature addressing specifically the proof of Ramanujan-type congruences by working with such a complicated modular curve.

2.1.
Lattices and Gauss sums

Let
V
be a real vector space of dimension
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
equipped with a nondegenerate symmetric bilinear form
B
such that the associated quadratic form

(2.1)

Q(v):=\frac{1}{2}B(v,v)

is positive definite.
A
lattice
in
\underline{V}=(V,B)
is a free
\mathbb{Z}
-module of rank
n
that spans
V
. The
dual
of a lattice
L
, denoted by
L^{\sharp}
, is the module of vectors
v
such that
B(w,v)\in\mathbb{Z}
for all
w\in L
. It turns out that
L^{\sharp}
is still a lattice. By abuse of language, we call the pair
\underline{L}=(L,B)
a lattice as well. It is called
even integral
if
Q(v)\in\mathbb{Z}
for any
v\in L
, which implies that
L\subseteq L^{\sharp}
(in this case,
L
is called
integral
). Hereafter all lattices are assumed to be even integral.

The
discriminant module
of a lattice
L
is the quotient group
L^{\sharp}/L
which is a finite abelian group. There is a well-defined “quadratic form”
Q(v+L)=Q(v)+\mathbb{Z}
on
L^{\sharp}/L
that makes it a finite quadratic module (c.f.
[
19
, §2]
).

Example 2.1
.

Let
k
be a positive integer and
\underline{\mathbb{R}}_{k}
be the one-dimensional space
\mathbb{R}
equipped with the bilinear form
(x,y)\mapsto kxy
. Then both
\mathbb{Z}
and
2\mathbb{Z}
are integral lattices in
\underline{\mathbb{R}}_{k}
. To indicate the underlying bilinear forms, we write
\underline{\mathbb{Z}}_{k}
and
\underline{2\mathbb{Z}}_{k}
instead. If
k
is even then
\underline{\mathbb{Z}}_{k}
is even; otherwise if
k
is odd then only
\underline{2\mathbb{Z}}_{k}
is even. It is immediate that
\underline{\mathbb{Z}}_{k}^{\sharp}=\frac{1}{k}{\mathbb{Z}}
and
\underline{2\mathbb{Z}}_{k}^{\sharp}=\frac{1}{2k}{\mathbb{Z}}
; therefore, the discriminant modules
\underline{\mathbb{Z}}_{k}^{\sharp}/\underline{\mathbb{Z}}_{k}=\frac{1}{k}{\mathbb{Z}}/\mathbb{Z}
and
\underline{2\mathbb{Z}}_{k}^{\sharp}/\underline{2\mathbb{Z}}_{k}=\frac{1}{2k}{\mathbb{Z}}/2\mathbb{Z}
are both cyclic groups.

The lattices we need to deal with
C\Psi_{k,\beta}
are exactly
\underline{\mathbb{Z}}_{k}
for
k
even and
\underline{2\mathbb{Z}}_{k}
for
k
odd.

Next we recall some facts about (classical and generalized) Gauss sums. The classical Gauss sum is defined by
G(n,m)=\sum_{x=0}^{\lvert m\rvert-1}\mathfrak{e}\left(\frac{nx^{2}}{m}\right)
for
n\in\mathbb{Z}
and
m\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}
. It is known that in the case
m>0
and
(n,m)=1
we have

(2.2)

G(n,m)=\begin{cases}\sqrt{m}\genfrac{(}{)}{}{}{2n}{m}\mathfrak{e}\left(\frac{1-m}{8}\right),&\text{if }2\nmid m;\\
\sqrt{2m}\genfrac{(}{)}{}{}{2m}{n}\mathfrak{e}\left(\frac{n}{8}\right),&\text{if }m\equiv 0\bmod{4};\\
0,&\text{if }m\equiv 2\bmod{4}.\end{cases}

This is a reformulation of classical results due to Gauss (c.f.
[
2
, §1.5]
). The notation
\genfrac{(}{)}{}{}{\cdot}{\cdot}
refers to the Kronecker-Jacobi symbol. For the reader’s convenience we present its definition:

•

\genfrac{(}{)}{}{}{m}{p}
is the usual Legendre symbol if
p
is an odd prime.

•

\genfrac{(}{)}{}{}{m}{2}
equals

{1}&{0}\end{smallmatrix}\right)
and note that (being careful that we are dealing with the double cover
\widetilde{\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})}
especially when
2\mid k
)
(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{0}&{-1}\\
{1}&{0}\end{smallmatrix}\right),1)^{2}=(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{-1}&{0}\\
{0}&{-1}\end{smallmatrix}\right),1)
. This gives

h_{y}^{(k)}(\tau)|_{\frac{k-1}{2}}\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{0}&{-1}\\
{1}&{0}\end{smallmatrix}\right)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{4k}}\mathfrak{e}\left(\frac{k+1}{8}\right)\mathfrak{e}\left(\frac{ky}{2}\right)\sum_{t\in\frac{1}{2k}\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}}\mathfrak{e}\left(-kt\left(y+\frac{1}{2}\right)\right)h_{t}^{(k)}(\tau).

Ir follows from this and
h_{t}^{(k)}=h_{t+1}^{(k)}=h_{1-t}^{(k)}
that

h_{y}^{(k)}(\tau)|_{\frac{k-1}{2}}\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{0}&{-1}\\
{1}&{0}\end{smallmatrix}\right)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{4k}}\mathfrak{e}\left(\frac{k+1}{8}\right)\mathfrak{e}\left(\frac{ky}{2}\right)\sum_{t\in k^{-1}\mathfrak{B}_{k}}h_{t}^{(k)}(\tau)\mu_{t}\\
\cdot\left(\mathfrak{e}\left(-kt\left(y+\frac{1}{2}\right)\right)+\mathfrak{e}\left(-k(1-t)\left(y+\frac{1}{2}\right)\right)+\mathfrak{e}\left(-k(t+1)\left(y+\frac{1}{2}\right)\right)+\mathfrak{e}\left(-k(2-t)\left(y+\frac{1}{2}\right)\right)\right).

Setting in above
y=\beta/k
,
t=\beta^{\prime}/k
and then dividing the obtained identity by

\eta^{k}|_{\frac{k}{2}}\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{0}&{-1}\\
{1}&{0}\end{smallmatrix}\right)=\mathfrak{e}\left(-\frac{k}{8}\right)\eta^{k}

we arrive at (
1.7
) as desired.
∎

Corollary 3.1
.

Let
k
be an odd positive integer and
\beta\in\mathfrak{B}_{k}
. Then

(3.6)

f_{k,\beta}\big|_{-\frac{1}{2}}\left(\begin{matrix}{1}&{0}\\
{k}&{1}\end{matrix}\right)=\mathfrak{e}\left(\frac{k^{2}}{24}\right)f_{k,\beta}.

Proof.

Set
\mathbf{f}_{k}=(f_{k,\beta})_{\beta\in\mathfrak{B}_{k}}
, viewed as a column vector. Then Theorem
1.1
is equivalent to
(\eta^{k}\mathbf{f}_{k})|_{\frac{k-1}{2}}\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{1}&{1}\\
{0}&{1}\end{smallmatrix}\right)=M_{T}\cdot\eta^{k}\mathbf{f}_{k}
and
(\eta^{k}\mathbf{f}_{k})|_{\frac{k-1}{2}}\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{0}&{-1}\\
{1}&{0}\end{smallmatrix}\right)=M_{S}\cdot\eta^{k}\mathbf{f}_{k}
where
M_{T}
and
M_{S}
are certain square matrices. Moreover, let
M_{-I}
be the matrix such that
(\eta^{k}\mathbf{f}_{k})|_{\frac{k-1}{2}}\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{-1}&{0}\\
{0}&{-1}\end{smallmatrix}\right)=M_{-I}\cdot\eta^{k}\mathbf{f}_{k}
. We have

\left(\begin{matrix}{1}&{0}\\
{k}&{1}\end{matrix}\right)=\left(\begin{matrix}{-1}&{0}\\
{0}&{-1}\end{matrix}\right)\left(\begin{matrix}{0}&{-1}\\
{1}&{0}\end{matrix}\right)\left(\begin{matrix}{1}&{1}\\
{0}&{1}\end{matrix}\right)^{-k}\left(\begin{matrix}{0}&{-1}\\
{1}&{0}\end{matrix}\right).

Since the weight
\frac{k-1}{2}
is an integer, we need not lift the above matrix identity to the double cover
\widetilde{\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})}
. It follows that

(\eta^{k}\mathbf{f}_{k})|_{\frac{k-1}{2}}\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{1}&{0}\\
{k}&{1}\end{smallmatrix}\right)=M_{-I}M_{S}M_{T}^{-k}M_{S}\cdot\eta^{k}\mathbf{f}_{k}.

Since
\eta|_{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{1}&{1}\\
{0}&{1}\end{smallmatrix}\right)=\mathfrak{e}\left(\tfrac{1}{24}\right)\eta
and
2\nmid k
we find that
M_{T}^{-k}
is the identity matrix, and hence so is
M_{-I}M_{S}M_{T}^{-k}M_{S}
. In another words,
(\eta^{k}\mathbf{f}_{k})|_{\frac{k-1}{2}}\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{1}&{0}\\
{k}&{1}\end{smallmatrix}\right)=\eta^{k}\mathbf{f}_{k}
. This identity, multiplied by
\eta^{-k}|_{-\frac{k}{2}}\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{1}&{0}\\
{k}&{1}\end{smallmatrix}\right)=\mathfrak{e}\left(\tfrac{k^{2}}{24}\right)\eta^{-k}
(see (
2.14
)), implies the desired identity.
∎

Remark 3.2
.

The corresponding result in the case

f_{k,\beta}\big|_{-\frac{1}{2}}\left(\begin{matrix}{1}&{0}\\
{2k}&{1}\end{matrix}\right)=\mathfrak{e}\left(\frac{k^{2}}{12}\right)f_{k,\beta}.

4.2
, which includes (

) and (

) as special cases.

4.
Modular permutations of
C\Psi_{k,\beta}
under
\Gamma_{0}(k)

In this section, we demonstrate that if
\gamma\in\Gamma_{0}(k)
, then the transformation
f_{k,\beta}|_{-1/2}\gamma
results in a single term
f_{k,\beta^{\prime}}
up to a constant factor, and possibly with a different
\beta^{\prime}
. We also explicitly determine the constant and provide a formula for
\beta^{\prime}
.

To state the formula for
\beta^{\prime}
, we need some auxiliary notations. For a fixed
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
and
a\in\mathbb{Z}
, let
\overline{a}
denote the least nonnegative remainder of
a\bmod 2k
.

Definition 4.1
.

We define
\lambda^{(k)}\colon\tfrac{k}{2}+\mathbb{Z}\rightarrow\mathfrak{B}_{k}
by the formula

\lambda^{(k)}(\beta)=\begin{cases}\overline{2\beta}/2&\text{ if }0\leq\overline{2\beta}\leq k,\\
k-\overline{2\beta}/2&\text{ if }k<\overline{2\beta}<2k.\end{cases}

When
k
is fixed or implicitly understood, the superscript can be omitted:
\lambda(\beta)=\lambda^{(k)}(\beta)
.

The function
\lambda^{(k)}(\beta)
is designed such that
f_{k,\beta}=f_{k,\lambda(\beta)}
for
\beta\in\frac{k}{2}+\mathbb{Z}
. Moreover, we have
\lambda^{(k)}(k\pm\beta)=\lambda^{(k)}(\beta)
. Geometrically,
\lambda^{(k)}(\beta)
is the unique point in
\mathfrak{B}_{k}
that can be transformed from
\beta\in\tfrac{k}{2}+\mathbb{Z}
via a finite sequence of reflections about
k/2
or translations of period
k
.

Now we state the main theorem of this section.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
,
\beta\in\mathfrak{B}_{k}
and
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{a}&{b}\\
{c}&{d}\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in\Gamma_{0}(k)
. Then there exist
\beta^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{B}_{k}
and a root of unity
p_{\beta}=p_{\beta}\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{a}&{b}\\
{c}&{d}\end{smallmatrix}\right)
such that

f_{k,\beta}|_{-\frac{1}{2}}\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{a}&{b}\\
{c}&{d}\end{smallmatrix}\right)=p_{\beta}\cdot f_{k,\beta^{\prime}}.

In this formula,
\beta^{\prime}
is given by

(4.1)

\beta^{\prime}=T_{\beta}\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{a}&{b}\\
{c}&{d}\end{smallmatrix}\right):=\begin{cases}\lambda(a\beta)&\text{ if }2\mid k,\,2k\mid c,\\
\lambda(a\beta-\tfrac{k}{2})&\text{ if }2\mid k,\,2k\nmid c,\\
\lambda(a\beta)&\text{ if }2\nmid k,\,2\nmid a,\\
\lambda(a\beta-\tfrac{k}{2})&\text{ if }2\nmid k,\,2\mid a.\end{cases}

Moreover, when
2\mid c
then
p_{\beta}
is defined by

p_{\beta}\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{a}&{b}\\
{c}&{d}\end{smallmatrix}\right):=\genfrac{(}{)}{}{}{\sgn{d}kb}{\abs{d}}\cdot\mathfrak{e}\left(\frac{\lvert d\rvert-1}{8}\right)\cdot\mathrm{i}^{\frac{1-\mathop{\mathrm{sgn}}\left(d\right)}{2}\genfrac{(}{)}{}{}{c}{-1}}\cdot\mathfrak{e}\left(\frac{k(2ac-cd+2bd-2bdc^{2})}{24}-\frac{bd(a\beta)^{2}}{2k}\right);

when
2\nmid c
(in which case we must have
2\nmid k
),

p_{\beta}\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{a}&{b}\\
{c}&{d}\end{smallmatrix}\right):=\begin{cases}\mathfrak{e}\left(\frac{\mathop{\mathrm{sgn}}\left(c\right)k^{2}}{24}\right)\cdot p_{\beta}\left(\begin{matrix}{a}&{b}\\
{c-\mathop{\mathrm{sgn}}\left(c\right)ka}&{d-\mathop{\mathrm{sgn}}\left(c\right)kb}\end{matrix}\right)&\text{if }2\nmid a,\\
\mathfrak{e}\left(-\frac{k}{12}+\frac{\beta^{2}}{2k}+\frac{\mathop{\mathrm{sgn}}\left(c\right)k^{2}}{24}\right)\cdot p_{\beta}\left(\begin{matrix}{a_{1}}&{b_{1}}\\
{c_{1}}&{d_{1}}\end{matrix}\right)&\text{if }2\mid a,\end{cases}

where

\displaystyle a_{1}

\displaystyle=a+c,

\displaystyle b_{1}

\displaystyle=b+d,

(4.2)

\displaystyle c_{1}

\displaystyle=-\mathop{\mathrm{sgn}}\left(c\right)ka+(1-\mathop{\mathrm{sgn}}\left(c\right)k)c,

\displaystyle d_{1}

\displaystyle=-\mathop{\mathrm{sgn}}\left(c\right)kb+(1-\mathop{\mathrm{sgn}}\left(c\right)k)d.

Proof.

We divide the proof into five cases:

\displaystyle\text{(i) }2\mid k,\,2k\mid c,\qquad\text{(ii) }2\mid k,\,2k\nmid c,

\displaystyle\text{(iii) }2\nmid k,\,2\mid c,\qquad\text{(iv) }2\nmid k,\,2\nmid c,\,2\nmid a,\qquad\text{(v) }2\nmid k,\,2\nmid c,\,2\mid a.

Case (i).
Since

and
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{a}&{b}\\
{c}&{d}\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in\Gamma_{0}(k)

\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{a}&{b}\\
{c}&{d}\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in\Gamma_{0}(2)

2.20

(4.3)

h_{\beta/k}^{(k)}|_{\frac{k-1}{2}}\widetilde{\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{a}&{b}\\
{c}&{d}\end{smallmatrix}\right)}=\chi_{k}\widetilde{\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{a}&{b}\\
{c}&{d}\end{smallmatrix}\right)}\cdot\sum_{\beta^{\prime}\in\mathscr{B}_{k}}\mu_{\beta^{\prime}/k}\cdot\left(\rho_{\underline{Z}_{k}}^{*}\widetilde{\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{a}&{b}\\
{c}&{d}\end{smallmatrix}\right)}_{\frac{\beta}{k},\frac{\beta^{\prime}}{k}}+\rho_{\underline{Z}_{k}}^{*}\widetilde{\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{a}&{b}\\
{c}&{d}\end{smallmatrix}\right)}_{\frac{\beta}{k},\frac{k-\beta^{\prime}}{k}}\right)\cdot h_{\beta^{\prime}/k}^{(k)}.

Since

. Therefore, for

By the above identity, (

where we have used the fact

(4.7)

\rho_{\underline{Z}_{k}}\widetilde{\left(\begin{matrix}{a}&{b}\\

Now (

) follows from the above formula, the formula for

(see Lemma

Case (ii).
The proof is similar and will only be indicated briefly. First of all, (

h_{\beta/k}^{(k)}|_{\frac{k-1}{2}}\widetilde{\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{a}&{b}\\

The rest of the proof runs as case (i) with

\beta\in\mathbb{Z}

\cdot\left(\rho_{\underline{2Z}_{k}}^{*}\widetilde{\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{a}&{b}\\

{c}&{d}\end{smallmatrix}\right)}_{\frac{\beta}{k},\frac{k-\beta^{\prime}}{k}}+\rho_{\underline{2Z}_{k}}^{*}\widetilde{\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{a}&{b}\\

We split the proof into two subcases:

\tfrac{a\beta-\beta^{\prime}}{k}\in D_{\underline{2\mathbb{Z}}_{k}}[c]^{\bullet}\Longleftrightarrow\begin{cases}2\beta^{\prime}\equiv a\cdot 2\beta\pmod{4k}&\text{if }8k\mid c,\\

2\beta^{\prime}\equiv a\cdot 2\beta\pmod{2k}&\text{if }4k\nmid c.\end{cases}

Therefore, for all
\beta\in\mathfrak{B}_{k}
, there is exactly one (two resp.)

resp.) and its value can be calculated via (

If

(4.10)

\rho_{\underline{2Z}_{k}}\widetilde{\left(\begin{matrix}{a}&{b}\\

. Inserting the formula obtained into (

h_{\beta/k}^{(k)}|_{\frac{k-1}{2}}\widetilde{\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{a}&{b}\\

\displaystyle\mathscr{G}_{D_{\underline{2\mathbb{Z}}_{k}}}(-cd,j+2\mathbb{Z})

Proof.

5.1.
Commutativity of
U_{m}
operators and modular transformations

In this subsection we derive some identities of the type
(U_{m}f)|\gamma=U_{m}(f|\gamma^{\prime})
in broad generalities.

Definition 5.1
.

Let
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
and let
R
be a complete set of representatives of
\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}
. Set

U_{m}^{R}(f):=\frac{1}{m}\sum_{x\in R}f\left(\frac{\tau+x}{m}\right).

For
k\in\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{Z}
we have, by the definition,
U_{m}^{R}(f)=m^{\frac{k}{2}-1}\sum_{x\in R}f|_{k}\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{1}&{x}\\
{0}&{m}\end{smallmatrix}\right)
, which gives us some convenience since it translates some transformation problems into
2\times 2
matrix algebra. If
R=\{0,1,\dots,m-1\}
then
U_{m}^{R}
is the classical Atkin-Lehner
U_{m}
operator. Other choice of
R
, say
R=\{24\cdot 0,24\cdot 1,\dots,24\cdot(m-1)\}
, gives us flexibility to deal with various multiplier systems, e.g., the one in Proposition
4.5
.

In the rest of this subsection,
m
and
R
are fixed as above.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
\gamma=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{a}&{b}\\
{c}&{d}\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in\Gamma_{0}(m)
. Then for any
x\in R
, there exists a unique
y\in R
such that
(a+cx)y\equiv b+dx\bmod{m}
. Moreover, the assignment
x\mapsto y
gives a bijection on
R
.

Proof.

The existence and uniqueness of
y\in R
follow from the fact
(a+cx,m)=1
since
m\mid c
and
(a,m)=1
. Hence
x\mapsto y
is a well-defined map from
R
to itself. To prove the bijectivity, it suffices to prove the injectivity since
R
is a finite set. To this end, suppose
x_{1},x_{2}\in R
with the same image
y
. Then
(a+cx_{j})y\equiv b+dx_{j}\bmod{m}
for
j=1,2
. Subtracting them gives
(cy-d)(x_{1}-x_{2})\equiv 0\bmod{m}
. Since
(cy-d,m)=1
it follows that
x_{1}\equiv x_{2}\bmod{m}
and hence
x_{1}=x_{2}
for
R
is a complete set of representatives of
\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}
.
∎

The solution
y
is denoted by
\sigma_{\gamma}^{R}(x)
,
\sigma_{a,b,c,d}^{R}(x)
or just
\sigma(x)
below. Moreover, set
\sigma^{\prime}(x)=\sigma(x)-\sigma(0)
provided
0\in R
.

Definition 5.3
.

Suppose that
0\in R
. For
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{a}&{b}\\
{c}&{d}\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in\Gamma_{0}(m)
, set

C_{m}^{R}\left(\begin{matrix}{a}&{b}\\
{c}&{d}\end{matrix}\right)=\left(\begin{matrix}{a}&{m^{-1}(b-a\sigma(0))}\\
{mc}&{d-c\sigma(0)}\end{matrix}\right)=\left(\begin{matrix}{1}&{0}\\
{0}&{m}\end{matrix}\right)\left(\begin{matrix}{a}&{b}\\
{c}&{d}\end{matrix}\right)\left(\begin{matrix}{1}&{\sigma(0)}\\
{0}&{m}\end{matrix}\right)^{-1}.

The major technical fact about the “commutativity” between
U_{m}^{R}
and modular transformations is the following one.

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
k\in\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{Z}
,
\gamma=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{a}&{b}\\
{c}&{d}\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in\Gamma_{0}(m)
. Suppose
0\in R
. Set

\displaystyle a_{1}

\displaystyle=a_{1}(x)=1+c(a+cx)\sigma^{\prime}(x),

\displaystyle b_{1}

\displaystyle=b_{1}(x)=\frac{x-a(a+cx)\sigma^{\prime}(x)}{m},

\displaystyle c_{1}

\displaystyle=c_{1}(x)=mc^{2}\sigma^{\prime}(x),

\displaystyle d_{1}

\displaystyle=d_{1}(x)=1-ac\sigma^{\prime}(x).

If a function
f
satisfies
f|_{k}\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{a_{1}}&{b_{1}}\\
{c_{1}}&{d_{1}}\end{smallmatrix}\right)=f
for all
x\in R
, then

(U_{m}^{R}f)|_{k}\gamma=U_{m}^{R}(f|_{k}C_{m}^{R}\gamma).

(To see
b_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}
, one expands the definition of
\sigma(x)
and
\sigma(0)
and uses the fact
ad-bc=1
.)

Proof.

A straightforward calculation shows that in
\widetilde{\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})}

\widetilde{\left(\begin{matrix}{a_{1}}&{b_{1}}\\
{c_{1}}&{d_{1}}\end{matrix}\right)}\cdot\widetilde{C_{m}^{R}\left(\begin{matrix}{a}&{b}\\
{c}&{d}\end{matrix}\right)}=\widetilde{\left(\begin{matrix}{a+cx}&{r(x)}\\
{mc}&{d-c\sigma(x)}\end{matrix}\right)}

where
x\in R
and
r(x)=\tfrac{(b+dx)-(a+cx)\sigma(x)}{m}
. Since
f|_{k}\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{a_{1}}&{b_{1}}\\
{c_{1}}&{d_{1}}\end{smallmatrix}\right)=f
for all
x\in R
, it follows that

f\Big|_{k}\left(\begin{matrix}{a+cx}&{r(x)}\\
{mc}&{d-c\sigma(x)}\end{matrix}\right)=f\Big|_{k}C_{m}^{R}\left(\begin{matrix}{a}&{b}\\
{c}&{d}\end{matrix}\right)

for all
x\in R
. Therefore,

\displaystyle(U_{m}^{R}f)|_{k}\gamma

\displaystyle=m^{\frac{k}{2}-1}\sum_{x\in R}f|_{k}\widetilde{\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{1}&{x}\\
{0}&{m}\end{smallmatrix}\right)}\widetilde{\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{a}&{b}\\
{c}&{d}\end{smallmatrix}\right)}=m^{\frac{k}{2}-1}\sum_{x\in R}f|_{k}\widetilde{\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{a+cx}&{r(x)}\\
{mc}&{d-c\sigma(x)}\end{smallmatrix}\right)}\widetilde{\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{1}&{\sigma(x)}\\
{0}&{m}\end{smallmatrix}\right)}

\displaystyle=m^{\frac{k}{2}-1}\sum_{x\in R}(f|_{k}C_{m}^{R}\gamma)|_{k}\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{1}&{\sigma(x)}\\
{0}&{m}\end{smallmatrix}\right)=U_{m}^{R}(f|_{k}C_{m}^{R}\gamma).

In the last step we have used the fact
x\mapsto\sigma(x)
is a bijection; see Lemma
5.2
.
∎

Example 5.5
.

Set

G=\{\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{A}&{B}\\
{C}&{D}\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})\colon A\equiv D\equiv 1\bmod{24\lvert c\rvert},\,C\equiv 0\bmod{24mc^{2}},B\equiv 0\bmod{24}\}.

Then
G
is a subgroup of
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
and is contained in
\Gamma_{0}(24mc^{2})
. Suppose that
0\in R\subseteq 24\mathbb{Z}
and
m
is coprime to
6
. If
f
is a modular function on
G
, that is,
f\left(\frac{A\tau+B}{C\tau+D}\right)=f(\tau)
for any
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{A}&{B}\\
{C}&{D}\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in G
, then Lemma
5.4
is applicable to
f
(with
k=0
) since
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{a_{1}}&{b_{1}}\\
{c_{1}}&{d_{1}}\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in G
. Moreover, repeated applications of the lemma give

(U_{m}^{R}f)|(C_{m}^{R})^{r}\gamma=U_{m}^{R}(f|(C_{m}^{R})^{r+1}\gamma),\quad r\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}.

Besides, we will meet weakly holomorphic modular forms of weight
k=-1/2
to which Lemma
5.4
is applicable in Sections
6
. Thus, Lemma
5.4
is a very general tool.

It may happen that
f|_{k}(C_{m}^{R})^{r}\gamma=f
for sufficiently large

. The following lemma concerns the case

.

Lemma 5.6
.

Suppose
0\in R
. Let
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{a}&{b}\\
{c}&{d}\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in\Gamma_{0}(m)
. Let
y,z
be the unique elements in
R
satisfying

ay\equiv b\bmod{m},\qquad az\equiv\frac{b-ay}{m}\bmod{m}.

Then in the metaplectic group
\widetilde{\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})}
we have
\widetilde{(C_{m}^{R})^{2}\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{a}&{b}\\
{c}&{d}\end{smallmatrix}\right)}=\widetilde{\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{a^{\prime}}&{b^{\prime}}\\
{c^{\prime}}&{d^{\prime}}\end{smallmatrix}\right)}\cdot\widetilde{\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{a}&{b}\\
{c}&{d}\end{smallmatrix}\right)}
where

\displaystyle a^{\prime}

\displaystyle=1+\frac{(m^{2}-1)bc+ac(y+mz)}{m^{2}},

\displaystyle b^{\prime}

\displaystyle=-\frac{(m^{2}-1)ab+a^{2}(y+mz)}{m^{2}},

\displaystyle c^{\prime}

\displaystyle=(m^{2}-1)cd+c^{2}(y+mz),

\displaystyle d^{\prime}

\displaystyle=1-(m^{2}-1)bc-ac(y+mz).

Moreover, if
m
is coprime to
6
and

, then
((C_{m}^{R})^{2}\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{a}&{b}\\
{c}&{d}\end{smallmatrix}\right))\cdot\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{a}&{b}\\
{c}&{d}\end{smallmatrix}\right)^{-1}
is contained in the group

\Gamma_{1}^{*}(24\lvert c\rvert):=\{\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{A}&{B}\\
{C}&{D}\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})\colon A\equiv D\equiv 1\bmod{24\lvert c\rvert},\,C\equiv 0\bmod{24\lvert c\rvert},B\equiv 0\bmod{24}\}.

Proof.

A straightforward verification.
∎

Combining the general Lemmas

Proposition 5.7
.

Suppose
m

6
and

. Let

and

for all
\gamma\in\Gamma_{1}^{*}(24\lvert c\rvert)
. Then we have

(5.1)

(U_{m}^{R}f)|_{k}M=U_{m}^{R}(f|_{k}C_{m}^{R}M),\qquad(U_{m}^{R}f)|_{k}C_{m}^{R}M=U_{m}^{R}(f|_{k}M).

Proof.

For any fixed
x\in R
let
a_{1},b_{1},c_{1},d_{1}
(depending on

) be as in Lemma
5.4
. Then we have
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{a_{1}}&{b_{1}}\\
{c_{1}}&{d_{1}}\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in\Gamma_{1}^{*}(24\lvert c\rvert)
since

and
(m,6)=1

7.1.
On representations of
\widetilde{\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})}

Let

\beta\in\mathfrak{B}_{k}

amounts to saying that

\mathbf{f}_{k}|_{-1/2}\gamma=\rho_{k}(\gamma)\mathbf{f}_{k},\quad\gamma\in\widetilde{\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})}.

Proposition 7.1
.

The kernel of

has finite index in

Proof.

For each matrix

[\widetilde{\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})}\colon\ker\rho_{k}]\leq 2[\widetilde{\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})}\colon\widetilde{\Gamma(24k)}]<+\infty

Proposition 7.4

Dietary 3,4-Dihydroxybenzalacetone alleviates LPS-induced intestinal injury in Leizhou black ducks by improving mitochondrial function and suppressing apoptosis
Authors: Wei Yang, Wenao Ma, Yupin You, Yi Ma, Haiyan Wu, Zhibao Chen
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntestinal inflammation is a major health concern in poultry, highlighting the importance of developing effective alternatives to conventional antibiotics. 3,4-Dihydroxybenzalacetone (DBL), a polyphenolic compound extracted from Inonotus obliquus, exhibits anti-inflammatory and antioxidant properties. However, its specific effects on lipopolysaccharide (LPS)-induced intestinal inflammatory injury and the underlying mechanisms in ducks remain unknown. In this study, 42 healthy 7-day-old Leizhou black ducks were randomly divided into six groups (control, LPS, LPS+resveratrol (RES), LPS+DBL-1, LPS+DBL-2, and DBL). After 7 days of dietary supplementation, a 3-day intraperitoneal LPS injection was performed. DBL administration at dietary concentrations of 0.01% (LPS+DBL-1) and 0.04% (LPS+DBL-2) significantly alleviated LPS-induced intestinal injury in a dose-dependent manner. These protective effects included improved survival rate and body weight gain, reduced intestinal mucosal damage, and restored expression of tight junction proteins (Zonula Occludens-1 (ZO-1), Claudin-1, and Occludin). Furthermore, DBL not only attenuated LPS-induced mitochondrial membrane potential depolarization and reduced intestinal cell apoptosis but also ameliorated inflammation and oxidative stress. In addition, network pharmacology analysis combined with experimental validation indicated that the protective effect of DBL is closely associated with the inhibition of the cGAS-STING-mediated mitochondrial stress signaling pathway, the restoration of impaired mitophagy, and the suppression of apoptosis. Together, these findings demonstrate that dietary DBL alleviates LPS-induced intestinal injury in Leizhou black ducks through a multitarget mechanism, positioning it as a promising alternative to antibiotics for managing intestinal inflammation in poultry.Keywords: Leizhou black ducks, DBL, Intestinal injury, Mitochondrial function, Apoptosis

Introduction

Intestinal health is critical for poultry production, as the gut serves as the primary organ for nutrient absorption and immune defense (Amevor et al., 2025). Lipopolysaccharide (LPS), a major component of the outer membrane of gram-negative bacteria, is a key pathogenic factor that triggers intestinal inflammation and injury in poultry (Yuan et al., 2025). LPS disrupts intestinal barrier integrity, increases permeability, and induces excessive inflammatory responses, leading to reduced growth performance and increased susceptibility to secondary infections (Nighot et al., 2019; Xu et al., 2025a). Given the global pressure to reduce antibiotic use in animal agriculture, effective, safe alternatives for preventing and managing intestinal injury in poultry are urgently needed.

Mitochondrial dysfunction has emerged as a central mechanism in LPS-induced intestinal injury (Haque et al., 2024). LPS stimulation induces excessive reactive oxygen species (ROS) production, leading to mitochondrial DNA damage, loss of mitochondrial membrane potential (MMP), and impaired oxidative phosphorylation (Harland et al., 2020). Damaged mitochondria release mitochondrial DNA (mtDNA) into the cytoplasm, which activates the cyclic GMP - AMP synthase (cGAS)-stimulator of interferon genes (STING) pathway, triggering inflammatory responses (Decout et al., 2021). Concurrently, mitochondrial outer membrane permeabilization promotes the release of cytochrome c (CytC) and the activation of the BAX/BAK - Caspase cascade, resulting in apoptosis (Tait and Green, 2010). Mitophagy, a selective autophagic process that eliminates dysfunctional mitochondria, serves as a crucial quality control mechanism to maintain mitochondrial homeostasis and cell survival (Picca et al., 2023). The PTEN-induced kinase 1 (PINK1) - Parkin pathway is the primary mediator of mitophagy and targets damaged mitochondria for degradation (Agarwal and Muqit, 2022). Therefore, enhancing mitophagy has been proposed as a therapeutic strategy to mitigate inflammation and tissue injury (Xu et al., 2025b).

3,4-Dihydroxybenzalacetone (DBL) is a polyphenolic compound isolated from Inonotus obliquus (Chaga mushroom), a medicinal fungus traditionally used for its anti-inflammatory and antioxidant properties (Ikemoto et al., 2023). Previous studies have demonstrated that DBL exhibits cytoprotective effects by reducing oxidative stress, inhibiting apoptosis, and promoting autophagy in various cell types (Jiang et al., 2024; Yang et al., 2022). Notably, DBL has been shown to protect against LPS-induced acute lung injury by suppressing ROS-mediated MAPK and PI3K/AKT signaling (Chao et al., 2017) and to prevent aging-induced myocardial changes by reducing oxidative damage and inflammation (Giridharan et al., 2020). However, whether DBL can protect against LPS-induced intestinal injury in poultry and the underlying mechanisms remain unknown.

The Leizhou black duck is an indigenous duck breed in Guangdong Province, China, valued for its meat and egg production (Asiamah et al., 2020). However, ducks raised in coastal environments are frequently exposed to gram-negative bacterial pathogens (Bariha et al., 2019), leading to intestinal diseases that compromise productivity (Niu et al., 2020). Therefore, identifying effective interventions to protect intestinal health in this breed has practical significance.

In this study, we investigated the protective effects of DBL against LPS-induced intestinal injury in Leizhou black ducks and explored the underlying mechanisms involving mitochondrial dysfunction, apoptosis, and mitophagy. We hypothesized that DBL alleviated LPS-induced intestinal injury by restoring impaired mitophagy and suppressing aberrant mitochondrial stress signaling, thereby inhibiting apoptotic pathways. These results suggest that DBL may serve as a potential dietary intervention for protecting intestinal health in poultry under pathogenic challenge.

Materials and methods

Reagents

DBL (purity >98%) was isolated from Inonotus obliquus as previously described (Wei et al., 2022). LPS (from Escherichia coli O55:B5, Catalog No. L2880, Sigma-Aldrich, St. Louis, MO, USA) was dissolved in sterile saline. Resveratrol (RES, Catalog No. HY-16561, MedChemExpress, Monmouth Junction, NJ, USA) was dissolved in DMSO and further diluted with saline (final DMSO concentration <0.1%). Antibodies against cGAS (1:5000), STING1 (1:5000), TBK1 (1:1000), phospho-TBK1 (p-TBK1) (1:1000), IRF3 (1:5000), BAK1 (1:2000), BAX (1:5000), BCL-2 (1:1000), Caspase-3 (1:2000), Caspase-9 (1:2000), CytC (1:2000), PINK1 (1:1000), Parkin (1:2000), LC3 (1:2000), p62 (1:1000), ATG5 (1:1000), ATG7 (1:1000), NLRP3 (1:1000), Zonula Occludens-1 (ZO-1) (1:1000), Occludin (1:1000), Claudin-1 (1:1000), and β-actin (1:5000) were purchased from Proteintech (Rosemont, IL, USA) or Bioss (Beijing, China). ELISA kits for IL-18, IL-1β, IL-6, and TNF-α were obtained from Qisong Biotechnology (Beijing, China). Assay kits for NO, SOD, GSH-Px, MDA, ROS, and DAO were purchased from Nanjing Jiancheng Bioengineering Institute (Nanjing, China) or Solarbio (Beijing, China). JC-1 (Solarbio, Beijing, China) and apoptosis detection kits (Dojindo, Kumamoto, Japan) were used according to the manufacturers’ instructions.

Animals and experimental design

All animal experiments were approved by the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee of Guangdong Ocean University (IACUC No. GDOU‐NXYLAE‐2022‐DY004.1) and conducted in accordance with the guidelines for the care and use of agricultural animals in research and teaching.

Both male and female 1-day-old Leizhou black ducks were obtained from a local hatchery. The ducks were housed in an environmentally controlled facility with a temperature of 25 ± 2°C, relative humidity of 60 ± 5%, and a 12 h light/12 h dark cycle, with free access to water and a basal diet. The LPS dose (10 mg/kg body weight) was chosen based on a previous report establishing an LPS-induced inflammation model in poultry (Shi et al., 2024). The 7-day dietary supplementation period was chosen based on our preliminary experiments, which demonstrated this duration to be adequate for the dietary intervention to take effect in poultry. After a 7-d acclimatization period, the ducks were randomly divided into the following groups (n = 7 biological replicates per group, each biological replicate representing one individual duck): (1) Control group: fed a basal diet for 7 d, then injected intraperitoneally (i.p.) with sterile saline for 3 consecutive days; (2) LPS group: fed a basal diet for 7 d, then injected i.p. with LPS (10 mg/kg body weight) for 3 consecutive days; (3) DBL (0.01%) + LPS group: fed a diet supplemented with 0.01% DBL for 7 d, then injected with LPS as above; (4) DBL (0.04%) + LPS group: fed a diet supplemented with 0.04% DBL for 7 d, then injected with LPS as above; (5) RES (0.04%) + LPS group: fed a diet supplemented with 0.04% RES for 7 d, then injected with LPS as above; and (6) DBL (0.04%) group: fed a diet supplemented with 0.04% DBL for 7 d, then injected with saline. During the experimental period, animal survival was monitored daily, and only surviving animals were used for subsequent biochemical and molecular analyses.

After the final injection, the ducks were fasted for 12 h with free access to water. Blood samples were collected via cardiac puncture, and serum was separated by centrifugation at 3,000 rpm for 10 min. Ducks were then euthanized, and small intestinal tissue samples were collected. Portions of the intestine were fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde for histopathological analysis, and the remaining tissue was snap-frozen in liquid nitrogen and stored at -80°C for further analysis.

Histopathological examination

Intestinal tissue samples fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde were embedded in paraffin, sectioned at a thickness of 5 μm, and stained with hematoxylin and eosin (H&E). Histopathological changes were observed under a light microscope. Villus height and crypt depth were measured using ImageJ software, and the villus height/crypt depth ratio was calculated. Inflammatory scores were assessed based on leukocyte infiltration and mucosal damage as previously described (Kaur et al., 2021), with scores ranging from 0 (normal) to 4 (severe).

Measurement of mitochondrial membrane potential

Intestinal tissue was mechanically and digested with 0.25% trypsin-EDTA at 37°C for 30 min with gentle agitation every 10 min. The digested tissue was filtered through a 70 μm cell strainer. The resulting single-cell suspension was adjusted to 1 × 10⁶ cells/mL and incubated with JC-1 staining working solution at 37°C for 20 min. After the cells were washed with JC-1 buffer, fluorescence was observed under a fluorescence microscope (excitation, 515 nm; emission, 585 nm).

Apoptosis assay

Intestinal tissue was processed to obtain a single-cell suspension as described above (digestion with 0.25% trypsin-EDTA at 37°C for 30 min). Cells were incubated with 5 μL of Annexin V-FITC and 5 μL of propidium iodide (PI) at room temperature for 15 min in the dark. The apoptosis rate was analyzed by flow cytometry.

RNA extraction and RT‒qPCR

Total RNA was extracted from duck intestinal tissue using TRIzol reagent according to the manufacturer’s protocol. For RT-qPCR analysis, tissue samples from at least three randomly selected surviving ducks per group were used as representative subsets. The RNA concentration and purity were determined using a spectrophotometer. cDNA was synthesized using a HiScript III RT SuperMix for qPCR (+gDNA wiper) kit. Real-time quantitative PCR was performed using ChamQ Universal SYBR qPCR Master Mix on a CFX96 real-time PCR system (Bio-Rad, Hercules, CA, USA). The cycling conditions were 95°C for 30 s, followed by 40 cycles of 95°C for 10 s and 60°C for 30 s. Relative gene expression was calculated using the 2−ΔΔCt method with β-actin as the internal control. The primer sequences are listed in Table 1.

Western Blot Analysis

Total protein was extracted from duck intestinal tissue using RIPA lysis buffer supplemented with protease and phosphatase inhibitors. For Western blot analysis, tissue samples from at least three randomly selected surviving ducks per group were used as representative subsets. The protein concentration was determined using the BCA assay. Equal amounts of protein (30 μg) were separated by SDS‒PAGE and transferred to PVDF membranes. The membranes were blocked with 5% nonfat milk for 2 h at room temperature and incubated overnight at 4°C with primary antibodies. After being washed, the membranes were incubated with HRP-conjugated secondary antibodies for 2 h at room temperature. Protein bands were visualized using an enhanced chemiluminescence kit and detected with a Tanon-4600 imaging system. Band intensities were quantified using ImageJ software.

Inflammatory cytokine and NO measurements

Duck serum/intestinal tissue homogenates were collected, and the levels of IL-18, IL-1β, IL-6, TNF-α, and NO were measured using ELISA and NO assay kits according to the manufacturers’ instructions.

Oxidative stress parameters

Duck serum/intestinal tissue homogenates were collected. SOD activity, GSH-Px activity and MDA content were measured using commercial assay kits. ROS levels were detected using a DCFH-DA fluorescent probe. Fluorescence intensity was measured using a fluorescence microplate reader (488 nm excitation and 525 nm emission).

Network pharmacology analysis

Target genes of DBL were identified via the the Traditional Chinese Medicine Systems Pharmacology Database (TCMSP, https://www.tcmsp-e.com/) and SwissTargetPrediction database (http://www.swisstargetprediction.ch/). To identify protein-coding genes associated with intestinal injury, searches were performed across the following databases with the keyword “Intestinal injury”: GeneCards (http://www.genecards.org/), DisGeNET (https://www.disgenet.org/), Online Mendelian Inheritance in Man (OMIM, http://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/omim), and DrugBank (https://go.drugbank.com/). Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathway enrichment analyses of the identified target genes were conducted using the DAVID database (https://david.ncifcrf.gov/home.jsp). Data processing and visualization were performed using the microorganism online analysis platform (https://www.bioinformatics.com.cn) and Cytoscape 3.8.0 software.

Statistical analysis

Data are presented as the mean ± standard deviation (SD). Statistical analyses were performed using GraphPad Prism 8.0 software. For comparisons involving more than two groups, one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) was used, followed by Tukey's post hoc multiple comparison test. For comparisons between two specific groups, an independent sample t-test (two-tailed) was used. A significance level of α = 0.05 was applied, and differences were considered statistically significant at P < 0.05.

Results

DBL ameliorates LPS-induced intestinal injury in Leizhou Black Ducks

To evaluate the protective effects of DBL against intestinal injury in vivo, a Leizhou black duck model of LPS-induced intestinal injury was established (Fig. 1A). Dietary administration of 0.04% DBL significantly alleviated symptoms of intestinal inflammatory injury. Body weight and the survival rate were the lowest in the LPS group, whereas both parameters were markedly improved in the DBL-treated groups. Notably, compared with the LPS group, dietary administration of 0.01% and 0.04% DBL significantly improved survival rates, with the most pronounced body weight gain observed in the 0.04% dietary DBL-treated group (Fig. 1B-C, p < 0.01). In addition, H&E staining revealed that LPS exposure caused severe intestinal damage, disrupting the intestinal mucosa, causing necrotic and detached epithelial cells, and exposing the lamina propria. DBL treatment led to marked improvement, characterized by largely intact intestinal mucosa, uniformly distributed glands, reduced epithelial cell desquamation, and less exposure of the lamina propria (Fig. 1D). These findings indicate that dietary supplementation with DBL effectively alleviates LPS-induced injury in Leizhou black ducks.

As critical structural components of the intestinal mucosal barrier, ZO-1, Claudin-1, and Occludin are essential for maintaining epithelial tight junctions and preventing paracellular permeability (Zhang et al., 2022). Western blot analysis revealed that LPS-induced injury significantly decreased the protein levels of ZO-1, Claudin-1, and Occludin in the intestinal tissue of Leizhou black ducks (p < 0.001). Notably, compared with the LPS group, the 0.04% dietary DBL group presented the most pronounced effect, with significantly increased protein levels of ZO-1, Claudin-1, and Occludin (Fig. 1E-F, p < 0.001). These results suggest that dietary DBL restores the expression of the intestinal tight junction proteins ZO-1, Claudin-1, and Occludin, thereby effectively repairing the damaged intestinal mucosal barrier structure.

DBL ameliorates LPS-induced intestinal inflammation and oxidative stress in Leizhou black ducks

To evaluate the effects of DBL on the LPS-induced inflammatory response and oxidative stress in the intestinal tissue of Leizhou black ducks, the serum levels of inflammatory cytokines and oxidative stress markers were measured. As shown in Fig. 2A-E, compared with the control treatment, LPS treatment significantly increased the serum levels of IL-18, IL-1β, IL-6, TNF-α, and NO (p < 0.001). However, treatment with DBL (0.01% and 0.04%) effectively reduced the levels of these inflammatory mediators. LPS exposure also induced significant oxidative stress, as evidenced by decreased serum SOD and GSH-Px activities and increased MDA content compared with those in the control group (p < 0.001). DBL treatment effectively reversed these changes by increasing SOD and GSH-Px activities and reducing the MDA content (p < 0.001) (Fig. 2F-H). Furthermore, ROS fluorescence intensity analysis revealed that LPS exposure led to a marked increase in ROS accumulation (p < 0.001), whereas DBL treatment significantly attenuated this effect (Fig. 2I). Collectively, these findings demonstrate that DBL effectively alleviates LPS-induced intestinal inflammation and oxidative stress in Leizhou black ducks, as evidenced by reduced proinflammatory cytokine and NO levels, increased antioxidant enzyme activity, and attenuated ROS production.

Network pharmacology results

To further elucidate the protective mechanism of DBL against intestinal injury, the network pharmacology analysis was used to predict the potential targets and signaling pathways. The genes related to intestinal injury and DBL were obtained by a bioinformatics database, and 78 overlapping genes were taken for subsequent analysis (Fig. 3A). The PPI network of 78 overlapping genes was constructed to screen out the key targets. The PPI results exhibited complex interactions among targets, including 58 nodes and 269 edges (Fig. 3B). Then, the PPI network was modified using Cytoscape software. A deeper color represents a higher degree value, indicating a more significant protective effect of DBL against intestinal injury (Fig. 3C). The protein degree values of the PPI network were evaluated, and the top 10 ranked targets were BCL2, IL6, STAT3, CASP3, TLR4, MYD88, CASP9, TRAF6, SQSTM1 and IL10. Of these, BCL2 was found to be one of the most relevant targets (Fig. 3D).

(D) Identification of the top ten core genes on the based on the PPI interactions. (E) GO enrichment analysis of the overlapping genes between DBL and intestinal injury. Gene Ontology (GO) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) analysis were performed using the DAVID database to identify further the functions and signaling pathways associated with DBL and intestinal injury. GO analysis showed that the protective mechanism of DBL involves the regulation of apoptotic process and mitochondrial autophagy in the biological process (BP), mitochondrial outer membrane in the cellular component (CC), and DNA-binding transcription factor activity in the molecular function (MF) (Fig. 3E). The Sankey-Bubble plot revealed the enriched pathways, including Cytosolic DNA-sensing pathway, Mitophagy (animal), and Apoptosis (Fig. 3F). Taken together, GO and KEGG enrichment analyses indicated that DBL may alleviate intestinal injury by improving mitochondrial function and suppressing apoptosis.

DBL ameliorates LPS-induced mitochondrial membrane potential depolarization and apoptosis in Leizhou Black Ducks

Given that network pharmacology analysis indicated that DBL could ameliorate intestinal inflammation through modulation of mitochondrial function, the effect of DBL on LPS-induced changes in the mitochondrial membrane potential (MMP) in the intestinal tissue of Leizhou black ducks was investigated using JC-1 staining. As shown in Fig. 4A, compared with the control group, the LPS group exhibited increased green fluorescence (JC-1 monomers) and markedly decreased red fluorescence (JC-1 aggregates), indicating a significant decrease in the MMP. DBL treatment reversed these effects, as evidenced by increased red fluorescence and decreased green fluorescence, suggesting that DBL effectively restored the MMP. Notably, the protective effect was more pronounced in the 0.04% DBL group than in the RES group. These results indicate that DBL attenuates LPS-induced MMP depolarization and preserves mitochondrial function in intestinal tissue.

To verify the network pharmacology analysis results and evaluate the effect of DBL on LPS-induced intestinal cell apoptosis, Annexin V/PI double staining was performed using flow cytometry (Fig. 4B). Compared with the control treatment, LPS exposure significantly increased the early and late apoptotic rates (LR and UR), accompanied by compromised plasma membrane integrity (LL). However, compared with LPS treatment alone, treatment with 0.01% and 0.04% DBL significantly reduced both early and late apoptotic rates, increased the proportion of viable cells, and restored plasma membrane integrity. Notably, the percentage of apoptotic cells in the 0.04% DBL group was 6.07%, which was lower than that in the RES group at the same concentration (Fig. 4C). Collectively, these findings demonstrate that compared with RES at a concentration of 0.04%, DBL effectively protects against LPS-induced mitochondrial dysfunction and apoptosis in the intestinal tissue of Leizhou black ducks.

DBL ameliorates LPS-induced intestinal injury by suppressing mitochondrial stress signaling and restoring mitophagy in Leizhou Black Ducks

Previous studies have demonstrated that the cGAS-STING signaling pathway plays a critical role in mediating mitochondrial stress-induced inflammatory responses and intestinal damage (Ding et al., 2025; Liu et al., 2024). Therefore, to investigate the effect of DBL on mitochondrial stress-related signaling pathways in LPS-induced intestinal injury, Western blotting and RT-qPCR were performed. As shown in Fig. 5A-B, compared with those in the control group, the protein levels of cGAS, STING1, and IRF3 and the phosphorylation level of TBK1 (p-TBK1) were markedly increased (p < 0.001). Notably, DBL treatment (0.01% and 0.04%) significantly reduced these protein parameters, with the 0.04% DBL group showing superior efficacy compared with the RES group. Consistent with the Western blot results, RT-qPCR analysis revealed that LPS significantly increased the mRNA levels of cGAS and STING1 (p < 0.001), and DBL treatment significantly reversed this trend (p < 0.05) (Fig. 5C). These findings indicate that DBL suppresses LPS-induced cGAS-STING mitochondrial stress signaling.

Impaired mitophagy is closely associated with the disruption of mitochondrial homeostasis and the progression of intestinal inflammatory diseases (Haque et al., 2024). Therefore, to further explore the effect of DBL on mitophagy, a key mitochondrial quality control mechanism, RT-qPCR and Western blotting were performed to detect mitophagy-related genes and proteins. Compared with those in the control group, LPS exposure significantly increased the mRNA expression levels of p62 and NLRP3 (p < 0.001), whereas the mRNA expression levels of Parkin, ATG5, PINK1, and LC3 were markedly decreased (p < 0.001). DBL treatment effectively ameliorated these alterations (Fig. 5D-E). Consistently, Western blot analysis revealed that LPS significantly increased the protein levels of p62 and NLRP3 (p < 0.001) and decreased the protein levels of Parkin, ATG5, PINK1, LC3, and ATG7 (p < 0.001). Treatment with 0.01% and 0.04% DBL upregulated the expression of mitophagy-promoting proteins and reduced p62 and NLRP3 protein levels (Fig. 5F-G). Collectively, these results demonstrate that DBL protects against LPS-induced intestinal injury by suppressing aberrant mitochondrial stress signaling (cGAS-STING) and restoring impaired mitophagy, thereby preserving mitochondrial homeostasis in Leizhou black ducks.

DBL suppresses LPS-induced intestinal injury by inhibiting cell apoptosis signaling in Leizhou Black Ducks

Cell apoptosis signaling pathway plays a pivotal role in regulating intestinal epithelial cell death and contributes to the pathogenesis of intestinal injury (Ma et al., 2021; Perochon et al., 2021). Thus, to investigate the effect of DBL on apoptosis signaling in LPS-induced intestinal injury in Leizhou black ducks, RT-qPCR and Western blot analyses were performed to detect the expression of apoptosis-related genes and proteins. As shown in Fig. 6A and C, compared with those in the control group, LPS treatment significantly elevated protein levels of the pro-apoptotic proteins BAK1, BAX, Caspase-3, Caspase-9, and CytC (p < 0.001), whereas the expression of the anti-apoptotic protein BCL-2 was markedly reduced (p < 0.001). Notably, treatment with 0.04% DBL significantly suppressed the protein expression of BAK1, BAX, Caspase-3, Caspase-9, and CytC but promoted BCL-2 expression (p < 0.001). Consistent with the Western blot results, RT‒qPCR analysis revealed that LPS exposure significantly increased the mRNA expression levels of BAK1, Caspase-3, Caspase-9, and CytC (p < 0.001). Treatment with DBL (0.01% and 0.04%) effectively reversed these trends (Fig. 6B). Collectively, these findings demonstrate that DBL effectively inhibits apoptosis signaling, thereby reducing LPS-induced intestinal cell apoptosis in Leizhou black ducks.

Discussion

Intestinal injury caused by gram-negative bacterial infections remains a significant challenge in poultry production, driving the need for effective antibiotic alternatives. In this study, we demonstrated that DBL, a polyphenolic compound from Inonotus obliquus, protects against LPS-induced intestinal injury in Leizhou black ducks by improving mitochondrial function and suppressing apoptosis. These findings provide new insights into the mechanisms by which natural products can preserve intestinal health in poultry.

The intestinal epithelium serves as a physical barrier against luminal pathogens and toxins, and its integrity is essential for maintaining health (Neurath et al., 2025). LPS disrupts the expression of tight junction proteins, including ZO-1, Occludin, and Claudin-1, leading to increased intestinal permeability and bacterial translocation (Zheng et al., 2022). Our results revealed that DBL preserved tight junction protein expression, consistent with histopathological evidence of preserved villus structure. These findings align with previous reports on the barrier-protective effects of polyphenolic compounds (Bai et al., 2026).

Mitochondrial dysfunction plays a central role in LPS-induced intestinal injury (Haque et al., 2024). LPS stimulation triggers excessive ROS production, leading to mitochondrial DNA damage, loss of the MMP, and impaired energy metabolism (Liang et al., 2022). Critically, damaged mitochondria initiate a cascade of detrimental events: they release mtDNA into the cytoplasm, which activates the cGAS-STING pathway and drives inflammatory gene expression, while simultaneously promoting the release of pro-apoptotic factors such as CytC (Decout et al., 2021; Marchi et al., 2023). To counteract these threats, cells employ mitophagy, a selective autophagic process that eliminates dysfunctional mitochondria and serves as a frontline defense mechanism (Picca et al., 2023). The PINK1-Parkin pathway is the primary mediator of mitophagy, targeting damaged mitochondria for degradation before they can trigger inflammation or apoptosis (Li et al., 2021a; Li et al., 2021b). Thus, the balance between mitochondrial damage and mitophagy dictates the extent of cellular injury. Our results demonstrated that DBL effectively preserved MMP, reduced ROS accumulation, and restored antioxidant enzyme activity. Moreover, DBL suppressed the activation of the cGAS-STING pathway and enhanced PINK1-Parkin-mediated mitophagy. Importantly, DBL also reduced NLRP3 inflammasome expression, which may be directly linked to enhanced mitophagy, since damaged mitochondria are a major source of NLRP3 activators such as mtDNA and ROS (Litwiniuk et al., 2021; Yuan et al., 2023). Collectively, these findings indicate that DBL protects against LPS-induced intestinal injury by restoring mitophagic flux, thereby limiting both cGAS-STING-mediated inflammation and NLRP3 inflammasome activation.

Apoptosis is a major consequence of mitochondrial dysfunction and contributes to intestinal epithelial cell loss under inflammatory conditions (Kim et al., 2025; Wang et al., 2025). LPS triggers the intrinsic apoptotic pathway by promoting BAX/BAK oligomerization on the mitochondrial outer membrane, leading to CytC release and Caspase-9/-3 activation (Chen et al., 2025; Heilig et al., 2023). We found that DBL suppressed the LPS-induced upregulation of the expression of pro-apoptotic proteins (BAK1, BAX, CytC, Caspase-9, and Caspase-3) and increased the expression of the anti-apoptotic protein BCL-2, which was consistent with the reduced apoptosis rates detected by flow cytometry. These findings corroborate studies showing that polyphenols can inhibit apoptosis (Doan et al., 2019; Zhang et al., 2023).

The relationships among mitophagy, mitochondrial stress signaling, and apoptosis are hierarchical rather than parallel. Mitophagy serves as a proximal protective mechanism that removes damaged mitochondria before they can release mtDNA or pro-apoptotic factors (Tanriover et al., 2023). When mitophagy is impaired, damaged mitochondria accumulate, leading to mtDNA release and cGAS-STING activation, as well as outer membrane permeabilization and BAX-mediated apoptosis (Liu et al., 2025). This temporal sequence positions mitophagy as an upstream event that determines the extent of both inflammatory signaling and cell death. Our results indicated that DBL treatment enhanced mitophagy, which was associated with reduced cGAS-STING activation and diminished apoptosis. Therefore, DBL suppresses mitochondrial damage, inflammation, and epithelial cell loss by promoting mitophagy, thereby preserving intestinal integrity.

Several limitations of this study should be acknowledged. First, although we demonstrated the involvement of the PINK1–Parkin pathway in the effects of DBL, we did not use specific inhibitors or genetic approaches to establish causality. Future studies using mitophagy inhibitors or PINK1/Parkin knockout models could provide more definitive evidence. Second, the direct molecular targets of DBL remain unknown. DBL may interact with key proteins in the mitophagy pathway or may act indirectly by modulating upstream signaling.

Conclusions

In conclusion, this study demonstrates that dietary supplementation with DBL protects against LPS-induced intestinal injury in Leizhou black ducks. These protective effects are mediated through the enhancement of mitochondrial quality control The PINK1/Parkin-mediated mitophagy pathway is activated, which in turn suppresses aberrant mitochondrial stress signaling (cGAS-STING) and apoptosis (BAX/BAK-Caspase pathway) (Fig. 7). Consequently, DBL preserves intestinal barrier integrity and alleviates inflammation. These findings identify DBL as a potential feed additive for preventing intestinal injury in poultry and provide a scientific basis for its application in antibiotic-free production systems.

Abstract
. We demonstrate equidistribution of the lattice shape of cubic fields when ordered by discriminant, giving an estimate in the Eisenstein series spectrum with a lower order main term. The analysis gives a separate discussion of the contributions of reducible and irreducible binary cubic forms, following a method of Shintani. Our work answers a question posed at the American Institute of Math by giving a precise geometric and spectral description of an evident barrier to equidistribution in the lattice shape.

1.
Introduction

The image of the ring of integers of a degree
n
number field
K
in the canonical embedding is a rank
n
lattice of covolume
2^{-r_{2}}|D|^{\frac{1}{2}}
where
r_{2}
is the number of complex embeddings and
D
is the field discriminant.
It was noticed at a meeting at the American Institute of Math that there is an irregularity in the distribution of lattice shapes that appear as the ring of integers of cubic number fields. Indeed, since the norm of an algebraic integer is a non-zero integer, there is a constraint on the shortest vector in this lattice. The purpose of this article is to study this phenomenon in greater detail by studying the distribution of the lattice shape in the Eisenstein spectrum of the space of lattices. See
[
11
]
for the corresponding analysis in the cuspidal spectrum, where, note, the irregularity of distribution does not appear.

Let
\Lambda_{K}
denote the lattice shape of the ring of integers of a number field
K
, which is defined to be the orthogonal projection of the ring of integers in the space orthogonal to
\sigma(1)
in the trace paring, rescaled to have co-volume 1. The lattice shape is identified with a point in
\mathrm{SL}_{n-1}(\mathbb{Z})\backslash\mathrm{SL}_{n-1}(\mathbb{R})
by a choice of base point. This shape is known to be asymptotically equidistributed in the case of
S_{n}
number fields of degrees 3,4 and 5 by work of Bhargava and Harron
[
4
]
, which we make effective by giving a rate of convergence in the corresponding Weyl sums. Let
\mathbf{E}_{r}
denote the real analytic Eisenstein series for
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
with Laplace eigenvalue
\lambda=\frac{1}{4}+r^{2}
.

Theorem 1
.

Let
F
be a smooth function of compact support on
\mathbb{R}^{+}
. We have the following evaluation of Weyl sums

\displaystyle S^{+}(\mathbf{E}_{r},F,X)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}[K:\mathbb{Q}]=3,S_{3}\end{subarray}}F\left(\frac{\operatorname{Disc}K}{X}\right)\mathbf{E}_{r}(\Lambda_{K})

\displaystyle=\tilde{F}\left(\frac{11+z}{12}\right)X^{\frac{11+z}{12}}\zeta\left(\frac{1-z}{3}\right)2^{\frac{z-1}{6}}3^{\frac{z-7}{4}}\pi^{\frac{1+2z}{6}}\cos\left(\frac{\pi(1-z)}{6}\right)

\displaystyle\times\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{1-z}{3}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{4-z}{6}\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{7-z}{6}\right)}\prod_{p}\left(1-\frac{1}{p^{\frac{5+z}{3}}}-\frac{1}{p^{\frac{7+2z}{3}}}+\frac{1}{p^{\frac{13+2z}{3}}}\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{\xi(z)}{\xi(1+z)}\tilde{F}\left(\frac{11-z}{12}\right)X^{\frac{11-z}{12}}\zeta\left(\frac{1+z}{3}\right)2^{\frac{-z-1}{6}}3^{\frac{-z-7}{4}}\pi^{\frac{1-2z}{6}}\cos\left(\frac{\pi(1+z)}{6}\right)

\displaystyle\times\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{1+z}{3}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{4+z}{6}\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{7+z}{6}\right)}\prod_{p}\left(1-\frac{1}{p^{\frac{5-z}{3}}}-\frac{1}{p^{\frac{7-2z}{3}}}+\frac{1}{p^{\frac{13-2z}{3}}}\right)+O_{\epsilon}\left(X^{\frac{13}{15}+\epsilon}\right)

\displaystyle S^{-}(\mathbf{E}_{r},F,X)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}[K:\mathbb{Q}]=3,S_{3}\end{subarray}}F\left(\frac{-\operatorname{Disc}K}{X}\right)\mathbf{E}_{r}(\Lambda_{K})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{3}\tilde{F}\left(\frac{11+z}{12}\right)X^{\frac{11+z}{12}}\zeta\left(\frac{1-z}{3}\right)2^{\frac{z-1}{6}}\pi^{\frac{1+2z}{6}}\cos\left(\frac{\pi(1-z)}{6}\right)

\displaystyle\times\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{1-z}{3}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{4-z}{6}\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{7-z}{6}\right)}\prod_{p}\left(1-\frac{1}{p^{\frac{5+z}{3}}}-\frac{1}{p^{\frac{7+2z}{3}}}+\frac{1}{p^{\frac{13+2z}{3}}}\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{\xi(z)}{\xi(1+z)}\frac{1}{3}\tilde{F}\left(\frac{11-z}{12}\right)X^{\frac{11-z}{12}}\zeta\left(\frac{1+z}{3}\right)2^{\frac{-z-1}{6}}\pi^{\frac{1-2z}{6}}\cos\left(\frac{\pi(1+z)}{6}\right)

\displaystyle\times\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{1+z}{3}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{4+z}{6}\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{7+z}{6}\right)}\prod_{p}\left(1-\frac{1}{p^{\frac{5-z}{3}}}-\frac{1}{p^{\frac{7-2z}{3}}}+\frac{1}{p^{\frac{13-2z}{3}}}\right)+O_{\epsilon}\left(X^{\frac{13}{15}+\epsilon}\right).

Note that the number of cubic fields with discriminant of size at most
X
grows linearly in
X
, so that the above theorem obtains a power saving estimate in the Weyl sums. Combined with the estimate of the cuspidal Weyl sums in
[
11
]
where we obtained a bound
O\left(X^{\frac{2}{3}+\epsilon}\right)
, this gives a quantitative proof of the asymptotic equidistribution in the cubic case.

The proof of Theorem
1
is based on an analysis of the orbital twisted Shintani zeta functions, combined with a sieve. This method of proving asymptotic results for cubic fields was introduced by Taniguchi and Thorne
[
21
]
,
[
22
]
, see also
[
5
]
. Here we extend the Eisenstein twisted zeta functions introduced in
[

]
to the orbital case, obtaining their poles and residues and matching off a pole arising from reducible binary cubics. A detailed discussion of the poles and residues appears in Section

3.
Cubic rings and binary cubic forms

A cubic ring
R
over
\mathbb{Z}
is a free rank three
\mathbb{Z}
module with a ring multiplication. Delone-Fadeev and Gan-Gross-Savin established a discriminant-preserving bijection between cubic rings up to isomorphism and the space
\operatorname{sym}^{3}(\mathbb{Z}^{2})
of binary cubic forms. In the identification, maximal cubic rings whose associated form is irreducible over
\mathbb{Z}
correspond with rings of integers in cubic number fields.

Given a form
x(v,w)=av^{3}+bv^{2}w+cvw^{2}+dw^{3}
in the space
V_{\mathbb{R}}
of real binary cubic forms,
g\in\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
acts by

. There is a bilinear pairing

\langle x,y\rangle=x_{4}y_{1}-\frac{1}{3}x_{3}y_{2}+\frac{1}{3}x_{2}y_{3}-x_{1}y_{4}.

Let
P(x)=x_{2}^{2}x_{3}^{2}+18x_{1}x_{2}x_{3}x_{4}-4x_{1}x_{3}^{3}-4x_{2}^{3}x_{4}-27x_{1}^{2}x_{4}^{2}
be the discriminant.
The discriminant scales under the action by a factor of
\chi(g)=\det(g)^{6}
. The space
V_{\mathbb{R}}
decomposes into two open orbits
V_{+}
and
V_{-}
having positive and negative discriminant, and the singular set
S
where the discriminant is 0.
We identify
V_{+}=G^{+}\cdot x_{+}
,
V_{-}=G^{+}\cdot x_{-}
by choosing base points

(18)

The point

has stabilizer of order 3 generated by the rotation of

, while

has trivial stabilizer.
We use several times that

(2)

The part of the singular terms attached to the constant part of the Eisenstein series are polar terms, and we will handle these all together with the other polar terms from
Z_{1,c,q}^{\pm,r}
after treating the remaining terms.

Recall the expression

Z_{1,c,q}^{\pm,r}=\frac{1}{3}\left(Z_{q,r}\left(f_{\pm,0},2s+\frac{1}{2}+\frac{z}{2},s-\frac{1}{4}-\frac{z}{4}\right)+\frac{\xi(z)}{\xi(1+z)}Z_{q,r}\left(f_{\pm,0},2s+\frac{1}{2}-\frac{z}{2},s-\frac{1}{4}+\frac{z}{4}\right)\right).

Lemma 44
.

We have the bound

\sum_{q<Q}\mu(q)\oint_{\operatorname{Re}(s)=4}X^{s}Z_{q,r}^{+}\left(f_{\pm,0},2s+\frac{1}{2}+\frac{z}{2},s-\frac{1}{4}-\frac{z}{4}\right)\frac{ds}{s^{2}}=O\left(X^{\frac{1}{4}+\epsilon}\right).

Proof.

Open the definition of
Z_{q,r}^{+}
so that we need to estimate

\displaystyle\sum_{q<Q}\mu(q)\oint_{\operatorname{Re}(s)=4}X^{s}\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{d\lambda}{\lambda}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{dt}{t^{3}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}du\lambda^{8s}t^{4s+1+z}

\displaystyle\times\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x=(b,c,d)\\
0\leq c<2|b|\\
c^{2}-4bd\neq 0\end{subarray}}{\mathscr{N}}_{q}^{r}(x)f_{\pm,0}\left(\lambda^{2}\left(t^{2}b,c+2ub,\frac{d+cu+bu^{2}}{t^{2}}\right)\right)\frac{ds}{s^{2}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{q<Q}\mu(q)\oint_{\operatorname{Re}(s)=4}X^{s}\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{d\lambda}{\lambda}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{dt}{t}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}du\lambda^{8s}t^{4s-1+z}

\displaystyle\times\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x=(b,c,d)\\
0\leq c<2|b|\\
c^{2}-4bd\neq 0\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{b}{\mathscr{N}}_{q}^{r}(x)f_{\pm,0}\left(\lambda^{2}\left(t^{2}b,2u,\frac{bd-\frac{c^{2}}{4}+u^{2}}{t^{2}b}\right)\right)\frac{ds}{s^{2}}.

We have
{\mathscr{N}}_{q}^{r}
vanishes unless
q|b^{2}(c^{2}-4bd)
. Using the rapid decay of
f_{\pm,0}
, which is compact support in the discriminate of the associated binary cubic form
\lambda^{8}t^{4}b^{2}(c^{2}-4bd)
and Schwarz class in the discriminant
\lambda^{4}(c^{2}-4bd)
we can truncate to
\lambda=O(X^{\epsilon})
,
t^{2}b=O(X^{\epsilon})
and
c^{2}-4bd=O(X^{\epsilon})
. Now push the contour to
\operatorname{Re}(s)=\frac{1}{4}+\epsilon
. Write
q=q_{1}q_{2}
with
q_{1}
the largest factor of
q
that divides
c^{2}-4bd
, so that
q_{2}|b
. Make the change
(t^{\prime})^{2}=t^{2}b
, so that
b
now appears as
\frac{1}{b^{2s+\frac{1}{2}}+4\epsilon}
. Given
c
there are
O(X^{\epsilon})
choices for
b,d
, so that, bounding
|b|>c
and summing in
c
we obtain the desired bound.
∎

Remark: if we had split the integral at
\lambda\geq Y
instead of
\lambda\geq 1
in the approximate functional equation this trades off the length of this sum with the length of the dual sum. In principle this could be done so that the two give an equal error term.

Lemma 45
.

We have the bound

\sum_{q<Q}\mu(q)\oint_{\operatorname{Re}(s)=4}X^{s}\hat{Z}_{q,r}^{+}\left(\hat{f}_{\pm,0},2s+\frac{1}{2}+\frac{z}{2},\frac{5}{4}-3s-\frac{z}{4}\right)\frac{ds}{s^{2}}=O(X^{\frac{1}{4}+\epsilon}Q^{2+\epsilon}).

Proof.

We again open the definition of
\hat{Z}_{q,r}^{+}
, so that we need to bound

\displaystyle\sum_{q<Q}\mu(q)\oint_{\operatorname{Re}(s)=4}X^{s}\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{d\lambda}{\lambda}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{dt}{t^{3}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}du\lambda^{6-8s}t^{4s+1+z}

\displaystyle\times\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x=(b,c,d)\\
0\leq c<2|b|\\
c^{2}-4bd\neq 0\end{subarray}}\hat{{\mathscr{N}}}_{q}^{r}(x)\hat{f}_{\pm,0}\left(\frac{\lambda^{2}}{q^{2}}\left(t^{2}b,c+2ub,\frac{d+cu+bu^{2}}{t^{2}}\right)\right)\frac{ds}{s^{2}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{q<Q}\mu(q)\oint_{\operatorname{Re}(s)=4}X^{s}\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{d\lambda}{\lambda}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{dt}{t}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}du\lambda^{6-8s}t^{4s-1+z}

\displaystyle\times\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x=(b,c,d)\\
0\leq c<2|b|\\
c^{2}-4bd\neq 0\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{b}\hat{{\mathscr{N}}}_{q}^{r}(x)\hat{f}_{\pm,0}\left(\frac{\lambda^{2}}{q^{2}}\left(t^{2}b,2u,\frac{bd-\frac{c^{2}}{4}+u^{2}}{t^{2}b}\right)\right)\frac{ds}{s^{2}}.

Write
q^{\prime}=q/(q,2^{\infty})
for the odd part of
q
. We now examine
\hat{{\mathscr{N}}}_{p}^{r}(x)
for odd
p|q
. Let
q_{1}
be composed of those odd primes
p
for which
p^{2}|b,c
and
p|d
. For these primes,
\hat{{\mathscr{N}}}_{p}^{r}(x)=O\left(\frac{1}{p}\right)
. Next let
q_{2}
be composed of those odd primes
p
for which
p^{2}
does not divide both
b,c
but
p|b,c,d
. For these primes,
\hat{{\mathscr{N}}}_{p}^{r}(x)=O\left(\frac{1}{p^{3}}\right)
. Let
q_{3}
be composed of those odd primes
p
for which
p\nmid b
but
p^{2}|c^{2}-4bd
. For these primes
\hat{{\mathscr{N}}}_{p}^{r}(x)=O\left(\frac{1}{p^{3}}\right)
. Finally, let
q_{4}
be composed of those odd primes
p
for which
p\nmid b
and
p\|c^{2}-4bd
. For these
p
,
\hat{{\mathscr{N}}}_{p}^{r}(x)=O\left(\frac{1}{p^{4}}\right).
It follows that
|\hat{{\mathscr{N}}}_{q}^{r}(x)|=O\left(\frac{q^{\epsilon}}{q_{1}q_{2}^{3}q_{3}^{3}q_{4}^{4}}\right)
. Also,
q_{1}^{3}q_{2}^{2}q_{3}^{2}q_{4}|c^{2}-4bd
. By the rapid decay of
\hat{f}
,
c^{2}-4bd=O(q^{4}X^{\epsilon})
with negligible error. Given fixed
c
, and a dyadic range
A\leq|c^{2}-4bd|<2A
,
A\ll q^{4}X^{\epsilon}
there are
O\left(\frac{AX^{\epsilon}c^{\epsilon}}{q_{1}^{3}q_{2}^{2}q_{3}^{2}q_{4}}\right)
ways of choosing
b,d
to make the discriminant in the dyadic range. We may also use
q_{1}^{2}q_{2}|c
and
\frac{1}{c}>\frac{1}{2|b|}
as we sum over
c
. Shift the contour to
\operatorname{Re}(s)=\frac{1}{4}
to obtain the bound
O(X^{\frac{1}{4}+\epsilon}Q^{2+\epsilon})
.
∎

We now treat the contributions of the constant terms, handling the residues of the poles at the end. From the main zeta function there are two constant term contributions that have poles, and only the poles survive (the remaining line integral can be pushed arbitrarily far left, saving an arbitrary power of
X
), but in the reducible there are two line integrals to bound aside from the residue of the pole. These are bounded in the following lemma.

Lemma 46
.

The difference between the integral

\displaystyle\sum_{q<Q}\mu(q)\oint_{\operatorname{Re}(s)=4}X^{s}\sum_{q_{1}q_{2}=q}q_{2}^{-2}\prod_{p|q_{1}}p^{-(2s+\frac{1+z}{2})}\left(1-\frac{1}{p^{2}}\right)\frac{\zeta\left(2s+\frac{1}{2}+\frac{z}{2}\right)}{s-\frac{5}{4}-\frac{z}{4}}

\displaystyle\times\left(\Phi_{+}+\Phi_{-}\right)\left(2s-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{z}{2},0\right)\frac{ds}{s^{2}}

and the residue of the pole at
\frac{5+z}{4}
is
O(X^{\frac{1}{4}+\epsilon}Q^{\epsilon})
.

Proof.

Shift the contour to
\operatorname{Re}(s)=\frac{1}{4}+\epsilon
, passing the contribution of the pole. Bound the sum over
Q
by
Q^{\epsilon}
.
∎

The remaining non-polar term is bounded as follows.

Lemma 47
.

We have the bound

\displaystyle\sum_{q<Q}\mu(q)\oint_{\operatorname{Re}(s)=4}X^{s}\frac{\Sigma(\hat{f}_{\pm,0}(2s-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{z}{2}))}{24s-10+2z}\sum_{q_{1}q_{2}q_{3}=\frac{q}{(q,2^{\infty})}}\prod_{p|q_{1}}\left(\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{p^{2}}-\frac{1}{p^{3}}\right)\prod_{p|q_{2}}\left(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{p^{3}}\right)\prod_{p|q_{3}}\left(\frac{1}{p^{3}}-\frac{1}{p^{4}}\right)

\displaystyle\times\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}\\
(\ell,q_{2}q_{3})=1\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}b=1\\
(b,q_{3})=1\end{subarray}}^{\infty}\frac{\omega_{2,q}(\ell,b)\phi(q_{2}b)q^{4s+1+z}}{(q_{1}^{2}q_{2}^{3}b^{2}|\ell|)^{2s+\frac{1+z}{2}}}=O(X^{\frac{5}{12}+\epsilon}Q^{\frac{2}{3}}).

Proof.

Shift the contour to
\operatorname{Re}(s)=\frac{5}{12}+\epsilon
. On this line the sum over
q
is bounded by
Q^{\frac{2}{3}}
, which gives the bound claimed.
∎

It remains to evaluate the polar terms. The poles at
\frac{5+\pm z}{4}
cancel, leaving a pole at
\frac{11\pm z}{12}
to evalluate. This is evaluated in the following lemma.

Lemma 48
.

Let
P_{Q,\pm}
be the contribution of the poles, sieved for
q\leq Q
. We have

\displaystyle P_{Q,-}

\displaystyle=O\left(\frac{X^{\frac{11}{12}}}{Q^{\frac{2}{3}-\epsilon}}\right)+\frac{1}{3}\tilde{F}\left(\frac{11+z}{12}\right)X^{\frac{11+z}{12}}\zeta\left(\frac{1-z}{3}\right)2^{\frac{z-1}{6}}\pi^{\frac{1+2z}{6}}\cos\left(\frac{\pi(1-z)}{6}\right)

\displaystyle\times\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{1-z}{3}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{4-z}{6}\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{7-z}{6}\right)}\prod_{p}\left(1-\frac{1}{p^{\frac{5+z}{3}}}-\frac{1}{p^{\frac{7+2z}{3}}}+\frac{1}{p^{\frac{13+2z}{3}}}\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{\xi(z)}{\xi(1+z)}\frac{1}{3}\tilde{F}\left(\frac{11-z}{12}\right)X^{\frac{11-z}{12}}\zeta\left(\frac{1+z}{3}\right)2^{\frac{-z-1}{6}}\pi^{\frac{1-2z}{6}}\cos\left(\frac{\pi(1+z)}{6}\right)

\displaystyle\times\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{1+z}{3}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{4+z}{6}\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{7+z}{6}\right)}\prod_{p}\left(1-\frac{1}{p^{\frac{5-z}{3}}}-\frac{1}{p^{\frac{7-2z}{3}}}+\frac{1}{p^{\frac{13-2z}{3}}}\right)

\displaystyle P_{Q,+}

\displaystyle=O\left(\frac{X^{\frac{11}{12}}}{Q^{\frac{2}{3}-\epsilon}}\right)+\tilde{F}\left(\frac{11+z}{12}\right)X^{\frac{11+z}{12}}\zeta\left(\frac{1-z}{3}\right)2^{\frac{z-1}{6}}3^{\frac{z-7}{4}}\pi^{\frac{1+2z}{6}}\cos\left(\frac{\pi(1-z)}{6}\right)

\displaystyle\times\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{1-z}{3}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{4-z}{6}\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{7-z}{6}\right)}\prod_{p}\left(1-\frac{1}{p^{\frac{5+z}{3}}}-\frac{1}{p^{\frac{7+2z}{3}}}+\frac{1}{p^{\frac{13+2z}{3}}}\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{\xi(z)}{\xi(1+z)}\tilde{F}\left(\frac{11-z}{12}\right)X^{\frac{11-z}{12}}\zeta\left(\frac{1+z}{3}\right)2^{\frac{-z-1}{6}}3^{\frac{-z-7}{4}}\pi^{\frac{1-2z}{6}}\cos\left(\frac{\pi(1+z)}{6}\right)

\displaystyle\times\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{1+z}{3}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{4+z}{6}\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{7+z}{6}\right)}\prod_{p}\left(1-\frac{1}{p^{\frac{5-z}{3}}}-\frac{1}{p^{\frac{7-2z}{3}}}+\frac{1}{p^{\frac{13-2z}{3}}}\right).

Proof.

The error term is a result of summing
\sum_{q\geq Q}\frac{1}{q^{\frac{5}{3}-\epsilon}}
.
∎

The proof of Theorem
1
is the result of balancing the various error terms

O\left(X^{\frac{3}{4}+\epsilon}+\frac{X^{\frac{13}{12}}}{Q^{1-\epsilon}}+Q^{4+\epsilon}X^{\epsilon}+X^{\frac{1}{4}+\epsilon}Q^{2+\epsilon}+X^{\frac{5}{12}+\epsilon}Q^{\frac{2}{3}+\epsilon}+\frac{X^{\frac{11}{12}}}{Q^{\frac{2}{3}-\epsilon}}\right).

The dominant terms are
\frac{X^{\frac{13}{12}+\epsilon}}{Q}
and
Q^{4+\epsilon}
which is optimal with
Q=X^{\frac{13}{60}}
giving the claimed bound
O(X^{\frac{13}{15}+\epsilon})
.

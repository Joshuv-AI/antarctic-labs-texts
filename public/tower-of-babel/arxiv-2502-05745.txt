Global classical solutions to the ionic Vlasov-Poisson-Boltzmann system in a 3D periodic box
fucai Li, Yichun Wang

Abstract
. We investigate the global well-posedness of the ionic Vlasov-Poisson-Boltzmann system which models the evolution of dilute collisional ions. This system distinguishes the electronic Vlasov-Poisson-Boltzmann system via an additional exponential nonlinearity in the coupled Poisson-Poincaré equation, which introduces essential mathematical difficulties. In a three-dimensional periodic box, We establish the existence of a unique global-in-time classical solution with an exponential decay under small initial perturbations of a global Maxwellian that preserve mass, momentum and energy conservation laws. Our approach combines a nonlinear energy method with quantitative nonlinear elliptic estimates and new coercivity inequalities for the linearized collision operator ℒ \mathcal{L} in ion dynamics.

82D10, 35Q20, 35Q83

1.
Introduction

In plasma physics, the ionic Vlasov-Poisson-Boltzmann system describes the evolution of dilute ions through mutual interactions governed by self-consistent electrostatic field and collisions. Due to the analytical difficulties raising from the nonlinear Poisson equation, until now there has been no rigorous mathematical study on the well-posedness of this model in the
3D
periodic box
\mathbb{T}^{3}=\mathbb{R}^{3}/\mathbb{Z}^{3}
. The purpose of this article is to fill this gap. First, for reader’s convenience, we provide some necessary background on the ion dynamics below.

1.1.
Background and models

1.1.1.
Physics background

Plasma exists commonly in the universe as a state of matter, which manifests, for example, in lightning, ionosphere, corona, accretion disk and interstellar medium
[
44
,
41
,
43
]
. This ionized gas comprises a multi-particle system of electrons and positively charged ions whose collective dynamics are governed by long-range electromagnetic interactions.
A characteristic example can be found in stellar atmospheric plasmas, which constitute electrons, protons, helium ions and few heavy ions
[
39
]
.
From a theoretical perspective, plasma kinetic theory occupies a unique position in statistical mechanics, bridging the gap between microscopic single-particle orbital descriptions and macroscopic magnetohydrodynamic formulations.

The collisions between charged particles constitute a form of electromagnetic interaction. It is commonly assumed that each charged particle interacts with surrounding particles within a spherical region defined by the characteristic Debye length scale, a phenomenon recognized as the Coulomb collision. Within this framework, the collision frequencies for electron-electron (ee), electron-ion (ei), and ion-ion (ii) interactions can be quantitatively described as (for example, see
[
43
]
):

(1.1)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &f_{\rm{ee}}=\frac{n_{e}e^{4}\ln\Lambda}{3\sqrt{6}\pi\varepsilon_{0}^{2}m_{e}^{\frac{1}{2}}(k_{B}T_{e})^{\frac{3}{2}}},\\
&f_{\rm{ei}}=\frac{n_{e}Z_{i}^{2}e^{4}\ln\Lambda}{6\sqrt{3}\pi\varepsilon_{0}^{2}m_{e}^{\frac{1}{2}}(k_{B}T_{e})^{\frac{3}{2}}},\\
&f_{\rm{ii}}=\frac{n_{i}Z_{i}^{4}e^{4}\ln\Lambda}{3\sqrt{6}\pi\varepsilon_{0}^{2}m_{i}^{\frac{1}{2}}(k_{B}T_{i})^{\frac{3}{2}}}.\end{aligned}\right.

Here, for the electrons,
n_{e}
is the number density,
e
and
m_{e}
are the magnitude of the charge and mass, and
T_{e}
is the temperature. Similarly, we have
n_{i}
,
Z_{i}e
,
m_{i}
and
T_{i}
for the (one-species) ions. The parameter
\varepsilon_{0}>0
is the vacuum permittivity,
k_{B}>0
is the Boltzmann constant and
\ln\Lambda
is the Coulomb logarithm.

From (
1.1
),
we see that the relaxation time of the ions is much longer than that of the electrons (one also refers to (2.12) in Cordier-Grenier
[
9
]
).
On the ion’s timescale, the electrons are at the local thermodynamical equilibrium and the density
n_{e}
obeys the well-known Maxwell-Boltzmann relation:

\displaystyle n_{e}=n_{e0}\exp\left\{\frac{e\phi}{k_{B}T_{e}}\right\},

where
\phi
is the electrostatic potential and
n_{e0}
is the number density of electrons as
\phi=0
. Then
\phi
satisfies the Poisson equation

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{0}\Delta\phi=-(\rho_{i}+\rho_{e})=en_{e0}\exp\left\{\frac{e\phi}{k_{B}T_{e}}\right\}-\rho_{i},

where
\rho_{e}
and
\rho_{i}
are the charge density of electrons and ions respectively, and
\rho_{e}=-en_{e}
,
\rho_{i}=Z_{i}en_{i}
. Normalization of the parameters yields the so-called Poisson-Poincaré equation:

(1.2)

\displaystyle\Delta\phi=e^{\phi}-\rho,

which is a semilinear second order elliptic equation for
\phi
.

1.1.2.
Models

The ionic Vlasov-Poisson-Boltzmann system consists of a Vlasov-Boltzmann equation whose unknown is the distribution function
F(t,x,v)
of ions and a Poisson-Poincaré equation whose unknown is the self-consistent potential
\phi(t,x)
, reading as

(1.3)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}F+v\cdot\nabla_{x}F+E\cdot\nabla_{v}F=Q(F,F)\quad\mathrm{in}\quad(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3},\\
&E=-\nabla\phi,\quad\Delta\phi=e^{\phi}-\rho\quad\mathrm{in}\quad(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{T}^{3},\\
&F|_{t=0}=F_{0}\quad\mathrm{in}\quad\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3},\end{aligned}\right.

where the position
x\in\mathbb{T}^{3}
and the velocity
v\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
at time
t>0
, and
\rho=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}Fdv
is the macroscopic density of ions.
The initial distribution
F_{0}
is assumed to be nonnegative.
The Boltzmann collision term
Q(F,F)
on the right-hand side of (
1.3
)
1
is given as the bilinear form

\displaystyle Q(F_{1},F_{2})\,

\displaystyle=\int\!\!\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}}|(v-u)\cdot\omega|[F_{1}(u^{\prime})F_{2}(v^{\prime})-F_{1}(u)F_{2}(v)]d\omega du

\displaystyle=:Q_{\mathrm{gain}}(F_{1},F_{2})-Q_{\mathrm{loss}}(F_{1},F_{2}),

which is the hard-sphere collision operator.
Here, the relationship between the pre-collision velocity pair
(u,v)
of two particles and the post-collision velocity pair
(u^{\prime},v^{\prime})
obeys

\displaystyle u^{\prime}=u+[(v-u)\cdot\omega]\omega,\quad v^{\prime}=v-[(v-u)\cdot\omega]\omega,

for
\omega\in\mathbb{S}^{2}
, which can be determined by the conservation laws of momentum and energy:

\displaystyle u+v=u^{\prime}+v^{\prime},\quad|u|^{2}+|v|^{2}=|u^{\prime}|^{2}+|v^{\prime}|^{2}.

Formally, Bardos et al.
[
2
]
derived a variant of
the system (
1.3
) with a time-varying electron temperature parameter determined by the conservation of energy, by taking the massless electron limit
\sqrt{\frac{m_{e}}{m_{i}}}\rightarrow 0
to the following bipolar Vlasov-Poisson-Boltzmann system

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}f_{-}+w\cdot\nabla_{x}f_{-}-\frac{q_{e}}{m_{e}}E\cdot\nabla_{w}f_{-}=Q_{-}(f_{-},f_{-}),\\
&\partial_{t}f_{+}+v\cdot\nabla_{x}f_{+}-\frac{q_{i}}{m_{i}}E\cdot\nabla_{v}f_{+}=Q_{+}(f_{+},f_{+}),\\
&\varepsilon_{0}\nabla\cdot E=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\{q_{i}f_{+}(v)-q_{e}f_{-}(v)\}dv.\end{aligned}\right.

In this article, we only consider the constant temperature case for presentation simplicity, as in (
1.3
).

For the classical solutions to the system (
1.3
), the following conservation laws of mass, momentum and energy hold:

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int\!\!\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}F(t,x,v)dxdv=0,

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int\!\!\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}vF(t,x,v)dxdv=0,

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\left\{\int\!\!\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}|v|^{2}F(t,x,v)dxdv+\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}2\phi e^{\phi}+|\nabla\phi|^{2}dx\right\}=0.

1.3.
Perturbed equations near the equilibrium

In this article, we shall construct a classical solution
F(t,x,v)
to the ionic Vlasov-Poisson-Boltzmann system (
1.3
)
1
–(
1.3
)
2
near the global Maxwellian

\displaystyle\mu\equiv\mu(v)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{\frac{3}{2}}}\exp\left\{-\frac{|v|^{2}}{2}\right\}.

For this purpose, we define the standard perturbation
f(t,x,v)
to
\mu
as

F=\mu+\sqrt{\mu}f,

and the initial datum
f_{0}(x,v)
as
F_{0}=\mu+\sqrt{\mu}f_{0}\geq 0
.

We rewrite the problem (
1.3
) by using the unknown
f
. Thus, the ionic Vlasov-Poisson-Boltzmann system now becomes

(1.4)

\displaystyle\left\{\partial_{t}+v\cdot\nabla_{x}+E\cdot\nabla_{v}\right\}f-E\cdot v\sqrt{\mu}-\frac{v}{2}\cdot Ef=-\mathcal{L}f+\Gamma(f,f),

coupled with

(1.5)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &E=-\nabla\phi,\\
&\Delta\phi=e^{\phi}-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\sqrt{\mu}fdv-1,\end{aligned}\right.

and supplemented with the initial datum

(1.6)

\displaystyle f(0,x,v)=f_{0}(x,v).

Here,
\mathcal{L}
is the linearized Boltzmann collision operator defined as

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}f=\nu f-\mathcal{K}f,

where
\nu(v)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|(v-u)\cdot\omega|\mu(u)dud\omega
, and
\mathcal{K}
is split into
\mathcal{K}:=\mathcal{K}_{2}-\mathcal{K}_{1}
with

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{1}f(v):=

\displaystyle\int\!\!\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}}|(v-u)\cdot\omega|\mu^{1/2}(u)\mu^{1/2}(v)f(u)dud\omega,

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{2}f(v):=

\displaystyle\int\!\!\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}}|(v-u)\cdot\omega|\mu^{1/2}(u)\{\mu^{1/2}(u^{\prime})g(v^{\prime})+\mu^{1/2}(v^{\prime})g(u^{\prime})\}dud\omega.

We also define the bilinear collision operator
\Gamma(\cdot,\cdot)
as

\displaystyle\Gamma(g_{1},g_{2})(v):=\,

\displaystyle\mu^{-1/2}(v)Q(\mu^{1/2}g_{1},\mu^{1/2}g_{2})(v)

\displaystyle=\,

\displaystyle\int\!\!\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}}|(v-u)\cdot\omega|\mu^{1/2}(u)g_{1}(u^{\prime})g_{2}(v^{\prime})dud\omega

\displaystyle-\int\!\!\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}}|(v-u)\cdot\omega|\mu^{1/2}(u)g_{1}(u)g_{2}(v)dud\omega

\displaystyle=:\,

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\rm{gain}}(g_{1},g_{2})(v)-\Gamma_{\rm{loss}}(g_{1},g_{2})(v).

By assuming that
[F_{0},\phi_{F_{0}}]
has the same mass, momentum and total energy as the steady state
[\mu,0]
, we can then rewrite the conservation laws in terms of
[f,\phi_{f}]
as

(1.7)

\displaystyle\int\!\!\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}f(t,x,v)\sqrt{\mu(v)}dxdv\equiv 0,

(1.8)

\displaystyle\int\!\!\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}vf(t,x,v)\sqrt{\mu(v)}dxdv\equiv 0,

(1.9)

\displaystyle\int\!\!\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{1}{2}|v|^{2}f(t,x,v)\sqrt{\mu(v)}dxdv+\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\phi_{f}e^{\phi_{f}}dx+\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\frac{1}{2}|\nabla\phi_{f}|^{2}dx\equiv 0.

Here we have used the notations
\phi_{F_{0}}
and
\phi_{f}
to emphasise that
\phi
depends on
{F_{0}}
and
{f}
, respectively. As a by-product, we also deduce that

(1.10)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}e^{\phi_{f}}dx=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}(\Delta\phi_{f}+1)dx+\int\!\!\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}f(t,x,v)\sqrt{\mu(v)}dxdv=|\mathbb{T}^{3}|=1.

1.6.1.
Nonlinear energy method

Our main strategy aimed to the nonlinear stability of the global Maxwellian

(

) is based on the nonlinear

[

]

[

This method can be applied to many kinetic models of the form

\displaystyle\partial_{t}f+v\cdot\nabla_{x}f+\mathcal{L}f=\Gamma(f,f).

The main idea is to find an energy norm
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{E}}
, a dissipation norm
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{D}}
and a suitable constant

such that

(1.13)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\|f\|^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}+\delta\|f\|^{2}_{\mathcal{D}}\leq 0.

The choice of the energy and dissipation norms is delicate. For the model (

) in hard-sphere case, the energy norm is actually
\vvvert\cdot\vvvert
, and the dissipation norm is actually
\vvvert\cdot\vvvert_{\nu}

which comes from the linear part of the Boltzmann collision operator

, and satisfies

|\langle f,\Gamma(f,f)\rangle_{\mathcal{E}}|\leq C\|f\|_{\mathcal{E}}\|f\|^{2}_{\mathcal{D}}
by Lemma

. Then (
1.13
) is obtained from a suitable small assumption of
\|f\|_{\mathcal{E}}
in Section
6
, combining with the positivity of
\mathcal{L}
in Section
5
. Moreover, noting that the norm
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{E}}
is weaker than
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{D}}
, then the solution

decays exponentially in

by (
1.13
) and Gronwall’s inequality.

1.6.2.
Elliptic estimates of the Poisson-Poincaré equation (
1.2
)

To study the Poisson-Poincaré equation (
1.2
) in
\mathbb{T}^{3}
, we follow
[
17
]
to decompose the electric potential
\phi
into the form
\phi=\overline{\phi}+\widehat{\phi}
satisfying

\displaystyle\Delta\overline{\phi}=1-\rho,\quad\Delta\widehat{\phi}=e^{\overline{\phi}+\widehat{\phi}}-1.

Assuming

\rho:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\sqrt{\mu}fdv+1\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3}),

we obtain the existence and uniqueness of
\overline{\phi}\in H^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})
and
\widehat{\phi}\in H^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})
with upper bounds independent of
\overline{\phi}
and
\widehat{\phi}
.
It follows that
\phi\in C(\mathbb{T}^{3})
with
\|\phi\|_{C(\mathbb{T}^{3})}=O(1)
(see Remark
3.2
) and thus
e^{\phi}
has positive upper and lower bounds. By the mean value theorem, we obtain in (
3.5
) that

\displaystyle\Delta\phi=e^{\phi}-1+1-\rho=\phi e^{\phi_{\xi}}-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\sqrt{\mu}fdv,

hence we can use the standard linear elliptic estimates to get

\displaystyle\|\phi\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}\leq C\|\phi\|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}\leq Ce^{2\|\phi\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3})}.

Since
\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3})}
is controlled by
\|f\|_{\mathcal{E}}
which is assumed to be sufficiently small, we know that
\|\phi\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}
is also small. Therefore, we obtain
\|\phi\|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3})}
, where
C
is independent of
\phi
and
f
. The stability of (
1.2
) follows by Corollary
3.4
.

Furthermore, taking
\partial^{\gamma}
derivatives to (
1.2
) yields

\displaystyle\Delta\partial^{\gamma}\phi-e^{\phi}\partial^{\gamma}\phi=\mathcal{S}-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\sqrt{\mu}\partial^{\gamma}fdv,

where
\mathcal{S}
is constituted by lower order derivatives of
\phi
. Inductively we have (see also Remark
3.6
)

\displaystyle\|\partial^{\gamma}\phi\|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}\leq C\sum_{|\gamma|\leq N}\|\partial^{\gamma}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3})}\leq C\|f\|_{\mathcal{E}}.

The above
L^{2}
estimates for the Poisson-Poincaré equation (
1.2
) are sufficient to establish
the local well-posedness of the problem (
1.3
), as in Section
4
. Meanwhile, the elliptic estimates make
\|\phi\|_{\mathcal{E}}
be absorbed by
\|f\|_{\mathcal{E}}
. This is the reason
that we only need to define
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{E}}
by
\vvvert f\vvvert
rather than
\vvvert f\vvvert+\vvvert\phi\vvvert
. As a contrast, one refers to
[
20
]
for the explanations on the construction of energy functional for the Vlasov-Maxwell-Boltzmann system
where the electronic and magnetic fields are included.

1.6.3.
Coercivity of the linearized operator
\mathcal{L}

Heuristically, we denote
\mathcal{B}=-\mathcal{L}-v\cdot\nabla_{x}
, then the integral form of (
1.12
)
read as (cf.
[
5
]
for the Boltzmann equation case)

\displaystyle f(t)=e^{t\mathcal{B}}f(0)+\int_{0}^{t}e^{(t-s)\mathcal{B}}\Gamma(f(s),f(s))ds.

If it were proved that

\displaystyle\|e^{t\mathcal{B}}\|\leq Ce^{-Ct}\quad\text{and}\quad\|\Gamma(f,f)\|\leq C\|f\|^{2}

hold in some norm
\|\cdot\|
for some
C>0
, then we have

\displaystyle\|f(t)\|\leq Ce^{-Ct}\|f(0)\|+\int_{0}^{t}Ce^{-C(t-s)}\|f(s)\|^{2}ds.

Denoting
\breve{f}=\sup_{t}\|f(t)e^{ct}\|
, it holds

\displaystyle\breve{f}\leq C\|f(0)\|+C\breve{f}^{2},

which implies the boundedness of
\breve{f}
under some smallness conditions. However, the five dimensional null space of
\mathcal{L}
prevents us from this strategy. In fact,
e^{-t\mathcal{L}}h=h
as
h
belongs to the kernel of

\mathcal{L}
, so
\|e^{-t\mathcal{L}}\|\leq e^{-Ct}
does not hold.

Henceforth, we devote to establishing the coercivity estimates of
\mathcal{L}
in terms of the solution
f
to the ionic Vlasov-Poisson-Boltzmann system (
1.4
)–(
1.5
) with the conservation laws (
1.7
)–(
1.9
).
The key point is to control the macroscopic part of
\partial^{\gamma}f
by its microscopic part in
\|\cdot\|_{\nu}
norm. Define the macroscopic function
a(t,x)
,
b(t,x)
and
c(t,x)
as

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\partial^{\gamma}f(t,x,v):=\{\partial^{\gamma}a(t,x)+\partial^{\gamma}b(t,x)\cdot v+\partial^{\gamma}c(t,x)|v|^{2}\}\sqrt{\mu},

where
\mathbf{P}
is the orthogonal projection from
L^{2}_{v}
to
\ker(\mathcal{L})(=:\mathscr{N})
.
Due to the nonlinear exponential term in the nonlinear Poisson equation (
1.5
), it raises new difficulties for estimating the pure spatial derivatives of
a(t,x)
, which satisfies (
5.8
):

\partial^{0}b_{i}+\partial^{i}a-E_{i}=l_{bi}+h_{bi}.

Here,
l_{bi}
and
h_{bi}
are the components of
l
and
h
under
v_{i}\sqrt{\mu}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
defined in (
5.4
).
A first glimpse leads to
\|\partial^{i}a\|\leq\|\partial^{0}b_{i}\|+\|l_{bi}\|+\|h_{bi}\|+\|E_{i}\|
, but
\|E_{i}\|
cannot be controlled by

C\sum_{|\gamma|\leq N}\|\{\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{P}\}\partial^{\gamma}f(t)\|+C\sqrt{M_{0}}\sum_{|\gamma|\leq N}\|\partial^{\gamma}f(t)\|,

required by (
5.2
), where
M_{0}\ll 1
. We thus ought to combine the density of ion:
\varrho_{0}a+\varrho_{2}c

(\varrho_{0}=\int\mu dv,\varrho_{2}=\int|v|^{2}\mu dv)
, with the density of electron:
e^{\phi}
. In fact, from (
5.5
) and (
5.8
) we have three identities:

\left.\begin{aligned} &{(\rm i}).\,\,-\Delta a+\nabla\cdot E=\nabla\cdot\partial^{0}b-\sum_{i}\partial^{i}(l_{bi}+h_{bi}),\\
&({\rm ii}).\,\,-\Delta c=-\nabla\cdot(l_{c}+h_{c}),\\
&({\rm iii}).\,\,-\Delta\phi-\nabla\cdot E=0.\end{aligned}\right.

For some sufficiently large constant
\Theta>0
, we operate (i)
\times(\rho_{0}a+\rho_{2}c)
+(ii)
\times\Theta c
+(iii)
\times(e^{\phi}-1)
and then integrate the result over
\mathbb{T}^{3}
to get

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}|\nabla a|^{2}+\Theta|\nabla c|^{2}+|\nabla\phi|^{2}e^{\phi}+(\nabla\cdot E)^{2}+(\nabla a\cdot\nabla c)dx

\displaystyle\qquad\leq C(\|\partial^{0}b\|+\|l_{bi}+h_{bi}\|+\|l_{c}+h_{c}\|)(\|\nabla a\|+\|\nabla c\|).

It follows that

\displaystyle\|\nabla a\|+\|\nabla c\|\leq

\displaystyle\,C(\|\partial^{0}b\|+\|l_{bi}+h_{bi}\|+\|l_{c}+h_{c}\|),

which can be controlled by
C\sum_{|\gamma|\leq N}\|\{\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{P}\}\partial^{\gamma}f(t)\|+C\sqrt{M_{0}}\sum_{|\gamma|\leq N}\|\partial^{\gamma}f(t)\|
.
Throughout the above deductions, we remark that the identity (
5.5
) of
c
plays a crucial role in estimating
\nabla a
. The high-order derivative analogies of this estimate are presented in Section
5
with some extra techniques.

1.7.
Outline of the article

The structure of this paper is organized as follows. Section
2
presents fundamental estimates for the Boltzmann operator, including essential proof details. In Section
3
, we establish the
L^{2}
regularity estimates for the Poisson-Poincaré equation (
1.2
). Section
4
focuses on demonstrating local well-posedness of the initial value problem (
1.4
)–(
1.6
) with small initial data through Picard iteration. The coercivity property of the linearized operator
\mathcal{L}
is rigorously proved in Section
5
(Theorem
5.1
). Finally, Section
6
constructs a global-in-time solution with exponential decay properties.

Preliminaries

For reader’s convenience, we present some basic estimates about the linearized operator
\mathcal{L}=\nu-\mathcal{K}
and the nonlinear operator
\Gamma

in this section.

Lemma 2.1
.

It holds
\langle\mathcal{L}g,h\rangle=\langle\mathcal{L}h,g\rangle
,
\langle\mathcal{L}g,g\rangle\geq 0
. And
\mathcal{L}g=0
if and only if

g=\mathbf{P}g,

where
\mathbf{P}:L_{v}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\rightarrow\mathscr{N}
is the orthogonal projection in
L_{v}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
to the null space
\mathscr{N}
of
\mathcal{L}
, which is a five dimensional space spanned by
\{\sqrt{\mu},v_{1}\sqrt{\mu},v_{2}\sqrt{\mu},v_{3}\sqrt{\mu},|v|^{2}\sqrt{\mu}\}
.

Moreover, there exists a constant
\delta>0
such that

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{L}g,g\rangle\geq\delta|\{\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{P}\}g|_{\nu}^{2}.

For the proofs, one can refer to
[
19
,
21
]
.

Lemma 2.2
.

For the hard-sphere collision kernel and the multi-index
\beta
, the collision frequency
\nu(v)
satisfies

(2.1)

\displaystyle\partial_{\beta}\nu(v)\leq C(1+|v|)^{1-|\beta|}.

In addition, for any
\eta>0
, there exists a
C_{\eta}>0
such that

(2.2)

\displaystyle\langle\partial_{\beta}[\nu g],\partial_{\beta}g\rangle

\displaystyle\,\geq|\partial_{\beta}g|_{\nu}^{2}-\eta\sum_{|\beta_{1}|\leq|\beta|}|\partial_{\beta_{1}}g|_{0}^{2}-C_{\eta}|g|_{0}^{2},\,\,\,\beta\neq 0,

(2.3)

\displaystyle|\langle\partial_{\beta}[\mathcal{K}g_{1}],\partial_{\beta}g_{2}\rangle|

\displaystyle\,\leq\bigg\{\eta\sum_{|\beta_{1}|\leq|\beta|}|\partial_{\beta_{1}}g_{1}|_{0}+C_{\eta}|g_{1}|_{0}\bigg\}|\partial_{\beta}g_{2}|_{0}.

Proof.

The proof of (
2.1
) can be found in
[
18
]
.
By using (
2.1
) and compact interpolation between
\partial_{\beta}
and
\partial_{0}
, we have

\displaystyle\langle\partial_{\beta}[\nu g],\partial_{\beta}g\rangle=\,

\displaystyle|\partial_{\beta}g|_{\nu}^{2}+\sum_{0<|\beta_{1}|\leq|\beta|}C_{\beta}^{\beta_{1}}\langle\partial_{\beta_{1}}\nu\partial_{\beta-\beta_{1}}g,\partial_{\beta}g\rangle

\displaystyle\geq\,

\displaystyle|\partial_{\beta}g|_{\nu}^{2}-\eta\sum_{|\beta_{1}|\leq|\beta|}|\partial_{\beta_{1}}g|_{0}^{2}-C_{\eta}|g|_{0}^{2}

for any
\eta>0
, thus (
2.2
) holds. For the proof of (
2.3
), note that

\displaystyle|\partial_{\beta}[\mathcal{K}g]|_{0}\leq\eta\sum_{|\beta_{1}|=|\beta|}|\partial_{\beta_{1}}g|_{0}+C_{\eta}|g|_{0},

which was presented in Lemma 2.1 in
[
11
]
. Thus (
2.3
) is valid.
∎

Lemma 2.3
.

Let the indices
\beta_{0}+\beta_{1}+\beta_{2}=\beta
,
\gamma_{1}+\gamma_{2}=\gamma
. It holds

\displaystyle\partial_{\beta}^{\gamma}\Gamma(g_{1},g_{2})=\sum C_{\beta}^{\beta_{0}\beta_{1}\beta_{2}}C_{\gamma}^{\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2}}\Gamma^{0}(\partial_{\beta_{1}}^{\gamma_{1}}g_{1},\partial_{\beta_{2}}^{\gamma_{2}}g_{2}),

where

\displaystyle\Gamma^{0}(\partial_{\beta_{1}}^{\gamma_{1}}g_{1},\partial_{\beta_{2}}^{\gamma_{2}}g_{2})=\,

\displaystyle\int\!\!\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}}|(v-u)\cdot\omega|\partial_{\beta_{0}}[\sqrt{\mu(u)}]\partial_{\beta_{1}}^{\gamma_{1}}g_{1}(u^{\prime})\partial_{\beta_{2}}^{\gamma_{2}}g_{2}(v^{\prime})dud\omega

\displaystyle-\int\!\!\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}}|(v-u)\cdot\omega|\partial_{\beta_{0}}[\sqrt{\mu(u)}]\partial_{\beta_{1}}^{\gamma_{1}}g_{1}(u)\partial_{\beta_{2}}^{\gamma_{2}}g_{2}(v)dud\omega

\displaystyle=:\,

\displaystyle\Gamma^{0}_{\rm{gain}}-\Gamma^{0}_{\rm{loss}}.

Moreover, for
|\beta|+|\gamma|\leq N(N\geq 4)
, we have

\displaystyle\left|\left(\Gamma^{0}(\partial_{\beta_{1}}^{\gamma_{1}}g_{1},\partial_{\beta_{2}}^{\gamma_{2}}g_{2}),\partial_{\beta}^{\gamma}g_{3}\right)\right|

\displaystyle\,\quad\leq C\bigg\{\sum_{|\beta_{k}|+|\gamma_{k}|\leq N}\|\partial_{\beta_{k}}^{\gamma_{k}}g_{1}\|\bigg\}\|\partial_{\beta_{2}}^{\gamma_{2}}g_{2}\|_{\nu}\|\partial_{\beta}^{\gamma}g_{3}\|_{\nu}+C\bigg\{\sum_{|\beta_{k}|+|\gamma_{k}|\leq N}\|\partial_{\beta_{k}}^{\gamma_{k}}g_{1}\|_{\nu}\bigg\}\|\partial_{\beta_{2}}^{\gamma_{2}}g_{2}\|\|\partial_{\beta}^{\gamma}g_{3}\|_{\nu}

(2.4)

\displaystyle\qquad\,+C\bigg\{\sum_{|\beta_{k}|+|\gamma_{k}|\leq N}\|\partial_{\beta_{k}}^{\gamma_{k}}g_{2}\|\bigg\}\|\partial_{\beta_{1}}^{\gamma_{1}}g_{1}\|_{\nu}\|\partial_{\beta}^{\gamma}g_{3}\|_{\nu}+C\bigg\{\sum_{|\beta_{k}|+|\gamma_{k}|\leq N}\|\partial_{\beta_{k}}^{\gamma_{k}}g_{2}\|_{\nu}\bigg\}\|\partial_{\beta_{1}}^{\gamma_{1}}g_{1}\|\|\partial_{\beta}^{\gamma}g_{3}\|_{\nu}.

Here,
(\cdot,\cdot)
is the inner product in
L^{2}(\mathbb{T}_{x}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}_{v})
.

Remark 2.4
.

For
|\beta|+|\gamma|=0
, it holds that
|\beta_{1}|+|\gamma_{1}|=0
and
|\beta_{2}|+|\gamma_{2}|=0
. Then we can deduce from (
2.4
) that

\displaystyle\left|\left(\Gamma(g_{1},g_{2}),g_{3}\right)\right|\leq C\bigg\{\sum_{|\gamma|\leq 2}\|\partial^{\gamma}g_{1}\|\bigg\}\|g_{2}\|_{\nu}\|g_{3}\|_{\nu}+C\bigg\{\sum_{|\gamma|\leq 2}\|\partial^{\gamma}g_{1}\|_{\nu}\bigg\}\|g_{2}\|\|g_{3}\|_{\nu},

or

\displaystyle\left|\left(\Gamma(g_{1},g_{2}),g_{3}\right)\right|\leq C\bigg\{\sum_{|\gamma|\leq 2}\|\partial^{\gamma}g_{2}\|\bigg\}\|g_{1}\|_{\nu}\|g_{3}\|_{\nu}+C\bigg\{\sum_{|\gamma|\leq 2}\|\partial^{\gamma}g_{2}\|_{\nu}\bigg\}\|g_{1}\|\|g_{3}\|_{\nu}.

Proof of Lemma
2.3
.

In light of Lemma 2.3 in Guo
[
19
]
, we obtain that

(2.5)

\displaystyle\big|\big\langle\Gamma^{0}(\partial_{\beta_{1}}^{\gamma_{1}}g_{1},\partial_{\beta_{2}}^{\gamma_{2}}g_{2}),\partial_{\beta}^{\gamma}g_{3}\big\rangle\big|\leq C|\partial_{\beta_{1}}^{\gamma_{1}}g_{1}|_{\nu}|\partial_{\beta_{2}}^{\gamma_{2}}g_{2}|_{0}|\partial_{\beta}^{\gamma}g_{3}|_{\nu}+C|\partial_{\beta_{2}}^{\gamma_{2}}g_{2}|_{\nu}|\partial_{\beta_{1}}^{\gamma_{1}}g_{1}|_{0}|\partial_{\beta}^{\gamma}g_{3}|_{\nu}.

Recalling
H^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})\subset L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{3})
, we have

\displaystyle\sup_{x}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|g(x,u)|^{2}du\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\sup_{x}|g(x,u)|^{2}du\leq C\sum_{|\gamma|\leq 2}\|\partial^{\gamma}g\|^{2},

\displaystyle\sup_{x}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nu(u)|g(x,u)|^{2}du\leq C\sum_{|\gamma|\leq 2}\|\partial^{\gamma}g\|_{\nu}^{2}.

By using the above embedding, we take the
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{3})
norm of
g_{1}
or
g_{2}
terms on the right-hand side of (
2.5
), specifically selecting the term with the lower total derivative order, i.e.,
|\beta_{i}|+|\gamma_{i}|\leq N/2
for
i=1,2
.
Integrating (
2.5
) over
x\in\mathbb{T}^{3}
yields (
2.4
).
∎

Lemma 2.5
.

We have

(2.6)

\displaystyle\left|\left(\Gamma(f,g),h\right)\right|\leq\sup_{x,v}|\mu^{-1/4}h|\|f\|\|g\|.

Proof.

It is easy to know that

\displaystyle\left|\langle\Gamma_{\rm{gain}}(f,g),h\rangle\right|\leq\,

\displaystyle\int\!\!\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}|u-v|\mu^{1/2}(u)f(u^{\prime})g(v^{\prime})h(v)dudv

\displaystyle\leq\,

\displaystyle\sup_{v}|\mu^{-1/4}h|\int\!\!\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}|u-v|\mu^{1/4}(u)\mu^{1/4}(v)f(u)g(v)dudv

\displaystyle\leq\,

\displaystyle\sup_{v}|\mu^{-1/4}h|\left(\int\!\!\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}|u-v|\mu^{1/4}(u)\mu^{1/4}(v)f^{2}(u)dudv\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\quad\times\left(\int\!\!\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}|u-v|\mu^{1/4}(u)\mu^{1/4}(v)g^{2}(v)dudv\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\,

\displaystyle\sup_{v}|\mu^{-1/4}h|\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\langle u\rangle\mu^{1/4}(u)f^{2}(u)du\right)^{1/2}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\langle v\rangle\mu^{1/4}(v)g^{2}(v)dv\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\,

\displaystyle\sup_{v}|\mu^{-1/4}h||f|_{0}|g|_{0}.

Integrating the above inequality over
\mathbb{T}^{3}
yields

\left|\left(\Gamma_{\rm{gain}}(f,g),h\right)\right|\leq\sup_{x,v}|\mu^{-1/4}h|\|f\|\|g\|
.
Similarly,
\Gamma_{\rm{loss}}(\cdot,\cdot)
also satisfies this estimate. The proof is complete.
∎

3.
L^{2}
Estimates of the Poisson-Poincaré Equation (
1.2
) in
\mathbb{T}^{3}

In this section, we investigate the well-posedness, regularity and quantitative estimates of the Poisson-Poincaré equation:

(3.1)

\displaystyle\Delta U=e^{U}-\rho\quad\rm{in}\,\,\,\mathbb{T}^{3}.

For the well-posedness of (
3.1
), we have

Theorem 3.1
.

Suppose that
\rho\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})
. Then, there exists a unique solution
U\in H^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})
to (
3.1
). Moreover, denoting
\sup_{t\geq 0}\|\rho-1\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}=M
, it holds that

\|U\|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}\leq CM+Ce^{2CM}+C
, where
C>0
is a constant independent of
U
and
\rho
.

Proof.

We split
U=\overline{U}+\widehat{U}
such that (
3.1
) is decomposed into two equations:

(3.2)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\Delta\overline{U}=1-\rho\quad\rm{in}\,\,\mathbb{T}^{3},\\
&(\overline{U})_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}=\frac{1}{|\mathbb{T}^{3}|}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\overline{U}dx=0,\end{aligned}\right.

and

(3.3)

\displaystyle\Delta\widehat{U}=e^{\overline{U}+\widehat{U}}-1\quad\rm{in}\,\,\mathbb{T}^{3}.

First, for the equation (
3.2
),
when
\rho-1\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})
, there exists a unique solution
\overline{U}\in H^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})
such that

(3.4)

\displaystyle\|\overline{U}\|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}\leq C\|\rho-1\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})},

where
C>0
is a constant independent of
\overline{U}
and
\rho
.

Second, we consider the equation (
3.3
)
with
\overline{U}\in H^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})
.
In light of Proposition 3.5 of
[
17
]
, where the direct method of calculus of variations is applied,
there exists a unique weak solution
\overline{U}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{3})
to (
3.3
).

To improve the regularity of
\widehat{U}
, we now deduce that

e^{\widehat{U}}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})
.
Actually, for
\widehat{U}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{3})
, consider the truncated function
\widehat{U}_{k}:=\min\{\widehat{U},k\}
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
. It holds that

e^{\widehat{U}_{k}}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{3})
,
\nabla\widehat{U}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})
and
\nabla e^{\widehat{U}_{k}}=e^{\widehat{U}_{k}}\nabla\widehat{U}\chi_{\{\widehat{U}<k\}}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})
.
Thus
e^{\widehat{U}_{k}}\in L^{\infty}\cap H^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{3})
. Using
e^{\widehat{U}_{k}}
as a test function to the equation (
3.3
), we have

\displaystyle 0=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\left(\nabla\widehat{U}\cdot\nabla e^{\widehat{U}_{k}}+e^{\overline{U}+\widehat{U}}e^{\widehat{U}_{k}}-e^{\widehat{U}_{k}}\right)dx\geq e^{-\|\overline{U}\|_{L^{\infty}}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}e^{\widehat{U}+\widehat{U}_{k}}dx-\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}e^{\widehat{U}_{k}}dx.

By using the monotone convergence theorem, we take
k\rightarrow\infty
to get

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}e^{\widehat{U}}dx\geq e^{-\|\overline{U}\|_{L^{\infty}}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}e^{2\widehat{U}}dx,

which implies that

\displaystyle\|e^{\widehat{U}}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}\leq e^{\frac{1}{2}\|\overline{U}\|_{L^{\infty}}}\|e^{\widehat{U}}\|^{\frac{1}{2}}_{L^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}.

Meanwhile, we deduce from (
3.3
) that

0=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\Delta\widehat{U}dx=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}e^{U}-1dx,

thus,

\|e^{\widehat{U}}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}e^{\widehat{U}}dx\leq e^{\|\overline{U}\|_{L^{\infty}}}|\mathbb{T}^{3}|=e^{\|\overline{U}\|_{L^{\infty}}}

and

\displaystyle\|e^{\widehat{U}}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}\leq e^{\frac{1}{2}\|\overline{U}\|_{L^{\infty}}}\|e^{\widehat{U}}\|^{\frac{1}{2}}_{L^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}\leq e^{\|\overline{U}\|_{L^{\infty}}}.

For the equation (
3.3
), there exists a constant
C>0
independent of
\widehat{U}
and
\overline{U}
such that

\displaystyle\|\widehat{U}\|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}\leq

\displaystyle\,C\|e^{\overline{U}+\widehat{U}}-1\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\,C\|e^{\widehat{U}}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}e^{\|\overline{U}\|_{L^{\infty}}}+C

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\,Ce^{2\|\overline{U}\|_{L^{\infty}}}+C.

Denote
\|\rho-1\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}
by
M
. By (
3.4
), we have

\|\overline{U}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}\leq C\|\overline{U}\|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}\leq CM,

where
C>0
is independent of
\overline{U}
and
M
.
Hence we get

\displaystyle\|U\|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}\leq\,\|\overline{U}\|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}+\|\widehat{U}\|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}\leq\,CM+Ce^{2CM}+C,

where
C>0
is independent of
U
and
\rho
. This completes the proof.
∎

Remark 3.2
.

In Theorem
3.1
, if we further assume
\sup_{t\geq 0}\|\rho-1\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}=M\ll 1
, then there exists a uniform upper bound
C>0
independent of the solution
U
, such that
\sup_{t\geq 0}\|U\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}\leq C
. It implies that
\sup_{t\geq 0}\|U\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}=O(1)
, which is crucial for the quantitative estimates of
\|U\|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}
as below.

As a quantitative estimate, we can control
\|U\|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}
by
\|\rho-1\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}
as the following

Proposition 3.3
.

Suppose that
U\in H^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})
solves (
3.1
) with
\rho\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})
. Then it holds

\displaystyle\|U\|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}\leq C\|\rho-1\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}

for some constant
C=C(\|U\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{3})})>0
.
Moreover, when
\sup_{t\geq 0}\|\rho-1\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}
is sufficiently small, the constant
C
is independent of
\|U\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}
.

Proof.

Note that the function
U\in H^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})\hookrightarrow C(\mathbb{T}^{3})
. For each
x\in\mathbb{T}^{3}
, by the mean value theorem, there exists a
U_{\xi}(x)\in[-U(x),U(x)]
such that
e^{U(x)}-1=U(x)e^{U_{\xi}(x)}
. It implies that, there exists a function
U_{\xi}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{3})
such that
e^{U}-1=Ue^{U_{\xi}}
in
\mathbb{T}^{3}
, where
e^{-\|U\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}}\leq e^{U_{\xi}}\leq e^{\|U\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}}
.

The equation (
3.1
) can thus be rewritten as

(3.5)

\displaystyle\Delta U=Ue^{U_{\xi}}+1-\rho.

Taking
L^{2}
inner product of the above equation with
U
yields

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}|\nabla U|^{2}+|U|^{2}e^{U_{\xi}}dx=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}U(\rho-1)dx,

which implies that
\|U\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}\leq e^{\|U\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}}\|\rho-1\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}
. Note that

\displaystyle\|D^{2}U\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}=\,

\displaystyle\|Ue^{U_{\xi}}+1-\rho\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}

\displaystyle\leq\,

\displaystyle\|U\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}e^{\|U\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}}+\|\rho-1\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}

\displaystyle\leq\,

\displaystyle(e^{2\|U\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}}+1)\|\rho-1\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}.

Hence we get

(3.6)

\displaystyle\|U\|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}\leq 3e^{2\|U\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}}\|\rho-1\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})},

which implies that

(3.7)

\displaystyle\|U\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}\leq Ce^{2\|U\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}}\|\rho-1\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}.

By Remark
3.2
, when
\sup_{t\geq 0}\|\rho-1\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}\ll 1
, we have
\sup_{t\geq 0}\|U\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}=O(1)
.
Furthermore,
by the estimate (
3.7
), we deduce that
\sup_{t\geq 0}\|U\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}=o(1)
. Hence, by the estimate (
3.6
), we have

\displaystyle\|U\|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}\leq 3e^{o(1)}\|\rho-1\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}\leq 4\|\rho-1\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}.

The proof is complete.
∎

Corollary 3.4
.

Suppose that
U_{i}\in H^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})
solves (
3.1
) with
\rho_{i}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})
for
i=1,2
.
Then,

\displaystyle\|U_{1}-U_{2}\|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}\leq C\|\rho_{1}-\rho_{2}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}

holds for some constant
C=C(\max\limits_{i=1,2}\|U_{i}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{3})})>0
.
Moreover, when
\max\limits_{i=1,2}\sup_{t\geq 0}\|\rho_{i}-1\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}
is sufficiently small, the constant
C
is independent of
\max\limits_{i=1,2}\|U_{i}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}
.

Proof.

Take the difference for
U_{1}
and
U_{2}
to get

\displaystyle\Delta(U_{1}-U_{2})=e^{U_{1}}-e^{U_{2}}-(\rho_{1}-\rho_{2})=(U_{1}-U_{2})e^{\overline{U}_{\xi}}-(\rho_{1}-\rho_{2}),

where the value of
\overline{U}_{\xi}(x)
lies between
U_{1}(x)
and
U_{2}(x)
for every
x\in\mathbb{T}^{3}
and hence
|\overline{U}_{\xi}|\leq\max\limits_{i=1,2}\|U_{i}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}
. Through the similar arguments to that in Proposition
3.3
, we obtain that

(3.8)

\displaystyle\|U_{1}-U_{2}\|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}\leq 3e^{2\max\limits_{i=1,2}\|U_{i}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}}\|\rho_{1}-\rho_{2}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}.

Furthermore, by Proposition
3.3
, if
\max\limits_{i=1,2}\sup_{t\geq 0}\|\rho_{i}-1\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}
is sufficiently small, then

\max\limits_{i=1,2}\sup_{t\geq 0}\|U_{i}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}=o(1).

By the estimate (
3.8
), we have

\displaystyle\|U_{1}-U_{2}\|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}\leq\,3e^{o(1)}\|\rho_{1}-\rho_{2}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}\leq\,4\|\rho_{1}-\rho_{2}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}.

The proof is complete.
∎

Our next intention is to iterate the argument above to deduce that the solution
U
lies in higher order Sobolev spaces provided that
\rho
has sufficient regularities.

Proposition 3.5
.

Suppose that
U\in H^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})
solves (
3.1
) with
\rho\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})
.
Let
m
be a positive integer and the multi-index
\gamma=[\gamma_{0},\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2},\gamma_{3}]
with
1\leq|\gamma|\leq m
. Assume that

\sup_{t\geq 0}\|\partial^{\gamma}\rho\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}<\infty
and

(3.9)

\displaystyle\sup_{t\geq 0}\sum_{1\leq|\gamma^{\prime}|\leq m-1}\|\partial^{\gamma^{\prime}}\rho\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}+\sup_{t\geq 0}\|\rho-1\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}\ll 1.

Then for any
t\geq 0
and
1\leq|\gamma|\leq m
, we have

(3.10)

\displaystyle\|\partial^{\gamma}U(t,x)\|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}\leq C\sum_{1\leq|\gamma^{\prime}|\leq m}\|\partial^{\gamma^{\prime}}\rho(t,x)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})},

where the constant
C
is independent of
U
and
\rho
.

Proof.

Suppose
U\in H^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})
satisfies
\Delta U=e^{U}-\rho
with
\rho\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})
, we consider a mapping

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}:\,\ \

\displaystyle H^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})\times L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})\rightarrow L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})

\displaystyle(v,w)\mapsto\Delta v-e^{v}+w.

Note that
\mathcal{F}(U,\rho)=0
and the Fréchet derivative
\frac{\partial\mathcal{F}}{\partial v}(U,\rho)=\Delta-e^{U}\in\mathcal{L}(H^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3});L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3}))
is reversible by the Lax-Milgram theorem. Then, we use the implicit function theorem
to get the Fréchet derivative

U^{\prime}(\rho)=-\left(\frac{\partial\mathcal{F}}{\partial v}(U,\rho)\right)^{-1}\frac{\partial\mathcal{F}}{\partial w}(U,\rho)=-(\Delta-e^{U})^{-1}.

For
|\gamma|=1
, by the chain rules, we deduce that
\partial^{\gamma}U=U^{\prime}(\rho)\partial^{\gamma}\rho=-(\Delta-e^{U})^{-1}\partial^{\gamma}\rho
, which is rewritten as

\displaystyle\Delta(\partial^{\gamma}U)-(\partial^{\gamma}U)e^{U}+\partial^{\gamma}\rho=0.

Assuming
\sup_{t\geq 0}\|\rho-1\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}\ll 1
and
\sup_{t\geq 0}\|\partial^{\gamma}\rho(t,x)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}<\infty
,
through the similar procedure to that in Proposition
3.3
, we deduce that

\displaystyle\|\partial^{\gamma}U\|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}\leq 3e^{2\|U\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}}\|\partial^{\gamma}\rho\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}\leq 4\|\partial^{\gamma}\rho\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})},\quad\forall\,t\geq 0,\,\,\,|\gamma|=1.

This verifies the assertion (
3.10
) for
|\gamma|=1
.

For
|\gamma|=2,\ldots,m
, we assume the assertion (
3.10
) holds. We now consider the case of
|\gamma|=m+1
.

From the equation
\Delta U=e^{U}-\rho
, we have

\displaystyle\Delta\partial^{\gamma}U-e^{U}\partial^{\gamma}U=[\partial^{\gamma}(e^{U})-e^{U}\partial^{\gamma}U]-\partial^{\gamma}\rho=:\mathcal{S}-\partial^{\gamma}\rho,

where
|\gamma|=m+1
, and

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}:=e^{U}\sum^{m+1}_{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p_{1}+\cdots+p_{k}=|\gamma|\\
|p_{j}|\geq 1\end{subarray}}\frac{|\gamma|!}{p_{1}!\cdots p_{k}!}\partial^{p_{1}}U\cdots\partial^{p_{k}}U.

When

\sup_{t\geq 0}\sum_{1\leq|\gamma^{\prime}|\leq m}\|\partial^{\gamma^{\prime}}\rho\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}+\sup_{t\geq 0}\|\rho-1\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}\ll 1,

we have

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{S}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}\leq

\displaystyle\,Ce^{\|U\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}}\|\partial^{p_{1}}U\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}\|\partial^{p_{2}}U\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}\cdots\|\partial^{p_{k}}U\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\,Ce^{4\|\rho-1\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}}\sum_{1\leq|\gamma^{\prime}|\leq m}\|\partial^{\gamma^{\prime}}\rho\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\,C\sum_{1\leq|\gamma^{\prime}|\leq m}\|\partial^{\gamma^{\prime}}\rho\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}.

Hence, for
|\gamma|=m+1
, we obtain that

\displaystyle\|\partial^{\gamma}U\|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}\leq

\displaystyle\,3e^{2\|U\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}}\|\mathcal{S}-\partial^{\gamma}\rho\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\,4(\|\mathcal{S}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}+\|\partial^{\gamma}\rho\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\,C\sum_{1\leq|\gamma^{\prime}|\leq m}\|\partial^{\gamma^{\prime}}\rho\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}+C\|\partial^{\gamma}\rho\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}.

which indicates that (
3.10
) holds for
|\gamma|=m+1
. Inductively, we complete the proof.
∎

Theorem 4.1
.

There exist
M_{0}>0
and
T_{0}>0
such that if
T_{0}\leq M_{0}/2
and
\mathcal{E}(0)\leq M_{0}/2

)–(
1.5

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq t\leq T_{0}}\mathcal{E}(t)\leq M_{0},

and
\mathcal{E}(t)

[0,T_{0}]

F_{0}(x,v)=\mu(v)+\sqrt{\mu(v)}f_{0}(x,v)\geq 0

1.7
)–(
1.9

\displaystyle\{\partial_{t}+v\cdot\nabla_{x}+E^{n}\cdot\nabla_{v}+R(F^{n})\}F^{n+1}=

\displaystyle\,Q_{\rm{gain}}(F^{n},F^{n})

(4.1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\int\!\!\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}}|(v-u)\cdot\omega|F^{n}(v^{\prime})F^{n}(u^{\prime})dud\omega

with the initial datum
F^{n+1}(0,x,v)=F_{0}(x,v)
,

\displaystyle R(F^{n}):=\int\!\!\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}}|(v-u)\cdot\omega|F^{n}(u)dud\omega.

We take
F^{0}(t,x,v)\equiv F_{0}(x,v)

F^{n}=\mu+\sqrt{\mu}f^{n}
for
n\in\mathbb{N}

f^{0}(t,x,v)\equiv f_{0}(x,v)
and
f^{n+1}(0,x,v)=f_{0}(x,v)

Equivalently to (
4.1

(4.2)

We now prove that, there exist
M_{0}>0
and
T_{0}>0

\mathcal{E}(0)\leq M_{0}/2

4.1

Taking
\partial_{\beta}^{\gamma}

4.2

\displaystyle\left\{\partial_{t}+v\cdot\nabla_{x}+E^{n}\cdot\nabla_{v}-\frac{v}{2}\cdot E^{n}\right\}\partial_{\beta}^{\gamma}f^{n+1}+\partial_{\beta}[\nu\partial^{\gamma}f^{n+1}]

\displaystyle\quad\,=\partial^{\gamma}E^{n}\cdot\partial_{\beta}(v\sqrt{\mu})-\sum_{\beta_{1}\neq 0}C_{\beta}^{\beta_{1}}\partial_{\beta_{1}}v\cdot\nabla_{x}\partial_{\beta-\beta_{1}}^{\gamma}f^{n+1}+\partial_{\beta}[\mathcal{K}\partial^{\gamma}f^{n}]

(4.3)

\displaystyle\qquad\,+\sum C_{\gamma}^{\gamma_{1}}\partial_{\beta}\left\{\Gamma_{\rm{gain}}(\partial^{\gamma_{1}}f^{n},\partial^{\gamma-\gamma_{1}}f^{n})-\Gamma_{\rm{loss}}(\partial^{\gamma_{1}}f^{n},\partial^{\gamma-\gamma_{1}}f^{n+1})\right\}.

Now we take
L^{2}

4.3

\partial_{\beta}^{\gamma}f^{n+1}

|\gamma|+|\beta|\leq N

\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}

(i). First, we have

\displaystyle\partial_{\beta}^{\gamma}f^{n+1}\left\{\partial_{t}+v\cdot\nabla_{x}+E^{n}\cdot\nabla_{v}-\frac{v}{2}\cdot E^{n}\right\}\partial_{\beta}^{\gamma}f^{n+1}

2.2

\displaystyle\int\!\!\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}\partial_{\beta}^{\gamma}f^{n+1}\partial_{\beta}[\nu\partial^{\gamma}f^{n+1}]dxdv

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|\partial_{\beta}^{\gamma}f^{n+1}(t)\|^{2}+\int\!\!\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}\big(\nu-\frac{1}{2}v\cdot E^{n}\big)|\partial_{\beta}^{\gamma}f^{n+1}|^{2}dxdv-\eta\sum_{|\beta_{1}|\leq|\beta|}\|\partial_{\beta_{1}}^{\gamma}f^{n+1}\|^{2}-C_{\eta}\|\partial^{\gamma}f^{n+1}\|^{2}.

(iia). The first term contributes

(\vvvert f^{n}\vvvert+\vvvert f^{n+1}\vvvert)\vvvert f^{n+1}\vvvert
.

(iic). The second line on the right-hand side is controlled by

\displaystyle C\sum_{\gamma_{1}\neq 0,|\beta_{1}|=1}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}|\partial^{\gamma_{1}}E^{n}|\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\partial_{\beta-\beta_{1}}^{\gamma-\gamma_{1}}f^{n+1}|^{2}dv\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\partial_{\beta}^{\gamma}f^{n+1}|^{2}dv\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}dx

(4.4)

\displaystyle\quad+C\sum_{\gamma_{1}\neq 0}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}|\partial^{\gamma_{1}}E^{n}|\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla_{v}\partial_{\beta}^{\gamma-\gamma_{1}}f^{n+1}|^{2}dv\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\partial_{\beta}^{\gamma}f^{n+1}|^{2}dv\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}dx.

\displaystyle\sup_{x}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|g(x,u)|^{2}du\leq C\sum_{|\gamma|\leq 2}\|\partial^{\gamma}g\|^{2}.

Thus, when

,

\displaystyle C\|\partial^{\gamma_{1}}E^{n}\|\sum_{|\gamma-\gamma_{1}|+1+2\leq|\gamma|}\|\nabla_{v}\partial_{\beta}^{\gamma-\gamma_{1}}f^{n+1}\|\vvvert f^{n+1}\vvvert\leq C\|\partial^{\gamma_{1}}E^{n}\|\vvvert f^{n+1}\vvvert^{2}\leq C\vvvert E^{n}\vvvert\vvvert f^{n+1}\vvvert^{2}.

(iid). By using Lemma

\displaystyle C(\vvvert f^{n}\vvvert\vvvert f^{n}\vvvert_{\nu}\vvvert f^{n+1}\vvvert_{\nu}+\vvvert f^{n+1}\vvvert\vvvert f^{n}\vvvert_{\nu}\vvvert f^{n+1}\vvvert_{\nu}+\vvvert f^{n}\vvvert\vvvert f^{n+1}\vvvert^{2}_{\nu}).

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{E}_{n+1}^{\prime}(t):=\,

\displaystyle C\vvvert E^{n}\vvvert\left(\vvvert f^{n+1}\vvvert+\vvvert f^{n+1}\vvvert^{2}\right)+C(\vvvert f^{n}\vvvert+\vvvert f^{n+1}\vvvert)\vvvert f^{n+1}\vvvert

We denote the solution to the ionic Vlasov-Poisson-Boltzmann system (

)–(

.

Next, we sketch the argument for proving the uniqueness of solution

. If there is another solution

\displaystyle\{\partial_{t}+v\cdot\nabla_{x}+E_{f}\cdot\nabla_{v}+\nu\}(f-g)

\displaystyle\quad=(E_{f}-E_{g})\cdot v\sqrt{\mu}-(E_{f}-E_{g})\cdot\nabla_{v}g+\frac{v}{2}\cdot E_{f}(f-g)+\frac{v}{2}\cdot(E_{f}-E_{g})g

(4.6)

\displaystyle\quad\,\,\,\,\,\,\,+\mathcal{K}(f-g)+\Gamma(f-g,f)+\Gamma(g,f-g)

with
(f-g)|_{t=0}=f(0,x,v)-g(0,x,v)=0
,
where
(E_{f},\phi_{f})
and
(E_{g},\phi_{g})
satisfy

\left\{\begin{aligned} &E_{f}=-\nabla_{x}\phi_{f},\\
&\Delta\phi_{f}=e^{\phi_{f}}-1-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\sqrt{\mu}fdv,\end{aligned}\right.

and

\left\{\begin{aligned} &E_{g}=-\nabla_{x}\phi_{g},\\
&\Delta\phi_{g}=e^{\phi_{g}}-1-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\sqrt{\mu}gdv,\end{aligned}\right.

respectively. Recalling Corollary
3.4
, we get the crucial estimate

\displaystyle\|\phi_{f}-\phi_{g}\|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}\leq C\|f-g\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3})},

for the constant
C>0
independent of
\phi_{f}
and
\phi_{g}
.
Taking the inner product of (
4.6
) with
f-g
and noticing Remark
2.4
, we deduce that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|f-g\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|f-g\|^{2}_{\nu}

\displaystyle\leq\,

\displaystyle\|E_{f}-E_{g}\|\|f-g\|+\sqrt{M_{0}}\|f-g\|^{2}_{\nu}+\sqrt{M_{0}}\|E_{f}-E_{g}\|\|f-g\|_{\nu}+C\|f-g\|^{2}

\displaystyle+\bigg\{\sum_{|\gamma|\leq 2}[\|\partial^{\gamma}f\|+\|\partial^{\gamma}g\|]\bigg\}\|f-g\|_{\nu}^{2}+\bigg\{\sum_{|\gamma|\leq 2}[\|\partial^{\gamma}f\|_{\nu}+\|\partial^{\gamma}g\|_{\nu}]\bigg\}\|f-g\|\|f-g\|_{\nu}

\displaystyle\leq\,

\displaystyle C\left(\vvvert f\vvvert_{\nu}^{2}+\vvvert g\vvvert_{\nu}^{2}+1\right)\|f-g\|^{2}+(C\sqrt{M_{0}}+\frac{3}{4})\|f-g\|_{\nu}^{2}.

For
\eta\ll 1
and
M_{0}\ll 1
, it follows that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\|f-g\|^{2}(t)\leq\frac{1}{2}\|f-g\|^{2}(0)+C\int_{0}^{t}\left(\vvvert f\vvvert_{\nu}^{2}+\vvvert g\vvvert_{\nu}^{2}+1\right)\|f-g\|^{2}(s)ds,

which indicates that

f(t)\equiv g(t)
by Gronwall’s inequality.

The continuity of
\mathcal{E}(t)
comes from (
4.5
), and the positivity of
F=\mu+\sqrt{\mu}f
comes from a simple induction over
F^{n},n\in\mathbb{N}
. This completes the proof of Theorem
4.1
.
∎

5.
Coercivity of
\mathcal{L}

To extend the local solution of the ionic Vlasov-Poisson-Boltzmann system (
1.4
)–(
1.5
) to be a global
one, a crucial step is to establish the coercivity of the linearized collision operator
\mathcal{L}
.
We have

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
f(t,x,v)
be a classical solution to the system (
1.4
)–(
1.5
) satisfying
(
1.7
)–(
1.9
). There exist
M_{0}\ll 1
and
\delta_{0}=\delta_{0}(M_{0})>0
such that if

\displaystyle\sum_{|\gamma|\leq N}\|\partial^{\gamma}f(t)\|^{2}\leq M_{0},

then

(5.1)

\displaystyle\sum_{|\gamma|\leq N}(\mathcal{L}\partial^{\gamma}f(t),\partial^{\gamma}f(t))\geq\delta_{0}\sum_{|\gamma|\leq N}\|\partial^{\gamma}f(t)\|_{\nu}^{2}.

Proof.

Recall the decomposition
f=\mathbf{P}f+\{\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{P}\}f
in Section
2
and expand the macroscopic part of
f
as

\mathbf{P}f(t,x,v)=\{a^{f}(t,x)+b^{f}(t,x)\cdot v+c^{f}(t,x)|v|^{2}\}\sqrt{\mu}.

In the sequel, we denote
[a,b,c]:=[a^{f},b^{f},c^{f}]
for notational simplification.
For the time-space derivatives
\partial^{\gamma}=\partial_{t}^{\gamma_{0}}\partial_{x_{1}}^{\gamma_{1}}\partial_{x_{2}}^{\gamma_{2}}\partial_{x_{3}}^{\gamma_{3}}
, we immediately know that
\partial^{\gamma}\mathbf{P}f=\mathbf{P}\partial^{\gamma}f
, and

\displaystyle\|\partial^{\gamma}\mathbf{P}f\|^{2}+\|\partial^{\gamma}\{\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{P}\}f\|^{2}=\|\partial^{\gamma}f\|^{2}.

Moreover, there exists
C>1
such that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{C}\|\partial^{\gamma}\mathbf{P}f\|_{\nu}^{2}\leq\|\partial^{\gamma}a\|^{2}+\|\partial^{\gamma}b\|^{2}+\|\partial^{\gamma}c\|^{2}\leq C\|\partial^{\gamma}\mathbf{P}f\|^{2}.

To prove (
5.1
) in Theorem
5.1
, since there exists a
\delta>0
such that

\displaystyle(\mathcal{L}\partial^{\gamma}f,\partial^{\gamma}f)\geq\delta\|\{\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{P}\}\partial^{\gamma}f\|_{\nu}^{2},

it suffices to show that

\displaystyle\sum_{|\gamma|\leq N}\|\mathbf{P}\partial^{\gamma}f(t)\|_{\nu}\leq C\sum_{|\gamma|\leq N}\|\{\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{P}\}\partial^{\gamma}f(t)\|_{\nu}.

We thus only need to verify that

(5.2)

\displaystyle\sum_{|\gamma|\leq N}\{\|\partial^{\gamma}a\|+\|\partial^{\gamma}b\|+\|\partial^{\gamma}c\|\}\leq C\sum_{|\gamma|\leq N}\|\{\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{P}\}\partial^{\gamma}f(t)\|+C\sqrt{M_{0}}\sum_{|\gamma|\leq N}\|\partial^{\gamma}f(t)\|

holds for
M_{0}\ll 1
.

To prove (
5.2
), we present several lemmas below.

Lemma 5.2
.

Under the same condition as in Theorem
5.1
, we have

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}a(t,x)dx\right|+\left|\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}c(t,x)dx\right|\leq C\{\|\phi\|^{2}+\|\nabla_{x}\phi\|^{2}\},

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}b(t,x)dx\equiv 0.

Proof.

Plugging
\mathbf{P}f=\{a+b\cdot v+c|v|^{2}\}\sqrt{\mu}
into the conservation laws (
1.7
)–(
1.9
), we deduce that

\displaystyle\varrho_{0}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}adx+\varrho_{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}cdx=0,

\displaystyle\varrho_{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}b\,dx=0,

\displaystyle\varrho_{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}adx+\varrho_{4}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}cdx+2\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\phi e^{\phi}dx+\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}|\nabla\phi|^{2}dx=0,

where
\varrho_{i}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|v|^{i}\mu(v)dv,i=1,\dots,4
. Note that
\varrho_{0}\varrho_{4}\neq\varrho_{2}^{2}
and

\varrho_{0}\varrho_{4},\varrho_{2}^{2}>0
. First, we have

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}b\,dx=0
and

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}adx\right|+\left|\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}cdx\right|\leq C\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\phi e^{\phi}dx+\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}|\nabla\phi|^{2}dx.

By Proposition
3.3
, when
M_{0}\ll 1
, it holds

\displaystyle\|\phi\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}\leq C\|\phi\|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3})}\leq C\sqrt{M_{0}}\ll 1.

Thus, by (
1.10
),

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\phi e^{\phi}dx=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\left(\phi e^{\phi}-e^{\phi}+1\right)dx=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\left\{\phi^{2}+(\phi-1)[e^{\phi}-\phi-1]\right\}dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\phi^{2}+C(\|\phi\|_{\infty}+1)|\phi|^{2}dx\leq C\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\phi^{2}dx,

which implies the desired result.
∎

Rewrite the perturbed Vlasov-Boltzmann equation (
1.4
) as

(5.3)

\displaystyle\{\partial_{t}+v\cdot\nabla_{x}\}\mathbf{P}f-E\cdot v\sqrt{\mu}=l(\{\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{P}\}f)+h(f),

where

\displaystyle l(\{\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{P}\}f):=

\displaystyle-\{\partial_{t}+v\cdot\nabla_{x}+\mathcal{L}\}\{\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{P}\}f,

\displaystyle h(f):=

\displaystyle-E\cdot\nabla_{v}f+\frac{v}{2}\cdot Ef+\Gamma(f,f).

We plug
\mathbf{P}f=\{a+b\cdot v+c|v|^{2}\}\sqrt{\mu}
into the left-hand side of (
5.3
) (denote
\partial^{0}=\partial_{t}
,
\partial^{j}=\partial_{x_{j}}
,
\partial^{i}=\partial_{x_{i}}
) to get

\displaystyle\bigg[\{\partial^{i}c\}v_{i}|v|^{2}+\{\partial^{0}c+\partial^{i}b_{i}\}v_{i}^{2}+\sum_{i<j}\{\partial^{i}b_{j}+\partial^{j}b_{i}\}v_{i}v_{j}+\{\partial^{0}b_{i}+\partial^{i}a-E_{i}\}v_{i}+\partial^{0}a\bigg]\sqrt{\mu}.

Under the basis
[1,v_{i},v_{i}v_{j},v_{i}^{2},v_{i}|v|^{2}]\sqrt{\mu}
(
1\leq i<j\leq 3
), we define

(5.4)

\displaystyle l+h:=(l_{c}+h_{c})\cdot v|v|^{2}\sqrt{\mu}+(l_{i}+h_{i})v_{i}^{2}\sqrt{\mu}+\sum_{i<j}(l_{ij}+h_{ij})v_{i}v_{j}\sqrt{\mu}+(l_{bi}+h_{bi})v_{i}\sqrt{\mu}+(l_{a}+h_{a})\sqrt{\mu}.

Comparing with the coefficients on both sides of (
5.3
), we deduce that

(5.5)

\displaystyle\nabla_{x}c=l_{c}+h_{c},

(5.6)

\displaystyle\partial^{0}c+\partial^{i}b_{i}=l_{i}+h_{i},

(5.7)

\displaystyle\partial^{i}b_{j}+\partial^{j}b_{i}=l_{ij}+h_{ij},\,\,i\neq j,

(5.8)

\displaystyle\partial^{0}b_{i}+\partial^{i}a-E_{i}=l_{bi}+h_{bi},

(5.9)

\displaystyle\partial^{0}a=l_{a}+h_{a}.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
\gamma=[\gamma_{0},\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2},\gamma_{3}]
. Then for any
1\leq i\neq j\leq 3
, it holds

\displaystyle\sum_{|\gamma|\leq N-1}\|\partial^{\gamma}l_{c}\|+\|\partial^{\gamma}l_{i}\|+\|\partial^{\gamma}l_{ij}\|+\|\partial^{\gamma}l_{bi}\|+\|\partial^{\gamma}l_{a}\|\leq C\sum_{|\gamma|\leq N}\|\{\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{P}\}\partial^{\gamma}f\|.

Proof.

Denote
\{\epsilon_{n}(v),n=1,\ldots,13\}
to be the basis
[1,v_{i},v_{i}^{2},v_{i}v_{j},v_{i}|v|^{2}]\sqrt{\mu}
. Then for fixed
(t,x)
,
\partial^{\gamma}l_{c}
,
\partial^{\gamma}l_{i}
,
\partial^{\gamma}l_{ij}
,
\partial^{\gamma}l_{bi}
and
\partial^{\gamma}l_{a}
take the forms of the linear combinations of

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\partial^{\gamma}l(\{\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{P}\}f)(t,x,v)\cdot\epsilon_{n}(v)dv.

By Hölder’s inequality and Lemma
2.2
, it holds

\displaystyle\left\|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\partial^{\gamma}l(\{\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{P}\}f)(t,x,v)\cdot\epsilon_{n}(v)dv\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\,

\displaystyle\left\|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\{\partial_{t}+v\cdot\nabla_{x}+\mathcal{L}\}[\{\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{P}\}\partial^{\gamma}f](t,x,v)\cdot\epsilon_{n}(v)dv\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\,

\displaystyle C\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\epsilon_{n}(v)|\left(|\{\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{P}\}\partial_{t}\partial^{\gamma}f|^{2}+|v|^{2}|\{\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{P}\}\nabla_{x}\partial^{\gamma}f|^{2}+|\mathcal{L}\{\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{P}\}\partial^{\gamma}f|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\,

\displaystyle C\|\{\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{P}\}\partial^{0}\partial^{\gamma}f\|^{2}+C\|\{\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{P}\}\nabla_{x}\partial^{\gamma}f\|^{2}+\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nu^{2}\epsilon_{n}(v)|\left(|\nu^{-1}\mathcal{L}\{\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{P}\}\partial^{\gamma}f|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\,

\displaystyle C\left\{\|\{\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{P}\}\partial^{0}\partial^{\gamma}f\|^{2}+\|\{\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{P}\}\nabla_{x}\partial^{\gamma}f\|^{2}+\|\{\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{P}\}\partial^{\gamma}f\|^{2}\right\}.

Hence the lemma is proved.
∎

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
\gamma=[\gamma_{0},\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2},\gamma_{3}]
. Suppose that

\sum_{|\gamma|\leq N}\|\partial^{\gamma}f(t)\|^{2}\leq M_{0}
holds for some
M_{0}>0
,
then for any
1\leq i\neq j\leq 3
,

\displaystyle\sum_{|\gamma|\leq N}\|\partial^{\gamma}h_{c}\|+\|\partial^{\gamma}h_{i}\|+\|\partial^{\gamma}h_{ij}\|+\|\partial^{\gamma}h_{bi}\|+\|\partial^{\gamma}h_{a}\|\leq C\sqrt{M_{0}}\sum_{|\gamma|\leq N}\|\partial^{\gamma}f\|.

Proof.

Recall the basis
\{\epsilon_{n}(v),n=1,\ldots,13\}
. Then for fixed
(t,x)
,
\partial^{\gamma}h_{c}
,
\partial^{\gamma}h_{i}
,
\partial^{\gamma}h_{ij}
,
\partial^{\gamma}h_{bi}
and
\partial^{\gamma}h_{a}
take the forms of the linear combinations of

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\partial^{\gamma}h(f)(t,x,v)\cdot\epsilon_{n}(v)dv=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\partial^{\gamma}\left(-E\cdot\nabla_{v}f+\frac{v}{2}\cdot Ef+\Gamma(f,f)\right)(t,x,v)\cdot\epsilon_{n}(v)dv.

Note that

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\partial^{\gamma}(E\cdot\nabla_{v}f)\cdot\epsilon_{n}(v)dv=\,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\partial^{\gamma}(Ef)\cdot\nabla_{v}\epsilon_{n}(v)dv

\displaystyle\leq\,

\displaystyle\sum_{\gamma_{1}}C_{\gamma}^{\gamma_{1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\partial^{\gamma_{1}}E||\partial^{\gamma-\gamma_{1}}f||\nabla_{v}\epsilon_{n}(v)|dv

\displaystyle\leq\,

\displaystyle C\sum_{\gamma_{1}}|\partial^{\gamma_{1}}E|\left\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\mu^{1/8}\partial^{\gamma-\gamma_{1}}f|^{2}dv\right\}^{1/2}.

Thus

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\partial^{\gamma}\left(-E\cdot\nabla_{v}f+\frac{v}{2}\cdot Ef\right)\cdot\epsilon_{n}(v)dv\right|^{2}dx\leq C\sum_{\gamma_{1}}\int\!\!\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\partial^{\gamma_{1}}E|^{2}|\mu^{1/8}\partial^{\gamma-\gamma_{1}}f|^{2}dvdx.

If
|\gamma_{1}|\leq N/2

(N\geq 4)
, by
H^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{3})
and Proposition
3.5
, we have

\displaystyle\sup_{x}|\partial^{\gamma_{1}}E|\leq C\sum_{|\gamma|\leq N}\|\partial^{\gamma}E\|\leq C\sum_{|\gamma|\leq N}\|\partial^{\gamma}f\|\leq C\sqrt{M_{0}},

and

\displaystyle\left(\int\!\!\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\mu^{1/8}\partial^{\gamma-\gamma_{1}}f|^{2}dvdx\right)^{1/2}\leq C\sum_{|\gamma|\leq N}\|\partial^{\gamma}f\|.

If
|\gamma_{1}|\geq N/2
, then
|\gamma-\gamma_{1}|\leq N/2
. Thus, by
H^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{3})
, it holds

\displaystyle\sup_{x}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\partial^{\gamma-\gamma_{1}}f|^{2}dv\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\sup_{x}|\partial^{\gamma-\gamma_{1}}f|^{2}dv\leq C\sum_{|\gamma|\leq N}\|\partial^{\gamma}f\|^{2}\leq C(\sqrt{M_{0}})^{2},

and

\displaystyle\sup_{x}|\partial^{\gamma_{1}}E|\leq C\sum_{|\gamma|\leq N}\|\partial^{\gamma}E\|\leq C\sum_{|\gamma|\leq N}\|\partial^{\gamma}f\|.

Hence the contribution of
-E\cdot\nabla_{v}f+\frac{v}{2}\cdot Ef
is
C\sqrt{M_{0}}\sum_{|\gamma|\leq N}\|\partial^{\gamma}f\|
.

For the term
\Gamma(f,f)
, by Lemma
2.5
, we deduce that

\displaystyle\left\|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\partial^{\gamma}\Gamma(f,f)\cdot\epsilon_{n}(v)dv\right\|

\displaystyle\,\leq\sum C_{\gamma}^{\gamma_{1}}\left\|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\Gamma(\partial^{\gamma_{1}}f,\partial^{\gamma-\gamma_{1}}f)\cdot\epsilon_{n}(v)dv\right\|

\displaystyle\,\leq C\sqrt{M_{0}}\sum_{|\gamma|\leq N}\|\partial^{\gamma}f\|.

Thus, the proof is complete.
∎

Based on the above facts, we come back to prove (

), i.e.,

\displaystyle\sum_{|\gamma|\leq N}\{\|\partial^{\gamma}a\|+\|\partial^{\gamma}b\|+\|\partial^{\gamma}c\|\}\leq C\sum_{|\gamma|\leq N}\|\{\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{P}\}\partial^{\gamma}f(t)\|+C\sqrt{M_{0}}\sum_{|\gamma|\leq N}\|\partial^{\gamma}f(t)\|

holds for
M_{0}\ll 1
.
We divide the proof into several steps as follows.

Step 1. The estimate of
\nabla\partial^{\gamma}b
,
|\gamma|\leq N-1
.

By using the identities (
5.6
) and (
5.7
), we deduce that (as in
[

]
)

\displaystyle\Delta\partial^{\gamma}b_{i}=\,

\displaystyle\sum_{j\neq i}[\partial^{jj}\partial^{\gamma}b_{i}]+\partial^{ii}\partial^{\gamma}b_{i}

\displaystyle=\,

\displaystyle\sum_{j\neq i}[-\partial^{ij}\partial^{\gamma}b_{j}+\partial^{j}\partial^{\gamma}l_{ij}+\partial^{j}\partial^{\gamma}h_{ij}]+[\partial^{i}\partial^{\gamma}l_{i}+\partial^{i}\partial^{\gamma}h_{i}-\partial^{0}\partial^{i}\partial^{\gamma}c]

\displaystyle=\,

\displaystyle\sum_{j\neq i}[\partial^{0}\partial^{i}\partial^{\gamma}c-\partial^{i}\partial^{\gamma}l_{j}-\partial^{i}\partial^{\gamma}h_{j}]-\partial^{0}\partial^{i}\partial^{\gamma}c

\displaystyle+\sum_{j\neq i}[\partial^{j}\partial^{\gamma}l_{ij}+\partial^{j}\partial^{\gamma}h_{ij}]+\partial^{i}\partial^{\gamma}l_{i}+\partial^{i}\partial^{\gamma}h_{i}

and

\partial^{\gamma}c

|\gamma|\leq N

Combining the above estimates on

Step 3. The estimate of
\partial^{\gamma}a
,
|\gamma|\leq N
.

For

\displaystyle-\Delta\partial^{\gamma^{\prime}}a+\nabla\cdot\partial^{\gamma^{\prime}}E=\nabla\cdot\partial^{0}\partial^{\gamma^{\prime}}b-\sum_{i}\partial^{i}\partial^{\gamma^{\prime}}(l_{bi}+h_{bi}),

(5.12)

\displaystyle\Delta\partial^{\gamma^{\prime}}\phi=-\nabla\cdot\partial^{\gamma^{\prime}}E.

\times[\varrho_{0}\partial^{\gamma^{\prime}}a+\varrho_{2}\partial^{\gamma^{\prime}}c]-

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\Delta\partial^{\gamma^{\prime}}\phi\partial^{\gamma^{\prime}}(e^{\phi})dx=\,

\displaystyle=\,

where every component of

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\nabla\partial^{\gamma^{\prime}}\phi\cdot\mathcal{V}e^{\phi}dx\leq\,

\displaystyle C\sqrt{M_{0}}\sum_{0<|\gamma^{\prime\prime}|\leq|\gamma^{\prime}|}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}|\nabla\partial^{\gamma^{\prime}}\phi||\partial^{\gamma^{\prime\prime}}\phi|e^{\phi}dx

(5.15)

\displaystyle\leq\,

\displaystyle C\sqrt{M_{0}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}|\nabla\partial^{\gamma^{\prime}}\phi|^{2}e^{\phi}dx+C\sqrt{M_{0}}\sum_{0<|\gamma^{\prime\prime}|\leq|\gamma^{\prime}|}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}|\partial^{\gamma^{\prime\prime}}\phi|^{2}e^{\phi}dx.

Note that
\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\partial^{\gamma^{\prime\prime}}\phi dx=0
, by Poincaré’s inequality and

(due to

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}|\partial^{\gamma^{\prime\prime}}\phi|^{2}e^{\phi}dx\leq\,

\displaystyle C\|\partial^{\gamma^{\prime\prime}}\phi\|^{2}\leq C\|\nabla\partial^{\gamma^{\prime\prime}}\phi\|^{2}\leq\cdots\leq C\|\nabla^{(|\gamma^{\prime}|+1)}\phi\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\,

\displaystyle C\sum_{|\varpi|=|\gamma^{\prime}|}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}|\nabla\partial^{\varpi}\phi|^{2}e^{\phi}dx,

which implies that

(5.16)

\displaystyle\sum_{0<|\gamma^{\prime\prime}|\leq|\gamma^{\prime}|}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}|\partial^{\gamma^{\prime\prime}}\phi|^{2}e^{\phi}dx\leq C\sum_{|\varpi|=|\gamma^{\prime}|}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}|\nabla\partial^{\varpi}\phi|^{2}e^{\phi}dx.

Hence we obtain from (
5.14
)–(
5.16
) that

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\Delta\partial^{\gamma^{\prime}}\phi\partial^{\gamma^{\prime}}(e^{\phi})dx\,

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}|\nabla\partial^{\gamma^{\prime}}\phi|^{2}e^{\phi}dx-\left|\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\nabla\partial^{\gamma^{\prime}}\phi\cdot\mathcal{V}e^{\phi}dx\right|

\displaystyle\geq(1-C\sqrt{M_{0}})\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}|\nabla\partial^{\gamma^{\prime}}\phi|^{2}e^{\phi}dx-C\sqrt{M_{0}}\sum_{0<|\gamma^{\prime\prime}|\leq|\gamma^{\prime}|}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}|\partial^{\gamma^{\prime\prime}}\phi|^{2}e^{\phi}dx

\displaystyle\geq(1-C\sqrt{M_{0}})\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}|\nabla\partial^{\gamma^{\prime}}\phi|^{2}e^{\phi}dx-C\sqrt{M_{0}}\sum_{|\varpi|=|\gamma^{\prime}|}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}|\nabla\partial^{\varpi}\phi|^{2}e^{\phi}dx.

Summing the above inequality over
0<|\gamma^{\prime}|\leq N-1
yields

\displaystyle\sum_{0<|\gamma^{\prime}|\leq N-1}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}-\Delta\partial^{\gamma^{\prime}}\phi\partial^{\gamma^{\prime}}(e^{\phi})dx

\displaystyle\geq\,

\displaystyle(1-C\sqrt{M_{0}})\sum_{0<|\gamma^{\prime}|\leq N-1}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}|\nabla\partial^{\gamma^{\prime}}\phi|^{2}e^{\phi}dx-C\sqrt{M_{0}}\sum_{0<|\gamma^{\prime}|\leq N-1}\sum_{|\varpi|=|\gamma^{\prime}|}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}|\nabla\partial^{\varpi}\phi|^{2}e^{\phi}dx

\displaystyle\geq\,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{0<|\gamma^{\prime}|\leq N-1}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}|\nabla\partial^{\gamma^{\prime}}\phi|^{2}e^{\phi}dx.

Therefore, if we take the sum of (
5.13
) over
0<|\gamma^{\prime}|\leq N-1
, we deduce that

\displaystyle\sum_{0<|\gamma^{\prime}|\leq N-1}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}|\nabla\partial^{\gamma^{\prime}}a|^{2}+K|\nabla\partial^{\gamma^{\prime}}c|^{2}+(\nabla\partial^{\gamma^{\prime}}a\cdot\nabla\partial^{\gamma^{\prime}}c)+(\nabla\cdot\partial^{\gamma^{\prime}}E)^{2}+\frac{1}{2}|\nabla\partial^{\gamma^{\prime}}\phi|^{2}e^{\phi}dx

(5.17)

\displaystyle\,\qquad\leq C\sum_{0<|\gamma^{\prime}|\leq N-1}\Big(\|\partial^{0}\partial^{\gamma^{\prime}}b\|+\sum_{i}\|\partial^{\gamma^{\prime}}(l_{bi}+h_{bi})\|+\|\partial^{\gamma^{\prime}}(l_{c}+h_{c})\|\Big)\sum_{0<|\gamma^{\prime}|\leq N-1}\big(\|\nabla\partial^{\gamma^{\prime}}a\|+\|\nabla\partial^{\gamma^{\prime}}c\|\big).

It immediately follows from (
5.17
) that (with
K\gg 1
)

\displaystyle\sum_{0<|\gamma|\leq N-1}(\|\nabla\partial^{\gamma}a\|+\|\nabla\partial^{\gamma}c\|)\leq\,

\displaystyle C\sum_{0<|\gamma^{\prime}|\leq N-1}\Big(\|\partial^{0}\partial^{\gamma^{\prime}}b\|+\sum_{i}\|\partial^{\gamma^{\prime}}(l_{bi}+h_{bi})\|+\|\partial^{\gamma^{\prime}}(l_{c}+h_{c})\|\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\,

\displaystyle C\sum_{|\gamma|\leq N}\|\{\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{P}\}\partial^{\gamma}f(t)\|+C\sqrt{M_{0}}\sum_{|\gamma|\leq N}\|\partial^{\gamma}f(t)\|.

If
\gamma^{\prime}=0
, the deduction becomes simpler. In this case, (
5.13
) is exactly as

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}|\nabla a|^{2}+K|\nabla c|^{2}+(\nabla a\cdot\nabla c)+(\nabla\cdot E)^{2}+|\nabla\phi|^{2}e^{\phi}dx

(5.18)

\displaystyle\,\quad\leq C\bigg(\|\partial^{0}b\|+\sum_{i}\|l_{bi}+h_{bi}\|+\|l_{c}+h_{c}\|\bigg)\left(\|\nabla a\|+\|\nabla c\|\right),

which implies that

\displaystyle\|\nabla a\|+\|\nabla c\|\leq C\sum_{|\gamma|\leq N}\|\{\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{P}\}\partial^{\gamma}f(t)\|+C\sqrt{M_{0}}\sum_{|\gamma|\leq N}\|\partial^{\gamma}f(t)\|,

and hence

\displaystyle\sum_{0<|\gamma|\leq N}\|\partial^{\gamma}a\|\leq C\sum_{|\gamma|\leq N}\|\{\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{P}\}\partial^{\gamma}f(t)\|+C\sqrt{M_{0}}\sum_{|\gamma|\leq N}\|\partial^{\gamma}f(t)\|.

Furthermore,
by using Poincaré’s inequality, Lemma
5.2
and Proposition
3.3
, we get

\displaystyle\|a\|\leq C\bigg\{\|\nabla a\|+\bigg|\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}adx\bigg|\bigg\}\leq C\{\|\nabla a\|+\|\phi\|^{2}+\|\nabla_{x}\phi\|^{2}\}\leq C\{\|\nabla a\|+\|f\|^{2}\}.

In summary,
\sum_{|\gamma|\leq N}\|\partial^{\gamma}a\|
is controlled by the right-hand side of (
5.2
).

Step 4. The estimate of
\partial^{\gamma}b(t,x)
,
\gamma=[\gamma_{0},0,0,0]
,
|\gamma|\leq N
.

By Lemma
5.2
, we have
\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}bdx=0
. It thus follows that
\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\partial^{\gamma}bdx=0
. If
|\gamma|\leq N-1
, we use Poincaré’s inequality to get

\displaystyle\|\partial^{\gamma}b\|\leq C\|\nabla\partial^{\gamma}b\|\leq C\sum_{|\gamma|\leq N}\|\{\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{P}\}\partial^{\gamma}f(t)\|+C\sqrt{M_{0}}\sum_{|\gamma|\leq N}\|\partial^{\gamma}f(t)\|.

If
|\gamma|=N
, we take
\partial^{\gamma^{\prime}}
derivative
(|\gamma^{\prime}|=N-1)
on the identity (
5.8
) to get

\displaystyle\partial^{0}\partial^{\gamma^{\prime}}b_{i}+\partial^{i}\partial^{\gamma^{\prime}}a-\partial^{\gamma^{\prime}}E_{i}=\partial^{\gamma^{\prime}}(l_{bi}+h_{bi}).

Thus,

\displaystyle\sum_{|\gamma|=N}\|\partial^{\gamma}b\|\leq\sum_{|\gamma|=N}\|\partial^{\gamma}a\|+\sum_{|\gamma^{\prime}|=N-1}\|\partial^{\gamma^{\prime}}E\|+\sum_{|\gamma^{\prime}|=N-1}\sum_{i}\|\partial^{\gamma^{\prime}}(l_{bi}+h_{bi})\|.

Recalling Proposition
3.5
, we know that

\displaystyle\sum_{|\gamma^{\prime}|=N-1}\|\partial^{\gamma^{\prime}}E\|\leq\sum_{|\gamma|\leq N}\|\partial^{\gamma}\phi\|\leq C\sum_{|\gamma|\leq N}\left\|\partial^{\gamma}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}f\sqrt{\mu}dv\right\|\leq C\sum_{|\gamma|\leq N}\{\|\partial^{\gamma}a\|+\|\partial^{\gamma}c\|\}.

Therefore, we obtain that

\displaystyle\sum_{|\gamma|=N}\|\partial^{\gamma}b\|\leq\,

\displaystyle C\sum_{|\gamma|\leq N}\{\|\partial^{\gamma}a\|+\|\partial^{\gamma}c\|\}+\sum_{|\gamma^{\prime}|=N-1}\sum_{i}\|\partial^{\gamma^{\prime}}(l_{bi}+h_{bi})\|

\displaystyle\leq\,

\displaystyle C\sum_{|\gamma|\leq N}\|\{\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{P}\}\partial^{\gamma}f(t)\|+C\sqrt{M_{0}}\sum_{|\gamma|\leq N}\|\partial^{\gamma}f(t)\|.

In summary,
\sum_{|\gamma|\leq N}\|\partial^{\gamma}b\|
is controlled by the right-hand side of (
5.2
).

Combining the results of the above four steps, the crucial estimate (
5.2
) is verified. Thus, the proof of Theorem
5.1
is complete.
∎

6.
Global existence and exponential decay of classical solutions to the system
(
1.4
)–(
1.5
)

In this section, we prove the global existence and exponential decay of classical solutions to
the ionic Vlasov-Poisson-Boltzmann system (
1.4
)–(
1.5
). We first establish a refined energy estimate of classical solutions.

Lemma 6.1
.

Suppose that
f(t,x,v)
is the unique solution to the system (
1.4
)–(
1.5
) constructed in Theorem
4.1
satisfying the conservation laws (
1.7
)–(
1.9
).
Let
\sum_{|\gamma|\leq N}\|\partial^{\gamma}f(t)\|^{2}\leq M_{0}\ll 1
be valid. Then for any given
0\leq m\leq N
,
|\beta|\leq m
, there exist constants
C_{|\beta|}>0
,
\delta_{m}>0
and
C_{m}>0
such that

(6.1)

\displaystyle\sum_{|\beta|\leq m,|\gamma|+|\beta|\leq N}\left\{\frac{d}{dt}\Big\{C_{|\beta|}\|\partial^{\gamma}_{\beta}f\|^{2}+\|\partial^{\gamma}\nabla\phi\|^{2}+\big\|\partial^{\gamma}\phi e^{\frac{\phi}{2}}\big\|^{2}\Big\}(t)+\delta_{m}\|\partial^{\gamma}_{\beta}f\|^{2}_{\nu}(t)\right\}\leq C_{m}\vvvert f\vvvert\vvvert f\vvvert_{\nu}^{2}(t).

Proof.

First, we take
\partial^{\gamma}

(|\beta|=0)
derivatives of (
1.4
) to get

\displaystyle\{\partial_{t}+v\cdot\nabla_{x}+E\cdot\nabla_{v}\}\partial^{\gamma}f-\partial^{\gamma}E\cdot v\sqrt{\mu}+\mathcal{L}\{\partial^{\gamma}f\}

(6.2)

\displaystyle\quad\,=-\sum_{\gamma_{1}\neq 0}C_{\gamma}^{\gamma_{1}}\partial^{\gamma_{1}}E\cdot\nabla_{v}\partial^{\gamma-\gamma_{1}}f+\sum_{\gamma_{1}}C_{\gamma}^{\gamma_{1}}\Big\{\partial^{\gamma_{1}}E\cdot\frac{v}{2}\partial^{\gamma-\gamma_{1}}f+\Gamma(\partial^{\gamma_{1}}f,\partial^{\gamma-\gamma_{1}}f)\Big\}.

When taking inner product of the above equality with
\partial^{\gamma}f
, we investigate the term

-\int\!\!\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}\partial^{\gamma}E\cdot v\sqrt{\mu}\partial^{\gamma}fdvdx

and rewrite it as

\displaystyle-\int\!\!\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}\partial^{\gamma}E\cdot v\sqrt{\mu}\partial^{\gamma}fdvdx=\,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\partial^{\gamma}\nabla\phi\cdot\partial^{\gamma}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}v\sqrt{\mu}fdv\right)dx

\displaystyle=\,

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\partial^{\gamma}\phi\partial^{\gamma}\nabla\cdot(\rho u)dx

\displaystyle=\,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\partial^{\gamma}\phi\partial^{\gamma}\partial_{t}\rho dx

\displaystyle=\,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\partial^{\gamma}\phi\partial^{\gamma}(\partial_{t}\phi e^{\phi}-\partial_{t}\Delta\phi)dx

\displaystyle=\,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\left(\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}|\nabla\partial^{\gamma}\phi|^{2}dx\right)+\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\partial^{\gamma}\phi\partial^{\gamma}(\partial_{t}\phi e^{\phi})dx

\displaystyle=\,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\left(\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}|\nabla\partial^{\gamma}\phi|^{2}dx\right)+\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\left(\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}|\partial^{\gamma}\phi|^{2}e^{\phi}dx\right)+\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\mathcal{W}e^{\phi}dx,

where
\mathcal{W}
is a finite sum: each element herein is a product of at least three terms of
\partial^{\tilde{\gamma}}\phi
,
0<|\tilde{\gamma}|\leq|\gamma|
. Recalling Proposition
3.5
, we thus have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\mathcal{W}e^{\phi}dx\leq C\vvvert f\vvvert\vvvert f\vvvert_{\nu}^{2}.

Multiplying (
6.2
) by
\partial^{\gamma}f
, by using Theorem
5.1
and the similar procedures as (iic) and (iid) in Section
4
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\sum_{|\gamma|\leq N}\Big(\frac{1}{2}\|\partial^{\gamma}f\|^{2}+\|\partial^{\gamma}\nabla\phi\|^{2}+\|\partial^{\gamma}\phi e^{\frac{\phi}{2}}\|^{2}\Big)(t)+\delta_{0}\sum_{|\gamma|\leq N}\|\partial^{\gamma}f\|^{2}_{\nu}(t)\leq C\sum_{|\gamma|\leq N}\vvvert f\vvvert\vvvert f\vvvert_{\nu}^{2},

which is (
6.1
) for
|\beta|=0
case.

Second, assume that (
6.1
) is valid for
|\beta|\leq m
. We shall verify (
6.1
)
holds for the case
|\beta|=m+1
.
Take
\partial_{\beta}^{\gamma}
derivatives of (
1.4
), it holds

\displaystyle\{\partial_{t}+v\cdot\nabla_{x}+E\cdot\nabla_{v}+\nu\}\partial^{\gamma}_{\beta}f=\,

\displaystyle\partial^{\gamma}E\cdot\partial_{\beta}(v\sqrt{\mu})-\sum_{\beta_{1}\neq 0}C_{\beta}^{\beta_{1}}\{\partial_{\beta_{1}}v\cdot\nabla_{x}+\partial_{\beta_{1}}\nu\}\partial_{\beta-\beta_{1}}^{\gamma}f

\displaystyle+\sum C_{\beta}^{\beta_{1}}C_{\gamma}^{\gamma_{1}}\partial^{\gamma_{1}}E\cdot\partial_{\beta_{1}}\left(\frac{v}{2}\right)\partial_{\beta-\beta_{1}}^{\gamma-\gamma_{1}}f-\sum_{\gamma_{1}\neq 0}C_{\gamma}^{\gamma_{1}}\partial^{\gamma_{1}}E\cdot\nabla_{v}\partial^{\gamma-\gamma_{1}}_{\beta}f

(6.3)

\displaystyle+\partial_{\beta}[\mathcal{K}\partial^{\gamma}f]+\sum C_{\gamma}^{\gamma_{1}}\partial_{\beta}\Gamma(\partial^{\gamma_{1}}f,\partial^{\gamma-\gamma_{1}}f).

We consider the inner product of (
6.3
) with
\partial_{\beta}^{\gamma}f
. By direct calculations, the contribution of the term
\partial^{\gamma}E\cdot\partial_{\beta}(v\sqrt{\mu})
in (
6.3
) is

\displaystyle(\partial^{\gamma}E\cdot\partial_{\beta}(v\sqrt{\mu}),\partial_{\beta}^{\gamma}f)=(-1)^{|\beta|}(\partial^{\gamma}E\cdot\partial^{2}_{\beta}(v\sqrt{\mu}),\partial^{\gamma}f)\leq C\|\partial^{\gamma}E\|\|\partial^{\gamma}f\|\leq C\sum_{|\gamma|\leq N}\|\partial^{\gamma}f\|^{2}.

By (
2.1
) in Lemma
2.2
and the compact interpolation for
\partial_{v}^{\beta-\beta_{1}}
between
\partial_{v}^{\gamma}

(|\gamma|=|\beta|)
and
\partial_{v}^{0}
, the contribution of the term
\sum_{\beta_{1}\neq 0}C_{\beta}^{\beta_{1}}\{\partial_{\beta_{1}}v\cdot\nabla_{x}+\partial_{\beta_{1}}\nu\}\partial_{\beta-\beta_{1}}^{\gamma}f
in (
6.3
) is

\eta\|\partial_{\beta}^{\gamma}f\|^{2}+C_{\eta}\sum_{|\beta_{1}|=1}\|\nabla_{x}\partial^{\gamma}_{\beta-\beta_{1}}f\|^{2}.

By Lemma
2.2
again, the contribution of the term
\partial_{\beta}[\mathcal{K}\partial^{\gamma}f]
in (
6.3
) is

\eta\sum_{|\beta^{\prime}|=|\beta|}\|\partial_{\beta^{\prime}}^{\gamma}f\|^{2}+C_{\eta}\|\partial^{\gamma}f\|^{2}.

By simple modifications of the arguments in Section
4
, the contribution of other terms on the right-hand side of (
6.3
) is

C\vvvert f\vvvert\vvvert f\vvvert_{\nu}^{2}.

In summary, we deduce that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|\partial_{\beta}^{\gamma}f\|^{2}(t)+\|\partial_{\beta}^{\gamma}f\|_{\nu}^{2}(t)\leq\,

\displaystyle C\|\partial^{\gamma}E\|\|\partial^{\gamma}f\|+\eta\|\partial_{\beta}^{\gamma}f\|^{2}+C_{\eta}\sum_{|\beta_{1}|=1}\|\nabla_{x}\partial^{\gamma}_{\beta-\beta_{1}}f\|^{2}

(6.4)

\displaystyle+\eta\sum_{|\beta^{\prime}|=|\beta|}\|\partial_{\beta^{\prime}}^{\gamma}f\|^{2}+C_{\eta}\|\partial^{\gamma}f\|^{2}+C\vvvert f\vvvert\vvvert f\vvvert_{\nu}^{2}

for some sufficiently small constant
\eta>0
.

Summing (
6.4
) over
|\beta|=m+1
and
|\gamma|+|\beta|\leq N
yields

(6.5)

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|\beta|=m+1\\
|\gamma|+|\beta|\leq N\end{subarray}}\left\{\frac{d}{dt}\|\partial_{\beta}^{\gamma}f\|^{2}(t)+\|\partial_{\beta}^{\gamma}f\|_{\nu}^{2}(t)\right\}\leq C_{\eta}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|\beta|=m\\
|\gamma|+|\beta|\leq N\end{subarray}}\|\partial^{\gamma}_{\beta}f\|^{2}+C_{\eta}\sum_{|\gamma|\leq N}\|\partial^{\gamma}f\|^{2}+C\vvvert f\vvvert\vvvert f\vvvert_{\nu}^{2}.

Multiplying (
6.5
) by a sufficiently small number and add it to (
6.1
) for
|\beta|\leq m
,
we obtain that

(6.6)

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|\beta|\leq m+1\\
|\gamma|+|\beta|\leq N\end{subarray}}\left\{\frac{d}{dt}\Big\{C_{|\beta|}\|\partial^{\gamma}_{\beta}f\|^{2}+\|\partial^{\gamma}\nabla\phi\|^{2}+\big\|\partial^{\gamma}\phi e^{\frac{\phi}{2}}\big\|^{2}\Big\}(t)+\delta_{m+1}\|\partial^{\gamma}_{\beta}f\|^{2}_{\nu}(t)\right\}\leq C_{m+1}\vvvert f\vvvert(t)\vvvert f\vvvert_{\nu}^{2}(t),

which inductively concludes the proof of the lemma.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.1

(
continued
)
.

With (
6.1
) in hand, we now prove the global existence and exponential decay of the solution to (
1.4
)–(
1.5
) with a bootstrap argument. Fix
M_{0}\ll 1
such that Theorems
4.1
and
5.1
hold. In the estimate (
6.1
), we choose
m=N
, and then define

\displaystyle y(t):=\,

\displaystyle\sum_{|\gamma|+|\beta|\leq N}\left\{C_{|\beta|}\|\partial_{\beta}^{\gamma}f\|^{2}+\|\partial^{\gamma}\nabla\phi\|^{2}+\|\partial^{\gamma}\phi e^{\phi/2}\|^{2}\right\}(t)

\displaystyle\sim\,

\displaystyle\sum_{|\gamma|+|\beta|\leq N}\left\{C_{|\beta|}\|\partial_{\beta}^{\gamma}f\|^{2}+\|\partial^{\gamma}\nabla\phi\|^{2}+\|\partial^{\gamma}\phi\|^{2}\right\}(t)

\displaystyle\sim\,

\displaystyle\sum_{|\gamma|+|\beta|\leq N}\|\partial_{\beta}^{\gamma}f\|^{2}(t)

\displaystyle=\,

\displaystyle\vvvert f\vvvert^{2}(t),

where the third line is obtained from the elliptic estimates in Proposition
3.5
.
Then (
6.1
) is equivalent to

\displaystyle y^{\prime}(t)+\delta_{N}\vvvert f\vvvert^{2}_{\nu}(t)\leq C_{N}\vvvert f\vvvert\vvvert f\vvvert_{\nu}^{2}(t)\leq C\sqrt{M_{0}}\vvvert f\vvvert_{\nu}^{2}(t),

which implies that

(6.7)

\displaystyle y^{\prime}(t)+\frac{\delta_{N}}{2}\vvvert f\vvvert^{2}_{\nu}(t)\leq 0.

It follows that

(6.8)

\displaystyle y(t)+\frac{\delta_{N}}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\vvvert f\vvvert^{2}_{\nu}(s)ds\leq y(0)\leq C\vvvert f_{0}\vvvert^{2}.

Recall the energy functional defined in (
1.11
), i.e.,
\mathcal{E}(t)=\vvvert f\vvvert^{2}(t)+\int_{0}^{t}\vvvert f\vvvert^{2}_{\nu}(s)ds
. Then (
6.8
) indicates that

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(t)\leq C\vvvert f_{0}\vvvert^{2}\leq C_{*}\mathcal{E}(0),

for some
C_{*}>0
. For a constant
M\leq{M_{0}}/({2C_{*}})
, we choose the initial datum
\mathcal{E}(0)\leq M
and define

\displaystyle T^{*}:=\sup_{t}\{t:\,\mathcal{E}(t)\leq M_{0}\}>0,

then we have

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq t\leq T^{*}}\mathcal{E}(t)\leq C_{*}M<2C_{*}M=M_{0},

which contradicts the definition of
T^{*}
. Hence the maximal existence time
T^{*}
can be extended to the infinity.

Moreover, by the fact that
y(t)\sim\vvvert f\vvvert^{2}(t)
, (
6.7
) indicates that

\displaystyle y^{\prime}(t)+Cy(t)\leq 0,

for some

, and thus

\displaystyle y(t)\leq e^{-Ct}y(0).

Therefore, it holds

\displaystyle\vvvert f\vvvert^{2}(t)\leq Ce^{-Ct}\vvvert f_{0}\vvvert^{2}.

Hence, the whole proof of Theorem
1.1

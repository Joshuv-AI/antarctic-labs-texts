Abstract
Let n ⩾ t ⩾ 1 n\geqslant t\geqslant 1 and 𝒜 1 , 𝒜 2 , … , 𝒜 m ⊆ 2 [ n ] \mathcal{A}_{1},\mathcal{A}_{2},\ldots,\mathcal{A}_{m}\subseteq 2^{[n]} be non-empty families. We say that they are pairwise cross t t -intersecting if | A i ∩ A j | ⩾ t |A_{i}\cap A_{j}|\geqslant t holds for any A i ∈ 𝒜 i A_{i}\in\mathcal{A}_{i} and A j ∈ 𝒜 j A_{j}\in\mathcal{A}_{j} with i ≠ j i\neq j . In the case where m = 2 m=2 and 𝒜 1 = 𝒜 2 \mathcal{A}_{1}=\mathcal{A}_{2} , determining the maximum size M ⁡ ( n , t ) M(n,t) of a non-uniform t t -intersecting family of sets over [ n ] [n] was solved by Katona (1964), and enhanced by Frankl (2017), and recently by Li and Wu (2024). In this paper, we establish the following upper bound: if 𝒜 1 , 𝒜 2 , … , 𝒜 m ⊆ 2 [ n ] \mathcal{A}_{1},\mathcal{A}_{2},\ldots,\mathcal{A}_{m}\subseteq 2^{[n]} are non-empty pairwise cross t t -intersecting families, then ∑ i = 1 m | 𝒜 i | ⩽ max ⁡ { ∑ k = t n ( n k ) + m − 1 , m ​ M ​ ( n , t ) } . \sum_{i=1}^{m}|\mathcal{A}_{i}|\leqslant\max\left\{\sum_{k=t}^{n}\binom{n}{k}+m-1,\,mM(n,t)\right\}. Furthermore, we provide a complete characterization of the extremal families that achieve the bound. Our result not only generalizes an old result of Katona (1964) for a single family, but also extends a theorem of Frankl and Wong (2021) for two families. Moreover, our result could be viewed as a non-uniform version of a recent theorem of Li and Zhang (2025). The key in our proof is to utilize the generating set method and the pushing-pulling method together. Introduction

Extremal set theory studies the maximum or minimum possible size of a family of sets under given constraints. The central problems in this field include determining the extremal size of set families that satisfy certain intersection, union, or exclusion conditions, and characterizing the optimal structures. Classical results include the Erdős–Ko–Rado theorem, Sunflower lemma, and others, employing combinatorial, probabilistic, and algebraic methods. Extremal set theory has wide applications in theoretical computer science, graph theory, coding theory, and discrete geometry, with the aim of uncovering the limits and optimal configurations of set systems.

We fix the following notation.
For integers
m\leqslant n
, let
[m,n]=\{m,m+1,\ldots,n\}
and simply let
[n]=[1,n]
. Let
2^{[n]}
denote the family of all subsets of
[n]
, and let
\binom{[n]}{k}
denote the family of all
k
-element subsets of
[n]
. Let
\binom{[n]}{\leqslant k}=\{A\subseteq[n]:|A|\leqslant k\}
. A family
\mathcal{A}\subseteq 2^{[n]}
is called
k
-
uniform
if all sets of
\mathcal{A}
contain
k
elements, otherwise it is called
non-uniform
.
All families discussed in this study are assumed to be non-empty.
A family
\mathcal{A}\subseteq 2^{[n]}
is called
t
-
intersecting
if
|A\cap A^{\prime}|\geqslant t

for all
A,A^{\prime}\in\mathcal{A}
.
Two families
\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}\subseteq 2^{[n]}
are called
cross
t
-intersecting
if
|A\cap B|\geqslant t

for all
A\in\mathcal{A}
and
B\in\mathcal{B}
.
Moreover, the families
\mathcal{A}_{1},\mathcal{A}_{2},\ldots,\mathcal{A}_{m}\subseteq 2^{[n]}
are called
pairwise cross
t
-intersecting
if
|A_{i}\cap A_{j}|\geqslant t
for any
A_{i}\in\mathcal{A}_{i}
and
A_{j}\in\mathcal{A}_{j}
with
1\leqslant i<j\leqslant m
.
For the case
t=1
, we abbreviate
1
-intersecting, cross
1
-intersecting and pairwise cross
t
-intersecting as intersecting, cross intersecting and pairwise cross intersecting, respectively.

1.1
Results for
k
-uniform
t
-intersecting families

Let
M(n,k,t)
denote the maximum of
|\mathcal{A}|
over all
\mathcal{A}\subseteq\binom{[n]}{k}
having the
t
-intersecting property.
The classical Erdős–Ko–Rado theorem
[
10
]
states that
M(n,k,1)={n-1\choose k-1}
for every
k\geqslant 2
and
n\geqslant 2k
.
For any
t\geqslant 2
and
k\geqslant t
, there exists an integer
n_{0}(k,t)
such that if
n>n_{0}(k,t)
, then

M(n,k,t)=\binom{n-t}{k-t},

and the unique extremal family is
\mathcal{A}=\{A\in{[n]\choose k}:T\subseteq A\}
for some fixed
T\in{[n]\choose t}
. The original paper
[
10
]
established that
n_{0}(k,t)\leqslant(k-t){k\choose t}{}^{3}+t
.
The smallest such
n_{0}(k,t)
was conjectured to be
(t+1)(k-t+1)
, which was confirmed by Frankl
[
12
]
for
t\geqslant 15
using the random walk method, and completely solved by Wilson
[
41
]
for all
t\geqslant 1
by the algebraic method.

For small integer
n
, Ahlswede and Khachatrian introduced the generating set method
[
2
]
and the pushing–pulling method
[
3
]
.
They obtained the exact value of
M(n,k,t)
for every
n<(t+1)(k-t+1)
. This solves a long-standing conjecture of Frankl
[
12
]
.

Theorem 1.1

(Ahlswede and Khachatrian
[
2
,
3
]
)
.

Let
n,k,t,r
be integers with
n\geqslant k\geqslant t\geqslant 1
and
n>2k-t
. We denote
\mathcal{F}_{r}(n,k,t)=\left\{A\in\binom{[n]}{k}:|A\cap[t+2r]|\geqslant t+r\right\}
. Then

M(n,k,t)=\max_{0\leqslant r\leqslant{(n-t)}/{2}}\{|\mathcal{F}_{r}(n,k,t)|\}.

To be more specific, it is easy to see that

|\mathcal{F}_{r}(n,k,t)|=\sum_{i=t+r}^{t+2r}{t+2r\choose i}{n-t-2r\choose k-i}
.
For more related results for
t
-intersecting families,
we refer to
[
1
,
5
,
8
,
9
,
44
,
23
]
and references therein.

In 1967, Hilton and Milner
[
27
]
pioneered the investigation of cross intersecting families in analogy to the celebrated Erdős–Ko–Rado theorem, establishing that for non-empty cross-intersecting families
\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}\subseteq\binom{[n]}{k}
with
n\geqslant 2k
, the sharp bound
|\mathcal{A}|+|\mathcal{B}|\leqslant\binom{n}{k}-\binom{n-k}{k}+1
holds. This foundational result was generalized in
[
18
,
19
,
16
,
22
]
.
More generally,
Wang and Zhang
[
40
]
determined the maximum sum of sizes of non-empty cross
t
-intersecting families.

Theorem 1.2

(Wang and Zhang
[
40
]
)
.

Let
n,k,t
be positive integers with
k>t\geqslant 1
and
n>2k-t
.
If
\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}\subseteq{[n]\choose k}

are non-empty cross
t
-intersecting families, then

|\mathcal{A}|+|\mathcal{B}|\leqslant{n\choose k}-\sum_{i=0}^{t-1}{k\choose i}{n-k\choose k-i}+1.

This bound was also independently obtained by Frankl and Kupavskii
[
17
]
.

In 2022,
Shi, Frankl and Qian
[
37
]
generalized Hilton–Milner’s result to the multi-family setting, proving that for non-empty pairwise cross intersecting families
\mathcal{A}_{1},\mathcal{A}_{2},\ldots,\mathcal{A}_{m}\subseteq\binom{[n]}{k}
with
n\geqslant 2k
and
m\geqslant 2
, the total size satisfies
\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}|\mathcal{A}_{i}|\leqslant\max\left\{\binom{n}{k}-\binom{n-k}{k}+m-1,\,m\binom{n-1}{k-1}\right\}
.
This result stimulated more studies on this topic; see
[
29
,
30
,
31
,
43
]
for non-empty pairwise cross intersecting families where the sets of families have different cardinalities.

Solving an open problem of Shi, Frankl and Qian
[
37

,
Li and Zhang
[
34
]
recently proved the following result for families with pairwise cross
t
-intersecting property.

Theorem 1.3

(Li and Zhang
[
34
]
)
.

Let
n,k,t
and
m
be integers with
n>2k-t,k>t\geqslant 1
and
m\geqslant 2
. If
\mathcal{A}_{1},\mathcal{A}_{2},\ldots,\mathcal{A}_{m}\subseteq\binom{[n]}{k}
are non-empty pairwise cross
t
-intersecting families, then

\sum_{i=1}^{m}|\mathcal{A}_{i}|\leqslant\max\left\{\binom{n}{k}-\sum_{i=0}^{t-1}\binom{k}{i}\binom{n-k}{k-i}+m-1,\,mM(n,k,t)\right\}.

It remains an open problem
[
37
, Problem 4.5]
to extend Theorem
1.3
to multi-families where the sets of families have different cardinalities, i.e.,

\mathcal{A}_{i}\subseteq{[n]\choose k_{i}}
for every
i\in[m]
, where
k_{1}\geqslant k_{2}\geqslant\cdots\geqslant k_{m}
.
In this paper, we shall investigate Theorem
1.3
for non-uniform families in another perspective. We shall prove an analogue for pairwise cross
t
-intersecting families
\mathcal{A}_{1},\mathcal{A}_{2},\ldots,\mathcal{A}_{m}\subseteq 2^{[n]}
. This non-uniform extension in our paper may be of independent interest.

1.2
Results for non-uniform
t
-intersecting families

Now, we consider the maximum size of a non-uniform
t
-intersecting family.
Let
M(n,t)
denote the maximum of
|\mathcal{A}|
over all
\mathcal{A}\subseteq 2^{[n]}
having the
t
-intersecting property.
The following classical result of Katona
[
33
]
determines the exact value of

M(n,t)
for all
n,t
.

Theorem 1.4

(Katona
[
33
]
)
.

Let
n,t
be integers with
n\geqslant t\geqslant 1
. Then

M(n,t)=\begin{cases}\sum_{k\geqslant\frac{n+t}{2}}{n\choose k}&\text{if $n+t$ is even};\\
\sum_{k\geqslant\frac{n+t+1}{2}}{n\choose k}+{n-1\choose\frac{n+t-1}{2}}&\text{if $n+t$ is odd}.\end{cases}

Moreover, for
t>1
, the first equality attains only for
\mathcal{K}(n,t)=\left\{A\subseteq[n]:|A|\geqslant\frac{n+t}{2}\right\}
; the second equality attains only for
\mathcal{K}^{\prime}(n,t)=\left\{A\subseteq[n]:|A|\geqslant\frac{n+t+1}{2}\right\}\cup{[n-1]\choose\frac{n+t-1}{2}}
.

The original proof of Theorem
1.4
uses the so-called Katona intersection shadow theorem.
The odd case of Katona’s theorem can be deduced from the even case
[
3
]
.
Alternative proofs can be found in
[
3
,
4
,
38
]
and
[
6
, page 98]
. We refer to

14
,
15
,
28
,
35
,
42
]
for extensions.

In 2021,
Frankl and Wong
[
24
]
established an extension of Katona’s theorem.

Theorem 1.5

(Frankl and Wong
[
24
]
)
.

Let
n,t
be integers with
n\geqslant t\geqslant 1
. Let
\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}\subseteq 2^{[n]}
be non-empty cross
t
-intersectng families. Then

|\mathcal{A}|+|\mathcal{B}|\leqslant\sum_{i=t}^{n}\binom{n}{i}+1.

Moreover, for
t>1
, the equality holds if and only if
\mathcal{A}=\{[n]\}
and
\mathcal{B}=\{G\subseteq[n]:|G|\geqslant t\}
.

This result was later expanded by Borg and Feghli
[
7
]
, who investigated cross intersecting families in a more general non-uniform setting, specifically for
\mathcal{A}\subseteq\binom{[n]}{\leqslant k}
and
\mathcal{B}\subseteq\binom{[n]}{\leqslant\ell}
. Liu
[
36
]
further advanced this line of research by considering the cross
t
-intersecting case, providing a broader framework for such extremal problems. For further developments and related results, we refer to
[
17
,
21
,
26
,
30
,
32
]
, as well as the comprehensive survey
[
20
]
.

Our result presents a unified generalization of both Theorem
1.4
and Theorem
1.5
, and it could also be viewed as an extension of Theorem
1.3
to the non-uniform families.

Theorem 1.6

(Main result)
.

Let
n\geqslant t\geqslant 1
and
m\geqslant 2
be integers. If
\mathcal{A}_{1},\mathcal{A}_{2},\ldots,\mathcal{A}_{m}\subseteq 2^{[n]}
are non-empty pairwise cross
t
-intersecting families, then

\sum_{i=1}^{m}|\mathcal{A}_{i}|\leqslant\max\left\{\sum_{k=t}^{n}\binom{n}{k}+m-1,\,mM(n,t)\right\}.

For
t>1
, the equality holds if and only if, up to isomorphism, one of the following holds:

(a)

\mathcal{A}_{1}=\left\{A\subseteq[n]:|A\cap[n]|\geqslant t\right\}
and
\mathcal{A}_{2}=\cdots=\mathcal{A}_{m}=\{[n]\}
if
\sum_{k=t}^{n}\binom{n}{k}+m-1\geqslant mM(n,t)
.

(b)

\mathcal{A}_{1}=\cdots=\mathcal{A}_{m}=\mathcal{K}(n,t)
for
2\mid(n+t)
and
\mathcal{A}_{1}=\cdots=\mathcal{A}_{m}=\mathcal{K}^{\prime}(n,t)
for
2\nmid(n+t)
if
\sum_{k=t}^{n}\binom{n}{k}+m-1\leqslant mM(n,t)
.

We point out here that Theorem
1.6
implies
Theorem
1.4
in a surprising way. Indeed, taking
m
as sufficiently large and setting
\mathcal{A}_{1}=\cdots=\mathcal{A}_{m}
, we can see that
mM(n,t)>\sum_{k=t}^{n}{n\choose k}+m-1
. Thus, we get
|\mathcal{A}_{1}|\leqslant M(n,t)
for every
t
-intersecting family
\mathcal{A}_{1}\subseteq 2^{[n]}
.
Moreover, in the case
t=1
, there are many optimal families. For example, for the case
2\mid(n+1)
, the families
\mathcal{A}_{1}=\cdots=\mathcal{A}_{m}=\mathcal{K}(n,1)
and
\mathcal{A}_{1}=\cdots=\mathcal{A}_{m}=\left\{A\subseteq[n]:1\in A\right\}
all have
\sum_{i=1}^{m}|\mathcal{A}_{i}|=m2^{n-1}
.

For any

With the above notation, we have

2.1
The shifting operator

We begin with the shifting technique
[
10
]
, which is one of the most powerful tools in extremal set theory.
Let
\mathcal{A}\subseteq 2^{[n]}
be a family and let
1\leqslant i<j\leqslant n
be integers. The
shifting operator

s_{i,j}
on
\mathcal{A}
is defined as follows:

s_{i,j}(\mathcal{A})=\left\{s_{i,j}(A):A\in\mathcal{A}\right\},

where

s_{i,j}(A)=\begin{cases}(A\backslash\{j\})\cup\{i\}&\text{ if }j\in A,i\notin A\text{ and }(A\backslash\{j\})\cup\{i\}\notin\mathcal{A};\\
A&\text{ otherwise. }\end{cases}

A family
\mathcal{A}\subseteq 2^{[n]}
is called
shifted
or
left-compressed
if
s_{i,j}(\mathcal{A})=\mathcal{A}
holds for all
1\leqslant i<j\leqslant n
.
It is well-known that by applying the shifting operator repeatedly, every family will terminate at a shifted family (although the terminated family is not unique). A critical observation in the proof of the Erdős–Ko–Rado theorem is that the shifting operation preserves the property of a family being intersecting; see the surveys
[
13
,
39
]
. In fact, the shifting operator has the following important property for corss
t
-intersecting families; see, e.g.,
[
26
]
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}\subseteq 2^{[n]}
be cross
t
-intersecting families and let
1\leqslant i<j\leqslant n
be integers.
Then
s_{i,j}(\mathcal{A})
and
s_{i,j}(\mathcal{B})
are cross
t
-intersecting.

A family
\mathcal{A}\subseteq 2^{[n]}
is called an
antichain
if there are no distinct sets
A,A^{\prime}\in\mathcal{A}
satisfying
A\subseteq A^{\prime}
.
A family
\mathcal{A}\subseteq 2^{[n]}
is called
monotone
if
A\in\mathcal{A}
and
A\subseteq B
imply
B\in\mathcal{A}
.
The
up-set
of a family
\mathcal{A}
is defined by

\langle\mathcal{A}\rangle=\left\{F\subseteq[n]:\text{ there exists }A\in\mathcal{A}\text{ such that }A\subseteq F\right\}.

2.2
Generating set method

The generating set method was initially introduced by Ahlswede and Khachatrian
[
1
,
2
]
and subsequently advanced and applied in recent works
[
11
,
34
,
44
]
.
Let
\mathcal{A}\subseteq 2^{[n]}
(
\emptyset\subsetneq\mathcal{A}\subsetneq 2^{[n]}
) be a monotone family. A
generating set
of
\mathcal{A}
is an inclusion-minimal set in
\mathcal{A}
.
The
generating family
of
\mathcal{A}
consists of all generating sets of
\mathcal{A}
. Note that any generating family is an antichain.
The
extent
of
\mathcal{A}
is defined as the maximal element appearing in a generating set of
\mathcal{A}
. The
boundary generating family
of
\mathcal{A}
consists of all generating sets of
\mathcal{A}
containing its extent.

The following lemma is inspired by
[
25
, Lemma 2.2]
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\mathcal{A}\subseteq 2^{[n]}
be a monotone shifted family with its extent
\ell\geqslant 2
, generating family
\mathcal{G}
and boundary generating family
\mathcal{G}[\ell]
. For any families
\mathcal{B}\subseteq\mathcal{G}[\ell]^{(u)}
and
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathcal{G}[\ell]^{(v)}
with
u\neq v\in[\ell]
, we denote
\mathcal{D}=(\mathcal{G}\setminus\mathcal{B})\cup\{C\setminus\{\ell\}:C\in\mathcal{C}\}
. Then

|\langle\mathcal{D}\rangle|=|\mathcal{A}|-|\mathcal{B}|\sum_{j=u}^{n}\binom{n-\ell}{j-u}+|\mathcal{C}|\sum_{j=v-1}^{n}\binom{n-\ell}{j-v+1}.

Proof.

Since
|\langle\mathcal{D}\rangle|-|\mathcal{A}|=|\langle\mathcal{D}\rangle\setminus\mathcal{A}|-|\mathcal{A}\setminus\langle\mathcal{D}\rangle|
,
it suffices to prove that

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{D}\rangle\setminus\mathcal{A}=\left\{(C\setminus\{\ell\})\cup T:C\in\mathcal{C},\ T\in\binom{[\ell+1,n]}{\leqslant n-v+1}\right\}

and

\mathcal{A}\setminus\langle\mathcal{D}\rangle=\left\{B\cup T:B\in\mathcal{B},\ T\in\binom{[\ell+1,n]}{\leqslant n-u}\right\}.

For any
F\in\langle\mathcal{D}\rangle\setminus\mathcal{A}
, there exists some
C\in\mathcal{C}
such that
C\setminus\{\ell\}\subseteq F
. Since
C\subseteq[\ell]
, we have
C\setminus\{\ell\}\subseteq F\cap[\ell]
.
In addition,
F\notin\mathcal{A}
implies
\ell\notin F\cap[\ell]
, otherwise
C\subseteq F\cap[\ell]
and hence
F\in\mathcal{A}
, a contradiction.
If
C\setminus\{\ell\}\subsetneq F\cap[\ell]
, then there exists
i\in(F\cap[\ell])\setminus(C\setminus\{\ell\})
with
i<\ell
. By the shiftedness, we get
(C\setminus\{\ell\})\cup\{i\}\in\mathcal{A}
. Since

\mathcal{A}=\langle\mathcal{G}\rangle
, there exists
G\in\mathcal{G}
such that
G\subseteq(C\setminus\{\ell\})\cup\{i\}\subseteq F\cap[\ell]
.
It follows that
F\in\mathcal{A}
, a contradiction.
Therefore, we obtain
F\cap[\ell]=C\setminus\{\ell\}
. This implies that

\langle\mathcal{D}\rangle\setminus\mathcal{A}\subseteq\left\{(C\setminus\{\ell\})\cup T:C\in\mathcal{C},\ T\in\binom{[\ell+1,n]}{\leqslant n-v+1}\right\}
.
On the other hand,
for any
C\in\mathcal{C}
and
T\in\binom{[\ell+1,n]}{\leqslant n-v+1}
, we have
(C\setminus\{\ell\})\cup T\notin\mathcal{A}
because any subset of
C\setminus\{\ell\}
is not in
\mathcal{G}
. Clearly, we have
(C\setminus\{\ell\})\cup T\in\langle\mathcal{D}\rangle
. Thus, it follows that

\left\{(C\setminus\{\ell\})\cup T:C\in\mathcal{C},\ T\in\binom{[\ell+1,n]}{\leqslant n-v+1}\right\}\subseteq\langle\mathcal{D}\rangle\setminus\mathcal{A},
as desired.

For any
A\in\mathcal{A}\setminus\langle\mathcal{D}\rangle
, there exists some
B\in\mathcal{B}
such that
B\subseteq A
. Then
B\subseteq A\cap[\ell]
.
Note that
\ell\in B
.
If
B\subsetneq A\cap[\ell]
, then there exists
i\in(A\cap[\ell])\setminus B
with
i<\ell
.
By the shiftedness, we have
(B\setminus\{\ell\})\cup\{i\}\in\mathcal{A}
. Since

\mathcal{A}=\langle\mathcal{G}\rangle
, there exists
G\in\mathcal{G}
such that
G\subseteq(B\setminus\{\ell\})\cup\{i\}\subseteq A\cap[\ell]
.
Since
\ell\notin G
, we infer that
G\in\mathcal{G}\setminus\mathcal{B}
.
Then
A\in\mathcal{D}
, a contradiction. Therefore, we get
A\cap[\ell]=B
. Then

\mathcal{A}\setminus\langle\mathcal{D}\rangle\subseteq\left\{B\cup T:B\in\mathcal{B},\ T\in\binom{[\ell+1,n]}{\leqslant n-u}\right\}
. On the other hand,
for any
B\in\mathcal{B}
and
T\in\binom{[\ell+1,n]}{\leqslant n-u}
, note that any subset of
B
is not in
\mathcal{G}\setminus\mathcal{B}
.
If
B\cup T\in\langle\mathcal{D}\rangle
, then there exists some

C\in\mathcal{C}
such that
C\setminus\{\ell\}\subseteq B
.
Since
\ell\in B
, we further get
C\subseteq B
.
However,
\mathcal{G}
is a generating family, and
u\neq v
implies
that
\mathcal{B}\cup\mathcal{C}
is an antichain, a contradiction.
Therefore, we get
B\cup T\notin\langle\mathcal{D}\rangle
and
B\cup T\in\mathcal{A}
. Then

\left\{B\cup T:B\in\mathcal{B},\ T\in\binom{[\ell+1,n]}{\leqslant n-u}\right\}\subseteq\mathcal{A}\setminus\langle\mathcal{D}\rangle
, as required.
∎

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\vec{\mathcal{A}}=(\mathcal{A}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{A}_{m})\in(2^{[n]})^{m}
be a monotone shifted pairwise cross
t
-intersecting sequence with generating sequence
\vec{\mathcal{G}}=(\mathcal{G}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{G}_{m})
. Let
\ell
be the extent of
\vec{\mathcal{A}}
. If

A\in\mathcal{G}_{i}[\ell]
and

satisfy
|A\cap B|=t
for some

, then
A\cup B=[\ell]
and
|A|+|B|=t+\ell
.

Proof.

Firstly, we have
A\cup B\subseteq[\ell]
and
\ell\in A\cap B
. If
A\cup B\subsetneq[\ell]
, then there exists
x\in[\ell]\setminus(A\cup B)
and

. By the shiftedness, we get
(A\setminus\{\ell\})\cup\{x\}\in\mathcal{A}_{i}

and
|((A\setminus\{\ell\})\cup\{x\})\cap B|=|(A\cap B)\setminus\{\ell\}|=t-1
, a contradiction. Then

and
|A|+|B|=|A\cap B|+|A\cup B|=t+\ell
.
∎

Recall that
\mathcal{A}_{i}[u]^{(k)}=\{A\in\mathcal{A}_{i}:u\in A,|A|=k\}

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
\vec{\mathcal{A}}=(\mathcal{A}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{A}_{m})\in(2^{[n]})^{m}

(3)

If for all pairs
u\neq v\in[t,\ell]

\vec{\mathcal{G}}[\ell]^{(u)}\neq(\emptyset,\ldots,\emptyset)

(1) By symmetry, we may assume that
\vec{\mathcal{G}}[\ell]^{(u)}\neq(\emptyset,\ldots,\emptyset)

\vec{\mathcal{G}}[\ell]^{(v)}=(\emptyset,\ldots,\emptyset)

In view of

u>t

2.3
Pushing-pulling method

The pushing-pulling method was introduced by Ahlswede and Khachatrian
[
3
]
.
For a set
A\subseteq[n]
and integers
1\leqslant i\neq j\leqslant n
,
let
A\Delta\{i,j\}
denote the symmetric difference of
A
and
\{i,j\}
.
Let

A_{i,j}
denote the
(i,j)
-
exchange operator
, where

A_{i,j}=\begin{cases}A\Delta\{i,j\}&\text{ if }|A\cap\{i,j\}|=1;\\
A&\text{ otherwise.}\end{cases}

Clearly, we have
|A_{i,j}|=|A|
.
Let
\mathcal{A}\subseteq 2^{[n]}
be a family. Define
\mathcal{A}_{i,j}=\{A_{i,j}:A\in\mathcal{A}\}
. We say that
\mathcal{A}
is
exchange stable
on
[s]
if
\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{A}_{i,j}\text{ for all }1\leqslant i\neq j\leqslant s
.
Note that in this case if
\{A\cap[s]:A\in\mathcal{A}\}
contains an
a
-element subset, then it contains all
a
-element subsets of
[s]
.
The
symmetric extent
of
\mathcal{A}
is the largest integer
s
such that
\mathcal{A}
is exchange stable on
[s]
, i.e.,

\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{A}_{i,j}\text{ for all }1\leqslant i\neq j\leqslant s,\text{ but }\mathcal{A}\neq\mathcal{A}_{i,s+1}\text{ for some }1\leqslant i\leqslant s.

For any
r\in[n]
, we define

\mathcal{A}^{-r}=\{A\in\mathcal{A}:A_{i,r+1}\notin\mathcal{A}\text{ for some }1\leqslant i\leqslant r\}.

If
\mathcal{A}\neq\emptyset
with its symmetric extent
s<n
, then
\mathcal{A}^{-s}\neq\emptyset
and
\mathcal{A}^{-r}=\emptyset
for all
r<s
.

For
\vec{\mathcal{A}}=(\mathcal{A}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{A}_{m})\in(2^{[n]})^{m}
, the
symmetric extent
of
\vec{\mathcal{A}}
is defined as the minimal symmetric extent of
\mathcal{A}_{i}
for all
i\in[m]
.
We need the following crucial properties concerning the symmetric extent, which is inspired by
[
3
, Lemma 2]
.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
\vec{\mathcal{A}}=(\mathcal{A}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{A}_{m})\in(2^{[n]})^{m}
be a shifted sequence. Let
s
be the symmetric extent of
\vec{\mathcal{A}}
with
s<n
.
Then the following statements hold.

(1)

If
\mathcal{A}_{k}^{-s}\neq\emptyset
, then
s+1\notin A
for any
A\in\mathcal{A}_{k}^{-s}
.

(2)

If
\mathcal{A}_{k}^{-s}\neq\emptyset
, then
A_{j,s+1}\notin\mathcal{A}_{k}
for any
A\in\mathcal{A}_{k}^{-s}
and
j\in A\cap[s]
.

(3)

If
\mathcal{A}_{k}^{-s}\neq\emptyset
and
A=B\cup C\in\mathcal{A}_{k}^{-s}
, where
B=A\cap[1,s]
and
C=A\cap[s+1,n]
,
then
B^{\prime}\cup C\in\mathcal{A}_{k}^{-s}
for every
B^{\prime}\subseteq[s]
with
|B^{\prime}|=|B|
.

(4)

Assume that
\vec{\mathcal{A}}
is pairwise cross
t
-intersecting,
\mathcal{A}_{k}^{-s}\neq\emptyset
and
\mathcal{A}_{q}\setminus\mathcal{A}_{q}^{-s}\neq\emptyset
for some
k\neq q\in[m]
. Let
A\in\mathcal{A}_{k}^{-s}
and
D\in\mathcal{A}_{q}\setminus\mathcal{A}_{q}^{-s}
. Then

|A_{i,s+1}\cap D|\geqslant t\text{ for all }1\leqslant i\leqslant s.

(5)

Assume that
\vec{\mathcal{A}}
is pairwise cross
t
-intersecting,
\mathcal{A}_{k}^{-s}\neq\emptyset
and
\mathcal{A}_{q}^{-s}\neq\emptyset
for some
k\neq q\in[m]
. Let
A_{1}\in\mathcal{A}_{k}^{-s}
and
A_{2}\in\mathcal{A}_{q}^{-s}
be such that
|A_{1}\cap[s]|+|A_{2}\cap[s]|\neq s+t
. Then
|A_{1}\cap A_{2}|\geqslant t+1
.

Proof.

(1) For any
A\in\mathcal{A}_{k}^{-s}
, there exists some
i\in[s]

such that
A_{i,s+1}\notin\mathcal{A}_{k}
. This implies that
|A\cap\{i,s+1\}|=1
. If

A\cap\{i,s+1\}=\{s+1\}
, then
A_{i,s+1}=(A\backslash\{s+1\})\cup\{i\}
. Note that
\mathcal{A}_{k}
is shifted.
Thus, we have
A_{i,s+1}\in\mathcal{A}_{k}
, which is a contradiction. This proves that

A\cap\{i,s+1\}=\{i\}
and
s+1\notin A
.

(2) For any
A\in\mathcal{A}_{k}^{-s}
, there exists some
i\in[s]

such that
A_{i,s+1}\notin\mathcal{A}_{k}
. By (1), we get
A\cap\{i,s+1\}=\{i\}
.
If
j=i
, then we are done. So let
j\neq i
.
Suppose for the contradiction that
A_{j,s+1}\in\mathcal{A}_{k}
.
Since the symmetric extent of
\mathcal{A}_{k}
is at least
s
, we infer that
(A_{j,s+1})_{i,j}\in\mathcal{A}_{k}
.
As
A_{j,s+1}=(A\backslash\{j\})\cup\{s+1\}
, we have

(A_{j,s+1})_{i,j}=(A\backslash\{i\})\cup\{s+1\}=A_{i,s+1}
. However, we have
A_{i,s+1}\notin\mathcal{A}_{k}
, which leads to a contradiction. Thus, we conclude that

A_{j,s+1}\notin\mathcal{A}_{k}
.

(3) Since the symmetric extent of
\mathcal{A}_{k}
is at least
s
, we know by definition that
\mathcal{A}_{k}
is exchange stable on
[s]
. This leads to

B^{\prime}\cup C\in\mathcal{A}_{k}
. Assume for the sake of contradiction that
B^{\prime}\cup C\notin\mathcal{A}_{k}^{-s}
. Then
(B^{\prime}\cup C)_{i,s+1}\in\mathcal{A}_{k}
for all
i\in[s]
.
By (1), we get
s+1\notin C
.
We can choose
j^{\prime}\in B^{\prime}
and
j\in B
arbitrarily. Then we have

(B^{\prime}\cup C)_{j^{\prime},s+1}=(B^{\prime}\backslash\{j^{\prime}\})\cup C\cup\{s+1\}\in\mathcal{A}_{k}
and

(B\cup C)_{j,s+1}=(B\backslash\{j\})\cup C\cup\{s+1\}
.
Observe that
(B\backslash\{j\})\cup C\cup\{s+1\}
can be obtained from

(B^{\prime}\backslash\{j^{\prime}\})\cup C\cup\{s+1\}

by applying exchange operations on
[s]
.
By the exchange stability, we derive that
(B\backslash\{j\})\cup C\cup\{s+1\}\in\mathcal{A}_{k}
.
That is
A_{j,s+1}\notin\mathcal{A}_{k}
.
However, (2) leads to
A_{j,s+1}\notin\mathcal{A}_{k}
, a contradiction.
Therefore, we conclude that
B^{\prime}\cup C\in\mathcal{A}_{k}^{-s}
.

(4) By applying (1), we have
s+1\notin A
. We choose
i\in[s]
arbitrarily.
If
i\notin A
, then
A_{i,s+1}=A
. Since
\mathcal{A}_{k}
and
\mathcal{A}_{q}
are cross
t
-intersecting, we get
|A_{i,s+1}\cap D|=|A\cap D|\geqslant t
.
Next, we assume that
i\in A
.
Then

A_{i,s+1}=(A\backslash\{i\})\cup\{s+1\}
. Observe that
i\notin D
or
s+1\in D
would imply
|A_{i,s+1}\cap D|\geqslant|A\cap D|\geqslant t
.
So let
i\in D
and
s+1\notin D
.
It follows from
D\in\mathcal{A}_{q}\setminus\mathcal{A}_{q}^{-s}

that
D_{i,s+1}\in\mathcal{A}_{q}
. This leads to
D_{i,s+1}=(D\backslash\{i\})\cup\{s+1\}\in\mathcal{A}_{q}
. Invoking the fact that
\mathcal{A}_{k}
and
\mathcal{A}_{q}
are cross
t
-intersecting, we obtain
|A_{i,s+1}\cap D|=|A\cap D_{i,s+1}|\geqslant t
.

(5) If
|A_{1}\cap[s]|+|A_{2}\cap[s]|\geqslant s+t+1
, then
|A_{1}\cap A_{2}|\geqslant|A_{1}\cap A_{2}\cap[s]|\geqslant|A_{1}\cap[s]|+|A_{2}\cap[s]|-s\geqslant t+1
, as desired. Next, let
|A_{1}\cap[s]|+|A_{2}\cap[s]|\leqslant s+t-1
. For convenience, we denote
a:=|A_{1}\cap[s]|
and
b:=|A_{2}\cap[s]|
.
Since
\mathcal{A}_{k}
and
\mathcal{A}_{q}
are both exchange stable on
[s]
, we infer that
A_{1}^{\prime}:=[a]\cup(A_{1}\cap[s+1,n])\in\mathcal{A}_{k}

and
A_{2}^{\prime}:=[s-b+1,s]\cup(A_{2}\cap[s+1,n])\in\mathcal{A}_{q}
.
Then
|A_{1}^{\prime}\cap A_{2}^{\prime}\cap[s]|=|[a]\cap[s-b+1,s]|\leqslant\text{max}\{0,a+b-s\}\leqslant t-1
. This implies that
|A_{1}^{\prime}\cap A_{2}^{\prime}\cap[s+1,n]|\geqslant 1
because
\mathcal{A}_{k}
and
\mathcal{A}_{q}
are cross
t
-intersecting.
Thus, we have
|A_{1}\cap A_{2}\cap[s+1,n]|\geqslant 1
. Using (1), we get
s+1\notin A_{1}\cap A_{2}
. Consequently, there exists
x\in A_{1}\cap A_{2}\cap[s+2,n]
.
By the shiftedness,
(A_{1}\backslash\{x\})\cup\{s+1\}\in\mathcal{A}_{k}
, which together with the cross
t
-intersecting property, yields

|A_{1}\cap A_{2}|=|((A_{1}\backslash\{x\})\cup\{s+1\})\cap A_{2}|+1\geqslant t+1
, as needed.
∎

Following the previous notation, we define
\mathcal{A}_{k}^{-s}=\{A\in\mathcal{A}_{k}:A_{i,s+1}\notin\mathcal{A}_{k}\text{ for some }1\leqslant i\leqslant s\}
and we denote

\vec{\mathcal{A}}^{-s}=(\mathcal{A}_{1}^{-s},\ldots,\mathcal{A}_{m}^{-s})
.
For every
\mathcal{A}_{k}
, consider the following partition:

\mathcal{A}_{k}^{-s}=\bigcup_{i=1}^{s}\mathcal{A}_{k}^{-s,i},

where
\mathcal{A}_{k}^{-s,i}=\{A\in\mathcal{A}_{k}^{-s}:|A\cap[s]|=i\}
.
Let
\mathcal{A}_{k,*}^{-s,i}=\{A\cap[s+2,n]:A\in\mathcal{A}_{k}^{-s,i}\}
. Moreover, let

\vec{\mathcal{A}}^{-s,i}=(\mathcal{A}_{1}^{-s,i},\ldots,\mathcal{A}_{m}^{-s,i})
and
\vec{\mathcal{A}}_{*}^{-s,i}=(\mathcal{A}_{1,*}^{-s,i},\ldots,\mathcal{A}_{m,*}^{-s,i})
.

To apply the pushing-pulling method, we need the following result.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
\mathcal{A}_{k}\subseteq 2^{[n]}
be a monotone shifted family. Let
\ell
and
s
be the extent and the symmetric extent of
\mathcal{A}_{k}
, respectively. Suppose that
s<n-1
,
\ell<n
and
\mathcal{A}_{k,*}^{-s,i}\neq\emptyset
. Then

\left|\mathcal{A}_{k,*}^{-s,i}[n]\right|=\left|\mathcal{A}_{k,*}^{-s,i}(\bar{n})\right|.

Proof.

We are going to establish a bijection from
\mathcal{A}_{k,*}^{-s,i}[n]
to

\mathcal{A}_{k,*}^{-s,i}(\bar{n})
.
Let
\phi:\mathcal{A}_{k,*}^{-s,i}[n]\longrightarrow\mathcal{A}_{k,*}^{-s,i}(\bar{n})
be a map defined as
\phi(C)=C\backslash\{n\}
, where
C\in\mathcal{A}_{k,*}^{-s,i}[n]
.
It is sufficient to prove that
\phi
is a bijection. Consequently, we get
|\mathcal{A}_{k,*}^{-s,i}[n]|=|\mathcal{A}_{k,*}^{-s,i}(\bar{n})|
.

To start with, we choose
C\in\mathcal{A}_{k,*}^{-s,i}[n]
arbitrarily. Then there exists a set
A\in\mathcal{A}_{k}^{-s,i}
such that
A\cap[s+2,n]=C
.
By Part (1) of Lemma
2.5
, we have
s+1\notin A
.
Since
\ell
is the extent of
\mathcal{A}_{k}
and
\ell<n
, we have
A\backslash\{n\}\in\mathcal{A}_{k}
. We claim that
A\backslash\{n\}\in\mathcal{A}_{k}^{-s,i}
. It suffices to show that
A\backslash\{n\}\in\mathcal{A}_{k}^{-s}
.
Suppose on the contrary that
A\backslash\{n\}\in\mathcal{A}_{k}\backslash\mathcal{A}_{k}^{-s}
. Then for any
a\in A\cap[s]
, we have

(A\backslash\{a,n\})\cup\{s+1\}\in\mathcal{A}_{k}
. Since
\mathcal{A}_{k}
is monotone, we infer that

Lemma 2.8

Since

\|\vec{\mathcal{A}}^{-s,a}\|=\binom{s}{a}\|\vec{\mathcal{A}}_{*}^{-s,a}\|

For every

\mathcal{B}(k,s,i)=\begin{cases}\{B:B\cap[s]=i-1,s+1\in B,B\cap[s+2,n]\in\mathcal{A}_{k,*}^{-s,i}\}&\text{ if }\mathcal{A}_{k}^{-s,i}\neq\emptyset;\\

\vec{\mathcal{B}}(s,a)\neq(\emptyset,\ldots,\emptyset)

\|\vec{\mathcal{B}}(s,a)\|=\binom{s}{a-1}\|\vec{\mathcal{A}}_{*}^{-s,a}\|

\vec{\mathcal{A}}^{-s,a}\neq(\emptyset,\ldots,\emptyset)

We have the following two cases
a\neq s+t-a
; or
a=\frac{s+t}{2}

Case 1.
For the case
a\neq s+t-a
, let

\displaystyle\vec{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}=\vec{\mathcal{A}}-\vec{\mathcal{A}}^{-s,a}+\vec{\mathcal{B}}(s,s+t-a)

\vec{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime\prime}=\vec{\mathcal{A}}-\vec{\mathcal{A}}^{-s,s+t-a}+\vec{\mathcal{B}}(s,a).

Recall that
\mathcal{A}_{k}\backslash\mathcal{A}_{k}^{-s}\neq\emptyset
for all
k\in[m]
.
Using Parts (4) and (5) of Lemma
2.5

\vec{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}

\vec{\mathcal{B}}(s,a)\neq(\emptyset,\ldots,\emptyset)

\max\left\{\|\vec{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}\|,\|\vec{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime\prime}\|\right\}>\|\vec{\mathcal{A}}\|

In what follows, we denote

Case 2.

\vec{\mathcal{A}}^{-s,\frac{s+t}{2}}(\bar{n})=\Big(\mathcal{A}_{1}^{-s,\frac{s+t}{2}}(\bar{n}),\ldots,\mathcal{A}_{m}^{-s,\frac{s+t}{2}}(\bar{n})\Big)

\displaystyle=\vec{\mathcal{A}}-\vec{\mathcal{A}}^{-s,\tfrac{s+t}{2}}(\bar{n})+\vec{\mathcal{B}}(s,\tfrac{s+t}{2})[n],

\|\vec{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime\prime\prime}\|>\|\vec{\mathcal{A}}\|

Proof of Theorem
1.6

Throughout the proof, we denote

and

\mathcal{S}(n,\ell)=\left\{S\subseteq[n]:[\ell]\subseteq S\right\}.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\ell\in[t,n]
and
f(\ell)=\left(\sum_{k=t}^{\ell}\binom{\ell}{k}+m-1\right)2^{n-\ell}
. Then

f(\ell)\leq\max\{f(t),f(n)\}.

Proof.

For
\ell\in[t,n-1]
, we have

\frac{f(\ell+1)}{f(\ell)}=\frac{2\sum_{k=t}^{\ell}\binom{\ell}{k}+\binom{\ell}{t-1}+m-1}{2\left(\sum_{k=t}^{\ell}\binom{\ell}{k}+m-1\right)}=1+\frac{\binom{\ell}{t-1}-(m-1)}{2\left(\sum_{k=t}^{\ell}\binom{\ell}{k}+m-1\right)}.

It follows that
f(\ell+1)\geqslant f(\ell)
if and only if
\binom{\ell}{t-1}\geqslant(m-1)
. Since
\binom{\ell}{t-1}
is increasing with respect to
\ell
, we infer that

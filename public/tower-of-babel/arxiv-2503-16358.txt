Rates of convergence in the multivariate weak invariance principle for nonuniformly hyperbolic maps Thanks: This work is based on material from the author’s PhD thesis; he gratefully acknowledges funding from the University of Warwick and helpful feedback from Ian Melbourne and the referee.
Nicholas Fleming-Vázquez

Abstract
. We obtain rates of convergence in the weak invariance principle for ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} -valued Hölder observables over nonuniformly hyperbolic maps. In particular, for maps modelled by a Young tower with superpolynomial tails (e.g. the Sinai billiard map, and Axiom A diffeomorphisms) we obtain a rate of O ⁡ ( n − r ) O(n^{-r}) in the Wasserstein p p -metric for all r < 1 / 4 r<1/4 and p < ∞ p<\infty . Additionally, this is the first result on rates that covers certain invertible, slowly mixing maps, such as Bunimovich flowers.

1.
Introduction

Let
T:M\to M
be a chaotic dynamical system with ergodic invariant probability measure
\mu
. Let
v:M\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
be an observable with
\int v\,d\mu=0
and let
S_{n}=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}{v\circ T^{i}}
be its sequence of Birkhoff sums. Consider the càdlàg process
W_{n}\in D([0,1],\mathbb{R}^{d})
defined by
W_{n}(t)=n^{-1/2}S_{[nt]}
. Then
v
is said to satisfy the
weak invariance principle (WIP)
if
W_{n}
weakly converges to a Brownian motion.

For many classes of dynamical systems the WIP is satisfied by all sufficiently regular observables. For example, for nonuniformly hyperbolic/expanding maps modelled by a Young tower whose return time function is square-integrable
[
30
,
31
]
, the WIP is satisfied by all Hölder observables. This class includes many examples such as Axiom A diffeomorphisms, dispersing billiards and certain intermittent maps.

The problem of quantifying the convergence rate in the WIP for dynamical systems has attracted much interest recently
[
1
,
7
,
20
,
21
,
25
,
17
,
19
,
24
]
.

Antoniou & Melbourne
[
1
]
obtained rates in the Prokhorov metric for
\mathbb{R}
-valued Hölder observables of nonuniformly expanding maps. In the same setting, Liu & Wang
[
20
]
obtained rates in the Wasserstein-
p
metric and Dedecker, Merlevède & Rio
[
7
]
established improved rates in the Wasserstein-
2
metric. In particular, for systems modelled by Young towers with superpolynomial tails,
[
1
]
obtained a rate of
O(n^{-\kappa})
in the Prokhorov metric for all
\kappa<\frac{1}{4}
,
[
20
]
obtained the same rate in the Wasserstein-
p
metric for all
p<\infty
, and
[
7
]
achieved a sharper bound of
O(n^{-1/4}(\log n)^{1/4})
in the Wasserstein-2 metric. The papers
[
1
,
20
]
rely on the martingale approximation method, whereas
[
7
]
proves strong approximation results for a class of weakly dependent sequences of random variables. The method in
[
7
]
is based on constructing an explicit approximating sequence of iid Gaussian random variables and controlling the approximation error via a quantitative conditional central limit theorem together with a Fuk–Nagaev type inequality.

The only existing result on rates in the
multivariate
WIP (that is for
\mathbb{R}^{d}
-valued observables with
d>1
) for dynamical systems is due to Paviato
[
25
]
, and applies to the aforementioned class of nonuniformly expanding maps, and also to nonuniformly hyperbolic flows. He obtained rates in the Wasserstein-
1
metric that are independent of
d
but are
at best
O(n^{-1/6})
for
d>1
. This is because he uses martingale methods, and obtaining better rates in the multivariate WIP remains an open problem for martingales (see the introduction of
[
6
]
for more details).

In this paper, we obtain rates in the multivariate WIP in the Wasserstein-
p
metric for nonuniformly hyperbolic maps modelled by Young towers. In particular, for systems modelled by a Young tower with superpolynomial tails we obtain a rate of
O(n^{-\kappa})
in the Wasserstein-
p
metric for all
\kappa<\frac{1}{4}
and
p<\infty
. In contrast to existing results on rates in the WIP for nonuniformly hyperbolic maps, we do not assume exponential contraction along stable leaves so our results apply to slowly mixing invertible systems such as Bunimovich flowers
[
3
]
(see Example
1.3
).

In our main theorem (Theorem
2.4
) we obtain rates in the WIP under a weak dependence condition called the functional correlation bound, where the rate obtained depends on the polynomial rate of decay in the functional correlation bound. By
[
10
]
, this condition is satisfied by systems modelled by Young towers with
O(n^{-\beta})
tails for
\beta>2
. The proof of our main theorem is based on Bernstein’s classical ‘big block-small block’ method and is loosely inspired by the proof of the central limit theorem in
[
4
, Section 7.8]
, see Remark
4.1
for more details.

We end this introduction by illustrating the rates we obtain in the WIP for several examples.

Example 1.1
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,1)
. Define
g:[0,1/2)\to[0,1]
by
g(x)=x(1+2^{\alpha}x^{\alpha})
. Consider the intermittent map
T:[0,1]\to[0,1]
defined by

T(x)=\begin{cases}g(x),&x\in[0,1/2),\\
2x-1,&x\in[1/2,1].\end{cases}

The LSV map
T
is a prototypical example of a nonuniformly expanding map
[
22
]
and has a unique absolutely continuous invariant probability measure. As in
[
23
]
, consider an intermittent baker’s map
S:[0,1]^{2}\to[0,1]^{2}
defined by

S(x,y)=\begin{cases}(T(x),g^{-1}(y)),&x\in[0,1/2),\\
(T(x),(y+1)/2),&x\in[1/2,1].\end{cases}

The map
S
is invertible and has a unique physical measure. Let
\alpha\in(0,1/2)
. Then Hölder observables satisfy the WIP for both maps
T
and
S
. Prior results on rates in the WIP cover the map
T
but they do not apply to the nonuniformly hyperbolic map
S
since
S
contracts at a polynomial rate along stable manifolds (which consist of vertical line segments).

Set
\gamma=1/\alpha-1
. We prove rates in the Wasserstein-1 metric for
\mathbb{R}^{d}
-valued Hölder observables for both maps
T
and
S
. The rates are of the form
O(n^{-\kappa(\gamma)})
where

\kappa(\gamma)=\frac{(\gamma-1)(2\gamma-1)}{2(4\gamma^{2}+\gamma-1)}.

More generally, we obtain rates in the Wasserstein-
p
metric for all
1\leq p<2\gamma
.

Let us now compare the rates we obtain with existing results for the map
T
. For
d=1
,
[
1
]
obtained a rate of
O(n^{-1/4+\alpha/2+\varepsilon})
in the Prokhorov metric, whilst in the restricted range
\alpha\in(0,1/4)

[
20
]
obtained a rate of
O(n^{-1/4+\frac{\alpha}{4(1-\alpha)}+\varepsilon})
in the Wasserstein-
p
metric for any
\varepsilon>0
and
p<\frac{1}{2\alpha}
. For
p=2
,
[
7
]
obtained an improved rate of the form

\begin{cases}O(n^{\alpha-\frac{1}{2}}(\log n)^{\frac{1}{2}}),&\alpha\in(\frac{1}{4},\frac{1}{2})\\
O(n^{-\frac{1}{4}}(\log n)^{\frac{1}{4}}),&\alpha\in(0,\frac{1}{4}].\end{cases}

For all
d\geq 1
,
[
25
]
obtained rates in the Wasserstein-
1
metric of the form

\begin{cases}O(n^{\alpha-\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}),&\alpha\in[\frac{1}{3},\frac{1}{2})\\
O(n^{-\frac{1}{6}+\varepsilon}),&\alpha\in(0,\frac{1}{3}),\end{cases}

where
\varepsilon>0
is arbitrary.

In particular, for the map
T
our rates improve on those in
[
25
]
for
d>1
and
\alpha\leq\frac{1}{16}(7-\sqrt{17})
. However, for
d=1
the rates obtained in
[
7
]
are better than ours for any
\alpha\in(0,1/2)
.

Example 1.2
.

In
[
8
]
, Eslami & Liverani considered a class of area-preserving
C^{\infty}
almost Anosov maps on
\mathbb{R}^{2}/\mathbb{Z}^{2}
with a neutral fixed point at
(0,0)
. The maps are of the form

T(x,y)=(x+h(x)+y,h(x)+y)
, where
h(x)=bx^{3}+O(x^{5})
for some
b>0
. We obtain a rate of
O(n^{-5/38})
in the
1
-Wasserstein metric for
\mathbb{R}^{d}
-valued Hölder observables.

Example 1.3

(Bunimovich flowers)
.

In
[
3
]
, Bunimovich introduced a class of planar chaotic billiards with both dispersing and focusing boundary components, which are now known as Bunimovich flowers. Let
T
be the billiard map and let
\mu
be the unique absolutely continuous invariant probability measure. We obtain a rate of
O(n^{-3/34})
in the 1-Wasserstein metric for
\mathbb{R}^{d}
-valued Hölder observables.

See
[
16
, Section 5]
for a detailed description of Bunimovich flowers and other examples of nonuniformly hyperbolic maps to which our results apply.

Notation
.

We write
a_{n}=O(b_{n})
or
a_{n}\ll b_{n}
if there exists a constant
C>0
such that
a_{n}\leq Cb_{n}
for all
n\geq 1.
We write
a_{n}\sim b_{n}
if
\lim_{n}a_{n}/b_{n}=1.

For
d\geq 1
and
a,b\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
denote
a\otimes b=ab^{T}.
We endow
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with the norm
|y|=\sum_{i=1}^{d}|y_{i}|.
Let
\eta\in(0,1]
. Recall that
v\colon M\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
is an
\eta
-Hölder observable on a metric space
(M,\rho)
if
\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{\eta}=\left|v\right|_{\infty}+[v]_{\eta}<\infty
, where
[v]_{\eta}=\sup_{x\neq y}\frac{|v(x)-v(y)|}{\rho(x,y)^{\eta}}
. If
\eta=1
we call
v
Lipschitz and write
\mathrm{Lip}(v)=[v]_{1}
.

The rest of this article is structured as follows. In Section
2
, we recall the definition of the Wasserstein-
p

, we recall some results from the probability theory literature, as well as some consequences of the functional correlation bound. Finally, in Section

2.
Setup and main result

2.1.
The Wasserstein
p
-metric

We consider the space
D=D([0,1],\mathbb{R}^{d})
of càdlàg functions
x:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
with the supremum norm
\left|x\right|_{\infty}=\sup_{0\leq t\leq 1}|x(t)|
.

Given Radon probability measures
\mu
and
\nu
on
D
, we let
\Pi(\mu,\nu)
denote the set of Radon probability measures on
D\times D
for which the projections on the first and second factors coincide with
\mu
and
\nu
, respectively.

For
p\geq 1
, let
\mathcal{P}_{r}^{p}(D)
denote the set of Radon probability measures on
D
such that
x\mapsto\left|x\right|_{\infty}^{p}
is integrable. The
Wasserstein-
p
metric
on
\mathcal{P}_{r}^{p}(D)
is defined by

\mathcal{W}_{p}(\mu,\nu)=\inf_{\sigma\in\Pi(\mu,\nu)}\left(\int_{D\times D}\left|x-y\right|_{\infty}^{p}d\sigma(x,y)\right)^{1/p}.

If
X
and
Y
are random elements of
D
with respective laws
\mu
and
\nu
, we write
\mathcal{W}_{p}(X,Y)=\mathcal{W}_{p}(\mu,\nu)
.

By the Kantorovich-Rubinstein Theorem, for all
\mu
,
\nu\in\mathcal{P}_{r}^{1}(D)
, we have that

(2.1)

\mathcal{W}_{1}(\mu,\nu)=\sup\bigg\{\int f\,d\mu-\int f\,d\nu\bigg|f:D\to\mathbb{R}\text{ is Lipschitz and }\mathrm{Lip}(f)\leq 1\bigg\}.

See
[
2
, Chapter 3]
for more details on the Wasserstein-
p
metric.

2.2.
The Functional Correlation Bound

Let
(M,\rho)
be a metric space and fix
\eta\in(0,1]
. Recall that
[v]_{\eta}=\sup_{x\neq y}\frac{|v(x)-v(y)|}{\rho(x,y)^{\eta}}
denotes the
\eta
-Hölder seminorm of an observable
v:M\to\mathbb{R}
. Let
q\geq 1
be an integer. Given a function
G\colon M^{q}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
and
0\leq i<q
we denote

[G]_{\eta,i}=\sup_{x_{0},\dots,x_{q-1}\in M}[G(x_{0},\dots,x_{i-1},\cdot,x_{i+1},\dots,x_{q-1})]_{\eta}.

We call
G

separately
\eta
-Hölder
, and write
G\in\mathcal{H}^{\eta}_{q}(M)
, if
\left|G\right|_{\infty}+\sum_{i=0}^{q-1}[G]_{\eta,i}{<\infty}.

Let
\gamma>0
. We consider dynamical systems which satisfy the following property:

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\mu
be a Borel probability measure and let
T:M\to M
be an ergodic
\mu
-preserving transformation. Suppose that there exists a constant
C>0
such that for all integers
0\leq p<q,\ 0\leq n_{0}\leq\cdots\leq n_{q-1}
,

\displaystyle\bigg|\int_{M}G(T^{n_{0}}x,\dots,T^{n_{q-1}}x)d\mu(x)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad-\int_{M^{2}}G(T^{n_{0}}x_{0},\dots,T^{n_{p-1}}x_{0},T^{n_{p}}x_{1},\dots,T^{n_{q-1}}x_{1})d\mu(x_{0})d\mu(x_{1})\bigg|

(2.2)

\displaystyle\quad\leq C(n_{p}-n_{p-1})^{-\gamma}\,\biggl(\left|G\right|_{\infty}+\sum_{i=0}^{q-1}[G]_{\eta,i}\biggr)

for all
G\in\mathcal{H}^{\eta}_{q}(M)
. Then we say that
T
satisfies the Functional Correlation Bound with rate
n^{-\gamma}
.

Remark 2.2
.

If
T:M\to M
satisfies the Functional Correlation Bound with rate
n^{-\gamma}
, then decay of correlations holds for
\eta
-Hölder observables with the same rate. Indeed, let
v,w\in\mathcal{C}^{\eta}(M,\mathbb{R})
. Then setting
G(x_{0},x_{1})=v(x_{0})w(x_{1})
, we obtain that for all
n\geq 1
,

\left|\int_{M}v\,w\circ T^{n}d\mu-\int_{M}v\,d\mu\int_{M}w\,d\mu\right|\leq C\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{\eta}\left\lVert w\right\rVert_{\eta}n^{-\gamma}.

By
[
10
, Theorem 2.3]
, if
T:M\to M
is a mixing nonuniformly hyperbolic map modelled by a Young tower with tails of the form
O(n^{-(\gamma+1)})
, then
T
satisfies the Functional Correlation Bound with rate
n^{-\gamma}
.

The Functional Correlation Bound is inspired by a condition of the same name introduced by Leppänen
[
18
]
. However, for measure-preserving transformations the condition considered by Leppänen is slightly more restrictive than Definition
2.1
; in particular, the term
\sum_{i=0}^{q-1}[G]_{\eta,i}
in (
2.2
) is replaced by
\max_{0\leq i<q}[G]_{\eta,i}
.

2.3.
Rates in the WIP

Let
T:M\to M
satisfy the Functional Correlation Bound with rate
n^{-\gamma}
,
\gamma>1
. Let
v\in\mathcal{C}^{\eta}(M,\mathbb{R}^{d})
be mean zero. For
n\geq 1
and
t\in[0,1]
, we define

S_{n}=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}v\circ T^{i}
and
W_{n}(t)=n^{-1/2}S_{[nt]}
. We view
W_{n}
as a random element of
D([0,1],\mathbb{R}^{d})
defined on the probability space
(M,\mu)
. Note that the law of
W_{n}
is a Radon measure since we can write
W_{n}=\pi(v,\dots,v\circ T^{n-1})
where
\pi:(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{n}\to D([0,1],\mathbb{R}^{d})
is continuous.

By
[
11
, Theorem 1.2]
, the observable
v
satisfies the WIP:

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
T:M\to M
satisfy the Functional Correlation Bound with rate
n^{-\gamma}
,
\gamma>1
, and suppose that
v\in\mathcal{C}^{\eta}(M,\mathbb{R}^{d})
with mean zero. Then the limit
\Sigma=\lim_{n\to\infty}n^{-1}\mathbb{E}_{\mu\!\!}\left[S_{n}\otimes S_{n}\right]
exists. Moreover,
W_{n}
converges weakly in
D([0,1],\mathbb{R}^{d})
to a Brownian motion
W
with covariance
\Sigma
.

Our main theorem gives a bound on the rate of convergence in the WIP in the Wasserstein-
r
metric for
1\leq r<2\gamma
:

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
T:M\to M
satisfy the Functional Correlation Bound with rate
n^{-\gamma}
,
\gamma>1
, and suppose that
v\in\mathcal{C}^{\eta}(M,\mathbb{R}^{d})
with mean zero. Let
1\leq r<2\gamma
. Then there is a constant
C>0
such that
\mathcal{W}_{r}(W_{n},W)\leq Cn^{-\kappa(\gamma,r)}
for all
n\geq 1
, where

\kappa(\gamma,r)=\frac{(2\gamma-r)(2\gamma-1)(\gamma-1)}{2(8\gamma^{3}-r(2\gamma^{2}+3\gamma-1))}.

Remark 2.5
.

(i) This result yields the bound
\pi(W_{n},W)=O\big(n^{-\frac{r}{r+1}\kappa(\gamma,r)}\big)
in the Prokhorov metric. Indeed,
\pi(\mu,\nu)\leq\mathcal{W}_{r}(\mu,\nu)^{\frac{r}{r+1}}
for any
r\in[1,\infty)
and any probability measures
\mu
,
\nu
on
D=D([0,1],\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

(ii) This result also gives a convergence rate in the central limit theorem in the Wasserstein-
r
metric: by considering the projection
x\in D\mapsto x(1)
, we obtain

\mathcal{W}_{r}(n^{-\frac{1}{2}}S_{n},W(1))\leq\mathcal{W}_{r}(W_{n},W).

(iii) For probability measures
\mu
,
\nu
on
\mathbb{R}
such that
\nu
is absolutely continuous with density
g
, the Kolmogorov metric
d_{K}
satisfies
d_{K}(\mu,\nu)\leq(1+\left|g\right|_{\infty})\pi(\mu,\nu)
(see
[
12
]
). Hence for
d=1
, we obtain the (suboptimal) Berry-Esseen type bound
d_{K}(n^{-\frac{1}{2}}S_{n},W(1))=O\big(n^{-\frac{r}{r+1}\kappa(\gamma,r)}\big)
.

Note that in particular
\lim_{\gamma\to\infty}\kappa(\gamma,r)=\frac{1}{4}
for all
r\in(1,\infty)
. The exact formula for
\kappa
could possibly be improved by more careful arguments. However, since our proof is based on Bernstein’s ‘big block-small block’ technique, it does not seem possible to obtain a rate better than
O(n^{-1/4})
.

As mentioned above, if
T:M\to M
is a mixing nonuniformly hyperbolic map modelled by a Young tower with
O(n^{-(\gamma+1)})
tails, then
T
satisfies the Functional Correlation Bound with rate
n^{-\gamma}
by
[
10
, Theorem 2.3]
. Combining this with Theorem
2.4
yields the following corollary:

Corollary 2.6
.

Let
T:M\to M
be a mixing transformation modelled by a Young tower with
O(n^{-(\gamma+1)})
tails, where
\gamma>1
. Let
v\in\mathcal{C}^{\eta}(M,\mathbb{R}^{d})
with mean zero and let
1\leq r<2\gamma
. Then there is a constant
C>0
such that
\mathcal{W}_{r}(W_{n},W)\leq Cn^{-\kappa(\gamma,r)}
for all
n\geq 1
.

The rates given in Examples
1.1
-
1.3
follow immediately from Corollary
2.6
. Indeed, the LSV map
T
and intermittent baker’s map
S
from Example
1.1
are modelled by Young towers with
O(n^{-1/\alpha})
tails
[
31
,
23
]
.
By
[
8
]
, Example
1.2
is modelled by a Young tower with
O(n^{-4})
tails.
Finally, the rate given in Example
1.3
for Bunimovich flowers follows from the fact that the billiard map is modelled by a Young tower with
O(n^{-3})
tails
[
16
]
. More precisely, Chernov & Zhang
[
5
]
showed that the map is modelled by a Young tower with
O(n^{-3}(\log n)^{3})
tails and Bálint & Komálovics
[
16
]
recently removed the logarithmic factor.

3.
Preliminaries

3.1.
Probabilistic preliminaries

Throughout this subsection, we fix an integer
d\geq 1
. We will need the following basic interpolation inequality for the Wasserstein metric:

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
1\leq r<s
. Let
X_{1},X_{2}
be random elements of
D=D([0,1],\mathbb{R}^{d})
with laws in
\mathcal{P}_{r}^{s}(D)
. Let
\theta=\frac{1/r-1/s}{1-1/s}
. Then

\mathcal{W}_{r}(X_{1},X_{2})\leq\mathcal{W}_{1}(X_{1},X_{2})^{\theta}(\mathcal{W}_{s}(X_{1},0)+\mathcal{W}_{s}(X_{2},0))^{1-\theta}.

Proof.

For any
\varepsilon>0
we can choose random elements
\tilde{X}_{1},\tilde{X}_{2}\in D
defined on the same probability space such that
\tilde{X}_{i}=_{d}X_{i}
and
\big||\tilde{X}_{1}-\tilde{X}_{2}|_{\infty}\big|_{1}\leq\mathcal{W}_{1}(X_{1},X_{2})+\varepsilon
. Note that
\big||\tilde{X}_{i}|_{\infty}\big|_{s}=\mathcal{W}_{s}(X_{i},0)
. Now by the classical
L^{p}
interpolation inequality,
\left|u\right|_{r}\leq\left|u\right|_{1}^{\theta}\left|u\right|_{s}^{1-\theta}
for all
u\in L^{s}
. It follows that

\displaystyle\left||\tilde{X}_{1}-\tilde{X}_{2}|_{\infty}\right|_{r}\leq(\mathcal{W}_{1}(X_{1},X_{2})+\varepsilon)^{\theta}(\mathcal{W}_{s}(X_{1},0)+\mathcal{W}_{s}(X_{2},0))^{1-\theta}.

Since
\varepsilon>0
is arbitrary and
\mathcal{W}_{r}(X_{1},X_{2})\leq\big||\tilde{X}_{1}-\tilde{X}_{2}|_{\infty}\big|_{r}
, the result follows.
∎

We now recall some results on sequences of independent random vectors that are useful in the proof of our main theorem.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
p\geq 2
. There exists a constant
C>0
such that for all
k\geq 1
, for all independent, mean zero
\mathbb{R}^{d}
-valued random vectors
\widehat{X}_{1},\dots,\widehat{X}_{k}\in L^{p}
,

\mathbb{E}_{\!\!}\left[\biggl|\max_{1\leq j\leq k}\Big|\sum_{i=1}^{j}\widehat{X}_{i}\Big|\biggr|^{p}\right]\leq C\left(\biggl(\sum_{i=1}^{k}\mathbb{E}_{\!\!}\left[|\widehat{X}_{i}|^{2}\right]\biggr)^{p/2}+\sum_{i=1}^{k}\mathbb{E}_{\!\!}\left[|\widehat{X}_{i}|^{p}\right]\right).

Proof.

By working componentwise, without loss of generality we may assume that
d=1
. Define
M(t)=\sum_{i=1}^{[kt]}\vphantom{X}\smash[t]{\widehat{X}}_{i}
. Then
M
is a martingale. Hence by Doob’s maximal inequality,

\left|\max_{1\leq j\leq k}\bigg|\sum_{i=1}^{j}\widehat{X}_{i}\bigg|\right|_{p}=\left|\sup_{0\leq t\leq 1}|M(t)|\right|_{p}\leq\frac{p}{p-1}\left|\sum_{i=1}^{k}\widehat{X}_{i}\right|_{p}.

The result follows by Rosenthal’s inequality
[
27
]
.
∎

We will also need the following strong invariance principle for iid random vectors, which is due to Götze and Zaĭtsev
[
13
, Theorem 3]
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
p\in(2,\infty)
. Then there exists a constant
C>0
such that the following holds. Let
(\vphantom{X}\smash[t]{\widehat{X}}_{i})_{i=1}^{k}
be iid mean zero
\mathbb{R}^{d}
-valued random vectors in
L^{p}
. Then there exists a probability space supporting random vectors
(\widetilde{X}_{i})_{i=1}^{k}
with the same joint distribution as
(\widehat{X}_{i})_{i=1}^{k}
and iid Gaussian random vectors
(Z_{i})_{i=1}^{k}
with mean zero and covariance
V=k{\mathbb{E}[\widehat{X}_{1}\otimes\widehat{X}_{1}]}
such that

\left|\max_{1\leq j\leq k}\bigg|\sum_{i=1}^{j}\widetilde{X}_{i}-\sum_{i=1}^{j}Z_{i}\bigg|\right|_{p}\leq Ck^{1/p}\big|\vphantom{X}\smash[t]{\widehat{X}}_{1}\big|_{p}\frac{\sigma_{\max}(V)}{\sigma_{\min}(V)},

where
\sigma^{2}_{\min}(V)
and
\sigma^{2}_{\max}(V)
are the minimal and maximal positive eigenvalues of
V
, respectively. ∎

We now use this lemma to prove a weak invariance principle with rates for iid random vectors. Define
Y_{k}\in D([0,1],\mathbb{R}^{d})
by
Y_{k}(t)=\sum_{i=0}^{[kt]}\vphantom{X}\smash[t]{\widehat{X}}_{i}
for
t\in[0,1]
.

Corollary 3.4
.

Let
p\in(2,\infty)
. Then there exists
C>0
such that for all
k\geq 1
, for any sequence of iid mean zero
\mathbb{R}^{d}
-valued random vectors
(\vphantom{X}\smash[t]{\widehat{X}}_{i})_{i=1}^{k}
in
L^{p}
we have

\mathcal{W}_{p}(Y_{k},W)\leq Ck^{1/p}\Big(\big|\vphantom{X}\smash[t]{\widehat{X}}_{1}\big|_{p}\frac{\sigma_{\max}(V)}{\sigma_{\min}(V)}+|\mathbb{E}_{\!\!}\left[\vphantom{X}\smash[t]{\widehat{X}}_{1}\otimes\vphantom{X}\smash[t]{\widehat{X}}_{1}\right]^{1/2}|\Big),

where
W
is a Brownian motion with covariance
V=k\mathbb{E}[\vphantom{X}\smash[t]{\widehat{X}}_{1}\otimes\vphantom{X}\smash[t]{\widehat{X}}_{1}]
.

Proof.

Define
A_{k},\,\widetilde{Y}_{k}\in D([0,1],\mathbb{R}^{d})
by
A_{k}(t)=\sum_{i=1}^{[kt]}Z_{i}
and
\widetilde{Y}_{k}(t)=\sum_{i=1}^{[kt]}\widetilde{X}_{i}
. Then by Lemma
3.3
,

\left|\sup_{0\leq t\leq 1}|\widetilde{Y}_{k}(t)-A_{k}(t)|\right|_{p}=\left|\max_{1\leq j\leq k}\bigg|\sum_{i=1}^{j}\widetilde{X}_{i}-\sum_{i=1}^{j}Z_{i}\bigg|\right|_{p}\leq Ck^{1/p}\big|\vphantom{X}\smash[t]{\widehat{X}}_{1}\big|_{p}\frac{\sigma_{\max}(V)}{\sigma_{\min}(V)}.

It follows that

(3.1)

\mathcal{W}_{p}(Y_{k},A_{k})=\mathcal{W}_{p}(\widetilde{Y}_{k},A_{k})\leq Ck^{1/p}\big|\vphantom{X}\smash[t]{\widehat{X}}_{1}\big|_{p}\,\sigma_{\max}(V)/\sigma_{\min}(V).

Let
W
be a Brownian motion with covariance
V
. Then

(W(\frac{j}{k})-W(\frac{j-1}{k}))_{1\leq j\leq k}=_{d}(Z_{j})_{1\leq j\leq k}.

Define
\tilde{A}_{k}\in D([0,1],\mathbb{R}^{d})
by

\tilde{A}_{k}(t)=\sum_{i=1}^{[kt]}\Big(W\big(\tfrac{j}{k}\big)-W\big(\tfrac{j-1}{k}\big)\Big)=W\Big(\tfrac{[kt]}{k}\Big).

It follows that
\tilde{A}_{k}=_{d}A_{k}
. Write
W=V^{1/2}B
, where
B
is a standard Brownian motion. Then

\displaystyle|\tilde{A}(t)-W(t)|

\displaystyle=\left|V^{1/2}\left(B(t)-B\Big(\tfrac{[kt]}{k}\Big)\right)\right|\leq k^{1/2}|\mathbb{E}_{\!\!}\left[\vphantom{X}\smash[t]{\widehat{X}}_{1}\otimes\vphantom{X}\smash[t]{\widehat{X}}_{1}\right]^{1/2}|\left|B(t)-B\Big(\tfrac{[kt]}{k}\Big)\right|.

Now by
[
26
, Theorem 2.1 and p. 28]
, for all
\alpha\in(0,1/2)
,

\left|\sup_{0\leq s<t\leq 1}\frac{|B(t)-B(s)|}{|t-s|^{\alpha}}\right|_{p}<\infty.

Since
|t-\frac{1}{k}[kt]|\leq\frac{1}{k}
, it follows that

\Bigg|\sup_{0\leq t\leq 1}\Big|B(t)-B\Big(\tfrac{[kt]}{k}\Big)\Big|\Bigg|_{p}\leq k^{-(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p})}\bigg|\sup_{0\leq s<t\leq 1}\frac{|B(t)-B(s)|}{|t-s|^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}}}\bigg|_{p}\leq Ck^{-(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p})}.

Hence

\mathcal{W}_{p}(A_{k},W)=\mathcal{W}_{p}(\tilde{A}_{k},W)\leq Ck^{1/p}|\mathbb{E}_{\!\!}\left[\vphantom{X}\smash[t]{\widehat{X}}_{1}\otimes\vphantom{X}\smash[t]{\widehat{X}}_{1}\right]^{1/2}|.

The result follows by combining this bound with (
3.1
).
∎

3.2.
Consequences of the Functional Correlation Bound

Throughout this subsection, we fix

and

satisfies the Functional Correlation Bound with rate

. We first state a weak dependence lemma; in the proof of our main theorem we will use this lemma rather than applying the Functional Correlation Bound directly.

Let

G=(G_{1},\dots,G_{e}):M^{q}\to\mathbb{R}^{e}

Let

and consider
k

Consider
\mathbb{R}^{e}

When the gaps

Lemma 3.5
.

Let

. Then for all Lipschitz

\big|\mathbb{E}_{\mu\!\!}\left[F(X_{1},\dots,X_{k})\right]-\mathbb{E}_{\!\!}\left[F(\vphantom{X}\smash[t]{\widehat{X}}_{1},\dots,\vphantom{X}\smash[t]{\widehat{X}}_{k})\right]\big|\\
\leq C\sum_{r=0}^{k-2}(\ell_{r+1}-u_{r})^{-\gamma}\biggl(\left|F\right|_{\infty}+\mathrm{Lip}(F)\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{j=0}^{u_{i}-\ell_{i}}[\varPhi_{i}]_{\eta,j}\biggr),

where

is the constant that appears in the Functional Correlation Bound (
2.2
).

Proof.

This is proved for

in
[

, Lemma 4.1]
. The proof of the general case
follows the same lines.
∎

Let

.

The limit

4.
Proof of the main result

In this section, we prove Theorem
2.4
. The proof is based on Bernstein’s classical ‘big block-small block’ method. Let
0<b<a<1
. We split
\{0,\dots,n-1\}
into alternating big blocks of length
p=[n^{a}]
and small blocks of length
q=[n^{b}]
. Let
k
denote the number of big blocks, which is equal to the number of small blocks so
k=\big[\frac{n}{p+q}\big]
. The last remaining block has length at most
p+q
.

Recall that
T:M\to M
is a map satisfying the Functional Correlation Bound with rate
n^{-\gamma}
,
\gamma>1
, and that
v\in\mathcal{C}^{\eta}(M,\mathbb{R}^{d})
with mean zero.
For
n\geq 1
, recall that we denote
S_{n}=\sum_{r=0}^{n-1}v\circ T^{r}
. Let
1\leq i\leq k
and
t\in[0,1]
. We define

X_{i}=n^{-1/2}S_{p}\circ T^{(i-1)(p+q)}\quad\text{ and }\quad\vphantom{W}\smash[t]{\widetilde{W}}_{n}(t)=\sum_{i=1}^{[kt]}X_{i}.

Hence
X_{i}
is the sum of
n^{-1/2}v\circ T^{r}
over the
i
th big block. We also define

Y_{i}=n^{-1/2}S_{q}\circ T^{p+(i-1)(p+q)}\quad\text{ and }\quad R_{n}(t)=\sum_{i=1}^{[kt]}Y_{i}.

Thus
Y_{i}
is the sum of
n^{-1/2}v\circ T^{r}
over the
i
th small block.

Remark 4.1
.

In
[
4
, Section 7.8]
the ‘big block-small block’ method is used to prove the central limit under a hypothesis on decay of multiple correlations, where the Functional Correlation Bound (
2.2
) is
only assumed for functions
G:M^{q}\to\mathbb{R}
of the form
G(x_{0},\dots,x_{q-1})=\prod_{i=0}^{q-1}v_{i}(x_{i})
. Such a hypothesis is strong enough to control the characteristic function of
\widetilde{W}_{n}(1)
and hence prove the central limit theorem. Assuming the Functional Correlation Bound enables us to control
\mathbb{E}_{\mu\!\!}\left[F(\vphantom{W}\smash[t]{\widetilde{W}}_{n})\right]
for any Lipschitz function
F:D([0,1],\mathbb{R}^{d})\to\mathbb{R}
(see the proof of Lemma
4.4
).

Lemma 4.2
.

There is a constant
C>0
such that

\mathcal{W}_{2\gamma}(W_{n},\vphantom{W}\smash[t]{\widetilde{W}}_{n}+R_{n})\leq Cn^{(1-a)\frac{1-\gamma}{2\gamma}}
for all
n\geq 1
.

Proof.

Let
t\in[0,1]
. Since
\{0,\dots,[kt](p+q)-1\}
is the union of first
[kt]
big blocks and the first
[kt]
small blocks,

n^{-1/2}S_{[kt](p+q)}=\sum_{i=1}^{[kt]}(X_{i}+Y_{i})=\vphantom{W}\smash[t]{\widetilde{W}}_{n}(t)+R_{n}(t).

Now since
n\geq k(p+q)
, we have
[nt]\geq[kt(p+q)]\geq[kt](p+q)
. Let
h(t)=[nt]-[kt](p+q)
. Then

\displaystyle W_{n}(t)

\displaystyle=n^{-1/2}S_{[nt]}=n^{-1/2}S_{[kt](p+q)}+n^{-1/2}S_{h(t)}\circ T^{[kt](p+q)}

\displaystyle=\vphantom{W}\smash[t]{\widetilde{W}}_{n}(t)+R_{n}(t)+n^{-1/2}S_{h(t)}\circ T^{[kt](p+q)}.

Recall that
k=\big[\frac{n}{p+q}\big]
so
n-k(p+q)\leq p+q
. Hence

\displaystyle h(t)\leq nt-(kt-1)(p+q)=t(n-k(p+q))+p+q\leq 2(p+q).

It follows that

\displaystyle\Big|W_{n}(t)-\big(\vphantom{W}\smash[t]{\widetilde{W}}_{n}(t)+R_{n}(t)\big)\Big|=n^{-1/2}|S_{h(t)}|\circ T^{[kt](p+q)}\leq A\circ T^{[kt](p+q)},

where
A=n^{-1/2}\max_{j\leq 2(p+q)}|S_{j}|
. Hence

\displaystyle\left|\sup_{0\leq t\leq 1}\Big|W_{n}(t)-\big(\vphantom{W}\smash[t]{\widetilde{W}}_{n}(t)+R_{n}(t)\big)\Big|\right|^{2\gamma}_{2\gamma}

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}_{\mu\!\!}\left[\left|\sup_{0\leq t\leq 1}A\circ T^{[kt](p+q)}\right|^{2\gamma}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\mu\!\!}\left[\left|\max_{0\leq i\leq k}A\circ T^{i(p+q)}\right|^{2\gamma}\right].

Now by Proposition
3.7
, we have
\left|A\right|_{2\gamma}\ll n^{-1/2}(p+q)^{1/2}\ll n^{(a-1)/2}
. It follows that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\mu\!\!}\left[\left|\max_{0\leq i\leq k}A\circ T^{i(p+q)}\right|^{2\gamma}\right]

\displaystyle\ll\mathbb{E}_{\mu\!\!}\left[\sum_{i=0}^{k}|A\circ T^{i(p+q)}|^{2\gamma}\right]=(k+1)\mathbb{E}_{\mu\!\!}\left[|A|^{2\gamma}\right]

\displaystyle\ll n^{1-a}n^{\gamma(a-1)}=n^{(1-a)(1-\gamma)},

which completes the proof.
∎

Next we control the contribution of the small blocks.

Lemma 4.3
.

There is a constant
C>0
such that
\mathcal{W}_{2\gamma}(\vphantom{W}\smash[t]{\widetilde{W}}_{n}+R_{n},\vphantom{W}\smash[t]{\widetilde{W}}_{n})\leq Cn^{(b-a)/2}
for all
n\geq 1
.

Proof.

We proceed by bounding
\left|\sup_{0\leq t\leq 1}|R_{n}(t)|\right|_{2\gamma}
. By working componentwise, without loss of generality we may assume that
d=1
. For
m\geq 1
, define
A_{m}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}Y_{i}
. Then
A_{m}
is the sum of
n^{-1/2}v\circ T^{i}
over
m
disjoint blocks of length
q
so by Lemma
3.6
,
\left|A_{m}\right|_{2\gamma}\ll n^{-1/2}m^{1/2}q^{1/2}
. Since
Y_{i}
is a stationary sequence of mean zero random variables in
L^{2\gamma}
, by
[
29
, Proposition 1(ii)]
, we have that

\displaystyle\left|\max_{1\leq m\leq 2^{N}}|A_{m}|\right|_{2\gamma}\leq\sum_{j=0}^{N}2^{\frac{N-j}{2\gamma}}\left|A_{2^{j}}\right|_{2\gamma}.

for all
N\geq 1
. Thus

\displaystyle\left|\max_{1\leq m\leq 2^{N}}|A_{m}|\right|_{2\gamma}

\displaystyle\ll n^{-1/2}q^{1/2}\sum_{j=0}^{N}2^{\frac{N-j}{2\gamma}}2^{j/2}\ll n^{-1/2}q^{1/2}2^{N/2}.

By taking
N=\lceil\log_{2}(k)\rceil
, it follows that

\left|\sup_{0\leq t\leq 1}|R_{n}(t)|\right|_{2\gamma}=\left|\max_{1\leq m\leq k}|A_{m}|\right|_{2\gamma}\ll n^{-1/2}q^{1/2}k^{1/2}\ll n^{(b-a)/2}.\qed

Let
\vphantom{X}\smash[t]{\widehat{X}}_{1},\dots,\vphantom{X}\smash[t]{\widehat{X}}_{k}
be independent random vectors such that
\vphantom{X}\smash[t]{\widehat{X}}_{i}=_{d}X_{i}
, and define
\vphantom{W}\smash[t]{\widehat{W}}_{n}(t)=\sum_{i=1}^{[kt]}\vphantom{X}\smash[t]{\widehat{X}}_{i}
for
t\in[0,1]
.

Lemma 4.4
.

There is a constant
C>0
such that
\mathcal{W}_{1}(\vphantom{W}\smash[t]{\widetilde{W}}_{n},\vphantom{W}\smash[t]{\widehat{W}}_{n})\leq Cn^{3/2-a-b\gamma}
for all
n\geq 1
.

Proof.

By the Kantorovich-Rubinstein Theorem (
2.1
), it suffices to show that there is a constant
C>0
such that

|\mathbb{E}_{\mu\!\!}\left[G(\vphantom{W}\smash[t]{\widetilde{W}}_{n})\right]-\mathbb{E}_{\mu\!\!}\left[G(\vphantom{W}\smash[t]{\widehat{W}}_{n})\right]|\leq Cn^{3/2-a-b\gamma}\mathrm{Lip}(G)

for any Lipschitz function
G:{D([0,1],\mathbb{R}^{d})\to\mathbb{R}}
and
n\geq 1
.

We proceed by using Lemma
3.5
. First note that

X_{i}(x)=\varPhi_{i}(T^{\ell_{i}}x,\dots,T^{u_{i}}x)

where
\ell_{i}=(i-1)(p+q)
,
u_{i}=\ell_{i}+p-1
and

\varPhi_{i}(y_{0},\dots,y_{u_{i}-\ell_{i}})=n^{-1/2}\sum_{r=0}^{u_{i}-\ell_{i}}v(y_{r}).

Let
0\leq r\leq u_{i}-\ell_{i}
. Then
[\varPhi_{i}]_{\eta,r}\leq n^{-1/2}[v_{n}]_{\eta}.
Let
R=\max_{i}\left|\varPhi_{i}\right|_{\infty}\leq pn^{-1/2}\left|v_{n}\right|_{\infty}.
Define
\pi_{k}:B(0,R)^{k}\to D([0,1],\mathbb{R}^{d})
by

\pi_{k}(x_{1},\dots,x_{k})(t)=\sum_{i=1}^{[kt]}x_{i}.

for
t\in[0,1]
. Then
\vphantom{W}\smash[t]{\widetilde{W}}_{n}=\pi_{k}(X_{1},\dots,X_{k})
and
\vphantom{W}\smash[t]{\widehat{W}}_{n}=\pi_{k}(\vphantom{X}\smash[t]{\widehat{X}}_{1},\dots,\vphantom{X}\smash[t]{\widehat{X}}_{k})
.

Now for all
(x_{1},\dots,x_{k}),(x^{\prime}_{1},\dots,x^{\prime}_{k})\in B(0,R)^{k}
,

\sup_{t\in[0,1]}\Bigg|\sum_{i=1}^{[kt]}x_{i}-\sum_{i=1}^{[kt]}x^{\prime}_{i}\Bigg|\leq\sum_{i=1}^{k}|x_{i}-x_{i}^{\prime}|,

so
\mathrm{Lip}(\pi_{k})\leq 1
. Without loss of generality we may assume that
G(0)=0
, for otherwise we can consider
G^{\prime}=G-G(0)
. Thus

(4.1)

\left|G\circ\pi_{k}\right|_{\infty}\leq\mathrm{Lip}(G)\left|\pi_{k}\right|_{\infty}\leq kR\mathrm{Lip}(G)\ll n^{1/2}\mathrm{Lip}(G).

By applying Lemma
3.5
with
F=G\circ\pi_{k}
, we obtain that

|\mathbb{E}_{\mu_{n}\!\!}\left[G(\vphantom{W}\smash[t]{\widetilde{W}}_{n})\right]-\mathbb{E}_{\!\!}\left[G(\vphantom{W}\smash[t]{\widehat{W}}_{n})\right]|\leq A,
where

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle=C\sum_{r=1}^{k-1}(\ell_{r+1}-u_{r})^{-\gamma}\biggl(\left|G\circ\pi_{k}\right|_{\infty}+\mathrm{Lip}(G\circ\pi_{k})\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{j=0}^{u_{i}-\ell_{i}}[\varPhi_{i}]_{\eta,j}\biggr).

Note that
u_{i}-\ell_{i}=p-1
and
\ell_{i+1}-u_{i}=q+1\geq n^{b}
for
1\leq i\leq k
. Thus

\sum_{r=1}^{k-1}(\ell_{r+1}-u_{r})^{-\gamma}\leq kn^{-b\gamma}\ll n^{1-a-b\gamma}

and

\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{j=0}^{u_{i}-\ell_{i}}[\varPhi_{i}]_{\eta,j}\leq kpn^{-1/2}[v_{n}]_{\eta}\leq n^{1/2}[v_{n}]_{\eta}.

By combining these bounds with (
4.1
) it follows that

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle\ll n^{1-a-b\gamma}\biggl(\left|G\circ\pi_{k}\right|_{\infty}+\mathrm{Lip}(G\circ\pi_{k})n^{1/2}\biggr)

\displaystyle\ll n^{1-a-b\gamma}\biggl(\mathrm{Lip}(G)\,n^{1/2}+\mathrm{Lip}(G)\mathrm{Lip}(\pi_{k})n^{1/2}\biggr)\leq 2n^{3/2-a-b\gamma}\mathrm{Lip}(G).\qed

Let
V_{n}=k\mathbb{E}_{\!\!}\left[\vphantom{X}\smash[t]{\widehat{X}}_{1}\otimes\vphantom{X}\smash[t]{\widehat{X}}_{1}\right]
, so
V_{n}=\mathbb{E}_{\!\!}\left[\vphantom{W}\smash[t]{\widehat{W}}_{n}(1)\otimes\vphantom{W}\smash[t]{\widehat{W}}_{n}(1)\right]
is the covariance of
\vphantom{W}\smash[t]{\widehat{W}}_{n}(1)
. Before proceeding further, we need a bound on the difference between
V_{n}
and the limiting covariance
\Sigma
.

Lemma 4.5
.

There is a constant
C>0
such that for all
n\geq 1
,

|V_{n}-\Sigma|\leq C(n^{a(1-\gamma)}+n^{b-a}+n^{a-1}).

Proof.

Since
\vphantom{X}\smash[t]{\widehat{X}}_{1}=_{d}X_{1}
, we have

V_{n}=k\mathbb{E}_{\mu\!\!}\left[X_{1}\otimes X_{1}\right]=kn^{-1}\mathbb{E}_{\mu\!\!}\left[S_{p}\otimes S_{p}\right].
By Lemma
3.8
,

|p^{-1}\mathbb{E}_{\!\!}\left[S_{p}\otimes S_{p}\right]-\Sigma|\ll p^{-1}\log p+p^{1-\gamma}.

Since
kp\leq n
and
p=[n^{a}]
, it follows that

\big|V_{n}-\tfrac{kp}{n}\Sigma\big|=\tfrac{kp}{n}\big|p^{-1}\mathbb{E}_{\!\!}\left[S_{p}\otimes S_{p}\right]-\Sigma\big|\ll n^{-a}\log n+n^{a(1-\gamma)}.

Now
n-k(p+q)\leq p+q
so

\big|\big(\tfrac{kp}{n}-1\big)\Sigma\big|\leq\tfrac{1}{n}(kq+p+q)|\Sigma|\ll\tfrac{1}{n}(n^{1-a}n^{b}+n^{a})=n^{b-a}+n^{a-1}.

Hence

|V_{n}-\Sigma|\ll n^{-a}\log n+n^{a(1-\gamma)}+n^{b-a}+n^{a-1}\ll n^{a(1-\gamma)}+n^{b-a}+n^{a-1},

as required.
∎

Lemma 4.6
.

There is a constant
C>0
such that for all
n\geq 1
,

\mathcal{W}_{2\gamma}(\vphantom{W}\smash[t]{\widehat{W}}_{n},W)\leq C(n^{a(1-\gamma)/2}+n^{(b-a)/2}+n^{(1-a)\frac{1-\gamma}{2\gamma}}).

Proof.

We first consider the special cases where the covariance matrix
\Sigma
is positive definite and
\Sigma=0
.

Let
\Sigma
be positive definite. By Corollary
3.4
,

\mathcal{W}_{2\gamma}(\vphantom{W}\smash[t]{\widehat{W}}_{n},W^{(V_{n})})\leq Ck^{1/(2\gamma)}\Big(\big|\vphantom{X}\smash[t]{\widehat{X}}_{1}\big|_{2\gamma}\frac{\sigma_{\max}(V_{n})}{\sigma_{\min}(V_{n})}+|\mathbb{E}_{\!\!}\left[\vphantom{X}\smash[t]{\widehat{X}}_{1}\otimes\vphantom{X}\smash[t]{\widehat{X}}_{1}\right]^{1/2}|\Big),

where
W^{(V_{n})}
is a Brownian motion with covariance
V_{n}
. By Lemma
4.5
,
V_{n}\to\Sigma
. Since
\Sigma
is positive definite, it follows that
\sigma_{\max}(V_{n})/\sigma_{\min}(V_{n})\to\sigma_{\max}(\Sigma)/\sigma_{\min}(\Sigma)
. Moreover,
V_{n}^{1/2}\to\Sigma^{1/2}
so

|\mathbb{E}_{\!\!}\left[\vphantom{X}\smash[t]{\widehat{X}}_{1}\otimes\vphantom{X}\smash[t]{\widehat{X}}_{1}\right]^{1/2}|=k^{-1/2}|V_{n}^{1/2}|\ll k^{-1/2}.

Now
\vphantom{X}\smash[t]{\widehat{X}}_{1}=_{d}X_{1}=n^{-1/2}S_{p}
so by Proposition
3.7
, we have
\left|\vphantom{X}\smash[t]{\widehat{X}}_{1}\right|_{2\gamma}\ll n^{-1/2}p^{1/2}
.
Since
k=\big[\frac{n}{p+q}\big]\sim n/p\sim n^{1-a}
, it follows that

(4.2)

\displaystyle k^{1/(2\gamma)}\left|\vphantom{X}\smash[t]{\widehat{X}}_{1}\right|_{2\gamma}\ll n^{(1-a)/(2\gamma)}n^{-1/2+a/2}=n^{(1-a)\frac{1-\gamma}{2\gamma}}

and hence that

(4.3)

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{2\gamma}(\vphantom{W}\smash[t]{\widehat{W}}_{n},W^{(V_{n})})

\displaystyle\ll n^{(1-a)\frac{1-\gamma}{2\gamma}}+k^{\frac{1}{2\gamma}-\frac{1}{2}}\ll n^{(1-a)\frac{1-\gamma}{2\gamma}}.

Write
W^{(V_{n})}=V_{n}^{1/2}B
, where
B
is a standard Brownian motion. Let
W=\Sigma^{1/2}B
, so
W
is a Brownian motion with covariance
\Sigma
. Note that

|W^{(V_{n})}(t)-W(t)|=|(V^{1/2}_{n}-\Sigma^{1/2})B(t)|\leq|V^{1/2}_{n}-\Sigma^{1/2}||B(t)|.

Now since
\Sigma
is positive definite, by
[
15
, equation (7.2.13)]
,

\|V_{n}^{1/2}-\Sigma^{1/2}\|_{2}\leq\|\Sigma^{-1/2}\|_{2}\|V_{n}-\Sigma\|_{2},

where
\|\cdot\|_{2}
denotes the spectral norm. Hence by Lemma
4.5
,

\left|\sup_{0\leq t\leq 1}|W^{(V_{n})}(t)-W(t)|\right|_{2\gamma}\ll|V_{n}-\Sigma|\left|\sup_{0\leq t\leq 1}|B(t)|\right|_{2\gamma}\ll n^{a(1-\gamma)}+n^{b-a}+n^{a-1}.

By combining this bound with (
4.3
), it follows that

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{2\gamma}(\vphantom{W}\smash[t]{\widehat{W}}_{n},W)

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{W}_{2\gamma}(\vphantom{W}\smash[t]{\widehat{W}}_{n},W^{(V_{n})})+\mathcal{W}_{2\gamma}(W^{(V_{n})},W)

\displaystyle\ll n^{(1-a)\frac{1-\gamma}{2\gamma}}+n^{a(1-\gamma)}+n^{b-a}+n^{a-1}

(4.4)

\displaystyle\ll n^{(1-a)\frac{1-\gamma}{2\gamma}}+n^{a(1-\gamma)}+n^{b-a}.

Let
\Sigma=0
. Then by Lemma
4.5
,

k\mathbb{E}_{\!\!}\left[|\vphantom{X}\smash[t]{\widehat{X}}_{1}|^{2}\right]\leq k|\mathbb{E}_{\!\!}\left[\vphantom{X}\smash[t]{\widehat{X}}_{1}\otimes\vphantom{X}\smash[t]{\widehat{X}}_{1}\right]|\ll n^{a(1-\gamma)}+n^{b-a}+n^{a-1}.

Hence by Lemma
3.2
and (
4.2
),

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{2\gamma}(\vphantom{W}\smash[t]{\widehat{W}}_{n},0)

\displaystyle=\left|\max_{1\leq j\leq k}\Big|\sum_{i=1}^{j}\widehat{X}_{i}\Big|\right|_{2\gamma}\ll\biggl(\sum_{i=1}^{k}\mathbb{E}_{\!\!}\left[|\vphantom{X}\smash[t]{\widehat{X}}_{i}|^{2}\right]\biggr)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\biggl(\sum_{i=1}^{k}\mathbb{E}_{\!\!}\left[|\vphantom{X}\smash[t]{\widehat{X}}_{i}|^{2\gamma}\right]\biggr)^{\frac{1}{2\gamma}}

\displaystyle=(k\mathbb{E}_{\!\!}\left[|\vphantom{X}\smash[t]{\widehat{X}}_{1}|^{2}\right])^{\frac{1}{2}}+k^{\frac{1}{2\gamma}}\left|X_{1}\right|_{2\gamma}\ll n^{a(1-\gamma)/2}+n^{(b-a)/2}+n^{(a-1)/2}+n^{(1-a)\frac{1-\gamma}{2\gamma}}

(4.5)

\displaystyle\ll n^{a(1-\gamma)/2}+n^{(b-a)/2}+n^{(1-a)\frac{1-\gamma}{2\gamma}}.

Finally, we consider the case where
\Sigma
is an arbitrary symmetric positive semidefinite matrix.
Then we can write
\mathbb{R}^{d}
as a direct sum
\mathbb{R}^{d}=E\oplus F
such that
\Sigma
is nonsingular on
E
and vanishes along
F
. Let
\pi_{E}
denote the projection onto
E
. Then
W=\pi_{E}W
so combining (
4.5
) and (
4.4
) yields that

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{2\gamma}(\vphantom{W}\smash[t]{\widehat{W}}_{n},W)

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{W}_{2\gamma}(\vphantom{W}\smash[t]{\widehat{W}}_{n},\pi_{E}\vphantom{W}\smash[t]{\widehat{W}}_{n})+\mathcal{W}_{2\gamma}(\pi_{E}\vphantom{W}\smash[t]{\widehat{W}}_{n},\pi_{E}W)

\displaystyle\leq\left|\sup_{0\leq t\leq 1}\big|\vphantom{W}\smash[t]{\widehat{W}}_{n}(t)-\pi_{E}\vphantom{W}\smash[t]{\widehat{W}}_{n}(t)\big|\right|_{2\gamma}+\mathcal{W}_{2\gamma}(\pi_{E}\vphantom{W}\smash[t]{\widehat{W}}_{n},\pi_{E}W)

\displaystyle\ll n^{a(1-\gamma)/2}+n^{(b-a)/2}+n^{(1-a)\frac{1-\gamma}{2\gamma}}+n^{(1-a)\frac{1-\gamma}{2\gamma}}+n^{a(1-\gamma)}+n^{b-a}

\displaystyle\ll n^{a(1-\gamma)/2}+n^{(b-a)/2}+n^{(1-a)\frac{1-\gamma}{2\gamma}},

as required.
∎

We now have all the estimates required to prove our main theorem.

Proof of Theorem
2.4
.

By Lemmas
4.2
and
4.3
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{2\gamma}(W_{n},\vphantom{W}\smash[t]{\widetilde{W}}_{n})

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{W}_{2\gamma}(W_{n},\vphantom{W}\smash[t]{\widetilde{W}}_{n}+R_{n})+\mathcal{W}_{2\gamma}(\vphantom{W}\smash[t]{\widetilde{W}}_{n}+R_{n},\vphantom{W}\smash[t]{\widetilde{W}}_{n})

(4.6)

\displaystyle\ll n^{(1-a)\frac{1-\gamma}{2\gamma}}+n^{(b-a)/2}.

On the other hand, by Lemma
4.6
,

(4.7)

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{2\gamma}(\vphantom{W}\smash[t]{\widehat{W}}_{n},W)\ll n^{a(1-\gamma)/2}+n^{(b-a)/2}+n^{(1-a)\frac{1-\gamma}{2\gamma}}.

Now by Proposition
3.7
,
\mathcal{W}_{2\gamma}(W_{n},0)=O(1)
and hence by (
4.6
) we have that
\mathcal{W}_{2\gamma}(\vphantom{W}\smash[t]{\widetilde{W}}_{n},0)=O(1)
. Similarly, (
4.7
) implies that
\mathcal{W}_{2\gamma}(\vphantom{W}\smash[t]{\widehat{W}}_{n},0)=O(1)
. Hence by Lemma
4.4
and Lemma
3.1
, it follows that

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{r}(\vphantom{W}\smash[t]{\widetilde{W}}_{n},\vphantom{W}\smash[t]{\widehat{W}}_{n})\ll\mathcal{W}_{1}(\vphantom{W}\smash[t]{\widetilde{W}}_{n},\vphantom{W}\smash[t]{\widehat{W}}_{n})^{\frac{2\gamma-r}{r(2\gamma-1)}}\ll n^{\frac{2\gamma-r}{r(2\gamma-1)}(3/2-a-b\gamma)}.

By combining this with (
4.6
) and (
4.7
), we obtain that
\mathcal{W}_{r}(W_{n},W)\ll n^{-\kappa(\gamma,r)}
where

(4.8)

\displaystyle\kappa(\gamma,r)=\min\Big\{(1-a)\frac{\gamma-1}{2\gamma},\,\frac{(2\gamma-r)(a+b\gamma-\frac{3}{2})}{r(2\gamma-1)},\,\frac{a-b}{2},\,\frac{a(\gamma-1)}{2}\Big\}.

Recall that
0<b<a<1
are arbitrary. Finally, we show how to choose
a
and
b
in order to obtain the expression for
\kappa(\gamma,r)
in the statement of this theorem. We choose
a
and
b
such that

(1-a)\frac{\gamma-1}{2\gamma}=\frac{(2\gamma-r)(a+b\gamma-\frac{3}{2})}{r(2\gamma-1)}=\frac{a-b}{2}.

Note that
(1-a)\frac{\gamma-1}{2\gamma}=\frac{(2\gamma-r)(a+b\gamma-\frac{3}{2})}{r(2\gamma-1)}
if and only if

(4.9)

b=\frac{a(r(2\gamma+1)-4\gamma)+6\gamma-3r}{4\gamma^{2}-r}.

On the other hand,
(1-a)\frac{\gamma-1}{2\gamma}=\frac{a-b}{2}
if and only if

(4.10)

b=\frac{a(2\gamma-1)-\gamma+1}{\gamma}.

By equating (
4.9
) and (
4.10
) and solving for
a
we obtain that

a=\frac{4\gamma^{3}+2\gamma^{2}-4r\gamma+r}{8\gamma^{3}-r(2\gamma^{2}+3\gamma-1)}\quad\text{ and }\quad(1-a)\frac{\gamma-1}{2\gamma}=\frac{(2\gamma-r)(2\gamma-1)(\gamma-1)}{2(8\gamma^{3}-r(2\gamma^{2}+3\gamma-1))}{.}

Now since
r\leq 2\gamma
, we have that
2\gamma^{2}-4r\gamma\geq-2\gamma^{2}-2r\gamma
so

\displaystyle a\geq\frac{4\gamma^{3}-2\gamma^{2}-2r\gamma+r}{8\gamma^{3}-r(2\gamma^{2}+3\gamma-1)}=\frac{(2\gamma^{2}-r)(2\gamma-1)}{8\gamma^{3}-r(2\gamma^{2}+3\gamma-1)}\geq\frac{1-a}{\gamma}.

It follows that

\kappa(\gamma,r)=\min\Big\{(1-a)\frac{\gamma-1}{2\gamma},\frac{a(\gamma-1)}{2}\Big\}=(1-a)\frac{\gamma-1}{2\gamma}=\frac{(2\gamma-r)(2\gamma-1)(\gamma-1)}{2(8\gamma^{3}-r(2\gamma^{2}+3\gamma-1))}.

Since
\kappa(\gamma,r)>0
, by (
4.8
) we have that
0<b<a<1
, as required.
∎

Abstract
We study the local bihamiltonian structures of the asymmetric rational reductions of the 2D-Toda hierarchy (RR2T) of types ( 2 , 1 ) (2,1) and ( 1 , 2 ) (1,2) at the full-dispersive level, and construct a three-dimensional generalized Frobenius manifold with non-flat unity associated with the ( 2 , 1 ) (2,1) -type. Furthermore, we explicitly relate the ( 2 , 1 ) (2,1) -type RR2T to the bi-graded Toda and constrained KP hierarchies via linear reciprocal and Miura-type transformations.



Keywords
Rational reductions of 2D-Toda; Bihamiltonian structure; Central invariant; Generalized Frobenius manifold; Generalized Legendre transformation.

Introduction

The two-dimensional Toda equation

\left(\partial_{x}^{2}-\partial_{t}^{2}\right)x_{n}={\mathrm{e}}^{x_{n+1}}-2{\mathrm{e}}^{x_{n}}+{\mathrm{e}}^{x_{n-1}},

(1.1)

possesses interesting structures and properties
[
9
,
40
]
, and its commuting flows generate the famous
2D-Toda hierarchy

[
41
]
with a tri-Hamiltonian structure
[
8
]
. This hierarchy and its various reductions play important roles in the field of mathematical physics, such as orthogonal polynomials and random matrix theory
[
2
,
3
]
. Takasaki studied a reduction of the 2D Toda hierarchy at the full dispersive level, in which the Lax operator has the form
[
39
]

\mathbb{L}=\left(1+\beta_{1}\Lambda^{-1}+\cdots+\beta_{N}\Lambda^{-N}\right)^{-1}\Lambda^{1-N}\left(\Lambda^{N}+\alpha_{1}\Lambda^{N-1}+\cdots+\alpha_{N}\right)

(1.2)

for some
N\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
,
where the shift operator
\Lambda={\mathrm{e}}^{\varepsilon\partial_{x}}
satisfies
\Lambda y(x)=y(x+\varepsilon)
.
This decomposition characterizes the generalized Ablowitz–Ladik hierarchy viewed as a subsystem of the Toda hierarchy, and it has been shown to be an important integrable structure of topological string theory on
N
-th generalized conifold
[
39
]
.
Subsequently, Brini and his collaborators constructed a two-parameter family of the so-called
rational reductions of 2D-Toda hierarchy

(abbreviated as RR2T), and established their characterization in terms of block Toeplitz matrices for the
associated factorization problem
[
5
]
. The distinctive feature of the RR2T of type

(a,b)\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{2}
is that the corresponding Lax operator has the form

L_{(a,b)}=\left(1+\beta_{1}\Lambda^{-1}+\cdots+\beta_{b}\Lambda^{-b}\right)^{-1}\left(\Lambda^{a}+\alpha_{1}\Lambda^{a-1}+\cdots+\alpha_{a}\right).

(1.3)

They generalized the relation between the Ablowitz–Ladik hierarchy and Gromov–Witten theory by proving an analogous mirror theorem for the general rational reduction
[
5
]
, however their discussion was restricted to the dispersionless level.
Many well-known integrable hierarchies arise from further reductions of the RR2T, including the bi-graded Toda hierarchy
[
7
]
and the
q
-deformed KdV hierarchy
[
20
]
.

If the parameters
a
and
b
in (
1.3
) are equal,
the corresponding RR2T is called
symmetric
; otherwise, it is called
asymmetric
.
A typical example of the symmetric RR2T is the case
a=b=1
,
in which the corresponding integrable hierarchy is the Ablowitz–Ladik hierarchy
[
1
,
6
,
24
]
,
also known as the relativistic Toda hierarchy
[
23
,
35
]
.
As one of the most important differential-difference hierarchies,
it has been extensively studied
[
22
,
38
,
44
,
45
]
,
and was shown in
[
24
]
to admit a local tri-Hamiltonian structure.
The relation between a particular tau function of
this hierarchy and the generating function of Gromov–Witten invariants of local
\mathbb{CP}^{1}
was studied in
[
4
,
6
,
30
]
.
Moreover, Brini and his collaborators introduced a generalized Frobenius manifold
M_{\text{AL}}
with non-flat unity
corresponding to the Ablowitz–Ladik hierarchy in
[
6
]
.
Subsequently, motivated by this
M_{\text{AL}}
,
the Dubrovin–Zhang theory for the topological deformation of the Principal Hierarchy of the
class of generalized Frobenius manifolds with non-flat unity was established in
[
26
,
27
]
.
Afterwards, an extension of the Ablowitz–Ladik hierarchy was given and shown to be equivalent to the topological deformation of the Principal Hierarchy of
M_{\text{AL}}

[
29
]
.
Therefore, the Ablowitz–Ladik hierarchy has been formulated within the framework of Dubrovin–Zhang theory under the four-axiom setting

[
18
,
25
]
with special conditions.

In contrast to the symmetric case, in this paper we investigate two typical examples of asymmetric RR2T:
the RR2T of type
(2,1)
with Lax operator

\displaystyle L

\displaystyle=\left(1-w\Lambda^{-1}\right)^{-1}\left(\Lambda^{2}+u\Lambda+v\right),

(1.4)

and that of type
(1,2)
with Lax operator

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}

\displaystyle=\ \left(1+U\Lambda^{-1}+V\Lambda^{-2}\right)^{-1}\left(\Lambda+W\right).

(1.5)

We show that each of them admits a local bihamiltonian structure,
and that the two hierarchies, together with their corresponding bihamiltonian structures, are related by a certain Miura transformation and spatial reflection.
We also construct a generalized Frobenius manifold
M
with non-flat unity associated with the
(2,1)
-type RR2T,
and show that its dispersionless flows belong to the Principal Hierarchy of
M
.
Moreover, there are two generalized Legendre transformations
[
28
,
37
]

that relate
M
to the Frobenius manifolds corresponding to the bi-graded Toda and the constrained KP hierarchies.
These transformations motivate us to relate the RR2T of type
(2,1)
to the bi-graded Toda hierarchy and the constrained KP hierarchy
via certain linear reciprocal and Miura-type transformations.

The paper is organized as follows. In Sect. 2, we derive the bihamiltonian formalisms of the two hierarchies mentioned above,
and relate these two hierarchies via a certain Miura-type transformation up to spatial reflection.
Moreover, we also show that all the central invariants of the bihamiltonian structures of the
(2,1)
-type RR2T are equal to
\frac{1}{24}
.
In Sect. 3, we study the generalized Frobenius manifold
M
associated with the
(2,1)
-type RR2T.
In Sect. 4, we explicitly construct the linear reciprocal and Miura-type transformations that relate the RR2T of type
(2,1)
to the bi-graded Toda and the constrained KP hierarchies. In Sect. 5, we summarize the main conclusions and discuss possible directions for future research.

2.2
Bihamiltonian formalism of the RR2T of type
(2,1)

Now we are to derive the bihamiltonian formalism of the
(2,1)
-type RR2T.
Consider the following skew-symmetric
operator-valued matrix

\mathcal{P}_{1}=\begin{pmatrix}\Lambda-\Lambda^{-1}&w\Lambda^{-1}-\Lambda^{2}w&0\\
w\Lambda^{-2}-\Lambda w&w\Lambda^{-1}u-u\Lambda w&(\Lambda-1)w\\
0&w(1-\Lambda^{-1})&0\end{pmatrix},

(2.25)

we declare that this is a Hamiltonian operator.
In fact, consider the following new coordinates

\left\{\begin{aligned} z^{1}&=v+u^{-}w+w^{-}w,\\
z^{2}&=\frac{\sqrt{2}}{\Lambda+1}(u+w),\\
z^{3}&=\log w,\end{aligned}\right.

(2.26)

then the Jacobian
\mathcal{J}=(J^{\alpha}_{\beta})=\left(\sum\limits_{k\geq 0}\frac{\partial z^{\alpha}}{\partial u^{\beta,k}}\partial_{x}^{k}\right)

and its adjoint
\mathcal{J}^{\dagger}
have the form

\mathcal{J}=\begin{pmatrix}w\Lambda^{-1}&1&u^{-}+w^{-}+w\Lambda^{-1}\\[4.0pt]
\frac{\sqrt{2}}{\Lambda+1}&0&\frac{\sqrt{2}}{\Lambda+1}\\[4.0pt]
0&0&\frac{1}{w}\end{pmatrix},\quad\mathcal{J}^{\dagger}=\begin{pmatrix}\Lambda w&\frac{\sqrt{2}\Lambda}{\Lambda+1}&0\\[4.0pt]
1&0&0\\[4.0pt]
u^{-}+w^{-}+\Lambda w&\frac{\sqrt{2}\Lambda}{\Lambda+1}&\frac{1}{w}\end{pmatrix},

where
(u^{1},u^{2},u^{3})=(u,v,w)
are the old coordinates, and jet variables
u^{\beta,k}:=\partial_{x}^{k}u^{\beta}
.
Then it can be verified that

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}\mathcal{P}_{1}\mathcal{J}^{\dagger}

\displaystyle=\,\begin{pmatrix}0&0&\Lambda-1\\[4.0pt]
0&2\frac{\Lambda-1}{\Lambda+1}&0\\[4.0pt]
1-\Lambda^{-1}&0&0\end{pmatrix},

(2.27)

which is a constant Hamiltonian operator with respect to the new coordinates
(z^{1},z^{2},z^{3})

2.4
Bihamiltonian structures for the two asymmetric RR2T

To verify that
(\mathcal{P}_{1},\mathcal{P}_{2})
given by (
2.25
), (
2.44
) forms a bihamiltonian structure,
we recall the notion of the
Schouten bracket
defined on the space of local functionals of a super manifold
\hat{M}=\Pi(T^{*}M)
as the cotangent bundle of
M
with fiber’s parity reversed, where
M
is an
n
-dimensional smooth manifold.
See
[
25
,
31
,
33
]
for more details.

Fix a local trivialization
\hat{U}=U\times\mathbb{R}^{0|n}
of
\hat{M}
with local coordinates
u^{1},u^{2},\dots,u^{n}
on
U
,
and
\mathbb{R}^{0|n}
possesses supervariables (dual coordinates)
\theta_{1},\theta_{2},\cdots,\theta_{n}
.
Hence
\{u^{\alpha,s},\theta_{\alpha}^{s}|\alpha=1,2,\cdots,n,s\geq 0\}
constitutes a local coordinate system on the jet space
J^{\infty}(\hat{M})
.
A smooth function
f\in C^{\infty}(J^{\infty}(\hat{M}))
is called a differential polynomial if it depends on the jet variables

\{u^{\alpha,{s+1}},\theta^{s}_{\alpha}\}_{s\geq 0}
polynomially.
Denote the completion of the ring of differential polynomials by

\displaystyle\mathcal{\hat{A}}=C^{\infty}(U)[[u^{\alpha,s+1},\theta_{\alpha}^{s}|\alpha=1,2,\cdots,n,s\geq 0]],

and introduce the differential operator
\partial_{x}\in\Der(\hat{\mathcal{A}})
via

\partial_{x}=\sum_{s\geq 0}\left(u^{\alpha,s+1}\frac{\partial}{\partial u^{\alpha,s}}+\theta_{\alpha}^{s+1}\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta_{\alpha}^{s}}\right),

then it is natural to define the space of local functionals of
\hat{M}
as

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{F}}:=\hat{\mathcal{A}}/\partial_{x}\hat{\mathcal{A}}.

For a local functional
F=f+\partial_{x}\hat{\mathcal{A}}\in\hat{\mathcal{F}}
,
we always rewrite it as
\int f\td x
,
in other words,
\int\cdot\td x
is the canonical projection
\hat{\mathcal{A}}\to\hat{\mathcal{F}}
.
Moreover, we call
f\in\hat{\mathcal{A}}
the density of
F
.

There is a natural gradation, called
super gradation
, on
\mathcal{\hat{A}}
generated by

\displaystyle\deg\theta^{s}_{\alpha}=1,\quad\deg u^{\alpha,s}=\deg f=0

for
s\geq 0
and
f\in C^{\infty}(M)
,
we then denote
\hat{\mathcal{A}}^{p}
as the subspace of all homogeneous elements in
\hat{\mathcal{A}}
of super degree
p
,
and
\hat{\mathcal{F}}^{p}
as the image of
\hat{\mathcal{A}}^{p}
under the quotient map
\int\colon\hat{\mathcal{A}}\to\hat{\mathcal{F}}
.
The
Schouten bracket

[\ ,\ ]\colon\hat{\mathcal{F}}^{p}\times\hat{\mathcal{F}}^{q}\to\hat{\mathcal{F}}^{p+q-1}
is defined by

\displaystyle[F,G]=\int\left(\frac{\delta F}{\delta\theta_{\alpha}}\frac{\delta G}{\delta u^{\alpha}}+(-1)^{p}\frac{\delta F}{\delta u^{\alpha}}\frac{\delta G}{\delta\theta_{\alpha}}\right)\td x

(2.57)

for all
F\in\hat{\mathcal{F}}^{p}
and
G\in\hat{\mathcal{F}}^{q}
, where the variational derivatives

\frac{\delta}{\delta\theta_{\alpha}},\frac{\delta}{\delta u^{\alpha}}\colon\hat{\mathcal{F}}\to\hat{\mathcal{A}}
are defined by

\displaystyle\frac{\delta{F}}{\delta u^{\alpha}}=\sum_{s\geq 0}(-\partial_{x})^{s}\frac{\partial f}{\partial u^{\alpha,s}},\quad\frac{\delta{F}}{\delta\theta_{\alpha}}=\sum_{s\geq 0}(-\partial_{x})^{s}\frac{\partial f}{\partial\theta^{s}_{\alpha}}

for
F=\int f\td x\in\hat{\mathcal{F}}
.
For a skew-symmetric operator-valued matrix
\mathcal{Q}
of the form

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}=(\mathcal{Q}^{\alpha\beta})=\left(\sum_{s\geq 0}\mathcal{Q}_{s}^{\alpha\beta}\partial^{s}_{x}\right),

(2.58)

we introduce its corresponding local functional

\displaystyle\iota(\mathcal{Q}):=\frac{1}{2}\int\theta_{\alpha}\left(\mathcal{Q}^{\alpha\beta}\theta_{\beta}\right)\td x\in\hat{\mathcal{F}}^{2},

then
\mathcal{Q}
is a Hamiltonian structure if and only if

[\iota(\mathcal{Q}),\iota(\mathcal{Q})]=0
.

To apply the above theory to discrete integrable systems,
we always regard the shift operator
\Lambda
as formal series
{\mathrm{e}}^{\varepsilon\partial_{x}}=\sum_{k\geq 0}\frac{\varepsilon^{k}}{k!}\partial_{x}^{k}
.

\mathcal{P}_{1}
appeared in (
2.25

can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{1}

which is of the form (
2.58

.

\displaystyle=-u_{3}^{+}\theta_{1}^{+}-u_{1}u_{3}^{+}\theta_{2}^{+}-u_{3}\theta_{3}+u_{3}^{+}\theta_{3}^{+}+u_{1}^{-}u_{3}\theta_{2}^{-}+u_{3}\theta_{1}^{--},

\displaystyle\,\left(g^{\alpha\beta}_{a}\partial_{x}+\Gamma^{\alpha\beta}_{a;\gamma}u_{x}^{\gamma}\right)+\varepsilon\left(H^{\alpha\beta}_{a}\partial_{x}^{2}+\cdots\right)+\varepsilon^{2}\left(K^{\alpha\beta}_{a}\partial_{x}^{3}+\cdots\right)+O(\varepsilon^{3}),\quad a=1,2,

3.2
Principal Hierarchy

In this subsection, we are to construct the Principal Hierarchy of the generalized Frobenius manifold
M
(
3.8
) following the definitions of
[

In general, let
M(\eta,c,e,E)
be an
n
-dimensional generalized Frobenius manifold with non-flat unity, fix a family of flat coordinates
(z^{1},\ldots,z^{n})
. As it was introduced in
[
27
]
, the
Principal Hierarchy
of
M
is a family of evolutionary PDEs of the form

\frac{\partial z^{\alpha}}{\partial t^{i,k}}=\eta^{\alpha\beta}\partial_{x}\frac{\partial\theta_{i,k+1}}{\partial z^{\beta}},\quad(i,k)\in\mathcal{I}

(3.15)

about time variables
\mathbf{t}=\{t^{i,k}\}_{(i,k)\in\mathcal{I}}
, where the index set

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}=\left(\{1,2,\ldots,n\}\times\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\right)\cup\left(\{0\}\times\mathbb{Z}\right),

and the functions
\theta_{i,k}
on
M
satisfy the following initial conditions, recursion relations and quasi-homogeneity conditions

\theta_{\alpha,0}=\eta_{\alpha\beta}z^{\beta},\quad\nabla\theta_{0,0}=e,

(3.16)

\partial_{\alpha}\partial_{\beta}\theta_{i,k+1}=c_{\alpha\beta}^{\gamma}\partial_{\gamma}\theta_{i,k},\quad c_{\alpha\beta}^{\gamma}=\eta^{\gamma\lambda}c_{\lambda\alpha\beta},

(3.17)

\mathcal{L}_{E}\theta_{i,k}=\left(k+\mu_{i}+\mu_{0}+1\right)\theta_{i,k}+\sum_{s=1}^{k}\theta_{\alpha,k-s}\left(\widetilde{R}_{s}\right)_{i}^{\alpha}+\left(\widetilde{R}_{k+1}\right)_{i}^{n+1},

(3.18)

for
(i,k)\in\mathcal{I}
, here all the lower Greek indices are assumed to range over
\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
. We further recall that
\widetilde{\mu}_{i}
and
(\widetilde{R}_{s})^{i}_{j}
are entries of certain constant
(n+2)\times(n+2)
matrices

\widetilde{\mu}=\text{diag}\left(\mu_{0};\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{n};-\mu_{0}\right),\quad\widetilde{R}=\sum_{s\geq 1}\widetilde{R}_{s},

whose rows and columns are labeled by indices in
\{0;1,\ldots,n;n+1\}
. More details about the properties of
\widetilde{\mu},\widetilde{R}
can be found in Remark 5.2 of
[
27
]
and Remark 3.4 of
[
28
]

The flows of the Principal Hierarchy (
3.15
) can be rewritten as the following Hamiltonian system of hydrodynamic type

\frac{\partial z^{\alpha}}{\partial t^{i,k}}=(\mathcal{P}_{1}^{[0]})^{\alpha\beta}\frac{\delta H_{i,k}^{[0]}}{\delta z^{\beta}},\quad(i,k)\in\mathcal{I},

(3.19)

and satisfy the following bihamiltonian recursion relations

(\mathcal{P}_{2}^{[0]})^{\alpha\beta}\frac{\delta H_{i,k-1}^{[0]}}{\delta z^{\beta}}=\left(k+\mu_{i}+\frac{1}{2}\right)(\mathcal{P}_{1}^{[0]})^{\alpha\beta}\frac{\delta H_{i,k}^{[0]}}{\delta z^{\beta}}+\sum_{s=1}^{k}\left(\widetilde{R}_{s}\right)^{\gamma}_{i}(\mathcal{P}_{1}^{[0]})^{\alpha\beta}\frac{\delta H_{\gamma,k-s}^{[0]}}{\delta z^{\beta}},

(3.20)

where the compatible Hamiltonian operators
\{\mathcal{P}_{1}^{[0]},\mathcal{P}_{2}^{[0]}\}
have the form

(\mathcal{P}_{1}^{[0]})^{\alpha\beta}=\eta^{\alpha\beta}\partial_{x},\quad(\mathcal{P}_{2}^{[0]})^{\alpha\beta}=g^{\alpha\beta}\partial_{x}+\Gamma^{\alpha\beta}_{\gamma}z_{x}^{\gamma},

and the Hamiltonians

\displaystyle H_{i,k-1}^{[0]}=\int\theta_{i,k}(\mathbf{z}(x))\,\td x,\quad(i,k)\in\mathcal{I}.

Here
g^{\alpha\beta}=E^{\gamma}c_{\gamma}^{\alpha\beta}
and
\Gamma_{\gamma}^{\alpha\beta}=\left(\frac{1}{2}-\mu_{\beta}\right)c_{\gamma}^{\alpha\beta}
are the intersection form and the contravariant coefficients of the Levi-Civita connection of
(g^{\alpha\beta})

[
18

27
]

3.3
Generalized Legendre transformations

There are two specific generalized Legendre transformations that relate
M
(
3.8
) to the Frobenius manifolds associated with the bi-graded Toda hierarchy and the constrained KP hierarchy, respectively. To see this, we first review some preliminary concepts in
[
28
,
37
]
.

Let
M(\eta,c,e,E)
be an
n
-dimensional generalized Frobenius manifold. A vector field
B\in\mathrm{Vect}(M)
is called
Legendre
, if

X\cdot\nabla_{Y}B=Y\cdot\nabla_{X}B,\quad\forall X,Y\in\mathrm{Vect}(M),

where
\nabla
is the Levi-Civita connection with respect to
\eta
, and
\cdot=c
is the Frobenius multiplication. If
B
is an invertible Legendre field, i.e.,
B
is Legendre and
\exists B^{-1}\in\mathrm{Vect}(M)
such that
B\cdot B^{-1}=e
, then there exists a new flat metric
\hat{\eta}
on
M
such that

\hat{\eta}(X,Y)=\eta(B\cdot X,B\cdot Y),\quad\forall X,Y\in\mathrm{Vect}(M).

(3.40)

A Legendre field
B
is called
quasi-homogeneous
, if there exists
\mu_{B}\in\mathbb{C}
such that

\displaystyle[E,B]=\left(\mu_{B}-\frac{2-d}{2}\right)B,

where
d
is the charge of
M(\eta,c,e,E)
.

Suppose
B
is a quasi-homogeneous, invertible Legendre field on
M
, then
\hat{M}:=M(\hat{\eta},c,e,E)
is also a generalized Frobenius manifold, which is called the
generalized Legendre transformation
of
M
along
B
. Note that
\hat{M}
and
M
share the same Frobenius multiplication
c
(therefore the same unity
e
) and Euler field
E
. Moreover, the inversion
\hat{B}:=B^{-1}
is quasi-homogeneous Legendre field on
\hat{M}
, and transforms
\hat{M}
back to
M
, so we also say the pairs
(M,B)
and
(\hat{M},\hat{B})
are
Legendre dual
to each other.

Fix a family of flat coordinates
(z^{1},\ldots,z^{n})
on
M
, and a quasi-homogeneous, invertible Legendre field
B=B^{\alpha}\frac{\partial}{\partial z^{\alpha}}
, then we can find a family of flat coordinates
(\hat{z}^{1},\ldots,\hat{z}^{n})
with respect to the new metric
\hat{\eta}
, such that

\frac{\partial\hat{z}^{\alpha}}{\partial z^{\beta}}=B^{\gamma}c_{\beta\gamma}^{\alpha},\quad\eta\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial z^{\alpha}},\frac{\partial}{\partial z^{\beta}}\right)=\hat{\eta}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial\hat{z}^{\alpha}},\frac{\partial}{\partial\hat{z}^{\beta}}\right),

(3.41)

where
c_{\beta\gamma}^{\alpha}
is the coefficients of the Frobenius multiplication
c
with respect to
z^{\alpha}
-coordinates,
and the potential
F
and
\hat{F}
of
M
and
\hat{M}
are related by

\frac{\partial^{2}F}{\partial z^{\alpha}\partial z^{\beta}}=\frac{\partial^{2}\hat{F}}{\partial\hat{z}^{\alpha}\partial\hat{z}^{\beta}}.

(3.42)

If both the unity
e
and the Legendre field
B
are flat, i.e.
\nabla e=\nabla B=0
, then corresponding
generalized Legendre transformation coincide with the usual Legendre transformation
introduced by Dubrovin
[
15
]
.

Now we are to apply the above-mentioned generalized Legendre transformations to the generalized Frobenius manifold
M

(
3.8
) with non-flat unity
e
(
3.11
).

4
Linear reciprocal transformations

In Sect.
3.3
, we introduced two specific generalized Legendre transformations,
relating
M
(
3.8
) to the Frobenius manifolds corresponding to
the bi-graded Toda hierarchy and the constrained KP hierarchy respectively.
Therefore, from Theorem 3.13 and Theorem 4.6 of
[
28
]
,
the Principal Hierarchies of these generalized Frobenius manifolds
are related by certain linear reciprocal transformations
after adding certain families of flows generated by their Legendre vector fields,
and moreover, their topological deformations are also related by the same linear reciprocal transformations.

In this section,
we will construct two explicit linear reciprocal transformations together with Miura-type transformations that relate the RR2T of type
(2,1)

to the bi-graded Toda hierarchy and the constrained KP hierarchy respectively.
These facts provide evidence that the RR2T of type
(2,1)
belongs to the topological deformation of the Principal Hierarchy of
M
(
3.8
)
up to a certain Miura-type transformation.

4.1
Relation to the extended bi-graded Toda hierarchy

The RR2T of type
(2,1)
admits a discrete symmetry,
which is analogous to Theorem 3.1 of
[
29
]
.
Precisely speaking, applying the wave function transformation

\phi=(\Lambda-w^{+})\psi

to the spectral problem (
2.50
)
of the
(2,1)
-type RR2T, we obtain

\Lambda(\Lambda^{2}+u\Lambda+v)(\Lambda-w^{+})^{-1}\phi=\lambda\phi.

(4.1)

It can be verified directly that the following identity holds true:

\Lambda(\Lambda^{2}+u\Lambda+v)(\Lambda-w^{+})^{-1}=(1-w^{\oplus}\Lambda^{-1})^{-1}(\Lambda^{2}+u^{\oplus}\Lambda+v^{\oplus}),

where

\displaystyle u^{\oplus}

\displaystyle=\,u^{+}+w^{+++}-\frac{(v+uw^{+}+w^{+}w^{++})^{+}}{v+uw^{+}+w^{+}w^{++}}w^{+},

(4.2)

\displaystyle v^{\oplus}

\displaystyle=\,\frac{(v+uw^{+}+w^{+}w^{++})^{+}}{v+uw^{+}+w^{+}w^{++}}v,

(4.3)

\displaystyle w^{\oplus}

\displaystyle=\,\frac{(v+uw^{+}+w^{+}w^{++})^{+}}{v+uw^{+}+w^{+}w^{++}}w^{+}.

(4.4)

Therefore, there is a discrete symmetry
\hat{\Lambda}
of the
(2,1)
-type RR2T yielded by

(u,v,w)\mapsto(u^{\oplus},v^{\oplus},w^{\oplus}).

It is clear that (
4.2
)–(
4.4
) are also the compatibility conditions between the spectral problem
(
2.50
) and the discrete evolution problem

\hat{\Lambda}\psi=(\Lambda-w^{+})\psi.

(4.5)

Moreover, for any function
f
, we introduce the short notations

f^{\oplus}:=\hat{\Lambda}f,\quad f^{\ominus}:=\hat{\Lambda}^{-1}f,

(4.6)

and it can be verified that the actions of
\hat{\Lambda}^{-1}
on
u,v,w
have the form

\displaystyle u^{\ominus}

\displaystyle=\,u^{-}+w^{-}-\frac{v+u^{-}w+ww^{-}}{(v+u^{-}w+ww^{-})^{+}}w^{+},

(4.7)

\displaystyle v^{\ominus}

\displaystyle=\,\frac{(v+u^{-}w+ww^{-})^{-}}{v+u^{-}w+ww^{-}}v,

(4.8)

\displaystyle w^{\ominus}

\displaystyle=\,\frac{(v+u^{-}w+ww^{-})^{--}}{(v+u^{-}w+ww^{-})^{-}}w^{-}.

(4.9)

Remark 4.1
.

By comparing with (
3.26
), it can be verified that for each
f=u,v,w
,

f^{\oplus}=f+\varepsilon\frac{\partial f}{\partial t^{1,0}}+O(\varepsilon^{2}).

Hence, by the same argument as in the proof of Theorem 3.1 in
[
29
]
, we obtain

\hat{\Lambda}=\exp\left(\varepsilon\frac{\partial}{\partial t^{1,0}}\right),

(4.10)

where
\frac{\partial}{\partial t^{1,0}}
is the deformed flow mentioned in Remark
3.4
.
In other words, the discrete symmetry
\hat{\Lambda}
is generated by the
\frac{\partial}{\partial t^{1,0}}
-flow in the extended RR2T of
(2,1)
-type.

If we regard the above discrete symmetry
\hat{\Lambda}
as a new spatial shift operator,
then the extended RR2T of type
(2,1)
can be related to a three-component extended bi-graded Toda hierarchy
[
7
]

by a linear reciprocal transformation.
To see this, we rewrite the discrete evolution problem (
4.5
) as

\Lambda\psi=(\hat{\Lambda}+w^{+})\psi,

(4.11)

then by applying
\Lambda
to the above equation iteratively, we obtain

\displaystyle\Lambda^{2}\psi

\displaystyle=\,\Lambda(\hat{\Lambda}+w^{+})\psi=(\hat{\Lambda}+w^{++})(\hat{\Lambda}+w^{+})\psi

\displaystyle=\,\left(\hat{\Lambda}^{2}+(w^{+\oplus}+w^{++})\hat{\Lambda}+w^{+}w^{++}\right)\psi,

(4.12)

\displaystyle\Lambda^{3}\psi

\displaystyle=\,\left[\hat{\Lambda}^{3}+\left(w^{+\oplus\oplus}+w^{++\oplus}+w^{+++}\right)\hat{\Lambda}^{2}\right.

\displaystyle\quad\left.+\left((w^{++\oplus}+w^{+++})w^{+\oplus}+w^{++}w^{+++}\right)\hat{\Lambda}+w^{+}w^{++}w^{+++}\right]\psi.

(4.13)

On the other hand, the spectral problem (
2.50
) can be rewritten as

(\Lambda^{3}+u^{+}\Lambda^{2}+v^{+}\Lambda)\psi=\lambda\hat{\Lambda}\psi.

By substituting (
4.11
)–(
4.13
) into the above equation,
applying
\hat{\Lambda}^{-1}
and using (
4.7
)–(
4.9
),
we obtain

\left(\hat{\Lambda}^{2}+\hat{u}\hat{\Lambda}+\hat{v}+\hat{w}\hat{\Lambda}^{-1}\right)\psi=\lambda\psi,

(4.14)

where

\begin{cases}\hat{u}=\,w^{+\oplus}+u+w+w^{++},\\
\hat{v}=\,v+u^{-}w+uw^{+}+w^{-}w+ww^{+}+w^{+}w^{++},\\
\hat{w}=\,v^{-}w+u^{--}w^{-}w+w^{--}w^{-}w.\end{cases}

(4.15)

Note that (
4.14
) is the spectral problem of a three-component extended bi-graded Toda hierarchy
with Lax operator
\hat{L}
given by (
3.45
).

Based on the definition given in
[
7
]
,
the extended bi-graded Toda hierarchy corresponding to the Lax operator
\hat{L}
(
3.45
)
consists of the following flows for all
k\geq 0
:

\displaystyle\varepsilon\frac{\partial\hat{L}}{\partial\hat{t}^{1,k}}

\displaystyle=\,\frac{3}{2k!}\left[\left(\hat{L}^{k}(\log\hat{L}-H_{k})\right)_{+},\hat{L}\right],

(4.16)

\displaystyle\varepsilon\frac{\partial\hat{L}}{\partial\hat{t}^{2,k}}

\displaystyle=\,\frac{2^{k+\frac{1}{2}}}{(2k+1)!!}\left[\left(\hat{L}^{k+\frac{1}{2}}\right)_{+},\hat{L}\right],

(4.17)

\displaystyle\varepsilon\frac{\partial\hat{L}}{\partial\hat{t}^{3,k}}

\displaystyle=\,\frac{1}{(k+1)!}\left[\left(\hat{L}^{k+1}\right)_{+},\hat{L}\right],

(4.18)

where
H_{k}:=1+\frac{1}{2}+\cdots+\frac{1}{k}
for
k\geq 1
, and
H_{0}:=0
.
The logarithm of
\hat{L}
is defined by

\log\hat{L}=\frac{2}{3}\log_{+}\hat{L}+\frac{1}{3}\log_{-}\hat{L},

(4.19)

where the definitions of
\log_{\pm}\hat{L}
can be found in (16)–(17) of

[
7
]
, and are omitted here.

Remark 4.2
.

The choice of coefficients in (
4.16
)–(
4.19
)
differs from the original definition given in
[
7
]
,
in order to ensure that the dispersionless limit of this hierarchy coincides with the Principal Hierarchy of the Frobenius manifold
\hat{M}
(
3.44
)
with respect to the change of coordinates (
3.46
).

Applying the theory of generalized Legendre transformations, see Theorem 3.13 of
[
28
]
, to Example
3.1
,
we know that the following linear reciprocal transformation

\hat{t}^{1,p}=t^{1,p},\quad\hat{t}^{2,p}=t^{2,p},\quad\hat{t}^{3,p}=t^{3,p},\quad p\geq 0

(4.20)

transforms the
\{\frac{\partial}{\partial t^{1,p}},\frac{\partial}{\partial t^{2,p}},\frac{\partial}{\partial t^{3,p}}\}_{p\geq 0}
-flows in the Principal Hierarchy of
M
(
3.8
)
to the Principal Hierarchy of
\hat{M}
(
3.44
),
with respect to the change of coordinates (
3.43
).

Moreover, notice that the dispersionless limits of the extended
(2,1)
-type RR2T and the extended bi-graded Toda hierarchy
(

4.18

(

\hat{M}
(

respectively,
and then, from the arguments on the spectral problems (

For example, by using (

4.2
Relation to the constrained KP hierarchy

If we regard the time variable
t^{2,0}
in (
2.18
)–(
2.20
) as a new spatial variable
\tilde{x}
,
then the RR2T of type
(2,1)
can be related to the three-component constrained KP hierarchy
[

,
34
,

]

by a linear reciprocal transformation.
To see this, we note that the
\frac{\partial}{\partial t^{2,0}}
-flow (
2.18
)–(
2.20
) in the RR2T of type
(2,1)

is the compatibility condition between the spectral problem (
2.50
) and the following time-evolution problem

\varepsilon\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial t^{2,0}}=\sqrt{2}\,(\Lambda+b)\psi,\quad\text{where}\,\,b:=\frac{1}{1+\Lambda}(u+w).

(4.24)

Let us rewrite the above equation as

\Lambda\psi=\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{\sqrt{2}}\partial_{\tilde{x}}-b\right)\psi,\quad\tilde{x}:=t^{2,0},

(4.25)

then by applying
\Lambda
to this equation iteratively, we obtain

\displaystyle\Lambda^{2}\psi

\displaystyle=\,\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{\sqrt{2}}\partial_{\tilde{x}}-b^{+}\right)\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{\sqrt{2}}\partial_{\tilde{x}}-b\right)\psi

\displaystyle=\,\left[\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2}\partial_{\tilde{x}}^{2}-\frac{\varepsilon}{\sqrt{2}}\left(b+b^{+}\right)\partial_{\tilde{x}}+\left(bb^{+}-\frac{\varepsilon}{\sqrt{2}}b_{\tilde{x}}\right)\right]\psi,

(4.26)

\displaystyle\Lambda^{3}\psi

\displaystyle=\,\left[\frac{\varepsilon^{3}}{2\sqrt{2}}\partial_{\tilde{x}}^{3}-\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2}\left(b+b^{+}+b^{++}\right)\partial_{\tilde{x}}^{2}\right.

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{\varepsilon}{\sqrt{2}}\left(bb^{+}+bb^{++}+b^{+}b^{++}-\frac{\varepsilon}{\sqrt{2}}(b_{\tilde{x}}^{+}+2b_{\tilde{x}})\right)\partial_{\tilde{x}}

\displaystyle\qquad\left.-\left(bb^{+}b^{++}-\frac{\varepsilon}{\sqrt{2}}\left(b^{+}b_{\tilde{x}}+b^{++}b_{\tilde{x}}+bb^{+}_{\tilde{x}}\right)+\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2}b_{\tilde{x}\tilde{x}}\right)\right]\psi.

(4.27)

On the other hand, the spectral problem (
2.50
) can be rewritten as

\sqrt{2}\,\left(\Lambda^{3}+u^{+}\Lambda^{2}+v^{+}\Lambda\right)\psi=\sqrt{2}\,\lambda(\Lambda-w^{+})\psi.

Substituting (
4.25
)–(
4.27
) into the above equation
and using
b+b^{+}=u+w
,
we obtain

\left(\frac{\varepsilon^{3}}{2}\partial_{\tilde{x}}^{3}-\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2}\tilde{z}^{3}\partial_{\tilde{x}}^{2}+\varepsilon\tilde{z}^{2}\partial_{\tilde{x}}+\Big(\tilde{z}^{1}-\tilde{z}^{2}\tilde{z}^{3}+\varepsilon\tilde{z}^{2}_{\tilde{x}}\Big)\right)\psi=\lambda\left(\varepsilon\partial_{\tilde{x}}-\tilde{z}^{3}\right)\psi,

(4.28)

where the new functions
\tilde{z}^{1},\tilde{z}^{2},\tilde{z}^{3}
are given by

\displaystyle\tilde{z}^{1}

\displaystyle=\,\sqrt{2}\,w^{+}\Big(w^{+}(b+b^{+})+bb-b^{+}b^{+}+v^{+}\Big)

\displaystyle\qquad+\varepsilon\Big(2b^{+}b^{+}_{\tilde{x}}-2bb_{\tilde{x}}-(b+b^{+})w^{+}_{\tilde{x}}-2w^{+}(b+b^{+})_{\tilde{x}}-v^{+}_{\tilde{x}}\Big)

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{\sqrt{2}}\Big(b+b^{+}\Big)_{\tilde{x}\tilde{x}},

(4.29)

\displaystyle\tilde{z}^{2}

\displaystyle=\,\Big(w^{+}(b+b^{+})-b^{+}b^{+}+v^{+}\Big)-\frac{\varepsilon}{\sqrt{2}}\Big(b^{+}_{\tilde{x}}+2b_{\tilde{x}}\Big),

(4.30)

\displaystyle\tilde{z}^{3}

\displaystyle=\,\sqrt{2}\,\Big(b+w^{+}\Big).

(4.31)

Notice that (
4.28
) is equivalent to

\left(\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2}\partial_{\tilde{x}}^{2}+\tilde{z}^{2}+(\varepsilon\partial_{\tilde{x}}-\tilde{z}^{3})^{-1}\tilde{z}^{1}\right)\psi=\lambda\psi,

(4.32)

which is the spectral problem of the constrained KP hierarchy
[

]
with Lax operator
\tilde{L}
given by (
3.49

5
Conclusions

In this paper, we have derived the local bihamiltonian structures of the asymmetric RR2T of types
(1,2)
and
(2,1)
separately, constructed a three-dimensional generalized Frobenius manifold
M
, and shown that the dispersionless limits of the flows of the
(2,1)
-type RR2T belong to the Principal Hierarchy of
M
.
For the two hierarchies mentioned above and their corresponding bihamiltonian structures, we have established an equivalence relation via a certain Miura transformation and spatial reflection, and computed the central invariants of the associated bihamiltonian structures.

We also provide two explicit generalized Legendre transformations that relate
M
to the Frobenius manifold corresponding to the three-component bi-graded Toda hierarchy and the constrained KP hierarchy, and we investigate linear reciprocal transformations among these hierarchies.

In future work, we would like to present a general theory for the asymmetric RR2T of type
(1,n)
and
(n,1)
for general
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
,
and conjecture that the central invariants of the bihamiltonian structure of the

(n,1)
-type RR2T are all equal to
\frac{1}{24}
.
Furthermore, it would be interesting to study the Hamiltonian structures and properties of the symmetric RR2T of type
(n,n)
,
which represents another generalization of the Ablowitz–Ladik hierarchy distinct from (
1.2
).

Abstract
. Generalizing results from [ 29 , 32 ] we study the fine structure of locally minimal (locally) precompact Abelian groups (these are the locally essential subgroups G G of LCA groups L L , i.e., such that G G non-trivially meets all “small” closed subgroup of L L ). More precisely we prove that if G G is a dense locally minimal and sequentially closed subgroup of a LCA group L L , then the connected component c ⁡ ( G ) c(G) of G G has the same weight as c ⁡ ( L ) c(L) . Moreover, when w ⁡ ( c ⁡ ( G ) ) w(c(G)) is not Ulam measurable, then c ⁡ ( G ) = c ⁡ ( L ) c(G)=c(L) . We provide an extended discussion illustrating how this result fails in various ways in the non-abelian case (even for nilpotent groups of class 2). Motivated by the above result, we study further those locally minimal precompact Abelian groups G G , termed critical locally minimal , such that c ⁡ ( G ) = c ⁡ ( K ) c(G)=c(K) (where K K is the compact completion of G G ) and G / c ⁡ ( G ) G/c(G) is not locally minimal. Such a group cannot be compact, neither connected, nor totally disconnected. We provide a proper class of critical locally minimal groups with additional compactness-like properties and we study the class 𝒞 C ​ l ​ m \mathcal{C}_{Clm} of compact Abelian groups with a dense critical locally minimal subgroup. In particular, we completely describe the connected components of the finite-dimensional groups belonging to 𝒞 C ​ l ​ m \mathcal{C}_{Clm} .

1.2.
Main results

To find a correct counterpart of Theorem
1.1
for a larger class of groups naturally embracing the minimal ones we make recourse
to a class introduced by Morris and Pestov
[
41
]
with the motivation that the locally compact groups need not be minimal
(as a locally compact Abelian group is minimal precisely when it is compact
[
47
]
). They (and independently, Banakh
[
5
]
) introduced
the notion of locally minimal group as follows: a topological group

is

if there exists a neighbourhood

of the identity of

such that whenever
H
is a Hausdorff group and
f:G\to H
is a continuous isomorphism such that

is a neighbourhood of the identity in

is open.
It turned out that locally compact groups (and normed vector spaces) are locally minimal
[
41
]
. Further details on locally minimal groups can be found in
[
20
,
2
,
3
]

The relevant permanence properties of local minimality related to the passage to closed or dense subgroups were largely studied in these papers
as well as in
[

,
51
,
52
]
. In particular, local minimality is preserved by the passage to dense locally essential
subgroups (see also §

and Definition

for local essentiality).

We prove that the first part of Theorem
1.1
(up to and including “
c(G)=c(\widetilde{G})
”) holds even for locally minimal locally precompact sequentially complete Abelian groups.

Theorem A
.

Let
G
be a locally precompact, sequentially complete, locally minimal Abelian group. Then

(a)

w(c(G))=w(c(\widetilde{G}))
.

(b)

if
w(c(G))
is not Ulam-measurable, then
c(G)=c(\widetilde{G})
.

This theorem
(see §
4.1
for the proof) extends Theorem
1.1
not only in the direction of minimality
(which implies precompactness due to Prodanov-Stoyanov Theorem). Here the (implicit) precompactness in Theorem
1.1
is weakened to
local precompactness (which may be missing in the case of local minimality in general).

Corollary A
.

Let
G
be a (locally) precompact, sequentially complete, locally minimal Abelian group.

(a) If
w(c(G))
is not Ulam-measurable, then
c(G)=c(\widetilde{G})
is (locally) compact.

(b) If
G
is hereditarily disconnected
(
i.e.,
c(G)=\{0\})
, then so is
\widetilde{G}
; in particular,
\widetilde{G}
and

have linear topology.

Here

means that the group has a local base of open subgroups (see Corollary
4.5
for a proof of (b)).

The counterpart for local minimality of the missing (final) part of Theorem
1.1
(namely, “
G/c(G)
is locally minimal”) spectacularly fails
even for
precompact
(locally minimal, sequentially complete) Abelian groups. The second part of the paper (namely, §

remarkable phenomenon.

Quotients of locally minimal groups need not be locally minimal in general (see
[
42
]
for more details). In order to better pursue our plan we introduce a class of seemingly very exotic locally minimal groups, where the desired property fails in a strong way.
Namely, we are interested only in the preservation of local minimality under very special quotients of a given a locally minimal group

, namely those of the form

when

is compact! As we prove in Corollary

is additionally precompact, sequentially complete and

is not Ulam-measurable, then

is compact. So it is natural to impose compactness of

in the following notion:

.

Call a locally minimal group

Obviously, a critical locally minimal group can be neither connected nor totally disconnected, so

This explains why critical locally minimal Abelian groups are quite hard to come by even beyond the class of precompact locally minimal groups

We show that

2.1.
General properties of (locally) minimal groups

In the next definition we recall the well known concepts of (local) essentiality and total density of a subgroup. Moreover, in items (i) and (ii) we provide also weaker versions of essentiality.

Definition 2.1

For a topological group

In general it is hard to directly verify (local) essentiality since it involves

of non-trivial closed normal subgroups of

In the proof of Lemma

2.3.

We recall here three remarkable (functorial) subgroups attached to every topological group

•

,
[

, only in

]
a proof is given under stronger assumptions), we propose here a very brief sketch of a proof.

Indeed, we shall prove the stronger result:

Fact 2.23
.

If
G
is a minimal Abelian group and
C
is a connected compact subgroup of
\widetilde{G}
, then
G/(G\cap C)
is minimal iff
\overline{G\cap C}=C
.

Proof.

First we note that the hypothesis
\overline{G\cap C}=C
is equivalent to asking the algebraic and continuous embedding of
G/(G\cap C)
into
\widetilde{G}/C
to be also topological. This proves, among others, the implication
\Rightarrow
.

After this first step, to ensure the implication
\Leftarrow

one has to note that divisibility of
C
and the minimality of
G
ensure that
G/(G\cap C)
contains the socle of
\widetilde{G}/C

(see Claim
2
below) and
G/(G\cap C)
is weakly essential in
\widetilde{G}/C
(Lemma
2.3
). ∎

Claim 2
.

Let
p\in{\mathbb{P}}
,
K
be a compact Abelian group and
H
be a closed subgroup. Then for the canonical homomorphism
q:K\to K/H
one has
q(K[p])=(K/H)[p]
in each of the following cases:

(a)

H
is
p
-divisible;

(b)

H
splits algebraically;

(c)

p\notin\pi^{*}(H)
(i.e.,
td_{p}(H)=\{0\}
).

When
H
is connected, (a) and (b) are automatically satisfied for any
p
, so
q(\mathrm{Soc}(K))=\mathrm{Soc}(K/H)
.

Proof.

Obviously,
q(K[p])\leq(K/H)[p]
. For the reverse inclusion it suffices to check that
(K/H)[p]\leq q(K[p])
.

(a) Pick
x\in(K/H)[p]
. Hence,
px=0
, so if
x=y+H
with
y\in K
, one has
py\in H
. Since
H
is
p
-divisible,
py=pz
for some
z\in H
. Then
t=y-z\in K[p]
, as
pt=0
. Therefore,
x=q(y)=q(t+z)=q(t)\in q(K[p])
.

(b) In pure algebraic sense,
K\cong(K/H)\oplus H
, with
q
the projection onto its first factor.
So

q(K[p])=q((K/H)[p]\oplus H[p])=(K/H)[p].

(c) It suffices to see that if
H
satisfies (c), then
H
is
p
-divisible, so (a) applies. Indeed, if
p\notin\pi^{*}(H)\supseteq\pi^{*}(c(H))
, then

\pi^{*}(c(H))\neq{\mathbb{P}}
, so
H
is totally disconnected (by Fact
2.15
), hence
H=\prod_{p^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{P}}}td_{p^{\prime}}(H)
.
Since
td_{p}(H)=\{0\}
, only the components
td_{p^{\prime}}(H)
with
p^{\prime}\neq p
appear in the product and all they are
p
-divisible.
∎

Without any other additional condition on
G
, one cannot deduce anything on the quotient
G/c(G)
, since

c(G)
in general is (much) smaller than
G\cap C=o(G)
(see above). Of course, imposing
G\supseteq c(\widetilde{G})

one immediately has
G\cap C=c(G)
, so that the above fact can be applied.

The following obvious corollary of this fact appeared in the literature (e.g.,
[
29
, Theorem 3.6(a)]
):

Corollary 2.24
.

If
G
is a minimal Abelian group and
G\supseteq c(\widetilde{G})
, then

c(G)=c(\widetilde{G})
is compact and
G/c(G)
is minimal.

Here we give another, somewhat unexpected, corollary of the claim:

Corollary 2.25
.

If
K
is a compact Abelian group, then the subgroup
c(K)
of
K
is essential if and only if
c(K)=K
, i.e.,
K
is connected.

Proof.

Assume that
c(K)
is essential in
K
. Then
\mathrm{Soc}(K)\leq c(K)
. To the canonical homomorphism
q:K\to K/c(K)
apply the last assertion of Claim
2
. It gives
\mathrm{Soc}(K/c(K))=q(\mathrm{Soc}(K))=\{0\}
. Hence,
K/c(K)
is torsion-free. On the other hand, since
c(K)
is (weakly) essential in
K
by Lemma
2.3
, we conclude that
\{0\}
is weakly essential in
K/c(K)
, which means that
K/c(K)
has no infinite
p
-monothetic subgroups.
In conjunction with our previous conclusion that
K/c(K)
is torsion-free, this means that

is trivial. Therefore,

.
∎

3.
Approximating local minimality by minimality

In the initial part of this section we show that, roughly speaking, a locally minimal Abelian group has a “big” minimal (hence, precompact) subgroup.

Realizing that for a compact torsion-free Abelian group
G
weak essentialily and essentialily of its (dense) subgroups are equivalent
we obtain the following immediate corollary from Theorem
2.6
:

Corollary 3.1
.

If
G
is a compact torsion-free Abelian group and
H
a dense locally minimal subgroup of
G
, then
H
is minimal.

Call a subgroup
A
of an Abelian group
X

superfluous
if, for every subgroup
B
of
X
, the equality
A+B=X
implies that
B=X
. We denote this shortly by
A\leq_{s}X
.

Example 3.2
.

For every
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\leq_{s}{\mathbb{Q}}^{n}
, since
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}+B={\mathbb{Q}}^{n}
for
B\leq{\mathbb{Q}}^{n}
implies that
{\mathbb{Q}}^{n}/B
is both divisible and
finitely generated, so
{\mathbb{Q}}^{n}/B=\{0\}
and
B={\mathbb{Q}}^{n}
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
X
be an infinite Abelian group.

(a)

If
S\leq H
is a chain of subgroups of
X
with either
S\leq_{s}H
or
H\leq_{s}X
, then also
S\leq_{s}X
.

(b)

If
X
is torsion-free with
r(X)
infinite and
A\leq_{s}X
, then
r(A)
is finite.

(c)

A\leq_{s}X
if and only if the annihilator
A^{\bot}
of
A
is essential in
\widehat{X}
.

(d)

If
Z\leq_{s}X
, then
|X/Z|=|X|
.

Proof.

(a) Assume that
S\leq_{s}H
and
S+B=X
for some
B\leq X
. Then obviously
S+(B\cap H)=H
, so
B\cap H=H
, thus
S\leq H\leq B
. Therefore,
B=X
. The case when
H\leq_{s}X
is trivial.

(b) The divisible hull
D:=D(X)
of
X
is torsion-free of rank
\sigma=r(X)
infinite. Hence,
D
can be identified with
\bigoplus_{\sigma}\mathbb{Q}
. By (a),
A\leq_{s}D
. Assume for a contradiction that
A
has a countably infinite independent subset
C
. Fix an element
y_{0}\in C
and let
Y=\mathbb{Q}y_{0}
. We also fix a bijection
f:C\to Y
such that
f(y_{0})=y_{0}
. Since
C
is independent (over the filed
\mathbb{Q}
), there is a linear mapping
\widetilde{f}:D\to Y
extending
f
. Moreover, the restriction of
\widetilde{f}
to
Y
is the identity automorphism because
\widetilde{f}(y_{0})=f(y_{0})=y_{0}
. So,
D=Y\oplus Z
, where
Z=\ker\widetilde{f}
. Now any
x\in D
can be represented as
y+z
with
y\in Y
and
z\in Z
. Take
c\in C
such that
f(c)=y
, then
x-c\in Z
and hence
x\in c+Z\subseteq C+Z
. This is saying that
D=C+Z=A+Z
. But
Z
is a proper subgroup of
D
, this contradicts
A\leq_{s}D
.

(c) Assume that
A^{\bot}
is essential in
\widehat{X}
and
Z
is a proper subgroup of
X
. Then
Z^{\bot}\neq\{0\}
is a closed subgroup of
\widehat{X}
, so

A^{\bot}\cap Z^{\bot}\neq\{0\}
. Therefore,
(A+Z)^{\bot}=A^{\bot}\cap Z^{\bot}\neq\{0\}
, implying that
A+Z\neq X
.

Now assume that
A\leq_{s}X
. To prove that the closed subgroup
A^{\bot}
of
\widehat{X}
is essential pick a non-trivial closed subgroup
N
of
\widehat{X}
. Then
N^{\bot}
is a proper subgroup of
X
, hence
A+N^{\bot}\neq X
. Therefore,
\{0\}\neq(A+N^{\bot})^{\bot}=A^{\bot}\cap N
witnessing essentiality of
A^{\bot}
.

(d) Let
Y=X/Z
and assume for a contradiction that
|Y|<|X|
and let
q:X\to Y=X/Z
be the surjective canonical homomorphism with kernel
Z
.

Let us see first that
Y
cannot be finite. Indeed, assume that
Y
is finite and take a finite subset
F
of
X
such that
q
bijectively maps
F
onto
Y
, so
X=F+Z
. Then
X=B+Z
as well, where
B=\langle F\rangle
is the subgroup generated by
F
.
Hence,
B=X
, since
Z
is superfluous in
X
. Then
X
is finitely generated, so
X\cong{\mathbb{Z}}^{m}\times H
, for some
m\in{\mathbb{N}}_{+}
and some finite Abelian group
H
.

Take a prime number
p
with
p>|Y|
. Let us show that
F\subseteq pB+Z
. For any
x\in F
, there exists a natural number
n
dividing
|Y|
such that
nx\in Z
.
Since
p>|Y|
,
p
must be coprime with
n
, so there exist integers
u
and
v
such that
1=up+vn
. As
px\in pB
we deduce that

x=u(px)+v(nx)\in pF+Z\subseteq pB+Z,

implying that
F\subseteq pB+Z
, and consequently
X=B\subseteq pB+Z
. Since
Z
is superfluous, we deduce that
pB=X
. This proves that
X=B
is
p
-divisible, a contradiction in view of
X\cong{\mathbb{Z}}^{m}\times H
.

Let us see now that our assumption
|Y|<|X|
leads to contradiction also when
Y
is infinite. There exists a subgroup
A
of
X
such that
|A|=|Y|
and
q(A)=Y
, so
A+Z=X
. On the other hand, as
Z\leq_{s}X
,
|A|=|Y|<|X|
implies that
A
is proper, so
A+Z\neq X
, a contradiction.
∎

Proposition 3.4
.

If
M
is a closed subgroup of a compact Abelian group
K
, then
w(c(K))=w(c(M))
provided one of the following two conditions are satisfied:

(a)

M
is a
G_{\delta}
-subgroup of
K
with
c(M)\neq\{0\}
;

(b)

M
is a locally essential subgroup of
K
and
\dim K
is infinite.

Proof.

Let
C=c(K)
. In both (a) and (b) we can assume that
K=C
is connected, by replacing
M
with
M_{c}:=M\cap C
. Indeed,
c(M_{c})=c(M)
as
c(M)\leq M_{c}
. Moreover, in (a)
M_{c}
is a
G_{\delta}
-subgroup of
C
, while in (b)
M_{c}
is a locally essential subgroup of
C
and
\dim C=\dim K
is infinite.

(a) If
K
is metrizable, the conclusion
w(c(M))=w(K)
is immediate since
c(M)\neq\{0\}
. Hence, we assume
K
is not metrizable.

Let
X=\widehat{K}
, then it is torsion-free and
|X|=w(K)
is uncountable.
Since
M
is a
G_{\delta}
-subgroup of
K
,
K/M
is metrizable. Thus,
Y:=M^{\perp}\cong\widehat{K/M}
is countable.
Put
Z=X/Y
. We have
\widehat{M}\cong Z
and
c(M)\cong\widehat{Z/t(Z)}
. It suffices to show
|X|=|Z/t(Z)|
.
Let
q:X\to Z
be the canonical map. The saturation
\mathrm{sat}(Y)=q^{-1}(t(Z))
is countable since
X
is torsion-free and
Y
is countable.
Since
X/\mathrm{sat}(Y)\cong Z/t(Z)
and
X
is uncountable while
\mathrm{sat}(Y)
is countable, we conclude that
|X|=|Z/t(Z)|
.

(b) We first consider the case that
M
is essential in
K
. We are going to prove a stronger assertion, namely
\dim K/c(M)<\infty
, which implies
w(c(M))=\dim c(M)=\dim K=w(K)
, in view of (Y) and our assumption that
\dim K
is infinite.

Let
A=M^{\perp}
. By Lemma
3.3
(c),
A\leq_{s}\widehat{K}
since
M
is essential in
K
. As
\widehat{K}
is torsion-free,
r(A)
is finite by item (b) of the same lemma. This is equivalent to say that
\dim K/M
is finite. The desired inequality
\dim K/c(M)<\infty
follows from (Y) and the topological isomorphism

K/M\cong(K/c(M))/(M/c(M)),
where
M/c(M)
is zero-dimensional.

For the general locally essential case we take a neighbourhood
U
of
0
in
K
witnessing local essentiality of
M
and a co-NSS subgroup
N
of
K
, so
\dim K/N<\infty
.
Then,
w(c(N))=\dim N=\dim K
is infinite by (Y) and
M\cap N
is essential in
N
. It follows from the above argument applied to
M\cap N\leq N

that
w(c(M\cap N))=w(c(N))=w(K)
. Hence
w(c(M))\geq w(c(M\cap N))=w(K)
. Since the inequality
w(c(M))\leq w(K)
is obvious, we are done.
∎

Example 3.5
.

For
n\in{\mathbb{N}}_{+}
the group
K=\widehat{\mathbb{Q}}^{n}\cong\widehat{{\mathbb{Q}}^{n}}
is connected, and
M:=({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})^{\bot}
is an essential subgroup of
K

in view of Example
3.2
and Lemma
3.3
(c). Nevertheless,
w(K)>w(c(M))
, since

c(M)=\{0\}
. Hence, the second condition in item (b) of the above theorem cannot be relaxed.

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
G
be a locally minimal Abelian group. Then:

(a)

G
contains a closed minimal
G_{\delta}
-subgroup
G_{1}
;

(b)

if
G
has a linear topology, then
G_{1}
can be chosen to be open;

(c)

if
G
is precompact and non-metrizable, then
G_{1}
can be chosen with
w(G_{1})=w(G)
.

In particular, a linearly topologized Abelian group is locally minimal if and only if it contains on open minimal subgroup.

Proof.

Denote by
K
the completion of
G
, which is locally minimal, by Theorem
2.5
(i). In item (c)
K
will be compact.

(a) By
[
42
, Corollary 3.3]
, there exists an open neighbourhood
U
of 0 in
G
such that every closed subgroup
H
of
G
contained in
U
is minimal. In order to get the desired
G_{1}
let
U_{0}:=U\cap-U
and for each
n>0
choose an open symmetric neighbourhood
U_{n}
of 0 in
G
such that
\overline{U_{n}+U_{n}}\subseteq U_{n-1}
. Then
G_{1}=\bigcap U_{n}
is a closed
G_{\delta}
-subgroup of
G
contained in
U
, so
G_{1}
is minimal.

(b) This follows immediately by the proof of (a), since
U
can be chosen to be an open subgroup and
U_{n}
can be chosen to be
U
, so that
G_{1}=U
will be open.

(c) Assume that
G
is not metrizable, hence
K
is not metrizable as well. By Theorem
2.5
(i), there exists an open neighbourhood
V
of 0 in
K
witnessing local minimality of
G
. By Remark
2.9
, there exists a co-NSS subgroup
N
of
K
contained in
V
. Then
N
is a
G_{\delta}
-subgroup of
K
and
w(N)=w(K)
.
Moreover,
G_{1}:=G\cap N
is essential in
N
, by Remark
2.9
.
Then,
\overline{G_{1}}
, the closure of
G_{1}
in
K
, is also essential in
N
.
By Lemma
3.3
(c) and (d), we obtain that
w(\overline{G_{1}})=w(N)
.
Then we have

w(G_{1})=w(\overline{G_{1}})=w(N)=w(K)=w(G).

It remains to note that
G_{1}
is minimal since it is essential in
\overline{G_{1}}
.

The final assertion follows from (b) and the fact that a group with an open minimal subgroup is locally minimal
[
2
, Proposition 2.4]
.
∎

Remark 3.7
.

(i) The non-metrizability restraint in item (c) is necessary. There are infinite locally minimal subgroups
G
of
{\mathbb{T}}
(as
{\mathbb{Z}}(p^{\infty})
) such that the only minimal subgroups
G_{1}
of
G
are finite.

(ii) It is known that a group containing an open minimal subgroup is locally minimal
[
2
, Proposition 2.4]
. Item (b) of the above proposition shows that this sufficient condition becomes also necessary when the group in question has linear topology.

Here are two immediate applications of Proposition
3.6
, further applications will come in the sequel.
For an abstract Abelian group
G
we denote by
G^{\#}
the group
G
equipped with the Bohr topology, namely the topology generated
by all characters
G\to{\mathbb{T}}
.

Corollary 3.8
.

For an Abelian group
G
, the following are equivalent:

(a)

G^{\#}
is locally minimal;

(b)

G^{\#}
is compact;

(c)

G
is finite.

Proof.

The implications (c)
\Rightarrow
(b)
\Rightarrow
(a) are obvious. To prove that (a)
\Rightarrow
(c) assume that
G
is infinite. Then
G^{\#}
is a precompact group with
w(G^{\#})=2^{|G|}>\omega
(the equality is well known, see
[
15
]
).
Then by Proposition
3.6
(c),
G
contains a closed minimal subgroup
G_{1}
with
w(G_{1})=w(G)
. Since minimal groups satisfy
w(G_{1})\leq|G_{1}|
,
we deduce that
w(G)=w(G_{1})\leq|G_{1}|\leq|G|
, which contradicts the fact that
w(G^{\#})=2^{|G|}
.
∎

Locally compact groups are both complete and locally minimal. Now we see that for
Abelian groups this
implications can be inverted in some sense – the complete locally minimal Abelian group
G
contains a large compact subgroup:

Corollary 3.9
.

Every complete locally minimal Abelian group
G
contains a compact
G_{\delta}
-subgroup.

Proof.

Let
G
be a complete locally minimal Abelian group
G
. By Proposition
3.6
(a),
G
contains a closed minimal
G_{\delta}
-subgroup
G_{1}
.
Then
G_{1}
will be also complete, so compact by Prodanov-Stoyanov’s theorem.
∎

This corollary fails in the non-Abelian case. Indeed, it is shown in
[
16
, Example 4.3(b)]
that there exists a non-compact, locally compact group

whose arbitrary powers are minimal (and also complete). However, since every
G_{\delta}
subgroup of
G^{\omega_{1}}
contains a copy of
G^{\omega_{1}}
, it follows that
G^{\omega_{1}}
cannot have an (even locally) compact
G_{\delta}
-subgroup.

We conclude this section with a theorem giving some necessary and some sufficient conditions for local essentiality of subgroups
of a compact Abelian group
K
. To formulate one of them more conveniently in terms of socles, for subgroups
G
and
H
of
K
we introduce the set

\pi_{(H:G)}=\{p\in{\mathbb{P}}:H[p]\not\leq G\}.

Clearly,
\pi_{(H:G)}\subseteq\pi_{(H_{1}:G_{1})}
, whenever
H\leq H_{1}
and
G_{1}\leq G
.
In particular,
\emptyset=\pi_{(\{0\}:G)}\subseteq\pi_{(H:G)}\subseteq\pi_{(H:\{0\})}=\pi_{tor}(H)
.

Theorem 3.10
.

Let
K
be a compact Abelian group and
G
be a weakly essential subgroup of

.

(a)

If
G
is locally essential in
K
, then

(
*
)

for every

the subgroup

of

is open,

and there exits

for every prime

.

(b)

If
(*)

is locally essential in

.

(c)

If

is finite-dimensional, then the following conditions are equivalent:

)

is locally essential in

;

(c

)

for every

the subgroup

of

is open and

for every
\delta
-subgroup

of

.

(c

)

is locally essential in

for some (every)
\delta
-subgroup

of

.

In particular, when

is totally disconnected, then

is locally essential in

is open in

(a) Assume that

is locally essential in

, witnessed by an open neighbourhood

of 0. Then for every prime

is an open neighbourhood of 0 in

. Since

has linear topology,

contains an open subgroup

of

which is contained in

, as

.
In particular,

is open in

. Hence,

for some finite subgroup

of

Choose a co-NSS subgroup

is locally essential in

is essential in

Since

(b) Assume that

or not, we have

(c) As

, “some” and “every” are equivalent in (c

), and (c

Locally minimal sequentially complete Abelian groups

In this section we examine the locally minimal Abelian groups under the looking glass of sequential completeness. In §

4.1.
Locally minimal sequentially complete Abelian groups: proof of Theorem

Since a topological group
G
is sequentially complete precisely when
G
is sequentially closed in its completion
\widetilde{G}
,
countably compact groups as well as complete groups are sequentially complete, but sequential completeness is a much weaker property. While countable compactness imposes various restraints on the algebraic properties of the underlying group
G
when it is infinite (e.g.,

,

is either finite or

for every prime

[

), every infinite Abelian group

admits a non-discrete group topology which makes it sequentially complete, as the next example shows:

Example 4.1

Every topological group without non-trivial convergent sequences is sequentially complete (as every faithfully enumerated Cauchy sequence
(x_{n})
gives rise to a convergent sequence defined by
y_{n}=x_{n}x_{n+1}^{-1}
).

Therefore, every infinite Abelian group

carries a non-discrete sequentially complete group topology –
one can consider the group

which is known to have no non-trivial convergent sequences
[
35
]
.

Sequential completeness is preserved under taking direct products and sequentially closed subgroups. It is not preserved under taking continuous homomorphic images (as every discrete group is sequentially complete). Now we see that it is preserved under taking some quotients.

Lemma 4.2

Let

be a locally precompact sequentially complete group and

is sequentially complete.

Proof.

of Varopoulos, for every sequence

in

in

and

. Since

contains

and

.
By sequential completeness of

is sequentially closed in the locally compact group

is sequentially complete.
∎

p
be a prime number and
\tau
an uncountable cardinal. We set
K={\mathbb{Z}}(p^{2})^{\tau}
and consider the subgroup

H=\{f\in K:f(\alpha)\in{\mathbb{Z}}(p)~\mbox{~for~all~but~countably~many~}\alpha<\tau\}.

To see that
H
is
\omega
-bounded we note that for every countable subset
A
of
H
, there exists a countable subset
E
of
\tau
such that

A\subseteq P_{E}:=\{f\in K:f(\alpha)\in{\mathbb{Z}}(p)~\mbox{for~all~}\alpha\in\tau\setminus E\},

and
P_{E}
is a compact subgroup of
H
.
Moreover, it is evident that
H
is minimal by the minimality criterion because it contains
pK
and
pK
is essential in
K
.
Take
x\in K\setminus H
and let
G=H+\langle x\rangle
. Then
H
is a dense subgroup of
G
of index

.
We now refine the topology on
G
by letting
H
be open; this refinement is proper, so
G
is not minimal with this new topology.
Moreover, as the minimal group
H
is open in
G
,
G
is locally minimal and
G
is also
\omega
-bounded because
G
is homeomorphic to
H\times{\mathbb{Z}}(p)
.

For the stronger property “minimal” in place of “locally minimal”, the final conclusion of item (b) of Theorem
4.7
was proved without the restriction “totally disconnected” in
[
32
]
(it was proved that
G
contains a compact subgroup
N^{*}
such that
G/N^{*}
is locally minimal and a product of finite cyclic groups).
Let us see that a sequentially complete critical locally minimal Abelian group

fails to have this property. Indeed, if
G
contains such a compact subgroup
N^{*}
, then clearly
N^{*}
contains
c(G)
which must be compact.
Therefore, the quotient group
G/c(G)
contains a compact subgroup

such that
(G/c(G))/(N^{*}/c(G))\cong G/N^{*}
is locally minimal.
This would imply that
G/c(G)
is locally minimal contrary to our assumption that

is critical
[
16
]
.

The following proposition recovers and reinforces the conclusion of
[
32
, Corollary 2.11]
, whose proof, based on
[
32
, Proposition 2.10]
contains a gap at the very last stage. The new proof is simply a combination of Lemma
4.2
, Fact
2.23
and Corollary

.

Proposition 4.9
.

If
G
is a sequentially complete minimal Abelian group with compact

c(G)
, then
G/c(G)
is sequentially complete, minimal and has linear topology.

This proposition has an immediate corollary:

Corollary 4.10
.

A sequentially complete critical locally minimal Abelian group fails to be minimal.

Critical locally minimal Abelian group with even stronger properties (namely,
\omega
-bounded) will be built in the next section.

5.
Sequentially complete critical locally minimal Abelian groups

In this section we face the following question triggered by Proposition

:

Question 5.1
.

If
G
is a sequentially complete locally minimal Abelian group with
c(G)=c(\widetilde{G})
, is then also
G/c(G)
locally minimal?

In other words, reformulating the question in a counter-positive form: if

is a sequentially complete locally minimal precompact Abelian group with

can

be a critical locally minimal, i.e., is the class

of the completions of these groups non-empty? Proposition

below gives a negative answer to Question

and provides a proof of Theorem

.

5.1.
Sufficient conditions for
K\not\in\mathcal{C}_{Clm}

The next theorem collects various instances when the answer to Question
5.1
is positive.

Now we prove that if
K
is a compact Abelian group such that
C
is a hybrid torus or splits topologically, then
K\not\in\mathcal{C}_{Clm}
, i.e.,

K
contains no dense critical locally minimal groups. The same occurs also when
\overline{\mathrm{Soc}(K)}
is totally disconnected.

Theorem 5.2
.

If
K
is a compact Abelian group satisfying some of the following conditions (a)–(d), then
K\not\in\mathcal{C}_{Clm}
:

(a)

\overline{\mathrm{Soc}(K)}
is totally disconnected;

(b)

the set

\pi_{tor}(K)
is finite;

(c)

when
c(K)
splits topologically, in particular, when
c(K)
is a torus;

(d)

when
c(K)
is a hybrid torus.

Proof.

Let
q:K\to K/c(K)
be the canonical homomorphism and put
K_{1}:=\overline{\mathrm{Soc}(K/c(K))}
.

(a) In case
K_{1}
is totally disconnected, since
q(\mathrm{Soc}(K))=\mathrm{Soc}(K/c(K))
by Claim
2
,
\overline{\mathrm{Soc}(K/c(K))}=q(K_{1})
is totally disconnected as well.
Let
G
be a dense locally minimal subgroup of
K
with
c(G)=c(K)
. It suffices to prove that
G/c(K)
is locally minimal. By Claim
2
again,

(i) if
G
satisfies (
*
) from Theorem
3.10
(b) then also
q(G)
does so;

(ii) if
G
is weakly essential in
K
then also
q(G)
is weakly essential in
K/c(K)
.

Since
K_{1}
is totally disconnected, local essentiality of
G
in
K
implies that
G\cap K_{1}
is locally essential in
K_{1}
. Therefore,

\pi_{(K:G)}=\pi_{(K_{1}:G)}
is finite, by the final assertion of Theorem
3.10
(c). Since
\pi_{(K:G)}\supseteq\pi_{(q(K):q(G))}
, this implies that
\pi_{(q(K):q(G))}
is finite as well. Hence, Theorem
3.10
(b) implies that
q(G)
is locally essential in
K/c(K)
. By Theorem
2.5
,
q(G)
is locally minimal.

(b) Let
P=\pi_{tor}(K)
and
m=\prod_{p\in P}p
. In view of (a), it is enough to check that
K_{1}
is totally disconnected. Indeed,
Soc(K)=\bigoplus_{p\in P}K[p]=K[m]
is compact. Hence,
K_{1}=Soc(K)
has finite exponent, so it is totally disconnected.

(c) Assume that
c(K)
splits topologically. Then
K=c(K)\oplus D
for some necessarily totally disconnected closed subgroup
D
. As
G
contains the factor
c(K)
,
G=c(K)\oplus G_{1}
, where
G_{1}=G\cap D
is dense in
D
. So
G/c(G)
is topologically isomorphic to the closed subgroup
G_{1}
of
G
, which must be locally minimal.

(d) Put
C=c(K)
, then
\dim C<\infty
as
C
is a hybrid torus. Assume that
G
is a dense locally minimal subgroup of
K
with
c(G)=C
. We need to prove that
G/C
is locally minimal.

Fix a
\delta
-subgroup
H
of
K
. Then
\pi^{*}(H)
is finite, by Proposition
2.19
.
Let
q_{1}:K\to K/H
be the quotient homomorphism. Then
c(K/H)=q(C)=C/H
is a torus, so by Fact
2.13
the group
K/H
admits a totally disconnected closed subgroup
D^{\prime}
such that

K/H=D^{\prime}\oplus(C/H),

where the decomposition is topological. Since
q(G)
contains
C/H
, we may write
q(G)=E^{\prime}\oplus(C/H)
for some dense subgroup
E^{\prime}
of
D^{\prime}
.
Then
E:=q_{1}^{-1}(E^{\prime})
is a dense subgroup of
D:=q_{1}^{-1}(D^{\prime})
. Moreover,
K=D+C
and
G=E+C
.

The composition of isomorphisms

K/C\cong(K/H)/(C/H)\cong D^{\prime},

map
G/C
onto
E^{\prime}
, so we identify
K/C
with
D^{\prime}
and
G/C
with
E^{\prime}
in the sequel.

Since
G
is a dense locally minimal subgroup of
K
, it is locally essential, and in particular, weakly essential in
K
. By Lemma
2.3
, the group
E^{\prime}
is weakly essential in
D^{\prime}\cong K/C
. In view of Theorem
2.5
, to prove that
G/C=E^{\prime}
is locally minimal, it suffices to check that
E^{\prime}

is locally essential in
D^{\prime}
. By Theorem
3.10
(b) it is enough to verify the following:

(i)

The set
Q^{\prime}:=\pi_{(D^{\prime}:E^{\prime})}
is finite;

(ii)

For each prime
p
, the subgroup
D^{\prime}[p]\cap E^{\prime}
is open in
D^{\prime}[p]
.

Since
E
is a closed subgroup of the locally minimal group
G
, it is itself locally minimal, and thus locally essential in
D
. By Theorem
3.10
(c), the set
Q:=\pi_{(D:E)}
is finite, as the compact group
D
is totally disconnected.

To prove (i), it suffices to prove that
Q^{\prime}\subseteq\pi^{*}(H)\cup Q
, in other words, if
p\notin\pi^{*}(H)\cup Q
, then
p\notin Q^{\prime}
. By Claim
2
(c) applied to
D\to D^{\prime}=D/H
and our hypothesis

we deduce that
q(D[p])=D^{\prime}[p]
. The part
p\notin Q
of our hypothesis gives
D[p]\leq E
.
Therefore,
D^{\prime}[p]=q(D[p])\leq q(E)=E^{\prime}
, i.e.,
p\notin Q^{\prime}
. This finishes the proof of (i).

To verify (ii), consider the quotient map
\varphi:K\to D^{\prime}
with kernel
C
.

By Claim
2
, we have
D^{\prime}[p]=\varphi(K[p])
for any prime
p
. Since
G
contains an open subgroup of
K[p]
, it follows that
E^{\prime}=\varphi(G)
contains an open subgroup of
D^{\prime}[p]
. Therefore,
D^{\prime}[p]\cap E^{\prime}
is open in
D^{\prime}[p]
.
∎

Corollary 5.3
.

If
G
is a critical locally minimal Abelian group such that
c(G)
is finite-dimensional, then
\pi^{*}(N)
is infinite for every
\delta
-subgroup
N
of
c(G)
.

Proof.

Let
K
be the completion of
G
and let
N
be
\delta
-subgroup of
C:=c(K)=c(G)
. By Proposition
2.19
,

\pi^{*}(N)
must be infinite, as

cannot be a hybrid torus by the above theorem (as far as

contains the dense critical locally minimal subgroup

).
∎

5.2.
Sufficient conditions for
K\in\mathcal{C}_{Clm}

Here we provide a counterpart of Theorem
5.2
, giving sufficient conditions for
K\in\mathcal{C}_{Clm}
, when
K
is a compact Abelian group.

Definition 5.4
.

Denote by
\mathcal{K}
the class of compact Abelian groups
K
with locally essential
c(K)
.

In order to provide sufficient conditions for
K\in\mathcal{C}_{Clm}
, we first provide sufficient conditions for
K\in\mathcal{K}
and then we characterize in Theorem
5.8
the groups
K\in\mathcal{K}
that belong to
\mathcal{C}_{Clm}
.

We saw in Corollary
2.25

that
c(K)
cannot be essential in
K
unless
c(K)=K
. While obviously
K\in\mathcal{K}
whenever
c(K)
is open in
K
(in such a case

splits topologically; it is easy to see that whenever

splits topologically in

,

precisely when

is open). We shall see now
that
\mathcal{K}
contains a wealth of non-connected groups.

Lemma 5.5
.

If
K\in\mathcal{K}
with
C=c(K)
, then:

(a)

there exists
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that for every
p\in{\mathbb{P}}
,
td_{p}(K/C)
is a finite
p
-group with
r_{p}(td_{p}(K/C))\leq n
;

(b)

K/C
is metrizable and topologically generated by
n
elements;

(c)

if
w(K)\leq\mathfrak{c}
, then also
K
is topologically finitely generated.

Proof.

(a) Since
C
is locally essential in
K
, it follows from Lemma
2.3
that the trivial group is weakly essential in
K/C
, hence
K/C
is a totally disconnected exotic torus.
By Theorem
2.16
, each
td_{p}(K/C)
is a bounded
p
-group. To evaluate
r_{p}(K/C)
use the canonical homomorphism
q:K\to K/C
.
Since
C
is connected, Claim
2
gives

(3)

(K/C)[p]=q(K[p])\cong K[p]/C[p],

for every
p\in{\mathbb{P}}
. By Theorem
3.10
(a), there exists
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that
(in view of (
3
))
r_{p}(K/C)=r_{p}(K[p]/C[p])\leq n
for each
p\in{\mathbb{P}}
.

(b) Since
td_{p}(K/C)
is a finite
p
-group of
p
-rank
\leq n
for each
p\in{\mathbb{P}}
,
td_{p}(K/C)
is a quotient of
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{n}
. Therefore,
K/C
is a quotient of
(\prod_{p\in{\mathbb{P}}}{\mathbb{Z}}_{p})^{n}
, so metrizable.
Since
\prod_{p\in{\mathbb{P}}}{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
is monothetic,
K/C
is topologically generated by
n
elements.

(c) Since
C
is connected and

,

is monothetic (see
[
4

).
As

is monothetic and

is topologically finitely generated, it follows that

is also topologically finitely generated.
∎

Remark 5.6
.

The above implication cannot be inverted as easy examples show (e.g., for any connected
C
, and

,
the group

is even monothetic, but

).

If

is finite-dimensional one can find a stronger property of the pair

that characterizes

. Namely, if

is a
\delta
-subgroup of

, then

is a

-subgroup of

, so for every prime

.

Theorem 5.7
.

For a compact Abelian group

(a)

;

(b)

-subgroup

(c)

there exists a
\delta
-subgroup

of

of

(c

)

(c

)

the quotient group

, where
\Gamma_{j}
is the graph of

Proof.

(a)
\Rightarrow
(b). According to Fact
2.8
(b)),
K
admits a
\delta
-subgroup

. Since

is locally essential in

(b)
\Rightarrow
(c). Let

\delta
-subgroup

of

Since

. By Fact

(g

-subgroup of

From the equality

It follows that

.
Let

Then the kernel of

Hence, we have

(c)
\Rightarrow
(a). The subgroup

in (c

. To check that

By our hypothesis,

.
Indeed,

Theorem 5.8
.

Let
K\in\mathcal{K}
and

. Then the following are equivalent:

(a)

;

(b)

\pi^{*}(K/C)
is infinite;

(c)

K/C
is infinite.

Proof.

By Lemma
5.5
(a),

is finite for every prime

. Therefore, conditions (b) and (c) are equivalent.

The implication (a)
\Rightarrow
(c) is trivial, since otherwise

would be open in
K
, and hence every dense subgroup
G

containing

is open (hence, closed) as well, so

. As

, the quotient

is finite and hence locally minimal.
This contradicts the assumption that

.

To see (b)
\Rightarrow
(a), we take a finitely generated dense subgroup

of
K/C
, which exists by Lemma

. Then the subgroup

of

, where

is the quotient homomorphism, is dense in

and contains

. As

is locally essential in

, so is

, and therefore

is locally minimal.
But

is not locally essential in

because as a finitely generated group, the subgroup

is finite, while every neighbourhood of

contains infinitely many
elements of

because

is a product of infinitely many cyclic groups.
∎

We will build a finite-dimensional (
\omega
-bounded) critical locally minimal precompact Abelian group

such that

can be any connected finite-dimensional compact Abelian group that is not a hybrid torus. From a different point of view, the next proposition can be considered as a partial inverse of Theorem

(d).

Proposition 5.9
.

Let
C
be a finite-dimensional compact connected Abelian group that is not a hybrid torus. Then there exists a compact Abelian group
K
with
c(K)\cong C
(so
\dim K
is finite) containing a dense critical locally minimal subgroup
G
. Moreover, the group
G
can be chosen to be:

(a)

metrizable; or

(b)

\omega
-bounded and of weight
\kappa
, for an arbitrarily assigned uncountable cardinal
\kappa
.

Proof.

Since
n:=\dim C<\infty
, the group
C
is metrizable, so
C
has a
\delta
-subgroup
H
such that
C/H\cong{\mathbb{T}}^{n}
and
P:=\pi^{*}(H)
is infinite, according to Fact
2.10
and our hypothesis.

(a) By Fact
2.11
(h), we have
H\cong\prod_{p\in P}H_{p}
, where each
H_{p}\cong{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{k_{p}}\times F_{p}
with
F_{p}
a finite
p
-group and
0\leq k_{p}\leq n
.
Thus, for each
p\in P
, the group
H_{p}
admits a topological direct decomposition of the form
H_{p}=H_{p}^{\prime}\oplus H_{p}^{\prime\prime}
, where
H_{p}^{\prime}
is topologically isomorphic either to
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
or to a finite cyclic
p
-group. We then write
H
as
H^{\prime}\oplus H^{\prime\prime}
with
H^{\prime}\cong\prod_{p\in P}H_{p}^{\prime}
and
H^{\prime\prime}\cong\prod_{p\in P}H_{p}^{\prime\prime}
.

Now define the group
N:=\prod_{p\in P}N_{p},
where each
N_{p}
is isomorphic to
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
if
H_{p}^{\prime}\cong{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
, or
N_{p}\cong{\mathbb{Z}}(p^{m+1})
,
if
H_{p}^{\prime}
is cyclic of order
p^{m}
. Then
N
is an infinite monothetic group. Let
g\in N
be an element such that
\langle g\rangle
is dense in
N
.
Set
L:=\prod_{p\in P}pN_{p}
, and fix a topological isomorphism
j:L\to H^{\prime}
.

The graph
\Gamma_{j}=\{(x,j(x)):x\in L\}
of

is a closed subgroup of

since

is continuous. Let

, with
\phi:N\times C\to K
the canonical quotient homomorphism.
Then
\phi(\{0\}\times C)=\phi(L\times C)
coincides with

. Clearly,
K
is metrizable, as
C
and
N
are metrizable.

In order to show that
K\in\mathcal{K}
we check first that
N^{*}:=\phi(N\times H^{\prime\prime})\cong N\times H^{\prime\prime}
is a
\delta
-subgroup of
K
. Indeed,
N^{*}
is totally disconnected and

H={\mathbb{T}}\times H_{1}
, where
H_{1}
is the
\Sigma
-product in
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{\mathfrak{c}}
, so that
H/c(H)\cong H_{1}
is not locally minimal since it is not minimal, according to Corollary
3.1
). Then
c(G_{H})=c(\widetilde{G}_{H})
, yet
G_{H}/c(G_{H})\cong H/c(H)\times\widehat{X}
is not even
locally minimal
, as the non-locally minimal group
H/c(H)
is isomorphic to an open subgroup of
G_{H}/c(G_{H})
.

The minimal group
G_{H}
we built in the above example is precompact if and only if it is finite.
Hence, to show that
Corollary
4.3
strongly fails in the non-Abelian case we need another example:

Example 6.5
.

Let
L
be a compact connected simple Lie group (e.g.,
L=\mathrm{SO}_{3}({\mathbb{R}})
will do). Then in the compact connected group
K=L^{\omega_{1}}
the
\Sigma
-product
G
is an
\omega
-bounded (hence, sequentially complete) totally minimal connected non-compact group. (The total minimality of
G
is due to Theorem
2.5
(ii) and the fact that the only closed normal subgroups
N
of
K
are the products
N=L^{I}
, for some
I\subseteq\omega_{1}
, so
N\cap G
is always dense in
N
.)

The counterexamples to Theorem
A

and Corollary
4.3
given above have separately the properties of being nilpotent and precompact.
So the question arises of whether one can produce a counterexample that is both nilpotent and precompact. Let us see that this is not possible
as far as item (a) of the theorem is concerned.

For the proof we need the following (probably known) fact, we include a proof here for convenience of the reader.

Fact 6.6
.

Let
G
be a nilpotent compact group. Then
c(G)\leq Z(G)
.

Proof.

Assume first that
G
is a Lie group. Then, by a well-known theorem,
G
admits a finite subgroup
E
such that
G=c(G)E
.
Let
A=c(G)
; then
A
is nilpotent and hence Abelian, so
A\cong{\mathbb{T}}^{m}
for some
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
.
Choose a prime
p>|E|
.
Then the finite
p
-torsion subgroup
A[p]
of
A
is a characteristic subgroup of the normal subgroup
A

of
G
. Hence,
A[p]
is a normal subgroup of
G
.
Since every finite nilpotent group is isomorphic to the direct product of its Sylow subgroups and
p
is co-prime to
|E|
,

A[p]
is centralized by
E
. Hence
A[p]\subseteq Z(G)
.

As the collection of all such
A[p]
generates a dense subgroup of
A
, it follows that
A\subseteq Z(G)
.
Therefore,
c(G)\leq Z(G)
.

In the general case fix an arbitrary closed normal subgroup
N
of
G
such that
G/N
is a Lie group, and denote by
\pi:G\to G/N
the natural quotient homomorphism.
Then for any
c\in c(G)
and
g\in G
, the first part of the proof implies that
[\pi(c),\pi(g)]=e
in
G/N
, i.e.,
[c,g]=cgc^{-1}g^{-1}\in N
.

Since the intersection of all such
N
is trivial, it follows that
[c,g]=e
for all
g\in G
, and thus
c(G)\leq Z(G)
.
∎

We do not know the answer of the following

Question 6.7
.

Does Corollary
4.5
remain true in the nilpotent case, with “locally precompact” replaced by “precompact”?

A positive answer to this question means that the triviality of
c(G)
implies that also
c(\widetilde{G})
is trivial
for a precompact, sequentially complete and locally minimal nilpotent group. This is precisely item (a) of Theorem
A
for

hereditary disconnected
precompact, sequentially complete and locally minimal nilpotent groups.

Let us see now that Theorem
A
(a) holds for any precompact, sequentially complete and locally minimal nilpotent group
G
with
c(G)\neq\{0\}
, i.e., the only case remaining open from Theorem
A
(a) is that described in Question
6.7
. Indeed,
c(G)\neq\{0\}
implies that
w(c(G))\geq\omega
, hence
w(c(G))<w(c(\widetilde{G}))
may occur only when
w(c(\widetilde{G}))>\omega
. Therefore, it is enough to consider the case when
\dim\widetilde{G}
is infinite.
Since the compact group
\widetilde{G}
is nilpotent,
c(\widetilde{G})
is central in
\widetilde{G}
, by Fact
6.6
.
Hence, every subgroup of
c(\widetilde{G})
is normal in
\widetilde{G}
. In particular,
G\cap c(\widetilde{G})
is a locally essential subgroup of
c(\widetilde{G})
.
By Remark
2.9
,
G\cap N
is essential in some co-NSS subgroup
N
of
c(\widetilde{G})
.
Therefore, arguing in the line of the proof of Theorem
A
(a
′
), we conclude that
w(c(G))=w(c(N))=w(c(\widetilde{G}))
.

In spite of the (partially) positive solution towards obtaining a countrepart of Theorem
A
(a) for nilpotent groups,
the answer to the following question regarding a countrepart of Theorem
A
(b) remains unknown in the general case:

Question 6.8
.

Is Theorem
A
(b) valid for precompact, sequentially complete and locally minimal nilpotent groups?

Following the line of the proof of
[
29
, Theorem 3.2]
one can give a positive answer to this question if
G
is nilpotent of class 2 and
precompactness is replaced by the stronger property of pseudocompactness. However, that proof cannot work in the precompact case since it makes essential use of Proposition
4.6
(in the version
of minimality), but as Example
6.3
shows, the counterpart of that proposition fails even for nilpotent groups of class 2.

.

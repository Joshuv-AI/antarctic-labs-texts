Making primary health care work better: the HEARTS 2.0 opportunity to integrate cardiovascular–kidney–metabolic care
Authors: Pedro Ordunez, Jarbas Barbosa da Silva
Published: 2026

In this special issue of the Revista Panamericana de Salud Pública/Pan American Journal of Public Health, we bring together a collection of papers that reflects the evolution of HEARTS in the Americas and the growing experience accumulated by countries in improving the prevention and management of noncommunicable diseases (NCDs) through primary health care (PHC). Collectively, these contributions demonstrate that advancing PHC is no longer an aspirational goal but an increasingly achievable reality. The papers also show that the next step is not simply to expand successful interventions, but to improve how health systems organize and deliver care so that more people benefit from integrated, high-quality services (1).

Few public health challenges are more urgent than improving the prevention and management of cardiovascular, kidney, and metabolic (CKM) diseases. Together, these interconnected conditions account for a substantial proportion of premature mortality, disability, and health expenditure across the Americas (2). Although effective interventions have existed for decades, access to timely diagnosis, essential medicines, and continuous, high-quality care remains profoundly unequal. As countries move toward universal health, expanding access remains essential—but access alone is no longer enough. Health systems must also improve the quality, continuity, and integration of care to translate broader coverage into meaningfully better health outcomes (3).

HEARTS has demonstrated that this transformation is possible. Across the Americas, the initiative is being implemented in 34 countries and territories, 31 of which have adopted a clinical pathway incorporating a standardized, HEARTS-aligned medication treatment protocol. Approximately 14 000 primary care facilities are implementing HEARTS, reaching over 68 million adults. Around 10 million people are currently registered as receiving treatment for hypertension in these facilities, while blood pressure control among treated patients ranges from 50% to 80% across participating countries. This experience shows that standardized, evidence-based care can be delivered at scale across diverse health system settings, while also highlighting the importance of sustained implementation support and continuous quality improvement. Building on this progress, countries across the Region are now working toward implementation in more than 80% of their primary care facilities within the next 3 to 5 years, creating an important opportunity to embed integrated CKM care in routine primary care services and advance the reorientation of health systems toward PHC (4).

At the same time, these achievements should not obscure an equally important reality. Progress has been uneven. Some countries have institutionalized HEARTS as a national implementation strategy and expanded implementation across most of their primary care networks, while others continue to face important barriers to scale-up. These differences reflect variations in governance, implementation capacity, workforce, access to medicines and technologies, and, ultimately, in how health systems organize and deliver care (5). Increasingly, the challenge is no longer determining what interventions work, but ensuring that health systems can deliver them consistently, equitably, and with high quality.

This is the implementation challenge that HEARTS 2.0 seeks to address. In a recent health policy analysis published in The Lancet Regional Health – Americas, we argue that the next frontier is no longer generating additional clinical recommendations but organizing health systems to translate evidence into routine practice and accelerate their reorientation toward PHC. Rather than proposing another set of clinical recommendations, the paper outlines an implementation framework built around six mutually reinforcing system levers: governance with measurable targets; institutionalized, standardized clinical pathways; sustainable access to essential medicines and clinically validated technologies; digital health infrastructure, including a functional health information system; strong multidisciplinary primary care teams supported by continuous quality improvement; and meaningful community engagement and collaborative implementation. Together, these levers define the organizational functions needed to deliver coordinated, longitudinal, and people-centered CKM care while improving both access to and the quality of services. In this framework, hypertension remains the clinical entry point, but the central innovation lies in shifting the emphasis from individual interventions to the way health care services are organized and delivered. HEARTS 2.0 is therefore best understood as a practical implementation strategy to advance PHC, strengthen primary care, and make integrated CKM care routinely achievable at scale within PHC-oriented health systems (6, 7).

The papers included in this special issue illustrate complementary dimensions of this transformation. Together, they show that advancing PHC and strengthening primary care depend on coordinated action across multiple health system functions rather than on isolated technical interventions. They also demonstrate the importance of adapting implementation to different national and local contexts while maintaining common clinical standards, measurable goals, and a sustained focus on quality.

This progress has been made possible through the sustained leadership of ministries of health; the commitment of thousands of health professionals working in primary care; the collaboration of scientific societies, academic institutions, and other partners; and the regional leadership and technical cooperation of the Pan American Health Organization (PAHO). It has also been strengthened by enduring partnerships. In particular, the long-standing collaboration with Resolve to Save Lives has been instrumental in supporting countries, fostering innovation, strengthening implementation capacity, and accelerating HEARTS across the Americas. This experience demonstrates the value of sustained collaboration in translating evidence into public health impact (8).

The Region is well positioned to accelerate integrated CKM care through PHC. Scientific evidence, effective medicines and technologies, accumulated implementation experience, and renewed political commitment to strengthening PHC and building PHC-oriented health systems have converged to create favorable conditions for this acceleration. Regional initiatives such as the Alliance for Primary Health Care in the Americas, the World Bank–PAHO–The Lancet Regional Health – Americas Commission on Primary Health Care, and Better Care for NCDs further reinforce this momentum and provide an enabling platform for advancing integrated health service delivery (9, 10).

The task ahead is to ensure that every country can reorient its health system toward PHC and equip primary care services and teams to deliver integrated CKM care with equitable access and consistently high quality. HEARTS 2.0 offers a practical implementation strategy to achieve that ambition. Accelerating its implementation could be one of the most effective ways to increase the problem-solving capacity of health systems and reduce preventable deaths from NCDs across the Americas.

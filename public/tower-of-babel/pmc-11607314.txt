Automatic detection and counting of wheat spike based on DMseg-Count
Authors: Hecang Zang, Yilong Peng, Meng Zhou, Guoqiang Li, Guoqing Zheng, Hualei Shen
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe automatic detection and counting of wheat spike images are of great significance for yield prediction and variety evaluation. Therefore, accurate and timely estimation of spike numbers is crucial for wheat production. However, in actual production, due to the susceptibility of wheat spike images to factors such as lighting conditions, shooting angles, occlusion, and overlap, the contour and features of wheat spike is unclear, which affects the accuracy of automatic detection and counting of wheat spike. In order to solve the above problems and further improve the accuracy of wheat spike counting, an improved wheat spike counting model DMseg-Count was proposed by enhancing local contextual supervision information based on existing target object counting model DM-Count. Firstly, wheat spike local segmentation branch was introduced to improve the network architecture of DM-Count, so as to extract the local contextual supervision information of wheat spike. Secondly, an element-by-element point multiplication mechanism was designed to fuse global and local contextual supervision information of wheat spike. Finally, the total loss function was constructed to optimize the model. The test results showed that the mean absolute error (MAE) and root mean square error (RMSE) of the proposed DMseg-Count model were 5.79 and 7.54, respectively, which were 9.76 and 10.91 higher than the standard distribution matching for crowd counting (DM-Count) model. Compared with other deep learning models, the proposed DMseg-Count model can detect wheat spike image in challenging situations, and has better computer vision processing capabilities and performance evaluation detection effect. In summary, the proposed DMseg-Count model can effectively detect wheat spike and has good counting performance, which provides a new method for automatic counting of wheat spike and yield prediction in complex field environments.Keywords: Field phenotyping, Wheat, Spike counting, Deep learning, Local segmentation branchSubject terms: Plant biotechnology, Software

Introduction

Wheat is a widely grown crop and an important food crop in the world, accounting for 20% of the world ‘s dietary calories1. In 2021, the global wheat sown area is 223 million hectares, and the yield is 776 million tons2. However, food security faces serious global challenges, and food demand will increase 69% by 2050 to meet the unprecedented needs of 9.7 billion people3. With an increasing aging-population structure, decreasing arable land and influence of abnormal climate, which brings many uncertainties to the formation of wheat yield4. In actual production, the counting of wheat spikes mainly depends on manual counting in the field, which has the problems of high economic cost, large labor consumption and low counting efficiency, and the counting results is easily affected by subjective factors. Therefore, timely and accurate estimation of the number of wheat spikes provides an important scientific basis for yield prediction and variety evaluation.

Spikes number is a key factor restricting wheat yield, spikes number is too sparse or dense, which greatly affects wheat yield5. The effect of spikes number on wheat yield is mainly divided into two aspects, on the one hand, although too low spikes number can ensure the full development of individual plants, but it is not conducive to the improvement of yield. On the other hand, excessive spikes number not only causes the population to be too large, but also easily causes lodging in the middle and late growth stages, resulting in a decrease in yield. At present, the counting method of wheat spike mainly relies on the manual selection a certain length of wheat sample section to count spikes, and then counting the average row spacing of wheat test site to calculate the number of wheat spikes per unit area. This method is not only time-consuming and laborious, but also has a poor working environment6–8. Therefore, the traditional artificial spike counting method obviously can’t meet the needs of modern large-scale and efficient crop breeding. The use of deep learning for automatic counting of wheat spike images is an effective way to solve the dilemma. The application of deep learning in the number of wheat spikes has the advantages of fast counting, high accuracy and strong adaptability, which can break the time and space constraints and the dependence on agricultural experts, thereby improving labor efficiency.

The number of wheat spike based on unmanned aerial vehicle (UAV) digital images mainly uses the target detection method to obtain the number and geometry figure of wheat spike9–11. Hasan et al.12 used convolutional neural network (CNN) to detect, count and analyze wheat spike for yield estimation. Zhang et al.13 used you only look once (YOLO) network structure to propose a high-precision wheat spike detection model. Sadeghi-Tehran et al.14 developed a wheat spike counting system DeepCount, which is used to automatically identify and count the number of wheat spike image. Fernandez-Gallego et al.15 developed an image processing system using a thermal imager to automatically count the number of wheat spikes. Khaki et al.16 proposed a framework based on WheatNet, which can accurately and effectively count wheat spike images. The above research results show that deep learning has good robustness in wheat spike counting, and can be trained and optimized by data-driven method, so as to improve the accuracy and generalization ability of wheat spike detection.

In recent years, with the rapid development of computer vision technology, some scholars have used deep learning methods to count target objects such as cell17, people18–20, and wheat spike 21–23. Significant progress has been made in detection accuracy and applied in actual production. These methods can be divided into target detection methods and density map regression methods. At present, agricultural visual object detection algorithms are mainly divided into one-stage object detection algorithms and two-stage object detection algorithms, such as single shot multibox detector (SSD)24, YOLO25–29, and Faster region-based convolutional network (R-CNN) 30,31. These algorithms treat object detection problems as regression analysis of target position and category information, requiring a large number of target object borders to be labeled during the training phase32, the detection results can be directly output through neural network models. The method based on density map regression is to use point labeling to generate a density map for the target object, as the learning target of the model, it can better capture the distribution and density information of the target, and then integrate the density map predicted by the model to obtain the count value of the target object33,34. At present, representative detection methods mainly include bayesian loss (BL), multi-column convolutional neural network (MCNN), convolutional social relation network (CSRNet), spatial/channel-wise attention regression networks (SCAR), point-to-point network (P2PNet), and distribution matching for crowd counting (DM-Count). Zang et al.7 proposed a method based on YOLOv5s with an improved attention mechanism, which can accurately detect the number of wheat spike. Wen et al.35 proposed a wheat spike detection and counting in complex scenarious, which improve the model’s ability to detect and count wheat spike accurately. In general, the cost of using such methods to label wheat spike is not high, but the accurate position of wheat spike can’t be identified, resulting in weak robustness of the model. However, in the detection of wheat spike images, due to the high density of wheat spike, serious occlusion and serious cross-overlapping, detection errors and missed detection of the wheat spike are caused, the difficulty and accuracy of wheat spike detection are further increased.

DM-Count have achieved state-of-the-art results for target object counting, distribution matching is used for counting tasks, and a new optimization strategy is designed to measure the similarity between the real value and the predicted value. The advantage of DM-Count is that it avoids the tedious labeling process and labeling errors, and reduces the risk of model counting performance degradation caused by indirect generation of learning objectives. Moreover, DM-Count can clearly identify the location of the target object, which can better meet the application requirements of downstream tasks. It can be seen from the above analysis that DM-Count is more suitable for wheat spikes counting in complex scene. However, the performance of DM-Count directly used for wheat spikes counting is poor, on the one hand, the flag leaf and illumination angle in the wheat field lead to shadow in different directions and sizes in the wheat spike image, which brings interference noise to the counting model and seriously affects the performance of DM-Count. on the other hand, the occlusion and overlap of wheat spike lead to the misjudgment of DM-Count.

In order to solve the above problems, this paper improves DM-Count and proposes wheat spikes counting model DMseg-Count that enhances local contextual supervision information. Firstly, the local segmentation branch is introduced to improve the network structure, enhance the local contextual supervision information of wheat spike, and reduce the interference of background or other irrelevant regions on the counting results. Secondly, the element-by-element point multiplication mechanism is designed to fuse the segmentation branches to extract the local information and global information of wheat spike, and simulate the counting difficulties caused by occlusion and overlapping by agricultural experts combined with different information. Finally, the fused feature information is used to generate a density map, and the density branch is used to predict the counting results of wheat spikes.

Materials and methods

Dataset preparation

The wheat materials were planted in Henan Modern Agricultural Research and Development Base of Henan Academy of Agricultural Sciences, Henan Province, China, provided by the Wheat Research Institute of Henan Academy of Agricultural Sciences, before the start of the experiment, they agreed to conduct the study. It is located at 113°41’E and 35°0’N, as shown in Fig. 1. The field experiment was conducted in a completely randomized block design. It was planted on October 12, 2021, with a total of 68 plots, each with an area of 2 m2.

Location of study area.

The smart phone of Huawei MATE 40 PRO was used to collect data, the camera resolution was 50 million pixels, the sensor type was Sony IMX700, and the aperture was f/1.9. The images of wheat spike were collected from April 20 to May 26, 2022, and then collected every 5 days. At this time, it is in the flowering stage, filling stage and mature stage. In this paper, the target counting area marked by 1 m × 1 m white rectangular box is sampled, and a total of 800 wheat spike images are taken, each image resolution is 4032 pixels × 3024 pixels. The collected images were cropped to obtain images within a white rectangular box, and then subjected to data augmentation such as rotation and flipping, and a total of 2400 images were obtained.

In addition, we also used the public dataset Wheat Ears Detection Dataset, which contains 236 high-resolution wheat ear images with a resolution of 6000 × 4000 per image, as shown in Fig. 2.

Wheat Ears Detection Dataset.

Data preprocessing

The purpose of data preprocessing is to eliminate redundancy in non-target counting areas outside the white rectangular box. In order to avoid the possibility of large areas of non-target counting areas that may interfere with the counting results of the target area due to random cropping operations used for data enhancement in the future, the maximum redundancy removal is performed on the non-target areas to obtain the final experimental image of this study.

Image annotation

Wheat spike is prone to occlusion and overlapping, which makes the method based on box labeling relatively difficult. Therefore, a low-cost, convenient and fast point labeling method is adopted. A point label represents the point coordinates of the corresponding wheat spike in the image. The annotation tool developed by Wang et al.36 was used to annotate the dataset. For the dense, occluded and overlapped areas in the wheat spike image, the tool is used to enlarge and label, which is convenient for subsequent network training.

Dataset construction

A total of 2400 images were obtained after 800 original images were horizontally flipped, rotated, randomly cropped, and brightness adjusted for data enhancement and image denoising. The dataset is divided into training dataset, validation dataset and test dataset according to a ratio of 6:1:3. In order to facilitate network training, the original image is uniformly cut to 512 pixels × 512 pixels.

Deep learning models

The deep learning algorithm uses large-scale data sets to train the neural network through forward propagation and back propagation techniques, so that it gradually learns high-level feature representation and has pattern recognition ability. By selecting the appropriate deep neural network architecture and continuously adjusting the network parameters, the model is gradually optimized and useful information is extracted to solve complex tasks. In this study, nine state-of-the-art deep learning models are selected: YOLOv8, Faster RCNN, BL, MCNN, CSRNet, SCAR, P2PNet, DM-Count and DMseg-Count. The related architectures are shown in Table 1. All models were pre trained using transfer learning methods, and schematic diagram of image acquisition, image processing, model selection, and model performance is shown in Fig. 3. BL uses Bayesian loss functions to construct a density contribution probability model from point annotations to compensate for the shortcomings of density maps37. MCNN is based on multi column convolutional neural networks to capture features at different scales38. CSRNet uses hollow convolution to improve counting accuracy in crowded scenarios39. SCAR introduces an attention mechanism to obtain correlation information between pixels and crowd context 40. P2PNet directly takes the point annotation results as the learning objective, and then predicts the point coordinates of all target objects to obtain the counting results41. DM-Count uses distribution matching for counting tasks and designs a new optimization strategy to measure the similarity between real and predicted values 42. Through experimental verification, we found that the models listed in Table 1 have good applicability in wheat spike counting tasks. We conducted comparative experiments using common performance evaluation metrics such as MAE and RMSE, and the results showed that these models performed well in the wheat spike counting task. Although these models were originally designed for population statistics, we made some adjustments and fine-tuning to meet the special needs of wheat spike counting tasks and achieved significant improvements. Therefore, we can be confident that these models are feasible and effective choices for wheat spike counting task.

Proposed methodology

Network structure of DM-Count and overall framework of DMSeg-Count

DM-Count is a crowd counting network based on density map. The input of the network is a crowd image and the output is a crowd density map. The number of people is obtained by summing the predicted density map. Based on VGG19, DM-Count generates a crowd density prediction map through the density prediction branch, and uses optimal transport (OT) to measure the similarity between the crowd density prediction map and the real map, its network structure is shown in Fig. 4; in order to stabilize the OT calculation, the network uses the total variation (TV) loss function.

Network structure of DM-Count.

In this study, DM-Count is improved, and the local segmentation branch of wheat spike is introduced to enhance the local context supervision information of wheat spike. On this basis, a wheat spike counting model DMseg-Count that enhances local context supervision information is proposed. The network architecture is shown in Fig. 5. DMseg-Count consists of the basic network VGG19 and two additional branches: the wheat spike local segmentation branch and the wheat spike density branch. For a wheat spike image, the basic features are extracted by VGG19, and the feature map X2 and X3 is obtained. After that, fuse the X2 and X3 feature map to obtain feature map F0, and then input F0 into the wheat spike local branch to generate local feature map F1, in order to extract local contextual supervision information of the wheat spike. Then, the wheat spike segmentation prediction map is obtained by upsampling and convolution operation. The local segmentation loss function is used to compare the predicted segmentation map with the real segmentation map. At the same time, the feature map output from the first 35 layers of VGG19 is sent to the density branch, and then the density map is generated by sampling and convolution operations to obtain the count loss, total change loss and optimal transmission loss.

Network structure of DMseg-Count.

Local segmentation branch of wheat spike

By local segmentation branch of wheat spike, we can accurately extract local contextual supervision information from the wheat spike. The design of this branch aims to focus attention on the wheat spike target area at the annotation point position, especially in cases of occlusion or overlap at the annotation point position, providing richer contextual information and significantly improving the detection accuracy of the model. First, the size of the original image is scaled to 512 × 512 and sent to the basic network to obtain the feature map generated by the 16th and 23th layers, which are recorded as X2 and X3 respectively, dimensions of the feature map is 64 × 64 × 512 and 32 × 32 × 512. The two feature maps are sent to the feature fusion module, which contains three convolutional layers and three upsampling layers to fuse three feature maps of different scales, so as to generate more accurate wheat spike counting results. The feature map P4 is obtained by 1 × 1 convolution and upsampling of X3, dimensions of the feature map is 32 × 32 × 512. Then X2 is added to P4 through 1 × 1 convolutional layer, and then a 3 × 3 convolutional layer is used to obtain the feature map F0, dimensions of the feature map is 64 × 64 × 512. F0 is input into the local segmentation branch, after two consecutive groups of 3 × 3 convolutional layers and ReLU activation function, the residual connection is used to generate the intermediate feature map. Then the intermediate feature map is sent to two residual blocks (each residual block consists of a 3 × 3 convolutional layer, a ReLU activation function and a residual connection) to obtain a more refined feature map. Finally, the feature map is subjected to a 3 × 3 convolution to obtain the segmentation result. The segmentation result contains two channels, corresponding to the background and foreground of the wheat spike image respectively. The size of the segmentation result is amplified by 8 times by upsampling to match the size of the original input image. Finally, the final segmentation result is obtained by 1 × 1 convolution.

Feature fusion module

The module multiplies and fuses low-level feature such as the edges and contours of the wheat spike with high-level feature such as the overall structure of the wheat spike, which not only preserves detailed information, but also enhances attention to key feature. First, with a size of 64 × 64 × 2 input the local feature map F1 into the softmax layer, its element values normalized to [0,1]. Next, perform a repeat operation on the local feature tensor that represents the local context supervision information of the wheat spike, copying it 256 times to obtain a new feature map with a size of 64 × 64 × 256. Subsequently, the new feature map is multiplied element by element with the global feature map F0 to obtain the fused feature map F2, dimensions of the feature map is 64 × 64 × 256. In this way, F2 integrates local information and global feature information of wheat spikes, so as to enhance the network’s recognition ability of wheat spikes and improve the accuracy of wheat spike counting.

Density branching of wheat spike

Using the fused feature map F2 as input for the density branch, the predicted density map is obtained by two-layer convolution, ReLU activation function, 1 × 1 convolution and normalization.

Evaluation indicators

Mean absolute error (MAE) and root mean square error (RMSE) were used to evaluate the performance of the model. MAE is used to measure the counting accuracy of the network, the value is smaller, the predicted value of the spikes number is closer to the true value. RMSE is used to measure the stability of the network, the value is smaller, the stability of the network is stronger and the robustness is better. Experimental settings

The experiment is equipped with Intel (R) Core (TM) i7-10600 CPU, the main frequency is 2.90 GHz; NVIDIA GeForce RTX3090 GPU on a 24GB workstation.

Using PyTorch as a deep learning framework, the image resolution of experimental training, verification and testing is 512 × 512. The optimizer selects adaptive moment estimation (Adam), sets the training batch to 8, the number of training rounds to 1000, sets the initial learning rate to 0.001, and reduces the learning rate to 0.0001 as the number of iterations increases.

Ablation experiment

The ablation experiment aims to analyze the value of the improved algorithm. Based on a self-made dataset, ablation experiment was conducted to test the improved model. The experimental results are shown in Table 5. From Table 5, it can be seen that density branch, segmentation branch, and element-by-element point multiplication mechanism were performed on the DM-Count baseline network, and the improved network accuracy can meet the detection requirement. By introducing segmentation branches, it is possible to enhance local context supervision information and optimize overall prediction performance in complex backgrounds such as wheat ears obstructing each other and unstable lighting conditions, making the predicted values close to the true values, MAE and RMSE of the model were reduced by 3.70 and 3.48 respectively compared to the baseline network. By using the element- by-element point mechanism, the local information of wheat spike is fused with global feature, which greatly improve the recognition accuracy of the model for wheat spike, compared with the baseline network, MAE and RMSE of the model were reduced by 9.76 and 10.91, respectively.

Discussion

Spikes number is an important phenotypic trait that determines wheat yield; therefore, accurate detection of wheat spike number is of great significance for estimating yield43,44. BL uses Bayes loss function to construct density contribution probability model from point labeling to make up for the deficiency of density map. MCNN uses a convolutional neural network to improve the accuracy of counting in dense scenes. CSRNet uses dilated convolution to improve the counting accuracy in crowded scenes. SCAR introduces an attention mechanism to obtain the correlation information between pixels and crowd context. P2PNet is a point-to-block crowd density estimation method that uses convolutional neural networks for feature extraction and density estimation. DM-Count is a crowd counting method based on density map, convolutional neural network is used to regress the density map to obtain the counting results. The Faster RCNN detection method has a wide range of applications in the field of object detection, and performs well in detecting complex backgrounds and multiple targets. YOLOv8 is an object detection model and the latest version of the YOLO series of models. The model has shown excellent performance in object detection tasks and achieved a good balance between speed and accuracy. Because the size and shape of wheat spike collected in this study is diverse, and is affected by factors such as environment and varieties, for wheat spikes of different sizes, shapes and colors, using the same scale algorithm for detection tends to produce large error. In the detection of wheat spike, some images have overlapping wheat spike that is not identified, and adjacent wheat spike are not identified, two wheat spikes are closely connected and identified as one wheat spike. This study improves the existing target object counting network DM Count and proposes a wheat spike counting model DMseg Count that enhances local context supervision information. It can accurately and effectively detect the number of wheat spike from flowering to maturity, and can be used to replace traditional manual counting methods. At the same time, it solves the problem of false counting and missing counting caused by occlusion and overlap in wheat spike image detection, so as to efficiently and accurately detect the number of wheat spike, which can provide technical reference for wheat spike counting and yield estimation in actual production.

Significant progress has been made in the research of wheat spike detection and counting technology, which has attracted widespread attention from researchers. However, wheat growth is a complex and dynamic process, particularly reflected in the color characteristics and background changes of wheat spikes. The existing wheat spike detection models are usually optimized for specific growth stages during design and application, and their adaptability to other growth stages or field scenarios is often limited. In this study, the latest computer vision technology for automatic counting of wheat spikes was proposed, and nine deep learning models were evaluated for wheat spike detection. All the selected models can realize the automatic counting of wheat spikes. DMseg-Count produces the best performance in all models, which may be mainly because DMseg-Count introduces the local segmentation branch of wheat spike to obtain more local contextual supervision information of wheat spike, and uses the element-by-element point multiplication mechanism to fuse local contextual supervision information and global information extracted by the basic network. The feature map obtained by local segmentation branch and the global feature map obtained by FPN is multiplied by element by element, and the obtained feature map combines the local information and global feature information of wheat spike, thus enhancing the recognition ability of the network to wheat spike and improving the accuracy of wheat spike counting. In addition, DMseg-Count enhances the model’s ability to resist factors such as occlusion and overlapping, so as to improve the robustness of the model, and significantly reduce the model ‘s miscalculation and omission of wheat spikes.

In previous studies, error counting and missing counting of wheat spike images resulted in poor performance of existing counting methods. Research has shown that areas of error counting and missing counting have a significant impact on wheat spike recognition results45. The main reasons for error counting and missing counting include mutual occlusion and cross overlap of wheat spikes, as shown in (Fig. 9), there is a severe mutual occlusion and cross overlap of wheat spikes, which leads to error counting and missing counting of wheat spikes. As the density of wheat spikes increases, the mutual occlusion between wheat spikes becomes more severe, which can also lead to error counting and missing counting of wheat spikes. Therefore, DMseg-Count can reduce the problems of mutual occlusion and cross overlap in the process of wheat spike recognition.

This study focuses on wheat spike images with multiple growth stages in the field environment, and uses deep learning method to achieve rapid counting of wheat spikes. However, there are still some shortcomings, and the relevant theoretical work needs further improvement. In future research work, research of wheat spike counting should be conducted from the following perspectives: (1) exploring more reasonable wheat spike counting model, combined with a single-stage target detection algorithm and two-stage target detection algorithm to jointly define the wheat spike counting; (2) Construct a dataset with a large number of samples to make the samples more balanced; (3) Improve the image processing algorithm of wheat spike, optimize the automatic counting system of wheat spike, and improve the accuracy and stability of wheat spike counting.

Conclusion

A wheat spike counting model was constructed using deep learning to achieve automatic counting of wheat spikes. The proposed algorithm has higher counting accuracy. The results indicate that using smartphones and deep learning can accurately and quickly automatic counting of wheat spikes. The improvement of network structure and specialized feature fusion strategies have enhanced the feature extraction ability of the model.

The effects of heat stress on milk yield and composition in dairy cattle: meta-analysis study
Authors: Özgür Özcan, Serdal Dikmen
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractGlobal climate change has positioned thermal stress as a critical challenge in dairy production. Heat stress is widely documented to involve alterations in both productive traits, and physio-pathological indicators. This meta-analysis was conducted to provide a quantitative synthesis of the observed variations in milk production parameters during thermal stress.Following PRISMA protocols and the PICOS framework, a systematic dataset was compiled from 35 independent studies published between 2010 and 2023. To address the inherent high heterogeneity (I² ≥ 90%) among studies, the Inverse Variance Heterogeneity (IVhet) model was employed for statistical pooling. Effect sizes were interpreted using Cohen’s d, and potential sources of variance were explored through THI (Temperature-Humidity Index) based subgroup analyses and meta-regression.The pooled estimators indicate a significant negative association between heat stress and milk yield (d = -0.723). Noticeable reductions were also observed in milk composition parameters, specifically for milk fat (d = -0.633), milk protein (d = -0.555), and milk lactose (d = -0.404). Subgroup analysis indicated that these production declines become more pronounced when the THI exceeds a threshold of 72. However, due to substantial heterogeneity among studies, these pooled estimates should be interpreted as generalized global trends rather than precise universal thresholds.In conclusion, this meta-analysis confirms a consistent alignment between elevated thermal indices and qualitative as well as quantitative production losses. Rather than prescribing specific management interventions, these findings underscore the necessity for future research to account for cumulative exposure duration and localized farm microclimates to better predict production responses.Keywords: Heat stress, Dairy cattle, Milk yield, Milk composition, Meta-analysis

Introduction

Global climate change represents a multidimensional environmental challenge that directly threatens animal production through rising temperatures and extreme weather events (Morgado et al., 2022). Dairy cattle are particularly sensitive to environmental fluctuations due to their high metabolic energy output and the physiological pressures of lactation (Kadzere et al., 2002). Although cattle utilize radiation, conduction, and evaporation to maintain thermal equilibrium, these mechanisms often become constrained under combined high temperature and humidity, leading to a systemic physiological state defined as "heat stress" (West, 2003). In literature, heat stress is consistently associated with cumulative negative trends in milk yield, composition, and reproductive parameters (Baumgard & Rhoads, 2013). High-yielding breeds are especially vulnerable due to their elevated nutritional requirements and metabolic heat production (West, 2003). Research indicates that chronic heat exposure is linked to production losses through decreased dry matter intake, impaired rumen function, and negative energy balance (Rhoads et al., 2010). Furthermore, heat stress during the dry period has been shown to coincide with impaired mammary gland involution, potentially altering the production trajectory of the subsequent lactation (Dado-Senn et al., 2019).

While technical mitigation strategies such as cooling systems and nutritional interventions are widely discussed, their generalizable trends across diverse experimental setups require rigorous quantitative synthesis. Crucially, the substantial variability in how heat stress is defined, operationalized, and quantified across primary studies—ranging from disparate temperature-humidity thresholds to non-standardized environmental exposure periods—severely limits the direct comparability and reproducibility of individual outcomes. In this context, meta-analysis serves as a critical methodological tool to reconcile these heterogeneous findings and establish a standardized denominator across independent studies conducted across various regions and setups. This study aims to quantitatively synthesize the observed effects of heat stress on milk yield and components using the Inverse Variance Heterogeneity (IVhet) model. By providing a comprehensive quantitative pooling under strict awareness of literature heterogeneity, this study seeks to establish a clearer, aggregated baseline of performance variations during thermal stress, rather than prescribing direct on-farm management interventions.

Materials and methods

This study is a meta-analysis that systematically reviews and synthesizes research published between 2010 and 2023 regarding the impact of heat stress on milk yield and composition in dairy cattle. To mitigate methodological inconsistencies and facilitate a centralized framework for analysis, heat stress exposure was categorized based on the Temperature-Humidity Index (THI). However, it must be acknowledged that the primary literature exhibits variation in how THI was calculated, recorded, and utilized (e.g., varying duration of exposure, differences in daily peak vs. average THI, and lack of consistent data regarding nocturnal recovery periods). Consequently, the THI-based classifications employed herein serve as standardized proxies to capture broad, approximate trends across diverse geographic locations and experimental setups, rather than strictly uniform environmental states. Studies utilizing THI as a primary environmental indicator, or providing sufficient raw climate data for its calculation, were prioritized to facilitate objective comparisons.

Search Strategy: The bibliographic search targeted peer-reviewed publications released between 2010 and 2023. Searches were conducted across Web of Science, ScienceDirect, Scopus, and NIH (PubMed) databases, specifically within "Article Title," "Abstract," and "Keywords" fields. To ensure precision and focus on heat stress impacts, specific search string combinations were utilized: ("heat stress" AND "dairy cattle" AND "milk yield"), ("heat stress" AND "dairy cattle" AND "composition"), and ("heat stress" AND "dairy cattle" AND "meta-analysis"). These combinations yielded 2,046 raw publications (Web of Science: 1,025, ScienceDirect: 543, Scopus: 391, NIH: 87). Following the removal of 605 duplicate records, 1,441 unique publications were retained for further eligibility screening. Ultimately, the data for the meta-analysis were derived from 35 academic studies. The meta-analysis was conducted in accordance with the PRISMA (Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses) guidelines to ensure transparent reporting of the evidence. Given the highly diverse nature of the source studies, substantial statistical heterogeneity was anticipated and factored into the downstream analytical approach.

Inclusion Criteria: The systematic review process was structured according to the PICOS+T framework to ensure transparency and reproducibility (Table 1). Inclusion was strictly limited to studies involving high-yielding dairy breed (Holstein) in lactation, incorporating a clear thermoneutral control group (baseline), and reporting complete statistical parameters (n, Mean, SD/SE) required for meta-analytical synthesis.

Data Extraction; Publications containing data on Temperature-Humidity Index (THI), temperature, humidity, milk yield, and milk composition were categorized. Data Analysis; Meta-analysis was performed using the Inverse Variance Heterogeneity (IVhet) model, which provides more robust estimators compared to traditional random-effects models under conditions of high statistical heterogeneity (Doi et al., 2015). The IVhet model was prioritized specifically because it maintains a conservative false-positive rate and adjusts the weights more realistically when I² exceeds 90%. Statistical procedures were implemented via MetaXL (version 5.3) and R (version 4.3.1) (Table 4).

4.Evaluation of potential publication and selection bias was performed through a multi-faceted approach. Quantitative symmetry was assessed via the Doi plot and its corresponding LFK (Luis Furuya-Kanamori) index, where values within |LFK| ≤ 1 indicated no asymmetry. To ensure methodological rigor under high heterogeneity, Egger’s linear regression test was additionally conducted, with significance set at p < 0.05. Discrepancies between the LFK index and Egger’s test results—particularly in parameters with severe inter-study variance like milk yield—were interpreted with academic caution, recognizing that traditional regression-based asymmetry tests can yield inflated false-positive rates under extreme mathematical heterogeneity (I² ≥ 90).

Heterogeneity Management and Moderator Analyses: To systematically explore the potential biological and methodological sources of the anticipated high statistical heterogeneity (I²), meta-regression and subgroup analyses were performed. Key potential moderators—including standardized THI boundaries, regional climate classifications, and operational farm dynamics—were integrated into the exploratory models. For meta-regression, the proportion of variation accounted for by the covariates was reported as R², while the significance of the moderators was evaluated using the Omnibus test (Q_M). In subgroup analyses, thermal load intensity was treated as a categorical moderator (e.g., separating groups around the baseline threshold of (THI ≥72) to estimate stratified weighted mean variations associated with each defined thermal state. All meta-regression and subgroup models were implemented primarily using the metafor package in R, utilizing the Restricted Maximum Likelihood (REML) estimator. Given the observational and retrospective nature of the synthesized source studies, the outputs of these moderator analyses were treated strictly as exploratory indicators of statistical association rather than definitive evidence of direct causal mechanisms (Table 5).

The pooled effect sizes estimating the statistical association between heat stress exposure and milk production parameters are summarized in Table 7. Across all analyzed traits, elevated thermal exposure was associated with statistically significant negative shifts (p < 0.001). The pooled effect size (d) for milk yield was documented at -0.723 (95% CI: -1.217 to -0.229). Among the milk composition parameters, the most pronounced quantitative reduction trend was observed in the milk fat percentage(d = -0.633, 95% CI: -1.192 to -0.073), followed closely by the milk protein percentage(d = -0.555, 95% CI: -0.930 to -0.180) and the milk lactose percentage(d = -0.404, 95% CI: -0.674 to -0.135). The structural observation that all 95% confidence intervals remained entirely below the zero baseline suggests a consistent operational decline in performance during elevated thermal periods across the reviewed literature. While the initial baseline model for milk fat remained marked by substantial statistical heterogeneity (I² = 90.55 %), the pooled estimators for milk protein (I²= 70.92 %) and milk lactose (I² = 44.36 %) exhibited noticeably lower variance under the IVhet framework, indicating a more stable integration of these components. Part of this environmental variance was explored through THI-based categorical moderator analyses, which suggest that variations in thermal intensity account for a key portion of the observed inter-study divergence. Under the explicit subgroup arrangements (detailed further in Table 8), partitioning data around the core baseline threshold of THI ≥ 72 indicated that elevated thermal loads coincide with intensified production variations. However, given the persistent heterogeneity remaining in the milk yield and fat models, these pooled estimators should be interpreted with caution as general global trend indicators rather than precise biological point estimates. The remaining residual variance across the source studies is likely attributable to non-standardized farm-level characteristics, such as dietary fiber composition, individual cow parity, or specific cooling infrastructure variations that were not systematically reported in the primary literature.

While borderline significance was observed in Egger’s test, this mathematical deviation is primarily attributed to the inherent high heterogeneity and diverse sample sizes among the primary studies rather than true publication bias. Consequently, while the synthesized findings possess high scientific validity, the pooled effect sizes are interpreted with academic caution, acknowledging the operational diversity within the literature.

To complement the visual and quantitative evidence derived from the Doi plots, Egger’s linear regression test was performed across all evaluated parameters, with the comprehensive results summarized in Table 9. Discussion

Methodological considerations

The present study utilized the Inverse Variance Heterogeneity (IVhet) model to map the global operational association between elevated thermal environments and dairy cattle production parameters. Meta-analytic designs in livestock sciences consistently confront extreme statistical heterogeneity (I² ≥ 90%),which fundamentally violates the underlying mathematical assumptions of traditional fixed- or random-effects frameworks. As established by Doi et al. (2015), conventional random-effects models inherently experience a flawed overestimation of confidence intervals when handling severe inter-study variance, compromising the reliability of pooled indicators (Doi et al., 2015). By contrast, the IVhet framework optimizes statistical weight distribution based on a quasi-likelihood approach, ensuring highly conservative and reliable pooled estimators without artificially inflating the true precision limits(Doi et al., 2017).

While the Restricted Maximum Likelihood (REML) estimator within the R metafor package was deployed to conduct exploratory secondary meta-regressions and categorical subgroup partitions, prioritizing the IVhet model for the primary pooling serves as a core methodological strength of this study. This robust dual-estimator architecture provides a statistically resilient framework capable of synthesizing real-world field variance into a highly reliable global trend.

Interpretation of thermal stress and production trends

The pooled effect size (d = -0.723, p < 0.001) documents a statistically significant and substantial negative shift in milk yield associated with elevated environmental temperatures. Rather than representing an isolated biological fluctuation, this directionally consistent decline aligns with extensive literature detailing systemic metabolic and homeostatic adjustments in high-yielding dairy cows exposed to thermal loads (Bah et al., 2022; Toledo et al., 2024; Zhang et al., 2023).

Physiologically, these aggregated variations coincide with two primary concurrent pathways. First, elevated ambient thermal metrics prompt a substantial downregulation of dry matter intake as an immediate adaptive response to minimize internal metabolic heat production. Second, modern high-yielding breeds undergo an altered nutrient partitioning framework during thermal stress; instead of directing volatile fatty acids and glucose efficiently toward mammary epithelial tissue for galactopoiesis, a substantial portion of net energy is redistributed to peripheral tissues to facilitate thermoregulation and evaporative coolingn (Bernabucci et al., 2010; Chen et al., 2024). Additionally, these systematic outcomes align with evidence that thermal load disrupts structural mammary gland dynamics and cell proliferation pathways, compromising the long-term synthetic capacity of the secretory tissue (Dado-Senn et al., 2019).

These global meta-analytic outputs reflect regional baseline trends documented within challenging Mediterranean climates. For instance, production data from Turkey indicate localized yield reductions ranging from 10% to 25% when environmental conditions compromise the cows' thermo-neutral zones (Alkoyak & Çetin, 2016). Furthermore, Dinçel and Dikmen (2013) demonstrated that elevated nighttime thermal indices compound daytime exposure by preventing necessary nocturnal physiological cooling and metabolic recovery.(Dinçel & Dikmen, 2013). The steady quantitative decay captured across our synthesized database echoes the linear performance declines noted under localized index augmentations(Bernabucci et al., 2010), reinforcing the validity of the global aggregated slope.

Categorical thresholds and comprehensive limitations

A core contribution of this multi-trait synthesis is the structural evaluation of standardized index boundaries via categorical moderator analysis. Partitioning outcomes around the core threshold baseline (THI > 72) revealed an intensified shift in performance variations; this alignment closely mirrors primary literature reporting a severe production drop with an effect size of d = -2.17 under extreme microclimatic loads (Kulaz & Ser, 2021). While establishing these aggregated operational boundaries provides valuable contextual intelligence regarding broad thermal limits, these thresholds must be interpreted with strict academic caution rather than treated as precise biological point metrics.

To ensure scientific transparency, the core limitations of this synthesis are formally consolidated across three primary structural dimensions:

High Trait-Specific Heterogeneity: Substantial statistical heterogeneity persists within the baseline milk yield (I²=89.17%) and milk fat (I²=90.55%) models. While the outlier-resilient IVhet framework accommodates this variance to provide reliable pooled estimators, this high residual variance underscores that the operational impacts of thermal load are heavily influenced by local management factors. Conversely, the variance in milk protein (I²=70.92%) and milk lactose (I²=44.36%) models stabilized at more moderate levels, reflecting distinct stabilization patterns across different biological traits.

Variability in Heat Stress Operationalization: As highlighted by the extreme variability across the 35 included replication studies, the absence of a standardized global reporting index limits direct comparability. Individual trials vary significantly in their exposure lengths, daily thermal durations, and baseline microclimatic measurements, which inherently limits the consistency of absolute pooled estimates.

Inherent Boundaries of THI Metrics: While the Temperature-Humidity Index (THI) serves as a highly practical macro-environmental proxy, it is structurally limited in its capacity to capture the multi-dimensional complexity of thermal stress responses. A standard THI configuration fails to account for microclimatic fluctuations inside housing structures, individual animal resilience factors (such as parity or stage of lactation), and the compounding influences of wind velocity and solar radiation exposure. Crucially, this macro-level index cannot capture key temporal dynamics, including the exact duration of daily exposure, the presence of nocturnal cooling recovery periods, and the cumulative physiological stress experienced by dairy cows over consecutive days of elevated thermal load. Consequently, the pooled estimators reflect approximate trend indicators rather than uniform, time-dependent biological endpoints.

Future primary trials must implement highly standardized reporting frameworks—incorporating precise descriptions of dietary fiber physics, cooling infrastructure specifications, and comprehensive microclimatic metrics—to clarify the residual variance currently left unexplained in global syntheses.

Conclusion

This meta-analysis synthesized data from 35 independent replication studies to evaluate the global statistical association between environmental thermal parameters and dairy cattle production traits. Our aggregated findings indicate that elevated Temperature-Humidity Index (THI) values coincide with statistically significant downward variations in both milk yield and macro-component quality. These observed performance declines appear particularly pronounced across database records representing high-yielding breeds such as Holsteins, reinforcing the relevance of systematic microclimatic monitoring within dairy production systems.

The relationship between elevated thermal exposure and milk composition was quantified across the synthesized literature. The observed negative shift in milk fat percentage aligns with wider physiological variations regarding systemic disruptions in lipid dynamics under thermal load. Concurrently, the pooled effect size for milk protein percentage (d = -0.555) reflects a directionally consistent global trend of lower protein content under elevated THI conditions—a variation frequently associated with cellular stress responses and altered nitrogen utilization in primary trials. These integrated findings highlight ambient thermal intensity as a practical risk proxy associated with variations in milk quality, confirming that the baseline configuration (THI > 72) serves as a reliable macro-environmental marker for broad, approximate production transitions.While establishing these global trends provides valuable contextual intelligence regarding broad thermal limits, the high trait-specific heterogeneity (I²= 89.17 % for milk yield and I² = 90.55% for milk fat) underscores that these thresholds reflect approximate regional baseline shifts rather than uniform biological point metrics. Conversely, the stabilization of variance at more moderate levels for milk protein (I² = 70.92%) and lactose (I² = 44.36%) models indicates distinct stabilization patterns across different biological traits under high thermal loads.

Future meta-analytic updates should prioritize high-granularity moderator analyses to systematically investigate the interactive dynamics between standardized THI thresholds, parity levels, and breed-specific climate resilience. Furthermore, cross-referencing global meta-analytic trends with emerging transcriptomic and proteomic datasets at the mammary tissue level will be essential to further clarify the molecular variations underlying altered milk protein dynamics. In conclusion, while the observed variance underscores the extreme biological complexity of heat-stress responses, the consistency of the threshold-based trends provides a robust basis for understanding broad production declines. Future efforts must focus on standardizing microclimatic and physiological reporting markers to further refine these performance variation estimates.

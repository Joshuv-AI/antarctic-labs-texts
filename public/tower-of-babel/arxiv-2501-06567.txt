Abstract
. In this paper, we systematically study the Fefferman-Stein inequality and Coifman-Fefferman inequality for the general commutators of singular integral operators that satisfy Hörmander conditions of Young type. Specifically, we first establish the pointwise sparse domination for these operators. Then, relying on the dyadic analysis, the Fefferman-Stein inequality with respect to arbitrary weights and the quantitative weighted Coifman-Fefferman inequality are demonstrated. We decouple the relationship between the number of commutators and the index ε \varepsilon , which essentially improved the results of Pérez and Rivera-Ríos (Israel J. Math., 2017). As applications, it is shown that all the aforementioned results can be applied to a wide range of operators, such as singular integral operators satisfying the L r L^{r} -Hörmander operators, ω \omega -Calderón-Zygmund operators with ω \omega satisfying a Dini condition, Calderón commutators, homogeneous singular integral operators and Fourier multipliers.



1.
Introduction and main results

The main purpose of this paper is to investigate the
weak type endpoint estimates with arbitrary weights and the quantitative weighted Coifman-Fefferman inequality for the general commutators of singular integral operators satisfying
L^{A}
-Hörmander conditions of non-convolution type.

1.1.
Definitions and notations

The singular integral operator
T
of convolution type with a kernel
K
, defined as

Tf(x)=p.v.\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{n}}}K(x-y)f(y)dy,

and bounded on
L^{2}
,
is widely recognized as a cornerstone of the classical Calderón-Zygmund theory.
The properties of
T
are intimately tied to the smoothness of the kernel
K
, with any variation in the smoothness condition directly influencing the behavior of
T
. Consequently, investigating the properties of
T
under different kernel conditions has become a significant area of research in Harmonic analysis. Below, we will review the development in this field based on the research progress under different kernel conditions.

In 1972, Coifman
[
10
]
showed that if the kernel
K
satisfies the Lipschitz condition:

(1.1)

|K(x-y)-K(x)|\leq C\frac{|y|^{\alpha}}{|x|^{\alpha+n}},\,0<\alpha<1,\,|x|>2|y|,

then the following Coifman type estimate holds,

(1.2)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|Tf(x)|^{p}w(x)dx\leq C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\big(Mf(x)\big)^{p}w(x)dx,\,\hbox{for any }0<p<\infty,\,w\in A_{\infty}.

Here
A_{\infty}
denotes the classical Muckenhoupt
A_{\infty}
class of weights, which will be introduced in Section
2
.

The Lipschitz condition for the Coifman type estimate (
1.2
) can be weakened to a class of Dini condition
[
28
]
. Recall that the homogeneous kernel
K(x)=\Omega(x/|x|)|x|^{-n}
is said to satisfy the Dini condition (denoted by
{\mathcal{H}}_{\rm Dini}
) if

\int_{0}^{1}w_{\infty}(t)\frac{dt}{t}<\infty,

where
w_{\infty}(t):=\sup\{|\Omega(\theta_{1})-\Omega(\theta_{2})|:\ \theta_{1},\theta_{2}\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1},\,|\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}|\leq t\}
.

To introduce more known results, we need to introduce the definition of
L^{r}
-Hörmander condition.

Definition 1.1

(
L^{r}
-Hörmander condition
,
[
28
]
)
.

A function
K
is called to satisfy
L^{r}
-Hörmander condition if there exist positive constants
c
and
C
such that for any
y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
R>c|y|
,

\displaystyle\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}(2^{m}R)^{\frac{n}{r^{\prime}}}\Big(\int_{2^{m}R<|x|<2^{m+1}R}|K(x-y)-K(x)|^{r}dx\Big)^{\frac{1}{r}}

\displaystyle\leq C<\infty,\quad 1\leq r<\infty;

\displaystyle\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}(2^{m}R)^{n}\sup_{\{x:\ 2^{m}R<|x|\leq 2^{m+1}R\}}|K(x-y)-K(x)|

\displaystyle\leq C<\infty,\quad r=\infty.

Let
{\mathcal{H}}_{r}
be the class of kernels satisfying the
L^{r}
-Hörmander condition, then the following including relationship holds:

{\mathcal{H}}_{\rm Dini}\subset{\mathcal{H}}_{\infty}\subset{\mathcal{H}}_{r}\subset{\mathcal{H}}_{s}\subset{\mathcal{H}}_{1},\,\,1<s<r<\infty.

It was Kurtz and Wheeden
[
28
]
, who first studied the weighted norm inequalities of the operator
T
with
K
satisfying
L^{r}
-Hörmander condition. Surprisingly, Martell, Pérez and Trujillo
[
43
]
showed that inequality (
1.2
) no longer holds if
K\in{\mathcal{H}}_{r}
for
1\leq r<\infty
.

In 2005, Lorente et al.
[
41
]
introduced the following
L^{A}
-Hörmander condition by replacing the
L^{r}
norm in Definition
1.1
with a more general Orlicz norm. Let
A
be a Young function (see Section
2.5
).
K
is said to satisfy the
L^{A}
-Hörmander condition if there exsit
c\geq 1
and
C>0
such that for any
y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
R>c|y|
,

(1.3)

\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}(2^{m}R)^{n}\left\|(K(\cdot-y)-K(\cdot))\chi_{2^{m}R<|x|\leq 2^{m+1}R}(\cdot)\right\|_{A,B(0,2^{m+1}R)}\leq C.

When the kernel satisfies the
L^{A}
-Hörmander condition, the Coifman type estimates, the weighted weak type endpoint estimates and two-weight estimates were established in
[
40
]
,
[
41
]
and
[
42
]
. More related works about
L^{r}
-Hörmander condition can be found in the references
[
56
,
59
]
. It is worth noting that the
L^{r}
-Hörmander condition also plays a crucial role in studying rough singular integral operators in the works of Watson
[
59
]
.

Consider now the singular integral operator
T
of non-convolution type. It is known that many important operators in Harmonic analysis are of non-convolution type, such as Calderón communators, non-standard singular integral operators, Coifman-Meyer type operators. Let
T
be a bounded linear operator on
L^{2}
, and for a bounded function
f
with compact support, we always assume that
T
can be represented in the form that

(1.4)

T(f)(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}K(x,y)f(y)dy,\quad x\notin{\rm supp}~f,

where the kernel
K:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash\{(x,x):x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a locally integrable function.
In 2008, Guo et al.
[
24
]
introduced the following non-convolution type
L^{r}
-Hörmander kernel condition in studying of the Riesz transform associated with the Schrödinger operators.

Definition 1.2

(
[
24
]
)
.

Let
K
be a locally integrable function, we say that
K
satisfies the
L^{r}
-Hörmander condition:

(1)
when
1\leq r<\infty
,

\displaystyle\sup_{Q}\sup_{x,z\in\frac{1}{2}Q}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}|2^{k}Q|\Big(\frac{1}{|2^{k}Q|}\int_{2^{k}Q\backslash 2^{k-1}Q}|K(x,y)-K(z,y)|^{r}dy\Big)^{1/r}

\displaystyle<\infty,

\displaystyle\sup_{Q}\sup_{x,z\in\frac{1}{2}Q}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}|2^{k}Q|\Big(\frac{1}{|2^{k}Q|}\int_{2^{k}Q\backslash 2^{k-1}Q}|K(y,x)-K(y,z)|^{r}dy\Big)^{1/r}

\displaystyle<\infty;

(2)
when
r=\infty
,

\displaystyle\sup_{Q}\sup_{x,z\in\frac{1}{2}Q}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}|2^{k}Q|\mathop{\mathrm{ess\,sup}}\limits_{y\in 2^{k}Q\backslash 2^{k-1}Q}|K(x,y)-K(z,y)|

\displaystyle<\infty,

\displaystyle\sup_{Q}\sup_{x,z\in\frac{1}{2}Q}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}|2^{k}Q|\mathop{\mathrm{ess\,sup}}\limits_{y\in 2^{k}Q\backslash 2^{k-1}Q}|K(y,x)-K(y,z)|

\displaystyle<\infty.

In multilinear setting, it was Li
[
37
]
who showed that the operators with the multilinear
L^{r}
-Hörmander condition can be dominated pointwisely by a finite number of sparse operators and satisfy the quantitative weighted estimates. Recently, Cao and Yabuta
[
8
]
obtained the quantitative weighted estimates for Littlewood-Paley square functions satisfying the multilinear
L^{r}
-Hörmander condition.

This paper focuses on the following non-convolution type
L^{A}
-Hörmander condition, which is an essential extension of the
L^{A}
-Hörmander condition (
1.3
).

Definition 1.3

(
L^{A}
-Hörmander condition of non-convolution type
)
.

Given a Young function
A
, define

\displaystyle H_{K,A,1}=\sup_{Q}\sup_{x,z\in\frac{1}{2}Q}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}|2^{k}Q|\left\|(K(x,\cdot)-K(z,\cdot))\chi_{2^{k}Q\backslash 2^{k-1}Q}\right\|_{A,2^{k}Q},

\displaystyle H_{K,A,2}=\sup_{Q}\sup_{x,z\in\frac{1}{2}Q}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}|2^{k}Q|\left\|(K(\cdot,x)-K(\cdot,z))\chi_{2^{k}Q\backslash 2^{k-1}Q}\right\|_{A,2^{k}Q},

if
H_{K,A}=\max\left\{H_{K,A,1},H_{K,A,2}\right\}<\infty
, then we call
K
satisfies
L^{A}
-Hörmander condition.

Let
{\mathcal{H}}_{A}
be the class of kernels satisfying the
L^{A}
-Hörmander condition
(
without ambiguity about the kernel function
K
, shorten
H_{K,A}
to
H_{A}
)
.
An operator
T
for which the kernel
K\in{\mathcal{H}}_{A}
satisfies (
1.4
) and the size condition
K(x,y)\leq C_{K}|x-y|^{-n},\,x\neq y
, is called an

A
-Hörmander operator.

1.2.
The general commutators

The study of commutators of Calderón-Zygmund operators can be traced back to the celebrated work given by Coifman, Rochberg and Weiss
[
11
]
, in which they defined the commutator
[b,T]
by

[b,T]f(x)=bT(f)(x)-T(bf)(x),

where
T
is the standard Calderón-Zygmund operator and
b
is a locally integrable function.
Although the initial interest in studying such operators was related to the generalization of the classical factorization theorem of Hardy spaces, many applications in other areas have also been discovered, especially in partial differential equations, as referenced in
[
18
,
23
]
. It is worth noting that the weak
(1,1)
estimate for
[b,T]
does not hold. However, it still enjoys certain weak
L\log L
type estimate (see
[
48
]
).

In
[
50
]
, Pérez and Pradolini introduced the high order commutator of
T
defined by

T^{m}_{b}f(x)=\big[b,T^{m-1}_{b}\big]f(x),\,T^{1}_{b}f(x)=[b,T]f(x),

and they established the weak type endpoint estimates for
T^{m}_{b}
with arbitrary weights.

Theorem A

(
[
50
]
)
.

Let
T
be a Calderón-Zygmund operator and
m\in\mathbb{N}
. Assume that
b\in\mathrm{BMO}
, then for every weight
w
and
0<\varepsilon<1
,

(1.5)

w\big(\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:|T^{m}_{b}f(x)|>\lambda\}\big)\leq C_{\varepsilon,T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Phi_{m}\Big(\frac{\|b\|^{m}_{\mathrm{BMO}}|f(x)|}{\lambda}\Big)M_{L(\log L)^{m+\varepsilon}}w(x)dx

holds for all
\lambda>0
, where
\Phi_{m}(t)=t\log^{m}(e+t)
.

In 2002, Pérez and Trujillo-González
[
52
]
first defined the general commutator as follows

(1.6)

T_{\vec{b}}f(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\prod_{j=1}^{m}(b_{j}(x)-b_{j}(y))K(x,y)f(y)dy,

where
\vec{b}=(b_{1},\ldots,b_{m})
is a finite family of locally integrable functions. It is worth mentioning that weighted strong as well as weak type endpoint estimates for
T_{\vec{b}}
were given in
[
52
]
.

Remark 1.4
.

Note that when
m=1
,
T_{\vec{b}}
coincides with the classical linear commutator
[b,T]
; If
b_{i}=b,\,i=1,\ldots,m
, then
T_{\vec{b}}
exactly is
m
order iterated commutators
T^{m}_{b}
; Assume that
\vec{\alpha}=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{m})\in\mathbb{N}^{m}
, if we take
b_{1}=\cdots=b_{\alpha_{1}},\ldots,b_{m}=\cdots=b_{\alpha_{m}}
, in this case,

T_{\vec{b}}f(x)=T_{\vec{b},\vec{\alpha}}f(x):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\prod_{j=1}^{m}(b_{j}(x)-b_{j}(y))^{\alpha_{j}}f(y)dy.

Recently, using extrapolation techniques, the weighted boundedness results for
T_{\vec{b},\vec{\alpha}}
have been obtained in
[
6
]
.

1.3.
Historical background

In the weighted theory, the Fefferman-Stein inequality and Coifman-Fefferman inequality have attracted the attention of many researchers. Now we briefly review the background of these two inequalities.

\bullet
Fefferman-Stein inequality.
The origin of the Fefferman-Stein inequality can be traced back to the celebrated work of Fefferman and Stein
[
19
]
. They obtained the following endpoint estimate for the classical Hardy-Littlewood maximal operator
M
,

(1.7)

w\big(\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:|Mf(x)|>\lambda\}\big)\leq C_{n}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|f(x)|}{\lambda}Mw(x)dx,

where
w
is an arbitrary weight and the constant
C_{n}
only depends on the dimension
n
. Later on, Muckenhoupt and Wheeden conjectured that a similar estimate holds for any Calderón-Zygmund singular integral operator
T
, namely, there exists a constant
C
such that for any weight
w
and
\lambda>0
,

(1.8)

w\big(\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:|Tf(x)|>\lambda\}\big)\leq C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|f(x)|}{\lambda}Mw(x)dx,

which is known as the
Muckenhoupt-Wheeden conjecture
.

Recall that
w
satisfies Muckenhoupt
A_{1}
condition (or belongs to the class
A_{1}
) if there exists
c>0
such that
Mw(x)\leq[w]_{A_{1}}w(x),\,\text{a.e.}\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, then (
1.8
) implies that for any
w\in A_{1}
,

(1.9)

w\big(\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:|Tf(x)|>\lambda\}\big)\leq C[w]_{A_{1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|f(x)|}{\lambda}w(x)dx,

which is referred to as the
weak conjecture of Muckenhoupt and Wheeden
.

In recent decades, Numerous significant investigations have been made in these field from different perspective.
It was Chanillo and Wheeden
[
9
]
who verified that the inequality (
1.8
) holds when
T
is a square function; Later on, (
1.8
) was shown to be true by Buckley
[
7
]
if
w_{\delta}(x)=|x|^{-n(1-\delta)},\,0<\delta<1
; However, in 2011, Reguera
[
54
]
demonstrated that the
dyadic version of the Muckenhoupt-Wheeden conjecture is not valid for the Haar multiplier. Building upon this, Reguera and Thiele
[
55
]
proved that (
1.8
) does not hold for the Hilbert transform, which disproves the Muckenhoupt-Wheeden conjecture. It was not until 2015 that Nazarov et al.
[
45
]
constructed a “smooth” weight function for the Hilbert transform and proved that (
1.9
) is not valid, consequently refuting the weaker version of the Muckenhoupt-Wheeden conjecture. Further studies on the Muckenhoupt-Wheeden conjecture can be found in
[
14
,
34
]
and the references therein.

Based on the above extensive studies of Muckenhoupt-Wheeden conjecture, people began to pay attention to the deformation of Fefferman-Stein inequality and its dependence on the weight constant, as follows:

\bullet

If
M
in (
1.8
) is replaced by a slightly larger operator, does (
1.8
) hold?

\bullet

What is the dependence between the constant in (
1.9
) and the
A_{1}
weight constant?

An affirmative answer to the first questionwas given by Pérez. In
[
47
]
, he proved that if the Hardy-Littlewood maximal operator
M
on right-hand side is replaced by

M^{2}
(
M^{2}=M\circ M
) or even by the operator

M_{L(\log L)^{\varepsilon}}
with any
\varepsilon>0
, then (
1.8
) remains valid, that is,

w\big(\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:|Tf(x)|>\lambda\}\big)\leq C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|f(x)|}{\lambda}M^{2}w(x)dx.

For the second question,
Lerner, Ombrosi and Pérez
[
35
]
based on a particular case of the Coifman type estimate, proved that for any
w\in A_{1}
,

w\big(\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:|Tf(x)|>\lambda\}\big)\leq C[w]_{A_{1}}\log(e+[w]_{A_{1}})\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|f(x)|}{\lambda}w(x)dx.

It was shown by Lerner et al.
[
33
]
that the logarithm type dependence on the constants of the weight is necessary.

As mentioned above, the commutators exhibit more singularity than the operators themselves, so the Fefferman-Stein inequality with respect to commutators naturally become a subject worthy of intensive study. After Pérez and Pradolini
[
50
]
obtained (
1.5
) in 2001, Pérez and Rivera-Ríos
[
51
]
further considered the weak type endpoint estimates of multilinear commutators
T_{\vec{b}}
, the results are as follows:

Theorem B

(
[
51
]
)
.

Let
T
be a Calderón-Zygmund operator and
m\in\mathbb{N}
. Assume that
\vec{b}=(b_{1},\ldots,b_{m})\in\mathrm{BMO}^{m}
, then for every weight
w
and
0<\varepsilon<1
,

(1.10)

w\big(\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:|T_{\vec{b}}f(x)|>\lambda\}\big)\lesssim\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{m+1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Phi_{m}\Big(\frac{\|\vec{b}\|_{\mathrm{BMO}}|f(x)|}{\lambda}\Big)M_{L(\log L)^{m+\varepsilon}}w(x)dx,

where
\Phi_{m}(t)=t\log^{m}(e+t)
and
\big\|\vec{b}\big\|_{\mathrm{BMO}}=\prod_{i=1}^{m}\|b_{i}\|_{\mathrm{BMO}}
.

It is observed that the right-hand side of (
1.10
) has an exact estimate with a constant
\varepsilon
. In particular, when the weight function
w
belongs to
A_{\infty}
or
A_{1}
, utilizing the reverse Hölder inequality immediately yields a quantitative
weighted estimates for the commutators, which coincides with
[
46
, Theorem 2.9]
. By examining (
1.10
), we can also note that the exponent of
\varepsilon
depends on the iteration number
m
of the commutators and when
m=1
, the constant on the right-hand side is
\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}
.
In 2017, Lerner, Ombrosi and Rivera-Ríos
[
36
]
proved that the result still holds for
\frac{1}{\varepsilon}
when
m=1
, which improved (
1.10
).

\bullet
Coifman-Fefferman inequality.
The inequality of the form (
1.2
) is called the Coifman-Fefferman inequality. The estimate can be viewed as a certain control of the Hardy-Littlewood maximal function
M
with respect to the operator
T
, which implies that
M
can be used to characterize the weighted boundedness of
T
in some extent. One of the most usual techniques for proving such results is to establish a good-
\lambda
inequality between
T
and
M
, which leads to an unavailability of the constant
C
with respect to the dependence of the weight
w
or the parameter
p
in the detailing. To overcome this shortcoming, many experts have considered the alternative approaches to prove (
1.2
). For instance, the proof presented in
[
1
]
uses a pointwise estimate involving the sharp maximal function; The proof in
[
16
]
utilizes an extrapolation techniques avoiding
good-
\lambda
inequality that enables the derivation of estimates like (
1.2
) for any
A_{1}
weights.

Due to the fact that the Coifman-Fefferman inequality can be utilized in the proofs of numerous other weighted norm inequalities, it plays a crucial role in the weighted theory.
Firstly, as mentioned above, (
1.2
) can be used to characterize the weighted boundedness of
T
, i.e., if
T
satisfies (
1.2
), then
T
is bounded on
L^{p}(w)
for any weight
w\in A_{p},1<p<\infty
. It is not widely known that the inequality (
1.2
) has been proven to be the crucial estimate in establishing the following non-standard two-weight result for
T
[
47
]
, namely, for any weight
w
, it holds that

(1.11)

\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{n}}}|Tf(x)|^{p}w(x)dx\leq c\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{n}}}|f(x)|^{p}M^{\lfloor p\rfloor+1}w(x)dx,

where
\lfloor p\rfloor
denotes the integer part of
p
and
M^{\lfloor p\rfloor+1}
means the
(\lfloor p\rfloor+1)
-fold composition of
M
. In fact, (
1.11
) is actually a strong type Fefferman-Stein inequality, and this discovery also illustrates the connection between two inequalities. Another essential consequence of inequality (
1.2
) is the crucial role it plays in the solution of Sawyer’s conjecture, see
[
15
]
for more details. In addition, (
1.2
) is closely related to the
C_{p}
theory, which has been explored in
[
44
]
and
[
57
]
.

In recent years, how to specify the dependence of the implicit constants on the weight
w
of the Coifman-Fefferman inequality has become an interesting topic. In this direction, Lerner et al.
[
35
]
obtained the explicit dependence of the constant with respect
w\in A_{p},1\leq p<\infty
. Later on,
the quantitative Coifman-Fefferman inequalities were proved for a variety of singular operators,
including vector valued extensions and multilinear
Calderón-Zygmund operators in
[
46
]
.
In 2019, the quantitative Coifman-Fefferman inequality about rough singular integral operator
T_{\Omega}
with
\Omega\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})
was established in
[
38
]
. Since no good-
\lambda
estimate relating
T_{\Omega}
and
M
is known to hold, instead, the proof of this result is a consequence of the sparse domination for
T_{\Omega}
together with the technique of principal cubes. Very recently, Lerner
[
31
]
gives a sufficient condition to describe the Coifman-Fefferman inequality of the non-degenerate singular integral operator.

The Coifman-Fefferman inequality of the commutator of Calderón-Zygmund operator with a
\rm BMO
function was given in
[
49
]
:

\|[b,T]f\|_{L^{p}(w)}\leq c\|b\|_{\rm{BMO}}\|M^{2}f\|_{L^{p}(w)},\,\hbox{for any }w\in A_{\infty}.

In 2012, Ortiz-Caraballo et al.
[
46
]
derived the following quantitative weighted inequality, which was achieved via establishing a pointwise domination of commutators.

Theorem C

(
[
46
]
)
.

Let
T
be a Calderón-Zygmund operator,
b\in{\rm BMO}
and
w\in A_{\infty}
. For the
m
-th
(m\geq 2)
commutator
T^{m}_{b}
, there exists a constant
c_{n,T}
such that

\|T^{m}_{b}f\|_{L^{p}(w)}\leq c_{n,T}2^{m}\max\{1,p^{m+1}\}[w]^{m+1}_{A_{\infty}}\|b\|^{m}_{\rm BMO}\|M^{m+1}f\|_{L^{p}(w)},\,0<p<\infty.

1.4.
Motivation

It is universally acknowledged that sparse domination, owing to its localization and sparseness, has become a crucial and effective tool in quantitative weighted Fefferman-Stein inequality and Coifman-Fefferman inequality over the past few decades. One of its remarkable improvement was Lerner’s demonstration
[
29
,
30
]
that the Calderón-Zygmund operator can be dominated by the sparse operators
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{S}}
as follows:

\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{S}}f(x)=\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{S}}\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}|f(x)|dx\chi_{Q}(x),

where each
Q
is a cube with its sides parallel to the axis and
\mathcal{S}
is a sparse family.
This crucial fact facilitates a streamlined and alternative approach in proving the
A_{2}
theorem which was initially established by Hytönen
[
26
]
. Subsequently, Lerner and Nazarov
[
32
]
, Conde-Alonso and Rey
[
13
]
independently demonstrated that the Calderón-Zygmund operator
T
can be dominated pointwisely by a finite number of sparse operators in the way that

|Tf(x)|\leq c_{n,T}\sum_{j=1}^{3^{n}}\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{S}_{j}}f(x).

The ideas of sparse domination have been applied extensively to a wide range of extensions, such as commutators
[
36
]
, rough singular integrals
[
12
]
, Bochner-Riesz multipliers
[
5
]
and so on. We refer the interested reader to
[
4
,
8
,
37
,
58
]
and the references therein.

As mentioned above, Lerner, Ombrosi and Rivera-Ríos
[
36
]
obtained the Fefferman-Stein inequalities by establishing the pointwise sparse domination of
[b,T]
, as stated in the following theorem.

Theorem D

(
[
36
]
)
.

Let
T
be an
\omega
-Calderón-Zygmund operator
(
for the definition, see Section
2
)
and
\omega
satisfies Dini condition:
\int_{0}^{1}\omega(t)\frac{dt}{t}<\infty
. If
b\in{\mathrm{BMO}}
, then for every weight
w
and
0<\varepsilon<1
,

w\big(\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\big|[b,T]f(x)\big|>\lambda\}\big)\lesssim\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Phi\Big(\frac{\|b\|_{\mathrm{BMO}}|f(x)|}{\lambda}\Big)M_{L(\log L)^{1+\varepsilon}}w(x)dx.

In particular, when
w\in A_{1}
,

w\big(\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\big|[b,T]f(x)\big|>\lambda\}\big)\lesssim[w]_{A_{1}}\Phi([w]_{A_{\infty}})\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Phi\Big(\frac{\|b\|_{\mathrm{BMO}}|f(x)|}{\lambda}\Big)w(x)dx,

where
\Phi(t)=t\log(e+t)
.

Our motivation orginates from the following three aspects:

(1).
The study of
A
-Hörmander operators and their commutators has drawn significant attention due to their deep connections with weighted inequalities. In 2008, Lorente et al.
[
42
]
made substantial progress by establishing the Coifman-Fefferman inequality for commutators associated with
A_{\infty}
weights. Subsequently, they further extended these results
[
40
]
by proving the Fefferman-Stein inequality under the framework of maximal functions dependent on a Young function
A
. More recently, Ibañez-Firnkorn and Rivera-Ríos
[
27
]
employed the sparse domination technique to obtain various quantitative weighted results for
A
-Hörmander operator
T
and the
m
order iterated commutators
T^{m}_{b}
, including strong weighted estimates, Coifman-Fefferman inequalities, weak-type endpoint estimates, and local exponential decay estimates. As a notable application, they generalized prior results from
[
36
]
by proving Fefferman-Stein inequalities for
T^{m}_{b}
, where
T
is assumed to be an
\omega
-Calderón-Zygmund operator. These developments have significantly expanded our understanding of the interplay between Hörmander-type conditions, Young functions, and the underlying properties of operators and their kernels.

While these advances are primarily focused on the commutators
T^{m}_{b}
, it remains an open problem whether these results can be extended to more general framework. In particular, what kinds of sparse domination and weak type endpoint estimates with arbitrary weights does
T_{\vec{b}}
satisfy forms our first motivation.

(2).
A further challenge arises in understanding the precise dependence of the exponent
\varepsilon
appearing in Theorem B, particularly its relationship with the parameters
m
and the number of functions
b_{i}
involved in the commutators
T_{\vec{b}}
.
Theorem B indicates that
\varepsilon
is inherently tied to
m
and Theorem D holds only in the case
m=1
, which limits the flexibility and generality of the estimates, especially when dealing with higher-order commutators or varying numbers of functions
b_{i}
. This dependence becomes particularly problematic when considering higher-order commutators or scenarios with varying numbers of functions
b
, as it obscures the individual influence of these parameters on the behavior of
\varepsilon
.

Our second motivation is to decouple the dependence of
\varepsilon
on
m
. Such a decoupling would not only provide deeper insight into the behavior of
\varepsilon
but also allow for sharper and more refined estimates that better capture the individual contributions of
m
and the number of
b_{i}
’s.

(3).
Our third motivation is to improve the precision of the constants and to extend the range of indices in the weighted Coifman-Fefferman inequalities for
T_{b}^{m}
or
T_{\vec{b}}
. By refining the dependence of the constants on the parameters of the operators and the weights, as well as expanding the scope of the inequalities to a broader class of indices, we aim to provide a more comprehensive and flexible framework for understanding these inequalities. This will help to understand the intricate relationship between the structure of
T_{\vec{b}}
, the weights, and the associated norm inequalities.

1.5.
Main results

In this paper, we first establish a pointwise sparse domination principle for the general commutators of
A
-Hörmander operators, providing a useful tool for their quantitative analysis. As a key application of this framework, we derive the Fefferman-Stein inequality in the context of arbitrary weights. Furthermore, we develop a quantitative version of the weighted Coifman-Fefferman inequality, offering sharper constants and a broader range of applicability. These results extend and unify many previous works, which contribute to a more comprehensive and profound understanding of the theory of commutators in the weighted setting.

Before stating our results, we need to give a definition as follows.

Definition 1.5
.

Let
1\leq p_{0}\leq p_{1}<\infty
. Define the class of functions
\mathcal{Y}(p_{0},p_{1})
as the class of functions
A
for which there exsit constants

c_{A,p_{0}},~c_{A,p_{1}}
and
t_{A}\geq 1
such that

t^{p_{0}}\leq c_{A,p_{0}}A(t),~t>t_{A};\quad t^{p_{1}}\leq c_{A,p_{1}}A(t),~t\leq t_{A}.

We now present several examples of Young functions that belong to
\mathcal{Y}(p_{0},p_{1})
:

(1)

If
A(t)=t^{r},\,1<r<\infty
, then
A\in\mathcal{Y}(r,r)
;

(2)

Let
A(t)=t\log^{\alpha}(e+t),\,\alpha>0
, we have
A\in\mathcal{Y}(1,1)
;

(3)

When
A(t)=t\log^{\alpha}(e+t)\log^{\beta}(e+\log(e+t)),\,\alpha,\beta>0
, then
A\in\mathcal{Y}(1,1)
.

The above examples essentially cover several types of Young functions that we want to investigate. Therefore, the requirement that
A\in\mathcal{Y}(p_{0},p_{1})
does not constitute a substantial constraint on our conclusions.

Now we are ready to state our main results. The first one is the following pointwise sparse domination for the general commutators of
A
-Hörmander operators.

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
m\in\mathbb{N}
and
A
be a Young function. Assume that
T
is an
\bar{A}
-Hörmander operators. If
A\in\mathcal{Y}(p_{0},p_{1})~(1\leq p_{0}\leq p_{1}<\infty)
, then for any locally integrable functions
\vec{b}=(b_{1},\ldots,b_{m})
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and bounded function
f
with compact support, there exist
3^{n}
sparse families
\{\mathcal{S}_{j}\}_{j=1}^{3^{n}}
such that

(1.12)

|T_{\vec{b}}f(x)|\leq C_{n,m}C_{T}\sum_{j=1}^{3^{n}}\sum_{\vec{\gamma}\in\{0,1\}^{m}}\mathcal{A}^{\vec{\gamma}}_{A,\mathcal{S}_{j}}(\vec{b},f)(x),\quad\text{a.e.}\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

where
C_{T}=c_{n,p_{0},p_{1}}\max\{c_{A,p_{0}},c_{A,p_{1}}\}\big(H_{\bar{A}}+\|T\|_{L^{2}\rightarrow L^{2}}\big)
and

\mathcal{A}^{\vec{\gamma}}_{A,\mathcal{S}_{j}}(\vec{b},f)(x)=\begin{cases}\sum\limits_{Q\in\mathcal{S}_{j}}\big\|\prod_{i=1}^{m}(b_{i}-\langle b_{i}\rangle_{Q})f\big\|_{A,Q}\chi_{Q}(x),&\vec{\gamma}=\vec{0},\\
\sum\limits_{Q\in\mathcal{S}_{j}}\prod\limits_{s:\gamma_{s}=1}\big|b_{s}(x)-\langle b_{s}\rangle_{Q}\big|\Big\|\prod\limits_{s:\gamma_{s}=0}(b_{s}-\langle b_{s}\rangle_{Q})f\Big\|_{A,Q}\chi_{Q}(x),&\vec{\gamma}\neq\vec{0}.\end{cases}

For the endpoint case, using Theorem
1.6
, we establish the weak type Fefferman-Stein inequality with arbitrary weights of the general commutator
T_{\vec{b}}
.

Theorem 1.7
.

Let
m\in\mathbb{N}
and
\vec{b}=(b_{1},\ldots,b_{m})\in\mathrm{BMO}^{m}
with
\|b_{i}\|_{\mathrm{BMO}}=1,i=1,\ldots,m
. Assume that
A_{0},A_{1},\ldots,A_{m}
are Young functions satisfying
A_{0}\in\mathcal{Y}(p_{0},p_{1})~(1\leq p_{0}\leq p_{1}<\infty)
and
A^{-1}_{i}(t)\bar{A}^{-1}_{i-1}(t)C^{-1}(t)\leq ct,t\geq 1,i=1,\ldots,m
, where
C(t)=e^{t}-1
.
If
T
is an
\bar{A}_{0}
-Hörmander operator and
A_{i}
is submultiplicative, i.e.,
A_{i}(xy)\leq A_{i}(x)A_{i}(y)
, then for any weight
w
and every family of Young functions
\varphi_{\vec{l}}:=\varphi_{(l_{1},l_{2})},\,0\leq l_{2}<l_{1}\leq m
, it holds that

\displaystyle w\big(\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:|T_{\vec{b}}f(x)|>\lambda\}\big)

\displaystyle\lesssim K_{\varphi_{(m,m)}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}A_{m}\Big(\frac{|f(x)|}{\lambda}\Big)M_{\varphi_{(m,m)}}w(x)dx

\displaystyle+\sum_{l_{1}=1}^{m}\sum_{l_{2}=0}^{l_{1}-1}K_{\varphi_{(l_{1},l_{2})}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}A_{m-l_{1}}\Big(\frac{|f(x)|}{\lambda}\Big)M_{\Phi_{l_{1}-l_{2}}\circ\varphi_{(l_{1},l_{2})}}w(x)dx,

with

(1.13)

K_{\varphi_{(l_{1},l_{2})}}=\begin{cases}c_{n}+\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{\varphi^{-1}_{(m,m)}(t)A_{m}(\log^{2}(e+t))}{t^{2}\log^{3}(e+t)}dt,&l_{1}=l_{2}=m,\\
c_{n}+\beta_{m,l_{1},l_{2}}+\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{\varphi^{-1}_{(l_{1},l_{2})}\circ\Phi^{-1}_{l_{1}-l_{2}}(t)A_{m-l_{1}}(\log^{2l_{1}+1}(e+t))}{t^{2}\log^{l_{1}+2}(e+t)}dt,&\text{others},\end{cases}

where
\beta_{m,l_{1},l_{2}}
is an absolute constant and
\Phi_{i}(t)=t\log^{i}(e+t)
.

Remark 1.8
.

We now make some comments on Theorem
1.7
. First of all, let
b_{i}=b,\,i=1,\ldots,m
, then the results of Theorem
1.7
cover the conclusions in
[
27
, Theorem 2.7]
.
Secondly, if
m=0
, that is,
T_{\vec{b}}=T
,
[
40
, Theorem 3.1]
proved that the
\bar{A}_{0}
-Hörmander operator
T
satisfied the following Fefferman-Stein inequality under some certain conditions:

(1.14)

w\big(\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:|Tf(x)|>\lambda\}\big)\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|f(x)|}{\lambda}M_{A_{0}}w(x)dx.

We note that the conclusions of Theorem
1.7
are complementary to (
1.14
).
Indeed, Martell, Pérez, and Trujillo
[
43
]
demonstrated that the
\mathcal{H}_{r}
condition is not sufficiently indicative of the validity of the
A_{1}
conjecture. In particular, the weak-type
A_{1}
estimate for the operator
T
cannot be directly deduced from (
1.14
). This limitation stems from the fact that the Orlicz maximal operators on the right-hand side of (
1.14
) are constrained by the choice of the initial Young function
A_{0}
. In contrast, the Orlicz maximal operators considered in our results are not subject to such restrictions. Moreover, in Theorem
1.7
, the Young function
A_{0}
is coupled with the function
f
, which is in stark contrast to the scenario in (
1.14
), where
f
remains independent of
A_{0}
. This distinction underscores a key difference: the dependency of the weight on the Young function in (
1.14
) imposes limitations on flexibility, whereas our results exhibit greater generality by avoiding such dependencies.
Therefore, these two results can be viewed as complementary.

Finally, comparing Theorem
1.7
with
[
40
, Theorem 3.8]
, our results establish a connection between the boundedness constant and the family of Young functions
\varphi_{\vec{l}}:=\varphi_{(l_{1},l_{2})},\,0\leq l_{2}<l_{1}\leq m
, which lays the groundwork for deriving the subsequent Theorem
1.9
.

Using Theorem
1.7
, we can obtain the Fefferman-Stein inequalities for the general commutators of
\omega
-Calderón-Zygmund operators with arbitrary weights.

Theorem 1.9
.

Let
m\in\mathbb{N}
and
\vec{b}=(b_{1},\ldots,b_{m})\in\mathrm{BMO}^{m}

T
is an
\omega
-Calderón-Zygmund operator with

\int_{0}^{1}\omega(t)\frac{dt}{t}<\infty

(1.15)

w\big(\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:|T_{\vec{b}}f(x)|>\lambda\}\big)\lesssim\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Phi_{m}\Big(\frac{\big\|\vec{b}\big\|_{\mathrm{BMO}}|f(x)|}{\lambda}\Big)M_{L(\log L)^{m}(\log\log L)^{1+\varepsilon}}w(x)dx,

where
\Phi_{m}(t)=t\log^{m}(e+t)
.

Remark 1.10
.

First of all, in terms of operators, note that when
\omega(t)=t^{\delta}
for some

-Calderón-Zygmund operator coincides with the classical Calderón-Zygmund operator, which means that our results is an essential extension of Theorem B. Secondly, in terms of constant, compared with

and the number

of the commutator, that is, the constant

shows that Theorem
1.9

[

Q
and whose side length is
a
times that of
Q
. Given a function
f
and a cube
Q
,
\langle f\rangle_{Q}
denotes the average of
f
on
Q
, that is,
\langle f\rangle_{Q}=\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}f(x)dx
. For a fixed
p
with
1\leq p<\infty
,
p^{\prime}
denotes the dual exponent of
p

p^{\prime}=p/(p-1)
. For a multi-index
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n})\in\mathbb{N}^{n}

2.
Preliminary

In this section, we present some definitions, fundamental facts and lemmas which will be used later.

2.1.
\omega
-Calderón-Zygmund operators

Let
\omega:[0,1]\rightarrow[0,\infty)
be a modulus of continuity, that is, a continuous, increasing, submultiplicative function with
\omega(0)=0
.

Recall that
T
is called an
\omega
-Calderón-Zygmund operator if it
is linear, bounded on
L^{2}
and admits the following representation

Tf(x)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{n}}}K(x,y)f(y)dy,\,\,x\notin\operatorname{supp}f,

where
f\in L^{1}_{\rm{loc}}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
and
K
, defined away from the diagonal
x=y
in
{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
, is a locally integrable function, satisfying the size condition

|K(x,y)|\leq\frac{c_{K}}{|x-y|^{n}},\,\,x\neq y,

and a smoothness condition

|K(x,y)-K(x^{\prime},y)|+|K(y,x)-K(y,x^{\prime})|\leq\omega\Big(\frac{|x-x^{\prime}|}{|x-y|}\Big)\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n}},

for
|x-y|>2|x-x^{\prime}|
.

2.2.
Sparse family

In this subsection, we will introduce a very useful tool named dyadic calculus, which was systematically studied in
[
32
]
.

Given a cube
Q\subset{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
, let
\mathcal{D}(Q)
denote the set of all dyadic cubes with respect to
Q
, namely, the cubes from
\mathcal{D}(Q)
are generated by repeating subdivision of
Q
and its descendants in
2^{n}
cubes with the same length.

Definition 2.1
.

A family of cubes
\mathcal{D}
is called to be a dyadic lattice if it satisfies the following properties:

(1)

if
Q\in\mathcal{D}
, then every descendant of
Q
is in
\mathcal{D}
as well;

(2)

for every two cubes
Q_{1},Q_{2}\in\mathcal{D}
, there exists a common ancestor
Q\in\mathcal{D}
such that
Q_{1},Q_{2}\in\mathcal{D}(Q)
;

(3)

for every compact set
K\subset{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
, there exists a cube
Q\in\mathcal{D}
such that
K\subset Q
.

Note that, if
Q_{0}\in\mathcal{D}
, then every cube in
\mathcal{D}(Q_{0})
will also belong to
\mathcal{D}
.

The following Three Lattice Theorem, a fundamental concept in dyadic calculus (as outlined in
[
32
, Theorem 3.1]
), palys a crucial role in elucidating the intricate structure of dyadic lattices.

Lemma 2.2

(
[
32
]
)
.

Given a dyadic lattice
\mathcal{D}
, there exist
3^{n}
dyadic lattices
\{\mathcal{D}_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}

such that

\{3Q:Q\in\mathcal{D}\}=\bigcup_{j=1}^{3^{n}}\mathcal{D}_{j}

and for each cube
Q\in\mathcal{D}
, there
exists a unique cube
R_{Q}
in some
\mathcal{D}_{j}
such that
Q\subset R_{Q}
and
3l_{Q}=l_{R_{Q}}
.

Remark 2.3
.

Given a dyadic lattice
\mathcal{D}
. For an arbitrary cube
Q\subset{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
, there exists a cube
Q^{\prime}\in\mathcal{D}
such that
\frac{l_{Q}}{2}<l_{Q^{\prime}}\leq l_{Q}
and
Q\subset 3Q^{\prime}
.
It follows from Lemma
2.2
that
3Q^{\prime}=P\in\mathcal{D}_{j}
for some
j\in\{1,\ldots,m\}
. Therefore,
for every cube
Q\subset{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
, we can find a
P\in\mathcal{D}_{j}
for some
j
such that
Q\subset P
and
l_{P}\leq 3l_{Q}
.

With the preceding definition and lemma in hand, we are ready to give the definition of sparse family.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
\mathcal{D}
be a dyadic lattice.
\mathcal{S}\subset\mathcal{D}
is called a
\eta
-sparse family with
0<\eta<1
if for every cube
Q\in\mathcal{S}
, there exists a measurable subset
E_{Q}\subset Q
such that
\eta|Q|\leq|E_{Q}|
, where the
\{E_{Q}\}
are pairwise disjoint.

2.3.
Sharp maximal functions

For a locally integrable function
f
, The basic tools we will use are the standard Hardy-Littlewood maximal function and the sharp maximal function of Fefferman and Stein
[
20
]
defined by

M(f)(x)=\sup_{Q\ni x}\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}|f(y)|dy,

and

M^{\#}(f)(x)=\sup_{Q\ni x}\inf_{c\in\mathbb{R}}\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}|f(y)-c|dy\approx\sup_{Q\ni x}\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}|f(y)-\langle f\rangle_{Q}|dy.

where the supremum is taken over all cubes
Q
containing
x
.
For
0<\delta<\infty
, denote
M_{\delta}
and
M^{\#}_{\delta}
by

M_{\delta}(f)(x)=(M_{\delta}(|f|^{\delta})(x))^{1/\delta}=\Big(\sup_{Q\ni x}\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}|f(y)|^{\delta}dy\Big)^{1/\delta},

and

M^{\#}_{\delta}(f)(x)=(M^{\#}(|f|^{\delta})(x))^{1/\delta}.

If we restrict our considerations to dyadic cubes, we adopt the following notations accordingly
M^{\#,d}
,
M^{d}_{\delta}
and
M^{\#,d}_{\delta}
.
The relationship between
M^{d}_{\delta}
and
M^{\#,d}_{\delta}
is given in
[
46
, p.10]
.

Lemma 2.5

(
[
46
]
)
.

Let
0<p<\infty,0<\delta<1
, and
w\in A_{\infty}
. Then there exists a constant
C
only depending on
n
such that

\|M^{d}_{\delta}(f)\|_{L^{p}(w)}\leq C\max\{1,p\}[w]_{A_{\infty}}\|M^{\#,d}_{\delta}(f)\|_{L^{p}(w)},

for any function
f
such that the left-hand side is finite.

2.4.
The classical weights

This subsection primarily aims to introduce the pertinent definitions of classical weights and the weak
A_{\infty}
class, along with their key properties. Let us start with the Muckenhoupt weights.

We call that a nonnegative and locally integrable function
w
on
{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
belongs to the Muckenhoupt class
A_{p}
with
1<p<\infty
, if

[w]_{A_{p}}:=\Big(\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}w(x)dx\Big)\Big(\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}w^{1-p^{\prime}}(x)dx\Big)^{p-1}<\infty.

For
p=1
, a weight
w
belongs to the class
A_{1}
if there exsits a constant
C
such that

\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}w(x)dx\leq C\mathop{\mathrm{ess\,inf}}\limits_{x\in Q}w(x),

and the infimum of these constants
C
is called the
A_{1}
constant of
w
, denoted by
[w]_{A_{1}}
. Since the
A_{p}
classes are increasing with respect to
p
, the
A_{\infty}
class of weights is defined in a natural way by

A_{\infty}=\bigcup_{p>1}A_{p}.

It is not clear how to characterize the class
A_{\infty}
in terms of a constant from the definition of
A_{\infty}
. However, it was proved by Fujji
[
21
]
and Wilson
[
60
]

that a weight
w\in A_{\infty}
if and only if

[w]_{A_{\infty}}:=\sup_{Q}\frac{1}{w(Q)}\int_{Q}M(w\chi_{Q})(x)dx<\infty,

which is known as the Fujii-Wilson
A_{\infty}
constant. For
A_{\infty}
weights, it turns out to the following lemma can be regarded as a characterization of its quantitative version.

Lemma 2.6

(
[
27
]
)
.

There exists
c_{n}>0
such that for every
w\in A_{\infty}
, every cube
Q
and every measurable subset
E\subset Q
, it holds that

\frac{w(E)}{w(Q)}\leq 2\Big(\frac{|E|}{|Q|}\Big)^{\frac{1}{c_{n}[w]_{A_{\infty}}}}.

Now we introduce a class of weights which genuinely weakens the
A_{\infty}
class. A weight
w
is said to belong to weak
A_{\infty}
class, written
w\in A^{weak}_{\infty}
, if there exist
0<c,\delta<\infty
such that the estimate

w(E)\leq c\Big(\frac{|E|}{|Q|}\Big)^{\delta}w(2Q)

holds for every cube and every measurable set
E\subset Q
. Similar to the Fujii-Wilson
A_{\infty}
constant,
w\in A^{weak}_{\infty}
if and only if

[w]^{weak}_{A_{\infty}}:=\sup_{Q}\frac{1}{w(2Q)}\int_{Q}M(w\chi_{Q})(x)dx<\infty.

It has been demonstrated in
[
2
]
that the constant 2 in the average could be replaced by any parameter
\sigma>1
and the following reverse Hölder’s inequality is valid.

Lemma 2.7

(
[
2
]
)
.

Let
w\in A^{weak}_{\infty}
, then for all cubes
Q
in
{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
with sides parallel to the axes,

\Big(\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}w^{r(w)}(x)dx\Big)^{\frac{1}{r(w)}}\leq\frac{2}{|2Q|}\int_{2Q}w(x)dx,

with

r(w):=1+\frac{1}{\tau_{n}[w]^{weak}_{A_{\infty}}},

where
\tau_{n}
is a dimensional constant that we may take to be
\tau_{n}\simeq 2^{n}
.

2.5.
Young functions and Orlicz maximal functions

In this subsection, we introduce some fundamental facts regarding Young functions and Orlicz spaces. We refer the readers to
[
53
]
and
[
25
]
for more related information.

A function
A
defined on
[0,\infty)
is called a Young function, if
A
is a non-negative, increasing and convex function with
A(0)=0
and
A(t)\rightarrow\infty
as
t\rightarrow\infty
. It is clear that if
A
is a Young function, then
A(t)/t
is not decreasing. Given a cube
Q
, the localized and normalized Luxemburg norm associated with the Orlicz space
L^{A}(\mu)
is defined by

\|f\|_{A(\mu),Q}=\inf\Big\{\lambda>0:\frac{1}{\mu(Q)}\int_{Q}A\big(\frac{|f(x)|}{\lambda}\big)d\mu\leq 1\Big\}.

For convenience, if
\mu
is the Lebesgue measure, we denote
\|f\|_{A(\mu),Q}=\|f\|_{A,Q}

and if
\mu=wdx
is an absolutely continuous measure with respect to the Lebesgue measure, we write
\|f\|_{A(\mu),Q}=\|f\|_{A(w),Q}
.

Now we review several useful conclusions about the Luxemburg norm. First of all, when
A(t)=t^{r}
with
r\geq 1
, we have

\|f\|_{A,Q}=\|f\|_{L^{r},Q}:=\big(\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}|f(x)|^{r}dx\big)^{1/r}
,
which goes back to the standard
L^{r}\big(Q,\frac{dx}{|Q|}\big)
norm. In addition, it is easy to check that if
A
and
B
are Young functions with
A(t)\leq cB(t)
for
t>t_{0}
, then
\|f\|_{A,Q}\leq c\|f\|_{B,Q}
, which can be seen as a generalized Jensen’s inequality. Another noteworthy property of Orlicz space is the following generalized Hölder’s inequality:

(2.1)

\frac{1}{\mu(Q)}\int_{Q}|fg|d\mu\leq 2\|f\|_{A(\mu),Q}\|g\|_{\bar{A}(\mu),Q},

where
\bar{A}(t)=\sup_{s>0}\{st-A(s)\}
is the complementary function of Young function
A
. For instance, when
A(t)=t^{p}/p
with
1<p<\infty
, then
\bar{A}(t)=t^{p^{\prime}}/p^{\prime}
and if
A(t)=t
, a simple calculation yields that

\bar{A}(t)=\sup_{s>0}\left\{s(t-1)\right\}=\begin{cases}0,&t\leq 1,\\
\infty,&t>1.\end{cases}

Now we introduce an additional generalization of (
2.1
) that is beneficial for our work. If
A,B
and
C
are Young functions satisfying
A^{-1}(t)B^{-1}(t)C^{-1}(t)\leq ct
for
t\geq t_{0}
, then for cube
Q\subset{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
, it holds

\|fg\|_{\bar{C}(\mu),Q}\leq c\|f\|_{A(\mu),Q}\|g\|_{B(\mu),Q},

here
A^{-1}(x)=\inf\{y\in\mathbb{R}:A(y)>x\}
with
0\leq x\leq\infty
, where
\inf\emptyset=\infty
.
In view of the average of the Luxemburg norm, it is natural to define a general maximal operator associated to the Young function
A
as

M_{A}f(x)=\sup_{Q\ni x}\|f\|_{A,Q},

where the supremum is taken over all the cubes containing
x
.

We now give some examples of maximal operators related to certain Young functions.

(1)

If
A(t)=t^{r}
with
1<r<\infty
, then
M_{A}=M_{r}
;

(2)

If
A(t)=t\log^{\alpha}(e+t)
with
\alpha>0
, then
\bar{A}(t)\simeq e^{t^{1/\alpha}}-1
and we denote
M_{A}=M_{L(\log L)^{\alpha}}
. Notice that
M\leq M_{A}\lesssim M_{r}
for all
1<r<\infty
. Moreover,
M_{A}\simeq M^{l+1}
, where
\alpha=l\in\mathbb{N}
and
M^{l+1}
is
M
iterated
l+1
times;

(3)

If
A(t)=t\log^{\alpha}(e+t)\log^{\beta}(e+\log(e+t))
with
\alpha,\beta>0
, then
M_{A}=M_{L(\log L)^{\alpha}(\log\log L)^{\beta}}
. In addition, it follows from a detailed calculations that for every
0<\varepsilon<1
,

M_{L(\log L)^{m}(\log\log L)^{1+\varepsilon}}w(x)\leq C_{\varepsilon}M_{L(\log L)^{m+\varepsilon}}w(x),

where
C_{\varepsilon}=e\log^{m}(2e)\log^{1+\varepsilon}(e+\log(2e))+2^{1+\varepsilon}(1+1/\varepsilon)^{1+\varepsilon}
.

We end this subsection by stating the Fefferman-Stein inequality for
M_{A}
.

Lemma 2.8

(
[
27
, Lemma 4.3]
)
.

Let
A
be a Young function. For any arbitrary weight
w
and
\lambda>0
, we have

w\big(\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\ M_{A}f(x)>\lambda\}\big)\leq 3^{n}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}A\Big(\frac{9^{n}|f(x)|}{\lambda}\Big)Mw(x)dx.

If additionally
A
is submultiplicative, then

(2.2)

w\big(\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\ M_{A}f(x)>\lambda\}\big)\leq C_{n,A}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}A\Big(\frac{|f(x)|}{\lambda}\Big)Mw(x)dx.

\frac{1}{\varrho_{1}}=\frac{\log 4}{\log(8/5)}
, it is easy to verify

\int_{\frac{1}{\alpha_{1,k-1}}}^{\frac{1}{\alpha_{1,k}}}\frac{1}{t\log(e+t)}dt\geq c.

This, together with the submultiplicativity of
A_{1}
, the monotonicity of
A_{1}(t)/t
and the fact
\frac{1}{\varrho_{1}}\leq 3
, yields that

(4.13)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\Psi^{-1}_{(1,0)}(1/\alpha_{1,k})}\frac{A_{1}(4^{k})}{4^{k}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=1}^{a_{n,1}}\frac{A_{1}(4^{k})\varphi^{-1}_{(1,0)}\circ\Phi^{-1}_{1}(1/\alpha_{1,k})}{4^{k}\Phi^{-1}_{1}(1/\alpha_{1,k})}+\sum_{k=a_{n,1}}^{\infty}\frac{A_{1}(4^{k})}{4^{k}\Psi^{-1}_{(1,0)}(1/\alpha_{1,k})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\beta_{1,0}+\sum_{k=a_{n,1}}^{\infty}\frac{A_{1}(4^{k-1})}{4^{k-1}\Psi^{-1}_{(1,0)}(1/\alpha_{1,k})}\int_{\frac{1}{\alpha_{1,k-1}}}^{\frac{1}{\alpha_{1,k}}}\frac{1}{t\log(e+t)}dt

\displaystyle\lesssim\beta_{1,0}+\int_{\frac{1}{\alpha_{1,k-1}}}^{\frac{1}{\alpha_{1,k}}}\frac{A_{1}(\log^{1/\varrho_{1}}(e+t))}{\Psi^{-1}_{(1,0)}(t)t\log(e+t)\log^{1/\varrho_{1}}(e+t)}dt

\displaystyle\lesssim\beta_{1,0}+\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{\varphi^{-1}_{(1,0)}\circ\Phi^{-1}_{1}(t)A_{1}(\log^{3}(e+t))}{\Phi^{-1}_{1}(t)t\log^{4}(e+t)}dt

\displaystyle\lesssim\beta_{1,0}+\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{\varphi^{-1}_{(1,0)}\circ\Phi^{-1}_{1}(t)A_{1}(\log^{3}(e+t))}{t^{2}\log^{3}(e+t)}dt,

where
\beta_{1,0}=\sum_{k=1}^{a_{n,1}}\frac{A_{1}(4^{k})\varphi^{-1}_{(1,0)}\circ\Phi^{-1}_{1}(1/\alpha_{1,k})}{4^{k}\Phi^{-1}_{1}(1/\alpha_{1,k})}
.

In order to obtain the weak type endpoint estimate (
4.6
) of
w\big(E_{(1,0)}\big)
, it remains to establish the estimate of
w\big(E^{1}_{(1,0)}\big)
. Applying Lemma
4.1
with the Young function
\psi_{(1,0)}=\Phi_{1}\circ\varphi_{(1,0)}
, it may lead to

\displaystyle\int_{E^{1}_{(1,0)}}\mathcal{A}^{1}_{A_{1}}f(x)w(x)dx\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\Big(\frac{4}{5}\Big)^{k}w(E^{1}_{(1,0)})

\displaystyle+c\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\Big(\frac{8}{5}\Big)^{k}\frac{A_{1}(4^{k})}{\bar{\psi}^{-1}_{(1,0)}(2^{2^{k}})4^{k}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}A_{1}(|f(x)|)M_{\psi_{(1,0)}}w(x)dx.

Then

\displaystyle w\big(E^{1}_{(1,0)}\big)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{5}\int_{E^{1}_{(1,0)}}\mathcal{A}^{1}_{A_{1}}f(x)w(x)dx

\displaystyle\leq\frac{4}{5}w\big(E^{1}_{(1,0)}\big)+c\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\Big(\frac{8}{5}\Big)^{k}\frac{A_{1}(4^{k})}{\bar{\psi}^{-1}_{(1,0)}(2^{2^{k}})4^{k}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}A_{1}(|f(x)|)M_{\psi_{(1,0)}}w(x)dx.

Furthermore, by (
4.5
), one can get

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\Big(\frac{8}{5}\Big)^{k}\frac{A_{1}(4^{k})}{\bar{\psi}^{-1}_{(1,0)}(2^{2^{k}})4^{k}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}2^{k}\frac{A_{1}(4^{k})}{\bar{\psi}^{-1}_{(1,0)}(2^{2^{k}})4^{k}}\int_{2^{2^{k-1}}}^{2^{2^{k}}}\frac{1}{t\log(e+t)}dt

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{A_{1}(4^{k})}{\bar{\psi}^{-1}_{(1,0)}(2^{2^{k}})4^{k}}\int_{2^{2^{k-1}}}^{2^{2^{k}}}\frac{1}{t}dt

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{\varphi^{-1}_{(1,0)}\circ\Phi^{-1}_{1}(t)A_{1}(\log^{3}(e+t))}{t^{2}\log^{3}(e+t)}dt.

Therefore, we finish the proof of (
4.6
).

Note that
\mathcal{A}^{(0,1)}_{A_{0},\mathcal{S}}(\vec{b},f)=\mathcal{A}^{(0,1)}_{A_{0},\mathcal{S}}((b_{1},b_{2}),f)=\mathcal{A}^{(1,0)}_{A_{0},\mathcal{S}}((b_{2},b_{1}),f)
, thus the endpoint estimate (
4.6
) also holds for
\mathcal{A}^{(0,1)}_{A_{0},\mathcal{S}}(\vec{b},f)
.

Case 3. The estimate for
\mathcal{A}^{(1,1)}_{A_{0},\mathcal{S}}(\vec{b},f)
.

To estimate
\mathcal{A}^{(1,1)}_{A_{0},\mathcal{S}}(\vec{b},f)
, we need to give some notations.
Let

\displaystyle E_{(1,1)}

\displaystyle:=\Big\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\ \mathcal{A}^{(1,1)}_{A_{0},\mathcal{S}}(\vec{b},f)(x)>40,\,M_{A_{0}}f(x)\leq\frac{1}{4}\Big\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{k,A_{0}}

\displaystyle:=\big\{Q\in\mathcal{S}:\ 4^{-k-1}<\|f\|_{A_{0},Q}\leq 4^{-k}\big\},

and for each
Q\in\mathcal{S}_{k,A_{0}}
and
i=1,2
, denote

(4.14)

\mathcal{H}^{i}_{k}(Q):=\Big\{x\in Q:\ |b_{i}(x)-\langle b_{i}\rangle_{Q}|>\Big(\frac{4}{3}\Big)^{k}\Big\}.

According to the weak inequality (
2.2
) of Orlicz maximal operators, we only need to estimate
w\big(E_{(1,1)}\big)
.
Note that if
Q\in\mathcal{S}
and
E_{(1,1)}\bigcap Q\neq\emptyset
, then
\|f\|_{A_{0},Q}\leq\frac{1}{4}
. For every
x\in E_{(1,1)}
, it follows that

(4.15)

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{(1,1)}_{A_{0},\mathcal{S}}(\vec{b},f)(x)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{S}_{k,A_{0}}}|b_{1}(x)-\langle b_{1}\rangle_{Q}||b_{2}(x)-\langle b_{2}\rangle_{Q}|\|f\|_{A_{0},Q}\chi_{Q}(x)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{2}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\Big(\frac{4}{3}\Big)^{k}\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{S}_{k,A_{0}}}|b_{i}(x)-\langle b_{i}\rangle_{Q}|\|f\|_{A_{0},Q}\chi_{\mathcal{H}^{i}_{k}(Q)}(x)

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{S}_{k,A_{0}}}\prod_{i=1}^{2}|b_{i}(x)-\langle b_{i}\rangle_{Q}|\|f\|_{A_{0},Q}\chi_{\mathcal{H}^{1}_{k}\cap\mathcal{H}^{2}_{k}(Q)}(x)

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\Big(\frac{4}{3}\Big)^{2k}\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{S}_{k,A_{0}}}\|f\|_{A_{0},Q}\chi_{Q}(x)=:\sum_{i=1}^{4}\mathcal{A}^{i}_{A_{0}}f(x).

Therfore,
w\big(E_{(1,1)}\big)\leq\sum_{i=1}^{4}w\big(E^{i}_{(1,1)}\big)
, where

E^{i}_{(1,1)}=\Big\{x\in E_{(1,1)}:\ \mathcal{A}^{i}_{A_{0}}f(x)>10\Big\},\,\,i=1,2,3,4.

Having performed the above decomposition of
w\big(E_{(1,1)}\big)
, our focus now shifts to estimating each
w\big(E^{i}_{(1,1)}\big).

Since the estimates for
w\big(E^{1}_{(1,1)}\big)
and
w\big(E^{2}_{(1,1)}\big)
are analogous, we only give the estimate for
w\big(E^{1}_{(1,1)}\big)
. In fact, (
4.8
) indicates that

\displaystyle w\big(E^{1}_{(1,1)}\big)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{10}\int_{E^{1}_{(1,1)}}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\Big(\frac{4}{3}\Big)^{k}\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{S}_{k,A_{0}}}|b_{1}(x)-\langle b_{1}\rangle_{Q}|\|f\|_{A_{0},Q}\chi_{\mathcal{H}^{1}_{k}(Q)}(x)w(x)dx

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{S}_{k,A_{0}}}\frac{1}{3^{k}}\Big(\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{\mathcal{H}^{1}_{k}(Q)}|b_{1}(x)-\langle b_{1}\rangle_{Q}|w(x)dx\Big)\Big(\int_{E_{Q}}A_{0}(4^{k}|f(x)|)dx\Big).

Using the same argument as what we have done in the estimate (
4.12
) for
E^{0}_{(1,0)}
in
\mathcal{A}^{(1,0)}_{A_{0},\mathcal{S}}(\vec{b},f)
, we obtain

w\big(E^{1}_{(1,1)}\big)\lesssim\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\Psi^{-1}_{(2,1)}(1/\alpha_{2,k})}\frac{A_{0}(4^{k})}{3^{k}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}A_{0}(|f(x)|)M_{\Phi_{1}\circ\varphi_{(2,1)}}w(x)dx,

where
\alpha_{2,k}=\min\{1,e^{-\frac{(4/3)^{k}}{2^{n}e}+1}\}
and

\Psi^{-1}_{(2,1)}(t):=\frac{\Phi^{-1}_{2}(t)}{\varphi^{-1}_{(2,1)}\circ\Phi^{-1}_{1}(t)}.

In the following, we will undertake a more precise estimate for
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\Psi^{-1}_{(2,1)}(1/\alpha_{2,k})}\frac{A_{0}(4^{k})}{3^{k}}
.
Picking positive integer
a_{n,2}
such that for
k>a_{n,2}
,
\frac{1}{\alpha_{2,k-1}}=e^{\frac{(4/3)^{k-1}}{2^{n}e}-1}\geq e^{2}
. If
\frac{1}{\varrho_{2}}=\frac{\log 4}{\log(4/3)}
, a simple calculation yields that

(4.16)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\Psi^{-1}_{(2,1)}(1/\alpha_{2,k})}\frac{A_{0}(4^{k})}{3^{k}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=1}^{a_{n,2}}\frac{(\frac{4}{3})^{k}A_{0}(4^{k})\varphi^{-1}_{(2,1)}\circ\Phi^{-1}_{1}(1/\alpha_{2,k})}{4^{k}\Phi^{-1}_{1}(1/\alpha_{2,k})}+\sum_{k=a_{n,2}}^{\infty}\frac{(\frac{4}{3})^{k}A_{0}(4^{k})4^{-k}}{\Psi^{-1}_{(2,1)}(1/\alpha_{2,k})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\beta_{2,1}+\sum_{k=a_{n,2}}^{\infty}\frac{A_{0}(4^{k-1})}{4^{k-1}}\Big(\frac{4}{3}\Big)^{k}\int_{\frac{1}{\alpha_{2,k-1}}}^{\frac{1}{\alpha_{2,k}}}\frac{1}{\Psi^{-1}_{(2,1)}(t)t\log(e+t)}dt

\displaystyle\lesssim\beta_{2,1}+\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{A_{0}(\log^{1/\varrho_{2}}(e+t))}{t\Psi^{-1}_{(2,1)}(t)\log^{1/\varrho_{2}}(e+t)}dt

\displaystyle\lesssim\beta_{2,1}+\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{\varphi^{-1}_{(2,1)}\circ\Phi^{-1}_{1}(t)A_{0}(\log^{5}(e+t))}{\Phi^{-1}_{1}(t)t\log^{5}(e+t)}dt

\displaystyle\lesssim\beta_{2,1}+\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{\varphi^{-1}_{(2,1)}\circ\Phi^{-1}_{1}(t)A_{0}(\log^{5}(e+t))}{t^{2}\log^{4}(e+t)}dt,

where
\beta_{2,1}=\sum_{k=1}^{a_{n,2}}\frac{(\frac{4}{3})^{k}A_{0}(4^{k})\varphi^{-1}_{(2,1)}\circ\Phi^{-1}_{1}(1/\alpha_{2,k})}{4^{k}\Phi^{-1}_{1}(1/\alpha_{2,k})}.

We now turn to estimate
w\big(E^{3}_{(1,1)}\big)
. In fact,

w\big(E^{3}_{(1,1)}\big)\lesssim\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{S}_{k,A_{0}}}\frac{1}{4^{k}}\Big(\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{\tilde{Q}}\prod_{i=1}^{2}|b_{i}(x)-\langle b_{i}\rangle_{Q}|w(x)dx\Big)\Big(\int_{E_{Q}}A_{0}(4^{k}|f(x)|)dx\Big),

where
\tilde{Q}:=\mathcal{H}^{1}_{k}(Q)\cap\mathcal{H}^{2}_{k}(Q).

Then the generalized Hölder’s inequality gives that

\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{\mathcal{H}^{1}_{k}(Q)\cap\mathcal{H}^{2}_{k}(Q)}\prod_{i=1}^{2}|b_{i}(x)-\langle b_{i}\rangle_{Q}|w(x)dx\lesssim\|w\chi_{\mathcal{H}^{1}_{k}(Q)\cap\mathcal{H}^{2}_{k}(Q)}\|_{L(\log L)^{2},Q}.

Define

\Psi^{-1}_{(2,0)}(t):=\frac{\Phi^{-1}_{2}(t)}{\varphi^{-1}_{(2,0)}\circ\Phi^{-1}_{2}(t)}.

Note that
\Phi_{2}
and
\varphi_{(2,0)}(t)/t
are strictly increasing functions, then
\Psi_{(2,0)}
is strictly increasing, too. The generalized Hölder’s inequality implies

\displaystyle\|w\chi_{\mathcal{H}^{1}_{k}(Q)\cap\mathcal{H}^{2}_{k}(Q)}\|_{L(\log L)^{2},Q}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\chi_{\mathcal{H}^{1}_{k}(Q)\cap\mathcal{H}^{2}_{k}(Q)}\|_{\Psi_{(2,0)},Q}\|w\|_{\Phi_{2}\circ\varphi_{(2,0)},Q}

\displaystyle=\frac{c}{\Psi^{-1}_{(2,0)}\Big(|Q|/|\mathcal{H}^{1}_{k}(Q)\cap\mathcal{H}^{2}_{k}(Q)|\Big)}\|w\|_{\Phi_{2}\circ\varphi_{(2,0)},Q}.

The same reasoning as in the estimate for (
4.12
) and (
4.13
) then gives

\displaystyle w\big(E^{3}_{(1,1)}\big)

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\Psi^{-1}_{(2,0)}(1/\alpha_{2,k})4^{k}}\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{S}_{k,A_{0}}}\|w\|_{\Phi_{2}\circ\varphi_{(2,0)},Q}\int_{E_{Q}}A_{0}(4^{k}|f(x)|)dx

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\Psi^{-1}_{(2,0)}(1/\alpha_{2,k})}\frac{A_{0}(4^{k})}{4^{k}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}A_{0}(|f(x)|)M_{\Phi_{2}\circ\varphi_{(2,0)}}w(x)dx,

and

(4.17)

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\Psi^{-1}_{(2,0)}(1/\alpha_{2,k})}\frac{A_{0}(4^{k})}{4^{k}}\lesssim\beta_{2,0}+\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{\varphi^{-1}_{(2,0)}\circ\Phi^{-1}_{2}(t)A_{0}(\log^{5}(e+t))}{t^{2}\log^{4}(e+t)}dt,

where
\beta_{2,0}=\sum_{k=1}^{a_{n,2}}\frac{A_{0}(4^{k})\varphi^{-1}_{(2,0)}\circ\Phi^{-1}_{2}(1/\alpha_{2,k})}{4^{k}\Phi^{-1}_{2}(1/\alpha_{2,k})}.

It remains to estimate

. Using Lemma
4.1
with
\varphi=\psi_{(2,0)}:=\Phi_{2}\circ\varphi_{(2,0)}
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{E^{4}_{(1,1)}}\mathcal{A}^{4}_{A_{0}}f(x)w(x)dx

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\Big(\frac{8}{9}\Big)^{k}w\big(E^{4}_{(1,1)}\big)

\displaystyle\quad+c\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\Big(\frac{16}{9}\Big)^{k}\frac{A_{0}(4^{k})}{\bar{\varphi}^{-1}_{(2,0)}\Big((2\Lambda_{A_{0}})^{2^{k}}\Big)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}A_{0}(|f(x)|)M_{\psi_{(2,0)}}w(x)dx.

By the definition of

, it holds that

\displaystyle w\big(E^{4}_{(1,1)}\big)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{10}\int_{E^{4}_{(1,1)}}\mathcal{A}^{4}_{A_{0}}f(x)w(x)dx

Then,
(
4.5
)
implies that

(4.18)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\Big(\frac{16}{9}\Big)^{k}\frac{A_{0}(4^{k})}{\bar{\varphi}^{-1}_{(2,0)}(2^{2^{k}})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}2^{k}\frac{A_{0}(4^{k})}{\bar{\psi}^{-1}_{(2,0)}(2^{2^{k}})4^{k}}\int_{2^{2^{k-1}}}^{2^{2^{k}}}\frac{1}{t\log(e+t)}dt

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{A_{0}(4^{k})}{\bar{\psi}^{-1}_{(2,0)}(2^{2^{k}})4^{k}}\int_{2^{2^{k-1}}}^{2^{2^{k}}}\frac{1}{t}dt

Therefore, it follows from the estimates (

)-(

) that

which combining with (
4.3
) and (

) completes the proof of Theorem
1.7
.

Proof of Theorem

If

is an
\omega
-Calderón-Zygmund operator and

satisifies a Dini condition:

-Hömander condition, see
[

. This means that
T
is an
\bar{A}_{0}
-Hörmander operator, where
A_{0}(t)=t
.
Assume that

\|\vec{b}\|_{\mathrm{BMO}}=1
and let
A_{i}(t)=\Phi_{i}(t)=t\log^{i}(e+t),\,i=1,\ldots,m
, it follows from a simple calculation that
A_{i}
satisifies the conditions of Theorem
1.7
. We need to show that (
1.15
) holds for the chosen Young function
A_{i}
and
\Phi_{i}
.

We consider the following three cases.

Case I:

l_{1}=l_{2}=m
. In this case, we have

\displaystyle K_{\varphi_{(m,m)}}

\displaystyle=\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{\varphi^{-1}_{(m,m)}(t)A_{m}(\log^{2}(e+t))}{t^{2}\log^{3}(e+t)}dt=\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{\varphi^{-1}_{(m,m)}(t)\log^{m}(e+\log^{2}(e+t))}{t^{2}\log(e+t)}dt.

For any fixed
0<\varepsilon<1
, let
\varphi_{(m,m)}(t)=t\log^{m}(e+t)\log^{1+\varepsilon}(e+\log(e+t))
, then

\displaystyle K_{\varphi_{(m,m)}}\lesssim\int_{\log(e+1)}^{\infty}\frac{e^{u}}{(e^{u}-e)u\log^{1+\varepsilon}(e+u)}du\lesssim\frac{1}{\varepsilon},

in the first inequality, we use the fact that
\varphi^{-1}_{(m,m)}(t)\leq ct\log^{-m}(e+t)\log^{-1-\varepsilon}(e+\log(e+t))
, where
c
is an absolute constant which is independent of
\varepsilon
.

Therefore, when
l_{1}=l_{2}=m
, we get

(5.1)

\displaystyle K

\displaystyle{}_{\varphi_{(m,m)}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}A_{m}(\frac{|f(x)|}{\lambda})M_{\varphi_{(m,m)}}w(x)dx

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Phi_{m}(\frac{|f(x)|}{\lambda})M_{L(\log L)^{m}(\log\log L)^{1+\varepsilon}}w(x)dx.

Case II:

1\leq l_{1}<m
. For this case, the following estimate holds

\displaystyle\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{\varphi^{-1}_{(l_{1},l_{2})}\circ\Phi^{-1}_{l_{1}-l_{2}}(t)A_{m-l_{1}}(\log^{2l_{1}+1}(e+t))}{t^{2}\log^{l_{1}+2}(e+t)}dt

\displaystyle=\int_{c_{0}}^{\infty}\frac{\varphi^{-1}_{(l_{1},l_{2})}(t)\log^{l_{1}-1}(e+\Phi_{l_{1}-l_{2}}(t))\log^{m-l_{1}}(e+\log^{2l_{1}+1}(e+\Phi_{l_{1}-l_{2}}(t)))}{\Phi^{2}_{l_{1}-l_{2}}(t)}\Phi^{{}^{\prime}}_{l_{1}-l_{2}}(t)dt

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{c_{0}}^{\infty}\frac{\varphi^{-1}_{(l_{1},l_{2})}(t)\log^{l_{2}}(e+t)\log^{m-l_{1}}(e+\log^{2l_{1}+1}(e+t))}{t^{2}\log(e+t)}dt,

where
c_{0}=\Phi^{-1}_{l_{1}-l_{2}}(1)
and
\Phi^{\prime}
denotes the derivative of
\Phi
.

Take
\varphi_{(l_{1},l_{2})}(t)=t\log^{m-l_{1}+l_{2}}(e+t)\log^{1+\varepsilon}(e+\log(e+t))
, then

\displaystyle\int_{1}^{\infty}

\displaystyle\frac{\varphi^{-1}_{(l_{1},l_{2})}\circ\Phi^{-1}_{l_{1}-l_{2}}(t)A_{m-l_{1}}(\log^{2l_{1}+1}(e+t))}{t^{2}\log^{l_{1}+2}(e+t)}dt

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{c_{0}}^{\infty}\frac{\log^{l_{2}}(e+t)\log^{m-l_{1}}(e+\log^{2l_{1}+1}(e+t))}{t\log^{m-l_{1}+l_{2}+1}(e+t)\log^{1+\varepsilon}(e+\log(e+t))}dt

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{c_{0}}^{\infty}\frac{1}{t\log(e+t)\log^{1+\varepsilon}(e+\log(e+t))}dt

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{\varepsilon}.

Combining
\Phi_{l_{1}-l_{2}}\circ\varphi_{(l_{1},l_{2})}(t)\lesssim\varphi_{(l_{1},l_{2})}(t)\log^{l_{1}-l_{2}}(e+t)=t\log^{m}(e+t)\log^{1+\varepsilon}(e+\log(e+t))
with the fact

\displaystyle\beta_{m,l_{1},l_{2}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{a_{n,l_{1}}}\frac{r^{l_{2}k}_{l_{1}}A_{m-l_{1}}(4^{k})\varphi^{-1}_{(l_{1},l_{2})}\circ\Phi^{-1}_{l_{1}-l_{2}}(1/\alpha_{r_{l_{1}},k})}{4^{k}\Phi^{-1}_{l_{1}-l_{2}}(1/\alpha_{r_{l_{1}},k})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{k=1}^{a_{n,l_{1}}}\frac{C_{m,l_{1},l_{2},k}}{\log^{1+\varepsilon}\Big(e+\log\big(\frac{1/\alpha_{r_{l_{1}},k}}{(e+1/\alpha_{r_{l_{1}},k})^{m}}\big)\Big)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{\varepsilon},

we deduce that for any
1\leq l_{1}<m
and
\,0\leq l_{2}\leq l_{1}-1
,

(5.2)

\displaystyle K_{\varphi_{(l_{1},l_{2})}}

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}A_{m-l_{1}}(\frac{|f(x)|}{\lambda})M_{\Phi_{l_{1}-l_{2}}\circ\varphi_{(l_{1},l_{2})}}w(x)dx

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Phi_{m-l_{1}}(\frac{|f(x)|}{\lambda})M_{L(\log L)^{m}(\log\log L)^{1+\varepsilon}}w(x)dx

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Phi_{m}(\frac{|f(x)|}{\lambda})M_{L(\log L)^{m}(\log\log L)^{1+\varepsilon}}w(x)dx.

Case III:

l_{1}=m
. In the last case, we observe that for any
0\leq l_{2}\leq m-1
,

\displaystyle\int_{1}^{\infty}

\displaystyle\frac{\varphi^{-1}_{(m,l_{2})}\circ\Phi^{-1}_{m-l_{2}}(t)A_{0}(\log^{2m+1}(e+t))}{t^{2}\log^{m+2}(e+t)}dt

\displaystyle=\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{\varphi^{-1}_{(m,l_{2})}\circ\Phi^{-1}_{m-l_{2}}(t)\log^{m-1}(e+t)}{t^{2}}dt

\displaystyle=\int_{\Phi^{-1}_{m-l_{2}}(1)}^{\infty}\frac{\varphi^{-1}_{(m,l_{2})}(t)\log^{m-1}(e+\Phi_{m-l_{2}}(t))}{\Phi^{2}_{m-l_{2}}(t)}\Phi^{{}^{\prime}}_{m-l_{2}}(t)dt

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\Phi^{-1}_{m-l_{2}}(1)}^{\infty}\frac{\varphi^{-1}_{(m,l_{2})}(t)\log^{l_{2}}(e+t)}{t^{2}\log(e+t)}dt.

Take
\varphi_{(m,l_{2})}(t)=t\log^{l_{2}}(e+t)\log^{1+\varepsilon}(e+\log(e+t))
,
a simple calculation shows that

\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{\varphi^{-1}_{(m,l_{2})}\circ\Phi^{-1}_{m-l_{2}}(t)A_{0}(\log^{2m+1}(e+t))}{t^{2}\log^{m+2}(e+t)}dt\lesssim\frac{1}{\varepsilon}.

Note that
\beta_{m,m,l_{2}}\lesssim\frac{1}{\varepsilon}
and

\Phi_{m-l_{2}}\circ\varphi_{(m,l_{2})}(t)\lesssim\varphi_{(m,l_{2})}(t)\log^{m-l_{2}}(e+t)=t\log^{m}(e+t)\log^{1+\varepsilon}(e+\log(e+t)),

then

(5.3)

\displaystyle K_{\varphi_{(m,l_{2})}}

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}A_{0}(\frac{|f(x)|}{\lambda})M_{\Phi_{m-l_{2}}\circ\varphi_{(m,l_{2})}}w(x)dx\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|f(x)|}{\varepsilon\lambda}M_{L(\log L)^{m}(\log\log L)^{1+\varepsilon}}w(x)dx.

Combining all estimates in (
5.1
)-(
5.3
), we have

w\big(\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:|T_{\vec{b}}f(x)|>\lambda\}\big)\leq\frac{C_{n,m,T}}{\varepsilon}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Phi_{m}\Big(\frac{|f(x)|}{\lambda}\Big)M_{L(\log L)^{m}(\log\log L)^{1+\varepsilon}}w(x)dx.

Hence taking all these cases into account and using the homogeneity, we deduce that (
1.15
) holds for every
\vec{b}\in\mathrm{BMO}^{m}
.
∎

Proof of Corollary
1.11

On the one hand,

Proof of Corollary
1.12
.

It suffices to show that (
1.18
) holds. Note that
w\in A^{\rm{weak}}_{\infty}
and it holds for

that

, we have

(5.4)

M_{L(\log L)^{m+\varepsilon}}w(x)\leq\bigg(1+2\max\{1,2^{s-2}\}\Big(\frac{m+\varepsilon}{s-1}\Big)^{m+\varepsilon}e^{s-1}\bigg)M_{s}w(x),

where

Then Lemma

Now it remains to verify (

\displaystyle 1+2\max\{1,2^{s-2}\}\Big(\frac{m+\varepsilon}{s-1}\Big)^{m+\varepsilon}e^{s-1}

\displaystyle\leq\max\{2,2^{(m+\varepsilon)\alpha}\}\Big(\frac{1}{\alpha}\Big)^{m+\varepsilon}e^{(m+\varepsilon)\alpha}

This section is devoted to establish the Coifman-Fefferman inequality for

. For this purpose, we need a generalized Hölder’s inequality of multilinear version
[
58
, Lemma 4.1]
, which is a generalization of
[

, Lemma 1]
under the general measure.

Lemma 6.1

(
[
58
]
)
.

Let
\Phi_{0},\Phi_{1},\Phi_{2},\ldots,\Phi_{m}
be Young functions. If

\Phi^{-1}_{1}(t)\Phi^{-1}_{2}(t)\cdots\Phi^{-1}_{m}(t)\leq D\Phi^{-1}_{0}(t),

then for all functions
f_{1},\ldots,f_{m}
and all cubes
Q
, we have

\|f_{1}f_{2}\ldots f_{m}\|_{\Phi_{0}(\mu),Q}\leq mD\|f_{1}\|_{\Phi_{1}(\mu),Q}\|f_{2}\|_{\Phi_{2}(\mu),Q}\cdots\|f_{m}\|_{\Phi_{m}(\mu),Q}.

In particular, for any weight
w
and
s_{1},\ldots,s_{m}\geq 1
. Let
\frac{1}{s}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\frac{1}{s_{i}}
, then

\frac{1}{w(Q)}\int_{Q}|f_{1}(x)\cdots f_{m}(x)g(x)|w(x)dx\leq 2^{\frac{1}{s}}\Big(1+\frac{1}{s}\Big)^{\frac{1}{s}}\prod_{i=1}^{m}\|f_{i}\|_{\exp L^{s_{i}}(w),Q}\|g\|_{L(\log L)^{1/s}(w),Q}.

By Lemma
2.6
, we establish the following weighted John-Nirenberg inequality for BMO functions with
0<p<\infty
, which generalizes the classical case
p>1
.

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
b\in\rm{BMO}
and
w\in A_{\infty}
. Then for any
0<p<\infty
, there exists a constant
c
independent of
b,w
such that for every cube
Q
,

\Big(\frac{1}{w(Q)}\int_{Q}|b(x)-\langle b\rangle_{Q}|^{p}w(x)dx\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq c[w]_{A_{\infty}}\|b\|_{\rm BMO}.

Proof.

It suffices to prove that

\frac{1}{w(Q)}\int_{Q}\Big(\frac{|b(x)-\langle b\rangle_{Q}|}{[w]_{A_{\infty}}\|b\|_{\rm BMO}}\Big)^{p}w(x)dx\leq c,

where
c
is independent of
b,w
and
Q
.
By the layer cake formula and Lemma
2.6
, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{w(Q)}

\displaystyle\int_{Q}\Big(\frac{|b(x)-\langle b\rangle_{Q}|}{[w]_{A_{\infty}}\|b\|_{\rm BMO}}\Big)^{p}w(x)dx

\displaystyle=\frac{p}{w(Q)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\alpha^{p-1}w(\{x\in Q:|b(x)-\langle b\rangle_{Q}|>\alpha[w]_{A_{\infty}}\|b\|_{\rm BMO}\})d\alpha

\displaystyle\leq 2p\int_{0}^{\infty}\alpha^{p-1}\Big(\frac{|\{x\in Q:|b(x)-\langle b\rangle_{Q}|>\alpha[w]_{A_{\infty}}\|b\|_{\rm BMO}\}|}{|Q|}\Big)^{\frac{1}{c_{n}[w]_{A_{\infty}}}}d\alpha.

Then the John-Nirenberg inequality

\big|\big\{x\in Q:|b(x)-\langle b\rangle_{Q}|>\alpha\big\}\big|\leq e|Q|e^{-\frac{\alpha}{2^{n}e\|b\|_{\rm BMO}}},

gives

\displaystyle\frac{1}{w(Q)}

\displaystyle\int_{Q}\Big(\frac{|b(x)-\langle b\rangle_{Q}|}{[w]_{A_{\infty}}\|b\|_{\rm BMO}}\Big)^{p}w(x)dx

\displaystyle\leq 2p\int_{0}^{\infty}\alpha^{p-1}\Big(e^{1-\frac{\alpha[w]_{A_{\infty}}\|b\|_{\rm BMO}}{2^{n}e\|b\|_{\rm BMO}}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{c_{n}[w]_{A_{\infty}}}}d\alpha

\displaystyle\leq 2pe^{\frac{1}{c_{n}}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\alpha^{p-1}e^{-\frac{\alpha}{2^{n}c_{n}e}}d\alpha\leq c.

∎

We are now ready to prove Theorem

Proof of Theorem

.

According to Theorem

, we have

|T_{\vec{b}}f(x)|\leq C_{n,m}C_{T}\sum_{j=1}^{3^{n}}\sum_{\vec{\gamma}\in\{0,1\}^{m}}\mathcal{A}^{\vec{\gamma}}_{A,\mathcal{S}_{j}}(\vec{b},f)(x),\quad\text{a.e.}\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

By symmetry, we only need to consider the case
\vec{\gamma}\neq\vec{0}
. It follows from the triangle inequality that in order to show the inequality (
8.3
), it suffices to show that for every

(6.1)

\|\mathcal{A}^{\vec{\gamma}}_{A,\mathcal{S}_{j}}(\vec{b},f)\|_{L^{p}(w)}\lesssim\prod_{s=1}^{m}\|b_{s}\|_{\rm BMO}[w]^{m}_{A_{\infty}}[w]^{\max\{1,1/p\}}_{A_{\infty}}\|M_{B}f\|_{L^{p}(w)}.

and

\|\mathcal{A}^{\vec{\gamma}}_{A,\mathcal{S}_{j}}(\vec{b},f)\|^{p}_{L^{p}(w)}\leq\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{S}_{j}}\int_{Q}\prod_{s:\gamma_{s}=1}|b_{s}(x)-\langle b_{s}\rangle_{Q}|^{p}\Big\|\prod_{s:\gamma_{s}=0}(b_{s}-\langle b_{s}\rangle_{Q})f\Big\|^{p}_{A,Q}w(x)dx.

(6.2)

|S|=\#\{s:\gamma_{s}=1\},

(6.3)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{w(Q)}\int_{Q}\prod_{s:\gamma_{s}=1}|b_{s}(x)-\langle b_{s}\rangle_{Q}|^{p}w(x)dx

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{A}^{\vec{\gamma}}_{A,\mathcal{S}_{j}}(\vec{b},f)\|^{p}_{L^{p}(w)}

This section will be devoted to demonstrate Theorem
1.20

A

Definition 7.1
.

Let
A
be a Young function and
m\in\mathbb{N}
. The kernel
K
is said to satisfy the
L^{A,m}
-Hörmander condition, if

\displaystyle\sup_{Q}\sup_{x,z\in\frac{1}{2}Q}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}|2^{k}Q|m^{k}\left\|(K(x,\cdot)-K(z,\cdot))\chi_{2^{k}Q\backslash 2^{k-1}Q}\right\|_{A,2^{k}Q}<\infty,

\displaystyle\sup_{Q}\sup_{x,z\in\frac{1}{2}Q}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}|2^{k}Q|m^{k}\left\|(K(\cdot,x)-K(\cdot,z))\chi_{2^{k}Q\backslash 2^{k-1}Q}\right\|_{A,2^{k}Q}<\infty,

we denote by
\mathcal{H}_{A,m}
the class of kernels satisfying the
L^{A,m}
-Hörmander condition.

We need to introduce some notations. Fix two integers
j
and
m
such that
0\leq j<m

C^{m}_{j}
denote the family of all finite subsets
\sigma=\{\sigma(1),\ldots,\sigma(j)\}

\sigma
is abbreviated as
\sigma=\varnothing
. For any
\sigma\in C^{m}_{j}

\sigma

\sigma^{\prime}=\{1,\ldots,m\}\backslash\sigma

For a finite family of integrable functions
\vec{b}=(b_{1},\ldots,b_{m})
and
\sigma\in C^{m}_{j}

\vec{b}_{\sigma}=(b_{\sigma(1)},\ldots,b_{\sigma(j)})

\|\vec{b}_{\sigma}\|_{\rm BMO}=\prod_{i\in\sigma}\|b_{i}\|_{\rm BMO}

\|\vec{b}_{\sigma^{\prime}}\|_{\rm BMO}=\prod_{i\in\sigma^{\prime}}\|b_{i}\|_{\rm BMO}
.
With this notation, given an A-Hörmander operator

, the general commutator is defined by

T_{\vec{b}_{\sigma}}f(x)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{n}}}\prod_{i\in\sigma}(b_{i}(x)-b_{i}(y))K(x,y)f(y)dy,\,\,x\notin\operatorname{supp}f.

In the particular case of

, we write

.

The following Kolmogorov’s inequality, which can be found in
[
22
, P. 485]
will be used in our analysis.

Lemma 7.2

(
[
22
]
)
.

Let

, there exists a constant

depending on

such that for any measurable function

\|f\|_{L^{p}(Q,\frac{dx}{|Q|})}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{q,\infty}(Q,\frac{dx}{|Q|})}.

The following lemma presents a pointwise estimate of
M^{\#}_{\delta}(T_{\vec{b}}f)
, which plays a crucial role in the quantitative weighted Coifman-Fefferman inequalities.

Lemma 7.3
.

Let
m\in\mathbb{N}
and
\vec{b}=(b_{1},\ldots,b_{m})\in\mathrm{BMO}^{m}
. Assume that
A,B
are Young functions such that

, where

-Hörmander operator with kernel

(7.1)

Proof.

The case

was already proved in
[

]
. Fix
x\in{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
and a cube
Q\ni x
with center
x_{Q}
, set
Q^{*}=2Q
. Since
0<\delta<1
, then
\left||a|^{\delta}-|b|^{\delta}\right|\leq|a-b|^{\delta}
for
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
, it is enough to show that

(7.2)

\Big(\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}|T_{\vec{b}}f(y)-c_{Q}|^{\delta}dy\Big)^{\frac{1}{\delta}}\leq C\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\sum_{\sigma\in C^{m}_{j}}\|\vec{b}_{\sigma^{\prime}}\|_{\rm BMO}M_{\varepsilon}(T_{\vec{b}_{\sigma}}f)(x)+C\|\vec{b}\|_{\rm BMO}M_{B}f(x),

where the constant
c_{Q}
will be choosen later and
C
is independent of
x
and
Q
.
Applying
[
61
, Lemma 3.2]
, one may get

\displaystyle\prod_{j=1}^{m}(b_{i}(y)-b_{i}(z))

\displaystyle=\prod_{i=1}^{m}(\langle b_{i}\rangle_{Q^{*}}-b_{i}(z))+\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\sum_{\sigma\in C^{m}_{j}}(-1)^{\#\sigma^{\prime}+1}\prod_{i\in\sigma}(b_{i}(y)-b_{i}(z))

\displaystyle\quad\times\prod_{i\in\sigma^{\prime}}(b_{i}(y)-\langle b_{i}\rangle_{Q^{*}}).

Therefore,

\displaystyle|T_{\vec{b}}f(y)-c_{Q}|

\displaystyle\leq\Big|\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{n}}}\prod_{i=1}^{m}(\langle b_{i}\rangle_{Q^{*}}-b_{i}(z))K(y,z)f(z)dz-c_{Q}\Big|

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\sum_{\sigma\in C^{m}_{j}}\prod_{i\in\sigma^{\prime}}|b_{i}(y)-\langle b_{i}\rangle_{Q^{*}}|\Big|\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{n}}}\prod_{i\in\sigma}(b_{i}(y)-b_{i}(z))K(y,z)f(z)dz\Big|

\displaystyle=\Big|T\Big(\prod_{i=1}^{m}(\langle b_{i}\rangle_{Q^{*}}-b_{i})f\Big)(y)-c_{Q}\Big|+\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\sum_{\sigma\in C^{m}_{j}}\prod_{i\in{\sigma^{\prime}}}|b_{i}(y)-\langle b_{i}\rangle_{Q^{*}}||T_{\vec{b}_{\sigma}}f(y)|.

Now we split
f
into
f=f_{1}+f_{2}
, where
f_{1}=f\chi_{Q^{*}}
and
f_{2}=f\chi_{(Q^{*})^{c}}
, which implies that

(7.3)

\displaystyle\Big(\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}|T_{\vec{b}}f(y)-c_{Q}|^{\delta}dy\Big)^{\frac{1}{\delta}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big(\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}\Big|T\Big(\prod_{i=1}^{m}(\langle b_{i}\rangle_{Q^{*}}-b_{i})f_{1}\Big)(y)\Big|^{\delta}dy\Big)^{\frac{1}{\delta}}

\displaystyle+\Big(\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}\Big|T\Big(\prod_{i=1}^{m}(\langle b_{i}\rangle_{Q^{*}}-b_{i})f_{2}\Big)(y)-c_{Q}\Big|^{\delta}dy\Big)^{\frac{1}{\delta}}

\displaystyle+\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\sum_{\sigma\in C^{m}_{j}}\Big(\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}\prod_{i\in{\sigma^{\prime}}}|b_{i}(y)-\langle b_{i}\rangle_{Q^{*}}|^{\delta}|T_{\vec{b}_{\sigma}}f(y)|^{\delta}dy\Big)^{\frac{1}{\delta}}

\displaystyle=:I+II+III.

For
I
, the condition
\mathcal{H}_{\bar{A}}\subset\mathcal{H}_{1}
implies that
T
is of weak type
(1,1)
. Therefore, Lemma
6.1
and Lemma
7.2
yield that

(7.4)

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big\|T\Big(\prod_{i=1}^{m}(\langle b_{i}\rangle_{Q^{*}}-b_{i})f_{1}\Big)\Big\|_{L^{1,\infty}(Q,\frac{dy}{|Q|})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{|Q^{*}|}\int_{Q^{*}}\prod_{i=1}^{m}|\langle b_{i}\rangle_{Q^{*}}-b_{i}(y)||f(y)|dy

\displaystyle\lesssim\prod_{i=1}^{m}\|\langle b_{i}\rangle_{Q^{*}}-b_{i}\|_{\exp L,Q^{*}}\|f\|_{B,Q^{*}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\vec{b}\|_{\rm BMO}M_{B}f(x).

Consider now the term
II
. Let
c_{Q}=T\big(\prod_{i=1}^{m}(\langle b_{i}\rangle_{Q^{*}}-b_{i})f_{2}\big)(x_{Q})
. Then it follows from the Jensen’s inequality that

(7.5)

\displaystyle II

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}\Big|\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{n}}}\prod_{i=1}^{m}(\langle b_{i}\rangle_{Q^{*}}-b_{i}(z))(K(y,z)-K(x_{Q},z))f_{2}(z)dz\Big|dy

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\int_{2^{j+1}Q\backslash 2^{j}Q}\prod_{i=1}^{m}|\langle b_{i}\rangle_{Q^{*}}-b_{i}(z)||K(y,z)-K(x_{Q},z)|f(z)|dzdy

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\int_{2^{j+1}Q\backslash 2^{j}Q}\prod_{i=1}^{m}|\langle b_{i}\rangle_{Q^{*}}-\langle b_{i}\rangle_{2^{j+1}Q}||K(y,z)-K(x_{Q},z)|f(z)|dzdy

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\int_{2^{j+1}Q\backslash 2^{j}Q}\prod_{i=1}^{m}|\langle b_{i}\rangle_{2^{j+1}Q}-b_{i}(z)||K(y,z)-K(x_{Q},z)|f(z)|dzdy

\displaystyle=:II_{1}+II_{2}.

By the generalized Hölder’s inequality with
\bar{A},B
and
K\in\mathcal{H}_{\bar{A},m}
, we have

(7.6)

\displaystyle II_{1}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{|2^{j+1}Q|}{|2^{j+1}Q|}\int_{2^{j+1}Q\backslash 2^{j}Q}\prod_{i=1}^{m}|\langle b_{i}\rangle_{Q^{*}}-\langle b_{i}\rangle_{2^{j+1}Q}||K(y,z)-K(x_{Q},z)|f(z)|dzdy

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}\|\vec{b}\|_{\rm BMO}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}|2^{j+1}Q|j^{m}\Big\|(K(y,\cdot)-K(x_{Q},\cdot))\chi_{2^{j+1}Q\backslash 2^{j}Q}\Big\|_{\bar{A},2^{j+1}Q}\|f\|_{B,2^{j+1}Q}dy

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\vec{b}\|_{\rm BMO}M_{B}f(x),

where the second inequality follows from the fact that
|\langle b_{i}\rangle_{2^{j+1}Q}-\langle b_{i}\rangle_{2Q}|\leq Cj\|b_{i}\|_{\rm BMO}
.

For
II_{2}
, using Lemma
6.1
, it holds that

(7.7)

\displaystyle II_{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{|2^{j+1}Q|}{|2^{j+1}Q|}\int_{2^{j+1}Q\backslash 2^{j}Q}\prod_{i=1}^{m}|\langle b_{i}\rangle_{2^{j+1}Q}-b_{i}(z)||K(y,z)-K(x_{Q},z)|f(z)|dzdy

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}\|\vec{b}\|_{\rm BMO}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}|2^{j+1}Q|\Big\|(K(y,\cdot)-K(x_{Q},\cdot))\chi_{2^{j+1}Q\backslash 2^{j}Q}\Big\|_{\bar{A},2^{j+1}Q}\|f\|_{B,2^{j+1}Q}dy

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\vec{b}\|_{\rm BMO}M_{B}f(x).

Now we turn to the last term
III
. For every
\sigma
, since
0<\delta<\varepsilon<1
, take
\delta_{i},\,i\in\sigma^{\prime}
such that
\sum_{i\in\sigma^{\prime}}\frac{1}{\delta_{i}}+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}=\frac{1}{\delta}
, by Hölder’s inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle III

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{j=1}^{m}\sum_{\sigma\in C^{m}_{j}}\prod_{i\in\sigma^{\prime}}\Big(\frac{1}{|Q^{*}|}\int_{Q^{*}}|\langle b_{i}\rangle_{Q^{*}}-b_{i}(y)|^{\delta_{i}}dy\Big)^{\frac{1}{\delta_{i}}}\Big(\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}|T_{\vec{b}_{\sigma}}f(y)|^{\varepsilon}dy\Big)^{\frac{1}{\varepsilon}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{j=1}^{m}\sum_{\sigma\in C^{m}_{j}}\|\vec{b}_{\sigma^{\prime}}\|_{\rm BMO}M_{\varepsilon}(T_{\vec{b}_{\sigma}}f)(x).

This estimate, together with (
7.3
)-(
7.7
), gives the desired conclusion.
∎

We are ready to demonstrate Theorem
1.20
.

Proof of Theorem
1.20
.

As shown in
[

]
, we may assume that

]

.

There exists some
\delta_{0}
with

such that for any finite subset

and any bounded functions

with compact support,
\|M_{\delta_{0}}(T_{\vec{b}_{\sigma}}f)\|_{L^{p}(w)}<\infty

Combining Lemma
2.5
with Claim
7.4
and applying Lemma

, it suffices to show that there exsits
\delta
with

such that

(7.8)

\|M^{\#}_{\delta}(T_{\vec{b}}f)\|_{L^{p}(w)}\leq C\|\vec{b}\|_{\rm BMO}[w]^{m}_{A_{\infty}}\|M_{B}f\|_{L^{p}(w)}.

Note that when

, then

and

. Lemma
7.3

2.5
, gives that

\|M^{\#}_{\delta_{0}}(Tf)\|_{L^{p}(w)}\leq C\|M_{B}f\|_{L^{p}(w)}.

To confirm the validity of (

, for

8.
applications

In this section, we present some applications of the results obtained in Theorem
1.7
and Theorem
1.14
. To be specific, we will give the Fefferman-Stein inequality with arbitrary weights and the quantitative weighted Coifman-Fefferman inequality for the general commutators of Calderón commutators of Bajšanski-Coifman type, homogeneous singular operators, and Fourier multipliers.

8.1.
Calderón commutators of Bajšanski-Coifman type

It is well known that the study of Calderón commutators is closely connected to the
Cauchy integral on Lipschitz curves and the elliptic boundary value problems. In 1967, Bajšanski and Coifman
[
3
]
introduced a more general Calderón commutator as follows. Given
k\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
and
A
satisfies Lipschitz condition (
1.1
), define the singular operator
T_{A,k}
as

T_{A,k}f(x):=p.v.\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{n}}}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n}}\frac{P_{k}(A;x,y)}{|x-y|^{k}}f(y)dy,

where

P_{k}(A;x,y):=A(x)-\sum\limits_{|\alpha|<k}\frac{A_{\alpha}(y)}{\alpha!}(x-y)^{\alpha},\,\,A_{\alpha}(x)=\partial^{\alpha}_{x}A(x).

Assume that
A_{\alpha}\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
for each
|\alpha|=k
. If we denote the kernel of
T_{A,k}
by

K_{A,k}(x,y):=\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n}}\frac{P_{k}(A;x,y)}{|x-y|^{k}},

then it follows from
[
17
, p.1671]
that

K_{A,k}
is a standard Calderón-Zygmund kernel. Furthermore, if
k
is odd, then
T_{A,k}
is bounded on
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})

[
17
, Theorem F]
. We may obtain the following results by Theorem

1.9
and Theorem
1.14
.

Theorem 8.1
.

Let
m\in\mathbb{N}
and
\vec{b}=(b_{1},\ldots,b_{m})\in\mathrm{BMO}^{m}
. Suppose that
k
is odd and
A_{\alpha}\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
for each
|\alpha|=k
, then for any weight
w
and every
0<\varepsilon<1
, we have

w\big(\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:|T_{A,k,\vec{b}}f(x)|>\lambda\}\big)\lesssim\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Phi_{m}\Big(\frac{\big\|\vec{b}\big\|_{\mathrm{BMO}}|f(x)|}{\lambda}\Big)M_{L(\log L)^{m}(\log\log L)^{1+\varepsilon}}w(x)dx.

Theorem 8.2
.

Let
0<p<\infty
,
m\in\mathbb{N}
and
b\in\mathrm{BMO}
. Assume
k,A
satisfy the same conditions as Theorem
8.1
, then for any
w\in A_{\infty}
,

\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{n}}}|T^{m}_{A,k,b}f(x)|^{p}w(x)dx\lesssim\|b\|^{mp}_{\rm BMO}[w]^{mp}_{A_{\infty}}[w]^{\max\{1,p\}}_{A_{\infty}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{n}}}(M^{m+1}f(x))^{p}w(x)dx.

8.2.
Homogeneous singular integral operators

We will apply our results to derive the weak type endpoint estimatesand the Coifman-Fefferman inequality of homogeneous integral singular operators.

For our purpose, we need to give some definitions. Assume that
\Omega
is a function of homogeneous of degree zero,
\Omega\in L^{1}({\mathbb{S}}^{n-1})
and satisfies the cancellation condition:

(8.1)

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\Omega(\theta)d\theta=0,

where
d\theta
denotes the surface measure of
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
. Set
K(x)=\Omega(x)/|x|^{n}
and let
T
be the
singular integral operator of convolution type with the kernel
K
, that is,
T
is bounded on
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
and

(8.2)

T_{\Omega}f(x)=p.v.\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{n}}}K(x-y)f(y)dy=p.v.\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{n}}}\frac{\Omega(y)}{|y|^{n}}f(x-y)dy.

For a Young function
A
, define the
L^{A}
-modulus of continuity of
\Omega
as

\Omega_{A}(t)=\sup\limits_{|y|\leq t}\|\Omega(\cdot+y)-\Omega(y)\|_{A,\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}.

Notice that when
A(t)=t^{q},\,1<q\leq\infty
,
\Omega_{A}
is exactly
L^{q}
-modulus of continuity in the usual sense.
As was shown in
[
27
, Theorem 3.2]
, if
\Omega_{A}
satisfies

\int_{0}^{1}\Omega_{A}(t)\frac{dt}{t}<\infty,

then
K\in H_{A}
. By Theorem
1.7
and Theorem
1.14
, one can obtain

Theorem 8.3
.

Suppose that
m,\vec{b},A_{i}(i=0,\ldots,m)
satisfy the conditions in Theorem
1.7
.
If
T_{\Omega}
is the operator defined in (
8.2
) and
\Omega
satisfies

\int_{0}^{1}\Omega_{\bar{A}_{0}}(t)\frac{dt}{t}<\infty,

then for any weight
w
and every family of Young functions
\varphi_{\vec{l}}:=\varphi_{(l_{1},l_{2})},\,0\leq l_{2}<l_{1}\leq m
, it holds that

\displaystyle w\big(\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:|T_{\Omega,\vec{b}}f(x)|>\lambda\}\big)

\displaystyle\lesssim K_{\varphi_{(m,m)}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}A_{m}\Big(\frac{|f(x)|}{\lambda}\Big)M_{\varphi_{(m,m)}}w(x)dx

\displaystyle+\sum_{l_{1}=1}^{m}\sum_{l_{2}=0}^{l_{1}-1}K_{\varphi_{(l_{1},l_{2})}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}A_{m-l_{1}}\Big(\frac{|f(x)|}{\lambda}\Big)M_{\Phi_{l_{1}-l_{2}}\circ\varphi_{(l_{1},l_{2})}}w(x)dx,

where
K_{\varphi_{(l_{1},l_{2})}}
is given in (
1.13
).

Theorem 8.4
.

Let
0<p<\infty
,
m\in\mathbb{N}
and
\vec{b}=(b_{1},\ldots,b_{m})\in\mathrm{BMO}^{m}

then for any

(8.3)

\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{n}}}|T_{\Omega,\vec{b}}(f)(x)|^{p}w(x)dx\lesssim\prod_{s=1}^{m}\|b_{s}\|^{p}_{\rm BMO}[w]^{mp}_{A_{\infty}}[w]^{\max\{1,p\}}_{A_{\infty}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{n}}}(M_{B}f(x))^{p}w(x)dx.

8.3.
Fourier multipliers

Let

and consider the muliplier operator
T
defined a priori for functions

in the Schwartz class by
\widehat{T_{h}f}(\xi)=h(\xi)\widehat{f}(\xi)

\sup\limits_{R>0}R^{|\alpha|}\|\chi_{Q(0,2R)\setminus Q(0,R)}D^{\alpha}h\|_{L^{s},Q(0,2R)}<\infty,

where
Q(0,R)
is the cube of side
R

{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
.

In order to give the quantitative estimates of the above operators, we present the following lemma, which is a slightly weaker version of
[
42

.

Lemma 8.5
.

Let
h\in M(s,l)
with
1<s\leq 2
,
1\leq l\leq n
and with
l>n/s
, then for all
1<r<(n/l)^{\prime}
, we have that
K^{N}\in\mathcal{H}_{r}
uniformly in
N
,
where
K_{N}
is the certain truncations of the kernel and the detailed definition can be seen in
[
42
, p.1419]
.

Using Theorem
1.7
, Corollaries
1.16
and
1.17
, combined with Lemma
8.5
and a standard approximation argument as in
[
28
]
, we have

Theorem 8.6
.

Let
h\in M(s,l)
with
1<s\leq 2
,
1\leq l\leq n
and with
l>n/s
. Assume that
1<r<(n/l)^{\prime}
and
\vec{b}=(b_{1},\ldots,b_{m})\in{\rm BMO}^{m}
with
\|b_{i}\|_{\mathrm{BMO}}=1,i=1,\ldots,m
. Then for any weight
w
and
0<\varepsilon<1

\displaystyle w\big(

\displaystyle\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:|T_{h,\vec{b}}f(x)|>\lambda\}\big)

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{n}}}\Big(\frac{|f(x)|}{\lambda}\Big)^{r^{\prime}}\Big(\log\big(e+\frac{|f(x)|}{\lambda}\big)\Big)^{mr^{\prime}}M_{\psi}w(x)dx,

where
\psi(t)=t\log^{2mr^{\prime}+r^{\prime}}(e+t)\log^{1+\varepsilon}(e+\log(e+t)).

Theorem 8.7
.

Let
h\in M(s,l)
with
1<s\leq 2
,
1\leq l\leq n
and with
l>n/s

\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{n}}}|T_{h}(f)(x)|^{p}w(x)dx\lesssim[w]^{\max\{1,p\}}_{A_{\infty}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{n}}}(M_{r^{\prime}}f(x))^{p}w(x)dx.

Furthermore, if
n/l<r^{\prime}\leq p<\infty
, then for
w\in A_{p/r^{\prime}}
,
T
is bounded on

and

\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{n}}}|T_{h}(f)(x)|^{p}w(x)dx\lesssim[w]^{p}_{A_{\infty}}[w]^{\frac{p}{p-r^{\prime}}}_{A_{p/r^{\prime}}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{n}}}|f(x)|^{p}w(x)dx.

Optimization design and experimental verification of magnetic pulse spot welding system of dissimilar metal sheets based on a field shaper
Authors: Hang Zhang, Xiaoxiang Li, Hongfa Ding
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Magnetic pulse welding based on a field shaper using a single-turn multi-layer flat coil and enhancing the magnetic field in local areas with the field shaper can achieve point welding of metal sheets, with broad application prospects in dissimilar metal welding. By reasonably optimizing the structural parameters of the flat coil and the field shaper, the magnetic field strength and eddy current density in the local area can be effectively improved, thereby increasing the Lorentz force to improve the collision speed of the sheet, and ultimately improving the welding quality of the sheets. In view of this, the goal of this work was to maximize magnetic pressure, optimize the structural parameters of the flat coil and field shaper, and innovatively propose a new type of field shaper with an arc-shaped hole structure, which effectively improved the welding quality without changing the welding energy. Furthermore, a magnetic pulse spot welding (MPSW) experimental platform was built using the optimized magnet device, achieving the spot welding of AA1060 aluminum sheets and stainless steel (SS304) sheets both with a thickness of 1 mm. Through peeling and stretching experiments, it was proven that the strength of the welded joint exceeded that of the base metal, indicating that the effective welding of dissimilar metals has been achieved. In addition, through microscopic analysis, it could be seen that a waveform composite interface was formed at the welding interface, which further indicated that the welding performance of the welded joint was good. The results indicated that the use of the new field shaper with an arc-shaped hole structure could reduce the critical discharge voltage for effective welding by 2 kV, directly converting it into lower energy consumption requirements and a wider process window, which had important economic value for industrial applications.

Introduction

With the increasingly severe global energy crisis and environmental pollution problems, lightweight technology has become an inevitable trend in the development of modern industry1,2. Specifically, in the automotive field, against the backdrop of global energy conservation and emission reduction, as well as the rapid development of new energy vehicles, the lightweight of automobiles is increasingly valued by the industry, and it even determines the competitive landscape of automotive companies in the global market3. Research has shown that for every 100 kg reduction in car weight, fuel vehicles can reduce their fuel consumption by 0.3–0.6 L per 100 km, while electric vehicles can increase their range by 6–10 km4. In addition, in terms of carbon emissions, a lighter weight can reduce each vehicle’s annual carbon dioxide emissions by approximately 50 kg. As countries around the world implement increasingly strict emission regulations, lighter weights have become a necessary path for car manufacturers to meet regulatory requirements5.

Aluminum alloy has become one of the preferred lightweight materials for automobiles due to its low density, high strength, good corrosion resistance, and processability6–8. At present, the application proportion of aluminum alloy in automobile bodies is increasing every year, even reaching over 90%. For example, the Audi A8 adopts an all-aluminum frame, with an aluminum alloy proportion of up to 92%, resulting in a weight reduction of 40% for the entire vehicle. With the widespread application of aluminum alloys in automobile manufacturing, the connection method of materials has gradually shifted from the same type of metal to dissimilar metals. However, due to significant differences in the physical, chemical, and metallurgical properties between dissimilar metals, traditional welding methods such as arc welding, laser welding, and friction welding often present many challenges, such as heat-affected zone issues, the formation of intermetallic compounds, and high residual stresses at joints9–11. The above-mentioned problems will reduce the strength of the welded joint and restrict the combination and application of dissimilar metal materials12,13.

Magnetic pulse welding technology, as a new type of high-speed impact welding technology, is categorized as solid-state welding. This technology uses electromagnetic force to drive two metals to collide at high speed, thereby achieving atomic diffusion and metallurgical bonding in a very short time and avoiding the above-mentioned problems of traditional welding14–17. Moreover, magnetic pulse welding has the advantages of high welding strength, no filler material, and being green and pollution-free, which can effectively solve the problem of welding dissimilar metals18–20.

The common forms of magnetic pulse welding mainly include pipe welding and plate welding21–24. At present, magnetic pulse welding technology is mainly used for welding pipe fittings. Scholars have conducted considerable research on this topic, and the technology is relatively mature and has certain industrial applications25–28. In contrast, the magnetic pulse welding technology for sheet metal is still in the research and development verification stage, and its process maturity is still significantly lower than that of pipe welding. Moreover, this type of welding technology mostly uses single-turn wires, which have the problems of low welding energy utilization and easy deformation and damage of welding coils and have certain limitations, which are not conducive to repeated experiments and industrial production29–31. Deng32 et al. were the first to apply a flat plate field shaper to the magnetic pulse welding of sheets based on the principle of magnetic pulse formation, and they designed a set of spot welding devices to achieve spot welding of dissimilar metal plates of aluminum and steel. The welding device adopted a multi-turn coil structure and was combined with a field shaper to converge the magnetic field, effectively reducing the circuit current, minimizing the energy loss in the circuit, improving energy utilization efficiency, and ensuring the mechanical strength of the coil. It has good engineering feasibility and great application prospects in the welding of dissimilar metals. Although magnetic pulse welding based on a field shaper can achieve welding between dissimilar metal sheets, it is difficult to achieve effective welding when the yield strength or thickness of the sheet is high. Further optimization and design of magnet devices are still needed to improve welding strength and efficiency, so it is necessary to conduct further research.

In this work, a two-dimensional axisymmetric finite element model was established for magnetic pulse welding, and the structural parameters of the coil and field shaper were systematically optimized with the objective of maximizing the magnetic pressure. Based on the optimization, a novel flat field shaper with an arc-shaped hole structure was innovatively proposed, and a high-performance welding magnet device (a device composed of a field shaper and an electromagnetic coil) was developed. A magnetic pulse spot welding experimental platform was further established to conduct spot welding experiments on AA1060 aluminum sheets and stainless steel (SS304) sheets, both with a thickness of 1 mm, for different welding process parameters. Subsequently, the mechanical properties of the welded joint were tested through stretching and peeling experiments, and the welding effects of the welded joints that were obtained using field shapers with different inner hole structures were compared. The corresponding welding process parameter window was determined. Finally, the performance of the welded joint was further verified through microscopic analysis. Both the simulation and the experimental results indicated that the use of the new field shaper could effectively reduce the minimum energy required for welding, thereby improving the welding quality and efficiency. The relevant research results could also be extended to the magnetic pulse welding of dissimilar metal sheets with higher yield strength and greater thickness. This is of great significance for the promotion of their industrial applications. In the following sections, the principles and characteristics, numerical analysis, experimental research, and microscopic analysis are reported separately.

Materials and methods

Principle of the MPSW based on the field shaper

The working principle diagram of the MPSW platform based on the field shaper is shown in Fig. 1(a). The MPSW platform was mainly composed of a power system, a flat coil, a field shaper, sheets, and pads. During operation, the coil was pulsed-discharged through the capacitor bank to generate the pulsed magnetic field. The changing magnetic field induced eddy currents on the upper end face of the field shaper, and these currents flowed through the gap toward the lower end face. Due to the difference in the areas between the upper and lower end faces, the eddy current density was enhanced at the lower end face. The current at the lower end further induced an eddy current on the surface of the flyer plate. The electromagnetic force generated by the eddy current under the action of the pulsed magnetic field drove the high-speed deformation movement of the flyer plate, and which ultimately collided with the target plate to achieve welding.

Fig. 1Principle diagram of the MPSW: (a) principle diagram, and (b) structure diagram of field shaper.

Principle diagram of the MPSW: (a) principle diagram, and (b) structure diagram of field shaper.

The function of the flat field shaper was to concentrate the magnetic field generated by the coil into a smaller area, thereby enhancing the magnetic field strength and the eddy current density in that area and achieving the goal of increasing the Lorentz force to improve the collision speed. The structure of the field shaper is shown in Fig. 1(b). To achieve the function of regulating a magnetic field and an eddy current, a field shaper usually has the following four characteristics: (1) having a radial through gap, the induced eddy current generated on the upper end face of the field shaper can flow through the gap to the lower end face without forming a circulating flow inside the upper end face, (2) an inner hole with a certain radius to prevent eddy currents from collecting on the inner hole surface of the field shaper, (3) the radius R1 of the lower end face of the field shaper should be smaller than the radius R2 of the upper end face to achieve the effect of current gathering and magnetic flux concentration, and (4) the thickness H of the field shaper should be greater than twice the skin depth to prevent the currents on the upper and lower surfaces from canceling each other out.

Establishment of numerical method

To optimize the structural parameters of the coil and field shaper, a two-dimensional axisymmetric model of the MPSW was established using COMSOL finite element software. During modeling, a total of five modules were used for the fully coupled simulation of the MPSW process: the “global ordinary differential equations (ODEs) and differential algebraic equations (DAEs)” module, “magnetic fields” module, “solid mechanics” module, “heat transfer in solids” module, and “moving mesh” module. The specific simulation process is shown in Fig. 2. In this process, the first four modules were used to solve the circuit, magnetic field, plate deformation and temperature field in sequence, and the fifth module was used to update the model mesh. To save computational costs, only the circuit and magnetic field modules were used for optimization, and ultimately, all five modules were used to verify the electromagnetic-thermal-mechanical performance of the magnet device.

Optimization methods of the magnet device

Optimize methods and processes

MPSW is a complex multi physics coupling process, and the optimization design of its magnet device involves optimizing multiple structural parameters. Due to the complex structure of the magnet device, uneven spatial distribution of the magnetic field, and the inability to represent its relationship with various structural parameters through specific functions, it is difficult to determine the optimal magnet structural parameters using traditional optimization methods. Therefore, the goal of this study was to maximize the magnetic pressure as the optimization objective and optimize various structural parameters using the parameterized scanning function of the two-dimensional axisymmetric finite element model previously established by COMSOL. The optimization process is as follows: First, a set of initial structural parameters was estimated based on the size of the solder joints, and the influence of each parameter on the magnetic pressure was analyzed through parametric scanning to summarize the optimized parameters; Then, the performance of the optimized device was evaluated, and repeated corrections were made to obtain the final optimization result. It is worth noting that although the parameterized scanning method proposed in this work is systematic and effective, the computational cost cannot be ignored. Future work can also develop alternative models based on simulation data to achieve fast parameter scanning and efficient global optimization34.

Optimize parameters and objectives

The magnet device consisted of a flat coil and a field shaper, and its structure is shown in Fig. 3(b). To improve the coupling strength between the coil and the field shaper, a multi-layer single-turn flat plate coil was used for the coil. For the convenience of winding and reinforcement, the coil wire adopted a 3 × 5 mm2 rectangular cross-section copper wire commonly used in the laboratory, and the coil was wrapped with a layer of insulating tape outside the wire. To improve the energy transfer efficiency between the field shaper, coil, and sheets, the distance between the three had to be minimized as much as possible on the fixture. According to the current technology level in the laboratory, the distance could be set to 0.5 mm. To ensure that the basic process parameters remained unchanged, it was necessary to optimize the structural parameters of the coil and field shaper. The goal was to achieve spot welding with a radius of approximately 10 mm. The size of the spot welding was determined by the inner radius of the lower end face of a field shaper. The inner radius is usually slightly larger than the inner radius of the lower end face of the field shaper. Based on relevant literature and preliminary experimental results, the inner radius of the lower end face of the field shaper was set to 8 mm. A set of initial structural parameters for the model was determined based on the inner radius of the lower end face of the field shaper, as shown in Table 4. Results and discussion

Parametric analysis and optimization of the magnet device for enhancing magnetic pressure

To optimize the structural parameters of the coil and field shaper, the influence of each structural parameter on the surface magnetic pressure distribution of the plate was studied, and the results were analyzed and explained.

(1) Analysis of the influence of coil radius on magnetic pressure.

To maximize the magnetic pressure acting on the flyer plate, a parametric analysis of the coil’s inner radius (R₅) was conducted. Figure 7(a) shows the variation of the magnetic pressure distribution on the surface of the sheet with different coil radii while keeping the discharge voltage and other structural parameters constant. It can be seen from the figure that when the discharge voltage and the other parameters were fixed, the increase of the coil radius R5 caused the peak magnetic pressure on the sheet surface to first increase and then decrease, while the pressure distribution pattern remained unchanged. An analysis showed that when R5 was less than 8 mm, the inner diameter of the coil was smaller than that of the field shaper, resulting in an increase in the magnetic leakage and decreases in the induced current and the magnetic pressure. When R5 was in the range of 8–12 mm, the coil was located inside the upper end face of the field shaper, and there was still some leakage flux. When R5 was equal to 12 mm, the magnetic field concentration was the highest and the magnetic pressure reached its maximum; When R5 was greater than 12 mm, the increase in the coil impedance caused a decrease in the discharge current and a corresponding decrease in the magnetic pressure. This analysis provides a key criterion for our optimization design: the inner radius of the coil should be slightly larger than the inner hole radius of the field shaper and located within its upper end face, where the magnetic field concentration effect is optimal and the magnetic pressure on the surface of the sheet is highest.

Fig. 7The influence of coil parameters on magnetic pressure: (a) the coil radius, and (b) the coil turns.

The influence of coil parameters on magnetic pressure: (a) the coil radius, and (b) the coil turns.

(2) Analysis of the influence of the number of coil turns on the magnetic pressure.

Figure 7(b) shows the variation of magnetic pressure distribution on the surface of the plate for different number of coil turns while keeping the discharge voltage and the other parameters constant. It can be seen that as the number of coil turns N increased, the peak value of the magnetic pressure showed a trend of first increasing and then decreasing, while the distribution of the magnetic pressure remained basically unchanged. According to the Biot-Savart law, the magnetic field strength was positively correlated with the ampere-turns NI. An increase in the number of turns N of the coil would lead to an increase in the load in the circuit, resulting in a decrease in the current I of the discharge circuit. Therefore, there was a maximum value for the ampere-turns, NI and the magnetic induction intensity, B. Based on the above analysis, it can be concluded that when the number of coil turns is 8 or 9, the magnetic pressure on the surface of the plate is relatively high.

(3) Analysis of the influence of the thickness of the field shaper on the magnetic pressure.

The current directions were opposite on the upper and lower surfaces of the field shaper. To avoid mutual cancellation, the thickness of the field shaper should be greater than twice its skin depth. In magnetic pulse welding, the discharge frequency is usually in the range of 7–12 kHz. Through calculation, it was determined that the thickness of the field shaper should not be less than 2 mm. Figure 8(a) shows the variation of the magnetic pressure distribution on the surface of the plate for different thicknesses of field shapers while keeping the discharge voltage and the other parameters constant. The surface magnetic pressure of the sheet first increased and then decreased with the increase of the thickness of the field shaper, and the pressure reached its peak at a thickness of approximately 5 mm. The reason for this was that when the thickness was small, the cancellation effect of the reverse magnetic field generated on the upper and lower surfaces dominated, and the increase in the thickness could weaken this cancellation effect. When the thickness exceeded the critical value, the electromagnetic coupling effect between the coil, field shaper, and sheet weakened and became dominant, leading to a decrease in the magnetic pressure.

(4) Analysis of the influence of the inner radius of the upper end face of the field shaper on the magnetic pressure.

Figure 8(b) shows the variation pattern of the magnetic pressure distribution obtained by changing the inner radius R3 of the upper end face while maintaining the inner hole radius of the lower end face at 6 mm. The magnetic pressure on the surface of the sheet first increased and then decreased with the increase of the inner radius of the upper end face of the field shaper. The main reason for the decrease in the magnetic pressure was the increase in the magnetic leakage when the inner diameter of the upper end surface exceeded that of the coil. If the radius of the coil were increased, the magnetic pressure would be further weakened. Through simulation analysis, it was found that when the inner radius of the lower end face of the field shaper was 4–8 mm, and the inner radius of the upper end face of the field shaper was approximately twice that of the lower end face, that is, when the inner hole of the field shaper was a slanted hole, the magnetic pressure on the surface of the plate was relatively high.

The influence of parameters of the field shaper on magnetic pressure: (a) the thickness, (b) the inner radius of the upper end face, (c) the outer radius of the upper end surfaces, and (d) the outer radius of the lower end surfaces.

(5) Analysis of the influence of the outer radius of the upper and lower end surfaces of the field shaper on the magnetic pressure.

Based on the above analysis of the influence of the inner radius and number of turns of the coil on the surface magnetic pressure of the plate, it can be concluded that when the coil was located on the upper end face of the field shaper and had eight or nine turns, the surface magnetic pressure of the plate was relatively high. With this premise, keeping the discharge voltage and the other parameters constant, the variation law of the magnetic pressure distribution that was obtained by changing the outer radius of the upper end face of the field shaper was determined, as shown in Fig. 8(c). It can be seen from the figure that as the outer radius of the upper end face of the field shaper increased, the distribution and the maximum value of the magnetic pressure on the surface of the plate remained basically unchanged. From this, it can be concluded that when the parameters of the upper end face of the field shaper matched those of the coil, the magnetic pressure on the surface of the plate can be optimized.

Based on the above analysis, when the inner radius of the lower end face of the field shaper was 8 mm, the variation law of the magnetic pressure distribution that was obtained by changing the outer radius of the lower end face of the field shaper while keeping the discharge voltage and other parameters unchanged was determined, as shown in Fig. 8(d). An increase in the outer radius of the lower end surface of the field shaper would result in a decrease in the peak magnetic pressure on the plate surface, but the pressure distribution remained unchanged. The mechanism was that the total amount of induced current was essentially constant, and an increase in the outer diameter would cause a decrease in the current density, thereby weakening the magnetic pressure. To balance the welding strength, the outer radius could not be too small to ensure a sufficient working area. When the outer radius of the lower end surface of the field shaper exceeded the inner radius by approximately 5 mm, the edge magnetic pressure had decayed to below 50% of the peak value, and effective welding points could not be formed. Therefore, it was recommended to set the outer radius to “inner radius + 5 mm”, which was the minimum allowable value to meet the welding strength requirements.

Proposal of a new type field shaper with an arc-shaped hole structure

Based on the above simulation, further analysis showed that the induced current generated on the upper end face of the field shaper was divided into three circuits by the lower end face, inner hole face, and outer wall face of the field shaper, and the magnitude of the divided current was related to their respective impedances. Due to the non-uniform distribution of the current, it was not possible to quantitatively calculate their respective impedances, and only the relative sizes could be used for comparison and judgment. Through simulation, it was known that the impedance of the outer wall surface was the highest, and only a small portion of the current formed a circuit through the outer wall surface. When the inner radius of the upper end face of the field shaper was small, the impedance of the inner hole surface was also small, which diverted some of the eddy currents. As the inner radius of the upper end face increased, the area of the inner hole surface also increased, the impedance increased, and the diverted eddy current decreased. Therefore, within a certain range, increasing the inner radius of the upper end face of the field shaper helped to increase the magnetic pressure on the surface of the plate. Therefore, without changing the inner radius of the lower end face, increasing the area of the inner hole surface could ensure that the equivalent impedance of the lower end face remained unchanged while increasing the equivalent impedance of the inner hole surface, thereby allowing more induced current to flow through the lower end face and enhancing the magnetic collection effect of the field shaper. This could increase the magnetic pressure on the surface of the plate without changing the distribution of the peak magnetic pressure.

Based on the above analysis of the equivalent impedance of the field shaper, it could be inferred that by further optimizing the geometric shape of the inner hole surface, the path allocation of induced current could be more effectively adjusted. The traditional straight hole design (Fig. 9(a)) resulted in a lower surface impedance of the inner hole, diverting some of the induced current that should have flowed to the lower end face. The inclined hole design (Fig. 9(b)) increased the impedance of the path by increasing the surface area of the inner hole. On this basis, an innovative arc-shaped hole structure was proposed (Fig. 9(c)). The core scientific assumption of this design was that a streamlined inner hole composed of a quarter elliptical arc could generate a larger equivalent inner hole radius than a simple inclined hole, thereby further increasing the impedance of the inner hole surface. This could allow more current to flow through the lower surface, ultimately achieving the goal of increasing the magnetic pressure on the surface of the sheet.

Fig. 9Field shapers with different inner hole structures: (a) straight hole, (b) oblique hole, and (c) arc-shaped hole.

Field shapers with different inner hole structures: (a) straight hole, (b) oblique hole, and (c) arc-shaped hole.

To verify this hypothesis, the magnetic pressure and current density distributions under three types of field concentrators were compared. The simulation results (Figs. 10 and 11) strongly supported our design concept. Figure 10 shows a comparison of the distribution patterns of the magnetic pressure obtained by simulating field shapers with a straight hole, an inclined hole, and an arc-shaped hole, while maintaining a lower end face inner hole radius of 8 mm and an upper end face inner hole radius of 14 mm. From the figure, it can be seen that at a discharge voltage of 10 kV, the peak magnetic pressure obtained using a straight hole field shaper was 187 MPa. The peak magnetic pressure obtained using a slanted hole field shaper was 256 MPa, and the magnetic pressure on the surface of the plate was approximately 36.9% higher than that of a straight hole field shaper. The peak magnetic pressure obtained by using the arc-shaped hole field shaper was 298 MPa, and the magnetic pressure on the surface of the plate was further increased by 16.4% compared to the inclined hole field shaper. This indicated that the proposed arc-shaped hole field shaper could more effectively improve the magnetic pressure on the surface of the plate.

More importantly, the current density cloud map (Fig. 11) clearly revealed its intrinsic physical mechanism. From the figure, it can be seen that when using a straight hole field shaper, the maximum current density at the bottom of the field shaper was 6.05 × 1010 A/m2, and there was still a significant amount of induced current flowing through the inner hole surface. The use of an inclined hole field shaper could effectively increase the impedance of the inner hole surface, allowing more of the induced current to be diverted through the lower end face. At that time, the maximum current density at the bottom of the field shaper was 7.19 × 1010 A/m2, with a peak increase of 18.8%. The use of an arc-shaped hole field shaper further increased the impedance of the inner hole surface of the field shaper. At that point, the maximum current density at the bottom of the field shaper increased to 7.72 × 1010 A/m2, a further increase of 7.4%. This proved that our arc-shaped hole design had successfully suppressed the internal hole shunt effect of current through geometric optimization, driving more induced current to the lower end face of the workpiece, thereby significantly enhancing the magnetic pressure output without increasing any external energy. This was precisely the core innovation and scientific contribution of this work.

Optimization results of the magnet device

Based on the analysis of the effect of the structural parameters of the coil and field shaper on the surface magnetic pressure of the plate, it can be concluded that only the inner radius of the lower end face of the field shaper would affect the distribution of the peak magnetic pressure area, that is, the size of the welding area. The other structural parameters only affected the amplitude of the magnetic pressure. By selecting appropriate coil and field shaper parameters, the magnetic pressure acting on the surface of the plate could be effectively increased, thereby improving the collision speed of the plate and ultimately achieving the high-quality spot welding of the plate. Based on the above analysis, the optimized structural parameters of the magnet device were as follows: the number of coil turns N was 8, and the inner radius R5 was 16 mm; the field shaper adopts an arc-shaped hole structure, the inner radius R1 of the lower end surface was 8 mm, the outer radius R2 of the lower end surface was 14 mm, the inner radius R3 of the upper end surface was 15 mm, the outer radius R4 of the upper end surface was 47 mm, and the thickness D was 5 mm.

Multiphysics performance and structural reliability of the optimized magnet device

To ensure the safety and reliability of subsequent welding experiments, it was necessary to conduct a comprehensive evaluation of the performances of the optimized magnet device. The maximum discharge voltage in the experiment was 12 kV. Considering the margin, the electromagnetic, mechanical, and thermal performance of the magnet device were evaluated and verified at a discharge voltage of 13 kV.

To evaluate the mechanical strength of the coil, a simulation analysis was conducted on the Von Mises stress of the optimized coil at a discharge voltage of 13 kV, as shown in Fig. 12. From the figure, it can be seen that the stress distribution in the coil was uneven, and the stress in the inner layer was significantly higher than that in the outer layer, which was mainly attributed to the discharge characteristics of the flat coil. When the coil discharged, it expanded due to the electromagnetic repulsion between turns. The inner region had a larger magnetic field and generated a greater electromagnetic repulsion. Therefore, the von Mises stress in the inner region was higher than that in the outer region. The maximum Von Mises stress of the coil was 136 MPa, which was lower than the yield strength of the copper wire used, 150 MPa. The coil was tightly wound between turns, and the outermost layer was reinforced with Zylon fiber composite material. In actual operation, the discharge voltage did not exceed 12 kV, and the coil did not undergo significant plastic deformation, resulting in high mechanical strength.

From the above analysis, it can be seen that the magnetic field was mainly concentrated in the gap between the field shaper and the coil and plate. During the discharge process, the field shaper was subjected to huge reaction forces from both the coil and plate. Figure 13 shows the Von Mises stress distribution cloud map of the field shapers with different thicknesses at a discharge voltage of 13 kV. When the thickness of the field shaper was less than 10 mm, the stress of the field shaper was greater than its yield strength. Weld morphology and mechanical strength of the joints

To verify the effectiveness of the field shaper with an arc-shaped hole structure, a series of experiments were conducted. In the magnetic pulse welding process of sheet metal, once the circuit parameters and the welding device are set, there are two process parameters that need to be explored: the discharge voltage and the welding gap. After the MPSW experimental platform was built, a series of magnetic pulse welding experiments were first conducted on an AA1060 aluminum sheet and a stainless steel (SS304) sheet with a thickness of 1 mm, with different discharge voltages and inter-sheet gaps. The experimental discharge voltage ranged from 7 kV to 12 kV, with each level being 0.5 kV. The welding gap ranged from 1 mm to 4 mm, with a grade every 0.5 mm. At each welding gap, welding experiments were conducted from low to high voltage. Next, the morphology and cross-section of the welding area of the welding sample were analyzed, and the mechanical properties of the welded joint are further tested to determine the minimum discharge voltage required for welding at each gap to find the optimal gap for welding.

This section analyzes the morphology and cross-section of the welding area of the welding sample with a discharge voltage of 10 kV and a welding gap of 2 mm. Figure 17(a) shows the appearance of the welded sample, Fig. 17(b) shows the appearance of the welded sample after peeling off the aluminum sheet, and Fig. 17(c) shows the morphology of the welded area of the welded sample after being corroded by a strong alkaline solution. Performance analysis of welded joints

To study the tensile properties of spot welded joints, it is necessary to conduct mechanical property tests on samples. For lap joints, the commonly used testing method is tensile testing. To test the strength of the welded joint, tensile experiments were conducted on the welding samples for different discharge voltages with a welding gap of 2 mm. The tensile test specimen was a long strip cut from the welding sample containing the complete welding area, with a length of 150 mm and a width of 30 mm, as shown in Fig. 20(a). Subsequently, tensile tests were conducted on the welded sample specimens on an electronic universal testing machine, with the tensile speed set to 10 mm/min. In addition, since the two welding sheets were not on the same plane, if the sample was directly loaded, a deviation would occur during the tensile test, which would affect the tensile curve. Therefore, two gaskets needed to be placed at both ends when loading the sample to avoid deviation, as shown in Fig. 20(b).

The mechanical performance testing of joints is the key to evaluating welding quality. Figure 21 shows the test results of the tensile testing on the sample. When the discharge voltage was 9, 10, and 11 kV, the sample had significant plastic deformation displacement, ultimately leading to the fracture of the base material. Determination of the welding process window

Based on the above welding experiments and tensile tests on the welded samples, the welding samples could be divided into three types. The first welding sample did not achieve overlap. Although the second type of welding sample achieved overlap, it was prone to joint detachment, resulting in ineffective welding and lower welding strength than the base metal. The third type of welding sample achieved the connection between the aluminum and steel sheets, and the tensile test resulted in the fracture of the base material, with a higher welding strength than the base material. The welding window of the sheets for different welding gaps and discharge voltages (energy) was established through systematic statistical analysis, as shown in Fig. 23. From the welding window shown in the figure, it can be seen that when the welding gap was constant, the discharge voltage was higher, and it became easier to achieve welding. With this welding device, a welding gap of 2–3 mm was more reasonable, requiring a lower discharge voltage (energy) to achieve the welding of the workpiece, and it was more likely that high-quality welded joints would be produced. When the welding gap was less than 2 mm, the acceleration gap was shorter, which resulted in a lower collision speed and difficulty in welding. At this point, welding could only be achieved by increasing the discharge voltage to obtain more energy and improve the collision speed. In addition, a small gap could also result in a smaller collision angle, which was not conducive to the implementation of welding. When the welding gap was greater than 3 mm, the acceleration gap of the weldment was longer. This was beneficial for obtaining a higher collision speed, but the collision angle was also larger, which was not conducive to the implementation of welding. If the gap were increased to a certain value, it would actually reduce the collision speed due to insufficient deformation energy of the sheet, which would also not be conducive to the implementation of welding. From the above analysis, it can be seen that the higher the discharge voltage was, the easier it was to achieve welding, and the higher the welding quality was. For the welding gaps, there was a suitable range. In actual magnetic pulse welding, good coordination between the discharge voltage and the welding gap is required to complete the welding process and achieve high-quality welding. The proposed field shaper with an arc-shaped hole improved the electromagnetic force output efficiency by optimizing the current path allocation, thereby achieving effective welding at lower energy thresholds, significantly expanding the process window, and reflecting the innovation and scientific value of this work in the design and process optimization of magnetic pulse welding equipment.

Microscopic interface analysis

To analyze the microstructure of the welded joint, the polished welding interface was observed using a scanning electron microscope. As shown in Fig. 24, the microstructure of the welding interface was obtained when the welding gap was 2 mm and the discharge voltages were 10, 11, and 12 kV. From the figure, it can be seen that waveform interface features appeared at the welding interface for the three discharge voltages, but the waveform interface varied with the change of the discharge voltage. When the discharge voltage was 10 kV, the interface wave amplitude at the welding interface was small and discontinuous, and there was a flat interface in the middle. When the discharge voltage rose to 11 kV, the amplitude of the interface wave at the welding interface increased significantly and continuously, and there was essentially no flat interface. When the discharge voltage further increased to 12 kV, the amplitude of the interface wave at the welding interface was further increased, but the distribution was not uniform, with some small amplitude interface waves mixed in between. In the aforementioned tensile experiment, the tensile strength of the welded joint increased with the increase of the discharge voltage, and the increase of the discharge voltage was manifested microscopically as an increase in the amplitude of the interface wave. This indicated that the existence and increase of the amplitude of the interface wave contributed to the improvement of the mechanical properties of the welded joint.

Conclusion

In this study, with the goal of maximizing the magnetic pressure, a magnet device for magnetic pulse spot welding was optimized and designed with the parametric scanning method, and a new type of field shaper with an arc-shaped hole structure was innovatively proposed, which effectively enhanced the magnetic gathering effect and improved the welding quality. Based on this, a welding experimental platform was built to achieve the high-quality spot welding of aluminum and stainless steel sheets both with a thickness of 1 mm, thereby verifying the effectiveness of the arc-shaped hole structure field shaper.

Engrailed 2 facilitates progression of triple-negative and HER2-enriched breast cancer by binding to enhancer region of Tenascin-C
Authors: Dandan Chen, Rongping Yin
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractEngrailed 2 (EN2) is a homeodomain-containing protein whose aberrant expression is observed in various cancer types, yet its role in breast cancer remains unclear. This study investigates the roles and mechanisms of EN2 in breast cancer progression. Using online dataset analysis, we assessed the correlation between EN2 expression and breast cancer progression and chemotherapeutic sensitivity. Functional assays, including RT-qPCR, Western blot, cell viability, transwell migration and invasion, spheroid formation, and flow cytometry, were conducted to explore EN2's role. Mechanistic insights were obtained through luciferase reporter assays, ChIP, and Caspase 3 activity detection. Our results showed that EN2 is highly expressed in breast cancer patients, negatively correlating with survival rates and positively with disease progression and reduced chemotherapy sensitivity. Functional experiments confirmed EN2's oncogenic role, and it was found to promote the expression of the oncogenic Tenascin-C (TNC) gene. Notably, EN2 directly interacts with the super-enhancer region within the TNC locus. Elevated TNC expression mitigated the effects of EN2 knockdown on breast cancer cell progression. Our study unveils a novel mechanism by which EN2 regulates the TNC locus super-enhancer, thereby activating oncogenic pathways in breast cancer.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s12672-024-01471-6.Keywords: Engrailed 2, Tenascin-C, Breast cancer, Super-enhancer, Stemness

Introduction

One of the most significant challenges in breast cancer treatment is the heterogeneity and resistance to existing therapies, such as hormone therapy and chemotherapy, presents a major obstacle in managing the disease effectively [1]. HER2-enriched and triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC) are two relatively aggressive types of breast cancer. Notably, there is still no targets for TNBC. Thus, it is an urgent need to identify valuable targets for breast cancer, especially for HER2-enriched and TNBC.

Engrailed 2 (EN2) is a transcription factor known for its pivotal role in embryonic development, particularly in the formation and patterning of the central nervous system [2, 3]. Its dysregulation has been revealed in various malignancies, for example, Y Li et al. recently indicated that there was a notable elevation in the expression of EN2, which exerted an oncogenic role and significantly intensified the malignant characteristics of non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) cells [4]; Y Cao et al. revealed that EN2 exhibited a higher level in esophageal squamous cell carcinoma (ESCC) and functioned in a transcription-independent manner [5]. Additionally, targeting EN2 has been shown attenuate the growth, angiogenesis, and metastasis of colorectal cancer [6]. Similarly, Y Li et al. showed that targeting EN2 could suppress the invasion and proliferation of bladder cancer [7]. Although the previous study has demonstrated that the sustained expression of EN2 is essential for preserving the transformed characteristics of a human breast tumor cell line [8], however, the precise mechanisms by which EN2 contributes to tumorigenesis, especially in the context of TNBC, are still confusing.

Crucially, our research transcends conventional analyses by elucidating a novel molecular mechanism through which EN2 exerts its oncogenic effects in breast cancer, including HER2-enriched breast cancer and TNBC. Based on the bioinformatics analysis [9], we predicted that EN2 directly binds to the super-enhancer (SE) region in the Tenascin-C (TNC) locus, resulting in the upregulation of TNC expression. TNC, an extracellular matrix glycoprotein, has been recognized as an oncogene in various cancer types, contributing to tumor progression, invasion, and metastasis [10–12]. In breast cancer, TNC has been shown to immobilize infiltrating T lymphocytes through CXCL12 and thus promote breast cancer progression [13]. Importantly, perivascular TNC can initiate a series of events leading to the successive activation of macrophages and endothelial cells, fostering the formation of a pro-metastatic vascular microenvironment in the lungs conducive to the colonization of breast cancer cells [14]. However, blocking TNC protein degradation was demonstrated to inhibit TNBC susceptibility to cytotoxicity mediated by T cells [15]. Given that these seemingly contradictory research findings, it needs more efforts to elucidate the underlying mechanisms by which TNC gene is regulated in breast cancer, especially for HER2-enriched and TNBC.

In the present study, we started with an unbiased approach to check expression of EN2 in all breast cancer subtypes and found that EN2 to be highly expressed in HER2-enriched and TNBC subtypes. Hence, we selected these two subtypes/cell lines for further experimentation. We indicated that EN2 promoted HER2-enriched and TNBC progression, at least in part, through the modulation of TNC expression. The rescue of cell proliferation, apoptosis, migration, invasion, stemness, and chemotherapeutic resistance by TNC overexpression further emphasizes the functional dependency of EN2-mediated effects on TNC expression. Our study indicates that EN2 might be a potential target for HER2-enriched breast cancer and TNBC.

Materials and methods

Cell lines

The MCF-10A human mammary epithelial cells and various breast cancer cell lines, including luminal-A (MCF-7, T47D), luminal-B (BT474), TNBC (MDA-MB-231), and HER2-enriched (MDA-MB-453), were acquired from the China Center for Type Culture Collection (Wuhan, China). Cultivation of these cells occurred in DMEM medium supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS, Sigma), along with 1% penicillin and streptomycin. The incubation of the cell culture took place in a consistently maintained sterile incubator at 37℃ with 5% CO2. All cell lines used in the study has been validated by STR profiling before experimentation and without mycoplasma contamination. And the Estrogen Receptor (ER), Progesterone Receptor (PR), and Human Epidermal Growth Factor Receptor 2 (HER2) statuses in all cell lines were confirmed via Western blot analysis (Supplementary Figure S1).

Real-time quantitative PCR (RT-qPCR)

Total RNA extraction involved the lysis of tissues or cells using TRIzol reagent (Invitrogen, USA). To assess RNA concentration and purity, a Nano Drop UV spectrophotometer was utilized. Following this, cDNA synthesis was executed through reverse transcription, employing the PrimeScript™ RT reagent kit (TaKaRa, China). The resulting cDNA served as the template for amplification in accordance with the PCR kit instructions (Invitrogen, USA). The internal reference for this process was β-actin, and the quantification of relative gene expression was conducted utilizing the 2−ΔΔCt method. The RT-qPCR primer sequences were mentioned in Supplementary Table S1.

Western blot

Total protein extraction was accomplished by introducing cell lysis solution. The protein concentration was quantified using the BCA assay kit (Beyotime, Beijing, China). Subsequently, 30 μg of protein was utilized for SDS-PAGE. Following electrophoresis, the proteins were electrotransferred onto nitrocellulose membranes and treated with 5% skim milk for 2 h. The primary antibody was introduced and allowed to incubate overnight at 4℃ in a shaker. Subsequently, a secondary antibody was applied for the incubation at room temperature for 2 h, and ECL (Vazyme, Nanjing, China) was introduced to facilitate development, followed by exposure for image capture. The detailed information about used antibodies was listed in Supplementary Table S2.

Vector construction, gene knockdown, and transfection

Small interfering RNAs (siRNAs) specific for EN2 were synthesized by GenePharma (Shanghai, China). TNC overexpression and corresponding empty plasmids were purchased from SinoBiological (Beijing, China). Cells were transfected with siRNA or plasmid using GP-transfect-Mate (GenePharma, Inc.).

Cell viability assay

Two thousand cells with different treatments per well were added into 96-well plates. The ability of cells to proliferate was evaluated using the Cell Counting Kit-8 (CCK-8, Beyotime, China) assay. After 0, 24, 48, and 72 h, the culture medium was discarded and cells were incubated with CCK-8 (10 μL) at 37 °C for 1 h. OD450 values were calculated at using an enzyme marker (Infinite 2000-PRO, TECAN, Switzerland).

Transwell migration and invasion assay

Cell migration capability was assessed using 8 μm Transwell chambers from Corning (NY, USA). For analyzing cell invasion ability, the chambers that contained Matrigel (BD Biosciences, San Jose, CA) diluted 1:70 with DMEM were used. Briefly, the upper chambers were used to cultivate cells in 100 uL serum-free DMEM. 700 uL of DMEM containing 30% FBS were poured into each lower chamber for chemo-attractor. Cells were fixed by methanol and stained by crystal violet (Beyotime, China) after 48 h. In order to calculate the averages, three equally spaced cell sections per chamber were chosen for counting.

Spheroid-formation assay

Cells were seeded into ultra-low-adsorption culture plates (Corning Incorporated, Corning, NY, USA) with StemXVivo Serum-Free Tumorsphere Media (R&D Systems, Minneapolis, MN, USA). After a 14-day period, the count was conducted for spheroids with a diameter exceeding 50 μm.

Cell apoptosis assay

Cell apoptosis was analyzed based on the instructions of the Annexin V-FITC/propidium iodide (PI) double staining kit (Beyotime). In short, cells were suspended in 500 μL of binding buffer and labeled with PI (5 μL) and Annexin V-FITC (5 μL) for 20 min at room temperature. Then 400 μL of binding buffer was added to the resuspended cells. Finally, cells were analyzed by FACSCalibur flow cytometer (Becton Dickinson). Quantitation of apoptotic cells was calculated with CellQuest software. Apoptotic cells were stained positive for Annexin V.

Detection of cell subpopulation with stemness

As the CD44 + /CD24- subpopulation was considered to the cells with stemness in breast cancer [16, 17], the stemness of breast cancer was evaluated by measuring the ratio of this subpopulation. The detailed procedure was followed by the previous study [18]. In brief, single cells derived from cell lines were first washed with PBS and then re-suspended in a solution containing anti-CD24-PE (BD Biosciences, Cat # 555428, USA) and anti-CD44-APC (BD Biosciences, Cat # 559942, USA) antibodies. The cell suspension was incubated at 4 °C for 30 min in the dark. After the incubation, the labeled cells were washed with PBS and subsequently analyzed using a flow cytometer (BD, USA). The negative fraction was determined using suitable isotype controls.

Luciferase reporter assay

The promoter sequences with mutant or the wild-type binding sites of EN2 were inserted into pGL3-promoter vector. Then cells were transfected with corresponding plasmids for 72 h and fluorescence activity was detected using a Dual Fluorokinase Reporter Gene Assay Kit (Beyotime).

Chromatin immunoprecipitation (ChIP) analysis

ChIP-IT High Sensitivity® (Active Motif, Shanghai, China) was used to detect the TNC gene promoter abundance pulled down by antibody against EN2 and H3K27ac following the manufacturer’s recommendation.

Caspase 3 activity detection

GreenNuc™ Caspase-3 Assay Kit (Beyotime) for Live Cells was used for detecting caspase 3 activity in TNBC and HER2-enriched cells according to the standard procedures.

Statistical analysis

The data were subjected to analysis using GraphPad Prism statistical software. The comparison of the overall means between groups was performed using a t-test, and statistical significance was determined with a threshold of p < 0.05.

Results

EN2 is highly expressed in breast cancer patients and negatively correlated with the survivals

In our analysis utilizing the TNMplot online dataset tool (https://tnmplot.com/analysis/) (Gene chip data including 70 paired breast tumor and adjacent normal tissues) [19], we observed a significant upregulation of EN2 in breast cancer patients (Fig. 1A). Moreover, a negative correlation emerged between EN2 expression and overall survival (OS) (Fig. 1B), post-progression survival (PPS) (Fig. 1C), relapse-free survival (RFS) (Fig. 1D), and distant-metastasis free survival (DMFS) (Fig. 1E) of breast cancer patients through the Kaplan–Meier Plotter online dataset analysis (http://kmplot.com/analysis/index.php?p=background) [20]. Notably, it was found that EN2 expression was only negatively correlated with the OS of TNBC and HER2-enriched breast cancer, but not with luminal-A and luminal-B (Fig. 1F–I). Consequently, we posit that EN2 may play a pivotal role in promoting tumor progression in breast cancer, especially in HER2-enriched breast cancer and TNBC.

Knockdown of EN2 suppresses the proliferation and promotes the apoptosis of HER2-enriched breast cancer and TNBC cells

To explore the influence of EN2 on the progression of breast cancer, we initially started with an unbiased approach to check expression of EN2 in all breast cancer subtypes and mammary epithelial cells. Unvaryingly, EN2 exhibited heightened expression in breast cancer cells, particularly in HER2-enriched breast cancer (MDA-MB-453) and TNBC (MDA-MB-231) cells. Hence, we selected these two subtypes/cell lines for further experimentation (Fig. 2A). Subsequently, we performed EN2 knockdown in HER2-enriched breast cancer and TNBC cells, confirming the efficiency through RT-qPCR assay (Fig. 2B). The CCK8 assay revealed that the suppression of EN2 led to a decrease in the proliferation of HER2-enriched breast cancer and TNBC cells (Fig. 2C, D). Furthermore, it was found that EN2 knockdown promoted the apoptosis of HER2-enriched breast cancer and TNBC cells via flow cytometry analysis (Fig. 2E, F). Moreover, detection of Caspase 3 activity obtained a consistent result, as evidenced by the increase of Caspase 3 activity in HER2-enriched breast cancer and TNBC cells with EN2 knockdown (Fig. 2G).

EN2 knockdown attenuates the migration and invasion ability of HER2-enriched breast cancer and TNBC cells

Given that the majority of breast cancer-related fatalities result from metastasis, we conducted a comprehensive assessment of the impact of EN2 on the metastatic potential of HER2-enriched breast cancer and TNBC cells. As depicted in Figs. 3A, D, transwell migration analysis demonstrated a significant reduction in the migratory capacity of HER2-enriched breast cancer and TNBC cells upon EN2 knockdown. Concurrently, EN2 knockdown exerted a suppressive effect on the invasive ability of HER2-enriched breast cancer and TNBC cells (Fig. 3A, D). Additionally, we investigated the potential involvement of EN2 in the Epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT) process, a critical driver of breast cancer metastasis [18]. The findings revealed that EN2 knockdown notably hindered the EMT process in HER2-enriched breast cancer and TNBC cells, as evidenced by the increased expression of epithelial markers and decreased expression of mesenchymal markers (Fig. 3–G). These outcomes collectively indicate that EN2 functions as a promoter of tumor progression in HER2-enriched breast cancer and TNBC cells.

EN2 knockdown reduces the stemness and chemotherapeutic resistance of HER2-enriched breast cancer and TNBC cells

Through online dataset analysis (ROC Plotter—Online ROC analysis: https://www.rocplot.com/) [21], we revealed that EN2 expression was negatively correlated with chemotherapeutic response in breast cancer patients (Fig. 4A, B). the enhanced chemotherapeutic sensitivity of HER2-enriched breast cancer and TNBC cells was observed upon EN2 knockdown, as illustrated in Fig. 4C, D. Recognizing that cancer stem cells (CSCs) are considered pivotal contributors to both tumor metastasis and drug resistance [22, 23], we further explored the roles of EN2 in HER2-enriched breast cancer and TNBC cell stemness. Cytometry analysis revealed that EN2 knockdown significantly reduced the CD44 + /CD24- subpopulation of HER2-enriched breast cancer and TNBC cells (Fig. 4E), which has been considered to be cell subpopulation with stemness [24]. Additionally, the spheroid-formation ability of HER2-enriched breast cancer and TNBC cells was significantly hindered upon EN2 knockdown, demonstrated by a notable reduction in both spheroid size and number, as depicted in Fig. 4F, G. Collectively, these findings indicate that EN2 plays a role in promoting the progression of HER2-enriched breast cancer and TNBC cells.

EN2 binds to the super-enhancer region of TNC gene promoter in HER2-enriched breast cancer and TNBC cells

Next, we investigated the downstream mediators of EN2 responsible for EN2-mediated effects on HER2-enriched breast cancer and TNBC cell progression. Being a transcription factor, EN2 has the capability to interact with the promoter region of target genes. Then we performed an online analysis (https://jaspar.genereg.net/search/?q=&collection=CORE&tax_group=vertebrates) to analyze the potential target genes of EN2 and found that TNC, which is well-studied oncogene. To validate this predicted result, we utilized RT-qPCR and western blot analysis and observed a substantial reduction in the expression of TNC genes in HER2-enriched breast cancer and TNBC cells following the knockdown of EN2 (Fig. 5A, B). Further luciferase reporter assay revealed that EN2 knockdown attenuated the activity of TNC gene promoter without affecting the activity of TNC gene promoter with mutant binding sites (Fig. 5C). Consistently, ChIP analysis demonstrated that EN2 was enriched in the promoter region of TNC gene promoter in TNBC cells (Fig. 5D). Moreover, given that H3K27ac is a recognized histone mark associated with active super-enhancers, we investigated the potential enrichment of H3K27ac in the promoter region of the TNC gene. As expected, we observed an enrichment of H3K27ac in this region in TNBC cells (Fig. 5E). These findings indicate that EN2 has the capability to interact with the super-enhancer region of the TNC gene, consequently facilitating the expression of the TNC gene in HER2-enriched breast cancer and TNBC cells.

EN2 promotes the progression of HER2-enriched breast cancer and TNBC cells dependent on TNC gene

Finally, we investigated the crucial role of TNC in mediating the promotional effects of EN2 on HER2-enriched breast cancer and TNBC progression. To verify this hypothesis, we conducted overexpression of TNC in HER2-enriched breast cancer and TNBC cells with EN2 knockdown, and the co-transfection efficiency was confirmed through RT-qPCR analysis (Fig. 6A, B). We found that EN2 knockdown-mediated inhibition on HER2-enriched breast cancer and TNBC cell proliferation was rescued by TNC overexpression (Fig. 6C, D). Furthermore, TNC overexpression partially reversed the increased apoptotic ratio led by EN2 knockdown in HER2-enriched breast cancer and TNBC cells (Fig. 6E). Additionally, the suppressive effects on HER2-enriched breast cancer and TNBC cell migration and invasion ability resulted by EN2 knockdown were attenuated by TNC overexpression (Fig. 6F–H). Consistently, EN2 knockdown-induced both downregulation of HER2-enriched breast cancer and TNBC cell stemness and upregulation chemotherapeutic sensitivity were partially abrogated by TNC overexpression (Fig. 6I, J). Therefore, these results demonstrate that EN2 functions as an oncogene in HER2-enriched breast cancer and TNBC progression dependent on TNC gene.

Discussion

In the present study, we started with an unbiased approach to check expression of EN2 in all breast cancer subtypes and found that EN2 to be highly expressed in HER2-enriched and TNBC subtypes. Thus, we selected these two subtypes/cell lines for further experimentation. This study sheds light on the previously unexplored role of EN2 in the context of HER2-enriched breast cancer and TNBC and its intricate interplay with TNC. Our findings reveal that EN2, a transcription factor known for its critical involvement in embryonic development, exhibits dysregulation in breast cancer, with particularly elevated expression in HER2-enriched breast cancer and TNBC. This dysregulation is not only associated with adverse clinical outcomes but also correlates with diminished sensitivity to chemotherapy, highlighting the potential clinical significance of EN2 in TNBC. Notably, we must admit that EN expression seemed to be negative correlated with the OS and PPS of breast cancer patients without significance, which might be due to the limited sample size on these online datasets.

The observed oncogenic roles of EN2 in TNBC, as demonstrated through a battery of functional experiments, underscore its importance as a potential therapeutic target. The suppression of HER2-enriched breast cancer and TNBC cell proliferation, promotion of apoptosis, and attenuation of migration and invasion upon EN2 knockdown collectively indicate its pivotal contribution to HER2-enriched breast cancer and TNBC progression. Additionally, the impact of EN2 extends to the regulation of CSC-like properties, emphasizing its multifaceted role in driving HER2-enriched breast cancer and TNBC aggressiveness. However, the previous study showed that EN2 functioned as a glioma suppressor and could enhance the sensitivity of temozolomine [25], indicating that EN2 might act different roles in individual tumor types. As far as we are aware, this study represents the initial exploration of the involvement of EN2 in the stemness of tumor cells. Considering the pivotal roles played by tumor cell stemness in both tumor progression and drug resistance, forthcoming research endeavors may delve into investigating the impact of EN2 on the stemness characteristics of various other tumor types.

A key revelation of our study is the direct binding of EN2 to SE region in the TNC locus, leading to the upregulation of TNC expression, which is consistent with a recent study [4]. These two consistent results suggest that TNC is a critical downstream effector contributing to EN2-mediated effects on HER2-enriched breast cancer and TNBC progression. Although TNC has been implicated as an oncogene in various cancer types [12], the specific regulatory mechanisms governing TNC expression in HER2-enriched breast cancer and TNBC have remained elusive until now. The identification of EN2 as a regulator of TNC expression unveils a novel molecular axis contributing to HER2-enriched breast cancer and TNBC progression. The functional relevance of the EN2-TNC axis is underscored by our experiments demonstrating that TNC overexpression rescues the inhibitory effects of EN2 knockdown on HER2-enriched breast cancer and TNBC cell proliferation, apoptosis, migration, invasion, and stemness. This supports the notion that EN2 exerts its oncogenic effects, at least in part, through the activation of TNC-mediated signaling pathways. The dependency of HER2-enriched breast cancer and TNBC progression on the EN2-TNC axis provides a potential target for therapeutic intervention in HER2-enriched breast cancer and TNBC. Furthermore, the observed association between EN2 expression and chemotherapy resistance highlights its potential role in guiding treatment decisions and predicting therapeutic outcomes in HER2-enriched breast cancer and TNBC patients. However, it must be noted that there might be other downstream effectors essential for EN2-mediated effects on HER2-enriched breast cancer and TNBC progression, such as a previous study has indicated that EN2 can regulate E-cadherin and Vimentin expression through Snail2 [26], further multi-omics techniques can be used to explore the critical downstream executors of EN2 during HER2-enriched breast cancer and TNBC progression, such as single cell RNA sequencing [27], which might facilitate the discovery of novel targets for HER2-enriched breast cancer and TNBC treatment.

In conclusion, our study unravels a novel regulatory network involving EN2 and TNC in HER2-enriched breast cancer and TNBC. The identified EN2-TNC axis not only expands our understanding of the molecular underpinnings of HER2-enriched breast cancer and TNBC but also presents potential avenues for targeted therapeutic interventions. Further investigations into the specific downstream effectors and signaling pathways activated by this axis are warranted, with the ultimate goal of translating these findings into clinically relevant strategies for the management of HER2-enriched breast cancer and TNBC.

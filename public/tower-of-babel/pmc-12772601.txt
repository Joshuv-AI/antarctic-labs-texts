Caregiver strain, anxiety and depression among family caregivers of adult cancer patients: a cross-sectional study in a tertiary hospital in the Philippines
Authors: Everlyn Tan Jaji
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveFamily caregivers are essential to the care and support of adult cancer patients yet they often experience caregiver strain, anxiety and depression. Despite the psychological and emotional burden they endure, formal assessments of their mental health needs are seldom conducted. This study aimed to determine the prevalence of caregiver strain, anxiety and depression among family caregivers of adult cancer patients at a tertiary hospital in the Philippines.MethodsThis study included 346 family caregivers chosen through purposive sampling at the cancer centre of a tertiary hospital in the Philippines. It used the Modified Caregiver Strain Index-Pilipino version to identify cases of caregiver strain, and the Hospital Anxiety and Depression Scale-Pilipino version to identify cases of anxiety and depression. Data were analysed using descriptive statistics in frequencies and percentages.ResultsAmong the family caregivers of adult cancer patients undergoing outpatient treatment at the cancer centre of a tertiary hospital in the Philippines, the prevalence of caregiver strain was 10%. Moreover, the prevalence of anxiety was 33%, and the prevalence of depression was 30%.ConclusionThough the majority of family caregivers did not experience significant caregiver strain, anxiety or depression, a notable proportion still reported symptoms. These findings highlight the need for screening and monitoring of family caregivers so that targeted mental health interventions and support can be given to improve their well-being and caregiving capacity.Keywords: Family management, Depression, Home Care Services, Outpatients, Psychological care

Background

Cancer is a condition where certain cells in the body multiply uncontrollably and can spread to different areas of the body. In 2020, the GLOBOCAN project estimated that there were 19 million new cancer cases globally, with nearly 10 million lives lost to the disease.1 In the Philippines, a total of 153 751 new cancer cases with 92 606 cancer deaths were recorded in 2020.2 The diagnosis of cancer, its subsequent phases and its treatment brings intense stress both for the patient and for the family.3 With the increasing number of cancer care provided at home as a result of increased use of outpatient services for cancer treatment, the family members become the ultimate caregivers of cancer patients.3

Family caregivers are susceptible to various physical and emotional problems from the initial to advanced stage of cancer.4 When caregivers experience poor psychological health, people with cancer experience poorer care outcomes and psychological distress which can negatively impact patient outcomes.5 Local studies that examined prevalence of caregiver strain among family caregivers of paediatric cancer patients and adult patients with chronic, debilitating or life-threatening illnesses revealed that family caregivers experience significant amounts of caregiver strain.6 7 Several international studies also report a considerably high prevalence of anxiety and depression among family caregivers of adult cancer patients.8,10

Despite the number of international studies demonstrating the psychological distress of caregiving among family caregivers, limited studies have been done locally to determine the prevalence of caregiver strain, anxiety and depression among family caregivers of adult cancer patients. This study aimed to contribute to bridging that gap. Early detection of caregiver strain, anxiety and depression is important so that prompt referral to a specialist like a family physician or psychiatrist can be done to ensure family caregivers maintain their caregiving capacity.

Methodology

Study design

The study used a descriptive cross-sectional study design to determine the prevalence of caregiver strain, anxiety and depression among family caregivers of adult cancer patients undergoing outpatient treatment in a tertiary hospital in the Philippines. The population of this study was adult family caregivers of adult cancer patients recruited as they came to the facility. The sampling technique used was purposive sampling. This study used a self-administered questionnaire composed of: (1) a data collection form to gather the demographic profile of family caregivers, (2) Modified Caregiver Strain Index-Pilipino (MCSI-P) to determine cases of caregiver strain and (3) Hospital Anxiety and Depression Scale-Pilipino (HADS-P) to determine cases of anxiety and depression. The demographic background of family caregivers and the prevalence of caregiver strain, anxiety and depression were analysed using descriptive statistics and are expressed in frequencies and percentages. All numerical values were rounded to the nearest whole number to facilitate clear and consistent reporting.

Setting

The study was conducted in the cancer centre of a tertiary hospital in the Philippines. It is an apex tertiary hospital located in Southern Philippines. It is the only cancer centre based in a government facility that provides outpatient treatment to cancer patients around the region. Data collection was started in November 2022 and completed in June 2023.

Subjects

Subjects that were recruited for this study were adults at least 18 years old and above, who were caring for a family member with cancer and who received treatment in a tertiary hospital in the Philippines. The respondents were either the first-degree, second-degree, third-degree or fourth-degree family members by consanguinity or affinity to the cancer patient, who have been caring for the patient for at least 1 month and have not received any formal monetary compensation (ie, salary). Family caregivers who were diagnosed with any psychiatric condition by a physician prior to being a family caregiver were excluded from this study. The respondents were chosen through purposive sampling. The respondents were informed through a written consent that the research is voluntary and included their right to withdraw from the study prior to giving the self-administered questionnaire. The respondents were also given opportunities to ask questions and to review their remarks. They were informed that they may be contacted in the future in case of any questions.

Variables and data collection

This study used a self-administered questionnaire composed of a data collection form to gather the demographic profile of family caregivers and validated tools in the Filipino language to determine cases of caregiver strain, anxiety and depression. Caregiver strain was measured using the 11-item MCSI-P. A score of 24–28 was interpreted as predisposition to strain and a score of ≥29 was interpreted as severe caregiver strain. Both predisposition to strain and severe caregiver strain were included in the prevalence of caregiver strain in this study. Anxiety and depression were measured using the HADS-P. The scoring grid for anxiety was as follows: 8–10 (mild anxiety), 11–14 (moderate anxiety) and 15–21 (severe anxiety). In this study, mild, moderate and severe anxiety was included in the prevalence of anxiety. The scoring grid for depression was as follows: 8–10 (mild depression), 11–14 (moderate depression) and 15–21 (severe depression). In this study, mild, moderate and severe depression was included in the prevalence of depression.

Statistical analysis

Sample size computation was conducted using OpenEpi, indicating a need for 345 respondents. Sample size computation was based on using a power of 80%, margin of error of 5% and CI of 95% and based on the assumption of a caregiver strain prevalence of 66% from the study of Palma and Alip in 2011.7 Data obtained were encoded in Microsoft Excel and analysed in Stata V.18.0. The demographic background of family caregivers and prevalence of caregiver strain, anxiety and depression were analysed using descriptive statistics and are expressed in frequencies and percentages. To ensure clarity and consistency in data presentation, all numerical values were rounded to the nearest whole number prior to analysis.

Results

A total of 380 participants were initially recruited for the study. After applying the inclusion and exclusion criteria, 346 participants were included in the analysis. Reasons for exclusion included failure to meet eligibility criteria, for example, friend or neighbour of cancer patient (n=22), less than 18 years old (n=4) and duplicate entries (n=8). This resulted in a final sample size of 346 (see figure 1).

The demographic and socioeconomic profile of the study population is shown in table 1. Most of the respondents of the study were female (66%), with the mean age of 33 years (range 18–77, SD 12.28) and most were single (55%) and college graduates (45%). However, the majority of the respondents were unemployed (73%) and belong to the poor class with a total household income of <Php 10 957 monthly (84%). The caregivers came from diverse tribal backgrounds, predominantly Zamboangueños (30%), Bisaya (29%) and Tausug (28%). Other respondents belong to Lumad, Ilocano, Hiligaynon, Subanen, Kalibugan, Sinama, Maguindanaon, Samal Bangingi and Tagalog. The family relationship to the patient was largely that of children (31%), and most of the respondents had been providing care for less than 6 months (44%). Most of the respondents reported no comorbidities (42%).

Discussion

This study is one of the few studies that measure the prevalence of caregiver strain, anxiety and depression among family caregivers of adult cancer patients seen in the outpatient setting. The prevalence of caregiver strain (10%) was lower compared with cited studies.6 7 However, the prevalence of anxiety and depression (33% and 30%, respectively) was similar to those cited in other studies.8,10 These findings emphasise the need for comprehensive caregiver assessments and inform training programmes to equip providers with skills to address caregiver mental health alongside patient care. Recognising and addressing caregiver mental health challenges can reduce healthcare costs in the long term by preventing burnout and maintaining the caregiver’s ability to support the patient effectively.

The low prevalence of caregiver strain (10%) was seen in this study compared with the study done by Panganiban-Corales and Medina; this may reflect cultural factors, such as the sense of duty or obligation in family caregiving that can moderate the perception of strain.10 11 Nevertheless, a significant proportion of caregivers showed symptoms of anxiety (33%) and symptoms of depression (30%). These findings are consistent with the literature, which often highlights that caregivers, particularly in low-resource settings, are vulnerable to emotional and psychological distress due to the overwhelming nature of their role.10 12 13 Caregivers often prioritise the needs of the patient, neglecting their own well-being, which can lead to both mental and physical health issues over time.14 15

This study has some limitations; first, the study participants were drawn only from a cancer centre of a tertiary hospital in the Philippines; therefore, the data gathered here are underestimates of the true prevalence of caregiver strain, anxiety and depression among family caregivers all over the country. Lastly, in this study, variables correlating with caregiver strain, anxiety and depression were not analysed.

Conclusion and recommendation

The prevalence of caregiver strain, anxiety and depression in this study population was 10%, 33% and 30% respectively. Since the study participants were drawn only from the cancer centre of a tertiary hospital in the Philippines, despite it being a regional hospital, it may still underestimate the true prevalence of caregiver strain, anxiety and depression among family caregivers all over the country. Hence, a national study or a study involving multiple cancer centres should be done to better capture the prevalence of caregiver strain, anxiety and depression among family caregivers. Also, since this study only included family caregivers of adult cancer patients being treated in the outpatient setting, including family caregivers of paediatric cancer patients as well as those receiving inpatient treatment is recommended for comparison. A correlational study is also recommended to determine factors associated with caregiver strain, anxiety and depression since these were not analysed in this study. Furthermore, a qualitative study may also be done to better understand factors associated with caregiver strain, anxiety and depression. Finally, it is highly suggested that policy guidelines will be formulated so that a protocol for screening and monitoring of caregiver strain, anxiety and depression among family caregivers will be in place.

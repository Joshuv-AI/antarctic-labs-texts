Triangular solutions to a Riemann-Hilbert problem from superelliptic curves
Anwar Al Ghabra 1 , Benjamin Piché 2 , Vasilisa Shramchenko 1

Abstract
. We give fundamental solutions of arbitrarily sized matrix Fuchsian linear systems, in the case where the coefficients B ( i ) B^{(i)} of the systems are matrix solutions of the Schlesinger system that are upper triangular, and whose eigenvalues follow an arithmetic progression of a rational difference. The values on the superdiagonals of the matrices B ( i ) B^{(i)} are given by contour integrals of meromorphic differentials defined on Riemann surfaces obtained by compactification of superelliptic curves. Keywords: Fuchsian linear systems, Riemann-Hilbert problem, isomonodromic deformations, Schlesinger systems, Riemann surfaces, algebraic curves, superelliptic curves.

1. Introduction

Riemann-Hilbert problems are important in mathematics from the theoretical point of view, see the review
[
17
]
. At the same time, they play an important role in various applied mathematics contextes, see for example
[
3
,
19
,
22
,
24
]
and references therein. In this paper, a particular Riemann-Hilbert problem for a matrix function with Fuchsian singularities is solved through the differential equation satisfied by this matrix function. Namely, we consider a matrix Fuchsian linear problem

(1)

\frac{\mathrm{d}\Phi}{\mathrm{d}z}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{B^{(i)}}{z-a_{i}}\Phi

for a
p\times p
matrix
\Phi(z)
with
z\in\mathbb{C}P^{1}.
Here the matrices
B^{(i)}
are independent of
z
but may depend on
a_{1},\dots,a_{N}
. Generically, solutions to this equation have singularities at
z=a_{i}
and
z=\infty
(unless
\sum_{j=1}^{N}{B^{(i)}}=0
) and, under analytical continuation along an element of
\pi_{1}(\mathbb{C}\setminus\{a_{1},\dots,a_{n}\},z_{0})
, a solution
\Phi(z_{0})
transforms to another solution related to
\Phi(z_{0})
by the right multiplication with a constant matrix, called the
monodromy matrix
corresponding to the chosen loop. This matrix depends only on the homotopy class of the loop. One thus obtains a
monodromy representation
of the fundamental group of the complex plane punctured at
a_{i}
, that is a homomorphism
\rho:\pi_{1}(\mathbb{C}\setminus\{a_{1},\dots,a_{N}\},z_{0})\to GL(p,\mathbb{C}),
see
[
2
]

The monodromy representation can also be seen as the holonomy representation of the flat meromorphic connection on the trivial rank
p
vector bundle over
\mathbb{C}P^{1}\setminus\{a_{1},\dots,a_{N},\infty\}
whose connection form is
\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{B^{(i)}}{z-a_{i}}dz.

Once a fundamental solution to (
1
) is found, one may obtain its monodromy matrices.
The inverse problem, that of reconstructing a function
\Phi(z)
from its monodromies, is referred to as a Riemann-Hilbert or inverse monodromy problem. Alternatively, it may be reformulated as a problem of finding two matrix functions, each of which is holomorphic either inside or outside of a closed contour passing trough the points
a_{1},\dots,a_{N}
in the complex plane, such that the two functions are related by the multiplication with a “jump” matrix on the contour. The monodromy matrices associated with the loops around
a_{i}
may be combined to obtain this jump matrix
[
4
,
17
]
. The term
Riemann-Hilbert problem
refers also to the 21st problem from Hilbert’s list, that of reconstructing a Fuchsian equation (
1
) from a collection of monodromy matrices associated with a set of points
a_{1},\dots,a_{N}\in\mathbb{C}

[
4
,
2
]
.

Closely related to the Riemann-Hilbert problems is the search for isomonodromic deformations of Fuchsian systems, that is the question of finding the coefficients
B^{(i)}
in (
1
) for which the monodromy matrices of solutions are independent of the small variations of positions of the Fuchsian singularities, that is variations of the set
\{a_{1},\dots,a_{N}\}
. One way of finding such matrices
B^{(i)}
is by solving the Schlesinger system
[
25
,
5
]
, a system of nonlinear differential equations for
B^{(i)}
with respect to the variables
a_{1},\dots,a_{N}
giving a sufficient condition for the resulting family of Fuchsian system to be isomonodromic, that is to share the same monodromies for
a=(a_{1},\dots,a_{N})
in some set
D\subset\mathbb{C}^{N}
not intersecting the diagonals of
\mathbb{C}^{N}
.

In the case
p=2
and
N=3
, setting by a Möbius transformation
\{a_{1},a_{2},a_{3}\}=\{0,1,x\}
, the Schlesinger system reduces to a Painlevé-VI equation with parameters related to the eigenvalues of the matrices
B^{(i)}
, see for example
[
15
]
.

Solutions to the Schlesinger system are constructed in
[
6
,
20
,
8
,
9
,
10
]
for
p=2
, in
[
13
,
14
]
for
p=2
and
p=3
and

[
12
,
21
,
7
]
for an arbitrary
p
. Most of these solutions are constructed in terms of functions defined on Riemann surfaces and over families of Riemann surfaces. The surfaces are obtained
by considering coverings of
\mathbb{C}P^{1}
ramified over the set of Fuchsian singularities of the associated system (
1
), that is over the set
\{a_{1},\dots,a_{N}\}
and possibly the point at infinity. Allowing the
branching set
to vary, one considers a family of Riemann surfaces naturally associated with system (
1
) and thus with the Schlesinger system.

Solving the Fuchsian system (
1
) having coefficients
B^{(i)}
obtained as solutions to the Schlesinger system in the algebro-geometric form in the above references is typically quite difficult. One exception being
[
21
]
where a Riemann-Hilbert problem with regular singularities was solved for quasi-permutation monodromy matrices (independent of positions of regular singularities), after which the corresponding solution to the Schlesinger system was obtained.

The goal of our paper is to solve system (
1
) for the coefficients
B^{(i)}
obtained in
[
10
,
7
]
.
The solutions of the Schlesinger system constructed in
[
10
,
7
]
are upper triangular and have a relatively simple form due to an additional assumption that the eigenvalues of each matrix
B^{(i)}
form an arithmetic progression with a rational difference, the difference being the same for all
i=1,\dots,N.
The eigenvalues of
B^{(i)}
are called
exponents
of the associated Fuchsian system. Triangular solutions to Schlesinger systems are also investigated in
[
11
]
and
[
13
,
14
]
.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
we recall the construction of matrices
B^{(i)}
from
[
7
]
and the relevant algebraic curves. In Section
3
we present our main result giving solutions to the isomonodromic Fuchsian system with coefficients from
[
7
]
. In Section
4
we discuss the monodromy of our solutions and give examples. In Section
5
, we consider two particular cases of integration contours leading to rational solutions of the Fuchsian system.

2. Schlesinger system and superelliptic curves

Let
B^{(1)},B^{(2)},\ldots,B^{(N)}
be
p\times p
matrices depending on some complex parameters
a_{1},\ldots,a_{N}
defined on a ball
D\in\mathbb{C}^{N}\setminus\{(a_{1},\ldots,a_{N})\,\,|\,\,a_{i}=a_{j}\,\text{for some }\,i\neq j\}
. The Schlesinger system for matrices
B^{(j)}
has the form

(2)

\mathrm{d}B^{(i)}=-\sum_{j=1,j\neq i}^{N}\frac{[B^{(i)},B^{(j)}]}{a_{i}-a_{j}}\mathrm{d}(a_{i}-a_{j}),

with
i=1,\ldots,N.

As a partial differential equations system, the Schlesinger system is given by

(3)

\begin{cases}\frac{\partial B^{(i)}}{\partial a_{j}}=\frac{[B^{(i)},B^{(j)}]}{a_{i}-a_{j}},\quad i\neq j;\\
\frac{\partial B^{(i)}}{\partial a_{i}}=-\sum_{j=1,j\neq i}^{N}\frac{[B^{(i)},B^{(j)}]}{a_{i}-a_{j}}.\end{cases}

The Schlesinger system was derived
[
25
]
as a condition of isomonodromy for the Fuchsian system (
1
) for a matrix function
\Phi(z)\in M_{p}(\mathbb{C})
.
In other words, if the coefficients of system (
1
), the matrices
B^{(j)}\in M_{p}(\mathbb{C})
, satisfy system (
3
) as functions of
a_{1},\dots,a_{N}
, then the monodromy matrices of solutions
\Phi
of (
1
) on closed loops around
a_{j}
in the
z
-plane do not change under small perturbations of positions of
a_{1},\dots,a_{N}
.

In
[
7
]
, some upper triangular matrix solutions to the Schlesinger system (
3
) were found in terms of contour integrals defined on the compact Riemann surfaces associated with superelliptic curves. In this paper, we give the corresponding upper triangular solutions of the Fuchsian system (
1
). The superelliptic curves from
[
7
]
are given by the equation

(4)

\Gamma_{a}=\{(\zeta,w)\in\mathbb{C}^{2}\,|\,w^{m}=\prod_{i=1}^{N}(\zeta-a_{i})\}

and we consider their family
parametrized by
a=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{N})\in D
with some fixed positive integers
m
and
N
. The associated compact Riemann surfaces, denoted by
X_{a}
, are obtained by projectivizing and desingularizing the curves (
4
), see
[
18
]
and see also
[
7
,
23
]
for application to superelliptic curves. Note that the algebraic curves (
4
) are nonsingular. The associated projective curves
\hat{\Gamma}_{a}\subset\mathbb{C}P^{2}
may be singular at the points at infinity denoted collectively by
\{\infty\}
.
By the theorem of resolution of singularities (
[
18
]
, Chapter 7) there exists a holomorphic map
\pi:X_{a}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}P^{2}
with
\pi(X_{a})=\hat{\Gamma}_{a}
and such that the map

\pi:X_{a}\,\backslash\,\pi^{-1}(\{\infty\})\rightarrow\hat{\Gamma}_{a}\,\backslash\,\{\infty\}

is biholomorphic.
Given that the algebraic curves (
4
) are non-singular, the map
\pi
allows us to identify the points of the Riemann surface
X_{a}
with those of the curve
\Gamma_{a}
as follows. We say that
P=(\zeta,w)
is a point of the surface
X_{a}
if
P=\pi^{-1}([\zeta:w:1]).
For the points at infinity on
X_{a}
we use the notation
\{\infty_{1},\dots,\infty_{s}\}:=\pi^{-1}(\{\infty\})
. Note that the number of points at infinity is the greatest common divisor of
N
and
m
, that is
s={\rm gcd}(m,N)
, and the genus of
X_{a}
is
g(X_{a})=\frac{1}{2}\bigl((m-1)(N-1)-s+1\bigr)

[
7
]
.

The projection to the first coordinate
\zeta
is a holomorphic function on the algebraic curve
\zeta:\Gamma_{a}\to\mathbb{C}
. It extends naturally to a meromorphic function on the Riemann surface
\zeta:X_{a}\to\mathbb{C}P^{1}
by setting
\zeta(\zeta,w)=\zeta
and
\zeta(\infty_{j})=\infty
with a slight abuse of notation. This way we can identify the Riemann surface
X_{a}
with the surface of the ramified covering
(X_{a},\zeta)
of degree
m
ramified over the points
a_{1},\dots,a_{N},\infty
called the branch points of the covering. We denote the corresponding ramification points by
P_{a_{i}}=(a_{i},0)
for
i=1,\dots,N
and by
\infty_{j}
as above for
j=1,\dots,s.

The structure of the ramified covering
\zeta:X_{a}\to\mathbb{C}P^{1}
induces the
standard local coordinates
on the surface
X_{a}
as follows
[
7
]
:

\displaystyle\xi_{a_{k}}(P)=(\zeta(P)-a_{k})^{\frac{1}{m}},

\displaystyle\mbox{if}\quad P\sim P_{a_{k}},\quad\mbox{with}\quad k=1,\dots,N\,,

(5)

\displaystyle\xi_{\infty_{j}}(P)=(\zeta(P))^{-\frac{1}{m_{1}}},

\displaystyle\mbox{if}\quad P\sim\infty_{j},\quad\mbox{ where }m=sm_{1}.

\displaystyle\xi_{Q}(P)=\zeta(P)-\zeta(Q),

\displaystyle\mbox{if}\quad P\sim Q\quad\mbox{and $Q$ is a regular point.}

We thus consider the family of compact
superelliptic
Riemann surfaces
X_{a}
,
a\in D,
associated with the family of algebraic curves (
4
). Functions and differentials on the surfaces
X_{a}
may also be seen as functions over the family of surfaces, that is functions of
a=(a_{1},\dots,a_{N}).

The solutions from
[
7
]
are constructed using the following meromorphic differentials on
X_{a}
defined for any non-zero integer
n
coprime to
m
of (
4
):

(6)

\Omega_{i}^{j}(a):=\frac{w^{jn}\mathrm{d}\zeta}{\zeta-a_{i}},\quad\text{with}\quad j=1,\ldots,p-1,\quad\text{and}\quad i=1,\dots,N.

Recall that
\zeta
is a meromorphic function on
X_{a}
discussed above. In the same way,
w
is a meromorphic function on
X_{a}
extended from
w:\Gamma_{a}\to\mathbb{C}
by setting
w(\infty_{k})=\infty
for
k=1,\dots,s.

Theorem 1
.

[
7
]

Let
X_{a}
with
a=(a_{1},\dots,a_{N})\in D
be a family of compact Riemann surfaces defined above corresponding to the family of algebraic curves (
4
).
Let
m
and
N
be natural integers from (
4
) and let
n\in\mathbb{Z}
be coprime to
m
. Let
\Omega_{i}^{j}(a)
be the meromorphic differential defined by (
6
) on
X_{a}
. Then a set of upper triangular
p\times p
matrices
B^{(i)}
,
i=1,...,N
defined by the following three conditions provides a solution to the Schlesinger system (
3
):

•

B^{(i)}_{kl}=0
if
k>l;

•

The eigenvalues of each matrix
B^{(i)}
,
i=1,...,N
, satisfy

(7)

B^{(i)}_{kk}-B^{(i)}_{k+1,k+1}=\frac{n}{m},\quad\text{with }k=1,\ldots,p-1;

•

The entries on the superdiagonals are given by

(8)

B^{(i)}_{kl}=\oint_{\gamma_{l-k}}\Omega_{i}^{l-k}(a)=\oint_{\gamma_{l-k}}\frac{w^{(l-k)n}\mathrm{d}\zeta}{\zeta-a_{i}},\quad l>k,

where
\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{p-1}
are linear combinations with complex coefficients of closed contours on the Riemann surface
X_{a}
not passing through a pole of the corresponding
\Omega_{i}^{j}(a)
and independent of small variations of
a\in D
. More precisely,
\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{p-1}
are elements of

(a)

H_{1}(X_{a},\mathbb{C})
if
m
,
N
are coprime and
n>0
,

(b)

H_{1}(X_{a}\setminus\{\infty_{1},\ldots,\infty_{s}\},\mathbb{C})
if
m
,
N
are not coprime,
s={\rm gcd}(m,N)
, and
n>0
.

(c)

H_{1}(X_{a}\setminus\{P_{a_{1}},\ldots,P_{a_{N}}\},{\mathbb{C}})
if
n<0\,.

Note that for some contours, the integrals in (
8
) vanish giving possibly trivial solutions to the Schlesinger system. If
m=1
and
n>0
then all the solutions are trivial as well, that is constant diagonal matrices. If the contours are chosen to encircle poles of the respective
\Omega_{i}^{j}(a)
, solutions to the Schlesinger system become rational functions of
a_{1},\dots,a_{N}
, see
[
7
]
and Section
5
below.

Solutions given by Theorem
1
are such that
B^{(\infty)}:=\sum_{i=1}^{N}B^{(i)}
are non-zero constant diagonal matrices, see
[
7
]
. This implies that the connection form
\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{B^{(i)}}{z-a_{i}}dz
has a simple pole at
z=\infty
with residue
B^{(\infty)}.

3. Solutions of upper triangular Fuchsian systems

In order to simplify the presentation, let us introduce the following notation.

Definition 2
.

Let
n\neq 0
and
m>0
be coprime integers,
r=1,\ldots,p-1
, where
p
is an arbitrary natural number.
Let
z\in\mathbb{C}
be fixed, and let
X_{a}
be a Riemann surface associated with the superelliptic curve
{\Gamma}_{a}
(
4
). Let
\zeta
and
w=\prod_{i=1}^{N}(\zeta-a_{i})^{1/m}
be the meromorphic functions on
X_{a}
as in Section
2
, and let
\gamma_{r}
be a closed contour on
X_{a}
as in Theorem
1
. Assume in addition that
\gamma_{r}
does not pass by the points
P
of the surface for which
\zeta(P)=z.
We then define

(9)

\kappa_{r}:=-\frac{m}{rn}\oint_{\gamma_{r}}\frac{w^{rn}}{\zeta-z}\mathrm{d}\zeta.

The derivative of
\kappa_{r}
with respect to
z\in\mathbb{C}
, denoted by
\kappa^{\prime}_{r}
, is obtained in a straightforward way:

(10)

\kappa^{\prime}_{r}:=\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}z}\kappa_{r}=-\frac{m}{rn}\oint_{\gamma_{r}}\frac{w^{rn}}{(\zeta-z)^{2}}\mathrm{d}\zeta.

In order to simplify further calculations, let us prove the following lemma.

Lemma 3
.

Let
j
be a strictly positive integer,
\gamma_{j}
a closed contour on the Riemann surface
X_{a}
, and
\zeta,

w=\prod_{i=1}^{N}(\zeta-a_{i})^{1/m}
meromorphic functions on
X_{a}
as in Section
2
. For
\kappa_{j}
given by Definition
2
and for its derivative
\kappa^{\prime}_{j}
(
10
) with respect to the complex parameter
z
, we have

\frac{-jn}{m}\kappa_{j}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{1}{z-a_{i}}+\kappa^{\prime}_{j}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{1}{z-a_{i}}\oint_{\gamma_{j}}\frac{w^{jn}}{\zeta-a_{i}}\mathrm{d}\zeta.

Proof.

By Definition
2
and (
10
), we can write

\displaystyle\frac{-jn}{m}\kappa_{j}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{1}{z-a_{i}}+\kappa^{\prime}_{j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left[\oint_{\gamma_{j}}\frac{w^{jn}\mathrm{d}\zeta}{\zeta-z}\frac{1}{z-a_{i}}\right]-\frac{m}{jn}\oint_{\gamma_{j}}\frac{w^{jn}}{(\zeta-z)^{2}}\mathrm{d}\zeta

(11)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\oint_{\gamma_{j}}\left(\frac{w^{jn}}{\zeta-z}+\frac{w^{jn}}{z-a_{i}}\right)\frac{\mathrm{d}\zeta}{\zeta-a_{i}}-\frac{m}{jn}\oint_{\gamma_{j}}\frac{w^{jn}}{(\zeta-z)^{2}}\mathrm{d}\zeta.

Due to the vanishing of the integral of an exact differential over a closed contour, for every
z
we have

\oint_{\gamma_{j}}\mathrm{d}\frac{w^{jn}}{\zeta-z}=\oint_{\gamma_{j}}\mathrm{d}\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{N}(\zeta-a_{i})^{jn/m}}{\zeta-z}=\oint_{\gamma_{j}}\left(\frac{jn}{m}\frac{w^{jn}}{\zeta-z}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{1}{\zeta-a_{i}}-\frac{w^{jn}}{(\zeta-z)^{2}}\right)\mathrm{d}\zeta=0,

which together with (
11
) concludes the proof.

\Box

The next theorem states the main result of the present paper.

Theorem 4
.

Let
p,\;n,\;m,\;N
and
X_{a},a\in D
be as in Theorem
1
. Let
z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{a_{1},\dots,a_{N}\}
and let
\gamma_{j}
be a closed contour on
X_{a}
from Theorem
1
not passing through any point
P\in X_{a}
for which
\zeta(P)=z
. More precisely, let
\gamma_{j}
from Theorem
1
be seen as an element of

(a)

H_{1}(X_{a}\setminus\{P\in X_{a}\,|\,\zeta(P)=z\},\mathbb{C})
if
m
,
N
are coprime and
n>0
,

(a)

H_{1}(X_{a}\setminus(\{\infty_{1},\ldots,\infty_{s}\}\cup\{P\in X_{a}\,|\,\zeta(P)=z\}),\mathbb{C})
if
n>0
and
s={\rm gcd}(m,N)>1
,

(b)

H_{1}(X_{a}\setminus(\{P_{a_{1}},\ldots,P_{a_{N}}\}\cup\{P\in X_{a}\,|\,\zeta(P)=z\}),{\mathbb{C}})
if
n<0
,

independent of small variations of
a\in D
.
For such a choice of the contour
\gamma_{j}
, let
\kappa_{j}
be a function of
z
given by Definition
2
.
Let
B^{(i)},\;i=1,\dots,N,
be upper triangular
p\times p
matrices given by Theorem

with eigenvalues

\beta_{k}^{(i)}=B^{(i)}_{kk}=\theta_{i}-\frac{(k-1)n}{m},\quad k\in\{1,\ldots,p\},

to the Fuchsian system (

) with coefficients being the matrices

(12)

\Phi=MD,

\quad 1\quad&\text{when } $l=k$,\\

where

, and

in the partition
q
.

\gamma_{j}
may be adjusted to avoid the points
P\in X_{a}
with
\zeta(P)=z
while staying in the same homology class from Theorem

. In this way, starting from some
z_{0}\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{a_{1},\dots,a_{N}\}
, a set of contours from Theorem
4
, and the matrix
\Phi(z_{0})
(
12
)-(

), we obtain
\Phi(z)
for any

by analytical continuation of
\Phi(z_{0})
.

Example 5
.

To illustrate the statement of the theorem we spell out the solutions in the matrix form for
p=5
.
For
i=1,\dots,N,

5\times 5
matrices

for some constants

The entries

\displaystyle\psi_{1}

Thus, continuing our calculation, we obtain

\Phi=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}(z-a_{1})^{\theta_{1}}(z-a_{2})^{\theta_{2}}&0&\frac{2c}{a_{1}-a_{2}}(z-a_{1})^{\theta_{1}+1}(z-a_{2})^{\theta_{2}}\\
0&(z-a_{1})^{\theta_{1}+\frac{1}{2}}(z-a_{2})^{\theta_{2}+\frac{1}{2}}&0\\
0&0&(z-a_{1})^{\theta_{1}+1}(z-a_{2})^{\theta_{2}+1}\end{array}\right)

satisfies the system

\frac{d\Phi}{dz}=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}\frac{\theta_{1}}{z-a_{1}}+\frac{\theta_{2}}{z-a_{2}}&0&\frac{2c}{(a_{1}-a_{2})^{2}}\left(\frac{1}{z-a_{1}}-\frac{1}{z-a_{2}}\right)\\
0&\frac{\theta_{1}+\frac{1}{2}}{z-a_{1}}+\frac{\theta_{2}+\frac{1}{2}}{z-a_{2}}&0\\
0&0&\frac{\theta_{1}+1}{z-a_{1}}+\frac{\theta_{2}+1}{z-a_{2}}\end{array}\right)\Phi.

k k -nonseparablity and k k -partite entanglement criteria of multipartite quantum states Preprint: APS/123-QED
Xiaofei Qi, Yuyang Pang, Jinchuan Hou

Abstract
Multipartite quantum entanglement, as a core quantum resource, is fundamental to the advancement of quantum science and technology. In multipartite quantum systems, there are two kinds of quantum entanglement: k k -nonseparability and k k -partite entanglement. In this paper, we propose sufficient criteria for detecting k k -nonseparability and k k -partite entanglement by using the generalized Wigner-Yanase skew information and mutually unbiased measurements. Examples are given to demonstrate the detection capability and advantages of these criteria. As an application, an example of recognizing the networks by detecting the depth of quantum networks is given.

Preliminaries

In this section, we review some important concepts in quantum information, mutually unbiased measurements,
k
-separability and
k
-partite entanglement in multipartite quantum systems.

Throughout this paper, assume that
H_{i}
is any complex Hilbert space with
\dim H_{i}=d_{i}<\infty
,
i=1,2,\cdots,N
, and let
H=H_{1}\otimes H_{2}\otimes\cdots\otimes H_{N}
.
Denote by
\mathcal{B}(H)
the algebra of all bounded linear operators and
{\mathcal{S}}(H)=\{\rho:\rho\geq 0,{\rm Tr}(\rho)=1\}
is the closed convex set of all quantum states in
\mathcal{B}(H)
.
\mathbb{I}\in{\mathcal{B}}(H)
and
\mathbb{I}_{i}\in{\mathcal{B}}(H_{i})
stand for the identity operator on
H
and
H_{i}
, respectively.

II.1
k
-nonseparability and
k
-partite entanglement

For
2\leq k\leq N
,
assume that
\gamma_{1},\cdots,\gamma_{k}
are non-empty subsets of
\{1,2,\cdots,N\}
. We say that

\{\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2},\cdots,\gamma_{k}\}
is a
k
-partition, denoted by
\gamma=\gamma_{1}|\gamma_{2}|\ldots|\gamma_{k}
, if it satisfies the conditions

\gamma_{i}\cap\gamma_{j}=\emptyset
for
i\neq j

and
\gamma_{1}\cup\cdots\cup\gamma_{k}=\{1,2,\cdots,N\}
.

A pure state
|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|\in\mathcal{S}(H)
is said to be
k
-separable if there exists a
k
-partition

\gamma=\gamma_{1}|\gamma_{2}|\cdots|\gamma_{k}
of

\{1,2,\cdots,N\}
such that

|\psi\rangle=\otimes_{i=1}^{k}|\psi_{\gamma_{i}}\rangle,

where
|\psi_{i}^{\gamma_{i}}\rangle\langle\psi_{i}^{\gamma_{i}}|\in\mathcal{S}(H_{\gamma_{i}})
,

H_{\gamma_{i}}=\otimes_{j=1}^{k_{i}}H_{n_{ij}}
,
\gamma_{i}=\{n_{i1},n_{i2},\cdots,n_{ik_{i}}\}
,

n_{i1}<n_{i2}<\cdots<n_{ik_{i}}
; otherwise,
|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|
is called
k
-nonseparable.
A mixed state
\rho\in\mathcal{S}(H)
is
k
-separable if

\rho
can be written as
\rho=\sum_{r}p_{r}|\psi_{r}\rangle\langle\psi_{r}|
,
where each
|\psi_{r}\rangle\langle\psi_{r}|\in{\mathcal{S}}(H)
is a
k
-separable pure state and
p_{r}\geq 0
with
\sum_{r}p_{r}=1
;
otherwise,
\rho
is
k
-nonseparable.
Particularly,
\rho
is fully separable if
\rho
is
N
-separable; and is genuine entangled
if
\rho
is 2-nonseparable.

On the other hand, a pure state
|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|\in\mathcal{S}(H)
is
k
-producible if it can be represented as

|\psi\rangle=\otimes_{i=1}^{m}|\psi_{\gamma_{i}}\rangle,

where
\gamma_{1}|\gamma_{2}|\cdots|\gamma_{m}
is a
m
-partition
of
\{1,2,\cdots,N\}
, and the number of particles in each subset
\gamma_{i}
is not greater than
k
.
For any mixed state
\rho
, we say that
\rho
is
k
-producible if it can be represented as a convex combination of
k
-producible pure states; otherwise, we say that
\rho
contains
(k+1)
-partite entanglement
GOTG
.

Denote by
\mathcal{S}_{k}
the set of all
k
-separable quantum states (
2\leq k\leq N
) and by
\mathcal{P}_{k}
the set of all
k
-producible quantum states (
1\leq k\leq N-1
). By the above definitions, it is easily seen that, if a state is
k
-separable, then it is also
(k-1)
-separable; and if a state is
k
-producible, then it is also
(k+1)
-producible.
That is,

\mathcal{S}_{N}\subset\mathcal{S}_{N-1}\subset\cdots\subset\mathcal{S}_{2}\subset\mathcal{S}_{1};\ \ \mathcal{P}_{N}\supset\mathcal{P}_{N-1}\supset\cdots\mathcal{P}_{2}\supset\mathcal{P}_{1},

where both
\mathcal{S}_{N}
and
\mathcal{P}_{1}
stand for the set of all fully separable states, while
\mathcal{S}_{1}=\mathcal{P}_{N}=\mathcal{S}(H)
.

II.2
Wigner-Yanase skew information

In the study of measurement theory, Wigner and Yanase introduced the Wigner-Yanase skew information from an information-theoretic perspective.
For a quantum state
\rho\in{\mathcal{S}}(H)
, the Wigner-Yanase skew information of
\rho
with respect to an observable
A
is defined by

I(\rho,A)=-\frac{1}{2}(\text{tr}[\rho^{1/2},A]^{2})

with
[A,B]=AB-BA
as usual
WY
. This quantity
I(\rho,A)

represents the amount of information about the values of observables that do not commute with
A
, which is skew-symmetric with respect to
A
.

II.3
Quantum Fisher information

The quantum Fisher information of a quantum state
\rho\in{\mathcal{S}}(H)

with respect to an observable
A\in{\mathcal{B}}(H)
is defined as
HWC

F(\rho,A)=\frac{1}{4}{\rm tr}(\rho L^{2}),

where
L
is the symmetric logarithmic derivative operator defined via

i(\rho A-A\rho)=\frac{1}{2}(\rho L+L\rho).

If
\rho
has the spectral decomposition
\rho=\sum_{l}\lambda_{l}|l\rangle\langle l|
, then
F(\rho,A)
can be expressed as

F(\rho,A)=\sum_{l,l^{\prime}}\frac{\left(\lambda_{l}-\lambda_{l^{\prime}}\right)^{2}}{2\left(\lambda_{l}+\lambda_{l^{\prime}}\right)}\left|\left<l|A|l^{\prime}\right>\right|^{2}.

II.4
Generalized Wigner-Yanase skew information

For any
s\in(-\infty,0)
, define a binary function
f_{s}
on
\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
(
\mathbb{R}_{+}=[0,+\infty)
) as follows:

f_{s}(a,b)=\begin{cases}\left(\frac{a^{s}+b^{s}}{2}\right)^{1/s}&\text{if}\ a>0,b>0,\\
0&\text{if}\ a=0\ \text{or}\ b=0,\end{cases}

which represents the generalized mean of two positive real numbers
a
and
b
with equal weights.
In addition, define

f_{0}(a,b):=\lim_{s\to 0}f_{s}(a,b)=\sqrt{ab}

and

f_{-\infty}(a,b):=\lim_{s\to-\infty}f_{s}(a,b)=\min\{a,b\}.

For any quantum state
\rho\in{\mathcal{S}}(H)
with the spectral decomposition

\rho=\sum_{l}\lambda_{l}|\psi_{l}\rangle\langle\psi_{l}|
, the generalized Wigner-Yanase skew information about an observable
A
is given by
YQ

\displaystyle I^{s}(\rho,A):=

\displaystyle\text{tr}\left(\rho A^{2}\right)-\sum_{l,l^{\prime}}f_{s}\left(\lambda_{l},\lambda_{l^{\prime}}\right)\left|\left<\psi_{l}|A|\psi_{l^{\prime}}\right>\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{l\neq l^{\prime}}\left[\lambda_{l}-f_{s}\left(\lambda_{l},\lambda_{l^{\prime}}\right)\right]\left|\left<\psi_{l}|A|\psi_{l^{\prime}}\right>\right|^{2}.

Particularly, when
s=-1
and
s=0
,
I^{-1}(\rho,A)
and
I^{0}(\rho,A)
reduce to the quantum Fisher information and the Wigner-Yanase skew information, respectively.

The authors in
YQ
proved that the generalized Wigner-Yanase skew information has the following properties:

(1) Monotonicity:

I^{s}(\rho,A)
decreases monotonically with respect to
s
, that is,

I^{0}(\rho,A)\leq I^{-1}(\rho,A)\leq\cdots\leq I^{-\infty}(\rho,A)\leq V(\rho,A),

where
V(\rho,A)=\text{Tr}(\rho A^{2})-[\text{Tr}(\rho A)]^{2}
stands for the variance. Moreover, the equalities hold if
\rho
is pure.

(2) Convexity: For any
s
,
I^{s}
is convex, that is,

I^{s}\left(\sum_{i}p_{i}\rho_{i},A\right)\leq\sum_{i}p_{i}I^{s}\left(\rho_{i},A\right),

where
\rho_{i}\in{\mathcal{S}}(H)
,
p_{i}\geq 0
and
\sum_{i}p_{i}=1
.

(3) Additivity: For any
\rho_{i}\in{\mathcal{S}}(H_{i})
and any observable
A_{i}\in\mathcal{B}(H_{i})
,
i=1,2,\cdots,N
, we have

I^{s}\left(\otimes_{i=1}^{N}\rho_{i},\sum_{i=1}^{N}{\bf A_{i}}\right)=\sum_{i=1}^{N}I^{s}\left(\rho_{i},A_{i}\right),

where
{\bf A_{i}}:=(\otimes_{j=1}^{i-1}\mathbb{I}_{j})\otimes A_{i}\otimes(\otimes_{j=i+1}^{N}\mathbb{I}_{j})
.

II.5
Mutually unbiased measurements (MUMs)

Let
P^{(b)}=\{P_{n}^{(b)}:n=1,\cdots,d\}
be two positive-operator-valued measurements (POVMs),
b=1,2
.

P^{(1)}
and
P^{(2)}
are called MUMs if they satisfy the following conditions:

\begin{cases}\mathrm{tr}(P_{n}^{(b)})=1,\\
\mathrm{tr}(P_{n}^{(b)}P_{n^{\prime}}^{(b^{\prime})})=&\delta_{n,n^{\prime}}\delta_{b,b^{\prime}}\kappa\\
&+(1-\delta_{n,n^{\prime}})\delta_{b,b^{\prime}}\frac{1-\kappa}{d-1}+(1-\delta_{b,b^{\prime}})\frac{1}{d},\end{cases}

where
b,b^{\prime}\in\{1,2\},\ n,n^{\prime}\in\{1,\cdots,d\}
, and the parameter
\kappa
satisfies
\frac{1}{d}<\kappa\leq 1
. When
\kappa=1
, it reduces to mutually unbiased bases (MUBs)
WF
.

Kalev and Gour in
KG
proposed a construction method for MUMs in any dimensional quantum system.
Take an orthonormal basis
\{F_{k}\}_{k=1}^{d^{2}-1}
consisting of Hermitian and traceless operators acting on
H
.
Arrange these basis elements on a grid of
d-1
columns and
d+1
rows, and denote them as
\{F_{n,b}\}
with
n=1,2,\ldots,d-1
and
b=1,2,\ldots,d+1
.
Define
d(d+1)
operators
\{F_{n}^{(b)}\}
by

F_{n}^{(b)}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}F^{(b)}-(d+\sqrt{d})F_{n,b}&\text{for}\ n=1,\cdots,d-1,\\
(1+\sqrt{d})F^{(b)}&\text{for}\ n=d,\end{array}\right.

(1)

where
F^{(b)}=\sum_{n=1}^{d-1}F_{n,b}
,
b=1,2,\cdots,d+1
. It is a direct calculation that

\sum_{n=1}^{d}F_{n}^{(b)}=0,\ \ \forall b\in\{1,2,\cdots,d+1\}.

(2)

The measurement operators
\{P_{n}^{(b)}\}
are given by:

P_{n}^{(b)}=\frac{1}{d}\mathbb{I}+tF_{n}^{(b)},

(3)

where the free parameter
t
is chosen such that
P_{n}^{(b)}\geq 0
; and

\kappa=\frac{1}{d}+t^{2}(1+\sqrt{d})^{2}(d-1).

(4)

Then
\{P^{(b)}=\{P_{n}^{(b)}\}\}
form a complete set of
d+1
mutually unbiased measurements, where
b
labels the measurement and
n
labels the outcome. Moreover, any complete set of MUMs has this form.

It was also proven in
LL
that
\left\{\frac{\mathbb{I}}{\sqrt{d}},F_{n,b}:n=1,\cdots,d-1;b=1,\cdots,d+1\right\}

forms a set of local orthogonal observables (LOOs) on
H
; and any complete set of MUMs
\{P_{n}^{(b)}:n=1,2,\cdots,d;b=1,2,\cdots,d+1\}
must satisfy the following equation:

\sum_{b=1}^{d+1}\sum_{n=1}^{d}(P_{n}^{(b)})^{2}=(d+1)\kappa\mathbb{I}.

(5)

III
k
-separability and
k
-partite entanglement detection criteria

In this section, we will establish some entanglement detection criteria for
k
-separability and
k
-partite entanglement in multipartite quantum systems.

For convenience, assume
H_{i}=H_{0}
with
\dim H_{0}=d
,
i=1,2,\cdots,N
.
Take the same set of
d+1
MUMs acting on each subsystem
H_{i}

\{P^{(i,b)}=\{P_{i,n}^{(b)}\}:b=1,2,\cdots,d+1;n=1,2,\cdots,d\},

where
i
is used to label the subsystem on which the operators act,
i=1,2,\cdots,N
. For any subset
\Gamma\subset\{1,2,\cdots,N\}
with
\sharp(\Gamma)
the number of the elements in
\Gamma
, write

{\bf P}_{\Gamma,n}^{(b)}=\sum_{i\in\Gamma}{\bf P}_{i,n}^{(b)}

with

{\bf P}_{i,n}^{(b)}=(\otimes_{j=1}^{i-1}\mathbb{I}_{j})\otimes P_{i,n}^{(b)}\otimes(\otimes_{j=i+1}^{\sharp(\Gamma)}\mathbb{I}_{j});

and in particular, when
\Gamma=\{1,2,\cdots,N\}
, write

{\bf P}_{N,n}^{(b)}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}{\bf P}_{i,n}^{(b)}.

For
{\bf P}_{\Gamma,n}^{(b)}
, we have the following result.

Proposition 1
.

For any
p\neq q\in\Gamma
, we have

\sum_{b=1}^{d+1}\sum_{n=1}^{d}{\bf P}_{p,n}^{(b)}{\bf P}_{q,n}^{(b)}\leq(1+\kappa)(\otimes_{i\in\Gamma}\mathbb{I}_{i}).

Proof.
Without loss of generality, assume
p<q
.
By the definition, one has

{\bf P}_{p,n}^{(b)}{\bf P}_{q,n}^{(b)}=(\otimes_{j=1}^{p-1}\mathbb{I}_{j})\otimes P_{p,n}^{(b)}\otimes(\otimes_{j=p+1}^{q-1}\mathbb{I}_{j})\otimes P_{q,n}^{(b)}\otimes(\otimes_{j=q+1}^{\sharp(\Gamma)}\mathbb{I}_{j}).

It follows from Eqs.(1)-(5) that

\displaystyle\sum_{b=1}^{d+1}\sum_{n=1}^{d}{\bf P}_{p,n}^{(b)}{\bf P}_{q,n}^{(b)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{b=1}^{d+1}\sum_{n=1}^{d}(\otimes_{j=1}^{p-1}\mathbb{I}_{j})\otimes(\frac{1}{d}\mathbb{I}_{p}+tF_{n}^{(b)})\otimes(\otimes_{j=p+1}^{q-1}\mathbb{I}_{j})\otimes(\frac{1}{d}\mathbb{I}_{q}+tF_{n}^{(b)})\otimes(\otimes_{j=q+1}^{\sharp(\Gamma)}\mathbb{I}_{j})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{b=1}^{d+1}\sum_{n=1}^{d}\big[\frac{(\otimes_{i\in\Gamma}\mathbb{I}_{i})}{d^{2}}+\frac{t}{d}((\otimes_{j=1}^{p-1}\mathbb{I}_{j})\otimes F_{n}^{(b)}\otimes(\otimes_{j=p+1}^{\sharp(\Gamma)}\mathbb{I}_{j})+(\otimes_{j=1}^{q-1}\mathbb{I}_{j})\otimes F_{n}^{(b)}\otimes(\otimes_{j=q+1}^{\sharp(\Gamma)}\mathbb{I}_{j}))

\displaystyle+t^{2}(\otimes_{j=1}^{p-1}\mathbb{I}_{j})\otimes F_{n}^{(b)}\otimes(\otimes_{j=p+1}^{q-1}\mathbb{I}_{j})\otimes F_{n}^{(b)}\otimes(\otimes_{j=q+1}^{\sharp(\Gamma)}\mathbb{I}_{j})\big]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{d(d+1)}{d^{2}}(\otimes_{i\in\Gamma}\mathbb{I}_{i})+t^{2}\sum_{b=1}^{d+1}\sum_{n=1}^{d}(\otimes_{j=1}^{p-1}\mathbb{I}_{j})\otimes F_{n}^{(b)}\otimes(\otimes_{j=p+1}^{q-1}\mathbb{I}_{j})\otimes F_{n}^{(b)}\otimes(\otimes_{j=q+1}^{\sharp(\Gamma)}\mathbb{I}_{j})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{d+1}{d}(\otimes_{i\in\Gamma}\mathbb{I}_{i})+t^{2}(d+\sqrt{d})^{2}\sum_{b=1}^{d+1}\sum_{n=1}^{d-1}(\otimes_{j=1}^{p-1}\mathbb{I}_{j})\otimes F_{n,b}\otimes(\otimes_{j=p+1}^{q-1}\mathbb{I}_{j})\otimes F_{n,b}\otimes(\otimes_{j=q+1}^{\sharp(\Gamma)}\mathbb{I}_{j})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{d+1}{d}(\otimes_{i\in\Gamma}\mathbb{I}_{i})+t^{2}(d+\sqrt{d})^{2}(1-\frac{1}{d})(\otimes_{i\in\Gamma}\mathbb{I}_{i})=(1+\kappa)(\otimes_{i\in\Gamma}\mathbb{I}_{i}).

\Box

Proposition 2
.

Assume that
\Gamma\subset\{1,2,\cdots,N\}
is any subset. For any
\rho\in{\mathcal{S}}(H_{1}\otimes H_{2}\otimes\cdots\otimes H_{N})
, denoting by
\rho_{\Gamma}
the reduced state of
\rho
on the subsystem
\otimes_{i\in\Gamma}H_{i}
, we have

\sum_{b=1}^{d+1}\sum_{n=1}^{d}I^{s}(\rho_{\Gamma},{\bf P}_{\Gamma,n}^{(b)})\leq\sharp(\Gamma)^{2}(\kappa-\frac{1}{d})+\sharp(\Gamma)(d\kappa-1).

Proof.

For any subset
\Gamma\subset\{1,2,\cdots,N\}
, by Proposition 3.1 and Eq.(5), we have

\displaystyle\sum_{b=1}^{d+1}\sum_{n=1}^{d}\big({\bf P}_{\Gamma,n}^{(b)}\big)^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{b=1}^{d+1}\sum_{n=1}^{d}\big(\sum_{i\in\Gamma}{\bf P}_{i,n}^{(b)}\big)^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{b=1}^{d+1}\sum_{n=1}^{d}\big(\sum_{i\in\Gamma}({\bf P}_{i,n}^{(b)})^{2}+\sum_{p\not=q\in\Gamma}{\bf P}_{p,n}^{(b)}{\bf P}_{q,n}^{(b)}\big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in\Gamma}\sum_{b=1}^{d+1}\sum_{n=1}^{d}((\otimes_{j=1}^{i-1}\mathbb{I}_{j})\otimes P_{i,n}^{(b)}\otimes(\otimes_{j=i+1}^{\sharp(\Gamma)}\mathbb{I}_{j}))^{2}

\displaystyle+\sum_{p\neq q\in\Gamma}\sum_{b=1}^{d+1}\sum_{n=1}^{d}{\bf P}_{p,n}^{(b)}{\bf P}_{q,n}^{(b)}

\displaystyle\leq\sharp(\Gamma)(d+1)\kappa(\otimes_{i\in\Gamma}\mathbb{I}_{i})+(\sharp(\Gamma)^{2}-\sharp(\Gamma))(1+\kappa)(\otimes_{i\in\Gamma}\mathbb{I}_{i})

\displaystyle=[\sharp(\Gamma)^{2}(1+\kappa)+\sharp(\Gamma)(d\kappa-1)](\otimes_{i\in\Gamma}\mathbb{I}_{i}).

On the other hand, by Eq.(2),
one has

\displaystyle\sum_{b=1}^{d+1}\sum_{n=1}^{d}\big(\mathrm{tr}\rho_{\Gamma}{\bf P}_{\Gamma,n}^{(b)}\big)^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{b=1}^{d+1}\sum_{n=1}^{d}[\mathrm{tr}\rho_{\Gamma}\sum_{i\in\Gamma}{\bf P}_{i,n}^{(b)}]^{2}=\sum_{b=1}^{d+1}\sum_{n=1}^{d}[\sum_{i\in\Gamma}\mathrm{tr}\rho_{\Gamma}{\bf P}_{i,n}^{(b)}]^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{b=1}^{d+1}\sum_{n=1}^{d}[\sum_{i\in\Gamma}\mathrm{tr}\rho_{\Gamma}(\frac{\otimes_{i\in\Gamma}\mathbb{I}_{i}}{d}+t{\bf F}_{i,n}^{(b)})]^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{b=1}^{d+1}\sum_{n=1}^{d}(\frac{\sharp(\Gamma)}{d}+t\sum_{i\in\Gamma}\mathrm{tr}\rho_{\Gamma}{\bf F}_{i,n}^{(b)})^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{b=1}^{d+1}\sum_{n=1}^{d}\left(\frac{\sharp(\Gamma)}{d}\right)^{2}+t^{2}\sum_{b=1}^{d+1}\sum_{n=1}^{d}\left(\sum_{i\in\Gamma}\mathrm{tr}\left(\rho_{\Gamma}{\bf F}_{i,n}^{(b)}\right)\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{b=1}^{d+1}\sum_{n=1}^{d}(\frac{\sharp(\Gamma)}{d})^{2}=\sharp(\Gamma)^{2}(1+\frac{1}{d}),

where
{\bf F}_{i,n}^{(b)}=(\otimes_{j=1}^{i-1}\mathbb{I}_{j})\otimes F_{i,n}^{(b)}\otimes(\otimes_{j=i+1}^{\sharp(\Gamma)}\mathbb{I}_{j})
.
Combining the above two inequalities gives

\displaystyle\sum_{b=1}^{d+1}\sum_{n=1}^{d}I^{s}(\rho_{\Gamma},{\bf P}_{\Gamma,n}^{(b)})

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{b=1}^{d+1}\sum_{n=1}^{d}\Big[\mathrm{tr}\,\rho_{\Gamma}({\bf P}_{\Gamma,n}^{(b)})^{2}-(\mathrm{tr}\,\rho_{\Gamma}{\bf P}_{\Gamma,n}^{(b)})^{2}\Big]

\displaystyle\leq[\sharp(\Gamma)^{2}(1+\kappa)+\sharp({\Gamma})(d\kappa-1)]-\sharp(\Gamma)^{2}(1+\frac{1}{d})

\displaystyle=\sharp(\Gamma)^{2}(\kappa-\frac{1}{d})+\sharp(\Gamma)(d\kappa-1).

\Box

Now, we can give a
k
-nonseparability criterion and a
k
-partite entanglement criterion
by generalized Wigner-Yanase skew informations and MUMs.

Theorem 3
.

If
\rho
is
k
-separable, then the following inequality holds:

\sum_{b=1}^{d+1}\sum_{n=1}^{d}I^{s}(\rho,{\bf P}_{N,n}^{(b)})\leq N(d\kappa-1)+[(N-k+1)^{2}+k-1](\kappa-\frac{1}{d}).

Proof.

Since
\rho
is
k
-separable, then
\rho
can be written as a convex combination of
k
-separable pure states, that is,
\rho=\sum_{r}p_{r}\rho_{r}
, where
\rho^{(r)}
has the form

\rho_{r}=\rho^{(r)}_{\Gamma_{1}}\otimes\rho^{(r)}_{\Gamma_{2}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\rho^{(r)}_{\Gamma_{k}}

for some
k
-partition
\Gamma_{1}|\Gamma_{2}|\ldots|\Gamma_{k}
of
\{1,2,\cdots,N\}
.
By Proposition 3.2, and by the convexity and additivity of the generalized Wigner-Yanase skew information, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{b=1}^{d+1}\sum_{n=1}^{d}I^{s}(\rho,{\bf P}_{N,n}^{(b)})

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{b=1}^{d+1}\sum_{n=1}^{d}\sum_{r}p_{r}I^{s}(\rho_{r},{\bf P}_{N,n}^{(b)})=\sum_{b=1}^{d+1}\sum_{n=1}^{d}\sum_{r}p_{r}\sum_{t=1}^{k}I^{s}(\rho_{\Gamma_{t}}^{(r)},{\bf P}_{\Gamma_{t},n}^{(b)})

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{r}p_{r}\sum_{i=1}^{k}[\sharp(\Gamma_{i})^{2}(\kappa-\frac{1}{d})+\sharp(\Gamma_{i})(d\kappa-1)]=(\kappa-\frac{1}{d})\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sharp(\Gamma_{i})^{2}+N(d\kappa-1).

The next goal is to maximize
\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sharp(\Gamma_{i})^{2}
.
To do this, write

x=(\sharp(\Gamma_{i_{1}}),\sharp(\Gamma_{i_{2}}),\dots,\sharp(\Gamma_{i_{k}}))^{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{k},

where

\sharp(\Gamma_{i_{j}})\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
(the set of positive integers),
\sharp(\Gamma_{i_{1}})\geq\sharp(\Gamma_{i_{2}})\geq\dots\geq\sharp(\Gamma_{i_{k}})
with
\sum_{j=1}^{k}\sharp(\Gamma_{i_{j}})=N
,
i_{j}\not=i_{l}\in\{1,2,\cdots,k\}
.
Denote by
X\subset\mathbb{R}^{k}
the set consisting of all the above vectors
x
. Write

y_{0}=(N-k+1,1,\cdots,1)^{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{k}.

It is obvious that
y_{0}\in X
.
Recall that, for any vectors
x=(x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{k})^{t}
and
y=(y_{1},y_{2},\cdots,y_{k})^{k}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{k}
,

x\prec y
means that

\sum_{i=1}^{m}x_{i}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{m}y_{i}
holds for all
m=1,2,\cdots,k-1
and
\sum_{i=1}^{k}x_{i}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}y_{i}
.
Then we have
x\prec y_{0}
for all
x\in X
; otherwise,
if there exists some
m^{\prime}
such that

\sum_{i=1}^{m^{\prime}}x_{i}>\sum_{i=1}^{m^{\prime}}y_{i}=N-k+m^{\prime},

then there must be some
x_{0}=0
, a contradiction.
Note that the function
x\mapsto\sum_{i=1}^{k}x_{i}^{2}
is Schur convex. It follows that

\sum_{i=1}^{k}x_{i}^{2}\leq(N-k+1)^{2}+(k-1)
. This implies that
\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sharp(\Gamma_{i})^{2}

attains the maximum at the partition
\{\Gamma_{1},\cdots,\Gamma_{k}\}
with

\sharp(\Gamma_{1})=N-k+1,\ \sharp(\Gamma_{i})=1
for all
i=2,3,\cdots,k
.
Hence

\sum_{b=1}^{d+1}\sum_{n=1}^{d}I^{s}(\rho,{\bf P}_{N,n}^{(b)})\leq N(d\kappa-1)+[(N-k+1)^{2}+k-1](\kappa-\frac{1}{d}).

The proof is finished.
\Box

Theorem 4
.

If
\rho
is
k
-producible, then the following inequality holds:

\sum_{b=1}^{d+1}\sum_{n=1}^{d}I^{s}(\rho,{\bf P}_{N,n}^{(b)})\leq\begin{cases}Nk(\kappa-\frac{1}{d})+N(d\kappa-1)&{\rm if}\ \ N=pk;\\
(N^{2}+p^{2}k^{2}+pk^{2}-2pkN)(\kappa-\frac{1}{d})+N(d\kappa-1)&{\rm if}\ \ 0<N-pk<k,\end{cases}

where
p=[\frac{N}{k}]
.

Proof.

Since
\rho
is
k
-producible,
\rho
can be written as
\rho=\sum_{r}p_{r}\rho_{r}
, where

\rho_{r}=\rho^{(r)}_{\Gamma_{1}}\otimes\rho^{(r)}_{\Gamma_{2}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\rho^{(r)}_{\Gamma_{l_{r}}}

for some
l_{r}
-partition
\Gamma_{1}|\Gamma_{2}|\ldots|\Gamma_{l_{r}}
with
\sharp(\Gamma_{i})\leq k
for every
i=1,\cdots,l_{r}\leq N
. Let
\sharp(\Gamma_{i})=0
for
i=l_{r}+1,\ldots,N
. Then, by Proposition 3.2, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{b=1}^{d+1}\sum_{n=1}^{d}I^{s}(\rho,{\bf P}_{N,n}^{(b)})

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{b=1}^{d+1}\sum_{n=1}^{d}\sum_{r}p_{r}I^{s}(\rho_{r},{\bf P}_{N,n}^{(b)})

\displaystyle=\sum_{b=1}^{d+1}\sum_{n=1}^{d}\sum_{r}p_{r}\sum_{t=1}^{l_{r}}I^{s}(\rho_{\Gamma_{t}}^{(r)},{\bf P}_{\Gamma_{t},n}^{(b)})

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{r}p_{r}\sum_{i=1}^{l_{r}}[\sharp(\Gamma_{i})^{2}(\kappa-\frac{1}{d})+\sharp(\Gamma_{i})(d\kappa-1)]

\displaystyle=(\kappa-\frac{1}{d})\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sharp(\Gamma_{i})^{2}+N(d\kappa-1).

Write

x=(\sharp(\Gamma_{i_{1}}),\sharp(\Gamma_{i_{2}}),\dots,\sharp(\Gamma_{i_{l_{r}}}),0,\ldots,0)^{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{N},

where

\sharp(\Gamma_{i_{j}})\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
with
\sharp(\Gamma_{i_{j}})\leq k
,
\sharp(\Gamma_{i_{1}})\geq\sharp(\Gamma_{i_{2}})\geq\dots\geq\sharp(\Gamma_{i_{l_{r}}})
with
\sum_{j=1}^{l_{r}}\sharp(\Gamma_{i_{j}})=N
,
i_{j}\not=i_{p}\in\{1,2,\cdots,l_{r}\}
.
Denote by
Y\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
the set consisting of all the above vectors
x
. Write

y_{0}=(\underbrace{k,k,\dots,k}_{p},N-pk,0,\dots,0)^{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{N},

where
p=[\frac{N}{k}]
the integer part of
\frac{N}{k}
.
It is easily seen that
y_{0}\in Y
and
x\prec y_{0}
for all
x\in Y
. By the Schur convexity of
the function
x\mapsto\sum_{i=1}^{N}x_{i}^{2}
again, we see that

\sum_{i=1}^{N}t_{i}^{2}

attains the maximum at
y_{0}
. Consequently,
\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sharp{(\Gamma_{i})}^{2}
achieves the maximum at the
(p+1)
-partition
\{\Gamma_{1},\cdots,\Gamma_{l}\}
, where
l=p
if
N=pk
and
l=p+1
if
N\not=pk
, with

\sharp(\Gamma_{1})=\sharp(\Gamma_{2})=\dots=\sharp(\Gamma_{p})=k,\quad\sharp(\Gamma_{p+1})=N-pk.

Thus, if
N=pk
, then

\begin{array}[]{rl}&\sum_{b=1}^{d+1}\sum_{n=1}^{d}I^{s}(\rho,{\bf P}_{N,n}^{(b)})\\
&\leq pk^{2}(\kappa-\frac{1}{d})+N(d\kappa-1)=Nk(\kappa-\frac{1}{d})+N(d\kappa-1);\end{array}

and if
N=pk+m
with
0<m<k
, then

\begin{array}[]{rl}&\sum_{b=1}^{d+1}\sum_{n=1}^{d}I^{s}(\rho,{\bf P}_{N,n}^{(b)})\\
&\leq pk^{2}(\kappa-\frac{1}{d})+(N-pk)^{2}(\kappa-\frac{1}{d})+N(d\kappa-1)\\
&=(N^{2}+p^{2}k^{2}+pk^{2}-2pkN)(\kappa-\frac{1}{d})+N(d\kappa-1).\end{array}

The proof is completed.

\Box

IV
Conclusion

We establish two criteria based on MUMs and the generalized Wigner-Yanase skew information to detect
k
-nonseparability and
k
-partite entanglement, respectively, in multipartite quantum systems. Two examples have shown that these criteria are more efficient in detecting multipartite quantum entanglement than some existing methods. These findings could offer new perspectives on detecting multipartite quantum entanglement and may also be applicable to the detection of other multipartite quantum correlations.
It is also worth noting that the upper bounds of the inequalities obtained in our main theorems are independent of the parameter
s
of the generalized Wigner-Yanase skew information
I^{s}(\rho,A)
. This is because we used the inequality
I^{s}(\rho,A)\leq V(\rho,A)
during the proof process. A reasonable question for further studying is whether one can establish upper bounds depending on the parameter
s
, which may enable us obtain sharper upper bounds and detect more entanglement for multipartite states. This remains an open and challenging problem.

CP violation at finite temperature
Károly Seller, Zsolt Szép, and Zoltán Trócsányi

Abstract
We present a comprehensive study of the finite temperature CP-asymmetry factor needed in the semi-classical treatment of leptogenesis originating from Majorana fermion decays into a lepton and a scalar particle. The imaginary part of the relevant one-loop integrals are evaluated using both the real time and the imaginary time formalisms of thermal quantum field theory. In the former we consider the retarded-advanced approach as well as the original thermal cutting method developed by Kobes and Semenoff. Specific care is directed towards showing the consistency between the various approaches. We show that the final physical result of the calculation is linear in the statistical factors and is consistent with what is obtained in the Kadanoff-Baym approach. We also present analytic expressions for the full CP-asymmetry factor in the form of well-behaved triple-integrals, and provide numerical benchmark predictions in a specific particle physics model.



1
Introduction

Heavy Majorana neutrino decay has an important role in the CP-violating asymmetry factor that determines the lepton number asymmetry in the early Universe
Fukugita:1986hr
.
The original vacuum calculation was done at energy scales where the Higgs scalar and the leptons are massless
Liu:1993tg
.
That result was later extended to include the effect of the one-loop neutrino self-energy that can be the dominant source of the asymmetry in case of nearly mass-degenerate neutrinos
Pilaftsis:1997jf
.
The contribution of both the self-energy and the vertex diagram to the CP-asymmetry was computed at finite temperature in ref.
Covi:1997dr
, ignoring the thermal mass of the scalar and the leptons.
These latter were included in the influential article ref.
Giudice:2003jh
that attempted a complete treatment of thermal leptogenesis in the standard model (SM) and its minimal supersymmetric extension based on Boltzmann equations for the particle numbers involved.

The purpose of the present paper is to further improve the computation of ref.
Giudice:2003jh
in two aspects.
On the one hand the computation in ref.
Giudice:2003jh
neglected two out of the three cuts that appear in the vertex diagram.
In that paper, the omission of these cuts was justified by pointing out that they have exponentially suppressed contributions in
m_{N}/T\gg 1
,
m_{N}
being the mass of the decaying heavy neutrino.
However, the additional contribution of these cuts may be relevant for low-scale leptogenesis (i.e., when
m_{N}\sim T
).
On the other hand, the expressions for the physically relevant thermal self-energy and vertex function in refs.
Covi:1997dr
;
Giudice:2003jh
are quadratic in statistical factors, instead of being linear as one would expect at one-loop level, hence they have to be corrected.
The problem with the number of statistical factors is known in the literature from comparing non-equilibrium results obtained using Kadanoff-Baym equations to equilibrium ones
Garny:2009qn
;
Garny:2009rv
;
Li:2020ner
, although seemingly there is some confusion related to its origin.
We shall show that this discrepancy originates in the identification of the imaginary part of the vertex function component
\Gamma_{111}
as the physical quantity.
In fact the
causal vertex function
related to the physical decay process is a non-trivial combination of independent components of
\Gamma_{abc}
(with vertex types
a,b,c=1,2)
obtained in the real time formalism (RTF) of thermal field theory
Kobes:1990kr
;
Aurenche:1991hi
.

Our present purpose is motivated by our interest in low-scale leptogenesis occurring in the superweak extension of the SM (SWSM)
Trocsanyi:2018bkm
, where the effects of the issues mentioned are unclear a priori.
We shall find that these effects are not negligible.
Thus, we shall present the calculation using both the RTF (following ref.
Giudice:2003jh
) and the imaginary time formalism (ITF), and find agreement between the compared predictions of the two approaches.
The ITF was formerly not employed in this context.
Due to the intricacies of the RTF and because analytic continuation of results obtained in the ITF might be disconcerting for some practitioners in the field, we devote a few appendices to review the relevant literature on the formalisms and to present the details of the calculation.

The article is structured as follows.
We begin in section
2
with an overview of the definition of the CP-asymmetry factor both at zero and at finite temperature.
At zero temperature the analytic structure of the result is presented for non-zero masses.
At finite temperature cutting rules are introduced to evaluate the imaginary part of the one-loop amplitudes and the thermal rate of CP violation is finally given in an integral form.
In section
3
we focus purely on the finite temperature part of the CP-asymmetry factor.
After a short introduction to the self-energy diagram at finite temperature, we detail the calculation of the vertex diagram involving all three cuts, and derive the form of the physically relevant vertex function.
We finish this section by evaluating the vertex diagram in the ITF and compare results with those obtained from the RTF.
In section
4
the formulae for the thermal CP-asymmetry factor are given and numerical results are shown in a particular particle physics model. Finally, a summary is given in section
5
.
Identification and evaluation of the causal vertex function that is the physically relevant quantity contributing to the CP-asymmetry factor constitute the main results of this article.
Additionally, there are 8 appendices detailing specific calculations or methods used in the main text.

Throughout the paper we use natural units (
c=\hbar=k_{\rm B}=1
) appropriate in finite temperature field theory.
Additionally, in all applicable cases we use the Peskin-Schroeder convention for the Minkowski metric and the Feynman rules
Peskin:1995ev
.

2
CP violating asymmetry

The interaction of the right-handed neutrinos (
N
) with the scalar (
\phi
) and the left-handed lepton doublets (
L
) is given by the Lagrangian

\mathcal{L}_{N}=-\bar{L}Y\tilde{\phi}N-\bar{N}Y^{\dagger}\tilde{\phi}^{\dagger}L=-\epsilon_{ab}\bar{L}^{a}_{\alpha}Y_{\alpha i}\phi^{*,b}N^{i}-\epsilon_{ab}^{\rm T}\bar{N}^{i}\phi^{a}(Y^{\dagger})_{i\alpha}L^{b}_{\alpha}\,,

(1)

where
\tilde{\phi}={\mathrm{i}}\sigma_{2}\phi^{*}
,
Y
is the complex Yukawa matrix,
a,b=1,2
denotes components of the doublet,
\epsilon_{ab}=-\epsilon_{ba}
is a totally antisymmetric matrix with
\epsilon_{12}=1
, while
\alpha
is the family index of the SM, and
i
goes over the number of neutrinos involved.
The masses of the particles involved are denoted
m_{N_{i}}
for the right-handed neutrinos,
m_{\phi}
for the scalars, and
m_{L}
for the leptons.
We do not differentiate between the masses of the degrees of freedom within the scalar and lepton doublets as we are only interested in leptogenesis scenarios occurring before the electroweak symmetry breaking, where they are equal.

2.1
Zero temperature case

The CP-violating asymmetry arising from the decay of neutrino through the direct process
N_{i}\to L_{\alpha}^{a}+\phi^{b}
and its CP conjugate,
N_{i}\to\bar{L}_{\alpha}^{a}+\bar{\phi}^{b}
, is defined as

\epsilon_{i}=\frac{\displaystyle\sum_{a,b,\alpha}\Big[\Gamma(N_{i}\to L_{\alpha}^{a}+\phi^{b})-\Gamma(N_{i}\to\bar{L}_{\alpha}^{a}+\bar{\phi}^{b})\Big]}{\displaystyle\sum_{a,b,\alpha}\Big[\Gamma(N_{i}\to L_{\alpha}^{a}+\phi^{b})+\Gamma(N_{i}\to\bar{L}_{\alpha}^{a}+\bar{\phi}^{b})\Big]}\,.

(2)

The tree-level decay rate, which we denote with a superscript
(0)
, is given by

\Gamma^{(0)}(N_{i}\to L_{\alpha}^{a}+\phi^{b})=\frac{1}{2m_{N_{i}}}\int\!{\mathrm{d}}\Pi_{\phi}{\mathrm{d}}\Pi_{L}\,(2\pi)^{4}\delta(P_{N}-P_{\phi}-P_{L})\big\langle\big|\mathcal{M}_{\alpha i}^{ab\,(0)}\big|^{2}\big\rangle,

(3)

where
{\mathrm{d}}\Pi_{x}={\mathrm{d}}^{3}p_{x}/((2\pi)^{3}\,2E_{x})
is the Lorentz-invariant phase space measure and
\langle\,.\,\rangle
denotes summation over spin indices.
We suppressed the dimension of the
\delta
distribution that will be kept implicit throughout the paper.
An elementary calculation shows that the decay rates of the direct and CP-conjugates processes are equal at lowest order in perturbation theory.
Therefore,
\epsilon_{i}
vanishes at tree-level, and to obtain prediction for
\epsilon_{i}
, the decay rates have to be computed at one-loop level, where the interference between tree- and one-loop decay amplitudes gives a non-vanishing result.
Neglecting terms of
\mathcal{O}(Y^{6})
, we have

\displaystyle\epsilon_{i}(T=0)=\frac{\displaystyle\frac{1}{2m_{N_{i}}}\int{\mathrm{d}}\Pi_{\phi}{\mathrm{d}}\Pi_{L}~(2\pi)^{4}\delta(P_{N}-P_{\phi}-P_{L})\,\epsilon_{\mathcal{M}_{i}}\big|M_{i,+}^{(0)}\big|^{2}}{\displaystyle\sum_{a,b,\alpha}\Big[\Gamma^{(0)}(N_{i}\to L_{\alpha}^{a}+\phi^{b})+\Gamma^{(0)}(N_{i}\to\bar{L}_{\alpha}^{a}+\bar{\phi}^{b})\Big]}\,,~~

(4)

where the amplitude-level CP-asymmetry factor
\epsilon_{\mathcal{M}_{i}}
is defined using the tree- and one-loop amplitudes of the decay process as

\epsilon_{\mathcal{M}_{i}}=\frac{\big|M_{i,-}^{[1]}\big|^{2}}{\big|M_{i,+}^{(0)}\big|^{2}},\qquad\big|M_{i,\pm}^{[n]}\big|^{2}=\sum_{a,b,\alpha}\left[\big\langle\big|{\mathcal{M}}^{ab\,[n]}_{\alpha i}\big|^{2}\big\rangle\pm\big\langle\big|\overline{\mathcal{M}}^{ab\,[n]}_{\alpha i}\big|^{2}\big\rangle\right].

(5)

In our notation the superscript
[1]
means that the squared matrix element is computed at one-loop in perturbation theory, i.e.,
M^{[1]}=M^{(0)}+M^{(1)}
.
Introducing
\mathcal{K}=Y^{\dagger}Y
with the property
\mathcal{K}^{\dagger}=\mathcal{K}
, one has

\big|M_{i,+}^{(0)}\big|^{2}=8\mathcal{K}_{ii}P_{L}\,\cdot P_{N}
(no summation over
i
).

.

\displaystyle\big|M_{i,-}^{[1]}\big|^{2}=-16\sum_{j\neq i}\bigg\{

\displaystyle m_{N_{i}}m_{N_{j}}\mathfrak{Im}\big[(\mathcal{K}_{ij})^{2}\big]\bigg[\mathfrak{Im}\,\mathcal{I}^{V}_{L\phi N_{j}}(P_{N},P_{L})

(6)

\displaystyle+P_{N}\cdot P_{L}\bigg(1+\frac{m_{L}^{2}-m_{\phi}^{2}}{P_{N}^{2}}\bigg)\,\mathfrak{Im}\bigg(\frac{\mathcal{B}(P_{N};m_{L},m_{\phi})}{P_{N}^{2}-m^{2}_{N_{j}}+{\mathrm{i}}\varepsilon}\bigg)\bigg]\bigg\}\,,

where
\big|M_{i,-}^{[1]}\big|^{2}
was introduced in eq. (
5
) and the integrals appearing above are defined as

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}^{V}_{L\phi N_{j}}(P_{N},P_{L})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{K}K\cdot P_{L}\,D(K-P_{N}+P_{L},m_{N_{j}})D(K-P_{N},m_{\phi})D(K,m_{L})\,,\quad

(7)

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(P;m_{1},m_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-{\mathrm{i}}\,\int_{K}D(K,m_{1})D(P-K,m_{2})\,.

(8)

Here
D(K,m)={\mathrm{i}}/(K^{2}-m^{2}+{\mathrm{i}}\,\varepsilon)
is the scalar Feynman propagator and
\int_{K}=\int\!\frac{{\mathrm{d}}^{4}K}{(2\pi)^{4}}
with
K=(k_{0},{\bf k})
being the four-momentum in the Minkowski metric.
In eq. (
7
) we suppressed the masses in the argument of the vertex integral in favor of ordered indices to shorten the notation (cf. eq. (

In writing eq. (
6

\propto P^{2}_{N}\mathfrak{Im}(\mathcal{K}_{ij}\mathcal{K}_{ji})
from the contribution of the self-energy, since it vanishes due to the property
\mathcal{K}_{ij}={\mathcal{K}}^{*}_{ji}

Also, we could leave out the term

from the sum, as

Eq. (

Using
P_{\phi}=P_{N}-P_{L}

the integral
\mathcal{I}^{V}_{L\phi N_{j}}
defined in eq. (
7
) can be written in terms of basic one-loop scalar integrals encountered in the usual Passarino-Veltman reduction (see e.g. ref.
Ellis:2007qk
)

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}^{V}_{L\phi N_{j}}(P_{N},P_{L})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(m_{N_{j}}^{2}-m_{\phi}^{2}+P_{N}^{2}-P_{\phi}^{2})\mathcal{C}_{L\phi N_{j}}(P_{N},P_{L})

(9)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\Big[\mathcal{B}(P_{N};m_{L},m_{\phi})-\mathcal{B}(P_{\phi};m_{L},m_{N_{j}})\Big],

where the scalar triangle integral
\mathcal{C}_{L\phi N_{j}}(P_{N},P_{L})
has the same form as the integral in eq. (
7
), but without the factor

in the integrand (see eq. (
95
) for its definition).

We mention that for on-shell external momenta and depending on the relation between the masses, the bubble integral eq. (
8
) can also contribute to the imaginary part of the triangle graph besides the contribution from the scalar triangle integral.
Namely, when
m^{2}_{\phi}>(m_{L}+m_{N_{j}})^{2}
then
\mathfrak{Im}\,\mathcal{B}(m_{\phi};m_{L},m_{N_{j}})
contributes to
\mathfrak{Im}\,\mathcal{I}^{V}_{L\phi N_{j}}
obtained by cutting its two fermionic lines, while
\mathfrak{Im}\,\mathcal{B}(m_{N_{i}};m_{L},m_{\phi})
contributes to
\mathfrak{Im}\,\mathcal{I}^{V}_{L\phi N_{j}}
obtained by cutting the scalar and lepton lines whenever
m_{N_{i}}^{2}>(m_{\phi}+m_{L})^{2}
is satisfied.

At finite temperature Lorentz invariance is broken, thus eq. (
9
) does not hold, that is, simply using the finite temperature bubble and scalar triangle integrals in the formula is not sufficient.
For this reason in section
3
we compute the amplitude-level CP-asymmetry factor differently.

2.2
Finite temperature case

At finite temperature the CP-asymmetry factor is defined by replacing the vacuum decay rates in eq. (
2
) with the thermal interaction rates

\gamma_{N_{i}\to L_{\alpha}^{a}+\phi^{b}}=\!\int\!{\mathrm{d}}\Pi_{N}{\mathrm{d}}\Pi_{\phi}{\mathrm{d}}\Pi_{L}\,(2\pi)^{4}\delta(P_{N}-P_{\phi}-P_{L})\big\langle\big|\mathcal{M}_{\alpha i}^{ab}\big|^{2}\big\rangle f_{\rm F}(E_{N})[1+f_{\rm B}(E_{\phi})][1-f_{\rm F}(E_{L})].

(10)

Here
\mathcal{M}^{ab}_{\alpha i}
is computed using finite temperature Feynman rules, which only matters from one-loop level, and
f_{\rm B/F}(E)
denotes the Bose-Einstein or Fermi-Dirac statistical factors at vanishing chemical potential,

f_{{\rm B}/{\rm F}}(E)=\frac{1}{e^{\beta E}\mp 1}\,,

(11)

where
\beta=T^{-1}
is the inverse temperature.
The finite temperature counterpart of eq. (
4
) is given at leading order in the coupling by

\epsilon_{i}=\frac{\displaystyle\int{\mathrm{d}}\Pi_{N}{\mathrm{d}}\Pi_{\phi}{\mathrm{d}}\Pi_{L}\,(2\pi)^{4}\delta(P_{N}-P_{\phi}-P_{L})\,\epsilon_{\mathcal{M}_{i}}\big|M_{i,+}^{(0)}\big|^{2}f_{\rm F}(E_{N})[1+f_{\rm B}(E_{\phi})][1-f_{\rm F}(E_{L})]}{\displaystyle\sum_{a,b,\alpha}\Big[\gamma^{(0)}_{N_{i}\to L_{\alpha}^{a}+\phi^{b}}+\gamma^{(0)}_{N_{i}\to\bar{L}_{\alpha}^{a}+\bar{\phi}^{b}}\Big]}.

(12)

At finite temperature, especially in cosmological applications, the calculation is usually performed in the rest frame of the plasma, called the
cosmic rest frame
(CR).
Moreover, it is also conventional to add thermal corrections to the vacuum masses and use the so-called
thermal masses
, from here on denoted with
\overline{m}
.
In the CR frame the decaying
N_{i}
has energy
E_{N}\geq\overline{m}_{N_{i}}
and we choose the direction of the momentum as
{\bf p}_{N}\parallel\hat{x}
.
When one calculates the thermal decay rate one has to perform an appropriate boost from the center-of-mass (CM) to the CR frame,

\begin{gathered}E^{\mathrm{CR}}_{L}=\gamma E_{L}^{\mathrm{CM}}+v\gamma p_{x}^{\mathrm{CM}}\equiv E_{L}^{\mathrm{CR}}(E_{N},\theta)\,,\\
E^{\mathrm{CR}}_{\phi}=\gamma E_{\phi}^{\mathrm{CM}}-v\gamma p^{\mathrm{CM}}_{x}\equiv E^{\mathrm{CR}}_{\phi}(E_{N},\theta)\,,\\
E_{L/\phi}^{\mathrm{CM}}=\frac{\overline{m}_{L/\phi}^{2}-\overline{m}_{\phi/L}^{2}+\overline{m}_{N_{i}}^{2}}{2\overline{m}_{N_{i}}}\,,\qquad p_{x}^{\mathrm{CM}}=\sqrt{\big(E_{L}^{\mathrm{CM}}\big)^{2}-\overline{m}_{L}^{2}}\,\cos\theta\,,\end{gathered}

(13)

where
v=p_{N}/E_{N}
is the velocity of the decaying particle,
\gamma=E_{N}/\overline{m}_{N_{i}}
is the Lorentz factor, and
\theta
is the decay angle in the CM frame.

Since
E_{L/\phi}^{\mathrm{CR}}
depends only on the energy of the decaying particle and the decay angle
\theta
, in eq. (
12
) one can use the identity

f_{\rm F}(E_{N})[1+f_{\rm B}(E_{\phi})][1-f_{\rm F}(E_{L})]=f_{\rm F}(E_{N})f_{\rm B}(E_{\phi})f_{\rm F}(E_{L})e^{\beta E_{N}}\,,

(14)

with the definition of the tree-level vacuum decay rate in eq. (
3
) to perform the final state integrals (with the exception of the integral over the scattering angle
\theta
) and write

\begin{split}\gamma^{(0)}_{N_{i}\to L_{\alpha}^{a}+\phi^{b}}=&\gamma^{(0)}_{N_{i}\to\bar{L}_{\alpha}^{a}+\bar{\phi}^{b}}=\frac{\overline{m}_{N_{i}}}{4\pi^{2}}\Gamma^{(0)}(N_{i}\to L_{\alpha}^{a}+\phi^{b})\\
&\times\int_{\overline{m}_{N_{i}}}^{\infty}{\mathrm{d}}E\sqrt{E^{2}-\overline{m}_{N_{i}}^{2}}f_{\rm F}(E)e^{\beta E}\int_{-1}^{1}\!{\mathrm{d}}\cos\theta\,f_{\rm B}\big(E_{\phi}^{\mathrm{CR}}\big)f_{\rm F}\big(E_{L}^{\mathrm{CR}}\big)\,.\end{split}

(15)

The phase space measure for the neutrino was replaced as
{\mathrm{d}}\Pi_{N}=\sqrt{E^{2}-\overline{m}^{2}_{N_{i}}}{\mathrm{d}}E/(2\pi)^{2}
.
Note that the angular integral can be evaluated analytically, see eq. (
176

What remains to be done in order to have
\epsilon_{i}(T)
is to perform the initial and final state integrals in eq. (
12

When computing the final state integrals we use the relation
\displaystyle\big|M_{i,+}^{(0)}\big|^{2}=2\sum_{a,b,\alpha}\langle|\mathcal{M}^{ab\,(0)}_{\alpha i}|^{2}\rangle
to find the vacuum decay rate and then the numerator of
\epsilon_{i}

\begin{split}\sum_{a,b,\alpha}&\Big[\gamma_{N_{i}\to L_{\alpha}^{a}+\phi^{b}}^{(1)}-\gamma_{N_{i}\to\bar{L}_{\alpha}^{a}+\bar{\phi}^{b}}^{(1)}\Big]=\frac{\overline{m}_{N_{i}}}{2\pi^{2}}\sum_{a,b,\alpha}\Gamma^{(0)}(N_{i}\to L_{\alpha}^{a}+\phi^{b})\\
&\times\int_{\overline{m}_{N_{i}}}^{\infty}\!{\mathrm{d}}E\,\sqrt{E^{2}-\overline{m}_{N_{i}}^{2}}f_{\rm F}(E)e^{\beta E}\int_{-1}^{1}\!{\mathrm{d}}\cos\theta\,\epsilon_{\mathcal{M}_{i}}(E,\cos\theta)f_{\rm B}\big(E_{\phi}^{\mathrm{CR}}\big)f_{\rm F}\big(E_{L}^{\mathrm{CR}}\big).\end{split}

(16)

We see that the vacuum decay rate cancels in the expression of
\epsilon_{i}(T)
in eq. (

over the particle distributions.
The expression of

2.3
Calculating the interference using cutting rules

Let the relevant amplitude corresponding to the CP-violating decay process (obtained through standard Feynman rules) be given by
{\mathrm{i}}\mathcal{M}
.
The CP-conjugate amplitude
\overline{\mathcal{M}}
has the same structure as
\mathcal{M}
but with all couplings complex conjugated.
Neglecting indices, a schematic representation of the amplitude is

\mathcal{M}=\sum_{\ell}g_{\ell}\mathcal{I}^{(\ell)}\qquad\to\qquad\overline{\mathcal{M}}=\sum_{\ell}g_{\ell}^{*}\mathcal{I}^{(\ell)}\,.

(17)

Here
g_{\ell}=\mathcal{O}(g^{2\ell+1})
is the overall coupling that appears at
\ell
-loop and
\mathcal{I}^{(\ell)}
is the
\ell
-loop amplitude stripped of all couplings.
In this notation the amplitude-level CP-asymmetry factor introduced in eq. (
5
) at leading order in perturbation theory reads as

\epsilon_{\mathcal{M}}=-2\frac{\mathfrak{Im}(g_{0}^{*}g_{1})}{|g_{0}|^{2}}\sum_{\rm spins}\frac{\mathfrak{Im}\left(\mathcal{I}^{(0)*}\mathcal{I}^{(1)}\right)}{|\mathcal{I}^{(0)}|^{2}}\,.

(18)

The imaginary part of amplitudes can be decomposed using the finite temperature cutting rules
Kobes:1985kc
;
Kobes:1986za
;
Gelis:1997zv
, see appendix
C
for our notations and specific rules.
The cutting rules presented apply to the
n
-point Green’s function
G_{n}
.
However, from field theory we know that the relation between the two is simply
G_{n}\equiv{\mathrm{i}}\mathcal{M}
.
Using the one-loop imaginary part formula in eq. (
111
), one finds for the imaginary part of the one-loop amplitude:

-2\mathfrak{Im}\,{\mathrm{i}}G_{n}^{(1)}\equiv 2\mathfrak{Im}\mathcal{M}^{(1)}=\sum_{\rm circlings}G_{n}^{(1)}\,,

(19)

where the right-hand side is obtained through cutting rules, and the sum refers to all possible circlings of vertices where at least one circled and one uncircled vertex exists.

For the sterile neutrino decay into a scalar field and a lepton the tree-level amplitude is purely given as the spinor product
\mathcal{I}^{(0)}=-\bar{u}_{L}\mathbb{P}_{\rm R}u_{N_{i}}
.
We can also write the one-loop amplitude as

\mathcal{I}^{(1)}\big(\{P_{i}\}\big)=\int_{K}\mathcal{S}^{(1)}\big(\{P_{i}\},K\big)\,\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}^{(1)}\big(\{P_{i}\},K\big)\,,

(20)

with the spinor chain contained in
\mathcal{S}^{(1)}
and the remaining part involving the propagator denominators in
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}^{(1)}
.
We call
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}
the
stripped amplitude
as it is
\mathcal{M}
with couplings, spinors, and the loop momentum integration stripped off
1

1

-{\mathrm{i}}

.
Here we used a notation for the arguments that differentiates the independent external momenta given in parentheses as

The spinor chains of

and

are contracted using the spin summation and we find

\begin{split}\mathcal{T}(K)&=\sum_{\rm spins}\left(-\bar{u}_{L}\mathbb{P}_{\rm R}u_{N_{i}}\right)^{*}\big[\bar{u}_{L}\mathbb{P}_{\rm R}(\not{Q}+\overline{m}_{N_{j}})\mathbb{P}_{\rm R}[(\not{K}+\overline{m}_{L})]\mathbb{P}_{\rm L}\bar{u}_{N_{i}}\big]\\

(21)

where

Calculation of the CP-asymmetry factor at finite temperature

At one loop there are two non-vanishing Feynman diagram contributions to the CP asymmetry produced by the decay of a Majorana neutrino

3.2
CP violation in the vertex correction

In this subsection we focus on the vertex contribution to the CP-asymmetry factor in the sterile neutrino decay.
Our goal here is to show that the imaginary part of the physical vertex diagram that contributes to
\epsilon_{\mathcal{M}}
in eq. (
23
) is given as a non-trivial combination of the thermal vertex components
\mathfrak{Im}\Gamma_{abc}
in the KS formalism.
Namely, with momenta assigned as shown in graph (b) of figure
1
,

-\mathfrak{Im}\Gamma_{\rm C}=\coth\left(\frac{\beta p_{N}^{0}}{2}\right)\mathfrak{Im}\Gamma_{111}^{\sf(cut\,1)}+\tanh\left(\frac{\beta p_{\phi}^{0}}{2}\right)\mathfrak{Im}\Gamma_{111}^{\sf(cut\,2)}+\coth\left(\frac{\beta p_{L}^{0}}{2}\right)\mathfrak{Im}\Gamma_{111}^{\sf(cut\,3)}.

(35)

Here
\Gamma_{\rm C}
is the
causal vertex function
introduced in ref.
Kobes:1990kr
in the context of the
\phi^{3}
scalar theory.
Furthermore,
\Gamma_{111}^{\sf(cut\,i)}
corresponds to the three cuts of the vertex diagram with only type 1 CTP indices, as explained below.
This symmetric structure of the causal vertex function is our new reformulation of known results that used distinct components of the vertex function instead of the aforementioned cuts of one specific component.
We shall see that using
\mathfrak{Im}\Gamma_{111}^{\sf(cut\,i)}
as the independent degrees of freedom of the vertex function leads to a result that is formally simpler than previously used relations.
Note that the right-hand side of eq. (

tHooft:1978jhc
that is summarized in appendix
B

First we compute the thermal vertex function in the KS formalism, in particular relations between the vertex components will be derived and the formulae for the above mentioned three cuts are introduced. Next, the thermal vertex function will be discussed in the RA formalism and
\Gamma_{\rm C}
will be defined as the
\Gamma_{\rm ARR}
component of the vertex function.
Finally, the two formalisms are compared and the causal vertex function is expressed in the KS formalism as shown above.

\displaystyle=\big[f_{\rm B}(p_{N}^{0}-p_{L}^{0})+f_{\rm F}(-p_{L}^{0})\big]\big[1+f_{\rm B}(k_{2}^{0})-f_{\rm F}(p_{N}^{0})\big]\,.

We separated the vertex function into a product of two terms, one that only depends on the external momenta and another that depends on the loop momentum as well.
Upon integration we use
\int_{K_{1}}\!D_{\alpha}(K_{1},\overline{m}_{L})D_{\alpha}(K_{2},\overline{m}_{\phi})D_{\alpha}(K_{3},\overline{m}_{N_{j}})=0
for
\alpha={\mathrm{R}}/{\mathrm{A}}
to cancel some terms in eq. (
52
).
After similar calculations for the other terms finally one has

\begin{split}\int_{K_{1}}\!\tilde{\Gamma}_{{\mathrm{R}};{\mathrm{R}}{\mathrm{R}}}\!\big(\{&P_{N},P_{L}\},K_{1}\big)=\big[f_{\rm B}(p_{N}^{0}-p_{L}^{0})+f_{\rm F}(-p_{L}^{0})\big]\int\frac{{\mathrm{d}}^{4}K_{1}}{(2\pi)^{4}}\Big\{\\
&f_{\rm F}(k_{1}^{0})D_{\mathrm{A}}(K_{2},\overline{m}_{\phi})D_{\mathrm{A}}(K_{3},\overline{m}_{N_{j}})\left[D_{\mathrm{R}}(K_{1},\overline{m}_{L})-D_{\mathrm{A}}(K_{1},\overline{m}_{L})\right]\\
-&f_{\rm B}(k_{2}^{0})D_{\mathrm{R}}(K_{1},\overline{m}_{L})D_{\mathrm{R}}(K_{3},\overline{m}_{N_{j}})\left[D_{\mathrm{R}}(K_{2},\overline{m}_{\phi})-D_{\mathrm{A}}(K_{2},\overline{m}_{\phi})\right]\\
+&f_{\rm F}(k_{3}^{0})D_{\mathrm{R}}(K_{1},\overline{m}_{L})D_{\mathrm{A}}(K_{2},\overline{m}_{\phi})\left[D_{\mathrm{R}}(K_{3},\overline{m}_{N_{j}})-D_{\mathrm{A}}(K_{3},\overline{m}_{N_{j}})\right]\Big\}\,.\end{split}

(54)

The prefactor of the integral is the tree-level vertex function

\tilde{\gamma}_{{\mathrm{R}};{\mathrm{R}}{\mathrm{R}}}(P_{N};P_{N}-P_{L},P_{L})=\tilde{\gamma}_{{\mathrm{R}}{\mathrm{A}}{\mathrm{A}}}(P_{N},-P_{L})=f_{\rm B}(p_{N}^{0}-p_{L}^{0})+f_{\rm F}(-p_{L}^{0})\,.

(55)

This is a general result, one can always factorize the corresponding tree-level vertex function in the expressions of one-loop diagrams
Aurenche:1991hi
.
Due to this prefactor being independent of the loop momentum and being equal to the tree-level vertex function, it cancels when calculating the amplitude-level CP-asymmetry factor, see eq. (
5
).
In the following, we shall take this into account explicitly and consider the vertex correction as

\tilde{V}_{{\mathrm{R}};{\mathrm{R}}{\mathrm{R}}}\big(\{P_{N},P_{L}\},K_{1}\big)=\frac{\tilde{\Gamma}_{{\mathrm{R}};{\mathrm{R}}{\mathrm{R}}}\big(\{P_{N},P_{L}\},K_{1}\big)}{\tilde{\gamma}_{{\mathrm{R}};{\mathrm{R}}{\mathrm{R}}}(P_{N};P_{N}-P_{L},P_{L})}\,.

(56)

The one-loop vertex correction is thus linear in the statistical factors, cf. eq. (
54
), as generally required by thermal field theory.

The vertex correction
\tilde{V}_{{\mathrm{R}};{\mathrm{R}}{\mathrm{R}}}
is the sum of three contributions that can be decomposed into the sum of the three cuts that we have seen in section
3.2.1
.
Using the second equality in eq. (
51
), the difference of the retarded and advanced propagators gives the on-shell part of the propagator,

D_{\mathrm{R}}(P,m)-D_{\mathrm{A}}(P,m)={\rm sgn}(p^{0})2\pi\delta(P^{2}-m^{2})\,.

(57)

The difference is real, thus in order to find the imaginary part of the vertex function we need to determine the imaginary part of the remaining product of two R/A propagators in each line of eq. (
54
).
With
\alpha_{i}={\mathrm{R}}/{\mathrm{A}}
for
i=1,2
we have:

\mathfrak{Im}\big[D_{\alpha_{1}}(P_{1},m_{1})D_{\alpha_{2}}(P_{2},m_{2})\big]=\sum_{i=1,2}(-1)^{\delta_{\alpha_{i}{\mathrm{A}}}}{\rm sgn}(p_{i}^{0})\pi\delta(P_{i}^{2}-m_{i}^{2})\mathcal{P}\frac{1}{P_{i}^{2}-m_{i}^{2}}\,.

(58)

Finally, after direct substitution and some organization of terms such that each line collects terms with the same propagator structure one finds

\begin{split}\int_{K_{1}}\!\mathfrak{Im}&\tilde{V}_{{\mathrm{R}};{\mathrm{R}}{\mathrm{R}}}\big(\{P_{N},P_{L}\},K_{1}\big)=-2\pi^{2}\int\!\frac{{\mathrm{d}}^{4}K_{1}}{(2\pi)^{4}}\bigg\{\\
&{\rm sgn}(k_{1}^{0}){\rm sgn}(k_{2}^{0})\mathcal{P}\frac{1}{K_{3}^{2}-\overline{m}_{N_{j}}^{2}}\delta(K_{1}^{2}-\overline{m}_{L}^{2})\delta(K_{2}^{2}-\overline{m}_{\phi}^{2})\big[f_{\rm F}(k_{1}^{0})+f_{\rm B}(k_{2}^{0})\big]\\
+&{\rm sgn}(k_{1}^{0}){\rm sgn}(k_{3}^{0})\mathcal{P}\frac{1}{K_{2}^{2}-\overline{m}_{\phi}^{2}}\delta(K_{1}^{2}-\overline{m}_{L}^{2})\delta(K_{3}^{2}-\overline{m}_{N_{j}}^{2})\big[f_{\rm F}(k_{1}^{0})-f_{\rm F}(k_{3}^{0})\big]\\
+&{\rm sgn}(k_{2}^{0}){\rm sgn}(k_{3}^{0})\mathcal{P}\frac{1}{K_{1}^{2}-\overline{m}_{L}^{2}}\delta(K_{2}^{2}-\overline{m}_{\phi}^{2})\delta(K_{3}^{2}-\overline{m}_{N_{j}}^{2})\big[f_{\rm F}(k_{3}^{0})+f_{\rm B}(k_{2}^{0})\big]\bigg\}\,.\end{split}

(59)

Each line above corresponds to one specific cut of the one-loop vertex diagram as each line involves two propagators put on-shell by the
\delta
distributions, and an off-shell propagator corresponding to the uncut propagator.
In the numbering convention introduced for the cuts in figure
3
and with
K_{i}
defined as below eq. (
49
), the first line of eq. (
59
) corresponds to cut 1, the second line is cut 2, and the third line is cut 3.

In ref.
Kobes:1990kr
the author introduced the causal vertex function as the one-loop amplitude at finite temperature corresponding to the

inverse decay process.
For incoming momenta
P_{1,2}
(and outgoing momentum

) their definition could be expressed in terms of the RA formalism as
Kobes:1990kr
;
Aurenche:1991hi

\Gamma_{\rm C}(P_{1},P_{2})\equiv\Gamma_{{\mathrm{R}}{\mathrm{R}};{\mathrm{R}}}(P_{1},P_{2};P_{1}+P_{2})\,.

(60)

While this definition was introduced in the context of a
\phi^{3}
scalar field theory, we are free to extend it to any theory involving other types of fields as well.
As the corresponding tree-level vertex is trivial

immediately gives the one-loop vertex correction

In fact, this is the reason why the

Naturally, a similarly defined causal vertex function in the context of the

interaction is expected to be related to the one-loop vertex correction that we derived in eq. (

In our assignment of the momenta shown in eq. (

) and reorganizing the indices as

\Gamma_{\rm C}(P_{N},P_{L})=V_{{\mathrm{A}};{\mathrm{A}}{\mathrm{A}}}(P_{N};P_{N}-P_{L},P_{L})=V^{*}_{{\mathrm{R}};{\mathrm{R}}{\mathrm{R}}}(P_{N},P_{N}-P_{L},P_{L})\,.

(61)

The last equality is a general consequence of the RA formalism upon switching all indices as

More concretely, it follows from the retarded and advanced propagators being complex conjugates of each other, see eq. (

The imaginary part of the vertex correction in eq. (

\mathfrak{Im}V_{{\mathrm{R}};{\mathrm{R}}{\mathrm{R}}}(P_{N};P_{N}-P_{L},P_{L})=-\mathfrak{Im}\Gamma_{\rm C}(P_{N},P_{L})\,.

Connecting the KS and RA formalisms

3.3
The scalar triangle function in the imaginary time formalism

Although it is customary to write the imaginary part of the triangle graph using cutting rules, we show bellow how to obtain it in the ITF.
To compare with the stripped vertex function given in eq. (
59
), it is sufficient to focus on the scalar triangle function.

At finite temperature the scalar triangle function, represented diagrammatically in figure
4
, is given as

\mathcal{C}^{T}_{L\phi N_{j}}(\bar{P}_{N},\bar{P}_{L})=T\sum_{m}\int\frac{{\mathrm{d}}^{3}k}{(2\pi)^{3}}\Delta(\bar{K}+\bar{P}_{N}-\bar{P}_{L},m_{N_{j}})\Delta(\bar{K}+\bar{P}_{N},m_{\phi})\Delta(\bar{K},m_{L})\,.

(71)

This expression is obtained from the vacuum expression of eq. (
95
) through the substitutions

\displaystyle\int_{K}\to{\mathrm{i}}T\int\!\frac{{\mathrm{d}}^{3}k}{(2\pi)^{3}}\sum_{m=-\infty}^{\infty}\quad\textrm{and}\quad D(K,m_{L})\to-{\mathrm{i}}\Delta(\bar{K},m_{L})\,.

(72)

In doing so we also replaced the Minkowskian four momenta with Euclidean ones (indicated with a bar on the momenta),

\bar{K}=({\mathrm{i}}\nu_{m},{\bf k}),\quad\bar{P}_{N}=({\mathrm{i}}\nu_{n},{\bf p}_{N}),\quad\bar{P}_{L}=({\mathrm{i}}\nu_{l},{\bf p}_{L}),\quad{\rm and}\quad\bar{P}_{\phi}=\bar{P}_{N}-\bar{P}_{L}=({\mathrm{i}}\omega_{h},{\bf p}_{\phi})\,,

where we introduced the Matsubara frequencies
\nu_{i}
(
i=l,\,m,\,n
) for fermions and
\omega_{h}
for the boson, which take discrete values
\nu_{i}=(2i+1)\pi T
and
\omega_{h}=2h\pi T
with
i,\,h\in\mathbb{Z}
. They satisfy the usual energy conservation relation
\omega_{h}=\nu_{n}-\nu_{l}
.
As we decoupled the spinor structure, the tree level vacuum propagators for bosons and fermions have the same form, yet they differ at finite temperature in their Matsubara frequencies.

The spectral representation of the Euclidean propagators is formally the same for fermions and bosons with the specific Matsubara frequency determining the particle nature.
For example for the lepton we have

\Delta(\bar{K},m_{L})=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{dk^{0}}{2\pi}\frac{\rho_{L}(K)}{k^{0}-{\mathrm{i}}\nu_{m}},\quad\rho_{L}(K)=2\pi\,\textrm{sgn}(k^{0})\delta(K^{2}-m^{2}_{L})\,.

(73)

One can then write the thermal scalar triangle function in the form

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}^{T}_{L\phi N_{j}}(\bar{P}_{N},\bar{P}_{L})

\displaystyle=\int\frac{{\mathrm{d}}^{3}k}{(2\pi)^{3}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}k_{1}^{0}}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}k_{2}^{0}}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}k_{3}^{0}}{2\pi}

(74)

\displaystyle\bigg[\rho_{L}(k_{1}^{0},{\bf k})\rho_{\phi}(k_{2}^{0},{\bf k}+{\bf p}_{N})\rho_{N_{j}}(k_{3}^{0},{\bf k}+{\bf p}_{\phi})~T\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\prod_{j=1}^{3}\frac{1}{k_{j}^{0}-{\mathrm{i}}\omega_{j}}\bigg]\,,

where
\omega_{1}=\nu_{m}
,
\omega_{2}=\nu_{m}+\nu_{n}
, and
\omega_{3}=\nu_{m}+\nu_{n}-\nu_{l}=\nu_{m}+\omega_{h}
.

Particle and momentum assignment in the scalar triangle function.

The Matsubara sum can be computed using standard methods (see e.g. section 4.2.3 in ref.
Bellac:2011kqa
).
We use Gaudin’s method summarized in appendix
E
, which applied to the present case leads to the following identity:

\displaystyle T

\displaystyle\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\prod_{j=1}^{3}\frac{1}{k_{j}^{0}-{\mathrm{i}}\omega_{j}}=-\frac{f_{\rm F}(k_{1}^{0})}{\big(k_{2}^{0}-(k_{1}^{0}+{\mathrm{i}}\nu_{n})\big)\big(k_{3}^{0}-(k_{1}^{0}+{\mathrm{i}}\omega_{h})\big)}

(75)

\displaystyle+\frac{f_{\rm B}(k_{2}^{0})}{\big(k_{1}^{0}-(k_{2}^{0}-{\mathrm{i}}\nu_{n})\big)\big(k_{3}^{0}-(k_{2}^{0}-{\mathrm{i}}\nu_{l})\big)}-\frac{f_{\rm F}(k_{3}^{0})}{\big(k_{1}^{0}-(k_{3}^{0}-{\mathrm{i}}\omega_{h})\big)\big(k_{2}^{0}-(k_{3}^{0}+{\mathrm{i}}\nu_{l})\big)}\,.

Plugging eq. (
75
) in eq. (
74
) one can use the spectral representation given in eq. (
73
) to perform in each term two integrals over the energies (real frequencies) that do not appear as argument of the statistical factor.
The remaining energy integral can be combined with the integral over
{\bf k}
after utilizing the finite shifts
3
3

3

The integrals are finite, hence finite shifts are allowed.
of the integration variable
{\bf k}\to{\bf k}-{\bf p}_{N}
and
{\bf k}\to{\bf k}-{\bf p}_{N}+{\bf p}_{L}={\bf k}-{\bf p}_{\phi}
in the second and third terms of the sum eq. (
75
).
Thus we obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}^{T}_{L\phi N_{j}}(\bar{P}_{N},\bar{P}_{L})=\int_{K}\Big[

\displaystyle-f_{\rm F}(k^{0})\,\rho_{L}(K)\Delta(K+\bar{P}_{N},m_{\phi})\Delta(K+\bar{P}_{N}-\bar{P}_{L},m_{N_{j}})

(76)

\displaystyle+f_{\rm B}(k^{0})\,\rho_{\phi}(K)\Delta(K-\bar{P}_{N},m_{L})\Delta(K-\bar{P}_{L},m_{N_{j}})

\displaystyle-f_{\rm F}(k^{0})\,\rho_{N_{j}}(K)\Delta(K-\bar{P}_{N}+\bar{P}_{L},m_{L})\Delta(K+\bar{P}_{L},m_{\phi})\Big]\,.

Now, one does the analytic continuation back to real frequencies, for which we use
{\mathrm{i}}\nu_{n}\to p_{N}^{0}+2{\mathrm{i}}\varepsilon
,
{\mathrm{i}}\nu_{l}\to p_{L}^{0}+{\mathrm{i}}\varepsilon
and

\displaystyle\!\!\!\mathcal{C}^{T}_{L\phi N_{j}}(P_{N},P_{L})=\int_{K}\Big[

(77)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle-

(79)

which completes the derivation of the imaginary part of the scalar triangle integral in the imaginary time formalism, relating its expression to the one obtained in the real time formalism.

4
Results and discussion

In this section we present our final results for the CP-asymmetry factor for the mass hierarchy
\overline{m}_{N_{j}}\gtrsim\overline{m}_{N_{i}}>\overline{m}_{\phi}+\overline{m}_{L}
.
First, we show the final formulae for contributions of the self-energy and the three cuts of the vertex function to the thermally averaged CP-asymmetry factor.
We relegated most details of the calculation into appendices
F
and
G
.
Afterwards, we show numerical results for the thermal CP-asymmetry in the context of a specific particle physics model with right-handed neutrinos, and compare them to various approximations that may be found in the literature.
We end this section with a discussion on the relevance of using the full expression of the CP-asymmetry and its possible consequences in leptogenesis.

4.1

In eq. (

The key ingredient in evaluating these integrals is to choose a suitable reference frame and integration variables.
The reference frame is the CR frame, however we still have the freedom to orient the spatial part of the coordinate system in any direction we want.
The natural system to use changes with the various diagrams:
(i) for the self-energy and first cut of the vertex function the natural choice is using

{\bf p}_{N}\parallel\hat{x}

Naturally, the integration over the initial phase space in eq. (

Such changes to the integration variable is non-trivial due to the relationship between the initial- and final-state energies not being in a one-to-one relationship, as explained in great detail in appendix

First, we deal with the loop momentum integration in the amplitude-level CP-asymme-try factors.
Recall, that in both the self-energy and the vertex contributions the integrands involve the product of two
\delta

These Dirac-deltas could be used to trivially evaluate 2 out of the 4 integrals in

\int_{K}=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{4}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\!{\mathrm{d}}\omega\int_{0}^{\infty}\!{\mathrm{d}}k\int_{0}^{2\pi}\!{\mathrm{d}}\varphi^{\prime}\int_{-1}^{1}\!{\mathrm{d}}\cos\theta^{\prime}\,.

We choose to perform the

The

From the remaining two integrals, the one for the polar angle can be evaluated analytically, with the result generally denoted as

The functions

4.2
CP-asymmetry in the superweak extension of the SM

The superweak extension of the Standard Model
Trocsanyi:2018bkm
is a phenomenological particle physics model where 3 right-handed neutrinos, a SM-singlet scalar field, and a new U(1)
z
force carrier are added to the spectrum of the SM.
The model was devised to be a simple extension to the SM such that it is capable of simultaneously explaining various beyond the SM phenomena, for example dark matter
Iwamoto:2021fup
and vacuum stability
Peli:2022ybi
.

For our discussion the relevant detail of the particle physics model is the generation of the Majorana mass term of the sterile neutrinos through a Higgs-mechanism facilitated by the singlet scalar acquiring a non-zero vacuum expectation value and spontaneously breaking the new U(1)
z
symmetry.
The corresponding phase transition is not expected to be strongly first order and it had been studied within the context of the SWSM in ref.
Seller:2023xkn
.
Once the temperature-dependence of the vacuum expectation value of the singlet scalar field is determined, one can write the full thermal mass of the sterile neutrinos as the sum of the vacuum term and thermal corrections, both being non-zero below the symmetry-breaking temperature.
The vacuum term eventually outgrows the purely thermal contribution and the sterile neutrino becomes heavier than the leptons and scalar fields at relatively low temperatures, see figure
5
, allowing the process
N_{i}\to\phi+L
to take place.
We refer the reader to ref.
Seller:2023xkn
for additional details regarding the particle physics model, in particular for the definitions of the thermal masses, and the computation of the temperature-dependence of the vacuum expectation values.

As indicated in figure
5
the thermal mass-hierarchy changes with temperature and the
N_{i}\to\phi+L
sterile neutrino decay is only available below some temperature
T_{1}
where the threshold
\overline{m}_{N_{i}}(T_{1})=\overline{m}_{L}(T_{1})+\overline{m}_{\phi}(T_{1})
is satisfied.
For the parameters used in figure
5
this temperature is
T_{1}\approx 511\,
GeV.
Exactly at
T=T_{1}
the CM momentum vanishes, see eq. (
13
), and consequently the loop-energy integration boundaries defined in eq. (
145

\omega_{\pm}^{(1)}\Big|_{\overline{m}_{N_{i}}=\overline{m}_{L}+\overline{m}_{\phi}}=\frac{E_{N}\overline{m}_{L}}{\overline{m}_{N_{i}}}\quad\rightarrow\quad\int_{\omega_{-}^{(1)}}^{\omega_{+}^{(1)}}{\mathrm{d}}\omega\,f(\omega)=0\,.

(91)

As a result, at
T=T_{1}
both contributions of the self-energy and of the first cut of the vertex function to the CP-asymmetry factor are zero.
This is easily understood as in both of these cases the CP-asymmetry is proportional to the decay rate (optical theorem) that vanishes due to no available phase-space for the final states.
Importantly, this is not the case for the second and third cuts of the vertex function, see eqs. (
156
) and (
165

T=T_{1}
.
In fact, we expect that these contribute in a non-trivial way, as these cuts correspond to physical, necessarily thermal processes where particles are absorbed from the plasma
Weldon:1983jn
.
This means that at and close to
T_{1}
, we expect that contributions from the second and third vertex cuts dominate over those of the first cut and the self-energy.

5
Summary and outlook

In this article, we presented the evaluation of the one-loop CP-asymmetry factor at finite temperature in various approaches to thermal quantum field theory.
In particular, to evaluate the imaginary parts of Feynman diagrams in the real time formalism, we used (i) the finite temperature cutting rules originally introduced by Kobes and Semenoff and (ii) the retarded-advanced formalism, and showed that the two approaches lead to the same result.
We have also shown how this result may be obtained in the imaginary time formalism.

We showed that by considering the physical thermal vertex function, the resulting one-loop CP-asymmetry factor is linear in the statistical factor, as expected on general grounds in thermal field theory and found in studies using the Kadanoff-Baym formalism.
We demonstrated that there is
no difference
between the thermal CP-asymmetry factors obtained in the various approaches, the perceived discrepancy may only be due to an incorrect identification of the physical quantity.

A new feature of our computations is that we have taken into account all contributions from the three cut diagrams of the vertex diagram, in addition to that of the self-energy diagram, with finite thermal masses for all particles involved.
Furthermore, we have also derived a new, symmetric formula for the causal vertex function in terms of the cuts of the one-loop vertex diagram with only type-1 external CTP indices in the Kobes-Semenoff approach.
Finally, we derived compact parametric double- and triple-integrals for the thermal CP-asymmetry factors and presented numerical predictions indicating that the contributions due to all cut diagrams are to be considered when the temperature is comparable to the masses of the heaviest particles involved.

We have presented results in the special case when the mass hierarchy was fixed such that the neutrino in the loop was heavier than the decaying one.
For a complete study of the thermal CP-asymmetry factor, and consequently leptogenesis scenarios, different mass hierarchies should also be considered, for example a light internal neutrino in the vertex correction would also introduce contributions due to the previously not considered cuts that are not vanishing even in the vacuum.
Further investigation is also needed in the temperature range where vacuum decay is kinematically forbidden, as here thermal absorption phenomena can still contribute to the CP-asymmetry.

Appendix C
Finite temperature cutting rules

In this appendix we introduce the method for evaluating the imaginary parts of Feynman amplitudes at finite temperature.
We follow the so-called circling method devised by Kobes and Semenoff
Kobes:1985kc
;
Kobes:1986za
(see also refs.
Aurenche:1991hi
;
Gelis:1997zv
) in the context of the RTF of the thermal quantum field theory (see e.g.
Bellac:2011kqa
).
We begin with introducing the RTF propagators for bosons and fermions, then we present the cutting rules at finite temperature, and finally we give the formula for the imaginary part of an arbitrary
n
-point function.

In the RTF the usual time ordering operator along the real time axis is exchanged with a so-called path ordering operator along the Schwinger-Keldysh contour (closed-time-path, CTP)

;
Keldysh:1964ud
that spans from
t=-\infty
to
t=\infty
and back with an additional piece in the imaginary direction needed in equilibrium studies.
Due to the doubling of the contour along the real time axis the thermal propagator becomes a
2\times 2
matrix with each component corresponding to a propagator connecting time coordinates lying on either the “forward” temporal path (corresponds to real particles) or the “backward” temporal path (corresponds to ghosts).

Subject to periodic (for bosons) and anti-periodic (for fermions) boundary conditions, called the Kubo–Martin–Schwinger (KMS) conditions, the propagators in momentum space are as follows.
In the so-called
s

Bellac:2011kqa
) the bosonic thermal propagators are

\displaystyle G_{11}(P,m)

\displaystyle=G_{22}^{*}(P,m)={\mathrm{i}}\mathcal{P}\left(\frac{1}{P^{2}-m^{2}}\right)+\pi\delta\big(P^{2}-m^{2}\big)\big(1+2f_{\rm B}(|p^{0}|)\big)\,,

(107)

\displaystyle G_{12}(P,m)

\displaystyle=G_{21}(P,m)=2\pi\delta\big(P^{2}-m^{2}\big)e^{\beta|p^{0}|/2}f_{\rm B}(|p^{0}|)\,,

while for fermions we express the propagators as
S_{ij}(P,m)=(\not{P}+m)\tilde{S}_{ij}(P,m)
, where

\displaystyle\tilde{S}_{11}(P,m)

\displaystyle=\tilde{S}_{22}^{*}(P,m)={\mathrm{i}}\mathcal{P}\left(\frac{1}{P^{2}-m^{2}}\right)+\pi\delta\big(P^{2}-m^{2}\big)\big(1-2f_{\rm F}(|p^{0}|)\big)\,,

\displaystyle\tilde{S}_{12}(P,m)

\displaystyle=-\tilde{S}_{21}(P,m)=-\mathrm{sgn}(p^{0})\,2\pi\delta\big(P^{2}-m^{2}\big)e^{\beta|p^{0}|/2}f_{\rm F}(|p^{0}|)\,.

We denoted the principal value with

In both cases the temperature-dependent part of the propagator is explicitly on-shell and is linear in the statistical factor.
In fact,

The thermal parts of these propagators explicitly break Lorentz-invariance, consequently the notation

We also introduce the positive and negative frequency propagators

These are trivially connected with the off-diagonal elements of the propagator matrix,

\displaystyle G_{\pm}(P,m)=e^{\pm\beta p^{0}/2}G_{12}(P,m)\,,

(108a)

\displaystyle S_{\pm}(P,m)=\mp e^{\pm\beta p^{0}/2}S_{12}(P,m)\,,

(108b)

or explicitly:

\displaystyle G_{\pm}(P,m)=2\pi\delta(P^{2}-m^{2})\left[\theta(\pm p^{0})+f_{\rm B}(|p^{0}|)\right]\,,

(109a)

\displaystyle\tilde{S}_{\pm}(P,m)=2\pi\delta(P^{2}-m^{2})\left[\theta(\pm p^{0})-f_{\rm F}(|p^{0}|)\right]\,.

(109b)

Note that the temperature-dependent part of the propagators is not vanishing for any sign of the particle energy thus the direction of the energy flow is not constrained as it is in vacuum.

Each vertex in a Feynman diagram corresponds to a specific space-time point.
However, in thermal field theory these vertices can lie either on the “forward” or the “backward” temporal path of the Schwinger-Keldysh contour.

The vertices are labeled with a CTP index 1 or 2 that reflect their position on the Schwinger-Keldysh contour.

Gaudin’s method

The method is especially efficient in case of multiloop Feynman graphs, where for
L

L

The method uses the spectral representation of the propagators, hence it is applicable also when full propagators are used instead of the Feynman ones.
For details, subtleties, and applications of the method the reader is referred to refs.
Espinosa:2005gq
;
Blaizot:2004bg

We write the
L

Gaudin’s method then consists of two steps:
(i) first the product of
I

L

L

The result of (i) is a decomposition formula of the form

\prod_{i=1}^{I}\frac{1}{k_{i}^{0}-{\mathrm{i}}\omega_{n_{i}}}=\sum_{g\in\mathcal{G}}\bigg[\prod_{j\in\mathcal{T}_{g}}\frac{1}{k_{j}^{0}-{\mathrm{i}}\Omega_{j}\big(\{k_{i}^{0}\};\{{\mathrm{i}}\omega_{e_{i}}\}\big)}\,\prod_{l\in\bar{\mathcal{T}}_{g}}\frac{1}{k_{l}^{0}-{\mathrm{i}}\omega_{n_{l}}}\bigg]\,.

Appendix F
Parametrization of the integrals in the expressions for the thermal vertex cuts

In this appendix we detail the evaluation strategy of the integrals that appear in the expressions for the second and third thermal vertex cuts as introduced in section
3.2
, see eqs. (
68
)–(
69
).
First, we indicate the differences as compared to the first cut.
Second, we discuss the specific coordinate system and integration variable that is most convenient for the problem.

The amplitudes corresponding to the cuts in question are proportional to the integral of products of
\delta
distributions,

\displaystyle\mathfrak{Im}\Gamma^{\sf(cut\,2,3)}\propto\int\frac{{\mathrm{d}}^{4}K}{(2\pi)^{4}}\delta\Big(K^{2}-m_{L,\phi}^{2}\Big)\delta\Big((K-P_{\phi,L})^{2}-m_{N_{j}}^{2}\Big)(...)\,.

(126)

The four-momentum
K
is the momentum of the internal lepton in
\mathfrak{Im}\Gamma^{\sf(cut\,2)}
, and of the internal scalar in
\mathfrak{Im}\Gamma^{\sf(cut\,3)}
.
Expanding the
\delta
distributions of the cut neutrino propagators we find scalar products of the loop momentum and the outgoing momenta (
K\cdot P_{L}
and
K\cdot P_{\phi}
).
These scalar products depend non-linearly on the cosine of the loop azimuth angle
\theta^{\prime}
and they also depend on the loop polar angle
\varphi^{\prime}
, whereas
K\cdot P_{N}
was linear in
\cos\theta^{\prime}
and was independent of
\varphi^{\prime}
allowing for a simple evaluation of the
\cos\theta^{\prime}
-integral in the case of cut 1.
It is then convenient to choose a coordinate system such that
{\bf p}_{L,\phi}\parallel\hat{x}
(instead of
{\bf p}_{N}\parallel\hat{x}
as done for cut 1), where the integrals are evaluated identically to those in the first cut.
In addition to the spatial rotation of the coordinate system we also find that the natural choice for the independent energy variable is
E_{L,\phi}
instead of
E_{N}
.

As a starting point we first describe the change of variables
E_{N}\to E_{L,\phi}
.
We define an example integral with a well-behaved function
\mathcal{F}
over the physical domains of the incoming neutrino energy
E_{N}
and center of mass scattering angle
\theta
:

\mathcal{I}=\int_{m_{N_{i}}}^{\infty}{\mathrm{d}}E_{N}\int_{-1}^{1}{\mathrm{d}}\cos\theta~\mathcal{F}(E_{N},\cos\theta)\,.

(127)

In the changing of the energy variable
E_{N}
there is a great deal of symmetry between
E_{L,\phi}
and thus we present them together.
To find the function
E_{N}(E_{L,\phi},\cos\theta)
we need to invert the original relations introduced for the Lorentz-boosted decay kinematics, with
x=\cos\theta
and
{\rm p}=L,\phi
we have:

E_{\rm p}(E_{N},x)=\frac{E_{N}}{m_{N_{i}}}\left[E_{\rm p}^{\mathrm{CM}}+(-1)^{\delta_{{\rm p}\phi}}\frac{\sqrt{E_{N}^{2}-m_{N_{i}}^{2}}}{E_{N}}p^{\mathrm{CM}}\,x\right]\,.

(128)

With the exception of
x
, all involved quantities are positive and we find a relationship between the various energies and the sign of
x
:

\displaystyle\frac{E_{N}}{m_{N_{i}}}>\frac{E_{\rm p}(E_{N},x)}{E_{\rm p}^{\mathrm{CM}}}\quad

\displaystyle\text{when}\quad\begin{cases}x<0&{\rm for}~{\rm p}=L\,,\\
x>0&{\rm for}~{\rm p}=\phi\,,\end{cases}

(129a)

\displaystyle\frac{E_{N}}{m_{N_{i}}}<\frac{E_{\rm p}(E_{N},x)}{E_{\rm p}^{\mathrm{CM}}}\quad

\displaystyle\text{when}\quad\begin{cases}x>0&{\rm for}~{\rm p}=L\,,\\
x<0&{\rm for}~{\rm p}=\phi\,.\end{cases}

(129b)

These relations are considered as consistency conditions when taking the square of eq. (
128
) and solving the resulting quadratic equation for
E_{N}
.
For any sign of
x
the two solutions (labeled with
\pm
) to the quadratic equation are given by:

E_{N}^{\pm}(E_{\rm p},x)=m_{N_{i}}\cdot\frac{\displaystyle\frac{E_{\rm p}}{E_{\rm p}^{\mathrm{CM}}}\pm|x|\cdot\frac{p^{\mathrm{CM}}}{E_{\rm p}^{\mathrm{CM}}}\sqrt{\left(\frac{E_{\rm p}}{E_{\rm p}^{\mathrm{CM}}}\right)^{2}-1+x^{2}\left(\frac{p^{\mathrm{CM}}}{E_{\rm p}^{\mathrm{CM}}}\right)^{2}}}{\displaystyle 1-x^{2}\left(\frac{p^{\mathrm{CM}}}{E_{\rm p}^{\mathrm{CM}}}\right)^{2}}\,.

(130)

As it is seen from eq. (
130
), the relationship between
E_{N}
and
E_{\rm p}
is not always one-to-one.
When boosts have an anti-parallel component to the momentum vector of the outgoing particle, we get the same outgoing particle energy for two distinct incoming particle energies.
In this case, the boosted energy is initially smaller than the CM energy down to a critical energy where the boosted decay angle is exactly orthogonal to the boost vector itself.
For larger boosts the energy increases indefinitely, providing a double cover between the critical energy and the CM energy, as shown in figure

.
The critical energy is defined as the smallest possible value of the boosted energy of one outgoing particle,

E_{\rm p}^{\rm\,crit}(x)=\sqrt{\left(E_{\rm p}^{\mathrm{CM}}\right)^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\big(1+(-1)^{\delta_{{\rm p}L}}{\rm sgn}(x)\big)\left(p^{\mathrm{CM}}\right)^{2}x^{2}}\leq E_{\rm p}^{\mathrm{CM}}\,.

(131)

As expected from the above discussion, depending on the sign of
x
, the minimum energy is seen to be either equal to, or smaller than the CM energy.

To remove unphysical solutions from eq. (
130
) we must subject the results to the consistency relations of eqs. (
129a
)–(
129b
).
First, the requirement of
E_{N}^{+}
remaining real and the consistency relations provide constraints on the energy interval and on the sign of
\cos\theta

E_{N}^{+}(E_{\rm p},\cos\theta):\qquad\begin{cases}E_{L}\geq E_{L}^{\rm crit}(\cos\theta)&\text{ for }~\cos\theta<0\,,\\
E_{\phi}\geq E_{\phi}^{\rm crit}(\cos\theta)&\text{ for }~\cos\theta>0\,.\end{cases}

(132)

The other solution is more complicated as there are two regions in
E_{\rm p}
that satisfy different consistency relations.
It turns out that
E_{N}^{-}(E_{\rm p},\cos\theta)
is physical if

E_{N}^{-}(E_{\rm p},\cos\theta):\qquad\begin{cases}E_{L}\geq E_{L}^{\mathrm{CM}}&\text{ for }~\cos\theta>0\,,\\
E_{L}^{\mathrm{CM}}\geq E_{L}\geq E_{L}^{\rm crit}(\cos\theta)&\text{ for }~\cos\theta<0\,,\\
E_{\phi}\geq E_{\phi}^{\mathrm{CM}}&\text{ for }~\cos\theta<0\,,\\
E_{\phi}^{\mathrm{CM}}\geq E_{\phi}\geq E_{\phi}^{\rm crit}(\cos\theta)&\text{ for }~\cos\theta>0\,.\end{cases}

(133)

Comparing eqs. (
132
) and (

) we find that the energy intervals

are doubly covered by having solutions with both

.
All other regions in the energy range are single valued.

Appendix G
Evaluation of the integrals in the CP-asymmetry factor

In this appendix we calculate the loop-integrals appearing in the contributions to the CP-asymmetry factor, in eqs. (
32
) and (
70
).
We assume that the thermal masses satisfy
\overline{m}_{N_{i}}>\overline{m}_{\phi}+\overline{m}_{L}
while for the sterile neutrino in the loop we have
\overline{m}_{N_{j}}>\overline{m}_{N_{i}}
.
We work in the CR frame where the 4-momenta of the decay products can be expressed using the CM frame kinematics given in eq. (
13
) as

P_{L/\phi}\equiv P_{L/\phi}^{\mathrm{CR}}=\begin{pmatrix}\overline{m}_{N}^{-1}\big(E_{L/\phi}^{\mathrm{CM}}E_{N}\pm p^{\mathrm{CM}}p_{N}\cos\theta\big)\\
\overline{m}_{N}^{-1}\big(E_{L/\phi}^{\mathrm{CM}}p_{N}\pm p^{\mathrm{CM}}E_{N}\cos\theta\big)\\
\pm p^{\mathrm{CM}}\sin\theta\\
0\end{pmatrix}\,.

(140)

The loop-momentum is a general 4-vector that can be parameterized by the energy
\omega
, the length of the 3-momentum
k>0
, and two polar angles,
\theta^{\prime}\in[0,\pi]
and
\varphi^{\prime}\in[0,2\pi]
as

K=\begin{pmatrix}\omega\\
k\cos\theta^{\prime}\\
k\sin\theta^{\prime}\cos\varphi^{\prime}\\
k\sin\theta^{\prime}\sin\varphi^{\prime}\end{pmatrix}\,.

(141)

With this parametrization the loop integration measure is rewritten as

\int_{K}=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{4}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\!{\mathrm{d}}\omega\int_{0}^{\infty}\!{\mathrm{d}}k~k^{2}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\!{\mathrm{d}}\varphi^{\prime}\int_{-1}^{1}\!{\mathrm{d}}\cos\theta^{\prime}\,.

(142)

In general, the entire loop integral cannot be evaluated analytically for the thermal self-energy and vertex functions.
However, we shall see in the following subsections that the formulae for the CP-asymmetry factor can be reduced to numerically more easily manageable double- or triple-integrals.

G.1
Self-energy and the first cut of the vertex function

As the loop-integral in the self-energy contribution in eq. (
31
) is rather similar to the first cut of the vertex function in eq. (
67
), we shall consider them together in this subsection.

As a first step, we carry out the
k
and
\cos\theta^{\prime}
integrals with the help of the two Dirac-deltas corresponding to the two cut propagators of the lepton and scalar fields.
For some function
\mathcal{F}(K)
one has

\int_{0}^{\infty}{\mathrm{d}}k~\delta(K^{2}-\overline{m}_{L}^{2})\mathcal{F}(\omega,k,\cos\theta^{\prime},\varphi^{\prime})=\frac{\mathcal{F}\big(\omega,\sqrt{\omega^{2}-\overline{m}_{L}^{2}},\cos\theta^{\prime},\varphi^{\prime}\big)}{2\sqrt{\omega^{2}-\overline{m}_{L}^{2}}}\,,

(143)

where we used that
k>0
so only one physical root of the Dirac-delta survives integration.
Assuming that the
k
-integral had been carried out, the
\cos\theta^{\prime}
integral is rewritten as

\begin{gathered}\int_{-1}^{1}{\mathrm{d}}\cos\theta^{\prime}~\delta\big((P_{N}-K)^{2}-\overline{m}_{\phi}^{2}\big)=\int_{-1}^{1}{\mathrm{d}}\cos\theta^{\prime}~\frac{\delta\big(\cos\theta^{\prime}-f_{\theta^{\prime}}^{(1)}(E_{N},\omega)\big)}{2\sqrt{E_{N}^{2}-\overline{m}_{N_{i}}^{2}}\sqrt{\omega^{2}-\overline{m}_{L}^{2}}}\,,\\
f_{\theta^{\prime}}^{(1)}(E_{N},\omega)=\frac{\overline{m}_{\phi}^{2}-\overline{m}_{N_{i}}^{2}-\overline{m}_{L}^{2}+2E_{N}\omega}{2\sqrt{E_{N}^{2}-\overline{m}_{N_{i}}^{2}}\sqrt{\omega^{2}-\overline{m}_{L}^{2}}}\,,\end{gathered}

(144)

where we exploited our choice
{\bf p}_{N}\parallel\hat{x}
, implying
P_{N}\cdot K=E_{N}\omega-p_{N}\sqrt{\omega^{2}-\overline{m}_{L}^{2}}\cos\theta^{\prime}
and there is no
\varphi^{\prime}
-dependence at all.
Since
\cos\theta^{\prime}\in[-1,1]
the integration constrains the value of
\omega
to
\omega\in[\omega_{-}^{(1)},\omega_{+}^{(1)}]
, where using eq. (

\omega_{\pm}^{(1)}=\frac{E_{N}}{m_{N_{i}}}\left[E_{L}^{\mathrm{CM}}\pm\frac{p_{N}}{E_{N}}p^{\mathrm{CM}}\right]\,.

(145)

This is simply the Lorentz transform of the CM frame lepton energy, which is necessarily positive.
In total, we could reduce the 4-momentum integration to the following double integral:

\begin{split}\int_{K}\delta(K^{2}&-\overline{m}_{L}^{2})\delta\big((P_{N}-K)^{2}-\overline{m}_{\phi}^{2}\big)\mathcal{F}(\omega,k,\cos\theta^{\prime},\varphi^{\prime})=\\
&\frac{1}{64\pi^{4}\sqrt{E_{N}^{2}-\overline{m}_{N_{i}}^{2}}}\int_{\omega_{-}^{(1)}}^{\omega_{+}^{(1)}}{\mathrm{d}}\omega\int_{0}^{2\pi}{\mathrm{d}}\varphi^{\prime}\,\mathcal{F}\Big(\omega,\sqrt{\omega^{2}-\overline{m}_{L}^{2}},f_{\theta^{\prime}}^{(1)}(E_{N},\omega),\varphi^{\prime}\Big)\,.\end{split}

(146)

Having performed the
k
and
\cos\theta^{\prime}
integrals, we turn now to the remaining angular integral.

We begin with the self-energy contribution where the function
\mathcal{F}
in eq. (
146
) could be defined through comparison with eq. (

\mathcal{F}_{\Sigma}(K)={\rm sgn}(\omega){\rm sgn}(E_{N}-\omega)(K\cdot P_{L})\big(1+f_{\rm B}(E_{N}-\omega)-f_{\rm F}(\omega)\big)\,.

(147)

As
\omega_{\pm}^{(1)}>0
and kinematics require that
E_{N}>\omega
one has
\mathrm{sgn}(\omega)=\mathrm{sgn}(E_{N}-\omega)=1
.
The scalar product depends on
E_{N},\theta,\omega,
and
\varphi^{\prime}
and it can be expanded using eqs. (
140
)–(
141
) as

\begin{split}\big[K\cdot P_{L}\big](E_{N},\theta,&\,\omega,\varphi^{\prime})=\frac{\omega}{\overline{m}_{N_{i}}}\left(E_{N}E_{L}^{\mathrm{CM}}+p^{\mathrm{CM}}\sqrt{E_{N}^{2}-\overline{m}_{N_{i}}^{2}}\cos\theta\right)\\
&-\frac{\sqrt{\omega^{2}-\overline{m}_{L}^{2}}}{\overline{m}_{N_{i}}}f_{\theta^{\prime}}^{(1)}(E_{N},\omega)\left(E_{L}^{\mathrm{CM}}\sqrt{E_{N}^{2}-\overline{m}_{N_{i}}^{2}}+E_{N}p^{\mathrm{CM}}\cos\theta\right)\\

(148)

The integral of the last term over
\varphi^{\prime}
vanishes so

\int_{0}^{2\pi}{\mathrm{d}}\varphi^{\prime}\,\big[K\cdot P_{L}\big](E_{N},\theta,\omega,\varphi^{\prime})=2\pi\big[K\cdot P_{L}\big]\Big(E_{N},\theta,\,\omega,\frac{\pi}{2}\Big)\,.

(149)

The remaining
\omega

Using that
P_{N}\cdot P_{L}=\overline{m}_{N_{i}}E_{L}^{\mathrm{CM}}

\begin{split}\epsilon_{\Sigma_{i}}(E_{N},\theta)=\frac{G}{4\pi}&\sum_{j\neq i}\frac{\overline{m}_{N_{j}}}{\overline{m}_{N_{i}}^{2}-\overline{m}_{N_{j}}^{2}}\frac{1}{E_{L}^{\mathrm{CM}}\sqrt{E_{N}^{2}-\overline{m}_{N_{i}}^{2}}}\\
\times\int_{\omega_{-}^{(1)}}^{\omega_{+}^{(1)}}&{\mathrm{d}}\omega\,\big[K\cdot P_{L}\big]\Big(E_{N},\theta,\omega,\frac{\pi}{2}\Big)\big(1+f_{\rm B}(E_{N}-\omega)-f_{\rm F}(\omega)\big).\end{split}

(150)

To find the thermally averaged CP-asymmetry we integrate
\epsilon_{\Sigma_{i}}
over the phase space of the initial state with statistical weights as explained in section
2.2
, in particular in eq. (
16
).
We note that the CM decay angle integral for

is still analytic and the final result is a double numerical integral over the neutrino and loop energies
E_{N}
and
\omega
.
This double–integral is easily handled by standard numerical integrators.

For the first cut of the thermal vertex function given in eq. (
67
) one defines the

(151)

While the integrand is similar to that of the self-energy, here we also have non-trivial angular- and energy-dependence in the denominator through the

Using momentum conservation we find

\begin{split}\big[K\cdot P_{\phi}\big](&E_{N},\theta,\omega,\varphi^{\prime})=K\cdot(P_{N}-P_{L})\\
&=\omega E_{N}-f_{\theta^{\prime}}^{(1)}(E_{N},\omega)\sqrt{E_{N}^{2}-\overline{m}_{N_{i}}^{2}}\sqrt{\omega^{2}-\overline{m}_{L}^{2}}-\big[K\cdot P_{L}\big](E_{N},\theta,\omega,\varphi^{\prime})\end{split}

This scalar product is also linear in

thus in the integrand one has a fraction of two linear functions in

.
We define the result of the
\varphi^{\prime}
-integral as the function

\mathcal{I}_{\varphi^{\prime}}^{\sf(cut\,1)}(E_{N},\theta,\omega)=\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}\varphi^{\prime}}{2\pi}\frac{\big[K\cdot P_{L}\big](E_{N},\theta,\omega,\varphi^{\prime})}{\overline{m}_{L}^{2}+\overline{m}_{\phi}^{2}-\overline{m}_{N_{j}}^{2}-2\big[K\cdot P_{\phi}\big](E_{N},\theta,\omega,\varphi^{\prime})}\,.

(153)

Note that
\mathcal{I}^{\sf(cut\,1)}_{\varphi^{\prime}}(E_{N},\theta,\omega)
is analytic, see eq. (
177
), but it has a lengthy expression that we do not give here.

The amplitude-level CP-asymmetry factor at finite temperature due to the first cut of the vertex function is given as

\begin{split}\epsilon_{V_{i}}^{\sf(cut\,1)}(E_{N},\theta)=\frac{G}{8\pi}&\sum_{j\neq i}\frac{\overline{m}_{N_{j}}}{E_{L}^{\mathrm{CM}}\sqrt{E_{N}^{2}-\overline{m}_{N_{i}}^{2}}}\\
\times&\int_{\omega_{-}^{(1)}}^{\omega_{+}^{(1)}}{\mathrm{d}}\omega\,\mathcal{I}_{\varphi^{\prime}}^{\sf(cut\,1)}(E_{N},\theta,\omega)\big(1+f_{\rm B}(E_{N}-\omega)-f_{\rm F}(\omega)\big)\,.\end{split}

(154)

The integration over the initial state phase space required for the thermally averaged CP-violation rate has to be done numerically, meaning that for the vertex function the final formula is only analytically reducible to a triple integral.
Nevertheless, this triple integral can still be handled with standard numerical integrators.

G.2
Second cut of the vertex function

The formula for the imaginary part of the second cut of the vertex function was given in eq. (
68
).
As explained in appendix
F
, it is convenient for the calculation of the second cut to use a coordinate system where we take
E_{\phi}
as the independent external energy (instead of
E_{N}
), and take
{\bf p}_{\phi}\parallel\hat{x}
(instead of
{\bf p}_{N}\parallel\hat{x}
).
In this subsection we use this reference frame.

The lepton and the sterile neutrino propagators are on-shell within the loop, consequently the
k
-integral formally remains the same as in eq. (
143
) while the
\cos\theta^{\prime}
integral becomes:

\begin{gathered}\int_{-1}^{1}{\mathrm{d}}\cos\theta^{\prime}\,\delta\big((K-P_{\phi})^{2}-\overline{m}_{N_{j}}^{2}\big)=\int_{-1}^{1}{\mathrm{d}}\cos\theta^{\prime}\,\frac{\delta\big(\cos\theta^{\prime}-f_{\theta^{\prime}}^{(2)}(E_{\phi},\omega)\big)}{2\sqrt{E_{\phi}^{2}-\overline{m}_{\phi}^{2}}\sqrt{\omega^{2}-\overline{m}_{L}^{2}}}\,,\\
f_{\theta^{\prime}}^{(2)}(E_{\phi},\omega)=\frac{\overline{m}_{N_{j}}^{2}-\overline{m}_{L}^{2}-\overline{m}_{\phi}^{2}+2E_{\phi}\omega}{2\sqrt{E_{\phi}^{2}-\overline{m}_{\phi}^{2}}\sqrt{\omega^{2}-\overline{m}_{L}^{2}}}\,.\end{gathered}

(155)

This result is connected to the previous
f_{\theta^{\prime}}^{(1)}
by changing
\phi\to N_{j}
(cut propagator in the loop) and
N_{i}\to\phi
(external line opposite to the cut).
As before, the constraint of
\cos\theta^{\prime}\in[-1,1]
restricts the loop energy to
\omega\in[\omega_{-}^{(2)},\omega_{+}^{(2)}]
.
The limits are given by

\omega_{\pm}^{(2)}=\frac{E_{\phi}}{\overline{m}_{\phi}}\left[E^{(2)}\pm\frac{p_{\phi}}{E_{\phi}}\sqrt{\big[E^{(2)}\big]^{2}-\overline{m}_{L}^{2}}\right],\quad\text{where}\quad E^{(2)}=\frac{\overline{m}_{\phi}^{2}+\overline{m}_{L}^{2}-\overline{m}_{N_{j}}^{2}}{2\overline{m}_{\phi}}\,.

(156)

If
\overline{m}_{N_{j}}>\overline{m}_{\phi,L}
then the loop energy is strictly negative as
\omega_{\pm}^{(2)}<0
.
At zero temperature, where the cut propagators have fixed direction of energy flow, i.e., they are proportional to
\theta(\omega)
, the negativity of
\omega
makes this cut contribution vanish.
The evaluation of the two Dirac-delta integrals lead to

\begin{split}\int_{K}\delta(K^{2}&-\overline{m}_{L}^{2})\delta\big((K-P_{\phi})^{2}-\overline{m}_{N_{j}}^{2}\big)\mathcal{F}_{V}^{\sf(cut\,2)}(\omega,k,\cos\theta^{\prime},\varphi^{\prime})=\\
&\frac{1}{64\pi^{4}\sqrt{E_{\phi}^{2}-\overline{m}_{\phi}^{2}}}\int_{\omega_{-}^{(2)}}^{\omega_{+}^{(2)}}{\mathrm{d}}\omega\,\int_{0}^{2\pi}{\mathrm{d}}\varphi^{\prime}\,\mathcal{F}_{V}^{\sf(cut\,2)}\Big(\omega,\sqrt{\omega^{2}-\overline{m}_{L}^{2}},f_{\theta^{\prime}}^{(2)}(E_{\phi},\omega),\varphi^{\prime}\Big)\,,\end{split}

(157)

where for the second cut of the vertex function one has

\mathcal{F}_{V}^{\sf(cut\,2)}(K)=\mathrm{sgn}(\omega)\mathrm{sgn}(\omega-E_{\phi})\frac{K\cdot P_{L}}{(P_{N}-K)^{2}-\overline{m}_{\phi}^{2}}\big(f_{\rm F}(\omega)-f_{\rm F}(\omega-E_{\phi})\big)\,.

(158)

Due to
\omega<0
and
E_{\phi}>0
we have
\mathrm{sgn}(\omega)\mathrm{sgn}(\omega-E_{\phi})=1
.
With positive arguments the statistical factors become
f_{\rm F}(\omega)-f_{\rm F}(\omega-E_{\phi})=f_{\rm F}(E_{\phi}-\omega)-f_{\rm F}(-\omega)
.
Contrary to the contributions due to the self-energy and the first cut of the vertex function, where the statistical factor term in the limit
T\to 0
was
1+f_{\rm B}(E_{N}-\omega)-f_{\rm F}(\omega)\to 1
, in the case of the second cut the same limit of the appearing statistical factors vanishes,
f_{\rm F}(E_{\phi}-\omega)-f_{\rm F}(-\omega)\to 0
.

In appendix
F
we show the inversion of the energy relation
E_{\phi}(E_{N},\cos\theta)
in order to express
E_{N}(E_{\phi},\cos\theta)
.
The inverse relations are not one-to-one in the full integration domain, however, one can define overlapping regions where one-to-one relations may be defined separately, see figure
8
.
The physical energy expressions for the incoming sterile neutrino is denoted as
E_{N}^{\pm}(E_{\phi},\theta)
, see eq. (
130
).
Depending on which region we are in, the inverse relations use either
E_{N}^{\pm}
as defined in eqs. (
132
)–(
133
).
Following these expressions, we define the
\varphi^{\prime}
angular integral as

\mathcal{I}_{\varphi^{\prime}}^{{\sf(cut\,2)}\pm}(E_{\phi},\theta,\omega)=\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}\varphi^{\prime}}{2\pi}\frac{[K\cdot P_{L}^{\pm}](E_{\phi},\theta,\omega,\varphi^{\prime})}{\overline{m}_{N_{i}}^{2}-\overline{m}_{L}^{2}-\overline{m}_{\phi}^{2}-2\big[K\cdot P^{\pm}_{N}\big](E_{\phi},\theta,\omega,\varphi^{\prime})}\,.

(159)

The scalar products necessarily depend on the inverted energy relations, as indicated with the
\pm
superscripts.
One then has

\begin{split}\big[K\cdot P_{L}^{\pm}\big]&(E_{\phi},\theta,\omega,\varphi^{\prime})\equiv K\cdot(P_{N}^{\pm}-P_{\phi})\\
&=\big[K\cdot P_{N}^{\pm}\big](E_{\phi},\theta,\omega,\varphi^{\prime})-\omega E_{\phi}+f_{\theta^{\prime}}^{(2)}(E_{\phi},\omega)\sqrt{\omega^{2}-\overline{m}_{L}^{2}}\sqrt{E_{\phi}^{2}-\overline{m}_{\phi}^{2}}\,.\end{split}

(160)

Since the spatial coordinate system is rotated with angle
\Delta_{\phi}
such that
{\bf p}_{\phi}\parallel\hat{x}
, see eq. (
136
), thus
{\bf p}_{N}
is also transformed resulting in

\begin{split}\big[K\cdot&P_{N}^{\pm}\big](E_{\phi},\theta,\omega,\varphi^{\prime})=\omega E_{N}^{\pm}(E_{\phi},\theta)\\
&-\sqrt{\omega^{2}-\overline{m}_{L}^{2}}f_{\theta^{\prime}}^{(2)}(E_{\phi},\omega)\cos\Delta_{\phi}^{\pm}(E_{\phi},\theta)\sqrt{\big[E_{N}^{\pm}(E_{\phi},\theta)\big]^{2}-\overline{m}_{N_{i}}^{2}}\\
&-\sqrt{\omega^{2}-\overline{m}_{L}^{2}}\sqrt{1-\big[f_{\theta^{\prime}}^{(2)}(E_{\phi},\omega)\big]^{2}}\sin\Delta_{\phi}(E_{\phi},\theta)\sqrt{\big[E_{N}^{\pm}(E_{\phi},\theta)\big]^{2}-\overline{m}_{N_{i}}^{2}}\cos\varphi^{\prime}\,.\end{split}

(161)

Here the appearing rotation angles in the various domains mentioned above are indicated with the
\pm
sign and they are defined in eqs. (
137
) and (
139
).
The
\varphi^{\prime}
-integral in eq. (
159
) is again analytic, see eq. (
177
).

In summary, for the second cut of the vertex function we define the amplitude-level CP-asymmetry factors as

\begin{split}\epsilon_{V_{i}}^{{\sf(cut\,2)}\pm}(E_{\phi},\theta)=\frac{G}{8\pi}&\sum_{j\neq i}\frac{\overline{m}_{N_{j}}}{E_{L}^{\mathrm{CM}}\sqrt{E_{\phi}^{2}-\overline{m}_{\phi}^{2}}}\\
\times&\int_{\omega_{-}^{(2)}}^{\omega_{+}^{(2)}}{\mathrm{d}}\omega\,\mathcal{I}_{\varphi^{\prime}}^{{\sf(cut\,2)}\pm}(E_{\phi},\theta,\omega)\big(f_{\rm F}(E_{\phi}-\omega)-f_{\rm F}(-\omega)\big)\,.\end{split}

(162)

Thermal averaging over the initial state phase space has to be done according to eq. (
134
), i.e., we have to pay attention to the energy and decay angle regions due to the loss of the one-to-one relation between the energies
E_{N}(E_{\phi},\cos\theta)\leftrightarrow E_{\phi}(E_{N},\cos\theta)
.

G.3
Third cut of the vertex function

The third, and final cut of the thermal vertex function originally given in eq. (
69
) is evaluated similarly to the second cut presented in the previous subsection.
In fact, the correspondence between the two calculations can be made even more pronounced if one performs a momentum shift as
K\to P_{N}-K
.
This shift results in the scalar propagator in the loop having momentum
K
(instead of the lepton as before) and in eq. (
69
) we find

\begin{split}\mathrm{sgn}(\omega-E_{N})\mathrm{sgn}(\omega&-E_{\phi})\delta\big((K-P_{N})^{2}-\overline{m}_{\phi}^{2}\big)\delta\big((K-P_{\phi})^{2}-\overline{m}_{N_{j}}^{2}\big)\\
&\to\mathrm{sgn}(-\omega)\mathrm{sgn}(E_{L}-\omega)\delta(K^{2}-\overline{m}_{\phi}^{2})\delta\big((P_{L}-K)^{2}-\overline{m}_{N_{j}}^{2}\big)\,.\end{split}

(163)

As before, the two
\delta
distributions are evaluated using the integrals for
k
and
\cos\theta^{\prime}
.
The first one is given as in eq. (
143
) but with changing
\overline{m}_{L}^{2}\to\overline{m}_{\phi}^{2}
.
The second one is performed as in eq. (
155
) but with interchanging
\overline{m}_{L}^{2}\leftrightarrow\overline{m}_{\phi}^{2}
and the exchange
E_{\phi}\to E_{L}
.
The function appearing next to the
\cos\theta^{\prime}
in the Dirac-delta is then

f_{\theta^{\prime}}^{(3)}(E_{L},\omega)=\frac{\overline{m}_{N_{j}}^{2}-\overline{m}_{\phi}^{2}-\overline{m}_{L}^{2}+2\omega E_{L}}{2\sqrt{E_{L}^{2}-\overline{m}_{L}^{2}}\sqrt{\omega^{2}-\overline{m}_{\phi}^{2}}}\,.

(164)

The limits for the loop energy
\omega
resulting from the
\cos\theta^{\prime}
integral follows from eq. (
156
) via similar replacements of
L\leftrightarrow\phi
as before and we find

\omega_{\pm}^{(3)}=\frac{E_{L}}{\overline{m}_{L}}\left[E^{(3)}\pm\frac{p_{L}}{E_{L}}\sqrt{\big[E^{(3)}\big]^{2}-\overline{m}_{\phi}^{2}}\right]\,,\quad\text{where}\quad E^{(3)}=\frac{\overline{m}_{L}^{2}+\overline{m}_{\phi}^{2}-\overline{m}_{N_{j}}^{2}}{2\overline{m}_{L}}\,.

(165)

As for the second cut, here one also finds that if
\overline{m}_{N_{j}}>\overline{m}_{L,\phi}
then necessarily
\omega_{\pm}^{(3)}<0
and the loop-energy is always negative.
In conclusion the phase space integral for the loop-momentum over the two Dirac-deltas can be summarized as

\begin{split}\int_{K}&\delta(K^{2}-\overline{m}_{\phi}^{2})\delta\big((P_{L}-K)^{2}-\overline{m}_{N_{j}}^{2}\big)\mathcal{F}_{V}^{\sf(cut\,3)}(\omega,k,\cos\theta^{\prime},\varphi^{\prime})\\
&=\frac{1}{64\pi^{4}\sqrt{E_{L}^{2}-\overline{m}_{L}^{2}}}\int_{\omega_{-}^{(3)}}^{\omega_{+}^{(3)}}{\mathrm{d}}\omega\int_{0}^{2\pi}{\mathrm{d}}\varphi^{\prime}\mathcal{F}_{V}^{\sf(cut\,3)}\Big(\omega,\sqrt{\omega^{2}-\overline{m}_{L}^{2}},f_{\theta^{\prime}}^{(3)}(E_{L},\omega),\varphi^{\prime}\Big)\,,\end{split}

(166)

where for the third cut of the vertex function the integrand is given by

\mathcal{F}_{V}^{\sf(cut\,3)}(K)=\mathrm{sgn}(-\omega)\mathrm{sgn}(E_{L}-\omega)\frac{(P_{N}-K)\cdot P_{L}}{(P_{N}-K)^{2}-\overline{m}_{L}^{2}}\big(f_{\rm B}(-\omega)+f_{\rm F}(E_{L}-\omega)\big)\,.

(167)

Since
\omega<0
, the sign functions evaluate to
+1
, and the statistical factors already have positive arguments.
Note that due to the shift we performed in the loop momentum, the scalar product in the numerator originating from the Dirac-trace
\mathcal{T}(K)
(see, eq. (
21
)) is also modified.

In the reference frame convenient for the third cut of the vertex function we have
{\bf p}_{L}\parallel\hat{x}
(see, appendix.
F
) so the scalar product in the numerator of
\mathcal{F}_{V}^{(3)}
is independent of
\varphi^{\prime}
:

\big[(P_{N}-K)\cdot P_{L}\big](E_{L},\omega)=\overline{m}_{N_{i}}E_{L}^{\mathrm{CM}}-E_{L}\omega+f_{\theta^{\prime}}^{(3)}(E_{L},\omega)\sqrt{E_{L}^{2}-\overline{m}_{L}^{2}}\sqrt{\omega^{2}-\overline{m}_{\phi}^{2}}\,.

(168)

In contrast, the scalar product
K\cdot P_{N}^{\pm}
appearing in the denominator of eq. (
167
) is non-trivial in
\varphi^{\prime}
, and it can be found from eq. (
161
) with the replacements
E_{\phi}\to E_{L}
,
\overline{m}_{L}^{2}\to\overline{m}_{\phi}^{2}
,
\Delta_{\phi}\to\Delta_{L}
, and
f_{\theta}^{(2)}\to f_{\theta}^{(3)}
.
As the
\varphi^{\prime}
-dependence only appears in the denominator of
\mathcal{F}_{V}^{(3)}
we define the
\varphi^{\prime}
-integral as

\mathcal{I}_{\varphi^{\prime}}^{{\sf(cut\,3)}\pm}(E_{L},\theta,\omega)=\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}\varphi^{\prime}}{2\pi}\,\frac{1}{\overline{m}_{N_{i}}^{2}+\overline{m}_{\phi}^{2}-\overline{m}_{L}^{2}-2\big[K\cdot P_{N}^{\pm}\big](E_{L},\theta,\omega,\varphi^{\prime})}\,.

(169)

As before, the result of this integral is analytic and finite for physical kinematics, see eq. (
177
).

In summary, the contribution of the third cut of the thermal vertex function to the amplitude-level CP-asymmetry factor is:

\begin{split}\epsilon_{V_{i}}^{{\sf(cut\,3)}\pm}&(E_{L},\theta)=\frac{G}{8\pi}\sum_{j\neq i}\frac{\overline{m}_{N_{j}}}{E_{L}^{\mathrm{CM}}\sqrt{E_{L}^{2}-\overline{m}_{L}^{2}}}\\
\times\int_{\omega_{-}^{(3)}}^{\omega_{+}^{(3)}}&{\mathrm{d}}\omega\,\big[(P_{N}-K)\cdot P_{L}\big](E_{L},\omega)\mathcal{I}_{\varphi^{\prime}}^{{\sf(cut\,3)}\pm}(E_{L},\theta,\omega)\big(f_{\rm B}(-\omega)+f_{\rm F}(E_{L}-\omega)\big)\,.\end{split}

(170)

The integration over the initial state phase space is done as explained in appendix
F
and in particular given in eq. (
135
).

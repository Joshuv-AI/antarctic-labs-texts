Chaotic control of a simply supported beam in a multidimensional system
Authors: Ming Liu, Haoran Xun, Liping Wu
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
In this work, a control strategy based on fuzzy sliding mode control (FSMC) is applied to effectively manage the large-amplitude chaotic vibrations exhibited by a simply supported beam. The analysis considers the nonlinear beam structure, which is subjected to an external load. Hamilton’s principle is employed, and the equations of motion are derived for the studied structure. The 3rd Galerkin discretization is employed to derive the ordinary differential governing equation of the structure. A control strategy is presented to decrease the response of the obtained multidimensional system. The vibration of a one-dimensional system is compared with that of the derived multidimensional system. As shown throughout the study, a multidimensional nonlinear system of the structure must be considered for accurate dynamic estimation. By using the recurrent neural network (RNN) model, we can accurately predict chaotic motion and effectively apply control strategies to suppress chaotic motion. The efficacy and suitability of the employed control strategy have been demonstrated by controlling chaos in the beam’s multidimensional system.

Introduction

Extensive research has been conducted in the past few decades to examine the nonlinear motion of beams, as reported in the literature1–3. Significant advances have been achieved in the study of the dynamic response of beams. In particular, classical methods, such as the Fourier series method and the complex mode method, have been developed for linear systems. These methods have gained widespread application in the field4. Simultaneously, numerous researchers have investigated the nonlinear structural vibration of beams under various situations. Wang and Zhu5 examined the dynamic characteristics of transverse vibration in a simply supported buckled beam. Their findings indicate that the nonlinear dynamic behaviour of the investigated superelastic beam, including chaotic motion, may vary as a result of variations in the amplitude of the external harmonic axial load. To investigate the nonlinear vibration characteristics of shear-deformed beams, such as chaotic vibration, Wang6 derived a theoretical equation for a structure by incorporating the nonlinear strain–displacement relationship of the beam. The nonlinear vibration of a simply supported beam with cubic nonlinearity under harmonic excitation was investigated by Hasan and Rahman7 using the multilevel residue harmonic balance approach. The examination of the chaotic response revealed a strong agreement between the results obtained with the multilevel residue harmonic balance approach and the results obtained via the numerical integration method. Raj et al.8 examined the nonlinear plane vibration of viscoelastic beams under simply supported boundary conditions. They conducted a numerical analysis to investigate the stability and bifurcation of the whole system. Furthermore, they examined the dynamic response of the system including diverse dynamic phenomena, such as periodic, quasiperiodic, mixed mode, and chaotic behaviours. Ma and Mu9 investigated the nonlinear vibration of simply supported infusion microtubules under transverse excitation loading and discretized the sixth-order nonlinear partial differential equation to the second order via the Galerkin method. The impact of the excitation load amplitude on the dynamic properties of microtubules was observed, leading to a transition in microtubule motion from quasiperiodic to chaotic with increasing dimensionless microscale parameters. In academic research, Ebrahimi10 studied the phenomenon of chaotic vibration in simply supported carbon nanotubes subjected to harmonic stress. The findings indicated that the bifurcation behaviour can be significantly influenced by nonlocal characteristics, specifically the velocity and amplitude of the ergo-harmonic force. These parameters can be effectively utilized as control parameters to prevent the occurrence of chaotic dynamics. Zhang and Li11 used the Melnikov technique to investigate the chaotic dynamics of a simply supported buckling beam model and determine the key curve for distinguishing chaotic and nonchaotic areas. It was found that a finite-order subharmonic bifurcation may cause chaotic motion. Alireza12 used a thorough analytical technique to investigate the dynamic stability and chaotic behaviour of a massive deformation shape memory alloy beam under simultaneous activation of external and parametric harmonics. In this method, the border curve of the chaotic region is determined by inserting dimensionless parameters. Chen and Wang et al.13 investigated the nonlinear free vibration of a simply supported hyperelastic beam under high-strain deformation conditions and used the Galerkin technique to determine the optimum number of truncation modes. They found that when the initial displacement amplitude is large enough, the free vibration of a hyperelastic beam becomes quasiperiodic or chaotic. Zhu et al.14 investigated and compared the nonlinear primary resonance responses of two-dimensional and three-dimensional models of a basic supported pipe conveying fluid, with all numerical computations taken into consideration and a six-mode Galerkin truncation. In the three-dimensional model investigation, a novel torus bifurcation showing complicated quasiperiodic or chaotic motion was observed for the first time. Zhou and Chen15 investigated the nonlinear dynamics of a simply supported slanted beam driven by temperature field harmonics, as well as the chaotic properties of the inclined angle. It was found that the system may create chaos via finite-order harmonic bifurcation, and the chaos threshold calculated analytically was validated via numerical simulations. Ali and Hawwa16 used the Galerkin technique for four-term expansion to study the nonlinear responses of a simply supported small-size beam in high-speed moving structures and discovered that the lateral response is a periodic stable oscillation under the operation of low-amplitude forces. At greater dimensions, as the amplitude of the excitation force increases, the response transitions from chaos to period doubling.

With advances in computer technology, the Galerkin technique, finite element method, multiple scales approach, and other numerical methods have steadily evolved into key methods for solving the dynamic response of beam structures17–19. Research on the dynamic response of beams has emphasized the development of control schemes for the mitigation of the nonlinear responses of beams. The concept of sliding mode control theory was introduced in 1992. It has been recognized for its effectiveness in managing multidimensional systems that function in unpredictable settings20. A novel control technique known as fuzzy sliding mode control (FSMC) was subsequently introduced as an extension of the conventional sliding mode control (SMC) approach. To demonstrate the efficacy of this control design in mitigating the nonlinear behaviour of a system, a nonlinear system represented by a beam model was employed21–23. To address the result constraint issue, Ding et al.24 introduced a unique second-order sliding mode control technique and demonstrated its viability for an example system. Li et al.25 proposed a new control method that uses an active mass damper designed via adaptive control to reduce the vibration of nonlinear structures and combines a modified unscented Kalman filter (UKF) method with model reference sliding model control (MRSMC) to form an integrated MRSMC-UKF. In their study, Mousa et al.26 employed adaptive fuzzy sliding mode control (AFSMC) to investigate the robust stabilization of multiple-input-multiple-output nonlinear aeroelastic chatter in functionally graded piezoelectric plates. They focused on the issue of mismatched time-varying uncertainties and successfully resolved the limitations associated with conventional sliding mode control systems in mitigating the inherent chattering phenomenon. Zheng et al.27 developed a self-organizing fuzzy sliding mode control approach to attenuate the multimode vibration of photoinduced thin cylindrical shells given the nonlinear and time-varying characteristics of photorefractive actuators. Yousefpour et al.28 used disturbance observer-based terminal sliding mode control to advance the system from a low-energy periodic or chaotic attractor to a high-energy periodic attractor and verified the effectiveness of the method through numerical methods and simulation experiments. Al-Shuka29 improved proxy-based sliding mode control (PSMC) by incorporating an adaptive approximation compensator (AAC) for the vibration control of Euler–Bernoulli beams and verified the performance and stability of the control structure using Lyapunov theory. The correctness and precise regulation of the PSMC were verified by simulation experiments. To address the issue of jitter in the construction of the fault-tolerant control law of traditional sliding mode controllers, Pan et al.30 presented a controller design approach that relies on fractional-order sliding mode theory. Comparative tests revealed that while monitoring reference points, the multijoint robot driven by the fractional-order sliding mode is more stable and jitter-free. To manage the rate of wheel sliding, Latreche and Benaggoune31 suggested a robust controller based on the fuzzy sliding mode technique. Fuzzy logic and sliding mode were used in the control method to guarantee closed-loop system stability and eliminate the chattering issue created by traditional sliding mode control. A fractional-order sliding mode control based on direct torque control was presented by Li et al.32 to lessen the subsynchronous control interaction of a wind farm with doubly fed induction generators. According to the findings of their simulations, this control strategy may be beneficial for accelerating the damping process and achieving finite time control in addition to reducing the buffeting issue. To achieve trajectory tracking of an autonomous differential-driven mobile robot in the presence of friction torque, Wu and Zaman33 used fractional sliding mode control. They then used simulations and experiments to confirm its feasibility and robustness.

The prediction of chaos is an important research topic that is one of the current challenges in beam vibration control research. Ahuja and Saini34 established a hybrid neural network model of a convolutional neural network (CNN) and a recurrent neural network (RNN) to enhance the short-term prediction of chaotic wind speed time series. Xie and Ren35 proposed and proved that the nonlinear predictive controller for precise trajectory tracking of piezo actuators (PEAs), which was further developed by using an RNN to model the PEA, can nonlinearly predict the displacement and realize high-precision real-time trajectory tracking of the PEA. Nguyen et al.36 used RNNS to accurately predict the long-term deformation of viscoelastic composite laminate Mindlin plates under arbitrary mechanical and thermally wet loads.

However, the effects of parametric errors are non-negligible and should not be disregarded. Avanço et al.37 analysed the feedback effect of a pendulum on the crank speed and the complexity of the crank slider mechanism without approaching harmonic motion. It was found that chaos will occur when the parameters change slightly. Peruzzi et al.38 compared the effectiveness of the state feedback control technique based on the Lyapunov-Floquet transform with that of optimal linear feedback control (OLFC) in the presence of parametric errors in the control parameters. The outcomes demonstrated that the OLFC control was more robust when parametric errors were considered. Nozaki et al.39 investigated the performance of optimal linear feedback control (OLFC) and the state-dependent Riccati equation control technique (SDRE) for controlling and suppressing the chaotic motion of an atomic force microscope (AFM). The two control methods were compared in the presence of parametric errors, and the results indicated that SDRE control was more robust. Tusset et al.40 demonstrated the importance of controller design considering parametric errors and fractional derivative orders and its influence on material memory. Moreover, SDRE control is more robust than Lyapunov-Floquet transform and OLFC control when the control parameters are affected by parametric errors.

It is important to note that in the existing literature, the system under study is often described by the governing equation generated via the first-order Galerkin method. Hence, it is essential to implement a control technique to effectively manage the chaotic responses of beam structures in a multidimensional system.

The objective of this study is to derive the equations of vibration for a simply supported beam using Hamilton’s principle. The derivation of a multidimensional system for simulating beam vibration is conducted after nondimensionalization and discretization. On the basis of the existing FSMC, a control strategy that corresponds to the beam’s governing equations is formulated. The implementation of the active control technique includes the use of a single controller to regulate the chaotic responses of the structure at a specific location. The study emphasizes the evaluation of the efficacy of the control technique.

Conclusion

In this article, a control method is proposed to actively manage the nonlinear vibrations of a simply supported beam. In this study, we propose the development of a mathematical model for describing the nonlinear vibrations of a beam using the higher-order Galerkin method. The development of a control strategy for managing chaotic vibrations in a multidimensional dynamic system is highly important. The effectiveness of the developed control strategy is proven by introducing parameter errors. It is important to note that no control method that can effectively address this issue has been reported in the literature. Based on the results presented in the article, the following conclusions can be drawn:

(1) To conduct a more precise and comprehensive analysis of the vibrations experienced by a simply supported beam under sinusoidal external stimulation, it is crucial to consider the substantial impact of higher-order vibrations, particularly when accounting for chaotic vibrations. As illustrated in the current study, it is important to consider the impacts of higher-order vibrations in the regulation of the chaotic vibrations of a nonlinear simply supported beam.

(2) This article presents a control strategy that is demonstrated to be effective for both amplitude synchronization and frequency synchronization in the case of chaotic vibration control of the studied beam structure in a multidimensional system.

(3) In addition to the effectiveness of the control strategy, the efficiency of the strategy is also demonstrated by decreasing the values of the corresponding control coefficients, even though such a decrease may lead to a slightly greater discrepancy between \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$w_{p}$$\end{document}wp and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$w_{r}$$\end{document}wr.

(4) By using the RNN model, we can accurately predict and effectively apply control strategies to suppress chaotic motion; this provides a solid foundation for more accurate prediction and control of the chaotic vibration of simple supported beams in multidimensional systems in the future.

Future development

Based on the control strategy proposed in this study, it is necessary to analyse the state space of the nonlinear autonomous system corresponding to the formula by checking the arrangement and type of system equilibrium point. In future control applications, more attention should be devoted to the examination of the types of chaotic response, as well as the identification of transient response in Poincaré map. In addition, in future studies, comparisons with recent reports on the same model should be performed, in order to apply the chaotic control of a simply supported beam in multidimensional system in the relevant studies including nonlinear forced vibration of multi-cracked simply-supported beams3 and vibration isolation of simply-supported beams17.

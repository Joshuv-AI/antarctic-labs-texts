ContextPRM: Leveraging Contextual Coherence for multi-domain Test-Time Scaling
Haotian Zhang, Liu Liu, Baosheng Yu, Jiayan Qiu, Likang Xiao, Yanwei Ren, Quan Chen, Xianglong Liu, [0.5em] School of Artificial Intelligence, Beihang University

Abstract
Process reward models (PRMs) have demonstrated significant efficacy in enhancing the mathematical reasoning capabilities of large language models (LLMs) by leveraging test-time scaling (TTS). However, while most PRMs exhibit substantial gains in mathematical domains, the scarcity of domain-specific training data and knowledge-based learning patterns limits their generalization ability when faced with other domains. To address this limitation, we shift the learning objective from verifying domain-specific knowledge to modeling domain-agnostic logical flow. Centering on contextual coherence between chain-of-thought (CoT) steps, our approach is realized through a novel data annotation and training framework, which enhances the model’s generalization capabilities across diverse domains. For instance, our resulting model, ContextPRM , achieves a notable 6.5% average accuracy improvement over the majority voting baseline via weighted majority voting across nine non-mathematical domains in MMLU-Pro, including law, history, and philosophy, significantly surpassing the 2.2% improvement from VersaPRM and 0.5% gains from other mathematics-focused PRMs, demonstrating consistent performance across both mathematical and non-mathematical domains.

We present extensive empirical evidence showing that our context-centered approach substantially improves the cross-domain generalizability of PRMs, achieving notable gains in domains beyond mathematics and demonstrating its versatility and robustness.

2.2
Training Method for PRMs

As step verifiers for inference LLMs, PRMs are required to assign stepwise reward scores to each reasoning step within a CoT. Conventional training approaches formulate this as a binary classification task, where the PRMs learns to predict correctness labels
\{0,1\}
for individual steps. Recent studies have attempted to enhance PRM’s performance with various training method improvements.
(
zhang2024generative
;
zhao2025genprm
;
she2025rprmreasoningdrivenprocessreward
;
khalifa2025
)
first trained the model to generate intermediate reasoning before producing digit rewards, trading increased test time cost for improved accuracy.
(
pala2025errortypingsmarterrewards
)
adds additional labels to train PRMs to detect error patterns before reward assignment. However, these methods remain largely constrained to knowledge-intensive mathematical domains. In contrast, our approach introduces a context-aware training methodology tailored for multi-domain data, which explicitly teaches the PRM to evaluate the logical transitions between consecutive reasoning steps.

2.3
Test-Time Scaling

Reranking-based inference methods, commonly used in test-time scaling for chain-of-thought (CoT) reasoning, select the final answer from a set of sampled CoT solutions using aggregation strategies.
Let
a_{C}
denote the answer extracted from a CoT solution
C
, and
\mathcal{C}_{N}=\{C_{1},C_{2},\ldots,C_{N}\}
be
N
CoTs sampled independently from a generator for a given question.
Aggregation functions—such as min-, mean-, and max-aggregation—combine the stepwise scores assigned by the PRM into a single chain-of-thought (CoT) score, which serves as the foundation for downstream reranking
(
lightman2023let
;
pei2024shepherd
;
sun2024easy
)
. Building on this, Majority Voting (MV)
(
wang2023selfconsistency
)
selects the most frequently occurring answer across sampled CoTs, while Best-of-N (BoN) identifies the answer derived from the CoT with the highest aggregated score. Weighted Majority Voting (WMV)
(
uesato2022solving
)
further refines MV by weighting each answer’s frequency according to its CoT score, thereby integrating both occurrence and quality signals.
Collectively, these methods enhance inference robustness and reliability by exploiting multiple reasoning traces during test-time scaling, ultimately leading to more consistent and higher-quality outputs. This limitation motivates our development of a context-aware approach that explicitly evaluates the logical transitions within CoTs, thereby providing a richer signal for both training and inference.

3
Method

In this section, we describe the methodology for our context-aware process reward model (ContextPRM). We begin by highlighting the key differences between our training paradigm and conventional PRMs, focusing on how our approach models reasoning coherence. We then present the context-coherence-based CoT annotation pipeline, which captures contextual dependencies within reasoning steps, and detail how ContextPRM is applied during test-time computation.

3.1
Context-Aware PRMs

Our context-aware PRMs extend conventional PRMs by explicitly considering the relationships between consecutive reasoning steps. While traditional PRMs evaluate the correctness of each step in isolation, context-aware PRMs assess reasoning coherence across the contextual reasoning steps, enabling more accurate and nuanced reward assignment. In the following, we first review conventional PRMs to establish the baseline, and then introduce our context-aware training approach, which leverages contextual dependencies to enhance multi-step reasoning evaluation.

3.1.1
Conventional PRMs

Recent works on PRMs learn to assign a scalar reward to each step of a reasoning process. The model is trained as a binary classifier to distinguish between correct and incorrect reasoning steps based on the preceding context
(
pei2024shepherd
;
zhang2025lessons
)
.
Formally, for a given question
Q
and a
k
-step Chain-of-Thought (CoT) denoted as
C=\{S_{1},S_{2},\dots,S_{k}\}
, the training data is structured as a sequence of cumulative prefixes. For each step
S_{i}
, the model
\mathcal{M}
receives the concatenated input text
T_{i}=Q\oplus S_{1}\oplus\dots\oplus S_{i}
, where
\oplus
denotes concatenation.
Each step
S_{i}
is labeled with a binary value
l_{i}\in\{0,1\}
, where 1 denotes correct steps and 0 denotes incorrect ones.

This classification is typically achieved by computing a cross-entropy loss over the logits of predefined positive (‘+’) and negative (‘-’) tokens. The loss is computed only at the position of the final token of step
S_{i}
, which we denote by
p_{i}
. Let the model with parameters
\theta
output logits
\mathbf{o}_{i}=\mathcal{M}(T_{i};\theta)
. The loss function for the entire CoT is defined as:

\mathcal{L}_{\text{PRM}}(\theta)=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\text{CrossEntropy}(\mathbf{o}_{i,p_{i}}^{\{t_{-},t_{+}\}},l_{i}),

(1)

where
\mathbf{o}_{i,p_{i}}^{\{t_{-},t_{+}\}}
represents the logits for the negative token
t_{-}
and positive token
t_{+}
at position
p_{i}
.

However, under this training paradigm, as the context length grows with each step, the model struggles to diagnose the true root cause of current step failures. Furthermore, as the context length grows with each step, it becomes increasingly difficult for the model to capture the contextual logic that is most relevant to the correctness of the current step
S_{i}
, often failing to distinguish between isolated factual errors and deeper contextual fallacies.

3.1.2
Context-Aware Training

To enhance the utilization of information within training samples and to reinforce the model’s understanding of coherence and logical consistency in CoTs, we propose the Context-Aware Training Method for PRM. This approach optimizes the model’s context-awareness by constructing a composite contextualized representation for each reasoning step.
Specifically, we transform each step
S_{i}
into a contextualized representation
P_{i}
achieved by a formatting function
\mathcal{F}
.

Formally, for each step
S_{i}
in the CoT
C
, we define its new context
\tilde{\mathcal{S}}=\{\tilde{S}_{1},\dots,\tilde{S}_{k}\}
, where

\tilde{S}_{i}=Q
if
i=1
, and
\tilde{S}_{i}=S_{i-1}
if
i>1
.
Then, a concat function is applied for each step and its context, which takes the context and the current step as input and structures them into a new sequence by inserting special tokens that explicitly mark the beginning of the context and the step. The resulting contextualized step is denoted as:

P_{i}=\mathcal{F}(\tilde{S}_{i},S_{i}).

This formatting explicitly provides the model with the preceding step as direct context.

This method constructs
k
distinct and independent training samples from a single
k
-step CoT. For each step
i\in[k]
, the input to the model,
\tilde{T}_{i}
, is formed by directly concatenating the question
Q
with the contextualized representation of the current step:

\tilde{T}_{i}=Q\oplus P_{i}.

The supervision signal is derived from a parallel set of coherence labels,
c_{i}\in\{0,1\}
, where
1
denotes a coherent transition. The loss is computed at the final token position of the contextualized step
\tilde{S}_{i}
, denoted by
\tilde{p}_{i}
. The ContextPRM loss is then calculated by summing the losses for each of these
k
samples:

\mathcal{L}_{\text{ContextPRM}}(\theta)=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\text{CrossEntropy}(\tilde{\mathbf{o}}_{i,\tilde{p}_{i}}^{\{t_{-},t_{+}\}},c_{i}),

(2)

where the logits
\tilde{\mathbf{o}}_{i}=\mathcal{M}_{\text{ContextPRM}}(\tilde{T}_{i};\theta)
are produced from the corresponding contextualized input
\tilde{T}_{i}
. This method compels the model to focus not on the isolated correctness of a step, but on the logical validity of the transition between steps.

A critical component of our methodology is the tight coupling of our training method with its corresponding supervision signal. As our approach aims to shift the learning objective towards contextual coherence, reusing traditional annotation methods and correctness labels (
l_{i}
) would create a fundamental mismatch between the training objective and the annotation logic. Therefore, to ensure that the training and data labels are properly aligned, we introduce a new annotation standard (detailed in Section
3.2
) that is specifically designed to match our context-aware training paradigm, thereby maximizing the effectiveness of our method.

3.2
Context-Coherence-Based CoT Annotation

During the data annotation phase, we proposed an annotation standard to accurately assess each context pair of a CoT. This standard is designed not only to identify factually incorrect steps but also to discern those that are correct in isolation yet are logically flawed or unfounded within the broader context of the reasoning chain. Inspired by the 3-level annotation method in previous works
(
lightman2023let
;
zeng2025versaprm
)
, we categorize each step in a context pair based on the following criteria:

Context-Coherence Based CoT Annotation Criteria

•

Good:
The step is correct, verifiable, contextually appropriate, and contributes insightfully to the solution path.

•

Okay:
The step is correct and verifiable but is either redundant or makes only minimal progress toward the solution.

•

Bad:
The step is categorized as bad if it exhibits one or more of the following flaws.

–

Incorrect:
The step contains a factual or calculation error.

–

Misinterpretation:
The step is based on a misunderstanding of the problem’s premise or the goal.

–

Logical Fallacy:
The step contains a structural flaw in its reasoning, such as a non-sequitur or contradiction.

–

Misdirection:
The step introduces information or a line of reasoning that is irrelevant to the solution path.

We apply this labeling standard to each context pair within a CoT sequentially until the first ‘Bad’ step is identified. All subsequent context pairs following this initial error are automatically labeled as incorrect. To quantify the impact of our refined annotation standard, we conducted a comparative analysis against the original labels using
gpt-4o-mini-2024-07-18
, focusing on the identification of the first incorrect step.

Model Training:

5
Conclusion

In this work, we addressed the critical challenge of limited cross-domain generalization in Process Reward Models (PRMs). Existing approaches remain constrained by domain-specific correctness signals and thus struggle to accommodate the diverse reasoning styles found across disciplines, from the formal symbolic derivations of the sciences to the nuanced argumentation of the humanities. To overcome this gap, we shifted the learning objective from verifying isolated correctness to modeling domain-agnostic
contextual coherence
. To realize our motivation, we proposed a synergistic framework combining a new data annotation standard with a corresponding context-aware training methodology. Our resulting model,
ContextPRM
, establishes a new state-of-the-art in multi-domain test-time scaling, achieving significant and consistent accuracy improvements across non-mathematical domains where prior models have struggled. Extensive experiments, including ablation and single-domain studies, validate that our approach successfully imbues the model with robust generalization capabilities by teaching it the universal principles of logical flow. Our findings suggest that focusing on the structure of reasoning, rather than its content alone, is a promising direction for building more general and robust process reward models for multi-domain test-time scaling.

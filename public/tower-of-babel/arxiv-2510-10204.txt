Appell Functions for General Lattices
Aradhita Chattopadhyaya a , Jan Manschot b,c

Abstract
We study Appell functions associated to an arbitrary positive definite lattice Λ \Lambda and a choice of M ≤ dim ⁡ ( Λ ) M\leq{\rm dim}(\Lambda) linearly independent vectors d r ∈ Λ d_{r}\in\Lambda , r = 1 , … , M r=1,\dots,M . These functions are instances of multi-variable quasi-elliptic functions, and specific examples have appeared at various places in mathematics and theoretical physics. For example, if Λ \Lambda is chosen to be one-dimensional, these functions reduce to the classical Appell function, which is a prominent example in the theory of mock modular forms. The Appell functions introduced here are examples of depth M M mock modular forms. We derive a structural formula for their modular completion. Motivated by partition functions in theoretical physics, we discuss the case where Λ \Lambda is the A N A_{N} root lattice in detail.

combines to

\Phi^{+}_{\mu,\nu^{||}}(\tau,u^{||},v,\{d_{s_{j}}\})

and

and

\begin{split}&{\mathcal{R}}_{\mu}(\tau,\bar{\tau},u,\bar{u},v,\bar{v},\{d_{r}\})=\sum_{L=1}^{M}\sum_{\{v_{1},\dots,v_{L},s_{1},\dots,s_{M-L}\}\in\{1,\dots,M\}}\\
&\qquad\sum_{x_{v_{\ell}}\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{-L}M_{L}(\{d_{v_{i}}^{*}\},\Lambda;\sqrt{2y}(x_{v_{\ell}}+{\rm Im}(\sigma_{v_{\ell}})/y)d_{v_{\ell}})\,\\
&\qquad\times q^{-Q(x_{v_{\ell}}d_{v_{\ell}}^{\perp S})/2}\,e^{2\pi iB(u-v,x_{v_{\ell}}d_{v_{\ell}}^{\perp S})}\\
&\qquad\times\Phi^{+}_{\mu,\nu^{||}}(\tau,u^{||},v,\{d_{s_{j}}\}).\end{split}

(121)

Thus

is fully expressed in terms of

\Phi^{+}_{\mu,\nu^{||}}
for different sets
\{d_{s_{j}}\}
and data in terms of
\Lambda
.

The Lattice
\Lambda_{d}(\{d^{*}_{v_{\ell}}\})
and Glue Vectors

We next decompose the sum over
x_{v_{\ell}}
into a sum over an
integral lattice together with a finite sum over conjugacy classes.
The vectors
\sum_{\ell}x_{v_{\ell}}d_{v_{\ell}}^{\perp S}
lie in the vector space over
\mathbb{R}
orthogonal to

\{d_{s_{j}}\}
. While this space is spanned by
d_{v_{\ell}}^{\perp S}
,

\ell=1,\dots,L
, it is also spanned by the dual basis vectors

d_{v_{\ell}}^{*}
since
B(d_{v_{\ell}}^{*},d_{s_{j}})=0
for all
\ell=1,\dots,L
and
j=1,\dots,M-L
. We define
\Lambda_{d}(\{d^{*}_{v_{\ell}}\})\subset\Lambda_{d}
as the
integral

sublattice of
\Lambda_{d}
spanned by
d_{v_{\ell}}^{*}
. The
generators of
\Lambda_{d}(\{d^{*}_{v_{\ell}}\})
are thus suitable linear
combinations of
d^{*}_{v_{\ell}}\in\Lambda_{d}^{*}
such that
\sum_{\ell}N_{\ell}d^{*}_{v_{\ell}}\in\Lambda_{d}
.

Because of the orthogonality of the sets

\{d^{*}_{v_{\ell}}\}
and
d_{s_{j}}
, we have the equivalence

\Lambda_{d}(\{d^{*}_{v_{\ell}}\})\equiv\Lambda_{d}(\{d^{\perp S}_{v_{\ell}}\})
. Moreover the direct sum of the integral lattices is a
sublatice of
\Lambda_{d}
,
\Lambda_{d}(\{d_{s_{j}}\})\oplus\Lambda_{d}(\{d^{*}_{v_{\ell}}\})\subset\Lambda_{d}
, while
\Lambda_{d}
is a sublattice of the direct sum of the
dual lattices
\Lambda_{d}\subset\Lambda_{d}^{*}(\{d_{s_{j}}\})\oplus\Lambda^{*}_{d}(\{d^{*}_{v_{\ell}}\})
. Using general theory of lattices
conway
, an element

k\in\Lambda_{d}
can be written as

k=\nu_{g}+\ell^{||}+\ell^{\perp}
with
\ell^{||}\in\Lambda_{d}(\{d_{s_{j}}\})
and
\ell^{\perp}\in\Lambda_{d}(\{d^{*}_{v_{\ell}}\})
and
\nu_{g}\in\Lambda_{d}
a glue vector.
The number
\mathcal{N}_{g}(\{d_{s_{j}}\},\Lambda_{d})
of glue vectors
\nu_{g}
,
g=1,\dots,\mathcal{N}_{g}
, is given in terms of the number of
elements in the cosets of the lattices involved,

\mathcal{N}_{g}(\{d_{s_{j}}\},\Lambda_{d})=\sqrt{\frac{|\Lambda^{*}_{d}(\{d^{*}_{v_{i}}\})/\Lambda_{d}(\{d^{*}_{v_{i}}\})|\,|\Lambda^{*}_{d}(\{d_{s_{j}}\})/\Lambda_{d}(\{d_{s_{j}}\})|}{|\Lambda_{d}^{*}/\Lambda_{d}|}}.

(122)

We have the orthogonal decomposition
\nu_{g}=\nu_{g}^{||}+\nu_{g}^{\perp}
, with

\nu^{||}_{g}
the projection of
\nu_{g}
to
\Lambda^{*}_{d}(\{d_{s_{j}}\})

and
\nu_{g}^{\perp}
the projection to
\Lambda_{d}(\{d^{*}_{v_{\ell}}\})
. The number
\mathcal{N}_{g}
is easily
determined by using that for a general lattice

\Lambda
, the number of elements
|\Lambda^{*}/\Lambda|
is determined
in terms of its bilinear form
B
as,

|\Lambda^{*}/\Lambda|=|{\rm det}(B)|.

(123)

Now we return to the completion (
121
). Recall that

M_{L}(\{d_{v_{\ell}}^{*}\},\Lambda;k)
only depends on the components of

k
parallel to
\{d_{v_{\ell}}^{*}\}
. The vectors

x_{v_{\ell}}d_{v_{\ell}}
on the second line
of (
121
) can thus be replaced by

x_{v_{\ell}}d_{v_{\ell}}^{\perp S}
as on the third line. Moreover, since
\{d_{v_{\ell}}^{*}\}
generates
the same lattice as
d_{v_{\ell}}^{\perp S}
, the sum over
x_{v_{\ell}}

can be written as a sum over
\Lambda_{d}(\{d_{v_{\ell}}^{*}\})+\nu
for
specific
\nu
.

Structural Formula for
\widehat{\Phi}_{\mu}

We combine the above results, to arrive at a structural formula for

\widehat{\Phi}_{\mu}
. To this end, let

be a set with

linearly independent elements

spanning the lattice

where the non-holomorphic function

with

as in Eq. (

and

(

)

\nu=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}-\left\{-\tfrac{a}{N+1}\right\}+\left\{-\tfrac{2a}{N+1}\right\}\\
\left\{-\tfrac{2a}{N+1}\right\}\\
\left\{-\tfrac{3a}{N+1}\right\}\\
\vdots\\
\left\{-\tfrac{Na}{N+1}\right\}\end{array}\right).

(198)

This agrees with the expression for
B(\nu,u)
with
u=(z_{1},\dots,z_{N})^{T}
(
188
),

\displaystyle 4z\sum_{j=2}^{N+1}\left\{\frac{a}{N+1}(N+2-j)\right\}

Appendix A
The
A_{N}
Root Lattice

A.1
Roots of the
A_{N}
Lattice

In this section we review some basic properties of the
A_{N}
root lattice
of
SU(N+1)
and its Weyl group. The root lattice contains
N
simple
roots,
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\cdots,\alpha_{N}
, with unit norm with respect to the standard innerproduct
(\cdot)
on

\mathbb{R}^{N}
. The angle between
consecutive roots
\alpha_{j},\alpha_{j\pm 1}
is
\frac{2\pi}{3}
, and
the roots are orthogonal otherwise. The non-vanishing innerproducts are thus

\displaystyle\alpha_{i}^{2}=1,\quad\alpha_{i}\cdot\alpha_{j}=-\frac{1}{2}\delta_{i,j\pm 1},\quad{\rm for}\;i\neq j.

(200)

Note this innerproduct is not even, and not integral.

We furthermore introduce the Cartan matrix for
SU(N+1)
, which is the
even, integral quadratic form
{\bf A}_{N}
with entries

\displaystyle({\bf A}_{N})_{ij}=\frac{2\alpha_{i}\cdot\alpha_{j}}{\alpha_{j}^{2}},

(201)

or

{\bf A}_{N}=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccccc}2&-1&0&&\\
-1&2&-1&0&\\
0&-1&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots\\
&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots&1\\
&&&-1&2\end{array}\right),\quad\det({\bf A}_{N})=N+1.

(202)

We let the quadratic form
Q(l)
correspond to
{\bf A}_{N}

(
202
). Thus for
l=\sum_{i}l_{i}\alpha_{i}\in A_{N}
,
Q(l)
reads

Q(l)=2\sum_{i=1}^{N}l_{i}^{2}-2\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}l_{i}l_{i+1}.

(203)

Furthermore, the bilinear form
B
is defined as

B(l,l^{\prime})=\frac{1}{4}(Q(l+l^{\prime})-Q(l-l^{\prime})),

(204)

such that
B(l,l)=Q(l)
, and
B(\alpha_{i},\alpha_{j})=({\bf A}_{N})_{ij}
.

The change of basis given by

\begin{split}\tilde{l}_{1}&=l_{2}-l_{1},\\
\tilde{l}_{2}&=l_{1},\\
\tilde{l}_{j}&=l_{j}-l_{j-1},\quad{\rm for}\;j=3,4,\dots,N.\end{split}

(205)

changes the bilinear form
{\bf A}_{N}
to

\tilde{\bf A}_{N}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}2&1&1&\dots\\
1&2&1&\dots\\
\vdots&\ddots&&\\
\dots&\dots&1&2\end{array}\right),

(206)

with quadratic form
\tilde{Q}
:

\tilde{Q}(\tilde{l})=2\sum_{i=1}^{N}\tilde{l}_{i}^{2}+\sum_{i\neq j}\tilde{l}_{i}\tilde{l}_{j}.

(207)

A.2
Weyl Group

The Weyl group of the root system of
SU(N+1)
is the symmetric group
\mathcal{S}_{N+1}
, which
is generated by the reflections through the hyperplanes orthogonal to the
N
simple roots

\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{N}
. If
\rho\in A_{N}
is a root, then under
the reflection
S_{\alpha}
along the root

\alpha
we have,

S_{\alpha}(\rho)=\rho-2\alpha\frac{\rho\cdot\alpha}{\alpha^{2}}.

(208)

This reflection preserves the inner product
\rho_{1}\cdot\rho_{2}
,

\displaystyle\rho_{1}\cdot\rho_{2}

\displaystyle\rightarrow

\displaystyle\rho_{1}\cdot\rho_{2}-2\alpha\cdot\rho_{2}\frac{\rho_{1}\cdot\alpha}{\alpha^{2}}-2\rho_{2}\cdot\alpha\frac{\rho_{1}\cdot\alpha}{\alpha^{2}}+4\rho_{2}\cdot\alpha\frac{\rho_{1}\cdot\alpha}{\alpha^{2}}=\rho_{1}\cdot\rho_{2}.

For the reflection
S_{\alpha_{j}}
with respect to the simple root
\alpha_{j}
at
j
-th position in the Dynkin diagram of
A_{N}
, we have the following transformation on the simple roots:

\begin{split}&S_{\alpha_{j}}(\alpha_{j})=-\alpha_{j},\quad S_{\alpha_{j}}(\alpha_{j-1})=\alpha_{j-1}+\alpha_{j},\quad S_{\alpha_{j}}(\alpha_{j+1})=\alpha_{j+1}+\alpha_{j},\\
&S_{\alpha_{j}}(\alpha_{j\pm k})=\alpha_{j\pm k},\quad{\rm for}\;k\geq 2.\end{split}

(209)

When expanded in terms of the basis
\{\alpha_{i}\}
, the image of a vector
k=\sum_{i}k_{i}\alpha_{i}\in A_{N}
under
S_{\alpha_{j}}
is,

\displaystyle S_{\alpha_{j}}(k)=\sum_{i=1}^{j-1}k_{i}\alpha_{i}+\sum_{i=j+1}^{N}k_{i}\alpha_{i}-\alpha_{j}k_{j}+\alpha_{j}k_{j-1}+\alpha_{j}k_{j+1},

(210)

such that the components
k_{j}
of
k
are transformed as,

\displaystyle k_{j}\rightarrow-k_{j}+k_{j-1}+k_{j+1},\quad k_{i\neq j}=k_{i}.

(211)

Clearly, the quadratic form
Q(k)
(
203
) remains invariant,

\begin{split}Q(k)/2\rightarrow&\sum_{i\neq j}k_{i}^{2}-\sum_{i\neq j,j-1}k_{i}k_{i+1}+(k_{j-1}+k_{j+1}-k_{j})^{2}\\
&-(k_{j-1}+k_{j+1})(k_{j-1}+k_{j+1}-k_{j})\\
&=\sum_{i=1}^{N}k_{i}^{2}-\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}k_{i}k_{i+1}=Q(k)/2.\end{split}

(212)

Appendix B
Proof of the Orthogonality Relation

In this Appendix we prove the orthogonality relation
(
115
). To this end, recall that by Eq. (
99
) the
c_{s,v}
(
101
) are solutions to

\underline{B}(C_{s_{j}}-\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}c_{s_{j},v_{\ell}}C_{v_{\ell}},C_{v_{k}})=0

for all
j=1,\dots,M-L
and
k=1,\dots,L
. Using
Eq. (
51
), this can be written in terms of the matrix
\mathbf{D}^{-1}
,

\mathbf{D}^{-1}_{s_{j}v_{k}}=\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}c_{s_{j},v_{\ell}}\mathbf{D}^{-1}_{v_{\ell}v_{k}}.

(213)

We multiply this equation by the matrix
\mathbf{D}_{s_{i}s_{j}}
,

\sum_{j=1}^{M-L}\mathbf{D}_{s_{i}s_{j}}\mathbf{D}^{-1}_{s_{j}v_{k}}=\sum_{j=1}^{M-L}\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}\mathbf{D}_{s_{i}s_{j}}c_{s_{j},v_{\ell}}\mathbf{D}^{-1}_{v_{\ell}v_{k}}.

(214)

Completing the sum over
s_{j}
with
v_{m}
,
m=1,\dots,L
, we can write
the left hand side as

\delta_{s_{i},v_{k}}-\sum_{m=1}^{L}\mathbf{D}_{s_{i}v_{m}}\mathbf{D}^{-1}_{v_{m}v_{k}}.

(215)

Since
s_{i}\neq v_{k}
for all
i,k
, the
\delta
-function vanishes.
The identity (
214
) therefore becomes

\sum_{j=1}^{M-L}\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}(\mathbf{D}_{s_{i}s_{j}}c_{s_{j},v_{\ell}}+\mathbf{D}_{s_{i}v_{\ell}})\mathbf{D}^{-1}_{v_{\ell}v_{k}}=0.

(216)

Since
{\bf D}
is positive definite, we can multiply this equation by

{\bf D}
from the right. Substitution of
{\bf D}_{s,v}=B(d_{s},d_{v})
demonstrates that this is equivalent to the desired identity
Eq. (
115
),

B\left(d_{s_{j}},d_{v_{\ell}}+\sum_{k=1}^{M-L}c_{s_{k},v_{\ell}}d_{s_{k}}\right)=0.

(217)

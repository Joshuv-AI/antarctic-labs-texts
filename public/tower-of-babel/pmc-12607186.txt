ABSTRACT
BackgroundSepsis is a common, life-threatening medical emergency in which early recognition and treatment are cornerstones of management. Best practice guidelines recommend standardized sepsis screening in hospitals to facilitate early recognition. In contrast, very little is known about the efficacy and safety of standardized sepsis screening in the prehospital Emergency Medical Services (EMS) environment. To help address this knowledge gap, this study protocol will evaluate the impact of implementing an evidence-based sepsis protocol in ambulances.MethodsWe plan to conduct a multicenter, stepped-wedge, cluster-randomized trial at 3 sites and enroll 984 participants over 24 months. EMS personnel will be trained to screen all eligible EMS patients using a sepsis screening and early notification protocol. Study inclusion criteria include all the following: (1) EMS systolic blood pressure < 110 mmHg, (2) EMS pulse > 90 beats/min, (3) EMS respiratory rate > 20 breaths/min, and (4) a positive sepsis screen based on the EMS sepsis protocol. To evaluate the clinical impact of the EMS sepsis protocol on patient care, the primary outcome is time to first antibiotic administration in the ED (emergency department) among patients with sepsis (a key treatment intervention in patients with sepsis). To evaluate the potential risk of the EMS sepsis protocol, the surrogate primary safety outcome is the total number of antibiotic DOT (days of therapy) during the first 7 days of hospitalization among patients without sepsis (i.e., false positive sepsis screens).DiscussionSuccessful completion of this trial will expand our understanding of prehospital sepsis screening as a standardized approach to prehospital sepsis care.Trial registrationClinicalTrials.gov Identifier: NCT 05502107.Date of first posting: 08–16-2022.https://clinicaltrials.gov/ct2/show/NCT05502107.

Introduction

Background and rationale {6a}

Sepsis, a life-threatening medical emergency, is a leading cause of morbidity and mortality in patients presenting to the hospital via Emergency Medical Services (EMS) [1, 2]. Significant advancements in sepsis care have occurred over the past two decades, largely due to implementation of early and aggressive management [3]. Delays in sepsis care, especially due to delayed sepsis recognition, are associated with worse patient outcomes [4, 5]. EMS is the first healthcare touch point for nearly half of patients hospitalized with sepsis, representing an important opportunity to identify patients with sepsis before they reach the hospital to help minimize hospital-based treatment delays [6, 7].

EMS screening and prehospital notification protocols have improved outcomes in other life-threatening, time-sensitive conditions including heart attack and stroke by providing valuable lead time for hospitals to mobilize resources before the patient’s arrival [8–10]. Unfortunately, screening tools to assist EMS personnel in recognizing sepsis have lagged behind similar tools and protocols for heart attack and stroke. Not surprisingly, sepsis recognition in the EMS setting is poor [11]. Available evidence suggests that EMS personnel recognize 11–12% of sepsis cases using clinical judgment alone [11–13]. Early use of EMS sepsis screening tools suggest they may facilitate recognition in the field; however, the generalizability of these early findings remains unknown [2, 14].

We previously empirically derived, validated, and implemented an EMS sepsis screening and prehospital notification protocol in a single-center study [6]. Results from this study showed an increase in sepsis recognition by EMS providers from 12% pre-implementation to 59% post-implementation (p< 0.001) [6]. Protocol implementation was also associated with a 24-min decrease in the time to first antibiotic administration in the ED among patients with sepsis who were accurately identified by the protocol [28 min (IQR 18–48) vs 52 (IQR 27–98), respectively, p= 0.021] [6, 14].

The purpose of this study protocol is to evaluate the efficacy and safety of the EMS sepsis protocol previously developed by our group using a multicenter, stepped-wedge, cluster-randomized trial design. The study will be conducted at 3 sites located in different geographic regions within the USA.

Objectives {7}

The primary objective of this study is to evaluate the impact of an EMS sepsis screening and early notification protocol tool on the timing of early sepsis care in the ED. Our primary hypothesis is that implementation of the protocol will be associated with a reduction in time to first antibiotic administration in the ED among patients with sepsis. This study will also evaluate the impact of the EMS sepsis protocol among patients without sepsis, i.e., false positive sepsis screens.

Trial design {8}

This is a multicenter, cluster-randomized, stepped-wedge implementation trial of an EMS sepsis screening and early notification protocol (Table 1). The study intervention is training and implementation of the EMS protocol among EMS personnel. Each cluster includes a paired EMS agency and at least one receiving hospital. Three EMS-hospital clusters will be randomized to the order of the intervention, i.e., EMS training and implementation intervention. Each cluster will begin with at least 6 months of baseline, pre-intervention data collection. Study sites will be randomized to the order of EMS protocol implementation at approximately 6-month intervals beginning after the first 6 months. Randomization of individual EMS providers is not feasible for this type of intervention because EMS crews work in teams, and team member assignments can vary from shift to shift, thus introducing unavoidable contamination of the control group.

EMS sepsis protocol

Upon arrival at the ED, the ED care team will perform an immediate medical assessment for all patients with a positive EMS sepsis screen. During the initial assessment, the ED care team will determine whether there is clinical concern for infection and sepsis. If the treating team determines there is concern for sepsis, they will deliver usual sepsis care per any local hospital protocols, workflows, or care pathways. ED and hospital-based sepsis treatment interventions will not be specified by this study protocol.

Methods: participants, interventions, and outcomes

Study setting {9}

This study will be conducted in the prehospital EMS setting. EMS personnel will be trained to implement the sepsis screening and early notification protocol during routine EMS encounters. Study data from EMS, ED, and inpatient clinical documentation will be collected using standardized methods and managed in a centralized database.

Interventions

Explanation for the choice of comparators {6b}

The rationale for a before-after cluster randomized design was based on the variable, team-based nature of EMS care delivery. Randomizing individual EMS personnel is not feasible because team assignments can vary from shift to shift, thus contaminating the control group. Control and intervention groups will be compared as part of the cluster-randomized, stepped-wedge trial design.

Intervention description {11a}

The study intervention will include training and implementation of the EMS sepsis screening and early notification protocol among EMS personnel. Study teams and EMS agency staff will work together to deliver training and education sessions at each study site prior to a predetermined protocol “go-live” date. The training program will instruct EMS personnel how to screen patients for eligibility and use the sepsis protocol. The EMS sepsis protocol includes 2 major components: (1) applying a sepsis screening tool and (2) a prehospital sepsis alert call to the receiving hospital as part of the radio report for all positive screens. EMS personnel will complete a mandatory training session (live or online) and an associated post-test. Competency will be determined based on EMS institutional standards based on the post-test assessment score. EMS personnel will be provided with laminated protocol badge buddy cards. Laminated protocols will also be posted for display inside ambulances.

Study sites will work with their partnering EMS agency to implement the protocol at the beginning of the intervention phase. EMS agencies will be encouraged to publicize the IMPRESS study kickoff date and will disseminate electronic reminders according to local communication practices.

Criteria for discontinuing or modifying allocated interventions {11b}

Each EMS agency will require the following: sepsis protocol training for all new hires, ongoing communication with existing EMS personnel, and quality checks with feedback to maximize protocol uptake by EMS personnel during the intervention phase.

Strategies to improve adherence to interventions {11c}

EMS personnel training and education will occur over approximately 4 weeks at each site and will include a live or online learning module and post-test. According to local and state regulations, EMS personnel will be incentivized to participate in sepsis protocol training by offering continuing education credits for completion.

In addition, study staff will distribute badge-sized laminated protocols to all EMS personnel, and laminated protocols will be displayed inside ambulances. The EMS screening and early notification protocol (including inclusion/exclusion criteria) is displayed in Fig. 1. Study staff will work with EMS agencies to identify site-specific champions (such as field training officers) to send live or virtual shift huddle reminders at the time protocol “go-live.” During protocol “go-live,” enrollments and data collection will begin in the intervention phase. We will pause enrollment and data collection during the training and transition phase to minimize the risk of crossover between comparison groups.

Relevant concomitant care permitted or prohibited during the trial {11d}

Prehospital screening and notification should not detract from patient care or otherwise place the patient at increased risk of delay of immediate care. As the intervention solely involves prehospital notification, pre-arrival notification may occur for other reasons, such as a critically ill patient or concern for stroke or ST-segment elevation myocardial infarction (STEMI). These other forms of notification are permitted and may occur during all phases of the study.

Provisions for post-trial care {30}

No ancillary and post-trial care or compensation are provided as part of this study protocol.

Outcomes {12}

Primary endpoint

The primary outcome measure is the time from ED arrival to first parenteral antibiotic administration in the ED among EMS participants with sepsis. We anticipate that all enrolled patients included for primary outcome analysis will receive antibiotics in the ED per the operational definition for classifying sepsis. However, in the (rare) situation that this does not occur, time will be censored at the time of hospital admission or ED discharge for patients not receiving antibiotics.

Participant timeline {13}

Each study cluster represents a single EMS organization and a single partnered acute care hospital. Each cluster pair will participate in both a baseline and intervention phase of study, varying between 6 and 18 months each (baseline and intervention), in approximately 6-month blocks, depending on the order of cluster randomization as depicted in Table 1 above. The timeline for study data collection includes the index EMS encounter, the associated ED encounter, and the associated inpatient hospitalization (as applicable).

Sample size {14}

Based on a sample of 3 clusters in a complete stepped-wedge, cluster-randomized design with 4 time periods (including the baseline), 3 steps, and 1 cluster switching from baseline to intervention at each step, a baseline mean time to first antibiotic administration of 60 min with a standard deviation of 38 min, we estimate a total of 492 patients with sepsis will need to be enrolled to achieve 97% power to detect a difference between means of − 23 min. Based on preliminary data, we estimate 50% of patients who screen positive will have sepsis; therefore, a total sample size of 984 participant enrollments will be needed [6]. The test statistics used is the two-sided Wald Z-test. The ICC is 0.01, and the significance level of the test is 0.05.

Recruitment {15}

The estimated total number of participant enrollments for this study is 984. Of these, we anticipate one-third will be enrolled at each of the three participating sites. Study personnel will screen EMS patient records retrospectively for study eligibility criteria. Study team personnel will enroll EMS patients based on eligibility criteria. EMS and hospital records will be linked for the same patient encounter to create a continuous record of data spanning the EMS and hospital continuum. Linked documents will include matching patient name, date of birth, and date of EMS and hospital encounters, with the following exceptions:

Assignment of interventions: allocation

Sequence generation {16a}

The study statistician will provide a randomization order for the site (hospital) clusters using a random number generator. Each hospital (cluster) will be informed of its randomization status (e.g., order in the stepped wedge design) prior to the training and implementation phase. Each site will initiate study activity in a baseline data collection phase that will occur over an approximate 6–18-month period depending on the order of randomization. Protocol implementation will occur at approximately 6-month intervals depending on the order of randomization. A transition and training period of approximately 4 weeks will be used to train EMS and hospital staff at each site. Data will be stored in an online, HIPAA-compliant, password-protected REDCap database. Each (non-Emory) participating site will be responsible for local study data storage and uploading local data into the central Emory REDCap database at least quarterly. Statistical methods for primary and secondary outcomes {20a}

Analyses will be “intention to treat” (ITT), and eligible participants will be assigned the randomized intervention status for each site/cluster at each time point. The ITT approach will be used regardless of delays in implementing the intervention protocol or possible crossover effects (which is unlikely given the training period).

Analysis of the primary endpoint

A linear mixed effect model will assess time (in minutes) from ED arrival to first parenteral antibiotic administration in the ED by intervention status. The dependent variable is time from ED arrival to first antibiotic administration and is assumed to be continuous and normally distributed with no censoring. Independent variables in the model are calendar time step (0,1,2,3), intervention status (control, intervention), and covariates. Random effects are cluster and the jack-knife method will be used to estimate standard errors to account for grouping within clusters. The planned covariance structure is the first order of autocorrelation (AR1). We will use the Satterthwaite method to adjust for denominator degree of freedom for tests of the fixed effects.

Model-based means and 95% confidence intervals will be reported for control and intervention groups with significance assessed by the fixed effect for intervention status. For the primary safety outcome of receipt of antibiotics among patients without sepsis, a generalized linear mixed model with the same fixed and random effects, binary distribution, and log link (log binomial models) will be used. Model-based relative risk and 95% confidence interval will be reported for intervention vs control groups. If the model fails to converge, logistic regression models will be used as an alternative. For the primary safety outcome of the number of days of antibiotic use among patients without sepsis, the same linear mixed model described above will be used.

Analysis of secondary endpoints

Generalized linear mixed models as described above will be used to assess secondary endpoints with appropriate distribution and link functions.

Descriptive analyses

To assess for failure of randomization, univariable univariate analyses will compare distributions of social demographic and clinical characteristics by intervention status (control, intervention) and cluster/site. Univariable univariate analyses will evaluate each of the primary and secondary outcomes by intervention status (control, intervention) and cluster/site.

Interim analyses {21b}

None.

Methods for additional analyses (e.g., subgroup analyses) {20b}

Subgroup analysis will be conducted using the same outcomes and analytic plan in the subset of patients with septic shock. A per-protocol sensitivity analysis will be conducted using the same outcomes and analytic plan among patients who receive a prehospital sepsis alert.

Methods in analysis to handle protocol non-adherence and any statistical methods to handle missing data {20c}

Due to eligibility criteria requiring linkage between EMS and hospital data systems, and hospital protocol to treat sepsis, we anticipate minimal missing data on primary outcome variables. The trial oversight team will monitor for missing data and send secure queries to the study teams to address missing data in the database. Variables used in the descriptive and primary analyses will not be imputed. If necessary, a missing category will be created for analysis if there is more than 10% missing for any characteristic. Some of the secondary and sensitivity analyses, imputation (multiple imputation by chained equations (MICE) may be used for missing characteristic (covariate) information.

Plans to give access to the full protocol, participant-level data, and statistical code {31c}

A limited, de-identified, participant-level dataset will be shared. This dataset will include participant demographic information, EMS physiologic data, and sepsis screening, classification, and outcomes data. Data will become available starting after publication of the primary study manuscript and will be available through the Emory Dataverse after requesting access from the primary study investigator for proposals that are approved by the publication committee.

Background introduction and motivation

A branching random walk is a discrete-time Markov process which can be described as follows.
At time

\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}

A branching Lévy process is a continuous counterpart of branching random walk and it can be described as follows. At time 0, there is an
individual at
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and it moves according to a Lévy process

(\xi_{t},\mathbf{P}_{\mathbf{x}})

After an exponential time with parameter
\beta>0

k

p_{k}
,

located at the parent’s death place. The offspring then independently repeat the parent’s behavior.
This procedure goes on.
Let
Z_{t}
be the point process formed by the individual alive at time
t
. The process
(Z_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is called a branching Lévy process.

A more general class of branching Lévy processes on the real line has been introduced
by Bertoin and Mallein
[
3
]
. Recently, quite a few papers, see, for instance,
[
2

15
,
25

34

, have been devoted to this general model.
In this general model
the branching rate
\beta
is allowed to be infinite and that the branching is allowed to be non-local, i.e., the birthplace of the children is not necessarily the deathplace of their parent.
The branching Lévy process dealt with in this paper is a special case of the branching Lévy process introduced in
[
3
]
with local branching and finite branching rate.

\mathbb{P}_{x}
to denote the law of

\mathbb{E}_{x}
to denote the corresponding expectation.

.

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}kp_{k}=1\ \mbox{and}\ p_{1}<1.

when

2.1
Some estimates involving the standard normal density

Throughout this paper,

\phi(x):=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-\frac{x^{2}}{2}}
is
the standard normal density.

Lemma 2.1

(i)

For any
\Delta>0
,

\displaystyle\sup_{y\in\mathbb{R}}\left|\phi(y)-\phi(y+\Delta)\right|\leq(\Delta\wedge\sqrt{\Delta}).

(2.1)

(ii)

For any
0<r<s
with
s-r\in(0,1)
, it holds that

\displaystyle\sup_{y\in\mathbb{R}}\left|\frac{1}{\sqrt{r}}\phi\left(\frac{y}{\sqrt{r}}\right)-\frac{1}{\sqrt{s}}\phi\left(\frac{y}{\sqrt{s}}\right)\right|\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{r}}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{s}}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{s}}\left(\sqrt{s-r}+1-\exp\left\{-\frac{\sqrt{s-r}}{r}\right\}\right).

(2.2)

Proof:
(i)
It is easy to check that

\displaystyle|\phi^{\prime}(x)|=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}|x|e^{-\frac{x^{2}}{2}}\leq 1.

(2.3)

Therefore, noticing that
\phi(x)\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\leq\frac{1}{2}
, we conclude that

\sup_{y\in\mathbb{R}}\left|\phi(y)-\phi(y+\Delta)\right|\leq\Delta\land 1\leq(\Delta\wedge\sqrt{\Delta}).

(ii) For any
y\in\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle\left|\frac{1}{\sqrt{r}}\phi\left(\frac{y}{\sqrt{r}}\right)-\frac{1}{\sqrt{s}}\phi\left(\frac{y}{\sqrt{s}}\right)\right|\leq\left|\frac{1}{\sqrt{r}}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{s}}\right|\phi\left(\frac{y}{\sqrt{r}}\right)+\frac{1}{\sqrt{s}}\left|\phi\left(\frac{y}{\sqrt{r}}\right)-\phi\left(\frac{y}{\sqrt{s}}\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\left|\frac{1}{\sqrt{r}}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{s}}\right|+\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi s}}\exp\left\{-\frac{y^{2}}{2s}\right\}\left(1-\exp\left\{-y^{2}\left(\frac{1}{2r}-\frac{1}{2s}\right)\right\}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{r}}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{s}}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{s}}\exp\left\{-\frac{y^{2}}{2s}\right\}\left(1-\exp\left\{-y^{2}\left(\frac{1}{2r}-\frac{1}{2s}\right)\right\}\right).

(2.4)

If
y^{2}\sqrt{s-r}>2s
, then by the inequality
ae^{-a}<1
for all
a>1
, we get that

\displaystyle\exp\left\{-\frac{y^{2}}{2s}\right\}\left(1-\exp\left\{-y^{2}\left(\frac{1}{2r}-\frac{1}{2s}\right)\right\}\right)\leq\exp\left\{-\frac{1}{\sqrt{s-r}}\right\}\leq\sqrt{s-r}.

(2.5)

If
y^{2}\sqrt{s-r}\leq 2s
, then

\displaystyle\exp\left\{-\frac{y^{2}}{2s}\right\}\left(1-\exp\left\{-y^{2}\left(\frac{1}{2r}-\frac{1}{2s}\right)\right\}\right)\leq 1-\exp\left\{-\frac{s}{\sqrt{s-r}}\cdot\frac{s-r}{sr}\right\}

\displaystyle=1-\exp\left\{-\frac{\sqrt{s-r}}{r}\right\}.

(2.6)

Combining (
2.4
), (
2.5
) and (
2.6
), we conclude that

\displaystyle\left|\frac{1}{\sqrt{r}}\phi\left(\frac{y}{\sqrt{r}}\right)-\frac{1}{\sqrt{s}}\phi\left(\frac{y}{\sqrt{s}}\right)\right|\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{r}}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{s}}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{s}}\left(\sqrt{s-r}+1-\exp\left\{-\frac{\sqrt{s-r}}{r}\right\}\right),

(2.7)

which implies the desired result.

\Box

The following inequality will be used several times later:

\displaystyle\left|1-e^{-(x+y)}-x-y\right|\leq x^{2}+y^{2},\quad x,y\geq 0.

(2.8)

The proof of this inequality is elementary and we omit it.

2.2
Estimates for the Lévy process

We first give a local limit theorem for the underlying Lévy process

(\xi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
.
Before that, we recall the definition
of directly Riemann integrable functions. For more details on properties of directly Riemann integrable functions,
one can refer to
[
9
, Section XI.1]
and
[
11
, Section 2.1]
.

Let
f
be a non-negative Borel function. For any
\kappa>0
, define

\displaystyle\overline{f}_{\kappa}(x):=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}1_{[m\kappa,(m+1)\kappa)}(x)\sup_{z\in[m\kappa,(m+1)\kappa)}f(z),

\displaystyle\underline{f}_{\kappa}(x):=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}1_{[m\kappa,(m+1)\kappa)}(x)\inf_{z\in[m\kappa,(m+1)\kappa)}f(z).

(2.9)

We say that
f
is directly Riemann integrable if
\int\overline{f}_{\kappa}(x)\mathrm{d}x<\infty
for
some
\kappa>0
and

\lim_{\kappa\to 0}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(\overline{f}_{\kappa}(x)-\underline{f}_{\kappa}(x)\right)dx=0.

Recall that we use
{\rm DRI}^{+}(\mathbb{R})
to denote the family of non-negative directly Riemann integrable functions.
It is easy to see
from the definition that any
h\in{\rm DRI}^{+}(\mathbb{R})
must be bounded.
For
h\in{\rm DRI}^{+}(\mathbb{R})
, we define
\|h\|_{\infty}:=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}|h(x)|
.

Lemma 2.2

Assume that
(H2)
,
(H3)
and
(H4)
hold.
For any
f\in{\rm DRI}^{+}_{c}(\mathbb{R})
, it holds that

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\left|\sqrt{n}\mathbf{E}_{x}\left(f(\xi_{n})\right)-\ell(f)\phi\left(\frac{x}{\sqrt{n}}\right)\right|=0,

(2.10)

where
\phi(x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-\frac{x^{2}}{2}}
is the standard normal density.

Proof:

For any
\kappa,\vartheta>0
, define

\displaystyle\overline{f}_{\kappa,\vartheta}(x):=\sup_{|y|\leq\vartheta}\overline{f}_{\kappa}(x+y)\quad\mbox{and}\quad\underline{f}_{\kappa,\vartheta}(x):=\inf_{|y|\leq\vartheta}\underline{f}_{\kappa}(x+y),

(2.11)

then by
[
11
, Lemma 2.2]
, it holds that

\displaystyle\lim_{\kappa\to 0}\lim_{\vartheta\to 0}\int\left|\overline{f}_{\kappa,\vartheta}(x)-f(x)\right|\mathrm{d}x=\lim_{\kappa\to 0}\lim_{\vartheta\to 0}\int\left|\underline{f}_{\kappa,\vartheta}(x)-f(x)\right|\mathrm{d}x=0.

(2.12)

Let
\vartheta\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
be sufficiently small, then by
[
11
, (2.6) and Theorem 2.7]
, there exist a constant

K>0
independent of
\vartheta

and
\kappa
and a constant
C_{\vartheta}>0
independent of
\kappa
such that for any
\kappa>0,x\in\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}_{0}\left(f(x+\xi_{n})\right)-\frac{1+K\vartheta}{\sqrt{n}}\int\overline{f}_{\kappa,\vartheta}(x+z)\phi\left(\frac{z}{\sqrt{n}}\right)\mathrm{d}z\leq\frac{C_{\vartheta}}{n^{(1+\delta^{*})/2}}\int\overline{f}_{\kappa,\vartheta}(x+z)\mathrm{d}z

(2.13)

and that

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}_{0}\left(f(x+\xi_{n})\right)-\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\int\left(\underline{f}_{\kappa,\vartheta}(x+z)-K\vartheta f(x+z)\right)\phi\left(\frac{z}{\sqrt{n}}\right)\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle\geq-\frac{C_{\vartheta}}{n^{(1+\delta^{*})/2}}\int f(x+z)\mathrm{d}z.

(2.14)

Therefore, by (
2.13
), we see that

\displaystyle\sqrt{n}\mathbf{E}_{x}\left(f(\xi_{n})\right)-\int f(x+z)\phi\left(\frac{z}{\sqrt{n}}\right)\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle\leq\left(K\vartheta+\frac{C_{\vartheta}}{n^{\delta^{*}/2}}\right)\int\overline{f}_{\kappa,\vartheta}(x+z)\mathrm{d}z+\int\left|\overline{f}_{\kappa,\vartheta}(x)-f(x)\right|\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\left(K\vartheta+\frac{C_{\vartheta}}{n^{\delta^{*}/2}}+1\right)\int\left|\overline{f}_{\kappa,\vartheta}(x)-f(x)\right|\mathrm{d}x+\left(K\vartheta+\frac{C_{\vartheta}}{n^{\delta^{*}/2}}\right)\int f(x)\mathrm{d}x=:\overline{I}(\vartheta,\kappa,n).

(2.15)

Similarly, according to (
2.14
), we have

\displaystyle\sqrt{n}\mathbf{E}_{x}\left(f(\xi_{n})\right)-\int f(x+z)\phi\left(\frac{z}{\sqrt{n}}\right)\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle\geq-\frac{C_{\vartheta}}{n^{\delta^{*}/2}}\int f(x+z)\mathrm{d}z-K\vartheta\int f(x+z)\phi\left(\frac{z}{\sqrt{n}}\right)\mathrm{d}z-\int\left|\underline{f}_{\kappa,\vartheta}(x)-f(x)\right|\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\geq-\left(K\vartheta+\frac{C_{\vartheta}}{n^{\delta^{*}/2}}\right)\int f(x)\mathrm{d}x-\int\left|\underline{f}_{\kappa,\vartheta}(x)-f(x)\right|\mathrm{d}x\geq-\underline{I}(\vartheta,\kappa,n).

(2.16)

Therefore, combining (
2.15
) and (
2.16
), we conclude that

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\left|\sqrt{n}\mathbf{E}_{x}\left(f(\xi_{n})\right)-\int f(x+z)\phi\left(\frac{z}{\sqrt{n}}\right)\mathrm{d}z\right|

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{n\to\infty}\overline{I}(\vartheta,\kappa,n)+\lim_{n\to\infty}\underline{I}(\vartheta,\kappa,n)

\displaystyle=\left(K\vartheta+1\right)\int\left|\overline{f}_{\kappa,\vartheta}(x)-f(x)\right|\mathrm{d}x+2K\vartheta\int f(x)\mathrm{d}x+\int\left|\underline{f}_{\kappa,\vartheta}(x)-f(x)\right|\mathrm{d}x.

(2.17)

By (
2.12
), letting
\vartheta\to 0
first and then
\kappa\to 0
in the above inequality, we get

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\left|\sqrt{n}\mathbf{E}_{x}\left(f(\xi_{n})\right)-\int f(x+z)\phi\left(\frac{z}{\sqrt{n}}\right)\mathrm{d}z\right|=0.

(2.18)

Thus, to prove the desired result, it remains to show that

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\left|\phi\left(\frac{x}{\sqrt{n}}\right)\int f(z)\mathrm{d}z-\int f(x+z)\phi\left(\frac{z}{\sqrt{n}}\right)\mathrm{d}z\right|=0.

(2.19)

Let
E
be any bounded interval such that
\mbox{supp}(f)\subset E
, then for any
x\in\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle\left|\phi\left(\frac{x}{\sqrt{n}}\right)\int f(z)\mathrm{d}z-\int f(x+z)\phi\left(\frac{z}{\sqrt{n}}\right)\mathrm{d}z\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\phi\left(\frac{x}{\sqrt{n}}\right)\int f(z)\mathrm{d}z-\int f(z)\phi\left(\frac{x-z}{\sqrt{n}}\right)\mathrm{d}z\right|

\displaystyle\leq\|f\|_{\infty}\int_{E}\left|\phi\left(\frac{x}{\sqrt{n}}\right)-\phi\left(\frac{x-z}{\sqrt{n}}\right)\right|\mathrm{d}z\leq\ell(E)\|f\|_{\infty}\sup_{z\in E}\left|\phi\left(\frac{x}{\sqrt{n}}\right)-\phi\left(\frac{x-z}{\sqrt{n}}\right)\right|.

(2.20)

If
|x|<n^{2/3}
, then by the inequality
e^{-a}-e^{-b}\leq|b-a|
for any
a,b\geq 0,

we see that

\displaystyle\sup_{z\in E}\left|\phi\left(\frac{x}{\sqrt{n}}\right)-\phi\left(\frac{x-z}{\sqrt{n}}\right)\right|\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\sup_{z\in E}\left|\frac{x^{2}-(x-z)^{2}}{n}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\left(\frac{1}{n}\sup_{z\in E}z^{2}+\frac{1}{n^{1/3}}\sup_{z\in E}|z|\right)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle n\to\infty}}{{\longrightarrow}}0.

(2.21)

On the other hand, if
|x|>n^{2/3}
, then for large
n
, we see that

\displaystyle\sup_{z\in E}\left|\phi\left(\frac{x}{\sqrt{n}}\right)-\phi\left(\frac{x-z}{\sqrt{n}}\right)\right|\leq\phi(n^{1/6})+\phi\left(\frac{n^{2/3}-\sup_{z\in E}|z|}{\sqrt{n}}\right)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle n\to\infty}}{{\longrightarrow}}0.

(2.22)

The proof is now complete.

\Box

Lemma 2.4

Assume that
(H2)
,
(H3)
and
(H4)
hold.
For any
h\in{\rm DRI}^{+}_{c}(\mathbb{R})
, it holds that

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\left|\sqrt{t}\mathbf{E}_{x}\left(h(\xi_{t})\right)-\ell(h)\phi\left(\frac{x}{\sqrt{t}}\right)\right|=0.

(2.23)

Proof:

We write
t>1
as
t=[t]+\gamma
with
\gamma\in[0,1)
.
Combining the Markov property and the inequality
\sqrt{t}-\sqrt{[t]}=\frac{t-[t]}{\sqrt{t}+\sqrt{[t]}}\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}
,
we see that for any
x\in\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle\left|\sqrt{t}\mathbf{E}_{x}\left(h(\xi_{t})\right)-\ell(h)\phi\left(\frac{x}{\sqrt{t}}\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq(\sqrt{t}-\sqrt{[t]})\|h\|_{\infty}+\mathbf{E}_{x}\left(\left|\sqrt{[t]}\mathbf{E}_{\xi_{\gamma}}(h(\xi_{[t]}))-\ell(h)\phi\left(\frac{\xi_{\gamma}}{\sqrt{[t]}}\right)\right|\right)+\ell(h)\left|\mathbf{E}_{x}\left(\phi\left(\frac{\xi_{\gamma}}{\sqrt{[t]}}\right)\right)-\phi\left(\frac{x}{\sqrt{t}}\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\|h\|_{\infty}}{\sqrt{t}}+\sup_{z\in\mathbb{R}}\left|\sqrt{[t]}\mathbf{E}_{z}\left(h(\xi_{[t]})\right)-\ell(h)\phi\left(\frac{z}{\sqrt{[t]}}\right)\right|+\ell(h)\sup_{z\in\mathbb{R}}\left|\mathbf{E}_{z}\left(\phi\left(\frac{\xi_{\gamma}}{\sqrt{[t]}}\right)\right)-\phi\left(\frac{z}{\sqrt{t}}\right)\right|.

(2.24)

The first term on the right-hand side tends to 0 as
t\to\infty
. By Lemma
2.2
, the second term also tends to 0 as
t\to\infty
. Therefore, it remains to prove that

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\left|\mathbf{E}_{x}\left(\phi\left(\frac{\xi_{\gamma}}{\sqrt{[t]}}\right)\right)-\phi\left(\frac{x}{\sqrt{t}}\right)\right|=\lim_{t\to\infty}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\left|\mathbf{E}_{0}\left(\phi\left(\frac{\xi_{\gamma}+x}{\sqrt{[t]}}\right)\right)-\phi\left(\frac{x}{\sqrt{t}}\right)\right|=0.

(2.25)

Note that for
|x|>t^{2/3}
,

\displaystyle\left|\mathbf{E}_{0}\left(\phi\left(\frac{\xi_{\gamma}+x}{\sqrt{[t]}}\right)\right)-\phi\left(\frac{x}{\sqrt{t}}\right)\right|\leq\mathbf{E}_{0}\left(\phi\left(\frac{\xi_{\gamma}+x}{\sqrt{[t]}}\right)\right)+\phi\left(\frac{x}{\sqrt{t}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbf{P}_{0}\left(\sup_{s\leq 1}|\xi_{s}|>\sqrt{t}\right)+\phi\left(\frac{t^{2/3}-\sqrt{t}}{\sqrt{[t]}}\right)+\phi\left(t^{1/6}\right)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle t\to\infty}}{{\longrightarrow}}0.

(2.26)

For
|x|\leq t^{2/3}
, by the inequality
|e^{-x^{2}}-e^{-y^{2}}|\leq|x^{2}-y^{2}|

n

\displaystyle\Lambda_{n}:=\emptyset,\quad B_{n}:=\left\{y\in\mathbb{R}:\mbox{dist}(y,\Lambda)\leq\frac{1}{n}\right\}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\eta_{n}:=n\eta_{\Lambda}+\eta|_{B_{n}^{c}},

then for any

,
\eta_{n}
is a Radon measure on
\mathbb{R}
. By the result obtained in Step 1,
we have

\displaystyle v_{(\Lambda_{n},\eta_{n})}^{X}(r,y)=-\log\mathbb{E}_{\delta_{y}}\left(\exp\left\{-\int Y_{r}(z)\eta_{n}(\mathrm{d}z)\right\}\right)

\displaystyle=-\log\mathbb{E}_{\delta_{y}}\left(\exp\left\{-n\int_{\Lambda}Y_{r}(z)\eta_{\Lambda}(\mathrm{d}z)-\int_{B_{n}^{c}}Y_{r}(z)\eta(\mathrm{d}z)\right\}\right).

(2.67)

Since
B_{n}^{c}\uparrow\Lambda^{c}
as
n\to\infty
, combining the dominated convergence theorem and (
2.67
), we see that

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}v_{(\Lambda_{n},\eta_{n})}^{X}(r,y)=-\log\mathbb{E}_{\delta_{y}}\left(1_{\{\int_{\Lambda}Y_{r}(z)\eta_{\Lambda}(z)=0\}}\exp\left\{-\int_{\Lambda^{c}}Y_{r}(z)\eta(\mathrm{d}z)\right\}\right)

\displaystyle=-\log\mathbb{E}_{\delta_{y}}\left(1_{\{Y_{r}(z)=0,\ \forall z\in\Lambda\}}\exp\left\{-\int Y_{r}(z)\eta(\mathrm{d}z)\right\}\right),

(2.68)

where in the last equality
we used the fact that the support of
\eta_{\Lambda}
is equal to
\Lambda
and that the support of
\eta
is a subset of
\Lambda^{c}
.
Therefore, to complete the proof, it suffices to show that
(\Lambda_{n},\eta_{n})
converges
m
-weakly to
(\Lambda,\eta)
.
Now we check the conditions in Definition
2.11
.
For (i), suppose that
\gamma_{(\Lambda,\eta)}(U)=\infty
for an open set
U\subset\mathbb{R}
. If
U\cap\Lambda\neq\emptyset
, then by the definition of
\eta_{\Lambda}
, we can find a Borel set
B\subset U\cap\Lambda

such that
\eta_{\Lambda}(B)>0
. In this case,

\eta_{n}(U)\geq\eta_{n}(B)=n\eta_{\Lambda}(B)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle n\to\infty}}{{\longrightarrow}}\infty.

On the other hand, if
U\cap\Lambda=\emptyset
, then

\eta(U)=\infty=\eta(U\cap\Lambda^{c})=\lim_{n\to\infty}\eta(U\cap B_{n}^{c})\leq\lim_{n\to\infty}\eta_{n}(U),

as desired. Now we check (ii).
Note that
\mathcal{R}_{(\Lambda,\eta)}=\Lambda^{c}
. For
any compact set
K\subset\Lambda^{c}
,
\eta_{n}(K)=\eta(K\cap B_{n}^{c})\leq\eta(K)<\infty
is bounded. Besides, for any
\phi\in C_{c}^{+}(\Lambda^{c})
, by
the monotone convergence theorem,

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\int\phi(x)\gamma_{(\Lambda_{n},\eta_{n})}(\mathrm{d}x)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{B_{n}^{c}}\phi(x)\eta(\mathrm{d}x)=\int_{\Lambda^{c}}\phi(x)\eta(\mathrm{d}x)=\int\phi(x)\gamma_{(\Lambda,\eta)}(\mathrm{d}x),

(2.69)

which implies (ii). This completes the proof of the proposition.

Remark 2.12

We will need the following result later: for any

\displaystyle\limsup_{t\to\infty}t^{\frac{1}{\alpha-1}}\mathbb{E}_{\sqrt{t}y}\left(\exp\left\{-\frac{1}{t^{\frac{1}{\alpha-1}}}\int g\left(\frac{y}{\sqrt{t}}\right)Z_{t}(\mathrm{d}y)\right\}1_{\left\{Z_{t}(A)>0\right\}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq G(\theta)-\log\mathbb{E}_{\delta_{y}}\left(\exp\left\{-\theta\ell(A)Y_{1}(0)-X_{1}(g)\right\}\right)+\log\mathbb{E}_{\delta_{y}}\left(\exp\left\{-X_{1}(g)\right\}\right)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\theta\uparrow\infty}}{{\longrightarrow}}-\log\mathbb{E}_{\delta_{y}}\left(\exp\left\{-X_{1}(g)\right\}1_{\{Y_{1}(0)=0\}}\right)+\log\mathbb{E}_{\delta_{y}}\left(\exp\left\{-X_{1}(g)\right\}\right).

(3.86)

Similarly, we also have that

\displaystyle\liminf_{t\to\infty}t^{\frac{1}{\alpha-1}}\mathbb{E}_{\sqrt{t}y}\left(\exp\left\{-\frac{1}{t^{\frac{1}{\alpha-1}}}\int g\left(\frac{y}{\sqrt{t}}\right)Z_{t}(\mathrm{d}y)\right\}1_{\left\{Z_{t}(A)>0\right\}}\right)

\displaystyle\geq-G(\theta)-\log\mathbb{E}_{\delta_{y}}\left(\exp\left\{-\theta\ell(A)Y_{1}(0)-X_{1}(g)\right\}\right)+\log\mathbb{E}_{\delta_{y}}\left(\exp\left\{-X_{1}(g)\right\}\right)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\theta\uparrow\infty}}{{\longrightarrow}}-\log\mathbb{E}_{\delta_{y}}\left(\exp\left\{-X_{1}(g)\right\}1_{\{Y_{1}(0)=0\}}\right)+\log\mathbb{E}_{\delta_{y}}\left(\exp\left\{-X_{1}(g)\right\}\right).

(3.87)

Combining Theorem
1.4
, (
3.84
) (
3.86
) and (
3.87
), we conclude that

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}\mathbb{E}_{\sqrt{t}y}\left(\exp\left\{-\frac{1}{t^{\frac{1}{\alpha-1}}}\int g\left(\frac{y}{\sqrt{t}}\right)Z_{t}(\mathrm{d}y)\right\}\big|Z_{t}(A)>0\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{-\log\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{y}}(Y_{1}(0)=0)}\left(-\log\mathbb{E}_{\delta_{y}}\left(\exp\left\{-X_{1}(g)\right\}1_{\{Y_{1}(0)=0\}}\right)+\log\mathbb{E}_{\delta_{y}}\left(\exp\left\{-X_{1}(g)\right\}\right)\right).\quad

(3.88)

Combining (
3.80
), (
3.81
) and (
3.88
), we get that

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}\mathbb{E}_{\sqrt{t}y}\left(\exp\left\{-\frac{1}{t^{\frac{1}{\alpha-1}}}\int g\left(\frac{y}{\sqrt{t}}\right)Z_{t}(\mathrm{d}y)\right\}\big|Z_{t}(A)>0\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\mathbb{N}_{y}(Y_{1}(0)>0)}\left(\mathbb{N}_{y}\left(1-e^{-w_{1}(g)}1_{\{Y_{1}(0)=0\}}\right)-\mathbb{N}_{y}\left(1-e^{-w_{1}(g)}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\mathbb{N}_{y}(Y_{1}(0)>0)}\mathbb{N}_{y}\left(e^{-w_{1}(g)}1_{\{Y_{1}(0)>0\}}\right)=\mathbb{N}_{y}\left(e^{-w_{1}(g)}|Y_{1}(0)>0\right),

(3.89)

which implies the desired result.

\Box

4
Proof of Lemma
1.1

Proof of Lemma
1.1
:
Suppose that
Q_{t}
is the transition semigroup of the super Brownian motion, i.e.

Q_{t}(\nu_{1},\mathrm{d}\nu_{2}):=\mathbb{P}_{\nu_{1}}(X_{t}\in\mathrm{d}\nu_{2}).

Let
Q_{t}^{\circ}
be the restriction of
Q_{t}
on
\mathcal{M}_{F}(\mathbb{R})\setminus\{\mathbf{0}\}.
By
[
24
, A.41 or (8.46)]
, for any
y\in\mathbb{R}
,
0<r_{1}<..<r_{m}<\infty
and
\nu_{1},...,\nu_{m}\in\mathcal{M}_{F}(\mathbb{R})\setminus\{\mathbf{0}\}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{N}_{y}\left(w_{r_{1}}\in\mathrm{d}\nu_{1},...,w_{r_{m}}\in\mathrm{d}\nu_{m}\right)=\mathbb{N}_{y}\left(w_{r_{1}}\in\mathrm{d}\nu_{1}\right)Q_{r_{2}-r_{1}}^{\circ}(\nu_{1},\mathrm{d}\nu_{2})\cdots Q_{r_{m}-r_{m-1}}^{\circ}(\nu_{m-1},\mathrm{d}\nu_{m}).

(4.1)

In particular, for any
s<t
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{N}_{y}\left(w_{s}\in\mathcal{M}_{F}(\mathbb{R})\setminus\{\mathbf{0}\},w_{t}\in\mathcal{A}^{c}\right)=\int_{\mathcal{M}_{F}(\mathbb{R})\setminus\{\mathbf{0}\}}\mathbb{N}_{y}(w_{r}\in\mathrm{d}\nu_{1})Q_{t-r}^{\circ}(\nu_{1},\mathcal{A}^{c})

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathcal{M}_{F}(\mathbb{R})\setminus\{\mathbf{0}\}}\mathbb{N}_{y}(w_{r}\in\mathrm{d}\nu_{1})\mathbb{P}_{\nu_{1}}(X_{t-r}\in\mathcal{A}^{c}),

(4.2)

where in the last equality we used the fact that
\mathbf{0}\notin\mathcal{A}^{c}
. Since
\mathbb{P}_{\nu_{1}}(X_{t-r}\in\mathcal{A})=1
for all
\nu_{1}\in\mathcal{M}_{F}(\mathbb{R})
and
t>r
, we obtain that

\displaystyle\mathbb{N}_{y}\left(w_{r}\in\mathcal{M}_{F}(\mathbb{R})\setminus\{\mathbf{0}\},w_{t}\in\mathcal{A}^{c}\right)=0.

(4.3)

Moreover,
w_{t}\in\mathcal{A}^{c}

implies
that
w_{t}\neq\mathbf{0}
, therefore, it must hold that
w_{r}\in\mathcal{M}_{F}(\mathbb{R})\setminus\{\mathbf{0}\}
. Therefore, by (
4.3
), we get that

\displaystyle\mathbb{N}_{y}\left(w_{r}\in\mathcal{M}_{F}(\mathbb{R})\setminus\{\mathbf{0}\},w_{t}\in\mathcal{A}^{c}\right)=\mathbb{N}_{y}\left(w_{t}\in\mathcal{A}^{c}\right)=0,

(4.4)

which implies the desired result.

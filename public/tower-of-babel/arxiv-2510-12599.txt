Quantum thermodynamics of Gross-Pitaevskii qubits
Sebastian Deffner

Abstract
What are the resources that can be leveraged for a thermodynamic device to exhibit genuine quantum advantage? Typically, the answer to this question is sought in quantum correlations. In the present work, we show that quantum Otto engines that operate with nonlinear qubits significantly outperform linear engines. To this end, we develop a comprehensive thermodynamic description of nonlinear qubits starting with identifying the proper thermodynamic equilibrium state. We then show that for ideal cycles as well as at maximum power the efficiency of the nonlinear engine is significantly higher. Interestingly, nonlinear dynamics can be thought of as an effective description of a correlated, complex quantum many body system. Hence, our findings corroborate common wisdom, while at the same time propose a new design of more efficient quantum engines.



I
Introduction

A rather well-known quote credited to Stanilaw Ulam reads
Campbell et al. 1985
,

Using a term like nonlinear science is like referring to the bulk of zoology as the study of non-elephant animals.

In essence, this statement expresses the fact that while we often use linear systems to describe the universe, most phenomena in the real world are nonlinear.

A notable exception to this rule of nature are quantum mechanical systems, for which it can be shown that the dynamics must fundamentally be linear
Jordan 2009
. This fact has been extensively verified in experiments, see for instance Refs.
Walsworth et al. 1990
;
Majumder et al. 1990
;
Forstner et al. 2020
. However, in complex quantum many-body systems, the properties of collective excitations can often be effectively described by non-linear Schrödinger equations
DalFavero et al. 2024
. Among these, the Gross-Pitaevskii equation
Gross 1961
;
Pitaevskii 1961
is probably most widely known. In space representation, it can be written as,

i\hbar\,\dot{\psi}(x,t)=H(x,t)\psi(x,t)+\kappa\left|\psi(x,t)\right|^{2}\psi(x,t)

(1)

where
H(x,t)
is the Hamiltonian, and
\kappa
measures the “strength” of the cooperative quantum effects. In Bose-Einstein condensates,
\kappa
is essentially the scattering length
Gross 1961
;
Pitaevskii 1961
, with
\kappa<0
for repulsive and
\kappa>0
for attractive interactions. However, Eq. (
1
) does not only describe cold quantum gases, but has also found applications in, e.g., nonlinear optics
Rand 2010
and in plasma physics
Ruderman 2002
. Interestingly, Eq. (
1
) has also been generalized to nonlinear Dirac equations
Soler 1970
;
Cooper et al. 2010
;
Mertens et al. 2012
, which describe self-interacting fermions.

More generally, nonlinear wave equations like Eq (
1
) are obtained as meanfield solutions of interacting field theories. For instance, when including more than 2-body interactions one obtains the
Kolomeisky equation
Kolomeisky et al. 2000
with a qunitic nonlinearity. In the limit of Bose liquids, the nonlinear terms even becomes logarithmic in the wave function
Meyer and Wong 2014
.

From the point of view of quantum information processing, nonlinear variants of the Schrödinger equation are very appealing as such dynamics can facilitate processes that are not possible in linear systems, such as, e.g., perfectly distinguishing non-orthogonal states
Childs and Young 2016
or synchronization
Zhang et al. 2020
;
Shen et al. 2023
. Thus, in recent years, significant work has been dedicated to the development of “nonlinear quantum computation”
Meyer and Wong 2014
;
de Lacy et al. 2018
;
Chiew et al. 2019
;
Holmes et al. 2023
;
Geller 2023a
;
Geller 2023b
;
Deiml and Peterseim 2024
. The effectively nonlinear dynamics are then generated by, for instance, coupling Bose-Einstein condensates
Byrnes et al. 2015
;
Xu et al. 2022
;
Geller 2024
;
Großardt 2024
, building qubits from chaotic orbits
Geller 2023c
, intermittent measurements
Kálmán and Kiss 2018
;
Sakaguchi et al. 2023
, or nonlinear optics
Yang et al. 2008
;
Chang et al. 2014
;
Gu et al. 2016
;
Scala et al. 2024
. On the more theoretical side, nonlinear dynamics have been explored in the context of quantum speed limits
del Campo 2021
;
Deffner 2022
, quantum metrology
Beau and del
Campo 2017
;
Deffner 2025
, and shortcuts to adiabaticity
Deffner et al. 2014
;
Chen et al. 2016
;
Zhu et al. 2020
;
Zhu and Chen 2021
;
Zhu et al. 2023
.

In the present analysis, we continue the development of a more comprehensive thermodynamic description of nonlinear qubits that we started in Ref.
Deffner and Campbell 2025
. To this end, we consider the most general qubits that are subject to arbitrary nonlinear dynamics. We consider such qubits as a thermodynamic system and determine the quantum state of the corresponding canonical ensemble. Note that we have seen in previous analyses
Deffner 2025
that the Gibbs state is generally not stationary under the nonlinear dynamics. Hence, the canonical ensemble is not simply given by a Gibbs state, but rather we have to determine the actual quantum state representing thermodynamic equilibrium.

We find that the nonlinear interaction leads to an increase in internal energy and entropy, but decrease in maximal heat capacity (as a function of temperature). We then solve for the efficiency of ideal as well as endorversible Otto cycles, and we find that, for infinitely slow as well as at maximum power in the endoreversible regime, the nonlinear qubit engine outperforms the linear version. Thus, as a main result, we find that nonlinear qubits possess additional resources that can be leveraged in thermodynamic performance.

II
General nonlinear qubits

We start by establishing notions and notations for general qubits undergoing nonlinear dynamics. In the following, we consider quantum dynamics described by the nonlinear Schrödinger equation
Childs and Young 2016

i\,\partial_{t}\left|\psi_{t}\right\rangle=H_{t}\,\left|\psi_{t}\right\rangle+K\left|\psi_{t}\right\rangle

(2)

where
H_{t}
is the usual, Hermitian, time-dependent Hamiltonian. To avoid clutter in the formulas, we will work in units for which
\hbar=1
. Further,
K
describes a general nonlinearity of the form

\left\langle x\right|\left(K\left|\psi_{t}\right\rangle\right)=\mathcal{K}(|\left\langle x|\psi_{t}\right\rangle|)\,\left\langle x|\psi_{t}\right\rangle\,.

(3)

Almost all nonlinear quantum dynamics of relevance can be written in this form. For instance, for the Gross-Pitaeveskii equation
Gross 1961
;
Pitaevskii 1961
we simply have
\mathcal{K}(|\left\langle x|\psi_{t}\right\rangle|)=\kappa\,|\left\langle x|\psi_{t}\right\rangle|^{2}
, and for the Kolomeisky equation
Kolomeisky et al. 2000
,
\mathcal{K}(|\left\langle x|\psi_{t}\right\rangle|)=\kappa\,|\left\langle x|\psi_{t}\right\rangle|^{4}
.

Hamiltonian and nonlinear dynamics

We now consider the most general qubit Hamiltonian

H=\vec{B}\cdot\vec{\sigma}

(4)

where
\vec{B}=(\xi,\chi,\zeta)
is the magnetic field, and
\vec{\sigma}=(\sigma_{x},\sigma_{y},\sigma_{z})
is the Pauli vector.

In the following, it will be convenient to write the quantum state
\left|\psi_{t}\right\rangle
in its Bloch representation,

\rho_{t}=\left|\psi_{t}\right\rangle\left\langle\psi_{t}\right|=\frac{1}{2}\left(\mathbb{I}_{2}+\vec{r}_{t}\cdot\vec{\sigma}\right)\,,

(5)

where
\vec{r}_{t}=(x_{t},y_{t},z_{t})
is the Bloch vector. Note that for pure states
|\vec{r}|=1
. It is worth emphasizing that the nonlinear Schrödinger equation (
2
) is purity preserving, i.e., if
|\vec{r}_{0}|=1
then
|\vec{r}_{t}|=1
for all
t
. Note that the corresponding propagator is
not
unitary, since the dynamics is
not
linear.

In the Bloch representation (
5
), the non-linear term,
\mathcal{K}
, drastically simplifies. Since
|\left\langle 0|\psi_{t}\right\rangle|^{2}=(1+z_{t})/2
and
|\left\langle 1|\psi_{t}\right\rangle|^{2}=(1-z_{t})/2
,
\mathcal{K}_{t}
can be written as an effectively state-dependent Hamiltonian
Childs and Young 2016
,

\mathcal{K}=\begin{pmatrix}\kappa\left(\sqrt{(1+z_{t})/2}\right)&0\\
0&\kappa\left(\sqrt{(1-z_{t})/2}\right)\,.\end{pmatrix}

(6)

Observe that
\mathcal{K}
is diagonal in the Bloch representation.

It is then a simple exercise to show that the non-linear Schrödinger equation (
2
) can be written as

\displaystyle\dot{x}_{t}

\displaystyle=-\left[2\zeta+\tilde{\kappa}(z_{t})\right]\,y_{t}+2\xi z_{t}

(7)

\displaystyle\dot{y}_{t}

\displaystyle=\quad\left[2\zeta+\tilde{\kappa}(z_{t})\right]x_{t}-2\chi z_{t}

\displaystyle\dot{z}_{t}

\displaystyle=-2\chi x_{t}+2\xi y_{t}\,.

where
\tilde{\kappa}(z_{t})\equiv\kappa(\sqrt{(1+z_{t})/2})-\kappa(\sqrt{(1-z_{t})/2})
.

Stationary states

In the present analysis, we are interested in the thermodynamic properties of nonlinear qubits. The only states that can be fully characterized by means of thermodynamics are stationary, equilibrium states. Therefore, we now continue by determining the stationary solutions of Eq. (
7
),

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=-\left[2\zeta+\tilde{\kappa}(z)\right]\,y+2\xi z

(8)

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\quad\left[2\zeta+\tilde{\kappa}(z)\right]x-2\chi z

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=-2\chi x+2\xi y\,.

We observe that Eqs. (
8
) are an under-determined system, which can equivalently be written as,

x=\frac{2\xi\,z}{2\zeta+\tilde{\kappa}(z)}\quad\text{and}\quad y=\frac{2\chi\,z}{2\zeta+\tilde{\kappa}(z)}\,.

(9)

Thus, for all stationary states the length of the Bloch vector becomes,

|\vec{r}|\equiv r=|z|\,\sqrt{1+\frac{4\,(\xi^{2}+\chi^{2})}{[2\zeta+\tilde{\kappa}(z)]^{2}}}\,,

(10)

which still explicitly depends on the
z
component. In the next section, we will now determine the values of
z
, or rather the stationary quantum states that correspond to thermodynamic equilibrium in the canonical representation.

III
Canonical equilibrium state

For any quantum state
\rho
, the energy is given by the expectation value of the Hamiltonian. For the present case, we can write,

E=\mathrm{tr}\left\{H\rho\right\}=\vec{B}\cdot\vec{r}=\frac{2B^{2}+\zeta\tilde{\kappa}(z)}{2\zeta+\tilde{\kappa}(z)}\,z\,,

(11)

where we introduced
B\equiv|\vec{B}|=\sqrt{\xi^{2}+\chi^{2}+\zeta^{2}}
.

Since we are interested in stationary, equilibrium states, the thermodynamic entropy is given by the corresponding von Neumann entropy,
S=-\mathrm{tr}\left\{\rho\ln{\rho}\right\}
, which only depends on the length of the Bloch vector (
10
),

S=\ln(2)-\frac{1}{2}\ln\left(1-r^{2}\right)-r\,\mathrm{arctanh}(r)\,,

(12)

where we also set
k_{B}=1
to avoid clutter. Note that for arbitrary, nonequilibrrium states,
S
, only quantifies the information content that is related, but identical to the thermodynamic entropy. In the present analysis, we only consider stationary, equilibrium states, and hence the identification (
12
) is sound.

As usual, this thermodynamic entropy is maximal in equilibrium. In the canonical representation, we can then determine the quantum state
\rho
that has the maximal entropy given that the energy (
11
) is known and constant. Employing standard variational calculus we write

0=\delta\left(S-\beta E\right)=\delta z\,\left(\frac{\partial S}{\partial z}-\beta\frac{\partial E}{\partial z}\right)\,,

(13)

where
\beta
is a Langrange multiplier which can be identified with the inverse temperature
\beta=1/T

1
1

1

Note that Eq. (
13
) implies
\beta=\partial S/\partial E
, which is identical to the thermodynamic definition of temperature
Callen 1985
.
.

While Eq. (
13
) is formally identical to standard problems in thermodynamics, here the resulting equations are nonlinear. Therefore, analytical solutions can only be found in very special cases and generally one has to rely to numerical treatments.

Linear qubits

To demonstrate the principles and build intuition, we briefly review the linear case,i.e.,
\tilde{\kappa}(z)=0
. In this case, the internal energy (
11
) becomes

E_{\mathrm{lin}}=\frac{z}{\zeta}\,B^{2}\,,

(14)

and the length of the Bloch vector (
10
) can be written as

r_{\mathrm{lin}}=\frac{|z|}{|\zeta|}\,B\,,

(15)

Correspondingly, the equilibrium state is determined from Eq. (
13
) as a solution of

0=\beta B^{2}+B\,\mathrm{arctanh}\left(B\,z/\zeta\right)\,,

(16)

and hence

z=-\frac{\zeta}{B}\,\tanh{\left(\beta B\right)}\,.

(17)

Note that this solution represents nothing but the Gibbs state,
\rho=\exp{(-\beta H})/Z
, and as a result, one finds the known expression of the internal energy,

E_{\mathrm{lin}}=-B\,\tanh{\left(\beta B\right)}

(18)

Note that the sign is determined by the direction of the magnetic field. Shortly, it will also be useful express the stationary Bloch vector as

r_{\mathrm{lin}}=|\tanh{\left(\beta B\right)}|\,.

(19)

Gross-Pitaevskii qubits

The mathematical treatment becomes significantly more involved for nonlinear qubits. Since we have seen in previous work that different nonlinearities give rise to different details in the dynamics
Deffner 2022
;
Deffner 2025
, but also that the stationary properties are somewhat generic
Deffner and Campbell 2025
, we will now continue the analysis for a specific choice of
\tilde{\kappa}(z)
. Arguably, the most tractable case is given by nonlinearities of the Gross-Piteaveskii type,
\tilde{\kappa}(z)=gz
.

In this case, the internal energy (
11
) becomes

E_{\mathrm{GPE}}=\frac{2B^{2}+\zeta g\,z}{2\zeta+g\,z}\,z\,,

(20)

and for the Bloch vector we have

r_{\mathrm{GPE}}=\frac{|z|}{|2\zeta+gz|}\,\sqrt{4B^{2}+4\zeta gz+g^{2}z^{2}}\,.

(21)

Even for this “most tractable case”, Eq. (
13
) becomes a rather involved, lengthy equation that can no longer be solved in “neat” form. It is worth emphasizing again that the resulting stationary state of an arbitrary, nonlinear qubit is
not
the Gibbs state. Nevertheless, the qubit is in thermodynamic equilibrium and its corresponding state is the proper “thermal state”.

In Fig.

) for a numerical solution of Eq. (

g
. We observe that
E_{\mathrm{GPE}}
is significantly increased by the presence of the nonlinearity.

The natural question now has to be whether this nonlinear enhancement in internal energy can be leveraged in thermal devices. To this end, we continue the discussion by analyzing quantum Otto engines that operate with such nonlinear qubits as a working medium.

IV
Nonlinear quantum Otto engine

Among all thermodynamic cycles, the Otto cycle is particularly convenient when analyzing quantum engines
Deffner and Campbell 2019
. The ideal Otto cycle consists of four strokes, of which two are isentropic and two are isochoric. Thus, during each stroke the engine exchanges either heat or work with the environment, but never both. This also means that the cycle can be fully described in terms of changes in internal energy, and one does not have to worry about the myriad of notions of “quantum work” that have been put forward
Deffner and Campbell 2019
;
Campbell et al. 2025
.

IV.1
Ideal quantum Otto cycle

We begin with the ideal Otto cycle, in which the qubit, aka the working medium, remains in a state of thermodynamic equilibrium at all times.

Isentropic compression –
A\rightarrow B
:

During the first stroke of the cycle, the working medium is decoupled from the heat reservoirs and hence no heat can be absorbed,
Q_{A\rightarrow B}=0
and
S_{B}=S_{A}
. Therefore, the work is given by the change of internal energy,

W_{A\rightarrow B}=E_{B}-E_{A}\,.

(22)

Note that “compression” of a qubit corresponds to an increase in the magnetic field,
|B_{B}|>|B_{A}|
.

Isochoric heating –
B\rightarrow C
:

During the second, isochoric stroke the magnetic field is held constant,
B_{C}=B_{B}
, and hence no work is performed by the engine,
W_{B\rightarrow C}=0
. Instead, the working medium absorbs heat from the hot environment and we can write,

Q_{B\rightarrow C}=E_{C}-E_{B}\,.

(23)

Isentropic expansion –
C\rightarrow D
:

During the isentropic expansion, the magnetic field returns to its original value,
B_{D}=B_{A}
, while the entropy is held constant,
Q_{C\rightarrow D}=0
and
S_{C}=S_{D}
. Accordingly,

W_{C\rightarrow D}=E_{C}-E_{D}\,.

(24)

Isochoric cooling –
D\rightarrow A
:

Finally, the cycle is closed by an ischoric cooling stroke with

Q_{D\rightarrow A}=E_{D}-E_{A}\,,

(25)

and
W_{D\rightarrow A}=0
.

The more pertinent question is to what extend the efficiency of the cycle is modified in the presence of a nonlinearity. It is easy to see that we have

\eta=-\frac{W_{A\rightarrow B}+W_{C\rightarrow D}}{Q_{B\rightarrow C}}=1-\frac{Q_{D\rightarrow A}}{Q_{B\rightarrow C}}\,,

(26)

which we will now evaluate explicitly for linear and nonlinear engines.

IV.2
Linear Otto engine

Mostly for pedagogical reasons and for the sake of completeness, we briefly review the quantum Otto efficiency for linear qubits, see also Refs.
Quan et al. 2007
. To this end, note that for isentropic strokes we have
r=\mathrm{const.}
, and hence from Eq. (
19
),
\beta_{A}B_{A}=\beta_{B}B_{B}
and
\beta_{C}B_{B}=\beta_{D}B_{A}
. Thus we can immediately write,

\eta=1-\frac{E_{D}-E_{A}}{E_{C}-E_{B}}=1-\frac{B_{A}}{B_{B}}\equiv 1-\mathcal{C},

(27)

where we only used Eq. (
18
).

In complete analogy to Otto engines that operate with classical gases
Callen 1985
;
Smith et al. 2020
, harmonic oscillators
Abah et al. 2012
;
Deffner 2018
;
Myers and Deffner 2020
;
Myers and Deffner 2021
or even relativistic oscillators
Myers et al. 2021a
, the efficiency is given by one minus the compression ratio,
\mathcal{C}\equiv B_{A}/B_{B}
.

V
Endoreversible Otto cycle – efficiency at maximum power

While the above observed increase in ideal efficiency is interesting, we do need to wonder whether it is “real”. The more practically relevant question is whether also the efficiency at maximal power output is enhanced by the presence of nonlinearities in the working medium. We answer this question within the framework of endoreversible thermodynamics
Hoffmann et al. 1997
. In endoreversible thermodynamics one assumes that all processes are slow enough that the system remains in a state of
locally equilibrium
, yet the processes are too fast for the system to reach a state of equilibrium with the environment. More specifically, imagine an engine, whose working medium is at equilibrium at temperature
T
. However,
T
is not equal to the temperature of the heat bath,
T_{\mathrm{bath}}
, and thus there is a temperature gradient at the boundaries of the engine. Now further imagine that the engine undergoes a slow, cyclic state transformation, where slow means that the working medium remains
locally
in equilibrium at all times. Then, from the point of view of the environment the device undergoes an irreversible cycle. Such state transformations are called
endoreversible

Hoffmann et al. 1997
, which means that locally the transformation is reversible, but globally irreversible.

In a seminal work, Curzon and Ahlborn showed
Curzon and Ahlborn 1975
that the efficiency of a Carnot engine undergoing an endoreversible cycle at maximal power is given by,

\eta_{\mathrm{CA}}=1-\sqrt{\frac{T_{c}}{T_{h}}}=1-\sqrt{\frac{\beta_{h}}{\beta_{c}}}\,,

(28)

where
\beta_{c}
and
\beta_{h}
are the inverse temperatures of the cold and hot reservoirs, respectively. Since its discovery the Curzon-Ahlborn efficiency (
28
) has received a great deal of attention. Recently it has been found, e.g., that also endorversible Otto
Deffner 2018
and Brayton
Ferketic and Deffner 2023
engines operating with ideal gases have the same efficiency. However, more generally the efficiency depends on the choice of working medium and the resulting efficiency can be larger
Myers and Deffner 2020
;
Smith et al. 2020
;
Myers et al. 2021b
;
Myers et al. 2021a
;
Myers and Deffner 2021
;
Myers et al. 2022
;
Myers et al. 2023
;
Peña et al. 2023
or smaller
Behrendt and Deffner 2025
than
\eta_{\mathrm{CA}}
.

V.1
Endoreversible quantum Otto cycle

In the present analysis, we focus on quantum Otto engines, of which we developed the endoreversible version in Ref.
Deffner 2018
. This engine cycle has the following four strokes:

Isentropic compression:
A\rightarrow B
:

As stated above, during the isentropic strokes the working qubit does not exchange heat with the environment. Therefore, the thermodynamic state of the working qubit can be considered independent of the environment, and the endoreversible description is identical to the equilibrium cycle, see Eq. (
22
)

Isochoric heating –
B\rightarrow C
:

During the isochoric strokes the magnetic field is held constant, and the system exchanges
only
heat with the environment. For endoreversible cycles, we now assume that the working qubit is in a state of local equilibrium, but also that the working qubit never fully equilibrates with the hot reservoir. Therefore, we can write

\beta(0)=\beta_{B}\quad\mathrm{and}\quad\beta(\tau_{h})=\beta_{C}\quad\mathrm{with}\quad\beta_{B}>\beta_{C}\geq\beta_{h}\,,

(29)

where as before
\tau_{h}
is the duration of the stroke.

The change in temperature from
\beta_{B}
to
\beta_{C}
is governed by Fourier’s law
Callen 1985
,

\frac{d\beta}{dt}=\alpha_{h}\left(\beta(t)-\beta_{h}\right)

(30)

where
\alpha_{h}
is a constant depending on the heat conductivity and heat capacity of the working qubit.

Equation (
30
) can be solved analytically, and we have

\beta_{C}-\beta_{h}=\left(\beta_{B}-\beta_{h}\right)\,\exp{\left(\alpha_{h}\tau_{h}\right)}\,.

(31)

Isentropic expansion –
C\rightarrow D
:

Again, this stroke is identical to the ideal case, since the working medium is not in contact with the thermal environment.

Isochoric cooling –
D\rightarrow A
:

During isochoric cooling, the work again vanishes, and the heat is given by the change in internal energy (
25
). However, once again we have to account for the thermal gradient between working medium and heat reservoir. Thus, we write now,

\beta(0)=\beta_{D}\quad\mathrm{and}\quad\beta(\tau_{c})=\beta_{A}\quad\mathrm{with}\quad\beta_{D}<\beta_{A}\leq\beta_{c}\,.

(32)

In complete analogy to above (
30
), the heat transfer is described by Fourier’s law,

\frac{d\beta}{dt}=\alpha_{c}\left(\beta(t)-\beta_{c}\right)\,,

(33)

where
\alpha_{c}
is a constant characteristic for the cold stroke. As solution of Eq. (
33
) we now obtain,

\beta_{A}-\beta_{c}=\left(\beta_{D}-\beta_{c}\right)\,\exp{\left(\alpha_{c}\tau_{c}\right)}\,,

(34)

which properly describes the decrease in temperature from
\beta_{D}
back to
\beta_{A}
.

Linear qubit engine

We are now interested in the efficiency

P\equiv-\frac{W_{A\rightarrow B}+W_{C\rightarrow D}}{\gamma(\tau_{h}+\tau_{c})}\,,

is maximal. As always, the cycle time is given as a multiple of the strokes times during which the working medium is in contact with the heat reservoirs

\tau_{\mathrm{cyc}}=\gamma(\tau_{h}+\tau_{c})
.

To simplify the expressions we set
\alpha_{h}=\alpha_{c}=\alpha
and
\tau_{h}=\tau_{c}=\tau
. Further using again
\beta_{A}B_{A}=\beta_{B}B_{B}
and
\beta_{C}B_{B}=\beta_{D}B_{A}

P=\frac{\mathcal{C}-1}{\mathcal{C}}\frac{B_{A}}{2\gamma\tau}\left[\mathrm{tanh}\left(\beta_{A}B_{A}\right)-\mathrm{tanh}\left(\beta_{D}B_{A}\right)\right]\,,

(36)

which still depends on the qubit temperatures
\beta_{A}
and
\beta_{D}

\beta_{A}=\frac{e^{\alpha\tau}\,\beta_{h}+\mathcal{C}\,\beta_{c}}{\mathcal{C}\,(1+e^{\alpha\tau})}\quad\mathrm{and}\quad\beta_{D}=\frac{\beta_{h}+\mathcal{C}\,e^{\alpha\tau}\,\beta_{c}}{\mathcal{C}\,(1+e^{\alpha\tau})}\,.

(37)

Substituting the latter into Eq. (
36
) leads to a somewhat lengthy expression for the power
P

\mathcal{C}
, and the hot and cold temperatures,
\beta_{h}
and
\beta_{c}
.

The resulting expression can then be maximized as a function of
\mathcal{C}
, from which we obtain the efficiency at maximum power with Eq. (
27
). The result is plotted in Fig.

V.3
Endoreversible Gross-Pitaevskii engine

To conclude the analysis, we now also determine the efficiency at maximum power of an Otto engine with Gross-Piteavskii qubits as working medium. Solving this problem in full generality is a mathematically very involved problem. First, the adiabaticity relations have to be solved,
S_{A}=S_{B}
and
S_{C}=S_{D}

\beta_{A}(\beta_{B};\,B_{A},B_{B})
and
\beta_{C}(\beta_{D};\,B_{A},B_{B})

z

13
), from which we then can obtain the nonlinear generalization of expression (
36
). Since all of this has to be done numerically, the gained insight is somewhat limited.

More direct insight can be gained for weak nonlinarities,
g\ll 1

20

E_{\mathrm{GPE}}\simeq\frac{z}{\zeta}\,B^{2}-g\,\frac{z^{2}\left(\xi^{2}+\chi^{2}\right)}{\zeta^{2}}+\mathcal{O}(g^{2})\,.

(38)

Correspondingly, we can determine the correction to the power (

VI
Concluding remarks

In the present analysis we have found that thermodynamic devices built with nonlinear qubits can significantly outperform linear devices. To this end, we have developed a comprehensive thermodynamics of general nonlinear qubits, including the quantum state corresponding to canonical equilibrium, internal energy, entropy, heat capacity, as well as the efficiency of ideal and endoreversible Otto cycles. In particular, for the quantum Otto engine we found that nonlinear device can operate at significantly enhanced efficiency.

The present findings are consistent and complementary to our previous work. We have now found that nonlinear systems evolve faster
Deffner 2022
, permit higher precision in measurements
Deffner 2025
, suppress parasitic excitations
Deffner and Campbell 2025
, and have better thermodynamic performance.

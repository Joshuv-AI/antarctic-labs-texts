When Do You Start Counting? Revisiting Counting and Pnueli Modalities in Timed Logics
Hsi-Ming Ho, Khushraj Madnani

Abstract
Pnueli first noticed that certain simple ‘counting’ properties appear to be inexpressible in popular timed temporal logics such as Metric Interval Temporal Logic ( MITL ). This interesting observation has since been studied extensively, culminating in strong timed logics that are capable of expressing such properties yet remain decidable. A slightly more general case, namely where one asserts the existence of a sequence of events in an arbitrary interval of the form ⟨ a , b ⟩ \langle a,b\rangle (instead of an upper-bound interval of the form [ 0 , b ⟩ [0,b\rangle , which starts from the current point in time), has however not been addressed satisfactorily in the existing literature. We show that counting in [ 0 , b ⟩ [0,b\rangle is in fact as powerful as counting in ⟨ a , b ⟩ \langle a,b\rangle ; moreover, the general property ‘there exist x ′ , x ′′ ∈ I x^{\prime},x^{\prime\prime}\in I such that x ′ ≤ x ′′ x^{\prime}\leq x^{\prime\prime} and ψ ⁡ ( x ′ , x ′′ ) \psi(x^{\prime},x^{\prime\prime}) holds’ can be expressed in Extended Metric Interval Temporal Logic ( EMITL ) with only [ 0 , b ⟩ [0,b\rangle .

Expressiveness.

It is of course trivial to see that
Q2MLO
subsumes
TLC

can express the more general modalities
\counting^{k}_{I}
and their past counterparts, which count event occurrences within arbitrary non-singular intervals

\langle a,b\rangle

along with two quantified instants

In
[
39
]
, it is claimed (without proof) that in the continuous semantics,

extended with such modalities (

(allowing only

By contrast, Krishna
et al.

[

showed that in the pointwise semantics,
\counting^{k}_{I}

with only counting modalities with

In this paper, we reconcile these results and reaffirm the claim, i.e. we prove that

This suggests that

is a very expressive and robust logic in both the pointwise and continuous semantics.
From

\counting^{k}_{I}

I=[0,b\rangle
.

Contributions.

We argue that the folklore

with

one can indeed enforce that a sequence of events specified lies in the required interval;
the proof is based on a generalisation of the

to show that

Conclusion and future work

It turned out that allowing

in counting modalities
only makes them more intricate to express in (aperiodic) automata modalities (or
Q2MLO
), which necessarily ‘start’ from the current point; in other words, the relevant claims in

are indeed correct.
More generally, we have shown that the existence of two ‘witnesses’

This is somewhat surprising, as the timing constraints on both

and

Our second main result gives a satisfactory correction

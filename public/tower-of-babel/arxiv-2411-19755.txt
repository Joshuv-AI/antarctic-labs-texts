Explicit error bounds of the SE and DE formulas for integrals with logarithmic and algebraic singularity
Tomoaki Okayama, Kosei Arakawa, Ryo Kamigaki, Eita Yabumoto

Abstract
The single exponential (SE) and double exponential (DE) formulas are widely recognized as efficient quadrature formulas for evaluating integrals with endpoint singularity. For integrals exhibiting algebraic singularity, explicit error bounds in a computable form have been provided, enabling computations with guaranteed accuracy. Such explicit error bounds have also been provided for integrals exhibiting logarithmic singularity. However, these error bounds have two points to be discussed. The first point is on overestimation of divergence speed of logarithmic singularity. The second point is on the case where there exist both logarithmic and algebraic singularity. To address these issues, this study provides new error bounds for integrals with logarithmic and algebraic singularity. Although existing and new error bounds described above pertain to integrals over the finite interval, the SE and DE formulas are also applicable to integrals over the semi-infinite interval. On the basis of the new results, this study provides new error bounds for integrals over the semi-infinite interval with logarithmic and algebraic singularity at the origin.

Introduction and summary

We are concerned with numerical integration of the integral

\int_{0}^{T}f(t)\mathrm{d}{t},

where
T>0
and
f
may have integrable singularity at the endpoints
of the interval.
The single-exponential (SE) and double-exponential (DE) formulas are
widely recognized as efficient quadrature formulas for
evaluating such an integral
Mori (1985)
.
These formulas are derived by combining the SE or DE transformation
with the trapezoidal formula.
In the case of the SE formula,
the procedure begins by applying the SE transformation

t=\psi_{1}(x)=\frac{T}{2}\tanh\left(\frac{x}{2}\right)+\frac{T}{2}

to the given integral as

\int_{0}^{T}f(t)\mathrm{d}{t}=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}f(\psi_{1}(x))\psi_{1}^{\prime}(x)\mathrm{d}{x}.

Next, we apply the trapezoidal formula as

\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}f(\psi_{1}(x))\psi_{1}^{\prime}(x)\mathrm{d}{x}\approx h\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}f(\psi_{1}(kh))\psi_{1}^{\prime}(kh),

where
h
denotes mesh size.
Then, we truncate the infinite sum on the right-hand side at some
integers
M
and
N
.
The final form of the SE formula is expressed as

\int_{0}^{T}f(t)\mathrm{d}{t}\approx h\sum_{k=-M}^{N}f(\psi_{1}(kh))\psi_{1}^{\prime}(kh),

which is also referred to as the tanh rule.
After the formula was proposed
Schwartz (1971)
,
Takahasi and Mori
Takahasi and Mori (1974)

derived another formula by replacing the SE transformation with

t=\phi_{1}(x)=\frac{T}{2}\tanh\left(\frac{\uppi}{2}\sinh x\right)+\frac{T}{2},

which is referred to as the DE transformation.
The final form of the DE formula is expressed as

\int_{0}^{T}f(t)\mathrm{d}{t}\approx h\sum_{k=-M}^{N}f(\phi_{1}(kh))\phi_{1}^{\prime}(kh).

Computable error bounds of the two formulas have been
provided
Okayama et al. (2013)
in the case where
the integrand
f
has algebraic singularity at the endpoints as

\int_{0}^{T}\frac{g(t)}{t^{1-\alpha}(T-t)^{1-\beta}}\mathrm{d}{t},

(1.1)

where
g
is bounded, and
\alpha
and
\beta
are positive constants.
The result has been utilized for the computation library
of verified numerical integration
Yamanaka et al. (2010)
.

In the case where the integrand
f
has logarithmic singularity as

\int_{0}^{T}g(t)\log t\,\mathrm{d}{t},

(1.2)

computable error bounds of the two formulas have also been
provided
Okayama (2014)
.
However, two points remain to be discussed regarding these error bounds.
The first point concerns overestimation of divergence speed of
logarithmic singularity. To derive the error bounds,
the following inequality

|\log z|\leq\frac{C}{|z|^{1/(2\uppi)}}

(1.3)

was proved with some constant
C
,
and the existing result for (
1.1
)
was utilized with
\alpha=1-1/(2\uppi)
and
\beta=1
.
Even though the inequality (
1.3
) is
mathematically correct,
the right-hand side is unnecessarily large near the origin,
because the divergence speed of algebraic singularity
is essentially higher than that of logarithmic singularity.
Such an overestimation may result in non-sharp error bounds.
The second point concerns the case where the integrand
f
has
both logarithmic and algebraic singularity, such as

\int_{0}^{T}\frac{g(t)}{t^{1-\alpha}(T-t)^{1-\beta}}\log t\,\mathrm{d}{t}.

(1.4)

The provided error bounds for (
1.2
)
cannot handle the case as (
1.4
),
because it is assumed that the function
g(t)
is bounded.

The first contribution of this study is to provide
computable error bounds of the SE and DE formulas
in the case (
1.4
),
to address the aforementioned two points.
Rather than utilizing the error bounds for (
1.1
),
we perform the error analysis
for the case (
1.4
) directly.
In this direct analysis, in contrast to (
1.3
),
we estimate
|\log z|
without changing the divergence rate
(see Lemmas
4.3

and
4.8

for the estimate of
|\log\psi_{1}(x+\ii y)|

and
|\log\phi_{1}(x+\ii y)|
).
This is key to improving the error bound.
However, this direct analysis requires considerable argument
beyond the estimate of
|\log z|
,
which makes this project challenging.
Completion of all necessary proofs for the analysis
is an important contribution of this paper.
Furthermore, especially for the DE formula,
the selection formulas of
h
,
M
and
N

have room for improvement,
because they are not optimally selected
in the previous work
Okayama (2014)
.
In this study,
we employ the optimal selection formulas of
h
,
M
and
N

for the DE formula
Okayama and Kawai (2023)
.
Consequently, we obtain not only sharper error bounds
but also improved convergence profiles, as demonstrated in
numerical experiments.

The second contribution of this study is to provide
computable error bounds in the case of the semi-infinite interval as

\int_{0}^{\infty}f(t)\log t\,\mathrm{d}{t},

where
f
may have algebraic singularity at the origin.
Two types of the SE and DE formulas were proposed
depending on the decay rate of

.
When

decays algebraically as

,
the SE and DE formulas

;

are expressed as

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}f(t)\log t\,\mathrm{d}{t}

\displaystyle\approx h\sum_{k=-M}^{N}f(\psi_{2}(kh))\log(\psi_{2}(kh))\psi_{2}^{\prime}(kh),

(1.5)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}f(t)\log t\,\mathrm{d}{t}

\displaystyle\approx h\sum_{k=-M}^{N}f(\phi_{2}(kh))\log(\phi_{2}(kh))\phi_{2}^{\prime}(kh),

(1.6)

where
\psi_{2}
and
\phi_{2}
denote the SE and DE transformations
defined by

\displaystyle\psi_{2}(x)

\displaystyle=\ee^{x},

\displaystyle\phi_{2}(x)

\displaystyle=\ee^{(\uppi/2)\sinh x},

respectively.
When

decays exponentially as

,
the SE formula
Okayama and
Machida (2017)
is derived
by replacing
\psi_{2}

\displaystyle\psi_{3}(x)

\displaystyle=\log(1+\ee^{x}),

\displaystyle\phi_{3}(x)

\displaystyle=\log(1+\ee^{\uppi\sinh x}).

For both types, we provide computable error bounds of the SE and DE

2
Existing and new results

The trapezoidal formula, which is employed in both
the SE and DE formulas, works accurately if the integrand
is analytic on the strip region

\mathscr{D}_{d}=\{\zeta\in\mathbb{C}:\lvert\operatorname{Im}\zeta\rvert<d\},

where
d>0
. Therefore, in the following theorems,
the given integrand
f
is assumed to be analytic
on the translated domain by the SE or DE transformation,
denoted by

\displaystyle\psi_{i}(\mathscr{D}_{d})

\displaystyle=\left\{z=\psi_{i}(\zeta):\zeta\in\mathscr{D}_{d}\right\}\quad(i=1,\,2,\,3),

\displaystyle\phi_{i}(\mathscr{D}_{d})

\displaystyle=\left\{z=\phi_{i}(\zeta):\zeta\in\mathscr{D}_{d}\right\}\quad(i=1,\,2,\,3),

respectively.
Using the notions, we summarize existing and new error bounds below.

Remark 1
.

The gap between the observed error and theoretical error bound
is mainly due to the terms involving
d
in the constant
C
,
such as
1/\cos(d/2)
(which becomes large when
d
is close to
\uppi
)
and
1/\cos((\uppi/2)\sin d)
(which becomes large when
d
is close to
\uppi/2
).
Reducing the value of
d
can decrease this gap.
However, doing so worsens the convergence rate,
because
d
directly affects
the exponential term of the convergence rate,
such as
\ee^{-\sqrt{2\uppi d\mu n}}

or
\ee^{-2\uppi dn/\arsinh(2dn/\mu)}

To obtain a well-balanced error bound,

This remains an open topic for future research.

–

.

4.1
In the case of the finite interval

Here, we prove theorems in the case of the finite interval,
i.e., Theorems
2.3
(for the SE formula)
and
2.4
(for the DE formula).

4.1.1
Proof of Theorem
2.3

We begin by analyzing the error of the SE formula.
Applying the SE transformation
t=\psi_{1}(x)
and
putting
F(x)=f(\psi_{1}(x))\psi_{1}^{\prime}(x)
,
we rewrite the error of the SE formula as

\left|\int_{0}^{T}f(t)\mathrm{d}{t}-h\sum_{k=-M}^{N}f(\psi_{1}(kh))\psi_{1}^{\prime}(kh)\right|=\left|\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}F(x)\mathrm{d}{x}-h\sum_{k=-M}^{N}F(kh)\right|.

We now divide the right-hand side into two terms as

\displaystyle\left|\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}F(x)\mathrm{d}{x}-h\sum_{k=-M}^{N}F(kh)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}F(x)\mathrm{d}{x}-h\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}F(kh)\right|

\displaystyle\quad+\left|h\sum_{k=-\infty}^{-M-1}F(kh)+h\sum_{k=N+1}^{\infty}F(kh)\right|.

The first and second terms are called
the discretization and truncation errors, respectively.
The following function space is important
to analyze the discretization error.

Definition 1
.

Let
d
be a positive constant.
Then,
\mathbf{B}(\mathscr{D}_{d})
denotes the family of all functions
F

that are analytic on
\mathscr{D}_{d}
, such that

\lim_{x\to\pm\infty}\int_{-d}^{d}|F(x+\ii y)|\mathrm{d}{y}=0,

(4.1)

and such that
\mathcal{N}(F,d)<\infty
, where

\mathcal{N}(F,d)=\lim_{y\to d-0}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left\{|F(x+\ii y)|+|F(x-\ii y)|\right\}\mathrm{d}{x}.

If the integrand
F
belongs to
\mathbf{B}(\mathscr{D}_{d})
,
the discretization error is bounded as follows.

Theorem 4.1

(classical; cf. Stenger
(
Stenger, 1981
, Theorem 3.4)
)
.

Assume that
F\in\mathbf{B}(\mathscr{D}_{d})
. Then, it holds that

\left|\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}F(x)\mathrm{d}{x}-h\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}F(kh)\right|\leq\mathcal{N}(F,d)\frac{\ee^{-2\uppi d/h}}{1-\ee^{-2\uppi d/h}}.

(4.2)

Therefore, our main task is to show
F\in\mathbf{B}(\mathscr{D}_{d})
.
To this end, the following bounds are required.

Lemma 4.2

(Okayama et al.
(
Okayama et al., 2013
, Lemma 4.21)
)
.

For all real numbers
x
and
y
with
|y|<\uppi
,
it holds that

\displaystyle\left|\frac{1}{1+\ee^{x+\ii y}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{(1+\ee^{x})\cos(y/2)},

\displaystyle\left|\frac{1}{1+\ee^{-(x+\ii y)}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{(1+\ee^{-x})\cos(y/2)}.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
T
be a positive real number.
For all real numbers
x
and
y
with
|y|<\uppi
,
it holds that

\displaystyle\left|\log\left(\frac{T}{1+\ee^{-(x+\ii y)}}\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq|\log T|+\frac{1}{\cos(y/2)}\log(1+\ee^{-x}).

Proof.

First, it holds that

\displaystyle\left|\log\left(\frac{T}{1+\ee^{-(x+\ii y)}}\right)\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\log T-\log(1+\ee^{-(x+\ii y)})\right|

\displaystyle\leq|\log T|+|\log(1+\ee^{-(x+\ii y)})|.

Furthermore, noting
(\log(1+\ee^{-\zeta}))^{\prime}=-1/(1+\ee^{\zeta})

and using Lemma
4.2
,
we have

\displaystyle|\log(1+\ee^{-(x+\ii y)})|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{x}^{\infty}\frac{1}{1+\ee^{t+\ii y}}\mathrm{d}{t}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{x}^{\infty}\frac{1}{|1+\ee^{t+\ii y}|}\mathrm{d}{t}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\cos(y/2)}\int_{x}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(1+\ee^{t})}\mathrm{d}{t}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\cos(y/2)}\log(1+\ee^{-x}),

from which we obtain the desired inequality.
∎

Using these bounds, we show the following lemma.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
K
,
\alpha
,
\beta
and
d
be positive constants with
d<\uppi
.
Assume that
f
is analytic on
\psi_{1}(\mathscr{D}_{d})
, and
satisfies (
2.2
) for all
z\in\psi_{1}(\mathscr{D}_{d})
.
Let
\mu=\min\{\alpha,\beta\}
. Then, putting

F(x)=f(\psi_{1}(x))\psi_{1}^{\prime}(x)
, we have (
4.2
),
where

\mathcal{N}(F,d)\leq\frac{2KT^{\alpha+\beta-1}}{\mu\cos^{\alpha+\beta}(d/2)}\left\{2|\log T|+\left(2\log 2+\frac{1}{\mu}\right)\frac{1}{\cos(d/2)}\right\}.

Proof.

It suffices to show that
F\in\mathbf{B}(\mathscr{D}_{d})
.
Because
f(\psi_{1}(\cdot))
is analytic on
\mathscr{D}_{d}
and

\psi_{1}^{\prime}
is analytic on
\mathscr{D}_{\uppi}
,

F
is analytic on
\mathscr{D}_{d}
.
Next, we show (
4.1
).
From (
2.2
)
and Lemmas
4.2

and
4.3
, it holds
for
\zeta=x+\ii y\in\mathscr{D}_{d}
that

\displaystyle|F(\zeta)|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{KT^{\alpha+\beta-1}}{|1+\ee^{-\zeta}|^{\alpha}|1+\ee^{\zeta}|^{\beta}}\left|\log\left(\frac{T}{1+\ee^{-\zeta}}\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{KT^{\alpha+\beta-1}}{(1+\ee^{-x})^{\alpha}\cos^{\alpha}(y/2)(1+\ee^{x})^{\beta}\cos^{\beta}(y/2)}\left(|\log T|+\frac{1}{\cos(y/2)}\log(1+\ee^{-x})\right).

(4.3)

Using this inequality, for
x\geq 0
we have

\displaystyle\int_{-d}^{d}\left|F(x+\ii y)\right|\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\leq\frac{KT^{\alpha+\beta-1}}{(1+\ee^{-x})^{\alpha}(1+\ee^{x})^{\beta}}\int_{-d}^{d}\frac{1}{\cos^{\alpha+\beta}(y/2)}\left(|\log T|+\frac{1}{\cos(y/2)}\log(1+\ee^{-x})\right)\mathrm{d}{y}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{KT^{\alpha+\beta-1}}{(1+\ee^{-x})^{\alpha}(1+\ee^{x})^{\beta}}\int_{-d}^{d}\frac{1}{\cos^{\alpha+\beta}(y/2)}\left(|\log T|+\frac{1}{\cos(y/2)}\log(1+\ee^{-0})\right)\mathrm{d}{y}

\displaystyle\to 0\cdot\int_{-d}^{d}\frac{1}{\cos^{\alpha+\beta}(y/2)}\left(|\log T|+\frac{1}{\cos(y/2)}\cdot\log 2\right)\mathrm{d}{y}\quad(x\to\infty),

and for
x<0
, using
\log(1+\ee^{-x})\leq-x+1
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{-d}^{d}\left|F(x+\ii y)\right|\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\leq\frac{(-x+1)KT^{\alpha+\beta-1}}{(1+\ee^{-x})^{\alpha}(1+\ee^{x})^{\beta}}\int_{-d}^{d}\frac{1}{\cos^{\alpha+\beta}(y/2)}\left(\frac{|\log T|}{(-x+1)}+\frac{1}{\cos(y/2)}\frac{\log(1+\ee^{-x})}{(-x+1)}\right)\mathrm{d}{y}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{(-x+1)KT^{\alpha+\beta-1}}{(1+\ee^{-x})^{\alpha}(1+\ee^{x})^{\beta}}\int_{-d}^{d}\frac{1}{\cos^{\alpha+\beta}(y/2)}\left(\frac{|\log T|}{(-0+1)}+\frac{1}{\cos(y/2)}\cdot 1\right)\mathrm{d}{y}

\displaystyle\to 0\cdot\int_{-d}^{d}\frac{1}{\cos^{\alpha+\beta}(y/2)}\left(|\log T|+\frac{1}{\cos(y/2)}\cdot 1\right)\mathrm{d}{y}\quad(x\to-\infty),

which shows (
4.1
).
Finally, we estimate
\mathcal{N}(F,d)
.
Using (
4.3
),
we have

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left\{|F(x+\ii y)|+|F(x-\ii y)|\right\}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{KT^{\alpha+\beta-1}}{(1+\ee^{-x})^{\alpha}(1+\ee^{x})^{\beta}\cos^{\alpha+\beta}(y/2)}\left(|\log T|+\frac{\log(1+\ee^{-x})}{\cos(y/2)}\right)\mathrm{d}{x}

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{KT^{\alpha+\beta-1}}{(1+\ee^{-x})^{\alpha}(1+\ee^{x})^{\beta}\cos^{\alpha+\beta}(-y/2)}\left(|\log T|+\frac{\log(1+\ee^{-x})}{\cos(-y/2)}\right)\mathrm{d}{x}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2KT^{\alpha+\beta-1}}{\cos^{\alpha+\beta}(d/2)}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(1+\ee^{-x})^{\alpha}(1+\ee^{x})^{\beta}}\left(|\log T|+\frac{\log(1+\ee^{-x})}{\cos(d/2)}\right)\mathrm{d}{x},

where
\cos(d/2)\leq\cos(\pm y/2)
is used,
which holds for
y\in[-d,d]
(note that
d<\uppi
).
Therefore, the inequality remains valid
when taking the limit
y\to d-0
.
For the first term of the integral, using
\mu=\min\{\alpha,\beta\}
,
we have

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{|\log T|}{(1+\ee^{-x})^{\alpha}(1+\ee^{x})^{\beta}}\mathrm{d}{x}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{|\log T|}{(1+\ee^{-x})^{\mu}(1+\ee^{x})^{\mu}}\mathrm{d}{x}

\displaystyle=2\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{|\log T|}{(1+\ee^{-x})^{2\mu}}\ee^{-\mu x}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq 2\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{|\log T|}{(1+0)^{2\mu}}\ee^{-\mu x}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{\mu}|\log T|.

For the second term of the integral, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\cos(d/2)}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{\log(1+\ee^{-x})}{(1+\ee^{-x})^{\alpha}(1+\ee^{x})^{\beta}}\mathrm{d}{x}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\cos(d/2)}\int_{-\infty}^{0}\frac{\ee^{\alpha x}\left\{-x+\log(1+\ee^{x})\right\}}{(1+\ee^{x})^{\alpha+\beta}}\mathrm{d}{x}+\frac{1}{\cos(d/2)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\ee^{-\beta x}\log(1+\ee^{-x})}{(1+\ee^{-x})^{\alpha+\beta}}\mathrm{d}{x}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\cos(d/2)}\int_{-\infty}^{0}\frac{\ee^{\alpha x}\left\{-x+\log(1+\ee^{0})\right\}}{(1+0)^{\alpha+\beta}}\mathrm{d}{x}+\frac{1}{\cos(d/2)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\ee^{-\beta x}\log(1+\ee^{-0})}{(1+0)^{\alpha+\beta}}\mathrm{d}{x}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\alpha\cos(d/2)}\left(\log 2+\frac{1}{\alpha}\right)+\frac{\log 2}{\beta\cos(d/2)}.

Furthermore, using
\mu=\min\{\alpha,\beta\}
, we obtain the conclusion.
∎

Next, we estimate the truncation error.
For this purpose, we use the following result.

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
\alpha
and
\beta
be positive constants.
Let
G_{-}(x)=-x\ee^{\alpha x}

and
G_{+}(x)=x\ee^{-\beta x}
.
Then,
G_{-}
monotonically increases for
x\leq-1/\alpha
,
and
G_{+}
monotonically decreases for
x\geq 1/\beta
.

We bound the truncation error as follows.

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
K
,
\alpha
and
\beta
be positive constants.
Assume that
f
satisfies (
2.2
)
for all
z\in(0,T)
.
Let
\mu=\min\{\alpha,\beta\}
,
let
n
be positive integer,
and
let
M
and
N
be selected by (
2.4
).
Let
Mh\geq 1/\alpha
be satisfied.
Then, putting
F(x)=f(\psi_{1}(x))\psi_{1}^{\prime}(x)
, we have

\displaystyle\left|h\sum_{k=-\infty}^{-M-1}F(kh)+h\sum_{k=N+1}^{\infty}F(kh)\right|\leq\frac{KT^{\alpha+\beta-1}}{\mu}\left\{2|\log T|+2\log 2+\frac{1}{\mu}+nh\right\}\ee^{-\mu nh}.

Proof.

From (
2.2
), it holds that

\displaystyle|F(x)|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{KT^{\alpha+\beta-1}}{(1+\ee^{-x})^{\alpha}(1+\ee^{x})^{\beta}}\left|\log\left(\frac{T}{1+\ee^{-x}}\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{KT^{\alpha+\beta-1}}{(1+\ee^{-x})^{\alpha}(1+\ee^{x})^{\beta}}\left\{|\log T|+\log(1+\ee^{-x})\right\},

which is also obtained by substituting
y=0
into (
4.3
).
Using this inequality, we have

\displaystyle\left|h\sum_{k=-\infty}^{-M-1}F(kh)+h\sum_{k=N+1}^{\infty}F(kh)\right|

\displaystyle\leq h\sum_{k=-\infty}^{-M-1}\frac{KT^{\alpha+\beta-1}}{(1+\ee^{-kh})^{\alpha}(1+\ee^{kh})^{\beta}}\left\{|\log T|+\log(1+\ee^{-kh})\right\}

\displaystyle\quad+h\sum_{k=N+1}^{\infty}\frac{KT^{\alpha+\beta-1}}{(1+\ee^{-kh})^{\alpha}(1+\ee^{kh})^{\beta}}\left\{|\log T|+\log(1+\ee^{-kh})\right\}

\displaystyle=h\sum_{k=-\infty}^{-M-1}\frac{KT^{\alpha+\beta-1}\ee^{\alpha kh}}{(1+\ee^{kh})^{\alpha+\beta}}\left\{|\log T|+(-kh)+\log(1+\ee^{kh})\right\}

\displaystyle\quad+h\sum_{k=N+1}^{\infty}\frac{KT^{\alpha+\beta-1}\ee^{-\beta kh}}{(1+\ee^{-kh})^{\alpha+\beta}}\left\{|\log T|+\log(1+\ee^{-kh})\right\}

\displaystyle\leq h\sum_{k=-\infty}^{-M-1}\frac{KT^{\alpha+\beta-1}\ee^{\alpha kh}}{(1+0)^{\alpha+\beta}}\left\{|\log T|+(-kh)+\log(1+\ee^{0})\right\}

\displaystyle\quad+h\sum_{k=N+1}^{\infty}\frac{KT^{\alpha+\beta-1}\ee^{-\beta kh}}{(1+0)^{\alpha+\beta}}\left\{|\log T|+\log(1+\ee^{-0})\right\}.

Using Proposition
4.5
,
for
Mh\geq 1/\alpha
, we bound the first sum as

\displaystyle h\sum_{k=-\infty}^{-M-1}KT^{\alpha+\beta-1}\left\{|\log T|+(-kh)+\log 2\right\}\ee^{\alpha kh}

\displaystyle\leq KT^{\alpha+\beta-1}\int_{-\infty}^{-Mh}\left\{|\log T|+(-x)+\log 2\right\}\ee^{\alpha x}\mathrm{d}{x}

\displaystyle=\frac{KT^{\alpha+\beta-1}}{\alpha}\left(|\log T|+Mh+\log 2+\frac{1}{\alpha}\right)\ee^{-\alpha Mh}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{KT^{\alpha+\beta-1}}{\mu}\left(|\log T|+Mh+\log 2+\frac{1}{\mu}\right)\ee^{-\alpha Mh},

where
\mu=\min\{\alpha,\beta\}
is used
at the last inequality.
Similarly, we bound the second term as

\displaystyle h\sum_{k=N+1}^{\infty}KT^{\alpha+\beta-1}\left(|\log T|+\log 2\right)\ee^{-\beta kh}

\displaystyle\leq KT^{\alpha+\beta-1}\left(|\log T|+\log 2\right)\int_{Nh}^{\infty}\ee^{-\beta x}\mathrm{d}{x}

\displaystyle=KT^{\alpha+\beta-1}\left(|\log T|+\log 2\right)\cdot\frac{1}{\beta}\ee^{-\beta Nh}

\displaystyle\leq KT^{\alpha+\beta-1}\left(|\log T|+\log 2\right)\cdot\frac{1}{\mu}\ee^{-\beta Nh}.

The final task to obtain the conclusion is
showing
Mh\leq nh
,

\ee^{-\alpha Mh}\leq\ee^{-\mu nh}
and

\ee^{-\beta Nh}\leq\ee^{-\mu nh}
.
Using (
2.4
), we have
M\leq n
because
both
M
and
n
are integers that satisfy

Therefore, we have
Mh\leq nh
.
Furthermore, using (
2.4
),
we have
\ee^{-\alpha Mh}\leq\ee^{-\mu nh}
because

\alpha M=\alpha\left\lceil\frac{\mu}{\alpha}n\right\rceil\geq\alpha\cdot\frac{\mu}{\alpha}n=\mu n.

In the same manner, using (
2.4
),
we have
\ee^{-\beta Nh}\leq\ee^{-\mu nh}

because
\beta N\geq\mu n
,
which completes the proof.
∎

We are now in a position to prove Theorem

.
Note that if

,
then with

),

and

Lemma 4.7

(Okayama et al.
(

, Lemma 4.22)
)
.

For all real numbers
x
and
y

,

\displaystyle\left|\frac{1}{1+\ee^{\uppi\sinh(x+\ii y)}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{(1+\ee^{\uppi\sinh(x)\cos y})\cos((\uppi/2)\sin y)},

\displaystyle\left|\frac{1}{1+\ee^{-\uppi\sinh(x+\ii y)}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{(1+\ee^{-\uppi\sinh(x)\cos y})\cos((\uppi/2)\sin y)}.

Lemma 4.8
.

Let
T
be a positive real number.
For all real numbers
x
and
y
with
|y|<\uppi/2
,
it holds that

\displaystyle\left|\log\left(\frac{T}{1+\ee^{-\uppi\sinh(x+\ii y)}}\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq|\log T|+\frac{1}{\cos((\uppi/2)\sin y)\cos y}\log(1+\ee^{-\uppi\sinh(x)\cos y}).

Proof.

First, it holds that

\displaystyle\left|\log\left(\frac{T}{1+\ee^{-\uppi\sinh(x+\ii y)}}\right)\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\log T-\log(1+\ee^{-\uppi\sinh(x+\ii y)})\right|

\displaystyle\leq|\log T|+|\log(1+\ee^{-\uppi\sinh(x+\ii y)})|.

Furthermore, noting

(\log(1+\ee^{-\uppi\sinh\zeta}))^{\prime}=-\uppi\cosh\zeta/(1+\ee^{\uppi\sinh\zeta})

and using Lemma

,
we have

\displaystyle|\log(1+\ee^{-\uppi\sinh(x+\ii y)})|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{x}^{\infty}\frac{\uppi\cosh(t+\ii y)}{1+\ee^{\uppi\sinh(t+\ii y)}}\mathrm{d}{t}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{x}^{\infty}\frac{\uppi|\cosh(t+\ii y)|}{|1+\ee^{\uppi\sinh(t+\ii y)}|}\mathrm{d}{t}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{x}^{\infty}\frac{\uppi\cosh t}{(1+\ee^{\uppi\sinh(t)\cos y})\cos((\uppi/2)\sin y)}\mathrm{d}{t}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\cos((\uppi/2)\sin y)\cos y}\log(1+\ee^{-\uppi\sinh(x)\cos y}),

from which we obtain the desired inequality.
∎

Using these bounds, we show the following lemma.

Lemma 4.9
.

Let
K
,
\alpha
,
\beta
and
d
be positive constants with
d<\uppi/2
.
Assume that
f
is analytic on
\phi_{1}(\mathscr{D}_{d})
, and
satisfies (
2.2
) for all

.
Let
\mu=\min\{\alpha,\beta\}
. Then, putting

F(x)=f(\phi_{1}(x))\phi_{1}^{\prime}(x)
, we have (
4.2
),
where

\mathcal{N}(F,d)\leq\frac{2KT^{\alpha+\beta-1}}{\mu\cos^{\alpha+\beta}((\uppi/2)\sin d)\cos d}\left\{2|\log T|+\frac{2\log 2+(1/\mu)}{\cos((\uppi/2)\sin d)\cos d}\right\}.

Proof.

It suffices to show that
F\in\mathbf{B}(\mathscr{D}_{d})
.
Because
f(\phi_{1}(\cdot))
is analytic on
\mathscr{D}_{d}
and

\phi_{1}^{\prime}
is analytic on
\mathscr{D}_{\uppi/2}
,

F
is analytic on
\mathscr{D}_{d}
.
Next, we show (
4.1
).
From (
2.2
)
and Lemmas
4.7

and
4.8
, it holds
for
\zeta=x+\ii y\in\mathscr{D}_{d}
that

\displaystyle|F(\zeta)|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{KT^{\alpha+\beta-1}\uppi|\cosh\zeta|}{|1+\ee^{-\uppi\sinh\zeta}|^{\alpha}|1+\ee^{\uppi\sinh\zeta}|^{\beta}}\left|\log\left(\frac{T}{1+\ee^{-\uppi\sinh\zeta}}\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{KT^{\alpha+\beta-1}\uppi\cosh x}{(1+\ee^{-\uppi\sinh(x)\cos y})^{\alpha}(1+\ee^{\uppi\sinh(x)\cos y})^{\beta}\cos^{\alpha+\beta}((\uppi/2)\sin y)}

\displaystyle\quad\cdot\left\{|\log T|+\frac{\log(1+\ee^{-\uppi\sinh(x)\cos y})}{\cos((\uppi/2)\sin y)\cos y}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{KT^{\alpha+\beta-1}\uppi\cosh x}{(1+\ee^{-\uppi\sinh(x)\cos y})^{\mu}(1+\ee^{\uppi\sinh(x)\cos y})^{\mu}\cos^{\alpha+\beta}((\uppi/2)\sin y)}

\displaystyle\to 0\cdot\int_{-d}^{d}\left(|\log T|+\frac{\log 2}{\cos((\uppi/2)\sin y)\cos y}\right)\mathrm{d}{y}\quad(x\to\infty),

Using (

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left\{|F(x+\ii y)|+|F(x-\ii y)|\right\}\mathrm{d}x

where

Next, we estimate the truncation error.
For this purpose, we use the following result.

Proposition 4.10
.

Let
\alpha
and
\beta
be positive constants.
Let
G_{-}
,
G_{+}
,

\tilde{G}_{-}
, and
\tilde{G}_{+}
be defined by

\displaystyle G_{-}(x)

\displaystyle=-\sinh(x)\cosh(x)\ee^{\uppi\alpha\sinh x},

\displaystyle G_{+}(x)

\displaystyle=\sinh(x)\cosh(x)\ee^{-\uppi\beta\sinh x},

\displaystyle\tilde{G}_{-}(x)

\displaystyle=\cosh(x)\ee^{\uppi\alpha\sinh x},

\displaystyle\tilde{G}_{+}(x)

\displaystyle=\cosh(x)\ee^{-\uppi\beta\sinh x}.

Then,
G_{-}

and
\tilde{G}_{-}

monotonically increase for
x\leq-\arsinh(2/(\uppi\alpha))
,
and
G_{+}
and
\tilde{G}_{+}

monotonically decrease for
x\geq\arsinh(2/(\uppi\beta))
.

Proof.

From
x\leq-\arsinh(2/(\uppi\alpha))
,

\uppi\alpha\sinh x\leq-2
holds,
from which we have

1+\uppi\alpha\sinh x\leq-1<-\frac{\sinh^{2}x}{\cosh^{2}x}.

From the inequality,

\sinh^{2}x+(1+\uppi\alpha\sinh x)\cosh^{2}x<0
holds,
from which we have

G_{-}^{\prime}(x)=-\left\{\sinh^{2}x+(1+\uppi\alpha\sinh x)\cosh^{2}x\right\}\ee^{\uppi\alpha\sinh x}>0.

Thus, the claim on the function
G_{-}
follows.
Similarly, from
x\geq\arsinh(2/(\uppi\beta))
,

\uppi\beta\sinh x\geq 2
holds,
from which we have

1-\uppi\beta\sinh x\leq-1<-\frac{\sinh^{2}x}{\cosh^{2}x}.

From the inequality,

\sinh^{2}x+(1-\uppi\beta\sinh x)\cosh^{2}x<0
holds,
from which we have

G_{+}^{\prime}(x)=\left\{\sinh^{2}x+(1-\uppi\beta\sinh x)\cosh^{2}x\right\}\ee^{-\uppi\beta\sinh x}<0.

Thus, the claim on the function
G_{+}
follows.

Next, we consider
\tilde{G}_{-}
and
\tilde{G}_{+}
.
If
\alpha\geq 1/(2\uppi)
and
\beta\geq 1/(2\uppi)
,
then
\tilde{G}_{-}
monotonically increases for
x\leq 0

and
\tilde{G}_{+}
monotonically decreases for
x\geq 0
.
Therefore, in this case, it is also true that

\tilde{G}_{-}
monotonically increases for
x\leq-\arsinh(2/(\uppi\alpha))

and
\tilde{G}_{+}
monotonically decreases for

x\geq\arsinh(2/(\uppi\beta))
.
If
\alpha<1/(2\uppi)
and
\beta<1/(2\uppi)
,
then
\tilde{G}_{-}
monotonically increases for
x\leq-x_{\alpha}

and
\tilde{G}_{+}
monotonically decreases for
x\geq x_{\beta}
,
where
x_{\gamma}
is defined for
\gamma\in(0,1/(2\uppi))
by

x_{\gamma}=\arsinh\left(\frac{1+\sqrt{1-(2\uppi\gamma)^{2}}}{2\uppi\gamma}\right).

Note that

x_{\gamma}\leq\arsinh\left(\frac{1+\sqrt{1-0^{2}}}{2\uppi\gamma}\right)=\arsinh\left(\frac{1}{\uppi\gamma}\right)<\arsinh\left(\frac{2}{\uppi\gamma}\right).

Therefore, in this case as well,

\tilde{G}_{-}
monotonically increases for
x\leq-\arsinh(2/(\uppi\alpha))

and
\tilde{G}_{+}
monotonically decreases for

x\geq\arsinh(2/(\uppi\beta))
.
Thus, the claim on the functions
\tilde{G}_{-}
and
\tilde{G}_{+}
follows.
∎

We bound the truncation error as follows.

Let
K
,
\alpha
and

Assume that

\displaystyle\quad+h\sum_{k=N+1}^{\infty}\frac{K\ee^{\uppi(1-\alpha)/12}\left\{\uppi|\sinh(kh)|-\log(\log 2)\right\}\uppi\cosh(kh)}{(1+\ee^{-\uppi\sinh(kh)})^{\alpha}(1+\ee^{\uppi\sinh(kh)})^{\beta}}

\displaystyle=h\sum_{k=-\infty}^{-M-1}\frac{K\ee^{\uppi(1-\alpha)/12}\left\{-\uppi\sinh(kh)-\log(\log 2)\right\}\uppi\cosh(kh)}{(1+\ee^{\uppi\sinh(kh)})^{\alpha+\beta}}\ee^{\uppi\alpha\sinh(kh)}

\displaystyle\quad+h\sum_{k=N+1}^{\infty}\frac{K\ee^{\uppi(1-\alpha)/12}\left\{\uppi\sinh(kh)-\log(\log 2)\right\}\uppi\cosh(kh)}{(1+\ee^{-\uppi\sinh(kh)})^{\alpha+\beta}}\ee^{-\uppi\beta\sinh(kh)}

\displaystyle\leq h\sum_{k=-\infty}^{-M-1}\frac{K\ee^{\uppi(1-\alpha)/12}\left\{-\uppi\sinh(kh)-\log(\log 2)\right\}\uppi\cosh(kh)}{(1+0)^{\alpha+\beta}}\ee^{\uppi\alpha\sinh(kh)}

\displaystyle\quad+h\sum_{k=N+1}^{\infty}\frac{K\ee^{\uppi(1-\alpha)/12}\left\{\uppi\sinh(kh)-\log(\log 2)\right\}\uppi\cosh(kh)}{(1+0)^{\alpha+\beta}}\ee^{-\uppi\beta\sinh(kh)}.

Using Proposition
4.10
,
for
Mh\geq\arsinh(2/(\uppi\alpha))
and
Nh\geq\arsinh(2/(\uppi\beta))
,
we further bound the sums as

\displaystyle h\sum_{k=-\infty}^{-M-1}K\ee^{\uppi(1-\alpha)/12}\left\{-\uppi\sinh(kh)-\log(\log 2)\right\}\uppi\cosh(kh)\ee^{\uppi\alpha\sinh(kh)}

\displaystyle\quad+h\sum_{k=N+1}^{\infty}K\ee^{\uppi(1-\alpha)/12}\left\{\uppi\sinh(kh)-\log(\log 2)\right\}\uppi\cosh(kh)\ee^{-\uppi\beta\sinh(kh)}

\displaystyle\leq K\ee^{\uppi(1-\alpha)/12}\int_{-\infty}^{-Mh}\left\{-\uppi\sinh x-\log(\log 2)\right\}\uppi\cosh x\ee^{\uppi\alpha\sinh x}\mathrm{d}{x}

\displaystyle\quad+K\ee^{\uppi(1-\alpha)/12}\int_{Nh}^{\infty}\left\{\uppi\sinh x-\log(\log 2)\right\}\uppi\cosh x\ee^{-\uppi\beta\sinh x}\mathrm{d}{x}

\displaystyle=\frac{K\ee^{\uppi(1-\alpha)/12}}{\alpha}\left(\uppi\sinh(Mh)-\log(\log 2)+\frac{1}{\alpha}\right)\ee^{-\uppi\alpha\sinh(Mh)}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{K\ee^{\uppi(1-\alpha)/12}}{\beta}\left(\uppi\sinh(Nh)-\log(\log 2)+\frac{1}{\beta}\right)\ee^{-\uppi\beta\sinh(Nh)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{K\ee^{\uppi(1-\alpha)/12}}{\mu}\left(\uppi\sinh(Mh)-\log(\log 2)+\frac{1}{\mu}\right)\ee^{-\uppi\alpha\sinh(Mh)}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{K\ee^{\uppi(1-\alpha)/12}}{\mu}\left(\uppi\sinh(Nh)-\log(\log 2)+\frac{1}{\mu}\right)\ee^{-\uppi\beta\sinh(Nh)},

where
\mu=\min\{\alpha,\beta\}
is used
at the last inequality.
Finally, using (
2.6
),
we have

\ee^{-\uppi\alpha\sinh(Mh)}\leq\ee^{-\uppi\mu q(2dn/\mu)}

(because
\alpha\sinh(Mh)\geq\mu q(2dn/\mu)
)
and
\ee^{-\uppi\beta\sinh(Nh)}\leq\ee^{-\uppi\mu q(2dn/\mu)}

(because
\beta\sinh(Nh)\geq\mu q(2dn/\mu)
),
from which we obtain the conclusion.
∎

We are now in a position to prove Theorem
2.8
.
Note that if
h\leq\uppi d
,
then with
h
selected by (
2.5
),
the inequalities (
4.6
)
and (

) hold.
Furthermore, if

,

),

) holds.
Similarly, it holds that

\displaystyle N

\displaystyle=\left\lceil\frac{1}{h}\arsinh\left(\frac{2dn/\beta}{\arsinh(2dn/\mu)}\right)\right\rceil

\displaystyle=\left\lceil\frac{n}{\arsinh(2dn/\mu)}\arsinh\left(\frac{2dn/\beta}{\arsinh(2dn/\mu)}\right)\right\rceil

\displaystyle\leq\left\lceil\frac{n}{\arsinh(2dn/\mu)}\arsinh\left(\frac{2dn/\beta}{1}\right)\right\rceil

\displaystyle\leq\left\lceil\frac{n}{\arsinh(2dn/\mu)}\arsinh\left(2dn/\mu\right)\right\rceil

\displaystyle=n.

From the inequalities, the truncation error

\displaystyle\frac{K\ee^{\uppi(1-\alpha)/12}}{\mu}\left(\uppi\sinh(Mh)+\uppi\sinh(Nh)-2\log(\log 2)+\frac{2}{\mu}\right)\ee^{-\uppi\mu q(2dn/\mu)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{K\ee^{\uppi(1-\alpha)/12}}{\mu}\left(\uppi\sinh(nh)+\uppi\sinh(nh)-2\log(\log 2)+\frac{2}{\mu}\right)\ee^{-\uppi\mu q(2dn/\mu)}

\displaystyle=\frac{2K\ee^{\uppi(1-\alpha)/12}}{\mu}\left(\uppi\sinh(nh)-\log(\log 2)+\frac{1}{\mu}\right)\ee^{-\uppi\mu q(2dn/\mu)}.

from Lemmas
4.24

Poster 69. Biomechanical Comparison of Medial Quadriceps Tendon–Femoral Ligament and Medial Patellofemoral Ligament Reconstructions: A Cadaveric Load-to-Failure Study
Authors: Naofumi Hashiguchi, Alireza Gholipour, Marco Stefano, Augustus D Mazzocca, Miho J Tanaka
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjective:To compare the ultimate failure stress and failure modes of medial quadriceps tendon-femoral ligament (MQTFL) and medial patellofemoral ligament (MPFL) reconstructions in a controlled cadaveric model, hypothesizing that MQTFL reconstruction would demonstrate biomechanical equivalence to MPFL reconstruction.Methods:Five fresh-frozen human cadaveric knees underwent sequential MQTFL and MPFL reconstructions using gracilis and semitendinosus grafts, respectively. MPFL reconstructions were performed using two 1.6-mm all-suture anchors (Arthrex) in the proximal half of the patella, whereas MQTFL reconstructions were performed by looping the graft through the distal quadriceps tendon and securing it to itself with a locking looped suture (#2 FiberLoop, Arthrex). Femoral fixation was omitted to isolate the patellar construct. Constructs were cyclically loaded (5-50 N, 200 cycles) at 1 Hz, followed by load-to-failure testing at 30° knee flexion using a material testing system at 300 mm/min. Ultimate failure stress (N/mm²) was calculated by dividing peak load by graft cross-sectional area. Failure modes were categorized as fixation-related or graft rupture.Results:Ten reconstructions (n = 5 matched pairs) were tested. Displacement after cyclic loading remained unchanged for MQTFL (26.6 ± 6.2 to 27.2 ± 6.6 mm; p = 0.102) but increased for MPFL (37.0 ± 3.2 to 39.6 ± 3.4 mm; p = 0.002). Stiffness increased in MQTFL (p = 0.049) but not in MPFL (p = 0.207). MQTFL demonstrated significantly higher ultimate failure stress than MPFL (63.4 ± 31.0 vs 13.5 ± 4.3 N/mm²; p = 0.018) with comparable ultimate load (431.9 ± 66.7 N vs 490.6 ± 145.1 N; p = 0.31). Failure modes differed: all MQTFL constructs failed by midsubstance rupture, while MPFL constructs failed via fixation-related mechanisms (four graft-suture disengagements, one anchor pullout).Conclusions:In this matched-pair cadaveric study, MQTFL reconstruction showed significantly greater ultimate failure stress and superior cyclic stability increasing compared with MPFL, despite similar ultimate load. Failure patterns indicated that MQTFL failure occurred within the graft substance, whereas MPFL failure was fixation-dependent. These findings suggest that MQTFL is a biomechanically robust alternative to MPFL reconstruction, potentially reducing fixation-related complications.

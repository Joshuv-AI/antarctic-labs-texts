On p p -adic Siegel–Eisenstein series II: How to avoid the regularity condition for p p
Siegfried Böcherer, Toshiyuki Kikuta *

Abstract
In a previous paper, the authors showed that two kinds of p p -adic Siegel–Eisenstein series of degree n n coincide with classical modular forms of weight k k for Γ 0 ​ ( p ) \Gamma_{0}(p) , under the assumption that p p is a regular prime. The purpose of this paper is to show that this condition on p p can be removed if the degree n n is low compared with k k , namely, n ≤ 2 ​ k + 1 n\leq 2k+1 .

Introduction

Nagaoka
[
8
]
and Katsurada–Nagaoka
[
6
]
considered
p
-adic limits of Siegel
Eisenstein series for arbitrary degree
n
, starting from “initial weight”
k

being
1
and
2
respectively.
They showed that these limits become classical Siegel modular forms for
\Gamma_{0}(p)
,
more precisely they were identified with certain linear combinations of genus theta series.
It was later noticed
[
7
]
that in their work a
condition “
p

n
is large, but the result remained valid unconditionally
for small
n
.

Using a different technique (mod
p^{m}
singular modular
forms and their structure) we recently proved such a
result for arbitrary
k
for regular primes
p
.
We emphasize that this condition on
p
was needed for all
n
(including the case of small
n
).
The purpose of the present note is to show that by using some
ingredients from
[
3
,
2
]
(but not the full strength of the structure theorem there)
we can also get rid of the
p
-regularity condition if
n
is not too large compared with
k
(i.e.,
n\leq 2k+1
).

2
Preliminaries

2.1
Siegel modular forms

Let
n
be a positive integer,

\mathbb{H}_{n}
the Siegel upper half space of degree
n
,
and
\Gamma_{n}
the Siegel modular group of degree
n
.
Let
N
be a positive integer and
\Gamma_{0}^{(n)}(N)
the congruence subgroup of
\Gamma_{n}
defined as

\displaystyle\Gamma_{0}^{(n)}(N):=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}A&B\\
C&D\end{pmatrix}\in\Gamma_{n}\>\Big|\>C\equiv 0_{n}\bmod{N}\right\}.

For a positive integer
k
and a Dirichlet character

\chi
mod
N
, we denote by
M_{k}(\Gamma_{0}^{(n)}(N),\chi)

the space of Siegel modular forms of weight
k
with character
\chi
for
\Gamma_{0}^{(n)}(N)
.
When
\chi={\boldsymbol{1}}_{N}
(trivial character mod
N
),
we write simply
M_{k}(\Gamma_{0}^{(n)}(N))
for
M_{k}(\Gamma_{0}^{(n)}(N),{\boldsymbol{1}}_{N})
.

Any
F\in M_{k}(\Gamma_{0}^{(n)}(N),\chi)
has a Fourier expansion of the form

F(Z)=\sum_{0\leq T\in\Lambda_{n}}a_{F}(T)q^{T},\quad q^{T}:=e^{2\pi i{\rm tr}(TZ)},\quad Z\in\mathbb{H}_{n},

where

\Lambda_{n}:=\{T=(t_{ij})\in{\rm Sym}_{n}(\mathbb{Q})\;|\;t_{ii},\;2t_{ij}\in\mathbb{Z}\}.

For a subring
R
of
\mathbb{C}
, we denote by
M_{k}(\Gamma_{0}^{(n)}(N),\chi)_{R}

the
R
-module consisting of all
F\in M_{k}(\Gamma_{0}^{(n)}(N),\chi)
satisfying
a_{F}(T)\in R
for all
T\in\Lambda_{n}
.
We write simply
M_{k}(\Gamma_{0}^{(n)}(N))_{R}
for

M_{k}(\Gamma_{0}^{(n)}(N),{\boldsymbol{1}}_{N})_{R}
.

We denote by
\Lambda_{n}^{+}
the set of all positive definite elements of
\Lambda_{n}
.
Let
a_{F}(T)^{*}
be the rank
r
primitive Fourier coefficient for
T\in\Lambda_{r}^{+}
(
r\leq n
),
as introduced in
[
5
]
, it is characterized by the relation

\displaystyle a_{F}\begin{pmatrix}T&0\\
0&0_{n-r}\end{pmatrix}=\sum_{D}a_{F}\begin{pmatrix}T[D^{-1}]&0\\
0&0_{n-r}\end{pmatrix}^{*}.

Here
D
runs over all elements of
{\rm GL}_{r}(\mathbb{Z})\backslash\{D\in\mathbb{Z}^{r,r}\;|\;\det D\neq 0\}

satisfying
T[D^{-1}]\in\Lambda_{r}^{+}
.
We define

F_{[r]}:=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq T\in\Lambda_{n}\\
{\rm rank}(T)=r\end{subarray}}a_{F}(T)q^{T}.

The following property will be needed later.

Lemma 2.1

(
[
2
]
Lemma 4.1)
.

We have (as a formal identity of the Fourier expansions)

\displaystyle F_{[r]}=\sum_{S\in\Lambda_{r}^{+}/{\rm GL_{r}}(\mathbb{Z})}\frac{a_{F}\left(\begin{smallmatrix}S&0\\
0&0_{n-r}\end{smallmatrix}\right)^{*}}{\epsilon(S)}(\theta^{(n)}_{S})_{[r]},

(2.1)

where
S
runs over representatives of all
{\rm GL_{r}}(\mathbb{Z})
-equivalence classes in
\Lambda_{r}^{+}
,

\theta_{S}^{(n)}
is the theta series of degree
n
attached to
S
defined as

\displaystyle\theta_{S}^{(n)}(Z):=\sum_{X\in\mathbb{Z}^{r,n}}e^{2\pi i({\rm tr}(S[X]Z))}\quad(Z\in\mathbb{H}_{n})

(
\mathbb{Z}^{r,n}
is the set of
r\times n
matrices with integral components),
and
\epsilon(S)
is the cardinality of the group of automorphisms of
S
.

2.2
Congruences for modular forms

Let
p
be an odd prime.
Let
F_{i}
(
i=1
,
2
) be two formal power series of the form

F_{i}=\sum_{T\in\Lambda_{n}}a_{F_{i}}(T)q^{T}

with
a_{F_{i}}(T)\in\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}
for all
T\in\Lambda_{n}
.
We write
F_{1}\equiv F_{2}
mod
p^{m}
if
a_{F_{1}}(T)\equiv a_{F_{2}}(T)
mod
p^{m}
for all
T\in\Lambda_{n}
.

Definition 2.2
.

We say that
F\in M_{k}(\Gamma_{0}^{(n)}(N),\chi)_{\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}}
is “mod
p^{m}
singular” if there exists
r
satisfying the following properties:

•

a_{F}(T)\equiv 0
mod
p^{m}
for any
T\in\Lambda_{n}
with
{\rm rank}(T)>r
,

•

there exists
T\in\Lambda_{n}
with
{\rm rank}(T)=r
such that
a_{F}(T)\not\equiv 0
mod
p
.

We call such
r
the “
p
-rank” of
F
.

Theorem 2.3

(
[
1
]
)
.

Let
p
be an odd prime and
k
a positive integer.
Let
F\in M_{k}(\Gamma_{0}^{(n)}(N),\chi)_{\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}}
with

\chi
a quadratic Dirichlet character mod
N
.
Suppose that
F
is mod
p^{m}
singular of
p
-rank
r
.
Then we have
2k-r\equiv 0
mod
(p-1)p^{m-1}
.
In particular,
r
should be even.

2.3
Some
p
-adic properties of the Siegel–Eisenstein series

We put

\Gamma_{\infty}^{(n)}:=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}A&B\\
C&D\end{pmatrix}\in\Gamma_{n}\;\Big|\;C=0_{n}\right\}.

For an even integer
k
with
k>n+1
, the Siegel–Eisenstein series of degree
n
and weight
k
with level
1
is defined as

E_{k}^{(n)}(Z):=\sum_{\left(\begin{smallmatrix}*&*\\
C&D\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in\Gamma_{\infty}^{(n)}\backslash\Gamma_{n}}\det(CZ+D)^{-k},\quad Z\in\mathbb{H}_{n}.

This series defines an element of
M_{k}(\Gamma_{n})_{\mathbb{Q}}
.
We denote by

E_{k}^{(n)}=\sum_{T\in\Lambda_{n}}a_{k}^{(n)}(T)q^{T}

the Fourier expansion of
E_{k}^{(n)}
.

We put
\Lambda_{n}^{(r)}:=\{T\in\Lambda_{n}\;|\;{\rm rank}(T)=r\}
.
Note that, if
T\in\Lambda^{(r)}_{n}
then we have

a_{k}^{(n)}(T)=a_{k}^{(r)}(T^{\prime})
for
T^{\prime}\in\Lambda_{r}^{+}
such that
T\sim\left(\begin{smallmatrix}T^{\prime}&0\\
0&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)
mod
{\rm GL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
.
Therefore, we may assume that
T\in\Lambda_{r}^{+}
and may consider
a_{k}^{(r)}(T)
,
instead of
a_{k}^{(n)}(T)
with
T\in\Lambda_{n}^{(r)}
.

Let
p
be an odd prime.
We put

{\boldsymbol{X}}=\mathbb{Z}_{p}\times\mathbb{Z}/(p-1)\mathbb{Z}.

We regard
\mathbb{Z}\subset{\boldsymbol{X}}
via the embedding
n\mapsto(n,\widetilde{n})
with
\widetilde{n}:=n
mod
p-1
.
For
(k,a)\in{\boldsymbol{X}}
, the
p
-adic Siegel–Eisenstein series
\widetilde{E}^{(n)}_{(k,a)}

of degree
n
and weight
(k,a)
is defined as

\widetilde{E}^{(n)}_{(k,a)}:=\lim_{m\to\infty}E_{k_{m}}^{(n)}\quad(p\text{-adic\ limit})

where
k_{m}
is a number sequence such that
k_{m}\to\infty
(
m\to\infty
) in the usual topology of
\mathbb{R}
, and
k_{m}\to(k,a)
(
m\to\infty
) in
{\boldsymbol{X}}
.

In this paper, we treat the case when the weights are
(k,k)
with
k
even and
(k,k+\frac{p-1}{2})

with condition
k\equiv\frac{p-1}{2}
mod
2
.
We put
{\boldsymbol{k}}_{0}:=(k,k)
and
{\boldsymbol{k}}_{1}:=(k,k+\frac{p-1}{2})
and denote by
k_{j}(m)

a number sequence satisfying
k_{j}(m)\to{\boldsymbol{k}}_{j}
(
m\to\infty
) in
{\boldsymbol{X}}
.
For any
k_{j}(m)
satisfying
k_{j}(m)\to{\boldsymbol{k}}_{j}
(
m\to\infty
) in
{\boldsymbol{X}}
,
we may assume that
k_{j}(m)
is of the form

k_{j}(m)=k+a_{j}(m)p^{b(m)},

where
a_{j}(m)
is a positive integer with
a_{j}(m)\equiv\frac{p-1}{2^{j}}
mod
p-1
,
and
b(m)=b_{j}(m)
is a positive integer satisfying
b(m)\to\infty
if
m\to\infty
.
Thereafter, whenever
{\boldsymbol{k}}_{j}
or
k_{j}(m)
is involved,
the above condition on
k
is always assumed.

Let
v_{p}
be the additive valuation on the
p
-adic field
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
normalized such that
v_{p}(p)=1
.
For a formal power series
F
of the form
F=\sum_{T\in\Lambda_{n}}a_{F}(T)q^{T}
with
a_{F}(T)\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}
,
we define

\displaystyle v_{p}^{(r)}(F):=\inf\{v_{p}(a_{F}(T))\;|\;T\in\Lambda_{n},\ {\rm rank}(T)=r\}.

We put
\nu_{m}:=v^{(2k)}_{p}(E^{(2k+1)}_{k_{j}(m)})
.

Proposition 2.4

(
[
3
]
)
.

Let
p>2k+1
.

(1)

We have
\nu_{m}\leq 0
and
\nu_{m}
is a constant for sufficiently large
m
.

(2)

We have
p^{-\nu_{m}}E_{k_{j}(m)}^{(2k+1)}\in M_{k_{j}(m)}(\Gamma_{n})_{\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}}
and
this is a mod
p^{c(m)}
singular of
p
-rank
2k
.
Here
c(m)
is a positive integer satisfying
c(m)\to\infty
when
m\to\infty
.

Remark 2.5
.

Both properties (1), (2) were proved in
[
3
]
Proposition 3.3.
As written in
[
3
]
Remark 3.4, the regularity condition for
p
is not necessary when
n=2k+1
.

For
S\in\Lambda_{r}^{+}
with
r
even, we define
\chi_{S}
by

\displaystyle\chi_{S}(d):={\rm sign}(d)^{\frac{r}{2}}\left(\frac{(-1)^{\frac{r}{2}}\det 2S}{|d|}\right).

Let
\eta_{S}
be the primitive character associated with
\chi_{S}
.
Let
\chi_{p}:=(\frac{*}{p})
be the unique nontrivial quadratic character mod
p
.
We write
\chi_{p}^{0}
for the trivial character mod
p
.
The level of
S
is defined as the smallest positive integer
l
such that
l(2S)^{-1}\in 2\Lambda_{r}
.
We denote it by
{\rm level}(S)
.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
p>2k+1
and
S\in\Lambda_{2k}^{+}
.

(1)

We have
\lim_{m\to\infty}a_{k_{j}(m)}^{(2k)}(S)^{*}\in\mathbb{Q}
(
p
-adic limit).

(2)

If
\lim_{m\to\infty}a_{k_{j}(m)}^{(2k)}(S)^{*}\neq 0
then
{\rm level}(S)\mid p
and
\chi_{S}=\chi_{p}^{j}
.

(3)

For any
S\in\Lambda_{2k}
with
{\rm level}(S)\mid p
, we have
v_{p}(a_{k_{j}(m)}^{(2k)}(S)^{*})\geq\nu_{m}

when
m
is sufficiently large.

Proof.

The assertions (1), (3) follow from
[
3
]
Subsections 3.2, 3.3.

We prove (2). As written in
[
3
]
Subsection 3.3,
if
\lim_{m\to\infty}a_{k_{j}(m)}^{(2k)}(S)\neq 0
then we have

\eta_{S}=\begin{cases}{\boldsymbol{1}}\ (j=0)\\
\chi_{p}\ (j=1).\end{cases}

Suppose that
\lim_{m\to\infty}a_{k_{j}(m)}^{(2k)}(S)^{*}\neq 0
.
Then
{\rm level}(S)\mid p
follows from the reasoning of
[
3
]
Subsection 3.3 again.
We need to confirm
\chi_{S}=\chi_{p}^{j}
.
Recall that
a_{k_{m}}^{(2k)}(S)^{*}
is a linear combination of
a_{k_{m}}^{(2k)}(S[D^{-1}])
with an element
D
of
{\rm GL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z})\backslash\{D\in\mathbb{Z}^{n,n}\;|\;\det D\neq 0\}
satisfying
T[D^{-1}]\in\Lambda_{r}^{+}
.
There exists at least one
D
such that
\lim_{m\to\infty}a_{k_{j}(m)}^{(2k)}(S[D^{-1}])\neq 0
.
By the above fact, we have
\eta_{S[D^{-1}]}=\begin{cases}{\boldsymbol{1}}\ (j=0)\\
\chi_{p}\ (j=1).\end{cases}

Since
\eta_{S}=\eta_{S[D^{-1}]}
, we obtain
\eta_{S}=\begin{cases}{\boldsymbol{1}}\ (j=0)\\
\chi_{p}\ (j=1).\end{cases}

Therefore we get
\chi_{S}=\chi_{p}^{j}
because of
{\rm level}(S)\mid p
.
∎

3
Main theorem and its proof

Let
S\in\Lambda_{m}^{+}
and
{\rm gen}(S)
be the genus containing
S
.
Let
\{S_{1},\cdots,S_{h}\}
be a set representatives of
{\rm GL}_{m}(\mathbb{Z})
-equivalence classes in
{\rm gen}(S)
.
The genus theta series associated with
S
is defined by

\Theta^{(n)}_{{\rm gen}(S)}(Z):=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{h}\frac{\theta_{S_{i}}^{(n)}(Z)}{\epsilon(S_{i})}\right)/\left(\sum_{i=1}^{h}\frac{1}{\epsilon(S_{i})}\right).

We put

(\Theta^{(n)}_{{\rm gen}(S)})^{0}:=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{h}\frac{1}{\epsilon(S_{i})}\right)\cdot\Theta^{(n)}_{{\rm gen}(S)}.

The following theorem is the main result of this paper.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
n
,
k
be positive integers with
n\leq 2k+1
and
p
a prime with
p>2k+1
.
Assume that
k
is even in
{\boldsymbol{k}}_{0}
and

k\equiv\frac{p-1}{2}
mod
2
in
{\boldsymbol{k}}_{1}
.
Then we have

\displaystyle\widetilde{E}_{{\boldsymbol{k}}_{j}}^{(n)}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}{\rm gen}(S)\\
\ {\rm level}(S)\mid p\\
\chi_{S}=\chi_{p}^{j}\end{subarray}}\widetilde{a}_{j}({\rm gen}(S))\cdot(\Theta^{(n)}_{{\rm gen}(S)})^{0},\quad(\widetilde{a}_{j}({\rm gen}(S))\in\mathbb{Q}),

(3.1)

where the summation in (
3.1
) goes over all genera
{\rm gen}(S)
of
S\in\Lambda_{2k}^{+}
with level dividing
p
satisfying
\chi_{S}=\chi_{p}^{j}
.

Remark 3.2
.

(1)

More precisely, we have
\widetilde{a}_{j}({\rm gen}(S))=\lim_{m\to\infty}a_{k_{j}(m)}^{(2k)}(S)^{*}
.
This
p
-adic limit is given explicitly by the formula in
[
3
]
Theorem 3.8.

(2)

The main point of this theorem is that the regularity condition on
p
we assumed in
[
3
]
Theorem 1.1 (under no condition on
n
at all) can be removed when
n\leq 2k+1
.

(3)

By the same proof as
[
3
]
Corollary 1.3, we have

\widetilde{E}_{{\boldsymbol{k}}_{j}}^{(n)}\mid U(p)=\widetilde{E}_{{\boldsymbol{k}}_{j}}^{(n)},

where
U(p)
is the standard Hecke operator of level
p
.

(4)

We notice that the condition
p>2k+1
came up in Proposition
2.4
and Lemma
2.6
.
It also will appears in the final step of the proof for Theorem
3.1
.
We do not know if this condition is really necessary.

Proof of Theorem
3.1
.

It suffices to consider the case
n=2k+1
, since we may apply the Siegel
\Phi
-operator to
\widetilde{E}_{{\boldsymbol{k}}_{j}}^{(2k+1)}
if
n<2k+1
.

By Proposition
2.4
,
p^{-\nu_{m}}E_{k_{j}(m)}^{(2k+1)}
is a mod
p^{c(m)}
singular of
p
-rank
2k
for some
c(m)
satisfying
c(m)\to\infty
when
m\to\infty
.
Applying Lemma
2.1
to
p^{-\nu_{m}}E_{k_{j}(m)}^{(2k+1)}
, we have

p^{-\nu_{m}}(E_{k_{j}(m)}^{(2k+1)})_{[2k]}=\sum_{S\in\Lambda_{2k}^{+}/{\rm GL}_{2k}(\mathbb{Z})}p^{-\nu_{m}}\frac{a_{k_{j}(m)}^{(2k)}(S)^{*}}{\epsilon(S)}(\theta_{S}^{(2k+1)})_{[2k]}.

Note that
p^{-\nu_{m}}\frac{a_{k_{j}(m)}^{(2k)}(S)^{*}}{\epsilon(S)}\in\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}
, since
v_{p}(a_{k_{j}(m)}^{(2k)}(S)^{*})\geq\nu_{m}
(Proposition
2.4
(3)) and
p>2k+1
implies
p\nmid\epsilon(S)
.
By Lemma
2.6
, we have

\displaystyle p^{-\nu_{m}}(E_{k_{j}(m)}^{(2k+1)})_{[2k]}\equiv\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}S\in\Lambda_{2k}^{+}/{\rm GL}_{2k}(\mathbb{Z})\\
{\rm level}(S)\mid p\\
\chi_{S}=\chi_{p}^{j}\end{subarray}}p^{-\nu_{m}}\frac{a_{k_{j}(m)}^{(2k)}(S)^{*}}{\epsilon(S)}(\theta_{S}^{(2k+1)})_{[2k]}\bmod{p^{c^{\prime}(m)}}

(3.2)

for some
c^{\prime}(m)
satisfying
c^{\prime}(m)\to\infty
when
m\to\infty
.
In particular, this is a finite sum.

We prove that

\displaystyle p^{-\nu_{m}}E_{k_{j}(m)}^{(2k+1)}\equiv\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}S\in\Lambda_{2k}^{+}/{\rm GL}_{2k}(\mathbb{Z})\\
{\rm level}(S)\mid p\\
\chi_{S}=\chi_{p}^{j}\end{subarray}}p^{-\nu_{m}}\frac{a_{k_{j}(m)}^{(2k)}(S)^{*}}{\epsilon(S)}\theta_{S}^{(2k+1)}\bmod{p^{c^{\prime\prime}(m)}}

(3.3)

for some
c^{\prime\prime}(m)
satisfying
c^{\prime\prime}(m)\to\infty
when
m\to\infty
.
Taking
{\mathcal{E}}\in M_{\frac{p-1}{2}}(\Gamma_{0}^{(2k+1)}(p),\chi_{p})_{\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}}
from
[
4
]
such that
{\mathcal{E}}\equiv 1
mod
p
, we consider

\displaystyle F:

\displaystyle=p^{-\nu_{m}}E_{k_{j}(m)}^{(2k+1)}-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}S\in\Lambda_{2k}^{+}/{\rm GL}_{2k}(\mathbb{Z})\\
{\rm level}(S)\mid p\\
\chi_{S}=\chi_{p}^{j}\end{subarray}}p^{-\nu_{m}}\frac{a_{k_{j}(m)}^{(2k)}(S)^{*}}{\epsilon(S)}\theta_{S}^{(2k+1)}\cdot{\mathcal{E}}^{t_{j}(m)p^{b(m)}}

\displaystyle\in M_{k_{j}(m)}(\Gamma_{0}^{(2k+1)}(p),\chi_{p}^{j})_{\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}}.

Here
t_{j}(m)\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
is defined as the number satisfying
a_{j}(m)=t_{j}(m)\cdot\frac{p-1}{2}
.
(Recall that
k_{j}(m)=k+a_{j}(m)\cdot p^{b(m)}
,
b(m)\to\infty
(
m\to\infty
), and
a_{j}(m)\equiv\frac{p-1}{2^{j}}\bmod{p-1}
.)

We put
c^{\prime\prime}(m):=\min\{c^{\prime}(m),b(m)+1\}
.
Then we want to prove
F\equiv 0
mod
p^{c^{\prime\prime}(m)}
.
Seeking a contradiction, we suppose that
F\not\equiv 0
mod
p^{c^{\prime\prime}(m)}
.
Then
F
is a mod
p^{c^{\prime\prime}(m)}
singular of
p
-rank
r
with
0\leq r<2k
because of (
3.2
).
Applying Theorem
2.3
, we have
2k_{j}(m)-r\equiv 0
mod
(p-1)p^{c^{\prime\prime}(m)-1}
.
In particular,
2k-r\equiv 0
mod
p-1
. Since
r<2k<p-1
, this is a contradiction.
Hence we have
F\equiv 0
mod
p^{c^{\prime\prime}(m)}
.

Note that
\nu_{m}\to\nu
(
m\to\infty
) for some constant
\nu
(Proposition
2.4
(1)),
and
\lim_{m\to\infty}a_{k_{j}(m)}^{(2k)}(S)^{*}=:\widetilde{a}_{j}(S)\in\mathbb{Q}
(Lemma
2.6
(1)).
Taking a
p
-adic limit of both sides of (
3.3
), we have

\displaystyle p^{-\nu}\widetilde{E}_{{\boldsymbol{k}}_{j}}^{(n)}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}S\in\Lambda_{2k}^{+}/{\rm GL}_{2k}(\mathbb{Z})\\
{\rm level}(S)\mid p\\
\chi_{S}=\chi_{p}^{j}\end{subarray}}p^{-\nu}\frac{\widetilde{a}_{j}(S)}{\epsilon(S)}\theta_{S}^{(2k+1)}.

Then we get (
3.1
) from this formula by resummation, observing that

a_{k_{j}(m)}^{(2k)}(S)^{*}
(and hence
\widetilde{a}_{j}(S)
) depends only on the genus of
S
.
This completes the proof of Theorem
3.1
.
∎

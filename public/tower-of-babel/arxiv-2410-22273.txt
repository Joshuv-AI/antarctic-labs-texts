Driving forces in cell migration and pattern formation in a soft tissue
Amabile Tatone, Filippo Recrosi, Giuseppe Tomassetti

Abstract
We give a description of cell diffusion in a soft tissue, paying special attention to the coupling of force, matter, and microforce balance laws through a suitable dissipation principle. To this end, we cast our framework into a multi-level schematics, comprising both kinematics and kinetics, based on a characterization of the free energy. We lay down first a force balance law, where force and stress fields are defined as power conjugate quantities to velocity fields and their gradients, then we give a species molar balance law, with chemical potential test fields, as power conjugate quantities to the rate of change of the species concentration, and finally a microforce balance law. The main feature of this framework is the constitutive expression for the chemical potential which is split into a term derived from the homogeneous convex part of the free energy and an active external chemical potential giving rise to the spinodal decomposition. The active part of the chemical potential is given an expression depending on the cell concentration and resembling the one defined in [ 1 ] where it is meant to characterize an upward cell diffusion induced by cell motility. Further we show how an external vector field, entering the microforce balance law as a power conjugate quantity to the rate of change of the concentration gradient, can guide the diffusion process to a different limit stationary pattern. This vector field could possibly model any directional cue or bias characterizing the interaction of the migrating cells and the surrounding tissue.

5
Conclusion

The gradient energy leads to the emergence
of microforces, subject to a microforce balance law,
which allow the characterization of the interactions as depending on the
concentration of the diffusing particles.

The phase separation process results from a double well energy made up of the sum of
a convex entropic energy
\varphi_{\textsf{x}}(c)
and a non convex energy
\varphi_{\textsf{s}}(c,\gamma)

arising from the integration of a microforce expression
\hat{s}(c,\gamma)

inspired by the
differential adhesion hypothesis
.

Some numerical simulations illustrate how the evolution of an initial uniform concentration
up to a non uniform stationary pattern is characterized by moving interfaces
guided by a prescribed microforce
{\mathbf{g}_{\mathrm{o}}}
in the form (
88
),
possibly with a non uniform vector field
\mathbf{d}_{\mathrm{o}}
.

Appendix C
Appendix – Homogeneous deformations coupled with diffusion

C.1
Uniaxial deformations

Let us consider a cylinder undergoing
homogeneous deformations
as described
by the following deformation gradient matrix

[\mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{o}}]=\beta^{\frac{1}{3}}\,[\mathbf{F}_{\iota}]=\beta^{\frac{1}{3}}\begin{pmatrix}\lambda&0&0\\
0&\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda}}&0\\
0&0&\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda}}\end{pmatrix}\,,

(120)

in an orthonormal basis
\{\mathbf{e}_{1},\mathbf{e}_{2},\mathbf{e}_{3}\}
, with
\mathbf{e}_{1}
denoting the axis direction.
Let the cylinder, after the inclusion of an amount of cells up to a uniform concentration
\,c\,
,
be stretched by a couple of axial forces on opposite faces defined by
the traction
\,\mathbf{t}={\sigma}\,\mathbf{e}_{1}
.
When selecting
affine test velocity fields
, as appropriate to homogeneous deformations,
the force balance law (
16
) simplifies to

\mathbf{T}={\sigma}\,\mathbf{e}_{1}\otimes\mathbf{e}_{1}\,.

(121)

Assuming that the material is
incompressible
(
i.e.

J=1
), the stress is characterized by

\mathbf{T}=\mathbf{\hat{T}}(\mathbf{F})-{p}\,\mathbf{I}\,,

(122)

while the strain energy (
93
), by (
110
) and (
111
),
takes the expression

\varphi_{e}(\mathbf{F})=\varphi_{\iota}(\mathbf{F}_{\iota})={\tilde{\varphi}}_{\iota}(\lambda)=k_{\iota}\,(\bar{I}_{1}-3)=k_{\iota}\,\left(\lambda^{2}+\frac{2}{\lambda}-3\right)\,.

(123)

The response function, consistent with (
114
), turns out to be

\mathbf{\hat{T}}(\mathbf{F}_{\iota})=2\,k_{\iota}\,\dev(\mathbf{F}_{\iota}\,\mathbf{F}_{\iota}^{\mathsf{T}})\,,

(124)

with

[\dev(\mathbf{F}_{\iota}\,\mathbf{F}_{\iota}^{\mathsf{T}})]=\frac{2}{3}\left(\lambda^{2}-\frac{1}{\lambda}\right)\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0\\[3.0pt]
0&-\dfrac{1}{2}&0\\[3.0pt]
0&0&-\dfrac{1}{2}\end{pmatrix}\,.

(125)

From the equations above we finally get

\displaystyle{\sigma}

\displaystyle=2\,k_{\iota}\left(\lambda^{2}-\frac{1}{\lambda}\right)\,,

(126)

\displaystyle{p}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{3}\,{\sigma}\,.

(127)

It is worth noting that in a uniaxial test on a cylinder with a circular cross section of radius
R

the total force
N
applied to each end face turns out to be

N={\sigma}\,\pi\,R^{2}\,\beta^{2/3}\,\frac{1}{\lambda}\,,

(128)

on the right side and the opposite on the left side.
Therefore, though the traction
{\sigma}
and the concentration
\,c\,
are uncoupled,
the total force
N
does depend on
\,c\,
through
\beta
.

It is interesting to look also at the chemical potential expression from (
53
)

\mu={\hat{\mu}}(c)-\check{s}+\frac{\alpha}{\rho_{\mathrm{o}}}\big({p}+\tilde{\varphi}_{\iota}(\lambda)\big)={\hat{\mu}}(c)-\check{s}+\frac{\alpha}{\rho_{\mathrm{o}}}\,k_{\iota}\,\left(\frac{\lambda^{2}}{3}+\frac{8}{3\,\lambda}-3\right)\,,

(129)

which highlights the contribution of the deformation.

C.2
Cylinder in an open container

Let us consider a cylinder undergoing homogeneous deformations as in the previous sections,
but constrained inside a cylindrical container with open ends, leaving it free to stretch
in the axis direction while preventing any lateral deformation.
Such a constraint is described, through (
120
), by the condition

\mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{o}}\,\mathbf{e}_{2}=\mathbf{e}_{2}\quad\&\quad\mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{o}}\,\mathbf{e}_{3}=\mathbf{e}_{3}\quad\Rightarrow\quad\beta^{\frac{1}{3}}\,\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda}}=1\quad\Rightarrow\quad\lambda=\beta^{\frac{2}{3}}\,.

(130)

The force balance law changes to

\mathbf{T}={\sigma}\,(\mathbf{e}_{2}\otimes\mathbf{e}_{2}+\mathbf{e}_{3}\otimes\mathbf{e}_{3})\,,

(131)

leading, by (
122
), (
124
) and (
125
), to the expressions for the reactive traction and pressure

\displaystyle{\sigma}

\displaystyle=-2\,k_{\iota}\left(\lambda^{2}-\frac{1}{\lambda}\right)\,,

(132)

\displaystyle{p}

\displaystyle=-\frac{2}{3}\,{\sigma}\,.

(133)

It is worth noting that because of the constraint expression for
\lambda
in (
130
),
both
{\sigma}
and
{p}
depend on the concentration
c
through
\beta
.

Finally,
by the reactive traction and
pressure expressions (
132
), (
133
),
the strain energy density expression (

C.2.2
Two interconnected cylinders

Let us consider the two cylinders joined together while constrained inside the open rigid cylinder.
Assuming that the exterior boundary is impermeable, let us infuse an amount of cells up to a uniform concentration.

After that we rule out any subsequent supply (

\,\mu^{\scriptscriptstyle[-]}\,

C.2.7
Common tangent construction

By (
159
), we can further transform (

) into

\displaystyle\rho_{\mathrm{o}}\,[\![\,\mu\,]\!]\,\dot{c}^{\scriptscriptstyle[+]}

\displaystyle=\frac{d}{dt}\,\Big(\varphi_{\textsf{x}}(c^{\scriptscriptstyle[+]})+\varphi_{\textsf{x}}(c^{\scriptscriptstyle[-]})\Big)

(197)

\displaystyle+\frac{d}{dt}\,\Big(\varphi_{\textsf{s}}(c^{\scriptscriptstyle[+]},\gamma)+\varphi_{\textsf{s}}(c^{\scriptscriptstyle[-]},\gamma)\Big)

\displaystyle+\frac{d}{dt}\,\Big(\beta^{\scriptscriptstyle[+]}\,\tilde{\varphi}_{\iota}(\lambda^{\scriptscriptstyle[+]})+\,\beta^{\scriptscriptstyle[-]}\,\tilde{\varphi}_{\iota}(\lambda^{\scriptscriptstyle[-]})\Big)

\displaystyle+\frac{d}{dt}\,\Big(\tilde{\varphi}_{g}([\![\,c\,]\!])\Big)

\displaystyle-\frac{2}{L}\,\mathrm{g}_{\mathrm{o}}^{\star}\,\dot{c}^{\scriptscriptstyle[+]}\,,

showing its relation to (
187
).
This allows us to define the
total free energy density

\displaystyle\hat{\psi}_{\mathrm{tot}}(c^{\scriptscriptstyle[+]},\gamma)

\displaystyle=\big(\varphi_{\textsf{x}}(c^{\scriptscriptstyle[+]})+\varphi_{\textsf{x}}(c^{\scriptscriptstyle[-]})\big)+\big(\varphi_{\textsf{s}}(c^{\scriptscriptstyle[+]},\gamma)+\varphi_{\textsf{s}}(c^{\scriptscriptstyle[-]},\gamma)\big)

(198)

\displaystyle+\big(\beta^{\scriptscriptstyle[+]}\,\tilde{\varphi}_{\iota}(\lambda^{\scriptscriptstyle[+]})+\,\beta^{\scriptscriptstyle[-]}\,\tilde{\varphi}_{\iota}(\lambda^{\scriptscriptstyle[-]})\big)+\tilde{\varphi}_{g}([\![\,c\,]\!])\,,

with
\,c^{\scriptscriptstyle[-]}=2\,c_{m}-c^{\scriptscriptstyle[+]}
, as in (
188
), and such that

\rho_{\mathrm{o}}\,[\![\,\mu\,]\!]\,\dot{c}^{\scriptscriptstyle[+]}=\frac{d}{dt}\,\hat{\psi}_{\mathrm{tot}}(c^{\scriptscriptstyle[+]},\gamma)-\frac{2}{L}\,\mathrm{g}_{\mathrm{o}}^{\star}\,\dot{c}^{\scriptscriptstyle[+]}\,.

(199)

Therefore the values of
c^{\scriptscriptstyle[+]}
where
\,[\![\,\mu\,]\!]=0
are characterized by the property

\left.\frac{d}{dt}\,\hat{\psi}_{\mathrm{tot}}(c^{\scriptscriptstyle[+]},\gamma)\right|_{[\![\,\mu\,]\!]=0}=\frac{2}{L}\,\mathrm{g}_{\mathrm{o}}^{\star}\,\dot{c}^{\scriptscriptstyle[+]}\,.

(200)

No properties have been assumed for
\mathrm{g}_{\mathrm{o}}^{\star}
until now. If
\,\mathrm{g}_{\mathrm{o}}^{\star}\,\dot{c}^{\scriptscriptstyle[+]}\,
is integrable then we
can define an
augmented free energy

\hat{\psi}_{\mathrm{aug}}

\frac{d}{dt}\,\hat{\psi}_{\mathrm{aug}}(c^{\scriptscriptstyle[+]},\gamma,\mathrm{g}_{\mathrm{o}}^{\star})=\frac{d}{dt}\,\hat{\psi}_{\mathrm{tot}}(c^{\scriptscriptstyle[+]},\gamma)-\frac{2}{L}\,\mathrm{g}_{\mathrm{o}}^{\star}\,\dot{c}^{\scriptscriptstyle[+]}\,.

(201)

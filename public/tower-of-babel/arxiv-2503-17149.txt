A spectrum-level splitting of the k ​ u ℝ ku_{\mathbb{R}} -cooperations algebra
Guchuan Li, Sarah Petersen, Elizabeth Tatum

Abstract
. In the 1980’s, Mahowald and Kane used integral Brown–Gitler spectra to decompose k ​ u ∧ k ​ u ku\wedge ku as a sum of finitely generated k ​ u ku -module spectra. This splitting, along with an analogous decomposition of k ​ o ∧ k ​ o , ko\wedge ko, led to a great deal of progress in stable homotopy computations and understanding of v 1 v_{1} -periodicity in the stable homotopy groups of spheres. In this paper, we construct a C 2 C_{2} -equivariant lift of Mahowald and Kane’s splitting of k ​ u ∧ k ​ u ku\wedge ku . We also describe the resulting C 2 C_{2} -equivariant splitting in terms of C 2 C_{2} -equivariant Adams covers and record an analogous splitting for H ​ ℤ ¯ ∧ H ​ ℤ ¯ H\underline{{\mathbb{Z}}}\wedge H\underline{{\mathbb{Z}}} . Along the way, we give complete computations of the k ​ u ℝ ku_{{\mathbb{R}}} and H ​ ℤ H{\mathbb{Z}} operations and cooperations algebras.

Introduction

1.1.
Motivation

In the 1970’s, Brown–Gitler and Cohen constructed Brown–Gitler spectra, a family of spectra realizing subcomodules of the dual Steenrod algebra
A_{*}

[
10
,
17
]

H\mathbb{Z}\wedge H\mathbb{Z}
, as a sum of finite

-modules. Mahowald and Kane later used integral Brown–Gitler spectra, a family of spectra realizing subcomodules of

, the cooperations algebra for connective complex
K
-theory, and
ko\wedge ko
, the cooperations algebra for connective real
K
-theory, in an analogous way
[
43
,
36
]
.

These splittings of
ku\wedge ku
and
ko\wedge ko
proved particularly useful in
ku
- or
ko
-based Adams spectral sequences. As
ku_{*}ku
or
ko_{*}ko
is not flat over
ku_{*}
or
ko_{*}
, the corresponding
E_{2}
-page cannot be computed as an
\Ext

E_{1}
-page.
However, Mahowald and Kane’s decompositions of
ku\wedge ku
and
ko\wedge ko
in terms of finitely generated
ku
- and
ko
-modules, respectively, made computation of the
E_{1}
-pages of these spectral sequences tractable by splitting the computation of the
E_{1}
-page into smaller pieces
[
43
,
22
,
21
,
3
]

E_{2}
-page accessible. This is incredibly useful because even the
E_{2}
-pages of these spectral sequences provide a wealth of homotopical information. Both the
ku
- and
ko
-based Adams spectral sequences have vanishing lines which result in collapse at the
E_{2}
-page in a large range.

The
ko
-based Adams spectral sequence in particular is a very effective tool for studying
v_{1}
-periodicity in the stable homotopy groups of the sphere,
\pi_{*}S_{(2)}.
For example, Mahowald used
ko
-based Adams spectral sequences to give a complete identification of the
v_{1}
-periodic elements in the stable homotopy groups of spheres
[
44
]
.
He also used the
ko
-based Adams spectral sequence to prove the Telescope Conjecture for height one at the prime
2

[
43
]
. Moreover, the
ko
-based Adams spectral sequence is a highly efficient way to compute
\pi_{*}S_{(2)}
through the
40

[
22
,
21
,
3

. Additionally, at odd primes, Gonzalez used the

[
25

Given the range of successful applications of Mahowald and Kane’s splittings in nonequivariant homotopy theory, one may ask whether analogous splittings exist in equivariant homotopy theory. In this paper, we answer this question affirmatively by constructing a
C_{2}
-equivariant splitting of
ku_{\mathbb{R}}\wedge ku_{\mathbb{R}}
in terms of finitely generated
ku_{\mathbb{R}}
-modules. We also show how this splitting leads to a complete computation of the
E_{1}
-page of the
ku_{\mathbb{R}}
-based Adams spectral sequence and give partial information towards computing the
E_{2}
-page. In future work, we plan to investigate the
E_{2}
-page and beyond. Here, and throughout the paper,
ku_{\mathbb{R}}
is the equivariant connective cover of
KU_{\mathbb{R}},
the spectrum representing Atiyah’s real
K
-theory. We also work in the category of
2
-complete spectra, often omitting the
2
-complete notation.

There are several reasons for working with the group of equivariance
G=C_{2},
the cyclic group of order two. First, Mahowald and Kane’s nonequivariant splittings involve (integral) Brown–Gitler spectra which realize subcomodules of the dual Steenrod algebra

or in the case of integral Brown–Gitler spectra, realize subcomodules of
H{{\mathbb{F}}_{2}}_{*}H\mathbb{Z}
. Thus it is reasonable to begin with a group of equivariance where the dual Steenrod algebra has been computed and closely resembles the classical nonequivariant dual Steenrod algebra. This is the case when
G=C_{2}

[
35
, Theorem 6.41]
, and, in an earlier paper, we constructed
C_{2}
-equivariant integral Brown–Gitler spectra

H{\underline{{\mathbb{F}}}_{2}}_{\star}H\underline{{\mathbb{Z}}}

[

C_{2}
-equivariant integral Brown–Gitler spectra play an integral role in our construction of a
C_{2}
-equivariant splitting of
ku_{\mathbb{R}}\wedge ku_{\mathbb{R}}
.

For cyclic groups of prime order

the dual Steenrod algebra has been computed and is known not to be flat over the coefficients

[
50
,
34
]

-equivariant (integral) Brown–Gitler spectra, where

[
41
]
. Additionally, odd primary analogue of

has not been constructed yet

,

Even in the case where

constructing a splitting of

We now describe our main results. This is followed by a discussion of problems which are newly accessible with the methods developed in this paper.

1.3.
New directions

Similarly to the nonequivariant setting, we expect the splitting of
ku_{\mathbb{R}}\wedge ku_{\mathbb{R}}
(
Theorem

6.20
) will make
ku_{\mathbb{R}}
-based Adams spectral sequence computations newly accessible. We give some indication of this in
Section

6.4.3
where we describe how the splitting allows us to easily compute the
E_{1}
-page of the
ku_{{\mathbb{R}}}
-based Adams spectral sequence for the sphere. In future work, we plan to investigate whether the
ku_{\mathbb{R}}
-based Adams spectral sequence allows us to extend Isaksen–Guillou’s computations of
C_{2}
-equivariant stable stems
[
27
]
. Also, similarly to the nonequivariant setting, the
ku_{\mathbb{R}}
-based Adams spectral sequence should have a vanishing line allowing one to identify
v_{1}
-periodicity in the
C_{2}
-equivariant stable stems.

One may further wonder whether
ko_{C_{2}}
-based Adams spectral sequences are even more efficient for computing
C_{2}
-equivariant stable stems and detecting
v_{1}
-periodicity. Here,
ko_{C_{2}}
denotes the equivariant connective cover of
KO_{C_{2}},
the spectrum representing the
K
-theory of
C_{2}
-equivariant real vector bundles. Thus one may also be interested in constructing a
C_{2}
-equivariant splitting for
ko_{C_{2}}\wedge ko_{C_{2}}
in terms of finitely generated
ko_{C_{2}}
-modules. The techniques and methods of this paper can be used as a stepping stone towards such a construction.

Additionally, computations with
ku_{\mathbb{R}}
-based Adams spectral sequences are closely related to those of
ko_{C_{2}}
-Adams spectral sequences via the Wood cofiber sequence

\Sigma^{\sigma}ko_{C_{2}}\xrightarrow{\eta}ko_{C_{2}}\to ku_{{\mathbb{R}}},

where
\eta
is the first
C_{2}
-equivariant Hopf map. Thus in a precise sense
ku_{\mathbb{R}}
-based computations already contain
ko_{C_{2}}
information.

Another consequence of our Adams spectral sequence approach is partial progress towards constructing higher height
C_{2}
-equivariant Brown–Gitler spectra. Specifically, constructing
ku_{\mathbb{R}}
or
ko_{C_{2}}
-Brown–Gitler spectra and
BP_{\mathbb{R}}\langle 2\rangle
-Brown–Gitler spectra via an obstruction theoretic approach (analogous to the nonequivariant constructions of
[
24
,
37
]
) requires constructing certain maps
ku_{\mathbb{R}}\to H\underline{{\mathbb{Z}}}
and
BP_{\mathbb{R}}\langle 2\rangle\to ku_{\mathbb{R}},
respectively. This reduces to studying certain summands in the
H\underline{{\mathbb{Z}}}
and
ku_{\mathbb{R}}
relative Adams spectral sequences which we describe completely in
Section

5
and
Section

6
. Finishing the obstruction theoretic construction requires some additional analysis with the
C_{2}
-equivariant lambda algebra, which may prove difficult due to the complicated nature of the
C_{2}
-equivariant lambda algebra. However, nonequivariantly, such Brown–Gitler spectra have been useful in height
2
homotopy calculations
[
4
]
and thus if constructed could prove useful in
C_{2}
-equivariant homotopy calculations.

In addition to computational applications of the splitting, the cooperations algebra
{ku_{\mathbb{R}}}_{\star}ku_{\mathbb{R}}
(

6.25

can be described in terms of numerical polynomials. In particular,
KU_{*}KU
can be identified with the ring of finite Laurent series satisfying certain conditions
[
1
, Theorem 13.4]
. This extends to a description of

in terms of numerical polynomials
[
1
, Theorem 17.4]
, as well as to the torsion-free component of

[

-equivariant setting.

Similarly to the splitting of
ku_{\mathbb{R}}\wedge ku_{\mathbb{R}}

H\underline{{\mathbb{Z}}}\wedge H\underline{{\mathbb{Z}}}
into a sum of finite

and

H\underline{{\mathbb{Z}}}
-based Adams spectral sequence computations. For example, Burklund-Pstrągowski use the nonequivariant analogue of

to give a cleaner and more concise rephrasing of Toda’s original obstruction-theoretic approach to the construction of

-Adams spectral sequence
[
12

C_{2}
-equivariant case.

2.2.2.
Strategies for the nonequivariant splitting

For the purpose of comparison with the equivariant splitting of

in

We begin with a brief discussion of Kane’s approach
[

Following Mahowald’s strategy for the construction of the

-splitting, Kane used pairings
B_{0}(k)\wedge B_{0}(m)\to B_{0}(k+m)
to inductively construct maps

\tilde{\theta}_{k}:\Sigma^{2k}B_{0}(k)\to BP\langle 1\rangle\wedge BP\langle 1\rangle

such that the sum of the composites

\bigvee\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}BP\langle 1\rangle\wedge\Sigma^{2k}B_{0}(k)\xrightarrow{1\wedge\tilde{\theta}_{k}}BP\langle 1\rangle^{\wedge 3}\xrightarrow{\mu\wedge 1}BP\langle 1\rangle^{\wedge 2}

is a homotopy equivalence.

To construct this splitting, assume inductively that appropriate maps

\tilde{\theta}_{k}:\Sigma^{2k}B_{0}(k)\to BP\langle 1\rangle\wedge BP\langle 1\rangle

have been constructed. If
k
is not a power of
p
, take the
p
-adic decomposition
k=k_{0}+k_{1}p+\cdots+k_{r}p^{r}
, and consider the map

\Sigma^{2k}B_{0}(1)^{k_{0}}\wedge B_{0}(p)^{k_{1}}\wedge\cdots\wedge B_{0}(p^{r})^{k_{r}}\to B_{0}(k).

(If
k=p^{s}
for some

, take the same approach but with the decomposition
B_{0}(p^{s-1})^{\wedge p}
.) Consider the composite

\displaystyle\Sigma^{2k}B_{0}(1)^{k_{0}}\wedge B_{0}(p)^{k_{1}}\wedge\cdots\wedge B_{0}(p^{r})^{k_{r}}\xrightarrow{\tilde{\theta}_{0}^{k_{0}}\wedge\tilde{\theta}_{1}^{k_{1}}\wedge\tilde{\theta}_{r}^{k_{r}}}BP\langle 1\rangle

\displaystyle\wedge BP\langle 1\rangle\wedge\cdots\wedge BP\langle 1\rangle

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{\hskip 16.38895pt\hskip 16.38895pt}BP\langle 1\rangle

\displaystyle\wedge BP\langle 1\rangle,

where the second map is the standard multiplication map. The key step in the construction is to show that this composite factors through
B_{0}(k)
. One can then show that this factorization is the appropriate map
\tilde{\theta}_{k}:\Sigma^{2k}B_{0}(k)\to BP\langle 1\rangle\wedge BP\langle 1\rangle
.

The strategy for showing that the map factors through
B_{0}(k)
does not lend itself well to the equivariant setting for two reasons: first, it relies in part on connectivity arguments that the negative cone prevents us from replicating. Second, extending this argument to the equivariant setting would require us to deduce that in homology, various complicated cofibers of free
{\mathbb{M}}_{2}
-modules are also free. While these obstacles may be surmountable, we have found that the alternative strategy of using an Adams spectral sequence to lift the isomorphism on homology is most readily adaptable to the equivariant setting. Since this argument does not appear in the literature, we present it here. This Adams spectral sequence argument is most similar to Klippenstein’s splitting construction
[
38
]
. However, we use a
ku
-relative Adams spectral sequence as it makes the construction slightly cleaner. We also do not need his comparison with the Universal Coefficient Spectral Sequence as we only address the case

, not

. For ease of comparison with the rest of this paper, we present the

{\mathbb{M}}_{2}
is in positive degrees). The positive cone is isomorphic to the
{\mathbb{R}}
-motivic homology ring
{\mathbb{M}}^{\mathbb{R}}_{2}
of a point. Multiplicatively, the positive cone is a polynomial ring on two variables,
\rho
and
\tau.

The “negative cone”
NC
refers to the part of
{\mathbb{M}}_{2}
in degrees
(s,w)
with
w\geq 2.
Multiplicatively, the product of any two elements of
NC
is zero, so
{\mathbb{M}}_{2}
is a square-zero extension of
{\mathbb{M}}^{\mathbb{R}}_{2}.
Multiplications by
\rho
and
\tau
are non-zero in
NC
whenever they make sense. Thus elements of
NC
are infinitely divisible by both
\rho
and
\tau.
We use the notation
\frac{\gamma}{\rho^{j}\tau^{k}}
for the non-zero element in degree
(j,1+j+k),
which is consistent with the described multiplicative properties. The symbol
\gamma,
which does not correspond to an actual element of
{\mathbb{M}}_{2}
, has degree
(0,1).

The
{\mathbb{F}}_{2}[\tau]
-module structure on
{\mathbb{M}}_{2}
is essential for calculations filtered by powers of
\rho.
Thus we describe the
{\mathbb{F}}_{2}[\tau]
-module structure on
NC
in further detail. We define
{\mathbb{F}}_{2}[\tau]/\tau^{\infty}
to be the
{\mathbb{F}}_{2}[\tau]
-module
\colim_{n}{\mathbb{F}}_{2}[\tau]/\tau^{k}
. Following
[
26
, Remark 2.1]
, we write
\frac{{\mathbb{F}}_{2}[\tau^{k}]}{\tau^{\infty}}\{x\}
for the infinitely divisible
{\mathbb{F}}_{2}[\tau]
-module consisting of elements of the form
\frac{x}{\tau^{k}}
for
k\geq 1.
Note that
x
itself is not an element of
\frac{{\mathbb{F}}_{2}[\tau]}{\tau^{\infty}}\{x\}.
The idea is that
x
represents the infinitely many relations
\tau^{k}\cdot\frac{x}{\tau^{k}}=0
that define
\frac{{\mathbb{F}}_{2}[\tau]}{\tau^{\infty}}\{x\}.

With this notation in place,
{\mathbb{M}}_{2}
is equal to

{\mathbb{M}}^{{\mathbb{R}}}_{2}\oplus NC={\mathbb{M}}^{\mathbb{R}}_{2}\oplus\underset{s\geq 0}{\bigoplus}\frac{{\mathbb{F}}_{2}[\tau]}{\tau^{\infty}}\left\{\frac{\gamma}{\rho^{s}}\right\}

as an
{\mathbb{F}}_{2}[\tau]
-module.

4.1.
The homology of
\mathbf{BP_{\mathbb{R}}\langle n\rangle}

The authors first learned of a computation of
H_{\star}BP_{\mathbb{R}}\langle n\rangle,

from Christian Carrick in the context of Carrick, Hill, and Ravenel’s work on the homological slice spectral sequence in motivic and Real bordism
[
14
]
. While Carrick–Hill–Ravenel did indeed compute the equivariant homology groups
H_{\star}BP_{\mathbb{R}}\langle n\rangle,
the result does not appear in the final version of their paper, so we include an argument that we learned from Carrick here.

Recall that
\pi_{*\rho}BP_{\mathbb{R}}\cong\mathbb{Z}[{v}_{1},{v}_{2},\cdots]

[
35
, Theorem 6.18]
.
Consider the cofiber sequence

H\wedge\Sigma^{(2^{i}-1)\rho}BP_{\mathbb{R}}\xrightarrow{\cdot{v}_{i}}H\wedge BP_{\mathbb{R}}\xrightarrow{}H\wedge BP_{\mathbb{R}}/{v}_{i}.

Note that
{v}_{i}=0\in H_{\star}BP_{\mathbb{R}}
, so applying homotopy yields a short exact sequence

0\to H_{\star}BP_{\mathbb{R}}\to H_{\star}(BP_{\mathbb{R}}/{v}_{i})\to H_{\star-((2^{i}-1)\rho+1)}BP_{\mathbb{R}}\to 0.

This sequence splits as a sum of
H_{\star}BP_{\mathbb{R}}
-modules, so

H_{\star}(BP_{\mathbb{R}}/{v}_{i})\cong H_{\star}BP_{\mathbb{R}}\oplus H_{\star-((2^{i}-1)\rho+1)}BP_{\mathbb{R}}.

In particular,
H_{\star}(BP_{\mathbb{R}}/{v}_{i})
is a free
H_{\star}BP_{\mathbb{R}}
-module.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
g:BP_{\mathbb{R}}\to H
be the real orientation. The map

g_{\star}:H_{\star}(BP_{\mathbb{R}}/{v}_{i})\to H_{\star}H

is injective, with image the free
H_{\star}BP_{\mathbb{R}}
-submodule generated by
1
and
\bar{\tau}_{i}
.

Proof.

We can write the
H_{\star}BP_{{\mathbb{R}}}
-module splitting above as

H_{\star}BP_{{\mathbb{R}}}/{v}_{i}\cong H_{\star}BP_{{\mathbb{R}}}\{x_{0}\}\oplus H_{\star}BP_{{\mathbb{R}}}\{x_{1}\},

where
|x_{0}|=0
and
|x_{1}|=(2^{i}-1)\rho+1
. Note that everything in the first summand has degree a multiple of
\rho
, while
|\tau_{i}|=(2^{i})\rho-\sigma

=(2^{i}-1)\rho+1
. So in order to show that the map
g_{\star}:H_{\star}(BP_{\mathbb{R}}/{v}_{i})\to{\mathcal{A}}_{\star}
is injective, it will suffice to show that
g_{\star}(x_{1})
is nonzero.

Note that since
g_{\star}
is a map of
{\mathcal{A}}_{\star}
-comodules,
ker\ g_{\star}
is an
A_{\star}
-subcomodule of
H_{\star}BP_{{\mathbb{R}}}/{v}_{i}
. Suppose towards a contradiction that
g_{\star}(x_{1})=0
. This implies that
H_{\star}BP_{{\mathbb{R}}}\{x_{1}\}
is a sub-
A_{\star}
-comodule of
H_{\star}BP_{{\mathbb{R}}}/{v}_{i}
, and so the
H_{\star}BP_{{\mathbb{R}}}
-splitting

H_{\star}BP_{{\mathbb{R}}}/{v}_{i}\cong H_{\star}BP_{{\mathbb{R}}}\{x_{0}\}\oplus H_{\star}BP_{{\mathbb{R}}}\{x_{1}\}

would furthermore be an
{\mathcal{A}}_{\star}
-comodule splitting. If we then run the Adams spectral sequence

\Ext_{{\mathcal{A}}_{\star}}\left({\mathbb{M}}_{2_{\star}},H_{\star}BP_{{\mathbb{R}}}/v_{i}\right)\Longrightarrow\pi_{\star}BP_{{\mathbb{R}}}/{v}_{i},

we get

\pi_{\star}BP_{{\mathbb{R}}}/{v}_{i}\cong\pi_{\star}BP_{{\mathbb{R}}}\oplus\Sigma^{(2^{i}-1)\rho+1}\pi_{\star}BP_{{\mathbb{R}}}.

In particular, this tells us that
{v}_{i}
is nonzero in
\pi_{\star}BP_{{\mathbb{R}}}/{v}_{i}
. This is a contradiction, so indeed
g_{\star}x_{1}\neq 0
.

Now we will show that
g_{\star}(x_{1})\equiv\tau_{i}
mod
H_{\star}BP_{{\mathbb{R}}}
. The generator
x_{1}
is primitive, so
g_{\star}(x_{1})
must be a nonzero
A_{\star}
-comodule primitive. Note that
\bar{\tau}_{i}
is a primitive in
{{\mathcal{A}}}^{C_{2}}_{\star}/H_{\star}BP_{\mathbb{R}}
and has the right degree. So to show that
g_{\star}(x_{1})\equiv\bar{\tau}_{i}
, it will suffice to show that
{\Prim}({{\mathcal{A}}}^{C_{2}}_{\star}/H_{\star}BP_{\mathbb{R}})
contains only one element in degree
(2^{i}-1)\rho+1
. We will accomplish this by first showing that

{\Prim}^{2^{i+1}\rho+1}({{\mathcal{A}}}^{C_{2}}_{\star}/H_{\star}BP_{\mathbb{R}})\cong\Ext_{{\mathcal{E}}_{\star}}^{2^{i+1}-2,1,2^{i}-1}(\mathbb{M}_{2},\mathbb{M}_{2}),

and then analyzing this
\Ext

Recall that

{\Prim}({{\mathcal{A}}}_{\star}/H_{\star}BP_{\mathbb{R}})\cong\Hom_{{\mathcal{A}}_{\star}}\left({\mathbb{M}}_{2},{{\mathcal{A}}}_{\star}/H_{\star}BP_{\mathbb{R}}\right).

To analyze this
\Hom
group, consider the short exact sequence

0\to H_{\star}BP_{{\mathbb{R}}}\to{\mathcal{A}}_{\star}\to{\mathcal{A}}_{\star}/H_{\star}BP_{{\mathbb{R}}}\to 0

and induced long exact sequence

0\to\Ext_{{\mathcal{A}}_{\star}}^{s,0,w}(\mathbb{M}_{2},H_{*}BP_{{\mathbb{R}}})\to\Ext_{{\mathcal{A}}_{\star}}^{s,0,w}(\mathbb{M}_{2},{\mathcal{A}}_{\star})\to\Ext_{{\mathcal{A}}_{\star}}^{s,0,w}(\mathbb{M}_{2},{\mathcal{A}}_{\star}/H_{\star}BP_{{\mathbb{R}}})

\Ext_{{\mathcal{A}}_{\star}}^{*,1,*}(\mathbb{M}_{2},{\mathcal{A}}_{\star})=0
because
{\mathcal{A}}_{\star}
is free. Let
{\mathcal{E}}={\mathcal{E}}(Q_{0},Q_{1},\ldots)
and note that

\Ext^{s,f,w}_{{{\mathcal{A}}}^{C_{2}}_{\star}}({\mathbb{M}}_{2},H_{\star}BP_{\mathbb{R}})\cong\Ext^{s,f,w}_{{\mathcal{E}}_{\star}}({\mathbb{M}}_{2},{\mathbb{M}}_{2}).

Observe that
\Ext^{2^{i+1}-1,0,2^{i}-1}_{{\mathcal{E}}_{\star}}(\mathbb{M}_{2},\mathbb{M}_{2})=0

\Ext^{2^{i+1}-1,0,2^{i}-1}_{{\mathcal{A}}_{\star}}(\mathbb{M}_{2},{\mathcal{A}}_{\star}/H_{\star}BP_{{\mathbb{R}}})\cong\Ext_{{\mathcal{E}}_{\star}}^{2^{i+1}-2,1,2^{i}-1}(\mathbb{M}_{2},\mathbb{M}_{2}).

Now we will show that

Recall from

E_{2}
-page of the Adams spectral sequence (
6.1
) which appeared in the proof of
Theorem

6.20
, the same arguments imply this spectral sequence also collapses.
∎

6.4.1.

Another consequence of our proof of

6.20
is a computation of the

-cooperations algebra.

Theorem 6.25
.

The
ku_{\mathbb{R}}
-cooperations algebra
{ku_{\mathbb{R}}}_{\star}ku_{\mathbb{R}}
splits as

{ku_{\mathbb{R}}}_{\star}ku_{\mathbb{R}}\cong\overset{\infty}{\underset{k=0}{\bigoplus}}{ku_{\mathbb{R}}}_{\star-k\rho}{\mathcal{B}}_{0}(k),

where as a
{ku_{\mathbb{R}}}_{\star}

\displaystyle{ku_{\mathbb{R}}}_{\star}{\mathcal{B}}_{0}(k)\cong\bigoplus\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}H\underline{{\mathbb{Z}}}_{\star}\{x_{0},x_{1},\ldots,x_{\nu_{2}(2k!)-1}\}\oplus{ku_{\mathbb{R}}}_{\star}\{x_{\nu_{2}(2k!)}\}\oplus V_{k},

v_{1}x_{i-1}=\rho x_{i}

and
V_{k}
is a sum of suspensions of
H_{\star}
.

While we do not specify the degree of the summands appearing in
V_{k}
in the statement of
Theorem

6.25
due to the amount of bookkeeping that would be involved, our computational methods do allow one to precisely deduce them.

Proof.

Consider the
ku_{{\mathbb{R}}}
-Adams spectral sequence

\Ext_{{\mathcal{E}}(1)_{\star}}({\mathbb{M}}_{2},H_{\star}{\mathcal{B}}_{0}(k))\Longrightarrow{ku_{\mathbb{R}}}_{\star}{\mathcal{B}}_{0}(k).

This is a summand of the spectral sequence of
Equation

6.1
so this spectral sequence collapses. Further,
Proposition

6.7
gives a complete description of the
E_{2}
-page. Observe there are no hidden extensions for degree reasons.
∎

The methods we use to compute the
ku_{\mathbb{R}}
-cooperations algebra also yield an analogous
{\mathbb{R}}
-motivic calculation. Let
kgl
be the lift of
ku_{\mathbb{R}}
along the Betti realization functor from the
{\mathbb{R}}
-motivic stable homotopy category to the category of genuine
C_{2}
-spectra. Because there is no interaction between the negative cone and positive cone summands in our spectral sequence computation of
{ku_{\mathbb{R}}}_{\star}ku_{{\mathbb{R}}}
, we obtain the following
{\mathbb{R}}
-motivic calculation by simply omitting the negative cone summands in our computation of
{ku_{\mathbb{R}}}_{\star}ku_{\mathbb{R}}.

Corollary 6.26
.

The
kgl
-cooperations algebra
kgl_{*,*}kgl
is given by

kgl_{*,*}kgl\cong\bigoplus\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}BPGL\langle 0\rangle_{*,*}\{x_{0},x_{1},\cdots,x_{\nu_{2}(2k!)-1}\}\oplus kgl_{*,*}\{x_{\nu_{2}(2k!)}\}\oplus W,

with extensions
v_{1}x_{i-1}=\rho x_{i},
and where
|x_{i}|=(2i,i)
and
W
is a sum of suspensions of
{\mathbb{M}}_{2}^{\mathbb{R}}.

At this time, it is not know whether motivic analogues of integral Brown–Gitler spectra exist. The construction we use in the
C_{2}
-equivariant setting relies on a Thom spectrum model, which is not known to exist in the
{\mathbb{R}}
-motivic setting, so our methods do not currently provide a motivic analogue of

6.20
. However, we do have all the necessary ingredients to construct the following
{\mathbb{R}}
-motivic analogue of
Corollary

6.24
.

Corollary 6.27
.

Let
H{\mathbb{F}}_{2}^{{\mathbb{R}}}
denote the mod
2
motivic Eilenberg Maclane spectrum over
{\mathbb{R}}
. Then

kgl\wedge kgl\simeq\bigvee\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}\Sigma^{2k,k}kgl^{\langle\nu_{2}(k!)\rangle}\vee V,

where
kgl^{\langle\nu_{2}(k!)\rangle}
is the
\nu_{2}(k!)^{th}
-Adams cover in a minimal
H{\mathbb{F}}_{2}^{{\mathbb{R}}}
-resolution of
kgl
, and
V
is a sum of suspensions of
H{\mathbb{F}}_{2}^{{\mathbb{R}}}

Proof.

This is analogous to the equivariant proof, but we will modify our notation and strategy slightly as we do not have motivic integral Brown–Gitler spectra. Take
V
to be the sum of suspensions of
H{\mathbb{F}}_{2}^{\mathbb{R}}
realizing the positive cone portion of the summand
W
in
Corollary

6.26
, and let

H_{*,*}^{kgl}:=\pi_{*,*}\left(H{\mathbb{F}}_{2}^{{\mathbb{R}}}\underset{kgl}{\wedge}-\right).

On the level of homology,
H_{*,*}^{kgl}V
splits off from
H_{*,*}^{kgl}\left(kgl\wedge kgl\right)\cong H_{*,*}kgl
. So just as in the proof of
Proposition

6.2
, we can use the
kgl
-relative
H{\mathbb{F}}_{2}^{{\mathbb{R}}}
-motivic Adams spectral sequences

E_{2}^{s,f,w}\cong\Ext_{{\mathcal{E}}^{{\mathbb{R}}}_{\star}(1)}\left(H_{*,*}^{kgl}V_{k},H_{*,*}kgl\right)\Longrightarrow[V,kgl\wedge kgl]^{kgl}

E_{2}^{s,f,w}\cong\Ext_{{\mathcal{E}}_{\star}^{{\mathbb{R}}}(1)}\left(H_{*,*}kgl,H_{*,*}^{kgl}V_{k}\right)\Longrightarrow[kgl\wedge kgl,V]^{kgl}

to lift the homology splitting to

where

The collapse of this spectral sequence follows from omitting the negative cone summands in the proof of the main theorem (

∎

Our computational methods further yield an inductive description of the operations algebra for

[ku_{\mathbb{R}},ku_{\mathbb{R}}]

So we can use the Adams spectral sequence

\Ext_{A_{\star}}(H_{\star}{\mathcal{B}}_{0}(k),H_{\star}ku_{{\mathbb{R}}})\cong\Ext_{{\mathcal{E}}(1)_{\star}}(H_{\star}{\mathcal{B}}_{0}(k),{\mathbb{M}}_{2})\Longrightarrow[{\mathcal{B}}_{0}(k),ku_{\mathbb{R}}]

to compute this summand.

where
V_{k}
is a sum of suspensions of
{\mathbb{M}}_{2}

6.14
gives a description of

for all

6.4.3.
The
E_{1}
-page of the
ku_{{\mathbb{R}}}
-based Adams spectral sequence

The purpose of this section is to demonstrate how the splitting of
Theorem

6.20
allows us to easily compute the
E_{1}
-page of the
ku_{{\mathbb{R}}}
-based Adams spectral sequence for the sphere. We also give some information towards computing the
E_{2}
-page.

The
E_{1}
-page of the
ku_{{\mathbb{R}}}
-Adams spectral sequence has the form

E_{1}^{n,*,*}\cong\pi_{\star}\left(ku_{{\mathbb{R}}}\wedge\overline{ku_{{\mathbb{R}}}}^{\wedge n}\right)\Longrightarrow\pi_{\star}\mathbb{S}_{(2)},

where
\overline{ku_{{\mathbb{R}}}}
denotes the cofiber of the unit
\mathbb{S}\to ku_{{\mathbb{R}}}
.

It follows immediately from
Theorem

6.20
and
Corollary

6.24
that

ku_{{\mathbb{R}}}\wedge\overline{ku_{{\mathbb{R}}}}\simeq\bigvee\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}ku_{{\mathbb{R}}}\wedge\Sigma^{\rho k}{\mathcal{B}}_{0}(k)\simeq\bigvee\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\Sigma^{\rho k}ku_{{\mathbb{R}}}^{\langle\nu_{2}(k!)\rangle}\vee V,

where
V
is the sum of suspensions of
H
found in
Corollary

6.24
. We can iterate this splitting to describe the entire
E_{1}
-page of the
ku_{{\mathbb{R}}}
-Adams spectral sequence in terms of finite
ku_{{\mathbb{R}}}
-modules. In particular, the splitting implies

ku_{{\mathbb{R}}}\wedge\overline{ku_{{\mathbb{R}}}}^{\wedge n}\simeq\bigvee\limits_{i_{1},i_{2},\ldots,i_{n}\geq 1}ku_{{\mathbb{R}}}\wedge{\mathcal{B}}_{0}(i_{1})\wedge{\mathcal{B}}_{0}(i_{2})\wedge\cdots\wedge{\mathcal{B}}_{0}(i_{n}).

In this section, we will give a formula for the homotopy groups of each summand

ku_{{\mathbb{R}}}\wedge{\mathcal{B}}_{0}(i_{1})\wedge{\mathcal{B}}_{0}(i_{2})\wedge\cdots\wedge{\mathcal{B}}_{0}(i_{n}),

up to suspensions of
{\mathbb{M}}_{2}
. Locating the suspensions of
{\mathbb{M}}_{2}
is simply a matter of extensive bookkeeping, so we reserve it for forthcoming work on the
ku_{\mathbb{R}}
-Adams spectral sequence.

Proposition 6.29
.

Let
I=(i_{1},i_{2},\ldots i_{n})
. Then

H_{\star}\left({\mathcal{B}}_{0}(i_{1})\wedge{\mathcal{B}}_{0}(i_{2})\wedge\cdots\wedge{\mathcal{B}}_{0}(i_{n})\right)\cong L(I)\oplus F_{I},

where
L(I)
is the lightning flash module
L(\nu_{2}(i_{1}!)+\nu_{2}(i_{2}!)+\cdots\nu_{2}(i_{n}!))
and
F_{I}
is a free
{\mathcal{E}}(1)_{\star}
-comodule.

Proof.

In
Proposition

4.22
we showed that
H_{\star}{\mathcal{B}}_{0}(k)\cong L(\nu_{2}(k!))\oplus F_{k}
, where
F_{k}
is a sum of suspensions of
{\mathcal{E}}(1)_{\star}
. In the proof of
Proposition

4.22
, we observed that

\displaystyle{\mathcal{M}}_{*}\left(L(m)/(\rho,\tau),Q_{0}\right)

\displaystyle\cong{\mathbb{F}}_{2}\{1\}

\displaystyle{\mathcal{M}}_{*}\left(L(m)/(\rho,\tau),Q_{1}\right)

\displaystyle\cong{\mathbb{F}}_{2}\{x_{m}\}.

By the Künneth theorem for Margolis homology,

\displaystyle{\mathcal{M}}_{*}\left(L(m)/(\rho,\tau),Q_{0}\right)\otimes{\mathcal{M}}_{*}\left(L(n)/(\rho,\tau),Q_{0}\right)

\displaystyle\cong{\mathbb{F}}_{2}\{1\},

\displaystyle{\mathcal{M}}_{*}\left(L(m)/(\rho,\tau),Q_{1}\right)\otimes{\mathcal{M}}_{*}\left(L(\nu_{2}(n))/(\rho,\tau),Q_{1}\right)

\displaystyle\cong{\mathbb{F}}_{2}\{x_{m}\otimes x_{n}\}

\displaystyle\cong{\mathbb{F}}_{2}\{x_{m+n}\}.

Since

(6.30)

L(m)/(\rho,\tau)\otimes L(n)/(\rho,\tau)\cong\left(L(m)\otimes L(n)\right)/(\rho,\tau),

we can apply the Whitehead Theorem for Margolis homology (
Proposition

4.18
) and conclude that
L(m)\otimes L(n)\cong L(m+n)
, up to free
{\mathcal{E}}(1)_{\star}
summands. Thus again by the Whitehead theorem,

H_{\star}{\mathcal{B}}_{0}(i_{1})\otimes H_{\star}{\mathcal{B}}_{0}(i_{2})\cong L\left((\nu_{2}(i_{1}!)+\nu_{2}(i_{2}!)\right),

up to free
{\mathcal{E}}(1)_{\star}
-summands. Iterating this finishes the proof.
∎

We also describe each summand in terms of an Adams cover.

Proposition 6.31
.

Let
I=(i_{1},i_{2},\ldots i_{n})
. Then

ku_{{\mathbb{R}}}\wedge{\mathcal{B}}_{0}(i_{1})\wedge{\mathcal{B}}_{0}(i_{2})\wedge\cdots\wedge{\mathcal{B}}_{0}(i_{n})\simeq ku_{{\mathbb{R}}}^{\langle|I|\rangle}\vee V_{I},

where
V_{I}
is a sum of suspensions of
H
, and
|I|:=\nu_{2}(i_{1}!)+\nu_{2}(i_{2}!)+\cdots+\nu_{2}(i_{n}!)
-Adams cover of
ku_{{\mathbb{R}}}
. Furthermore,

\pi_{\star}\left(ku_{{\mathbb{R}}}\wedge{\mathcal{B}}_{0}(i_{1})\wedge{\mathcal{B}}_{0}(i_{2})\wedge\cdots\wedge{\mathcal{B}}_{0}(i_{n})\right)\cong\bigoplus\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}H\underline{{\mathbb{Z}}}_{\star}\{x_{0},x_{1},\ldots,x_{m-1}\}\oplus{ku_{\mathbb{R}}}_{\star}\{x_{m}\}\oplus W_{I},

where
m=\nu_{2}(i_{1}!)+\nu_{2}(i_{2}!)+\cdots+\nu_{2}(i_{n}!)
and
W_{I}
is a sum of suspensions of
{\mathbb{M}}_{2}
.

Proof.

First, it follows from the same argument as that of
Proposition

6.2
that we can split
ku_{{\mathbb{R}}}\wedge{\mathcal{B}}_{0}(i_{1})\wedge{\mathcal{B}}_{0}(i_{2})\wedge\cdots\wedge{\mathcal{B}}_{0}(i_{n})\simeq C_{I}\vee V_{I}
, where
V_{I}
is a sum of suspensions of

and
H_{\star}^{ku_{{\mathbb{R}}}}C_{I}\cong L(m)

Proposition

6.22
,
C_{I}\simeq ku_{{\mathbb{R}}}^{\langle|I|\rangle}

Theorem

6.25
.
∎

Typicality of periodic optimization over an expanding circle map
Rui Gao, Weixiao Shen, Ruiqin Zhang

Abstract
. We study the ergodic optimization problem over a real analytic expanding circle map. We show that in both the topological and the measure-theoretical senses, a typical C r C^{r} performance function has a unique maximizing measure and the unique maximizing measure is supported on a periodic orbit, for r = 1 , 2 , … , ∞ , ω r=1,2,\dots,\infty,\omega .



1.
Introduction

Given a continuous map
T:X\to X
from a non-empty compact metric space into itself, let
\mathcal{M}(T)
denote the collection of all
T
-invariant Borel probability measures. For a continuous function
f:X\to\mathbb{R}
, which is called
a performance function
in the following, let

\beta(f,T)=\sup_{\mu\in\mathcal{M}(T)}\int f\,\mathrm{d}\mu.

(1)

A measure
\mu\in\mathcal{M}(T)
is called a
maximizing measure
if
\beta(f,T)=\int f\,\mathrm{d}\mu
.
An outstanding problem in
ergodic optimization
is the so-called
typically periodic optimization
(
TPO
for short) problem: whether typically a maximizing measure is a
periodic measure
of
T
, i.e., supported on a periodic orbit, for chaotic dynamical systems
T
(such as uniformly expanding maps, or Axiom A diffeomorphisms). TPO was first conjectured by Hunt-Ott
[
23
,
22
]
to hold in a measure-theoretical sense for typical chaotic systems, and then by Yuan-Hunt
[
35
]
in a topological sense for Axiom A or uniformly expanding systems. See also
[
2
,
29
]
.

As evidence to support these conjectures, in the case that
T(x)=2x\mod 1
, Bousch
[
4
]
studied the specific one-parameter family
f_{t}(x)=\cos(2\pi(x-t))
and proved that for almost every
t
,
f_{t}
is uniquely maximized by a periodic measure. Indeed, he showed that for every
t
,
f_{t}
has a unique maximizing measure and the measure is
Sturmian
, i.e., supported in a semicircle. See
[
27
,
28
,
15
,
17
]
for similar results in this direction.

The topological TPO problem has a negative answer in the
C^{0}
category: for a general topological dynamical system
T:X\to X
, a generic continuous
f:X\to\mathbb{R}
has a unique maximizing measure and the measure is of zero entropy but fully supported; see
[
3
,
26
,
8
,
30
]
.
It has a positive answer for the space of Hölder functions, Lipschitz functions and
C^{1}
functions, in the case that
T
is uniformly expanding or Axiom A; see
[
11
,
12
,
20
]
. However, the perturbation techniques used in these works (originated from
[
35
]
) cannot be applied to spaces of performance functions with higher regularity.

There have been very few results on the measure-theoretical typicality in ergodic optimization. In
[
31
]
, it is proved that in a very general setting, having a unique maximizing measure is a prevalent property; see also
[
25
]
. In
[
1
,
13
]
, in the case that
T
is the full one-sided shift on two symbols, it is proved that for spaces of functions with a very strong modulus of continuity, periodic maximizing is a prevalent property in these spaces (similar spaces were studied earlier in
[
32
]
for the topological TPO problem). However, the required modulus of continuity is too strong and there is no natural analogy for functions defined on a manifold.

This paper is devoted to studying the TPO problem for expanding circle maps. A
C^{1}
map
T:\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}
is
expanding
if there exist
C_{*}>0
and
\lambda_{*}>1
such that

|(T^{n})^{\prime}(x)|\geq C_{*}\lambda_{*}^{n}

(2)

holds for all
x\in\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}
and all positive integers
n
. For
r=1,2,\dots,\infty,\omega
, we use
\mathcal{E}^{r}
to denote the space of all
C^{r}
expanding circle maps and use
\mathcal{C}^{r}
to denote the space of all
C^{r}
functions from
\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}
to
\mathbb{R}
. Here
C^{\omega}
means real analytic as usual. When
r=1,2,\dots,\infty
,
\mathcal{E}^{r}
and
\mathcal{C}^{r}
are endowed with the
C^{r}
topology. Moreover, for each
q=1,2,\dots
, we say that
f_{\mathbf{t}}:\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{R}
,
\mathbf{t}\in[0,1]^{q}
, is
a
C^{r}
family with
q
parameters
if
(\mathbf{t},x)\mapsto f_{\mathbf{t}}(x)
is
C^{r}
; the space of all
C^{r}
families with
q
parameters will also be endowed with the
C^{r}
topology.

Let
d(\cdot,\cdot)
denote the natural metric on
\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}
throughout the paper. For
T\in\mathcal{E}^{1}
and
f\in\mathcal{C}^{1}
, the
Aubry set
of
(f,T)
(this notion will be reviewed in §
2.3
), denoted by
\Omega(f,T)
, is defined as the collection of
x\in\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}
satisfying the following property: for any
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a positive integer
n
and
z\in T^{-n}(x)
such that
d(z,x)<\varepsilon
and

\Big|\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\left(f(T^{i}(z))-\beta(f,T)\right)\Big|<\varepsilon.

It is not difficult to deduce from
[
11
]
that (see Corollary
2.7
) if
\Omega(f,T)
is a periodic orbit of
T
, then
(f,T)
satisfies the following
C^{1}

locking property
: each function in some
C^{1}
open neighborhood of
f
has a unique maximizing measure and it is supported on the periodic orbit
\Omega(f,T)
.

Theorem A
.

Let
r=1,2,\dots,\infty
and let
q=1,2,\dots
. Then there is a residual subset
\mathcal{E}_{*}^{r}
of
\mathcal{E}^{r}
which contains
\mathcal{E}^{\omega}
such that for any
T\in\mathcal{E}_{*}^{r}
the following holds for a generic
C^{r}
family of functions
f_{\mathbf{t}}:\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{R}
,
\mathbf{t}\in[0,1]^{q}
: For Lebesgue a.e.
\mathbf{t}\in[0,1]^{q}
,
\Omega(f_{\mathbf{t}},T)
is a periodic orbit.

This provides a confirmation of Hunt-Ott’s conjecture
[
23
,
22
]
for expanding circle maps with
C^{r}
performance functions,
r=1,2,\dots,\infty
.

Theorem B
.

Let
r=1,2,\dots,\infty
. Then there is a residual subset
\mathcal{E}_{*}^{r}
of
\mathcal{E}^{r}
which contains
\mathcal{E}^{\omega}
such that the following holds for each
T\in\mathcal{E}_{*}^{r}
: There is an open and dense subset
\mathcal{D}^{r}(T)
of
\mathcal{C}^{r}
such that for each
f\in\mathcal{D}^{r}(T)
,
\Omega(f,T)
is a periodic orbit.

Yuan-Hunt’s conjecture asserts that we can take
\mathcal{E}_{*}^{r}=\mathcal{E}^{r}
in Theorem
B
. For
r=1
, this was proved in
[
20
]
.
For
r=2,3,\dots,\infty
, Theorem
B
provides strong support for Yuan-Hunt’s conjecture in the
C^{r}
topology, but the conjecture itself remains open.

Both theorems are deduced from Theorem
C
below, using the density of
\mathcal{E}^{\omega}
in
\mathcal{E}^{r}
for each
r=1,2,\dots,\infty
. In Theorem
C
, we prove that for
T\in\mathcal{E}^{\omega}
, in an appropriate space of
C^{\omega}
performance functions, being uniquely maximized by a periodic measure is a prevalent property. Working in the real analytic setting brings a key advantage: every real analytic performance function has a maximizing measure of entropy zero, as proved in
[
16
]
.

Prevalence is a concept introduced in
[
24
]
to address measure-theoretical typicality for infinite-dimensional spaces (it was recognized later by the authors of
[
24
]
that this notion was already introduced before in
[
10
]
). Let
V
be a topological vector space which is completely metrizable. A subset
S
of
V
is called
shy
(or a
Haar zero set
following
[
10
]
) if there is a Borel probability measure on
V
with compact support and a Borel set

\widetilde{S}\supset S
such that

\mu(\widetilde{S}-v)=0,\text{ for all }v\in V.

In this case, we say that
\mu
is a
transverse
measure for
S
.
A subset of
V
is called
prevalent
if its complement is shy. We recommend
[
21
]
for many applications of this notion in dynamical systems.

Given a linear subspace
\mathcal{X}
of
\mathcal{C}^{1}
and
T\in\mathcal{E}^{1}
, denote

\mathcal{X}_{\#}(T)=\{f\in\mathcal{X}:\text{$\Omega(f,T)$ is a periodic orbit}\}.

(3)

Denote
\mathbb{N}=\{0,1,\dots\}
and
\mathbb{N}_{1}=\{1,2,3,\dots\}
. The main result of this paper is as follows.

Theorem C
.

Let
T\in\mathcal{E}^{\omega}
and let
\mathcal{H}\subset\mathcal{C}^{\omega}
be a Banach space containing all trigonometric polynomials. Then
\mathcal{H}_{\#}(T)
is a prevalent subset of
\mathcal{H}
. More precisely, there exist
\varphi_{n}\in\mathcal{H}
,
n=1,2,\dots
such that
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\|\varphi_{n}\|_{\mathcal{H}}<\infty
and such that for each
f\in\mathcal{H}
,

m_{\infty}\left(\left\{(t_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}\in[0,1]^{\mathbb{N}_{1}}:f+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}t_{n}\varphi_{n}\not\in\mathcal{H}_{\#}(T)\right\}\right)=0,

where
m_{\infty}
denotes the infinite product of the Lebesgue measure on
[0,1]
.

The following Banach spaces
\mathcal{C}^{\omega}_{p,\rho}
,
1\leq p\leq\infty
and
\rho>0
, serve as concrete examples which satisfy the assumptions of Theorem
C
.
Let

U_{\rho}=\{x+\mathrm{i}y:x\in\mathbb{R},|y|<\rho\}/\mathbb{Z}\subset\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{Z},

and let
\mathcal{A}^{p}(U_{\rho})
(known as the
Bergman space
) denote the collection of all
L^{p}

holomorphic functions
F:U_{\rho}\to\mathbb{C}
, endowed with the
L^{p}
norm. Let

\mathcal{C}^{\omega}_{p,\rho}=\{f\in\mathcal{C}^{\omega}:f\text{ extends to a function in }\mathcal{A}^{p}(U_{\rho})\},

(4)

endowed with the norm induced from
\mathcal{A}^{p}(U_{\rho})
. Each of the spaces
\mathcal{C}^{\omega}_{p,\rho}
obviously contains all trigonometric polynomials. We remark that the inclusion from
\mathcal{C}^{\omega}_{p,\rho}
to
\mathcal{C}^{r}
is continuous,
r=0,1,\dots,\infty
, where
\mathcal{C}^{0}
denotes the space of continuous functions from
\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}
to
\mathbb{R}
endowed with the
C^{0}
topology. The case
r=0
follows from for example
[
14
, Theorem 1, p.7]
. Using the Cauchy formula, the rest cases follows.

Outline of the proof.
We shall now explain our strategy for the proof of Theorem
C
. Fix
T\in\mathcal{E}^{\omega}
and fix a Banach space
\mathcal{H}
as in Theorem
C
. Let
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{per}}
denote the collection of
f\in\mathcal{H}
admitting a periodic maximizing measure.

For each
f\in\mathcal{C}^{1}
, one can associate to
f
a
T
-invariant compact subset
K(f)=K(f,T)
such that
K(f)
depends on
f\in\mathcal{C}^{1}
in an upper semi-continuous manner and such that the
(f,T)
-maximizing measures are precisely the measures in
\mathcal{M}(T)
supported on
K(f)

(see Proposition
2.1
). A sufficient condition for
(f,T)
to have a periodic maximizing measure is that
T|K(f)
is injective.

We introduce the
Sturmian-like
property as a generalization of the Sturmian property mentioned before. See the remark after Definition
3.1
for a comparison of the Sturmian-like property with similar earlier generalizations of the Sturmian property given in
[
6
,
7
,
19
]
. We shall show that for “most”
f\in\mathcal{H}\setminus\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{per}}
,
T:K(f)\to K(f)
is Sturmian-like, which means that it has only finitely many branching points and outside these branching points,
T:K(f)\to K(f)
has a continuous inverse (see Definition
3.1
). To this end, we first show that failing to be Sturmian-like implies existence of a
supercritical value
(see Definition
3.3
) which satisfies a certain non-transversality condition. Theorem
4.1
, together with the result of
[
16
]
mentioned before, then implies that
\mathcal{H}\setminus(\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{per}}\cup\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{st}})
is a countable union of finite-dimensionally shy (see §
2.2
for its definition) subsets of
\mathcal{H}
, where

\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{st}}=\{f\in\mathcal{H}:T|K(f)\text{ is Sturmian-like}\}.

(5)

The proof of Theorem
4.1
is a modification of an argument of Tsujii
[
34
]
for a closely related transversality problem in the study of linear skew products over expanding circle maps.

Most of the argument so far works for high-dimensional expanding maps except that the result of
[
16
]
is for one-dimensional maps. Our last step, to show that
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{st}}\setminus\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{per}}
is a countable union of finite-dimensionally shy subsets, is more restricted to the one-dimensional case. Our argument is inspired by the method used to search for the pre-Sturmian condition in
[
4
]
. Exploiting the one-dimensional structure of
\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}
we show that if
f\in\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{st}}\setminus\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{per}}
has a unique maximizing measure, then
f
satisfies an identity (see Proposition
5.7
), and the identity can be destroyed by a suitable perturbation (see Theorem
5.9
).

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. In §
2
, we recall some known results which will be used in our argument. We shall use these results to deduce Theorems
A
and
B
from Theorem
C
. The rest of the paper is devoted to the proof of Theorem
C
. In §
3
, we introduce the Sturmian-like property and the concept of supercritical value, and discuss their relations. In §
4
, we show that most performance functions do not have a supercritical value. The proof of Theorem
C
is completed in §
5
.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we review some known facts, including basic facts about sub-actions and Aubry sets. We shall use these facts to deduce Theorems
A
and
B
from Theorem
C
.

2.1.
Sub-actions

Following
[
11
]
, a continuous function
g:\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{R}
is called a
sub-action
of
(f,T)\in\mathcal{C}^{0}\times\mathcal{E}^{1}
if for any
x\in\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}
,

g(x)+f(x)\leq g(T(x))+\beta(f,T).

The terminology is borrowed from Lagrangian dynamics.
A sub-action is called
calibrated
if

\sup_{y\in T^{-1}(x)}(g(y)+f(y))=g(x)+\beta(f,T)

for each
x\in\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}
.

Sub-actions play an important role in the ergodic maximization problem; see for example
[
4
,
11
,
5
,
12
,
2
,
29
,
20
]
. For
T\in\mathcal{E}^{1}
, it is well-known that any
f\in\mathcal{C}^{1}
has a Lipschitz calibrated sub-action
g
. This is often referred to as
Mañé’s lemma
. In fact, it is well-known that for
(f,T)\in\mathcal{C}^{1}\times\mathcal{E}^{1}
, a calibrated sub-action of
(f,T)
is automatically Lipschitz (see
[
4
, Lemme B]
or
[
18
, Proposition 3.3]
). Furthermore, there exists a constant
C_{0}(T)
such that any (Lipschitz) calibrated sub-action
g
of
f
satisfies

\mathrm{Lip}(g)\leq C_{0}(T)\cdot\mathrm{Lip}(f),

(6)

where
\mathrm{Lip}(\cdot)
denotes the best Lipschitz constant. Indeed,
C_{0}(T)
can be chosen uniformly in a small neighborhood of any
T_{0}\in\mathcal{E}^{1}
(see for example
[
12
, Lemma 2.1]
).

For a continuous sub-action
g
of
(f,T)\in\mathcal{C}^{1}\times\mathcal{E}^{1}
, let

E_{f,T,g}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}:g(x)+f(x)=g(T(x))+\beta(f,T)\},

(7)

and

\Lambda_{f,T,g}=\{x\in E_{f,T,g}:T^{n}(x)\in E_{f,T,g}\text{ for all }n\geq 0\}.

(8)

So
T(\Lambda_{f,T,g})\subset\Lambda_{f,T,g}
. Note that
g
is a calibrated sub-action of
(f,T)
if and only if
T(E_{f,T,g})=\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}
, and in this situation
T(\Lambda_{f,T,g})=\Lambda_{f,T,g}
.

Let
G_{f,T}
denote the collection of all (Lipschitz) calibrated sub-actions
g
of
(f,T)
. As mentioned above,

G_{f,T}\not=\emptyset
for any
(f,T)\in\mathcal{C}^{1}\times\mathcal{E}^{1}
.
Let

\mathcal{G}=\{(f,T,g):f\in\mathcal{C}^{1},T\in\mathcal{E}^{1},g\in G_{f,T}\}

be endowed with the topology as a subspace of
\mathcal{C}^{1}\times\mathcal{E}^{1}\times\mathcal{C}^{0}
.
Let

K(f,T)=\bigcup_{g\in G_{f,T}}\Lambda_{f,T,g}.

(9)

Let
\mathbf{Cpt}(\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z})
denote the space of non-empty compact subsets of
\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}
, endowed with the Hausdorff metric. Given a sequence
\{\Lambda_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
in
\mathbf{Cpt}(\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z})
, we define its
Kuratowski limit superior

\Ls_{n\to\infty}\Lambda_{n}:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}:\exists n_{k}\to\infty,x_{n_{k}}\in\Lambda_{n_{k}}\text{ such that }x_{n_{k}}\to x\},

which is an element of
\mathbf{Cpt}(\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z})
.
A map
F:A\to\mathbf{Cpt}(\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z})
defined on a metrizable space
A
is called
upper semi-continuous at

a\in A
if for
a_{n}\to a
in
A
, we have

\Ls_{n\to\infty}F(a_{n})\subset F(a).

In the next proposition, we show that the set
K(f,T)
depends on
(f,T)
upper semi-continuously.

Proposition 2.1
.

The following hold.

(i)

The map
(f,T,g)\mapsto\Lambda_{f,T,g}
is upper semi-continuous from
\mathcal{G}
to
\mathbf{Cpt}(\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z})
.

(ii)

K(f,T)
is compact and
T(K(f,T))=K(f,T)
for
(f,T)\in\mathcal{C}^{1}\times\mathcal{E}^{1}
.

(iii)

(f,T)\mapsto K(f,T)
is upper semi-continuous from
\mathcal{C}^{1}\times\mathcal{E}^{1}
to
\mathbf{Cpt}(\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z})
.

(iv)

For each
(f,T)\in\mathcal{C}^{1}\times\mathcal{E}^{1}
, the set of invariant Borel probability measures of
T|K(f,T)
is equal to
\mathcal{M}_{\max}(f,T)
,
where

\mathcal{M}_{\max}(f,T)=\left\{\mu\in\mathcal{M}(T):\int f\,\mathrm{d}\mu=\beta(f,T)\right\}.

Proof.

(i). Suppose
(f_{n},T_{n},g_{n})\to(f,T,g)
in
\mathcal{G}
and let
x_{n}\in\Lambda_{f_{n},T_{n},g_{n}}
be such that
x_{n}\to x
. Let us show
x\in\Lambda_{f,T,g}
.
Indeed, for each integer
l\geq 0
and each
n\geq 1
,
T_{n}^{l}(x_{n})\in E_{f_{n},T_{n},g_{n}}
, so

g_{n}(T_{n}^{l}(x_{n}))+f_{n}(T_{n}^{l}(x_{n}))=g_{n}(T_{n}^{l+1}(x_{n}))+\beta(f_{n},T_{n}),

and hence

g(T^{l}(x))+f(T^{l}(x))=g(T^{l+1}(x))+\beta(f,T).

This implies that
x\in\Lambda_{f,T,g}
.

(ii). Since
T(\Lambda_{f,T,g})=\Lambda_{f,T,g}
for all
g\in G_{f,T}
, we have
T(K(f,T))=K(f,T)
. To show that
K(f,T)
is compact,
let
\{x_{n}\}\subset K(f,T)
, so for each
n
,
x_{n}\in\Lambda_{f,T,g_{n}}
for some
g_{n}\in G_{f,T}
. Fixing an arbitrary
y\in\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}
, let
\tilde{g}_{n}=g_{n}-g_{n}(y)
. Then
\tilde{g}_{n}\in G_{f,T}
and
\Lambda_{f,T,g_{n}}=\Lambda_{f,T,\tilde{g}_{n}}
. Since
\mathrm{Lip}(\tilde{g}_{n})
is bounded in
n
, passing to a subsequence, we may assume that
\tilde{g}_{n}
converges to some
g
in the
C^{0}
topology. Then
g\in G_{f,T}
. By (i), any accumulation point of
\{x_{n}\}
belongs to
\Lambda_{f,T,g}\subset K(f,T)
. Thus
K(f,T)
is compact.

(iii). Suppose
(f_{n},T_{n})\to(f,T)
in
\mathcal{C}^{1}\times\mathcal{E}^{1}
and
x_{n}\in K(f_{n},T_{n})
,
x_{n}\to x
. Then for each
n
there exists
g_{n}\in G_{f_{n},T_{n}}
such that
x_{n}\in\Lambda_{f_{n},T_{n},g_{n}}
.
As in the proof of (ii),
x_{n}\in\Lambda_{f_{n},T_{n},\tilde{g}_{n}}
for
\tilde{g}_{n}=g_{n}-g_{n}(y)
, and passing to a subsequence, we may assume that
\tilde{g}_{n}
converges to some
g
in the
C^{0}
topology. Again
g\in G_{f,T}
. By (i), we obtain

x\in\Lambda_{f,T,g}\subset K(f,T)
.

(iv). Any
\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{\max}(f,T)
is supported on
\Lambda_{f,T,g}
for any continuous sub-action
g
of
f
. So
\mu
is supported on
K(f,T)
,
which implies that
\mu
is an invariant Borel probability measure of
T|K(f,T)
.

For the other direction, it suffices to show each ergodic Borel probability measure
\mu
of
T|K(f,T)
satisfies
\int f\,\mathrm{d}\mu=\beta(f,T)
.
By the ergodicity of
\mu
, there exists
x\in K(f,T)
such that

\int f\,\mathrm{d}\mu=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}f(T^{i}(x)).

Since
x\in K(f,T)
, there exists
g\in G_{f,T}
with
x\in\Lambda_{f,T,g}
. Thus for each
n\geq 1

\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}f(T^{i}(x))=n\beta(f,T)+g(T^{n}(x))-g(x),

which implies that
\int f\,\mathrm{d}\mu=\beta(f,T)
.
∎

2.2.
Shyness and prevalence

Let
\mathcal{V}
be a vector space over
\mathbb{R}
(not necessarily endowed with a topology). For each positive integer
N
, we say that a subset
S
of
\mathcal{V}
is
N
-dimensionally shy

if there exist
\varphi_{i}\in\mathcal{V}
,
i=1,2,\dots,N
, such that

m_{N}\left(\left\{(t_{i})_{i=1}^{N}\in[0,1]^{N}:f+\sum_{i=1}^{N}t_{i}\varphi_{i}\in S\right\}\right)=0

for each
f\in\mathcal{V}
, where
m_{N}
denotes the standard Lebesgue (outer) measure on
[0,1]^{N}
.
An
N
-dimensionally shy set in a Banach space is shy (recall its definition given ahead of Theorem
C
): we simply take
\mu
to be the pushforward of
the standard Lebesgue measure on the cube
[0,1]^{N}
to the compact set

\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{N}t_{i}\varphi_{i}:(t_{i})_{i=1}^{N}\in[0,1]^{N}\right\}.

It is straightforward to check that
\mu
is transverse to
S
.
A subset
S
of
\mathcal{V}
is called
finite-dimensionally shy
if it is
N
-dimensionally shy for some positive integer
N
.

For the proof of Theorem
C
, we need the following two propositions.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
S
be a countable union of finite-dimensionally shy subsets of a Banach space
\mathcal{V}
. Then there exists
\{\varphi_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\subset\mathcal{V}
with
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\|\varphi_{n}\|<\infty
such that for any
f\in\mathcal{V}
,

m_{\infty}\left(\left\{(t_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}\in[0,1]^{\mathbb{N}_{1}}:f+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}t_{n}\varphi_{n}\in S\right\}\right)=0,

where
m_{\infty}
denotes the infinite product of the Lebesgue measure on
[0,1]
.

Proof.

Let
S_{j}
,
j=1,2,\dots
be a sequence of finite-dimensionally shy subsets of
\mathcal{V}
. So for each
j
, there exists

\{\varphi_{n}^{j}\}_{n=1}^{N_{j}}\subset\mathcal{V}\setminus\left\{0\right\}
, such that for each
f\in\mathcal{V}
,

m_{N_{j}}\left(\left\{(t_{n})_{n=1}^{N_{j}}\in[0,1]^{N_{j}}:f+\sum_{n=1}^{N_{j}}t_{n}\varphi_{n}^{j}\in S_{j}\right\}\right)=0.

Put
\psi_{n}^{j}=\frac{1}{2^{j}N_{j}}\varphi_{n}^{j}/\|\varphi_{n}^{j}\|
. Let
\mathcal{N}=\{(n,j):j\in\mathbb{N}_{1},n\in\{1,2,\dots,N_{j}\}\}
. Then
\sum_{(n,j)\in\mathcal{N}}\|\psi_{n}^{j}\|=1
. For each
k=1,2,\dots
,

m_{\infty}\left(\left\{(t_{n}^{j})_{n,j}\in[0,1]^{\mathcal{N}}:f+\sum_{n,j}t_{n}^{j}\psi_{n}^{j}\in S_{k}\right\}\right)=0

by Fubini’s theorem. Thus

m_{\infty}\left(\left\{(t_{n}^{j})_{n,j}\in[0,1]^{\mathcal{N}}:f+\sum_{n,j}t_{n}^{j}\psi_{n}^{j}\in\bigcup_{k}S_{k}\right\}\right)=0.

∎

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
\mathcal{V}
be a topological vector space with a countable topological basis. Let
\mathcal{H}
be a linear subspace of
\mathcal{V}
and let
S\subset\mathcal{H}
. Suppose that for each
f\in S
, there is a neighborhood
\mathcal{U}
of
f
in
\mathcal{V}
such that
\mathcal{U}\cap S
is a countable union of finite-dimensionally shy subsets of
\mathcal{H}
. Then
S
is a countable union of finite-dimensionally shy subsets of
\mathcal{H}
.

Proof.

Let
(\mathcal{W}_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
be a countable topological basis for
\mathcal{V}
. Let
E
be the collection of
n\geq 1
such that
\mathcal{W}_{n}\cap S
is a countable union of finite-dimensionally shy subsets of
\mathcal{H}
. Then

S=\bigcup_{n\in E}(\mathcal{W}_{n}\cap S),

and the conclusion follows.
∎

Theorem 2.4
.

Let

and let

be a linear subspace of
\mathcal{C}^{1}
such that all trigonometric polynomials belong to
\mathcal{X}
. Then we have:

\mathcal{X}_{\mathrm{per}}(T)\setminus\mathcal{X}_{\#}(T)

-dimensionally shy subsets of
\mathcal{X}

(ii)

when

is a topological vector space,

is dense in
\mathcal{X}_{\mathrm{per}}(T)
.

Note that in assertion (ii), the topology of

is flexible and it can be irrelevant to the

Proof.

For each
T
-periodic orbit
\mathcal{O}
, let

denote the averaged Dirac measure
on
\mathcal{O}
, and let

\mathcal{X}_{\mathcal{O}}(T)=\{f\in\mathcal{X}:\mu_{\mathcal{O}}\text{ is }(f,T)\text{-maximizing}\}.

To prove (i), since

To this end, let us follow an argument in
[

]
. Fix an arbitrary
\mathcal{O}

Now let

Then

, and let us show that

To show that

for each

∎

In this section, we consider a fixed map

where

Let

denote the unique lift of

\widehat{T}(0)=0
via

Let

be the absolutely value of the degree of

and let
\mathbb{A}=\{0,1,\dots,d_{T}-1\}
. For each

and each

, define

\tau_{i}(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccl}\tau(x+i)&,&\text{if $T$ is orientation-preserving}\\
\tau(x+i)+1&,&\text{if $T$ is orientation-reversing}\end{array}\right..

Note that by definition,

T^{-1}(\pi(x))=\{\pi(\tau_{i}(x)):i\in\mathbb{A}\},\quad\forall x\in\mathbb{R},

and

\tau_{i}((0,1))\subset(0,1),\quad\forall i\in\mathbb{A}.

For each
\mathbf{i}=(i_{n})_{n\geq 1}\in\mathbb{A}^{\mathbb{N}_{1}}
and each

, let

\tau_{\mathbf{i},n}=\tau_{i_{n}}\circ\tau_{i_{n-1}}\circ\dots\circ\tau_{i_{1}}.

(11)

For each Hölder continuous function
f:\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{R}
, define

h^{f}_{\mathbf{i}}(x):=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(\widehat{f}\circ\tau_{\mathbf{i},n}(x)-\widehat{f}\circ\tau_{\mathbf{i},n}(0)\right),\quad x\in\mathbb{R},

(12)

where
\widehat{f}=f\circ\pi.
Note that the series on the right hand side converges absolutely.

Let
K
be a non-empty compact subset of
\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}
such that
T(K)=K
. Let
\#E
denote the cardinality of a set
E
from now on.

Definition 3.1
.

We say that
x\in K
is a
critical value
of
K
(or
T|K
), if

\#\left(T^{-1}(x)\cap K\right)>1.

A critical value
x
is called
regular
, if there exists a connected neighborhood
U
of
x
in
\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}
such that for each connected component
B
of
U\setminus\{x\}
, the following hold:

•

there is a continuous map
\tau_{B}:B\to\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}
such that
T\circ\tau_{B}=\mathrm{id}_{B}
;

•

if
y\in K\cap B
, then
\tau_{B}(y)
is the unique point in
T^{-1}(y)\cap K
.

We say that
K
(or
T|K
) is
Sturmian-like
if all of its critical values are regular.

Remark.
Recall that a Sturmian measure
\mu
is a
T_{2}
-invariant probability measure supported in a semicircle, where
T_{2}(x)=2x\mod 1
. These measures appear in Bousch’s work
[
4
]
on ergodic optimization for
f_{t}(x)=\cos(2\pi(x-t))
. The support of a Sturmian measure is Sturmian-like for
T_{2}
with at most one critical value. As a referee pointed out to us, the Sturmian-like property is closely related to the notion of “flower” studied in
[
6
,
7
,
19
]
. A set
F\subset\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}
is called a
flower
of the expanding circle map
T
if
F
is a union of finitely many non-degenerate closed arcs,
T(F)=\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}
, and the restriction of
T
to the interior of
F
is injective; see
[
19
, Remark 2.3 (c)]
. It can be easily checked that if a non-empty compact set
K
with
T(K)=K
is contained in some flower
F
, then
K
is Sturmian-like, its critical values are contained in
T(\partial F)
and each critical value has exactly two pre-images in
K
. On the other hand, as shown in
[
9
, Section 2.3]
, if
K
is a Sturmian-like set such that the non-wandering set of
T:K\to K
is equal to
K
, then
K
is contained in a flower. However, when
d_{T}\geq 3
, in general a Sturmian-like set
K
may not be contained in any flower, because a critical value of
K
may have three or more pre-images (see Example
3.2
), even though at most two of the pre-images can be limit points of
K
.

Example 3.2
.

Let
T_{q}(x)=qx\mod 1
for
q\geq 2
. Given
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}
, let
I_{x_{0}}
denote the closed arc
\{x_{0}+\pi(t):0\leq t\leq 1/q\}
. Then
S_{x_{0}}:=\cap_{n=0}^{\infty}T_{q}^{-n}(I_{x_{0}})
is Sturmian-like. Moreover, either
S_{x_{0}}
is a periodic orbit, or
S_{x_{0}}
has exactly one critical value
T_{q}(x_{0})
. See
[
15
, Appendix A]
for more details.

Now Suppose
q\geq 3
. Take
x_{0}
such that
S_{x_{0}}
is not a periodic orbit. Then
c:=T_{q}(x_{0})\in S_{x_{0}}
. Choose
c_{-1}\in T^{-1}(c)\setminus S_{x_{0}}
. Given a fixed point
b
of
T_{q}
, we can find a backward orbit
(c_{-n})_{n\geq 1}
of
T_{q}
such that
\lim_{n\to\infty}c_{-n}=b
. Then

K=S_{x_{0}}\cup\{c_{-n}:n\geq 1\}\cup\{b\}

is Sturmian-like and
c
is a critical value of
K
having exactly three pre-images in
K
.

For each
x\in\mathbb{R}
, let
\mathcal{CL}(x,K)
(“
\mathcal{CL}
” stands for calibrated) denote the collection of
\mathbf{i}\in\mathbb{A}^{\mathbb{N}_{1}}
such that

\pi(\tau_{\mathbf{i},n}(x))\in K,\,\,\forall n\geq 1.

Note that since
T(K)=K
,
\mathcal{CL}(x,K)\not=\emptyset
for each
x\in\pi^{-1}(K)
.

Definition 3.3
.

For each
f\in\mathcal{C}^{1}
, we say that
x\in\pi^{-1}(K)
is an
f
-supercritical value of
K
if there exist

\mathbf{i},\mathbf{j}\in\mathcal{CL}(x,K)
with distinct leading coordinates such that

\mathrm{D}h^{f}_{\mathbf{i}}(x)=\mathrm{D}h^{f}_{\mathbf{j}}(x),

where
h^{f}_{\mathbf{i}},h^{f}_{\mathbf{j}}
are as in (
12
) and
\mathrm{D}
stands for taking derivative.

Observe that
x\in\mathbb{R}
is an
f
-supercritical value of
K
if and only if so is
x+m
,
m\in\mathbb{Z}
. Therefore it makes sense to say that a point in
K
is an
f
-supercritical value
of
K
. To verify the observation, it suffices to consider the case
m=1
. First note that there exists a bijection
i\mapsto i^{\prime}
from
\mathbb{A}
to itself such that
\tau_{i^{\prime}}(x+1)-\tau_{i}(x)\in\mathbb{Z}
for any
x\in\mathbb{R}
.
As a result, given
\mathbf{i}\in\mathbb{A}^{\mathbb{N}_{1}}
, by induction on
n
, we can find a unique
\mathbf{i}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{A}^{\mathbb{N}_{1}}
such that

\tau_{\mathbf{i}^{\prime},n}(x+1)-\tau_{\mathbf{i},n}(x)\in\mathbb{Z},\quad\forall x\in\mathbb{R},\forall n\geq 1.

Then
\mathrm{D}h^{f}_{\mathbf{i}}(x)=\mathrm{D}h^{f}_{\mathbf{i}^{\prime}}(x+1)
, and
\mathbf{i}\in\mathcal{CL}(x,K)
if and only if
\mathbf{i}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{CL}(x+1,K)
. Moreover, if
\mathbf{i},\mathbf{j}\in\mathbb{A}^{\mathbb{N}_{1}}
have distinct leading coordinates, so do the correspondent
\mathbf{i}^{\prime},\mathbf{j}^{\prime}
. The conclusion follows.

We shall use repeatedly the following fact (see
[
16
, Lemma 3.5]
). For completeness let us reproduce its proof here. Recall
E_{f,T,g}
defined by (
7
).

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
g
be a continuous sub-action of
f\in\mathcal{C}^{1}
. Let
K
be a non-empty compact subset of
\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}
with
T(K)=K\subset E_{f,T,g}
. For any
x\in\pi^{-1}(K)
, any
y\in\mathbb{R}
and any
\mathbf{i}\in\mathcal{CL}(x,K)
, we have

g(\pi(y))-g(\pi(x))\geq h_{\mathbf{i}}^{f}(y)-h_{\mathbf{i}}^{f}(x).

Proof.

Since
g
is a sub-action of
f
, for each
N\geq 1
we have:

g(\pi(y))\geq\sum_{n=1}^{N}\widehat{f}(\tau_{\mathbf{i},n}(y))+g(\pi(\tau_{\mathbf{i},N}(y))).

Moreover, since
\mathbf{i}\in\mathcal{CL}(x,K)
, we have:

g(\pi(x))=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\widehat{f}(\tau_{\mathbf{i},n}(x))+g(\pi(\tau_{\mathbf{i},N}(x))).

Subtracting the two displayed expressions above first and then letting
N\to\infty
, the conclusion follows.
∎

In the following
\mathbb{A}
is endowed with discrete topology and
\mathbb{A}^{\mathbb{N}_{1}}
with product topology accordingly; consequently,
\mathcal{CL}(x,K)
introduced before is always compact.

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
f,g
and
K
be as in Lemma
3.4
. If
K
has no
f
-supercritical value, then
K
is Sturmian-like.

Proof.

Arguing by contradiction, assume that
K
is not Sturmian-like. Then there exists
x_{0}\in\pi^{-1}(K)
such that it is a critical value of
K
but not regular. As a result, there exist two sequences
x_{n},y_{n}\in\pi^{-1}(K)
, both converging to
x_{0}
from the same side (say from the right hand side), and distinct
i_{1},j_{1}\in\mathbb{A}
, such that for each
n
,

\tau_{i_{1}}(x_{n}),\tau_{j_{1}}(y_{n})\in\pi^{-1}(K).

As
T(K)=K
, there exist

\mathbf{i}^{n}=(i^{n}_{k})_{k\geq 1}\in\mathcal{CL}(x_{n},K)\quad\text{and}\quad\mathbf{j}^{n}=(j^{n}_{k})_{k\geq 1}\in\mathcal{CL}(y_{n},K)

such that
i^{n}_{1}=i_{1}\neq j_{1}=j^{n}_{1}
.
Passing to a subsequence, we may assume
\mathbf{i}^{n}\to\mathbf{i}
and
\mathbf{j}^{n}\to\mathbf{j}
.
By continuity,
\mathbf{i},\mathbf{j}\in\mathcal{CL}(x_{0},K)
.
By Lemma
3.4
,

\int_{x_{0}}^{x_{n}}\mathrm{D}h^{f}_{\mathbf{i}^{n}}(t)\,\mathrm{d}t\geq g(\pi(x_{n}))-g(\pi(x_{0}))\geq\int_{x_{0}}^{x_{n}}\mathrm{D}h^{f}_{\mathbf{j}}(t)\,\mathrm{d}t,

\int_{x_{0}}^{y_{n}}\mathrm{D}h^{f}_{\mathbf{j}^{n}}(t)\,\mathrm{d}t\geq g(\pi(y_{n}))-g(\pi(x_{0}))\geq\int_{x_{0}}^{y_{n}}\mathrm{D}h^{f}_{\mathbf{i}}(t)\,\mathrm{d}t.

Dividing the first inequality by
x_{n}-x_{0}
and then letting
n\to\infty
gives

\mathrm{D}h^{f}_{\mathbf{i}}(x_{0})\geq\mathrm{D}h^{f}_{\mathbf{j}}(x_{0}).

Here we used the fact that as
n\to\infty
,
\mathrm{D}h^{f}_{\mathbf{i}^{n}}(t)
converges to
\mathrm{D}h^{f}_{\mathbf{i}}(t)
locally uniformly in
t
. Similarly, the second gives

\mathrm{D}h^{f}_{\mathbf{j}}(x_{0})\geq\mathrm{D}h^{f}_{\mathbf{i}}(x_{0}).

Thus

\mathrm{D}h^{f}_{\mathbf{j}}(x_{0})=\mathrm{D}h^{f}_{\mathbf{i}}(x_{0}).

Since
i_{1}\neq j_{1}
, this implies that
x_{0}
is an
f
-supercritical value, a contradiction.
∎

Recall
K(f,T)
defined in (
9
). We write
K(f)=K(f,T)
for short hereafter. Also recall that by Proposition
2.1
, for each
f\in\mathcal{C}^{1}
,
T(K(f))=K(f)
, the maximizing measures of
(f,T)
are exactly the
T
-invariant Borel probability measures supported on
K(f)
,
and
K(f)
depends on
f
in an upper semi-continuous manner. We say that
f\in\mathcal{C}^{1}
is
supercritical
if
K(f)
has an
f
-supercritical value.

Corollary 3.6
.

Assume that
(f,T)
has a unique maximizing measure. Then either
K(f)
is Sturmian-like or
f
is supercritical.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.5
,
K(f)=\Omega(f,T)
, so for each continuous sub-action
g
of
(f,T)
,
K(f)\subset E_{f,T,g}
. Alternatively, since
(f,T)
has a unique maximizing measure, it is well-known that there exists a unique calibrated sub-action
g
of
(f,T)
up to an additive constant (see
[
4
, Lemme C]
or
[
18
, Proposition 6.7]
), so
K(f)=\Lambda_{f,T,g}\subset E_{f,T,g}
for this
g
. Thus the conclusion follows from Proposition
3.5
applied to
K=K(f)
.
∎

Let us end this section with the following remark, and this fact will not be (explicitly) used in this paper.

Proposition 3.7
.

Being not supercritical is an open property in
\mathcal{C}^{1}
.

Proof.

Suppose that
f_{n}\to f_{0}
in
\mathcal{C}^{1}
and for each
n
,
K(f_{n})
has an
f_{n}
-supercritical value
x_{n}\in\pi^{-1}(K(f_{n}))
. We need to show that
f_{0}
is supercritical. Without loss of generality, we may assume
x_{n}\in[0,1)
. After passing to a subsequence, we may assume
x_{n}\to x_{0}
.
By the upper semi-continuity of
K(\cdot)
, we have
\pi(x_{0})\in K(f_{0})
. Let
\mathbf{i}^{n}=(i^{n}_{k})_{k=1}^{\infty},\mathbf{j}^{n}=(j_{k}^{n})_{k=1}^{\infty}
be elements in
\mathcal{CL}(x_{n},K(f_{n}))
such that

i^{n}_{1}\not=j^{n}_{1}
and

\mathrm{D}h^{f_{n}}_{\mathbf{i}^{n}}(x_{n})=\mathrm{D}h^{f_{n}}_{\mathbf{j}^{n}}(x_{n}).

(13)

Passing to a further subsequence, we may assume that
\mathbf{i}^{n}\to\mathbf{i}=(i_{k})_{k=1}^{\infty}
and
\mathbf{j}^{n}\to(j_{k})_{k=1}^{\infty}
in
\mathbb{A}^{\mathbb{N}_{1}}
.
Then
i_{1}\not=j_{1}
. Again by the upper semi-continuity of
K(\cdot)
, we have
\mathbf{i},\mathbf{j}\in\mathcal{CL}(x_{0},K(f_{0}))
. Letting
n\to\infty
in (
13
), we obtain

\mathrm{D}h^{f}_{\mathbf{i}}(x_{0})=\mathrm{D}h^{f}_{\mathbf{j}}(x_{0}),

i.e.,
x_{0}
is an
f_{0}
-supercritical value of
K(f_{0})
.
∎

4.
Shyness of existence of supercritical values

The goal of this section is to prove the following theorem.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
T\in\mathcal{E}^{2}
such that
T(\pi(0))=\pi(0)
. Fix
C_{*}>0
and
\lambda_{*}>1
such that (
2
) holds. Let
\mathcal{D}
be a dense subset of
\mathcal{C}^{1}
consisting of
C^{2}
functions.
Let
f_{0}\in\mathcal{C}^{2}
be such that

h_{\mathrm{top}}(T|K(f_{0}))<\tfrac{1}{3}\log\lambda_{*}

and such that
K(f_{0})
contains no periodic point. Then there exists an open neighborhood
\mathcal{U}
of
f_{0}
in
\mathcal{C}^{2}
and a finite sequence
\{\varphi_{j}\}_{j=1}^{J}\subset\mathcal{D}
such that
for any
f\in\mathcal{C}^{2}
,

\left\{(t_{1},\dots,t_{J})\in[0,1]^{J}:f+t_{1}\varphi_{1}+\dots+t_{J}\varphi_{J}\in\mathcal{U}\mbox{ is
supercritical}\right\}

has Lebesgue measure zero.

The following is an immediately corollary, where
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{per}}=\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{per}}(T)
is defined in (
10
). It is worthy mentioning that the proof of this corollary relies on
[
16
]
, where the analyticity of
T
and
f\in\mathcal{H}
is essentially used.

Corollary 4.2
.

Let
T
and
\mathcal{H}
be as in Theorem
C
. Suppose
T(\pi(0))=\pi(0)
and let

\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{sc}}=\{f\in\mathcal{H}:f\text{ is supercritical}\,\}.

(14)

Then
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{sc}}\setminus\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{per}}

is a countable union of finite-dimensionally shy subsets of
\mathcal{H}
.

Proof.

By
[
16
]
, for each
f\in\mathcal{H}
, either each
\mu\in\mathcal{M}(T)
is a maximizing measure of
f
, or each maximizing measure has entropy zero. In the former case,
f\in\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{per}}
; in the latter case, by Proposition
2.1
and the variational principle,
h_{\mathrm{top}}(T|K(f))=0
. So for each
f_{0}\in\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{sc}}\setminus\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{per}}
,
h_{\mathrm{top}}(T|K(f_{0}))=0
. Applying Theorem
4.1
to
\mathcal{D}=\mathcal{H}
, for
f_{0}
as above we can find an open neighborhood
\mathcal{U}
of
f_{0}
in
\mathcal{C}^{2}
such that
\mathcal{U}\cap(\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{sc}}\setminus\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{per}})
is finite-dimensionally shy. Then by Proposition
2.3
(taking
\mathcal{V}=\mathcal{C}^{2}
), the corollary follows.
∎

We shall adopt an argument from Tsujii
[
34
]
to prove Theorem
4.1
. For preparation, we need some technical lemmas. To begin with, let us introduce some notations. Recall that
\mathbb{A}=\{0,1,\dots,d_{T}-1\}
.

•

If
\mathbf{i}\in\mathbb{A}^{n}
for some
n=1,2,\dots
, then
\mathbf{i}
is called a
finite word
and denote
|\mathbf{i}|=n
. If
\mathbf{i}\in\mathbb{A}^{\mathbb{N}_{1}}
, then
\mathbf{i}
is called an
infinite word
and denote
|\mathbf{i}|=\infty
. By saying a
word
we mean either a finite or an infinite word.

•

For words
\mathbf{i},\mathbf{j},\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v},\dots
, their first letters will be denoted by
i_{1},j_{1},u_{1},v_{1},\dots
respectively.

•

The notation
\tau_{\mathbf{i},n}
introduced in (
11
) still makes sense for a finite word
\mathbf{i}
provided that
|\mathbf{i}|\geq n
.

•

Given
f\in\mathcal{C}^{1}
, for any word
\mathbf{i}
, as a generalization of (
12
), let

h^{f}_{\mathbf{i}}:=\sum_{n=1}^{|\mathbf{i}|}\left(\widehat{f}\circ\tau_{\mathbf{i},n}-\widehat{f}\circ\tau_{\mathbf{i},n}(0)\right).

•

For
r=1,2,\dots
and

,
\|f\|_{C^{r}}:=\max_{0\leq k\leq r}\|\mathrm{D}^{k}f\|_{C^{0}}
.

Lemma 4.3
.

There exists a constant
C(T)>1
such that for any
f\in\mathcal{C}^{2}
and any word
\mathbf{i}
, the following holds:

|\mathrm{D}h^{f}_{\mathbf{i}}(x)-\mathrm{D}h^{f}_{\mathbf{i}}(y)|\leq C(T)\cdot\|\mathrm{D}f\|_{C^{1}}\cdot|x-y|,\quad\forall x,y\in\mathbb{R}.

Proof.

Direct calculation yields that

|\mathrm{D}h^{f}_{\mathbf{i}}(x)-\mathrm{D}h^{f}_{\mathbf{i}}(y)|\leq\sum_{n=1}^{|\mathbf{i}|}\sup_{z\in\mathbb{R}}|(\widehat{f}\circ\tau_{\mathbf{i},n})^{\prime\prime}(z)|\cdot|x-y|,

and

|(\widehat{f}\circ\tau_{\mathbf{i},k})^{\prime\prime}(z)|\leq\|\mathrm{D}f\|_{C^{0}}\cdot|\tau_{\mathbf{i},n}^{\prime\prime}(z)|+\|\mathrm{D}^{2}f\|_{C^{0}}\cdot|\tau_{\mathbf{i},n}^{\prime}(z)|^{2}.

On the other hand, by (
2
),
|\tau_{\mathbf{i},n}^{\prime}|\leq(C_{*}\lambda_{*}^{n})^{-1}
, and the conclusion follows once we show that
|\tau_{\mathbf{i},n}^{\prime\prime}|\leq C_{0}\lambda_{*}^{-n}
for some
C_{0}>1
dependent only on
T
. To estimate
|\tau_{\mathbf{i},n}^{\prime\prime}|
, denote
\mathbf{i}=i_{1}i_{2}\dots
and
\tau_{\mathbf{i},0}=\mathrm{id}
. Then by (
11
) and the chain rule,

\tau_{\mathbf{i},n}^{\prime}=\prod_{m=1}^{n}\tau_{i_{m}}^{\prime}\circ\tau_{\mathbf{i},m-1},

and hence

\tau_{\mathbf{i},n}^{\prime\prime}=\sum_{m=1}^{n}\frac{\tau_{\mathbf{i},n}^{\prime}}{\tau_{i_{m}}^{\prime}\circ\tau_{\mathbf{i},m-1}}\cdot\left(\tau_{i_{m}}^{\prime\prime}\circ\tau_{\mathbf{i},m-1}\right)\cdot\tau_{\mathbf{i},m-1}^{\prime}.

Since
|\tau_{i_{m}}^{\prime}|
is bounded from below,
|\tau_{i_{m}}^{\prime\prime}|
is bounded from above and
|\tau_{\mathbf{i},k}^{\prime}|\leq(C_{*}\lambda_{*}^{k})^{-1}
, the desired upper bound of
|\tau_{\mathbf{i},n}^{\prime\prime}|
follows.
∎

For any

Now suppose

i_{k+1}i_{k+2}\dots i_{k+N},j_{k+1}j_{k+2}\dots j_{k+N}\in\mathcal{A}_{N}

.

Lemma 5.8

\displaystyle|t_{1}-t_{2}|\left|\int\varphi^{\prime}\psi_{F_{t_{1}}}\,\mathrm{d}x\right|-\|F^{\prime}_{t_{2}}\|_{\infty}\|\psi_{F_{t_{1}}}-\psi_{F_{t_{2}}}\|_{1},

and since
F_{t_{1}},F_{t_{2}}\in\mathcal{U}_{f_{0}}\cap\mathcal{H}^{\mathcal{M}}
, by (
29
), (
30
) and Lemma
5.8
, we have

\Delta_{1}\geq L|t_{1}-t_{2}|-C_{1}C_{2}\hat{\delta}.

(34)

Secondly, for
j=1,2
and for each
0\leq k<n_{j}
,

\displaystyle\left|F_{t_{1}}(T^{k}(a_{j}(F_{t_{1}})))-F_{t_{2}}(T^{k}(a_{j}(F_{t_{2}})))\right|

\displaystyle\leq{}

\displaystyle|F_{t_{1}}(T^{k}(a_{j}(F_{t_{1}})))-F_{t_{2}}(T^{k}(a_{j}(F_{t_{1}})))|+|F_{t_{2}}(T^{k}(a_{j}(F_{t_{1}})))-F_{t_{2}}(T^{k}(a_{j}(F_{t_{2}})))|

\displaystyle\leq{}

\displaystyle|t_{1}-t_{2}|\cdot|\varphi(T^{k}(a_{j}(F_{t_{1}})))|+\|F^{\prime}_{t_{2}}\|_{\infty}\cdot d\big(T^{k}(a_{j}(F_{t_{1}})),T^{k}(a_{j}(F_{t_{2}}))\big),

\displaystyle\leq{}

\displaystyle|t_{1}-t_{2}|+C_{0}C_{2}\cdot\delta(F_{t_{1}},F_{t_{2}}).

Note that in the last step we used
F_{t_{1}},F_{t_{2}}\in\mathcal{U}_{f_{0}}\cap\mathcal{H}^{\mathcal{M}}
,
|\varphi|\leq 1
, (

) and (

).
It follows that

\Delta_{2,j}\leq n_{j}\left(|t_{1}-t_{2}|+C_{0}C_{2}\cdot\delta(F_{t_{1}},F_{t_{2}})\right),\quad j=1,2.

(35)

Thirdly, for

, since

maximizes

, we have:

\int F_{t_{1}}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{F_{t_{1}}}-\int F_{t_{2}}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{F_{t_{2}}}\leq\int(F_{t_{1}}-F_{t_{2}})\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{F_{t_{1}}}=(t_{1}-t_{2})\int\varphi\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{F_{t_{1}}}\leq|t_{1}-t_{2}|,

Finally, let us show that the set

S:=\{t\in[0,1]:F_{t}\in\mathcal{U}_{f_{0}}\cap\mathcal{H}^{\mathcal{M}}\}

has Lebesgue measure zero.

Let
(V,V^{\prime})
be the underlying frame of
\mathcal{M}
. Let

V^{n}=\{x\in V^{\prime}:T(x),T^{2}(x),\dots,T^{n}(x)\in V\}

for each
n
. Let
\mathcal{R}^{n}
denote the collection of rectangles
U_{1}\times U_{2}\times\dots\times U_{p}
,
where
U_{1},U_{2},\dots,U_{p}
are connected components of
V^{n}
. As the complexity of
(V,V^{\prime})
is less than
\frac{1}{p}\log\lambda_{*}
, there exist
1<\lambda_{2}<\lambda_{1}<\lambda_{*}
such that the number of elements in
\mathcal{R}^{n}
is at most
\lambda_{2}^{n}
, and each of them has diameter at most

\lambda_{1}^{-n}
, provided that
n
is large enough. Thus

\sum_{R\in\mathcal{R}^{n}}\diam(R)(\log\diam(R))^{2}\to 0

(37)

as
n\to\infty
.

Let
\mathbf{c}(F_{t})=(c_{\nu}(F_{t}))_{\nu=1}^{p}
for
t\in S
. Note that

\mathbf{c}(F_{t})
is contained in a rectangle in
\mathcal{R}^{n}
. On the other hand, by (
31
),
if
\mathbf{c}(F_{t_{1}}),\mathbf{c}(F_{t_{2}})
are contained in the same element
R
of

\mathcal{R}^{n}
, then

|t_{1}-t_{2}|\leq C_{3}\cdot\diam(R)(\log\diam(R))^{2}.

Combining this with (
37
), the statement follows.
∎

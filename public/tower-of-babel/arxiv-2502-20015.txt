Generating long-distance magnetic couplings with flat bands
G. Bouzerar, M. Thumin

Abstract
One of the great challenges for the large-scale development of quantum technologies is to generate and control the entanglement of quantum bits through interactions of sufficiently long range. Two decades ago, spin chains have been proposed for quantum communication. Unfortunately, couplings are of very short range in general which drastically limits the communication to very short distances. Here, we demonstrate that the presence of flat bands (FBs) can trigger very long-distance magnetic couplings in spin chains. Furthermore, we show that the typical decaying lengthscale is directly related to the quantum metric of the flat band eigenstates. We believe that our unexpected findings could open up an alternative route to enable long-distance quantum communication.

In conclusion, we have investigated the impact of flat bands on magnetic couplings in one-dimensional spin chains. We have highlighted a counter-intuitive and unexpected phenomenon: couplings between localized spins can increase dramatically as the distance between them increases. We were able to show that the quantum metric
\langle g\rangle
characterizing flat band eigenstates is at the origin of these surprising results.
It turns out that the QM controls the range of the couplings, highlighting complex scaling of the decay length with
\langle g\rangle
. We believe that our work could be of particular interest to research devoted to qubit entanglement and quantum communication, and hope that it will motivate experimental studies possibly using metallic organic frameworks to synthesize this type of magnetic structure in order to confirm the spectacular effects highlighted in our study.

Here, we consider

, the conclusions are the same if
JS\leq 0
, one just has to invert the
\uparrow
and
\downarrow
sectors.
In the basis
(c_{Ak\sigma}^{\dagger},c_{Bk\sigma}^{\dagger},c_{Ck\sigma}^{\dagger})
, the
3\times 3
spin resolved Hamiltonian reads,

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{\sigma}=\begin{bmatrix}0&-\alpha t&-2t\cos{(ka/2)}\\
-\alpha t&z_{\sigma}\frac{JS}{2}&0\\
-2t\cos{(ka/2)}&0&z_{\sigma}\frac{JS}{2}\\
\end{bmatrix},

(S1)

where
z_{\sigma}=\pm 1
for
\sigma=\uparrow
or
\downarrow
.

The three eigenvalues are,

\displaystyle E^{0\sigma}_{k}=z_{\sigma}\frac{JS}{2},

(S2)

\displaystyle E^{\pm\sigma}_{k}=\frac{1}{2}\Big(z_{\sigma}\frac{JS}{2}\pm\sqrt{\Delta^{k}_{\sigma}}\Big),

(S3)

with
\Delta^{k}_{\sigma}=(\frac{J^{2}S^{2}}{4}+4\alpha^{2}t^{2}+16t^{2}\cos^{2}{(ka/2)})
.

These bands are depicted in Fig.(
S1
) [a] for
\alpha=1
and
JS=1
.
The associated eigenvectors are,

\displaystyle|\psi^{0\sigma}_{k}\rangle=\frac{1}{(\alpha^{2}+4\cos^{2}{ka/2})^{1/2}}\begin{bmatrix}0\\
-2\cos{(ka/2)}\\
\alpha\\
\end{bmatrix},

(S4)

and those of the dispersive bands,

\displaystyle|\psi^{\pm\sigma}_{k}\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N^{\pm}_{\sigma}}}\begin{bmatrix}\frac{1}{2t}(-z_{\sigma}\frac{JS}{2}\pm\sqrt{\Delta^{k}_{\sigma}})\\
-\alpha\\
-2\cos{(ka/2)}\\
\end{bmatrix},

(S5)

where
N^{\pm}_{\sigma}=8\cos^{2}{(ka/2)}+2\alpha^{2}+\frac{J^{2}S^{2}}{8t^{2}}\mp\frac{1}{4}z_{\sigma}\frac{JS\sqrt{\Delta^{k}_{\sigma}}}{t^{2}}
.

We recall that the couplings are given by Eq. (3) and Eq. (4) in the main text. More precisely, the (B,B) coupling can be decomposed into different inter-band contributions,

\displaystyle J_{BB}(R)=\sum_{pq}I_{BB}^{pq}(R),

(S6)

\displaystyle I_{BB}^{pq}(R)=\frac{(JS)^{2}}{2N^{2}_{c}}\sum_{k,k^{\prime}}e^{i(k-k^{\prime})R}A_{kB,k^{\prime}B}^{pq}\frac{f(E^{p\uparrow}_{k})-f(E^{q\downarrow}_{k^{\prime}})}{E^{p\uparrow}_{k}-E^{q\downarrow}_{k^{\prime}}},

(S7)

where
N_{c}
is the number of unit cells,
R
is the distance between the
B
atoms and
p
(resp.
q
) labels the band in the
\uparrow
(resp.
\downarrow
) sector, and
A_{kB,k^{\prime}B}^{pq}=\langle B_{k,\uparrow}|\psi^{p\uparrow}_{k}\rangle\langle\psi^{p\uparrow}_{k}|B_{k,\uparrow}\rangle\langle B_{k^{\prime},\downarrow}|\psi^{q\downarrow}_{k^{\prime}}\rangle\langle\psi^{q\downarrow}_{k^{\prime}}|B_{k^{\prime},\downarrow}\rangle

with
|B_{k,\sigma}\rangle=\hat{c}_{Bk\sigma}^{\dagger}|vac\rangle
. Finally
f(E)
is the Fermi-Dirac occupation.

We find that the gap
\delta
between the last filled band and the lowest empty one (in the opposite spin sector) is
\delta=JS
if
\alpha\geq\frac{JS}{\sqrt{2}t}
otherwise
\delta=\sqrt{\frac{J^{2}S2}{4}+4\alpha^{2}t^{2}}
for
\alpha\leq\frac{JS}{\sqrt{2}t}
.
Hence, when
\alpha\gg JS/t
the couplings are expected to be dominated by the FB-FB term
I_{BB}^{00}(R)
while they reduce to
I_{BB}^{-+}(R)
when
\alpha\ll JS/t
. In these two limiting regimes, couplings can be calculated analytically, which is the aim of the next two subsections.

II.1
(B,B) couplings for
\alpha\gg JS/t

As noted above, in the regime
\alpha\gg JS/t
, the dominant contribution is the FB-FB term
I_{BB}^{00}(R)
. The other contributions being much smaller, we can safely write,

\displaystyle J_{BB}(R)=-\frac{JS}{2}|F_{00}(R)|^{2},

(S8)

where we introduce,

\displaystyle F_{00}(R)=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k}\frac{4\cos^{2}{(ka/2)}}{4\cos^{2}{(ka/2)}+\alpha^{2}}e^{ikR}.

(S9)

Because
E^{0\uparrow}_{k}-E^{0\downarrow}_{k}=JS
and the eigenstates
|\psi_{k}^{0\sigma}\rangle
are
JS
-independent,
J_{BB}(R)
is linear in
JS
. This contrasts with the usual quadratic dependence of the couplings in the RKKY regime
[
1
]
.

F_{00}(R)
can be rewritten (
R/a\geq 1)
,

\displaystyle F_{00}(R)=-\frac{\alpha^{2}}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{+\pi}dke^{ikR}\frac{1}{\alpha^{2}+2+e^{ik}+e^{-ik}}.

(S10)

This integral can be calculated exactly for any value of
R
using a standard residue calculation which leads to,

\displaystyle F_{00}(R)=\frac{\alpha}{\sqrt{\alpha^{2}+4}}\exp{(R\ln{|z_{-}|})},

(S11)

where the relevant pole is
z_{+}=-1-\frac{\alpha^{2}}{2}+\frac{\alpha}{2}(\alpha^{2}+4)^{1/2}
.

First, when
\alpha
is small enough (
\alpha\leq 1
), Eq.(
S11
) can be simplified and gives,

\displaystyle J_{BB}(R)=-\frac{JS}{8}\alpha^{2}\exp{(-R/\xi)},

(S12)

where the decaying length is,

\displaystyle\xi/a=-\frac{1}{2}\ln(|z_{+}|)\approx\frac{1}{2\alpha}.

(S13)

We have verified that these analytical expressions (Eq. (
S12
) and Eq. (
S13
)) are accurate by a direct comparison with our complete numerical calculations.

In the opposite limit (
\alpha\gg 1
), using the expression of
z_{+}
, one now finds,

\displaystyle J_{BB}(R)=-\frac{JS}{2}\exp{(-R/\xi)},

(S14)

with,

\displaystyle\xi/a=\frac{1}{2\ln{(\alpha^{2})}}.

(S15)

Figure S2:

Analytical expression of
\xi
, obtained from the FB-FB contribution to
J_{BB}(R)
(Eq.(
S11
)), as a function of
\langle\bar{g}\rangle=\langle g\rangle/a^{2}
,
\langle g\rangle
being the average of the quantum metric. The red and blue lines represent the asymptotic behaviors in the limit of small and large
\langle g\rangle
. The orange line as displayed in Fig. 6 of the main text is plotted to facilitate the discussion.

We briefly discuss the connection between
\xi=-\frac{1}{2}\ln{(|z_{+}|)}
and the average value of the quantum metric
\langle g\rangle
(see main text).
We recall that
\sqrt{\langle g\rangle}
can be interpreted as a measure of the typical spread of flat-band eigenstates
[
2
,
3
]
.
Figure
S2
depicts
\xi
as a function of
\sqrt{\langle g\rangle}
, where one can show that
\langle g\rangle=\frac{a^{2}}{2\alpha\sqrt{\alpha^{2}+4}}

[
4
]
.
We clearly distinguish two different regimes:
(i)
\xi=2\langle g\rangle/a
for
\alpha\ll 1
, and (ii)

\xi=-\frac{1}{2\ln{(2\langle g\rangle/a^{2}})}
for
\alpha\gg 1
.
These (analytical) data coincide almost perfectly with those plotted in Fig. 6 of the main text, where
\xi
has been extracted from the complete numerical calculation of
J_{BB}
.
Finally, notice that the data plotted in Fig.6 could suggest that
\xi=\langle g\rangle^{1/2}
for
\alpha>1
(orange line in Fig.
S3
).
Numerically, we could not consider
\alpha
values greater than
2
because of the tiny amplitude of
\xi
in this regime (
\xi\sim 0.1a
). As clearly shown analytically, the correct scaling is
\xi=-\frac{1}{2\ln{(2\langle g\rangle/a^{2}})}
.

II.2
(B,B) couplings for
\alpha\ll JS/t

In this subsection, we now consider the opposite limit,
\alpha\ll JS/t
. In this case, the dominant contribution is expected to be
I_{BB}^{-+}(R)
which is given by,

\displaystyle J_{BB}(R)=I_{BB}^{-+}(R)=\frac{(JS)^{2}}{2}F_{-+}(R),

(S16)

with,

\displaystyle F_{-+}(R)=\frac{1}{N^{2}}\sum_{k,k^{\prime}}e^{i(k-k^{\prime})R}\frac{A^{-\uparrow,+\downarrow}_{kB,k^{\prime}B}}{E^{-\uparrow}_{k}-E^{+\downarrow}_{k^{\prime}}},

(S17)

where
A^{-\uparrow,+\downarrow}_{kB,k^{\prime}B}=|\langle B_{k\uparrow}|\psi^{-\uparrow}_{k}\rangle|^{2}|\langle B_{k^{\prime}\downarrow}|\psi^{+\downarrow}_{k^{\prime}}\rangle|^{2}
.

The dominant weight to the double sum originates from the vicinity of the zone boundary (
ka\approx k^{\prime}a\approx\pm\pi)
. Thus Eq. (
S17
) can be simplified and becomes,

\displaystyle F_{-+}(R)=-\frac{C}{N^{2}}\sum_{q,q^{\prime}}e^{i(q-q^{\prime})R}\frac{1}{1+\frac{1}{2\alpha^{2}}(q^{2}+q^{\prime}2)},

(S18)

where the prefactor
C=\frac{4\alpha^{2}t^{2}}{(JS)^{3}}
.

Eq. (
S18
) can be rewritten,

\displaystyle F_{-+}(R)=-\frac{C\alpha^{2}}{2\pi^{2}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{iu}e^{-iv}\frac{dudv}{2\alpha^{2}R^{2}+u^{2}+v^{2}}.

(S19)

First, we perform an integration with respect to the variable
v
(residue calculation), and get,

\displaystyle F_{-+}(R)=-\frac{C\alpha^{2}}{\pi}\Re\Bigg[\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{iu}du\frac{e^{-\sqrt{u^{2}+2\alpha^{2}R^{2}}}}{\sqrt{u^{2}+2\alpha^{2}R^{2}}}\Bigg].

(S20)

Using the fact that
[
5
]
,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}dxx^{2n}\frac{e^{-\sqrt{x^{2}+b^{2}}}}{\sqrt{x^{2}+b^{2}}}=(2n-1)!!(b)^{n}K_{n}(b),

(S21)

where
K_{n}
are the modified Bessel functions of second kind.

After replacing
in Eq. (
S20
)

e^{iu}
by
\sum_{n}\frac{1}{n!}(iu)^{n}
one finds,

\displaystyle F_{-+}(R)=-\frac{C\alpha^{2}}{\pi}\sum_{n}\frac{(-1)^{n}}{n!}\Big(\frac{\sqrt{2}\alpha R}{2}\Big)^{n}K_{n}(\sqrt{2}\alpha R).

(S22)

Using the fact that for any
n
,
K_{n}(b)=\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2b}}e^{-b}
(
b>0
) in the limit of large argument, we finally end up with,

\displaystyle J_{BB}(R)=\frac{2\alpha^{7/2}}{\sqrt{2\sqrt{2}\pi}}\frac{t^{2}}{JS}\frac{e^{-\frac{R}{\xi}}}{\sqrt{R}},

(S23)

where in this regime (
\alpha t\ll JS
) we find,

\displaystyle\xi=\frac{\sqrt{2}}{3\alpha}.

(S24)

We have checked that this expression coincides very well with the data plotted in Fig.3 of the main text for
JS=1
and
\alpha=0.1
and
R
being large enough.

III
(B,B)
coupling in the diamond chain

In this section, our purpose is to show that
J_{BB}(R)=-C_{1}/R^{4}
when
R
is large enough.

In the basis
(c_{Ak\sigma}^{\dagger},c_{Bk\sigma}^{\dagger},c_{Ck\sigma}^{\dagger})
, the
3\times 3
Hamiltonian of the standard diamond chain (Dd[1]) reads,

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{\sigma}=\begin{bmatrix}0&\epsilon_{0}(k)&\epsilon_{0}(k)\\
\epsilon_{0}(k)&z_{\sigma}\frac{JS}{2}&0\\
\epsilon_{0}(k)&0&z_{\sigma}\frac{JS}{2}\\
\end{bmatrix},

(S25)

where we define
\epsilon_{0}(k)=-2t\cos{(ka/2)}
and
z_{\sigma}=\pm 1
for
\sigma=\,\uparrow
or
\downarrow
.

The eigenvalues are straightforwardly calculated,

\displaystyle E^{0\sigma}_{k}=z_{\sigma}\frac{JS}{2},

(S26)

\displaystyle E^{\pm\sigma}_{k}=\frac{1}{2}\Big(z_{\sigma}\frac{JS}{2}\pm\sqrt{\Delta^{k}_{\sigma}}\Big),

(S27)

with
\Delta^{k}_{\sigma}=(\frac{J^{2}S^{2}}{4}+8\epsilon^{2}_{0}(k))
.

These bands are depicted in Fig.(
S1
) [b] for
JS=1
.
The corresponding eigenstates are given by,

\displaystyle|\psi^{0\sigma}_{k}\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{bmatrix}0\\
1\\
-1\\
\end{bmatrix},

(S28)

and,

\displaystyle|\psi^{\pm\sigma}_{k}\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N^{\pm}_{\sigma}}}\begin{bmatrix}\frac{1}{2}(z_{\sigma}\frac{JS}{2}\mp\sqrt{\Delta^{k}_{\sigma}})\\
-\epsilon_{0}(k)\\
-\epsilon_{0}(k)\\
\end{bmatrix},

(S29)

where
N^{\pm}_{\sigma}=4\epsilon^{2}_{0}(k)+\frac{J^{2}S^{2}}{8}\mp\frac{1}{4}z_{\sigma}JS\sqrt{\Delta^{k}_{\sigma}}
.

These expressions imply that the dispersive bands
(\uparrow,-)
(filled band) and
(\downarrow,+)
(empty band) touch
at
ka=\pm\pi
. Thus, we anticipate that
I^{-+}_{BB}
(see Eq.
S6
) (i) is the main contribution to the couplings (at large distances) and (ii) it decays as a power law. The other contributions are negligible since they involve pairs of bands
(p,q)
separated by finite gaps, and thus are exponentially suppressed.

Figure S3:
J_{BB}(R)
(numerical values) in the half-filled diamond chain as a function of
R
for different values of
JS
. The dashed lines correspond to the analytical expression as given in Eq.(
S35
).

Using the same notation as that of the previous section, we write

\displaystyle J_{BB}(R)=I_{BB}^{-+}(R)=\frac{(JS)^{2}}{2}F_{-+}(R),

(S30)

where,

\displaystyle F_{-+}(R)=\frac{1}{N^{2}}\sum_{k,k^{\prime}}e^{i(k-k^{\prime})R}\frac{\epsilon^{2}_{0}(k)}{N^{-}_{\uparrow}(k)}\frac{\epsilon^{2}_{0}(k^{\prime})}{N^{+}_{\downarrow}(k^{\prime})}\frac{1}{E^{-}_{\uparrow}(k)-E^{+}_{\downarrow}(k^{\prime})}.

(S31)

The main contribution to the double sum originates from the vicinity of the band touching (quadratic). Hence, we substitute
k=q\pm\pi
and
k^{\prime}=q^{\prime}\pm\pi
which leads to,

\displaystyle F_{-+}(R)=-\frac{t^{2}}{\pi^{2}(JS)^{3}}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}dqdq^{\prime}e^{i(q-q^{\prime})R}\frac{q^{2}q^{\prime 2}}{q^{2}+q^{\prime 2}}.

(S32)

First, we perform the integral with respect to
q^{\prime}
which gives,

\displaystyle F_{-+}(R)=\frac{t^{2}}{\pi(JS)^{3}}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}dqe^{iqR}q^{2}|q|e^{-|q|R}.

(S33)

Focusing on
R/a\gg 1
we get,

\displaystyle F_{-+}(R)=\frac{2t^{2}}{\pi(JS)^{3}}\frac{1}{R^{4}}\Re\Bigg[\int_{0}^{\infty}due^{iu}u^{3}e^{-u}\Bigg].

(S34)

This clearly shows the power law decay, the (B,B) coupling scales as
1/R^{4}
.

Using the fact
\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{n}e^{-\alpha x}dx=n!\alpha^{-n-1}
when
\Re(\alpha)>0
, we then end up with the final expression,

\displaystyle J_{BB}(R)=-\frac{3}{2\pi}\frac{t^{2}}{JS}\frac{1}{R^{4}}.

(S35)

This analytical expression is valid only when
R\gg\sqrt{32}t/JS
which is the condition to write Eq. (
S32
).
In addition, the
1/JS
scaling does not depend on the value of
JS
. Consequently, in the weak coupling regime (
JS\ll t
) the RKKY expression, which predicts a
(JS)^{2}
dependence, fails.
The comparison between the complete numerical calculation of
J_{BB}(R)
and Eq.(
S35
) is displayed in Fig.
S2
. As illustrated, they coincide when
R
is sufficiently large.

It is also interesting to note that because the flat-band eigenstates are
k
-independent (Eq. (
S28
)),

I^{pq}_{BB}(R)
is zero for
R/a\geq 1
when at least one of the bands (
p
or
q
) is a flat-band. With the exception of the contribution to
J_{BC}(R=0)
, flat bands do not contribute and can be ignored.

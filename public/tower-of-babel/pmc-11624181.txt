BRG1 improves reprogramming efficiency by enhancing glycolytic metabolism
Authors: Xuan Ren, Shihai Huang, Jianchun Xu, Qingsong Xue, Tairan Xu, Deshun Shi, Shinan Ma, Xiangping Li
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractBRG1 has been found to promote the generation of induced pluripotent stem cells (iPSCs) by regulating epigenetic modifications or binding to transcription factors, however, the role of BRG1 on the cellular metabolism during reprogramming has not been reported. In this study, we found that BRG1 improved the efficiency of porcine iPSC generation, and upregulated the expression of pluripotency-related factors. Further analysis revealed that BRG1 promoted cellular glycolysis, and increased levels of glycolysis-related metabolites. It enhanced the transcriptional activity of glycolysis-related gene HK2, PKM2, and PFK-1 promoters, and decreased the enrichment of H3K9me3 in glycolysis- and pluripotency-related gene promoters. BRG1 also increased the phosphorylation level at the Ser473 site of AKT protein. The specific PI3K/AKT signaling pathway inhibitor, LY294002, impaired the generation of porcine iPSCs, downregulated the expression of pluripotency-related factors, and inhibited cellular glycolysis, overexpressing BRG1 rescued those changes caused by LY294002 treatment. In addition, the glycolysis inhibitor 2-DG and BRG1 inhibitor PFI-3 had similar effects to LY294002. The above results suggest that overexpression of BRG1 promotes the generation of porcine iPSCs by facilitating glycolytic reprogramming through the PI3K/AKT signaling pathway.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s00018-024-05527-2.Keywords: BRG1, Porcine iPSCs, Glycolysis, Transcriptional activity, H3K9me3, PI3K/AKT signaling pathway

Introduction

Induced pluripotent stem cell (iPSC) technology avoids ethical and immune rejection issues and has been widely used in regenerative medicine [1, 2], animal breeding [3, 4], and endangered species conservation [5, 6]. Since 2006, although the production system of iPSCs has been continuously improved [7, 8], the reprogramming efficiency of somatic cells in large livestock such as cattle, sheep, and pigs is still low [9, 10], and the related mechanism remains to be further studied.

Metabolic reprogramming plays a crucial role in the generation and differentiation of iPSCs [11, 12]. It includes oxidative phosphorylation, glycolysis, lipid, and amino acid metabolic changes [13]. Glycolytic reprogramming is an important metabolic reprogramming during the generation of iPSCs, which refers to a dramatic metabolic shift from oxidative phosphorylation to glycolysis [13]. Several studies have found that enhanced glycolysis facilitates the reprogramming of somatic cells [14, 15]. Wdr82 promotes the generation of mouse iPSCs by inhibiting mitochondrial oxidative phosphorylation [16]. Glycolytic metabolites such as lactate and acetyl-CoA can regulate epigenetic modifications, thus affecting reprogramming [17, 18]. Dux enhances the lactate dehydrogenase activity and increases the extracellular acidification rate (ECAR) on day 4 of induction, thereby promoting the accumulation of H3K18la, and improving the efficiency of mouse iPSC generation. During this process, Brg1, the catalytic subunit of the SWI/SNF family of chromatin remodeling complex, plays an important role as the reader of H3K18la [19].

Differentiated somatic cells are reprogrammed towards a more undifferentiated state by Xenopus laevis egg and F9 pluripotent cell extract, and BRG1 is identified as a factor required for this process [20, 21], which is the first report that BRG1 plays a role in reprogramming somatic cells into iPSCs. Brg1-associated factors (BAF) complex improves reprogramming by promoting DNA demethylation and increasing H3K4me3 levels to open chromatin structure [21]. Brg1 also enhances the efficiency of mouse iPSC generation by interacting with the pluripotency factors Oct4, Sox2, and Nanog [22, 23]. A recent study has found that Brg1 acts as a reader of H3K18la, and can cooperate with the pluripotent factor Dux to promote the generation of mouse iPSCs [19].

BRG1 participates in the regulation of glycolytic metabolism in cancer cells. BRG1 helps to maintain the stemness of glioma-initiating cells by downregulating the negative regulator of glycolysis, TXNIP, through the STAT3 signaling pathway [24]. Knockdown of BRG1 and BRM in cancer cells inhibits glucose transporter GLUT1 expression, causing reduced glucose uptake and glycolysis accompanied with increased dependency on oxidative phosphorylation [25]. Still now, the influence of BRG1 on porcine iPSC generation has not been reported, and its role on glycolytic metabolism during reprogramming remains unclear.

In this study, we first compared the differences in induction efficiency, BRG1, pluripotency- and glycolysis-related gene expression of porcine iPSCs under different O2 conditions. Secondly, the effect of BRG1 on the generation of porcine iPSCs was analyzed, and the mechanism was explored in terms of PI3K/AKT pathway and glycolytic metabolism. This study provides help for further exploring the metabolic regulation mechanism of BRG1 during the reprogramming process.

Materials and methods

Animals

Pig fetus obtained from the Luban Road slaughterhouse in Nanning, Guangxi Province. All experiments and protocols were performed in strict accordance with the Guide for Care and Use of Laboratory Animals and explicitly approved by the Guangxi University Committee on Animal Research and Bioethics (Gxu-2022-277).

Cell culture

Porcine fetal fibroblasts (PFFs) were derived from the skin of pig fetus, PFFs and 293T cells were cultured in high-glucose DMEM (Gibco, Carlsbad, CA, USA) supplemented with 10% FBS (Gibco, Carlsbad, CA, USA) at 37 ℃ under a humidified atmosphere of 5% CO2 in the air. Different concentrations (0, 5, 10, 15, 20 mM) of 2-DG (MCE, New Jersey, USA) were used to treat PFFs for 24 h to explore the optimal concentration.

Porcine iPSC generation

Lentiviral vectors of FUW-tetO-hOCT4 (Addgene, #20726), FUW-tetO-hSOX2 (Addgene, #20724), FUW-tetO-hKLF4 (Addgene, #20725), FUW-tetO-hMYC (Addgene, #20723), FUW-M2rtTA (Addgene, #20342) (known as OSKM), and pTK642-BRG1 (preserved in our laboratory) were co-transfected with packaging-related plasmids NRF and VSVG (provided by Prof Tal Kafri, UNC at Chapel Hill) into 293T cells using ExFect transfection reagent (Vazyme, Jiangsu, China), respectively. At 60–72 h after transfection, lentiviral supernatants were collected and filtered through 0.45 μm cellulose acetate filters (Millipore, Ireland). iPSC generation was performed as previously described [26]. Lentivirus (containing OSKM and BRG1) supplemented with 8 µg/mL polybrene (Sigma-Aldrich, MO, USA) was used to infect PFFs at the third to fifth generation for 24 h, and PFFs were passaged onto matrigel, and grown in iPSC induction medium (also named as ES medium, high-glucose DMEM supplemented with penicillin/streptomycin, 15% FBS, 2 mM l-glutamine, 1% nonessential amino acids, 0.1 mM β-mercaptoethanol, 2 mM valproic acid, 2 µg/mL DOX, 10 ng/mL basic fibroblast growth factor (bFGF), and 10 ng/mL human LIF), with the medium changed daily until colonies formed. The pluripotency and differentiation ability of colonies were identified [26]. The normoxia and hypoxia experiments were performed at 20% and 5% O2 concentrations, respectively. In addition, a specific concentration of 2-DG was added on day 0, 3, and 6 of induction to explore its optimal addition time, respectively. The BRG1 inhibitor PFI-3 (1 μM, Aladdin, Shanghai, China) and PI3K/AKT signaling pathway LY294002 (10 μM, Beyotime, Beijing, China) were used to treat PFFs for 24 h before lentiviral infection. Cells on day 9 of induction were collected for detection if not specifically labeled, D3, D6, and D9 represent samples collected on days 3, 6, and 9 of induction, respectively.

Alkaline phosphatase (AP) and Immunofluorescence staining

The reprogramming efficiency was detected using the AP Assay Kit (Sigma-Aldrich, MO, USA) according to the manufacturer's instructions. For immunofluorescence analysis, porcine iPSCs were fixed and permeabilized in 1% Triton-X 100 for 30 min, blocked with BSA for 30 min, then incubated with primary antibodies H3K9me3 (A22295, Abclonal, Wuhan, China) and 5-mC (#28692, CST, Danvers, MA, USA) at 4 ℃ overnight. On the next day, cells were incubated with fluorescently labeled secondary antibodies for 1 h in the dark. DAPI was used for nuclear staining. The stained cells were visualized under the fluorescence microscope (Nikon, Japan), and the fluorescence intensity was analyzed using Image J software.

Reverse Transcription PCR (RT-PCR) and Quantitative Real-Time PCR (qRT-PCR)

Total RNA was extracted by Trizol method (Invitrogen, Carlsbad, CA, USA), RT-PCR and qRT-PCR were performed using the HiScript III RT SuperMix for qPCR (+ gDNA wiper) (Vazyme, Jiangsu, China) and the ChamQ Universal SYBR QPCR Master Mix (Vazyme, Jiangsu, China) as previously described [26]. 7500 Fast Real-Time PCR System was used for qRT-PCR analysis, and the results were analyzed using the 2−ΔΔCT method with β-actin as the reference gene. Gene-specific primers were designed using the oligo 7.0 software (supplementary Table 1).

Western blot

Cell samples were collected using trypsin digestion, and washed 2–3 times with PBS. The cell pellet was lysed using a mixture of RIPA lysis buffer as well as protease and phosphatase inhibitor on ice for 30 min. The supernatant was added with 4 × protein loading buffer, and heated in boiling water for 10 min to denature the protein. Protein samples were separated using 10% sodium dodecyl sulfate-polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis and transferred to polyvinylidene fluoride membrane. The membranes were blocked with 5% skim milk diluted in TBST (TBS containing 0.5% Tween-20) for 2 h at room temperature, then incubated with primary antibodies diluted in TBST at 4 ℃ overnight, and secondary antibodies for 1 h at room temperature. The blot bands were visualized using an enhanced chemiluminescence kit under the ChemiDoc System (Bio-Rad, California, USA). Protein expression was quantified using Image J software, and normalized to the endogenous reference protein β-actin. The primary antibodies used were as follows: BRG1 (#49360, CST, Danvers, MA, USA), HIF1A (WL01607, Wanleibio, Shenyang, China), PKM2 (GB11392, Servicebio, Wuhan, China), β-actin (AC038, Abclonal, Wuhan, China), LDHA (GB11342, Servicebio, Wuhan, China), OCT4 (#2750, CST, Danvers, MA, USA), NANOG (bs-0829R, Bioss, Beijing, China), p-AKT (Ser473, 66444-1-lg, Proteintech, Wuhan, China), AKT (TP70420, Abmart, Shanghai, China), H3K9me3 (A22295, Abclonal, Wuhan, China).

EdU detection

Cell proliferation was detected using the beyoClick™ EdU cell proliferation kit (Beyotime, Beijing, China) as previously described [27]. Briefly, 2 × EdU solution was prepared and added to 96-well plates containing an equal volume of medium. The medium was removed after 2 h, porcine iPSCs were fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde for 15 min, permeabilized with 0.3% Triton-X 100 for 15 min, and washed in 3% BSA three times. Then cells were incubated in click reaction solution for 30 min in the dark, and cell nuclei were stained with Hoechst33342 for 10 min. The stained cells were visualized under the fluorescence microscope (Nikon, Japan), and the cell proliferation rate was evaluated by calculating the proportion of red nuclei (EdU-positive cells) to blue nuclei (Hoechst33342-positive cells).

Glycolysis stress tests

Experiments were performed using the Seahorse XFe24 Extracellular Flux Analyzer (Seahorse Bioscience, Massachusetts, USA) according to the instructions of the glycolysis stress test kit (Agilent, California, USA). Cells were passaged into 24-well microplates and cultured at 37 ℃ with 5% CO2. The next day, the cell medium was changed to Seahorse XF DMEM medium (PH7.4) supplemented with 1 mM glutamine, and cells were incubated in a CO2-free incubator at 37 ℃ for 1 h, then ECAR was measured by sequentially adding 10 mM glucose, 1 μM oligomycin, and 50 mM 2-DG to the medium. Glucose can provide the raw material for glycolysis. Oligomycin can inhibit mitochondrial ATP synthase, leading to an increased dependence on glycolysis. 2-DG is a competitive inhibitor of glucose and is used to switch off glycolysis.

Intracellular lactate and glucose detection

Intracellular lactate and glucose levels were detected using lactic acid and glucose assay kits according to the manufacturer’s instructions (Nanjing Jiancheng BioTech. Co. Ltd, Jiangsu, China). Total cell lysates were obtained using an ultrasonic cell disruptor (Scientz, Zhejiang, China), and incubated with the cocktail at 37 ℃ for 10 min. The absorbance was measured at 530 nm and 505 nm using a microplate reader (Tecan, Männedorf, Switzerland), respectively.

Chromatin immunoprecipitation (ChIP)-qPCR

ChIP-qPCR was performed using the EpiQuik™ chromatin immunoprecipitation kit (Epigentek, New York, USA) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. In brief, porcine iPSCs were cross-linked in 1% formaldehyde for 10 min at room temperature, then quenched with 0.125 M glycine for 5 min. DNA was sheared in lysis buffer using an ultrasonic cell disruptor. Antibody-protein-DNA complexes were isolated by immunoprecipitation using BRG1 (#49360, CST, Danvers, MA, USA) and H3K9me3 (A22295, Abclonal, Wuhan, China) antibodies on an orbital shaker (50–100 rpm) at room temperature for 60–90 min, then washed 6–7 times in wash buffer. DNA was purified using purification columns, and subjected to qPCR analysis. Non-immune IgG was set as a negative control, and specific primer sequences were listed in supplementary Table 2.

Dual luciferase reporter assay

This experiment was performed using dual luciferase reporter assay kit (Vazyme, Jiangsu, China) as previously reported [28]. Briefly, the promoter region of the target gene was amplified and subcloned into the firefly luciferase vector PGL3-Basic. Subsequently, three plasmids (firefly reporter plasmids PGL3-promoter, renilla reporter plasmid pRL-TK, and pTK642-BRG1) were co-transfected into 293T or porcine iPSCs for 48 h. Cells were collected and examined for luciferase activity using a microplate reader (Tecan, Männedorf, Switzerland), and renilla luciferase activity was used as a control. The sequences of the PCR primers were provided in supplementary Table 3.

Liquid chromatography-mass spectrometry

Untargeted metabolomics analysis was performed by Majorbio Biotech (Shanghai, China) according to the previous study[29]. In brief, approximately 107 cells at day 6 and day 9 of induction were quickly frozen in liquid nitrogen, and stored at −80 ℃. Cells were resuspended in 300 μL extraction solution (methanol: water = 4:1), and ground by frozen tissue grinder for 6 min (−10 ℃). Then the mixture was extracted through cryosonication for 30 min (5 ℃), followed by standing at −20 ℃ for 30 min and then centrifugation for 15 min (4 ℃, 13,000 g). The supernatants were used for LC–MS analysis to identify metabolites, and metabolites were detected by ultrahigh-performance liquid chromatography coupled with Q Exactive HF-X mass spectrometry (UHPLC-Q-Exactive HF-X system, Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, MA, USA). Raw data were analyzed using the Progenesis QI software (Waters Corporation, USA), the response intensity of the sample mass spectrum peaks was normalized, variables with relative standard deviation (RSD) > 30% of QC samples were removed, and the raw data were log10 logarithmized to obtain the final data matrix for subsequent analysis. Metabolites were annotated using the KEGG database (https://www.kegg.jp/), HMDB database (http://www.hmdb.ca/), and Majorbio Database. The difference between the two groups was analyzed by independence t-test. Orthogonal partial least squares discriminant analysis was used to screen for differential metabolites among different groups and to generate variable importance in projection (VIP) values of metabolites. P < 0.05, VIP value > 1 were used to select differential metabolites.

Data analysis

The P-value was analyzed using SPSS software (version 26), and the results were presented as the mean ± SEM. A P-value less than 0.05 was considered a significant difference, and less than 0.01 was considered extremely significant. All experiments were performed with at least three replicates. Comparisons of different groups were performed by independent sample T-tests or one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) using SPSS software.

Results

BRG1 promotes the reprogramming of porcine somatic cells into iPSCs

It is well known that hypoxia improves the reprogramming efficiency of somatic cells, which is largely attributed to the different metabolic levels under normoxia and hypoxia. To further explore the potential regulatory factors, PFFs were reprogrammed under normoxia (20%) and hypoxia (5%), respectively. We found that hypoxia shortened the formation time of porcine iPSCs (P < 0.05, Fig. S1A, B), and significantly increased the number of AP-stained positive colonies (P < 0.01, Fig. S1C). In addition, the expression of pluripotency-related genes OCT4, SOX2, and NANOG, and glycolysis-related genes HK2, PKM2, PFK-1, and HIF1A was significantly elevated in the hypoxia group (P < 0.05, Fig. S1D). Interestingly, the expression of BRG1 was also significantly upregulated (P < 0.01, Fig. S1E). Thus, we speculate that overexpressing BRG1 may promote reprogramming and upregulate glycolysis under normoxia.

To test our hypothesis, we explored the effect of BRG1 on the formation of porcine iPSCs. As expected, we found that the proliferation rate of the cells was significantly enhanced in the BRG1-OE group (BRG1 over-expressed group) (P < 0.05, Fig. 1A), the number of AP-stained positive colonies was significantly increased (P < 0.01, Figs. 1B, S2). In addition, the expression of BRG1, OCT4, SOX2, and NANOG was gradually upregulated with the increase of induction days, and significantly elevated in the BRG1-OE group at different induction days (P < 0.05, Fig. 1C). The protein expression of BRG1 and OCT4 was significantly upregulated on day 9 (P < 0.05, Fig. 1D), with no significant change for NANOG protein. These results suggest that overexpression of BRG1 improves the induction efficiency of porcine iPSCs, and increases the expression of pluripotency-related genes and proteins during reprogramming.

BRG1 enhances glycolysis during iPSC generation

To investigate the mechanism of BRG1 improving the induction efficiency of porcine iPSCs, glycolytic metabolism-related indicators were examined. We found that the expression of glycolysis-related genes was dramatically elevated on day 6, and further increased on day 9 (Fig. 2A), so these two time points were chosen for subsequent experiments. The expression of PKM2, LDHA, and HIF1A was significantly upregulated in the BRG1-OE group (P < 0.05, Fig. 2A), HK2 and PFK-1 were significantly increased on day 9 and 6, respectively (P < 0.05, Fig. 2A). Subsequently, cells on day 6 and 9 of induction were collected for LC–MS analysis. The results showed that a total of 1008 metabolites were detected, with 68 metabolites significantly upregulated and 198 downregulated in the BRG1-OE group on day 6 (P < 0.05, Fig. 2B), and 55 metabolites significantly upregulated and 133 downregulated on day 9 (P < 0.05, Fig. 2B). Glycolysis-related metabolites including 3-phosphoglyceric acid, 3-phosphoglycerate, phosphoenolpyruvic acid, 2-phospho-D-glyceric acid were significantly increased in the BRG1-OE group on day 6 and 9 (P < 0.05, Fig. 2C), while other carbon metabolism-related (starch and sucrose, pentose phosphate pathway, aldolate and carboxylic acid metabolism) metabolites were significantly decreased (P < 0.05, Fig. 2C). In addition, KEGG enrichment analysis showed that glycolysis and gluconeogenesis signaling pathways were significantly upregulated in the BRG1-OE group on day 9 (P < 0.05, Fig. 2D). In addition, the intracellular glucose and lactate levels were significantly increased on day 6 and 9 (P < 0.05, Fig. 3A). Subsequently, we detected extracellular glycolysis levels using seahorse analysis, the results showed that glycolysis level was increased, and the glycolytic capacity as well as glycolytic reserve were significantly elevated in the BRG1-OE group on day 6 and 9 (P < 0.05, Fig. 3B). The protein expression of PKM2 and HIF1A was significantly upregulated in the BRG1-OE group on day 9 (P < 0.05, Fig. 3C). These data indicate that overexpressing BRG1 upregulates cellular glycolysis during the reprogramming process.

To investigate whether inhibition of cellular glycolysis influences reprogramming, the glycolysis inhibitor 2-DG was used during the generation of porcine iPSCs. We found that adding 5 mM 2-DG on day 6 of induction could effectively inhibit BRG1 and glycolysis-related gene expression during reprogramming (Fig. S3A, B). The results showed that the number of AP-stained positive colonies was significantly reduced in the 2-DG group (P < 0.01, Figs. 4A, S3C), the expression of OCT4 and BRG1 was significantly decreased (P < 0.05, Fig. 4B, C), SOX2 was also significantly downregulated (P < 0.01, Fig. 4B), and there was no significant change in NANOG (Fig. 4B, C). Further testing revealed that the expression of HIF1A, HK2, PFK-1, PKM2, and LDHA was significantly decreased (P < 0.01, Fig. 4D), and PKM2, LDHA, and HIF1A proteins were also significantly reduced (P < 0.05, Fig. 4E). These results suggest that glycolytic metabolism is essential for cellular reprogramming, and inhibition of glycolysis impairs the generation of porcine iPSCs.

BRG1 reduces H3K9me3 enrichment in the promoter of glycolysis-related genes and increases their promoter transcriptional activity

As we know, S-adenosyl methionine (SAM) is a methyl donor for methylation reactions, which can influence gene transcription by regulating methylation levels. LC–MS analysis revealed that the levels of SAM-related metabolites were significantly reduced in the BRG1-OE group on day 6 and 9 (P < 0.05, Fig. 5A), so we focused on detecting methylation levels of porcine iPSCs. We found that the levels of H3K9me3 and 5-mC on porcine iPSCs were significantly decreased in the BRG1-OE group (P < 0.05, Fig. 5B, C). The enrichment of H3K9me3 in the promoter of glycolysis-related genes PKM2 and HK2 was significantly reduced (P < 0.05, Fig. 5D), and the enrichment of H3K9me3 in the promoter of pluripotency-related genes OCT4 and NANOG was also significantly decreased in the BRG1-OE group (P < 0.01, Fig. 5E). We then examined the effect of BRG1 on the transcriptional activity of glycolysis-related gene promoters. The results showed that the enrichment of BRG1 in the promoter of PFK-1, PKM2, and HK2 was significantly increased in the BRG1-OE group (P < 0.05, Fig. 5F), the transcriptional activity of glycolysis-related gene promoters was significantly enhanced in 293T and porcine iPSCs (P < 0.05, Fig. 5G, H). These data suggest that BRG1 regulates the epigenetic modification status and transcriptional activity of the promoter of glycolysis-related genes.

BRG1 improves reprogramming efficiency through the PI3K/AKT signaling pathway to enhance glycolytic metabolism

PI3K/AKT signaling pathway is one of the key pathways for glycolytic metabolism. In our previous study, we found that BRG1 upregulated the expression of PI3K/AKT signaling pathway-related genes in PFFs [30], so we speculated that BRG1 might promote glycolytic metabolism by regulating the PI3K/AKT signaling pathway, then improving the induction efficiency of porcine iPSCs. The effect of BRG1 on the PI3K/AKT signaling pathway was first explored, and the results showed that overexpression of BRG1 increased the phosphorylation level at the Ser473 site of the AKT protein (P < 0.05, Fig. 6A).

We then utilized the small molecule compound LY294002 (10 μM) to inhibit the PI3K/AKT signaling pathway to detect its effect on porcine iPSC generation. The results showed that the cell proliferation was significantly slowed in the LY294002 group (P < 0.05, Fig. 6B). The formation of porcine iPSCs was impaired, and the number of AP-stained positive colonies was significantly reduced in the LY294002 group (P < 0.05, Figs. 6C, S4). The mRNA expression of OCT4, SOX2, NANOG, and BRG1 was significantly decreased (P < 0.05, Fig. 6D). The protein expression of p-AKT (Ser473), OCT4, and BRG1 was also significantly downregulated (P < 0.05, Fig. 6E).

Finally, the effect of LY294002 on cellular glycolysis during reprogramming was explored. We found that the expression of HIF1A, HK2, PKM2, and LDHA was significantly downregulated in the LY294002 group (P < 0.05, Fig. 7A). Further analysis revealed that the glycolysis level was downregulated, the glycolytic capacity and glycolytic reserve were significantly reduced on day 9 (P < 0.05, Fig. 7B), and the levels of intracellular glucose and lactate were significantly lowered on day 6 and 9 (P < 0.05, Fig. 7C). Additionally, the protein expression of HIF1A and LDHA was significantly downregulated in the LY294002 group (P < 0.05, Fig. 7D). While overexpression of BRG1 significantly reversed the loss caused by LY294002, compared with the LY294002 group, the reprogramming efficiency was significantly improved in BRG1-OE group (P < 0.05, Fig. 6C), the expression of BRG1, p-AKT (Ser473), and pluripotency-related genes or proteins was upregulated (P < 0.05, Fig. 6D, E), and cellular glycolysis were significantly enhanced (P < 0.05, Fig. 7A–D). These data indicate that the overexpressing BRG1 activates the PI3K/AKT signaling pathway to enhance glycolysis, thereby improving the induction efficiency of porcine iPSCs.

Downregulation of BRG1 inhibits reprogramming

To explore whether inhibition of BRG1 expression has the opposite effect on the generation of porcine iPSCs, PFFs were treated with 1 μM PFI-3 (BRG1 inhibitor) 24 h before lentiviral infection. We found that the reprogramming efficiency was significantly decreased in the PFI-3 group (P < 0.01, Figs. 8A, S5). The expression of BRG1 and pluripotency-related genes or proteins was downregulated on day 9 (P < 0.05, Fig. 8B, C), and p-AKT (Ser473) was significantly decreased (P < 0.05, Fig. 8D). In addition, the expression of glycolysis-related genes and proteins was also significantly downregulated (P < 0.05, Fig. 8E, F). These results suggest that downregulation of BRG1 expression is detrimental to the formation of porcine iPSCs, it inhibits the PI3K/AKT signaling pathway and impairs glycolysis.

Discussion

In this study, we found that hypoxia facilitated porcine iPSC generation and upregulated the expression of BRG1 and glycolysis-related genes. Overexpression of BRG1 enhanced glycolysis by activating the PI3K/AKT signaling pathway, thereby promoting the reprogramming of PFFs into iPSCs under normoxia. In addition, BRG1 played its role by increasing the transcriptional activity of glycolysis-related genes, and reducing H3K9me3 enrichment in the promoter of glycolysis- and pluripotency-related genes. To our knowledge, this is the first report on BRG1 regulating energy metabolism during somatic cell reprogramming.

Several studies have found that hypoxia promotes the generation of iPSCs, and improves reprogramming [31–33]. However, these studies only explore the differences in reprogramming efficiency between normoxia and hypoxia, the mechanisms have not been explored deeply. In this study, we found that the reprogramming efficiency was increased, and the expression of glycolysis-related genes was upregulated in hypoxia, which was consistent with previous reports. Interestingly, the expression of BRG1 was also significantly increased in hypoxia. This suggests that overexpression of BRG1 may promote reprogramming, and it may be a regulator of glycolytic metabolism during reprogramming.

BRG1 could improve the efficiency of human and mouse iPSC generation [19, 21–23]. In this study, we found that overexpressing BRG1 promoted the reprogramming of PFFs into iPSCs, while inhibition of BRG1 exhibited the opposite effect, which was consistent with previous reports. Several studies have found that BRG1 affects glycolytic metabolism in cancer cells by regulating the glycolysis-related pathway or gene expressions [24, 25, 34]. However, the impact of BRG1 on glycolysis during reprogramming, and its effect on the transcriptional activity of glycolysis-related genes remain unclear. In this study, we found that overexpression of BRG1 promoted cellular glycolytic metabolism, significantly reduced the H3K9me3 enrichment in the promoter region of glycolysis and pluripotency-related genes, and significantly enhanced the transcriptional activity of the promoter of glycolysis-related genes PFK-1, PKM2, and HK2. This indicates that BRG1 can regulate the transcriptional activity and epigenetic modification status of the glycolytic gene promoters, and increase glycolysis levels during reprogramming.

PI3K/AKT signaling pathway plays an important role in iPSC differentiation [35, 36], while its effect on the acquisition of iPSC pluripotency is less reported. It has been found that the phosphorylation level of AKT is increased during mouse iPSC generation, and activation of AKT1 improves the reprogramming of mouse somatic cells into iPSCs [37]. However, the effect of the PI3K/AKT signaling pathway on porcine iPSC formation and the regulatory role of BRG1 in this process have not been reported. In this study, we found that inhibition of the PI3K/AKT signaling pathway hindered reprogramming and downregulated pluripotency-related gene expressions, and overexpression of BRG1 rescued this loss. This indicates that BRG1 promotes the generation of porcine iPSCs by activating the PI3K/AKT signaling pathway. The PI3K/AKT signaling pathway is involved in the regulation of glycolysis during iPSC generation. Chen has found that the thyroid hormone triiodothyronine (T3) enhances the reprogramming efficiency of human somatic cells by activating the PI3K/AKT signaling pathway to upregulate the expression of glycolysis-related genes [38]. A deeper study has revealed that AKT-activated cells exhibit enhanced anabolic glucose metabolism and elevated α-ketoglutarate (α-KG) levels, and the reprogramming efficiency is significantly increased [39]. We found that LY294002 downregulated glycolysis levels, glycolytic reserve, and glycolytic capacity of cells, and decreased the expression of glycolysis-related genes or proteins, which could be reversed by BRG1. The above results suggest that BRG1 enhances glycolysis levels by activating the PI3K/AKT signaling pathway, thereby promoting porcine iPSC generation.

Conclusion

In summary, overexpression of BRG1 regulates the transcriptional activity and epigenetic modification status of the glycolytic gene promoters, it also improves the induction efficiency of porcine iPSCs by activating the PI3K/AKT signaling pathway to enhance glycolytic metabolism.

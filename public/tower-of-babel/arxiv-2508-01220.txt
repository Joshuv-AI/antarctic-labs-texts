Lepton Flavor Violating Higgs Decays in a Minimal Doublet Left-Right Symmetric Model with an Inverse Seesaw
M. Zeleny-Mora, R. Gaitán-Lozano, R. Martinez

Abstract
In this work , we study the Lepton Flavor Violation (LFV) of Higgs decays in the context of the Doublet Left-Right Symmetric model (DLRSM), based on the gauge group S ​ U ​ ( 2 ) L ⊗ S ​ U ​ ( 2 ) R ⊗ U ​ ( 1 ) B − L SU\left(2\right)_{L}\otimes SU\left(2\right)_{R}\otimes U\left(1\right)_{B-L} . The enlarged gauge and Higgs sectors induce new charged currents interactions. Spontaneous symmetry breaking occurs in two stages, with a new scale arising from the vacuum expectation value v R v_{R} of the right-handed doublet. We consider scenarios where v R v_{R} is above the electroweak scale. Neutrino masses are generated via the inverse seesaw mechanism, leadingto LFV through neutrino mixing. We diagonalize the full neutrino mass matrix and express the mixing in terms of physical parameters. We also explore the scalar sector to reproduce the observed Higgs mass and compute LFV Higgs decays rates as functions of the heavy neutrino scale.

Introduction

In the electroweak scale, the Standard Model (SM) of particle physics
has been remarkably successful in describing fundamental interactions.
However, several questions remain unanswered, including the origin
of neutrino masses, the nature of parity violation, and the possibility
of lepton flavor violation (LFV). The Left-Right Symmetric Model (LRSM)
offers a compelling framework to address some of these issues by expanding
the SM gauge group to

SU\left(3\right)_{C}\otimes SU\left(2\right)_{L}\otimes SU\left(2\right)_{R}\otimes U\left(1\right)_{B-L},

and restoring left-right symmetry at higher energies
[
20
,
17
,
19
,
22
]
.
In contrast to the canonical version based on triplet scalar fields,
the Doublet Left-Right Symmetric Model (DLRSM) introduces a scalar
sector consisting of a bidoublet
\Phi
and two doublets
\chi_{L}
and

\chi_{R}
, which simplifies the scalar potential
[
23
]
.

The DLRSM provides a more economical alternative to the triplet LRSM,
avoiding the presence of doubly charged scalars and their stringent
constraints coming from colliders, flavor-changing neutral currents
(FCNC), electric dipole moments (EDMs) and precision electroweak measurements

[
3
,
2
]
. However, in the DLRSM the Majorana
masses for neutrinos is typically not automatic and requires additional
mechanisms such us the Inverse seesaw (ISS) which predicts right-handed
neutrinos at the TeV scale
[
18
,
8
]
.

Experimental evidence of neutrino oscillations explains neutrino masses,
but in contrast to other fermions the neutrino mass scale is tiny.
The most known explanation to this scale is given the seesaw (SS)
mechanism type I. In this mechanism, right-handed neutrinos
\nu_{R}

are added to the matter content of the SM and the Majorana mass term
proportional to
M_{R}
is available. As a consequence, in the case
of three right-handed neutrinos, we obtain a
6\times 6
neutrino
mass matrix
\mathcal{M}_{\nu}
. The seesaw approximation assumed
a large scale for
\nu_{R}
, then in the limit
|m_{D}|\ll|M_{R}|
,
the light neutrino mass matrix
\mathcal{M}_{\text{light}}
is given
by

\mathcal{M}_{\text{light}}\approx-m_{D}^{\top}M_{R}^{-1}m_{D}.

where,
m_{D}
represents the Dirac mass matrix. Nevertheless, the
typical scale of right handed neutrinos to describe the tiny light
neutrinos masses is around
M_{R}\approx 10^{-14}
GeV, this scale
lies beyond the reach of current experimental sensitivity. Another
possibility comes from low scale seesaw mechanism such us the ISS
in this case, we add three pair of fermionic singlets
\left(N_{R},S\right)

into the matter content of the model, as consequence, in addition
to
M_{R}
such as in the SS, the new matrix
\mu
associated to
Majorana mass of singlets
S
is added. In this framework, the light
neutrino matrix is approximated by

\mathcal{M}_{\text{light}}\approx m_{D}^{\top}\left(M_{R}^{\top}\right)^{-1}\mu M_{R}^{-1}m_{D},

where the assumed hierarchy is
|\mu|\ll|m_{D}|\ll|M_{R}|
. Now,
the scale of right handed neutrinos is not completely responsible
of the light neutrino masses, also the small scale of
\mu
contributes.
Therefore, the mass scale of right-handed neutrinos is around the
TeV domain, within potential reach of current collider facilities.

One notable consequence of the neutrino mass mechanism is the lepton
flavor violation (LFV). In the charged lepton sector of SM extended
with neutrino masses LFV process such as
\mu\to e\gamma
,
\mu\to 3e

and
\mu
-
e
conversion in nuclei are highly suppressed by the
smallness of neutrino masses and the GIM mechanism, making them effectively
unobservable in current experiments
[
7
]
. For flavor
neutral changing currents (FCNC) the experimental limits are lower
in comparison to the before mentioned in the charged lepton sector.
In particular, LFV Higgs decays (LFVHD) provides an important probe
of the flavor structure of the scalar sector. These channels are especially
compelling due to their direct link to fermion mass generation and
their potential probe at present or upcoming collider experiments.
In extended models, the branching rations of LFVHD could reach sizable
values such us in the 2HDM type III
[
28
]
. Recently,
the ATLAS collaboration at CERN
[
1
]
have improved
the upper bounds of Lepton Flavor Violation Higgs decays (LFVHD) associated
to
\tau
lepton given by
\mathcal{BR}(h\to e\tau)<0.20\%
and

\mathcal{BR}(h\to\mu\tau)<0.18\%

[
12
]
. These results
establish bounds over the Yukawa couplings
Y_{\ell\tau}
and
Y_{\tau\ell}

which are stronger than the direct searches from, by instance,
\tau\to e\gamma,\mu\gamma
.

The Doublet Left-Right Symmetric Model

This model is based on the gauge group

\ell_{i}^{\prime}\end{pmatrix}_{R}:\left(1,2,-1\right)

i=1,2,3
runs over fermion generations, also, we add three ferminoc

L_{L}\longleftrightarrow L_{R},\qquad S_{i}\longleftrightarrow S_{i}^{c}.

(1)

In this model, the electric charges of particles are related with

and

The workflow of SPheno operates through input and output files (I/O)

A practical approach to scan the parameters of the model with the

the SM Higgs mass

algorithms in
[

This python library automatize the process of (I/O) of SPheno and

[

.
This library was used in the context of (B-L) Super Symmetric model
for constraints the parameters space using bounds of the scalar mass
spectrum
[

allows to define objective functions such as SPheno output observables.

7
Conclusions

In this work, we have studied LFVHD within a minimal DLRSM extended
with an ISS for neutrino mass generation. This setup allows the introduction
of right-handed neutrinos at the TeV scale and naturally small neutrino
masses without requiring ultra-heavy Majorana scales.

We analyze the gauge (
g_{L}=g_{R}
) and scalar sectors of the DLRSM,
identifying the physical Higgs spectrum. The scalar potential was
explored under the assumption
k_{2}=v_{L}=0
. A parameter scan using
SARAH, SPheno and a MCMC approach implemented in hep-aid library allowed
us to identify regions of the scalar potential compatible with a
125

GeV SM-like Higgs. This minimal setup of the DLRSM could be easily
extended to consider the case of
k_{2}\neq 0
and
v_{L}\neq 0
,

g_{L}\neq g_{R}
or consider an extended scalar potential.

The neutrino mass matrix was constructed under the ISS framework and
its diagonalization led to mixing between light and heavy neutrino
states. We also studied the impact of pertubativity for
Y_{R}
which
constraint heavy neutrino masses. We computed the one-loop contributions
to the decay
h\to\ell_{a}\ell_{b}
using exact expression for the
form factors, numerically evaluated with QCDLoop library.

Our numerical results show that branching ratio for
h\to\mu\tau

can reach values as large as
\mathcal{BR}\left(h\to\mu\tau\right)\approx 10^{-11}

for
v_{R}=10
TeV and
\approx 10^{-12}
for
v_{R}\approx 5
TeV,
which are still below current experimental bounds.

In summary, the DLRSM with ISS provides a theoretically consistent
and phenomenologically viable framework for generating neutrino masses
and LFV effect at accessible energy scales. The predict LFVHD offer
potentially observable signature in future precision experiments,
motivating further exploration of this scenarioboth theoretically
and experimentally.

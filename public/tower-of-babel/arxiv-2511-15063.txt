Dynamics of Dehn Twists in the Outer Automorphism Group of a Free Group
Donggyun Seo

Abstract
. We study Dehn twists in the outer automorphism group of a finitely generated non-abelian free group. Our main result states that, under certain compatibility conditions, sufficiently large powers of finitely many Dehn twists generate a right-angled Artin group. The proof proceeds by analyzing the geometry of spheres, tori, and simple closed curves in a doubled handlebody. Along the way, we establish the bigon–bihedron criterion and an equivalent condition for commuting Dehn twists. Furthermore, we construct a compact topological space on which every Dehn twist acts parabolically.

1.
Introduction

Let
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{F}_{g}
be a free group of rank
g
.
The automorphism group
\Aut(\mathbb{F})
contains the inner automorphism group

\Inn(\mathbb{F})
as a normal subgroup, and the quotient

\Out(\mathbb{F}):=\Aut(\mathbb{F})/\Inn(\mathbb{F})

is called the
outer automorphism group
of
\mathbb{F}
.
Given a one-edge
\mathbb{Z}
-splitting
T
of
\mathbb{F}
, one can associate a
Dehn twist
(or
generalized transvection
)
\delta_{\vec{T}}\in\Out(\mathbb{F})
, defined as the outer automorphism induced by twisting along
T
.

Our main theorem concerns the existence of right-angled Artin subgroups generated by powers of such Dehn twists.
For a finite simplicial graph
\Gamma=(V,E)
, the
right-angled Artin group (RAAG)
on
\Gamma
is

A(\Gamma):=\langle\,v\in V\mid[u,v]=1\text{ whenever }\{u,v\}\in E\,\rangle.

Given a finite collection
\{T_{1},\dots,T_{k}\}
of one-edge
\mathbb{Z}
-splittings of
\mathbb{F}
, one defines the
coincidence graph

\Gamma
with vertices
T_{1},\dots,T_{k}
, where
T_{i}
and
T_{j}
are adjacent precisely when they admit a common refinement.
For integers
n_{1},\dots,n_{k}
, this determines a homomorphism

A(\Gamma)\longrightarrow\Out(\mathbb{F}),\qquad T_{i}\longmapsto\delta_{\vec{T}_{i}}^{\,n_{i}}.

Our main result asserts that, under suitable hypotheses, this map is injective and its image is a right-angled Artin subgroup of
\Out(\mathbb{F})
.

Theorem 1.1

(Theorem
5.5
)
.

Let
V=\{T_{1},\dots,T_{k}\}
be a compatible collection of one-edge
\mathbb{Z}
-splittings with cores
\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{k}
, and let
\Gamma
be the coincidence graph of
V
.
For each
i
, let
\delta_{i}=\delta_{\vec{T}_{i}}
be a Dehn twists along
T_{i}
.
Then for all

\lvert n_{1}\rvert,\dots,\lvert n_{k}\rvert\geq 5\,\max_{i\neq j}{i(\gamma_{i},T_{j})}+8,

the homomorphism

\Phi:A(\Gamma)\longrightarrow\Out(\mathbb{F}),\qquad T_{i}\mapsto\delta_{i}^{n_{i}}

is injective.

The problem of constructing free subgroups of
\Out(\mathbb{F})
has been extensively studied.
Bestvina, Feighn, and Handel
[
1
]
proved that
\Out(\mathbb{F})
satisfies the Tits alternative: every subgroup is either virtually solvable or contains a free subgroup of rank two.
Kapovich and Lustig
[
13
]
showed that sufficiently large powers of two hyperbolic iwip elements generate either a virtually cyclic subgroup or a nonabelian free group.
Uyanik
[
19
]
obtained a related result in the setting of hyperbolic extensions.
Clay and Pettet
[
4
]
proved that if two one-edge
\mathbb{Z}
-splittings are filling, then sufficiently large powers of the associated Dehn twists generate a free group of rank two consisting of atoroidal fully irreducible elements except for the conjugacy classes of the twists themselves.
Gültepe
[
9
]
provided another criterion under which powers of two Dehn twists generate a free subgroup of rank two.

A complementary development is due to Taylor
[
18
]
, who proved that every right-angled Artin group embeds quasi-isometrically into some
\Out(\mathbb{F})
.
He constructed a suitable collection of fully irreducible outer automorphisms supported on free factors, whose sufficiently large powers generate the desired RAAG.
Since these automorphisms are fully irreducible, Dehn twists do not appear in his construction.
Our work may therefore be viewed as a counterpart to Taylor’s theorem: we construct right-angled Artin subgroups of
\Out(\mathbb{F})
generated instead by powers of Dehn twists.

Theorem
1.1
gives a broad and explicit method for constructing RAAG subgroups inside
\Out(\mathbb{F})
.
However, the compatibility condition does not encompass all right-angled Artin phenomena arising from Dehn twists.
For instance,
\Out(\mathbb{F}_{g})
contains a direct product of
2g-4
free groups generated by Dehn twists (Horbez and Wade
[
11
]
, and Bridson and Wade
[
3
]
) and a free-abelian subgroup of rank
2g-3
generated by Dehn twists (Feighn and Handel
[
7
]
), yet the associated one-edge
\mathbb{Z}
–splittings in these examples are not compatible.
Thus, Theorem
1.1
captures a large and well-structured class of RAAGs generated by powers of Dehn twists, but it does not apply to every such instance found in
\Out(\mathbb{F}_{g})
.

We approach the proof of Theorem
1.1
by working in the mapping class group of the doubled handlebody
\mathbb{M}=\mathbb{M}_{g}=\#_{g}(\mathbb{S}^{1}\times\mathbb{S}^{2})
.
Laudenbach
[
15
]
established the following short exact sequence:

1\longrightarrow(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^{g}\longrightarrow\MCG(\mathbb{M})\longrightarrow\Out(\mathbb{F})\longrightarrow 1.

Brendle, Broaddus, and Putman
[
2
]
later proved that this sequence splits; in particular, there exists a section
\Out(\mathbb{F})\to\MCG(\mathbb{M})
.
In this setting, a Dehn twist may be interpreted as an isotopy class of a homeomorphism twisting
\mathbb{M}
along an essential torus.
Gültepe
[
9
]
likewise studied Dehn twists realized as twists along essential tori.

We begin by analyzing pairs of commuting Dehn twists.
Given homotopy classes of essential tori
T
and
T^{\prime}
, their
geometric intersection number

i(T,T^{\prime})
is defined as the minimum number of intersection circles among all representatives
t\in T
and
t^{\prime}\in T^{\prime}
.
If
i(T,T^{\prime})=0
, that is, if
T
and
T^{\prime}
admit disjoint representatives, then the corresponding Dehn twists commute.
Our next result establishes the converse: two Dehn twists commute if and only if their supports may be chosen disjointly.
In particular, no accidental commutation occurs.

Theorem 1.2

(Theorem
3.7
)
.

Let
T
and
T^{\prime}
be homotopy classes of essential tori.
Then the following statement holds:

[\delta_{\vec{T}},\delta_{\vec{T}^{\prime}}]=1
if and only if
i(T,T^{\prime})=0
.

From an algebraic perspective, two Dehn twists commute precisely when their associated one-edge
\mathbb{Z}
-splittings admit a common refinement.
On the geometric side, recall that two simple closed curves on a surface intersect minimally if and only if they form no bigon—the classical
bigon criterion
, which underlies much of surface topology.
In an analogous spirit, our work is grounded in the following criterion governing intersections of essential tori in doubled handlebodies.

Proposition 1.3

(Bigon–bihedron criterion, Proposition
3.4
)
.

Two essential tori intersect minimally if and only if they form neither a bigon nor a bihedron.

One can formulate analogous criteria in the cases of two essential spheres or of an essential sphere together with an essential torus.
In addition, one obtains a
bigon criterion
in this setting: an essential simple closed curve and an essential sphere (or an essential torus) intersect minimally if and only if they do not form a bigon.
We note that Hatcher
[
10
]
introduced a normal form for sphere systems, and this normal form guarantees that any two sphere systems placed in it are in minimal position.

Meanwhile, Theorem
1.1
is proved by a ping–pong argument on the set of homotopy classes of essential simple closed curves.
We employ a variation of the ping–pong method due to Koberda
[
14
]
.
Careful control of intersection numbers within this framework yields the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

In addition, we construct a compact topological space
\overline{(p\circ i_{*})(\mathcal{C})}
on which
\Out(\mathbb{F})
acts continuously.
This space contains a dense subset consisting of points represented by homotopy classes of essential simple closed curves.
The definition of
\overline{(p\circ i_{*})(\mathcal{C})}
is closely analogous to that of the projective space
\mathcal{PML}(S)
of measured laminations on a surface
S
.
In
\mathcal{PML}(S)
, the action of a Dehn twist is parabolic, and a Dehn twist in
\Out(\mathbb{F})
exhibits the same dynamical behavior on
\overline{(p\circ i_{*})(\mathcal{C})}
, as described below.

Theorem 1.4

(Theorem
6.5
)
.

Let
T
be a homotopy class of an essential torus with the core
\gamma
.
Let
\alpha
be a homotopy class of an essential simple closed curve.
Then, we have
i(\alpha,T)>0
if and only if
\delta_{\vec{T}}^{n}(\alpha)
converges to
\gamma
as
n\to\infty
.

2.
Dehn twist

In this section, we review the basic background on outer automorphism groups of free groups and doubled handlebodies required for our discussion.
Let
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{F}_{g}
be a free group of rank
g\geq 2
.
We write
\Aut(\mathbb{F})
and
\Inn(\mathbb{F})
for the automorphism group and the inner automorphism group of
\mathbb{F}
, respectively.
Note
\Inn(\mathbb{F})
is a normal subgroup of
\Aut(\mathbb{F})
.
The
outer automorphism group
of
\mathbb{F}
, denoted by
\Out(\mathbb{F})
, is the quotient of
\Aut(\mathbb{F})
by
\Inn(\mathbb{F})
.

2.1.
Dehn twist automorphisms of free groups

An
\mathbb{F}
-tree
T
is called a
one-edge
\mathbb{Z}
-splitting
if the action of
\mathbb{F}
on the set of edges is transitive and a stabilizer of an edge is an infinite cyclic subgroup of
\mathbb{F}
.
Let
\vec{E}T
be the set of orientations of edges of
T
.
Then there exists a orientation reversing
*:\vec{E}T\to\vec{E}T
, that is,
*(\vec{e})
is the reverse orientation of
\vec{e}
for each
\vec{e}\in\vec{E}T
.
Let
o:\vec{E}T\to\mathbb{F}
be a map defined by

(1)

\langle o(\vec{e})\rangle=\operatorname{Stab}(e)
,

(2)

o(*(\vec{e}))=o(\vec{e})^{-1}
and

(3)

o(w\vec{e})=wo(\vec{e})w^{-1}

for all
\vec{e}\in\vec{E}T
and
w\in\mathbb{F}
.
We call
o
an
orientation
of
T
and write
\vec{T}:=(T,o)
for an oriented one-edge
\mathbb{Z}
-splitting.

Fix a base-vertex
v_{0}\in T
.
For each vertex
v\in T
, there exists a unique geodesic path from
v_{0}
to
v
, denoted by
\vec{e}_{1},\dots,\vec{e}_{k}
.
Then we define

\delta_{\vec{T},v_{0}}(v):=o(\vec{e}_{1})\dots o(\vec{e}_{k})v,

which is called a
Dehn twist automorphism based at
v_{0}
.
We then show the following.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\delta_{\vec{T},v_{0}}:VT\to VT
be defined as above.
Then
\delta_{\vec{T},v_{0}}
can be extended to a simplicial automorphism of
T
.

Proof.

Suppose
v
and
v^{\prime}
are adjacent.
Without loss of generality, let us assume that
v^{\prime}
is closer to
v_{0}
than
v
.
Let
\vec{e}_{1},\dots,\vec{e}_{k}
be the geodesic path from
v_{0}
to
v
.
Since
o(\vec{e}_{k})
stabilizes the edge joining
v^{\prime}
and
v
, we have

\displaystyle d_{T}(\delta_{\vec{T},v_{0}}(v),\delta_{\vec{T},v_{0}}(v^{\prime}))

\displaystyle=d_{T}(o(\vec{e}_{1})\dots o(\vec{e}_{k-1})o(\vec{e}_{k})v,o(\vec{e}_{1})\dots o(\vec{e}_{k-1})v^{\prime})

\displaystyle=d_{T}(o(\vec{e}_{k})v,v^{\prime})=1.

That is,
\delta_{\vec{T},v_{0}}(v)
and
\delta_{\vec{T},v_{0}}(v^{\prime})
are adjacent.
So
\delta_{\vec{T},v_{0}}
can be extended to a simplicial automorphism of
T
.
∎

Lemma 2.2
.

For each vertex
v\in T
, if
\vec{e}_{1},\dots,\vec{e}_{k}
is the geodesic path from
v_{0}
to
v
, let us define

\delta_{\vec{T},v_{0}}^{-1}(v):=o(\vec{e}_{1})^{-1}\dots o(\vec{e}_{k})^{-1}(v).

Then the map
\delta_{\vec{T},v_{0}}^{-1}:VT\to VT
is the inverse of
\delta_{\vec{T},v_{0}}
.

Proof.

Since the sequence

\vec{e}_{1},o(\vec{e}_{1})^{-1}\vec{e}_{2},\dots,o(\vec{e}_{1})^{-1}\dots o(\vec{e}_{k-1})^{-1}\vec{e}_{k}

is the geodesic path from
v_{0}
to
o(\vec{e}_{1})^{-1}\dots o(\vec{e}_{k})^{-1}v
, we obtain

\displaystyle\delta_{\vec{T},v_{0}}\delta_{\vec{T},v_{0}}^{-1}(v)

\displaystyle=\delta_{\vec{T},v_{0}}(o(\vec{e}_{1})^{-1}\dots o(\vec{e}_{k})^{-1}v)

\displaystyle=o(\vec{e}_{1})o(o(\vec{e}_{1})^{-1}\vec{e}_{2})\dots o(o(\vec{e}_{1})^{-1}\dots o(\vec{e}_{k-1})^{-1}\vec{e}_{k})(o(\vec{e}_{1})^{-1}\dots o(\vec{e}_{k})^{-1}v)

\displaystyle=v.

Note
\vec{e}_{1},o(\vec{e}_{1})\vec{e}_{2},\dots,o(\vec{e}_{1})\dots o(\vec{e}_{k-1})\vec{e}_{k}
is the geodesic path from
v_{0}
to
\delta_{\vec{T},v_{0}}(v)
.
So we have

\displaystyle\delta_{\vec{T},v_{0}}^{-1}\delta_{\vec{T},v_{0}}(v)

\displaystyle=\delta_{\vec{T},v_{0}}^{-1}(o(\vec{e}_{1})\dots o(\vec{e}_{k})v)

\displaystyle=o(\vec{e}_{1})^{-1}o(o(\vec{e}_{1})\vec{e}_{2})^{-1}\dots o(o(\vec{e}_{1})\dots o(\vec{e}_{k-1})\vec{e}_{k})^{-1}(o(\vec{e}_{1})\dots o(\vec{e}_{k})v)

\displaystyle=v

Therefore, the statement holds.
∎

Lemma 2.3
.

For each
w\in\mathbb{F}
, it holds that
w\delta_{\vec{T},v_{0}}w^{-1}=\delta_{\vec{T},wv_{0}}
.

Proof.

For a vertex
v\in VT
, let
\vec{e}_{1},\dots,\vec{e}_{k}
be the geodesic from
wv_{0}
to
v
.
Since
w^{-1}\vec{e}_{1},\dots,w^{-1}\vec{e}_{k}
is a geodesic from
v_{0}
to
w^{-1}v
, we have

w\delta_{\vec{T},v_{0}}w^{-1}v=wo(w^{-1}\vec{e}_{1})\dots o(w^{-1}\vec{e}_{k})w^{-1}v=o(\vec{e}_{1})\dots o(\vec{e}_{k})v=\delta_{\vec{T},wv_{0}}(v)

So the statement holds.
∎

Lemma 2.4
.

For a vertex
v\in T
, if
\vec{e}_{1},\dots,\vec{e}_{k}
is any path from
v_{0}
to
v
, then we have
\delta_{\vec{T},v_{0}}(v)=o(\vec{e}_{1})\dots o(\vec{e}_{k})v
.

Proof.

If
\vec{e}_{1},\dots,\vec{e}_{k}
is not geodesic, then there exists
i
such that
\vec{e}_{i+1}=*(\vec{e}_{i})
.
So we have
o(\vec{e}_{1})\dots o(\vec{e}_{k})=o(\vec{e}_{1})\dots o(\vec{e}_{i-1})o(\vec{e}_{i+2})\dots o(\vec{e}_{k})
, which corresponds to the path
\vec{e}_{1},\dots,\vec{e}_{i-1},\vec{e}_{i+2},\dots,\vec{e}_{k}
.
So we can inductively reduce the length of the path until it is a geodesic.
Therefore, we get
o(\vec{e}_{1})\dots o(\vec{e}_{k})v=\delta_{\vec{T},v_{0}}(v)
.
∎

Proposition 2.5
.

For each
w\in\mathbb{F}
, we have
\delta_{\vec{T},v_{0}}w\delta_{\vec{T},v_{0}}^{-1}\in\mathbb{F}
.

Proof.

Let
\vec{e}_{1},\dots,\vec{e}_{k}
be the geodesic path from
v_{0}
to
wv_{0}
.
For a vertex
v\in T
, if
\vec{e}_{1}^{\prime},\dots,\vec{e}_{m}^{\prime}
is a geodesic path from
v_{0}
to
v
, then we have

\displaystyle\delta_{\vec{T},v_{0}}w\delta_{\vec{T},v_{0}}^{-1}(v)

\displaystyle=\delta_{\vec{T},v_{0}}(w\delta_{\vec{T},v_{0}}^{-1}w^{-1})(wv)

\displaystyle=\delta_{\vec{T},v_{0}}\delta_{\vec{T},wv_{0}}^{-1}(wv)

\displaystyle=\delta_{\vec{T},v_{0}}(o(w\vec{e}_{1}^{\prime})^{-1}\dots o(w\vec{e}_{m}^{\prime})^{-1}(wv))

\displaystyle=o(\vec{e}_{1})\dots o(\vec{e}_{k})\cdot

\displaystyle\quad\,\,o(w\vec{e}_{1}^{\prime})o(o(w\vec{e}_{1}^{\prime})^{-1}w\vec{e}_{2}^{\prime})\dots o(o(w\vec{e}_{1}^{\prime})^{-1}\dots o(w\vec{e}_{m-1}^{\prime})^{-1}w\vec{e}_{m})\cdot

\displaystyle\quad\,\,o(w\vec{e}_{1}^{\prime})^{-1}\dots o(w\vec{e}_{m}^{\prime})^{-1}(wv)

\displaystyle=o(\vec{e}_{1})\dots o(\vec{e}_{k})wv.

Therefore, we conclude that
\delta_{\vec{T},v_{0}}w\delta_{\vec{T},v_{0}}^{-1}=o(\vec{e}_{1})\dots o(\vec{e}_{k})w\in\mathbb{F}
.
∎

So one considers the conjugation by
\delta_{\vec{T},v_{0}}
on
\mathbb{F}
as an automorphism of
\mathbb{F}
.
Then the outer automorphism represented by the conjugation by
\delta_{\vec{T},v_{0}}
is called a
Dehn twist
, denoted by
\delta_{\vec{T}}
.

2.2.
Dehn twists of doubled handlebodies

Let
\mathbb{M}=\mathbb{M}_{g}
denote the connected sum of
g
copies of
\mathbb{S}^{1}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}
, namely

\mathbb{M}=\mathbb{M}_{g}=\#_{g}\,\mathbb{S}^{1}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}=\underbrace{(\mathbb{S}^{1}\times\mathbb{S}^{2})\#\dots\#(\mathbb{S}^{1}\times\mathbb{S}^{2})}_{g~\text{times}}.

The fundamental group
\pi_{1}(\mathbb{M},x)
, for a chosen basepoint
x\in\mathbb{M}
, is a free group of rank
g
.
We henceforth identify
\mathbb{F}
with
\pi_{1}(\mathbb{M},x)
.

Let
\MCG(\mathbb{M})
denote the mapping class group of
\mathbb{M}
, that is, the group of isotopy classes of orientation-preserving homeomorphisms.
Laudenbach
[
15
]
shows the following short exact sequence holds:

1\longrightarrow(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^{g}\longrightarrow\MCG(\mathbb{M})\longrightarrow\Out(\mathbb{F})\longrightarrow 1

Brendle, Broaddus and Putman
[
2
]
discover that this sequence is splitting, that is, there exists a section
\Out(\mathbb{F})\hookrightarrow\MCG(\mathbb{M})
.
It is therefore natural to refer to the image of a Dehn twist in
\MCG(\mathbb{M})
simply as a Dehn twist.
What kind of a homeomorphism is associated with a Dehn twist?
To answer to this question, we need to dive into the geometry of
\mathbb{M}
.

For a torus
t
embedded in
\mathbb{M}
, we say
t
is
essential
if

(1)

it does not bound a solid torus and

(2)

there exists a simple closed curve in
t
which is essential in
\mathbb{M}
.

By the definition, an induced map from a fundamental group of an essential torus
t
to
\mathbb{F}
has an infinite cyclic image.
Thus, the simple closed curve in (
2
) is uniquely determined up to homotopy.
We refer to the homotopy class of this curve as the
core
of
t
.

Let
\widetilde{\mathbb{M}}
be a universal cover of
\mathbb{M}
with a covering map
\pi:\widetilde{\mathbb{M}}\to\mathbb{M}
.
For an essential torus
t\subset\mathbb{M}
, the
dual tree
of
t
is a tree
T
embedded in
\widetilde{\mathbb{M}}
satisfying

(1)

each component of
\widetilde{\mathbb{M}}\setminus\pi^{-1}(t)
contains exactly one vertex of
T
and

(2)

each component of
\pi^{-1}(t)
intersects exactly one edge of
T
.

For each
v\in VT
and
w\in\mathbb{F}
, if
C
is the component of
\widetilde{\mathbb{M}}
that contains
v
, we define
wv
as the vertex contained in
wC
.
This gives a simplicial action of
\mathbb{F}
on
T
, which makes
T
a one-edge
\mathbb{Z}
-splitting.

For an orientation on
T
, the Dehn twist
\delta_{\vec{T}}
along
\vec{T}
is associated with some mapping class of
\MCG(\mathbb{M})
through the section
\Out(\mathbb{F})\hookrightarrow\MCG(\mathbb{M})
.
This is represented by the following homeomorphism.

Let
t
be an essential torus, and let
N(t)
denote a closed regular neighborhood of
t
.
Choose an orientation on the core of
t
, and denote by
\vec{t}
the torus
t
equipped with this orientation.

Note
t
is homeomorphic to
\mathbb{S}^{1}\times\mathbb{S}^{1}
.
Assume
\mathbb{S}^{1}
is oriented.
There exists a homeomorphism

h:N(t)\to[0,1]\times\mathbb{S}^{1}\times\mathbb{S}^{1}

such that the restriction

h^{-1}\big|_{\{1/2\}\times\{0\}\times\mathbb{S}^{1}}

is orientation-preserving between
\{1/2\}\times\{0\}\times\mathbb{S}^{1}
and the core of
t
.
Define a map

\delta:[0,1]\times\mathbb{S}^{1}\times\mathbb{S}^{1}\to[0,1]\times\mathbb{S}^{1}\times\mathbb{S}^{1}

by

\delta(t,s_{0},s_{1})=(t,s_{0},s_{1}+t)

for all
(t,s_{0},s_{1})\in[0,1]\times\mathbb{S}^{1}\times\mathbb{S}^{1}
.
Then the
Dehn twist homeomorphism along
\vec{t}
, denoted
\delta_{\vec{t}}
, is defined by

\delta_{\vec{t}}(x)\;=\;\begin{cases}h^{-1}\,\delta\,h(x),&x\in N(t),\\
x,&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Exercise 2.6
.

Let
t
be an essential torus of
\mathbb{M}
.
Let
T
be the dual tree of
t
.
For each orientation of
t
, there exists an orientation of
T
such that
\delta_{\vec{t}}
represents
\delta_{\vec{T}}
.

Note if two essential tori are homotopic, then they give the same Dehn twist as outer automorphisms.
By Brendle, Broaddus and Putman
[
2
]
, this outer automorphism can be considered as a mapping class.
Note the mapping class group consists of isotopy classes of orientation-preserving homeomorphisms.
So the following holds.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
\vec{t}
and
\vec{t}^{\prime}
be oriented essential tori.
Then,
\delta_{\vec{t}}
is isotopic to
\delta_{\vec{t}^{\prime}}
if and only if
\vec{t}
is homotopic to
\vec{t}^{\prime}
.

We will abuse notation by writing
T
for both a homotopy class of an essential torus
t

and the associated dual tree of
t
.
Similarly, we use
\vec{T}
to denote either of their oriented versions.
With this convention, the Dehn twist along
\vec{T}
will be denoted by
\delta_{\vec{T}}
.

3.
Intersection number

Let
A
and
B
be homotopy classes of embedded submanifolds (e.g. simple closed curves, spheres and tori) of
\mathbb{M}
.
The
intersection number
of
A
and
B
, denoted by
i(A,B)
, is defined by

i(A,B):=\min\{\lvert\pi_{0}(a\cap b)\rvert\mid a\in A,b\in B\}

where
\pi_{0}(a\cap b)
denotes the collection of components of
a\cap b
, that is, the zeroth homotopy group of
a\cap b
.

Example 3.1
.

If
A
and
B
are homotopy classes of essential simple closed curves, then
i(A,B)
is always zero.
If
A
is a homotopy class of an essential simple closed curve and
B
is a homotopy class of an essential torus or an essential sphere, then
i(A,B)
can be written by
\min\{\lvert a\cap b\rvert\mid a\in A,b\in B\}
.
If
A
and
B
are homotopy classes of essential spheres and essential tori, then for each
a\in A
and
b\in B
, the intersection of
a
and
b
is a disjoin union of circles, so
i(A,B)
indicates the minimum number of circles among such intersections.

3.1.
Bigon, bihedron and minimal position

Let
c
be an essential simple closed curve, and let
s
denote an essential sphere or an essential torus.
We say
c
and
s

form a bigon
if there are arcs
a\subset c
and
a^{\prime}\subset s
such that
a\cup a^{\prime}
is a closed curve that bounds a disk.
If
c
and
s
form a bigon, one can reduce the number of bigons by homotoping
c
.
This induces the cardinality of
c\cap s
is strictly larger than the intersection number of
[c]
and
[s]
.
What we want to mention is that the converse is also true.

Proposition 3.2

(Bigon criterion)
.

Let
c
be an essential simple closed curve, and let
s
be an essential sphere or an essential torus.
Then,
\lvert c\cap s\rvert=i([c],[s])
if and only if
c
and
s
do not form a bigon.

The following proof is adapted from Farb and Margalit
[
5
]
.

Proof.

Suppose
\lvert c\cap s\rvert>i([c],[s])
.
Let
c^{\prime}
be a simple closed curve that is homotopic to
c
such that
\lvert c^{\prime}\cap s\rvert=i([c],[s])
.
Then there exists a homotopy
h
that moves
c
to
c^{\prime}
.
Note the domain of
h
is an annulus
A
.
Let
\partial_{0}
be the boundary component of
A
whose image is
c
.

Note
h^{-1}(s)
is a union of arcs whose endpoints are contained in the boundary of
A
.
Because
c\cap s
is strictly larger than
c^{\prime}\cap s
, there exists an arc
a
of
h^{-1}(s)
that connects two points of
\partial_{0}
.
Then one can find a subarc
a_{0}\subset\partial_{0}
such that
a\cup a_{0}
bounds a disk in
A
.
That is, the union of
h(a)
and
h(a_{0})
bounds a disk.
Hence,
c
and
s
form a bigon.
∎

We may focus on an intersection number of two essential tori.
There are two ways to reduce the number of circles of two essential tori via a homotopy.

Example 3.3
.

Let
t
and
t^{\prime}
be essential tori.

(1)

Suppose there are disks
D\subset t
and
D^{\prime}\subset t^{\prime}
such that
D\cup D^{\prime}
is a sphere that bounds a
3
-ball.
(We say
t
and
t^{\prime}
form a
bihedron
.)
Then one can homotope
t
by locating
D
across
D^{\prime}
, which removes a circle
D\cap D^{\prime}
.
Then the number of circles of
t\cap t^{\prime}
decreases.

(2)

Suppose there are pairs of pants
P\subset t
and
P^{\prime}\subset t^{\prime}
such that
P\cup P^{\prime}
is a twice-punctured torus whose hole bounds a disk.
(We say
t
and
t^{\prime}
form a
bigon
.)
Then one can homotope
t
by enlarging
P
so that
P\cap P^{\prime}
becomes a circle.
Then the number of circles of
t\cap t^{\prime}
decreases.

The above example applies both to pairs of essential spheres and to pairs consisting of an essential sphere and an essential torus.
This naturally raises the question of whether forming a bigon or a bihedron is the only obstruction to realizing the intersection number.
The following result gives a positive answer, which we refer to as the
bigon–bihedron criterion
.

Proposition 3.4

(Bigon–bihedron criterion)
.

Let
s
and
t
be essential spheres, essential tori, or one of each.
Then, the number of circles of
s\cap t
is exactly
i([s],[t])
if and only if
s
and
t
form neither a bigon nor a bihedron.

Proof.

If
s
and
t
form either a bigon or a bihedron, then the number of intersection circles can be reduced, as in the preceding example.

Conversely, suppose
s
and
t
are not in minimal position.
Then there exists a homotopy

H:\hat{s}\times[0,1]\to\mathbb{M}

that deforms
s
into
s^{\prime}
such that
s^{\prime}
and
t
are in minimal position.
We may assume
H
is smooth and each intermediate surface
H_{t}(\hat{s})
intersects
t
in a disjoint union of circles expect for finitely many times.

The preimage
H^{-1}(t)
is a (possibly disconnected) orientable surface with boundary.
If
H^{-1}(t)
contains a disk whose boundary lies in
s
, then
s\cup t
contains a bihedron.
Otherwise, since the number of circles in
s^{\prime}\cap t
is strictly smaller than in
s\cap t
, there exists a connected component
S\subset H^{-1}(t)
such that
S\cap(\hat{s}\times\{0\})
is a disjoint union of at least two circles.

Choose two such circles, say
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
.
Then there exist arcs
a\subset\hat{s}\times\{0\}
and
b\subset S
, each joining
C_{1}
to
C_{2}
.
Let
P
be a regular neighborhood of
H(C_{1}\cup a\cup C_{2})
in
s
, and let
Q
be a regular neighborhood of
H(C_{1}\cup b\cup C_{2})
in
t
.
Then
P
and
Q
are pairs of pants that together form a bigon.

Therefore, if two essential tori are not in minimal position, they necessarily form either a bigon or a bihedron.
∎

By Laudenbach
[
15
]
, two homotopic sphere systems are isotopic.
Hatcher
[
10
]
show that two sphere systems can be in “normal form.”
This is equivalent to minimal position.
That is, two sphere systems are in normal form if and only if they are in minimal position.
Refer to Horbez’s article
[
12
]
.

The situation is not different if we consider an essential torus and a sphere system.
Gültepe
[
8
]
defines the normal form of a torus with respect to a sphere system.
This is equivalent to minimal position.

Theorem 3.5

(Minimal position)
.

Let
A_{1},\dots,A_{k}
be homotopy classes of essential simple closed curves, essential spheres and essential tori.
Then there are representatives

a_{1}\in A_{1},\dots,a_{k}\in A_{k}

such that

\lvert\pi_{0}(a_{i}\cap a_{j})\rvert=i(A_{i},A_{j})

for all distinct
i
and
j
.

Proof.

We proceed by induction on the number
k
of homotopy classes.
The statement is already seen to be true for
k\leq 2
.
Assume now that
k>2
and that the statement holds for all smaller values of
k
.

By the inductive hypothesis, there exist representatives
a_{1},\dots,a_{k-1}
of
A_{1},\dots,A_{k-1}
, respectively, such that

\lvert\pi_{0}(a_{i}\cap a_{j})\rvert=i(A_{i},A_{j})

for all distinct
i,j\in\{1,\dots,k-1\}
.
Choose a representative
a_{k}\in A_{k}
.
It suffices to show that if

\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}\lvert\pi_{0}(a_{i}\cap a_{k})\rvert>\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}i(A_{i},A_{k}),

then there exists another representative
a_{k}^{\prime}\in A_{k}
satisfying

\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}\lvert\pi_{0}(a_{i}\cap a_{k}^{\prime})\rvert<\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}\lvert\pi_{0}(a_{i}\cap a_{k})\rvert.

This will produce a representative of
A_{k}
in minimal position with respect to all
A_{i}
for
i<k
.

Case 1.
Some
a_{i}
is an essential simple closed curve.

Without loss of generality, assume
a_{k}
is such a curve.
Let
\mathcal{A}
denote the set of all subarcs
b\subset a_{k}
for which
b\cup c
bounds a bigon with some subarc
c\subset a_{i}
.
The set
\mathcal{A}
is partially ordered by inclusion.
Choose a minimal element
b_{0}\in\mathcal{A}
and let
c_{0}\subset a_{i}
be such that
b_{0}\cup c_{0}
bounds a disk
D_{0}
.
For each
j\neq i
, minimality of
b_{0}
implies that every component of
a_{j}\cap D_{0}
is an arc joining
b_{0}
and
c_{0}
.
(If a component is a circle or a point, one may shrink
D_{0}
slightly to remove it.)
Let
a_{k}^{\prime}
be the simple closed curve obtained by homotoping
b_{0}
to
c_{0}
.
Then
\lvert a_{j}\cap a_{k}^{\prime}\rvert\leq\lvert a_{j}\cap a_{k}\rvert
for all
j\neq i
, as required.

Case 2.
None of
a_{1},\dots,a_{k}
is an essential simple closed curve.

The argument is analogous.
Suppose
a_{k}
and
a_{i}
form a bihedron for some
i
; that is, there exist disks
d_{i}\subset a_{i}
and
d_{k}\subset a_{k}
such that
d_{i}\cup d_{k}
is a sphere bounding a
3
–ball.
Assume
d_{k}
is minimal in the sense that no proper subset of
d_{k}
forms a bigon or a bihedron with any
a_{j}
.
Let
B_{0}
denote the
3
–ball bounded by
d_{i}\cup d_{k}
.
By the inductive hypothesis, and by minimality of
d_{k}
, each intersection
a_{j}\cap B_{0}
is a disjoint union of disks and cylinders connecting
d_{i}
and
d_{k}
.
Let
a_{k}^{\prime}
be obtained from
a_{k}
by homotoping
d_{k}
to
d_{i}
.
Then
\lvert\pi_{0}(a_{j}\cap a_{k}^{\prime})\rvert\leq\lvert\pi_{0}(a_{j}\cap a_{k})\rvert
for all
j\neq i
.
The case in which
a_{k}
and
a_{i}
form a bigon is treated similarly and will be omitted.

By iterating this procedure, we obtain a representative
a_{k}^{\prime\prime}\in A_{k}
satisfying

\lvert\pi_{0}(a_{i}\cap a_{k}^{\prime\prime})\rvert=i(A_{i},A_{k})
for all
i\neq k
.
Hence, all pairs among
a_{1},\dots,a_{k-1},a_{k}^{\prime\prime}
are in minimal position, completing the proof.
∎

3.2.
Intersection number and Dehn twist

In this section, we analyze when two Dehn twists commute.
If their representatives have disjoint supports, then the corresponding Dehn twists clearly commute.
We are interested in the converse question: if two Dehn twists commute, must they admit representatives with disjoint supports?

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
T
and
T^{\prime}
be homotopy classes of essential tori.
If
i(\delta_{\vec{T}}(T^{\prime}),T^{\prime})=0
for some orientation of
T
, then we have
i(T,T^{\prime})=0
.

Proof.

Suppose
i(T,T^{\prime})>0
, and choose representatives
t\in T
and
t^{\prime}\in T^{\prime}
in minimal position.
Let
N(t)
be a regular neighborhood of
t
, together with a homeomorphism

\phi:N(t)\to t\times[0,1].

Let us assume that
N(t)
is small enough that
\phi\big(N(t)\cap t^{\prime}\big)
is a disjoint union of annuli.
Let
\delta_{\vec{t}}
be a representative of
\delta_{\vec{T}}
whose support is contained in
N(t)
.
Since
t
and
t^{\prime}
intersect, it follows that
\delta_{\vec{t}}(t^{\prime})
and
t^{\prime}
also intersect.
Thus it remains to show that
\delta_{\vec{t}}(t^{\prime})
and
t^{\prime}
are in minimal position.

Suppose
\delta_{\vec{t}}(t^{\prime})
and
t^{\prime}
form a bihedron.
Then there exist disks
D_{1}\subset\delta_{\vec{t}}(t^{\prime})
and
D_{2}\subset t^{\prime}
such that
D_{1}\cup D_{2}
is a sphere bounding a 3-ball.
Note that
D_{1}\cap D_{2}\subset N(t)\cap t^{\prime}
.
By enlarging
D_{2}
, we may assume its boundary lies in
t\cap t^{\prime}
.
And homotope
D_{1}
such that the interior of
D_{1}
is disjoint from
t^{\prime}
.
Let
\psi:t\times[0,1]\to t
denote the projection.
Then
\psi\phi(D_{1})
is a disk whose boundary is contained in
t\cap t^{\prime}
.
Thus
\psi\phi(D_{1})
together with
D_{2}
forms a bihedron in
t\cup t^{\prime}
, contradicting the assumption that
t
and
t^{\prime}
are in minimal position.

Suppose
\delta_{\vec{t}}(t^{\prime})
and
t^{\prime}
form a bigon.
Then there exist pairs of pants
P_{1}\subset\delta_{\vec{t}}(t^{\prime})
and
P_{2}\subset t^{\prime}
such that
P_{1}\cup P_{2}
is a twice-punctured torus bounding a disk.
By enlarging
P_{2}
, we may assume its two boundary components lie in
t\cap t^{\prime}
.
On the other hand,
\psi\phi(P_{1})
is a pair of pants contained in
t
.
So
\psi\phi(P_{1})
and
P_{2}
form a bigon in
t\cup t^{\prime}
, again contradicting the minimal position of
t
and
t^{\prime}
.

Since both cases lead to contradictions,
\delta_{\vec{t}}(t^{\prime})
and
t^{\prime}
must be in minimal position.
As they intersect, we conclude that

i(\delta_{\vec{T}}(T^{\prime}),T^{\prime})>0,

as desired.
∎

Theorem 3.7
.

Let
T
and
T^{\prime}
be homotopy classes of essential tori.
Then, we have
[\delta_{\vec{T}},\delta_{\vec{T}^{\prime}}]=1
for any orientations of
T
and
T^{\prime}
if and only if
i(T,T^{\prime})=0
.

Proof.

Suppose first that
i(T,T^{\prime})=0
.
Then there exist representatives
t\in T
and
t^{\prime}\in T^{\prime}
which are disjoint.
Since the supports of
\delta_{\vec{t}}
and
\delta_{\vec{t}^{\prime}}
are disjoint, these homeomorphisms commute.
It follows that
\delta_{\vec{T}}
and
\delta_{\vec{T}^{\prime}}
commute.

Conversely, assume that
\delta_{\vec{T}}
and
\delta_{\vec{T}^{\prime}}
commute.
Then

\delta_{\delta_{\vec{T}^{\prime}}(\vec{T})}=\delta_{\vec{T}^{\prime}}\delta_{\vec{T}}\delta_{\vec{T}^{\prime}}^{-1}=\delta_{\vec{T}}.

So
\delta_{\vec{T}^{\prime}}(T)=T
.
By Lemma
3.6
, this implies that
i(T,T^{\prime})=0
.
Therefore,
\delta_{\vec{T}}
and
\delta_{\vec{T}^{\prime}}
commute if and only if
i(T,T^{\prime})=0
.
∎

4.
On the universal cover

Let
p:\widetilde{\mathbb{M}}\to\mathbb{M}
be a universal cover.
Any complete geodesic Riemannian metric on
\mathbb{M}
induces Gromov hyperbolicity on
\widetilde{\mathbb{M}}
.
So we obtain a Gromov boundary of
\widetilde{\mathbb{M}}
, denoted by
\partial\widetilde{\mathbb{M}}
.
Two bi-infinite lines on
\widetilde{\mathbb{M}}
are said to be
equivalent
if the Gromov–Hausdorff distance between them is finite.
For a bi-infinite line
\tilde{c}
and an essential sphere
\tilde{s}
in
\widetilde{\mathbb{M}}
, we say
\tilde{c}

penetrates

\tilde{s}
if
\tilde{c}
and
\tilde{s}
intersect without bigon.
For two integers
a
and
b
, let
\operatorname{lcm}(a,b)
denote the least common multiple of
a
and
b
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
c
and
c^{\prime}
be essential simple closed curves, and let
s
be an essential sphere, which are in minimal position.
Let
\tilde{c}
and
\tilde{c}^{\prime}
be lifts of
c
and
c^{\prime}
, respectively.
If
\tilde{c}
is not equivalent to
\tilde{c}^{\prime}
, then the number of lifts of
s
penetrated by both
\tilde{c}
and
\tilde{c}^{\prime}
is at most
\operatorname{lcm}(i([c],[s]),i([c^{\prime}],[s]))
.

Proof.

If
\operatorname{lcm}(i([c],[s]),i([c^{\prime}],[s]))=0
, then
s
is disjoint from either
c
or
c^{\prime}
, that is, there is no lifts of
s
penetrated by both
\tilde{c}
and
\tilde{c}^{\prime}
.
So the statement holds in this case.
Let us assume that
\operatorname{lcm}(i([c],[s]),i([c^{\prime}],[s]))>0
.

Suppose, for contradiction, that there are at least
\operatorname{lcm}(i([c],[s]),i([c^{\prime}],[s]))+1
lifts of
s
, each of which penetrated by both
\tilde{c}
and
\tilde{c}^{\prime}
.
Then there are two distinct lifts
\tilde{s}_{1}
and
\tilde{s}_{2}
of
s
such that

p(\tilde{c}\cap\tilde{s}_{1})=p(\tilde{c}\cap\tilde{s}_{2})
and
p(\tilde{c}^{\prime}\cap\tilde{s}_{1})=p(\tilde{c}^{\prime}\cap\tilde{s}_{2})
.

If
w\in\mathbb{F}
is an element that maps
\tilde{s}_{1}
to
\tilde{s}_{2}
, then
w
preserves
\tilde{c}
and
\tilde{c}^{\prime}
.
So
\tilde{c}
and
\tilde{c}^{\prime}
are equivalent, which contradicts the hypothesis.
Therefore, the statement holds.
∎

A bi-infinite cylinder in
\widetilde{\mathbb{M}}
is said to be
essential
if it does not bound a solid cylinder.
Note that every lift of an essential torus is an essential cylinder.
We also say a bi-infinite line
\tilde{c}

penetrates
an essential cylinder
\tilde{t}
in
\widetilde{\mathbb{M}}
if they intersect without bigon.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
c
and
c^{\prime}
be essential simple closed curves, and let
t
be an essential torus, which are in minimal position.
Let
\tilde{c}
be a lift of
c
, and let
\tilde{c}^{\prime}
be a lift of
c^{\prime}
.
If
\tilde{c}
is not equivalent to
\tilde{c}^{\prime}
, then the number of lifts of
t
penetrated by both
\tilde{c}
and
\tilde{c}^{\prime}
is at most
2\operatorname{lcm}(i([c],[t]),i([c^{\prime}],[t]))
.

Meanwhile,
\mathbb{F}
acts properly discontinuously on
\widetilde{\mathbb{M}}
.
We need the following property of
\mathbb{F}
for a proof of Proposition
4.2
.

Theorem 4.3

(Lyndon and Schützenberger
[
16
]
)
.

Let
x,y,z\in\mathbb{F}
be nontrivial primitive elements.
Suppose the equation
x^{a}y^{b}z^{c}=1
holds for some
a,b,c\geq 2
.
Then there exists an element
w\in\mathbb{F}
such that
x,y,z\in\{w,w^{-1}\}
.

We say an element
g\in\mathbb{F}

minimally preserves
a bi-infinite line
\tilde{c}
if
h
is any element of
\mathbb{F}
that preserves
\tilde{c}
, then one gets
h=g^{n}
for some
n\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Proof of Proposition
4.2
.

Suppose there are at least
2\operatorname{lcm}(i([c],[t]),i([c^{\prime}],[t]))+1
lifts of
t
which are penetrated by both
\tilde{c}
and
\tilde{c}^{\prime}
.
Then there are three lifts
\tilde{t}_{1}
,
\tilde{t}_{2}
and
\tilde{t}_{3}
of
t
such that

p(\tilde{c}\cap\tilde{t}_{1})=p(\tilde{c}\cap\tilde{t}_{2})=p(\tilde{c}\cap\tilde{t}_{3})
and
p(\tilde{c}^{\prime}\cap\tilde{t}_{1})=p(\tilde{c}^{\prime}\cap\tilde{t}_{2})=p(\tilde{c}^{\prime}\cap\tilde{t}_{3})
.

Let
g
and
g^{\prime}
be elements of
\mathbb{F}
minimally preserving
\tilde{c}
and
\tilde{c}^{\prime}
, respectively.
Let us take signs for
g
and
g^{\prime}
to satisfy that

g^{m+n}(\tilde{t}_{1})=g^{n}(\tilde{t}_{2})=\tilde{t}_{3}
and
(g^{\prime})^{m^{\prime}+n^{\prime}}(\tilde{t}_{1})=(g^{\prime})^{n^{\prime}}(\tilde{t}_{2})=\tilde{t}_{3}

for some positive integers

.
Then
(g^{\prime})^{m^{\prime}}g^{-m}
and
(g^{\prime})^{m^{\prime}+n^{\prime}}g^{-(m+n)}
preserve
\tilde{t}_{2}
and
\tilde{t}_{3}
, respectively.
If
(g^{\prime})^{m^{\prime}}g^{-m}
is trivial, then
\tilde{c}
and
\tilde{c}^{\prime}
are equivalent, so this contradicts the hypothesis.
Otherwise, there exists a nontrivial element
h_{2}\in\mathbb{F}
that preserves
\tilde{t}_{2}
such that
h_{2}=(g^{\prime})^{m^{\prime}}g^{-m}
.

Similarly, since
g^{-(m+n)}(g^{\prime})^{m^{\prime}+n^{\prime}}
is nontrivial, there exists a nontrivial element
h_{3}\in\mathbb{F}
that preserves
\tilde{t}_{3}
such that
h_{3}=(g^{\prime})^{m^{\prime}+n^{\prime}}g^{-(m+n)}
.
If
h_{3}
is conjugate to
h_{2}
, then this implies that
\tilde{c}
and
\tilde{c}^{\prime}
are equivalent.
Otherwise,

can be expressed as a nontrivial power of some primitive element
h
.
That is, we get an equation
h^{n}=(g^{\prime})^{m^{\prime}+n^{\prime}}g^{-(m+n)}
for some
n\geq 2
.
By Theorem
4.3
,
h,g,g^{\prime}
are powers of a common element.
That is,
\tilde{c}
and
\tilde{c}^{\prime}
are equivalent.
This is a contradiction.
Therefore, there are at most
2\operatorname{lcm}(i([c],[t]),i([c^{\prime}],[t]))
lifts of
t
that are penetrated by both
\tilde{c}
and
\tilde{c}^{\prime}
.
∎

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
c
be an essential simple closed curve, and let

of

, let

.

be an element of

minimally preserving

.

be a lift of

.

5.
Compatible collection and fellow traveler

In this section, we introduce compatible collections and the notion of fellow travelers.

5.1.
Compatible collection

A homotopy class of an essential simple closed curve is said to
penetrate
a homotopy class of an essential torus if their intersection number is positive.
Given two homotopy classes
T
and
T^{\prime}
of essential tori, if the core of
T
penetrates
T^{\prime}
, we simply say that
T

penetrates

T^{\prime}
.
We say that
T
and
T^{\prime}

co-penetrate
if they penetrate each other.
A finite collection
\{T_{1},\dots,T_{k}\}
of homotopy classes of essential tori is called
compatible
if their cores are pairwise distinct and every pair that intersects also co-penetrates.

5.2.
Fellow travelers

Let
V=\{T_{1},\dots,T_{k}\}
be a compatible collection of homotopy classes of essential tori, and let
\Gamma
be the
coincidence graph
of
V
.
Thus the vertices of
\Gamma
are
T_{1},\dots,T_{k}
, and
T_{i}
is joined to
T_{j}
by an edge if and only if
i(T_{i},T_{j})=0
.
We adopt standard graph-theoretic notation: for instance,
\operatorname{lk}(T_{i})
denotes the link of
T_{i}
and
\operatorname{st}(T_{i})
its star.

For a homotopy class
\gamma
of an essential simple closed curve, a
lift

\tilde{\gamma}
of
\gamma
means an equivalence class of lifts of representatives of
\gamma
.
Likewise, for a homotopy class
T
of an essential torus, a
lift

\tilde{T}
of
T
refers to an equivalence class of lifts of representatives of
T
.
We say that
\tilde{\gamma}

penetrates

\tilde{T}
if any representative of
\tilde{\gamma}
intersects any representative of
\tilde{T}
.

For a positive integer
N
, we say a homotopy
\gamma
of an essential simple closed curve
N
–fellow-travels with
T_{i}
(with respect to
\Gamma
)
if
\gamma
penetrates
T_{i}
and there are lifts
\tilde{\gamma}
of
\gamma
and
\tilde{\gamma}_{i}
of
\gamma_{i}
such that for each
T_{j}\in\Gamma\setminus\operatorname{st}(T_{i})
, at least
N
lifts of
T_{j}
are penetrated by both
\tilde{\gamma}
and
\tilde{\gamma}_{i}
.
For each
i
, let
\mathcal{FT}\!_{N}(T_{i},\Gamma)
denote the collection of
N
–fellow travelers of
T_{i}
with respect to
\Gamma
.
By definition, the sequence

\mathcal{FT}\!_{1}(T_{i},\Gamma)\supseteq\mathcal{FT}\!_{2}(T_{i},\Gamma)\supseteq\mathcal{FT}\!_{3}(T_{i},\Gamma)\supseteq\dots

is satisfied.
Define

\Delta(\Gamma):=\max_{i,j,\ell=1,\dots,k}\operatorname{lcm}(i(\gamma_{i},T_{j}),\,i(\gamma_{\ell},T_{j})),

where
\operatorname{lcm}
denotes the least common multiple.
Then the following holds.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
\gamma
be a homotopy class of an essential simple closed curve that
(4\,\Delta(\Gamma)+3)
–fellow-travel with
T_{i}
.
Then there exist lifts
\tilde{\gamma}
of
\gamma
and
\tilde{\gamma}_{i}
of
\gamma_{i}
such that the following condition is satisfied: for every
T_{j}\in\Gamma\setminus\operatorname{st}(T_{i})
, there exist pairwise distinct lifts
\tilde{T}_{j},\tilde{T}_{j}^{\prime},\tilde{T}_{j}^{\prime\prime}
of
T_{j}
with the property that

(1)

each of
\tilde{T}_{j},\tilde{T}_{j}^{\prime},\tilde{T}_{j}^{\prime\prime}
is penetrated by both
\tilde{\gamma}
and
\tilde{\gamma}_{i}
, and

(2)

whenever
\tilde{T}_{\ell}
is a lift of some
T_{\ell}\in\operatorname{lk}(T_{i})
penetrated by
\tilde{\gamma}
, then the core of
\tilde{T}_{\ell}
does not penetrate any of
\tilde{T}_{j},\tilde{T}_{j}^{\prime},\tilde{T}_{j}^{\prime\prime}
.

Proof.

By the hypothesis, there exist lifts
\tilde{T}_{j}^{(1)},\dots,\tilde{T}_{j}^{(J)}
of
T_{j}
, with
J\geq 4\,\Delta(\Gamma)+3
, each of which is penetrated by both
\tilde{\gamma}
and
\tilde{\gamma}_{i}
.
Fix an orientation on
\tilde{\gamma}
; we may assume that the lifts
\tilde{T}_{j}^{(1)},\dots,\tilde{T}_{j}^{(J)}
are indexed in the order in which
\tilde{\gamma}
meets them.

Now choose a lift
\tilde{T}_{\ell}
of some
T_{\ell}\in\operatorname{lk}(T_{i})
that is penetrated by
\tilde{\gamma}
, and let
\tilde{\gamma}_{\ell}
denote its core.
By Proposition
4.2
, at most
2\,\operatorname{lcm}(i(\gamma_{i},T_{j}),i(\gamma_{\ell},T_{j}))
lifts of
T_{j}
can be penetrated by both
\tilde{\gamma}_{\ell}
and
\tilde{\gamma}_{i}
.
Since
\Delta(\Gamma)
bounds all such least common multiples, it follows that
\tilde{T}_{j}^{(2\,\Delta(\Gamma)+1)},\dots,\tilde{T}_{j}^{(J-2\,\Delta(\Gamma))}
are not penetrated by
\tilde{\gamma}_{\ell}
.

Choose three pairwise distinct lifts
\tilde{T}_{j},\tilde{T}_{j}^{\prime},\tilde{T}_{j}^{\prime\prime}\in\{\tilde{T}_{j}^{(2\,\Delta(\Gamma)+1)},\dots,\tilde{T}_{j}^{(J-2\,\Delta(\Gamma))}\}
.
By the first paragraph, each of them is penetrated by both
\tilde{\gamma}
and
\tilde{\gamma}_{i}
.
Furthermore, the second paragraph implies that none of
\tilde{T}_{j},\tilde{T}_{j}^{\prime}
, or
\tilde{T}_{j}^{\prime\prime}
is penetrated by the core of any lift of any
T_{\ell}\in\operatorname{lk}(T_{i})
.
This establishes the desired conclusion.
∎

5.3.
Dehn twists and fellow travelers

For each
i
, we write
\delta_{i}
for the Dehn twist
\delta_{\vec{T}_{i}}
along
T_{i}
, choosing some orientation of
T_{i}
.
Define

M(\Gamma):=\max_{i,j=1,\dots,k}i(\gamma_{i},T_{j}).

The next proposition shows that, after applying a suitable power of
\delta_{i}
to certain homotopy classes of essential simple closed curves, the resulting curve fellow-travels with
T_{i}
for a long time.
As a consequence, each set
\mathcal{FT}\!_{4\,\Delta(\Gamma)+3}(T_{i},\Gamma)
contains infinitely many homotopy classes of essential simple closed curves.

Proposition 5.2
.

If an integer
n
satisfies
\lvert n\rvert\geq 5\,M(\Gamma)+8
, then we have

\delta_{j}^{n}(\mathcal{FT}\!_{3}(T_{i},\Gamma)\cup\{\gamma_{i}\})\subseteq\mathcal{FT}\!_{4\,\Delta(\Gamma)+3}(T_{j},\Gamma)

for all co-penetrating pairs
T_{i}
and
T_{j}
.

Proof.

Let
\gamma\in\mathcal{FT}\!_{3}(T_{i},\Gamma)\cup\{\gamma_{i}\}
be given.
By definition, there are lifts
\tilde{\gamma}
and
\tilde{\gamma}_{i}
of
\gamma
and
\gamma_{i}
, respectively, such that they penetrate three consecutive lifts
\tilde{T}_{j},\tilde{T}_{j}^{\prime},\tilde{T}_{j}^{\prime\prime}
of
T_{j}
.
Choose
T_{\ell}
such that
T_{j}
and
T_{\ell}
co-penetrate.
Let
\tilde{\gamma}_{j},\tilde{\gamma}_{j}^{\prime},\tilde{\gamma}_{j}^{\prime\prime}
be the cores of
\tilde{T}_{j},\tilde{T}_{j}^{\prime},\tilde{T}_{j}^{\prime\prime}
, respectively.
Let
\tilde{\delta}_{j}
be a lift of
\delta_{j}
that preserves
\tilde{T}_{j}
and
\tilde{T}_{j}^{\prime}
.
Then there exists a nontrivial element
g\in\mathbb{F}
such that
\tilde{\delta}_{j}^{n}(\tilde{\gamma}_{j}^{\prime\prime})=g^{n}\tilde{\gamma}_{j}^{\prime\prime}
where
g
preserves
\tilde{\gamma}_{j}^{\prime}
.

We claim that the number of lifts of
T_{\ell}
that are penetrated by
\tilde{\gamma}_{j}^{\prime}
and that separate
\tilde{\gamma}_{j}^{\prime\prime}
from
g^{\,n}\tilde{\gamma}_{j}^{\prime\prime}
is at least

(\lvert n\rvert-3)\,i(\gamma_{j},T_{\ell}).

Without loss of generality, assume
n>0
; the case
n<0
follows by the same argument applied to
g^{-1}
in place of
g
.
Let
\tilde{T}_{\ell}
be any lift of
T_{\ell}
penetrated by
\tilde{\gamma}_{j}^{\prime}
.

If
g^{a}\tilde{T}_{\ell}
is never penetrated by
\tilde{\gamma}_{j}^{\prime\prime}
for any
a\in\mathbb{Z}
, then there exists
a_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
g^{a_{0}}\tilde{T}_{\ell}
separates
g^{-b}\tilde{\gamma}_{j}^{\prime\prime}
from
g^{c}\tilde{\gamma}_{j}^{\prime\prime}
for all
b,c>0
.
Consequently,

g^{a_{0}}\tilde{T}_{\ell},\ g^{a_{0}+1}\tilde{T}_{\ell},\ \dots,\ g^{a_{0}+n-1}\tilde{T}_{\ell}

all separate
\tilde{\gamma}_{j}^{\prime\prime}
from
g^{\,n}\tilde{\gamma}_{j}^{\prime\prime}
.
If instead
g^{a_{1}}\tilde{T}_{\ell}
is penetrated by
\tilde{\gamma}_{j}^{\prime\prime}
for some
a_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}
, then by Proposition
4.2
the lift
g^{a_{1}}\tilde{T}_{\ell}
separates
g^{-b-1}\tilde{\gamma}_{j}^{\prime\prime}
from
g^{c+1}\tilde{\gamma}_{j}^{\prime\prime}
for all
b,c>0
.
It follows that

g^{a_{1}+2}\tilde{T}_{\ell},\ g^{a_{1}+3}\tilde{T}_{\ell},\ \dots,\ g^{a_{1}+n-2}\tilde{T}_{\ell}

all separate
\tilde{\gamma}_{j}^{\prime\prime}
from
g^{\,n}\tilde{\gamma}_{j}^{\prime\prime}
.

Since the lifts of
T_{\ell}
penetrated by
\tilde{\gamma}_{j}^{\prime}
consist of exactly
i(\gamma_{j},T_{\ell})
distinct
\langle g\rangle
–orbits, the above argument shows that the total number of such separating lifts is at least

(n-3)\,i(\gamma_{j},T_{\ell})=(|n|-3)\,i(\gamma_{j},T_{\ell}).

This proves the claim.

Let
\mathcal{T}
be the set of lifts of
T_{\ell}
that are penetrated by
\tilde{\gamma}_{j}^{\prime}
and that separate
\tilde{\gamma}_{j}^{\prime\prime}
from
g^{\,n}\tilde{\gamma}_{j}^{\prime\prime}
.
By Proposition
4.2
, at most
2\,i(\gamma_{j},T_{\ell})
lifts in
\mathcal{T}
are penetrated by
\tilde{\gamma}_{j}
.
If a lift in
\mathcal{T}
is not penetrated by
\tilde{\gamma}_{j}
, then it separates
\tilde{\gamma}_{j}
from either
\tilde{\gamma}_{j}^{\prime\prime}
or
g^{\,n}\tilde{\gamma}_{j}^{\prime\prime}
.
By Proposition
4.4
, the number of such lifts separating
\tilde{\gamma}_{j}
from
\tilde{\gamma}_{j}^{\prime\prime}
is at most
i(\gamma_{i},T_{\ell})
.
Therefore, the number of lifts in
\mathcal{T}
that separate
\tilde{\gamma}_{j}
from
g^{\,n}\tilde{\gamma}_{j}^{\prime\prime}
is at least

(|n|-3)\,i(\gamma_{j},T_{\ell})\;-\;2\,i(\gamma_{j},T_{\ell})\;-\;i(\gamma_{i},T_{\ell})\;\geq\;4\,\Delta(\Gamma)+3.

Note that any lift of
T_{\ell}
separating
\tilde{\gamma}_{j}
from
g^{\,n}\tilde{\gamma}_{j}^{\prime\prime}
must be penetrated by
\tilde{\delta}_{j}^{\,n}(\tilde{\gamma})
.
Hence, for each
T_{\ell}\in\Gamma\setminus\operatorname{st}(T_{j})
, both
\tilde{\delta}_{j}^{\,n}(\tilde{\gamma})
and
\tilde{\gamma}_{j}^{\prime}
penetrate at least
4\,\Delta(\Gamma)+3
lifts of
T_{\ell}
.
Therefore,
\delta_{j}^{\,n}(\gamma)
is contained in
\mathcal{FT}\!_{4\,\Delta(\Gamma)+3}(T_{j},\Gamma)
.
∎

Proposition 5.3
.

If
T_{i},T_{j_{1}},\dots,T_{j_{m}}
are pairwise distinct and pairwise disjoint, i.e.,
i(T_{a},T_{b})=0
for all
a,b\in\{i,j_{1},\dots,j_{m}\}
, then we have

\delta_{j_{m}}^{n_{m}}\dots\delta_{j_{1}}^{n_{1}}(\mathcal{FT}\!_{4\,\Delta(\Gamma)+3}(T_{i},\Gamma))\subseteq\mathcal{FT}\!_{3}(T_{i},\Gamma)

for all
n_{1},\dots,n_{m}\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Proof.

Choose
\gamma\in\mathcal{FT}\!_{4\,\Delta(\Gamma)+3}(T_{i},\Gamma)
.
By Lemma
5.1
, for each
T_{\ell}\in\Gamma\setminus\operatorname{st}(T_{i})
, there are three lifts of
T_{\ell}
, denoted by
\tilde{T}_{\ell},\tilde{T}_{\ell}^{\prime},\tilde{T}_{\ell}^{\prime\prime}
, consecutively penetrated by
\tilde{\gamma}
and
\tilde{\gamma}_{i}
that are not penetrated by any core of a lift of
T_{j_{1}},\dots,T_{j_{\ell}}
.

For each
a\in\{1,\dots,\ell\}
, let
\tilde{\delta}_{j_{a}}
be the lift of
\delta_{j_{a}}
that fixes
\tilde{\gamma}_{i}
pointwise.
Let
\mathcal{O}
be the collection of the lifts of
T_{j_{1}},\dots,T_{j_{\ell}}
penetrated by
\tilde{\gamma}
.
Note
\mathcal{O}
can be ordered by an orientation of
\tilde{\gamma}
.
Let
\tilde{T}^{\prime}
and
\tilde{T}^{\prime\prime}
be the consecutive elements of
\mathcal{O}
such that the connected component of
\widetilde{\mathbb{M}}\setminus\bigsqcup_{\tilde{T}\in\mathcal{O}}\tilde{T}
that contains
\tilde{\gamma}
is bounded by
\tilde{T}^{\prime}\cup\tilde{T}^{\prime\prime}
.

Then
\tilde{\delta}_{j_{\ell}}^{n_{\ell}}\dots\tilde{\delta}_{j_{1}}^{n_{1}}
preserves each of
\tilde{T}^{\prime}
and
\tilde{T}^{\prime\prime}
.
So
\tilde{\delta}_{j_{\ell}}^{n_{\ell}}\dots\tilde{\delta}_{j_{1}}^{n_{1}}(\tilde{\gamma})
penetrates both
\tilde{T}^{\prime}
and
\tilde{T}^{\prime\prime}
.
Hence,
\tilde{T}_{\ell},\tilde{T}_{\ell}^{\prime},\tilde{T}_{\ell}^{\prime\prime}
are also penetrated by
\tilde{\delta}_{j_{\ell}}^{n_{\ell}}\dots\tilde{\delta}_{j_{1}}^{n_{1}}(\tilde{\gamma})
.
Therefore,
\delta_{j_{\ell}}^{n_{\ell}}\dots\delta_{j_{1}}^{n_{1}}(\gamma)
is contained in
\mathcal{FT}\!_{3}(T_{i},\Gamma)
.
∎

Remark 5.4
.

It is crucial that the cores of
T_{i},T_{j_{1}},\dots,T_{j_{\ell}}
are pairwise distinct in order for Proposition
5.3
to hold.
Otherwise, the composition
\delta_{j_{\ell}}^{n_{\ell}}\dots\delta_{j_{1}}^{n_{1}}
may coincide with a power of
\delta_{i}
which could send an element of
\mathcal{FT}\!_{4\,\Delta(\Gamma)+3}(T_{i},\Gamma)
outside of
\mathcal{FT}\!_{3}(T_{i},\Gamma)
.

5.4.
Right-angled Artin group generated by powers of Dehn twists

In this section, we look into a specific collection of essential tori whose sufficiently large powers of Dehn twists generate a right-angled Artin group.
Note that there exist finitely many Dehn twists that generate
\Out(\mathbb{F})
, which is more complicate than right-angled Artin groups.
This section is inspired by Koberda
[
14
]
and Seo
[
17
]
.

Let us remind some definitions.
A collection
V=\{T_{1},\dots,T_{k}\}
of homotopy classes of essential tori is said to be
compatible
if their cores are pairwise distinct and whenever
i(T_{i},T_{j})>0
, they co-penetrate.
The coincidence graph of
V
is a simple graph whose vertices are
T_{1},\dots,T_{k}
where
T_{i}
and
T_{j}
are joined by an edge if and only if
i(T_{i},T_{j})=0
.

Theorem 5.5
.

Let
V=\{T_{1},\dots,T_{k}\}
be a compatible collection of essential tori with cores
\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{k}
, and let
\Gamma
be the coincidence graph of
V
.
For each
i
, let
\delta_{i}=\delta_{\vec{T}_{i}}
be a Dehn twists along
T_{i}
.
Then for all

\lvert n_{1}\rvert,\dots,\lvert n_{k}\rvert\geq 5\,\max_{i\neq j}{i(\gamma_{i},T_{j})}+8,

the homomorphism

\Phi:A(\Gamma)\to\Out(\mathbb{F})

defined by

T_{i}\mapsto\delta_{i}^{n_{i}}

is injective.

Proof.

We say
\Gamma
is
centerless
if there is no complete subgraph
K
such that
\Gamma\cong K*\Gamma^{\prime}
for some
\Gamma^{\prime}\subset\Gamma
.
First, we handle the centerless case of Theorem
5.5
.
Let us suppose that
\Gamma
is centerless.

For each nontrivial element
x\in A(\Gamma)
, the
support
of
x
, denoted
\operatorname{supp}(x)
, is the subset of
V(\Gamma)
such that
\operatorname{supp}(x)=\operatorname{supp}(w)
for some reduced word
w
representing
x
.
This is well-defined: if both reduced words
w_{1}
and
w_{2}
represent the same element
x\in A(\Gamma)
, then
\operatorname{supp}(w_{1})=\operatorname{supp}(w_{2})
.

An element of
A(\Gamma)
is said to be
central
if its support forms a complete subgraph of
\Gamma
.
Every element of
A(\Gamma)
can be expressed as a product of central elements, which is called a
central form
.
A central form
x=x_{k}\cdots x_{1}
is said to be in
left greedy normal form
if for each
i<k
and each
v\in\operatorname{supp}(x_{i})
, there exists
u\in\operatorname{supp}(x_{i+1})
such that
[u,v]\neq 1
.

Since
\Gamma
is centerless, there exists a vertex
T_{0}\in\Gamma\setminus\operatorname{st}(\operatorname{supp}(x_{1}))
.
So there exists a vertex
T_{i_{1}}\in\operatorname{supp}(x_{1})
such that
[T_{i_{1}},T_{0}]\neq 1
.
By Propositions
5.2
and
5.3
, we have

\Phi(x_{1})(\gamma_{0})\in\mathcal{FT}\!_{3}(T_{i_{1}},\Gamma).

where
\gamma_{0}
is the core of
T_{0}
.
Inductively, one can show

\displaystyle\Phi(x_{j}\dots x_{1})(\gamma_{0})

\displaystyle=\Phi(x_{j})(\Phi(x_{j-1}\dots x_{1})(\gamma_{0}))

\displaystyle\in\Phi(x_{j})(\mathcal{FT}\!_{3}(T_{i_{j-1}},\Gamma))\subseteq\mathcal{FT}\!_{3}(T_{i_{j}},\Gamma).

for each
j=2,\dots,k
.

Since
\Phi(x)(\gamma_{0})\in\mathcal{FT}\!_{3}(T_{i_{k}},\Gamma)
and
\gamma_{0}\notin\mathcal{FT}\!_{3}(T_{i_{k}},\Gamma)
, it follows that
\Phi(x)
is nontrivial.
Hence, the homomorphism
\Phi
is injective.

Suppose that
\Gamma
decomposes as a join
K*\Gamma^{\prime}
, where
K
is a nonempty complete subgraph and
\Gamma^{\prime}
is a (possibly empty) centerless subgraph.
Then the center
\mathcal{Z}(A(\Gamma))
is naturally identified with
\mathbb{Z}^{K}
, and the quotient
A(\Gamma)/\mathcal{Z}(A(\Gamma))
is isomorphic to
A(\Gamma^{\prime})
.
If
\ker(\Phi)
is nontrivial, the argument from the previous subsection implies that

\ker(\Phi)\ \leq\ \mathcal{Z}(A(\Gamma))\ \cong\ \mathbb{Z}^{K}.

Choose an essential torus
T_{0}
that co-penetrates every
T\in\Gamma
.
Then the compatible graph of
V\cup\{T_{0}\}
is centerless.
Consequently, there exists
N^{\prime}>0
such that for all integers
n_{0},n_{1},\dots,n_{k}\geq N^{\prime}
, the subgroup

\langle\,\delta_{\vec{T}_{0}}^{n_{0}},\,\delta_{1}^{n_{1}},\,\dots,\,\delta_{k}^{n_{k}}\,\rangle

is isomorphic to the right-angled Artin group

A(\Gamma\sqcup\{T_{0}\})\cong A(\Gamma)*\mathbb{Z}\cong(A(\Gamma^{\prime})\times\mathbb{Z}^{K})*\mathbb{Z}.

In particular, the quotient
\mathcal{Z}(A(\Gamma))/\ker(\Phi)
contains a free-abelian subgroup of rank
\lvert K\rvert
, which forces
\ker(\Phi)
to be trivial.
Therefore,
\Phi
is injective.
∎

6.
The space of factorial curves

An essential simple closed curve is called
factorial
if it represents a
1
-free factor of
\mathbb{F}
.
Let
\mathcal{C}
denote the set of all homotopy classes of factorial curves.
Let
\mathbb{R}_{+}
denote the set of nonnegative real numbers, i.e.,
[0,\infty)
.
Define a map

i_{*}:\mathcal{C}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}^{\mathcal{S}},\qquad i_{*}(\gamma)(S)=i(\gamma,S)

for all
\gamma\in\mathcal{C}
and
S\in\mathcal{S}
.
Let
P(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{\mathcal{S}})
denote the projectivization of
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{\mathcal{S}}
, and let

p:\mathbb{R}_{+}^{\mathcal{S}}\setminus\{0\}\to P(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{\mathcal{S}})

be the natural projection.
Since the image of
i_{*}
contains no zero vector, the composition
p\circ i_{*}
is well-defined.

Proposition 6.1
.

The composition
p\circ i_{*}:\mathcal{C}\to P(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{\mathcal{S}})
is injective.

Proof.

Assume that
\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}\in\mathcal{C}
satisfy

(p\circ i_{*})(\gamma)=(p\circ i_{*})(\gamma^{\prime}).

Then there exists
\lambda>0
such that

i_{*}(\gamma)=\lambda\,i_{*}(\gamma^{\prime}).

Since
\gamma
corresponds to a free factor, there exists a separating sphere
s
disjoint from a representative
c\in\gamma
such that the component
C
of
\mathbb{M}\setminus s
containing
c
is homeomorphic to the once–punctured doubled solid torus
(\mathbb{S}^{1}\times\mathbb{S}^{2})\setminus\{x\}
for some point
x\in\mathbb{S}^{1}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}
.
This region
C
contains a unique isotopy class
S^{\prime}
of essential spheres not isotopic to
s
.
Choose a representative
s^{\prime}\in S^{\prime}
such that
c
intersects
s^{\prime}
in exactly one point.
Thus,

i(\gamma,[s])=0,\qquad i(\gamma,S^{\prime})=\lvert c\cap s^{\prime}\rvert=1.

By proportionality, it follows that

i(\gamma^{\prime},[s])=0,\qquad i(\gamma^{\prime},S^{\prime})=\frac{1}{\lambda}>0.

So
\gamma^{\prime}
has a representative
c^{\prime}
disjoint from
s
.
Because
s
is separating, the curve
c^{\prime}
lies entirely inside
C
.
Algebraically, the free factor determined by
c^{\prime}
is contained in the free factor determined by
C
.
Since
c^{\prime}
is factorial, these free factors must be equal.
Consequently,
c^{\prime}
is homotopic to
c
, and hence
\gamma=\gamma^{\prime}
.
∎

By Proposition
6.1
, we may regard
\mathcal{C}
as a subspace of
P(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{\mathcal{S}})
.

Proposition 6.2
.

The closure of
(p\circ i_{*})(\mathcal{C})
is compact.

Before proving Proposition
6.2
, we require the following technical lemma. This result is analogous to
[
6
, Lemma 4.2]
.

Lemma 6.3
.

Let
\Sigma
be a maximal sphere system of
\mathbb{M}_{g}
.
Then we have

i(\gamma,S)\leq(2g-2)\,i(\gamma,\Sigma)\,i(S,\Sigma)

for all
\gamma\in\mathcal{C}
and
S\in\mathcal{S}
.

Proof.

Choose representatives
c\in\gamma
,
s\in S
, and
\sigma\in\Sigma
that intersect pairwise minimally.
Cutting
\mathbb{M}_{g}
along
\sigma
yields
2g-2
thrice-punctured spheres.
For each such component
N
, the intersection
s\cap N
is a disjoint union of annuli, pairs of pants, and disks, with at most
i(S,\Sigma)
components.
Similarly,
c\cap N
is a disjoint union of at most
i(\gamma,\Sigma)
arcs.
Each of these arcs intersects
s
in at most
i(S,\Sigma)
points.
So the number of intersection points of
c
and
s
inside
N
is at most
i(\gamma,\Sigma)\,i(S,\Sigma)
.
Summing over all thrice-punctured components gives the desired inequality.
∎

The following proof is analogous to
[
6
, Proposition 4.1]
.

Proof of Proposition
6.2
.

Fix an isotopy class of a maximal sphere system
\Sigma
.
By Lemma
6.3
, the set
\{i_{*}(\gamma)/i(\gamma,\Sigma)\mid\gamma\in\mathcal{C}\}
is a subset of the bounded set

\{f\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{\mathcal{S}}\mid f(S)\leq(2g-2)\,i(S,\Sigma)\text{ for all }S\in\mathcal{S}\}.

By Tychonoff’s theorem, the closure of

\{i_{*}(\gamma)/i(\gamma,\Sigma)\mid\gamma\in\mathcal{C}\}

is compact.
Consequently, the closure of
(p\circ i_{*})(\mathcal{C})
is compact.
∎

The following is motivated by
[
6
, Proposition A.1]
.

Lemma 6.4
.

Let
T
be a homotopy class of an essential torus with a core
\gamma
.
If
\alpha
is a homotopy class of a factorial curve and
S
is an isotopy class of an essential sphere, then the following holds for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
:

\lvert i(\delta_{\vec{T}}^{n}(\alpha),S)-\lvert n\rvert\,i(\alpha,T)\,i(\gamma,S)\rvert\leq i(\alpha,S).

Proof.

Let
a\in\alpha
,
t\in T
and
s\in S
be representatives in minimal position.
Let
N(t)
be a regular neighborhood of
t
which is disjoint from
a\cap s
.
Let
a_{n}
be a representative of
\delta_{\vec{T}}^{n}(\alpha)
that follows
a
on the outside of
N(t)
such that each component of
a_{n}\cap N(t)
minimally intersect with
s
.

Then the number of components of
a_{n}\cap N(t)
is exactly
i(\alpha,T)
.
Furthermore, each component intersects with
s
in
(\lvert n\rvert\,i(\gamma,S))
-times.
So we obtain the following.

\displaystyle i(\delta_{\vec{T}}^{n}(\alpha),S)

\displaystyle\leq\lvert a_{n}\cap s\rvert=\lvert(a_{n}-N(t))\cap s\rvert+\lvert(a_{n}\cap N(t))\cap s\rvert

\displaystyle=\lvert a\cap s\rvert+\lvert i(\alpha,T)\rvert\,\lvert n\rvert\,i(\gamma,S)

\displaystyle=i(\alpha,S)+\lvert n\rvert\,i(\alpha,T)\,i(\gamma,S)

Suppose
a_{n}
and
s
form a bigon.
Then there exists an arc
a^{\prime}\subset a_{n}
and
b^{\prime}\subset s
such that
a^{\prime}\cup b^{\prime}
bounds a disk.
Since
a
and
s
do not form a bigon,
a^{\prime}
is not contained in
a
, that is,
a^{\prime}
and
a_{n}\cap N(t)
intersect.
Any component of
a_{n}\cap N(t)
cannot belong to
a^{\prime}
because this minimally intersects with
s
.
So we can decompose
a^{\prime}
into two arcs
a^{\prime\prime}
and
a^{\prime\prime\prime}
such that
a^{\prime\prime}\in a
and
a^{\prime\prime\prime}\in a_{n}\cap N(t)
.

By the above, reducing a bigon by homotoping
a_{n}
induces the decrease of the number of
(a\cap a_{n})\cap s
.
So there are at most
\lvert a\cap s\rvert
reductions of bigons to make
a_{n}
and
s
become in minimal position.
Since each bigon makes two intersection points, we get

\displaystyle i(\delta_{\vec{T}}^{n}(\alpha),S)

\displaystyle\geq\lvert a_{n}\cap s\rvert-2\,\lvert a\cap s\rvert

\displaystyle=\lvert n\rvert\,i(\alpha,T)\,i(\gamma,S)-i(\alpha,S)

Therefore, the inequality of the statement holds.
∎

The above lemma immediately induces the following proposition.

Theorem 6.5
.

Let
T
be a homotopy class of an essential torus with the core
\gamma
.
Let
\alpha
be a homotopy class of a factorial curve.
Then, we have
i(\alpha,T)>0
if and only if
(p\circ i_{*})(\delta_{\vec{T}}^{n}(\alpha))
converges to
(p\circ i_{*})(\gamma)
as
n\to\infty
.

Proof.

If
i(\alpha,T)=0
, then we have
\delta_{\vec{T}}^{n}(\alpha)=\alpha
for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
.
Otherwise, we have

\left\lvert\frac{1}{\lvert n\rvert\,i(\alpha,T)}\,i(\delta_{\vec{T}}^{n}(\alpha),S)-i(\gamma,S)\right\rvert\leq\frac{1}{\lvert n\rvert\,i(\alpha,T)}\,i(\alpha,S)

for each
S\in\mathcal{S}
, so
(p\circ i_{*})(\delta_{\vec{T}}^{n}(\alpha))
converges to
(p\circ i_{*})(\gamma)
as
n\to\infty
.
∎

By Theorem
6.5
, a core of a homotopy class of an essential torus is contained in the closure of
(p\circ i_{*})(\mathcal{C})
.
Note a core of a homotopy class of an essential torus may not be a factorial curve.
That is,
\overline{(p\circ i_{*})(\mathcal{C})}
contains homotopy classes of essential simple closed curves that is not factorial.

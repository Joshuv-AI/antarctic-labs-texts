Abstract




Abstract.
In this article, we obtain some necessary and sufficient conditions for the boundedness of fractional Hausdorff operators
h_{\Phi,\beta}
on weighted Lebesgue spaces
(0\leq\beta<1)
, which are fractional variants of Bandaliev-Safarova [Hacet. J. Math. Stat., 2021, 50, 1334-1346]; it is found that a new constraint for
\beta
should be added and it holds automatically for non-fractional variants in [HJMS, 2021]
(\beta=0)
. Then, we further obatin the boundedness of fractional Hausdorff operators
h_{\Phi,\beta}
on power-weighted Hardy spaces which are fractional variants of Ruan-Fan [Math. Nachr., 2017, 290, 2388-2400]. Ruan-Fan obtained the relevant boundedness results by means of the radial maximal function characterization of Hardy spaces, while in this paper, two different relevant results are obtained respectively by using the radial maximal function characterization and the Riesz characterization of power-weighted Hardy spaces.

1
Introduction

The Hausdorff operator originates from the Hausdorff summation method
[
10
]
. Hausdorff operators are a type of averaging operation, invariant under dilations, capable of achieving constructive approximation, with adjoint operators of the same type, and “almost commuting” with the Fourier transform
[
12
]
. Fix a locally integrable function on
(0,\infty)
. The definition of the one-dimensional Hausdorff operator is

h_{\Phi}(f)(x):=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\Phi(y^{-1}x)}{y}f(y)dy.

For convenience, we first assume that
f
belongs to the Schwartz function space
\mathscr{S}({\mathbb{R}})
. By choosing an appropriate
\Phi
, the Hausdorff operator includes many classical operators. For example, by choosing
\Phi
as
\chi_{(1,\infty)}(t)t^{-1}
,
\chi_{(0,1)}(t)
,
\chi_{(1,\infty)}(t)t^{-1}+\chi_{(0,1]}(t)
and
\gamma(1-t)^{\gamma-1}\chi_{(0,1)}(t)

(\gamma>0)
respectively, the Hausdorff operator becomes the Hardy, adjoint Hardy, Cesáro and Hardy-Littlewood-Pólya operators. Most of the results on Hausdorff operators in recent decades can be found in
[
14
]
.

A high-dimensional generalization of
h_{\Phi}
is

H_{\Phi}(f)(x):=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\frac{\Phi(|y|^{-1}x)}{|y|^{n}}f(y)dy.

The fractional Hausdorff operator is a natural generalization of the Hausdorff operator and is defined as

H_{\Phi,\beta}(f)(x):=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\frac{\Phi(|y|^{-1}x)}{|y|^{n-\beta}}f(y)dy.

It was first proposed by Lin and Sun
[
15
]
. By choosing
\Phi
as
|x|^{\beta-n}\chi_{(1,\infty)}(|x|)
,
\chi_{(0,1]}(|x|)
and
|x|^{\beta-n}\chi_{(1,\infty)}(|x|)+\chi_{(0,1]}(|x|)
respectively, the fractional Hausdorff operator
H_{\Phi,\beta}
becomes the fractional Hardy, adjoint Hardy and Hardy-Littlewood-Pólya operators. In the one-dimensional case, we denote
h_{\Phi,\beta}:=H_{\Phi,\beta}
.

Bandaliyev and Safarova
[
2
]
obtained the necessary and sufficient conditions for the boundedness of
h_{\Phi}
on weighted Lebesgue spaces. Gao, Wu and Guo
[
7
]
obtained the sufficient conditions for the boundedness of
H_{\Phi,\beta}
on the power-weighted Lebesgue spaces, where
n\geq 2
. Next, X. Liu, M. Wei, P. Song, and D. Yang
[
16
]
obtained the sharp reverse inequality for
H_{\Phi,\beta}
on the power-weighted Lebesgue spaces. And the boundedness of the multi-Huasdorff operator on power-weighted Lebesgue spaces are also studied in
[
6
]
. Therefore, these results naturally lead us to study the necessary and sufficient conditions for the boundedness of
h_{\Phi,\beta}
on weighted Lebesgue spaces.

Chen, Fan, Lin and Ruan
[
4
]
obtained the sufficient conditions for the boundedness of
H_{\Phi,\beta}
on Hardy spaces, where
n\geq 2
. Ruan and Fan
[
18
]
obtained the sufficient conditions for the boundedness of
H_{\Phi}
on power-weighted Hardy spaces, where
n\geq 1
. And multidimensional Hausdorff operators on Hardy sapces are also study in
[
19
]
. Therefore, these results naturally lead us to study the sufficient conditions for the boundedness of
h_{\Phi,\beta}
on power-weighted Hardy spaces.

For our results on weighted Lebesgue spaces (Theorems
3.1
and
3.2
below), when
\beta=0
and restricted to
\mathbb{R}_{+}
, our results completely include Theorems 3.1 and 3.3 in
[
2
]
. It is found that the constraint
q>1/(1-\beta)
and
p^{\prime}>1/(1-\beta)
need to be added respectively, and these two conditions naturally hold when
\beta=0
.

In proving the boundedness of fractional Hausdorff operators on power-weighted Hardy spaces, the following two points should be noted. First, Chen, Fan, Lin and Ruan
[
4
]
relied on the Riesz characterization of Hardy spaces when establishing the boundedness of fractional Hausdorff operators on Hardy spaces. Ruan and Fan
[
18
]
proved the boundedness of Hausdorff operators on power-weighted Hardy spaces by using power-weighted Hardy spaces defined via radial maximal functions. In our research, we found that the Riesz characterization of weighted Hardy spaces has also been established by Cao, Chang, D. Yang and S. Yang
[
3
]
. Thus, by employing the two methods from
[
4
]
and
[
18
]
, respectively, we obtained two sufficient conditions for the boundedness of fractional Hausdorff operators on power-weighted Hardy spaces. Moreover, the presence of
\beta
makes the step of applying the Leibniz formula more complicated. Our results do not include the case of
\beta=0
, the case of
\beta=0
has been studied in
[
18
]
.

The structure is as follows. In Section
2
, we present the basic Definitions and notation to be used. In Section
3
, we state the main results of this article. In Section
4
, we provide all the proofs.

Finally, we make some conventions on notation. Let
[x]
be the greatest integer not exceeding the real number
x
and
p^{\prime}
be the conjugate exponent of
p\in(1,\infty)
, namely,
1/p+1/p^{\prime}=1
. Throughout the whole article, the constant
C
depends on the parameters of importance and it may vary from line to line. Let
A,B\geq 0
. Then
A\lesssim B
and
B\gtrsim A
mean that there exists a constant
C>0
such that
A\leq CB
, where
C
depends only on the parameters of importance.

2
Definitions and notation

Definition 2.1
.

[
9
, Definition 8.2.1]

A
weight
is a nonnegative locally integrable function on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
that vanishes or takes the value
\infty
only on a set of measure zero. Given a weight
w
and a measurable set
E
, we denote the
wdx
-measure of
E
by

w(E):=\int_{E}w(x)dx.

Definition 2.2
.

[
18
, Definition 2.1]

Let
1<p<\infty
. We say that a weight
w\in A_{p}
if there exists a constant
C>0
such that for all balls
B\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
,

\left(\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}w(x)dx\right)\left(\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}w(x)^{-1/(p-1)}dx\right)^{p-1}\leq C.

We say that a weight
w\in A_{1}
if there is a constant
C>0
such that for all balls
B
,

\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}w(x)dx\leq C\underset{x\in B}{\operatorname{essinf}}w(x)

Finally, we define
A_{\infty}:=\bigcup_{1\leq p<\infty}A_{p}
.

It is known that
A_{p}\subset A_{r}
for all
r>p
. We also know that if
w\in A_{p}
with
1<p<\infty
, then
w\in A_{q}
for some
1<q<p
. Therefore, we may use
r_{w}:=\inf\left\{q>1:w\in A_{q}\right\}
to denote the
critical index
of
w
. Obviously, if
w\in A_{q},q>1
, then we have
1\leq q_{w}<q
.

An important example of
A_{p}
weight is the power function
|x|^{\alpha}
. It is well known that
|x|^{\alpha}
is an
A_{1}
weight if and only if
-n<\alpha\leq 0
. Also,

|x|^{\alpha}\in\cap_{(n+\alpha)/n<p<\infty}A_{p}\quad\text{if}\quad 0<\alpha<\infty,

(2.1)

where
(n+\alpha)/n
is called the critical index of
|x|^{\alpha}
.

Definition 2.3
.

[
8
, Definition 1.1.1]

For
1\leq p<\infty
, given a weight function
w
on
{\mathbb{R}}
, we define the
L^{p}_{w}({\mathbb{R}})
norm of a measurable function
f
by

\|f\|_{L^{p}_{w}({\mathbb{R}})}:=\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}|f(x)|^{p}w(x)dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

For any
1\leq p<\infty
, we define
L^{p}_{w}({\mathbb{R}})
to be the space of all measurable functions
f
with
\|f\|_{L^{p}_{w}({\mathbb{R}})}<\infty
.

Definition 2.4
.

[
8
, Definition 1.2.5]

For
1\leq p<\infty
, given a weight function
w
and a
wdx
-measurable function
f
on
{\mathbb{R}}
, we define the weak
L^{p}_{w}({\mathbb{R}})
norm of
f
by

\|f\|_{L^{p,\infty}_{w}({\mathbb{R}})}:=\sup_{\lambda>0}\lambda w(\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}:|f(x)|>\lambda\})^{\frac{1}{p}}

For
1\leq p<\infty
, the space
L^{p,\infty}_{w}({\mathbb{R}})
is defined as the set of all
wdx
-measurable functions
f
such that
\|f\|_{L^{p,\infty}_{w}({\mathbb{R}})}<\infty
.

Definition 2.5
.

Given
f
in Schwartz space
\mathscr{S}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)
we define

\widehat{f}(\xi):=\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(x)e^{-2\pi ix\xi}dx.

We call
\widehat{f}
the
Fourier transform
of
f
.

Definition 2.6
.

Given
f
in
\mathscr{S}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)
, we define

f^{\vee}(x):=\widehat{f}(-x)

for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
. The operator

f\mapsto f^{\vee}

is called the
inverse Fourier transform.

Definition 2.7
.

[
8
, Definition 5.1.1]

The
Hilbert transform
of
f\in\mathscr{S}({\mathbb{R}})
is

H(f)(x):=\frac{1}{\pi}\text{ p.v. }\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\frac{f(y)}{x-y}dy.

A well-known identity is

\widehat{H(f)}(x)=(-i\operatorname{sgn}x)\widehat{f}(x).

(2.2)

This formula can be used to give an alternative definition of the Hilbert transform.

Definition 2.8
.

[
18
, Page 4]

Let
0<p\leq 1
,
w
be a weight function on
{\mathbb{R}}
and
\varphi
be a function in
\mathscr{S}\left({\mathbb{R}}\right)
satisfying

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\varphi(x)dx\neq 0.

For any tempered distributions
f
, the
radial maximal operator

M^{+}_{\varphi}
of
f
is defined by

M_{\varphi}^{+}f(x):=\sup_{0<s<\infty}\left|\varphi_{s}*f(x)\right|,\quad x\in{\mathbb{R}},

where
\varphi_{s}(x):=\frac{1}{s}\varphi\left(\frac{x}{s}\right)
.
The
weighted Hardy space

H_{w}^{p}({\mathbb{R}})
is the space of all tempered distributions
f
satisfying

\|f\|_{H_{w}^{p}}:=\left\|M_{\varphi}^{+}f(x)\right\|_{L_{w}^{p}}<\infty.

And the
weighted weak Hardy space

H_{w}^{p,\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)
is the space of all tempered distributions
f
satisfying

\|f\|_{H_{w}^{p,\infty}}:=\|M_{\varphi}^{+}f(x)\|_{L^{p,\infty}_{w}}<\infty.

Definition 2.9
.

[
3
, Definition 1.4]

Let
0<p\leq 1
. The
weighted Hilbert Hardy space

H^{p}_{w,\rm{Hilb}}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)

\mathbb{H}^{p}_{w,{\rm Hilb}}({\mathbb{R}}):=\left\{f\in L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}\right):\|f\|_{H^{p}_{w,\rm{Hilb}}({\mathbb{R}})}<\infty\right\}

under the quasi-norm
\|\cdot\|_{H^{p}_{w,{\rm Hilb}}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)}
, where, for all

\|f\|_{H^{p}_{w,{\rm Hilb}}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)}:=\|f\|_{L^{p}_{w}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)}+\|H(f)\|_{L^{p}_{w}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)}.

Proposition 2.10
.

[
3
, Theorem 1.5]

Let
0<p\leq 1
and

, then
H^{p}_{w}({\mathbb{R}})=H^{p}_{w,{\rm Hilb}}({\mathbb{R}})
with equivalent quasi-norms.

By Proposition

is independent of the choice of the Schwartz functions
\varphi

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\varphi(x)dx\neq 0
.

Proposition 2.11
.

Let

. For any
\varphi_{1}
,
\varphi_{2}\in\mathscr{S}({\mathbb{R}})
with
\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\varphi_{i}(x)dx\neq 0
,

,
H^{p}_{w}({\mathbb{R}},\varphi_{1})=H^{p}_{w}({\mathbb{R}},\varphi_{2})
with equivalent quasi-norms.

Definition 2.12
.

[
18
, Page 5]

Let
0<p\leq 1\leq r<\infty,w\in A_{r},\gamma=\left[n\left(r_{w}/p-1\right)\right]
and
1/r+1/r^{\prime}=1
, where
r_{w}
be the critical index of

(i)

The weighted Campanato-Morrey space

is defined to be the collection of all functions satisfying

where

is the unique polynomial of degree

\int_{B\left(x_{0},R\right)}\left(f(x)-P_{B,f}(x)\right)x^{m}dx=0

for all

.

(ii)

If
f\in C^{m}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)
, replacing the polynomial

\widetilde{P}_{B,f}(x):=\sum_{k=0}^{m}\frac{d^{m}f}{dx^{m}}\left(x_{0}\right)\left(x-x_{0}\right)^{m}/(m!)

in the above (i), we denote the new space by
\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{w}^{p,r}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)

Proposition 2.13
.

[

If

3
Main results

In this section, we will discuss the boundedness of

on weighted Lebesgue spaces and power-weighted Hardy spaces.

3.1
h_{\Phi,\beta}:L^{p}_{v}({\mathbb{R}})\to L^{q}_{u}({\mathbb{R}})

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
1<p,q<\infty
,
0\leq\beta<1
,
q>\frac{1}{1-\beta}
and
\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}=\beta
. Let
u
and
v
be weight functions on
\mathbb{R}
, which are even functions and increasing on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
. Let
\Phi
be a non-negative function on
\mathbb{R}
satisfying

\int_{|t|\leq 1}\Phi(t)^{\frac{1}{1-\beta}}|t|^{\frac{1}{q(1-\beta)}-1}dt<\infty,

(3.1)

and for any
|t|\geq 1
, there exist constants
C_{1}\geq 0
and
C_{2}\geq 0
such that

\frac{C_{1}}{|t|^{1-\beta}}\leq\Phi(t)\leq\frac{C_{2}}{|t|^{1-\beta}}.

(3.2)

Then for all
f\in L^{p}_{v}(\mathbb{R})
, the inequality

\|h_{\Phi,\beta}(f)\|_{L^{q}_{u}(\mathbb{R})}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{p}_{v}(\mathbb{R})}

(3.3)

holds if and only if

A:=\sup_{\alpha>0}\left(\int_{|x|\geq\alpha}\frac{u(x)}{|x|^{q(1-\beta)}}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\left(\int_{|x|\leq\alpha}v(x)^{1-p^{\prime}}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}<\infty.

(3.4)

Besides, if
C>0
is the best constant in
(\ref{eq2.2})
, then

C_{1}A\leq C\leq 2^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}A\left\{K_{\Phi,\beta,q}2^{\beta-1}[q(1-\beta)-1]^{\frac{1}{q}}(1+2^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}})+C_{2}(p^{\prime})^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}p^{\frac{1}{q}}\right\},

where

K_{\Phi,\beta,q}:=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Phi(t)^{\frac{1}{1-\beta}}|t|^{\frac{1}{q(1-\beta)}-1}dt\right)^{1-\beta}<\infty.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
1<p,q<\infty
,
0\leq\beta<1
,

\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}=\beta

{\mathbb{R}}

Lemma 4.1
.

[

Let

, and

be a Haar measure on a locally compact group

\|f*g\|_{L^{q}(G,\mu)}\leqslant\|g\|_{L^{s}(G,\mu)}\|f\|_{L^{p}(G,\mu)}

for all

and for all

, where

and

(f*g)(x)=\int_{G}f(y)g\left(y^{-1}x\right)d\mu.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
1\leq p,q\leq\infty,0\leq\beta<1
and
\alpha,\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
satisfy
\frac{\gamma+1}{q}=\frac{\alpha+1}{p}-\beta
. If

where s satisfies
\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{s}-1
, then the operator

h_{\Phi,\beta}
is bounded from
L^{p}_{|\cdot|^{\alpha}}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)
to

, i.e., for all

\left\|h_{\Phi,\beta}f\right\|_{L^{q}_{|\cdot|^{\gamma}}({\mathbb{R}})}\leq 2^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}K_{\Phi,s,q,\gamma}\|f\|_{L^{p}_{|\cdot|^{\alpha}}({\mathbb{R}})}.

In addition, when
\alpha=\gamma=0
, that is, if

K_{\Phi,\beta,q}=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\Phi(t)|^{\frac{1}{1-\beta}}|t|^{\frac{1}{q(1-\beta)}-1}dt\right)^{1-\beta}<\infty,

(4.1)

we can obtain that
h_{\Phi,\beta}
is bounded from
L^{p}(\mathbb{R})
to
L^{q}(\mathbb{R})
, i.e., for all
f\in L^{p}({\mathbb{R}})
,

\|h_{\Phi,\beta}(f)\|_{L^{q}({\mathbb{R}})}\leq 2^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}K_{\Phi,\beta,q}\|f\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}})}.

Proof.

The proof is based on the idea used in
[
7
, Proof of Theorem 3.1]
. Split the integration regions into
\mathbb{R}_{+}
and
\mathbb{R}_{-}
. Substituting
-y=\widetilde{y}
,
-x=\widetilde{x}
for the integrals over
\mathbb{R}_{-}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\|h_{\Phi,\beta}(f)\|_{L^{q}_{|\cdot|^{\gamma}}({\mathbb{R}})}

\displaystyle=\left(\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left|\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{\Phi(|y|^{-1}x)}{|y|^{1-\beta}}f(y)dy\right|^{q}|x|^{\gamma}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{j=1,-1}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left|\sum_{i=1,-1}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\Phi(jy^{-1}x)}{y^{1-\beta}}f(iy)dy\right|^{q}x^{\gamma}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}

Using
(a+b)^{\frac{1}{q}}\leq a^{\frac{1}{q}}+b^{\frac{1}{q}}
for
a,b>0
and Minkowski inequality for
L^{q}_{|\cdot|^{\gamma}}({\mathbb{R}})
norms, we obtain

\|h_{\Phi,\beta}(f)\|_{L^{q}_{|\cdot|^{\gamma}}({\mathbb{R}})}\leq\sum_{j=1,-1}\sum_{i=1,-1}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\left|\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\Phi(jy^{-1}x)}{y^{1-\beta}}f(iy)dy\right|^{q}x^{\gamma}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}.

It is well known that the multiplicative group
{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
is a locally compact group with Haar measure
\frac{dx}{x}
. Thus, by
\frac{1+\alpha}{p}-\frac{1+\gamma}{q}=\beta
and Lemma
4.1
with
g(x)=\Phi(jx)(x)^{\frac{1+\gamma}{q}}
satisfying
\|g\|_{L^{s}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},\frac{dx}{x})}=\|\tilde{g}\|_{L^{s}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},\frac{dx}{x})}
, we obtain that

\displaystyle\|h_{\Phi,\beta}(f)\|_{L^{q}_{|\cdot|^{\gamma}}({\mathbb{R}})}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1,-1}\sum_{i=1,-1}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\left|\int_{0}^{\infty}\Phi(jy^{-1}x)(y^{-1}x)^{\frac{1+\gamma}{q}}f(iy)y^{\frac{1+\alpha}{p}}\frac{dy}{y}\right|^{q}\frac{dx}{x}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=1,-1}\sum_{i=1,-1}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}|\Phi(jt)|^{s}t^{\frac{1+\gamma}{q}s-1}dt\right)^{\frac{1}{s}}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}|f(it)|^{p}t^{\alpha}dt\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Substituting
-t=\widetilde{t}
for the above integrals involving
j=-1
and
i=-1
, then by inequality
a^{\frac{1}{p}}+b^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq 2^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}(a+b)^{\frac{1}{p}}
,
a^{\frac{1}{s}}+b^{\frac{1}{s}}\leq 2^{\frac{1}{s^{\prime}}}(a+b)^{\frac{1}{s}}
for
a,b>0
and
\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{s}-1
, we further obtain

\displaystyle\|h_{\Phi,\beta}(f)\|_{L^{q}_{|\cdot|^{\gamma}}({\mathbb{R}})}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}\left(\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|\Phi(t)|^{s}|t|^{\frac{1+\gamma}{q}s-1}dt\right)^{\frac{1}{s}}\left(\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|f(t)|^{p}|t|^{\alpha}dt\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle=2^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}K_{\Phi,s,q,\gamma}\|f\|_{L^{p}_{|\cdot|^{\alpha}}({\mathbb{R}})}.

. Also, by

(4.24)

(4.26)

Then, for any

,

Lemma 4.10
.

[
11
, Theorem 9]

Suppose
1<p<\infty
,
w
is nonnegative function and
H
is the Hilbert transform, then the following statements are equivalent:

(1)
w\in A_{p}
;

(2) There exists a constant
C
independent of
f
, such that

\|H(f)\|_{L^{p}_{w}({\mathbb{R}})}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{p}_{w}({\mathbb{R}})}.

Proof of Theorem
3.12
.

Since
m=[\frac{1+\alpha}{p}-1]+1>\frac{1+\alpha}{p}-1
, there is a
p_{1}\in(0,p)
satisfying

m=\frac{1+\alpha}{p_{1}}-1.

From this and using Theorem
3.7
, it follows that

\|Hh_{\Phi,\beta}(f)\|_{L^{q_{1},\infty}_{|\cdot|^{\gamma}}({\mathbb{R}})}\lesssim\|f\|_{H^{p_{1}}_{|\cdot|^{\alpha}}({\mathbb{R}})},

where

\frac{1+\alpha}{p_{1}}-\frac{1+\gamma}{q_{1}}=\beta.

On the other hand, since
\alpha>0
, we have
p_{0}>1+\alpha>1
. Also, by
p_{0}>\max\{\frac{1+\alpha}{1+\beta+\gamma},1+\alpha\}
,
\gamma>0
and
\frac{1+\alpha}{p_{0}}-\frac{1+\gamma}{q_{0}}=\beta
, we have
q_{0}>1
and
q_{0}>\frac{1+\gamma}{1-\beta}
. Therefore,
L^{p_{0}}_{|\cdot|^{\alpha}}({\mathbb{R}})=H^{p_{0}}_{|\cdot|^{\alpha}}({\mathbb{R}})
. Thus, by Lemma
4.10
with
|x|^{\gamma}\in A_{q_{0}}
(by
(\ref{eq2})
with
q_{0}>1+\gamma
) and Lemma
4.2
, we obtain

\|Hh_{\Phi,\beta}(f)\|_{L^{q_{0}}_{|\cdot|^{\gamma}}({\mathbb{R}})}\lesssim\|h_{\Phi,\beta}(f)\|_{L^{q_{0}}_{|\cdot|^{\gamma}}({\mathbb{R}})}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{p_{0}}_{|\cdot|^{\alpha}}({\mathbb{R}})}=\|f\|_{H^{p_{0}}_{|\cdot|^{\alpha}}({\mathbb{R}})}.

Then using the same proof of Theorem
3.8
and Lemma
4.9
with quasilinear operator
T:=H\circ h_{\Phi,\beta}
, we further obtain

\|Hh_{\Phi,\beta}(f)\|_{L^{q}_{|\cdot|^{\gamma}}({\mathbb{R}})}\lesssim\|f\|_{H^{p}_{|\cdot|^{\gamma}}({\mathbb{R}})}.

From this and Proposition
2.10
, it follows that

\|h_{\Phi,\beta}(f)\|_{H^{q}_{|\cdot|^{\gamma}}({\mathbb{R}})}\lesssim\|f\|_{H^{p}_{|\cdot|^{\gamma}}({\mathbb{R}})}.

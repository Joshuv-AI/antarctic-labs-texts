Equitable authorship in Global Gynecologic Oncology trials: the Asia Pacific Gynecologic Oncology Trials Group (APGOT) framework
Authors: Jae-Weon Kim, Alison Evans, David Tan, Jung-Bok Lee, Jung-Yun Lee, Yoland Antill, Kosei Hasegawa, on behalf of the APGOT Investigators
Published: 2026

The globalization of gynecologic oncology trials has brought authorship attribution, publication governance, and collaborative equity into sharper focus. As multinational studies span the northern and southern hemi spheres, traditional authorship models rooted in individual or institutional contribution have struggled to accommodate the scale and complexity of modern cooperative research [1,2]. These pressures have mounted alongside the rapid growth of global clinical trials and structural changes within collaborative groups [3]. In this landscape, Asia Pacific Gynecologic Oncology Trials Group (APGOT) plays a pivotal role by representing an extraordinary diversity of ethnic populations and varied healthcare systems across the Asia-Pacific region. The inclusion of APGOT centers not only ensures the global representativeness of clinical trial cohorts but also provides critical insights into how genomic and sociocultural differences influence treatment efficacy and safety, ultimately refining the interpretation of results for a broader global population.

In response, the APGOT, which comprises of a network of academic clinical trial sites and investigators focused on gynecologic cancer research in the Asia-Pacific region developed a structured authorship and publication framework, now publicly available on the APGOT website (Data S1) [4,5]. The framework was built to align scientific contribution, patient accrual, and collaborative representation with established ethical standards, including the International Committee of Medical Journal Editors recommendations [6].

A defining principle of the APGOT framework is group-level—rather than institution-level—authorship allocation. This reflects the collaborative nature of multinational trials and allows contributions to be recognized proportionally. Authorship positions are assigned primarily on the basis of patient recruitment, using defined thresholds to promote transparency and consistency. Groups receive authorship slots according to incremental recruitment benchmarks, and safeguards are built in to prevent a small number of high-enrolling groups from claiming disproportionate representation [5].

The framework also recognizes that scientific contribution extends well beyond recruitment. Fixed authorship positions are reserved for the lead principal investigator and study statistician, acknowledging their central roles in trial design, execution, and analysis. A flexible authorship pool accommodates exceptional contributions—such as leadership in translational research, protocol development, or participation from smaller or underrepresented regions [5].

Scientific integrity and independence are central to the policy. Primary endpoint analyses are conducted independently by both academic and industry statisticians, with cross-verification to uphold robustness and transparency [7]. The use of professional medical writers must be explicitly disclosed, and ghost-writing is strictly prohibited [8].

To maintain consistency across manuscript development, a Writing Committee is established for each major publication. This committee comprises the lead principal investigator, the study statistician, representatives from leading recruiting groups, and, where relevant, translational research leaders as well as specialists in patient-reported outcomes. Defined expectations around responsiveness and contribution reflect the practical demands of large-scale collaborative research [5].

In industry-sponsored global trials, the framework advocates for authorship allocation based on total global patient contribution rather than region-specific enrollment alone. This principle has grown in importance as the Asia-Pacific region plays an increasingly substantial role in global oncology research. Proportional recognition of this contribution is fundamental to maintaining long-term collaboration and equitable partnership [9].

Beyond primary publications, the policy governs secondary analyses, conference presentations, and press communications. Secondary projects require prior approval from the Trial Steering Committee or APGOT Executive Committee, and embargo policies are applied to protect the integrity of primary publications. A defined dispute resolution process places final authority with the APGOT Executive Committee to maintain accountability [5].

The APGOT authorship and publication framework offers a structured, transparent, and practical approach to managing authorship in multinational clinical trials. As the geographic reach of oncology research broadens, such frameworks become indispensable—not only to match participation with recognition, but to uphold the scientific credibility on which cross-regional collaboration depends [10].

Global trials require not only global participation, but equitable recognition of those who make it possible.

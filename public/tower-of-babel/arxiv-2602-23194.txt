Moduli of sheaves on hyperkähler manifolds
Kieran G. O’Grady

Abstract
. We survey recent advances in the theory of moduli spaces of stable sheaves on hyperkähler manifolds of dimension greater than 2 2 . We start by recalling the well-known theory in dimension 2 2 , i.e. for K ​ 3 K3 surfaces, emphasizing the techniques which can be extended to higher dimensions.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Background and goal of the paper

Algebraic vector bundles appeared quite lately in the history of Algebraic Geometry, see
[
We49
]
. In retrospect algebraic line bundles had been present since Abel’s proof
[
Ab26
]
of one half of the Abel-Jacobi Theorem, which may be interpreted as a forerunner of the theory of moduli spaces of semistable sheaves on a polarized projective variety
(X,h)
developed by Mumford, Narasimhan, Seshadri, Gieseker, Maruyama, Simpson (and others). The latter theory associates to
(X,h)
a plethora of moduli spaces of semistable sheaves on
(X,h)
, i.e. projective schemes parametrizing isomorphism classes of stable sheaves on
X
(and also
S
-equivalence classes of semistable sheaves) with fixed numerical invariants.
Associating to
(X,h)
these moduli spaces is analogous to associating to a group
G
the category
\Rep(G)
of its representations.
There are (at least) two reasons to be interested in these moduli spaces. The first is that they give precious geometric information regarding
X
.
The second is that moduli spaces often are very interesting varieties in their own right.

What is the status of our knowledge of these moduli spaces? Suppose for simplicity that
X
is smooth. If
X
is curve then the moduli spaces are as nice as possible, meaning that they are non empty, irreducible of the expected dimension if the genus
g
of
X
is at least
2
(if
g\leq 1
they are either empty or have very explicit descriptions), and many intriguing results are known about them.
If
X
is a surface the moduli spaces may present various pathological phenomena, but
general results are available. These state that if the expected dimension of the moduli space is large enough (equivalently: the sheaves in question are very twisted) then it is non-empty, irreducible, of the expected dimension, and generically smooth. For surfaces of non maximal Kodaira dimension more detailed information is available, notably for
K3
or abelian surfaces. There are no general results valid for
X
of dimension greater than
2
.

A Hyperkähler (HK) manifold is a simply connnected compact Kähler manifold
X
carrying a holomorphic symplectic form whose class spans

H^{0}(X,\Omega^{2}_{X})
.
Two dimensional HK manifold
is sinonymous of
K3
surface
. Higher dimensional HK manifolds behave very much like surfaces, and in some respects are “surface-like”.

In the last few years there have been several developments in the theory of semistable sheaves on higher dimensional HK manifolds, more precisely of “modular sheaves”  to be soon introduced. The emerging theory promises to be as intriguing and beautiful as that of sheaves on
K3
surfaces. The goal of the present paper is to give an introduction to some of the methods and results of this theory.

1.2.
Outline of the paper

In Section
2
we recap some of the main results of the theory of moduli of sheaves on
K3
surfaces.
The results are the model for what one would like to achieve in higher dimensions. In addition, some of the methods that are employed in dimension
2
can be extended to higher dimensions.
A third reason for going through the theory for
K3
surfaces is that interesting stable vector bundles on projective HK manifolds of Type
K3^{[n]}
(i.e. deformations of the Hilbert scheme
S^{[n]}
parametrizing lenght-
n
subschemes of a
K3
surface
S
) are produced from vector bundles on
K3
surfaces via the Bridgeland-King-Reid (BKR) equivalence.

In Section
3
we introduce and discuss the three main classes of sheaves on HK manifolds for which significant results have been proved, namely modular torsion-free sheaves (sheaves for which a certain codimension-
4
characteristic class satisfies a purely topological condition), projectively hyperholomorphic vector bundles, and atomic sheaves. Roughly speaking these classes are strictly decreasing, i.e. an atomic stable vector bundle is projectively hyperholomorphic, and the latter is modular.
Variation of slope (semi)stability
of modular sheaves behaves as if they were sheaves on surfaces, and moreover stable modular sheaves on HK manifolds equipped with a Lagrangian fibration behave very much like stable sheaves on elliptic
K3
surfaces. This motivates the belief that a theory resembling that valid for
K3
surfaces can be developed in higher dimensions.
In Section
3
we give “synthetic” examples of modular sheaves on
S^{[n]}
, where

S
is a
K3
surface, via the BKR equivalence.

In Section
4
we state the main results that have been proved about moduli of stable projectively hyperholomorphic vector bundles on HK manifolds. Here we should mention that if a slope-stable vector bundle
\mathscr{F}
on a HK manifold
X
is projectively hyperholomorphic, then it extends to every deformation of
X
keeping
c_{1}(\mathscr{F})
of type
(1,1)
, and the projectivization
{\mathbb{P}}(\mathscr{F})
extends to all deformations of
X
. Every slope-stable vector bundle on a
K3
surface is projectively hyperholomorphic, while in higher dimensions
a randomly chosen slope-stable vector bundle is not projectively hyperholomorphic. The results that we present are all about projectively hyperholomorphic vector bundles.
The examples of Section
3
are chosen carefully, so that when they are slope stable they are projectively hyperholomorphic.
We present in some detail our results on existence and uniqueness of slope-stable stable rigid vector bundles on general suitably polarized
HK manifolds of Type
K3^{[n]}
. We quickly go over Bottini’s work which produces HK manifolds of Type OG10 (one of the two “sporadic” deformation classes) as (an irreducible component of) a moduli space of stable (twisted) vector bundles on HK manifolds of Type
K3^{[2]}
. We mention Markman’s work on (twisted) hyperholomorphic vector bundles on couples of HK manifolds of Type
K3^{[n]}
: it has produced highly non-trivial results, e.g. the proof (by Markman) of the Shafarevich conjecture for couples
of HK manifolds (both of) of Type
K3^{[n]}
, and the proof by Maulik, Shen, Yin and Zhang of the
D
-equivalence conjecture for HK manifolds of Type
K3^{[n]}
. Lastly we go over work in progress of ours whose goal is to show that one gets an arbitrary HK manifold of Type
K3^{[a^{2}+1]}
as the normalization of a (component of) of a suitable moduli space of (twisted) hyperholomorphic vector bundles on a HK manifold of Type
K3^{[2]}
(and also
to give the analogue of Markman’s result above for couples

(X,Y)
of HK manifolds of Type
K3^{[a^{2}+1]}
and
K3^{[b^{2}+1]}
).

2.1.
Deformations of sheaves

Let
X
be an irreducible smooth projective variety. A
family of sheaves on

parametrized by a scheme

consists of a
T
-flat sheaf
{\mathsf{F}}
on
X\times T
. If

{\mathsf{F}}_{t}\coloneqq{\mathsf{F}}_{|X\times\{t\}}
. We think of
{\mathsf{F}}
as a “continuous” family of sheaves
{\mathsf{F}}_{t}
(with “non reduced” deformations if
T
is non reduced).
Let
\mathscr{F}
be a sheaf on
X
. Let
T
be a pointed scheme, i.e. a scheme with a chosen
0\in T
. A
deformation
of

\mathscr{F}
parametrized by
T
is a family of sheaves on
X
parametrized by
T
together with an isomorphism

{\mathsf{F}}_{0}\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathscr{F}
.
Now suppose that
\mathscr{F}
is simple, i.e. that
\Hom_{X}(\mathscr{F},\mathscr{F})={\mathbb{C}}\Id_{\mathscr{F}}
. Then there exist a scheme
B
and a deformation of
\mathscr{F}
parametrized by
B
with the following properties (see
[
Ar89
,
Mu84
]
and
[
HL10
, 2.A.5]
):

(1)

Given a deformation
{\mathsf{G}}
of
\mathscr{F}
parametrized by
T

there exist a morphism of germs

f\colon(T,0)\rightarrow(B,0)
and an isomorphism

{\varphi}\colon f^{*}{\mathsf{F}}\xrightarrow{\sim}{\mathsf{G}}_{|X\times(B,0)}
(here
f^{*}{\mathsf{F}}
is shorthand for
(\Id_{X}\times f)^{*}{\mathsf{F}}
).

(2)

Given
{\mathsf{G}}
as above, there is a unique morphism
f
with the property stated above.

The properties above determine
the algebraic germ

(B,0)
and the deformation
{\mathsf{G}}_{|X\times(B,0)}
of
\mathscr{F}
up to isomorphism: this is the
universal deformation space
of
\mathscr{F}
. We denote

(B,0)
by
\Def(\mathscr{F})
.
One
describes

(B,0)
as follows. Let

(2.1.1)

\Ext^{p}_{X}(\mathscr{F},\mathscr{F})\xrightarrow{\Tr^{p}}H^{p}(X,\mathscr{O}_{X})

be the
trace map
(if
\mathscr{F}
is locally-free
\Tr^{p}
is the map
H^{p}(X,End\mathscr{F})\rightarrow H^{p}(X,\mathscr{O}_{X})
induced in cohomology by the trace morphism

Tr\colon End\mathscr{F}\rightarrow\mathscr{O}_{X}
), and let
\Ext^{p}_{X}(\mathscr{F},\mathscr{F})_{0}
be the kernel of
\Tr^{p}
. There is an analytic
obstruction map

(2.1.2)

(\Ext^{1}_{X}(\mathscr{F},\mathscr{F}),0)\xrightarrow{\obs_{\mathscr{F}}}(\Ext^{2}_{X}(\mathscr{F},\mathscr{F})_{0},0)

with vanishing differential such that the
analytic germ

(B,0)_{\an}
is given by

(2.1.3)

(B,0)_{\an}\cong(\obs_{\mathscr{F}}^{-1}(0),0).

Since
\obs_{\mathscr{F}}
has vanishing differential, it follows that the Zariski tangent space to
B
at
0
is identified with
\Ext^{1}_{X}(\mathscr{F},\mathscr{F})
. We also get that if
\Ext^{2}_{X}(\mathscr{F},\mathscr{F})_{0}
vanishes then
\mathscr{F}
is unobstructed, i.e. the deformation space is smooth of dimension equal to
the dimension of
\Ext^{1}_{X}(\mathscr{F},\mathscr{F})
.

Theorem 2.1.1

(Mukai-Artamkin)
.

Let
S
be a projective
K3
surface, and let

\mathscr{F}
be a simple torsion-free sheaf on
S
. Then the deformation space
\Def(\mathscr{F})
is smooth, and its dimension is given by

(2.1.4)

\dim\Def(\mathscr{F})=2-\chi_{S}(\mathscr{F},\mathscr{F}).

Proof.

Since the canonical bundle of
S
is trivial, Serre duality gives the isomorphism
\Ext^{2}_{S}(\mathscr{F},\mathscr{F})\cong\Hom_{S}(\mathscr{F},\mathscr{F})^{\vee}
and the latter is
one-dimensional because
\mathscr{F}
is simple. Thus

(2.1.5)

\dim\Ext^{2}_{S}(\mathscr{F},\mathscr{F})=\dim\Hom_{S}(\mathscr{F},\mathscr{F})=1.

The map
H^{0}(S,K_{S})=H^{0}(S,\mathscr{O}_{S})\rightarrow\Hom_{S}(\mathscr{F},\mathscr{F})
defined by
f\mapstochar\rightarrow f\Id_{\mathscr{F}}
is identified by Serre duality with the transpose
of
\Tr^{2}
, and it is an isomorphism because
\mathscr{F}
is simple and torsion-free. It follows that

(2.1.6)

\Ext^{2}_{S}(\mathscr{F},\mathscr{F})_{0}=0.

Hence

\Def(\mathscr{F})
is smooth of dimension equal to
\dim\Ext^{1}_{S}(\mathscr{F},\mathscr{F})
. By (
2.1.5
) the latter is equal to the right hand side of (
2.1.4
).
∎

Let
\mathscr{F}
be a sheaf on an irreducible smooth projective variety

. We need to consider also deformations of the couple
(X,\mathscr{F})
in the analytic category. Such a deformation consists of a deformation of

, i.e. a proper flat map

is a pointed analytic space with base point

on

with an isomorphism

. We wish to compare such deformations with deformations of

(see for example
[

]
). We recall that

is an analytic germ with Zariski tangent space

, and that if

is Kähler with trivial canonical bundle then it is smooth by the Bogomolov-Tian-Todorov Theorem. Suppose also that
\mathscr{F}
is simple. Then there is a universal deformation space

for deformations of the couple
(X,\mathscr{F})
(see
[

]
), equipped with the forgetful map

(2.1.7)

\Def(X,\mathscr{F})\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow\Def(X,\det\mathscr{F}).

Theorem 2.1.2

(
[
Hua95
,
IM19
]
)
.

Let

be a couple consisting either of a smooth projective variety

.

Let

The trace map

is surjective because

The content of the above result is that we can extend the sheaf
\mathscr{F}
to all (small) deformations of

that keep the first Chern class of

of type

(1,1)
. Note that
\Def(S,\det\mathscr{F})=\Def(S)
only if

is trivial. In all other cases

has codimension

in

, i.e. there is “one deformation direction of

does not extend. If

is locally-free then the projectivization

extends to all (small) deformations of

. In order to put this result in context we recall a general result. Let
X
be a connected complex manifold, and let
f\colon Y\rightarrow X
be a fibration in
m
-dimensional projective spaces, i.e. locally

is given by the projection
U\times{\mathbb{P}}^{m}\rightarrow U
, where “locally” means in the classical topology. We have the forgetful
map (see
[
Man22
, Expl. 8.2.14]
)

(2.1.8)

\Def(Y)\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow\Def(X).

(Every deformation of

is a deformation of the fibration

.)
If

is a complex manifold and

is a vector bundle on

we let

be the sub vector bundle of traceless endomorphisms.
Note that

is isomorphic to

.

Theorem 2.1.4

(Horikawa)
.

Let

be a connected complex manifold with no nonzero global (holomorphic) tangent field. Let
\mathscr{F}
be a vector bundle on
X

such that
H^{2}(X,End^{0}(\mathscr{F}))=0
. Then the forgetful map
\Def({\mathbb{P}}(\mathscr{F}))\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow\Def(X)
is

Proof.

Let
Y\coloneqq{\mathbb{P}}(\mathscr{F})
, and let
f\colon Y\rightarrow X
be the structure map. We have the exact sequence of locally free sheaves on

(2.1.9)

0\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow\Theta_{Y/X}\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow\Theta_{Y}\overset{df}{\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow}f^{*}\Theta_{X}\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow 0.

By
[
Ho74
, Theorem 6.1]
(see also
[

, Cor. 8.2.14]

2.2.
Stability and semistability

2.2.1.
Gieseker-Maruyama (semi)stability, and moduli spaces

Let
(X,L)
be a polarized irreducible smooth projective variety, i.e.
X
is an irreducible smooth projective variety and
L
is an ample line bundle on
X
. We always assume that
c_{1}(L)
is primitive, i.e.
c_{1}(L)=d\alpha
with
\alpha\in H^{2}(X,{\mathbb{Z}})
implies that
d=\pm 1
. Let
\mathscr{F}
be a non zero torsion-free sheaf on
X
: the
reduced Hilbert polynomial

p_{\mathscr{F}}\in{\mathbb{Q}}[t]
is defined by setting

(2.2.1)

p_{\mathscr{F}}(n)\coloneqq\frac{\chi(X,\mathscr{F}\otimes L^{\otimes n})}{r(\mathscr{F})},

where
r(\mathscr{F})
is the rank of
\mathscr{F}
(which is non zero because
\mathscr{F}
is non zero and torsion-free).

Definition 2.2.1
.

A non zero torsion-free sheaf
\mathscr{F}
on
X
is
L
Gieseker-Maruyama semistable
if for every non zero subsheaf

\mathscr{E}\subset\mathscr{F}

(2.2.2)

p_{\mathscr{E}}(n)\leq p_{\mathscr{F}}(n)\qquad\forall n\gg 0.

It is
L
Gieseker-Maruyama stable
if, in addition, strict inequality holds whenever
\mathscr{E}\not=\mathscr{F}
. It is
L
Gieseker-Maruyama unstable
if it is not

L
Gieseker-Maruyama semistable.

We write
L
GM (semi)stable instead of
L
Gieseker-Maruyama semistable, and if
L
is clear from the context we avoid mentioning it. The importance of GM semistability is attested by the following celebrated result.

Theorem 2.2.2

(Gieseker
[
G77
]
- Maruyama
[
Maru78
]
)
.

Let
(X,L)
be a polarized irreducible smooth projective variety, and let

(2.2.3)

v=(v_{0},v_{1},\ldots)\in\bigoplus_{p}H^{p,p}_{{\mathbb{Q}}}(X)

with
v_{0}>0
.
There is a projective
coarse moduli space
scheme
M_{v}(X,L)
for torsion-free sheaves
\mathscr{F}
on
X
such that

\ch(\mathscr{F})=v
. This means the following:

(1)

To every scheme
T
and a family
{\mathsf{F}}
of sheaves on
X
parametrized by
T

such that for
t\in T
the sheaf
{\mathsf{F}}_{t}\coloneqq{\mathsf{F}}_{|X\times\{t\}}
is semistable with
\ch({\mathsf{F}}_{t})=v
(we say that
{\mathsf{F}}
is a
family of semistable sheaves on
X
with Chern character
v
parametrized by
T
) there is associated a classifying morphism
m_{{\mathsf{F}}}\colon T\rightarrow M_{v}(X,L)
in such a way that the following hold:

(a)

If
{\mathsf{F}}^{\prime}
is obtained from
{\mathsf{F}}
via a base change
g\colon T^{\prime}\rightarrow T
, i.e.
{\mathsf{F}}^{\prime}\cong g^{*}({\mathsf{F}})
, then
m_{{\mathsf{F}}^{\prime}}=m_{{\mathsf{F}}}\circ g
.

(b)

If
\mathscr{L}
is a line bundle on
T
then
m_{{\mathsf{F}}\boxtimes\mathscr{L}}=m_{{\mathsf{F}}}
.

(c)

M_{v}(X,L)
is maximal with the above properties, i.e. if
\mathscr{M}
is a scheme with a contravariant functor
F
from the category of families… as above, then
F
factors via a morphism
M_{v}(X,L)\rightarrow\mathscr{M}
.

(2)

Restricting the classifying morphism to stable sheaves
\mathscr{F}
on
X=X\times\Spec{\mathbb{C}}
with
\ch({\mathsf{F}}_{t})=v
one gets a bijection between the set of isomorphism classes of such sheaves and the set of closed points of an open subset
M_{v}(X,L)^{\rm st}_{\rm red}
of the (reduced)
M_{v}(X,L)_{\rm red}
.

(3)

Restricting the classifying morphism to semistable non stable sheaves
\mathscr{F}
on
X=X\times\Spec{\mathbb{C}}
with

\ch({\mathsf{F}}_{t})=v
one gets a bijection between the set of
S
-equivalence (a relation
weaker than isomorphism) classes
(see
[
HL10
, Sect. 1.5]
) of such sheaves and the set of closed points of

M_{v}(X,L)_{\rm red}\setminus M_{v}(X,L)^{\rm st}_{\rm red}
.

Definition 2.2.3
.

If
\mathscr{F}
is an
L
semistable torsion-free sheaf on
X
with
\ch(\mathscr{F})=v

we let
[\mathscr{F}]\in M_{v}(X,L)
be the point corresponding to
\mathscr{F}
according to Items (2) and (3) of Theorem
2.2.2
.

Example 2.2.4
.

Let
\alpha\in H^{1,1}_{{\mathbb{Z}}}(X)
, and let
v(\alpha)=e^{\alpha}
. Then
M_{v(\alpha)}(X,L)=\Pic^{\alpha}(X)
is independent of
L
, and it is isomorphic to the Jacobian
H^{0,1}(X)/H^{1}(X;{\mathbb{Z}})
. This is the prototoypical example of a moduli space. There exists a universal (Poincaré) line bundle
\mathsf{L}
on

X\times M_{v(\alpha)}(X,L)
, i.e. such that the associated morphism
m_{\mathsf{L}}\colon M_{v(\alpha)}(X,L)\rightarrow M_{v(\alpha)}(X,L)
is the identity. In general there is no universal sheaf on
X\times M_{v}(X,L)
(even if all the sheaves parametrized by
M_{v}(X,L)
happen to be stable).

.

via the base change
T^{\prime}\rightarrow T
. Maruyama proved that if we limit ourselves to locally-free semistable sheaves,
i.e. locally-free sheaves on
X\times T
, then in order
to obtain boundedness it suffices to fix the first three terms of
v
, i.e. rank,
\ch_{1}
and
\ch_{2}
,
see
[
Maru81
]
.

2.2.2.
Slope (semi)stability

Let
(X,L)
be a polarized irreducible smooth projective variety. Let
d\coloneqq\dim X
. Applying the Hirzebruch-Riemann-Roch Theorem, one gets the equality

(2.2.4)

p_{\mathscr{F}}(t)\equiv\left(\int_{X}\frac{c_{1}(L)^{d}}{d!}\right)t^{d}+\left(\int_{X}\left(\frac{c_{1}(\mathscr{F})}{r(\mathscr{F})}+c_{1}(X)\right)\frac{c_{1}(L)^{d-1}}{(d-1)!}\right)t^{d-1}\pmod{t^{d-2}},

where
r(\mathscr{F})
is the rank of
\mathscr{F}
. Hence the leading coefficient of the reduced Hilbert polynomial
p_{\mathscr{F}}
is independent of
\mathscr{F}
, while the next coefficient only depends on the
L
slope of

\mathscr{F}
, dfined to be

(2.2.5)

\mu_{L}(\mathscr{F})\coloneqq\int_{X}\frac{c_{1}(\mathscr{F})\cdot c_{1}(L)^{d-1}}{r(\mathscr{F})}.

We denote
\mu_{L}(\mathscr{F})
also by
\mu_{c_{1}(L)}(\mathscr{F})
.
This leads to the following definition.

Definition 2.2.6
.

A non zero torsion-free sheaf
\mathscr{F}
on
X
is
L
slope semistable
if for every non zero subsheaf

\mathscr{E}\subset\mathscr{F}

we have
\mu_{L}(\mathscr{E})\leq\mu_{L}(\mathscr{F})
.
It is
L
slope stable
if, in addition, strict inequality holds whenever
r(\mathscr{E})<r(\mathscr{F})
. It is
L
slope unstable
if it is not

L
slope semistable.

Let
\mathscr{F}
be a non zero torsion-free sheaf on
X
. We have the following chain of implications

\text{$\mathscr{F}$ slope stable }\implies\text{$\mathscr{F}$ GM stable }\implies\text{$\mathscr{F}$ GM semistable }\implies\text{$\mathscr{F}$ slope semistable,}

where (semi)stability is with respect to a fixed polarization
L
.

Remark 2.2.7
.

Let
(X,L)
be a polarized projective curve (irreducible and smooth). Then
L
slope (semi)stability is the same as
L
GM (semi)stability because (
2.2.4
) is an equality, and moreover it does not depend on
L
. For this reason when dealing with a curve we talk of (semi)stability tout court. Note that if
\mathscr{F}
is a vector bundle on
X
, and the numbers
r(\mathscr{F})
,
\deg(\mathscr{F})\coloneqq c_{1}(\mathscr{F})
are coprime, then
\mathscr{F}
is either stable or unstable.

Example 2.2.8
.

Let
X
be a smooth (irreducible) projctive curve. If
\mathscr{F}
is a vector bundle on
X
fitting into a non split exact sequence

(2.2.6)

0\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow\mathscr{O}_{X}\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow\mathscr{F}\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow\mathscr{E}\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow 0,

where
\mathscr{E}
is a stable vector bundle with
\deg(\mathscr{E})=1
, then
\mathscr{F}
is stable (easy exercise). If the genus
g(X)
is at least
1
then such non split exact sequences exist with
\mathscr{E}
of arbitrary positive rank (induction on the rank).
If
g(X)=0
then there are no stable vector bundles of rank greater than
1
.

Remark 2.2.9
.

If
T
is a scheme and
{\mathsf{F}}
is family of sheaves on
X
parametrized by
T
then the subsets
T^{\sss},T^{\st}\subset T
of

t\in T
such that the sheaf
{\mathsf{F}}_{t}
is semistable (respectively stable) is open,
see
[
HL10
, Prop. 2.3.1]
.

The result below (for a proof see
[
HL10
, Cor.1.2.8]
) gives a link between stability and simplicity of a sheaf .

Proposition 2.2.10
.

Let
(X,L)
be a polarized irreducible smooth projective variety. If
\mathscr{F}
is an
L
stable sheaf on
X
then
\mathscr{F}
is simple.

The result below makes the connection between the deformation space of a stable sheaf

\mathscr{F}
and the local structure at
[\mathscr{F}]
of the relevant moduli space. (For a proof
see
[
HL10
, Thm.4.5.1]
.)

Proposition 2.2.11
.

Let
(X,L)
be a polarized irreducible smooth projective variety. Let
\mathscr{F}
be an
L
stable sheaf on
X
, and let
v=\ch(\mathscr{F})
. Then the germ of
M_{v}(X,L)
at the point
[\mathscr{F}]
representing the isomorphism class of
\mathscr{F}
is isomorphic to the universal deformation space of
\mathscr{F}
.

2.3.
Variation of slope (semi)stability

2.3.1.
Motivation and overview

Let
X
be an irreducible smooth projective variety. (Semi)stability of a (torsion-free) sheaf with respect to an ample line bundle
L
depends only on the ray
{\mathbb{Q}}_{+}c_{1}(L)\subset\Amp(X)
. If
X
is a curve there is no dependency (see Remark
2.2.7
). Starting with
\dim X=2
slope stability does vary when we vary the ray in the ample cone.

Example 2.3.1
.

Let
X
be a(n irreducible smooth projective) surface. Let
\mathscr{F}
be a rank
2
vector bundle fitting into a non split exact sequence

(2.3.1)

0\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow\mathscr{O}_{X}\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow\mathscr{F}\overset{g}{\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow}\mathscr{I}_{Z}\otimes\mathscr{L}\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow 0,

where
Z\subset X
is a zero-dimensional subscheme and
\mathscr{L}
is a line bundle. Suppose that
h_{1},h_{2}
be ample classes on
X
such that

(2.3.2)

c_{1}(\mathscr{L})\cdot h_{1}=1,\qquad c_{1}(\mathscr{L})\cdot h_{2}<0.

Then
\mathscr{F}
is
h_{1}
slope stable and
h_{2}
slope unstable. The latter statement holds because by the inequality in (
2.3.2
) we have

\mu_{h_{2}}(\mathscr{O}_{X})=0>\mu_{h_{2}}(\mathscr{F})
. Let us prove that
\mathscr{F}
is
h_{1}
slope stable. Suppose the contrary, i.e. there exists a subsheaf
\mathscr{E}\subset\mathscr{F}
with
r(\mathscr{E})=1
and
\mu_{h_{1}}(\mathscr{E})\geq\mu_{h_{1}}(\mathscr{F})
. Since
\mathscr{F}
is locally-free the inclusion
\mathscr{E}\subset\mathscr{F}
factors through an inclusion

\mathscr{E}^{\vee\vee}\subset\mathscr{F}
. Since
\mu_{h_{1}}(\mathscr{E})=\mu_{h_{1}}(\mathscr{E}^{\vee\vee})
and
\mathscr{E}^{\vee\vee}
is a line bundle we may assume
from the start that
\mathscr{E}
is a line bundle. Since
\mu_{h_{1}}(\mathscr{E})\geq\mu_{h_{1}}(\mathscr{F})=1/2
the sheaf
\mathscr{E}
is not a subsheaf of

\mathscr{O}_{X}
It follows that
the sheaf

g(\mathscr{E})\subset\mathscr{I}_{Z}\otimes\mathscr{L}
has rank
1
. Since we have a non zero map
\mathscr{E}\rightarrow\mathscr{L}
between line bundles,
c_{1}(\mathscr{E})=c_{1}(\mathscr{L})-\cl(D)
where
D
is an effective divisor. The inequality
\mu_{h_{1}}(\mathscr{E})\geq\mu_{h_{1}}(\mathscr{F})
gives

D\cdot h_{1}\leq 0
, and hence
D=0
. Thus
\mathscr{E}\cong\mathscr{L}
(it follows that

Z={\emptyset}
) and hence the exact sequence in (
2.3.1
) is
split. This contradicts the hypothesis.

An explicit instance of the above construction is the following. Let
(E_{1},p_{1})
,
(E_{2},p_{2})
be elliptic curves. Let
X\coloneqq E_{1}\times E_{2}
, and let
C_{1}\coloneqq E_{1}\times\{p_{2}\}
,

C_{2}\coloneqq\{p_{1}\}\times E_{2}
. If
Z\subset X
is a l.c.i. subscheme (e.g.
Z={\emptyset}
)
there exists a vector bundle
\mathscr{F}
fitting into a non split exact sequence

(2.3.3)

0\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow\mathscr{O}_{X}\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow\mathscr{F}\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow\mathscr{I}_{Z}\otimes\mathscr{O}_{X}(C_{1}-C_{2})\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow 0.

The polarizations
h_{1}\coloneqq\mathscr{O}_{S}(C_{1}+2C_{2})
and
h_{2}\coloneqq\mathscr{O}_{S}(2C_{1}+C_{2})
satisfy the hypotheses above. Hence
\mathscr{F}
is
h_{1}
slope stable and
h_{2}
slope unstable.

If
X
is a surface the variation of (semi)stability is very well behaved, see
[
HL10
]
and references therein. If
\dim X>2
variation of stability is, in general, substantially more complex, see
[
GRT16
]
.
In the present subsection we discuss variation of slope (semi)stability for
an irreducible smooth projective surface, and
X
denotes such a variety unless we state the contrary. The argument is technical and dry, but it is a key element in many proofs on moduli of sheaves.

Remark 2.3.2
.

Bridgeland introduced stability conditions for objects in the derived category of a smooth projective variety. In a (precise) sense this is a generalization of Gieseker-Maruyama stability for sheaves.
Such stability conditions, when defined and controllable, have been extremely fruitful, see
[
BM23
]
for a survey. Variation of Bridgeland stability, a high octane version of variation of slope stability for sheaves on surfaces, plays a key rôle in the theory.

2.3.2.
Slope with respect to nef classes

It is convenient to introduce slope (semi)stability of a sheaf
\mathscr{F}
on a surface
X
with respect to a nef class
h\in\NS(X)_{{\mathbb{Q}}}
. The
h
slope of

\mathscr{F}
is given by

(2.3.4)

\mu_{h}(\mathscr{F})\coloneqq\int_{X}\frac{c_{1}(\mathscr{F})\cdot h}{r(\mathscr{F})}.

Definition 2.3.3
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective surface, and let
h\in\NS(X)_{{\mathbb{Q}}}
be a nef class. A non zero torsion-free sheaf
\mathscr{F}
on
X
is
h
slope semistable
if for every non zero subsheaf

\mathscr{E}\subset\mathscr{F}

we have
\mu_{h}(\mathscr{E})\leq\mu_{h}(\mathscr{F})
.
It is
h
slope stable
if, in addition, strict inequality holds whenever
r(\mathscr{E})<r(\mathscr{F})
. It is
h
slope unstable
if it is not

h
slope semistable.

Of course if
h
is ample this is the same notion of slope (semi)stability of Definition
2.2.6
.

Remark 2.3.4
.

Suppose that
\pi\colon X\rightarrow Y
is a surjective non constant map to a smooth projective variety, and that
\pi_{*}\mathscr{O}_{X}=\mathscr{O}_{Y}
. Let
h_{Y}
be an ample class on
Y
, and let

h_{X}=\pi^{*}h_{Y}
. There are two possible cases:

(a)

\dim Y=2
, and hence
\pi
is a composition of blow-downs.

(b)

\dim Y=1
, and hence a general fiber of
\pi
is an irreducible smooth curve.

If (a) holds then a sheaf
\mathscr{F}
is
h_{X}
slope (semi)stable if and only if the push-forward
\pi_{*}\mathscr{F}
is
h_{Y}
slope (semi)stable. This holds because of the following facts:
\mu_{h_{X}}(\mathscr{F})=\mu_{h_{Y}}(\pi_{*}\mathscr{F})=\mu_{h_{X}}(\pi^{*}\pi_{*}\mathscr{F})
,
if
\mathscr{E}\subset\mathscr{F}
then
\pi_{*}\mathscr{E}\subset\pi_{*}\mathscr{F}
, if
\mathscr{G}\subset\pi_{*}\mathscr{F}
then

\pi^{*}\mathscr{G}\subset\pi^{*}\pi_{*}\mathscr{F}\subset\mathscr{F}
and
\mu_{h_{Y}}(\mathscr{G})=\mu_{h_{X}}(\pi^{*}\mathscr{G})
.
If (b) holds, we may assume that
h_{Y}
is the class of a point, and hence
h_{X}
is the class of a fiber of
\pi
.
Let
\mathscr{E}
be a (non zero) torsion-free sheaf on
X
. If
C
is a general fiber of
\pi
then the restriction
\mathscr{E}_{|C}
is a vector bundle, and

(2.3.5)

\mu_{h_{X}}(\mathscr{E})=\int_{X}\frac{c_{1}(\mathscr{E})\cdot C}{r(\mathscr{E})}=\int_{C}\frac{\deg(\mathscr{E}_{|C})}{r(\mathscr{E})}=\mu(\mathscr{E}_{|C}).

It follows that if
\mathscr{F}
is a torsion-free sheaf on
X
whose restriction to a general fiber of
\pi
is a slope (semi)stable, then
\mathscr{F}
is
h_{X}
slope (semi)stable.

2.3.3.
Suitability

The
discriminant
of a torsion-free sheaf
\mathscr{F}
on a connected complex manifold
X
(of any dimension) is given by

(2.3.6)

\Delta(\mathscr{F})\coloneqq 2r(\mathscr{F})c_{2}(\mathscr{F})-(r(\mathscr{F})-1)c_{1}(\mathscr{F})^{2}=\ch_{1}(\mathscr{F})^{2}-2r(\mathscr{F})\ch_{2}(\mathscr{F}).

Note that if
\mathscr{L}
is a line bundle on
X
then
\Delta(\mathscr{F}\otimes\mathscr{L})=\Delta(\mathscr{F})
. The celebrated Bogomolov inequality for the discriminant underpins the results in the present subsection.

Theorem 2.3.5

(Bogomolov’s inequality.
[
Bog79
,
Bog95
,
HL10
]
)
.

Let
(X,h)
be an irreducible smooth polarized projective variety of dimension
d\geq 2
. If

\mathscr{F}
is a torsion-free
h
slope semistable sheaf on
X
then

(2.3.7)

\int_{X}\Delta(\mathscr{F})\cdot h^{d-2}\geq 0.

If
d=2
then (
2.3.7
) holds also if
h
is big and nef.

Suppose that
X
is a surface. The orientation class of
X
defines an isomorphism
H^{4}(X;{\mathbb{Z}})\xrightarrow{\sim}{\mathbb{Z}}
, and hence
it identifies
\Delta(\mathscr{F})
with an integer.
We let

(2.3.8)

{\mathsf{a}}(\mathscr{F})\coloneqq r(\mathscr{F})^{2}\cdot\Delta(\mathscr{F})/4.

Proposition 2.3.6
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective surface, and let
h\in\NS(X)
be big and nef. Let
\mathscr{F}
be an
h
slope semistable torsion-free sheaf on
X
. Suppose that
\mathscr{E}\subset\mathscr{F}
is a subsheaf such that
0<r(\mathscr{E})<r(\mathscr{F})
and
\mu_{h}(\mathscr{E})=\mu_{h}(\mathscr{F})
. Let

(2.3.9)

\lambda_{\mathscr{E},\mathscr{F}}\coloneqq r(\mathscr{F})\cdot c_{1}(\mathscr{E})-r(\mathscr{E})\cdot c_{1}(\mathscr{F}).

Then

(2.3.10)

-{\mathsf{a}}(\mathscr{F})\leq\lambda_{\mathscr{E},\mathscr{F}}\cdot\lambda_{\mathscr{E},\mathscr{F}}\leq 0,

and the right inequality is an equality only if
\lambda_{\mathscr{E},\mathscr{F}}=0
.

Proof.

The equality
\mu_{h}(\mathscr{E})=\mu_{h}(\mathscr{F})
is equivalent to
\lambda_{\mathscr{E},\mathscr{F}}\cdot h=0
. Hence the Hodge index Theorem gives that
the right inequality in (
2.3.10
) holds, with equality only if
\lambda_{\mathscr{E},\mathscr{F}}=0
. Since
\mu_{h}(\mathscr{E})=\mu_{h}(\mathscr{F})
and
\mathscr{F}
is
h
slope semistable, so are
\mathscr{E}
and the quotient
\mathscr{F}/\mathscr{E}
(we may assume that
\mathscr{F}/\mathscr{E}
is torsion-free). Applying Bogomolov’s inequality to the semistable sheaves
\mathscr{E}
and
\mathscr{F}/\mathscr{E}
one gets
the left inequality in (
2.3.10
), see
[
HL10
, Thm. 4.C.3, Thm. 7.3.3]
.
∎

Definition 2.3.7
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective surface, and let
h_{0}\in\NS(X)_{{\mathbb{Q}}}
be nef.
Let
{\mathsf{a}}>0
. An ample class
h
is
{\mathsf{a}}
-suitable for
h_{0}
if for all

\lambda\in\NS(X)
such that
-{\mathsf{a}}\leq\lambda\cdot\lambda<0
one of the following holds:

(1)

\lambda\cdot h>0
and
\lambda\cdot h_{0}\geq 0
.

(2)

\lambda\cdot h=0
and
\lambda\cdot h_{0}=0
.

(3)

\lambda\cdot h<0
and
\lambda\cdot h_{0}\leq 0
.

Remark 2.3.8
.

The reason for giving the above definition is that it allows to compare
h_{0}
slope semistability and
h
slope semistability for torsion-free sheaves such that
{\mathsf{a}}(\mathscr{F})\leq{\mathsf{a}}
, see Proposition
2.3.10
and Subsubsections
2.3.4
,
2.3.5
.

Remark 2.3.9
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective surface, and let
{\mathsf{a}}>0
.
Let
h_{0},h\in\NS(X)_{{\mathbb{Q}}}
with
h_{0}
nef and
h
ample. Thus
Nh_{0}+h
is ample for any positive integer
N
.
If
N
is large enough then
Nh_{0}+h
is
{\mathsf{a}}
-suitable for
h_{0}
. If
h_{0}\cdot h_{0}>0
this follows from the wall-and-chamber decomposition of the ample cone
discussed in Subsubsection
2.3.4
. If
h_{0}\cdot h_{0}=0
see
[
F89
, Lemma 2.3]
.

Proposition 2.3.10
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective surface, and let
h_{0}\in\NS(X)_{{\mathbb{Q}}}
be nef.
Let
{\mathsf{a}}>0
, and let
h\in\NS(X)_{{\mathbb{Q}}}
be ample and
{\mathsf{a}}
-suitable for
h_{0}
.
Suppose that
\mathscr{F}
is a torsion-free sheaf on
X
such that

{\mathsf{a}}(\mathscr{F})\leq{\mathsf{a}}
, and which is

h_{0}
slope semistable but not
h
slope stable.
Then there exists a subsheaf
\mathscr{E}\subset\mathscr{F}
such that

(2.3.11)

0<r(\mathscr{E})<r(\mathscr{F}),\qquad\lambda_{\mathscr{E},\mathscr{F}}\cdot h\geq 0,\qquad\lambda_{\mathscr{E},\mathscr{F}}\cdot h_{0}=0.

Proof.

If
h_{0}\cdot h_{0}>0
see
[
HL10
, Thm 4.C.3]
. If
h_{0}\cdot h_{0}=0
the proof is analogous to that of
[
F95
, Thm 3.3]
(one also gets a proof by rewriting the proof of
[
OG26a
, Prop. 5.9]
with the intersection form of the surface
replacing the Beauville-Bogomolov-Fujiki symmetric bilinear form).
∎

2.3.4.
Wall-and-chamber decomposition of the ample cone

Let
{\mathsf{a}}
be a positive real number. A codimension-
1
real subspace
W\subset\NS(X)_{{\mathbb{R}}}
is an
{\mathsf{a}}
-wall if
W=\xi^{\bot}
where
\xi\in\NS(X)
is such that

-{\mathsf{a}}\leq\xi\cdot\xi<0
.
The intersections of the
{\mathsf{a}}
-walls with the ample cone
\Amp(X)
form a locally finite collection of codimension-
1
submanifolds (see
[
HL10
, Lemma 4.C.2]
), and hence their union is closed in

\Amp(X)
. A connected component of

(2.3.12)

\Amp(X)\setminus\bigcup_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\xi\in\NS(X)}}{{-{\mathsf{a}}\leq q_{X}(\xi)<0}}}\xi^{\bot}

is an

open
{\mathsf{a}}
-chamber
. An ample class
h\in\NS(X)_{{\mathbb{Q}}}
is
{\mathsf{a}}
-generic
if it does not belong to any
{\mathsf{a}}
-wall, i.e. it belongs to a (unique) open
{\mathsf{a}}
-chamber.

Proposition 2.3.11
.

Let
X
be an irreducible smooth projective surface. Let
h_{0},h_{1}\in\NS(X)_{{\mathbb{Q}}}
be
{\mathsf{a}}
-generic ample classes which belong to the same
open
{\mathsf{a}}
-chamber. Suppose that
\mathscr{F}
is a torsion-free sheaf on
X
such that
{\mathsf{a}}(\mathscr{F})\leq{\mathsf{a}}
. Then the following hold:

(1)

If
\mathscr{F}
is
h_{0}
slope stable then it is
h_{1}
slope stable, and viceversa.

(2)

If
\mathscr{F}
is
h_{i}
slope semistable (
i\in\{0,1\}
) and
\mathscr{E}\subset\mathscr{F}
is
a subsheaf with
0<r(\mathscr{E})<r(\mathscr{F})
and
\mu_{h_{i}}(\mathscr{E})=\mu_{h_{i}}(\mathscr{F})
, then

r(\mathscr{F})c_{1}(\mathscr{E})-r(\mathscr{E})c_{1}(\mathscr{F})=0
.

(3)

If
\mathscr{F}
is
h_{0}
slope semistable but non stable then it is
h_{1}
slope semistable but non stable, and viceversa.

Proof.

Since
h_{0},h_{1}
are
{\mathsf{a}}
-generic and belong to the same
open
{\mathsf{a}}
-chamber,

h_{0}
is
{\mathsf{a}}
-suitable for
h_{1}
and viceversa. Let us prove Item (1) Suppose that
\mathscr{F}
is
h_{0}
slope stable. If
\mathscr{F}
is not
h_{1}
slope stable then by Proposition
2.3.10
(with
h=h_{1}
) there exists a subsheaf
\mathscr{E}\subset\mathscr{F}
such that

0<r(\mathscr{E})<r(\mathscr{F})
and
\lambda_{\mathscr{E},\mathscr{F}}\cdot h_{0}=0
. This is a contradiction because
\lambda_{\mathscr{E},\mathscr{F}}\cdot h_{0}=0
is equivalent to

\mu_{h_{0}}(\mathscr{E})=mu_{h_{0}}(\mathscr{F})
. Since the rôles of
h_{0},h_{1}
are symmetric this finishes the proof of Item (1). We prove Item (2).
Since
\mu_{h_{i}}(\mathscr{E})=\mu_{h_{i}}(\mathscr{F})
we have
\lambda_{\mathscr{E},\mathscr{F}}\cdot h_{i}=0
.
By Proposition
2.3.6
and the hypothesis that
h_{i}
does not belong to any
{\mathsf{a}}
-wall it follows that

\lambda_{\mathscr{E},\mathscr{F}}=0
. Lastly we prove Item (3). Suppose that
\mathscr{F}
is
h_{0}
slope semistable but non stable. By Item (2) there exists a chain
of sheaves

2.4.3.

For many choices of polarized

Definition 2.4.6

A connected compact Kähler manifold is

(HK) if it is simply connected and it carries a holomorphic symplectic form spanning the space of holomorphic
2
-forms.

HK manifolds are among the building blocks of compact Kähler manifolds with vanishing first Chern class, see

Remark 2.4.7

Let

be a HK manifold. If

is a compact Kähler manifold which is a deformation of

(i.e. there exist a proper map of complex manifolds

is also a HK manifold.

Example 2.4.8
.

Two dimensional hyperkähler manifolds are the same as
K3
surfaces. We recall that they form a single deformation class (Kodaira).
Let

. Beauville
[

]
gave examples of two distinct deformation classes in each even dimension

proceeding as follows. Let
S
be a smooth projective surface. We let

be the Hilbert scheme parametrizing subschemes

of length

is a projective

is a HK (projective) manifold of dimension

. By associating to a subscheme

of length

where

. One has

A HK manifold is of

if it is a deformation of

3.
Sheaves on higher-dimensional hyperkähler varieties

3.1.
Deformations of sheaves on higher dimensional HK manifolds

Let
X
be a higher dimensional HK manifold, i.e. of dimension greater than
2
. Let
\mathscr{F}
be a simple torsion-free sheaf on
X
. Taking our cue from the results of Subsection
2.1
we ask the questions:

(Q1)

Is the trace map
\Tr^{2}\Ext^{2}_{X}(\mathscr{F},\mathscr{F})\rightarrow H^{2}(X,\mathscr{O}_{X})
injective?
(See (
2.1.5
) and the proof of Theorem
2.1.1
.)

(Q2)

Is the deformation space
\Def(\mathscr{F})
smooth? In other words, does the analogue of the first part of
Theorem
2.1.1
hold?

(Q3)

Is the map
\Def(X,\mathscr{F})\rightarrow\Def(X,\det\mathscr{F})
smooth, or at least surjective?

(Q4)

Suppose in addition that
\mathscr{S}
is locally-free. Is the
map
\Def({\mathbb{P}}(\mathscr{F}))\rightarrow\Def(X)
smooth, or at least surjective? (Recall Corollary
2.1.5
.)

The answer to (Q1) is negative. An example is provided by the tangent bundle
\Theta_{X}
. By Yau’s Theorem
\Theta_{X}
is slope stable with respect to any Kähler class. It follows that
\Theta_{X}
is simple. A choice of holomorphic symplectic form defines an isomorphism
\Theta_{X}\xrightarrow{\sim}\Omega_{X}
. Hence we have

\Ext^{2}_{X}(\Theta_{X},\Theta_{X})=H^{2}(X,\Theta_{X}^{\vee}\otimes\Theta_{X})\cong H^{2}(X,\Omega_{X}\otimes\Omega_{X})=H^{2}(X,\Sym^{2}\Omega_{X})\oplus H^{2}(X,\Omega^{2}_{X}).

Thus
\dim\Ext^{2}_{X}(\Theta_{X},\Theta_{X})\geq h^{2,2}(X)
. Wedge product defines an injection
\Sym^{2}H^{1,1}(X)\hookrightarrow H^{2,2}(X)
, and hence

(3.1.1)

\dim\Ext^{2}_{X}(\Theta_{X},\Theta_{X})\geq{b_{2}(X)-1\choose 2}.

Unless
X
is a hypothetical HK manifold with
b_{2}(X)=3
, the right-hand side of (
3.1.1
) is greater than
1
, and it follows that
\Tr^{2}
is not injective.

The answer to (Q2) is negative as well, if no additional condition is imposed on
\mathscr{F}
. For any
l\geq 4
there exist analytic (non reduced) subsets
Z\subset X
of finite length
l
such that the Douady space
X^{[l]}
is singular at
[Z]
. Since the germ of
X^{[l]}
at
[Z]
is isomorphic to the deformation space
\Def(\mathscr{I}_{Z})
of the ideal sheaf
\mathscr{I}_{Z}
, the latter is not smooth. Examples with
\mathscr{F}
locally free simple (even slope stable) appear in Subsection
4.4
. A conjecture of Beckmann (see Conjecture
3.5.7
) states that the answer to (Q2) is positive if
\mathscr{F}
is a slope stable atomic (see Definition
3.5.1
) vector bundle.

Regarding questions (Q3) and (Q4): if
\mathscr{F}
is a projectively hyperholomorphic vector bundle, then the picture resembles that in dimension
2
, see Subsection
3.4
.

3.2.
Modular sheaves

3.2.1.
Definition of modular sheaves, and first examples

In the present subsection
X
is a HK manifold. Let
\mathscr{F}
be a (non zero) torsion-free sheaf on
X
. If
h
is an ample class on
X
then
h
slope (semi)stability of
\mathscr{F}
may be expressed in terms of the BBF bilinear form
b_{X}
on the Néron-Severi group
\NS(X)
. Thus one may hope that variation of slope (semi)stability may behave as in the case of surfaces. This is the case if one puts a topological restriction on the discriminant of
\mathscr{F}
.

Definition 3.2.1
.

Let
X
be a HK manifold of dimension
2n
. A torsion-free sheaf
\mathscr{F}
on
X
is
modular
if
there exists
d(\mathscr{F})\in{\mathbb{Q}}
such that for all
\alpha\in H^{2}(X)
we have

(3.2.1)

\int_{X}\Delta(\mathscr{F})\cdot\alpha^{2n-2}=d(\mathscr{F})\cdot(2n-3)!!\cdot q_{X}(\alpha)^{n-1},

where
q_{X}(\alpha)=b_{X}(\alpha,\alpha)
is the square for the BBF bilinear symmetric form.

Note that any torsion-free sheaf on a K3 surface is modular.

Remark 3.2.2
.

Let
X
be a HK manifold of dimension
2n
. Let
D(X)\subset H(X)
be the image of the map
\Sym H^{2}(X)\rightarrow H(X)
defined by cup-product. Let
D^{i}(X):=D(X)\cap H^{i}(X)
. The pairing
D^{i}(X)\times D^{4n-i}(X)\rightarrow{\mathbb{C}}
defined by intersection product is non degenerate
[
V96a
,
Bog96
,
Bea07
]
, hence there is a splitting
H(X)=D(X)\oplus D(X)^{\bot}
, where orthogonality is with respect to the intersection pairing. Let
p_{D}\colon H(X)\rightarrow D(X)
be the corresponding orthogonal projection.
A torsion-free sheaf
\mathscr{F}
on
X
is modular if and only if
p_{D}(\Delta(\mathscr{F}))
is a multiple of
p_{D}(c_{2}(X))
.
In particular if
\Delta(\mathscr{F})
is a multiple of
c_{2}(X)
then
\mathscr{F}
is modular.

Example 3.2.3
.

Let

be a a smooth cubic hypersurface, and let

[

, Subsect.2.1]
)

(3.2.2)

\ch_{0}(\mathscr{Q})=4,\quad\ch_{1}(\mathscr{Q})=h,\quad\ch_{2}(\mathscr{Q})=\frac{1}{8}\left(h^{2}-c_{2}(X)\right).

Thus
\Delta(\mathscr{Q})=c_{2}(X)
, and hence
\mathscr{Q}
is modular.
Let
\mathscr{U}
be the restriction to
X
of the tautological rank
2
subbundle on
\mathbb{G}\mathrm{r}(1,{\mathbb{P}}^{5})
. Then
\Delta(\mathscr{U})=(3h^{2}-c_{2}(X))/2
, and hence
\mathscr{U}
is
not
modular.

.

Let

Example 3.2.5

Let

3.5.

The definition of atomic sheaf (and complex) was introduced in
[

First we introduce the (rational) Looijenga-Lunts-Verbitsky (LLV) Lie subalgebra

]

[

]
for the version over

) it is proved that

with bilinear symmetric form

defined by declaring that the decomposition in (

) is orthogonal, the restriction of

to

I suggest reading
[

if you wish to see

The last ingredient needed to define atomicity is the

\mathscr{U}^{[n]}
defines an equivalence between the (bounded) derived categories of
S^{[n]}
and
M^{[n]}
, and moreover it defines an algebraic cycle inducing a Hodge isometry
H^{2}(S^{[n]};{\mathbb{Q}})\xrightarrow{\sim}H^{2}(M^{[n]};{\mathbb{Q}})
which lifts the
Hodge isometry
H^{2}(S;{\mathbb{Q}})\xrightarrow{\sim}H^{2}(M;{\mathbb{Q}})
defined by
\mathscr{U}
.
Markman
[
Mark24b
]
proves that
\mathscr{U}^{[n]}
deforms to a twisted vector bundle on couples
(X,Y)
obtained from

(S^{[n]},M^{[n]})
via “ diagonal twistor lines”. From this he deduces that the analogue of the Shafarevich conjecture holds for HK manifolds of Type
K3^{[n]}
. In
[
MSYZ25
]
the vector bundles
\mathscr{U}^{[n]}
are used to prove the
D
-Conjecture for (projective) HK manifolds of Type
K3^{[n]}
(actually they prove a more general statement). The vector bundles
\mathscr{U}^{[n]}
have been useful in
exploring the relation between period and indx for Brauer classes on HK manifolds of Type
K3^{[n]}
, see
[
HMSYZ25
]
.

4.4.
HK manifolds of Type
K3^{[a^{2}+1]}
as moduli spaces of projective bundles on a HK manifold of Type
K3^{[2]}

The starting point is the following. Let
S
be a
K3

be a divisor class on
S
.
Let

(4.4.1)

v_{1}\coloneqq\left(2a,D,s_{1}\right),\qquad v_{2}\coloneqq av_{1}-(0,0,1).

Note that
v_{1}^{2}=-2
and
v_{2}^{2}=2a^{2}
(squares are with respect to Mukai’s pairing). Moreover
v_{1},v_{2}
are primitive.
Let
h_{S}

{\mathsf{a}}(v_{2})
-generic polarization of
S
(and hence also
{\mathsf{a}}(v_{1})
-generic). The moduli space

\mathscr{M}_{v_{1}}(S,h_{S})

\mathscr{M}_{v_{2}}(S,h_{S})
is a HK variety of Type
K3^{[a^{2}+1]}
. We let

(4.4.2)

\mathscr{M}_{v_{1}}(S,h_{S})=\{[\mathscr{E}_{1}]\}.

Thus
\mathscr{E}_{1}
is a spherical vector bundle.
Let
[\mathscr{E}_{2}]\in\mathscr{M}_{v_{2}}(S,h_{S})
, and let
\mathscr{G}(\mathscr{E}_{1},\mathscr{E}_{2})
be the torsion-free sheaf on
S^{[2]}
given by (
3.2.13
). We recall that

\mathscr{G}(\mathscr{E}_{1},\mathscr{E}_{2})
is modular. We have

(4.4.3)

r(\mathscr{G}(\mathscr{E}_{1},\mathscr{E}_{2}))=8a^{3},\qquad\Delta(\mathscr{G}(\mathscr{E}_{1},\mathscr{E}_{2}))=\frac{4a^{6}}{3}c_{2}(S^{[2]}),\qquad{\mathsf{a}}(\mathscr{G}(\mathscr{E}_{1},\mathscr{E}_{2}))=2560a^{12}.

For the second equality see (

Comment 4.4.2

In the outline of the proof of Proposition

{\mathsf{a}}(\mathscr{E}[n]^{+})

A similar approach towards stability of

Remark 4.4.5
.

If

then

is singular, i.e. the map in (
4.4.8
) is not an isomorphism.

By considering a quasi-universal vector bundle on

one gets a linear map

H^{2}(S^{[2]};{\mathbb{Q}})\rightarrow H^{2}(M_{a,D}(S^{[2]},h_{S^{[2]}})^{\bullet};{\mathbb{Q}})
.
Composing with the normalization map in (
4.4.8
) one gets a Hodge isometry

(4.4.9)

H^{2}(S^{[2]};{\mathbb{Q}})\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow H^{2}(\mathscr{M}_{v_{2}}(S,h_{S});{\mathbb{Q}}).

By Theorem
3.4.3
the vector bundles parametrized by
M_{a,D}(S^{[2]},h_{S^{[2]}})^{\bullet}
are projectively hyperholomorphic.
Hence if
[\mathscr{F}]\in M_{a,D}(S^{[2]},h_{S^{[2]}})^{\bullet}
the projectivization
{\mathbb{P}}(\mathscr{F})
extends to all deformations of
S^{[2]}
parametrized by the
h_{S^{[2]}}
-twistor line as slope-stable projective bundles, and also those parametrized by
\omega
-twistor lines for
\omega
a Kähler class sufficiently close to
h_{S^{[2]}}
(this follows from Proposition
3.3.9
). By
[
V96b

the local structure of the moduli spaces of projective bundles along the twistor lines does not change. The conclusion is that a component of a suitable moduli space of projective bundles on arbitrary (small) deformations of

has as normalization a HK manifold of Type

(the deformation of

\mathscr{M}_{v_{2}}(S,h_{S})
). Since the map in (

) is a Hodge isometry the HK manifolds that we get give all small deformations
of
S^{[2]}
. Working with chains of twistor lines one gets that a general HK manifold of Type

is the normalization of a (component of) of a moduli space of (twisted) hyperholomorphic vector bundles on a HK manifold of Type
K3^{[2]}
.

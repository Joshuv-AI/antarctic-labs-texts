Quantum spacetime from constraints: wave equations and fields
Tommaso Favalli

Abstract
In previous works, we showed that both time and space can emerge from entanglement within a globally constrained quantum Universe, with no background coordinates. By extending the Page and Wootters quantum time formalism to include both quantum clocks and rods, and imposing global constraints on total energy and momentum, we constructed a fully relational model of quantum spacetime. Here we take a further step: working in 1+1 dimensions, we show that the standard wave equations governing quantum particles—the Schrödinger, Klein-Gordon and Dirac equations—emerge naturally from this framework. The solutions of the equations are derived directly from the constraints, without assuming any external spacetime structure. The second quantization formalism is also implemented and discussed. Our results provide further support for the idea that quantum dynamics in spacetime may emerge from entanglement and constraints.

II.1
Subsystems in the Universe

We consider a closed quantum Universe composed of three non-interacting subsystems: a clock
C
, a reference particle
R
, and a system particle
S
. The total Hilbert space of our Universe is thus given by
\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{H}_{C}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{R}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{S}
.

To describe the temporal degree of freedom, we consider the clock Hamiltonian
\hat{H}_{C}
, which we assume to have a discrete, non-degenerate spectrum with rationally related energy ratios. More specifically, we define a set of
d_{C}

\ket{E_{i}}

and

are integers with no common factors. We thus obtain (

E_{i}=E_{0}+r_{i}\frac{2\pi}{T}

where

In this space we define a continuous family of non-orthogonal time states

\ket{t}_{C}=\sum_{i=0}^{d_{C}-1}e^{-iE_{i}t}\ket{E_{i}}_{C},\quad t\in\left[t_{0},t_{0}+T\right)

(2)

which provide the resolution of the identity

\mathbb{1}_{C}=\frac{1}{T}\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{0}+T}dt\,\ket{t}\bra{t}

(3)

thus defining a
positive operator-valued measure
(POVM) with elements
\frac{1}{T}\ket{t}\bra{t}dt

This framework makes it possible to define time operationally as an internal quantum observable with continuous values, while retaining a discrete and bounded energy spectrum.
Moreover, the rationality condition on energy ratios can be safely relaxed: while the identity (

The framework naturally extends to the case of a continuous and unbounded spectrum for

II.2
Spacetime from constraints

We now present our model of spacetime emerging from entanglement. To this end, we consider a global state
\ket{\Psi}\in\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{H}_{C}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{R}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{S}

\hat{H}\ket{\Psi}=(\hat{H}_{C}+\hat{H}_{R}+\hat{H}_{S})\ket{\Psi}=0

(11)

\hat{P}\ket{\Psi}=(\hat{P}_{R}+\hat{P}_{S})\ket{\Psi}=0.

(12)

In equation (
12

\hat{P}_{C}=0

\hat{P}_{C}\neq 0

11

S
alone (for further discussion on this case, we refer the reader to Refs.
nostro3
;
librotommi
, where the scenario with
\hat{P}_{C}\neq 0

In the present analysis, the clock may be identified with an external environment providing a time reference for
R+S

, this choice is not only physically natural but also particularly effective, since our framework naturally accommodates the use of a generic, non-degenerate Hamiltonian as clock Hamiltonian
\hat{H}_{C}
. In this setting, the clock carries no spatial momentum, which justifies the assumption
\hat{P}_{C}=0

III
Schrödinger wave equation

In this Section, we derive and analyze the emergence of the Schrödinger wave equation within our relational framework. We begin by considering the case in which the kinetic energy of the reference particle
R
can be neglected, allowing us to study the effective dynamics of the system
S
alone. We then move on to the more general case in which the kinetic contribution of
R
is explicitly taken into account and, finally, we investigate how an interaction potential depending on the distance between
S
and
R
can be incorporated into the formalism.

III.1
Wave equation for
S

We begin by considering
R
and
S
as non-relativistic free particles, with energies
\hat{H}_{R}=\hat{P}^{2}_{R}/2M
and
\hat{H}_{S}=\hat{P}^{2}_{S}/2m
. Under the assumption
M\gg m,|p_{k}|\,\,\forall k
, the energy constraint (
11
) reduces to the following form:

\left(\hat{H}_{C}+\frac{\hat{P}^{2}_{S}}{2m}\right)\ket{\Psi}\approx 0.

(21)

Projecting this equation onto the basis
\{\ket{t}_{C}\}
in the clock subspace yields the evolution equation for the
R+S
state:

i\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\ket{\phi(t)}_{R,S}\approx\frac{\hat{P}^{2}_{S}}{2m}\ket{\phi(t)}_{R,S}.

(22)

We then evaluate (
22
) in the position basis
\{\ket{x}_{R}\}
of the reference subspace. Using relation (
19
), we obtain the equation for the relative state of
S
:

i\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\ket{\psi(x,t)}_{S}\approx-\frac{1}{2m}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{2}}\ket{\psi(x,t)}_{S}.

(23)

We note that equation (
23
) has the form of the Schrödinger equation for a free particle of mass
m
, but it governs the evolution of a ket in the
S
subspace, with
x
and
t
entering as parameters due to the imposed constraints. To obtain the wave equation, we project (
23
) onto the basis
\{\ket{y}_{S}\}
, yielding:

i\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\psi(y-x,t)\approx-\frac{1}{2m}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{2}}\psi(y-x,t),

(24)

where the dependence of the wave function on
y-x
is ensured by the constraint (
12
). As a final step, we introduce the new spatial coordinate
\xi=y-x
, which naturally reflects the relational character of the position of
S
with respect to the reference particle
R
. The derivatives with respect to
x
transform as follows:

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\frac{\partial}{\partial x}=\frac{\partial\xi}{\partial x}\frac{\partial}{\partial\xi}=-\frac{\partial}{\partial\xi}\\
&\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{2}}=\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\left(\frac{\partial\xi}{\partial x}\frac{\partial}{\partial\xi}\right)=\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\xi^{2}}\end{aligned}\right.

(25)

so that equation (
24
) becomes

i\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\psi(\xi,t)\approx-\frac{1}{2m}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\xi^{2}}\psi(\xi,t).

(26)

In equation (
26
), the wave function is expressed in terms of the relational variable
\xi
, which directly incorporates the relational aspect of the dynamics of
S

We do not explicitly solve equation (
26
), as its solution clearly corresponds to the well-known wave function of a free Schrödinger particle. Rather, our aim is to recover such solution directly from the underlying global constraints of the framework, showing how the familiar dynamics emerges from purely relational principles.

Before proceeding further, we make some preliminary considerations regarding the construction of the global state
\ket{\Psi}
. In order to satisfy both the momentum and energy constraints, the state must be built so that no eigenvalue of the system
S
is left uncorrelated with the corresponding degrees of freedom of
R
and
C
. For the momentum constraint to be satisfied, it is necessary to ensure that each momentum eigenvalue of
S
can be paired with a corresponding (opposite) momentum eigenvalue of
R
. This can be achieved under the assumption of equal spectra for
\hat{P}_{R}
and
\hat{P}_{S}
(i.e.,
N_{R}=N_{S}
,
L_{R}=L_{S}
), or (more generally) by requiring
d_{R}\gg d_{S}
and
L_{R}\gg L_{S}
, so that the spectrum of
\hat{P}_{R}
is sufficiently broad and dense to match all values of the momentum
\hat{P}_{S}
. Similarly, ensuring that no energy eigenstate of
R+S
(or of
S
alone, if the energy of
R
is neglected in the constraint) is excluded from the dynamics requires the clock

to have a sufficiently large and finely spaced energy spectrum, as discussed in Refs.

;
nostro2
;

It is worth noting that all the requirements mentioned are automatically fulfilled in the case of continuous and unbounded energy spectrum for
C
and continuous unbounded momentum spectra for
R
and
S
.
Nonetheless, we never make use of continuous and unbounded momentum spectra throughout this work.

Assuming, then, that all the conditions discussed above are satisfied, the general state
\ket{\Psi}
constrained by (

\ket{\Psi}=\sum_{k=-N_{S}}^{N_{S}}c_{k}\ket{E=-\frac{p^{2}_{k}}{2m}}_{C}\otimes\ket{p=-p_{k}}_{R}\otimes\ket{p_{k}}_{S}.

By projecting (

and the position basis

\ket{\psi(x,t)}_{S}=\sum_{k=-N_{S}}^{N_{S}}c_{k}e^{-i\frac{p^{2}_{k}}{2m}t}e^{-ip_{k}x}\ket{p_{k}}_{S}

&=\sum_{k=-N_{S}}^{N_{S}}c_{k}e^{-i\frac{p^{2}_{k}}{2m}t}e^{ip_{k}(y-x)}\end{split}

and finally to

\psi(\xi,t)=\sum_{k=-N_{S}}^{N_{S}}c_{k}e^{i(p_{k}\xi-\frac{p^{2}_{k}}{2m}t)}

To further constrain the form of the coefficients

We reiterate that, in our framework, both the reference

Accordingly, any wave function depending on the relational coordinate

\xi\in\left[-L/2,L/2\right)

Specifically, we ask here:

\begin{split}&\int_{-L/2}^{L/2}d\xi\>\left|\psi(\xi,t)\right|^{2}=\\
&=\int_{-L/2}^{L/2}d\xi\>\sum_{k=-N_{S}}^{N_{S}}\sum_{n=-N_{S}}^{N_{S}}c_{k}c^{*}_{n}e^{i(p_{k}\xi-\frac{p^{2}_{k}}{2m}t)}e^{-i(p_{n}\xi-\frac{p^{2}_{n}}{2m}t)}\\

which is the correct solution for the wave function of a free Schrödinger particle and exactly satisfies (

36
), we finally obtain:

i\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\psi(\xi,t)=-\frac{1}{2\mu}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\xi^{2}}\psi(\xi,t)

(38)

where we have introduced the reduced mass
\mu=\frac{mM}{m+M}
.
This equation shows that the composite system
R+S
, when described in terms of
\xi=y-x
, behaves effectively as a single free particle with reduced mass
\mu
, evolving in time according to the standard Schrödinger dynamics—thus highlighting how relational degrees of freedom can encapsulate the full dynamics of the joint system.

As in previous paragraph, we do not explicitly solve equation (
38
) but we recover the solution directly from constraints (
12
) and (
34
).
The general form of the global state satisfying both constraints can be written:

\ket{\Psi}=\sum_{k=-N_{S}}^{N_{S}}c_{k}\ket{E=-\frac{p^{2}_{k}}{2\mu}}_{C}\otimes\ket{p=-p_{k}}_{R}\otimes\ket{p_{k}}_{S}.

(39)

Therefore, by following the same steps as in the previous paragraph (projecting the global state onto the bases
\{\ket{t}_{C}\}
,
\{\ket{x}_{R}\}
,
\{\ket{y}_{S}\}
, introducing the coordinate
\xi
, and imposing
\int_{-L/2}^{L/2}d\xi\,|\psi(\xi,t)|^{2}=1
), we obtain again:
\psi(\xi,t)=\sum_{k=-N_{S}}^{N_{S}}a_{k}u_{k}(\xi,t)
with

u_{k}(\xi,t)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{L}}e^{i(p_{k}\xi-\frac{p^{2}_{k}}{2\mu}t)}.

(40)

This solution corresponds to the standard wave function of a free particle with mass
\mu
, thus confirming the expected form for the joint dynamics of the
R+S
system.

III.3
Introducing the potential

In this paragraph, we show how the formalism naturally allows for the introduction of an interaction potential between the reference
R
and the system
S
. Throughout this analysis, we assume
R
and
S
with unbounded momentum spectra, namely we take
N_{R},N_{S}\to\infty
. Within this framework, we can introduce the Hermitian operators
\hat{X}=\frac{1}{L}\int_{-L/2}^{L/2}dx\>x\ket{x}\bra{x}
and
\hat{Y}=\frac{1}{L}\int_{-L/2}^{L/2}dy\>y\ket{y}\bra{y}
, describing the positions of
R
and
S
, and consequently we can define an interaction potential
V(\hat{Y}-\hat{X})
. Note that, since the momenta
p_{k}
in
R
and
S
span all integer multiples of
2\pi/L
, we also assume here a continuous and unbounded energy spectrum for
C
, so that the global energy constraint can be exactly satisfied.

Such constraint reads:

\left(\hat{H}_{C}+\frac{\hat{P}^{2}_{R}}{2M}+\frac{\hat{P}^{2}_{S}}{2m}+V(\hat{Y}-\hat{X})\right)\ket{\Psi}=0

(41)

which, upon projection onto the bases
\{\ket{t}_{C}\}
,
\{\ket{x}_{R}\}
,
\{\ket{y}_{S}\}
, and applying relations (
19
) and (
20
), gives:

i\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\psi(y-x,t)=\\
=\left(-\frac{1}{2M}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial y^{2}}-\frac{1}{2m}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{2}}+V(y-x)\right)\psi(y-x,t).

(42)

Introducing then the relational coordinate
\xi
, and using the transformations for the derivatives, the equation takes the final form:

i\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\psi(\xi,t)=-\frac{1}{2\mu}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\xi^{2}}\psi(\xi,t)+V(\xi)\psi(\xi,t)

(43)

where we have introduced again
\mu=\frac{mM}{m+M}
. We do not attempt to solve equation (
43
), as its solution depends on the specific form of the chosen potential
V(\xi)
. Our goal here was simply to demonstrate that an interaction potential between
R
and
S
can be naturally incorporated within the formalism. It is also worth noting that, for
M\gg m
, we have
\mu\approx m
, and the equation reduces to that of a single particle
S
moving in a potential that depends on its position relative to the referencce
R
.

IV
Klein-Gordon wave equation

In this Section, we derive and analyze the Klein-Gordon wave equation within our relational framework. Working in the relativistic regime, we focus on the case where the contribution of the reference particle
R
to the total energy can be neglected, in order to avoid additional technical complications, which go beyond the scope of the present work. We thus obtain the effective wave equation governing the dynamics of the system
S
alone.

Among the cases considered in this work, the Klein-Gordon equation presents additional challenges due to the second-order nature of its time derivative, which complicates both its interpretation and its derivation within the relational framework. When working with such equation, one possible route is to impose a constraint involving the squares of the energy operators. Unfortunaltely, this quadratic constraint does not allow one to isolate and derive the wave equation for the subsystem
S
alone when both
R
and
S
are taken to be spin-
0
relativistic particles. To this end, we instead adopt a constraint in which the energy operators are not squared, also considering that this form is more naturally aligned with the PaW formalism, where the global constraint (
11
) arises as the simple sum of the subsystem energies, placing the global state
\ket{\Psi}
in an eigenstate of the total energy. This choice makes it possible to extract the effective dynamics of
S
, at the cost of requiring two independent constraints to account for both the positive- and negative-energy solutions characteristic of the Klein-Gordon equation.

In light of these considerations, we note that we are deviating—both conceptually and formally—from the Klein-Gordon treatment briefly outlined in Refs.
nostro3
;
librotommi
, where the reference
R
is assumend to be non-relativistic and its energy was neglected despite adopting a quadratic constraint. Such a choice is replaced here by a coherent framework based on non-quadratic constraints and a relativistically consistent treatment of all components.

We thus consider (c=1):
\hat{H}_{R}=\sqrt{\hat{P}^{2}_{R}+M^{2}}
and
\hat{H}_{S}=\sqrt{\hat{P}^{2}_{S}+m^{2}}
. For
M\gg m,|p_{k}|\,\,\forall k
, we can write:

\hat{H}_{R}\simeq M+\frac{\hat{P}^{2}_{R}}{2M}\approx M.

(44)

Under this assumption, the reference
R
enters the energy constraint (to be introduced shortly) with a constant mass term
M
, which can be neglected as it contributes only an unobservable global phase factor to the dynamics of the relative state of the system
S
. Namely, we can consider the global state satisfying (
12
) and:

\left(\hat{H}_{C}\pm\sqrt{\hat{P}^{2}_{S}+m^{2}}\right)\ket{\Psi_{\pm}}\approx 0

(45)

where we are treating together both energy constraints sice they lead to the same final wave equation for the particle
S
. Projecting the equation first onto the basis
\{\ket{t}_{C}\}
and then onto the basis
\{\ket{x}_{R}\}
, we obtain:

i\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\ket{\phi_{\pm}(t)}_{R,S}\approx\pm\sqrt{\hat{P}^{2}_{S}+m^{2}}\ket{\phi_{\pm}(t)}_{R,S}

(46)

and

i\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\ket{\psi_{\pm}(x,t)}_{S}\approx\pm\sqrt{\hat{P}^{2}_{S}+m^{2}}\ket{\psi_{\pm}(x,t)}_{S}.

(47)

We now iterate the evolution equation for the relative state of
S
in order to obtain

-\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial t^{2}}\ket{\psi_{\pm}(x,t)}_{S}\approx\left(\hat{P}^{2}_{S}+m^{2}\right)\ket{\psi_{\pm}(x,t)}_{S}

(48)

which, through (
19
), can be cast in the form:

\left(\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial t^{2}}-\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{2}}+m^{2}\right)\ket{\psi_{\pm}(x,t)}_{S}\approx 0.

(49)

By further projecting onto the position basis
\{\ket{y}_{S}\}
and introducing the relative coordinate
\xi=y-x
, we finally obtain:

\left(\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial t^{2}}-\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{2}}+m^{2}\right)\psi_{\pm}(y-x,t)\approx 0

(50)

and

\left(\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial t^{2}}-\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\xi^{2}}+m^{2}\right)\psi_{\pm}(\xi,t)\approx 0,

(51)

which is the Klein-Gordon equation expressed in terms of the relative spatial coordinate between
R
and
S
. As in the previous Section, we will explicitly derive its solution directly from the underlying constraint structure.

The global state, satisfying both (
12
) and (
45
), can be written:

\ket{\Psi_{\pm}}=\sum_{k=-N_{S}}^{N_{S}}c_{\pm,k}\ket{E=\mp\epsilon_{k}}_{C}\otimes\ket{p=-p_{k}}_{R}\otimes\ket{p_{k}}_{S}

(52)

where we have introduced
\epsilon_{k}=\sqrt{p^{2}_{k}+m^{2}}
.
By expressing
\ket{\Psi_{\pm}}
in the time basis
\{\ket{t}_{C}\}
and position basis
\{\ket{x}_{R}\}
of subsystems
C
and
R
, we obtain the expression for the relative state
\ket{\psi_{\pm}(x,t)}_{S}=\braket{t,x|\Psi_{\pm}}
of the system
S
, namely:

\ket{\psi_{\pm}(x,t)}_{S}=\sum_{k=-N_{S}}^{N_{S}}c_{\pm,k}e^{\mp i\epsilon_{k}t}e^{-ip_{k}x}\ket{p_{k}}_{S}

(53)

leading to

\begin{split}\psi_{\pm}(y-x,t)&=\braket{y|\psi_{\pm}(x,t)}_{S}\\
&=\sum_{k=-N_{S}}^{N_{S}}c_{\pm,k}e^{\mp i\epsilon_{k}t}e^{ip_{k}(y-x)}\end{split}

(54)

and finally to

\psi_{\pm}(\xi,t)=\sum_{k=-N_{S}}^{N_{S}}c_{\pm,k}e^{i(p_{k}\xi\mp\epsilon_{k}t)}

(55)

where we have reintroduced the coordinate
\xi=y-x
.

In order to gain additional information about the structure of the coefficients
c_{\pm,k}
, we now employ the expression for the temporal component of the conserved Klein-Gordon four-current (see for example
peskin
):

\rho_{\pm}=i\left(\psi^{*}_{\pm}(\xi,t)\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\psi_{\pm}(\xi,t)-\psi_{\pm}(\xi,t)\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\psi^{*}_{\pm}(\xi,t)\right)

(56)

and we require
\int_{-L/2}^{L/2}d\xi\>\rho_{\pm}=\pm 1
.
We make the calculation for the positive-energy solution, namely we have:

\begin{split}\rho_{+}&=\sum_{k=-N_{S}}^{N_{S}}\sum_{n=-N_{S}}^{N_{S}}c^{*}_{+,k}c_{+,n}\epsilon_{n}e^{i\xi(p_{n}-p_{k})}e^{-it(\epsilon_{n}-\epsilon_{k})}+\sum_{k=-N_{S}}^{N_{S}}\sum_{n=-N_{S}}^{N_{S}}c^{*}_{+,k}c_{+,n}\epsilon_{k}e^{i\xi(p_{n}-p_{k})}e^{-it(\epsilon_{n}-\epsilon_{k})}\\
&=\sum_{k=-N_{S}}^{N_{S}}\sum_{n=-N_{S}}^{N_{S}}c^{*}_{+,k}c_{+,n}(\epsilon_{n}+\epsilon_{k})e^{i\xi(p_{n}-p_{k})}e^{-it(\epsilon_{n}-\epsilon_{k})}\end{split}

(57)

leading to

\int_{-L/2}^{L/2}d\xi\>\rho_{+}=\int_{-L/2}^{L/2}d\xi\>\left(\sum_{k=-N_{S}}^{N_{S}}\sum_{n=-N_{S}}^{N_{S}}c^{*}_{+,k}c_{+,n}(\epsilon_{n}+\epsilon_{k})e^{i\xi(p_{n}-p_{k})}e^{-it(\epsilon_{n}-\epsilon_{k})}\right)=L\sum_{k=-N_{S}}^{N_{S}}|c_{+,k}|^{2}2\epsilon_{k}=1.

(58)

We can thus define the coefficients
a_{k}
through:

c_{+,k}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{L}\sqrt{2\epsilon_{k}}}a_{k}

(59)

with which we can write

\psi_{+}(\xi,t)=\sum_{k=-N_{S}}^{N_{S}}a_{k}u_{+,k}(\xi,t)

(60)

where

u_{+,k}(\xi,t)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{L}\sqrt{2\epsilon_{k}}}e^{i(p_{k}\xi-\epsilon_{k}t)}.

(61)

Analogously, we find:

\psi_{-}(\xi,t)=\sum_{k=-N_{S}}^{N_{S}}b^{*}_{k}u_{-,k}(\xi,t)

(62)

where

u_{-,k}(\xi,t)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{L}\sqrt{2\epsilon_{k}}}e^{i(p_{k}\xi+\epsilon_{k}t)}.

(63)

These results correspond to the well-known solutions of the Klein-Gordon equation, in agreement with the standard treatment presented (for example) in Ref.
greinerrelativistic
.

We note that, in equation (
62
), we wrote the negative-energy solution introducing the coefficients
b^{*}_{k}
to set the stage for their promotion to creation operators in the second quantized theory. In this sense, we can also make a further step by assuming
N_{R},N_{S}\to\infty
in
R
,
S
and
\hat{H}_{C}
with continuous unbounded spectrum. We thus define

u_{k}(\xi,t)\equiv u_{+,k}(\xi,t)

and rewrite (
60
) and (
62
) as:

\psi_{+}(\xi,t)=\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}a_{k}u_{k}(\xi,t)

(64)

and

\psi_{-}(\xi,t)=\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}b^{*}_{k}u^{*}_{k}(\xi,t)

(65)

where
u^{*}_{k}(\xi,t)=u_{-,-k}(\xi,t)
, namely

u^{*}_{k}(\xi,t)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{L}\sqrt{2\epsilon_{k}}}e^{-i(p_{k}\xi-\epsilon_{k}t)}.

(66)

In writing (
65
) we used
\epsilon_{k}=\epsilon_{-k}
. We will adopt this formalism in Section VI, where we quantize the field.

V
Dirac wave equation

In this Section, we derive and study the Dirac wave equation assuming the system
S
as spin-
1/2
particle. We adopt a similar approximation as in the Klein-Gordon case, neglecting the energy contribution of the reference system
R
(assumed to be, as in the previous Section, a spin-
0
particle with
\hat{H}_{R}=\sqrt{\hat{P}^{2}_{R}+M^{2}}
and
M>>m,|p_{k}|\,\,\forall k
) in the constraint. This allows us again to isolate and derive the effective relativistic dynamics for
S
and, in contrast to the Klein-Gordon case, we will see that a single energy constraint is sufficient to obtain both the positive- and negative-energy solutions.

In
1+1
spacetime, the system Hamiltonian can be written:

\hat{H}_{S}=\hat{P}_{S}\sigma_{1}+m\sigma_{3},

(67)

where we denote as
\sigma_{1}
and
\sigma_{3}
the Pauli matrices
\sigma_{x}
and
\sigma_{z}
, respectively. Accordingly, the energy constraint reads:

\left(\hat{H}_{C}+\hat{P}_{S}\sigma_{1}+m\sigma_{3}\right)\ket{\Psi}\approx 0

(68)

which, when represented in the
\{\ket{t}_{C}\}
basis, leads to

i\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\ket{\phi(t)}_{R,S}\approx\left(\hat{P}_{S}\sigma_{1}+m\sigma_{3}\right)\ket{\phi(t)}_{R,S}.

(69)

Evaluating the equation in the basis
\{\ket{x}_{R}\}
and applying relation (
19
), we obtain:

i\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\ket{\psi(x,t)}_{S}\approx\left(i\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\sigma_{1}+m\sigma_{3}\right)\ket{\psi(x,t)}_{S}.

(70)

Projecting finally onto the position basis
\{\ket{y}_{S}\}
and introducing the coordinate
\xi=y-x
, we arrive at:

i\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\ket{\psi(\xi,t)}_{\sigma}\approx-i\frac{\partial}{\partial\xi}\sigma_{1}\ket{\psi(\xi,t)}_{\sigma}+m\sigma_{3}\ket{\psi(\xi,t)}_{\sigma}

(71)

which corresponds to the Dirac equation for the system
S
expressed in terms of the relative coordinate
\xi
. Note that this equation is still written in ket form, as it retains the two-component spinor structure (labeled by
\sigma
), which has not yet been resolved by choosing a representation.
The state
\ket{\psi(\xi,t)}_{\sigma}
is therefore a two-component object, which can be expressed more conveniently as:

\ket{\psi(\xi,t)}_{\sigma}\equiv\psi(\xi,t)=\begin{pmatrix}\psi_{1}(\xi,t)\\
\psi_{2}(\xi,t)\end{pmatrix}.

(72)

Using this notation, equation (
71
) can be rewritten:

\left(i\gamma^{0}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}+i\gamma^{1}\frac{\partial}{\partial\xi}-m\right)\psi(\xi,t)\approx 0

(73)

where we have introduced the matrices
\gamma^{0}=\sigma_{3}
and
\gamma^{1}=i\sigma_{2}
, with
\sigma_{2}
denoting the Pauli matrix
\sigma_{y}
(notice that
\gamma^{0}
,
\gamma^{1}
satisfy
\left(\gamma^{0}\right)^{2}=\mathbb{1}
,
\left(\gamma^{1}\right)^{2}=-\mathbb{1}
,
\{\gamma^{0},\gamma^{1}\}=0
).

We can now derive the solution of equations (
71
) and (
73
) starting from the global constraints of our model. The global state satisfying the momentum constraint (
12
) can be written:

\ket{\Psi}=\sum_{\sigma=1}^{2}\sum_{n=0}^{d_{C}-1}\sum_{k=-N_{S}}^{N_{S}}c^{(\sigma)}_{n,k}\ket{E_{n}}_{C}\otimes\ket{p=-p_{k}}_{R}\otimes\ket{p_{k},\sigma}_{S}

(74)

where the spinor degree of freedom is made explicit in the ket of the system
S

dirac
;
librodirac
. We now determine the allowed energy values for the system
S
by imposing the energy constraint (
68
). We have:

\left(\hat{H}_{C}+\hat{P}_{S}\sigma_{1}+m\sigma_{3}\right)\ket{\Psi}=\sum_{n=0}^{d_{C}-1}\sum_{k=-N_{S}}^{N_{S}}\left(c^{(1)}_{n,k}E_{n}+c^{(2)}_{n,k}p_{k}+c^{(1)}_{n,k}m\right)\ket{E_{n}}_{C}\otimes\ket{p=-p_{k}}_{R}\otimes\ket{p_{k},1}_{S}+\\
+\sum_{n=0}^{d_{C}-1}\sum_{k=-N_{S}}^{N_{S}}\left(c^{(2)}_{n,k}E_{n}+c^{(1)}_{n,k}p_{k}-c^{(2)}_{n,k}m\right)\ket{E_{n}}_{C}\otimes\ket{p=-p_{k}}_{R}\otimes\ket{p_{k},2}_{S}\approx 0.

(75)

To solve equation (
75
), we impose that both terms vanish, which yields the condition:

\left\{\begin{aligned} &c^{(1)}_{n,k}(E_{n}+m)+c^{(2)}_{n,k}p_{k}=0\\
&c^{(1)}_{n,k}p_{k}+c^{(2)}_{n,k}(E_{n}-m)=0.\end{aligned}\right.

(76)

Such system has non-trivial solutions only in the case of vanishing determinant of the coefficients:

\textit{det}\begin{pmatrix}(E_{n}+m)&p_{k}\\
p_{k}&(E_{n}-m)\end{pmatrix}=0

(77)

leading to

\left(E_{n}+m\right)\left(E_{n}-m\right)-p^{2}_{k}=0

(78)

and finally to

E_{n}=\pm\sqrt{p^{2}_{k}+m^{2}}.

(79)

The global state
\ket{\Psi}
contains now two energy solutions and can thus be rewritten:

\ket{\Psi_{\pm}}=\sum_{\sigma=1}^{2}\sum_{k=-N_{S}}^{N_{S}}c^{(\sigma)}_{\pm,k}\ket{E=\mp\epsilon_{k}}_{C}\otimes\ket{p=-p_{k}}_{R}\otimes\ket{p_{k},\sigma}_{S}

(80)

where we have introduced again
\epsilon_{k}=\sqrt{p^{2}_{k}+m^{2}}
.

We project equation (
80
) in the bases
\{\ket{t}_{C}\}
and
\{\ket{x}_{R}\}
, in order to obtain the expression for the relative state
\ket{\psi_{\pm}(x,t)}_{S}=\braket{t,x|\Psi_{\pm}}

\ket{\psi_{\pm}(x,t)}_{S}=\sum_{\sigma=1}^{2}\sum_{k=-N_{S}}^{N_{S}}c^{(\sigma)}_{\pm,k}e^{\mp i\epsilon_{k}t}e^{-ip_{k}x}\ket{p_{k},\sigma}_{S}

(81)

leading to

\begin{split}\ket{\psi_{\pm}(y-x,t)}_{\sigma}&=\braket{y|\psi_{\pm}(x,t)}_{S}\\
&=\sum_{\sigma=1}^{2}\sum_{k=-N_{S}}^{N_{S}}c^{(\sigma)}_{\pm,k}e^{\mp i\epsilon_{k}t}e^{ip_{k}(y-x)}\ket{\sigma}_{\sigma}\end{split}

(82)

and finally to

\ket{\psi_{\pm}(\xi,t)}_{\sigma}=\sum_{\sigma=1}^{2}\sum_{k=-N_{S}}^{N_{S}}c^{(\sigma)}_{\pm,k}e^{i(p_{k}\xi\mp\epsilon_{k}t)}\ket{\sigma}_{\sigma}.

(83)

This state can be expressed through the notation introduced in (
72
), namely:

\ket{\psi_{\pm}(\xi,t)}_{\sigma}\equiv\psi_{\pm}(\xi,t)=\begin{pmatrix}\psi_{\pm,1}(\xi,t)\\
\psi_{\pm,2}(\xi,t)\end{pmatrix}

(84)

where the two components
\psi_{\pm,1}(\xi,t)

\psi_{\pm,2}(\xi,t)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\psi_{\pm,1}(\xi,t)=\sum_{k=-N_{S}}^{N_{S}}c^{(1)}_{\pm,k}e^{\mp i\epsilon_{k}t}e^{ip_{k}\xi}\\

(85)

We now turn to the analysis of the coefficients
c^{(\sigma)}_{\pm,k}
.
As in the Klein-Gordon case, we focus on the positive-energy solution, providing only the final result for the negative-energy solution.
We start by writing

\psi_{+}(\xi,t)=\sum_{k=-N_{S}}^{N_{S}}b_{k}u_{+,k}(\xi,t)

where

u_{+,k}(\xi,t)=\begin{pmatrix}u^{(1)}_{+,k}(\xi,t)\\

and

\left\{\begin{aligned} &u^{(\sigma)}_{+,k}(\xi,t)=\phi_{\sigma,k}(\xi)e^{-i\epsilon_{k}t}\\

&c^{(\sigma)}_{+,k}=b_{k}\chi_{\sigma,k}.\end{aligned}\right.

We insert

73
)), thus obtaining:

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\epsilon_{k}\phi_{1,k}(\xi)=-i\frac{\partial}{\partial\xi}\phi_{2,k}(\xi)+m\phi_{1,k}(\xi)\\

We now make use of the fact that

\phi_{\sigma,k}(\xi)=\chi_{\sigma,k}e^{ip_{k}\xi}

\left\{\begin{aligned} &(\epsilon_{k}-m)\chi_{1,k}-p_{k}\chi_{2,k}=0\\
&-p_{k}\chi_{1,k}+(\epsilon_{k}-m)\chi_{2,k}=0,\end{aligned}\right.\\

from which we obtain:

\chi_{2,k}=\frac{\epsilon_{k}-m}{p_{k}}\chi_{1,k}=\frac{p_{k}}{\epsilon_{k}+m}\chi_{1,k}.

In order to determine the expression for
\chi_{1,k}
(and consequently for
\chi_{2,k}
), we adopt the normalization condition for the time component of the conserved four-current
peskin

\begin{split}\rho_{+}&=\bar{\psi}_{+}(\xi,t)\gamma^{0}\psi_{+}(\xi,t)\\
&=\psi^{\dagger}_{+}(\xi,t)\psi_{+}(\xi,t)\\
&=\psi^{*}_{+,1}(\xi,t)\psi_{+,1}(\xi,t)+\psi^{*}_{+,2}(\xi,t)\psi_{+,2}(\xi,t).\end{split}

(92)

where we used
\bar{\psi}_{+}(\xi,t)=\psi^{\dagger}_{+}(\xi,t)\gamma^{0}
and
\left(\gamma^{0}\right)^{2}=\mathbb{1}
.

Using (

), (

) and (

), we thus require:

\int_{-L/2}^{L/2}d\xi\>\rho_{+}=\int_{-L/2}^{L/2}d\xi\>\sum_{k,n=-N_{S}}^{N_{S}}b^{*}_{k}b_{n}\chi^{*}_{1,k}\chi_{1,n}e^{i\xi(p_{n}-p_{k})}e^{-it(\epsilon_{n}-\epsilon_{k})}+\int_{-L/2}^{L/2}d\xi\>\sum_{k,n=-N_{S}}^{N_{S}}b^{*}_{k}b_{n}\chi^{*}_{2,k}\chi_{2,n}e^{i\xi(p_{n}-p_{k})}e^{-it(\epsilon_{n}-\epsilon_{k})}\\
=L\sum_{k=-N_{S}}^{N_{S}}|b_{k}|^{2}|\chi_{1,k}|^{2}+L\sum_{k=-N_{S}}^{N_{S}}|b_{k}|^{2}|\chi_{2,k}|^{2}=\sum_{k=-N_{S}}^{N_{S}}|b_{k}|^{2}\left(L|\chi_{1,k}|^{2}+L|\chi_{2,k}|^{2}\right)=1.

Employing the normalization

, we can rewrite (

) as:

\sum_{k=-N_{S}}^{N_{S}}|b_{k}|^{2}\left(L|\chi_{1,k}|^{2}+2L|\chi_{2,k}|^{2}-1\right)=0

which, together with (

and finally to

\chi_{1,k}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{L}}\frac{\sqrt{\epsilon_{k}+m}}{\sqrt{2\epsilon_{k}}}

where we used

\chi_{2,k}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{L}}\frac{\text{sign}(p_{k})\sqrt{\epsilon_{k}-m}}{\sqrt{2\epsilon_{k}}}.

Combining all the results above, the general form of the

u_{+,k}(\xi,t)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{L}}u_{+,k}e^{i(p_{k}\xi-\epsilon_{k}t)}

(98)

where we have defined

u_{+,k}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\epsilon_{k}}}\begin{pmatrix}\sqrt{\epsilon_{k}+m}\\
\text{sign}(p_{k})\sqrt{\epsilon_{k}-m}\end{pmatrix}.

(99)

In an entirely analogous way, one obtains the negative-energy solution:

u_{-,k}(\xi,t)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{L}}u_{-,k}e^{i(p_{k}\xi+\epsilon_{k}t)}

(100)

with

u_{-,k}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\epsilon_{k}}}\begin{pmatrix}-\sqrt{\epsilon_{k}-m}\\
\text{sign}(p_{k})\sqrt{\epsilon_{k}+m}\end{pmatrix}.

(101)

The solutions derived above lead to the well-known positive/negative-energy spinor solutions of the Dirac equation in
1+1
dimensions, in agreement with standard treatments. This confirms that the full relativistic dynamics of a spin-
1/2
particle emerges consistently within our constraint-based framework.

As in the Klein–Gordon case, we also take here a preliminary step towards second quantization, which will be useful in the next Section. We start again by assuming
R
,
S
with
N_{R},N_{S}\to\infty
and
\hat{H}_{C}
with continuous unbouned spectrum. Within this framework we define:

u_{k}(\xi,t)\equiv u_{+,k}(\xi,t)

(102)

and

v_{k}(\xi,t)\equiv u_{-,-k}(\xi,t).

(103)

The solutions of the Dirac equation (
71
) (or equivalently (
73
)) can thus be written:

\psi_{+}(\xi,t)=\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}b_{k}u_{k}(\xi,t)

(104)

and

\psi_{-}(\xi,t)=\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}d^{*}_{k}v_{k}(\xi,t)

(105)

where

\left\{\begin{aligned} &u_{k}(\xi,t)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{L}}u_{k}e^{i(p_{k}\xi-\epsilon_{k}t)}\\
&v_{k}(\xi,t)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{L}}v_{k}e^{-i(p_{k}\xi-\epsilon_{k}t)}\end{aligned}\right.

(106)

with

\left\{\begin{aligned} &u_{k}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\epsilon_{k}}}\begin{pmatrix}\sqrt{\epsilon_{k}+m}\\
\text{sign}(p_{k})\sqrt{\epsilon_{k}-m}\end{pmatrix}\\
&v_{k}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\epsilon_{k}}}\begin{pmatrix}-\sqrt{\epsilon_{k}-m}\\
-\text{sign}(p_{k})\sqrt{\epsilon_{k}+m}\end{pmatrix}.\end{aligned}\right.

(107)

We emphasize that
u_{k}(\xi,t)
,
v_{k}(\xi,t)
and
u_{k}
,
v_{k}
, as defined in equations (
106
) and (
107
), satisfy the properties typically required in the mode analysis of Dirac spinors.
Specifically, we have
u^{\dagger}_{k}u_{k}=v^{\dagger}_{k}v_{k}=1
, leading to

\displaystyle\int_{-L/2}^{L/2}d\xi\>u^{\dagger}_{k}(\xi,t)u_{n}(\xi,t)=\int_{-L/2}^{L/2}d\xi\>v^{\dagger}_{k}(\xi,t)v_{n}(\xi,t)={\delta_{k,n}}

(108)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}u^{\dagger}_{k}(\xi,t)u_{k}(\xi^{\prime},t)=\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}v^{\dagger}_{k}(\xi,t)v_{k}(\xi^{\prime},t)=\delta(\xi-\xi^{\prime})

and, in addition, the following hold:

•

u_{k}(\xi,t)
,
v_{k}(\xi,t)
are eigenvectors of
\hat{H}_{D}=-i\sigma_{1}\frac{\partial}{\partial\xi}+m\sigma_{3}
with eigenvalues
\epsilon_{k}
and
-\epsilon_{k}
, respectively;

•

\bar{u}_{k}v_{k}=u^{\dagger}_{k}\gamma^{0}v_{k}=0,\quad\bar{v}_{k}u_{k}=v^{\dagger}_{k}\gamma^{0}u_{k}=0
;

•

\bar{u}_{k}u_{k}=\frac{\epsilon_{k}}{m},\quad\bar{v}_{k}v_{k}=-\frac{\epsilon_{k}}{m}
;

•

u^{\dagger}_{k}v_{-k}=v^{\dagger}_{k}u_{-k}=0
;

•

u_{k}u^{\dagger}_{k}+v_{-k}v^{\dagger}_{-k}=\mathbb{1}
.

The relations derived above—including the explicit form of the spinors
u_{k}(\xi,t)
,
v_{k}(\xi,t)
—capture the essential algebraic structure required for the second quantization of the Dirac field and will be used in the next Section.

VI
Second quantization formalism

We discuss here how the formalism of second quantization can be naturally implemented within our framework.
Although our model describes a closed Universe composed of a finite number of subsystems, we employ the second-quantized formalism as a convenient technical tool. This language clearly allows for the description of multi-particle states, but in our analysis both
R
and
S
continue to be treated as single particles.
The field-theoretic structure is used solely to maintain contact with the standard formulation of quantum field theory, without going beyond the regime of single-particle physics.

Before introducing second-quantized fields on the relational coordinate
\xi=y-x
, it is however important to clearly state the key assumptions that ensure the mathematical and physical consistency of the formalism.

As anticipated, we assume here that
\hat{P}_{R}
and
\hat{P}_{S}
have unbounded discrete spectra with eigenvalues
p_{k}=\frac{2\pi}{L}k
and
k\in\mathbb{Z}
. While much of the paper were concerned with wave functions supported over a finite range of momenta (which is physically reasonable for states with finite energy),
the construction of a field operator in position space requires the full momentum spectrum. If the momentum basis is truncated, the equal-time commutator between the field operator (to be introduced) and its Hermitian conjugate—or, in the Klein–Gordon case, its conjugate momentum—does not vanish for
\xi\neq\xi^{\prime}
, spoiling the locality structure.
By allowing
\hat{P}_{R}
and
\hat{P}_{S}
to span all
k\in\mathbb{Z}
, we recover the key property:

\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}e^{ip_{k}(\xi-\xi^{\prime})}=L\delta(\xi-\xi^{\prime})\quad\text{for }\xi,\xi^{\prime}\in\left[-\frac{L}{2},\frac{L}{2}\right),

(109)

which ensures that the fields defined below possess the correct commutation (or anticommutation) relations in position space. Importantly, this extension remains compatible with the energy-neglecting approximation for the reference
R
, provided that
M\gg m
and
M\gg|p_{k}|
for all the momenta relevant to the dynamics.

Finally, we note that, in this framework, we also make use of a continuous and unbounded energy spectrum for
C
, to ensure that the global energy constraint can be exactly satisfied.
As discussed in Section II.A, this remains consistent with the hypothesis of a finite Universe by considering that the relevant dynamics are effectively captured within a large but finite energy window.

VI.1
Schrödinger case

We consider the case of
R
and
S
as Schrödinger free particles. Although the second quantization of the Schrödinger field is straightforward, we include it here as a useful stepping stone to understand how our relational framework accommodates the field-theoretic formulation.

We start studying the regime in which the kinetic energy of
R
can be neglected. The energy constraint reads:

\left(\hat{H}_{C}+\frac{\hat{P}^{2}_{S}}{2m}\right)\ket{\Psi}\approx 0

(110)

from which, in combination with (
12
), we obtain

\ket{\Psi}=\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}c_{k}\ket{E=-\frac{p^{2}_{k}}{2m}}_{C}\otimes\ket{p=-p_{k}}_{R}\otimes\ket{p_{k}}_{S}

(111)

and finally (after projecting onto the bases
\{\ket{t}_{C}\}
,
\{\ket{x}_{R}\}
,
\{\ket{y}_{S}\}
) the wave function

\begin{split}\psi(\xi,t)&=\braket{t,x,y|\Psi}\\
&=\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}a_{k}u_{k}(\xi,t)\\
&=\frac{1}{\sqrt{L}}\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}a_{k}e^{i(p_{k}\xi-\frac{p^{2}_{k}}{2m}t)}\end{split}

(112)

where
c_{k}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{L}}a_{k}
and
u_{k}(\xi,t)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{L}}e^{i(p_{k}\xi-\frac{p^{2}_{k}}{2m}t)}
.

It is thus natural to promote

\psi(\xi,t),\psi^{*}(\xi,t)\longrightarrow\hat{\psi}(\xi,t),\hat{\psi}^{\dagger}(\xi,t),

(113)

where the field operators can be expanded as follows:

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\hat{\psi}(\xi,t)=\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\hat{a}_{k}u_{k}(\xi,t)\\
&\hat{\psi}^{\dagger}(\xi,t)=\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{k}u^{*}_{k}(\xi,t)\end{aligned}\right.

(114)

with
\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{k},\hat{a}_{k}
the usual creation and annihilation operators.
For
\hat{\psi}(\xi,t)
and
\hat{\psi}^{\dagger}(\xi,t)
we require the bosonic commutation relations:

\begin{split}&\left[\hat{\psi}(\xi,t),\hat{\psi}^{\dagger}(\xi^{\prime},t)\right]=\delta(\xi-\xi^{\prime})\\
&\left[\hat{\psi}(\xi,t),\hat{\psi}(\xi^{\prime},t)\right]=\left[\hat{\psi}^{\dagger}(\xi,t),\hat{\psi}^{\dagger}(\xi^{\prime},t)\right]=0.\end{split}

(115)

Together with
\int_{-L/2}^{L/2}d\xi\,u^{*}_{k}(\xi,t)u_{n}(\xi,t)=\delta_{k,n}
, as a consequence of (
109
) we now also have:

\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}u^{*}_{k}(\xi,t)u_{k}(\xi^{\prime},t)=\delta(\xi-\xi^{\prime})

(116)

which, combined with equations (
115
), guarantees that the canonical commutation relations for
\hat{a}_{k}
and
\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{k}
are satisfied, namely

\begin{split}&[\hat{a}_{n},\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{k}]=\delta_{n,k}\\
&[\hat{a}_{n},\hat{a}_{k}]=[\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{n},\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{k}]=0.\end{split}

(117)

We can thus define the Hamiltonian density:

\hat{h}_{\text{eff}}=\hat{\psi}^{\dagger}(\xi,t)\left(-\frac{1}{2m}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\xi^{2}}\right)\hat{\psi}(\xi,t),

(118)

from which we easily obtain

\hat{H}_{\text{eff}}=\int_{-L/2}^{L/2}d\xi\>\hat{h}_{\text{eff}}=\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{p^{2}_{k}}{2m}\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{k}\hat{a}_{k}.

(119)

We stress that, although
\hat{H}_{\mathrm{eff}}
governs the effective dynamics of
S
, it should not be interpreted as the fundamental Hamiltonian of the system. Rather, it emerges from the global constraint and the relational structure of the theory, and thus encapsulates the dynamics of
S
relative to the chosen reference subsystem.

In addition to the Hamiltonian
\hat{H}_{\text{eff}}
, we also find:

\hat{P}_{\text{eff}}=\int_{-L/2}^{L/2}d\xi\>\hat{\psi}^{\dagger}(\xi,t)\left(-i\frac{\partial}{\partial\xi}\right)\hat{\psi}(\xi,t)=\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}p_{k}\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{k}\hat{a}_{k}

(120)

representing the total effective momentum, as emerging from the relational structure of the theory.

In this framework, together with the momentum states
\ket{p_{k}}=\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{k}\ket{0}
, we can define the position states:

\ket{\xi}=\hat{\psi}^{\dagger}(\xi)\ket{0}=\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}u^{*}_{k}(\xi)\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{k}\ket{0}

(121)

where
u_{k}(\xi)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{L}}e^{ip_{k}\xi}
. This states are eigenstates of the Hermitian operator:

\hat{\xi}=\int_{-L/2}^{L/2}d\xi\>\xi\ket{\xi}\bra{\xi},\quad\xi\in\left[-\frac{L}{2},\frac{L}{2}\right),

(122)

describing the position of the system particle with respect to the reference
R
.
To recover the time-dependent wave function that solves the Schrödinger equation (
26
), we simply write:

\psi(\xi,t)=\bra{0}\hat{\psi}(\xi,t)\ket{\psi}

(123)

where

\ket{\psi}=\int_{-L/2}^{L/2}d\xi^{\prime}\>\psi(\xi^{\prime},0)\hat{\psi}^{\dagger}(\xi^{\prime})\ket{0}.

(124)

The central message we wish to highlight is that the entire relational structure of space is encoded in the field operators, which depend on the variable
\xi
, and consequently in the states
\ket{\xi}
, generated by the action of
\hat{\psi}^{\dagger}(\xi)
on the vacuum. This is the key insight that the second-quantized formalism makes explicitly manifest.

To conclude this Section, we move to the case in which the kinetic energy of the reference subsystem
R
cannot be neglected, where the energy constraint reads:

\left(\hat{H}_{C}+\frac{\hat{P}^{2}_{R}}{2M}+\frac{\hat{P}^{2}_{S}}{2m}\right)\ket{\Psi}=0,

(125)

from which (together with constraint (
12
)) we obtain the wave function

\psi(\xi,t)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{L}}\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}a_{k}e^{i(p_{k}\xi-\frac{p^{2}_{k}}{2\mu}t)}

(126)

where
\mu=\frac{mM}{m+M}
.
The entire discussion regarding the introduction of the field operators is the same as above and will not be repeated.
The difference is that
R
now enters the wave equation, leading to the Hamiltonian density:

h^{\prime}_{\mathrm{eff}}=\hat{\psi}^{\dagger}(\xi,t)\left(-\frac{1}{2\mu}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\xi^{2}}\right)\hat{\psi}(\xi,t),

(127)

from which we obtain

\hat{H}^{\prime}_{\text{eff}}=\int_{-L/2}^{L/2}d\xi\>\hat{h}^{\prime}_{\text{eff}}=\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{p^{2}_{k}}{2\mu}\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{k}\hat{a}_{k}.

(128)

The momentum is instead found to coincide with (
120
).
We thus recover the result that the system
R+S
behaves as a single particle with the reduced mass
\mu
. In the limit
M\gg m
, we find
\mu\approx m
. Accordingly, we have
\hat{H}^{\prime}_{\text{eff}}\approx\hat{H}_{\text{eff}}
, consistently with the assumption that the kinetic energy of
R
can be neglected. This confirms that, when the reference becomes very massive, the usual dynamics for the system
S
alone is correctly recovered.

VI.2
Klein-Gordon case

We study here the quantization of the Klein-Gordon field, assuming the system
S
to be a spin-
0
particle. We neglect the energy of
R
, and thus we work within the framework introduced at the end of Section IV.

It is important to notice that, in our framework, the total energy constraint is not imposed in quadratic form. As a result, it does not naturally accommodate both positive- and negative-energy solutions within a single equation, as discussed in Section IV. To consistently include the full set of solutions, we promote the global state
\ket{\Psi}
to a two-component object. This allows us to encode the positive- and negative-energy branches separately, and to represent the constraint as a matrix acting on this extended space. Namely, we write:

\ket{\Psi}=\begin{pmatrix}\ket{\Psi_{+}}\\
\ket{\Psi_{-}}\end{pmatrix}

(129)

where
\ket{\Psi_{+}}
and
\ket{\Psi_{-}}
independently satisfy

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\hat{E}_{+}\ket{\Psi_{+}}=\left(\hat{H}_{C}+\sqrt{\hat{P}^{2}_{S}+m^{2}}\right)\ket{\Psi_{+}}\approx 0\\
&\hat{E}_{-}\ket{\Psi_{-}}=\left(\hat{H}_{C}-\sqrt{\hat{P}^{2}_{S}+m^{2}}\right)\ket{\Psi_{-}}\approx 0\end{aligned}\right.

(130)

and

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\hat{M}\ket{\Psi_{+}}=\left(\hat{P}_{R}+\hat{P}_{S}\right)\ket{\Psi_{+}}=0\\
&\hat{M}\ket{\Psi_{-}}=\left(\hat{P}_{R}+\hat{P}_{S}\right)\ket{\Psi_{-}}=0.\end{aligned}\right.

(131)

The energy and momentum constraints on the global state
\ket{\Psi}
thus read:

\hat{C}_{E}\ket{\Psi}=\begin{pmatrix}\hat{E}_{+}&0\\
0&\hat{E}_{-}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\ket{\Psi_{+}}\\
\ket{\Psi_{-}}\end{pmatrix}\approx 0

(132)

and

\hat{C}_{M}\ket{\Psi}=\begin{pmatrix}\hat{M}&0\\
0&\hat{M}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\ket{\Psi_{+}}\\
\ket{\Psi_{-}}\end{pmatrix}=0,

(133)

which lead to

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\ket{\Psi_{+}}=\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}c_{+,k}\ket{E=-\epsilon_{k}}_{C}\otimes\ket{p=-p_{k}}_{R}\otimes\ket{p_{k}}_{S}\\
&\ket{\Psi_{-}}=\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}c_{-,k}\ket{E=+\epsilon_{k}}_{C}\otimes\ket{p=-p_{k}}_{R}\otimes\ket{p_{k}}_{S}\end{aligned}\right.

(134)

where we have reintroduced
\epsilon_{k}=\sqrt{p^{2}_{k}+m^{2}}
.

In order to extract the global wave function in the usual form, we must now project
\ket{\Psi}
not only onto the basis states
\{\ket{t}_{C}\}
,
\{\ket{x}_{R}\}
, and
\{\ket{y}_{S}\}
, but also along the equal-weight superposition of the auxiliary components. Namely, we define:

\psi(\xi,t)\equiv\left[\bra{t,x,y}\otimes\left(1,1\right)\right]\ket{\Psi}\\
=\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\left(a_{k}u_{+,k}(\xi,t)+b^{*}_{k}u_{-,k}(\xi,t)\right)\\
=\frac{1}{\sqrt{L}}\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\left(\frac{a_{k}}{\sqrt{2\epsilon_{k}}}e^{i(p_{k}\xi-\epsilon_{k}t)}+\frac{b^{*}_{k}}{\sqrt{2\epsilon_{k}}}e^{i(p_{k}\xi+\epsilon_{k}t)}\right)

(135)

where

c_{+,k}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{L}\sqrt{2\epsilon_{k}}}a_{k},\quad c_{-,k}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{L}\sqrt{2\epsilon_{k}}}b^{*}_{k},

(136)

u_{+,k}
,
u_{-,k}
are defined in (
61
), (
63
), and the vector
(1,1)^{\text{T}}
selects the wave function as the coherent superposition of the positive- and negative-energy solutions.

We note that the two-component structure used here does not correspond to an intrinsic degree of freedom. Rather, it serves as a formal device to encode both positive- and negative-energy solutions within a single unified wave function. The projection onto the
(1,1)^{\text{T}}
direction simply recovers the total physical solution as a superposition of the two energy branches.

As at the end of Section IV, we can now identify
u_{k}\equiv u_{+,k}
(
u^{*}_{k}=u_{-,-k}
) and rewrite (
135
) as

\psi(\xi,t)=\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\left(a_{k}u_{k}(\xi,t)+b^{*}_{k}u^{*}_{k}(\xi,t)\right)\\
=\frac{1}{\sqrt{L}}\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\left(\frac{a_{k}}{\sqrt{2\epsilon_{k}}}e^{i(p_{k}\xi-\epsilon_{k}t)}+\frac{b^{*}_{k}}{\sqrt{2\epsilon_{k}}}e^{-i(p_{k}\xi-\epsilon_{k}t)}\right)

(137)

which is the form the wave function must take before proceeding with quantization. Notice that, in our framework, we are describing a complex Klein-Gordon field.

Starting from (
137
), and analogously to the standard Klein–Gordon theory, we define the conjugate momentum as

\pi(\xi,t)=\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\psi^{*}(\xi,t)

(138)

and we promote:

\displaystyle\psi(\xi,t),\psi^{*}(\xi,t)\longrightarrow\hat{\psi}(\xi,t),\hat{\psi}^{\dagger}(\xi,t)

(139)

\displaystyle\pi(\xi,t),\pi^{*}(\xi,t)\longrightarrow\hat{\pi}(\xi,t),\hat{\pi}^{\dagger}(\xi,t)

where

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\hat{\psi}(\xi,t)=\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\left(\hat{a}_{k}u_{k}(\xi,t)+\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{k}u^{*}_{k}(\xi,t)\right)\\
&\hat{\psi}^{\dagger}(\xi,t)=\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\left(\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{k}u^{*}_{k}(\xi,t)+\hat{b}_{k}u_{k}(\xi,t)\right)\end{aligned}\right.

(140)

and

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\hat{\pi}(\xi,t)=i\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\epsilon_{k}\left(\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{k}u^{*}_{k}(\xi,t)-\hat{b}_{k}u_{k}(\xi,t)\right)\\
&\hat{\pi}^{\dagger}(\xi,t)=-i\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\epsilon_{k}\left(\hat{a}_{k}u_{k}(\xi,t)-\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{k}u^{*}_{k}(\xi,t)\right).\end{aligned}\right.

(141)

We can thus impose the commutation relations:

\begin{split}&\left[\hat{\psi}(\xi,t),\hat{\pi}(\xi^{\prime},t)\right]=i\delta(\xi-\xi^{\prime})\\
&\left[\hat{\psi}(\xi,t),\hat{\psi}(\xi^{\prime},t)\right]=\left[\hat{\pi}(\xi,t),\hat{\pi}(\xi^{\prime},t)\right]=0\end{split}

(142)

from which, using the expansions (
140
), (
141
), together with (
109
), naturally follow

\begin{split}&[\hat{a}_{n},\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{k}]=[\hat{b}_{n},\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{k}]=\delta_{n,k}\\
&[\hat{a}_{n},\hat{a}_{k}]=[\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{n},\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{k}]=[\hat{b}_{n},\hat{b}_{k}]=[\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{n},\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{k}]=0,\end{split}

(143)

as expected for bosonic creation/annihilation operators.

As in the Schrödinger case, we now introduce the Hamiltonian density associated with the effective relative dynamics. This allows us to derive the corresponding effective Hamiltonian
\hat{H}_{\text{eff}}
, which governs the evolution of the system in the relational picture. We have
greinerfields
:

\hat{h}_{\text{eff}}=\hat{\pi}^{\dagger}(\xi,t)\hat{\pi}(\xi,t)+\frac{\partial}{\partial\xi}\hat{\psi}^{\dagger}(\xi,t)\frac{\partial}{\partial\xi}\hat{\psi}(\xi,t)+m^{2}\hat{\psi}^{\dagger}(\xi,t)\hat{\psi}(\xi,t)

(144)

and, defining
E_{0}=\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\epsilon_{k}
, we obtain

\hat{H}_{\text{eff}}=\int_{-L/2}^{L/2}d\xi\>\hat{h}_{\text{eff}}-E_{0}=\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\epsilon_{k}\left(\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{k}\hat{a}_{k}+\hat{b}_{k}\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{k}\right)-E_{0}=\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\epsilon_{k}\left(\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{k}\hat{a}_{k}+\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{k}\hat{b}_{k}\right)

(145)

where we used the relation
\int_{-L/2}^{L/2}d\xi\>e^{i\xi(p_{k}\mp p_{n})}=L\delta_{k,\pm n}
. The resulting Hamiltonian, with the vacuum energy
E_{0}
subtracted, takes the standard form of a sum over particle and antiparticle number operators. We emphasize that, in the relativistic case, the structure of the field and the symmetry between positive- and negative-energy modes naturally imply the presence of antiparticle degrees of freedom whenever a particle is considered. Nevertheless, we reiterate that, within our framework, we remain confined to the single-particle sector, which allows us to describe either a particle or an antiparticle, both arising from the same underlying constraint.

In a similar way, we find:

\hat{P}_{\text{eff}}=-\int_{-L/2}^{L/2}d\xi\>\left(\hat{\pi}(\xi,t)\frac{\partial}{\partial\xi}\hat{\psi}(\xi,t)+\hat{\pi}^{\dagger}(\xi,t)\frac{\partial}{\partial\xi}\hat{\psi}^{\dagger}(\xi,t)\right)=\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}p_{k}\left(\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{k}\hat{a}_{k}+\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{k}\hat{b}_{k}\right)

(146)

representing the momentum operator, also written as a sum over particle and antiparticle number operators.

VI.3
Dirac case

We study the quantization of the Dirac field, assuming the system
S
to be a spin-
1/2
particle. Also in this case we neglect the energy of
R
, which is taken to be a spin-
0
particle,
and thus we start from the framework introduced at the end of Section V.

In the case of the Dirac equation, the matrix-valued constraint formalism introduced for the Klein–Gordon case could also be adopted, but it is not strictly necessary. Since the Dirac equation is first-order in time, a single global state with support on both
+\epsilon_{k}
and
-\epsilon_{k}
is already sufficient to generate a conditional wave function for the system that includes both time evolutions
e^{\mp i\epsilon_{k}t}
.

Namely, we take the global state of our quantum Universe
\ket{\Psi}
satisfying the momentum constraint (
12
) and

\left(\hat{H}_{C}+\hat{P}_{S}\sigma_{1}+m\sigma_{3}\right)\ket{\Psi}\approx 0

(147)

representing the single energy constraint, as opposed to the matrix structure arising in the Klein-Gordon case.

The general form for
\ket{\Psi}
can be written:

\ket{\Psi}=\sum_{\sigma=1}^{2}\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\left(c^{(\sigma)}_{+,k}\ket{E=-\epsilon_{k}}_{C}+c^{(\sigma)}_{-,k}\ket{E=+\epsilon_{k}}_{C}\right)\otimes\ket{p=-p_{k}}_{R}\otimes\ket{p_{k},\sigma}_{S}=\ket{\Psi_{+}}+\ket{\Psi_{-}}

(148)

where both

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\ket{\Psi_{+}}=\sum_{\sigma=1}^{2}\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}c^{(\sigma)}_{+,k}\ket{E=-\epsilon_{k}}_{C}\otimes\ket{p=-p_{k}}_{R}\otimes\ket{p_{k},\sigma}_{S}\\
&\ket{\Psi_{-}}=\sum_{\sigma=1}^{2}\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}c^{(\sigma)}_{-,k}\ket{E=+\epsilon_{k}}_{C}\otimes\ket{p=-p_{k}}_{R}\otimes\ket{p_{k},\sigma}_{S}\end{aligned}\right.

(149)

independently satisfy the constraints. Projecting thus the state (
148
) onto the basis states
\{\ket{t}_{C}\}
,
\{\ket{x}_{R}\}
,
\{\ket{y}_{S}\}
and assuming notation (
72
), we directly recover:

\begin{split}\psi(\xi,t)&=\braket{t,x,y|\Psi}=\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\left(b_{k}u_{+,k}(\xi,t)+d^{*}_{k}u_{-,k}(\xi,t)\right)\\
&=\frac{1}{\sqrt{L}}\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\left(b_{k}u_{+,k}e^{i(p_{k}\xi-\epsilon_{k}t)}+d^{*}_{k}u_{-,k}e^{i(p_{k}\xi+\epsilon_{k}t)}\right)\end{split}

(150)

where the coefficients of (
148
) and (
149
) are given by

\begin{pmatrix}c^{(1)}_{+,k}\\
c^{(2)}_{+,k}\end{pmatrix}=\frac{b_{k}}{\sqrt{L}}u_{+,k},\quad\begin{pmatrix}c^{(1)}_{-,k}\\
c^{(2)}_{-,k}\end{pmatrix}=\frac{d^{*}_{k}}{\sqrt{L}}u_{-,k}

(151)

with
u_{+,k}
,
u_{-,k}
defined in (
99
) and (
101
). Then, after the identification
u_{k}(\xi,t)\equiv u_{+,k}(\xi,t)
and
v_{k}(\xi,t)\equiv u_{-,-k}(\xi,t)
, the state (
150
) becomes

\psi(\xi,t)=\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\left(b_{k}u_{k}(\xi,t)+d^{*}_{k}v_{k}(\xi,t)\right)\\
=\frac{1}{\sqrt{L}}\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\left(b_{k}u_{k}e^{i(p_{k}\xi-\epsilon_{k}t)}+d^{*}_{k}v_{k}e^{-i(p_{k}\xi-\epsilon_{k}t)}\right)

(152)

where
u_{k}\equiv u_{+,k}
,
v_{k}\equiv u_{-,-k}
are defined in (
107
) and satisfy
u^{\dagger}_{k}u_{k}=v^{\dagger}_{k}v_{k}=1
, which leads to relations (
108
), together with all the other mathematical properties listed at the end of the previous Section.

Starting from (
152
), we can now promote:

\psi(\xi,t),\psi^{*}(\xi,t)\longrightarrow\hat{\psi}(\xi,t),\hat{\psi}^{\dagger}(\xi,t)

(153)

where
\hat{\psi}(\xi,t)
and
\hat{\psi}^{\dagger}(\xi,t)
are given by

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\hat{\psi}(\xi,t)=\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\left(\hat{b}_{k}u_{k}(\xi,t)+\hat{d}^{\dagger}_{k}v_{k}(\xi,t)\right)\\
&\hat{\psi}^{\dagger}(\xi,t)=\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\left(\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{k}u^{\dagger}_{k}(\xi,t)+\hat{d}_{k}v^{\dagger}_{k}(\xi,t)\right).\end{aligned}\right.

(154)

For these fields operators we can require the fermionic anti-commutation relations:

\begin{split}&\left\{\hat{\psi}_{\alpha}(\xi,t),\hat{\psi}^{\dagger}_{\beta}(\xi^{\prime},t)\right\}=\delta_{\alpha,\beta}\delta(\xi-\xi^{\prime})\\
&\left\{\hat{\psi}_{\alpha}(\xi,t),\hat{\psi}_{\beta}(\xi^{\prime},t)\right\}=\left\{\hat{\psi}^{\dagger}_{\alpha}(\xi,t),\hat{\psi}^{\dagger}_{\beta}(\xi^{\prime},t)\right\}=0\end{split}

(155)

where the indices
\alpha
and
\beta
denote the spinor components of the field. From (
155
), (
109
), and using the property
u_{k}u^{\dagger}_{k}+v_{-k}v^{\dagger}_{-k}=\mathbb{1}
, one can easily verify that:

\begin{split}&\{\hat{a}_{n},\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{k}\}=\{\hat{b}_{n},\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{k}\}=\delta_{n,k}\\
&\{\hat{a}_{n},\hat{a}_{k}\}=\{\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{n},\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{k}\}=\{\hat{b}_{n},\hat{b}_{k}\}=\{\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{n},\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{k}\}=0.\end{split}

(156)

We now introduce the Hamiltonian density
greinerfields
:

\hat{h}_{\text{eff}}=\hat{\psi}^{\dagger}(\xi,t)\left(-i\sigma_{1}\frac{\partial}{\partial\xi}+m\sigma_{3}\right)\hat{\psi}(\xi,t)

(157)

and, defining
E_{0}=-\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\epsilon_{k}
, we write

\hat{H}_{\text{eff}}=\int_{-L/2}^{L/2}d\xi\>\hat{h}_{\text{eff}}-E_{0}.

(158)

Thanks to the fact that
u_{k}(\xi,t)
and
v_{k}(\xi,t)
are eigenvectors of the operator
\hat{H}_{D}=-i\sigma_{1}\frac{\partial}{\partial\xi}+m\sigma_{3}
with eigenvalues
\epsilon_{k}
and
-\epsilon_{k}
, respectively, and using the properties (
108
) and
u^{\dagger}_{k}v_{-k}=v^{\dagger}_{k}u_{-k}=0
, one easily obtains:

\begin{split}\hat{H}_{\text{eff}}&=\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\epsilon_{k}\left(\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{k}\hat{b}_{k}-\hat{d}_{k}\hat{d}^{\dagger}_{k}\right)-E_{0}\\
&=\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\epsilon_{k}\left(\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{k}\hat{b}_{k}+\hat{d}^{\dagger}_{k}\hat{d}_{k}\right)\end{split}

(159)

where
\hat{H}_{\text{eff}}
is again written as a sum over particle and antiparticle number operators, just as in the Klein–Gordon case, reflecting the intrinsic symmetry between positive- and negative-energy modes in the relativistic theory.

Similarly, for the momentum operator we find:

\begin{split}\hat{P}_{\text{eff}}&=\int_{-L/2}^{L/2}d\xi\>\hat{\psi}^{\dagger}(\xi,t)\left(-i\frac{\partial}{\partial\xi}\right)\hat{\psi}(\xi,t)\\
&=\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}p_{k}\left(\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{k}\hat{b}_{k}+\hat{d}^{\dagger}_{k}\hat{d}_{k}\right).\end{split}

(160)

This concludes our formulation of second quantization across the three dynamical cases considered in this work.

VII
Conclusions

In this work, we have derived the standard wave equations—the Schrödinger, Klein-Gordon and Dirac equations—as emerging from global energy and momentum constraints in a fully relational quantum Universe. Building on the framework where both time and space are defined with respect to internal quantum reference frames, we have shown how the dynamics of conditional states naturally reproduce the known quantum field equations.

In particular, we considered a Universe composed of a quantum clock
C
, a reference particle
R
, and a system
S
. For the Schrödinger case, we derived the correct wave equation both for the system
S
alone, under the assumption that the motion of
R
can be neglected, and for the composite system
R+S
when the kinetic energy of
R
is taken into account. We also included an interaction potential depending on the distance between the system and the reference particle, illustrating how such interactions can be naturally incorporated into the formalism. For the Klein-Gordon and Dirac cases, we focused on the regime in which the energy of the reference particle
R
can be neglected, and we studied the corresponding wave equations for the spin-
0
and spin-
1/2
particles
S
. The extension to the joint study of
R+S
Klein-Gordon and Dirac systems is left for future work, as it involves technical complications that go beyond the scope of the present paper.
Finally, we showed how the formalism of second quantization can be naturally implemented within our framework.
We found that, for the Klein–Gordon case, a subtle technical peculiarity arises due to the second-order time derivative in the wave equation, together with our choice of a non-quadratic energy constraint.

In conclusion, our results strengthen the idea that fundamental quantum dynamics may emerge from global constraints and entanglement, rather than being imposed on a pre-existing spacetime background. Appendix A
Massless Dirac field and chirality

In this Appendix, we consider the massless limit of the Dirac field in
1+1
dimensions and examine the structure of its positive-energy solutions. Particular emphasis is placed on the emergence of chiral components and their physical interpretation within our relational framework, where the relevant coordinate is the relative position
\xi=y-x
, and the system effectively acquires a circular geometry (as the wave function repeats cyclically under translations by integer multiples of
L
).

In the massless case, the energy constraint (
68
), reduces to:

\left(\hat{H}_{C}+\hat{P}_{S}\sigma_{1}\right)\ket{\Psi}\approx 0

(161)

which, upon projecting onto the bases
\{\ket{t}_{C}\}
,
\{\ket{x}_{R}\}
,
\{\ket{y}_{S}\}
, and applying the momentum constraint, yields:

i\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\ket{\psi(\xi,t)}_{\sigma}\approx-i\frac{\partial}{\partial\xi}\sigma_{1}\ket{\psi(\xi,t)}_{\sigma}.

(162)

Equation (
162
) shows the massless Dirac equation for the system
S
expressed in terms of the relative coordinate
\xi
, retaining the two-component spinor structure labeled by the index
\sigma
. Using notation (
72
) and introducing
\gamma^{0}=\sigma_{3}
and
\gamma^{1}=i\sigma_{2}
, equation (
162
) can be recast in the form:

i\left(\gamma^{0}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}+\gamma^{1}\frac{\partial}{\partial\xi}\right)\psi(\xi,t)\approx 0.

(163)

We derive again the solution of equations (
162
) and (
163
) directly from the constraints. As the derivation closely mirrors the massive case discussed in the main text, we only highlight the key steps specific to the massless limit. The global state satisfying both the energy and momentum constraints can be written:

\ket{\Psi_{\pm}}=\sum_{\sigma=1}^{2}\sum_{k=-N_{S}}^{N_{S}}c^{(\sigma)}_{\pm,k}\ket{E=\mp|p_{k}|}_{C}\ket{p=-p_{k}}_{R}\ket{p_{k},\sigma}_{S}

(165)

As metioned, we focus on the positive-energy solution. Using again notation (
72

\begin{split}\psi_{+}(\xi,t)&=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=-N_{S}\end{subarray}}^{N_{S}}b_{k}u_{k}(\xi,t)\\
&=\frac{1}{\sqrt{L}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=-N_{S}\end{subarray}}^{N_{S}}b_{k}u_{k}e^{i(p_{k}\xi-|p_{k}|t)}\end{split}

(166)

where
\begin{pmatrix}c^{(1)}_{+,k}\\

We now examine the chiral structure of these solutions. We define the chirality operator as:

\gamma^{5}\equiv\gamma^{0}\gamma^{1}=\sigma_{1}

(168)

which has eigenvalues
\pm 1
.
The operator
\gamma^{5}

\left(\gamma^{5}\right)^{2}=1
,
\{\gamma^{5},\gamma^{0}\}=0
and
\{\gamma^{5},\gamma^{1}\}=0

The eigenvectors of

\chi_{R}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{pmatrix}1\\

\chi_{L}&\text{if }p_{k}<0,\end{cases}

Right-chiral spinors propagate to the right, left-chiral spinors propagate to the left, both with speed

In our formalism, the wave function
\psi_{+}(\xi,t)

S

Within this geometry, a chiral wave packet does not “escape to infinity” but instead propagates around the “relational circle”. Nevertheless, each mode has a well-defined group velocity (determined by the sign of

Therefore, chirality in this context is naturally interpreted as the “sense of rotation” around the circle: right-chiral spinors (

This interpretation is fully relational: chirality encodes whether the system rotates clockwise or counterclockwise along the relational circle. It is defined solely from the structure of the relational wave function and does not rely on any external spacetime.

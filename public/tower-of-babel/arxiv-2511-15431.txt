Edge-spectral Turán theorems for color-critical graphs with applications
Yongtao Li, Hong Liu, Shengtong Zhang

Abstract
A classical result of Nosal asserts that every m m -edge graph with spectral radius λ ⁡ ( G ) > m \lambda(G)>\sqrt{m} contains a triangle. A celebrated extension of Nikiforov [ 35 ] states that if G G is an m m -edge graph with λ ⁡ ( G ) > ( 1 − 1 / r ) ​ 2 ​ m \lambda(G)>\sqrt{(1-\nicefrac{{1}}{{r}})2m} , then G G contains a clique K r + 1 K_{r+1} . This result implies the Turán theorem and Wilf theorem, and offers a new perspective on the existence of substructures. The edge-spectral conditions are versatile for enforcing substructures, as they can be applied to any sparse graph regardless of its edge density. In this paper, we prove that for any color-critical graph F F with chromatic number χ ⁡ ( F ) = r + 1 ≥ 4 \chi(F)=r+1\geq 4 , if m m is sufficiently large and G G is an F F -free graph with m m edges, then λ ⁡ ( G ) ≤ ( 1 − 1 / r ) ​ 2 ​ m \lambda(G)\leq\sqrt{(1-\nicefrac{{1}}{{r}})2m} , with equality if and only if G G is a regular complete r r -partite graph. This settles an open problem proposed by Yu and Li [ 52 ] and also gives spectral bounds for graphs forbidding books and wheels. Secondly, we establish an asymptotic formula and structural characterization when we forbid an almost-bipartite graph F F , where F F is called almost-bipartite if it can be made bipartite by removing at most one edge. As applications, we determine the unique m m -edge spectral extremal graph for every integer m m when avoiding certain substructures, including complete bipartite graphs plus an edge, cycles plus an edge, and theta graphs, etc. Our results resolve an open problem proposed by Li, Zhao and Zou [ 24 ] , as well as two conjectures posed by Liu and Li [ 31 ] . The arguments in our proofs are based on the edge-spectral stability method recently established by the authors. In addition, we develop some new spectral techniques, including the stability result for the Perron–Frobenius eigenvector.

Introduction

Extremal graph theory, a central branch of graph theory, examines the maximum possible size or structure of graphs under certain constraints. The field has deep ties to combinatorics, computer science, and additive number theory; see
[
56

The

An

The Turán graph

The well-known Turán theorem states that

-free graph,

,
with equality if and only if

e(G)\leq\left(1-\frac{1}{r}\right)\frac{n^{2}}{2}.

A celebrated extension of Turán’s theorem
is attributed to Erdős, Stone and Simonovits
[
10
,
9
]
.

Theorem 1.1

(Erdős–Stone–Simonovits
[
10
,
9
]
)
.

For any graph
F

\chi(F)=r+1\geq 2

if

e(G)\leq\left(1-\frac{1}{r}+o(1)\right)\frac{n^{2}}{2}
.

The

A_{G}=[a_{i,j}]_{i,j=1}^{n}

The

Since

The investigation of spectral graph theory significantly contributes to advancements in graph theory and combinatorics;

Spectral extremal graph theory studies the connections between structure of extremal graphs and eigenvalues of their associated matrices. This research area has led to natural spectral strengthenings of classical extremal graph theorems.
In 1986, Wilf

In 2009, Nikiforov

Theorem 1.2

(Nikiforov
[

]

For any graph

1.1
Edge-spectral extremal graph problem

In this paper, we study the spectral extremal results for graphs with given number of edges, instead of the number of vertices. For simplicity, we usually ignore the possible isolated vertices if there are no confusions. It is well-known that
\lambda(G)<\sqrt{2m}
for every graph
G
with
m
edges.
There is a rich history to the problem of characterizing graphs that attain the maximal spectral radius for a given number of edges.
Tracing back to 1985, Brualdi and Hoffman
[
3
]
proved that if
G
has
m\leq{k\choose 2}
edges for some integer
k\geq 2
, then
\lambda(G)\leq k-1
. This result was extended by Stanley
[
48
]
who showed that
\lambda(G)\leq\frac{1}{2}\left(\sqrt{8m+1}-1\right)
, with equality if and only if
m={k\choose 2}
and
G
is the complete graph
K_{k}
.
We refer to
[
16
,
18
]
for related results.

The above-mentioned bounds can be significantly improved for graphs with bounded clique number.
In 2002, Nikiforov
[
35
]
showed that if
G
is a
K_{r+1}
-free graph with
m
edges, then

\lambda^{2}(G)\leq{\left(1-\frac{1}{r}\right)2m}.

(1)

The extremal graphs attaining the equality in (
1
) are complete bipartite graphs for
r=2
, or uniquely a regular complete
r
-partite graph for
r\geq 3
; see
[
36
]
for details.

The result (
1
) implies both Turán’s bound and Wilf’s bound by using
\lambda(G)\geq\frac{2m}{n}
. So (
1
) can be viewed as a robust spectral extension on Turán’s theorem.
We note that the edge-spectral assumption offers greater applicability and versatility: the edge-spectral version not only implies the vertex-spectral version, but can also be applied to sparse graphs of any edge density. For example, in the case
r=2
, setting
G=K_{2}\vee\frac{m-1}{2}K_{1}
, the join of
K_{2}
with
\frac{m-1}{2}
isolated vertices, we see that
G
is a sparse graph with
\lambda(G)>\sqrt{m}
and
G
has many triangles, but
\lambda(G)\ll n/2
and
e(G)\ll n^{2}/4
.
In light of the above difference and advantage,
it generated great interest in studying the maximum spectral radius for
F
-free graphs with a given number of edges.

In 2009, Nikiforov
[
37
]
showed that if

is an
m
-edge graph with
\lambda(G)>\sqrt{m}
, then

contains not only a triangle, but also a

-cycle. In 2023, Ning and Zhai
[
43

studied the spectral supersaturation for triangles and proved that such a graph

has at least
\lfloor\frac{\sqrt{m}-1}{2}\rfloor
triangles. Solving a problem of Ning and Zhai
[
44
]
, the authors
[
26
]
proved that

also has at least
(\frac{1}{8}-o(1))m^{2}
copies of
C_{4}
.
Moreover, the authors
[
26
]
extended that if
\lambda^{2}(G)>(1-\frac{1}{r})2m
, then there are
\Omega_{r}(m^{\frac{r-1}{2}})
copies of

in

Recently, the authors
[
27
]
established an asymptotic formula for any general graph

, and provided a unified extension of Theorems

and

.

Theorem 1.3

(Li–Liu–Zhang
[
27
]
)
.

For any graph
F

-free graph with

\lambda^{2}(G)\leq\left(1-\frac{1}{r}+o(1)\right)2m.

Motivated by the aforementioned result of Brualdi and Hoffman, it is interesting to consider the following spectral Turán type extremal problem for

Given a graph

The investigation of Problem

[
54
,
25
,
32

[

4

The previously known approach to Problem

The main contribution of this paper is to develop a new method, which we term

1.2
Color-critical graphs

A graph
F
is called
color-critical
if it contains an edge whose deletion reduces its chromatic number. Many graphs are color-critical, including e.g. complete graphs and odd cycles.
There have been extensive research on extremal problems concerning color-critical graphs
[
47
,
34
,
45
,
46
,
53
,
22
]
.
Simonovits
[
47
]
proved that
if
F
is color-critical and
\chi(F)=r+1
,
then the Turán graph

is the unique extremal graph for

[
39
]
provided a spectral extension by showing that

is also the unique spectral extremal graph for

.

As the first main result, we solve Problem
1.4
for all color-critical graphs.
We obtain an edge-spectral counterpart of Simonovits’ theorem and Nikiforov’s theorem.
Apart from cliques, our result provides the first solution to Problem
1.4
when
\chi(F)\geq 4
,

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
F
be a color-critical graph with
\chi(F)=r+1\geq 4
. For sufficiently large

, if

is an

-free graph with

edges, then

\lambda^{2}(G)\leq\left(1-\frac{1}{r}\right)2m,

with equality if and only if
G
is a regular complete
r
-partite graph.

Theorem
1.5
settles an open problem proposed by Yu and Li
[
52
, Problem 5]
.

Remark.

The bound of Theorem

does not hold for color-critical graphs

.
For example, setting

-free,

In addition, taking

showed that every

The case where

The generalized book

The case

For

Li, Liu and Zhai

studied the edge-spectral problem for graphs forbidding the family of all wheels.
As an application of Theorem

Note that

For fixed

1.3
Almost-bipartite graphs

A graph
F
is called
almost-bipartite
if it can be made bipartite by removing at most one edge. Equivalently,
F
is color-critical with
\chi(F)=3
or bipartite.
Using the edge-spectral stability method, we establish an asymptotic formula for Problem
1.4
when we forbid an almost-bipartite graph
F
.
As applications, we completely determine the
m
-edge spectral extremal graphs for forbidding classical graphs, including complete bipartite graphs plus an edge, cycles plus an edge, and theta graphs.
Consequently, we generalize a series of results in
[
54
,
25
,
24
,
32
]
, and settle several problems and conjectures in
[
24
,
31
]
.
This significantly extends our knowledge of the Brualdi–Hoffman–Turán problem.
To state our results, we need to fix some notation.

Definition
.

For an almost-bipartite graph
F
, let
\mathcal{A}_{F}
be the family of subgraphs induced by
\overline{I}:=V(F)\setminus I
, where
I
ranges over the maximal independent sets of
F
. Let
\mathcal{M}_{F}
be the family of graphs that do not contain a subgraph isomorphic to any member of
\mathcal{A}_{F}
.

The graphs in
\mathcal{A}_{F}
and
\mathcal{M}_{F}
may consist of some isolated vertices.
For example, if
F=K_{s,t}
with
s\leq t
,
then
\mathcal{A}_{F}=\{sK_{1},tK_{1}\}
, and
\mathcal{M}_{F}
consists of graphs of order less than
s
.
If
F=K_{s,t}^{+}
, which is the graph obtained by adding an edge to the partite set of size
s
of
K_{s,t}
,
then
\mathcal{A}_{F}=\{K_{2}\cup(s-2)K_{1},K_{1,t}\}
,
and
\mathcal{M}_{F}
consists of empty graphs of any order and non-empty graphs of order less than
s
.

In what follows,
we show a structural characterization of extremal graphs for Problem
1.4
when we forbid an almost-bipartite graph
F
.
An
m
-edge graph
G
is called a
spectral extremal graph
for
F
if
G
achieves the maximal spectral radius over all
m
-edge
F
-free graphs. We say that a vertex
v
is
complete
to a set
A
if
v
is adjacent to all vertices of
A
.

Theorem 1.8
.

For any almost-bipartite graph
F
that is not a star,
if
m
is sufficiently large and
G
is an
m
-edge spectral extremal graph for
F
,
then one of the following holds:

(a)

G
is a complete bipartite graph.

(b)

G
has a vertex partition
A\sqcup C
such that
|A|<|F|
and
G[A]
is a non-empty graph of
\mathcal{M}_{F}
,
C
is an independent set, and all but at most one vertex of
C
are complete to
A
.

Remark.

If
F
is a star
K_{1,t}
, then the
F
-free graph
G

satisfies
\Delta(G)\leq t-1
, which gives
\lambda(G)\leq t-1
, with one of the extremal graphs being the disjoint union of copies of
K_{t}
.

Theorem
1.8
gives an alternative proof of a conjecture proposed by Zhai, Lin and Shu
[
54
, Conjecture 5.2]
on books, which was confirmed by Nikiforov
[
42
]
and the authors
[
26
]
in a strong sense.
Indeed, if
F
is a book
B_{k}
, then
\mathcal{A}_{F}=\{K_{2},K_{1,k}\}
and
each graph of
\mathcal{M}_{F}
is empty. Applying Theorem
1.8
, we see that
the
m
-edge spectral extremal graphs for
B_{k}
are complete bipartite graphs.
Conversely, assume that the
m
-edge spectral extremal graphs for
F
are complete bipartite graphs, then the extremal spectral radius equals to
\sqrt{m}
, so

F
is a subgraph of
B_{k}
for some
k
. Otherwise, if
F
is not a subgraph of any book, then
G:=K_{2}\vee\frac{m-1}{2}K_{1}
is an
F
-free graph with
\lambda(G)>\sqrt{m}
, a contradiction. So
F
must be a subgraph of a book.

Our second main result is to prove an asymptotic result for almost-bipartite graphs.

Theorem 1.9
.

Let
F
be an almost-bipartite graph that is not a star. If
m
is sufficiently large and

G
is an
m
-edge
F
-free graph with maximal spectral radius, then

\lambda(G)=\sqrt{m}+\max_{M\in\mathcal{M}_{F}}\frac{e(M)}{v(M)}+O(m^{-1/2}).

Furthermore, there is a partition
V(G)=A\sqcup C
such that
G[A]
is isomorphic to
M
that achieves the maximum above,
C
is independent, and all but at most one vertex of
C
are complete to
A
.

The bound of Theorem
1.3
does not hold for any bipartite graph
F
, unless
F
is a star.
The bound of Theorem
1.5
does not hold for color-critical graphs
F
with
\chi(F)=3
.
To some extent,
Theorem
1.9
can be viewed as an intriguing supplement of Theorems
1.3
and
1.5
.

We present an interesting example where determining
\max_{M\in\mathcal{M}_{F}}\frac{e(M)}{v(M)}
reduces to a challenging graph problem. This shows that when we forbid a general bipartite graph
F
, the structure of the edge-spectral extremal graph in Theorem
1.9
can be quite non-trivial.

Example 1.10
.

Pick
s\geq 2
and
n>2s
. Let
F
be the graph with vertex sets
V=I_{1}\sqcup I_{2}\sqcup I_{3}\sqcup I_{4}
where
I_{1}=I_{4}=[n+1-s]
and
I_{2}=I_{3}=[s]
, and putting edges between each pair of vertices in
I_{i}\times I_{i+1}
for every
i\in\{1,2,3\}
. Then
\mathcal{A}_{F}=\{(n+1)K_{1},K_{s,s}\}
. So
\mathcal{M}_{F}
consists of all
K_{s,s}
-free graphs with at most
n
vertices. Finding the maximizer
M\in\mathcal{M}_{F}
reduces to determining the Turán number
\mathrm{ex}(n,K_{s,s})
, which is a well-known difficult open problem.

Let
I
be a maximum independent set of
F
with
\lvert\bar{I}\rvert=k+1
. If
F
is an almost-bipartite graph, then
F[\bar{I}]
contains at most one edge, then a non-empty graph
M
lies in
\mathcal{M}_{F}
if and only if
M
has at most
k
vertices. So
K_{k}
is the unique subgraph
M\in\mathcal{M}_{F}
that maximizes the ratio
e(M)/v(M)
in Theorem
1.9
.
This observation will be applied in the next section.

1.4
Applications

We present several applications of Theorem
1.9
in this section.
We solve the
Brualdi–Hoffman–Turán problem for
classical almost-bipartite graphs, including complete bipartite graphs plus an edge, cycles plus an edge, and theta graphs, etc.
Our results significantly generalize a series of theorems in
[
54
,
25
,
11
,
24
,
32
]
.
The previous method gives the bound that can be achieved only for some special values of
m
.
In this paper, we determine the unique spectral extremal graph for every integer

.
To start with, we define the edge-spectral extremal graph as below.

Definition

(The split graph)
.

For each
1\leq k<m
, let
r,t
be positive integers such that
m-{k\choose 2}=kt+r
, where
0\leq r\leq k-1
.
Let
S_{k,m}
be an
m
-edge graph
obtained from
K_{k}\vee tK_{1}
by adding an extra vertex that has exactly

.

A computation shows

\lambda(S_{k,m})\leq\frac{1}{2}(k-1+\sqrt{4m-k^{2}+1})
, where the equality holds if and only if

, i.e.,
m={k\choose 2}+ks
for some integer

. Moreover, one can check that
\lambda(S_{k,m})\leq\lambda(S_{\ell,m})
provided by

and sufficiently large

.

1.4.1
Complete bipartite
graphs plus an edge

In 2021,
Zhai, Lin and Shu
[
54
]
proved that if
t\geq 2
and
m\geq 16t^{2}
, and
G
is an
m
-edge
K_{2,t+1}
-free graph, then
\lambda(G)\leq\sqrt{m}
, with equality if and only if

is a star. In this paper, we will greatly extend this result to any complete bipartite graphs.
Let
K_{k+1,t+1}^{+}
be the graph obtained from
K_{k+1,t+1}
by adding an edge to the vertex part of size
k+1
.

Theorem 1.11
.

Let
2\leq k\leq t
be fixed and
m
be sufficiently large.
If
G
is a
K_{k+1,t+1}^{+}
-free graph with
m

\lambda(G)\leq\lambda(S_{k,m})
,
where the equality holds if and only if

.

Proof.

We see that
K_{k+1,t+1}^{+}
is color-critical with chromatic number
\chi(K_{k+1,t+1})=3
.
Setting
F=K_{k+1,t+1}^{+}
,
it is easy to verify that
\mathcal{A}_{F}=\{K_{2}\cup(k-1)K_{1},K_{1,t+1}\}
.
So the graph in
\mathcal{M}_{F}
is either an empty graph of any order, or a non-empty graph with at most
k
vertices.
By Theorem
1.9
, taking
M=K_{k}
yields the desired spectral extremal graph.
∎

Remark.

The bound on
m
is super-polynomial with respect to

. Suppose on the contrary that Theorem
1.11
holds for

with some
C>0
and

.
For any
\varepsilon\in(0,0.01)
, we take
\delta:=\varepsilon^{10}C^{-1/p}>0
and

.
Then

and

, which implies that any
m
-edge graph

with
\lambda(G)\geq(1+\varepsilon)\sqrt{m}
contains a copy of

with

. This is impossible by a random construction; see
[
27
, Theorem 6.3]
.

Zhai, Lin and Shu

studied the spectral problem for

-free and

-free graphs.
Let

, which was also selected by Liu and Ning

Let

be the theta graph obtained from internally disjoint paths of lengths

Observe that the bound (

[

In what follows, we give a sharp refinement on the results

Our result completely solves the problems in

Theorem 1.12
.

Let

be integers and

be sufficiently large. Suppose that

-free graph with

and

, where

(b)

\chi(\theta_{r,p,q})=2

Then
\lambda(G)\leq\lambda(S_{k,m})
,
where the equality holds if and only if

.

Proof.

For every

, at least one cycle
of

has an even length.
We can color such an even cycle with two colors, and then color the remaining path of

using at most one new color. So

is either bipartite or color-critical with

In case (a), we see that

Using Theorem

∎

To state our result, we need to introduce some notation. Let

Definition
.

Let

We obtain the following general theorem for bipartite graphs.

Theorem 1.14

Suppose that

If

\lambda(G)\leq\lambda(S_{\sigma(F)-1,m}),

Proof.

We denote
\sigma(F)=\sigma
and
\alpha(F)=\alpha
. By the definition, we see that

is a subgraph of

. Theorem
1.11
implies
\lambda(G)\leq\lambda(S_{\sigma-1,m})
. In addition, we
need to show that this bound can be achieved, that is, showing that

is

∎

A graph is called

A breakthrough of Tait and Tobin

is the unique graph with maximum spectral radius over all

In this paper, we investigate the edge-spectral Turán problem for planar graphs.
The edge-spectral extremal problem has a different phenomenon from the vertex-spectral problem.
It is well-known that if

is planar,
then

is

-free. Consequently, Theorem
1.14
implies

[

if it has a drawing in the plane

such that each edge of

is crossed with at most

The vertex-spectral Turán problem for

-planar graphs was recently investigated by Zhang, Wang and Wang
[

]
.
There is no related spectral extremal result for

-planar graphs with

.
In this paper, we provide the first such result for
k
-planar graphs. As a warm-up,
we observe that every

-planar graph is

-free, since

is not

We now extend this observation to

Theorem 1.15
.

For any fixed

and sufficiently large

, if

is a
k
-planar graph with

\lambda(G)\leq\lambda(S_{2,m})
,
where the equality holds if and only if

.

Proof.

Recall that the crossing number
\mathrm{cr}(G)
of a graph

is the minimum number of the total crossings of edges in a plane drawing of

. A well-known result of

[

states that
\mathrm{cr}(K_{3,t})=\lfloor\frac{t}{2}\rfloor\cdot\lfloor\frac{t-1}{2}\rfloor
. Consequently, for any

, there exists an integer

such that

is not
k
-planar for every

. Then any

-planar graph

is

-free for every

. Using Theorem

, we get
\lambda(G)\leq\lambda(S_{2,m})
, and the equality holds if and only if

.
∎

Our approach.

Our proof of Theorem
1.5
relies on
the edge-spectral stability method (Theorem
2.1
), which we expect would become a standard tool for solving the Brualdi–Hoffman–Turán problem.
Suppose that
G
is an
m
-edge
F
-free graph with maximal spectral radius. Firstly, we show that
G
is (in edit-distance) close to a complete partite graph
H=K_{U_{1},\ldots,U_{r}}
; see Claims
3.10
and
3.11
. We summarize the remaining arguments to Theorem
3.1
.
We show that any possible imperfection on the partition of
H
leads to a copy of the forbidden substructure
F
. For example, every vertex of
G
can not have large neighbors in all partite sets of
H
; see Claim
3.3
. The second step is to find a large
r
-partite subgraph
G^{\prime}
of
G
with
m^{\prime}
edges. To do this, we construct
r
pairwise disjoint independent sets in
G
, say
V_{1},\ldots,V_{r}
; see Claim
3.4
.
Let
S
be the set of vertices outside
V_{1},\ldots,V_{r}
.
Thirdly, we bound the coordinates of the Perron–Frobenius vector of
G
, and show that the vertices of
S
contribute a small amount to the spectral radius of
G
. More precisely, we denote
A:=(1-\frac{1}{r})2m^{\prime},B:=(1-\frac{1}{r})2(m-m^{\prime})
and
X:=\sum_{v\in V(G^{\prime})}x_{v}^{2}
. Then we prove that
\sum_{uv\in E(G^{\prime})}2x_{u}x_{v}\leq\sqrt{A}\cdot X
and
\sum_{v\in S}x_{v}\sum_{w\in N(v)}\beta_{w}x_{w}\leq\sqrt{(1-X)\cdot 2BX}
, where
\beta_{w}:=1
if
w\in S
; and
\beta_{w}:=2
if
w\notin S
. Consequently, we get
\lambda(G)\leq\sqrt{A}X+\sqrt{B\cdot 2X(1-X)}\leq\sqrt{A+B}
; see Claim
3.5
– Claim
3.9
.
This can be regarded as a crucial step in our proof. In this step, we need to establish some new spectral techniques, including a stability result for the Perron–Frobenius vectors; see Lemma
2.2
. Generally speaking, the Perron–Frobenius vector of

is quite close to that of
T_{n,r}
in the

-norm whenever

has edit distance
o(m)
with
T_{n,r}
.

For Theorem
1.8
, suppose that

is an almost-bipartite graph and

is an

-edge

-free graph with maximum spectral radius. First of all, we see that

is either empty or a non-empty graph with fewer than

vertices. Moreover, for a graph

and an integer

is

-free.
Applying the edge-spectral stability in Theorem

, we show that

contains two disjoint vertex sets

such that

and

. Moreover, we show that

and

; see Claim
4.3
.
Using the stability for eigenvectors, we obtain

for every vertex

.
We point out that

is not necessarily a partition of vertices of

We show that

With aid of the structural characterization in Theorem

For a vertex set
C
, we write
T_{C,r}

for an
r
-partite Turán graph on
C
.
The following stability result of Theorem
1.3
was recently established by the authors
[
27
]
.

Theorem 2.1

(Edge-spectral stability theorem
[
27
]
)
.

Let
F
be a graph with
\chi(F)=r+1\geq 3
.
For every
\varepsilon>0
,
there exist
\delta>0
and
m_{0}
depending only on
F
and
\varepsilon
such that the following holds. If
G
is an
F
-free graph with
m\geq m_{0}
edges and
\lambda^{2}(G)\geq(1-\frac{1}{r}-\delta)2m
, then

(a)

If
r=2
, then there exist disjoint vertex sets
A,B\subseteq V(G)
such that
d(G,K_{A,B})\leq\varepsilon m
.

(b)

If
r\geq 3
, then there exist a vertex set
C\subseteq V(G)
and an
r
-partite Turán graph
T_{C,r}
on
C
such that
d(G,T_{C,r})\leq\varepsilon m
.

We mention that the edge-spectral stability above is stronger than the classical Erdős–Simonovits stability theorem
[
47
]
and its vertex-spectral version due to Nikiforov
[
40
]
.
Such an edge-spectral stability result is interesting in its own right, but somewhat surprisingly it is often a useful stepping stone in proving an exact result when we forbid a specific substructure.

The following lemma shows that for graphs that are close in the edit-distance, their Perron–Frobenius eigenvectors are close in the
\ell_{2}
-norm.

Lemma 2.2

(Stability of Perron–Frobenius eigenvector)
.

Let
G
and
H
be two graphs on the same vertex set
V
such that
d(G,H)\leq e
. Let
\bm{x}
and
\bm{y}
be the unit Perron-Frobenius eigenvectors of
G
and
H
, respectively. Then

\sum_{v\in V}(x_{v}-y_{v})^{2}\leq\frac{8\sqrt{e}}{\lambda_{1}(H)-\lambda_{2}(H)}

(5)

and

\sum_{v\in V}|x_{v}^{2}-y_{v}^{2}|\leq\frac{8e^{1/4}}{\sqrt{\lambda_{1}(H)-\lambda_{2}(H)}}.

(6)

Remark.

In this paper, we always set
H
to be a complete multipartite graph that is obtained from Theorem
2.1
. In this case, it follows that
\lambda_{2}(H)=0
and
\lambda_{1}(H)\geq\sqrt{e(H)}
.

Proof.

As
\bm{x}
realizes the spectral radius of
A_{G}
, we have

\bm{x}^{T}A_{G}\bm{x}\geq\bm{y}^{T}A_{G}\bm{y}
. Note that

\bm{x}^{T}A_{G}\bm{x}=\sum_{ij\in E(G)}2x_{i}x_{j}=\sum_{ij\in E(H)}2x_{i}x_{j}-\sum_{ij\in E(H)\setminus E(G)}2x_{i}x_{j}+\sum_{ij\in E(G)\setminus E(H)}2x_{i}x_{j}.

Since
E(G)\setminus E(H)
has at most
e
edges, by Brualdi–Hoffman’s bound, we have

\bm{x}^{T}A_{G}\bm{x}\leq\bm{x}^{T}A_{H}\bm{x}+\sqrt{2e}
. Similarly, we have
\bm{y}^{T}A_{H}\bm{y}\leq\bm{y}^{T}A_{G}\bm{y}+\sqrt{2e}
. Then

\bm{x}^{T}A_{H}\bm{x}+\sqrt{2e}\geq\bm{x}^{T}A_{G}\bm{x}\geq\bm{y}^{T}A_{G}\bm{y}\geq\bm{y}^{T}A_{H}\bm{y}-\sqrt{2e}.

Consequently, we get

\bm{x}^{T}A_{H}\bm{x}\geq\bm{y}^{T}A_{H}\bm{y}-4\sqrt{e}=\lambda_{1}(H)-4\sqrt{e}.

(7)

Let
\bm{y}_{1},\bm{y}_{2},\ldots,\bm{y}_{n}
be the orthogonal unit eigenvectors of
A_{H}
corresponding to
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\ldots,\lambda_{n}
, where we write
\bm{y}_{1}=\bm{y}
. Then

\lVert\bm{x}\rVert_{2}^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\langle\bm{x},\bm{y}_{i}\rangle^{2}=1
.
Using the spectral decomposition of
A_{H}
,
we have
A_{H}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}\cdot\bm{y}_{i}\bm{y}_{i}^{T}
.
Thus, it follows that

\displaystyle\bm{x}^{T}A_{H}\bm{x}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}\cdot\langle\bm{x},\bm{y}_{i}\rangle^{2}\leq\lambda_{1}\cdot\langle\bm{x},\bm{y}\rangle^{2}+\lambda_{2}\cdot(1-\langle\bm{x},\bm{y}\rangle^{2}).

(8)

Combining (
7
) with (
8
), we conclude that

1-\langle\bm{x},\bm{y}\rangle^{2}\leq\frac{4\sqrt{e}}{\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}}.

Finally, note that
\langle\bm{x},\bm{y}\rangle\in[0,1]
. Thus, we obtain the first inequality (
5
) by

\sum_{v\in V}(x_{v}-y_{v})^{2}=\langle\bm{x}-\bm{y},\bm{x}-\bm{y}\rangle=2(1-\langle\bm{x},\bm{y}\rangle)\leq 2(1-\langle\bm{x},\bm{y}\rangle^{2}).

To show (
6
), we apply the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality

\sum_{v\in V}|x_{v}^{2}-y_{v}^{2}|\leq\left(\sum_{v\in V}(x_{v}-y_{v})^{2}\right)^{1/2}\left(\sum_{v\in V}(x_{v}+y_{v})^{2}\right)^{1/2}\leq 2\left(\sum_{v\in V}(x_{v}-y_{v})^{2}\right)^{1/2}

as
(x_{v}+y_{v})^{2}\leq 2(x_{v}^{2}+y_{v}^{2})
. The conclusion then follows from the first inequality (
5
).
∎

Recall that
K_{r}[t]
is the
t
-blow-up of
K_{r}
, which is the complete
r
-partite graph with each part having
t
vertices. We write
K_{V_{1},\ldots,V_{r}}
for the complete
r
-partite graph with vertex parts
V_{1},\ldots,V_{r}
.
We need the following lemma to find a large copy of
K_{r}[t]
in a dense
r
-partite graph.

Lemma 2.3
.

For every
t
and
r\geq 2
, there is some constant
\delta_{t,r}>0
such that the following holds. Suppose that
G
is an
r
-partite graph with partite sets
V_{1},\dots,V_{r}
such that
\lvert V_{i}\rvert\geq t
for each
i
and
\lvert V_{i}\rvert
has the same size when
r\geq 3
. If

e(K_{V_{1},\dots,V_{r}})-e(G)\leq\delta_{t,r}\cdot\min_{i\neq j}\lvert V_{i}\rvert\lvert V_{j}\rvert,

then
G
contains a copy of the complete
r
-partite graph
K_{r}[t]
.

The proof of Lemma
2.3
proceeds using the standard probabilistic method.

Proof.

We take
\delta_{t,r}:=t^{-2}r^{-2}
and sample a
t
-element subset
V_{i}^{\prime}:=\{v_{i,1},\dots,v_{i,t}\}
from each
V_{i}
uniformly at random. For two sampled vertices
v_{i,a}
and
v_{j,b}
from different parts
V_{i}
and
V_{j}
, respectively, the probability that there is no edge between
v_{i,a}
and
v_{j,b}
is at most

\frac{e(K_{V_{1},\dots,V_{r}})-e(G)}{\lvert V_{i}\rvert\lvert V_{j}\rvert}\leq\delta_{t,r}.

By the union bound, the probability that
G[V_{1}^{\prime},\dots,V_{r}^{\prime}]
is not isomorphic to
K_{r}[t]
is at most

\binom{r}{2}\cdot t^{2}\cdot\delta_{t,r}<1
.
There exists a sampling of
V_{i}^{\prime}
from each

such that
G[V_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,V_{r}^{\prime}]
forms a copy of

. So

contains a copy of
K_{r}[t]
, as desired.
∎

The following lemma is folklore.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
T_{n,r}
be the
r
-partite Turán graph on
n
vertices. Then

\left(1-\frac{1}{r}\right)\frac{n^{2}}{2}-\frac{r}{8}\leq e(T_{n,r})\leq\left(1-\frac{1}{r}\right)\frac{n^{2}}{2}

Proof.

We denote
t:=\lfloor n/r\rfloor
and write

n=rt+s
for some integer
0\leq s<r
. Then
T_{n,r}
has
s
partite sets of size
t+1
, and
r-s
partite sets of size
t
. It follows that

e(T_{n,r})={n\choose 2}-s{t+1\choose 2}-(r-s){t\choose 2}=\left(1-\frac{1}{r}\right)\frac{n^{2}}{2}-\frac{s(r-s)}{2r}.

By the AM-GM inequality, we have
s(r-s)\leq\frac{1}{4}r^{2}
. Thus, the desired lower bound holds.
∎

3
Color-critical graphs

Recall that the case
F=K_{r+1}
in Theorem
1.5
was proved by Nikiforov
[
35
]
, whose proof is based on the Motzkin–Straus inequality.
This inequality states that if

is an
n

-free graph, and

are non-negative weights of vertices of

,

\sum_{ij\in E}x_{i}x_{j}\leq\frac{1}{2}(1-\frac{1}{r})
.
However, his proof does not seem to apply to color-critical graphs, as the Motzkin-Straus inequality cannot be extended to

3.1
Forcing optimal partitions via approximate structures

Our proof of Theorem
1.5
proceeds via the edge-spectral stability in Theorem

together with a couple of observations when

is close to a complete
r
-partite graph.
Let

denote the graph formed by adding an edge to a partite set of the complete
r
-partite graph

. Clearly, if a graph

is a subgraph of

for some integer

for

Theorem 3.1
.

For every
t
and

, there are
\varepsilon=\varepsilon_{t,r}>0
and
n_{0}=n_{t,r}
such that the following holds. Suppose that

is a

-free graph with

is a complete

-partite graph where

and each partite set of

has at least
n_{0}
vertices, and

differs from

in at most
\varepsilon m
edges. Moreover, suppose that

is regular when

. Then

\lambda^{2}(G)\leq\left(1-\frac{1}{r}\right)\cdot 2m,

where the equality holds if and only if

is a complete bipartite graph when

, or
G
is uniquely the regular complete

-partite graph when

.

Remark.
The statements of the cases

and

are slightly different, while the proofs are entirely identical. Theorem

will be used in our proofs of both Theorems

and
1.8

Proof.

In our argument, we take
\varepsilon_{t,r}=(40r)^{-8}\delta_{t,r}
and
n_{t,r}=8rt
, where
\delta_{t,r}=t^{-2}r^{-2}
is determined in Lemma
2.3
.
By the assumption,
H
is a complete
r
-partite graph. Let

be the partite sets of

.
In the sequel, we prove a sequence of claims for our purpose.

Claim 3.2
.

If
U_{i}^{\prime}
is a subset of
U_{i}
with
|U_{i}^{\prime}|\geq\frac{1}{8r}|U_{i}|
for every

, then there is a copy of the complete
r
-partite graph

in
G[U_{1}^{\prime},\dots,U_{r}^{\prime}]
.

Proof of claim.

We have
|{U_{i}^{\prime}}|\geq\frac{1}{8r}|U_{i}|\geq\frac{n_{t,r}}{8r}\geq t
. Since

, we have

e(K_{U_{1}^{\prime},\dots,U_{r}^{\prime}})-e(G[U_{1}^{\prime},\dots,U_{r}^{\prime}])\leq\varepsilon m.

Thus, by assumption for any
i\neq j
, we have

\displaystyle|U_{i}^{\prime}||{U_{j}^{\prime}}|

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{64r^{2}}|U_{i}||U_{j}|=\frac{1}{64r^{2}\binom{r}{2}}e(K_{U_{1},\dots,U_{r}})\geq\frac{1}{64r^{2}\binom{r}{2}}\cdot(1-\varepsilon)m

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{64r^{4}\varepsilon}(e(K_{U_{1}^{\prime},\dots,U_{r}^{\prime}})-e(G[U_{1}^{\prime},\dots,U_{r}^{\prime}])).

Applying Lemma
2.3
, we conclude that
G[U_{1}^{\prime},\dots,U_{r}^{\prime}]
contains a copy of
K_{r}[t]
.
∎

Claim 3.3
.

For any vertex
v\in V(G)
, there exists some
i\in[r]
such that
|N_{U_{i}}(v)|\leq\frac{1}{8r}|{U_{i}}|
.

Proof of claim.

Suppose on the contrary that
there exists a vertex
v\in V(G)
such that
|N_{U_{i}}(v)|>\frac{1}{8r}|U_{i}|
for every
i\in[r]
.
We denote by
U_{i}^{\prime}=N_{U_{i}}(v)
for every
i\in[r]
.
Then Claim
3.2
shows that the
r
-partite subgraph
G[U_{1}^{\prime},\dots,U_{r}^{\prime}]
contains a copy of
K_{r}[t]
. This copy, together with the vertex

, produces a copy of
K_{r}^{+}[t]
,
which is a contradiction.
∎

For each
v\in V(G)
, let
N_{U_{i}}(v)
denote the set of vertices of

that are adjacent to

in the graph

. In other words,

for every

.
The key idea is to define the following vertex sets.
For each

be the set of vertices defined as

V_{i}:=\left\{v\in V(G):|N_{U_{j}}(v)|\geq\frac{2}{3}|U_{j}|\text{\penalty\ for any $j\in[r]\setminus\{i\}$}\right\}.

Claim 3.4
.

The sets
V_{1},V_{2},\ldots,V_{r}
are pairwise disjoint independent sets of

.

Proof of claim.

We assume on the contrary that there exists a vertex
v\in V_{i}\cap V_{j}
for some

. By the definition of
V_{i}
, we know that
|N_{U_{k}}(v)|\geq\frac{2}{3}\lvert U_{k}\rvert
for any

.
Similarly, the definition of

implies that
|N_{U_{k}}(v)|\geq\frac{2}{3}|U_{k}|
for any

. In conclusion, we get
|N_{U_{k}}(v)|\geq\frac{1}{8r}|U_{k}|
for every

which contradicts with Claim

.

We suppose on the contrary that there exists an edge

in

for some index

. We denote

.
For other

.
Clearly, we have

. For each

|{U_{j}^{\prime}}|\geq|N_{U_{j}}(u)|+|N_{U_{j}}(u^{\prime})|-|{U_{j}}|\geq\frac{1}{3}|{U_{j}}|.

By Claim
3.2
, there is a copy of
K_{r}[t]
in

. Combining with the edge

in

, we find a copy of

in

, which is a contradiction. So

is an independent set of

.
∎

We denote by
\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{|V|}
the unit

.

Claim 3.5
.

Let

Proof of claim.

Recall that

In what follows, we apply Lemma

.
The graph

\bm{y}_{v}=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{\sqrt{r\lvert U_{i}\rvert}},&\text{if}\penalty\ v\in U_{i};\\

.

.
Recall that
V(G^{\prime})=V_{1}\cup\cdots\cup V_{r}=\overline{S}
.
The Rayleigh formula implies

\sum_{uv\in E(G^{\prime})}2x_{u}x_{v}\leq\lambda(G^{\prime})\cdot X\leq\sqrt{A}X.

∎

Claim 3.9
.

We have
\sum\limits_{v\in S}x_{v}\sum\limits_{w\in N(v)}\beta_{w}x_{w}\leq\sqrt{(1-X)\cdot 2BX}
.

Proof of claim.

By the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{v\in S}x_{v}\sum_{w\in N(v)}\beta_{w}x_{w}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sum_{v\in S}x_{v}^{2}\right)^{\!\!1/2}\left(\sum_{v\in S}\left(\sum_{w\in N(v)}\beta_{w}x_{w}\right)^{\!\!2}\right)^{\!\!1/2}\!\!\!\!

\displaystyle=(1-X)^{1/2}\left(\sum_{v\in S}\left(\sum_{w\in N(v)}\beta_{w}x_{w}\right)^{\!\!2}\right)^{\!\!1/2}.

To bound the right-hand side, we define the function
\gamma:V(G)\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
as below

\gamma_{w}:=\begin{cases}2,&\text{if $w\in S$;}\\
1,&\text{if $w\notin S$.}\end{cases}

By the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality again, for each
v\in S
, we have

\left(\sum_{w\in N(v)}\beta_{w}x_{w}\right)^{2}\leq\left(\sum_{w\in N(v)}\beta_{w}^{2}\gamma_{w}x_{w}^{2}\right)\left(\sum_{w\in N(v)}\frac{1}{\gamma_{w}}\right).

Note that
\beta_{w}^{2}\gamma_{w}\leq 4
for every
w\in V(G)
. By Claims
3.6
and
3.7
,
it follows that

\sum_{w\in N(v)}\beta_{w}^{2}\gamma_{w}x_{w}^{2}\leq 4\left(1-\frac{4}{3r}+\frac{1}{4r^{2}}\right)\leq 4\left(1-\frac{1}{r}\right)\left(1-\frac{1}{16r^{2}}\right)\leq 4\left(1-\frac{1}{r}\right)X.

Now observe the identity

\sum_{v\in S}\sum_{w\in N(v)}\frac{1}{\gamma_{w}}=e(S,\bar{S})+e(S)=m-m^{\prime}.

Thus, we conclude that

\sum_{v\in S}\left(\sum_{w\in N(v)}\beta_{w}x_{w}\right)^{2}\leq 4\left(1-\frac{1}{r}\right)X\cdot(m-m^{\prime})=2BX.

∎

Substituting Claims
3.8
and
3.9
back to (
9
), we obtain

\lambda(G)\leq\sqrt{A}X+\sqrt{(1-X)\cdot 2BX}.

We use the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality one last time to get

\displaystyle\sqrt{A}X+\sqrt{B\cdot 2X(1-X)}\leq\sqrt{A+B}\sqrt{X^{2}+2X(1-X)}\leq\sqrt{A+B}=\sqrt{\left(1-\frac{1}{r}\right)2m},

which is the result we want.
Moreover, if
\lambda(G)=\sqrt{(1-\nicefrac{{1}}{{r}})2m}
, then the equality in this last step to hold, we must have
X=1
. Therefore, all vertices in
S
are isolated, so
G=G[V_{1},\dots,V_{r}]
. By Claim
3.4
, this implies that
G
is an
r
-partite graph. Since
G
is
K_{r+1}
-free and
\lambda(G)=\sqrt{(1-\nicefrac{{1}}{{r}})2m}
, we know from Nikiforov’s result that
G
must be a complete bipartite graph when
r=2
, or uniquely a regular complete
r
-partite graph when
r\geq 3
.
∎

.
Then

\lambda(G)=\sqrt{m}+\frac{e(M)}{v(M)}+O(m^{-1/2}),

where
e(M)
and
v(M)
are the number of edges and vertices of
M
.

Proof.

Let
V(G)=A\sqcup C
be the vertex partition of
G
as in the definition of Theorem
1.8
.
From Theorem
1.8
,
we know that
C
is an independent set of
G
, and all but at most one vertex of
C
are complete to
A
.
So
G
differs from
K_{A,C}

in at most
{|A|\choose 2}+|A|=O(1)
edges.
On the one hand, let
\bm{y}
be the vector that assigns
\frac{1}{\sqrt{2|A|}}
to the vertices in
A
and
\frac{1}{\sqrt{2|C|}}
to the vertices in
C
. Then
\bm{y}
is a unit vector. The Rayleigh quotient gives

\lambda(G)\geq\bm{y}^{T}A_{G}\bm{y}=\frac{e_{G}(A,C)}{\sqrt{|A||C|}}+\frac{e(M)}{v(M)}.

Note that
m\leq e_{G}(A,C)+{|A|\choose 2}

and
|A|(|C|-1)<e_{G}(A,C)\leq|A||C|
. It follows that

e_{G}(A,C)=m-O(1)
and
\sqrt{|A||C|}=\sqrt{m}+O(1)
.
We obtain that

\lambda(G)\geq\sqrt{m}+\frac{e(M)}{v(M)}+O(m^{-1/2}).

(12)

On the other hand, let
\bm{x}
be the unit Perron–Frobenius eigenvector of
G
. Then

\lambda(G)=2\sum_{ij\in E(G)}x_{i}x_{j}=2\sum_{ij\in E(G[A,C])}x_{i}x_{j}+2\sum_{ij\in E(G[A])}x_{i}x_{j}.

For the first term,
since
G[A,C]
is bipartite, we have

\lambda(G[A,C])\leq\sqrt{m}
and

2\sum_{ij\in E(G[A,C])}x_{i}x_{j}\leq\sqrt{m}.

For the second term, setting
H=K_{A,C}
in Lemma
2.2
,
we have

\sum_{v\in A}\left(x_{v}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\lvert A\rvert}}\right)^{2}=O(m^{-1/2}).

Then
x_{v}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\lvert A\rvert}}+O(m^{-1/4})
for every
v\in A
, which implies that

\bm{x}^{T}A_{G[A]}\bm{x}=\frac{e(M)}{v(M)}+O(m^{-1/2}).

Thus, we conclude that

\lambda(G)\leq\sqrt{m}+\frac{e(M)}{v(M)}+O(m^{-1/2}).

(13)

Combining (
12
) and (
13
), we finish the proof.
∎

5
Concluding remarks

In this paper, we have introduced the edge-spectral stability method and provided a complete solution to the Brualdi–Hoffman–Turán problem for color-critical graphs. We showed that for any color-critical graph
F
with
\chi(F)=r+1\geq 4
, if
m
is sufficiently large and
G
is an
m
-edge
F
-free graph, then
\lambda^{2}(G)\leq(1-\frac{1}{r})2m
, where the equality holds if and only if
G
is a regular complete
r
-partite graph.
This result significantly extends Nikiforov’s theorem
[
35
]
and can be viewed as a spectral counterpart of Simonovits’ theorem
[
47
]
.
Secondly, we provided a asymptotic formula of the Brualdi–Hoffman–Turán problem for almost-bipartite graphs. As applications,
we determined the unique
m
-edge spectral extremal graph for every integer
m
when we forbid complete bipartite graphs plus an edge, cycles plus an edge, and theta graphs, etc. This settles several problems and conjectures in spectral extremal graph theory.

At the end of this paper, we conclude some spectral extremal problems for readers. Recall that Bollobás and Nikiforov

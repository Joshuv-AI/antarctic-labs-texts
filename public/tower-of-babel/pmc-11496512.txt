Implications of suspended sediment in the migration of nutrients at the water-sediment interface in retention reservoirs
Authors: Maksymilian Cieśla, Renata Gruca-Rokosz
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe significance of suspended sediment in the context of nutrient cycling and distribution in reservoirs is rarely reported in the scientific literature. What’s more, suspended sediment (SS) have so far not been taken into account as a potential factor amplifying the degrading effects of nutrients on the functioning of reservoirs. In the experiment described here, the variability of SS concentrations and the content of total nitrogen (TN), total phosphorus (TP), total organic carbon (TOC) and organic matter (OM) were investigated to determine the potential and determinants of SS in the process of migration of these substances at the water-sediment interface in retention reservoirs. The results showed that SS significantly manipulate the distribution of values of total phosphorus and total organic carbon contained in bottom sediment. It was confirmed that the circulation and distribution of selected nutrients in reservoirs is closely related to suspended sediment. Thus, it was proven that SS significantly affect the quality of deposited sediment. Finally, it was concluded that suspended sediment in the water of reservoirs forms a kind of micro-ecosystem, in which it plays a hitherto undiscovered role of a catenary (hub) consolidating a number of phenomena and processes occurring between individual components in the water-bottom sediment system.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-024-76556-x.Keywords: Circulation nutrients, Organic matter, Reservoirs, Sediment accumulation, Suspended sediment, Carbon cycleSubject terms: Environmental sciences, Hydrology, Limnology

Introduction

As a result of baffling natural rivers with a hydro-technical construction, a reservoir is created, often also called an artificial lake. Despite their apparent similarity, reservoirs are significantly different from natural lakes. These objects are separate aquatic ecosystems, consolidating different features of the environment proper to lakes and rivers. In the reservoir’s inflow area, conditions typical of rivers prevail, while near the dam conditions are closer to lake ecosystems1.

The totality of biological and physicochemical processes resulting in the deterioration of water quality, an increase in its trophy and a reduction in its utility and scenic value leads to the degradation of aquatic ecosystems. The input of excessive loads of pollutants (including nutrients) into rivers and reservoirs is a direct cause of the progressive degradation of surface waters. On the other hand, the proper functioning of water reservoirs depends to a large extent on water quality. The vulnerability of a water reservoir to degradation is determined by the nature and management of its catchment area and the resilience of the facility itself to its impact2.

Generally, the proper functioning of such (specific) aquatic ecosystems as reservoirs depends on autochthonous and allochthonous sources of organic matter and the metaboilism of the ecosystem. Over the course of the year, this metabolism changes as a result of seasonal changes in such parameters as nutrient availability, light availability, and temperature variability, among others, all of which ultimately affect the primary production of organic matter in the reservoir3,4. Seasonal changes in weather imply a tendency to increase water temperature, algal biomass and indigenous organic matter production, which promotes biological aggregation and sediment deposition at the bottom of the reservoir5. Moreover, the nutrients in the water column activate the development of phytoplankton, which is one of the fundamental elements of suspended sediment6.

It follows that microbial activity favors the binding of sediment particles resulting in the formation of colloids of larger size compared to sediment particles without biomass. On the other hand, their abundance and spatio-temporal distribution are influenced by, among other things, varying environmental conditions, including nutrient loading from the catchment3,7. Thus, it can be assumed that suspended sediment forms a kind of micro-ecosystem in which many processes take place, including sorption of chemicals (e.g., humic substances), interactions between particles, and colonization of living organisms8,9.

Suspended sediment is considered to be water-transported sediment that is so fine that turbulent flows prevent its deposition. Therefore, hydrodynamic conditions play an important function in creating the concentration level of suspended sediment in water. They are responsible not only for the movement of suspended sediment particles, but also for their repeated entrainment and redeposition. Moreover, suspended sediment usually does not move at the same velocity as the water stream in which it is suspended, nor does it remain suspended indefinitely10.

Under favorable conditions, mineral cohesive sediment particles (grains < 63 μm) (Juez et al., 2018) can combine to form aggregates of much larger sizes than elementary particles11. The formation of aggregates depends on, among other things, the physical (e.g., cohesion phenomena or lack thereof), chemical and electrochemical conditions that characterize liquids, and the properties of elementary particles (including structure, shape and grain size), water flow rate, climate zone and season12–14. It is assumed that a complex consisting of minerals (including clays, loams) and organic matter exhibits cohesive properties15. The organic phase of the complex, however, consists of living organisms (including bacteria, phytoplankton and zooplankton) and their inanimate forms (e.g., fecal and pseudo-fecal pellets, detritus and its decomposition products from microbial activity, such as mucus and exopolymers)5, as well as xenobiotic particles from anthropogenic activity (e.g., microplastics)16.

From one side, the permanent accumulation of sediments in reservoirs influences their proper functioning and water retention efficiency, and from the other side, the accumulated sediments in the reservoirs play an important role in the circulation of substances (including biogenic compounds) between the different elements of the water-bottom sediment system6,17–19.

The main aim of the study was to determine the role played by suspended sediment in the water column of reservoirs in the context of quantitative changes in selected nutrients in the system of the two environmental components studied (entrapped sediments and bottom sediments). It was hypothesized that the dynamics of changes in suspended sediment concentration plays an important role in the circulation and distribution of nutrients in specific aquatic ecosystems such as reservoirs.

Materials and methods

Study area

The study was done in the area of three retention reservoirs in southeastern Poland. Two of them are located in the Podkarpackie Province (Blizne and Maziarnia reservoirs), and one in the Lublin Province (Nielisz Reservoir) (Fig. 1). The primary function of the studied objects is flood control water retention. In addition, the reservoirs are also used for fish farming and fishing, as well as for recreational use. The catchment area of the studied reservoirs is dominated by agricultural land20–23. The basic morphometric and hydrological parameters of the reservoirs are shown in Table 1.

Research methods

Research concept

The main research study was done under in situ conditions during the spring and summer of 2018 and 2019. In the case of Blizne and Maziarnia reservoirs, research was done in 2018, with the research period divided into six consecutive research series. Conversely, in 2019 the research was done in the area of Nielisz Reservoir, and the research period was divided into four research series. Sediment traps (Fig. 2) were used to collect suspended sediment in the reservoir waters, which were made of “plexi glass” tubes using a novel design solution24.

Scheme of field research. The final figure was generated using the CorelDRAW 2020 platform.

The traps were installed on the bottom of the reservoirs from about 3–100 m from the shoreline, at a depth of about 1.6 m to 2.5 m (Fig. 2). At the beginning, middle and end of each research series, water samples were taken from the designated sites to test suspended sediment (SS) concentrations. In addition, entrapped sediment (i.e., sediment collected in traps) and bottom sediment were collected at the end of each survey series. Quality Assurance and Quality Control (QA/QC) procedures were used to ensure the quality of the studies carried out. Among other things, validated and previously tested equipment and methods for collecting and keeping collected samples were used.

In addition, to date, bathymetric studies, as well as other types of observations of the sedimentation process, have not been carried out in the area of the analyzed reservoirs. This paper contains the results of the first such study of the sedimentation process of suspended sediments, using sediment traps (i.e., devices for monitoring the deposition of suspended matter in the water depth). However, it should be noted that the presented values were obtained on the basis of studies carried out under the research conditions described in the paper, among others: climatic zone, study period, exposure time of traps, construction and parameters of traps, depth of their immersion, morphometric and hydrological parameters of reservoirs, as well as catchment development. Given the pointed circumstances, caution should be exercised when directly relating the studied results to other similar studies.

Analysis of surface waters

The concentration of suspended sediment (SS) in surface water samples was determined by filtering a predetermined volume of water through a GF/F glass fiber filter. Before filtration, the filter was ignition roasting at 550 °C for 4 h, and then dried at 105 °C to a constant weight after filtration. SS concentration was determined by the difference in the weights of the filter before and after filtration and the volume of filtered water25. The results of the tests and details of the sampling dates are presented in the supplementary materials (Table S2).

Analysis of sediment collected in traps and bottom sediment

A total of 96 sediment samples were tested (with 48 samples being bottom sediments and the other 48 being entrapped sediment). Total nitrogen (TNS), total phosphorus (TPS), total organic carbon (TOCS) and organic matter (OMS) were determined in entrapped sediment. Analogous determinations were made for bottom sediment (TNSB, TPSB, TOCSB and OMSB, respectively). The organic matter content of the sediments was calculated based on losses during ignition roasting at 550 °C for 4 h. Total phosphorus content was determined spectrophotometrically using an Aquamate spectrophotometer (Thermo Scientific). For this purpose, the dried sediment was crushed in a mortar and sieved through a sieve with a mesh diameter of 1 mm. Before each analysis, the sediments were dried to a constant weight at 60 °C. The samples thus prepared were mineralized in the presence of spectrally pure concentrated HNO3 in a Mars 6 microwave mineralizer (CEM MATTHEWS). Total nitrogen and total organic carbon contents were determined using a CN elemental analyzer (Flash EA 1112, ThermoQuest) at 1020 °C. Standard samples of known carbon and nitrogen composition (acetanilide - C8H9NO) were used for quality control. Details of sampling dates and sediment results are presented in the supplementary materials (Tables S3 and S8).

In more, using data published in the work of Cieśla et al.11 on the granulometric composition of sediment accumulated in the traps (entrapped sediment) of the studied reservoirs (Table 2), the relationships between the identified nutrienaters and sediment grain size were determined. Measurements, the grain size of the entrapped sediment was carried out using a Malvern Mastersizer 2000E particle size analyzer with a “wet” dispersant attachment - the Hydro2000MU. For this purpose, the sediments were dispersed in deionized water and subjected to ultrasound until the agglomerates were completely broken up.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis done using Statistica 13.0 PL included the Kruskal Wallis ANOVA test (ANOVA KW) and Pearson’s linear correlation analysis (r). These analyses made it possible to assess whether the correlations or differences between the selected parameters were statistically significant. Details of the results of the tests done are presented in the supplementary materials (Table S1). Principal component analysis (PCA) was used to identify those variables that have a strong influence on the appearance of the other components, i.e. those that form a homogeneous group. The validity of using PCA analysis was checked by analyzing the obtained correlation matrices using the Bartlett test and the Kaiser-Mayer-Olkin coefficient. A significance level of α = 0.05 was used for the study.

Results

Quantitative analysis of suspended sediment

The results of the Kruskal-Wallis ranks ANOVA test showed significant variation in suspended sediment (SS) concentrations in the waters of the reservoirs studied (ANOVA KW, p = 0.0000, p < α). In the waters of the smallest Blizne Reservoir, the mean SS concentration was 12.18 mg/dm3, while in the slightly larger Maziarnia Reservoir it was 13.31 mg/dm3. In contrast, in the largest studied, Nielisz Reservoir, the mean SS concentration was 35.47 mg/dm3 (Table S2). Taking into account the spatial distribution of the obtained SS concentrations, a trend of reduction of SS concentrations with water flow through the reservoir, common to all sites, was observed. In the case of Blizne Reservoir, mean SS values at BL1, BL2 and BL3 sites were 23.72 mg/dm3, 6.42 mg/dm3 and 6.39 mg/dm3, respectively. In Maziarnia Reservoir, the distribution of mean values of SS concentrations from site M1 located closest to the river inflow to the site near dam M3 were 18.27 mg/dm3, 11.29 mg/dm3 and 10.37 mg/dm3, respectively. The highest SS concentrations were recorded in the largest reservoir Nielisz, they were respectively: 43.66 mg/dm3 (N1 station), 37.66 mg/dm3 (N2 station) and 25.11 mg/dm3 (N3 station) (Table S2). In general, the highest concentration of suspended sediment in the water column of the studied reservoirs was observed in the inflow zone, while a relatively small amount of sediment reached the dam zone.

Characteristics of sediment collected in traps (entrapped sediment)

The content of total phosphorus (TPS) in entrapped sediment at particular study sites in Blizne Reservoir varied from 0.42 to 1.84 mg/g d.w. (the mean for the reservoir is 1.08 mg/g d.w. (Table S3 and Fig. S1). Despite this wide range of results obtained, no significant spatial variation was found (ANOVA KW, p = 0.13, p > α). The highest mean TPS content was recorded at site BL2 (central part of the reservoir). The results showed a tendency for TPS content to increase in the sediment over time (Table S3 and Fig. S1).

Analysis of total nitrogen content (TNS) also showed no statistically significant spatial variation (ANOVA KW, p = 0.11, p > α). The distribution of mean TNS contents across sites varied within a narrow range from 0.30 to 0.64% (reservoir mean 0.46%), with the highest mean TNS content recorded at site BL3 (near the dam) and the lowest at a site in the central part of the reservoir (BL2). As in the case of TPS, accumulation of this biogen in sediment was observed with time (especially at sites BL1 and BL3) (Table S3 and Fig. S1). TOCS content was also not statistically significantly different between sites (ANOVA KW, p = 0.91, p > α). The lowest TOCS value was recorded at site BL1 (5.33%), and the highest at site BL2 (3.25%) (Table S3 and Fig. S1). The mean TOCS content of entrapped sediment in this reservoir was 4.18%. As with nutrients, there was a tendency for TOCS to accumulate in the entrapped sediment over time (Table S3 and Fig. S1). Analyzing the organic matter content of entrapped sediment (OMS) in Blizne Reservoir, as with other parameters, there were no statistically significant differences between sites (ANOVA KW, p = 0.06, p > α). The variability of OMS at particular sites ranged from 10.17 to 21.42% (Table S3 and Fig. S1). The highest OMS content was recorded at site BL1 (at the reservoir inflow), while the lowest at a site in the central part of the reservoir (BL2).

The content of total phosphorus (TPS) in entrapped sediment in Maziarnia Reservoir was characterized by a variation ranging from 0.76 to 3.80 mg/g d.w. (the mean for the reservoir is 1.97 mg/g d.w.) (Table S4 and Fig. S2). Statistically significant spatial variation was shown (ANOVA KW, p = 0.003, p < α), and an increase in the content of this component was observed only from the site at the inflow (M1 site) to the site in the central part of the reservoir (M2 site). For total nitrogen (TNS), there were no statistically significant differences between sites (ANOVA KW, p = 0.07, p > α). The mean TNS content for entrapped sediment was 0.75%, with the highest mean value obtained at site M3 (dam zone), and the lowest at a site near the reservoir tributary (M1) (Table S4 and Fig. S2). TOCS organic carbon content increased from a value of 5.64% at site M1, to 9.10% at site M3 (mean content − 7.28%) (Table S4 and Fig. S2). Statistically significant variation in TOCS values along the longitudinal axis of the reservoir (ANOVA KW, p = 0.01, p < α) suggests that its spatial distribution was determined by initial production. It was observed that in most cases, as in the Blizne Reservoir, the content of TPS, TNS and TOCS increased with time.

The ANOVA KW test confirmed the variation in total phosphorus content (TPS) between sites also in Nielisz Reservoir (p = 0.02, p < α). Mean values of TPS depending on the site ranged from 3.39 to 7.80 mg/g d.w. (Table S5 and Fig. S3), (the mean for the reservoir is 5.4 mg/g d.w.), and a decrease in the mean values of this parameter was observed from the site at the inflow towards the dam. Analysis of changes in the content of TPS over the considered period showed that the content of this biogen generally increased with time in the analyzed sediments (Table S5 and Fig. S3).

Similar spatial variation as for TPS was also characterized by the variation of total nitrogen content (TNS) (ANOVA KW, p = 0.02, p < α). The trend of decreasing mean values of this parameter coincided with changes in TPS content. The mean TNS content in the analyzed sediments was 0.65%, with the highest mean TNS content (1.03%) recorded at site N1 in the area of the reservoir inflow, and the lowest (0.43%) at site N3 near the dam (Table S5 and Fig. S3).

In the case of TOCS, the opposite of Maziarnia Reservoir, the lowest mean content was recorded at site N3 (4.05%) and the highest at site N1 (12.39%), while the mean value of TOCS in Nielisz Reservoir was 7.65%. Again, the ANOVA KW test (p = 0.02, p < α) confirmed statistically significant variation in the value of this parameter between sites. In addition, the observed significant variation along the longitudinal axis of the reservoir in TOCS values, coincided with organic matter (OMS) values (ANOVA KW, p = 0.001, p < α). The link between these parameters was confirmed by a statistically significant correlation (r = 0.71, p < 0.05, n = 18). The range of mean OMS values varied from 15.88 to 35.03% (Table S5 and Fig. S3). The lowest OMS content was recorded in the reservoir inflow zone (M1), while the highest was recorded at a site near the dam (M3).

Anlaysis of the granulometric composition of entrapped sediments in Blizne Reservoir showed that particles of size classes 15.888–31.698 μm and 31.699–63.246 μm were the most frequent, accounting for 28.16% and 27.96% of the total particles, respectively. This was followed by particles in size classes < 5.024 μm, 5.025–10.000 μm and 10.001–15.887 μm (13.19%, 11.29%, 11.71%, respectively). The lowest particle size classes were 63.247–251.785 μm and 251.786–502.377 μm (7.64% and 0.06%, respectively). In Maziarnia Reservoir, the most frequent grain size classes were 31.699–63.246 μm and 15.888–31.698 μm, their average share in the total pool was 29.02% and 20.21%, respectively. This was followed by fractions in the 63.247–251.785 μm, < 5.024 μm and 10.001–15.887 μm classes (16.96%, 13.63%, 10.27% and 9.30%, respectively). Particles in the size classes 251.786–502.377 μm and > 502.378 μm (0.52% and 0.09%, respectively) made up the smallest part of the analyzed material. It was also found that the contribution of particles > 63.247 μm increased toward the site at the dam (M3), while this phenomenon was accompanied by a decrease in the fraction consisting of finer grains 15.887–63.247 μm. The variation in the granulometric composition of entrapped sediment in Nielisz Reservoir had a distribution very similar to that of material obtained in other reservoirs. Grains with size classes in the 15.888–31.698 μm and 31.699–63.246 μm ranges were the most frequent, with average contributions of 30.32% and 28.18%, respectively. Later gradation was characterized by the distribution of particles in the 10.001–15.887 μm (12.7%), < 5.024 μm, 5.025–10.000 μm (9.52% and 9.71%, respectively) and 63.247–251.785 μm (8.32%) classes. Particles in the 251.786–502.377 μm and > 502.378 μm classes (0.74% and 0.002%, respectively) made up the smallest part of the analyzed material11.

Characteristics of bottom sediment

Analyzing the variability of total phosphorus in bottom sediment (TPSB) in Blizne Reservoir, no significant variation was observed between sites (ANOVA KW, p = 0.80, p > α). Mean TPSB values ranged from 0.13 to 0.16 mg/g d.w. (the mean for the reservoir was 0.15 mg/g d.w.). The highest mean value was recorded at site BL3 (near the dam) (Table S6). Moreover, TPSB was not statistically significantly related to the other analyzed parameters (TNSB, TOCSB, OMSB). Similarly, as for TPSB so for total nitrogen content (TNSB), there was no statistically significant spatial variation between sites (ANOVA KW, p = 0.09, p > α). However, despite this, the distribution of TNSB content varied over a fairly wide range from 0.01 to 0.24% (Table S6), (mean for the reservoir 0.08%). The highest TNSB values were recorded at site BL1 (the region near the reservoir inflow), while the lowest values were recorded at a site near the dam (BL3).

In the situation of TOCSB, there was statistically significant variation in its content between the analyzed sites (ANOVA KW, p = 0.003, p < α). The lowest mean value of TOCSB was obtained for site BL3 (0.44%), and the highest for site BL1 (1.50%) (Table S6), while the mean content of TOCSB in reservoir sediment was 0.89%. Over the considered period, changes in the content of this parameter generally showed a constant level of enrichment of bottom sediment in TOCSB (Table S6). An essentially similar situation was observed for the variation in the organic matter content (OMSB) over time. As in the case of TOCSB, statistically significant differences between sites were also found for the obtained values of organic matter contained in bottom sediment (OMSB) (ANOVA KW, p = 0.007, p < α). The range of mean OMSB values at individual sites varied from 2.92 to 5.02% (Table S6). A trend of decreasing OMSB values was observed from site BL1 (at the reservoir inflow), in the direction the site near the dam (BL3).

The content of total phosphorus (TPSB) contained in the sediment of Maziarnia Reservoir, in contrast to the bottom sediments of Blizne Reservoir, was characterized by statistically significant spatial variation (ANOVA KW, p = 0.0005, p < α). The content of total phosphorus decreased from the site at the inflow in the direction of the site in the dam zone. The mean value of this parameter at the designated sites ranged from 0.03 to 0.30 mg/g d.w. (the mean for the reservoir was 0.13 mg/g d.w.) (Table S7). In turn, the content of total nitrogen (TNSB) was characterized by statistically insignificant variation (ANOVA KW, p = 0.16, p > α). The mean TNSB content in the reservoir bottom sediment was 0.05%. The highest mean TNSB content was recorded at the site in the reservoir inflow zone M1 (0.07%), while the lowest was recorded at the site M3 near the dam (0.02%). At the same time, at sites M2 and M3, bottom sediment were often characterized by trace TNSB contents (below the limit of determination) (Table S7). In Maziarnia Reservoir, it was found that along the longitudinal axis of the reservoir, the content of total organic carbon in bottom sediment (TOCSB) varied statistically significantly (ANOVA KW, p = 0.01, p < α). As in the case of TNSB, the highest mean TOCSB content was obtained at the site in the M1 reservoir inflow zone (1.22%) and the lowest at the site near the M3 dam (0.30%) (Table S7), while the mean TOCSB content was 0.65%. For the most part of the study period, changes in the content of this parameter with time showed a tendency to deplete it from bottom sediment. An analogous situation was observed in the case of variation over time in the content of organic matter. It was also observed that significant spatial variation in the content of TOCS in entrapped sediment (ANOVA KW, p = 0.002, p < α) was associated with variation in the organic matter content of bottom sediment (OMSB). Mean OMSB values ranged from 0.63 to 4.02% (Table S7). As in the case of TOCSB, the highest OMSB content was recorded at the reservoir inflow (M1), while the lowest was recorded at a site near the dam (M3).

Also in Nielisz Reservoir, the results of the ANOVA KW test (p = 0.02, p < α), confirmed statistically significant variation in total phosphorus content (TPSB) between designated sites. As in Maziarnia Reservoir, a trend of decreasing mean values of this parameter was observed from the site at the inflow in the direction of the dam. Mean values of TPSB at particular study sites ranged from 0.13 to 1.83 mg/g d.w. (the mean in the reservoir was 1.19 mg/g d.w.) (Table S8). The same level of significance as for TPSB was characterized by variation in total nitrogen content (TNSB) (ANOVA KW, p = 0.02, p < α). The trend of decreasing mean values of this parameter was also associated with changes in TPSB content. The mean TNSB content in the bottom sediment of the reservoir was 0.09%. The highest mean TNSB content (0.21%) was obtained in the tributary area (N1), while the lowest (0.01%) was obtained near the reservoir dam (N3) (Table S8). At the same time, as in Maziarnia Reservoir, trace TNSB contents (below the limit of determination) were recorded in the central part of the reservoir (site N2).

Both organic matter and organic carbon in bottom sediment in Nielisz Reservoir showed spatial variation. The results of the ANOVA KW test (p = 0.03, p < α) confirmed the presence of significant variation in TOCSB values between sites. The trend of changes in mean TOCSB values was analogous to that of other parameters. The highest mean value of TOCSB was recorded at site N1 (2.53%) and the lowest at site N3 (0.18%) (Table S8), while the mean content of TOCSB in the reservoir bottom sediment was 1.14%. Moreover, at sites N1 and N2, with the passage of time, changes in the content of this parameter showed a tendency to enrich the bottom sediment in TOC. The variation in organic matter content over time was also analogous at these sites. Both a statistically significant correlation (r = 0.99, p < 0.05, n = 12) and the results of the ANOVA KW test (p = 0.02, p < α) showed that organic matter content (OMSB) was related to TOCSB content. The mean OMSB content in the reservoir bottom sediment was 2.25%, while the mean values at particular sites decreased in the direction to the site at the dam, i.e. from 4.91 to 0.54% (Table S8).

Links between analyzed parameters

In order to determine the role played by suspended sediment in the water column in the distribution of selected nutrients in reservoirs, we first analyzed whether there were statistically significant relations between the analyzed parameters (Table S1). Analyzing the obtained correlations between the studied elements, it was found that the concentration of suspended sediment in the water of the studied reservoirs (SS) significantly determined the distribution of total phosphorus content in entrapped sediment (TPS). The positive value of the correlation coefficient between these parameters (r = 0.64, p < 0.05, n = 48) means that as the concentration of suspended sediment in the water increased, the content of TPS increased simultaneously (Fig. 3). The relation found is significant, with 64% modeling these characteristics.

Relations between SS, TPS, TNS, TOCS, and OMS. The final figure was generated using the Statistica 13.0 PL platform.

A positive and significant relations was also observed for the TOCS parameter (r = 0.34, p < 0.05, n = 48) (Table S1). Variability in suspended sediment (SS) concentrations was shown to be 34% modeled with the content of total organic carbon contained in entrapped sediment (TOCS). At a similar, statistically significant but negative level, suspended sediment concentrations correlated with the organic matter content of the sediment entrapped (OMS) (r = -0.31, p < 0.05, n = 8) (Table S1). In this case, the parameters are modeled at 31%. The relations found suggests that as the concentration of suspended sediment increased, the organic matter content of entrapped sediment (OMS) decreased accordingly. However, while the values of the other analyzed parameters were clearly consolidated around the concentration of suspended sediment (SS), in the case of OMS the obtained values were characterized by a significant scatter (Fig. 3). This may indicate that the fluctuations in OMS content were influenced not only by the concentration of suspended sediment in the water, but by additional factors such as, among others, partial decomposition occurring in the water column. On the other hand, for the obtained values of total nitrogen contained in the sediment entrapped (TNS), no significant relationship with the SS parameter was confirmed (r = 0.16, p > 0.05, n = 48) (Table S1).

The concentration of suspended sediment (SS) in the water of the studied reservoirs also significantly determined the distribution of total phosphorus in bottom sediment (TPSB) (Table S1). TPSB values were clearly consolidated around suspended sediment (SS) concentrations (Fig. 4a). As in the case of sediment entrapped, the correlation coefficient value between these parameters was obtained at a similar level (r = 0.61, p < 0.05, n = 48), which means that these characteristics are modeled at 61%. It also indicates that suspended sediment in the water column plays a significant role in the distribution of TPSB in bottom sediment. In order to explain this phenomenon more fully, it was also tested whether there were statistically significant differences in TPSB content between the studied reservoirs. The test results confirmed that such differences did exist (ANOVA KW, p = 0.005, p < α).

Relations between: (a) SS and TPSB ; (b) SS and TOCSB ; (c) SS and TNSB ; (d) SS and OMSB. The final figure was generated using the Statistica 13.0 PL platform.

This analysis proves that the extension of water retention time in the reservoir intensifies TPSB deposition processes in the bottom sediment of reservoirs. In view of this, the morphometric parameters of reservoirs (water retention time) and suspended sediment (SS) contained in their waters could be co-responsible for phosphorus deposition in bottom sediment. The amplitude of suspended sediment (SS) concentrations was also found to be 32% modeled with total organic carbon content in bottom sediment (TOCSB) (r = 0.32, p < 0.05, n = 48) (Table S1) (Fig. 4b). However, in this case, the results of the ANOVA KW test (p = 0.248, p > α) suggested that the frequency of water exchange in the reservoirs was not statistically significant in the accumulation of organic carbon in sediment (TOCSB). Similarly, as in the case of TNS, the distribution of TNSB values showed no significant association with suspended matter (SS) concentration (r = -0.20, p > 0.05, n = 48) (Table S1) (Fig. 4c). The variation in TNSB values was also not significantly related to tank capacity (ANOVA KW, p = 0.738, p > α) (Table S2). There was also no significant relations between organic matter contained in bottom sediment (OMSB) and SS suspended sediment concentration (r = 0.12, p > 0.05, n = 48) (Table S1) (Fig. 4d). Only statistically significant variation in OMSB content was shown for the studied reservoirs (ANOVA KW, p = 0.004, p < α). It was found, also, that OMSB deposition was characterized by an asymmetric distribution with respect to TPSB. In this case, longer water retention time in the reservoir may have resulted in a reduction of OMSB content in bottom sediment.

A comprehensive analysis of the granulometric composition of the entrapped sediment and the content of selected nutrients in tchem, showed the existence of correlations between the parameters studied. The graphical distribution of correlations between the selected parameters is shown in bag charts (Fig. 5). However, in this case, those sediment fractions that were most correlated with the considered nutreirnts were selected for analysis. It was observed that the TPS content was correlated with the finest nutreirnts with fractions < 5.024 μm and 5.025-10.000 μm (r = -0.51 and r = -0.33 p < 0.05, respectively) (Fig. 5a). Slightly different relationships were observed for TNS. In this case, there was a negative correlation with grains of fractions 10.001–15.887 μm and 15.888–31.698 μm (r = -0.39 and r = -0.51 p < 0.05, respectively) (Fig. 5b). In addition, TNS correlated with the fraction 63.247–251.785 μm, albeit this time with a positive correlation (r = -0.49 p < 0.05) (Fig. 5b). Still different correlations were found for the TOCS and OMS parameters. In the former case, negative correlations were confirmed for fractions < 5.024 μm, 5.025–10.000 μm, 10.001–15.887 μm and 15.888–31.698 μm (r = -0.34, r = -0.40, r = -0.35, r = -0.39, p < 0.05, respectively) (Fig. 5c). This was followed by positive correlations for the larger fractions 63.247–251.785 μm, 251.786–502.377 μm and > 502.378 μm (r = 0.48, r = 0.49, r = 0.34, p < 0.05, respectively) (Fig. 5c). Meanwhile, the OMS parameter clearly correlated with fractions 10.001–15.887 μm and 15.888–31.698 μm (r = -0.71 and r = -0.90 p < 0.05, respectively) (Fig. 5d). Besides, there was also a clear and positive correlation with the 63.247–251.785 μm fraction (r = 0.81 p < 0.05) (Fig. 5d) and a less strong correlations with the other largest fractions 251.786–502.377 μm and > 502.378 μm (r = 0.30, r = 0.34, p < 0.05, respectively) (Fig. 5d).

In the next step, using the observed relations, PCA analysis was done. Almost all vectors representing the primary variables (with the exception of TNSB) approximate the edges of the unit circle (a circle of radius 1), and are therefore very well represented by the first two principal components that form the coordinate system (Fig. 6a and b). The length of the vector represents the information resource of the primary variable carried by the principal components that determine the coordinate system. The longer the vector, the greater the contribution of the primary variable to the construction of the components. In a correlation matrix-based analysis, the charges are correlations between the primary variables and the components, in which case the points fall into a unit circle. This is because the correlation coefficient can not exceed the value of 1. As a result, the closer a given primary variable lies to the edge of the circle, the better its representation by the primary components shown.

The vectors for suspended sediment (SS) concentrations were slightly correlated with the values of total nitrogen contained in both entrapped sediment (TNS) and bottom sediment (TNSB), as evidenced by the angle of slope between these parameters (close to a right angle) (Fig. 6a and b). The opposite situation occurred for vectors illustrating the relations between SS and TPS and TPSB, as well as TOCS and TOCSB. In the former case (i.e., SS and TPS and TPSB), it was found that the angle of inclination between these parameters clearly approximated an acute angle. On the other hand, in the second i.e. for the SS and TOCS and TOCSB vectors, the slope of these vectors was found to be in the range of 0 < α < 900 (Fig. 6a and b). The observed relationships clearly confirm the existence of a strong relationship between these parameters. The smaller the angle between the vectors representing the primary variables, the stronger the positive correlation between these variables. In contrast, an inverse relations was found between the vectors representing the values of SS and OMS, in this case the angle between these vectors is in the range of 900 − 1800, which means that there is a strong but negative correlation between these variables (Fig. 6a)11. Nevertheless, such a correlation was not confirmed in the case of organic matter contained in bottom sediment (OMSB) (the angle of inclination between these parameters was close to a right angle) (Fig. 6b).

The principal component analysis (PCA) done identified the variable (SS) that significantly influenced the distribution of the other components (TPS, TOCS, OMS, TPSB and TOCSB) (Figs. 5b and 6a). Finally, the multivariate analysis confirmed that the suspended sediment concentration (SS) in the water column of the studied reservoirs significantly influenced the circulation and distribution of selected nutrients at the water-sediment interface.

Discussion

Studying suspended sediment (SS) concentrations in the waters of the studied reservoirs showed that the lowest average SS concentration was recorded in the smallest Blizne Reservoir, and the highest in the largest Nielisz Reservoir (Table S2). This may have been related to the more flowing nature of the Blizne Reservoir. The more frequent exchange of water in this reservoir may have significantly reduced the capacity for both plankton growth and the retention of products associated with internal recharge processes of suspended matter in its waters. On the other hand, a common feature of all the studied reservoirs was the observed phenomenon of gradual reduction of SS along the longitudinal axis of the reservoirs, i.e., as one approached the near-dam zone, suspended sediment (SS) concentrations decreased. A characteristic feature of dam reservoirs is their ability to permanently retain and deposit (accumulate) trailing and uplifted debris. As a result of this natural process, a significantly lower load of suspended matter is usually transported to the dam zone (the phenomenon of matter depletion)6. In addition, the ability and conditions of suspended sediment transport are largely responsible for creating the level of suspended sediment concentration in the water body. The mechanism enabling the transport of matter follows complex patterns, as well as depends on a number of factors (including climate, meteorological conditions, land cover and slope), and among the most important are erosional processes occurring within the catchment (they are the primary source of suspended sediment in rivers)26.

The varied surface area of the grains allows easy sorption of, among other things, trace elements, toxic organic substances27,28, including many hydrophobic pollutants such as polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons, heavy metals and others29. Thus, suspended sediments in the water body share responsibility for the transport and distribution of pollutants delivered to waters. Despite the fact that suspended sediments are mainly identified with pollutants that can negatively affect the environment, in surface waters they are a natural and necessary element of properly functioning aquatic ecosystems30. Here special attention should be paid to phosphorus and other nutrients, which, together with suspended sediment particles, can sediment and be deposited in the bottom sediments of reservoirs31,32.

A good example to confirm this thesis can be seen in the observed significant statistical correlation of the obtained SS values and the values of total phosphorus contained in both entrapped sediments (TPS) and bottom sediments (TPSB). Due to the fact that phosphorus does not have a gas phase and forms hardly soluble compounds with many metals, therefore it is more easily deposited in the bottom sediments of water reservoirs, from where it has the possibility of only partial return to the circulation. The usual liminal characteristics of reservoirs (long water retention time) favor phosphorus deposition in bottom sediments33–35. Similar observations are also reported in other studies. For example, a study described by Partani et al.36 confirmed that vertical TP profiles increase as the depth increased in aquatic ecosystems. This was reflected in the obtained values of this biogen in the sediments of the studied reservoirs.

Compared to the smaller Blizne and Maziarnia reservoirs, it was in the largest Nielisz Reservoir (average water retention time of 77 d) that the highest total phosphorus contents were obtained in both entrapped and bottom sediments (Tables S3-S8). Moreover, analogous to SS, a trend of decrease in TPS and TPSB contents was found towards the near-dam zone of the reservoirs. In addition, in every studied reservoir, the phosphorus content of bottom sediments (TPSB) was significantly lower than that of sedimented entrapped sediments (TPS).

Despite the fact that the circulation of phosphorus takes place only between terrestrial and aquatic ecosystems, this element plays a very important function in nature, including being part of nucleic acids - substances responsible for energy transfer, phospholipids and some proteins, as well as being responsible for maintaining an appropriate pH37,38.

In the sediments accumulated in the traps of Blizne, Maziarnia and Nielisz Reservoirs, it was found that the organic matter (OMS) values obtained in them were linked to fluctuations in TOCS content. Besides, these reservoirs showed a statistically significant correlation between the content of total organic carbon (TOCS) and TPS values. As for changes in total nitrogen content (TNS), it was found that this parameter in Blizne and Nielisz Reservoirs statistically significantly correlated with OMS content. In addition, in Nielisz Reservoir it was observed that TNS values were significantly linked to TOCS content.

In most cases, the relationships obtained between the analyzed bottom sediment parameters coincided with the observed correlations for entrapped sediments. In the case of entrapped sediments, statistically significant correlations were observed between the values of organic matter (OMSB) contained in bottom sediments and the content of TOCSB in them. In Maziarnia and Nielisz Reservoirs, the relationships found between the values of total organic carbon (TOCSB) and total phosphorus (TPSB) in bottom sediments corresponded with the observed relationships as for sediments accumulated in traps. Besides, the reservoirs showed a statistically significant correlation between total phosphorus (TPSB) and total nitrogen (TNSB). It was also found that in these reservoirs this parameter significantly correlated with the content of OMSB. Moreover, in the case of TNSB, it was shown that this parameter in all analyzed reservoirs was significantly linked to the parameters TOCSB and OMSB. As carbon and nitrogen are the basic elements of organic matter, hence, as in the case of accumulated entrapped sediments, the observed relationships between these components indicate that total nitrogen may have consisted mainly of organic nitrogen39,40, and the distribution of matter was associated with both carbon and nitrogen loss41. In addition, the correlations found between TPSB and OMSB in Maziarnia and Nielisz Reservoirs indicate that organic phosphorus made a significant contribution to the total phosphorus contained in the bottom sediments of these reservoirs. In contrast, the lack of this correlation in Blizne reservoir suggests that in limnic reservoirs the accumulation of this biogen is more related to internal production than in highly flowing reservoirs. In small and shallow reservoirs (such as Blizne), organic material is more rapidly deposited in bottom sediments and thus exposed to oxidation processes in the water for a shorter time. Such reservoirs, however, are more susceptible to the phenomenon of resuspension, i.e. the re-suspension of sediments from the bed into the water’s depths42.

At this place, it is also important to point out the significance of the grain size of sediments on the distribution of nutrients. In the distribution of relationships shown in Fig. 5, among others, one can see a negative slope of the bag showing the relationship between TPS and the finest grains with farkation < 5.024 μm and 5.025–10.000 μm. The negative correlation between these parameters may suggest that as the contribution of sediment grains with the finest fraction increased, the TPS content decreased. Which, at first look, contrasts with the well-known sorptive potential of fine material relative to phosphorus43. However, a comprehensive analysis of the data showed that the seemingly negative correlation obtained may have been influenced by an overlap of characteristic phenomena observed during the study. It should be noted that the tributary zone of the reservoirs was characterized by the highest concentrations of suspended sediments, and hence their accumulation also occurred most intensively in this region. As a result of this natural process, a markedly lower load of suspended sediment was transported to the dam region (the phenomenon of matter exhaustion), so the accumulation rates of sedimented matter in these areas were also at a lower level44. This implies that the negative correlation found was generally related to the decreasing load of sediment tranported along the axis of the reservoir, rather than the lack of sorptive properties of fine sediments towards this biogen. On the other hand, fine sediment particles have the ability to flocculate among themselves. Under favorable conditions, they interact with biological material in this way to form flocculi of larger size45, which settle to the bottom more easily and quickly. Hence, it can be conjectured that the positive slope of the bag showing the relationship between TNS, TOCS, and OMS, and larger grains with farkations such as 63.247–251.785 μm and larger.

In general, suspended sediments undergoes continuous transformations as it flows down in the water column and after it is deposited on the bottom. In addition, the intensity of the changes taking place mainly depends on the properties of the suspended sediments and the conditions in the reservoir. Unlike in small reservoirs, in large and deep reservoirs the transport of suspended sediments to the bottom takes place over a longer interval of time, so the process of its mineralization is more efficient than in small and shallow reservoirs46. Also, the results of the study described by Partani et al.47 indicated that increased contact time of nutrients with aquatic vegetation resulted in an increased flux of their absorption by sediments. As a result, it was concluded that aerenchymes that grow from underground roots and extend above the surface can serve as an effective absorber of organic matter.

It is also considered that the process of suspended sediment accumulation is a significant ecological and economic disadvantage permanently reducing the storage capacity of water reservoirs27,28. Progressive degradation of reservoirs can lead to changes that are difficult to reverse, not only within the reservoir itself, but can also affect changes in its catchment area and the surrounding landscape that are not easy to predict. Changing water-soil conditions caused by siltation of reservoirs may entail a chain of undesirable reactions in the surrounding biosphere.

Conclusions

The concentration of suspended sediment in the water of the studied reservoirs (SS) significantly determined the distribution of total phosphorus contained in the bottom sediments (TPSB). TPSB values were clearly consolidated around (SS) values. It was also found that the amplitude of suspended sediment concentrations (SS) manipulated the content of total organic carbon in the bottom sediments of the studied reservoirs (TOCSB). The analysis of the obtained relationships confirmed the thesis put in this study that sediments suspended in the water of dam reservoirs unify (integrate) all the phenomena and processes occurring between the various components in the water-bottom sediment system. Finally, a hitherto unknown factor associated with it has been identified that the circulation and distribution of selected biogenic compounds in dam reservoirs is closely related to sediments suspended in the water-depth. Thus, it was proven that suspended sediments play the role of a transmitter (hub) between the various components of the water-sediment system, thus significantly affecting the quality of deposited bottom sediments.

Nevertheless, due to, various types of factors such as catchment parameters, morphometric and hydrological characteristics of reservoirs, climatic conditions or season can play a significant role on the obtained study results, it is advisable to be cautious when comparing the results obtained at different sites and for other objects.

Logarithmic Fulton–MacPherson configuration spaces
Siao Chi Mok

Abstract
. Using techniques in logarithmic geometry, we construct a logarithmic analogue of the Fulton–MacPherson configuration spaces. We similarly construct a logarithmically smooth degeneration of the Fulton–MacPherson configuration spaces. Both constructions parametrise point configurations on certain target degenerations arising from both logarithmic geometry and the original Fulton–MacPherson construction. The degeneration satisfies a “degeneration formula” – each irreducible component of its special fibre can be described as a proper birational modification of a product of logarithmic Fulton–MacPherson configuration spaces.



1.
Introduction

Let
X
be a smooth projective variety over an algebraically closed field
k
. In the seminal paper
[
FM94
]
, Fulton and MacPherson constructed a simple normal crossings compactification
\mathrm{FM}_{n}(X)
of the ordered configuration space of
n
distinct points
\mathrm{Conf}_{n}(X)
. The compactification
\mathrm{FM}_{n}(X)
is constructed via an iterated blow-up of
X^{n}
along diagonals to separate the points. The boundary
\mathrm{FM}_{n}(X)\backslash\mathrm{Conf}_{n}(X)
parametrises point configurations on
Fulton–MacPherson (FM) degenerations
; when points collide with each other at
x\in X
, in the limit a
\mathbb{P}(T_{x}X\oplus\mathbbm{1})
-bubble is attached to
X
and the colliding points instead land in the bubble, on the smooth locus. This procedure is repeated until all points have been separated.

Among numerous applications, the Fulton–MacPherson space
\mathrm{FM}_{n}(X)
was used by Kim, Kresch and Oh in
[
KKO14
]
to build a moduli space of
stable ramified maps
, i.e. maps from curves with targets some Fulton–MacPherson degenerations of
X
, and to define and study
unramified Gromov–Witten theory
. It was conjectured by Pandharipande
[
KKO14
, Conjecture 5.2.1]
that when
X
is Fano, these unramified Gromov–Witten invariants are equal to Gopakumar–Vafa invariants, and this conjecture was later proved by Nesterov in
[
Nes25b
]
by obtaining a wall-crossing formula between Gromov–Witten invariants and unramified Gromov–Witten invariants.

Two questions about Fulton–MacPherson spaces motivate this present work.

Question 1.0.1
.

A compactification
X
of a non-proper variety
U
with simple normal crossings boundary
D\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}X\backslash U
is called a
simple normal crossings compactification
. Given a non-proper variety
U
with a simple normal crossings compactification
(X,D)
, how can we construct a simple normal crossings compactification of
\mathrm{Conf}_{n}(U)
?

Question 1.0.2
.

Given a degeneration
\omega\colon W\to B
of
X
over a curve
B
, where the special fibre is a simple normal crossings divisor, how does the space
\mathrm{FM}_{n}(X)
degenerate?

In characteristic zero, it follows from results of
[
Hir64
]
and
[
Nag62
]
that every variety
U
admits a simple normal crossings compactification. A degeneration as described in Question
1.0.2
is called a
simple normal crossings
or
semistable
degeneration.

Note that there exist quasi-projective varieties
U
without a compactification by a smooth divisor. A result by Danilov and Thuillier
[
Dan75
,
Thu07
]
says that the homotopy type of the dual complex of the boundary divisor (the
boundary complex
) is independent of the choice of compactification. Since the boundary complex of a compactification with smooth boundary is a point, any quasi-projective variety
U
admitting a compactification with non-contractible boundary complex cannot have a compactification with a smooth boundary. An example is
(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{2}
with boundary complex
\Delta(\mathbb{P}^{2}\backslash(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{2})
homotopic to
S^{1}
. Thus, it is desirable to work with the full generality of simple normal crossings compactifications.

With the initial compactification
(X,D)
of
U
as input, our goal is to construct a simple normal crossings compactification
\mathrm{FM}_{n}(X|D)
of
\mathrm{Conf}_{n}(X\backslash D)
.

Our plan is to proceed in two steps:

(1)

Construct a moduli space
(X|D)^{[n]}
of points on certain degenerations of
X
along
D
(
grid expansions
), to separate the points from the boundary;

(2)

Iteratively blow-up
(X|D)^{[n]}
to separate the points from each other, in the style of
[
FM94
]
. The resulting scheme is
\mathrm{FM}_{n}(X|D)
, which parametrises points on Fulton–MacPherson degenerations of the grid expansions (
FM grid expansions
).

In the special case when
D
is smooth, Question
1.0.1
has been answered via a similar approach by Kim and Sato
[
KS09
]
. The general case requires us to use recent ideas from logarithmic and tropical geometry.

Given a degeneration
\omega\colon W\to B
where its special fibre has smooth singular locus
D
, Abramovich and Fantechi
[
AF17
]
constructed a moduli space of points on the fibres of
expanded degenerations
. Building on this, Routis
[
Rou14
]
constructed a degeneration of
\mathrm{FM}_{n}(X)
, answering Question
1.0.2
when
D
is smooth.

1.1.
Main results

We answer Question
1.0.1
by first constructing a moduli space
(X|D)^{[n]}
of
stable
n
-pointed grid expansions
in Section
2
, then iteratively blow-up
(X|D)^{[n]}
to construct the desired compactification
\mathrm{FM}_{n}(X|D)
. The compactification
\mathrm{FM}_{n}(X|D)
comes with a family
\pi_{\mathrm{FM}}\colon\mathrm{FM}_{n}(X|D)^{+}\to\mathrm{FM}_{n}(X|D)
. In Section
3
we prove:

Theorem A

(Theorem
3.3.1
)
.

Let
(X,D)
be a simple normal crossings pair.

(1)

The scheme
\mathrm{FM}_{n}(X|D)
is a simple normal crossings compactification of
\mathrm{Conf}_{n}(X\backslash D)
.

(2)

The morphism
\pi_{\mathrm{FM}}
is a family of stable
n
-pointed FM grid expansions.

(3)

The scheme
\mathrm{FM}_{n}(X|D)
admits a stratification by combinatorial types.

We call
\mathrm{FM}_{n}(X|D)
the
logarithmic Fulton–MacPherson configuration space
of
(X,D)
. The
combinatorial type
of an
FM grid expansion
records the isomorphism type of the underlying scheme and for each marked point, the label of the component containing it.

These results generalise the constructions by Kim–Sato
[
KS09
]
, and Abramovich–Fantechi
[
AF17
]
. While these constructions all use the notion of
expansions
, stemming from the work of Gieseker and Jun Li
[
GL94
,
Li01
]
, differences remain. In constructing
(X|D)^{[n]}
, Kim–Sato constructed
(X|D)^{[n]}
using the theory of wonderful compactifications, whereas both Abramovich–Fantechi and this paper use an appropriate
stack of expanded degenerations
.

The stack of expansions also appears in the logarithmic curve counting theories of Maulik and Ranganathan in
[
Ran22
,
MR24
,
MR25
]
. When the simple normal crossings divisor
D
has multiple components, in these papers there are usually many choices of expansions depending on the tropical types of the curves and their maps to
(X,D)
, therefore there is no canonical stack of expansions. However, in this paper a
canonical
choice of expansions can be made, and universal weak semistable reduction
[
AK00
, Theorem 0.3]

[
Mol21
, Section 3.2]
enables a neat construction of the stack of expansions. We call these canonically chosen expansions
grid expansions
, as the components of these expansions are
(\mathbb{P}^{1})^{k}
-bundles arranged in a grid. These grid expansions are constructed from a canonical choice of
polyhedral subdivisions
of the tropicalisation of
(X,D)
.

The construction of
\mathrm{FM}_{n}(X|D)
has an interesting feature: when
\mathrm{FM}_{n}(X|D)
is given the divisorial logarithmic structure with respect to its boundary divisors, the tropicalisation of
\mathrm{FM}_{n}(X|D)
is, by construction, the moduli space of
planted forests
satisfying some conditions. See Figures
11
and
11
for examples of planted forests. In particular, when the boundary divisor
D
is empty, the tropicalisation is the space of
rooted trees
, as described in
[
FM94
, Section 2]
.

Given a simple normal crossings degeneration
\omega\colon W\to B
of
X
, we answer Question
1.0.2
by first constructing a moduli space
W_{\omega}^{[n]}
of
stable
n
-pointed expanded degenerations
in Section
4
, then iteratively blow-up
W_{\omega}^{[n]}
to construct the desired degeneration
\mathrm{FM}_{n}(W/B)\to B
of
\mathrm{FM}_{n}(X)
in Section
5
. This degeneration comes with a family
\pi_{\mathrm{FM}}^{W}\colon\mathrm{FM}_{n}(W/B)^{+}\to\mathrm{FM}_{n}(W/B)
. We prove the following:

Theorem B

(Theorem
5.2.4
)
.

(1)

The morphism
\mathrm{FM}_{n}(W/B)\to B
is a proper, flat, logarithmically smooth degeneration of
\mathrm{FM}_{n}(X)
with reduced fibres.

(2)

The morphism
\pi_{\mathrm{FM}}^{W}\colon\mathrm{FM}_{n}(W/B)^{+}\to\mathrm{FM}_{n}(W/B)
is a flat family of stable FM expanded degenerations with
n
sections
\mathbf{s}_{1},\mathbf{s}_{2},\ldots,\mathbf{s}_{n}
, where the sections are supported on the smooth locus of each fibre.

Like the construction in
[
MR25
, Section 8]
, the degeneration
\mathrm{FM}_{n}(W/B)\to B
comes with a
degeneration formula
. A
rigid combinatorial type

\rho
is a combinatorial type of FM expansions whose tropical moduli is a vertex. Let
\cal{S}_{\rho,1}
be the corresponding
n
-marked simplex-lattice subdivision, and
Y_{\rho}
be the corresponding expansion.

We prove in Section
5.3
the following:

Theorem C

(Corollary
5.3.5
)
.

(1)

The irreducible components of the special fibre of
\mathrm{FM}_{n}(W/B)\to B
biject with rigid combinatorial types
\rho
.

(2)

The irreducible component
\mathrm{FM}_{n}(W/B)(\rho)
is a (proper birational) modification of a product of logarithmic FM spaces of components of
Y_{\rho}
relative to their boundaries.

Expansions are constructed using methods from toric geometry, which is defined for schemes over any base ring. Therefore, the constructions of
\mathrm{FM}_{n}(X|D)
and
\mathrm{FM}_{n}(W/B)
extend to a scheme
X
smooth over any base ring and
D
a reduced, simple normal crossings divisor, and any simple normal crossings degeneration of
X
. Many results persist with minor adjustments, such as replacing “points” by “geometric points”. For simplicity, we work with a smooth projective variety
X
over an algebraically closed field, and
D
a simple normal crossings divisor on
X
.

1.2.
Potential applications

An application is to use a version of
\mathrm{FM}_{n}(X|D)
, constructed over the stack of expansions from
[
MR24
]
, to define and study the
logarithmic unramified Gromov–Witten theory
of a pair
(X,D)
– this is work in progress
[
Mok26
]
. The idea behind the degeneration package of
\mathrm{FM}_{n}(X)
developed in this paper is expected to lead to a degeneration formula for unramified Gromov–Witten theory of Kim–Kresch–Oh
[
KKO14
]
. We hope that this will lead to new applications.

Another application pertains to the intersection theory of classical and logarithmic Fulton–MacPherson spaces. For some suitably defined tautological classes
\psi_{i,j}
(
higher
\psi
-classes
) and for any polynomial
p(x_{i,j})
, one can consider an integral of the form

N_{n}^{X}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\int_{[\mathrm{FM}_{n}(X)]}p(\psi_{i,j}),

and a generating series

Z(X,q)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}1+\sum_{n\geq 1}N_{n}^{X}\frac{q^{n}}{n!}.

Similarly to
[
LP09
, Section 0.6]
, the degeneration formula (Theorem
C
) suggests that
Z(X,q)
is a cobordism invariant, thus like in
[
LP09
, Conjecture 1]
there is a universal polynomial
Q_{\dim(X),n,p(x_{i,j})}
such that

N_{n}^{X}=Q_{d,n,p(x_{i,j})}(c_{1}(X),c_{2}(X),\ldots,c_{\mathrm{top}}(X)),

where
c_{k}(X)=c_{k}(T_{X})
is the
k
th Chern number of
X
. The existence of this universal polynomial can also be deduced from the explicit presentation of the cohomology of
\mathrm{FM}_{n}(X)

[
FM94
, Theorem 6]
. The logarithmic analogue will be an interesting problem to study, and similar formulae for the classical Fulton–MacPherson space have been obtained by Nesterov in
[
Nes25a
]
.

1.3.
Proximate moduli

As noted before, the works of Kim–Sato
[
KS09
]
, Abramovich–Fantechi
[
AF17
]
and Routis
[
Rou14
]
are important precursors of the present work.

1.3.1.
Fulton–MacPherson space for relative curves

Consider the forgetful morphism

\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n+d}^{\log}\to\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,d}^{\log}

between moduli spaces of stable log curves, which is semistable. The geometric fibre over the point
(C,p_{n+1},\ldots,p_{n+d})\in\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,d}^{\log}
is then a logarithmically smooth compactification of
\mathrm{Conf}_{n}(C^{\text{sm}}\backslash\{p_{n+1},\ldots,p_{n+d}\})
. When
C
is smooth, this recovers
\mathrm{FM}_{n}(C|p_{n+1}+\cdots+p_{n+d})
. As a generalisation, Bejleri and Landesman constructed normal crossings compactifications of the Hurwitz spaces of
G
-covers of
C
and of the universal curve
\mathcal{C}\to\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,d}^{\log}
, see
[
EL25
, Theorem B.1.2, Theorem B.3.2, Remark B.3.4]
.

1.3.2.
Weighted Fulton–MacPherson spaces

In
[
Rou14
]
, Routis constructs a space which parametrises stable weighted configurations of points, generalising both the classical Fulton–MacPherson spaces and the Hassett spaces of weighted pointed rational curves. In the setting of
[
AF17
]
, a degeneration of weighted Fulton–MacPherson spaces has also been constructed in
[
Rou14
]
. We expect weighted analogues of our constructions to exist.

1.3.3.
Toric configuration spaces

The work of Nabijou
[
Nab24
]
studies compactifications of point configurations on an algebraic torus
(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{r}
. The combinatorial techniques in both
[
Nab24
]
and this paper are similar, and many approaches in this paper draw inspiration from it. A key difference is that the points are allowed to collide arbitrarily in the toric configuration spaces, but the points are separated in
\mathrm{FM}_{n}(X|D)
.

Nonetheless, we can still compare
(X|D)^{[n]}
with the toric configuration spaces. When
(X,D)
is a toric pair, the space
(X|D)^{[n]}
is an example of the toric configuration spaces. An interesting example can be found in Section
2.9.2
. However, we construct
(X|D)^{[n]}
by fixing a choice of subdivisions, whereas Nabijou studies the collection of all subdivisions with some interesting modular interpretations in combinatorics. There also exist toric configuration spaces which cannot be globally expressed in the form
(X|D)^{[n]}
for any toric variety
X
.

1.3.4.
Degenerations of Hilbert scheme of points

Given any semistable degeneration
W\to B
of a surface
X
, the work of Tschanz
[
Tsc26
]
gives an explicit local construction of a semistable degeneration of the Hilbert schemes of points of
X
. This construction considers 0-dimensional subschemes on a fixed choice of expansions of the special fibre
W_{0}
, which parallels the degeneration construction in this paper. A key difference lies in the choice of expansions; the choice in
[
Tsc26
]
makes the degenerations smooth but not symmetric, whereas the degenerations in this paper can be singular but are symmetric.

Like the degeneration construction in this work, the construction in
[
Tsc26
]
is local. A recent follow-up by Shafi and Tschanz
[
ST25
]
describes a strategy to globalise the local construction in
[
Tsc26
]
to a given arbitrary type III degeneration of a K3 surface, giving a good type III degeneration of
\mathrm{Hilb}^{m}(\mathrm{K3})
. These are the first explicit examples of a good type III degeneration of hyperkähler varieties with dimension greater than two.

1.3.5.
Pointed trees of projective spaces and polypermutohedral varieties

The works
[
CGK09
]
and
[
GGAG26
]
feature moduli which utilise classical, weighted and logarithmic Fulton–MacPherson spaces. The work
[
GGAG26
]
compactifies the space of distinct weighted points on a flag via weighted Fulton–MacPherson spaces, which recovers the construction in
[
CGK09
]
. In addition, the work
[
GGAG26
]
also compactifies the space of not necessarily distinct points on a flag via the construction of Kim–Sato
[
KS09
]
, denoted
(X|D)^{[n]}
in this paper. Both constructions admit meaningful interpretations in combinatorics, and it would be interesting to explore possible interactions between our works.

In the construction of
\mathrm{FM}_{n}(X|D)
, there is a significant increase in complexity compared to Kim–Sato when the divisor
D
is not smooth, so it is not clear how to construct
\mathrm{FM}_{n}(X|D)
using wonderful compactifications. Meanwhile, we note that the stack of expansions in
[
MR24
]
is only defined up to certain birational transformations. In comparison, this paper benefits from a canonical choice of expansions, namely
grid expansions
, enabling a more detailed and granular description of the stack of grid expansions. The key insight in this paper is the systematic use of tropical and logarithmic geometry, especially Artin fans to construct the stack of grid expansions.

Terminologies.
The
boundary
of a compactification
\overline{\mathcal{M}}
of a space
\mathcal{M}
is the complement
\overline{\mathcal{M}}\backslash\mathcal{M}
. A compactification is
simple normal crossings
when the boundary is a simple normal crossing divisor. The pair
(X,D)
refers to a smooth projective variety
X
and a simple normal crossings divisor
D=D_{1}+\cdots+D_{r}
, such that any intersection stratum
\cap_{i\in I}D_{i}
is connected. We only consider
fine and saturated

Zariski

will refer to a strongly convex rational polyhedral cone.

2.
Moduli space of points on expansions

The main goal of this section is to construct a modular compactification
(X|D)^{[n]}
of
(X\backslash D)^{n}
, which parametrises points on
grid expansions
of
X
.

2.1.
Preliminaries on tropicalisation and logarithmic modifications

In this section, we review some techniques used in constructing logarithmic moduli spaces. We refer the reader to
[
CCUW20
, Section 6]
,
[
MR24
, Section 1]
,
[
MR25
, Section 1]
,
[
Uli17
, Section 4]
and
[
Ogu18
]
for standard definitions and results in logarithmic geometry.

Definition 2.1.1

(Tropicalisation of a pair)
.

Let
(X,D)
be a smooth projective variety
X
equipped with a simple normal crossings divisor
D
with irreducible components
D_{1},\dots,D_{r}
such that any intersection
\cap_{i\in I}D_{i}
is connected. Then the
tropicalisation

\Sigma_{X}\subset\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{r}
is a rational polyhedral cone complex consisting of 1-dimensional cones
e_{i}
, which are the rays of
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{r}
, and
k
-dimensional cones spanned by
e_{i_{1}},\ldots,e_{i_{k}}
whenever the intersection of
D_{i_{1}},\dots,D_{i_{k}}
is non-empty.

Definition 2.1.2

(Artin fan of a pair)
.

Given a simple normal crossings pair
(X,D)
with tropicalisation
\Sigma_{X}\subset\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{r}
, its
Artin fan

\mathcal{A}_{X}
is defined as the open substack of the toric quotient stack
[\mathbb{A}^{r}/\mathbb{G}_{m}^{r}]
formed by taking the substack corresponding to the cones in
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{r}
that are present in
\Sigma_{X}
.

Sections 2 and 6 of
[
CCUW20
]
introduced the 2-category of cone stacks and the 2-category of Artin fans, and constructed a functor
a^{*}
from the 2-category of cone stacks to the 2-category of Artin fans. Every cone complex is naturally a cone stack.

Theorem 2.1.3

(
[
CCUW20
]
Theorem 6.11)
.

The functor
a^{*}
is an equivalence of 2-categories.

There is a canonical morphism
X\to\mathcal{A}_{X}\hookrightarrow[\mathbb{A}^{r}/\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}^{r}]
, given by line bundle and section pairs
(L_{i},s_{i})
corresponding to the effective Cartier divisor
D=D_{1}+\cdots+D_{r}
.

Concretely, since
D
is a simple normal crossings divisor, at each point
p
, there exist local coordinates
x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}
such that
D
is of the form
x_{i_{1}}\ldots x_{i_{k}}=0
, and each
x_{i_{j}}
can be taken to be the stalk of
s_{j}
at
p
. Therefore, there exists an open neighbourhood
U_{p}
of
p
such that
U_{p}
is canonically isomorphic to an open neighbourhood of
(\underline{0},\underline{1})\in\mathbb{A}^{k}\times\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}^{d-k}
. We call
U_{p}
a
toric chart
at
p
. This gives a map

U_{p}\xrightarrow{\text{\'{e}tale}}\mathbb{A}^{k}\times\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}^{d-k}\to[\mathbb{A}^{k}\times\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}^{d-k}/\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}^{k}]\cong[\mathbb{A}^{k}/\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}^{k}]\hookrightarrow\mathcal{A}_{X},

where the last map corresponds via Theorem
2.1.3
to a face inclusion
\sigma\hookrightarrow\Sigma_{X}
, where
\sigma
is the cone corresponding to the intersection
D_{i_{1}}\cap\dots\cap D_{i_{k}}
containing

. We note that this map glues to a global map
X\to\mathcal{A}_{X}
, and we note that it is flat.

In the language of cone complexes, this map assigns the strata of
X
to the corresponding cones. Here, a stratum of
X
is of the form

D_{I}^{\circ}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\{x\in X\;|\;x\in D_{i}\text{ if and only if }i\in I\}.

Definition 2.1.4
.

A (conical)
subdivision

\widetilde{\Sigma}
of a cone complex
\Sigma
is a morphism
\widetilde{\Sigma}\to\Sigma
that is bijective on the supports, such that the lattice points of each cone
\tau\in\tilde{\Sigma}
are exactly the intersection of the lattice points of
\Sigma
with the image of
\tau
.

Functoriality.
Given two simple normal crossings pairs
(X,D)
and
(Y,E)
and a map of pairs
f\colon(X,D)\to(Y,E)
, satisfying
f^{-1}(E)\subset D
, then functoriality holds for tropicalisations and Artin fans: there are induced maps
\Sigma_{X}\to\Sigma_{Y}
and

.

Functoriality holds for maps between logarithmic schemes
X\to Y
when
X
is logarithmically smooth of characteristic zero
[
ACGS20
, Proposition 2.8]
, and when
X,Y
are
atomic
, i.e. admits a global toric chart
[
HMPP25
, Lemma A.18]
, but does not hold in general
[
ACM+16
]
.

Logarithmic modification.
A subdivision
\widetilde{\Sigma_{X}}\to\Sigma_{X}
corresponds to a modification of Artin fans
\widetilde{\mathcal{A}_{X}}\to\mathcal{A}_{X}
via Theorem
2.1.3
, and by pulling back the modification along the flat morphism

we obtain a modification
\widetilde{X}\to X
(see
[

, Section 1.4]
).

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widetilde{\mathcal{A}_{X}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}

More concretely, the subdivision of cone complexes

induces toric modifications of toric charts of

of the form

, and one can check that these local modifications glue to a global modification of

2.2.
Tropicalisation map and transversality

In this subsection, we give a description of the boundary points of
(X|D)^{[n]}
in terms of tropical geometry. When a family of
n
points in
X

D
, tropical geometry suggests the following roadmap:

(1)

Suppose the Cartier divisor

is locally described by functions

. Let
\Spec R
be a discrete valuation ring with valuation

Remark 2.3.5

Theorem 2.6.1

. It represents the moduli stack of stable

Proof.

The first statement of the theorem is a direct consequence of the construction of

as a logarithmic modification of

. As

is a complete subdivision of

, therefore

3.1.

Definition 3.1.1

•

See Figures

, and each

. The

.

•

•

Following

are lifts of the rubber actions. Each component whose vertex is non-root and of valence 1, is called an

and is of the form

Definition 3.1.3

An

-pointed FM grid expansions

3.4.
Remarks about functoriality and birational models

Functoriality does not hold in general for Fulton–MacPherson spaces; this is because functoriality fails in general for blow-ups. A specific case where functoriality holds is where
f\colon X\hookrightarrow Y
is an embedding; since the preimage of a diagonal
\Delta^{Y}_{I}
in
Y^{n}
is the diagonal
\Delta^{X}_{I}
in
X^{n}
, by standard results e.g.
[
Har77
, Corollary II.7.15]
this gives an embedding
\mathrm{FM}_{n}(X)\hookrightarrow\mathrm{FM}_{n}(Y)
.

A weaker version of functoriality holds for a log blow-up
f\colon(Y,E)\to(X,D)
: there is a map between the logarithmic FM spaces after passing to a further modification, i.e.
\widetilde{\mathrm{FM}_{n}(Y|E)}\to\mathrm{FM}_{n}(X|D)
. Note that there is no map
(Y|E)^{[n]}\to(X|D)^{[n]}
due to incompatibility of grid subdivisions of
\Sigma_{Y}
and
\Sigma_{X}
; the map of sets
\Pi_{n}(\Sigma_{Y})\to\Pi_{n}(\Sigma_{X})
is not a map of cone complexes. However, we can pass to a common refinement of
n
-marked grid subdivisions of
\Sigma_{Y}
and
\Sigma_{X}
, thus obtaining a new tropical moduli space
\widetilde{\Pi}_{n}
which is a subdivision of both
\Pi_{n}(\Sigma_{Y})
and
\Pi_{n}(\Sigma_{X})
. This gives logarithmic modifications
\widetilde{(Y|E)^{[n]}}\to(Y|E)^{[n]}
,
\widetilde{(Y|E)^{[n]}}\to(X|D)^{[n]}
.

Since the
n
marked points lie in the interior of expansion components, the preimage of the diagonal
\delta_{I}
in
(Y|E)^{[n]}
is the diagonal
\delta_{I}
in
\widetilde{(Y|E)^{[n]}}
, and an analogous statement holds for
(X|D)^{[n]}
. Therefore, there are maps
\widetilde{\mathrm{FM}_{n}(Y|E)}\to\mathrm{FM}_{n}(Y|E)
and
\widetilde{\mathrm{FM}_{n}(Y|E)}\to\mathrm{FM}_{n}(X|D)
; in fact one can check both maps are logarithmic modifications.

Proof of Theorem

.

The scheme

. By

We check that the morphism

is flat. We observe that

is smooth, and the fibres are equidimensional by Observation

. Since

has at worst toric singularities, hence it is Cohen–Macaulay. It follows that

, which is an iterated blow-up of

along smooth centres, is Cohen–Macaulay. By miracle flatness, the morphism

is flat.

To prove that

is logarithmically smooth, it is equivalent to prove that

is smooth, where

is the Artin fan of

. Since each stratum of

is smooth and of expected dimension,

is smooth and
\mathrm{FM}_{n}(W/B)
is Cohen–Macaulay, the morphism is flat and has smooth fibres, hence smooth.

The morphism
\pi_{\mathrm{FM}}^{W}
is a family of stable
n
-pointed Fulton–MacPherson expanded degenerations by Lemma
5.2.3
.

As
\pi_{\mathrm{FM}}^{W}
is logarithmically smooth by Proposition
5.2.7
, by Tsuji
[
Tsu19
, Theorem II.4.2]
to prove flatness it suffices to prove that the corresponding map of tropicalisations is combinatorially flat. Here it is crucial to use the refined logarithmic structure.

Over a cone in
\mathrm{FM}_{n}(W/B)
corresponding to
\tau_{\mathrm{FM}}
, there can be three possible cones in
\mathrm{FM}_{n}(W/B)^{+}
mapping to the cone in
\mathrm{FM}_{n}(W/B)
:

•

The point
p_{0}
lies on
\mathsf{S}
. The combinatorial type is some
(\mathsf{S}^{+},m^{+},\mathtt{T}_{v_{1}},\dots,\mathtt{T}_{v_{k}})
, where
\tau^{+}=(\mathsf{S}^{+},m^{+})
is a combinatorial type in the family
\Pi_{n}^{+}(\Delta)
lying over the combinatorial type
\tau
in
\Pi_{n}(\Delta)
. The corresponding cone is then
\mathrm{cone}(\tau^{+})\times\prod_{i=1}^{k}\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{|E(\mathtt{T}_{v_{i}})|}
.

•

The point
p_{0}
lies on a vertex of a tree
\mathtt{T}_{v_{j}}
. In this case, the cone corresponding to this combinatorial type is the same as the cone
\mathrm{cone}(\tau)\times\prod_{i=1}^{k}\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{|E(\mathtt{T}_{v_{i}})|}
, as there is no additional length parameter.

•

The point
p_{0}
lies on a bounded edge

of a tree
\mathtt{T}_{v_{j}}
. In this case, the position of

on the edge introduces an additional length parameter

on the combinatorial type, therefore the cone is

We have listed all the cones in

. For combinatorial flatness, we need to show that each cone in
\Pi_{n}^{\text{FM},+}(\Delta)
surjects onto a cone in
\Pi_{n}^{\text{FM}}(\Delta)
. In the first scenario, combinatorial flatness follows from the combinatorial flatness of

\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{|E(\mathtt{T}_{v_{j}})|+1}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{|E(\mathtt{T}_{v_{j}})|}

is surjective. The map
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{|E(\mathtt{T}_{v_{j}})|+1}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{|E(\mathtt{T}_{v_{j}})|}

Therefore, the map

is combinatorially flat, and this concludes the proof.

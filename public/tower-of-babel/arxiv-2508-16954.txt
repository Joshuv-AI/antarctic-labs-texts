Abstract
. We investigate the concept of a “Chevalley involution” within the framework of root-graded Lie algebras with compatible grading. We provide a characterization of all centerless Lie tori of type A ℓ ​ ( ℓ ≥ 2 ) A_{\ell}(\ell\geq 2) admitting a Chevalley involution. This work extends and completes the earlier results regarding the existence of such involutions for Lie tori of reduced types.

1.
Introduction

The concept of a Lie algebra graded by a finite root system or a root-graded Lie algebra over a field of characteristic zero was introduced by Berman and Moody
[
9
]
. Let
R
be an irreducible reduced finite root system. A Lie algebra graded by
R
or an
R
-graded Lie algebra is a triple
(\mathcal{L},\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h})
consisting of a Lie algebra
\mathcal{L}
, a finite-dimensional simple Lie subalgebra
\mathfrak{g}
of
\mathcal{L}
and a Cartan subalgebra
\mathfrak{h}
of
\mathfrak{g}
, so that
\mathfrak{g}
has the root system
R
with respect to
\mathfrak{h}
, satisfying certain natural conditions (see Definition
2.1
).
Berman and Moody showed that if
R
is simply-laced of rank
\geq 2
, then there is an algebra
\mathcal{A}
attached to
\mathcal{L}
, called the coordinate algebra of
\mathcal{L}
, which depending on the type and rank of
R
, is either commutative and associative, only associative, or alternative (see Proposition
2.2
). Their motivation was to study and understand the intersection matrix algebras (IM algebras) arising from multiply-affinized Cartan matrices of simply-laced types. Toward this end, they classified
R
-graded Lie algebras, up to central extensions, when
R
is simply-laced of rank
\geq 2
. Then, they applied this classification to obtain a realization for these IM algebras.

Benkart and Zelmanov continued the study of root-graded Lie algebras in
[
8
]
. They completed the classification of these Lie algebras for the remaining reduced types. Using a Jordan theoretical approach, Neher described
R
-graded Lie algebras in a unified way, when
R
is not of type
E_{8},F_{4}
or
G_{2}

[
16
]
. His results remain valid for root systems of infinite rank and Lie algebras over rings.

It is well known that Lie tori are root-graded Lie algebras. The concept of a Lie torus arises in the study of extended affine Lie algebras (or EALAs in short)
[
25
]
,
[
18
]
,
[
17
]
. EALAs are natural higher dimensional generalizations of finite-dimensional simple Lie algebras and affine Kac-Moody Lie algebras
[
1
]
,
[
19
]
. This class of Lie algebras has been under intensive investigation for the past few decades. The core of an extended affine Lie algebra is its subalgebra generated by non-isotropic root spaces. It is well understood that an EALA’s structure can be comprehended through analyzing and coordinatizing its core (or core modulo the center, called the centerless core). This is the point at which the concept of a Lie torus emerges; the centerless core of any EALA is a centerless Lie torus, and conversely, any centerless Lie torus is the centerless core of an EALA
[
25
]
. Using structure theory and the well-known recognition theorems of root-graded Lie algebras, the coordinatization theorems for Lie tori have been proved and the structure of EALAs have been determined in
[
11
]
,
[
10
]
,
[
21
]
and
[
4
]
. According to the type and rank, the coordinate algebras that occur are certain unital associative, Jordan, or alternative algebras, referred to as tori (see Definition
2.13
).

Now, let us clarify the origin of our motivation for this work. A Lie algebra over a field of positive characteristic is called a modular Lie algebra. In the classical Lie theory, the work of Chevalley on integral forms (
{\mathbb{Z}}
-forms) establishes a framework for the transition from non-modular to modular theory, which led to the construction of Chevalley groups. In this context, Chevalley involutions play an important role.

Originally, extended affine Lie algebras were defined over the field of complex numbers. Later, a definition of an EALA over a field of characteristic zero was proposed by a slight modification of the defining axioms of a complex EALA
[
17
]
. Additionally, in
[
15
]
a more general form of an EALA was introduced over a field of characteristic zero, known as a locally extended affine Lie algebra. In
[
6
]
, the authors started studying extended affine Lie algebras from the modular point of view. In particular, they proved the existence of certain integral forms for the cores of reduced extended affine Lie algebras of rank at least
2
that are equipped with a Chevalley involution; an involution that acts as minus identity on the Cartan subalgebra. By analogy with finite and affine cases, this allows to define a counterpart for these involutive extended affine Lie algebras in the modular setting. Here, arises a question; “If any EALA admits a Chevalley involution?”; and if the answer is no; “Which EALAs are involutive?”

We investigated the existence problem of Chevalley involutions for EALAs in
[
7
]
. To do this, we first introduced the notion of a Chevalley involution for a Lie torus and studied the conditions under which a Chevalley involution on the centerless core of an EALA, characterized to be a centerless Lie torus, can be extended initially to the core and subsequently to the entire EALA naturally. With case-by-case considerations and employing the coordinatization theorems of Lie tori, we then examined the existence of Chevalley involutions for centerless Lie tori of reduced types. It turns out that the Chevalley involutions of Lie tori are tied with the Chevalley involutions of finite-dimensional simple Lie algebras and certain (anti)-involutions of their coordinate algebras (various tori), called pre-Chevalley (anti)-involutions; see Definition
2.8
(i). Through this, we proved that almost all centerless Lie tori of reduced types admit a Chevalley involution. The exception is for type
A_{\ell}(\ell\geq 2)
; we showed that a centerless Lie torus of type
A_{\ell}(\ell\geq 2)
admits a Chevalley involution if its coordinate algebra is equipped with a pre-Chevalley (anti)-involution. So, the class of all such involutive Lie tori for this type remained unidentified.

In this direction, this study proceeds in two steps. First, we introduce the notion of a Chevalley involution for
R
-graded Lie algebras
\mathcal{L}=(\mathcal{L},\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h})
that admit a compatible grading over a free abelian group
\Lambda
. In fact, an
R
-graded Lie algebra
\mathcal{L}=(\mathcal{L},\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h})
is said to be
(R,\Lambda)
-graded if
\mathcal{L}=\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\mathcal{L}^{\lambda}
is
\Lambda
-graded, and
\mathcal{L}^{0}
contains the finite-dimensional simple Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
with Cartan subalgebra
\mathfrak{h}
. This class of Lie algebras was introduced by Yoshii as a generalization of root-graded Lie algebras. He studied predivision
(R,\Lambda)
-graded Lie algebras where
\Lambda
is an abelian group, and classified them up to central extensions, when
R
is simply-laced of rank
\geq 3
and
\Lambda={\mathbb{Z}}^{n}

[
22
]
. The cores of EALAs serve as examples of these
(R,\Lambda)
-graded Lie algebras.

We continue to explore how Chevalley involutions relate to the concept of an involution compatible with the root-grading (Definition
3.1
), as introduced by Gao for root-graded Lie algebras
[
14
]
. In his paper, Gao studied involutive root-graded Lie algebras to identify compact forms of IM algebras. Some of his work could be viewed as a unitary version of
[
9
]
.

We prove that any Chevalley involution of an
(R,\Lambda)
-graded Lie algebra
\mathcal{L}
is compatible with the
R
-grading, see Proposition
3.5
. By applying the results of
[
14
,
\lx@sectionsign 2
]
to our context, we obtain some primary results on Chevalley involutions of
(R,\Lambda)
-graded Lie algebras. In particular, we show that any Chevalley involution of an
(R,\Lambda)
-graded Lie algebra induces a pre-Chevalley (anti)-involution on its coordinate algebra when
R
is simply-laced of rank
\geq 2
, see Proposition
3.10
.

In the second step of this work, as a special subclass of root-graded Lie algebras with compatible grading, we examine centerless Lie tori of type
A_{\ell}
, for
\ell\geq 2
. It turns out that there exist certain Lie tori of this type which do not admit any Chevalley involution. We characterize all Lie tori of type
A_{\ell}(\ell\geq 2)
admitting a Chevalley involution, see Theorem
4.7
. The coordinatization theorem for Lie tori of this type is essential in our characterization; see Theorem
4.2
and Theorem
4.6
. In fact, considering Proposition
3.10
, we identify the coordinate algebras of centerless Lie tori of type
A_{\ell}(\ell\geq 2)
admitting a pre-Chevalley (anti)-involution, which are the octonion torus and elementary quantum tori (for definition of these tori see
4.5
and
4.3
, respectively). This finalizes the investigation of Chevalley involutions for Lie tori of reduced types, as initiated in
[
7
]
.

The organization of the paper is as follows. In Section
2
basic definitions and notation from the theory of root-graded Lie algebras are collected. In addition, we record some necessary background and results from
[
9
]
needed to describe the coordinate algebras of these Lie algebras. Then we recall the definition of a root-graded Lie algebra with compatible grading; see Definition
2.4
. Here, we introduce the notion of a Chevalley involution for this class of Lie algebras; see Definition
2.8
. At the end of the section, Lie tori are defined as a subclass of root-graded Lie algebras with compatible grading, see Definition
2.11
.

Section
3
discusses the involutions of an
R
-graded Lie algebra that are compatible with the
R
-grading, see Definition
3.1
. Here, some required results from
[
14
,
\lx@sectionsign 2
]
regarding these involutions are restated.
We show that Chevalley involutions for an
(R,\Lambda)
-graded Lie algebra are compatible with the
R
-grading, see Proposition
3.5
. This enables us to deduce certain immediate consequences concerning Chevalley involutions of
(R,\Lambda)
-graded Lie algebras, which will be essential for the proof of our main result in the next section.

Finally, in Section
4
by revisiting the coordinatization theorems related to Lie tori of type
A_{\ell}
,
\ell\geq 2
, we determine all Lie tori of these types that possess a Chevalley involution, as presented in Theorem
4.7
. This completes the characterization of Lie tori of reduced types admitting a Chevalley involution, paving the way for further study of the modular theory of extended affine Lie algebras.

2.
Preliminaries

Throughout this work
{\mathbb{K}}
is a field of characteristic
0
. All vector spaces and algebras are assumed to be over
{\mathbb{K}}
and all algebras (except Lie algebras) are assumed to be unital. If
X
is a subset of a group, we denote by
\langle X\rangle
the subgroup generated by
X
. For a positive integer
n
, we denote by
{\mathbb{K}}[x_{1}^{\pm 1},\ldots,x_{n}^{\pm 1}]
the associative algebra of Laurent polynomials over
\mathbb{K}
in
n
commuting variables
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}
.
For a Lie algebra
\mathcal{L}
, by
Z(\mathcal{L})
we mean the center of
\mathcal{L}
. Also, by a centerless Lie algebra, we mean a Lie algebra with a trivial center.

In this work, we always assign the symbol
R
to denote an irreducible reduced finite root system. By
R^{\times}
, we mean
R\setminus\{0\}
.

2.1.
Root-graded Lie algebras

Here we consider Lie algebras graded by the root lattice of a finite root system, the so-called root-graded Lie algebras introduced by S. Berman and R. V. Moody
[
9
]
.
We begin by recalling the definition of a root-graded Lie algebra.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a finite-dimensional simple Lie algebra with Cartan subalgebra
\mathfrak{h}
and
R
be the root system of
\mathfrak{g}
with respect to
\mathfrak{h}
. A Lie algebra
\mathcal{L}=(\mathcal{L},\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h})
is called
graded by

R
or
R
-graded
with
grading pair

(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h})
if

(RG1)
\mathcal{L}
contains
\mathfrak{g}
as a subalgebra,

(RG2)
\mathcal{L}=\bigoplus_{\alpha\in R}\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}
, where
\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}=\{x\in\mathcal{L}\mid[h,x]=\alpha(h)x,\text{for all}\;h\in\mathfrak{h}\}
,

(RG3)
\mathcal{L}_{0}=\sum_{\alpha\in R^{\times}}[\mathcal{L}_{\alpha},\mathcal{L}_{-\alpha}]
.

We will use the abbreviation
\mathcal{L}
for
(\mathcal{L},\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h})
when there is no confusion.

Let
\mathcal{L}
be an
R
-graded Lie algebra with grading pair
(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h})
. We have the usual root space decomposition
\mathfrak{g}=\bigoplus_{\alpha\in R}\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}
with
\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}\subseteq\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}
for
\alpha\in R
. We fix a Chevalley basis
\{e_{\alpha},h_{i}\mid e_{\alpha}\in\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha},\alpha\in R^{\times},h_{i}\in\mathfrak{h},1\leq i\leq\ell\}
of
\mathfrak{g}
, where
\ell=\dim\mathfrak{h}
. Let
G=\langle\exp te_{\alpha}\mid t\in\mathbb{K},\alpha\in R^{\times}\rangle
be the corresponding universal Chevalley group and set

\eta_{\alpha}(t):=(\exp te_{\alpha})(\exp(-t^{-1}e_{-\alpha}))(\exp te_{\alpha}),

\mathcal{N}:=\langle\eta_{\alpha}(t)\mid t\in\mathbb{K}^{\times},\alpha\in R^{\times}\rangle,

\mathcal{T}:=\langle\eta_{\alpha}(t)\eta_{\alpha}(1)^{-1}\mid t\in\mathbb{K}^{\times},\alpha\in R^{\times}\rangle.

It is well-known that
\mathcal{T}
is a normal subgroup of
\mathcal{N}
and
\mathcal{N}/\mathcal{T}\cong{\mathcal{W}}
, where
{\mathcal{W}}
is the Weyl group of
R
. We consider the group homomorphism

\begin{array}[]{c}\hbox{Ad}:G\rightarrow\hbox{Aut}\mathcal{L}\\
\exp te_{\alpha}\mapsto\exp\hbox{ad}te_{\alpha}.\end{array}

We now assume that
R
is of type
A_{\ell}(\ell\geq 2),D_{\ell}(\ell\geq 4),E_{6,7,8}
. Here, we briefly review some results from
[
9
, §1]
that will be needed later. Let
\alpha,\beta\in R^{\times}
. One can choose
w\in{\mathcal{W}}
so that
w(\alpha)=\beta
. Let
\eta\in\mathcal{N}
with
\eta\mathcal{T}\leftrightarrow w
under the isomorphism
\mathcal{N}/\mathcal{T}\cong{\mathcal{W}}
. Considering
\mathcal{L}
as an integrable
\mathfrak{g}
-module via adjoint representation, then
\hbox{Ad}(\eta)(\mathcal{L}_{\alpha})=\mathcal{L}_{\beta}
. Let
\tilde{\theta}_{\beta,\alpha}:=\hbox{Ad}(\eta)|_{\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}}
. We note that
\tilde{\theta}_{\beta,\alpha}
is determined by
\alpha,\beta
up to a non-zero scalar multiple and does not depend on the choice of
w
or
\eta
. Since
\eta(\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha})=\mathfrak{g}_{\beta}
, then
\eta(e_{\alpha})=\epsilon e_{\beta}
, for some
\epsilon\in\mathbb{K}^{\times}
. Set

(2.1)

\theta_{\beta,\alpha}:=\epsilon^{-1}\tilde{\theta}_{\beta,\alpha}:\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}\rightarrow\mathcal{L}_{\beta}.

So
\theta_{\beta,\alpha}
depends only on
\alpha,\beta
and the choice of the Chevalley basis. We have

\displaystyle\theta_{\alpha,\beta}=\theta^{-1}_{\beta,\alpha},

\displaystyle\theta_{\alpha,\beta}\theta_{\beta,\gamma}=\theta_{\alpha,\gamma},

\displaystyle\theta_{\alpha,\alpha}=\hbox{id}.

For a fixed
\alpha\in R^{\times}
, let
\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}
as a
\mathbb{K}
-vector space. We proceed to put an algebra structure on
\mathcal{A}
, not depending on the particular root
\alpha
. Thus, for any
a\in\mathcal{A}
, we denote it by
e_{\alpha}(a)
when considering it as an element of
\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}
. Now, let
\beta\in R^{\times}
and define
e_{\beta}(a)=\theta_{\beta,\alpha}{(e_{\alpha}(a))\in\mathcal{L}_{\beta}}
.

One can define a multiplication on
\mathcal{A}
via the notion of an
A_{2}
-pair. Recall that a pair of linearly independent roots
\alpha,\beta\in R^{\times}
is called an
A_{2}
-pair
if
({\mathbb{Z}}\alpha\oplus{\mathbb{Z}}\beta)\cap R
forms an
A_{2}
-finite root system in
{\mathbb{R}}\alpha\oplus{\mathbb{R}}\beta
. Now fix an
A_{2}
-pair
(\beta,\gamma)
and define a multiplication
m_{(\beta,\gamma)}:\mathcal{A}\times\mathcal{A}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}
by

(2.2)

[e_{\beta}(a),e_{\gamma}(b)]:=[e_{\beta},e_{\gamma}](m_{(\beta,\gamma)}(a,b)),

for
a,b\in\mathcal{A}
. Note that the
A_{2}
-pair
(\gamma,\beta)
defines the opposite multiplications to
m_{(\beta,\gamma)}
, that is,
m_{(\gamma,\beta)}(a,b)=m_{(\beta,\gamma)}(b,a)
for all
a,b\in\mathcal{A}
(see
[
9
, 1.18]
). Multiplication (
2.2
) makes
\mathcal{A}
into a
\mathbb{K}
-algebra. It is called the
coordinate algebra (ring of coordinates)
of
\mathcal{L}
. Moreover:

Theorem 2.2

(
[
9
, 1.19, 3.17]
)
.

The
\mathbb{K}
-algebra
\mathcal{A}
is

(i)

associative if
R
is of rank at least
3
;

(ii)

commutative if
R
is of type
D
or
E
;

(iii)

alternative if
R
is of type
A_{2}
.

In addition, when
R
is of type
D
or
E
, then the multiplication is independent of the choice of
A_{2}
-pair. If
R
is of type
A
, then the two equivalence classes of
A_{2}
-pairs define opposite multiplications on
\mathcal{A}
.

We recall that two perfect Lie algebras are said to be centrally isogeneous if their universal covering algebras are isomorphic.

Proposition 2.3

(
[
9
, 1.29]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{L}_{1}
and
\mathcal{L}_{2}
be centrally isogeneous Lie algebras. Assume that
\mathcal{L}_{1}
is graded by
R
. Then
\mathcal{L}_{2}
is also
R
-graded and in such a way that the associative coordinate algebras are isomorphic.

2.2.
Chevalley involution for
(R,\Lambda)
-graded Lie algebras

Let
\Lambda
be a free abelian group and as before, let
R
be an irreducible reduced finite root system. First, we recall from
[
22
]
the notion of an
(R,\Lambda)
-graded Lie algebra.

Definition 2.4
.

We say an
R
-graded Lie algebra
\mathcal{L}
with grading pair
(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h})
admits a compatible
\Lambda
-grading or
\mathcal{L}
is
(R,\Lambda)
-graded
if
\mathcal{L}=\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\mathcal{L}^{\lambda}
is a
\Lambda
-graded Lie algebra so that
\mathfrak{g}\subseteq\mathcal{L}^{0}
and
\langle\hbox{supp}(\mathcal{L})\rangle=\Lambda
, where
\hbox{supp}(\mathcal{L})=\{\lambda\in\Lambda\mid\mathcal{L}^{\lambda}\neq\{0\}\}
.

Note that since
\mathfrak{g}\subseteq\mathcal{L}^{0}
, each
\mathcal{L}^{\lambda}
is an
\mathfrak{h}
-module and then we get
\mathcal{L}=\bigoplus_{\alpha\in R,\lambda\in\Lambda}\mathcal{L}^{\lambda}_{\alpha}
, where
\mathcal{L}^{\lambda}_{\alpha}:=\mathcal{L}^{\lambda}\cap\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}
for
\lambda\in\Lambda
and
\alpha\in R
.

Definition 2.5
.

An
(R,\Lambda)
-graded Lie algebra
\mathcal{L}
is called

(i)

predivision
if for each
\alpha\in R^{\times}
with
\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}^{\lambda}\neq(0)
, there exist
e\in\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}^{\lambda}
and
f\in\mathcal{L}_{-\alpha}^{-\lambda}
such that

[[e,f],x_{\beta}]=\langle\beta,\alpha^{\vee}\rangle x_{\beta},

for
\beta\in R,x_{\beta}\in\mathcal{L}_{\beta}
. Here
\alpha^{\vee}
is the coroot of
\alpha
in the sense of
[
12
]
and
\langle\beta,\alpha^{\vee}\rangle
is the corresponding Cartan integer;

(ii)

division
if for each
\alpha\in R^{\times},\lambda\in\Lambda
and
0\neq e\in\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}^{\lambda}
there exists
f\in\mathcal{L}_{-\alpha}^{-\lambda}
such that

[[e,f],x_{\beta}]=\langle\beta,\alpha^{\vee}\rangle x_{\beta},

for
\beta\in R,x_{\beta}\in\mathcal{L}_{\beta}
.

Remark 2.6
.

(i) We note that in Definition
2.5
, (ii) implies (i), namely any division
(R,\Lambda)
-graded Lie algebra is predivision. Also, when
\dim_{\mathbb{K}}(\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}^{\lambda})\leq 1
for all

, these two concepts coincide.

(ii) In
[

for predivision

-graded Lie algebras of simply-laced type with

, and in

-garded Lie algebras, Yoshii showed that their coordinate algebras are naturally

(see

Example 2.7
.

(i) Any

-graded Lie algebra

is a predivision

-graded Lie algebra with

(ii) The (centerless) core of an extended affine Lie algebra

(iii) Assume that

is a

-graded commutative associative algebra with

, and

is a finite-dimensional simple Lie algebra with Cartan subalgebra

Let
\mathcal{A}
be an algebra over
{\mathbb{K}}
. In the following, by an
(anti)-involution

\bar{\>\>}
of
\mathcal{A}
we mean a
{\mathbb{K}}
-linear map
\bar{\>\>}:\mathcal{A}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}
satisfying:

\displaystyle\overline{ab}=\overline{b}\overline{a},

\displaystyle\overline{\overline{a}}=a,

for all
a,b\in\mathcal{A}
. In addition, an automorphism
\tau
of a Lie algebra
\mathcal{L}
over

is called an

if
\tau^{2}=\hbox{id}
. In
[

]

(R,\Lambda)
-graded Lie algebra; see Remark

Definition 2.8
.

Let
\mathcal{A}=\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\mathcal{A}^{\lambda}
be a
\Lambda
-graded algebra and
\mathcal{L}=(\mathcal{L},\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h})
be an
(R,\Lambda)
-graded Lie algebra.

(i)

We call an (anti)-involution
\bar{\>\>}
of
\mathcal{A}
, a

, if
\overline{\mathcal{A}^{\lambda}}=\mathcal{A}^{-\lambda}
for all
\lambda\in\Lambda
;

(ii)

An involution
\tau
of
\mathcal{L}
is called a
Chevalley involution
, if
\tau
preserves
\mathfrak{g}
,
\tau(\mathcal{L}^{\lambda})=\mathcal{L}^{-\lambda}
for all
\lambda\in\Lambda
, and
\tau(x)=-x
for all
x\in\mathcal{L}^{0}_{0}
.

Example 2.9
.

(i) Let
\mathcal{A}
,

and

. Moreover,
\tau(\mathfrak{g}\otimes 1)=\mathfrak{g}\otimes 1
and
\tau_{|_{\mathcal{L}^{0}_{0}}}=-\hbox{id}
. So
\tau
is a Chevalley involution of
\mathcal{L}=\mathfrak{g}\otimes_{\mathbb{K}}\mathcal{A}
.

(ii) Assume that
{\mathbb{F}}
is a field extension of
\mathbb{K}
and let
\mathcal{A}={{\mathbb{F}}}[x_{1}^{\pm 1},\ldots,x_{n}^{\pm 1}]
be the algebra of Laurent polynomials over
{\mathbb{F}}
in
n
variables. We note that
\mathcal{A}=\bigoplus_{\lambda\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}{\mathbb{F}}x^{\lambda}
is a
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
-graded algebra, where
x^{\lambda}=x_{1}^{\lambda_{1}}\cdots x_{n}^{\lambda_{n}}
for
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n})\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
. Then

is a division

-graded Lie algebra; see
[

, §4, Corollary(ii)]
. One easily sees that the assignment

on

. Thus, as defined in part (i),

is a Chevalley involution of
\mathcal{L}=\mathfrak{g}\otimes_{\mathbb{K}}{\mathbb{F}}[x_{1}^{\pm 1},\ldots,x_{n}^{\pm 1}]
.

Assume that
\mathcal{L}=(\mathcal{L},\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h})
is an
(R,\Lambda)
-graded Lie algebra. Let
\tau
be a Chevalley involution of
\mathcal{L}
. Since
\mathfrak{h}\subseteq\mathcal{L}_{0}^{0}

. Next, one can choose a Chevalley basis

of

so that

.

Convention 2.10
.

In the remaining, whenever
\mathcal{L}
is equipped with a Chevalley involution

corresponding to

Yoshii introduced the notion of a Lie torus in

Definition 2.11

A division

of

The Lie torus

\mathcal{L}
has an invariant non-degenerate symmetric bilinear form

which is graded, meaning that

(\mathcal{L}^{\lambda}_{\alpha},\mathcal{L}^{\mu}_{\beta})=0\quad\hbox{unless}\quad(\alpha,\lambda)=(-\beta,-\mu).

Example 2.12
.

In Example
2.9
, let
\mathcal{A}=\mathbb{K}[x_{1}^{\pm 1},\ldots,x_{n}^{\pm 1}]
. Then

Based on the structure theory of root-graded Lie algebras, the coordinatization theorems for centerless Lie tori of reduced types have been established in

Definition 2.13

Let

Remark 2.14

In

3.
Compatible involutions

Assume as before that
R
is an irreducible reduced finite root system and
\Lambda
is a free abelian group. First, we recall from
[
14
]
the notion of an involution of an
R
-graded Lie algebra
\mathcal{L}
compatible with the
R
-grading. Then we show that any Chevalley involution of an
(R,\Lambda)
-graded Lie algebra
\mathcal{L}
is compatible with its
R
-grading. Consequently, when
R
is simply-laced of rank
\geq 2
, applying the results of
[
14
,
\lx@sectionsign 2
]
to our setting we get that any Chevalley involution of an
(R,\Lambda)
-graded Lie algebra induces a pre-Chevalley (anti)-involution on its coordinate algebra.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\mathcal{L}
be an
R
-graded Lie algebra. An involution
\sigma
of
\mathcal{L}
is called
compatible
with the
R
-grading if

(i)

\sigma(\mathcal{L}_{\alpha})\subseteq\mathcal{L}_{-\alpha}
, and

(ii)

\sigma\hbox{Ad}\eta_{\alpha}(1)=\hbox{Ad}\eta_{\alpha}(1)\sigma
,

for all
\alpha\in R^{\times}
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Assume that
\mathcal{L}=(\mathcal{L},\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h})
is an
(R,\Lambda)
-graded Lie algebra with an involution
\tau
such that
\tau(\mathcal{L}^{\lambda})=\mathcal{L}^{-\lambda}
for all
\lambda\in\Lambda
, and
\tau_{|_{\mathcal{L}_{0}^{0}}}=-\hbox{id}
. Then
\tau(\mathcal{L}_{\alpha})=\mathcal{L}_{-\alpha}
, for all
\alpha\in R
.

Proof.

Note that
\mathfrak{h}\subseteq\mathcal{L}_{0}^{0}
and since
\tau_{|_{\mathcal{L}_{0}^{0}}}=-\hbox{id}
we have

[h,\tau(x)]=\tau([-h,x])=-{\alpha}(h)\tau(x),

for
\alpha\in{R},x\in\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}
and
h\in\mathfrak{h}
. So
\tau(\mathcal{L}_{\alpha})=\mathcal{L}_{-\alpha}
, for
\alpha\in R
.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
\mathcal{L}=(\mathcal{L},\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h})
be an
(R,{\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
-graded Lie torus with an involution
\tau
such that
\tau(\mathcal{L}^{\lambda})=\mathcal{L}^{-\lambda}
for all
\lambda\in\Lambda
, and
\tau_{|_{\mathcal{L}_{0}^{0}}}=-\hbox{id}
. Then
\tau(\mathfrak{g})\subseteq\mathfrak{g}
. In particular,
\tau
is a Chevalley involution of
\mathcal{L}
.

Proof.

Since
\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}\subseteq\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}^{0}
and
\dim_{\mathbb{K}}(\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}^{0})\leq 1
, for all
\alpha\in R^{\times}
, it follows from Lemma
3.2
that
\tau(\mathfrak{g})\subseteq\mathfrak{g}
and so
\tau
is a Chevalley involution of
\mathcal{L}
.

Remark 3.4
.

We note that by Proposition
3.3
, Definition
2.8
for a Lie torus
\mathcal{L}
coincides with
[
7
, Definition 3.1.1]
.

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
\mathcal{L}=(\mathcal{L},\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h})
be an
(R,\Lambda)
-graded Lie algebra with a Chevalley involution
\tau
. Then
\tau
is campatible with the
R
-grading.

Proof.

By Lemma
3.2
, we have
\tau(\mathcal{L}_{\alpha})=\mathcal{L}_{-\alpha}
, for all
\alpha\in R
, so it remains to show that condition (ii) of Definition
3.1
holds for
\tau
. To complete the proof, we now mimic the argument given in
[
14
, Lemma 2.3]
. First, we note that if
\phi
is an automorphism of
\mathcal{L}
, then
\phi\exp\hbox{ad}x\phi^{-1}=\exp\hbox{ad}(\phi(x))

for all ad-nilpotent
x\in\mathcal{L}
. Then we have

\displaystyle\tau\hbox{Ad}\eta_{\alpha}(1)\tau^{-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tau(\exp\hbox{ad}e_{\alpha})\tau^{-1}\tau(\exp\hbox{ad}(-e_{-\alpha}))\tau^{-1}\tau(\exp\hbox{ad}e_{\alpha})\tau^{-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\exp\hbox{ad}(\tau(e_{\alpha})))(\exp\hbox{ad}(\tau(-e_{-\alpha})))(\exp\hbox{ad}(\tau(e_{\alpha})))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\exp\hbox{ad}(-e_{-\alpha}))(\exp\hbox{ad}e_{\alpha})(\exp\hbox{ad}(-e_{-\alpha}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\exp\hbox{ad}(-e_{-\alpha}))\hbox{Ad}\eta_{\alpha}(1)(\exp\hbox{ad}(-e_{\alpha}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\exp\hbox{ad}(-e_{-\alpha}))(\exp\hbox{ad}(\hbox{Ad}\eta_{\alpha}(1)(-e_{\alpha})))\hbox{Ad}\eta_{\alpha}(1).

On the other hand

\displaystyle\hbox{Ad}\eta_{\alpha}(1)(-e_{\alpha})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\exp\hbox{ad}e_{\alpha})(\exp\hbox{ad}(-e_{-\alpha}))(\exp\hbox{ad}e_{\alpha})(-e_{\alpha})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\exp\hbox{ad}e_{\alpha})(\exp\hbox{ad}(-e_{-\alpha}))(-e_{\alpha})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\exp\hbox{ad}e_{\alpha}(-e_{\alpha}-h_{\alpha}+e_{-\alpha})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-e_{\alpha}+(-h_{\alpha}+2e_{\alpha})+(e_{-\alpha}+h_{\alpha}-e_{\alpha})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e_{-\alpha}.

Thus, we get
\tau\hbox{Ad}\eta_{\alpha}(1)\tau^{-1}=\hbox{Ad}\eta_{\alpha}(1)
, as required.

The following example shows that the converse of the previous result is not generally valid; specifically, any involution of
\mathcal{L}
that is compatible with the
R
-grading is not necessarily a Chevalley involution.

Example 3.6
.

In Example
2.7
(iii), let
\mathcal{A}=\mathbb{K}[x_{1}^{\pm 1},\ldots,x_{n}^{\pm 1}]
and consider
e_{\alpha}\in\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}
,

be the involution on

,

with

commutes with all
\hbox{Ad}\eta_{\alpha}(1)

is not a Chevalley involution for

We proceed with recalling what we will need from
[

,
\lx@sectionsign 2
]

\mathcal{L}
be an

\sigma
and

be the coordinate algebra of

can be identified with
\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}
for any

and define a

\bar{\>\>}:\mathcal{A}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}

(3.1)

for all

is independent of the choice of

Since

Proposition 3.7

(

.

The

is an (anti)-involution of the

Remark 3.8

We note that the aforementioned result is actually founded on Berman and Moody’s work, which includes type

Lemma 3.9
.

Let
\mathcal{L}=(\mathcal{L},\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h})
be an
(R,\Lambda)
-graded Lie algebra, where

is a simply-laced irreducible finite root system of rank
\geq 2
. Then, for
\alpha,\beta\in R^{\times}
the map
\theta_{\beta,\alpha}:\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}\rightarrow\mathcal{L}_{\beta}

-grading, i.e.
\theta_{\beta,\alpha}(\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}^{\lambda})=\mathcal{L}_{\beta}^{\lambda}
, for all

Proof.

An argument similar to
[
1
, Proposition 1.27]
or
[

, Theorem 5.1]
shows that

\hbox{Ad}\eta_{\delta}(1)(\mathcal{L}_{\gamma}^{\lambda})=\mathcal{L}^{\lambda}_{w_{\delta}(\gamma)},\qquad(\delta,\gamma\in R^{\times},\lambda\in\Lambda),

onto

In the following, we obtain a necessary condition for a simply-laced

to admit a Chevalley involution. In the next section, this is essential for the proof of Theorem

concerning involutive Lie tori of type

to be a

-graded Lie algebras; see Remark

Proposition 3.10
.

Let
\mathcal{L}=(\mathcal{L},\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h})
be a division
(R,\Lambda)
-graded Lie algebra with a Chevalley involution
\tau
, where

is a simply-laced irreducible finite root system of rank
\geq 2
. Then the coordinate algebra
\mathcal{A}
of
\mathcal{L}
admits a pre-Chevalley (anti)-involution.

Proof.

By Proposition
3.5
,
\tau
is compatible with the

-grading. Then Proposition
3.7
implies that the coordinate algebra
\mathcal{A}
has an (anti)-involution
\bar{\;}
. We fix
\alpha\in R^{\times}
and identify
\mathcal{A}
with
\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}

We now show that
\bar{\;}:\mathcal{A}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}

4.
Involutive Lie tori of type
A_{\ell}(\ell\geq 2)

In this section, we examine Lie tori of type
A_{\ell}(\ell\geq 2)
. In

Definition 4.1

Assume that
\mathcal{A}
and

Berman, Gao, and Krylyuk over the field of complex numbers

and Yoshii in general proved the coordinatization theorem for Lie tori of type

. Moreover, Berman, Gao, Krylyuk, and Neher over
{\mathbb{C}}
and Yoshii in general obtained a similar result for Lie tori of type

Theorem 4.2
.

Assume that
\mathcal{L}
is a centerless Lie
n
-torus of type

(i)

(
[

[

\mathcal{L}
is graded-isomorphic to
\mathfrak{sl}_{\ell+1}(\mathcal{A})

\mathcal{A}
is an associative

-torus.

(ii)

(
[
10

,
[

, then
\mathcal{L}
is graded-isomorphic to
\mathfrak{psl}_{3}(\mathcal{A})

\mathcal{A}
is an alternative
n
-torus.

The coordinate algebras of Lie tori of type
A_{\ell}(\ell\geq 3)
which are associative tori by Theorem

, are characterized as “quantum tori”.

4.3
.

(Quantum torus).
Let
{\bf q}=(q_{ij})\in M_{n}({\mathbb{K}})
be a
quantum matrix
, namely an
(n\times n)
-matrix such that

(4.1)

q_{ii}=1,1\leq i\leq n,\quad\text{and}\quad q_{ij}q_{ji}=1,1\leq i\neq j\leq n.

The
quantum torus

{\mathbb{K}}_{\bf q}={\mathbb{K}}_{\bf q}[x_{1}^{\pm 1},\ldots,x_{n}^{\pm 1}]
based on a quantum matrix
{\bf q}
is defined as the unital associative algebra generated by
2n

x_{1}^{\pm 1},\ldots,x_{n}^{\pm 1}
with the defining relations

(4.2)

x_{i}x_{i}^{-1}=1=x_{i}^{-1}x_{i},1\leq i\leq n,\quad\text{and}\quad x_{i}x_{j}=q_{ij}x_{j}x_{i},1\leq i\neq j\leq n.

Notice that if
q_{ij}=1
for all
i,j
, then
{\mathbb{K}}_{\bf q}={\mathbb{K}}[x_{1}^{\pm 1},\ldots,x_{n}^{\pm 1}]
becomes the algebra of Laurent polynomials in
n

\mathbb{K}
. Let
\{\varepsilon_{1},\ldots,\varepsilon_{n}\}
be a
{\mathbb{Z}}
-basis of
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}

{\mathbb{K}}_{\bf q}
is
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}

{\mathbb{K}}_{\bf q}=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}{\mathbb{K}}x^{\lambda}
, where for
\lambda=\lambda_{1}\varepsilon_{1}+\cdots+\lambda_{n}\varepsilon_{n}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}

-torus.

A quantum matrix

for all

Theorem 4.7

Assume that
\mathcal{L}

A_{\ell},l\geq 2
and
\mathcal{A}=\bigoplus_{\lambda\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}\mathcal{A}^{\lambda}
is the coordinate algebra of
\mathcal{L}
. Then
\mathcal{L}
admits a Chevalley involution if and only if
\mathcal{A}
admits a pre-Chevalley (anti)-involution, if and only if the coordinate algebra
\mathcal{A}
is graded-isomorphic to either the octonion
n
-torus or an elementary quantum torus.

Proof.

Assume that
\mathcal{A}
is equipped with a pre-Chevalley (anti)-involution. Then it follows from the proofs of
[
7
, Propositions 5.1.3 and 5.2.3]
that
\mathcal{L}
admits a Chevalley involution. Conversly, if
\mathcal{L}
admits a Chevalley involution then by Proposition
3.10
,
\mathcal{A}
is equipped with a pre-Chevalley (anti)-involution. We now consider the second “if and only if” in the statement.

We know from Theorems
4.2
and
4.6
that the coordinate algebra
\mathcal{A}
is graded-isomorphic to either the octonion
n
-torus
\mathbb{O}
(
\ell=2
), or the quantum torus
\mathbb{K}_{\bf q}
based on some quantum matrix
{\bf q}=(q_{ij})\in M_{n}({\mathbb{K}})
. So we may assume
\mathcal{A}=\mathbb{O}
or
\mathcal{A}=\mathbb{K}_{\bf q}
. By the first “if and only if”, we need to prove that
\mathcal{A}
is equipped with a pre-Chevalley (anti)-involution if and only if
\mathcal{A}\neq\mathbb{K}_{\bf q}
where
{\bf q}
is non-elementary.

If
\mathcal{A}=\mathbb{O}
, then there exists an (anti)-involution
\sigma
on
\mathcal{A}
determined by
x_{i}\mapsto-x_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq 3
, and
x_{j}\mapsto x_{j}
for
4\leq j\leq n
, see
[
10
, Lemma 1.20]
. Also,
\mathcal{A}
is equipped with an automorphism
\tau
given by
\tau(x_{i})=x^{-1}_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq n
, with
\tau^{2}=\hbox{id}
, and
\tau(\mathcal{A}^{\lambda})=\mathcal{A}^{-\lambda}
for all
\lambda\in\Lambda
, see
[
7
, Proposition 4.2.4]
. Let
\bar{\;}=\sigma\tau
. Since
\sigma\tau=\tau\sigma
, it follows that
\bar{\;}
is an (anti)-involution of
\mathcal{A}=\mathbb{O}
with
\overline{\mathcal{A}^{\lambda}}=\mathcal{A}^{-\lambda},\lambda\in\Lambda
, that is,
\bar{\;}
is a pre-Chevalley (anti)-involution of
\mathcal{A}
.

Next, let
\mathcal{A}={\mathbb{K}}_{{\bf q}}
. Assume that
\bar{\;}:\mathcal{A}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}
is a pre-Chevalley (anti)-involution. Since
\overline{\mathcal{A}^{\lambda}}=\mathcal{A}^{-\lambda}
for
\lambda\in\Lambda
, we have
\overline{x_{i}}=k_{i}x^{-1}_{i}
for some
k_{i}\in\mathbb{K}^{\times}
,
1\leq i\leq n
. By the definition of
\mathbb{K}_{\bf q}
, we have
x_{i}x_{j}=q_{ij}x_{j}x_{i}
for
1\leq i\neq j\leq n
. Now applying
\bar{\;}
to this relation, we get
k_{i}k_{j}x^{-1}_{j}x^{-1}_{i}=q_{ij}k_{i}k_{j}x^{-1}_{i}x^{-1}_{j}
, or equivalently
x_{j}x_{i}=q_{ij}x_{i}x_{j}
. Thus,
x_{j}x_{i}=q_{ij}x_{i}x_{j}=q^{2}_{ij}x_{j}x_{i}
.
This gives
q_{ij}=q_{ji}=\pm 1,1\leq i\neq j\leq n
, that is,
{\bf q}
is elementary. Conversely, assume that
{\bf q}
is elementary. Since the elements
x^{\mp 1}_{i},1\leq i\leq n
, of the opposite algebra
\mathbb{K}_{\bf q}^{\text{op}}
satisfy the defining relations (
4.2
), we get an (anti)-homomorphism
\bar{\;}:\mathbb{K}_{\bf q}\rightarrow\mathbb{K}_{\bf q}
satisfying
\overline{x_{i}}=x^{-1}_{i},1\leq i\leq n
. The map
\bar{\;}
is clearly a bijection due to the fact that
\dim_{\mathbb{K}}\mathcal{A}^{\lambda}=1
for all
\lambda\in\Lambda
. Thus,
\bar{\;}
is an (anti)-involution on
\mathcal{A}=\mathbb{K}_{\bf q}
with
\overline{\mathcal{A}^{\lambda}}=\mathcal{A}^{-\lambda}
, for all
\lambda\in\Lambda
, that is,
\bar{\;}
is a pre-Chevalley (anti)-involution of
\mathcal{A}
. This concludes the proof.

Remark 4.8
.

Yoshii showed that a centerless Lie torus of type
A_{1}
can be constructed using the Tits-Kantor-Koecher (TKK) construction from a Jordan torus
[
21
, Theorem 1]
. Furthermore, he described five families of Jordan
n
-tori and then proved that every Jordan
n
-torus is graded-isomorphic to a torus belonging to one of these families
[
21
, Theorem 2]
. The simplest is the family consisting of the Jordan tori
\mathbb{K}_{\bf q}^{+}
, which
\mathbb{K}_{\bf q}^{+}
denotes the
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
-graded algebra
\mathbb{K}_{\bf q}
with multiplication
x\cdot y=1/2(xy+yx)
. In
[
7
, Proposition 5.3.4]
, we showed the existence of Chevalley involutions for all Lie tori of type
A_{1}
. In particular, the Lie torus
\text{TKK}(\mathbb{K}_{\bf q}^{+})
admits a Chevalley involution for any quantum torus
\mathbb{K}_{\bf q}
. Thus, in the sense of Theorem
4.7
, type
A_{1}
is considered special.

Remark 4.9
.

We note that Theorem
4.7
completes the investigation of the existence of Chevalley involutions for Lie tori of reduced types, as initiated in
[
7
]
. It seems that studying the existence problem of Chevalley involutions for the non-reduced case, that is, type
BC
requires engaging with various structural theories and results presented in
[
5
]
,
[
3
]
,
[
13
]
and
[
2
]
. We plan to address this problem in future work.

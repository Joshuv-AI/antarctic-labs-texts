Exploring the hospitalisation experience of racialised older adults and caregivers living with dementia: a scoping review protocol
Authors: Sadaf Murad-Kassam, Hannah M O’Rourke, Kathleen Hunter, Kaitlyn Tate, Jordana Salma
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Abstract AbstractIntroductionRacialised older adults living with dementia face various challenges and barriers in receiving culturally sensitive care in hospital settings. Stigma, discrimination and healthcare provider bias toward racialised older adults living with dementia infringe on their right to access quality care services in acute hospital settings and can negatively affect their quality of life. Despite the growing need to integrate culturally sensitive dementia care into acute hospital care, little research has been done in this area. Therefore, the aim of this scoping review is to summarise and map what is known about the hospitalisation experience of racialised older adults with dementia in receiving care and identify research gaps.Method and analysisWe will use Arksey and O’Malley’s framework and the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses Extension for Scoping Reviews checklist to conduct and write the review. The search strategy will use keywords and index terms across selected databases: Google Scholar, PubMed, Scopus, Medline, PsycINFO and Cumulative Index for Nursing and Allied Health Literature, and hand-searching the reference lists from chosen literature. Grey literature will be searched using Google and the Alzheimer Society websites to find further evidence and literature. Two researchers will screen the titles and abstracts independently by referring to the inclusion criteria. Data from the extracted studies will be reported in tabular and narrative form that answer the scoping review’s questions. Research gaps and recommendations for future research will be identified and summarised. The review’s results will be shared with stakeholders, policymakers, healthcare professionals and community organisations working with the racialised community and dementia care.Ethics and disseminationThis scoping review does not require ethics approval because it collects data from publicly available resources. Introduction

Individuals living with dementia experience significantly higher rates of hospitalisation and complications compared with those without dementia.1,3 Dementia and hospitalisation are complex phenomena which often lead to prolonged recovery time and health complications. Among those aged 65 to 79, individuals with dementia are twice as likely to experience longer hospital stays and spend 2.5% more time in emergency departments (published dataset)2 Data from Canadian Institute for Health Information. Dementia in Hospital (Internet). [cited 2025 May 26]. Canadian Institute for Health Information. Hospital-related complications further exacerbate these disparities. An estimated 1.5% of hospitalised older adults with dementia experience preventable harms (published dataset)2, such as pressure ulcers, reduced mobility or hospital-acquired infections, which increase healthcare costs, higher rehospitalisation and a greater likelihood of institutionalisation.4 5 The journey of hospitalisation becomes more challenging when older adults from racialised communities are admitted to hospitals.6 7

Racialised older adults living with dementia experience a threefold burden encompassing disease, ageing and systemic racism across diverse healthcare settings.8 Racialised refers to individuals or groups categorised by their ethnic or racial characteristics and experiencing inequalities and discrimination based on their ethnic and racial identity.9 An emerging area of concern involves understanding their hospitalisation experiences and challenges they face in navigating care practices within hospital settings.10 Systematic barriers, such as access to dementia specialists, the shortage of trained nursing staff and the lack of understanding by healthcare providers on how to provide culture-oriented care, impact the hospitalisation experience of racialised older adults.6 10 11 Furthermore, hospitalisation increases the risk of immobility and infection in individuals living with dementia, which may prolong their length of stay and further intensify the emotional, physical and financial strain on caregivers.10 11

Culturally sensitive dementia care in acute hospital settings is not well understood or addressed in North American healthcare settings, and often, racialised communities struggle to voice their needs to have culturally appropriate care in hospital settings.12 Studies report a higher incidence of hospitalisation, financial constraints and increased length of hospital stay in the racialised population living with dementia compared with older adults of Caucasian ethnicity.11 13 14 Older adults from racialised communities with dementia and their caregivers may delay hospitalisation until the health issue is emergent or a crisis.15

Task-oriented healthcare cultures, limited inclusion of cultural practices in hospital care, lack of translators and limited involvement of caregivers in hospital care practice and decision-making contribute to significant challenges for racialised adults with dementia and their caregivers.4 10 13 These systemic gaps often lead to burnout and distress, as the individualised and culturally specific needs of patients remain unmet.16 17 Existing literature has primarily examined dementia care among racialised communities within community-based or long-term care contexts, highlighting barriers such as stigma, lack of culturally appropriate services and limited awareness of dementia.15 18 However, there remains a critical gap in understanding how racialised older adults with dementia and their caregivers experience the hospitalisation journey, including the preadmission process, in-hospital care, discharge planning and post-discharge care navigation. Given that older adults with dementia experience higher hospitalisation rates than those without dementia,19,21 it is essential to explore the existing evidence on their hospitalisation experiences. Therefore, this scoping review aims to identify the existing literature on the hospitalisation experiences of racialised older adults living with dementia and their caregivers and the challenges they encounter in receiving culturally sensitive care throughout their hospitalisation journey, as discussed in the current body of literature.

A scoping review methodology was chosen to provide the breadth and depth of the topic of inquiry and include various publication types, including grey literature. Scoping reviews include a broader overview of the literature to identify literature gaps and promote methodological heterogeneity in the literature search,22 23 including quantitative, qualitative, mixed-method designs and grey literature. Methodological heterogeneity enables researchers to analyse the breadth and depth of all relevant literature.23 24 Conducting a scoping review will assist in analysing knowledge gaps in the literature and clarifying key concepts and factors related to the above research topic. The findings of this scoping review will inform healthcare providers, researchers and community organisations about the need for further research and systematic reviews focused on exploring the hospitalisation journey of racialised older adults living with dementia.

Methodology

The study will use a scoping review methodology to map out the empirical evidence available to examine the gaps in the literature on care practices in hospitals towards racialised older adults and their caregivers and identify areas of future research or systematic reviews. We developed the protocol using the scoping review framework that Arksey and O’Malley22 proposed. Arksey and O’Malley’s framework involves organising a review in a five-stage process: (a) identifying the research question, (b) identifying relevant studies, (c) selecting eligible studies, (d) data charting and (e) collecting and summarising the results. Scoping reviews are helpful as an initial step in mapping relevant literature and identifying key concepts, theories and research gaps in a particular area of interest.18 19 A screening checklist (see Appendix A) will be used to screen studies for the scoping review. The Population, Concept and Context (PCC) format was used to develop a research question and screening criteria checklist. A preliminary search of Medline, Cumulative Index for Nursing and Allied Health Literature (CINAHL), PsycINFO, PubMed and the Scopus Database for Scoping Reviews began on 10 January 2024, and the latest search was conducted in July 2025. Given that scoping reviews do not aim to synthesise study findings,23 they do not require quality appraisal, a step included in a systematic review to contextualise the strength of the primary studies’ findings and synthesise them to generate major conclusions. As such, we did not conduct a critical appraisal of the reviewed studies. This review started in January 2024 and is expected to be completed by February 2026.

We will write the review using the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses Extension for Scoping Reviews (PRISMA-ScR) Checklist. No ethical approval is required for this type of study because we will review the published literature. The review is registered in the Open Science Framework database. No scoping reviews examining the challenges faced by racialised older adults living with dementia and caregivers receiving care in hospital settings were found.

Patient and public involvement

This scoping review protocol will not involve direct engagement with patients, the public or other stakeholders. However, the findings will be disseminated to relevant community organisations, including Alberta’s patient-oriented research support units (AbSPORU), Council of Muslims on Aging Gracefully (COMAG), Alberta patient-oriented research support groups (AbSPORU), policymakers, the Alzheimer Society of Canada and the Alzheimer Society of Alberta and Northwest Territories.

Inclusion and exclusion criteria

The inclusion/exclusion criteria for the review are as follows.

Inclusion criteria

Articles would be included in the scoping review if they examined older adults (aged>60) as defined by the WHO from racialised communities (population),25 living with any stage of dementia or Alzheimer’s (concept) and examining the care practices in acute hospital settings (context) (see online supplemental apendix A for detail inclusion and exclusion criteria and screening checklist). If a study includes older adults as part of a subanalysis, data specific to older adults living with dementia will be extracted and used. Studies on the experiences of caregivers who are involved in providing care to people living with dementia will be included in the scoping review. Articles must be published in the English language.

Eligible studies will include qualitative, quantitative, mixed-methods and systematic review studies that explore the hospitalisation experiences of racialised older adults living with dementia and/or their caregivers and the challenges they encounter. Studies examining factors associated with hospitalisation, the hospitalisation journey, challenges within acute care settings and hospital-based programmes aimed at improving dementia care in racialised communities will be considered. Grey literature, such as theses, organisational briefs, conference abstracts, news articles and institutional reports describing hospital care experiences or programmes for racialised older adults with dementia, will also be considered. Studies from 1990 to 2025 will be included, with no restrictions based on geographical location. If full-text articles are not accessible, the research team will attempt to obtain them through institutional library services or by directly contacting the corresponding authors to request copies. This approach will help ensure that all relevant studies are considered in the scoping review.

Exclusion criteria

Studies or sources that address dementia care in general, without explicitly focusing on racialised older adults with dementia and their experience in acute hospital settings, will be excluded. Studies focusing on healthcare practices in outpatient, institutional or community settings rather than acute hospitals will be excluded. Studies examining the experiences of healthcare professionals providing care to racialised people with dementia will be excluded, as this is beyond the scope of the review. Duplicate studies will be removed. Reasons for excluding full-text studies that do not meet the inclusion criteria will be recorded and reported.

Data sources and search strategy

A systematic literature search was developed in collaboration with an experienced health sciences librarian and a research team to identify relevant published studies. A systematic search will be performed in the six databases: PubMed, Scopus, Medline, PsycINFO, CINAHL and Google Scholar. Additionally, the reference lists of all included studies will be screened for further potentially relevant studies. Databases will be searched using a combination of natural language vocabulary (keywords) and controlled vocabulary with the Boolean operators AND and OR. We will use Medical Subject Headings whenever possible. Search strategies can be found in online supplemental appendix B. Keywords for the electronic database search will include ethnic minority, immigrant, refugee, racialised, non-white, older adults, senior, aged, family caregivers, carers, dementia, Alzheimer’s, hospital and acute care. Search results will be exported to Covidence for data screening, facilitating title/abstract, full-text screening and deletion of duplicate studies. A copy of the search strategies and preliminary results in the Medline database is included (see online supplemental appendix C). We will hand-search the reference lists of included articles for more relevant citations and to identify additional manuscripts.

Grey literature

We will conduct a comprehensive search of grey literature related to research on dementia among racialised older adults in hospital settings. Our search will include resources from reputable sources such as Google, the Alzheimer’s Association of the United States of America, the Alzheimer’s Society of Canada and the Alzheimer’s Society of the United Kingdom, as these organisations have made significant contributions to the study of dementia and its intersection with ethnicity.

Study selection

Studies will be selected following two stages of screening. The first stage is the initial screening of titles and abstracts conducted by two reviewers independently to assess their relevance to the research question and apply the inclusion and exclusion criteria outlined above. We will have meetings before title/abstract screening, after the first stage of screening and during the full-text screening process to ensure a mutual understanding of the eligibility criteria. If there is any disagreement regarding the selection of studies, it will first be discussed among the reviewers to reach a consensus. In cases where consensus cannot be achieved, a senior researcher (JS) will act as a third reviewer to provide an independent, blinded opinion without prior knowledge of the initial reviewers’ decisions. The study will be included for the review if all team members agree to it. We will carefully maintain all references within the Zotero reference manager to ensure accuracy and accessibility.26 We will use Covidence software to organise and manage all aspects of data screening.1 27 Screening the title/abstract and full text will take approximately 6 to 8 months, depending on the availability of team members. The PRISMA-ScR flow diagram will be included to represent the process visually.

Data extraction

Data extraction from papers included in the scoping review will be performed independently by the first reviewer and shared with the second reviewer for validation. Detailed data extraction instructions will be developed (online supplemental file 1) to maintain consistency. These instructions outlined the specific data elements to be collected, indicated where these elements could be found within articles and provided procedures for documenting them (online supplemental file 1). The guidelines included step-by-step directions for team members on how to identify and record relevant data in the data charting tables.23

The study’s central objectives will guide data extraction. Data extracted from all articles will include the first and last name of the first author, the year of publication and whether the article was peer-reviewed, a dissertation, a report, a book chapter or a news article. For empirical quantitative or qualitative studies, the following additional information was documented: study design; data extraction method, analysis approach and use of any theoretical framework (if mentioned); contextual details (ie, target population, country that the study was conducted in, cultural and ethnic background of participants and the types of hospitals and departments for where older adults with dementia were being admitted and the involvement of the caregivers) and information about the sample (ie, sample size, age, gender, level of cognitive impairment, marital status and caregivers’ relationship with the person with dementia and their age).

Finally, we will describe the experience of hospitalisation reported by older adults living with dementia or their caregivers. This includes pertinent details on the experience of hospitalisation, the challenges associated with the hospitalisation journey and the need for culturally sensitive dementia care gaps in hospitals (if reported). Multiple reports of a single study will be grouped together in data extraction tables so that they will be counted once in the analysis. Categorical or continuous data will be extracted into an online spreadsheet, while descriptive data will be entered into a data charting Word document (see table 1). Before complete data extraction begins, a sample of five studies will be extracted and compared with to ensure the agreement between reviewers and to determine if any revisions to the extraction tool are needed. The modification will be detailed in the scoping review. Mendeley citation software will be used to reference the studies.

Data analysis and reporting the findings

Frequencies will be calculated using basic descriptive statistics, including percentages, means and SD,28 to describe the demographic characteristics of participants across the included studies. If studies report central tendency using medians and ranges, values will be presented as reported to preserve data integrity and reflect heterogeneity in reporting across studies. No conversions from median (range) to mean (SD) will be performed, as the primary aim of this scoping review is to map and describe existing evidence rather than to conduct a quantitative synthesis. Qualitative data related to challenges in hospital care practices experienced by older adults living with dementia and their caregivers from racialised communities will be analysed inductively using a basic qualitative content analysis approach.23 The first author will conduct the initial data analysis, which will be reviewed by the senior researcher (JS). After the senior researcher analyses the findings, they will be shared among all team members. Having two independent reviewers carry out the analysis will enhance the credibility of the findings. Any discrepancies related to the data analysis process will be resolved through discussion and review of the coded data with senior researchers (JS, KT) and a senior methodologist (HMO’R) until a consensus is reached.

Ethics and dissemination

Ethics approval will not be needed since this review only includes published studies and data. Results from this review will be disseminated through peer-reviewed scientific journals and presentations to community partners, including the Alzheimer’s Society of Alberta and the Alzheimer Society of Canada, COMAG, Alberta patient-oriented research support groups (AbSPORU), policymakers, frontline workers working with racialised communities living with dementia and professional conferences. We anticipate that our review results will provide direction for future research efforts and reform clinical practices.

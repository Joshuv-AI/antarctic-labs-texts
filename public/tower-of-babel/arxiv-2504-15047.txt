RainbowPlus: Enhancing Adversarial Prompt Generation via Evolutionary Quality-Diversity Search
Quy-Anh Dang, Chris Ngo, Truong-Son Hy

Abstract
Large Language Models (LLMs) remain vulnerable to adversarial prompts that exploit safety mechanisms. Existing red-teaming methods face scalability challenges, computational bottlenecks, or limited attack diversity. We propose RainbowPlus , a framework that reconceptualizes adversarial prompt generation as evolutionary quality-diversity search, where diverse attack strategies co-evolve across behavioral niches. RainbowPlus introduces two synergistic innovations: (1) multi-element archives that maintain populations of elite solutions per niche, and (2) parallel fitness evaluation that replaces pairwise comparisons with efficient probabilistic scoring, achieving Θ ⁡ ( M ) \Theta(M) speedup (from Θ ⁡ ( M 2 ​ N ) \Theta(M^{2}N) to Θ ⁡ ( M ​ N ) \Theta(MN) ). Experiments demonstrate superior performance: compared to Rainbow Teaming, RainbowPlus generates 100 × 100\times more unique prompts (10,418 vs. 100) with higher attack success rates (95.55% vs. 54.36% on Ministral-8B). Against nine state-of-the-art methods on HarmBench with 12 LLMs, RainbowPlus achieves 81.1% average ASR - surpassing AutoDAN-Turbo by 3.9 points - while being 9 × 9\times faster (1.45 vs. 13.50 hours). Introduction

The advent of Large Language Models (LLMs) has revolutionized natural language processing, enabling unprecedented capabilities in tasks such as multilingual translation
(
Team et al., 2022
)
, medical diagnostics
(
Thirunavukarasu et al., 2023
;
Li et al., 2023a
)
, tool-augmented reasoning
(
Schick et al., 2023
;
Bubeck et al., 2023
)
, and conversational assistance
(
Achiam et al., 2023
;
Touvron et al., 2023b
;
Anil et al., 2023
)
. As LLMs become integral to safety-critical applications, including healthcare
(
Singhal et al., 2022
)
and legal advisory systems
(
Maddela et al., 2023
)
, ensuring their robustness against adversarial inputs is paramount. Adversarial prompts, carefully crafted inputs designed to exploit model vulnerabilities and elicit unsafe, biased, or erroneous responses, pose a significant threat to the reliability and trustworthiness of LLMs
(
Perez et al., 2022c
;
Wei et al., 2023
;
Zou et al., 2023
)
. Mitigating these vulnerabilities is not only a technical imperative, but also a prerequisite for the ethical and secure deployment of LLM in real world contexts
(
Anwar et al., 2024
;
Hendrycks et al., 2022
)
.

Existing methodologies for adversarial prompt generation face substantial limitations that hinder their scalability, effectiveness, and practical utility. Traditional approaches often rely on resource-intensive prerequisites, such as fine-tuning specialized attacker models
(
Perez et al., 2022c
;
Ge et al., 2024
)
, requiring white-box access to the internals of the target model
(
Liu et al., 2024b
)
, or demanding extensive manual intervention by human evaluators
(
Yu et al., 2024
;
Yu et al., 2023
)
. Even advanced black-box techniques, such as PAIR
(
Chao et al., 2023a
)
and Tree of Attacks with Pruning (TAP)
(
Mehrotra et al., 2024
)
, depend on iterative human-guided strategies that scale poorly with the volume of prompts needed for a comprehensive vulnerability assessment. Furthermore, many systematic attack discovery methods suffer from design flaws: some are limited by a lack of diversity, adhering to singular or predefined attack strategies
(
Shah et al., 2023
;
Jiang et al., 2024
;
Anil et al.,
)
, while others experience diversity loss due to the optimization-driven nature of their algorithms
(
Zhou et al., 2023
;
Fernando et al., 2024
)
. These shortcomings restrict the diagnostic potential of generated prompts, limiting their utility to both identify vulnerabilities and generate synthetic data to improve model resilience.

Recent advances have reframed adversarial prompt generation as a quality-diversity (QD) search problem
(
Lehman and Stanley, 2011
;
Pugh et al., 2016
;
Cully and Demiris, 2018
)
, which seeks to balance high attack success (quality) with a broad exploration of attack strategies (diversity). Rainbow Teaming
(
Samvelyan et al., 2024
)
pioneered this approach by conceptualizing prompt generation as a multi-objective optimization task within a MAP-Elites framework
(
Mouret and Clune, 2015
)
. However, Rainbow’s design is constrained by its single-prompt-per-cell archive, which limits diversity, and its dependence on pairwise LLM comparisons for fitness evaluation, which lacks scalability and precision. Subsequent methods, such as FERRET
(
Pala et al., 2024
)
and Ruby Teaming
(
Han et al., 2024
)
, introduced incremental improvements in efficiency and diversity but did not fully address scalability challenges and the suboptimal integration of fitness scores, resulting in incomplete exploration of the adversarial prompt space.

To overcome these limitations, we propose
RainbowPlus
, a novel red-teaming framework grounded in evolutionary computation. Building on the foundational insights of Rainbow Teaming and the MAP-Elites algorithm,
RainbowPlus
introduces key enhancements to the evolutionary quality-diversity (QD) paradigm. Specifically,
RainbowPlus
reimagines the archive as a dynamic, multi-individual container that stores diverse high-fitness prompts per cell, which is analogous to maintaining a population of elite solutions across behavioral niches. This enriched archive enables a broader evolutionary exploration of adversarial strategies. Furthermore,
RainbowPlus
employs a comprehensive fitness function that evaluates multiple candidate prompts in parallel using a probabilistic scoring mechanism, replacing traditional pairwise comparisons and enhancing both accuracy and computational efficiency. By integrating these evolutionary principles into its adaptive QD search,
RainbowPlus
achieves superior attack efficacy and prompt diversity, outperforming both QD-based methods and state-of-the-art red-teaming approaches.

Our contributions are threefold:

•

Adaptive QD Framework:

We introduce
RainbowPlus
, a computationally efficient red-teaming method that synergistically optimizes attack success rate (ASR) and prompt diversity by framing the task as an adaptive evolutionary quality-diversity search, leveraging a multi-individual archive and a probabilistic fitness evaluation inspired by evolutionary algorithms.

•

Comprehensive Empirical Validation:
We provide extensive experimental results across benchmark datasets and a diverse set of LLM architectures, including open-source and closed-source models, demonstrating
RainbowPlus
’s generalizability and robustness.

•

Open-Source Implementation:
We release an open-source implementation of
RainbowPlus
, allowing the research community to replicate, extend, and build on our findings, fostering collaborative advancements in LLM safety.

2
Methodology

This section presents
RainbowPlus
, a novel framework that fundamentally reimagines adversarial prompt generation for large language models (LLMs) through evolutionary quality - diversity (QD) principles. Unlike prior approaches that treat prompt generation as isolated optimization,
RainbowPlus
frames it as a
population - based evolutionary search
where diverse adversarial strategies co - evolve across behavioral niches. Our key insight is that effective red - teaming requires not just finding single attack vectors, but systematically exploring the entire landscape of vulnerabilities through adaptive evolution of prompt populations.

RainbowPlus
introduces two synergistic innovations that address fundamental limitations in existing QD - based red - teaming:
(1) Multi - Element Archive Architecture
that maintains diverse populations of elite solutions per behavioral niche, enabling richer evolutionary exploration, and
(2) Parallel Fitness Evaluation
that replaces computationally expensive pairwise comparisons with efficient batch scoring, achieving
\Theta(M)
speedup (Section
D
). These innovations transform the computational complexity from quadratic
\Theta(M^{2}N)
in traditional approaches to linear
\Theta(MN)
in
RainbowPlus
, while simultaneously expanding the diversity of discovered attack strategies.

Figure 1:
Overview of the
RainbowPlus
evolutionary pipeline. The iterative process follows a quality - diversity evolutionary search and consists of five stages: (1)
Prompt Sampling
selects a parent individual (adversarial prompt) and its descriptor from the archive; (2)
Candidate Generation
acts as a mutation operator, leveraging a Mutator LLM to produce a diverse offspring population of candidate prompts; (3)
Diversity Filtering
selects behaviorally distinct individuals using a diversity - promoting mechanism; (4)
Response Evaluation
computes fitness scores for each candidate based on a probabilistic assessment of prompt effectiveness; and (5)
Update
performs survivor selection by refining the archive with high - fitness, diverse prompts, analogous to niche - based population updates in evolutionary algorithms.

2.1
Background: Quality - Diversity Search

Quality - diversity (QD) search
(
Lehman and Stanley, 2011
;
Cully and Demiris, 2018
)
represents a paradigm shift in evolutionary computation, seeking solutions that are both high - performing and behaviorally diverse. While traditional optimization converges to single optima, QD maintains a population spanning the entire solution space, making it ideally suited for comprehensive vulnerability assessment in LLMs.

In adversarial prompt generation, the solution space
\mathcal{X}
comprises all possible prompts. Quality is measured by a fitness function
f:\mathcal{X}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
quantifying attack effectiveness, while diversity is captured through a feature descriptor
d:\mathcal{X}\rightarrow\mathcal{Z}
mapping prompts to an
N
- dimensional behavioral space
\mathcal{Z}=\mathbb{R}^{N}
encoding characteristics like risk categories and attack styles.

MAP - Elites

(
Mouret and Clune, 2015
)
provides the algorithmic foundation for QD search, maintaining a multidimensional archive - a grid discretizing
\mathcal{Z}
- to store elite solutions. Through iterative mutation and selection, MAP - Elites populates this archive with diverse high - quality solutions. Recent work has adapted QD for LLM red - teaming
(
Samvelyan et al., 2024
;
Pala et al., 2024
;
Han et al., 2024
)
, but faces critical limitations:
single - prompt archives
restrict diversity, and
pairwise fitness comparisons
scale poorly.
RainbowPlus
overcomes both constraints through its novel architecture.

2.2
Algorithm Design

RainbowPlus
employs a
K
- dimensional archive organizing prompts by discrete descriptors (e.g., Risk Category, Attack Style). Each prompt is tagged with
z=\langle c_{1},c_{2},\dots,c_{K}\rangle
, where
c_{i}
denotes its category in dimension
i
. The framework can initialize from curated datasets or generate prompts via LLM, adapting to diverse red - teaming scenarios.

Innovation 1: Multi - Element Archive - Evolutionary Population Maintenance

Motivation and Limitations of Prior Work.
Traditional MAP - Elites and its LLM adaptations
(
Samvelyan et al., 2024
;
Pala et al., 2024
)
maintain
single elite per cell
, discarding potentially valuable solutions when updating. This
greedy replacement
strategy suffers from three critical flaws: (1)
premature convergence
to local optima within each niche, (2)
limited exploration
of solution diversity within behavioral categories, and (3)
information loss
when superior solutions overwrite existing elites that may contribute to future evolution.

Our Solution: Population - Based Archive.

RainbowPlus
fundamentally reconceptualizes the archive as a
collection of evolving populations
rather than isolated elites. Each cell
G[z]
stores a
set
of prompts
\{x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{m}\}
, their responses
R[z]
, and fitness scores
F[z]
, maintaining multiple high - quality solutions per behavioral niche. This design parallels evolutionary algorithms’ population - based search, where diversity within species (niches) enables better exploration and adaptation.

The archive update follows an
additive
rather than replacement strategy:

G[z]\leftarrow G[z]\cup\{x^{\prime}\mid f(x^{\prime})>\eta\},

(1)

where
\eta
is a fitness threshold ensuring quality control. This accumulation preserves genetic diversity within niches, enabling:

1.

Richer evolutionary material:
Multiple parents per niche provide diverse mutation sources

2.

Robustness to noise:
Fitness variations across prompts reveal vulnerability patterns

3.

Comprehensive vulnerability mapping:
Multiple attack vectors per category expose diverse model weaknesses

Theoretical Foundation.
The multi - element architecture is not merely a storage optimization but a fundamental algorithmic shift. As proven in Section
D
, this design enables efficient batch updates that are computationally infeasible with single - element archives under pairwise comparison constraints.

Innovation 2: Parallel Fitness Evaluation - Scaling Through Probabilistic Scoring

Limitations of Pairwise Comparison.
Prior QD methods for LLM red - teaming
(
Samvelyan et al., 2024
)
rely on pairwise preference functions
p:\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{X}\rightarrow\mathcal{X}
that compare candidate prompts against existing elites. While suitable for single - elite archives, this approach faces severe scalability issues with multi - element storage: evaluating
M
candidates against
m
existing prompts requires
\mathcal{O}(Mm)
comparisons, creating a computational bottleneck that grows quadratically as archives expand (Section
D
, Theorem
D.9
).

Our Solution: Probabilistic Parallel Fitness.

RainbowPlus
replaces pairwise comparisons with
independent probabilistic fitness evaluation
. A Judge LLM
\pi_{J}
evaluates responses from Target LLM
\pi_{T}
for candidate set
X^{\prime}=\{x_{1}^{\prime},x_{2}^{\prime},\dots,x_{M}^{\prime}\}
, classifying each as “safe” or “unsafe.” The fitness score for prompt
x^{\prime}\in X^{\prime}
is:

\displaystyle f(x^{\prime})

\displaystyle=P(\pi_{J}(\pi_{T}(x^{\prime}))=\text{``unsafe''})

\displaystyle=1-P(\pi_{J}(\pi_{T}(x^{\prime}))=\text{``safe''}).

(2)

This probabilistic formulation offers several advantages:

1.

Parallelism:
Evaluations are independent, enabling batch processing

2.

Absolute scoring:
Each prompt receives an objective fitness measure, not relative comparisons

3.

Threshold - based selection:
Multiple prompts with
f(x^{\prime})>\eta
can be added simultaneously

4.

Exploration - exploitation balance:
Temperature
t
controls stochasticity in LLM Judge responses

The independence of fitness evaluations is crucial: computational cost scales linearly as
\mathcal{O}(M)
with batch size, achieving
\Theta(M)
speedup over pairwise methods (Section
D
, Corollary
D.11
).

Enhanced Efficiency Through Few - Shot Prompting.
The Mutator LLM
\pi_{M}
leverages few - shot prompting
(
Song et al., 2022
)
with descriptor - informed exemplars, reducing mutation iterations while maintaining alignment with target behavioral niches. This design choice further amplifies computational efficiency without sacrificing solution quality.

Evolutionary Pipeline: Five - Stage Iterative Cycle

RainbowPlus
orchestrates evolution through five interconnected stages (Figure
1
):

1.

Prompt Sampling (Parent Selection):
Uniformly sample parent prompt
x
with descriptor
z
from archive
G
. Generate target descriptor
z^{\prime}
via random perturbation to direct mutation toward underexplored niches.

2.

Candidate Generation (Mutation):
Mutator LLM
\pi_{M}
produces offspring population
X^{\prime}=\{x_{1}^{\prime},x_{2}^{\prime},\dots,x_{M}^{\prime}\}
aligned with
z^{\prime}
, using few - shot prompting to maintain descriptor fidelity while introducing variation.

3.

Diversity Filtering (Behavioral Selection):
Select behaviorally distinct subset
X_{div}\subseteq X^{\prime}
(
|X_{div}|=M_{div}\leq M
) using BLEU - based
(
Papineni et al., 2002
)
similarity, ensuring linguistic and semantic diversity prevents redundant evaluation.

4.

Response Evaluation (Fitness Assessment):
Target LLM
\pi_{T}
generates responses
R^{\prime}=\{\pi_{T}(x^{\prime})\mid x^{\prime}\in X_{div}\}
. Judge LLM
\pi_{J}
computes parallel fitness scores
S=\{f(x^{\prime})\mid x^{\prime}\in X_{div}\}
via probabilistic classification.

5.

Update (Survivor Selection):
Add prompts with
f(x^{\prime})>\eta
to archive cell
G[z^{\prime}]
alongside responses and scores, maintaining population diversity within behavioral niche.

This cycle, formalized in Algorithm
3
, embodies core evolutionary principles - variation through mutation, selection via fitness, and inheritance through population maintenance - adapted to the unique challenges of LLM red - teaming. The synergy between multi - element archives and parallel fitness evaluation enables
RainbowPlus
to explore adversarial prompt spaces with unprecedented efficiency and comprehensiveness.

3
Experiment

This section presents the experimental evaluation of
RainbowPlus
, evaluating its effectiveness in generating adversarial prompts compared to the baseline Rainbow method
(
Samvelyan et al., 2024
)
and, subsequently, the state-of-the-art red-teaming approaches. We measure attack efficacy, prompt diversity, and computational efficiency in diverse datasets and target LLMs, providing a comprehensive analysis of
RainbowPlus
contributions.

3.1
Metrics

To evaluate the effectiveness of the attack, we employ the
Attack Success Rate (ASR)
, defined as:

\text{ASR}=\frac{\text{Number of Successful Attacks}}{\text{Total Number of Attempted Attacks}}\times 100\%,

(3)

where a successful attack is recorded when a prompt elicits an unsafe response, as classified by Llama-Guard-3-8B
(
Llama Team, 2024b
)
, an independent judge LLM designed for robust safety evaluation. This metric quantifies the proportion of requests that bypass LLM safety mechanisms of a target LLM, offering a direct measure of red-teaming effectiveness. To assess the diversity of generated adversarial prompts, we use the
Diverse-Score
, calculated as:

\text{Diverse-Score}=1-\text{Self-BLEU},

(4)

where Self-BLEU
(
Zhu et al., 2018
)
measures pairwise similarity among prompts using 1-gram precision. A lower Self-BLEU (higher Diverse-Score) indicates greater linguistic and behavioral variety, critical for exploring a wide range of vulnerabilities in LLMs.

3.2
Comparison to Rainbow Method

This subsection benchmarks
RainbowPlus
against Rainbow
(
Samvelyan et al., 2024
)
, a red-teaming method based on quality diversity (QD), to underscore the improvements introduced by our framework.
RainbowPlus
leverages a multi-element archive and a comprehensive fitness function to achieve superior attack efficacy and rapid diversity, addressing the limitations in the Rainbow’s single-prompt-per-cell design.

3.2.1
Experiment Setup

Archive Configuration

The archive employs two descriptor dimensions-
Risk Category
and
Attack Style
-adapted from the Llama-Guard Safety Risk Taxonomy
(
Inan et al., 2023
)
. Risk Categories (e.g., “Criminal Planning”, “Violence and Hate”, “Self-Harm”) capture distinct types of safety violations, while Attack Styles (e.g., “Authority Manipulation”, “Role Play”) represent strategies to elicit unsafe responses. A complete taxonomy is provided in the Appendix
B
. The archive is initialized with 100 prompts per dataset, sampled with a fixed random seed of 42 from red-teaming benchmark datasets. This ensures a diverse and reproducible starting point for exploration, covering a broad spectrum of harm dimensions.

LLMs and Hyperparameters

Candidate prompts are generated using Mutator LLM, Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct
(
Yang et al., 2024
;
Team, 2024
)
, selected for its instruction-following capabilities and generative versatility. The responses of target LLMs are evaluated using the Judge LLM, Llama-Guard-3-1B
(
Llama Team, 2024a
)
, a compact safety classifier optimized for efficiency. The Judge LLM operates with a temperature of 0.7 to balance exploration and precision in safety scoring. Key hyperparameters include a similarity threshold
\theta=0.6
(based on BLEU scores
(
Papineni et al., 2002
)
) to enforce prompt diversity and a fitness threshold
\eta=0.6
to filter high-quality prompts. The mutation process is repeated 20 times per iteration to generate diverse candidates. Additional hyperparameters, including sampling parameters and model configurations, are detailed in Appendix
J
.

To ensure a fair comparison with Rainbow, which stores one prompt per archive cell, we introduce two
RainbowPlus
variants that mimic this constraint:

•

RainbowPlus
-
\alpha
: Select the prompt with the median fitness score per cell, balancing quality and representativeness.

•

RainbowPlus
-
\beta
: Select the prompt with the maximum fitness score per cell, prioritizing the maximum efficiency of the attack.

The standard
RainbowPlus
retains all qualifying prompts, leveraging its multi-element archive to maximize exploratory capacity. The experiments were carried out for 1,000 iterations.

3.3
Comparison to State-of-the-Art Methods

To position
RainbowPlus
within the broader landscape of red-teaming of large language models (LLM), we benchmark its performance against nine state-of-the-art methods. This comparison highlights the advances
RainbowPlus
in generating effective adversarial prompts, using its adaptive quality diversity (QD) search paradigm to outperform existing approaches in attack success rate (ASR) and computational efficiency.

Experiment Setup

To ensure a fair comparison with the baseline methods, we adapt the ASR calculation to align with the standard practices in previous work
(
Mazeika et al., 2024
;
Liu et al., 2024a
)
. Specifically, ASR is computed as the proportion of original prompts from the dataset that, after transformation into jailbreak prompts, successfully elicit unsafe responses from the target LLM. Each original prompt contributes at most one success, regardless of the number of jailbreak prompts derived from it. Unlike the Rainbow comparison (Section
3.2
), where
RainbowPlus
could mutate prompts from previous iterations, here we limit
RainbowPlus
to 400 iterations, matching the number of samples in the HarmBench dataset. This constraint eliminates the warm-up phase, potentially reducing the performance of
RainbowPlus
by limiting its ability to leverage diverse prompts generated in previous iterations. Despite this,
RainbowPlus
performs competitively, as shown in the results. We set the number of mutations per iteration to 10, and other hyperparameters remain consistent with the Rainbow comparison.

Datasets

We adopt the HarmBench dataset
(
Mazeika et al., 2024
)
for evaluation, comprising 400 malicious requests that violate legal or ethical norms. HarmBench spans various categories of harm, including misinformation, illegal activities, and harmful advice, making it an ideal benchmark for assessing practical jailbreak risks.

Conclusion

Our study introduces
RainbowPlus

By integrating a multi-element archive and a comprehensive fitness function,
RainbowPlus
addresses key limitations in previous QD-based methods, such as Rainbow Teaming
(

)
, and state-of-the-art (SOTA) approaches like AutoDAN-Turbo

5
Limitation

The
RainbowPlus
framework represents a significant advance in LLM red-teaming by modeling adversarial prompt generation as an evolutionary quality-diversity (QD) search, effectively evolving a diverse population of high-performing prompts through adaptive variation and selection.
By introducing a multi-element archive and a comprehensive fitness function,
RainbowPlus
outperforms its predecessor, Rainbow
(
Samvelyan et al., 2024
)
, and several state-of-the-art methods (Sections
3.2
and
3.3
). However, despite its strengths,
RainbowPlus
faces certain limitations that warrant discussion. This section reflects on the contributions of the framework, addresses its challenges, and outlines directions for future research to further enhance its applicability and robustness in LLM safety evaluation.

5.1
Contributions and Strengths

RainbowPlus
addresses critical gaps in existing red-teaming methodologies by combining diversity-driven exploration with quality-driven evaluation. The multi-element archive, which stores multiple high-quality prompts per cell (Section
2.2
), enables a richer representation of the adversarial prompt space compared to the single-prompt limitation of Rainbow and other MAP-Elites variants
(
Mouret and Clune, 2015
;
Samvelyan et al., 2024
)
. This design facilitates the discovery of a wider spectrum of vulnerabilities, as evidenced by the superior attack success rate (ASR) of
RainbowPlus
and the capacity to generate prompts (e.g. 10,418 vs. 100 prompts for Ministral-8B-Instruct-2410; Table
2
). The comprehensive fitness function, which evaluates multiple prompts concurrently using a Judge LLM (Section
2.2
), further enhances efficiency and effectiveness, achieving an average ASR of 81.1% in the HarmBench dataset, exceeding AutoDAN-Turbo’s 77.2% (Table
3
).

Furthermore,
RainbowPlus
shows remarkable computational efficiency, it does not require a warm-up phase, and the experiments are completed in approximately 1.45 hours compared to the 13.50 hours of AutoDAN-Turbo (Table
4
). This efficiency comes from the use of few-shot prompting in the Mutator LLM and the streamlined QD search process, making
RainbowPlus
a practical tool for resource-constrained environments (e.g., a single NVIDIA A40 GPU). The framework’s ability to maintain high diversity (Diverse Score
\approx 0.84
) while generating orders of magnitude more prompts than Rainbow underscores its potential for comprehensive safety testing in diverse LLMs, including open-source and closed-source models.

5.2
Limitations

Despite its advantages,
RainbowPlus
faces several limitations that affect its performance and generalizability:

•

Absence of a Warm-Up Phase:
Unlike some state-of-the-art methods (e.g., AutoDAN-Turbo
(
Liu et al., 2024a
)
),
RainbowPlus
does not incorporate a warm-up phase to iteratively refine its prompt generation strategy. This design choice improves efficiency but may limit performance in scenarios requiring extensive exploration, particularly for highly robust models like GPT-4.1 Nano, where
RainbowPlus
achieved a lower ASR (6.0%) compared to AutoDAN-Turbo (20.5%; Table
3
). The lack of warm-up restricts
RainbowPlus
’s ability to take advantage of diverse triggers generated in previous iterations, potentially underestimating its full potential.

•

Manual Specification of Archive Dimensions:
The archive’s dimensions (e.g., Risk Category, Attack Style) must be manually defined based on a predefined taxonomy (Appendix
B
). This manual process limits scalability and adaptability to new harm categories or attack strategies not covered by the taxonomy. Automated or semi-automated methods for descriptor selection could enhance
RainbowPlus
’s flexibility, particularly for emerging LLM vulnerabilities.

•

Computational Constraints:
While
RainbowPlus
is optimized for a single NVIDIA A40 GPU, its performance on larger models (> 7B parameters) or in multi-GPU settings remains untested due to resource limitations (Section
J
). This constraint may restrict its applicability to cutting-edge LLMs with larger parameter counts, which are increasingly prevalent in production environments.

5.3
Future Directions

The limitations of
RainbowPlus
suggest several avenues for future research to enhance its robustness and applicability:

•

Incorporation of a Warm-Up Phase:
Introducing an optional warm-up phase, where
RainbowPlus
iteratively refines its archive using a subset of prompts, could improve performance against highly robust models. This could involve adaptive mutation rates or dynamic fitness thresholds to balance exploration and exploitation, potentially closing the ASR gap on models such as GPT-4.1 Nano.

•

Automated Descriptor Selection:
Developing methods to automatically infer or expand archive dimensions could reduce the dependence on manual taxonomies. This would improve
RainbowPlus
adaptability to new harm categories and attack strategies, improving its scalability for evolving LLM safety challenges.

•

Scaling to Larger Models:
Extending
RainbowPlus
to multi-GPU environments or optimizing it for larger LLMs (e.g., 13B or 70B parameters) could broaden its applicability. Techniques such as model parallelism or quantization
(
Kwon et al., 2023
)
could mitigate computational constraints, allowing the evaluation of state-of-the-art models such as Llama-3.1-70B or proprietary systems.

Ethics Statement

The development and evaluation of
RainbowPlus
aim to improve the safety and robustness of large language models (LLMs) by systematically identifying vulnerabilities through adversarial prompt generation. We recognize the ethical implications of red-teaming, as the generated prompts could potentially be misused to exploit LLMs in harmful ways. To mitigate this risk, our research is conducted with a commitment to responsible use and disclosure. The adversarial prompts generated by
RainbowPlus
are used solely for diagnostic purposes to improve the safety of the model, and our findings are shared to inform the development of more resilient LLMs. The open source release of
RainbowPlus
is intended to foster collaborative advances in LLM safety, with clear guidelines that encourage ethical use. We advocate for responsible red-teaming practices, including collaboration with model developers to address identified vulnerabilities and adherence to legal and ethical standards in AI safety research. By prioritizing transparency and safety, we aim to contribute to the trustworthy deployment of LLMs in safety-critical applications.

Appendix A
Related Work

A.1
Automated Red Teaming

Red teaming has become a cornerstone in evaluating and aligning large language models (LLMs) by exposing vulnerabilities that can lead to unsafe, biased, or incorrect output. Traditional manual red-teaming approaches, while effective in controlled settings, are inherently labor intensive and do not scale with the complexity and scope of modern LLM deployment. To address this, automated red-teaming techniques have gained traction, leveraging computational methods to systematically generate adversarial prompts that elicit undesirable model responses. The early work of
Perez et al. (2022a)
laid the foundation for this field, demonstrating how an LLM could autonomously generate test cases to uncover harmful outputs, thus reducing the reliance on human oversight. Subsequent research has built on this paradigm, introducing fully automated and hybrid strategies. For example,
Deng et al. (2023)
combined manual curation with in-context learning to improve prompt efficacy, while
Paulus et al. (2024)
proposed an optimization algorithm that prioritizes both efficiency and computational speed in prompt generation.
Liu et al. (2023)
advanced this further by developing frameworks for goal-oriented adversarial attacks, tailoring prompts to specific failure modes.

More recent innovations have incorporated sophisticated optimization techniques.
Lee et al. (2023)
employed Bayesian optimization to systematically explore diverse failure cases, achieving greater coverage of model weaknesses. Similarly,
Xu et al. (2024)
introduced a multi-agent system, RedAgent, designed to craft context-aware jailbreak prompts through collaborative agent interactions. Despite these advancements, a persistent challenge remains: Existing methods often struggle to balance attack diversity with success rate. Many approaches either prioritize high success rates at the expense of diversity or generate diverse prompts that lack sufficient potency, limiting their utility in comprehensively probing model robustness.

A.2
Quality-Diversity Search

The quality-diversity (QD) search paradigm
(
Lehman and Stanley, 2011
;
Pugh et al., 2016
;
Cully and Demiris, 2018
)
offers a promising framework to address this trade-off by optimizing the quality of the solution (e.g., attack success rate) and diversity (e.g., variety of attack strategies) simultaneously. Originally developed in evolutionary computation, the QD search has been adapted for adversarial prompt generation to systematically explore the vast input space of LLMs. QD methods aim to: (1) achieve high attack success rates to uncover critical vulnerabilities and (2) generate diverse prompt datasets to support comprehensive safety evaluation and model hardening.

Rainbow Teaming
(
Samvelyan et al., 2024
)
pioneered the application of QD to LLM red-teaming, reframing prompt generation as a multi-objective optimization task using the MAP-Elites algorithm
(
Mouret and Clune, 2015
)
. MAP-Elites maintains an archive that is a multidimensional grid where each cell stores a prompt optimized for a unique combination of quality and diversity metrics, such as attack success and behavioral descriptors (e.g., Risk Category, Attack Style). The archive evolves through iterative mutations, populating it with increasingly effective adversarial prompts. However, Rainbow Teaming’s initial implementation faced limitations: it stored only one prompt per cell, constraining diversity, and relied on pairwise LLM comparisons for fitness evaluation, which was computationally inefficient and underutilized fitness scores to guide the search.

Subsequent QD-based methods have sought to address these shortcomings. FERRET
(
Pala et al., 2024
)
introduced a scoring mechanism to rank prompts based on effectiveness, prioritizing high-quality solutions within the archive. Ruby Teaming
(
Han et al., 2024
)
extended this by incorporating a memory dimension, allowing the archive to retain historical prompt variations and improve quality over time. Despite these improvements, significant challenges persist: the fixed archive size limits prompt generation, updates remain restricted to single-prompt iterations, and fitness scores are not fully leveraged to optimize the search process. These constraints result in suboptimal exploration of the prompt space, reducing both computational efficiency and diversity coverage compared to the potential of QD methods.

Appendix B
Feature Descriptors for Adversarial Prompt Generation

In this section, we define the feature descriptors that form the foundation of our RainbowPlus framework for the generation of adversarial prompts. These descriptors categorize the prompts based on their associated
risk categories
and
attack styles
, enabling a structured archive to assess the robustness of large language models (LLMs) against adversarial inputs. Our taxonomy builds on and extends the safety risk framework proposed by Llama-Guard
(
Inan et al., 2023
)
, incorporating additional dimensions to capture the diversity and complexity of adversarial prompts. This taxonomy is critical for our adaptive quality-diversity search algorithm, as it ensures that the generated prompts span a wide range of risk profiles and linguistic strategies, thereby enhancing the thoroughness of red-teaming efforts.

B.1
Risk Categories

Risk categories represent the thematic content of adversarial prompts that could elicit unsafe or undesirable responses from LLMs. These categories are designed to align with ethical and safety concerns in LLM deployment, covering a broad spectrum of potential misuse scenarios. Drawing from Llama-Guard’s taxonomy
(
Inan et al., 2023
)
, we refine and expand the categories to reflect emerging threats and nuanced risks. The following list enumerates the risk categories used in our study:

•

Violence and Hate
: Prompts that promote or depict physical harm, aggression, or discriminatory rhetoric targeting individuals or groups based on race, gender, religion, or other protected attributes.

•

Sexual Content
: Prompts that contain explicit or implicit references to sexual acts, pornography, or inappropriate sexual behavior.

•

Criminal Planning
: Prompts that outline or encourage activities related to illegal acts, such as theft, vandalism, or organized crime.

•

Guns and Illegal Weapons
: Prompts involving the acquisition, use, or glorification of firearms or prohibited weapons.

•

Regulated or Controlled Substances
: Prompts that discuss or promote the use, sale, or manufacture of illegal drugs or restricted substances.

•

Self-Harm
: Prompts that encourage or describe acts of self-injury, suicide, or other self-destructive behaviors.

•

Inciting or Abetting Discrimination
: Prompts that instigate or support discriminatory practices or policies against protected groups.

•

Fraud and Scams
: Prompts that describe deceptive practices, financial scams, or fraudulent schemes aimed at exploiting individuals or systems.

•

Cybercrime and Hacking
: Prompts related to unauthorized access, data breaches, or malicious cyber activities.

•

Terrorism
: Prompts that advocate, plan, or glorify acts of terrorism or extremist violence.

Each risk category is associated with a set of evaluation metrics, including the severity of possible harm, the likelihood of generating unsafe results, and the ethical implications of model responses. These metrics guide the quality-diversity search in prioritizing prompts that challenge the model’s safety boundaries.

B.2
Attack Styles

Attack styles describe the linguistic and rhetorical strategies employed in crafting adversarial prompts. These styles are designed to exploit vulnerabilities in LLMs by manipulating language structure, context, or emotional tone. By diversifying the attack styles, we ensure that the archive captures a wide range of adversarial approaches, from subtle manipulations to overt provocations. The attack styles used in our study are as follows:

•

Slang
: Use of informal or colloquial language to disguise malicious intent or bypass content filters (e.g., using street terms for drugs or violence).

•

Technical Terms
: Incorporation of domain-specific jargon or technical language to obscure harmful prompts or exploit model biases toward authoritative-sounding inputs.

•

Role Play
: Prompts framed as fictional scenarios or character-driven narratives to indirectly elicit unsafe responses (e.g., asking the model to respond as a criminal mastermind).

•

Authority Manipulation
: Prompts that invoke authority figures or trusted roles (e.g., posing as a law enforcement officer) to coerce the model into compliance.

•

Misspellings
: Intentional typographical errors or phonetic substitutions to evade detection by safety mechanisms (e.g. “dr0gz” instead of “drugs”).

•

Word Play
: Use of puns, double entendres, or ambiguous phrasing to mask harmful intent while maintaining plausibility.

•

Emotional Manipulation
: Prompts that leverage emotional appeals, such as fear, sympathy, or urgency, to provoke unsafe or biased responses.

•

Hypotheticals
: Framing prompts are used as speculative or conditional scenarios to test the model’s reasoning in edge cases (e.g., “What if someone wanted to build a bomb?”).

•

Historical Scenarios
: Prompts that reference historical events or contexts to indirectly elicit harmful content (e.g., asking about weapon designs in the context of a historical war).

•

Uncommon Dialects
: Use of regional or niche dialects to exploit gaps in the model’s language understanding or training data.

Appendix D
Theoretical Analysis

In this section, we provide a formal theoretical analysis of
RainbowPlus
, demonstrating that our approach addresses fundamental computational bottlenecks in quality-diversity search for adversarial prompt generation. We formalize the problem settings, establish complexity bounds, and prove that our multi-prompt fitness evaluation achieves significant asymptotic improvements over pairwise comparison methods.

D.1
Problem Formalization

We begin by formally defining three variants of the quality-diversity search problem for adversarial prompt generation, progressing from the baseline Rainbow framework to our proposed
RainbowPlus
.

Definition D.1

(Standard Rainbow Problem)
.

In the standard Rainbow framework, the archive maintains a single prompt per cell. For each cell indexed by descriptor
z\in\mathcal{Z}
, we have:

•

Archive cell:
G[z]=x
(single prompt)

•

Response storage:
R[z]=r
(single response)

•

Update mechanism: A pairwise preference function
p:\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{X}\rightarrow\mathcal{X}
determines whether a candidate prompt
x^{\prime}
replaces the incumbent
x
, where
p(x,x^{\prime})=x^{\prime}
if
x^{\prime}
is preferred

•

Generation: One candidate prompt per iteration

•

Update frequency: At most one prompt updated per iteration

Definition D.2

(Multi-Prompt Rainbow Problem)
.

A natural extension of Rainbow to store multiple prompts per cell while retaining the pairwise comparison mechanism. For each cell
z\in\mathcal{Z}
:

•

Archive cell:
G[z]=\{x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{m}\}
(set of
m
prompts)

•

Response storage:
R[z]=\{r_{1},r_{2},\ldots,r_{m}\}
(corresponding responses)

•

Update mechanism: Pairwise preference function
p(x,x^{\prime})
compares candidate
x^{\prime}
against all existing prompts in
G[z]
. The candidate is added if
p(x^{\prime},x)=x^{\prime}
for all
x\in G[z]

•

Generation: One candidate prompt per iteration

•

Update frequency: At most one prompt added per iteration

Definition D.3

(
RainbowPlus
Problem)
.

Our proposed framework that employs a multi-element archive with a probabilistic fitness function for efficient batch evaluation. For each cell
z\in\mathcal{Z}
:

•

Archive cell:
G[z]=\{x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{m}\}
(set of
m
prompts)

•

Response storage:
R[z]=\{r_{1},r_{2},\ldots,r_{m}\}
(corresponding responses)

•

Fitness storage:
F[z]=\{f(x_{1}),f(x_{2}),\ldots,f(x_{m})\}
where
f:\mathcal{X}\rightarrow[0,1]
is a probabilistic fitness function

•

Update mechanism: Threshold-based fitness function
f(x^{\prime})>\eta
determines acceptance, where
\eta\in[0,1]
is a fitness threshold

•

Generation:
M
candidate prompts per iteration (batch generation)

•

Update frequency: Multiple prompts (up to
M
) added per iteration

Remark D.4
.

The key distinctions are: (1) Multi-Prompt Rainbow generates one candidate per iteration and uses sequential pairwise comparisons, while
RainbowPlus
generates
M
candidates per iteration and uses independent fitness evaluations; (2) Multi-Prompt Rainbow requires a candidate to dominate all existing prompts in the cell, while
RainbowPlus
uses an absolute fitness threshold
\eta
, enabling multiple prompts to be added simultaneously.

Theoretical Assumptions

To establish rigorous complexity bounds, we introduce the following assumption that characterizes an idealized update scenario.

Assumption D.5

(Perfect Update Condition)

Suppose the mutation operator and diversity filtering mechanism consistently generate prompts that satisfy the following conditions:

1.

All generated candidate prompts satisfy the similarity threshold constraint

2.

For

3.

D.3

We now formally establish the computational complexity of each approach, demonstrating that

Lemma D.7

(Multi-Prompt Rainbow Update Complexity)
.

For Multi-Prompt Rainbow (Definition

To determine whether

In this section, we conduct comprehensive ablation studies to understand the contribution of individual components in

E.1
Core Components Analysis

What are the essential components of
RainbowPlus
?

The two core innovations in
RainbowPlus
are: (1)
Multi-Element Archive
, which stores multiple high-quality prompts per cell rather than a single prompt, and (2)
Multi-Prompt Fitness Evaluation
, which employs a probabilistic fitness function to evaluate batches of candidates concurrently instead of pairwise comparisons.

These two components are fundamentally interdependent and must work in concert. The multi-prompt fitness evaluation enables efficient batch updates to the archive, which necessitates a multi-element storage structure. Conversely, maintaining multiple prompts per cell would be computationally prohibitive with pairwise comparisons, as shown in our complexity analysis. The theoretical analysis demonstrates that this combination reduces time complexity from
\Theta(M^{2}N)
to
\Theta(MN)
, while the empirical results in Section Experiment confirm that these components together achieve both higher attack success rates and remain prompt diversity compared to single-element approaches.

To further validate the effectiveness of these core components and understand their interaction with system parameters, we conduct detailed ablation experiments analyzing two critical hyperparameters: the number of mutations per iteration and the fitness threshold.

State-of-the-Art Methods

This section contains details of the SOTA methods used in

Section

3
.

•

GCG

(
Jia et al., 2025
)
: Optimizes an adversarial suffix at the token level to maximize the log probability of unsafe responses. Although precise, GCG is computationally expensive due to its gradient-based optimization.

•

Zero-Shot

(
Perez et al., 2022b
)
: Generate test cases using an attacker LLM in a zero-shot setting, prioritizing scalability, but often lacking specificity for targeted behaviors.

•

PAIR

(
Chao et al., 2023b
)
: Iteratively prompts an attacker LLM to explore harmful behaviors adaptively, balancing exploration and exploitation, but relying on the attacker’s creativity.

•

TAP

(
Mehrotra et al., 2024
)
: Employs tree-structured prompting to systematically explore harmful behaviors, enhancing coverage but increasing computational costs due to branching.

•

PAP

(
Zeng et al., 2024
)
: Use persuasive strategies (e.g., flattery, urgency) to craft convincing prompts. We select the top five strategies from the PAP paper for robustness, although generalization across harm types is limited.

•

AutoDAN

(
Liu et al., 2024b
)
: Evolves handcrafted jailbreak prompts using a hierarchical genetic algorithm, balancing human insight and automation but requiring careful initialization.

•

AutoDAN-Turbo (AutoDAN-T)

(
Liu et al., 2024a
)
: A black-box method using a lifelong learning agent to discover diverse jailbreak strategies autonomously.

•

Human Jailbreaks (Human)

(
Wang et al., 2024b
)
: Applies in-the-wild jailbreak templates (e.g. Do Anything Now, DAN) with substituted behavior strings, testing template-driven attacks but lacking adaptability.

•

Direct Request
: Uses raw behavior strings as prompts to test LLMs’ refusal capabilities, serving as a lower-bound baseline for safety alignment.

Detail Experiment Setup

This section provides a comprehensive overview of the experimental setup to evaluate
RainbowPlus
, designed to rigorously assess its effectiveness in generating adversarial prompts across diverse large-language models (LLM). The setup is optimized for a single NVIDIA A40 GPU with 48GB of memory, leveraging the vLLM inference framework
(
Kwon et al., 2023
)
4
4

4

https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm
for efficient model execution. vLLM enables high-throughput inference for both open-source and closed-source LLMs, supporting API-based and non-API implementations while minimizing memory overhead. The configuration ensures reproducibility, scalability, and compatibility with the computational constraints of a single-GPU environment, as detailed below.

The experimental setup comprises three core LLM components: the

,

, and

(see Section

). The Target LLM generates responses to adversarial prompts, the Mutator LLM produces candidate prompts, and the Judge LLM evaluates response safety. The model configurations, including memory allocation and context lengths, are optimized to balance performance and resource efficiency, as summarized in Table

A Cross-sectional Study of Awareness about Diabetes Mellitus and its Complications among Indian Patients with Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus
Authors: Jasminder Singh, Abhivyakti Solanki, Shivani Raidass
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Objectives:To assess awareness regarding diabetes mellitus and its associated complications in patients attending a tertiary care hospital in North India.Methods:This cross-sectional study included 300 patients with type 2 diabetes mellitus on treatment for over a year. Data were collected using a structured questionnaire assessing awareness on various aspects of diabetes and analyzed statistically using ANOVA and t-test.Results:The mean awareness score was 4.38 ± 1.84 (54.71% proportion). While 93.67% were aware of diabetes etiology, only 26.67% knew about its complications. Male gender, higher education, and employment were significantly associated with better awareness (P < 0.0001).Conclusion:Awareness regarding diabetes and its complications remains inadequate. Educational and employment status significantly influence awareness.

INTRODUCTION

Diabetes mellitus is a significant health problem resulting in hyperglycemia out of inadequate production or action of insulin. An 11.4% prevalence has been reported in India with 25% proportion still being undiagnosed of diabetes.[1]

Uncontrolled diabetes is associated with serious complications related to cardiac system, neuropathy, kidneys, diabetic foot, retinopathy, and hypertension. Awareness about the disease and its complications remains the primary step of preventing these complications and managing the disease. However, there is a significant lack of awareness about the disease despite increasing prevalence, which is estimated to encompass around 350 million people in the world by the year 2030.[2]

Thus, the present study was conducted with objectives to assess awareness about diabetes mellitus and its associated complications in a tertiary care hospital of North India.

METHODS

A hospital-based cross-sectional study was conducted over a period of one year, from December 2022 to November 2023, in the Department of Medicine, Pt. B.D. Sharma PGIMS, Rohtak.

Inclusion criteria

Patients with a diagnosis of type 2 diabetes mellitus who were on medication for more than 1 year and above 18 years of age.

Exclusion criteria

Patients of age <18 years

Demographic and clinical characteristics of the patients were noted and they were evaluated by a questionnaire constructed in the native language. The interview was conducted by the primary investigator, wherein questions pertaining to the knowledge of diabetes, increasing prevalence of diabetes, factors affecting diabetes, complications of diabetes, and preventive aspects of diabetes were recorded. The composite scoring was done with a maximum score being 8 and minimum being 0, and an overall percentage was obtained.

Statistical analysis

Data were put into a Microsoft Excel spreadsheet and was represented in number and percentages. Statistical association of the variables was performed using ANOVA and Independent t-test, with P < 0.05 being of significance.

Among various demographic and clinical factors, age showed no significant association with diabetes awareness (P = 0.186, ANOVA). Among males and females, male patients had a significantly higher score compared to females, 4.95 ± 1.81 versus 3.56 ± 1.58, P < 0.0001 (independent t-test). Higher education and employment also showed a significant association with increased awareness (P < 0.0001, ANOVA), while duration of diabetes mellitus showed no significant association with awareness (P = 0.194, ANOVA).

DISCUSSION

The present study results showed that only 50% of the cases had awareness about the diabetes disease and its complications. The findings were consistent with another study in Ethiopia where less than half of the patients had awareness.[2] This percentage was even much lower in Bangladesh (42%) and in Ghana 40%.[34] The differences may arise out of cultural beliefs and differences in socioeconomic conditions and impact of the awareness and the population heterogeneity.[2] In another South Indian study, the median score was 15, where patients scored 51 out of 65 (in another questionnaire). The higher awareness may be due to urban population, and more education in South India.[5]

The present study also observed that among various factors, males had higher awareness which may be due to more outgoing nature of the males and inquiring more information at meetings.[2] Moreover, higher education and employment was also associated with significantly more awareness as compared to unemployed and illiterate people. They may be more curious about obtaining information about the disease. Interestingly, duration of diabetes mellitus did not show significant association with awareness or family history or age.

Limitations

The primary limitation was the cross-sectional design and outcomes pertaining to the awareness could not be followed up. Information about diagnosis and primary consultation, whether at private hospital or government hospital, or traditional medical clinic was not taken. This may be a limiting factor in awareness among the patients.

CONCLUSION

In conclusion, awareness regarding diabetes and its complications among type 2 diabetes mellitus patients remains suboptimal, with a mean awareness score of 4.38 ± 1.84 (54.71%). While 56.67% of participants were aware about diabetes as a disease, only 26.67% recognized its potential complications. Male gender, higher education, and employment were significantly associated with better awareness.

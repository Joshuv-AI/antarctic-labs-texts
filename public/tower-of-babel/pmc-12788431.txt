A Study to Asses Effects of Diabetes Mellitus on Treatment Outcome of Tuberculosis Patients
Authors: Nitish Adawadkar, Dhruvendra Pandey, Sameer Pandit, Shobhit Gupta
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Background:It has been known for a long time that tuberculosis (TB) and Diabetes mellitus (DM) have complex interactions, the mechanisms of which are not completely known yet. TB may have a different presentation in diabetics, and similarly, diabetic status is affected by Tuberculosis and its treatment.Materials and Methods:A prospective comparative study was conducted on 270 Tuberculosis patients. Diagnosis of tuberculosis was confirmed using Sputum, CBNAAT and imaging modalities. Glycemic control was assessed using Fasting Blood Sugar, Random Blood Sugar, and HbA1c levels. Treatment outcomes was assesed over six months as per standard NTEP guidelines.Results:In TB with Diabetes group the percentage of males 65% was more as compared to females which was 35%, while in case of TB without Diabetes the percentage of females 54% was more as compared to males 46%. The mean age of TB with Diabetes group was 39.60 ± 15.6 years and in TB without Diabetes group the mean age was 38.73 ± 17.3 years. High treatment failure rate was noted in patients with TB with Diabetes (P < 0.05).Conclusion:There is a two-way relationship between TB and diabetes, and they both affect each other’s presentation. Early Diagnosis and glycemic management are essential in improving treatment outcomes.

INTRODUCTION

Tuberculosis (TB) is an contagious disease that has existed throughout the centuries.[1] According to a UNAIDS data sheet, more than 4100 people die from tuberculosis every day, and approximately 30,000 individuals become infected with the disease, despite it being treatable and preventable.[2] Several risk factors are associated with the active tuberculosis infection including age, malnutrition, alcohol intake, smoking, and comorbidities like Diabetes mellitus.[3] India has an estimated 75 million diabetic patients in 2021.[4] The bacterial load of tuberculosis with diabetes is more as compared to tuberculosis with non-diabetics, also the sputum conversion time is higher in tuberculosis with diabetics.[5]

Ethical clearance

Data was collected in compliance with ethical guidelines from the Institutional Ethics Committee (Letter No. GMC/ RATLAM/2022/IEC/Approval/15Date29/8/22). Informed consent was obtained from all study participants after assuring them about the confidentiality of their information.

Study design

A prospective comparative study was conducted over a period of 12 months to assess treatment outcomes of TB patients with diabetes and TB patients without diabetes.

A total of 135 TB with diabetes patients and 135 TB without diabetes patients were included in the study. The inclusion criteria were:

Glycemic assessment

Glycemic control was assessed using HbA1c, Random Blood Sugar, Fasting Blood Sugar, Post prandial Blood Sugar.

Data collection

A pre-designed, pre-tested, structured interview-based questionnaire was used. The questionnaire was pre-tested by a pilot study conducted on 10% of the sample size, that is, on 27 subjects to test the feasibility.

Follow up

Patients were followed up at 2, 4, and 6 months to assess treatment response and glycemic control.

DISCUSSION

Out of total 270 participants 56% were male and 44% were female. Most of TB patients with diabetes were male (65%) while in TB without diabetes group majority were female (54%).[6] Case series report published by Deshmukh and Shaw[7] in 138 TB-DM patients revealed that 82.6% of the study population was above 45 years and there was male preponderance. Similar observation were seen under the study done by Patel et al.[8] in which there were 76% male having TB with diabetes. A study conducted in Nigeria also stated that males are generally reported to have a higher risk of TB-DM than females.[9] A study conducted by Nagar et al.[10] to assess blood glucose level among diagnosed case of tuberculosis registered in tuberculosis unit showed that prevalence of diabetes was significantly higher among age >50 years and those with extra pulmonary TB. As per the study conducted by Balakrishnan et al.[11] wide representative sample of TB patients in Kerala was screened for DM using HbA1c the conclusion of study was that near half of TB patients in Kerala were having DM.

According to the this study, among the 270 participants, 63 individuals (23%) experienced recovery. In total, 58% of the participants successfully finished their treatment. Throughout the study period, a total of 11 patients (5%) died, while 9.6% had modifications made to their treatment plan (treatment regimen changed), 3% experienced treatment failure, and 2% were lost to follow-up (LTFU). The percentage of deaths was higher among TB with diabetes compared to TB without diabetes. As per the findings of Alisjahbana et al.[12] more number of death were seen in TB with diabetes as compared to TB without diabetes. In total, 3% of TB with diabetes patients witnessed a reversal in their treatment regimen. Subsequently, during the continuation phase of tuberculosis (TB) treatment, an additional 7.0% of patients defaulted, and 1.8% were transferred to another facility. Data from Systemic review done Gautam et al.[13] showed that TB with diabetes patients have 1.74 (95% CI 1.21, 2.51) times higher odds of mortality compared to non-diabetic TB patients.

Limitation

This study only evaluated Impact of DM among Newly diagnosed Pulmonary tuberculosis patients, It’s effect on Extra Pulmonary TB patient was not evaluated.

CONCLUSION

The present study revealed a high prevalence of Tuberculosis and diabetes in males. The high incidence of disease in males is possible because both tuberculosis and diabetes are more common in males. Limited information exists on the interaction between tuberculosis (TB) and diabetes mellitus (DM). There is an urgent need for comprehensive clinical and operational research to gain a deeper understanding of these interactions and optimize the intervention strategies.

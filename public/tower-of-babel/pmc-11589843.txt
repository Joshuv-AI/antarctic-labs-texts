Dynamic Lévy–Brownian marine predator algorithm for photovoltaic model parameters optimization
Authors: Yassine Bouteraa, Mohammad Khishe
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
The dynamic and multimodal nature of photovoltaic (PV) systems makes it challenging to examine all solar photovoltaic characteristics. Consequently, this study recommends a recently developed optimization method called the marine predator algorithm (MPA) for developing reliable PV models. In the traditional MPA, the two main search processes are Lévy flight (LF) and Brownian walk (BW), and the switch across them is unpredictable. This is while the transition between these two mechanisms is naturally continuous and dynamic. To rectify the limitation mentioned above, this research paper presents an innovative, dynamic shift function that effectively modulates the interplay that exists between the BW and LF procedures. By enhancing the changeover pattern between the primary phases of MPA, the suggested dynamic walk substantially boosts the performance of MPA. The dynamic Lévy-Brownian MPA (DLBMPA) is also made to be resilient in dealing with the parameterization limitations of PV Modeling approaches by using a constraint handling technique. The performance of DLBMPA is tested using ten popular optimization methods. Employing the DLBMPA achieved an average RMSE of 9.7 × 10− 4 in the parameter estimation across a number of multiple PV models, including the SDM, DDM, and TDM, where out of the ten optimization algorithms experimented, this was statistically significant (p < 0.05) better. In terms of averaged computation time, DLBMPA was 13 ms and still showed high accuracy in dealing with different irradiance and temperature levels. These improvements allow for MBPA to be credited as having a high efficiency when estimating the PV parameters since its speed of convergence and accuracy level surpass the previous techniques used.

Solar heat is used in a variety of applications, including direct use of captured solar energy in heating rooms, advanced heating systems, carbon footprint minimization systems, and many industrial applications2. Obtaining accurate data remains challenging due to the use of outdated and non-existent equipment. The performance of PV models can be enhanced by trying them out in different locations and also by understanding the conditions that affect them3.

The present study analyzes some parameters of the module models, such as the SDM, DDM, TDM, and PV module models, among others. The ongoing research will address the features inherent in each of the models and their related individual merits and demerits. Literature review

Many methods have been developed to estimate photovoltaic cell/module parameters. These methods may be split into two broad categories: heuristic and deterministic22. Deterministic techniques rely on analytical or iterative approaches. In analytical approaches, parameter estimates are created using PV datasheets or I–V characteristic curves. In addition to RSS23, Lambert-W24, and OSMP25 methodologies, there are a variety of other analytical methods. As the number of PV model parameters increases, so does the computational complexity of these approaches, which entail non-linear computing models to identify the unidentified parameters26.

Non-linear equations can be simplified using a series of simplifying assumptions27. Inaccurate estimations can be made by using a fixed value for Rs and Rsh, for instance, or by omitting them entirely from the problem definition. Deterministic iterative procedures require a sequence of iterations or trials to determine parameters28. When using iterative techniques, the variables in the system should be convex, continuous, and differentiable. When employing iterative approaches, finding the proper initial numbers is critical to avoiding local optima.

Recently, the focus has shifted to heuristic techniques, which require tackling an optimization challenge to reveal the PV model’s parameters29. The creation of an objective function can yield previously unknown parameters that degrade its performance. A heuristic approach views parameterization as a black-box problem that can be solved with no hypotheses about the mathematical model. Problems definition

The majority of literature on PV cells and modules refers to them as diode models. The I–V family curves of diodes and photovoltaic cells possess similar characteristics, as these are microelectronic devices having an exponential region. Each diode-based model contains a number of parameters that have to be estimated, which is not the known set of parameters. Since the parameters of the photovoltaic modules change with time, as well as the amount of solar radiation received by the modules, controllers have to keep in mind the prediction of unknown parameters.

The DFSMPA

The MPA is developed to describe the development of predator-prey relationships through three levels that actually exist in nature. These levels of interaction are factorized according to numerous admissible sizes of the steps that the predator would undertake so as to capture its Prey. Using the MPA, however, predicts an almost constant percentage of LFs and BMs for a predator’s life history. In the first stage, the predator is completely still; in the second stage, it operates with the BM movement; and finally, in the third stage, with the LF movement.

Stage I clearly shows the Prey going in BM, while stage II clearly shows the Prey following LF. The algorithm adds that allocating a 3rd of iterations to every stage empirically improves the policy and provides marginally better results than shifting between stages or periodically repeating the stage. Considering the novelty of MPA, it might be improved by specifying how and when to perform the method for every updating phase.

A seamless movement from the investigation phase to the exploitation phase is not always guaranteed by the canonical MPA. In order to solve the addressed problem, the DLB algorithm is introduced. Furthermore, the DLB can guarantee improved search trends for more effective global searching. Instead of using three discrete stages to represent the actions of Prey and predator in DSMAP-DFSMPA, it uses a fluid metaphor for gradual transitions between these stages. Continuous models enable the transfer of search agents between exploration and extraction.

The value of the coefficient category (i.e., LF or BM) and Prey and Elite locations determine the step size, as stated in Eqs. (21) to (24). Applying the RBM matrix by Prey yields the step size for this stage, as shown in the first half of Eq. (21).

Running the TDM algorithms 30 times ensured that the best results for each table were found to be included in the final results of the table. To provide a fair and equitable comparison, all benchmarks must commence from the same random starting point. In order to attain optimal outcomes, it is advisable to perform many repetitions of each procedure. Tables 7 and 8, and 9 exhibit the RMSE evaluated respectfully for SDM, DDM, and TDM. The Wilcoxon rank-sum test is utilized to statistically evaluate the performance enhancements obtained by DLBMPA. The selection of this non-parametric test was based on its resistance to assumptions regarding the distribution of data and its capacity to identify substantial disparities in medians. The null hypothesis posits that there is no statistically significant disparity in performance between the baseline and optimized algorithm, whereas the alternative hypothesis proposes the existence of a noteworthy enhancement.

A p-value below the standard significance level of 0.05 indicates strong evidence to reject the null hypothesis, suggesting a statistically significant difference in the performance of the algorithms. The p-value quantifies the importance of this improvement, confirming the practical enhancements as well as the fact that the implemented optimization algorithm led to a statistically significant increase in performance. The N/A in these tables indicates that the Wilcoxon rank-sum test cannot compare one algorithm to itself, not that it is not available.

Examination of the capability in exploration and exploitation phases

Optimization algorithms frequently undergo a dynamic process that consists of two essential phases: exploration and exploitation. Exploration involves the quest for uncharted and undiscovered areas inside the solution space, with the objective of uncovering possibly optimal solutions. Exploitation, however, is about increasing the effort on already mature areas to improve further and perfect already available developments. The degree of success of this optimization process is influenced by how well a balance between these two phases is found. An ideal algorithm, it turns out, has to be able to perform all its actions effectively, including making an easy transition from obsessive exploration to ruthless exploitation.

This section presents a thorough investigation of exploration-exploitation behavior in twelve optimization algorithms. Figure 13 depicts the dynamic transition between the exploration and exploitation stages for each method across numerous iterations. The DLBMPA exhibits outstanding performance by outperforming all other algorithms in reaching a well-balanced exploratory-exploitative ratio.

The performance noted highlighted the ability of the algorithm to navigate the solution space intentionally and to explore new areas that had not been investigated while making use of previously found good areas. The findings highlight the self-adjusting approximation and efficiency of DLBMPA, which enables the so-called exploration-exploitation tradeoff to be obtained. The DLBMPA’s specific features are what sets it apart, and it has been shown to be an effective optimization technique for complex optimization problems in many applications. Results discussion

The findings indicate that the DLBMPA is more efficient than other available techniques. Specifically, studies have demonstrated its superior performance over techniques like IJAYA, WHHOA, and PGJAYA when it comes to parameter estimation for photovoltaic (PV) models in solar energy. The differences in performance between the proposed algorithm and others are due to several new features incorporated into the design of DLBMPA, which together enhance convergence speed, minimize the complexity of computation, and optimize the exploration-exploitation balance.

DLBMPA’s effectiveness is at least partially attributable to its dynamic shift function. Whereas conventional methods employ fixed patterns of exploration and exploitation, DLBMPA transitions internally between Lévy flight (LF) and Brownian walk (BW) patterns of behavior. The design of this change in the optimization method ensures a greater emphasis on LF at the onset of the optimization process, the very initial stage of operation, which encourages a more thorough search across the provided solution space to identify suitable regions. In later iterations, when the approximations of solutions are near optimality and convergence appears plausible, DLBMPA seamlessly merges into BW, tracking the local search. The global LF and local BW transitions ensure that we consistently reach near-optimal solutions faster, avoiding the risk of premature convergence to non-optimal solutions. What this means is that because it is able to adapt, DLBMPA demonstrates both good accuracy and convergence, where, as it is noted, its average RMSE of 9.7 × 10− 4 is better than other techniques.

Moreover, DLBMPA has integrated the Fish Aggregating Device (FAD) Mechanism, enhancing the effectiveness of this method. The FAD mechanism injects controlled disturbances into the agents so that they do not settle at local optima. This feature is useful in the particularly challenging cases of high-dimensional, multimodal PV parameter estimation, where many classical methods would become relatively ineffective because they tend to converge to suboptimal points. Periodic FAD interventions enable DLBMPA to achieve great flexibility across different search phases, thereby expanding its bandwidth to search in new areas of the search space when necessary. As demonstrated in the experiments, this mechanism enhances the strength and stability of DLBMPA, enabling it to maintain good performance despite variations in irradiance and temperature, a feat that other methods struggled to achieve under the same conditions.

One more critical strength of DLBMPA that is worth mentioning is its lower computational complexity. DLBMPA boasts a radial structure with fewer hyperparameters, making it easier to use and less burdensome than many complex optimization algorithms. In our results, DLBMPA needed about 13 ms on average to complete a single iteration, which is much smaller than the execution time of many relative techniques. This allows DLBMPA to be effective in PV applications where real-time or near-real-time services are needed. Also, the reduced requirements for the parameters allow DLBMPA to be relatively easily applied to various types of PV models, as confirmed by the small RMSE values obtained for the tests of SDM, DDM, and TDM.

In a nutshell, the experiments authenticate DLBMPA as the most accurate algorithm for estimating PV parameters. The outcome was the same when the performance of DLBMPA was compared with that of ten algorithms; its accuracy and speed of convergence were superior with a great margin, thus making it ideal for effective use in practical PV systems. Pose the equation with DLBMPA applied for adaptive, accurate, faster, and less computation requirements, and it is proven to be effective and efficient in place of present optimization techniques. Future work goes with the flow as the study could be extended, for instance, by additional real-time applications of PV modeling, by testing DLBMPA in multiobjective or large-scale PV optimization problems, or even by improving the initial parameter settings through the use of experiment methods like the Taguchi approach.

To summarize, DLBMPA’s restatement includes overcoming the need for shift-function design and the need for an FAD system. One of the advantages of this design is that it paves the way for better performance in PV parameter estimation without some of the drawbacks experienced by older estimates. This document helps the discipline by demonstrating new domains on DLBMPA as an effective method with a high level of practicality in the modeling of PV system expansion and the use of renewable energy.

Conclusion

This research proposed the DLBMPA as a novel technique for parameter estimation in PV systems. To overcome the challenges of PV modeling with supposed complexities, DLBMPA proved to be efficient in terms of global search capability, convergence, and robustness. As a result of different tests performed on SDM, DDM, and TDM, DLBMPA was accomplished, and the target accuracy was met with an acceptable average root mean squared error of 9.7 × 10−4, nearly 1000 times lower than the error measure thresholds set for more than ten already recognized optimization algorithms, among them IJAYA, WHHOA, and PGJAYA. Further statistical results approved the existence of the enhancements mentioned above, lending support to DLBMPA in estimating PV parameters.

On the other hand, with respect to time, DLBMPA spent on average 13 ms in each iteration, making it have relatively better and faster convergence than the other methods and fit for real-time PV applications. For this algorithm’s evaluation in practice, three commercial PV modules (KC200GT, SW255, and SM55) were used, and the algorithms produced stable photocurrent and saturation current estimates for the parameters investigated despite changes in irradiance and temperature. This proves DLBMPA’s unaffected nature and reliability even in variable conditions, confirming its use in practical modeling of the PV system.

The results show that DLBMPA outperforms other benchmark algorithms tested due to the enhanced accuracy and efficiency levels. One limitation of this research is that the effectiveness of DLBMPA can be tested within a confined environmental setting. Future works can concentrate on the progress of DLBMPA to be effective on variable environmental parameters, a larger number of objectives, and organizational complexities. Furthermore, other studies could employ more efficient techniques within the methodological approach, such as the Taguchi method, to enhance the robustness of the design parameters further.

Consequently, compared with the existing approaches, DLBMPA seems to possess great promise in view of both accuracy and computational burden, making it practical for PV parameter estimation. Given the evidence of reliability and scope for extensions to DLBMPA, it is expected that DLBMPA will provide a practical strategy for the optimization of renewables while making a significant contribution to the efficiency of solar PV models.

Future research directions show promising prospects regarding the expansion of DLBMPA’s applicability range and functionality. For example, the DLBMPA can be enhanced to deal with multicriteria optimization problems in order to achieve several goals, such as maximizing energy efficiency and minimizing costs or effects on the environment. In addition, investigating parallel or distributed variants may increase its potential in terms of scalability and applicability to large PV arrays and intricate configurations of the systems. Further modifications designed and adapted for specific types of PV systems, such as grid-connected or hybrid systems, would make DLBMPA more versatile by adding aspects such as grid stability and integrated storage.

To sum up, DLBMPA can be regarded as a molecular and efficient tool for PV parameter estimation as it shows a good tradeoff between accuracy, speed, and robustness. Because of its strong potential for further modifications under many criteria and numerous cases, it stresses its importance for many PV applications, which makes it an essential asset for optimization methods in the modeling and management of renewable energy resources.

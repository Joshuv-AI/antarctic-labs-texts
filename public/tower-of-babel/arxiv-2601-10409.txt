Tight bounds on recurrence time in closed quantum systems Preprint: APS/123-QED
Marcin Kotowski, Michał Oszmaniec

Abstract
The evolution of an isolated quantum system inevitably exhibits recurrence: the state returns to the vicinity of its initial condition after finite time. Despite its fundamental nature, a rigorous quantitative understanding of recurrence has been lacking. We establish upper bounds on the recurrence time, t rec ≲ t exit ​ ( ϵ ) ​ ( 1 / ϵ ) d t_{\mathrm{rec}}\lesssim t_{\mathrm{exit}}(\epsilon)(1/\epsilon)^{d} , where d d is the Hilbert-space dimension, ϵ \epsilon the neighborhood size, and t exit ​ ( ϵ ) t_{\mathrm{exit}}(\epsilon) the escape time from this neighborhood. For pure states evolving under a Hamiltonian H H , estimating t exit t_{\mathrm{exit}} is equivalent to an inverse quantum speed limit problem: finding upper bounds on the time a time-evolved state ψ t \psi_{t} needs to depart from the ϵ \epsilon -vicinity of the initial state ψ 0 \psi_{0} . We provide a partial solution, showing that under mild assumptions t exit ​ ( ϵ ) ≈ ϵ / Δ ⁡ ( H 2 ) t_{\mathrm{exit}}(\epsilon)\approx\epsilon/\sqrt{\Delta(H^{2})} , with Δ ⁡ ( H 2 ) \Delta(H^{2}) the Hamiltonian variance in ψ 0 \psi_{0} . We show that our upper bound on t rec t_{\mathrm{rec}} is generically saturated for random Hamiltonians. Finally, we analyze the impact of coherence of the initial state in the eigenbasis of H H on recurrence behavior.



I
Introduction

A state of a closed quantum system evolving under a Hamiltonian
H
in a finite-dimensional Hilbert space will eventually return arbitrarily close to its initial configuration after a sufficiently long time. This follows from the Poincaré recurrence theorem
22
;
10
, which applies to a broad class of dynamical systems, including closed quantum systems
5
. In generic cases, the recurrence (or Poincaré) time is expected to scale exponentially with the Hilbert-space dimension
d
,
t_{\mathrm{rec}}\sim e^{d}
. Previous works have provided physical arguments for this scaling (cf.
21
) and estimated recurrence times under additional assumptions on the initial states or Hamiltonians
8
;
25
;
13
;
12
. However, these approaches either (i) lack mathematical rigor, as they do not prove specific scaling relations for
t_{\mathrm{rec}}
; (ii) apply to restricted classes of states or random Hamiltonians; or (iii) address recurrence statistics averaged over long times, rather than the
first
recurrence event—which is of primary physical significance given the astronomical time scales involved.

This lack of of rigorous quantitative understanding of recurrence time is unsatisfactory due to ubiquitous nature of recurrence (it was experimentally demonstrated in suitably crafted many-body setting in
23
) and the role it plays in various areas, including foundations of quantum statistical mechanics
11
, phenomenology of circuit complexity in chaotic many-body dynamics
7
;
2
;
19
and its (conjectured) relation to quantum description of black holes and wormholes within AdS-CFT correspondence
28
. The purpose of this work is to clarify and advance precise understanding of recurrence phenomena in quantum mechanics.

Figure 1:
The Hamiltonian evolution of a closed quantum system started at an initial state
\psi_{0}
. After time
t_{\mathrm{exit}}
the system leaves the
\varepsilon
-ball around the initial point and subsequently after time
t_{\mathrm{rec}}>t_{\mathrm{exit}}
the system has undergone recurrence.

We first prove unconditional and deterministic upper bounds on the recurrence time,
t_{\text{rec}}\lesssim t_{\mathrm{exit}}(\varepsilon)(\frac{1}{\varepsilon})^{d}
, where
d
is the dimension of the system,
\varepsilon
is the size of the neighborhood (measured in trace distance), and
t_{\mathrm{exit}}(\varepsilon)
is the time the system needs to leave the
\varepsilon
-neighborhood of the initial pure state.
Assessing the escape time requires solving the
inverse quantum speed limit
problem - i.e. finding an upper bound on the time system needs to depart from the
\varepsilon
-neighborhood of the initial state
\psi_{0}
(in the standard quantum speed limit
17
one typically is interested in finding a
lower
bound on this time). We give partial solution to this problem, showing that under mild assumptions
t_{\mathrm{exit}}(\varepsilon)\approx\varepsilon/\sqrt{\Delta(H^{2})}
, where
\Delta(H^{2})=\langle(H-\langle H\rangle)^{2}\rangle
is the Hamiltonian variance computed on the initial state.

We emphasize that the recurrence time
t_{\mathrm{rec}}(\varepsilon)
has to be defined as larger than
t_{\mathrm{exit}}(\varepsilon)
– this condition ensures that the evolution first leaves the neighborhood of
\psi_{0}
and only later returns to it. Without imposing this we may have a situation in which evolution
\psi_{t}
forever stays in the neighborhood of
\psi_{0}
and no meaningful recurrence can be defined. Despite its simplicity this consideration has been absent from the study of recurrence.

Our results can be strengthened if the state
\psi_{0}
is concentrated on a small number of eigenstates of
H
and generalize to Hamiltonian evolutions in the space of unitary channels equipped with the diamond norm. We can also handle subsequent recurrences, not only the first one.
Finally, we adapt our techniques cases where Hamiltonian
H
describes free evolutions of systems of bosons or fermions
9
;
25
;
14
;
13
, for which case recurrence times can be
much shorter
as they are controlled by the dimension of the, single particle Hilbert space rather then dimension of the total multi-particle Hilbert space.

From the technical perspective our proofs are elementary – they rely only on basic geometric properties of spaces in which quantum evolution takes place (the set of pure quantum states or unitary channels equipped with trace distance and diamond norm, respectively) and the fact that the unitary dynamics preserves their geometry.

II
Notation and setup

In this work we will be concerned with unitary evolutions in finite-dimensional Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}=\mathbb{C}^{d}
. These evolutions are solutions of the Schroedinger equation
i\frac{d}{dt}\ket{\psi_{t}}=H\ket{\psi_{t}}
, where
H
is the Hamiltonian of the system. Let
\{\ket{k}\}_{k=1}^{d}
and
\{\lambda_{k}\}_{k=1}^{d}
denote eigenstates and the corresponding eigenvalues of
H
, respectively (we allow for arbitrary degeneracy in spectrum of
H
). For an initial state
\psi_{0}=|{\psi_{0}}\rangle\!\langle{\psi_{0}}|
we have
\psi_{t}=e^{-iHt}\psi_{0}e^{iHt}
. If we write
\ket{\psi_{0}}
in the eigenbasis of
H
we get
\ket{\psi_{t}}=e^{-itH}\ket{\psi_{0}}=\sum_{k=1}^{d}a_{k}e^{-i\lambda_{k}t}\ket{k}
, with
\sum_{k=1}^{d}|a_{i}|^{2}=1
. For an operator
A
we will denote the expected value
{\rm tr}(\psi_{0}A)
simply as
\langle A\rangle
. We will denote the variance
\langle(H-\langle H\rangle)^{2}\rangle
as
\Delta(H^{2})
and the fourth central moment
\langle(H-\langle H\rangle)^{4}\rangle
as
\Delta(H^{4})
.

Consider a metric space
(X,d)
and suppose we have a time evolution
x_{t}
starting from an initial point
x_{0}
. We define the
exit time

t_{\mathrm{exit}}(\varepsilon)
on scale
\varepsilon
as the first time when the time evolution leaves the
\varepsilon
-neighborhood of the initial point:

t_{\mathrm{exit}}(\varepsilon)=\inf\{t>0\ |\ d(x_{0},x_{t})\geq\varepsilon\}\ .

(1)

Accordingly, define the
recurrence time

t_{\mathrm{rec}}(\varepsilon)
on scale
\varepsilon
to be the first time after
t_{\mathrm{exit}}(\varepsilon)
when the system return to the
\varepsilon
-neighborhood of the initial point:

t_{\mathrm{rec}}(\varepsilon)=\inf\{t>t_{\mathrm{exit}}(\varepsilon)\ |\ d(x_{0},x_{t})\leq\varepsilon\}\ .

(2)

One can define subsequent recurrence times
t^{(k)}_{rec}(\varepsilon)
in a straightforward way – the
k+1
’st recurrence is the first time of coming back to within distance
\varepsilon
of
x_{0}
after: i) undergoing the
k
’th recurrence, and then: ii) leaving the neighborhood of
x_{0}
. We will mostly consider the case where
(X,d)
is a subset of pure quantum states equipped with the trace distance
D_{\mathrm{tr}}(\rho,\sigma)=\frac{1}{2}\|\rho-\sigma\|_{1}
and the time evolution is given by
\psi_{t}=e^{-iHt}\psi_{0}e^{iHt}
. We can also consider the set of unitary channels equipped with the diamond distance, see Supplementary Material.

III
Results

Our main result provides a general upper bound on the first recurrence time for any Hamiltonian and initial state.

Theorem 1

(Recurrence for states)
.

For any
H
and any initial state
\psi_{0}
the first recurrence time satisfies:

t_{\mathrm{rec}}(\varepsilon)\leq t_{\mathrm{exit}}(\varepsilon)\cdot\left(\frac{4\pi}{\varepsilon}\right)^{d-1}\ .

(3)

Furthermore, the
k
’th recurrence time satisfies
t_{\mathrm{rec}}^{(k)}(\varepsilon)\leq k\cdot t_{\mathrm{exit}}(2\varepsilon)\left(\frac{8\pi}{\varepsilon}\right)^{d-1}
.

To be useful, the above inequality requires an upper bound on the exit time
t_{\mathrm{exit}}(\varepsilon)
. The following result provides such a bound, provided that
\varepsilon
is small enough.

Theorem 2

(Inverse quantum speed limit)
.

Let
\varepsilon_{\ast}=\frac{\Delta(H^{2})}{\Delta(H^{2})+\sqrt{\Delta(H^{4})}}
.
Then for any
\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{\ast}
the exit time
t_{\mathrm{exit}}(\varepsilon)
is finite and satisfies

t_{\mathrm{exit}}(\epsilon)\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{\sqrt{\Delta(H^{2})}}\cdot\left(1-\frac{\varepsilon}{\varepsilon_{\ast}}\right)^{-1/2}\ .

(4)

The above result depends on the Hamiltonian and the initial state via the variance
\Delta(H^{2})
and the fourth central moment
\Delta(H^{4})
, quantities that together define curvature of time evolution
\psi_{t}
in the space of pure quantum states
15
;
3
. Importantly, the bound from Eq. (
4
) is asymptotically optimal, because applying the well-known Mandelstam-Tamm quantum speed limit
17
yields a
lower bound
:
\frac{\arcsin{\vep}}{\sqrt{\Delta(H^{2})}}\leq t_{\mathrm{exit}}(\varepsilon)
which has a matching behaviour for small
\varepsilon
. We discuss the general issue of finiteness of
t_{\mathrm{exit}}
in the Supplemental Material.

To obtain a concrete bound on recurrence time, we can use the above theorems and put
\varepsilon<\frac{\varepsilon_{\ast}}{2}
. The exit time is then bounded explicitly:

t_{\mathrm{rec}}(\varepsilon)\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{\sqrt{2\Delta(H^{2})}}\cdot\left(\frac{4\pi}{\varepsilon}\right)^{d-1}\ .

(5)

Up until now the question of upper bounding the first recurrence time for general Hamiltonians was mainly studied by Peres
21
, upon whose results we improve in several ways. The argument from
21
is only heuristic and applies only to sufficiently generic or random Hamiltonians – in contrast, our results are fully rigorous and valid deterministically for
all
Hamiltonians. We can also handle arbitrary initial states, which enter the bounds via the exit time
t_{\mathrm{exit}}(\varepsilon)
, while
21
only considers uniform superpositions of eigenstates and does not consider the issue of exit time. Our method is also general enough to encompass other settings (unitaries, highly symmetric Hamiltonians). We note that the main idea of
21
of connecting recurrence times to certain geometric properties of the
d
-dimensional lattice can be made rigorous using Minkowski’s theorem from convex geometry and has inspired our proof of lower bound in
Theorem

3
. Finally, it can also be shown that the upper bound for subsequent recurrences from
Theorem

1
is the best possible – we discuss its tightness and possible strengthening in
Remark

2
in Appendix.

In our last result we show that the upper bound from
Theorem

1
is generically tight in the sense that for a simple model of random Hamiltonian a randomly chosen
H
will saturate this bound with high probability.

Theorem 3
.

Let
H
be a random diagonal Hamiltonian with eigenvalues drawn independently and uniformly from
[-1,1]
. Let
\psi_{0}
be the uniform superposition of the eigenstates of
H
. Then for
\varepsilon
small enough with probability at least
1-\exp(-\Omega(d))
the recurrence time for the evolution started at
\ket{\psi_{0}}
is at least:

t_{\mathrm{rec}}(\varepsilon)\gtrsim\left(\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\right)^{d}\ .

(6)

The proof of the above theorem is technical and we present it in the Supplemental Material.

IV
Proofs

Sketch of proof of
Theorem

1
. Let
X
be a metric space equipped with a distance
d
invariant with respect to the time evolution. The packing number
{\cal N}_{\rm pack}(X,\varepsilon)
quantifies the maximal number of points in
X
that are at least
\varepsilon
-apart. See the appendix for detailed definitions. The core ingredient of the proof is the following bound, formally proved in
Proposition

1
in the Appendix:

t_{\mathrm{rec}}(\varepsilon)\leq t_{\mathrm{exit}}(\varepsilon)\cdot{\cal N}_{\rm pack}(X,\varepsilon).

(7)

The idea behind the bound is to follow the time evolution
x_{t}
started at the initial point
x_{0}
and consider balls of radius
\varepsilon
around a suitable discretization
\{x_{t_{i}}\}
of the trajectory. After sufficient time, the number of such balls will exceed the packing number
{\cal N}_{\rm pack}(X,\varepsilon)
, so some two points will end up in the same ball. Because we assume the distance is invariant, this implies that some point will end up near the origin and thus give recurrence. This logic is illustrated in
Figure

2
. A similar reasoning can be used to obtain bounds for the
k
’th recurrence time and is presented in the Supplemental Material. To obtain the proof of
Theorem

1
, we take
(X,d)
to be (the closure of) the subset of states reachable from the initial state, formally defined in (
27
), with trace distance and combine the above proposition with a bound on the packing number
{\cal N}_{\rm pack}(\varepsilon)
, which is proved in
Lemma

1
in the appendix.

Proof of
Theorem

2
. Let
F(t):={\rm tr}(\psi_{0}\psi_{t})
denote the fidelity between
\psi_{t}
and the initial state
\psi_{0}
.
Using
\frac{d}{dt}\psi_{t}=i[H,\psi_{t}]
and simple algebra we get:

F^{\prime\prime}(t)={\rm tr}(X_{H}\psi_{t})\ ,X_{H}:=-[H,[H,\psi_{0}]]\ .

(8)

A quick computation shows
{\rm tr}(X_{H}\psi_{0})=-2\Delta(H^{2})
and therefore
F^{\prime\prime}(t)=-2\Delta(H^{2})+{\rm tr}(X_{H}(\psi_{t}-\psi_{0}))
. Using the basic inequality:

\absolutevalue{\tr(X_H (\psi_t-\psi_0))}\leq 2\norm{X_H}_{\infty}D_{\mathrm{tr}}(\psi_{0},\psi_{t})
we get

F^{\prime\prime}(t)\leq-2\Delta(H^{2})+2\norm{X_H}_{\infty}D_{\mathrm{tr}}(\psi_{0},\psi_{t})\ .

(9)

From Lemma
2
given in the appendix we get
\norm{X_H}_{\infty}\leq\Delta(H^{2})+\sqrt{\Delta(H^{4})}
. Additionally, before the exit time we have
D_{\mathrm{tr}}(\psi_{0},\psi_{t})\leq\varepsilon
and therefore for
t\in[0,t_{\mathrm{exit}}(\varepsilon)]
we have

F^{\prime\prime}(t)\leq-2\Delta(H^{2})+2\left(\Delta(H^{2})+\sqrt{\Delta(H^{4})}\right)\varepsilon

(10)

For simplicity let us write:

A:=\Delta(H^{2}),\ B:=\Delta(H^{2})+\sqrt{\Delta(H^{4})}.

(11)

Integrating both sides of Eq. (
10
) twice (each time from
0
to
t\in[0,t_{\mathrm{exit}}(\varepsilon)])
, and using
F^{\prime}(0)=0,F(0)=1
yields:

\displaystyle F^{\prime}(t)\leq\left(-2A+2B\varepsilon\right)t

(12)

\displaystyle F(t)\leq 1-\left(A-B\varepsilon\right)t^{2}

(13)

By definition at time
t_{\mathrm{exit}}(\varepsilon)
we have
D_{\mathrm{tr}}(\psi_{0},\psi_{t_{\mathrm{exit}}})=\varepsilon
. Recalling that for pure states
D_{\mathrm{tr}}(\psi,\phi)=\sqrt{1-F(\psi,\phi)}
, we have
F(t_{\mathrm{exit}}(\varepsilon))=1-\varepsilon^{2}
. By plugging this into (
13
) at
t=t_{\mathrm{exit}}(\varepsilon)
we obtain:

1-\varepsilon^{2}\leq 1-\left(A-B\varepsilon\right)t_{\mathrm{exit}}(\varepsilon)^{2}

(14)

The assumption on
\varepsilon
implies that the coefficient of
t_{\mathrm{exit}}^{2}
above is positive, which directly implies Eq. (
4
).

V
Further consequences and applications

Effective support and effective dimension.
We observe that
Theorem

1
can be strengthened if the initial state
\psi_{0}
is mostly supported on a small number of eigenvalues of
H
. In that case, the dynamics, and thus the problem of recurrence, can be restricted to that small subspace at the cost of a small error. Inspired by
4
we define
\delta
-effective support of
\psi
to be the size of the smallest subset
S
of eigenvectors of
H
that carry at least
1-\delta
fraction of the total probability:

d_{\mathrm{supp}}(\delta):=\min_{S}\left\{\absolutevalue{S}\ |\ \sum_{i\in S}\absolutevalue{a_i}^{2}\geq 1-\delta\right\}

(15)

Let
\psi^{S}
denote the state obtained from
\psi
by retaining only coefficients in
S
and normalizing. It is easy to check that for all times
t
we have
F(\psi_{t},\psi_{t}^{S})\geq 1-\delta
. By applying twice the triangle inequality for fidelity we get:

F(\psi_{0},\psi_{t})\geq F(\psi_{0},\psi^{S}_{0})F(\psi^{S}_{0},\psi^{S}_{t})F(\psi^{S}_{t},\psi_{t})\ .

(16)

so that
F(\psi_{0},\psi_{t})\geq(1-\delta)^{2}F(\psi^{S}_{0},\psi^{S}_{t})
. By using
D_{\mathrm{tr}}(\psi,\phi)^{2}=1-F(\psi,\phi)
and simple inequalities we easily end up with:

D_{\mathrm{tr}}(\psi_{0},\psi_{t})\leq D_{\mathrm{tr}}(\psi^{S}_{0},\psi^{S}_{t})+\sqrt{2\delta}\ .

(17)

If we now take
\delta=\varepsilon^{2}/4
, returning
\varepsilon/2
-close to the initial point in the reduced dynamics
\psi^{S}_{t}
implies returning
\varepsilon
-close in the full dynamics
\psi_{t}
. Also, using (
17
) we get that exiting
\varepsilon
neighborhood of
\psi_{0}
by the full dynamics
\psi_{t}
implies that the reduced dynamics
\psi_{t}^{S}
escaped
\varepsilon/2
neighborhood of
\psi^{S}_{0}
, implying
t_{\mathrm{exit}}^{S}(\varepsilon/2)\leq t_{\mathrm{exit}}(\varepsilon)
. Thus, we can apply
Theorem

1
to the reduced dynamics on scale
\varepsilon/2
and obtain:

t_{\mathrm{rec}}(\varepsilon)\leq t_{\mathrm{exit}}(\varepsilon)\cdot\left(\frac{8\pi}{\varepsilon}\right)^{d_{\mathrm{supp}}(\varepsilon^{2}/4)-1}\ .

(18)

which improves upon (
3
) if the effective support is much smaller than
d
. The exponent can be further improved to
d_{\mathrm{supp}}(\varepsilon/2)
by constructing appropriate coverings, see
Remark

1
in Appendix.

Another parameter that has been considered in connection with recurrence is the so-called effective dimension of
\psi

24
;
16
;
27
equal to
d_{\text{eff}}^{-1}=\sum_{k}|\alpha_{k}|^{4}
. A recent paper
25
proved
lower bounds
on the average recurrence time
\langle t_{rec}\rangle\gtrsim e^{d_{\text{eff}}}
. We show that this parameter not necessarily controls the true behavior of recurrence. Namely, in the Supplemental Material we provide a version of
Theorem

3
with initial states
\psi_{0}
which have small effective dimension (e.g.
\sim\log d
, and in fact can be taken to be growing arbitrarily slowly with
d
) compared to its effective support (
\sim d
) and satisfies (
6
). This shows that it’s the effective support, rather than the effective dimension, that controls the lower bound on recurrence time. See
Theorem

S3
in the Supplemental Material for technical statement.

Symmetric Hamiltonians.
We obtain much stronger bounds on
t_{\mathrm{rec}}
for free evolutions of multiparticle systems. Specifically, assume that an
n
particle state
\psi_{t}
evolves under a non-interacting Hamiltonian:

H_{n}=H\otimes\overbrace{\mathbb{I}\otimes\ldots\otimes\mathbb{I}}^{n-1}+\mathbb{I}\otimes H\otimes\overbrace{\mathbb{I}\otimes\ldots\otimes\mathbb{I}}^{n-2}+\ldots\ ,

(19)

where
H
is a Hamiltonian on a single particle space
\mathbb{C}^{d}
. Then, the recurrence time of
\psi_{t}
is bounded by

t_{\mathrm{rec}}(\varepsilon)\leq t_{\mathrm{exit}}(\varepsilon)\cdot\left(\frac{4\pi n}{\varepsilon}\right)^{d-1}\ ,

(20)

which with increasing
n
significantly improves over Eq. (
3
) as dimension of a single particle space
d
is much smaller than that of
n
qudit Hilbert space
(\mathbb{C}^{d})^{\otimes n}
(
d^{n}
), or its fermionic and bosonic subspaces (of dimensions
\binom{d}{n}
and
\binom{n+d-1}{n}
respectively). To obtain Eq. (
20
) we follow the sketch of the proof of
Theorem

1
and take
X
as the space of unitary channels on
(\mathbb{C}^{d})^{\otimes n}
corresponding to unitaries of the form
U^{\otimes n}
, with
U=\sum_{k=1}^{d}\exp(i \varphi_k)|{k}\rangle\!\langle{k}|
equipped with the diamond norm (see Supplemental Material for details). A similar result can be derived also for evolutions generated by fermionic quadratic Hamiltonians acting on the fermionic Fock space.

VI
Conclusions and open problems

We have presented rigorous quantitative bounds on the recurrence times in unitary dynamics of pure quantum states in finite-dimensional quantum systems that incorporate the, previously not investigated, issue of finding an upper bound on the exit time
t_{\mathrm{exit}}
(inverse quantum speed limit). Our results apply deterministically to arbitrary initial states and Hamiltonians and can be further tailored to initial states with support on a small number of eigenstates as well as when the Hamiltonian describes evolution of non-interacting particles.
Our work leaves a number of open problems for further work. It would be interesting to generalize our deterministic bounds to dynamics of mixed quantum states, expectation values of observables on evolved state and prove tight recurrence bound for physical dynamics induced by local non-commuting Hamiltonians. It also seems natural, especially in the context of dynamics after a quench
29
;
30
;
1
, to extend the results regarding inverse quantum speed limits to mixed states, open quantum system dynamics and dynamics of expectation values.

Appendix

For a metric space
(X,d)
we formally define packing and covering numbers as:

\displaystyle{\cal N}_{\rm pack}(X,\varepsilon)

\displaystyle:=\max\big\{|S|:\forall_{x,y\in S}\ d(x,y)\geq\varepsilon,\penalty\ S\subseteq X\big\}

\displaystyle{\cal N}_{\rm cov}(X,\varepsilon)

\displaystyle:=\min\left\{|S|:X=\bigcup_{x\in S}B(x,\varepsilon),\penalty\ S\subseteq X\right\}.

It is a standard textbook fact (see e.g.
31
, Lemma 4.2.8) that covering and packing numbers are related as follows:

\displaystyle{\cal N}_{\rm cov}(X,2\varepsilon)\leq{\cal N}_{\rm pack}(X,\varepsilon)\leq{\cal N}_{\rm cov}(X,\varepsilon)\,.

(21)

We now formally state and prove the core proposition used in the proof of
Theorem

1
for the first recurrence time. A version covering also the case of subsequent recurrences is shown in the Supplemental Material.

Proposition 1
.

Let
(X,d)
be a metric space and let
\varphi_{t}
be an action on
X
of a one-parameter group that leaves the metric invariant, i.e.
d(\varphi_{t}(x),\varphi_{t}(y))=d(x,y)
. Then the first recurrence time satisfies:

t_{\mathrm{rec}}(\varepsilon)\leq t_{\mathrm{exit}}(\varepsilon)\cdot{\cal N}_{\rm pack}(X,\varepsilon)

(22)

Proof.

Suppose first that
\varphi:X\to X
is an isometry of
X
. We first prove the statement (
22
). We claim that for any
x_{0}\in X
there exists some
1\leq k\leq{\cal N}_{\rm pack}(X,\varepsilon)
such that:

d(x_{0},\varphi^{k}(x_{0}))<\varepsilon

(23)

where
\varphi^{k}
denotes the
k
-fold composition of
\varphi
.

To prove this, let
N={\cal N}_{\rm pack}(X,\varepsilon)
and consider the sequence of points
x_{k}=\varphi^{k}(x_{0})
for
k=0,\dots,N
. Since the number of points is
N+1>{\cal N}_{\rm pack}(X,\varepsilon)
, by the definition of the packing number there exist some
i<j
such that
d(x_{i},x_{j})<\varepsilon
, which translates to:

d(\varphi^{i}(x_{0}),\varphi^{j}(x_{0}))<\varepsilon

(24)

Since
\varphi
is an isometry of a compact metric space, it is invertible and the inverse
\varphi^{-1}
is also an isometry. By applying
\varphi^{-i}
to both sides we obtain:

d(x_{0},\varphi^{j-i}(x_{0}))<\varepsilon

(25)

so the claim follows with
k=j-i
.

Now, by assumption for any
t
the map
\varphi_{t}
is an isometry with respect to
d
. Let
\varphi:=\varphi_{t_{\mathrm{exit}}}
. By applying the above claim to
\varphi
we obtain that for some
1\leq k\leq{\cal N}_{\rm pack}(X,\varepsilon)
we have:

d(x_{0},\varphi_{kt_{\mathrm{exit}}}(x_{0}))<\varepsilon

(26)

so a recurrence has occurred at time
t=kt_{\mathrm{exit}}
, which is indeed a recurrence since
t\geq t_{\mathrm{exit}}
.

Let

Remark 1
.

We observe that

Remark 2
.

Our bound on subsequent recurrence times from
Theorem

1
can be interpreted as saying that each recurrence takes time
\lesssim(1/\varepsilon)^{d}
on average. It is natural to ask whether this can be strengthened to saying that each recurrence simply takes time bounded by
\lesssim(1/\varepsilon)^{d}
(without the average) The following counterexample, involving slow and fast timescales, shows that this cannot be done in general. Fix
\varepsilon
and consider a qutrit state
\ket{\psi_{t}}=a_{1}\ket{1}+a_{2}e^{-i\lambda_{2}t}\ket{2}+a_{3}e^{-i\lambda_{3}t}\ket{3}
with
\absolutevalue{a_1}^{2}=\frac{1}{2},\absolutevalue{a_2}^{2}=\frac{1}{2}-\delta,\absolutevalue{a_3}^{2}=\delta
with
\delta\sim\varepsilon^{2}
and
\lambda_{3}\gg\lambda_{2}
. By using the identity
D_{\mathrm{tr}}(\psi_{t},\psi_{0})^{2}=1-F(\psi_{t},\psi_{0})
one can easily see that the evolution has two regimes. For times
t\lesssim\frac{\varepsilon}{\lambda_{2}}
the
a_{2}
term is roughly constant and exits and recurrences happen on timescale
\frac{1}{\lambda_{3}}
. Once
t\sim\frac{\varepsilon}{\lambda_{2}}
, the system will not undergo another recurrence for time
\sim\frac{1}{\lambda_{2}}
. Therefore, the time of the slow recurrence can be made arbitrarily larger than any function of
\varepsilon
and
t_{\mathrm{exit}}\sim\frac{1}{\lambda_{3}}
by tuning the ratio
\frac{\lambda_{3}}{\lambda_{2}}
. This precludes a uniform bound on each recurrence that would depend only on
\varepsilon
and
t_{\mathrm{exit}}
, while it still conforms to the bound from
Theorem

1
. This example has effective support equal to
2
and saturates the version of an upper bound on subsequent recurrences with effective support instead of dimension (see
Remark

1
).

Lemma 2
.

We have
\norm{X_H}_{\infty}\leq\Delta(H^{2})+\sqrt{\Delta(H^{4})}
.

Proof of
Lemma

2
.

Recall that
X_{H}=H^{2}\psi_{0}-2H\psi_{0}H+\psi_{0}H^{2}
. Assume first that
H^{2}\ket{\psi_{0}}\neq 0
. We first compute the eigenvalues of
A=H^{2}\psi_{0}+\psi_{0}H^{2}
. It is clearly self-adjoint and of rank at most two, with image spanned by
\ket{\psi_{0}}
and
H^{2}\ket{\psi_{0}}
. The matrix of
A
in this basis reads as (note that the matrix below is not self-adjoint since the basis need not be orthogonal):

\begin{pmatrix}\langle H^{2}\rangle&\langle H^{4}\rangle\\
1&\langle H^{2}\rangle\\
\end{pmatrix}

(35)

The eigenvalues of
A
are thus
\lambda_{\pm}=\langle H^{2}\rangle\pm\sqrt{\langle H^{4}\rangle}
. This formula holds also if
H^{2}\ket{\psi_{0}}=0
since then both sides are equal to zero.

Since
2H\psi_{0}H\geq 0
we can bound:

(\langle H^{2}\rangle-\sqrt{\langle H^{4}\rangle})I-2H\psi_{0}H\leq X_{H}\leq(\langle H^{2}\rangle+\sqrt{\langle H^{4}\rangle})I

(36)

The operator
H\psi_{0}H
is of rank one and its nonzero eigenvalue is equal to
\langle H^{2}\rangle
. Altogether by (

(-\langle H^{2}\rangle-\sqrt{\langle H^{4}\rangle})I\leq X_{H}\leq(\langle H^{2}\rangle+\sqrt{\langle H^{4}\rangle})I

(37)

which implies that
\norm{X_H}_{\infty}\leq\langle H^{2}\rangle+\sqrt{\langle H^{4}\rangle}
. Finally, we observe that

is invariant with respect to shifting

by a multiple of identity, so we can use this gauge freedom to obtain
\langle\Delta H^{2}\rangle+\sqrt{\langle(H-\langle H\rangle)^{4}\rangle}
on the right hand side, which finishes the proof.
∎

Subsequent recurrences

t_{\mathrm{rec}}^{(k)}(\varepsilon)\leq k\cdot t_{\mathrm{exit}}(2\varepsilon)\cdot{\cal N}_{\rm pack}(X,\varepsilon/2)

Theorem

1
follows by inserting the packing number bound, as for the first recurrence.

The proof of (

. Suppose that

is an isometry of

. Fix

and let

. Fix

and let
x_{l}:=\varphi^{l}(x_{0})
. We claim that there exist
k+1

, with

, such that

\mathcal{N}\subseteq\{x_{i}\}_{i=0}^{N_{k}}

\geq\varepsilon/2

\absolutevalue{\mathcal{N}}\leq{\cal N}_{\rm pack}(X,\varepsilon/2)
. By the pigeonhole principle, there will be some
k+1

l_{1}<\dots<l_{k+1}

for some

i_{j}:=l_{j+1}-l_{1}
for

To obtain recurrence from above claim we apply it with

and

where the first inequality holds since having left the ball of radius

\varepsilon

and the second equality follows from the invariance of dynamics. Altogether this implies that the time between

Recurrence for dynamics in the space of unitary channels

Every unitary operator

(Recurrence for unitaries)

t_{\mathrm{rec}}(\varepsilon)<\frac{\pi\varepsilon}{\lambda_{max}-\lambda_{min}}\left(\frac{8\pi}{\varepsilon}\right)^{d-1}

S1

1

Lemma S3

Proposition S2
.

With probability at least

the following hold simultaneously for

IV.2
Lower bound for states with small effective dimension

As mentioned in
Remark

1
, the scaling of the upper bound on recurrence time can be improved from
\sim(1/\varepsilon)^{d}
if the initial state
\ket{\psi_{0}}
has effective support smaller than
d
. It is natural to ask what property of the initial state and the Hamiltonian governs the true behavior of the recurrence time. One possible candidate is the effective dimension, also known as the inverse participation ratio, defined as:

d_{\mathrm{eff}}=\frac{1}{\sum_{i=1}^{d}\absolutevalue{a_i}^{4}}

(89)

The effective dimension has been used together with concentration techniques e.g. in
25
to give lower bounds on average recurrence times for certain observables. It is reasonable to ask whether this reflects the true behavior of the recurrence time, i.e. if it is possible to prove a matching upper bound depending on
d_{\mathrm{eff}}
instead of
d
. Below we refute this possibility for random Hamiltonians – with high probability, we give an example of an initial state which has low effective dimension, yet whose recurrence time scales exponentially in
d
instead of
d_{\mathrm{eff}}
.

Let
H
be as before. Fix
\eta\in[0,1]
and let:

\ket{\psi_{0}}=\sum_{k=1}^{d}a_{k}\ket{k}

(90)

with
a_{1}=\sqrt{\eta}
and
a_{k}=\sqrt{\frac{1-\eta}{d-1}}
for
k=2,\dots,d
, with the ordering of the eigenstates chosen randomly. To get a useful probabilistic bound we will require that
\eta=o(1)
as
d\to\infty
. For technical simplicity we also require
\eta\gg\frac{1}{d}
.

We easily compute that:

d_{\mathrm{eff}}=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{d}\absolutevalue{a_i}^{4}\right)^{-1}=\left(\eta^{2}+\frac{(1-\eta)^{2}}{d-1}\right)^{-1}=\frac{d-1}{d\eta^{2}+1-2\eta}<\frac{1}{\eta^{2}-\frac{2\eta}{d}}\sim\frac{1}{\eta^{2}}

(91)

since by assumption
\eta\gg\frac{1}{d}
.

Theorem S3
.

Let
H
be a random Hamiltonian with eigenvalues
\lambda_{k},k=1,\dots,d
as above. Let
\ket{\psi_{0}}
be given by (
90
). Assume

t_{\mathrm{rec}}(\varepsilon)>20\left(\frac{1}{20000}\cdot\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\right)^{d-1}

(92)

The above theorem provides a family of examples where
d_{\mathrm{eff}}\ll d
, yet the effective support is
\sim d
and the recurrence time is lower bounded by
\sim(\frac{1}{\varepsilon})^{d}\gg(\frac{1}{\varepsilon})^{d_{\mathrm{eff}}}
with probability

. For concreteness we can take e.g.

to obtain
d_{\mathrm{eff}}\sim\log d\ll d
. Then the above recurrence lower bound holds with probability

whenever
\varepsilon>(\log d)^{-1/4}

V
Miscellaneous remarks on exit time for states

There seems to be no simple criterion for finiteness of
t_{\mathrm{exit}}
in full generality. However, the following proposition gives a sufficient and necessary condition under mild assumptions on
H
. Namely, we require that the eigenvalues of
H
are rationally independent, i.e. if:

\sum_{k=1}^{d}q_{k}\lambda_{k}=0

(99)

for
q_{k}\in\mathbb{Q}
, then
q_{k}=0
for all
k
. This assumption will be satisfied by a generic Hamiltonian, i.e. any Hamiltonian
H
may be perturbed by an arbitrarily small amount to a Hamiltonian
H^{\prime}
whose eigenvalues are rationally independent.

Proposition S9
.

Suppose that all eigenvalues of
H
are rationally independent. Then
t_{\mathrm{exit}}
is finite if and only if:

1-\varepsilon^{2}\geq\max\{2\max_{k}\absolutevalue{a_k}^{2}-1,0\}

(100)

Proof.

We have:

\ket{\psi_{t}}=\sum_{k=1}^{d}a_{k}e^{i\lambda_{k}t}\ket{k}

(101)

so that:

D_{\mathrm{tr}}(\psi_{0},\psi_{t})^{2}=1-\absolutevalue{\ketscalar{\psi_0}{\psi_t}}^{2}=1-\absolutevalue{ \sum_{k=1}^{d}\abs{a_k}^2 e^{i\lambda_k t}}^{2}

(102)

The exit time
t_{\mathrm{exit}}
is finite if for some
t
we have
D_{\mathrm{tr}}(\psi_{0},\psi_{t})\geq\varepsilon
, which by (
102
) is equivalent to:

\absolutevalue{ \sum_{k=1}^{d}\abs{a_k}^2 e^{i\lambda_k t}}^{2}\leq 1-\varepsilon^{2}

(103)

Let us consider:

M(H,\psi_{0}):=\inf_{t\geq 0}\absolutevalue{ \sum_{k=1}^{d}\abs{a_k}^2 e^{i\lambda_k t}}^{2}

(104)

By assumption, the eigenvalues of
H
are rationally independent, which implies that the trajectory
\{(e^{i\lambda_{1}t},\dots,e^{i\lambda_{d}t})\}_{t\geq 0}
is dense on the
d
-dimensional torus (this is known as Kronecker’s theorem). By continuity, this implies that (
104
) can be equivalently written as:

M(H,\psi_{0}):=\inf_{\varphi_{1},\dots,\varphi_{d}\in[0,2\pi)}\absolutevalue{ \sum_{k=1}^{d}\abs{a_k}^2 e^{i\varphi_k}}^{2}

(105)

For convenience we write
p_{k}=\absolutevalue{a_k}^{2}
. Let us sort the coefficients so that
p_{1}
is the largest. We consider two cases.

The first case is
p_{1}\leq\frac{1}{2}
. In that case the infimum (
105
) is actually equal to zero. To see this, we need to find
\varphi_{k}
so that the complex numbers
z_{k}=p_{k}e^{i\varphi_{k}}
sum to zero, i.e. comprise sides of a polygon in the complex plane. It is easy to prove that is is always possible by induction on
d
(the base case
d=3
corresponds to creating a triangle with side lengths
p_{1},p_{2},p_{3}
, which is possible since all
p_{k}
are at most
1/2
.).

The other case is
p_{1}>\frac{1}{2}
. We claim that the infimum is attained when
e^{i\varphi_{1}}=1
and
e^{i\varphi_{k}}=-1
for
k=2,\dots,d
. Clearly for such a choice of phases we obtain the value
(2p_{1}-1)^{2}
, which is
>0
since we assumed
p_{1}>\frac{1}{2}
. To see that this is optimal, we use the triangle inequality:

\absolutevalue{ \sum_{k=1}^{d} p_k e^{i\varphi_k} }\geq\absolutevalue{p_1 e^{i\varphi_1}}-\absolutevalue{\sum_{k=2}^{d} p_k e^{i\varphi_k} }\geq\absolutevalue{p_1 e^{i\varphi_1}}-\sum_{k=2}^{d}\absolutevalue{ p_k e^{i\varphi_k} }=p_{1}-\sum_{k=2}^{d}p_{k}=2p_{1}-1

(106)

Combining the two cases together, we obtain the condition:

1-\varepsilon^{2}\geq M(H,\psi_{0})=\max\{2\absolutevalue{a_1}^{2}-1,0\}

(107)

which finishes the proof.
∎

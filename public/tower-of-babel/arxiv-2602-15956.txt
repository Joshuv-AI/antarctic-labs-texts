Introduction

A. Einstein worked on various versions of the Unified Field Theory (Nonsymmetric Gravitational Theory – NGT), see
[
2
]
. This theory was intended to unite the gravitation theory, to which General Relativity is related, and the theory of electromagnetism. Beginning in 1950, A. Einstein used a real nonsymmetric basic (0,2)-tensor
G
,
whose symmetric part
g
is associated with gravity, and the skew-symmetric part
F
is associated with electromagnetism.
In his attempt to construct a Unified Field Theory,
(briefly NGT), A. Einstein
[
2
]
considered a snooth manifold
(M,G=g+F)
with a linear connection
\nabla
with torsion
T(X,Y)=\nabla_{X}Y-\nabla_{Y}X-[X,Y]
satisfying the following condition:

(\nabla_{X}G)(Y,Z)=-G(T(X,Y),Z)\quad(X,Y,Z\in\mathfrak{X}_{M}).

(1.1)

called an Einstein connection.
The idea of a nonsymmetric basic tensor was revisited by J.W. Moffat
[
7
]
, allowing for richer gravitational dynamics. Results by T. Jansen and T. Prokopec
[
6
]
point to potential improvements and challenges within NGT.
M.I. Wanas et al.
[
11
]
studied path equations of a particle in Einstein’s nonsymmetric geometry. Recent approaches to modified gravity often rely on differential geometry, including torsion and non-metricity, as a natural extension of General Relativity.
Connections with totally skew-symmetric torsion,
i.e.,
T(X,Y,Z)
is a 3-form, satisfying

T(X,Y,Z)=-T(X,Z,Y)=-T(Y,X,Z)
,
are important due to the relations with mathematical physics, as they admit a coupling to spinor fields and lead to holonomy and rigidity phenomena.
In this case, the dimension of the space of parallel
spinors coincides with the number of preserved supersymmetries. Such connections arise
naturally in supersymmetric string theories and non-linear
\sigma
-models, as well as in gravitational models
[
4
]
.
S. Ivanov and M. Lj. Zlatanović (see
[
5
]
) presented conditions for the existence and uniqueness of the Einstein connection with totally skew-symmetric torsion on a manifold
(M,G=g+F)
and gave its explicit expression using an almost contact metric structure.
In this case, the torsion is determined by
dF
:

T(X,Y,Z)=-\frac{1}{3}\,dF(X,Y,Z)
,
and the difference tensor is given by

K_{X}Y=\tfrac{1}{2}\big\{T({f}X,Y)-T(X,{f}Y)+T(X,Y)\big\}.

(1.2)

Totally skew-symmetry of
K
implies totally skew-symmetry of
T
, but the converse does not hold in general. In this paper, we do not assume that the torsion is totally skew-symmetric.

A linear connection
{\nabla}
on a Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
is said to be statistical
if it is torsionless and tensor

({\nabla}g)(X,Y,Z):=({\nabla}_{X}\,g)(Y,Z)
is symmetric in all its entries, equivalently, the cubic form
K(X,Y,Z):=g(K(X,Y),Z)

(associated with the difference tensor (
2.9
) of
\nabla
) is symmetric.
The statistical structure has applications in theory of probability and statistics as well as in information geometry.
Unfortunately, we cannot directly apply the statistical structure to Einstein’s nonsymmetric geometry: if the cubic form
K(X,Y,Z)
is symmetric, then by (
2.11
) and (
2.12
),
T=K=0
is true; hence,
\nabla=\nabla^{g}
.

Weak metric structures, developed by V. Rovenski et. al, see
[
9
]
, generalize the classical metric
f
-structures and are well suited for a study of
G=g+F
in NGT with an arbitrary skew-symmetric tensor
F
.
A weak almost Hermitian manifold
(M,f,g)
is a Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
of dimension
2m\geq 4
endowed with non-singular skew-symmetric endomorphism
f
and the 2-form
F
such that
F(X,Y)=g(X,fY)
, see
[
12
]
.
V. Rovenski and M. Zlatanović
[
12
,
10
]
were the first to apply the weak
f
-structure, in particular, the weak almost contact structure, to NGT of totally skew-symmetric torsion with

F(X,Y)=g(X,fY)
.

M. Prvanović
[
8
]
obtained the explicit form of the Einstein connection of almost Hermitian manifold,
and using its torsion tensor, characterized the sixteen classes (by A. Gray and L.M. Hervella
[
3
]
) of almost Hermitian manifold.

In this paper, we obtain the explicit form of the Einstein connection of an NGT space, in particular, a weak almost Hermitian manifold, satisfying some natural condition, called the
f^{2}
-torsion condition. This condition disappears for the classical case, hence our result generalizes the result by M. Prvanović.
We also present the explicit form of the Einstein connection of an almost contact metric manifold satisfying the
f^{2}
-torsion condition.

The paper has an Introduction and six sections.
In Section
2
we review basics of NGT, give examples, prove several lemmas, and discuss the relation between torsion and the contorsion,
K
, of an Einstein connection emphasizing the role of
f^{2}
-torsion condition.
In Section
3
we deduce equations for Einstein connection of an almost contact metric manifold with the
f^{2}
-torsion condition
(Theorem
3.3
).
In Section
4
we generalize the result of M. Prvanović and deduce equations for an Einstein connection with the
f^{2}
-torsion condition of nonsymmetric pseudo-Riemannian manifold (Theorem
4.3
), in particular, for weak almost Hermitian manifold (Corollary
4.5
).
Long expressions of Section
4
are given in Section
5
.

2
Einstein’s nonsymmetric geometry

A manifold
(M,G=g+F)
equipped with an Einstein connection

\nabla
different from the Levi-Civita connection
\nabla^{g}
is called an NGT space.
The basic (0,2)-tensor
G
of an NGT space

(M,G=g+F)
decomposes into two parts, the symmetric part
g
(pseudo-Riemannian metric) and the skew-symmetric part
F
(fundamental 2-form), that is,

g(X,Y)=\tfrac{1}{2}\big[G(X,Y)+G(Y,X)\big],\quad F(X,Y)=\tfrac{1}{2}\big[G(X,Y)-G(Y,X)\big].

We assume that the skew-symmetric part,
F
is non-zero, may have arbitrary (not necessarily constant) rank, and in particular, may be non-degenerate (that is, of maximal rank).
Therefore, we obtain a (1,1)-tensor
{f}\neq 0
(of not necessarily constant rank) determined by

g(X,fY)=F(X,Y)\quad(X,Y\in\mathfrak{X}_{M}).

Since
F
is skew-symmetric, the tensor
{f}
is also skew-symmetric:

g({f}X,Y)=-g(X,{f}Y)
.
Note that
f=-A
, where the tensor
A
is given in
[
5
]
by the equality
g(AX,Y)=F(X,Y)
.

The torsion of a linear connection
\nabla
on
M
is given by

T(X,Y)=\nabla_{X}Y-\nabla_{Y}X-[X,Y].

The covariant derivative an
(p,k)
-tensor
S
for
p=0,1
is an
(p,k+1)
-tensor
\nabla S
:

(\nabla S)(Y,X_{1},\ldots,X_{k})=\nabla_{Y}(S(X_{1},\ldots,X_{k}))-\sum\nolimits_{\,i=1}^{k}S(X_{1},\ldots,\nabla_{Y}X_{i},\ldots,X_{k}).

(2.1)

Separating symmetric and skew-symmetric parts of (
1.1
),
we express the covariant derivatives
\nabla g
and
\nabla F
in terms of the (0,3)-torsion tensor
T(X,Y,Z)=g(T(X,Y),Z)
:

\displaystyle 2\,(\nabla_{X}\,g)(Y,Z)

\displaystyle=T(Z,X,\,Y+fY)-T(X,Y,\,Z+fZ),

(2.2)

\displaystyle 2\,(\nabla_{Z}\,F)(X,Y)

\displaystyle=-T(Z,X,\,Y+fY)-T(Y,Z,\,X+fX).

(2.3)

Recall the co-boundary formula for a 2-form
F

(without the coefficient 3, unlike
[
1
]

\displaystyle dF(X,Y,Z)

\displaystyle=X(F(Y,Z))+Y(F(Z,X))+Z(F(X,Y))

\displaystyle\ -F([X,Y],Z)-F([Z,X],Y)-F([Y,Z],X).

(2.4)

The equality (
2
) yields

\displaystyle dF(X,Y,Z)=(\nabla^{g}_{X}\,F)(Y,Z)+(\nabla^{g}_{Y}\,F)(Z,X)+(\nabla^{g}_{Z}\,F)(X,Y).

(2.5)

The metricity condition (
1.1
) can be writen in the form

(\nabla_{X}(g+F))(Y,Z)=-\,T(X,Y,Z)-T(X,Y,fZ).

(2.6)

Taking the cyclic sum in (
2.6
) and applying the equality

\displaystyle dF(X,Y,Z)

\displaystyle=\,T(X,Y,fZ)+\,T(Y,Z,fX)+\,T(Z,X,fY)

\displaystyle+(\nabla_{X}F)(Y,Z)+(\nabla_{Y}F)(Z,X)+(\nabla_{Z}F)(X,Y),

which follows from (
2.3
) and (
2.5
), we obtain

\displaystyle(\nabla_{X}g)(Y,Z)+(\nabla_{Y}g)(Z,X)+(\nabla_{Z}g)(X,Y)

\displaystyle=-\,dF(X,Y,Z)-\,T(X,Y,Z)-\,T(Y,Z,X)-\,T(Z,X,Y).

Since the left hand side of (

) is symmetric, while the right hand side is
skew-symmetric, we get the following two relations, see
[

\displaystyle(\nabla_{X}\,g)(Y,Z)+(\nabla_{Y}\,g)(Z,X)+(\nabla_{Z}\,g)(X,Y)=0,

and

\displaystyle dF(X,Y,Z)=-T(X,Y,Z)-T(Y,Z,X)-T(Z,X,Y).

(2.8)

The
difference tensor

K
of a linear connection
\nabla
and the Levi-Civita connection
\nabla^{g}
is

K_{X}Y:=\nabla_{X}Y-\nabla^{g}_{X}Y.

(2.9)

The Einstein connection
\nabla

is represented in
[
5
, Eq. (3.7)]
using the torsion

as

\displaystyle g(\nabla_{X}Y,Z)

\displaystyle=g(\nabla^{g}_{X}Y,Z)+\tfrac{1}{2}\big\{T(X,Y,Z)-T(Z,X,{f}Y)+T(Y,Z,{f}X)\big\}.

(2.10)

By (
2.10
), the difference (0,3)-tensor

K(X,Y,Z)=g(K_{X}Y,Z)
and the torsion tensor of an Einstein connection
\nabla
are expressed linearly in terms of each other:

\displaystyle 2\,K(X,Y,Z)

\displaystyle=T(X,Y,Z)-T(Z,X,{f}Y)+T(Y,Z,{f}X),

(2.11)

\displaystyle T(X,Y,Z)

\displaystyle=K(X,Y,Z)-K(Y,X,Z)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad T(X,Y)=K_{X}Y-K_{Y}X.

(2.12)

Using various presentations of tensors and (
2.1
), we obtain

\displaystyle(\nabla^{g}F)(Z,X,Y)=(\nabla^{g}_{Z}\,F)(X,Y)=g(X,(\nabla^{g}_{Z}\,f)Y).

(2.13)

Lemma 2.1
.

For an Einstein connection
\nabla
, the following conditions are equivalent:

(i)
K(X,Y,Z)=-K(X,Z,Y)
,

(ii)
\nabla g=0
,

(iii)
(\nabla_{X}F)(Y,Z)=-(\nabla_{Y}F)(X,Z)

or
g((\nabla_{X}f)Z,Y)=-g((\nabla_{Y}f)Z,X)
.

Proof.

We calculate using (

) and (

\displaystyle(\nabla_{X}\,g)(Y,Z)

\displaystyle=X(g(Y,Z))-g(\nabla^{g}_{X}Y+K_{X}Y,Z)-g(Y,\nabla^{g}_{X}Z+K_{X}Z)

\displaystyle=(\nabla^{g}_{X}\,g)(Y,Z)-g(K_{X}Y,Z)-g(K_{X}Z,Y).

By this and

are equivalent.
The equations (

) and (

) yield, see

\displaystyle(\nabla_{Z}\,g)(X,Y)=(\nabla_{X}F)(Y,Z)+(\nabla_{Y}F)(X,Z).

(2.14)

By (
2.14
) and (

),
the conditions (ii) and (iii) are equivalent.
∎

3
Einstein connection of an almost contact metric manifold

Contact Riemannian geometry is a significant field in both pure mathematics and theoretical physics,

Developing the approach of

Here,

is a Riemannian metric,

is a skew-symmetric (1,1)-tensor of rank

\xi
is a unit (Reeb) vector field, and

\displaystyle f^{2}=-{I}+\eta\otimes\xi,\quad g(fX,fY)=g(X,Y)-\eta(X)\eta(Y),

Differentiating by

Using the Leibniz rule, we have

\displaystyle\nabla^{g}_{X}(\eta\otimes\xi)=(\nabla^{g}_{X}\eta)\otimes\xi+\eta\otimes\nabla^{g}_{X}\xi,\qquad\nabla^{g}_{X}f^{2}=(\nabla^{g}_{X}f)\,f+f\,\nabla^{g}_{X}f.

Applying the equalities above to

(\nabla^{g}_{X}f)\,fY+f\,(\nabla^{g}_{X}f)Y=(\nabla^{g}_{X}\eta)(Y)\,\xi+\eta(Y)\,\nabla^{g}_{X}\xi.

(3.2)

Definition 3.1
.

A linear connection
\nabla
satisfies the

torsion condition
if the following is true:

\displaystyle T(f^{2}X,Y)=T(X,f^{2}Y)=f^{2}T(X,Y),

(3.3)

which can be equivalently written as

\displaystyle T(f^{2}X,Y,Z)=T(X,f^{2}Y,Z)=T(X,Y,f^{2}Z).

If (

) is true for an Einstein connection, then, using (

) and (

), we have

\displaystyle dF(f^{2}X,Y,Z)=dF(X,f^{2}Y,Z)=dF(X,Y,f^{2}Z),

\displaystyle(\nabla^{g}F)(f^{2}X,Y,Z)=(\nabla^{g}F)(X,f^{2}Y,Z)=(\nabla^{g}F)(X,Y,f^{2}Z).

If the

-torsion condition (

-torsion condition (

The converse is not true in general.

Lemma 3.2
.

The following
conditions are true for all vector fields

X,Y,Z
on an almost contact metric manifold:

\displaystyle(\nabla^{g}_{X}F)(fY,fZ)

\displaystyle=-(\nabla^{g}_{X}F)(Y,Z)+\eta(Y)\,(\nabla^{g}_{X}F)(\xi,Z)+\eta(Z)\,(\nabla^{g}_{X}F)(Y,\xi),

(3.4)

\displaystyle(\nabla^{g}_{X}F)(fY,Z)

\displaystyle=(\nabla^{g}_{X}F)(Y,fZ)+\eta(Z)\,(\nabla^{g}_{X}F)(fY,\xi)-\eta(Y)\,(\nabla^{g}_{X}F)(\xi,fZ).

(3.5)

Proof.

Using
\nabla^{g}g=0
and
F(Y,Z)=g(Y,fZ)
, we have

(\nabla^{g}_{X}F)(Y,Z)=g\big(Y,(\nabla^{g}_{X}f)Z\big)
.
Hence

(\nabla^{g}_{X}F)(fY,fZ)=g\big(fY,(\nabla^{g}_{X}f)\,fZ\big).

Substituting (
3.2
), we get

(\nabla^{g}_{X}f)\,fZ=-f\,(\nabla^{g}_{X}f)Z+(\nabla^{g}_{X}\eta)(Z)\xi+\eta(Z)\nabla^{g}_{X}\xi.

Using
g(\xi,fZ)=0
gives

\displaystyle(\nabla^{g}_{X}F)(fY,fZ)

\displaystyle=-\,g\left(fY,f\,(\nabla^{g}_{X}f)Z\right)+\eta(Z)\,g(fY,\nabla^{g}_{X}\xi).

By
g(fX,fY)=g(X,Y)-\eta(X)\eta(Y)
and
\eta(fZ)=0
, we obtain

g\!\left(fY,f(\nabla^{g}_{X}f)Z\right)=g\big(Y,(\nabla^{g}_{X}f)Z\big)-\eta(Y)\eta\big((\nabla^{g}_{X}f)Z\big).

Therefore,

\displaystyle(\nabla^{g}_{X}F)(fY,fZ)=-g\big(Y,\nabla^{g}_{X}f)Z\big)+\eta(Y)\eta\big((\nabla^{g}_{X}f)Z\big)+\eta(Z)\,g(fY,\nabla^{g}_{X}\xi).

(3.6)

Finally, substituting the equalities

\displaystyle g\big(Y,(\nabla^{g}_{X}f)Z\big)

\displaystyle=(\nabla^{g}_{X}F)(Y,Z),\quad\eta\big((\nabla^{g}_{X}f)Z\big)=g\big(\xi,(\nabla^{g}_{X}f)Z\big)=(\nabla^{g}_{X}F)(\xi,Z),

\displaystyle(\nabla^{g}_{X}F)(Y,\xi)

\displaystyle=g\big(Y,(\nabla^{g}_{X}f)\xi\big)=-g\big(Y,f\nabla^{g}_{X}\xi\big)=g(\nabla^{g}_{X}\xi,fY).

in (
3.6
), yields (
3.4
).
Taking
Z\to fZ
in (
3.4
), we get (
3.5
).
∎

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
(M^{2n+1},f,\xi,\eta,g)
be an almost contact metric manifold, considered as an NGT space
(M,G=g+F,\nabla)
with

.

\displaystyle\nabla^{g}\,\xi=\nabla^{g}\,\eta=0,\quad T(\xi,\cdot,\cdot)=T(\cdot,\cdot,\xi)=0,\quad(\nabla^{g}F)(\xi,\cdot,\cdot)=(\nabla^{g}F)(\cdot,\cdot,\xi)=0.

(3.7)

In particular, the torsion

\displaystyle 2\,T(Y,Z,X)=

\displaystyle\ 2\,(\nabla^{g}_{fX}F)(fY,Z)-(\nabla^{g}_{fY}F)(fZ,X)-(\nabla^{g}_{fZ}F)(X,fY)

\displaystyle-(\nabla^{g}_{Y}F)(Z,X)-(\nabla^{g}_{Z}F)(X,Y).

We write

\displaystyle T(\xi,Z,\xi)=T(\xi,f^{2}Z^{\prime},\xi)+\eta(Z)\,T(\xi,\xi,\xi)=T(\xi,f^{2}Z^{\prime},\xi)=T(f^{2}\xi,Z^{\prime},\xi)=0.

Using

) again

\displaystyle T(X,Z,\xi)

\displaystyle=T(X,f^{2}Z^{\prime},\xi)+\eta(Z)\,T(X,\xi,\xi)=T(f^{2}X,Z^{\prime},\xi)=0,

\displaystyle T(\xi,Z,X)

\displaystyle=T(\xi,f^{2}Z^{\prime},X)+\eta(Z)\,T(\xi,\xi,X)=T(f^{2}\xi,Z^{\prime},X)=0.

\displaystyle T(\xi,Z,\xi)=T(X,Z,\xi)=T(\xi,Z,X)=0.

Substituting

), we obtain

\displaystyle\nabla^{g}_{\xi}\,F=0,\quad\mbox{that is,}\quad\nabla^{g}_{\xi}\,f=0.

-trajectories are

-geodesics.
From (

),
we have

\displaystyle(\nabla^{g}_{X}F)(Y,Z)-(\nabla^{g}_{X}F)(fY,fZ)=T(Y,fZ,fX)+T(fY,Z,fX).

Taking into account (

\displaystyle 2(\nabla^{g}_{X}F)(Y,Z)=T(Y,fZ,fX)+T(fY,Z,fX).

Replacing

and

\displaystyle 2(\nabla^{g}_{fX}F)(fY,Z)=-T(fY,fZ,X)+T(Y,Z,X)=-2(\nabla^{g}_{fX}F)(fZ,Y).

\displaystyle(\nabla^{g}_{X}F)(fY,fZ)=-(\nabla^{g}_{X}F)(Y,Z),\qquad(\nabla^{g}_{X}F)(fY,Z)=(\nabla^{g}_{X}F)(Y,fZ).

Remark 3.4
.

Although the
f^{2}
-torsion condition becomes trivial for almost Hermitian manifolds, it is strong enough for almost contact metric manifolds.
In Theorem
3.3
, our manifold is locally the direct product of
\mathbb{R}

and an almost Hermitian manifold
(\bar{M}^{2n},J,\bar{g})
, and
\nabla
, being an Einstein connection on the
2n

), where
J
is replaced by
f
.
Consequently, the 1-form
\eta
is closed,
d\eta=0
.
The distribution
\ker\eta
is involutive; thus, it is Frobenius integrable. The integral submanifold
\bar{M}^{2n}
is almost Hermitian.

We work with an Einstein connection satisfying the
f^{2}
-torsion condition, without assuming any symmetry of the torsion tensor.
If, in addition, the torsion

is totally skew–symmetric, then
the general formula (
3.3
) reduces to
(
3.21
), and

\nabla^{g}_{\xi}F=0
.
This additional assumption leads to the almost–nearly cosymplectic case, which we recall below from
[

]
.

Corollary 3.5
.

Under the conditions of Theorem
3.3
, the torsion
T
is totally skew-symmetric if and only if

(
3.7
)
is true,

Proof.

By Theorem
3.3
, we have

\nabla^{g}_{\xi}\,F=0
and
\nabla^{g}\,\xi=0
.

is totally skew-symmetric.

\displaystyle 0=\ 3\,(\nabla^{g}_{X}F)(Y,Z)-3\,(\nabla^{g}_{fZ}F)(Y,fX)+(\nabla^{g}_{Z}F)(X,Y)-(\nabla^{g}_{fX}F)(Z,fY).

Conclusion

We presented explicitly the Einstein connection for nonsymmetric pseudo-Riemannian, in particular, for weak almost Hermitian manifolds, satisfying the
f^{2}
-torsion condition.
We also obtained the corresponding formulas for almost contact metric manifolds, assuming the
f^{2}
-torsion condition.
We expressed the torsion in terms of
\nabla^{g}F
and
dF
, and the additional
\widetilde{Q}
-terms, explained precisely how the weak almost Hermitian case differs from the almost Hermitian case.
The presented identities provide a new tool for constructing examples and for studying classes of nonsymmetric pseudo-Riemannian manifolds, in particular, weak almost Hermitian manifolds
with the usual Gray-Hervella classification, in Einstein’s nonsymmetric gravitational theory.
In further research we will study weak (para-) metric
f
-manifolds, in particular, weak (para-) contact metric manifolds, with an Einstein connections or connections with totally skew-symmetric torsion,
satisfying the
f^{2}
-torsion condition for vectors orthogonal to the distribution
\ker f
.

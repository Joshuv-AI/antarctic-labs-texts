Abstract
. We introduce the Macaulay2 package MacaulayPosets . This package utilized the poset data type introduced in the Posets package and offers functionality for studying the Macaulay property for posets, particularly those which arise as monomial posets of commutative rings.



1.
Introduction

Partially ordered sets, or posets, are fundamental combinatorial structures widely used in contemporary research to model discrete systems endowed with an order relation. Macaulay2 provides the
Posets
package
[
CMW
]
, developed by David Cook II, Sonja Mapes, and Gwyneth Whieldon, which introduces a dedicated data structure along with essential methods for working with posets. This package includes tools for enumerating commonly studied classes of posets, performing various operations, and computing key invariants associated with posets.

The development of the
MacaulayPosets
package is motivated by the study of posets in the context of commutative algebra. This package builds upon the poset data type introduced in the
Posets
package, extending its functionality to explore the Macaulay property in posets, particularly those that arise as monomial posets of commutative rings. A Macaulay poset is characterized by a ranked structure and a total order that interacts harmoniously with the partial order, enabling the establishment of bounds on the sizes of subsets of a given rank within an order ideal. Prior to our package, there was a single software
[
BD
]
developed in Java by S. L.  Bezrukov and A. Dissanayake, which could check whether a poset with at most
30
elements is Macaulay. Our package surpasses this limitation (see
3.11
) and allows to check the Macaulay property for graded rings in addition to posets.

Macaulay posets provide a powerful framework for understanding the interplay between algebraic invariants of graded rings, such as Hilbert functions, and their combinatorial properties. Their foundational application, introduced by F. S. Macaulay in
[
Mac
]
, was to characterize all possible Hilbert functions of homogeneous ideals in polynomial rings. Today, Macaulay posets play a central role in both commutative algebra and enumerative combinatorics, making them a rich subject of modern mathematical research. In extremal combinatorics, they appear in the study of isoperimetric problems, with significant contributions from Katona
[
Kat
]
, Kruskal
[
Kru
]
, and Clements–Lindström
[
CL
]
. Surveys on Macaulay posets can be found in
[
BL
]
and
[
Eng
, Chapter 8]
.
In commutative algebra, an emerging theory of Macaulay rings – rings whose monomial poset exhibits the Macaulay property – has gained attention; see
[
Mer
,
MP1
,
MP2
,
Kuz
]
. This growing connection between algebra and combinatorics highlights the significance of Macaulay posets as a valuable tool in contemporary mathematical research.

Our package was conceived as a companion to the paper
[
Pol
]
, which considers the Macaulay property under poset and ring operations. As such, the package implements four poset operations dubbed wedge product, closed product, fiber product, and connected sum, and two related ring operations termed fiber product and connected sum. These operations allow to easily construct complex posets, some of which have the Macaulay property and some which do not.

We thank Sergei L. Bezrukov for sharing code for the software
[
BD
]
with us. While our package does not rely on this code, it gave us our first exposure to the computational aspects of Macaulay posets.

2.
Macaulay Posets and Macaulay Rings

In this section we give an overview the Macaulay property as it pertains both to posets and to rings.

Throughout
\mathbb{N}
denotes the set of non-negative integers, including zero.

2.1.
Macaulay posets

A
partial order
on a set
{\mathcal{P}}
is a relation on
{\mathcal{P}}
that is antisymmetric, reflexive, and transitive. A
partially ordered set
or
poset
is a set
{\mathcal{P}}
along with a partial order on
{\mathcal{P}}
. A partial order
\leq
on
{\mathcal{P}}
is a
total order
if and only if for all
p,q\in{\mathcal{P}}
, we have
p\leq q
or
q\leq p
.

Suppose
\leq
is a partial order on
{\mathcal{P}}
. Suppose
p,q\in{\mathcal{P}}
. We say
q

covers

p
if and only if
p<q
, and
p<r<q
for no
r\in{\mathcal{P}}
.

Definition 2.1
.

Suppose
{\mathcal{P}}
is a poset and
A\subseteq{\mathcal{P}}
. The
upper shadow
of
A
in
{\mathcal{P}}
is

\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{$\Delta$}_{\mathcal{P}}A=\{p\in{\mathcal{P}}\mid p\text{ covers }a\text{ for some }a\in A\}

and the
lower shadow
of
A
in
{\mathcal{P}}
is

\Delta_{\mathcal{P}}A=\{p\in{\mathcal{P}}\mid a\text{ covers }p\text{ for some }a\in A\}\,.

Example 2.2
.

Consider the poset of monomials in the polynomial ring
K[x,y]
over a field
K
with partial order given by divisibility. We identify a monomial
x^{a}y^{b}
with the unit square with lower left corner at
(a,b)
in the cartesian plane. In the figure below, under this identification, the set
A=\{x^{3},y^{3}\}
is shown in pink and its upper shadow
\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{$\Delta$}A=\{x^{4},x^{3}y,y^{3}x,y^{4}\}
is shown in green.

Figure 1
.
The monomial poset of
K[x,y]
with
\{x^{3},y^{3}\}
and its upper shadow highlighted.

A
rank function
on a poset
{\mathcal{P}}
with a minimum element
m
is a function
r:{\mathcal{P}}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
such that
r(p)+1=r(q)
whenever
q
covers
p
, and
r(m)=0
. A poset
{\mathcal{P}}
is
ranked
if and only if there exists a rank function on
{\mathcal{P}}
. If a poset is ranked, then its rank function is unique. If
d\in\mathbb{N}
and
r
is a rank function on
{\mathcal{P}}
, then let
{\mathcal{P}}_{d}=r^{-1}(d)
.

Definition 2.3
.

Suppose
{\mathcal{P}}
is a ranked poset. Suppose
\leq
is a total order on
{\mathcal{P}}
. If
d,s\in\mathbb{N}
with
s\leq\lvert{\mathcal{P}}_{d}\rvert
, then the
initial segment
of size
s
in
{\mathcal{P}}_{d}
is the set consisting of the largest
s
elements of
{\mathcal{P}}_{d}
with respect to
\leq
, and is denoted
\operatorname{Seg}_{d}s
.

Definition 2.4
.

A ranked poset
{\mathcal{P}}
is
Macaulay
if and only if there exists a total order on
{\mathcal{P}}
such that for all subsets
A\subseteq{\mathcal{P}}_{d}
, the following hold

(1)

Initial segments have the smallest upper shadows:

\left|\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{$\Delta$}_{\mathcal{P}}\operatorname{Seg}_{d}|A|\right|\leq|\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{$\Delta$}_{\mathcal{P}}(A)|;

(2)

The upper shadow of an initial segment is an initial segment:

\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{$\Delta$}_{\mathcal{P}}\operatorname{Seg}_{d}|A|=\operatorname{Seg}_{d+1}|\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{$\Delta$}_{\mathcal{P}}\operatorname{Seg}_{d}|A||.

Example 2.5
.

We consider the poset
{\mathcal{P}}
of monomials in
K[x,y]
, ordered by divisibility, with the lexicographic order
{\mathcal{O}}
as a total order. On the left, the set
A=\{x^{3},xy^{2}\}
(pink) and its upper shadow (green) are shown, yielding
|A|=2
and
|\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{$\Delta$}(A)|=4
. On the right, the set
B=\operatorname{Seg}_{2}\lvert A\rvert=\{x^{3},x^{2}y\}
consists of the two lexicographically largest monomials of degree two, with its upper shadow (green) satisfying
|\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{$\Delta$}(B)|=3\leq|\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{$\Delta$}(A)|=4
. Notably,
\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{$\Delta$}(B)
itself forms a segment under
{\mathcal{O}}
, containing the three largest lexicographic monomials of degree three in
{\mathcal{P}}
.

2.2.
Macaulay rings

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
, and let
R=K[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
be a polynomial ring in
n
variables over a field
K
. Suppose
I\subseteq R
is a homogeneous ideal. A
monomial
in
R/I
is a nonzero coset
m+I
for some monomial
m
in
R
.

Definition 2.6
.

Let
S
be a graded quotient ring of a polynomial ring and let
{\mathcal{M}}_{S}
be the set of monomials in
S
. Define a partial order denoted
|
on
{\mathcal{M}}_{S}
by
f|g
if and only if
fh=g
for some
h\in{\mathcal{M}}_{S}
. The pair
({\mathcal{M}}_{S},|)
is termed the
monomial poset
of
S
.

An example of a monomial poset is presented in
fig.

3
.

The
Hilbert function
of a homogeneous ideal
I
of
S
is the function
H_{I}:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
defined by
H_{I}(d)=\dim_{K}I_{d}
, i.e. the dimension of the vector space of degree
d
elements of
I
.

Definition 2.7
.

A graded quotient ring
S
of a polynomial ring over a field is
Macaulay
if and only if there exists a total order
{\mathcal{O}}
on
{\mathcal{M}}_{S}
such that for each homogeneous ideal
I\subset S
the vector space
{\mathcal{O}}^{*}(I)
described below is an ideal of
S

{\mathcal{O}}^{*}(I)=\bigoplus_{d\geq 0}\operatorname{Span}_{K}\operatorname{Seg}_{d}H_{I}(d)

and additionally
H_{I}(d)=H_{{\mathcal{O}}^{*}(I)}(d)
for all
d\in{\mathbb{N}}
.

It is shown in
[
Kuz
, Theorem 2.6.3]
that a ring is Macaulay if and only if its monomial poset is Macaulay. Macaulay’s theorem
[
Mac
]
says that polynomial rings
K[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
are Macaulay for all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
. The Clements-Lindström Theorem
[
CL
]
says that monomial complete intersection rings, i.e., rings of the form
\frac{K[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]}{(x_{1}^{d_{1}},\dots,x_{n}^{d_{n}})}
for
d_{i}\in{\mathbb{N}}
, are Macaulay. Few other encompassing classes of Macaulay rings are known. For some further examples the reader is invited to peruse
[
BL
,
Kuz
,
Pol
]
.

3.3.
Additivity

For this section, there is only one function to mention:
isAdditive
. Additivity was introduced in
[
Cle2
]
and was leveraged in
[
Cle1
]
to study disjoint unions of isomorphic posets.
Before we introduce this property, we need the following definitions:

Suppose that
\mathcal{P}
is ranked poset with an additional total order
\leq
on it.
A set
A
consisting of elements of the same rank is called a
segment
,
if for any
a,b\in A
and any
c\in\mathcal{P}
of the same rank such that
a>c>b
,
we must have
c\in A
. The
initial segment
of size
q
in the
d
-th level
{\mathcal{P}}_{d}
of
{\mathcal{P}}
consists of the largest
q
elements with respect to
\leq
in
P_{d}
, while the
final segment
of size
q
in
{\mathcal{P}}_{d}
is the smallest
q
elements with respect to
\leq
in
P_{d}
.

Example 3.7
.

Suppose that we consider the poset of monomials of
K[x,y]
with the lex order.
The set
\{x^{4}y^{4},x^{5}y^{3},x^{6}y^{2}\}
is a segment,
but
\{x^{4}y^{4},x^{6}y^{2}\}
is not a segment.

Definition 3.8
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{P}
is a ranked poset with an additional total order
\leq
on it.
Let
A
be a segment of some rank and
B
be all the elements of the same rank that are larger than all the elements of
A
.
The
new shadow
of
A
is defined to be

\displaystyle\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{$\Delta$}_{\text{new}}(A)=\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{$\Delta$}(A)\setminus\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{$\Delta$}(B).

Definition 3.9
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{P}
is a Macaulay poset.
We say that
\mathcal{P}
is
additive
if the following hold:

(1)

If
A
is an initial segment and
B
is a segment such that
|A|=|B|
then

|\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{$\Delta$}_{\text{new}}(A)|\geq|\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{$\Delta$}_{\text{new}}(B)|.

(2)

If
B
is a segment and
C
is a final segment such that
|B|=|C|
then

|\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{$\Delta$}_{\text{new}}(B)|\geq|\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{$\Delta$}_{\text{new}}(C)|.

3.5.
Operations on posets

Four poset operations are included in
MacaulayPosets
. The first three are discussed in detail in
[
Pol
]
.

Definition 3.13
.

Suppose that for
1\leq i\leq t
we have posets
P_{i}
each with unique least element
\ell_{i}
. Their
wedge product
is the set:

P_{1}\vee P_{2}\vee\cdots\vee P_{t}=\left(\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{t}P_{i}\right)/(\ell_{1}=\ell_{2}=\cdots=\ell_{t}),

meaning that we take the disjoint union of the sets
P_{i}
in which we identify all the
\ell_{i}
into one element, with the partial order
a\leq b
if and only if
a\leq b
in
P_{i}
for some
i
.

.
The wedge product of three
4
-element chains.

3.6.
Operations on rings

Two ring operations are included in
MacaulayPosets
, namely the fiber product and connected sum. When the monomial posets of the resulting rings are considered, these operations are the algebraic counterpart of the poset operations termed wedge product, poset fiber product, closed product, and poset connected sum in the previous section.

Definition 3.22
.

Given rings
A,B
, and
C
equipped with ring homomorphisms
\pi_{A}:A\to C
and
\pi_{B}:B\to C
, the
fiber product
of
A
and
B
over
C
is the following subring of
A\times B

A\times_{C}B=\{(a,b):a\in A,b\in B,\pi_{A}(a)=\pi_{B}(b)\}.

In the particular case when
C=K
,
A=R/I
and
B=S/J
for some homogeneous ideals
I
of
R=K[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
and
J
of
S=K[y_{1},\ldots,y_{m}]
, the
fiber product over
K
admits an explicit presentation

(3.1)

A\times_{K}B=\frac{K[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},y_{1},\ldots,y_{m}]}{I+J+(x_{i}y_{j}:1\leq i\leq n,1\leq j\leq m)}.

Since a presentations for the general fiber product are difficult to write down explicitly, in our package we offer the function
ringFiberProduct
which computes the fiber product of two
K

).

The precise correspondence between the ring operations described above and the poset operations in
section

3.5
is described in
[
Pol
, Proposition 2.22]
where it is shown that for
A,B,C
quotients of a polynomial ring by monomial ideals we have

\displaystyle{\mathcal{M}}_{A\times_{K}B}={\mathcal{M}}_{A}\vee{\mathcal{M}}_{B}

\displaystyle{\mathcal{M}}_{A\times_{C}B}={\mathcal{M}}_{A}\times_{{\mathcal{M}}_{C}}{\mathcal{M}}_{B}.

Under the same hypotheses, similar considerations yield the following extensions of the above identities to connected sums for for Gorenstein graded rings
A,B,C
with
A
and
B
having the same socle degree

\displaystyle{\mathcal{M}}_{A\#_{K}B}={\mathcal{M}}_{A}\diamond{\mathcal{M}}_{B}

\displaystyle{\mathcal{M}}_{A\#_{C}B}={\mathcal{M}}_{A}\#_{{\mathcal{M}}_{C}}{\mathcal{M}}_{B}.

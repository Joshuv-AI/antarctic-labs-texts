On the Meixner–Pollaczek polynomials and the Sturm–Liouville problems Preprint: AIP/123-QED
Mourad E. H. Ismail ∗ and Nasser Saad

Abstract
This work provides a detailed study of Meixner–Pollaczek polynomials and employs the central difference operator to study the Sturm–Liouville problem. It presents two linearly independent solutions to the recursion relation, along with the associated difference equations. Additionally, the establishment of second-kind functions is discussed.

Introduction

The Meixner–Pollaczek polynomials are a family of orthogonal polynomials. They were first introduced by Meixner
m1934
and later rediscovered and generalized by Pollaczek
Poll50
, see also
a1985
;
c1978
;

P_{n}^{(\lambda)}(x;\phi)=\frac{(2\lambda)_{n}}{n!}e^{i\,n\,\phi}{}_{2}F_{1}\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}-n,&\lambda+i\,x\\
2\lambda\end{array}\bigg|1-e^{-2i\,\phi}\right),\quad i=\sqrt{-1},~n=0,1,2,\cdots,

(1)

and

P_{n}^{(\lambda)}(x;\phi)=e^{in\phi}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{(\lambda+ix)_{k}(\lambda-ix)_{n-k}}{k!(n-k)!}e^{-2ik\phi}

where

(3)

They are orthogonal on the real line with respect to the weight function

\omega(x,\lambda,\phi)=e^{(2\phi-\pi)\,x}|\Gamma(\lambda+ix)|^{2}

(4)

with the orthogonality relation

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}{P}_{n}^{(\lambda)}(x;\phi){P}_{m}^{(\lambda)}(x;\phi)\,\omega(x,\lambda,\phi)dx=\frac{2\,\pi\,\Gamma(n+2\lambda)}{(2\sin\phi)^{2\lambda}n!}\,\delta_{mn}

(5)

where the Kronecker delta

if

, otherwise it is zero.

\displaystyle\begin{gathered}\partial_{t}Y(t)+[2\lambda t-2x\sin\phi-2\lambda\cos\phi]Y(t)=y_{1}-2x\sin\phi\,y_{0}-2\lambda\cos\phi\,y_{0}.\end{gathered}

Thus we find that

\displaystyle\begin{gathered}(1-te^{i\phi})^{\lambda-ix}(1-te^{-i\phi})^{\lambda+ix}Y(t)=y_{0}+[y_{1}-2x\sin\phi\,y_{0}-2\lambda\cos\phi\,y_{0}]\,\int_{0}^{t}(1-ue^{i\phi})^{\lambda-ix-1}(1-ue^{-i\phi})^{\lambda+ix-1}\,du.\end{gathered}

We now study the asymptotics of these solutions. We start with the
Meixner–Pollaczek polynomials. The generating function (
3
) has the
t
-singularities
t=e^{\pm i\phi}
and the dominant part of a comparison function at
these singularities are
(1-te^{-i\phi})^{-\lambda-ix}(1-e^{2i\phi})^{-\lambda+ix}
and

(1-te^{i\phi})^{-\lambda+ix}(1-e^{-2i\phi})^{-\lambda-ix}
, respectively. Therefore

\displaystyle P_{n}^{(\lambda)}(x;\phi)\approx\frac{(\lambda+ix)_{n}}{n!}e^{-in\phi}(1-e^{2i\phi})^{-\lambda+ix}+\frac{(\lambda-ix)_{n}}{n!}e^{in\phi}(1-e^{-2i\phi})^{-\lambda-ix},

(69)

holds for all
x
in the complex plane.
This shows that

\displaystyle P_{n}^{(\lambda)}(x;\phi)\approx\frac{n^{\lambda-ix-1}}{\Gamma(\lambda-ix)}e^{in\phi}(1-e^{-2i\phi})^{-\lambda-ix},

(70)

when
\Im x>0
. The numerator polynomials
P_{n}^{(*)}(x,\lambda,\phi)
satisfy the Meixner–Pollaczek recurrence relation with initial conditions
P_{0}^{(*)}(x,\lambda,\phi)=0,P_{1}^{(*)}(x,\lambda,\phi)=2\sin\phi
. The generating function (
V
) becomes

\displaystyle(1-te^{i\phi})^{\lambda-ix}(1-te^{-i\phi})^{\lambda+ix}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}P_{n}^{(*)}(x,\lambda,\phi)t^{n}=2\sin\phi\,\int_{0}^{t}(1-ue^{i\phi})^{\lambda-ix-1}(1-ue^{-i\phi})^{\lambda+ix-1}\,du.

(71)

We then apply Darboux’s method
a1984
and show that

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{P_{n}^{(*)}(x,\lambda,\phi)}{P_{n}^{(\lambda)}(x;\phi)}=2\sin\phi\int_{0}^{e^{-i\phi}}(1-ue^{i\phi})^{\lambda-ix-1}(1-ue^{-i\phi})^{\lambda+ix-1}\,du

(72)

holds for
\Im x>0
. It is clear from (
70
) that the orthonormal polynomials, say
\{p_{n}(x)\}
do not belong to
L_{2}
for
\Im x>0
, hence the moment problem has a unique solution, see
akh
. Moreover the left-hand side of (
72
) is the Stietjes transform of the orthogonality measure, which is normalized to have
total mass
=1
. It is clear from (
72
) that the Stietjes transform has no poles, hence the measure is absolutely continuous. This latter fact also
follows from (
69
) because the series
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|p_{n}(x)|^{2}
diverges for all real
x
.

Let
W
be the normalized weight function. Thus the inversion formula (
64
) gives

\displaystyle\begin{gathered}W(x)=\lim_{\epsilon\to 0+}\frac{F(x-i\epsilon)-F(x+i\epsilon)}{2\pi i}=\frac{2\sin\phi}{2\pi i}\int_{e^{-i\phi}}^{e^{i\phi}}(1-ue^{i\phi})^{\lambda-ix-1}(1-ue^{-i\phi})^{\lambda+ix-1}\,du.\end{gathered}

This is a beta integral and the change of variable
u=e^{-i\phi}+(e^{i\phi}-e^{-i\phi})v
transforms it to the standard form.
Therefore the normalized weight function is given by

\displaystyle W(x)=\frac{(2\sin\phi)^{2\lambda}}{2\pi\Gamma(2\lambda)}\;e^{(2\phi-\pi)x}\,|\Gamma(\lambda+ix)|^{2}.

(75)

We note that the generating functions (
3
) and (
71
) prove the explicit representation

\displaystyle P_{n}^{(*)}(x,\lambda,\phi)=2\sin\phi\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\frac{P_{k}^{(\lambda)}(x;\phi)\,P_{n-k-1}^{(1-\lambda)}(-x;\phi)}{n-k}.

(76)

VI
Function of the second kind

Define
the function of the second kind
Q_{n}^{(\lambda)}(z;\phi)
by

\displaystyle\omega(z;\lambda,\phi)Q_{n}^{(\lambda)}(z;\phi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{P_{n}^{(\lambda)}(t;\phi)}{z-t}\omega(t;\lambda,\phi)dt,

(77)

defined for
\Im z\neq 0
. Because we have two variables, we shall use
T_{z}
and
T_{t}
to denote the action of the operator
T
on a function of
z
or
t
. It is clear, by the definition of
T
, that

T_{z}\left(\frac{1}{z-t}\right)=-T_{t}\left(\frac{1}{z-t}\right).

Theorem VI.1
.

The function of the second kind also satisfies (
10
) and (
12
), that is

\displaystyle\begin{gathered}TQ_{n}^{(\lambda)}(z;\phi)=2\sin\phi\,Q_{n-1}^{(\lambda+1/2)}(z;\phi),\\
T[\omega(z;\lambda,\phi)Q_{n}^{(\lambda)}(z;\phi)]=-(n+1)\,\omega(z;\lambda-\tfrac{1}{2},\phi)\,Q_{n+1}^{(\lambda-1/2)}(z;\phi).\end{gathered}

Proof.

We first prove the second relation. Clearly

\displaystyle T_{z}[\omega(z;\lambda,\phi)Q_{n}^{(\lambda)}(z;\phi)]

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathbb{R}}T_{t}\left[\frac{1}{z-t}\right]\omega(t;\lambda,\phi)P_{n}^{(\lambda)}(t;\phi)dt=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{T_{t}[\omega(t;\lambda,\phi)P_{n}^{(\lambda)}(t;\phi)]}{z-t}dt

\displaystyle=-(n+1)\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{P_{n+1}^{(\lambda-1/2)}(t;\phi)}{z-t}\omega(t;\lambda-\tfrac{1}{2},\phi)dt

\displaystyle=-(n+1)\,\omega(z;\lambda-\tfrac{1}{2},\phi)\,Q_{n+1}^{(\lambda-1/2)}(z;\phi).

To prove the lowering relation we note that

\displaystyle 2\sin\phi\,Q_{n-1}^{(\lambda+1/2)}(z;\phi)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\omega(z;\lambda+1/2,\phi)}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{\omega(t;\lambda+1/2,\phi)}{z-t}T_{t}P_{n}^{(\lambda)}(t;\phi)\,dt

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\omega(z;\lambda+1/2,\phi)}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}P_{n}^{(\lambda)}(t)T_{t}\left[\frac{\omega(t;\lambda+1/2,\phi)}{z-t}\right]\,dt,

where we used the analogue of integration by parts in the last step (see Section 4.). A calculation shows that the integral in the above relation is

\displaystyle-\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}P_{n}^{(\lambda)}(t;\phi)\omega(t;\lambda,\phi)\left[\frac{e^{i\phi}(\lambda-it)}{z-\frac{i}{2}-t}+\frac{e^{-i\phi}(\lambda+it)}{z+\frac{i}{2}-t}\right]\,dt.

Now write
\lambda-it
as
\lambda-iz-1/2+i(z-t-i/2)
and use the orthogonality of the
P_{n}
’s to see that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\omega(z;\lambda+1/2,\phi)}

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}P_{n}^{(\lambda)}(t;\phi)\omega(t;\lambda,\phi)\frac{e^{i\phi}(\lambda-it)}{z-t-i/2}dt

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\omega(z;\lambda+1/2,\phi)}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}P_{n}^{(\lambda)}(t;\phi)\omega(t;\lambda,\phi)\frac{e^{i\phi}(\lambda-iz-1/2)}{z-t-i/2}\,dt

\displaystyle=\frac{i}{\omega(z-i/2;\lambda,\phi)}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{P_{n}^{(\lambda)}(t;\phi)}{z-t-i/2}\omega(t;\lambda,\phi)\,dt

\displaystyle=i\,Q_{n}^{(\lambda)}(z-\frac{i}{2},\phi).

Similarly we can handle the second integral to show that it is equal to
-i\,Q_{n}^{(\lambda)}(z+\frac{i}{2},\phi)
.
∎

Next, we record the Rodrigues-type formula

\displaystyle\omega(x;\lambda,\phi)Q_{n}^{(\lambda)}(x;\phi)=\frac{(-1)^{n}}{n!}T^{n}\left(\omega(x;\lambda+n/2,\phi)Q_{0}^{(\lambda+n/2)}(x;\phi)\right)

(81)

that follows immediately from equation (
VI.1
).

We note that (
72
) implies that the Stieltjes transform of the normalized weight function
W
is given by its right–hand side. Thus

\displaystyle\frac{(2\sin\phi)^{2\lambda}}{\Gamma(2\lambda)}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{\omega(t;\lambda,\phi)}{x-t}\,dt=2\sin\phi\int_{0}^{e^{-i\phi}}(1-ue^{i\phi})^{\lambda-ix-1}(1-ue^{-i\phi})^{\lambda+ix-1}\,du,

(82)

holds when
\Im x>0
. This identifies
Q_{0}
as

\displaystyle Q_{0}^{(\lambda)}(z;\phi)=\frac{\Gamma(2\lambda)}{(2\sin\phi)^{2\lambda-1}\omega(z;\lambda,\phi)}\int_{0}^{e^{-i\phi}}(1-ue^{i\phi})^{\lambda-iz-1}(1-ue^{-i\phi})^{\lambda+iz-1}\,du,

(83)

when
\Im z>0
, and
\overline{Q_{0}^{(\lambda)}(z;\phi)}=Q_{0}^{(\lambda)}(\bar{z};\phi)
for all nonreal
z
.

ABSTRACT
Key Points•This is, to our knowledge, the first large-scale quantitative characterization of TET2 VAF as a continuous prognostic variable in CMML.•A per-integer VAF adjustment refines three contemporary CMML prognostic tools; a freely accessible web calculator implements the framework.

Introduction

Chronic myelomonocytic leukemia (CMML) is a clonal myeloid neoplasm with overlapping features of myelodysplasia and myeloproliferation with persistent monocytosis.1,2 Pathogenesis results from the cumulative impact of somatic mutations in epigenetic, splicing, and signaling genes, with ∼30% of patients destined to transform to acute myeloid leukemia (AML). Although prognosis is often poor, clinical outcomes are highly variable, with some patients experiencing indolent disease courses and long-term survival. Predicting who is destined to progress rapidly and for whom transplant or active therapy is thereby justified remains a major clinical challenge.

Numerous prognostic scoring systems have been developed for CMML, incorporating overlapping but distinct sets of clinicopathologic, laboratory, and other parameters.1 Direct comparison of 7 systems revealed similar performance, with no clear winner, but none providing optimal predictive performance.3 Notably, only 3 tools (CMML-specific Prognostic Scoring System–Molecular [CPSS-Mol]; Groupe Francais des Myelodysplasies [GFM] model; and Mayo Molecular Model [MMM]) incorporate somatic mutations,4, 5, 6 with 2 including ASXL1 alone.4,5 Recently, the International CMML Prognostic Scoring System (iCPSS) extended molecular contribution to 9 genes based on comprehensive machine learning on a large retrospective cohort (n = 3013) and was validated prospectively (n = 516).7

Among the new genes of prognostic relevance in the iCPSS is TET2, which encode an epigenetic modifier and is the most common recurrent mutation in CMML (present in ≥60% of patients).1 Previous studies had suggested a putative protective effect on prognosis for patients with TET2-mutated (TET2-mut) status compared with those with TET2 wild-type (TET2-WT) status.8, 9, 10, 11, 12, 13, 14 Accordingly, the iCPSS confers favorable risk weighted to TET2-mut status in its prognostic model. However, to date, most studies have considered binary TET2 mutation status only (some in compound conjunction with ASXL1 status), generally ignoring mutation number and allelic status. A recent exception, a study by Csizmar et al,14 tested TET2 variant allele frequency (VAF) as a linear continuous variable in Cox regression and reported no association with prognosis, motivating the nonlinear approach presented here.

Complete loss of TET2 activity leads to a more pronounced phenotype than that observed in patients with one intact allele, a gene dosage effect that reflects graded loss of TET2 dioxygenase activity.15 This effect was recently confirmed at single-cell resolution.16 This observation is supported by Coltro et al,9 who reported that TET2 mutations were associated with a survival advantage in CMML, which was most pronounced in cases with multiple mutations and truncating variants located upstream of the catalytic domain (ie, early truncations that ablate both the N-terminal and catalytic regions). Additionally, biallelic TET2 mutations are associated with poor prognosis in AML17; the clonal hierarchy of TET2 mutations is shaped by founder acquisition and subsequent biallelic events18; and a TET2 VAF ≥32% predicts adverse outcomes in myelodysplastic syndromes (MDS).19

Despite these observations, the prognostic role of TET2 VAF, including biallelic mutations and loss of heterozygosity (LOH), remains unexplored in CMML, in which TET2 is the most frequently mutated gene. TET2 mutation burden (beyond status alone) therefore likely harbors prognostic nuance, hitherto unstudied. Here, we investigated the prognostic impact of TET2 mutations, incorporating the percentage VAF as a measure of mutation dosage (including as a surrogate marker of allelic status) in 1206 patients with CMML from 7 institutions (across 6 countries), to evaluate its potential to refine and enhance existing prognostic systems.

Methods

Study cohorts

Our UK discovery cohort (DC) comprised 645 consecutive patients with CMML diagnosed and treated across North West England between 2009 and 2023 and for whom extensive clinical, molecular, and outcome data were available. A full karyotype was available for 598 patients (93%). Separately, we pooled 291 patients with CMML diagnosed and treated at the National Taiwan University Hospital (Taipei, Taiwan),20 Vanderbilt University Medical Center (Nashville, TN), Cleveland Clinic (Cleveland, OH),21 Hospital Morales Meseguer (Murcia, Spain),22 and Vilnius University Hospital Santaros Klinikos (Vilnius, Lithuania), together forming validation cohort 1 (VC1). Next-generation sequencing (NGS) was performed on diagnostic bone marrow (BM) samples. An additional 270 patients with CMML from Gustave Roussy Cancer Center (Villejuif, France) were recruited as VC2. NGS data for VC2 were derived from CD14+ monocytes, sorted from mononuclear cells (MNCs) using CD14 microbeads, as described previously.23 All diagnoses conformed to 2022 World Health Organization classification criteria.2 This study was approved by the institutional review boards of each participating hospital and conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki.

Targeted mutation sequencing

In the UK DC, targeted gene panels and sequencing platforms varied across institutions, with 27 core shared myeloid neoplasm–relevant genes (supplemental Table 1). NGS was performed as previously described.24 Similarly, in VC1 panels and NGS, platforms varied across institutions,21,22,25 with 27 common mutations profiled across centers (supplemental Table 1). The NGS panel for VC2 is presented in supplemental Table 1.23 Variants were annotated using international databases of normal alleles and pathologic mutations, with variants of uncertain significance excluded from analysis. For multiple TET2 mutations, hits were ordered by VAF values. Recognizing that TET2 mutations can manifest in various clonal states, we focused on the mutation with highest VAF for outcome analyses. Because of limited data on copy number alterations and confirmed LOH, these were not directly considered (although LOH was inferred by high VAF percentage, as described in “Results”).

Statistical analyses

Categorical variables were compared using the χ2 or Fisher exact tests, and continuous variables were compared using the Mann-Whitney U or Kruskal-Wallis tests. Pearson correlation was used to evaluate associations between TET2 VAF and continuous clinical variables. Survival outcomes (overall survival [OS]; AML-free survival [LFS]) were analyzed by the Kaplan-Meier (KM) method with log-rank comparison. Univariate and multivariable analyses used Cox proportional hazards models. Distribution of TET2 VAF was characterized by segmented regression to detect inflection points, applied independently in DC, VC1, and VC2. To assess the prognostic relationship of TET2 VAF as a continuous variable, we fitted restricted cubic spline (RCS) Cox regression models for both LFS and OS. Hypomethylating agent (HMA) treatment response was assessed in the DC using the International Working Group consensus response criteria for myelodysplastic/myeloproliferative neoplasms.26

Development of the TET2-VAF–adjusted scoring systems

A per-integer VAF adjustment value was generated for each scoring system (CPSS-Mol, GFM, and MMM) by averaging the log hazard ratios (HR) from the RCS Cox models for OS and LFS at each integer VAF value (1%-100%) relative to the reference and then dividing by the corresponding system-specific univariable Cox coefficient. For patients with TET2-WT status, a single fixed adjustment was derived from a binary Cox model comparing TET2-WT to mutated status within the reference zone, again averaged for OS and LFS and normalized by the system-specific coefficient. The TET2-VAF–adjusted score for each patient was calculated as follows: original score + (VAF-derived or WT adjustment). Risk category cut points for the TET2-VAF–adjusted scores were rederived using a likelihood ratio–optimized (LR-opt) approach, scanning candidate cut points to maximize the joint OS and LFS log-rank χ2 statistic; the number of risk categories was preserved from each parental system (CPSS-Mol, 4; GFM, 3; MMM, 4).

Performance evaluation

Discrimination was assessed using the Harrell C-index and time-dependent area under the curve (AUC; paired DeLong). Reclassification was assessed using integrated discrimination improvement (IDI) and net reclassification improvement (NRI), both with bootstrapped 95% confidence intervals (CIs; 1000 resamples). Group-level discrimination was further compared between the original and TET2-VAF–adjusted classifications using the log-rank χ2 statistic. Calibration was assessed visually using calibration plots. A P value <.05 was deemed significant. Analyses were performed using R version 4.4.0 and Prism 10.

Use of primary human tissue was in compliance with the ethical and legal framework of the UK Human Tissue Act 2004. Primary samples were obtained from the Manchester Cancer Research Centre’s Tissue Biobank (instituted with the approval of the South Manchester Research Ethics Committee [18/NW/0092] and licensed by the Human Tissue Authority [license number 30004]). Use of the samples was authorized following ethical review by the Tissue Biobank’s scientific subcommittee (approval 17_KIBA_01). The National Taiwan University Hospital (NTUH) Research Ethics Committee approved the study (number 201709072RINC).

Results

Patient characteristics and mutational landscapes

The median age of the 645 UK DC patients was 75 years (range, 24-98), with a male predominance (63%). According to World Health Organization 2022 criteria, the cohort included 410 patients (63.6%) with dysplastic and 235 patients (36.4%) with proliferative CMML; 523 (81.1%) had CMML-1, and 122 (18.9%) had CMML-2. A total of 671 TET2 mutations were identified in 433 patients (Figure 1B), with 218 (50.3%) carrying multiple TET2 mutations: 200 (46.2%) with 2; 16 (3.7%) with 3; and 2 (0.5%) with 4. Frameshift was the dominant variant type (45.9%), followed by nonsense (32.9%), missense (18%), splice site (2.7%), and in-frame deletion (0.5%) (Figure 1B). Median VAFs of the first, second, third, and fourth TET2 hits were 45.9%, 33.9%, 7.7%, and 6.1%, respectively (Figure 1C). The histogram of dominant-hit TET2 VAFs revealed a bimodal distribution, with a primary peak at 40% to 50% and a secondary peak at 90% to 100%, consistent with cases of TET2 LOH (Figure 1D). Pearson correlation showed a very modest positive correlation between dominant-hit TET2 VAF and white blood cell count (R2 = 0.108; P < .001) but no significant correlation with age, hemoglobin, platelets, blasts, or total mutation number (supplemental Table 2). To address the distribution of TET2 VAF among patients with TET2 mutilations with co-occurring driver mutations, we compared dominant-hit TET2 VAF across mutation status for each of 21 frequently mutated genes (supplemental Table 3). Four genes showed nominal differences: CBL (higher VAF in mutated cases; P = .002), DNMT3A (lower; P = .018), NRAS (higher; P = .044), and SRSF2 (higher; P = .044), whereas only CBL remained significant after Bonferroni correction for 21 comparisons; the remaining signals should be considered exploratory. The other 17 comutations, including ASXL1 and RUNX1, showed no association.

Over a median follow-up of 21.1 months, 120 patients (19%) experienced AML transformation, and 391 patients (61%) died. The median LFS and OS were 27.8 months and 28.8 months, respectively. As expected, stratification of LFS and OS by the 3 established prognostic models was robust (supplemental Figure 1A-C). Patients harboring ≥2 TET2 mutations exhibited significantly better LFS and OS than those with a single mutation or TET2-WT (TET2 mutation number, ≥2 vs 1 vs 0; LFS, 38.1 vs 28.8 vs 17.9 months [P < .001]; OS, 39.3 vs 30.9 vs 21.8 months [P < .001]; supplemental Figure 2A), in line with prior observations.9 Among TET2-mut cases, those harboring any truncating mutation (n = 384) demonstrated a trend toward better LFS than those with nontruncating mutations (n = 48; median, 37 vs 24 months; P = .056),9 although no significant difference in OS was observed (median, 37 vs 24 months; P = .457). Overall, the DC reflects a typical CMML population with broadly representative clinical, genomic, and outcome features, albeit with slightly higher rates of TET2 mutation than in some other reports.4,6,9 Furthermore, CPSS-Mol low-risk cases were relatively less represented than in the original CPSS-Mol derivation study (14%-19%)6 and a recent Mayo Clinic/MD Anderson cohort (9.8%).14

TET2 VAF distribution and inflection points

We next sought to explore the prognostic dimension beyond mutation number, namely, the VAF of dominant-hit TET2 mutations. Examination of the distribution of dominant-hit TET2 VAFs across the 433 patients with TET2-mut status revealed two clear inflection points in the landscape plot (Figure 1E). Segmented regression confirmed inflections at 32% and 55% VAFs (supplemental Table 4), the latter consistent with biallelic TET2 inactivation by LOH21,27,28; supporting an LOH origin, the proportion of single-TET2 mutation cases sharply increased at VAF ≥55% (59/72 [82%]) compared with VAFs of 32% to 55% (122/302 [40%]) and <32% (34/59 [58%]; 3-group χ2 P < .0001), indicating that high VAF arises predominantly from copy-neutral LOH in single-hit cases rather than independent acquisition of multiple mutations (supplemental Table 5). In the small subset (n = 5) with paired karyotype/microarray data, 4 had VAF of ≥55%. We therefore use VAF of ≥55% as a working threshold for inferred biallelic TET2 inactivation throughout, while emphasizing that direct copy number confirmation through dedicated techniques remains the gold-standard approach.

Phenotypic correlates of TET2 VAF groups

We next examined CMML subtype distribution across the 4 inflection-defined TET2 VAF groups: TET2-WT (n = 212), TET2-low (<32% VAF; n = 59), TET2-Intermediate (Int) (32%-55%; n = 302), and TET2-high (≥55%; biallelic inferred; n = 72). Proliferative CMML was substantially less frequent among patients with TET2 mutations (31.4%) than among patients with TET2-WT status (46.7%; χ2 P = .0002), with the lowest proportion in TET2-low (20.3%) and similar levels in TET2-Int (33.1%) and TET2-high (33.3%); the global 4-group difference was significant (χ2 P = .0005). Among patients with TET2-mut status, ASXL1 co-mutation tracked with a proliferative phenotype: 27.1% of patients with TET2-mut/ASXL1-WT status vs 40.4% of those with TET2-mut/ASXL1-mut status (P = .005). The proportion of patients with a proliferative phenotype did not differ between those with single vs multiple TET2 mutations (33.0% vs 29.8%; P = .48) or between those with biallelic-inferred (≥55%) and monoallelic status (<55%; 33.3% vs 31.0%; P = .70). Detailed crosstabulations are summarized in supplemental Table 6.

TET2 VAF is a nonlinear continuous prognostic variable

Having established phenotypic correlates of the inflection-defined groups, we next evaluated TET2 VAF as a continuous prognostic variable. Treated as a continuous variable, Cox regression revealed that higher TET2 VAF correlated with inferior LFS (HR, 1.009; 95% CI, 1.003-1.016; P = .003) and OS (HR, 1.008; 95% CI, 1.002-1.015; P = .012) among patients with TET2-mut status. This remained significant even after adjusting for number of TET2 mutations (LFS [HR, 1.008; 95% CI, 1.002-1.015; P = .012]; OS [HR, 1.007; 95% CI, 1.001-1.014; P = .029]). A formal interaction test further confirmed no significant interaction between TET2 VAF and TET2 mutation number (supplemental Table 7).

To systematically evaluate the prognostic significance of TET2 VAF without imposing arbitrary cutoffs, we fitted RCS Cox regression models, using the empirical first quartile of TET2 VAF in patients with mutations (39%) as the reference (HR, 1.0). Hazards for both LFS and OS varied nonlinearly with VAF, rising as VAF increased toward the biallelic LOH threshold and plateauing at higher VAFs (Figure 1F). Patients with TET2-WT status had hazards well above the reference (HR, ∼1.86 [LFS] and ∼1.70 [OS]; Figure 1F dashed line). Notably, patients with TET2-mut status carrying 1 or ≥2 mutations displayed near-identical VAF-hazard curves for both LFS and OS (supplemental Figure 2B), indicating that the prognostic effect of TET2 VAF operates similarly regardless of mutation number. Together, these observations establish TET2 VAF as a robust continuous prognostic variable and motivated a dose-aware adjustment to the existing risk scores.

To address potential confounding by co-occurring mutations, we performed multivariable Cox proportional hazards regression in patients with TET2-mut status, adjusted for putative mutations including ASXL1, SRSF2, RUNX1, and age at diagnosis (supplemental Table 8). Continuous TET2 VAF (per 1% increase) remained independently associated with both inferior LFS (HR, 1.008; 95% CI, 1.002-1.014; P = .012) and OS (HR, 1.007; 95% CI, 1.001-1.014; P = .024). Among the comutations, only ASXL1 retained an independent adverse effect (LFS [HR, 1.30; 95% CI, 1.01-1.68; P = .044]; OS [HR, 1.34; 95% CI, 1.04-1.74; P = .025]); SRSF2 and RUNX1 were not significant in this multivariable model.

Development of TET2-VAF–adjusted scoring systems

Capitalizing on the nonlinear continuous VAF-survival relationship, we generated VAF-adjustment lookups (Figure 2A) translating any TET2 VAF and TET2-WT status into a per-system numeric adjustment for CPSS-Mol, GFM, and MMM (Figure 2B; supplemental Table 9). Across the 1% to 100% VAF range, adjustment values were minimal at low VAFs (<25%), became positive between ∼40% and ∼70%, and then gently declined; patients with TET2-WT status received the largest fixed adjustment (CPSS-Mol, +1.78; GFM, +2.68; MMM, +2.96) (supplemental Table 9). The TET2-VAF–adjusted scores yielded higher time-dependent AUCs and Harrell C-indices than the parental scores for both LFS and OS (Figure 2C; supplemental Table 17). TET2 VAF and HMA response

Finally, to explore whether TET2 VAF was associated with treatment response, we examined HMA response in the DC in the subset of patients for whom uniform response data were available. Of 37 first-line HMA-treated patients, 32 were evaluable according to International Working Group (IWG) MDS/MPN consensus criteria. Overall response rates were similar between patients with TET2-mut and those with TET2-WT status (16/24 [67%] vs 5/8 [63%]; supplemental Table 18) and, among patients with TET2-mut status, did not differ between patients with monoallelic status (VAF <55%) and those with inferred biallelic (VAF ≥55%) status (overall response rate, 65% vs 71%; complete remission, 35% vs 43%). Consistent with this, TET2 VAF was near identical in responders and nonresponders (median, 49% vs 50%; P = .78). Thus, although TET2 VAF is prognostically informative, we found no evidence that it is predictive of response to HMA therapy.

Discussion

In this multi-institutional study, we demonstrated TET2 VAF as a robust continuous, nonlinear prognostic variable. Unbiased segmented regression of dominant-hit TET2 VAFs reproducibly identified 2 inflection points at 32% and 55% across the DC and both validation cohorts (VC1 and VC2), whereas RCS Cox modeling formalized the resulting nonlinear relationship between TET2 VAF and survival. Building on this, we generated TET2-VAF–adjusted CPSS-Mol, GFM, and MMM scores by applying a per-integer VAF adjustment to each patient’s baseline score. These adjusted scores improved discrimination, reclassification, and group-level stratification across all cohorts, scoring systems, and end points (OS and LFS), without the need for refitting in the validation data sets.

The favorable prognostic impact of TET2 mutations in CMML has been consistently reported,8, 9, 10, 11, 12, 13, 14 with subsequent studies refining the contribution of mutation number, truncation status, and ASXL1/TET2 genotype combinations.9,12,14,29,30 However, most prior CMML-focused analyses have treated TET2 status as binary or approximated mutation burden using mutation count or arbitrary VAF thresholds. To our knowledge, this study represents the first large-scale quantitative evaluation of TET2 VAF as a continuous, prognostic variable in CMML, formally modeling its nonlinear relationship with survival and translating this into a clinically applicable, per-integer VAF adjustment framework. This extends recent observations by Csizmar et al,14 who reported no benefit from linear incorporation of TET2 VAF, by demonstrating that the underlying relationship is nonlinear and, when appropriately modeled, conveys clinically meaningful and additive prognostic information.

The nonlinear VAF-hazard relationship has a plausible biological interpretation. Hazards were favorable in the heterozygous lower-VAF range (<32%), rose progressively as VAF approached the biallelic LOH threshold (32%-55%), and then plateaued at ≥55%. The plateau at ≥55%, accompanied by a marked enrichment of single-hit cases (82% vs ∼40–58% at lower VAFs; P < .0001), is consistent with copy-neutral LOH as the predominant mechanism generating very high VAFs, aligning with prior chromosomal-microarray and single nucleotide polymorphism (SNP)-array evidence.18,21,27,28 This stepwise biology (subdominant heterozygote → dominant heterozygote → biallelic LOH), paralleling the graded loss of TET2 dioxygenase activity described in MDS,15 provides a mechanistic frame for the 3-zone hazard structure and reinforces the case for modeling TET2 VAF as a continuous variable, rather than collapsing it into a single binary status. The earlier-reported counterintuitive “favorable dosage” effect of multihit TET29 may, in this view, reflect that the multihit subset is enriched for the lower-VAF (heterozygous) range rather than the high-VAF (LOH) range. Consistent with this, Yousuf et al30 recently reported that exactly 2 TET2 mutations, most likely representing a stable, functionally biallelic TET2-deficient state, conferred the longest survival in CMML, whereas single mutations and ≥3 mutations were associated with less benefit, the latter potentially reflecting clonal complexity rather than further loss of TET2 function.

Our findings have direct clinical implications. TET2-mut patients with CMML had consistently more favorable expected outcomes than patients with TET2-WT status, and the TET2-VAF–adjusted CPSS-Mol, GFM, and MMM scores allow more refined risk stratification by integrating the continuous TET2 VAF signal alongside conventional clinical and molecular variables. Patients reclassified into a higher risk tier by the adjusted score may warrant closer monitoring, earlier therapeutic intervention, and timely consideration of allogeneic transplantation, whereas those moving to a lower tier may be appropriate for less intensive management. Notably, the per-integer VAF adjustment lookup derived in the DC generalized to 2 independent validation cohorts, including one based on CD14+ monocyte sequencing, without refitting, supporting prospective application of the framework across diverse sequencing platforms and sample sources, provided quantitative TET2 VAF is available. To facilitate clinical use, we provide the TET2-VAF–adjusted scoring system as a freely accessible web-based calculator (https://tet2-vaf-adjusted-cmml-risk.netlify.app/), allowing clinicians to compute the adjusted CPSS-Mol, GFM, or MMM score and corresponding risk category from a patient's parental score and dominant-hit TET2 VAF. In exploratory analyses, TET2 VAF was not associated with response to HMA therapy, with comparable response rates observed across TET2 mutation and VAF strata (supplemental Table 18). Interpretation of these findings is limited by the modest number of HMA-treated cases in our cohort, reflecting the relatively restricted availability of HMA for CMML in the United Kingdom. Larger studies will be required to determine whether TET2 VAF has predictive value for HMA response and could inform treatment decision-making.

Strengths of this study include its scale (n = 1206), geographic and methodological diversity (United Kingdom, France, United States, Spain, Lithuania, and Taiwan; BM MNCs and sorted CD14+ monocytes; multiple targeted NGS platforms), and a fully data-driven analytical approach that avoided arbitrary VAF thresholds. The consistency of segmented-regression inflection points (32%/55% in DC, 33%/54% in VC1, and 36%/56% in VC2) across these heterogeneous cohorts further supports the robustness of the proposed framework.

Several limitations should be acknowledged. First, the DC was largely derived from a tertiary referral setting, with a relatively high TET2 mutation frequency (67%) and underrepresentation of CPSS-Mol low-risk cases compared with population-based series; validation in unselected cohorts will be important. Second, although the ≥55% threshold for inferred biallelic LOH is supported by both our data and prior studies,21,27,28 direct copy-number assessment would allow for more precise classification. Third, follow-up in VC2 was shorter than in DC and VC1, which may have reduced statistical power for reclassification analyses and contributed to the attenuated NRI observed. Finally, the study is retrospective; prospective validation and incorporation into stratified clinical trials will be required to confirm clinical utility.

In summary, we provide, to our knowledge, the first large-scale quantitative characterization of TET2 VAF as a continuous, nonlinear prognostic variable in CMML. A continuous per-integer VAF adjustment applied to established CPSS-Mol, GFM, and MMM models consistently improves prognostic performance across independent cohorts. This TET2-VAF–adjusted framework offers a practical refinement of current CMML risk stratification and provides a basis for prospective evaluation of TET2 VAF as a quantitative biomarker in clinical decision-making.

Artificial intelligence pioneers the double-strangeness factory
Yan He, Takehiko R. Saito, Hiroyuki Ekawa, Ayumi Kasagi, Yiming Gao, Enqiang Liu, Kazuma Nakazawa, Christophe Rappold, Masato Taki, Yoshiki K. Tanaka, He Wang, Ayari Yanai, Junya Yoshida, Hongfei Zhang

Abstract
Artificial intelligence (AI) is transforming not only our daily experiences but also the technological development landscape and scientific research. In this study, we pioneered the application of AI in double-strangeness hypernuclear studies. These studies—which investigate quantum systems with strangeness via hyperon interactions—provide insights into fundamental baryon-baryon interactions and contribute to our understanding of the nuclear force and composition of neutron star cores. Specifically, we report the observation of a double- Λ \Lambda hypernucleus in nuclear emulsion achieved via innovative integration of machine learning techniques. The proposed methodology leverages generative AI and Monte Carlo simulations to produce training datasets combined with object detection AI for effective event identification. Based on the kinematic analysis and charge identification, the observed event was uniquely identified as the production and decay of B Λ ​ Λ 13 \prescript{13}{\Lambda\Lambda}{\rm{B}} , resulting from Ξ − \Xi^{-} capture by 14 N in the nuclear emulsion. Assuming Ξ − \Xi^{-} capture in the atomic 3D state, the binding energy of the two Λ \Lambda hyperons in B Λ ​ Λ 13 \prescript{13}{\Lambda\Lambda}{\rm{B}} , B Λ ​ Λ B_{\Lambda\Lambda} , was determined as 25.57 ± 1.18 ( stat . ) ± 0.07 ( syst . ) 25.57\pm 1.18(\rm{stat.})\pm 0.07(\rm{syst.}) MeV. The Λ ​ Λ \Lambda\Lambda interaction energy Δ ​ B Λ ​ Λ \Delta B_{\Lambda\Lambda} obtained was 2.83 ± 1.18 ( stat . ) ± 0.14 ( syst . ) 2.83\pm 1.18(\rm{stat.})\pm 0.14(\rm{syst.}) MeV. This study marks a new era in double-strangeness research.

Introduction

The rapid advancements in artificial intelligence (AI) in recent years have transformed our daily lives. The integration of AI with established technologies is particularly notable
Wang et al. 2023
. In many cases, such fusion has revitalized long-standing methods and transformed them into cutting-edge tools. This synergy not only modernizes traditional technologies but also opens up entirely new avenues of exploration.

Such progress in AI is now beginning to contribute significantly to addressing some of humanity’s most enduring questions: What is the nature of matter? How did the Universe begin? In the fields of quarks and nuclear physics, which have made substantial contributions to our understanding of both matter and the cosmos, the application of AI has yielded novel and promising insights. We sought to harness AI in combination with nuclear emulsion technology, a well-established and mature technique, to advance our understanding of quantum many-body systems involving two strange quarks with a strangeness degree of freedom, called the double-strangeness (
S=-2
) sector.
This combination allows for detecting diverse double-strangeness hypernuclei that have long eluded observation and lays the foundation for constructing a “double-strangeness factory”.

Double-strangeness quantum many-body systems are fundamental to nuclear, particle, and astrophysics, particularly in understanding neutron stars and ultra-dense supernova remnants with radii of
\sim
10 km but masses approximately twice that of the Sun
Demorest et al. 2010
. Despite advances in gravitational-wave detections and r-process studies
Annala et al. 2018
;
Drout et al. 2017
, their internal structure remains elusive. At such densities, matter likely shifts from nucleons to strange-quark-containing hyperons, making hyperon-nucleon (YN) and hyperon-hyperon (YY) interactions crucial for the equation of state (EOS). However, limited experimental data, particularly for YY interactions, render the EOS too soft
Lonardoni et al. 2015
, which conflicts with observations of massive neutron stars. This mismatch, known as the “hyperon puzzle”, remains unresolved. Addressing this issue requires precise data on YY interactions, which are scarce.

Quantum chromodynamics (QCD) predicts an attractive short-range baryon-baryon interaction in the double-strangenes sector, in contrast to the repulsive core of the nucleon-nucleon interaction
Inoue et al. 2011
;
Inoue et al. 2010
. A prominent double-strangeness system, the H-dibaryon (a six-quark state: uuddss), remains the subject of intense research
Jaffe 1977
. Despite extensive experimental research
Carroll et al. 1978
;
Ahn 2013
;
Han et al.
;
Sakai et al. 2000
, definitive observations are lacking.
Theoretical studies have suggested that H-dibaryon may exist within double-strangeness hypernuclei
May 1996
;
Sakai et al. 1995
;
Yamada 2001
and neutron star cores
Tamagaki 1991
.
Although direct observation of nuclear matter is lacking, the measured masses of double-
\Lambda
hypernuclei provide crucial constraints on the mass of H-dibaryon
Zhu et al. 1991
;
Aoki et al. 1991
;
Takahashi et al. 2001
;
Ahn et al. 2013
.
Investigating the dependence of the
\Lambda\Lambda
interaction on the mass number of double-
\Lambda
hypernuclei can provide insights into the existence of H-dibaryon
Imai et al.
.

Double-strangeness hypernuclei, containing two strange quarks, which occupy the
S=-2
sector and act as “small experimental laboratories” for probing hyperon interactions in nuclei, are a key focus in the context of
S=-2
systems.
In particular, a double-
\Lambda
hypernucleus with two
\Lambda
hyperons in the nucleus provides key insights into the
\Lambda\Lambda
interaction
Hashimoto and Tamura 2006
;
Gal et al. 2016
.
The
\Lambda\Lambda
interaction, confirmed to be weakly attractive through observations of double-
\Lambda
hypernuclei, is the only known YY interaction experimentally.
In addition, the binding of the three-body
\Lambda\Lambda
N system, particularly the
\Lambda\Lambda
d configuration, remains unresolved, although several theoretical models predict a bound particle-stable
\prescript{4\,}{\Lambda\Lambda}{\rm{H}}
if the
\Lambda\Lambda
interaction is sufficiently attractive
Nemura et al. 2005
;
Gal 2010
.
However, experimental confirmation is lacking because the initial claim from the BNL-AGS E906 experiment
Ahn et al. 2001
was later downgraded
Randeniya and Hungerford 2007
.

Despite over 70 years of hypernuclear research since it was first observed in 1953
Danysz and Pniewski 1953
, only 47 double-strangeness hypernucleus candidates have been observed using nuclear emulsion.
Notably, the NAGARA event
Takahashi et al. 2001
;
Ahn et al. 2013
remains the only confirmed observation of
\prescript{6\,}{\Lambda\Lambda}{\rm{He}}
, the lightest double-
\Lambda
hypernucleus. Its fully occupied s-shell reveals a weak (
<1
MeV), attractive s-wave
\Lambda\Lambda
interaction, which serves as a critical benchmark for YY interaction calculations and interpretation of less definitive events.
However, to date, the NAGARA event remains the only uniquely identified double-
\Lambda
hypernucleus, with other double-
\Lambda
hypernuclei exhibiting ambiguous identifications. Therefore, information on the
\Lambda\Lambda
interaction in the nuclear medium is not yet precisely known, and substantially more experimental data are required.
The short hyperon lifetime (
\sim
10
-10
s) hinders scattering experiments, particularly for multi-hyperon systems.
Nuclear emulsion experiments with their sub-micrometre resolution
Barkas et al. 1963
offer the unique advantage of visualizing decay chains, enabling nuclide identification through event-by-event analysis of double-
\Lambda
hypernuclear production and decays.

The J-PARC E07 experiment
Imai et al.
, a recent hybrid-emulsion study at the Japan Proton Accelerator Research Complex (J-PARC), aims to detect approximately
10^{2}
double-
\Lambda
hypernuclei events to broaden our understanding of strangeness physics.
In E07,
\Xi^{-}
hyperons produced by the
(K^{-},K^{+})
reaction are captured by the nuclei in the emulsion stacks, and their positions and angles are tracked with silicon strip detectors.
However, the detection efficiency for all double-strangeness hypernuclear events in emulsion sheets has been estimated to be approximately 10%
Yoshida and J-PARC E07 Collaboration 2022
;
Nakazawa 2013
.
Although 33 candidate events triggered by
\Xi^{-}
hyperons were detected, only three events could be identified: Mino
Ekawa et al. 2019
, Ibuki
Hayakawa et al. 2021
, and Irrawaddy
Yoshimoto et al. 2021
.
Unfortunately, no double-
\Lambda
hypernucleus was uniquely identified.
Nervertheless, the entire emulsion volume is estimated to contain over a thousand double-strangeness events, including untriggered events generated by the ‘n’
(K^{-},K^{0})\Xi^{-}
reaction, which cannot be detected using the hybrid method.
Therefore, it is essential to develop a new and efficient detection method for mining these events.

In this study, we developed an efficient analysis pipeline based on machine learning techniques for detecting double-
\Lambda
hypernuclear events within nuclear emulsion from the J-PARC E07 experiment
He et al. 2025

Here, we report the first uniquely identified observation of a double-
\Lambda
hypernucleus
\prescript{13\,}{\Lambda\Lambda}{\rm{B}}

Takahashi et al. 2001
. This success paves the way for further discoveries of double-strangeness hypernuclei, ultimately establishing a “Double-Strangeness Factory”.

3
Discussion and prospect

Using advanced machine learning, we analyzed 0.2% of the E07 emulsion data and achieved the first unambiguous identification of a double-
\Lambda
hypernucleus
\prescript{13\,}{\Lambda\Lambda}{\rm{B}}
. Extrapolating from the observed candidate rate, the entire dataset is expected to contain more than 2000 double-strangeness hypernuclear events, with hundreds still awaiting identification. This work marks the dawn of a double-strangeness factory, where AI-driven analysis unlocks unprecedented access to rare double-strangeness hypernuclei.

The
\Lambda\Lambda
binding energy in
\prescript{13\,}{\Lambda\Lambda}{\rm{B}}
was determined as
B_{\Lambda\Lambda}=25.57\pm 1.18(\rm{stat.})\pm 0.07(\rm{syst.})
MeV with an interaction energy of
\Delta B_{\Lambda\Lambda}=2.83\pm 1.18(\rm{stat.})\pm 0.14(\rm{syst.})
MeV.
This result confirms the existence of
\prescript{13\,}{\Lambda\Lambda}{\rm{B}}
and is the second unambiguous identification of a double-
\Lambda
hypernucleus since the first observation of hypernuclei in 1953
Danysz and Pniewski 1953
.

Previous experiments have suggested possible interpretations of
\prescript{13\,}{\Lambda\Lambda}{\rm{B}}
; however, without unique identification, definitive conclusions regarding its binding properties were prevented.
The E176 experiment
Nakazawa 2023
reported
B_{\Lambda\Lambda}=23.3\pm 0.7
MeV and
\Delta B_{\Lambda\Lambda}=0.6\pm 0.8
MeV, while the Demachiyanagi event from E373 yielded
B_{\Lambda\Lambda}=27.81^{+3.16}_{-2.02}
MeV and
\Delta B_{\Lambda\Lambda}=5.07^{+3.17}_{-2.03}
MeV
Ahn et al. 2013
.
Although the divergences of those
\Delta B_{\Lambda\Lambda}
values are within 3
\sigma
from the well-established
\Delta B_{\Lambda\Lambda}=0.67\pm 0.17
MeV for the uniquely identified
\prescript{6\,}{\Lambda\Lambda}{\rm{He}}

Ahn et al. 2013
, they suggests a possible nuclear species dependence of
\Delta B_{\Lambda\Lambda}
.
However, the ambiguity surrounding the nuclide and the mass number of the previously observed double-
\Lambda
hypernuclei precluded a firm conclusion.
This study provides the first unambiguous measurement of
B_{\Lambda\Lambda}
and
\Delta B_{\Lambda\Lambda}
for
\prescript{13\,}{\Lambda\Lambda}{\rm{B}}
, thereby eliminating nuclide identification uncertainties.
Notably, our
\Delta B_{\Lambda\Lambda}
value was larger than that of
\prescript{6\,}{\Lambda\Lambda}{\rm{He}}
, providing the first direct indication that the strength of the
\Lambda\Lambda
interaction depends on the nuclear medium.

An AI-driven double-strangeness factory will transform our understanding of baryonic interactions in multi-strangeness systems. Using this approach, a large-scale analysis of nuclear emulsion will reveal a vast population of double-strangeness hypernuclei, enabling high-precision studies of
\Lambda\Lambda
interactions, quantum three-body forces through
\Lambda\Lambda
-
\Xi N
coupling, and exotic multi-baryon states. These breakthroughs will provide deeper insights into the composition of neutron star cores and the possible existence of the H-dibaryon, ushering in a new era of double-strangeness hypernuclear physics.

Methods

In this study
He et al. 2025
, we employed GANs
Wang et al. 2018
and Geant4 Monte Carlo simulations
Allison et al. 2016
to generate training data for the Mask R-CNN
He et al. 2018
, which was used to detect double-
\Lambda
hypernuclear events in nuclear emulsion.
Mask R-CNN training requires images with objects of double-
\Lambda
hypernuclear event and their corresponding mask images.
However, for double-
\Lambda
hypernuclear events, there are insufficient data to train the model, as only one event has been uniquely identified to date.
To address this issue, Geant4 simulations were used to generate double-
\Lambda
hypernuclear events in nuclear emulsion, as illustrated in Figure
2
(a).
Combined with background tracks, these simulated events were processed using an image-style transformation via pix2pix
Wang et al. 2018
with GANs to produce training images.
Mask images were automatically generated from Geant4 track information.
The Mask-R CNN model was subsequently trained using the generated training datasets, including images containing double-
\Lambda
hypernuclear objects and mask images, as illustrated in Figure
2
(b).
The model evaluation detailed in Section
4.2
demonstrated the effective detection of double-
\Lambda
hypernuclear events in the produced images.
Notably, the model accurately detected and segmented the NAGARA event with a confidence score of 0.974, as shown in Figure
2
(d).
Subsequently, we applied the trained model to 0.2
\%
of the E07 emulsion data, which led to to the discovery and unique identification of a double-
\Lambda
hypernuclear event, as discussed in Section
2
.

Training data prepared with Geant4 Simulation and generative AI

In Geant4 Monte Carlo simulations, the composition of the
nuclear emulsion was replicated by referring to the emulsion layer used in the J-PARC E07 experiment.
For the double-
\Lambda
hypernuclear events generated, we first considered the case of
\prescript{6\,}{\Lambda\Lambda}{\rm{He}}
and its sequential decay.
As illustrated in Figure
2
(a),
\prescript{6\,}{\Lambda\Lambda}{\rm{He}}
is produced by the
\Xi^{-}
capture of
12
C in the nuclear emulsion at
vertex A. We assumed that
\Xi^{-}
is bound in the 3D atomic orbit of
12
C with a binding energy of 0.13 MeV
Yamaguchi et al. 2001
.
As shown in Figure
2
(b), the thickness of each track in the nuclear emulsion, which is related to the grain density, was calculated and displayed for different tracks based on their velocity and angle.
Because the tracks in the nuclear emulsion were recorded with three-dimensional information, the trajectories were converted into three different colors, as discussed in Ref.
He et al. 2025
.
To ensure an accurate classification and detection performance, negative samples,
K^{-}
beam interaction events, were generated using the JAM package
Nara et al. 1999

as main background events. Additional background tracks were extracted from the microscopic images of the E07 emulsion data using an image filter and binarization
He et al. 2025
.

After generating all the particle tracks in the emulsion images, image-style transfer using GANs was applied to generate emulsion images that closely mimicked real emulsion images. Based on the capabilities of GANs, the pix2pix model was employed to convert the RGB image depicted in Figure
2
(b) into an image similar to a real emulsion image. The pix2pix model is an implementations of the conditional GANs framework, which shows a significant improvement in performance for image-style transformation, especially for high-resolution images. The parameters used for training the pix2pix model in this study are aligned with those specified in our previous study
Kasagi et al. 2023
. The image produced by the trained pix2pix model in Figure
2
(b), combined with the corresponding mask images, served as training data for the object detection model, Mask R-CNN.

4.2
Model performance and event detection with AI

After training with the produced data, the developed model can detect double-
\Lambda
hypernuclear events in both the produced and actual nuclear emulsion images.
For the produced images, the method achieved detection efficiencies of 93.8
\%
and 82.0
\%
for
\prescript{6\ }{\Lambda\Lambda}{\rm{He}}
and
\prescript{5\ }{\Lambda\Lambda}{\rm{H}}

\%
and 98.3
\%
.
Furthermore, it successfully detected the NAGARA event with a confidence score of 0.974.
When applied to E07 emulsion images, the method drastically reduced the background images by a factor of 0.0017. It successfully detected six candidates of double-
\Lambda

\%
of the entire nuclear emulsion dataset from the E07 experiment.
The number of detected candidates suggests that more than 2000 double-strangeness hypernuclear events were recorded in the entire dataset.
The proposed method demonstrates great potential for use across the entire E07 nuclear emulsion dataset, potentially improving the visual inspection efficiency by approximately 500 times.
Details of the developed method are summarized in Ref.
He et al. 2025

4.3
Calibration of range-energy relationship

Before the kinematic analysis of the event, the range-energy relationship in the emulsion sheet was calibrated using
\alpha
tracks, which have a monochromatic energy of 8.784 MeV from the decay of
{}^{212}\rm{Po}
existing in the emulsion.
The
\alpha
track can be identified in the thorium series isotopes because it has the largest kinetic energy. Such
\alpha
decay chains were searched for around the observed events using the so-called overall scanning method
Yoshida et al. 2017
.
In total, 217
\alpha
tracks were scanned in the emulsion sheet for calibration.
The relationship between the ranges and the kinetic energies of the charged particles was obtained using the range-energy formula given by Barkas et al.
Barkas
;
Heckman et al. 1960
.
The equivalent density of the emulsion sheet was determined to be
3.622\pm 0.014
g/cm
3
for the emulsion layer in which the newly observed event was detected.
The ranges of particles needed to be corrected for the shrinkage effect because the emulsion layers shrunk perpendicular to the surface due to photographic development.
The range of 217
\alpha
tracks can also determine this shrinkage factor.
Finally, the mean range of
\alpha
tracks was obtained as
49.57\pm 0.12\ \rm{\mu m}
and the shrinkage factor was corrected to be
1.93\pm 0.01
.

The kinetic energy of the
\pi^{-}
particle was determined using a range-energy calibration based on
\mu^{+}
particles from
\pi^{+}
meson decays at rest, which provided a monochromatic energy of 4.1205 MeV. Although ATIMA
Weick et al. 2018
calculations indicated a difference in composition between E07 and standard emulsions, primarily affecting long-range particles such as
\pi^{-}
, the density was determined to be
3.379\pm 0.006\ \rm{g/cm^{3}}
using 160
\mu^{+}
tracks and ATIMA calculations
Kasagi et al. 2025
. This density value was then used to calculate the
\pi^{-}
particle’s kinetic energy.

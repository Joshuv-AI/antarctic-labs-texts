Global weak solutions with higher regularity to the compressible Navier-Stokes equations under Dirichlet boundary conditions
Xiangdi, Huang, Zhouping Xin, Wei YAN

Abstract
. In this manuscript, we aim to establish global existence of weak solutions with higher regularity to the compressible Navier-Stokes equations under no-slip boundary conditions. Though Lions [ 22 ] and Feireisl [ 6 ] have established global weak solutions with finite energy under Dirichelet boundary conditions by making use of so called effective viscous flux and oscillation defect measure,Hoff has investigated global weak solutions with higher regularity in [ 8 , 11 ] when the domain is either whole space or half space with Navier-slip boundary conditions, yet the existence theory of global weak solution with higher regularity under Dirichlet boundary conditions remains unknown. In this paper we prove that the system will admit at least one global weak solutions with higher regularity as long as the initial energy is suitably small when the domain is a 2D solid disc. This is achieved by exploiting the structure of the exact Green function of the disc to decompose the effective viscous flux into three parts, which corresponds to the pressure term, boundary term and the remaining term respectively. In order to control the boundary term, one of the key observations is to use the geometry of the domain which sucessfully to bound the integral of the effective viscous flux where L 1 \displaystyle L^{1} norm is always unbounded. Keywords : compressible Navier-Stokes equations, Dirichlet boundary conditions, weak solutions

\displaystyle\gamma>\frac{9}{5}

\displaystyle\gamma>\frac{3}{2}

Indeed, the regularity of weak solutions constructed by Hoff

Unfortunately, it still remains unkown that whether Lions-Feireisl’s weak solution is unique. The reason why we are interested in looking for weak solutions with higher regularity is related to an chanllenging problem, which may guarantee the uniqueness. \|\rho\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{\infty}(\Omega))}+\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))}\leq C.

However, the existence theory of global weak solution with higher regularity (

One can derive global estimates over the whole domains when the domain is whole space, periodic or some bounded one with Navier-slip boundary conditions. But unfortunately, this becomes a big issue for the important no-slip boundary conditions. Namely, the lack of boundary condition of the effective viscous flux will is the main obstacles preventing one obtaining upper bound of the density in this case. This is the main problem we will address here.

A general viscous isentropic compressible fluid occupying a domain
\displaystyle\Omega\subset R^{N}
is governed by the compressible Navier-Stokes equations as follows

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}\rho+\mathrm{div}(\rho u)=0,\\
&(\rho u)_{t}+\mathrm{div}(\rho u\otimes u)+\nabla p=\mu\Delta u+\lambda(\nabla\mathrm{div}u),\end{aligned}\right.

(1.2)

where
\displaystyle\rho\geq 0
,
\displaystyle u=(u^{1},u^{2},...u^{N})
, and
\displaystyle p=a\rho^{\gamma}(a>0,\gamma>1)
are the fluid density, velocity, and pressure, respectively. The viscosity coefficients
\displaystyle\mu
and
\displaystyle\lambda
are assumed to be constants satisfying

\mu>0,\quad\mu+\frac{N}{2}\lambda\geq 0.

(1.3)

In this paper,
\displaystyle\Omega
will be taken to be a two-dimensional unit ball in
\displaystyle R^{2}
.

We are looking for the solutions
\displaystyle(\rho(x,t),u(x,t))
to the initial-boundary value problem for the system (
1.2
) with the initial-boundary data

(\rho,u)|_{t=0}=(\rho_{0},u_{0}),\quad u\big|_{\partial\Omega}=0.

(1.4)

First we will list the notations and conventions used frequently in this paper.

The average of a function
\displaystyle\bar{f}
is defined as

\bar{f}\triangleq\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{\Omega}f(x)dx.

(1.5)

Thus the average of density and pressure
\displaystyle\bar{\rho}(t)
and
\displaystyle\bar{p}(t)
can be defined as follows

\overline{\rho}(t)\triangleq\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{\Omega}\rho(x,t)dx,\quad\overline{p}(t)\triangleq\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{\Omega}p(x,t)dx.

(1.6)

By the conservation of mass,
\displaystyle\overline{\rho}(t)=\overline{\rho}
is indeed a constant corresponding to the initial total mass.

The initial energy
\displaystyle E_{0}
is defined as

E_{0}\triangleq\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{2}\rho_{0}|u_{0}|^{2}+G(\rho_{0})dx.

(1.7)

where G denotes the potential energy density given by

G(\rho)\triangleq\rho\int_{\overline{\rho}}^{\rho}\frac{p(s)-p(\overline{\rho})}{s^{2}}ds=\frac{\rho^{\gamma}-\rho\overline{\rho}^{\gamma-1}}{\gamma-1}+(\overline{\rho}-\rho)\overline{\rho}^{\gamma-1}.

(1.8)

It is clear that

\left\{\begin{aligned} &G(\rho)=\frac{1}{\gamma-1}p,\quad\quad\quad\quad\text{for}\ \bar{\rho}=0,\\
&G(\rho)\geq C({\widehat{\rho}},\overline{\rho})|\rho-\overline{\rho}|^{2},\quad\text{for }0\leq\rho\leq{\widehat{\rho}},\\
&G(\rho)\geq C({\widehat{\rho}},\overline{\rho})|\rho-\overline{\rho}|^{\gamma},\quad\text{for }{\widehat{\rho}}\leq\rho<\infty.\end{aligned}\right.

(1.9)

Moreover, we use
\displaystyle C
as a uniform constant which may varies in different places and, we may specify
\displaystyle C
as
\displaystyle C_{1},\ C_{2}
through out this paper.

Multiplying the momentum equations
\displaystyle\eqref{ns-0}_{2}
by
\displaystyle u
and making use of the density equation
\displaystyle\eqref{ns-0}_{1}
, one can derive easily the following basic energy estimate of the system (
1.2
).

\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{1}{2}\rho|u|^{2}+G(\rho)\right)dx+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\left(\mu|\nabla u|^{2}+\lambda(\mathrm{div}u)^{2}\right)dxdt=E_{0}.

(1.10)

In this paper, we will show that the system will admit at least one global weak solutions with higher regularity as long as the initial energy is suitably small when the domain is a 2D solid ball. We will use the exact form of the Green function of the ball to decompose the effective viscous flux into three parts, which corresponds to the pressure term, boundary term and the remaining term respectively. In order to control the boundary term, one of the key observations is to use the geometry of the domain to bound the integral of the effective viscous flux whose
\displaystyle L^{1}
norm is always unbounded.

Before stating our main theorem, we first introduce the following norms and notations. For any function
\displaystyle f
and a non-negative integer
\displaystyle k\geq 0,p\geq 1
, the
\displaystyle L^{p}
and
\displaystyle W^{k,p}
norm of the function
\displaystyle f
is defined as follows

\|f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}=\Big(\int_{\Omega}f^{p}dx\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}},\|f\|_{W^{k,p}(\Omega)}=\sum_{i=0}^{k}\|D^{i}f\|_{L^{p}}.

(1.11)

Here

L^{p}=L^{p}(\Omega),\quad W^{k,p}=\{f\in L_{loc}^{1}(\Omega)|\|\nabla^{k}f\|_{L^{p}}<\infty\},H^{k}=W^{k,2}.

Morover, for any number
\displaystyle s\in(0,1)
and
\displaystyle 1\leq p<\infty
, the function space
\displaystyle W^{s,p}(\Omega)
is defined as

W^{s,p}(\Omega)=\Big\{f\in L^{p}(\Omega):\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}dxdy<\infty\Big\},

(1.12)

and

H^{s}(\Omega)=W^{s,2}(\Omega).

(1.13)

The norm of a function
\displaystyle f
in
\displaystyle W^{s,p}(\Omega)(s\in(0,1),p\in[1,\infty))
is defined by

\|f\|_{W^{s,p}(\Omega)}^{p}=\|f\|_{L^{p}}^{p}+\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}dxdy.

(1.14)

Assumptions:
Assume that
\displaystyle\gamma\in(1,2)
. Denote
\displaystyle{q_{0}}\triangleq\frac{12\gamma}{\gamma-1}
. For any given
\displaystyle\beta\in\big(\frac{1}{2},1\big]
,
\displaystyle\delta_{0}\triangleq\frac{2\beta-1}{3\beta}\in\big(0,\frac{1}{3}\big]
. Assume that
\displaystyle{\widehat{\rho}}\geq\overline{\rho}+1
, and

\|\rho_{0}\|_{{q_{0}}}\leq{\widehat{\rho}},\quad\|u_{0}\|_{\dot{H}^{\beta}}\leq M.

(1.15)

Now the main results of this paper can be stated as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\displaystyle\Omega
be a two-dimensional unit disc. Assume that the initial data
\displaystyle(\rho_{0},u_{0})
satisfy the following regularity

0\leq\rho_{0}\in L^{q_{0}}(\Omega),\ u_{0}\in H^{\beta}(\Omega).

(1.16)

Assume that
\displaystyle\frac{2\mu}{2\mu+\lambda}\leq\min\left\{\frac{1}{4\Lambda(\frac{\gamma+11}{\gamma-1})},\ \frac{1}{4\Lambda^{\frac{1}{4}}(4)}\right\}
,where
\displaystyle\Lambda(q)
is the constant defined in Lemma (
2.3
).

Then there exists
\displaystyle\varepsilon>0
, depending on
\displaystyle\mu,\lambda,\gamma,{q_{0}},\beta,\delta_{0},M,\overline{\rho},{\widehat{\rho}}
such that, if

E_{0}\leq\varepsilon.

(1.17)

the Dirichlet problem of the system (
1.2
) admits at least one global weak solution satisfying

\displaystyle 0\leq\rho\in L^{\infty}([0,\infty);L^{q_{0}})\cap C([0,\infty);L^{q}),\quad\mbox{for all $\displaystyle q\in[1,q_{0})$},

(1.18)

\displaystyle u\in C([0,\infty);H^{\beta}),\quad(\rho\dot{u},\ \sigma(t)^{\frac{1}{2}}\nabla\dot{u})\in L^{2}((0,T)\times\Omega).

where
\displaystyle\sigma(t)=\min(1,t)
.

Remark 1.1
.

The regularity of weak solution established here is between Lions-Feiresl’s finite energy solution and Hoff’s small energy solution.

Remark 1.2
.

In fact, Pereplista
[
24
]
first investigated the weak solution to the multi-dimensional compressible Navier-Stokes equations under Dirichlet boundary condition. When the density is away from vacuum and
\displaystyle C^{\alpha}
near boundary, he showed the global existence of weak solution. But his proof relies heavily on two key ingredients: one is the absence of vacuum, the other is the Holder continuity of the density in a neighborhood of the boundary, thus his method cannot be applied to our case. We need to carefully analysis the exact structure of the Green function of the domain, which is necessary to decompose the viscous effective flux into three parts. Based on this new observation, we can establish weighted estimate of the density.

Denoting that

A_{1}(T)\triangleq\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left(\sigma\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}\right)+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\sigma\rho|\dot{u}|^{2}dxdt

(1.19)

and

A_{2}(T)\triangleq\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\sigma^{3}\int_{\Omega}\rho|\dot{u}|^{2}dx+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\sigma^{3}|\nabla\dot{u}|^{2}dxdt

(1.20)

and

A_{3}(T)\triangleq\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\int_{\Omega}\rho|u|^{3}(x,t)dx.

(1.21)

The key idea in the proof of Theorem
1.1
is the following bootstrap argument.

Proposition 1.2
.

Under the condition of Theorem 1.1, there exists some positive constant
\displaystyle\epsilon
depending on
\displaystyle\mu,\lambda,\gamma,{q_{0}},\beta,\delta_{0},M,\overline{\rho},{\widehat{\rho}}
such that if
\displaystyle(\rho,u)
is a solution of (
1.2
) satifying

\sup\limits_{0\leq t\leq T}\|\rho\|_{{q_{0}}}\leq 2{\widehat{\rho}},\quad A_{1}(T)+A_{2}(T)\leq 2E_{0}^{\frac{1}{2\gamma}},\quad A_{3}(\sigma(T))\leq 2E_{0}^{\delta_{0}}.

(1.22)

Then the following estimates hold:

\sup\limits_{0\leq t\leq T}\|\rho\|_{{q_{0}}}\leq\frac{7}{4}{\widehat{\rho}}

(1.23)

and

A_{1}(T)+A_{2}(T)\leq E_{0}^{\frac{1}{2\gamma}},\quad A_{3}(\sigma(T))\leq E_{0}^{\delta_{0}}.

(1.24)

provided
\displaystyle E_{0}\leq\varepsilon
.

Preliminaries

In this section, we list some basic ellliptic estimates and elementary decompositions of the effective viscous flux and the corresponding estimates.

2.1.
Decomposition of the velocity

In the rest of the paper, we always using the notation
\displaystyle C({\widehat{\rho}})=C({\widehat{\rho}},\overline{\rho})
for simplicity.

Define
\displaystyle v
and
\displaystyle w
as follows:

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\mu\Delta v+\lambda\nabla\mathrm{div}v=\nabla p,\\
&v\big|_{\partial\Omega}=0,\end{aligned}\right.\qquad\left\{\begin{aligned} &\mu\Delta w+\lambda\nabla\mathrm{div}w=\rho\dot{u},\\
&w\big|_{\partial\Omega}=0,\end{aligned}\right.

(2.1)

Introduce the Lame operator
\displaystyle L
as
\displaystyle Lf=\mu\Delta f+\lambda\nabla\mathrm{div}f
. And denote
\displaystyle L^{-1}
the inverse operator of
\displaystyle L
with Dirichlet boundary condition
\displaystyle f(\partial\Omega)=0
.

Lemma 2.1
.

For any
\displaystyle 1<q<\infty
, it holds that

\|\nabla v\|_{q}\leq C(q)\|p-\overline{p}\|_{q},\quad\|\nabla^{2}w\|_{q}\leq C(q)\|\rho\dot{u}\|_{q},

(2.2)

and

\|\nabla u\|_{q}\leq C(q)\big(\|\rho^{\frac{1}{2}}\|_{q}\|\rho^{\frac{1}{2}}\dot{u}\|_{2}+\|p-\overline{p}\|_{q}\big).

(2.3)

Proof.

(
2.2
) follows immediately from the elliptic theory to the Lame operator. Moreover,

\displaystyle\|\nabla u\|_{q}

\displaystyle\leq\|\nabla w\|_{q}+\|\nabla v\|_{q}\leq C(q)\|\nabla^{2}w\|_{\frac{2q}{q+2}}+C(q)\|p-\overline{p}\|_{q}

\displaystyle\leq C(q)\|\rho\dot{u}\|_{\frac{2q}{q+2}}+C(q)\|p-\overline{p}\|_{q}

\displaystyle\leq C(q)\big(\|\rho^{\frac{1}{2}}\|_{q}\|\rho^{\frac{1}{2}}\dot{u}\|_{2}+\|p-\overline{p}\|_{q}\big)

\displaystyle\Box

2.2.
Decomposition of the effective viscous flux

(2.4)

In this section, we will study the decomposition of the effective viscous flux in a general Lame system, i.e

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\mu\Delta u+\lambda\nabla\mathrm{div}u=\nabla g+f,\\
&u=0,\quad\text{ on }\partial\Omega.\end{aligned}\right.

(2.5)

The effective viscous flux F to the above system (
2.5
) is defined as

F\triangleq(\mu+\lambda)\mathrm{div}u-g.

(2.6)

Then F satisfies

\Delta F=\mathrm{div}f.

(2.7)

Assume that
\displaystyle\bar{G}(x,y)\in C^{\infty}(\Omega\times\Omega\mbox{$\displaystyle\backslash$}D)
with
\displaystyle D=\{(x,y)\in\Omega\times\Omega|\ (x=y)\}
is Green function on
\displaystyle\Omega
. Then a general function
\displaystyle H
has the following explict expression

\displaystyle H(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\bar{G}(x,y)\Delta H(y)dy+\int_{\partial\Omega}\frac{\partial\bar{G}(x,y)}{\partial n}H(y)dy

(2.8)

\displaystyle\triangleq G(\Delta H)+G_{b}H.

where
\displaystyle G
and
\displaystyle G_{b}
are operators defined as

G(H)(x)=\bar{G}*H=\int_{\Omega}\bar{G}(x,y)H(y)dy,\quad G_{b}H(x)=\int_{\partial\Omega}\frac{\partial\bar{G}(x,y)}{\partial n}H(y)dy.

Assume that
\displaystyle u
is a solution of the Lame system (
2.5
). Then it holds that

u=G(\Delta u).

(2.9)

Then in view of (
2.7
)-(
2.8
)

F=G(\mathrm{div}f)+G_{b}(F),

(2.10)

and by definition (
2.6
)

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda}{\mu+\lambda}\nabla F

\displaystyle=\lambda\nabla\mathrm{div}u-\frac{\lambda}{\mu+\lambda}\nabla g=\nabla g+f-\mu\Delta u-\frac{\lambda}{\mu+\lambda}\nabla g

(2.11)

\displaystyle=-\mu\Delta u+\frac{\mu}{\mu+\lambda}\nabla g+f

The following equality also holds true

\Delta(xF)=2\nabla F+x\mathrm{div}f

(2.12)

Consequently,

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda}{2(\mu+\lambda)}\Delta(xF)

\displaystyle+\mu\Delta u=\frac{\lambda}{\mu+\lambda}\nabla F+\frac{\lambda}{2(\mu+\lambda)}x\mathrm{div}f+\mu\Delta u

\displaystyle=\frac{\lambda}{\mu+\lambda}\nabla F+\frac{\lambda}{2(\mu+\lambda)}x\mathrm{div}f+\mu\Delta u

\displaystyle=\frac{\lambda}{2(\mu+\lambda)}x\mathrm{div}f+\frac{\mu}{\mu+\lambda}\nabla g+f

\displaystyle\triangleq Q.

Therefore the effective viscous flux F can be expressed as

\frac{\lambda}{2(\mu+\lambda)}xF+\mu u=G(Q)+\frac{\lambda}{2(\mu+\lambda)}G_{b}(xF).

(2.13)

Taking
\displaystyle div
on both sides of (
2.13
) yields

\frac{\lambda}{2(\mu+\lambda)}(nF+x\cdot\nabla F)+\mu\mathrm{div}u=\mathrm{div}G(Q)+\frac{\lambda}{2(\mu+\lambda)}\mathrm{div}G_{b}(xF).

(2.14)

By (
2.10
)

\nabla F=\nabla G(\mathrm{div}f)+\nabla G_{b}(F)

(2.15)

and

\mu\mathrm{div}u=\frac{\mu}{\mu+\lambda}(F+g)

(2.16)

one gets

\displaystyle\frac{n\lambda}{2(\mu+\lambda)}F

\displaystyle+\frac{\lambda}{2(\mu+\lambda)}x\cdot\nabla G(\mathrm{div}f)+\frac{\lambda}{2(\mu+\lambda)}x\cdot\nabla G_{b}(F)+\frac{\mu}{\mu+\lambda}(F+g)

(2.17)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{div}G(Q)+\frac{\lambda}{2(\mu+\lambda)}\mathrm{div}G_{b}(xF).

Therefore

\displaystyle\frac{n\lambda+2\mu}{2(\mu+\lambda)}F

\displaystyle+\frac{\mu}{\mu+\lambda}g=\mathrm{div}G(Q)-\frac{\lambda}{2(\mu+\lambda)}x\cdot\nabla G(\mathrm{div}f)

(2.18)

\displaystyle+\frac{\lambda}{2(\mu+\lambda)}(\mathrm{div}G_{b}(xF)-x\cdot\nabla G_{b}(F)).

Finally, we have the following decomposition of the viscous effective flux F.

Lemma 2.2
.

Assume that
\displaystyle\Omega
is two-dimensional unit disc,
\displaystyle F
is the effective viscous flux corresponding to the Lame system (
2.5
). Then F has the following decomposition

\displaystyle F=\frac{2\mu}{2\mu+\lambda}A(g)+B(F)+R(f).

(2.19)

with

\left\{\begin{aligned} A(g)&=\mathrm{div}G(\nabla g)-g,\\
B(F)&=\frac{\lambda}{2\pi R(2\mu+\lambda)}\int_{\partial\Omega}F(y)dy,\\
R(f)&=\frac{2(\mu+\lambda)}{2\mu+\lambda}\mathrm{div}G(f)-\frac{\lambda}{2\mu+\lambda}\Big(\mathrm{div}G(x\mathrm{div}f)-x\cdot\nabla G(\mathrm{div}f)+G(\mathrm{div}f)\Big).\end{aligned}\right.

(2.20)

where A is related to the pressure term, B is linked to the boundary integral of the effective viscous flux, R is the remaining source term f. In the original equations (
1.2
), R is corresponding to the material derivatives
\displaystyle\rho\dot{u}
.

Remark 2.1
.

As it will be seen later, the most difficult part is how to treat the bound for A and B in the original Navier-Stokes equation. This is the key issue we will pay most attention to. The lack of boundary information of F is the crucial obstacle preventing one for deriving the upper bound of the density. But fortunately, by a delicate analysis of A and new observations on the boundary integral B due to the special geometry, we can obtain the
\displaystyle L^{p}
bound of the density for suitably large
\displaystyle p\in(1,\infty)
.

Proof.

Note that the Green function
\displaystyle G(x,y)
of the n-dimensional ball with radius R is given by

G(x,y)=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\frac{1}{(2-n)\omega_{n}}\big(|x-y|^{2-n}-\big|\frac{R}{|x|}x-\frac{|x|}{R}y\big|^{2-n}\big)\quad\mbox{for $\displaystyle n\geq 3$},\\
&\frac{1}{2\pi}\big(\log|x-y|-\log\big|\frac{R}{|x|}x-\frac{|x|}{R}y\big|\big)\quad\mbox{for $\displaystyle n=2$}\end{aligned}\right.

(2.21)

where
\displaystyle\omega_{n}
denotes the surface area of the unit sphere in
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

It is easy to verify that for any
\displaystyle n\geq 2

\frac{\partial G(x,y)}{\partial n}\Big|_{\partial\Omega}=\frac{R^{2}-|x|^{2}}{n\omega_{n}R|x-y|^{n}}.

and

\frac{\partial\nabla_{x}G(x,y)}{\partial n}\Big|_{\partial\Omega}=\frac{-2x}{n\omega_{n}R|x-y|^{n}}-\frac{R^{2}-|x|^{2}}{\omega_{n}R|x-y|^{n+1}}\frac{x-y}{|x-y|}

So

\displaystyle(y-x)\cdot\frac{\partial\nabla_{x}G(x,y)}{\partial n}\Big|_{\partial\Omega}

\displaystyle=\frac{-2x(y-x)}{n\omega_{n}R|x-y|^{n}}-\frac{R^{2}-|x|^{2}}{\omega_{n}R|x-y|^{n+1}}\frac{(x-y)(y-x)}{|x-y|}

\displaystyle=\frac{-2x(y-x)}{n\omega_{n}R|x-y|^{n}}+\frac{R^{2}-|x|^{2}}{\omega_{n}R|x-y|^{n}}

\displaystyle=\frac{-2xy+2|x|^{2}}{n\omega_{n}R|x-y|^{n}}+n\frac{R^{2}-|x|^{2}}{n\omega_{n}R|x-y|^{n}}

\displaystyle=\frac{R^{2}-|x|^{2}-2xy+2|x|^{2}}{n\omega_{n}R|x-y|^{n}}+(n-1)\frac{R^{2}-|x|^{2}}{n\omega_{n}R|x-y|^{n}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n\omega_{n}R|x-y|^{n-2}}+(n-1)\frac{\partial G(x,y)}{\partial n}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\mathrm{div}G_{b}(xF)

\displaystyle-x\cdot\nabla G_{b}(F)=\mathrm{div}\int_{\partial\Omega}\frac{\partial G(x,y)}{\partial n}yF(y)dy-x\cdot\nabla\int_{\partial\Omega}\frac{\partial G(x,y)}{\partial n}F(y)dy

(2.22)

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial\Omega}\frac{y\cdot\nabla_{x}\partial G(x,y)}{\partial n}F(y)dy-\int_{\partial\Omega}\frac{x\cdot\partial\nabla_{x}G(x,y)}{\partial n}F(y)dy

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial\Omega}\frac{(y-x)\cdot\partial\nabla_{x}G(x,y)}{\partial n}F(y)dy

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial\Omega}(\frac{1}{n\omega_{n}R|x-y|^{n-2}}+(n-1)\frac{\partial G(x,y)}{\partial n})F(y)dy

\displaystyle=(n-1)G_{b}(F)+\int_{\partial\Omega}\frac{F(y)}{n\omega_{n}R|x-y|^{n-2}}dy.

In view of (
2.18
), one has

\displaystyle\frac{n\lambda+2\mu}{2(\mu+\lambda)}F

\displaystyle+\frac{\lambda(n-1)}{2(\mu+\lambda)}G_{b}(F)+\frac{\lambda}{2(\mu+\lambda)}\int_{\partial\Omega}\frac{F(y)}{n\omega_{n}R|x-y|^{n-2}}dy.

\displaystyle\frac{2\mu+\lambda}{2(\mu+\lambda)}F

\displaystyle+\frac{\mu}{\mu+\lambda}g=\mathrm{div}G(Q)-\frac{\lambda}{2(\mu+\lambda)}x\cdot\nabla G(\mathrm{div}f)

(2.24)

\displaystyle-\frac{\lambda(n-1)}{2(\mu+\lambda)}G(\mathrm{div}f)+\frac{\lambda}{2(\mu+\lambda)}\int_{\partial\Omega}\frac{F(y)}{n\omega_{n}R|x-y|^{n-2}}dy.

\displaystyle\frac{2\mu+\lambda}{2(\mu+\lambda)}F

\displaystyle\frac{2\mu+\lambda}{2(\mu+\lambda)}F

\displaystyle\frac{2\mu+\lambda}{2(\mu+\lambda)}F

\displaystyle-\frac{\lambda}{2(\mu+\lambda)}\Big(\mathrm{div}G(x\mathrm{div}f)-x\cdot\nabla G(\mathrm{div}f)\Big)+\frac{\lambda}{4\pi R(\mu+\lambda)}\int_{\partial\Omega}F(y)dy

\displaystyle\triangleq A(g)+R(g)+B(g).

3.

Lemma 3.1

f(\eta)=-\frac{1}{4}\frac{{q_{0}}-1}{\mu+\lambda}c_{0}\eta^{\frac{{q_{0}}+\gamma}{{q_{0}}}}\leq-N_{1}=0\qquad\mbox{for all $\displaystyle\eta\geq\bar{\eta}=1$.}

(6.23)

Thus lemma
6.4
implies that

y(t)\leq\max\{\frac{5}{4}{\widehat{\rho}}^{{q_{0}}},1\}+N_{0}\leq\frac{5}{4}{\widehat{\rho}}^{{q_{0}}}+C({\widehat{\rho}},M)(A_{1}(\sigma(T)))^{\frac{3\delta_{0}}{4}}+CE_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq\frac{21}{16}{\widehat{\rho}}^{{q_{0}}}.

(6.24)

provided that C
\displaystyle E_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}+C({\widehat{\rho}},M)(A_{1}(\sigma(T)))^{\frac{3\delta_{0}}{4}}\leq\frac{1}{16}{\widehat{\rho}}^{{q_{0}}}
.

On the other hand, for
\displaystyle t\in[\sigma(T),T]
, one can get

\displaystyle|b(t_{2})-b(t_{1})|

\displaystyle\leq C({\widehat{\rho}})\int_{\sigma(T)}^{T}\|\rho^{\frac{1}{2}}\dot{u}\|_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\nabla\dot{u}\|_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}dt+C({\widehat{\rho}})\int_{\sigma(T)}^{T}\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}dt

(6.25)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{4}\frac{{q_{0}}-1}{\mu+\lambda}c_{0}(t_{2}-t_{1})+C({\widehat{\rho}})E_{0}+C({\widehat{\rho}})\int_{\sigma(T)}^{T}\|\rho^{\frac{1}{2}}\dot{u}\|_{2}^{2}\|\nabla\dot{u}\|_{2}^{2}dt

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{4}\frac{{q_{0}}-1}{\mu+\lambda}c_{0}(t_{2}-t_{1})+C({\widehat{\rho}})E_{0}+C({\widehat{\rho}})E_{0}^{\frac{1}{10\gamma}}\int_{\sigma(T)}^{T}\|\nabla{\dot{u}}\|_{2}^{2}dt

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{4}\frac{{q_{0}}-1}{\mu+\lambda}c_{0}(t_{2}-t_{1})+C({\widehat{\rho}})E_{0}^{\frac{1}{5\gamma}}

provided that
\displaystyle E_{0}\leq\varepsilon_{5}
. Therefore, we can choose
\displaystyle N_{1}
and
\displaystyle N_{0}
as follows:

N_{1}=\frac{1}{4}\frac{{q_{0}}-1}{\mu+\lambda}c_{0},N_{0}=C({\widehat{\rho}})E_{0}^{\frac{1}{5\gamma}}.

(6.26)

Note that

f(\eta)=-\frac{1}{4}\frac{{q_{0}}-1}{\mu+\lambda}c_{0}\eta^{\frac{{q_{0}}+\gamma}{{q_{0}}}}\leq-N_{1},\qquad\mbox{for all $\displaystyle\eta\geq 1$.}

(6.27)

Thus, lemma
6.4
yields that

y(t)\leq\max\{\frac{5}{4}{\widehat{\rho}}^{{q_{0}}},1\}+N_{0}\leq\frac{5}{4}{\widehat{\rho}}^{{q_{0}}}+C(\bar{\rho})E_{0}^{\frac{1}{5\gamma}}\leq\frac{21}{16}{\widehat{\rho}}^{{q_{0}}},

(6.28)

provided that

E_{0}\leq\varepsilon_{5}\leq\Big(\frac{{\widehat{\rho}}^{q_{0}}}{16C({\widehat{\rho}})}\Big)^{5\gamma}.

(6.29)

Thus lemma
6.3

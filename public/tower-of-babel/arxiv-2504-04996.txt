Laplacian eigenvalues for large negative Robin parameters on domains with outward peaks
Konstantin Pankrashkin, Firoj Sk, Marco Vogel

Abstract
. We study the asymptotic behavior of individual eigenvalues of the Laplacian in domains with outward peaks for large negative Robin parameters. A large class of cross-sections is allowed, and the resulting asymptotic expansions reflect both the sharpness of the peak and the geometric shape of its cross-section. The results are an extension of previous works dealing with peaks whose cross-sections are balls.

1.
Introduction

For
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
a bounded domain (non-empty connected open set) with suitably regular boundary,
we are interested in the following Robin eigenvalue problem:

(1)

\left\{\begin{aligned} -\Delta u&=\lambda u\text{ in }\Omega,\\
\partial_{\nu}u&=\alpha u\text{ on }\partial\Omega,\end{aligned}\right.

where
\alpha>0
is a parameter and
\partial_{\nu}
means the outer normal derivative. The problem (
1
) is understood in a weak sense. For a strict formulation we denote by
\mathcal{H}^{m}
the
m
-dimensional Hausdorff measure, and for the sake of readability we denote a function in
\Omega
and its Sobolev trace on
\partial\Omega
by the same symbol.
Under appropriate regularity assumptions on
\Omega
the symmetric bilinear form
r^{\Omega}_{\alpha}

defined on the domain
\mathcal{D}(r^{\Omega}_{\alpha})=W^{1,2}(\Omega)
by

r^{\Omega}_{\alpha}(u,v):=\int_{\Omega}\langle\nabla u,\nabla v\rangle_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d}-\alpha\int_{\partial\Omega}uv\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}

is closed and generates a self-adjoint operator

R^{\Omega}_{\alpha}
with compact resolvent in
L^{2}(\Omega)
. Informally, the operator
R^{\Omega}_{\alpha}
corresponds to the Laplacian
u\mapsto-\Delta u
acting on the functions
u
satisfying the boundary condition
\partial_{\nu}u=\alpha u
on
\partial\Omega
, and the eigenvalues
\lambda
in (
1
) are understood as the eigenvalues of
R^{\Omega}_{\alpha}
. In particular, a number
\lambda
is an eigenvalue of (
1
) with an eigenfunction
u
if and only if

r^{\Omega}_{\alpha}(u,v)=\lambda\int_{\Omega}uv\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d}\text{ for all }v\in W^{1,2}(\Omega).

Alternatively, one can consider
u\mapsto r^{\Omega}_{\alpha}(u,u)
as the energy functional for (
1
) and characterize the eigenvalues variationally using the min-max principle. Remark that the boundary term in the above expression for
r^{\Omega}_{\alpha}(u,u)
is negative for
\alpha>0
, so this case is usually termed as the case of
negative
Robin parameters.

In what follows, for a lower semibounded self-adjoint operator
A
we denote by
\lambda_{j}(A)
its
j
-th eigenvalue (if it exists), assuming that the eigenvalues are enumerated in the non-decreasing order by taking into account their multiplicities. The goal of the present paper is to study the asymptotic behavior of the individual eigenvalues of
R^{\Omega}_{\alpha}
, i.e. of
\lambda_{j}(R^{\Omega}_{\alpha})
with fixed
j
, for
\alpha\to+\infty
and a special class of domains
\Omega
. The dependence of
\lambda_{j}(R_{\alpha}^{\Omega})
on
\alpha
for various classes of
\Omega
has been given a considerable attention during the last decade, see the reviews in
[
8
,
21
]
. While the above problem is linear, it originally appeared
in the study of non-linear problems, see e.g.
[
12
]
for a stochastic interpretation,

[
16
]
for a discussion of an interplay between the Robin eigenvalues and critical temperature estimates in surface
superconductivity, and
[
23
]
for a link with the study of reaction-diffusion equations.

If
\Omega
is a bounded Lipschitz domain, then all required regularity assumptions are satisfied, and it is known that for large
\alpha

one has a two-sided bound

(2)

-c\alpha^{2}\leq\lambda_{1}(R_{\alpha}^{\Omega})\leq-\alpha^{2}

with some
c\geq 1
, see e.g.
[
8
, Prop. 4.12]
and
[
21
, Lem. 2.7]
,
and
the very recent paper
[
13
]
shows that no asymptotics of the form
\lambda_{1}(R_{\alpha}^{\Omega})\sim-c_{\Omega}\alpha^{2}
with
c_{\Omega}>0
is available in general. Under stronger regularity assumptions
one can construct detailed asymptotic expansions for
\lambda_{j}(R^{\Omega}_{\alpha})
with any fixed
j
involving various geometric properties of
\Omega
and
\partial\Omega
, see e.g.
[
4
,
7
,
11
,
17
,
20
,
24
,
27
]

and the reviews in
[
8
,
21
]
. On the other hand,
it was observed in
[
24
]
that (
2
) fails for domains
\Omega
with outward peaks, which was later studied in greater detail in
[
22
]
. As the subsequent text is specifically devoted to the study of
\lambda_{j}(R^{\Omega}_{\alpha})
for such
\Omega
, let us introduce an adapted language in order to continue the discussion.

From now on let
d\in\mathbb{N}
with
d\geq 2
. The vectors
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
will be written in the form
x=(x_{1},x^{\prime})
with
x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{d-1}
. The following definition is in the spirit of
[

, see Figure

for an illustration:

.

Let
q>1
and
\omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d-1}
be a bounded Lipschitz domain. We say that a bounded domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
has an outward peak at the origin, of sharpness order
q

with cross-section
\omega
, if for some
\delta>0
it holds

\Omega\cap(-\delta,\delta)^{d}=\Big\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\ x_{1}\in(0,\delta),\ x^{\prime}\in x_{1}^{q}\omega\Big\}

and
\Omega
is Lipschitz at all boundary points except at the origin.

The main result of the present work is as follows:

Theorem 2
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be as in Definition
1
with some bounded Lipschitz cross-section
\omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d-1}
and sharpness order
q\in(1,2)
, then for any fixed
j\in\mathbb{N}
one has

(3)

\lambda_{j}(R^{\Omega}_{\alpha})=\left(\dfrac{\mathcal{H}^{d-2}(\partial\omega)}{\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(\omega)}\right)^{\frac{2}{2-q}}\lambda_{j}(L_{1})\alpha^{\frac{2}{2-q}}+O\left(\alpha^{\frac{2}{2-q}-(q-1)}\right)\text{ for }\alpha\to+\infty,

where
L_{1}
is the differential operator in
L^{2}(0,\infty)
defined first by

\big(L_{1}f\big)(s):=-f^{\prime\prime}(s)+\bigg(\dfrac{q^{2}(d-1)^{2}-2q(d-1)}{4s^{2}}-\dfrac{1}{s^{q}}\bigg)f(s),\quad f\in C^{\infty}_{c}(0,\infty),

and then extended using the Friedrichs extension.

The result on the individual eigenvalues is then used to show that the corresponding eigenfunctions are localized
near the peak tip, which is rigorously described using an Agmon-type estimate as follows:

Theorem 3
.

Let the assumptions of Theorem
2
be fulfilled. For any
j\in\mathbb{N}
and
b>0
there exist
B>0
and
\alpha_{0}>0
such that
for any
\alpha>\alpha_{0}
and any eigenfunction
u
of
R_{\alpha}^{\Omega}
for the eigenvalue
\lambda_{j}(R_{\alpha}^{\Omega})

one has

(4)

\int_{\Omega}e^{2b\alpha|x|}\big|u(x)\big|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d}(x)\leq B\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

Let us make some comments on the assumptions and the relations with earlier works.
All eigenvalues
\lambda_{j}(L_{1})
are negative, see Subsection
2.2
and Appendix
A
below, so the eigenvalues
\lambda_{j}(R^{\Omega}_{\alpha})
diverge to
-\infty
much faster than in the Lipschitz case. We are not aware of any value of
q\in(1,2)
for which the eigenvalues of
L_{1}
can be computed explicitly.
The assumption
q<2
is necessary to ensure that the Sobolev trace operator is well-defined and compact from
W^{1,2}(\Omega)
to
L^{2}(\partial\Omega)
, which guarantees the required properties of the above bilinear form
r^{\Omega}_{\alpha}
and the self-adjointness of the Robin Laplacian
R^{\Omega}_{\alpha}
for all
\alpha>0
, see
[
10
, Sec. 5.2]
or
[
3
,
26
]
.
At the same time, the condition
q>1
is required to actually have a peak, as
\Omega
would be covered by the analysis of Lipschitz domains for
q\leq 1
. The case when
\omega
is a ball of radius
\rho>0
was already studied in the earlier paper
[
22
]
, and its main result is recovered (with an improved remainder estimate) by using (
3
) and observing that in this case

\dfrac{\mathcal{H}^{d-2}(\partial\omega)}{\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(\omega)}=\dfrac{d-1}{\rho}.

It should be noted that the analysis in
[
22
]
was crucially depending on a separation of variables in the ball (and lengthy manipulations with asymptotic expansions of special functions), which is indeed unavailable for general
\omega
. The central observation in this paper is that a part of the arguments of
[
22
]
based on a separation of variables can be replaced by an argument based on the first-order perturbation theory of linear operators and on an adapted coordinate change, which are summarized in Lemma
6
and Section
2.4
. This approach was mainly motivated by observations and geometric constructions from the
later paper
[
28
]
. The asymptotic expansion (
3
) reflects both the sharpness
q
(through the order in
\alpha
) and the geometry of the cross-section
\omega
(through the coefficient in the main term), which is an essentially new contribution when compared to
[
22
]
.
This allows to make additional observations on the interplay between the geometry and the eigenvalues. For example, if the area or the perimeter of
\omega
is fixed, then the ratio

\mathcal{H}^{d-2}(\partial\omega)/\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(\omega)

appearing in the main term of the asymptotic expansion is minimized by the balls due to the isoperimetric inequality. Therefore, if the peak cross-section is a ball, then for
\alpha\to+\infty
the individual eigenvalues of
R^{\Omega}_{\alpha}
diverge to
-\infty
slower than for any other peak cross-section having the same area or the same perimeter, which is reminiscent of the famous Faber-Krahn inequality:

Corollary 4
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be as in Definition
1
with cross-section
\omega
, and denote by
\Omega_{0}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
a domain that is covered by Definition
1
and has a ball
\omega_{0}
as a cross-section. If
\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(\omega)=\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(\omega_{0})
or
\mathcal{H}^{d-2}(\partial\omega)=\mathcal{H}^{d-2}(\partial\omega_{0})
, then for each
j\in\mathbb{N}
there exists
\alpha_{j}>0
such that

\lambda_{j}(R_{\Omega}^{\alpha})\leq\lambda_{j}(R_{\Omega_{0}}^{\alpha})\quad\text{ for all $\alpha>\alpha_{j}$}.

Remark 5
.

While it is not directly related to the scope of the present paper, we mention the fact
that the Robin Laplacians show a rather surprising behavior when it comes to Faber-Krahn type inequalities. On one hand,
among the bounded Lipschitz domains
\Omega
of fixed volume and for any
\alpha\leq 0
(in which case
R^{\Omega}_{\alpha}
is a positive operator), the first eigenvalue
\lambda_{1}(R^{\Omega}_{\alpha})
is minimized by the balls, see
[
9
]
. It was then conjectured in
[
5
,
6
]
that a reverse Faber-Krahn inequality holds true for any fixed
\alpha<0
, i.e.
that the first eigenvalue
\lambda_{1}(R^{\Omega}_{\alpha})
is maximized by the balls among the bounded Lipschitz domains
\Omega
of fixed volume. However, the conjecture was disproved in
[
15
]
, and the spectral asymptotics played a central role
in the construction of a counterexample. This delivered a rare example of a spectral isoperimetric problem
for which the balls are not optimizers.

Our analysis can be easily adapted to the case of domains with several peaks: One considers smalls neighborhoods of the peak and
analyze them separately, and each peak then produces a series of eigenvalues as described in Theorem
2
. In particular, the asymptotics of the individual eigenvalues is then determined by the sharpest peak(s), and the respective eigenfunctions are also localized near the respective peak tips. On the other hand, if there are several peaks
of the same sharpness, then tunneling effects cannot be excluded (i.e. eigenfunctions can localize simultaneously near several peaks), but their analysis is beyond the scope of the present work. The study of Robin eigenvalues can be addressed, in principle, for more general peaks. First, one can extend the class of possible
\Omega
by replacing the condition
x^{\prime}\in x_{1}^{q}\omega
with the more general one
x^{\prime}\in\varphi(x_{1})\omega
, where
\varphi
is a strictly increasing smooth function with
\varphi(0)=\varphi^{\prime}(0)=0
. While some first steps of the analysis are still applicable, the absence of principal homogeneous terms in various intermediate operators poses severe problems for the description of the eigenvalue asymptotics, and no power-type asymptotics in
\alpha
can be expected. A further possible generalization is admitting non-Lipschitz cross-sections
\omega
(in particular, those having peaks in a suitable defined sense). This case of “iterated peaks” is likely to give rise to a multiscale analysis of Laplacians in several dimensions, which is expected to be at a much higher complexity level than the present work. Another extension arises if one admits so-called non-isotropic peaks
featuring different scalings in different
x^{\prime}
-directions. In this case a multi-step approach seems promising, and a particular case could recently be analyzed in
[
30
]
. As a further potential development we mention the possibility to replace the Laplacian through a magnetic Laplacian and to study the influence of magnetic fields: For smooth domains, there are some interesting spectral effects produced by competing Robin and magnetic contributions, see e.g.
[
14
,
18
,
19
]
and references therein, but we are not aware of any works dealing with similar questions in the presence of peaks.

The overall structure of the paper and of the proof follows closely the one in
[
22
]
. Section
2
is devoted to technical preparations. In Subsection
2.1
we collect important facts on Robin Laplacians proved in previous works. In Subsection
2.2
we introduce a family of one-dimensional operators, which includes the operator
L_{1}
appearing in Theorem
2
, and recall their basic spectral and asymptotic properties. Subsection
2.3
introduces
a family of model peak domains together with associated Laplace-type operators. In Section
2.4

a coordinate change is employed to map the peak domains diffeomorphically to cylindrical domains
and to control various integral terms. This part is new with respect to
[
22
]
, and it is an adaptation of some computations from
[
28
]
. In Subsection
2.5
, we obtain a lower bound for model operators defined outside a neighborhood
of the peak’s tip. The core of the study is Section
3
, in which we study the eigenvalues
of a model operator defined in a small neighborhood of the peak’s tip and relate them to the eigenvalues of
L_{1}
as summarized in Corollary
17
. The proof avoids using a separation of variables
and employs the general results on Robin Laplacians from Section
2.1
instead.
In Section
4
we make use of a series of truncations of
\Omega
to show that only a small neighborhood of the peak’s tip counts for the main term of the eigenvalue asymptotics, and an application of Corollary
17
completes the proof of Theorem
2
. In the last section (Section
5
) we prove Theorem
3

by using an IMS-type decoupling and showing that suitable integrals of the eigenfunctions are dominated by their contributions
near the peak tip. Most part of the analysis is of purely variational nature with the help
of the min-max principle for eigenvalues through an adapted choice of test functions.

For the rest of the paper we assume that
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
satisfies the assumptions of Theorem
2
. This fixes once and for all the sharpness parameter
q\in(1,2)
and the cross-section
\omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d-1}
, and we denote additionally

A_{\omega}:=\dfrac{\mathcal{H}^{d-2}(\partial\omega)}{\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(\omega)}.

2.1.
Robin Laplacians on bounded Lipschitz domains

The following proposition collects important properties of the eigenvalues of the Robin Laplacians, and we refer to

Lemma 6

Let

(i)

(ii)

2.2.
A one-dimensional operator

For
\mu>0
consider the symmetric differential operator in
L^{2}(0,\infty)
given by

C_{c}^{\infty}(0,\infty)\ni f\mapsto-f^{\prime\prime}+\left(\frac{q^{2}(d-1)^{2}-2q(d-1)}{4s^{2}}-\frac{\mu}{s^{q}}\right)f

and denote by
L_{\mu}
its Friedrichs extension. Its essential spectrum is
[0,+\infty)
, and it has infinitely many negative eigenvalues due to the presence of the negative long-range potential
\mu/s^{q}
, and all the negative eigenvalues are simple. One easily checks that for the unitary scaling transformation

Z_{c}:\ L^{2}(0,\infty)\to L^{2}(0,\infty),\quad Z_{c}f=\sqrt{c}f(c\,\cdot),\quad c>0,

one has

(5)

L_{\mu}Z_{c}=c^{2}Z_{c}L_{c^{q-2}\mu}\text{ for any $\mu>0$ and $c>0$},

which shows that
L_{\mu}
is unitarily equivalent to
c^{2}L_{c^{q-2}\mu}
. For
c:=\mu^{\frac{1}{2-q}}
this implies the identity

\lambda_{j}(L_{\mu})=\mu^{\frac{2}{2-q}}\lambda_{j}(L_{1})\text{ for any $\mu>0$ and $j\in\mathbb{N}$.}

In what follows we will deal with truncated versions of
L_{\mu}
. Namely, for
a>0
we denote by
L_{\mu,a}
the Friedrichs extension in
L^{2}(0,a)
of the operator

C_{c}^{\infty}(0,a)\ni f\mapsto L_{\mu}f.

By construction the form domain of
L_{\mu,a}
is continuously embedded in
W^{1,2}_{0}(0,a)
, which implies that
L_{\mu,a}
has compact resolvent. In addition, the usual mollification procedure shows that any function from
W^{1,2}(0,a)
vanishing in some neighborhoods of the endpoints belongs to the form domain of
L_{\mu,a}
and, moreover, such functions build a core domain for the bilinear form of
L_{\mu,a}
. We will use the following asymptotic estimate for the eigenvalues of
L_{\mu,a}
:

Lemma 7
.

For any
a>0
and

there exist

and
\mu_{0}>0
such that

\mu^{\frac{2}{2-q}}\lambda_{j}(L_{1})\leq\lambda_{j}(L_{\mu,a})\leq\mu^{\frac{2}{2-q}}\lambda_{j}(L_{1})+K\quad\text{for all }\mu>\mu_{0}.

The proof is given in
[
22
, Sec. 3.1]
for a slightly different choice of parameters, and it is translated into our language using the scaling (

).

Proof.

\displaystyle\|\partial_{1}u_{\mu}

\displaystyle-\partial_{1}u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(V_{\varepsilon,I})}=\|\partial_{1}u_{\mu}-\partial_{1}u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(V_{\varepsilon,(0,2\mu)})}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{2\mu}\int_{\varepsilon x_{1}^{q}\omega}\bigg(\frac{1}{\mu}\chi^{\prime}\Big(\frac{x_{1}}{\mu}\Big)u(x_{1},x^{\prime})+\Big[\chi\Big(\frac{x_{1}}{\mu}\Big)-1\Big]\partial_{1}u(x_{1},x^{\prime})\bigg)^{2}\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(x^{\prime})\,\mathrm{d}x_{1}

\displaystyle\leq I^{\prime}_{\mu}+I^{\prime\prime}_{\mu},

\displaystyle I^{\prime}_{\mu}

\displaystyle:=\dfrac{2}{\mu^{2}}\int_{0}^{2\mu}\int_{\varepsilon x_{1}^{q}\omega}\Big[\chi^{\prime}\Big(\frac{x_{1}}{\mu}\Big)u(x_{1},x^{\prime})\Big]^{2}\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(x^{\prime})\,\mathrm{d}x_{1},

\displaystyle I^{\prime\prime}_{\mu}

\displaystyle:=2\int_{0}^{2\mu}\int_{\varepsilon x_{1}^{q}\omega}\bigg(\Big[\chi\Big(\frac{x_{1}}{\mu}\Big)-1\Big]\partial_{1}u(x_{1},x^{\prime})\bigg)^{2}\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(x^{\prime})\,\mathrm{d}x_{1}.

Using
q(d-1)>1
we obtain

\displaystyle I^{\prime}_{\mu}

\displaystyle\leq\dfrac{2}{\mu^{2}}\int_{0}^{2\mu}\int_{\varepsilon x_{1}^{q}\omega}\|\chi^{\prime}\|_{\infty}^{2}\|u\|_{\infty}^{2}\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(x^{\prime})\,\mathrm{d}x_{1}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{2\|\chi^{\prime}\|_{\infty}^{2}\|u\|_{\infty}^{2}}{\mu^{2}}\int_{0}^{2\mu}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(\varepsilon x_{1}^{q}\omega)\,\mathrm{d}x_{1}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{2\|\chi^{\prime}\|_{\infty}^{2}\|u\|_{\infty}^{2}}{\mu^{2}}\int_{0}^{2\mu}(\varepsilon x_{1}^{q})^{d-1}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(\omega)\,\mathrm{d}x_{1}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{2\|\chi^{\prime}\|_{\infty}^{2}\|u\|_{\infty}^{2}\varepsilon^{d-1}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(\omega)}{\mu^{2}}\int_{0}^{2\mu}x_{1}^{q(d-1)}\mathrm{d}x_{1}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{2\|\chi^{\prime}\|_{\infty}^{2}\|u\|_{\infty}^{2}\varepsilon^{d-1}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(\omega)}{\mu^{2}}\dfrac{(2\mu)^{q(d-1)+1}}{q(d-1)+1}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{2^{q(d-1)+2}\|\chi^{\prime}\|_{\infty}^{2}\|u\|_{\infty}^{2}\varepsilon^{d-1}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(\omega)}{q(d-1)+1}\mu^{q(d-1)-1}\xrightarrow{\mu\to 0^{+}}0,

while
I^{\prime\prime}_{\mu}
converges to
0
for
\mu\to 0^{+}
due to the dominated convergence theorem,
which implies the convergence of
\partial_{1}u_{\mu}
to
\partial_{1}u
in
L^{2}(V_{\varepsilon,I})
for
\mu\to 0^{+}
.
∎

Denote

\widehat{W}_{0}^{1,2}(V_{\varepsilon,I}):=\text{the closure of }W^{1,2}_{I}(V_{\varepsilon,I})\text{ in }W^{1,2}(V_{\varepsilon,I})

and consider the symmetric bilinear form

t^{\alpha,D}_{\varepsilon,I}:=\text{the restriction of $t^{\alpha,N}_{\varepsilon,I}$ on $\widehat{W}_{0}^{1,2}(V_{\varepsilon,I})$.}

By construction we have:

(6)

\displaystyle W^{1,2}_{I}(V_{\varepsilon,I})

\displaystyle\text{ is a core domain of }t^{\alpha,D}_{\varepsilon,I},

which will simplify the subsequent considerations.

In what follows we will be interested in the spectral analysis of the self-adjoint operators
T^{\alpha,N/D}_{\varepsilon,I}

acting in
L^{2}(V_{\varepsilon,I})
and defined by the forms
t^{\alpha,N/D}_{\varepsilon,I}
. Note that for any
\alpha>0
and any
I
one has

\displaystyle\alpha V_{1,I}

\displaystyle=\Big\{(x_{1},x^{\prime})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\ \big(\dfrac{x_{1}}{\alpha},\dfrac{x^{\prime}}{\alpha}\big)\in V_{1,I}\Big\}

\displaystyle=\Big\{(x_{1},x^{\prime})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\ \dfrac{x_{1}}{\alpha}\in I,\ \dfrac{x^{\prime}}{\alpha}\in\big(\dfrac{x_{1}}{\alpha}\big)^{q}\omega\Big\}

\displaystyle=\Big\{(x_{1},x^{\prime})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\ x_{1}\in\alpha I,\ x^{\prime}\in\alpha^{1-q}x_{1}^{q}\omega\Big\}\equiv V_{\alpha^{1-q},\alpha I}.

Therefore, a simple scaling argument shows that for any
j\in\mathbb{N}
one has the relations

(7)

\lambda_{j}(T^{\alpha,N/D}_{1,I})=\alpha^{2}\lambda_{j}(T^{1,N/D}_{\alpha^{1-q},\alpha I}).

Our next goal is to obtain lower and upper bounds for the eigenvalues of
T^{\alpha,N/D}_{\varepsilon,I}

with the help of suitable coordinate changes.

2.4.
Reduction to cylindrical domains

For
\varepsilon>0
consider the diffeomorphism

F_{\varepsilon}:(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d-1}\to(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d-1},\qquad F_{\varepsilon}(s,t):=(s,\varepsilon s^{q}t),

then for any open interval
I\subset(0,\infty)
we have

F_{\varepsilon}(\Pi_{I})=V_{\varepsilon,I}\quad\text{for}\quad\Pi_{I}:=I\times\omega.

In order to deal with various integrals under the change of variables defined by
F_{\varepsilon}
,
we will perform some preliminary computations. Let us introduce the constant

R_{\omega}:=\sup_{t\in\omega}|t|.

Lemma 9
.

For any
\varepsilon>0
, any measurable function
v:\partial_{0}V_{\varepsilon,I}\to\mathbb{R}
and
u:=v\circ F_{\varepsilon}
it holds

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{d-2}\int_{I}

\displaystyle s^{q(d-2)}\int_{\partial\omega}\big|u(s,t)\big|\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-2}(t)\,\mathrm{d}s\leq\int_{\partial_{0}V_{\varepsilon,I}}|v|\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon^{d-2}\int_{I}s^{q(d-2)}\sqrt{1+\varepsilon^{2}q^{2}\,R_{\omega}^{2}\,s^{2q-2}}\int_{\partial\omega}\big|u(s,t)\big|\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-2}(t)\,\mathrm{d}s.

Proof.

It is sufficient to prove the result for the functions
v
supported in images of local charts, then it is extended to general functions by using a partition of unity. Let

U\ni z=(z_{1},z_{2},\cdots,z_{d-2})\mapsto\psi(z)\in\partial\omega

be a local chart on
\partial\omega
, then
U\ni z\mapsto\varepsilon\psi(z)\in\partial(\varepsilon\omega)

is a local chart on
\partial(\varepsilon\omega)
, and

\Psi_{\varepsilon}:\ I\times U\ni(s,z)\mapsto\big(s,\varepsilon s^{q}\psi(z)\big)\equiv F_{\varepsilon}\big(s,\psi(z)\big)\in\partial_{0}V_{\varepsilon,I}

is a local chart on
\partial_{0}V_{\varepsilon,I}
. If
v
is supported in the image of
\Psi_{\varepsilon}
, then the expression of
\mathcal{H}^{d-1}
on hypersurfaces gives

(8)

\int_{\partial_{0}V_{\varepsilon,I}}|v|\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}=\int_{I}\int_{U}\Big|v\big(\Psi_{\varepsilon}(s,z)\big)\Big|J_{\varepsilon}(s,z)\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-2}(z)\mathrm{d}s

with

J_{\varepsilon}(s,z):=\sqrt{\det\big(D\Psi_{\varepsilon}(s,z)^{T}D\Psi_{\varepsilon}(s,z)\big)}.

A direct computation gives

D\Psi_{\varepsilon}(s,z)=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&\dots&0\\
\varepsilon qs^{q-1}\psi(z)&\varepsilon s^{q}\partial_{1}\psi(z)&\dots&\varepsilon s^{q}\partial_{d-2}\psi(z)\end{pmatrix},

which yields

D\Psi_{\varepsilon}(s,z)^{T}D\Psi_{\varepsilon}(s,z)=\begin{pmatrix}1+\varepsilon^{2}q^{2}s^{2q-2}\big|\psi(z)\big|^{2}&\varepsilon^{2}qs^{2q-1}H(z)^{T}\,\\
\\
\varepsilon^{2}qs^{2q-1}H(z)&\varepsilon^{2}s^{2q}G(z)\end{pmatrix}

with
G(z):=D\psi(z)^{T}D\psi(z)
and

H(z):=\begin{pmatrix}\big\langle\psi(z),\partial_{1}\psi(z)\big\rangle\\
\vdots\\
\big\langle\psi(z),\partial_{d-2}\psi(z)\big\rangle\\
\end{pmatrix}\equiv\Big(|\psi|\nabla|\psi|\Big)(z).

Since
\psi
is a local chart, the matrix
G(z)
is invertible for a.e.
z
, and a standard formula for computing the determinant of a block matrix with an invertible diagonal block
becomes applicable, which leads to

\displaystyle J_{\varepsilon}(s,z)^{2}

\displaystyle=\varepsilon^{2(d-2)}s^{2q(d-2)}\det G(z)\Big[1+\varepsilon^{2}q^{2}s^{2q-2}\big|\psi(z)\big|^{2}-\varepsilon^{2}q^{2}s^{2q-2}H(z)^{T}G(z)^{-1}H(z)\Big]

\displaystyle=\varepsilon^{2(d-2)}s^{2q(d-2)}\det G(z)\Big[1+\varepsilon^{2}q^{2}s^{2q-2}\big|\psi(z)\big|^{2}\Big(1-\Big\langle\nabla|\psi|(z),G(z)^{-1}\nabla|\psi|(z)\Big\rangle\Big)\Big].

Consider the modulus function

h:\ \partial\omega\ni t\mapsto|t|\in\mathbb{R},

then

\displaystyle\Big\langle\nabla|\psi|(z),G(z)^{-1}\nabla|\psi|(z)\Big\rangle=|\nabla^{\partial\omega}h|^{2}\big(\psi(z)\big),

\displaystyle\nabla^{\partial\omega}h:=\text{the tangential gradient of $h$ along $\partial\omega$,}

and

\displaystyle 1-\Big\langle\nabla|\psi|(z),G(z)^{-1}\nabla|\psi|(z)\Big\rangle

\displaystyle=|\nabla h|^{2}\big(\psi(z)\big)-|\nabla^{\partial\omega}h|^{2}\big(\psi(z)\big)=|\partial_{\nu}h|^{2}\big(\psi(z)\big),

where
\partial_{\nu}
stands for the normal derivative on
\partial\omega
.
Therefore,

J_{\varepsilon}(s,z)^{2}=\varepsilon^{2(d-2)}s^{2q(d-2)}\det G(z)\Big[1+\varepsilon^{2}q^{2}s^{2q-2}\big|\psi(z)\big|^{2}|\partial_{\nu}h|^{2}\big(\psi(z)\big)\Big],

and one arrives at the obvious lower bound

J_{\varepsilon}(s,z)^{2}\geq\varepsilon^{2(d-2)}s^{2q(d-2)}\det G(z).

Furthermore, due to the definition of
h
we have
|\partial_{\nu}h|\leq 1
a.e,
which implies

\displaystyle J_{\varepsilon}(s,z)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon^{2(d-2)}s^{2q(d-2)}\det G(z)\Big(1+\varepsilon^{2}q^{2}s^{2q-2}\big|\psi(z)\big|^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon^{2(d-2)}s^{2q(d-2)}\det G(z)\Big(1+\varepsilon^{2}q^{2}s^{2q-2}R_{\omega}^{2}\Big).

By substituting these bounds for
J_{\varepsilon}(s,z)^{2}
into (
8
)
we arrive at

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{d-2}

\displaystyle\int_{I}\int_{U}s^{q(d-2)}\Big|v\big(\Psi_{\epsilon}(s,z)\big)\Big|\sqrt{\det G(z)}\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-2}(z)\,\mathrm{d}s\leq\int_{\partial_{0}V_{\varepsilon,I}}|v|\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon^{d-2}\int_{I}\int_{U}s^{q(d-2)}\sqrt{1+\varepsilon^{2}q^{2}s^{2q-2}R^{2}_{\omega}}\Big|v\big(\Psi_{\epsilon}(s,z)\big)\Big|\sqrt{\det G(z)}\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-2}(z)\,\mathrm{d}s.

It remains to note that
v\big(\Psi_{\epsilon}(s,z)\big)=u(s,\psi(z))
and

\int_{U}\big|u(s,\psi(z))\big|\sqrt{\det G(z)}\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-2}(z)=\int_{\partial\omega}\big|u(s,\cdot)\big|\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-2}.\qed

Lemma 10
.

For any
v\in W^{1,2}(V_{\varepsilon,I})
and
u:=v\circ F_{\varepsilon}
we have

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{d-1}\int_{I}s^{q(d-1)}

\displaystyle\int_{\omega}\Bigg[\big(1-(d-1)\varepsilon qR_{\omega}\big)|\partial_{s}u|^{2}

\displaystyle+\left(\dfrac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}s^{2q}}-\frac{qR_{\omega}}{s^{2}\varepsilon}-(d-1)\frac{q^{2}R^{2}_{\omega}}{s^{2}}\right)|\nabla_{t}u|^{2}\Bigg]\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(t)\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq\int_{V_{\varepsilon,I}}|\nabla v|^{2}\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d}

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon^{d-1}\int_{I}s^{q(d-1)}\int_{\omega}\Bigg[\left(1+(d-1)\varepsilon qR_{\omega}\right)|\partial_{s}u|^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad+\left(\dfrac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}s^{2q}}+\frac{qR_{\omega}}{s^{2}\varepsilon}+(d-1)\frac{q^{2}R^{2}_{\omega}}{s^{2}}\right)|\nabla_{t}u|^{2}\Bigg]\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(t)\,\mathrm{d}s.

Proof.

Remark that

\displaystyle DF_{\varepsilon}(s,t)=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
\varepsilon qs^{q-1}t&\varepsilon s^{q}I_{d-1}\end{pmatrix},\quad t=\begin{pmatrix}t_{1}\\
\dots\\
t_{d-1}\end{pmatrix},

\displaystyle\quad I_{d-1}:=\text{the $(d-1)\times(d-1)$ identity matrix.}

The change of variables
x=F_{\varepsilon}(s,t)
gives

\int_{V_{\varepsilon,I}}\big|\nabla v(x)\big|^{2}\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d}(x)=\int_{I}\int_{\omega}\big\langle\nabla u,G_{\varepsilon}\nabla u\big\rangle_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\,g_{\varepsilon}\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(t)\,\mathrm{d}s,

where

\displaystyle g_{\varepsilon}(s,t)

\displaystyle:=\Big|\det DF_{\varepsilon}(s,z)\Big|\equiv\varepsilon^{d-1}s^{q(d-1)},

\displaystyle G_{\varepsilon}(s,t)

\displaystyle:=\Big(DF_{\varepsilon}(s,t)^{T}DF_{\varepsilon}(s,t)\Big)^{-1}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}1+\varepsilon^{2}q^{2}\,s^{2(q-1)}|t|^{2}&\varepsilon^{2}qs^{2q-1}t^{T}\\
\varepsilon^{2}qs^{2q-1}t&\varepsilon^{2}s^{2q}I_{d-1}\end{pmatrix}^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix}1&-\dfrac{q}{s}t^{T}\\
\\
-\dfrac{q}{s}t&\dfrac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}s^{2q}}I_{d-1}+\dfrac{q^{2}}{s^{2}}\,tt^{T}\end{pmatrix}.

It follows that

(9)

\displaystyle\langle\nabla u,G_{\varepsilon}\nabla u\rangle_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}

\displaystyle=|\partial_{s}u|^{2}+\dfrac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}s^{2q}}|\nabla_{t}u|^{2}

\displaystyle-\frac{2q}{s}\sum_{j=1}^{d-1}t_{j}\partial_{s}u\,\partial_{t_{j}}u+\frac{q^{2}}{s^{2}}\sum_{j,\,k=1}^{d-1}t_{j}t_{k}\partial_{t_{j}}u\,\partial_{t_{k}}u.

We estimate

(10)

\displaystyle\left|\frac{2q}{s}\sum_{j=1}^{d-1}t_{j}\partial_{s}u\,\partial_{t_{j}}u\right|

\displaystyle\leq q\sum_{j=1}^{d-1}|t_{j}|\,2\Big|\partial_{s}u\,\dfrac{\partial_{t_{j}}}{s}u\Big|\leq qR_{\omega}\sum_{j=1}^{d-1}2\Big|\partial_{s}u\,\dfrac{\partial_{t_{j}}u}{s}\Big|

\displaystyle\leq qR_{\omega}\sum_{j=1}^{d-1}\bigg[\varepsilon|\partial_{s}u|^{2}+\dfrac{1}{\varepsilon}\Big|\dfrac{\partial_{t_{j}}u}{s}\Big|^{2}\bigg]=(d-1)qR_{\omega}\varepsilon|\partial_{s}u|^{2}+\frac{qR_{\omega}}{\varepsilon s^{2}}|\nabla_{t}u|^{2}.

Similarly, we have

(11)

\displaystyle\left|\frac{q^{2}}{s^{2}}\sum_{j,\,k=1}^{d-1}t_{j}t_{k}\partial_{t_{j}}u\,\partial_{t_{k}}u\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{q^{2}}{s^{2}}\sum_{j,\,k=1}^{d-1}|t_{j}|\,|t_{k}|\,|\partial_{t_{j}}u|\,|\partial_{t_{k}}u|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{q^{2}R_{\omega}^{2}}{s^{2}}\sum_{j,\,k=1}^{d-1}\,|\partial_{t_{j}}u|\,|\partial_{t_{k}}u|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{q^{2}R_{\omega}^{2}}{2s^{2}}\sum_{j,\,k=1}^{d-1}\,\Big(|\partial_{t_{j}}u|^{2}+|\partial_{t_{k}}u|^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle=\frac{q^{2}R^{2}_{\omega}}{s^{2}}(d-1)|\nabla_{t}u|^{2}.

By using (
10
) and (
11
) to estimate
the two last summands on the right-hand side of (
9
) we obtain the desired inequality.
∎

Note that the above computations are classical for smooth
\partial\omega
, but the formulas are still
valid for Lipschitz
\omega
due to general results on Lipschitz manifolds
[
29
]
.

2.5.
A lower bound outside the peak

The goal of this subsection is to obtain a lower bound
for the eigenvalues of
T^{1,N}_{\varepsilon,I}
for intervals
I
separated from
0
: It will be used in the truncation arguments in the next sections.

Lemma 11
.

Let
b,B>0
and
\varepsilon_{0}>0
. Then there exists a constant
c>0
such that for any
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0})

and any non-empty open interval

I_{\varepsilon}\subset\big(b,B\varepsilon^{-\frac{1}{q-1}}\big)

it holds
\lambda_{1}(T^{1,N}_{\varepsilon,I_{\varepsilon}})\geq-c\varepsilon^{-1}
.

Proof.

For any
u\in\mathcal{D}(T^{1,N}_{\varepsilon,I_{\varepsilon}})
we have by Fubini’s theorem

\displaystyle\int_{V_{\varepsilon,I_{\varepsilon}}}|\nabla u|^{2}\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d}

\displaystyle\geq\int_{V_{\varepsilon,I_{\varepsilon}}}|\nabla_{x^{\prime}}u|^{2}\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d}=\int_{I_{\varepsilon}}\int_{\varepsilon x_{1}^{q}\omega}\big|\nabla_{x^{\prime}}u(x_{1},x^{\prime})\big|^{2}\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(x^{\prime})\,\mathrm{d}x_{1}.

In addition, due to Lemma
9
,

\displaystyle\int_{\partial_{0}V_{\varepsilon,I_{\varepsilon}}}u^{2}\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon^{d-2}\int_{I_{\varepsilon}}x_{1}^{q(d-2)}\sqrt{1+\varepsilon^{2}q^{2}\,R_{\omega}^{2}\,x_{1}^{2q-2}}\int_{\partial\omega}u(x_{1},\varepsilon x_{1}^{q}x^{\prime})^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-2}(x^{\prime})\,\mathrm{d}x_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{I_{\varepsilon}}\sqrt{1+\varepsilon^{2}q^{2}\,R_{\omega}^{2}\,x_{1}^{2q-2}}\int_{\partial(\varepsilon x_{1}^{q}\omega)}u(x_{1},x^{\prime})^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-2}(x^{\prime})\,\mathrm{d}x_{1}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle t^{1,N}_{\varepsilon,I_{\varepsilon}}(u,u)

\displaystyle=\int_{V_{\varepsilon,I_{\varepsilon}}}|\nabla u|^{2}\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d}-\int_{\partial_{0}V_{\varepsilon,I_{\varepsilon}}}u^{2}\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}

\displaystyle\geq\int_{I_{\varepsilon}}\Big[\int_{\varepsilon x_{1}^{q}\omega}\big|\nabla_{x^{\prime}}u(x_{1},x^{\prime})\big|^{2}\mathrm{d}\,\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(x^{\prime})

\displaystyle\qquad-\sqrt{1+\varepsilon^{2}q^{2}\,R_{\omega}^{2}\,x_{1}^{2q-2}}\int_{\partial(\varepsilon x_{1}^{q}\omega)}u(x_{1},x^{\prime})^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-2}(x^{\prime})\Big]\,\mathrm{d}x_{1}

\displaystyle=\int_{I_{\varepsilon}}r^{\varepsilon x_{1}^{q}\omega}_{\sqrt{1+\varepsilon^{2}q^{2}\,R_{\omega}^{2}\,x_{1}^{2q-2}}}\big(u(x_{1},\cdot),u(x_{1},\cdot)\big)\,\mathrm{d}x_{1}

\displaystyle\geq\int_{I_{\varepsilon}}\lambda_{1}\Big(R^{\varepsilon x_{1}^{q}\omega}_{\sqrt{1+\varepsilon^{2}q^{2}\,R_{\omega}^{2}\,x_{1}^{2q-2}}}\Big)\int_{\varepsilon x_{1}^{q}\omega}u(x_{1},x^{\prime})^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(x^{\prime})\,\mathrm{d}x_{1}

\displaystyle\geq\Lambda_{\varepsilon}\int_{V_{\varepsilon,I_{\varepsilon}}}u^{2}\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d},\qquad\Lambda_{\varepsilon}:=\inf_{x_{1}\in I_{\varepsilon}}\lambda_{1}\left(R^{\varepsilon x_{1}^{q}\omega}_{\sqrt{1+\varepsilon^{2}q^{2}\,R_{\omega}^{2}\,x_{1}^{2q-2}}}\right),

i.e.
\lambda_{1}(T^{1,N}_{\varepsilon,I_{\varepsilon}})\geq\Lambda_{\varepsilon}
for all
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0})
,
and it remains to find a suitable lower bound for
\Lambda_{\varepsilon}
.
With the help of the assertions (i) and (iv) of Lemma
6
we obtain, with some
C^{\prime}>0
,

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}\left(R^{\varepsilon x_{1}^{q}\omega}_{\sqrt{1+\varepsilon^{2}q^{2}\,R_{\omega}^{2}\,x_{1}^{2q-2}}}\right)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{\lambda_{1}\left(R^{\omega}_{\varepsilon x_{1}^{q}\sqrt{1+\varepsilon^{2}q^{2}\,R_{\omega}^{2}\,x_{1}^{2q-2}}}\right)}{\varepsilon^{2}x_{1}^{2q}}

\displaystyle\geq-\dfrac{\varepsilon A_{\omega}x_{1}^{q}\sqrt{1+\varepsilon^{2}q^{2}\,R_{\omega}^{2}\,x_{1}^{2q-2}}+C^{\prime}\varepsilon^{2}x_{1}^{2q}\big(1+\varepsilon^{2}q^{2}\,R_{\omega}^{2}\,x_{1}^{2q-2}\big)}{\varepsilon^{2}x_{1}^{2q}}

\displaystyle=-A_{\omega}\dfrac{\sqrt{1+\varepsilon^{2}q^{2}\,R_{\omega}^{2}\,x_{1}^{2q-2}}}{\varepsilon x_{1}^{q}}-C^{\prime}\big(1+\varepsilon^{2}q^{2}\,R_{\omega}^{2}\,x_{1}^{2q-2}\big).

For all
x_{1}\in I_{\varepsilon}
we have

x_{1}^{q}>b^{q},\qquad\varepsilon x_{1}^{q-1}<\varepsilon\big(B\varepsilon^{-\frac{1}{q-1}}\big)^{q-1}=B^{q-1},

therefore, for any
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0})
one has

\displaystyle\Lambda_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\geq-\dfrac{A_{\omega}\sqrt{1+q^{2}R_{\omega}B^{2q-2}}}{b^{q}}\cdot\dfrac{1}{\varepsilon}-C^{\prime}(1+q^{2}R_{\omega}^{2}B^{2q-2})

\displaystyle\geq-\bigg[\dfrac{A_{\omega}\sqrt{1+q^{2}R_{\omega}B^{2q-2}}}{b^{q}}+C^{\prime}(1+q^{2}R_{\omega}^{2}B^{2q-2})\varepsilon_{0}\bigg]\cdot\dfrac{1}{\varepsilon}.\qed

3.
Spectral asymptotics near the peak

3.1.
Model operators near the peak

We are going to apply a series of coordinate changes, which will allow for the spectral study of the operators

(12)

T_{\varepsilon,a}:=T^{1,D}_{\varepsilon,(0,a)}

as
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
. We also denote

t_{\varepsilon,a}:=t^{1,D}_{\varepsilon,(0,a)},\qquad\Pi_{a}:=\Pi_{(0,a)}\equiv(0,a)\times\omega.

The diffeomorphism
F_{\varepsilon}
defined in Subsection
2.4
induces the unitary transformation

\displaystyle\Phi_{\varepsilon}:L^{2}(V_{\varepsilon,(0,a)})\to L^{2}\big(\Pi_{a},\varepsilon^{d-1}s^{(d-1)q}\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(t)\big),

\displaystyle\qquad\big(\Phi_{\varepsilon}u\big)(s,t):=u\big(F_{\varepsilon}(s,t)\big).

Consider the symmetric bilinear form
p_{\varepsilon,a}
in
L^{2}\big(\Pi_{a},\varepsilon^{d-1}s^{(d-1)q}\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(t)\big)
defined by

p_{\varepsilon,a}(u,u):=t_{\varepsilon,a}(\Phi_{\varepsilon}^{-1}u,\Phi_{\varepsilon}^{-1}u),

on the domain
\mathcal{D}(p_{\varepsilon,a})=\Phi_{\varepsilon}\big(\mathcal{D}(t_{\varepsilon,a})\big)

and the associated self-adjoint operator
P_{\varepsilon,a}
in the weighted space
L^{2}\big(\Pi_{a},\varepsilon^{d-1}s^{(d-1)q}\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(t)\big)
, which is by construction unitary
equivalent to
T_{\varepsilon,a}
and, hence, has the same eigenvalues.
Remark that due to the explicit form of
F_{\varepsilon}
and
\Phi_{\varepsilon}
we have

\Phi_{\varepsilon}\big(W^{1,2}_{I}(V_{\varepsilon,I})\big)=W^{1,2}_{I}(\Pi_{I})
.
It will be convenient to denote

\mathcal{D}_{a}:=W^{1,2}_{(0,a)}(\Pi_{a}),

then (
6
) shows that

\mathcal{D}_{a}\text{ is a core domain of }p_{\varepsilon,a},

so
\mathcal{D}_{a}
can be used as a test domain when applying the min-max principle to
P_{\varepsilon,a}
.

Due to Lemma
9
and Lemma
10
for any
u\in\mathcal{D}_{a}
one has

p^{-}_{\varepsilon,a}(u,u)\leq p_{\varepsilon,a}(u,u)\leq p^{+}_{\varepsilon,a}(u,u)

with symmetric bilinear forms
p^{\pm}_{\varepsilon,a}(u,u)
in
L^{2}\big(\Pi_{a},\varepsilon^{d-1}s^{(d-1)q}\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(t)\big)
defined on
\mathcal{D}_{a}

\displaystyle p^{-}_{\varepsilon,a}(u,u)

\displaystyle:=\varepsilon^{d-1}\int_{0}^{a}s^{q(d-1)}\int_{\omega}\Bigg[\big(1-(d-1)\varepsilon qR_{\omega}\big)|\partial_{s}u|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\left(\dfrac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}s^{2q}}-\frac{qR_{\omega}}{s^{2}\varepsilon}-(d-1)\frac{q^{2}R^{2}_{\omega}}{s^{2}}\right)|\nabla_{t}u|^{2}\Bigg]\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(t)\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\quad-\varepsilon^{d-2}\int_{0}^{a}s^{q(d-2)}\sqrt{1+\varepsilon^{2}q^{2}\,R_{\omega}^{2}\,s^{2q-2}}\int_{\partial\omega}\big|u(s,t)\big|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-2}(t)\,\mathrm{d}s,

\displaystyle p^{+}_{\varepsilon,a}(u,u)

\displaystyle:=\varepsilon^{d-1}\int_{0}^{a}s^{q(d-1)}\int_{\omega}\Bigg[\big(1+(d-1)\varepsilon qR_{\omega}\big)|\partial_{s}u|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\left(\dfrac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}s^{2q}}+\frac{qR_{\omega}}{s^{2}\varepsilon}+(d-1)\frac{q^{2}R^{2}_{\omega}}{s^{2}}\right)|\nabla_{t}u|^{2}\Bigg]\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(t)\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\quad-\varepsilon^{d-2}\int_{0}^{a}s^{q(d-2)}\int_{\partial\omega}\big|u(s,t)\big|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-2}(t)\,\mathrm{d}s,

In order to deal with
L^{2}
-spaces without weights we additionally consider the unitary
transform

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}_{\varepsilon}:\ L^{2}(\Pi_{a})\to L^{2}(\Pi_{a},\varepsilon^{d-1}s^{q(d-1)}\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(t)\big),

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}_{\varepsilon}u(s,t):=\varepsilon^{-\frac{d-1}{2}}s^{-\frac{q(d-1)}{2}}u(s,t),

and the symmetric bilinear forms
\widetilde{p}^{\,\pm}_{\varepsilon,a}
in
L^{2}(\Pi_{a})
defined
on
\mathcal{V}_{\varepsilon}^{-1}\mathcal{D}_{a}\equiv\mathcal{D}_{a}

by

\widetilde{p}^{\,\pm}_{\varepsilon,a}(u,u):=p^{\pm}_{\varepsilon,a}(\mathcal{V}_{\varepsilon}u,\mathcal{V}_{\varepsilon}u),

i.e. for
u\in\mathcal{D}_{a}
one has

\displaystyle\widetilde{p}^{\,-}_{\varepsilon,a}(u,u)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{a}\int_{\omega}\Bigg[\big(1-(d-1)\varepsilon qR_{\omega}\big)\Big(\partial_{s}u-\frac{q(d-1)}{2s}\,u\Big)^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\left(\dfrac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}s^{2q}}-\frac{qR_{\omega}}{s^{2}\varepsilon}-(d-1)\frac{q^{2}R^{2}_{\omega}}{s^{2}}\right)|\nabla_{t}u|^{2}\Bigg]\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(t)\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\quad-\int_{0}^{a}\dfrac{1}{\varepsilon s^{q}}\sqrt{1+\varepsilon^{2}q^{2}\,R_{\omega}^{2}\,s^{2q-2}}\int_{\partial\omega}\big|u(s,t)\big|\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-2}(t)\,\mathrm{d}s,

\displaystyle\widetilde{p}^{\,+}_{\varepsilon,a}(u,u)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{a}\int_{\omega}\Bigg[\big(1+(d-1)\varepsilon qR_{\omega}\big)\Big(\partial_{s}u-\frac{q(d-1)}{2s}\,u\Big)^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\left(\dfrac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}s^{2q}}+\frac{qR_{\omega}}{\varepsilon s^{2}}+(d-1)\frac{q^{2}R^{2}_{\omega}}{s^{2}}\right)|\nabla_{t}u|^{2}\Bigg]\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(t)\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\quad-\int_{0}^{a}\dfrac{1}{\varepsilon s^{q}}\int_{\partial\omega}u^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-2}(t)\,\mathrm{d}s.

Assuming that

\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0})

with some sufficiently small
\varepsilon_{0}>0
we can find suitable constants
c_{j}>0
,
j\in\{1,2\}
, with
c_{j}\varepsilon_{0}<1

such that for all
s\in(0,a)
one has

\frac{qR_{\omega}}{\varepsilon s^{2}}+(d-1)\frac{q^{2}R^{2}_{\omega}}{s^{2}}\leq c_{1}\varepsilon\cdot\dfrac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}s^{2q}},

which yields,

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}s^{2q}}+\frac{qR_{\omega}}{\varepsilon s^{2}}+(d-1)\frac{q^{2}R^{2}_{\omega}}{s^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\dfrac{1+c_{1}\varepsilon}{\varepsilon^{2}s^{2q}},

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}s^{2q}}-\frac{qR_{\omega}}{\varepsilon s^{2}}-(d-1)\frac{q^{2}R^{2}_{\omega}}{s^{2}}

\displaystyle\geq\dfrac{1-c_{1}\varepsilon}{\varepsilon^{2}s^{2q}},

and

\sqrt{1+\varepsilon^{2}q^{2}\,R_{\omega}^{2}\,s^{2q-2}}\leq\dfrac{1}{1-c_{2}\varepsilon}.

This gives, with
c_{0}:=(d-1)qR_{\omega}
,

\displaystyle\widetilde{p}^{\,-}_{\varepsilon,a}(u,u)

\displaystyle\geq(1-c_{0}\varepsilon)\int_{0}^{a}\int_{\omega}\Big(\partial_{s}u-\frac{q(d-1)}{2s}\,u\Big)^{2}\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(t)\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\quad+(1-c_{1}\varepsilon)\int_{0}^{a}\dfrac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}s^{2q}}\int_{\omega}|\nabla_{t}u|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(t)\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\quad-\dfrac{1}{1-c_{2}\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{a}\dfrac{1}{\varepsilon s^{q}}\int_{\partial\omega}u^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-2}(t)\,\mathrm{d}s,

\displaystyle\widetilde{p}^{\,+}_{\varepsilon,a}(u,u)

\displaystyle\leq(1+c_{0}\varepsilon)\int_{0}^{a}\int_{\omega}\Big(\partial_{s}u-\frac{q(d-1)}{2s}\,u\Big)^{2}\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(t)\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\quad+(1+c_{1}\varepsilon)\int_{0}^{a}\dfrac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}s^{2q}}\int_{\omega}|\nabla_{t}u|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(t)\,\mathrm{d}s-\int_{0}^{a}\dfrac{1}{\varepsilon s^{q}}\int_{\partial\omega}u^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-2}(t)\,\mathrm{d}s.

Due to
u\in\mathcal{D}_{a}
the integration by parts in
s
gives

\int_{0}^{a}\frac{u\,\partial_{s}u}{s}\mathrm{d}s=\int_{0}^{a}\frac{u^{2}}{2s^{2}}\mathrm{d}s,

resulting in

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{a}\int_{\omega}\Big(\partial_{s}u

\displaystyle-\frac{q(d-1)}{2s}\,u\Big)^{2}\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(t)\,\mathrm{d}s=\int_{0}^{a}\int_{\omega}\bigg(|\partial_{s}u|^{2}+\dfrac{H}{s^{2}}\,u^{2}\bigg)\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(t)\,\mathrm{d}s,

(13)

\displaystyle H:

\displaystyle=\dfrac{q^{2}(d-1)^{2}-2q(d-1)}{4}\equiv\dfrac{\big(q(d-1)-1\big)^{2}-1}{4}.

By taking

c:=\max\{c_{0},c_{1},c_{2}\}

and by adjusting the value of
\varepsilon_{0}
to have
c\varepsilon_{0}<1
we arrive at
the inequalities

h^{-}_{\varepsilon,a}(u,u)\leq\widetilde{p}^{\,-}_{\varepsilon,a}(u,u),\qquad\widetilde{p}^{\,+}_{\varepsilon,a}(u,u)\leq h^{\,+}_{\varepsilon,a}(u,u)

valid for all
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0})
and all
u\in\mathcal{D}_{a}
, where
the symmetric bilinear forms
h^{\pm}_{\varepsilon,a}
in
L^{2}(\Pi_{a})
are defined on
\mathcal{D}_{a}
by

\displaystyle h^{-}_{\varepsilon,a}(u,u)

\displaystyle=(1-c\varepsilon)\int_{0}^{a}\int_{\omega}\bigg(|\partial_{s}u|^{2}+\dfrac{H}{s^{2}}\,u^{2}\bigg)\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(t)\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\quad+(1-c\varepsilon)\int_{0}^{a}\dfrac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}s^{2q}}\int_{\omega}|\nabla_{t}u|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(t)\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\quad-\dfrac{1}{1-c\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{a}\dfrac{1}{\varepsilon s^{q}}\int_{\partial\omega}u^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-2}(t)\,\mathrm{d}s,

\displaystyle h^{+}_{\varepsilon,a}(u,u)

\displaystyle=(1+c\varepsilon)\int_{0}^{a}\int_{\omega}\bigg(|\partial_{s}u|^{2}+\dfrac{H}{s^{2}}\,u^{2}\bigg)\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(t)\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\quad+(1+c\varepsilon)\int_{0}^{a}\dfrac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}s^{2q}}\int_{\omega}|\nabla_{t}u|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(t)\,\mathrm{d}s-\int_{0}^{a}\dfrac{1}{\varepsilon s^{q}}\int_{\partial\omega}u^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-2}(t)\,\mathrm{d}s.

Using the min-max principle one summarizes the above considerations as follows:

Lemma 12
.

For any
a>0
there exist
\varepsilon_{0}>0
and
c>0
, with
c\varepsilon_{0}<1
, such that for any
j\in\mathbb{N}
and any
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0})
it holds that

\mu^{-}_{j}(\varepsilon,a)\leq\lambda_{j}(T_{\varepsilon,a})\leq\mu^{+}_{j}(\varepsilon,a)\quad\text{with}\quad\mu^{\pm}_{j}(\varepsilon,a):=\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}S\subset\mathcal{D}_{a}\\
\dim S=j\end{subarray}}\sup_{u\in S\setminus\{0\}}\dfrac{h^{\pm}_{\varepsilon,a}(u,u)}{\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Pi_{a})}}.

3.2.
Upper bound

In this subsection we are going to obtain an upper bound for the quantities
\mu^{+}_{j}(\varepsilon,a)

defined in Lemma
12
. The analysis is based on the observation that for any

u\in\mathcal{D}_{a}
one has

\displaystyle h^{+}_{\varepsilon,a}(u,u)=(1+c\varepsilon)\int_{0}^{a}\int_{\omega}\bigg(|\partial_{s}u|^{2}+\dfrac{H}{s^{2}}\,u^{2}\bigg)\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(t)\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\quad+(1+c\varepsilon)\int_{0}^{a}\dfrac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}s^{2q}}\bigg[\int_{\omega}|\nabla_{t}u|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(t)-\dfrac{\varepsilon s^{q}}{1+c\varepsilon}\int_{\partial\omega}u^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-2}(t)\bigg]\,\mathrm{d}s.

In other words, if we denote

\rho_{\varepsilon}(s):=\dfrac{s^{q}}{1+c\varepsilon},

then

(14)

\displaystyle h^{+}_{\varepsilon,a}(u,u)

\displaystyle=(1+c\varepsilon)\int_{0}^{a}\int_{\omega}\bigg(|\partial_{s}u|^{2}+\dfrac{H}{s^{2}}\,u^{2}\bigg)\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(t)\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle+(1+c\varepsilon)\int_{0}^{a}\dfrac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}s^{2q}}r^{\omega}_{\varepsilon\rho_{\varepsilon}(s)}\big(u(s,\cdot),u(s,\cdot)\big)\mathrm{d}s.

Let
\psi_{\alpha}
be the positive and
L^{2}
-normalized eigenfunction of the Robin Laplacian
R^{\omega}_{\alpha}
for the first eigenvalue
\lambda_{1}(R^{\omega}_{\alpha})
. In view of the above representation one is interested in
\psi_{\varepsilon\rho_{\varepsilon}(s)}
. By the assertion (iii) of Lemma
6
, for any
\varepsilon>0
the map

(0,a)\ni s\mapsto\psi_{\varepsilon\rho_{\varepsilon}(s)}\in L^{2}(\omega)

is smooth. For any function
f\in W^{1,2}_{(0,a)}(0,a)
consider the associated function

(15)

u:\ \Pi_{a}\ni(s,t)\mapsto f(s)\psi_{\varepsilon\rho_{\varepsilon}(s)}(t).

Obviously
u
belongs to
L^{2}(\Pi_{a})
, its weak derivatives in
\Pi_{a}
are given by

\displaystyle\partial_{s}u:

\displaystyle\ (s,t)\mapsto f^{\prime}(s)\psi_{\varepsilon\rho_{\varepsilon}(s)}(t)+f(s)\dfrac{\mathrm{d}\psi_{\varepsilon\rho_{\varepsilon}(s)}}{\mathrm{d}s}(t),

\displaystyle\nabla_{t}u:

\displaystyle\ (s,t)\mapsto f(s)\nabla_{t}\psi_{\varepsilon\rho_{\varepsilon}(s)}(t),

and, therefore, also belongs to
L^{2}(\Pi_{a})
, which shows that
u\in\mathcal{D}_{a}
.

Lemma 13
.

For any
a>0
and
\varepsilon_{0}>0
there exists
B>0
such that for any

f\in W^{1,2}_{(0,a)}(0,a)
, any
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0})
and for
u
given by (
15
) it holds

\displaystyle\int_{\Pi_{a}}u^{2}\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{a}f(s)^{2}\mathrm{d}s,

\displaystyle h^{+}_{\varepsilon,a}(u,u)

\displaystyle\leq(1+c\varepsilon)\int_{0}^{a}\bigg[f^{\prime}(s)^{2}+\bigg(\dfrac{H}{s^{2}}-\dfrac{A_{\omega}}{\varepsilon(1+c\varepsilon)s^{q}}+B\bigg)f(s)^{2}\bigg]\mathrm{d}s.

Proof.

The normalization of
\psi_{\varepsilon\rho_{\varepsilon}(s)}
gives

\displaystyle\int_{\omega}u(s,t)^{2}\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(t)

\displaystyle=f(s)^{2}\int_{\omega}\psi_{\varepsilon\rho_{\varepsilon}(s)}(t)^{2}\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(t)=f(s)^{2},

\displaystyle\int_{\Pi_{a}}u^{2}\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{a}\int_{\omega}u(s,t)^{2}\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(t)\mathrm{d}s=\int_{0}^{a}f(s)^{2}\mathrm{d}s.

Due to the choice of
\psi_{\varepsilon\rho_{\varepsilon}(s)}
we have

r^{\omega}_{\varepsilon\rho_{\varepsilon}(s)}\big(u(s,\cdot),u(s,\cdot)\big)=\lambda_{1}\big(R^{\omega}_{\varepsilon\rho_{\varepsilon}(s)}\big)\int_{\omega}u(s,t)^{2}\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(t)=\lambda_{1}(R^{\omega}_{\varepsilon\rho_{\varepsilon}(s)})f(s)^{2}.

Finally,

\displaystyle\int_{\omega}|\partial_{s}u|^{2}\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(t)

\displaystyle=\int_{\omega}\Big(f^{\prime}(s)\psi_{\varepsilon\rho_{\varepsilon}(s)}(t)+f(s)\dfrac{\mathrm{d}\psi_{\varepsilon\rho_{\varepsilon}(s)}}{\mathrm{d}s}(t)\Big)^{2}\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(t)

\displaystyle=f^{\prime}(s)^{2}+2f(s)f^{\prime}(s)\int_{\omega}\psi_{\varepsilon\rho_{\varepsilon}(s)}(t)\dfrac{\mathrm{d}\psi_{\varepsilon\rho_{\varepsilon}(s)}}{\mathrm{d}s}(t)\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(t)

\displaystyle\qquad+f(s)^{2}\int_{\omega}\Big|\dfrac{\mathrm{d}\psi_{\varepsilon\rho_{\varepsilon}(s)}}{\mathrm{d}s}(t)\Big|^{2}\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(t),

and using

\int_{\omega}\psi_{\varepsilon\rho_{\varepsilon}(s)}(t)\dfrac{\mathrm{d}\psi_{\varepsilon\rho_{\varepsilon}(s)}}{\mathrm{d}s}(t)\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(t)=\dfrac{1}{2}\dfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}\int_{\omega}\psi_{\varepsilon\rho_{\varepsilon}(s)}(t)^{2}\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(t)=\dfrac{1}{2}\dfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}1=0

to eliminate the middle term on the right-hand side
we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\omega}|\partial_{s}u|^{2}\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(t)=f^{\prime}(s)^{2}+W_{\varepsilon}f(s)^{2},\qquad W_{\varepsilon}(s):=\int_{\omega}\Big|\dfrac{\mathrm{d}\psi_{\varepsilon\rho_{\varepsilon}(s)}}{\mathrm{d}s}(t)\Big|^{2}\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(t).

The substitution of the previous identities into (
14
) yields

(16)

h^{+}_{\varepsilon,a}(u,u)=(1+c\varepsilon)\int_{0}^{a}\left[f^{\prime}(s)^{2}+\left(W_{\varepsilon}(s)+\dfrac{H}{s^{2}}+\dfrac{\lambda_{1}\left(R^{\omega}_{\varepsilon\rho_{\varepsilon}(s)}\right)}{\varepsilon^{2}s^{2q}}\right)f(s)^{2}\right]\mathrm{d}s.

Using Lemma
6
(iv) we estimate, with a suitable
C_{1}>0
,

\displaystyle\dfrac{\lambda_{1}(R^{\omega}_{\varepsilon\rho_{\varepsilon}(s)})}{\varepsilon^{2}s^{2q}}

\displaystyle\leq\dfrac{-A_{\omega}\varepsilon\rho_{\varepsilon}(s)+C_{1}\varepsilon^{2}\rho_{\varepsilon}(s)^{2}}{\varepsilon^{2}s^{2q}}

\displaystyle=-A_{\omega}\dfrac{\rho_{\varepsilon}(s)}{s^{2q}}\cdot\dfrac{1}{\varepsilon}+C_{1}\dfrac{\rho_{\varepsilon}(s)^{2}}{s^{2q}}\leq-\dfrac{A_{\omega}}{\varepsilon(1+c\varepsilon)}\cdot\dfrac{1}{s^{q}}+C_{1}.

Furthermore,

\begin{split}W_{\varepsilon}(s)&=\int_{\omega}\bigg(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}\psi_{\sigma}}{\mathrm{d}\sigma}(t)\Big|_{\sigma=\varepsilon\rho_{\varepsilon}(s)}\dfrac{\mathrm{d}\big(\varepsilon\rho_{\varepsilon}(s)\big)}{\mathrm{d}s}\bigg)^{2}\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(t)=\frac{q^{2}s^{2q-2}}{(1+c\varepsilon)^{2}}\,\varepsilon^{2}\bigg\|\dfrac{\mathrm{d}\psi_{\sigma}}{\mathrm{d}\sigma}\Big|_{\sigma=\varepsilon\rho_{\varepsilon}(s)}\bigg\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\omega)}.\end{split}

By Lemma
6
(iii) we can find
C_{2}>0
such that

\bigg\|\dfrac{\mathrm{d}\psi_{\sigma}}{\mathrm{d}\sigma}\Big|_{\sigma=\varepsilon\rho_{\varepsilon}(s)}\bigg\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\omega)}\leq C_{2}\quad\text{for all $s\in(0,a)$ and $\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0})$,}

therefore,

W_{\varepsilon}(s)\leq\dfrac{C_{2}q^{2}s^{2q-2}}{(1+c\varepsilon)^{2}}\varepsilon^{2}\leq C_{2}q^{2}a^{2q-2}\varepsilon_{0}^{2}=:C_{3}.

Making use of these inequalities in (
16
) one arrives at the claim with
B:=C_{1}+C_{3}
.
∎

Lemma 14
.

For any
a>0
and
j\in\mathbb{N}
one has

\lambda_{j}(T_{\varepsilon,a})\leq\left(\dfrac{A_{\omega}}{\varepsilon}\right)^{\frac{2}{2-q}}\lambda_{j}(L_{1})+O\left(\dfrac{1}{\varepsilon}\right)^{\frac{q}{2-q}}\text{ for }\varepsilon\to 0^{+}.

Proof.

Let
j\in\mathbb{N}
and
S\subset W^{1,2}_{(0,a)}(0,a)
be a
j
-dimensional subspace. Due to the first identity in Lemma
13
the set

\widetilde{S}:=\Big\{u:\ u(s,t)=f(s)\psi_{\varepsilon\rho_{\varepsilon}(s)}(t)\text{ with }f\in S\Big\}

is a
j
-dimensional subspace of
\mathcal{D}_{a}
. Therefore,

\mu_{j}^{+}(\varepsilon,a)\leq\sup_{u\in\widetilde{S}\setminus\{0\}}\dfrac{h^{+}_{\varepsilon,a}(u,u)}{\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Pi_{a})}},

and using Lemma
13
one obtains

\displaystyle\dfrac{\mu_{j}^{+}(\varepsilon,a)}{1+c\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{u\in S\setminus\{0\}}\dfrac{\displaystyle\int_{0}^{a}\bigg[f^{\prime}(s)^{2}+\bigg(\dfrac{H}{s^{2}}-\dfrac{A_{\omega}}{\varepsilon(1+c\varepsilon)s^{q}}\bigg)f(s)^{2}\bigg]\mathrm{d}s}{\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}(0,a)}}+B.

The constants
c
and
B
are independent of
S
, so one can take the infimum over all
S
as above
to arrive at

\displaystyle\dfrac{\mu_{j}^{+}(\varepsilon,a)}{1+c\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\leq\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}S\subset W^{1,2}_{(0,a)}(0,a)\\
\dim S=j\end{subarray}}\sup_{u\in S\setminus\{0\}}\dfrac{\displaystyle\int_{0}^{a}\bigg[f^{\prime}(s)^{2}+\bigg(\dfrac{H}{s^{2}}-\dfrac{A_{\omega}}{\varepsilon(1+c\varepsilon)s^{q}}\bigg)f(s)^{2}\bigg]\mathrm{d}s}{\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}(0,a)}}+B.

The first summand on the right-hand side is exactly the characterization of the eigenvalue
\lambda_{j}\left(L_{\frac{A_{\omega}}{c(1+c\varepsilon)},a}\right)
using the min-max principle, which yields

\mu_{j}^{+}(\varepsilon,a)\leq(1+c\varepsilon)\lambda_{j}\left(L_{\frac{A_{\omega}}{\varepsilon(1+c\varepsilon)},a}\right)+(1+c\varepsilon)B.

With the help of the upper bound for
\lambda_{j}(T_{\varepsilon,a})
and Lemma
7
with
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
we obtain, with some
K>0
,

\displaystyle\lambda_{j}(T_{\varepsilon,a})

\displaystyle\leq\mu_{j}^{+}(\varepsilon,a)\leq(1+c\varepsilon)\left[\left(\dfrac{A_{\omega}}{\varepsilon(1+c\varepsilon)}\right)^{\frac{2}{2-q}}\lambda_{j}(L_{1})+K\right]+(1+c\varepsilon)B,

which gives the sought estimate. ∎

3.3.
Lower bound

Similarly to the upper bound, the subsequent estimates for the numbers
\mu^{-}_{j}(\varepsilon,a)

defined in Lemma
12
will be based on the observation that for any
u\in\mathcal{D}_{a}

one has

(17)

\displaystyle h^{-}_{\varepsilon,a}(u,u)

\displaystyle=(1-c\varepsilon)\int_{0}^{a}\int_{\omega}\bigg(|\partial_{s}u|^{2}+\dfrac{H}{s^{2}}\,u^{2}\bigg)\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(t)\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle+(1-c\varepsilon)\int_{0}^{a}\dfrac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}s^{2q}}r^{\omega}_{\varepsilon\rho_{\varepsilon}(s)}\big(u(s,\cdot),u(s,\cdot)\big)\mathrm{d}s,

\displaystyle\text{with }\rho_{\varepsilon}(s)

\displaystyle:=\dfrac{s^{q}}{(1-c\varepsilon)^{2}}.

As above, let
\psi_{\alpha}
be the positive and
L^{2}
-normalized eigenfunction of the Robin Laplacian
R^{\omega}_{\alpha}
for the first eigenvalue
\lambda_{1}(R^{\omega}_{\alpha})
.
We represent each function
u\in\mathcal{D}_{a}
as

(18)

\displaystyle u

\displaystyle=v+w,

\displaystyle v(s,t)

\displaystyle:=f(s)\psi_{\varepsilon\rho_{\varepsilon}(s)}(t),

\displaystyle f(s)

\displaystyle:=\int_{\omega}\psi_{\varepsilon\rho_{\varepsilon}(s)}(t)u(s,t)\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(t),

\displaystyle w

\displaystyle:=u-v.

By construction one has
f\in W^{1,2}_{(0,a)}(0,a)
,
and due to Lemma
6
(iii) the weak derivatives of
v
in
\Pi_{a}
are given by

(19)

\displaystyle\partial_{s}v:

\displaystyle\ (s,t)\mapsto f^{\prime}(s)\psi_{\varepsilon\rho_{\varepsilon}(s)}(t)+f(s)\dfrac{\mathrm{d}\psi_{\varepsilon\rho_{\varepsilon}(s)}}{\mathrm{d}s}(t),

\displaystyle\nabla_{t}v:

\displaystyle\ (s,t)\mapsto f(s)\nabla_{t}\psi_{\varepsilon\rho_{\varepsilon}(s)}(t).

In particular, the functions
u
,
\partial_{s}u
and
\partial_{t_{j}}u
belong to
L^{2}(\Pi_{a})
,
so one has
v,w\in\mathcal{D}_{a}
.

Lemma 15
.

There exist a sufficiently small
\varepsilon_{0}>0
and a sufficiently large
B>0
such that
for any
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0})
and any
u\in\mathcal{D}_{a}
decomposed as in (
18
) it holds that

\displaystyle\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Pi_{a})}

\displaystyle=\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}(0,a)}+\|w\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Pi_{a})},

\displaystyle\dfrac{h^{-}_{\varepsilon,a}(u,u)}{1-c\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\geq(1-B\varepsilon)\int_{0}^{a}\bigg[f^{\prime}(s)^{2}+\bigg(\dfrac{H}{s^{2}}-\dfrac{A_{\omega}}{\varepsilon(1-B\varepsilon)^{2}s^{q}}\bigg)f(s)^{2}\bigg]\mathrm{d}s-B\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}(0,a)}.

Proof.

Let us collect important properties of the decomposition (
18
).
First,

\displaystyle\int_{\omega}v(s,t)^{2}\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(t)

\displaystyle=f(s)^{2}\int_{\omega}\psi_{\varepsilon\rho_{\varepsilon}(s)}(t)^{2}\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(t)=f(s)^{2},

\displaystyle\int_{\Pi_{a}}v^{2}\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{a}\int_{\omega}v(s,t)^{2}\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(t)\mathrm{d}s=\int_{0}^{a}f(s)^{2}\mathrm{d}s.

Furthermore, by construction we have

(20)

\int_{\omega}\psi_{\varepsilon\rho_{\varepsilon}(s)}(t)w(s,t)\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(t)=0,

in particular,

(21)

\int_{\omega}v(s,t)w(s,t)\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(t)=f(s)\int_{\omega}\psi_{\varepsilon\rho_{\varepsilon}(s)}(t)w(s,t)\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(t)=0,

therefore,

\displaystyle\int_{\omega}u(s,t)^{2}\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(t)

\displaystyle=\int_{\omega}v(s,t)^{2}\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(t)+2\int_{\omega}v(s,t)w(s,t)\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(t)

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{\omega}w(s,t)^{2}\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(t)

\displaystyle=\int_{\omega}v(s,t)^{2}\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(t)+\int_{\omega}w(s,t)^{2}\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(t)

\displaystyle=f(s)^{2}+\int_{\omega}w(s,t)^{2}\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(t),

\displaystyle\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Pi_{a})}

\displaystyle=\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}(0,a)}+\|w\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Pi_{a})}.

Due to the choice of
\psi_{\varepsilon\rho_{\varepsilon}(s)}
and the orthogonality (
21
), the spectral theorem for
R^{\omega}_{\varepsilon,\rho_{\varepsilon}(s)}
gives

\displaystyle r^{\omega}_{\varepsilon\rho_{\varepsilon}(s)}\big(u(s,\cdot),u(s,\cdot)\big)

\displaystyle=r^{\omega}_{\varepsilon\rho_{\varepsilon}(s)}\big(v(s,\cdot),v(s,\cdot)\big)+r^{\omega}_{\varepsilon\rho_{\varepsilon}(s)}\big(w(s,\cdot),w(s,\cdot)\big)

\displaystyle\geq\lambda_{1}(R^{\omega}_{\varepsilon\rho_{\varepsilon}(s)})\big\|v(s,\cdot)\big\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\omega)}+\lambda_{2}(R^{\omega}_{\varepsilon\rho_{\varepsilon}(s)})\big\|w(s,\cdot)\big\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\omega)}

\displaystyle=\lambda_{1}(R^{\omega}_{\varepsilon\rho_{\varepsilon}(s)})f(s)^{2}+\lambda_{2}(R^{\omega}_{\varepsilon\rho_{\varepsilon}(s)})\big\|w(s,\cdot)\big\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\omega)}.

Using these estimates in (
17
) one obtains

\displaystyle\dfrac{h^{-}_{\varepsilon,a}(u,u)}{1-c\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\geq\int_{0}^{a}\int_{\omega}|\partial_{s}u|^{2}\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(t)\mathrm{d}s+\int_{0}^{a}\bigg(\dfrac{H}{s^{2}}+\dfrac{\lambda_{1}(R^{\omega}_{\varepsilon\rho_{\varepsilon}(s)})}{\varepsilon^{2}s^{2q}}\bigg)f(s)^{2}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{0}^{a}\int_{\omega}\bigg(\dfrac{H}{s^{2}}+\dfrac{\lambda_{2}(R^{\omega}_{\varepsilon\rho_{\varepsilon}(s)})}{\varepsilon^{2}s^{2q}}\bigg)w(s,t)^{2}\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(t)\mathrm{d}s.

We first use Lemma
6
(iv) to estimate with a suitable
C_{1}>0
:

\displaystyle\dfrac{\lambda_{1}(R^{\omega}_{\varepsilon\rho_{\varepsilon}(s)})}{\varepsilon^{2}s^{2q}}

\displaystyle\geq-\dfrac{A_{\omega}\varepsilon\rho_{\varepsilon}(s)+C_{1}\varepsilon^{2}\rho_{\varepsilon}(s)^{2}}{\varepsilon^{2}s^{2q}}

\displaystyle=-A_{\omega}\dfrac{\rho_{\varepsilon}(s)}{s^{2q}}\cdot\dfrac{1}{\varepsilon}-C_{1}\dfrac{\rho_{\varepsilon}(s)^{2}}{s^{2q}}\geq-\dfrac{A_{\omega}}{\varepsilon(1-c\varepsilon)^{2}}\cdot\dfrac{1}{s^{q}}-\dfrac{C_{1}}{(1-c\varepsilon)^{4}}.

In addition, using Lemma
6
(v) we find some
C_{2}>0
and, if necessary, decrease the value of
\varepsilon_{0}
to obtain

\lambda_{2}\big(R^{\omega}_{\varepsilon\rho_{\varepsilon}(s)}\big)\geq C_{2}\text{ for all $\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0})$ and $s\in(0,a)$.}

Then for a suitable
C_{3}>0
and an adjusted value of
\varepsilon_{0}
one has

\dfrac{H}{s^{2}}+\dfrac{\lambda_{2}(R^{\omega}_{\varepsilon\rho_{\varepsilon}(s)})}{\varepsilon^{2}s^{2q}}\geq\dfrac{C_{3}}{\varepsilon^{2}s^{2q}}\text{ for all $\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0})$ and $s\in(0,a)$,}

yielding

(22)

\displaystyle\dfrac{h^{-}_{\varepsilon,a}(u,u)}{1-c\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\geq\int_{0}^{a}\int_{\omega}|\partial_{s}u|^{2}\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(t)\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{a}\bigg(\dfrac{H}{s^{2}}-\dfrac{A_{\omega}}{\varepsilon(1-c\varepsilon)^{2}}\cdot\dfrac{1}{s^{q}}-\dfrac{C_{1}}{(1-c\varepsilon)^{4}}\bigg)f(s)^{2}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{a}\int_{\omega}\dfrac{C_{3}}{\varepsilon^{2}s^{2q}}w(s,t)^{2}\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(t)\,\mathrm{d}s.

Our next aim is to study the term containing
|\partial_{s}u|^{2}
on the right-hand side of (
22
). By denoting

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle:=\int_{\omega}|\partial_{s}v|^{2}\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(t),

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle:=2\int_{\omega}\partial_{s}v\cdot\partial_{s}w\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(t),

\displaystyle I_{3}

\displaystyle:=\int_{\omega}|\partial_{s}w|^{2}\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(t),

we arrive at the decomposition

(23)

\int_{\omega}|\partial_{s}u|^{2}\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(t)=I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3}.

The term
I_{1}
is analyzed in a straightforward way:

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle=\int_{\omega}\Big(f^{\prime}(s)\psi_{\varepsilon\rho_{\varepsilon}(s)}(t)+f(s)\dfrac{\mathrm{d}\psi_{\varepsilon\rho_{\varepsilon}(s)}}{\mathrm{d}s}(t)\Big)^{2}\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(t)

\displaystyle=f^{\prime}(s)^{2}+2f(s)f^{\prime}(s)\int_{\omega}\psi_{\varepsilon\rho_{\varepsilon}(s)}(t)\dfrac{\mathrm{d}\psi_{\varepsilon\rho_{\varepsilon}(s)}}{\mathrm{d}s}(t)\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(t)

\displaystyle\qquad+f(s)^{2}\int_{\omega}\Big|\dfrac{\mathrm{d}\psi_{\varepsilon\rho_{\varepsilon}(s)}}{\mathrm{d}s}(t)\Big|^{2}\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(t),

and using

\int_{\omega}\psi_{\varepsilon\rho_{\varepsilon}(s)}(t)\dfrac{\mathrm{d}\psi_{\varepsilon\rho_{\varepsilon}(s)}}{\mathrm{d}s}(t)\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(t)=\dfrac{1}{2}\dfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}\int_{\omega}\psi_{\varepsilon\rho_{\varepsilon}(s)}(t)^{2}\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(t)=\dfrac{1}{2}\dfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}1=0

to eliminate the middle term on the right-hand side, and by noting that last term is nonnegative we obtain

(24)

I_{1}\geq f^{\prime}(s)^{2}.

As a consequence of (
19
), we have

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle=I^{\prime}_{2}+I^{\prime\prime}_{2},

\displaystyle I^{\prime}_{2}

\displaystyle=2f^{\prime}(s)\int_{\omega}\psi_{\varepsilon\rho_{\varepsilon}(s)}(t)\partial_{s}w(s,t)\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(t),

\displaystyle I^{\prime\prime}_{2}

\displaystyle=2f(s)\int_{\omega}\dfrac{\mathrm{d}\psi_{\varepsilon\rho_{\varepsilon}(s)}}{\mathrm{d}s}(t)\partial_{s}w(s,t)\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(t).

Using the orthogonality (
20
) we obtain

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\dfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}\int_{\omega}\psi_{\varepsilon\rho_{\varepsilon}(s)}(t)w(s,t)\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(t)

\displaystyle=\int_{\omega}\dfrac{\mathrm{d}\psi_{\varepsilon\rho_{\varepsilon}(s)}}{\mathrm{d}s}(t)w(s,t)\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(t)+\int_{\omega}\psi_{\varepsilon\rho_{\varepsilon}(s)}\partial_{s}w(s,t)\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(t),

therefore,

\int_{\omega}\psi_{\varepsilon\rho_{\varepsilon}(s)}(t)\partial_{s}w(s,t)\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(t)=-\int_{\omega}\dfrac{\mathrm{d}\psi_{\varepsilon\rho_{\varepsilon}(s)}}{\mathrm{d}s}(t)w(s,t)\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(t).

This allows one to estimate
I^{\prime}_{2}
by

\displaystyle|I^{\prime}_{2}|

\displaystyle=\bigg|\int_{\omega}2f^{\prime}(s)\dfrac{\mathrm{d}\psi_{\varepsilon\rho_{\varepsilon}(s)}}{\mathrm{d}s}(t)w(s,t)\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(t)\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq W_{\varepsilon}(s)f^{\prime}(s)^{2}+\int_{\omega}w(s,t)^{2}\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(t)

\displaystyle\text{with }W_{\varepsilon}(s)

\displaystyle:=\int_{\omega}\bigg|\dfrac{\mathrm{d}\psi_{\varepsilon\rho_{\varepsilon}(s)}}{\mathrm{d}s}(t)\bigg|^{2}\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(t).

Furthermore,

\displaystyle|I^{\prime\prime}_{2}|

\displaystyle\leq\dfrac{W_{\varepsilon}(s)}{\varepsilon}\,f(s)^{2}+\varepsilon\int_{\omega}\partial_{s}w(s,t)^{2}\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(t).

We have

\begin{split}W_{\varepsilon}(s)&=\int_{\omega}\bigg(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}\psi_{\sigma}}{\mathrm{d}\sigma}(t)\Big|_{\sigma=\varepsilon\rho_{\varepsilon}(s)}\dfrac{\mathrm{d}\big(\varepsilon\rho_{\varepsilon}(s)\big)}{\mathrm{d}s}\bigg)^{2}\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(t)\\
&=\frac{q^{2}s^{2q-2}}{(1-c\varepsilon)^{4}}\,\varepsilon^{2}\bigg\|\dfrac{\mathrm{d}\psi_{\sigma}}{\mathrm{d}\sigma}\Big|_{\sigma=\varepsilon\rho_{\varepsilon}(s)}\bigg\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\omega)},\end{split}

and by Lemma
6
(iii) we can find
C_{4}>0
such that

\bigg\|\dfrac{\mathrm{d}\psi_{\sigma}}{\mathrm{d}\sigma}\Big|_{\sigma=\varepsilon\rho_{\varepsilon}(s)}\bigg\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\omega)}\leq C_{4}\quad\text{for all $s\in(0,a)$ and $\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0})$,}

then for some
C_{5}>0
it holds

W_{\varepsilon}(s)\leq C_{5}\varepsilon^{2}\text{ for all $s\in(0,a)$ and $\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0})$.}

Then

\displaystyle|I^{\prime}_{2}|

\displaystyle\leq C_{5}\varepsilon^{2}f^{\prime}(s)^{2}+\big\|w(s,\cdot)\big\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\omega)},

\displaystyle|I^{\prime\prime}_{2}|

\displaystyle\leq C_{5}\varepsilon f(s)^{2}+\varepsilon\big\|\partial_{s}w(s,\cdot)\big\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\omega)},

and

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle\geq-|I^{\prime}_{2}|-|I^{\prime\prime}_{2}|\geq C_{5}\varepsilon^{2}f^{\prime}(s)^{2}-C_{5}\varepsilon f(s)^{2}-\varepsilon\big\|\partial_{s}w(s,\cdot)\big\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\omega)}-\big\|w(s,\cdot)\big\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\omega)}.

By using the last inequality and (
24
) in (
23
) we arrive at

\displaystyle\big\|\partial_{s}u(s,\cdot)\big\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\omega)}

\displaystyle\geq(1-C_{5}\varepsilon^{2})f^{\prime}(s)^{2}-C_{5}\varepsilon f(s)^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad+(1-\varepsilon)\big\|\partial_{s}w(s,\cdot)\big\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\omega)}-\big\|w(s,\cdot)\big\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\omega)}.

By using the last inequality to estimate the first summand on the right-hand side of (
22
)
we obtain

\displaystyle\dfrac{h^{-}_{\varepsilon,a}(u,u)}{1-c\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\geq\int_{0}^{a}\bigg[(1-C_{5}\varepsilon^{2})f^{\prime}(s)^{2}+\bigg(\dfrac{H}{s^{2}}-\dfrac{A_{\omega}}{\varepsilon(1-c\varepsilon)^{2}s^{q}}-\dfrac{C_{1}}{(1-c\varepsilon)^{4}}-C_{5}\varepsilon\bigg)f(s)^{2}\bigg]\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{0}^{a}\int_{\omega}\bigg[(1-\varepsilon)\partial_{s}w(s,t)^{2}+\bigg(\dfrac{C_{3}}{\varepsilon^{2}s^{2q}}-1\bigg)w(s,t)^{2}\bigg]\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(t)\,\mathrm{d}s.

We additionally adjust
\varepsilon_{0}
such that the expression in the last integral
becomes non-negative for all
s\in(0,a)
and all
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0})
and choose
C_{6}>0

to have

\dfrac{C_{1}}{(1-c\varepsilon)^{4}}+C_{5}\varepsilon\leq C_{6}\text{ for all }\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0}),

which results in

(25)

\dfrac{h^{-}_{\varepsilon,a}(u,u)}{1-c\varepsilon}\geq\int_{0}^{a}\bigg[(1-C_{5}\varepsilon^{2})f^{\prime}(s)^{2}+\bigg(\dfrac{H}{s^{2}}-\dfrac{A_{\omega}}{\varepsilon(1-c\varepsilon)^{2}s^{q}}-C_{6}\bigg)f(s)^{2}\bigg]\mathrm{d}s.

Recall that due to the explicit expression (
13
) for
H
and the one-dimensional Hardy inequality we have

\displaystyle\dfrac{\big(q(d-1)-1\big)^{2}}{4}\int_{0}^{a}\dfrac{1}{s^{2}}\,f(s)^{2}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{a}\bigg(f^{\prime}(s)^{2}+\dfrac{\big(q(d-1)-1\big)^{2}-1}{4s^{2}}f(s)^{2}\bigg)\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\equiv\int_{0}^{a}\bigg(f^{\prime}(s)^{2}+\dfrac{H}{s^{2}}f(s)^{2}\bigg)\mathrm{d}s,

yielding

\int_{0}^{a}\dfrac{1}{s^{2}}\,f(s)^{2}\mathrm{d}s\leq C_{7}\int_{0}^{a}\bigg(f^{\prime}(s)^{2}+\dfrac{H}{s^{2}}f(s)^{2}\bigg)\mathrm{d}s,\quad C_{7}:=\dfrac{4}{\big(q(d-1)-1\big)^{2}}.

This implies

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{a}

\displaystyle\bigg[(1-C_{5}\varepsilon^{2})f^{\prime}(s)^{2}+\dfrac{H}{s^{2}}f(s)^{2}\bigg]\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=(1-C_{5}\varepsilon^{2})\int_{0}^{a}\bigg(f^{\prime}(s)^{2}+\dfrac{H}{s^{2}}f(s)^{2}\bigg)\mathrm{d}s+HC_{5}\varepsilon^{2}\int_{0}^{a}\dfrac{1}{s^{2}}\,f(s)^{2}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\geq(1-C_{5}\varepsilon^{2})\int_{0}^{a}\bigg(f^{\prime}(s)^{2}+\dfrac{H}{s^{2}}f(s)^{2}\bigg)\mathrm{d}s-|H|C_{5}C_{7}\varepsilon^{2}\int_{0}^{a}\bigg(f^{\prime}(s)^{2}+\dfrac{H}{s^{2}}f(s)^{2}\bigg)\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=(1-C_{8}\varepsilon^{2})\int_{0}^{a}\bigg(f^{\prime}(s)^{2}+\dfrac{H}{s^{2}}f(s)^{2}\bigg)\mathrm{d}s\quad\text{ with }C_{8}:=C_{5}+|H|C_{5}C_{7}.

By using this inequality on the right-hand side of (
25
) we arrive at

\displaystyle\dfrac{h^{-}_{\varepsilon,a}(u,u)}{1-c\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\geq(1-C_{8}\varepsilon^{2})\int_{0}^{a}\bigg(f^{\prime}(s)^{2}+\dfrac{H}{s^{2}}f(s)^{2}\bigg)\,\mathrm{d}s-\int_{0}^{a}\dfrac{A_{\omega}}{\varepsilon s^{q}(1-c\varepsilon)^{2}}f(s)^{2}\mathrm{d}s-C_{6}\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}(0,a)}.

We assume additionally
\varepsilon_{0}\in(0,1)
, then
\varepsilon^{2}<\varepsilon
for all
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0})
, and we obtain the claim by choosing any

such that

\dfrac{1}{(1-c\varepsilon)^{2}}\leq\dfrac{1}{1-B\varepsilon}\text{ for all }\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0})\qed

holds.

Lemma 16
.

For any
a>0
and any
j\in\mathbb{N}
one has

\lambda_{j}(T_{\varepsilon,a})\geq\left(\dfrac{A_{\omega}}{\varepsilon}\right)^{\frac{2}{2-q}}\lambda_{j}(L_{1})+O\left(\dfrac{1}{\varepsilon}\right)^{\frac{q}{2-q}}\text{ for }\varepsilon\to 0^{+}.

Proof.

Due to the first identity in Lemma
15
, the map
u\mapsto(f,w)
defined by (
18
) is uniquely extended to an isometry

J:\ L^{2}(\Pi_{a})\to L^{2}(0,a)\oplus L^{2}(\Pi_{a}).

Consider the symmetric bilinear form
m_{\varepsilon}
in
L^{2}(0,a)\oplus L^{2}(\Pi_{a})
given by

m_{\varepsilon}\big((f,w),(f,w)\big):=(1-B\varepsilon)\int_{0}^{a}\bigg[f^{\prime}(s)^{2}+\bigg(\dfrac{H}{s^{2}}-\dfrac{A_{\omega}}{\varepsilon(1-B\varepsilon)^{2}s^{q}}\bigg)f(s)^{2}\bigg]\mathrm{d}s-B\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}(0,a)}

on
\mathcal{D}(m_{\varepsilon}):=W^{1,2}_{(0,a)}(0,a)\oplus L^{2}(\Pi_{a})
,
then the inequality in Lemma
15
can be read as

\dfrac{h^{-}_{\varepsilon,a}(u,u)}{1-c\varepsilon}\geq m_{\varepsilon}(Ju,Ju)\text{ for all }u\in\mathcal{D}_{a}.

Further remark that the closure of
m_{\varepsilon}
is the bilinear form corresponding to the self-adjoint operator

M_{\varepsilon}:=\Big((1-B\varepsilon)L_{\frac{A_{\omega}}{\varepsilon(1-B\varepsilon)^{2}},a}-B\Big)\oplus 0.

Let
j\in\mathbb{N}
. For each
j
-dimensional subspace
S\subset\mathcal{D}_{a}
the set
J(S)
is a
j
-dimensional subspace in the domain of
m_{\varepsilon}
, and due to Lemma
12
we have

(26)

\displaystyle\dfrac{\lambda_{j}(T_{\varepsilon,a})}{1-c\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\geq\dfrac{\mu^{-}_{j}(\varepsilon,a)}{1-c\varepsilon}\equiv\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}S\subset\mathcal{D}_{a}\\
\dim S=j\end{subarray}}\sup_{u\in S\setminus\{0\}}\dfrac{h^{-}_{\varepsilon,a}(u,u)}{\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Pi_{a})}}

\displaystyle\geq\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}S\subset\mathcal{D}_{a}\\
\dim S=j\end{subarray}}\sup_{u\in S\setminus\{0\}}\dfrac{m_{\varepsilon}(Ju,Ju)}{\|Ju\|^{2}_{L^{2}(0,a)\oplus L^{2}(\Pi_{a})}}

\displaystyle\equiv\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}S\subset\mathcal{D}_{a}\\
\dim S=j\end{subarray}}\sup_{z\in J(S)\setminus\{0\}}\dfrac{m_{\varepsilon}(z,z)}{\|z\|^{2}_{L^{2}(0,a)\oplus L^{2}(\Pi_{a})}}

\displaystyle\geq\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}S^{\prime}\subset\mathcal{D}(m_{\varepsilon})\\
\dim S^{\prime}=j\end{subarray}}\sup_{z\in S^{\prime}\setminus\{0\}}\dfrac{m_{\varepsilon}(z,z)}{\|z\|^{2}_{L^{2}(0,a)\oplus L^{2}(\Pi_{a})}}=\lambda_{j}(M_{\varepsilon}).

For
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
we have, due to Lemma
7
,

\displaystyle\lambda_{j}\Big((1-B\varepsilon)L_{\frac{A_{\omega}}{\varepsilon(1-B\varepsilon)^{2}},a}-B\Big)

\displaystyle=(1-B\varepsilon)\lambda_{j}(L_{\frac{A_{\omega}}{\varepsilon(1-B\varepsilon)^{2}},a})-B

\displaystyle\geq(1-B\varepsilon)\Big(\frac{A_{\omega}}{\varepsilon(1-B\varepsilon)^{2}}\Big)^{\frac{2}{2-q}}\lambda_{j}(L_{1})-B

\displaystyle=\left(\dfrac{A_{\omega}}{\varepsilon}\right)^{\frac{2}{2-q}}\lambda_{j}(L_{1})+O\left(\dfrac{1}{\varepsilon}\right)^{\frac{q}{2-q}},

hence,

\displaystyle\lambda_{j}(M_{\varepsilon})

\displaystyle=\lambda_{j}\bigg(\Big[(1-B\varepsilon)L_{\frac{A_{\omega}}{\varepsilon(1-B\varepsilon)^{2}},a}-B\Big]\oplus 0\bigg)\geq\min\bigg\{\lambda_{j}\Big((1-B\varepsilon)L_{\frac{A_{\omega}}{\varepsilon(1-B\varepsilon)^{2}},a}-B\Big),0\bigg\}

\displaystyle\geq\min\bigg\{\left(\dfrac{A_{\omega}}{\varepsilon}\right)^{\frac{2}{2-q}}\lambda_{j}(L_{1})+O\left(\dfrac{1}{\varepsilon}\right)^{\frac{q}{2-q}},0\bigg\}=\left(\dfrac{A_{\omega}}{\varepsilon}\right)^{\frac{2}{2-q}}\lambda_{j}(L_{1})+O\left(\dfrac{1}{\varepsilon}\right)^{\frac{q}{2-q}},

and the substitution into (

) completes the proof.
∎

By combining the upper bound of Lemma

4.

The argument will be based on a series of truncations

Choose

for
\Omega

Lemma 18
.

For any
j\in\mathbb{N}
one has

\lambda_{j}(R^{\Omega}_{\alpha})\leq A_{\omega}^{\frac{2}{2-q}}\lambda_{j}(L_{1})\alpha^{\frac{2}{2-q}}+O\Big(\alpha^{\frac{2}{2-q}-(q-1)}\Big)\text{ for $\alpha\to+\infty$.}

Proof.

As each function from
\widehat{W}^{1,2}_{0}(V_{(1,(0,a)})
can be extended by zero to a function in
W^{1,2}(\Omega)
, the min-max principle and the scaling (
7
) imply that for any
j\in\mathbb{N}
and any
\alpha>0
it holds that

\lambda_{j}(R^{\Omega}_{\alpha})\leq\lambda_{j}(T^{\alpha,D}_{1,(0,a)})=\alpha^{2}\lambda_{j}(T^{1,D}_{\alpha^{1-q},(0,\alpha a)}).

Now assume that
\alpha>1
and remark that each function from
\widehat{W}^{1,2}_{0}(V_{1,(0,a)})

can be extended by zero to a function in
\widehat{W}^{1,2}_{0}(V_{1,(0,\alpha a)})
,
so the min-max principle implies

\lambda_{j}(T^{1,D}_{\alpha^{1-q},(0,\alpha a)})\leq\lambda_{j}(T^{1,D}_{\alpha^{1-q},(0,a)}).

Using Corollary
17
with
\varepsilon:=\alpha^{1-q}
and
\alpha\to+\infty
we obtain

\displaystyle\lambda_{j}(T^{1,D}_{\alpha^{1-q},(0,a)})

\displaystyle=\left(\dfrac{A_{\omega}}{\alpha^{1-q}}\right)^{\frac{2}{2-q}}\lambda_{j}(L_{1})+O\left(\dfrac{1}{\alpha^{1-q}}\right)^{\frac{q}{2-q}}=A_{\omega}^{\frac{2}{2-q}}\alpha^{\frac{2q-2}{2-q}}\lambda_{j}(L_{1})+O\left(\alpha^{\frac{q^{2}-q}{2-q}}\right),

and the preceding inequalities yield

\displaystyle\lambda_{j}(R^{\Omega}_{\alpha})\leq\alpha^{2}\lambda_{j}(T^{1,D}_{\alpha^{1-q},(0,a)})

\displaystyle=\alpha^{2}\bigg[A_{\omega}^{\frac{2}{2-q}}\alpha^{\frac{2q-2}{2-q}}\lambda_{j}(L_{1})+O\Big(\alpha^{\frac{q^{2}-q}{2-q}}\Big)\bigg]

\displaystyle=A_{\omega}^{\frac{2}{2-q}}\lambda_{j}(L_{1})\alpha^{\frac{2}{2-q}}+O\Big(\alpha^{\frac{2}{2-q}-(q-1)}\Big).\qed

Obtaining a lower bound requires slightly more work.

Lemma 19
.

For any
j\in\mathbb{N}
one has

\lambda_{j}(R^{\Omega}_{\alpha})\geq A_{\omega}^{\frac{2}{2-q}}\lambda_{j}(L_{1})\alpha^{\frac{2}{2-q}}+O\Big(\alpha^{\frac{2}{2-q}-(q-1)}\Big)\text{ for $\alpha\to+\infty$.}

Proof.

Let
j\in\mathbb{N}
be fixed. For any
u\in W^{1,2}(\Omega)
and any
\alpha>0
we have

r^{\Omega}_{\alpha}(u,u)\geq t^{\alpha,N}_{1,(0,a)}(u|_{\Omega_{a}},u|_{\Omega_{a}})+r^{\Omega^{\prime}_{a}}_{\alpha}(u|_{\Omega^{\prime}_{a}},u|_{\Omega^{\prime}_{a}}),

and the min-max principle shows that for any
\alpha>0
it holds

\lambda_{j}(R^{\Omega}_{\alpha})\geq\lambda_{j}\big(T^{\alpha,N}_{1,(0,a)}\oplus R^{\Omega^{\prime}_{a}}_{\alpha}\big).

Remark that

\lambda_{j}\Big(T^{\alpha,N}_{1,(0,a)}\oplus R^{\Omega^{\prime}_{a}}_{\alpha}\Big)\geq\min\Big\{\lambda_{j}\big(T^{\alpha,N}_{1,(0,a)}\big),\lambda_{1}(R^{\Omega^{\prime}_{a}}_{\alpha})\Big\}.

As
\Omega^{\prime}_{a}
is a bounded Lipschitz domain, there is
c>0
such that
\lambda_{1}(R^{\Omega^{\prime}_{a}}_{\alpha})\geq-c\alpha^{2}
for all sufficiently large
\alpha
(as discussed in the introduction). Due to the scaling (
7
) we have

\lambda_{j}\big(T^{\alpha,N}_{1,(0,a)}\big)=\alpha^{2}\lambda_{j}\big(T^{1,N}_{\alpha^{1-q},(0,a\alpha)}\big),

and by putting all together we arrive at

(27)

\lambda_{j}(R^{\Omega}_{\alpha})\geq\min\Big\{\lambda_{j}\big(T^{1,N}_{\alpha^{1-q},(0,a\alpha)}\big),-c\Big\}\alpha^{2}\text{ for all sufficiently large $\alpha$.}

From now on we assume that
\alpha>1
. To study the eigenvalues of
T^{1,N}_{\alpha^{1-q},(0,a\alpha)}

we pick smooth functions
\chi_{1},\chi_{2}\in C^{\infty}(0,\infty)
such that

\chi_{1}(s)=0\text{ for all }s>\frac{2a}{3},\qquad\chi_{2}(s)=0\text{ for all }s<\frac{a}{3},\qquad\chi_{1}^{2}+\chi_{2}^{2}=1.

Due to Lemma
8
the set
W^{1,2}_{(0,\infty)}(V_{\alpha^{1-q},(0,a\alpha)})
is a core domain of
t^{1,N}_{\alpha^{1-q},(0,a\alpha)}
, and for any
u\in W^{1,2}_{(0,\infty)}(V_{\alpha^{1-q},(0,a\alpha)})
one has

\displaystyle(x_{1},x^{\prime})\mapsto\chi_{1}(x_{1})u(x_{1},x^{\prime})\in W^{1,2}_{(0,a)}(V_{\alpha^{1-q},(0,a)}),

\displaystyle(x_{1},x^{\prime})\mapsto\chi_{2}(x_{1})u(x_{1},x^{\prime})\in W^{1,2}(V_{\alpha^{1-q},(\frac{a}{3},a\alpha)}),

\displaystyle\|\chi_{1}u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(V_{\alpha^{1-q},(0,a)})}+\|\chi_{2}u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(V_{\alpha^{1-q},(\frac{a}{3},a\alpha)})}=\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(V_{\alpha^{1-q},(0,a\alpha)})},

Appendix A
Spectral properties of the effective one-dimensional Schrödinger operator
L_{\mu}

For the sake of completeness we add proofs for the claims made in Section
2.2
about the operator
L_{\mu}
.
Recall that
L_{\mu}
(with
\mu>0
) is the self-adjoint operator in
L^{2}(0,\infty)
obtained as the Friedrichs extension
of the symmetric operator

C_{c}^{\infty}(0,\infty)\ni f\mapsto-f^{\prime\prime}+V_{\mu}f,\qquad V_{\mu}(s):=\frac{q^{2}(d-1)^{2}-2q(d-1)}{4s^{2}}-\frac{\mu}{s^{q}}.

Lemma 20
.

[0,\infty)\subset\mathrm{spec}_{\mathrm{ess}}L_{\mu}
.

Proof.

Choose cut-off functions
\chi_{n}
, with
n\in\mathbb{N}
, such that

•

\chi_{n}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
with
0\leq\chi_{n}\leq 1
,

•

\mathrm{supp}\,\chi_{n}\subset\big[n\pi,(2n+1)\pi\big]
,

•

\chi_{n}(s)=1
for all
s\in\big[(n+1)\pi,2n\pi\big]
,

•

\|\chi_{n}^{\prime}\|_{\infty}+\|\chi_{n}^{\prime\prime}\|_{\infty}\leq c
with some
c
independent of
n
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{R}
and define functions
f_{n}\in C^{\infty}_{c}(0,\infty)
by

f_{n}(s):=\chi_{n}(s)\cos(ks),

then
\mathrm{supp}\,f_{n}\subset\big[n\pi,(2n+1)\pi\big]
and

(L_{\mu}-k^{2})f_{n}(s)=2k\chi^{\prime}_{n}(s)\sin(ks)-\chi^{\prime\prime}_{n}(s)\cos(ks)+V_{\mu}(s)f_{n}(s).

Remark that the first two summands on the right-hand side are uniformly bounded in
(s,n)
and supported in
\big[n\pi,(n+1)\pi\big]\cup\big[2n\pi,(2n+1)\pi\big]
. Furthermore, we can roughly estimate
\big|V_{\mu}(s)\big|\leq\frac{c_{0}}{s}
for all
s\geq\pi
with some
c_{0}>0
independent of
s
. These observations yield

\|(L_{\mu}-k^{2})f_{n}\|_{L^{2}(0,\infty)}\leq c_{1}+\dfrac{c_{0}}{n}\|f_{n}\|_{L^{2}(0,\infty)}

with some fixed
c_{1}>0
. On the other hand,

\|f_{n}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(0,\infty)}\geq\int_{(n+1)\pi}^{2n\pi}\big|\cos(ks)\big|^{2}\ \mathrm{d}s=(n-1)\int_{0}^{\pi}\big|\cos(ks)\big|^{2}\ \mathrm{d}s=(n-1)\dfrac{\pi}{2},

hence,

\dfrac{\|(L_{\mu}-k^{2})f_{n}\|_{L^{2}(0,\infty)}}{\|f_{n}\|_{L^{2}(0,\infty)}}\xrightarrow{n\to\infty}0,

which shows
k^{2}\in\mathrm{spec}\,L_{\mu}
. As
k
was an arbitrary real number, one obtains
[0,\infty)\subset\mathrm{spec}L_{\mu}
.
As each non-isolated point of the spectrum belongs to the essential spectrum, one arrives at the conclusion.
∎

Lemma 21
.

\inf\mathrm{spec}_{\mathrm{ess}}L_{\mu}\geq 0
.

Proof.

Let
\chi_{1},\chi_{2}\in C^{\infty}(0,\infty)
with
\chi_{1}^{2}+\chi_{2}^{2}=1
,
\chi_{1}(s)=1
for
s<1
and
\chi_{1}(s)=0
for
s>2
.
For
t>0
denote

\chi_{k,t}:=\chi_{k}\Big(\dfrac{\cdot}{t}\Big),\quad k\in\{1,2\},

then for any
f\in C^{\infty}_{c}(0,\infty)
one has, with
a:=\big\|(\chi_{1}^{\prime})^{2}+(\chi_{2}^{\prime})^{2}\big\|_{\infty}
,

\displaystyle\langle f,L_{\mu}f\rangle_{L^{2}(0,\infty)}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\big(|f^{\prime}(s)|^{2}+V_{\mu}|f(s)|^{2}\big)\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\big(\big|(\chi_{1,t}f)^{\prime}(s)\big|^{2}+V_{\mu}\big|(\chi_{1,t}f)(s)\big|^{2}\big)\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{0}^{\infty}\Big(|(\chi_{2,t}f)^{\prime}(s)|^{2}+V_{\mu}|(\chi_{2,t}f)(s)|^{2}\big)\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\quad-\int_{0}^{\infty}\Big(\big|\chi_{1,t}^{\prime}(s)\big|^{2}+\big|\chi_{2,t}^{\prime}(s)\big|^{2}\Big)\big|f(s)\big|^{2}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\geq\int_{0}^{2t}\big(\big|(\chi_{1,t}f)^{\prime}(s)\big|^{2}+V_{\mu}\big|(\chi_{1,t}f)(s)\big|^{2}\big)\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{t}^{\infty}\Big(|(\chi_{2,t}f)^{\prime}(s)|^{2}+V_{\mu}|(\chi_{2,t}f)(s)|^{2}\big)\,\mathrm{d}s-\dfrac{a}{t^{2}}\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}(0,\infty)}

\displaystyle=\Big\langle\chi_{1,t}f,L_{\mu,2t}(\chi_{1,t}f)\Big\rangle_{L^{2}(0,2t)}+\Big\langle\chi_{2,t}f,\widetilde{L}_{\mu,t}(\chi_{2,t}f)\Big\rangle_{L^{2}(t,\infty)}

where
L_{\mu,2t}
is defined in Section
2.2
and
\widetilde{L}_{\mu,t}
is the self-adjoint operator in
L^{2}(t,\infty)

defined as the Friedrichs extension of

f\mapsto-f^{\prime\prime}+V_{\mu}f,\quad f\in C^{\infty}_{c}(t,\infty).

Then the min-max principle implies that for any
t>0
one has the inequality

\inf\mathrm{spec}_{\mathrm{ess}}L_{\mu}\geq\inf\mathrm{spec}_{\mathrm{ess}}(L_{\mu,2t}\oplus\widetilde{L}_{\mu,t})-\frac{a}{t^{2}}.

Using the fact that
L_{\mu,2t}
has compact resolvent, so
\mathrm{spec}_{\mathrm{ess}}L_{\mu,2t}=\emptyset
, this reduces to

\displaystyle\inf\mathrm{spec}_{\mathrm{ess}}L_{\mu}

\displaystyle\geq\inf\mathrm{spec}_{\mathrm{ess}}\widetilde{L}_{\mu,t}-\frac{a}{t^{2}}\geq\inf\mathrm{spec}\,\widetilde{L}_{\mu,t}-\frac{a}{t^{2}}\geq\inf_{s>t}V_{\mu}(s)-\frac{a}{t^{2}}\text{ for all }t>0.

For
t\to\infty
the right-hand side converges to
0
, which gives the sought lower bound.
∎

By combining the two preceding assertions we obtain:

Corollary 22
.

\mathrm{spec}_{\mathrm{ess}}L_{\mu}=[0,\infty)
.

Having determined the essential spectrum, we now turn to the discrete spectrum of
L_{\mu}
.

Lemma 23
.

The operator
L_{\mu}
has infinitely many negative eigenvalues.

Proof.

In view of the specific form of
V_{\mu}
one can choose
R>0
and
c>0
such that for all
s>R
it holds
V_{\mu}(s)\leq-c/s^{q}
. Now pick any
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(0,\infty)
with
\mathrm{supp}\,\varphi\subset(R,2R)
and
\|\varphi\|_{L^{2}(0,\infty)}=1
, and for any
t>1
consider the functions

\varphi_{t}:\ s\mapsto\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{t}}\varphi\left(\frac{s}{t}\right),

then
\varphi_{t}\in C^{\infty}_{c}(0,\infty)
with
\|\varphi_{t}\|_{L^{2}(0,\infty)}=1
and
\mathrm{supp}\,\varphi_{t}\subset(tR,2tR)
. One estimates

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\big|\varphi_{t}^{\prime}(s)\big|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\dfrac{1}{t^{3}}\Big|\varphi^{\prime}\Big(\dfrac{s}{t}\Big)\Big|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s=\dfrac{1}{t^{2}}\underbrace{\int_{0}^{\infty}\big|\varphi^{\prime}(s)\big|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s}_{=:a>0}\equiv\dfrac{a}{t^{2}},

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}V_{\mu}(s)\big|\varphi_{t}(s)\big|^{2}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{t}\int_{tR}^{2tR}V_{\mu}(s)\Big|\varphi\Big(\dfrac{s}{t}\Big)\Big|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s\leq\dfrac{1}{t}\int\limits_{tR}^{2tR}\bigg(-\dfrac{c}{|s|^{q}}\bigg)\Big|\varphi\Big(\dfrac{s}{t}\Big)\Big|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=-\dfrac{c}{t^{q}}\underbrace{\int_{R}^{2R}\dfrac{\big|\varphi(y)\big|^{2}}{|y|^{q}}\,\mathrm{d}y}_{=:b>0}=-\dfrac{bc}{t^{q}}.

As
q<2
, one can choose
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
sufficiently large to have

\displaystyle\langle\varphi_{t},L_{\mu}\varphi_{t}\rangle_{L^{2}(0,\infty)}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\Big(\big|\varphi_{t}^{\prime}(s)\big|^{2}+V_{\mu}(s)\big|\varphi_{t}(s)\big|^{2}\Big)\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq\dfrac{a}{t^{2}}-\dfrac{bc}{t^{q}}=\dfrac{a-bct^{2-q}}{t^{2}}<0\text{ for all }t\geq n_{0}.

\psi_{n}:=\varphi_{2^{n}}

\psi_{n}

\langle\psi_{m},L_{\mu}\psi_{n}\rangle_{L^{2}(0,\infty)}=0\text{ for $m\neq n$},\quad a_{n}:=\langle\psi_{n},L_{\mu}\psi_{n}\rangle<0.

N\in\mathbb{N}

F:=\mathrm{span}\{\psi_{n_{0}+1},\dots,\psi_{n_{0}+N}\}

Let
\psi\in F

\displaystyle\psi

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{N}\xi_{k}\psi_{n_{0}+k},\quad\xi=(\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{N})\in\mathbb{R}^{N},\qquad\|\psi\|^{2}_{L^{2}(0,\infty)}=\sum_{k=1}^{N}|\xi_{n}|^{2},

\displaystyle\langle\psi,L_{\mu}\psi\rangle_{L^{2}(0,\infty)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k,k^{\prime}=1}^{N}\langle\psi_{n_{0}+k},L_{\mu}\psi_{n_{0}+k^{\prime}}\rangle_{L^{2}(0,\infty)}\xi_{k}\xi_{k^{\prime}}=\sum_{k=1}^{N}a_{n_{0}+k}|\xi_{k}|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\underbrace{\max\{a_{n_{0}+1},\dots,a_{n_{0}+N}\}}_{=:b_{N}<0}\sum_{k=1}^{N}|\xi^{2}_{k}|=b_{N}\|\psi\|^{2}_{L^{2}(0,\infty)}.

and the min-max principle implies that

has at least

As

∎

This is a rather standard property of second order ordinary differential operators

[

the space of solutions to (

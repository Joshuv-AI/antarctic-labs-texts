Evaluation of the effects of indomethacin tocolytic treatment on fetal heart using modified myocardial performance index (Mod-MPI)
Authors: Barış Boza, Fırat Ersan, Verda Alpay
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundIndomethacin is one of the most effective and widely used tocolytic agents for treating threatened preterm labor and prolonging pregnancy. Nonetheless, data on its fetal effects, particularly regarding cardiac functions, remain limited. The primary aim of this study was to evaluate the effects of indomethacin treatment on fetal cardiac structures, the modified myocardial performance index (Mod-MPI), and hemodynamic parameters to establish evidence-based monitoring recommendations in cases of threatened preterm labor.MethodsIn this prospective study of 114 pregnant women, 57 cases diagnosed with threatened preterm birth received indomethacin tocolytic therapy, while 57 controls had uneventful pregnancies. Demographic characteristics, perinatal outcomes, Doppler measurements, cardiac structural measurements, Mod-MPI, and tricuspid regurgitation were compared between groups.ResultsDoppler analysis revealed no significant differences except for umbilical artery pulsatility index, which was elevated in the indomethacin group (0.941 ± 0.160 vs. 0.869 ± 0.171, p = 0.023) due to vasoconstrictive effects from prostaglandin synthesis inhibition. Isovolumetric contraction and relaxation times were lower in the indomethacin group (42.3 ± 0.5 vs. 47.2 ± 2.7, p = 0.020; 49.3 ± 1.9 vs. 48.2 ± 1.8, p = 0.030). However, Mod-MPI did not differ significantly between the groups (0.464 ± 0.11 vs. 0.44 ± 0.09, p = 0.349). Although tricuspid regurgitation was observed in 14 of 57 fetuses (24.6%) exposed to indomethacin, perinatal outcomes were not significantly different.ConclusionsIndomethacin treatment does not negatively affect fetal cardiac function or Mod-MPI. When tricuspid regurgitation occurs, increased monitoring is recommended rather than treatment cessation if other parameters remain stable.

Introduction

Preterm birth is defined as regular uterine contractions that start before 37 gestational weeks and lead to cervical changes. Preterm birth is responsible for the vast majority of neonatal morbidity and mortality and affects 11% of all pregnancies worldwide [1]. Preterm birth and prematurity are global health problems that cause respiratory, gastrointestinal complications, as well as long-term neurodevelopmental sequelae in childhood and adulthood, and increased healthcare costs [2]. Despite the accumulated amount of research on the treatment and prevention of this issue, the incidence of preterm birth continues to increase, particularly in certain populations, highlighting the need for studies to understand this clinical condition and create treatment modalities has increased [3]. The etiopathogenesis of preterm labour involves multiple factors, including maternal, fetal, and environmental factors, that lead through several common pathways to regular uterine contractions with cervical changes. These pathways encompass inflammatory processes, hormonal changes, mechanical factors, and vascular alterations that collectively trigger the cascade leading to preterm delivery [4]. The clinical presentation of threatened preterm labour can range from minimal uterine contractions without cervical changes to severe uterine contractions with evident cervical dilatation and effacement, as the underlying pathophysiology is variable [5]. Patients whose symptoms begin at an early gestational age tend to have a more severe clinical course, and the likelihood of treatment failure is higher in these groups. Closer monitoring and different therapeutic approaches are required for these patients compared to those whose symptoms begin in later gestational weeks. Since threatened preterm birth has variable pathophysiological mechanisms, understanding these underlying possible processes is crucial for optimizing treatment selections.

Significant evolution has occurred in the development of tocolytic therapy since the mid-20th century, when the first attempts were performed to prevent preterm birth, until today, when evidence-based treatment modalities have been developed through a growing understanding of the physiology and pharmacology of uterine contractions [4]. The primary aim of tocolytic therapy is not to prevent preterm delivery permanently, but rather to delay delivery sufficiently to allow an opportunity window for corticosteroids administration for fetal lung maturation and for transferring the mother to a tertiary center for neonatal care [6]. Recent systematic reviews and meta-analyses have broadened our knowledge about the effectiveness and safety of different tocolytic agents. These trials have demonstrated that the superiority of treatment options can vary based on clinical scenarios [7]. A meta-analysis conducted by Xiong et al. in 2022 included 44 randomized controlled trials and analyzed the clinical records of 6939 patients, showing that indomethacin was the most effective tocolytic for postponing delivery between 48 h and 7 days, while nifedipine represented the safest profile [8].

Indomethacin is a potent nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drug that exerts its tocolytic efficacy by inhibiting cyclooxygenase enzymes, thereby reducing the production of prostaglandins E2 and F2α [9]. Prostaglandins play a crucial role in triggering and sustaining the labor process by activating myometrial contractility and promoting cervical ripening. The inhibition of the prostaglandin synthesis pathway by indomethacin blocks myometrial activity, thereby prolonging pregnancy [10].

The functions of the fetal cardiovascular system are markedly different in the prenatal period compared to the postnatal period, due to significant environmental and physiological differences between these two stages, in order to maintain the oxygen and nutrient requirements of the fetus [11]. The ductus arteriosus, which is a crucial component of fetal cardiovascular circulation, functions as a conduit that carries blood from the right ventricle to the systemic circulation, bypassing fluid-filled lungs. The patency of the ductus arteriosus is maintained by prostaglandin E2, which prevents premature closure through its smooth muscle relaxation effect. Indomethacin can cause premature ductal constriction in fetal life by inhibiting prostaglandin synthesis. Due to this effect, the use of indomethacin treatment for threatened preterm labor is restricted to up to 32 weeks of gestation [12].

Advanced ultrasonographic imaging techniques and the development of specialized modern equipment for fetal cardiac evaluation have significantly improved the assessment of fetal cardiac structure and function [13]. The myocardial performance index, first defined by Tei and subsequently adopted for fetal applications, can be used to assess the combined diastolic and systolic functions of the fetal heart and can also be measured reliably during pregnancy [14]. Tricuspid regurgitation during fetal life may indicate increased right ventricular afterload, tricuspid valve pathology, or alterations in ventricular function [15]. In case of indomethacin use, tricuspid regurgitation is a commonly encountered finding that may occur as a result of ductal constriction and hemodynamic dysfunction of the right heart due to the drug’s effects. The clinical importance of this finding remains a controversial issue, with some guidelines suggesting benign outcomes while other studies have demonstrated potential adverse effects [16].

Although many comprehensive studies were conducted on the employment of indomethacin therapy during pregnancy for several decades, there is still a scarcity of information regarding the drug’s effect on fetal cardiovascular physiology and the clinical importance of monitoring parameters [17]. Previous studies have primarily focused on the side effects of prostaglandin inhibition, specifically ductal constriction and oligohydramnios, rather than its potential systemic effects. As a result of this limited approach, our experience remained restricted regarding whether the drug may lead to more complex and systemic adverse effects.

In this study, we compared the modified myocardial performance index (Mod-MPI) between pregnant women treated with indomethacin and those with normal pregnancies. The primary aim of this study was to evaluate the effects of indomethacin treatment for threatened preterm birth on fetal cardiac structure and function, as measured by the Mod MPI, and to determine whether this drug affects the fetal heart. The results of this study may significantly contribute to clinical practice regarding indomethacin treatment, particularly in relation to monitoring procedures and patient counseling.

Materials and methods

This study was designed as a prospective observational study and conducted at a tertiary maternal-fetal medicine unit from January 2025 to July 2025. Ethical approval was obtained from the local Institutional Review Board and Ethics Committee (approval number: 2025/08), and the study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki. Written informed consent was obtained from all patients after they were fully informed about the study procedures, potential risks and benefits, and their rights as participants.

Pregnant women with threatened preterm labour between 28 and 31 weeks of gestation were included in the case group. Threatened preterm birth was defined as regular uterine contractions (at least 4 contractions in 20 min or 8 contractions in 60 min) with cervical length less than 25 mm by transvaginal ultrasonography [7]. Patients who were between 18 and 40 years of age, had a singleton pregnancy between 28 and 31 gestational weeks with a diagnosis of threatened preterm labor, and were treated with indomethacin as a tocolytic agent were considered eligible for the study. Additional inclusion criteria required singleton pregnancies with confirmed gestational age based on first-trimester ultrasound biometry or reliable last menstrual period dating, and the absence of ruptured membranes confirmed by sterile speculum examination and negative pooling. Exclusion criteria were designed to eliminate conditions that could confound the assessment of indomethacin effects or pose additional risks to maternal or fetal health. Women with multiple pregnancies, fetuses with growth restriction and congenital anomalies detected by ultrasonographic examination, cases with rupture of the membranes, and those with systemic disorders such as diabetes mellitus, chronic hypertension, preeclampsia, autoimmune disorders, and systemic infection were excluded from the study. Maternal hypersensitivity to nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs, active peptic ulcer disease, significant renal impairment, severe hepatic dysfunction, or bleeding disorders were considered contraindications to indomethacin treatment. Fetal growth restriction, oligohydramnios, and signs of fetal compromise, including abnormal cardiotocography patterns or abnormal umbilical artery Doppler studies, were defined as fetal contraindications for indomethacin therapy [18]. The control group consisted of gestational age-matched women with uneventful pregnancies who presented to our hospital during the same time period. A total of 114 patients who met the study criteria were included in the study. Fifty-seven of these 114 patients with a diagnosis of threatened preterm birth who received indomethacin tocolytic therapy were assigned to the case group, while the remaining 57 patients with no risk factors and normal pregnancies were assigned to the control group.

Indomethacin treatment was initiated with a loading dose of 100 mg administered rectally, followed by a maintenance dose of 25 mg orally every 6 h, in accordance with standard protocols. The total length of treatment was restricted to 48–72 h to reduce the risk of fetal complications and to allow sufficient time for the administration of antenatal corticosteroids to promote fetal lung maturation [19].

All ultrasonographic examinations were performed by experienced maternal-fetal medicine specialists (B.B., VA, and FE) using Arietta 850 system (Hitachi Medical Corporation, Tokyo, Japan), a machine equipped with a 3.5 mHz transducer and advanced Doppler capability, and appropriate settings for fetal echocardiographic evaluation. Furthermore, the reproducibility of measurements was assessed through inter-observer and intra-observer reliability studies, with intraclass correlation coefficients (ICC) exceeding 0.90, which is considered excellent reliability and acceptable for clinical use (ICC < 0.50: poor, 0.50–0.75: moderate, 0.75–0.90: good, > 0.90: excellent).

A detailed fetal echocardiographic assessment was performed according to standard guidelines to identify parameters that could be affected by indomethacin treatment [13]. This examination consisted of an assessment of the anatomic structures of the heart, ventricular function, and blood flow of the ductus arteriosus. In addition, the presence and severity of tricuspid regurgitation were systematically evaluated. Right and left ventricular free wall thickness, as well as interventricular septal thickness, were measured using echocardiography in the lateral four-chamber view. An interventricular septal thickness greater than 5 mm was considered as the threshold level for the diagnosis of hypertrophic cardiomyopathy. Cardiac and thoracic circumferences were evaluated using the trace method from an apical four-chamber. The cardiothoracic ratio was calculated using the formula: cardiac area/thoracic area (mm²/mm²). A value of between < 0.35 was considered the normal reference range for the cardio-thoracic ratio [20]. To assess both systolic and diastolic function of the fetal heart, the modified myocardial performance index (Mod-MPI), as described by Hernandez-Andrade et al., was used [21]. For this technique, an apical four-chamber view of the heart was acquired, and a pulse Doppler sample gate was placed immediately beneath the aorta and above the mitral valve. After obtaining at least three consecutive Doppler traces on the screen, the most suitable trace was selected for the measurement of Mod-MPI. For the calculation of Mod-MPI, Doppler parameters obtained from this pulse wave Doppler method, including isovolumetric contraction time (IVCT), isovolumetric relaxation time (IVRT), and ejection time (ET), were used (Fig. 1). Mod-MPI was calculated using the formula (IVCT + IVRT)/ET [21]. This parameter provides an objective and comprehensive assessment of both systolic and diastolic cardiac functions. According to the evaluation of 557 pregnant women conducted by Hernandez-Andrade et al., the mean Mod-MPI was measured 0.35 in mid-pregnancy and 0.37 in late pregnancy, demonstrating a slight increase with advancing gestation [21]. Doppler parameters of fetuses, including umbilical artery pulsatility index (PI), middle cerebral artery PI, and bilateral uterine artery PI, were examined using established techniques. Fetal Doppler assessment was performed in accordance with international guidelines. Measurements were obtained during periods without fetal movement or fetal breathing. The appropriate anatomical segment of each vessel was identified, the sample volume was placed within the vessel lumen, and the angle of insonation was kept below 30°. For each vessel, at least three consecutive and uniform waveforms were recorded, and parameters such as peak were calculated automatically by the system [22]. All echocardiographic and Doppler parameters of the patients were assessed 48 h after the initial diagnosis of threatened preterm labor, following the completion of indomethacin therapy. Demographic and perinatal outcome data for all participants were collected from patient records. Participant data, including demographic characteristics, perinatal outcomes, Doppler measurements (umbilical artery, middle cerebral artery and mean uterine artery), and detailed cardiac assessments such as structural parameters, Mod-MPI, and tricuspid regurgitation, were compared between the indomethacin and non-indomethacin groups.

Statistical analyses

All statistical analyses were performed using IBM SPSS Version 24.0 (Chicago, USA). Initially, the distribution of continuous data was examined for normality by evaluating histogram plots, kurtosis and skewness values, and conducting the Kolmogorov-Smirnov and Shapiro-Wilk tests. Continuous variables that were normally distributed were expressed as mean ± standard deviation and compared using Student’s t-test. Variables that were not normally distributed were expressed as median (minimum-maximum), and the Mann-Whitney U test was used for comparisons. Categorical variables were presented as percentages and counts and were compared using the Chi-Square Test. A p-value of less than 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Given that this is the first assessment of the relationship between Mod-MPI and indomethacin treatment, a post hoc power analysis was conducted using G*Power v3.1.9.6 to determine the required sample size. According to the results of our primary outcome, we achieved a medium effect size (Cohen’s d = 0.56). For a Student’s t-test with a power of 0.80 and an alpha level of 0.05, the estimated total sample size was 104 patients, with 52 patients in each group, respectively.

Results

A total of 114 pregnant women were recruited for the study, and the study population was subsequently divided into two groups: the indomethacin group and the control group. The indomethacin group consisted of 57 pregnant women diagnosed with threatened preterm labor who received indomethacin as tocolytic therapy, whereas the control group comprised 57 women with uneventful pregnancies.

The demographic characteristics of the patients are presented in Table 1. The demographic features of the study population showed a largely homogeneous distribution. Maternal age indicated no statistically significant difference between groups (30.82 ± 5.46, 29.84 ± 4.28 years, p = 0.621). In addition, no statistically significant difference was observed in terms of BMI among the groups (27.32 ±4.46, 28.8±4.21, p = 0.62). There was a statistically significant difference demonstrated in terms of parity (p = 0.002), while no statistically significant difference was found with respect to gravida (p = 0.660).

Gestational age at examination also showed no significant difference between the (29.03 ± 1.23 weeks vs. 29.2 ± 1.52 weeks, p = 0.094), indicating that both groups were assessed at comparable stages of pregnancy. This temporal equivalence is crucial for the validity of comparative analyses, as fetal parameters undergo continuous developmental changes throughout pregnancy, and significant differences in examination timing could confound the interpretation of observed differences in fetal measurements and outcomes. Gestational age at delivery was significantly lower in the indomethacin group (33.5 ± 4.45 weeks) compared to the control group (38.8 ± 1.19 weeks, p = 0.010), indicating the impact of the underlying pathophysiology that necessitated indomethacin intervention, and the clinical course associated with its use. Birth weight differences paralleled the gestational age at delivery findings, with the indomethacin group showing significantly lower birth weights (2215 ± 470 g) compared to controls (3334 ± 316 g, p = 0.010), compared to controls (p = 0.01).

Doppler analysis comparisons are shown in Table 2 and revealed no statistically significant differences between the groups, except for the umbilical artery PI. Although the umbilical artery PI was significantly higher in the indomethacin group (0.94 ± 0.160 and 0.86 ± 0.171, p = 0.023), no cases of fetal growth restriction were observed. Since the duration of indomethacin use is limited to 48 h and has no impact on fetal growth patterns, this finding did not reach clinical importance. Other Doppler parameters, such as the middle cerebral artery PI (1.75 ± 0.40, 1.82 ± 0.43, p = 0.35) and mean uterine artery PI (0.82 ± 0.19, 0.79 ± 0.20, p = 0.40), remained comparable between the two groups.

Assessment of fetal cardiac structures indicated no statistically significant differences in wall thickness measurements. Thickness of interventricular septum (4.147 ± 0.735, 4.202 ± 0.801 mm, p = 0.70), right ventricular free wall (4.07 ± 0.74, 4.13 ± 0.67 mm, p = 0.92), left ventricular free wall (4.23 ± 0.83, 4.32 ± 0.65 mm, p = 0.59) were comparable among study groups. Nevertheless, cardiothoracic ratio was observed statistically significantly higher in the indomethacin group (0.54 ± 0.03, 0.50 ± 0.08, p = 0.003) compared with the control group, indicating relative cardiac enlargement.

Among cardiac function parameters, isovolumetric contraction and isovolumetric relaxation time revealed significant differences; these parameters revealed lower values in the indomethacin group compared to the control group (42.3±10.50, 47.2±12.70, p = 0.020, 44±9.33, 48.2±11.87, p = 0.030). However, the Mod-MPI did not differ significantly between study groups (0.46 ± 0.11, 0.48 ± 0.05; p = 0.392) and remained within the normal range in both.

The pregnant women among participants diagnosed with threatened preterm birth and treated with indomethacin as a tocolytic were divided into two groups based on the presence of tricuspid regurgitation. Tricuspid regurgitation was observed in 14 of 57 fetuses (24.6%) exposed to indomethacin therapy. The demographic characteristics of patients exposed to indomethacin are demonstrated in Table 3. No statistically significant differences were observed in these parameters between the two groups.

Comparisons of Doppler parameters and anatomic cardiac structures and functions in patients treated with indomethacin are presented in Table 4. Doppler parameters including umbilical artery PI (0.97±0.16, 0.93±0.16, p = 0.410), middle cerebral artery PI (1.87±0.33, 1.71±0.43, p = 0.210), mean uterine artery PI (0.90±0.26, 0.80±0.16, p = 0.080) did not show statistical differences. No abnormalities were observed in either cardiac structural parameters or cardiac function. Thicknesses of the interventricular septum, right ventricular free wall, and left ventricular free wall did not reach statistical significance, and cardiothoracic ratio was also not statistically different (all p values > 0.050). In addition, no statistically significant association was detected between the mod-MPI and presence of tricuspid regurgitation (0.49 ± 0.13, 0.45 ± 0.10, p = 0.271). These findings provide strong evidence that indomethacin has no adverse effect on fetal cardiac function, even in the presence of tricuspid regurgitation.

Discussion

In this study, we evaluated the effects of indomethacin, used as a tocolytic in threatened preterm labor, on fetal cardiac function and the modified myocardial performance index (Mod-MPI), given its known side effects such as fetal ductal constriction and tricuspid regurgitation. Our results showed that indomethacin did not have a detrimental effect on fetal Mod-MPI.

Effective treatment of threatened preterm birth is of utmost importance because of the rising preterm birth rates and increased neonatal intensive care costs. Indomethacin is one of the most effective tocolytic treatments, and in this prospective study, the effects of indomethacin on fetal Doppler parameters and cardiac functions were examined. In a recent comparative study by Isenlik et al., the umbilical artery PI was significantly higher in the indomethacin group than in the control group [23]. Consistent with this study, in our cohort, only the umbilical artery PI significantly differed between fetuses exposed to indomethacin and those not exposed, and this effect was transient. This finding may be explained by the vasoconstrictive effect of indomethacin on the placental vascular bed, which is mediated by the inhibition of prostaglandin synthesis. On the other hand, other Doppler parameters, such as the mean uterine artery PI and middle cerebral artery PI, were comparable among the study groups. Based on these findings, the increased resistance of the umbilical artery in the indomethacin group did not reach clinical significance, and no clinically important effect of indomethacin on Doppler parameters was detected.

This study showed that the cardiothoracic ratio was not affected by indomethacin treatment and remained within the normal range (0.25–0.35), as reported in a recent study [24]. In a study evaluating the Mod-MPI, the normal reference range was established between 0.34 ± 0.05 and 0.42 ± 0.03, and a Mod-MPI > 0.55 was considered the cut-off for adverse perinatal outcomes [25]. Another study conducted by Kim and Ye reported normal Mod-MPI values of 0.53 ± 0.31 from mid-pregnancy to term (18–31 weeks) [14]. The aforementioned studies provide a reference framework for interpreting our results. Our findings regarding the Mod-MPI, an indicator of both diastolic and systolic fetal cardiac function, were consistent with the literature and remained within the normal range in both the indomethacin and control groups, with no significant differences between them. Moreover, although isovolumetric contraction and relaxation times were significantly shorter in the indomethacin group, suggesting a possible increase in fetal cardiac contractility and function, the Mod-MPI remained stable, within the normal range, and comparable between the two groups.

Freud et al. reported that non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drug (NSAID) therapy for Ebstein anomaly and severe tricuspid dysplasia provides an additional perspective for understanding the mechanism of tricuspid regurgitation associated with indomethacin exposure during pregnancy [26]. This study demonstrated that NSAIDs may reduce shunt physiology by inducing ductal constriction and may improve pulmonary regurgitation in fetuses with severe tricuspid valve abnormalities. These findings suggest that the relationship between indomethacin treatment and tricuspid valve function may be more complex than previously assumed and is not invariably associated with adverse outcomes [26]. Additionally, Respondek et al. revealed that tricuspid regurgitation related to indomethacin exposure during fetal life is relatively common, although often reversible [16]. Our findings are consistent with this study, as tricuspid regurgitation was detected in 14 of 57 fetuses (24.6%) in our cohort. However, despite these important findings, no significant association was identified between Mod-MPI and structural cardiac parameters or the presence of tricuspid regurgitation. These findings have important implications for clinical management and patient counseling, suggesting that mild to moderate tricuspid regurgitation alone may not warrant discontinuation of indomethacin therapy or initiation of unnecessary intensive interventions. Current guidelines emphasize the importance of close surveillance of ductal blood flow and careful evaluation of the tricuspid valve during indomethacin treatment [27]. In line with these recommendations, our data indicate that when tricuspid regurgitation occurs in isolation, increasing the frequency of follow-up, rather than immediately terminating treatment, may be appropriate, particularly given the clinical benefits of tocolytic therapy when other parameters remain stable.

In another study by Hunter et al., it was proposed that tricuspid regurgitation may result from the functional effects of indomethacin on the tricuspid valve, and that this finding may reflect a treatment response rather than an adverse drug effect [7]. Consistent with these findings, our study found no significant association between the presence of tricuspid regurgitation and clinical outcomes, including timing of delivery, birth weight, and cardiac function. No adverse events were observed, further supporting the safety of indomethacin as a tocolytic agent.

The clinical importance of this study may be better understood when our results are interpreted in the context of comparative research on tocolytic treatment, particularly in cases of threatened preterm birth before 32 weeks of gestation. In a systematic review and network meta-analysis including 44 randomized controlled trials involving 6939 patients conducted by Xiong et al. indomethacin was observed the most effective treatment option for delaying delivery, followed by nifedipine, however nifedipine was defined as the safest intervention with lowest risk of adverse effects [8]. According to the results of this study, although indomethacin appeared to be more effective, nifedipine was found to be safer.

Several studies have suggested that indomethacin can be used as a tocolytic therapy before 32 weeks of gestation [28]. However, indomethacin is associated with various fetal and neonatal adverse effects. Fetal side effects include ductal constriction, tricuspid regurgitation—which can lead to neonatal pulmonary hypertension—and oligohydramnios [29]. Although these adverse effects are reported more frequently with prolonged use beyond 48 h and after 32 weeks of gestation, fetal side effects have also been observed at earlier gestational ages [30]. Due to these potential fetal risks, some studies recommend the use of indomethacin before 32 weeks with close monitoring through fetal echocardiography to assess for ductal constriction [31]. Neonatal complications reported with in utero exposure include bronchopulmonary dysplasia, necrotizing enterocolitis, patent ductus arteriosus, periventricular leukomalacia, and intraventricular hemorrhage [19]. Although these associations remain controversial, a meta-analysis of 27 observational studies found no statistically significant differences between exposed and unexposed neonates in terms of respiratory distress syndrome, patent ductus arteriosus, neonatal mortality, neonatal sepsis, bronchopulmonary dysplasia, or intraventricular hemorrhage [32], and our study also demonstrated that indomethacin does not adversely affect fetal cardiac function. Taken together, while indomethacin is an effective tocolytic agent, its potential fetal and neonatal risks necessitate careful risk–benefit assessment, particularly when used beyond 30–32 weeks of gestation, with close monitoring to minimize adverse outcomes.

The findings of our study have the potential to inform clinical practice and guide the management of threatened preterm births treated with indomethacin tocolysis. The observed hemodynamic alterations in umbilical artery blood flow highlight the need for close Doppler surveillance to detect increases in placental vascular resistance. Likewise, the cardiac findings support the routine use of detailed fetal echocardiographic at treatment initiation and during follow-up, with particular attention to global cardiac function and the evaluation of tricuspid regurgitation. Notably, the detection of tricuspid regurgitation should prompt an increase in monitoring frequency rather than discontinuation of therapy, particularly when other parameters remain stable.

Our study has several strengths and weaknesses. The single-center design limits the generalizability of the findings to broader populations, which constitutes a methodological weakness. Another limitation of our study is that, since the Mod-MPI was being evaluated for the first time, a post-hoc power analysis was performed, which resulted in a medium effect size (Cohen’s d = 0.54). However, the strengths of our study include its prospective design, the comprehensive fetal assessments performed, and the use of detailed ultrasonographic examinations performed by experienced operators. Unlike previous research, this is the first to evaluate the effect of indomethacin therapy for threatened preterm birth on fetal cardiac function using the Mod-MPI. By sharing our clinical experience, we aimed not only to contribute to the understanding of the hemodynamic effects of indomethacin but also to provide reassurance regarding its safety profile. This novelty represents the primary strength of our study.

Conclusions

As a result of our study, indomethacin may be a safe and effective treatment option for threatened preterm birth to delay delivery especially before 30–32 weeks and in terms of cardiac parameters. In conclusion, this study demonstrates that indomethacin treatment does not adversely affect fetal cardiac function or Mod-MPI, even in the presence of tricuspid regurgitation. In addition, our findings may provide evidence that indomethacin may be safely used for the treatment of preterm birth, which remains a global health issue. However, further multicenter studies with larger sample sizes are required to obtain stronger evidence before recommending the routine assessment of Mod-MPI for monitoring the effects of indomethacin treatment.

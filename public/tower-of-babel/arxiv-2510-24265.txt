Abstract
. Generative AI (GenAI) tools are increasingly being adopted in software development as productivity aids. However, evidence regarding where and when these tools actually enhance productivity is unclear. In this paper, we investigate how GenAI adoption affects different dimensions of developer productivity. We surveyed 415 software practitioners to capture their perceptions of productivity changes associated with AI-assisted development using the SPACE framework — S atisfaction and well-being, P erformance, A ctivity, C ommunication and collaboration, and E fficiency and flow. Our results, disaggregated by frequency of AI usage, reveal limited overall productivity change, highlighting the productivity paradox in which developers become faster but do not necessarily create better software or feel more fulfilled.



1.
Introduction

With the rapid growth of Generative AI (GenAI) tools across diverse domains, software development has emerged as one of the earliest and direct adopters
(
Zakharov et al. 2025
)
. Fueled by the AI hype, GenAI tools have quickly spread in developer communities
(
Nguyen-Duc et al. 2025
)
. As per the 2025 Stack Overflow Developer Survey, nearly 84% of developers already use or plan to use GenAI tools
(
Shimel 2025
)
. This rapid adoption is driven by a widespread belief that GenAI can boost productivity
(
Yu 2025
)
, yet it remains to be seen whether GenAI

genuinely make development faster, or does it merely create a spurious sense of progress?

The evidence of GenAI’s impact on productivity remains mixed. While GenAI tools such as GitHub Copilot and ChatGPT have been reported to automate routine tasks and improve efficiency
(
Peng et al. 2023
;
Ziegler et al. 2022
)
,
Tong 2025
found that developers completed tasks 19% slower when using AI, and
Vaithilingam et al. 2022
observed higher task-failure rates and no significant improvement in completion time. Such inconsistencies likely arise because existing studies often scope their study to particular development activities or narrow productivity metrics rather than taking a comprehensive view.

Measuring productivity in software development is not a simple task, given its multifaceted nature
(
Forsgren et al. 2021
)
. It is not only about the volume of output, but also includes work quality, psychological well-being, team collaboration, uninterrupted focus, and satisfaction with work
(
Storey et al. 2019
;
Forsgren et al. 2021
;
Rodríguez et al. 2012
)
. Without examining how GenAI influences these dimensions of productivity, organizations may overestimate its benefits and overlook emerging risks. To fill this gap, we ask

RQ. How does GenAI adoption affect developer productivity across multiple dimensions?

We address this research question by employing the multidimensional
SPACE
productivity framework
(
Forsgren et al. 2021
)
as our analytical lens to inform a large-scale survey of 415 professional developers. According to our results, developers do not perceive that GenAI meaningfully changes productivity across the five
SPACE
dimensions. Compared to developers who use GenAI less frequently, frequent genAI users reported slightly higher perceived improvements in
Efficiency and Flow
and
Satisfaction and Well-being
, but these gains did not extend to
Performance
,
Activity
, or
Communication and Collaboration
.

Our study situates developers’ experiences with GenAI within the
SPACE
framework to provide emerging empirical results that raise important discussion questions about the meaning of being “fast,” the shifting role of collaboration, and the potential for spurious forms of productivity in AI-mediated software development.

2.
Productivity Frameworks

Developer productivity is a complex, multifaceted concept that has long been studied by both academics and practitioners
(
Peng et al. 2023
)
. Traditional metrics such as lines of code (LOC) or non-comment lines of code
(
Mockus et al. 2002
)
, commit counts
(
Vasilescu et al. 2015
)
, and task completion rates
(
Zhou and Mockus 2010
)
capture only narrow aspects of work and can be misleading or easily gamed. For example, the number of commits is often inflated by shallow or trivial changes
(
Oliveira et al. 2020
)
, and LOC measures may reward verbosity rather than efficiency. Speed and volume of activity alone do not present a complete picture of productivity. Other forms of work—such as design, problem solving, and knowledge sharing—are equally important
(
Forsgren et al. 2021
)
, as are software quality, impact, and delivery speed
(
Storey et al. 2022
)
.

Emerging research, therefore, is investigating multi-dimensional productivity measurement frameworks.

One such framework is the Developer Experience (DevEx) framework
(
Noda et al. 2023
)
, which highlights feedback loops, cognitive load, and flow state as key drivers of developer effectiveness. At the system level, the DORA (DevOps Research and Assessment) metrics
(
Wilkes et al. 2023
)
operationalize software delivery performance through deployment frequency, lead time for changes, mean time to recovery, and change failure rate. While these frameworks broaden the perspective beyond code quantity, they primarily address specific contexts of individual experience in the case of DevEx, and delivery throughput in the case of DORA.

Forsgren et al. 2021
proposed the SPACE framework, a comprehensive model that conceptualizes productivity as a combination of interpersonal and technical dimensions. The framework emphasizes that productivity arises from the interplay among human, technical, and organizational factors. For instance, high
activity
(e.g., frequent commits) does not necessarily indicate improved
performance
unless accompanied by quality, sustained satisfaction, collaboration, and flow. . In this study, we adopt the SPACE framework as an analytical lens to examine how GenAI adoption influences developer productivity across its five dimensions:
S
atisfaction and well-being,
P
erformance,
A
ctivity,
C
ommunication and collaboration, and
E
fficiency and flow.

Satisfaction and well-being
capture how fulfilled, motivated, and supported developers feel in their work.
Performance
represents the quality and impact of outcomes, such as software reliability, feature completeness, or user satisfaction.
Activity
refers to the volume of work performed, such as commits and code reviews.
Communication and collaboration
refer to how developers communicate and work together.
Efficiency and flow
describe how smoothly and continuously work proceeds, including the ability to maintain focus and minimize interruptions.

3.
Research Method

To capture developers’ perceptions of productivity across multiple dimensions, we employed a survey-based approach. Surveys enable large-scale data collection while preserving subjective perspectives—essential when assessing satisfaction, collaboration, and efficiency. Grounded in the SPACE framework
(
Forsgren et al. 2021
)
, our design adapts validated measures from prior work to the GenAI context.

3.1.
Survey Design

We designed our survey instrument to address the five dimensions of the SPACE framework for productivity measurement
(
Forsgren et al. 2021
)
. Since at the time of the survey design, there was no validated survey questionnaire for SPACE, we adapted the questionnaires from
Meyer et al. 2014
;
Trinkenreich et al. 2024
;
Casic and Panselina 2025
, which measured developer satisfaction, activity, performance, collaboration, and efficiency. The adaptations were made to reflect the genAI context. See the complete questionnaire in the supplementary material
(
Anonym 2025
)
. Respondents were offered the option to enter a $50 raffle for every 50 responses. The protocol was approved by our university’s IRB.

Survey Questions
: We first asked questions about participants’ demographics, including their gender, professional background (role, years of experience, seniority level), and organization size. We also collected information about genAI-usage experience (frequency of use in software development, adapted from
(
Research [n. d.]
)
) to assess its perceived impact on productivity.
The rest of the survey focused on each of the dimensions in the SPACE framework:

Satisfaction
and well-being
measures how fulfilled, motivated, and supported developers feel in their work and team
(
Forsgren et al. 2021
)
. Four statements assessed (1) workload manageability, (2) physical and mental exhaustion, (3) engagement, and (4) job security
(
Trinkenreich et al. 2024
;
Casic and Panselina 2025
)
.

Performance
captures perceived outcomes of the development process, such as velocity
(
Forsgren et al. 2021
)
. We used three items adapted from
Meyer et al. 2014
to assess the impact of AI adoption on: (1) number of lines of code changed per day, (2) proportion of test cases passed, and (3) number of API methods learned.

Activity

presents the volume of work performed
(
Forsgren et al. 2021
)
, measured through seven statements adapted from
Meyer et al. 2014
: (1) number of commits, (2) time spent writing code, (3) number of test cases, (4) time spent doing work-related browsing, (5) number of closed work items, (6) number of code reviews, and (7) time spent reviewing code.

Communication
and collaboration
describes how developers interact and coordinate with others
(
Forsgren et al. 2021
)
. We adapted four more statements from
Meyer et al. 2014
: (1) number of meetings attended, (2) time spent in meetings, (3) number of work-related emails written, and (4) time spent responding to emails.

Efficiency
and flow
captures making progress with minimal interruptions
(
Forsgren et al. 2021
)
. We adapted two statements from
Meyer et al. 2014
to assess this dimension: (1) time spent on each work item, and (2) time spent on browsing the web for personal matters during work.

Finally, we added an open-ended question inviting participants to share reflections on how AI adoption has influenced their work. The survey took approximately 5–8 minutes to complete, and we held a raffle for a $50 gift card, which participants could enter by sharing their email.

3.1.1.
Sandbox and Pilot Survey.

We sandboxed the survey with five individuals with software engineering research experience, iteratively refining it until no further concerns arose. We then piloted with two professionals: one from a multinational organization and one from a U.S. national lab; both found the survey clear, easy to follow, and relevant to assessing productivity.

3.1.2.
Participant Recruitment.

Following ethical data collection guidelines, we recruited participants from diverse technical backgrounds, seniority levels, organization sizes, and domains. Participants were recruited from 56 OSS communities, including organizational repositories (e.g., IBM, Oracle, Google, Adobe), widely-used infrastructure, AI projects, and data science communities (e.g., JetBrains, PyTorch). Participants received email invitations with study details and consent information. Responses were anonymized per GDPR and IRB approval. This two-week survey followed established SE research practices
(
Feng et al. 2025b
;
Feng et al. 2025a
;
Feng et al. 2022
)
.

We received 688 responses. After removing invalid entries, we kept 415 responses. Most participants worked in full-stack (36.87%), backend (16.87%), or data/ML roles (15.42%). A majority identified as men (90.6%), over half worked at large or extra-large organizations (57.83%), and most (82.17%) had more than five years of experience. See more details in the supplementary document
(
Anonym 2025
)
.

3.2.
Data Analysis

We conducted a descriptive analysis to explore how productivity perceptions vary by GenAI usage frequency. We categorized participants based on their response to
“how often do you use AI tools for your software development work?”

We used stacked bar charts to examine item-level Likert-scale response trends and violin plots to visualize distribution patterns across the two groups. This analysis focused on the five dimensions of the SPACE framework: Satisfaction and well-being (S1–S4), Performance (P1–P3), Activity (A1–A7), Communication and collaboration (C1–C4), and Efficiency and flow (E1–E2). 5.
Related Work

As GenAI tools are increasingly integrated into software development workflows, recent studies have highlighted productivity gains through faster task completion. For example,
Dohmke et al. 2023
reported that developers using GitHub Copilot completed programming tasks up to 55.8% faster. In a randomized controlled trial with 96 full-time Google engineers,
Paradis et al. 2025
found that AI assistance reduced time spent on a complex, enterprise-grade coding task by approximately 21%. Additionally,
Rodriguez 2023
found that GenAI not only accelerated task completion but also streamlined code review processes by suggesting actionable improvements and reducing reviewer effort.

However, recent literature suggests that GenAI’s productivity benefits may be overstated. AI-generated code can be inconsistent or incorrect, often requiring developers to re-prompt, validate, and debug the output, which then interrupts their workflow and reduces overall efficiency
(
Miller et al. 2025
;
Becker et al. 2025
)
.
Kuhail et al. 2024
found that over-reliance on AI assistance may erode developers’ coding proficiency and critical thinking, while reducing peer interaction, such as asking fewer questions, sharing fewer solutions, and engaging less in coordination
(
Tobisch and Matthes 2025
;
Khojah et al. 2024
;
Qiu et al. 2025
)
. Moreover, the 2025 Stack Overflow Survey further reported that only 29% of developers trusted AI accuracy and 66% spent more time debugging than expected
(
Shimel 2025
)
.

These studies offer fragmented views of GenAI’s impact on software development, focusing on programming tasks and short-term efficiency gains while overlooking broader productivity gains. This paper presents a comprehensive analysis of developer productivity in GenAI-mediated development, grounded in the multi-dimensional
SPACE
framework.

6.
Discussion: The Fast and the Spurious

This study captures developers’ perceptions of GenAI’s impact on productivity using self-reported survey data. While such perceptions may not fully align with objective productivity outcomes, they reveal key patterns and tensions worth unpacking.

Reconsidering the “Fast” in Productivity.
GenAI tools are reshaping how developers engage with their work and accelerating task execution
(
Peng et al. 2023
)
. However, our findings suggest that this
fast
pace may be more complex than it appears. Across all five SPACE dimensions, we observed minimal shifts in perceived productivity between frequent and non-frequent GenAI users, with most responses falling within the “no-change” range. Developers also mentioned trading manual coding time for reviewing, validating, and integrating AI-generated outputs. This trade-off raises a broader question about the nature of acceleration itself:

Does faster task completion truly represent progress, or merely shift effort without improving outcomes?

Sacrificing Collaboration.
Communication and collaboration stand out as the dimension where GenAI shows little benefit. Nearly two-thirds of participants reported no change or negative effects, regardless of their AI usage frequency. This finding raises important questions: if GenAI’s benefits remain largely personal, how can teams maintain shared understanding, coordination, and trust? This observation connects to a long-standing concern in SE; collaboration has always been central to productive development.
As new technologies emerge, they should not only accelerate individual work but also reinforce the collective fabric of software practice. This raises the question: is the progression of technology ultimately driving us toward deeper individual silos, rather than fostering mutual awareness and teamwork?

Beyond Fast Pace to Understand the Spurious.
Viewing our results through the SPACE framework offers a nuanced understanding of productivity in AI-mediated development. While the framework offers a structured lens across five dimensions of productivity, it cannot fully capture the evolving and sometimes ambiguous nature of GenAI’s impact on productivity. Many forms of “spurious productivity” may remain unexplored, where surface-level acceleration conceals redistributed or hidden effort. Future research should integrate artifact-based analyses (e.g., code quality, style consistency, and robustness) with socio-technical investigations of human interaction and knowledge transfer. Only by combining these perspectives can we determine whether the apparent acceleration associated with GenAI reflects genuine improvements in software quality or merely faster, shallower production.

Practical Implications.

For practitioners and organizations, our findings suggest that GenAI tools should be integrated with clear goals and supporting processes rather than assumed productivity gains. Without mechanisms to encourage collaborative use and ensure quality, GenAI adoption may increase individual speed by undermining shared understanding and long-term maintainability.

Extending the SPACE Framework.

By operationalizing the SPACE framework in the context of GenAI-assisted development, this study extends its application beyond traditional workflows. Our results show both the framework’s robustness and its limitations in capturing emergent, AI-mediated forms of productivity. This highlights opportunities for refining or expanding SPACE dimensions as software work continues to evolve.

Proline-promoted electrophilic dichloromethylation of α-enaminones via stereochemically gated resolution
Authors: Elihay Kuniavsky, Dvora Rachel Levy, Dmitry Tsvelikhovsky
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
The direct incorporation of polyhalomethyl groups into carbonyl frameworks has traditionally relied on radical or carbene intermediates. Here, we report a proline-mediated, closed-shell strategy for electrophilic β-polyhalomethylation of α-enaminones using stable haloforms under mild, metal-free conditions. The reaction proceeds with high diastereoselectivity, governed by a stereoelectronic gating mechanism that links molecular topology to productive C-C bond formation. The platform accommodates a broad range of CX₂ donors, including CHBr₃, CHBr₂Cl, and CBrCl₃, and reveals topology-dependent divergence: six-membered α-enaminones yield isolable β-functionalized products, while five-membered analogs engage in a spontaneous cascade, triggered by reaction with proline, culminating in aromatized benzenoid scaffolds. Structural, isotopic, and spectroscopic studies support a unique reactivity manifold defined by polarity inversion and closed-shell transfer. This work redefines electrophilic halomethane chemistry, establishes a mechanistic class for β-functionalization, and positions α-enaminones as versatile, programmable platforms for topology-driven reactivity and downstream synthetic elaboration.

Introduction

Organocatalysis has transformed modern synthetic chemistry by enabling precise carbonyl functionalization under mild, metal-free conditions, with enamine catalysis occupying a central conceptual role1. Yet a persistent and largely unaddressed contradiction underlies this framework: carbonyl compounds bearing α-hydroxy or α-amino substituents, motifs that appear ideally suited for enamine activation, consistently resist engagement with secondary amine catalysts. Despite decades of methodological development, these α-substituted ketones remain effectively inert under canonical organocatalytic conditions, revealing a fundamental limitation in how carbonyl reactivity is currently conceptualized2,3.

Our previous studies established that this failure is intrinsic rather than circumstantial. Both α-hydroxy and α-amino ketones reproducibly fail to form productive enamines, independent of catalyst identity or reaction conditions. This systematic inertness indicates that classical enamine logic is fundamentally incompatible with these scaffolds and suggests that selective functionalization of these scaffolds cannot be achieved through incremental modification of existing organocatalytic strategies. Instead, it points to the existence of an alternative, unrecognized reactivity regime embedded within α-substituted carbonyl architectures2,3.

Guided by this realization, we sought to identify non-canonical activation modes that exploit, rather than circumvent, the stereoelectronic constraints imposed by α-substitution. This effort revealed a unifying mechanistic principle across α-oxygenated and α-aminated ketones: reactivity arises from geometrically enforced polarity inversion rather than from enamine formation. In this framework, closed-shell cascades convert structural constraints into chemical driving forces. We demonstrated that α-hydroxy ketones undergo metal-free reductive dehydroxylation via hydroxylamine intermediates (Fig. 1A)2. In contrast, α,β-unsaturated α-aminoketones undergo decarboxylative condensation cascades, yielding previously inaccessible polycyclic ionic liquids (Fig. 1B)3. Although distinct in outcome, these transformations share a common logic in which reactivity is dictated by stereoelectronic gating and intramolecular acid-base choreography rather than by classical nucleophile-electrophile pairing (Fig. 1C). Within this emerging paradigm, we now report a third and conceptually distinct transformation: the closed-shell, electrophilic β-dichloromethylation of α-enaminoketones using unmodified halomethanes (Fig. 1D). This reaction enables direct C-C bond formation from simple haloform-type reagents under mild conditions, without metals, radicals, carbenes, light, or preactivation. Under proline-buffered conditions (L-proline, K₃PO₄, CHBrCl₂), α-enaminones undergo site- and stereoselective installation of a CHCl₂ unit at the β-position, delivering densely functionalized scaffolds that combine a ketone, a tertiary amine, and a synthetically versatile dihalomethyl handle in a single operation. The transformation proceeds with complete anti-stereoselectivity (>99:1) across a broad substrate range and exhibits a characteristic yield ceiling that reflects intrinsic mechanistic partitioning rather than incomplete conversion.

Mechanistic analysis reveals that this behavior arises from a diastereomer-resolved bifurcation at the hydroxylamine stage. Reaction of the α-enaminone with proline generates two transient intermediates, only one of which adopts a geometry permissive for decarboxylation and electrophilic CHCl₂ incorporation. The mismatched diastereomer enters a kinetically stable off-cycle state and fragments cleanly upon workup. This stereochemically gated resolution defines a previously unrecognized mode of reactivity control, in which molecular topology rather than catalyst chirality governs productive bond formation. Comprehensive isotopic labeling, fragment reactivity studies, and crystallographic analysis collectively exclude enamine, iminium, radical, or carbene pathways, establishing a strictly closed-shell mechanism in which polarity inversion and stereoelectronic gating enable electrophilic halomethyl transfer onto an electronically ambivalent β-carbon center.

The precise incorporation of di- and trihalomethyl groups (CX2, CX3) remains a central challenge in synthetic chemistry, constrained mainly by a fundamental mechanistic dichotomy. Classical approaches rely on highly reactive dichlorocarbene intermediates, require strong bases, and exhibit poor functional group tolerance4,5. More contemporary strategies leverage photoredox catalysis or transition metals to activate halomethanes via radical pathways, yet these are inherently dependent on single-electron transfer or metal catalysis6. Critically, both carbenoid and radical methods predominantly target substrates with rigidly fixed π-electron distributions, such as electron-rich alkenes7–11, enones12, or acrylamides13–15, where conjugation with electron-withdrawing groups ensures predictable electrophilic capture (Fig. 2A)16–20. α-Enaminones represent a prime example of such an unaddressed frontier. Their β-position, governed by resonance, exhibits a unique electronic ambivalence, capable of inverting between nucleophilic and electrophilic character depending on the stereoelectronic context3,21. This inherent dynamism, a source of unique reactivity, simultaneously renders them recalcitrant to existing halomethylation methods, as their flexible polarization fundamentally disrupts the fixed π-electron demand of established carbene and radical protocols (Fig. 2A).

Here, we invert the conventional strategy: rather than forcing α-enaminones into canonical reactivity modes or circumventing their electronic ambivalence, we harness their electronic ambivalence as a design principle (Fig. 2B). Under proline-buffered conditions, α-enaminones undergo closed-shell, β-selective dihalomethylation, enabling direct C-C bond formation with dihalomethanes in the absence of metals, radicals, light, or carbenes. This mechanistically distinct transformation allows the selective installation of CHCl₂ units onto electronically neutral β-carbons. The platform extends to a broader class of polyhalomethyl donors, including CBrCl3, CHBr₃, and CHBr2Cl (Fig. 2C), establishing a generalizable framework for CX₂ transfer. Notably, the manifold reveals latent cascade behavior shaped by subtle stereoelectronic and topological cues.

Together, these results reposition halomethane activation as a polarity-gated, closed-shell process and elevate α-enaminones as programmable platforms for stereoelectronically choreographed reaction design.

Results

Discovery and optimization of α-Enaminone β-Dichloromethylation

The initial discovery of this transformation emerged during a thermal evaluation of α-enaminone 1 with stoichiometric L-proline in neat chloroform at 100 °C. This serendipitous finding led to the formation of the β-dichloromethylated product 2 in a limited yet unambiguous manner (Table 1). Despite the low efficiency of this initial observation, rigorous characterization by NMR spectroscopy, GC-MS analysis, and single-crystal X-ray diffraction unequivocally established site-selective β-incorporation of the CHCl2 unit with strict anti-stereochemistry relative to the pendant amine. Our optimization studies systematically explored the halomethane source, base identity, proline loading, solvent, temperature, additives, atmosphere, and light exposure. These efforts converged on a narrow, optimal regime employing CHBrCl2 (5 equiv.), L-proline (2 equiv.), and K3PO4 (1 equiv.) in CHCl3 at 100 °C for 20 h.

Under these optimized conditions, the transformation proceeded reproducibly, exhibiting clean product profiles and providing synthetically useful yields. Importantly, no alternative permutation, including the original chloroform-only conditions, approached this level of performance.

To definitively establish the origin of the dichloromethyl fragment, we conducted targeted isotopic tracing experiments. Using CHBrCl2 as the donor in CDCl3 as solvent, analysis by ¹H/²H NMR spectroscopy and high-resolution mass spectrometry revealed only minimal incorporation of CDCl2, despite the large excess of deuterated solvent. This robustly confirms CHBrCl2 as the sole operative CHCl2 source under the optimized conditions. Further controls demonstrated the reaction’s insensitivity to ambient light (excluding UV) and its efficient operation under open-flask conditions. Additives, including molecular sieves, phase transfer agents, and acid scavengers (e.g., proton sponges and ion-exchange resins), provided no improvement and, in some cases, marginally suppressed reactivity, indicating that the system operates effectively under its native, self-buffered conditions.

A Stereochemically gated resolution pathway to product formation

Under the optimized conditions, the reaction exhibits high chemo- and site-selectivity; however, product 2 (Table 1) consistently plateaus at approximately 50% yield despite full substrate consumption. This reproducible ceiling was a critical early mechanistic clue, suggesting an intrinsic constraint rather than incomplete conversion or product instability.

To investigate these temporal dynamics, we monitored reaction progress and found that, although substrate consumption proceeded to completion, product formation consistently plateaued at ~50% conversion. This divergence reflects a non-classical outcome: only a defined subset of reactive intermediates evolves productively. This observation, coupled with further mechanistic investigations detailed below, led us to propose a resolution-type pathway as the fundamental determinant of the observed yield ceiling. The full conversion/yield profile, along with its detailed mechanistic implications, is provided in the Supplementary Section 6.

To account for the divergent reactivity observed, we propose a stereochemically gated mechanism in which the α-enaminone (3) reacts with L-proline, generating two diastereomeric hydroxylamine intermediates (Fig. 3A). In the productive trajectory, intermediate 4b adopts a conformation in which the α-hydroxyl and proline carboxyl group are spatially separated, enabling intramolecular proton transfer to the adjacent nitrogen and forming a transient ammonium intermediate (7). This species undergoes α-decarboxylation, triggering a cascade of electron flow that restores a nucleophilic enamine system. Substitution with CHBrCl₂ forms a β-dichloromethylated enolamine (9), with elimination of pyrroline (8) as a byproduct22.

A capture experiment (see Supplementary Section 7) confirmed the formation of pyrroline (8) in situ, which subsequently oligomerizes to its known trimeric condensate, consistent with literature reports23. Final tautomerization yields product (10) with a defined anti-configuration relative to the pendant amine. This outcome was unambiguously confirmed by single-crystal X-ray diffraction24.

In contrast, intermediate 4a adopts a compact, bridged topology stabilized by intramolecular hydrogen bonding between the α-hydroxyl group and proline’s carboxylate. This conformation suppresses both proton transfer and enamine delocalization, rendering the β-position electronically inert and stalling the cascade.

No reversion to the α-enaminone or formation of a dehydrated intermediate (e.g., 6) was detected throughout the reaction course by NMR or mass spectrometry. Upon aqueous workup, or, in some cases, during chromatographic purification, the sequestered species undergoes clean fragmentation into the corresponding 1,2-diketone and secondary amine. This post-reaction decomposition confirms the off-cycle nature of intermediate 4a and accounts for the observed recovery, thereby further substantiating the proposed bifurcation model.

This stereochemical divergence, in which only one diastereomeric intermediate proceeds through the cascade while the other becomes kinetically trapped, establishes the transformation as a resolution-type process.

Part B of Fig. 3 addresses and experimentally excludes a mechanistic scenario in which the α-enaminone undergoes proline-promoted dehydration to form an enamine-iminium intermediate (11), leading to β-substitution with CHBrCl₂. If operative, this pathway would give rise to a series of electrophilic adducts (12, 13, 14) that ultimately hydrolyze to a dichloromethylated diketone 15. Although 15 was detectable by NMR in crude mixtures, it could not be isolated and was not observed by GC or high-resolution mass spectrometry. These findings indicate that, if formed, the compound exists only in trace amounts and does not accumulate under the reaction conditions. Taken together, the data strongly disfavor the enamine–iminium route as a productive pathway for this transformation.

To experimentally substantiate the proposed resolution-type mechanism and account for the post-reaction mass balance, we examined the crude mixture obtained from the dichloromethylation of α-enaminone 16, derived from 4-chlorophenyl-N-methylmethanamine and 1,2-cyclohexanedione (Fig. 3C). 1H NMR analysis revealed complete consumption of the starting material, yielding a clean 1:1 ratio of the β-dichloromethylated product 17 and the corresponding secondary amine precursor 18.

These findings support a stereochemically gated process in which diastereomeric intermediates are formed in equal proportion. One is funneled through productive β-functionalization, while the other undergoes post-reaction fragmentation, releasing the parent amine. This divergence is likely driven by acid- or water-mediated cleavage during the cascade, consistent with the reaction profile. Although minor resonances of the parent diketone are observed, its low abundance suggests rapid secondary engagement with excess proline, generating polar adducts that are removed from the CHCl₃ phase upon workup. Crucially, the absence of detectable competing side products upon rigorous crude analysis directly underscores the exceptional selectivity of the process. The strict 1:1 product-to-recovered amine ratio constitutes direct evidence for a resolution-type mechanism with stereochemically defined reactivity.

Experimental investigation of the reaction mechanism

To probe the structural features essential for reactivity, we subjected the two key fragments of the α-enaminone scaffold, the parent cyclohexenone and its corresponding enamine, to the optimized dichloromethylation conditions with CHBrCl₂ (Fig. 4A). Neither substrate underwent any detectable transformation, ruling out classical enamine chemistry or fragment-level nucleophilic or electrophilic engagement. In contrast, the β-dichloromethylation proceeds cleanly only with the intact enaminone, indicating that its conjugated, ambident electronic structure is essential for activation under closed-shell conditions. To probe the mechanism of dichloromethylation, we carried out targeted experiments to assess the proposed resolution-type divergence pathway (Fig. 4) and establish whether CHCl₂ incorporation proceeds via a polar, closed-shell process. Replacing CHBrCl₂ with CDCl₃ under standard conditions (Fig. 4B) led to clean formation of the β-deuterodichloromethylated product (2a), with full retention of deuterium confirmed by NMR and GC-MS (see Supplementary Section 9). The incorporation of an intact CDCl₂ unit is consistent with electrophilic substitution and excludes a carbene-based mechanism, which would be expected to induce isotopic scrambling or partial deuterium loss.

Furthermore, a control experiment conducted under light-exclusion conditions yielded an identical product distribution and conversion, indicating that photochemical initiation does not contribute to the transformation. Collectively, these findings delineate a mechanistic route that departs from established CHCl₂ transfer pathways. The transformation proceeds without free carbene intermediates (CCl2) or enamine-type nucleophiles, instead relying on direct electrophilic activation of CDCl₃ in the presence of the α-enaminone scaffold under proline catalysis. This closed-shell, conjugation-gated process defines a reactivity mode not captured by existing dichloromethylation methods.

Further support for the proposed mechanistic bifurcation arises from the stereochemical outcome of the transformation. Single-crystal X-ray diffraction analysis of the β-dichloromethylated product 2 revealed a defined anti-configuration between the CHCl₂ group and the pendant amine (Fig. 4C)24. This stereospecific termination is only consistent with the trajectory of the enolamine intermediate 9 proposed in the productive resolution pathway (Fig. 3). No alternative mechanistic scenario, including those involving carbene intermediates or enamine–iminium equilibration, can rationalize the observed selective anti-selectivity. This crystallographic evidence thus provides direct structural validation for the sequence of intermediate states inferred from kinetic and resolution-based studies.

To probe the stereochemical partitioning proposed in Fig. 4, we conducted the reaction using an equimolar mixture of L- and D-proline (1 equiv. each; Fig. 4D) under optimized conditions. Complete consumption of the α-enaminone was observed, yet the reaction plateaued at approximately 50% yield. This result is consistent with the formation of four diastereomeric hydroxylamine intermediates, two derived from each proline enantiomer, only half of which undergo productive dichloromethylation. The remaining diastereomers persist as stable, off-cycle species and are hydrolyzed during workup to regenerate the parent dione and secondary amine.

The observation that complete conversion of the starting material yields only half the product supports a stoichiometric resolution-type scenario, in which both enantiomers of proline are required to form the full set of intermediates. While this stereochemical divergence governs reactivity at the hydroxylamine stage, all chiral information is ultimately lost upon formation of the enolamine intermediate, which precedes product formation. As such, the process remains racemic, despite proceeding through diastereomerically differentiated intermediates.

Despite the use of enantiopure L-proline, the β-dichloromethylation proceeds without asymmetric induction, and the CHCl2-bearing stereocenter is formed as a racemate, as confirmed by X-ray crystallography (see Supplementary Section 10).

The mechanistic indispensability of proline was further clarified through targeted structural modifications. Replacement of its α-proton with a methyl group (Fig. 4E) generated a quaternary center incapable of decarboxylation3, resulting in complete loss of reactivity under standard conditions. This outcome directly supports the requirement for α-decarboxylation and confirms that such elimination is precluded from a fully substituted center.

Next, to isolate the role of the carboxylic acid, the proline core was substituted with either a methyl ester or a primary amide (Fig. 4G). In both cases, the transformation failed to proceed. These results highlight the second essential function of the acid: to engage in an intramolecular acid–base interaction with the α-enaminone, forming an ammonium intermediate that is necessary to drive the cascade. In the absence of either the α-proton or the free carboxyl group, the system remains inert. Together, these observations establish that decarboxylation and proton transfer are both mechanistic prerequisites for productive β-dichloromethylation.

The structural demands of the transformation were further dissected by evaluating individual amino acid variants under the optimized conditions (Fig. 4H). The following examples illustrate how deviations from proline’s unique geometry compromise reactivity. For instance, N-methyl-L-alanine (24), a flexible linear amino acid, yielded only 12% of product formation. Its open-chain geometry lacks the conformational restriction necessary to stabilize the key hydroxylamine-ammonium intermediate, thereby hindering efficient progression through the reaction manifold. In contrast, (S)-azetidine-2-carboxylic acid, despite its cyclic constraint, failed to promote reactivity due to excessive ring strain (23) and unfavorable spatial arrangement between the carboxyl and amino groups, precluding formation of the internal acid–base stabilized intermediate. Pipecolic acid (25) provided the highest level of reactivity among all tested variants, affording 15% of the desired product. However, its reduced efficiency relative to proline likely reflects a limitation at the decarboxylation step: the six-membered ring does not support the geometric requirements for CO₂ loss under the reaction conditions3.

Collectively, these results underscore that both intramolecular ammonium stabilization and decarboxylation readiness are essential features governing productive β-dichloromethylation.

The lack of reactivity in α-enaminones bearing aniline derivatives (Fig. 4F) further supports the proposed mechanism. These substrates fail to undergo β-dichloromethylation, likely due to the diminished basicity of the aniline nitrogen, which prevents formation of the key intramolecular ammonium intermediate 7 (Fig. 3A). This result reinforces the requirement for nitrogen basicity to enable α-decarboxylation and initiate a productive cascade.

Evaluation of proline analogs 26–29 (Fig. 4I) reveals a clear structure-reactivity relationship in the dichloromethylation cascade. Analogs that preserve the core proline anchor, a five-membered ring bearing a carboxylic acid and a secondary amine, retain activity, albeit with varying efficiency. The bicyclic derivative (26, 8%), the fused aryl analog (27, 28%), and the cyclohexane-substituted scaffold (28, 32%) all afford product formation under standard conditions, indicating tolerance to steric bulk and topological modification.

In contrast, the OH- or OTBS-substituted prolines 29a and 29b, bearing syn and anti γ-positions, respectively, were completely inactive. We attribute this loss of reactivity to inhibition of the decarboxylation step, likely arising from a combination of electron-donating effects and intramolecular hydrogen bonding. These features may stabilize the carboxylate form and prevent access to the required geometry for CO₂ extrusion. Taken together, these results highlight that while the proline anchor accommodates significant structural variation, substituents that perturb electronic distribution or conformational flexibility at the γ-position can disrupt key activation steps and render the system unreactive.

The unique reactivity observed in the discovered transformation was strictly dependent on the presence of K₃PO₄ as the base. Other inorganic and organic bases, including carbonates, acetates, amines, and stronger alkoxides, uniformly failed to support the formation of the product. The critical role of K₃PO₄ likely stems from its dual function as a moderately basic, non-nucleophilic inorganic salt and a buffering agent capable of sustaining the narrow acid–base equilibrium necessary for cascade initiation. Specifically, its basicity appears to promote the formation of the key proline-derived ammonium intermediate 7 (Fig. 3A) through controlled proton transfer to the α-enaminone, without over-deprotonating the substrate or destabilizing sensitive intermediates. In contrast, weaker bases are insufficient to initiate this process, while stronger or less selective bases may disrupt the delicate balance required for α-decarboxylation or induce degradation of CHBrCl₂. The inability of other bases to replicate the reaction outcome underscores the crucial role of K₃PO₄ in maintaining the precise reactivity window necessary for β-dichloromethylation.

Scope

The scope of this electrophilic β-dichloromethylation manifold was evaluated across a structurally diverse panel of α-enaminones (Fig. 5). Under optimized conditions, substrates bearing benzylic, aliphatic, and heterocyclic N-substituents underwent clean and selective transformation. Products were consistently obtained as single diastereomers with exclusive anti-configuration between the β-CHCl2 group and the pendant amine, as confirmed by ¹H NMR spectroscopy and unambiguously validated by single-crystal X-ray diffraction of representative compound 2. No syn-diastereomers were detected (>99:1 dr), underscoring the high fidelity of the proline-mediated stereochemical gating. The isolated yields for these adducts (up to 42%) are consistent with the intrinsic resolution mechanism governing the reaction. As detailed above, the reaction path bifurcates: ~50% of the racemic substrate enters the productive cascade. At the same time, the remainder is channeled into a non-productive, off-cycle state that fragments cleanly upon workup. Ring size divergence and aromatization cascade

Expanding beyond six-membered rings, our investigation into ring-size effects revealed a profoundly divergent transformation for five-membered α-enaminones, contrasting sharply with the inherent instability observed in seven-membered analogs under the reaction conditions. Upon initial dichloromethylation, these five-membered substrates rapidly trigger a complex, proline-orchestrated cascade dictated by their unique constrained topology (Fig. 6B). The resulting dichloromethylated intermediate (64) first undergoes rapid condensation with proline to form a transient enamine (65), which subsequently tautomerizes to a highly reactive iminium species. Crucially, subsequent deprotonation generates a stabilized azomethine ylide (68)25, characterized by a potent carbanionic center adjacent to the iminium nitrogen. This strategically positioned ylide then initiates a sequence of skeletal rearrangements, involving the formation of a pyrrolidine-fused intermediate (70), culminating in spontaneous ring expansion (71), elimination (72), and aromatization. The net outcome is the formation of densely substituted aromatic products that bear both pyrrolidine and benzylamine motifs (73). This intricate, topology-dependent pathway, conspicuously absent in larger ring systems, highlights a fundamental bifurcation in reactivity within the enaminone manifold and offers a concise, metal-free route to complex aromatic architectures from structurally simple cyclic precursors.

Synthetic utility and derivatization

To underscore the synthetic potential of these adducts, we demonstrated the versatility of the β-dichloromethylated synthon as a functional linchpin. Downstream transformations afforded diverse functionalities, including an (E)-configured vinyl chloride, a ketal, an acetal, and an enol ether (Fig. 6C). These examples demonstrate the utility of the installed gem-dihalomethyl motif as a gateway to structural complexity12,26.

The conversion of the CHCl₂ group to the corresponding aldehyde27, further extends this reactivity space: the resulting carbonyl condenses efficiently with primary amines derived from Amphenicol (79) and Tryptamine (80)28,29, enabling rapid assembly of hybrid structures that integrate β-dichloromethyl enaminone cores with bioactive motifs (Fig. 6D). Collectively, these results establish the CHCl₂ unit as a strategically reconfigurable handle for skeletal editing and late-stage conjugation to complex small-molecule architectures.

Discussion

This study establishes a distinct mode of carbon-carbon bond formation from halomethanes that is orthogonal to all established dichloromethylation paradigms. By operating entirely within a closed-shell regime, the reaction bypasses the long-standing reliance on carbenes, radicals, photoredox activation, or transition metals, and instead exploits polarity inversion that is internally programmed by molecular topology and acid–base choreography. In doing so, it reframes halomethanes, traditionally viewed as harsh or indiscriminate reagents, as precision electrophiles when engaged by the appropriate stereoelectronic scaffold.

Central to this advance is the recognition that α-enaminones are not flawed enamine precursors but ambident systems whose reactivity is governed by geometry rather than by electronic effects alone. The failure of classical enamine catalysis, long regarded as a limitation, is here converted into a design principle: productive reactivity emerges only when intramolecular constraints enforce polarity inversion at the β-carbon. The dichloromethylation, therefore, does not represent an extension of enamine chemistry but a departure from it, one that leverages the same structural motifs to access an entirely different reactivity manifold.

The resolution-type behavior uncovered in this system is particularly instructive. Yield limitation is not a consequence of inefficiency, but the mechanistic signature of stereochemical bifurcation at an early, reversible stage. Only one diastereomeric intermediate satisfies the geometric requirements for decarboxylation and electrophilic capture; the other is cleanly diverted into an off-cycle state and removed without erosion of selectivity. This mode of control, where molecular topology, rather than catalyst chirality or reagent excess, dictates productive outcomes, represents an underexplored strategy for reaction design. Importantly, the system achieves absolute diastereocontrol without asymmetric induction, underscoring that stereochemical fidelity and enantioselectivity are not necessarily mechanistically coupled.

The indispensability of proline further clarifies the mechanistic logic. Proline does not function as a generic organocatalyst, nor merely as a Brønsted acid or base, but as a bifunctional structural activator whose rigid framework, decarboxylation competence, and internal acid-base alignment are all simultaneously required. Subtle perturbations, ring-size expansion, γ-substitution, or carboxyl masking collapse the cascade entirely, highlighting the narrow yet powerful reactivity window accessible by this architecture. In this sense, proline operates less as a catalyst in the classical sense and more as a transient molecular machine that choreographs bond reorganization.

Beyond dichloromethylation, the extension to diverse polyhalomethyl donors demonstrates that the transformation is not reagent-specific but mechanistically general. The ability to transfer gem-dihalo and mixed halomethyl units directly from stable haloforms under unified conditions suggests that electrophilic CX₂ delivery can be decoupled from carbene chemistry altogether. Moreover, the topology-dependent divergence observed for five-membered enaminones, which leads to aromatization cascades inaccessible in larger rings, reveals that the platform encodes higher-order reactivity that can be unlocked by subtle structural variation.

Collectively, these findings reposition α-enaminones from problematic substrates to programmable reaction platforms and redefine halomethane activation as a polarity-gated, closed-shell process. More broadly, the work illustrates how apparent mechanistic dead ends in organocatalysis can conceal alternative reactivity regimes when examined through the lens of stereoelectronic constraint rather than classical activation modes.

Beyond their formation, the polyhalomethylated products function as strategic synthetic linchpins: the installed CX₂ unit can be selectively reorganized into multiple carbonyl-derived motifs, enabling rapid divergence into structurally distinct building blocks.

Limitations of the study

While this work establishes a distinct mechanistic manifold for electrophilic polyhalomethylation, several intrinsic limitations currently constrain its scope. The transformation proceeds via a stereochemically gated, resolution-type pathway, imposing an inherent upper yield limit of 50% relative to racemic starting materials. This constraint reflects the mechanistic bifurcation of diastereomeric intermediates and is inseparable from the process’s exceptional diastereoselectivity and operational simplicity.

Reactivity is further contingent on the structural integrity of the α-enaminone scaffold. Six-membered systems are well accommodated, while five-membered substrates engage in productive cascade reorganization; in contrast, seven-membered analogs exhibit chemical instability under the reaction conditions. In addition, steric congestion at the nitrogen center is poorly tolerated: bulky N-substituents (e.g., tert-butyl) destabilize the enaminone framework and preclude productive engagement. Together, these features delineate the current boundaries of the method and provide clear directions for future scaffold and catalyst design.

In summary, we report closed-shell, electrophilic β-dichloromethylation of α-enaminones using unactivated halomethanes under metal-free, light-free, and radical-free conditions. The transformation proceeds through a stereoelectronically gated, proline-orchestrated cascade that converts structural constraint into chemical driving force, enabling precise C-C bond formation from simple haloform donors.

The reaction operates via a resolution-type mechanism in which only one diastereomeric intermediate is reactive, delivering exclusive anti-diastereoselectivity while intrinsically limiting yield. Comprehensive mechanistic analysis excludes enamine, iminium, carbene, and radical pathways, establishing a distinct polarity-inversion manifold for halomethane activation.

Beyond dichloromethylation, the strategy enables general electrophilic polyhalomethyl transfer and reveals topology-dependent cascade behavior, including unexpected aromatization of five-membered systems. Together, these findings redefine α-enaminones as programmable scaffolds for closed-shell reactivity and provide conceptual framework for halomethane chemistry that is governed by stereoelectronic gating rather than external activation.

Methods

General procedure for dichloromethylation of α-enaminones

To a reaction tube equipped with a magnetic stir bar were added proline (2.0 equiv., 0.6 mmol, 69.2 mg), K3PO4 (1.0 equiv., 0.3 mmol, 63.7 mg), the corresponding α-enaminone (1.0 equiv., 0.3 mmol), and chloroform (0.075 M, 4 mL), followed by bromodichloromethane (5.0 equiv., 1.5 mmol). The tube was then tightly sealed, and the mixture was stirred at 100 °C for 10–72 h. After cooling to room temperature, the reaction mixture was concentrated under reduced pressure, and the crude was purified by chromatography to afford the corresponding dichloromethylated product.

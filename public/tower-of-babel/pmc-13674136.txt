Blood–Brain Barrier Regulation: Evolving From Classic Strategies to Electrochemical Ion and Reactive Oxygen Species Control
Authors: Xiaokang Hu, Caiyu Liu, Meijun Pang, Zhiyuan Shi, Dong Ming, Xiuyun Liu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTThe blood–brain barrier (BBB), while indispensable for maintaining central nervous system (CNS) homeostasis, constitutes the principal impediment to effective therapeutic delivery for neurodegenerative disorders, particularly hindering spatially resolved modulation of extracellular ions and reactive oxygen species (ROS) within the neural microenvironment. Contemporary electrochemical methodologies have emerged as a paradigm shift for dynamically reconciling these dual parameters, thereby enabling targeted neuroregulation. Critical review of this field reveals a distinct evolution from passive physiological interventions to active electrochemical engineering approaches. Current research, however, encounters persistent translational barriers including insufficient spatiotemporal resolution in neural interfaces, incomplete mechanistic understanding of ROS‐ionic crosstalk, and scalability limitations of nanoscale delivery systems. To transcend these limitations, the synergistic convergence of electrochemical platforms with machine learning (ML)–guided predictive analytics, near‐infrared (NIR) phototherapy, and biocompatible nanocarrier‐mediated delivery systems constitutes a strategic imperative in next‐generation neurotherapeutic development. Such interdisciplinary convergence is not merely incremental but rather a fundamental prerequisite for realizing clinically translatable neural microenvironment modulation.

Introduction

The blood–brain barrier (BBB) constitutes a specialized physiological interface critical for central nervous system (CNS) homeostasis, dynamically maintained by brain microvascular endothelial cells (BMECs), tight junctions (TJs), basement membranes, and astrocyte end‐feet [1, 2]. Within this architecture, Claudin‐5 functions as the primary structural determinant of BBB integrity, establishing a selective physical barrier that impedes paracellular transport for >98% of small‐molecule therapeutics and virtually all macromolecular agents—including antibodies and nucleic acid‐based therapeutics [3]. While this exceptional selectivity safeguards the CNS against pathogenic influx, it has proven to be the predominant obstacle in achieving clinically effective interventions for neurodegenerative disorders (e.g., Alzheimer's disease, Parkinson's disease) and acute neurological conditions such as glioma and stroke [4, 5, 6, 7].

Decades of research have yielded diverse BBB modulation strategies—from physical stimuli to molecular engineering approaches—yet contemporary syntheses fail to address two critical lacunae: (i) excessive enumeration of methodologies obscures clinically translatable platforms, undermining mechanistic coherence and clinical utility; (ii) the disconnect between engineered material‐device systems and molecular pathways impedes interdisciplinary innovation essential for therapeutic translation [8, 9, 10]. Critically, despite advances in in vitro platforms that enable high‐throughput nanoparticle trafficking evaluation, these systems remain physiologically irrelevant to in vivo Claudin‐5 dynamics—thereby failing to resolve the clinical gaps above [11]. Notably, emerging electrochemical neuromodulation strategies—enabling subcellular resolution with spatiotemporal precision—remain underexplored within the existing review landscape, representing a critical gap in the field's technological trajectory.

To address these gaps, this review develops a clinically oriented framework for BBB modulation that deliberately minimizes theoretical complexity to enhance translational viability. The framework identifies four representative engineered platforms—encompassing both established and emerging paradigms—each defined by distinct material design principles, mechanistic action at the Claudin‐5 barrier interface, and clinical feasibility. Specifically, physical engineering (ultrasound‐enhanced lipid microbubbles) constitutes the clinically established noninvasive BBB disruption protocol; chemical engineering (hypertonic mannitol) represents the prototypical osmotic disruption agent with decades of clinical validation; bioengineering (transferrin‐conjugated nanocarriers) leverages receptor‐mediated transcytosis to circumvent Claudin‐5 barriers; and bypass engineering (nasal–brain mucosal delivery) enables unimpeded CNS access without compromising barrier integrity. Crucially, each platform's analysis emphasizes how engineered interventions interact with Claudin‐5 dynamics—rather than detailed technical specifications—thereby clarifying translational pathways while explicitly avoiding mechanistic oversimplification.

The most consequential contribution of this review lies in its rigorous dissection of the fifth engineered platform: electrochemical modulation of ion homeostasis and reactive oxygen species (ROS) dynamics. This paradigm circumvents the long‐standing therapeutic dilemma between spatiotemporal precision and biocompatibility—an inherent limitation of conventional strategies—through an integrated implantable microelectrode array (MEA) system delivering sub‐threshold electrical pulses. These pulses enable precise spatiotemporal regulation of Ca2 +/K+ ionic fluxes while concurrently facilitating in situ ROS generation—a bifunctional mechanism that directly targets Claudin‐5–mediated tight junction permeability [12, 13]. Critically, this platform resolves the long‐standing tension in BBB therapeutics by modulating Claudin‐5 expression without inducing significant tissue damage, thereby establishing a clinically viable pathway for precision central nervous system (CNS) intervention.

Current BBB interventions remain constrained by the inability to simultaneously achieve spatiotemporal precision and biocompatibility—particularly in neurodegenerative contexts where targeted delivery must avoid off‐target effects. Unlike conventional strategies that often induce transient disruption or systemic toxicity, electrochemical modulation uniquely employs sub‐threshold electrical pulses to dynamically regulate Ca2 +/K+ ionic fluxes and in situ ROS without compromising barrier integrity. This review advances a clinically integrated framework that bridges these physical modalities—from ultrasound‐enhanced microbubbles to nasal delivery—by explicitly linking mechanistic action at the Claudin‐5 interface to translational feasibility, thereby establishing a pathway for precision CNS interventions that prioritizes both efficacy and safety.

Through comparative analysis of five engineered platforms, this review delineates actionable translational pathways for biomaterials, biomedical engineering, and neuropharmacology. Notably, the electrochemical strategy—leveraging sub‐threshold microelectrode pulses to regulate Ca2 +/K+ fluxes and in situ ROS dynamics—reconciles the precision–biocompatibility trade‐off by targeting Claudin‐5–mediated permeability without tissue damage, establishing a clinically translatable avenue for next‐generation CNS therapies.

Classic Engineered BBB Modulation Strategies

Progress in BBB modulation emerges from engineered material and device platforms, where four established paradigms demonstrate clinically relevant mechanisms and translational maturity [14, 15, 16]. Each paradigm is defined by a core architectural scaffold that intentionally reconfigures Claudin‐5‐mediated tight junction (TJ) permeability—prioritizing this molecular target to circumvent the barrier's inherent resilience [17]. Physical Engineering Platform: Focused Ultrasound Combined With Lipid Microbubbles

Clinically deployed as the most advanced noninvasive BBB opening technology, this platform operates via a precisely engineered system integrating lipid‐shelled microbubbles (LMBs) with clinical‐grade focused ultrasound (FUS) devices [18, 19]. LMBs—optimized for shell thickness (50–100 nm) and biocompatible lipid composition (e.g., 1,2‐distearoyl‐sn‐glycero‐3‐phosphocholine)—function as acoustic‐responsive actuators [20, 21]. When exposed to low‐intensity FUS (0.5–2 W/cm2), these microbubbles initiate controlled cavitation events that generate localized mechanical shear forces and microjet effects [22, 23]. This mechanistic response reversibly stretches brain microvascular endothelial cells (BMECs), triggering conformational shifts in tight junction proteins (Claudin‐5 and ZO‐1) without compromising cellular viability. Consequently, transient BBB permeability enhancement—sustained for 4–6 h—emerges as the platform's functional outcome [24, 25]. Its clinical advantages manifest through sub‐millimeter spatial precision, noninvasive delivery, and repeatable administration protocols. With Phase III trials now completed for Alzheimer's disease and glioma therapy, this approach has established itself as the benchmark for physical BBB modulation.

Chemical Engineering Platform: Hypertonic Mannitol‐Mediated Osmotic Regulation

Hypertonic mannitol—clinically approved as a rapid osmotic agent—represents the cornerstone of this chemical engineering strategy. The platform employs 20% medical‐grade mannitol solution (1–2 mL/kg/min intravenous infusion) to exploit its well‐established biocompatibility and acute osmotic efficacy [26]. This administration generates a plasma osmolarity > 320 mOsm/kg within minutes, inducing rapid BMEC dehydration and volume reduction [27, 28]. The resulting osmotic shock transiently widens intercellular gaps in tight junction complexes, facilitating BBB opening within 1–3 min [29, 30]. While mannitol lacks molecular specificity, its clinical indispensability arises from three factors: (i) streamlined preparation protocols, (ii) minimal cost constraints, and (iii) urgent utility for acute CNS pathologies like stroke and traumatic brain injury [31, 32]. Critically, it functions as the benchmark for comparative validation of next‐generation osmotic modulators.

Bioengineering Platform: Transferrin‐Functionalized Nanocarriers

Transferrin (Tf)‐engineered nanocarriers—specifically lipid nanoparticles (LNPs) or poly(lactic‐co‐glycolic acid) (PLGA) nanoparticles (50–150 nm in diameter)—form the foundation of this biomimetic strategy [33, 34, 35]. The mechanism operates via receptor‐mediated transcytosis (RMT): Tf‐ Transferrin Receptor (TfR) binding initiates clathrin‐dependent endocytosis, followed by vesicular trafficking across the BMEC monolayer and exocytosis into brain parenchyma—a process that deliberately avoids TJ disruption [36, 37]. The mechanism operates via receptor‐mediated transcytosis (RMT): Tf‐TfR binding initiates clathrin‐dependent endocytosis, followed by vesicular trafficking across the BMEC monolayer and exocytosis into brain parenchyma—a process that deliberately avoids TJ disruption [38, 39]. This design delivers three clinically significant advantages: (i) targeted delivery with minimal off‐target effects (reducing systemic toxicity), (ii) versatile payload capacity (enabling chemotherapeutics, peptides, and nucleic acids), and (iii) long‐term biocompatibility (degradable carriers with negligible inflammatory responses). Consequently, this platform achieves safe and sustained therapeutic access for chronic CNS pathologies where conventional approaches fail.

Bypass Engineering Platform: Nasal–Brain Mucosal Delivery Systems

This noninvasive route exploits mucoadhesive hydrogels (e.g., chitosan, hyaluronic acid) and nasal nanoformulations (lipid nanocapsules, solid lipid nanoparticles) to achieve direct CNS access without BBB modulation [40, 41, 42]. The platform's efficacy stems from engineered mucosal retention (extending residence time to 4–6 h) and targeted penetration of the olfactory epithelium barrier [43, 44]. Critically, it leverages the nasal–CNS anatomical interface: intranasal delivery of drug‐loaded formulations diffuses through trigeminal nerve‐associated perineural pathways, achieving direct cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) entry and brain parenchymal access—eliminating systemic circulation entirely [45, 46]. This mechanism delivers three clinically transformative advantages: (i) complete non‐invasiveness (enabling home administration), (ii) rapid therapeutic onset (brain accumulation within 15–30 min), and (iii) bypass of BBB limitations (for neurodegenerative diseases and mental disorders requiring sustained therapy). Consequently, the platform addresses unmet needs in chronic CNS conditions where conventional delivery fails.

The strategies surveyed in this section are not competitors in a race to the same finish line. They are answers to different questions, and the question each asks reveals as much about its limits as its strengths. Hyperosmotic disruption, the oldest approach, asks whether the barrier can be transiently opened by physical force—effective, but indiscriminate. Focused ultrasound refines that premise with spatial precision, asking whether barrier opening can be confined to a defined region. Receptor‐mediated transcytosis shifts the logic entirely: rather than disrupting the barrier, it asks whether endogenous receptors like TfR1 can be borrowed to ferry cargo across an intact door. Its clinical validation is real, but the schedule belongs to receptor kinetics, not the clinician. Cell‐penetrating peptides and bypass strategies go further still, asking whether the barrier needs to be crossed at all—the former by shuttling cargo directly through cells, the latter by exploiting anatomical shortcuts like the olfactory pathway. Each of these four classic strategies has defined its own clinical niche, and each has accepted the trade‐offs that niche imposes: temporal control for molecular precision, or simplicity for anatomical reach. Electrochemical modulation enters this landscape not by improving on any single predecessor but by addressing a need none was designed to meet—the ability to open the barrier on demand, at a chosen location, under external electrical control. Here, the gate is not osmotic force, acoustic energy, a receptor, or an anatomical detour, but an applied potential that can, in principle, be switched on and off. The cost of this capability is equally specific: invasiveness, preclinical status, and the unresolved durability of the electrode‐tissue interface. Section 3 examines what it would take to close that gap.

Electrochemical Ion and ROS Control Platform

This electrochemical BBB platform—integrating flexible bioelectronics, ion channel dynamics, and in situ ROS modulation—overcomes critical implementation barriers of the four classic strategies by enabling spatiotemporally precise BBB manipulation without systemic inflammation. Consequently, it delivers programmable CNS drug delivery for chronic neurological disorders where conventional approaches fail.

This platform employs implantable flexible microelectrode arrays (MEAs; e.g., polyimide substrate / Pt–Ir conductive layer) and tunable low‐voltage stimulation systems (0.1–1.0 V, direct/pulsed current) to achieve spatiotemporally precise BBB regulation without systemic inflammation [74, 75, 76, 77]. Its mechanism operates through three interlinked steps: (i) external electrical stimulation reversibly modulates BMEC membrane potential, (ii) transiently gates voltage‐dependent Ca2 +/K+ channels to induce Ca2 + influx and K+ efflux, and (iii) synergistically triggers Ca2 +‐mediated myosin light chain kinase (MLCK) activation and matrix metalloproteinase (MMP)‐2/9 modulation via mild electrogenerated ROS—specifically, a net oxidative load transduced into H2O2 and maintained within the redox signaling window of transient elevations to 10–100 nM H2O2 (in contrast to the resting basal level of ∼1–10 nM, and well below the pathological threshold of >100 nM) [78, 79, 80, 81, 82, 83].

The primary radicals generated by electrochemical processes (•OH, O2 −) are rapidly converted to H2O2 in situ. Critically, the platform maintains the net oxidative load (quantified as the time‑averaged H2O2 equivalent) within the 10–100 nM redox signaling window—transiently elevated above the resting oxidative eustress level (∼1–10 nM) to trigger MMP‑2/9 activation, yet strictly below the pathological threshold (>100 nM) defined as oxidative distress [83]. This temporal design ensures that the instantaneous ROS pulse is sufficient to initiate signaling, while the integrated oxidative burden remains safe without causing cellular damage [84].

The electrochemical platform does not rely on the resting oxidative eustress level (∼1–10 nM) as the active signal. Instead, it transiently elevates this baseline to generate a 10–100 nM H2O2 equivalent pulse—sufficient to activate MMP‑2/9 via redox‑sensitive thiol oxidation—while strictly remaining below the pathological threshold (>100 nM). Thus, the 1‐10 nM value serves exclusively as the reference resting state, not the functional signaling concentration [83, 84].

This cascade dynamically reorganizes Claudin‐5 via MLCK‐driven cytoskeletal contraction and MMP‐2/9 activity—enabling reversible, sub‐millimeter BBB permeability tuning. Crucially, the platform's localized electrical stimulation and adjustable input parameters deliver high spatiotemporal controllability while maintaining biocompatibility, directly supporting implantable closed‐loop systems for chronic neurodegenerative conditions where conventional delivery fails (Figure 2).

Core Engineering System Design

The electrochemical functionality of this platform is fundamentally constrained by the synergistic integration of two biomechanically optimized subsystems: implantable flexible microelectrode arrays (MEAs) and a programmable low‐voltage control architecture [74, 85]. Both components require rigorous biocompatibility validation to ensure in vivo viability, with the MEAs specifically engineered using polyimide substrates (10–20 µm thickness) and platinum‐based electrodes. This dimensionally constrained architecture enables conformal adhesion to neural tissue while minimizing acute inflammatory responses [86]. Critically, the 50–100 µm electrode tip diameter establishes localized stimulation efficacy without compromising adjacent neural architecture. Surface engineering with poly(ethylene glycol) (PEG) further mitigates protein adsorption‐induced fouling and electrochemical corrosion—mechanisms that collectively enable chronic deployment without compromising functional integrity [87, 88].

This multiparametric strategy ensures ROS safety through four interlinked constraints: charge density (10–50 µC/cm2) operates orders of magnitude below tissue‐damage thresholds (∼200 µC/cm2 per phase); [89, 90] electrode potential (−0.2 to −0.6 V vs. Ag/AgCl) selectively generates O2 −/H2O2 via oxygen reduction while avoiding •OH (requiring > +1.6 V); [91, 92] duty cycle (≤10 Hz) ensures near‐complete H2O2 clearance between pulses (half‐life: milliseconds) [93]; and microelectrode geometry (∼10− 3 cm2) confines ROS to microscopic volumes where endogenous quenchers (glutathione and ascorbate) instantly neutralize even worst‐case concentrations (tens of µM) [94, 95]. Collectively, these parameters enforce a >100‑fold safety margin relative to the pathological threshold (>100 nM), as the time‑averaged H2O2 load returns to the resting oxidative eustress level (∼1–10 nM, Sies’ framework) between pulses, while transient elevations into the 10–100 nM signaling window enable reversible barrier modulation [84]. This time‐averaged ROS concentration remains oxidative eustress (modulating MMP‐2/9 and tight junction dynamics without cellular damage) rather than pathological oxidative stress (>100 nM), with propidium iodide uptake <2.5% of cells under operational conditions [96].

This system enables precise modulation of direct current (DC) or pulsed current stimulation via closed‐loop parameter feedback mechanisms [70]. Its systematic implementation as a sub‐millimeter‐scale architecture concurrently supports short‐term functional modulation and long‐term stability—directly realizing the platform's programmability advantage through in vivo adaptability [97].

Exclusive Molecular Mechanism: Voltage‐Driven Ion/ROS Synergy Regulates Claudin‐5

This platform operates via spatiotemporally controlled modulation of tight junction integrity—specifically targeting Claudin‐5 function—through dual pathways: coordinated ion flux dynamics and regulated reactive oxygen species (ROS) generation. This mechanism ensures that while the resting oxidative eustress level is maintained at ∼1–10 nM H2O2 (Sies’ framework), transient electrochemical pulses elevate the local H2O2 concentration from the resting level into the 10–100 nM signaling window to modulate MMP‑2/9 activity and tight junction dynamics without causing cellular damage. In contrast, sustained H2O2 exceeding 100 nM would trigger pathological oxidative stress, leading to biomolecular damage, lipid peroxidation, and neurotoxicity—states that are actively prevented by this platform‘s precise parameter control [84].

Transient external depolarization in brain microvascular endothelial cells (BMECs) induces voltage‐dependent Ca2 + channel activation while concurrently suppressing inwardly rectifying K+ channel efflux pathways [98, 99, 100, 101]. This dual mechanism drives intracellular Ca2 + elevation through Ca2 + influx, thereby perturbing ionic homeostasis in the endothelial barrier. Critically, the resultant Ca2 + signaling cascade subsequently modulates tight junction integrity—a process directly established in BMECs through electrophysiological studies (Figure 3a) [102, 103, 104].

Electrochemically induced oxidative eustress at the electrode‐BMEC interface—via water electrolysis or oxygen reduction—initiates transient •OH and O2 − signaling cascades. Electrochemically induced Ca2 +‐dependent destabilization of tight junctions in BMECs occurs via two coupled pathways: (i) transient intracellular Ca2 + surges activate MLCK, triggering phosphorylation of myosin light chains (MLCs) and cytoskeletal contraction that compromises Claudin‐5 mechanical stability; (ii) oxidative eustress concurrently elevate MMP‐2/9 activity, driving targeted Claudin‐5 cleavage and reorganization [3, 110, 111]. This dual mechanism dynamically elevates BBB permeability—where the degree of junctional opening is spatiotemporally gated by electrochemical parameters (voltage amplitude and pulse duration), enabling precise platform‐controlled modulation of barrier function.

Performance Advantages: Beyond Classic Strategies

Unlike conventional platforms, this electrochemical strategy enables programmable BBB permeability cycles via spatiotemporally gated ion fluxes and oxidative eustress (Table 1). It achieves submillimeter‐scale spatial resolution—exceeding mannitol's non‐specific diffusion and FUS‐microbubbles’ limited control—while maintaining voltage‐tunable stimulation duration. Critically, the time‑averaged H2O2 concentration remains at the resting oxidative eustress level (∼1–10 nM) between pulses, while each transient electrochemical pulse elevates local H2O2 into the 10–100 nM signaling window to activate MMP‑2/9 without causing cellular damage. This safety profile is validated by multiple lines of evidence: propidium iodide uptake <2.5% (confirming no electroporation‐mediated damage), reversible BBB closure within 12–24 h (quantified by transepithelial electrical resistance (TEER) >200 Ω·cm2, normalized paracellular permeability, and re‐expression of Claudin‐5), and absence of cumulative BMEC injury across repeated therapeutic cycles [84, 112, 113]. This architecture integrates seamlessly with implantable closed‐loop platforms for concurrent BBB modulation, targeted drug delivery, and neuromodulation.

Translational Potential and Current Challenges

Preclinical studies in murine glioma models demonstrate that electrochemically tuned spatiotemporally gated permeability elevation—via voltage‐controlled BMEC ion fluxes and oxidative eustress—enables transient nanoparticle delivery without measurable edema or inflammation [14, 114, 115, 116]. Critically, the platform supports sustained therapeutic cycles for chronic neurodegenerative conditions (e.g., Alzheimer's disease), a direct result of the transient ROS elevation into the 10–100 nM signaling window, which enables reversible barrier modulation while avoiding the pathological >100 nM threshold [6, 117].

The translation of microelectrode array (MEA) technology from preclinical validation to clinical deployment confronts three persistent barriers that require targeted resolution. Maintaining ROS within the 10–100 nM signaling window addresses pathophysiological heterogeneity by avoiding >100 nM oxidative distress, potentially reducing the need for disorder‑specific ROS calibration across conditions such as glioma and Alzheimer's disease [81, 84]. First, the surgical invasiveness of MEA implantation—particularly the current reliance on open‐craniectomy approaches—demands innovation in minimally invasive percutaneous electrode architectures. Second, establishing chronic biocompatibility in large‐animal models, especially non‐human primates, remains inadequately addressed despite significant preclinical evidence. Third, the heterogeneity of central nervous system pathophysiology necessitates precise calibration of stimulation parameters across distinct disorders, such as the divergent requirements for glioma versus Alzheimer's disease progression. Resolving these translational hurdles is not merely a prerequisite for advancing the platform into clinical trials but constitutes a critical pathway toward realizing its potential as a clinically viable blood‐brain barrier modulation technology capable of enabling precision neurotherapeutic interventions.

Future Direction

Recent advances in BBB modulation have demonstrated significant clinical relevance through five distinct engineered platforms. Among these, four established strategies—focused ultrasound coupled with lipid microbubbles, hypertonic mannitol administration, transferrin‐targeted nanocarriers, and nasal–brain delivery systems—have provided clinically validated pathways for noninvasive intervention. Each approach addresses specific translational needs: the ultrasound‐microbubble technique achieves sub‐millimeter precision without surgical access, hypertonic mannitol delivers rapid cerebral edema reversal, transferrin‐functionalized carriers ensure biocompatible long‐term accumulation, and nasal routes bypass physiological barriers while maintaining patient compliance.

Conversely, the electrochemical modulation platform represents a fundamental shift in therapeutic design. By integrating microelectrode arrays to dynamically regulate intracellular calcium and potassium ion gradients alongside localized ROS generation, this approach enables spatiotemporal precision in BBB permeability. Critically, it resolves persistent constraints of conventional methods—including target heterogeneity, unpredictable modulation kinetics, and off‐target effects—through real‐time reconfiguration of barrier properties. This capability establishes a new paradigm for condition‐specific CNS therapeutics where therapeutic efficacy can be engineered rather than merely achieved.

Despite substantial clinical progress in BBB engineering, four critical challenges persist that demand immediate interdisciplinary intervention. First, the long‐term biocompatibility of implantable electrochemical devices remains unresolved, with electrode corrosion and persistent inflammatory responses representing significant failure points under chronic in vivo conditions; Second, classic strategies—while clinically effective—face inherent trade‐offs between delivery efficiency and BBB structural integrity, requiring dynamic equilibrium management. Third, translational scalability constraints emerge at multiple stages: nanocarrier production must meet clinical‐grade consistency, while novel electrochemical systems encounter regulatory pathways that lag behind technological maturity. Fourth, the absence of real‐time monitoring for BBB permeability dynamics prevents precise modulation feedback, a critical gap for closed‐loop therapeutic applications.

Future breakthroughs in this field will necessitate strategic refinement of electrochemical mechanisms rather than incremental enhancements to existing platforms. The most promising pathways converge on optimizing in situ electrochemical regulation—specifically by engineering real‐time barrier feedback systems, enhancing device biocompatibility through nanoscale surface modifications, and establishing scalable manufacturing protocols that align with clinical regulatory timelines.

Dynamic Electrochemical–Biohybrid Nanosystem: Core Design Principles

To address the critical gaps in real‐time BBB modulation, we propose a dynamic electrochemical‐biohybrid nanosystem as a transformative platform that integrates electrochemical actuators, bio‐responsive biomolecules, and targeted nanocarriers into a single, programmable architecture capable of spatiotemporally precise, transient modulation of BBB permeability while enabling site‐specific therapeutic delivery. Unlike conventional approaches, this system operates through three convergent mechanisms: (i) electrochemical transient opening of the BBB via localized ion flux regulation (Ca2 +/K+), (ii) biohybrid targeting of endothelial receptors (e.g., transferrin‐mediated endocytosis), and (iii) ROS–ionic crosstalk modulation to prevent pathological oxidative stress. Its design principles center on dynamic reconfigurability—the system self‐adjusts stimulation parameters (voltage, duration) in response to real‐time biomarkers (e.g., ROS levels, TEER) to avoid chronic damage, thereby achieving transient rather than permanent BBB permeability changes and resolving the long‐standing trade‐off between efficacy and barrier integrity. Critically, the system leverages closed‐loop electrochemistry–artificial intelligence (AI) integration to predict optimal modulation windows, ensuring therapeutic precision without off‐target effects. Construction of such a system demands three hierarchical layers: a nanoelectrode interface with high surface area‐to‐volume ratio (e.g., graphene–oxide composites) to minimize corrosion and maximize ion flux; a biohybrid layer incorporating transferrin or other endothelial‐targeting ligands to enhance specificity and reduce immune recognition; and a stimulation‐responsive nanocarrier core (e.g., ROS‐sensitive lipid nanoparticles) that releases cargo exclusively at the modulation site via pH/redox triggers. This layered architecture ensures clinical scalability while preserving the transient operation critical for CNS safety. By unifying electrochemical precision, biohybrid targeting, and real‐time feedback, this system constitutes the first clinically viable mechanism for reversible, condition‐specific BBB modulation without permanent physiological disruption—a capability absent in all existing platforms.

Electrochemistry‐Integrated AI for Closed‐Loop Regulation

Clinical challenges in achieving spatiotemporally precise BBB permeability modulation for chronic neurodegenerative conditions necessitate adaptive electrochemical interfaces. Recent advances demonstrate that machine learning frameworks—trained on integrated in vitro BMEC cultures and in vivo animal models—enable real‐time prediction of optimal electrochemical parameters (voltage, current, and duration) through continuous physiological signal integration (EEG, permeability biomarkers) [118, 119, 120, 121]. This system dynamically recalibrates stimulation protocols to mitigate pathological oxidative stress (e.g., ROS accumulation) and calcium dysregulation (Ca2 + overload), thereby preserving endothelial integrity during repeated BBB opening events. Critically, AI‐optimized device engineering—specifically spatially resolved electrode arrays and biocompatible elastomeric substrates—further enhances long‐term implant stability and tissue integration. Together, this closed‐loop electrochemistry‐AI framework delivers patient‐specific BBB permeability control with clinically actionable precision, directly addressing the therapeutic imperative for sustained modulation in conditions requiring prolonged neural intervention (Figure 4).

Near‐Infrared (NIR)‐Triggered Electrochemical BBB Regulation

The strategic coupling of NIR photothermal/photodynamic mechanisms with electrochemical modulation represents a critical advance in achieving spatiotemporal control of the BBB without invasive intervention [122, 123, 124]. Notably, although NIR wavelengths (700–1100 nm) allow fNIRS systems to obtain signals with source–detector separations of up to 4–5 cm, their therapeutically effective penetration through scalp and skull is limited to around 1–2 cm under clinically tolerable irradiances, with over 99% of incident energy attenuated before reaching brain parenchyma [125]. Even with low thermal and photochemical perturbation, such shallow penetration restricts the noninvasive activation of intracranially embedded electrochemical devices [6, 126, 127]. For instance, NIR‐responsive materials such as graphene oxide and gold nanorods convert incident light into localized thermal gradients or mild electrical currents, thereby initiating in situ electrochemical reactions that generate ROS via type I/II photochemical pathways and modulate ion channel activity. This dual mechanism circumvents the requirement for external electrical connections, achieving targeted BBB disruption in deep brain regions through a synergy between NIR's inherent non‐invasiveness and electrochemistry's precision. Critically, this approach resolves the fundamental constraint of conventional implantable systems that necessitate invasive wiring for operation—demonstrating how integrated photophysical–electrochemical strategies can enable remote, reversible BBB modulation with clinically relevant safety margins. However, intracranial application of these materials requires careful safety evaluation. Graphene oxide can cross the BBB and induce cytotoxicity, with PEGylation exacerbating toxicity in brain endothelial cells [128, 129, 130, 131]. Gold nanorods show shape‐dependent BBB penetration (rod‐shaped > spherical), and their NIR‐II photothermal effect reversibly opens the BBB via occludin regulation, with TEER and permeability recovering within 24 h [132, 133]. These safety profiles—BBB penetration, surface modification‐dependent toxicity, and reversibility—must be rigorously characterized for clinical translation.

Electrochemically Assisted Nanocarrier Targeted Delivery

Electrochemical modulation of the BBB represents a dual mechanism for overcoming the inherent limitations of conventional drug delivery systems: it concurrently targets transcellular transport barriers through Claudin‐5 regulation while enhancing nanocarrier internalization via BMECs through electropermeabilization or electrostatic interactions [134, 135, 136]. Critically, this approach enables spatiotemporally constrained therapeutic engagement—shown by transferrin‐functionalized nanocarriers co‐delivered with mild electrochemical stimulation. The stimulation transiently upregulates TfR expression on BMECs, facilitating enhanced receptor‐mediated endocytosis of chemotherapeutic payloads while simultaneously inducing transient BBB permeability. Moreover, electrochemically responsive nanocarriers (e.g., ROS‐sensitive lipid nanoparticles) can be engineered to execute localized cargo release at the stimulation site through pH/redox‐triggered mechanisms, thereby suppressing off‐target effects. This integrated strategy achieves three convergent objectives—BBB opening, targeted nanocarrier delivery, and site‐specific cargo release—within a single system. By circumventing systemic circulation bottlenecks, the approach yields clinically meaningful therapeutic indices with reduced off‐target toxicity.

Clinical Translational Trends

Future research priorities must prioritize three critical pathways: (i) Engineering in situ degradable electrochemical devices that eliminate secondary surgical interventions; (ii) Elucidating the long‐term impact of electrochemical modulation on BBB homeostatic feedback loops—including potential adaptive changes in Claudin‐5 expression and endothelial metabolic fluxes; (iii) Accelerating clinical translation via large‐animal models compliant with Food and Drug Administration (FDA) / European Medicines Agency (EMA) pathways and real‐world regulatory frameworks. Critically, however, the convergence of machine learning (ML), near‐infrared (NIR) phototherapy, and electrochemical modulation—despite its intellectual promise—lacks preclinical validation of real‐world co‐operation. While closed‐loop electrochemical sensing and ML‐optimized photothermal regimens have been demonstrated in silico, no study has yet validated artifact‐free electrochemical recording during concurrent NIR irradiation in tissue‐mimicking phantoms or shown that ML‐driven adaptive NIR dosing yields a statistically superior therapeutic index over fixed protocols in small‐animal models [137, 138]. Addressing these gaps is therefore essential to ensure translational credibility, as they represent foundational milestones for the safe, spatiotemporally precise control of BBB modulation without permanent physiological disruption. These advances are essential for developing clinically translatable BBB modulation technologies that achieve safe, spatiotemporally precise control while overcoming the persistent therapeutic barrier for central nervous system disorders.

Despite the promising preclinical evidence, several critical translational hurdles must be overcome before electrochemical BBB modulation can enter routine clinical practice. Current clinical deployment of implantable electrochemical systems faces significant hurdles in long‐term electrode stability, where chronic exposure to physiological ionic fluxes induces corrosion and inflammatory responses at the interface. Miniaturization of device components to achieve sub‐millimeter precision for BBB modulation risks compromising signal fidelity due to increased electrical noise and reduced thermal management capacity. Crucially, wireless power transfer for chronic operation must avoid thermal damage to neural tissue while maintaining sufficient energy for electrochemical reactions—currently achievable only through highly specialized resonant coupling mechanisms. Additionally, the risk of off‐target effects escalates in the CNS due to the high density of neural structures, where even localized electrochemical perturbations may trigger unintended signaling cascades. These practical constraints collectively necessitate integrated engineering solutions that prioritize both device longevity and spatial precision to realize the clinical promise of reversible BBB modulation without permanent physiological disruption.

This field has matured from isolated techniques into a confluence of biomaterials engineering, flexible electronics, neurovascular physiology, and computational modeling. The emerging paradigm—where electrochemical ion fluxes and ROS dynamics are engineered with cellular‐scale precision—enables a fundamental shift from non‐specific barrier perturbation toward personalized neurovascular regulation. Such systems hold the potential to transform previously intractable CNS pathologies by establishing the first clinically viable mechanisms for targeted, reversible BBB modulation without permanent physiological disruption.

Beyond electrochemical strategies, other emerging approaches for brain drug delivery continue to progress toward clinical application. Receptor‐mediated transcytosis (RMT), particularly transferrin receptor (TfR1)‐based platforms, has demonstrated the ability to transport therapeutic antibodies, enzymes, and nanoparticles across the intact BBB, with several candidates now in clinical trials [139]. In parallel, intranasal delivery has attracted attention as a noninvasive route that bypasses the BBB via the olfactory and trigeminal pathways, offering advantages in patient compliance and repeated dosing [140]. While these approaches differ fundamentally in mechanism and spatiotemporal control from electrochemical modulation, they represent complementary strategies in the evolving landscape of brain drug delivery, and their clinical maturation may inform the translational path for electrochemical methods as well.

Conclusion

The blood–brain barrier (BBB), while essential for CNS homeostasis, critically impedes therapeutic efficacy in neurodegenerative disorders by restricting spatially resolved modulation of extracellular ions and reactive oxygen species (ROS) within the neural microenvironment. Although physical, chemical, biological, and bypass strategies have been developed to penetrate the BBB, these approaches remain clinically constrained by insufficient spatiotemporal resolution in neural interfaces, incomplete mechanistic understanding of ROS‐ionic crosstalk, and scalability limitations of nanoscale delivery systems. This review not only synthesizes advances in electrochemical platforms, machine learning–guided analytics, near‐infrared phototherapy, and biocompatible nanocarriers but also introduces a fifth transformative platform: a dynamic electrochemical‐biohybrid nanosystem that achieves real‐time ROS‐ionic microenvironment regulation via transient BBB permeability induction and targeted nanocarrier delivery. Future breakthroughs will require synergistic integration of AI‐driven closed‐loop feedback, programmable NIR‐photothermal strategies, and clinically scalable biodegradable nanocarriers to overcome translational barriers and realize clinically translatable, precision neuroregulation for neurodegenerative diseases.

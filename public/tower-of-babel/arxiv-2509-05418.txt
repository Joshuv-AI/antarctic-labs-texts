A class of regularization schemes for linear ill-posed problems in Banach spaces under low order source conditions
Robert Plato

Abstract
In this work we consider, in a Banach space framework, the regularization of linear ill-posed problems. Our focus is on the recovery of solutions that have a logarithmic source representation. Such cases typically occur in exponentially ill-posed problems like the backwards heat equation. The mathematical framework on logarithms of operators is provided, and convergence rates for a class of parametric regularization schemes are deduced. This class includes the iterated version of Lavrentiev’s method and the method of the abstract Cauchy problem. The presentation includes both a priori and a posteriori parameter choice strategies. Finally, we present an example for a logarithmic source representable function with respect to the integration operator.



Keywords
:

linear ill-posed problem, inverse problem,
logarithmic source condition, low order source condition,
exponentially ill-posed problem,
mixed source condition,
regularization, Lavrentiev’s method, method of abstract Cauchy problem,
a priori parameter choice strategy, discrepancy principle,
logarithmic interpolation inequality.

1
Introduction

In this paper, we consider linear equations

\displaystyle Au=f,

(1)

where
A:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{X}
is a bounded linear operator in a Banach space
\mathcal{X}
with a non-closed range
\mathcal{R}(A)
. Moreover we suppose that
the right-hand side
f
belongs to the range
\mathcal{R}(A)

of
A
and is given approximately
by
f^{\delta}\in\mathcal{X}
as follows,

f\in\mathcal{R}(A),\quad f^{\delta}\in\mathcal{X},\qquad\|\hskip 1.13809ptf-f^{\delta}\hskip 1.13809pt\|\leq\delta,

where
\delta\geq 0
is a given noise level. The fact that
A
has a non-closed range implies that equation (
1
) is ill-posed, i.e., the solution depends discontinuously on the data
f
. We assume here for the moment that the operator
A
is one-to-one, which means that solutions of equations
Au=f
are unique for each
f\in\mathcal{R}(A)
.
Some examples are considered in Example
1
below.
The main subject of the present paper is the consideration of equations (
1
) having solutions with a
logarithmic source-wise representation, i.e.,
u\in\mathcal{D}((\log A)^{\nu})
for some integer
\nu\geq 1
. This in fact is a typical situation for exponentially ill-posed problems, e.g., the backwards heat equation. In that case, the operator
A
is of the form

A=\exp(-tB)
with some
t>0
and some differential operator
B
. We then have

\log A=-tB
and thus
\mathcal{D}((\log A)^{\nu})=\mathcal{D}(B^{\nu})
, so that exactly the elements of
\mathcal{D}((\log A)^{\nu})
have certain differentiability properties.

Equations with logarithmic source-wise representable solutions are considered in several works, see e.g.,
[
16
,
26
,
9
,
17
,
8
,
14
,
10
,
12
,
11
,
24
,
7
]
. The latter two papers include a Banach space setting, but the considerations are restricted to Tikhonov regularization. In the present work, our focus is on other regularization schemes in a Banach space framework, e.g., the Lavrentiev method. The considerations are restricted to linear problems, however.

Our consideration are not restricted to genuine logarithmic source-wise representations. More generally, we will consider mixed source conditions of the form

u=A^{p}v
for some
v\in\mathcal{D}((\log A)^{\nu})
,
with some
p\geq 0,\ \nu\in\mathbb{N}
. Those extentions are obtained without much additional efforts and maybe of independent interest.

The present work can be considered as an extention of the work

[
22
]
, where a similar setting is considered for power-type source-wise representable solutions
u\in\mathcal{R}(A^{p})
for some
p>0
. For related work
on power-type source-wise representable solutions for linear operators in Banach spaces, we refer to
[
18
]
.

The outline of the remainder of the paper is as follows. The following section provides some basic facts on positive type operators and its fractional powers,
which serve as a basic ingredient in the analysis. Moreover, a class of regularization schemes is introduced in that section, which includes the Lavrentiev method and its iterated version. Section
3
introduces the logarithm of a bounded positive type operator and some of its properties with impact to regularization. In Section
4
,
for the considered class of regularization schemes, we present convergence rates
under mixed source conditions. This is done
both for appropriate a priori- as well as a posteriori parameter choice strategies, where the latter rule serves as a discrepancy principle.
As a by-product, we obtain a logarithmic interpolation inequality which may be of independent interest.
In Section
5
, we present an example of a function that satisfies a logarithmic source condition.
In an appendix, some useful results for a special class of real-valued, logarithm-based functions are provided that can be utilized for the derivation of error estimates under mixed source conditions.

2
Positive type operators, fractional powers, and regularization operators

2.1
Positive type operators

Let
\mathcal{X}
be a Banach space and
A:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{X}
be a bounded linear operator of positive type, i.e.,

\displaystyle A+\alpha I:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{X}\ \textup{one-to-one and onto},\qquad\|\hskip 1.13809pt(A+\alpha I)^{-1}\hskip 1.13809pt\|\leq\frac{\kappa_{*}}{\alpha},\quad\alpha>0,

(2)

for some finite constant
\kappa_{*}>0
.
Property (
2
) provides the basic ingredient both for defining fractional powers and the logarithm of the operator
A
, respectively. It is also needed for
the Lavrentiev method and its iterated version.

Example 1
.

(a)

For every bounded linear operator which is self-adjoint and positive semi-definite on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{X}
, condition (
2
) is satisfied with
\kappa_{*}=1
.

(b)

Consider the integration operator

\displaystyle(Ju)(x)=\mathop{\raisebox{-0.28453pt}{\mbox{\Large$\textstyle\int$}}}\nolimits_{\hskip-2.84526pt0}^{x}\hskip-0.56905ptu(y)\,dy\qquad(0\leq x\leq a,\quad u\in\mathcal{X}).

It defines a bounded linear operator
J:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{X}
of positive type in each of the following cases:

\mathcal{X}=C[0,a]:=\{\,u:[0,a]\to\mathbb{R}\mid u\textup{ continuous }\,\},\ \mathcal{X}=C_{0}[0,a]:=\{\,u\in C[0,a]:u(0)=0\,\}
, and

\mathcal{X}=L^{r}(0,a)
for
1\leq r\leq\infty
, cf.
, e.g.,
[
21
,
23
]
.

(c)

Consider the Abel integration operator

\displaystyle(J_{p}u)(x)=\dfrac{\mbox{\footnotesize\raisebox{-1.42262pt}{$1$}}}{\mbox{\footnotesize\raisebox{2.27621pt}{$\Gamma(p)$}}}\mathop{\raisebox{-0.28453pt}{\mbox{\Large$\textstyle\int$}}}\nolimits_{\hskip-2.84526pt0}^{x}\hskip-0.56905pt\dfrac{\mbox{\footnotesize\raisebox{-1.42262pt}{$u(y)$}}}{\mbox{\footnotesize\raisebox{2.27621pt}{$(x-y)^{1-p}$}}}\,dy\quad\text{for}\ \ 0\leq x\leq a,\quad u\in\mathcal{X},

(3)

where
p>0
, and
\Gamma
denotes Euler’s gamma function. Moreover,

\mathcal{X}
denotes one of the spaces considered
in part
(b)
of this example.
For each
0<p<1
and each such space
\mathcal{X}
, the Abel integral operator

J_{p}:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{X}
is of positive type, respectively, cf.

[
21
,
23
]
.

2.2
Fractional powers

Fractional powers of a positive type operator
A:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{X}
in a Banach space
\mathcal{X}
may be utilized to introduce an intermediate degree of smoothness of solutions of
Au=f
. In the present paper, they will be also used to introduce the logarithm of the operator
A
.

Fractional powers of a bounded positive type operator
A:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{X}
may be defined as follows, cf.

[
1
,
2
]
or
[
5
, Proposition 3.1.12]
:

(a)

For
0<p<1
, the fractional power
A^{p}:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{X}
is defined by the improper Bochner integral

\displaystyle A^{p}u:=\dfrac{\mbox{\footnotesize\raisebox{-1.42262pt}{$\sin\pi p$}}}{\mbox{\footnotesize\raisebox{2.27621pt}{$\pi$}}}\mathop{\raisebox{-0.28453pt}{\mbox{\Large$\textstyle\int$}}}\nolimits_{\hskip-2.84526pt0}^{\infty}\hskip-0.56905pts^{p-1}(A+sI)^{-1}Au\,ds\quad\text{for}\ \ u\in\mathcal{X}.

(4)

(b)

For arbitrary values
p>0
,
the bounded linear operator
A^{p}:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{X}
is defined by

\displaystyle A^{p}:=A^{p-\lfloor p\rfloor}A^{\lfloor p\rfloor},

(5)

where
\lfloor p\rfloor
denotes the largest integer
\leq p
, and moreover we use the notation
A^{0}=I
.

Example 2
.

(a)

For a bounded linear operator which is self-adjoint and positive semi-definite on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{X}
, representations (
4
), (
5
) on one side and the standard definition of fractional powers based on spectral calculus result in the same operators.

(b)

The fractional powers of the integration operator
J
coincide with the Abel operators, i.e.,
J^{p}=J_{p}
for
p>0
. This holds in each of the spaces
\mathcal{X}
considered in Example
1

(b)
.
For details, cf.
e.g.,
[
21
,
23
]
.

\vartriangle

We note that

\displaystyle A^{p+q}=A^{p}A^{q}\quad\text{for}\ \ p,q\geq 0,\quad A^{p}u\to u\quad\text{as}\ \ p\downarrow 0\quad\text{for}\ \ u\in\overline{\mathcal{R}(A)},

(6)

cf.
[
5
, Propositions 3.1.1 and 3.1.15]
).
The statements in (
6
)
imply that the operators
A^{p},\,p\geq 0,
define a
C_{0}
-semigroup on
\overline{\mathcal{R}(A)}
. This fact has great impact throughout the paper.

From (
6
) and the definition
(
4
), it moreover follows that

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}(A^{p_{2}})\subset\mathcal{R}(A^{p_{1}})\subset\overline{\mathcal{R}(A)}\textup{ for all }\;0<p_{1}<p_{2}<\infty.

(7)

In what follows, we shall need the interpolation inequality for fractional powers of operators, see, e.g.,

[
13
]
or

[
5
, Proposition 6.6.4]
:
for each pair of real numbers
0<p<q
,
there exists some finite constant
c_{\ref{ccinterpol}}=c_{\ref{ccinterpol}}(p,q)>0
such that

\displaystyle\|\hskip 1.13809ptA^{p}u\hskip 1.13809pt\|\leq c_{\ref{ccinterpol}}\|\hskip 1.13809ptA^{q}u\hskip 1.13809pt\|^{\frac{p}{q}}\|\hskip 1.13809ptu\hskip 1.13809pt\|^{1-\frac{p}{q}}\quad\text{for}\ \ u\in\mathcal{X}.

(8)

For
0<p<1=q
, the constant may be chosen as
c_{\ref{ccinterpol}}=2(\kappa_{*}+1)
, cf., e.g.,
[
21
, Corollary 1.1.19]
.

Throughout the paper, we assume that the operator
A
is one-to-one,
with a possibly non-dense range in
\mathcal{X}
,
and in addition the inverse
A^{-1}
is an unbounded operator.

2.3
A class of regularization schemes

In what follows, we consider bounded linear regularization operators associated with a bounded positive type operator
A:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{X}
,

\displaystyle R_{\alpha}:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{X}\quad\text{for}\ \ \alpha>0

(9)

and its companion operators

\displaystyle S_{\alpha}:=I-R_{\alpha}A\quad\text{for}\ \ \alpha>0.

(10)

We assume that the following conditions are satisfied:

\displaystyle\|\hskip 1.13809ptR_{\alpha}\hskip 1.13809pt\|

\displaystyle\leq\dfrac{\mbox{\footnotesize\raisebox{-1.42262pt}{$e_{*}$}}}{\mbox{\footnotesize\raisebox{2.27621pt}{$\alpha$}}}\quad\text{for}\ \ \alpha>0,

(11)

\displaystyle\|\hskip 1.13809ptS_{\alpha}A^{p}\hskip 1.13809pt\|

\displaystyle\leq e_{p}\alpha^{p}\quad\text{for}\ \ \alpha>0\qquad(0\leq p\leq p_{0}),

(12)

\displaystyle R_{\alpha}A

\displaystyle=AR_{\alpha}\quad\text{for}\ \ \alpha>0,

(13)

where
0<p_{0}\leq\infty
denotes the possibly infinite saturation, and
e_{*}
and
e_{p}
denote finite constants. We assume that
e_{p}
is bounded on bounded intervals for
p
. In the case
p_{0}=\infty
,
condition (
12
) is considered for
p<\infty
.
Note that condition (
12
) in particular implies

S_{\alpha}u\to 0
as
\alpha\to 0
for each
u\in\mathcal{X}
and thus

R_{\alpha}f\to A^{-1}f
as
\alpha\to 0
for each
f\in\mathcal{R}(A)
, if

A
is a one-to-one operator with dense range.

We are now in a position to introduce
regularizing elements

\displaystyle{u}_{\alpha}^{\delta}:=\overline{u}-R_{\alpha}(A\overline{u}-f^{\delta})\quad\text{for}\ \ \alpha>0,

(14)

where
\overline{u}\in\mathcal{X}
denotes an initial guess to the sought-for-solution of the equation
Au=f
.

Example 3
.

We first consider the
m
-times iterated Lavrentiev method, where

m\geq 1
denotes a fixed integer. For
f^{\delta}\in\mathcal{X}
and
v_{0}=\overline{u}\in\mathcal{X}
, the element
{u}_{\alpha}^{\delta}\in\mathcal{X}

is given by

\displaystyle(A+\alpha I)v_{n}

\displaystyle=\alpha v_{n-1}+f^{\delta}\quad\text{for}\ \ n=1,2,\ldots,m,\qquad{u}_{\alpha}^{\delta}:=v_{m}.

This method is of the form (
14
) with

R_{\alpha}=\alpha^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\alpha^{j}(A+\alpha I)^{-j},

and the companion operator is given by
S_{\alpha}=\alpha^{m}(A+\alpha I)^{-m}
.
For
m=1
, this gives Lavrentiev’s classical regularization method,

R_{\alpha}=(A+\alpha I)^{-1}
.
For the
m
-times iterated Lavrentiev method, the conditions (
11
)–(
13
) are satisfied with

p_{0}=m
. In fact,
for any integer
0\leq p\leq m
,
estimate (
12
) holds with constant
e_{p}=(\kappa_{*}+1)^{m}
, see

[
21
, Lemma 1.1.8]
.
From this intermediate result and the interpolation inequality (
8
), inequality (
12
) then follows
for non-integer values
0<p<m
, with constant
e_{p}=2(\kappa_{*}+1)^{p+1}
.

\vartriangle

Example 4
.

Let
A:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{X}
be a bounded linear operator on a complex Banach space
\mathcal{X}
.
In the present example, we assume that
A
satisfies a sectorial condition stronger than
(
2
) considered for positive type operators. More precisely, we assume that
for each

\lambda=re^{\textup{i}\varphi}
, with

r>0,\ |\hskip 0.85358pt\varphi\hskip 0.85358pt|\leq\frac{\pi}{2}+\varepsilon
with some
\varepsilon>0
,
the operator
A+\lambda I:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{X}
is one-to-one and onto, and its inverse operator satisfies
\|\hskip 1.13809pt(A+\lambda I)^{-1}\hskip 1.13809pt\|\leq\frac{\kappa_{*}}{|\hskip 0.85358pt\lambda\hskip 0.85358pt|}
, where
\kappa_{*}>0
denotes a finite constant.
Examples of this type are given by the Abel integral operators
J_{p},\ 0<p<1
, considered in
Example
1
. On the other hand, the simple integration operator considered in the same example does not satisfy such a strong sectorial condition.
We refer to
[
21
,
23
]
for more details.

For any operator
A
satisfying such a strong sectorial condition and any initial approximation
\overline{u}\in\mathcal{X}
, the abstract Cauchy problem,

\displaystyle(u^{\delta})^{\prime}(t)+Au^{\delta}(t)=f^{\delta}\quad\text{for}\ \ t>0,\quad u^{\delta}(0)=\overline{u},

(15)

has a unique solution
u^{\delta}(t),\ t\geq 0
.
The regularizing elements are then defined as

\displaystyle{u}_{\alpha}^{\delta}:=u^{\delta}(t)\quad\text{for}\ \ \alpha=\tfrac{1}{t},\ t>0,

and they in fact can be written in the form (
14
) with

R_{\alpha}f=\mathop{\raisebox{-0.28453pt}{\mbox{\Large$\textstyle\int$}}}\nolimits_{\hskip-2.84526pt0}^{t}\hskip-0.56905pte^{-sA}f\,ds\quad\text{for}\ \ f\in\mathcal{X},\ t=\tfrac{1}{\alpha},\ \alpha>0.

The companion operator is given by
S_{\alpha}=e^{-tA}
for
t=\frac{1}{\alpha},\ \alpha>0
.
For this method, the conditions (
11
)–(
13
) are satisfied with
qualification
p_{0}=\infty
. See
[
21
, Theorems 1.2.10 and 2.1.4]
for more details.

3
The logarithm
\bm{\log A}

3.1
Definition of
\bm{\log A}
, first properties

We next consider logarithmic source conditions, occasionally called low order smoothness. For this, we need to introduce the logarithm
\log A
of a bounded positive type operator
A
. For selfadjoint operators in Hilbert spaces, this can be done by spectral analysis, and we refer in this context for example to
[
9
,
26
]
.
In Banach spaces, the operator
\log A
may be defined as the infinitesimal generator of the
C_{0}
-semigroup of operators
A^{p},\,p\geq 0,
considered on
\overline{\mathcal{R}(A)}
:

\displaystyle(\log A)u

\displaystyle=\lim_{p\downarrow 0}\tfrac{1}{p}(A^{p}u-u),\quad u\in\mathcal{D}(\log A),

(16)

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}(\log A)

\displaystyle=\{\,u\in\mathcal{X}:\lim_{p\downarrow 0}\tfrac{1}{p}(A^{p}u-u)\ \textup{exists}\,\},

cf., e.g.,
[
19
,
4
]
or
[
5
, Proposition 3.5.3]
.
For Tikhonov regularization with oversmoothing penalty term to solve nonlinear ill-posed problems in Banach scales,
the definition (
16
) has already been utilized in the two papers

[
24
,
7
]

to describe low order smoothness of solutions.
In the present paper, low order smoothness of an element
u\in\mathcal{X}
by definition means

u\in\mathcal{D}((\log A)^{\nu})
for some
\nu\in\mathbb{N}
.
Note that we obviously have
\mathcal{D}((\log A)^{\nu})\subset\overline{\mathcal{R}(A)}
. In addition,
\mathcal{R}(A^{p})\subset\mathcal{D}((\log A)^{\nu})
is valid for arbitrarily small
p>0
,
which follows from
[
19
, Satz 1]
. Summarizing the above notes, we have a chain of inclusions in
\mathcal{X}
as

\mathcal{R}(A^{p})\subset\mathcal{D}((\log A)^{\nu_{2}})\subset\mathcal{D}((\log A)^{\nu_{1}})\subset\overline{\mathcal{R}(A)}\quad\textup{for}\ \nu_{1},\nu_{2}\in\mathbb{N},\ \nu_{1}\leq\nu_{2},\ p>0.

(17)

This in particular means that any Hölder-type smoothness is stronger than low order smoothness, a property that is already well known in the Hilbert space setting.

3.2
Auxiliary results for
\bm{\log A}

Throughout this subsection, we consider a bounded linear operator

A:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{X}
of positive type on a Banach space
\mathcal{X}
.

3.2.1
Introductory remarks

Below, for elements
u\in\mathcal{X}
we consider mixed smoothness conditions of the form

\displaystyle u=A^{p}v\textup{ for some }v\in\mathcal{D}((\log A)^{\nu}),\quad 0\leq p<p_{0},\quad\nu\in\mathbb{N}.

(18)

We define a source element for
u
as follows,

\displaystyle w=(\lambda I-\log A)^{\nu}v,\quad\textup{ with }\lambda>\log\|\hskip 1.13809ptA\hskip 1.13809pt\|\textup{ arbitrary but fixed}.

(19)

Here and in what follows, for any real number
x>0
, we denote by
\log x
the natural logarithm of
x
.
The lower bound for
\lambda
considered in (
19
)
guarantees that
the operator
\lambda I-\log A
has a bounded inverse,
cf.
the proof of the following theorem.
This in fact is the reason for considering source elements
w
in the shifted form (
19
). It allows
to rewrite smoothness condition (
18
) in the form

\displaystyle u=A^{p}(\lambda I-\log A)^{-\nu}w,

(20)

so that the element
w
indeed can be conceived as a source element for
u
.
In (
20
), the operator
(\lambda I-\log A)^{-\nu}
denotes the inverse operator of
(\lambda I-\log A)^{\nu}
.

Remark 5
.

(a)

Note that the operators
\lambda I-\log A
and
\log A
have the same domains of definition, so the shift of
\log A
considered in (
19
) does not really change the mixed smoothness condition (
18
).

(b)

In the case
\|\hskip 1.13809ptA\hskip 1.13809pt\|<1
,
the source representation (
19
) can be obtained without any shift, i.e.,
w=(\log A)^{\nu}v
, since we may choose
\lambda=0
then.
Note that the norm condition on
A
can always be obtained by rescaling the equation
Au=f
to
aAu=af
, with
a>0
sufficiently small such that the inequality
a\|\hskip 1.13809ptA\hskip 1.13809pt\|<1
is satisfied.
Note also that such a rescaling does not change the smoothness condition
(
18
) since we have
(aA)^{p}=a^{p}A^{p}
and

\log(aA)=(\log a)I+\log A
, which easily follows from the respective definitions.

3.2.2
Decay of
\|\hskip 1.13809ptS_{\alpha}u\hskip 1.13809pt\|

Throughout this subsection, we consider regularizing operators
(
9
)
that satisfy the conditions (
11
)–(
13
).
It is immediate from (
12
) that
for elements of the form
u\in\mathcal{R}(A^{p})
with
p\leq p_{0}
, we have

\|\hskip 1.13809ptS_{\alpha}u\hskip 1.13809pt\|=\mathcal{O}(\alpha^{p})
as
\alpha\to 0
.
The following theorem provides a similar
decay property of

S_{\alpha}u
as
\alpha\to 0

for elements
u
satisfying a mixed source condition (
18
), which in fact serves as the fundamental ingredient for the results presented below.
Its proof utilizes
C_{0}
-semigroup theory, in particular the Hille–Yoshida theorem. The theorem is an extention of
[
24
, Lemma 11]
, where
the case
\nu=1

is considered.

Theorem 6
.

Under the mixed smoothness condition (
18
), we have

\|\hskip 1.13809ptS_{\alpha}u\hskip 1.13809pt\|\leq c_{\ref{cclor_a}}\|\hskip 1.13809ptw\hskip 1.13809pt\|{\alpha^{p}(\log\tfrac{1}{\alpha})^{-\nu}}\quad\text{for}\ \ 0<\alpha<1,

where
w
is given by (
19
), and
c_{\ref{cclor_a}}>0
denotes some finite constant which may depend on
\lambda
considered in
(
19
).

Proof.

There holds
\|\hskip 1.13809ptA^{q}\hskip 1.13809pt\|\leq Ce^{\omega q}
for
q\geq 0
,
where
\omega=\log\|\hskip 1.13809ptA\hskip 1.13809pt\|
(if
A\neq 0
), and
C>0
denote a suitable constant.
This estimate follows easily, e.g., from the interpolation inequality (
8
)
applied with
0<p<1=q
, the semigroup property (
6
) as well as the submultiplicativity of the norm of products of operators.
Thus each real
\lambda>\omega
belongs to the resolvent set of the operator
\log A:\overline{\mathcal{R}(A)}\supset\mathcal{D}(\log A)\to\overline{\mathcal{R}(A)}
, i.e.,

(\lambda I-\log A)^{-1}:\overline{\mathcal{R}(A)}\to\overline{\mathcal{R}(A)}
exists and defines a bounded operator,
cf.

[
20
, Theorem 5.3, Chapter 1]
.
Since

\mathcal{R}((\lambda I-\log A)^{-\nu})=\mathcal{D}((\lambda I-\log A)^{\nu})=\mathcal{D}((\log A)^{\nu}),

we may represent
u
as

u=A^{p}(\lambda I-\log A)^{-\nu}w,

for some
w\in\overline{\mathcal{R}(A)}
.
As a preparation for the considerations below, we now introduce the finite number
p_{1}
, which is defined by
p_{1}=p_{0}
, if the saturation
p_{0}
from (
12
) is finite,
and otherwise choose
p_{1}>p
arbitrary but finite.
Since (cf.
[
20
, proof of Theorem 5.3, Chapter 1]
)

(\lambda I-\log A)^{-\nu}w=\tfrac{1}{(\nu-1)!}\int_{0}^{\infty}q^{\nu-1}e^{-\lambda q}A^{q}w\,dq,

we have

\displaystyle S_{\alpha}u=\int_{0}^{\infty}h(q)\,dq=\int_{0}^{p_{1}-p}h(q)\,dq+\int_{p_{1}-p}^{\infty}h(q)\,dq=:y_{1}+y_{2},

(21)

where

\displaystyle h(q)=\tfrac{1}{(\nu-1)!}q^{\nu-1}e^{-\lambda q}S_{\alpha}A^{p+q}w,\quad q>0.

The element
y_{1}
in (
21
) can be estimated as

\|\hskip 1.13809pty_{1}\hskip 1.13809pt\|\leq\tfrac{c_{\ref{cclor_b}}}{(\nu-1)!}\|\hskip 1.13809ptw\hskip 1.13809pt\|\int_{0}^{p_{1}-p}q^{\nu-1}\alpha^{p+q}\,dq
, for some finite constant
c_{\ref{cclor_b}}>0
,
which follows from (
12
) and the corresponding boundedness assumption on the involved coefficient
e_{p}
introduced in (
12
).
Repeated partial integration finally gives the estimate

\tfrac{1}{(\nu-1)!}\int_{0}^{p_{1}-p}q^{\nu-1}\alpha^{p+q}\,dq\leq\alpha^{p}\frac{1}{|\hskip 0.85358pt\log\alpha\hskip 0.85358pt|^{\nu}}

\displaystyle y_{2}=\tfrac{1}{(\nu-1)!}\int_{p_{1}-p}^{\infty}q^{\nu-1}e^{-\lambda q}S_{\alpha}A^{p_{1}}A^{q-(p_{1}-p)}w\,dq,

and thus we can estimate as follows:

\displaystyle\|\hskip 1.13809pty_{2}\hskip 1.13809pt\|

\displaystyle\leq c_{\ref{cclor_c}}\|\hskip 1.13809ptw\hskip 1.13809pt\|\int_{p_{1}-p}^{\infty}q^{\nu-1}e^{-\lambda q}\alpha^{p_{1}}e^{\omega(q-(p_{1}-p))}\,dq

\displaystyle=c_{\ref{cclor_c}}\|\hskip 1.13809ptw\hskip 1.13809pt\|e^{-\omega(p_{1}-p)}\int_{p_{1}-p}^{\infty}q^{\nu-1}e^{-(\lambda-\omega)q}\,dq\ \alpha^{p_{1}},

for some finite constant

where the latter integral is finite and independent of
\alpha

Finally, elementary calculations show that
w(x)\to 0
holds as
x\to 0
,
since
\kappa>1
.
Thus the function
Su
is also differentiable at
x=0
, with

(Su)^{\prime}(0)=0
as claimed.

6
Conclusions and outlook

In this work, we have considered the regularization of linear ill-posed problems
Au=f
with positive type operators
A:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{X}
in a Banach space
\mathcal{X}
.
Our focus is on the stable recovery of solutions
u^{\dagger}\in\mathcal{X}
that allow a logarithmic source representation
u^{\dagger}=A^{p}v\textup{ for some }v\in\mathcal{D}((\log A)^{\nu})
, with some
p\geq 0
and some integer
\nu\geq 1
.
Convergence rates for a class of parametric regularization schemes have been deduced that include the iterated version of Lavrentiev’s method and the method of the abstract Cauchy problem. The presentation includes both a priori and a posteriori parameter choice strategies.

Further projects in this direction not considered in this paper are the implementation of numerical experiments, the consideration of iterative methods as regularization schemes, and, last but not least, smoothness conditions of the form

u^{\dagger}=(\log A)^{\nu}A^{p}v
.

In the analysis of mixed order rates, the functions

\displaystyle\chi_{q,\pm\mu}(t)

\displaystyle=t^{q}(\log\tfrac{1}{t})^{\pm\mu},\quad 0<t<1\qquad(q\geq 0,\,\mu>0),

(40)

are of major relevance. Below we state some elementary properties of those functions, cf. also
[
9
,
16
,
26
]
.

(a)

The function
\chi_{q,-\mu}
is strictly increasing on the interval
(0,1)
, with
\chi_{q,-\mu}(t)\to 0
as
t\to 0
.

(b)

For
q>0
,
the inverse function
\chi^{-1}_{q,-\mu}:(0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}

satisfies

\chi^{-1}_{q,-\mu}(s)\sim q^{-\mu/q}s^{1/q}(\log\tfrac{1}{s})^{\mu/q}
as
s\to 0
.
This implies in particular that, for each fixed
0<s_{0}<1
, we have

\displaystyle\chi^{-1}_{q,-\mu}(s)\asymp s^{\frac{1}{q}}(\log\tfrac{1}{s})^{\frac{\mu}{q}}=\chi_{\frac{1}{q},\frac{\mu}{q}}(s),\quad 0<s\leq s_{0}.

(c)

For each constant
\kappa>0
, we have

\chi_{q,\pm\mu}(\kappa t)\sim\kappa^{q}\chi_{q,\pm\mu}(t)
as
t\to 0
,
and thus in particular

\displaystyle\chi_{q,\pm\mu}(\kappa t)\asymp\chi_{q,\pm\mu}(t),\quad 0<t\leq t_{0},

for each
t_{0}<\min\{1,1/\kappa\}
fixed.

Here, for two positive, real-valued functions
f,g:(0,t_{0})\to\mathbb{R}
, the notation
f(t)\sim g(t)
as
t\to 0
means
f(t)/g(t)\to 1
as
t\to 0
. In addition,
f(t)\asymp g(t)
for
t\in I\subset(0,t_{0})
means that there are finite positive constants
a_{1},a_{2}
such that

a_{1}f(t)\leq g(t)\leq a_{2}f(t)
for
t\in I
.

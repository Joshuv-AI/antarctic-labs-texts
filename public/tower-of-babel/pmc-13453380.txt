Chrysoeriol Alleviates Psoriasis Inflammation and Keratinocyte Hyperproliferation Possibly Through Inhibiting AKT/NF-κB Signaling Activation
Authors: Xiaoyue Qi, Luoming Zhang, Yaoxin Gao, Shoukui Hu, Yiming Shao, Xiaoyan Jia, Shujuan Zhang, Baodong Ma
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
PurposeChrysoeriol (CHR), a naturally derived and non-toxic compound, exhibits potent immunomodulatory and anti-inflammatory activities. This investigation assessed CHR’s anti-inflammatory effects and elucidated the mechanism.MethodsFor assessing CHR’s anti-psoriatic efficacy, imiquimod (IMQ)-induced murine models and LPS-treated HaCaT cells were used. The anti-inflammatory characteristics of CHR were analyzed using the Psoriasis Area Severity Index (PASI), immunohistochemistry, immunofluorescence, and flow cytometry. Furthermore, transcriptome sequencing, immunoblotting, and qRT-PCR were applied to investigate how CHR regulates psoriasis at the molecular level.ResultsIn our research, we found that topical CHR administration significantly alleviated IMQ-induced psoriatic manifestations in mice, demonstrating reduced erythema, decreased epidermal thickening, and diminished scaling. Histopathological improvements correlated with lower PASI scores and reduced splenomegaly. CHR restrained excessive activation of Th1/Th17 cells, inhibited keratinocyte hyperproliferation and blunted inflammatory responses, accompanied by decreased phosphorylation of AKT and p65.ConclusionCHR exerts robust anti-psoriatic effects by suppressing keratinocyte over-proliferation and restoring Th1/Th17 immune homeostasis. Our data reveal a correlation between CHR intervention and suppressed AKT/NF-κB signaling activation, suggesting CHR may serve as a potential candidate for psoriasis treatment.

Introduction

Psoriasis characterized with immune dysregulation triggered by interaction between polygenic inheritance and environmental factors.1 The pathophysiological basis of psoriasis encompasses the formation of red, raised patches (erythematous plaques), accelerated epidermal cell proliferation, excessive scaling, and abnormal differentiation of keratinocytes.2–4 At the core of the disease’s pathological development lies the disrupted communication between keratinocytes and malfunctioning immune cells. The IL-23/IL-17 signaling pathway plays a pivotal role in sustaining chronic inflammation in the skin. IL-23 stimulates the growth of immune cells that produce IL-17. In turn, IL-17 prompts keratinocytes to release pro-inflammatory substances.5 This creates a vicious cycle that fuels epidermal thickening, attracts neutrophils, and leads to an influx of inflammatory cells. Given this self-reinforcing mechanism, targeting the overexpression of IL-23/IL-17 is crucial for therapeutic interventions aimed at halting the autoimmune processes underlying psoriasis.6–8

According to available epidemiological data, psoriasis affects roughly 1–4% of the global populace, which amounts to about 125 million individuals across the globe. Additionally, people with psoriasis have a higher likelihood of developing concurrent conditions, including psoriatic arthritis, inflammatory bowel disease, and cardiovascular problems.9 These comorbidities lead to considerable psychological distress and socioeconomic hardships for patients. At present, a range of psoriasis therapies, including glucocorticoids, retinoids, immunosuppressive agents, systemic medications, and biological drugs, are in widespread use. Nevertheless, their efficacy is hindered by multiple factors. These encompass low retention of the drug in the skin, inadequate skin permeability, unfavorable physical and chemical properties of topical formulations, and gastrointestinal complications arising from systemic drug intake.10 Hence, there is a critical need to create new and well-tolerated therapeutic options that can reduce the pathological manifestations of psoriasis.

Traditional Chinese Medicine (TCM) has a long-standing history of practice spanning centuries and has recently seen a resurgence of interest on a global scale, especially after Chinese pharmacologist Youyou Tu’s groundbreaking discovery of artemisinin.11 Chrysoeriol has been found in a variety of medicinal plants and demonstrates numerous pharmacological activities.12,13 These include antioxidant, smooth muscle relaxant, anti-inflammatory, anti-obesity, and immunomodulatory effects.14–16 A prior study has shown that chrysoeriol can mitigate the upregulation of COX-2 in LPS-activated murine macrophages via mediating NF-κB and MAPK signaling pathways.17 Moreover, Law et al reported that chrysoeriol decreased pro-inflammatory cytokines expression.18 However, the existing literature offers scant evidence on the therapeutic efficacy and underlying mechanisms of this flavone in autoimmune dermatoses, which significantly limits its potential for clinical translation.

In the current study, we indicated that CHR could alleviate psoriasis by suppressing the hyperproliferation of keratinocytes, production of pro-inflammatory cytokines, as well as infiltration of Th1/Th17 cells. From a mechanistic perspective, CHR achieves these outcomes by inhibiting AKT/NF-κB. These findings position CHR as a highly promising candidate for the clinical management of psoriasis.

Materials and Methods

For in vitro cell experiments, each independently cultured cell batch was regarded as one biological replicate, while repeated detection of the same sample was defined as a technical replicate. All cell viability assays were performed with three independent biological replicates. For animal studies, five mice were set as biological replicates for each experimental group.

Pinpointing the Prospective Targets That CHR May Act Upon in Psoriasis

To identify chrysoeriol (CHR) targets, Traditional Chinese Medicine Systems Pharmacology (TCMSP, https://tcmsp-e.com/), Encyclopedia of Traditional Chinese Medicine (ETCM, http://www.tcmip.cn/ETCM/index.php/Home/), and Swiss Target Prediction (http://www.swisstargetprediction.ch/) were employed to find the tartets. These targets were then mapped to standardized human gene names via the Universal Protein Resource (https://www.uniprot.org) with the species restricted to Homo sapiens. After eliminating duplicates across the three databases, the final set of CHR targets was established. Relevant targets were retrieved from the GeneCards (https://www.genecards.org/) databases to defined the related genes using the keyword “psoriasis”.

Standardized compound and disease targets were imported into Venny 2.1.0 (https://bioinfogp.cnb.csic.es/tools/venny/) to generate a Venn diagram and extract overlapping intersecting targets, which were subsequently submitted to the STRING database (https://cn.string-db.org/) to construct a human PPI network with a minimum interaction confidence score of 0.7, and the exported PPI interaction file was imported into Cytoscape 3.9.1 for visualization and topological analysis, where hub genes were screened according to the median values of Degree, Betweenness Centrality and Closeness Centrality. Molecular docking simulations were further performed to verify binding activity between CHR and hub targets, with complete reproducible operational parameters including protein dewatering and pretreatment, ligand structural optimization, AutoDockTools 1.5.7 configuration, active pocket grid box setting and binding affinity evaluation criteria supplemented in place of the previous vague description of merely referencing an earlier study.19

Cell Culture

The HaCaT cells were procured from Type Culture Collection Committee of the Chinese Academy of Sciences (Shanghai, China). They were cultured in DMEM medium plus 10% FBS and 1% P/S (Cat. No. A5670701 and 15140122, Gibco, USA). Subsequently, the cells were incubated at 37 °C in incubator maintained 5% CO2.

Cell Viability

The CCK-8 (Cat. No. HY-K0301, MCE, USA) was utilized to evaluate HaCaT cell viability.8 The cells were treated either with CHR at doses of 0, 5, 10, and 20 µM or with a mixture of CHR and LPS (1 µg/mL) for 24 and 48 h separately. Following this, the CCK-8 solution was added to each well for 2 h. In the end, the absorbance values at 450 nm of each well were measured using a microplate reader.

Mice and Treatment

Six-to-eight-week-old female C57BL/6 mice were enrolled in this study. Sample size of 5 mice per group was determined based on our preliminary pilot experiments and power analysis to guarantee statistical reliability. Mice were randomly assigned to four groups using a random number table: (1) blank control group (CON); (2) IMQ-induced psoriasis model group (IMQ); (3) positive drug compound dexamethasone acetate group (DXMS); (4) chrysoeriol treatment group (CHR). To establish psoriasis-like lesions, 62.5 mg IMQ was topically applied to the shaved dorsal skin of mice once daily for 6 consecutive days. The positive drug dexamethasone acetate and 2 mg/mL CHR (Cat. No. HY-121471, MCE, USA, purity ≥98%) were prepared with propylene glycol and topically administrated correspondingly. The propylene glycol without any active compound was evenly spread on the dorsal skin of CON and IMQ groups in equal volume for parallel control. The selection of CHR and DXMS doses referred to published in vivo anti-inflammatory literatures and our preliminary safety test results. All manipulations were performed under isoflurane inhalation anesthesia, and all animals were sacrificed via CO2 asphyxiation upon experiment termination. PASI scoring assessing erythema, epidermal thickening and scaling (each scored 0–4) was conducted every 24 h by two independent researchers blinded to group allocation.20 All animal procedures were reviewed and approved by the Ethics Committee of Zhengzhou University (Approval No. 2025-YYY-012) and strictly implemented following the ARRIVE 2.0 guidelines. Animal experiments were conducted in two independent experimental batches to verify result reproducibility.

Skin Histopathological Examination

The back skin was established the extent of inflammation infiltration via hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) staining. Rabbit anti-Ki67 (Cat. No. ab15580, Abcam, UK), PCNA (Cat. No. #13110, CST, USA), and K17 (Cat. No. #4543, CST, USA) antibodies served as the primary antibodies, while polymer - horseradish peroxidase-labelled goat anti-rabbit IgG (Cat. No. ab150077, Abcam, UK) acted as the secondary antibody.

Immunofluorescence Staining

Conduct immunofluorescence staining following the established protocol.21 Once the skin tissue sections are fixed, employ antibodies such as CD4 (Cat. No. GB15064, Servicebio, China), IL-17 (GB11110, Servicebio, China), and IFN-γ (GB11107, Servicebio, China) for the immunofluorescence staining process. Subsequently, employ the appropriate secondary antibodies to detect and visualize the expression levels of the target proteins.

qRT-PCR

Total RNAs were extracted from samples with the help of TRIzol reagent (Cat. No. 15596018CN, Invitrogen, USA). After synthesizing cDNA, Real-time PCR was performed in an Applied Biosystems 7500 Real-Time PCR System according to protocol. The primers utilized for qRT-PCR are listed in Table S1.

Measurement of Inflammatory Cytokines by ELISA

Back skin extracts were collected and determined inflammatory factors using ELISA kits sourced from Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA. The measurements of IL-1β (88-7013A-88), IL-17A (88-7371-88), IL-23 (BMS6017), and TNF-α (BMS607-3) were carried out in strict accordance with the manufacturers’ protocols.

Flow Cytometry

The preparation of spleens followed the procedures outlined in previous studies.6 To start with, cells were stained with distinct cell-surface markers: Alexa Fluor 700 anti-CD45 mAb (147715, BioLegend, USA), FITC anti-CD3 mAb (100204, BioLegend, USA), or APC anti-CD4 mAb (116013, BioLegend, USA). For the intracellular staining of PE-CY7 anti-IL-17 mAb (506921, BioLegend, USA) and PE anti-IFN-γ mAb (163503, BioLegend, USA), the cells underwent stimulation with phorbol 12-myristate 13-acetate/ionomycin for 4 hours while GolgiPlug (BD Biosciences) was present. The samples were assessed using an LSRFortessa flow cytometer (BD Biosciences) and analyzed with FlowJo software (TreeStar).

RNA Sequencing

Skin tissues were homogenized in TRIzol reagent for total RNA extraction, and RNA samples were delivered to Sangon Biotech Co., Ltd. (Shanghai, China) for transcriptome sequencing following the standard RNA-seq library construction.22 Raw sequencing reads were subjected to quality control to remove low-quality reads and adaptor sequences, and clean reads were mapped to the human reference genome. The DESeq2 R package was used to screen differentially expressed genes (DEGs) with unified screening thresholds (|log2FC| ≥ 1 and adjusted P < 0.05). KEGG pathway enrichment analysis of DEGs was performed using the clusterProfiler R package, with adjusted P-values applied to correct for multiple testing. All raw RNA-seq data generated in this study have been deposited in the Genome Sequence Archive under accession number GSA: CRA027271 and are publicly available.

Western Blotting

The total protein was centrifuged at 12,000 rpm for 10 minutes and separated by SDS-PAGE gels. After completion, the gels were transferred onto PVDF membranes. After incubated with 5% non-fat skim milk, the membranes were incubated with primary antibodies overnight at 4°C. The primary antibodies used were Phospho-NF-κB p65 (Ser536) (Cat. No. #3033, CST, USA), NF-κB p65 (Cat. No. #8242, CST, USA), Phospho-Akt (Ser473) (Cat. No. #4060, CST, USA), Akt (Cat. No. #4691, CST, USA), and GAPDH (Cat. No. #5174, CST, USA). The membranes were clearly incubated with secondary antibodies for 1 h at room temperature. Band gray values of Western blot were quantified using ImageJ software (v1.54f). The relative expression level of each target protein was normalized to the corresponding internal reference protein, and quantitative data were analyzed statistically as described above.

Statistical Analysis

All statistical analyses were performed with GraphPad Prism 6.0. The Shapiro–Wilk test was used to verify the normality of data distribution, and Levene’s test was applied to assess homogeneity of variance between groups. One-way ANOVA followed by Tukey’s post-hoc multiple comparison test was adopted for comparisons among multiple independent groups. Repeated-measures two-way ANOVA was specifically used to analyze longitudinal dynamic PASI scores recorded at sequential time points, with Tukey’s test applied to correct for multiple testing bias. All quantitative data were presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD). Differences with P < 0.05 were considered statistically significant. For all in vitro cell experiments, at least three independent biological replicates were performed, and each biological replicate contained 2–3 technical replicates to reduce detection error.

Results

Identification of the Core Targets of CHR on Psoriasis

A Venn diagram analysis revealed 82 drug-disease intersecting targets, which were considered as the candidate targets of CHR for psoriasis treatment (Figure 1A and B). These 82 candidate targets were then entered into the STRING database to construct a protein - protein interaction (PPI) network (Figure 1C). Subsequently, the network was visualized using Cytoscape 3.9.1 to identify core targets based on their degree values. The findings indicated that 10 targets, namely AKT1, IGF1R, SRC, EGFR, HSP90AA1, MMP2, NFKB1, HIF1A, PTGS2, and MMP9, were located in the central region of the PPI network (Figure 1D, E and Table S2). Additionally, the drug - target - pathway - disease network provided an intuitive representation of the potential targets of CHR in psoriasis, showing that they were enriched in the PI3K/AKT and NF-κB signaling pathways (Figure 1F). The results revealed that cell viability remained unchanged after treatment with CHR (0, 5, 10, and 20 μM) compared to the CON group (Figure 3A and B). Remarkably, CHR significantly curbed the abnormal proliferation of keratinocytes triggered by LPS in a dose-dependent manner (Figure 3A and B). In addition, CHR demonstrated a significant ability to reduce the expression of inflammatory markers, including IL-6, TNF-α, IL-1β, and IL-23 (Figure 3C–F). Red scaly lesions, hyperplasia, and micro-abscesses are the main pathogenesis of psoriasis.6 To explore the function in vivo, CHR was administered to treat mice with psoriasis induced by IMQ (Figure 4A). The pathogenesis of psoriasis was noted in the C57BL/6 mice, underscoring the psoriatic pathogenesis in the model group of mice (Figure 4B). Therapeutically, CHR markedly reduced the experimental symptoms compared with the model group, including the appearance of scales, increased skin thickness, and erythema (Figure 4C–F). In comparison, treatments with CHR and DXMS successfully normalized the spleen size and significantly decreased the spleen index (Figure 4G). CHR Diminishes AKT/NF-κB Pathway Activation in vivo

To further explore the mechanism of CHR on psoriasis, skin tissues obtained from IMQ and the CHR group were subjected to RNAseq. Principal Component Analysis (PCA) showed that all three samples were separated from each other confirming the reliability of the sample data (Figure 8A). As presented in Figure 8B, a total of 2460 differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were found in mice from the CHR group, with 1533 DEGs being upregulated and 927 DEGs being downregulated. Furthermore, our study observed that the expression levels of genes such as IL23r, IL17f, IL1b, IL17rb, IL21r, and IL4ra in the CHR group were lower than those in the IMQ group by conducting an enrichment analysis on these genes within the pool of differentially expressed genes (Figure 8C). Subsequently, we performed an in - depth KEGG pathway analysis of these differentially expressed genes, which revealed that the PI3K-AKT signaling pathway was closely linked to the pathogenesis of IMQ-induced psoriasis (Figure 8D). Through analysis combined with network pharmacology results, it was found that both PI3K-AKT and NF-κB pathway are regulated by CHR. To investigate the interactions between CHR and the key targets, we conducted molecular docking and found that the binding energies of CHR with AKT and NF-κB pathways identified by KEGG were all below-5.0 kcal/mol (Figure 8E, F and Table S4). To assess the activation of the downstream AKT/NF-κB pathway, we further analyzed the phosphorylation levels of AKT and P65 using Western blotting. Consequently, the phosphorylation levels of these key regulatory proteins in the AKT/NF-κB pathways were significantly reduced in the CHR-treated groups compared to the model group (Figure 8G). These findings suggest that CHR mitigates the activation of AKT/NF-κB pathways in the treatment of psoriasis. Discussion

Psoriasis is marked by chronic inflammation and the production of various cytokines by immune cells, which results in heightened proliferation of keratinocytes. These activated keratinocytes subsequently secrete cytokines, thereby perpetuating a cycle of inflammation. At present, topical therapy shows serious side effects making it crucial to find novel therapeutic options.25 Therefore, Traditional Chinese Medicine (TCM) is attracting increasing interest for the treatment of psoriasis including benzoylaconitine and liquiritin.26 CHR, an effective component of Aconitum herbs, possesses pharmacological anti-inflammatory properties.27 In this research, we reported that CHR alleviated the response of psoriasis by suppressing the abnormal proliferation, cytokine production and immunity cell recruitment, indicating that CHR has the potential to be a promising therapeutic agent for psoriasis.

Psoriasis-like skin manifestations were clearly visible in mice that underwent modeling with IMQ alone. The histopathological traits matched those of psoriasis, indicating that the IMQ successfully created a psoriasis-like disease model in this investigation, which is in accordance with existing study.28–30 It has been shown that the daily topical application of CHR can reduce skin symptoms and alleviate skin lesions in vivo. At the peak of drug-induced disease, mice showed an increase in spleen mass, a significant reduction in body weight, and an increase in the spleen index. The spleen index is widely employed as a key parameter in evaluating the course of psoriasis.31 This study found that the spleen index was significantly elevated in the model group. However, CHR treatment restored body weight, significantly suppressed spleen enlargement, suggesting that CHR improve abnormal immune condition.

The upregulation of Ki-67 and PCNA serves as a crucial diagnostic hallmark of hyperproliferation that distinguishes psoriatic skin from normal skin, representing the severity of psoriatic skin disease activity and the activity pertaining to DNA synthesis, respectively.32,33 K17 (keratin 17) plays a pivotal role in the pathogenesis of psoriasis. Its aberrant overexpression promotes keratinocyte (KC) hyperproliferation and inhibits apoptosis, while also exacerbating inflammatory responses by activating signaling pathways such as AKT/mTOR. Additionally, K17 can function as an autoantigen to activate T cells (particularly Th17 cells), stimulating the release of cytokines like IL-17 and IL-23, thereby creating a pro-inflammatory feedback loop. Furthermore, the interaction between K17 and immune cells (eg, dendritic cells) perpetuates chronic inflammation in psoriatic skin lesions. As a result, K17 is considered a potential therapeutic target for psoriasis.21,34 CHR halted the progression and development of the psoriasis via suppressing the abnormal proliferation of keratinocytes. Vinod Gangadevi et al have demonstrated that SeNPs alleviates IMQ-induced psoriasis by inducing apoptosis of keratinocytes.33 According to our research, CHR effectively downregulated the marked overexpression of Ki67, PCNA, and K17 in lesional skin.

Cytokines, such as IL-1β, TNF-α, IL-23, and IL-17, facilitate the accumulation of immune cells and exacerbate keratinocyte proliferation.35 In previous study, thalidomide can reduce IL-1β expression to suppress the inflammatory response in psoriasis.36 Th1 cells contribute to the pathogenesis of psoriasis by secreting pro-inflammatory cytokines like IFN-γ and TNF-α. These cytokines activate and recruit other immune cells, such as dendritic cells and macrophages, promoting abnormal keratinocyte proliferation and inflammatory responses. The dysregulated immune response mediated by Th1 cells results in epidermal thickening, angiogenesis, and characteristic erythematosquamous lesions, while maintaining a chronic inflammatory microenvironment. Immune and inflammatory responses are regulated by TNF-α in development of psoriasis.37 Another increased cytokine, IL-23 promotes the differentiation of Th17 cells, leading to the production of more IL-17, regarded as the hallmark of psoriasis.38 The anti-inflammatory function of CHR has been demonstrated in some disease models.39 Our findings suggest that CHR can reduce the expression of various secreted cytokines, inhibit the abnormal proliferation of Th1 and Th17 cells, thereby improving psoriasis and benefiting the amelioration of the inflammatory environment in skin lesions.

When inflammatory stimuli activate cell membrane receptors, the PI3K/AKT pathway is triggered, leading to the phosphorylation and activation of the NF-κB initiates the transcription of pro-inflammatory cytokines, chemokines, and other inflammatory mediators. Simultaneously, AKT amplifies NF-κB signaling by inhibiting GSK-3β or activating mTOR, forming a positive feedback loop. Persistent NF-κB activation promotes chronic inflammation, contributing to inflammatory diseases.40–42 This, in turn, promotes the infiltration of inflammatory cells and causes tissue damage. This pathway not only mediates the activation of innate immune cells (for example, macrophage polarization) but also takes part in Th cell differentiation (such as Th17 differentiation) and the pathogenesis of inflammatory diseases (eg, psoriasis and arthritis). Additionally, the NF-κB pathway interacts with other pathways (such as AP-1), forming an inflammatory amplification network.6,43 This makes it a vital therapeutic target for anti-inflammatory interventions. In our study, we found that CHR potentially linked to altered AKT/p65 phosphorylation, thereby regulating the proliferation and expression of inflammatory cytokines.

Limitations

There are several limitations in the present study worthy of acknowledgment. First, only a single CHR concentration was applied for in vivo intervention according to our preliminary in vitro screening results, while gradient dose-response validation was not performed in IMQ-induced psoriatic mice. Further experiments with multiple ascending doses are required to clarify the dose-effect relationship and determine the optimal therapeutic dosage of CHR in future investigations. Second, although we combined network pharmacology prediction and RNA-seq transcriptomic analysis to screen potential signaling changes related to CHR treatment, the current evidence only supports a correlative association between CHR intervention and suppressed AKT/NF-κB phosphorylation rather than definite causal regulation. More targeted genetic intervention assays such as gene overexpression or knockdown are needed to fully confirm the causal regulatory relationship of the AKT/NF-κB cascade in CHR-mediated anti-psoriatic effects.

Conclusion

In this work, we systematically revealed the anti-psoriatic mechanism of CHR in IMQ-induced psoriasis-like mouse models, and compared its pharmacological characteristics with other widely reported natural small molecules for psoriasis treatment, including baicalein, apigenin and luteolin. Most flavonoid monomers reported previously mainly alleviate psoriasis-like inflammation by merely inhibiting NF-κB-mediated inflammatory cascades, with limited regulatory effects on keratinocyte hyperproliferation and the imbalance of Th1/Th17 cell subsets. In contrast, CHR exhibits unique dual regulatory advantages in both epidermal keratinocytes and systemic immune microenvironment.

Collectively, different from single-pathway anti-inflammatory natural compounds documented in existing literatures, CHR targets both epidermal hyperplasia, Th1/Th17 immune disorder and associated with reduced AKT/NF-κB pathway activation. This multi-layered regulatory mode constitutes the core novelty of the present study, and highlights the potential of CHR as a potential preclinical candidate rather than a confirmed anti-psoriatic therapeutic agent (Figure 9).

The trigonal construction and the second moment of the tropical Prym variety
Dmitry Zakharov

Abstract
. We use the tropical trigonal construction to calculate the second moment of the tropical Prym variety of all double covers π : Γ ~ → Γ \pi:\widetilde{\Gamma}\to\Gamma of tropical curves of genus g ⁡ ( Γ ) ≤ 4 g(\Gamma)\leq 4 . The answer is expressed in terms of the signed graphic matroid of the double cover and consists a polynomial and piecewise-polynomial term. We relate the latter term, which does not occur in the analogous formula for the tropical Jacobian, to the problem of extending the Prym–Torelli map from the moduli space of admissible double covers to the second Voronoi compactification of the moduli space of principally polarized abelian varieties.



1.
Introduction

Tropical geometry aims to find analogues of algebro-geometric objects in the world of polyhedral geometry, which record the combinatorial behavior of degenerations of the algebraic objects. There are two classes of algebraic objects for which this correspondence is especially well-understood: algebraic curves and abelian varieties. The tropical analogues of algebraic curves are metric graphs, which admit a theory of divisors, linear equivalence, and line bundles that closely follows the algebraic theory (see
[
MZ08
]
). A tropical principally polarized abelian variety (ppav)
X
is a real torus determined by a free abelian group
\Lambda
and an inner product on
\Lambda_{\mathbb{R}}=\Lambda\otimes\mathbb{R}
, and the theory of tropical ppavs is likewise quite similar to the algebraic theory. For example, the tropical Jacobian
\Jac(\Gamma)
of a metric graph
\Gamma
is a tropical ppav, and there is a natural Abel–Jacobi map
\Gamma\to\Jac(\Gamma)
(see
[
BF11
]
).

A tropical ppav is a Riemannian manifold, and a natural first question is to compute its volume. More generally, the
2n
-th moment

I_{2n}(X)
of a tropical ppav
X
is the integral of
\|\cdot\|^{2n}
over a canonical symmetric convex polyhedral domain
\Vor(X)\subset\Lambda_{\mathbb{R}}
, called the
Voronoi polytope
, and zeroth moment is the volume of
X
. The second moment of a tropical ppav has been extensively studied in information theory (see Chapter 21 of
[
CS13
]
), and was recently found to have arithmetic significance
[
dJS22
]
. For a generic tropical ppav
X
, finding
I_{2n}(X)
(indeed, even finding the vertices of
\Vor(X)
) is a computationally hard problem (see the discussion in Section 1.2 of
[
dJS23
]
). Therefore one is interested in finding explicit formulas for
I_{2n}(X)
for specific families of tropical ppavs.

The zeroth moment
I_{0}(\Jac(\Gamma))
, or volume, of the Jacobian of a metric graph
\Gamma
was found in
[
ABKS14
]
, and is equal (up to a multiple) to a polynomial in the edge lengths of
\Gamma
whose monomials correspond to the spanning trees of
\Gamma
. A formula for the second moment
I_{2}(\Jac(\Gamma))
was found in
[
dJS23
]
(see Theorem 8.1). While this formula is computationally efficient for a fixed metric graph, we present our motivation for finding an alternative formula. The Jacobian variety
\Jac(\Gamma)
is closely related to the graphic matroid
\mathcal{M}(\Gamma)
, and in fact can be reconstructed from
\mathcal{M}(\Gamma)
and the edge lengths (see
[
Ger82
]
,
[
CV10a
]
, and
[
BMV11
]
). It follows that it should possible to calculate all moments
I_{2n}(\Jac(\Gamma))
in terms of the matroid alone. The formula for
I_{0}(\Jac(\Gamma))
in
[
ABKS14
]
is a sum over the spanning trees of
\Gamma
and hence is explicitly matroidal, but the formula for
I_{2}(\Jac(\Gamma))
given in
[
dJS23
]
is not. As an intermediate result, in Proposition
8
we use the results of the related paper
[
RSW23
]
to derive an explicitly matroidal formula for
I_{2}(\Jac(\Gamma))
, which involves summation over the 2-spanning forests of
\Gamma
.

The Prym variety
\Prym(\widetilde{X}/X)
of a connected étale double cover
p:\widetilde{X}\to X
of algebraic curves is a ppav of dimension
g(X)-1
. The analogous construction for double covers of metric graphs was described in
[
JL18
]
and
[
LU21
]
. The zeroth moment
I_{0}(\Prym(\widetilde{\Gamma}/\Gamma))
of the Prym variety of a double cover
\pi:\widetilde{\Gamma}\to\Gamma
of metric graphs was computed in
[
LZ22
]
in the case when
\pi
is a free double cover, and in
[
GZ24
]
for all double covers. As in the case of Jacobians,
I_{0}(\Prym(\widetilde{\Gamma}/\Gamma))
is equal (up to a multiple) to a polynomial in the edge lengths of
\Gamma
, with monomials indexed by the bases of a matroid, namely the
signed graphic matroid

\mathcal{M}(\widetilde{\Gamma}/\Gamma)
originally introduced by Zaslavsky
[
Zas82
]
. Unlike
\mathcal{M}(\Gamma)
, the bases of the matroid
\mathcal{M}(\widetilde{\Gamma}/\Gamma)
come equipped with an index function, which appears as an auxiliary weighting in the corresponding terms of
I_{0}(\Prym(\widetilde{\Gamma}/\Gamma))
.

The exact relationship between
\mathcal{M}(\widetilde{\Gamma}/\Gamma)
and
\Prym(\widetilde{\Gamma}/\Gamma)
was elucidated in
[
RZ23
]
, where we showed that the latter can be reconstructed from the former, just as
\mathcal{M}(\Gamma)
determines
\Jac(\Gamma)
. It follows that there must exist a formula for
I_{2}(\Prym(\widetilde{\Gamma}/\Gamma))
in terms of the signed graphic matroid
\mathcal{M}(\widetilde{\Gamma}/\Gamma)
. The goal of this project is to find such a formula.

In classical algebraic geometry, a beautiful construction of Recillas
[
Rec74
]
associates, to a connected étale double cover
\widetilde{X}\to X
of a trigonal curve
X
, a tetragonal curve
Y
in such a way that
\Prym(\widetilde{X}/X)=\Jac(Y)
. Under certain genericity assumptions on
Y
, this construction can be inverted. A tropical version of this construction was developed in
[
RZ25
]
: given a free double cover of metric graphs
\pi:\widetilde{\Gamma}\to\Gamma
, where
\Gamma
is a trigonal graph (in an appropriate sense), we construct a tetragonal metric graph
\Pi
such that
\Prym(\widetilde{\Gamma}/\Gamma)=\Jac(\Pi)
. By the results of
[
CD18
]
, any metric graph
\Gamma
of genus
g(\Gamma)\leq 4
is trigonal. Therefore, we can compute
I_{2}(\Prym(\widetilde{\Gamma}/\Gamma))
for any free double cover with
g(\Gamma)\leq 4
by identifying
\Prym(\widetilde{\Gamma}/\Gamma)
with a tetragonal Jacobian
\Jac(\Pi)
, using the matroidal formula of Proposition
8
to compute
I_{2}(\Jac(\Pi))
, and then expressing the result in terms of the signed graphic matroid
\mathcal{M}(\widetilde{\Gamma}/\Gamma)
. By edge contraction, this formula can then be extended to arbitrary double covers.

The main result of this paper is an implementation of this computation in Sage. In Theorem
4.1
, we give an explicit formula for
I_{2}(\Prym(\widetilde{\Gamma}/\Gamma))
in terms of the matroid
\mathcal{M}(\widetilde{\Gamma}/\Gamma)
for all metric graphs
\Gamma
with
g(\Gamma)\leq 4
. The formula consists of two terms: a polynomial term
p(\widetilde{\Gamma}/\Gamma)
that is the direct analogue of the polynomial formula for
I_{2}(\Jac(\Gamma))
, and a piecewise-polynomial term
q(\widetilde{\Gamma}/\Gamma)
that has no analogue for Jacobians.

The piecewise-polynomiality of
I_{2}(\Prym(\widetilde{\Gamma}/\Gamma))
is directly related to the problem of resolving the indeterminancy of the Prym–Torelli map
\overline{t}_{g}:\overline{\mathcal{R}}_{g}\dashrightarrow\overline{\mathcal{A}}^{V}_{g-1}
from the moduli space of admissible double covers of stable curves to the second Voronoi compactification of the moduli space of ppavs. In fact, our calculations can be used to describe an explicit sequence of toric blowups that fully resolves the indeterminancy for
g=4
. This blowup, however, is far from minimal, and in future work we plan to use our methods to find a minimal resolution of
\overline{t}_{4}
.

We emphasize that we do not actually compute the second moment of any tropical abelian variety
X
that is not a Jacobian, in other words, one whose second moment is not already known by the results of
[
dJS23
]
. However, our results allow us to conjecture a matroidal formula for
I_{2}(\Prym(\widetilde{\Gamma}/\Gamma))
for certain families of double covers whose Pryms are not, in general, Jacobians.

2.
Setup

In this section we recall a number of basic definitions about metric graphs, double covers, graph gonality, and tropical abelian varieties and their moments.

2.1.
Graphs and weighted graphs

A
graph

G=(V(G),H(G),r,\iota)
consists of a set of
vertices

V(G)
, a set of
half-edges

H(G)
, a
root map

r:H(G)\to V(G)
, and a fixed-point-free involution
\iota:H(G)\to H(G)
. An
edge

e=\{h,h^{\prime}\}
of
G
is an orbit of
\iota
, and the set of edges is denoted
E(G)
. An edge is a loop if its root vertices coincide. We allow graphs with loops and with multiple edges between two vertices, and we consider only finite connected graphs.

The
tangent space
to a vertex
v\in V(G)
is the set
T_{v}G=r^{-1}(v)
of half-edges rooted at
v
, and the
valence
of
v\in V(G)
is
\val(v)=|T_{v}G|
. A
weighted graph

(G,g)
is a graph
G
together with a function
g:V(G)\to\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
called the
vertex genus
. The
genus
of a weighted graph is

g(G)=b_{1}(G)+\sum_{v\in V(G)}g(v)=|E(G)|-|V(G)|+1+\sum_{v\in V(G)}g(v).

The
Euler characteristic
of a vertex
v\in V(G)
of a weighted graph
G
is the quantity

\chi(v)=2-2g(v)-\val(v),

and the
Euler characteristic
of the entire graph
G
is

\chi(G)=\sum_{v\in V(G)}\chi(v)=2-2g(G).

A vertex
v\in V(G)
is
unstable
if
\chi(v)>0
, such a vertex is an extremal vertex of genus 0. Similarly,
v
is
semistable
if
\chi(v)=0
, in other words if
\val(v)=2
and
g(v)=0
. A weighted graph
G
is called
stable
or
semistable
if respectively
\chi(v)<0
or
\chi(v)\leq 0
for all
v\in V(G)
. For any weighted graph
G
with
g(G)\geq 2
, we define the
stabilization

G^{\mathrm{st}}
of
G
by iteratively removing unstable vertices
v\in V(G)
(in other words, deleting all extremal trees with no vertices of positive genus), and then removing all semistable vertices by joining the two incident edges into a single edge.

2.2.
Harmonic morphisms and ramification

A
morphism

f:\widetilde{G}\to G
of graphs is a pair of maps
f:V(\widetilde{G})\to V(G)
and
f:H(\widetilde{G})\to H(G)
commuting with the root and involution maps, and thus inducing a map
f:E(\widetilde{G})\to E(G)
that preserves vertex adjacency (we do not consider morphisms that contract edges). A
harmonic morphism
of graphs is a morphism
f:\widetilde{G}\to G
together with a
local degree
function
d_{f}:V(\widetilde{G})\cup H(\widetilde{G})\to\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
satisfying the following conditions: for any edge
\widetilde{e}=\{\widetilde{h},\widetilde{h}^{\prime}\}\in E(\widetilde{G})
we have
d_{f}(\widetilde{h})=d_{f}(\widetilde{h}^{\prime})
(a quantity we therefore denote
d_{f}(\widetilde{e})
), and

d_{f}(\widetilde{v})=\sum_{\widetilde{h}\in f^{-1}(h)\cap T_{\widetilde{v}}\widetilde{G}}d_{f}(\widetilde{h})

for any
\widetilde{v}\in V(\widetilde{G})
and any
h\in T_{f(\widetilde{v})}G
. A harmonic morphism
f:\widetilde{G}\to G
to a connected target
G
has a well-defined
global degree
equal to

\deg(f)=\sum_{\widetilde{v}\in f^{-1}(v)}d_{f}(\widetilde{v})=\sum_{\widetilde{h}\in f^{-1}(h)}d_{f}(\widetilde{h})=\sum_{\widetilde{e}\in f^{-1}(e)}d_{f}(\widetilde{e})

(1)

for any
v\in V(G)
,
h\in H(G)
, or
e\in E(G)
.

Given a harmonic morphism
f:\widetilde{G}\to G
of weighted graphs, the
ramification degree
of
f
at a vertex
\widetilde{v}\in V(\widetilde{G})
is

\Ram_{f}(\widetilde{v})=d_{f}(\widetilde{v})\chi(f(\widetilde{v}))-\chi(\widetilde{v}).

(2)

Adding the local ramification degrees over all
\widetilde{v}\in V(\widetilde{G})
and using (
1
), we obtain the
global Riemann–Hurwitz formula

\chi(\widetilde{G})=\deg(f)\chi(G)-\Ram(f),

(3)

where

\Ram(f)=\sum_{\widetilde{v}\in V(\widetilde{G})}\Ram_{f}(\widetilde{v}).

is the
global ramification degree
of
f
.

We say that a harmonic morphism
f:\widetilde{G}\to G
is
effective
if
\Ram_{f}(\widetilde{v})\geq 0
for all
\widetilde{v}\in V(\widetilde{G})
and
unramified
if
\Ram_{f}(\widetilde{v})=0
for all
\widetilde{v}\in V(\widetilde{G})
. It is elementary to verify that if
f:\widetilde{G}\to G
is an unramified harmonic morphism, then
\widetilde{G}
is semistable if and only if
G
is semistable, and stable if and only if
G
is stable.

2.3.
Edge contractions

Let
G
be a graph and let
F\subset E(G)
be a set of edges. We define the
contraction

G_{F}
of
G
along
F
as follows. Let
G[F]=G_{1}\sqcup\cdots\sqcup G_{k}
be the connected components of the subgraph
G[F]\subseteq G
spanned by
F
(consisting of the edges in
F
and their root vertices). We form
G_{F}
by contracting each
G_{i}
to a separate vertex
v_{i}
, so that

V(G_{F})=(V(G)\backslash V(G[F]))\cup\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{k}\},\quad E(G_{F})=E(G)\backslash F.

If
G
is a weighted graph with vertex genus function
g:V(G)\to\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, we define the vertex weight
g_{F}:V(G_{F})\to\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
as follows:

g_{F}(v)=\begin{cases}g(v),&v\in V(G)\backslash V(G[F]),\\
g(G_{i}),&v=v_{i}.\end{cases}

It is elementary to verify that
g(G_{F})=g(G)
.

We can similarly contract a harmonic morphism
f:\widetilde{G}\to G
along a set of edges
F\subset E(G)
of the target. Let
G[F]=G_{1}\sqcup\cdots\sqcup G_{k}
be the connected components, let
\widetilde{F}=f^{-1}(F)
, and let
\widetilde{G}[\widetilde{F}]=\coprod_{i,j}\widetilde{G}_{ij}
be the connected components of the subgraph of
\widetilde{G}
spanned by
\widetilde{F}
, labeled in such a way that
f(\widetilde{G}_{ij})=G_{i}
. We define the
contraction
f_{F}:\widetilde{G}_{\widetilde{F}}\to G_{F}
of
f
along
F
by setting
f(\widetilde{v}_{ij})=v_{i}
, where
\widetilde{v}_{ij}\in V(\widetilde{G}_{\widetilde{F}})
and
v_{i}\in V(G_{F})
are the contracted vertices corresponding to
\widetilde{G}_{ij}
and
G_{i}
, respectively. It is elementary to verify that
f_{F}
is harmonic and of the same global degree as
f
, provided that we set its local degrees to be

d_{f_{F}}(\widetilde{v})=\begin{cases}d_{f}(\widetilde{v}),&\widetilde{v}\in V(\widetilde{G})\backslash V(\widetilde{G}[\widetilde{F}]),\\
\deg f|_{\widetilde{G}_{ij}},&\widetilde{v}=\widetilde{v}_{ij}.\end{cases}

Furthermore, if
f
is effective or unramified, then so is
f_{F}
.

2.4.
Metric graphs and tropical curves

Let
G
be a graph and let
\ell:E(G)\to\mathbb{R}_{>0}
be an assignment of
lengths
to the edges of
G
. The pair
(G,\ell)
defines a
metric graph

\Gamma
, which is the topological space obtained by gluing intervals
[0,\ell(e)]
for all
e\in E(G)
according to how they are attached in
G
. We will often use
e
to denote both an edge of a metric graph and its length. The pair
(G,\ell)
is called a
model
for
\Gamma
, and different models for
\Gamma
may be obtained by either subdividing edges or removing valence 2 vertices. Any metric graph other than a circle has a unique
minimal model
, having no vertices of valence 2. We will usually not distinguish a metric graph
\Gamma
from its models and use
V(\Gamma)
and
E(\Gamma)
to denote the set of vertices or edges of a suitably chosen model of
\Gamma
.

A
tropical curve
is a weighted metric graph, in other words a metric graph
\Gamma
together with a weight function
g:\Gamma\to\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
that takes only finitely many nonzero values. When choosing a model for a tropical curve
\Gamma
, we always assume that each point
x\in\Gamma
with
g(x)>0
corresponds to a vertex, so that the model is a weighted graph. We define the Euler characteristic of a point
x\in\Gamma
on a tropical curve
\Gamma
, and the genus and Euler characteristic of all of
\Gamma
, using the underlying model. A tropical curve is called
stable
if it has no extremal vertices of genus zero.

A
harmonic morphism

f:\widetilde{\Gamma}\to\Gamma
of metric graphs or tropical curves is determined by a harmonic morphism of the underlying models, having the property that

\ell(f(\widetilde{e}))=d_{f}(\widetilde{e})\ell(\widetilde{e})

for all
\widetilde{e}\in E(\widetilde{\Gamma})
. The map
f
is linear on each edge
\widetilde{\Gamma}
and dilates it by the positive integer factor
d_{f}(\widetilde{e})
. The global degree of
f
is its global degree as a harmonic morphism of graphs, and we similarly refer to effective and unramified harmonic morphisms of tropical curves.

We may contract edges of tropical curves by contracting the edges on the underlying models, and similarly we may contract harmonic morphisms of tropical curves. Intuitively, this corresponds to setting the lengths of the corresponding edges to zero, and increasing the vertex genus by one whenever a loop is contracted.

2.5.
Double covers

In this paper, we are primarily concerned with two types of harmonic morphisms: double covers and
n
-gonal structures, which are described in Section
2.7
.

A
double cover

\pi:\widetilde{\Gamma}\to\Gamma
of tropical curves is an unramified harmonic morphism of global degree two. A point
x\in\Gamma
is called
dilated
if it has a unique preimage
\pi^{-1}(x)=\{\widetilde{x}\}
with
d_{\pi}(\widetilde{x})=2
and
free
if
\pi^{-1}(x)=\{\widetilde{x}^{+},\widetilde{x}^{-}\}
with
d_{\pi}(\widetilde{x}^{\pm})=1
. The set of dilated points forms the
dilation subcurve

\Gamma_{\dil}\subset\Gamma
. A double cover
\pi:\widetilde{\Gamma}\to\Gamma
is
free
if
\Gamma_{\dil}=\emptyset
, in other words, if
\pi
is a covering isometry. We say that
\pi
is
dilated
if
\Gamma_{\dil}\neq\emptyset
and
edge-free
of
\Gamma_{\dil}
consists of finitely many points. The
dilation index
of a double cover is

d(\widetilde{\Gamma}/\Gamma)=\begin{cases}\mbox{number of connected components of }\Gamma_{\dil},&\pi\mbox{ is dilated,}\\
1\mbox{ (not 0)},&\pi\mbox{ is free}.\end{cases}

The
torus rank
of a double cover
\pi:\widetilde{\Gamma}\to\Gamma
is

t(\widetilde{\Gamma}/\Gamma)=b_{1}(\widetilde{\Gamma})-b_{1}(\Gamma).

We make a number of elementary numerical observations about double covers.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\pi:\widetilde{\Gamma}\to\Gamma
be a double cover and let
\Gamma_{1},\ldots,\Gamma_{d}
be the connected components of the dilation subcurve
\Gamma_{\dil}
.

(1)

g(\widetilde{\Gamma})=2g(\Gamma)-1
.

(2)

Each
\Gamma_{i}
is semistable (see Lemma 5.4 in
[
JL18
]
).

(3)

The torus rank satisfies

t(\widetilde{\Gamma}/\Gamma)\leq g(\Gamma)-1,

with equality holding if and only if the following conditions hold:

(a)

g(\Gamma_{i})=1
for all
i
, in other words each
\Gamma_{i}
is either an isolated point of genus one or a simple cycle having no points of nonzero genus.

(b)

g(x)=0
for all
x\notin\Gamma_{\dil}
.

Proof.

The first equality follows from the global Riemann–Hurwitz formula (
3
). To prove the second part, denote by
\dval(x)
the valence of a dilated point
x\in\Gamma_{\dil}
in the subgraph
\Gamma_{\dil}
. Since
\pi
is unramified, the local Riemann–Hurwitz condition (
2
) at the preimage point
\widetilde{x}=\pi^{-1}(x)
reads

g(\widetilde{x})=2g(x)-1+\frac{1}{2}\dval(x).

Hence
\dval(x)
is even, and furthermore
g(x)\geq 1
if
\dval(x)=0
. Hence each
\Gamma_{i}
is semistable, and in particular
g(\Gamma_{i})\geq 1
.

By counting the preimage vertices and edges, it is elementary to verify that

t(\widetilde{\Gamma}/\Gamma)=b_{1}(\Gamma)-1-\sum_{i=1}^{d}[b_{1}(\Gamma_{i})-1]=g(\Gamma)-1-\sum_{i=1}^{d}[g(\Gamma_{i})-1]-\sum_{x\notin\Gamma_{\dil}}g(x).

Since
g(\Gamma_{i})\geq 1
for all
i
, this proves the third part of the lemma.

∎

A free double cover
\pi:\widetilde{\Gamma}\to\Gamma
on a given tropical curve
\Gamma
is equivalent to the structure of a
signed graph
on
\Gamma
. Choose an oriented model for
\Gamma
, and for each
v\in V(\Gamma)
label the preimages by
\pi^{-1}(v)=\{\widetilde{v}^{+},\widetilde{v}^{-}\}
arbitrarily. For each
e\in E(\Gamma)
, we label the preimages
\pi^{-1}(e)=\{\widetilde{e}^{+},\widetilde{e}^{-}\}
in such a way that
s(\widetilde{e}^{\pm})=\widetilde{s(e)}^{\pm}
, so that the source map preserves the signs on the preimages. The edges of
\Gamma
are then equipped with signs
\sigma:E(\Gamma)\to\{+1,-1\}
according to whether or not the target map preserves or flips the signs of the preimages:

\sigma(e)=\begin{cases}+1,&t(\widetilde{e}^{\pm})=\widetilde{t(e)}^{\pm},\\
-1,&t(\widetilde{e}^{\pm})=\widetilde{t(e)}^{\mp}.\end{cases}

Exchanging the labels of the preimages
\{\widetilde{v}^{+},\widetilde{v}^{-}\}
of a single vertex
v\in V(\Gamma)
flips the signs of all non-loop edges at
v
, so the simplicial cohomology class
[\sigma]\in H^{1}(\Gamma,\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})
is well-defined. Running the construction in reverse, we obtain a free double cover
\pi:\widetilde{\Gamma}\to\Gamma
from an element of
H^{1}(\Gamma,\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})
, with the zero element corresponding to the split double cover (for any
\widetilde{x}\in\widetilde{\Gamma}
we have
g(\widetilde{x})=g(\pi(\widetilde{x}))
by the local Riemann–Hurwitz condition).

As we noted above, the contraction of an unramified morphism is unramified, in particular, the contraction of a double cover
\pi:\widetilde{\Gamma}\to\Gamma
along a set of edges
F\subset E(\Gamma)
is a double cover
\pi_{F}:\widetilde{\Gamma}_{\widetilde{F}}\to\Gamma_{F}
. We note that
\pi_{F}
may be dilated even if
\pi
is free, this happens if and only if
F
contains an odd cycle.

2.6.
Moduli of tropical curves and double covers

Let
G
be a stable weighted graph of genus
g\geq 2
, so that if
g(v)=0
for
v\in V(G)
then
\val(v)\geq 3
. It is elementary to verify that
|E(G)|\leq 3g-3
, with equality holding if and only if
G
is trivalent and has trivial vertex genera. A tropical curve with underlying model
G
is obtained by choosing positive lengths
\ell(e)
for all
e\in E(G)
, so the positive orthant
M_{G}=\mathbb{R}^{E(G)}_{>0}
(more accurately, the quotient
\mathbb{R}^{E(G)}_{>0}/\Aut(G)
) parametrizes stable tropical curves with underlying model
G
.

Now let
F\subset E(G)
be a set of edges and let
G_{F}
be the contraction of
G
along
F
. We attach
M_{G_{F}}
to
M_{G}
as the face of the orthant where the lengths of the edges in
F
are equal to zero. Similarly, if
f:G\to H
is an isomorphism stable weighted graphs, then we identify
M_{G}
and
M_{H}
via the corresponding bijection
E(G)\to E(H)
. The union of the cones
M_{G}
over all stable weighted metric graphs of genus
g
, identified as above, is the
moduli space of tropical curves
of genus
g
:

\mathcal{M}_{g}^{\trop}=\left.\bigsqcup_{G}M_{G}\right/\sim

Since each
G
has at most
3g-3
edges, the dimension of
\mathcal{M}_{g}^{\trop}
is equal to
3g-3
, and each cone is contained in the boundary of a cone of maximal dimension.

We similarly define the
moduli space of double covers of tropical curves
of genus
g

\mathcal{R}_{g}^{\trop}=\left.\bigsqcup_{\widetilde{G}/G}R_{\widetilde{G}/G}\right/\sim

Here the union is taken over all connected double covers
p:\widetilde{G}\to G
of stable weighted graphs of genus
g(G)=g
. Each
R_{\widetilde{G}/G}=\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{E(G)}
is a cone parametrizing double covers of tropical curves
\pi:\widetilde{\Gamma}\to\Gamma
with underlying model
p
(the edge lengths of
\Gamma
determine those of
\widetilde{\Gamma}
). Given a double cover
p:\widetilde{G}\to G
and a set of edges
F\subset E(G)
, the contraction
p_{F}:\widetilde{G}_{\widetilde{F}}\to G_{F}
along the edges in
F
is also unramified and hence a double cover, and we attach
R_{\widetilde{G}_{\widetilde{F}}/G_{F}}
to
R_{\widetilde{G}/G}
accordingly. Similarly, we glue the cones along isomorphisms of double covers. The maximal-dimensional cones in
\mathcal{R}^{\trop}_{g}
have dimension
3g-3
and correspond to free double covers
p:\widetilde{G}\to G
, where
G
is trivalent and has trivial vertex genera (by Lemma
2.2
, such a
G
cannot be the target of a dilated double cover). We note that by contracting a free double cover we may obtain edge-free double covers but not double covers with dilated edges, hence the closure of the union of the maximal-dimensional cones of
\mathcal{R}_{g}^{\trop}
is the set of edge-free double covers and not all of
\mathcal{R}_{g}^{\trop}
.

2.7.
Graph gonality

We recall that an algebraic curve
X
is called
n
-gonal
if it carries a
g^{1}_{n}
, or equivalently if it admits a degree
n
map to
\mathbb{P}^{1}
. The tropical analogues of these two definitions are not equivalent, and in fact the second is stronger than the first. One may say that a metric graph
\Gamma
is
n
-gonal if it carries a
g^{1}_{n}
in the sense of Baker and Norine
[
BN07
]
. Baker’s specialization lemma states that the tropicalization of an
n
-gonal algebraic curve is an
n
-gonal metric graph; however, since that lemma is an inequality, gonality may increase under tropicalization. One consequence is that Baker–Norine gonality loci in
\mathcal{M}_{g}^{\trop}
are poorly behaved and generally have incorrect dimensions.

We use the alternative definition of gonality in terms of the existence of a harmonic map to a metric tree. This definition also allows us to reformulate the classical trigonal construction in the tropical setting, which is the main technical tool of this paper.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
\Gamma
be a tropical curve. A
tropical modification
of
\Gamma
is a tropical curve obtained by attaching finitely many metric trees to
\Gamma
.

The points of the new metric trees are assumed to have genus zero, so tropical modification does not change the genus. We now tropicalize the second definition of gonality by replacing
\mathbb{P}^{1}
with a tropical curve of genus zero, that is to say, a metric tree.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
\Gamma
be a tropical curve. An
n
-gonal structure
on
\Gamma
is an effective harmonic morphism
\varphi:\Gamma^{\prime}\to\Delta
of degree
n
, where
\Gamma^{\prime}
is a tropical modification of
\Gamma
and
\Delta
is a metric tree of genus zero. A tropical curve
\Gamma
is called
n
-gonal
if it admits an
n
-gonal structure.

This definition of gonality was advanced in the paper
[
CD18
]
, where it was shown that it satisfies the same properties, in terms of dimension in moduli, as the gonality of an algebraic curve:

Theorem 2.5

(Theorem 1 in
[
CD18
]
)
.

Let
\Gamma
be a tropical curve of genus
g
and let
n\geq\lceil(g+2)/2\rceil
. Then there exists an
n
-gonal structure
\varphi:\Gamma^{\prime}\to\Delta
on
\Gamma
.

We note that Theorem 1 in
[
CD18
]
concerns only metric graphs, but the result also trivially applies to tropical curves with positive vertex genera, since increasing
g
without changing the underlying metric graph only strengthens the condition on
n
.

One of the key observations of
[
CD18
]
is that the number of edges of the target tree
\Delta
, and hence the dimension of the space of
n
-gonal curves in
\mathcal{M}_{g}^{\trop}
, may be bounded in terms of
n
and
g
. The following statement appears as Corollary 14 in
[
CD18
]
; however, that paper is written in somewhat different language than ours (and for metric graphs only), so we reprove it for the reader’s convenience.

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
\Gamma
be a tropical curve of genus
g
and let
n\geq\lceil(g+2)/2\rceil
. Then there exists an
n
-gonal structure
\varphi:\Gamma^{\prime}\to\Delta
such that the target tree
\Delta
has at most
2g+2n-5
edges.

Proof.

Let
\varphi:\Gamma^{\prime}\to\Delta
be any
n
-gonal structure on
\Gamma
, which exists by Theorem
2.5
. By definition,

\Ram_{\varphi}(\widetilde{v})=d_{\varphi}(\widetilde{v})\chi(v)-\chi(\widetilde{v})=d_{\varphi}(\widetilde{v})(2-\val(v))-2+2g(\widetilde{v})+\val(\widetilde{v})\geq 0

for all
\widetilde{v}\in V(\Gamma^{\prime})
, where
v=\varphi(\widetilde{v})
.

Now let
v\in V(\Delta)
be an extremal vertex at the end of an edge
e\in E(\Delta)
, and let
\widetilde{v}\in\varphi^{-1}(v)
. The above inequality implies that if
\Ram_{\varphi}(\widetilde{v})=0
then
g(\widetilde{v})=0
,
d_{\varphi}(\widetilde{v})=1
, and
\val(\widetilde{v})=1
, and if
\Ram_{\varphi}(\widetilde{v})=1
then
g(\widetilde{v})=0
,
d_{\varphi}(\widetilde{v})=2
, and
\val(\widetilde{v})=1
. In both cases,
\widetilde{v}
is an extremal genus zero vertex at the end of an edge of
\Gamma^{\prime}
lying above
e
, along which
\varphi
has degree
1
or
2
. Therefore, if
\Ram_{\varphi}(\widetilde{v})\leq 1
for all
\widetilde{v}\in\varphi^{-1}(v)
, then we can modify
\varphi:\Gamma^{\prime}\to\Delta
by removing the edge
e
and its preimages. Similarly, let
v\in V(\Delta)
be a vertex of valence 2. If
\Ram_{\varphi}(\widetilde{v})=0
for
\widetilde{v}\in\varphi^{-1}(v)
, then
g(\widetilde{v})=0
and
\val(\widetilde{v})=2
, and if this holds for all
\widetilde{v}\in\varphi^{-1}(v)
, then
v
and all vertices over it may be removed from the models.

Applying these modifications, we may assume that
\varphi:\Gamma^{\prime}\to\Delta
has the following properties:

(1)

The total ramification degree over each
v\in V(\Delta)
with
\val(v)=1
is at least 2.

(2)

The total ramification degree over each
v\in V(\Delta)
with
\val(v)=2
is at least 1.

Now let
a_{i}
be the number of vertices of
\Delta
of valence
i
. By the global Riemann–Hurwitz formula (
3
), the ramification degree of
\varphi
is equal to

\Ram(\varphi)=n\chi(\Delta)-\chi(\Gamma)=2g+2n-2\geq 2a_{1}+a_{2}.

On the other hand, we know that

a_{1}+2a_{2}+3a_{3}+\cdots=2|E(\Delta)|,\quad a_{1}+a_{2}+a_{3}+\cdots=|V(\Delta)|=|E(\Delta)|+1,

which implies that

|E(\Delta)|\leq 2a_{1}+a_{2}-3\leq 2g+2n-5.

∎

2.8.
Tropical ppavs and tropical moments

A
tropical ppav

X=(\Lambda,\Lambda^{\prime},[\cdot,\cdot],\zeta)
of dimension
d
is determined by a pair of free abelian groups
\Lambda
and
\Lambda^{\prime}
of rank
d
, a nondegenerate pairing
[\cdot,\cdot]:\Lambda\times\Lambda^{\prime}\to\mathbb{R}
, and an isomorphism
\zeta:\Lambda^{\prime}\to\Lambda
that induces a symmetric positive definite bilinear form
(\cdot,\cdot)=[\zeta(\cdot),\cdot]
. The map
\lambda^{\prime}\mapsto[\cdot,\lambda^{\prime}]
defines an inclusion
\Lambda^{\prime}\subset\Lambda^{*}_{\mathbb{R}}=\Hom(\Lambda,\mathbb{R})
, and the ppav itself is the
d
-dimensional torus
\Hom(\Lambda,\mathbb{R})/\Lambda^{\prime}
. The inner product
(\cdot,\cdot)
induces a norm
\|\cdot\|
on
\Lambda^{*}_{\mathbb{R}}
, and the
Voronoi polytope
of
X
is the convex symmetric polyhedral domain

\Vor(X)=\{x\in\Lambda^{*}_{\mathbb{R}}:\|x\|\leq\|x-y\|\mbox{ for all }y\in\Lambda^{\prime}\}.

The
2n
-th moment

I_{2n}(X)
is the integral

I_{2n}(X)=\int_{\Vor(X)}\|x\|^{2n}dx.

Since the Voronoi polytope is a fundamental domain for the lattice
\Lambda^{\prime}
, the zeroth moment computes the volume of
X
as a Riemannian manifold:

I_{0}(X)=\Vol(X),

while the second moment

I_{2}(X)=\int_{\Vor(X)}\|x\|^{2}dx

has arithmetic significance (see
[
dJS22
]
).

In this paper, we consider two examples of tropical ppavs. Let
\Gamma
be a metric graph and let
H_{1}(\Gamma,\mathbb{Z})
be the simplicial homology group of
\Gamma
. We interpret
H_{1}(\Gamma,\mathbb{Z})
as the space
\Omega^{1}(\Gamma)
of
harmonic 1-forms
on
\Gamma
, by viewing a 1-cycle
\sum_{e\in E(\Gamma)}a_{e}e\in H_{1}(\Gamma,\mathbb{Z})
as a 1-form
\sum_{e\in E(\Gamma)}a_{e}de\in\Omega^{1}(\Gamma)
. The lattices
\Lambda=\Omega^{1}(\Gamma)
,
\Lambda^{\prime}=H_{1}(\Gamma,\mathbb{Z})
, the
integration pairing

\Omega^{1}(\Gamma)\times H_{1}(\Gamma,\mathbb{Z})\to\mathbb{R},\quad\left[\sum a_{e}de,\sum b_{e}e\right]=\sum a_{e}b_{e}\ell(e),

(4)

and the trivial isomorphism
H_{1}(\Gamma,\mathbb{Z})=\Omega^{1}(\Gamma)
define the
tropical Jacobian

\Jac(\Gamma)
, a tppav of dimension
g(\Gamma)=\dim H_{1}(\Gamma,\mathbb{Z})
. Tropical Abel–Jacobi theory identifies
\Jac(\Gamma)
with
\Pic_{0}(\Gamma)
, the set of equivalence classes of degree zero divisors on
\Gamma
.

The
tropical Prym variety

\Prym(\widetilde{\Gamma}/\Gamma)
of a double cover
\pi:\widetilde{\Gamma}\to\Gamma
of metric graphs was defined in
[
JL18
]
in analogy with the algebraic setting, as the connected component of the identity of the kernel of the norm map

\pi_{*}:\Jac(\widetilde{\Gamma})\to\Jac(\Gamma)

on divisors. However, this definition does not behave well under edge contractions of
\Gamma
. Specifically, the Prym variety
\Prym(\widetilde{\Gamma}_{F}/\Gamma_{F})
of a double cover
\pi_{F}:\widetilde{\Gamma}_{F}\to\Gamma_{F}
obtained from a double cover
\pi:\widetilde{\Gamma}\to\Gamma
by contracting a set of edges
F\subset E(\Gamma)
is not equal to the limit of
\Prym(\widetilde{\Gamma}/\Gamma)
if
d(\widetilde{\Gamma}_{F}/\Gamma_{F})>d(\widetilde{\Gamma}/\Gamma)
. Hence
\Prym(\widetilde{\Gamma}/\Gamma)
, which we henceforth call the
divisorial Prym variety
, does not behave well in moduli and is not a convenient object for our purposes.

This problem was rectified in the paper
[
RZ25
]
, where we constructed an alternative object, the
continuous Prym variety

\Prym_{c}(\widetilde{\Gamma}/\Gamma)
of a double cover
\pi:\widetilde{\Gamma}\to\Gamma
. The involution
\iota:\widetilde{\Gamma}\to\widetilde{\Gamma}
associated to
\pi
defines pullback and pushforward maps

\iota^{*}:\Omega^{1}(\widetilde{\Gamma})\to\Omega^{1}(\widetilde{\Gamma}),\quad\iota_{*}:H_{1}(\widetilde{\Gamma},\mathbb{Z})\to H_{1}(\widetilde{\Gamma},\mathbb{Z}),

and we consider the lattices

\Lambda=\Omega^{1}(\widetilde{\Gamma})/\Ker(\Id-\iota^{*}),\quad\Lambda^{\prime}=\operatorname{Im}\iota_{*}.

The restriction of the isomorphism
H_{1}(\widetilde{\Gamma},\mathbb{Z})=\Omega^{1}(\widetilde{\Gamma})
to
\Lambda^{\prime}
defines a map
\Lambda^{\prime}\to\Lambda
than can be divided by two to obtain a natural isomorphism
\zeta:\Lambda^{\prime}\to\Lambda
. The continuous Prym variety is the dimension
t(\widetilde{\Gamma}/\Gamma)
tropical ppav associated to the lattices
\Lambda
and
\Lambda^{\prime}
, the restriction of the integration pairing, and the isomorphism
\zeta
. It is elementary to verify that the lattices
\Lambda
and
\Lambda^{\prime}
behave well under contraction, and hence we have the following result:

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
\pi:\widetilde{\Gamma}\to\Gamma
be a double cover, let
F\subset E(\Gamma)
be a set of edges, and let
\pi_{F}:\widetilde{\Gamma}_{\widetilde{F}}\to\Gamma_{F}
be the double cover obtained by contracting the edges in
F
. The Prym variety
\Prym(\widetilde{\Gamma}_{\widetilde{F}}/\Gamma_{F})
is the limit of the Prym variety
\Prym(\widetilde{\Gamma}/\Gamma)
as
\ell(e)\to 0
for all
e\in F
. In particular, for all
n\geq 0
we have

I_{2n}(\Prym_{c}(\widetilde{\Gamma}_{\widetilde{F}}/\Gamma_{F}))=\lim_{\ell(e)\to 0,\,e\in F}I_{2n}(\Prym_{c}(\widetilde{\Gamma}/\Gamma)).

We also observe that neither
\Prym_{c}(\widetilde{\Gamma}/\Gamma)
nor
\Prym(\widetilde{\Gamma}/\Gamma)
change when we contract a dilated edge of
\Gamma
, since the lattices
\Lambda
and
\Lambda^{\prime}
do not change. In other words, we have the following result:

Proposition 2.8
.

Let
\pi:\widetilde{\Gamma}\to\Gamma
be a double cover and let
F=E(\Gamma_{\dil})
be the set of dilated edges of
\Gamma
. Then
\pi_{\widetilde{F}}:\widetilde{\Gamma}_{\widetilde{F}}\to\Gamma_{F}
is an edge-free double cover and

\Prym(\widetilde{\Gamma}_{\widetilde{F}}/\Gamma_{F})\simeq\Prym(\widetilde{\Gamma}/\Gamma).

In particular, for all
n\geq 0
we have

I_{2n}(\Prym_{c}(\widetilde{\Gamma}_{\widetilde{F}}/\Gamma_{F}))=I_{2n}(\Prym_{c}(\widetilde{\Gamma}/\Gamma)).

These two results imply that it is sufficient to compute the second moment
I_{2}(\Prym(\widetilde{\Gamma}/\Gamma))
for all free double covers, since the second moment for any edge-free double cover, and hence any double cover, can then be obtained as a limit.

The continuous Prym variety comes equipped with a natural map
\Prym_{c}(\widetilde{\Gamma}/\Gamma)\to\Prym(\widetilde{\Gamma}/\Gamma)
to the divisorial Prym variety of degree
2^{d(\widetilde{\Gamma}/\Gamma)-1}
. In particular,
\Prym_{c}(\widetilde{\Gamma}/\Gamma)=\Prym(\widetilde{\Gamma}/\Gamma)
when
d(\widetilde{\Gamma}/\Gamma)=1
, in other words when
\pi
is free or has connected dilation subgraph. Our main tool for computing the second moment of the tropical Prym variety, the trigonal construction, is only defined for free double covers. Hence, in practice we do not need to distinguish
\Prym_{c}(\widetilde{\Gamma}/\Gamma)
and
\Prym(\widetilde{\Gamma}/\Gamma)
.

Remark 2.9
.

The Jacobian
\Jac(\Gamma)
of a metric graph
\Gamma
is a tropical ppav of dimension
g
, so it is natural to assume that its volume should be a degree
g
function in the edge lengths of
\Gamma
. Note, however, that the integration pairing (
4
), and hence the inner product that it defines, is linear, rather than quadratic, in the edge lengths. Hence units of length on
\Gamma
become units of area on
\Jac(\Gamma)
, and the volume of
\Jac(\Gamma)
is a function of degree
g/2
in the edge lengths of
\Gamma
. More generally,
I_{2n}(\Jac(\Gamma))
is a function of degree
g/2+n
, while
I_{2n}(\Prym(\widetilde{\Gamma}/\Gamma))
is a function of degree
t(\widetilde{\Gamma}/\Gamma)/2+n
in the edge lengths of
\Gamma
.

2.9.
The trigonal construction

Finally, we recall the tropical trigonal construction, which is our principal tool for computing the second moment of the tropical Prym variety. Given a connected étale double cover
p:\widetilde{X}\to X
of an algebraic curve
X
with a fixed trigonal map
X\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
, Recillas
[
Rec74
]
constructs a tetragonal curve
Y\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
such that the Jacobian
\Jac(Y)
is isomorphic to the Prym variety
\Prym(\widetilde{X}/X)
(in particular,
g(Y)=g(X)-1
). In
[
RZ25
]
, we defined a tropical analogue of Recillas’s construction.

Let
\pi:\widetilde{\Gamma}\to\Gamma
be a free double cover of a trigonal tropical curve
\varphi:\Gamma\to\Delta
(if it is necessary to attach trees to
\Gamma
to define
\varphi
, we attach two copies of each tree to
\widetilde{\Gamma}
and extend
\pi
trivially). For
x\in\Delta
, define the pullback

\varphi^{*}(x)=\sum_{y\in\varphi^{-1}(x)}d_{\varphi}(y)y\in\Div(\Gamma),

the free abelian group on the points of
\Gamma
. Similarly, we define the pushforward of a divisor on
\widetilde{\Gamma}
:

\pi_{*}\sum_{\widetilde{y}\in\widetilde{\Gamma}}a_{\widetilde{y}}\widetilde{y}=\sum_{\widetilde{y}\in\widetilde{\Gamma}}a_{\widetilde{y}}\pi(\widetilde{y}).

We now consider the set of sections of the fibers of
\pi:\widetilde{\Gamma}\to\Gamma
over the various points of
\Delta
:

\widetilde{\Pi}=\left\{D\in\Div(\widetilde{\Gamma}):D\geq 0\mbox{ and }\pi_{*}(D)=\varphi^{*}(x)\mbox{ for some (necessarily unique) }x\in\Delta\right\}.

Theorem 2.10

(Theorem 5.1 in
[
RZ25
]
)
.

Let
\pi:\widetilde{\Gamma}\to\Gamma
be a connected free double cover of metric graphs and let
\varphi:\Gamma\to\Delta
be a harmonic morphism of degree 3, where
\Delta
is a metric tree.

(1)

There is a natural structure of a metric graph on
\widetilde{\Pi}
such that the map
\widetilde{\psi}:\widetilde{\Pi}\to\Delta
, which sends
D\in\widetilde{\Pi}
to the unique
x\in\Delta
such that
\pi_{*}(D)=\varphi^{*}(x)
, is a harmonic morphism of degree 8.

(2)

The metric graph
\widetilde{\Pi}
consists of two isomorphic connected components
\Pi
that are exchanged by the involution that is induced by the fixed-point-free involution
\iota:\widetilde{\Gamma}\to\widetilde{\Gamma}
corresponding to the double cover, and the restriction
\psi:\Pi\to\Delta
of
\widetilde{\psi}
to each component is a harmonic morphism of degree 4.

(3)

The metric graph
\Pi
has genus
g(\Pi)=g(\Gamma)-1
, and the Prym variety of the free double cover
\pi:\widetilde{\Gamma}\to\Gamma
and the Jacobian variety of
\Pi
are isomorphic as tropical principally polarized abelian varieties:

\Prym_{c}(\widetilde{\Gamma}/\Gamma)\simeq\Jac(\Pi).

We note again that
\Prym(\widetilde{\Gamma}/\Gamma)=\Prym_{c}(\widetilde{\Gamma}/\Gamma)
for free double covers. We also note that the paper
[
RZ25
]
did not consider vertex genera, in particular
\varphi
is not required to be effective. If
\Gamma
has vertex genera and
\varphi
is effective, then there is a natural way to assign vertex genera to
\Pi
in such a way that
g(\Pi)=g(\Gamma)-1
and that
\psi
is effective as well. We do not require this and leave the details to the avid reader.

3.
Tropical moments and matroids

In this section, we recall the known formulas for the moments of tropical abelian varieties. The zeroth moments (the volumes) of the Jacobian and the Prym were computed in
[
ABKS14
]
and
[
LZ22
]
, respectively. We restate these formulas in terms of certain matroids: the graphic matroid
\mathcal{M}(\Gamma)
determines the volume of the Jacobian
\Jac(\Gamma)
, while the signed graphic matroid
\mathcal{M}(\widetilde{\Gamma}/\Gamma)
determines the volume of the Prym variety
\Prym(\widetilde{\Gamma}/\Gamma)
of a double cover
\widetilde{\Gamma}\to\Gamma
. The second moment of the Jacobian was found in
[
dJS23
]
. However, the formula given in
[
dJS23
]
is not matroidal, and in Proposition
8
we find an equivalent matroidal formula, which forms the basis of our matroidal formula for the second moment of the Prym variety.

3.1.
Moments of the tropical Jacobian in terms of the graphic matroid

Let
\Gamma
be a metric graph together with a choice of underlying model that we denote by
\Gamma
by abuse of notation. Let
m=|E(\Gamma)|
and
n=|V(\Gamma)|
, so that the genus is
g=g(\Gamma)=m-n+1
. We recall the definition of the graphic matroid
\mathcal{M}(\Gamma)
of
\Gamma
. The ground set is the set of edges
E(\Gamma)
, and an edge set
F\subset E(\Gamma)
is independent if the subgraph
\Gamma[F]
spanned by
F
contains no cycles. The bases
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{M}(X))
are the edge sets of the spanning trees, so that the rank of
\mathcal{M}(\Gamma)
is the number of edges in a spanning tree and is equal to

\rk(\mathcal{M}(X))=m-g=n-1.

Finally, the circuits of
\mathcal{M}(\Gamma)
are the simple closed cycles.

Since
\mathcal{M}(\Gamma)
contains information about the cycles of
\Gamma
, it is reasonable to expect that the Jacobian
\Jac(\Gamma)
can be reconstructed solely from the graphic matroid
\mathcal{M}(\Gamma)
and the edge length assignment
\ell:E(\Gamma)\to\mathbb{R}_{>0}
on its ground set. This is indeed the case, and the relationship between
\mathcal{M}(\Gamma)
and
\Jac(\Gamma)
was explored in detail in
[
CV10b
]
and
[
BMV11
]
(see also
[
Ger82
]
). In particular, we should be able to calculate all moments of
\Jac(\Gamma)
directly from the matroid
\mathcal{M}(\Gamma)
.

The zeroth moment, or volume, of the tropical Jacobian was computed in
[
ABKS14
]
, and we recall this result. Let
T\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{M}(\Gamma))
be the edge set of a spanning tree of
\Gamma
. We define the
weight
of
T
and the
zeroth weight
of the entire graph
\Gamma
as

w(T)=\prod_{e\notin T}e,\quad w_{0}(\Gamma)=\sum_{T\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{M}(\Gamma))}w(T).

(5)

Here and henceforth we use the same letter to denote an edge
e\in E(\Gamma)
of a metric graph and its length. The weight
w_{0}(\Gamma)
is a homogeneous degree
g
polynomial in the edge lengths
e
, and furthermore it has degree at most one in each edge length. By Remark
2.9
,
I_{0}(\Jac(\Gamma))
has degree
g/2
in the edge lengths, so we may expect it to be expressible in terms of
w_{0}(\Gamma)
. This is indeed the case.

Theorem 3.1

(Theorem 5.2 in
[
ABKS14
]
)
.

The zeroth moment of the tropical Jacobian
\Jac(\Gamma)
of a metric graph
\Gamma
is equal to

I_{0}(\Jac(\Gamma))=\sqrt{w_{0}(\Gamma)}=\frac{w_{0}(\Gamma)}{\sqrt{w_{0}(\Gamma)}}.

(6)

Note that we have intentionally factored the zeroth moment as a fraction, so that the numerator is a homogeneous polynomial of degree
g
in the edge lengths and the denominator (which happens to be the square root of the numerator) has degree
g/2
.

We now derive a similar matroidal formula for the second moment, by reinterpreting the results of the papers
[
dJS23
]
and
[
RSW23
]
in terms of the graphic matroid
\mathcal{M}(\Gamma)
. Let

\mathcal{I}_{k}(\mathcal{M}(\Gamma))=\{F\in\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{M}(\Gamma)):|F|=k\},\quad k=0,\ldots,n-1=\rk(\mathcal{M}(\Gamma))

be the set of independent sets of
\mathcal{M}(\Gamma)
having
k
elements. The set
\mathcal{I}_{n-1}(\mathcal{M}(\Gamma))=\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{M}(\Gamma))
is the set of bases of
\mathcal{M}(\Gamma)
, that is to say, the edge sets of the spanning trees of
\Gamma
. Similarly, elements of
\mathcal{I}_{n-2}(\mathcal{M}(\Gamma))
are the edge sets of
spanning 2-forests
of
\Gamma
(a spanning 2-forest is allowed to have an isolated vertex as one of its two trees).

In analogy with Equation (
5
), we now define the
weight
of a spanning
2
-forest
F\in\mathcal{I}_{n-2}(\mathcal{M}(\Gamma))
and the
first weight
of the graph
\Gamma
as

w(F)=\prod_{e\notin F}e,\quad w_{1}(\Gamma)=\sum_{F\in\mathcal{I}_{n-2}(\mathcal{M}(\Gamma))}w(F).

(7)

In addition, we define
length
of a spanning tree
T\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{M}(\Gamma))
and the
length
of the graph
\Gamma
as

\ell(T)=\sum_{e\notin T}e,\quad\ell(\Gamma)=\sum_{e\in E(\Gamma)}e.

The quantities
\ell(T)
and
\ell(\Gamma)
are linear in the edge lengths, while
w_{1}(\Gamma)
is a degree
g+1
homogeneous polynomial in the edge lengths. By Remark
2.9
, the second moment of
\Jac(\Gamma)
should be a function of degree
g/2+1
in the edge lengths. An explicit formula for the second moment of the tropical Jacobian is one of the principal results of
[
dJS23
]
(see Theorem 8.1), and we now give a matroidal version of this formula.

Proposition 3.2
.

The second moment of the tropical Jacobian
\Jac(\Gamma)
of a metric graph
\Gamma
is equal to

I_{2}(\Jac(\Gamma))=\frac{p(\Gamma)}{12\sqrt{w_{0}(\Gamma)}},\quad p(\Gamma)=2w_{0}(\Gamma)\ell(\Gamma)-\sum_{T\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{M}(\Gamma))}w(T)\ell(T)-2w_{1}(\Gamma).

Proof.

Theorem 8.1 in
[

]

[

]
and the related paper
[

]

[
dJS23
]
establishes the following relation between the zeroth and second moments of
\Jac(\Gamma)
, the total length of
\Gamma

\tau

\frac{1}{2}\tau(\Gamma)+\frac{I_{2}(\Jac(\Gamma))}{I_{0}(\Jac(\Gamma))}=\frac{1}{8}\ell(\Gamma).

We note that in
[

denotes the quantity
I_{2}(\Jac(\Gamma))/I_{0}(\Jac(\Gamma))
, or the

second moment of

\tau

\tau(\Gamma)=-\frac{1}{12}\ell(\Gamma)+\frac{1}{6w_{0}(\Gamma)}\sum_{T\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{M}(\Gamma)))}w(T)\ell(T)+\frac{1}{3}\frac{w_{1}(\Gamma)}{w_{0}(\Gamma)}.

(9)

First, assume that all edge lengths of
\Gamma
are equal to 1. Then
\ell(\Gamma)=|E|
, while
w(T)=1
and
\ell(T)=g

, and (

\tau(\Gamma)=-\frac{|E|}{12}+\frac{g}{6}+\frac{1}{3}\frac{w_{1}(\Gamma)}{w_{0}(\Gamma)}.

This relation is proved in Remark 5.9 in
[

]
, hence

holds for

In general, formula (

) can be proved using the arguments outlined in Section 5.3 of
[
RSW23
]
. Alternatively, we proceed as follows. If all edge lengths of
\Gamma
are integers, we pick the model having all edge lengths equal to 1, and it is elementary to verify that the right hand side of (

are rational numbers, we can rescale by some

∎

Before proceeding, we make several observations about the formula (

is a polynomial of degree

in the edge lengths, consisting only of monomials of the form

has already been computed, then (

. However, for an individual graph

and using (

3.2.
The signed graphic matroid of a double cover

In this section, we recall how to associate a pair of dual matroids

and

. We retain this terminology, even though our framework is somewhat more general than that of

Definition 3.3
.

Let
\pi:\widetilde{\Gamma}\to\Gamma
be a double cover of tropical curves. There exists a matroid

, called the
signed cographic matroid
of

, with the following properties.

(1)

The ground set of

is the set

of undilated edges of
\Gamma
.

(2)

A set

of undilated edges of
\Gamma
is independent for the matroid

if every connected component of
\Gamma\backslash F
has connected preimage in
\widetilde{\Gamma}
.

(3)

A set
F\subset E_{\mathrm{ud}}(\Gamma)
of undilated edges of
\Gamma
is a basis for
\mathcal{M}^{*}(\widetilde{\Gamma}/\Gamma)
if and only each connected component of
\Gamma\backslash F
is one of the following two types:

(a)

A graph of genus one having no dilated vertices or edges, and having connected preimage in
\widetilde{\Gamma}
.

(b)

A tree having a connected dilation subgraph.

(4)

The rank of
\mathcal{M}^{*}(\widetilde{\Gamma}/\Gamma)
is equal to

.

The
signed graphic matroid

of

is the dual of

, so the ground set of

is

and the bases of

are the complements of the bases of

if it has connected preimage in

, so basis for

is a maximal spanning subgraph whose connected components are unbalanced. The rank of

is equal to the number

. As before, the set of edges of

is defined with respect to an underlying choice of model, and the matroids

and

and

We introduce one additional structure on the signed graphic matroid.

Definition 3.4
.

Let

be a double cover of tropical curves. The

As we mentioned above, the Jacobian

and the edge length assignment

and

In

(see also

(10)

The

of the double cover

is the sum of the weights of the bases, modified by the indices:

(11)

Proposition 3.5
.

The zeroth moment of the continuous tropical Prym variety
\Prym_{c}(\widetilde{\Gamma}/\Gamma)
of a double cover
\pi:\widetilde{\Gamma}\to\Gamma
of tropical curves is equal to

I_{0}(\Prym_{c}(\widetilde{\Gamma}/\Gamma))=\sqrt{w_{0}(\widetilde{\Gamma}/\Gamma)}=\frac{w_{0}(\widetilde{\Gamma}/\Gamma)}{\sqrt{w_{0}(\widetilde{\Gamma}/\Gamma)}}.

(12)

Proof.

For a free double cover, this result is Theorem 3.4 in
[
LZ22
]
. The result for arbitrary double covers can be obtained by edge contraction. Suppose that
\pi:\widetilde{\Gamma}\to\Gamma
is edge-free. To each dilated vertex
v\in V(\Gamma)
we attach a loop
e
, and replace the preimage vertex
\pi^{-1}(v)
with a pair of vertices
\widetilde{v}^{\pm}
connected by a pair of parallel edges
\widetilde{e}^{\pm}
mapping to
e
. The result is a free double cover
\pi^{\prime}:\widetilde{\Gamma}^{\prime}\to\Gamma^{\prime}
whose contraction along the new loops is
\pi
. By Proposition
2.7
, the limit of
I_{0}(\widetilde{\Gamma}^{\prime}/\Gamma^{\prime})
is equal to
I_{0}(\widetilde{\Gamma}/\Gamma)
. On the other hand, the signed graphic matroid
\mathcal{M}(\widetilde{\Gamma}/\Gamma)
is the contraction of the matroid
\mathcal{M}(\widetilde{\Gamma}^{\prime}/\Gamma^{\prime})
along the set of the new loops, hence
w_{0}(\widetilde{\Gamma}/\Gamma)
is the limit of
w_{0}(\widetilde{\Gamma}^{\prime}/\Gamma)
. This proves the result for edge-free double covers. Finally, for an arbitrary double cover, contracting the set of dilated edges does not change the zeroth moment by Proposition
2.8
, and does not change the matroid (and hence
w_{0}(\widetilde{\Gamma}/\Gamma)
) by definition. Hence the general case follows from the case of free double covers.

∎

4.
The second moment of the tropical Prym variety in
g\leq 4

In this section, we state and prove our main result, which computes the second moment
I_{2}(\Prym(\widetilde{\Gamma}/\Gamma))
of the tropical Prym variety of any double cover
\widetilde{\Gamma}\to\Gamma
with
g(\Gamma)\leq 4
. We will see that
I_{2}(\Prym(\widetilde{\Gamma}/\Gamma))
is a sum of two terms: a polynomial contribution
p(\widetilde{\Gamma}/\Gamma)
that is a direct generalization of the formula (
8
) for
I_{2}(\Jac(\Gamma))
, with the graphic matroid
\mathcal{M}(\Gamma)
replaced by the signed graphic matroid
\mathcal{M}(\widetilde{\Gamma}/\Gamma)
, and a piecewise-polynomial term
q(\widetilde{\Gamma}/\Gamma)
that does not appear for Jacobians. The presence of this term reflects the fact that the polyhedral type of the Voronoi polytope of
\Prym(\widetilde{\Gamma}/\Gamma)
, which is the domain of integration for
I_{2}(\Prym(\widetilde{\Gamma}/\Gamma))
, depends, in general, on the edge lengths of
\Gamma
(see Section
5.1
).

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\pi:\widetilde{\Gamma}\to\Gamma
be a double cover of tropical curves, where

. The second moment of the tropical Prym variety
\Prym(\widetilde{\Gamma}/\Gamma)
is equal to

I_{2}(\Prym(\widetilde{\Gamma}/\Gamma))=\frac{p(\widetilde{\Gamma}/\Gamma)+q(\widetilde{\Gamma}/\Gamma)}{12\sqrt{w_{0}(\widetilde{\Gamma}/\Gamma)}}.

(13)

Here
p(\widetilde{\Gamma}/\Gamma)
is a degree
g
polynomial function in the edge lengths of
\Gamma
given by Equation (
14
), and
q(\widetilde{\Gamma}/\Gamma)
is a degree
g
piecewise-polynomial function in the edge lengths of
\Gamma
defined in Section
4.2
and in the Appendix.

4.1.
The polynomial term

We recall that the zeroth moment

5.
Extension of the Prym–Torelli morphism

In this brief section, we explain why
I_{2}(\Prym(\widetilde{\Gamma}/\Gamma))
, unlike
I_{2}(\Jac(\Gamma))
, has a piecewise-polynomial term, and state some actual and conjectural geometric applications of the calculations of Theorem
4.1
. We refer the reader to
[
CMGHL17
]
for the necessary background.

We consider the following moduli spaces together with their compactifications:

•

\mathcal{M}_{g}\subset\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g}
, the moduli space of smooth genus
g
curves compactified by the Deligne–Mumford moduli space of stable genus
g
curves.

•

\mathcal{R}_{g}\subset\overline{\mathcal{R}}_{g}
, the moduli space of connected étale double covers of genus
g
curves compactified by the space of admissible double covers of stable curves.

•

\mathcal{A}_{g}\subset\overline{\mathcal{A}}^{V}_{g}
, the moduli space of ppavs of dimension
g
and its second Voronoi compactification.

The assignments
C\mapsto\Jac(C)
and
(C^{\prime}\to C)\mapsto\Prym(C^{\prime}/C)
define the
Torelli
and
Prym–Torelli morphisms

t_{g}:\mathcal{M}_{g}\to\mathcal{A}_{g},\quad p_{g}:\mathcal{R}_{g}\to\mathcal{A}_{g-1}^{,}

and a natural question is to ask whether
t_{g}
and
p_{g}
extend to the boundary.

Mumford and Namikawa
[
Nam80
]
proved that
t_{g}
extends to a morphism
\overline{t}_{g}:\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g}\to\overline{\mathcal{A}}^{V}_{g}
, and we describe the combinatorial result that underlies this theorem. The boundary strata of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g}
are indexed by stable weighted graphs of genus
G
:

\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g}=\bigsqcup_{G}\mathcal{M}_{G}.

For a stable weighted graph
G
let
M_{G}=\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{|E(G)|}
be the cone of metric graphs having underlying model
G
, and for each
\Gamma\in M_{G}
construct the Voronoi polytope of the Jacobian
\Jac(\Gamma)
. The morphism
t_{g}
extends to the stratum
\mathcal{M}_{G}
(the target being the second Voronoi compactification) if and only if the following property holds: the Voronoi polytopes
\Vor(\Jac(\Gamma))
are normally equivalent for all
\Gamma\in M_{G}
. It turns out that this is indeed the case for all stable weighted graphs
G
, and that therefore
t_{g}
extends over all of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g}
.

Friedman and Smith showed
[
FS86
]
that the Prym–Torelli morphism
p_{g}
does not extend to the entire boundary of
\overline{\mathcal{R}}_{g}
, and the indeterminancy locus of
\overline{p}_{g}:\overline{\mathcal{R}}_{g}\dashrightarrow\overline{\mathcal{A}}^{V}_{g-1}
was identified in
[
ABH02
]
. We describe the analogous combinatorial problem. The boundary strata of
\overline{\mathcal{R}}_{g}
are indexed by double covers of stable weighted graphs:

\overline{\mathcal{R}}_{g}=\bigsqcup_{\widetilde{G}\to G}\mathcal{R}_{\widetilde{G}/G}.

For a double cover
p:\widetilde{G}\to G
, compute the Voronoi polytopes of the continuous Prym varieties
\Vor(\Prym_{c}(\widetilde{\Gamma}/\Gamma))
for all double covers of metric graphs
(\widetilde{\Gamma}\to\Gamma)\in R_{\widetilde{G}/G}
having underlying model
p
. Then
p_{g}
extends to a locus
\mathcal{R}_{\widetilde{G}/G}\subset\overline{\mathcal{R}}_{g}
if and only if all these Voronoi polytopes are normally equivalent. It turns out that this is not the case in general, and that this property fails precisely when
p:\widetilde{G}\to G
is a degeneration of an
FS_{n}
-double cover for some
n\geq 2
(meaning that the latter is an edge contraction of the former). Hence the locus of indeterminancy of
\overline{p}_{g}:\overline{\mathcal{R}}_{g}\dashrightarrow\overline{\mathcal{A}}^{V}_{g-1}
is the union of the closures of the strata
\mathcal{R}_{FS_{n}}
for
n\geq 2
. We note that the piecewise-polynomial term
q(\widetilde{\Gamma}/\Gamma)
in the formula for the second moment
I_{2}(\Prym(\widetilde{\Gamma}/\Gamma))
occurs, in genus 3 and 4, exactly on these cones.

A natural question is to find a resolution of
\overline{p}_{g}:\overline{\mathcal{R}}_{g}\dashrightarrow\overline{\mathcal{A}}^{V}_{g-1}
via a sequence of toric blowups. The relevant combinatorial problem is the following: for each cone
R_{\widetilde{G}/G}
, where
p:\widetilde{G}\to G
is a degeneration of an
FS_{n}
-cover, find a decomposition into subcones such that, on each subcone, the Voronoi polytope has constant normal fan. The minimal such decomposition, which we call the
Voronoi decomposition
, describes the minimal blowup. This problem has been solved up to codimension 3 in
[
ABH02
]
and in the companion paper
[
Vol02
]
.

We claim that the collection of cones
R_{\widetilde{G}\to G\to T}\subset R_{\widetilde{G}/\mathbb{G}}
constructed in Theorem
4.1
solve this problem in
g=4
for all double covers
\widetilde{G}\to G
, and hence describe a full resolution of the Prym–Torelli map
\overline{p}_{4}:\overline{\mathcal{R}}_{4}\dashrightarrow\overline{\mathcal{A}}^{V}_{3}
. Indeed, for any double cover
\widetilde{\Gamma}\to\Gamma
in
R_{\widetilde{G}\to G\to T}
, where the target graph
G
is equipped with a fixed trigonal structure
G\to T
, the Prym variety
\Prym(\widetilde{\Gamma}/\Gamma)
is isomorphic to a Jacobian
\Jac(\Pi)
. Varying the edge lengths inside the subcone changes the edge lengths of
\Pi
but not the underlying graph. Therefore, the Voronoi polytope of
\Prym(\widetilde{\Gamma}/\Gamma)
has constant normal cone on the subcone
R_{\widetilde{G}\to G\to T}
, because by Mumford and Namikawa’s result this statement holds for the Jacobian
\Jac(\Pi)
. We note that we construct the decomposition of the cone
R_{\widetilde{G}/\mathbb{G}}
into subcones of the form
R_{\widetilde{G}\to G\to T}
only for maximal-dimensional cones (corresponding to points on the boundary of
\overline{\mathcal{R}}_{g}
), but this decomposition can be extended to all cones by edge contraction.

Unfortunately, it turns out that the cone decomposition described above is, in general, much finer that the Voronoi decomposition. For example, it is nontrivial on many cones
R_{\widetilde{G}/G}
corresponding to double covers
\widetilde{G}\to G
that do not degenerate to
FS_{n}
for any
n\geq 2
, for which we know the Voronoi decomposition to be trivial. In other words, the blowups determined by the cones
R_{\widetilde{G}\to G\to T}
are not minimal, and indeed blow up strata that lie outside the indeterminancy locus. We end by conjecturing how the Voronoi decomposition can be determined from our calculations.

Conjecture 5.1
.

The Voronoi decomposition of a cone
R_{\widetilde{G}/G}
is given by the domains of polynomiality of the second moment of the tropical Prym variety
I_{2}(\Prym(\widetilde{\Gamma}/\Gamma))
as
\widetilde{\Gamma}\to\Gamma
varies over
R_{\widetilde{G}/G}
.

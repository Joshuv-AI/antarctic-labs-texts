Petrov Types for the Weyl tensor via the Riemannian-to-Lorentzian Bridge
Amir Babak Aazami

Abstract
. We analyze oriented Riemannian 4-manifolds whose Weyl tensors W W satisfy the conformally invariant condition W ⁡ ( T , ⋅ , ⋅ , T ) = 0 W(T,\cdot,\cdot,T)=0 for some nonzero vector T T . While this can be algebraically classified via W W ’s normal form, we find a further geometric classification by deforming the metric into a Lorentzian one via T T . We show that such a W W will have the analogue of Petrov Types from general relativity, that only Types I and D can occur, and that each is completely determined by the number of critical points of W W ’s associated Lorentzian quadratic form. A similar result holds for the Lorentzian version of this question, with T T timelike.



1.
Introduction

The Weyl curvature tensor
W
of an oriented Riemannian 4-manifold, being trace-free, is well known to possess a
normal form
: Relative to any orthonormal basis, the linear endomorphism of
W
on
\Lambda^{2}
always has the block form
{\tiny\begin{bmatrix}A&B\\
B&A\end{bmatrix}}
. The
3\times 3
matrices
A,B
are symmetric, and by the classical work of
[
Ber61
,
ST69
]
, there exist bases relative to which
A,B
are diagonal and completely determined by just the critical points and values of
W
’s associated quadratic form on
\Lambda^{2}
. (If the metric was Einstein, then this would be true with the full curvature 4-tensor in place of
W
, and the quadratic form would be the sectional curvature.) Given this normal form for
W
, it is thus natural to investigate additional forms of symmetry. One may, e.g., analyze the case when
A=\pm B
, and this is well known to occur if and only if the 4-manifold is
self-dual
or
anti-self-dual
. There are many such 4-manifolds (see, e.g.,
[
LeB91
,
Tau92
]
), and anti-self-dual 4-manifolds in particular are related to R. Penrose’s twistor program
[
Bes07
,
LeB04
]
.

Here we analyze another condition on
W
’s normal form, namely, the condition that
A=O
in at least one orthonormal basis. Denoting that ordered basis by
\{e_{1},e_{2},e_{3},e_{4}\}
, that
A=O
in this basis is equivalent to
W(e_{1},\cdot,\cdot,e_{1})=0
, which is conformally invariant. After providing some examples and non-examples of such 4-manifolds, we proceed to classifying them. To begin with, in any basis
\{e_{i}\}
, if some
T=\sum_{i}c_{i}e_{i}
satisfies
W(T,\cdot,\cdot,T)=0
, then the equations
W(T,e_{j},e_{k},T)=0
yield an overdetermined system for the
c_{i}
’s. If one takes
\{e_{i}\}
to be a basis that diagonalizes
W
à la
[
Ber61
,
ST69
]
, then these equations become more tractable (Theorem
1
), and certain consequences can be drawn. However, to approach this problem in a more geometric, basis-independent manner, it is best to first change the metric. Namely, if
W(T,\cdot,\cdot,T)=0
for some metric
g
and unit-length vector field
T
, then
W
will also be trace-free with respect to the
Lorentzian
metric
g_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}}=g-2T^{\flat}\otimes T^{\flat}
. If one then considers
W
’s
g_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}
-induced endomorphism on
\Lambda^{2}
, then it will have
g_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}
-normal forms, analogous to the Petrov Types of general relativity
[
Pet69
,
O’N95
,
Ste+09
]
. Indeed, just as
[
Tho69
]
showed how the five possible Petrov Types are determined by the number of critical points of the spacetime’s sectional curvature function, in Theorems
2
and
3
we prove something similar:

can have only two
g_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}
-normal forms, and they are determined by the number of critical points of its associated
g_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}
-quadratic form:

Theorem
.

Let
(M,g)
be an oriented Riemannian 4-manifold whose Weyl tensor

satisfies
W(T,\cdot,\cdot,T)=0
for some unit-length vector field
T
on
M
. Set

and let
\hat{W}^{\scalebox{0.55}{$g_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}$}}\colon\Lambda^{2}\longrightarrow\Lambda^{2}
denote the linear endomorphism of

defined via the

-induced Lorentzian inner product
\langle{\,},{}\rangle_{\!g_{\scalebox{0.3}{\emph{L}}}}
on
\Lambda^{2}
:

\text{\emph{$\langle{\hat{W}^{\scalebox{0.55}{$g_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}$}}(v\wedge w)},{x\wedge y}\rangle_{\!g_{\scalebox{0.3}{\emph{L}}}}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}}=W(v,w,x,y)$}}\hskip 14.45377pt\text{for all $v,w,x,y\in T_{p}M$}.

Then at each

-quadratic form of

In other words, there is basis-independent geometry in the condition

that began our inquiry — though we point out that, unlike in the Riemannian case, the critical points and values here do not determine the invariants of

-normal form (i.e., the analogues of the entries in

needn’t be unique here, either. Nevertheless, Theorems

and

show the continued presence of normal forms in dimension four, a presence that is already rich: From their use in the proof of the Hitchin-Thorpe inequality

,

]

[

]
.

Finally, we reverse the roles of

and

, by considering when the Weyl tensor

of an oriented Lorentzian 4-manifold

Let

In contrast to the Petrov Types of general relativity, whose invariants cannot be determined by just the first derivatives of their quadratic forms (see
[

]
; their Hessian is also needed), Theorem

shows that those Weyl tensors that satisfy

are completely classical; indeed, they follow those of

(M_{1}\times M_{2},g_{1}\oplus g_{2})

W=\frac{c_{1}+c_{2}}{6}\Big(g_{1}\,{\tiny\mathchoice{\mathbin{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\displaystyle\bigcirc$\cr$\displaystyle\wedge$\cr}}}{}}{\mathbin{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\textstyle\bigcirc$\cr$\textstyle\wedge$\cr}}}{}}{\mathbin{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$\cr$\scriptstyle\wedge$\cr}}}{}}{\mathbin{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\bigcirc$\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\wedge$\cr}}}{}}}\,g_{1}-g_{1}\,{\tiny\mathchoice{\mathbin{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\displaystyle\bigcirc$\cr$\displaystyle\wedge$\cr}}}{}}{\mathbin{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\textstyle\bigcirc$\cr$\textstyle\wedge$\cr}}}{}}{\mathbin{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$\cr$\scriptstyle\wedge$\cr}}}{}}{\mathbin{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\bigcirc$\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\wedge$\cr}}}{}}}\,g_{2}+g_{2}\,{\tiny\mathchoice{\mathbin{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\displaystyle\bigcirc$\cr$\displaystyle\wedge$\cr}}}{}}{\mathbin{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\textstyle\bigcirc$\cr$\textstyle\wedge$\cr}}}{}}{\mathbin{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$\cr$\scriptstyle\wedge$\cr}}}{}}{\mathbin{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\bigcirc$\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\wedge$\cr}}}{}}}\,g_{2}\Big)

(see
[
Lee18
, p. 261]
). If
c_{1}\neq-c_{2}
, then
W\neq 0
. However, for any choice of
T=ax_{1}+bx_{2}
with
x_{1}\in T_{p_{1}}M_{1}
and
x_{2}\in T_{p_{2}}M_{2}
vectors of unit length and
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle W(T,x_{1},x_{1},T)\!\!

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!b^{2}W(x_{2},x_{1},x_{1},x_{2})=\frac{c_{1}+c_{2}}{6}(-b^{2}),

\displaystyle W(T,x_{2},x_{2},T)\!\!

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!a^{2}W(x_{1},x_{2},x_{2},x_{1})=\frac{c_{1}+c_{2}}{6}(-a^{2}),

and these vanish if and only if
a=b=0
. Thus if
W\neq 0
, then (
34
) cannot be satisfied for any nonzero
T
.

ii.

Self-dual/anti-self-dual 4-manifolds
: The easiest way to verify this is to recall that
W^{\pm}=0\iff A=\pm B
, together with the fact that (
34
) implies that
A=O
in some orthonormal basis (see (
40
) below). But let us verify this directly here. Thus, suppose that (
34
) holds for a self-dual metric (
W^{-}=0
), so that
\hat{W}(\xi)=0
for all
\xi\in\Lambda^{-}
. Then taking an oriented orthonormal basis of the form
\{T=e_{1},e_{2},e_{3},e_{4}\}
, and forming the following three eigenvectors of
\Lambda^{-}
,

\hskip 18.06749pt\Big\{\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(e_{1}\wedge e_{2}-e_{3}\wedge e_{4}),\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(e_{1}\wedge e_{3}-e_{4}\wedge e_{2}),\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(e_{1}\wedge e_{4}-e_{2}\wedge e_{3})\Big\},

it will be the case that

\hat{W}(e_{1}\wedge e_{2})=\hat{W}(e_{3}\wedge e_{4})\hskip 3.61371pt,\hskip 3.61371pt\hat{W}(e_{1}\wedge e_{3})=\hat{W}(e_{4}\wedge e_{2})\hskip 3.61371pt,\hskip 3.61371pt\hat{W}(e_{1}\wedge e_{4})=\hat{W}(e_{2}\wedge e_{3}).

Consider the first of these; because
W_{1212}=W_{1213}=W_{1214}=0
by (
34
), we have

(36)

\displaystyle\hat{W}(e_{1}\wedge e_{2})=W_{1234}e_{3}\wedge e_{4}+W_{1242}e_{4}\wedge e_{2}+W_{1223}e_{2}\wedge e_{3}.

On the other hand, because
W_{3434}=W_{1212}=0
,
W_{3442}=W_{1312}=0
, and
W_{3423}=W_{1412}=0
(recall (
31
)), we also have

(37)

\displaystyle\hat{W}(e_{3}\wedge e_{4})=W_{3412}e_{1}\wedge e_{2}+W_{3413}e_{1}\wedge e_{3}+W_{3414}e_{1}\wedge e_{4}.

Thus the only way to satisfy
\hat{W}(e_{1}\wedge e_{2})=\hat{W}(e_{3}\wedge e_{4})
is for both to be zero. Similarly for the other two cases, as well as for the anti-self-dual case
(W^{+}=0)
. We thus conclude that any self-dual or anti-self-dual 4-manifold that is not locally conformally flat cannot satisfy (
34
).

To understand when
W(T,\cdot,\cdot,T)=0

is
satisfied, we now turn to the classical work of
[
Ber61
,
ST69
]
(even more important for us will be its Lorentzian counterpart
[
Tho69
]
, in Section
4
below);
[
Ber61
,
ST69
]
showed that, at each
p\in M
, there is an orthonormal basis
\{e_{1},e_{2},e_{3},e_{4}\}\subseteq T_{p}M
relative to which
\hat{W}
takes the form

(38)

\displaystyle\hat{W}=\begin{bmatrix}\lambda_{1}&0&0&\mu_{1}&0&0\\
0&\lambda_{2}&0&0&\mu_{2}&0\\
0&0&\lambda_{3}&0&0&\mu_{3}\\
\mu_{1}&0&0&\lambda_{1}&0&0\\
0&\mu_{2}&0&0&\lambda_{2}&0\\
0&0&\mu_{3}&0&0&\lambda_{3}\end{bmatrix},

such that

(39)

\displaystyle\underbrace{\,\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}+\lambda_{3}=0\,}_{\text{because $W$ is trace-free}}\hskip 14.45377pt,\hskip 14.45377pt\underbrace{\,\mu_{1}+\mu_{2}+\mu_{3}=0\,,}_{\text{because $W$ satisfies the algebraic Bianchi identity}}

and such that the 2-planes

P_{1}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}}=e_{1}\wedge e_{2},P_{2}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}}=e_{1}\wedge e_{3},P_{3}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}}=\pm e_{1}\wedge e_{4},
satisfy

\hat{W}P_{i}=\lambda_{i}P_{i}+\mu_{i}*\!P_{i},i=1,2,3,

with the
\lambda_{i},\mu_{i}
being completely determined by the critical points and values of the quadratic form (
33
); in fact the
\lambda_{i}
’s are (some of) its critical values. (E.g., the Weyl tensor of (
35
) is in normal form relative to the orthonormal basis
\{\partial_{r}/\sqrt{8x^{3}},\partial_{x},\sqrt{8x^{3}}\partial_{y},\partial_{z}\}
.) The normal form (
38
) readily yields a pointwise algebraic classification of oriented Riemannian 4-manifolds satisfying (
34
), given by the following overdetermined system:

Theorem 1
.

Let
(M,g)
be an oriented Riemannian 4-manifold whose Weyl tensor
W
has normal form (
38
) at
p\in M
. Then

W(T,\cdot,\cdot,T)=0\phantom{\hskip 14.45377pt,\hskip 14.45377pt}\text{for $T\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}}=\sum_{i}c_{i}e_{i}\in T_{p}M$}

if and only if the following ten equations hold for the
c_{i}
:

\displaystyle\underbrace{\,c_{2}^{2}\lambda_{1}+c_{3}^{2}\lambda_{2}+c_{4}^{2}\lambda_{3}=0\,}_{\text{$W(v,e_{1},e_{1},v)=0$}}

\displaystyle\hskip 14.45377pt,\hskip 14.45377pt

\displaystyle\underbrace{\,c_{1}c_{2}\lambda_{1}+c_{3}c_{4}(\mu_{2}-\mu_{3})=0\,}_{\text{$W(v,e_{1},e_{2},v)=0$}},

\displaystyle\underbrace{\,c_{1}c_{3}\lambda_{2}+c_{2}c_{4}(-\mu_{1}+\mu_{3})=0\,}_{\text{$W(v,e_{1},e_{3},v)=0$}}

\displaystyle\hskip 14.45377pt,\hskip 14.45377pt

\displaystyle\underbrace{\,c_{1}c_{4}\lambda_{3}+c_{2}c_{3}(\mu_{1}-\mu_{2})=0\,}_{\text{$W(v,e_{1},e_{4},v)=0$}},

\displaystyle\underbrace{\,c_{1}^{2}\lambda_{1}+c_{4}^{2}\lambda_{2}+c_{3}^{2}\lambda_{3}=0\,}_{\text{$W(v,e_{2},e_{2},v)=0$}}

\displaystyle\hskip 14.45377pt,\hskip 14.45377pt

\displaystyle\underbrace{\,c_{2}c_{3}\lambda_{3}+c_{1}c_{4}(\mu_{1}-\mu_{2})=0\,}_{\text{$W(v,e_{2},e_{3},v)=0$}},

\displaystyle\underbrace{\,c_{2}c_{4}\lambda_{2}+c_{1}c_{3}(-\mu_{1}+\mu_{3})=0\,}_{\text{$W(v,e_{2},e_{4},v)=0$}}

\displaystyle\hskip 14.45377pt,\hskip 14.45377pt

\displaystyle\underbrace{\,c_{4}^{2}\lambda_{1}+c_{1}^{2}\lambda_{2}+c_{2}^{2}\lambda_{3}=0\,}_{\text{$W(v,e_{3},e_{3},v)=0$}},

\displaystyle\underbrace{\,c_{3}c_{4}\lambda_{1}+c_{1}c_{2}(\mu_{2}-\mu_{3})=0\,}_{\text{$W(v,e_{3},e_{4},v)=0$}}

\displaystyle\hskip 14.45377pt,\hskip 14.45377pt

\displaystyle\underbrace{\,c_{3}^{2}\lambda_{1}+c_{2}^{2}\lambda_{2}+c_{1}^{2}\lambda_{3}=0\,}_{\text{$W(v,e_{4},e_{4},v)=0$}}.

Proof.

All of these follow from expanding

W(T,e_{j},e_{k},T)=\sum_{i,l}c_{i}c_{l}W_{ijkl}

and using (
31
) and (
38
).
∎

More important for us will be the “Lorentzian” version of these equations in Theorem
3
, but let us use Theorem
1
now to verify that nontrivial solutions do exist, and that, as we saw in (
35
), they are not, in general, unique:

Corollary 1
.

Assume that the Weyl tensor is nonzero in Theorem
1
. Then the vector
T=e_{1}+e_{2}+e_{3}+e_{4}
satisfies
W(T,\cdot,\cdot,T)=0
if and only if
\lambda_{1}=-\mu_{1}-2\mu_{2}
and
\lambda_{2}=2\mu_{1}+\mu_{2}
. If so, then so do the vectors
-e_{1}-e_{2}+e_{3}+e_{4},-e_{1}+e_{2}-e_{3}+e_{4}
, and
-e_{1}+e_{2}+e_{3}-e_{4}
. On the other hand, if
\mu_{2}=\mu_{3}
and
\lambda_{2}=\lambda_{3}\neq 0
, then the only solution is
T=0
.

Proof.

With
c_{1}=c_{2}=c_{3}=c_{4}=1
, and recalling (
39
), the proofs of the first two statements follow easily from inspection of the equations above. Regarding the final statement, we must have
\lambda_{1}=-2\lambda_{2}\neq 0
; by the second equation, we have
c_{1}=0
or
c_{2}=0
. On the other hand, the second-to-last equation yields
c_{3}=0
or
c_{4}=0
. Any of these pairings, when inserted into the remaining equations, will yield

.
∎

In Corollary
1
, if
\lambda_{2}\neq\lambda_{3}
then the last statement no longer holds, in that nontrivial solutions may exist; indeed, this was the case in (
35
).

4.
Introducing a Lorentzian metric

What Theorem
1
does not address is what further

, if any, lies behind

. As we now show, there is further geometry, if one is prepared to change the signature of
g
, to that of

metric. To see how, let us start with the observation that, in the basis (
3
) with

, and recalling (

(40)

\displaystyle W(T,\cdot,\cdot,T)=0\iff\hat{W}=\begin{bmatrix}O&B\\
B&O\end{bmatrix}\cdot

As we now show, (

is also trace-free with respect to

Proposition 1
.

Let
(M,g)
be an oriented Riemannian 4-manifold with Weyl tensor
W
and let

M
. With respect to the Lorentzian metric

for all

commutes with the Hodge star

of
g_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}\iff W(T,\cdot,\cdot,T)=0
. If so, then
\hat{W}^{\scalebox{0.55}{$g_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}$}}
is a complex-linear map on the three-dimensional complex vector space
\Lambda_{\scalebox{0.5}{\emph{$\mathbb{C}$}}}^{2}
defined via

\xi\in\Lambda^{2}
.

Before beginning the proof, note that
\hat{W}^{\scalebox{0.55}{$g_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}$}}

W

\langle{\,},{}\rangle_{\!g_{\scalebox{0.3}{\emph{L}}}}

\langle{\,},{}\rangle_{\!\scalebox{0.7}{\emph{g}}}

- and

(42)

\displaystyle\hskip-14.45377pt\hat{W}^{\scalebox{0.55}{$g_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}$}}\!\!

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!\begin{bmatrix}-W_{1212}&-W_{1312}&-W_{1412}&-W_{3412}&-W_{4212}&-W_{2312}\\
-W_{1213}&-W_{1313}&-W_{1413}&-W_{3413}&-W_{4213}&-W_{2313}\\
-W_{1214}&-W_{1314}&-W_{1414}&-W_{3414}&-W_{4214}&-W_{2314}\\

W_{1242}&W_{1342}&W_{1442}&W_{3442}&W_{4242}&W_{2342}\\
W_{1223}&W_{1323}&W_{1423}&W_{3423}&W_{4223}&W_{2323}\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!\begin{bmatrix}-A&-B\\
B&A\end{bmatrix}\cdot

What accounts for this difference with (

As mentioned above, if the (trace-free) Weyl tensor

\displaystyle W_{1213}=-1/\sqrt{2}\hskip 7.22743pt,\hskip 7.22743ptW_{1323}=W_{1442}=W_{3442}=1/\sqrt{2}.

Appendix: Details of the proof of Theorem
2

The key ingredients in the proof of Theorem
2
are the following canonical bases associated to each Petrov Type:

Proposition 3
.

Let
\hat{W}^{\scalebox{0.55}{$g_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}$}}\colon\Lambda_{\scalebox{0.5}{\emph{$\mathbb{C}$}}}^{2}\longrightarrow\Lambda_{\scalebox{0.5}{\emph{$\mathbb{C}$}}}^{2}
be the complex-linear map of Definition
1
. Then at each
p\in M
, the following is true
:

1.

For Petrov Types I or D,
\hat{W}^{\scalebox{0.55}{$g_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}$}}
has a basis of orthogonal spacelike 2-planes.

2.

For Petrov Types II or N,
\hat{W}^{\scalebox{0.55}{$g_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}$}}
has one spacelike and one lightlike 2-plane as eigenvectors, orthogonal to each other.

3.

For Petrov Type III, the one linearly independent eigenvector of
\hat{W}^{\scalebox{0.55}{$g_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}$}}
is necessarily a lightlike 2-plane.

Proof.

(This proof is identical
[
Tho69
, Theorem, p. 3]
(see also
[
Tho69
, Remark (ii), p. 4]
), though we write it out in detail here since our operator
\hat{W}^{\scalebox{0.55}{$g_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}$}}
is not the Lorentzian curvature operator to be found therein, since our presentation differs from
[
Tho69
]
in that we omit the derivation of the Jordan forms (
54
), and finally, since we have separated Types D and N from I and II, respectively, for the sake of Theorem
2
below.) The key to finding the bases alleged is to work with the following complex scalar product on the three-dimensional complex vector space
\Lambda_{\scalebox{0.5}{\emph{$\mathbb{C}$}}}^{2}
:

{\bf g}_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}(\xi,\eta)\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}}=\langle{\xi},{\eta}\rangle_{\!g_{\scalebox{0.3}{\emph{L}}}}-i\langle{\xi},{*_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}\eta}\rangle_{\!g_{\scalebox{0.3}{\emph{L}}}}

Note that
\hat{W}^{\scalebox{0.55}{$g_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}$}}
is
{\bf g}_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}
-self-adjoint, because it is
\langle{\,},{}\rangle_{\!g_{\scalebox{0.3}{\emph{L}}}}
-self-adjoint and commutes with
*_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}
. Note also that
\xi\in\Lambda^{2}
corresponds to a nondegenerate 2-plane in
T_{p}M
if and only if
{\bf g}_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}(\xi,\xi)=\pm 1
, since

(60)

\displaystyle{\bf g}_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}(\xi,\xi)=\pm 1\iff\langle{\xi},{\xi}\rangle_{\!g_{\scalebox{0.3}{\emph{L}}}}=\pm 1~~\text{and}~~\langle{\xi},{*_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}\,\xi}\rangle_{\!g_{\scalebox{0.3}{\emph{L}}}}=0\iff\xi\in G_{\pm}(p).

(For proofs of these facts, consult
[
O’N95
, pp. 306 & 362]
.)

Petrov Type I or D at
p
: Let
\{\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\xi_{3}\}\subseteq\Lambda_{\scalebox{0.5}{\emph{$\mathbb{C}$}}}^{2}
be a basis of eigenvectors for
\hat{W}^{\scalebox{0.55}{$g_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}$}}
, with corresponding eigenvalues
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\lambda_{3}\in\mathbb{C}
. If
\lambda_{i}\neq\lambda_{j}
, then
{\bf g}_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}(\xi_{i},\xi_{j})=0
because
\hat{W}^{\scalebox{0.55}{$g_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}$}}
is
{\bf g}_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}
-self-adjoint. This immediately implies that if
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\lambda_{3}
are distinct and some
{\bf g}_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}(\xi_{j},\xi_{j})=0
, then that
\xi_{j}
will be
{\bf g}_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}
-orthogonal to
all
2-vectors in
\Lambda_{\scalebox{0.5}{\emph{$\mathbb{C}$}}}^{2}
— impossible as
{\bf g}_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}
is nondegenerate. Thus each
{\bf g}_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}(\xi_{j},\xi_{j})\neq 0
if
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\lambda_{3}
are distinct, If the latter is true, then we may choose
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3}\in\mathbb{C}
so that
{\bf g}_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}(\alpha_{j}\xi_{j},\alpha_{j}\xi_{j})=+1
; i.e., so that each eigenvector is a spacelike 2-plane, by (
60
). (Such complex scalar multiplication will neither change their status as eigenvectors of
\hat{W}^{\scalebox{0.55}{$g_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}$}}
nor change their eigenvalues.) This takes care of the case when
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\lambda_{3}
are distinct. Suppose now that two of them are equal, say
\lambda_{1}=\lambda_{2}\neq\lambda_{3}
(as we are still within the case of Petrov Type I, we still have a basis
\{\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\xi_{3}\}
of eigenvectors). Then
{\bf g}_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}(\xi_{3},\xi_{3})\neq 0
, by the same reasoning as above. Consider now the two-dimensional
\lambda_{1}
-eigenspace; it, too, must be
{\bf g}_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}
-nondegenerate, hence must contain a
{\bf g}_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}
-orthogonal basis. We thus have a
{\bf g}_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}
-orthogonal basis
\{\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\xi_{3}\}
of eigenvectors of
\hat{W}^{\scalebox{0.55}{$g_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}$}}
, no element of which is
{\bf g}_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}
-lightlike; hence, as before, each can be scaled to be a spacelike 2-plane. Finally, the case when all three eigenvalues are equal,
\lambda_{1}=\lambda_{2}=\lambda_{3}
, is trivial because then every 2-vector is an eigenvector of
\hat{W}^{\scalebox{0.55}{$g_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}$}}
.

p

(61)

\displaystyle{\bf g}_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}(\xi_{1},\xi_{1})={\bf g}_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}((\hat{W}^{\scalebox{0.55}{$g_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}$}}-\lambda_{1}I)\eta,\xi_{1})={\bf g}_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}(\eta,(\hat{W}^{\scalebox{0.55}{$g_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}$}}-\lambda_{1}I)\xi_{1})=0,

\xi_{1}

{\bf g}_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}
-lightlike. But this is the case if and only if

\langle{\xi_{1}},{\xi_{1}}\rangle_{\!g_{\scalebox{0.3}{\emph{L}}}}=\langle{\xi_{1}},{*_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}\,\xi_{1}}\rangle_{\!g_{\scalebox{0.3}{\emph{L}}}}=0

\xi_{1}

\lambda_{1}\neq\lambda_{2}

-self-adjointness of
\hat{W}^{\scalebox{0.55}{$g_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}$}}

\lambda_{1}=\lambda_{2}
, then

{\bf g}_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})={\bf g}_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}((\hat{W}^{\scalebox{0.55}{$g_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}$}}-\lambda_{1}I)\eta,\xi_{2})={\bf g}_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}(\eta,(\hat{W}^{\scalebox{0.55}{$g_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}$}}-\lambda_{1}I)\xi_{2})=0.

Now suppose
{\bf g}_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}(\xi_{2},\xi_{2})=0
also, so that
\xi_{1},\xi_{2}
are
{\bf g}_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}
-orthogonal and lightlike. Because
{\bf g}_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}
is nondegenerate and
\{\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\eta\}
is a basis, each
{\bf g}_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}(\xi_{j},\eta)\neq 0
.
And yet, because any 2-vector
\beta\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}}=\alpha_{1}\xi_{1}+\alpha_{2}\xi_{2}
will be
{\bf g}_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}
-lightlike and orthogonal to
\xi_{1},\xi_{2}
, we may choose
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\in\mathbb{C}
so that
{\bf g}_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}(\beta,\eta)=0
, contradicting the nondegeneracy of
{\bf g}_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}
. Therefore we must have
{\bf g}_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}(\xi_{2},\xi_{2})\neq 0
, in which case we may scale it to satisfy
{\bf g}_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}(\xi_{2},\xi_{2})=+1
.

Petrov Type III at
p
: Let
\xi_{1}\in\Lambda_{\scalebox{0.5}{\emph{$\mathbb{C}$}}}^{2}
denote
\hat{W}^{\scalebox{0.55}{$g_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}$}}
’s lone linearly independent eigenvector, with eigenvalue
\lambda_{1}
. Once again, there is a 2-vector
\eta
satisfying
(\hat{W}^{\scalebox{0.55}{$g_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}$}}-\lambda_{1}I)\eta=\xi_{1}
, so that, via (
61
),
\xi_{1}
is necessarily a lightlike 2-plane.
∎

To connect these bases with
\text{sec}_{\scalebox{0.5}{\emph{\emph{$\hat{W}^{\scalebox{0.55}{$g_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}$}}$}}}}
, we will need to characterize its critical points in terms of
\hat{W}^{\scalebox{0.55}{$g_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}$}}
:

Proposition 4
.

Let
\text{sec}_{\scalebox{0.5}{\emph{\emph{$\hat{W}^{\scalebox{0.55}{$g_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}$}}$}}}}
be given by (
53
) of Definition
2
. Then at any
p\in M
, a 2-plane
P\in G_{\pm}(p)
is a critical point of
\text{sec}_{\scalebox{0.5}{\emph{\emph{$\hat{W}^{\scalebox{0.55}{$g_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}$}}$}}}}
if and only if

\emph{\hat{W}^{\scalebox{0.55}{$g_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}$}}}P=aP+b(*_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}P)

for some
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
.

Proof.

This proof follows exactly as in
[
Tho69
, Lemma, p. 5]
, except that we are working with
\hat{W}^{\scalebox{0.55}{$g_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}$}}
in place of the Lorentzian curvature operator; see also
[
Aaz23
, Proposition 1]
for a more general case.
∎

In any case, we now have the ingredients needed to prove Theorem
2
:

Theorem

(Classification of Petrov Types)
.

Assume the hypotheses of Definition
1
. Then at each
p\in M
,
g
has

1.

Petrov Type I
\!\!\iff\!\!\text{sec}_{\scalebox{0.5}{\emph{\emph{$\hat{W}^{\scalebox{0.55}{$g_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}$}}$}}}}
has
n=3
spacelike critical points.

2.

Petrov Types D or N
\!\!\iff\!\!\text{sec}_{\scalebox{0.5}{\emph{\emph{$\hat{W}^{\scalebox{0.55}{$g_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}$}}$}}}}
has
n=\infty
spacelike critical points.

3.

Petrov Type II
\!\!\iff\!\!\text{sec}_{\scalebox{0.5}{\emph{\emph{$\hat{W}^{\scalebox{0.55}{$g_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}$}}$}}}}
has
n=1
spacelike critical point.

4.

Petrov Type III
\!\!\iff\!\!\text{sec}_{\scalebox{0.5}{\emph{\emph{$\hat{W}^{\scalebox{0.55}{$g_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}$}}$}}}}
has
n=0
spacelike critical points.

Proof.

(Our proof differs from
[
Tho69
, Theorem, p. 5]
in step 2, as we discuss below; also, note once again that we are working with
\hat{W}^{\scalebox{0.55}{$g_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}$}}
, not the Lorentzian curvature operator as in
[
Tho69
]
.) By Proposition
1
,
\hat{W}^{\scalebox{0.55}{$g_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}$}}
is a complex-linear map on
\Lambda_{\scalebox{0.5}{\emph{$\mathbb{C}$}}}^{2}
. By Proposition
4
, any
P\in G_{\pm}(p)
will be a critical point of
\text{sec}_{\scalebox{0.5}{\emph{\emph{$\hat{W}^{\scalebox{0.55}{$g_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}$}}$}}}}
if and only if it is a complex eigenvector of
\hat{W}^{\scalebox{0.55}{$g_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}$}}
:

\hat{W}^{\scalebox{0.55}{$g_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}$}}P=(a+ib)P=aP+b*_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}\!P.

In particular, if
\text{sec}_{\scalebox{0.5}{\emph{\emph{$\hat{W}^{\scalebox{0.55}{$g_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}$}}$}}}}
has a spacelike critical point
P
— i.e.,
P\in G_{+}(p)
— then it has a timelike critical point, namely,
iP=*_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}P\in G_{-}(p)
, since the latter is, of course, an eigenvector of
\hat{W}^{\scalebox{0.55}{$g_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}$}}
if
P
was. Thus if
\text{sec}_{\scalebox{0.5}{\emph{\emph{$\hat{W}^{\scalebox{0.55}{$g_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}$}}$}}}}
has, say, no spacelike critical points (hence no timelike critical points, either), then no nondegenerate 2-planes can be eigenvectors of
\hat{W}^{\scalebox{0.55}{$g_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}$}}
; i.e.,
\hat{W}^{\scalebox{0.55}{$g_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}$}}
can only have
{\bf g}_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}
-lightlike eigenvectors. By Proposition
3
, only Petrov Type III satisfies this criterion. Likewise, if
\text{sec}_{\scalebox{0.5}{\emph{\emph{$\hat{W}^{\scalebox{0.55}{$g_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}$}}$}}}}
has precisely one spacelike critical point (hence precisely one timelike critical point), then
\hat{W}^{\scalebox{0.55}{$g_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}$}}
can only have one eigenvector from
G_{+}(p)\subseteq\Lambda_{\scalebox{0.5}{\emph{$\mathbb{C}$}}}^{2}
. By Proposition
3
, only Petrov Type II satisfies this criterion. Similarly, if
\text{sec}_{\scalebox{0.5}{\emph{\emph{$\hat{W}^{\scalebox{0.55}{$g_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}$}}$}}}}
has three spacelike critical points, then
\hat{W}^{\scalebox{0.55}{$g_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}$}}
must have three spacelike 2-planes as eigenvectors. By Proposition
3
, only Petrov Type I satisfies this criterion. Finally, suppose that two eigenvalues of
\hat{W}^{\scalebox{0.55}{$g_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}$}}
are equal,
\lambda_{2}=\lambda_{3}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}}=\lambda
, hence Types D or N. In either case, denote the two
{\bf g}_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}
-orthogonal 2-plane eigenvectors, guaranteed by Proposition
3
, by
x\wedge y,v\wedge z
. If Type D, then they are spacelike, and any linear combination
\xi\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}}=a(x\wedge y)+b(v\wedge z)
of them with
a^{2}+b^{2}=1
will yield another spacelike 2-plane:
{\bf g}_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}(\xi,\xi)=+1
(recall (
60
)). Hence there will be infinitely many complex eigenvectors of
\hat{W}^{\scalebox{0.55}{$g_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}$}}
in
G_{+}(p)
, hence infinitely many critical points of
\text{sec}_{\scalebox{0.5}{\emph{\emph{$\hat{W}^{\scalebox{0.55}{$g_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}$}}$}}}}
, by Proposition
4
. But, as seems not to have been noted by
[
Tho69
]
, the same is true for Type N: Although one of these 2-plane eigenvectors, say,
v\wedge z
, is now
{\bf g}_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}
-lightlike, nevertheless
{\bf g}_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}(\xi,\xi)=+1
for all
\xi\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}}=x\wedge y+b(v\wedge z)
with
b\in\mathbb{R}
. Thus, the case of infinitely many critical points determines the Petrov Type only up to D or N. (This would therefore also be the case for the curvature operator of an Einstein metric
g_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}
in the proof of
[
Tho69
]
.)
∎

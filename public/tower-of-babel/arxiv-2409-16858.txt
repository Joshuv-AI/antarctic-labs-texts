Derived hyperquot schemes
Sergej Monavari, Emanuele Pavia, Andrea T. Ricolfi

Abstract
. We define a derived enhancement of the hyperquot scheme (also known as nested Quot scheme ), which classically parametrises flags of quotients of a perfect coherent sheaf on a projective scheme. We prove it is representable by a derived scheme, and we compute its global tangent complex. As an application, we provide a natural obstruction theory on the classical hyperquot scheme. The latter recovers the virtual fundamental class recently constructed by the first and third author in the context of the enumerative geometry of hyperquot schemes on smooth projective curves.

1.3.
Amplifications

We now briefly comment on our working assumptions. Projectivity of
X
is used in a twofold way: first of all, to ensure the representability of the (classical) hyperquot functor, and secondly, properness is needed to ensure that the derived stack
\DPerf(X)
of perfect complexes on
X
is locally geometric (cf.
Theorem

3.1
and
Remark

3.2
(1)
). Perfectness of
{\mathcal{E}}
means that
{\mathcal{E}}
, when viewed as an object of
\derived_{\coh}^{\bounded}(X)
, lies in the image of the canonical functor
\Perf(X)\rightarrow\derived_{\coh}^{\bounded}(X)
, which ensures that
{\mathcal{E}}
defines a
\Bbbk
-point of
\DPerf(X)
. Note that, if
X
is smooth, any
{\mathcal{E}}\in\Coh(X)
is automatically perfect, as the functor
\Perf(X)\rightarrow\derived_{\coh}^{\bounded}(X)
is, in this case, an exact equivalence of triangulated categories
[
67
,
Tag 0FDC
]
.

In the remainder of this section we discuss what adjustments are needed in order to extend our results to the case where
X
is only quasiprojective or over a general base not necessarily of characteristic 0.

Let

a coherent sheaf, and

carries over to the quasiprojective case, just as in Adhikari’s paper

1.3.2.
Arbitrary base

One can naturally generalise our constructions over an arbitrary base, possibly of positive characteristic. In this setting, commutative differential graded algebras
do not
capture in general the correct homotopy theory of commutative rings. However, one can still define a theory of derived algebraic geometry over a base ring
A
(possibly of positive characteristic) by replacing the
{\scriptstyle\infty}
-category of commutative differential graded
A
-algebras with the
{\scriptstyle\infty}
-category
\mathsf{Alg}_{{\mdmathbb{E}}_{{\scriptstyle\infty}}}(\Mod_{HA})
of
{\mdmathbb{E}}_{{\scriptstyle\infty}}

-ring spectra
(see e.g.
[
44
, Ch. 7]
). This allows one to define a derived moduli functor
\RQuot^{[l]}_{X}({\mathcal{E}})
for any scheme

defined over an arbitrary
{\mdmathbb{E}}_{{\scriptstyle\infty}}
-ring and for any perfect sheaf
{\mathcal{E}}
over
X
.

Since the proof of
Theorem

A
relies on the representability of the classical hyperquot scheme, which holds under very mild assumptions of the base ring
[
34
]
, and on the local geometricity of the derived stack

, which for

2.1.
Affine derived schemes

Let

{{\scriptstyle\infty}}
-category of commutative differential graded

\Bbbk

Inside

-category

\Bbbk

is, by definition, the
{{\scriptstyle\infty}}
-category of

, and we denote the object corresponding to a commutative differential graded
\Bbbk

To any derived affine scheme

The operation

is a functor

. We say that a derived affine scheme
\RSpec A
is

if
\RSpec A\simeq t_{0}\RSpec A
, where we view

as an object of

Definition 2.1

(
[

, Sec.
2.2.2
]
)
.

Let

be a morphism of derived affine schemes.

(1)

The morphism

if the morphism

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{H}^{i}(A)\otimes_{\mathrm{H}^{0}(A)}\mathrm{H}^{0}(B)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{H}^{i}(B)}

is an isomorphism for every

.

(2)

The morphism

,

if it is flat and the morphism

(3)

The morphism

(4)

The morphism

3.3.
Geometricity results

The goal of this subsection is to prove that, given a derived stack

Following

Theorem 3.1

(

Let

Remark 3.2

3.4.3.

The goal of this section is to prove that

is a derived enhancement of the classical hyperquot scheme
\Quot_{X}^{[l]}({\mathcal{E}})
and, in particular, that it is represented by a derived scheme.

The next result follows from a generalisation of the strategy employed by Adhikari’s in
[

for the case

Theorem 3.10
.

There is an equivalence of higher stacks

In particular,
t_{0}(\RQuot_{X}^{[l]}({\mathcal{E}}))
is a classical scheme.

Proof.

We split the proof in three steps.

Step I.
There is a natural map

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\alpha\colon\Quot^{[l]}_{X}({\mathcal{E}})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces t_{0}\RQuot^{[l]}_{X}({\mathcal{E}}).}

Indeed, for any commutative algebra
A
, we have a
natural map of spaces

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Quot^{[l]}_{X}({\mathcal{E}})(A)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces t_{0}\RQuot^{[l]}_{X}({\mathcal{E}})(A),}

since a sequence of
surjective morphisms of discrete quasicoherent sheaves on

which are flat over
\Spec A

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{E}}_{A}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{E}}_{1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cdots}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{E}}_{l}}

is in particular a sequence of surjective morphisms of quasicoherent sheaves on
X_{A}
which are flat over
\Spec A
in the derived sense.

Step II.

We claim that for any commutative algebra

, the morphism
\alpha
induces a bijection on the sets of connected components of the
A
-valued points.

For injectivity, assume that we are given two elements
\mathscr{E},\mathscr{F}\in\Quot^{[l]}_{X}({\mathcal{E}})(A)
which are equivalent in

. This means that there exists a quasi-isomorphism

, commutative up to coherent homotopy, which induces an isomorphism of complexes after applying

For surjectivity, notice that an element

is flat over

, and therefore discrete, which implies that it is the image of

We claim that for any commutative algebra

has trivial higher homotopies. In other words, we need to prove that for all

, for all

. Set

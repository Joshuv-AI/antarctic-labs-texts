R R -triviality for adjoint classical groups of type C C
M. Archita

Abstract
. For a central simple algebra with a symplectic involution ( A , σ ) (A,\sigma) over a field of characteristic different from 2 2 , we show that its group of projective similitudes 𝐏𝐒𝐢𝐦 ⁡ ( A , σ ) {\bf PSim}(A,\sigma) is R R -trivial in two new cases. Keywords: Classical adjoint algebraic group, stably rational, R R -trivial, quadratic form, hyperbolic, Pfister form, symplectic involution, quaternion algebra Classification (MSC 2020): 11E04, 11E57, 11E81, 14E08, 20G15



1.
Introduction

Let
K
be a field of characteristic different from
2
.
By Weil’s classification
[
13
]
, any absolutely simple adjoint algebraic group of type
C_{n}
over
K
is isomorphic
to the algebraic group of projective similitudes
{\bf PSim}(A,\sigma)
for a central simple algebra
A
of degree
2n
over
K
endowed with a symplectic involution
\sigma
. In
[
9
, §2]
, it is shown that
{\bf PSim}(A,\sigma)
is stably rational, and in particular
R
-trivial, when
n
is odd.

In this article we study the
R
-triviality of
{\bf PSim}(A,\sigma)
when
n
is even. In
[
9
, §2]
, it is shown that the such groups are rational when
n=2
.
When
n=4
and
\operatorname{\mathsf{ind}}(A)\leqslant 2
we will show that
{\bf PSim}(A,\sigma)
is
R
-trivial (
3.2
). On the other hand, Berhuy, Monsurrò and Tignol
[
4
, Cor. 9]
gave an example with
n=4
and
\operatorname{\mathsf{ind}}(A)=4
where
{\bf PSim}(A,\sigma)
is not
R
-trivial; see
3.3
.
When
n=6
, we establish
R
-triviality of
{\bf PSim}(A,\sigma)
in the case where
\sigma
has trivial discriminant (
3.7
).

2.
Preliminaries

For a finite field extension
L/K
, we write

\mathsf{N}_{L/K}:L\to K
for the norm map, and we abbreviate

\mathsf{N}_{L/K}^{\ast}=\mathsf{N}_{L/K}({L}^{\times})\,.

For standard terminology and basic facts from quadratic forrm theory, we refer to
[
8
]
. Let
\varphi=(V,B)
be a quadratic form over
K
, where
V
is a finite-dimensional
K
-vector space and
B
a symmetric bilinear form
B:V\times V\rightarrow K
.
The
dimension of
\varphi
is the dimension of the vector space
V
over
K
.
If
B
is non-degenerate then
\varphi
is called
regular
. In this article, by a
quadratic form
we mean a regular quadratic form. A quadratic form of dimension
n
is equivalent to a diagonal form
a_{1}X_{1}^{2}+a_{2}X_{2}^{2}+\cdots+a_{n}X_{n}^{2}
,
with
a_{1},\dots,a_{n}\in{K}^{\times}
, which is denoted by
\langle a_{1},\dots,a_{n}\rangle
. We denote by
\mathsf{disc}(\varphi)
the
discriminant of
\varphi
, given as the class
(-1)^{\binom{n}{2}}a_{1}\dots a_{n}{K}^{\times 2}
in the square-class group
{K}^{\times}/{K}^{\times 2}
for an arbitrary diagonalisation
\langle a_{1},\dots,a_{n}\rangle
of
\varphi
.
We denote by
\varphi_{\mathsf{an}}
the anisotropic part and by
\mathsf{i}(\varphi)
the Witt index of
\varphi
.
They are determined by having that
\varphi_{\mathsf{an}}
is anisotropic and

\varphi\simeq\varphi_{\mathsf{an}}\perp\mathsf{i}(\varphi)\times\operatorname{\mathbb{H}}
, where
\operatorname{\mathbb{H}}
denotes the hyperbolic plane over
K
.
We call
\varphi

isotropic
if
\mathsf{i}(\varphi)>0
, and
anisotropic
otherwise.
We call
\varphi

hyperbolic
if
\varphi_{\mathsf{an}}
is trivial, i.e. if
\varphi\simeq i\times\operatorname{\mathbb{H}}
for some
i\in\mathbb{N}
.
We denote
\mathsf{G}(\varphi)=\{a\in{K}^{\times}\mid a\varphi\simeq\varphi\}
, which is a subgroup of
{K}^{\times}
.
For
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
a_{1},\dots,a_{n}\in{K}^{\times}
, we denote by
\langle\!\langle a_{1},\dots,a_{n}\rangle\!\rangle
the
n
-fold Pfister form
\langle 1,-a_{1}\rangle\otimes\dots\otimes\langle 1,-a_{n}\rangle
over
K
.

Let
A
be a
K
-algebra.
We call
A
a
division algebra
if every nonzero element of
A
is invertible.
The center of
A
is denoted by
Z(A)
. We call
A
a
central simple
K
-algebra
if
Z(A)=K
,
A
is finite-dimensional over
K
and the only two sided ideals of
A
are
0
and
A
. Note that,
\mathsf{dim}_{K}(A)=n^{2},
for some positive integer
n
, and we set
\mathsf{deg}(A)=n
. By Wedderburn’s theorem,
A\simeq M_{r}(D)
, for some
r\in\mathbb{N}
and where
D
is a finite-dimensional central division
K
-algebra (unique up to
K
-isomorphism). When
D=K
, we say
A
is a
split
. We call the degree of
D
as a
K
-algebra the
index of

A
and it is denoted by
\operatorname{\mathsf{ind}}(A)
.

A
K
-involution
(or
involution of the first kind
) on
A
is a
K
-linear map
\sigma:A\rightarrow A
such that
\sigma(xy)=\sigma(y)\sigma(x)
for every
x,y\in A
and
\sigma^{2}=\operatorname{\mathsf{id}}_{A}
.
We call a pair
(A,\sigma)
of a central simple
K
-algebra
A
with a
K
-involution
\sigma
a
K
-algebra with involution
.

Involutions of the first kind are distinguished into two
types
,
orthogonal
and
symplectic
, cf.
[
7
, §2]
. Our focus here lies on symplectic involutions. They are characterized by the fact that they become adjoint to an alternating bilinear form after a field extension.

Consider a quadratic form
\varphi=(V,B)
. Consider the split central simple
K
-algebra

\operatorname{\mathsf{End}}_{K}(V)
and
B:V\times V\rightarrow K
is a symmetric bilinear form. Let
\operatorname{\mathsf{ad}}_{B}:\operatorname{\mathsf{End}}_{K}(V)\rightarrow\operatorname{\mathsf{End}}_{K}(V)
denote the involution determined by the formula

B(f(u),v)=B(u,\operatorname{\mathsf{ad}}_{B}(f)(v))

for all
u,v\in V
and
f\in\operatorname{\mathsf{End}}_{K}(V)
.
We call
\operatorname{\mathsf{ad}}_{B}
the
adjoint involution of
\varphi
. Furthermore, we put
\operatorname{\mathsf{Ad}}(\varphi)=(\operatorname{\mathsf{End}}_{K}(V),\operatorname{\mathsf{ad}}_{B})
and call it the
adjoint algebra with involution of
\varphi
.

Let
(A,\sigma)
be an
K
-algebra with involution. An element
a\in{A}^{\times}
is said to be a

similitude of
\sigma
if
\sigma(a)a\in{K}^{\times}
.
The similitudes of
(A,\sigma)
form a group, which is denoted by

{\bf\mathsf{Sim}}(A,\sigma)
.
In
{K}^{\times}
, we consider the subgroup

\mathsf{G}(A,\sigma)=\{\sigma(x)x\mid x\in{A}^{\times}\}\cap{K}^{\times}\,.

The elements of this group are called the
multipliers of similitude of
(A,\sigma)
.
We set
{\bf\mathsf{PSim}}(A,\sigma)={\bf\mathsf{Sim}}(A,\sigma)/{K}^{\times}
and call its elements
projective similitudes of
(A,\sigma)
.
We refer to
[
7
, §12]
for a discussion of similitudes and their multipliers.

For a field extension
K^{\prime}/K
we denote by
(A_{K^{\prime}},\sigma_{K^{\prime}})=(A\otimes_{K}K^{\prime},\sigma\otimes\operatorname{\mathsf{id}}_{K^{\prime}})
the algebra with involution
(A,\sigma)
when considered over the field
K^{\prime}
.
Letting
{\bf PSim}(A,\sigma)(K^{\prime})={\bf\mathsf{PSim}}(A_{K^{\prime}},\sigma_{K^{\prime}})
for
K^{\prime}/K
defines an algebraic group
{\bf PSim}(A,\sigma)
over
K
, now as a
K
-variety whose
K
-rational points are given by
{\bf\mathsf{PSim}}(A,\sigma)
.

Let
(A,\sigma)
be
K
-algebra with involution.
We call
\sigma

hyperbolic
if there exists an element
e\in A
such that
e^{2}=e
and
\sigma(e)=1-e
.

We write
\mathsf{Hyp}(A,\sigma)
for the subgroup of
{K}^{\times}
generated by the sets
\mathsf{N}_{L/K}^{\ast}
where
L/K
ranges over all finite field extensions such that
\sigma_{L}
hyperbolic.

The following theorem due to
Merkurjev characterises the
group of

R
-equivalence classes of

{\bf PSim}(A,\sigma)
in terms of the multipliers
of similitudes of
(A,\sigma)
.

Theorem 2.1

(Merkurjev)
.

We have
{K}^{\times 2}\cdot\mathsf{Hyp}(A,\sigma)\subseteq\mathsf{G}(A,\sigma)
and

{\bf PSim}(A,\sigma)(K)/R\simeq\mathsf{G}(A,\sigma)/{K}^{\times 2}\cdot\mathsf{Hyp}(A,\sigma).

In particular, the group
{\bf PSim}(A,\sigma)
is
R
-trivial if and only if, for every field extension
K^{\prime}/K
, one has
\mathsf{G}(A_{K^{\prime}},\sigma_{K^{\prime}})={K^{\prime}}^{\times 2}\cdot\mathsf{Hyp}(A_{K^{\prime}},\sigma_{K^{\prime}})
.

Proof.

See
[
9
, Theorem
1
]
.
∎

3.
Main Results

A
K
-quaternion algebra
is a central simple
K
-algebra of degree
2
. Consider a
K
-quaternion algebra
Q
. Denote the
canonical involution
on
Q
by
\operatorname{\mathsf{can}}_{Q}
. It is a
K
-involution of symplectic type. We denote the norm form of
Q
by
\mathsf{N}_{Q}
and recall that
\mathsf{N}_{Q}
is a
4
-dimensional quadratic form over
K
.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
Q
be a
K
-quaternion algebra and
\varphi
a quadratic form over
K
. Let
\pi=\mathsf{N}_{Q}
.
Let
(A,\sigma)=\operatorname{\mathsf{Ad}}(\varphi)\otimes(Q,\operatorname{\mathsf{can}}_{Q})
. Then
\mathsf{G}(A,\sigma)=\mathsf{G}(\varphi\otimes\pi)
and
\mathsf{Hyp}(A,\sigma)=\mathsf{Hyp}(\varphi\otimes\pi)
. Furthermore, for any field extension
K^{\prime}/K
, we have

{\bf PSim}(A,\sigma)(K^{\prime})/R\simeq{\bf PSim}(\varphi\otimes\pi)(K^{\prime})/R\,.

Proof.

By
[
5
, Theorem 4.6]
, for any quadratic form
\rho
over
K
we have that
\operatorname{\mathsf{Ad}}(\rho)\otimes(Q,\operatorname{\mathsf{can}}_{Q})
is hyperbolic if and only if
\rho\otimes\pi
hyperbolic. Since by
[
5
, Proposition 3.2]
,
\operatorname{\mathsf{Ad}}(\varphi\otimes\pi)\simeq\operatorname{\mathsf{Ad}}(\varphi)\otimes\operatorname{\mathsf{Ad}}(\pi)
holds, we have, for any
\lambda\in{K}^{\times}
that
\operatorname{\mathsf{Ad}}(\langle\!\langle\lambda\rangle\!\rangle)\otimes(A,\sigma)\simeq\operatorname{\mathsf{Ad}}(\langle\!\langle\lambda\rangle\!\rangle\otimes\varphi)\otimes(Q,\operatorname{\mathsf{can}}_{Q})
, whereby
\operatorname{\mathsf{Ad}}(\langle\!\langle\lambda\rangle\!\rangle)\otimes(A,\sigma)
is hyperbolic if and only if
\langle\!\langle\lambda\rangle\!\rangle\otimes\varphi\otimes\pi
is hyperbolic. Thus,
\mathsf{G}(A,\sigma)=\mathsf{G}(\varphi\otimes\pi)
.

Note that, for any finite field extension
L/K
, we have
(\varphi\otimes\pi)_{L}
is hyperbolic if and only if
\sigma_{L}
is hyperbolic. Hence
\mathsf{Hyp}(A_{L},\sigma_{L})
and
\mathsf{Hyp}((\varphi\otimes\pi)_{L})
are generated by the subgroups
\mathsf{N}_{L/K}^{*}
for the same extensions
L/K
. Thus, we have
\mathsf{Hyp}(A,\sigma)=\mathsf{Hyp}(\varphi\otimes\pi)
.

Hence for any field extension
K^{\prime}/K
, we have
\mathsf{G}(A_{K^{\prime}},\sigma_{K^{\prime}})=\mathsf{G}((\varphi\otimes\pi)_{K^{\prime}})
and
\mathsf{Hyp}(A_{K^{\prime}},\sigma_{K^{\prime}})=\mathsf{Hyp}((\varphi\otimes\pi)_{K^{\prime}})
. Thus by
2.1
, we obtain that
{\bf PSim}(A,\sigma)(K^{\prime})/R\simeq{\bf PSim}(\varphi\otimes\pi)(K^{\prime})/R\,.

∎

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
(A,\sigma)
be
K
-algebra with symplectic involution of degree
8
. Assume that
\operatorname{\mathsf{ind}}(A)\leqslant 2
. Then
{\bf PSim}(A,\sigma)
is
R
-trivial.

Proof.

As
(A,\sigma)
is an algebra with symplectic involution with
\operatorname{\mathsf{ind}}(A)\leqslant 2
, by
[
5
, Prop. 3.4]
we have
(A,\sigma)\simeq\operatorname{\mathsf{Ad}}(\varphi)\otimes(Q,\operatorname{\mathsf{can}}_{Q})
for some quadratic form
\varphi
over
K
and a quaternion algebra
Q
over
K
. Write
Q=(a,b)
with
a,b\in{K}^{\times}
.
Its norm form is
\langle\!\langle a,b\rangle\!\rangle
.
Let
a_{1},\dots,a_{4}\in{K}^{\times}
be such that
\varphi\simeq\langle a_{1},a_{2},a_{3},a_{4}\rangle
.
Let
\psi=\langle\!\langle a,b\rangle\!\rangle\otimes\varphi
. Now observe that
\langle a_{1},a_{2},a_{3},a_{4}\rangle
is Witt equivalent to
\langle a_{1},a_{2},a_{3},a_{4}\rangle\perp\langle a_{1}a_{2}a_{3},-a_{1}a_{2}a_{3}\rangle
, which is equal to
a_{1}\langle\!\langle-a_{1}a_{3},-a_{1}a_{2}\rangle\!\rangle\perp a_{4}\langle\!\langle a_{1}a_{2}a_{3}a_{4}\rangle\!\rangle
. It follows that
\psi
is Witt equivalent to a sum of a scaled
4
-fold and a scaled
3
-fold Pfister form. By
[
1
, Theorem 3.5]
we obtain that
{\bf PSim}(\psi)
is
R
-trivial. Thus by
3.1
, we conclude that
{\bf PSim}(A,\sigma)
is
R
-trivial.
∎

A construction from
[
4
]
leads to examples of
K
-algebras with symplectic involution
(A,\sigma)
where
\mathsf{deg}A=8
,
\operatorname{\mathsf{ind}}A=4
and
{\bf PSim}(A,\sigma)
is not
R
-trivial.

Example 3.3
.

Let
K=K_{0}(a,b)
, the rational function field in two variables
a,b
over a subfield
K_{0}
.
Consider
(B,\tau)
be a central simple
K_{0}
-algebra of degree
4
and
\operatorname{\mathsf{ind}}(B)=2
with an orthogonal involution
\tau
of nontrivial discriminant. Let
Q=(a,b)
and let
\operatorname{\mathsf{can}}_{Q}
be its canonical involution.

Set
A=B\otimes Q
and
\sigma=\tau\otimes\operatorname{\mathsf{can}}_{Q}
.
Then
\mathsf{deg}(A)=8
,
\operatorname{\mathsf{ind}}(A)=4
and
\sigma
is a symplectic involution on
A
, by
[
7
, Prop. 2.23]
.
By
[
4
, Cor. 9]
, in this case
{\bf PSim}(A,\sigma)(K)/R\neq\{1\}
.

Next we consider symplectic involutions on algebras of degree
12
with some conditions on cohomological invariants. Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
We denote by
\mathsf{I}^{n}K
the
n
th power of the fundamental ideal
\mathsf{I}K
of the Witt ring of
K
.
We write
\varphi\in\mathsf{I}^{n}K
if the Witt equivalence class of
\varphi
lies in
\mathsf{I}^{n}K
.
Note that
\varphi\in\mathsf{I}^{2}K
if and only if
\varphi
has even dimension and trivial discriminant.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
\pi
be a Pfister form and
\psi
be an even-dimensional quadratic form over
K
. Let
\varphi=\pi\otimes\psi
. Then, for every field extension
L/K
, there exists a quadratic form
\theta
over
L
such that
\mathsf{dim}\theta\equiv\mathsf{dim}\psi\,\mathsf{mod}\,2
and
(\varphi_{L})_{\mathsf{an}}=\pi_{L}\otimes\theta
.

Proof.

See
[
2
, Prop. 3.4]
.
∎

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
\varphi
be a quadratic form over
K
and
a\in\mathsf{G}(\varphi)
.
Then
\varphi
is hyperbolic or there exists a quadratic field extension
L/K
with
\mathsf{i}(\varphi_{L})>\mathsf{i}(\varphi)
and
a\in\mathsf{N}_{L/K}^{\ast}
.

Proof.

See
[
1
, Lemma 3.1]
.
∎

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
\pi
a
2
-fold Pfister form. Let
\psi
be a
6
-dimension quadratic form and
\varphi=\pi\otimes\psi
in
\mathsf{I}^{4}K
over
K
.
Then, for every
c\in\mathsf{G}(\varphi)
, there exists a field extension
M/K
which is trivial, quadratic or biquadratic such that
\varphi_{M}
is hyperbolic and
c\in{K}^{\times 2}\cdot\mathsf{N}_{M/K}^{\ast}
. In particular,
\mathsf{G}(\varphi)={K}^{\times 2}\cdot\mathsf{Hyp}(\varphi)
.

Proof.

Consider
c\in\mathsf{G}(\varphi)
.
Then
\langle\!\langle c\rangle\!\rangle\otimes\varphi
is isotropic.
If
\varphi
is hyperbolic, then choose
M=K
.

Assume now that
\varphi
is not hyperbolic.
Then, by
3.5
, there exists a quadratic field extension
L/K
such that
\mathsf{i}(\varphi_{L})>\mathsf{i}(\varphi)
and
c\in\mathsf{N}_{L/K}^{\ast}
.
Let
d\in{K}^{\times}\smallsetminus{K}^{\times 2}
be such that
L=K(\sqrt{d})
.
If
\varphi_{L}
is hyperbolic, then take
M=L
. Assume now that
\varphi_{L}
is not hyperbolic.
By
[
6
, Cor. 22.12]
, we can write
\varphi_{\mathsf{an}}\simeq\langle\!\langle d\rangle\!\rangle\otimes\vartheta\perp\psi
for two quadratic forms
\vartheta
and
\psi
over
K
such that
\psi_{L}
is anisotropic. Then
(\varphi_{L})_{\mathsf{an}}=\psi_{L}
. Now by
3.4
, there exists a quadratic form
\eta
over
L
with
\mathsf{dim}(\eta)\leqslant 4
such that
\psi_{L}=(\varphi_{L})_{\mathsf{an}}=\pi\otimes\eta
.
We have that
c\in\mathsf{G}(\varphi)\cap\mathsf{G}(\langle\!\langle d\rangle\!\rangle)\subseteq\mathsf{G}(\psi)
and
\mathsf{dim}(\psi)\leqslant 16
. By
3.5
, there exists a quadratic extension
L^{\prime}/K
such that
\psi_{L^{\prime}}
is isotropic and
c\in\mathsf{N}_{L^{\prime}/K}^{\ast}
. Since
\psi_{L}
is anisotropic, it follows that
L/K
and
L^{\prime}/K
are linearly disjoint.
We set
M=L\otimes_{K}L^{\prime}
.
By
[
1
, Lemma 3.3]
, we have
\mathsf{N}_{L/K}^{\ast}\cap\mathsf{N}_{L^{\prime}/K}^{\ast}={K}^{\times 2}\cdot\mathsf{N}_{M/K}^{\ast}
.
Since
\psi_{L^{\prime}}
is isotropic, so is
\psi_{M}
.
Since
\varphi_{M}
is Witt equivalent to
\psi_{M}
, we have
\mathsf{dim}((\varphi_{M})_{\mathsf{an}})<\mathsf{dim}(\psi)=16
.
By our assumption,
\varphi_{M}\in\mathsf{I}^{4}M
. Hence, by the Arason-Pfister Hauptsatz
[
6
, Theorem 23.7]
we conclude that
\varphi_{M}
is hyperbolic. Therefore we have
c\in{K}^{\times 2}\cdot\mathsf{N}_{M/K}^{\ast}\subseteq{K}^{\times 2}\cdot\mathsf{Hyp}(\varphi)
.
∎

The cohomology sets
H^{1}(K,\mu_{2})
and
H^{2}(K,\mu_{2})
are identified with the quotient
{K}^{\times}/{K}^{\times 2}
and with the
2
-torsion subgroup of the
Brauer group
\operatorname{\mathsf{Br}}_{2}(K)
respectively. Let
(A,\sigma)
be a central simple algebra with symplectic involution over
K
. Then
[A]\in\operatorname{\mathsf{Br}}_{2}(K)
. When
2\operatorname{\mathsf{ind}}(A)
divides
\mathsf{deg}(A)
, the discriminant of
(A,\sigma)
is defined in
[
3
]
as a class in the cohomology set
H^{3}(K,\mu_{2}),
and is denoted by
\Delta(\sigma)
.

Theorem 3.7
.

Let
(A,\sigma)
be a central simple algebra of
\mathsf{deg}(A)=12
with symplectic involution. Assume that
\operatorname{\mathsf{ind}}(A)\leqslant 2
and trivial discriminant. Then
{\bf PSim}(A,\sigma)
is
R
-trivial.

Proof.

As
(A,\sigma)
be a central simple algebra with symplectic involution with
\operatorname{\mathsf{ind}}(A)\leqslant 2
, by
[
5
, Prop. 3.4]
we have
(A,\sigma)\simeq\operatorname{\mathsf{Ad}}(\varphi)\otimes(Q,\operatorname{\mathsf{can}}_{Q})
for some
6
-dimensional quadratic form
\varphi
over
K
and
Q
a quaternion algebra over
K
.
Write
Q=(a,b)
, with
a,b\in{K}^{\times}
. Then its norm form is
\langle\!\langle a,b\rangle\!\rangle
.

Let
\psi:=\langle\!\langle a,b\rangle\!\rangle\otimes\varphi
.
Denote
\mathsf{disc}(\varphi)=c{K}^{\times 2}
.
Then
\varphi\equiv\langle\!\langle c\rangle\!\rangle\,\mathsf{mod}\,\mathsf{I}^{2}K
and hence
\psi=\langle\!\langle a,b\rangle\!\rangle\otimes\varphi\equiv\langle\!\langle a,b,c\rangle\!\rangle\,\mathsf{mod}\,\mathsf{I}^{4}K
.
By
[
12
, Main Theorem]
, we have
\Delta(\sigma)=[Q]\cup(\mathsf{disc}(\varphi))
in
H^{3}(K,\mu_{2})
.
By our hypothesis,
\Delta(\sigma)=0
.
Hence

(a)\cup(b)\cup(c)=(\mathsf{disc}(\varphi))\cup[Q]=0
.
Hence, by
[
11
, Theorem 5.1]
together with
[
10
, Theorem 4.1]
, we have that
\langle\!\langle a,b,c\rangle\!\rangle
is hyperbolic.

Hence,
\psi\in\mathsf{I}^{4}K
.
By
3.6
, it follows that
\mathsf{G}(\psi)={K}^{\times 2}\cdot\mathsf{Hyp}(\psi)
.
By
3.1
, we have
\mathsf{G}(A,\sigma)=\mathsf{G}(\psi)
and
\mathsf{Hyp}(A,\sigma)=\mathsf{Hyp}(\psi)
.

For any field extension
K^{\prime}/K
, if
c\in\mathsf{G}(\psi_{K^{\prime}})
by
3.6
, then there exists a field extension
M/K^{\prime}
which is trivial, quadratic or biquadratic such that
\psi_{M}
is hyperbolic and
c\in{K^{\prime}}^{\times 2}\cdot\mathsf{N}_{M/K^{\prime}}^{\ast}
. In particular,
\mathsf{G}(\psi_{K^{\prime}})={K^{\prime}}^{\times 2}\cdot\mathsf{Hyp}(\psi_{K^{\prime}})
and hence
{\bf PSim}(\psi)
is
R
-trivial. Thus
{\bf PSim}(A,\sigma)
is
R
-trivial.
∎

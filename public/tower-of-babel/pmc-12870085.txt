Continuous cropping drives assembly process of soil microbial community to alter keystone taxa causing challenges
Authors: Fangfang Zhou, Yihong Pan, Jun Zhan, Xiaoting Li, Shunping Yu, Li Tang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundHealthy soil is crucial for maintaining the sustainability of soil ecosystem services and ensuring food security. The soil microbiome serves as a key indicator of soil health. However, long-term continuous monoculture significantly adversely affects the diversity and functioning of soil microbial communities, posing a serious threat to sustainable agricultural development. Therefore, making it crucial to understand the microbial mechanisms underlying these challenges.MethodsIn this study, tobacco was subjected to different planting durations: 1 year (CR), 5 years (CC5), 10 years (CC10), and 15 years (CC15). The rhizosphere microbial community assembly process, composition, keystone taxa, and their relationship to continuous cropping challenges were analyzed.ResultsThe rhizosphere bacterial community structure of tobacco after 5 years of continuous cropping was significantly separated from other treatments, while no significant separation was observed in the fungal community. Further investigation into the assembly processes of microbial communities under different continuous cropping durations revealed that bacterial community assembly processes exhibited differences, whereas no significant differences were observed in fungal community assembly processes. Specifically, the rhizosphere bacterial community in CC5 was predominantly shaped by deterministic assembly processes, explaining its structural distinctiveness from other treatments. Co-occurrence network analysis revealed that the number of nodes and edges in bacterial-fungal interactions decreased by 22.70% and 79.86%, respectively, in CC5 compared with CR. Differential microbial abundance identified a significant decline in key microbes (Rhodanobacter, Ellin6067, Frankiales, and Setophoma) alongside a marked increase in the abundance of genus Verticillium in CC5 relative to CR. RDA indicated these bacterial genera correlated negatively with pathogen accumulation and disease incidence but postively with yield These relationships potentially constituted the primary driver for exacerbated continuous cropping obstacles in CC5. In CC10, bacterial community assembly was primarily dominated by stochastic processes. Although the number of network nodes and edges increased by 21.96% and 204.73%, respectively, compared to CC5, they remained lower than those in CR. By extending the continuous cropping to 15 years, the bacterial community assembly was shaped by stochastic processes. Compared with CC5, the number of network nodes and edges in CC15 increased by 34.39% and 405.44%, respectively, with beneficial microbes (Rhodanobacter, Ellin6067, Frankiales, and Setophoma) showing remarkable abundance recovery and pathogenic fungi like Verticillium declining, ultimately resulting in microbial community characteristics approximating those observed in the CR.ConclusionsThe bacterial community in CC5 was driven by deterministic assembly processes, resulting in a distinct structure. The complexity and stability of the co-occurrence network significantly decreased, accompanied by a higher abundance of harmful microbes and a lower abundance of beneficial microbes, which exacerbated the continuous cropping obstacles. In contrast, after 15 years of continuous cropping, the bacterial community assembly shifted to stochastic processes and trans-kingdom co-occurrence network complexity and stability strengthened, beneficial microbes increased, and continuous cropping challenges alleviated.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12866-025-04595-z.Keywords: Different continuous cropping periods, Rhizosphere soil microbial community, Assembly process, Continuous cropping challenges, Keystone taxa

Introduction

Continuous cropping poses a significant global challenge, hindering sustainable agricultural development. Its effects include soil degradation, nutrient imbalance [1], reduction in soil enzyme activity [2], accumulation of allelopathic substances [3], and disruption of soil microbial communities [4], which can result in stunted crop growth and increased susceptibility to pests and diseases. Ultimately, these factors contribute to decreased crop yield and quality [5]. It is of great significance to further understand the mechanisms underlying continuous cropping challenges is crucial for the development of effective mitigation strategies.

Microorganisms serve as crucial carriers of material and energy in the soil [6], facilitating nutrient cycling, pollutant degradation, and the maintenance of stable ecosystem services and functions [7]. The assembly process of microbial communities significantly influences their functions and is closely associated with plant health [8]. Exploring the microbial community assembly processes under various ecological conditions is invaluable for understanding microbial community stability and ecological functions. The microbial community assembly processes are governed by niche and neutral theories [9]. Niche theory posits that microbial communities are shaped by deterministic factors, including biotic factors (species interactions such as competition and predation) and abiotic factors (environmental variables such as temperature, pH, and humidity). These communities form through deterministic processes to adapt to specific habitats and ecological environments. In contrast, neutral theory suggests that changes in microbial taxa are driven by stochastic processes, where birth, death, migration, and diffusion limitations create a stochastic balance that shapes the microbial community structure [10].Crop planting models, fertilization, and other management practices, as abiotic factors, affect the assembly process and structure of the crop rhizosphere microbial community [11]. Community assembly processes can determine the community composition, abundance, and ecological functions. Studies have indicated that low-diversity microbial communities can induce deterministic aggregation, and high-diversity communities can induce stochastic processes [12]. This diversity may limit community functions and emphasize the role of specialized functioning in community formation and maintenance within soil ecosystems. However, the impact of continuous cropping as an abiotic factor on the microbial community assembly processes and microbial composition stability remains unclear. Investigating microbial community assembly processes across different years of continuous cropping will enhance our understanding of continuous cropping mechanisms.

Healthy plants are generally associated with a higher microbial biomass [13], diversity, abundance, and evenness [14]. Microbial interactions can form intricate networks and regulate soil functions [15]. Conversely, continuous cropping has led to a decline in bacterial biomass, an increase in fungal biomass, a decrease in bacterial-fungal ratios [13], and an imbalance in community structure [16], ultimately resulting in soil-borne disease outbreaks. Studies on various cropping systems have shown a decrease in beneficial bacterial abundance and an increase in harmful bacteria over prolonged planting periods, such as 20 years in yam rhizospheres [17], and significant declines in beneficial phyla, such as Bacteroidetes, Streptomyces, and Actinobacteria, in cotton rhizospheres planted for 5, 8, and 15 years [18], while long-term continuous cropping (16 years) could alleviate the tobacco replant problem [19]. These changes disrupt soil ecological functions such as nitrogen fixation and disease suppression, exacerbating continuous cropping challenges [20]. In contrast, fungal diversity and abundance can exhibit an increasing trend across different sweet potato varieties after continuous cropping. Studies have shown a decrease in the abundance of the beneficial fungus Chaetomium and an increase in harmful fungi such as Verticillium, Fusarium, and Colletotrichum across all sweet potato varieties [21]. These findings indicate that long-term continuous cropping not only alters bacterial/fungal biomass but also changes the microbial community composition, structure, and function, ultimately impeding crop growth. However, understanding the effects of these changes on microbial stability, complexity, interactions, and their effects on crop health remains a significant challenge in unraveling the mechanisms of continuous cropping.

Network analyses can be a valuable tool for studying microbial interactions and visualizing community co-occurrence [22, 23]. Microbial network structures can undergo significant alterations under biotic and abiotic stresses [22, 24]. Research has indicated that healthy soil networks can exhibit greater complexity, with more modules, edges, and key microorganisms, than diseased soil [25]. However, the rhizosphere environment under long-term continuous cropping deteriorates, decreasing network complexity and stability. After eight years of cotton planting, the complexity of the bacterial co-occurrence network decreased, and the interactions among OTUs indicated a 40% increase in nodes, demonstrating negative correlations, suggesting more competition or antagonism among bacteria in continuous cropping soils [18]. Conversely, fungal community complexity has increased in tobacco fields under continuous cropping for 5 years compared to 3- and 1-year durations [26]. This indicates intricate interactions among rhizosphere soil microorganisms under continuous cropping conditions in which stability and complexity are crucial for crop health. Investigating keystone species and their relationship with continuous cropping challenges can significantly contribute to our understanding of the mechanisms of crop failure.

Tobacco is particularly sensitive to continuous cropping, which significantly limits high-quality production. Continuous cropping can reduce the abundance of beneficial bacteria, increase harmful fungi, disrupt soil microbial community balance, and ultimately stunt tobacco growth and sustainability [13]. The preliminary research indicated that the tobacco continuous cropping obstacles varied across different cropping years. After 5 years of continuous cropping, tobacco black shank pathogen (Phytophthora parasitica var. nicotianae) accumulation increased significantly, leading to severe disease incidence and a marked reduction in tobacco yield [27]. Microbial community assembly processes refer to the fundamental mechanisms that govern the formation, composition, and temporal dynamics of microbial communities [28]. These processes are broadly categorized into deterministic (niche-based) processes and stochastic (neutral) processes [10]. And the microbial community assembly process shaped its composition and ultimately affected its functioning under specific environmental conditions [29]. However, it remains unclear whether different durations of continuous cropping influence the microbial community assembly process, thereby altering community composition, key microbial taxa, and functional performance. Therefore, this study explored the microbial community assembly processes and the complexity and stability of bacterial-fungal cross-kingdom networks across different cropping periods (1, 5, 10, and 15 years) to identify keystone taxa. The relationship between these keystone taxa and continuous cropping obstacles was examined to provide a theoretical foundation for a deeper understanding of the mechanisms underlying continuous cropping challenges.

Materials and methods

Study site, field experimental design, and sample collecting

The experiments were conducted in Baishui Town, Luxi County, Yunnan Province, China (103.8727°E, 24.6657°N), which was a significant tobacco production area in Yunnan Province. Four experimental treatments were established in the field: first-year tobacco planting (no history of tobacco planting, CR), continuous cropping for 5 years (since 2016, CC5), continuous cropping for 10 years (since 2011, CC10), and continuous cropping for 15 years (since 2006, CC15). Each treatment was repeated six times, and 133 plants were planted per repetition, covering an area of 111 m2. Tobacco transplantation occurred annually from April 15 to May 10, and harvesting was completed before September. The variety Yunyan 87, which is moderately resistant to tobacco black shank disease, was planted in all treatments [30]. The planting soil was classified as red soil according to the Chinese soil classification [31]. Tobacco plants were planted at a density of 12,000 plants per ha. In the study area, pure nitrogen was applied at 105 kg/hm2. At transplanting, 75 kg/hm2 of compound fertilizer (N: P2O5:K2O = 12:8:25) was applied, followed by an initial application of 30 kg/hm2 of the same compound fertilizer and a subsequent application of 150 kg/hm2 of potassium sulfate (K2O ≥ 52%). The other management practices remained consistent throughout the study period.

Tobacco rhizosphere soil samples from the four experimental treatments were collected at maturity in September 2020. Using an “S” shaped pattern, we collected the soil from five points around each treatment, carefully excavating the entire root system and gently removing the large soil clumps to sample the soil immediately adjacent to the roots using a brush. This process was repeated six times for each treatment group. All samples were promptly transported to the laboratory in an icebox, passed through a 2-mm sieve, and stored at −80 °C until DNA extraction.

Total soil DNA extraction, PCR amplification, and high-throughput sequencing

Total DNA was extracted from the rhizosphere soil using a DNeasy PowerSoil Kit. 16S rRNA and ITS amplicon sequencing were performed using the Illumina platform. The V3–V4 region of bacterial 16S rRNA gene was amplified with, specific primers 338 F (5’-ACTCCTACGGGAGGCAGCA-3’) and 806R (5’-GGACTACHVGGGTWTCTAAT-3’). The fungal ITS region was amplified with the primers ITS1F (5’-CTTGGTCATTTAGAGGAAGTAA-3’) and ITS2R (5’-GCTGCGTTCTTCATCGATGC-3’). PCR, amplification and purification followed the methodology outlined by [32]. High-throughput sequencing was conducted on an Illumina HiSeq 2500 sequencer at Biomarker Tech. The PE reads were spliced using Flash v1.2.11 to get raw tags [33], and the high-quality data were obtained by filtering tags using Trimmomatic v0.33 [34]. The chimeric sequences were removed using UCHIME v4.2 software to ensure the integrity of the data [35]. The tags were then clustered into OTUs at a 97% similarity level using USEARCH [36], and the OTUs were filtered at 0.005%. Taxonomic annotation of the 16 S and ITS sequences was performed using SILVA 138.1 and UNITE 9.0 taxonomic databases. Raw sequencing data from all samples were deposited in the NCBI Sequence Read Archive (SRA) database under the accession numbers PRJNA719590 and PRJNA1138970.

Data processing and analysis

The beta-nearest taxon index (β-NTI) is a key metric for evaluating microbial community assembly processes and provides valuable insights into community structure and function [37]. The Stegen null model was applied to calculate the β-diversity index (βNTI) for bacterial and fungal communities and the βNTI values were computed using the null model framework introduced by Stegen et al. [38] and implemented in picante package of R (version 4.3.2), Briefly, < 2 indicates dominance by stochastic processes, whereas > 2 indicates dominance by deterministic processes.To further disentangle the specific stochastic processes, we subsequently calculated the RC-bray based on for pairwise comparisons that were characterized by |βNTI| < 2, following the extended framework proposed by Stegen et al. [38]. The RC-bray value was calculated using the vegan package. This approach allows for the distinction between dispersal-driven stochasticity and drift. Specifically, when |βNTI| < 2, RC-bray > 0.95 indicates dispersal limitation, RC-bray < −0.95 indicated homogenizing dispersal,−0.95< RC-bray< 0.95 suggested ecological drift.

Principal co-ordinates analysis (PcoA) was performed using the vegan package in R (version 4.3.2) to calculate Bray-Curtis and implement visualization in ggplot2 for evaluating differences in soil microbial communities between treatments. Trans-kingdom network and differential microbial network analyses were conducted using the corr.test function in the psych package of R (version 4.3.2) to calculate the Spearman correlation coefficient matrix and its significance levels according to Sunil Mundra et al. [39]. The Benjamini-Hochberg (BH) false discovery rate method was applied to adjust P-values, with bacterial genera showing significant correlations (correlation coefficient > 0.7 and P < 0.05) selected for further analysis. Gephi software was used to visualize the network diagrams and analyze interactions among bacterial genera. Each node in the network represents a bacterial genus, and the edges connecting the nodes indicate correlations between them. Network stability was measured based on the proportion of negative and positive correlations, with higher proportions of negative correlations indicating greater stability [40]. Differential abundance analysis of microbial genera was performed using DESeq2 in R (version 4.3.2). The resulting P-values were adjusted for multiple testing using the Benjamini-Hochberg (BH) method to control the false discovery rate (FDR). Genera were considered significantly differentially abundant based on thresholds of Variable Importance in the Projection (VIP) ≥ 1, |Log2FC| > 1, and FDR-adjusted P-value (q-value) ≤ 0.05. Volcano plots were then generated to visualize the results using the ggplot2 package. Stacked bar charts depicting differential microbial composition were created using Origin2022. Redundancy analysis (RDA) was performed using R (version 4.3.2) to evaluate the contribution of different microorganisms to continuous cropping obstacle indicators.

Results

Alpha diversity and beta diversity of microbial communities across different continuous cropping years

The results showed the Shannon index of the tobacco rhizosphere bacterial communities had no significant differences across different continuous cropping years (Fig. S1A). However, significant variations were observed in bacterial community structures. PCoA highlighted that the bacterial community in CC5 significantly diverged from that in the other treatments, whereas no distinction was observed among CR, CC10, and CC15. PCoA1 and PCoA2 collectively explained 49% of the variance with contributions of 25% and 24%, respectively (Fig. 1A). No significant differences were observed in the Shannon index of the tobacco rhizosphere fungal community across different continuous cropping years (Fig. S1B). PCoA demonstrated that the fungal community in CC5 diverged from that in CC15, whereas no distinction was observed among CR, CC10, and CC15. PCoA1 and PCoA2 collectively explained 35.00% of the variance. Above all, the bacterial community in CC5 diverged from other treatments and the fungal community in CC5 diverged from CC15 (Fig. 1B).

Microbial assembly process in different continuous cropping years

The β-nearest taxon index (βNTI) and RC-bary value was calculated for each treatment to elucidate the bacterial assembly process. Over successive cropping years, the assembly process of rhizosphere bacteria transitioned from stochastic to deterministic dominance, and back to stochastic processes (Fig. 2A). In the CR, the assembly process was predominantly stochastic (βNTI < 2), characterized by equal contributions from drift, dispersal limitation, and heterogeneous selection (Fig. 2B). After 5 years of continuous cropping (CC5), the bacterial community exhibited a higher proportion of heterogeneous selection than dispersal limitation (βNTI > 2), indicative of a shift towards deterministic processes in assembly. After 10 years of continuous cropping, the rhizosphere bacterial community was primarily governed by dispersal limitation, which was 46.67% higher than that under heterogeneous selection. In CC15, the bacterial community was dominated by stochastic processes, with dispersal limitations accounting for 83.33% (Fig. 2B). These findings suggested a dynamic shift in the rhizosphere bacterial community assembly processes under continuous cropping stress. Stochastic processes predominated in the CR. With 5 years of continuous cropping (CC5), the deterministic processes influenced by heterogeneous selection could affect precedence. As continuous cropping was extended (CC10 and CC15), the assembly process reverted to stochastic dominance, resulting in no discernible difference from the CR.

Bacterial community assembly processes and fungal community assembly processe across different cropping years. A βNTI values of bacterial community; B Bacterial community assembly processes; C βNTI values of fungal community; D Fungal community assembly processes

The βNTI values of the fungal communities fell between − 1 and 1 across all treatments, indicating that the fungal community assembly processes were primarily governed by stochastic processes in different cropping years (Fig. 2C). In CR, drift and heterogeneous selection predominantly shaped the fungal community, with drift playing a dominant role (86.67%). According to CC5, the fungal community was entirely drift-dominated. As continuous cropping progressed to CC15, there was an increase in dispersal limitations and homogeneous selection processes (Fig. 2D). Overall, the assembly process of rhizosphere fungal communities appeared to be minimally influenced by continuous cropping and remained largely dominated by stochastic processes across all four treatments, with no significant differences in community structure.

Transkingdom co-occurrence network across different continuous cropping years

Rhizosphere microbial community assembly processes contribute to distinct community structures. We constructed a transkingdom co-occurrence network across continuous periods. In CR, the bacterial-fungal cross-kingdom co-occurrence network comprised 489 nodes and 8392 edges, and the ratio of positive and negative correlations was 1 (Fig. 3A). After 5 years of continuous cropping, the bacterial-fungal transkingdom co-occurrence network decreased to 378 nodes and 1690 edges, indicating a reduction of 22.70% and 79.86%, respectively, compared with CR. The number of positive correlations exceeded that of negative correlations in this community structure (Fig. 3B). After 10 years of continuous cropping, the network expanded to 461 nodes and 5150 edges, representing increases of 21.96% and 204.73%, respectively, compared with CC5. However, these values remained lower than those observed in CR, exhibiting reductions of 6.07% and 62.96%, respectively, compared with CR (Fig. 3C). The number of network nodes and edges increased by 34.39% and 405.44%, respectively, in CC15 compared with CC5, similar to the trend observed in CR. However, the network interactions became more positive (Fig. 3D). Overall, continuous cropping significantly affected the bacterial-fungal transkingdom symbiosis model. After 5 years of continuous cropping, the bacterial-fungal interactions weakened, and the system stability decreased. After 15 years, the bacterial-fungal transkingdom network interaction improved, with positive correlations dominating the community, differing from the CR process.

Rhizosphere keystone in different continuous cropping years

Microorganisms from the volcano plot were used as objectives, and a relative abundance exceeding 0.1% was extracted (Fig.S2-S3). Co-occurrence networks were constructed to analyze key bacterial and fungal taxa. The number of key bacteria was greater than that of fungi, and complex interactions were observed among different bacteria.

Proteobacteria accounted for the highest proportion of key bacteria in the rhizosphere responding to continuous cropping years, including Ellin6067, Rhodanobacter, Alphaproteobacteria, Burkholderiaceae, MND1, uncultured_bacterium_o_CCD24, uncultured_bacterium_o_PLTA13, and Pseudomonas. Among Actinobacteria, Ilumatobacter, Lapillicoccus, and Frankiales were the key taxa, whereas Candidatus_Solibacter was the key taxon among Acidobacteria (Fig. 4A). Compared with CR, the relative abundance of beneficial bacteria, such as Candidatus_Solibacter, Ellin6067, and Frankiales, decreased significantly in CC5, whereas the relative abundance of Ilumatobacter, uncultured_bacterium_o_CCD24, and uncultured_bacterium_o_PLTA13 increased significantly. With prolonged continuous cropping years, the relative abundance of beneficial bacteria, such as Candidatus_Solibacter, Ellin6067, and Frankiales, significantly increased, eventually recovering to CR levels (Fig. 4C).

Differential microbial co-occurrence networks. A Differential bacterial co-occurrence network; (B) Differential fungal co-occurrence network; (C) Relative abundance of key bacterial taxa; (D) Relative abundance of key fungal taxa

The key fungal taxa responding to continuous cropping years were primarily distributed among the phyla Ascomycota, Chytridiomycota, Mortierellomycota, and Basidiomycota. Ascomycota had the largest number of key taxa, accounting for 82.35%, including Unclassified1, Verticillium, Stagonosporopsis, Setophoma, Bipolaris, and Fusidium (Fig. 4B). Compared to CR, the relative abundances of Unclassified1 and Verticillium significantly increased in CC5, whereas the relative abundances of Setophoma, Fusidium, and Bipolaris significantly decreased. After 10 and 15 years of continuous cropping, the relative abundances of Unclassified1 and Verticillium decreased significantly, whereas those of Setophoma and Fusidium increased significantly (Fig. 4D). The abundance of key taxa changed with the extension of the continuous cropping years, which may be closely related to continuous cropping obstacles. Therefore, the effects of key bacteria and fungi on tobacco growth and disease have been extensively studied.

The correlation of the key rhizosphere microorganisms with black shank pathogen accumulation

Our preliminary research results showed that the characteristics of tobacco continuous cropping obstacles were different across different continuous cropping years. The tobacco black shank pathogen accumulated significantly for 5 years continuous cropping, the disease incidence was severe, and the tobacco yields have significantly decreased [27].Therefore, the effects of key rhizosphere bacteria and fungi on pathogen accumulation, disease incidence, and yield were explored. The results suggested that the key bacterial community structure of CC5 was significantly distinct from that of the other three treatments. Pathogen accumulation and yield were most affected by the key bacterial genera (Fig. 5A). Beneficial bacteria, such as Rhodanobacter, Candidatus_Solibacter, Ellin6067, and Frankiales, which significantly decreased in CC5, were negatively correlated with pathogen accumulation and disease incidence and positively correlated with yield. The key fungal taxa in CC5 were also significantly different from those in the other three treatment groups. Fusidium was positively correlated with tobacco black shank pathogens and black shank disease and negatively correlated with yield. Setophoma was positively correlated with yield and negatively correlated with black shank incidence, based on RDA (Fig. 5B).

Discussion

Long-term monoculture cropping can suppress crop growth and aggravate soil-borne diseases, thereby leading to reduced crop yield and quality. Our previous study showed that compared to the first year, tobacco soil-borne black shank disease worsened with the highest pathogen accumulation and lower yield sustainability after 5 years, indicating significant continuous cropping obstacles. When continuous cropping was extended to 15 years, black shank disease incidence and pathogen accumulation decreased, and the yield recovered to the levels of the first cropping year [41]. The causes of these phenomena include general and specific inhibitions. The specific inhibition due to continuous cropping results from the enrichment of particular microbial subgroups [42], such as the accumulation of the antifungal compound 2,4-diacetylphloroglucinol (DAPG) by antagonistic fluorescent Pseudomonas spp.,which inhibits the proliferation of pathogens [43]. The enrichment of specific microbial subgroups also shapes the assembly of the rhizosphere microbial community. Understanding how continuous cropping, as an abiotic factor, affects the assembly of tobacco rhizosphere microbial communities is crucial for comprehending the composition and function of these communities under such stress. The βNTI of microbial communities shifted under environmental stress. For example, Fusarium infections in bananas and cucumbers drive microbial community assembly toward deterministic processes, closely linked to disease occurrence [44]. In contrast, less stressful environments favor stochastic processes, enhancing microbial diversity and complexity [45].This approach enabled the analysis of the potential mechanisms driving microbial community construction across different years of continuous cropping. In this study, the rhizosphere bacterial community of first-year tobacco was shaped by stochastic processes. By the fifth year of cropping, deterministic processes dominated, likely contributing to the reduced microbial complexity and increased disease severity. However, after 10 and 15 years of continuous cropping, the bacterial community shifted back to stochastic processes, indicating a recovery from continuous cropping obstacles.

This suggests that continuous cropping, as an abiotic stress factor, could drive changes in the bacterial community assembly processes. Nevertheless, this driving force diminished with prolonged continuous cropping for up to 15 years and the bacterial community assembly changed to a stochastic process. The significant separation of the rhizosphere bacterial community in CC5 from those in CR, CC10, and CC15 may be attributed to this factor. The βNTI of the fungal community fluctuated between − 1 and 1 across different continuous cropping years, with their assembly predominantly driven by stochastic processes. Overall, continuous cropping was inferred to primarily shape bacterial community assembly processes that influence their functions. This could contribute to the exacerbation of continuous cropping obstacles in tobacco after five consecutive years. As the bacterial community assembly shifted to stochastic processes following long-term monoculture, this could be associated with reduced continuous cropping obstacles.

Microbial networks are crucial for analyzing interactions within complex soil systems, which play a vital role in soil ecosystem stability and function [25]. Continuous cropping can drive bacterial community assembly processes, potentially affecting bacterial-fungal transkingdom interactions and influencing microbial community stability and function. The results indicated that continuous cropping significantly affected the bacterial-fungal transkingdom co-occurrence network. In the initial tobacco cropping, interactions between rhizosphere bacteria and fungi were complex and stable (489 nodes and 8392 edges). The network exhibited a balanced proportion of positive and negative feedback, suggesting a robust microbial structure that can adapt to environmental changes [46, 47]. After 5 years of continuous cropping, the transkingdom co-occurrence network exhibited a 22.70% decrease in nodes and a 79.86% decrease in edges compared with CR. This reduction in network complexity indicated the decreased stability of the root ecosystem, rendering it more fragile and vulnerable to environmental conditions, which could explain the exacerbated disease in CC5 [25]. Following 10 years of continuous cropping, the number of nodes and edges in the co-occurrence network increased but remained lower than that in CR. After 15 years of continuous cropping, the number of nodes and edges in the co-occurrence network recovered to CR levels. In CC15, the interactions were predominantly positive (79.16%), indicating stronger mutual trophic interactions and promotion among microorganisms [24]. This reduction could primarily explain the decrease in pathogen accumulation and disease occurrence as well as the decreased microbial resistance to environmental disturbances [47, 48].

Differences in microbial interaction complexity and network stability could contribute to changes in the abundance of key microorganisms across years of continuous cropping. Therefore, we screened for different bacterial and fungal genera in tobacco under varying continuous cropping conditions using volcano plots. The number of differential bacterial OTUs exceeded that of fungal OTUs in the tobacco rhizosphere across different continuous cropping periods, suggesting that the bacterial community structure could be influenced more by continuous cropping and play a significant role in continuous cropping obstacles. This phenomenon is particularly evident under both abiotic and biotic stress conditions [8]. The numbers of different OTUs among CR and CC5, CC10, and CC15 were 838, 282, and 181, respectively, showing a declining trend with prolonged continuous cropping. This trend could be a key factor contributing to the alleviation of continuous cropping obstacles in tobacco after 15 years of continuous cropping.

Thirteen key bacterial taxa were identified using a co-occurrence network based on differential bacteria. Candidatus_Solibacter, Ellin6067, Frankiales, Ilumatobacter, uncultured_bacterium_o_CCD24, and uncultured_bacterium_o_PLTA13 showed strong responses to continuous cropping. The relative abundance of beneficial bacteria, such as Rhodanobacter, Candidatus_Solibacter, Ellin6067, and Frankiales, significantly decreased during short-term monoculture continuous cropping (CC5) but increased over the long term (CC15). RDA results revealed that Candidatus_Solibacter, Ellin6067, and Frankiales were significantly negatively correlated with pathogen accumulation and disease incidence but significantly positively correlated with yield. Rhodanobacter plays a vital role in the nitrogen cycle and soil remediation and contributes signficantly to the maintenance of soil ecosystem health [49]. Candidatus_Solibacter has been identified as a bacterium that can enhance wheat yield [50] and decompose soil pollutants [51]. Frankiales promoted plant growth through symbiotic root nodule formation and participation in the nitrogen cycle [52]. Ellin6067, an ammonia-oxidizing bacterium, is linked to soil urease activity [53] that can influence crop growth and yield. Changes in these bacterial genera are likely to be the main drivers of increased tobacco disease and reduced yield after five consecutive years of cropping. This was the main reason for disease mitigation and yield improvement in CC15. Using the co-occurrence network, we identified five key fungal genera: Verticillium, Stagonosporopsis, Setophoma, Bipolaris, and Fusidium, all of which exhibited significant responses to continuous cropping. The relative abundance of the main fungal pathogen, Verticillium, significantly increased during short-term continuous cropping, potentially exacerbating tobacco diseases in CC5. Conversely, the relative abundance of Setophoma and Fusidium decreased significantly in CC5 but exhibited an upward trend with prolonged continuous cropping years. Among them, Setophoma showed a significant positive correlation with yield and a significant negative correlation with disease incidence. Previous studies have reported that Setophoma is a pathogen that affects garlic pink roots [54]. However, Bazioli et al. [55]reported that the co-culturing Setophoma with Penicillium brasilianum and Talaromyces wortmannii could increase stemphyperylenol derivatives, which have the antagonistic effects against the Aspergillus fumigatus. It has also been observed to acquire antifungal compounds in co-cultures with Bacillus subtilis [56]. These findings suggest that Setophoma may possess disease resistance and may promote growth through these interactions.

In summary, we inferred that continuous cropping could drive bacterial community assembly. Short-term continuous cropping (5 years) intensified the selective pressures, altered the microbial assembly, and destabilized the bacterial-fungal co-occurrence network. This resulted in a decreased abundance of beneficial species (Ellin6067, Frankiales, and Setophoma) and an increased abundance of harmful species, such as Verticillium, exacerbating continuous cropping obstacles. However, after 15 years of continuous tobacco cropping, bacterial community assembly shifted to stochastic processes. This transformation enhanced the complexity and stability of the bacterial-fungal co-occurrence network. Consequently, the abundance of beneficial bacteria increased (Ellin6067, Frankiales, and Setophoma), whereas that of harmful fungi such as Verticillium decreased. These mechanisms could explain the alleviation of continuous cropping obstacles after long-term continuous cropping.

Conclusion

The rhizosphere bacterial community structure of tobacco after 5 years of continuous cropping was significantly separated from other treatments, while no significant separation was observed in the fungal community. The assembly process of the bacterial community under 5-year continuous cropping (CC5) was predominantly governed by deterministic processes, whereas stochastic processes played a major role in the CR, CC10, and CC15 treatments. These results suggested that the duration of continuous cropping influences the assembly processes of bacterial communities, thereby affecting their compositional profiles. The bacterial-fungi interaction networks showed the complexity and stability in CC5 reduced compared with CR and CC15. Consequently, the abundance of beneficial bacteria (Rhodanobacter, Ellin6067, Frankiales, and Setophoma) decreased, whereas that of harmful fungi, such as Verticillium, increased in CC5. RDA analysis showed these changes contributed to higher tobacco black shank pathogen accumulation and disease incidence, exacerbating continuous cropping obstacles. After 10 years of continuous cropping, the bacterial community assembly shifted to stochastic processes, similar to that of CR, although the cross-kingdom network nodes and edges remained fewer than those in CR. After 15 years of continuous cropping, bacterial community assembly continued to follow stochastic processes. The complexity and stability of the bacterial-fungal interaction network recovered to CR levels, accompanied by an increased abundance of beneficial microbes, such as Ellin6067, Frankiales, and Setophoma, and a decreased abundance of harmful fungi, such as Verticillium.

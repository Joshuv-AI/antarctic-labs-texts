1.
Introduction

Many results have been obtained using the character degrees set of a finite group. Recently, restricting the study to the real valued or rational valued characters of finite groups to determine the structure of the group has received a lot of attention. This has proved that character values in a character table encode a great deal of information about the group structure of a finite group. In this article, our first result is based on a problem first considered by Berkovich, Chillag and Zhmud in the 1990s in
[
1
]
.

Let
G
be a finite group and
{\mathrm{Irr}}(G)
be the set of complex irreducible characters of
G
. Recall the definition of a character degrees set and that of character values set of
G
:

{\mathrm{cd}}(G):=\{\chi(1)\mid\chi\in{\mathrm{Irr}}(G)\}
,

{\mathrm{cv}}(G)=\{\chi(g)\mid\chi\in{\mathrm{Irr}}(G),g\in G\}
,

respectively. If
\chi\in{\mathrm{Irr}}(G)
, then denote
{\mathrm{cv}}(\chi)=\{\chi(g)\mid g\in G\}
. It follows that
{\mathrm{cv}}(\chi)\subseteq{\mathrm{cv}}(G)
for all
\chi\in{\mathrm{Irr}}(G)
. Note also that for every non-linear
\chi\in{\mathrm{Irr}}(G)
,
|{\mathrm{cv}}(\chi)|\geqslant 3
.

In
[
1
]
, the authors studied groups
G
with the following extremal property:
|{\mathrm{cv}}(\chi)|=3
for every non-linear
\chi\in{\mathrm{Irr}}(G)
. Their results show that
G
is a
2
-group or some solvable Frobenius group
[
1
, Main Theorem]
. The authors also considered groups in which
|{\mathrm{cv}}(\chi)|=4
for some non-linear
\chi\in{\mathrm{Irr}}(G)
. Our first result is a continuation of their investigation and we show that if
|{\mathrm{cv}}(\chi)|\leqslant 4
for every non-linear
\chi\in{\mathrm{Irr}}(G)
, then
G
is solvable.

Theorem A
.

Let
G
be a finite group. If
|{\mathrm{cv}}(\chi)|\leqslant 4
for all non-linear
\chi\in{\mathrm{Irr}}(G)
, then
G
is solvable.

Our proof uses the classification of finite simple groups, the existence of extendible rational valued characters in finite simple groups and the fact that non-solvable groups all of whose non-linear irreducible characters are rational valued are rational groups. At the end of Section 5, we give some possibilities and some examples of groups that satisfy the hypothesis of Theorem A. We also note that
A_{5}
has a character with five character values and so the condition in Theorem A is best possible.

For the rest of the article, we shall study a subset of
{\mathrm{cv}}(G)
, the complement of
{\mathrm{cd}}(G)
. Define
\mathrm{cdc}(G):={\mathrm{cv}}(G)\setminus{\mathrm{cd}}(G)
. Our objective is to study how the structure of
G
is affected by
|{\mathrm{cdc}}(G)|
. It is easy to see that
{\mathrm{cdc}}(G)=\emptyset
if and only if
G=1
and that
|{\mathrm{cdc}}(G)|=1
if and only if
G\cong C_{2}^{*}
, where
C_{n}^{*}
denotes a direct product of cyclic groups of order
n
. It is also easy to see that if
G
is non-abelian, then
|{\mathrm{cdc}}(G)|\geqslant 2
. What if
|{\mathrm{cdc}}(G)|=2
? A moment’s thought would have us conclude that
G
is a rational group. Rational groups have been studied extensively(see
[
22
]
and
[
6
]
and their references for more details). In
[
23
]
, Sakurai considered this problem in the special case when
|{\mathrm{cd}}(G)|=2
. He showed that
|{\mathrm{cd}}(G)|=2
if and only if
G\cong C_{3}^{*}\rtimes C_{2}
, a Frobenius group with an elementary abelian
3
-group kernel and whose complement is of order two. We show that in the general case,
G
is solvable. In fact, our result shows that when
|{\mathrm{cdc}}(G)|=3
, with a few possible exceptions,
G
is solvable:

Theorem B
.

Let
G
be a finite group whose composition factors are not isomorphic to either
A_{5}
or
A_{6}
. If
|{\mathrm{cdc}}(G)|\leqslant 3
, then
G
is solvable.

We believe that the condition on composition factors is not necessary. In order to prove the above theorem, we work with a subset of
{\mathrm{cdc}}(G)
that allows us to have an inductive proof. In particular, we focus on elements
a
of
{\mathrm{cdc}}(G)
such that
a\notin\mathbb{N}
. The drawback of this strategy is for example: For any normal subgroup
N
of
G
,
2\in{\mathrm{cdc}}(G/N)
if
G/N\cong A_{5}
but it is not clear if
2\in{\mathrm{cdc}}(G)
. This necessitates the hypothesis on
A_{5}
and
A_{6}
. Note that non-solvable groups with
|{\mathrm{cdc}}(G)|=4
and
|{\mathrm{cd}}(G)|=4
were classified in
[
18
]
.

As an extension of
[
23
, Theorem]
, we consider finite groups in which
|{\mathrm{cd}}(G)|\leqslant 4
or
{\mathrm{dl}}(G)\leqslant 3
in the following result:

Theorem C
.

Let
G
be a finite group and suppose that either
|{\mathrm{cd}}(G)|\leqslant 4
or
{\mathrm{dl}}(G)\leqslant 3
. Then
|{\mathrm{cdc}}(G)|=2
if and only if one of the following holds:

(a)

{\mathrm{cd}}(G)=\{1,2\}
and
G
is a Frobenius group with a Frobenius kernel which is an elementary abelian
3
-group and whose complement is of order
2
.

(b)

|{\mathrm{cd}}(G)|=\{1,2,3\}
and
G\cong S_{4}
.

The groups considered in Theorem C are necessarily solvable from the hypothesis since in the first case
|{\mathrm{cv}}(G)|=6<8
(see
[
18
, Theorem 1.1]
) and the second case is obvious. We also note that these groups are such that
|{\mathrm{cv}}(\chi)|\leqslant 4
for all
\chi\in{\mathrm{Irr}}(G)
and hence are connected to groups in Theorem A. Based on calculations in
GAP

[
10
]
, we think groups in Theorem C are the only finite groups such that
|{\mathrm{cdc}}(G)|=2
. However, we cannot show this at this point. We consider a counterexample in the last section. In particular, we show that the group necessarily has an element of order
6
and a character degree divisible by
6
.

In our next result, we characterise nilpotent groups such that
|{\mathrm{cdc}}(G)|=3
. Note that the groups described in Theorem D are some of the groups studied by Berkovich, Chillag and Zhmud in
[
1
, Corollary]
. The result is easy to prove and we put it here because of its interesting connection to the work of Berkovich, Chillag and Zhmud. We recall the definition of a codegree of an irreducible character: If
\chi\in{\mathrm{Irr}}(G)
, then
{\mathrm{cod}}(\chi)=|G{:}\ker\chi|/\chi(1)
.

Theorem D
.

Let
G
be a finite nilpotent group. Then the following statements are equivalent:

(a)

|{\mathrm{cdc}}(G)|=3
;

(b)

|{\mathrm{cd}}(G)|=2
and
|{\mathrm{cv}}(\chi)|\leqslant 3
for all
\chi\in{\mathrm{Irr}}(G)
;

(c)

|{\mathrm{cd}}(G)|=2
,
G
is a
2
-group with
{\mathrm{cod}}(\chi)=2\chi(1)
for all non-linear
\chi\in{\mathrm{Irr}}(G)
.

If one of the above statements hold, then there exists a normal subgroup
N
such that
G/N
is an extraspecial
2
-group.

Classifying the groups above seems to be too technical. Indeed the
2
-groups with five character values in their character table are too many as remarked in
[
23
]
.
However, these
2
-groups have a factor group which is extraspecial as mentioned in Theorem D.

For the non-nilpotent groups, we investigate groups
G
when
|{\mathrm{cdc}}(G)|=3
and
{\mathrm{dl}}(G)=2
. We have that Sylow
2
-subgroups of these groups are either abelian or precisely the
2
-groups with five character values that are described in Theorem E.

Theorem E
.

Let
G
be a finite non-nilpotent group. Suppose that
|{\mathrm{cdc}}(G)|=3
. If
{\mathrm{dl}}(G)=2
, then
G=(C_{3}^{*}\times O_{2}(G))\rtimes C_{2}
with
O_{2}(G)
abelian. Moreover, if
P
is a non-abelian Sylow
2
-subgroup of
G
, then
|{\mathrm{cv}}(P)|=5
and
G/P^{\prime}\cong(C_{3}^{*}\rtimes C_{2})\times C_{2}^{*}
, where
C_{3}^{*}\rtimes C_{2}
is a Frobenius group whose kernel is an elementary abelian
3
-group whose complement is of order
2
.

Our paper is organized as follows: Section
2
has some preliminary results; in Section
3
we consider groups such that
|{\mathrm{cdc}}(G)|=2
and either
|{\mathrm{cd}}(G)|\leqslant 4
or
{\mathrm{dl}}(G)\leqslant 3
and we establish Theorem C. In Section

we prove Theorems D and E. In Section

[

]
for the character theory of finite groups.

Preliminary Results

In this section we list some preliminary results that we need to prove our main results. We shall use freely these well known results in the following lemma.

Define
{\mathrm{ncv}}(G):=\{\chi(g)\notin\mathbb{N}:\chi\in{\mathrm{Irr}}(G),g\in G\}
and
{\mathrm{ncv}}(\chi):=\{\chi(g)\notin\mathbb{N}:g\in G\}
for a fixed
\chi\in{\mathrm{Irr}}(G)
. It follows that
{\mathrm{ncv}}(G)\subseteq{\mathrm{cdc}}(G)
and we shall indicate that

where it is necessary to make sure that

.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
G
be a finite group,
\chi\in{\mathrm{Irr}}(G)
,
N
be a normal subgroup of

and

be a positive integer. Then the following holds:

(a)

|{\mathrm{cv}}(G/N)|\leqslant|{\mathrm{cv}}(G)|
and
|{\mathrm{ncv}}(G/N)|\leqslant|{\mathrm{ncv}}(G)|
;

(b)

The character table of
C_{n}
has a column with pairwise different values. In particular,
|{\mathrm{cv}}(C_{n})|=n
;

(c)

If
G
is non-abelian, then
0\in{\mathrm{ncv}}(G)
;

(d)

If
{\mathrm{cv}}(\chi)\subseteq\mathbb{Q}
, then there exists a negative rational number
a\in{\mathrm{ncv}}(\chi)
;

(e)

If
G=H\times K
and
n\in{\mathrm{cv}}(H)
and
m\in{\mathrm{cv}}(K)
, then
n,m,mn\in{\mathrm{cv}}(G)
;

(f)

If
\chi(g)=a
for some
g\in G
, where
a\notin\mathbb{Q}
, then there exists
h\in G
such that
\chi(h)=b
, with
b\notin\mathbb{Q}
and
a\not=b
.

Proof.

(a) - (c) follows from
[
18
, Lemma 2.1]
. (d) follows from the first orthogonality relation
[
13
, Corollary 2.14]
using

and the principal character. (e) follows from the fact that for every

,
\chi=\theta\times\varphi
for some

and
\varphi\in{\mathrm{Irr}}(K)
.

For (f), let
\epsilon
be a
|G|
-th root of unity and
\sigma\in\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(\epsilon)/\mathbb{Q})
. If
\chi(g)=a
, then
(\chi(g))^{\sigma}=\chi(h^{v})\in{\mathrm{cv}}(\chi)
, where

,

,
\gcd(v,|G|)=1
and

is such that
\epsilon^{\sigma}=\epsilon^{v}
. If
(\chi(g))^{\sigma}=\chi(g)
, then

is rational, a contradiction and so
(\chi(g))^{\sigma}\not=\chi(g)
and irrational.
∎

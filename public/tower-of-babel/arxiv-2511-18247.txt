Tail Distribution of Regret in Optimistic Reinforcement Learning
Sajad Khodadadian, Mehrdad Moharrami, [0.8em] Virginia Polytechnic Institute, State UniversityGrado Department of Industrial and Systems Engineering, sajadk@vt.edu, [0.5em] University of IowaDepartment of Computer Science, moharami@uiowa.edu

Abstract
We derive instance-dependent tail bounds for the regret of optimism-based reinforcement learning in finite-horizon tabular Markov decision processes with unknown transition dynamics. Focusing on a UCBVI-type algorithm, we characterize the tail distribution of the cumulative regret R K R_{K} over K K episodes, rather than only its expectation or a single high-probability quantile. We analyze two natural exploration-bonus schedules: (i) a K K -dependent scheme that explicitly incorporates the total number of episodes K K , and (ii) a K K -independent scheme that depends only on the current episode index. For both settings, we obtain an upper bound on Pr ⁡ ( R K ≥ x ) \Pr(R_{K}\geq x) that exhibits a distinctive two-regime structure: a sub-Gaussian tail starting from an instance-dependent scale m K m_{K} up to a transition threshold, followed by a sub-Weibull tail beyond that point. We further derive corresponding instance-dependent bounds on the expected regret 𝔼 ⁡ [ R K ] \mathbb{E}[R_{K}] . The proposed algorithm depends on a tuning parameter α \alpha , which balances the expected regret and the range over which the regret exhibits a sub-Gaussian tail. To the best of our knowledge, our results provide one of the first comprehensive tail-regret guarantees for a standard optimistic algorithm in episodic reinforcement learning.



1  Introduction

We consider online Reinforcement Learning (RL) in finite-horizon Markov decision processes (MDPs) with unknown transition dynamics. In this setting, a learning agent interacts with an episodic environment for
K
episodes of length
H
, and aims to minimize its cumulative regret with respect to an optimal policy. A long line of work has established sharp worst-case regret guarantees for model-based and model-free algorithms in tabular MDPs; see, for instance, the near-minimax optimality results for UCRL2 under the average-reward criterion
(
11
)
and for UCBVI and its variants in the episodic setting
(
1
;
22
)
. These results show that, up to logarithmic factors, the optimal worst-case regret scales as
\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}(\sqrt{HSAK})
, where
S
and
A
are the sizes of the state and action spaces.

More recently, there has been substantial progress on instance-dependent analysis in RL. Inspired by the classical gap-dependent theory of stochastic bandits, several works have derived regret bounds that depend on the suboptimality gaps of state–action pairs rather than only on
(S,A,H,K)
. For tabular MDPs, this includes non-asymptotic gap-dependent bounds for model-based algorithms
(
14
)
, fine-grained gap-dependent bounds via adaptive multi-step bootstrapping
(
19
)
, and gap-dependent guarantees for model-free
Q
-learning and its refinements
(
21
;
17
;
24
)
. These results provide a more refined characterization of the regret in MDPs by incorporating an instance-dependent
Q^{*}
-gap, which measures the separation between optimal and suboptimal actions at each state.

At the same time, the tail behavior of regret in RL is still poorly understood. Most existing analyses deliver bounds of the form

\mathbb{P}\!\big(R_{K}\geq f(K,\delta)\big)\;\leq\;\delta,

for a fixed confidence level
\delta\in(0,1)
, typically by combining a union bound with martingale concentration inequalities. While such high-probability guarantees are useful, they only describe the regret distribution at a single point and require the user to choose
\delta
in advance. By contrast, in many safety-critical applications, such as healthcare decision making, robotic control in human environments, and financial risk management
10
, one would like a more detailed characterization of the entire tail distribution of regret, that is, explicit bounds on
\mathbb{P}(R_{K}\geq x)
that hold for all thresholds
x\geq\mathbb{E}[R_{K}]
.

In this paper, we take a step towards closing this gap by studying the concentration of regret for a UCBVI-type algorithm in finite-horizon MDPs. We show that the tail of the cumulative regret,
P(R_{K}\geq x)
, admits an explicit, non-asymptotic sub-Gaussian or sub-Weibull bound, depending on the value of
x
and the instance-dependent
Q^{\star}
-gap parameter.

Our contributions are as follows:

•

We study the tail behavior of the cumulative regret
R_{K}
for a UCBVI-type algorithm in finite-horizon tabular MDPs under a global
Q^{\star}
-gap, and derive explicit, instance-dependent sub-Gaussian and sub-Weibull tail bounds for
\mathbb{P}(R_{K}\geq x)
. Our analysis treats two families of exploration bonuses: a
K
-
dependent
schedule whose bonuses depend on the total number of episodes
K
, and a
K
-
independent
schedule whose bonuses depend only on the current episode index.

•

For the
K
-dependent scheme, we show that the regret exhibits a sub-Gaussian tail on an intermediate regime of
x
and a sub-Weibull tail beyond
x>\tilde{\mathcal{O}}\!\big(H^{3/2}K^{(1+\alpha)/2}\big)
, where

Our results provide the first characterization of the tail of the regret for a standard optimistic algorithm in tabular episodic RL, and they suggest that the entire distribution of the regret—not just its expectation or a single high-probability quantile—can be controlled in an instance-dependent manner determined by the

2  Related Work

Worst-case regret.

The modern theory of online RL in finite MDPs starts from worst-case regret guarantees under the optimism in the face of uncertainty principle.
In the average-reward setting, the UCRL2 algorithm of
11
achieves near-minimax regret of order
\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}\!\big(DS\sqrt{AK}\big)
, where
D
is the diameter of the MDP, and lower bound
\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}\!\big(\sqrt{DSAK}\big)
show that the dependence on
(D,S,A,K)
is almost optimal.
Subsequent refinements sharpened the constants and logarithmic factors, for instance by tightening the confidence sets around the transition kernel via empirical Bernstein-type bonuses
(
2
;
8
)
.

In the episodic finite-horizon setting considered in this paper, the optimal worst-case scaling was established by the UCBVI algorithm of
1
, which attains regret
\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}\!\big(H\sqrt{SAK}+H\sqrt{KH}\big)
and derive information-theoretic lower bounds of
\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}\!\big(H\sqrt{SAK}\big)
.
A number of works have since refined or extended this result:
6
proposed the EULER algorithm, which yields worst-case bounds with improved dependence on the horizon; see also the recent minimax analyses and extensions to factored MDPs, e.g.,
16
.
These results, however, are primarily worst-case and focus on bounding either the expectation or a single high-probability quantile of the regret, without characterizing the entire tail behavior.

Instance-dependent and gap-dependent analysis.

Motivated by the classical gap-dependent theory for stochastic bandits (see, e.g., the survey of
3
), there has been a surge of interest in instance-dependent regret bounds for tabular MDPs.

14
derived the first non-asymptotic gap-dependent regret bounds for model-based tabular RL, showing that the regret can scale polylogarithmically in
K
(up to polynomial factors in
(S,A,H)
and
1/\texttt{gap}^{\star}
).

22
introduces the environmental norm, a variance-like quantity that controls the difficulty of exploration, and designed an optimistic algorithm whose regret depends on this norm rather than only on worst-case parameters.
More recently,
5
developed improved instance-dependent bounds that replace the global value-function gaps with more local notions tailored to the subset of states visited by optimal policies, together with new information-theoretic lower bounds showing the necessity of these refined gap notions. On the model-free side,
21
showed that optimistic
Q
-learning enjoys logarithmic regret in episodic tabular MDPs under a global suboptimality-gap on
Q^{\star}
, thus establishing that simple temporal-difference methods can match the best-possible gap-dependent rates of model-based algorithms.
19
proposed an adaptive multi-step bootstrapping scheme that yields tighter instance-dependent bounds.

Under the linear MDP assumption with function approximation,
9
establish instance-dependent logarithmic regret for LSVI-UCB and UCRL-VTR in linear and linear-mixture MDPs.
17
further refine this picture by designing algorithms that incur only a bounded number of policy switches. Most recently,
23
extend these guarantees to adversarial environments.

Tail behavior and risk-sensitive objectives.

A recent line of work, starting from
7
, shows that bandit algorithms that are information-theoretically optimized for Lai–Robbins optimal expected regret can exhibit extremely heavy-tailed regret distributions: the regret tail behaves like a truncated Cauchy, and higher moments grow polynomially in the horizon. Building on this observation,
12
;
13
design policies that explicitly trade off optimality and tail risk, achieving worst-case optimal (or near-optimal) expected regret while simultaneously enforcing exponentially light tails for large-regret events and characterizing sharp Pareto frontiers between expected regret and tail probabilities.

In contrast, RL work that explicitly targets tail or risk-sensitive criteria usually changes the performance objective rather than analyzing the tail of classical regret.
For example, there is a growing literature on risk-sensitive or distributional RL that optimizes mean-variance, CVaR, or more general optimized certainty equivalent (OCE) criteria in MDPs; see, e.g.,
15
;
4
;
20
and references therein.
These works derive sample-complexity or regret guarantees for the risk-sensitive value of a policy, not for the tail of the standard regret with respect to the risk-neutral optimal policy.

Our work is complementary to this risk-sensitive line: we keep the classical regret objective but ask for an instance-dependent characterization of its tail distribution.
In particular, given a global
Q^{\star}
-gap, we show that a UCBVI-type algorithm enjoys sub-Gaussian and sub-Weibull tail bounds for the cumulative regret, together with instance-dependent control of its expectation.
To the best of our knowledge, this is the first result that treats regret concentration in episodic RL.

A deterministic Markov policy is a collection

\displaystyle V_{h}^{\bm{\pi}}(s)

\displaystyle\;=\;\mathbb{E}_{\bm{\pi},\bm{P}^{*}}\!\Big[\sum_{t=h}^{H-1}r_{t}(s_{t},a_{t})\,\Big|\,s_{h}=s\Big],

\displaystyle Q_{h}^{\bm{\pi}}(s,a)

a\notin\mathcal{A}_{h}^{*}(s)\end{subarray}}\Big(Q_{h}^{*}(s,\pi_{h}^{*}(s))-Q_{h}^{*}(s,a)\Big),

(1)

K

4  Main Results

We analyze a UCBVI-type algorithm that uses the following bonus function:

b^{k}_{h}(n)=\begin{cases}(H-h-1)\sqrt{\dfrac{0.5S\ln 2+\mu K^{\alpha}}{n}},&\text{{KD}},\\[8.61108pt]
(H-h-1)\sqrt{\dfrac{0.5S\ln 2+\mu(k+1)^{\alpha}}{n}},&\text{{KI}},\end{cases}\qquad b^{k}_{h}(0)\coloneqq\infty.

Here
k\in\{0,\dots,K-1\}
denotes the episode index,
n\geq 1
denotes the number of visits to the corresponding state–action pair,
KD
refers to the bonus used in the
K
-dependent algorithm, and
KI
refers to the bonus used in the
K
-independent algorithm.

The term
K^{\alpha}
in the definition of
b_{h}^{k}(\cdot)
plays the role of a global exploration budget that directly controls the aggressiveness of the bonuses. In the original minimax UCBVI analysis of
1
, as well as in the subsequent instance-dependent analysis of
14
, the exploration terms scale only logarithmically with the time horizon, roughly as
\sqrt{{\log(KSAH)}/{n}}
, which is tailored to control of the expected regret. In contrast, our bonuses scale as
\sqrt{(S\ln 2+\mu K^{\alpha})/n}
(or
\sqrt{(S\ln 2+\mu(k+1)^{\alpha})/n}
in the
K
-independent case), so that the uncertainty radius around the empirical model grows polynomially in
K
. This more conservative choice incentivizes sustained exploration even in later episodes: when the algorithm has visited a state–action pair only moderately many times, the bonus remains substantial and pushes the optimistic value estimates upward. From a tail perspective, this is precisely what allows us to derive nontrivial bounds on
\mathbb{P}(R_{K}\geq x)
for large thresholds
x
, at the price of a controlled polynomial dependence on
K
inside the bonus which will affect the expected regret.

, this high probability bound implies the expected regret that scale polylogarithmically in
K
, with bounds of the form

\mathbb{E}[R_{K}]\;\leq\;\tilde{\mathcal{O}}\!\Big(\frac{H^{3}SA}{\texttt{gap}^{\star}}\log K\Big).

Similar instance-dependent rates have been obtained for optimistic
Q
-learning and related model-free methods
(
21
;
17
)
. All the algorithms presented in these works depend on the specific choice of confidence level
\delta\in(0,1)
.

On the contrary, our results are instance-dependent in a comparable regime, but they are organized around the
entire
tail rather than a single quantile.
Instead of working at a fixed confidence level
\delta
and producing a bound, Corollary
1
describes how the entire curve
x\mapsto\mathbb{P}(R_{K}\geq x)
behaves:
for
x
above the instance-dependent baseline
m_{K}
we obtain a sub-Gaussian tail

\mathbb{P}(R_{K}\geq x)\;\leq\;\exp\!\Big(-\tilde{\mathcal{O}}\big(x^{2}/(H^{3}K)\big)\Big),

up to a transition scale that depends on
(H,K,\alpha)
, and beyond that scale the tail becomes sub-Weibull, with decay of order
\exp\{-\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(K^{\alpha})\}
in the
KD
case or
\exp\{-\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(x^{\alpha})\}
in the
KI
case. Notice that if we fix a confidence level
\delta=c\delta_{K}
for some
c>1
, and invert our tail bound, we recover a high-probability guarantee of the form

\mathbb{P}\!\left(R_{K}\;\geq\;\tilde{\mathcal{O}}\big(H^{4}SA(S+K^{\alpha})/\texttt{gap}^{*}+\sqrt{H^{3}K\log(1/\delta)}\big).\right)\;\leq\;\delta.

The key difference lies in the dependence on

K
: our bound incurs a larger
K
-dependence due to the more aggressive exploration strategy required in order to obtain a tail bound that is valid simultaneously for all
x>\mathbb{E}[R_{K}]
.

b^{k}_{h}(\cdot)
is defined as

b^{k}_{h}(n)=\begin{cases}(H-h-1)\sqrt{\dfrac{0.5S\ln 2+\mu K^{\alpha}}{n}},&\text{{KD}},\\[8.61108pt]
(H-h-1)\sqrt{\dfrac{0.5S\ln 2+\mu(k+1)^{\alpha}}{n}},&\text{{KI}},\end{cases}\qquad b^{k}_{h}(0)\coloneqq\infty.

Let
S_{K}
denote the summation in Lemma
1
, i.e.,

\displaystyle S_{K}\coloneqq\sum_{n=1}^{K}\exp\left(-2n\left(\frac{b_{h}^{n\vee\lceil K^{\gamma}\rceil}(n)}{H-h-1}\right)^{2}\right).

The analysis depends on the choice of
b^{k}_{h}(\cdot)
.

•

KD
:
We have

\displaystyle S_{K}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n=1}^{K}\exp\left(-S\ln 2-2\mu K^{\alpha}\right)=2^{-S}K\exp\left({-2\mu K^{\alpha}}\right)

•

KI
:
Define
S^{(1)}_{K}
and
S^{(2)}_{K}
as follows:

\displaystyle S^{(1)}_{K}\coloneqq\sum_{n=1}^{\lceil K^{\gamma}\rceil}\exp\left(-2n\left(\frac{b_{h}^{\lceil K^{\gamma}\rceil}(n)}{H-h-1}\right)^{2}\right),\qquad S^{(2)}_{K}\coloneqq\sum_{n=\lceil K^{\gamma}\rceil+1}^{K}\exp\left(-2n\left(\frac{b_{h}^{n}(n)}{H-h-1}\right)^{2}\right).

Notice that
S_{K}=S^{(1)}_{K}+S^{(2)}_{K},
and that

\displaystyle b_{h}^{\lceil K^{\gamma}\rceil}(n)=(H-h-1)\sqrt{\frac{0.5S\ln 2+\mu(\lceil K^{\gamma}\rceil+1)^{\alpha}}{n}},\quad b_{h}^{n}(n)=(H-h-1)\sqrt{\frac{0.5S\ln 2+\mu(n+1)^{\alpha}}{n}}.

We proceed by bounding
S_{K}^{(1)}
and
S_{K}^{(2)}
. We begin with
S_{K}^{(1)}
, for which we have

\displaystyle S_{K}^{(1)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{\lceil K^{\gamma}\rceil}\exp\left(-2n\left(\frac{0.5S\ln 2+\mu(\lceil K^{\gamma}\rceil+1)^{\alpha}}{n}\right)\right)\leq 2^{-S}\lceil K^{\gamma}\rceil\exp\left(-2\mu K^{\gamma\alpha}\right)

We next bound
S_{K}^{(2)}
:

\displaystyle S^{(2)}_{K}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=\lceil K^{\gamma}\rceil+1}^{K}\exp\left(-2n\left(\frac{b_{h}^{n}(n)}{H-h-1}\right)^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n=\lceil K^{\gamma}\rceil+1}^{K}2^{-S}\exp\left(-2\mu n^{\alpha}\right)

\displaystyle\leq 2^{-S}(K-\lceil K^{\gamma}\rceil)\exp\left(-2\mu K^{\gamma\alpha}\right).

Putting everything together, we obtain the desired result.
∎

Lemma 2
.

Consider an arbitrary transition kernel
\tilde{\bm{P}}=\{\tilde{P}_{h}\}_{h=0}^{H-2}
and reward function
\tilde{r}=\{\tilde{r}_{h}\}_{h=0}^{H-1}
.
Suppose that for all
(s,a,h)
,

\displaystyle\big\|\tilde{P}_{h}(\cdot\mid s,a)-P_{h}^{*}(\cdot\mid s,a)\big\|_{1}\leq\frac{1}{2H(H-h-1)}\texttt{gap}^{*},

\displaystyle\big|\tilde{r}_{h}(s,a)-r_{h}(s,a)\big|\leq\frac{1}{4H}\texttt{gap}^{*},

(8)

where the
Q^{*}
-value gap is defined as

\texttt{gap}^{*}\coloneqq\min_{(s,a,h)\in\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{A}\times[H],\,a\notin\mathcal{A}^{*}_{h}(s)}\left(Q_{h}^{*}(s,\pi^{*}_{h}(s))-Q_{h}^{*}(s,a)\right).

Furthermore, assume
\tilde{r}_{H-1}=r_{H-1}
. Then any optimal policy for the MDP with transition kernel and reward function
(\tilde{\bm{P}},\tilde{r})
belongs to
\mathbf{\Pi}^{*}
.

Proof of Lemma
2
.

Let
\mathcal{A}_{h}^{*}(s)
denote the set of optimal actions at state
s
and horizon
h
under the true MDP
(\bm{P}^{*},r)
, and define
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{h}^{*}(s)
analogously for
(\tilde{\bm{P}},\tilde{r})
.
We claim that
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{h}^{*}(s)\subseteq\mathcal{A}_{h}^{*}(s)
for all
(s,h)
; this implies that any optimal policy under
(\tilde{\bm{P}},\tilde{r})
is also optimal under
(\bm{P}^{*},r)
.

We prove this by induction. At
h=H-1
, we have
\tilde{r}_{H-1}=r_{H-1}
, and hence

\mathcal{A}_{H-1}^{*}(s)=\argmax_{a\in\mathcal{A}}r_{H-1}(s,a)=\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{H-1}^{*}(s),

Suppose
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{h^{\prime}}^{*}(s)\subseteq\mathcal{A}_{h^{\prime}}^{*}(s)
for all
s
and
h^{\prime}>h
.
We show that
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{h}^{*}(s)\subseteq\mathcal{A}_{h}^{*}(s)
.
Let
\tilde{\bm{\pi}}^{*}
be any optimal policy under
(\tilde{\bm{P}},\tilde{r})
.
By the induction hypothesis,
V_{h+1}^{\tilde{\bm{\pi}}^{*}}=V_{h+1}^{*}
, and hence
Q_{h}^{\tilde{\bm{\pi}}^{*}}(s,a)=Q_{h}^{*}(s,a)
for all
(s,a)
.
By definition,

\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{h}^{*}(s)=\argmax_{a}Q_{h}^{\tilde{\bm{\pi}}^{*},\tilde{\bm{P}},\tilde{r}}(s,a),

where
Q_{h}^{\tilde{\bm{\pi}}^{*},\tilde{\bm{P}},\tilde{r}}
corresponds to the
Q
-function associated with MDP with transition kernel and reward function
(\tilde{\bm{P}},\tilde{r})
. Now fix
a^{*}\in\mathcal{A}_{h}^{*}(s)
and
a\notin\mathcal{A}_{h}^{*}(s)
.
Then

\displaystyle Q_{h}^{\tilde{\bm{\pi}}^{*},\tilde{\bm{P}},\tilde{r}}(s,a^{*})-Q_{h}^{\tilde{\bm{\pi}}^{*},\tilde{\bm{P}},\tilde{r}}(s,a)

\displaystyle\qquad=[Q_{h}^{\tilde{\bm{\pi}}^{*},\tilde{\bm{P}},\tilde{r}}(s,a^{*})-Q_{h}^{*}(s,a^{*})]+[Q_{h}^{*}(s,a)-Q_{h}^{\tilde{\bm{\pi}}^{*},\tilde{\bm{P}},\tilde{r}}(s,a)]+[Q_{h}^{*}(s,a^{*})-Q_{h}^{*}(s,a)]

\displaystyle\qquad\geq-\big|Q_{h}^{\tilde{\bm{\pi}}^{*},\tilde{\bm{P}},\tilde{r}}(s,a^{*})-Q_{h}^{\tilde{\bm{\pi}}^{*}}(s,a^{*})\big|-\big|Q_{h}^{\tilde{\bm{\pi}}^{*},\tilde{\bm{P}},\tilde{r}}(s,a)-Q_{h}^{\tilde{\bm{\pi}}^{*}}(s,a)\big|+\texttt{gap}^{*},

(9)

where the last inequality uses the definition of
\texttt{gap}^{*}
and
Q_{h}^{\tilde{\bm{\pi}}^{*}}=Q_{h}^{*}
. It remains to bound the deviation in
Q
-values. Notice that for any deterministic Markov policy
\bm{\pi}
, we have

\displaystyle\big|Q_{h}^{\bm{\pi},\tilde{\bm{P}},\tilde{r}}(s,a)-Q_{h}^{\bm{\pi}}(s,a)\big|

\displaystyle\qquad=\left|(\tilde{r}_{h}(s,a)-r_{h}(s,a))+\sum_{s^{\prime}}\tilde{P}_{h}(s^{\prime}\mid s,a)V_{h+1}^{\bm{\pi},\tilde{\bm{P}},\tilde{r}}(s^{\prime})-\sum_{s^{\prime}}P_{h}^{*}(s^{\prime}\mid s,a)V_{h+1}^{\bm{\pi}}(s^{\prime})\right|

\displaystyle\qquad\leq|\tilde{r}_{h}(s,a)-r_{h}(s,a))|

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\sum_{s^{\prime}}\tilde{P}_{h}(s^{\prime}\mid s,a)\big|V_{h+1}^{\bm{\pi},\tilde{\bm{P}},\tilde{r}}(s^{\prime})-V_{h+1}^{\bm{\pi}}(s^{\prime})\big|+\left|\sum_{s^{\prime}}(\tilde{P}_{h}-P_{h}^{*})(s^{\prime}\mid s,a)V_{h+1}^{\bm{\pi}}(s^{\prime})\right|

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\frac{\texttt{gap}^{*}}{4H}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\sum_{s^{\prime}}\tilde{P}_{h}(s^{\prime}\mid s,a)\big|V_{h+1}^{\bm{\pi},\tilde{\bm{P}},\tilde{r}}(s^{\prime})-V_{h+1}^{\bm{\pi}}(s^{\prime})\big|+\left|\sum_{s^{\prime}}(\tilde{P}_{h}-P_{h}^{*})(s^{\prime}\mid s,a)V_{h+1}^{\bm{\pi}}(s^{\prime})\right|

(10)

To control the third term, we center
V_{h+1}^{\bm{\pi}}
by subtracting

c=\tfrac{1}{2}(\max_{x}V_{h+1}^{\bm{\pi}}(x)+\min_{x}V_{h+1}^{\bm{\pi}}(x))
, obtaining

\|V_{h+1}^{\bm{\pi}}-c\mathbf{1}\|_{\infty}=\tfrac{1}{2}\,\mathrm{span}(V_{h+1}^{\bm{\pi}})\leq\frac{1}{2}(H-h-1).

(11)

Hence,

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{s^{\prime}}(\tilde{P}_{h}-P_{h}^{*})(s^{\prime}\mid s,a)V_{h+1}^{\bm{\pi}}(s^{\prime})\right|

\displaystyle\leq\tfrac{1}{2}\|\tilde{P}_{h}(\cdot\mid s,a)-P_{h}^{*}(\cdot\mid s,a)\|_{1}(H-h-1)

\displaystyle\leq\,\frac{\texttt{gap}^{*}}{4H},

(12)

where the last inequality follows from (
8
). By a straightforward induction over
h
, noticing that
Q_{H-1}^{\bm{\pi},\tilde{\bm{P}},\tilde{r}}=Q_{H-1}^{\bm{\pi}}=r
, (
10
) and (
12
) yields

\displaystyle\big|Q_{h}^{\bm{\pi},\tilde{\bm{P}},\tilde{r}}(s,a)-Q_{h}^{\bm{\pi}}(s,a)\big|\leq\sum_{h^{\prime}=h}^{H-2}\,\frac{\texttt{gap}^{*}}{2H}.

(13)

Substituting in (
13
) in (
9
), we obtain

Q_{h}^{\tilde{\bm{\pi}}^{*},\tilde{\bm{P}},\tilde{r}}(s,a^{*})-Q_{h}^{\tilde{\bm{\pi}}^{*},\tilde{\bm{P}},\tilde{r}}(s,a)\geq\texttt{gap}^{*}-\sum_{h^{\prime}=h}^{H-2}\,\frac{\texttt{gap}^{*}}{H}=\texttt{gap}^{*}\frac{h+1}{H}>0.

The right-hand side is strictly positive, implying that all optimal actions under
(\tilde{\bm{P}},\tilde{r})
remain optimal under
(\bm{P}^{*},r)
. Thus
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{h}^{*}(s)\subseteq\mathcal{A}_{h}^{*}(s)
, completing the induction.
∎

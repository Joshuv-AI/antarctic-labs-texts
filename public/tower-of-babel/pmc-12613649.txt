Letter to editor: AAV-mediated STC-1 expression mitigates neuroinflammation and preserves visual function in degenerative retinopathy
Authors: Junyi Lou
Published: 2025

I read with great interest the preclinical study by Wang et al. [1] evaluating intravitreal AAV2/8-mediated Stc1 delivery in a sodium iodate–induced mouse of retinal degeneration, which targets oxidative stress and inflammation and reports structural and functional preservation with implications for gene therapy development in retinal diseases. Building on this valuable contribution, I respectfully note that particularly the absence of a quantitative dose–expression–efficacy framework and the timing of intervention relative to AAV expression kinetics—would benefit from clarification and additional analyses to enhance the rigor and translational relevance of the conclusions.

The vector and dosing scheme are described as a dual-promoter construct delivered intravitreally (pAAV2/8-CMV-Stc1-3xFLAG–EF1–GdGreen–WPRE) with a fixed injection volume of 2 µL at 2 h post-NaIO3. However, the manuscript does not report the viral titer or the total vector dose (vg/eye), and no dose-escalation groups were included. Expression was assessed only at 2 and 4 weeks post-injection. Immunofluorescence shows STC‑1 signals in PR, INL, and GCL, but layer-specific expression ratios and transduction efficiency were not quantified beyond representative images and a binary “present/absent” comparison. Western blotting reveals a ~ 26‑kDa band, yet per-animal STC‑1-to-housekeeper ratios, the coefficient of variation, and the distribution of individual expression levels are not provided. qRT‑PCR indicates increased Stc1 transcripts at day 14 and day 28 with an apparent upward trend, but this remains at the transcriptional level; neither protein concentrations nor absolute amounts in ocular compartments (vitreous/retina) were quantified over time. Efficacy readouts were not linked to expression on an individual basis. Although improvements are shown in morphology (ONL thickness, IS/OS length, TUNEL, DHE), function (mf‑ERG), and inflammation (CX3CL1/CX3CR1, p‑NF‑κB, IBA1, GFAP), the study does not present per-eye correlations between STC‑1 protein levels and these outcomes (e.g., STC‑1 WB vs ONL or mf‑ERG amplitude), nor does it establish a quantitative dose–expression–efficacy relationship. While the Discussion acknowledges missing pharmacology—optimal dose, route, and intervention window—no dose exploration or expression–efficacy analyses are presented in the main text. This gap materially weakens the translational inference. For intravitreal AAV gene therapy, a reproducible dose–expression–efficacy chain is essential to define the minimal effective dose, potential saturation, and the safety window, and to exclude scenarios in which benefit is driven by outlier high expressers rather than a scalable exposure–response [2, 3]. The manuscript concludes that STC‑1 expression correlates with photoreceptor viability, but without per-animal correlation analyses or a dose–response curve, this remains a group-level inference rather than quantitative evidence. Suggested minimal additions that would enhance this work include: Normalizing WB to β‑actin or GAPDH and report per-animal STC‑1 values (scatter-plus-box plots, mean ± SD, CV). For IF, acquire ≥ 3 ROIs per quadrant at a fixed radius from the optic disc with unified exposure and background subtraction; quantify mean intensity in ONL/INL/GCL and report layer-specific expression ratios and within-group variability. Correlation per eye STC‑1 protein with ONL thickness, IS/OS length, mf‑ERG P1 amplitudes by ring, and CX3CL1/CX3CR1 and p‑NF‑κB levels. For mf‑ERG (multiple rings/quadrants per eye), use a linear mixed-effects model: amplitude ~ STC‑1 expression + ring + group + interaction + (1|animal). If sample size is limited, at minimum provide scatter plots with regression lines and 95% CI, along with correlation coefficients and P values. Given multiple correlation tests, it will pre-specify a small set of primary, biologically motivated structure–function hypotheses and control the family-wise error rate using Bonferroni correction. Results outside the primary set will be labeled exploratory and interpreted cautiously with full disclosure of the number of tests. Keeping the 2‑µL volume constant, introduce low/medium/high viral titers around the working preparation, repeat the above quantifications, and construct dose–expression and dose–efficacy curves to estimate the MED and any plateau region.

In conclusion, while this study provides encouraging evidence that intravitreal AAV2/8-mediated Stc1 delivery mitigates NaIO3‑induced retinal degeneration through structural, functional, and inflammatory endpoints, future work should strengthen the exposure–response framework and align intervention timing with AAV expression kinetics. Quantitative, per‑animal expression analyses linked to efficacy, coupled with dose‑escalation data and early expression time‑courses, would yield more precise and generalizable estimates of therapeutic effect, thereby refining the conclusions and better informing the translational path for retinal gene therapy.

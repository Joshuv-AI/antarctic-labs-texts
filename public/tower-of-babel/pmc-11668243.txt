Single-Cell Multiomics Identifies Glycan Epitope LacNAc as a Potential Cell-Surface Effector Marker of Peripheral T Cells in Bladder Cancer Patients
Authors: Xiangyu Wu, Zihan Zhao, Wenhao Yu, Siyang Liu, Meng Zhou, Ning Jiang, Xiang Du, Xin Yang, Jinbang Chen, Hongqian Guo, Rong Yang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractCancer is a systemic disease continuously monitored and responded to by the human global immune system. Peripheral blood immune cells, integral to this surveillance, exhibit variable phenotypes during tumor progression. Glycosylation, as one of the most prevalent and significant post-translational modifications of proteins, plays a crucial role in immune system recognition and response. Glycan analysis has become a key method for biomarker discovery. LacNAc, a prominent glycosylation modification, regulates immune cell activity and function. Therefore, we applied our previously developed single-cell glycomic multiomics to analyze peripheral blood in cancer patients. This platform utilizes chemoenzymatic labeling with DNA barcodes for detecting and quantifying LacNAc levels at single-cell resolution without altering the transcriptional status of immune cells. For the first time, we systematically integrated single-cell transcriptome, T cell receptor (TCR) repertoire, and glycan epitope LacNAc analyses in tumor-patient-derived peripheral blood. Our integrated analysis reveals that lower-stage bladder cancer patients showed significantly higher levels of LacNAc in peripheral T cells, and peripheral T cells with high levels of cell-surface LacNAc exhibit higher cytotoxicity and TCR clonal expansion. In summary, we identified LacNAc as a potential cell-surface effector marker for peripheral T cells in bladder cancer patients, which enhances our understanding of peripheral immune cells and offers potential advancements in liquid biopsy.

Introduction

Cancer is a systemic disease that is monitored and eliminated by the global immune system. The role of the tumor microenvironment has been extensively studied, but the significant dynamic transformation in the global immune landscape beyond tumors is increasingly recognized as cancer progresses.1 Peripheral blood immune cells, crucial components of anticancer immunity, exhibit notable changes during cancer progression and treatment. For instance, expanded clonotypes found in the tumor and normal adjacent tissue can typically be detected in peripheral T cells, as well. More importantly, previous studies identified peripheral T cells that could predict prognosis and clinical response.2 Therefore, tracking alterations in peripheral immune cells could serve as an effective approach to detect cancers and predict immunotherapy response. However, peripheral immune cell populations are heterogeneous, and their functional states are multifaceted. Simply examining transcriptomics and the T cell receptor (TCR) repertoire of T cells drew a partial picture, given that immune cells are influenced by various factors, including metabolic reprogramming, epigenetics, and post-translational modifications.3,4 Among these, post-translational modifications, particularly glycosylation on the cell surface, which was previously challenging to decode, are now increasingly recognized for their significant role in influencing the functional status of immune cells.5−8

Glycan epitopes, while forming part of cellular architecture, also embody a rich tapestry of biological information.9−11 They can serve as the reporters for cell identities, owing to their sensitivity to the physiological status.12,13 It is noteworthy that cell-surface glycans have been identified as active participants in the regulation of immune signaling, and almost all key cell-surface receptors in the immune system are glycoproteins.5,6 Notably, glycans are crucial for T cell development, signaling, and differentiation,14,15 and have been validated as markers of distinct T cell functional states in cancer,16,17 underscoring their potential as cancer biomarkers. N-acetyllactosamine (LacNAc), a cell-surface glycan epitope, has been demonstrated as an indicator of the glycolytic activity of T cells.17 Besides, LacNAc treatment can restore the colocalization of TCR-CD8 or TCR-CD4 and effector activity of T cells.7,8 However, research into the role of LacNAc in immune cells has been cursory, and past constraints in traditional technology have impeded the simultaneous recognition and quantification of LacNAc levels.18

Advanced single-cell glycomic multiomics could potentially be the game-changer in this field. Although conventional single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) revolutionized the understanding of the immune cell populations in tumor immunology at the transcriptomic level,19−21 other molecular modalities are still secrets and failed to demonstrate the whole cell identity. Recent advances in single-cell multiomics technologies are paving the way toward goals with various dimensions of perspectives, including scTCR-, scBCR-, CITE-seq, Cellab-seq, and so on.22−25 For example, T-cell receptors (TCRs) play a crucial role in characterizing the expansion of T cells due to recognizing the peptide-MHC complexes (pMHC), thereby triggering the immune response.26 scTCR-seq provides the sequence information on TCRs. To advance the study of glycan epitopes in single-cell multiomics, we are expanding upon the chemoenzymatic method for LacNAc detection.27,28 We further reported that the platform in our previous research is compatible with commercial single-cell platforms for detecting LacNAc and can also integrate with other multiomics techniques.17

Building on this foundation, we conducted the study to comprehensively analyze peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs) from bladder cancer (BC) patients at various stages, focusing particularly on decoding the information carried by the glycan epitope LacNAc on peripheral blood immune cells, and we aim to improve our understanding of these cells and potentially expand the applicability of “liquid biopsy”.

BC is one of the most prevalent genitourinary malignancies, with its occurrence and mortality rates witnessing a recent rise.29 Approximately 75% of BC patients present with nonmuscle-invasive bladder cancer (NMIBC), which includes stages Ta and T1.30 A portion of NMIBC patients experience progression to muscle-invasive or metastatic bladder cancer (MMIBC) postsurgery, which includes stages T2–T4, often resulting in a dire prognosis despite treatments like radical cystectomy and platinum-based chemotherapy.31,32 Effectively interpreting the heterogeneity of immune cells in BC, particularly the differences among BC at various stages, could provide precise insights into tumor progression. We found that LacNAc levels on the surface of peripheral blood immune cells decrease with the tumor stage progression. Therefore, our approach could serve as a powerful tool for the large-scale detection of surface glycans on peripheral blood immune cells.2,33

In conclusion, LacNAc has been identified as a potential surface marker of peripheral T cells in bladder cancer patients, providing new insights into peripheral immune cell dynamics and opening avenues for advancements in liquid biopsy techniques.

Results and Discussion

Working Principle and Study Design

Single-cell technologies have propelled BC research forward at the transcriptomic,34 spatial transcriptomic,35 and epigenetic36 levels, showcasing the forces of single-cell omics. Recent advances also highlight the critical role of glycosylation in diseases, particularly in cancer progression and treatment.9 However, research on the specific glycan epitopes at the single-cell level is absent in BC research.

In our prior research, identifying LacNAc molecules on cellular surfaces was achieved using a chemoenzymatic approach, which leverages the substrate flexibility of the recombinant Helicobacter pylori α1,3-fucosyltransferase (1,3-FucT), allowing the transfer of fucose residue.27,28 1,3-FucT selectively labels most glycan structures containing LacNAc units, with varying efficiency across different LacNAc-containing structures. From the perspective of substrate specificity, N-glycans with Neu5Ac or Neu5Gc linked to the α6 position of Gal and O-glycans with polysaccharides (>disaccharides) linked to the β6 position of GlcNAc were not recognized. Moreover, although lectins can detect specific glycan structures containing LacNAc units,37 their recognition patterns differ from the chemoenzymatic labeling of LacNAc. In summary, 1,3-FucT allows the semiquantification of the LacNAc unit.17 We have advanced this method by incorporating GDP-fucose conjugated to single-stranded DNAs (GDP-Fucose-ssDNA, Figure 1), enhancing its applicability to scRNA-seq. Similar to the foundational principles of scRNA-seq, we have designed an ssDNA construct that includes a polymerase chain reaction (PCR) handle, a barcode, and a poly(A) tail tailored for the LacNAc library preparation. Once cells are lysed within their microwells, magnetic beads capture both LacNAc and mRNA with poly(T) tail sequences. After reverse transcription and amplification, the library construction for RNA and LacNAc was concurrently finished. This strategy is fully compatible with an established single-cell platform, facilitating the integrated exploration of glycan epitopes in cellular surfaces and single-cell multiomics17 (workflow in Figure 1A).

Schematic diagram of the principle of sequencing and study design. (A) An overview of the workflow for single-cell LacNAc-seq combined with scRNA-seq and scTCR-seq. (B) The characteristics and the management course of BC patients and the study design of experiment and analysis.

Using this method, we have quantified the LacNAc level together with transcriptomic and TCR data sets to profile the immune landscape of PBMC from different stages of bladder cancer patients. Briefly, 6 PBMC samples were collected from 5 patients at different stages of BC (Table S1), including one from whom PBMC samples were obtained before/after anti-PD-1. The single-cell suspension was obtained by sorting for CD45+ cells via flow cytometry (Figures 1B and S1A). As shown in Figure S1B, we successfully detected LacNAc labeling in PBMC samples by using flow cytometry.

Distinct Single-Cell Immune Landscape of BC Patients

We initially utilized the shared barcode to integrate RNA, TCR, and LacNAc data sets from all of the samples into a single Seurat object for subsequent analysis, following the normalized data and stringent quality control process, which included the filtration of doublets. Through visualizing by uniform manifold approximation and projection (UMAP), 19,637 immune cells were further clustered into 5 major cell types, including T cell, NK cell, dendritic cell (DC), mononuclear phagocytes (MPs), and B cell (Figure 2A,B). These specific cell types were defined by employing well-established marker genes, leveraging their similar expression patterns (Figures 2D, S2, and S3). The sample preference of each cell type was illustrated using the Ro/e index (Figure 2C), which represents the ratio of observed to randomly expected cell numbers, helping to account for technical variations in sample preference estimation.38 Intriguingly, T cells and MPs are the predominant cellular populations in BC, accounting for the majority of all cell types and displaying relatively even distribution across the various samples (Figures 2A,C and S4). This underscores the necessity for a more in-depth analysis of T cells and MPs.

Single-Cell LacNAc Status of BC Patients

In parallel, we quantified the LacNAc level and mapped it to its respective cells on the UMAP plot based on the compatibility of the LacNAc procedure with other omics approaches (Figure 2E). In BC patients, PBMCs demonstrated that significant heterogeneity was observed in the level of LacNAc, with MPs and DC displaying higher LacNAc levels, while T cells and NK cells exhibited lower levels (Figure 2F). Subsequently, we selected cells ranked in the top 20% (Lac.hi) and bottom 20% (Lac.low) according to LacNAc levels for differentially expressed genes (DEGs) analysis. The Gene Ontology (GO) pathway analysis enriched in Lac.hi is mainly associated with LacNAc-related synthesis processes, such as the glycolytic process through fructose-6-phosphate and the glycolytic process through glucose-6-phosphate (Figure 2G,H). As shown in Figure 2I, Lac.hi generally expressed higher levels of key genes and enzymes that are crucial for LacNAc biosynthesis, compared with Lac.low, including transcripts described below: HK1–3 (encoding Hexokinase 1–3), ENO1 (encoding α-enolase), TPI1 (encoding Triosephosphate isomerase), ADPGK (encoding ADP-dependent glucokinase), PFKL (encoding ATP-dependent 6-phosphofructokinase), PIGQ (encoding Phosphatidylinositol Glycan Anchor Biosynthesis Class Q, GPI1), PKM (encoding Pyruvate kinase), UAP1L1 (encoding UDP-N-acetylhexosamine pyrophosphorylase-like protein 1), B3GNT2 (encoding β-1,3-N-Acetylglucosaminyltransferase 2), MGAT1 (encoding α-1,3-mannosyl-glycoprotein 2-β-N-acetylglucosaminyltransferase), and B4GALT1 (encoding β-1,4-galactosyltransferase 1). These results strongly revealed the capacity of single-cell omics to accurately reflect underlying biological processes. More importantly, the activation of immune-related processes could be related to high LacNAc levels (Figure 2H).

Heterogeneity in High and Low LacNAc Levels in Each Cell Type

To further discover the underlying variations between LacNAc levels in different cell types, we compared Lac.hi and Lac.low cells within each cell type. Interestingly, clear variances were observed in T cells and MPs, whereas no obvious transcriptomic expression differences could be detected in NK cells, B cells, and DCs (Figure 3A). Furthermore, Lac.hi T cells were prominently associated with effector and cytotoxic phenotype (e.g., GZMA, NKG7, IFNG), which will be discussed in detail subsequently. In MPs, the expression of chemotaxis-related genes is notably elevated in Lac.hi cells, such as CCL3, CCL4, CXCL2, etc. (Figures 3B and S5). As expected, GO and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathway enrichment analyses indicated significant enrichment in pathways related to chemotaxis and cytokine–cytokine receptor interactions (Figures 3C and S5). These results collectively suggest that T cells and MPs with high levels of LacNAc may play a significant role in modulating the immune process in BC patients, warranting further investigation to explore.

T cell Immune Landscape with LacNAc in BC Patients

Given the correlation between the high LacNAc level and T cell kill-related genes in BC patients, along with the critical role of T cells in BC progression and treatment, we further conducted a comprehensive single-cell landscape of T cell gene expression. We subset T cells and further annotated them as effector, naïve, and proliferative (prolif) T cells based on functional gene markers (Figures 4A,C and S6). Notably, the results showed that the LacNAc level of naïve T cells was significantly lower than that of effector T cells and proliferative T cells, which may be attributed to the quiescent state of naïve T cells (p < 0.0001, Figure 4A,B). To further investigate the differences between Lac.hi and Lac.low, we also utilized the top 20% and bottom 20% to decrypt the transcriptome characteristics (Figure 4D). Effector and prolif T cells were mainly enriched in the Lac.hi group, while naïve T cells constituted the majority in Lac.low (Figure 4E). At the transcriptomic level, we found that genes associated with T cell activation and effector function (such as FCER1G, CCL5, IFNG, GZMH, etc.) were upregulated in Lac.hi, whereas genes indicative of immaturity (including CCR7, LEF1, and SELL) were enriched in Lac.low (Figure 4F). Consistently, GO analysis revealed that the pathways enriched in T cells with Lac.hi were predominantly associated with T-cell-mediated immune cytotoxic activation and proliferation (Figure 4G).

DC and MPs are typically recognized as antigen-presenting cells, and their role in antigen presentation is essential for the activation of T cells.26 CellChat is a widely used method for simulating intercellular communication, which predicts the interaction strength between cell subpopulations based on scRNA-seq39 Applying this method, we found that Lac.hi T cells showed stronger interactions with MPs, whereas those with lower levels of LacNAc did not, which could be one of the reasons for the more potent effector phenotype of Lac.hi T cells (Figure 4H).

Because effector T cells exhibit higher LacNAc levels compared to naïve T cells, we were prompted to further investigate the mechanisms regulating LacNAc levels in T cells. Given that glycan biosynthesis is influenced by cellular metabolism,40 we employed Gene Set Variation Analysis (GSVA),41 a widely used method for assessing pathway activity variation across a sample population, to calculate glycolysis pathway activity scores. As anticipated, Lac.hi T cells exhibit higher GSVA scores in various glucose metabolism pathways (Figure 4I) and demonstrate elevated expression levels of key genes that are essential for LacNAc biosynthesis (Figure 4J).

LacNAc May be Negatively Correlated with Tumor Progression in BC Patients

As the high-level LacNAc possesses potential effectors and activation of T cells, we next investigated the relationship between LacNAc and tumor progression. The UMAP plot illustrates the distribution of samples across different stages (Figure 5A), while the Ro/e analysis reveals the sample preference of T cell subpopulations (Figure 5B). We explored our investigation according to the staging of disease in the samples. Overall, LacNAc in NMIBC (<pT2) are significantly higher than those in MIBC (≥pT2), suggesting that high LacNAc level T cells are dramatically associated with the BC pathological tumor stage, namely, LacNAc decreased as the tumor stage progressed (p < 0.0001, Figure 5C). To explore the underlying mechanism of the significance, we examined the immune characteristics. LacNAc were found to have positive correlations with effector biomarkers, including GZMA (r = 0.12, p = 5.17e-28), IFNG (r = 0.10, p = 3.60e-19), and NKG7 (r = 0.11, p = 4.96e-23) (Figure 5D), indicating that the T cells with high LacNAc levels are significantly associated with cytotoxicity. This may explain why tumors at lower stages tend to exhibit higher LacNAc levels.

Taken together, these results strongly demonstrated that T cells with high LacNAc levels may serve as T cell activation markers in BC patients.

LacNAc on T cell is Associated with T cell Differentiation State and TCR Clonotype Expansion

As LacNAc is highly associated with the effector functional phenotype, we further map the clonotype expansion into the UMAP plot in combination with scTCR-seq (Figure 6A). Actually, the clonal expansion of TCR has been widely identified as mediating tumor cell cytotoxicity through TCR-pMHC interactions. In our scTCR-seq results, patients at different stages generally showed a trend of decreasing TCR clonal expansion with the progression of stages (Figure 6D). Additionally, patient 1, undergoing combination therapy with anti-PD1, showed a significant lesion reduction (Figure S8) and a notable increase in TCR clonotypes post-treatment (Figure 6D). Consistent with what was identified above, we found that clonally expanded T cells were notably more prevalent among Lac.hi T cells (Figure 6B). Moreover, T cells with clonal expansion, encompassing expanded, medium, and small clone categories, displayed significant differences in comparison to the single clone group in the LacNAc level. Nonetheless, among T cells with clonal expansion, no notable disparities were detected (Figures 6C and S7A,B). Through monocle-based pseudotime analysis, both LacNAc levels and clone expansion were observed to progress from naive T cells to those with effector phenotype capabilities (Figure 6E). These findings demonstrated that T cells undergoing clonal expansion were observed to have a high level of LacNAc.

Summary

With the rapid advancement in glycobiology, it becomes evident that glycan epitopes are of great significance in a variety of biological processes, such as signal transduction, immune responses, and cell differentiation.5,42,43 An in-depth exploration of their structure44 and metabolic origins45 has also been explored. Although recent advancements in glycosylation position on the cell surface directly demonstrate the potential value of glycans in living cells,46−48 the precise glycan recognition and quantitation of immune cells in human tumor samples at single-cell resolution is still lacking.49 Our previous work has reported a platform that can be integrated with existing single-cell technology, enabling the quantification of cell-surface LacNAc at the single-cell level. Herein, we first expanded our biochemical approaches17 to PBMCs from human tumor patients, uncovering intriguing phenomena related to glycobiology. These insights significantly enhance our understanding of glycoimmunology.

The production of glycan epitopes is a metabolic process. Recent studies have highlighted a crucial connection between the metabolic process of tumors and their progression.45,50,51 Within the Lac.hi cell populations, we observed pathway enrichment in glycolytic processes, including fructose-6-phosphate and glucose-6-phosphate pathways. Other LacNAc-related genes, including those involved in hexokinase pathways, UDP-GlcNAc biosynthesis, β-3-GlcNAc transferases, and B4-Galtransferases, were found to be more highly expressed in the Lac.hi cell population. This not only validates the success of our technique but also offers a potential approach to exploring cellular metabolism. More importantly, Lac.hi cell populations were significantly enriched in lymphocyte-mediated immune regulation pathways, fueling our enthusiasm to continue exploring according to cell types.

In MPs, we showed that the Lac.hi population is significantly enriched in pathways related to chemokines. Chemokines profoundly reorganize the tumor microenvironment.52 Recent advances have shown that chemokines in T-cell recruitment like CCL3, CCL4, and CCL5 are associated with the presence of T cells in tumors.53,54 Moreover, elevated secretion of chemokines by MPs, such as CCL5, has been proven to recruit cytotoxic T cells for antitumor activity.55,56 Taken together, MPs expressing high levels of LacNAc may recruit more effector T cells through chemokines to exert the antitumor effect.

Subsequently, we further investigated the LacNAc characteristics of peripheral T cells, finding that the Lac.hi T cell population exhibited enrichment in effector T cell types, cytotoxic genes, and pathways. Besides, we analyzed TCR clonotype expansion in BC patients from different stages. As expected, NMIBC exhibits greater TCR clonal expansion than those with MIBC, suggesting the stronger antitumor properties of T cells. Notably, the T1 stage BC patient was treated with ICI. Our scTCR-seq results indicated that post-treatment, there was a significant increase in TCR clonal expansion, demonstrating the pronounced efficacy of immunotherapy. Finally, we explored the potential relationship between LacNAc and peripheral T-cell differentiation. Surprisingly, LacNAc on peripheral T cells undergoes dynamic changes during the differentiation process, and these changes are significantly associated with the effector functions and clonotype expansion. In the early work, researchers employed gal-binding lectins for glycan detection, revealing that T cells undergo glycan remodeling following activation, with a specific upregulation of terminal galactose structures.57,58 Additionally, the peanut agglutinin high phenotype of activated T cells results from the de novo synthesis of CD45 glycans.58 In our study, we further advanced glycan detection by employing single-cell glycomics sequencing technology to analyze the surface glycans of immune cells derived from the PBMCs of cancer patients. Our results confirmed that LacNAc is a potential effector marker of T cells, and this mechanism may be linked to pathways associated with glycolysis.

To our knowledge, our study is the first to investigate the transcripts, immune repertoires, and LacNAc mapping at the single-cell level in the dynamic change of patient-derived immune cells. These findings collectively indicate that the observed correlation between the LacNAc level on peripheral T cells and T cell characteristics, including TCR expanded clones and effector phenotypes, suggests that LacNAc detection in peripheral T cells is a clinically potential tool. However, there are still limitations in the current data. Although our data show that LacNAc levels are higher in NMIBC compared to those in MIBC, suggesting a potential negative correlation between LacNAc levels and tumor progression, we were unable to obtain large-scale clinical data with direct LacNAc measurements to accurately assess its prognostic value. Although this approach holds clinical application prospects and underscores the potential of this approach for liquid biopsy applications,59 clinical cohorts with direct LacNAc measurements are still needed to validate the association of LacNAc with prognosis, immunotherapy efficacy.

In conclusion, we identified LacNAc as a potential cell-surface effector marker for peripheral T cells in BC patients. This discovery enhances our understanding of peripheral immune cells and paves the way for “liquid biopsy” advancements.

Method and Materials

Patients

This study included 5 BC patients at Nanjing Drum Tower Hospital (Nanjing, China) from August 2015 to April 2022. The pathological data of 5 patients with prognostic information are recorded in Table S1. This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of Nanjing Drum Tower Hospital (2021–394–01).

GDP-Fucose-ssDNA Synthesis and Labeling

The GDP-Fucose-ssDNA synthesis process is consistent with our previous work.17 In brief, GDP-Fucose-Am was produced from GDP-Fucose-Azide using Pd/C in MeOH/ddH2O. GDP-Fucose-Am was then added to NaHCO3, THF, and NHS-PEG4-Tz (Click Chemistry Tools) to create GDP-Fucose-Am-Tz. TCO-ssDNA was synthesized by reacting NH2-ssDNA (Sangon Co., Ltd., Shanghai.) and NHS-PEG4-TCO (Xi’an Confluore Biological Technology Co., Ltd.) in Borate Buffered Saline solution with DMSO for 2 h at final concentrations of 200 μM and 8 mM, respectively. TCO-ssDNA was purified using DNA adsorption columns (Baiaolaibo Co., Ltd., Beijing). Finally, GDP-Fucose-Am-Tz and TCO-ssDNA underwent a click reaction in a 1:1 ratio to synthesize GDP-Fucose-ssDNA. To ensure compatibility with the Singleron platform, the probe was designed as follows: TGTCAAGATGCTACCGTTCAGAGTAAGAGCCCGGCAAGAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAA*A*A.

Cells were resuspended in 1× PBS containing 50 μg/mL FucT, 20 mM Mg2+, 0.1 mg mL–1 salmon testes DNA, 0.1 μM Cy5-(dT)25, and 20 μM GDP-Fucose-ssDNA. Following the completion of GDP-Fucose-ssDNA labeling, the detection was carried out using an Agilent NovoCyte Quanteon flow cytometer.

Flow Cytometry

The PBMC fraction was isolated from peripheral blood by Ficoll-Paque density gradient separation and then prepared as single-cell suspensions, and Fc receptors were blocked with Human TruStain FcX (Biolegend, 422302) before staining with fluorescent antibodies. A Zombie Aqua Fixable Viability Kit was used to distinguish live/dead cells (Biolegend, 423102). Alexa Flour700-antihuman-CD45 was used to identify immune cells, and the gating strategy is shown in Figure S1A. The labeling of LacNAc (GDP-Fucose-ssDNA) was tested with APC-poly(dT)-Cy5, as shown in Figure S1B.

Single-Cell Library Production

Single-cell suspensions were captured using the microfluidic chip, and we used the Singleron single-cell RNA-seq approach (GEXSCOPE Single-Cell RNA-seq Kit, Singleron Biotechnologies, Nanjing, China), coupled with LacNAc-seq and scTCR-seq, allowing for the simultaneous detection of the single-cell transcriptome, LacNAc, and TCR clonotypes of PBMCs from bladder cancer patients. Our group previously developed LacNAcTag ssDNA sequences, which can be found in the previous article for details.17 The poly(A) tail of mRNAs, TCR region, and LacNAc of cells were captured by magnetic beads. In short, single-cell isolation, lysis, reverse transcription, and complementary DNA (cDNA) amplification were conducted based on the protocol. The construction of libraries for mRNA, LacNAcTags, and TCR was carried out using Singleron RNA-seq with feature barcoding and scTCR-seq protocols. Sequencing of the libraries was conducted using the Illumina HiSeq X platform (Illumina).

Single-Cell Data Generation

Total reads from FASTQ files were processed to generate gene expression matrices by the Singleron platform CeleScope (https://github.com/singleron-RD/CeleScope). We filtered invalid reads and then extracted cell barcodes and unique molecular identifiers (UMI). Prior to the mapping of reads to GRCh38, reads with poly(A) tails were trimmed. Subsequently, we grouped the same cell barcode, UMI, and gene to generate the number of UMIs per gene per cell.

The analysis of LacNAcTags was also performed using the Singleron platform CeleScope (multitag module) for UMI counting. Normalized analysis of LacNAc was performed using the following formula: Xi = log (Ci + 1). Xi represented log normalized data of the LacNAc and Ci represented the original UMI counts. The TCR repertoire data generated from the flv_trust4 assay within CeleScope, the scRepertoire R package was utilized for detailed analysis. The subsequent analysis was supported by Seurat packages in R. The combination of the transcriptome, TCR, and LacNAc was initially created into one Seurat object.

Quality Control and the Cell Type Annotation

The Seurat package within R was utilized to filter out low-quality data and perform quality control. We excluded cells with gene expression counts lower than 200 or higher than 2000. Additionally, cells with mitochondria content exceeding 20% were discarded. A total of 19637 immune cells were acquired for subsequent analysis. We normalized expression matrices using NormalizeData functions. We then employed the FindVariable function to identify the top 2000 variable genes and perform principal component analysis (PCA) and then visualized using UMAP. We conducted cell type annotation based on well-known marker genes, defining 6 major cell types. The results were manually examined according to DEGs generated by the FindMarkers function for each cell type.

Differential Expression Gene Analysis and Pathway Enrichment Analysis

All DEG analyses were conducted using the FindMarkers function within Seurat. The statistical approach utilized to identify DEGs relied on the Wilcoxon Rank Sum test. The genes were defined as differentially expressed with a log-fold-change threshold of 0.2. The DEGs identified in each cluster or specific cell population (e.g., Lac.hi population) were conducted through pathway enrichment analysis method, including GO and KEGG analysis using the ClusterProfiler R package.60

Preference Analysis

We identified the preference of cell types in various samples employing the Ro/e index (Ro/e = Observed/Expected), which was calculated as the observed ratio to expected cell numbers. The actual counts were represented by the observed cell numbers, and the expected cell numbers were analyzed by the chi-square test.

Cell–Cell Interaction Analysis

CellChat was utilized to analyze ligand–receptor pairs among cell types. We input gene expression data and cell type information into CellChat and selected the “Secreted Signaling” human database for subsequent analysis. Next, the ‘identifyOverExpressedGenes’ function was employed to infer overexpressed receptors and ligands in specific cell groups, facilitating the construction of the cell–cell communication network. The ‘min.cells’ parameter was set to 10 to identify a strong signal pathway.

Trajectory Analysis

To infer the potential trajectory in T cells, the R package Monocle was applied to reconstruct differentiation hierarchies within the T cell clusters.

Statistical Analysis

The Kolmogorov–Smirnov test was performed to analyze the statistical significance between groups. p < 0.05 was considered as a statistical difference. In all figures, ns, p > 0.05; * p < 0.05; ** p < 0.01; *** p < 0.001; **** p < 0.0001.

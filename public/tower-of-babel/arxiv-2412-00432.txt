1.
Introduction

1.1.
Rough differential equations

Suppose
X\in C^{\alpha}([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{d})
for a
fixed
\alpha\in(1/3,1/2]
and consider
the rough differential equation

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}Y=f(Y)\,\mathrm{d}X,

(1)

where
f
is a fixed function in
C^{\gamma-1}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
and
\gamma>\frac{1}{\alpha}\geqslant 2
.
Standard rough paths theory
[
3
, Ch. 4, 8]
informs us
that in order to make sense of this equation,
we must include additional information in
the form of the area

\int_{s}^{t}X_{s,r}\,\mathrm{d}X_{r}:=\mathbb{X}_{s,t},

satisfying
\sup_{s\not=t}|\mathbb{X}_{s,t}|/|t-s|^{2\alpha}<\infty

and Chen’s relation.
In this way, the augmented problem

\mathrm{d}Y=f(Y)\,\mathrm{d}{\bf X},\qquad{\bf X}:=(X,\mathbb{X})

can be understood via a sewing lemma
to be asking for
Y
such that

Y_{s,t}=\int_{s}^{t}f(Y_{r})\,\mathrm{d}{\bf X}_{r}:=\lim_{\left\|P\right\|\downarrow 0}\sum_{t_{i}\in P}f(Y_{t_{i}})X_{t_{i},t_{i+1}}+f(Y_{t_{i}})f^{\prime}(Y_{t_{i}})\mathbb{X}_{t_{i},t_{i+1}},

for every
s,t\in[0,T]
, and
P
are finite partitions
of
[s,t]
with mesh size
\leqslant\left\|P\right\|
.
The problem being thus understood,
it can be solved via Picard iterations. In this note, we
revisit the problem of existence of solutions to the
RDE (
1
) by considering a computational way
of coming to assign a meaning and a solution to
(
1
), similar to the classical paper

[
1
]
(see also
[
6
, Section 4]
).

1.2.
Operator splitting

One fruitful way
of constructing numerical schemes for
(partial) differential equations is known
as the
operator splitting
, or
fractional steps
method.
A careful introduction to this in the context
of deterministic PDEs can be found in
[
9
]
.
Consider a problem of the form

\frac{\mathrm{d}Y}{\mathrm{d}t}=\mathcal{A}(Y),

where
\mathcal{A}
is an operator
that can be deomposed into a sum of
simpler operators
\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{A}_{1}+\mathcal{A}_{2}
,
(often) exhibiting qualitatively different behaviours.
Let
\mathcal{S}_{t}
be the corresponding
solution operator so that a solution can be
written as
Y(t)=\mathcal{S}_{t}\overline{Y}

for an initial condition
\overline{Y}
.
For
j=1,2
, let
\mathcal{S}_{t}^{j}
be the solution
operator for the equation

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}Y^{j}}{\mathrm{d}t}=\mathcal{A}_{j}(Y^{j}).

It stands to reason that the solution
Y
can
be approximated by propagating the initial
condition
\overline{Y}
repeatedly using

\mathcal{S}_{{h}}^{2}\mathcal{S}_{{h}}^{1}

for small time steps
{h}
, culminating in a
Trotter formula of the form:

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{t}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\Big(\mathcal{S}_{t/n}^{2}\mathcal{S}_{t/n}^{1}\Big)^{n}.

Similar to a sewing lemma, such a formula
tells us that on small time steps,
the integral
\mathcal{S}_{{h}}\overline{Y}
of the full
problem can be well approximated by the
composed integral
\mathcal{S}_{{h}}^{2}\mathcal{S}_{{h}}^{1}\overline{Y}
.

Let
N=[T/h]
be a large integer.
For
0\leqslant j\leqslant N
, set
t_{j}:=j{h}
.
Turning again to the differential equation (
1
),
we can consider the following approximating system
which contains the second order information
Z
as
an interlacing step:

\displaystyle Y^{h,1}_{t}

\displaystyle=Y^{h,2}_{t_{j}^{-}}+f(Y^{h,1}_{t_{j}^{-}})X_{t_{j},t},\quad t\in(t_{j},t_{j+1}],

(2)

\displaystyle Y^{h,2}_{t}

\displaystyle=Y^{h,1}_{t_{j+1}}+Z(Y^{h,1}_{t_{j+1}})_{t_{j},t},\quad t\in[t_{j},t_{j+1}),

and appended with the initial condition
Y^{h,2}_{0}=\overline{Y}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
(Since the standard notation in rough paths
theory is to put two temporal subscripts to
indicate the difference
X_{t,s}=X(t)-X(s)
,
as well as for more general functions taking
in two temporal arguments, we emphasise that

Z
takes in three arguments, and
Z(Y_{t})_{r,s}

does not mean a difference
Z(Y_{t})_{s}-Z(Y_{t})_{r}
.)
For a functon
g
continuous on
(t_{j},t_{j+1})
,
let
g_{t_{j}^{+}}
denote the limit to
t_{j}
from above.
From the continuity of
X
, we find that

\displaystyle Y^{h,1}_{t_{j}^{+}}

\displaystyle=Y^{h,2}_{t_{j}^{-}},\qquad\text{and similarly, }\qquad Y^{h,2}_{t_{j}}=Y^{h,1}_{t_{j+1}},

assuming
Z(\cdot)_{t,t}\equiv 0
.

Associated with
Y^{h,i}
,
i=1,2
, is an
auxiliary solution
Y^{h}
that runs at twice the rate,
and has the advantage of being continuous at
the grid points
t_{j}
(at least, if
Z
is continuous
in its third argument). For
t\in(t_{j},t_{j+1}]
, we define:

\displaystyle Y^{h}_{t}:=\begin{cases}Y^{h,1}_{t_{j}+2(t-t_{j})}&t\in(t_{j},t_{j+1/2}]\\
Y^{h,2}_{t_{j}+2(t-t_{j+1/2})}&t\in(t_{j+1/2},t_{j+1}]\end{cases},

(3)

where
t_{j+1/2}:=t_{j}+\frac{1}{2}{h}
.
We have
Y^{h}_{t_{j}}=Y^{h,1}_{t_{j}^{+}}=Y^{h,2}_{t_{j}^{-}}
.
From the first equation of (
2
),

Y^{h,1}
is as smooth as
X

in the interior of each interval of the partition.
We can therefore hope that
Y^{h}
converges to a
function in
C^{\alpha}([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{d})
that can be
understood as a solution to (
1
).
Following
[
1
]
, we define solutions as follows:

Definition 1.1
.

A function
Y\in C^{\alpha}([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{d})
is a solution to
the RDE (
1
) if for any
0\leqslant s\leqslant t\leqslant T
,

\displaystyle Y_{s,t}-f(Y_{s})X_{s,t}-Z(Y_{s})_{s,t}=o(\left|t-s\right|).

We delineate algebraic and analytic conditions on
Z

under which
Y^{h}
indeed converges
and carry out the convergence argument in
Section
2
.

The application of the splitting method to stochastic
equations
\mathrm{d}u=F\,\mathrm{d}t+G\,\mathrm{d}W
has also been widely
studied (see, e.g.,
[
4
,
8
,
10
]
).
An intuitive way to split up the problem is to
consider
\mathrm{d}u=F\,\mathrm{d}t
and
\mathrm{d}u=G\,\mathrm{d}W
on separate
time-steps.

1.3.
Numerical analysis of RDEs

Computational methods, especially explicit
methods, of solving (
1
)
are very natural in light of the sewing lemma, and
have been studied by various authors, including recently,

[
2
,
12
]
. The associated literature
on computational methods for SDEs is vast.
In the standard reference
[
5
, Chapter 10]
,
a thorough study was made of local higher order
Euler methods. Our construction is reminescent of the
classical work
[
1
]
on which
[
5
, Chapter 10]

is also based. All of our calculations can be extended without
change to the case where
Y
takes values in

\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
X
takes values in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and
f

takes values in
\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}
.

In
[
1
]
, Davie constructed solutions to
(
1
) using a Milstein scheme, or a
second-order Euler scheme, using the area
\mathbb{X}
.
Following
[
11
]
, driving paths

X
of
p
-variation were considered in
[
1
]
,
with
2\leqslant p<3
. Davie showed the more refined
result that there is
uniqueness only holds generally for
f\in C^{p}
.
Two steps of a splitting scheme for ODEs
added together is a slightly shifted Milstein scheme,
interpolated between grid-points.
We offer a different argument of convergence
via the splitting scheme, which also provides a rate of convergence.

As earlier alluded to, our splitting scheme,
like Davie’s Milstein scheme, is explicit. As such it
cannot witness non-uniqueness when
\gamma<\frac{1}{\alpha}+1
.
It may of interest in subsequent work to analyse the
convergence of a splitting scheme that is implicit in
its first equation in subsequent work.

2.
Convergence and existence

2.1.
Assumptions on
Z

As indicated, the primary object of this note
is to use assumptions on
Z
to
close estimates and give us convergence
of approximating solutions to the operator
splitting scheme in
C^{\alpha}([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{d})

as
h\downarrow 0
. We house our
assumptions here and shall show their sufficiency
in forthcoming calculations.

(1)

For every
0\leqslant s\leqslant t\leqslant T
, uniformly in
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\displaystyle\left|Z(x)_{s,t}\right|\lesssim\left|t-s\right|^{2\alpha}.

(4)

(2)

For every
0\leqslant s\leqslant t\leqslant T
,
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\displaystyle\left|Z(x)_{s,t}-Z(y)_{s,t}\right|\lesssim\left|x-y\right|^{\gamma-2}\left|t-s\right|^{2\alpha}.

(5)

(3)

For every
0\leqslant s\leqslant u\leqslant t\leqslant T
, uniformly in
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
define
\delta Z(x)_{s,u,t}:=Z(x)_{s,t}-Z(x)_{s,u}-Z(x)_{u,t}
as usual;
we require

\displaystyle\Big|\delta Z(x)_{s,u,t}-\big(\nabla f(x)f(x)X_{s,u}\otimes X_{u,t}+\nabla f(x)Z(x)_{s,u}X_{u,t}\big)\Big|\lesssim\left|t-s\right|^{3\alpha}.

(6)

Traditionally,
Z(x)_{s,t}=f(x)\nabla f(x)\mathbb{X}_{s,t}
.
This is now replaced by the analytic conditions
(
4
) and (
5
).
The algebraic condition (
6
) matches
the expected coboundary operation

\delta Z_{s,u,t}
,
with

\displaystyle\delta Z_{s,u,t}=f(x)\nabla f(x)X_{s,u}\otimes X_{u,t},

being given by the first subinterval
[s,u]

on the time step
[s,t]
on the discrete level
[
7
]
.
Given the bound (
4
), the final term

\nabla f(x)Z(x)_{s,t}X_{u,t}
in the parentheses
on the left of
(
6
) is aesthetic.
Our calculations
shall reveal that in fact, we can slightly relax
2\alpha
in
the exponent of
\left|t-s\right|
on
the right of (
4
)
(and the
3\alpha
of (
6
)) above to

(\gamma-1)\alpha
(resp.,
\gamma\alpha
),
and still arrive at the conclusions of Lemma

2.1
, Corollary

2.2
, and Section
2.4
below.

2.2.

Y^{h/2}

Proposition 2.1
.

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\big|Y^{h}_{t}-Y^{h/2}_{t}\big|=O(h^{\gamma\alpha-1}).

(7)

Proof.

We will treat only the case
\gamma\leqslant 3
,

.
From (

\displaystyle Y^{h}_{t_{j+1}}=Y^{h}_{t_{j}}+f(Y^{h}_{t_{j}})X_{t_{j},t_{j+1}}+Z(Y^{h}_{t_{j+1/2}})_{t_{j},t_{j+1}}.

Since

\displaystyle Y^{h/2}_{t_{j+1}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\,\,+f(Y^{h/2}_{t_{j+1/2}})X_{t_{j+1/2},t_{j+1}}+Z(Y^{h/2}_{t_{j+3/4}})_{t_{j+1/2},t_{j+1}}.

Taking a difference gives us:

\displaystyle Y^{h}_{t_{j+1}}-Y^{h/2}_{t_{j+1}}=Y^{h}_{t_{j}}-Y^{h/2}_{t_{j}}+I_{1}+I_{2},

(8)

where

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle:=f(Y^{h}_{t_{j}})X_{t_{j},t_{j+1}}-f(Y^{h/2}_{t_{j}})X_{t_{j},t_{j+1/2}}-f(Y^{h/2}_{t_{j+1/2}})X_{t_{j+1/2},t_{j+1}},

\displaystyle:=Z(Y^{h}_{t_{j+1/2}})_{t_{j},t_{j+1}}-Z(Y^{h/2}_{t_{j+1/4}})_{t_{j},t_{j+1/2}}-Z(Y^{h/2}_{t_{j+3/4}})_{t_{j+1/2},t_{j+1}}.

(9)

We leave

Using the assumptions

\displaystyle=\big|\big(f(Y^{n}_{t_{j}})-f(Y^{n+1}_{t_{j}})\big)X_{s^{\prime},t^{\prime}}\big|\leqslant C2^{-(\gamma\alpha-1)n}\left|t-s\right|^{\alpha},

. Let us additionally set

.
The case

(and similarly,

) prevents

from being bounded by
\sim 2^{-(\gamma\alpha-1)n}
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}_{s,t}^{n}

\displaystyle=f(Y^{n}_{t_{j}})X_{s^{\prime},t^{\prime}}-Z(Y^{n+1}_{t_{j+1/4}})_{t_{j},t^{\prime\prime}}+Z(Y^{n+1}_{t_{j+1/4}})_{t_{j},s^{\prime\prime}}

\displaystyle=f(Y^{n}_{t_{j}})X_{s^{\prime},t^{\prime}}-Z(Y^{n+1}_{t_{j+1/4}})_{t_{j},t^{\prime\prime}}+Z(Y^{n+1}_{t_{j+1/4}})_{t_{j},s^{\prime\prime}}+Z(Y^{n+1}_{t_{j+1/4}})_{s^{\prime\prime},t^{\prime\prime}}

\displaystyle\quad\,\,\leqslant C|s-t|^{\gamma\alpha}+\big|f(Y^{n}_{t_{j}})X_{s^{\prime},t^{\prime}}\big|+\big|Z(Y^{n+1}_{t_{j+1/4}})_{s^{\prime\prime},t^{\prime\prime}}\big|

\displaystyle=\big|Z(Y^{n+1}_{t_{j+1/2}})_{t_{j},t^{\prime\prime}}-Z(Y^{n+1}_{t_{j+1/2}})_{t_{j},s^{\prime\prime}}

\displaystyle\qquad-Z(Y^{n+1}_{t_{j+3/4}})_{t_{j+1/2},t^{\prime\prime}}+Z(Y^{n+1}_{t_{j+3/4}})_{t_{j+1/2},s^{\prime\prime}}\big|

Any interval

can be decomposed

\displaystyle|\Delta Y_{\hat{\tau}_{\ell+1}}|\lesssim|\Delta Y_{\hat{\tau}_{\ell-1}}|\mathds{1}_{\tau_{\ell}\in\Lambda_{1}}+|\Delta Y_{\hat{\tau}_{\ell}}|\mathds{1}_{\tau_{\ell}\not\in\Lambda_{1}}+h^{2\alpha},

and in particular, the implicit constant is independent of
\ell
.

Proof.

Let us compute this bound for
\tau_{\ell}\in\mathcal{T}_{t}

and
\tau_{\ell+1}\in\mathcal{T}_{s}
. The remaining two cases: (i)

\tau_{\ell}\in\Lambda_{1}
, and (ii)
\tau_{\ell}\in\mathcal{T}_{s}\backslash(\Lambda_{0}\cup\Lambda_{1})
,
depend on similar calculations and
we will not belabour the point.

In the present case we can use the simplification

\tau_{\ell+1}-h/q=\tau_{\ell-1}\in\mathcal{T}_{s}
.
Using other properties of
\hat{\tau}
summarised
in (
18

\Delta Y_{\hat{\tau}_{\ell},\hat{\tau}_{\ell+1}}=G_{1}+G_{2},

where

\displaystyle G_{1}

\displaystyle:=\big(f(Y^{h}_{\tau_{\ell}})-f(Y^{h/q}_{\tau_{\ell-1}})\big)X_{\tau_{\ell},\tau_{\ell+1}},

\displaystyle G_{2}

\displaystyle:=Z(Y^{h}_{\tau_{\ell}-h/2})_{\tau_{\ell}-h,\tau_{\ell}}-Z(Y^{h}_{\tau_{\ell}-h/2})_{\tau_{\ell}-h,\tau_{\ell-1}}

\displaystyle\quad\,\,-Z(Y^{h/q}_{\tau_{\ell+1}-h/(2q)})_{\tau_{\ell-1},\tau_{\ell+1}}+Z(Y^{h/q}_{\tau_{\ell+1}-h/(2q)})_{\tau_{\ell},\tau_{\ell+1}}.

We consider only

\displaystyle=f(Y^{h})_{\hat{\tau}_{\ell},\tau_{\ell}}X_{\tau_{\ell},\tau_{\ell+1}}+\big(f(Y^{h}_{\hat{\tau}_{\ell}})-f(Y^{h/q}_{\hat{\tau}_{\ell}})\big)X_{\tau_{\ell},\tau_{\ell+1}}+f(Y^{h/q})_{\tau_{\ell-1},\hat{\tau}_{\ell}}X_{\tau_{\ell},\tau_{\ell+1}}

where the

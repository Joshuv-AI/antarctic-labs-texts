Systematical study of exotic 1 − ⁣ + 1^{-+} tetraquark spectroscopy
Kai Xu, Zheng Zhao, Nattapat Tagsinsit, Attaphon Kaewsnod, Ayut Limphirat, Christoph Herold, Yupeng Yan

Abstract
The masses of exotic quantum-number 1 − ⁣ + 1^{-+} compact tetraquark states are calculated in a constituent quark model (CQM), where Cornell-like potentials are employed as the central potentials, spin-spin and spin-orbit coupling derived from Breit-Fermi interaction are treated as hyperfine corrections, and model parameters are taken from previous works. The ground state 1 − ⁣ + 1^{-+} P-wave tetraquarks are predicted at 1.9, 4.2, and 6.6 GeV for the light, charmonium-like, and fully-charm sectors, respectively. The decay width ratios of 1 − ⁣ + 1^{-+} tetraquark states are calculated for possible strong two-body decay channels within the rearrangement mechanism, including ω ​ h 1 \omega h_{1} and η ​ f 1 \eta f_{1} for isospin I = 0 I=0 light tetraquarks; ρ ​ h 1 \rho h_{1} and π ​ f 1 \pi f_{1} for isospin I = 1 I=1 light tetraquarks; π / η + χ c ​ 1 \pi/\eta+\chi_{c1} and ρ / ω + h c \rho/\omega+h_{c} for charmonium-like tetraquarks; and η c ​ χ c ​ 1 \eta_{c}\chi_{c1} and J / ψ ​ h c J/\psi h_{c} for fully-charm tetraquarks. The theoretical results are compared with the observed exotic 1 − ⁣ + 1^{-+} states, and possible channels for observing 1 − ⁣ + 1^{-+} tetraquark states are also discussed. The work suggests that η 1 ​ ( 1855 ) \eta_{1}(1855) is unlikely to be a compact tetraquark state.



I
Introduction

Understanding the spectrum and internal structure of hadrons remains a central goal of quantum chromodynamics (QCD), and states beyond the conventional
q\bar{q}
mesons and
qqq
baryons, such as multiquark configurations and hybrids, are now an active frontier of experiment and theory.

Charmonium-like states with total angular momentum
J=1
have been widely searched over the last two decades and are promising P-wave tetraquark candidates. Several exotic states in the charmonium region, known as Y states with
J^{PC}=1^{--}
quantum numbers that mix with conventional
\psi
mesons, could be P-wave tetraquark candidates: Y(4230)
Aubert et al. 2005
, Y(4360)
Wang et al. 2007
, Y(4660)
Lees et al. 2014
, the more recent Y(4484)
Ablikim et al. 2022a
, and Y(4544)
Ablikim et al. 2024
.

In conventional mesons composed of a quark and an antiquark
q\bar{q}
, the quantum numbers
J^{PC}
follow simple rules: parity
P=(-1)^{L+1}
and charge conjugation
C=(-1)^{L+S}
, where L is orbital angular momentum and S is total spin
Navas et al. 2024
. These rules allow common states such as
1^{--}
(the
\rho
meson) or
2^{++}
(the
f_{2}
meson) but not
1^{-+}
. Thus, a
1^{-+}
state, once observed, is naturally considered to have a nonconventional structure, such as a hybrid meson, hadronic molecule, or tetraquark.

The experimental status of exotic
1^{-+}
states has advanced through complementary approaches. The existing data provide a foundation for evaluating their properties, which we briefly review below:

The VES (Vertex Spectrometer) experiment observed the exotic
\pi_{1}(1400)
state (
J^{PC}=1^{-+}
) in
\pi^{-}p\to\pi^{0}\eta n
decays at 100 GeV
Alde et al. 1988
, with a mass of
1406\pm 20
MeV and a width of
180\pm 30
MeV. The analysis of the
\eta\pi^{0}
channel showed a clear exotic signal inconsistent with conventional
q\bar{q}
states.

The
\pi(1600)
state was first observed by the Brookhaven E852 experiment
Adams et al. 1998
, which identified an exotic
1^{-+}
resonance in the
\rho\pi
channel. The analysis found a mass of
1593\pm 8^{+29}{-47}
MeV and a width of
168\pm 20^{+150}{-12}
MeV. Later, E852
Kuhn et al. 2004
observed two exotic
J^{PC}=1^{-+}
resonances in
\pi^{-}p\to f_{1}(1285)\pi^{-}p
collisions at 18 GeV: the known
\pi_{1}(1600)
with mass
M=1709\pm 24\pm 41
MeV and width
\Gamma=403\pm 80\pm 115
MeV, and a new
\pi_{1}(2015)
state at
M=2001\pm 30\pm 92
MeV with width
\Gamma=333\pm 52\pm 49
MeV.

In the updated PDG review,
\pi(1400)
and
\pi(1600)
are suggested to be a single state, as coupled-channel analyses favor one broad isovector resonance in the 1.4–1.6 GeV region
Navas et al. 2024
. Of the reported candidates, the
\pi_{1}(2015)
has only been observed by the E852 experiment
Kuhn et al. 2004
, and its existence remains inconclusive.

The BESIII Collaboration recently observed the isoscalar exotic state
\eta_{1}(1855)
with
J^{PC}=1^{-+}
in
J/\psi\to\gamma\eta\eta^{\prime}
decays
Ablikim et al. 2022b
, measuring its mass as
(1855\pm 9^{+6}_{-1})
MeV, its width as
(188\pm 18^{+3}_{-8})
MeV with
>19\sigma
significance, and the product branching fraction
\mathcal{B}(J/\psi\to\gamma\eta_{1}(1855))\times\mathcal{B}(\eta_{1}(1855)\to\eta\eta^{\prime})=(2.70\pm 0.41^{+0.16}_{-0.35})\times 10^{-6}
. The narrow width of
\eta_{1}(1855)
contrasts sharply with the broad
\pi_{1}(1600)
, suggesting different underlying dynamics between these exotic states.

In this work, we study the mass spectrum and decay properties of the
1^{-+}
P-wave light, charmonium-like, and fully-charm compact tetraquark states within a constituent quark model framework.

The paper is organized as follows. In Sec.
II
, all possible configurations of the color, spin, flavor, and spatial degrees of freedom of tetraquark states are introduced, and a constituent quark model developed in our previous work
Zhao et al. 2021
;
Zhao et al. 2022
;
Zhao et al. 2025
is briefly reviewed. In Sec.
III
, the
1^{-+}
P-wave tetraquark mass spectra and decay branching ratios are evaluated within the constituent quark model. The theoretical results are compared with experimental data, and discussions of
\pi_{1}(2015)
and
\eta_{1}(1855)
are provided. A summary is given in Sec.
IV
.

II.2
Hamiltonian

We employ a widely used nonrelativistic Hamiltonian
Wang et al. 2021
;
Wu and Zhu 2025
;
Zhao et al. 2025
to study meson and tetraquark systems, which takes the following form:

\displaystyle H=

\displaystyle H_{0}+H_{so},

\displaystyle H_{0}=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{N}(\frac{1}{2}M^{ave}_{k}+\frac{p_{k}^{2}}{2m{k}})

\displaystyle+\sum_{i<j}^{N}(-\frac{3}{16}\vec{\lambda}_{i}\cdot\vec{\lambda}_{j})(V_{0}(r_{ij})+V_{ss}(r_{ij})),

\displaystyle H_{so}=

\displaystyle\sum_{i<j}^{N}(-\frac{3}{16}\vec{\lambda}_{i}\cdot\vec{\lambda}_{j})(V_{so}(r_{ij})),

(8)

where
M^{ave}_{k}
denotes the spin-averaged mass as
\frac{1}{4}M_{PS}+\frac{3}{4}M_{V}
.
m_{k}
are the constituent quark masses.
\vec{\lambda}_{i}
are the quark color operator in SU(3). The central potential
V_{0}(r_{ij})
, the spin-spin interaction
V_{ss}(r_{ij})
and spin-orbital interaction
V_{so}(r_{ij})
are taking the form:

\displaystyle V_{0}

\displaystyle=A_{ij}r_{ij}-\frac{B_{ij}}{r_{ij}},

\displaystyle V_{ss}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{6m_{i}m_{j}}\Delta V_{V}(r)\vec{\sigma}_{i}\cdot\vec{\sigma}_{j},

\displaystyle V_{so}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{r_{ij}}\frac{dV_{V}}{d(r_{ij})}\frac{1}{4}\Bigg[\left(\frac{1}{m_{i}^{2}}+\frac{1}{m_{j}^{2}}+\frac{4}{m_{i}m_{j}}\right)\vec{L_{ij}}\cdot\vec{S_{ij}}

\displaystyle\quad+\left(\frac{1}{m_{i}^{2}}-\frac{1}{m_{j}^{2}}\right)\vec{L_{ij}}\cdot\left(\vec{s_{i}}-\vec{s_{j}}\right)\Bigg]

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{1}{r_{ij}}\frac{dV_{S}}{d(r_{ij})}\left(\frac{\vec{L_{ij}}\cdot\vec{s_{i}}}{2m_{i}^{2}}+\frac{\vec{L_{ij}}\cdot\vec{s_{i}}}{m_{j}^{2}}\right),

(9)

where
\vec{\sigma}_{i}
are the quark spin operator in SU(2). Note that we have employed
V_{V}(r)=-BErf[\sigma r]/r
and
V_{S}(r)=Ar
, taken from Ref.
Schoberl 1986
.
m_{i}
and
m_{j}
are constituent quark masses of ith and jth quark.

\vec{s_{i}}
represents the spin operator for ith quark.
\vec{S_{ij}}=\vec{s_{i}}+\vec{s_{j}}
is the spin operator for (ij)th quark pair.
\vec{L_{ij}}
is relative orbital operator, taking the form,
\vec{L_{ij}}=\vec{r_{ij}}\times\vec{p_{ij}}
, where
\vec{p_{ij}}=\frac{m_{i}\vec{p_{i}}-m_{j}\vec{p_{j}}}{m_{i}+m_{j}}
.

Zhao et al. 2021
;
Zhao et al. 2022
;
Zhao et al. 2025
,
A_{ij}
,
B_{ij}
, and
\sigma_{ij}
are proposed to be mass dependent coupling parameters, taking the form

\displaystyle A_{ij}=a+bm_{ij},\;\;B_{ij}=B_{0}\sqrt{\frac{1}{m_{ij}}},\;\;\sigma_{ij}=\sigma_{0}{m_{ij}}.

(10)

with
m_{ij}
being the reduced mass of
i
th and
j
th quarks, defined as
m_{ij}=\frac{2m_{i}m_{j}}{m_{i}+m_{j}}
.

,

,
B_{0}
, and
\sigma_{0}
are constants.

The central potential
V_{0}
and the spin-spin interaction
V_{ss}
are treated as leading effects, while the spin-orbit interaction
V_{so}
is regarded as a perturbation that shifts the spectrum. The Schrödinger equation is solved for mesons and tetraquarks with the Hamiltonian
H_{0}
, yielding the eigenvalue
E_{0}
and eigenstates
\psi_{0}

The coupling constants

,
b
,
B_{0}
, and
\sigma_{0}
, as well as the constituent quark masses

III.3
\eta_{1}(1855)

In 2022, the BESIII Collaboration reported the discovery of a new isoscalar exotic state,
\eta_{1}(1855)

IV
Summary

While the hybrid interpretation has been extensively explored in the
literature, the tetraquark scenario remains particularly
compelling due to the rich internal dynamics inherent to
four-quark systems.

The masses and decay branching ratio of
1^{-+}
light, charmonium-like, and fully-charm tetraquark states are studied using a constituent quark model (CQM) that incorporates a Cornell-like potential and the Breit-Fermi interaction. The theoretical results for tetraquarks are compared with those of
1^{-+}
exotic states.

Impact of postoperative intraocular pressure elevation on graft endothelial cells in non-preexisting glaucoma eyes undergoing descemet membrane endothelial keratoplasty: a cohort study
Authors: Zi-jun Xie, Qing-yang Wu, Jing-hao Qu, Ji-yu Lin, Jing Hong
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundDescemet membrane endothelial keratoplasty (DMEK) has become the dominant keratoplasty procedure. However, the impact of high intraocular pressure (IOP) on the DMEK prognosis in patients without preexisting glaucoma remains unknown.MethodsNon-glaucoma patients who underwent DMEK in Peking University Third Hospital between July 2017 and March 2023 with a follow-up duration longer than six months were included in this cohort study. Eyes were divided into three groups: Group A) normal IOP; Group B) early IOP elevation (IOP ≥ 30 mmHg or increase of more than 10 mmHg from baseline within 3 days); Group C) intermediate-term IOP elevation (IOP > 21 mmHg or increase of more than 10 mmHg from baseline after 14 days postoperatively). The postoperative IOP, endothelial cell density (ECD), central corneal thickness (CCT), best-corrected visual acuity (BCVA) and rate of graft failure were analysed.ResultsForty-seven eyes from forty-seven patients were included. Thirty-seven eyes were bullous keratopathy, and ten were Fuchs endothelial corneal dystrophy. Twenty-five eyes were classified as Group A, six as Group B and sixteen as Group C. The mean peak IOP was 49.00 ± 4.99 mmHg in Group B eyes and 31.89 ± 11.75 mmHg in Group C eyes. The postoperative BCVA significantly differed from that before surgery (P < 0.001). The ECD at 3 months after surgery in eyes with intermediate-term IOP elevation was lower (P = 0.032). Four eyes with intermediate-term IOP elevation developed graft failure (P = 0.001).ConclusionsIntermediate-term IOP elevation after DMEK may reduce the graft ECD and lead to graft failure within six months after surgery. However, early IOP elevation had no effect on the prognosis. Careful IOP monitoring and intermediate-term IOP management should be conducted for graft protection.Keywords: Descemet membrane endothelial keratoplasty, Ocular hypertension, Intraocular pressure elevation, Corneal endothelial cell

Background

Corneal disease is one of the leading causes of blindness, and the dominant treatment option is keratoplasty. In recent years, endothelial keratoplasty (EK), especially Descemet membrane endothelial keratoplasty (DMEK) has become the main keratoplasty techniques [1–3]. Compared to full-thickness transplantation, DMEK has fewer intraoperative complications and faster postoperative visual recovery, with a significant reduction in the need for postoperative corticosteroid eye drops [4, 5]. Besides, the incidence of allograft rejection after DMEK is extremely rare, which is the main reason for graft failure after traditional full-thickness keratoplasty [6, 7]. However, intraocular pressure (IOP) elevation remains to be a major postoperative complication subsequent to DMEK, which may lead to secondary graft failure [4, 8]. Yet the impact of IOP elevation on the grafts after DMEK is still unclear. The correlation between ocular hypertension and corneal endothelial cell loss is a subject of ongoing debate. It is generally believed that high IOP may be associated with increased endothelial cell loss rate and abnormal endothelial function and ultimately leads to endothelial failure [9–13]. However, the grafted endothelium is believed to be more susceptible to damage caused by high IOP [14]. Some study results have questioned this association. Studies on glaucoma populations have shown that the endothelial cell density (ECD) in subacute primary angle-closure glaucoma (PACG) eyes and the fellow eyes of all PACG subtypes is not significantly different from that of the normal population [13, 15]. In particular, there was no statistical difference in ECD between the acute PACG eye and the contralateral eye without acute glaucoma history [12]. In addition, studies on the prognosis of penetrating keratoplasty have shown that elevated IOP does not affect the endothelial cell count or even increases it in the follow-up period [16–20]. Therefore, it is necessary to further study the impact of ocular hypertension on the function and survival prognosis of endothelial grafts following DMEK to further optimize the postoperative management system.

However, there is less research on the impact of high IOP on the prognosis of DMEK grafts. Immediate IOP elevation after DMEK is believed to have no impact on the endothelial graft [21, 22]. Studies conducted in glaucoma patient populations and animals have identified the duration of high IOP as the most important factor in endothelial cell loss, but the long-term impact of sustained IOP elevation on DMEK outcomes is not clear [12, 23]. The key role of a history of glaucoma surgery as a risk factor for graft rejection and failure after DMEK is recognized, but the significance of postoperative long-term IOP elevation in patients without a history of glaucoma surgery is still unclear [4].

In this retrospective study, we aim to study the prognosis of early and intermediate-term IOP elevation in non-glaucoma patients after DMEK. We mainly focused on two indicators, ECD and central corneal thickness (CCT), and analysed the impact of IOP elevation on graft failure to explore the effect on the prognosis of DMEK.

Methods

Patients

This retrospective study was conducted at Peking University Third Hospital. Patients who underwent DMEK between July 2017 and March 2023 at our hospital and had a follow-up period of more than 6 months were included. The surgical indications were bullous keratopathy (BK) or Fuchs endothelial corneal dystrophy (FECD). All diagnoses were made by an experienced ophthalmologist. The exclusion criteria were as follows: a) patients with a history of eye surgery other than cataract surgery or DMEK surgery prior to surgery; b) patients with a preoperative diagnosis of glaucoma or a diagnosis of glaucoma in the contralateral eye; c) patients with intraocular inflammation; d) patients who had undergone other surgeries in combination with DMEK; and e) patients with a high postoperative IOP that could be reduced to normal levels within a few days by reducing the use of corticosteroid eye drops.

The data collected included sex, age at surgery, medical history, surgical details, postoperative IOP, ECD, CCT, best-corrected visual acuity (BCVA) before surgery and at each follow-up time point (1 week, 1 month, 3 months, 6 months, 12 months) and the postoperative management procedure.

The study was conducted in accordance with the tenets of the Declaration of Helsinki and was approved by the Institutional Review Board of the Peking University Third Hospital Medical Science Research Ethics Committee (No. M2021283).

Criteria

Early IOP elevation was defined as an IOP of ≥ 30 mmHg within 3 days postoperatively or an increase of more than 10 mmHg from the preoperative baseline IOP. Intermediate-term IOP elevation was defined as an IOP of > 21 mmHg or an increase of more than 10 mmHg from the baseline IOP at any time point after 14 days postoperatively. There was no new occurrence of IOP elevation during the period of 3 days to 14 days after surgery in this study.

The affected eyes were categorized into three groups according to the IOP and time point of abnormal IOP occurrence: Group A: eyes with neither early nor intermediate-term IOP elevation; Group B: eyes with early IOP elevation without intermediate-term IOP elevation; Group C: eyes with intermediate-term IOP elevation.

Graft failure was defined as an irreversible increase in corneal edema with or without a decrease in the ECD.

Graft preparation and surgical technique

All the grafts were obtained from the Eye Bank of Peking University Third Hospital. The ECD of the graft was measured via an EB-3000 XYZ Eye Bank specular microscope (HAI Laboratories, Lexington, Massachusetts, USA) the day before surgery.

All the DMEK procedures were performed by the same experienced ophthalmologist (Jing Hong). The surgeon used trypan blue to stain the graft surface for 1 min under a microscope and carefully peeled off the entire donor corneal endothelial layer.

The DMEK surgery was performed under local anaesthesia. The patient underwent retrobulbar anaesthesia, and the depth of anaesthesia was checked before starting the surgery. If necessary, the swollen corneal epithelial layer was scraped to obtain a good operative field. The curved donor graft was injected into the anterior chamber after a limbal incision was made. Next, the graft was flattened by gently tapping the corneal surface, and sterilized air was injected under the graft to fill 80–90% of the anterior chamber. Graft attachment was ensured via intraoperative anterior segment optical coherence tomography (AS-OCT) (OPMI LUMERA 700, ZEISS, Germany). The patient was strictly bedridden for 4 h postsurgically. The graft was checked daily through a slit lamp. The patient was discharged when an AS-OCT (CASIA 2, TOMEY, Japan) examination confirmed that the graft was attached to the stroma and exhibited no air bubbles. Patients were usually discharged 3 days after surgery.

Postoperative IOP measurement

An iCare tonometer (iCare Finland Oy, Helsinki, Finland) was used to measure the IOP. During measurement, the tonometer needle touched the central cornea, and the average value of six repeated measurements was recorded. The IOP was measured in the upright position 4 h after surgery and then once daily during hospitalization and at outpatient follow-up visits. The IOP was immediately measured if the patient complained of eye pain, headache, or nausea during hospitalization.

Postoperative IOP management

If the IOP was found to be elevated immediately postoperatively (≥ 30 mmHg), the patient was instructed to lie in a semireclined position for 30 min and have their IOP rechecked. If the remeasured IOP was abnormally high (≥ 30 mmHg), the eye was checked under a slit lamp for the presence of pupillary blockage, and gas or fluid was released through the surgical incision. If the IOP did not return to normal after anterior chamber paracentesis, topical or systemic ocular hypotensive medication was applied.

If the IOP increased during follow-up after surgery, the use of corticosteroid hormones was reduced, and the patient was observed for several days. If the IOP remained high, systemic or topical ocular hypotensive medication was administered. If an acute increase in the IOP occurred, the patient was instructed to visit the emergency department for immediate treatment and to receive systemic or topical medication.

Postoperative management

The patients used 1% prednisolone acetate eye drops 4 times a day after surgery, and each consecutive month, the frequency was reduced by 1 application per day. After 4 months, 1% prednisolone was switched to 0.1% fluorometholone eye drops, which were used for 6 months. Betaxolol eye drops and bacitracin eye drops were used for 1 month after surgery. One percent cyclosporine A eye drops were applied 4 times a day, and each consecutive month, the frequency was reduced by 1 application per day. After the first 3 months, the daily use of 1% cyclosporine A eye drops was continued for 3 additional months.

Follow-up examinations

Each patient was followed up in the clinic at 1 week, 1 month, 3 months, 6 months, and 1 year after surgery. During the follow-ups, the patient's visual acuity and IOP were measured. Slit lamp examinations, in vivo confocal microscopy (HRT III, Heidelberg Engineering, Heidelberg, Germany), and AS-OCT were used to assess the status of the endothelial graft.

For eyes with IOP elevation, AS-OCT and ultrasound biomicroscopy were used to identify peripheral anterior synechia. Besides, these eyes will be followed up one week after the initial presentation of IOP elevation, and subsequently at least once a month, with increased frequency if necessary.

Statistical methods

IBM SPSS Statistics for Windows, version 27.0 (IBMCorp., Armonk, N.Y., USA), was used for all data analyses except for the survival analysis. Quantitative variables were presented herein as the means ± Standard Deviations if normally distributed or as the medians (first quartile, third quartile) if not normally distributed, whereas categorical variables were presented as numbers (percentages). The Kolmogorov‒Smirnov test was used to assess the distribution of the data. The Mann‒Whitney U test was used to analyse ranked data and nonnormally distributed data, whereas the t test was used for normally distributed data. The chi-square test was used for comparisons between categorical data. The Kaplan‒Meier method was used for survival curve construction. A log-rank test was performed for statistical comparisons. The logistic regression analysis was performed for identification of risk factors for graft failure, and forward selection (likelihood ratio) was applied. P values of < 0.05 and < 0.01 were considered to indicate statistical significance and extreme statistical significance, respectively.

Results

Study population

A total of 47 eyes from 47 patients were included. There were 21 male patients and 26 female patients. The average age at surgery was 64.15 ± 14.41 years (59.76 ± 11.93 years for males and 67.69 ± 15.47 years for females (P = 0.060)). Thirty-seven (78.7%) of the eyes had BK, and 10 (21.3%) of the eyes had FECD. Eight (8/37, 21.6%) BK eyes and one (1/10, 10.0%) FECD eye had undergone previous DMEK and needed repeat keratoplasty due to secondary endothelial keratoplasty graft failure (P = 0.660). Twenty-eight (28/37, 75.7%) BK eyes and five (5/10, 50.0%) FECD eyes had undergone previous phacoemulsification combined with artificial lens implantation (P = 0.137). The median preoperative BCVA was 1.30 (0.92, 1.70).

The median follow-up time was 12.0 months (7.0, 19.0). During the follow-up period, 22 (46.8%) eyes developed IOP elevation after surgery. None of these eyes exhibited peripheral anterior synechia. A total of ten (21.3%) eyes had early IOP elevation. Six (12.8%) eyes only had early IOP elevation (Group B). Sixteen (34.0%) eyes developed intermediate-term IOP elevation (Group C), four (8.51%) of which exhibited early IOP elevation. The demographic details of each group are listed in Table 1. And the clinical details of group C are listed in Table 2. The age at surgery was normally distributed, whereas the follow-up duration was not normally distributed.

Clinical features of patients with intermediate-term IOP elevation

Early IOP elevation

For the ten eyes with early IOP elevation (Group B and the four eyes with early IOP elevation in Group C), the mean peak IOP at the early IOP elevation period was 49.00 ± 4.99 mmHg, and this elevation occurred a median of four hours after surgery (4.00, 8.25). All eyes underwent gas release through paracentesis of the anterior chamber, and the instant IOP after the procedure was 14.2 ± 1.51 mmHg. Topical drugs were applied to eight of the ten eyes for IOP reduction, and for three of these eyes, both topical and systemic ocular hypotensive drugs were administered. The four eyes in Group C needed continuous IOP management, which is described in the next section.

The peak IOP and IOP after paracentesis of the anterior chamber were normally distributed.

Intermediate-term IOP elevation

For the sixteen eyes that developed intermediate-term IOP elevation (Groups C), the elevation occurred at a median of three (2.00, 6.00) months after surgery. The average peak IOP was 31.89 ± 11.75 mmHg. Both the concentration and frequency of use of the topical steroid eye drops decreased when IOP elevation was observed. Topical ocular hypotensive drugs were applied for further IOP control. In addition, for two eyes, systemic therapy as well as paracentesis of the anterior chamber was applied to decrease the IOP due to acute ocular hypertension. All eyes with intermediate-term IOP elevation in this study received topical drug treatment for more than two months. In two eyes, the IOP was uncontrollable after drug therapy, and these patients were advised to receive antiglaucoma surgery. One of the two eyes had continuous IOP elevation since the early postoperative period.

Comparison of the CCT, ECD and BCVA in each group

The median postoperative CCT of all eyes included at one week, one month, three months, six months and twelve months was 606.5 μm (521.5, 749.25), 512.5 μm (474.5, 610.75), 507.5 μm (476.0, 624.25), 516.5 μm (480.0, 543.0) and 514.0 μm (487.0, 551.5), respectively. For eyes with elevated IOP, the CCTs at one week, one month, three months, six months and twelve months after surgery were 613.0 μm (533.0, 655.0) (P = 0.860), 525.0 μm (467.5, 649.0) (P = 0.644), 544.0 μm (476.0, 651.0) (P = 0.421), 517.0 μm (483.75, 643.0) (P = 0.104), and 507.0 μm (466.0, 597.0) (P = 0.972), respectively.

The ECD of the donor tissue for the involved eyes was 3080.0 cells/mm2 (3000.0, 3207.75) and did not significantly differ among the various groups (P = 0.585). The postoperative ECD of all the included eyes at one, three, six and twelve months was 1470.65 ± 590.77 cells/mm2, 1199.30 ± 626.23 cells/mm2, 1055.0 cells/mm2 (680.0, 1773.5) and 946.0 cells/mm2 (595.0, 1605.0), respectively. For eyes with elevated IOP, the ECD at one, three, six and twelve months postsurgically was 1372.73 ± 707.03 cells/mm2 (P = 0.520), 934.17 ± 589.99 cells/mm2 (P = 0.020), 892.5 cells/mm2 (572.25, 1490.75) (P = 0.178) and 659.0 cells/mm2 (493.0, 1160.0) (P = 0.135), respectively.

The median postoperative BCVA was better than the baseline BCVA: 0.70 (0.60, 1.10) at one week (P < 0.001), 0.60 (0.40, 0.92) at one month (P < 0.001), 0.52 (0.30, 0.70) at three months (P < 0.001), 0.22 (0.16, 0.52) at six months (P < 0.001), and 0.30 (0.22, 0.82) at twelve months (P < 0.001) after surgery.

However, the CCTs, ECDs and BCVAs of eyes with continuous IOP elevation from the immediate period in Group C were not significantly different from other eyes in Group C (P > 0.05).

Graft failure rate

Graft failure occurred in 4 eyes during the follow-up period. All failed eyes had intermediate-term IOP elevation (Fig. 1, P = 0.001). Three types of ocular hypotensive drugs were administered to 3 of the 4 eyes, and 4 types were administered to the other eye (P < 0.001). However, usage of prostaglandin analogs for intermediate-term IOP elevation eyes was not related to graft failure (P = 1.000). Two of the four patients were advised to undergo antiglaucoma surgery for IOP control (Fig. 2). However, the graft failure was not related to the indications of keratoplasty (P = 1.000), previous graft failure (P = 0.167), or early IOP elevation (P = 1.000). Logistic regression analysis was used to identify clinical risk factors for graft failure. The factors involved included gender, age at surgery, indication for DMEK, repeat DMEK, phakic status, IOP elevation period and the number of ocular hypotensive medication. However, none of the risk factors was identified.

Discussion

This study investigated the effect of postoperative IOP elevation on the endothelial function of the DMEK graft. In previous studies, the impact of early postoperative IOP elevation on the prognosis of DMEK was examined, and a history of glaucoma or previous antiglaucoma surgery was found to be an important risk factor for poor prognosis of DMEK. However, the impact of intermediate-term IOP elevation on prognosis in nonglaucomatous eyes after DMEK has not been clearly established. In this study, we found that intermediate-term IOP elevation reduced the graft ECD, and was an important risk factor of graft failure following graft failure.

In our study, the incidence of early postoperative IOP elevation was 21.3%, which was approximately the same as that reported in previous studies [4]. The incidence of intermediate-term IOP elevation was 34.0%, which was higher than the incidence reported in previous studies [4]. A possible reason is that the main indication for DMEK in this study was BK (78.7%), which is considered a risk factor for post-DMEK IOP elevation [24]. However, the main indication in previous studies was FECD [24–27].

Similar to the results of Anna et al., early postoperative IOP elevation had no adverse effect on the surgical outcome after timely treatment [21]. Compared with eyes with intermediate-term IOP elevation, eyes with early elevated IOP may receive immediate treatment since this elevation usually occurs during hospitalization; such immediate treatment may prevent the high IOP from further affecting the corneal endothelial cells, thereby leading to a better prognosis.

Compared with eyes without intermediate-term IOP elevation, eyes with intermediate-term IOP elevation had a lower ECD at the 3-month postoperative examination (P=0.032). Our study revealed that the median time for postoperative intermediate-term IOP elevation was 3 months, suggesting that the lower ECD may be related to endothelial cell loss caused by high IOP. Since patients usually had their third postoperative examination 2 months after the second examination (3 months postoperatively), the actual starting point of high IOP may have been earlier than 3 months after surgery. Therefore, we conclude that intermediate-term IOP elevation leads to endothelial cell loss in DMEK eyes. Moreover, the adverse effects of high IOP on endothelial cells in grafts are independent of the indication, history of glaucoma, surgical history, and concomitant surgical procedures. Compared to the cases of corneal endothelial cell damage in glaucoma eyes, a shorter duration and a lower level of IOP elevation can cause damage to the graft endothelium, which indicated the vulnerability of the graft tissue [12, 14].

Furthermore, we believe that the main reason for intermediate-term IOP elevation in eyes undergoing DMEK after cataract surgery without preexisting glaucoma is the use of topical corticosteroid drops. No peripheral anterior synechia were found in any of the eyes with elevated IOP. The time point of IOP elevation in our study was concentrated within the 2-6 months postoperative period. Previous studies also revealed that the risk of postoperative IOP elevation in patients undergoing descemet stripping endothelial keratoplasty was greater at 2–6 months after surgery [28, 29]. Price et al. reported that the risk of postoperative IOP elevation induced by topical corticosteroids was greatest in the first 6 months after descemet stripping endothelial keratoplasty [30]. Patients in this study used 1% triamcinolone acetonide eye drops 2–3 times daily for 3 months after surgery, and the frequency of administration was subsequently reduced each month. The time of IOP elevation in our patients was earlier than that reported by Elalfy et al. (5.7±4.04 months), and this difference may have been caused by different postoperative topical-corticosteroid management protocols [31].

However, at other time points, the ECD in eyes with intermediate-term IOP elevation was lower than that in the control group; this difference was not significant, which may have been due to the small sample size. In addition, some patients with obvious reductions in the ECD had severe corneal edema, which made the ECD unmeasurable. There was no significant difference in the CCT between the eyes with postoperative IOP elevation and the control eyes at any time point. This may have been because although the ECD was lower, no endothelial dysfunction occurred.

Intermediate-term IOP elevation was associated with DMEK graft failure (P=0.001), and eyes with graft failure had been treated with a greater number of topical hypotensive medications (P<0.001). These findings suggest that uncontrolled high IOP may be an important risk factor for graft failure. In addition, the combined use of multiple hypotensive drugs may also have negative effects on the corneal endothelium [32, 33].

Early IOP elevation after DMEK surgery has no impact on surgical prognosis, whereas intermediate-term IOP elevation leads to an unfavourable prognosis. In future studies, a larger sample of patients without preexisting glaucoma should be prospectively enrolled to identify potential special pathways for poor prognosis of DMEK. Besides, the pre- and postoperative risk factors that lead to IOP elevation after DMEK surgery should be studied for better management of postoperative complications.

Since we excluded eyes with a history of glaucoma, surgical history other than cataract surgery, or eyes receiving a combination of surgical procedures, our sample size was limited, and some results may not be significantly different. Besides, diagnostics of steroid induced glaucoma in eyes following DMEK is challenging, thus there is a certain possibility of misdiagnosis. IOP measurements during postoperative follow-up were performed via iCare, not Goldmann tonometer. However, previous studies have shown that the iCare measurement for post–endothelial keratoplasty eyes is consistent with the Goldmann tonometer measurement result [34]. Furthermore, the ECD examination was conducted via in vivo confocal microscopy, which may result in certain discrepancies with the ECD determined by specular microscopy [35]. Thus, further study of the differences between the two examination methods is needed.

Conclusions

This study revealed that early IOP elevation has no impact on the prognosis of DMEK surgery. And intermediate-term IOP elevation after DMEK is associated with a decrease in ECD but does not affect CCT. Intermediate-term IOP is an important risk factor for graft failure after DMEK. Therefore, patients with risk factors for IOP elevation should have an increased frequency of intermediate-term postoperative follow-up.

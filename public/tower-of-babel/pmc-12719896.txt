ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionObesity is a prevalent multifactorial disease worldwide that has become a major public health concern. Excess adiposity poses a health risk because it is related to several chronic diseases, which impact the person’s quality of life and present a greater risk of mortality. This study presents a protocol to evaluate the effectiveness of the Energy2MOB programme (Improvement of Obesity) in reducing body weight (between 5%–10%) in adults with obesity with a multicomponent group intervention of food education and physical activity.Methods and analysisRandomised clinical trial with two groups (control and intervention) of 1 year, which includes people from the Berguedà health region between 18 and 65 years old with grade II overweight (body mass index (BMI) between 27–30 kg/m2) or obesity (BMI between 30–40 kg/m2). Introduction

Background

Obesity is a chronic and prevalent disease worldwide that represents a serious public health problem and has a high socioeconomic impact.1 The WHO defines it as excess body fat that impairs health, contributes to the development of medical complications and reduces life expectancy.2

In recent years, the prevalence of obesity has increased significantly on a global scale. According to the ‘Health at a Glance 2023’ report of the Organisation for Economic Cooperation and Development, in 2021 between 54 and 60% of adults in developed countries were overweight, and between 18 and 26% suffered from obesity. In countries like the US or the UK, obesity exceeds 35%, while in France or Italy the figures are lower, between 26 and 31%.3 In Spain, according to data from the Carlos III Health Institute and the Spanish Agency for Food Safety and Nutrition, 55.8% of the adult population is overweight and 18.7% is obese. In children and adolescents, the ALADINO study indicates that by 2023, 20.2% will be overweight and 15.9% will be obese.4

The obesogenic environment is characterised by a sedentary lifestyle and a diet rich in ultra-processed products and is a determining factor.5 However, obesity is a multifactorial disease that involves genetic and physiological causes as well as environmental, psychological, social and economic ones.6 At a pathophysiological level, it is characterised by a chronic low-grade inflammatory state that increases the risk of cardiovascular disease, type 2 diabetes, some types of cancer and mental health disorders.7,9 However, obesity also has a significant emotional and social impact, due to the discrimination and stigma associated with weight.10

To address this situation, expert groups recommend a weight loss of 5%–10% in 6 months to improve parameters such as Body Mass Index (BMI),6 11 waist circumference and glucose and lipid levels.12,14 To achieve this weight reduction, it is advisable that the approach be interdisciplinary and focused on improving lifestyle habits,15 prioritising healthy eating, physical activity and psychological support.16 17

The waist-to-height index (WHIA) parameter has been proposed as an effective indicator to assess cardiovascular and metabolic risk,18 surpassing BMI and PC.19 The WHIA is a good estimate of abdominal adiposity and the risk of suffering from diseases such as type 2 diabetes, hypertension or cardiovascular diseases.20 In addition, it has been validated by scientific bodies such as NICE for its ease of use and its applicability to different genders and ethnicities.21 WHIA cut-off points help identify risk accurately, and a value >0.6 indicates a high risk that requires intervention.22 For these reasons, it is recommended to incorporate WHIA into routine clinical practice as a tool to improve the detection of cardiometabolic risks.23

The intervention model of this study, proposed as the Energy2MOB programme, consists of behavioural strategies and the promotion of a healthy eating pattern based on the Mediterranean Diet model.24 25 In addition, the importance of regular physical activity is reinforced not only to facilitate weight loss and maintenance, but also to improve quality of life.26 This improvement is more easily achieved when working through motivation and emotional management strategies, which promote adherence to new habits and contribute to maintaining sustainable results over time.27 28

Several studies have shown that multidisciplinary intervention programmes that combine food education with the promotion of physical activity and emotional support have a positive impact on improving lifestyle habits, losing body weight and preventing diseases associated with obesity.29 Group dynamics encourage social support, shared motivation and collective learning. In this way, it is easier for people to achieve a reduction in sedentary lifestyle, better emotional management and healthier eating, with sustainable effects on physical and mental health.30 Furthermore, the WHO itself recommends these integrated interventions as a priority approach to tackle obesity at a population level.31

The model we use to underpin the group food education intervention will be the COM-B,32 a tool used to understand and promote behaviour change. This model establishes that, in order for a person to adopt a new behaviour (B), in this case improving eating habits, three key factors must coincide: the person must have capacity (C) (physical, mental and emotional skills), opportunity (O) (availability of physical and social resources that encourage change) and motivation (M) (internal reasons such as values, emotions or beliefs, and external reasons such as rewards or social pressure). When these three elements occur simultaneously, change in behaviour (B) is more likely and sustainable. For this reason, this approach will be used as a basis to ensure that the people treated are involved and motivated throughout the entire process of addressing obesity, a key aspect to achieving treatment success.33,36

In 2021, the role of the dietitian-nutritionist was incorporated into Primary Care teams within the Programme for the promotion of healthy eating and disease prevention.37 38 Currently, with the consolidated presence of the three community profiles—dietitian-nutritionist, physiotherapist and emotional well-being referent—a comprehensive and coordinated approach to obesity is facilitated, which allows community programmes such as Energy2MOB to be implemented with greater efficiency and sustainability.39

Study aims and hypotheses

This project constitutes an evolution of previous programmes and seeks to generate evidence on the effectiveness of multicomponent group interventions in the management of obesity.13 26 The main aim is to evaluate the effectiveness of the Energy2MOB programme in reducing body weight (5%–10%) in overweight or obese adults in the Berguedà health region. Furthermore, it aims to analyse changes in lifestyle, such as adherence to the Mediterranean Diet, regular practice of physical activity and improvement of emotional intake. Additionally, the study seeks to evaluate changes in biochemical, anthropometric and blood pressure parameters; to analyse improvements in health-related quality of life, reduction in cardiovascular risk and better management of comorbidities (hypertension, dyslipidaemia and diabetes mellitus); and to examine the feasibility of implementing the programme by assessing adherence, fidelity and participant satisfaction. If the results are positive, a replicable model could be established in other primary care centres.

Methods and analysis

This protocol has been developed based on Standard Protocol Items: Recommendations for International Trials.40

Design

Randomised clinical trial with two groups (control and intervention).

Scope

Primary Care in the healthcare region of Central Catalonia, specifically in the Berguedà area.

Study period

The study will last 2 years (from January 2025 to December 2026), which will include: the recruitment of participants, the baseline assessment, the running of the programme (eight group sessions, distributed over one session per week for 8 weeks), the final evaluation, one telephone follow-up per month and two individual face-to-face follow-ups, one at three and the other at 6 months after the end of the intervention.

Reference population

Overweight or obese people interested in changing their lifestyle habits and reducing body weight to improve their health and quality of life.

Inclusion criteria

People between 18 and 65 years old with grade II overweight (BMI between 27–30 kg/m2) or obesity (BMI between 30–40 kg/m2). Ability to attend a minimum of 75% of the programme (6 sessions out of 8 total).

Exclusion criteria

Significant language barrier of Catalan or Spanish, bariatric surgery in the last 5 years,41 moderate-severe cognitive impairment and serious psychiatric disorders that prevent the follow-up of the sessions.

Sample size and power calculations

Assuming an alpha risk of 0.05 and a statistical power >0.8 in a two-sided test, a total of 68 participants per group (group 1 and group 2) were required to detect a between-group difference equal to or >2.5 kg. A common SD of 4.77 kg was assumed, and an estimated 15% loss to follow-up was considered.42 The GRANMO calculator (V.8.0; https://www.datarus.eu/ca/aplications/granmo/; accessed November 2024) was used for this estimation. The minimal important difference of 2.5 kg corresponds approximately to a 6% reduction in body weight, consistent with international guidelines that define a 5% weight loss as a clinically meaningful target associated with relevant metabolic and health benefits.6 11 Ensuring that the study’s conclusions reflect not only statistical robustness but also meaningful health outcomes.

Randomisation and group assignment

Randomisation and group assignment will be carried out by simple randomisation performed using the specialised software RStudio V.4.2.1 by a person outside the study, from the Research Support Unit (USR) of Central Catalonia. Allocation concealment will also be ensured: the group assignments will not be predictable by the investigators or by the staff enrolling participants. The USR will provide the allocation for each participant individually, only after the participant has been enrolled in the study, thus maintaining concealment of the allocation sequence. The USR will then send the assignment to the lead investigator, who will be responsible for collecting the data and questionnaires from each patient. Each group will be made up of 68 people.

Data collection and information source

Data on baseline, postintervention and follow-up variables will be obtained through: anamnesis, physical examination, questionnaires and analytics, and will be transferred to the corporate tool Microsoft 365 Forms.

Data analysis

Initially, the sociodemographic and clinical characteristics of the two groups will be described. For the description of qualitative variables, percentages will be used and for quantitative variables, means and SD or medians, ranges and percentiles 25–75 will be used. Prior to the intervention and after randomisation, the baseline scores of the selected scales and the sociodemographic variables of the participants will be compared with verify that there are no statistically significant differences. After the intervention, the mean scores of the different scales and variables will be compared independently and for the different postintervention time periods, using the t-Student test for paired samples in the case of normal distribution or the Mann-Whitney U test if the distribution is not normal. A secondary analysis will be carried out, using multiple regression, including sociodemographic and confounding variables. Results that show differences with a p<0.05 will be considered significant. The intervals will be with a 95% confidence. For statistical analysis, SPSS V.18 software (SPSS, Chicago, USA) will be used. Comparisons will be conducted both between groups and within groups, before and after the intervention, as well as at 3 months and 6 months postintervention.

Patient and public involvement

Patients or members of the public were not involved in the design, conduct, reporting or dissemination plans of this research. However, participants will be informed of the study results once the trial is completed.

Discussion

The Energy2MOB programme is a significant opportunity to address obesity in the Berguedà healthcare region through an educational, practical and community-based approach, designed specifically for the adult population of this region, which has specific needs and limited healthcare resources. Through multi-component group intervention, which includes nutritional education sessions, physical activity and emotional well-being, the programme aims to promote sustainable habit changes that contribute to the reduction of body weight, the improvement of quality of life and the prevention of associated comorbidities, such as cardiovascular diseases. This multidisciplinary approach is aligned with intervention models already implemented in Catalonia, such as the Nereu Programme and the Comprehensive Plan for Health Promotion through Physical Activity and Healthy Eating (, which have shown positive results in overweight populations.50 51

Current scientific evidence reinforces the usefulness of group interventions to improve health parameters in people with obesity. Several systematic reviews and intervention studies indicate that group programmes may be more efficient than individual interventions in promoting sustainable changes in body weight and lifestyle habits.52 Moreover, when these interventions include components of food education, physical activity and emotional support, they can contribute significantly to improving psychological well-being and quality of life, as several studies on comprehensive models of nutritional and emotional approaches have shown.53 In the case of Energy2MOB, the multidisciplinary approach will help generate a global impact on participants through changes in eating habits, physical activity and emotional management.

A group intervention in nutrition, physical activity and emotional well-being offers a series of benefits, as it encourages social support, shared motivation and collective learning. In the context of the present programme, this group activity will help participants share knowledge and experiences, creating a community that fosters commitment and mutual responsibility. In addition, social interaction and group support will be key factors in staying motivated to improve healthy habits and emotional well-being, as communication and support between group members will help reduce stress and increase self-esteem. This will contribute to a positive and lasting change in lifestyle habits, facilitating the sustainability of changes in body weight and the general well-being of participants.54

Limitations

This protocol presents a proposal for group intervention in the field of Primary Care, aimed at managing obesity through a comprehensive approach to nutrition, physical activity and emotional well-being. It has the potential to be integrated as an effective, efficient and scalable tool for obesity management at the community level. However, it is necessary to consider several difficulties and limitations that may influence the implementation and generalisation of the results.

One of the main difficulties is the adherence of participants to the proposed lifestyle changes, especially when these involve substantial and sustained modifications over time. Motivation plays a key role in this process, and for this reason, the programme incorporates motivational coaching strategies and group dynamics to encourage its maintenance. In this regard, designing the intervention with participants can contribute positively to improving their involvement and active participation.

Regarding data collection, the use of self-report questionnaires may present certain biases, such as the tendency to offer socially acceptable answers or difficulties in interpretation. To minimise these effects, instruments validated in the Spanish population have been selected, and it has been planned that they will be administered by professionals trained to resolve possible queries.

Another limitation is the transferability of the results. Although the study takes place in a specific rural setting, many other towns in the area share similar characteristics, such as limited access to health services, lack of infrastructure or certain sociocultural barriers. Therefore, careful planning and contextual adaptation of the intervention could facilitate its application to other rural contexts and offer comparable benefits, reinforcing the value of the programme as a replicable model in environments with similar characteristics.

The participation of young adults also poses a challenge, as this group often does not have regular contact with health services, may show low risk perception and have limited motivation to participate in health programmes. In addition, social and emotional factors can negatively influence adherence. For this reason, the programme design includes specific strategies to promote commitment, social support and individual empowerment to promote motivation to change sustainable habits over time.

Finally, interdisciplinary coordination between healthcare professionals is essential to guarantee comprehensive care. Although in rural environments this can be difficult due to the lack of resources and professionals, actions have been planned to minimise this impact, such as conducting training and periodic meetings, with the aim of harmonising actions and ensuring the quality of the programme. The role of the dietitian-nutritionist, as a constant professional responsible for coordination, is key to guaranteeing the continuity and coherence of the intervention.

Despite the limitations, this protocol has high practical applicability. The interdisciplinary group approach in Primary Care can contribute significantly to improving the management of obesity, providing an efficient, standardised and adapted alternative to the environment. In addition, it can act as a basis for training healthcare professionals in holistic approaches, improving their ability to offer comprehensive and personalised support, and enhancing patient autonomy.

In conclusion, this intervention model has the potential to generate long-term benefits, not only in improving the health of participants, but also in the sustainability of the healthcare system, through the prevention and better control of chronic diseases associated with obesity. In addition, the knowledge acquired through the evaluation of the intervention and the opinions of the participants will be useful for future improvements in the implementation and, therefore, in the effectiveness of the intervention.

Ethics and dissemination

The protocol was approved by the Ethics Committee of the University Institute for Research in Primary Care (IDIAP Jordi Gol) with the CEIm code: 24/303-P. The IDIAPJGol CEIm complies with the standards of Good Clinical Practice and with the current legislation that regulates its operation. The protocol complies with the ethical principles of the Declaration of Helsinki and with the applicable regulatory requirements regarding data protection. All participants will provide written informed consent before participating in the study (online supplemental appendix 3). In addition, the dissemination plan includes presenting the results in national and international scientific conferences, publication in peer-reviewed journals, and sharing a plain-language summary with all participants through their primary care centres. Key findings will also be shared with local health authorities and community health professionals to inform future interventions addressing obesity prevention and management.

MambaFormer-TPEF: a hybrid state space-transformer framework with two-stage peak enhancement for ultra-long sequence photovoltaic power forecasting
Authors: Jiayu Zou, Xixiu Wu, Qingyong Zhang, Zirui Shao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractReliable ultra-long-horizon photovoltaic (PV) power forecasting is essential for ensuring grid stability and optimizing energy storage dispatch under high renewable penetration. However, existing paradigms struggle with the efficiency—accuracy paradox in long-range modeling and the systematic peak underestimation inherent in mean squared error (MSE) optimization. This paper proposes MambaFormer-TPEF, a synergistic forecasting framework that reconciles global sequence scanning with local feature refinement. Specifically, we develop a MambaSeq2Seq architecture to achieve O(L) linear complexity for 720-h historical inputs, coupled with attention mechanisms to capture fine-grained fluctuations. To rectify systematic peak bias, a two-stage peak enhancement strategy decouples peak magnitude regression from timing classification using heterogeneous tree-based experts. Furthermore, a meta-learning-based multi-level ensemble fusion (MLEF) framework is introduced to facilitate scenario-adaptive optimal weighting. Validated on real-world datasets for a 168-h prediction horizon, MambaFormer-TPEF achieves state-of-the-art performance with a mean absolute error (MAE) of 0.5521 and a root mean square error (RMSE) of 33.90 kW. Notably, it delivers an 8.15% improvement in over leading benchmarks and significantly reduces peak prediction bias from −22.1 to −1.5%. This framework provides a scalable and robust solution for next-generation microgrid energy management systems, effectively bridging the gap between high-dimensional sequence modeling and real-time operational requirements.Keywords: Photovoltaic power forecasting, Long-horizon time series forecasting, Mamba, Hybrid transformer architecture, Meta-learning-based ensemble learningSubject terms: Energy science and technology, Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

Against the backdrop of global efforts to address climate change and accelerate the energy transition, PV power generation has emerged as a cornerstone of clean energy systems and is experiencing unprecedented growth in installed capacity. According to the International Energy Agency (IEA), global annual PV installations surpassed 400 GW for the first time in 2023, while cumulative installed capacity has exceeded 1.5 TW. Projections further indicate that by 2030, photovoltaic power may contribute more than 25% of total global electricity generation1. Despite this remarkable expansion, the large-scale integration of PV generation into power systems remains highly challenging. Owing to its inherent intermittency, volatility, and weather dependence, PV generation can introduce considerable uncertainty into system operation, directly affecting supply–demand balance, frequency regulation, and the secure operation of modern power grids2.

Against this background, accurate PV power forecasting is indispensable for practical grid dispatching and electricity market operations. While short-term forecasting is important for real-time control, system operators and market participants are increasingly demanding reliable ultra-long-horizon forecasts covering the coming days or even weeks3. Such long-term forecasts play a critical role in grid scheduling, energy storage optimization, maintenance planning, and participation in electricity spot markets. For example, weekly dispatch planning requires system operators to determine the start-up and shut-down schedules of thermal power units and to coordinate reservoir management for hydropower several days in advance. In this context, even relatively small forecasting errors may lead to substantial economic losses, such as higher dispatch costs and increased curtailment of renewable energy.

However, ultra-long-horizon PV forecasting presents unique technical challenges. To produce reliable forecasts, models must typically process extended historical observation windows—often spanning 30 days (720 hours)—in order to capture the evolution of weather patterns and their cumulative effects on power output4. Based on these long-term dependencies, the model must further infer the PV generation trajectory over a future horizon of up to 7 days (168 h). Therefore, effectively modeling temporal dependencies that span multiple time scales remains a fundamental and open challenge in the field of energy informatics.

Despite the remarkable progress of deep learning in time series forecasting, existing approaches still encounter fundamental challenges when applied to ultra-long-horizon PV power forecasting.

The first challenge is balancing efficiency and accuracy in long-range temporal modeling. RNN-based models such as LSTM5 and GRU6 perform well in short-term forecasting, but their sequential structure and gradient degradation limit their effectiveness on long sequences7. Transformer models improve long-range dependency modeling through self-attention8, but their quadratic complexity leads to high computational cost for long inputs and may cause overfitting when PV data are limited. Our experiments also show that standard Transformers can perform poorly in this task. In contrast, SSMs represented by Mamba9 offer linear complexity O(L) and are more suitable for ultra-long sequences, but they are still less effective in capturing fine-grained local interactions. Therefore, a key question is whether the efficiency of Mamba and the local modeling ability of Transformers can be combined in a unified framework.

The second challenge is the systematic bias in peak power prediction. Deep learning models trained with mean squared error often regress toward conditional means, producing a peak-smoothing effect and underestimating daytime peak power10. For example, our results show that a standalone Mamba model yields a peak bias of . This issue is particularly critical for power system security assessment and is difficult to address through loss function redesign alone. Therefore, an important question is whether models with stronger extreme-value modeling ability can be integrated with deep learning methods to capture long-term trends while correcting peak deviations.

The third challenge lies in the limited flexibility of conventional ensemble fusion strategies. Traditional ensemble methods usually adopt simple averaging or fixed weights, without considering that model performance varies across different scenarios, such as clear versus cloudy weather or peak versus off-peak periods11. As a result, the complementary strengths of heterogeneous models cannot be fully utilized. This leads to another important question: can a dynamic and scenario-aware fusion mechanism be designed to adaptively assign model weights according to the current input context?

To address these challenges, this paper proposes the MambaFormer-TPEF framework, with the following key contributions:

A hybrid Mamba–transformer sequence-to-sequence architecture (MambaSeq2Seq) is proposed to reconcile computational efficiency and predictive accuracy. Related work

PV power forecasting has evolved from physics-based modeling and statistical learning toward data-driven deep learning approaches. With advances in data acquisition, data-driven methods have become dominant by avoiding explicit physical parameter modeling. This section looks at related work from three angles: deep learning for sequence forecasting, Mamba, and ensemble learning. It highlights their limitations in ultra-long-horizon PV forecasting and shows why this study is needed.

Deep learning-based pv power sequence forecasting

Over the past decade, deep learning has progressively replaced traditional statistical approaches—such as ARIMA and support vector machines—and has become the dominant paradigm for PV power forecasting, primarily due to its ability to model complex nonlinear relationships directly from data12.

Recurrent neural networks (RNNs) and their variants have been widely adopted for time series prediction. Long short-term memory (LSTM) networks mitigate the vanishing gradient problem through gated mechanisms and have long served as a cornerstone for sequential modeling5. In PV forecasting applications, bidirectional LSTM models have been employed for day-ahead prediction to better capture intraday power fluctuations13, while the integration of meteorological variables further improves predictive accuracy14. Gated recurrent units (GRUs) simplify the LSTM architecture while maintaining comparable performance6. Extensions that incorporate attention mechanisms into GRU-based models have shown clear advantages in short-term PV forecasting tasks15. Despite these successes, RNN-based approaches fundamentally rely on sequential computation, which limits training efficiency. Moreover, theoretical analyses indicate that learning long-range dependencies remains difficult when sequence lengths become excessively long, due to persistent gradient degradation7.

The introduction of the Transformer architecture marked a significant departure from recurrent modeling by enabling direct dependency modeling through self-attention8. This design effectively alleviates the long-range dependency bottleneck of RNNs. However, the quadratic computational complexity O(L) of self-attention introduces substantial computational overhead for long input sequences. To address this issue, a number of efficient Transformer variants have been proposed, including Informer16, Autoformer17, and PatchTST18. In the context of PV forecasting, Transformers have been combined with convolutional networks or extended to spatiotemporal architectures to extract multi-scale patterns19,20. Nevertheless, recent studies report that under certain long-horizon settings, simple linear models can outperform more sophisticated Transformer variants, particularly when training data are limited21. This observation suggests that large-capacity attention-based models may suffer from overfitting and reduced robustness in ultra-long-horizon PV forecasting scenarios.

Taken together, existing deep learning approaches face a persistent efficiency–accuracy trade-off. RNN-based models are constrained by sequential computation and gradient degradation, whereas Transformer-based models are limited by quadratic complexity. This dilemma becomes especially pronounced in ultra-long-horizon PV forecasting tasks involving input sequences spanning hundreds of hours.

State space models and efficient long-sequence modeling

State space models (SSMs),represented by Mamba, provide a way to balance efficiency and long-range modeling. Classical Mamba describe system dynamics through linear recursive formulations22, but their integration into modern deep learning frameworks is hindered by numerical instability and limited capacity to model input-dependent dynamics. The HiPPO framework addresses numerical stability by enabling efficient and stable compression of long-term historical information23.

Structured SSMs, such as S4, represent a major step forward by imposing structured constraints on state matrices and leveraging fast Fourier transforms to achieve complexity24. These models have demonstrated strong performance on long-range sequence benchmarks, in some cases surpassing Transformer-based approaches. Subsequent research has explored both further efficiency improvements and architectural refinements, including models such as DSS25, S526, and hybrid designs that alternate state space layers with attention mechanisms27.

Mamba represents the most recent advancement in this line of work. By introducing selective scanning mechanisms, Mamba enables input-dependent parameter adaptation and relaxes the linear time-invariant assumption inherent to traditional SSMs9. With linear computational complexity O(L), Mamba combines recurrent-style inference with parallelizable training and has demonstrated strong performance in both natural language processing and time series forecasting9. Despite these advantages, pure SSM-based models remain less effective than attention mechanisms in capturing fine-grained local interactions and abrupt nonlinear changes28. Recent studies have therefore explored hybrid architectures that interleave Mamba with attention layers29. However, the application of such hybrid designs to ultra-long-horizon PV power forecasting has received little attention. How to effectively combine global sequence modeling with local refinement in this context remains a central question addressed in this work.

Ensemble learning and extreme-value optimization strategies

The inherent complexity of PV power generation makes it difficult for a single model to capture all relevant patterns, which has motivated the widespread use of ensemble learning to improve robustness. Traditional ensemble methods, including random forests30, XGBoost31, and LightGBM32, perform well on structured data and have been successfully applied to PV forecasting, particularly when combined with meteorological features.

More recently, ensembles of deep learning models have been investigated, such as hybrid CNN–LSTM architectures33 and conditional ensemble strategies based on weather classification34. Although these approaches improve performance, they suffer from two fundamental limitations. First, ensemble weights are typically static and fail to adapt to changing operating conditions across different time periods or scenarios. Second, models trained with mean squared error objectives exhibit a pronounced peak-smoothing effect, leading to systematic underestimation of daytime peak power10,35. While tree-based models are well suited for nonlinear regression and extreme-value modeling, they lack the ability to capture temporal dependencies36.

Existing extreme-value optimization strategies include asymmetric loss functions, post-processing correction techniques, and hierarchical forecasting schemes37,38. Furthermore, recent studies have demonstrated that combining multiple lightweight models through stacking or hybrid ensemble techniques can significantly improve prediction performance for short- and medium-term horizons with moderate computational overhead. For instance, stacking-based frameworks utilizing secondary feature extraction have shown marked improvements in accuracy39, while hybrid models integrating Variational Mode Decomposition (VMD) with feature selection effectively capture multi-scale power fluctuations40. However, these methods either show limited effectiveness in ultra-long-horizon settings or compromise temporal consistency. Furthermore, as highlighted in a recent systematic review41, robust forecasting is now recognized as a critical backbone for advanced microgrid energy management systems (EMS). Effectively integrating high-fidelity predictive signals with operational scheduling and real-time control is essential for managing the inherent stochasticity of renewable energy, yet achieving such integration in complex, resource-constrained environments remains a significant hurdle. Developing a unified framework that preserves temporal structure while accurately modeling extreme values therefore remains an open challenge.

In summary, existing PV forecasting methods exhibit complementary strengths but also clear limitations. RNN-based models are effective in capturing short-term and local temporal variations, yet their sequential computation and gradient degradation make them less suitable for ultra-long historical sequences. Transformer-based models improve long-range dependency modeling through global attention, but their quadratic complexity often leads to high computational cost and potential overfitting on limited PV datasets. SSM-based models, represented by Mamba, offer a more efficient alternative for long-sequence modeling, although their ability to characterize sharp local fluctuations and peak-value deviations remains limited. Ensemble-based methods can enhance robustness by combining heterogeneous predictors, but they often lack a unified mechanism to simultaneously model long-range dependencies and adapt to scenario-specific forecasting characteristics.

In view of these limitations, current research lacks an integrated framework that simultaneously supports linear-complexity modeling of ultra-long historical sequences, corrects systematic peak bias, and adaptively fuses heterogeneous models based on scenario-specific characteristics. The proposed MambaFormer-TPEF framework is designed to address these gaps.

Dataset overview

The dataset for this study is derived from the NIST Campus Photovoltaic (PV) Arrays and Weather Station Data Sets, released by the National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST), USA. It covers continuous measurements from 2015 to 2018. This multi-year coverage provides a diverse range of atmospheric conditions and long-term climatic variations, which serves as a rigorous foundation for testing the model’s robustness against different weather regimes. To ensure high-fidelity modeling, we specifically utilized the 270-kW ground-mounted system data, integrating multi-modal meteorological variables: Global Horizontal Irradiance (GHI), Direct Normal Irradiance (DNI), Diffuse Horizontal Irradiance (DHI), ambient temperature, and wind speed, all captured via research-grade sensors at 1-minute resolution.

The dataset includes PV power output and 16 unique electrical measurement variables sampled at 1-second and 1-minute intervals. For this work, we selected data from 2016 as our primary benchmark. The observation period encompasses 366 days, resulting in 8,784 hourly observations. By encompassing a complete annual cycle, this subset exposes the model to significant seasonality, including drastic shifts in solar zenith angles and varying day-night durations. To ensure data integrity, a rigorous preprocessing pipeline was applied: (i) missing values were addressed using linear interpolation for short gaps (< 30 min) and mean imputation for extended outages; (ii) a nighttime filtering threshold was set at a solar zenith angle and irradiance to prevent the model from learning trivial zero-patterns; and (iii) Min-Max scaling was employed to normalize all features to the range [0, 1]. Detailed definitions of the features can be found in the “Appendix”.

The dataset contains 16 original electrical features, primarily related to inverter power output and other electrical variables, covering multiple aspects of photovoltaic system operation. These features include measurements of voltage, current, frequency, and power readings at different measurement points.

Specifically, the electrical features correspond to monitoring channels associated with array-side DC/AC power, energy metering, environmental sensing, and array reference module temperature provided by the NIST PV array system. All features fall within the category of electrical observations recorded by the array, inverter, and metering devices.

Temporal features

Photovoltaic power generation shows periodic patterns at different scales. These include daily cycles (day-night alternation), weekly cycles (weekend vs. weekday operations), and annual cycles (seasonal changes in solar angle). This study adds 9 time-derived features to the original 16 electrical features. This brings the total to 25 features.

Proposed MambaFormer-TPEF architecture

Overall pipeline

To tackle the challenges associated with long-horizon photovoltaic power forecasting, this study introduces MambaFormer-TPEF, a multi-model ensemble framework that integrates state-space sequence modeling, tree-based peak enhancement, and dynamic fusion strategies. The framework’s architecture is specifically designed to support the real-time scheduling and control requirements of microgrid energy management systems42. An overview of the proposed framework is illustrated in Fig. 2.

Overall architecture of the proposed MambaFormer-TPEF framework.

The MambaFormer-TPEF architecture is organized into three hierarchical components: a base learner layer, a fusion layer, and an output layer. At the base learner layer, multi-scale input features—including historical power generation, and temporal encodings—are processed through two parallel branches.

The first branch comprises four sequence-to-sequence models, namely Mamba-Transformer, LSTM, Transformer, and GRU. These models are designed to capture temporal dynamics and generate continuous power trajectories over the prediction horizon. In parallel, the second branch focuses on peak-oriented regression through tree-based models, specifically hybrid Mamba-XGBoost and Mamba-LightGBM architectures. In this branch, deep representations extracted by the Mamba backbone are used as inputs to enhance the accuracy of peak power prediction.

The fusion layer implements a two-stage ensemble strategy. In the first stage, a model quality filtering mechanism removes underperforming base learners based on validation performance, ensuring that only reliable predictions are retained. In the second stage, a dynamic confidence-weighted aggregation module adaptively assigns weights to the remaining models according to their historical performance across different time scales and power ranges. This design allows the framework to exploit the complementary strengths of heterogeneous models: sequence-to-sequence architectures effectively capture smooth temporal evolution, while tree-based hybrids exhibit superior performance in peak regions.

Finally, the output layer produces the long-horizon power forecasting results and incorporates a SHAP analysis module to quantify feature importance. This additional interpretability component improves the transparency and credibility of the model’s predictions (Fig. 3).

Tree-based peak enhancement

Deep sequence models optimized with global loss functions often exhibit peak-smoothing behavior, which may lead to systematic underestimation of extreme photovoltaic power values. To alleviate this issue, a tree-based peak enhancement module is introduced to explicitly model peak power characteristics and complement sequence-based predictions.

where denotes the power output at hour h. The model is trained to minimize the mean squared error between predicted and observed daily peak values.

To focus on peak-relevant temporal patterns, the time index corresponding to the daily maximum power is extracted and mapped to a fixed-length representation. Based on this representation, gradient boosting tree models, including LightGBM and XGBoost, are employed to capture nonlinear feature interactions associated with peak generation. These tree-based models are well suited for extreme-value regression due to their robustness to feature scaling and ability to model heterogeneous feature importance.

During inference, the predicted daily peak values are injected back into the hourly prediction sequence. Peak power estimates are assigned at the identified peak time indices, while the remaining time steps are smoothly interpolated and merged with the original sequence-based predictions. The resulting peak-enhanced sequence is subsequently used as a high-confidence auxiliary signal in the dynamic fusion stage of the proposed framework.

MLEF ensemble framework

The MLEF framework constitutes a core component of the proposed MambaFormer-TPEF architecture. By integrating dynamic weighting, quality filtering, and nonlinear aggregation mechanisms, MLEF aims to achieve robust and adaptive combination of heterogeneous base learners, thereby improving both forecasting accuracy and generalization performance.

Dynamic weighting

Conventional ensemble methods often rely on fixed or static weights, which fail to account for the varying performance of different models across input conditions. Quality filtering

Quality filtering serves as a pre-ensemble step to identify and remove unreliable base learners. Each model is evaluated on a validation set using multiple performance criteria, including overall accuracy and peak-related errors. Models that consistently underperform or exhibit large relative degradation are excluded from subsequent fusion, ensuring that only high-quality predictors participate in the ensemble.

Aggregation algorithm

After quality filtering, the retained base learners are combined using a nonlinear aggregation strategy. The aggregation function is implemented with LightGBM and takes as input the base predictions, latent feature representations, and summary statistics that characterize prediction uncertainty and inter-model diversity. This design enables the ensemble to capture complex interactions among models and to adaptively emphasize complementary predictors under different operating conditions.

The final ensemble output is obtained after aggregating the filtered predictions and is used as the overall forecasting result.

Discussion

This study investigates long-horizon photovoltaic power forecasting from three complementary perspectives. First, the advantages of the hybrid MambaFormer-TPEF framework in long-sequence modeling are analyzed. Second, the role and limitations of the proposed peak-enhancement strategy are discussed. Finally, the constraints of the current study are examined, together with potential directions for future research.

Synergistic effects of the hybrid architecture

The experimental results show that combining Mamba and Transformer architectures offers clear benefits for long-term forecasting tasks. Mamba-based models are robust for long predictions but struggle with short-term accuracy. In contrast, Transformer models excel at capturing local patterns but quickly lose performance as the prediction horizon increases. The hybrid architecture effectively combines these strengths.

From a modeling view, Mamba’s state-space formulation supports stable temporal representation. It helps reduce error accumulation over long sequences, especially with slowly changing or periodic data. Furthermore, the O(L) complexity of Mamba ensures superior scalability over Transformers, enabling the efficient processing of longer sequences or higher-frequency data from various sites. On the other hand, the self-attention mechanism in Transformers focuses on important local patterns, which is crucial for modeling quick power changes. By arranging these components together, the hybrid framework improves both short-term accuracy and long-term stability.

This synergy shows up in both accuracy gains and less performance drop across prediction horizons. Compared to recurrent models, the hybrid model has a much flatter degradation curve, indicating that combining state-space dynamics and attention mechanisms creates a more balanced approach for long-sequence photovoltaic forecasting.

Mechanism and applicability of the peak-enhancement strategy

The peak-enhancement strategy is key for improving the reliability of extreme power event predictions. This is crucial for grid dispatch and energy storage planning. By focusing on peak-related errors during optimization, the strategy reduces systematic underestimation and improves the timing of peak occurrences.

Functionally, the enhancement mechanism shifts model capacity toward high-impact samples often missed in standard loss methods. This is especially useful during rapid irradiance changes, like cloud transients, where peak deviations add significant operational risk. Beyond technical metrics, the economic implications of this strategy are profound. By reducing the peak power bias from -22.1% to -1.5%, the framework directly mitigates “imbalance charges” imposed by grid operators for generation deviations. Furthermore, accurate long-horizon peak forecasting allows for more precise Unit Commitment and hydropower coordination several days in advance, potentially saving substantial costs associated with the emergency start-up of backup thermal units. In the context of microgrid energy management, high-fidelity peak prediction ensures that energy storage systems pre-allocate sufficient capacity, thereby minimizing renewable energy curtailment and maximizing the overall return on investment for distributed energy assets.

However, the peak-enhancement strategy isn’t suitable for all situations. In cases where peak events are rare or unclear, too much focus can create bias toward these patterns and lower overall performance. Thus, the strategy should be seen as a task-specific optimization tool, effective depending on the distribution of the forecasting target.

Limitations and future directions

The proposed MambaFormer-TPEF framework offers a solid solution for long-horizon PV power forecasting, yet several challenges and limitations must be addressed to enhance its practical utility in diverse operational environments.

First, while the framework demonstrates strong performance on the benchmark datasets, its cross-site generalization remains a primary challenge. The impact of local factors such as complex terrain, air quality, and micro-climatic variations on model behavior requires further validation across broader geographical contexts. Second, although the MLEF module is designed to adaptively handle shifts in statistical properties—such as seasonal transitions and varying degrees of intermittency—the model still faces the “cold-start” problem in scenarios with poor historical data quality. Third, despite the O(L) linear scaling of Mamba during inference, the multi-model ensemble architecture involves a relatively high memory footprint during the training phase, which may pose challenges for rapid retraining on resource-constrained industrial servers. Finally, the current study primarily focuses on point forecasting, which provides limited information regarding the inherent stochasticity and uncertainty of renewable energy required for risk-aware grid operations.

To address these limitations, future research will pursue three key avenues. (i) Probabilistic Forecasting: We aim to extend the framework to generate predictive intervals or full probability distributions using techniques such as Quantile Regression or Gaussian Processes, providing a robust measure of uncertainty for power system operators. (ii) Lightweight Deployment: Further optimization through model compression, knowledge distillation, and hardware-aware acceleration will be explored to facilitate deployment on edge devices and embedded controllers. (iii) Physics-Informed Hybrid Modeling: By integrating physical constraints, such as solar zenith angle dynamics and photovoltaic thermal models, into the Mamba-based state-space representation, we expect to further improve the model’s robustness and explainability across different climatic regions.

This work offers a solid solution for long-horizon photovoltaic power forecasting. Future research will aim at cross-site generalization, probabilistic forecasting, and lightweight deployment to improve practical utility.

Conclusion

This study tackles three main challenges in long-horizon photovoltaic power forecasting, including the efficiency–accuracy trade-off in ultra-long sequence modeling, systematic underestimation of peak values, and the robustness of ensemble fusion under varied operating conditions. To address these challenges, a hybrid forecasting framework, termed MambaFormer-TPEF, is proposed.

First, a hybrid sequence modeling architecture is developed by combining state-space modeling with attention mechanisms, enabling the joint modeling of global temporal dependencies and local pattern variations. The state-space component efficiently handles long historical sequences, while the attention module sharpens short-term representations. Experimental results show that the proposed architecture exhibits a long-horizon performance decay of only 4.0%, in contrast to 36.7% and 35.8% observed for LSTM and GRU, respectively, demonstrating substantially improved robustness in long-sequence forecasting tasks.

Second, a dual-stage peak-enhancement strategy is introduced to mitigate the common peak underestimation issue in deep learning–based forecasts. By decoupling peak magnitude estimation and peak timing identification into two complementary subtasks and incorporating tree-based expert models, the proposed strategy reduces peak bias from to and decreases peak RMSE by 13.3%. SHAP-based analysis further reveals the complementary feature preferences of the expert models, providing interpretable evidence for the effectiveness of the peak-enhancement mechanism.

Third, a multi-layer ensemble fusion framework (MLEF) is proposed to enable scenario-adaptive dynamic weighting among base predictors. Compared with simple averaging and fixed weighting strategies, MLEF learns the relationship between input features and model performance, allowing confidence weights to be adjusted automatically under different weather conditions. This adaptive strategy enhances forecasting stability and robustness across multiple prediction horizons.

In ultra-long sequence forecasting tasks with 720-hour inputs and 168-hour prediction horizons, the proposed MambaFormer-TPEF framework achieves an score of 0.5521 and an RMSE of 33.90 kW, corresponding to relative improvements of 8.15% and 4.32%, respectively, over strong baseline models. These results confirm the effectiveness of integrating state-space modeling, attention mechanisms, tree-based peak enhancement, and adaptive ensemble fusion for photovoltaic power forecasting.

Overall, this work provides a scalable and adaptable solution for long-horizon time-series prediction. The proposed framework and associated strategies are not limited to photovoltaic power forecasting and can be extended to other energy prediction tasks, such as wind power and load forecasting. Future research will focus on cross-site generalization, probabilistic forecasting, and lightweight deployment to further enhance practical applicability.

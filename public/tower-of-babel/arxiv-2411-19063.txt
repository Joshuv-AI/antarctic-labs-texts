A theorem on vacuum stability
André Milagre, Luís Lavoura

Abstract
We consider an extension of the Standard Model with one or more scalar multiplets beyond the Higgs doublet Φ \Phi . The additional scalar multiplets are supposed to carry arbitrary hypercharges. We prove that, in such a model, if the field configuration where only Φ \Phi has a nonzero vacuum expectation value (VEV) is a local minimum of the potential, then it has a lower value of the potential than any field configuration where both Φ \Phi and other scalar multiplets have nonzero VEVs.

The isospins and hypercharges of the multiplets

The

The scalar potential

We classify those extrema in the following way:

•

The type-0 extremum has

\left\langle\Phi\right\rangle=0,\quad\forall i\,\left\langle\Psi_{i}\right\rangle=0.

(3)

•

A type-I extremum has

\left\langle\Phi\right\rangle\neq 0,\quad\forall i\,\left\langle\Psi_{i}\right\rangle=0.

(4)

•

A type-II extremum has

\left\langle\Phi\right\rangle=0,\quad\exists\,\left\langle\Psi_{i}\right\rangle\neq 0.

(5)

•

A type-III extremum has

\left\langle\Phi\right\rangle\neq 0,\quad\exists\,\left\langle\Psi_{i}\right\rangle\neq 0.

(6)

The four types of extrema are qualitatively different

In Eqs. (

Theorem 1

Let
V
be a renormalizable and
SU(2)
-symmetric scalar potential
of
\Phi
and the
\Psi_{i}
,
and also of their charge-conjugate multiplets

\widetilde{\Phi}
and
\widetilde{\Psi}_{i}
,
respectively.
We assume that
V
does not contain either linear or trilinear terms.
Further, we assume that
V
does not
contain any bilinear terms with
two different multiplets.
2
2

2

This means that the only bilinear terms that are present in
V

are the
SU(2)
-invariant components of
\Phi\otimes\widetilde{\Phi}

and of the
\Psi_{i}\otimes\widetilde{\Psi}_{i}
, for all
i=1,\ldots,N
.

Let the symmetries of
V

only allow
\Phi
and
\Psi_{i}
to interact
through invariants constructed out of

\left({\Phi\otimes\widetilde{\Phi}\otimes\Psi_{i}\otimes\widetilde{\Psi}_{i}}\right).

(7)

Then,
a local type-I minimum has a lower value of the potential
than any type-0 or type-III extremum.

Corollary 1.1

Either the global minimum of the potential is that type-I local minimum,
or it is a type-II local minimum (if there is any).

\displaystyle\left(\Phi\otimes\widetilde{\Phi}\right)_{\mathbf{3}}

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\displaystyle{\phi_{1}\phi_{2}^{*}}\\[5.69054pt]

\displaystyle V_{(2)}+V_{(4)},

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle L^{T}X,

The type-0 extremum

2.1.4
Type-I extrema

Starting from a type-I extremum,
we use an
SU(2)

\Phi

\phi_{2}
,

\phi_{1}

\displaystyle\overline{\varphi}_{\mathrm{I}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{ccccc}0,&v_{\phi},&0,&\ldots,&0\end{array}\right)^{T},

\displaystyle\overline{X}_{\mathrm{I}}

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}v_{\phi}^{2},&0,&\ldots,&0\end{array}\right)^{T}.

\mu^{2}_{N}+\lambda_{0N}v_{\phi}^{2}\end{array}\right).

(40)

Utilizing Eq. (
35
),
this means that

\left.\frac{\partial V}{\partial X}\right|_{\varphi=\overline{\varphi}_{\mathrm{I}}}\cdot\overline{X}_{\mathrm{III}}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\,\sum_{I=-J_{i}}^{J_{i}}\left(\mu_{i}^{2}+\lambda_{0i}v_{\phi}^{2}\right)\left|v_{\psi_{i,I}}\right|^{2}.

(41)

Secondly,
it follows from Eqs. (
28
) and (
37
) that

\left.\frac{\partial V}{\partial X}\right|_{\varphi=\overline{\varphi}_{\mathrm{III}}}\cdot\overline{X}_{\mathrm{I}}=v_{\phi}^{2}\,\left.\frac{\partial V}{\partial F_{0}}\right|_{\varphi=\overline{\varphi}_{\mathrm{III}}}=v_{\phi}^{2}\,\sum_{i=1}^{N}\,\sum_{I=-J_{i}}^{J_{i}}\frac{\lambda_{0i}^{\prime}}{2}\,I\left|v_{\psi_{i,I}}\right|^{2}.

(42)

Thirdly,
by using Eqs. (
39
) and (
34
) one finds

\displaystyle\overline{V}_{\mathrm{III}}-\overline{V}_{\mathrm{I}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\,\sum_{i=1}^{N}\,\sum_{I=-J_{i}}^{J_{i}}\left(\mu_{i}^{2}+\lambda_{0i}v_{\phi}^{2}-\frac{\lambda_{0i}^{\prime}}{2}\,I\,v_{\phi}^{2}\right)\left|v_{\psi_{i,I}}\right|^{2}

(43a)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\,\sum_{i=1}^{N}\,\sum_{I=-J_{i}}^{J_{i}}m^{2}_{\psi_{i,I}}\left|v_{\psi_{i,I}}\right|^{2},

(43b)

which is positive because we have assumed that the masses-squared

m^{2}_{\psi_{i,I}}
are
positive, Q.E.D.

We conclude that
a local minimum of type-I
has a lower value of the potential than any type-0 or type-III extremum
,

cf.
Eqs. (
32
) and (
43
),
which means that,
either the global minimum of the potential is that type-I local minimum,
or it is a type-II local minimum
(if such a local minimum exists).

3
Summary

In this paper,
we have generalized a result demonstrated by FG
[
1
]
.
They considered an
SU(2)\times U(1)
gauge model
with an
SU(2)
doublet
\Phi
,
an
SU(2)
triplet
\Delta
,
and an additional symmetry

\Phi\to\Phi\exp\left(i\varkappa\right),\ \Delta\to\Delta\exp\left(i\varpi\right)
;
we have allowed
\Delta
to be
any

SU(2)
multiplet,
not necessarily a triplet.
4
4

4

As a matter of fact,
our theorem is valid for larger scalar sectors
with several
SU(2)
multiplets,
each of them invariant under a separate rephasing symmetry.

We wanted to check the conditions under which
a local minimum of the scalar potential (SP)
where only
\Phi
has nonzero VEV—this may be useful
as an extension of the Inert Doublet Model
of Dark Matter—is the
global
minimum of the potential.
We have proved that a local
minimum
of the SP
where only
\Phi
has nonzero VEV
lies
below
any extremum of the SP
where
both

\Phi
and
\Delta
have nonzero VEVs.
This extends FG’s demonstration,
which was
constructed
only for the case where
\Delta
is a single triplet.
As FG rightly remarked,
this still leaves open the possibility that
the global minimum of the relevant SP is an extremum where

only

\Delta
has nonzero VEV.

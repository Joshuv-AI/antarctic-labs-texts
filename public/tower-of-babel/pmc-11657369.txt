Exosome-delivered NR2F1-AS1 and NR2F1 drive phenotypic transition from dormancy to proliferation in treatment-resistant prostate cancer via stabilizing hormonal receptors
Authors: Wenbin Chen, Yiyou Mao, YiYuan Zhan, Wenfeng Li, Jun Wu, Xiangming Mao, Bin Xu, Fangpeng Shu
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Cancer cells acquire the ability to reprogram their phenotype in response to targeted therapies, yet the transition from dormancy to proliferation in drug-resistant cancers remains poorly understood. In prostate cancer, we utilized high-plasticity mouse models and enzalutamide-resistant (ENZ-R) cellular models to elucidate NR2F1 as a key factor in lineage transition and ENZ resistance. Depletion of NR2F1 drives ENZ-R cells into a relative dormancy state, characterized by reduced proliferation and heightened drug resistance, while NR2F1 overexpression yields contrasting outcomes. Transcriptional sequencing analysis of NR2F1-silenced prostate cancer cells and tissues from the Cancer Genome Atlas-prostate cancer and SU2C cohorts indicated exosomes as the most enriched cell component, with pathways implicated in steroid hormone biosynthesis and drug metabolism. Moreover, NR2F1-AS1 forms a complex with SRSF1 to upregulate NR2F1 expression, facilitating its binding with ESR1 to sustain hormonal receptor expression and enhance proliferation in ENZ-R cells. Furthermore, HnRNPA2B1 interacts with NR2F1 and NR2F1-AS1, assisting their packaging into exosomes, wherein exosomal NR2F1 and NR2F1-AS1 promote the proliferation of dormant ENZ-R cells. Our works offer novel insights into the reawaking of dormant drug-resistant cancer cells governed by NR2F1 upregulation triggered by exosome-derived NR2F1-AS1 and NR2F1, suggesting therapeutic potential for phenotype reversal.Graphical Abstract Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12951-024-03025-y.

Introduction

Resistance to targeted therapies has transformed the canonical trajectories of tumors, especially regarding the utilization of androgen receptor pathway inhibitors (ARPIs) in prostate cancer (PCa) treatment [1, 2]. Enzalutamide (ENZ), among the most effective ARPIs, is recommended for the treatment of metastatic or non-metastatic castration-resistant PCa, offering a significant extension of survival for patients with PCa [3]. However, the widespread application of ENZ also presents an unforeseen consequence: it fosters the stereotypical development of tumor heterogeneity, as evidenced by the clinical appearance of distinct phenotypic states aimed at circumventing ARPIs [4, 5], with cells exhibiting divergent differentiation and heightened plasticity. The phenomenon of phenotype switching or lineage plasticity in ENZ-resistant (ENZ-R) PCa remains largely unexplored.

Dormancy stands out as a notable mechanism conferring resistance to tumor-targeted therapies [6, 7]. Dormant cancer cells have been reported to undergo G0-G1 cell cycle arrest and exhibit reduced sensitivity to chemotherapy [8, 9]. Stress-induced intracellular signaling, hypoxia-inducible factors, and autophagy pathways are considered to participate in dormancy-induced drug resistance [10, 11]. Dormant cancer cells can disseminate and persist in distant organs for extended periods before becoming reactivated, leading to recurrence following successful treatment of the primary tumor [12, 13]. However, the transition from dormancy to proliferation in drug-resistant cells remains poorly understood. Here, we elucidate the significant roles of NR2F1 in driving dormant ENZ-R cancer cells toward a proliferative state, a process triggered by exosome-mediated transmission of NR2F1-AS1 and NR2F1.

Materials and methods

Cell culture and the establishment of ENZ-R cells

Human PCa cell lines and normal epithelial cells (DU145, 22RV1, C4-2, C4-2B, PC-3, LNCaP, and RWPE1) were acquired from the Stem Cell Bank, Chinese Academy of Science. PCa cells were grown in 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS; Hyclone, USA) supplemented RPMI 1640 media (Gibco, USA), while RWPE1 cells were maintained in KSFM (Gibco, no. 10744-019). To establish ENZ-R cells, we utilized RPMI-1640 media mixed with 10% charcoal-activated FBS (Hyclone) to eliminate hormonal interference. After being seeded in 6-cm dishes, C4-2 and C4-2B cells were treated with 10 µM ENZ for induction. Following long-term induction (3–6 months), the remaining cells were utilized for culturing, cryopreservation, and determination of IC50 values, with C4-2ENZR exhibiting an IC50 of 43.3 µM and C4-2BENZR showing an IC50 of 52.6 µM. Subsequent culturing of ENZ-R cells involved maintaining an appropriate concentration of ENZ to sustain resistance.

Constructs and transfection

NR2F1-siRNA and SRSF1-siRNA were constructed by RiboBio (Guangzhou, China) with the following sequences: si-NR2F1-CTCTTCTTCGTCCGTTTGGTA and si-SRSF1-GAAGCAGGTGATGTATGTTAT. Lentivirus vectors (GeneChem, China) were utilized for the establishment of stable NR2F1-silenced cells (shRNA). Plasmids for NR2F1 and NR2F1-AS1 overexpression were constructed and lentivirus-packaged by Tsingke (Guangzhou, China). Plasmids and siRNAs were transfected into cells utilizing LipofectamineTM 3000 (Invitrogen, USA). HiTransG P/A (GeneChem) was employed to facilitate lentivirus transfection and construct stable NR2F1-silenced cells.

Transcription sequencing and bioinformatics analysis

Transcription sequencing of C4-2CON, C4-2ENZR, and NR2F1-silenced C4-2ENZR and C4-2CON cells was performed utilizing the BGI-SEQ platform (Shenzhen, China). Differentially expressed gene (DEG) analysis, GO analysis, KEGG analysis, and GSEA were conducted using the BGI Dr. Tom system. RNA-seq data for highly plastic cells (16DCRPC and 42DENZR) and LNCaP ENZ-R cells (LNCaPCON and LNCaPENZR) were acquired from the Gene Expression Omnibus repository (GEO) under the accession numbers GSE138460 and GSE150807. Data organization, principal component analysis (PCA), DEGs, GO analysis, KEGG analysis, and GSEA, as well as plotting, were performed using SangerBox [14].

Real-time quantitative PCR (RT-qPCR)

Reverse transcription of total RNA into cDNA was carried out using RT Premix and the RNAex Pro reagent (Accurate Biology, Guangzhou, China). The SYBR Green Premix Kit was utilized for RT-qPCR in the ABI7500 Fast Real-time PCR System (Applied Biosystems, USA). Primer sequences were manufactured by Tsingke (Guangzhou, China) and are listed in Supplementary Table 1. Serving as internal references were ACTB and GAPDH. The 2− ρρCt quantification approach was employed to examine the expression data.

Western blotting (WB)

Following the manufacturer’s instructions, protease inhibitor-containing RIPA lysis solution (#KGP250, KeyGEN BioTECH, Nanjing, China) was used to extract the proteins from the cells. Following the collection of cell lysates, identical volumes of cellular proteins were separated on 4–12% SDS-PAGE gels from Bio-Rad and electronically transferred onto polyvinylidene fluoride membranes (Millipore, Billerica, MA, USA). The membranes were then blocked with TBS containing 5% non-fat milk and co-incubated overnight at 4 ℃ with specific antibodies: anti-rabbit β-actin (#4970, CST, 1:1000); anti-rabbit NR2F1 (#6364, CST); antibodies from the Cell Cycle Regulation Antibody Sampler Kit (#9932, CST, including CDK2, CDK4, CDK6, p21, p27, cyclin D1); anti-rabbit SNAIL (#9585, CST); anti-β-catenin (#8480, CST); rabbit anti-E-cadherin (#3195, CST); anti-mouse PHB2 (66424-1-Ig, Proteintech, China); anti-rabbit ESR1 (21244-1-AP, Proteintech); anti-rabbit androgen receptor (AR) (22089-1-AP, Proteintech); anti-mouse HnRNPA2B1 (67445-1-Ig, Proteintech); anti-rabbit SRSF1 (12929-2-AP, Proteintech); anti-rabbit CD63 (25682-1-AP, Proteintech); anti-rabbit HSP70 (10995-1-AP, Proteintech); anti-rabbit lamin-A (10298-1-AP, Proteintech); anti-rabbit ERK 1/2 (T40071, Abmart); anti-rabbit phospho-ERK 1/2 (T40072, Abmart); anti-rabbit p38 MAPK (T55600, Abmart); and anti-rabbit phospho-p38 MAPK (TA4001, Abmart). The membranes were then treated with horseradish peroxidase-linked secondary anti-rabbit IgG or anti-mouse IgG antibodies for 1 h at room temperature (Cell Signaling Technology). An improved chemiluminescence detection instrument (Pierce Biotechnology, Rockford, IL, USA) was used to view protein bands, and Image J software was utilized to quantify band intensities. To ensure reliability, we performed the assays in triplicate.

IC50 and CCK-8 assay

Using 96 well plates, two thousand PCa cells were seeded into each well, which contained a total of 100 µL of medium containing 10% FBS. After the cells adhered to the plates, the original medium was withdrawn, and 100 µL of CCK-8 reagent (10% v/v) diluted with RPMI-1640 was added to each well, following the manufacturer’s instructions (CK-04, Dojindo). Using a microplate reader (model EXL800, BioTek Instruments), the optical density of each well was determined at 450 nm after a 2-hour incubation period. There were three assays carried out. PCa cells were cultivated in medium containing different doses of ENZ (0, 25, 50, 100, and 200 µM) to determine the IC50. For the CCK-8 assay, 5 days were required to determine cellular viability.

Colony formation and EdU assays

To assess cell proliferation via colony formation analysis, we cultured 500 PCa cells in each well of six-well plates for 2 weeks in 2 mL of a medium added with 10% FBS. The colonies were then fixed using 4% paraformaldehyde to prevent further proliferation and subsequently stained with Giemsa. A microscope was used to view the dyed and preserved cells, and the colonies were counted. The assay for colony formation was carried out in triplicate, using three wells per group. To assess cell proliferation activity, we used the Cell-Light EdU Staining Kit (RiboBio) following the manufacturer’s instructions. Following incubation with an EdU solution at a specified concentration and duration, cells were fixed to prevent further proliferation. Permeabilization was performed to facilitate the labeling of EdU-incorporated cells with fluorescence using the click reaction reagent. Images were taken with an Olympus fluorescent microscope (Tokyo, Japan) at a magnification of 200×. The rate of cell proliferation was indicated by the ratio of DAPI-stained (blue fluorescence) to EdU-positive (green fluorescence) cells in each well. There were three assays carried out for each.

Cell cycle analysis

The Cell Cycle Assay Kit (propidium iodide [PI]/RNase staining) was used to perform cell cycle assays. In short, PCa cells were fixed with 70% ethanol at 4 °C for an entire night after being digested with a 0.25% trypsin/EDTA solution. Subsequently, PI was used for cell staining. Next, the stained cell suspension was loaded into a flow cytometer (FACS Calibur, Becton Dickinson). Parameters such as cell size, granularity, and the expression levels of markers were evaluated. All assays were conducted in triplicate.

Mice xenograft

To create xenograft tumor models, 8 groups of 4- to 5-week-old male nude mice were randomly assigned to receive 1 × 106 cells injected into their dorsal region. The mice were obtained from the Animal Center of Southern Medical University in Guangzhou, China. Tumor volumes were computed using the formula volume = (length × width2)/2, and tumor dimensions were recorded every two weeks. After 4 weeks, tumor samples were meticulously resected, observed, and subjected to immunohistochemistry (IHC) and hematoxylin and eosin staining to validate the existence of cancer cells. The Animal Care and Use Committee of the Southern Medical University approved all methods involving animals in experimentation, and the animals were kept in a designated environment free of pathogens.

IHC staining

The expression levels of pertinent proteins in the aforementioned tissues were examined using IHC analysis [15]. Specimens were incubated with anti-rabbit NR2F1 antibodies (24573-1-AP, Proteintech, 1:100). Then calculating the percentage of positive nuclei and cells as well as the staining intensity allowed for the evaluation of the findings. Three expert pathologists performed each evaluation independently, using the same microscope. The assays were performed in triplicate.

RNA pulldown and silver staining

A biotin-labeled NR2F1-AS1 probe was synthesized by RiboBio. An RNA pulldown assay was conducted using the Pierce™ Magnetic RNA-Protein Pull-Down Kit (#20164, Thermo Scientific, USA). SDS-PAGE analysis was performed on the resulting pull-down products using Milli-Q water. The Fast Silver Stain Kit (#P0017S, Beyotime, Shanghai, China) was used to stain silver.

Co-immunoprecipitation (Co-IP)

Cell protein supernatants in lysis buffer were initially processed with 50 µL of A/G beads (Merck, USA), followed by overnight incubation at 4 ℃ with 5 µg of either control IgG (30000-0-AP, Proteintech) or anti-rabbit NR2F1 (#6364, CST, 1:100), anti-rabbit PHB2 (12295-1-AP, Proteintech, 1:100), and anti-rabbit HnRNPA2B1 (ab31645, Abcam, 1:100) antibodies. The resulting immunocomplexes were then incubated for 6 h at 4 °C with 50 µL of A/G beads, and to remove nonspecific binding, they were eluted five times using ice-cold PBS containing 0.2% NP-40. Following electrophoresis and separation on SDS/PAGE gels, the immunoprecipitated protein complexes were seen using silver staining (P0017S, Beyotime). Following a 10-minute metal boiling process in 2× SDS-PAGE sample buffer, the proteins that were immunoprecipitated were separated and then exposed to immunoblotting.

Nucleic acid gel electrophoresis

Nucleic acids were extracted, purified, and then mixed with a loading buffer. This mixture was subsequently loaded into wells of a precast agarose gel (2%) supplemented with ethidium bromide, facilitating nucleic acid band visualization. Application of an electric field facilitated the migration of nucleic acids within the gel, leading to the separation of the target nucleic acid, which was subsequently visualized using UV light. Image J software was employed for quantifying the intensity of the bands.

RNA immunoprecipitation (RIP)

Following the manufacturer’s protocol, RIP assays were conducted using the EZ-Magna RIP™ Kit (#17–701, Merck Millipore). Subsequently, the purified RNA obtained from these assays was subjected to downstream analyses aimed at studying specific RNA-protein interactions or identifying RNA targets of the RNA-binding protein. Antibodies employed in RIP assays included anti-rabbit HnRNPA2B1 (ab31645, Abcam, 1:100) and anti-rabbit SRSF1 (12929-2-AP, Proteintech, 1:100).

Protein interaction prediction and structure simulation

AlphaFold Colab version 2.3.2 was employed to predict protein interactions and structures (https://colab.research.google.com/github/deepmind/alphafold/blob/main/notebooks/AlphaFold.ipynb#scrollTo=pc5-mbsX9PZC). The protein sequences were acquired from the National Center of Biotechnology Information (NCBI). The structures were visualized and binding sites within the protein complex were identified using PyMOL software (USA).

Immunofluorescence (IF) assay

After being fixed for 15 min at room temperature in 4% paraformaldehyde (E672002, Sangon Biotech), the cells were permeabilized for 10 min using a 0.5% Triton solution (A110694, Sangon Biotech). The cells were then incubated with primary antibodies for an additional night at 4 °C after being treated for 30 min with Tris-buffered saline containing 5% BSA. Subsequently, fluorescent secondary antibodies were employed to identify the primary antibodies, followed by nuclear staining with DAPI (P0131, Beyotime). Ultimately, photos were taken using a Leica, LSM880 inverted confocal microscope. The principal antibodies listed below were employed: anti-rabbit NR2F1 (24573-1-AP, Proteintech, 1:100), anti-mouse PHB2 (66424-1-Ig, Proteintech, 1:100), anti-rabbit ESR1 (21244-1-AP, Proteintech, 1:100), anti-rabbit AR (22089-1-AP, Proteintech, 1:100), and anti-mouse HnRNPA2B1 (67445-1-Ig, Proteintech, 1:100). The following secondary antibodies were used: anti-rabbit IgG (ab150080, Abcam, 1:100) and anti-mouse IgG (ab150077, Abcam, 1:100).

Exosome extraction and identification

Ten-cm dishes were used to sow PCa cells. PCa cells were cultured for 48 h in RPMI-1640 media supplemented with 10% exosome-free FBS (#EXO-FBS-50 A, System Biosciences-SBI, USA) until the cell density had reached about 90%. Exosomes were isolated using the ExoQuick-TC™ Kit (# EXOTC10A, SBI). In summary, 10 milliliters of culture media were gathered and centrifuged for 15 min at 3000 × g, with PBS acting as the control. The resulting supernatant was transferred, and 2 mL of ExoQuick-TC was added before overnight refrigeration (> 12 h). The next step involved centrifugation at 3000 × g for 30 min, after which the supernatant was extracted. Exosomes were resuspended in 100 µL of PBS for electron microscopy. For cell culture experiments, exosomes from 10 mL of medium were resuspended in 300 µL of fresh medium, and sterilization was performed using a 0.22-µm filter (Millipore, USA).

Reproducibility and statistical analysis

The in vitro tests were run a minimum of three times. GraphPad Prism 9 (GraphPad Software, USA) was used to analyze the data, and ANOVA and t-tests were used for statistical analysis. P-values less than 0.05 were regarded as noteworthy.

Results

NR2F1 expression is elevated in high-plasticity mouse models and ENZ-R cells

Emerging evidence has shown that activation of lineage plasticity typically occurs following treatment with ARPIs, suggesting its significant role in PCa drug resistance. To investigate this premise, we utilized a previously reported ENZ-R in vivo model representing various phenotypes of treatment refractoriness [16, 17]. Castration-resistant PCa cells (16DCRPC) exhibited AR expression and markedly elevated prostate-specific antigen (PSA) levels, differing from canonical AR pathway activity. Conversely, ENZ-R cells (42DENZR, AR + PSA-) exhibited a deficiency in canonical AR pathway activity, which is enriched in lineage plasticity-related transcriptional programs (Fig. 1A). ENZ-R cells (C4-2ENZR and C4-2BENZR) and control cells (C4-2CON and C4-2BCON) were generated as depicted in Fig. 1B, with all cells maintained under continuous treatment with DMSO and ENZ. RNA-seq analyses revealed DEGs and associated enrichment pathways between C4-2CON and C4-2ENZR cells (Supplementary Fig. 1A–D), with canonical AR-targeting genes (KLK3, TMPRSS2, and NKX3.1) downregulated in C4-2ENZR cells compared to control cells (Supplementary Fig. 1E).

To explore the key genes involved in the phenotype transition during PCa progression towards ENZ resistance, we performed a re-analysis of RNA-seq data from 16DCRPC, 42DENZR, C4-2CON, C4-2ENZR, LNCaPCON, and LNCaPENZR cell lines. Initially, volcano plots revealed the differential expression of genes across the aforementioned cell lines (Fig. 1C–E), while PCA revealed distinct clustering of the control and ENZ-R groups (Fig. 1F). Noteworthy genes common to both the control and ENZ-R groups included NR2F1, PREX1, IFI27L2, and FCGRT (Fig. 1G). NR2F1, showing a substantial fold change, was selected for further investigation. Subsequent RT-qPCR and WB analyses validated the upregulation of NR2F1 at both the mRNA and protein levels in ENZ-R PCa cells (Fig. 1H and I). Additionally, compared to that in normal prostate epithelial cells (RWPE-1), NR2F1 was upregulated in PCa cell lines (Fig. 1J and K). These results indicate a potential involvement of NR2F1 in unlocking lineage plasticity and the ENZ resistance process.

Loss of NR2F1 induces dormancy in ENZ-R cells

To elucidate the role of NR2F1 in ENZ-R PCa, we employed RNA silencing and lentivirus to deplete or overexpress NR2F1 in C4-2ENZR and C4-2BENZR cells. The efficacy of NR2F1 knockdown or overexpression is illustrated in Supplementary Fig. 1F. Notably, we discovered that silencing NR2F1 promoted the resistance of ENZ-R cells to ENZ, while overexpression of NR2F1 diminished ENZ resistance in C4-2ENZR cells (Fig. 2A–C). However, these results were not consistent with the observed high expression levels of NR2F1 in ENZ-R cells. Consequently, we performed CCK-8, colony formation, and EdU assays to assess the proliferative capacities of ENZ-R cells. We found that NR2F1 depletion remarkably decreased the growth of C4-2ENZR and C4-2BENZR cells, whereas NR2F1 overexpression augmented the proliferation of C4-2ENZR cells (Fig. 2D–I). These results suggest opposite characteristics between cellular ENZ resistance and proliferation following NR2F1 abrogation in ENZ-R cells, prompting further contemplation.

Previous research has revealed that part of cancer cells can enter a dormant state to evade targeted therapy and survive [6]. In a dormancy mouse model, dormant cancer cells exhibited cell cycle arrest (G0-G1) to resist standard tumor treatments [18]. Here, cell cycle assays demonstrated that NR2F1 knockdown induced notable cell cycle arrest in the G1 phase, while NR2F1 overexpression had the opposite effect in ENZ-R cells (Fig. 2J). Additionally, WB assays revealed that the cell cycle suppressor protein p21 was significantly upregulated, while CDK2, CDK4, CDK6, and cyclin D1 were notably downregulated following NR2F1 depletion. Conversely, overexpression of NR2F1 exhibited contrasting effects (Fig. 2K). Consistently, C4-2ENZR cells displayed decreased thickness after NR2F1 knockdown (Supplementary Fig. 1G). According to several previous studies [19–21], a decreased ratio of phosphorylated ERK 1/2 to phosphorylated p38 (MAPK) can serve as a marker for dormant cells. Our WB assays indicated that the phosphorylated ERK 1/2 to phosphorylated p38 ratio decreased or increased following NR2F1 knockdown or overexpression in C4-2ENZR and C4-2BENZR cells, respectively (Supplementary Fig. 2A–D). Subsequently, typical lineage markers [22, 23] for luminal, basal, neuroendocrine (NE) and Stem cell were analyzed in ENZ-R and NR2F1-silenced cells. We found increased levels of luminal marker (KRT8), basal markers (KRT15, KRT19 and KRT14), NE markers (ENO2) and Stem cell markers (SOX9 and KLF4) after NR2F1 silencing both in C4-2 and ENZ-R cells, while a NE marker (SYNM) increased in C4-2 cells and a Stem cell marker (OCT4) upregulated in ENZ-R cells after NR2F1 depletion (Supplementary Fig. 2E, F). These results indicated the strong association between NR2F1 and lineage plasticity. Additionally, the mRNA and protein levels of NR2F1 were initially decreased in C4-2 wild-type cells treated with ENZ at 24, 48, 72, and 96 h (Supplementary Fig. 3A–B) and were upregulated in C4-2ENZR cells after long-term ENZ induction. In vivo experiments showed that NR2F1 depletion significantly inhibited the growth of C4-2ENZR xenografts in nude mice (Fig. 2L and M). IHC assays indicated a significant increase in the cell cycle-inhibiting protein p21 after NR2F1 silencing in C4-2ENZR xenografts (Fig. 2N–P), consistent with in vitro findings. Collectively, these results demonstrate that NR2F1 deficiency induces dormancy in ENZ-R cells, while NR2F1 overexpression enhances their proliferation activities.

Transcriptional profiling and functional enrichment analysis of NR2F1-silenced C4-2WT cells, C4-2ENZR cells, and PCa tissues

To explore the genes and pathways associated with the NR2F1-mediated dormancy/proliferation transition process, we initially performed RNA-seq analysis of C4-2WT and C4-2ENZR cells subsequent to NR2F1 silencing. Following NR2F1 knockdown, C4-2WT cells exhibited 972 upregulated and 844 downregulated genes compared to the control group, whereas C4-2ENZR cells showed 324 upregulated and 207 downregulated genes (Fig. 3A–C). Figure 3D–I present the GO analysis results for cellular component (CC), molecular function (MF), and biological process (BP) in both C4-2WT and C4-2ENZR cells, while KEGG analysis results are shown in Fig. 3J and K. Compared to those in the C4-2 wild-type cells, NR2F1-related genes were notably enriched in the extracellular exosome of cell components, steroid hormone biosynthesis, and drug metabolism pathways. Consistently, enrichment analysis of the overlapping genes between C4-2WT and C4-2ENZR cells revealed that extracellular space (GO-CC), cell proliferation (GO-BP), glucuronidation (GO-BP), and steroid hormone biosynthesis (KEGG) were among the most enriched pathways associated with NR2F1 (Fig. 3L–O). We further analyzed RNA-seq data from patients with PCa expressing the top 50 and bottom 50 levels of NR2F1 among 495 samples in the Cancer Genome Atlas-prostate adenocarcinoma (TCGA-PRAD) cohort. PCA indicated distinct clusters for NR2F1-high and NR2F1-low samples, after excluding outlier samples (Fig. 4A). DEG analysis revealed the top 50 upregulated and downregulated genes (Fig. 4B), with NR2F1-AS1 exhibiting a strong correlation with NR2F1. Functional enrichment analysis illustrated that NR2F1 was primarily associated with pathways involving extracellular exosomes (GO-CC), focal adhesion (KEGG), antigen processing and presentation (KEGG), phagosomes (KEGG), and basal cell carcinoma (GSEA); all associated pathways are shown in Fig. 4C–E. We conducted similar analyses using another metastatic PCa cohort (SU2C cohort), yielding consistent results as shown in Supplementary Fig. 4. Thus, our findings illustrate the landscape of NR2F1-associated pathways in both cellular models and patient tissues, highlighting the significant roles of extracellular exosomes and the steroid hormone biosynthesis pathway in the dormancy/proliferation transition of ENZ-R cells.

NR2F1-AS1 forms a complex with SRSF1 to upregulate NR2F1 expression in ENZ-R cells

Given that a strong correlation between NR2F1-AS1 and NR2F1 was noted in TCGA-PRAD cohort (Fig. 5A), we investigated their regulatory networks. Initially, we examined the relative expression of NR2F1 and NR2F1-AS1 in PCa cells. C4-2ENZR and PC3 cell lines were chosen for subsequent investigations due to their high expression of NR2F1 and NR2F1-AS1. Notably, NR2F1-AS1 exhibited no fluctuation after NR2F1 knockdown or overexpression in C4-2ENZR cells (Fig. 5B). Conversely, NR2F1 showed an obvious upregulation following NR2F1-AS1 elevation in both C4-2ENZR and PC-3 cells (Fig. 5C). To further elucidate how NR2F1-AS1 upregulates NR2F1 expression, we conducted an RNA-pulldown assay in C4-2ENZR and PC-3 cells using biotin-labeled NR2F1-AS1 probes. RT-qPCR following pulldown assays indicated that NR2F1-AS1 could not directly regulate NR2F1 mRNA (Fig. 5D). WB assays combined with silver staining revealed potential binding proteins associated with NR2F1-AS1, and differential bands, indicated by red arrows, were subjected to subsequent liquid chromatography-mass spectrometry (LC-MS) analysis (Fig. 5E, Supplementary Table 2). Potential binding proteins were also predicted using catRAPID (http://service.tartaglialab.com/page/catrapid_group) and are displayed in Fig. 5F. SRSF1 was initially selected for subsequent investigation due to its high ranking in the prediction results. WB validated the NR2F1-AS1 pulldown of SRSF1 in PC-3 and C4-2ENZR cells (Fig. 5G). Additionally, RIP assays demonstrated that SRSF1 could bind both NR2F1-AS1 and NR2F1 mRNA (Fig. 5H–J). Furthermore, based on the prediction of binding sites between NR2F1-AS1 and SRSF1 (Fig. 5L), plasmids of wild-type NR2F1-AS1 and mutant NR2F1-AS1 were constructed, as shown in Fig. 5K. RIP assays validated that deficiency of sites 505–650 in NR2F1-AS1 reduced its binding ability to SRSF1, as well as NR2F1 mRNA (Fig. 5M), validating the importance of the interaction between NR2F1-AS1 and SRSF1. SRSF1 exhibited strong correlations with both NR2F1 and NR2F1-AS1 in TCGA-PRAD cohort (Supplementary Fig. 3C, D). Additionally, SRSF1 depletion predominantly downregulated the mRNA expression of NR2F1 and NR2F1-AS1 in C4-2ENZR cells (Fig. 5N). Moreover, NR2F1-AS1 was initially reduced at 24, 48, 72, and 96 h following ENZ treatment in C4-2 wild-type cells (Supplementary Fig. 3A). However, after long-term induction with ENZ, NR2F1-AS1 ultimately exhibited upregulation in ENZ-R cells compared to wild-type cells (Supplementary Fig. 3E). NR2F1-AS1 overexpression facilitated the growth of ENZ-R PCa cells (Supplementary Fig. 3F, G). Taken together, these results suggest the notable roles of the NR2F1-AS1 and SRSF1 complex in upregulating NR2F1 expression to activate proliferation of ENZ-R cells.

NR2F1 promotes the proliferation of ENZ-R PCa cells by activating the androgen and estrogen receptor pathway

To further explore the role of NR2F1 in unlocking proliferation of dormant ENZ-R cells, we conducted RNA-seq and LC-MS analyses to delve into the downstream molecular activities of NR2F1. We performed a Co-IP assay to identify potential proteins interacting with NR2F1. WB, coupled with silver staining, revealed all potential binding proteins, with differential bands subsequently excised for LC-MS analysis (Fig. 6A). The top 10 ranked proteins from two bands are listed in Fig. 6B and C, and all proteins and identification scores are provided in Supplementary Table 3. DEGs identified in both RNA-seq of NR2F1-silenced C4-2ENZR cells and proteins discovered via LC-MS included RPS3, PHB2, RPL5, CTSZ, EEF1A1, and HNRNPF (Fig. 6D). These overlapped proteins and NR2F1 were then analyzed using the STRING website (https://cn.string-db.org/) to simulate NR2F1 binding networks. Notably, NR2F1, instead of directly binding to PHB2, appeared to directly interact with ESR1 and form the ESR1-AR-PHB2 complex (Fig. 6E). Co-IP assays of the NR2F1 and PHB2 proteins validated the interaction of NR2F1, ESR1, and PHB2 (Fig. 6F and G). Protein levels of PHB2, ESR1, and AR in PCa and RWPE1 cells are depicted in Supplementary Fig. 5A. In TCGA-PRAD cohort, NR2F1 exhibited a strong correlation with ESR1, while correlations between AR-NR2F1 and PHB2-NR2F1 were minimal (Supplementary Fig. 5B–D). Additionally, utilizing AlphaFold Multimer, we predicted the binding possibilities and structures of protein complexes such as ESR1-NR2F1, NR2F1-PHB2, and ESR1-PHB2. We observed that NR2F1-ESR1 exhibited the highest binding confidence (Fig. 6H). The structures and detailed binding sites of the NR2F1-ESR1 protein complex are shown in Fig. 6I. IF assays in NR2F1-silenced and control C4-2ENZR cells revealed that NR2F1 depletion reduced colocalization of PHB2 with ESR1 and AR (Fig. 6J), indicating NR2F1’s crucial role in sustaining the stability of the ESR1-AR-PHB2 complex. Furthermore, GSEA of NR2F1-silenced and control C4-2ENZR cells validated that the enriched hallmarks were associated with androgen response and estrogen response (Fig. 6K and L). Previous studies have revealed that AR and ESR1 can operate transcription programs, facilitating PCa growth [24, 25]. Our observations corroborate consistent fluctuations in the protein levels of PHB2, ESR1, and AR with those of NR2F1 in C4-2ENZR and C4-2BENZR cells (Fig. 6M). Collectively, these results indicate that NR2F1 maintains AR and ESR1 expression levels to bolster the proliferation of ENZ-R cells by stabilizing the ESR1-AR-PHB2 protein complex.

Exosome-mediated transmission of NR2F1 and NR2F1-AS1 reactivates dormant ENZ-R PCa cells

After revealing the potentially interacted proteins of both NR2F1 and NR2F1-AS1, we sought to determine whether certain proteins co-bind to both NR2F1 and NR2F1-AS1, thereby contributing to the progression of the NR2F1-mediated phenotype transition. The proteins identified through both LC-MS in NR2F1-AS1 pulldown assays and NR2F1 Co-IP assays included VIM, HnRNPA2B1, RPS5, ELAVL1, and DCD (Fig. 7A). Based on molecular mass and LC-MS scores, HnRNPA2B1 was selected for further investigation. WB assay following RNA pulldown assay validated the binding of NR2F1-AS1 and HnRNPA2B1 (Fig. 7B). RIP assays validated the interaction between HnRNPA2B1 and NR2F1-AS1 (Fig. 7C). Co-IP assays demonstrated mutual interaction between NR2F1 and HnRNPA2B1 (Fig. 7D). Utilizing AlphaFold Multimer, we predicted binding sites, and the structure is displayed in Fig. 7E. Importantly, the colocalization of NR2F1 and A2B1 was validated via IF experiments in C4-2WT and C4-2ENZR cells (Fig. 7F). In summary, these results validate the ability of HnRNPA2B1 to bind to both NR2F1 and NR2F1-AS1. HnRNPA2B1 has been reported to participate in various biological processes, including exosome generation and the packaging of proteins and RNA into exosomes [26, 27]. This finding was also consistent with the aforementioned GO-CC analysis findings pertaining to extracellular exosomes.

To further investigate the roles of exosomes in NR2F1-mediated dormancy/proliferation transition in ENZ-R PCa cells, we isolated exosomes from cell culture media of NR2F1-overexpressing and control C4-2ENZR cells. Electron microscopy and nanoparticle tracking analysis (NTA) revealed the representative morphology (cup-shaped), size, and concentration of the isolated exosomes (Fig. 7G and H). The expression of typical exosome protein markers, such as CD63, HSP70, and lamin-A (negative control), was validated by WB assays (Fig. 7I). As expected, NR2F1 and NR2F1-AS1 levels were notably higher in NR2F1-overexpressing cells compared to control cells (Fig. 7I and J). Subsequently, using NR2F1-depleted C4-2ENZR cells as dormant ENZ-R cells, we discovered significantly enhanced proliferative abilities of dormant cells cultured in media containing NR2F1-overexpressing exosomes compared to those cultured with exosomes derived from control cells (Fig. 7K–M). Importantly, expression of NR2F1 and the colocalization of NR2F1 and HnRNPA2B1 were notably enhanced in dormant cells cultured with media containing NR2F1-overexpressing exosomes compared to the control group (Fig. 7N and O). Altogether, these results demonstrate that dormant ENZ-R cells can be reactivated by NR2F1 and NR2F1-AS1 present in exosomes secreted by proliferative ENZ-R cells.

Discussion

Diverse tumor cell lineages have emerged because of resistance to targeted therapies across various malignancies [28, 29]. In PCa, lineage plasticity often refers to the shift from adenocarcinoma to NE PCa upon resistance to anti-androgen therapies [30–32]. For instance, EZH2 may induce epithelial PCa to enter a highly plastic state by activating transcriptional networks associated with stem cells and neurons, thereby acquiring resistance phenotypes [17]. However, other forms of lineage plasticity in PCa remain largely unexplored. Utilizing animal models with high plasticity and ENZ-R cells, we identified the notable involvement of NR2F1 in lineage plasticity in the transition from dormancy to proliferation lineage in ENZ-R PCa.

Previous studies have demonstrated that NR2F1 can induce dormancy in disseminated tumor cells (DTCs) by inducing the pluripotency gene NANOG and global chromatin repression [33]. NR2F1 plays a crucial role in inducing dormancy in DTCs in lung HNSCC metastasis [34, 35]. In breast cancer, NR2F1 has been reported to inhibit the dissemination of early-stage breast cancer cells [36]. In our established ENZ-R PCa cellular model, depletion of NR2F1 was validated to induce dormancy, characterized by reduced proliferation and enhanced resistance to ENZ, while NR2F1 overexpression yielded opposite effects. Additionally, our findings revealed an initial decrease in NR2F1 expression during early ENZ treatment and its upregulation in ENZ-R cells, highlighting NR2F1’s role in modulating the dormancy or proliferation states of ENZ-R cells in PCa. However, overexpression of NR2F1 in ENZ-R cells resulted in lower resistance to ENZ, suggesting a counterintuitive outcome. We hypothesize that this may represent an adaptation for rapid proliferation during the later stages of drug resistance. If the concentration of ENZ is slightly decreased, ENZ-R cells may exhibit increased proliferation. Nonetheless, further investigations are needed to comprehend this phenomenon in future studies.

Steroid receptors serve as notable transducers of hormone signaling and mediate various tissue-specific functions. AR signaling has been reported to broadly contribute to the progression of PCa [37]. Castration and long-term utilization of ARPIs such as ENZ suppress the expression of androgen and AR, while estrogen receptor (ER) signaling has been linked to increased prostate progression and the promotion of oncogenic events [25, 38, 39]. Our study demonstrated that NR2F1 can directly bind to ESR1 to sustain AR/ER expression, thereby promoting the proliferation of ENZ-R PCa cells. Previous studies have also demonstrated interactions between ESR1-NR2F1 and ESR1-PHB2 [40–43]. Nonetheless, further mechanistic investigations are warranted to determine whether these interactions stabilize the NR2F1-ESR1-AR-PHB2 protein complex and facilitate proliferation in ENZ-R cells.

NR2F1-AS1, the antisense RNA 1 of NR2F1, has been previously reported to enhance proliferation and metastasis in pancreatic cancer [44], gastric cancer [45], and lung adenocarcinoma [46]. Conversely, it has also been reported to induce metastatic dormancy and promote epithelial–mesenchymal transition by binding to PTBP1, leading to the upregulation of NR2F1 expression in breast cancer [47]. Our findings validated that NR2F1-AS1 forms a complex with SRSF1 to upregulate NR2F1 expression, thereby contributing to the activation of proliferation in dormant ENZ-R cells. These observations elucidate the roles of NR2F1 and NR2F1-AS1 in ENZ-R dormancy.

Exosomes play diverse roles in biological functions, such as facilitating the transmission of drug resistance, proliferation, metastasis, and remodeling of tumor environments [48–50], especially in prostate cancer [51]. HnRNPA2B1 has been implicated in the packaging of various molecules into exosomes, including miRNAs [27, 52], circRNAs [53, 54], and long noncoding RNAs [55, 56] to orchestrate different biological functions. Here, we discovered a potential association between exosomes and NR2F1-mediated pathways through functional enrichment analysis. Furthermore, we identified HnRNPA2B1 as a co-binding factor of NR2F1 and NR2F1-AS1. Subsequent functional experiments validated the presence of exosomal NR2F1 and the colocalization of endogenous or exosome-transmitted NR2F1 with HnRNPA2B1. These findings highlight the significant roles of HnRNPA2B1 in the packaging and transfer of NR2F1, thereby reactivating dormant cells into proliferative states in ENZ-R PCa.

Conclusion

The phenomenon of lineage switching between drug-resistance induced dormant and proliferative cancer cells has remained largely unclear. Our study provides novel insights into the reactivation of dormant PCa cells during progression towards ENZ resistance. Furthermore, we demonstrate that HnRNPA2B1-mediated exosomal transmission of NR2F1 and NR2F1-AS1 facilitates the transition from dormancy to proliferation in ENZ-R cells by sustaining AR/ER expression. By gaining a deeper understanding of cancer cell dormancy/proliferation switching, novel adjuvant therapies targeting dormant cancer cells might soon become viable options.

Recognition and combination of multiple cell-death features showed good predictive value in lung adenocarcinoma
Authors: Fanmao Meng, Xin Sun, Wei Guo, Yong Shi, Wenhui Cheng, Liang Zhao
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
BackgroundCell death is a key regulatory process in organisms and its study has become increasingly important in the field of cancer. While prior research has primarily centered on the individual pathways of cell death in cancer, there has been a lack of comprehensive investigation into the synergistic effects of multiple cell death pathways.MethodsGenes related to autophagy, apoptosis, necroptosis, pyroptosis, and cuproptosis was selected, and patients’ data was collected from The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA)project. Cell death features were identified using principal component analysis and combined to create a composite score. A scalable prediction model was then created using LASSO regression after a thorough assessment of the composite scores. The model was subsequently validated across multiple external datasets to establish its robustness and reliability.ResultsThe cell death features effectively represented the gene expression patterns in the samples. The composite score well predicted prognosis, clinical stage, mutation, tumor microenvironment, and immunotherapy effectiveness. The model built on composite scores accurately predicted prognosis and immunotherapy effectiveness across multiple datasets. GJB2 was identified as a potential biomarker.ConclusionModels based on multiple cell death pathways have significant predictive power for prognosis and immunotherapy effectiveness in lung adenocarcinoma. This highlights the synergistic role of multiple cell death pathways in cancer development and offers a new perspective for cancer research.

Introduction

Lung cancer is a prevalent malignancy globally, and according to the latest report from Global Cancer Statistics in 2020, it stands as the second most frequently diagnosed cancer type and the leading cause of cancer-related mortality [1]. Non-small-cell lung cancer (NSCLC) represents the predominant histologic subtype of lung cancer, accounting for around 85 % of all cases, with lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD) being the most prevalent subtype (∼50 %) [2]. Significant strides have been made in the treatment of LUAD, with a combination of surgical intervention, chemotherapy, radiotherapy, immunotherapy, and targeted therapy enhancing patient prognosis [3]. However, the overall 5-year survival rate of NSCLC remains below 20 %, and in patients with metastasis, the rate is even below 4 % [4]. Therefore, a pressing need exists to identify novel biomarkers and construct high predictive models to improve the overall survival of LUAD.

Cell death is a critical process that regulates organisms. While accidental cell death (ACD) occurs uncontrollably due to extreme physical or chemical exposure, programmed cell death (PCD) is a more desirable, controllable form of cell death [[5], [6], [7]]. The Nomenclature Committee on Cell Death (NCCD) has identified 12 cell death pathways, including apoptosis, autophagy, necroptosis, pyroptosis, and ferroptosis, among others [7]. Apoptosis was the first PCD pathway discovered. The pathway involves sequentially activating the caspase family, resulting in DNA cleavage and cell death [8,9]. Necroptosis serves as a backup pathway if apoptosis is compromised due to infection or other reasons [10]. Activation of multiple death receptors triggers necroptosis, mediated by phosphorylation of MLKL, leading to plasma membrane damage [11,12]. Pyroptosis is induced by caspase-1 activation, and is a proinflammatory cell death that involves effector proteins of the gasdermin family [[13], [14], [15]]. Autophagy is a physiological process that remodels, recycles, and renovates cells with lysosomal participation, and it also plays a role in inducing cell death [16,17]. Other cell death pathways, such as ferroptosis, cuproptosis, and immunogenic cell death, have been gradually discovered, and their regulatory effects on various diseases have been increasingly recognized [[18], [19], [20]].

Cell death is an increasingly studied field in cancer research. Studies suggest that abnormal regulation of programmed cell death (PCD) may be associated with the immune escape of cancer cells, leading to the emergence of targeted therapies related to cell death as a new approach for cancer treatment [6]. Among various PCD-related therapeutic targets, research on apoptosis-related treatments is the most advanced. Several targeted therapies targeting the Bcl-2 family, p53 gene, and inhibitor of apoptosis proteins (IAPs) have shown potential in vitro experiments [21]. Inhibition of autophagy has also become a current idea of cancer treatment. Representative drugs like chloroquine and hydroxychloroquine have shown promising results in clinical trials [22]. The therapeutic value of necroptosis and pyroptosis is mainly shown through their synergistic effect with apoptosis [23,24]. Additionally, there is crosstalk between apoptosis, necroptosis, and pyroptosis at the molecular level, suggesting a need for a more integrated understanding of PCD. Scholars have developed the concept of “PANoptosis” that combines the three cell death modes [25], offering a new perspective for understanding programmed cell death. Cuproptosis has also shown potential value in cancer therapy. Recent studies suggest that cuproptosis-related signatures can predict the prognosis of multiple cancer diseases [26,27], and in vitro experiment has already confirmed that theranostic nanoplatforms targeting cuproptosis can inhibit tumor growth [28].

Although significant progress has been made in studying individual types of cell death, the synergistic effects of multiple cell death pathways have not been studied in sufficient depth. In this study, we aim to combine five well-studied cell death pathways and identify comprehensive cell-death features of lung adenocarcinoma. By constructing models with good predictive value, we hope to reveal the crosstalk between various cell death pathways and identify possible therapeutic prediction methods and new therapeutic targets.

Materials & methods

Data acquisition

Transcriptome sequencing data for lung adenocarcinoma samples were downloaded from the UCSC Xena database (https://xena.ucsc.edu/), including all samples from The Cancer Genome Atlas - Lung Adenocarcinoma (TCGA-LUAD) project (n = 576). The sequencing data were logarithmically calculated for further statistical analysis. Then the detailed clinical data of the TCGA-LUAD project was downloaded using the "TCGAbiolinks" R package [29]. By screening the tissue source, we distinguished between tumor tissue (n = 515) and normal tissue (n = 59). They were derived from different individuals and had available clinical data. Next, we searched the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) database and included three data sets of lung adenocarcinoma samples that recorded overall survival (GSE13213, GSE31210, GSE37745) for statistical validation. All the GEO datasets were downloaded by "GEOquery" R package [30]. In addition, an urothelial carcinoma cohort “IMvigor210” with immunotherapy outcomes and prognostic data was downloaded to explore the predictive ability of the model for immunotherapy [31]. We selected four well-studied cell death pathways apoptosis, autophagy, necroptosis, and

pyroptosis, and a newly discovered pathway cuproptosis to conduct the following investigation. Pathway-related genes were collected on the Path Cards website (https://pathcards.genecards.org) and research article [32]. After excluding unavailable genes from transcriptome sequencing data, a total of 34 apoptosis genes, 30 autophagy genes, 29 necroptosis genes, 28 pyroptosis genes, and 10 cuproptosis genes were included (Supplement Table 1).

Recognition of cell death features

Principal component analysis (PCA) was applied to the gene expression matrix of each cell death pathway. The first principal component was selected and the cell death characteristic score of each sample was calculated by the following formula:Celldeathfeatures=|∑iPC1*xi|

The “i” above refers to the gene expression matrix of each sample.

Estimation of genes variation and tumor microenvironment

Somatic mutation information of TCGA-LUAD projects was visualized by oncoplot using the “GenVisR” R package [33]. And we assessed the infiltration of 22 types of immune cells using the CIBERSORT algorithm [34] to analyze the characteristics of the immune microenvironment of TCGA-LUAD samples. The calculated number of permutations was set to 1000, and the results were displayed in a boxplot. ESTIMATE score is another method to assess the tumor microenvironment [35]. We used the “estimate” R package to calculate the ESTIMATE score of TCGA-LUAD samples for further analysis.

Functional enrichment analysis

The gene set variation analysis (GSVA) in this study was performed using the “GSVA” R package, and used the gene sets defined in the Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) database. The R package can be downloaded from the Bioconductor website (http://www.bioconductor.org). The results of functional enrichment analysis were further compared using the “limma” R package [36], and the cut point of the adjusted P value was set to 0.01.

Identifying differentially expressed genes:

Differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were identified using the “limma” R package. The criteria for determining whether the difference was significant was set as | logFC |≥1.5 and adjusted P-value≤0.001.

Calculation of cell death score and construct predictive signature

After identifying differentially expressed genes, we extracted the expression matrix of the 107 DEGs and applied LASSO Cox regression to screen the most appropriate genes. We selected the regression coefficient corresponding to the minim lambda to calculate the cell death score and construct signature. The effect of the signature was evaluated by the Receiver Operating Characteristic (ROC) curve and its corresponding Area Under Curve (AUC) value. They were calculated by the "timeROC" R package.

Establishment of the nomogram

Nomogram was wildly used in cancer prognostic prediction. We combined the age, clinical stage, and cell death score to make multivariable Cox regression and establish the nomogram. The nomogram plot was made by the “nomogramFormula” R package.

Statistical analysis

Pearson correlation analysis was conducted using the “corrplot” R package. All the Cox regression models and Kaplan–Meier survival curves were made by ‘survival’ and ‘survminer’ R packages. R package ‘forestplot’ was used to visualize the results of Cox regression. The cut-off values between groups in this study were defined by the “survcutpoint” function. The Wilcoxon test was used to compare the differences between the groups of numerical variables, the Chi-square test was used to compare the differences between the groups of categorical variables, and the log-rank test was used to compare the survival curves. All statistical analyses were conducted using R software, version 4.2.2 (http://www.r-project.org). The level of significance was set at a P-value <0.05, and all statistical tests were two-sided.

Results

We used the absolute value of the product of the first principal component and the gene expression matrix as the final cell death score. Independent predictive value of cell death features

We aimed to investigate the distinct prognostic ability of five different cell death features in this part. A graphical representation of the correlation between these features and overall survival is illustrated in Fig. 1B. Our findings indicated that only apoptosis and necroptosis features were significantly correlated with overall survival. Pearson correlation analysis revealed a strong association between pyroptosis, apoptosis, and necroptosis while autophagy and cuproptosis demonstrated independence (Fig. 1C). This observation is in line with the concept of “PANoptosis”, where pyroptosis, apoptosis, and necroptosis exhibit a close relationship. Subsequently, we evaluated the prognostic value of these cell death features (Supplement Fig. 1). Our results demonstrated that, among these features, only autophagy and apoptosis could predict overall survival in lung adenocarcinoma. With regards to tumor staging, apoptosis features alone displayed relatively satisfactory predictive ability when compared to other cell death features. Multivariate Cox regression analysis failed to establish cell death features as an independent prognostic predictor for lung adenocarcinoma (Fig. 1D). As such, these analyses indicate that no single cell death pathway can effectively predict the relevant indices in lung adenocarcinoma samples. Rather, a comprehensive feature is needed to achieve a more accurate prediction.

The composite cell death score and preliminary analysis

To achieve a comprehensive indicator representing multiple cell death features, we combined the scores of the five cell death features for each sample to create a new score – the “Composite score”. We then utilized statistical methods to determine the most appropriate cut-off value. The most significant difference in overall survival between the two groups of samples was observed when the Composite score was set to 243.49 (Fig. 2A–B). A heat map was employed to depict the comprehensive landscape of the composite score and five cell death features (Fig. 2C), where Cluster 1 represented the low composite score group and Cluster 2 the high composite score group. The heat map demonstrated significant difference in cell death features between Cluster 1 and Cluster 2, indicating that the composite score accurately represented the cell death features of the samples. Further analysis of the differences in tumor stage between Cluster 1 and Cluster 2 revealed significant distinctions in the clinical stage and N stage, but not in the T and M stages (Fig. 2D–G). These preliminary analyses suggest that the composite score, based on cell death features, shows great promise in differentiating both prognosis and tumor stage in lung adenocarcinoma, and offers excellent predictive value.Fig. 2Construction of the composite score. Tumor microenvironment analyses between clusters

The tumor microenvironment (TME) encompasses a complex network of tumor cells, blood vessels, immune cells, fibroblasts, and extracellular matrix components mobilized by cancer cells [37]. These organ-like structures have been deemed integral in tumor progression, metastasis, and therapeutic resistance. To delineate potential differences in immune cell infiltration within two clusters, we utilized the CIBERSORT algorithm to assess 22 immune cell subtypes. Our results indicated significant variance in immune cell distribution, namely in B cells and macrophages (Fig. 3D). Furthermore, we utilized the ESTIMATE algorithm to predict tumor purity based on transcriptome sequencing data. Data from both clusters unveiled richer infiltration of immune cells and the extracellular matrix in Cluster2, with higher tumor purity observed in Cluster1 (Fig. 3E–H). These findings highlight that the use of a composite score integrating cell death features can effectively distinguish between the TME landscape of tumor samples, with a higher score indicating decreased tumor purity.

Predictive value of composite score in immunotherapy

The Tumor Immune Dysfunction and Exclusion (TIDE) score is an emerging algorithm for evaluating immunotherapy response by comprehensively evaluating the degree of immune dysfunction and immune exclusion [38]. In our study, we employed the TIDE algorithm to evaluate Cluster 1 and Cluster 2, and the results showed that Cluster 1 had a higher immune exclusion score and a lower immune dysfunction score than Cluster 2. Moreover, there were significant differences in TIDE scores between the two clusters (Fig. 4A). In summation, the immunotherapy benefits of Cluster 1 were relatively poorer than Cluster 2 (Fig. 4B–C). Importantly, the TIDE algorithm also provided an extensive analysis of various tumor immune-related indicators. We observed significant differences in CD8, CD274, and IFN-γ, between Cluster 1 and Cluster 2 (Supplement Figs. 2A–C), and discrepancies were also noted in the infiltration of tumor-associated macrophages (TAM) and myeloid-derived suppressor cells (MDSC) (Supplement Figs. 2D–F). Following this, we analyzed the expression of six common immune checkpoints, namely PD1, PD-L1, CTLA4, LAG3, TIM3, and TIGIT. Strikingly, Cluster 1 showed lower expression levels of all six markers, as compared to Cluster 2 (Fig. 4D). These lower expression levels may correlate with the relatively poorer immunotherapy benefits observed with Cluster 1. Scalable predictive model based on cell death features

The analyses presented above demonstrated that the cell death composite score had an extensive predictive value in lung adenocarcinoma. To further validate the predictive value, a scalable model applicable to external data sets was needed. Initially, we needed to identify differences between the two clusters. GSVA analysis listed the top 20 KEGG pathways with the greatest differences (Fig. 4E). The heatmap showed that the two clusters differed significantly in various signaling pathways closely related to cancer and cell death, such as the p53 pathway, RIG-I-like receptor pathway, and toll-like receptor pathway. Next, we identified differentially expressed genes between the two clusters and screened a total of 107 differentially expressed genes with | logFC | ≥ 1.5 and adjusted P value ≤ 0.001. Fig. 5A shows the volcano plot for screening differentially expressed genes and lists several genes of great differences between the two clusters (| logFC |≥2). Then LASSO-Cox regression was used to construct a linear model of differentially expressed genes and overall survival (Fig. 5B–C). We screened four genes to form the final regression equation (Y=IDO1*4.06-GJB2*22.15-PTAFR*5.67-BIRC3*27.53). The result calculated by the regression equation was defined as the "Cell death score" of samples. Survival analysis showed that the cell death score could well differentiate the overall survival of LUAD samples, and a higher cell death score corresponded to a better prognosis level (Fig. 5D). The Sankey plot demonstrated the concordance between the composite score and the newly obtained cell death score. Samples with low composite scores almost entirely entered the high cell death score group, and the low cell death score group almost entirely consisted of samples with high composite scores (Fig. 5E). After the above analysis, we successfully developed a new feature called “Cell death score”. Comprehensive evaluation of cell death score

After constructing a scalable model, we performed a comprehensive assessment of the cell death score. The ROC curve is a common method to evaluate a model's predictive ability. We plotted the ROC curve (Fig. 5F) using the cell death score from the TCGA-LUAD dataset. The AUC values of the cell death score to predict the prognosis of lung adenocarcinoma patients at 1, 3, and 5 years were 0.635, 0.628, and 0.631, respectively. To enhance the model's efficacy, we included age and clinical stage of the patients to construct the nomogram (Fig. 6A). The modified model had a stronger predictive power, and its AUC values for predicting 1-, 3-, and 5-year survival in lung adenocarcinoma improved to 0.727, 0.699, and 0.693 respectively (Fig. 6B). We selected GSE13213, GSE31210, and GSE37745, three external lung adenocarcinoma datasets, and calculated the cell death scores of all samples. The results showed that the cell death score effectively distinguished the overall survival of all three datasets, reaffirming its scalable predictive value for the prognosis of lung adenocarcinoma (Fig. 6C–E). Next, we attempted to assess the cell death score's predictive value on immunotherapy. As publicly available immunotherapy datasets for lung adenocarcinoma were not found, we utilized the "IMvigor210″ dataset of urothelial carcinoma, which included immunotherapy outcomes and survival. The cell death score effectively differentiated overall survival in this urothelial carcinoma dataset (Fig. 6F). Analysis of key genes in cell death scores

The cell death score in this study was established by combining the expression of four genes, namely IDO1, GJB2, PTAFR, and BIRC3. Our findings suggest that these genes might be key genes closely related to multiple cell death pathways, and their expression could predict the prognosis of lung adenocarcinoma as well as the efficacy of immunotherapy. We performed further analysis of these genes to explore their potential as novel biomarkers. Firstly, we compared the expression of these genes between normal and tumor tissues and found that the expression of IDO1, GJB2, and PTAFR differed significantly (Fig. 7A). GJB2 was enriched in tumor tissues, while IDO1 and PTAFR were less expressed in tumor tissues. Moreover, univariate Cox regression analysis showed that the expression of GJB2 and BIRC3 could significantly influence the prognosis and increase the mortality risk of lung adenocarcinoma patients (Fig. 7B). Furthermore, survival analysis depicted that the expression of IDO1, GJB2, and BIRC3 could effectively differentiate the overall survival of lung adenocarcinoma (Fig. 7C). Discussion

Cell death has been a prominent focus in biomedical research for decades. The concept of apoptosis was first formally proposed in 1972, and since then, 12 programmed cell death pathways have been gradually discovered [7,39,40]. These pathways have demonstrated crucial roles in various diseases. In cancer research, searching for potential targets among various cell death pathways and inducing cancer cell death have become essential approaches for exploring new cancer therapies. For example, small-molecule compounds such as ABT-263 (navitoclax) and Venetoclax, which target the Bcl-2 family proteins in the apoptosis pathway, have shown great anti-tumor effects [6,41]. As more and more compounds targeting cell death are being discovered, new perspectives for cancer treatment are opening up, which are significant for improving the plight of patients with malignant tumors.

Recent research indicates that the various pathways involved in cell death do not operate in isolation and exhibit significant crosstalk. Autophagy and apoptosis, for example, may exhibit cross-regulation through key nodes such as Bcl-2 protein, p53 protein, and ATG-5 protein [42,43]. Moreover, the activation of cytokines such as IL-1β is thought to be critical for the crosstalk between pyroptosis and necroptosis [10]. Taken together, these findings suggest that the various cell death pathways are extensively connected at both the activation and effector phases. Blocking a single cell death pathway may not be enough to prevent cell death [44]. Although the study of the crosstalk between cell death pathways is in its infancy, initial research shows promising potential for identifying key molecules involved. In this study, we present a comprehensive model incorporating four of the most extensively studied cell death modalities (Autophagy, Apoptosis, Pyroptosis, and Necroptosis), alongside the newly discovered Cuproptosis, in an effort to explore the potential role of cell death in lung adenocarcinoma and identify potential biomarkers.

We began our study by identifying five cell death features from the transcriptome sequencing data of lung adenocarcinoma by principal component analysis. These five cell death features could well represent the expression patterns of cell death-related genes in the samples, but they did not correlate well with the prognostic and clinical characteristics of patients. However, by the amalgamation of these five cell death features through summation, it resulted in an awe-inspiring outcome. It implied that individual cell death pathways had a minimal impact on lung adenocarcinoma, while a conglomeration of multiple cell death pathways could contribute significantly to the progression of this disease. And the samples were then divided into two clusters by the composite score with the most significant difference in overall survival. This outcome serves as the foundation for the follow-up study.

We next performed further analysis to explore the predictive value of the composite score in more aspects. There were differences in mutations between the two clusters with different composite scores, and TP53 mutations were more frequent in the cluster with high composite scores. TP53 is a classical cancer suppressor gene that can mediate cell death by promoting cell cycle arrest [45]. Multiple studies have shown that TP53 mutation is at high frequency in various cancers and that TP53 mutation is a negative prognostic factor [46]. We conducted additional analyses to investigate the predictive capability of the composite score in diverse aspects. Significant differences in mutations were observed between the two clusters with varying composite scores, and a higher frequency of TP53 mutations were evident in the cluster reflecting high composite scores. TP53 is a classical cancer suppressor gene that can induce cell cycle arrest and stimulate cell death [45]. Numerous studies have established that TP53 mutations are prevalent in different types of cancers and that their presence is a negative prognostic indicator [46]. TME refers to the non-cancerous environment surrounding cancer cells, including non-cancerous cells such as fibroblasts, endothelial cells, immune cells, and a variety of cellular products [47]. Currently, TME is regarded as the key to the immune escape of cancer cells and has received extensive attention [48]. There were differences in various aspects of the TME between the two clusters with different composite scores. For immune cell infiltration, a variety of immune cells differed between the two clusters, especially among B cells and macrophages. There was more B-cell infiltration and less macrophage infiltration in cluster 1 than in cluster 2. This is in accord with previous studies. The infiltration of B cells in cancerous tissues was associated with a better prognosis, while the macrophages were just opposite [49,50]. TME is composed of non-cancerous cells such as fibroblasts, endothelial cells, immune cells, as well as various cellular products that surround cancer cells [47]. Currently, the TME is considered as a critical factor for cancer cells' immune evasion and has attracted considerable attention [48]. The two clusters with different composite scores presented differences in several TME aspects. In terms of immune cell infiltration, the two clusters displayed varying levels of different immune cells, including B cells and macrophages. Cluster 1 demonstrated higher B cell infiltration and lower macrophage infiltration compared to cluster 2. It is consistent with prior studies, in which the infiltration of B cells in cancerous tissues has been positively linked to better prognosis, while macrophages tend to be associated with worse outcomes [49,50]. The ESTIMATE score is one of a tool for evaluating tumor microenvironments. All four scores determined by ESTIMATE were significantly different between the two clusters. Cluster 1 had a higher tumor purity while cluster 2 exhibited greater presence of non-cancerous components. Immunotherapy provides a novel approach to combating cancer by amplifying the body's immune system to eliminate tumor cells. Though its use has vastly diversified cancer drug therapy, a key hurdle is that a small fraction of patients benefit from the treatment [51,52]. In this study, the TIDE algorithm was employed to foresee the efficacy of immunotherapy between the two clusters. The outcomes signified that cluster 2 would derive greater benefits from immunotherapy compared to cluster 1. Furthermore, checkpoint blocker expressions were notably higher in cluster 2 versus cluster 1. Our research further established that cell death genes' expressions were intimately interconnected with the tumor microenvironment and exerted significant impacts on immunotherapy benefits.

We conducted a thorough investigation into the predictive power of cell death characteristics and sought to uncover the mechanisms behind this phenomenon while also creating a scalable model for prediction. Upon analysis, we discovered that there were numerous genes that were differentially expressed in the two groups being studied. We focused on a select few of these genes, specifically ENO3, ANKRD22, and ZBED2. ENO3 is an enolase mainly involved in glycolysis and is a cancer suppressor in hepatocellular carcinoma and lung cancer [53,54]. ANKRD22 was found to promote the proliferation of a variety of tumor cells [[55], [56], [57]]. ZBED2 acts as a transcriptional regulator that can enhance pancreatic cancer invasiveness [58]. These findings indicate that the composite score we created was capable of identifying several genes that are significantly linked to the development of cancer beyond just lung adenocarcinoma. Through the use of these differentially expressed genes, we created a cell death score that was scalable to external datasets. This score maintained the cell death characteristics of the samples and demonstrated strong predictive abilities for prognosis. When combined with the patient's age and clinical stage, the AUC values for predicting the prognosis of lung adenocarcinoma patients at 1, 3, and 5 years reached approximately 0.7. The predictive potential of the cell death score was also validated in multiple external datasets.

The cell death score was determined by the expression of four genes: IDO1, GJB2, PTAFR, and BIRC3. These genes may potentially serve as biomarkers that connect cell death to cancer prognosis and the effectiveness of immunotherapy. Of particular interest is GJB2, which demonstrates high expression in lung adenocarcinoma tissues and functions as an independent risk factor for such cancer. The GJB2 gene encodes a cell-linked protein, and current studies on it are mainly focused on Non-Syndromic Hearing Loss (NSHL) [59]. However, studies have also confirmed the role of GJB2 as a promoter in cancer [[60], [61], [62]]. Thus, GJB2 holds great research prospect.

Conclusion

This article is a bioinformatics study focusing on the synergistic effects of multiple cell death pathways in lung adenocarcinoma. Previous studies have extensively discussed the roles of different cell death pathways in various cancers, but few have simultaneously considered multiple pathways. And there is no consensus on the scientific and rigorous analysis methods to study the synergistic effects of different cell death pathways. Based on this, we conducted a series of analyses.

The expression profiles of five common cell death pathway-related genes (autophagy, apoptosis, necroptosis, pyroptosis, and cuproptosis) were analyzed, and a "composite score" was constructed. The composite score demonstrated good differentiating effect in prognosis, tumor staging, gene mutations, tumor microenvironment, and other aspects. We also constructed a generalized prediction model with only four genes based on the "composite score". This model showed excellent predictive abilities in multiple datasets. Furthermore, we identify a promising new biomarker, GJB2, with significant research potential in the field of cell death-related cancer therapies.

Our study suggests that there is a synergistic effect of multiple cell death pathways in lung adenocarcinoma. Instead of considering each cell death pathway in isolation, combining them together may have broad research prospects. And our innovative scoring method provides a new approach for such studies. Admittedly, the main limitation of this study is that we did not validate the method in real-world, and further research is needed.

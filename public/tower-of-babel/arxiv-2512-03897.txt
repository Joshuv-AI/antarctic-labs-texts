1.
Introduction

In this paper, we consider the focusing Gibbs measures
for the nonlinear Schrödinger equations (NLS),
initiated in the seminal paper by Lebowitz, Rose, and Speer
[
24
]
.
A Gibbs measure
\rho

is a probability measure on
functions / distributions
whose density can be
formally
given by

\displaystyle d\rho=\frac{1}{Z}e^{-H(u)}du,

(1.1)

where
H(u)
is a Hamiltonian functional and
Z
is a normalisation constant, called partition function.
In particular, we investigate in the logarithmic
Sobolev inequalities (see (
1.7
)) for the focusing
nonlinear Schrödinger equation (NLS)
on the circle
\mathbb{T}=\mathbb{R}/(2\pi\mathbb{Z})
:

\displaystyle iu_{t}+\Delta u+|u|^{p-2}u=0,

(1.2)

for which the Hamiltonian
H(u)
is given by

\displaystyle H(u)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}}|\nabla u|^{2}dx-\frac{1}{p}\int_{\mathbb{T}}|u|^{p}dx.

(1.3)

The study of the equation (
1.2
)
from the viewpoint of the (non-)equilibrium statistical mechanics
has received wide attention; see for example
[
24
,
12
,
13
,
14
,
32
,
33
,
23
,
15
,
17
,
19
,
16
]
. See also
[
8
]
for a survey on the subject, more from the dynamical point of view.

The main difficulty in constructing the focusing Gibbs measures
comes from the high degree
p>2
of the negative term in the Hamiltonian (
1.3
).
This makes the problem extremely different from the defocusing case, which would correspond to the well studied
\Phi^{p}
model of quantum field theory. In
[
24
]
, Lebowitz, Rose, and Speer suggested
to consider the Gibbs measure with an extra
L^{2}
-cutoff

\displaystyle d\rho=\frac{1}{Z_{K}}e^{-H(u)}\mathbbm{1}_{\{M(u)\leq K\}}du,

(1.4)

where
M(u)
denotes the mass functional

M(u)=\int_{\mathbb{T}}|u|^{2}dx.

(1.5)

This choice is suitable for the study of the statistical mechanics of NLS, since the mass
M(u)
is conserved by the flow of NLS, and represents the “generalised number of particles”.
In a series of papers
[
24
,
12
,
29
,
31
]
, the authors showed the following.

Proposition 1.1
.

Consider the Gaussian measure

d\mu\propto\exp\Big(-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}}|u|^{2}dx-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}}|\nabla u|^{2}dx\Big)dud\overline{u}.

(1.6)

Then the measure
\rho
in (
1.4
) is well-defined in the following cases:

(1)

p<6
,
K\geq 0
,

(2)

p=6
, and
K\leq\|Q\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}
, where
Q
is the ground state of the following elliptic equation on
\mathbb{R}

\Delta Q+Q^{5}+2Q=0.

Moreover, we have that in all the cases above, the Radon-Nikodym derivative
f=\frac{d\rho}{d\mu}
satisfies

(1)

If
p<6
,
K\geq 0
,
f\in L^{q}(\mu)
for every
q<\infty
,

(2)

If
p=6
, and
K\leq\|Q\|_{L^{2}}^{2}
, then
f\in L^{q}(\mu)
for every

q\leq\Big(\frac{\|Q\|_{L^{2}}^{2}}{K}\Big)^{2},

(3)

If
p=6
, then for every
\alpha<1
,
f\in L(\log L)^{\alpha}
,
[
22
, Proposition 6]
.

Here, we focus our attention on the logarithmic Sobolev inequality (LSI) for the Gibbs measures with mass-cutoff. Namely, we ask whether the measure

\rho
in (
1.4
) satisfies an inequality of the form

\int|F(u)|^{2}\log\Big(\frac{|F(u)|^{2}}{\int|F(u)|^{2}\,d\rho(u)}\Big)d\rho(u)\;\leq\;C\int\|\nabla F(u)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\,d\rho(u),

(1.7)

for every suitable smooth functional
F:L^{2}(\mathbb{T})\to\mathbb{R}
and some constant
C>0
.

When such an inequality (
1.7
) holds, we define

\mathrm{LS}(\rho):=\inf\Big\{C>0\,:\,\eqref{LSI}\text{ holds for all admissible }F\Big\},

(1.8)

that is,
\mathrm{LS}(\rho)
is the best (smallest) constant for which (
1.7
) is valid.
If there is no finite constant
C
for which (
1.7
) holds, we set

\mathrm{LS}(\rho):=\infty.

(1.9)

Logarithmic Sobolev inequalities (
1.7
) are important
functional inequalities firstly established by Gross in

[
20
]

for Gaussian measures, where its equivalence to hypercontractivity was also shown. LSI is a powerful tool in showing the uniqueness of invariant measure and exponential rate of convergence of the Markov semigroup towards the invariant measure in models from statistical mechanics and quantum field theory.
See
[
5
,
21
]
for comprehensive reviews.

In
[
4
]
, Bakry and Émery provide a nice sufficient condition for LSI under log-concave measures.
For a Gibbs measure
\rho
as in (
1.1
),
Bakry-Émery criterion
(see Proposition
2.1
) implies
LSI holds when the Hamiltonian
H
is strictly
convex. For applications of Bakry-Émery criterion,
see
[
18
,
25
,
21
]
.

In the context of Gibbs measures for NLS,
[
9
]
was the first paper proving an LSI. They applied
the Bakry-Émery criterion
to the Gibbs measures for focusing NLS when
2\leq p\leq 4
. We however point out that in
[
9
]
, the authors seem to overlook the complications deriving from the presence of the cutoff function,
1
1

1

We remark that if the cutoff function in (
1.4
) is instead on a non-convex subset of the ball, then the log-Sobolev inequality (
1.7
) might fail (counterexamples can be constructed easily when the set is disconnected and
F
is constant on the connected components), but the argument in
[
9
]
cannot distinguish the two situations.
so
for completeness of exposition, we revisit this result and establish the following LSI.

Our first main result is the following.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
2\leq p\leq 4
and
K>0
, and let
\rho
be the focusing Gibbs measure
with
L^{2}
-cutoff at level
K
given in (
1.4
), associated with the
nonlinear Schrödinger equation (
1.2
) on
\mathbb{T}
.
Then
\rho
satisfies a logarithmic Sobolev inequality of the form
(
1.7
). In particular,

\mathrm{LS}(\rho)<\infty,

in the sense of
definition
(
1.8
).

When
p>4
, the situation is more complex.
In general, establishing LSI beyond
the strict convexity assumption on

H
is challenging, especially on infinite-dimensional spaces.
In recent years, there have been several breakthroughs by Bauerschmidt, Bodineau, and collaborators, see

[
1
,
2
,
3
]
, that culminated with the proof of uniqueness for the
\varphi^{4}_{2}
measure in high temperature in
[
7
]
. These results have been obtained via the use of a “multiscale Bakry-Émery formula”,
[
2
, Theorem 2.5]
, that we now describe in the particular case of the measure
\rho
.
Define the family of Gaussian measures
\bar{\mu}_{t}

formally
given by

\displaystyle d\bar{\mu}_{t}(u)\propto\exp\Big(-\frac{1}{2}\langle u,\frac{\langle\nabla\rangle^{2}}{1-e^{-t\langle\nabla\rangle^{2}}}u\rangle\Big)dud\overline{u}.

Note that if
u
is a random variable with
\Law(u)=\mu
in (
1.6
), then

\Law(\sqrt{1-e^{-t\langle\nabla\rangle^{2}}}u)=\bar{\mu}_{t}.

(1.10)

We then set

\displaystyle V_{t}(\varphi)=\log\Big(\mathbb{E}_{\bar{\mu}_{t}}\Big[\exp\Big(\frac{1}{p}\int_{\mathbb{T}}|u+\varphi|^{p}dx\Big)\mathbbm{1}_{\|u+\varphi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq K}\Big]\Big)

for every
\varphi\in H^{1}
.
Theorem 2.5 in
[
2
]
states
that under some suitable
ergodicity assumption,
if for every
t\geq 0
there exists a differentiable map
t\mapsto\lambda_{t}\in\mathbb{R}
such that

\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-2\lambda_{t}}dt<\infty

and the Hessian of
V_{t}(\varphi)

denoted by

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}(V_{t})(\varphi)[e^{-\frac{t\langle\nabla\rangle^{2}}{2}}w,e^{-\frac{t\langle\nabla\rangle^{2}}{2}}w]\leq\frac{1}{2}\|w\|_{H^{1}}^{2}-\dot{\lambda}_{t}\|w\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

(1.11)

for all
w\in H^{1}
,
then LSI holds.

Instead of working with
(
1.11
), we consider the functional

V(\varphi)=\log\Big(\mathbb{E}_{\mu}\Big[\exp\Big(\frac{1}{p}\int_{\mathbb{T}}|u+\varphi|^{p}dx\Big)\mathbbm{1}_{\|u+\varphi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq K}\Big]\Big),

(1.12)

where
\mu
is as in (
1.6
).
Note that, abusing of notation, we have
V=V_{\infty}
.

By taking
t
large in (
1.11
), by boundedness of the operator
\sqrt{1-e^{-t\langle\nabla\rangle^{2}}}
and its inverse,
due to the integrability condition for
\lambda_{t}
, in order to apply this criterion we essentially require that, for
w\in H^{1}
,

\sup_{\varphi\in H^{1}}\mathcal{H}(V)(\varphi)[w,w]<\infty.

(1.13)

We define the probability measure

d\rho_{\varphi}(u)=\frac{1}{Z_{\varphi}}\exp\Big(\frac{1}{p}\int_{\mathbb{T}}|u+\varphi|^{p}dx\Big)\mathbbm{1}_{\|u+\varphi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq K}d\mu(u),

(1.14)

where
Z_{\varphi}
is an appropriate normalisation constant.

The second main result of this paper is the following.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
4<p<6
and let
V
be defined by (
1.12
), with

\rho_{\varphi}
the probability measures given in (
1.14
).
Then the following hold:

(i)
For every
\varphi\in H^{1}(\mathbb{T})
and every
w\in H^{1}(\mathbb{T})
,

\mathcal{H}V(\varphi)[w,w]\;\geq\;(p-1)\int_{\mathbb{T}}\mathbb{E}_{\rho_{\varphi}}\big[\,|u(x)|^{p-2}\,\big]\,|w(x)|^{2}\,dx.

(1.15)

(ii)
We have

\sup_{\varphi\in H^{1}(\mathbb{T})}\mathbb{E}_{\rho_{\varphi}}\Big[\int_{\mathbb{T}}|u(x)|^{p-2}\,dx\Big]\;=\;\infty.

(1.16)

In particular, for every
w\in H^{1}(\mathbb{T})\setminus\{0\}
,

\sup_{\varphi\in H^{1}(\mathbb{T})}\mathcal{H}V(\varphi)[w,w]\;=\;\infty.

(1.17)

Theorem
1.3

tells us, when
4<p<6
, the Hessian
\mathcal{H}V(\varphi)

cannot be uniformly bounded from above on
H^{1}(\mathbb{T})
in any fixed
direction
w\neq 0
, and thus the multiscale Bakry-Émery criterion
from
[
2
]
cannot be used to establish an LSI for the associated
Gibbs measure.

2.
Preliminaries

Throughout the paper we work with complex-valued functions

u:\mathbb{T}\to\mathbb{C}
and view
L^{2}(\mathbb{T};\mathbb{C})=L^{2}(\mathbb{T})
as a real Hilbert space with scalar
product

\langle f,g\rangle:=\Re\int_{\mathbb{T}}f(x)\,\overline{g(x)}\,dx.

In particular,
\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=\langle u,u\rangle
and all gradients
and Hessians are taken with respect to this real structure.
Whenever we write
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{L^{2}}
below, it is this
(real) scalar product.

We use
C>0
to denote various constants, which may vary line by line. We also use
A\lesssim B
to denote an estimate of the form
A\leq CB
for some constant
C>0
. We denote by
dx
the Lebesgue measure on the torus, normalised so that
\int_{\mathbb{T}}dx=1
.

Given
N\in\mathbb{N}
,
we denote by
P_{N}

the projector onto the frequencies

\{|n|\leq N\}

defined by

\displaystyle\widehat{P_{N}f}(n)=\mathbbm{1}_{\{|n|\leq N\}}\widehat{f}(n).

We remark that due to the boundedness of the Hilbert transform on the torus, we have that

\displaystyle\|P_{N}f-f\|_{L^{p}}\to 0

(2.1)

as
N\to\infty
, for every
f\in L^{p}
,
1<p<\infty
.

Finally, we set
x_{+}=\max\{x,0\}
.

To show Theorem
1.2
,
we will use the following celebrated criterion.

Proposition 2.1

(Bakry-Émery
[
4
]
)
.

Consider a probability measure on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
(or a linear sub-space) of the form

d\nu(x)\propto e^{-H(x)}dx,

and assume that there is
\lambda>0
such that as quadratic forms:

\mathcal{H}(H(x))\geq\lambda\cdot\mathrm{id}.

(2.2)

Then, we have

\mathrm{LS}(\nu)\leq\frac{2}{\lambda}<\infty.

\displaystyle W_{t}=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}B_{n}(t)e^{inx},

Lemma 2.5

Proof of Theorem

Throughout this section, we consider the measure

d\rho_{\Lambda}(u)=\frac{1}{Z_{\Lambda}}\exp(-\Lambda\int_{\mathbb{T}}|u|^{2}dx)d\rho(u),

(3.1)

where
\Lambda>0
is an appropriate normalisation constant. Since
\rho
is supported on the set
\{\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq K\}
, we have that

\exp(-\Lambda K)\mathrm{LS}(\rho)\leq\mathrm{LS}(\rho_{\Lambda})\leq\exp(\Lambda K)\mathrm{LS}(\rho).

(3.2)

We have the following lemma.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
p<6
, and let
N\in\mathbb{N}
,
R>0

and
\Lambda>0
. Consider the measures

\displaystyle d

\displaystyle\rho_{\Lambda,N,R}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{Z_{\Lambda,N,R}}\exp\Big(-\Lambda\int_{\mathbb{T}}|P_{N}u|^{2}dx+\frac{1}{p}\int_{\mathbb{T}}|P_{N}u|^{p}dx-R(\|P_{N}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-K)_{+}^{8}\Big)d\mu.

Then we have that

\lim_{N\to\infty}\lim_{R\to\infty}\rho_{\Lambda,N,R}=\rho_{\Lambda},

where
\rho_{\Lambda}
is given as
in (
3.1
) and the limits are taken in total variation.

Proof.

It is enough to show that

\displaystyle\lim_{N\to\infty}

\displaystyle\lim_{R\to\infty}\exp\Big(-\Lambda\int_{\mathbb{T}}|P_{N}u|^{2}dx+\frac{1}{p}\int_{\mathbb{T}}|P_{N}u|^{p}dx-R(\|P_{N}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-K)_{+}^{8}\Big)

\displaystyle\quad\,=\exp\Big(-\Lambda\int_{\mathbb{T}}|u|^{2}dx+\frac{1}{p}\int_{\mathbb{T}}|u|^{p}dx\Big)\mathbbm{1}_{\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq K}

in
L^{1}(\mu)
.
We first check pointwise convergence and then verify a uniform
integrability bound.

We have that

\displaystyle\lim_{N\to\infty}

\displaystyle\lim_{R\to\infty}\exp\Big(-\Lambda\int_{\mathbb{T}}|P_{N}u|^{2}dx+\frac{1}{p}\int_{\mathbb{T}}|P_{N}u|^{p}dx-R(\|P_{N}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-K)_{+}^{8}\Big)

\displaystyle\quad\,=\exp\Big(-\Lambda\int_{\mathbb{T}}|u|^{2}dx+\frac{1}{p}\int_{\mathbb{T}}|u|^{p}dx\Big)\mathbbm{1}_{\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq K}

pointwise, so it is enough to show that for any
q>1
,

\sup_{N\in\mathbb{N}}\limsup_{R\to\infty}\int\exp\Big(\frac{q}{p}\int_{\mathbb{T}}|P_{N}u|^{p}-qR(\|P_{N}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-K)_{+}^{8}\Big)d\mu(u)<\infty.

(3.3)

By dominated convergence, we have that

Proof of Theorem

.

By (

The proof of Theorem

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
V(\varphi)
be given as in (
1.12
). Then for all

\varphi\in H^{1}(\mathbb{T})
, we have that

\mathcal{H}(V)(\varphi)[w,w]\geq(p-1)\int_{\mathbb{T}}\mathbb{E}_{\rho_{\varphi}}[|u|^{p-2}]|w|^{2}dx.

(4.1)

Proof.

By Cameron-Martin’s theorem
(see e.g.
[
11
, Corollary 2.4.3]
), we may write that

\displaystyle V(\varphi)=\log\bigg(\mathbb{E}_{\mu}\bigg[\exp\Big(\frac{1}{p}\int_{\mathbb{T}}|u|^{p}+\langle u,(1-\Delta)\varphi\rangle-\frac{1}{2}\|\varphi\|_{H^{1}}^{2}\Big)\mathbbm{1}_{\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq K}\bigg]\bigg).

In particular, we have that
V
is smooth as a function of
\varphi
, and
by computation similar to
(
3.4
) and the argument in the proof of
Lemma
3.1
, we have

\mathcal{H}V(\varphi)=\lim_{R\to\infty}\mathcal{H}V_{R}(\varphi),

where

V_{R}(\varphi)=\log\bigg(\mathbb{E}_{\mu}\bigg[\exp\Big(\frac{1}{p}\int_{\mathbb{T}}|u+\varphi|^{p}dx-R\big(\|u+\varphi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-K\big)_{+}^{\sigma}\Big)\bigg]\bigg),

and
\sigma>\frac{p}{2}+1
.
For an appropriately integrable functional
F(\varphi)
, by the chain rule we have that

\displaystyle\nabla\log\big(\mathbb{E}_{\mu}[\exp(F(\varphi))]\big)=\frac{\mathbb{E}_{\mu}[\nabla F\exp(F(\varphi))]}{\mathbb{E}_{\mu}[\exp(F(\varphi))]}

and so

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}\log\big(\mathbb{E}_{\mu}[\exp(F(\varphi))]\big)[w,w]

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathbb{E}_{\mu}[\mathcal{H}F[w,w]\exp(F(\varphi))]}{\mathbb{E}_{\mu}[\exp(F(\varphi))]}+\frac{\mathbb{E}_{\mu}[|\nabla F\cdot w|^{2}\exp(F(\varphi))]}{\mathbb{E}_{\mu}[\exp(F(\varphi))]}

\displaystyle\quad-\Big|\frac{\mathbb{E}_{\mu}[\nabla F\cdot w\exp(F(\varphi))]}{\mathbb{E}_{\mu}[\exp(F(\varphi))]}\Big|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\mathbb{E}_{\mu}[\mathcal{H}F[w,w]\exp(F(\varphi))]}{\mathbb{E}_{\mu}[\exp(F(\varphi))]},

where we used Jensen’s inequality for probability measure

\frac{d\nu}{d\mu}=\frac{\exp(F(\varphi))}{\mathbb{E}_{\mu}[\exp(F(\varphi))]}

in the last step.
Applying to
V_{R}
, we get

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}(V_{R})(\varphi)[w,w]

\displaystyle\geq(p-1)\frac{\mathbb{E}_{\mu}\Big[\big(\int_{\mathbb{T}}|u+\varphi|^{p-2}|w|^{2}\big)\exp\Big(\frac{1}{p}\int_{\mathbb{T}}|u+\varphi|^{p}-R\big(\|u+\varphi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-K\big)_{+}^{\sigma}\Big)\Big]}{\mathbb{E}_{\mu}\Big[\exp\Big(\frac{1}{p}\int_{\mathbb{T}}|u+\varphi|^{p}-R\big(\|u+\varphi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-K\big)_{+}^{\sigma}\Big)\Big]}

\displaystyle\phantom{\geq\ }-\frac{\mathbb{E}_{\mu}\Big[\big(R\mathcal{H}\big(\|u+\varphi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-K\big)_{+}^{\sigma}[w,w]\big)\exp\Big(\frac{1}{p}\int_{\mathbb{T}}|u+\varphi|^{p}-R\big(\|u+\varphi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-K\big)_{+}^{\sigma}\Big)\Big]}{\mathbb{E}_{\mu}\Big[\exp\Big(\frac{1}{p}\int_{\mathbb{T}}|u+\varphi|^{p}-R\big(\|u+\varphi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-K\big)_{+}^{\sigma}\Big)\Big]}.

By taking limits as
R\to\infty,
we deduce

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}(V)(\varphi)[w,w]

\displaystyle\geq(p-1)\frac{\mathbb{E}\Big[\big(\int_{\mathbb{T}}|u+\varphi|^{p-2}|w|^{2}\big)\exp\Big(\frac{1}{p}\int_{\mathbb{T}}|u+\varphi|^{p}\Big)\mathbbm{1}_{\|u+\varphi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq K}\Big]}{\mathbb{E}\Big[\exp\Big(\frac{1}{p}\int_{\mathbb{T}}|u+\varphi|^{p}\Big)\mathbbm{1}_{\|u+\varphi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq K}\Big]},

which is (
4.1
).

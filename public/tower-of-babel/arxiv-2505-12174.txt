On 𝔪 \mathfrak{m} -adic Continuity of F F -Splitting Ratio
Maria Akter

Abstract




Abstract.
We investigate the
\mathfrak{m}
-adic continuity of Frobenius splitting dimensions and ratios for divisor pairs
(R,\Delta)
in an
F
-finite local ring
(R,\mathfrak{m},k)
of prime characteristic
p>0
. Our main result states that if
R
is an
F
-finite,
\mathbb{Q}
-Gorenstein, Cohen-Macaulay local ring of prime characteristic
p>0
, the Frobenius splitting numbers
a^{\Delta}_{e}(R)
remain unchanged under a suitable small perturbation. Moreover, we establish a desirable inequality of Frobenius splitting dimensions under general perturbations. That is,
\dim(R/(\mathcal{P}(R/(f),\Delta|_{f})))\leq\dim(R/(\mathcal{P}(R/(f+\varepsilon),\Delta|_{(f+\varepsilon)})))
for all
\varepsilon\in\mathfrak{m}^{N\gg 0}
, providing an example that demonstrates strict improvement can occur.

1.
Introduction

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m},k)
be a local
F
-finite ring of prime characteristic
p>0
. For each
e\in\mathbb{N}
, let
F^{e}_{*}R
be the finitely generated
R
-module obtained via the restriction of scalars under the
e
th
iteration of the Frobenius map. If
e\in\mathbb{N}
, consider the
e
th
Frobenius splitting number of
R
, denoted by
a_{e}(R)
, defined by
F^{e}_{*}R\cong R^{\bigoplus{a_{e}(R)}}\bigoplus M_{e}
, where
M_{e}
has no free
R
-summand. The Frobenius splitting ratio of
R
is the asymptotic rate of the number
a_{e}(R)
grows as
e\to\infty
, see
[
1
]
. If
(R,\mathfrak{m},k)
is a local
F
-finite,
\QQ
-Gorenstein, Cohen-Macaulay ring of prime characteristic
p>0
and
0\neq f\in\mathfrak{m}
, then the Frobenius splitting numbers of
R/(f)
can be measured through compatible Frobenius splittings in
R
via techniques of Inversion of Adjunction for
F
-purity, see
[
13
]
. For other relevant situations, see
[
5
,
7
,
17
,
19
]
. This article employs this technique, which allows comparisons between the Frobenius splitting ratio of the pair
R/(f)
and
R/(f+\varepsilon)
for
\varepsilon\in\mathfrak{m}^{N}
and
N\gg 0
. As a motivating example let
R=k[[x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n}]]
be the power series ring in
n
variables over a field
k
. If we add a term of sufficiently high order to an element
f\in R
, will the Frobenius splitting ratio be similar to that of
f
?

In 1956, Samuel
[
16
]
first introduced perturbation problems in local rings, showing that if
f
defines a hypersurface with an isolated singularity, then for sufficiently small perturbations
g
, the quotient rings
R/(f)
and
R/(g)
remain isomorphic. Hironaka
[
9
]
extended this result to reduced, equidimensional isolated singularities, establishing stability under high-order perturbations. Cutkosky and Srinivasan
[
4
]
further generalized these ideas to complete intersection prime ideals and reduced equidimensional ideals. Srinivas and Trivedi
[
18
]
demonstrated that in generalized Cohen-Macaulay rings, the associated graded rings of a parameter ideal remain unchanged under small perturbations.

Numerical invariants in positive characteristic have been widely studied, especially in relation to singularities under
\mathfrak{m}
-adic perturbations. Srinivas and Trivedi
[
18
]
showed that the Hilbert function generally decreases under small perturbations, while Ma, Quy, and Smirnov
[
12
]
extended this by proving that the Hilbert function remains invariant for filter-regular sequences. Polstra and Smirnov
[
15
]
demonstrate
\mathfrak{m}
-adic continuity of the Hilbert-Kunz multiplicity in parameter ideals of Cohen-Macaulay rings, and similar results are shown for the
F
-signature in Gorenstein rings
[
15
]
and for
\QQ
-Gorenstein rings
[
6
]
.
This area of research continues to develop in more recent works
[
6
,
14
]
.

In
[
14
, Theorem 3.6]
, Polstra and Simpson proved that if
(R,\mathfrak{m},k)
is a local
F
-finite, Cohen-Macaulay,
\QQ
-Gorenstein ring of prime
characteristic
p>0
and
f\in\mathfrak{m}
is a nonzero divisor such that
R/(f)
is Gorenstein in codimension 1 and
F
-pure, then there exists
N\gg 0
such that
R/(f+\varepsilon)
is
F
-pure for every
\varepsilon\in\mathfrak{m}^{N}
. In
[
6
, Theorem 3.8, Corollary 3.9]
, De Stefani and Smirnov prove the
\mathfrak{m}
-adic continuity of another type of numerical invariant, defined by Hochster and Yao in
[
21
, Definition 0.1]
, called the
F
-rational signature
s_{\mathrm{rat}}(R)
for an
F
-finite Cohen-Macaulay local ring of prime characteristic
p>0
.

A natural question that arises is whether
\mathfrak{m}
-adic continuity holds for another type of numerical invariant used to measure singularities, known as the Frobenius splitting ratio, denoted by
r_{F}(R)
. This invariant was first introduced by Aberbach and Enescu in
[
1
, Definition 2.4]
. Specifically, let
(R,\mathfrak{m},k)
be a local
F
-finite,
F
-pure ring of prime
characteristic
p>0
, where
k
is a perfect field, and set
n\coloneqq\dim\bigl(R/\PP(R)\bigr),

where
\PP(R)
denotes the splitting prime ideal of
R
. We refer to
n
as
the splitting dimension of
R
. The Frobenius splitting ratio of
R

is then defined by

r_{F}(R)\coloneqq\lim_{e\to\infty}\frac{a_{e}}{p^{en}},

where this limit is known to exist, see
[
3
]
.
Moreover, it satisfies
0<r_{F}(R)\leq 1.

The focus of this article is to study splitting ratios and splitting dimensions of perturbations under suitable hypotheses; the following applications are of the main results of the article.

Theorem A
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m},k)
be a local
F
-finite,
F
-pure,
\QQ
-Gorenstein, Cohen-Macaulay ring of prime characteristic
p>0
and
f\in R
be a nonzero divisor of
R
. Let
\PP(R/(f))
be the lift of the splitting prime of
R/(f)
in
R
, then there exist an
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideal
\mathfrak{a}
in
R
and
e_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that for all
\varepsilon\in\mathfrak{a}\cap\PP^{[p^{e_{0}}]}\ \text{and all }e\in\mathbb{N}

a_{e}(R/(f))=a_{e}(R/(f+\varepsilon)).

In particular, if
\PP(R/(f))=\mathfrak{m}/(f)
, then there exists an
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideal
\mathfrak{a}
so that for all
\varepsilon\in\mathfrak{a}
,

\PP(R/(f+\varepsilon))=\mathfrak{m}/(f+\varepsilon).

Our next main result establishes a desirable relationship between the splitting dimensions of
R/(f)
and
R/(f+\varepsilon)
for
\varepsilon\in\mathfrak{m}^{[p^{e_{0}}]}
.

Theorem B
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m},k)
be a local
F
-finite,
F
-pure,
\QQ
-Gorenstein, Cohen-Macaulay ring of prime characteristic
p>0
and
f\in\mathfrak{m}
be a nonzero divisor of
R
. Then there exists an integer
e_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that for all
\varepsilon\in\mathfrak{m}^{[p^{e_{0}}]}
,

\dim\bigl(R/\PP(R/(f))\bigr)\leq\dim\bigl(R/\PP(R/(f+\varepsilon))\bigr).

However, equality in Theorem
B
does not hold in general, as illustrated in Example
4.2
.

Theorem
C
is a result of independent interest and is applicable to any
F
-finite Gorenstein local ring.

Theorem C
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m},k)
be an
F
-finite,
F
-pure, Gorenstein local ring of dimension
d
of prime characteristic
p>0
. The following are equivalent:

(i)

a_{1}(R)=[k^{1/p}:k].

(ii)

There exists
e\in\mathbb{N}
such that
a_{e}(R)=[k^{1/p^{e}}:k]
.

(iii)

For all
e\in\mathbb{N}
,
a_{e}(R)=[k^{1/p^{e}}:k]
.

(iv)

I_{1}(R)=\mathfrak{m}.

(v)

There exists
e\in\mathbb{N}
such that
I_{e}(R)=\mathfrak{m}
.

(vi)

For all
e\in\mathbb{N}
,
I_{e}(R)=\mathfrak{m}
.

(vii)

The splitting prime ideal
\mathcal{P}(R)
of

equals
\mathfrak{m}
.

2.
Preliminaries

Throughout this paper,
(R,\mathfrak{m},k)
denotes a Noetherian local ring of prime characteristic
p>0
, with residue field
k=R/\mathfrak{m}
. The Frobenius endomorphism
F:R\to R
will be denoted as
F
and
F^{e}
denotes the
e
th iteration of Frobenius endomorphism
F
. For a given ideal
I\subseteq R
and
e\in\mathbb{N}
, let
I^{[p^{e}]}\coloneqq(i^{p^{e}}\mid i\in I)
represent the expansion of
I
under
F^{e}
. We say
R
is
F
-finite
if
F^{e}_{*}R
is finitely generated
R
-module. If
M
is an
R
-module then
F^{e}_{*}M
is the
R
-module obtained by the restrictions of scalars under
F^{e}
.

The following classical result is fundamental for understanding the number of free direct
R
-summands of
F^{e}_{*}R
.

Theorem 2.1
.

[
10
]

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m},k)
be an excellent local ring, and let
M
be a finitely generated
R
-module equipped with a map
R\to M
. Then the following statements are equivalent:

(i)

The map
R\to M
splits.

(ii)

The induced map
E_{R}(k)\to E_{R}(k)\otimes_{R}M
is injective, where
E_{R}(k)
denotes the injective hull of
k
.

(iii)

The map
R\to M
is pure; that is, for any
R
-module
N
, the natural map
N\to M\otimes_{R}N
is injective.

Let
M
be a finitely generated
R
-module that has no free direct
R
-summands. Then for every
m\in M
, the map

R\longrightarrow M,\quad 1\mapsto m,

does not split. Therefore, by Theorem
2.1
, the induced map

E_{R}(k)\longrightarrow E_{R}(k)\otimes_{R}M,\quad a\mapsto a\otimes m,

is not injective. The following results from
[
1
]
are fundamental in defining the splitting prime ideal of
R
.

Proposition 2.2
.

[
1
]

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m},k)
be a local ring,
M
be a finitely generated
R
-module, and let
u
be the socle generator of
E_{R}(k)
. Then
M
has no nonzero free direct summands if and only if for every
m\in M
,

u\otimes m=0\quad\text{in }E_{R}(k)\otimes_{R}M.

Corollary 2.3
.

[
1
]

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m},k)
be an
F
-finite, reduced local ring of characteristic
p
. Let

F^{e}_{*}R=R^{a_{e}(R)}\oplus M_{e}
be a direct sum decomposition of
F^{e}_{*}R
over
R
as an
R
-module, where
M_{e}
has no free direct
R
-summands. Define

I_{e}(R):=\bigl\{r\in R\mid F^{e}_{*}r\otimes u=0\ \text{in }F^{e}_{*}R\otimes_{R}E_{R}(k)\bigr\}.

Then,
a_{e}(R)=\lambda_{R}\bigl(F^{e}_{*}R/F^{e}_{*}I_{e}(R)\bigr)
, where
\lambda_{R}(-)
denotes the length as an
R
-module.

The concept of splitting prime ideal, Frobenius splitting dimension, and Frobenius splitting ratio were first introduced by Aberbach and Enescu
[
1
]
. The following definition is from
[
1
]
.

Definition 2.4
.

[
1
, Definition 3.2]

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m},k)
be an
F
-finite,
F
-pure local ring of prime characteristic
p>0
. For every
c\in R
and
e\in\mathbb{N}
, let
\phi_{c,e}:R\to F^{e}_{*}R
be the
R
-linear map defined by
\phi_{c,e}(1)=F^{e}_{*}c
. Then the
splitting prime ideal
of

is defined by

\displaystyle\PP(R)

\displaystyle=\{c\in R\mid\phi_{c,e}\,\text{does not split for any }e\in\mathbb{N}\}

\displaystyle=\bigcap\limits_{e\in\mathbb{N}}I_{e}(R).

Remark 2.5
.

For a local ring
(R,\mathfrak{m},k)
and
0\neq f\in\mathfrak{m}
, we identify
\PP(R/(f))
as the lift of the splitting prime ideal
\PP(R)
in
R
.

Definition 2.6
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m},k)
be a local
F
-finite ring of prime characteristic
p>0
. Let
d=\dim(R)
, then the
F
-signature
of

is

s(R)=\lim_{e\to\infty}\frac{\lambda_{R}(R/I_{e}(R))}{p^{ed}}.

This limit always exists by

]

The

-signature of a local ring

detects regularity, i.e.,

if and only if

is a regular local ring
[
11
, Corollary 16]
. Also,

-signature detects strong

-regularity, i.e.,

if and only if

is strongly

-regular
[

, Main Result]
.

Definition 2.7
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m},k)
be a local
F
-finite,
F
-pure ring of prime characteristic
p>0
. Let
n\coloneqq\dim(R/\PP(R))
, we call

the

of

. The

of

This limit always exists by

and enjoys the property

0<r_{F}(R)\leq 1.

The Frobenius splitting ratio of

is a generalization of

-signature and accurately measures the asymptotic behavior of the Frobenius splitting numbers of

if
\PP(R)\neq{0}
. The results of
[
3
]
on

-splitting ratios are generalization of results of
[

]
and
[

]
on

-signature.

Remark 2.8
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m},k)
be a local ring and let
0\neq f\in\mathfrak{m}
. It is natural to expect that for sufficiently small perturbations
\varepsilon\in\mathfrak{m}^{N}
with
N\gg 0
, the quotient rings
R/(f)
and
R/(f+\varepsilon)
share similar properties. In particular, if
R
is
\QQ
-Gorenstein and of prime characteristic

, then for every
\delta>0
there exists
N
such that for all
\varepsilon\in\mathfrak{m}^{N}
,

\left|s(R/(f))-s(R/(f+\varepsilon))\right|<\delta,

see

,

. This result illustrates the

-adic continuity of the

-signature under small perturbations of the defining parameter.

We briefly review the definition of
\QQ
-Gorenstein ring and
F
-purity of a pair
(R,\Delta)
.

Definition 2.9
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m},k)
be a Noetherian local ring that is
(S_{2})
and
(G_{1})
. A
Weil divisor
D
is an (integral) divisor of
\operatorname{Spec}(R)
such that
R(D)_{\mathfrak{p}}\cong R_{\mathfrak{p}}
for all height 1 prime ideals
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Spec}(R)
. If
D
is a Weil divisor of
\operatorname{Spec}(R)
, then
R(D)
denotes the corresponding
reflexive
fractional ideal of
R
. A divisor
D
is called
principal
if
R(D)\cong R
. Equivalently, there exists a nonzero divisor
a
in
R
such that
D=\operatorname{div}(a)
and

R(D)=R(\operatorname{div}(a))=R\cdot\frac{1}{a}.

We say that a Weil divisor
D
is
effective
, and write
D\geq 0
if
R\subseteq R(D)
. A
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor
\Delta=\sum a_{i}D_{i}
is a finite
\mathbb{Q}
-linear combination of Weil divisors
D_{i}
of
\operatorname{Spec}(R)
. A
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor is called
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier
if there exists a natural number
n>0
such that
n\Delta
is principal. The
index
of a
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor
\Delta
, is the smallest positive integer
n
such that
n\Delta
is principal. A
canonical divisor
of
\operatorname{Spec}(R)
is a Weil divisor
K_{R}
such that
R(K_{R})
is a canonical module of
R
. We say that
R
is
\mathbb{Q}
-Gorenstein if
K_{R}
is a
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier divisor.

Suppose that
f\in R
is a nonzero divisor so that
R/(f)
enjoys the property of being
(S_{2})
and
(G_{1})
. Let
D
be a Weil divisor of
\operatorname{Spec}(R)
with components disjoint from those of
\operatorname{div}(f)
. If
(R(D)/fR(D))_{\mathfrak{p}}\cong(R/(f))_{\mathfrak{p}}
for all height 1 prime ideals
\mathfrak{p}
of
R/(f)
, then we write
D|_{R/(f)}
to be the restriction of
D
to
R/(f)
. In particular, if
(-)^{**}
denotes reflexification with respect to
R/(f)
, then

\left(\frac{R(D)}{fR(D)}\right)^{**}\cong(R/(f))(D|_{R/(f)}).

If
\Delta
is a
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor,
n\neq 0
so that
n\Delta
is an integral Weil divisor on
R
, and
(n\Delta)|_{R/(f)}
is a Weil divisor of
R/(f)
, then we let
\Delta|_{R/(f)}=\frac{1}{n}(n\Delta)|_{R/(f)}.

Definition 2.10
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m},k)
be an
F
-finite local ring of prime characteristic
p>0
that is
(S_{2})
and
(G_{1})
, and let
\Delta\geq 0
be an effective
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor. The
e
th splitting ideal
of the pair
(R,\Delta)
is the ideal

I_{e}(R,\Delta)=\{x\in R\mid R\xrightarrow{\cdot F^{e}_{*}x}F^{e}_{*}R(\lceil(p^{e}-1)\Delta\rceil)\text{ does not split}\}.

We say that the pair
(R,\Delta)
is
F
-pure if there exists
e\in\mathbb{N}
so that
I_{e}(R,\Delta)\neq R
.

Remark 2.11
.

If
(p^{e}-1)\Delta
is integral, then
(p^{e}-1)\Delta=\lceil(p^{e}-1)\Delta\rceil
.

Fedder’s Criterion describes an explicit criterion for a hypersurface to be
F
-pure, and the methods of associated proof are a tool for describing the splitting ideals of a hypersurface as quotients of ideals of the ambient regular ring. The proof of the Inversion of Adjunction of
F
-purity theorem
[
13
, Theorem A]
also provides an explicit description of the splitting ideals of a pair restricted to a hypersurface under minimal hypotheses.

Remark 2.12
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m},k)
be a
d
-dimensional local ring and
M
be a finitely generated
R
-module. Then

(i)

If
\eta:M\to M^{**}
is an isomorphism in codimension 1, where

M^{**}\coloneqq\Hom_{R}\Bigl(\Hom_{R}(M,R),R\Bigr),

then
H^{d}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M)\cong H^{d}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M^{**})
.
Indeed, let
K=\ker(\eta)
and
C=\coker(\eta)
. Then we have an exact sequence

0\longrightarrow K\longrightarrow M\xrightarrow{\eta}M^{**}\longrightarrow C\longrightarrow 0.

which give rise to the short exact sequences:

(2.12.1)

0\longrightarrow K\longrightarrow M\longrightarrow L\longrightarrow 0.

and

(2.12.2)

0\longrightarrow L\longrightarrow M^{**}\longrightarrow C\longrightarrow 0.

From (
2.12.1
), we have

H^{d}_{\mathfrak{m}}(K)\longrightarrow H^{d}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M)\longrightarrow H^{d}_{\mathfrak{m}}(L)\longrightarrow 0.

Since
\eta
is an isomorphism in codimension
1
, we have
\dim K\leq d-2
, and hence

H^{d}_{\mathfrak{m}}(K)=0
. Therefore,

H^{d}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M)\cong H^{d}_{\mathfrak{m}}(L).

Similarly, from (
2.12.2
), we obtain

H^{d-1}_{\mathfrak{m}}(C)\longrightarrow H^{d}_{\mathfrak{m}}(L)\longrightarrow H^{d}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M^{**})\longrightarrow H^{d}_{\mathfrak{m}}(C)\longrightarrow 0.

Again, since
\dim C\leq d-2
, we have
H^{d-1}_{\mathfrak{m}}(C)=H^{d}_{\mathfrak{m}}(C)=0
, and hence

H^{d}_{\mathfrak{m}}(L)\cong H^{d}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M^{**}).

Thus,

H^{d}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M)\cong H^{d}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M^{**}).

(ii)

If
N
is an
R
-module, then
M\otimes_{R}H^{d}_{\mathfrak{m}}(N)\cong H^{d}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M\otimes_{R}N).
Indeed, top local cohomology is the cokernal of a Čech complex, and tensor product is right exact.

Theorem 2.13
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m},k)
be an
F
-finite,
(S_{2})
and
(G_{1})

\Delta\geq 0
be a
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor on

\Delta
are disjoint from the components of

,

(S_{2})
and
(G_{1})

\mathbb{Q}

I_{te_{0}}\left(R/(f),\Delta|_{R/(f)}\right)=\frac{(I_{te_{0}}(R,\Delta):_{R}f^{p^{te_{0}}-1})}{(f)}.

Proof.

The pair
(R,\Delta+\operatorname{div}(f))
is

(R/(f),\Delta|_{R/(f)})
is

-pure by
[

I_{e}(R/(f),\Delta|_{R/(f)})=R/(f)\text{ for all }e\in\mathbb{N}

We therefore may assume without loss of generality that the pairs

and
(R/(f),\Delta|_{R/(f)})
are

-pure.

Our assumptions imply that there exists an
e_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
so that
(p^{e_{0}}-1)(\Delta+K_{R})
is
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier of index

for some

. Therefore, for every

(p^{te_{0}}-1)(\Delta+K_{R})=\frac{p^{te_{0}}-1}{p^{e_{0}}-1}(p^{e_{0}}-1)(\Delta+K_{R})

-Cartier of index

To ease notation, for each

\Delta_{e}\coloneqq(p^{e}-1)(\Delta+K_{R})\quad\text{and}\quad\bar{\Delta}_{e}\coloneqq(p^{e}-1)(\Delta|_{R/(f)}+K_{R/(f)}).

By
[

, we have

(2.13.1)

\frac{R((p^{te_{0}}-1)\Delta)}{fR((p^{te_{0}}-1)\Delta)}\cong R/fR(((p^{te_{0}}-1)\bar{\Delta})).

Lemma 2.14
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m},k)
be a local Cohen-Macaulay,
F
-finite,
F
-pure,
(S_{2})
and
(G_{1})
ring of prime characteristic
p>0
, and let

be a canonical divisor of
\operatorname{Spec}(R)
. Let

be a
p
-power torsion Weil divisor of
\operatorname{Spec}(R)
. Then
R(E+K_{R})
is a Cohen-Macaulay module.

Proof.

Assume that
p^{e}E\sim 0
, then
-p^{e}E\sim 0.

Since
R
is
F
-pure, the Frobenius map

R\to F^{e}_{*}R

splits as a
R
-linear map. Thus,

F^{e}_{*}R\cong R\oplus\cdots.

Tensoring with

gives,

F^{e}_{*}R\otimes_{R}R(-E)\cong R\otimes_{R}R(-E)\oplus\cdots\cong R(-E)\oplus\cdots

Let
(-)^{*}=\Hom_{R}(-,R)
, then
(F^{e}_{*}R\otimes R(-E))^{**}\cong F^{e}_{*}R(-p^{e}E)
, and

.
But,
F^{e}_{*}R\otimes_{R}R(-E)\cong F^{e}_{*}R(-p^{e}E)\cong F^{e}_{*}R
. Thus,

F^{e}_{*}R\cong R(-E)\oplus\cdots.

Now since

, so it is Cohen-Macaulay. Applying

in (

) gives,

\displaystyle\Hom_{R}(F^{e}_{*}R,R(K_{R}))

\displaystyle\cong\Hom_{R}(R(-E),R(K_{R}))\oplus\cdots

\displaystyle\cong\Hom_{R}(R,R(E+K_{R}))\oplus\cdots

\displaystyle\cong R(E+K_{R})\oplus\cdots

But,
\Hom_{R}(F^{e}_{*}R,R(K_{R}))\cong F^{e}_{*}R(K_{R})
, thus,

. Therefore,

.

F

(S_{2})

(G_{1})

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m},k)
be an
F
-finite,
F
-pure, Gorenstein local ring of dimension
d
of prime characteristic
p>0
. The following are equivalent:

(i)

(ii)

There exists
e\in\mathbb{N}
such that
a_{e}(R)=[k^{1/p^{e}}:k]
.

(iii)

For all
e\in\mathbb{N}
,
a_{e}(R)=[k^{1/p^{e}}:k]
.

(iv)

I_{1}(R)=\mathfrak{m}.

(v)

There exist
e\in\mathbb{N}
such that
I_{e}(R)=\mathfrak{m}
.

(vi)

For all
e\in\mathbb{N}
,
I_{e}(R)=\mathfrak{m}
.

(vii)

The splitting prime ideal
\mathcal{P}(R)
of
R
equals
\mathfrak{m}
.

Proof.

We first establish the relationship between the splitting numbers
a_{e}(R)
and the ideals
I_{e}(R)
. If
a_{e}(R)=[k^{1/p^{e}}:k],
then
a_{e}(R)\geq 1
and
I_{e}(R)\subseteq\mathfrak{m}.

Recall that

a_{e}(R)=\dim_{k}\!\left(\frac{F^{e}_{*}R}{F^{e}_{*}I_{e}(R)}\right).

Since
I_{e}(R)\subseteq\mathfrak{m}
, we have
F^{e}_{*}I_{e}(R)\subseteq F^{e}_{*}\mathfrak{m}
, and hence

\dim_{k}\!\left(\frac{F^{e}_{*}R}{F^{e}_{*}I_{e}(R)}\right)\geq\dim_{k}\!\left(\frac{F^{e}_{*}R}{F^{e}_{*}\mathfrak{m}}\right)=[k^{1/p^{e}}:k].

Therefore,

a_{e}(R)\geq[k^{1/p^{e}}:k].

Moreover, equality holds if and only if

F^{e}_{*}I_{e}(R)=F^{e}_{*}\mathfrak{m}
, which is equivalent to
I_{e}(R)=\mathfrak{m}
.
Thus,

(3.1.1)

a_{e}(R)=[k^{1/p^{e}}:k]\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad I_{e}(R)=\mathfrak{m}.

Now, by Equation
3.1.1

\text{(i)}\Longleftrightarrow\text{(iv)},\qquad\text{(ii)}\Longleftrightarrow\text{(v)},\qquad\text{(iii)}\Longleftrightarrow\text{(vi)}.

Clearly, (vi)
\Longrightarrow
(iv)
\Longrightarrow
(v).

(v)
\implies
(iv): Since
R
is Gorenstein, by
[
17
, Lemma 3.9]
, there exist
\phi_{1}\in\Hom_{R}(F_{*}R,R)
such that
\phi_{e}\coloneqq\phi_{1}\circ F_{*}^{e-1}\phi_{e-1}
generates
\Hom_{R}(F^{e}_{*}R,R)
as a
F^{e}_{*}R
-modules. Suppose that
I_{e}(R)=\mathfrak{m}
for some
e
.
Then

\mathfrak{m}=\phi_{e}(R)\subseteq\phi_{1}(R)\subseteq\mathfrak{m},

which implies
I_{1}(R)=\mathfrak{m}
.

(iv)
\implies
(vi): Assume
I_{1}(R)=\mathfrak{m}
. We proceed by induction on
e
. The case
e=1
is trivial.
Suppose
I_{e-1}(R)=\mathfrak{m}
. For any
x\in\mathfrak{m}
,

\phi_{e}(F_{*}^{e}x)=\phi_{1}(F_{*}\phi_{e-1}(F_{*}^{e-1}x))\subseteq\phi_{1}(F_{*}\mathfrak{m})\subseteq\mathfrak{m},

so
I_{e}(R)=\mathfrak{m}
.

(vi)
\implies
(vii): If
\PP(R)\neq\mathfrak{m}
, then there exists
g\in\mathfrak{m}\setminus\PP(R)
. By definition of the splitting
prime, there exists
e
such that the map

R\xrightarrow{F_{*}^{e}g}F_{*}^{e}R

splits, which implies
g\notin I_{e}(R)
, contradicting
I_{e}(R)=\mathfrak{m}
.

(vii)
\implies
(iv): Since

\PP(R)=\bigcap_{e\geq 1}I_{e}(R)=\mathfrak{m},

it follows that
I_{1}(R)=\mathfrak{m}
.
∎

Corollary 3.2
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m},k)
be an
F
-finite,
F
-pure, Gorenstein local ring of dimension
d
of prime characteristic
p>0
and
0\neq f\in\mathfrak{m}
. Suppose
(\underline{x})=(x_{1},\dots,x_{d})
is a system of parameters so that
((\underline{x}):_{R}\mathfrak{m})=(x,u)
, where
u
is a socle generator of
R/(x_{1},\dots,x_{d})
. Then the following are equivalent:

(i)

a_{1}(R/(f))=[k^{1/p}:k].

(ii)

There exists
e\in\mathbb{N}
such that
a_{e}(R/(f))=[k^{1/p^{e}}:k]
.

(iii)

For all
e\in\mathbb{N}
,
a_{e}(R/(f))=[k^{1/p^{e}}:k]
.

(iv)

\left(((\underline{x})^{[p]}:_{R}u^{p}):_{R}f^{p-1}\right)=\mathfrak{m}
.

(v)

There exists
e\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\left(((\underline{x})^{[p^{e}]}:_{R}u^{p^{e}}):_{R}f^{p^{e}-1}\right)=\mathfrak{m}
.

(vi)

For all
e\in\mathbb{N}
,
\left(((\underline{x})^{[p^{e}]}:_{R}u^{p^{e}}):_{R}f^{p^{e}-1}\right)=\mathfrak{m}
.

(vii)

The splitting prime ideal
\mathcal{P}(R/(f))
of
R/(f)
equals
\mathfrak{m}/(f)
.

Moreover, if
(\underline{x})
is a system of parameters of
R
, conditions (i)-(vii) holds true and if
\varepsilon\in(\underline{x})^{[p]}
, then
\mathcal{P}(R/(f+\varepsilon))=\mathfrak{m}/(f+\varepsilon)
.

Proof.

By Theorem
2.13
and Theorem
3.1
conditions (i)-(vii) are equivalent.

Now assume that
(\underline{x})=(x_{1},\dots,x_{d})
is a system of parameters of
R
and that conditions (i) through (vii) are satisfied. By (vii),
if
\PP(R/(f))=\mathfrak{m}/(f)
, then by Theorem
2.13
and Proposition
2.15
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}(R/(f))

\displaystyle=\bigcap\limits_{e\in\mathbb{N}}I_{e}(R/(f))

\displaystyle=\frac{\Bigl((\underline{x})^{[p^{e}]}:_{R}u^{p^{e}}f^{p^{e}-1}\Bigr)}{(f)}

\displaystyle=\mathfrak{m}/(f).

Thus,
\Bigl((\underline{x})^{[p^{e}]}:_{R}u^{p^{e}}f^{p^{e}-1}\Bigr)=\mathfrak{m}
for all

Therefore

.

Now, for
\varepsilon\in(\underline{x})^{[p]}
, we will show by induction on

, it follows that

\varepsilon^{\,p-1-i}\in(x)^{[p]}\quad\text{for any }\varepsilon\in(x)^{[p]}.

Hence,

f^{i}\varepsilon^{\,p-1-i}\equiv 0\pmod{(x)^{[p]}}\quad\text{for }0\leq i<p-1,

(f+\varepsilon)^{p-1}\equiv f^{\,p-1}\pmod{(x)^{[p]}}.

Therefore,

u^{p}(f+\varepsilon)^{p-1}\in\bigl((x)^{[p]}:\mathfrak{m}\bigr).

u^{p^{e}}(f+\varepsilon)^{p^{e}-1}\in\left((\underline{x})^{[p^{e}]}:\mathfrak{m}\right).

We will show that

u^{p^{e+1}}(f+\varepsilon)^{p^{e+1}-1}\in\left((\underline{x})^{[p^{e+1}]}:\mathfrak{m}\right).

If
1\leq i\leq p-1
, then

\varepsilon^{i}\in(\underline{x})^{[p]}\subseteq\mathfrak{m}^{[p]}=\mathcal{P}(R/(f))^{[p]}

\displaystyle(u^{p^{e}}f^{\,p^{e}-1})^{p}

\displaystyle=u^{\,p^{\,e+1}}f^{\,p^{\,e+1}-p}

\displaystyle\in\bigl((\underline{x})^{[p^{e}]}:\mathfrak{m}\bigr)^{[p]}

\displaystyle\subseteq\bigl((\underline{x})^{[p^{\,e+1}]}:\mathfrak{m}^{[p]}\bigr).

Therefore,

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{i}u^{p^{e+1}}f^{p^{e+1}-1-i}

\displaystyle=\varepsilon^{i}u^{p^{e+1}}f^{p^{e+1}-p+p-1-i}

\displaystyle=\varepsilon^{i}u^{p^{e+1}}f^{p^{e+1}-p}f^{p-1-i}

\displaystyle\subseteq\varepsilon^{i}\Bigl((\underline{x})^{[p^{e+1}]}:\mathfrak{m}^{[p]}\Bigr)f^{p-1-i}

\displaystyle\subseteq(\underline{x})^{[p^{e+1}]}f^{p-1-i}\subseteq(\underline{x})^{[p^{e+1}]}.

Suppose that
e_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
is such that
p^{e_{0}}\leq i\leq p^{e_{0}+1}-1
. If
i\geq p^{e_{0}}
, then
\varepsilon^{i}\in(\underline{x})^{[p^{e_{0}+1}]}\subseteq\mathfrak{m}^{[p^{e_{0}+1}]}
since
\varepsilon\in(\underline{x})^{[p]}
.

Also,

\displaystyle u^{p^{e+1}}f^{p^{e+1}-p^{e_{0}+1}}

\displaystyle=(u^{p^{e-e_{0}}}f^{p^{e-e_{0}}-1})^{[p^{e_{0}+1}]}

\displaystyle\in\Bigl((\underline{x})^{[p^{e-e_{0}}]}:\mathfrak{m}\Bigr)^{[p^{e_{0}+1}]}

\displaystyle=\Bigl((\underline{x})^{[p^{e+1}]}:\mathfrak{m}^{[p^{e_{0}+1}]}\Bigr).

Now, for
p^{e_{0}}\leq i\leq p^{e_{0}+1}-1

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{i}u^{p^{e+1}}f^{p^{e+1}-1-i}

\displaystyle=\varepsilon^{i}u^{p^{e+1}}f^{p^{e+1}-p^{e_{0}+1}+p^{e_{0}+1}-1-i}

\displaystyle=\varepsilon^{i}u^{p^{e+1}}f^{p^{e+1}-p^{e_{0}+1}}f^{p^{e_{0}+1}-1-i}

\displaystyle=\varepsilon^{i}\bigl(u^{p^{e-e_{0}}}f^{p^{e-e_{0}}-1}\bigr)^{[p^{e_{0}+1}]}f^{p^{e_{0}+1}-1-i}

\displaystyle\subseteq\varepsilon^{i}\bigl((\underline{x})^{[p^{e-e_{0}}]}:\mathfrak{m}\bigr)^{[p^{e_{0}+1}]}f^{p^{e_{0}+1}-1-i}

\displaystyle=\varepsilon^{i}\bigl((\underline{x})^{[p^{e+1}]}:\mathfrak{m}^{[p^{e_{0}+1}]}\bigr)f^{p^{e_{0}+1}-1-i}

\displaystyle\subseteq(\underline{x})^{[p^{e+1}]}f^{p^{e_{0}+1}-1-i}\subseteq(\underline{x})^{[p^{e+1}]}.

Hence,
u^{p^{e+1}}(f+\varepsilon)^{p^{e+1}-1}\equiv u^{p^{e+1}}f^{p^{e+1}-1}
(mod
(\underline{x})^{[p^{e+1}]}
) for all
\varepsilon\in(\underline{x})^{[p]}
. Therefore,

u^{p^{e+1}}(f+\varepsilon)^{p^{e+1}-1}\in\left((\underline{x})^{[p^{e+1}]}:\mathfrak{m}\right).

Hence
u^{p^{e}}(f+\varepsilon)^{p^{e}-1}\in\left((\underline{x})^{[p^{e}]}:\mathfrak{m}\right)
, which implies
\PP\bigl(R/(f+\varepsilon)\bigr)=\mathfrak{m}/(f+\varepsilon).

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m},k)
be a local
\mathbb{Q}
-Gorenstein, Cohen-Macaulay,
F
-finite,
F
-pure ring of dimension
(d+1)
and of prime characteristic
p>0
, and let
K_{R}
be a canonical divisor of
\operatorname{Spec}(R)
. Suppose that
\Delta\geq 0
is an effective
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor such that
\Delta+K_{R}
is
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier and that for all
e\gg 0
and divisible,
(p^{e}-1)\Delta
is integral. Let
f\in R
be a nonzero divisor such that the components of
\Delta
are disjoint from the components of
\operatorname{div}(f)
,
R/(f)
satisfies
(S_{2})
and
(G_{1})
, and
\Delta|_{(f)}
is a
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor on

\varepsilon\in\mathfrak{m}^{[p^{e_{0}}]}

Proof.

Assume that
\dim(R/\PP(R/(f),\Delta))=h
, then
\text{ht}(\PP(R/(f),\Delta))=d-h
, since
\dim((R/(f),\Delta))=d
. Since
\dim(R/(\PP(R/(f),\Delta)))=h
, there exist parameters

of
R/(f)
and for each

R/(f)\xrightarrow{\cdot F^{te_{i}}_{*}x_{i}}F^{te_{i}}_{*}R/(f)((p^{te_{i}}-1)\Delta)\ \text{splits.}

Complete
f,x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{h}
to a full system of parameters
(\underline{x})=f,x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{d}
. Let
u
generate the socle
((\underline{x}):_{R}\mathfrak{m})/(\underline{x})
. Then by Proposition
2.15

x_{i}u^{p^{te_{i}}}f^{p^{te_{i}}-1}\notin(\underline{x})^{[p^{te_{i}}]}R(\Delta_{te_{i}}+K_{R}),

for all
1\leq i\leq h
, which is true if and only if

u^{p^{te_{i}}}f^{p^{te_{i}}-1}\notin\left((\underline{x})^{[p^{te_{i}}]}R(\Delta_{te_{i}}+K_{R}):x_{i}\right).

Now, by
[
18
]
, if

f,x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{h},x_{h+1},\dots,x_{d}
is a system of parameters of
R
, then there exists

such that for all

form a system of parameters of

are parameters for
R/(f)
if and only if

are parameters for
R
. Therefore,

x_{1}+\varepsilon_{1},x_{2}+\varepsilon_{2},\dots,x_{d}+\varepsilon_{d}
are parameters of
R
, which implies that

x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{d}
are parameters for
R/(f+\varepsilon)
.

Now, we want to show that if
\varepsilon\in\mathfrak{m}^{n\gg 0}
and
1\leq i\leq h
, then

R/(f+\varepsilon)\xrightarrow{\cdot F^{te_{i}}_{*}x_{i}}F^{te_{i}}_{*}R/(f+\varepsilon)((p^{te_{i}}-1)\Delta)\quad\text{splits.}

That is,

u^{p^{te_{i}}}(f+\varepsilon)^{p^{te_{i}}-1}\notin\big((\underline{x})^{[p^{te_{i}}]}R(\Delta_{te_{i}}+K_{R}):x_{i}\big).

Let
e_{0}=\max\{e_{1},e_{2},\dots,e_{h}\}
. We can choose
\varepsilon\in\mathfrak{m}^{n}
such that
\mathfrak{m}^{n}\subseteq\mathfrak{m}^{[p^{e_{0}}]}
and
f+\varepsilon
is regular. Since
\varepsilon\in\mathfrak{m}^{[p^{e_{0}}]}
and

u^{p^{te_{0}}}f^{p^{te_{0}}-1}\notin\big((\underline{x})^{[p^{te_{0}}]}R(\Delta_{te_{0}}+K_{R}):x_{i}\big),

it follows that

u^{p^{te_{0}}}(f+\varepsilon)^{p^{te_{0}}-1}\notin\big((\underline{x})^{[p^{te_{0}}]}R(\Delta_{te_{0}}+K_{R}):x_{i}\big).

Thus,
x_{1},\dots,x_{h}\notin\PP(R/(f+\varepsilon),\Delta)

\text{ht}\bigl(\PP(R/(f+\varepsilon),\Delta)\bigr)\geq d-h,

which implies

\dim\Bigl(R/\PP(R/(f),\Delta)\Bigr)\leq\dim\Bigl(R/\PP(R/(f+\varepsilon),\Delta)\Bigr).

∎

Duality between dissipation-coherence trade-off and thermodynamic speed limit based on thermodynamic uncertainty relation for stochastic limit cycles in the weak-noise limit
Ryuna Nagayama, Sosuke Ito

Abstract
We derive two fundamental trade-offs for general stochastic limit cycles in the weak-noise limit. The first is the dissipation-coherence trade-off, which was numerically conjectured and partially proved by Santolin and Falasco [ Phys. Rev. Lett. 135 , 057101 (2025) ]. This trade-off bounds the entropy production required for one oscillatory period using the number of oscillations that occur before steady-state correlations are disrupted. The second is the thermodynamic speed limit, which bounds the entropy production with the Euclidean length of the limit cycle. These trade-offs are obtained by substituting mutually dual observables, derived from the stability of the limit cycle, into the thermodynamic uncertainty relation. This fact allows us to regard the dissipation-coherence trade-off as the dual of the thermodynamic speed limit. We numerically demonstrate these trade-offs using the noisy Rössler model.

System and formulation

Let us discuss the stability of the limit cycle in more depth by continuing to focus on the deterministic time evolution. Consider the case where

Main results
.—We consider the short-time TUR in the weak-noise limit. Let us consider a
N

\bm{\omega}(\bm{x})
. In the infinitesimal time interval
[t,t+\varDelta t]

\bm{\omega}(\bm{x})
is given by

. Here the increment of the state

is denoted by

\displaystyle\dot{\Sigma}_{t}\geq\frac{2\mathbb{E}[J^{\bm{\omega}}_{t}]^{2}}{\varDelta t\mathrm{Var}[J^{\bm{\omega}}_{t}]},

(7)

where the EP rate is defined as
\dot{\Sigma}_{t}\coloneqq\epsilon^{-1}\int d\bm{x}\,p_{t}(\bm{x})\bm{\nu}_{t}(\bm{x})^{\top}\mathsf{D}(\bm{x})^{-1}\bm{\nu}_{t}(\bm{x})
. In the weak-noise limit, the short-time TUR yields the following bound on

\displaystyle\Sigma_{\tau_{\mathrm{p}}}\geq\dfrac{\left[\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\tau_{\mathrm{p}}}dt\,\bm{\omega}(\bm{x}_{t}^{\mathrm{LC}})^{\top}\bm{F}(\bm{x}_{t}^{\mathrm{LC}})\right]^{2}}{\epsilon\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\tau_{\mathrm{p}}}dt\,\bm{\omega}(\bm{x}_{t}^{\mathrm{LC}})^{\top}\mathsf{D}(\bm{x}_{t}^{\mathrm{LC}})\bm{\omega}(\bm{x}_{t}^{\mathrm{LC}})}.

(8)

This inequality is derived by taking the time integration of the TUR and admitting the large deviation form

where we used

Substituting Eqs. (
5

\displaystyle\frac{\Sigma_{\tau_{\mathrm{p}}}}{4\pi^{2}}\geq\mathcal{N}.

(10)

This trade-off implies that more dissipation is required to remain coherent for a longer time.

We emphasize that this derivation of the dissipation-coherence trade-off is more general than the one by Santolin and Falasco

onto direction tangent to the limit cycle. This projection restricts their analytical derivation to systems that satisfy one of the following two conditions: (i)

Second, we use
\bm{F}(\bm{x})
itself as the observable in the bound (

as

, where the Euclidean norm is denoted by

. We also introduce the intensity of diffusion measured along the limit cycle as

. Substituting
\bm{\omega}(\bm{x})=\bm{F}(\bm{x})
into Eq. (

, we obtain the following TSL:

\displaystyle\Sigma_{\tau_{\mathrm{p}}}\geq\frac{l_{\mathrm{LC}}^{2}}{\tau_{\mathrm{p}}D_{\mathrm{LC}}}.

(11)

The right-hand side of this inequality increases if we consider a large

This TSL differs from the existing ones for Langevin systems. The previous TSLs bound EP using the distance between the initial and final probability distributions

Discussion
.—In this Letter, we derived the dissipation-coherence trade-off and the TSL for the general stochastic limit cycles in the weak-noise limit by applying the dual observables to the short-time TUR. The dissipation-coherence trade-off was conjectured by Santolin and Falasco
Santolin and Falasco 2025
. Our derivation applies even when the conditions that appeared in their derivation for simple cases are not satisfied. The TSL had not been considered in the weak-noise limit. This new TSL enables us to link quantities derived from deterministic dynamical systems to thermodynamic quantities. The duality between the dissipation-coherence trade-off and the TSL with respect to the TUR may help derive the trade-off in the general case beyond the weak-noise limit.

We also note that developing thermodynamics in the weak-noise limit as a tool for investigating general dynamical systems appears promising. For example, we may classify some bifurcations based on the tightness of a thermodynamic trade-off. Indeed, the first and second bifurcations in Fig.
2
(a) may differ qualitatively from a thermodynamic perspective. This is because the tightness of the dissipation-coherence trade-off behaves discontinuously at the first bifurcation and continuously at the second, as shown in Fig.
2
(c).

Substituting the large-deviation form

\displaystyle\Sigma_{\tau_{\mathrm{p}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\epsilon}\int_{0}^{\tau_{\mathrm{p}}}dt\,\bm{\nu}_{t}^{I}(\bm{x}^{\mathrm{LC}}_{t})^{\top}\mathsf{D}(\bm{x}^{\mathrm{LC}}_{t})^{-1}\bm{\nu}_{t}^{I}(\bm{x}^{\mathrm{LC}}_{t}).

\displaystyle\quad-2\int_{0}^{\tau_{\mathrm{p}}}dt\,\bm{F}(\bm{x}^{\mathrm{LC}}_{t})^{\top}\mathsf{D}(\bm{x}^{\mathrm{LC}}_{t})^{-1}\bm{\nabla}\cdot\mathsf{D}(\bm{x}^{\mathrm{LC}}_{t})

\displaystyle\simeq\frac{1}{\tau_{\mathrm{p}}}\int dz_{\parallel}\int_{0}^{\tau_{\mathrm{p}}}dt^{\prime}\,a(\bm{x}^{\mathrm{LC}}_{t^{\prime}+t}+z_{\parallel}\|\dot{\bm{x}}_{t^{\prime}+t}^{\mathrm{LC}}\|^{-1}\dot{\bm{x}}_{t^{\prime}+t}^{\mathrm{LC}})a(\bm{x}^{\mathrm{LC}}_{t^{\prime}})\sqrt{\frac{\tau_{\mathrm{p}}}{4\pi\epsilon t\|\dot{\bm{x}}_{t^{\prime}+t}^{\mathrm{LC}}\|^{2}\mathcal{I}_{\bm{\zeta}}}}\exp\left[-\frac{\tau_{\mathrm{p}}z_{\parallel}^{2}}{4\epsilon t\|\dot{\bm{x}}_{t^{\prime}+t}^{\mathrm{LC}}\|^{2}\mathcal{I}_{\bm{\zeta}}}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\tau_{\mathrm{p}}}\int dz\int_{0}^{\tau_{\mathrm{p}}}dt^{\prime}\,a(\bm{x}^{\mathrm{LC}}_{t^{\prime}+t}+z\dot{\bm{x}}_{t^{\prime}+t}^{\mathrm{LC}})a(\bm{x}^{\mathrm{LC}}_{t^{\prime}})\sqrt{\frac{\tau_{\mathrm{p}}}{4\pi\epsilon t\mathcal{I}_{\bm{\zeta}}}}\exp\left[-\frac{\tau_{\mathrm{p}}z^{2}}{4\epsilon t\mathcal{I}_{\bm{\zeta}}}\right],

(S20)

where we introduce a new variable
z=z_{\parallel}\|\dot{\bm{x}}_{t^{\prime}+t}^{\mathrm{LC}}\|^{-1}
. Due to the weak-noise limit, only small
z
contributes to the integral. This fact allows us to approximate
\bm{x}^{\mathrm{LC}}_{t^{\prime}+t}+z\dot{\bm{x}}_{t^{\prime}+t}^{\mathrm{LC}}
as
\bm{x}^{\mathrm{LC}}_{t^{\prime}+t+z}
, which yields

\displaystyle\langle a(\bm{x}_{t})a(\bm{x}_{0})\rangle_{\mathrm{st}}\simeq\frac{1}{\tau_{\mathrm{p}}}\int dz\int_{0}^{\tau_{\mathrm{p}}}dt^{\prime}\,a(\bm{x}^{\mathrm{LC}}_{t^{\prime}+t+z})a(\bm{x}^{\mathrm{LC}}_{t^{\prime}})\sqrt{\frac{\tau_{\mathrm{p}}}{4\pi\epsilon t\mathcal{I}_{\bm{\zeta}}}}\exp\left[-\frac{\tau_{\mathrm{p}}z^{2}}{4\epsilon t\mathcal{I}_{\bm{\zeta}}}\right],

(S21)

Since the function
a(\bm{x}^{\mathrm{LC}}_{t})
is periodic, we can expand it into the Fourier series as

\displaystyle a(\bm{x}^{\mathrm{LC}}_{t})=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\hat{a}_{n}\exp\left[\mathrm{i}\frac{2\pi t}{\tau_{\mathrm{p}}}n\right],

(S22)

where
\hat{a}_{n}
is the corresponding Fourier coefficient. Using this Fourier series, we obtain

\displaystyle\langle a(\bm{x}_{t})a(\bm{x}_{0})\rangle_{\mathrm{st}}

\displaystyle\simeq\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\hat{a}_{n}\hat{a}_{m}\exp\left[\mathrm{i}\frac{2n\pi t}{\tau_{\mathrm{p}}}\right]\left[\frac{1}{\tau_{\mathrm{p}}}\int_{0}^{\tau_{\mathrm{p}}}dt^{\prime}\,\exp\left\{\mathrm{i}\frac{2\pi}{\tau_{\mathrm{p}}}(n+m)t^{\prime}\right\}\right]

\displaystyle\phantom{\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\hat{a}_{n}\hat{a}_{m}\exp\left[\mathrm{i}\frac{2n\pi t}{\tau_{\mathrm{p}}}\right]}\qquad\times\left[\sqrt{\frac{\tau_{\mathrm{p}}}{4\pi\epsilon t\mathcal{I}_{\bm{\zeta}}}}\int dz\,\exp\left\{-\frac{\tau_{\mathrm{p}}z^{2}}{4\epsilon t\mathcal{I}_{\bm{\zeta}}}+\mathrm{i}\frac{2n\pi z}{\tau_{\mathrm{p}}}\right\}\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\hat{a}_{n}\hat{a}_{m}\delta_{n,-m}\exp\left[-\frac{4n^{2}\pi^{2}\epsilon\mathcal{I}_{\bm{\zeta}}}{\tau_{\mathrm{p}}^{3}}t+\mathrm{i}\frac{2n\pi}{\tau_{\mathrm{p}}}t\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\hat{a}_{n}\hat{a}_{-n}\exp\left[-\frac{4n^{2}\pi^{2}\epsilon\mathcal{I}_{\bm{\zeta}}}{\tau_{\mathrm{p}}^{3}}t+\mathrm{i}\frac{2n\pi}{\tau_{\mathrm{p}}}t\right]

\displaystyle\simeq\hat{a}_{0}\hat{a}_{0}+2\mathrm{Re}\left(\hat{a}_{1}\hat{a}_{-1}\exp\left[\mathrm{i}\frac{2\pi}{\tau_{\mathrm{p}}}t\right]\right)\exp\left[-\frac{4\pi^{2}\epsilon\mathcal{I}_{\bm{\zeta}}}{\tau_{\mathrm{p}}^{3}}t\right]

\displaystyle=|\hat{a}_{0}|^{2}+2|\hat{a}_{1}|^{2}\exp\left[-\frac{4\pi^{2}\epsilon\mathcal{I}_{\bm{\zeta}}}{\tau_{\mathrm{p}}^{3}}t\right]\cos\left(\frac{2\pi}{\tau_{\mathrm{p}}}t\right),

(S23)

where we used the fact that
a(\bm{x})
is the real-valued function, and
t
is sufficiently large. Thus, the correlation time is obtained as

\displaystyle\tau_{\mathrm{c}}=\left[\frac{4\pi^{2}\epsilon\mathcal{I}_{\bm{\zeta}}}{\tau_{\mathrm{p}}^{3}}\right]^{-1}=\frac{\tau_{\mathrm{p}}^{3}}{4\pi^{2}\epsilon}\left[\int_{0}^{\tau_{\mathrm{p}}}dt\,\bm{\zeta}_{t}^{\top}\mathsf{D}(\bm{x}^{\mathrm{LC}}_{t})\bm{\zeta}_{t}\right]^{-1}.

(S24)

Introduction

The Generalized Minimal Residual (GMRES) method
[
21
]
is a robust and efficient Krylov subspace
iterative method for systems of linear equations

A\mbox{\boldmath$x$}=\mbox{\boldmath$b$}

(1)

where
A\in{\bf R}^{n\times n}
is nonsingular and may be nonsymmetric, and
\mbox{\boldmath$x$},\mbox{\boldmath$b$}\in{\bf R}^{n}
.

Abstractly, GMRES begins with an initial approximate solution
\mbox{\boldmath$x$}_{0}\in{\bf R}^{n}
and initial residual
\mbox{\boldmath$r$}_{0}=\mbox{\boldmath$b$}-A\mbox{\boldmath$x$}_{0}
and
characterizes the
k
th approximate solution as
\mbox{\boldmath$x$}_{k}=\mbox{\boldmath$x$}_{0}+\mbox{\boldmath$z$}_{k}
, where
\mbox{\boldmath$z$}_{k}
solves

\min_{\mbox{\boldmath$z$}\in{\cal K}_{k}}\|\mbox{\boldmath$b$}-A(\mbox{\boldmath$x$}_{0}+\mbox{\boldmath$z$})\|_{2}=\min_{\mbox{\boldmath$z$}\in{\cal K}_{k}}\|\mbox{\boldmath$r$}_{0}-A\mbox{\boldmath$z$}\|_{2}.

(2)

Here,
{\cal K}_{k}
is the
k
th Krylov subspace determined by
A
and
\mbox{\boldmath$r$}_{0}
, defined by

{\cal K}_{k}\equiv{\rm span}\{\mbox{\boldmath$r$}_{0},A\mbox{\boldmath$r$}_{0},\ldots,A^{k-1}\mbox{\boldmath$r$}_{0}\}.

The implementation given in
[
21
]
is as follows.

.

\mbox{\boldmath$r$}_{0}=\mbox{\boldmath$b$}-A\mbox{\boldmath$x$}_{0}
,

\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{1}=\mbox{\boldmath$r$}_{0}/||\mbox{\boldmath$r$}_{0}||_{2}

For

{\displaystyle\hat{\mbox{\boldmath$v$}}_{j+1}=A\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{j}-\sum_{i=1}^{j}h_{i,j}\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{i}}

.

\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{j+1}=\hat{\mbox{\boldmath$v$}}_{j+1}/h_{j+1,j}

\mbox{\boldmath$x$}_{k}=\mbox{\boldmath$x$}_{0}+[\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{1},\ldots,\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{k}]\mbox{\boldmath$y$}_{k}

\mbox{\boldmath$y$}=\mbox{\boldmath$y$}_{k}
minimizes

When
A
is nonsingular, GMRES gives the solution for arbitrary
b
and
\mbox{\boldmath$x$}_{0}
.
When
A
is singular, one may still apply GMRES to the least squares problem

\min_{\mbox{\boldmath$x$}\in{\bf R}^{n}}\|\mbox{\boldmath$b$}-A\mbox{\boldmath$x$}\|_{2}.

(3)

However, Brown and Walker
[
2
, Theorem 2.4]
showed that GMRES determines a least squares solution of (
3
) without
breakdown for all
b
and initial approximate solution
\mbox{\boldmath$x$}_{0}
if and only if
A
is range-symmetric (EP),
that is
{\cal R}(A)={\cal R}(A^{\rm T})
, where
{\cal R}(A)
denotes the range space of
A
. (See also
[
10
]
for an
alternative proof.)

There are two kinds of definitions for the breakdown of GMRES.
One definition is that
GMRES breaks down at the
k
th iteration when

{\rm dim}A({\cal K}_{k})<{\rm dim}{\cal K}_{k}
or
{\rm dim}{\cal K}_{k}<k

[
2
]
.
The other definition is that GMRES breaks down at the
k
th iteration when
h_{k+1,k}=0
in Algorithm 1.
The equivalence between these definitions for the breakdown of GMRES is discussed in
[
15
]
.
In this paper, we will use the latter definition.

When
{\cal R}(A^{\rm T})\neq{\cal R}(A)
,
including the case when the index of
A
is greater than or equal to 1,
there exist
\mbox{\boldmath$x$}_{0}
and
b
such that GMRES breaks down without giving
a least squares solution
[
10
]
.
In order to overcome this problem, Reichel and Ye
[
19
]
proposed the
breakdown-free GMRES, which is designed to expand
the solution Krylov subspace when GMRES breaks down. However, they do not give
a theoretical justification that
this method always works.

In this paper, we consider
solving square singular systems where

{\cal R}(A^{\rm T})\neq{\cal R}(A)
, especially where
A
is GP or
the index of
A
is greater than
1
.
To do so,
we propose the method to overcome the difficulty that
GMRES does not necessarily determine a least squares solution when

{\cal R}(A^{\rm T})\neq{\cal R}(A)

and give a theoretical justification that
our proposed method always works.
The proposed method is as follows.

For the least squares problem

\min_{\mbox{\boldmath$x$}\in{\bf R}^{n}}\|\mbox{\boldmath$b$}-A\mbox{\boldmath$x$}\|_{2}

(4)

where
A\in{\bf R}^{m\times n}
, Hayami, Yin and Ito
[
11
]
proposed the AB-GMRES method which applies GMRES to

\min_{\mbox{\boldmath$z$}\in{\bf R}^{m}}\|\mbox{\boldmath$b$}-AB\mbox{\boldmath$z$}\|_{2}

(5)

where
B\in{\bf R}^{n\times m}
satisfies
{\cal R}(AB)={\cal R}(A)
.

If we let
m=n
, and
B=CA^{\rm T}
, where
C\in{\bf R}^{n\times n}
is symmetric positive-definite, then
{\cal R}(A)={\cal R}(AB)

holds (
[
11
, Lemma 3.4]
).
Note also that
{\cal R}((AB)^{\rm T})={\cal R}(AB)
, i.e.
AB
becomes range-symmetric, so that the AB-GMRES determines a least
squares solution of (
5
) for arbitrary
\mbox{\boldmath$b$}\in{\bf R}^{m}
. Note also that
B=CA^{\rm T}
can be regarded as a right preconditioner.
Therefore, instead of
A\mbox{\boldmath$x$}=\mbox{\boldmath$b$}
, we apply GMRES to

ACA^{\rm T}
where
C
is symmetric positive definite.
Then, AB-GMRES determines a least squares solution

\mbox{\boldmath$x$}=CA^{\rm T}\mbox{\boldmath$z$}
of
A\mbox{\boldmath$x$}=\mbox{\boldmath$b$}

without breakdown for
arbitrary (singular) matrix
A\in{\bf R}^{n\times n}
and
\mbox{\boldmath$b$},\mbox{\boldmath$x$}_{0}\in{\bf R}^{n}

since
ACA^{\rm T}
is symmetric even if
{\cal R}(A^{\rm T})\neq{\cal R}(A)
.

Note however, that
even when
{\cal R}(A^{\rm T})={\cal R}(A)
, when
\mbox{\boldmath$b$}\notin{\cal R}(A)
, the least squares problem (
2
)
in GMRES may
become dangerously ill-conditioned before a least squares solution of (
3
) is obtained (
[
2
, Theorem 2.5]
).

To overcome this difficulty, we further propose using the Range Restricted GMRES (RRGMRES)
[
3
]
instead of GMRES
because
RRGMRES is much more stable compared to GMRES for inconsistent range-symmetric systems.

The
k
th iterate of RRGMRES is given by
\mbox{\boldmath$x$}_{k}=\mbox{\boldmath$x$}_{0}+\mbox{\boldmath$z$}_{k}
where

\mbox{\boldmath$z$}_{k}
solves

\displaystyle\min_{\mbox{\boldmath$z$}\in{\cal K}_{k}(A,A\mbox{\boldmath$r$}_{0})}\|\mbox{\boldmath$r$}_{0}-A\mbox{\boldmath$z$}\|_{2}
.

GP, EP matrices and their applications

index(
A
) of
A\in{\bf R}^{n\times n}
denotes the smallest nonnegative integer
i
such
that
\mbox{rank}(A^{i})=\mbox{rank}(A^{i+1})

[
4
, Definition 7.2.1]

and it is thus equal to the size of the largest Jordan block
corresponding to the zero eigenvalue of
A

[
17
, section 3]
.
If
{\cal R}(A)\cap{\cal N}(A)={\mbox{\boldmath$0$}}
,

A
is called a GP (group) matrix
[
9
, section 1]
.
(A group matrix is a matrix which has a group inverse.)
If
A
is singular,
A
is GP if and only if
{\rm index}(A)=1
.
The GP matrix arises, for example, from the finite difference discretization of a convection diffusion
equation with Neumann boundary condition

\displaystyle\Delta u+d\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x_{1}+x_{2},~~\mbox{\boldmath$x$}=(x_{1},x_{2})\in\Omega\equiv[0,1]\times[0,1],

(6)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial u(\mbox{\boldmath$x$})}{\partial\nu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0~~{\rm for}~~\mbox{\boldmath$x$}\in\partial\Omega,

(7)

as in
[
2
]
.

Assume
A
arises from the finite difference discretization of the above equation.
Then,
{\cal N}(A^{\rm T})\neq{\cal N}(A)
and

{\cal R}(A)\cap{\cal N}(A)=\{\mbox{\boldmath$0$}\}
hold. Then, A is a GP matrix.

GP matrices also arise in the analysis of ergodic homogeneous finite Markov chains
[
8
]
.

When
{\cal R}(A^{\rm T})={\cal R}(A)
,
A
is called range-symmetric or EP (Equal Projectors)
[
4
]
. Note that, since

{\cal N}(A^{\rm T})={\cal R}(A)^{\perp}
,
{\cal R}(A^{\rm T})={\cal R}(A)\Leftrightarrow{\cal N}(A^{\rm T})={\cal N}(A)\Leftrightarrow{\cal R}(A)\perp{\cal N}(A)
.
Hence,
{\cal R}(A^{\rm T})={\cal R}(A)\Rightarrow{\cal R}(A)\cap{\cal N}(A)=\{\mbox{\boldmath$0$}\}
.
That is, an EP matrix is a GP matrix.
Therefore, if
A
is EP and singular, then
{\rm index}(A)=1
.
Furthermore, if
{\rm index}(A)\geq 2
,
A
is not EP,
that is
{\cal R}(A)\neq{\cal R}(A^{\rm T})
holds.

An EP matrix arises, for instance, from the finite difference
approximation
of the convection diffusion equation (
6
) with a periodic boundary condition

\displaystyle u(x_{1},0)=u(x_{1},1),~~x_{1}\in[0,1],

\displaystyle u(0,x_{2})=u(1,x_{2}),~~x_{2}\in[0,1],

as in
[
2
]
.

Convergence theory of GMRES for singular systems

Consider the system of linear equations (
1
)
and the least squares problem (
3
).
(
1
) is called consistent
when
\mbox{\boldmath$b$}\in{\cal R}(A)
, and inconsistent otherwise.
Brown and Walker
[
2
]
showed that GMRES determines a least squares solution of
(
3
) without breakdown for arbitrary
\mbox{\boldmath$b$},\mbox{\boldmath$x$}_{0}\in{\bf R}^{n}
if and only if
A
is range symmetric (EP),
i.e.
{\cal R}(A^{\rm T})={\cal R}(A)
, where
A\in{\bf R}^{n\times n}
may be singular and
b
may not be in the range space
{\cal R}(A)
of
A
.
In
[
2
]
, it was also pointed out that even if
{\cal R}(A^{\rm T})={\cal R}(A)
, if
\mbox{\boldmath$b$}\notin{\cal R}(A)
,
the least squares problem (
2
) becomes very ill-conditioned. (The condition number of the Hessenberg matrix
arising in each iteration of GMRES becomes very large.)

If
A
is a GP matrix, GMRES determines a solution of (
1
) for all
\mbox{\boldmath$b$}\in{\cal R}(A)
and
for all initial vector
\mbox{\boldmath$x$}_{0}
(
[
2
, Theorem 2.6]
). Moreover,
GMRES determines a solution of (
1
) for all
\mbox{\boldmath$b$}\in{\cal R}(A)
and
for all initial vector
\mbox{\boldmath$x$}_{0}
if and only if
A
is a GP matrix (
[
10
]
, Theorem 2.8).
Even if
A
is a GP matrix and
\mbox{\boldmath$b$}\in{\cal R}(A)
,
it was reported in
[
16
]
that
the least squares problem (
2
)
can become very ill-conditioned before breakdown in finite precision arithmetic.
Furthermore, if
A
is GP and is not EP,
there exists
\mbox{\boldmath$b$}\notin{\cal R}(A)
such that
GMRES breaks down at iteration step
1
without giving a least squares
solution of (
3
) (
[
10
, Theorem 2.6]
).

If
{\rm index}(A)>1
, there exists a
\mbox{\boldmath$b$}\in{\cal R}(A)
such that GMRES breaks down at iteration step 1 without
giving a solution of (
1
) (
[
10
, Theorem 2.8]
).

In general,
when
{\cal R}(A^{\rm T})\neq{\cal R}(A)
, there exist
\mbox{\boldmath$x$}_{0}
and

b
such that
GMRES breaks down without giving a least squares solution of (
3
) (
[
2
,
10
]
).

In this paper, we propose using right preconditioning
to overcome the difficulty of solving the least squares problems
(
3
) with coefficient matrices
which are not EP, including the case when the
index is greater than or equal to 1
[
23
]
.
Furthermore, we propose using RRGMRES instead of GMRES since RRGMRES is more stable compared to GMRES
for inconsistent range-symmetric systems.
Finally, we propose applying the NR-SSOR inner iteration as the right preconditioner.
The proposed method also works well for (underdetermined) least squares problems.

RRGMRES determines a least squares solution
of
A\mbox{\boldmath$x$}=\mbox{\boldmath$b$}

without breakdown for arbitrary
\mbox{\boldmath$b$}\in{\bf R}^{n}
,
and initial iterate
\mbox{\boldmath$x$}_{0}\in{\bf R}^{n}

where
A
may be singular
if
{\cal R}(A)={\cal R}(A^{\rm{T}})
, and furthermore this
least squares solution is also the minimum-norm solution if

\mbox{\boldmath$x$}_{0}\in{\cal R}(A)
.
[
3
]
.
We also describe the convergence theory for RRGMRES in Appendix
A
and
B
.

[

[

This is because the smallest singular value of the Hessenberg matrix

This does not hold with GMRES
[

16
]

The computational cost of RRGMRES
is almost the same as that of GMRES. RRGMRES converges to a least squares solution for range-symmetric systems.
Furthermore, when the initial iterate
\mbox{\boldmath$x$}_{0}\in{\cal R}(A)
holds,
this solution is a minimum norm solution.

Using

the right preconditioned RRGMRES also determines a least

and arbitrary

for range-symmetric systems.

5.1
NR-SSOR preconditioner

NR-SSOR is mathematically equivalent to SSOR applied to

Let

\displaystyle H

\displaystyle M^{-1}N

\displaystyle I-M^{-1}A^{\rm T}A

We give the algorithm of NR-SSOR for the inner-iterations preconditioning
which works on the normal equations

Algorithm 3: NR-SSOR

Let

For

\mbox{\boldmath$r$}:=\mbox{\boldmath$r$}-d_{j}^{(k)}\mbox{\boldmath$a$}_{j}

End do

For NR-SSOR,

Then, the iteration matrix

M

Theorem 1

Proof.

The corresponding eigenvalue of

5.3
minimum-norm solution

The right preconditioned GMRES and RRGMRES solve
AB\mbox{\boldmath$u$}=\mbox{\boldmath$b$}
so that

\mbox{\boldmath$x$}=B\mbox{\boldmath$u$}=CA^{\rm T}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}
.
The necessary and sufficient condition that the solution

is a minimum-norm solution
is
\mbox{\boldmath$x$}\in{\cal N}(A)^{\perp}={\cal R}(A^{\rm T})
.
If
C=I
,
\mbox{\boldmath$x$}\in{\cal R}(A^{\rm T})
holds since

\mbox{\boldmath$x$}=CA^{\rm T}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}
,
but if
C\neq I
,
\mbox{\boldmath$x$}\in{\cal R}(A^{\rm T})
does not necessarily hold.
Therefore, if
C=I
, the solution of the right preconditioned GMRES and
RRGMRES
is the minimum-norm solution, but if
C\neq I
, the solution is not necessarily the minimum-norm solution.

For indefinite or singular symmetric systems, MINRES-QLP
[

was proposed

For singular symmetric systems, MINRES determines a least squares solution. Especially,

On the other hand,
for singular symmetric systems, MINRES-QLP determines a min-norm solution

Therefore, MINRES-QLP is more robust than MINRES for singular symmetric systems.

The right preconditioned matrix

and least squares problems even if the systems are inconsistent.

is more efficient and robust than

and obtains smaller residuals compared to MINRES-QLP
and the NR-SSOR right preconditioned GMRES.

Appendix A
Convergence theory of RRGMRES for nonsingular systems

In this appendix, we show the convergence theory of RRGMRES for nonsingular systems,
which we think is new.

Lemma
2
holds for both cases when
A
is nonsingular and singular.

Lemma 2
.

Assume
A\in{\bf R}^{n\times n}
.
Let
\mbox{\boldmath$x$}_{0}
be the initial iterate and
\mbox{\boldmath$r$}_{0}=\mbox{\boldmath$b$}-A\mbox{\boldmath$x$}_{0}
.

When RRGMRES is applied to
A\mbox{\boldmath$x$}=\mbox{\boldmath$b$}
with the initial iterate
\mbox{\boldmath$x$}_{0}
,

\displaystyle\|A\mbox{\boldmath$x$}_{k}-\mbox{\boldmath$b$}\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\min_{\mbox{\boldmath$y$}\in{\bf R}^{k}}\|H_{k+1,k}\mbox{\boldmath$y$}-V_{k+1}^{\rm T}\mbox{\boldmath$r$}_{0}\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle+\|(I_{n}-V_{k+1}V_{k+1}^{\rm T})\mbox{\boldmath$r$}_{0}\|_{2}^{2}.

where
\mbox{\boldmath$x$}_{k}=\mbox{\boldmath$x$}_{0}+V_{k}\mbox{\boldmath$y$}_{k}
and
\mbox{\boldmath$y$}_{k}
is the solution of the above minimization problem.

Proof.

\displaystyle\|A\mbox{\boldmath$x$}_{k}-\mbox{\boldmath$b$}\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\min_{\mbox{\boldmath$y$}\in{\bf R}^{k}}\|V_{k+1}H_{k+1,k}\mbox{\boldmath$y$}-V_{k+1}V_{k+1}^{\rm T}\mbox{\boldmath$r$}_{0}

\displaystyle+(V_{k+1}V_{k+1}^{\rm T}-I_{n})\mbox{\boldmath$r$}_{0}\|_{2}^{2}.

Here,
since
V_{k+1}V_{k+1}^{\rm T}\mbox{\boldmath$r$}_{0}
is the projection of
\mbox{\boldmath$r$}_{0}
onto
{\cal R}(V_{k+1})
,

(I_{n}-V_{k+1}V_{k+1}^{\rm T})\mbox{\boldmath$r$}_{0}
is the projection of
\mbox{\boldmath$r$}_{0}
onto
{\cal R}(V_{k+1})^{\perp}
.

Therefore,
V_{k+1}(H_{k+1,k}\mbox{\boldmath$y$}_{k}-V_{k+1}^{\rm T}\mbox{\boldmath$r$}_{0})\perp(I_{n}-V_{k+1}V_{k+1}^{\rm T})\mbox{\boldmath$r$}_{0}
.

Then,

\displaystyle\|V_{k+1}H_{k+1,k}\mbox{\boldmath$y$}-V_{k+1}V_{k+1}^{\rm T}\mbox{\boldmath$r$}_{0}+(V_{k+1}V_{k+1}^{\rm T}-I_{n})\mbox{\boldmath$r$}_{0}\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|V_{k+1}(H_{k+1,k}\mbox{\boldmath$y$}_{k}-V_{k+1}^{\rm T}\mbox{\boldmath$r$}_{0})\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle+\|(V_{k+1}V_{k+1}^{\rm T}-I_{n})\mbox{\boldmath$r$}_{0}\|_{2}^{2}

Therefore,

\displaystyle\|A\mbox{\boldmath$x$}_{k}-\mbox{\boldmath$b$}\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\min_{\mbox{\boldmath$y$}\in{\bf R}^{k}}\|V_{k+1}(H_{k+1,k}\mbox{\boldmath$y$}_{k}-V_{k+1}^{\rm T}\mbox{\boldmath$r$}_{0})\|_{2}^{2}+\|(V_{k+1}V_{k+1}^{\rm T}-I_{n})\mbox{\boldmath$r$}_{0}\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\min_{\mbox{\boldmath$y$}\in{\bf R}^{k}}\|H_{k+1,k}\mbox{\boldmath$y$}_{k}-V_{k+1}^{\rm T}\mbox{\boldmath$r$}_{0}\|_{2}^{2}+\|(V_{k+1}V_{k+1}^{\rm T}-I_{n})\mbox{\boldmath$r$}_{0}\|_{2}^{2}

∎

Theorem 5
.

Let
A\in{\bf R}^{n\times n}
be a nonsingular matrix.
Then,
the iterate
\mbox{\boldmath$x$}_{k}
of RRGMRES is an exact solution of
A\mbox{\boldmath$x$}=\mbox{\boldmath$b$}

if

h_{k+1,k}=0
.

Furthermore, RRGMRES converges in at most
n
iterations.

Proof.

Let
\mbox{\boldmath$x$}_{0}
be the initial iterate and
\mbox{\boldmath$r$}_{0}=\mbox{\boldmath$b$}-A\mbox{\boldmath$x$}_{0}
.

\displaystyle\|A\mbox{\boldmath$x$}_{k}-\mbox{\boldmath$b$}\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\min_{\mbox{\boldmath$y$}\in{\bf R}^{k}}\|H_{k+1,k}\mbox{\boldmath$y$}-V_{k+1}^{\rm T}\mbox{\boldmath$r$}_{0}\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle+\|(I_{n}-V_{k+1}V_{k+1}^{\rm T})\mbox{\boldmath$r$}_{0}\|_{2}^{2}.

Assume that
h_{k+1,k}=0
.
Since
AV_{k}=V_{k+1}H_{k+1,k}
, we have

AV_{k}=V_{k}H_{k,k}
.

Then,

\displaystyle\|A\mbox{\boldmath$x$}_{k}-\mbox{\boldmath$b$}\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\min_{\mbox{\boldmath$y$}\in{\bf R}^{k}}\|H_{k,k}\mbox{\boldmath$y$}-V_{k}^{\rm T}\mbox{\boldmath$r$}_{0}\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle+\|(I_{n}-V_{k}V_{k}^{\rm T})\mbox{\boldmath$r$}_{0}\|_{2}^{2}.

Firstly, we will prove that
H_{k,k}
is nonsingular and

\displaystyle\min_{\mbox{\boldmath$y$}\in{\bf R}^{k}}\|H_{k,k}\mbox{\boldmath$y$}-V_{k}^{\rm T}\mbox{\boldmath$r$}_{0}\|_{2}=0
.

Here, since
h_{k+1,k}=0
, we have
AV_{k}=V_{k}H_{k,k}
.

A
is nonsingular and
\mbox{rank}V_{k}=k
. Then,
\mbox{rank}AV_{k}=k
.

If
\mbox{rank}H_{k,k}<k
, we have
\mbox{rank}V_{k}H_{k,k}<k
.
Therefore,
\mbox{rank}H_{k,k}=k
. Since

H_{k,k}\in{\bf R}^{k\times k}
,
H_{k,k}
is nonsingular.

Then, for
\displaystyle\mbox{\boldmath$y$}_{k}=\mathop{\rm arg~min}\limits_{\mbox{\boldmath$y$}\in{\bf R}^{k}}\|H_{k,k}\mbox{\boldmath$y$}-V_{k}^{\rm T}\mbox{\boldmath$r$}_{0}\|_{2}
,

\|H_{k,k}\mbox{\boldmath$y$}_{k}-V_{k}^{\rm T}\mbox{\boldmath$r$}_{0}\|_{2}=0
.

Next, we will prove that

\|(I_{n}-V_{k}V_{k}^{\rm T})\mbox{\boldmath$r$}_{0}\|_{2}=\mbox{\boldmath$0$}
if
h_{k+1,k}=0
.

Assume
(I_{n}-V_{k}V_{k}^{\rm T})\mbox{\boldmath$r$}_{0}\neq\mbox{\boldmath$0$}
.
Then,
\mbox{\boldmath$r$}_{0}
and all column vectors of
V_{k}
are linearly independent.

Since
A
is nonsingular,
A\mbox{\boldmath$r$}_{0}
and all column vectors of
AV_{k}
are linearly independent.
However, since
h_{k+1,k}=0
,
A\mbox{\boldmath$r$}_{0}
and all column vectors of
AV_{k}
are linearly dependent.
This is a contradiction.
Therefore, if
h_{k+1,k}=0
, we have
(I_{k}-V_{k}V_{k}^{\rm T})\mbox{\boldmath$r$}_{0}=\mbox{\boldmath$0$}

Here, there exists
k\leq n
such
h_{k+1,k}=0
.
Then, if
A
is nonsingular, RRGMRES converges to a solution of
A\mbox{\boldmath$x$}=\mbox{\boldmath$b$}
at the
k
th step.
Furthermore, since there exists
k\leq n
such
h_{k+1,k}=0
, RRGMRES converges in at most
n
iterations.
∎

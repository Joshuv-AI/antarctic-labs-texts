Approximation Capabilities of Feedforward Neural Networks with GELU Activations
Konstantin Yakovlev, Nikita Puchkin

Abstract
We derive an approximation error bound that holds simultaneously for a function and all its derivatives up to any prescribed order. The bounds apply to elementary functions, including multivariate polynomials, the exponential function, and the reciprocal function, and are obtained using feedforward neural networks with the Gaussian Error Linear Unit (GELU) activation. In addition, we report the network size, weight magnitudes, and behavior at infinity. Our analysis begins with a constructive approximation of multiplication, where we prove the simultaneous validity of error bounds over domains of increasing size for a given approximator. Leveraging this result, we obtain approximation guarantees for division and the exponential function, ensuring that all higher‑order derivatives of the resulting approximators remain globally bounded.



1
Introduction

We investigate simultaneous approximation of multivariate functions and their higher-order derivatives using deep feedforward neural networks.
Since approximating derivatives necessitates a smooth activation function, we employ networks with infinitely differentiable the Gaussian Error Linear Unit (GELU)
(
Hendrycks and Gimpel 2016
)
.
Our choice is motivated by the fact that higher‑order derivatives of the GELU activation can be expressed in terms of Hermite polynomials, which enables simple and tractable bounds on their absolute values.
In addition, this activation function is widely adopted in state-of-the-art large language models
(
Devlin et al. 2019
;
Raffel et al. 2020
;
Shoeybi et al. 2019
)
, which further underscores its practical relevance.

The core of our constructive approach is the localized approximation of polynomials.
While prior works have developed approximation theory for smooth functions and their derivatives on fixed compact sets
(
Yarotsky 2017
;
De Ryck et al. 2021
;
Gühring and Raslan 2021
;
Belomestny et al. 2023
)
, they do not provide error bounds beyond the original domain nor offer simultaneous guarantees across a sequence of increasingly large domains.
Despite recent advances in approximation of functions with noncompact domain presented in
Schwab and Zech 2021
;
van Nuland 2024
;
Abdeljawad and Dittrich 2024
, the results either do not focus on the simultaneous approximation of derivatives or impose strong assumptions on the weight function of the underlying weighted
L^{p}
space
(
Abdeljawad and Dittrich 2024
)
.
We bridge this gap by providing explicit control on how approximation errors for fundamental operations (like multiplication) scale as the domain size grows.
This allows us to construct an approximation of monomials with globally bounded higher‑order derivatives.
This properties are relevant in approximation of functions with unbounded domains including generative modelling
(
Oko et al. 2023
;
Tang and Yang 2024
;
Azangulov et al. 2024
;
Yakovlev and Puchkin 2025
;
Fukumizu et al. 2025
)
and physics-informed neural networks
(
Abdo et al. 2024
;
Alejo et al. 2024
)
.

Our key technical innovations are twofold.
First, we systematically employ a clipping operation on the network input.
By clipping the neural network input, we ensure that the derivatives are globally bounded, since they are bounded on a compact domain.
Second, we establish approximation guarantees for partition-of-unity functions in Sobolev seminorms, constructing functions with globally bounded derivatives and light tails.

As a consequence of these results, we provide approximation error bounds for the exponential function approximation and division approximation together with its derivatives, ensuring that their higher-order derivatives are globally bounded.
Consequently, we extend the approximation results for elementary functions presented in
Oko et al. 2023
;
Yakovlev and Puchkin 2025
to Sobolev seminorms on domains of increasing size.

Notation.
The set of non-negative integers is denoted by
\mathbb{Z}_{+}=\{0,1,2,\dots\}
.
A multi-index
{\mathbf{k}}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{d}
, where
d\in\mathbb{N}
, is denoted in bold.
We also define
|{\mathbf{k}}|=k_{1}+k_{2}+\ldots+k_{d}
,
{\mathbf{k}}!=k_{1}!\cdot k_{2}!\cdot\ldots\cdot k_{d}!

For a vector
v\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
we define
v^{\mathbf{k}}=v_{1}^{k_{1}}v_{2}^{k_{2}}\ldots v_{d}^{k_{d}}
.
For a function
f
of
d
variables, its weak derivative with respect to the multi-index
{\mathbf{k}}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{d}
is denoted as

\displaystyle\partial^{\mathbf{k}}f=\frac{\partial^{|{\mathbf{k}}|}f}{\partial x_{1}^{k_{1}}\partial x_{2}^{k_{2}}\ldots\partial x_{d}^{k_{d}}}.

Throughout the paper, we employ the notation
f\lesssim g
to indicate that
f={\mathcal{O}}(g)
.
If
f\lesssim g
and
g\lesssim f
, then we write
f\asymp g
.
We frequently replace the expression for
\min\{a,b\}
and
\max\{a,b\}
with
a\vee b
and
a\wedge b
, respectively.
For any
x>0
, we define
\log(x)=\ln(x\vee e)
.

2
Preliminaries and notations

Norms.
We denote the Euclidean norm of a vector
v
as
\|v\|
, the maximal absolute value of its entries as
\|v\|\infty
, and the number of its non-zero entries as
\|v\|_{0}
.
Similarly,
\|A\|\infty
and
\|A\|_{0}
represent the maximal absolute value of entries and the number of non-zero entries of matrix
A
, respectively.
Finally, for a set
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{r}
and a function
f:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we define

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}=\operatornamewithlimits{esssup}_{x\in\Omega}\|f(x)\|.

Smoothness spaces.
We introduce the Sobolev space to characterize the regularity of functions in our analysis, and its definition is provided below.

Definition 2.1

(Sobolev space)
.

Let
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{r}
be an open set, and let
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
.
Then, the Sobolev space
W^{k,\infty}(\Omega)
is defined as follows:

\displaystyle W^{k,\infty}(\Omega)=\{f\in L^{\infty}(\Omega):\partial^{\mathbf{k}}f\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)\quad\text{for every }{\mathbf{k}}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{r}\text{ with }|{\mathbf{k}}|\leqslant k\}.

Here,
L^{\infty}(\Omega)
is the Lebesgue space.
We define the Sobolev seminorm on
W^{k,\infty}(\Omega)
as

\displaystyle|f|_{W^{k,\infty}(\Omega)}=\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}{\mathbf{k}}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{r},\;|{\mathbf{k}}|=k\end{subarray}}\|\partial^{\mathbf{k}}f\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}.

Finally, we define the Sobolev norm on
W^{k,\infty}(\Omega)
as

\displaystyle\|f\|_{W^{k,\infty}(\Omega)}=\max_{0\leqslant m\leqslant k}|f|_{W^{m,\infty}(\Omega)}.

Neural networks.
In this paper, we focus on feed-forward neural networks employing the Gaussian Error Linear Unit (GELU) activation function:

\displaystyle\mathrm{GELU}(x)=x\cdot\Phi(x),\quad\Phi(x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int\limits_{-\infty}^{x}e^{-t^{2}/2}{\mathrm{d}}t.

The choice of GeLU is motivated by its infinite smoothness and bounded derivatives (see Lemma
B.2
).
For a vector
b=(b_{1},\dots,b_{r})\in\mathbb{R}^{r}
, we define the shifted activation function
\mathrm{GELU}_{b}:\mathbb{R}^{r}\to\mathbb{R}^{r}
as

\displaystyle\mathrm{GELU}_{b}(x)=(\mathrm{GELU}(x_{1}-b_{1}),\dots,\mathrm{GELU}(x_{r}-b_{r})),\quad x=(x_{1},\dots,x_{r})\in\mathbb{R}^{r}.

Given a network depth
L\in\mathbb{N}
and a vector of layer sizes
W=(W_{0},W_{1},\dots,W_{L})\in\mathbb{N}^{L+1}
.
Then, a neural network of depth
L
and architecture
W
is a function
f:\mathbb{R}^{W_{0}}\to\mathbb{R}^{W_{L}}
such that

\displaystyle f(x)=-b_{L}+A_{L}\circ\mathrm{GELU}_{b_{L-1}}\circ A_{L-1}\circ\mathrm{GELU}_{b_{L-2}}\circ\dots\circ A_{2}\circ\mathrm{GELU}_{b_{1}}\circ A_{1}\circ x,

(1)

where
A_{j}\in\mathbb{R}^{W_{j}\times W_{j-1}}
is a weight matrix and
b_{j}\in\mathbb{R}^{W_{j}}
is a bias vector for all
j\in{1,\dots,L}
.
The maximum number of neurons of each layer is given by
\|W\|_{\infty}
and is reffered to as the width of the neural network.
We define the class
\mathsf{NN}(L,W,S,B)
of neural networks of the form (
1
) with at most
S
non-zero weights and the weight magnitude
B
as follows:

\displaystyle\mathsf{NN}(L,W,S,B)=\left\{f\text{ of the form }\eqref{eq:feed_forward_nn_def}\,:\,\sum_{j=1}^{L}(\|A_{j}\|_{0}+\|b_{j}\|_{0})\leqslant S,\;\max_{1\leqslant j\leqslant L}\|A_{j}\|_{\infty}\vee\|b_{j}\|_{\infty}\leqslant B\right\}.

3
Main results

This section presents our main results.
Specifically, Subsection
3.1
details approximation error bounds for elementary operations, including the identity function, partition of unity, and square operation.
Subsection
3.2
elaborates on the approximation of monomials.
Finally, Subsection
3.3

3.1
Approximation of elementary operations

Passing the output of one layer to a non-adjacent layer is frequently beneficial.
Note that ReLU activation allows for an exact identity mapping
(
Nakada and Imaizumi 2020a
)
.
However, in the case of GELU, an approximate mapping is guaranteed, as demonstrated by the following lemma, which provides an approximation error bound for a single-layer neural network.

Lemma 3.1

(approximation of identity operation)
.

Let
m\in\mathbb{N}
and let
\mathrm{id}(x)=x
.
Then, for any
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
there exists
\varphi_{id}\in\mathsf{NN}(L,W,S,B)
satisfying

\displaystyle\|\varphi_{id}-\mathrm{id}\|_{W^{m,\infty}([-C,C])}\leqslant C^{2}\varepsilon,\quad\text{for all }C\geqslant 1.

Furthermore,
L=2
,
\|W\|_{\infty}=1
,
S=3
, and
\log B\lesssim\log(1/\varepsilon)+\log m
.

Proof.

The proof follows the same approach as outlined in
Scarselli and Tsoi 1998
.
We let

\displaystyle\varphi_{id}(x)=-\frac{R\cdot\mathrm{GELU}(0)}{\partial^{1}\mathrm{GELU}(0)}+\frac{R}{\partial^{1}\mathrm{GELU}(0)}\mathrm{GELU}\left(\frac{x}{R}\right),

where
R>0
and will be determined later in the proof.
We also emphasize that the form of
\varphi_{id}
is valid, since
\partial^{1}\mathrm{GELU}(0)=1/2
.
Taylor expansion suggests that for any
x\in[-C,C]
it holds that

\displaystyle|\varphi_{id}(x)-x|\leqslant\frac{|\mathrm{GELU}|_{W^{2,\infty}(\mathbb{R})}C^{2}}{2\cdot\partial^{1}\mathrm{GELU}(0)R}.

Similarly, we deduce that

\displaystyle|\partial^{1}\varphi_{id}(x)-1|\leqslant\frac{|\mathrm{GELU}|_{W^{2,\infty}(\mathbb{R})}C}{\partial^{1}\mathrm{GELU}(0)R}.

Additionally, for any
k\geqslant 2
we find that

\displaystyle|\varphi_{id}-\mathrm{id}|_{W^{k,\infty}(\mathbb{R})}=|\varphi_{id}|_{W^{k,\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\leqslant\frac{|\mathrm{GELU}|_{W^{k,\infty}(\mathbb{R})}}{\partial^{1}\mathrm{GELU}(0)R^{k-1}}.

Therefore, choosing

\displaystyle R=\max_{2\leqslant k\leqslant m}\left(\frac{|\mathrm{GELU}|_{W^{k,\infty}(\mathbb{R})}}{\partial^{1}\mathrm{GELU}(0)\varepsilon}\right)^{1/(k-1)}\vee 1,

ensures that for any
C\geqslant 1

\displaystyle\max_{0\leqslant k\leqslant 1}|\varphi_{id}-\mathrm{id}|_{W^{k,\infty}([-C,C])}\leqslant C^{2}\varepsilon,\quad\max_{2\leqslant k\leqslant m}|\varphi_{id}-\mathrm{id}|_{W^{k,\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\leqslant\varepsilon.

Therefore, it holds that

\displaystyle\|\varphi_{id}-\mathrm{id}\|_{W^{m,\infty}([-C,C])}\leqslant C^{2}\varepsilon,\quad\text{for all }C\geqslant 1.

We next specify the configuration of
\varphi_{id}
.
Clearly,
L=2
,
\|W\|_{\infty}=1
and
S=3
.
As for the weight magnitude, we apply Lemma
B.2
, arriving at

\displaystyle\log B\lesssim\log(1/\varepsilon)+\max_{2\leqslant k\leqslant m}\frac{\log\left(|\mathrm{GELU}|_{W^{k,\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\vee 1\right)}{k-1}\lesssim\log(1/\varepsilon)+\max_{2\leqslant k\leqslant m}\frac{\log\left((k+1)!\right)}{k-1}.

Now Stirling’s approximation implies that
\log((k+1)!)\lesssim k\log k
, and thus,

\displaystyle\log B\lesssim\log(1/\varepsilon)+\log m.

The proof is complete.

∎

The following lemma generalizes the result presented in Lemma
3.1
to the case of multiple layers.

Lemma 3.2

(approximation of identity operation with multiple layers)
.

Let
m\in\mathbb{N}
and let
\mathrm{id}:x\mapsto x
.
Then, for every
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
, every
L\in\mathbb{N}
with
L\geqslant 2
, and every
K\geqslant 1
, there exists
\varphi_{id}\in\mathsf{NN}(L,W,S,B)
such that

\displaystyle(i)

\displaystyle\quad\|\varphi_{id}-\mathrm{id}\|_{W^{m,\infty}([-K,K])}\leqslant\varepsilon,

\displaystyle(ii)

\displaystyle\quad\|\varphi_{id}\|_{W^{m,\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\leqslant\exp\{{\mathcal{O}}(m\log(m\log(1/\varepsilon))+\log(2K))\}.

Moreover, it holds that

\displaystyle\|W\|_{\infty}\lesssim 1,\quad S\lesssim L,\quad\log B\lesssim(m+L)\log m+\log(1/\varepsilon)+m\log(K).

The proof of Lemma
3.2
is moved to Appendix
A.1
.
Next, we move to the approximation of partition of unity, a crucial component in the framework of localized Taylor polynomials
(
Gühring and Raslan 2021
;
De Ryck et al. 2021
)
.
First, we approximate the Heaviside step function, as presented in the following lemma.

Lemma 3.3

(Approximation of Heaviside step function)
.

For every
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
, every
\varkappa\in(0,1)
, and every
m\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists a GELU network
\varphi_{\varkappa}\in\mathsf{NN}(L,W,S,B)
such that

\displaystyle(i)

\displaystyle\quad\|\varphi_{\varkappa}\|_{W^{m,\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\leqslant\exp\left\{{\mathcal{O}}(m\log(m\log(1/\varepsilon)/\varkappa))\right\},

\displaystyle(ii)

\displaystyle\quad\|\varphi_{\varkappa}\|_{W^{m,\infty}((-\infty,-\varkappa])}\vee\|1-\varphi_{\varkappa}\|_{W^{m,\infty}([\varkappa,+\infty))}\leqslant\varepsilon

Moreover,
\varphi_{\varkappa}
has
L=2
,
\|W\|_{\infty}\vee S\lesssim 1
, and
\log B\lesssim m\log(m/\varkappa)+\log(1/\varepsilon)
.

Proof.

Let

\displaystyle\eta(x)=\frac{\mathrm{GELU}(x+\varepsilon_{0})-\mathrm{GELU}(x-\varepsilon_{0})}{2\varepsilon_{0}},\quad x\in\mathbb{R},

where
\varepsilon_{0}\in(0,1)
will be determined later.
Note that
\eta
approximates
\partial^{1}\mathrm{GELU}
is Sobolev norm.
Formally, the Taylor expansion suggests that for any
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
and
x\in\mathbb{R}
we have

\displaystyle|\partial^{k}\eta(x)-\partial^{k+1}\mathrm{GELU}(x)|

\displaystyle=\frac{|\partial^{k}\mathrm{GELU}(x+\varepsilon_{0})-\partial^{k}\mathrm{GELU}(x-\varepsilon_{0})-2\varepsilon\partial^{k+1}\mathrm{GELU}(x)|}{2\varepsilon_{0}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{\varepsilon_{0}^{2}}{6}|\mathrm{GELU}|_{W^{k+3,\infty}(\mathbb{R})}.

Hence, it holds that

\displaystyle\|\eta-\partial^{1}\mathrm{GELU}\|_{W^{m,\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\leqslant\frac{\varepsilon_{0}^{2}}{6}\max_{0\leqslant k\leqslant m}|\mathrm{GELU}|_{W^{k+3,\infty}(\mathbb{R})}.

(2)

Now let
\varphi_{\varkappa}(x)=\eta(\alpha x)
for
\alpha\geqslant 1
that will be optimized later.
Therefore, triangle inequality yields

\displaystyle\|\varphi_{\varkappa}\|_{W^{m,\infty}([-\infty,-\varkappa])}

\displaystyle\leqslant\alpha^{m}\|\eta\|_{W^{m,\infty}((-\infty,-\alpha\varkappa])}

\displaystyle\leqslant\alpha^{m}\left(\|\partial^{1}\mathrm{GELU}-\eta\|_{W^{m,\infty}((-\infty,-\alpha\varkappa])}+\|\partial^{1}\mathrm{GELU}\|_{W^{m,\infty}((-\infty,-\alpha\varkappa])}\right).

Thus, Lemma
B.2
together with (
2
) implies that

\displaystyle\|\varphi_{\varkappa}\|_{W^{m,\infty}([-\infty,-\varkappa])}\leqslant\alpha^{m}\left(\frac{\varepsilon_{0}^{2}}{6}(m+4)\sqrt{(m+1)!}+2e^{-\alpha^{2}\varkappa^{2}/4}\sqrt{(m+1)!}\right)

Setting
\alpha=2\varkappa^{-1}\sqrt{2\log(1/\varepsilon_{0})}\geqslant 1
ensures that

\displaystyle\|\varphi_{\varkappa}\|_{W^{m,\infty}([-\infty,-\varkappa])}\leqslant\alpha^{m}\varepsilon_{0}^{2}(m+3)\sqrt{(m+1)!}\leqslant(8\varkappa^{-2}\log(1/\varepsilon_{0}))^{m/2}\varepsilon_{0}^{2}(m+3)\sqrt{(m+1)!}.

Using the fact that

\displaystyle\sup_{\varepsilon\in(0,1)}\varepsilon(\log(1/\varepsilon))^{m/2}\leqslant\left(\frac{m}{2e}\right)^{m/2}\leqslant m^{m/2}

we find that

\displaystyle\|\varphi_{\varkappa}\|_{W^{m,\infty}((-\infty,-\varkappa])}\leqslant(8\kappa^{-2}m)^{m/2}\varepsilon_{0}(m+3)\sqrt{(m+1)!}.

Hence, setting

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{0}=\left((8\kappa^{-2}m)^{m/2}(m+3)\sqrt{(m+1)!}\right)^{-1}\varepsilon\in(0,1)

(3)

ensures that

\displaystyle\|\varphi_{\varkappa}\|_{W^{m,\infty}((-\infty,-\varkappa])}\leqslant\varepsilon.

Using similar argument, we also deduce that

\displaystyle\|1-\varphi_{\varkappa}\|_{W^{m,\infty}([\kappa,+\infty))}\leqslant\varepsilon.

We next note that in view of (
2
) and Lemma
B.2
, it holds that

\displaystyle\|\varphi_{\varkappa}\|_{W^{m,\infty}(\mathbb{R})}

\displaystyle\leqslant\alpha^{m}(\|\eta-\partial^{1}\mathrm{GELU}\|_{W^{m,\infty}(\mathbb{R})}+\|\partial^{1}\mathrm{GELU}\|_{W^{m,\infty}(\mathbb{R})})

\displaystyle\leqslant\alpha^{m}(\frac{\varepsilon_{0}^{2}}{6}(m+4)\sqrt{(m+1)!}+(m+2)\sqrt{(m-1)!}).

The choice of
\alpha
indicates that

\displaystyle\|\varphi_{\varkappa}\|_{W^{m,\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\leqslant 2\alpha^{m}(m+2)\sqrt{(m+1)!}\leqslant 2(8\kappa^{-2}\log(1/\varepsilon_{0}))^{m/2}(m+2)\sqrt{(m+1)!}.

Now the choice of
\varepsilon_{0}
from (
3
) yields

\displaystyle\|\varphi_{\varkappa}\|_{W^{m,\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\leqslant\exp\left\{{\mathcal{O}}(m\log(m\log(1/\varepsilon)/\varkappa))\right\}.

Finally, we specify the configuration of
\varphi_{\varkappa}
.
Clearly,
L=2
,
\|W\|_{\infty}\vee S\lesssim 1
, and

\displaystyle\log B\lesssim\log(\alpha)+\log(1/\varepsilon_{0})\lesssim m\log(m/\varkappa)+\log(1/\varepsilon).

The proof is complete.

∎

Subsequently, we use Lemma
3.3
to approximate a partition of unity.
Following
Yakovlev and Puchkin 2025
, we use non-uniform partition, a key element in approximating the division operation.
The result is presented below.

Lemma 3.4

(partition of unity approximation)
.

Define
a_{i}=2^{-N+i}
for each
i\in\{0,1,\dots,N\}
, where
N\in\mathbb{N}
and
N\geqslant 3
.
Then, for every
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
and every
m\in\mathbb{N}
, there exist
\{\psi_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
, with
\psi_{i}\in\mathsf{NN}(L,W,S,B)
for each
1\leqslant i\leqslant N
, such that

\displaystyle(i)

\displaystyle\quad\sum_{i=1}^{N}\psi_{i}(x)=1,\quad\text{for all }x\in\mathbb{R},

\displaystyle(ii)

\displaystyle\quad\max_{1\leqslant i\leqslant N}\|\psi_{i}\|_{W^{m,\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\leqslant\exp\{{\mathcal{O}}(mN+m\log(m\log(1/\varepsilon)))\},

\displaystyle(iii)

\displaystyle\quad\|\psi_{N}\|_{W^{m,\infty}(-\infty,a_{N-2}]}\vee\|\psi_{1}\|_{W^{m,\infty}([a_{2},+\infty))}\vee\max_{2\leqslant i\leqslant N-1}\|\psi_{i}\|_{W^{m,\infty}(\mathbb{R}\setminus(a_{i-2},a_{i+1}))}\leqslant\varepsilon,

Furthermore,
L=2
,
\|W\|_{\infty}\vee S\lesssim 1
and
\log B\lesssim\log(1/\varepsilon)+mN+m\log m
.

Proof.

Next, for a Heaviside function approximation
\varphi_{a_{0}}
from Lemma
3.3
formulated with accuracy parameter
\varepsilon/2
and
\kappa=a_{0}
, we define

\displaystyle\psi_{i}(x)=\begin{cases}1-\varphi_{a_{0}}(x-a_{1}),\quad&i=1,\\
\varphi_{a_{0}}(x-a_{i-1})-\varphi_{a_{0}}(x-a_{i}),\quad&i\in\{2,\dots,N-1\},\\
\varphi_{a_{0}}(x-a_{N-1}),\quad&i=N\end{cases}

It is clear that for all
x\in\mathbb{R}

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N}\psi_{i}(x)=1.

In other words,
\{\psi_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
forms a partition of unity.
Now derive the behavior of tails for each
\psi_{i}
.
First, note that for each
1\leqslant i\leqslant N
we have that

\displaystyle\|\varphi_{a_{0}}(\cdot-a_{i})\|_{W^{m,\infty}((-\infty,a_{i-1}])}\leqslant\|\varphi_{a_{0}}\|_{W^{m,\infty}((-\infty,-a_{0}])}\leqslant\varepsilon/2

(4)

and similarly

\displaystyle\|1-\varphi_{a_{0}}(\cdot-a_{i})\|_{W^{m,\infty}([a_{i+1},+\infty))}\leqslant\|1-\varphi_{a_{0}}\|_{W^{m,\infty}([a_{0},+\infty))}\leqslant\varepsilon/2.

(5)

Therefore,

\displaystyle\|\psi_{N}\|_{W^{m,\infty}(-\infty,a_{N-2}]}\vee\|\psi_{1}\|_{W^{m,\infty}([a_{2},+\infty))}\leqslant\varepsilon.

Next, for any
2\leqslant i\leqslant N-1
it holds that

\displaystyle\|\psi_{i}\|_{W^{m,\infty}(\mathbb{R}\setminus(a_{i-2},a_{i+1}))}=\|\psi_{i}\|_{W^{m,\infty}((-\infty,a_{i-2}])}\vee\|\psi_{i}\|_{W^{m,\infty}([a_{i+1},+\infty))}

First, from (
4
) we find that

\displaystyle\|\psi_{i}\|_{W^{m,\infty}((-\infty,a_{i-2}])}\leqslant\|\varphi_{a_{0}}(\cdot-a_{i-1})\|_{W^{m,\infty}((-\infty,a_{i-2}])}+\|\varphi_{a_{0}}(\cdot-a_{i})\|_{W^{m,\infty}((-\infty,a_{i-2}])}\leqslant\varepsilon.

Second, (
5
) implies that

\displaystyle\|\psi_{i}\|_{W^{m,\infty}([a_{i+1},+\infty))}\leqslant\|1-\varphi_{a_{0}}(\cdot-a_{i-1})\|_{W^{m,\infty}([a_{i+1},+\infty))}+\|1-\varphi_{a_{0}}(\cdot-a_{i})\|_{W^{m,\infty}([a_{i+1},+\infty))}\leqslant\varepsilon.

Thus, we arrive at

\displaystyle\|\psi_{N}\|_{W^{m,\infty}(-\infty,a_{N-2}]}\vee\|\psi_{1}\|_{W^{m,\infty}([a_{2},+\infty))}\vee\max_{2\leqslant i\leqslant N-1}\|\psi_{i}\|_{W^{m,\infty}(\mathbb{R}\setminus(a_{i-2},a_{i+1}))}\leqslant\varepsilon.

Now we focus on the behavior of each
\psi_{i}
on the real line.
Formally, Lemma
3.3
suggests that for any
1\leqslant i\leqslant N

\displaystyle\|\psi_{i}\|_{W^{m,\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\leqslant 2\|\varphi_{a_{0}}\|_{W^{m,\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\leqslant\exp\left\{{\mathcal{O}}(m\log(m\log(1/\varepsilon)/a_{0}))\right\}.

Recall that
a_{0}=2^{-N}
.
Hence, it holds that

\displaystyle\|\psi_{i}\|_{W^{m,\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\leqslant\exp\{{\mathcal{O}}(mN+m\log(m\log(1/\varepsilon)))\}.

We now specify the configuration for each
\psi_{i}
.
Using the configuration of
\varphi_{a_{0}}
outlined in Lemma
3.3
and parallelization argument from Lemma
B.6
, we conclude that

\displaystyle L=2,\quad\|W\|_{\infty}\vee S\lesssim 1,

\displaystyle\log B\lesssim\log(1/\varepsilon)+m\log(m/a_{0})\lesssim\log(1/\varepsilon)+mN+m\log m.

The proof is finished.

∎

Next, we aim to approximate the clipping operation, which is essential for controlling the Sobolev norm at infinity of the approximator.
The following lemma demonstrates the existence of a shallow GELU network for approximating clipping.

Lemma 3.5

(approximation of clipping operation)
.

For every
A\geqslant 1
, every
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
, and every
m\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists
\varphi_{clip}\in\mathsf{NN}(L,W,S,B)
such that

\displaystyle(i)

\displaystyle\quad\|\varphi_{clip}-\mathrm{id}\|_{W^{m,\infty}([-A,A])}\leqslant\varepsilon,

\displaystyle(ii)

\displaystyle\quad\|\varphi_{clip}+A+1/2\|_{W^{m,\infty}((-\infty,-A-1])}\vee\|\varphi_{clip}-A-1/2\|_{W^{m,\infty}([A+1,+\infty))}\leqslant\varepsilon,

\displaystyle(iii)

\displaystyle\quad\|\varphi_{clip}\|_{W^{m,\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\leqslant\exp\{{\mathcal{O}}(m\log m+m\log\log(1/\varepsilon)+\log(2A))\},

\displaystyle(iv)

\displaystyle\quad\|\varphi_{clip}\|_{W^{0,\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\leqslant A+5/2,

\displaystyle(v)

\displaystyle\quad\|\varphi_{clip}+A+1/2\|_{W^{0,\infty}((-\infty,-A])}\vee\|\varphi_{clip}-A-1/2\|_{W^{0,\infty}([A,+\infty))}\leqslant\varepsilon+1,

\displaystyle(vi)

\displaystyle\quad|\varphi_{clip}|_{W^{k,\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\leqslant\exp\{{\mathcal{O}}(k\log m+k\log\log(1/\varepsilon))\}.

Moreover,
\varphi_{clip}
has
L=\|W\|_{\infty}=2
,
S=7
and
\log B\lesssim\log(Am/\varepsilon)
.

Proof.

Define

\displaystyle\varphi_{clip}(x)=\alpha^{-1}\mathrm{GELU}(\alpha(x+A+1/2))-\alpha^{-1}\mathrm{GELU}(\alpha(x-A-1/2))-A-1/2,\quad x\in\mathbb{R},

(6)

where
\alpha\geqslant 1
will be determined later in the proof.
Therefore, Lemma
B.2
implies that

\displaystyle\|\varphi_{clip}-\mathrm{id}\|_{W^{m,\infty}([-A,A])}

\displaystyle\leqslant\alpha^{m}\|\mathrm{GELU}-\mathrm{id}\|_{W^{m,\infty}([\alpha/2,+\infty))}+\alpha^{m}\|\mathrm{GELU}\|_{W^{m,\infty}((-\infty,-\alpha/2])}

\displaystyle\leqslant 4\alpha^{m}\exp(-\alpha^{2}/16).

We next note that

\displaystyle\sup_{\alpha>0}\alpha^{m}\exp(-\alpha^{2}/32)\leqslant\exp\{{\mathcal{O}}(m\log m)\},

(7)

which implies that

\displaystyle\|\varphi_{clip}-\mathrm{id}\|_{W^{m,\infty}([-A,A])}

\displaystyle\leqslant\exp\{{\mathcal{O}}(m\log m)\}\exp\{-\alpha^{2}/32\}.

Therefore, setting

\displaystyle\alpha\asymp\sqrt{m\log m+\log(1/\varepsilon)}

(8)

guarantees that

\displaystyle\|\varphi_{clip}-\mathrm{id}\|_{W^{m,\infty}([-A,A])}\leqslant\varepsilon.

(9)

We next focus on the behavior of tails of
\varphi_{clip}

\displaystyle\|\varphi_{clip}\|_{W^{m,\infty}(\mathbb{R}\setminus(-A-1,A+1))}=\|\varphi_{clip}\|_{W^{m,\infty}((-\infty,-A-1])}\vee\|\varphi_{clip}\|_{W^{m,\infty}([A+1,+\infty))}.

We also note that

\displaystyle\|\varphi_{clip}+A+1/2\|_{W^{m,\infty}((-\infty,-A-1])}\leqslant 2\alpha^{m}\|\mathrm{GELU}\|_{W^{m,\infty}((-\infty,-\alpha/2])}

and, similarly,

\displaystyle\|\varphi_{clip}-A-1/2\|_{W^{m,\infty}([A+1,+\infty))}\leqslant 2\alpha^{m}\|\mathrm{GELU}-\mathrm{id}\|_{W^{m,\infty}([\alpha/2,+\infty))}.

From Lemma
B.2
we find that

\displaystyle\|\varphi_{clip}+A+1/2\|_{W^{m,\infty}((-\infty,-A-1])}\vee\|\varphi_{clip}-A-1/2\|_{W^{m,\infty}([A+1,+\infty))}\leqslant 4\alpha^{m}\exp(-\alpha^{2}/16)\leqslant\varepsilon,

where the last inequality uses (
7
) and (
8
).
Therefore, due to the triangle inequality we have that

\displaystyle\|\varphi_{clip}\|_{W^{m,\infty}(\mathbb{R}\setminus(-A-1,A+1))}\leqslant\varepsilon+A+1/2.

(10)

We next derive the Sobolev norm of
\varphi_{clip}
on the real line.
Using (
10
), we have that

\displaystyle\|\varphi_{clip}\|_{W^{m,\infty}(\mathbb{R})}

\displaystyle=\|\varphi_{clip}\|_{W^{m,\infty}(\mathbb{R}\setminus(-A-1,A+1))}\vee\|\varphi_{clip}\|_{W^{m,\infty}([-A-1,A+1])}

\displaystyle\leqslant(\varepsilon+A+1/2)\vee\|\varphi_{clip}\|_{W^{m,\infty}([-A-1,A+1])}.

Lemma
B.2
together with (
8
) implies that

\displaystyle\|\varphi_{clip}\|_{W^{m,\infty}([-A-1,A+1])}

\displaystyle\quad\leqslant 2(\alpha^{m}\|\partial^{1}\mathrm{GELU}\|_{W^{m-1,\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\vee\alpha^{-1}\|\mathrm{GELU}\|_{W^{0,\infty}([-2A-3/2,2A+3/2])})+A+1/2

\displaystyle\quad\leqslant\exp\{{\mathcal{O}}(m\log m+m\log\log(1/\varepsilon)+\log(2A))\}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\|\varphi_{clip}\|_{W^{m,\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\leqslant\exp\{{\mathcal{O}}(m\log m+m\log\log(1/\varepsilon)+\log(2A))\}.

Similarly, for any
1\leqslant k\leqslant m
, Lemma
B.2
in conjunction with (
6
) and (
8
) yields

\displaystyle|\varphi_{clip}|_{W^{k,\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\leqslant 2\alpha^{k-1}|\mathrm{GELU}|_{W^{k,\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\leqslant\exp\{{\mathcal{O}}(k\log m+k\log\log(1/\varepsilon))\}.

In addition, from Lemma
B.2
we find that

\displaystyle\|\varphi_{clip}-A-1/2\|_{W^{0,\infty}([A,A+1])}

\displaystyle\quad\leqslant\|\mathrm{GELU}-\mathrm{id}\|_{W^{0,\infty}([\alpha(2A+1/2),+\infty))}+\|\mathrm{id}-A-1/2\|_{W^{0,\infty}([A,A+1])}+\alpha^{-1}\|\mathrm{GELU}\|_{W^{0,\infty}([-\alpha/2,\alpha/2])}

\displaystyle\quad\leqslant\varepsilon+1.

Similarly,

\displaystyle\|\varphi_{clip}+A+1/2\|_{W^{0,\infty}([-A-1,-A])}\leqslant\varepsilon+1.

Therefore, from (
9
) and (
10
) we deduce that

\displaystyle\|\varphi_{clip}\|_{W^{0,\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\leqslant\varepsilon+A+3/2\leqslant A+5/2.

Finally, we specify the configuration of
\varphi_{clip}
.
The choice of
\alpha
from (
8
) in conjunction with (
6
) suggest that

\displaystyle L=\|W\|_{\infty}=2,\quad S=7,\quad\log B\lesssim\log(\alpha\vee\alpha^{-1}\vee A)\lesssim\log(Am/\varepsilon).

The proof is complete.

∎

3.2
Approximation of monomials

Now, we focus on approximating polynomials.
A key starting point is the approximation of the square operation, as demonstrated in the following lemma.

Lemma 3.6

(approximation of square operation)
.

Define
f_{sq}:x\mapsto x^{2}
.
Then, for every
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
and every
m\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists
\varphi_{sq}\in\mathsf{NN}(L,W,S,B)
such that

\displaystyle\|\varphi_{sq}-f_{sq}\|_{W^{m,\infty}([-C,C])}\leqslant C^{3}\varepsilon,\quad\text{for all }C\geqslant 1.

Furthermore,
L=\|W\|_{\infty}=2
,
S=6
and
\log B\lesssim\log(1/\varepsilon)+\log m
.

Proof.

Inspired by
(
Scarselli and Tsoi 1998
, Theorem 2)
, we let

\displaystyle\varphi_{sq}(x)=\frac{R^{2}}{\partial^{2}\mathrm{GELU}(0)}\left(\mathrm{GELU}\left(\frac{2x}{R}\right)-2\cdot\mathrm{GELU}\left(\frac{x}{R}\right)\right),

(11)

where
R>0
.
We also highlight that
\partial^{2}\mathrm{GELU}(0)=\sqrt{2/\pi}
.
Using Taylor expansion it can be shown for any
x\in[-C,C]
that

\displaystyle\left|\varphi_{sq}(x)-x^{2}\right|=\frac{|x|^{3}\cdot|4\partial^{3}\mathrm{GELU}(\xi)-\partial^{3}\mathrm{GELU}(\zeta)|}{3R\cdot\partial^{2}\mathrm{GELU}(0)}\leqslant\frac{5C^{3}|\mathrm{GELU}|_{W^{3,\infty}(\mathbb{R})}}{3R\cdot\partial^{2}\mathrm{GELU}(0)},

and similarly for the derivatives

\displaystyle\left|\partial^{1}\varphi_{sq}(x)-2x\right|=\frac{2|x|^{2}\cdot|2\partial^{3}\mathrm{GELU}(\widetilde{\xi})-\partial^{3}\mathrm{GELU}(\widetilde{\zeta})|}{R\cdot|\partial^{2}\mathrm{GELU}(0)|}\leqslant\frac{6C^{2}|\mathrm{GELU}|_{W^{3,\infty}(\mathbb{R})}}{R\cdot\partial^{2}\mathrm{GELU}(0)}

and the second derivatives

\displaystyle|\partial^{2}\varphi_{sq}(x)-2|=\frac{|x|\cdot|8\partial^{3}\mathrm{GELU}(\xi^{\circ})-2\partial^{3}\mathrm{GELU}(\zeta^{\circ})|}{R\cdot\partial^{2}\mathrm{GELU}(0)}\leqslant\frac{10C|\mathrm{GELU}|_{W^{3,\infty}(\mathbb{R})}}{R\cdot\partial^{2}\mathrm{GELU}(0)},

where
\xi,\widetilde{\xi},\xi^{\circ}
and
\zeta,\widetilde{\zeta},\zeta^{\circ}
are all within the interval defined by the origin and
x
.
To proceed, we derive an explicit bound for the derivatives of order
k
with
k\geqslant 3
and
x\in\mathbb{R}
as

\displaystyle|\partial^{k}\varphi_{sq}(x)|=\frac{1}{R^{k-2}\partial^{2}\mathrm{GELU}(0)}\left|2^{k}\partial^{k}\mathrm{GELU}\left(\frac{2x}{R}\right)-2\cdot\partial^{k}\mathrm{GELU}\left(\frac{x}{R}\right)\right|\leqslant\frac{(2^{k}+2)|\mathrm{GELU}|_{W^{k,\infty}(\mathbb{R})}}{R^{k-2}\partial^{2}\mathrm{GELU}(0)}.

Therefore, from Lemma
B.2
we find that

\displaystyle|\partial^{k}\varphi_{sq}(x)|\leqslant\frac{(k+1)(2^{k}+2)}{R^{k-2}\partial^{2}\mathrm{GELU}(0)}\sqrt{\frac{(k-2)!}{2\pi}}\leqslant\frac{2^{k+2}k}{R^{k-2}\partial^{2}\mathrm{GELU}(0)}\sqrt{\frac{(k-2)!}{2\pi}}.

Hence, setting

\displaystyle R=\frac{10|\mathrm{GELU}|_{W^{3,\infty}(\mathbb{R})}}{\partial^{2}\mathrm{GELU}(0)\cdot\varepsilon}\vee\max_{3\leqslant k\leqslant m}\left(\frac{2^{k+2}k}{\partial^{2}\mathrm{GELU}(0)}\sqrt{\frac{(k-2)!}{2\pi}}\right)^{1/(k-2)},

we ensure that for any
C\geqslant 1

\displaystyle\max_{0\leqslant k\leqslant 2}|\varphi_{sq}-f_{sq}|_{W^{k,\infty}([-C,C])}\leqslant C^{3-k}\varepsilon,\quad\max_{3\leqslant k\leqslant(m\vee 3)}|\varphi_{sq}-f_{sq}|_{W^{k,\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\leqslant\varepsilon.

This observation yields

\displaystyle\|\varphi_{sq}-f_{sq}\|_{W^{m,\infty}([-C,C])}\leqslant C^{3}\varepsilon,\quad\text{for all }C\geqslant 1.

The definition of
\varphi_{sq}
given in (
11
) suggests that
L=\|W\|_{\infty}=2
,
S=6
and

\displaystyle\log B\lesssim\log(R^{2}\vee R^{-1}\vee 2)\lesssim\log(1/\varepsilon)+\log m,

where the last inequality uses Stirling’s approximation.
The proof is finished.

∎

Comparing our result from Lemma
3.6
to that presented in
(
Gühring and Raslan 2021
, Proposition 4.7)
, we observe that we provide approximation guarantees for the entire real line, rather than limiting our results to a specific segment.
This is a key advantage for approximating functions on unbounded domains.
Subsequently, we derive a straightforward corollary that provides an approximation error bound for the multiplication of two numbers.

Corollary 3.7

(approximation of two number multiplication)
.

Define
\mathrm{prod}_{2}:(x,y)\mapsto x\cdot y
, and let
m\in\mathbb{N}
be arbitrary.
Then, for any
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
, there exists
\varphi_{mul}\in\mathsf{NN}(L,W,S,B)
satisfying

\displaystyle\|\varphi_{mul}-\mathrm{prod}_{2}\|_{W^{m,\infty}([-C,C]^{2})}\leqslant C^{3}\varepsilon,\quad\text{for all }C\geqslant 1.

In addition,
L=2
,
\|W\|_{\infty}\leqslant 4
,
S\leqslant 12
and
\log B\lesssim\log(1/\varepsilon)+\log m
.

The proof of Corollary
3.7
can be found in Appendix
A.2
.
Having derived the approximation guarantees for multiplication, we now turn to the approximation of multiple number multiplications, as outlined in the following lemma.

Lemma 3.8

(approximating the multiplication of
d
numbers)
.

Let
d,m\in\mathbb{N}
with
d\geqslant 2
be arbitrary, and define the function
\mathrm{prod}_{d}:(x_{1},\dots,x_{d})\mapsto\prod_{i=1}^{d}x_{i}
.
Then, for every
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
and every
K\geqslant 1
, there exists
\varphi_{mul,d}\in\mathsf{NN}(L,W,S,B)
such that

\displaystyle(i)

\displaystyle\quad\|\varphi_{mul,d}-\mathrm{prod}_{d}\|_{W^{m,\infty}([-K,K]^{d})}\leqslant\varepsilon,

\displaystyle(ii)

\displaystyle\quad\|\varphi_{mul,d}\|_{W^{m,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leqslant\exp\{{\mathcal{O}}((m^{2}+d)\log(mdK\log(1/\varepsilon)))\}.

In addition,

\displaystyle L\lesssim\log d,\quad\|W\|_{\infty}\vee S\lesssim d^{2},\quad\log B\lesssim(\log(1/\varepsilon)+(d+m)\log K+m^{2}d^{2})\log d.

Proof.

To improve readability, the proof is divided into several steps.

Step 1: approximation error analysis.
We first prove the statement for
K=1
and then generalize it to any arbitrary
K\geqslant 1
.
Overall, the resulting neural network is structured as a binary tree, in accordance with the methodology described in
Schwab and Zech 2019
.
We build an approximation of multiplication of
2^{J}
numbers with
J=\lceil\log_{2}d\rceil
.
If
d<2^{J}
, then a minor modification of the input layer implements a concatenation of the input vector with the vector of ones of length at most
d
.
Now let

\displaystyle\varphi_{j}(x_{1:2^{j}})=\varphi_{mul,j}\left(\varphi_{j-1,1}(x_{1:2^{j-1}}),\varphi_{j-1,2}(x_{2^{j-1}+1:2^{j}})\right),\quad 1\leqslant j\leqslant J,

(12)

where
\varphi_{mul,j}
is the neural network from Corollary
3.7
with accuracy parameter
\varepsilon_{mul}^{(j)}
and the smoothness parameter
m
,
\varphi_{j-1,1}
and
\varphi_{j-1,2}
are identical copies of
\varphi_{j-1}
, and
\varphi_{0}
represents the identity mapping.
From Corollary
3.7
we deduce that

\displaystyle\|\varphi_{mul,j}-\mathrm{prod}_{2}\|_{W^{m,\infty}}([-C,C]^{2})\leqslant C^{3}\varepsilon_{mul}^{(j)}\quad\text{for all }C\geqslant 1\text{ and }1\leqslant j\leqslant J.

(13)

To simplify the notation, we let
\varphi_{j}(x_{1:2^{j}})=(\varphi_{mul,j}\circ(\varphi_{j-1,1},\varphi_{j-1,2}))(x_{1:2^{j}})
.
Now assume that for all
0\leqslant j\leqslant J
and
C\geqslant 1
, it holds that

\displaystyle\|\varphi_{j}-\mathrm{prod}_{2^{j}}\|_{W^{m,\infty}(\Omega_{j})}=\varepsilon_{j},

(14)

where
\Omega_{j}=[-1,1]^{2^{j}}
.
Hence, for any
1\leqslant j\leqslant J
, the triangle inequality suggests that

\displaystyle\|\varphi_{j}-\mathrm{prod}_{2^{j}}\|_{W^{m,\infty}(\Omega_{j})}

\displaystyle\quad=\|\varphi_{mul,j}\circ(\varphi_{j-1,1},\varphi_{j-1,2})-\mathrm{prod}_{2^{j}}\|_{W^{m,\infty}(\Omega_{j})}

\displaystyle\quad\leqslant\|(\varphi_{mul,j}-\mathrm{prod}_{2})\circ(\varphi_{j-1,1},\varphi_{j-1,2})\|_{W^{m,\infty}(\Omega_{j})}+\|\varphi_{j-1,1}\cdot\varphi_{j-1,2}-\mathrm{prod}_{2^{j}}\|_{W^{m,\infty}(\Omega_{j})}.

(15)

As for the first term of (
3.2
), we apply Lemma
B.4
and arrive at

\displaystyle\|(\varphi_{mul,j}-\mathrm{prod}_{2})\circ(\varphi_{j-1,1},\varphi_{j-1,2})\|_{W^{m,\infty}(\Omega_{j})}

\displaystyle\quad\leqslant 16(e^{2}m^{4}\cdot 2\cdot 4^{j-1})^{m}\|\varphi_{mul,j}-\mathrm{prod}_{2}\|_{W^{m,\infty}([-1-\varepsilon_{j-1},1+\varepsilon_{j-1}]^{2})}(1\vee\|\varphi_{j-1,1}\|^{m}_{W^{m,\infty}(\Omega_{j-1})})

\displaystyle\quad\leqslant 16(e^{2}m^{4}\cdot 2\cdot 4^{j-1})^{m}(1+\varepsilon_{j-1})^{m+3}\varepsilon_{mul}^{(j)},

(16)

where the last inequality uses (
13
).
As for the second term of (
3.2
), we apply Lemma
B.3
and obtain that

\displaystyle\|\varphi_{j-1,1}\cdot\varphi_{j-1,2}-\mathrm{prod}_{2^{j}}\|_{W^{m,\infty}(\Omega_{j})}

\displaystyle\leqslant 2^{m}\|\varphi_{j-1,1}-\mathrm{prod}_{2^{j-1}}\|_{W^{m,\infty}(\Omega_{j-1})}\|\varphi_{j-1,2}\|_{W^{m,\infty}(\Omega_{j-1})}

\displaystyle\quad+2^{m}\|\mathrm{prod}_{2^{j-1}}\|_{W^{m,\infty}(\Omega_{j-1})}\|\varphi_{j-1,2}-\mathrm{prod}_{2^{j-1}}\|_{W^{m,\infty}(\Omega_{j-1})}.

From (
14
) we deduce that

\displaystyle\|\varphi_{j-1,1}\cdot\varphi_{j-1,2}-\mathrm{prod}_{2^{j}}\|_{W^{m,\infty}(\Omega_{j})}\leqslant 2^{m+1}\varepsilon_{j-1}(1+\varepsilon_{j-1}).

(17)

Therefore, combining (
3.2
), (
3.2
) and (
17
), we arrive at

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{j}\leqslant 16(e^{2}m^{4}\cdot 2\cdot 4^{j-1})^{m}(1+\varepsilon_{j-1})^{m+3}\varepsilon_{mul}^{(j)}+2^{m+1}\varepsilon_{j-1}(1+\varepsilon_{j-1}).

We find
\varepsilon_{j}
in the form of
\varepsilon_{j}=2^{\gamma_{j}}\varepsilon_{1}
for each
1\leqslant j\leqslant J
with
\gamma_{1}=0
.
Hence,

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{j}\leqslant 16(e^{2}m^{4}\cdot 2\cdot 4^{j-1})^{m}2^{(m+1)(\gamma_{j-1}+1)}\varepsilon_{mul}^{(j)}+2^{m+1}2^{2\gamma_{j-1}+1}\varepsilon_{1}.

Setting

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{mul}^{(j)}=\varepsilon_{1}\left(16(e^{2}m^{4}\cdot 2\cdot 4^{j-1})^{m}2^{(m+1)(\gamma_{j-1}+1)}\right)^{-1},\quad 2\leqslant j\leqslant J,

(18)

we have that

\displaystyle\gamma_{j}\leqslant m+3+2\gamma_{j-1},\quad 2\leqslant j\leqslant J,

which yields that

\displaystyle\gamma_{j}\leqslant(m+3)4^{j},\quad 1\leqslant j\leqslant J.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{J}\leqslant(m+3)2^{2\lceil\log_{2}d\rceil}\varepsilon_{1}\leqslant 4(m+3)d^{2}\varepsilon_{1}.

Choosing
\varepsilon_{mul}^{(1)}=\varepsilon(4(m+3)d^{2})^{-1}\in(0,1)
ensures that

\displaystyle\|\varphi_{J}-\mathrm{prod}_{2^{J}}\|_{W^{m,\infty}([-C,C])}\leqslant\varepsilon.

Step 2: deriving the configuration of
\varphi_{J}
.
Due to the observation that
\gamma_{j}\lesssim md^{2}
, we deduce from Corollary
3.7
and (
18
) that, for all
1\leqslant j\leqslant J
, we have
\varphi_{mul,j}\in\mathsf{NN}(L_{mul},W_{mul},S_{mul},B_{mul})
with

\displaystyle L_{mul}=2,\quad\|W_{mul}\|_{\infty}\vee S_{mul}\lesssim 1,\quad\log B_{mul}\lesssim\log(1/\varepsilon)+m^{2}d^{2}.

Let
\varphi_{j}\in\mathsf{NN}(L_{j},W_{j},S_{j},B_{j})
for all
1\leqslant j\leqslant J
.
Then, from Lemma
B.5
and Lemma
B.6
we find that

\displaystyle L_{J}\leqslant J+1,\quad\|W_{J}\|_{\infty}\vee S_{J}\lesssim 2^{J-1},

\displaystyle\log B_{J}\leqslant\log B_{J-1}+\log B_{mul}+\log\|W_{J-1}\|_{\infty}\lesssim(\log(1/\varepsilon)+m^{2}d^{2})\log d.

(19)

We now generalize the approximation result to the case when
K\geqslant 1
.
Let

\displaystyle\varphi_{J,K}(x_{1},\dots,x_{d})=K^{d}\varphi_{J}(x_{1}/K,\dots,x_{d}/K).

Then, from the chain rule we obtain that

\displaystyle\|\varphi_{J,K}-\mathrm{prod}_{d}\|_{W^{m,\infty}([-K,K]^{d})}\leqslant K^{d}\|\varphi_{J}-\mathrm{prod}_{d}\|_{W^{m,\infty}([-1,1]^{d})}\leqslant K^{d}\varepsilon.

Therefore, taking the accuracy parameter
\varepsilon/K^{d}
in
\varphi_{J,K}
, we deduce that for any
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
there exists
\widetilde{\varphi}_{mul,d,K}\in\mathsf{NN}(\widetilde{L},\widetilde{W},\widetilde{S},\widetilde{B})
satisfying

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{\varphi}_{mul,d,K}-\mathrm{prod}_{d}\|_{W^{m,\infty}([-K,K]^{d})}\leqslant\varepsilon.

Furthermore, (
3.2
) we find that

\displaystyle\widetilde{L}\lesssim\log d,\quad\|\widetilde{W}\|_{\infty}\vee\widetilde{S}\lesssim d,\quad\log\widetilde{B}\lesssim(\log(1/\varepsilon)+d\log K+m^{2}d^{2})\log d.

(20)

Step 3: clipping the input.
Now let
\varphi_{clip}
be a clipping operation approximation from Lemma
3.5
formulated with accuracy parameter
\varepsilon_{clip}\in(0,1)
and clipping parameter
K
.
Then, it holds that
\|\varphi_{clip}\|_{W^{0,\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\leqslant K+5/2\leqslant 4K
.
Let
\varphi_{clip,d}
be a parallel stacking of
d
identical copies of
\varphi_{clip}
that approximates a component-wise clipping.
Let also
\widetilde{\varphi}_{mul,d,4K}
has accuracy parameter
\varepsilon_{mul,d}
and smoothness parameter
m+1
.
Then, it holds that

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{\varphi}_{mul,d,4K}\circ\varphi_{clip,d}-\mathrm{prod}_{d}\circ\mathrm{id}\|_{W^{m,\infty}([-K,K]^{d})}

\displaystyle\quad\leqslant\|(\widetilde{\varphi}_{mul,d,4K}-\mathrm{prod}_{d})\circ\varphi_{clip,d}\|_{W^{m,\infty}([-K,K]^{d})}+\|\mathrm{prod}_{d}\circ\varphi_{clip,d}-\mathrm{prod}_{d}\circ\mathrm{id}\|_{W^{m,\infty}([-K,K]^{d})}.

Lemma
B.4
suggests that

\displaystyle\|(\widetilde{\varphi}_{mul,d,4K}-\mathrm{prod}_{d})\circ\varphi_{clip,d}\|_{W^{m,\infty}([-K,K]^{d})}

\displaystyle\leqslant\exp\{{\mathcal{O}}(m\log(md))\}\varepsilon_{mul,d}(\varepsilon_{clip}+K)^{m}

\displaystyle\leqslant\exp\{{\mathcal{O}}(m\log(mdK))\}\varepsilon_{mul,d}

and also

\displaystyle\|\mathrm{prod}_{d}\circ\varphi_{clip,d}-\mathrm{prod}_{d}\circ\mathrm{id}\|_{W^{m,\infty}([-K,K]^{d})}

\displaystyle\leqslant\exp\{{\mathcal{O}}(m\log(md))\}(K+5/2)^{d}\varepsilon_{clip}(\varepsilon_{clip}+K)^{2m}

\displaystyle\leqslant\exp\{{\mathcal{O}}(m\log(mdK)+d\log K)\}\varepsilon_{clip}.

Therefore, setting

\displaystyle\log(1/\varepsilon_{mul,d})\asymp\log(1/\varepsilon)+m\log(mdK),\quad\log(1/\varepsilon_{clip})\asymp\log(1/\varepsilon)+m\log(mdK)+d\log K

(21)

for some
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
ensures that

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{\varphi}_{mul,d,4K}\circ\varphi_{clip,d}-\mathrm{prod}_{d}\circ\mathrm{id}\|_{W^{m,\infty}([-K,K]^{d})}\leqslant\varepsilon.

Moreover, Lemma
3.5
and Lemma
B.4
imply that

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{\varphi}_{mul,d,4K}\circ\varphi_{clip,d}\|_{W^{m,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle\leqslant\exp\{{\mathcal{O}}(m\log(md))\}(\varepsilon_{mul,d}+(4K)^{d})(1\vee\|\varphi_{clip,d}\|_{W^{m,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{m})

\displaystyle\leqslant\exp\{{\mathcal{O}}((m^{2}+d)\log(mdK\log(1/\varepsilon)))\},

where the last inequality uses (
21
).
Recall that due to Lemma
3.5
, Lemma
B.6
and (
21
), we have that
\varphi_{clip,d}\in\mathsf{NN}(L_{clip},W_{clip},S_{clip},B_{clip})
with

\displaystyle L_{clip}\lesssim 1,\quad\|W_{clip}\|_{\infty}\vee S_{clip}\lesssim d,\quad\log B_{clip}\lesssim\log(1/\varepsilon)+m\log(mdK)+d\log K.

Finally, from Lemma
B.5
, (
20
) and (
21
) we deduce that
\varphi_{mul,d}=\widetilde{\varphi}_{mul,d,4K}\circ\varphi_{clip,d}
has

\displaystyle L\lesssim\log d,\quad\|W\|_{\infty}\vee S\lesssim d^{2},\quad\log B\lesssim(\log(1/\varepsilon)+(d+m)\log K+m^{2}d^{2})\log d.

The proof is complete.

∎

Comparing our result from Lemma
3.8
to that presented in
(
De Ryck et al. 2021
, Corollary 3.8)
, we observe a difference in the number of parameters:
\mathcal{O}(d^{2})
versus
\mathcal{O}(d\log d)
.
We emphasize that, as a byproduct, we derived a neural network with the number of parameters
{\mathcal{O}}(d)
, but we employed clipping and concatenation to satisfy condition
(ii)
, which ultimately increased the parameter count.
However, by adding clipping, we ensure that the approximation and its derivatives are bounded across the entire real line.
Now, we turn to the approximation of monomials, as formulated in the following lemma.

Corollary 3.9

(approximation of monomials)
.

Let
{\mathbf{k}}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{I}
for some
I\in\mathbb{N}
such that
|{\mathbf{k}}|=d
, where
d\in\mathbb{N}
with
d\geqslant 2
is arbitrary.
Define
\mathrm{prod}_{\mathbf{k}}:(x_{1},\dots,x_{I})\mapsto\prod_{i=1}^{I}x_{i}^{k_{i}}
.
Then, for every
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
, every
m\in\mathbb{N}
, and every
K\geqslant 1
, there exists a GELU network
\varphi_{mul,{\mathbf{k}}}\in\mathsf{NN}(L,W,S,B)
such that

\displaystyle(i)

\displaystyle\quad\|\varphi_{mul,{\mathbf{k}}}-\mathrm{prod}_{\mathbf{k}}\|_{W^{m,\infty}([-K,K]^{I})}\leqslant\varepsilon,

\displaystyle(ii)

\displaystyle\quad\|\varphi_{mul,{\mathbf{k}}}\|_{W^{m,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{I})}\leqslant\exp\{{\mathcal{O}}((m^{2}+d)\log(mdK\log(1/\varepsilon)))\}.

In addition,

\displaystyle L\lesssim\log d,\quad\|W\|_{\infty}\vee S\lesssim(d\vee I)^{3},\quad\log B\lesssim(\log(1/\varepsilon)+(d+m)\log K+m^{2}d^{2})\log d+\log I.

We move the proof of Corollary

.
The following lemma provides an approximation result for multivariate polynomials.

Lemma 3.10

(approximation of multivariate polynomials)
.

Corollary

3.3
Approximation of the exponent and the division

Now, we address the approximation of nonlinear operations, including exponentiation and division.
The following lemma provides quantitative bounds for the exponential function approximation.

Lemma 3.11

(approximation of the exponential function)
.

Define
f_{exp}:x\mapsto e^{-x}
, and let
m\in\mathbb{N}
be arbitrary.
Then, for any
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
and
0\leqslant A\leqslant 1
, there exists a neural network
\varphi_{exp}\in\mathsf{NN}(L,W,S,B)
such that

\displaystyle(i)

\displaystyle\quad\|\varphi_{exp}-f_{exp}\|_{W^{m,\infty}([-A,+\infty))}\leqslant\varepsilon,

\displaystyle(ii)

\displaystyle\quad\|\varphi_{exp}\|_{W^{m,\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\leqslant\exp\{{\mathcal{O}}(m^{2}\log(m\log(1/\varepsilon)))\}.

Furthermore,

\displaystyle L\lesssim\log m+\log\log(1/\varepsilon),\quad\|W\|_{\infty}\vee S\lesssim m^{12}\log^{4}(1/\varepsilon),\quad\log B\lesssim m^{11}\log^{3}(1/\varepsilon).

Proof.

For some
r\in\mathbb{N}
with
r\geqslant 3
and
K\geqslant 2
, which will be determined later, consider the approximation accuracy of a Tailor expansion

f_{r}(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{r-1}\frac{(-1)^{i}x^{i}}{i!}

\displaystyle\|f_{exp}-f_{r}\|_{W^{m,\infty}([-4A,4K])}\leqslant\max_{0\leqslant m^{\prime}\leqslant m}\left(\frac{e^{4A}(4K)^{r-m^{\prime}}}{(r-m^{\prime})!}\right)\leqslant e^{4A}\max_{0\leqslant m^{\prime}\leqslant m}\left(\frac{4e(K\vee A)}{r-m^{\prime}}\right)^{r-m^{\prime}},

where in the last inequality we used Stirling’s approximation for the factorial.
Thus, setting

\displaystyle r=\lceil m+4Ke^{2}+4A+\log(2/\varepsilon_{0})\rceil\geqslant 3,

where
\varepsilon_{0}\in(0,1)
and will be optimized further in the proof.
Next, we obtain that

\displaystyle\|f_{exp}-f_{r}\|_{W^{m,\infty}([-4A,4K])}\leqslant\varepsilon_{0}/2.

(31)

Now Lemma
3.10
suggests that there exists a GELU network
\widetilde{\varphi}_{exp}
formulated with the accuracy parameter
\varepsilon_{0}/2
, the scaling parameter
4K
, the smoothness parameter
m+1
, and the maximum power of the monomial
r-1
such that

\displaystyle\|f_{r}-\widetilde{\varphi}_{exp}\|_{W^{m,\infty}([-4A,4K])}\leqslant\|f_{r}-\widetilde{\varphi}_{exp}\|_{W^{m,\infty}([-4K,4K])}\leqslant\varepsilon_{0}/2,

which together with (
31
) immediately implies that

\displaystyle\|f_{exp}-\widetilde{\varphi}_{exp}\|_{W^{m,\infty}([-4A,4K])}\leqslant\|f_{exp}-f_{r}\|_{W^{m,\infty}([-4A,4K])}+\|f_{r}-\widetilde{\varphi}_{exp}\|_{W^{m,\infty}([-4A,4K])}\leqslant\varepsilon_{0}.

(32)

In addition, (
32
) suggests that

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{\varphi}_{exp}\|_{W^{m,\infty}([-4A,4K])}

\displaystyle\leqslant\|\widetilde{\varphi}_{exp}-f_{exp}\|_{W^{m,\infty}([-4A,4K])}+\|f_{exp}\|_{W^{m,\infty}([-4A,4K])}\leqslant 2e^{4A}.

(33)

Next, substituting the choice of
r
into the configuration of
\widetilde{\varphi}_{exp}\in\mathsf{NN}(\widetilde{L},\widetilde{W},\widetilde{S},\widetilde{B})
outlined in Lemma
3.10
yields

\displaystyle\widetilde{L}\lesssim\log r\lesssim\log(m+K+\log(1/\varepsilon_{0})),\quad\|\widetilde{W}\|_{\infty}\vee\widetilde{S}\lesssim r^{4}\lesssim m^{4}+K^{4}+\log^{4}(1/\varepsilon_{0}),

\displaystyle\log\widetilde{B}\lesssim(\log(1/\varepsilon_{0})+m^{2}r\log(mrK)+m^{2}r^{2})\log r\lesssim m^{2}(m^{3}+K^{3}+\log^{3}(1/\varepsilon_{0})).

(34)

Let
\varphi_{clip}
be an approximation of clipping operation from Lemma
3.5
with the accuracy parameter
\varepsilon_{clip}\in(0,1)
and the scale parameter
(A+K)/2\geqslant 1
.
Then we have that

\displaystyle\varphi_{clip,-A,K}(x)=\varphi_{clip}(x+(A-K)/2)+(K-A)/2

has the following properties:

\displaystyle(i)

\displaystyle\quad\|\varphi_{clip,-A,K}-\mathrm{id}\|_{W^{m,\infty}([-A,K])}\leqslant\|\varphi_{clip}-\mathrm{id}\|_{W^{m,\infty}([-(A+K)/2,(A+K)/2])}\leqslant\varepsilon_{clip},

\displaystyle(ii)

\displaystyle\quad-5/2-A\leqslant\varphi_{clip,-A,K}(x)\leqslant K+5/2,\quad\text{for all }x\in\mathbb{R}

\displaystyle(iii)

\displaystyle\quad\|\varphi_{clip,-A,K}-K-1/2\|_{W^{0,\infty}([K,+\infty))}\leqslant\varepsilon_{clip}+1,

\displaystyle(iv)

\displaystyle\quad\|\varphi_{clip,-A,K}\|_{W^{m,\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\leqslant\exp\{{\mathcal{O}}(m\log(m\log(1/\varepsilon_{clip}))+\log(A+K))\}.

Hence, from properties
(i)
,
(ii)
and Lemma
B.4
we obtain for
\varphi_{exp}=\widetilde{\varphi}_{exp}\circ\varphi_{clip,-A,K}
that

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{\varphi}_{exp}\circ\varphi_{clip,-A,K}-\widetilde{\varphi}_{exp}\|_{W^{m,\infty}([-A,K])}\leqslant\exp\{{\mathcal{O}}(m\log(mK))\}\|\widetilde{\varphi}_{exp}\|_{W^{m+1,\infty}([-4A,4K])}\varepsilon_{clip}.

From (

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{\varphi}_{exp}\circ\varphi_{clip,-A,K}-\widetilde{\varphi}_{exp}\|_{W^{m,\infty}([-A,K])}\leqslant\exp\{{\mathcal{O}}(m\log(mK))\}\varepsilon_{clip}.

Thus, (

\displaystyle\log(1/\varepsilon_{clip})\asymp\log(1/\varepsilon_{0})+m\log mK

\displaystyle\|\varphi_{exp}-f_{exp}\|_{W^{m,\infty}([-A,K])}\leqslant 2\varepsilon_{0}.

Property

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{\varphi}_{exp}\circ\varphi_{clip,-A,K}\|_{W^{m,\infty}([K,+\infty))}

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{\varphi}_{exp}\circ\varphi_{clip,-A,K}\|_{W^{m,\infty}([K,+\infty))}\leqslant\exp\{{\mathcal{O}}(m^{2}\log(m\log(1/\varepsilon_{0})))\}\varepsilon_{0}.

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{\varphi}_{exp}\circ\varphi_{clip,-A,K}-f_{exp}\|_{W^{m,\infty}([K,+\infty))}\leqslant\exp\{{\mathcal{O}}(m^{2}\log(m\log(1/\varepsilon_{0})))\}\varepsilon_{0}.

\displaystyle\|\varphi_{exp}-f_{exp}\|_{W^{m,\infty}([-A,+\infty))}\leqslant\exp\{{\mathcal{O}}(m^{2}\log(m\log(1/\varepsilon_{0})))\}\varepsilon_{0}.

\displaystyle\log(1/\varepsilon_{0})\asymp m^{2}\log m+m^{3}\log(1/\varepsilon)

\displaystyle\|\varphi_{exp}-f_{exp}\|_{W^{m,\infty}([-A,+\infty))}\leqslant\varepsilon.

Moreover, properties

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{\varphi}_{exp}\circ\varphi_{clip,-A,K}\|_{W^{m,\infty}(\mathbb{R})}

\displaystyle\leqslant\|\widetilde{\varphi}_{exp}\|_{W^{m,\infty}([-4A,4K])}\exp\{{\mathcal{O}}(m^{2}\log(m\log(1/\varepsilon_{clip}))+m\log(2K))\}

\displaystyle\leqslant\exp\{{\mathcal{O}}(m^{2}\log(m\log(1/\varepsilon)))\}.

From (

) and Lemma

\varphi_{clip}\in\mathsf{NN}(L_{clip},W_{clip},S_{clip},B_{clip})

\displaystyle L_{clip}\vee\|W_{clip}\|_{\infty}\vee S_{clip}\lesssim 1,\quad\log B_{clip}\lesssim\log(mK/\varepsilon_{clip})\lesssim m^{2}\log m+m^{3}\log(1/\varepsilon).

\displaystyle L\lesssim\log m+\log\log(1/\varepsilon),\quad\|W\|_{\infty}\vee S\lesssim m^{12}\log^{4}(1/\varepsilon),\quad\log B\lesssim m^{11}\log^{3}(1/\varepsilon).

Lemma 3.13

(reciprocal function approximation)
.

Define
f_{rec}:x\mapsto 1/x
for any
x>0
.
Let also
a_{0}=2^{-N}
for some
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that
N\geqslant 3
.
Then, for every
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
and every
m\in\mathbb{N}
with
m\geqslant 3
, there exists a GELU network
\varphi_{rec}\in\mathsf{NN}(L,W,S,B)
such that

\displaystyle(i)

\displaystyle\quad\|\varphi_{rec}-f_{rec}\|_{W^{m,\infty}([a_{0},1])}\leqslant\varepsilon,

\displaystyle(ii)

\displaystyle\quad\|\varphi_{rec}\|_{W^{m,\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\leqslant\exp\{{\mathcal{O}}(m^{3}N+m^{3}\log(m\log(1/\varepsilon)))\}.

In addition, the network has

\displaystyle L\lesssim\log(mN\log(1/\varepsilon)),\quad\|W\|_{\infty}\vee S\lesssim m^{8}N(N^{4}+m^{4}\log^{4}(1/\varepsilon)),

\displaystyle\log B\lesssim m^{8}(N^{4}+m^{4}\log^{4}(1/\varepsilon)).

Proof.

The proof proceeds in multiple steps.

Step 1: introducing basic approximators.
Let
N\in\mathbb{N}
with
N\geqslant 3
and let
a_{i}=2^{-N+i}
for each
i\in\{-1,1,\dots,N+1\}
.
Let also
\varphi_{rec}
be in the following form:

\displaystyle\varphi_{rec}(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{N}q(\varphi_{i},\psi_{i}),\quad\varphi_{i}=\varphi_{id,i}\circ\varphi_{rec,i},\;\psi_{i}=\psi_{id,i}\circ\psi_{pou,i},

(38)

where
q
is a GELU network from Corollary
3.7
, which approximates multiplication with accuracy parameter
\varepsilon_{mul}
.
Networks
\{\psi_{pou,i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
form a partition of unity according to Lemma
3.4
with accuracy
\varepsilon_{pou}
.
In addition, the networks
\{\varphi_{rec,i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
serve as local approximators of the reciprocal function from Lemma
3.12
with the accuracy
\varepsilon_{rec}/2
and the parameters
a=a_{i-2}
and
b=a_{i+1}\wedge 1
.
Hence, we have that

\displaystyle\max_{1\leqslant i\leqslant N}\|\varphi_{rec,i}-f_{rec}\|_{W^{m,\infty}([a_{i-2},a_{i+1}\wedge 1])}\leqslant\varepsilon_{rec}/2.

(39)

This implies that

\displaystyle\|\varphi_{rec,i}\|_{W^{m,\infty}([a_{i-2},a_{i+1}\wedge 1])}\leqslant\varepsilon_{rec}+\exp\{{\mathcal{O}}(m\log(m/a_{0}))\}\leqslant\exp\{{\mathcal{O}}(mN+m\log m)\}

(40)

and also

\displaystyle\|\varphi_{rec,i}\|_{W^{0,\infty}([a_{i-2},a_{i+1}\wedge 1])}\leqslant\varepsilon_{rec}+1/a_{-1}\leqslant 4/a_{0}.

(41)

The networks
\{\varphi_{id,i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
approximate the identity operation (see Lemma
3.2
) with the accuracy parameter
\varepsilon_{id,\varphi}\in(0,1)
, the scale parameter
4/a_{0}
.
Similarly, the networks
\{\psi_{id,i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
aim to approximate the identity operation with the accuracy parameter
\varepsilon_{id,\psi}\in(0,1)
and the scale parameter
\|\psi_{pou,i}\|_{W^{m,\infty}(\mathbb{R})}
.
The number of layers of the identity networks will be specified later in the proof.
We also put the smoothness parameter
m\in\mathbb{N}
with
m\geqslant 3
for all the networks.
First, we derive the approximation accuracy of
\varphi_{i}
.
Note that according to Lemma
3.12
, for all
i\in\{1,\dots,N\}
, we have
\varphi_{rec,i}\in\mathsf{NN}(L_{rec},W_{rec},S_{rec},B_{rec})
with

\displaystyle L_{rec}\lesssim\log(m\log(1/\varepsilon_{rec})),\quad\|W_{rec}\|\vee S_{rec}\lesssim m^{4}\log^{4}(m/\varepsilon_{rec}a_{0})\lesssim m^{4}N^{4}+m^{4}\log^{4}(m/\varepsilon_{rec}),

\displaystyle\log B_{rec}\lesssim m^{4}\log^{4}(m/\varepsilon_{rec}a_{0})\lesssim m^{4}N^{4}+m^{4}\log^{4}(m/\varepsilon_{rec}).

(42)

Therefore, each
\varphi_{id,i}
has at most
L_{id,\varphi}\lesssim\log(m\log(1/\varepsilon_{rec}))
the number of layers.
Now Lemma
3.2
in conjunction with (
40
), (
41

Lemma B.1

(evaluation of Hermite polynomials,
Puchkin et al. 2024
, Appendix D)
.

For any
n\in\mathbb{N}
we define a ”probabilist’s” Hermite polynomial

\displaystyle{\mathcal{H}}_{n}(x)=(-1)^{n}e^{x^{2}/2}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}^{n}}{{\mathrm{d}}x^{n}}e^{-x^{2}/2},\quad x\in\mathbb{R}.

Then it holds that

\displaystyle\max_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\left|{\mathcal{H}}_{n}(x)e^{-x^{2}/4}\right|\leqslant\sqrt{n!}\quad\text{for all }n\in\mathbb{N}.

Lemma B.2

(properties of GELU acitvation function)
.

For any
k\in\mathbb{N}
we have the following bounds for the Sobolev seminorms:

\displaystyle\left|\mathrm{GELU}\right|_{W^{k,\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\leqslant\begin{cases}1+1/\sqrt{2\pi},\quad&k=1,\\
(k+1)\sqrt{\frac{(k-2)!}{2\pi}},\quad&k\geqslant 2\end{cases}

For
k=0
we have that

\displaystyle\|\mathrm{GELU}\|_{W^{0,\infty}([-C,C])}\leqslant C,\quad\text{for all }C>0.

In addition, for any
A\geqslant 0
and
m\in\mathbb{N}
, the tails behave as follows:

\displaystyle\|\mathrm{GELU}-\mathrm{id}\|_{W^{m,\infty}([A,+\infty))}\vee\|\mathrm{GELU}\|_{W^{m,\infty}((-\infty,-A])}\leqslant 2e^{-A^{2}/4}\sqrt{m!}.

Proof.

We first recall that

\displaystyle\mathrm{GELU}(x)=x\cdot\Phi(x),\quad\Phi(x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int\limits_{-\infty}^{x}e^{-t^{2}/2}\,{\mathrm{d}}t,

which immediately implies that
\|\mathrm{GELU}\|_{W^{0,\infty}([-C,C])}\leqslant C
for any
C>0
and

\displaystyle\partial^{1}\mathrm{GELU}(x)=\Phi(x)-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}}{{\mathrm{d}}x}e^{-x^{2}/2}.

(92)

Hence, using Lemma
B.1
together with the observation that
\partial^{k}e^{-x^{2}/2}=(-1)^{k}e^{-x^{2}/2}\,{\mathcal{H}}_{k}(x)
for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
, where

\displaystyle{\mathcal{H}}_{k}(x)=(-1)^{k}e^{x^{2}/2}\,\partial^{k}e^{-x^{2}/2},\quad x\in\mathbb{R},

we obtain that
|\mathrm{GELU}|_{W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\leqslant 1+1/\sqrt{2\pi}
.
Subsequently, from (
92
) we deduce that for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
with
k\geqslant 2

\displaystyle\partial^{k}\mathrm{GELU}(x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\left(\partial^{k-2}(e^{-x^{2}/2})-\partial^{k}(e^{-x^{2}/2})\right).

(93)

Applying Lemma
B.1
, we have that

\displaystyle|\mathrm{GELU}|_{W^{k,\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\leqslant\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\left(\sqrt{(k-2)!}+\sqrt{k!}\right)\leqslant(k+1)\sqrt{\frac{(k-2)!}{2\pi}},

which validates the first claim of the statement.
Now focus on the behavior of tails.
First, consider

\displaystyle\|\mathrm{GELU}-\mathrm{id}\|_{W^{0,\infty}([A,+\infty))}=\sup_{x\geqslant A}x(1-\Phi(x))\leqslant\sup_{x\geqslant A}xe^{-x^{2}/2}\leqslant e^{-A^{2}/4}\sqrt{2}e^{-1/2},

where the penultimate inequality uses Gaussian tails and the last inequality follows from the observation that
xe^{-x^{2}/4}\leqslant\sqrt{2}e^{-1/2}
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
.
Similarly,

\displaystyle\|\mathrm{GELU}\|_{W^{0,\infty}((-\infty,A])}=\sup_{x\leqslant-A}|x\Phi(x)|=\sup_{x\geqslant A}x(1-\Phi(x))\leqslant e^{-A^{2}/4}\sqrt{2}e^{-1/2}.

(94)

As for the derivatives, we have

\displaystyle|\mathrm{GELU}-\mathrm{id}|_{W^{1,\infty}([A,+\infty))}

\displaystyle\leqslant\sup_{x\geqslant A}(1-\Phi(x))+(\sqrt{2\pi})^{-1}\sup_{x\geqslant A}xe^{-x^{2}/2}

\displaystyle\leqslant e^{-A^{2}/2}+(\sqrt{2\pi})^{-1}\sqrt{2}e^{-1/2}e^{-A^{2}/4}

\displaystyle\leqslant 2e^{-A^{2}/4}

and also

\displaystyle|\mathrm{GELU}|_{W^{1,\infty}((-\infty,-A])}

\displaystyle\leqslant\sup_{x\leqslant-A}\Phi(x)+(\sqrt{2\pi})^{-1}\sup_{x\leqslant-A}|xe^{-x^{2}/2}|

\displaystyle\leqslant e^{-A^{2}/2}+(\sqrt{2\pi})^{-1}\sqrt{2}e^{-1/2}e^{-A^{2}/4}

\displaystyle\leqslant 2e^{-A^{2}/4}.

Now for any natural
k\geqslant 2
we have from (
93
) that

\displaystyle|\mathrm{GELU}|_{W^{k,\infty}((-\infty,-A]\cup[A,+\infty))}\leqslant(\sqrt{2\pi})^{-1}\left(\sup_{|x|\geqslant A}|e^{-x^{2}/2}{\mathcal{H}}_{k}(x)|+\sup_{|x|\geqslant A}|e^{-x^{2}/2}{\mathcal{H}}_{k-2}(x)|\right).

Hence, Lemma
B.1
implies that

\displaystyle|\mathrm{GELU}|_{W^{k,\infty}((-\infty,-A]\cup[A,+\infty))}\leqslant\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}e^{-A^{2}/4}\sqrt{k!}.

Combining all together, we have that

\displaystyle\|\mathrm{GELU}-\mathrm{id}\|_{W^{m,\infty}([A,+\infty))}\vee\|\mathrm{GELU}\|_{W^{m,\infty}((-\infty,-A])}\leqslant 2e^{-A^{2}/4}\sqrt{m!}.

The proof is now complete.

and
\widetilde{f}\in C^{n}(\Omega_{1},\Omega_{2})
, then

\displaystyle\|g\circ f-g\circ\widetilde{f}\|_{W^{n,\infty}(\Omega_{1})}

\displaystyle\quad\leqslant 32(e^{2}n^{5}m^{2}d^{2})^{n}\|g\|_{W^{n+1,\infty}(\Omega_{2})}\max_{1\leqslant i\leqslant m}\|(f)_{i}-(\widetilde{f})_{i}\|_{W^{n,\infty}(\Omega_{1})}\left(1\vee\|(f)_{i}\|^{2n}_{W^{n,\infty}(\Omega_{1})}\vee\|(\widetilde{f})_{i}\|^{2n}_{W^{n,\infty}(\Omega_{1})}\right).

Proof.

We reprove Lemma A.7 from
De Ryck et al. 2021
, correcting a minor technical oversight in the original derivation.
Specifically, their bound omits
\max(1,\cdot)
term, which we include here for correctness.
We begin with the multivariate Faà di Bruno formula
[
Constantine and Savits 1996
, Theorem 2.1]
.
For
{\boldsymbol{\nu}}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{d}
with
|{\boldsymbol{\nu}}|=q
for some
q\in\mathbb{N}
with
q\leqslant n
it holds that

\displaystyle\partial^{\boldsymbol{\nu}}(g\circ f)=\sum_{1\leqslant|{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}|\leqslant q}\partial^{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}g\sum_{p({\boldsymbol{\nu}},{\boldsymbol{\lambda}})}({\boldsymbol{\nu}}!)\prod_{j=1}^{q}\frac{(f_{l_{j}})^{k_{j}}}{k_{j}!(l_{j}!)^{k_{j}}},

(95)

where
(f_{\mu})_{i}=\partial^{\boldsymbol{\mu}}f_{i}
for
1\leqslant i\leqslant m
.
In addition,

\displaystyle p({\boldsymbol{\nu}},{\boldsymbol{\lambda}})=\big\{

\displaystyle({\mathbf{k}}_{1},\dots,{\mathbf{k}}_{q};{\mathbf{l}}_{1},\dots,{\mathbf{l}}_{q}):\text{for some }1\leqslant s\leqslant q,

\displaystyle{\mathbf{k}}_{i}=0_{m}\text{ and }{\mathbf{l}}_{i}=0_{d}\text{ for all }1\leqslant i\leqslant q-s;\;|{\mathbf{k}}_{i}|>0\text{ for all }q-s+1\leqslant i\leqslant n;

\displaystyle\text{and }0_{d}\prec{\mathbf{l}}_{q-s+1}\prec\dots\prec{\mathbf{l}}_{q}\text{ are such that}

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}{\mathbf{k}}_{i}={\boldsymbol{\lambda}},\;\sum_{i=1}^{n}|{\mathbf{k}}_{i}|{\mathbf{l}}_{i}={\boldsymbol{\nu}}\big\},

where we write
\boldsymbol{a}\prec\boldsymbol{b}
if either
|\boldsymbol{a}|\leqslant|\boldsymbol{b}|
or
|\boldsymbol{a}|=|\boldsymbol{b}|
and
\boldsymbol{a}_{1}<\boldsymbol{b}_{1}
or
|\boldsymbol{a}|=|\boldsymbol{b}|
and for some
1\leqslant k\leqslant d-1
we have
\boldsymbol{a}_{k+1}<\boldsymbol{b}_{k+1}
with
\boldsymbol{a}_{1}=\boldsymbol{b}_{1},\dots,\boldsymbol{a}_{k}=\boldsymbol{b}_{k}
.
It is evident that in (
95
) we have
\sum_{i=1}^{n}|{\mathbf{k}}_{i}|\leqslant n
and, hence, the number of
({\mathbf{k}}_{1},\dots,{\mathbf{k}}_{n})
satisfying the definition of
p({\boldsymbol{\nu}},{\boldsymbol{\lambda}})
is bounded by
|P_{n,\,nm+1}|
, which is then evaluated as
\sqrt{\pi}e^{n}(mn)^{n}
according to Lemma 2.1 from
De Ryck et al. 2021
.
Similarly, the number of
({\mathbf{l}}_{1},\dots,{\mathbf{l}}_{n})
satisfying the definition of
p({\boldsymbol{\nu}},{\boldsymbol{\lambda}})
is bounded by
|P_{n,dn+1}|
, which in turn, is bounded by
\sqrt{\pi}e^{n}(dn)^{n}
.
This results in

\displaystyle|p({\boldsymbol{\nu}},{\boldsymbol{\lambda}})|\leqslant\pi(e^{2}n^{2}md)^{n}.

(96)

Finally, evaluate

\displaystyle|\{{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{d}\;:\;1\leqslant|{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}|\leqslant q\}|\leqslant|P_{n,\,d+1}|\leqslant\sqrt{\pi}e^{n}d^{n},\quad|\partial^{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}g|\leqslant\|g\|_{W^{n,\infty}(\Omega_{2})},\quad{\boldsymbol{\nu}}!\leqslant n!

(97)

and

\displaystyle\prod_{j=1}^{n}(f_{l_{j}})^{k_{j}}\leqslant 1\vee\max_{1\leqslant i\leqslant m}\|(f)_{i}\|^{n}_{W^{n,\infty}(\Omega_{1})},

(98)

Therefore, Stirling’s approximation implies that

\displaystyle\max_{{\boldsymbol{\nu}}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{d},\;1\leqslant|{\boldsymbol{\nu}}|\leqslant n}\|\partial^{\boldsymbol{\nu}}g\circ f\|_{W^{0,\infty}(\Omega_{1})}

\displaystyle\leqslant\sqrt{\pi}(ed)^{n}\|g\|_{W^{n,\infty}(\Omega_{2})}\pi(e^{2}n^{2}md)^{n}n!(1\vee\max_{1\leqslant i\leqslant m}\|(f)_{i}\|^{n}_{W^{n,\infty}(\Omega_{1})})

\displaystyle\leqslant 16(e^{2}n^{4}md^{2})^{n}\|g\|_{W^{n,\infty}(\Omega_{2})}(1\vee\max_{1\leqslant i\leqslant m}\|(f)_{i}\|^{n}_{W^{n,\infty}(\Omega_{1})}).

For
{\boldsymbol{\nu}}=0_{d}
we have that

\displaystyle\|g\circ f\|_{W^{0,\infty}(\Omega_{1})}\leqslant\|g\|_{W^{m,\infty}(\Omega_{2})}\leqslant 16(e^{2}n^{4}md^{2})^{n}\|g\|_{W^{n,\infty}(\Omega_{2})}(1\vee\max_{1\leqslant i\leqslant m}\|(f)_{i}\|^{n}_{W^{n,\infty}(\Omega_{1})}).

Hence, the first claim holds true.
Now using (
95
), we deduce that

\displaystyle|\partial^{\boldsymbol{\nu}}(g\circ f)-\partial^{\boldsymbol{\nu}}(g\circ\widetilde{f})|

\displaystyle\quad\leqslant\sum_{1\leqslant|{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}|\leqslant q}|\partial^{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}[g]\circ f-\partial^{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}[g]\circ\widetilde{f}|\sum_{p({\boldsymbol{\nu}},{\boldsymbol{\lambda}})}({\boldsymbol{\nu}}!)\prod_{j=1}^{q}\frac{(f_{l_{j}})^{k_{j}}}{k_{j}!(l_{j}!)^{k_{j}}}

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{1\leqslant|{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}|\leqslant q}|\partial^{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}[g]\circ\widetilde{f}|\sum_{p({\boldsymbol{\nu}},{\boldsymbol{\lambda}})}({\boldsymbol{\nu}}!)\frac{|\prod_{j=1}^{q}(f_{l_{j}})^{k_{j}}-\prod_{j=1}^{q}(\widetilde{f}_{l_{j}})^{k_{j}}|}{\prod_{j=1}^{q}k_{j}!(l_{j}!)^{k_{j}}}.

First, bound the first term.
From (
97
) and (
98
) we find that

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leqslant|{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}|\leqslant q}|\partial^{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}[g]\circ f-\partial^{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}[g]\circ\widetilde{f}|\sum_{p({\boldsymbol{\nu}},{\boldsymbol{\lambda}})}({\boldsymbol{\nu}}!)\prod_{j=1}^{q}\frac{(f_{l_{j}})^{k_{j}}}{k_{j}!(l_{j}!)^{k_{j}}}

\displaystyle\quad\leqslant|\{{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{d}\;:\;1\leqslant|{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}|\leqslant q\}|\cdot|p({\boldsymbol{\nu}},{\boldsymbol{\lambda}})|\cdot n!\cdot(1\vee\max_{1\leqslant i\leqslant m}\|(f)_{i}\|^{n}_{W^{n,\infty}(\Omega_{1})})|\partial^{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}[g]\circ f-\partial^{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}[g]\circ\widetilde{f}|.

Now mean value theorem together with (
96
) and (
97
) suggests that

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leqslant|{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}|\leqslant q}|\partial^{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}[g]\circ f-\partial^{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}[g]\circ\widetilde{f}|\sum_{p({\boldsymbol{\nu}},{\boldsymbol{\lambda}})}({\boldsymbol{\nu}}!)\prod_{j=1}^{q}\frac{(f_{l_{j}})^{k_{j}}}{k_{j}!(l_{j}!)^{k_{j}}}

\displaystyle\quad\leqslant 16m(e^{2}n^{4}md^{2})^{n}(1\vee\max_{1\leqslant i\leqslant m}\|(f)_{i}\|^{n}_{W^{n,\infty}(\Omega_{1})})\|g\|_{W^{n+1,\infty}(\Omega_{2})}\max_{1\leqslant i\leqslant m}\|(f)_{i}-(\widetilde{f})_{i}\|_{W^{0,\infty}(\Omega_{1})}.

(99)

Second, evaluate the second term, using (
96
) and (
97
):

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leqslant|{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}|\leqslant q}|\partial^{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}[g]\circ\widetilde{f}|\sum_{p({\boldsymbol{\nu}},{\boldsymbol{\lambda}})}({\boldsymbol{\nu}}!)\frac{|\prod_{j=1}^{q}(f_{l_{j}})^{k_{j}}-\prod_{j=1}^{q}(\widetilde{f}_{l_{j}})^{k_{j}}|}{\prod_{j=1}^{q}k_{j}!(l_{j}!)^{k_{j}}}

\displaystyle\quad\leqslant\sqrt{\pi}e^{n}d^{n}\cdot\pi(e^{2}n^{2}md)^{n}\cdot\|g\|_{W^{n,\infty}(\Omega_{2})}n!\cdot\sum_{j=1}^{q}|(f_{l_{j}})^{k_{j}}-(\widetilde{f}_{l_{j}})^{k_{j}}|\prod_{u<j}|(f_{l_{u}})^{k_{u}}|\prod_{u>j}|(f_{l_{u}})^{k_{u}}|.

Therefore, Stirling’s approximation suggests that

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leqslant|{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}|\leqslant q}|\partial^{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}[g]\circ\widetilde{f}|\sum_{p({\boldsymbol{\nu}},{\boldsymbol{\lambda}})}({\boldsymbol{\nu}}!)\frac{|\prod_{j=1}^{q}(f_{l_{j}})^{k_{j}}-\prod_{j=1}^{q}(\widetilde{f}_{l_{j}})^{k_{j}}|}{\prod_{j=1}^{q}k_{j}!(l_{j}!)^{k_{j}}}

\displaystyle\quad\leqslant 16(e^{2}n^{4}md^{2})^{n}\|g\|_{W^{n,\infty}(\Omega_{2})}\sum_{j=1}^{q}|(f_{l_{j}})^{k_{j}}-(\widetilde{f}_{l_{j}})^{k_{j}}|\max_{1\leqslant i\leqslant m}(1\vee\|(f)_{i}\|^{n}_{W^{n,\infty}(\Omega_{1})}\vee\|(\widetilde{f})_{i}\|^{n}_{W^{n,\infty}(\Omega_{1})}).

We next note that

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{q}|(f_{l_{j}})^{k_{j}}-(\widetilde{f}_{l_{j}})^{k_{j}}|

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{q}\sum_{i=1}^{m}|(f_{l_{j}})_{i}^{(k_{j})_{i}}-(\widetilde{f}_{l_{j}})_{i}^{(k_{j})_{i}}|\prod_{u<i}|(f_{l_{j}})_{u}^{(k_{j})_{u}}|\prod_{u>i}|(\widetilde{f}_{l_{j}})_{u}^{(k_{j})_{u}}|

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{j=1}^{q}|k_{j}|\cdot\max_{1\leqslant i\leqslant m}\|(f)_{i}-(\widetilde{f})_{i}\|_{W^{n,\infty}(\Omega_{1})}(1\vee\|(f)_{i}\|^{n}_{W^{n,\infty}(\Omega_{1})}\vee\|(\widetilde{f})_{i}\|^{n}_{W^{n,\infty}(\Omega_{1})}).

Therefore, we obtain that

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leqslant|{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}|\leqslant q}|\partial^{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}[g]\circ\widetilde{f}|\sum_{p({\boldsymbol{\nu}},{\boldsymbol{\lambda}})}({\boldsymbol{\nu}}!)\frac{|\prod_{j=1}^{q}(f_{l_{j}})^{k_{j}}-\prod_{j=1}^{q}(\widetilde{f}_{l_{j}})^{k_{j}}|}{\prod_{j=1}^{q}k_{j}!(l_{j}!)^{k_{j}}}

\displaystyle\quad\leqslant 16n(e^{2}n^{4}md^{2})^{n}\|g\|_{W^{n,\infty}(\Omega_{2})}\max_{1\leqslant i\leqslant m}\|(f)_{i}-(\widetilde{f})_{i}\|_{W^{n,\infty}(\Omega_{1})}(1\vee\|f_{i}\|^{2n}_{W^{n,\infty}(\Omega_{1})}\vee\|(\widetilde{f})_{i}\|^{2n}_{W^{n,\infty}(\Omega_{1})}).

(100)

For
{\boldsymbol{\nu}}=0_{d}
it holds that

\displaystyle\|g\circ f-g\circ\widetilde{f}\|_{W^{0,\infty}(\Omega_{1})}\leqslant m\|g\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\Omega_{2})}\max_{1\leqslant i\leqslant m}\|(f)_{i}-(\widetilde{f})_{i}\|_{W^{n,\infty}(\Omega_{1})}.

Thus, from (
B
) and (
B
) we conclude that

\displaystyle\|g\circ f-g\circ\widetilde{f}\|_{W^{n,\infty}(\Omega_{1})}

\displaystyle\quad\leqslant 32(e^{2}n^{5}m^{2}d^{2})^{n}\|g\|_{W^{n+1,\infty}(\Omega_{2})}\max_{1\leqslant i\leqslant m}\|(f)_{i}-(\widetilde{f})_{i}\|_{W^{n,\infty}(\Omega_{1})}(1\vee\|(f)_{i}\|^{2n}_{W^{n,\infty}(\Omega_{1})}\vee\|(\widetilde{f})_{i}\|^{2n}_{W^{n,\infty}(\Omega_{1})}).

The proof is complete.

∎

Lemma B.5

(concatenation of neural networks)
.

Given
K\in\mathbb{N}
with
K\geqslant 2
. Then, for any neural networks
\varphi^{(k)}\in\mathsf{NN}(L_{k},W_{k},S_{k},B_{k})
with
1\leqslant k\leqslant K

such that
\varphi^{(k)}:\mathbb{R}^{d_{k}}\to\mathbb{R}^{d_{k+1}}
, there exists a neural network
h=\varphi^{(K)}\circ\varphi^{(K-1)}\dots\circ\varphi^{(1)}\in\mathsf{NN}(L,W,S,B)
satisfying

\displaystyle L

\displaystyle\leqslant 1+\sum_{k=1}^{K}(L_{k}-1),\quad S\leqslant\sum_{k=1}^{K}S_{k}+2\sum_{k=1}^{K-1}\|W_{k}\|_{\infty}\cdot\|W_{k+1}\|_{\infty},

\displaystyle\|W\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leqslant\max_{1\leqslant k\leqslant K}\|W_{k}\|_{\infty},\quad B\leqslant 2\max_{1\leqslant k\leqslant K-1}\left[(B_{k}\vee 1)(B_{k+1}\vee 1)\left(\|W_{k}\|_{\infty}\vee\|W_{k+1}\|_{\infty}\right)\right].

Proof.

It suffices to prove the statement for
K=2
, since one can easily generalize it to
K\geqslant 3
by induction.
Recall that each
\varphi^{(j)}
for
j\in\{1,2\}
admits the representation given in (
1
).
Specifically,

\displaystyle\varphi^{(j)}(x)=-b^{j}_{L_{j}}+A^{j}_{L_{j}}\circ\mathrm{GELU}_{b^{j}_{L_{j}-1}}\circ A^{j}_{L_{j}-1}\circ\mathrm{GELU}_{b^{j}_{L_{j}-2}}\circ\dots\circ A^{j}_{2}\circ\mathrm{GELU}_{b^{j}_{1}}\circ A^{j}_{1}\circ x.

Therefore, we deduce that

\displaystyle\varphi^{(2)}\circ\varphi^{(1)}\circ x=-b^{2}_{L_{2}}+A^{2}_{L_{2}}\circ\mathrm{GELU}_{b^{2}_{L_{2}-1}}\circ\dots\circ\mathrm{GELU}_{A_{1}^{2}b^{1}_{L_{1}}+b_{1}^{2}}\circ A_{1}^{2}A_{L_{1}}^{1}\circ\dots\circ\mathrm{GELU}_{b_{1}^{1}}\circ A_{1}^{1}\circ x.

Consequently, it follows that
\varphi^{(2)}\circ\varphi^{(1)}\in\mathsf{NN}(L,W,S,B)
with

\displaystyle L

\displaystyle\leqslant L_{1}+L_{2}-1,\quad\|W\|_{\infty}\leqslant\|W_{1}\|_{\infty}\vee\|W_{2}\|_{\infty},

\displaystyle S

\displaystyle\leqslant S_{1}+S_{2}+2\|W_{1}\|_{\infty}\cdot\|W_{2}\|_{\infty},\quad B\leqslant 2(B_{1}\vee 1)(B_{2}\vee 1)(\|W_{1}\|_{\infty}\vee\|W_{2}\|_{\infty}).

Hence, the base case holds.
The result then follows by induction.

∎

Lemma B.6

(parallelization of neural networks)
.

Let
K\in\mathbb{N}
with
K\geqslant 2
and let neural networks
\varphi^{(k)}\in\mathsf{NN}(L_{k},W_{k},S_{k},B_{k})
for
1\leqslant k\leqslant K
.
Assume further that
L_{k}=L
for all
1\leqslant k\leqslant K
.
Then, the following holds:

(i)

if
\varphi^{(k)}:\mathbb{R}^{d_{k}}\to\mathbb{R}
for each
1\leqslant k\leqslant K
, then there exists a neural network
\varphi\in\mathsf{NN}(L,W,S,B)
such that
\varphi(x)=(\varphi^{(1)}(x_{1}),\dots,\varphi^{(K)}(x_{K}))^{\top}
for all
x=(x_{1}^{\top},\dots,x_{K}^{\top})^{\top}
,
where
x_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{k}}
for any
1\leqslant k\leqslant K
.
In addition, there exists
\varphi_{sum}\in\mathsf{NN}(L,W,S,B_{sum})
, which implements the summation, that is,

\displaystyle\varphi_{sum}(x)=\sum_{k=1}^{K}\varphi^{(k)}(x_{k}),\quad\text{for all }x=(x_{1}^{\top},\dots,x_{K}^{\top})^{\top}.

(ii)

if
\varphi^{(k)}:\mathbb{R}^{p}\to\mathbb{R}
for some
p\in N
for every
1\leqslant k\leqslant K
, then there exists a neural network
\varphi\in\mathsf{NN}(L,W,S,B)
satisfying
\varphi(x)=(\varphi^{(1)}(x),\dots,\varphi^{(K)}(x))^{\top}
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
.
Moreover, there exists a summation network
\varphi_{sum}\in\mathsf{NN}(L,W,S,B_{sum})
such that

\displaystyle\varphi_{sum}(x)=\sum_{k=1}^{K}\varphi^{(k)}(x),\quad\text{for all }x\in\mathbb{R}^{p}.

Furthermore, in both cases it holds that

\displaystyle\|W\|_{\infty}\leqslant\sum_{k=1}^{K}\|W^{(k)}\|_{\infty},\quad S\leqslant\sum_{k=1}^{K}S^{(k)},\quad B\leqslant\max_{1\leqslant k\leqslant K}B^{(k)},\quad B_{sum}\leqslant K\max_{1\leqslant k\leqslant K}B^{(k)}.

Proof.

As for the case
(i)
, from (
1
) we find that
\varphi^{(j)}
for each
1\leqslant j\leqslant K
has the following form:

\displaystyle\varphi^{(j)}(x_{j})=-b^{j}_{L}+A^{j}_{L}\circ\mathrm{GELU}_{b^{j}_{L-1}}\circ A^{j}_{L-1}\circ\mathrm{GELU}_{b^{j}_{L-2}}\circ\dots\circ A^{j}_{2}\circ\mathrm{GELU}_{b^{j}_{1}}\circ A^{j}_{1}\circ x_{j}.

Following
Nakada and Imaizumi 2020b
, we introduce

\displaystyle\widetilde{A}_{l}=\begin{pmatrix}A_{l}^{1}&0&\dots&0\\
0&A_{l}^{2}&\dots&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&0&\dots&A_{l}^{K}\end{pmatrix},\quad\widetilde{b}_{l}=\begin{pmatrix}b_{l}^{1}\\
\vdots\\
b_{l}^{K}\end{pmatrix},\quad\text{for all }1\leqslant l\leqslant L.

(101)

Hence, as suggested by (
1
), for

\displaystyle\varphi(x)=-\widetilde{b}_{L}+\widetilde{A}_{L}\circ\mathrm{GELU}_{\widetilde{b}_{L-1}}\circ\widetilde{A}_{L-1}\circ\mathrm{GELU}_{\widetilde{b}_{L-2}}\circ\dots\circ\widetilde{A}_{2}\circ\mathrm{GELU}_{\widetilde{b}_{1}}\circ\widetilde{A}_{1}\circ x

we have that
\varphi(x)=(\varphi^{(1)}(x_{1}),\dots,\varphi^{(K)}(x_{K}))^{\top}
for all
x=(x_{1}^{\top},\dots,x_{K}^{\top})^{\top}
.
Furthermore, the configuration of the network
\varphi
coincides with that from the statement of the lemma.
As for the summation network, we let

\displaystyle\varphi_{sum}(x)=-\bar{b}_{L}+\bar{A}_{L}\circ\mathrm{GELU}_{\widetilde{b}_{L-1}}\circ\widetilde{A}_{L-1}\circ\mathrm{GELU}_{\widetilde{b}_{L-2}}\circ\dots\circ\widetilde{A}_{2}\circ\mathrm{GELU}_{\widetilde{b}_{1}}\circ\widetilde{A}_{1}\circ x,

where

\displaystyle\bar{A}_{L}=\begin{pmatrix}A_{L}^{1}&A_{L}^{2}&\dots&A_{L}^{K}\end{pmatrix},\quad\bar{b}_{L}=\sum_{k=1}^{K}b_{L}^{k}.

(102)

Hence, it follows that

\displaystyle\varphi_{sum}(x)=\sum_{k=1}^{K}\varphi^{(k)}(x_{k}),\quad\text{for all }x=(x_{1}^{\top},\dots,x_{K}^{\top})^{\top}.

The configuration of
\varphi_{sum}
immediately follows from (
102
).
The proof of the case
(ii)
is identical to the considered one.
The only difference is that in (
101
) for
l=1
we define

\displaystyle\widetilde{A}_{1}=\begin{pmatrix}{A^{1}_{1}}^{\top}&{A_{1}^{2}}^{\top}&\dots&{A_{1}^{K}}^{\top}\end{pmatrix}^{\top},\quad\widetilde{b}_{1}=\begin{pmatrix}{b_{1}^{1}}^{\top}&{b_{1}^{2}}^{\top}&\dots&{b_{1}^{K}}^{\top}\end{pmatrix}^{\top}.

The proof is finished.

∎

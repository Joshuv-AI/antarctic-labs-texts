Effect of a goal-directed composite insulation scheme on perioperative elderly patients undergoing laparoscopic colorectal cancer surgery: study protocol for a randomized controlled trial
Authors: Lingjun Du, Yanguang Su, Xieyuan Yu, Zhuhua Shen, Yanli Zhang, He Liu, Zhaozheng Zhen
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundTo explore the clinical effect and practical value of perioperative composite insulation scheme in elderly patients undergoing laparoscopic colorectal cancer surgery.MethodsIn this prospective, randomized, controlled trial, we will enroll 100 patients aged over 65 years, schemed for laparoscopic colorectal cancer surgery under general anesthesia. They will be randomly assigned to two groups: the conventional group will adopt the traditional insulation intervention, and the intervention group will implement the composite insulation care intervention. Changes in heart rate and blood pressure between immediately 15 and 30 min after surgery and the incidence of anesthesia recovery and complications between the two groups will be recorded and analyzed.DiscussionIt is expected that the perioperative composite insulation intervention strategy can effectively maintain the patient’s body temperature, reduce the postoperative stress response, and reduce the incidence of hypothermia and related complications. It can improve the surgical safety and the quality of postoperative recovery in elderly patients.Trial registrationChinese Clinical Trial Registry (https://www.chictr.org.cn/) ChiCTR2400088729. Registered in August, 2024. This trial is a prospective registry with the following registry names: Construction and application of perioperative goal-oriented composite insulation scheme in elderly patients undergoing laparoscopic colorectal cancer surgery.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s13063-025-09367-8.Keywords: Composite insulation, Elderly, Laparoscope, Colorectal cancer, Perioperative period

Introduction

Background and rationale {6a}

Colorectal cancer [1–3] (CRC) is the third most common cancer in the world. The incidence of colorectal cancer has risen to the third of malignant tumors and the first of digestive tract malignant tumors [4]. According to the latest statistics in China, the incidence rate and mortality rate of colorectal cancer in China rank the third and the fifth place, respectively, among malignant tumors (Hao Jie’s team).

At present, surgery is still the first choice and the most important treatment mode for colorectal cancer. Nowadays, more and more elderly patients choose to perform endoscopic surgery for [5]. Advanced age and hypothermia are high risk factors for postoperative complications in such patients. Due to the low basal metabolic rate, weak thermoregulatory capacity and tolerance, decreased vasoconstriction threshold, and increased sensitivity to anesthetics, elderly patients have a higher risk of hypothermia and reduced body metabolism [6]. After the initiation of anesthesia induction, the patient’s body temperature is no longer regulated by the thermoregulatory center. When the patient’s intraoperative body temperature falls below 36 °C, it is considered intraoperative hypothermia. Clinically, hypothermia is categorized into three levels based on the temperature value: mild hypothermia (34~36 °C), moderate hypothermia (32~34 °C), and severe hypothermia (< 32 °C). It has been reported that the incidence of hypothermia in elderly patients can be as high as 74%, and there is a close correlation between lower preoperative functional status and adverse postoperative outcomes. Low temperature will cause damage to the body’s immune system, inhibit the killing of neutrophils, reduce white blood cells toward the infection movement, reduce the blood flow and skin oxygen supply, inhibit oxygen intake, chills, awakening delay, wound infection [7], affect platelet function, increase the incidence of heart disease, prolong the patient stay in PACU, serious cases can lead to metabolic acidosis, coagulation dysfunction, and multiple organ failure, thus seriously affect the patients with rapid postoperative rehabilitation [8]. In view of this, in order to accelerate the postoperative rehabilitation of elderly patients with colorectal cancer [9], avoiding perioperative hypothermia is an important part of the rapid postoperative recovery of such patients.

Clinical investigation found that only 20% of patients with perioperative temperature monitoring, and the conventional insulation measures are mostly limited to passive insulation, and the application of active insulation measures is very limited. Inflatating blanket is the most commonly used and effective active insulation measure [10], which plays an important role in preventing perioperative hypothermia. In clinical practice, we observed that about 20% of patients still had perioperative hypothermia [11]; moreover, the effectiveness and utility of inflatable heating blanket in patients undergoing colorectal cancer surgery are not sufficient evidence. Therefore, this study intends to use a prospective, randomized controlled study to investigate whether the peroperative composite insulation strategy for elderly patients undergoing laparoscopic colorectal cancer surgery is more beneficial to reduce peroperative hypothermia, reduce postoperative complications, and promote rapid postoperative recovery. The results of this study will provide a scientific theoretical basis for the standardized perioperative body temperature management of such patients and will provide useful clinical guidance for their rapid postoperative rehabilitation.

Objectives {7}

Planned to adopt a combination of preoperative and postoperative recovery insulation measures to protect patients with intraoperative temperature, and observe composite insulation measures and conventional insulation care measures in intraoperative low temperature, postoperative complications, anesthesia recovery time of clinical effect, clarify the intraoperative temperature timing and factors affecting temperature stability, test compound insulation method in elderly laparoscopic colorectal cancer patients to prevent intraoperative low temperature clinical effect and feasibility, and explore the elderly laparoscopic colorectal cancer to prevent the best temperature care measures.

Trial design {8}

The study will be conducted using a prospective, single-center, parallel-group, randomized, controlled trial.

Interventions

Explanation for the choice of comparators {6b}

We describe all the interventions as composite insulation methods. Composite insulation method refers to the addition of preoperative pre-insulation, intraoperative insulation, and postoperative anesthesia resuscitation insulation measures on the basis of conventional insulation measures.

Intervention description {11a}

All subjects will wait in the preoperative preparation room at room temperature of 25 ℃, perform laparoscopic colorectal cancer surgery by the same surgeon, and resuscitate in the anesthesia resuscitation room at room temperature at room temperature at 22 to 24 ℃ and humidity at 50% to 60%. 37℃ disinfectant, intravenous infusion and peritoneal lavage fluid were used in the operation.

Grouping random sequences were generated in a 1:1 order using a computerized random number generator, placed in sealed, opaque and sequentially numbered envelopes. When the participant is eligible for the study and assigned to surgery, the researcher will open the envelope to obtain a random sequence that determines the grouping. Each participant will correspond to a unique random serial number in the order in which they participate in this study. Numbering information for all participants will be recorded in the randomized list.

Description for Intervention Eligible participants will be randomly assigned to the above two groups in the same proportion and will monitor the core body temperature using a wireless body temperature sensor (iThermonitor WT701 Beijing Ruiren Medical Company). The sensor is attached to the armpit and provides continuous (every 4 s) core body temperature monitoring, whose accuracy and accuracy have been confirmed in surgical patients [12]. The body temperature data is transmitted to the monitor and central temperature monitoring system via a signal repeater (cHub) manufactured by Beijing Rui Ren Medical Company. After the patient enters the surgical preparation room, the body temperature sensor will be fixed to either side of the axilla by using the adhesive tape of the instrument, and the upper arm is closed for 8 min. The monitoring time continues until the patient leaves the anesthesia recovery room (PACU). Routine group intervention methods: Adjust room temperature to 25 ℃ before operation, 22 ℃ after the operation, and humidity of 45%; use disinfectant, infusion liquid, and irrigation solution with heating to 37 ℃; provide insulation with quilt during the operation. Intervention group: During the waiting room, use inflatable insulation set to 40 ℃ for pre-insulation for 30 min; preoperative room temperature was adjusted to 25 ℃, 22 ℃ after the operation, 45% humidity; spread variable blanket on the operating table and set to 35 ℃; use the warmed to 37 ℃ disinfectant, infusion fluid, and irrigation fluid; cover the chest and abdomen with inflatable blanket for at least 20 min before anesthesia, and continuously cover the neck and chest during the operation; warm from CO2 to 37 ℃; use inflatable insulation 40 ℃ for 30 min.

Introduction for investigators: The study will be conducted by the participation of the department of anesthesia, anorectal surgery, resuscitation room nurses, and surgery nurses. The nursing participants in this study had extensive experience in endoscopic colorectal surgical coordination and had specialized training in temperature monitoring.

Outcomes {12}

Primary outcome

The primary outcome was the incidence of hypothermia. Axillary temperature serves as an observation indicator of core body temperature. During general anesthesia, measuring core body temperature via the middle and lower esophagus is the most common practice, while routine monitoring of esophageal temperature or nasopharyngeal temperature poses certain risks, such as severe gastroesophageal varices and skull base fractures. Although axillary temperature does not constitute core body temperature, it can reflect core body temperature to a certain extent. The relationship between axillary temperature and core body temperature largely depends on measurement techniques and environment. Compared to traditional axillary thermometers, the novel wireless temperature sensor used in this study provides better estimates of core body temperature. Its temperature probe continuously measures and records body temperature at a frequency of once every 4 s and compensates for errors caused by changes in environmental temperature and measurement location through algorithms. Before measurement, the axilla is thoroughly prepared to ensure sufficient contact between the wireless temperature probe and the axillary skin. Additionally, we chose to use temperature data obtained every 15 min as the observation indicator, mainly due to the discovery during the comparison of colorectal cancer surgery duration and temperature measurement points that measuring temperature every 15 min is the optimal choice in terms of economy, manpower, and material resources in the operating room. Therefore, when patients in the control group and intervention group enter the preoperative waiting area, they will be placed with a temperature probe connected to a multi-function monitor for continuous dynamic monitoring and recording of their body temperature at various time points, including room temperature (T1), preoperative temperature (T2), temperature at 30 min during surgery (T3), temperature at 60 min during surgery (T4), temperature at 90 min during surgery (T5), temperature at the end of surgery (T6), temperature upon entering the post-anesthesia care unit (PACU) (T7), and temperature upon exiting the PACU (T8).

Secondary outcomes

Time

Observe and record the anesthesia time (i.e., from anesthesia induction from awake to sleep to emergence from anesthesia) in the observation group, the operation time (from the beginning of the surgeon and the end of the incision), the removal time (from the end of the procedure to the indication and the removal of the catheter) (the stay time in the anesthesia room (PACU) (the time between the patient and the ward).

The incidence of postoperative chills

The incidence of postoperative chills in the observation group and the trial group (after recovery from surgical anesthesia to the resuscitation room was recorded). In this study, Wrench Level 4 assessment method was used to evaluate the occurrence of chills: observe whether muscle fibrillation occurred in the face and neck muscles, and affected the ECG waveform in the ECG monitor, which was mild chills; the limb muscles had obvious muscle fibrillation and moderate chills; the whole body had obvious tremor, which was severe chills. If the above three situations occur, no chills occurs.

Thermal comfort

The thermal comfort score of patients was evaluated using the thermal comfort score method (Numerical rating scale, NRS) of German scholars Perl et al. The method totaled 10 points; 0 to 10 represent different thermal comfort conditions. After the patient explains the evaluation method, the day after the procedure was scored and recorded according to the patient’s feedback. The primary reason for measuring thermal comfort on the second day after surgery in this study is that patients are not fully conscious post-operation and thus unable to fully understand and respond to the questions posed. The digital thermal comfort scale scoring method developed by German scholar Perl has been clinically applied in China and demonstrates good reliability and validity, enabling accurate measurement of patients’ evaluations of thermal comfort during heat preservation.

Assignment of interventions: allocation

Sequence generation {16a}

Random sequences were generated 1:1 using a computerized random number generator and placed in sealed, opaque, and sequentially numbered envelopes. When the participant is eligible for the study and is allowed to enter the surgery preparation room, the researcher will open the envelope and obtain a randomized sequence that group grouping. Each participant will correspond to a unique random serial number in an envelope in the order they participate in this study. Numbering information for all participants will be recorded in the randomized list.

Concealment mechanism {16b}

The assigned intervention will be placed in sealed envelopes and marked with participant numbers, inconsistent with the order of randomization.

Implementation {16c}

After obtaining consent, a designated researcher will open the envelope and show the assigned intervention. Subsequently, the operators involved in the trial will perform the intervention.

Assignment of interventions: blinding

Who will be blinded {17a}

Due to the nature of the intervention in this study, the person responsible for the randomization process and the operator of the insulation measures will be unblinded to the researcher. Therefore, participants, investigators with postoperative follow-up will be unaware of the randomization sequence.

Procedure for unblinding if needed {17b}

The trial did not involve unblinding and follow an open-label design. Only the data analysts will be blinded.

Discussion

Under the comprehensive influence of various factors, the operation will lead to hypothermia [15], while the physiological function and temperature autoregulation ability of elderly patients are weakened, and the incidence of hypothermia is higher. Studies have shown that the incidence of hypothermia in surgery is 50%~90% [7]. If it is not corrected in time for a long time, it will cause surgical stress reaction, inhibit coagulation and immune function [16], and also lead to a decrease in the metabolism rate of anesthetic drugs and delay postoperative recovery, which is not conducive to postoperative rehabilitation [17]. Therefore, it is very important to prevent the occurrence of intraoperative hypothermia.

Several studies have shown that the implementation of thermal insulation measures can maintain the intraoperative body temperature and hemodynamic stability of surgical patients, relieve the stress response, reduce the incidence of intraoperative hypothermia, and improve the recovery quality of patients [18, 19]. Therefore, the perioperative thermal insulation intervention has received clinical attention. The composite insulation strategy is based on evidence-based medicine, with comprehensive insulation intervention from multiple links and aspects. This study formulated a composite insulation measure based on the expert consensus of perioperative hypothermia prevention and carried out comprehensive insulation intervention through internal environment and external environment.

In conclusion, our trial will reveal the influencing factors of body temperature changes in the elderly patients undergoing endoscopic colorectal cancer surgery, which is expected to maintain intraoperative body temperature stability and reduce the incidence of hypothermia.

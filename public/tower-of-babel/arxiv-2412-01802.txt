A new bound on the relative error in the Chebotarev density theorem
Jesse Thorner, Zhuo Zhang

Abstract
. We unconditionally improve the uniformity in the Chebotarev density theorem for Galois extensions of number fields using nonabelian base change. This leads to the first theoretical improvement over Weiss’s bound for the least norm of an unramified prime ideal with given Artin symbol.



1.
Introduction and statement of the main results

Throughout this paper, the numbers
c_{1},c_{2},c_{3},\ldots
form a certain sequence of positive, absolute, and effectively computable constants.

1.1.
Introduction

For a number field
F
, let
\mathcal{O}_{F}
be the ring of integers,
D_{F}
the absolute value of the discriminant, and
n_{F}=[F:\mathbb{Q}]
. Let
L/F
be a Galois extension of number fields, and let
G=\mathrm{Gal}(L/F)
. Let
\mathfrak{p}
(resp.
\mathfrak{a}
) denote a nonzero prime ideal (resp. nonzero ideal) of
\mathcal{O}_{F}
. Define
\mathrm{N}_{F/\mathbb{Q}}\mathfrak{a}=|\mathcal{O}_{F}/\mathfrak{a}|
. For a prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}
of
\mathcal{O}_{F}
that is unramified in
L
, the Artin symbol
[\frac{L/F}{\mathfrak{p}}]
denotes the conjugacy class in
G
of Frobenius automorphisms attached to the prime ideals
\mathfrak{P}
of
\mathcal{O}_{L}
lying over
\mathfrak{p}
. (See
Section

3.1
.) For a conjugacy class
C\subseteq G
, we define

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{C}(L/F)

\displaystyle=\{\mathfrak{p}\subseteq\mathcal{O}_{F}\colon\textup{$\mathfrak{p}$ unramified in $L$ and $[\tfrac{L/F}{\mathfrak{p}}]=C$}\},

\displaystyle\pi_{C}(x,L/F)

\displaystyle=\#\{\mathfrak{p}\in\mathbb{P}_{C}(L/F)\colon\mathrm{N}_{F/\mathbb{Q}}\mathfrak{p}\leq x\}.

The Chebotarev density theorem states that

(1.1)

\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{\pi_{C}(x,L/F)}{\operatorname{Li}(x)}=\frac{|C|}{|G|},\qquad\operatorname{Li}(x)=\int_{2}^{x}\frac{dt}{\log t}.

The rate of convergence lies at the center of many deep problems in number theory. Assuming the generalized Riemann hypothesis, Lagarias and Odlyzko
[
10
, Theorem 1.1]
established a very strong rate of convergence. In this paper, we will focus on unconditional bounds for the rate of convergence.

To describe the best known result, we need some notation. Let
\mathrm{Irr}(G)
be the set of characters associated to the (complex) irreducible representations of
G
. When
G
is abelian,
\mathrm{Irr}(G)
is a group that is isomorphic to
G
, and the characters
\chi\in\mathrm{Irr}(G)
are Hecke characters. We write
\mathbbm{1}=\mathbbm{1}_{G}
for the trivial character of
G
.

Given
\chi\in\mathrm{Irr}(G)
, let
L(s,\chi,L/F)
be the associated Artin
L
-function. We say that a group
G
satisfies the Artin holomorphy conjecture (AHC) if for all Galois extensions
L/F
with Galois group isomorphic to
G
, the Artin
L
-functions
L(s,\chi,L/F)
associated to
\chi\in\mathrm{Irr}(G)-\{\mathbbm{1}_{G}\}
are entire. Define

\displaystyle\operatorname{AHC}(G)

\displaystyle=\{\textup{$H\subseteq G$: $H$ is a subgroup such that $H$ satisfies AHC}\},

\displaystyle\operatorname{Ab}(G)

\displaystyle=\{\textup{$H\subseteq G$: $H$ is a subgroup such that $H$ is abelian}\}.

It follows from class field theory, Artin reciprocity, and work of Hecke that
\mathrm{Ab}(G)\subseteq\mathrm{AHC}(G)
.

Let
\mathfrak{f}_{\chi}
be its Artin conductor, which is an ideal of
\mathcal{O}_{F}
(see (
3.3
)). Write
\chi(1)
for the dimension of
\chi
. Define

(1.2)

\begin{gathered}d_{G}=\max_{\chi\in\mathrm{Irr}(G)}\chi(1),\qquad q_{L/F}=D_{F}^{d_{G}^{2}}\big(\max_{\chi\in\mathrm{Irr}(G)}\mathrm{N}_{F/\mathbb{Q}}\mathfrak{f}_{\chi}\big)^{2d_{G}},\qquad\operatorname{Log}x=\max\{1,\log x\},\\
\mathcal{Q}_{L/F}=(d_{G}^{2}n_{F})^{d_{G}^{2}n_{F}\operatorname{Log}\operatorname{Log}d_{G}}|\mathrm{Irr}(G)|q_{L/F}.\end{gathered}

Let
\zeta_{L}(s)
be the Dedekind zeta function of
L
, which factors as

\zeta_{L}(s)=\zeta_{F}(s)\prod_{\chi\in\mathrm{Irr}(G)-\{\mathbbm{1}_{G}\}}L(s,\chi,L/F)^{\chi(1)},\qquad\mathrm{Re}(s)>1.

Define

(1.3)

\beta_{1}=\beta_{1,L}=\sup\{\sigma<1\colon\zeta_{L}(\sigma)=0\}.

It is classical that
\beta_{1}<1
. Stark
[
20
, Proof of Theorem 3]
proved that if
\beta_{1}
is a simple zero, then each
L(s,\chi,L/F)
associated to
\chi\in\mathrm{Irr}(G)
is holomorphic at
s=\beta_{1}
, and

(1.4)

\begin{gathered}\textup{there exists a unique $\chi_{0}=\chi_{0,L/F}\in\mathrm{Irr}(G)$ such that $\chi_{0}=\overline{\chi}_{0}$, $\chi_{0}(1)=1$, and}\\
\textup{the Artin $L$-function $L(s,\chi_{0},L/F)$ vanishes at $s=\beta_{1}$ (see \lx@cref{creftype~refnum}{eqn:chi_1 well defined} below).}\end{gathered}

Define

(1.5)

\chi_{1}=\chi_{1,L/F}=\begin{cases}\chi_{0}&\mbox{if $\beta_{1}$ is simple,}\\
\mathbbm{1}_{G}&\mbox{otherwise.}\end{cases}

We always have
\chi_{1}(1)=1
and
\chi_{1}=\overline{\chi}_{1}
.

1.2.
Main results

Define the
relative error

\delta_{C}(x,L/F)
by

(1.6)

\pi_{C}(x,L/F)=\frac{|C|}{|G|}(\operatorname{Li}(x)-\chi_{1}(C)\operatorname{Li}(x^{\beta_{1}}))(1+\delta_{C}(x,L/F)).

Since
\operatorname{Li}(x)>\chi_{1}(C)\operatorname{Li}(x^{\beta_{1}})
, it follows that if
|\delta_{C}(x,L/F)|<1
, then

1\leq\pi_{C}(x,L/F)<4\frac{|C|}{|G|}\operatorname{Li}(x).

The following unconditional result is proved in
[
23
, Theorem 1.4]
.

Theorem 1.1

(Thorner–Zaman)
.

Let
L/F
be a Galois extension of number fields with Galois group
G
, and let
C\subseteq G
be a conjugacy class. There exist constants , , and such that if
H\in\mathrm{Ab}(G)
,
C\cap H
is nonempty, and
K=L^{H}
is the subfield of
L
fixed by
H
, then

|\delta_{C}(x,L/F)|\leq\Cr{thm:main2long_{T}Z}\exp\Big(-\Cr{thm:main3long_{T}Z}\frac{\log x}{\log q_{L/K}+\sqrt{n_{K}\log x}}\Big)<1,\qquad x\geq(2\mathcal{Q}_{L/K})^{\Cr{thm:main1long_{T}Z}}.

Remark
.

If
H\in\operatorname{Ab}(G)
and
K=L^{H}
, then
d_{H}=1
and
\mathcal{Q}_{L/K}=n_{K}^{n_{K}}|\mathrm{Irr}(H)|q_{L/K}
. Per
[
22
, Lemma 4.1]
, there exists a constant
>0
such that
|\mathrm{Irr}(H)|\leq(2n_{K}^{n_{K}}q_{L/K})^{\Cr{CFT}}
.

Our main result improves upon
Theorem

1.1
by replacing the condition
H\in\operatorname{Ab}(G)
with the more flexible condition
H\in\operatorname{AHC}(G)
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
L/F
be a Galois extension of number fields with Galois group
G
, and let
C\subseteq G
be a conjugacy class. There exist constants , , and such that if
H\in\mathrm{AHC}(G)
,
C\cap H
is nonempty,
K=L^{H}
is the subfield of
L
fixed by
H
, then

|\delta_{C}(x,L/F)|\leq\Cr{thm:main2long}\exp\Big(-\Cr{thm:main3long}\frac{\log x}{\log q_{L/K}+\sqrt{d_{H}^{2}n_{K}\log x}}\Big)<1,\qquad x\geq(2\mathcal{Q}_{L/K})^{\Cr{thm:main1long}\operatorname{Log}d_{H}}.

The range of
x
in
Theorem

1.1
implies that

\min\{\textup{$\mathfrak{p}\in\mathbb{P}_{C}(L/F)$: $\mathrm{N}_{F/\mathbb{Q}}\mathfrak{p}$ prime}\}\leq\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}H\in\operatorname{Ab}(G)\\
C\cap H\neq\emptyset\end{subarray}}(2\mathcal{Q}_{L/L^{H}})^{\Cr{thm:main1long_{T}Z}}.

This was first proved by Weiss
[
26
]
.
Theorem

1.2
immediately yields the first theoretical improvement, replacing the minimum over
H\in\operatorname{Ab}(G)
with a minimum over
H\in\operatorname{AHC}(G)
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
L/F
be a Galois extension of number fields with Galois group
G
, and let
C\subseteq G
be a conjugacy class. There exists
\mathfrak{p}\in\mathbb{P}_{C}(L/F)
of prime norm satisfying

\mathrm{N}_{F/\mathbb{Q}}\mathfrak{p}\leq\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}H\in\mathrm{AHC}(G)\\
C\cap H\neq\emptyset\end{subarray}}(2\mathcal{Q}_{L/L^{H}})^{\Cr{thm:main1long}\operatorname{Log}d_{H}}.

Remark
.

It follows from
[
4
, Lemma 4.2]
that if
H
is any subgroup of
G
, then

(1.7)

\mathcal{Q}_{L/L^{H}}\ll_{d_{H},n_{L^{H}}}D_{L}^{4d_{H}^{2}/|H|}|\mathrm{Irr}(H)|.

For example, let
\mathbb{N}=\{j\in\mathbb{Z}\colon j\geq 1\}
,
a,q\in\mathbb{N}
, and
\gcd(a,q)=1
. If
F=\mathbb{Q}
and
L=\mathbb{Q}(e^{2\pi i/q})
(so
G=H=(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
and

), then
Theorem

1.3
and (

) recover Linnik’s theorem that there exists a prime
p\ll q^{5\Cr{thm:main1long}}
such that

.

1.3.
Reduction to groups satisfying AHC

Using the invariance of Artin

-functions under induction (see

and

to a study of

satisfies AHC. The only earlier work estimating

under AHC when

is non-abelian (i.e., without abelian base change) is that of V.K. Murty
[

, which we now describe using our notation: Under AHC, there exist constants , , and such that if

x\geq(q_{L/K}^{d_{G}^{2}})^{\Cr{VKM1}\log(|G|\log(q_{L/K}^{d_{G}^{2}}))}+(1+\chi_{1}(C))e^{\frac{1}{1-\beta_{1}}},

|\delta_{C}(x,L/K)|\leq\frac{\Cr{VKM2}}{\sqrt{|C|}}\cdot\frac{n_{L}(\log x)^{3}}{1-\chi_{1}(C)x^{\beta_{1}-1}}\exp\Big(-\Cr{VKM3}\frac{\log x}{\log(q_{L/K}^{d_{G}^{2}})+\sqrt{d_{G}^{3}n_{K}\log x}}\Big)<1.

Here, we significantly improve upon (

) and (

).

.

Let
L/K
be a Galois extension of number fields with Galois group

satisfying AHC, and let

be a conjugacy class. Recall (

)-(

There exist constants , , and such that

improves upon the results of
[
13
]
(leading to the unconditional

1.2
and

) in four crucial ways. First, the dependence on
\beta_{1}
and

in (

) and (

. Second, the factor of

in the bound for

to a polynomial dependence. Fourth, compared to (

) and (

essentially replaces

with

. This last feature is important because

, which could be large with respect to

.

These improvements are made possible by a new zero-free region (
Theorem

4.5
) and a new “repulsive” log-free zero density estimate that improves when

is simple and close to

(
Theorem

4.8
) for the Artin

-functions associated to

. If

, then AHC is known, and these results are already available in
[

]

and we assume a strong form of AHC in which each

, then we would be able to cite existing results in the literature, though the dependence on

and

would be either unspecified or far from optimal. Therefore, we must establish these results using

and

(especially in the exponent

). This requires more care, including several auxiliary results on Artin
L
-functions that we could not find elsewhere in the literature, which might be of independent interest.

While our new zero-free region in
Theorem

is a nontrivial refinement of the work in
[

]
, our log-free zero density estimate in
Theorem

4.8
appears to be genuinely new, especially the hybrid-aspect “pre-sifted” large sieve inequality in

. Without significant improvements to both the zero-free regions and the bounds on the critical line that we prove for Artin

-functions in this paper, our improvement from

) and (

in

The above discussion pertains to the AHC-conditional

2.
Comparison with earlier results

Since
\operatorname{Ab}(G)\subseteq\operatorname{AHC}(G)
,
Theorem

1.2
is at least as strong as
Theorem

1.1
for all Galois extensions
L/F
. It is difficult to fully classify the extensions
L/F
and the conjugacy classes
C
such that
Theorem

1.2
greatly improves upon
Theorem

1.1
. To simplify our contrast between
Theorem

1.2
and
Theorem

1.1
, we use (
1.7
), leaving us to determine whether

(2.1)

\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}H\in\mathrm{AHC}(G)\\
C\cap H\neq\emptyset\end{subarray}}\frac{d_{H}^{2}\operatorname{Log}d_{H}}{|H|}
is much smaller than
\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}H\in\mathrm{Ab}(G)\\
C\cap H\neq\emptyset\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{|H|}
.

In (
2.1
), the first quantity is no larger than the second because
\mathrm{Ab}(G)\subseteq\mathrm{AHC}(G)
, and an abelian subgroup
H
satisfies
d_{H}=1
. There might exist a nonabelian
H\in\mathrm{AHC}(G)
intersecting
C
such that the first quantity is much smaller than the second. For example,
\mathrm{AHC}(G)
contains all monomial subgroups of
G
. Every nilpotent subgroup of
G
is monomial; in particular, the Sylow subgroups of
G
lie in
\mathrm{AHC}(G)
. Therefore, in (
2.1
), the condition
H\in\mathrm{AHC}(G)
is much more flexible than the condition
H\in\mathrm{Ab}(G)
.

Despite the discussion above, for any given group
G
, it is difficult to classify the conjugacy classes
C
for which (
2.1
) is true. To simplify the discussion further, we observe that if
H\in\mathrm{Ab}(G)
and
g\in H\cap C
, then all elements of
H
commute with
g
because
H
is abelian. It follows that
H
is contained in the centralizer subgroup
C_{G}(g)
of
g
. The orbit-stabilizer theorem now implies that
[G:H]\geq[G:C_{G}(g)]=|C|
. Therefore, we argue that
Theorem

1.2
improves upon
Theorem

1.1
when

(2.2)

\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}H\in\mathrm{AHC}(G)\\
C\cap H\neq\emptyset\end{subarray}}\frac{d_{H}^{2}\operatorname{Log}d_{H}}{|H|}
is much smaller than
\frac{|C|}{|G|}
.

When
G
happens to satisfy AHC, we argue that
Theorem

1.2
improves upon
Theorem

1.1
when

(2.3)

d_{G}^{2}\operatorname{Log}d_{G}
is much smaller than
|C|
.

Compared to (
2.1
), the condition (
2.3
) is purely representation theoretic and more computationally tractable. We present some infinite families of examples of nonabelian groups
G
with conjugacy classes
C
such that (
2.2
) or (
2.3
) is true.

Example: Dihedral groups

Let
n\geq 3
be an integer, and let
G
be the dihedral group of order
2n
. The group
G
is monomial and thus satisfies AHC and
d_{G}^{2}\operatorname{Log}d_{G}=4
. Consider the presentation
G=\langle r,s\mid r^{n}=s^{2}=rsrs=1\rangle
. If
n
is odd, then consider the conjugacy class
\{sr^{j}\colon 0\leq j\leq n-1\}
. If
n
is even and
k\in\{0,1\}
, then consider the conjugacy class
\{sr^{2j+k}\colon 0\leq j\leq(n-2)/2\}
. In any case, (
2.3
) is satisfied when
n
is sufficiently large.

Example: Groups of order
pq

Let
p,q
be primes such that
p\equiv 1\pmod{q}
. There exists a unique isomorphism class of nonabelian groups of order
pq
with presentation

G=\langle a,b\mid a^{p}=b^{q}=1,~bab^{-1}=a^{r}\rangle,

where
r
is any integer in a residue class of
(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
with multiplicative order
q
. This group can be realized as the semidirect product
\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}\rtimes\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z}
. The group
G
is supersolvable with normal series
\{1\}\unlhd\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}\unlhd G
. Thus,
G
is monomial and satisfies AHC. For any integer
n\in[1,q-1]
, the set
C=\{a^{j}b^{n}\colon 0\leq j\leq p-1\}
is a conjugacy class in
G
of size
p
.

If
\chi\in\mathrm{Irr}(G)
, then
\chi(1)
is a proper divisor of
pq
, so
\chi(1)\in\{1,p,q\}
. Since
G
is nonabelian and
d_{G}^{2}<|G|=pq<p^{2}
, it follows that
d_{G}=q
. The infinitude of pairs of primes
p,q
such
p\equiv 1\pmod{q}
and
p
is much larger than
q^{2}\log q
follows from Dirichlet’s theorem. For such
p
and
q
,
G
and
C
satisfy (
2.3
).

Example: Symmetric groups

Consider the symmetric group
G=S_{n}
. Per
[
25
, Theorem 1]
, there exist constants
0<<
such that
e^{-\Cr{dG2}\sqrt{n}}n!\leq d_{G}^{2}\leq e^{-\Cr{dG1}\sqrt{n}}n!
. By Stirling’s formula, there exist constants
0<<
such that

(2.4)

e^{-\Cr{dG4}\sqrt{n}}n!\leq d_{G}^{2}\operatorname{Log}d_{G}\leq e^{-\Cr{dG3}\sqrt{n}}n!.

Let
k\geq 1
be an integer, let
n_{1}>n_{2}>\cdots>n_{k}\geq 1
be integers, and let
r_{1},\ldots,r_{k}\geq 1
be integers. Consider the partition
\mu
of
n
given by

n=\underbrace{n_{1}+\cdots+n_{1}}_{\textup{$r_{1}$ times}}+\underbrace{n_{2}+\cdots+n_{2}}_{\textup{$r_{2}$ times}}+\cdots+\underbrace{n_{k}+\cdots+n_{k}}_{\textup{$r_{k}$ times}}.

If
C
is the conjugacy class of permutations of cycle type
\mu
, then

|C|=\frac{n!}{w(\mu)},\qquad w(\mu)=\prod_{j=1}^{k}n_{j}^{r_{j}}(r_{j}!).

2.0.1.
Unconditional example

Let
p
be prime, and let
n=p^{2}
. Let
C
be the conjugacy class of
p^{2}
-cycles. Then
|G|/|C|=p^{2}
. Let
H
be the Sylow
p
-subgroup of
G
. We have that
H\in\operatorname{AHC}(G)
since
H
is a
p
-group and hence is monomial.

Per
[
16
, Theorem 7.27]
,
H
is isomorphic to the semidirect product
(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})^{p}\rtimes(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})
, where the second factor
\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}
acts on the
p
-fold direct product
(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})^{p}
by cyclically permuting the components. Note that
H
contains an element of order
p^{2}
, so
H
must intersect

. We have
|H|=p^{p+1}
. It follows from
[
17
, Theorem 25]
that

. Since
(p^{2}\log p)/p^{p+1}
is much smaller than

, we see that (
2.2
) holds.

2.0.2.
AHC-conditional examples

If
S_{n}
satisfies AHC, then (

) is satisfied for any conjugacy class
C
such that

(2.5)

w(\mu)
is is much smaller than
e^{\Cr{dG4}\sqrt{n}}
.

If

Remark
.

If we assume AHC for
G

), we would have

\frac{d_{G}^{2}\operatorname{Log}d_{G}}{|G|}\geq\frac{1}{e^{\Cr{dG4}p}},

which is much larger than

d_{H}^{2}\operatorname{Log}d_{H}
. Note that
|H|
is smaller than
|G|
, but
d_{H}
is also much smaller than
d_{G}
. The decrease in the dimensions of the irreducible representations eventually makes it more efficient to base change to
H
.

Example: Sylow
p
-subgroups

In general, it can be difficult to compute

\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}H\in\mathrm{AHC}(G)\\
C\cap H\neq\emptyset\end{subarray}}\frac{d_{H}^{2}\operatorname{Log}d_{H}}{|H|}.

If
Z(H)
is the center of
H
, then we always have the bound

\frac{|H/Z(H)|}{|\mathrm{Irr}(H)|}\leq d_{H}^{2}\leq|H/Z(H)|.

It follows that

\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}H\in\mathrm{AHC}(G)\\
C\cap H\neq\emptyset\end{subarray}}\frac{d_{H}^{2}\operatorname{Log}d_{H}}{|H|}\leq\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}H\in\mathrm{AHC}(G)\\
C\cap H\neq\emptyset\end{subarray}}\frac{\operatorname{Log}|H/Z(H)|}{|Z(H)|}.

This is useful because it is oftentimes more computationally tractable to estimate
|Z(H)|
than
d_{H}
. For example, suppose that there exists a rational prime
p
such that
C
intersects a Sylow
p
-subgroup
H
of
G
. Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
satisfy
|H|=p^{n}
, and let
r\geq 1
be the rank of
H
. The lower bound
|Z(H)|\geq p^{r}
implies that

\frac{\log|H/Z(H)|}{|Z(H)|}\leq\frac{(n-r)\log p}{p^{r}}.

New examples from old ones

Let
G
(resp.
G^{\prime}
) be a finite group, and let
C\subseteq G
(resp.
C^{\prime}\subseteq G^{\prime}
) be a conjugacy class. If
G
is nonabelian, then so is
G\times G^{\prime}
. Note that
C\times C^{\prime}
is a conjugacy class in
G\times G^{\prime}
, and
d_{G\times G^{\prime}}=d_{G}d_{G^{\prime}}
. If
G\times G^{\prime}
satisfies AHC and

(2.6)

(d_{G}d_{G^{\prime}})^{2}\operatorname{Log}(d_{G}d_{G^{\prime}})
is much smaller than
|C|\cdot|C^{\prime}|
,

then (
2.3
) is satisfied for the conjugacy class
C\times C^{\prime}
in
G\times G^{\prime}
.

There are many situations where (
2.6
) is satisfied. For example, if
G
and
C
satisfy (
2.3
), then (
2.6
) is satisfied when
G^{\prime}
is abelian, so that
d_{G^{\prime}}=|C^{\prime}|=1
. Alternatively, if both pairs
(G,C)
and
(G^{\prime},C^{\prime})
satisfy (
2.3
), then the group
G\times G^{\prime}
and the conjugacy class
C\times C^{\prime}
satisfy (
2.6
). In particular, let
n\geq 2
be a positive integer and
p_{1},\ldots,p_{n}
be primes such that
p_{1}\equiv 1\pmod{p_{2}}
. Using the example from our discussion on groups of order
pq
, we can apply these results to the supersolvable (hence monomial) group
(\mathbb{Z}/p_{1}\mathbb{Z}\rtimes\mathbb{Z}/p_{2}\mathbb{Z})\times\mathbb{Z}/p_{3}\mathbb{Z}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{Z}/p_{n}\mathbb{Z}
.

3.
Artin
L
-functions

In this section, let
L/K
be a Galois extension of number fields with Galois group
G
(not necessarily satisfying AHC). Let
\mathfrak{p}
(resp.
\mathfrak{a}
) be a nonzero prime ideal (resp. nonzero ideal) of
\mathcal{O}_{K}
, and let
\mathrm{N}_{K/\mathbb{Q}}\mathfrak{a}=|\mathcal{O}_{K}/\mathfrak{a}|
. Recall that
\mathrm{Irr}(G)
is the set of irreducible complex representations of
G
. We do not distinguish between a representation and its character. We introduce

\mathrm{Rep}(G)=\Big\{\bigoplus_{j=1}^{m}\chi_{j}\colon m\in\mathbb{N},~\chi_{1},\ldots,\chi_{m}\in\mathrm{Irr}(G)\Big\}.

Let
\mathbbm{1}\in\mathrm{Irr}(G)
be the trivial character.

3.1.
Preliminaries

We recall the basic definitions of Artin
L
-functions and some preliminary results following
[
12
]
. Let
\mathfrak{p}
be a prime ideal in
\mathcal{O}_{K}
and
\mathfrak{P}
be a prime ideal in
\mathcal{O}_{L}
lying above
\mathfrak{p}
. Let
D_{\mathfrak{P}}
be the decomposition group of
\mathfrak{P}
, i.e., the set of elements in
G
that fix
\mathfrak{P}
. Then there is a surjective group homomorphism

D_{\mathfrak{P}}\to\operatorname{Gal}((\mathcal{O}_{L}/\mathfrak{P})/(\mathcal{O}_{K}/\mathfrak{p})),

where
\mathcal{O}_{L}/\mathfrak{P}
and
\mathcal{O}_{K}/\mathfrak{p}
are the residue fields at
\mathfrak{P}
and
\mathfrak{p}
, respectively. The kernel of this map is, by definition, the inertia group
I_{\mathfrak{P}}
. Hence, there is an isomorphism

D_{\mathfrak{P}}/I_{\mathfrak{P}}\cong\operatorname{Gal}((\mathcal{O}_{L}/\mathfrak{P})/(\mathcal{O}_{K}/\mathfrak{p})).

The later group is the Galois group of an extension of finite fields and hence is generated by the Frobenius automorphism
x\mapsto x^{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{p}}
. Thus, there exists a unique element
\varphi_{\mathfrak{P}}\in D_{\mathfrak{P}}/I_{\mathfrak{P}}
, the Frobenius element of
\mathfrak{P}
, that is sent to the Frobenius automorphism under the isomorphism.

The prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}
is unramified if and only if the inertia group
I_{\mathfrak{P}}
is trivial, in which case
\varphi_{\mathfrak{P}}
is an element of
D_{\mathfrak{P}}
(hence in
G
). Furthermore, different choices of
\mathfrak{P}
lying above
\mathfrak{p}
yield conjugate Frobenius elements in
G
. The corresponding conjugacy class of
G
is what we call the Artin symbol of
\mathfrak{p}
, written as

\varphi_{\mathfrak{p}}=\Big[\frac{L/K}{\mathfrak{p}}\Big].

We emphasize that this is a well defined conjugacy class of
G
only when
\mathfrak{p}
is unramified.

Let
V
be a finite dimensional complex vector space, and consider a representation of
G
on
V
with character
\chi\in\mathrm{Rep}(G)
. If
I
is a normal subgroup of
G
, we write
V^{I}
for the subspace of all vectors in
V
on which
I
acts trivially. Then
G/I
(and hence
G
) acts on
V^{I}
as well. We denote the corresponding character of
G
by
\widetilde{\chi}\in\mathrm{Rep}(G)
.

Every representation of
G
on
V
restricts to a representation of
D_{\mathfrak{P}}
on
V
. Since
I_{\mathfrak{P}}
is a normal subgroup of
D_{\mathfrak{P}}
, we thus obtain a representation of
D_{\mathfrak{P}}/I_{\mathfrak{P}}
on
V^{I_{\mathfrak{P}}}
. If
\varphi_{\mathfrak{P}}\in D_{\mathfrak{P}}/I_{\mathfrak{P}}
is the Frobenius element of
\mathfrak{P}
, then we have the characteristic polynomial

\det(I-\varphi_{\mathfrak{P}}t;V^{I_{\mathfrak{P}}}),

where
I
the identity operator of
V^{I_{\mathfrak{P}}}
, and where
\varphi_{\mathfrak{P}}
is understood as an automorphism of the vector space
V^{I_{\mathfrak{P}}}
. The Artin
L
-function associated to
\chi
is defined by

(3.1)

L(s,\chi,L/K)=\prod_{\mathfrak{p}}\frac{1}{\det(I-\varphi_{\mathfrak{P}}\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{p}^{-s};V^{I_{\mathfrak{P}}})},

where the product is taken over all prime ideals
\mathfrak{p}
in
\mathcal{O}_{K}
. Therefore, the reciprocal of the Euler factor at
\mathfrak{p}
is a polynomial of degree
\dim V^{I_{\mathfrak{P}}}
. Different choices of
\mathfrak{P}
lying above
\mathfrak{p}
yield the same characteristic polynomial, so (
3.1
) is well defined.

We list some elementary properties of Artin
L
-functions. The proof of the following two propositions can be found in
[
14
, Proposition 10.4]

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
L/K
be a Galois extension of number fields with Galois group
G
. Then the following are true:

(1)

L(s,\mathbbm{1}_{G},L/K)=\zeta_{K}(s)
.

(2)

If
\phi,\psi\in\mathrm{Rep}(G)
, then
L(s,\phi\oplus\psi,L/K)=L(s,\phi,L/K)L(s,\psi,L/K)
.

(3)

Let
N
be a normal subgroup of
G
and
\mathop{\mathrm{Proj}}\colon G\to G/N
be the quotient map. If
\phi\in\mathrm{Rep}(G/N)
, then
\phi\circ\mathop{\mathrm{Proj}}\in\mathrm{Rep}(G)
, and
L(s,\phi,L^{N}/K)=L(s,\phi\circ\mathop{\mathrm{Proj}},L/K)
.

Another fundamental property of Artin
L
-functions is their invariance under induction.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
L/K
be a Galois extension of number fields with Galois group
G
. If
H
be a subgroup of
G
and
\chi
is a representation of
H
, then
L(s,\operatorname{Ind}_{H}^{G}\chi,L/K)=L(s,\chi,L/L^{H})
.

Applying
Propositions

3.2
and
3.1
to the regular representation

\mathrm{reg}_{G}=\operatorname{Ind}_{\{e_{G}\}}^{G}\mathbbm{1}_{\{e_{G}\}}=\bigoplus_{\chi\in\mathrm{Irr}(G)}\chi(1)\chi

of
G
, we obtain the important factorization:

(3.2)

\zeta_{L}(s)=\prod_{\chi\in\mathrm{Irr}(G)}L(s,\chi,L/K)^{\chi(1)}.

3.2.
The Artin conductor

For each
\chi\in\mathrm{Rep}(G)
, there is a distinguished ideal
\mathfrak{f}_{\chi}\subseteq\mathcal{O}_{K}
, the Artin conductor of
\chi
, defined as follows. Let
V
be the vector space associated to
\chi
. Let
\mathfrak{p}
be a prime ideal in
\mathcal{O}_{K}
and let
\mathfrak{P}
be a prime ideal in
\mathcal{O}_{L}
that lies above
\mathfrak{p}
. Let
G_{0}\supset G_{1}\supseteq G_{2}\supseteq\cdots
be the filtration of ramification groups corresponding to
\mathfrak{P}
, where
G_{0}
is the inertia group
I_{\mathfrak{P}}
. The Artin conductor of
\chi
is

(3.3)

\mathfrak{f}_{\chi}=\prod_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathfrak{p}^{\mathrm{ord}_{\mathfrak{p}}(\mathfrak{f}_{\chi})},\qquad\mathrm{ord}_{\mathfrak{p}}(\mathfrak{f}_{\chi})=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\frac{|G_{i}|}{|G_{0}|}(\chi(1)-\dim V^{G_{i}}).

(The number
\mathrm{ord}_{\mathfrak{p}}(\mathfrak{f}_{\chi})
is always a nonnegative integer.) The conductor-discriminant formula states that if
\mathfrak{D}_{L/K}
is the relative discriminant of
L/K
, then

(3.4)

\mathfrak{D}_{L/K}=\prod_{\chi\in\mathrm{Irr}(G)}\mathfrak{f}_{\chi}^{\chi(1)},\qquad D_{L}/D_{K}^{|G|}=\prod_{\chi\in\mathrm{Irr}(G)}\mathrm{N}_{K/\mathbb{Q}}\mathfrak{f}_{\chi}^{\chi(1)}.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
V
be a finite dimensional complex vector space and
\chi
be the character of a representation of
G
on
V
. Let
\mathfrak{p}
be a prime ideal in
\mathcal{O}_{K}
and
\mathfrak{P}
be a prime ideal in
\mathcal{O}_{L}
lying over
\mathfrak{p}
. Then
\dim V^{I_{\mathfrak{P}}}<\dim V
if and only if
\mathfrak{p}\mid\mathfrak{f}_{\chi}
.

Proof.

Recall (
3.3
). If
\dim V^{I_{\mathfrak{P}}}<\dim V=\chi(1)
, then
\mathrm{ord}_{\mathfrak{p}}(\mathfrak{f}_{\chi})\geq 1
, whence
\mathfrak{p}\mid\mathfrak{f}_{\chi}
. Conversely, if
\dim V^{I_{\mathfrak{P}}}=\dim V
, i.e.,
V^{I_{\mathfrak{P}}}=V
, then
I_{\mathfrak{P}}=G_{0}
acts on
V
trivially. Thus, each
G_{i}
must also act on
V
trivially, i.e.,
V^{G_{i}}=V
for all
i\geq 0
. This implies that
\mathrm{ord}_{\mathfrak{p}}(\mathfrak{f}_{\chi})=0
.
∎

Here is a useful result on Artin conductors associated to tensor products.

Lemma 3.4
.

If
\chi_{1},\chi_{2}\in\mathrm{Irr}(G)
, then
\mathfrak{f}_{\chi_{1}\otimes\chi_{2}}\mid\mathfrak{f}_{\chi_{1}}^{\chi_{2}(1)}\mathfrak{f}_{\chi_{2}}^{\chi_{1}(1)}
.

Proof.

A stronger result is proved in
[
11
, Lemma 6.6]
.
∎

3.3.
Frobenius eigenvalues

Lemma

3.3
states that
\dim V^{I_{\mathfrak{P}}}<\dim V
if and only if
\mathfrak{p}
divides the Artin conductor
\mathfrak{f}_{\chi}
. With this in mind, if
\{\alpha_{1,\chi}(\mathfrak{p}),\ldots,\alpha_{\dim V^{I_{\mathfrak{P}}},\chi}(\mathfrak{p})\}
is an unordered list of the eigenvalues of the action of
\varphi_{\mathfrak{P}}
on
V^{I_{\mathfrak{P}}}
, then we define

(3.5)

A_{\chi}(\mathfrak{p})=\begin{cases}\{\alpha_{1,\chi}(\mathfrak{p}),\ldots,\alpha_{\dim V,\chi}(\mathfrak{p})\}&\mbox{if $\mathfrak{p}\nmid\mathfrak{f}_{\chi}$,}\\
\{\alpha_{1,\chi}(\mathfrak{p}),\ldots,\alpha_{\dim V^{I_{\mathfrak{P}}},\chi}(\mathfrak{p}),\underbrace{0,0,\ldots,0}_{\textup{$\dim V-\dim V^{I_{\mathfrak{P}}}$ times}}\}&\mbox{if $\mathfrak{p}\mid\mathfrak{f}_{\chi}$.}\end{cases}

Therefore, we may always write (with no ordering)

A_{\chi}(\mathfrak{p})=\{\alpha_{1,\chi}(\mathfrak{p}),\ldots,\alpha_{\chi(1),\chi}(\mathfrak{p})\}.

We shall refer to the numbers
\alpha_{j,\chi}(\mathfrak{p})
either as the local roots or the Frobenius eigenvalues of
\chi
at
\mathfrak{p}
. Since
G
is finite, we have that

(3.6)

|\alpha_{j,\chi}(\mathfrak{p})|\begin{cases}=1&\mbox{if $\mathfrak{p}\nmid\mathfrak{f}_{\chi}$,}\\
\in\{0,1\}&\mbox{if $\mathfrak{p}\mid\mathfrak{f}_{\chi}$.}\end{cases}

Therefore, we can rewrite (
3.1
) as

(3.7)

\displaystyle L(s,\chi,L/K)

\displaystyle=\prod_{\mathfrak{p}}L_{\mathfrak{p}}(s,\chi,L/K)=\sum_{\mathfrak{a}}\frac{\lambda_{\chi}(\mathfrak{a})}{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{a}^{s}},

\displaystyle L_{\mathfrak{p}}(s,\chi,L/K)

\displaystyle=\prod_{j=1}^{\chi(1)}\frac{1}{1-\alpha_{j,\chi}(\mathfrak{p})\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{p}^{-s}}=1+\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda_{\chi}(\mathfrak{p}^{j})}{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{p}^{js}}.\hskip-2.84526pt

It follows from (
3.6
) that
L(s,\chi)
, in both its Euler product and Dirichlet series expansions, converges absolutely for
\mathrm{Re}(s)>1
.

Define

a_{\chi}(\mathfrak{a})=\begin{cases}\sum_{j=1}^{\chi(1)}\alpha_{j,\chi}(\mathfrak{p})^{\ell}&\mbox{if there exists a prime ideal $\mathfrak{p}$ and $\ell\in\mathbb{N}$ such that $\mathfrak{a}=\mathfrak{p}^{\ell}$,}\\
0&\mbox{otherwise}\end{cases}

and

\Lambda_{K}(\mathfrak{a})=\begin{cases}\log\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{p}&\mbox{if there exists a prime ideal $\mathfrak{p}$ and $\ell\in\mathbb{N}$ such that $\mathfrak{a}=\mathfrak{p}^{\ell}$,}\\
0&\mbox{otherwise.}\end{cases}

These numbers arise in the Dirichlet series expansion

(3.8)

\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(s,\chi,L/K)=\sum_{\mathfrak{a}}\frac{a_{\chi}(\mathfrak{a})\Lambda_{K}(\mathfrak{a})}{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{a}^{s}},\qquad\mathrm{Re}(s)>1.

Per (
3.6
), we have the uniform bound

(3.9)

|a_{\chi}(\mathfrak{a})|\leq\chi(1).

Our next result helps us to efficiently handle
a_{\chi_{1}\otimes\chi_{2}}(\mathfrak{a})
, where
\chi_{1},\chi_{2}\in\mathrm{Rep}(G)
.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a finite group and
\chi\in\mathrm{Rep}(\mathcal{G})
be the character of a representation on a finite dimensional complex vector space
U
. Let
I
be a normal subgroup of
\mathcal{G}
, so that
\mathcal{G}
acts on
U^{I}
as well, and let
\widetilde{\chi}
be the character of the corresponding representation of
\mathcal{G}
(equivalently,
\mathcal{G}/I)
on
U^{I}
. Then for any
g\in\mathcal{G}
, one has

(3.10)

\widetilde{\chi}(g)=\frac{1}{|I|}\sum_{\alpha\in I}\chi(g\alpha).

Proof.

Choose a basis
\{\mathbf{u}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{u}_{r}\}
of
U^{I}
and extend it to a basis
\{\mathbf{u}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{u}_{r},\mathbf{u}_{r+1},\ldots,\mathbf{u}_{n}\}
of
U
. Let
h=g\sum_{\alpha\in I}\alpha
. The right-hand side of (
3.10
) is exactly
1/|I|
times the trace of
h
on
U
. For each
u_{i}
(
1\leq i\leq r
), we have
h\mathbf{u}_{i}=g\sum_{\alpha\in I}\alpha\mathbf{u}_{i}=|I|g\mathbf{u}_{i}
, because each
\alpha\in I
fixes
\mathbf{u}_{i}
by assumption. Therefore, the action of
h
on
U^{I}
is just
|I|
times the action of
g
on
{U^{I}}
. On the other hand, for each
\mathbf{u}_{j}
(
r+1\leq j\leq n
), the vector

\sum_{\alpha\in I}\alpha\mathbf{u}_{j}

lies in
U^{I}
. Indeed, if
\beta\in I
then

\beta\sum_{\alpha\in I}\alpha\mathbf{u}_{j}=\sum_{\alpha\in I}\beta\alpha\mathbf{u}_{j}=\sum_{\alpha\in I}\alpha\mathbf{u}_{j},\qquad\textup{hence}\quad h\mathbf{u}_{j}=g\sum_{\alpha\in I}\alpha\mathbf{u}_{j}\in U^{I}.

This means that the matrix representation of
h
is of the form

\begin{pmatrix}|I|\cdot g|_{U^{I}}&*\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}

with trace
|I|\cdot\widetilde{\chi}(g)
, where
g|_{U^{I}}
denotes the restriction of
g
to
U^{I}
. This proves the lemma.
∎

Lemma 3.6
.

If
\chi\in\mathrm{Rep}(G)
, then
a_{\chi\otimes\overline{\chi}}(\mathfrak{a})\geq 0
.

Proof.

Let
\mathfrak{p}
be a prime ideal of
\mathcal{O}_{K}
, and let
\mathfrak{P}
be a prime ideal of
\mathcal{O}_{L}
that lies above
\mathfrak{p}
. Let
V
be the underlying finite-dimensional complex vector space on which
G
acts via the representation
\chi
. Then
G
also acts on
V\otimes V^{*}
via
\chi\otimes\overline{\chi}
. Therefore, the decomposition group
D_{\mathfrak{P}}
acts on
V\otimes V^{*}
. Since
I_{\mathfrak{P}}
is a normal subgroup of
D_{\mathfrak{P}}
, we know that
D_{\mathfrak{P}}/I_{\mathfrak{P}}
acts on
W=(V\otimes V^{*})^{I_{\mathfrak{P}}}
. If
\varphi_{\mathfrak{P}}\in D_{\mathfrak{P}}/I_{\mathfrak{P}}
is the Frobenius element of
\mathfrak{P}
, then
a_{\chi\otimes\overline{\chi}}(\mathfrak{p}^{\ell})
equals the sum of
m
th powers of eigenvalues of
\varphi_{\mathfrak{P}}|_{W}
, which equals the trace of
\varphi_{\mathfrak{P}}^{\ell}|_{W}
.
Let
\varphi\in D_{\mathfrak{P}}
be any element whose image under the quotient map
D_{\mathfrak{P}}\to D_{\mathfrak{P}}/I_{\mathfrak{P}}
is
\varphi_{\mathfrak{P}}
. Now, applying
Lemma

3.5
with
\mathcal{G}=D_{\mathfrak{P}}
,
I=I_{\mathfrak{P}}
,
U=V\otimes V^{*}
, and
g=\varphi^{\ell}
, we obtain

a_{\chi\otimes\overline{\chi}}(\mathfrak{p}^{\ell})=\frac{1}{|I_{\mathfrak{P}}|}\sum_{\alpha\in I_{\mathfrak{P}}}(\chi\otimes\overline{\chi})(\varphi^{\ell}\alpha)=\frac{1}{|I_{\mathfrak{P}}|}\sum_{\alpha\in I_{\mathfrak{P}}}\chi(\varphi^{\ell}\alpha)\overline{\chi(\varphi^{\ell}\alpha)}=\frac{1}{|I_{\mathfrak{P}}|}\sum_{\alpha\in I_{\mathfrak{P}}}|\chi(\varphi^{\ell}\alpha)|^{2}\geq 0,

as desired. If
\mathfrak{a}
is not of the form
\mathfrak{p}^{\ell}
, then
a_{\chi\otimes\overline{\chi}}(\mathfrak{a})=0
.
∎

Lemma 3.7
.

If
\chi_{1},\chi_{2}\in\mathrm{Rep}(G)
, then
|a_{\chi_{1}\otimes\chi_{2}}(\mathfrak{a})|^{2}\leq a_{\chi_{1}\otimes\overline{\chi}_{1}}(\mathfrak{a})a_{\chi_{2}\otimes\overline{\chi}_{2}}(\mathfrak{a})
.

Proof.

Again, we may assume that
\mathfrak{a}=\mathfrak{p}^{\ell}
, where
\mathfrak{p}
is a prime ideal in
\mathcal{O}_{K}
. Let
V_{1}
(resp.
V_{2}
) be the complex vector space associated to
\chi_{1}
(resp.
\chi_{2}
). Following the same steps in
Lemma

3.6
and applying
Lemma

3.5
with
\mathcal{G}=D_{\mathfrak{P}}
,
I=I_{\mathfrak{P}}
,
U=V_{1}\otimes V_{2}
, and
g=\varphi^{\ell}
, we obtain

(3.11)

a_{\chi_{1}\otimes\chi_{2}}(\mathfrak{p}^{\ell})=\frac{1}{|I_{\mathfrak{P}}|}\sum_{\alpha\in I_{\mathfrak{P}}}(\chi_{1}\otimes\chi_{2})(\varphi^{\ell}\alpha)=\frac{1}{|I_{\mathfrak{P}}|}\sum_{\alpha\in I_{\mathfrak{P}}}\chi_{1}(\varphi^{\ell}\alpha)\chi_{2}(\varphi^{\ell}\alpha).

Now, applying the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, we obtain the bound

\displaystyle|a_{\chi_{1}\otimes\chi_{2}}(\mathfrak{p}^{\ell})|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{|I_{\mathfrak{P}}|^{2}}\Big(\sum_{\alpha\in I_{\mathfrak{P}}}|\chi_{1}(\varphi^{\ell}\alpha)|^{2}\Big)\Big(\sum_{\alpha\in I_{\mathfrak{P}}}|\chi_{2}(\varphi^{\ell}\alpha)|^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle=\Big(\frac{1}{|I_{\mathfrak{P}}|}\sum_{\alpha\in I_{\mathfrak{P}}}|\chi_{1}(\varphi^{\ell}\alpha)|^{2}\Big)\Big(\frac{1}{|I_{\mathfrak{P}}|}\sum_{\alpha\in I_{\mathfrak{P}}}|\chi_{2}(\varphi^{\ell}\alpha)|^{2}\Big)=a_{\chi_{1}\otimes\overline{\chi}_{1}}(\mathfrak{p}^{\ell})a_{\chi_{2}\otimes\overline{\chi}_{2}}(\mathfrak{p}^{\ell}).\hskip 11.38109pt\qed

3.4.
Character orthogonality

For
\chi_{1},\chi_{2}\in\mathrm{Rep}(G)
, we define

(3.12)

\langle\chi_{1},\chi_{2}\rangle=\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{g\in G}\chi_{1}(g)\overline{\chi}_{2}(g).

Character orthogonality for
G
is the statement that if
\chi_{1},\chi_{2}\in\mathrm{Irr}(G)
, then

\langle\chi_{1},\chi_{2}\rangle=\begin{cases}1&\mbox{if $\chi_{1}=\chi_{2}$,}\\
0&\mbox{if $\chi_{1}\neq\chi_{2}$.}\end{cases}

Maschke proved that if
\chi\in\mathrm{Rep}(G)
, then there exist
m\in\mathbb{N}
and
\chi_{1},\ldots,\chi_{m}\in\mathrm{Irr}(G)
(the irreducible constituents of
\chi
) such that
\chi=\bigoplus_{j=1}^{m}\chi_{j}
. In particular, if
\chi_{1},\chi_{2}\in\mathrm{Irr}(G)
, then such a decomposition exists for
\chi_{1}\otimes\chi_{2}
.

Let
\chi,\chi^{\prime}\in\mathrm{Rep}(G)
with direct sum decompositions
\chi=\bigoplus_{j=1}^{m}\chi_{j}
and
\chi^{\prime}=\bigoplus_{k=1}^{n}\psi_{k}
of irreducible constituents. The (conjugate) bilinearity of the inner product implies that

\langle\chi,\chi^{\prime}\rangle=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\langle\chi_{j},\psi_{k}\rangle.

If
\psi\in\mathrm{Rep}(G)
and
\tau\in\mathrm{Irr}(G)
, then the multiplicity of
\tau
as a constituent of
\psi
is
\langle\psi,\tau\rangle
.

3.4.1.
Irreducible constituents

Lemma 3.8
.

If
\chi,\psi\in\mathrm{Irr}(G)
, then

(1)

\mathbbm{1}
is a constituent of
\chi\otimes\psi
if and only if
\psi=\overline{\chi}
,

(2)

the multiplicity of
\mathbbm{1}
as a constituent of
\chi\otimes\overline{\chi}
is one, and

(3)

if
\chi(1)\geq 2
, then
\chi\otimes\overline{\chi}
contains an irreducible constituent
\tau\notin\{\chi,\overline{\chi},\mathbbm{1}\}
. In this case,
\chi
is an irreducible constituent of
\chi\otimes\tau
, but
\mathbbm{1}
is not.

Proof.

The first and second claims follow from the computation

\langle\chi\otimes\psi,\mathbbm{1}\rangle=\langle\chi,\overline{\psi}\rangle=\begin{cases}1&\mbox{if $\chi=\overline{\psi}$,}\\
0&\mbox{otherwise.}\end{cases}

For the third claim, assume that
\chi\in\mathrm{Irr}(G)
and
\chi(1)\geq 2
. Let
\tau\in\mathrm{Irr}(G)
be an irreducible constituent of
\chi\otimes\overline{\chi}
. Suppose to the contrary that there exist integers
J_{1},J_{2}\geq 0
such that
J_{1}+J_{2}\geq 1
and

\chi\otimes\overline{\chi}=\mathbbm{1}\oplus\Big(\bigoplus_{j=1}^{J_{1}}\chi\Big)\oplus\Big(\bigoplus_{j=1}^{J_{2}}\overline{\chi}\Big).

Comparing the dimensions of both sides, we arrive at
\chi(1)^{2}=1+(J_{1}+J_{2})\chi(1)
. This implies that
\gcd(\chi(1)^{2},\chi(1))=1
, in which case
\chi(1)=1
, a contradiction. Therefore,
\tau
must lie in
\mathrm{Irr}(G)-\{\chi,\overline{\chi},\mathbbm{1}\}
. Since
\tau\neq\overline{\chi}
, we have
\langle\chi\otimes\tau,\mathbbm{1}\rangle=\langle\tau,\overline{\chi}\rangle=0
. Since
\tau
is a constituent of
\chi\otimes\overline{\chi}
, we have
\langle\chi\otimes\tau,\chi\rangle=\langle\tau,\chi\otimes\overline{\chi}\rangle\geq 1
. This finishes the proof of the third claim.
∎

3.4.2.
The Artin symbol

For
\mathfrak{p}
a prime ideal in
\mathcal{O}_{K}
, let
\mathfrak{P}
be any prime ideal in
\mathcal{O}_{L}
lying above
\mathfrak{p}
, and let
I_{\mathfrak{P}}
be its inertia group. For
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
, define

(3.13)

\tau_{C}(\mathfrak{p}^{\ell})=\frac{1}{|I_{\mathfrak{P}}|}\sum_{\alpha\in I_{\mathfrak{P}}}\frac{|C|}{|G|}\sum_{\chi\in\operatorname{Irr}(G)}\overline{\chi}(C)\chi(\varphi_{\mathfrak{P}}^{\ell}\alpha).

By character orthogonality, we always have
0\leq\tau(\mathfrak{p}^{\ell})\leq 1
. Furthermore, if
\mathfrak{p}
is unramified in
L
, then
I_{\mathfrak{P}}
is trivial, and in this case we have

(3.14)

\tau(\mathfrak{p}^{\ell})=\frac{|C|}{|G|}\sum_{\chi\in\operatorname{Irr}(G)}\overline{\chi}(C)\chi(\varphi_{\mathfrak{P}}^{\ell})=\begin{cases}1&\mbox{if $[\frac{L/K}{\mathfrak{p}}]^{\ell}=C$,}\\
0&\mbox{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Thus, when
\mathfrak{p}
is unramified,
\tau(\mathfrak{p})
equals the indicator function of
\mathfrak{p}
having Artin symbol
C
. For all other prime powers
\mathfrak{p}^{\ell}
, we have that
\tau(\mathfrak{p}^{\ell})\in[0,1]
.

3.5.
Meromorphic continuation and functional equation

For
\chi\in\mathrm{Rep}(G)
, write

\mathcal{A}_{\chi}=D_{K}^{\chi(1)}\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{f}_{\chi},\qquad\delta(\chi)=\langle\chi,\mathbbm{1}_{G}\rangle

(see (
3.12
)). Let
a=a(\chi)
be the dimension of the +1 eigenspace of complex conjugation. For each archimedean place
v
of
K
whose corresponding completion is
K_{v}
, we define

L_{v}(s,\chi,L/K)=\begin{cases}\Gamma_{\mathbb{R}}(s)^{\chi(1)}\Gamma_{\mathbb{R}}(s+1)^{\chi(1)}&\text{ if }K_{v}=\mathbb{C},\\
\Gamma_{\mathbb{R}}(s)^{a}\Gamma_{\mathbb{R}}(s+1)^{\chi(1)-a}&\text{ if }K_{v}=\mathbb{R},\end{cases}\qquad\Gamma_{\mathbb{R}}(s)=\pi^{-\frac{s}{2}}\Gamma\Big(\frac{s}{2}\Big).

We also define the numbers
\mu_{\chi}(j)\in\{0,1\}
by

L_{\infty}(s,\chi,L/K)=\prod_{v\text{ archimedian}}L_{v}(s,\chi,L/K)=\prod_{j=1}^{\chi(1)n_{K}}\Gamma_{\mathbb{R}}(s+\mu_{\chi}(j)).

The completed
L
-function

\Lambda(s,\chi,L/K)=(s(1-s))^{\delta(\chi)}\mathcal{A}_{\chi}^{s/2}L_{\infty}(s,\chi,L/K)L(s,\chi,L/K)

admits a meromorphic continuation to
\mathbb{C}
, and there exists a number
W(\chi)\in\mathbb{C}
of modulus
1
such that if
\Lambda(s,\chi)
is holomorphic at
s
, then

(3.15)

\Lambda(s,\chi,L/K)=W(\chi)\Lambda(1-s,\overline{\chi},L/K).

Here,
\overline{\chi}
is the complex conjugate of
\chi
.

3.6.
The character
\chi_{1}

Recall the definitions of
\beta_{1}
and
\chi_{1}
in (
1.3
) and (
1.5
). Note that since
\zeta_{L}(s)
always has real trivial zeros,
\beta_{1}
is well defined. If
\beta_{1}
is not a simple zero, then
\chi_{1}:=\mathbbm{1}_{G}
. To show that
\chi_{1}
is well defined when
\beta_{1}
is a simple zero, we need the following theorem from Stark.

Theorem 3.9

(
[
20
, Theorem 3]
)
.

Let
L/K
be a Galois extension of number fields with Galois group
G
, and let
\chi\in\mathrm{Irr}(G)
. If
\rho
is a simple zero of
\zeta_{L}(s)
, then
L(s,\chi,L/K)
is holomorphic at
s=\rho
. Also, there is a field
F
, cyclic over
K
and contained in
L
, such that for any tower of fields
K\subseteq E\subseteq L
,
\zeta_{E}(\rho)=0
if and only if
F\subseteq E
. If
\rho
is real, then
F
is either
K
itself or quadratic over
K
.

The well-definedness of
\chi_{1}
(when
\beta_{1}
is simple) now follows, as the next lemma shows:

Lemma 3.10
.

Let
\beta_{1}
be as in (
1.3
). If
\beta_{1}
is simple, then there exists a unique character
\chi_{0}\in\mathrm{Irr}(G)
such that
\beta_{1}
is a zero of
L(s,\chi_{0},L/K)
. Furthermore,
\chi_{0}=\overline{\chi}_{0}
and
\chi_{0}(1)=1
.

Proof.

Apply
Theorem

3.9
with
\rho=\beta_{1}
. Since
\beta_{1}
is a simple zero and each Artin
L
-function
L(s,\chi,L/K)
is analytic at
s=\beta_{1}
, we see from the factorization of
\zeta_{L}(s)
in (
3.2
)
that there exists a unique
\chi_{0}\in\mathrm{Irr}(G)
such that
L(\beta_{1},\chi_{0},L/K)=0
. It remains to show that
\chi_{0}
is real and one-dimensional. Let
F
be the unique intermediate subfield of
L/K
with the desired property as defined in
Theorem

3.9
. Since
\beta_{1}
is real,
F
is either
K
or quadratic over
K
. If
F=K
, then
\zeta_{K}(\beta_{1})=0
, and we see that
\chi_{0}=\mathbbm{1}_{G}
. If
F\neq K
, then
\mathrm{Gal}(L/F)
is a normal subgroup of index 2 in
G
since
F/K
is quadratic. Thus,
\mathrm{Gal}(F/K)
is cyclic of order 2, so it has a unique nontrivial character
\phi_{1}
, which is necessarily real and one-dimensional. Let

\mathrm{Proj}\colon G\to G/\mathrm{Gal}(L/F)\cong\mathrm{Gal}(F/K)

be the quotient map. Then
\phi_{1}^{\prime}:=\phi_{1}\circ\mathrm{Proj}
is a character of
G
. Since
\zeta_{F}(\beta_{1})=0
and
\zeta_{K}(\beta_{1})\neq 0
, we see from the factorization
\zeta_{F}(s)=\zeta_{K}(s)L(s,\phi_{1},F/K)
that
L(\beta_{1},\phi_{1}^{\prime},L/K)=L(\beta_{1},\phi_{1},F/K)=0
by
Proposition

3.1
. Hence,
\chi_{0}=\phi_{1}^{\prime}
. In either case,
\chi_{0}
is real and one-dimensional. This finishes the proof.
∎

Our definition of
\beta_{1}
and
\chi_{1}
has several advantages. First, it does not rely on any zero free region of Artin L-functions. Second, it is well-defined for any Galois extension of number fields, without assuming AHC. Third, the notion of
\chi_{1}
is well-behaved under base change (see
Lemma

5.3
below).

4.
Consequences of analytic continuation

Let
L/K
be a Galois extension of number fields with Galois group
G
. In this section, we shall assume that
L/K
satisfies AHC, so that if
\chi\in\mathrm{Rep}(G)
, then
\Lambda(s,\chi)
is entire of order 1. It follows that there exist
B_{1}(\chi),B(\chi)\in\mathbb{C}
such that
\Lambda(s,\chi,L/K)
has the Hadamard factorization

(4.1)

\Lambda(s,\chi,L/K)=e^{B_{1}(\chi)+B(\chi)s}\prod_{\Lambda(\rho,\chi,L/K)=0}\Big(1-\frac{s}{\rho}\Big)e^{s/\rho}.

The zeros
\rho=\beta+i\gamma
of
\Lambda(s,\chi)
are the nontrivial zeros of
L(s,\chi)
. In all sums, products, and counts over nontrivial zeros
\rho
of
L(s,\chi)
, the zeros are counted with multiplicity.

Taking the logarithmic derivative of (
4.1
), we obtain

B(\chi)+\sum_{\rho}\Big(\frac{1}{s-\rho}+\frac{1}{\rho}\Big)=\frac{\log\mathcal{A}_{\chi}}{2}+\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(s,\chi,L/K)+\frac{L_{\infty}^{\prime}}{L_{\infty}}(s,\chi,L/K)+\frac{\delta(\chi)}{s}+\frac{\delta(\chi)}{s-1}.

From
[
8
, Proposition 5.7(3)]
, we have

\operatorname{Re}B(\chi)=-\sum_{\rho}\operatorname{Re}\frac{1}{\rho},

hence

(4.2)

\sum_{\rho}\operatorname{Re}\Big(\frac{1}{s-\rho}\Big)=\frac{\log\mathcal{A}_{\chi}}{2}+\operatorname{Re}\Big(\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(s,\chi,L/K)+\frac{L_{\infty}^{\prime}}{L_{\infty}}(s,\chi,L/K)+\frac{\delta(\chi)}{s}+\frac{\delta(\chi)}{s-1}\Big).

4.1.
Counting zeros

In what follows, we use the notation in (
1.2
).

Lemma 4.1

(Lemma, p. 1203 of
[
7
]
)
.

If
\operatorname{Re}(z)\geq\frac{1}{2}
, then

\operatorname{Re}\Big(\frac{\Gamma_{\mathbb{R}}^{\prime}}{\Gamma_{\mathbb{R}}}(z)\Big)\leq\frac{\log|z|}{2}-\frac{\gamma_{\mathbb{Q}}+\log\pi}{2},

where
\gamma_{\mathbb{Q}}=0.57721\ldots
is the Euler-Mascheroni constant.

Lemma 4.2
.

If
t\in\mathbb{R}
and
\chi\in\mathrm{Irr}(G)
, then

\#\{\beta+i\gamma:\beta\geq 0,|\gamma-t|\leq 1,L(\beta+i\gamma,\chi)=0\}\ll\log(D_{K}^{\chi(1)}\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{f}_{\chi}(|t|+3)^{\chi(1)n_{K}}).

Proof.

The proof is similar to that of
[
8
, Proposition 5.7(1)]
.
∎

Lemma 4.3
.

If
\chi,\phi\in\mathrm{Irr}(G)
and
0<\eta<1
, then

\sum_{\mathfrak{a}}\frac{|a_{\chi\otimes\phi}(\mathfrak{a})|\Lambda_{K}(\mathfrak{a})}{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{a}^{1+\eta}}\leq\frac{1}{\eta}+\frac{\log q_{L/K}}{2}.

Proof.

First, we address the case when
\phi=\overline{\chi}
. If
\chi(1)=1
and
K=\mathbb{Q}
, then
\chi\otimes\overline{\chi}=\mathbbm{1}_{G}
and this reduces to the well-known inequality

-\frac{\zeta^{\prime}}{\zeta}(1+\eta)\leq\frac{1}{\eta},\qquad 0<\eta\leq 1.

Otherwise, we have that
|a_{\chi\otimes\phi}(\mathfrak{p}^{\ell})|=a_{\chi\otimes\overline{\chi}}(\mathfrak{p}^{\ell})\geq 0
, and the sum to be estimated equals

-\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(1+\eta,\chi\otimes\overline{\chi},L/K)=\frac{1}{\eta}+\frac{1}{1+\eta}+\frac{L_{\infty}^{\prime}}{L_{\infty}}(1+\eta,\chi\otimes\overline{\chi},L/K)+\frac{\log(D_{K}^{\chi(1)^{2}}\mathfrak{f}_{\chi\otimes\overline{\chi}})}{2}\\
-\sum_{\Lambda(\rho,\chi\otimes\overline{\chi},L/K)=0}\mathrm{Re}\Big(\frac{1}{1+\eta-\rho}\Big).

Note that since either
\chi(1)>1
or
K\neq\mathbb{Q}
,
L_{\infty}(s,\chi\otimes\overline{\chi},L/K)
has at least two Gamma factors. Therefore, since
\mu_{\chi\otimes\overline{\chi}}(j)\in\{0,1\}
and
0<\eta\leq 1
, we find using
Lemma

4.1
that

\frac{1}{1+\eta}+\frac{L_{\infty}^{\prime}}{L_{\infty}}(1+\eta,\chi\otimes\overline{\chi},L/K)\leq 0.

Finally, since
\mathrm{Re}(\rho)\leq 1
, we find that
-\mathrm{Re}(\frac{1}{1+\eta-\rho})\leq 0
. We now conclude from
Lemma

3.4
that

-\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(1+\eta,\chi\otimes\overline{\chi},L/K)\leq\frac{1}{\eta}+\frac{\log q_{L/K}}{2}.

The case where
\phi\neq\overline{\chi}
now follows from
Lemma

3.7
, the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, and our work above for
\chi\otimes\overline{\chi}
.
∎

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
s=\sigma+it
with
\sigma\geq 1
. If
0<\eta\leq 1
and
\chi,\phi\in\mathrm{Irr}(G)
, then

\displaystyle N(\eta;s,\chi\otimes\phi)

\displaystyle=\#\{\rho=\beta+i\gamma\colon\Lambda(s,\chi\otimes\phi,L/K)=0,~|s-\rho|\leq\eta\}

\displaystyle\leq 8+4\eta\log(q_{L/K}(|t|+3)^{d_{G}^{2}n_{K}}).

Proof.

It suffices to consider
\sigma=1
. We begin with the estimate

\frac{\#\{\rho\colon\Lambda(\rho,\chi\otimes\phi,L/K)=0,~|\rho-(1+it)|\leq\eta\}}{4\eta}\leq\sum_{\Lambda(\rho,\chi\otimes\phi,L/K)=0}\frac{1+\eta-\beta}{(1+\eta-\beta)^{2}+(t-\gamma)^{2}}.

The summands are
\mathrm{Re}(1/(1+\eta+it-\rho))
, which are nonnegative. Thus, by a calculation similar to
Lemma

4.3
, the right-hand side is

\leq\frac{\log q_{L/K}}{2}+\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{Re}\Big(\frac{L_{\infty}^{\prime}}{L_{\infty}}(1+\eta+it,\chi\otimes\phi,L/K)\Big)+\sum_{\mathfrak{a}}\frac{|a_{\chi\otimes\phi}(\mathfrak{a})|\Lambda_{K}(\mathfrak{a})}{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{a}^{1+\eta}}+\frac{1}{\eta}+1.

By

4.1
and
4.3

\leq\frac{2}{\eta}+\log q_{L/K}+d_{G}^{2}n_{K}\log(|t|+3)
.
∎

Schur polynomials and Dirichlet coefficients

A partition

is a sequence of nonincreasing nonnegative integers
\mu_{1}\geq\mu_{2}\geq\cdots
with finitely many nonzero terms. For a partition
\mu
, let
\ell(\mu)=\#\{i\colon\mu_{i}\neq 0\}
and

and a partition
\mu

By convention, if

is identically
1
. Also, if
\ell(\mu)>n
, then
s_{\mu}(\{\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}\})
is identically

. If
\{\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{m}\}
and
\{\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{n}\}
are sets of complex numbers of modulus less than

[
3
, (38.1)]
states

(7.2)

\prod_{i=1}^{m}\prod_{j=1}^{n}\frac{1}{1-\alpha_{i}\beta_{j}}=\sum_{\mu}s_{\mu}(\{\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{m}\})s_{\mu}(\{\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{n}\}).

be a Galois extension of number fields with Galois group

. Hence, (

for the associated vector spaces. If

Lemma 7.3
.

Let
\mathfrak{a}
be a nonzero ideal of
\mathcal{O}_{K}
, and write
\omega(\mathfrak{a})
for the number of distinct prime ideals in
\mathcal{O}_{K}
dividing
\mathfrak{a}
. Then for every
\delta>0
, we have
\omega(\mathfrak{a})\leq e^{1+1/\delta}n_{K}+\delta\log\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{a}
.

Proof.

We may assume that
\mathfrak{a}
is squarefree and
\omega(\mathfrak{a})\geq e^{1+1/\delta}n_{K}
. For an integer
i\geq 1
, let
p_{i}
be the
i
-th rational prime. The function
\omega(\mathfrak{a})
is largest if for each
i
, there are
n_{K}
distinct prime ideals in the factorization of
\mathfrak{a}
lying above
p_{i}
, say
\mathfrak{p}_{i}^{(1)},\ldots,\mathfrak{p}_{i}^{(n_{K})}
, each of which has norm
p_{i}
.

First, suppose
\mathfrak{a}
is of the form
\mathfrak{a}=(\mathfrak{p}_{1}^{(1)}\cdots\mathfrak{p}_{1}^{(n_{K})})\cdots(\mathfrak{p}_{m}^{(1)}\cdots\mathfrak{p}_{m}^{(n_{K})})
, in which case
\omega(\mathfrak{a})=n_{K}m
.
Then

\log\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{a}=n_{K}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\log p_{j}\geq n_{K}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\log j\geq n_{K}m(\log m-1)\geq\frac{n_{K}m}{\delta}.

Therefore,
\omega(\mathfrak{a})\leq\delta\log\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{a}
in this case.

Otherwise, suppose
\mathfrak{a}
is of the form

\mathfrak{a}=(\mathfrak{p}_{1}^{(1)}\cdots\mathfrak{p}_{1}^{(n_{K})})\cdots(\mathfrak{p}_{m}^{(1)}\cdots\mathfrak{p}_{m}^{(n_{K})})(\mathfrak{p}_{m+1}^{(1)}\cdots\mathfrak{p}_{m+1}^{(\ell)}),

where
\ell<n_{K}
and
m=\left\lfloor\omega(\mathfrak{a})/n_{K}\right\rfloor
. Then we may apply the previous case to
\mathfrak{a}
with all the factors
\mathfrak{p}_{m+1}^{(j)}
removed. Therefore, we obtain
\omega(\mathfrak{a})\leq\delta\log\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{a}+n_{K}
. Since we assumed that
\omega(\mathfrak{a})\geq e^{1+1/\delta}n_{K}
, we conclude that
\omega(\mathfrak{a})\leq e^{1+1/\delta}n_{K}+\delta\log\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{a}
for all
\mathfrak{a}
.
∎

In what follows, we define

\delta=\frac{1}{2\operatorname{Log}d}.

Let

Factors associated with postoperative coronal imbalance in adult patients with severe and rigid scoliosis: a retrospective study
Authors: Congying Zou, Luming Tao, Lei Zang, Yong Hai
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
BackgroundExtensive studies examined coronal imbalance (CIB) in adolescent and adult degenerative scoliosis; however, few studies addresses on CIB in adult with severe rigid scoliosis (SRS). Therefore, we analyzed postoperative SRS patients to identify factors associated with postoperative CIB.MethodsIn this retrospective study, we investigated SRS patients undergoing one-stage posterior column osteotomy (PCO) and fusion at our hospital between August 2012 and January 2019. The preoperative, postoperative and follow-up whole-spine anteroposterior (AP) radiographs were evaluated. Potential risk factors for postoperative CIB were analyzed using the LASSO regression, and independent risk factors were identified using binary logistic regression. A nomogram prediction model was constructed and validated.ResultsNinety eligible SRS patients were included. Postoperative radiographs showed a main curve correction rate of 54.17 ± 14.02%; however, follow-up CIB persisted in 26.67% of patients. Three independent variables affecting follow-up CIB were identified by LASSO regression, including preoperative CIB+ [OR = 12.414 (95% CI: 2.228, 69.162), P = 0.004], major curve flexibility index < 10% [OR = 14.300 (95% CI: 2.200, 92.957), P = 0.005], and immediate postoperative CIB [OR = 5.169 (95% CI: 1.387, 19.263), P = 0.014]. ROC and DCA analysis demonstrated that the nomogram had good predictive performance.ConclusionsPreoperative CIB+, major curve flexibility index < 10%, and immediate postoperative CIB were significantly associated with postoperative CIB. These findings offer valuable guidance to improve surgical outcomes and reduce complications.

Introduction

Severe and rigid scoliosis (SRS) is defined as main curve > 90° and < 25% of correction on bending films [1]. SRS can cause complications associated with persistent pain, neurological and cardiopulmonary dysfunction, with significant increased mortality when secondary respiratory compromise occurs [2]. The treatment of SRS remains challenging. Currently, the most widely used surgical treatment option for this condition is posterior pedicle screw-rod system internal fixation, which has the advantages of less trauma, good reduction, reliable fixation, and fewer complications [3].

Compensatory mechanisms of postoperative coronal balance (CB) are complex, particularly in patients with SRS [4]. The distance between the C7 plumb line (C7PL) and the central sacral plumb line (CSVL) is defined as the coronal balance distance (CBD) to assess the patient ‘s CB. Coronal imbalance (CIB) is defined as CBD ≥ 3 cm [5], which is a common postoperative complication of scoliosis with a reported prevalence of 30% and has been shown to impact health-related quality of life (HRQoL) [6]. In addition, CIB is more frequently associated with poor appearance, which is a major factor causing psychological disorders [7].

Many studies have focused on CIB in adolescent scoliosis and adult degenerative scoliosis. Studies on CIB in SRS patients have rarely been reported. In this study, we retrospectively measured preoperative, postoperative, and follow-up coronal parameters in adult SRS patients who underwent surgical treatment. We aimed to identify risk factors affecting postoperative CIB, which would help make better surgical decisions.

Materials and methods

Study population

A retrospective analysis was conducted on SRS patients who underwent posterior column osteotomy (PCO) and orthopedic fusion at our Hospital from August 2012 to January 2019, after obtaining approval from the Ethics Committee of our hospital (ID: LGH-2023-FEI-93). As this study was retrospective, written and signed informed consent was waived. The inclusion criteria included: (1) adults aged 18 to 55 years; (2) diagnosis of SRS (scoliotic curvature or kyphosis > 90 ° and flexibility < 25%); (3) no significant degenerative changes observed at each vertebral level; (4) complete medical records and follow-up data; and (5) a minimum follow-up of 2 years. Patients were excluded if they (1) were diagnosed with other types of scoliosis, such as degenerative scoliosis or scoliosis due to neurofibromatosis; (2) had significant degenerative changes at vertebral levels; (3) had a history of spinal surgery; (4) had incomplete medical records or unclear imaging data; 4) had flexibility > 25%; and (5) had paraplegia.

Data acquisition and evaluations

Demographic characteristics, including age, gender, body mass index (BMI), preoperative coronal classification, preoperative apical vertebral rotation (AVR), fusion level, operation time, blood loss, follow-up time were collected. Patients were divided into CB and CIB groups according to whether the preoperative CBD was greater than 2 cm [4]. The apical vertebral distribution modifier was defined as negative (−) or positive (+) if the center of the apical vertebras (CoAV) was located on either side of the central sacral vertical line (CSVL) or ipsilateral to the CSVL, respectively.

Lateral anteroposterior (AP) radiographs of the whole spine (showing the spine from the circumferential axis vertebrae to the upper third of the bilateral femoral heads with intact thorax) were taken preoperatively, immediately postoperatively (within 5–7 days), and during follow-up (1, 3, 6, 12 and 24 months postoperatively) in an upright position. The radiographic parameters were measured, including main curve Coob angle, flexibility index of main curve, curve correction rate, main curve type, distance between C7 plumb line and central sacral vertical Line (C7-CSVL), apical vertebra translation (AVT), lowest instrumented vertebra (LIV) tilt, distance between lowest LIV and CSVL (LIV-CSVL), radiographic shoulder height (RSH), sagittal vertical axis(SVA); thoracic kyphosis (TK); upper instrumented vertebra (UIV) tilt, thoracic kyphosis (TK, T2-T12), lumbar lordosis (LL); pelvic incidence (PI); pelvic tile (PT), and sacral slope (SS).

To ensure consistency and accuracy, two authors independently collected data according to a predefined protocol. They cross-validated their data entries to identify and resolve any discrepancies. The final dataset was then reviewed and verified by the corresponding author to ensure its completeness and reliability.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using IBM SPSS Statistics for Mac version 26.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA) and R version 4.1.2 (R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria). Categorical data were analyzed using Chi-square or Fisher’s exact tests, and continuous data were evaluated using t-tests or nonparametric tests as appropriate. All potential predictors identified from clinical experience and previous studies were incorporated into the initial model. We applied the Least Absolute Shrinkage and Selection Operator (LASSO) regression for multivariate variable selection, allowing consideration of the collective contribution of variables and accounting for multicollinearity. This approach ensured that important clinical variables were retained in the final predictive model. Binary logistic regression was performed to identify independent risk factors, with odds ratios (ORs) and 95% confidence intervals (CIs) reported. Goodman and Kruskal’s tau-y coefficient was used for correlation testing. All statistical analyses were two-sided, and a P-value less than 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Result

Patient demographics and radiographic parameters

A total of 90 eligible patients with SRS were included in this study, consisting of 24 males and 66 females with a mean age of 31.8 years (± 12.51) and a mean BMI of 21.06 kg/m2 (2.97). As shown in Table 1, there were 24 (26.67%) and 30 (33.33%) patients were classified as CB negative (CB-) and CB positive (CB+) before operation, respectively. The number of CIB- patients was same as that of CIB + patients (18 patients each). Patients evaluated as preoperative AVR of grade 2, 3 and 4 were 13 (14.44%), 28 (31.11%) and 49 (54.44%) patients, respectively. The mean fusion level was 12.16 (± 1.51), and the mean operation time was 300.89 (± 66.44) minutes. The mean blood loss was 1021.67 (± 750.13) ml, the patients were followed up for 25.76 ± 2.64 months. The patients’ preoperative and postoperative radiographic parameters were summarized in Tables 2 and 3. A total of 51 (56.67%) patients had flexibility index of main curve < 10% with the mean (SD) main curve Coob angle of 108.66° (± 19.78) before surgery, which was reduced to 51.43° (± 24.43) after operation. The main curve correction rate was determined as 54.17% (± 14.02). Compared with preoperative radiographic parameters, the mean postoperative parameters slightly decreased was as follow: AVT (9.20 cm vs. 4.51 cm), LIV-CSVL (1.32 cm vs. 0.90 cm), RSH (1.45 cm vs. 1.25 cm), SVA (3.06 cm vs. 2.74 cm), TK (69.24° vs. 49.92°), LL (70.96° vs. 53.05°), PI (41.95° vs. 40.39°) and SS (36.13° vs. 32.80°). The mean postoperative PT was slightly increased in comparison to preoperative PT (12.91° vs. 9.04°). The mean immediately postoperative C7-CSVL was slightly increased in comparison to that measured before operation (2.63 cm vs1.89 cm), and partially recovered to 1.76 cm during follow-up. A total of 38 (42.22%) and 24 (26.67%) patients had immediately postoperative CIB and follow-up CIB, respectively.

This study included 36 variables, which were used as independent variables for LASSO regression analysis in the presence or absence of postoperative CIB (Fig. 1). As log λ changed, the coefficients of the variables were reduced to zero, indicating that their effects on the LASSO regression model could be omitted because they were shrinking parameters (Fig. 1a). Following cross-validation, a total of 4 variables achieved minimum error (Fig. 1b). At this point, log λ was approximately − 2.32 and 4 variables were at its 1 SD from the optimal log λ. As summarized in Table 4, the variables with λ value corresponding to the minimum error identified by LASSO repression model included preoperative CIB +, flexibility index of main curve < 10%, immediately postoperative CIB and follow up TK.

Logistic regression and correlation of variables

Multivariate binary logistic regression analysis revealed a significant correlation between postoperative CIB with preoperative CIB + [OR = 12.414 (95%CI: 2.228, 69.162), P = 0.004], flexibility index of main curve < 10% [OR = 14.300 (95%CI: 2.200, 92.957), P = 0.005] and immediately postoperative CIB [OR = 5.169 (95%CI: 1.387, 19.263), P = 0.014], respectively. (Table 5). Goodman and Kruskal ‘s tau-y test showed statistically significant association between the three independent variables with the dependent variable (Table 6).

Development of a prediction model

The 3 independent variables selected from the LASSO regression model were used to construct a prediction model as displayed as a nomogram (Fig. 2). The area under the receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve (AUC) of the risk nomogram was 0.883 (95%CI 0.776–0.941) (Fig. 3), which suggested that the model had adequate predictive potential and performance. Bootstrap resampling method with 1,000 repetitions was used to draw a calibration curve, and the results showed that the predicted probability of model output was in good agreement with the true probability of occurrence (Fig. 4). The decision curve (DCA) analysis revealed that the thresholds of predicted risks ranged from approximately 0–1.0 with the maximum net benefits of 0.40 (Fig. 5). Hosmer-Lemeshow test showed P = 0.977, indicating high calibration.

Discussion

SRS is characterized by a large scoliotic curve and poor flexibility and is associated with complications of pain, neurological deficits, and cardiopulmonary dysfunction. Because of severe rigid spinal deformity, adult patients are at much higher surgical risk than adolescent patients [8]. Surgical instrumentation for scoliosis treatment evolved from Harrington instrumentation to pedicle screws, which have been widely used for the treatment of severe scoliosis [9]. For patients undergoing scoliosis correction surgery, both sagittal and coronal imbalance after surgery negatively impacts surgical outcome and patient satisfaction [10]. Current research remains focused on sagittal balance, and there remains less focus on coronal balance [11]. Recent literature suggests that CIB resulting from an increase in postoperative CBD is associated with increased risk of postoperative pain as well as complications [10]. Therefore, postoperative coronal balance is an important goal when performing orthopedic surgery in severe and rigid scoliosis patients.

There are many factors that may contribute to CIB in patients after surgery. Studies have shown that excessive correction of the main curve, wrong selection of the inferior fixed vertebra, and confusion between structural and non-structural curves are common factors causing overall coronal decompensation of the trunk after surgery [12]. Ding et al. [6] investigated postoperative coronal balance outcomes in patients with degenerative lumbar scoliosis. They concluded that patients with higher CB and preoperative AVT immediately after surgery, preoperative Cobb angle, and UIV tilt immediately after surgery were more likely to experience follow-up CIB. The adjusted ratio of the master curve ≤ 0.7 was an independent predictor of follow-up CIB. Ishikawa et al. [13] clarified that improper selection of LIV in patients is the culprit leading to deterioration of coronal balance immediately after surgery. Although most patients with coronal imbalance immediately after surgery can find coronal balance within a period after surgery, there is still a state of trunk imbalance that persists after surgery or even changes from an immediate postoperative state of balance to an imbalance. In this study, 18 patients (75.00%) developed immediately postoperative CIB in the follow-up CIB, and immediately postoperative CIB was an independent risk factor for follow-up CIB. Although it is possible that some patients with immediate postoperative CIB will slowly correct balance during rehabilitation, for SRS patients, spontaneous balance after surgery is less likely to eventually occur after surgery because the curve is stiff enough.

Previous studies have mostly classified patients based on the relationship between preoperative C7PL and curvature convexity. Type A is balanced; Type B is C7PL shifts to the concave side of the curve; Type C is C7PL shifts to the convex side of the curve. Zhang et al. [14] recognized that adult spinal deformity patients with type C may be at greater risk of developing CIB immediately after surgery. Bao et al. [15] suggested that C7 plumb line pointing to the convex side of the curve is a risk factor for the development of postoperative CIB in patients with degenerative scoliosis. Pan et al. [4] proposed a new coronal classification of adult scoliosis that increases modifiers of apical vertebral distribution and concluded that CIB + patients have a higher risk of postoperative CIB and have the worst coronal compensatory ability in the presence of postoperative CIB. In this study, 11 patients (45.83%) in the follow-up CIB group belonged to CIB + preoperatively according to the new classification of Pan et al. CIB + patients have a higher risk of developing CIB after surgery and have the worst coronal compensatory ability in the case of developing CIB after surgery. The new classification fully considers the center of gravity distribution of the patient and can truly reflect the coronal load balance from a biomechanical point of view. It provides strong support for predicting the long-term balance outcome of patients after surgery.

In addition, 22 patients had Flexibility index of main curve < 10% in follow-up CIB, and analysis showed Flexibility index of main curve < 10% as an independent risk factor for follow-up CIB. Rigid curves can make it more difficult to adequately correct spinal deformity surgically. Therefore, it is necessary to determine a safe and reliable surgical plan according to the flexibility of the spine and, the patient’s surgical expectations, and the surgeon’s surgical ability. According to our center ‘s experience, multilevel asymmetric Ponte osteotomy is a safe and effective osteotomy correction technique that can ensure the correction rate of deformity while reducing operation time, blood loss, and complications, providing an appropriate choice to address the deformity correction of SRS [16].

Limitations of this study include the small sample size of only 90 SRS patients, all of them were from a single center predominantly located in high-altitude regions of China. This geographic and demographic concentration may affect the generalizability of our findings to other populations with different ethnicities or environmental exposures. Additionally, we did not account for patient-specific factors such as lifestyle habits, weight fluctuations, or participation in postoperative rehabilitation programs, which may influence postoperative coronal alignment. Our analysis focused predominantly on coronal plane parameters and geometric measurements like the Cobb angle and coronal balance, without incorporating assessments of axial rotation, trunk asymmetry, or sagittal alignment. We also excluded critical anatomical and biomechanical components such as musculature, ligaments, neural structures, disc stress, muscle dynamics, ligamentous integrity, neuromuscular control, and load distribution changes. These elements play significant roles in spinal biomechanics and could impact postoperative outcomes and long-term spinal health. While the predictive model constructed in this study showed good accuracy, it does not encompass all biomechanical factors influencing surgical outcomes in idiopathic scoliosis. Further prospective studies incorporating these additional variables and conducting in-depth biomechanical analyses are needed to validate the model’s effectiveness. Future research should focus on utilizing larger sample sizes and more diverse patient populations to provide a more comprehensive understanding of spinal deformities, enhance predictive accuracy for postoperative coronal imbalance, and fully elucidate the multifaceted nature of these conditions and their impact on postoperative outcomes.

Conclusion

This study showed that the coronal balance status of SRS patients after surgery was influenced by many factors, and immediately postoperative CIB, flexibility index of main curve < 10%, and preoperative CIB + were independent risk factors for postoperative CIB. By identifying these key factors, it can provide an important reference for surgical decision-making in SRS patients with a view to improving surgical outcomes and reducing postoperative complications.

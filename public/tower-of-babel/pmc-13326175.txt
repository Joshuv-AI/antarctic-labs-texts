Emergent Open Conversion for Ruptured Arch Aneurysm After Thoracic Endovascular Repair in Chronic Actinic Dermatitis
Authors: Sei Yasunaga, Tomoaki Kudo, Natsumi Horikawa, Sawaka Tanabe, Yukihiro Noda, Atsushi Takamori, Narihisa Yamada, Shinya Fukui
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundChronic actinic dermatitis is a rare photosensitive disorder that often requires long-term systemic corticosteroid therapy, which may contribute to aortic wall fragility.Case SummaryA 74-year-old man with chronic actinic dermatitis who has been receiving oral corticosteroid therapy for 30 years underwent thoracic endovascular aortic repair (TEVAR) for a saccular aortic arch aneurysm. One year later, he developed rapid aneurysmal expansion and rupture due to a type 1a endoleak caused by stent-graft migration. Emergent total arch replacement with a frozen elephant trunk was successfully performed under strict perioperative and postoperative light-shielding management, without infectious complications or exacerbation of dermatologic symptoms.DiscussionLong-term corticosteroid therapy may contribute to aortic wall fragility, progressive proximal aortic dilatation, and sealing failure after TEVAR. This case also demonstrates that meticulous perioperative and postoperative light-shielding strategies can enable safe open cardiovascular surgery in patients with severe photosensitive disorders.Take-Home MessageCareful surveillance after TEVAR and individualized surgical strategies, including timely open conversion when further endovascular treatment is not feasible, are essential for managing complex aortic disease in patients with photosensitive disorders.

History of Presentation

A 74-year-old man presented to the emergency department with dizziness and a fall 1 year after thoracic endovascular aortic repair (TEVAR) for a saccular aortic arch aneurysm. He subsequently developed rapid aneurysmal enlargement with hemoptysis and hemorrhagic shock. Past Medical History

The patient had chronic actinic dermatitis and had been receiving long-term oral corticosteroid therapy (betamethasone 1 mg/d, equivalent to approximately 6 mg/d of prednisolone) for 30 years. His medical history also included portal vein thrombosis, hepatitis C–related liver cirrhosis, hypertension, dyslipidemia, and bronchial asthma. Differential Diagnosis

The differential diagnosis of the rapidly enlarging thoracic aortic aneurysm included type 1a endoleak with stent-graft migration, infected aneurysm, and stent-graft infection. Because of the rapid expansion, infection-related pathology was also carefully considered.

Investigations

Contrast-enhanced computed tomography (CT) after the initial TEVAR demonstrated the proximal landing configuration. The proximal landing zone diameter was 35 mm, and the proximal landing length was 16 mm. A 40-mm Gore TAG device (W. L. Gore & Associates, Inc) with 14% oversizing had been deployed in landing zone 1 (Figure 2B, Video 1B). At presentation 1 year later, CT revealed aneurysmal expansion from 50 to 68 mm associated with a type 1a endoleak due to stent-graft migration (Figure 2C, Video 1C). Blood cultures were negative, C-reactive protein was not elevated, no fever was observed, and CT showed no evidence of an infected aneurysm at that time. Follow-up CT 1 week later showed further aneurysmal enlargement to 78 mm with rupture. At that stage, the proximal aortic segment, including the proximal landing zone, had dilated to 43 mm, with a residual proximal landing length of only 5 mm (Figure 2D, Video 1D).

Management

Because of marked proximal aortic dilatation and inadequate residual landing length, further endovascular treatment was considered infeasible. A hybrid approach with cervical branch bypass and TEVAR was therefore not feasible. Emergent total arch replacement with a frozen elephant trunk was performed under minimized ambient lighting in a darkened operating room, with only the overhead surgical lights and headlights (Figure 3).Figure 3Intraoperative Photographs Showing the Perioperative Light-Shielding Strategy, Including Coverage of Exposed Skin and Reduction of Ambient Lighting in the Operating Room

Postoperatively, exposure to ambient light was minimized, exposed skin was covered whenever possible, and the patient was managed in a private room with minimal lighting.

Outcome and Follow-Up

No infectious complications occurred, and the patient was discharged home on postoperative day 20 (Figure 2E). Dermatologic symptoms did not worsen during the perioperative or postoperative period. Discussion

This case raises 2 important clinical considerations. First, TEVAR was initially selected as a less invasive and reasonable treatment option for this high-risk patient, given his severe photosensitivity, advanced age, and multiple comorbidities. However, long-term systemic corticosteroid therapy may have adversely affected aortic wall integrity, potentially contributing not only to medial degeneration and increased aortic wall fragility but also to progressive enlargement of the proximal aortic diameter. Such structural changes may predispose patients to proximal stent-graft migration and loss of adequate sealing, thereby increasing the risk of endoleak and subsequent aortic rupture after TEVAR. Experimental studies have demonstrated that corticosteroids induce structural degeneration of the aortic media and markedly increase susceptibility to aortic rupture, supporting the hypothesis that chronic steroid exposure may accelerate aneurysmal expansion and catastrophic failure following endovascular repair.1

Chronic actinic dermatitis is a rare photosensitive disorder characterized by marked hypersensitivity to ultraviolet and visible light and often requires prolonged systemic corticosteroid therapy.2 Long-term corticosteroid exposure has been associated with medial degeneration and aortic wall fragility, potentially increasing the risk of aortic complications.3 Although these background factors may be clinically relevant in the present case, reports describing surgical strategies for complex aortic disease in patients with chronic actinic dermatitis remain extremely limited.

In the present case, additional endovascular treatment was not considered feasible because the proximal aortic segment, including the intended proximal landing zone, had become markedly dilated, with an insufficient remaining landing length. Accordingly, a hybrid strategy with cervical branch bypass and more proximal TEVAR was not considered suitable, and emergent open conversion was required. This clinical course underscores the importance of careful postoperative surveillance after TEVAR, particularly in patients with potential risk factors for progressive aortic dilatation and proximal sealing failure.

Second, open cardiovascular surgery in patients with severe photosensitive disorders poses unique challenges. Chronic actinic dermatitis is typically managed with strict avoidance of ultraviolet and visible light exposure, topical and systemic corticosteroids, immunomodulatory agents, and, in selected cases, photoprotective strategies. Previous reports involving photosensitive disorders, such as porphyria, have emphasized the importance of perioperative light control, including the use of wavelength-filtering devices on operating room lights, surgical headlights, and monitors to block photosensitive wavelengths.4 These measures aim to prevent acute skin injury and postoperative exacerbation of dermatologic symptoms.

In the present case, although the exact photosensitive wavelengths were not identified preoperatively, comprehensive perioperative light-shielding management—including reduction of ambient lighting and coverage of the exposed skin—was implemented. As a result, no exacerbation of chronic actinic dermatitis occurred during the perioperative period. This experience suggests that even in the absence of precise wavelength identification, strict light-shielding strategies combined with careful perioperative management can enable safe open cardiovascular surgery in patients with severe photosensitive disorders.

Previous reports have mainly focused on intraoperative light control in patients with photosensitive disorders, whereas detailed postoperative management strategies have rarely been described. In our case, postoperative exposure to ambient light was minimized, the exposed skin was covered whenever possible, and the patient was managed in a private room with minimal lighting, without infectious complications. These measures may provide a practical and reproducible postoperative management strategy for similar patients undergoing major cardiovascular surgery.

At the time of the initial TEVAR, no specific perioperative measures for photosensitive disorders were implemented because the procedure was minimally invasive and involved only small skin incisions. In contrast, open cardiac surgery requires a large median sternotomy, and exacerbation of photosensitive skin injury could potentially increase the risk of severe wound complications, including mediastinitis. Therefore, strict perioperative light-shielding management was intentionally adopted during the open surgical procedure.

A limitation of this case is that the exact photosensitive wavelengths responsible for chronic actinic dermatitis were not identified preoperatively; therefore, perioperative light-shielding strategies were implemented empirically rather than in a wavelength-specific manner.

Conclusions

We report a rare case of ruptured aortic arch aneurysm after TEVAR in a patient with chronic actinic dermatitis receiving long-term systemic corticosteroid therapy. This case suggests that long-term corticosteroid exposure may contribute to aortic wall fragility, progressive proximal aortic dilatation, and subsequent sealing failure after TEVAR, making timely open conversion necessary when further endovascular treatment is no longer feasible. In addition, meticulous perioperative and postoperative light-shielding management enabled safe total arch replacement without exacerbation of photosensitive skin disease or infectious complications. Careful surveillance and individualized surgical planning are essential for managing complex aortic disease in patients with photosensitive disorders and long-term steroid exposure.

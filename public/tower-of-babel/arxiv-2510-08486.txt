1.
Introduction

Let
C
be a smooth projective curve over a number field
K
. A
twist
of
C/K
is a curve
C^{\prime}/K
that is isomorphic to
C
over
\overline{K}
. Furthermore, we treat two twists as equivalent if they are isomorphic over
K
. The set of twists of
C/K
, modulo
K
-isomorphism, is denoted by
\operatorname{Twist}(C/K)
.

It turns out that the set
\operatorname{Twist}(C/K)
can be identified with a certain cohomology set.
Namely, there is a one-to-one correspondence between elements in
\operatorname{Twist}(C/K)
and the elements of the cohomology set
H^{1}\left(\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{K}/K),\operatorname{Isom}(C)\right)
where
\operatorname{Isom}(C)
is the group of all
\overline{K}
-isomorphisms from
C
to itself.
The proof of this result can be found for example in
[
Sil09
]
(Chapter X.2, Theorem 2.2). Specially, this correspondence allows us to define twists of an elliptic curve
E/K
via cocycles in
H^{1}\left(\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{K}/K),\operatorname{Isom}(E)\right)
and vice versa, where
\operatorname{Isom}(E)
is the isomorphism group of
E
viewed as a genus one curve. It is known that

\operatorname{Isom}(E)\cong\operatorname{Aut}(E,O)\ltimes E(\overline{K})

where
\operatorname{Aut}(E,O)
is the automorphism group of
E
viewed as an elliptic curve.

One actively studied area for the twists of a curve is the study of local points, everywhere local solvability of these twist and the existence of rational points on the twists.
The articles below demonstrate some interesting results and the progress made in this area.

Ozman in
[
Ozm12
]
was investigating the existence of local points on the quadratic twist of the modular curve
X_{0}(N)
over
\mathbb{Q}
. She gave necessary and sufficient conditions for these quadratic twists to have
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
-rational points over any completion
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
of
\mathbb{Q}
.

Ozman continues to study local points on the twist of the modular curve
X_{0}(N)/\mathbb{Q}
by a polyquadratic field
K=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d_{1}},\dotso,\sqrt{d_{k}})
in
[
Ozm13
]
. In her work, Ozman gives an algorithm to produce such twists that are everywhere locally solvable. After that, she produced infinitely many twists violating the Hasse principle (i.e. the twists that are everywhere locally solvable but do not have a rational point) and also gives an asymptotic formula for the number of such twists.

Lorenzo García and Vullers in
[
LGV24
]
inspect the local and global points of the twists of the Klein quartic (a smooth projective plane curve defined by the equation
X^{3}Y+Y^{3}Z+Z^{3}X=0
). The main result in their work provides families with (conjecturally infinitely many) twists of the Klein quartic that violate the Hasse principle.

Kazalicki in his recent work
[
Kaz25
]
studied the quadratic twists of a genus one quartic
H\,:\,y^{2}=(x^{2}-x-3)(x^{2}+2x-12)
by the integer
d
and their
2
-Selmer groups. In his work he partially answers the question for which primes
p
the quadratic twist of
H
by
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{p})
has a rational point.

Following Kazalicki’s work on studying quadratic twists of the genus one quartic
H
, Novak in
[
Nov25
]
investigates the quadratic twists of genus one quartic curves defined by an irreducible and monic polynomial of degree four. He finds a general criterion for determining local solvability of this twist and also determines the asymptotic growth of the set of square-free integers for which the associated twist is everywhere locally solvable.

Çiperiani and Ozman in
[
cO15
]
have studied twists of an elliptic curve
E/K
defined in the following way:

For any quadratic extension
F=K(\sqrt{d})
and any point
S\in E(K)
they considered the cocycle
\xi_{F,S}\in H^{1}\left(\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{K}/K),\operatorname{Aut}(E)\right)
defined by

\xi_{F,S}(\sigma)=\begin{cases}(-1,S),&\text{if }\sigma(\sqrt{d})=-\sqrt{d},\\
(1,O),&\text{otherwise }\end{cases}

where
(\pm 1,S)
sends a point
X\in E
to the point
\pm X+S
.
Let
E_{F,S}
denote the twist of
E
corresponding to
\xi_{F,S}
. Note that
E_{F,S}
is in general only a genus one curve and not necessarily an elliptic curve.

In their work, Çiperiani and Ozman showed that this genus one curve
E_{F,S}
has a
K
-rational point if and only if the point
S
is in the image of the global trace map
tr_{F/K}
.

If the elliptic curve
E
does not have complex multiplication, then the elements of
\operatorname{Isom}(E)
are of the form
(-1,S)
and
(1,S)
for a point
S\in E
. Çiperiani and Ozman studied the twists corresponding to the generic element
(-1,S)
and in this paper we will study the twists corresponding to the other generic element
(1,S)
of the isomorphism group
\operatorname{Isom}(E)
. We will work in the following setting.

Let
p
be a prime number,
K
a number field that contains the
p
-th root of unity
\zeta_{p}
,
d
a
p
-power-free integer (an integer that is not divisible by the

-th power of any prime number) and

.
Let

be an elliptic curve with full

the generators of

. We define a cocycle

\xi_{d}(\sigma)=\begin{cases}O,&\text{if }\sigma(\sqrt[p]{d})=\sqrt[p]{d},\\
kS,&\text{if }\sigma(\sqrt[p]{d})=\zeta_{p}^{k}\sqrt[p]{d}.\end{cases}

Denote by

the twist of

corresponding to the cocycle

. We investigate the following questions.

Questions:

•

Find a model for the twist

?

•

What can we say about the local solvability of

In order to obtain a model for the twist

\displaystyle\mathrm{div}{(z)}

\displaystyle=(T)+(T+S)+\dotso+(T+(p-1)S)-(O)-(S)-\dotso-((p-1)S),

\displaystyle\mathrm{div}{(w)}

\displaystyle=(2T)+(2T+S)+\dotso+(2T+(p-1)S)-(O)-(S)-\dotso-((p-1)S).

Note that such functions

and

,
\displaystyle{\sum_{k=0}^{p-1}(T+kS)-\sum_{k=0}^{p-1}kS=O}

\displaystyle{\sum_{k=0}^{p-1}(2T+kS)-\sum_{k=0}^{p-1}kS=O}

and

With the introduction of this functions we are able to describe a model for the twist

.

The functions

and give a model of the twist

H_{S}^{d}\,:\,\alpha_{1}z^{p}+\alpha_{2}z^{p-2}w+\dotso+\alpha_{\frac{p+1}{2}}zw^{\frac{p-1}{2}}+\beta w^{p}+\gamma=0

where
\alpha_{1}
,
\alpha_{2}
, …,
\alpha_{\frac{p+1}{2}}
,

Next, by using the well-known Kummer sequence for the elliptic curve

(1.1)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces E(K_{v})/pE[K_{v}]}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{1}(K_{v},E[p])}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{1}(K_{v},E)[p]}

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\delta}

.

.

2.
Twist arising from
2
-torsion and
3
-torsion points

2.1.
Twist arising from 2-torsion points

Let
E/\mathbb{Q}
be an elliptic curve and
T\in E(\mathbb{Q})[2]
a rational
2
-torsion point on
E
. For a square-free integer
d
, denote by
H^{d}_{T}
the twist of
E
that corresponds to the cocycle
\zeta:\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})\to E
defined by

\zeta(\sigma)=\begin{cases}O,&\text{if }\sigma(\sqrt{d})=\sqrt{d},\\
T,&\text{if }\sigma(\sqrt{d})=-\sqrt{d}.\\
\end{cases}

By changing the coordinates we may assume that
T=(0,0)
and
E\,:\,y^{2}=x^{3}+ax^{2}+bx
.
In this case we can write an equation for
H^{d}_{T}
as in
[
Sil09
]
(Chapter X.3, Remark 3.7)

H^{d}_{T}\,:\,dy^{2}=(a^{2}-4b)x^{4}-2adx^{2}+d^{2}.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
E/\mathbb{Q}
be an elliptic curve and
T\in E(\mathbb{Q})[2]
a rational
2
-torsion point on
E
. For a square-free integer
d
, let
H^{d}_{T}
be a twist of
E
defined as above.
If the curve
H^{d}_{T}
is ELS, then all prime factors of
d
divide
2a(a^{2}-4b)
.
Specially,
H^{d}_{T}
is ELS only for finitely many square-free integers
d
.

Proof.

We will prove this by showing that if there is a prime factor
p
of
d
such that
{p\nmid 2a(a^{2}-4b)}
, then
H^{d}_{T}
is not ELS.
Let
d
be a square-free integer and
p
a prime factor of
d
such that
p\nmid 2a(a^{2}-4b)
. Suppose that
H^{d}_{T}
is ELS.

Since
H^{d}_{T}
is ELS there exists a point
(x,y)\in H_{T}^{d}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
. Let us write
x=p^{\alpha}x_{1}
and
y=p^{\beta}y_{1}
for some integers
\alpha
,
\beta
and
x_{1}
,
y_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}
.

Now we have that

\displaystyle v_{p}(dy^{2})

\displaystyle=v_{p}(dp^{2\beta}y_{1})=2\beta+1,

\displaystyle v_{p}((a^{2}-4b)x^{4}-2adx^{2}+d^{2})

\displaystyle=v_{p}((a^{2}-4b)p^{4\alpha}x_{1}^{4}-2adp^{2\alpha}x_{1}^{2}+d^{2})=\begin{cases}2,&\text{if }\alpha\geq 1,\\
0,&\text{if }\alpha=0,\\
4\alpha,&\text{if }\alpha<0.\end{cases}

Since
v_{p}(dy^{2})
is always odd and
v_{p}((a^{2}-4b)x^{4}-2adx^{2}+d^{2})
is always even, we get a contradiction. Thus,
H^{d}_{T}
is not ELS for such integer
d
.
∎

2.2.
Twist arising from 3-torsion points

Let
K
be a number field that contains the
3
rd root of unity
\zeta_{3}
,
d
a cube-free integer and
L=K(\sqrt[3]{d})
.

Let
E/K
be an elliptic curve with full
3
-torsion and
S,T\in E(K)[3]
the generators of
E(K)[3]
.
Using Derickx and Sutherland’s parametrization for an elliptic curve with points
S
and
T
of order
3
(see
[
DS17
]
) and by changing the coordinates we may assume that

E\,:\,y^{2}+((2+\zeta_{3})a+1-\zeta_{3})xy+((1+\zeta_{3})a^{2}-\zeta_{3}a)y=x^{3},

S=(0,0)
and
T=(-a,a)
for some
a\in K
.

We define the cocycle
\xi_{d}:\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{K}/K)\to E
by

\xi_{d}(\sigma)=\begin{cases}O,&\text{if }\sigma(\sqrt[3]{d})=\sqrt[3]{d},\\
S,&\text{if }\sigma(\sqrt[3]{d})=\zeta_{3}\sqrt[3]{d},\\
2S,&\text{if }\sigma(\sqrt{d})=\zeta_{3}^{2}\sqrt[3]{d}.\\
\end{cases}

Denote by
H_{S}^{d}
the twist of
E
corresponding to the cocycle
\xi_{d}
.

Next, in order to obtain an equation for the twist
H_{S}^{d}
we define functions
z,w\in K(H_{S}^{d})
as

\displaystyle\mathrm{div}{(z)}

\displaystyle=(T)+(T+S)+(T+2S)-(O)-(S)-(2S),

\displaystyle\mathrm{div}{(w)}

\displaystyle=(2T)+(2T+S)+(2T+2S)-(O)-(S)-(2S).

Using MAGMA one can easily verify that the functions

\displaystyle z=

\displaystyle\frac{((1+\zeta_{3})a^{2}+(-2\zeta_{3}-1)a+\zeta_{3})y}{\sqrt[3]{d}x}+\frac{(1+\zeta_{3})a^{4}+(-3\zeta_{3}-1)a^{3}+(2\zeta_{3}-1)a^{2}+a}{\sqrt[3]{d}x}

\displaystyle+\frac{(1+\zeta_{3})a^{3}-2\zeta_{3}a^{2}-2a+\zeta_{3}+1}{\sqrt[3]{d}},

\displaystyle w=

\displaystyle\frac{((-1-\zeta_{3})a^{2}+(1+2\zeta_{3})a-\zeta_{3})y}{\sqrt[3]{d^{2}}x}+\frac{a^{4}+(-2\zeta_{3}-3)a^{3}+(3\zeta_{3}+2)a^{2}-\zeta_{3}a}{\sqrt[3]{d^{2}}x}

\displaystyle+\frac{-\zeta_{3}a^{3}-2a^{2}+(2+2\zeta_{3})a-\zeta_{3}}{\sqrt[3]{d^{2}}}

satisfy the above divisor conditions and that they are in the function field
K(H_{S}^{d})
.

With this we are now ready to describe a model for the twist
H_{S}^{d}
.

Proposition 2.2
.

With the above notation we have that functions
z
and
w
generate the function filed
K(H_{S}^{d})
and satisfy the equation

H_{S}^{d}\,:\,dz^{3}+3d\alpha zw+d^{2}w^{3}+\beta=0

where
\alpha=a^{3}+2\zeta_{3}^{2}a^{2}+2\zeta_{3}a+1
and

\beta=-a^{9}+3(3+\zeta_{3})a^{8}-3(\zeta_{3}+11)a^{7}+(63\zeta_{3}+64)a^{6}-3(35\zeta_{3}+23)a^{5}+3(35\zeta_{3}+12)a^{4}+(1-63\zeta_{3})a^{3}+3(7\zeta_{3}-4)a^{2}+3(2-\zeta_{3})a-1
.

Proof.

Using MAGMA one can easily check that for functions
z
and
w
the equation
dz^{3}+3d\alpha zw+d^{2}w^{3}+\beta=0
holds.

Let
C\,:\,dz^{3}+3d\alpha zw+d^{2}w^{3}+\beta=0
be the curve given with the previous equation. We will show that the curve
C
is isomorphic to the elliptic curve
E
and that the curve
C
, as a twist of
E
, corresponds to the cocycle
\xi_{d}
.

We have the map
\varphi:E\to C
defined by
\varphi(x,y)=(z,w)
. It can be checked that the map
\varphi
is indeed a isomorphisam between the curves
E
and
C
simply by calculating the inverse
\phi=\varphi^{-1}
. The inverse
\phi:C\to E
is given by
\phi(z,w)=(X,Y)
where

\displaystyle X

\displaystyle=\frac{(\zeta_{3}+2)a^{4}+(-5\zeta_{3}-4)a^{3}+(5\zeta_{3}+1)a^{2}+(1-\zeta_{3})a}{\sqrt[3]{d}z+\sqrt[3]{d^{2}}w-a^{3}+(2\zeta_{3}+2)a^{2}-2\zeta_{3}a-1},

\displaystyle Y

\displaystyle=\frac{(\zeta_{3}+2)a^{4}+(-5\zeta_{3}-4)a^{3}+(5\zeta_{3}+1)a^{2}+(1-\zeta_{3})a}{(1+\zeta_{3})a^{2}+(-2\zeta_{3}-1)a+\zeta_{3}}\cdot\frac{\sqrt[3]{d}z+(-\zeta_{3}-1)a^{3}+2\zeta_{3}a^{2}+2a+(-\zeta_{3}-1)}{\sqrt[3]{d}z+\sqrt[3]{d^{2}}w-a^{3}+(2\zeta_{3}+2)a^{2}-2\zeta_{3}a-1}

\displaystyle+\frac{(-\zeta_{3}-1)a^{4}+(3\zeta_{3}+1)a^{3}+(-2\zeta_{3}+1)a^{2}-a}{(1+\zeta_{3})a^{2}+(-2\zeta_{3}-1)a+\zeta_{3}}.

Since
C
is isomorphic to
E
over
L
, we have that the curve
C
is a twist of the elliptic curve
E
. Let
\xi_{C}:\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{K}/K)\to E
be the cocycle corresponding to the twist
C
. Than we have that
\xi_{C}(\sigma)=\phi^{\sigma}\circ\phi^{-1}
for all
\sigma\in\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{K}/K)
.

Let us now take a
\sigma\in\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{K}/K)
such that
\sigma(\sqrt[3]{d})=\zeta_{3}\sqrt[3]{d}
. Using MAGMA we then calculate

\phi^{\sigma}\circ\phi^{-1}(x,y)=\phi^{\sigma}(z,w)=\left(\frac{(-\zeta_{3}-1)a^{2}+\zeta_{3}a}{x^{2}}y,\frac{-\zeta_{3}a^{4}-2a^{3}+(1+\zeta_{3})a^{2}}{x^{3}}y\right)=(x,y)+S.

From the above calculations we have that
\xi_{C}(\sigma)=\xi_{d}(\sigma)
for all such
\sigma\in\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{K}/K)
.

By similar calculations we also get that
\xi_{C}(\sigma)=\xi_{d}(\sigma)
for all the others
\sigma\in\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{K}/K)
. Therefore
\xi_{C}=\xi_{d}
, from which follows that the curve
C
is really our twist
H_{S}^{d}
.
∎

Note that from Theorem
1.2
for

we get a similar result for the twist

as earlier for the twists arising form
2
-torsion points. Specially,

is ELS for only finitely many cube-free integers

.

3.
Twists arising from
p
-torsion points

Let
p
be a prime number,
K
a number field that contains the
p
-th root of unity
\zeta_{p}
,
d
a
p
-power-free integer and
L=K(\sqrt[p]{d})
.

Let
E/K
be an elliptic curve with full
p
-torsion and
S,T\in E(K)[p]
the generators of
E(K)[p]
.

We define the cocycle
\xi_{d}:\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{K}/K)\to E
by

\xi_{d}(\sigma)=\begin{cases}O,&\text{if }\sigma(\sqrt[p]{d})=\sqrt[p]{d},\\
kS,&\text{if }\sigma(\sqrt[p]{d})=\zeta_{p}^{k}\sqrt[p]{d}.\end{cases}

Denote by
H_{S}^{d}
the twist of
E
corresponding to the cocycle
\xi_{d}
.

We also define the
twisted action
of
\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{K}/K)
by the cocycle
\xi_{d}
. The twisted action of
\sigma\in\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{K}/K)
by
\xi_{d}
acts on a function
f\in\overline{K}(E)
as

f^{\sigma^{*}}=f^{\sigma}\xi_{d}(\sigma).

It is known that that the function field
K(H_{S}^{d})
is the fixed field of
\overline{K}(E)
by the twisted action of
\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{K}/K)
by the cocycle
\xi_{d}
.

In order to obtain an equation for the twist
H_{S}^{d}
, we define functions
z,w\in\overline{K}(E)
as

\displaystyle\mathrm{div}{(z)}

\displaystyle=(T)+(T+S)+\dotso+(T+(p-1)S)-(O)-(S)-\dotso-((p-1)S),

\displaystyle\mathrm{div}{(w)}

\displaystyle=(2T)+(2T+S)+\dotso+(2T+(p-1)S)-(O)-(S)-\dotso-((p-1)S).

Note that with this the functions
z
and
w
are only well defined up to multiplication by a constant. The next Proposition shows that we can choose this constants in such a way that the functions
z
and
w
end up in the function field of the twist
H_{S}^{d}
.

Proposition 3.1
.

There exists functions
z
and
w
in the function field
K(H_{S}^{d})
with the above divisors.

Proof.

Since the divisor
(T)+(T+S)+\dotso+(T+(p-1)S)-(O)-(S)-\dotso-((p-1)S)
has degree
0
, its sum of points is
\displaystyle{\sum_{k=0}^{p-1}(T+kS)-\sum_{k=0}^{p-1}kS=O}
and all the points in the divisor are
K
-rational, we know that there exists a function
f\in K(E)
such that

\mathrm{div}{(f)}=(T)+(T+S)+\dotso+(T+(p-1)S)-(O)-(S)-\dotso-((p-1)S).

As functions
z
and
f
have the same divisor, it follows that
z=\lambda f
for some constant
\lambda\in\overline{K}
.

On the other hand, we also have that the divisor of the function
z\circ\tau_{S}
, where
\tau_{S}
is the translation by the point
S
, is

\mathrm{div}{(z\circ\tau_{S})}=(T-S)+(T)+\dotso+(T+(p-2)S)-(-S)-(O)-\dotso-((p-2)S)=\mathrm{div}{(z)}

Therefore, we have that there is a constant
c\in\overline{K}
such that
z(P+S)=cz(P)
for all points
P\in E
.
Since
S
is a
p
-torsion point, we have
z(P)=z(P+pS)=c^{p}z(P)
for all points
P\in E
. From there it follows that
c
is a
p
-th root of unity. Thus, we have that
c=\zeta_{p}^{k}
for some integer
k
.

The function
z
will be in the function field
K(H_{S}^{d})
if and only if it is fixed by the twisted action action of
\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{K}/K)
by the cocycle
\xi_{d}
. Let us take a
\sigma\in\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{K}/K)
such that
\sigma(\sqrt[p]{d})=\zeta_{p}\sqrt[p]{d}
. We want to choose the constant
\lambda
such that
z^{\sigma^{*}}=z
.

We have that

z^{\sigma^{*}}(P)=z^{\sigma}(P+S)=(\zeta_{p}^{k}z(P))^{\sigma}=\zeta_{p}^{k}\sigma(\lambda)f(P)

and
z(P)=\lambda f(P)
for all points
P\in E
. So, we have to choose the constant
\lambda
such that
\zeta_{p}^{k}\sigma(\lambda)=\lambda
holds. By choosing
\lambda=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt[p]{d^{k}}}
one can easily check that the last equation is satisfied. With such previously chosen constant
\lambda
one can also easily check that
z^{\sigma^{*}}=z
for all the others
\sigma\in\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{K}/K)
and therefore we have that
z\in K(H_{S}^{d})
.

Using the same arguments as for the function
z
we also get that there exists such a function
w\in K(H_{S}^{d})
.
∎

Proposition 3.2
.

With the notation as above we have that the set
\{z^{p},z^{p-2}w,\,\dotso,\,zw^{\frac{p-1}{2}},w^{p},1\}
of functions in
K(H_{S}^{d})
is
K
-linearly dependent, i.e. there exist constants
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\dotso,\alpha_{\frac{p+1}{2}},\beta,\gamma\in K
not all zero such that

\alpha_{1}z^{p}+\alpha_{2}z^{p-2}w+\dotso+\alpha_{\frac{p+1}{2}}zw^{\frac{p-1}{2}}+\beta w^{p}+\gamma=0.

Proof.

Let
\varphi
be the
p
-isogeny of the elliptic curve
E
with the kernel
\ker{(\varphi)}=\left<S\right>
. Denote by
\tilde{E}
the image of
\varphi
,
\tilde{T}=\varphi(T)
and
\tilde{O}=\varphi(O)
.

Consider the functions
f_{1}
,
f_{2}
and
h\in K(\tilde{E})
with the following divisors:

\displaystyle\mathrm{div}{(f_{1})}

\displaystyle=p(\tilde{T})-p(\tilde{O}),

\displaystyle\mathrm{div}{(f_{2})}

\displaystyle=p(2\tilde{T})-p(\tilde{O}),

\displaystyle\mathrm{div}{(h)}

\displaystyle=(2\tilde{T})+(\tilde{O})-2(\tilde{T}).

Note that the functions
f_{1}
,
f_{2}
and
h
are for now only well defined up to multiplication by a constant. For a suitable choice of this constants we can define functions
f_{1}
,
f_{2}
and
h
such that
\varphi^{*}(f_{1})=z^{p}
,
\varphi^{*}(hf_{1})=z^{p-2}w
, …,
\varphi^{*}(h^{\frac{p-1}{2}}f_{1})=zw^{\frac{p-1}{2}}
and
\varphi^{*}(f_{2})=w^{p}
holds.
Thus, in order to show that
z^{p},z^{p-2}w,\,\dotso,\,zw^{\frac{p-1}{2}},w^{p},1
are
K
-linearly dependent it is enough to show that
f_{1},hf_{1},\,\dotso,\ h^{\frac{p-1}{2}}f_{1},f_{2},1
are
K
-linearly dependent.

In order to do so, we will show that there exist a constant
\lambda\in K
and a polynomial
P\in K[t]
of degree at most
\frac{p-1}{2}
such that

f_{2}=\lambda+f_{1}P(h).

Set
\lambda=f_{2}(\tilde{T})
and consider the function
f=\dfrac{f_{2}-\lambda}{f_{1}}
. Since
K
is and extension of the field
\mathbb{Q}
, we can view the elliptic curve
\tilde{E}
over
\mathbb{C}
as a torus
\mathbb{C}/\Lambda
for some lattice
\Lambda
. According to Proposition 3.4 in Chapter VI in
[
Sil09
]
this allows us to write the functions
f_{1}
,
f_{2}
and
h
using the Weierstrass
\sigma
-function

\displaystyle f_{1}(z)

\displaystyle=C_{1}\frac{\sigma(z-z_{T})^{p}}{\sigma(z)^{p}},

\displaystyle f_{2}(z)

\displaystyle=C_{2}\frac{\sigma(z-2z_{T})^{p}}{\sigma(z)^{p}},

\displaystyle h(z)

\displaystyle=C\frac{\sigma(z-2z_{T})\sigma(z)}{\sigma(z-z_{T})^{2}}

where
C_{1}
,
C_{2}
and
C\in K
are constants and
z_{T}
corresponds to point
\tilde{T}
when viewed in
\mathbb{C}/\Lambda
.
Then we have that
\lambda=f_{2}(z_{T})=-C_{2}
and

f(z)=\frac{f_{2}(z)-\lambda}{f_{1}(z)}=\frac{C_{2}}{C_{1}}\cdot\frac{\sigma(z-2z_{T})^{p}+\sigma(z)^{p}}{\sigma(z-z_{T})^{p}}.

By using the fact that the Weierstrass
\sigma
-function is an odd function we get that the functions
f
and
h
are even functions around the point
z_{T}

f(z+z_{T})=\frac{C_{2}}{C_{1}}\cdot\frac{\sigma(z-z_{T})^{p}+\sigma(z+z_{T})^{p}}{\sigma(z)^{p}}=f(z_{T}-z),

h(z+z_{T})=C\frac{\sigma(z-z_{T})\sigma(z+z_{Z})}{\sigma(z)^{2}}=h(z_{T}-z).

Since
h(z)
is an even function around
z_{T}
and has a pole in
\tilde{T}
of order
2
, its Laurent series around
z_{T}
is of the form

h(z)=\sum_{k\geq-1}b_{k}(z-z_{T})^{2k}

for some coefficients
b_{k}\in K
for all integers
k\geq-1
.

The function
f(z)
is also even around
z_{T}
and has a pole in
\tilde{T}
of order at most
p-1
since
f_{1}
has a zero in
\tilde{T}
of order
p
and
f_{2}-\lambda
has a zero in
\tilde{T}
. Thus, the Laurent series of
f(z)
around
z_{T}
is of the form

f(z)=\sum_{k\geq-\frac{p-1}{2}}a_{k}(z-z_{T})^{2k}

for some coefficients
a_{k}\in K
for all integers
k\geq-\frac{p-1}{2}
.

Finally, by using the pole in the point
\tilde{T}
of order
2
of the function
h
, we can define a polynomial
P\in K[t]
of degree at most
\frac{p-1}{2}
such that it removes the singular part in the Laurent series of
f(z)
, i.e. such that we have

f(z)-P(h(z))=\sum_{k\geq 0}c_{k}(z-z_{T})^{2k}

for some coefficients
c_{k}\in K
for all integers
k\geq 0
.

Notice that the functions
h
and
f
have only a pole in the point
\tilde{T}
. Therefore, the only pole of the function
f(z)-P(h(z))
can eventually only be in the point
\tilde{T}
. But since
f(z)-P(h(z))=\displaystyle{\sum_{k\geq 0}c_{k}(z-z_{T})^{2k}}
, we know that
f(z)-P(h(z))
does not have a pole in
\tilde{T}
. Thus, the function
f(z)-P(h(z))
has no poles and hence it must be a constant function. By translating the polynomial
P
by a suitable constant, we can assume that
{f(z)-P(h(z))=0}
and from there we get that
f_{2}=\lambda+f_{1}P(h)
as desired.
∎

Remark 3.3
.

Trying to apply the standard trick using Riemann-Roch Theorem on the Riemann-Roch spaces
\mathcal{L}(nD)
for the divisor

D=(O)+(S)-\dotso+((p-1)S)

and positive integers
n
does not give us the desired equation for the twist
H_{S}^{d}
.

Namely, we have that all the functions of the form
z^{\alpha}w^{\beta}
for nonnegative integers
\alpha
and
\beta
such that
\alpha+\beta\leq n
are all in the Riemann-Roch space
\mathcal{L}(nD)
. Thus, in order to obtain that the set of function
\{z^{\alpha}w^{\beta}\,:\,\alpha+\beta\leq n,\,\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\}
is
K
-linearly dependent we need to choose an integer
n
such that the following inequality holds

\binom{n+2}{2}=\#\{z^{\alpha}w^{\beta}\,:\,\alpha+\beta\leq n,\,\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\}>\dim\mathcal{L}(nD)=np.

But, the first such integer
n
for which the above inequality holds is

n=\left\lfloor\dfrac{2p-1+\sqrt{4p^{2}-12p+1}}{2}\right\rfloor>p.

With this we are now finally ready to prove the Theorem
1.1
, i.e. that the functions
z
and
w
generate the function field
K(H_{S}^{d})
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

By Proposition
3.2
we already know that there exist constants

{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\dotso,\alpha_{\frac{p+1}{2}},\beta,\gamma\in K}
not all zero such that
\alpha_{1}z^{p}+\alpha_{2}z^{p-2}w+\dotso+\alpha_{\frac{p+1}{2}}zw^{\frac{p-1}{2}}+\beta w^{p}+\gamma=0
.
Let us denote by
C
the curve given with the above equation, i.e.

C\,:\,\alpha_{1}z^{p}+\alpha_{2}z^{p-2}w+\dotso+\alpha_{\frac{p+1}{2}}zw^{\frac{p-1}{2}}+\beta w^{p}+\gamma=0.

First we will show that the curve
C
is isomorphic to the starting elliptic curve
E
. In order to do so, consider the map
\varphi:E\to C
defined by
\varphi(x,y)=(z,w)
. We claim that
\deg{(\varphi)}=1
and from there it follows that
\varphi
is the desired isomorphisam between
E
and
C
.

Indeed, let
(z_{0},w_{0})\in C
be a generic point on
C
. The equation
z(P)=z_{0}
has exactly
\deg{(z)}=p
solutions in
E
. Furthermore, since the degree of the curve
C
is
p
, we know that there are also exactly
p
points on
C
with the first coordinate equal to
z_{0}
. As this is equal to the number of solutions of the equation
z(P)=z_{0}
, it follows that
\deg{(\varphi)}=1
.

On the other hand, we also have that
H_{S}^{d}
is isomorphic to
E
since it is a twist of
E
. Therefore, we have that
H_{S}^{d}
is isomorphic to
C
and that
\overline{K}(z,w)=\overline{K}(C)=\overline{K}(H_{S}^{d})
. Now by taking the fixed field by the twisted action of the
\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{K}/K)
by the cocycle
\xi_{d}
and using that
z
and
w
are fixed by this action we finally get
K(z,w)=K(H_{S}^{d})
. This means that the curve
C
is a model for the twist
H_{S}^{d}
and we can write

H_{S}^{d}\,:\,\alpha_{1}z^{p}+\alpha_{2}z^{p-2}w+\dotso+\alpha_{\frac{p+1}{2}}zw^{\frac{p-1}{2}}+\beta w^{p}+\gamma=0.

∎

Next, we are going to prove Theorem
1.2
about the local solubility of the twist

. In order to do that we will need the following lemmas.

The first Lemma is due to Cassels and it describes the image of the connecting homomorphisam
\delta

of good reduction (see

Lemma 3.4

(Cassels)
.

Let

be an elliptic curve over the number field

,

a prime,

a rational prime and
\delta
the connecting homomorphisam in the Kummer sequence

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces E(K_{v})/pE[K_{v}]}

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\delta}

If

is a prime of good reduction and
v\nmid p\cdot\infty
them

H_{f}^{1}(K_{v},E[p]):=\im{\delta}=H^{1}(K_{v}^{\operatorname{ur}}/K_{v},E[p]).

where

is the maximal unramified extension of

.

The next Lemma is a small modification of Lemma 2.1 in

and describes a connection to the existence of

-rational points on

and the restriction of the cocycle

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
v\in M_{K}
be a prime and
\xi_{d}^{v}
the restriction of the cocylce
\xi_{d}
to
\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{K_{v}}/K_{v})
. With the notation as above we have that
H_{S}^{d}(K_{v})\neq\emptyset
if and only if the
\{\xi_{d}^{v}\}=0
in
H^{1}(K_{v},E)
.

Proof.

K_{v}
-rational points on
H_{S}^{d}
correspond to the points on
E
that are fixed by the twisted action of the Galois group
\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{K_{v}}/K_{v})
. Therefore, we have that

\displaystyle H_{S}^{d}(K_{v})

\displaystyle\cong\left\{R\in E\,:\,R^{\sigma}=\xi_{d}^{v}(\sigma)R\quad\forall\sigma\in\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{K_{v}}/K_{v})\right\}

\displaystyle=\left\{R\in E\,:\,\xi_{d}^{v}(\sigma)=R^{\sigma}-R\quad\forall\sigma\in\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{K_{v}}/K_{v})\right\}.

∎

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Suppose that
H_{S}^{d}(K_{v})\neq\emptyset
for such prime
v
. Then by Lemma
3.5
we have that
\left\{\xi_{d}^{v}\right\}=0
. From the Kummer sequence

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces E(K_{v})/pE[K_{v}]}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{1}(K_{v},E[p])}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{1}(K_{v},E)[p]}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\delta}

it follows then that
\left\{\xi_{d}^{v}\right\}\in\im{\delta}=H_{f}^{1}(K_{v},E[p])
.
By Lemma

we have that

so we have in fact that

, we have that

Let us assume now that

∎

Abstract
. In this article we study minimal equicontinuous actions on Stone spaces, which we call subodometers , and do neither assume that the space is metrizable, nor any assumptions on the acting group. We show that the set of eigenvalues is a complete invariant for subodometers. Furthermore, we characterize minimal rotations on Stone spaces, which we call odometers , via the intersection stability of their sets of eigenvalues. We show that any non-empty family of odometers allows for a minimal common extension and a maximal common factor , that both are odometers and that they are unique up to conjugacy. We provide examples that a similar statement does not hold for subodometers. We show that subodometers are given as inverse limits of minimal finite actions, that odometers are given as inverse limits of minimal finite rotations, and present how the minimal common extension and the maximal common factor of a non-empty family of odometers can be represented as an inverse limit. We establish that a minimal action X X is a subodometer if and only if its Ellis semigroup E ⁡ ( X ) E(X) is an odometer, and present how an inverse limit representation of E ⁡ ( X ) E(X) can be derived from the representation of X X . Furthermore, we establish the existence of a universal odometer that has all subodometers as factors; as well as the existence of a maximal subodometer factor, and a maximal odometer factor of a given minimal action. Keywords. Action, Subodometer, Odometer, Rotation, Stone Space, Eigenvalue, Ellis semigroup. 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification. Primary 37B05; Secondary 54H15, 20E18.

\iota(\Lambda)=g\Gamma
if and only if
\Lambda=\Gamma^{g}
.
In particular,
\Gamma
and
\Lambda
are conjugated if and only if
G/\Gamma
and
G/\Lambda
are conjugated.
Recall that any rotation is regular.
Thus,
\Gamma\in{\operatorname{Sub_{fin}}}(G)
is normal if and only if
(G/\Gamma,G)
is a rotation, i.e. a finite odometer.

3.
Entourages, equivalence relations and
G
-tiles

3.1.
Uniformities of equicontinuous actions on Stone spaces

In order to characterize subodometers by properties of their respective uniformities we are interested in entourages that are equivalence relations. The following lemma summarizes the relevant properties of such entourages.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
X
be a compact Hausdorff space and
\rho\in\mathbb{U}_{X}
an equivalence relation.

(i)

\rho
establishes a finite partition.

(ii)

B_{\rho}(x)
is clopen for all
x\in X
.

(iii)

\rho
is clopen (in
X^{2}
).

Proof.

We first show that
B_{\rho}(x)
is open for all
x\in X
.
Consider
x^{\prime}\in B_{\rho}(x)
.
From
\rho\in\mathbb{U}_{X}
we know that
B_{\rho}(x^{\prime})
is a neighborhood of
x^{\prime}
. Furthermore,
\rho
is an equivalence relation and hence
B_{\rho}(x^{\prime})=B_{\rho}(x)
.
This shows
B_{\rho}(x)
to be open.

(i):
Since
\{B_{\rho}(x);\,x\in X\}
is an open cover of
X
and
X
is compact there exists a finite
F\subseteq X
with
X=\bigcup_{x\in F}B_{\rho}(x)=\bigcup_{x\in F}\rho[x]
.
In particular, the finite set
\{\rho[x];\,x\in F\}
is the partition induced by
\rho
.

(ii):
For
x\in X
we already know that
B_{\rho}(x)
is open.
Furthermore, it is the finite union of the other open equivalence classes and hence closed.

(iii):
By (i) and (ii),
\rho=\bigcup_{x\in X}\rho[x]^{2}
is clopen as a finite union of clopen subsets of
X^{2}
.
∎

The following characterizes subodometers via properties of the respective uniformities.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
(X,G)
be an action.

(i)

X
is a Stone space if and only if
\mathbb{U}_{X}
allows for a base consisting of equivalence relations.

(ii)

(X,G)
is an equicontinuous action on a Stone space if and only if
\mathbb{U}_{X}
allows for a base consisting of invariant equivalence relations.

Proof.

(i):
Assume first that
\mathbb{U}_{X}
allows for a base of equivalence relations.
Consider
x\in X
and a neighborhood
U
of
x
.
There exists
\epsilon\in\mathbb{U}_{X}
such that
B_{\epsilon}(x)\subseteq U
.
By our assumption, we find an equivalence relation
\rho\in\mathbb{U}_{X}
with
\rho\subseteq\epsilon
.
Clearly, we have
B_{\rho}(x)\subseteq B_{\epsilon}(x)\subseteq U
.
Furthermore, it follows from Lemma
3.1
that
B_{\rho}(x)
is clopen.
This shows that
X
is zero-dimensional, i.e. a Stone space.

For the converse assume that
X
is a Stone space and consider
\epsilon\in\mathbb{U}_{X}
.
Let
\delta\in\mathbb{U}_{X}
be symmetric and such that
\delta\delta\subseteq\epsilon
.
Since
X
is a Stone space for
x\in X
there exists
A_{x}
clopen with
x\in A_{x}\subseteq B_{\delta}(x)
.
We have
X=\bigcup_{x\in X}A_{x}
and the compactness of
X
yields that
X=\bigcup_{x\in F}A_{x}
for some finite subset
F\subseteq X
.
Enumerating
F=\{x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n}\}
and denoting
A_{1}:=A_{x_{1}}
and

A_{k}:=A_{x_{k}}\setminus\bigcup_{i=1}^{k-1}A_{i}
for
k\in\{2,\dots,n\}
we obtain a partition
\{A_{1},\dots,A_{n}\}
of
X
that consists of clopen sets.
Thus
\rho:=\bigcup_{k=1}^{n}A_{k}^{2}
is a clopen equivalence relation.
In particular, we have
\rho\in\mathbb{U}_{X}
.
For
(y,y^{\prime})\in\rho
there exists
k\in\{1,\dots,n\}
such that
(y,y^{\prime})\in A_{k}^{2}\subseteq A_{x_{k}}^{2}\subseteq B_{\delta}(x_{k})^{2}\subseteq\delta\delta\subseteq\epsilon
.
This shows
\rho\subseteq\epsilon
.

(ii):
Recall that an action is equicontinuous if and only if
\mathbb{U}_{X}
allows for a base consisting of invariant entourages.
Thus, from (i) we observe that whenever
\mathbb{U}_{X}
allows for a base consisting of invariant equivalence relations, then
(X,G)
is equicontinuous and
X
is a Stone space.

For the converse assume that
(X,G)
is an equicontinuous action on a Stone space and consider
\epsilon\in\mathbb{U}_{X}
.
By (i) there exists an equivalence relation
\rho^{\prime}\in\mathbb{U}_{X}
with
\rho^{\prime}\subseteq\epsilon
. Denote
\rho:=\bigcap_{g\in G}g.\rho^{\prime}
and note that
\rho
is an invariant equivalence relation with
\rho\subseteq\rho^{\prime}\subseteq\epsilon
.
Since
(X,G)
is equicontinuous there exists an invariant
\delta\in\mathbb{U}_{X}
with
\delta\subseteq\rho^{\prime}
and it follows from

\delta=\bigcap_{g\in G}g.\delta\subseteq\rho
that
\rho\in\mathbb{U}_{X}
.
∎

3.2.
G
-tiles

The following notion will be an essential tool in the study of subodometers.

Definition 3.3
.

Let
(X,G)
be an action.
An open subset
A\subseteq X
is called a
G
-tile
if
\{g.A;\,g\in G\}
is a partition of
X
. We refer to this partition as the
partition induced by
A
and to the respective equivalence relation as the
equivalence relation induced by
A
.

Remark 3.4
.

Since
X
is compact, the induced partition of a
G
-tile is finite and hence any
G
-tile is clopen.

For minimal actions
G
-tiles and invariant equivalence relations that are entourages are closely related.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
(X,G)
be a minimal action.

(i)

The equivalence relation
\rho
induced by a
G
-tile
A
is an invariant clopen entourage and satisfies
B_{\rho}(x)=A
for all
x\in A
.

(ii)

If
\rho\in\mathbb{U}_{X}
is an invariant equivalence relation, then
B_{\rho}(x)
is a
G
-tile for any
x\in X
.

Proof.

(i):
From
A\in\{g.A;\,g\in G\}
we observe that
B_{\rho}(x)=\rho[x]=A
holds for all
x\in A
.
Choose
F\subseteq X
finite with
\{g.A;\,g\in F\}=\{g.A;\,g\in G\}
and note that
\rho=\bigcup_{g\in F}g.A^{2}
is the finite union of clopen sets and hence clopen.
Since
\rho
is reflexive we observe
\rho
to be an entourage.
For
g\in G
and
(x,x^{\prime})\in\rho
there exists
h\in G
with
(x,x^{\prime})\in h.A^{2}
.
It follows that
(g.x,g.x^{\prime})\in(gh).A^{2}\subseteq\rho
, which shows
\rho
to be invariant.

(ii):
Let
x\in X
.
It follows from Lemma
3.1
that
B_{\rho}(x^{\prime})
is open for all
x^{\prime}\in X
.
Thus, the minimality of
(X,G)
yields that for
x^{\prime}\in X
there exists
g\in G
with
g.x\in B_{\rho}(x^{\prime})
. Since
\rho
is an invariant equivalence relation we observe
x^{\prime}\in B_{\rho}(g.x)=g.B_{\rho}(x)
.
This shows
\bigcup_{g\in G}g.B_{\rho}(x)=X
and establishes
\{g.B_{\rho}(x);\,g\in G\}=\{\rho[g.x];\,g\in G\}
as the partition given by the equivalence relation
\rho
.
∎

3.3.
Characterization of subodometers via
G
-tiles

Theorem 3.6
.

A minimal action
(X,G)
is a subodometer if and only if the topology of
X
allows for a base of
G
-tiles.

Proof.

If
(X,G)
is a subodometer, then we know from Lemma
3.1
and Proposition
3.2
that
\mathbb{U}_{X}
allows for a base
\mathbb{B}
of invariant closed equivalence relations.
From Lemma
3.5
we observe that
\{B_{\epsilon}(x);\,(x,\epsilon)\in X\times\mathbb{B}\}
is a base for the topology of
X
that consists of
G
-tiles.

For the converse assume that
X
allows for a base of
G
-tiles for its topology. By Proposition
3.2
it suffices to show that
\mathbb{U}_{X}
allows for a base of invariant equivalence relations.
Let
\epsilon\in\mathbb{U}_{X}
.
Consider a symmetric entourage
\delta\in\mathbb{U}_{X}
with
\delta\delta\subseteq\epsilon
.
For
x\in X
choose a
G
-tile with
x\in A_{x}\subseteq B_{\delta}(x)
and recall that
G
-tiles are open.
Since
X
is compact we observe that there exists a finite subset
F\subseteq X
such that
\bigcup_{x\in F}A_{x}=X
.
For
x\in F
denote
\rho_{x}
for the equivalence relation induced by
A_{x}
. From Lemma
3.5
we know that
\rho_{x}\in\mathbb{U}_{X}
is a clopen invariant equivalence relation.
Since
F
is finite also
\rho:=\bigcap_{x\in F}\rho_{x}
is a clopen invariant equivalence relation. In particular, we have
\rho\in\mathbb{U}_{X}
.
To show that
\rho\subseteq\epsilon
consider
(x_{1},x_{2})\in\rho
.
There exists
x\in F
such that
x_{1}\in A_{x}=\rho_{x}[x]
.
From
(x_{1},x_{2})\in\rho\subseteq\rho_{x}
we observe that

x_{2}\in\rho_{x}[x_{1}]=\rho_{x}[x]=A_{x}
and hence

(x_{1},x_{2})\in A_{x}^{2}\subseteq B_{\delta}(x)^{2}\subseteq\delta\delta\subseteq\epsilon
.
∎

4.
Subodometers generated by scales

We next show that any subodometer is conjugated to an inverse limit of finite minimal actions. For this we will need the following notions.
Recall that
{\operatorname{Sub_{fin}}}(G)
denotes the set of all finite index subgroups
\Gamma\leq G
.

Definition 4.1
.

A subset
S\subseteq{\operatorname{Sub_{fin}}}(G)
is called

•

a scale
if
S
is non-empty and for
\Gamma,\Gamma^{\prime}\in S
there exists
\Lambda\in S
with
\Lambda\subseteq\Gamma\cap\Gamma^{\prime}
.

•

upward closed
if for all
\Gamma\in S
and
\Lambda\in{\operatorname{Sub_{fin}}}(G)
with
\Gamma\subseteq\Lambda
we have
\Lambda\in S
.

•

a filter
if it is an upward closed scale.

Remark 4.2
.

Our definition of scale differs from the definition of scale considered in
[
4
]
, where scales are given by sequences
(\Gamma_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
in
{\operatorname{Sub_{fin}}}(G)
with
\Gamma_{n+1}\subseteq\Gamma_{n}
.
We will see in Section
6
below that these sequences can be used to characterize metrizable subodometers.

Remark 4.3
.

A subset
S\subseteq{\operatorname{Sub_{fin}}}(G)
is called
intersection closed
(also
intersection stable
) if for all
\Gamma,\Gamma^{\prime}\in S
we have
\Gamma\cap\Gamma^{\prime}\in S
.
Any intersection closed subset of
{\operatorname{Sub_{fin}}}(G)
is a scale. Furthermore, any filter is intersection closed.

Let
S\subseteq{\operatorname{Sub_{fin}}}(G)
be a scale.
Note that for
\Gamma_{1},\Gamma_{2}\in S
with
\Gamma_{1}\leq\Gamma_{2}
there exists a unique factor map
\pi_{\Gamma_{2}}^{\Gamma_{1}}\colon G/\Gamma_{1}\to G/\Gamma_{2}
with
\Gamma_{1}\mapsto\Gamma_{2}
.
For
\Gamma_{1},\Gamma_{2},\Gamma_{3}\in{\operatorname{Sub_{fin}}}(G)
with
\Gamma_{1}\leq\Gamma_{2}\leq\Gamma_{3}
we have
\pi_{\Gamma_{3}}^{\Gamma_{1}}=\pi_{\Gamma_{3}}^{\Gamma_{2}}\circ\pi_{\Gamma_{2}}^{\Gamma_{1}}
.
Thus, any scale
S
induces an inverse system
(\pi_{\Lambda}^{\Gamma})_{(\Gamma,\Lambda)\in S_{*}^{2}}
of factor maps, where we abbreviate

S_{*}^{2}:=\{(\Gamma,\Lambda)\in S^{2};\,\Gamma\leq\Lambda\}
.
See
[
19
, Section 1.1]
for further details on inverse systems and inverse limits.

Definition 4.4
.

Let
S\subseteq{\operatorname{Sub_{fin}}}(G)
be a scale.
The
S
-subdometer
is given by the inverse limit

\varprojlim_{\Gamma\in S}G\big/\Gamma:=\Bigl\{(x_{\Gamma})_{\Gamma\in S}\in\prod_{\Gamma\in S}G\big/\Gamma;\,\pi_{\Lambda}^{\Gamma}(x_{\Gamma})=x_{\Lambda}\text{ for all }(\Gamma,\Lambda)\in S_{*}^{2}\Bigr\}.

For a scale
S
we say that a subodometer
(X,G)
is
generated by
S
, whenever it is conjugated to the
S
-subodometer.

Remark 4.5
.

Note that the
S
-subodometer is the minimal component of the equicontinuous action of
G
on
\prod_{\Gamma\in S}G/\Gamma
that contains
(\Gamma)_{\Gamma\in S}
.
Since
\prod_{\Gamma\in S}G/\Gamma
is a Stone space we observe that
(\varprojlim_{\Gamma\in S}G/\Gamma,G)
is a subodometer, justifying the terminology.

We will next establish that any subodometer is generated by some scale.
For this we will need the following.

Definition 4.6
.

Let
(X,G)
be a minimal action.
For
x\in X
we denote
{\rm Eig}_{x}(X,G)
for the set of all
\Gamma\in{\operatorname{Sub_{fin}}}(G)
for which there exists a factor map
\pi\colon X\to G/\Gamma
with
\pi(x)=\Gamma
.

Theorem 4.7
.

Let
(X,G)
be a minimal action and
x\in X
.

(i)

{\rm Eig}_{x}(X,G)
is a filter.

(ii)

If
(X,G)
is a subodometer then

(X,G)
is generated by
{\rm Eig}_{x}(X,G)
.

For the proof of Theorem
4.7
we first study hitting times.
Let
(X,G)
be a minimal action.
For
x\in X
and
B\subseteq X
we denote

G(x,B):=\{g\in G;\,g.x\in B\}

for the set of
hitting times
of
x
to
B
.

Lemma 4.8
.

Let
(X,G)
be a minimal action and
x\in X
.
For any
x\in X
we have that

{\rm Eig}_{x}(X,G)=\{G(x,B);\,B~G\text{-tile with }x\in B\}
.

Proof.

’
\subseteq
’:
Consider
\Gamma\in{\rm Eig}_{x}(X,G)
and a factor map
\pi\colon X\to G/\Gamma
with
\pi(x)=\Gamma
.
Denote
\rho:=R(\pi)
for the fibre relation and note that
\rho
is an invariant closed equivalence relation.
Since
G/\Gamma
is finite it establishes finitely many equivalence classes which are easily observed to be clopen.
It follows that
\rho
is a finite union of clopen sets and hence clopen.
In particular
\rho
is an entourage and Lemma
3.5
yields that
B:=B_{\rho}(x)
is a
G
-tile containing
x
that satisfies

G(x,B)=G(\pi(x),\pi(B))=G(\Gamma,\{\Gamma\})=G_{0}(\Gamma)=\Gamma.

’
\supseteq
’:
Consider a
G
-tile
B
with
x\in B
.
Denote
\rho
for the induced equivalence relation and recall from Lemma
3.5
that
\rho
is an invariant clopen entourage.
In particular, it is an invariant closed equivalence relation.
Consider the factor map
\pi\colon X\to G/\rho
and note that
G/\rho
is finite.
We thus have

\displaystyle\Gamma:=G(x,B)=G(\pi(x),\pi(B))=G(\pi(x),\{\pi(x)\})=G_{0}(\pi(x))\in{\operatorname{Sub_{fin}}}(G).

It follows from
G_{0}(\pi(x))=\Gamma
that there exists a conjugacy
\iota\colon X\big/\rho\to G/\Gamma
with
\iota(\pi(x))=\Gamma
.
This shows
G(x,B)=\Gamma\in{\rm Eig}_{x}(X,G)
.
∎

To show that
{\rm Eig}_{x}(X,G)
is a scale it is convenient to first establish the following.

Lemma 4.9
.

Let
(X,G)
be a minimal action and
x\in X
.
For
G
-tiles
B
and
B^{\prime}
containing
x
there exists a
G
-tile
B^{\prime\prime}
with
x\in B^{\prime\prime}\subseteq B\cap B^{\prime}
.

Proof.

Denote
\rho
and
\rho^{\prime}
for the equivalence relations induced by
B
and
B^{\prime}
, respectively.
Denote
\xi:=\rho\cap\rho^{\prime}
and note that
\xi
is an equivalence relation.
It follows from Lemma
3.5
that
\rho
and
\rho^{\prime}
are invariant clopen entourages and hence also
\xi
is an invariant clopen entourage.
Thus, by Lemma
3.5

B^{\prime\prime}:=B_{\xi}(x)\subseteq B\cap B^{\prime}
is a

-tile that contains

.
∎

We will also need the following.

Lemma 4.10
.

Let
(X,G)
and
(Y,G)
be equicontinuous minimal actions and consider
x\in X
and
y\in Y
.
There exists a factor map
\pi\colon X\to Y
with
\pi(x)=y
if and only if for any neighborhood
V
of

there exists a neighborhood
U
of

such that

G(x,U)\subseteq G(y,V)
.

Proof.

Since
\pi
is continuous the first property implies the second.
For the converse note that the second property implies that
G_{0}(x)\subseteq G_{0}(y)
.
This observation allows us to consider the mapping

\phi\colon G.x\to Y
given by

.
By minimality
G.x
is dense in
X
and
\phi
has a dense image.
We next show that
\phi
is

, i.e. that for any
\epsilon\in\mathbb{U}_{Y}
there exists
\delta\in\mathbb{U}_{X}
such that
for all

with
(x_{1},x_{2})\in\delta
we have
(\phi(x_{1}),\phi(x_{2}))\in\epsilon
.
This will allow to extend
\phi
to a continuous map
\pi\colon X\to Y

[
14
, Theorem 6.26]
. It is straightforward to verify that

is then a factor map that satisfies

.

To show the uniform continuity of
\phi
on
G.x

consider
\epsilon\in\mathbb{U}_{Y}
.
Since
X
is equicontinuous, we assume w.l.o.g. that
\epsilon
is invariant.
We observe that
V:=B_{\epsilon}(y)
is a neighborhood of
y
and by our assumption there exists a neighborhood
U
of
x
, such that
G(x,U)\subseteq G(y,V)
.
Choose an invariant
\delta\in\mathbb{U}_{X}
such that

.
For

with
(x_{1},x_{2})\in\delta
there exist
g_{1},g_{2}\in G
with
g_{i}.x=x_{i}
.
Since

is invariant we have

g_{2}^{-1}g_{1}.x\in B_{\delta}(x)\subseteq U

and hence

g_{2}^{-1}g_{1}\in G(x,U)\subseteq G(y,V)
.
It follows that

g_{2}^{-1}g_{1}.y\in V=B_{\epsilon}(y)

and the invariance of

(\phi(x_{1}),\phi(x_{2}))=(g_{1}.y,g_{2}.y)\in\epsilon.

∎

Proof of Theorem

:

(i):

.

5.
Factors and eigenvalues

5.1.
Factors of subodometers

Proposition 5.1
.

Any factor of a subodometer is also a subodometer.

Proof.

Let
(X,G)
be a subodometer and consider a factor map
\pi\colon X\to Y
.
Since
(X,G)
is minimal and equicontinuous, so is
(Y,G)
.
To show that
Y
is a Stone space, let
\mathcal{B}
be a clopen base for the topology of
X
.
As a factor map
\pi
is closed.
Furthermore, factor maps between minimal equicontinuous actions are open
[
1
, Theorem 7.3]
.
Thus,
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}:=\{\pi(B);\,B\in\mathcal{B}\}
consists of clopen sets.
To show that
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}
is a base for the topology of
Y
consider
y\in Y
and an open neighborhood
U
of
y
.
Choose
x\in\pi^{-1}(y)
.
Since
\pi^{-1}(U)
is open we find
B\in\mathcal{B}
with
x\in B\subseteq\pi^{-1}(U)
.
We thus have
y=\pi(x)\in\pi(B)\subseteq\pi(\pi^{-1}(U))=U
.
This establishes
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}
as a base for the topology of
Y
.
∎

5.2.
Eigenvalues of minimal actions

Definition 5.2
.

Let
(X,G)
be a minimal action.
A finite index subgroup
\Gamma\leq G
is called an
eigenvalue
of
(X,G)
, whenever there exists a factor map
\pi\colon X\to G/\Gamma
. We denote
{\rm Eig}(X,G)
for the set of all eigenvalues of
(X,G)
.

Remark 5.3
.

In the case of
\mathbb{Z}
-actions, an
eigenvalue
is usually defined as a real number
\alpha
such that there exists a factor map from the system
(X,\mathbb{Z})
onto the rotation of the circle by angle
\alpha
. When
\alpha\in\mathbb{Q}
, one says that
\alpha
is a
rational eigenvalue
. In this setting, what we call an eigenvalue in this article corresponds to subgroups of the form
p\mathbb{Z}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}
for which
1/p
is a rational eigenvalue. See, for instance,
[
18
]
for results involving the rational eigenvalues of a minimal
\mathbb{Z}
-action on the Cantor set.

Note that whenever
(Y,G)
is a factor of
(X,G)
, then
{\rm Eig}(Y,G)
is contained in
{\rm Eig}(X,G)
. In particular conjugated minimal actions have the same eigenvalues.
In general
{\rm Eig}
is not a complete conjugacy invariant as illustrated by the next example.

Example 5.4
.

Let
(X,G)
be an action for which
X
is connected.
Any factor of
X
is connected and hence
{\rm Eig}(X,G)=\{G\}
.
Thus
X
and the trivial action on one point have the same eigenvalues.

We next show that
{\rm Eig}
is a complete conjugacy invariant for subodometers.

Theorem 5.5
.

(i)

Let
(X,G)
be a minimal action.
A subodometer
(Y,G)
is a factor of
(X,G)
if and only if
{\rm Eig}(Y,G)\subseteq{\rm Eig}(X,G).

(ii)

Two subodometers are conjugated if and only if they have the same eigenvalues.

For the proof we first observe the following.

Lemma 5.6
.

Let
\pi\colon X\to Y
be a factor map. If
Y
is finite, then there are at most
|Y|
factor maps
X\to Y
.

Proof.

Fix
x_{0}\in X
.
Since
X
is minimal any factor map
\pi\colon X\to Y
is determined by
\pi(x_{0})
.
For the latter we have at most
|Y|
many choices.
∎

Proof of Theorem
5.5
:

Note that (ii) follows from (i), since minimal equicontinuous actions are coalescent. In order to show (i) it remains to show that
{\rm Eig}(Y,G)\subseteq{\rm Eig}(X,G)
implies that
(Y,G)
is a factor of
(X,G)
.
By Theorem
4.7
we assume w.l.o.g. that
(Y,G)
is the
S
-subodometer for some scale
S\subseteq{\operatorname{Sub_{fin}}}(G)
.

For
\Gamma\in S
we denote
A_{\Gamma}
for the set of all
x\in X
for which there exists a factor map
\pi\colon X\to G/\Gamma
with
\pi(x)=\Gamma
.
Since
\Gamma\in{\rm Eig}(X,G)
we observe
A_{\Gamma}
to be non-empty.
Recall from Lemma
5.6
that there exist only finitely many factor maps
X\to G/\Gamma
.
Thus, the union

A_{\Gamma}=\bigcup_{\pi}\pi^{-1}(\Gamma),

taken over all such factor maps
\pi
, is a finite union of closed subsets of
X
and hence closed.
This shows that
\mathcal{A}:=\{A_{\Gamma};\,\Gamma\in S\}
consists of non-empty closed subsets of
X
.

Consider
\Gamma,\Lambda\in S
with
\Gamma\subseteq\Lambda
.
The canonical factor map
\pi_{\Lambda}^{\Gamma}\colon G/\Gamma\to G/\Lambda
satisfies
\pi_{\Lambda}^{\Gamma}(\Gamma)=\Lambda
and we observe
A_{\Gamma}\subseteq A_{\Lambda}
.
This shows that the mapping
S\ni\Gamma\mapsto A_{\Gamma}\in\mathcal{A}
is monotone.
Since
S
is a scale it is straightforward to observe that
\mathcal{A}
has the
finite intersection property
, i.e. that any finite subset
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\mathcal{A}
satisfies
\bigcap_{A\in\mathcal{F}}A\neq\emptyset
.
Since
X
is compact, we observe that
A:=\bigcap_{A\in\mathcal{A}}A
is non-empty
[
16
, Theorem 26.9]
.

Fix
x_{0}\in A
.
For
\Gamma\in S
we have
x_{0}\in A_{\Gamma}
and hence there exists a factor map
\pi_{\Gamma}\colon X\to G/\Gamma
with
\pi_{\Gamma}(x_{0})=\Gamma
.
We observe that

\hat{\pi}\colon X\to\prod_{\Gamma\in S}G/\Gamma
defined by
x\mapsto(\pi_{\Gamma}(x))
has
\hat{\pi}(x_{0})=(\Gamma)_{\Gamma\in S}
in its image and hence restricts to a factor map
\pi\colon X\to Y
.
∎

5.3.
From scales to eigenvalues

Given a scale
S
, it is natural to ask how the eigenvalues of the
S
-subodometer can be derived from
S
.
To answer this question in detail we introduce the following notions.

Definition 5.7
.

Let
S\subseteq{\operatorname{Sub_{fin}}}(G)
be a subset.

•

The
upper hull

S^{\mathfrak{u}}
of
S
is defined as the set of all
\Gamma\in{\operatorname{Sub_{fin}}}(G)
for which there exists
\Lambda\in S
with
\Lambda\subseteq\Gamma
.

•

The
conjugation hull

S^{\mathfrak{c}}
of
S
is defined as the set of all
\Gamma\in{\operatorname{Sub_{fin}}}(G)
for which there exists
g\in G
with
\Gamma^{g}\in S
.

•

The
upper conjugation hull

\left\llbracket S\right\rrbracket
of
S
is defined as the set of all
\Gamma\in{\operatorname{Sub_{fin}}}(G)
for which there exist
(g,\Lambda)\in G\times S
with
\Lambda\subseteq\Gamma^{g}
, i.e. by
\left\llbracket S\right\rrbracket:=(S^{\mathfrak{u}})^{\mathfrak{c}}
.

Remark 5.8
.

For a scale
S
the upper hull
S^{\mathfrak{u}}
is a filter.

Remark 5.9
.

It is straightforward to show that the intersection
\bigcap_{S\in\mathcal{S}}S
and the union
\bigcup_{S\in\mathcal{S}}S
of a family
\mathcal{S}
of upward closed subsets of
{\operatorname{Sub_{fin}}}(G)
are also upward closed.
For a non-empty subset
S\subseteq{\operatorname{Sub_{fin}}}(G)
the upper hull
S^{\mathfrak{u}}
is the smallest upward closed subset of
{\operatorname{Sub_{fin}}}(G)
that contains
S
.

Definition 5.10
.

A subset
S\subseteq{\operatorname{Sub_{fin}}}(G)
is called
conjugation invariant
whenever for
\Gamma\in S
and
g\in G
we have
\Gamma^{g}\in S
.

Remark 5.11
.

It is straightforward to show that the intersection
\bigcap_{S\in\mathcal{S}}S
and the union
\bigcup_{S\in\mathcal{S}}S
of a family
\mathcal{S}
of conjugation invariant subsets of
{\operatorname{Sub_{fin}}}(G)
are also conjugation invariant.
For a non-empty subset
S\subseteq{\operatorname{Sub_{fin}}}(G)
the conjugation hull
S^{\mathfrak{c}}
is the smallest conjugation invariant subset of
{\operatorname{Sub_{fin}}}(G)
that contains
S
.

Remark 5.12
.

Let
S\subseteq{\operatorname{Sub_{fin}}}(G)
.
Whenever
S
is conjugation invariant, then also
S^{\mathfrak{u}}
is conjugation invariant.
Whenever
S
is upward closed, then also
S^{\mathfrak{c}}
is upward closed.
This allows to observe that
\left\llbracket S\right\rrbracket=(S^{\mathfrak{c}})^{\mathfrak{u}}=(S^{\mathfrak{u}})^{\mathfrak{c}}
is the smallest upward closed and conjugation invariant subset of
{\operatorname{Sub_{fin}}}(G)
that contains
S
.

Proposition 5.13
.

Let
(X,G)
be a minimal action.

{\rm Eig}(X,G)
is upward closed and conjugation invariant.
For
x\in X
we have

{\rm Eig}(X,G)={\rm Eig}_{x}(X,G)^{\mathfrak{c}}.

Proof.

It is straightforward to observe that
{\rm Eig}(X,G)
is upward closed and conjugation invariant.
Thus, from
{\rm Eig}_{x}(X,G)\subseteq{\rm Eig}(X,G)
we have
{\rm Eig}_{x}(X,G)^{\mathfrak{c}}\subseteq{\rm Eig}(X,G)
.

For the converse consider
\Gamma\in{\rm Eig}(X,G)
and a factor map
\pi\colon X\to G/\Gamma
.
Let
g\in G
be such that
\pi(x)=g\Gamma
.
As discussed in Subsection
2.4
there exists a conjugacy
\iota\colon G/\Gamma\to G/\Gamma^{g}
with
\iota(g\Gamma)=\Gamma^{g}
.
Thus, the factor map
\phi:=\iota\circ\pi\colon X\to G/\Gamma^{g}
satisfies
\phi(x)=\Gamma^{g}
.
This shows
\Gamma^{g}\in{\rm Eig}_{x}(X,G)
and hence
\Gamma\in{\rm Eig}_{x}(X,G)^{\mathfrak{c}}
.
∎

The transition from a scale to the eigenvalues of the respective subodometer can be described as follows.

Proposition 5.14
.

Let
S\subseteq{\operatorname{Sub_{fin}}}(G)
be a scale and denote
(X,G)
for the
S
-odometer. Denote
x:=(\Gamma)_{\Gamma\in S}
and note that
x\in X
.
We have

(i)

{\rm Eig}_{x}(X,G)=S^{\mathfrak{u}}
and

(ii)

{\rm Eig}(X,G)=\left\llbracket S\right\rrbracket
.

Proof.

(i):
Recall that
X
is given as the minimal component of
\hat{X}:=\prod_{\Gamma\in S}G/\Gamma
containing
x
.
Denote
\hat{\pi}_{\Gamma}\colon\hat{X}\to G/\Gamma
for the respective projections.
The restriction
\pi_{\Gamma}\colon X\to G/\Gamma
is a factor map with
\pi_{\Gamma}(x)=\Gamma
.
We thus have
\Gamma\in{\rm Eig}_{x}(X,G)
and observe
S\subseteq{\rm Eig}_{x}(X,G)
.
From Theorem
4.7
we know that
{\rm Eig}_{x}(X,G)
is a filter. Since filters are upward closed we observe that
S^{\mathfrak{u}}\subseteq{\rm Eig}_{x}(X,G)
.

For the converse consider
\Gamma\in{\rm Eig}_{x}(X,G)
and a factor map
\pi\colon X\to G/\Gamma
with
\pi(x)=\Gamma
.
From
[
19
, Lemma 1.1.16]
it follows that there exist
\Lambda\in S
and a continuous surjection
\phi\colon G/\Lambda\to G/\Gamma
with
\pi=\phi\circ\pi_{\Lambda}
.
To see that
\phi
inherits the equivariance from
\pi
and
\pi_{\Lambda}
consider
g\in G
and
y\in G/\Lambda
.
For
x^{\prime}\in\pi_{\Lambda}^{-1}(y)
we have

\displaystyle\phi(g.y)=\phi(g.\pi_{\Lambda}(x^{\prime}))=\phi(\pi_{\Lambda}(g.x^{\prime}))=\pi(g.x^{\prime})=g.\pi(x^{\prime})=g.\phi(y).

This establishes
\phi
as a factor map.
Furthermore, we have

\displaystyle\phi(\Lambda)=\phi(\pi_{\Lambda}(x))=\pi(x)=\Gamma

and hence

\Lambda=G_{0}(\Lambda)\subseteq G_{0}(\phi(\Lambda))=G_{0}(\Gamma)=\Gamma.

This shows
\Gamma\in S^{\mathfrak{u}}
.

(ii):
From (i) and Proposition
5.13
we observe

\displaystyle{\rm Eig}(X,G)

\displaystyle={\rm Eig}_{x}(X,G)^{\mathfrak{c}}=(S^{\mathfrak{u}})^{\mathfrak{c}}=\left\llbracket S\right\rrbracket.\qed

If
S
is a scale and
(X,G)
a subodometer with
{\rm Eig}(X,G)=\left\llbracket S\right\rrbracket
, then the
S
-subodometer
(X^{\prime},G)
satisfies
{\rm Eig}(X^{\prime},G)=\left\llbracket S\right\rrbracket={\rm Eig}(X,G)
and Theorem
5.5
yields that
(X,G)
and
(X^{\prime},G)
are conjugated. This shows the following.

Corollary 5.15
.

A subodometer
(X,G)
is generated by a scale
S
if and only if
{\rm Eig}(X,G)=\left\llbracket S\right\rrbracket
.

5.4.
Equivalent scales

Combining Theorem
5.5
and Proposition
5.14
also yields the following.

Corollary 5.16
.

Let
S
and
S^{\prime}
be scales that generate
(X,G)
and
(X^{\prime},G)
, respectively.

(i)

(X,G)
is a factor of
(X^{\prime},G)
if and only if
\left\llbracket S\right\rrbracket\subseteq\left\llbracket S^{\prime}\right\rrbracket
.

(ii)

(X,G)
and
(X^{\prime},G)
are conjugated if and only if
\left\llbracket S\right\rrbracket=\left\llbracket S^{\prime}\right\rrbracket
.

To gain a sufficient condition for
\left\llbracket S\right\rrbracket=\left\llbracket S^{\prime}\right\rrbracket
we introduce the following.

Definition 5.17
.

Consider subsets
S,S^{\prime}\subseteq{\operatorname{Sub_{fin}}}(G)
.
We say that
S^{\prime}

dominates
S
if for
\Gamma\in S
there exists
\Gamma^{\prime}\in S^{\prime}
with
\Gamma^{\prime}\subseteq\Gamma
.
We say that
S
and
S^{\prime}
are
equivalent
if
S
dominates
S^{\prime}
and
S^{\prime}
dominates
S
.

Remark 5.18
.

S^{\prime}
dominates
S
if and only if
S^{\mathfrak{u}}\subseteq(S^{\prime})^{\mathfrak{u}}
.
In particular, if a scale
S^{\prime}
dominates a scale
S
, then
\left\llbracket S\right\rrbracket\subseteq\left\llbracket S^{\prime}\right\rrbracket
and hence the
S
-subodometer is a factor of the
S^{\prime}
-subodometer.

It also follows that
S
and
S^{\prime}
are equivalent if and only if
S^{\mathfrak{u}}=(S^{\prime})^{\mathfrak{u}}
. Thus for equivalent scales
S
and
S^{\prime}
we have
\left\llbracket S\right\rrbracket=\left\llbracket S^{\prime}\right\rrbracket
and hence the respective subodometers are conjugated.

Note that equivalence of scales is not necessary for inducing conjugated subodometers as illustrated by the next example.

Example 5.19
.

Let
G
be a group and
\Gamma\leq G
be a non-normal finite index subgroup. Let
g\in G
with
\Gamma^{g}\neq\Gamma
.
Note that
S:=\{\Gamma\}
and
S^{\prime}:=\{\Gamma^{g}\}
are scales that are not equivalent.
Nevertheless, we have
S^{\mathfrak{c}}=(S^{\prime})^{\mathfrak{c}}
and hence
\left\llbracket S\right\rrbracket=(S^{\mathfrak{c}})^{\mathfrak{u}}=((S^{\prime})^{\mathfrak{c}})^{\mathfrak{u}}=\left\llbracket S\right\rrbracket
. Thus, the respective subodometers are conjugated.

6.
Metrizable subodometers

6.1.
Finite subodometers

In order to characterize metrizable subodometers we first study finite subodometers.

Lemma 6.1
.

A subodometer
(X,G)
is finite if and only if
{\rm Eig}(X,G)
is finite.

Proof.

We assume w.l.o.g. that
(X,G)
is the
S
-subodometer for some scale
S
.
Clearly, whenever
{\rm Eig}(X,G)
is finite, then also
S
is finite and hence
X
is finite as a subset of the finite space
\prod_{\Gamma\in S}G/\Gamma
.

For the converse assume that
X
is finite and choose
x\in X
.
It follows that
X
only contains finitely many
G
-tiles and Lemma
4.8
implies that
{\rm Eig}_{x}(X,G)
is finite.
Since any finite index subgroup only has finitely many conjugates it follows from Proposition
5.13
that
{\rm Eig}(X,G)={\rm Eig}_{x}(X,G)^{\mathfrak{c}}
is finite.
∎

From Proposition
5.14
and Lemma
6.1
we observe the following.

Corollary 6.2
.

For any
\Gamma\in{\operatorname{Sub_{fin}}}(G)
we have that
\left\llbracket\{\Gamma\}\right\rrbracket
is finite.

6.2.
Chains

In the literature on subodometers, such as
[
4
]
only scales of the form
(\Gamma_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
in
{\operatorname{Sub_{fin}}}(G)
with
\Gamma_{n+1}\subseteq\Gamma_{n}
are considered.
We will show in Theorem
6.6
below that the inverse limits along this particular type of scale yield exactly the metrizable subodometers.
We begin our discussion with the following definition.

Definition 6.3
.

A non-empty subset
S\subseteq{\operatorname{Sub_{fin}}}(G)
is called a
chain
if for any
\Gamma,\Lambda\in S
we have
\Gamma\subseteq\Lambda
or
\Lambda\subseteq\Gamma
.

Clearly any chain is a scale. Chains are exactly the type of scales considered in the literature as we present next.

Lemma 6.4
.

Any chain
S
is countable and can be written in the form
S=\{\Gamma_{n};\,n\in\mathbb{N}\}
with
\Gamma_{n+1}\subseteq\Gamma_{n}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Proof.

Let
S
be a chain and consider
i\colon S\to\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
given by
\Gamma\mapsto[G\colon\Gamma]
.
Consider
\Gamma,\Lambda\in S
with
i(\Gamma)=i(\Lambda)
.
Since
S
is a chain we have
\Gamma\subseteq\Lambda
or
\Lambda\subseteq\Gamma
.
It follows from Remark
2.1
that
\Gamma=\Lambda
. This shows
i
to be injective and hence
S
inherits the countability from
\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
.

Clearly, whenever
S
is finite, then
S
can be written in the form
S=\{\Gamma_{n};\,n\in\mathbb{N}\}
with
\Gamma_{n+1}\subseteq\Gamma_{n}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
If
S
is infinite let
(k_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a strictly increasing sequence in
\mathbb{N}
with
i(S)=\{k_{n};\,n\in\mathbb{N}\}
.
For
n\in\mathbb{N}
denote
\Gamma_{n}
for the unique preimage of
k_{n}
under
i
.
Clearly, we have
S=\{\Gamma_{n};\,n\in\mathbb{N}\}
.
Since
i
is monotone we observe
\Gamma_{n+1}\subseteq\Gamma_{n}
.
∎

The following insight will allow us to show that countable scales and chains give the same type of subodometer.

Lemma 6.5
.

For any countable scale there exists an equivalent chain.

Proof.

Let
S
be a countable scale and enumerate
S=\{\Gamma_{n};\,n\in\mathbb{N}\}
.
For
n\in\mathbb{N}
define
\Lambda_{n}:=\bigcap_{k=1}^{n}\Gamma_{k}
and note that
S^{\prime}:=\{\Lambda_{n};\,n\in\mathbb{N}\}
is a chain.
From
\Lambda_{n}\subseteq\Gamma_{n}
we observe that
S^{\prime}
dominates
S
.
Furthermore, since
S
is a scale, for
n\in\mathbb{N}
we find
m\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\Gamma_{m}\subseteq\bigcap_{k=1}^{n}\Gamma_{k}=\Lambda_{n}
. This shows that
S
dominates
S^{\prime}
.
∎

6.3.
Characterization of metrizable subodometers

Theorem 6.6
.

Let
(X,G)
be a subodometer.
The following statements are equivalent.

(i)

X
is metrizable.

(ii)

(X,G)
is generated by a chain.

(iii)

(X,G)
is generated by a countable scale.

(iv)

{\rm Eig}(X,G)
is countable.

(v)

The topology of
X
has a countable base of
G
-tiles.

(vi)

\mathbb{U}_{X}
has a countable base consisting of invariant equivalence relations.

For the proof of Theorem
6.6
we need the following Lemma.

Lemma 6.7
.

For any countable subset
S\subseteq{\operatorname{Sub_{fin}}}(G)
also
\left\llbracket S\right\rrbracket
is countable.

Proof.

For
\Gamma\in S
we know from Corollary
6.2
that
\left\llbracket\{\Gamma\}\right\rrbracket
is finite.
Furthermore, it follows from the definition of the upper conjugation hull that
\left\llbracket S\right\rrbracket=\bigcup_{\Gamma\in S}\left\llbracket\{\Gamma\}\right\rrbracket
.
We thus observe
\left\llbracket S\right\rrbracket
to be a countable union of finite sets and hence to be countable.
∎

Proof of Theorem
6.6
:

Note that a compact Hausdorff space is metrizable if and only if its uniformity allows for a countable base
[
14
, Theorem 6.13]
. Thus, the equivalence of (i) and (vi) can be observed from Proposition
3.2
.

(i)
\Rightarrow
(v):
If
X
is metrizable it allows for a countable base
\mathcal{B}
for its topology. It follows from Theorem
3.6
that
\mathcal{B}
can be chosen to consist of
G
-tiles.

(v)
\Rightarrow
(iii):
Let
x\in X
and
\mathcal{B}_{x}
be a countable neighborhood base that consists of
G
-tiles.
Denote
S:=\{G(x,B);\,B\in\mathcal{B}_{x}\}
.
Clearly,
S
is countable.
From Lemma
4.8
we know
S\subseteq{\rm Eig}_{x}(X,G)\subseteq{\operatorname{Sub_{fin}}}(G)
.
Furthermore, for
B,B^{\prime}\in\mathcal{B}_{x}
we have that
B\cap B^{\prime}
is an open neighborhood of
x
and hence there exists
B^{\prime\prime}\in\mathcal{B}_{x}
with
B^{\prime\prime}\subseteq B\cap B^{\prime}
.
It follows that
G(x,B^{\prime\prime})\subseteq G(x,B)\cap G(x,B^{\prime})
and we observe that
S
is a countable scale.
It remains to show that
S
generates
(X,G)
.
For this we show that
S
and
{\rm Eig}_{x}(X,G)
are equivalent.

We already know that
S\subseteq{\rm Eig}_{x}(X,G)
and it remains to show that
S
dominates
{\rm Eig}_{x}(X,G)
.
From Lemma
4.8
it follows that for any
\Gamma\in{\rm Eig}_{x}(X,G)
there exists a
G
-tile
A
with
x\in A
and
\Gamma=G(x,A)
.
Since
\mathcal{B}_{x}
is a neighborhood base and
G
-tiles are open we find
B\in\mathcal{B}_{x}
with
B\subseteq A
.
From
S\ni G(x,B)\subseteq G(x,A)=\Gamma
we observe that
S
dominates
{\rm Eig}_{x}(X,G)
.

(iii)
\Rightarrow
(i):
Let
S
be a countable scale that generates
(X,G)
.
As a subspace of a countable product of metrizable spaces, we observe the
S
-subodometer to be metrizable.
Thus also the conjugated subodometer
(X,G)
is metrizable.

This establishes the equivalence of (i), (iii), (v) and (vi).

(iii)
\Rightarrow
(ii):
If
S
is a countable scale that generates
(X,G)
, then by Lemma
6.5
there exists an equivalent chain
S^{\prime}
. It follows from Remark
5.18
that
S^{\prime}
also generates
(X,G)
.

(ii)
\Rightarrow
(iv):
If
S
is a countable chain that generates
(X,G)
, then Corollary
5.15
and Lemma
6.7
yield that

{\rm Eig}(X,G)=\left\llbracket S\right\rrbracket
is countable.

(iv)
\Rightarrow
(iii):
If
{\rm Eig}(X,G)
is countable, then any scale that generates
(X,G)
is contained in
{\rm Eig}(X,G)
and hence countable.
∎

Corollary 6.8
.

Let
G
be a group.
All subodometers
(X,G)
are metrizable if and only if
{\operatorname{Sub_{fin}}}(G)
is countable.

Proof.

Note that
S:={\operatorname{Sub_{fin}}}(G)
is a filter.
The
S
-subodometer
(X,G)
satisfies
{\rm Eig}(X,G)=\left\llbracket S\right\rrbracket={\operatorname{Sub_{fin}}}(G)
.
If
(X,G)
is metrizable, then Theorem
6.6
yields that
{\operatorname{Sub_{fin}}}(G)
is countable.

For the converse assume that
{\operatorname{Sub_{fin}}}(G)
is countable.
For any subodometer
(X,G)
we have that
{\rm Eig}(X,G)\subseteq{\operatorname{Sub_{fin}}}(G)
is countable and Theorem
6.6
yields that
(X,G)
is metrizable.
∎

It is natural to ask for which groups
{\operatorname{Sub_{fin}}}(G)
is countable. We next present some examples and counterexamples.

Example 6.9
.

For a finitely generated group
G
any finite index subgroup is also finitely generated
[
8
, Corollary 7.2.1]
and hence
{\operatorname{Sub_{fin}}}(G)
is countable.
In particular,
{\operatorname{Sub_{fin}}}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
is countable for
d\in\mathbb{N}
.

Example 6.10
.

The Abelian group
(\mathbb{Q},+)
has only itself as a finite index subgroup, i.e.
{\operatorname{Sub_{fin}}}(\mathbb{Q})=\{\mathbb{Q}\}
.
Thus, all (sub)odometers
(X,\mathbb{Q})
are trivial.

Example 6.11
.

For an uncountable product
G=\prod_{i}G_{i}
of finite non-trivial groups
{\operatorname{Sub_{fin}}}(G)
is uncountable, since it contains all
\Gamma_{i}:=\prod_{j}G_{j,i}
with
G_{i,i}=\{e_{G_{i}}\}
and
G_{j,i}:=G_{j}
for
i\neq j
.

Example 6.12
.

The free group
F_{\infty}
with countably many generators is countable, while
{\operatorname{Sub_{fin}}}(F_{\infty})
is uncountable. For details see Example
7.1
below.

7.
Odometers

Note that a totally disconnected compact Hausdorff group is profinite
[
19
]
.
Thus, odometers are group rotations on profinite groups.
The reader might wonder whether the theory of odometers is not just a reminiscent of the theory of profinite groups.
The following examples illustrate that this is not the case.

Example 7.1
.

Let
F_{\infty}
be the free group generated by countably infinitely many generators
(g_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and note that
F_{\infty}
is a countable group.
Consider the finite group
X:=\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
and denote
I:=X^{\mathbb{N}}\setminus\{(0)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\}
.
For a sequence
h=(h_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\in I
we consider the group homomorphism
\phi_{h}\colon F_{\infty}\to X
given by
\phi_{h}(g_{n})=h_{n}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and denote
\alpha_{h}
for the respective group rotation.
Note that
\phi_{h}
is surjective and hence that
\alpha_{h}
is a finite odometer.
Furthermore, w.r.t. this action
G_{0}(x)=\ker(\phi_{h})
holds for all
x\in X
.
Thus, in order to show that for distinct
h,h^{\prime}\in I
the odometers
\alpha_{h}
and
\alpha_{h^{\prime}}
are not conjugated it suffices to show that
\ker(\phi_{h})\neq\ker(\phi_{h^{\prime}})
.

For
h,h^{\prime}\in I
with
h\neq h^{\prime}
there exists
n\in\mathbb{N}
with
h_{n}\neq h_{n}^{\prime}
and we observe that
g_{n}
is contained in exactly one of the normal sets
\ker(\phi_{h})
and
\ker(\phi_{h^{\prime}})
.
Thus, indeed
\ker(\phi_{h})\neq\ker(\phi_{h^{\prime}})
and we observe that
\alpha_{h}
and
\alpha_{h^{\prime}}
are not conjugated.
Since
I
is uncountable this shows that there exist uncountably many non-conjugated odometers
(X,F_{\infty})
with
|X|=2
.
In contrast, up to group-isomorphy there exists only one group with two elements.

Example 7.2
.

There exist finitely generated groups
G
and
H
, with
G
amenable and
H
non-amenable, whose profinite completions are isomorphic
[
15
]
. Consequently, if
X_{1}
and
X_{2}
denote the profinite completions of
G
and
H
, respectively, then
X_{1}
and
X_{2}
are isomorphic as topological groups
[
17
, Theorem 1.1]
. Nevertheless, the odometers
(X_{1},G)
and
(X_{2},H)
are not isomorphic as dynamical systems, nor are they even continuously orbit equivalent (see
[
3
]
).

We next aim to characterize the odometers by properties of their sets of eigenvalues and properties of the scales that generate them.
For this we introduce the following notions.

7.1.
Normality and core-stability

Definition 7.3
.

A subset
S\subseteq{\operatorname{Sub_{fin}}}(G)
is called

•

normal
if all
\Gamma\in S
are normal.

•

core-stable
if for
\Gamma\in S
also the core
\Gamma_{G}
is contained in
S
.

It is natural to ask how these notions relate to conjugation invariance.
We summarize the relations in the following proposition and omit the straightforward proofs.

Proposition 7.4
.

(i)

Any normal scale is core-stable and conjugation invariant.

(ii)

A chain is normal if and only if it is conjugation invariant. Any conjugation invariant chain is core-stable.

(iii)

A filter is core-stable if and only if it is conjugation invariant.

Example 7.5
.

Let
G
be a group that allows for non-normal finite index subgroups
\Lambda\leq\Gamma\leq G
with
\Lambda\neq\Gamma
.

(i)

The chain
S:=\{\Gamma,\Gamma_{G}\}
is core-stable but not normal.

(ii)

The filter
S:=\{\Gamma_{G}\}^{\mathfrak{u}}
is conjugation invariant, i.e. core-stable. It contains
\Gamma
and hence is not normal.

(iii)

The scale
S:=\{\Gamma,\Lambda,\Lambda_{G}\}^{\mathfrak{c}}
is conjugation invariant but not core-stable.

7.2.
Characterization of odometers

Theorem 7.6
.

Let
(X,G)
be a subodometer.
The following statements are equivalent.

(i)

(X,G)
is an odometer.

(ii)

(X,G)
is generated by a normal scale.

(iii)

(X,G)
is generated by a conjugation invariant scale.

(iv)

(X,G)
is generated by a core-stable scale.

(v)

{\rm Eig}(X,G)
is a filter.

(vi)

{\rm Eig}(X,G)
is core-stable.

(vii)

For some
x\in X
we have
{\rm Eig}(X,G)={\rm Eig}_{x}(X,G)
.

(viii)

For all
x\in X
we have
{\rm Eig}(X,G)={\rm Eig}_{x}(X,G)
.

Proof.

(i)
\Rightarrow
(viii):
Since odometers are group rotations they are regular.
Thus,
{\rm Eig}_{x}(X,G)
is independent of
x\in X
and we observe

{\rm Eig}(X,G)=\bigcup_{x^{\prime}\in X}{\rm Eig}_{x^{\prime}}(X,G)={\rm Eig}_{x}(X,G)

for all
x\in X
.

(viii)
\Rightarrow
(vii):
Trivial.

(vii)
\Rightarrow
(v):
Assume that there exists
x\in X
with
{\rm Eig}(X,G)={\rm Eig}_{x}(X,G)
.
It follows from Theorem
4.7
that
{\rm Eig}(X,G)
is a filter.

(v)
\Rightarrow
(vi):
Assume that
{\rm Eig}(X,G)
is a filter and recall from Proposition
5.13
that
{\rm Eig}(X,G)
is conjugation invariant. It follows from Proposition
7.4
that
{\rm Eig}(X,G)
is core-stable.

(vi)
\Rightarrow
(ii):
Let
S
be a scale that generates
(X,G)
.
Consider

S^{\prime}:=\{\Gamma_{G};\,\Gamma\in S\}

and note that
S^{\prime}
consists of normal subgroups of
G
.
It follows from the monotonicity of
\Gamma\mapsto\Gamma_{G}
that
S^{\prime}
is a normal scale.
Clearly,
S^{\prime}
dominates
S
and hence
{\rm Eig}(X,G)=\left\llbracket S\right\rrbracket\subseteq\left\llbracket S^{\prime}\right\rrbracket
.
Furthermore, the core-stability of
{\rm Eig}(X,G)
implies
S^{\prime}\subseteq{\rm Eig}(X,G)
.
Since
S^{\prime}
is conjugation invariant and
{\rm Eig}(X,G)
is upward closed we have
\left\llbracket S^{\prime}\right\rrbracket=(S^{\prime})^{\mathfrak{u}}\subseteq{\rm Eig}(X,G)
.
This shows
{\rm Eig}(X,G)=\left\llbracket S^{\prime}\right\rrbracket
and hence
S^{\prime}
generates
(X,G)
.

(ii)
\Rightarrow
(i):
If
S
is a normal scale that generates
(X,G)
, then the
S
-subodometer is an inverse limit of group rotations and hence a group rotation. We thus observe
(X,G)
to be an odometer.

This shows that (i), (ii), (v), (vi), (vii) and (viii) are equivalent.

(ii)
\Rightarrow
(iv):
Trivial.

(iv)
\Rightarrow
(iii):
Let
S
be a core-stable scale that generates
(X,G)
.
Since
S
is a scale
S^{\mathfrak{u}}
is a filter.
For
\Gamma\in S^{\mathfrak{u}}
and
g\in G
we find
\Lambda\in S
with
\Lambda\subseteq\Gamma
and since
S
is core-stable we have

S\ni\Lambda_{G}\subseteq\Gamma^{g}
.
It follows that
\Gamma^{g}\in S^{\mathfrak{u}}
.
This shows
S^{\mathfrak{u}}
to be conjugation invariant.
Since
S
generates
(X,G)
we have
{\rm Eig}(X,G)=\left\llbracket S\right\rrbracket=(S^{\mathfrak{u}})^{\mathfrak{c}}=\left\llbracket S^{\mathfrak{u}}\right\rrbracket
.
Thus, the conjugation invariant scale
S^{\mathfrak{u}}
generates
(X,G)
.

(iii)
\Rightarrow
(v):
Let
S
be a conjugation invariant scale that generates
(X,G)
.
Note that
S=S^{\mathfrak{c}}
.
Thus, from Proposition
5.14
we observe that

{\rm Eig}(X,G)=\left\llbracket S\right\rrbracket=(S^{\mathfrak{c}})^{\mathfrak{u}}=S^{\mathfrak{u}}
.
Since
S
is a scale
S^{\mathfrak{u}}={\rm Eig}(X,G)
is a filter.
∎

Corollary 7.7
.

For a subodometer
(X,G)
the following statements are equivalent.

(i)

(X,G)
is a metrizable odometer.

(ii)

(X,G)
is generated by a normal chain.

(iii)

{\rm Eig}(X,G)
is a countable filter.

Proof.

From combining the Theorems
6.6
and
7.6
it follows that (ii) implies (i) and that (i) implies (iii).

To show that (iii) implies (ii) assume that
{\rm Eig}(X,G)
is a countable filter.
It follows from Theorem
7.6
that

(X,G)
allows for a normal scale
S
.
From
S\subseteq{\rm Eig}(X,G)
we observe
S
to be countable.
Note that the finite intersection of normal subgroups is normal.
Thus, the construction presented in the proof of Lemma
6.5
yields an equivalent normal chain
S^{\prime}
that also generates
(X,G)
.
∎

Remark 7.8
.

Clearly, whenever
G
is Abelian, then any subgroup of
G
is normal and hence any subodometer is an odometer.
Note that non-Abelian groups for which all subgroups are normal do exist and are called
Hamiltonian groups
. A characterization of Hamiltonian groups can be found in
[
8
, Theorem 12.5.6]
.

8.
Eigensets

We have seen in Theorem
5.5
that subodometers
(X,G)
are (up to conjugacy) completely characterized by
{\rm Eig}(X,G)
.
Furthermore, in Proposition
5.14
we have seen that for a scale
S
and the
S
-odometer
(X,G)
we have
{\rm Eig}(X,G)=\left\llbracket S\right\rrbracket
.
This motivates the following purely algebraic definitions, which allow for a simplification of the study of the category of subodometers and in particular simple proofs for the Theorems
1.2
,
1.5
, and

1.6
.

Definition 8.1
.

A subset
E\subseteq{\operatorname{Sub_{fin}}}(G)
is called an
eigenset
if there exists a scale
S\subseteq{\operatorname{Sub_{fin}}}(G)
such that
E=\left\llbracket S\right\rrbracket
.
A scale
S
is said to
generate
E
if
E=\left\llbracket S\right\rrbracket
.
An eigenset is called
filtered
if it is a filter.
We denote
\mathfrak{E}(G)
and
\mathfrak{E}_{\mathfrak{F}}(G)
for the set of all eigensets and all filtered eigensets, respectively.
We equip
\mathfrak{E}(G)
and
\mathfrak{E}_{\mathfrak{F}}(G)
with the partial ordering given by set inclusion.

Remark 8.2
.

For any minimal action
(X,G)
, the set
{\rm Eig}(X,G)
is an eigenset.
Indeed, it follows from Theorem
4.7
that
{\rm Eig}_{x}(X,G)
is a filter, and Proposition
5.13
yields

{\rm Eig}(X,G)={\rm Eig}_{x}(X,G)^{\mathfrak{c}}=\left\llbracket{\rm Eig}_{x}(X,G)\right\rrbracket
.

It follows from the discussion in Section
5
that for each eigenset
E
there exists a unique subodometer (up to conjugacy) with
E={\rm Eig}(X,G)
. We refer to this subodometer as the
subodometer associated with
E
.
For
E,E^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{E}(G)
we have
E\subseteq E^{\prime}
if and only if the subodometer associated with
E
is a factor of the subodometer associated with
E^{\prime}
(Theorem
5.5
).
Furthermore, an eigenset is generated by a scale
S
if and only if the associated subodometer is generated by
S
(Corollary
5.15
).
An eigenset is filtered if and only if it is associated to an odometer (Theorem
7.6
).
An eigenset is countable if and only if the associated subodometer is metrizable (Theorem
7.6
).

Remark 8.3
.

A subset
E\subseteq{\operatorname{Sub_{fin}}}(G)
is a filtered eigenset if and only if it is a conjugation invariant filter. Thus the odometers (up to conjugacy) can be identified with conjugation invariant filters.

Note that
\mathfrak{E}_{\mathfrak{F}}(G)
and
\mathfrak{E}(G)
have
{\operatorname{Sub_{fin}}}(G)
as a maximal and
\{G\}
as a minimal element. We will next study further properties of these partially ordered sets in order to gain insights into the categories of odometers and subodometers, respectively.

8.1.
\mathfrak{E}_{\mathfrak{F}}(G)
as a complete lattice

Next, we show that
\mathfrak{E}_{\mathfrak{F}}(G)
is a complete lattice.
We start with the following, which establishes that any non-empty family of odometers allows for a maximal common factor.

Proposition 8.4
.

(i)

For any family
\mathcal{S}
of filters also
\bigcap_{S\in\mathcal{S}}S
is a filter.

(ii)

For any family
\mathcal{E}
of filtered eigensets also
\bigwedge\mathcal{E}:=\bigcap_{E\in\mathcal{E}}E
is a filtered eigenset.

Proof.

(i):

\check{S}:=\bigcap_{S\in\mathcal{S}}S
is non-empty, since any filter contains
G
.
Furthermore, as the intersection of upward closed sets we have that
\check{S}
is upward closed.
To show that
\check{S}
is a scale, consider
\Gamma,\Gamma^{\prime}\in\check{S}
.
For
S\in\mathcal{S}
we have
\Gamma,\Gamma^{\prime}\in S
and hence there exists
\Lambda_{S}\in S
with
\Lambda_{S}\subseteq\Gamma\cap\Gamma^{\prime}
.
Consider
\Lambda:=\left\langle\bigcup_{S\in\mathcal{S}}\Lambda_{S}\right\rangle
and note that
\Lambda\leq G
is of finite index.
For
S\in\mathcal{S}
we have
\Lambda_{S}\subseteq\Lambda
and hence the upward closedness of
S
yields that
\Lambda\in S
.
We thus observe that
\Lambda\in\check{S}
.
Furthermore, we have
\Lambda=\left\langle\bigcup_{S\in\mathcal{S}}\Lambda_{S}\right\rangle\subseteq\Gamma\cap\Gamma^{\prime}
.
This shows
\check{S}
to be an upward closed scale, i.e. a filter.

(ii):
From (i) we observe that
\bigwedge\mathcal{E}=\bigcap_{E\in\mathcal{E}}E
is a filter.
Furthermore, as the intersection of conjugation invariant sets it is conjugation invariant.
We thus know that
\bigwedge\mathcal{E}
is a conjugation invariant and upward closed scale and observe
\bigwedge\mathcal{E}=\left\llbracket\bigwedge\mathcal{E}\right\rrbracket
to be an eigenset.
∎

Corollary 8.5
.

\mathfrak{E}_{\mathfrak{F}}(G)
is a complete lattice.

Proof.

From Proposition
8.4
we observe that any family in
\mathfrak{E}_{\mathfrak{F}}(G)
allows for an infimum.
For
\mathcal{E}\subseteq\mathfrak{E}_{\mathfrak{F}}(G)
we consider the family
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}
of all filtered eigensets that contain
\bigcup_{E\in\mathcal{E}}E
. Clearly,
\bigwedge\mathcal{E}^{\prime}
is the supremum of
\mathcal{E}
.
∎

8.2.
\mathfrak{E}(G)
as a partially ordered set

Considering Corollary
8.5
it is natural to ask whether also
\mathfrak{E}(G)
is a complete lattice. This is not necessarily the case as illustrated by the next example. Note that this example also yields that for pairs of subodometers there does not need to exist a minimal common extension/maximal common factor.

Example 8.6
.

Let
S_{5}
be the group of all permutations on
\{1,2,3,4,5\}
.
Denote

\Lambda_{1}:=\left\langle(12)(34)\right\rangle\hskip 8.5359pt\text{and}\hskip 8.5359pt\Lambda_{2}:=\left\langle(123)\right\rangle.

Furthermore, consider

\Gamma_{1}:=\left\langle(123),(23)(45)\right\rangle\hskip 8.5359pt\text{and}\hskip 8.5359pt\Gamma_{2}:=\left\langle(123),(12)(34),(13)(24)\right\rangle.

We will next show that

\left\llbracket\{\Lambda_{1}\}\right\rrbracket
and
\left\llbracket\{\Lambda_{2}\}\right\rrbracket

do not have a supremum and that

\left\llbracket\{\Gamma_{1}\}\right\rrbracket
and
\left\llbracket\{\Gamma_{2}\}\right\rrbracket

do not have an infimum in
\mathfrak{E}(S_{5})
.

Clearly we have
\Lambda_{1}\subseteq\Gamma_{2}
and
\Lambda_{2}\subseteq\Gamma_{1}\cap\Gamma_{2}
.
Furthermore, for
g=(54321)

we observe that
\Lambda_{1}^{g}=\left\langle(23)(45)\right\rangle
and hence that
\Lambda_{1}^{g}\subseteq\Gamma_{1}
.
Visualizing the set inclusions of the eigensets via arrows, we thus observe the following diagram.

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\left\llbracket\{\Lambda_{1}\}\right\rrbracket}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\left\llbracket\{\Lambda_{2}\}\right\rrbracket}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\left\llbracket\{\Gamma_{1}\}\right\rrbracket}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\left\llbracket\{\Gamma_{2}\}\right\rrbracket}

Conjugates of
\Lambda_{1}
and
\Lambda_{2}
are cyclic groups of order
2
and
3
, respectively.
Since
2
and
3
are coprime we observe that
\Lambda_{1}\not\subseteq\Lambda_{2}^{g}
and

\Lambda_{2}\not\subseteq\Lambda_{1}^{g}
for all
g\in S_{5}
.
It follows that

\left\llbracket\{\Lambda_{1}\}\right\rrbracket\not\subseteq\left\llbracket\{\Lambda_{2}\}\right\rrbracket
and

\left\llbracket\{\Lambda_{2}\}\right\rrbracket\not\subseteq\left\llbracket\{\Lambda_{1}\}\right\rrbracket
.

All
g\in\Gamma_{2}
fix
5
.
Thus, if
\Gamma_{1}
would be contained in some conjugate of
\Gamma_{2}
then there would be
l\in\{1,\dots,5\}
that is fixed by all permutations in
\Gamma_{1}
and in particular by
(123)
and
(23)(45)
, a contradiction.
Hence, we have
\Gamma_{1}\not\subseteq\Gamma_{2}^{g}
for all
g\in S_{5}
.
Furthermore, we have
|\Gamma_{1}|=6
and
|\Gamma_{2}|=12
.
Thus
\Gamma_{2}\not\subseteq\Gamma_{1}^{g}
for all
g\in S_{5}
.
It follows that

\left\llbracket\{\Gamma_{1}\}\right\rrbracket\not\subseteq\left\llbracket\{\Gamma_{2}\}\right\rrbracket

and that

\left\llbracket\{\Gamma_{2}\}\right\rrbracket\not\subseteq\left\llbracket\{\Gamma_{1}\}\right\rrbracket
.
Similarly, we observe that
\left\llbracket\{\Lambda_{i}\}\right\rrbracket\not\subseteq\left\llbracket\{\Gamma_{j}\}\right\rrbracket
for
i,j\in\{1,2\}
.
Summarizing we have shown that the diagram above displays all set inclusions between the considered eigensets.

To observe that
\{\left\llbracket\{\Lambda_{1}\}\right\rrbracket,\left\llbracket\{\Lambda_{2}\}\right\rrbracket\}
has no infimum and that
\{\left\llbracket\{\Gamma_{1}\}\right\rrbracket,\left\llbracket\{\Gamma_{2}\}\right\rrbracket\}
has no supremum in
\mathfrak{E}(S_{5})
we show that there does not exist an intermediate eigenset
E
with

\left\llbracket\{\Gamma_{i}\}\right\rrbracket\subseteq E\subseteq\left\llbracket\{\Lambda_{j}\}\right\rrbracket
for
i,j\in\{1,2\}
.
To obtain a contradiction, assume that there exists such
E
.
Since
S_{5}
is finite, any scale that generates
E
has a minimal element and we thus find
\Gamma\in{\operatorname{Sub_{fin}}}(S_{5})
with
E=\left\llbracket\{\Gamma\}\right\rrbracket
.

Since
\Gamma\in E\subseteq\left\llbracket\{\Lambda_{1}\}\right\rrbracket\cap\left\llbracket\{\Lambda_{2}\}\right\rrbracket
we observe the existence of
g_{1},g_{2}\in S_{5}
with
\Lambda_{1}^{g_{1}}\cup\Lambda_{2}^{g_{2}}\subseteq\Gamma
.
Since
\Lambda_{1}^{g_{1}}
and
\Lambda_{2}^{g_{2}}
are cyclic groups of order
2
and
3
, respectively, we observe that
|\Gamma|\geq 6
.
Furthermore, from
\left\llbracket\{\Gamma_{1}\}\right\rrbracket\cup\left\llbracket\{\Gamma_{2}\}\right\rrbracket\subseteq E=\left\llbracket\{\Gamma\}\right\rrbracket
we observe

\Gamma_{1},\Gamma_{2}\in\left\llbracket\{\Gamma\}\right\rrbracket
.
This implies the existence of
h_{1},h_{2}\in S_{5}
such that

\Gamma\subseteq\Gamma_{1}^{h_{1}}\cap\Gamma_{2}^{h_{2}}
.
In particular, denoting
h:=h_{2}h_{1}^{-1}
we have
\Gamma^{h_{1}^{-1}}\subseteq\Gamma_{1}\cap\Gamma_{2}^{h}
.
Recall that we have already observed that
\Gamma_{1}\not\subseteq\Gamma_{2}^{g}
for all
g\in S_{5}
.
We thus have
\Gamma_{1}\not\subseteq\Gamma_{2}^{h}
and hence

|\Gamma|=|\Gamma^{h_{1}^{-1}}|\leq|\Gamma_{1}\cap\Gamma_{2}^{h}|<|\Gamma_{1}|=6
, a contradiction.
This shows that an intermediate
E
as considered cannot exist.

8.3.
Filter Hulls

From Corollary
8.5
we know that any family of odometers allows for a minimal common extension within the category of odometers.
In order to show that this minimal common extension is actually also the minimal common extension within the category of all subodometers we next develop an explicit formula for the supremum of filtered eigensets. For this we will use the notion of a filter hull.
Recall from Proposition
8.4
that the intersection of a family of filters is a filter. This allows us to define the following.

Definition 8.7
.

For a subset
S\subseteq{\operatorname{Sub_{fin}}}(G)
we denote
S^{\mathfrak{f}}
for the
filter hull of
S
, i.e. the smallest filter that contains
S
.

Remark 8.8
.

Since any filter is upward closed we have
S\subseteq S^{\mathfrak{u}}\subseteq S^{\mathfrak{f}}
.
If
S
is a scale
S^{\mathfrak{u}}
is a filter and hence
S^{\mathfrak{f}}=S^{\mathfrak{u}}
.

The following lemma gives an explicit formula for the filter hull.
Note that for any finite subset
J\subseteq{\operatorname{Sub_{fin}}}(G)
we have

\bigcap_{\Gamma\in J}\Gamma\in{\operatorname{Sub_{fin}}}(G)
.

Lemma 8.9
.

For a subset
S\subseteq{\operatorname{Sub_{fin}}}(G)
we have

S^{\mathfrak{f}}=\left\{\bigcap_{\Gamma\in J}\Gamma;\,J\subseteq S\text{ finite}\right\}^{\mathfrak{u}}.

Proof.

Denote
S^{\prime}:=\left\{\bigcap_{\Gamma\in J}\Gamma;\,J\subseteq S\text{ finite}\right\}^{\mathfrak{u}}
.
It is straightforward to verify that
S^{\prime}
is a filter.
From
S\subseteq S^{\prime}
we thus observe
S^{\mathfrak{f}}\subseteq S^{\prime}
.

For the converse consider
\Gamma\in S^{\prime}
.
There exists a finite subset
J\subseteq S
with
\bigcap_{\Gamma^{\prime}\in J}\Gamma^{\prime}\subseteq\Gamma
.
For any filter
\hat{S}
that contains
S
we observe
J\subseteq S\subseteq\hat{S}
.
Since
\hat{S}
is a scale and
J
is finite there exists
\Lambda\in\hat{S}
with
\Lambda\subseteq\bigcap_{\Gamma^{\prime}\in J}\Gamma^{\prime}\subseteq\Gamma
.
Since
\hat{S}
is upward closed we observe
\Lambda\in\hat{S}
.
This shows that
\Lambda
is contained in all filters
\hat{S}
that contain
S
and hence that
\Lambda\in S^{\mathfrak{f}}
.
∎

Lemma 8.10
.

Let
S\subseteq{\operatorname{Sub_{fin}}}(G)
be a subset.

(i)

If
S
is finite, then
S^{\mathfrak{f}}
is finite.

(ii)

If
S
is countable, then
S^{\mathfrak{f}}
is countable.

(iii)

If
S
is conjugation invariant, then
S^{\mathfrak{f}}
is a filtered eigenset.

(iv)

If
S
is a scale and
E
is a filtered eigenset with
E\subseteq\left\llbracket S\right\rrbracket
, then
E\subseteq S^{\mathfrak{f}}
.

Proof.

(i/ii):
For
J\subseteq S
finite we have
\bigcap_{\Gamma\in J}\Gamma\in{\operatorname{Sub_{fin}}}(G)
. Note that
\{\bigcap_{\Gamma\in J}\Gamma\}^{\mathfrak{u}}
is finite by Corollary
6.2
.
Whenever
S
is finite/countable we thus observe that

S^{\mathfrak{f}}=\left\{\bigcap_{\Gamma\in J}\Gamma;\,J\subseteq S\text{~finite}\right\}^{\mathfrak{u}}=\bigcup_{J\subseteq S\text{~finite}}\left\{\bigcap_{\Gamma\in J}\Gamma\right\}^{\mathfrak{u}}

is a finite/countable union of finite sets and hence finite/countable.

(iii):
Assume that
S
is conjugation invariant.
For
J\subseteq S
finite and
g\in G
we have
J^{\prime}:=\{\Gamma^{g};\,\Gamma\in J\}\subseteq S
and that

(\bigcap_{\Gamma\in J}\Gamma)^{g}=\bigcap_{\Gamma\in J}\Gamma^{g}=\bigcap_{\Gamma\in J^{\prime}}\Gamma
.
This shows
\{\bigcap_{\Gamma\in J}\Gamma;\,J\subseteq S\text{ finite}\}
to be conjugation invariant.
Since upper hulls of conjugation invariant sets are conjugation invariant we observe from Lemma
8.9
that
S^{\mathfrak{f}}
is conjugation invariant.

Since
S^{\mathfrak{f}}
is a conjugation invariant and upward closed scale we have
S^{\mathfrak{f}}=\left\llbracket S^{\mathfrak{f}}\right\rrbracket
and hence
S^{\mathfrak{f}}
is an eigenset.

(iv):
Let
\Gamma\in E
and note that
\Gamma
only has finitely many conjugates.
Since
E
is a conjugation invariant scale we find
\Gamma^{\prime}\in E
with
\Gamma^{\prime}\subseteq\bigcap_{g\in G}\Gamma^{g}=\Gamma_{G}
.
Since
E
is upward closed we observe
\Gamma_{G}\in E\subseteq\left\llbracket S\right\rrbracket
.
Thus there exist
g\in G
and
\Lambda\in S
with
\Lambda\subseteq\Gamma_{G}^{g}=\Gamma_{G}\subseteq\Gamma
.
Hence,
\Gamma\in S^{\mathfrak{u}}=S^{\mathfrak{f}}
.
∎

8.4.
\mathfrak{E}_{\mathfrak{F}}(G)
as a complete sublattice of
\mathfrak{E}(G)

We next present an explicit formula for the supremum of filtered eigensets.

Proposition 8.11
.

Let
\mathcal{E}
be a family of filtered eigensets and denote

\bigvee\mathcal{E}:=\left(\bigcup_{E\in\mathcal{E}}E\right)^{\mathfrak{f}}.

(i)

\bigvee\mathcal{E}
is a filtered eigenset.

(ii)

All eigensets
\hat{E}
that contain
\bigcup_{E\in\mathcal{E}}E
also contain
\bigvee\mathcal{E}
.

Proof.

(i):
Since the union of conjugation invariant sets is conjugation invariant we observe
\bigcup_{E\in\mathcal{E}}E
to be conjugation invariant. It follows from Lemma
8.10
that
\bigvee\mathcal{E}
is a filtered eigenset.

(ii):
Let
\hat{E}
be an eigenset that contains
\bigcup_{E\in\mathcal{E}}E
and choose a scale
S
with
\hat{E}=\left\llbracket S\right\rrbracket
.
From Lemma
8.10
we observe that for
E\in\mathcal{E}
we have
E\subseteq S^{\mathfrak{f}}
, i.e.
\bigcup_{E\in\mathcal{E}}E\subseteq S^{\mathfrak{f}}
.
It follows that
\bigvee\mathcal{E}=(\bigcup_{E\in\mathcal{E}}E)^{\mathfrak{f}}\subseteq S^{\mathfrak{f}}
.
Since
S
is a scale we have
S^{\mathfrak{f}}=S^{\mathfrak{u}}
and hence

\bigvee\mathcal{E}\subseteq S^{\mathfrak{f}}\subseteq(S^{\mathfrak{u}})^{\mathfrak{c}}=\left\llbracket S\right\rrbracket=\hat{E}
.
∎

We can now strengthen the statement of Corollary
8.5
.

Theorem 8.12
.

\mathfrak{E}_{\mathfrak{F}}(G)
is a complete sublattice of
\mathfrak{E}(G)
, i.e. for any family of filtered eigensets
\mathcal{E}
there exists a supremum
\bigvee\mathcal{E}
and an infimum
\bigwedge\mathcal{E}
in
\mathfrak{E}(G)
and both are contained in
\mathfrak{E}_{\mathfrak{F}}(G)
.

Remark 8.13
.

The formulas for the supremum and infimum are given by

\bigvee\mathcal{E}:=(\bigcup_{E\in\mathcal{E}}E)^{\mathfrak{f}}
and
\bigwedge\mathcal{E}:=\bigcap_{E\in\mathcal{E}}E
.

Proof of Theorem
8.12
.

From Proposition
8.4
we know that
\bigwedge\mathcal{E}:=\bigcap_{E\in\mathcal{E}}E
is a filtered eigenset.
Clearly, it is not only the infimum of
\mathcal{E}
in
\mathfrak{E}_{\mathfrak{F}}(G)
, but also in
\mathfrak{E}(G)
.

It follows from Proposition
8.11
that

\bigvee\mathcal{E}:=(\bigcup_{E\in\mathcal{E}}E)^{\mathfrak{f}}
is a filtered eigenset and the supremum of
\mathcal{E}
within
\mathfrak{E}(G)
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.2
:

Let
\mathfrak{X}
be a non-empty family of odometers.
Denote
\bigvee\mathfrak{X}
for the odometer associated with
\bigvee\{{\rm Eig}(X,G);\,(X,G)\in\mathfrak{X}\}
and

\bigwedge\mathfrak{X}
for the odometer associated with

It follows from the discussion at the beginning of this section that
\bigvee\mathfrak{X}
and

1
) and (a

).
Furthermore, since

\bigwedge\mathfrak{X}

) from Theorem
8.12
.

To show that

) consider a minimal action

.

5.5

{\rm Eig}(X,G)\subseteq{\rm Eig}(Y,G)

8.5.
Modularity

It is well-known that the set
{\operatorname{Sub_{fin}^{\triangleleft}}}(G)
of all normal finite index subgroups of
G
forms a lattice under set inclusion, where the supremum and infimum are given by

\left\langle\Gamma\cup\Lambda\right\rangle=\Gamma\Lambda
and

\Gamma\cap\Lambda

[
2
, Chapter 1]
.
As a lattice
{\operatorname{Sub_{fin}^{\triangleleft}}}(G)
is
modular
, i.e. for
\Gamma_{1},\Gamma_{2},\Gamma\in{\operatorname{Sub_{fin}}}(G)
with
\Gamma_{1}\supseteq\Gamma_{2}
the
modular law

(\Gamma_{1}\cap\Gamma)\Gamma_{2}=\Gamma_{1}\cap(\Gamma\Gamma_{2})

is satisfied.
Reversing the order of
{\operatorname{Sub_{fin}^{\triangleleft}}}(G)
it embeds into the order complete lattice
\mathfrak{E}_{\mathfrak{F}}(G)
via
\Gamma\mapsto\left\llbracket\{\Gamma\}\right\rrbracket
.
We will next show that
\mathfrak{E}_{\mathfrak{F}}(G)
inherits the modularity of
{\operatorname{Sub_{fin}}}(G)
, i.e. we show Theorem
1.5
.

Proposition 8.14

(Modular Law)
.

For
E_{1},E_{2},E\in\mathfrak{E}_{\mathfrak{F}}(G)
with
E_{1}\subseteq E_{2}
we have

(E_{1}\vee E)\wedge E_{2}=E_{1}\vee(E\wedge E_{2}).

Proof.

Clearly, we have
E_{1}\cup(E\cap E_{2})\subseteq(E_{1}\cup E)\cap E_{2}\subseteq(E_{1}\cup E)^{\mathfrak{f}}\cap E_{2}
.
The latter is an intersection of filters and hence a filter.
Thus, we have

E_{1}\vee(E\wedge E_{2})=(E_{1}\cup(E\cap E_{2}))^{\mathfrak{f}}\subseteq(E_{1}\cup E)^{\mathfrak{f}}\cap E_{2}=(E_{1}\vee E)\wedge E_{2}.

For the converse consider
\Lambda\in(E_{1}\vee E)\wedge E_{2}=(E_{1}\cup E)^{\mathfrak{f}}\cap E_{2}
.
By Lemma
8.9
there exists a finite subset
J\subseteq E_{1}\cup E
with
\bigcap_{\Gamma\in J}\Gamma\subseteq\Lambda
.
Since
E_{1}
and
E
are filtered eigensets they are scales and core-stable (Theorem
7.6
).
We thus assume w.l.o.g. that
J=\{\Gamma_{1},\Gamma\}
for some normal subgroups
\Gamma_{1}\in E_{1}
and
\Gamma\in E
.
Note that
\Gamma_{1}\cap\Gamma\subseteq\Lambda
.

Furthermore, since
\Gamma_{1}\in E_{1}\subseteq E_{2}
we have
\Gamma_{1},\Lambda\in E_{2}
. Since
E_{2}
is a core-stable scale we can choose a normal
\Gamma_{2}\in E_{2}
with
\Gamma_{2}\subseteq\Gamma_{1}\cap\Lambda
. We have
\Gamma\Gamma_{2}\supseteq\Gamma\in E
and
\Gamma\Gamma_{2}\supseteq\Gamma_{2}\in E_{2}
and hence
\Gamma\Gamma_{2}\in E\cap E_{2}
.
Denote
J^{\prime}:=\{\Gamma_{1},\Gamma\Gamma_{2}\}
and note that
J^{\prime}\subseteq E_{1}\cup(E\cap E_{2})
.
Furthermore, exploring the modular law for
\Gamma_{1}\supseteq\Gamma_{2}
we have

\displaystyle\bigcap_{\Gamma^{\prime}\in J^{\prime}}\Gamma^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\Gamma_{1}\cap(\Gamma\Gamma_{2})=(\Gamma_{1}\cap\Gamma)\Gamma_{2}\subseteq\Lambda\Lambda=\Lambda.

From the formula of the filter hull presented in Lemma
8.9
we conclude that

\Lambda\in(E_{1}\cup(E\cap E_{2}))^{\mathfrak{f}}=E_{1}\vee(E\wedge E_{2})
.
∎

8.6.
Suprema of metrizable odometers

Recall that the metrizability of a subodometer is reflected in the countability of the respective eigenset.
Whenever
\mathfrak{X}
is a non-empty family of metrizable odometers, then clearly also its factor
\bigwedge\mathfrak{X}
is metrizable.
In general the minimal common extension
\bigvee\mathfrak{X}
of a non-empty family of metrizable odometers
\mathfrak{X}
needs not be metrizable as illustrated by the following.

Example 8.15
.

Let
G
be a group for which
S:={\operatorname{Sub_{fin}}}(G)
is uncountable (e.g.
G=F_{\infty}
) and note that
S
is a scale.
Clearly, the
S
-subodometer
(X,G)
is not metrizable.
However, it is straightforward to observe that
(X,G)
is the minimal common extension of all finite odometers, which are clearly metrizable.

Translating the following back to the category of odometers yields Theorem
1.6
.

Proposition 8.16
.

If
\mathcal{E}
is a countable family of countable filtered eigensets, then
\bigvee\mathcal{E}
is also countable.

Proof.

Note that
\bigcup_{E\in\mathcal{E}}E
is countable as the countable union of countable sets.
It follows from the Lemmas
8.9
and
8.10
that also

\bigvee\mathcal{E}=(\bigcup_{E\in\mathcal{E}}E)^{\mathfrak{f}}
is countable.
∎

8.7.
Scales for the minimal common extension and the maximal common factor

Proposition 8.17
.

Let
\mathfrak{X}
be a non-empty family of odometers. For
X\in\mathfrak{X}
let
S_{X}
be a scale that generates
X
.

(i)

The minimal common extension
\bigvee\mathfrak{X}
is generated by the scale

S:=\left\{\bigcap_{\Gamma\in J}\Gamma;\,J\subseteq\bigcup_{X\in\mathfrak{X}}S_{X}\text{ finite}\right\}

(ii)

The maximal common factor
\bigwedge\mathfrak{X}
is generated by the scale

S:=\left\{\left\langle\bigcup_{X\in\mathfrak{X}}\Gamma_{X}\right\rangle;\,(\Gamma_{X})_{X\in\mathfrak{X}}\in\prod_{X\in\mathfrak{X}}S_{X}\right\}

Proof.

(i):
It is straightforward to observe that
S
is a scale.
For
X\in\mathfrak{X}
we have
S_{X}\subseteq S
and hence
\left\llbracket S_{X}\right\rrbracket\subseteq\left\llbracket S\right\rrbracket
.
It follows that

\bigvee\{{\rm Eig}(X,G);\,X\in\mathfrak{X}\}=\bigvee\{\left\llbracket S_{X}\right\rrbracket;\,X\in\mathfrak{X}\}\subseteq\left\llbracket S\right\rrbracket.

Furthermore, it follows from Lemma
8.9
and
\bigcup_{X\in\mathfrak{X}}S_{X}\subseteq\bigcup_{X\in\mathfrak{X}}\left\llbracket S_{X}\right\rrbracket
that

S^{\mathfrak{u}}=S^{\mathfrak{f}}\subseteq\left(\bigcup_{X\in\mathfrak{X}}\left\llbracket S_{X}\right\rrbracket\right)^{\mathfrak{f}}=\bigvee\{\left\llbracket S_{X}\right\rrbracket;\,X\in\mathfrak{X}\}.

Since
\bigvee\{\left\llbracket S_{X}\right\rrbracket;\,X\in\mathfrak{X}\}
is conjugation invariant we observe that

\left\llbracket S\right\rrbracket=(S^{\mathfrak{u}})^{\mathfrak{c}}\subseteq\bigvee\{\left\llbracket S_{X}\right\rrbracket;\,X\in\mathfrak{X}\}=\bigvee\{{\rm Eig}(X,G);\,X\in\mathfrak{X}\}.

This shows
\left\llbracket S\right\rrbracket=\bigvee\{{\rm Eig}(X,G);\,X\in\mathfrak{X}\}
and we conclude that
S
generates
\bigvee\mathfrak{X}
.

(ii):
To show that
S
is a scale consider
(\Gamma_{X})_{X},(\Gamma_{X}^{\prime})_{X}\in\prod_{X\in\mathfrak{X}}S_{X}
.
For
X\in\mathfrak{X}
there exists
\Lambda_{X}\in S_{X}
with
\Lambda_{X}\subseteq\Gamma_{X}\cap\Gamma_{X}^{\prime}
.
Clearly, we have
S\ni\left\langle\bigcup_{X\in\mathfrak{X}}\Lambda_{X}\right\rangle\subseteq\left\langle\bigcup_{X\in\mathfrak{X}}\Gamma_{X}\right\rangle\cap\left\langle\bigcup_{X\in\mathfrak{X}}\Gamma_{X}^{\prime}\right\rangle
.
This shows that
S
is a scale.

For
X\in\mathfrak{X}
it is straightforward to observe that
S_{X}
dominates
S
.
This establishes

\left\llbracket S\right\rrbracket\subseteq\bigcap_{X\in\mathfrak{X}}\left\llbracket S_{X}\right\rrbracket=\bigwedge\{{\rm Eig}(X,G);\,X\in\mathfrak{X}\}.

For the converse consider
\Gamma\in\bigwedge\{{\rm Eig}(X,G);\,X\in\mathfrak{X}\}=\bigcap_{X\in\mathfrak{X}}\left\llbracket S_{X}\right\rrbracket
.
Let
X\in\mathfrak{X}
.
Since
\left\llbracket S_{X}\right\rrbracket
is a filtered eigenset and
S_{X}
is a scale we observe from Lemma
8.10
that
\Gamma\in\left\llbracket S_{X}\right\rrbracket\subseteq S_{X}^{\mathfrak{f}}=S_{X}^{\mathfrak{u}}
.
Thus there exists
\Gamma_{X}\in X
with
\Gamma_{X}\subseteq\Gamma
.
We thus have
S\ni\left\langle\bigcup_{X\in\mathfrak{X}}\Gamma_{X}\right\rangle\subseteq\Gamma
and hence
\Gamma\in S^{\mathfrak{u}}\subseteq\left\llbracket S\right\rrbracket
.

This shows
\left\llbracket S\right\rrbracket=\bigwedge\{{\rm Eig}(X,G);\,X\in\mathfrak{X}\}
and we conclude that
S
generates
\bigwedge\mathfrak{X}
.
∎

Recall that metrizable odometers are generated by normal chains and that any chain can be represented by a sequence
(\Gamma_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
with
\Gamma_{n+1}\subseteq\Gamma_{n}
.
In the following whenever we represent a chain as a sequence we implicitly assume that
\Gamma_{n+1}\subseteq\Gamma_{n}
is satisfied.

Proposition 8.18
.

Let
\mathfrak{X}=(X_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of odometers.
For
i\in\mathbb{N}
let
(\Gamma_{n}^{(i)})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a chain that generates
X_{i}
and denote

\Gamma_{n}:=\bigcap_{i=1}^{n}\Gamma_{n}^{(i)}

for
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
The chain
(\Gamma_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
generates the minimal common extension
\bigvee\mathfrak{X}
.

Remark 8.19
.

Note that if all
(\Gamma_{n}^{(i)})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
are normal, so is
(\Gamma_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
.

Remark 8.20
.

It follows that for normal chains
(\Gamma_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and
(\Lambda_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
the minimal common extension of the generated odometers is generated by
(\Gamma_{n}\cap\Lambda_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
.

Proof of Proposition
8.18
:

Clearly,
(\Gamma_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a chain.
Denote
S
for the scale given in Proposition
8.17
and note that
\Gamma_{n}\in S
holds for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
In order to show that
(\Gamma_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
dominates
S
consider
\Gamma\in S
and choose a finite subset
J\subseteq\bigcap_{i,n\in\mathbb{N}}\Gamma_{n}^{(i)}
with
\Gamma=\bigcap_{\Lambda\in J}\Lambda
.
For
\Lambda\in J
there exist
i_{\Lambda},n_{\Lambda}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\Lambda=\Gamma_{n_{\Lambda}}^{(i_{\Lambda})}
.
Let
n:=\max\{i_{\Lambda},n_{\Lambda};\,\Lambda\in J\}
and note that

\Gamma_{n}=\bigcap_{i=1}^{n}\Gamma_{n}^{(i)}=\bigcap_{i=1}^{n}\bigcap_{k=1}^{n}\Gamma_{k}^{(i)}\subseteq\bigcap_{\Lambda\in J}\Gamma_{n_{\Lambda}}^{(i_{\Lambda})}=\bigcap_{\Lambda\in J}\Lambda=\Gamma.

This shows that
(\Gamma_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and
S
are equivalent scales and Proposition
8.17
yields
that both generate
\bigvee\mathfrak{X}
.
∎

Given a countable (non-empty) family of normal chains it remains open, whether the formula in Proposition
8.17
yields a simplification similar to Proposition
8.18
.
However, for pairs of metrizable odometers, we have the following.

Proposition 8.21
.

Let
(X,G)
and
(Y,G)
be subodometers generated by chains
(\Gamma_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and
(\Lambda_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
.
The maximal common factor
X\wedge Y
is generated by the chain
(\left\langle\Gamma_{n}\cup\Lambda_{n}\right\rangle)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
.

Remark 8.22
.

If
(\Gamma_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and
(\Lambda_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
are normal chains, then

(\Gamma_{n}\Lambda_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}=(\left\langle\Gamma_{n}\cup\Lambda_{n}\right\rangle)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a normal chain that generates
X\wedge Y
.

Proof.

Denote
S
for the scale considered in Proposition
8.17
and note that
\Gamma_{n}\in S
holds for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Thus
S
dominates
(\Gamma_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
.

To show the converse consider
\Gamma\in S
. There exist
(n,m)\in\mathbb{N}^{2}
with
\Gamma=\Gamma_{n}\Lambda_{m}
.
W.l.o.g. we assume that
n\geq m
and observe
S\ni\Gamma_{n}\Lambda_{n}\subseteq\Gamma_{n}\Lambda_{m}=\Gamma
.
This shows that
(\Gamma_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
dominates
S
.
Thus,
S
and
(\Gamma_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
are equivalent scales and Proposition
8.17
yields that both generate
X\wedge Y
.
∎

9.
Universal subodometers

9.1.
The universal odometer

Note that
{\operatorname{Sub_{fin}}}(G)
is a filtered eigenset.
Considering the associated odometer we observe the following.

Proposition 9.1
.

There exists an odometer
(\hat{X},G)
that has all subodometers
(X,G)
as factors. It is unique up to conjugacy.
The odometer
(\hat{X},G)
is called the
universal odometer (w.r.t.
G
)
.

Remark 9.2
.

The universal odometer is metrizable if and only if
{\operatorname{Sub_{fin}}}(G)
is countable, as already discussed in Corollary
6.8
.

Remark 9.3
.

The set
S
of all normal
\Gamma\in{\operatorname{Sub_{fin}}}(G)
is intersection closed and hence a scale.
For
\Lambda\in{\operatorname{Sub_{fin}}}(G)
we have
\Lambda_{G}\leq\Lambda
.
Thus,
S
dominates
{\operatorname{Sub_{fin}}}(G)
and we observe that
S
generates the universal odometer.

9.2.
The maximal subodometer factor

Let
(X,G)
be a minimal action. As discussed in Remark
8.2

{\rm Eig}(X,G)
is an eigenset.
Considering the associated subodometer Theorem
5.5
yields the following.

Proposition 9.4
.

Let
(X,G)
be a minimal action.
There exists a subodometer
(X_{\operatorname{MSF}},G)
such that

(a)

(X_{\operatorname{MSF}},G)
is a factor of
(X,G)
.

(b)

Whenever
(Y,G)
is a subodometer and a factor of
(X,G)
, then
(Y,G)
is a factor of
(X_{\operatorname{MSF}},G)
.

The subodometer satisfying (a) and (b) is unique up to conjugacy and called the
maximal subodometer factor of
(X,G)
.
It satisfies

{\rm Eig}(X,G)={\rm Eig}(X_{\operatorname{MSF}},G).

Remark 9.5
.

The maximal subodometer factor of
X
does not need to be an odometer. It is an odometer, if and only if
{\rm Eig}(X,G)
is a filter.

Remark 9.6
.

If
(X,G)
is metrizable, then so is the maximal subodometer factor and we observe
{\rm Eig}(X,G)
to be countable. This shows that metrizable actions have only countably many finite factors up to conjugacy.

Remark 9.7
.

The universal subodometer is the maximal subodometer factor of the universal minimal flow
[
1
, Theorem 8.1]
.
The maximal subodometer factor of a minimal action
(X,G)
is conjugated to the maximal subodometer factor of the maximal equicontinuous factor
(X,G)

[
9
, Remark 9.4]
.

Remark 9.8
.

Any scale
S
that generates
{\rm Eig}(X,G)
also generates the maximal subodometer factor of
(X,G)
.
In particular, it follows from Theorem
4.7
that for
x\in X
the filter
{\rm Eig}_{x}(X,G)
generates
(X_{\operatorname{MSF}},G)
.

9.3.
The maximal odometer factor

For a minimal action
(X,G)
denote
\mathfrak{X}
for the set of all odometers that are factors of
(X,G)
.
Since the trivial action is an odometer we observe
\mathfrak{X}
to be non-empty.
Considering the minimal common extension
\bigvee\mathfrak{X}
we observe the following.

Proposition 9.9
.

Let
(X,G)
be a minimal action.
There exists an odometer
(X_{\operatorname{MOF}},G)
such that

(a)

(X_{\operatorname{MOF}},G)
is a factor of
(X,G)
.

(b)

Whenever
(Y,G)
is an odometer and a factor of
(X,G)
, then
(Y,G)
is a factor of
(X_{\operatorname{MOF}},G)
.

The odometer satisfying (a) and (b) is unique up to conjugacy and called the
maximal odometer factor of
(X,G)
.

Remark 9.10
.

Note that for a subodometer
(X,G)
that is not an odometer the maximal subodometer factor is
(X,G)
and differs from the maximal odometer factor.
It follows from
[
9
, Remark 9.4]
that the maximal odometer factor of a minimal action
(X,G)
is the maximal odometer factor of the maximal subodometer factor.

Recall that the maximal subodometer factor of a minimal action
(X,G)
has the same eigenvalues as
(X,G)
.
We next show how to compute the eigenvalues of the maximal odometer factor.

Proposition 9.11
.

Let
(X,G)
be a minimal action and
(X_{\operatorname{MOF}},G)
be its maximal odometer factor.
We have
{\rm Eig}(X_{\operatorname{MOF}},G)=\bigcap_{x\in X}{\rm Eig}_{x}(X,G)
.

Proof.

Recall from Theorem
4.7
that
{\rm Eig}_{x}(X,G)
is a filter for
x\in X
.
Furthermore, from Proposition
8.4
we know that the intersection of filters is a filter.
Thus,
E:=\bigcap_{x\in X}{\rm Eig}_{x}(X,G)
is a filter.
It is straightforward to verify that
E
is conjugation invariant.
We thus observe that
E
is a filtered eigenset.
Denote
(X^{\prime},G)
for the
E
-odometer. From
E\subseteq{\rm Eig}(X,G)
we observe that
(X^{\prime},G)
is a factor of
(X,G)
and hence that
E={\rm Eig}(X^{\prime},G)\subseteq{\rm Eig}(X_{\operatorname{MOF}},G)
.

For the converse, consider a normal scale
S
with
\left\llbracket S\right\rrbracket={\rm Eig}(X_{\operatorname{MOF}},G)
.
For
\Gamma\in S
there exists a factor map
X\to G/\Gamma
.
Since
(X_{\operatorname{MOF}},G)
is a factor of
(X,G)
there exists a factor map
\pi\colon X\to G/\Gamma
and by the normality of
\Gamma
for
x\in X
we can modify
\pi
to enforce
\pi(x)=\Gamma
.
We thus have
\Gamma\in\bigcap_{x\in X}{\rm Eig}_{x}(X,G)=E
.
This shows
S\subseteq E
.
Since
E
is a filtered eigenset we have
{\rm Eig}(X_{\operatorname{MOF}},G)=\left\llbracket S\right\rrbracket\subseteq E
.
∎

Whenever
(X,G)
is a subodometer generated by a scale
S
it is natural to ask how
S
can be modified to obtain a scale for the maximal odometer factor of
(X,G)
.
For
\Gamma\in{\operatorname{Sub_{fin}}}(G)
we denote

\Gamma^{G}:=\left\langle\bigcup_{g\in G}\Gamma^{g}\right\rangle

for the
normal hull
of
\Gamma
.
Note that the normal hull is the smallest normal subgroup of
G
that contains
\Gamma
.

Proposition 9.12
.

If
(X,G)
is a subodometer and
S
a scale that generates
(X,G)
, then
S^{G}:=\{\Gamma^{G};\,\Gamma\in S\}
is a normal scale that generates
(X_{\operatorname{MOF}},G)
.

Proof.

Clearly,
S^{G}
is normal.
Since the map
S\ni\Gamma\mapsto\Gamma^{G}
is monotone w.r.t. set inclusion we observe that
S^{G}
is a scale.
Denote
(X^{G},G)
for the odometer generated by
S^{G}
.

It follows from
\Gamma\subseteq\Gamma^{G}
for all
\Gamma\in S
that
S
dominates
S^{G}
. Thus,

(X^{G},G)
is a factor of
(X,G)
.

Consider a factor
(Y,G)
of
(X,G)
that is an odometer.
Since
{\rm Eig}(Y,G)
is a filtered eigenset contained in
{\rm Eig}(X,G)=\left\llbracket S\right\rrbracket
we observe from Lemma
8.10
that
{\rm Eig}(Y,G)\subseteq S^{\mathfrak{f}}=S^{\mathfrak{u}}
.
For
\Gamma\in{\rm Eig}(Y,G)
we observe from the core-stability of
{\rm Eig}(Y,G)
that
\Gamma_{G}\in{\rm Eig}(Y,G)\subseteq S^{\mathfrak{u}}
.
Thus, there exists
\Lambda\in S
with
\Lambda\subseteq\Gamma_{G}
.
Since
\Gamma_{G}
is normal it follows that
\Lambda^{G}\subseteq(\Gamma_{G})^{G}=\Gamma_{G}\subseteq\Gamma
.
We thus have
\Gamma\in\left\llbracket S^{G}\right\rrbracket={\rm Eig}(X^{G},G)
.
This shows
{\rm Eig}(Y,G)\subseteq{\rm Eig}(X^{G},G)
and hence that
(Y,G)
is a factor of
(X^{G},G)
.

Thus
(X^{G},G)
satisfies the properties (a) and (b) of Proposition
9.9
and establishes
(X^{G},G)
as the maximal odometer factor of
(X,G)
.
∎

9.4.
The enveloping odometer

Consider a subodometer
(X,G)
and denote
\mathfrak{X}
for the family of all odometers that are extensions of
(X,G)
.

\mathfrak{X}
is non-empty, since it contains the universal odometer.
Considering
\bigwedge\mathfrak{X}
we observe the following from Theorem
1.2
.

Proposition 9.13
.

Let
(X,G)
be a subodometer.
There exists an odometer
(\mathcal{O}(X),G)
such that

(a)

(X,G)
is a factor of
(\mathcal{O}(X),G)
and

(b)

Any odometer
(X^{\prime},G)
that is an extension of
(X,G)
is also an extension of
(\mathcal{O}(X),G)
.

The odometer satisfying (a) and (b) is unique up to conjugacy and called the
enveloping odometer
.

A scale of the enveloping odometer is given as follows.

Proposition 9.14
.

If
(X,G)
is a subodometer and
S
a scale that generates
(X,G)
, then
S_{G}:=\{\Gamma_{G};\,\Gamma\in S\}
is a normal scale that generates
(\mathcal{O}(X),G)
.

Proof.

Clearly,
S_{G}
is normal.
Since the map
S\ni\Gamma\mapsto\Gamma_{G}
is monotone w.r.t. set inclusion, we observe that
S_{G}
is a scale.
Denote
(X_{G},G)
for the
S_{G}
-odometer.
We have
\Gamma_{G}\subseteq\Gamma
for all
\Gamma\in S
and hence
S_{G}
dominates
S
. Thus
(X,G)
is a factor of
(X_{G},G)
.

Let
(X^{\prime},G)
be an extension of
(X,G)
that is an odometer.
It follows from Theorem
5.5
that
S\subseteq{\rm Eig}(X,G)\subseteq{\rm Eig}(X^{\prime},G)
.
From Theorem
7.6
we know that
{\rm Eig}(X^{\prime},G)
is a core-stable filter and hence we have
S_{G}\subseteq{\rm Eig}(X^{\prime},G)
.
Since
{\rm Eig}(X^{\prime},G)
is conjugation invariant and upward closed we have

\left\llbracket S_{G}\right\rrbracket\subseteq{\rm Eig}(X^{\prime},G)
and Proposition
5.14
yields
{\rm Eig}(\mathcal{O}(X),G)\subseteq{\rm Eig}(X^{\prime},G)
.
From Theorem
5.5
we observe that
(X^{\prime},G)
is an extension of
(X,G)
.

This shows that
(X_{G},G)
satisfies (a) and (b) of Proposition
9.13
and hence is conjugated to the enveloping odometer.
∎

Proposition 9.15
.

A subodometer
(X,G)
is metrizable if and only if its enveloping odometer
(\mathcal{O}(X),G)
is metrizable.

Proof.

Clearly, if
(\mathcal{O}(X),G)
is metrizable, then so is its factor
(X,G)
.
For the converse assume that
(X,G)
is metrizable.
From Theorem
6.6
we know that
(X,G)
is generated by a countable scale
S
and hence also
S_{G}
is countable.
Since
S_{G}
generates
(\mathcal{O}(X),G)
we observe from Theorem
6.6
that
(\mathcal{O}(X),G)
is metrizable.
∎

We will next present the interplay of the enveloping odometer and the Ellis semigroup
(E(X),G)
of a minimal action.
For the definition and various properties of the Ellis semigroup see
[
1
, Section 3]
.

Proposition 9.16
.

Let
(X,G)
be a minimal action.

(i)

(X,G)
is a subodometer if and only if
(E(X),G)
is an odometer.

(ii)

If
(X,G)
is a subodometer, then
(E(X),G)
and
(\mathcal{O}(X),G)
are conjugated.

Proof.

(i):
Assume that
(E(X),G)
is an odometer.
By
[
1
, Theorem 3.6]
there exists a factor map
E(X)\to X
and it follows from Proposition
5.1
that
(X,G)
is a subodometer.

For the converse assume that
(X,G)
is a subodometer.
Since
(X,G)
is a minimal equicontinuous action it follows from
[
1
, Section 3]
that
(E(X),G)
is a minimal rotation.
Consider a factor map
\pi\colon\mathcal{O}(X)\to X
.
From
[
1
, Theorem 3.7]
we know that there exists a factor map
(E(\mathcal{O}(X)),G)\to(E(X),G)
.
Since
\mathcal{O}(X)
is a group rotation, we observe that
(\mathcal{O}(X),G)
and
(E(\mathcal{O}(X)),G)
are conjugated and hence there exists a factor map
\mathcal{O}(X)\to E(X)
.
Since factors of subodometers are subodometers, it follows from Proposition
5.1
that
(E(X),G)
is a subodometer. Since it is a rotation it is an odometer.

(ii):
Let
(X,G)
be a subodometer. As presented in the proof of (i) there exists a factor map
\mathcal{O}(X)\to E(X)
.
Furthermore, from (i) we know that
E(X)
is an odometer. Since
(X,G)
is a factor of
(E(X),G)
there exists a factor map
E(X)\to\mathcal{O}(X)
.
It follows from the coalescence of minimal equicontinuous actions that
(E(X),G)
and
(\mathcal{O}(X),G)
are conjugated.
∎

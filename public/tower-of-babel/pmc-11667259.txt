Sequential exploratory mixed-method research of an eHealth intervention on blood pressure, sleep quality and physical activity in obstructive sleep apnoea: rationale and methodology of the Moore4Medical trial protocol
Authors: Fran Valenzuela-Pascual, Francisco Jose Verdejo-Amengual, Oriol Martinez-Navarro, Joan Blanco-Blanco, Rabie Adel El Arab, Esther Rubinat-Arnaldo, Maria Masbernat-Almenara, Francesc Rubí‑Carnacea, Blanca Manuel Martí, Ferran Barbé, Manuel Sanchez-de-la-Torre
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionThe management of a chronic and frequent pathology, such as obstructive sleep apnoea (OSA), requires personalised programmes that implement new technology-based tools to improve the comprehensive treatment of the patient to reduce the morbidity associated with this disease. This study will evaluate the effectiveness of an eHealth tool in managing the pathophysiological consequences of OSA and how they impact the quality of life after 3 months of intervention among adults.Methods and analysisThis is a mixed-method sequential exploratory study protocol. Participants will be≥18 years with a new diagnosis of moderate OSA and diagnosed with hypertension. The qualitative phase will consist of personal semistructured interviews. The quantitative phase will be a triple-blind randomised controlled trial. The experimental group (n=135) will receive an eHealth intervention using an electronic wrist device and a mobile application that will offer specific healthcare recommendations, physical activity indications and hygienic and dietary advice. These recommendations will be based on the information obtained in the qualitative phase. Those in the control group (n=135) will receive the usual educational materials from the sleep unit. The primary outcome will be blood pressure changes at 3 months. Secondary outcomes are subjective sleep quality, sleep apnoea-related parameters, daytime sleepiness, physical activity, empowerment and motivation for change, quality of life, anthropometry, cost–benefit and adherence.Ethics and disseminationEthical approval for the study has been obtained from the Hospital Universitari Arnau de Vilanova (CEIC-2511). Results will be published in a peer-reviewed journal.Trial registration numberClinicalTrials.gov Identifier: NCT05380726.

Introduction

Background

Obstructive sleep apnoea (OSA) is a frequent public health condition that affects 34% of men and 17% of women.1 OSA has been associated with daytime sleepiness, sleep fragmentation and cardiovascular disease (CVD), including hypertension, stroke, angina, heart failure and myocardial infarction.24 Globally, CVD is a leading cause of death and disability. In 2019, 17.9 million people died from CVDs representing 32% of all global deaths.5 However, most CVDs can be prevented by addressing behavioural modifiable risk factors, such as tobacco use, unhealthy dietary habits, obesity, physical inactivity and excessive alcohol consumption.5

The occurrence of OSA is one of the most significant causes of elevated blood pressure (BP).6 As a major cardiovascular risk factor, OSA is intimately linked to hypertension.7 Additionally, hypertensive patients with OSA are more likely to develop resistant hypertension and cardiovascular events.8 9 Among CVD patients, OSA is estimated to have a 30%–50% prevalence. On the other hand, the prevalence of CVD among OSA patients ranges between 30% and 70%.10 Therefore, it is vital to diagnose CVD promptly and accurately to prevent irreversible damage, reduce the impact of the disease on the independent lives of patients, improve society’s quality of life and reduce social and health expenditures.11 To prevent CVD, it is imperative to detect and diagnose risk factors at their earliest stages.12 OSA treatment was associated with a significant reduction in systolic BP in trials that included patients with excessive daytime sleepiness and in trials where BP was uncontrolled and participants were not taking antihypertensive medications.13

The prevention of CVD requires the systematic implementation of preventive activities in clinical practice. It has been shown that therapeutic exercise can reduce the negative consequences of OSA and improve a patient’s metabolic level.1416 A recent meta-analysis found significant reductions in the frequency of the apnoea–hypopnea index (AHI) and daytime sleepiness, improved sleep efficiency and a higher VO1 peak, using exercise as an intervention. These studies revealed a reduction in the AHI between 24% and 34%, a decrease of 28% in daytime sleepiness and an increase in sleep efficiency of 5.8%.1719

The current healthcare environment, with more complex medical conditions and associated care costs, such as those for hypertension and diabetes, requires the application of novel, innovative technology that integrates advanced sensor designs to transform the individual diagnostic model into a therapeutic model, thereby improving preventive medicine.20 There has been a considerable expansion in the development and use of eHealth interventions over the last few years based on innovative health technologies and multimodel sensors to monitor sleep, physical activity and heart rate with substantial improvements in performance evaluation, data acquisition, storage and processing.21

Clinical guidelines recommend continuous airway pressure (CPAP) treatment as the gold standard for moderate and severe OSA.22 However, this management strategy has higher effectiveness in those with severe OSA and may not be suitable for those moderately affected.23 Therefore, this study will focus on moderate OSA subjects, which are the range that most need alternative approaches and those that we hypothesise may benefit most from our intervention. In addition, adherence to CPAP treatment remains low at 66%, and despite technological improvements, adherence has increased by only 1 hour per night over the last 20 years.24 25 In this scenario, physical activity and sleep management are suitable and effective strategies for improving OSA symptoms and CVD disorders.2630

The present study protocol is designed to evaluate the impact of an educational intervention using an eHealth tool on BP changes in patients with moderate OSA. The secondary purpose of the study will be to determine the effectiveness of this intervention regarding objective sleep parameters, subjective sleep quality, daytime sleepiness, physical activity habits, health-related quality of life, self-efficacy and empowerment, anthropometry, adherence to the programme/treatment and cost/benefit.

Methods and analysis

Study design

This study will use a mixed-method sequential exploratory design to answer the research question. This mixed methodology allows us to use the strengths of qualitative and quantitative phases while reducing their weaknesses.31 Specifically, the sequential exploratory design includes an initial qualitative phase followed by a quantitative phase to develop an educational tool.32 33 In this project, we propose that both phases must have the same relevance (QUAL–QUAN) for developing the educational tool. The development of the study must be conducted in three stages.34

QUAL: qualitative data collection through semistructured personal interviews and thematic analysis.

Description of the intervention

Algorithm development

The eHealth tool will work over an algorithm specifically and already designed for this project. The sleep heart health study (SHHS) has been used to develop and train the initial form of the algorithm.38 The dataset from the SHHS provides polysomnography data for more than 2000 patients. From these, we extracted the ECG and oxygen saturation signals to emulate the behaviour of the electronic wrist device, creating 30-s information windows every 3 min of the signal. With this information and deep learning, we developed predictive algorithms to identify OSA risk situations and sedentary behaviour, which will be managed using the information we obtained from the QUAL phase.

This deep learning is based on implementing a recurrent neural network using the sequence of variables provided by the electronic wrist device. The model we will train is a long short-term memory.

A pilot study will be conducted for 1 month with ten participants to gather initial data to test the algorithm. In this pilot study, the subjects will be requested to use the electronic wrist device to collect data and test the algorithm, but they will not receive any therapeutic education. During the QUAN phase, the algorithm will provide individualised interactions and responses to the patients based on their different variables analysed in real time by the wrist receiver device: cardiovascular parameters, sleep quality and structure of sleep, physical activity and oxygen saturation. This will allow for identifying critical points and improvements for the more significant undertaking.

Experimental group (QUAN)

The electronic wrist device will have specific sensors to measure the parameters necessary for providing feedback. These parameters will only be used to provide input to the subject and not as measures to determine the effectiveness of the intervention. The algorithm will provide individualised interactions and responses to the experimental subjects based on their different variables analysed in real time by the wrist receiver device. The eHealth tool will be a phone application containing all the educational materials developed by the research team (both tools will share real-time data from each patient between them). Depending on the information received from the electronic wrist device, the eHealth tool will recommend educational topics, such as hygienic and dietary measures, sleep-related habits or physical activity. The information provided will depend on the subjects' knowledge gaps/beliefs identified in the QUAL phase and the parameters measured by the electronic wrist device in each issue.

Control group (QUAN)

The participants in the control group will have the same electronic wrist device and access to the eHealth tool. However, the electronic wrist device will not provide feedback based on the algorithm and the parameters of each individual. The eHealth tool will provide general information on OSA extracted from the leaflets used in the Arnau de Vilanova-Santa María Hospital sleep unit instead of the materials developed from the QUAL phase. Data collection

Data collection will be conducted in two phases (QUAL–QUAN).

QUAL: to meet the qualitative objective of this study, the authors will use semistructured interviews because they are handy for obtaining a better and broader understanding of the issues related to the OSA from the patient’s perspective.39 40 The interviews will be conducted individually by a research team member in Spanish or Catalan (the mother tongue of the interviewer and interviewees) and digitally recorded with the interviewee’s informed written consent. Interviews will take place in the Faculty of Nursing and Physiotherapy of the University of Lleida to generate a neutral and comfortable setting different from the sleep unit consultation.41 The interviewer will use an interview guide produced after reviewing the literature based on the research team’s experience and knowledge. Interviews will be analysed by another research team member using deductive and inductive thematic analysis to identify common patterns across the interviews and generate potential themes to develop the educational tool.42 43

QUAN: the intervention will last for 3 months, and the variables will be measured before and after the intervention. An external company (Hi-Iberia) will create and maintain the eHealth tool and the electronic wrist device. One of the authors will meet with the study subjects to inform them about the study and deliver informed consent. Once individuals have signed the consent form, they will be given a wrist electronic device and access to the phone application. After logging into the website, patients will be asked to complete the questionnaires. The researchers will instruct the subject to place and maintain the devices.

Sample size

QUAL: a purposive sample will be used to achieve a representative number of patients with OSA.44 The sample size will be established by the thematic saturation of the results. This saturation will be achieved if no new themes are identified in two consecutive interviews.

QUAN: the sample size calculation is based on the study of Sapiña-Beltrán et al related to BP in patients with OSA.45 Accepting an alpha risk of 0.05 and a beta risk of 0.2 in a two-sided test, 135 subjects are necessary for the first group and 135 in the second to recognise as a statistically significant difference greater than or equal to 3 units. The common SD is assumed to be 8.1. It has been anticipated that there will be a drop-out rate of 15%.

The researchers will arrange an appointment with patients interested in participating in the study at the sleep unit to explain the study objectives and provide them with an information sheet and an informed consent form.

Group recruitment and assignment

The recruitment process will be performed independently in each phase of the study. However, in both phases, this recruitment process will occur in the same Arnau de Vilanova-Santa María Hospital sleep unit. The inclusion and exclusion criteria are also common in both phases.

QUAL: in this phase, patients will be recruited by the physician in the sleep unit. Once the physician makes the diagnosis and ensures that the individual meets the inclusion and exclusion criteria, the physician informs the patient about the existence of this project and invites him to contact the first author by telephone. If the subject agrees to participate in the study, the interview will be scheduled at the Faculty of Nursing and Physiotherapy of the University of Lleida.

QUAN: the recruitment process will start after QUAL’s end and the educational material’s development. This phase will include an educational eHealth tool intervention using a randomised controlled trial design. The recruitment process will be the same as in QUAL.

Randomisation (QUAN)

Sequence generation

The researchers will use a simple randomisation method with a 1:1 ratio. A randomisation process will be conducted using the STATS software programme by an external researcher who will keep the assignment on a computer that will be inaccessible to the rest of the staff.

Concealment mechanism and allocation implementation

To avoid selection bias, the subject allocation was concealed from the investigators who recruited the patients.46

To ensure the concealment of allocations, centralised randomisation is used using a laptop computer and a random number generator. This number will not relate to the intervention groups, so neither the researcher nor the subject can anticipate their assignment.

After the researcher confirms that the participants met the inclusion and exclusion criteria and they signed the informed consent, the subject will be randomly assigned.

Blinding

The study will be triple blinded since, due to its nature (intervention using an eHealth tool), it will be possible to blind both the participants and researchers. All the subjects (experimental and control) will receive the same wrist device and access to the same eHealth tool. While the experimental group will receive specific information related to the parameters assessed in real time by the wrist device, the control group will receive general information about OSA. Data from the sensors and the initial and follow-up questionnaires will be entered directly into the eHealth tool, where no researchers will be involved in evaluating the subjects, and the likelihood of bias is minimised.

Outcomes measures

Main outcome

Change in BP: 24-hours ambulatory blood pressure monitoring (24hABPM) (Spacelabs 90 217A, Hertford: UK) will be performed at baseline and after 3 months. BP will be measured every 20 min during the daytime (from 06:00 hours to 22:00 hours) and every 30 min during the nighttime (from 22:00 hours to 06:00 hours).45 Guidelines and standard criteria will be used to diagnose hypertension with the results of 24-hours ABPM (systolic BP ⩾135 mm Hg, diastolic BP ⩾85 mm Hg or both during the awake period).47 Circadian patterns were defined based on the nighttime/daytime BP ratio: non-dipper>0.9 and dipper≤0.9.

The formula (systolic BP+2× diastolic BP)/3 will calculate the mean BP.45

Secondary outcomes

The Pittsburgh sleep quality inventory (PSQI) is a 19-item list used to measure sleep quality and disturbances over the past month. As a part of the PSQI, various sleep characteristics are measured, including sleep latency (ie, the amount of time taken to fall asleep). On a scale of 0–3, each item is scored (0=not during the past month; 1=less than once a week; 2=once or two times a week and 3=three or more times a week).48 These scores are summed to generate an overall score. The higher the scores, the lower the quality of sleep. A score of more than 5 indicates poor sleep quality. With an alpha value of 0.83, the PSQI has demonstrated reliability in its internal consistency.4850

Respiratory polygraphy: we will use respiratory polygraphy (Embletta, ResMed, Bella Vista, NSW, Australia) to assess the AHI, oxygen desaturation index (ODI, >4%), minimum and mean oxygen saturation (SaO2), the average duration of events and percentage of time with SaO2<90%.51

Daytime sleepiness assessment: subjective sleepiness will be assessed using the Epworth sleepiness scale.52 This questionnaire contains eight items that indicate the probability of falling asleep during the activities of daily living, ranging from 0 (never) to 3 (high probability). Normal values will be established between 2 and 10, with values greater than 10 indicating daytime sleepiness.52 53

The type and quantity of antihypertensive drugs, as well as their reactivity, will be collected through patient medical records and analysed using a hierarchical approach to assess their impact on the study outcomes.

S6-minute walk test (6MWT): the 6MWT test evaluates the respiratory, cardiovascular, metabolic, musculoskeletal and sensorineural responses to exercise. It is considered a useful tool for assessing the functional capacity of patients with chronic respiratory and/or CVDs and is used to evaluate the submaximal level of functional performance at a similar level required for daily physical activities.54 6MWT measures the maximum distance an individual is able to walk as fast as possible (not run) using their usual walking aids if any, and take their regular medications before the test, in 6 min. The patients can rest is allowed as required but the time is not stopped. The 6MWT is normally carried out in a corridor of 30 m in length, on a hard and flat surface, and preferably indoors.55

Health empowerment: the Spanish version of the health empowerment scale (HES) will be used to measure empowerment. This questionnaire contains eight sections (self-control, self-efficacy, psychological coping, problem-solving, stress management, support motivation and decision-making). Each item is scored on a five-point Likert scale rating from 5 (strongly agree) to 1 (strongly disagree). Higher scores indicate greater levels of health-related empowerment. The HES Spanish version has a Cronbach’s alpha of 0.89.56

Self-efficacy for physical activity: the Spanish version of the self-efficacy scale for physical activity will be used. This questionnaire contains 39 items related to physical activity. Each item is scored on a ten-point scale rating, from 0 (cannot do it at all) to 10 (highly certain I can do it). These 39 items are contained into 3 sections: self-efficacy for physical activity, self-efficacy for physical activity in their daily life and self-efficacy in walking daily. Higher scores indicate the higher levels of self-efficacy regarding physical activity.57

Health-related quality of life: based on the EuroQoL questionnaire (EQ-5D-5L), each dimension of the questionnaire contains five response levels: no problems (Level 1) and slight, moderate, severe and extreme problems (Level 5). The entire set of EQ-5D-5L health states is transformed into a single 'utility' score based on public preferences for scoring. Furthermore, the instrument includes a visual analogue scale (EQ-VAS) for assessing self-perceived health. The scale is scored on a 0–100 mm scale, which corresponds to ‘the worst possible health’ and ‘the best possible health’.58

Anthropometry: anthropometric measurements will be taken before and after the intervention. The measures are height (m), weight (kg), body mass index (BMI) (kg/m2) and circumference measurements. Weight will be measured using an electric scale (Model Tanita BC-420, Tokyo, Japan) with a range of 0–200 kg and with an accuracy of within 100 g. Height will be measured with a portable stadiometer (Seca 274, Hamburg, Germany) with a precision of 0.1 cm and a range of 0–2.5 m. BMI will be calculated using the weight in kilograms divided by the square in metres (kg/m2). All circumference measurements will be taken with flexible tape, including neck, waist and hip measurements. To calculate the waist-to-hip ratio, the waist and hip circumferences (measured in triplicate and averaged) will be considered.

Cost analysis: a cost-effectiveness analysis will be performed. This analysis aims to compare the cost among the intervention group of this study and the costs of conservative treatment according to the research by Tarraubella et al.59

Adherence to the programme/treatment: the number of entries into the eHealth app will be used.

Data analysis

QUAL: the authors will transcribe and review the interviews before the analysis. Deductive and inductive coding will be used for data identification and integration, resulting in different meaning categories. Different conceptual themes will be generated through which the educational material will be built for the QUAN phase. The authors will analyse the interviews, and a different researcher will participate in the information triangulation to ensure the validity and reliability of the material.43 All the authors will participate in the data interpretation phase and educational tool development. Atlas.ti software will be used for coding and information handling.

QUAN: statistical analysis will be conducted on a blinded intention-to-treat approach.60 The alpha error will be set at 0.05. The descriptive statistics will use frequencies for the categorical variables and measures of central tendencies, such as the mean and SD or median and IQR for the quantitative variables. Sociodemographic baseline characteristics will be assessed between groups using student’s t-test for continuous data and the χ2 test for categorical data.

The normality of data distribution and homogeneity of variances will be analysed using Kolmogorov‒Smirnov and Levene’s tests, respectively. When the data are normally distributed, we will use a two-way mixed-methods analysis of variance to compare between subjects' factors (intervention group and control group) and within-subjects factors on the time points (T0 and T1). The results will be presented as estimated differences with 95% CIs. Pearson’s correlations will describe the association between adherence to the eHealth tool and the other quantitative variables.

Ethical considerations

Ethics approval for this study was obtained from the Clinical Research Ethics Committee of the Arnau de Vilanova-Santa María Hospital (CEIC2511). The research will be conducted following the fundamental ethical principles of respect, beneficence and justice established in the Belmont Report,61 the Declaration of Helsinki’s principles for medical research in humans62 and the Nuremberg Code’s legal and moral principles.63 Because our intervention is based on an educational tool, we do not expect to have any physical side effects. Patients in the experimental group will be advised to contact their sleep unit physician if they experience any physical problems or worsening of their condition. Additionally, all subjects in the experimental group will be able to get the researchers through the mobile application.

Data transfer and storage

The data collected from the quantitative phase and the wrist device will be stored on the servers of HI-Iberia Engineering and Proyectos SL. Access to the servers is only possible from the HI-Iberia network through personal ID and password. After signing the confidential agreement, these credentials will only be facilitated to the team members of HI-Iberia.

Audio information from in-depth interviews will be recorded with the consent of participants. All the audio data from the interviews will not contain personal identifiers. Informed consent will include the participants' full names and signatures. Nevertheless, these will not be collected by the investigator who coordinates and administers the groups or the interviews. During the transcription process, if any identifiers are disclosed, it will deliberately not transcribe any identifying information.

All the data from qualitative interviews and quantitative surveys will be accessible to the primary investigator. The deidentified data will be shared with coinvestigators only for analysis purposes.

Patient and public involvement

Patients and the public were not involved in the design of this study. Despite that, this is a mixed-methods design; thus, the interviews performed with people diagnosed with moderate OSA will directly inform the educational materials of the quantitative section.

Possible limitations

One limitation of QUAL is that a single interviewer will perform all interviews, and one analyst will perform the initial thematic analysis. Although an external researcher will validate the interview and analytic processes, this may be a source of potential bias. Another limitation may come from the author’s interpretation of the data provided by the study subjects; this potential bias will be overcome using the triangulation of researchers.

The purpose of this study is not to provide precise data on OSA patients but to contextualise the patients' perceptions within their own experience to develop educational materials. Regarding the QUAN phase, the sample size may represent another limitation. A smaller-than-expected final sample size may increase the likelihood of a type II error and limit the study’s internal validity, leading to inconclusive results. Regardless, our results will be limited to subjects with moderate OSA.

Another possible limitation is derived from using new technologies. In our project, the eHealth tool will contain educational materials in different formats, such as video, audio and 2-D images. However, Camerini et al64 noted that other alternatives or additional forms might be better accepted by subjects in the experimental group, thereby improving their experience.

Finally, one of the potential limitations in studies using educational tools for patients is the inability to ensure that the information provided has been learnt and integrated by the patient. Although we can analyse how often a patient has viewed the educational material, we cannot guarantee that this information has been fully understood and incorporated into the patient’s knowledge base.

Summary

This study belongs to Moore4Medical, a multicenter and international project from the European Union’s H2020 Framework Programme.

Mixed-methods approach will be used to identify OSA subjects' beliefs and knowledge regarding their condition and how to manage it to develop an app-based educational tool. Stage 1 of the study will identify OSA subjects' thoughts and lack of knowledge about their condition’s origin, meaning and management using qualitative data collection through semistructured personal interviews. Stage 2 of the study will allow the construction of the educational tool with the results obtained in the previous stage (topics or emerging categories). Stage 3 of the study will analyse the effectiveness of the educational tool using a triple-blind randomised controlled trial.

Knowledge-Embedded and Hypernetwork-Guided Few-Shot Substation Meter Defect Image Generation Method
Jackie Alex, Justin Petter

Abstract
Substation meters play a critical role in monitoring and ensuring the stable operation of power grids, yet their detection of cracks and other physical defects is often hampered by a severe scarcity of annotated samples. To address this few-shot generation challenge, we propose a novel framework that integrates Knowledge Embedding and Hypernetwork-Guided Conditional Control into a Stable Diffusion pipeline, enabling realistic and controllable synthesis of defect images from limited data. First, we bridge the substantial domain gap between natural-image pre-trained models and industrial equipment by fine-tuning a Stable Diffusion backbone using DreamBooth-style knowledge embedding. This process encodes the unique structural and textural priors of substation meters, ensuring generated images retain authentic meter characteristics. Second, we introduce a geometric crack modeling module that parameterizes defect attributes—such as location, length, curvature, and branching pattern—to produce spatially constrained control maps. These maps provide precise, pixel-level guidance during generation. Third, we design a lightweight hypernetwork that dynamically modulates the denoising process of the diffusion model in response to the control maps and high-level defect descriptors, achieving a flexible balance between generation fidelity and controllability. Extensive experiments on a real-world substation meter dataset demonstrate that our method substantially outperforms existing augmentation and generation baselines. It reduces Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) by 32.7%, increases diversity metrics, and—most importantly—boosts the mAP of a downstream defect detector by 15.3% when trained on augmented data. The framework offers a practical, high-quality data synthesis solution for industrial inspection systems where defect samples are rare. Keywords: Defect Image Generation, Few-Shot Learning, Stable Diffusion, Knowledge Embedding, Hypernetwork, Substation Equipment, Conditionally Controllable Synthesis

Introduction

Substation equipment plays a vital role in ensuring the safety and stable operation of power systems, with meters serving as critical instruments for power dispatch and operational monitoring. However, prolonged exposure to harsh environmental conditions—such as extreme weather, temperature fluctuations, and physical stressors—often leads to the development of defects like cracks and deformations in meter equipment. These defects can compromise measurement accuracy, disrupt normal operations, and even pose significant risks to grid reliability. Early detection of such defects is therefore essential for preventing cascading failures and maintaining system integrity.

Related Works

2.1
Traditional Data Augmentation Techniques

Conventional data augmentation methods have been widely adopted to address data scarcity in various computer vision tasks. These techniques primarily involve geometric transformations such as flipping, rotation, cropping, scaling, and noise addition. While these methods are computationally efficient and easy to implement, they exhibit significant limitations when applied to complex industrial scenarios. Traditional augmentation approaches often fail to capture semantic content and cannot generate novel defect patterns that deviate substantially from original samples. This limitation becomes particularly pronounced in specialized domains like substation meter inspection, where defect characteristics require precise geometric and contextual understanding. Furthermore, these methods primarily address intra-class variation but cannot create fundamentally new defect patterns that are essential for robust model training in few-shot conditions.

2.2
Generative Adversarial Networks

Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) represent a major breakthrough in generative modeling, enabling the synthesis of high-quality images through adversarial training between generator and discriminator networks. Subsequent improvements have significantly enhanced GAN performance and stability. Wasserstein GAN (WGAN) introduced gradient penalty mechanisms to improve convergence properties. The StyleGAN series introduced style-based generation mechanisms and regularization techniques that enabled fine control over image details and consistency. Recent work has demonstrated how pre-training strategies could enhance GAN performance in complex industrial scenarios. In industrial applications, region- and strength-controllable GANs have been developed for defect generation and segmentation in industrial images, while memory-augmented adversarial autoencoders have been proposed for surface defect detection.

2.3
Diffusion Models and Advanced Generation Techniques

Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models (DDPM) have emerged as powerful alternatives to GANs, offering improved training stability and generation quality

23

Denoising Diffusion Implicit Models (DDIM) reduced inference steps while maintaining generation quality. Latent Diffusion Models (LDM) reduced computational costs by operating in latent space while incorporating cross-attention mechanisms for multimodal generation. Recent advances have focused on improving controllability through methods such as classifier-free guidance and self-attention guidance mechanisms. The superior performance of diffusion models in generating high-quality, diverse images makes them particularly suitable for industrial defect generation tasks where both quality and variety are crucial for enhancing downstream detection performance.

3
Method

This section presents the proposed framework for few-shot generation of substation meter defect images. Our approach addresses the key challenges of domain adaptation, defect control, and generation diversity through three interconnected components: meter knowledge embedding, crack feature modeling, and conditional control generation. The overall methodology leverages the Stable Diffusion architecture while introducing novel mechanisms for industrial adaptation and precise defect specification.

3.1
Meter Knowledge Embedding

The significant domain gap between natural images (used for pre-training generative models) and specialized substation meter images necessitates effective domain adaptation strategies. To address this challenge, we employ a knowledge embedding approach based on DreamBooth fine-tuning, which associates unique identifiers with meter-specific characteristics. This process enables the model to learn the structural features of substation meters while preserving the generative capabilities of the pre-trained Stable Diffusion model.

The fine-tuning objective combines subject loss and prior preservation loss to balance between learning new concepts and maintaining pre-trained knowledge. The subject loss term optimizes the model’s ability to generate images containing specific defect features under unique identifier conditions:

L_{\text{sbj}}=\mathbb{E}_{x,c,\epsilon,t}\left[\omega_{t}\left\|\hat{x}_{\theta}(\alpha_{t}x+\sigma_{t}\epsilon,c)-x\right\|^{2}\right]

(1)

where
x
represents the real image,
c
denotes the text condition,
\hat{x}_{\theta}
indicates the model’s predicted image,
\alpha_{t}
and
\sigma_{t}
are diffusion noise scheduling parameters, and
\epsilon
is random noise. The prior preservation loss term constrains the generation results to prevent deviation from the original data distribution:

L_{\text{pr}}=\mathbb{E}_{x_{\text{pr}},c_{\text{pr}},\epsilon^{\prime},t^{\prime}}\left[\omega_{t}\left\|\hat{x}_{\theta}(\alpha_{t^{\prime}}x_{\text{pr}}+\sigma_{t^{\prime}}\epsilon^{\prime},c_{\text{pr}})-x_{\text{pr}}\right\|^{2}\right]

(2)

The combined loss function achieves dynamic balance through weighted combination:

L=L_{\text{sbj}}+\lambda L_{\text{pr}}

(3)

where
\lambda\in[0,1]
is a weighting parameter that adjusts the relative importance of the two objectives. This approach ensures that the model effectively incorporates meter-specific knowledge while retaining its general generation capabilities.

3.2
Crack Feature Modeling

To achieve precise control over defect characteristics, we develop a crack feature modeling module that generates geometrically constrained control images. This module processes normal meter images through structured preprocessing and integrates them with defect masks to produce control images with accurate spatial constraints.

For an input original image
I_{o}\in\mathbb{R}^{H\times W\times 3}
, we first extract the edge map
I_{l}\in\mathbb{R}^{H\times W\times 1}
through edge detection, image smoothing, and style enhancement operations. The edge map is then fused with the crack mask
I_{d}\in\mathbb{R}^{H\times W\times 1}
using pixel-wise maximum operation:

I_{c}^{\prime}(x,y)=\max(I_{d}(x,y),I_{l}(x,y))

(4)

where
I_{c}^{\prime}(x,y)
,
I_{d}(x,y)
, and
I_{l}(x,y)
represent the grayscale values at pixel
(x,y)
for the control image, crack image, and edge map, respectively, with values constrained to [0, 255].

To ensure realistic defect placement and prevent unreasonable crack distributions outside the meter dial area, we incorporate the Segment Anything Model (SAM) for precise region constraints. For input image
I_{o}
and user-provided prompt condition
c
, SAM first encodes the image and prompt:

f=E(I_{o}),\quad g=E^{\prime}(c)

(5)

where
f
represents the embedded features and
g
denotes the prompt features. The mask decoder then generates the dial constraint map
I_{r}
:

I_{r}=D(f,g),\quad I_{r}\in\mathbb{R}^{H\times W\times 1}

(6)

The final control image incorporates spatial constraints through conditional fusion:

I_{c}(x,y)=\begin{cases}I_{c}^{\prime}(x,y),&I_{r}(x,y)=255\\
I_{l}(x,y),&I_{r}(x,y)=0\end{cases}

(7)

This approach ensures that crack features are realistically constrained to appropriate regions while maintaining the structural integrity of the meter.

3.3
Conditional Control Generation

The final component of our method involves a hypernetwork-based conditional generation mechanism that enables precise control over the generation process while maintaining image diversity. This approach addresses the limitation of traditional generation methods in controlling defect characteristics during the generation process.

We implement a hypernetwork architecture that dynamically adjusts the generation parameters based on control conditions. The main Stable Diffusion model weights are frozen to preserve pre-trained knowledge, while a trainable hypernetwork branch is added to incorporate conditional information. The control image
I_{c}
is processed through an encoder to generate corresponding latent representations, which are combined with hypernetwork-generated dynamic weights to regulate the generation process.

The training objective for the conditional generation is defined as:

L=\mathbb{E}_{x,c,c^{+},\epsilon,t}\left[\left\|\hat{x}_{\theta}(\alpha_{t}x+\sigma_{t}\epsilon,c,c^{+})-x\right\|^{2}\right]

(8)

where
c
represents the text prompt condition,
c^{+}
denotes the control image condition information, and
\alpha_{t}x+\sigma_{t}\epsilon
represents the image representation at the current diffusion step. The control signals are injected at multiple feature layers throughout the diffusion process, providing both initial structural constraints and intermediate guidance to ensure the generated results conform to the specified conditions.

The complete methodology integrates these three components to achieve high-quality, controllable generation of meter defect images under few-shot conditions. The meter knowledge embedding ensures domain adaptation, the crack feature modeling provides geometric constraints, and the conditional control generation enables precise defect specification while maintaining generation diversity.

4
Experiments and Results

4.1
Dataset and Experimental Setup

To evaluate the proposed method, we constructed the Substation Meter Dataset (SMD), specifically designed for few-shot defect generation research. The dataset comprises 629 high-resolution images, including 100 samples with crack defects and 529 normal meter images. All images were collected from real-world substation inspection scenarios and underwent meticulous annotation by domain experts. The dataset was partitioned into training and validation sets with an 8:2 ratio to ensure proper evaluation of generalization performance.

All images were preprocessed to maintain consistency, with each image cropped to contain a single meter and resized to
512\times 512
pixel resolution. The defect masks used in crack feature modeling were sourced from existing industrial datasets and supplemented by hand-drawn annotations from domain experts, ensuring comprehensive coverage of various crack patterns and characteristics.

4.2
Evaluation Metrics

We employed both generation quality metrics and downstream task performance indicators for comprehensive evaluation. For generation quality assessment, we used:

•

Fréchet Inception Distance (FID)
: Measures the similarity between generated and real image distributions, with lower values indicating better generation quality.

•

Inception Score (IS)
: Evaluates both clarity and diversity of generated images, with higher scores representing better performance.

For downstream defect detection evaluation, we used standard object detection metrics:

\text{Precision}=\frac{TP}{TP+FP},\quad\text{Recall}=\frac{TP}{TP+FN}

\text{mAP}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}AP_{i},\quad\text{mAP50}=\text{mAP at IoU threshold of 50\%}

(10)

where
TP
,
FP
, and
FN
represent true positives, false positives, and false negatives, respectively.

4.6
Discussion

The experimental results comprehensively validate the effectiveness of our proposed method across multiple dimensions. The superior performance in both quantitative metrics and qualitative assessments demonstrates the method’s capability to address the unique challenges of few-shot meter defect generation. The significant improvements in downstream detection tasks further confirm the practical utility of the generated images.

The ablation studies highlight the complementary nature of the two main modules: meter knowledge embedding ensures domain adaptation and style consistency, while conditional control generation provides precise defect specification. Their combination achieves an optimal balance between generation quality, diversity, and controllability.

The method’s consistent performance across different base models (SD1.5, SDXL, FLUX) also demonstrates its flexibility and scalability, suggesting broad applicability to various generative backbones and potential for future extensions to more complex industrial scenarios.

5
Conclusion

This paper has presented a comprehensive framework for few-shot generation of substation meter defect images based on the Stable Diffusion model. Our work addresses critical challenges in industrial defect generation, including domain adaptation, precise defect control, and generation diversity under data-scarce conditions. The proposed method integrates three innovative components that work synergistically to achieve superior performance in both quantitative metrics and practical applications.

The
meter knowledge embedding
component effectively bridges the domain gap between pre-trained models and specialized industrial equipment. By fine-tuning the Stable Diffusion model with unique identifiers and a balanced loss function, we successfully incorporate substation meter characteristics while preserving the model’s generative capabilities. This adaptation ensures that generated images maintain structural fidelity to real meters, addressing a fundamental limitation of existing methods that often produce images with significant domain discrepancies.

The
crack feature modeling
approach introduces a novel methodology for geometric constraint specification in defect generation. By integrating edge maps, crack masks, and constraint maps through structured processing, we generate control images that provide precise spatial guidance for defect characteristics. The incorporation of the Segment Anything Model (SAM) enables accurate region constraints, ensuring that defects are realistically placed within appropriate meter areas. This component represents a significant advancement in controlled defect generation, particularly for industrial applications requiring precise defect localization.

The
conditional control generation
mechanism, based on hypernetwork architecture, enables dynamic adjustment of generation parameters while maintaining the diversity and quality of output images. This approach effectively balances the competing demands of controllability and creativity, allowing for precise defect specification without sacrificing image quality or variation. The method’s ability to inject control signals at multiple feature levels throughout the diffusion process ensures consistent adherence to defect specifications while preserving overall image coherence.

Experimental results on the Substation Meter Dataset (SMD) demonstrate the effectiveness of our approach, with superior performance in both generation quality metrics (FID: 76.72, IS: 2.45) and downstream detection tasks (26.9% precision improvement, 19.1% mAP50 increase). Comparative analyses with state-of-the-art methods confirm the advantages of our approach in handling the unique challenges of industrial defect generation. The ablation studies further validate the complementary nature of our proposed components, showing that their integration achieves optimal performance that surpasses any individual module.

The practical implications of this research extend beyond substation meter inspection to various industrial applications where data scarcity and precise defect specification are critical concerns. The generated high-quality defect images provide reliable data support for training robust detection models, contributing to the development of more efficient and reliable power inspection systems. The method’s flexibility across different base models (SD1.5, SDXL, FLUX) further demonstrates its adaptability and potential for broader applications.

Despite these achievements, certain limitations and future research directions deserve attention. The current method primarily focuses on crack-type defects, while real-world industrial environments often involve multiple defect types with varying characteristics. Future work should explore extensions to other defect categories, such as corrosion, deformation, and contamination. Additionally, investigating more sophisticated control mechanisms for complex defect patterns and exploring few-shot generation in multi-defect scenarios would further enhance the method’s practical utility. The integration of temporal information for video-based defect generation and the development of more efficient training strategies for resource-constrained environments also present promising research directions.

In conclusion, this research contributes a robust framework for few-shot industrial defect generation that effectively balances quality, diversity, and controllability. The proposed method demonstrates significant potential for enhancing power system inspection capabilities and provides a solid foundation for future advancements in industrial visual inspection technologies.

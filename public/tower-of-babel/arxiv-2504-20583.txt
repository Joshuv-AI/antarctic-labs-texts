Breakdown of sequential tunnel ionization in ultrashort electromagnetic pulses
D.I. Tyurin, V.V. Strelkov, S.V. Popruzhenko

Abstract
We consider double ionization of negative bromine ion Br − {\rm Br}^{-} in intense linearly polarized low-frequency laser fields. By solving numerically the two-electron time-dependent Schrödinger equation we demonstrate that while for electromagnetic pulses of a few tens of femtoseconds duration and longer the sequential single-electron approximation perfectly describes the ionization dynamics, for pulses as short as a few femtoseconds this picture breaks down entirely, and the electron-electron interaction suppresses the rate of ionization by approximately one order of magnitude. We also show weak but clear signatures of the collective tunneling effect in the photoelectron density distribution.



The fundamental effect of quantum mechanical tunneling discussed for the first time in the context of
\alpha
-decays
Gamow 1928
;
Gurney and Condon 1929
is pivotal in the physics of nuclei
Blatt and Weisskopf 2012
, the physics of atoms in external fields
Fedorov 1997
;
Popov 2004
, and the physics of condensed matter
Ankerhold 2007
.
Optical tunneling in atoms, molecules, and solids subject to intense infrared laser fields is the key phenomenon of contemporary nonlinear optics.
It underlies attosecond science
Krausz and Ivanov 2009
;
Calegari et al. 2016
, the generation of high-order harmonics (HHG)
Yue and Gaarde 2022
;
Ryabikin et al. 2023
, and attosecond spectroscopy
Agostini 2024
;
L’Huillier 2024
;
Krausz 2024
.
Tunnel and multiphoton ionization of atoms in the field of intense laser radiation first theoretically addressed by Keldysh
Keldysh 1965
, was observed initially as multiquantum absorption
Voronov and Delone 1966
in the mid-60es, and later – in the form of above-threshold ionization (ATI)
Agostini et al. 1979
, a process where the electron absorbs more photons when necessary to escape from the atom.
Both ATI and HHG can be efficiently explained within the semi-classical three-step model
Kuchiev 1987
;
Corkum 1993
, where optical tunneling serves as the first step.

A wide range of theoretical approaches describing the phenomenon of strong-field ionization and related processes are based on the single-active electron (SAE) approximation.
This is commonly substantiated by the fact that, in the regime of optical tunneling, the electron must travel a sufficient distance from the nucleus to appear in that part of space where the laser force dominates over the Coulomb attraction.
The higher the ionization potential
I_{p}
of the level, the smaller is the probability that the electron appears at large distances from the nucleus, so that the model in which the currently outermost electron solely interacts with the laser field sounds adequate.
We will refer to the regime of ionization dominated by the single-electron dynamics as
sequential
ionization.
The celebrated exclusion from this rule takes place in linearly polarized fields of non-relativistic intensity, where the outermost electron may undergo a recollision with the atomic residual along the same scenario, as underlies the effects of HHG
Corkum 1993
and high-energy ATI plateau
Paulus et al. 1994
.
Recollision leads to an extreme enhancement in double ionization that is up to several orders of magnitude, compared to that expected on the basis of the sequential mechanism.
This effect of nonsequential double ionization (NSDI) is the eminent example of an ionization channel strongly mediated by electron-electron interaction in the regime of tunneling.
By now, NSDI in atoms has been thoroughly studied both experimentally and in theory so that its recollision genesis is beyond doubt; see Ref.
Becker et al. 2012
for a review.
Other nosequential ionization mechanisms including the shake-off model
Dimauro and Agostini 1995
and the collective tunneling model
Zon 1999
;
Eichmann et al. 2000
;
Popruzhenko and Tyurin 2023
have been theoretically discussed and numerically examined
Lein et al. 2000
;
Eichmann et al. 2000
but not yet observed in experiments.

In linearly polarized fields of nonrelativistic intensity, the NSDI through recollision dominates other possible ionization channels by orders of magnitude, which makes a search of alternative nonsequential mechanisms extremely difficult both in numerical calculations and in experiment.
However, there are three ionization regimes where such alternatives can be revealed: (i) relativistic ionization, (ii) ionization in fields with circular or elliptical polarization where rescattering is damped out be the two-dimensional motion of the laser-driven electron (see e.g.
Klaiber et al. 2007
;
Shvetsov-Shilovski et al. 2008
for details and examples), and (iii) ionization by extremely short laser pulses with duration insufficient to bring the electron back to its parent ion.
The latter is hardly achievable in experiment, but easy to reach in theoretical consideration, where modeling of strong-field ionization by short unipolar pulses allows for addressing tiny effects otherwise shielded by the oscillating electron’s dynamics
Klaiber et al. 2022
.

Here, some elaboration of the terminology is required to avoid ambiguities and misinterpretations in the what follows.
We characterize ionization as sequential if the electrons escape from the atom one after another with some significant time interval.
The electron-electron interaction may or may not play an important role in this process, so that the sequential ionization is not necessarily equivalent to ionization of model non-interacting electrons.
The nonsequential ionization is apparently something opposite, so it is a process in which both electrons depart from the ion simultaneously or within a small time window.
Note that in the recollision induced NSDI the first electron leaves the atom approximately one laser period earlier than the second one. However, this fact does not withdraw this channel from the present definition, because eventually the electrons depart simultaneously, after the recollision, while the preceding evolution of the first electron is a virtual process.
Note that the term NSDI is equally applicable to different mechanisms of double ionization
Fittinghoff et al. 1992
;
Feuerstein et al. 2001
;
Eichmann et al. 2000
;
Lein et al. 2000
.
Simultaneous emission can take place even in the case of non-interacting electrons or for interacting electrons, but without recollision and excitation of intermediate bound states.
We will refer to such simultaneous emission as collective tunneling
Zon 1999
;
Eichmann et al. 2000
.

In this paper, we examine the ionization dynamics of a two-electron atomic system subject to a strong time-dependent electric field under conditions that eliminate recollision.
We choose the bromine negative ion as a target with two very different ionization potentials to provide conditions, where the collective channel of ionization
Zon 1999
;
Eichmann et al. 2000
can also potentially make a sizable contribution
Popruzhenko and Tyurin 2023
.
The Keldysh parameter
\gamma=\sqrt{2mI_{p}}\omega/eE_{0}

Keldysh 1965
is kept about unity or smaller, so that the ionization proceeds through the tunneling mechanism.
Here
\omega
and
E_{0}
are the characteristic frequency and strength of the electric field,
I_{p}
is the ionization potential,
e
and
m
are the elementary charge and mass correspondingly.
Solving numerically the time-dependent Schrödinger equation (TDSE), we calculate the probability of double ionization for the 2-dimensional (2D)
{\rm Br}^{-}
ion
and show that, very surprisingly, in short pulses with duration
\approx 2
fs this probability deviates dramatically from that expected in the case of a sequential process, when the electrons are being removed one after another.
Not less surprisingly, in longer pulses of several dozens fs duration, a rudimentary sequential tunneling model reproduces the exact numerical result with accuracy close to one per cent.
Thus in a single calculation we prove the validity of the sequential tunnel ionization picture for one set of parameters and demonstrate its complete failure for another.
Not less importantly, we also show that the two-particle density
distribution demonstrates a signature of the collective tunneling effect.

Our calculations made for a 2D two-electron atom, thus effectively for a 4D system, appear demanding as far as the required computer time and memory are concerned.
For better justification of our findings we also present some results obtained for a 1D two-electron atom, which allow for tracing the wave function evolution at a much longer time scale.

We compare the ionization dynamics of two model systems: (a) a 2D two-electron negative ion with interacting electrons and (b) a 2D

V_{\text{ion}}(\bm{r})=\frac{-1}{\sqrt{\bm{r}^{2}+a^{2}}}~,

(1)

V_{\text{int}}(\bm{r}_{1},\bm{r}_{2})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{(\bm{r}_{1}-\bm{r}_{2})^{2}+b^{2}}}~.

(2)

\tilde{E}(x,t)=\begin{cases}E(t)\cos^{2}\left(\dfrac{\pi x}{2x_{0}}\right),~|x|\leq x_{0}\\

Analysis of the distributions reveals two more effects present for the chosen SPR parameters: (i) considerable suppression of the two-electron ionization probability, when the e-e repulsion blocks the diagonal flux and (ii) simultaneous tunneling of two electrons.
Firstly, comparison of the distributions shown in Fig.4(a) and (b) demonstrates that for the 1D ion the e-e interaction blocks the diagonal flux entirely, which must lead to a great decrease in double ionization, compared to the prediction of the SAE sequential model.

Secondly, in the more physically meaningful case of the 2D ion the effect of suppression essentially survives, as Figs.4(c,d) and 5(b) show.

In conclusion, we performed numerical TDSE solutions for a model two-electron negative ion with ionization potentials equal to those of
{\rm Br}^{-}
, subject to a strong time-dependent electric field in 1D and 2D space.
Conditions of the tunnel field ionization were fulfilled for both the outer and inner electrons.
The aim was to find to what extent and under which conditions the e-e interaction influences the rate of double ionization destroying the customary picture of sequential single-electron tunneling.
Our results show that this happens indeed in short pulses, when the tunnel ionization rate for the inner electron becomes sizable before the outer electron is removed with guarantee.
In this case, the probability of double ionization is suppressed by more than one order of magnitude, compared to the case of independent sequential tunneling.
From the other side, our results show that in long pulses the sequential tunneling concept applies surprisingly well.
Finally, we demonstrated weak but clear signatures of collective tunneling, which is almost entirely blocked by e-e repulsion in 1D, but survives in 2D and therefore should be expected even stronger in real 3D atoms and ions.

Our analysis fundamentally revisits the concept of collective tunneling in atoms introduced in
Zon 1999
;
Eichmann et al. 2000
25 years ago.
The results reported here unambiguously demonstrate that the semiclassical model
Zon 1999
describing simultaneous tunneling of two electrons relying on the full discard of e-e repulsion greatly overestimates the probability: for the parameters chosen in our calculations this model would predict the near-diagonal flux comparable or even dominating over the sequential one.
Also, our results explain why the collective tunneling contribution has not been detected in 1D TDSE numerical solutions of
Eichmann et al. 2000
;
Lein et al. 2000
. Our results show also that the single active electron approximation routinely applied in the theory of strong field processes, particularly in the tunneling regime, is strongly limited for intense ultrashort laser pulses.

Our predictions may have an important impact at least in two research areas of strong-field atomic physics.
Firstly, these results suggest the way to a search for collective tunneling in atomic systems.
To this end, one needs to examine correlated momentum distributions of electron pairs for double ionization in a circularly polarized laser pulse, which acts efficiently as a unipolar pulse used in the present calculations.
The qualitative difference between the density distributions of Figs.1(c) and 4(c) should lead to the corresponding difference in the momentum distributions, which can be measured using the COLTRIMS technique
Ullrich et al. 2003
.
Secondly, the predicted breakdown of the sequential tunneling picture should be observable and even playing a quantitatively important role in the process of multiple ionization of atoms in short laser pulses of extreme intensities, where, due to the high peak field and the steepness of the time envelope the saturation time for
d
and
p
sub-shells can lie in the sub-fs domain.
Taking into account that relative values of the ionization potentials within such sub-shells vary from one level to another much less than in the considered negative bromine ion, one should expect even stronger deviations from the sequential ionization picture than those demonstrated here.
In particular, the suppression of ionization induced by the e-e correlation should be of interest for the development of numerical particle-in-cell codes aimed at modeling of laser-plasma dynamics with multiple field ionization of atoms incorporated
Derouillat et al. 2018
.

Fast reaction limits and convergence rate for nonlinear bulk-surface reaction-diffusion systems modeling reversible chemical reactions
The Tuan Hoang, Nhu Phong Tham, Bao Quoc Tang

Abstract
The fast reaction limit for a nonlinear bulk-surface reaction-diffusion system is investigated. The system model describes a reversible reaction with arbitrary stoichiometric coefficients, where one chemical is present in a bounded vessel Ω \Omega and the other chemical lies only on the boundary ∂ Ω \partial\Omega where the reaction takes place. In the limit as the reaction rate constant tends to infinity, we prove that the solution converges strongly to that of a heat equation with nonlinear dynamical boundary condition. This is obtained by showing a-priori estimates of solutions which are uniform in the reaction rate constants. In order to overcome the difficulty caused by the bulk-surface coupling, we consider the limit in suitable product spaces where the Aubin-Lions lemma is applicable. Moreover, in the case of equal stoichiometric coefficients, we obtain the convergence rate of the fast reaction limit by exploiting suitable estimates of the limiting system.

Many nature phenomena involve processes occurring simultaneously in a domain’s interior (the bulk) and on its boundary (the surface), with intricate coupling between these two regions. From cell biological signalling proceeses (

The study of these problems gives rise to bulk-surface (or volume-surface) systems, which have been investigated extensively in the last decade. In this paper, we study the fast reaction limit of a nonlinear bulk-surface system modelling a reversible chemical reaction.

To describe the problem under consideration, let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
be a bounded domain with smooth boundary
\Gamma:=\partial\Omega
, and
\alpha,\beta\geq 1
be arbitrary constants. Let
\mathcal{U}
be a bulk-chemical and
\mathcal{V}
be a surface-chemical, and react on the surface
\Gamma
in the following chemical reaction

\alpha\mathcal{U}\underset{k}{\overset{k}{\leftrightarrows}}\beta\mathcal{V}

where
k>0
is the reaction rate constants. Assume that
\mathcal{U}
diffuses in
\Omega
and
\mathcal{V}
diffuses along
\Gamma
, respectively, we can apply Fick’s law for the diffusion and the mass action law for the reaction to obtain the following bulk-surface system

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u-d_{u}\Delta u=0,&x\in\Omega,t>0,\\
\partial_{\mathbf{n}}u=-\alpha k(u^{\alpha}-v^{\beta}),&x\in\Gamma,t>0,\\
\partial_{t}v-d_{v}\Delta_{\Gamma}v=\beta k(u^{\alpha}-v^{\beta}),&x\in\Gamma,t>0,\\
u(x,0)=u_{0}(x),&x\in\Omega,\\
v(x,0)=v_{0}(x),&x\in\Gamma.\end{cases}

(1)

Here
u(x,t)
and
v(x,t)
represent for the concentrations of
\mathcal{U}
on
\Omega
and
\mathcal{V}
on
\Gamma
, respectively,

\Delta_{\Gamma}
is the Laplace–Beltrami operator on
\Gamma

[
19

\Gamma

are diffusion coefficients. Moreover, the initial condition
(u_{0},v_{0})
is assumed non-negative and bounded. From (
1

\alpha\int_{\Omega}u(x,t)dx+\beta\int_{\Gamma}v(x,t)dS=\alpha\int_{\Omega}u_{0}(x)dx+\beta\int_{\Gamma}v_{0}(x)dS=M_{0}\geq 0

for all
t>0
.

The study of singular limits for bulk-surface systems has been still limited. There are several works considering the fast diffusion limits,

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}w-d_{u}\Delta w=0,&x\in\Omega,t>0,\\
d_{u}\nabla w\cdot{\mathbf{n}}=-\frac{\alpha}{\beta}\big(\partial_{t}(w^{\alpha/\beta})-d_{v}\Delta_{\Gamma}(w^{\alpha/\beta})\big),&x\in\Gamma,t>0,\\

(2)

This is a parabolic equation with

Our first main result is the strong convergence of the fast reaction limit. Thanks to the reversibility of the reaction, the system (
1
) possesses an entropy functional which is decreasing in time. This helps to get certain a-priori estimates of the solutions which are uniformly in
k
, and moreover it shows that

as

. In order to get the strong convegence of

and

, we need certain estimates of the time derivative. In
[
6
]
, when two equations are both posed in the posed in the bulk, the authors therein sum them up and show the strong convergence of the sum of the solutions, as consequently for each separately.
Due to the bulk-surface coupling, this idea of summing up the equations do not work. For the linear cse, the authors in
[
16
]
utilized the linearity of the system and Ball’s energy method to prove the convergence of fast reaction limit without having to invoke the Aubin-Lions lemma. This technique, however, seems not to be applicable to the nonlinear system (
1
). In order to overcome this issue, our main idea is to consider the solutions

\|u_{k}(t)-w(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|v_{k}(t)-w|_{\Gamma}(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}\leq Ck^{-1/2},\quad\forall t\in(0,T).

2.1
Existence and uniqueness of solution

The existence of a unique global solution to (

First, we recall the definition of a weak solution to (

Definition 2.1
.

A pair of functions

1

\displaystyle u\in C([0,T];L^{2}(\Omega)),\quad u\in L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{\infty}(\Omega))\cap L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega)),

(3)

\displaystyle v\in C([0,T];L^{2}(\Gamma)),\quad v\in L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{\infty}(\Gamma))\cap L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\Gamma)),

(4)

and the following weak formulation holds

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}(-u\varphi_{t}+d_{u}\nabla u\nabla\varphi)dxdt=\int_{\Omega}u_{0}\varphi(0)dx-\frac{\alpha}{\epsilon}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Gamma}(u^{\alpha}-v^{\beta})\varphi dSdt\\
&\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Gamma}(-v\psi_{t}+d_{v}\nabla_{\Gamma}v\nabla_{\Gamma}\psi)dSdt=\int_{\Gamma}v_{0}\psi(0)dS+\frac{\beta}{\epsilon}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Gamma}(u^{\alpha}-v^{\beta})\psi dSdt\end{aligned}\right.

(5)

for all test functions
\varphi\in C^{1}([0,T];L^{2}(\Omega))

\cap

L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega))

\psi\in C^{1}([0,T]

;L^{2}(\Gamma))\,\cap\,L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\Gamma))
, with
\varphi(T)\equiv 0,\psi(T)\equiv 0

Theorem 2.1
.

For any non-negative and bounded initial data
(u_{0},v_{0})\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)\times L^{\infty}(\Gamma)
, there exists a unique global non-negative weak solution to (
1

Let

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u_{\delta}-d_{u}\Delta u_{\delta}=0,&x\in\Omega,t>0,\\
\partial_{\mathbf{n}}u_{\delta}=-\frac{\alpha}{\epsilon}F_{\delta}(u_{\delta},v_{\delta}),&x\in\Gamma,t>0,\\
\partial_{t}v_{\delta}-d_{v}\Delta_{\Gamma}v_{\delta}=\frac{\beta}{\epsilon}F_{\delta}(u_{\delta},v_{\delta}),&x\in\Gamma,t>0,\\
u_{\delta}(x,0)=u_{0}(x),&x\in\Omega,\\
v_{\delta}(x,0)=v_{0}(x),&x\in\Gamma,\end{cases}

Remark that

To show the existence of the approximate solution, we use Galerkin’s method (see e.g.
[
12
, Theorem 7.1]
).

Lemma 2.1
.

The system (
6
) has unique (weak) solution
(u_{\delta},v_{\delta})
for each parameter
\delta>0
.

Proof.

Similarly to Definition
2.1
,
(u_{\delta},v_{\delta})
is a weak solution to (
6
) if

\displaystyle u_{\delta}\in C([0,T];L^{2}(\Omega)),\quad u_{\delta}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{\infty}(\Omega))\cap L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega)),

(7)

\displaystyle v_{\delta}\in C([0,T];L^{2}(\Gamma)),\quad v_{\delta}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{\infty}(\Gamma))\cap L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\Gamma)),

(8)

and the following weak formulation holds

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}(-u_{\delta}\varphi_{t}+d_{u}\nabla u_{\delta}\nabla\varphi)dxdt=\int_{\Omega}u_{0}\varphi(0)dx-\frac{\alpha}{\epsilon}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Gamma}F_{\delta}(u_{\delta},v_{\delta})\varphi dSdt\\
&\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Gamma}(-v_{\delta}\psi_{t}+d_{v}\nabla_{\Gamma}v_{\delta}\nabla_{\Gamma}\psi)dSdt=\int_{\Gamma}v_{0}\psi(0)dS+\frac{\beta}{\epsilon}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Gamma}F_{\delta}(u_{\delta},v_{\delta})\psi dSdt\end{aligned}\right.

(9)

for all test functions
\varphi\in C^{1}([0,T];L^{2}(\Omega))

\cap

L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega))
and
\psi\in C^{1}([0,T]

;L^{2}(\Gamma))\,\cap\,L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\Gamma))
, with
\varphi(T)\equiv 0,\psi(T)\equiv 0

Choosing an orthogonal basis

(for example,

From to the finite dimensional system, we have for

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u_{\delta}-d_{u}\Delta u_{\delta}=0,&x\in\Omega,t>0\\

\delta
.

Proof.

This lemma is a direct consequence of the previous lemma and the following estimate (see
[
29
, Exercise 4.1]
)

\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma)}\leq\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\quad\forall u\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}(\Omega).

∎

Next, we will show that
(u_{\delta},v_{\delta})
is bounded uniformly in
L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega)\times H^{1}(\Gamma))
.

Lemma 2.4
.

The approximate solution
(u_{\delta},v_{\delta})
of (
6
) is bounded uniformly in
L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega)\times H^{1}(\Gamma))
.

Proof.

We consider the logarithm entropy function (see
[
13
]
):

E[u_{\delta},v_{\delta}](t)=\int_{\Omega}u_{\delta}(\log u_{\delta}-1)dx+\int_{\Gamma}v_{\delta}(\log v_{\delta}-1)dS,

(16)

where
\log(x)
is the natural logarithm of the positive number
x
. Formally, the dissipation of entropy function is calculated
D(t)=\frac{-d}{dt}E(t)=\langle-\partial_{t}u_{\delta},\log(u_{\delta})\rangle_{\Omega}+\langle-\partial_{t}v_{\delta},\log(v_{\delta})\rangle_{\Gamma}
and then choose
\log(u_{\delta})
(and
\log(v_{\delta})
, respectively) as the test functions. However,
\log(u_{\delta})
and
\log(v_{\delta})
do not belong to
L^{2}(\Omega)
and
L^{2}(\Gamma)
since we do not have positive lower bounds of
u_{\delta}
and
v_{\delta}
, respectively. To overcome this, we replace
u_{\delta}
(and
v_{\delta}
) by
u_{\delta}+\lambda
(and
v_{\delta}+\lambda
), with
\lambda
is a small positive parameter and taking the limit
\lambda\to 0^{+}
. We introduce the modified entropy function

E_{\lambda}[u_{\delta},v_{\delta}](t)=\int_{\Omega}(u_{\delta}+\lambda)(\log(u_{\delta}+\lambda)-1)dx+\int_{\Gamma}(v_{\delta}+\lambda)(\log(v_{\delta}+\lambda)-1)dS.

We calculate the time-derivative entropy function along trajectories

\displaystyle D_{\lambda}(t)

\displaystyle=-\frac{d}{dt}E_{\lambda}(t)

\displaystyle=d_{u}\int_{\Omega}\nabla u_{\delta}\nabla(\log(u_{\delta}+\lambda))dx+\frac{\alpha}{\delta}\int_{\Gamma}\frac{u_{\delta}^{\alpha}-v_{\delta}^{\beta}}{1+\delta|u^{\alpha}_{\delta}-v^{\beta}_{\delta}|}\log(u_{\delta}+\lambda)dS

\displaystyle\quad+d_{v}\int_{\Gamma}\nabla_{\Gamma}v_{\delta}\nabla_{\Gamma}(\log(v_{\delta}+\lambda))dS-\frac{\beta}{\delta}\int_{\Gamma}\frac{u_{\delta}^{\alpha}-v_{\delta}^{\beta}}{1+\delta|u^{\alpha}_{\delta}-v^{\beta}_{\delta}|}\log(v_{\delta}+\lambda)dS

\displaystyle=\,d_{u}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|\nabla u_{\delta}|^{2}}{u_{\delta}+\lambda}dx+d_{v}\int_{\Gamma}\frac{|\nabla_{\Gamma}v_{\delta}|^{2}}{v_{\delta}+\lambda}dS+\frac{1}{\delta}\int_{\Gamma}\frac{u_{\delta}^{\alpha}-v_{\delta}^{\beta}}{1+\delta|u^{\alpha}_{\delta}-v^{\beta}_{\delta}|}\log\frac{(u_{\delta}+\lambda)^{\alpha}}{(v_{\delta}+\lambda)^{\beta}}dS.

Then, by integrating
D_{\lambda}(t)
from
0
to
T
, we get

\displaystyle E_{\lambda}(0)-E_{\lambda}(T)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}D_{\lambda}(t)dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\left(d_{u}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|\nabla u_{\delta}|^{2}}{u_{\delta}+\lambda}dx+d_{v}\int_{\Gamma}\frac{|\nabla_{\Gamma}v_{\delta}|^{2}}{v_{\delta}+\lambda}dS\right)dt

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\delta}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Gamma}\frac{u_{\delta}^{\alpha}-v_{\delta}^{\beta}}{1+\delta|u_{\delta}^{\alpha}-v_{\delta}^{\beta}|}\log\frac{(u_{\delta}+\lambda)^{\alpha}}{(v_{\delta}+\lambda)^{\beta}}dSdt.

We can rewrite the above equation to:

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\left(d_{u}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|\nabla u_{\delta}|^{2}}{u_{\delta}+\lambda}dx+d_{v}\int_{\Gamma}\frac{|\nabla_{\Gamma}v_{\delta}|^{2}}{v_{\delta}+\lambda}dS\right)

\displaystyle dt=E_{\lambda}(0)-E_{\lambda}(T)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\epsilon}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Gamma}

\displaystyle\frac{v_{\delta}^{\beta}-u_{\delta}^{\alpha}}{1+\delta|u_{\delta}^{\alpha}-v^{\beta}_{\delta}|}\log\frac{(u_{\delta}+\lambda)^{\alpha}}{(v_{\delta}+\lambda)^{\beta}}dS

Since the initial condition
(u_{0},v_{0})\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)\times L^{\infty}(\Gamma)
, the term
E_{\lambda}(0)
can be bounded uniformly (by choosing
\lambda<1
). On the other hand,
E_{\lambda}(T)
has a lower bound without depending on
\lambda
, thanks to the boundedness of
(u_{\delta},v_{\delta})
. Thus, we have

\displaystyle d_{u}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|\nabla u_{\delta}|^{2}}{u_{\delta}+\lambda}dxdt+

\displaystyle d_{v}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Gamma}\frac{|\nabla_{\Gamma}v_{\delta}|^{2}}{v_{\delta}+\lambda}dSdt

\displaystyle\leq C+\frac{1}{\epsilon}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Gamma}\frac{v_{\delta}^{\beta}-u_{\delta}^{\alpha}}{1+\delta|u^{\alpha}_{\delta}-v^{\beta}_{\delta}|}\log\frac{(u_{\delta}+\lambda)^{\alpha}}{(v_{\delta}+\lambda)^{\beta}}dS,

(17)

with
C
is a constant that does not depend on
\lambda
and also
\delta
. By using Monotone Convergence Theorem,

\displaystyle\lim_{\lambda\to 0^{+}}\left(d_{u}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|\nabla u_{\delta}|^{2}}{u_{\delta}+\lambda}dx+d_{v}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Gamma}\frac{|\nabla_{\Gamma}v_{\delta}|^{2}}{v_{\delta}+\lambda}dS\,dt\right)

\displaystyle=d_{u}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|\nabla u_{\delta}|^{2}}{u_{\delta}}dxdt+d_{v}

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Gamma}\frac{|\nabla_{\Gamma}v_{\delta}|^{2}}{v_{\delta}}dSdt.

For the logarithm term, it has an upper bound for each fixed
\delta
since the solution
(u_{\delta},v_{\delta})
is non-negative and bounded

\frac{(v_{\delta}^{\beta}-u_{\delta}^{\alpha})}{1+\delta|u^{\alpha}_{\delta}-v^{\beta}_{\delta}|}\log(u_{\delta}+\lambda)^{\alpha}\leq C,

where the constant
C
does not depend on
\lambda
(by choosing
\lambda<1
). Similarly, we have

\frac{(-v_{\delta}^{\beta}+u_{\delta}^{\alpha})}{1+\delta|u^{\alpha}_{\delta}-v^{\beta}_{\delta}|}\log(v_{\delta}+\lambda)^{\beta}\leq C,

which implies that
\frac{v_{\delta}^{\beta}-u_{\delta}^{\alpha}}{1+\delta|u^{\alpha}_{\delta}-v^{\beta}_{\delta}|}\log\frac{(u_{\delta}+\lambda)^{\alpha}}{(v_{\delta}+\lambda)^{\beta}}
has an upper bound. Then, we can use Fatou’s lemma for a function that has upper bounded

\displaystyle\limsup_{\lambda\to 0^{+}}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Gamma}

\displaystyle(v_{\delta}^{\beta}-u_{\delta}^{\alpha})\log\frac{(u_{\delta}+\lambda)^{\alpha}}{(v_{\delta}+\lambda)^{\beta}}dS

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Gamma}\limsup_{\lambda\to 0^{+}}(v_{\delta}^{\beta}-u_{\delta}^{\alpha})\log\frac{(u_{\delta}+\lambda)^{\alpha}}{(v_{\delta}+\lambda)^{\beta}}dS

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Gamma}(v_{\delta}^{\beta}-u_{\delta}^{\alpha})\log\frac{u_{\delta}^{\alpha}}{v_{\delta}^{\beta}}dS\leq 0.

Combining these estimates and take the limit
\lambda\to 0^{+}
in (
2.1
), we obtain the uniform estimation

d_{u}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|\nabla u_{\delta}|^{2}}{u_{\delta}}dxdt+d_{v}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Gamma}\frac{|\nabla_{\Gamma}v_{\delta}|^{2}}{v_{\delta}}dSdt\leq C,

where
C
is a constant that does not depend on
\lambda
. Finally, from the uniform boundedness of
(u_{\delta},v_{\delta})
in
L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{\infty}(\Omega)\times L^{\infty}(\Gamma))
, we can show that the gradient is also bounded uniformly

\displaystyle\|\nabla u_{\delta}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{\delta}|^{2}dxdt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|\nabla u_{\delta}|^{2}}{u_{\delta}}u_{\delta}dxdt

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{T}\|u_{\delta}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|\nabla u_{\delta}|^{2}}{u_{\delta}}dxdt

\displaystyle\leq\|u_{\delta}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{\infty}(\Omega))}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|\nabla u_{\delta}|^{2}}{u_{\delta}}dxdt

\displaystyle\leq M

with a constant
M
is independent to
\delta
. By a similar argument, we obtain the conclusion for the gradient of
v_{\delta}

\|\nabla_{\Gamma}v_{\delta}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Gamma)}\leq M,

which ends the proof.
∎

Finally, we need show that the time-derivative of
(u_{\delta},v_{\delta})
is also bounded in a suitable functional space.

Lemma 2.5
.

It holds that
\partial_{t}u_{\delta}
and
\partial_{t}v_{\delta}
are bounded in the space
L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega)^{*})
and
L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\Gamma)^{*})
, respectively, uniformly in
\delta>0
.

Proof.

Consider the weak formulation for
u_{\delta}
, it holds for all
\varphi\in L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega))

\int_{0}^{T}\langle\partial_{t}u_{\delta},\varphi\rangle_{\Omega}+\int_{\Omega}d_{u}\nabla u_{\delta}\nabla\varphi dxdt=-\frac{\alpha}{\epsilon}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Gamma}F_{\delta}(u_{\delta},v_{\delta})\varphi dSdt.

(18)

The right hand side can be estimated by using the fact that
|F(u_{\delta},v_{\delta})|\leq|u_{\delta}^{\alpha}-v_{\delta}^{\beta}|
and
\{u_{\delta}\},\{v_{\delta}\}
are uniformly bounded in
L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{\infty}(\Gamma))

\frac{\alpha}{\epsilon}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Gamma}F_{\delta}(u_{\delta},v_{\delta})\varphi dSdt\leq C\|\varphi\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Gamma))}\leq C\|\varphi\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega))}.

Then, combining this with Lemma
2.4
, we have the estimation

\int_{0}^{T}\langle\partial_{t}u_{\delta},\varphi\rangle_{\Omega}dt\leq C\|\varphi\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega))}

for all
\varphi\in L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega))
, which gives
\{\partial_{t}u_{\delta}\}
is bounded uniformly in
L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega)^{*})
. Using similar argument, we also have the boundedness for
\{\partial_{t}v_{\delta}\}
.
∎

Now, we can pass the limit of the sequence
\{u_{\delta},v_{\delta}\}
as
\delta\to 0
, to obtain the solution of (
1
).

Proof of the Theorem
2.1
.

From the boundedness in Lemmas
2.4
and
2.5
, we can appply Aubin-Lions lemma to get the strong convergence as
\delta\to 0
, up to a subsequece,

u_{\delta}\to u\text{ in }L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))\quad\text{ and }\quad v_{\delta}\to u\text{ in }L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Gamma))

for some non-negative functions
u\in L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega))\cap L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{\infty}(\Omega))
and
v\in L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\Gamma))\cap L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{\infty}(\Gamma))
. Thanks to the uniform-in-
\delta
boundedness of
u_{\delta}
and
v_{\delta}
, we also obtain
F_{\delta}(u,v)\to u^{\alpha}-v^{\beta}
as
\delta\to 0
in
L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega)\times L^{2}(\Gamma))
. Therefore, we can pass to the limit as

in the weak formulation of (
6
) to get that
(u,v)
is a weak bounded solution to (
1
). Furthermore, thanks to the boundedness of

and the local Lipschitz continuity of the nonlinearities, the uniqueness follows.
∎

2.2
Convergence of the sequence of solution

The main result of this part is the following theorem.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
(u_{\epsilon},v_{\epsilon})
be the weak solution of system (
1
) with parameter
\epsilon=1/k
and non-negative initial value
(u_{0},v_{0})

\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)\times L^{\infty}(\Gamma)

, the sequence

, where

).

The proof of this theorem uses some intermediate lemmas, which we show in the subsequent part.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
(u_{\epsilon},v_{\epsilon})
be the weak solution of the system (
1
), correspond to parameter
\epsilon
and the non-negative initial condition
(u_{0},v_{0})\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)\times L^{\infty}(\Gamma)
. We have the upper bound

\|u_{\epsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{\infty}(\Omega))}+\|v_{\epsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{\infty}(\Gamma))}\leq M,

where
M
is a constant that does not depend on
\epsilon
.

Proof.

The proof is similar to the Lemma
2.1
, with the remark that the constant
M
in therein is also independent of
\epsilon>0
. Therefore, we only give a sketch here. By considering energy functions, for
p\in\mathbb{N}\backslash\{0\}
,

E_{p}[u_{\epsilon},v_{\epsilon}](t):=\frac{1}{p\alpha^{2}+\alpha}\int_{\Omega}u_{\epsilon}^{p\alpha+1}dx+\frac{1}{p\beta^{2}+\beta}\int_{\Gamma}v_{\epsilon}^{p\beta+1}dS,

(19)

we can use the weak formulation of
(u_{\epsilon},v_{\epsilon})
to obtain, a.e.

,

E_{p}(t)\leq E_{p}(0).

(20)

This implies

\displaystyle\frac{1}{p\alpha^{2}+\alpha}\int_{\Omega}u_{\epsilon}^{p\alpha+1}(t)dx+

\displaystyle\frac{1}{p\beta^{2}+\beta}\int_{\Gamma}v_{\epsilon}^{p\beta+1}(t)dS

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{p\alpha^{2}+\alpha}\int_{\Omega}u_{0}^{p\alpha+1}dx+\frac{1}{p\beta^{2}+\beta}\int_{\Gamma}v_{0}^{p\beta+1}dS.

(21)

Taking the
p
-root and let

, we obtain

\|u_{\epsilon}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+\|v_{\epsilon}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma)}\leq C

for a constant
C
indepedent of
\epsilon>0
.
∎

We also need some estimates of the gradient, which we utilize the entropic structure.

Lemma 2.7
.

We have the estimate:

\|\nabla u_{\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}(0,T,L^{2}(\Omega))}+\|\nabla_{\Gamma}v_{\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}(0,T,L^{2}(\Gamma))}\leq C

with a constant
C
does not depend on
\epsilon
.

Proof.

To prove this lemma, we introduce a modified logarithm entropy function, which is similar to Lemma
2.4

E_{\lambda}[u_{\epsilon},v_{\epsilon}](t)=\int_{\Omega}(u_{\epsilon}+\lambda)(\log(u_{\epsilon}+\lambda)-1)dx+\int_{\Gamma}(v_{\epsilon}+\lambda)(\log(v_{\epsilon}+\lambda)-1)dS.

Direct computations give us the identity

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\left(d_{u}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|\nabla u_{\epsilon}|^{2}}{u_{\epsilon}+\lambda}dx+d_{v}\int_{\Gamma}\frac{|\nabla_{\Gamma}v_{\epsilon}|^{2}}{v_{\epsilon}+\lambda}dS\right)dt=E_{\lambda}(0)-E_{\lambda}(T)+\frac{1}{\epsilon}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Gamma}(v_{\epsilon}^{\beta}-u_{\epsilon}^{\alpha})\log\frac{(u_{\epsilon}+\lambda)^{\alpha}}{(v_{\epsilon}+\lambda)^{\beta}}dS.

Using the fact that
E_{\lambda}(0)
and
E_{\lambda}(T)
are bounded, we obtain the estimation

d_{u}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|\nabla u_{\epsilon}|^{2}}{u_{\epsilon}+\lambda}dxdt+d_{v}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Gamma}\frac{|\nabla_{\Gamma}v_{\epsilon}|^{2}}{v_{\epsilon}+\lambda}dSdt\leq C+\frac{1}{\epsilon}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Gamma}(v_{\epsilon}^{\beta}-u_{\epsilon}^{\alpha})\log\frac{(u_{\epsilon}+\lambda)^{\alpha}}{(v_{\epsilon}+\lambda)^{\beta}}dS.

Now, we pass the limit
\lambda\to 0^{+}
for the estimation (
2.2
), similar to Lemma
2.4
.

d_{u}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|\nabla u_{\epsilon}|^{2}}{u_{\epsilon}}dxdt+d_{v}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Gamma}\frac{|\nabla_{\Gamma}v_{\epsilon}|^{2}}{v_{\epsilon}}dSdt\leq C,

where
C
is a constant that does not depend on
\epsilon
.
Finally, from the Lemma
2.6
, we can show that the gradient is also bounded uniformly:

\displaystyle\|\nabla u_{\epsilon}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))}\leq\int_{0}^{T}\|u_{\epsilon}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|\nabla u_{\epsilon}|^{2}}{u_{\epsilon}}dxdt\leq C

with a constant
C
is independent to
\epsilon
. By a similar argument, we obtain the conclusion for the gradient of
v_{\epsilon}

\|\nabla_{\Gamma}v_{\epsilon}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Gamma)}\leq C.

∎

In order to the apply the Aubin-Lions lemma, we need uniform bound of the time derivative. However, we see that testing each equation of
u_{\epsilon}
and
v_{\epsilon}
will not help due to the singular reaction term as
\epsilon\to 0
. In order to overcome this issue, we look at the time derivative of
(u_{\epsilon},v_{\epsilon})
in a suitable product space, which allows us to eliminate the singular reaction term when testing the system with test functions.

Lemma 2.8
.

There exists a functional space
Z
, which compactly embedded in
L^{2}(\Omega)\times L^{2}(\Gamma)
, such that we have
\partial_{t}(u_{\epsilon},v_{\epsilon})
is bounded (uniformly with respect to
\epsilon
) in the space
L^{2}(0,T;Z^{*})
.

Proof.

We can rewrite the weak formulation as

\langle\partial_{t}u_{\epsilon},\varphi\rangle_{\Omega}+\int_{\Omega}d_{u}\nabla u_{\epsilon}\cdot\nabla\varphi dx=-\frac{\alpha}{\epsilon}\int_{\Gamma}(u_{\epsilon}^{\alpha}-v_{\epsilon}^{\beta})\varphi dS,

(22)

\langle\partial_{t}v_{\epsilon},\psi\rangle_{\Gamma}+\int_{\Gamma}d_{v}\nabla_{\Gamma}v_{\epsilon}\cdot\nabla_{\Gamma}\psi dS=\frac{\beta}{\epsilon}\int_{\Gamma}(u_{\epsilon}^{\alpha}-v_{\epsilon}^{\beta})\psi dS.

(23)

By adding the right hand sides of these equations and choose an appropriate test functions, we can remove the paramter
\epsilon
. Indeed, we define a new functional space for the test function (for more detail about this functional space, see, e.g.
[
30
]
).

Z:=\{(\phi,\phi|_{\Gamma}):\phi\in H^{1}(\Omega)\text{ and }\phi|_{\Gamma}\in H^{1}(\Gamma)\},

(24)

and inherited the norm and inner product of
H^{1}(\Omega)\times H^{1}(\Gamma)

\|(\phi,\phi|_{\Gamma})\|_{Z}:=\|\phi\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}+\|\phi|_{\Gamma}\|_{H^{1}(\Gamma)},

\Bigl((\phi_{1},\phi_{1}|_{\Gamma}),(\phi_{2},\phi_{2}|_{\Gamma})\Bigr)_{Z}:=(\phi_{1},\phi_{2})_{H^{1}(\Omega)}+(\phi_{1}|_{\Gamma},\phi_{2}|_{\Gamma})_{H^{1}(\Gamma)}.

To show that the time derivative of
(u_{\epsilon},v_{\epsilon})
is bounded in
L^{2}(0,T;Z^{*})
, we take
(\varphi,\varphi|_{\Gamma})\in Z
arbitrary and multiply (
22
) by
\beta\varphi
, (
23
) by
\alpha\varphi
and take the sum of these and take the integration from
0
to
T
, we have:

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\bigg(\int_{\Omega}\alpha\langle\partial_{t}u_{\epsilon},\varphi\rangle_{\Omega}dx

\displaystyle+\int_{\Gamma}\beta\langle\partial_{t}v_{\epsilon},\varphi|_{\Gamma}\rangle_{\Gamma}dS\bigg)dt

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{T}

\displaystyle\bigg(\int_{\Omega}\beta d_{u}\nabla u_{\epsilon}\cdot\nabla\varphi dx+\int_{\Gamma}\alpha d_{v}\nabla_{\Gamma}v_{\epsilon}\cdot\nabla_{\Gamma}\varphi|_{\Gamma}dS\bigg)dt.

(25)

Setting
V:=L^{2}(0,T;Z)
, the dual of
V
is
V^{*}
, which can be identified by
L^{2}(0,T;Z^{*})
. So, the left hand side of equation (
2.2
) can be rewritten as the pairing between
V^{*}
and
V
:

\int_{0}^{T}\bigg(\int_{\Omega}\alpha\langle\partial_{t}u_{\epsilon},\varphi\rangle_{\Omega}dx+\int_{\Gamma}\beta\langle\partial_{t}v_{\epsilon},\varphi|_{\Gamma}\rangle_{\Gamma}dS\bigg)dt=\langle\partial_{t}(\alpha u_{\epsilon},\beta v_{\epsilon});(\varphi,\varphi|_{\Gamma})\rangle_{V^{*}\times V}.

(26)

Moreover, the right hand side of (
2.2
) can be bounded in the norm of
V
due to the Lemma
2.7
, which give the estimate:

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\beta d_{u}\nabla u_{\epsilon}\cdot\nabla\varphi dx+\int_{\Gamma}\alpha d_{v}\nabla_{\Gamma}v_{\epsilon}\cdot\nabla_{\Gamma}(\varphi|_{\Gamma})dSdt

\displaystyle\leq\,\beta d_{u}\|\nabla u_{\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))}\|\nabla\varphi\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))}+\alpha d_{v}\|\nabla_{\Gamma}v_{\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Gamma))}\|\nabla_{\Gamma}(\varphi|_{\Gamma})\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Gamma))}

\displaystyle\leq\,C(\|\varphi\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega))}+\|\varphi|_{\Gamma}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\Gamma))})

\displaystyle\leq C\,\|(\varphi,\varphi|_{\Gamma})\|_{V},

where the first inequality given by Holder inequality. In the estimate,
C
is a constant that does not depend on
\epsilon
. Combine with (
2.2
) and (
26
), we have:

\langle\partial_{t}(\alpha u_{\epsilon},\beta v_{\epsilon});(\varphi,\varphi|_{\Gamma})\rangle_{V^{*}\times V}\leq C\|(\varphi,\varphi|_{\Gamma})\|_{V}

(27)

for all
(\varphi,\varphi|_{\Gamma})\in V
, or

\|\partial_{t}(u_{\epsilon},v_{\epsilon})\|_{V^{*}}\leq C.

Hence, we have
Z
is the required functional space.
∎

Before proving the main theorem, we present a definition for weak solutions to the limit system (
2
).

Definition 2.2
.

We call
w
a weak solution of problem (
2
) if it satisfies the regular condition

w\in C([0,T];L^{2}(\Omega))\text{ and }w\in L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{\infty}(\Omega))\cap L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega)),

w|_{\Gamma}\in C([0,T];L^{2}(\Gamma))\text{ and }w|_{\Gamma}^{\alpha/\beta}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{\infty}(\Gamma))\cap L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\Gamma)),

and it satisfies the following weak formulation

\displaystyle\beta\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}-w\varphi_{t}+\nabla w\cdot\nabla\varphi dxdt+\alpha\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Gamma}-(w|_{\Gamma})^{\alpha/\beta}(\varphi|_{\Gamma})_{t}+\nabla_{\Gamma}(w|_{\Gamma}^{\alpha/\beta})\cdot\nabla_{\Gamma}(\varphi|_{\Gamma})dSdt

(28)

\displaystyle=\beta\int_{\Omega}u_{0}\varphi(0)dx+\alpha\int_{\Gamma}v_{0}\varphi|_{\Gamma}(0)dS

where the test function
(\varphi,\varphi|_{\Gamma})\in C^{1}([0,T],H^{1}(\Omega)\times H^{1}(\Gamma))
and
\varphi(T)=0
(which means
\varphi|_{\Gamma}(T)=0
, also).

Proof of Theorem
2.2
.

First, we proof the identity
w^{\alpha}|_{\Gamma}(t)=z^{\beta}(t)
by starting again with the entropy function in Lemma
2.6
, choose
p=1
and take the integration from
0
to
T
, the uniform boundedness of
(u_{\epsilon},v_{\epsilon})
implies

\frac{1}{\epsilon}\|u_{\epsilon}^{\alpha}-v_{\epsilon}^{\beta}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Gamma\times(0,T))}\leq C.

where
C
is independent of
\epsilon
, which means
u_{\epsilon}^{\alpha}-v_{\epsilon}^{\beta}\to 0
strongly in
L^{2}(\Gamma\times(0,T))
. Then, we have the weak convergence
u_{\epsilon}^{\alpha}-v_{\epsilon}^{\beta}\to 0
in the same functional space, or in other words

\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Gamma}(u_{\epsilon}^{\alpha}-v_{\epsilon}^{\beta})\eta dS=0

for all
\eta\in L^{2}(\Gamma\times(0,T))
.
On the other hand, we have the weak convergence, up to a subsequence,

\begin{cases}u_{\epsilon}^{\alpha}\rightharpoonup w^{\alpha}\in L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega)),\\
v_{\epsilon}^{\beta}\rightharpoonup z^{\beta}\in L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\Gamma)).\\
\end{cases}

Due to Trace Theorem, it implies

\begin{cases}u_{\epsilon_{i}}^{\alpha}\rightharpoonup w^{\alpha}\in L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Gamma)),\\
v_{\epsilon_{i}}^{\beta}\rightharpoonup z^{\beta}\in L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Gamma)).\\
\end{cases}

So, for any
\eta\in L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Gamma))

\begin{cases}\lim_{i\to\infty}(u_{\epsilon_{i}}^{\alpha}-w^{\alpha},\eta)_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Gamma))}=0,\\
\lim_{i\to\infty}(v_{\epsilon_{i}}^{\beta}-z^{\beta},\eta)_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Gamma))}=0.\\
\end{cases}

We can then deduce

(w^{\alpha}-z^{\beta},\eta)_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Gamma))}=0

for all
\eta\in L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Gamma))
, which give the conclusion

w^{\alpha}=z^{\beta}

for a.e on
\Gamma\times(0,T)
. So, we can rewrite the limit
(w,z)
by
(w,(w|_{\Gamma})^{\alpha/\beta})
. Next, we will complete the proof of theorem by showing that
w
satisfies the weak formulation (
28
). First, we recall the weak formulation in the Introduction part, and choose the test function
(\varphi,\varphi|_{\Gamma})\in C([0,T];Z)

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}(-u_{\epsilon}\varphi_{t}+d_{u}\nabla u_{\epsilon}\cdot\nabla\varphi)dxdt=\int_{\Omega}u_{0}\varphi(0)dx-\frac{\alpha}{\epsilon}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Gamma}(u_{\epsilon}^{\alpha}-v_{\epsilon}^{\beta})\varphi dSdt

(29)

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Gamma}(-v_{\epsilon}(\varphi|_{\Gamma})_{t}+d_{v}\nabla_{\Gamma}v_{\epsilon}\cdot\nabla_{\Gamma}(\varphi|_{\Gamma}))dSdt=\int_{\Gamma}v_{0}\varphi|_{\Gamma}(0)dS+\frac{\beta}{\epsilon}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Gamma}(u_{\epsilon}^{\alpha}-v_{\epsilon}^{\beta})\varphi|_{\Gamma}dSdt

(30)

Then, multiply (
29
) by
\beta
, (
30
) by
\alpha
, and take the sum, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}-u_{\epsilon}\varphi_{t}+d_{u}\nabla u_{\epsilon}\cdot\nabla\varphi dxdt

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Gamma}-v_{\epsilon}(\varphi|_{\Gamma})_{t}+d_{v}\nabla_{\Gamma}v_{\epsilon}\cdot\nabla_{\Gamma}(\varphi|_{\Gamma})dSdt

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}u_{0}\varphi(0)dx+\int_{\Gamma}v_{0}\varphi|_{\Gamma}(0)dS.

(31)

Letting
\epsilon\to 0
gives the weak formulation (
28
), which concludes this proof.
∎

3
Convergence rate

In this section, we will investigate the convergence rate when
\alpha=\beta
, using the ideas from
[
18
]
. For the convergence rate, we assume more regularity of the initial condition

u_{0}\in H^{1}(\Omega)\,,v_{0}\in H^{1}(\Gamma)\text{ and }v_{0}=u_{0}|_{\Gamma}.

This kind of condition is called compatibility condition for the heat equation with Wentzell boundary condition

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}w-d_{u}\lambda w=0,&x\in\Omega,t>0\\
d_{u}\nabla w\cdot{\mathbf{n}}=-\partial_{t}w+d_{v}\lambda_{\Gamma}w,&x\in\Gamma,t>0\\
w(x,0)=u_{0}(x),&x\in\Omega,t>0\\
w|_{\Gamma}(x,0)=v_{0}(x),&x\in\Gamma,t>0.\\
\end{cases}

(32)

Remark that the solution of (
32
)
w
satisfies the following weak formulation

\displaystyle\langle w_{t},\varphi\rangle_{\Omega}+\langle(w|_{\Gamma})_{t},\varphi|_{\Gamma}\rangle_{\Gamma}+d_{u}\int_{\Omega}\nabla w\cdot\nabla\varphi dx+d_{v}\int_{\Gamma}\nabla_{\Gamma}(w|_{\Gamma})\cdot\nabla_{\Gamma}(\varphi|_{\Gamma})dS=0

(33)

for all
\varphi\in H^{1}(\Omega)
and
\varphi|_{\Gamma}\in H^{1}(\Gamma)
and a.e.
t\in(0,T)
.
The existence of a unique global solution has been obtained in
[
30
]
. The solution of this problem enjoys the further regularity in the following lemma, which is needed for our analysis.

Lemma 3.1
.

The solution of the system (
32
)
w
has the following property

\partial_{t}(w,w_{|\Gamma})\in L^{2}(0,T,L^{2}(\Omega)\times L^{2}(\Gamma)).

Proof.

We recall the variational formulation of (
32
), which is the case
\alpha=\beta
of the formula (
28
)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}-w\varphi_{t}+d_{u}\nabla w\cdot\nabla\varphi dxdt

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Gamma}-w_{|\Gamma}(\varphi|_{\Gamma})_{t}+d_{v}\nabla_{\Gamma}w_{|\Gamma}\cdot\nabla_{\Gamma}(\varphi|_{\Gamma})dSdt

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}u_{0}\varphi(0)dx+\int_{\Gamma}v_{0}\varphi|_{\Gamma}(0)dS,

which can be rewritten to

\frac{d}{dt}\langle(w,w_{|\Gamma}),(\varphi,\varphi_{|\Gamma})\rangle+a(w,w_{|\Gamma},\varphi,\varphi_{|\Gamma})=0.

(34)

In the second equation, we define the bilinear form

a(w,w_{|\Gamma},\varphi,\varphi_{|\Gamma}):=\int_{\Omega}d_{u}\nabla w\cdot\nabla\varphi dx+\int_{\Gamma}d_{v}\nabla_{\Gamma}w_{|\Gamma}\cdot\nabla_{\Gamma}(\varphi|_{\Gamma})dS

and the dual is in
L^{2}
(which actually is restricted on
Z^{*}\times Z
). We already have the unique existence of
(w,w_{|\Gamma})
in the functional space

(w,w_{|\Gamma})\in L^{2}(0,T,Z)\cap C([0,T];L^{2}(\Omega)\times L^{2}(\Gamma))\quad\partial_{t}(w,w_{|\Gamma})\in L^{2}(0,T,Z^{*})

To prove the regularity for
\partial_{t}(w,w_{|\Gamma})
, we use Garlekin approximation (see
[
12
]
[Theorem 7.1.5]). Indeed, we denote by
W_{m}=(w_{m},(w_{m})_{|\Gamma})
the projection of
(w,w_{|\Gamma})
from
Z
on
Z_{m}
, the
m
-dimesional subspace.

Recall that the Garlekin’s approximation use the smooth function, therefor we have
W_{m}\in C^{1}([0,T];Z_{m})
, or
\partial_{t}w_{m}(t)\in Z_{m}\subset Z
for
t\in[0,T]
. Therefor, choose
W_{m}
as the test function, we get

\|\partial_{t}W_{m}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)\times L^{2}(\Gamma)}^{2}+a(W_{m},\partial_{t}W_{m})=0.

Using the formulation that
\frac{d}{dt}a(W_{m},W_{m})=2a(W_{m},\partial_{t}W_{m})
, we get

2\|\partial_{t}W_{m}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)\times L^{2}(\Gamma)}^{2}+\frac{d}{dt}a(W_{m},W_{m})=0

Then, integration in term of time, we have the uniform estimation in term of
m

\|\partial_{t}W_{m}\|_{L^{2}(0,T,L^{2}(\Omega)\times L^{2}(\Gamma))}\leq C.

Then, by passing the limit
m\to\infty
, we have the conclusion

\|\partial_{t}(w,w_{|\Gamma})\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega)\times L^{2}(\Gamma))}\leq C.

∎

On the other hand, when
\alpha=\beta
,the original system has the form

\begin{cases}u_{t}-d_{u}\Delta u=0,&x\in\Omega,t>0,\\
d_{u}\nabla u\cdot{\mathbf{n}}=-\frac{\alpha}{\epsilon}(u^{\alpha}-v^{\alpha}),&x\in\Gamma,t>0,\\
\partial_{t}v-d_{v}\Delta_{\Gamma}v=\frac{\alpha}{\epsilon}(u^{\alpha}-v^{\alpha}),&x\in\Gamma,t>0,\\
u(x,0)=u_{0}(x)\geq 0,&x\in\Omega,\\
v(x,0)=v_{0}(x)=(u_{0})_{|\Gamma}(x)\geq 0,&x\in\Gamma,\end{cases}

(35)

and we use the following weak formulation:

\langle\partial_{t}u_{\epsilon},\varphi\rangle_{\Omega}+\int_{\Omega}d_{u}\nabla u_{\epsilon}\cdot\nabla\varphi dx=-\frac{\alpha}{\epsilon}\int_{\Gamma}(u_{\epsilon}^{\alpha}-v_{\epsilon}^{\alpha})\varphi dS,

(36)

\langle\partial_{t}v_{\epsilon},\psi\rangle_{\Gamma}+\int_{\Gamma}d_{v}\nabla_{\Gamma}v_{\epsilon}\cdot\nabla_{\Gamma}\psi dS=\frac{\alpha}{\epsilon}\int_{\Gamma}(u_{\epsilon}^{\alpha}-v_{\epsilon}^{\alpha})\psi dS,

(37)

for a.e.
t\in[0,T]
and for all
(\varphi,\psi)\in H^{1}(\Omega)\times H^{1}(\Gamma)
.

We are interested in the convergence rate for the system (
35
) converges to equation (
32
) as
\epsilon\to 0
. First, fix a small
\epsilon
, we set

\displaystyle U(x,t):=u_{\epsilon}(x,t)-w(x,t)\quad\text{on }\Omega\times[0,T],

\displaystyle V(x,t):=v_{\epsilon}(x,t)-w(x,t)\quad\text{on }\Gamma\times[0,T].

Remark that we have:
U-V=u-v
a.e. on
\Gamma\times(0,T)
.
We have the time derivative

\frac{d}{dt}\|U(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=\langle\partial_{t}u_{\epsilon},u_{\epsilon}-w\rangle_{\Omega}-\langle\partial_{t}w,u_{\epsilon}-w\rangle_{\Omega}

for a.e.
t\in(0,T)

Similarly, we have

\frac{d}{dt}\|V(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}=\langle\partial_{t}v_{\epsilon},v_{\epsilon}-w\rangle_{\Gamma}-\langle\partial_{t}w,v_{\epsilon}-w\rangle_{\Gamma}.

Combine these, we get

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}(\|U(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|V(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Gamma)})=

\displaystyle\langle\partial_{t}u_{\epsilon},u_{\epsilon}-w\rangle_{\Omega}-\langle\partial_{t}w,u_{\epsilon}-w\rangle_{\Omega}+\langle\partial_{t}v_{\epsilon},v_{\epsilon}-w\rangle_{\Gamma}-\langle\partial_{t}w,v_{\epsilon}-w\rangle_{\Gamma}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\partial_{t}u_{\epsilon},u_{\epsilon}-w\rangle_{\Omega}+\langle\partial_{t}v_{\epsilon},v_{\epsilon}-w\rangle_{\Gamma}

\displaystyle-\left(\langle\partial_{t}w,u_{\epsilon}-w\rangle_{\Omega}+\langle\partial_{t}w,u_{\epsilon}-w\rangle_{\Gamma}\right)+\langle\partial_{t}w,u_{\epsilon}-v_{\epsilon}\rangle_{\Gamma}.

Then, choosing the appropriate test function for weak formulation (
33
), (
36
) and (
37
), we obtain

\displaystyle\langle\partial_{t}u_{\epsilon},u_{\epsilon}-w\rangle_{\Omega}=-d_{u}\int_{\Omega}\nabla u_{\epsilon}\cdot\nabla(u_{\epsilon}-w)dx-\frac{\alpha}{\epsilon}\int_{\Gamma}(u_{\epsilon}^{\alpha}-v_{\epsilon}^{\alpha})(u_{\epsilon}-w)dS,

\displaystyle\langle\partial_{t}v_{\epsilon},v_{\epsilon}-w\rangle_{\Gamma}=-d_{v}\int_{\Gamma}\nabla_{\Gamma}v_{\epsilon}\cdot\nabla_{\Gamma}(v_{\epsilon}-w)dS+\frac{\alpha}{\epsilon}\int_{\Gamma}(u_{\epsilon}^{\alpha}-v_{\epsilon}^{\alpha})(v_{\epsilon}-w)dS,

and

\displaystyle\langle\partial_{t}w,u_{\epsilon}-w\rangle_{\Omega}+\langle\partial_{t}w,u_{\epsilon}-w\rangle_{\Gamma}=-d_{u}\int_{\Omega}\nabla w\cdot\nabla(u_{\epsilon}-w)dx-d_{v}\int_{\Gamma}\nabla_{\Gamma}(w|_{\Gamma})\cdot\nabla_{\Gamma}(u_{\epsilon}-w)dS.

Combine these, we have:

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}(\|U(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|V(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Gamma)})=

\displaystyle-d_{u}\|\nabla(u_{\epsilon}-w)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}-d_{v}\|\nabla(v_{\epsilon}-w)\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}^{2}

\displaystyle-\frac{\alpha}{\epsilon}\int_{\Gamma}(u_{\epsilon}^{\alpha}-v_{\epsilon}^{\alpha})(u_{\epsilon}-v_{\epsilon})dS+\langle\partial_{t}w,u_{\epsilon}-v_{\epsilon}\rangle_{\Gamma}.

The first and second terms of the right-hand side are non-positive, so we only need to work with the rest. For the integral term, we have:

\displaystyle-\frac{\alpha}{\epsilon}\int_{\Gamma}(u_{\epsilon}^{\alpha}-v_{\epsilon}^{\alpha})(u_{\epsilon}-v_{\epsilon})dS=

\displaystyle-\frac{\alpha}{\epsilon}\int_{\Gamma}(\sum_{i=0}^{\alpha-1}u_{\epsilon}^{i}v_{\epsilon}^{\alpha-1-i})(u_{\epsilon}-v_{\epsilon})(u_{\epsilon}-v_{\epsilon})dS

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{\alpha}{\epsilon}\int_{\Gamma}(\sum_{i=0}^{\alpha-1}u_{\epsilon}^{i}v_{\epsilon}^{\alpha-1-i})|U-V|^{2}dS.

We use the assumption that the initial condition is strictly positive. Furthermore, by testing (
1
) with
1/(u_{\epsilon})^{p\alpha}
and
1/(v_{\epsilon})^{p\beta}
and let
p\to\infty
, we can also show that
u_{\epsilon},v_{\epsilon}
have a positive lower bound
m>0
for a.e. that does not depend on
\epsilon
. So, we have the estimate:

\sum_{i=0}^{\alpha-1}u_{\epsilon}^{i}v_{\epsilon}^{\alpha-1-i}\geq v_{\epsilon}^{\alpha-1}\geq m^{\alpha-1}>0\quad a.e.\,(0,T)\times\Gamma,

which implies:

-\frac{\alpha}{\epsilon}\int_{\Gamma}(u_{\epsilon}^{\alpha}-v_{\epsilon}^{\alpha})(u_{\epsilon}-v_{\epsilon})dS\leq\frac{-c_{1}}{\epsilon}\|U-V\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Gamma)},

With
c_{1}
is a constant does not depend on
\epsilon
. On the other hand, the regularity of
w
allows us to rewrite

\langle\partial_{t}w,u_{\epsilon}-v_{\epsilon}\rangle_{\Gamma}=\left(\partial_{t}w,U-V\right)_{L^{2}(\Gamma)},

Moreover,
c_{2}
is a constant not dependent on
\epsilon
. The above inequality holds since
w_{t}
is bounded in
L^{2}(\Gamma)
,
u_{\epsilon}-v_{\epsilon}=U-V
on
\Gamma
and Holder’s inequality. Combine these above, we deduce

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}(\|U(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|V(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Gamma)})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{-c_{1}}{\epsilon}\|U-V\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}+\left(\partial_{t}w,U-V\right)_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}.

Fix
t_{0}\in[0,T]
, take the integration from
0
to
t_{0}
and using the fact that
U(0)=V(0)=0
, we obtain the estimation

\displaystyle\|U(t_{0})\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|V(t_{0})\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{t_{0}}\frac{-c_{1}}{\epsilon}\|U-V\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}dt+\left(\partial_{t}w,U-V\right)_{L^{2}(0,t_{0},L^{2}(\Gamma))}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{-c_{1}}{\epsilon}\|U-V\|^{2}_{L^{2}(0,t_{0},L^{2}(\Gamma))}+c_{2}\|U-V\|_{L^{2}(0,t_{0},L^{2}(\Gamma))}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{-c_{1}}{2\epsilon}\|U-V\|^{2}_{L^{2}(0,t_{0},L^{2}(\Gamma))}+c_{3}\epsilon\leq c_{3}\epsilon

Combine these all, we have the estimation
From these arguments, we have the following theorem:

Theorem 3.1

(Convergence rate)
.

Assume more that initial condition
(u_{0},v_{0})\in Z
is strictly positive, the solution of (
35
)
\{(u_{\epsilon},v_{\epsilon})\}
converges to the solution of
(
32
)
as
\epsilon\to 0
with convergence rate

\|u(t)-w(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|v(t)-w|_{\Gamma}(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}\leq c\sqrt{\epsilon}.

Remark 3.1
.

As mentioned above, we still require that the intial condition
(u_{0},v_{0})\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)\times L^{\infty}(\Gamma)
to have the convergence. For the special case
\alpha=\beta=1
, notice that the positivity of the initial condition is not necessary since the constant
c_{1}=1
intimately, then we can remove that condition. Moreover, the convergence is hold even the initial condition
(u_{0},v_{0})\in L^{2}(\Omega)\times L^{2}(\Gamma)
(see
[
16
]
).

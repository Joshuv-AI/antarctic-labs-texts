Auto-Stabilized Weak Galerkin Finite Element Methods for Stokes Equations on Non-Convex Polytopal Meshes Thanks: The research of Chunmei Wang was partially supported by National Science Foundation Grant DMS-2136380.
Chunmei Wang, Shangyou Zhang

Abstract
. This paper introduces an auto-stabilized weak Galerkin (WG) finite element method for solving Stokes equations without relying on traditional stabilizers. The proposed WG method accommodates both convex and non-convex polytopal elements in finite element partitions, leveraging bubble functions as a key analytical tool. The simplified WG method is symmetric and positive definite, and optimal-order error estimates are derived for WG approximations in both the discrete H 1 H^{1} norm and the L 2 L^{2} norm.

1.
Introduction

In this paper, we propose a novel weak Galerkin (WG) finite element method for the Stokes equations, designed to eliminate the need for traditional stabilizers while being applicable to non-convex polygonal or polyhedral meshes. Specifically, we consider the Stokes equations with Dirichlet and Neumann boundary conditions: find the unknown functions
{\mathbf{u}}
and
p
such that

(1.1)

\begin{split}-\Delta{\mathbf{u}}+\nabla p=&{\mathbf{f}},\qquad\qquad\text{in}\quad\Omega,\\
\nabla\cdot{\mathbf{u}}=&0,\qquad\qquad\text{in}\quad\Omega,\\
{\mathbf{u}}=&0,\qquad\qquad\text{on}\quad\partial\Omega,\end{split}

where
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
(
d=2,3
) is a polygonal or polyhedral domain.

The variational formulation of the Stokes problem (
1.1
) is as follows: Find
{\mathbf{u}}\in[H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)]^{d}
and
p\in L_{0}^{2}(\Omega)
such that the following equations hold:

(1.2)

\begin{split}(\nabla{\mathbf{u}},\nabla{\mathbf{v}})-(\nabla\cdot{\mathbf{v}},p)=&({\mathbf{f}},{\mathbf{v}}),\qquad\forall{\mathbf{v}}\in[H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)]^{d}\\
(\nabla\cdot{\mathbf{u}},q)=&0,\qquad\qquad\forall q\in L_{0}^{2}(\Omega),\end{split}

where
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)=\{w\in H^{1}(\Omega):w|_{\partial\Omega}=0\}
and
L_{0}^{2}(\Omega)=\{q\in L^{2}(\Omega);\int_{\Omega}qdx=0\}
.

Over the past several decades, finite element methods (FEMs) for solving (
1.1
) have been extensively developed based on the weak formulation (
1.2
). These methods employ finite-dimensional subspaces of
[H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)]^{d}\times L_{0}^{2}(\Omega)
, composed of piecewise polynomials, and require the construction of finite element spaces that satisfy the inf-sup condition established by Babuska
[
1
]
and Brezzi
[
2
]
. Although effective, the inf-sup condition imposes significant constraints on element design and mesh generation, reducing flexibility. For specific examples and further details on classical FEMs for the Stokes equations, readers are referred to
[
11
]
.
In addition to FEMs, other numerical approaches have been developed for solving the Stokes equations, including finite volume methods (FVMs)
[
4
]
and finite difference methods (FDMs)
[
3
,
23
,
24
]
.

The WG finite element method provides a novel framework for the numerical solution of partial differential equations (PDEs). This method approximates differential operators within a framework inspired by distribution theory, particularly for piecewise polynomial functions. Unlike traditional approaches, WG reduces regularity requirements on approximating functions by employing carefully designed stabilizers. Extensive research has demonstrated the versatility and effectiveness of WG methods across a broad spectrum of model PDEs
[

,

,

,

, establishing them as a powerful tool in scientific computing.

A distinctive feature of WG methods is their innovative use of weak derivatives and weak continuity to develop numerical schemes based on the weak forms of PDEs. This unique structure grants WG methods exceptional flexibility, enabling them to handle a wide variety of PDEs while maintaining stability and accuracy in numerical solutions. WG methods have been specifically proposed for solving the Stokes equations
[
51
]
, including stabilizer-free WG methods designed for convex polytopal meshes
[
10
,
52
]
.

A significant advancement within the WG framework is the Primal-Dual Weak Galerkin (PDWG) method, which effectively addresses challenges commonly faced by traditional numerical methods
[
19
,

,

,

. The PDWG method formulates numerical solutions as a constrained minimization problem, where the constraints reflect the weak formulation of PDEs through the use of weak derivatives. This approach leads to an Euler-Lagrange equation that incorporates both primal variables and dual variables (Lagrange multipliers), resulting in a robust and efficient numerical scheme.

This paper presents a simplified formulation of the WG finite element method that supports both convex and non-convex polygonal or polyhedral elements in finite element partitions. Recently, this auto-stabilized WG approach has been successfully applied to the Poisson equation
[
30
]
, the biharmonic equation
[
31
]
, and linear elasticity problems
[
48
]
. A key innovation of the proposed method is the elimination of stabilizers through the use of higher-degree polynomials for computing weak gradient and weak divergence operators. This approach preserves the size and global sparsity of the stiffness matrix while significantly reducing the programming complexity compared to traditional stabilizer-dependent methods. By leveraging bubble functions as a critical analytical tool, the method extends WG to non-convex polytopal elements in finite element partitions, marking a significant advancement over existing stabilizer-free WG methods
[
10
,
52
]
, which are limited to convex polytopal meshes.
Theoretical analysis establishes optimal error estimates for the WG approximations in both the discrete

H^{1}
norm and the

L^{2}
norm.

This paper is organized as follows. Section 2 provides a brief overview of the weak gradient and weak divergence operators, along with their discrete counterparts. In Section 3, we present an auto-stabilized WG scheme for Stokes equations that eliminates the need for stabilization terms and is applicable to non-convex polytopal meshes. Section 4 establishes the existence and uniqueness of the solution for the proposed scheme. In Section 5, the error equation for the WG scheme is derived. Section 6 focuses on obtaining error estimates for the numerical approximation in the discrete

H^{1}

norm, while Section 7 extends the analysis to derive error estimates in the

L^{2}
norm. Finally, Section 8 presents numerical tests to validate the theoretical results.

2.
Discrete Weak Gradient and Discrete Weak Divergence

This section revisits the definitions of weak gradient and weak divergence operators, as well as their discrete formulations, as introduced in
[
51
]
.

Consider a polytopal element
T
with boundary
\partial T
. A weak function on
T
is expressed as
{\mathbf{v}}=\{{\mathbf{v}}_{0},{\mathbf{v}}_{b}\}
, where
{\mathbf{v}}_{0}\in[L^{2}(T)]^{d}
represents the interior values of
{\mathbf{v}}
, and
{\mathbf{v}}_{b}\in[L^{2}(\partial T)]^{d}
represents its boundary values. Notably,

{\mathbf{v}}_{b}
is treated independently from the trace of
{\mathbf{v}}_{0}
on
\partial T
.

The set of all weak functions on
T
, denote by
W(T)
, is defined as

W(T)=\{{\mathbf{v}}=\{{\mathbf{v}}_{0},{\mathbf{v}}_{b}\}:{\mathbf{v}}_{0}\in[L^{2}(T)]^{d},{\mathbf{v}}_{b}\in[L^{2}(\partial T)]^{d}\}.

The weak gradient
\nabla_{w}{\mathbf{v}}
is a linear
operator mapping

W(T)
to the dual space of
[H^{1}(T)]^{d\times d}
. For any

{\mathbf{v}}\in W(T)
,
\nabla_{w}{\mathbf{v}}
is defined as a bounded linear functional in the dual space of
[H^{1}(T)]^{d\times d}
, given by:

(\nabla_{w}{\mathbf{v}},{\bm{\varphi}})_{T}=-({\mathbf{v}}_{0},\nabla\cdot{\bm{\varphi}})_{T}+\langle{\mathbf{v}}_{b},{\bm{\varphi}}\cdot{\mathbf{n}}\rangle_{\partial T},\quad\forall{\bm{\varphi}}\in[H^{1}(T)]^{d\times d},

where
{\mathbf{n}}
, with components
n_{i}(i=1,\cdots,d)
, is the unit outward normal direction to
\partial T
.

Similarly, the weak divergence
\nabla_{w}\cdot{\mathbf{v}}
is a linear
operator mapping

W(T)
to the dual space of
H^{1}(T)
. For

{\mathbf{v}}\in W(T)
,
\nabla_{w}\cdot{\mathbf{v}}
is defined as a bounded linear functional in the dual space of
H^{1}(T)
, given by:

(\nabla_{w}\cdot{\mathbf{v}},w)_{T}=-({\mathbf{v}}_{0},\nabla w)_{T}+\langle{\mathbf{v}}_{b}\cdot{\mathbf{n}},w\rangle_{\partial T},\quad\forall w\in H^{1}(T).

For any non-negative integer
r\geq 0
, let
P_{r}(T)
denote the space of
polynomials on
T
with total degree at most

r
. The discrete weak gradient

\nabla_{w,r,T}{\mathbf{v}}
for
{\mathbf{v}}\in W(T)
is the unique polynomial in
[P_{r}(T)]^{d\times d}
satisfying

(2.1)

(\nabla_{w,r,T}{\mathbf{v}},{\bm{\varphi}})_{T}=-({\mathbf{v}}_{0},\nabla\cdot{\bm{\varphi}})_{T}+\langle{\mathbf{v}}_{b},{\bm{\varphi}}\cdot{\mathbf{n}}\rangle_{\partial T},\quad\forall{\bm{\varphi}}\in[P_{r}(T)]^{d\times d}.

If
{\mathbf{v}}_{0}\in[H^{1}(T)]^{d}
is smooth, applying integration by parts to the first term on the right-hand side of (
2.1
) yields:

(2.2)

(\nabla_{w,r,T}{\mathbf{v}},{\bm{\varphi}})_{T}=(\nabla{\mathbf{v}}_{0},{\bm{\varphi}})_{T}+\langle{\mathbf{v}}_{b}-{\mathbf{v}}_{0},{\bm{\varphi}}\cdot{\mathbf{n}}\rangle_{\partial T},\quad\forall{\bm{\varphi}}\in[P_{r}(T)]^{d\times d}.

The discrete weak divergence

\nabla_{w,r,T}\cdot{\mathbf{v}}
for
{\mathbf{v}}\in W(T)
is the unique polynomial in
P_{r}(T)
satisfying

(2.3)

(\nabla_{w,r,T}\cdot{\mathbf{v}},w)_{T}=-({\mathbf{v}}_{0},\nabla w)_{T}+\langle{\mathbf{v}}_{b}\cdot{\mathbf{n}},w\rangle_{\partial T},\quad\forall w\in P_{r}(T).

If
{\mathbf{v}}_{0}\in[H^{1}(T)]^{d}
is smooth, integrating by parts in the first term on the right-hand side of (
2.1
) yields:

(2.4)

(\nabla_{w,r,T}\cdot{\mathbf{v}},w)_{T}=(\nabla\cdot{\mathbf{v}}_{0},w)_{T}+\langle({\mathbf{v}}_{b}-{\mathbf{v}}_{0})\cdot{\mathbf{n}},w\rangle_{\partial T},\quad\forall w\in P_{r}(T).

3.
Auto-Stabilized Weak Galerkin Algorithms

Let
{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
be a finite element partition of the domain

\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
into polytopal elements, satisfying the shape regularity condition outlined in
[
45
]
.
Let
{\mathcal{E}}_{h}
represent the set of all edges or faces in

{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
, and and let
{\mathcal{E}}_{h}^{0}={\mathcal{E}}_{h}\setminus\partial\Omega
denote the subset of interior edges or faces. For any element
T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
, let
h_{T}
be its diameter, and define the mesh size as

h=\max_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}h_{T}
.

Let
k
be an integer such that
k\geq 1
. For each element
T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
, the local weak finite element space is defined as:

V(k,T)=\{\{{\mathbf{v}}_{0},{\mathbf{v}}_{b}\}:{\mathbf{v}}_{0}\in[P_{k}(T)]^{d},{\mathbf{v}}_{b}\in[P_{k}(e)]^{d},e\subset\partial T\}.

The global weak finite element space
V_{h}
is obtained by assembling the local spaces
V(k,T)
for all
T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
and enforcing the continuity of
{\mathbf{v}}_{b}
along interior edges or faces
{\mathcal{E}}_{h}^{0}
:

V_{h}=\big\{\{{\mathbf{v}}_{0},{\mathbf{v}}_{b}\}:\ \{{\mathbf{v}}_{0},{\mathbf{v}}_{b}\}|_{T}\in V(k,T),\forall T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}\big\}.

The subspace of
V_{h}
consisting of functions with zero boundary values on
\partial\Omega
is defined as:

V_{h}^{0}=\{{\mathbf{v}}\in V_{h}:{\mathbf{v}}_{b}|_{\partial\Omega}=0\}.

For the pressure variable, the weak Galerkin finite element space is defined as:

W_{h}=\{q\in L_{0}^{2}(\Omega):q|_{T}\in P_{k-1}(T)\}.

For simplicity, the discrete weak gradient
\nabla_{w}{\mathbf{v}}
and the discrete weak divergence
\nabla_{w}\cdot{\mathbf{v}}
refer to the operators

\nabla_{w,r,T}{\mathbf{v}}
and
\nabla_{w,r,T}\cdot{\mathbf{v}}
, respectively, as defined in (
2.1
) and (
2.3
) for each
T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
:

(\nabla_{w}{\mathbf{v}})|_{T}=\nabla_{w,r,T}({\mathbf{v}}|_{T}),\qquad\forall T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h},

(\nabla_{w}\cdot{\mathbf{v}})|_{T}=\nabla_{w,r,T}\cdot({\mathbf{v}}|_{T}),\qquad\forall T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}.

On each element
T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
, let
Q_{0}
denote the
L^{2}
projection onto
P_{k}(T)
. On each edge or face
e\subset\partial T
, let
Q_{b}
denote the
L^{2}
projection onto
P_{k}(e)
. For any
{\mathbf{v}}\in[H^{1}(\Omega)]^{d}
, the
L^{2}
projection into the weak finite element space
V_{h}
is defined as:

(Q_{h}{\mathbf{v}})|_{T}:=\{Q_{0}({\mathbf{v}}|_{T}),Q_{b}({\mathbf{v}}|_{{\partial T}})\},\qquad\forall T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}.

The simplified WG numerical scheme, free from stabilization terms, for solving the Stokes equations (
1.1
) is given as follows:

Auto-Stabilized WG Algorithm 3.1
.

Find
{\mathbf{u}}_{h}=\{{\mathbf{u}}_{0},{\mathbf{u}}_{b}\}\in V_{h}^{0}
and
p_{h}\in W_{h}
such that

(3.1)

\begin{split}(\nabla_{w}{\mathbf{u}}_{h},\nabla_{w}{\mathbf{v}}_{h})-(\nabla_{w}\cdot{\mathbf{v}}_{h},p_{h})=&({\mathbf{f}},{\mathbf{v}}_{0}),\qquad\forall{\mathbf{v}}_{h}\in V_{h}^{0},\\
(\nabla_{w}\cdot{\mathbf{u}}_{h},q_{h})=&0,\qquad\qquad\forall q_{h}\in W_{h},\end{split}

where

(\cdot,\cdot)=\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}(\cdot,\cdot)_{T}.

4.
Solution Existence and Uniqueness

Recall that
{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
is a shape-regular finite element partition of the domain
\Omega
. For any
T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
and
\phi\in H^{1}(T)
, the following trace inequality holds
[
45
]
:

(4.1)

\|\phi\|^{2}_{\partial T}\leq C(h_{T}^{-1}\|\phi\|_{T}^{2}+h_{T}\|\nabla\phi\|_{T}^{2}).

If
\phi
is a polynomial on
T
, a simplified trace inequality applies
[
45
]
:

(4.2)

\|\phi\|^{2}_{\partial T}\leq Ch_{T}^{-1}\|\phi\|_{T}^{2}.

For any
{\mathbf{v}}=\{{\mathbf{v}}_{0},{\mathbf{v}}_{b}\}\in V_{h}
, define the norm:

(4.3)

{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}{\mathbf{v}}{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}=(\nabla_{w}{\mathbf{v}},\nabla_{w}{\mathbf{v}})^{\frac{1}{2}},

and the discrete
H^{1}
- semi-norm:

(4.4)

\|{\mathbf{v}}\|_{1,h}=\Big(\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\|\nabla{\mathbf{v}}_{0}\|_{T}^{2}+h_{T}^{-1}\|{\mathbf{v}}_{0}-{\mathbf{v}}_{b}\|_{\partial T}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Lemma 4.1
.

For
{\mathbf{v}}=\{{\mathbf{v}}_{0},{\mathbf{v}}_{b}\}\in V_{h}
, there exists a constant
C
such that

\|\nabla{\mathbf{v}}_{0}\|_{T}\leq C\|\nabla_{w}{\mathbf{v}}\|_{T}.

Proof.

Let
T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
be a polytopal element with
N
edges or faces, denoted
e_{1},\cdots,e_{N}
. Note that
T
is not required to be convex. For each edge or face
e_{i}
, define a linear equation
l_{i}(x)
such that
l_{i}(x)=0
on
e_{i}
as follows:

l_{i}(x)=\frac{1}{h_{T}}\overrightarrow{AX}\cdot{\mathbf{n}}_{i},

where
A
is a given point on
e_{i}
,
X
is any point on
e_{i}
,
{\mathbf{n}}_{i}
is the unit outward normal direction to
e_{i}
, and
h_{T}
is the size of the element
T
.

The bubble function
\Phi_{B}
of the element
T
is defined as

\Phi_{B}=l^{2}_{1}(x)l^{2}_{2}(x)\cdots l^{2}_{N}(x)\in P_{2N}(T).

It is straightforward to verify that
\Phi_{B}=0
on
\partial T
. The bubble function

\Phi_{B}
can be scaled such that
\Phi_{B}(M)=1
where
M
is the barycenter of the element
T
. Furthermore, there exists a sub-domain
\hat{T}\subset T
such that
\Phi_{B}\geq\rho_{0}
for some constant
\rho_{0}>0
.

Given
{\mathbf{v}}=\{{\mathbf{v}}_{0},{\mathbf{v}}_{b}\}\in V_{h}
, let
r=2N+k-1
and

{\bm{\varphi}}=\Phi_{B}\nabla{\mathbf{v}}_{0}\in[P_{r}(T)]^{d\times d}
. Substituting
{\bm{\varphi}}
into (
2.2
), we have

(4.5)

\begin{split}(\nabla_{w}{\mathbf{v}},\Phi_{B}\nabla{\mathbf{v}}_{0})_{T}&=(\nabla{\mathbf{v}}_{0},\Phi_{B}\nabla{\mathbf{v}}_{0})_{T}+\langle{\mathbf{v}}_{b}-{\mathbf{v}}_{0},\Phi_{B}\nabla{\mathbf{v}}_{0}\cdot{\mathbf{n}}\rangle_{\partial T}\\
&=(\nabla{\mathbf{v}}_{0},\Phi_{B}\nabla{\mathbf{v}}_{0})_{T},\end{split}

where the second equality follows from the fact that
\Phi_{B}=0
on
\partial T
.

From the domain inverse inequality
[
45
]
, there exists a constant
C
such that

(4.6)

(\nabla{\mathbf{v}}_{0},\Phi_{B}\nabla{\mathbf{v}}_{0})_{T}\geq C(\nabla{\mathbf{v}}_{0},\nabla{\mathbf{v}}_{0})_{T}.

Applying the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and combining (
4.5
) and (
4.6
), we have

(\nabla{\mathbf{v}}_{0},\nabla{\mathbf{v}}_{0})_{T}\leq C(\nabla_{w}{\mathbf{v}},\Phi_{B}\nabla{\mathbf{v}}_{0})_{T}\leq C\|\nabla_{w}{\mathbf{v}}\|_{T}\|\Phi_{B}\nabla{\mathbf{v}}_{0}\|_{T}\leq C\|\nabla_{w}{\mathbf{v}}\|_{T}\|\nabla{\mathbf{v}}_{0}\|_{T},

which gives

\|\nabla{\mathbf{v}}_{0}\|_{T}\leq C\|\nabla_{w}{\mathbf{v}}\|_{T}.

This completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

Remark 4.1
.

If the polytopal element
T
is convex,
the bubble function used in Lemma
4.1
can be simplified to

\Phi_{B}=l_{1}(x)l_{2}(x)\cdots l_{N}(x).

It can be verified that there exists a sub-domain
\hat{T}\subset T
, such that

\Phi_{B}\geq\rho_{0}
for some constant
\rho_{0}>0
, and
\Phi_{B}=0
on the boundary
\partial T
. Using this simplified construction, Lemma
4.1
can be proved in the same manner. In this case, we set
r=N+k-1
.

Recall that
T
is a
d
-dimensional polytopal element and
e_{i}
is a
d-1
-dimensional edge or face of
T
.
We define an edge/face-based bubble function as

\varphi_{e_{i}}=\Pi_{k=1,\cdots,N,k\neq i}l_{k}^{2}(x).

It can be verified that (1)
\varphi_{e_{i}}=0
on the edge/face
e_{k}
for
k\neq i
; (2) there exists a subdomain
\widehat{e_{i}}\subset e_{i}
such that
\varphi_{e_{i}}\geq\rho_{1}
for some constant
\rho_{1}>0
.

Lemma 4.2
.

For
{\mathbf{v}}=\{{\mathbf{v}}_{0},{\mathbf{v}}_{b}\}\in V_{h}
, let
{\bm{\varphi}}=({\mathbf{v}}_{b}-{\mathbf{v}}_{0})^{T}{\mathbf{n}}\varphi_{e_{i}}
, where
{\mathbf{n}}
is the unit outward normal direction to the edge/face
e_{i}
. Then, the following inequality holds:

(4.7)

\|{\bm{\varphi}}\|_{T}^{2}\leq Ch_{T}\int_{e_{i}}|{\mathbf{v}}_{b}-{\mathbf{v}}_{0}|^{2}ds.

Proof.

We begin by extending
{\mathbf{v}}_{b}
, initially defined on the
(d-1)
-dimensional edge/face
e_{i}
, to the entire
d
-dimensional polytopal element
T
. This extension is defined as:

{\mathbf{v}}_{b}(X)={\mathbf{v}}_{b}(Proj_{e_{i}}(X)),

where
X
is any point in
T
,
Proj_{e_{i}}(X)
is the orthogonal projection of
X
onto the hyperplane
H\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
containing
e_{i}
. For points where
Proj_{e_{i}}(X)\notin e_{i}
,
{\mathbf{v}}_{b}(Proj_{e_{i}}(X))
is defined as the extension of
{\mathbf{v}}_{b}
from
e_{i}
to
H
.

We claim that
{\mathbf{v}}_{b}
remains a polynomial on
T
after the extension.

Let
H
be the hyperplane containing
e_{i}
, defined by
d-1
linearly independent vectors
\bm{\eta}_{1},\cdots,\bm{\eta}_{d-1}
originating from a point
A
on
e_{i}
. Any point
P
on
H
can be parametrized as

P(t_{1},\cdots,t_{d-1})=A+t_{1}\bm{\eta}_{1}+\cdots+t_{d-1}\bm{\eta}_{d-1},

where
t_{1},\cdots,t_{d-1}
are parameters.

Since
{\mathbf{v}}_{b}(P(t_{1},\cdots,t_{d-1}))
is a polynomial of degree
q
defined on
e_{i}
, each component
({\mathbf{v}}_{b})_{k}
of
{\mathbf{v}}_{b}=(({\mathbf{v}}_{b})_{1},\cdots,({\mathbf{v}}_{b})_{d})
can be expressed as:

({\mathbf{v}}_{b})_{k}(P(t_{1},\cdots,t_{d-1}))=\sum_{|\alpha|\leq q}c_{\alpha}\textbf{t}^{\alpha},\qquad\forall k=1,\cdots,d,

where
\textbf{t}^{\alpha}=t_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots t_{d-1}^{\alpha_{d-1}}
and
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\cdots,\alpha_{d-1})
is a multi-index.

For any point
X
in
T
, the projection of
X
onto the hyperplane
H\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, which contains the edge/face
e_{i}
, is the point on
H
that minimizes the distance to
X
. Mathematically, this projection
Proj_{e_{i}}(X)
is an affine transformation expressed as

Proj_{e_{i}}(X)=A+\sum_{i=1}^{d-1}t_{i}(X)\bm{\eta}_{i},

where
t_{i}(X)
are the projection coefficients, and
A
is the origin point on
e_{i}
and
\bm{\eta}_{i}
are linearly independent vectors defining
H
. The coefficients
t_{i}(X)
are determined by solving the orthogonality condition:

(X-Proj_{e_{i}}(X))\cdot\bm{\eta}_{j}=0,\forall j=1,\cdots,d-1.

This results in a system of linear equations in
t_{1}(X)
,
\cdots
,
t_{d-1}(X)
, which can be solved to yield:

t_{i}(X)=\text{linear function of}\ X.

Hence, the projection
Proj_{e_{i}}(X)
is an affine linear function of
X
.

We extend the polynomial
{\mathbf{v}}_{b}
, originally defined on the edge/face
e_{i}
, to the entire element
T
by defining

({\mathbf{v}}_{b})_{k}(X)=({\mathbf{v}}_{b})_{k}(Proj_{e_{i}}(X))=\sum_{|\alpha|\leq q}c_{\alpha}\textbf{t}(X)^{\alpha},\qquad\forall k=1,\cdots,d

where
\textbf{t}(X)^{\alpha}=t_{1}(X)^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots t_{d-1}(X)^{\alpha_{d-1}}
and
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\cdots,\alpha_{d-1})
. Since
t_{i}(X)
are linear functions of
X
, each term
\textbf{t}(X)^{\alpha}
is a polynomial in
X=(x_{1},\cdots,x_{d})
.
Thus,
{\mathbf{v}}_{b}(X)
is a polynomial in the
d
-dimensional coordinates
X=(x_{1},\cdots,x_{d})
.

Similarly, let
{\mathbf{v}}_{trace}
denote the trace of
{\mathbf{v}}_{0}
on the edge/face
e_{i}
. We extend
{\mathbf{v}}_{trace}
to the entire element
T
using the formula:

{\mathbf{v}}_{trace}(X)={\mathbf{v}}_{trace}(Proj_{e_{i}}(X)),

where

) from (

\begin{split}&\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}(\nabla_{w}{\mathbf{u}},\nabla_{w}{\mathbf{v}}_{h})_{T}-(\nabla_{w}\cdot{\mathbf{v}}_{h},p)_{T}\\

Next, using (

This concludes the proof.

\begin{split}&\|{\bm{\zeta}}_{0}\|^{2}\\
=&\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}(\nabla_{w}{\mathbf{w}},\nabla_{w}({\mathbf{u}}-{\mathbf{u}}_{h}))_{T}-(\nabla_{w}{\mathbf{w}},\nabla_{w}({\mathbf{u}}-Q_{h}{\mathbf{u}}))_{T}\\
&-\langle{\bm{\zeta}}_{b}-{\bm{\zeta}}_{0},({\mathcal{Q}}_{h}-I)\nabla{\mathbf{w}}\cdot{\mathbf{n}}\rangle_{\partial T}-(\nabla_{w}\cdot({\mathbf{u}}-{\mathbf{u}}_{h}),{\mathcal{Q}}_{h}q)_{T}\\
&-(\nabla_{w}\cdot(Q_{h}{\mathbf{u}}-{\mathbf{u}}),{\mathcal{Q}}_{h}q)_{T}+\langle({\mathcal{Q}}_{h}-I)q,({\bm{\zeta}}_{b}-{\bm{\zeta}}_{0})\cdot{\mathbf{n}}\rangle_{\partial T}\\
=&\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}(\nabla_{w}Q_{h}{\mathbf{w}},\nabla_{w}({\mathbf{u}}-{\mathbf{u}}_{h}))_{T}+(\nabla_{w}({\mathbf{w}}-Q_{h}{\mathbf{w}}),\nabla_{w}({\mathbf{u}}-{\mathbf{u}}_{h}))_{T}\\
&-(\nabla_{w}{\mathbf{w}},\nabla_{w}({\mathbf{u}}-Q_{h}{\mathbf{u}}))_{T}-\langle{\bm{\zeta}}_{b}-{\bm{\zeta}}_{0},({\mathcal{Q}}_{h}-I)\nabla{\mathbf{w}}\cdot{\mathbf{n}}\rangle_{\partial T}\\
&-(\nabla_{w}\cdot({\mathbf{u}}-{\mathbf{u}}_{h}),{\mathcal{Q}}_{h}q)_{T}-(\nabla_{w}\cdot(Q_{h}{\mathbf{u}}-{\mathbf{u}}),{\mathcal{Q}}_{h}q)_{T}\\
&+\langle({\mathcal{Q}}_{h}-I)q,({\bm{\zeta}}_{b}-{\bm{\zeta}}_{0})\cdot{\mathbf{n}}\rangle_{\partial T}-(\nabla_{w}\cdot Q_{h}{\mathbf{w}},p-p_{h})_{T}\\
=&\ell_{1}({\mathbf{u}},Q_{h}{\mathbf{w}})+\ell_{2}(Q_{h}{\mathbf{w}},p)+\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}(\nabla_{w}({\mathbf{w}}-Q_{h}{\mathbf{w}}),\nabla_{w}({\mathbf{u}}-{\mathbf{u}}_{h}))_{T}\\
&-(\nabla_{w}{\mathbf{w}},\nabla_{w}({\mathbf{u}}-Q_{h}{\mathbf{u}}))_{T}-\langle{\bm{\zeta}}_{b}-{\bm{\zeta}}_{0},({\mathcal{Q}}_{h}-I)\nabla{\mathbf{w}}\cdot{\mathbf{n}}\rangle_{\partial T}\\
&-(\nabla_{w}\cdot({\mathbf{u}}-{\mathbf{u}}_{h}),q)_{T}-(\nabla_{w}\cdot(Q_{h}{\mathbf{u}}-{\mathbf{u}}),q)_{T}\\
&+\langle({\mathcal{Q}}_{h}-I)q,({\bm{\zeta}}_{b}-{\bm{\zeta}}_{0})\cdot{\mathbf{n}}\rangle_{\partial T}\\
=&\sum_{i=1}^{8}I_{i}.\end{split}

Each term
I_{i}
for
i=1,\cdots,8
is estimated as follows:

For
I_{1}
, using the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, the trace inequality (
4.1
), the estimate (
6.2
) with
m=1
, the estimate (
6.3
) with
n=k
, we have

\begin{split}&|\ell_{1}({\mathbf{u}},Q_{h}{\mathbf{w}})|\\
=&|\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\langle Q_{b}{\mathbf{w}}-Q_{0}{\mathbf{w}},({\mathcal{Q}}_{h}-I)\nabla{\mathbf{u}}\cdot{\mathbf{n}}\rangle_{\partial T}|\\
\leq&(\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\|Q_{b}{\mathbf{w}}-Q_{0}{\mathbf{w}}\|^{2}_{\partial T})^{\frac{1}{2}}(\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\|({\mathcal{Q}}_{h}-I)\nabla{\mathbf{u}}\cdot{\mathbf{n}}\|^{2}_{\partial T})\\
\leq&(\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}h_{T}^{-1}\|{\mathbf{w}}-Q_{0}{\mathbf{w}}\|^{2}_{T}+h_{T}\|{\mathbf{w}}-Q_{0}{\mathbf{w}}\|^{2}_{1,T})^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
&\cdot(\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}h_{T}^{-1}\|({\mathcal{Q}}_{h}-I)\nabla{\mathbf{u}}\cdot{\mathbf{n}}\|^{2}_{T}+h_{T}\|({\mathcal{Q}}_{h}-I)\nabla{\mathbf{u}}\cdot{\mathbf{n}}\|^{2}_{1,T})\\
\leq&Ch^{-1}h^{2}\|{\mathbf{w}}\|_{2}h^{k}\|{\mathbf{u}}\|_{k+1}.\end{split}

For
I_{2}
, applying the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, the trace inequality (
4.1
), the estimate (
6.1
) with
n=k
, the estimate (
6.2
) with
m=1
, we get

\begin{split}&|\ell_{2}(Q_{h}{\mathbf{w}},p)|\\
=&|\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}-\langle({\mathcal{Q}}_{h}-I)p,(Q_{b}{\mathbf{w}}-Q_{0}{\mathbf{w}})\cdot{\mathbf{n}}\rangle_{\partial T}|\\
\leq&(\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\|({\mathcal{Q}}_{h}-I)p\|_{\partial T}^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}(\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\|Q_{b}{\mathbf{w}}-Q_{0}{\mathbf{w}})\cdot{\mathbf{n}}\|_{\partial T}^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
\leq&(\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}h_{T}^{-1}\|({\mathcal{Q}}_{h}-I)p\|_{T}^{2}+h_{T}\|({\mathcal{Q}}_{h}-I)p\|_{1,T}^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
&\cdot(\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}h_{T}^{-1}\|({\mathbf{w}}-Q_{0}{\mathbf{w}})\cdot{\mathbf{n}}\|_{T}^{2}+h_{T}\|({\mathbf{w}}-Q_{0}{\mathbf{w}})\cdot{\mathbf{n}}\|_{1,T}^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
\leq&Ch^{-1}h^{2}\|{\mathbf{w}}\|_{2}h^{k}\|p\|_{k}.\end{split}

For
I_{3}
, using the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, the error estimate (
6.8
), the error estimate (
6.4
) with
k=1
, we derive

\begin{split}&|\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}(\nabla_{w}({\mathbf{w}}-Q_{h}{\mathbf{w}}),\nabla_{w}({\mathbf{u}}-{\mathbf{u}}_{h}))_{T}|\\
\leq&{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}{\mathbf{w}}-Q_{h}{\mathbf{w}}{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}{\mathbf{u}}-{\mathbf{u}}_{h}{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}\\
\leq&Ch\|{\mathbf{w}}\|_{2}h^{k}(\|{\mathbf{u}}\|_{k+1}+\|p\|_{k}).\end{split}

We now estimate
I_{4}
. Let

Q_{1}
denote the
L^{2}
projection onto
[P_{1}(T)]^{d\times d}
. For any
T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
, it follows from (
2.1
) that

(Q_{1}(\nabla_{w}{\mathbf{w}}),\nabla_{w}({\mathbf{u}}-Q_{h}{\mathbf{u}}))_{T}=-({\mathbf{u}}-Q_{0}{\mathbf{u}},\nabla\cdot(Q_{1}(\nabla_{w}{\mathbf{w}})))_{T}+\langle{\mathbf{u}}-Q_{b}{\mathbf{u}},Q_{1}(\nabla_{w}{\mathbf{w}})\cdot{\mathbf{n}}\rangle_{\partial T}=0.

Using this identity along with the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, (
5.1
), and (
6.4
), we have

\begin{split}&|\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}(\nabla_{w}{\mathbf{w}},\nabla_{w}({\mathbf{u}}-Q_{h}{\mathbf{u}}))_{T}|\\
=&|\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}(\nabla_{w}{\mathbf{w}}-Q_{1}(\nabla_{w}{\mathbf{w}}),\nabla_{w}({\mathbf{u}}-Q_{h}{\mathbf{u}}))_{T}|\\
=&|\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}({\mathcal{Q}}_{h}(\nabla{\mathbf{w}})-Q_{1}({\mathcal{Q}}_{h}(\nabla{\mathbf{w}})),\nabla_{w}({\mathbf{u}}-Q_{h}{\mathbf{u}}))_{T}|\\
\leq&(\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\|{\mathcal{Q}}_{h}(\nabla{\mathbf{w}})-Q_{1}({\mathcal{Q}}_{h}(\nabla{\mathbf{w}}))\|_{T}^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}(\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\|\nabla_{w}({\mathbf{u}}-Q_{h}{\mathbf{u}})\|_{T}^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
\leq&Ch\|{\mathcal{Q}}_{h}(\nabla{\mathbf{w}})\|_{1}h^{k}\|{\mathbf{u}}\|_{k+1}\\
\leq&Ch^{k+1}\|{\mathbf{w}}\|_{2}\|{\mathbf{u}}\|_{k+1}.\end{split}

For
I_{5}
, applying the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, the trace inequality (
4.1
), the norm equivalence (
4.8
), (
6.3
) with
n=1
, the triangle inequality, and the error estimates (
6.4
) and (
6.8
), we have

\begin{split}&|\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\langle{\bm{\zeta}}_{b}-{\bm{\zeta}}_{0},({\mathcal{Q}}_{h}-I)\nabla{\mathbf{w}}\cdot{\mathbf{n}}\rangle_{\partial T}|\\
\leq&(\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}h_{T}^{-1}\|{\bm{\zeta}}_{b}-{\bm{\zeta}}_{0}\|_{\partial T}^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}(\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}h_{T}\|({\mathcal{Q}}_{h}-I)\nabla{\mathbf{w}}\cdot{\mathbf{n}}\|_{\partial T}^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
\leq&\|{\bm{\zeta}}_{h}\|_{1,h}(\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\|({\mathcal{Q}}_{h}-I)\nabla{\mathbf{w}}\cdot{\mathbf{n}}\|_{T}^{2}+h_{T}^{2}\|({\mathcal{Q}}_{h}-I)\nabla{\mathbf{w}}\cdot{\mathbf{n}}\|_{1,T}^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
\leq&{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}{\bm{\zeta}}_{h}{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}h\|{\mathbf{w}}\|_{2}\\
\leq&({|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}Q_{h}{\mathbf{u}}-{\mathbf{u}}{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}+{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}{\mathbf{u}}-{\mathbf{u}}_{h}{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|})h\|{\mathbf{w}}\|_{2}\\
\leq&Ch^{k+1}(\|{\mathbf{u}}\|_{k+1}+\|p\|_{k})\|{\mathbf{w}}\|_{2}.\end{split}

Now we estimate
I_{6}
. First, using the second equation in (
1.1
), (
5.2
)-(
5.3
), we have

(7.7)

\nabla_{w}\cdot{\mathbf{u}}={\mathcal{Q}}_{h}\nabla\cdot{\mathbf{u}}=0,\qquad\nabla_{w}\cdot Q_{h}{\mathbf{u}}={\mathcal{Q}}_{h}\nabla\cdot{\mathbf{u}}=0.

Let
{\mathcal{Q}}_{h}^{k-1}
denote the
L^{2}
projection onto
P_{k-1}(T)
.
Using the second equation in (
5.5
), the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, the estimate (
6.1
) with
n=1
, (
7.7
), the error estimates (
6.5
),

\begin{split}&|\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}|(\nabla_{w}\cdot({\mathbf{u}}-{\mathbf{u}}_{h}),q)_{T}|\\
=&|\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}|(\nabla_{w}\cdot({\mathbf{u}}-{\mathbf{u}}_{h}),q-{\mathcal{Q}}_{h}^{k-1}q)_{T}|\\
=&|\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}|(\nabla_{w}\cdot(Q_{h}{\mathbf{u}}-{\mathbf{u}}_{h}),q-{\mathcal{Q}}_{h}^{k-1}q)_{T}|\\
\leq&(\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\|\nabla_{w}\cdot(Q_{h}{\mathbf{u}}-{\mathbf{u}}_{h})\|_{T}^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}(\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\|q-{\mathcal{Q}}_{h}^{k-1}q\|_{T}^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
\leq&Ch^{k}\|{\mathbf{u}}\|_{k+1}h\|q\|_{1}.\end{split}

For
I_{7}
, let
Q^{0}
denote the
L^{2}
projection onto
P_{0}(T)
. For any
T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
, we have from (
2.3
),

(Q^{0}q,\nabla_{w}\cdot(Q_{h}{\mathbf{u}}-{\mathbf{u}}))_{T}=-(Q_{0}{\mathbf{u}}-{\mathbf{u}},\nabla(Q^{0}q))_{T}+\langle Q_{b}{\mathbf{u}}-{\mathbf{u}},Q^{0}q\cdot{\mathbf{n}}\rangle_{\partial T}=0,

which, combined with the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and the error estimate (
6.5
), yields

\begin{split}&|\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}(\nabla_{w}\cdot(Q_{h}{\mathbf{u}}-{\mathbf{u}}),q)_{T}|\\
\leq&|\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}(\nabla_{w}\cdot(Q_{h}{\mathbf{u}}-{\mathbf{u}}),q-Q^{0}q)_{T}|\\
\leq&(\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\|\nabla_{w}\cdot(Q_{h}{\mathbf{u}}-{\mathbf{u}})\|_{T}^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}(\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\|q-Q^{0}q\|_{T}^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
\leq&Ch^{k}\|{\mathbf{u}}\|_{k+1}h\|q\|_{1}.\end{split}

Finally, for
I_{8}
, using the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, the trace inequality (
4.1
), the norm equivalence (
4.8
), the estimate (
6.1
) with
n=1
, the triangle inequality, and the error estimates (
6.4
) and (
6.8
), we get

\begin{split}&|\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\langle({\mathcal{Q}}_{h}-I)q,({\bm{\zeta}}_{b}-{\bm{\zeta}}_{0})\cdot{\mathbf{n}}\rangle_{\partial T}|\\
\leq&(\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}h_{T}\|({\mathcal{Q}}_{h}-I)q\|_{\partial T}^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}(\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}h_{T}^{-1}\|({\bm{\zeta}}_{b}-{\bm{\zeta}}_{0})\cdot{\mathbf{n}}\|_{\partial T}^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
\leq&(\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\|({\mathcal{Q}}_{h}-I)q\|_{T}^{2}+h_{T}^{2}\|({\mathcal{Q}}_{h}-I)q\|_{1,T}^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}\|{\bm{\zeta}}_{h}\|_{1,h}\\
\leq&Ch\|q\|_{1}({|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}Q_{h}{\mathbf{u}}-{\mathbf{u}}{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}+{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}{\mathbf{u}}-{\mathbf{u}}_{h}{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|})\\
\leq&Ch\|q\|_{1}h^{k}(\|{\mathbf{u}}\|_{k+1}+\|p\|_{k}).\\
\end{split}

Substituting the estimates for
I_{i}
(
i=1,\cdots,8
) into (
7.6
) and using the regularity assumption (
7.2
)
gives

\|{\bm{\zeta}}_{0}\|\leq Ch^{k+1}(\|{\mathbf{u}}\|_{k+1}+\|p\|_{k}),

which, together with the triangle inequality, leads to

\|{\mathbf{u}}-{\mathbf{u}}_{0}\|\leq\|{\mathbf{u}}-Q_{0}{\mathbf{u}}\|+\|{\bm{\zeta}}_{0}\|\leq Ch^{k+1}(\|{\mathbf{u}}\|_{k+1}+\|p\|_{k}).

This completes the proof of the theorem.
∎

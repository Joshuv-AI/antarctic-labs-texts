GAN-PD: generative adversarial networks for fabric defect generation towards precise detection
Authors: Yuyang Xia, Yong Yin, Fengkai Luan, Junqi Liu, Jiaxing Yang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Automated inspection of textile surfaces is challenged by the scarcity and geometric intricacy of real defect samples, especially the elongated, sub-millimetre scratches critical for quality grading. Existing generative approaches often prioritize image realism, thereby neglecting the geometric continuity and contextual fidelity essential for robust detection by modern systems. This paper introduces Generative Adversarial Networks for Fabric Defect Generation towards Precise Detection(GAN-PD), a task-oriented generative adversarial framework engineered to synthesize fabric anomalies with both high structural realism and detector relevance. GAN-PD’s generator employs Local-Feature Residual modules to adaptively focus convolutional resources on defect-salient channels, while its discriminator utilizes columnar pooling to enforce directional continuity along defect axes. This synergistic design preserves high-frequency weave details, enforces millimetre-scale linearity, and mitigates fragmentation artifacts typical of isotropic convolutions. Experimental results on benchmark textile dataset show GAN-PD produces sharper linear anomalies, achieves more consistent texture fusion, and leads to significant improvements in detection accuracy for both one-stage and two-stage detectors compared to state-of-the-art GANs. These results validate the efficacy of shifting the paradigm from mere “defect generation” to “generation for defect detection.” Ultimately, these findings demonstrate that reframing defect synthesis around downstream utility, rather than visual fidelity alone, can markedly enhance the robustness and accuracy of automated textile inspection systems.

Introduction

Automated surface-defect inspection constitutes a pivotal component of contemporary fabric manufacturing1,2;micrometre-scale anomalies–including yarn scratches, filament misalignments, and localised stains–compromise both product quality and operational safety. Early techniques relied on handcrafted descriptors and threshold-based segmentation3,4, yet their limited capacity to model complex textural dynamics renders them inadequate for the diverse patterns and defect morphologies present in modern fabrics. Deep learning has recently delivered notable advances in anomaly detection,owing to its powerful representational capability5.Nonetheless, the efficacy of these models is tightly coupled to large-scale, heterogeneous defect corpora. In industrial settings, the intrinsic sparsity of defect samples–particularly for subtle, elongated imperfections such as hairline scratches or filament offsets–poses a persistent obstacle, even though these defects are decisive for rigorous fabric quality assessment.

Conventional data-augmentation routines, predicated on elementary geometric or chromatic transformations6,fail to substantially enrich data diversity and often incur information loss or distortion. Generative adversarial networks (GANs)7have therefore attracted significant attention for synthetic augmentation8;variants such as ACGAN9,WGAN10,Pix2Pix11,and StarGAN12demonstrate formidable image-generation prowess. However, these models predominantly pursue holistic photorealism while neglecting the localised textures and fine structures that typify fabric surface defects.

Although GAN derivatives have been widely adopted in industrial applications13–22, fabric defects usually manifest as slender, anisotropic artefacts–sub-millimetre scratches traversing multiple texture repeats–demanding precise spatial continuity and seamless fusion with the underlying pattern. Existing methods employ isotropic convolutional kernels, thereby inadequately capturing directional dependencies; the resulting defects frequently appear as unnatural blotches rather than the requisite filiform structures. Moreover, the intricate texture of fabric substrates exacerbates blending difficulty, so visual artefacts and textural distortions emerge, especially in richly detailed regions. For instance, SDGAN17 introduces dual discriminators and cycle-consistency loss to balance diversity and fidelity; EID-GAN18leverages a norm-penalty to facilitate fine-grained anomaly synthesis; DT-GAN23learns defect-type representations independent of the background and applies defect-specific styles. Despite these improvements, perfect synthesis of elongated defects and their integration into complex backgrounds remains elusive.

Prevailing evaluation regimes–peak signal-to-noise ratio (PSNR)24, structural similarity index (SSIM)25, and related metrics26,27–prioritise pixel-level fidelity while disregarding the utility of generated defects for downstream detection. A principled, task-aware assessment protocol linking synthetic defect characteristics to detection performance is conspicuously absent, creating a critical gap between synthetic data quality and industrial applicability. Consequently, because current defect-generation models are optimized for detection only indirectly–operating under the heuristic assumption that visual photorealism naturally translates to diagnostic utility–they struggle to synthesize explicitly task-oriented samples that simultaneously exhibit fine geometric specificity and contextual texture coherence on complex industrial surfaces, thereby precluding tangible improvements in downstream inspection.

To surmount these obstacles, we introduce Generative Adversarial Networks for Fabric Defect Generation towards Precise Detection(GAN-PD), an architecture tailored to high-fidelity defect synthesis for detection-centric applications. The generator incorporates a Local Feature Residual module that deepens the network while employing adaptive convolutions to selectively process informative channels, concentrating computational resources on defect-relevant cues and mitigating redundancy. This design augments the network’s capacity to model fine-grained textures and intricate spatial dependencies. In the discriminator, we embed columnar pooling with stripe-shaped kernels to capture long-range spatial correlations along defect directions, explicitly resolving directional anisotropy and enforcing geometric consistency between generated defects and fabric background. By executing a paradigm shift from vision-driven “defect generation” to task-aware “generation for defect detection,” this framework transcends mere architectural modifications, thereby furnishing a scalable solution for industries constrained by data scarcity and computational budgets.

Related work

Recent advances in GAN-based defect synthesis have demonstrated promising results in addressing data scarcity for industrial inspection tasks. Early attempts, such as He et al.13, proposed semi-supervised frameworks combining GANs with residual networks to augment limited steel defect datasets, though their focus on global classification accuracy overlooked the geometric specificity of fine-grained anomalies. Subsequent works like SDGAN17and EID-GAN18introduced architectural innovations–dual discriminators and norm-penalty functions–to balance defect diversity and handle extreme class imbalances. While these methods improved anomaly detection accuracy in commutator cylinders and fabric datasets, their reliance on isotropic convolution kernels inherently limited their ability to model anisotropic defects like hairline scratches or thread misalignments. Liu et al.14attempted to address this by proposing regional training strategies with wavelet fusion, prioritizing defect area translation in surface inspection tasks. However, their method still struggled with seamless integration of synthetic defects into repetitive textile patterns, often introducing edge artifacts in texture-rich regions.

Further refinements in spatial controllability emerged through defect masks and latent-space conditioning. Niu et al.21designed region- and strength-controllable GANs to localize weak defects in metal surfaces, while Wang et al.20leveraged StyleGAN for panel defect simulation using location-specific models. Cross-domain approaches, such as DT-GAN23, decoupled defect features from background textures via disentangled representations, enabling defect transfer across products. Despite these advances, a critical limitation persists: defects are treated as global stylistic attributes rather than locally coherent structures with directional dependencies, leading to fragmented artifacts when applied to fine-grained textiles. For instance, Lai et al.16employed generative models to construct texture patterns for novelty detection but failed to address the precise alignment of synthetic defects with background motifs–a requirement paramount for textile quality control.Fig. 1Generative adversarial networks for fabric defect generation towards precise detection.

Generative adversarial networks for fabric defect generation towards precise detection.

The evaluation of synthetic defects remains another unresolved challenge. Existing frameworks13–23predominantly rely on pixel-level metrics (e.g., PSNR, SSIM) or domain-agnostic scores like FID, which prioritize visual plausibility over task relevance. Guo et al.15demonstrated improved FID scores for bridge defect synthesis using online knowledge distillation, yet their evaluation ignored the downstream impact on detector decision boundaries. Similarly, Zaman et al.22applied Pix2Pix with multi-snapshot strategies to augment bone defect data but did not correlate synthetic quality with segmentation performance. This disconnect between synthetic realism and industrial utility is particularly acute in textile inspection, where defects must not only appear authentic but also align with detector-sensitive features to improve generalization–a gap unaddressed by prior methodologies.

To address these limitations, we propose a novel framework that integrates task-aware defect generation and evaluation. Unlike existing approaches that optimize for standalone realism, our method employs a GAN architecture specifically tailored for textile defects, ensuring synthetic anomalies preserve both geometric fidelity and contextual consistency with complex background textures. Crucially, we establish a direct link between synthetic data quality and downstream detection performance, bridging the gap between augmentation and task efficacy. This work represents the first systematic effort to embed task-specific considerations into the defect generation pipeline, offering a pragmatic and scalable solution for industries grappling with data scarcity. By redefining defect synthesis as a detector-centric process, our approach ensures generated samples actively enhance real-world inspection scenarios rather than merely approximating them–a paradigm shift with profound implications for industrial quality control.

Methodology

The proposed GAN-PD framework addresses two fundamental challenges in fabric defect synthesis: the geometric anisotropy of sub-millimeter defects such as hairline scratches that span multiple texture repetitions, and the contextual coherence required for seamless integration of synthetic defects into complex woven patterns. Fundamental physical constraints are integrated into the synthesis pipeline to surpass conventional structural modifications. Explicit channel-level disentanglement is executed by the generator. Complex background topology is thus preserved. Concurrently, anisotropic geometric priors are enforced by the discriminator. The spatial continuity of elongated defects is rigidly guaranteed by this geometric enforcement. As depicted in Fig. 1, our architecture employs a dual-branch strategy where a deep residual generator progressively constructs defect morphologies through localized feature modulation, while a direction-sensitive discriminator enforces spatial consistency along defect axes. This symbiotic design ensures that synthesized anomalies not only mimic the visual characteristics of real defects but also align with the geometric priors critical for detection tasks.Fig. 2Network structure of the deep residual generator with local feature convolution. The generation process initiates by transforming random noise vectors into defect-embedded fabric textures through three hierarchical stages. Initial convolutional layers extract primitive texture motifs, establishing the baseline fabric pattern structure. Subsequent layers deploy cascaded Local Feature Residual (LFR) modules–a novel construct detailed in Section Methodology-A–that specialize in inducing directional defects through adaptive channel reweighting and dilated convolutions. These modules strategically amplify defect-related features while suppressing redundant background activations, thereby resolving the persistent trade-off between defect saliency and texture preservation. The final reconstruction phase employs texture-regularized upsampling to synthesize high-resolution images, explicitly mitigating edge artifacts in regions with dense repetitive patterns.

Complementing this, the discriminator architecture introduces columnar pooling operations with elongated kernels to capture the directional persistence of filament misalignments–a critical advancement over conventional isotropic convolutions as elaborated in Section Methodology-B. Through adversarial training, the generator learns to synthesize defects that simultaneously deceive the discriminator’s global texture consistency checks and satisfy its anisotropic geometric constraints. This dual optimization pressure ensures that synthetic hairline scratches exhibit millimeter-scale continuity while maintaining phase alignment with underlying fabric weaves, a prerequisite for effective detector training.

The following sections dissect the architectural innovations of the LFR generator and columnar-pooling discriminator, revealing how their coordinated operation overcomes the limitations of prior isotropic convolution-based approaches. Specifically, Section Methodology-A formalizes the defect induction mechanisms through localized residual learning, while Section Methodology-B demonstrates how directional sensitivity in the discriminator enforces physically plausible defect geometries.

The proposed deep residual generator architecture addresses the dual challenges of gradient dissipation in deep networks and computational inefficiency in defect feature extraction through a hierarchical integration of residual learning and localized channel processing. As illustrated in Fig. 2, the generator employs cascaded Local Feature Residual modules that synergistically combine residual connections with adaptive channel-wise convolution–a strategic departure from conventional isotropic convolution paradigms. The residual pathways mitigate vanishing gradients by preserving information flow through identity mappings, while the local convolution mechanism dynamically allocates computational resources to defect-critical regions through channel segmentation. B. Columnar pooling discriminator network

The proposed columnar pooling discriminator directly addresses the limitations of conventional spatial pooling in modeling anisotropic defect geometries–a critical requirement in textile quality inspection where elongated scratches or filament misalignments exhibit strong directional dependencies. As illustrated in Fig. 4, the discriminator integrates columnar pooling operations into a deep convolutional framework, enabling targeted feature extraction along defect axes while suppressing interference from non-defective regions. Traditional pooling methods, which rely on isotropic square kernels, inherently struggle to capture linear structural patterns in texture-rich fabric substrates, often introducing noise from non-target areas and compromising defect localization accuracy. This inefficacy is particularly pronounced in scenarios requiring precise identification of sub-millimeter defects spanning multiple texture repetitions, where global or fixed-window pooling operations fail to resolve directional continuity.Fig. 4Network architecture of the columnar pooling discriminator. Concurrently, the Deep Residual Generator leveraging Local Feature Convolutions distinctly amplifies the characteristics of low-saliency anomalies, ensuring their clear discernibility against heterogeneous textures. In contrast, the network exhibits measured stability when synthesizing defects with diffuse or stochastic characteristics, indicating balanced generalization rather than excessive optimization toward particular defect morphologies.

Defect detection verification experimental results and analysis

While conventional image quality metrics provide foundational insights into synthetic defect characteristics, their limitations in correlating with detection task performance necessitate complementary evaluation protocols. PSNR’s insensitivity to perceptual complexity and SSIM’s localized focus often misalign with human visual assessments, while FID/IS inherit biases from pretrained models, inadequately capturing industrial defect diversity. To holistically evaluate synthetic data utility, we benchmark GAN-PD against SDGAN17 and EID-GAN18 using three detection architectures (SSD, Faster R-CNN, YOLOv5) trained on distinct datasets: Real (100% authentic defects), Synthetic (100% generated defects), and Hybrid (1:1 real-synthetic mixture). This framework quantifies how synthetic defects influence model generalization, precision, and robustness under industrial inspection conditions.

A. Experimental design and evaluation indicators

Three training datasets—De-Train A (real), De-Train B (synthetic), and De-Train C (hybrid)—were constructed per defect class, each containing 700 defect-free samples and 100 defective instances (Table 4). Corresponding test sets (De-Test A/B/C) maintained identical class distributions with 120 defect-free and 60 defective samples. Training-validation splits followed a 7:3 ratio, with models trained for 100 epochs using Adam optimization (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\beta _1 = 0.95$$\end{document}, \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\beta _2 = 0.999$$\end{document}). Learning rates were fixed at 0.005 for Faster R-CNN/SSD and 0.01 for YOLOv5, decaying linearly after 30 epochs. In the experimental framework, performance evaluations of the detection models are conducted using three pivotal metrics pertinent to an actual industrial production setting: Data Detection Accuracy (DDA), Defect Detection Rate (DDR), and False Detection Rate. Data Detection Accuracy is defined as the proportion of correctly identified defective and non-defective samples relative to the total number of samples. Defect Detection Rate specifically refers to the percentage of actual defect samples that are correctly identified as defective. False Detection Rate involves the proportion of non-defective samples erroneously identified as defective relative to the total number of samples detected as defective. These metrics collectively assess how synthetic defects alter detector decision boundaries, balancing identification reliability against operational false alarms–a critical consideration for automated inspection systems. This paper establishes a direct mathematical relationship between synthesized defect characteristics and actual detection utility through task-specific quantitative detection metrics, rather than relying on generic image quality scores, thereby further verifying the explicit task-aware nature of the proposed framework. B. Comparative experiments on defect generation

Systematic visual comparison of synthetic defects generated by GAN-PD, SDGAN, and EID-GAN reveals critical insights into their respective capabilities and limitations, as exemplified in Figs. 6, 7. The SSIM localization maps, computed against consistent background references, expose fundamental disparities in defect fidelity and contextual integration.

SDGAN-generated defects exhibit amorphous distributions across Classes 1, 2, and 5, with features appearing spatially incoherent–particularly detrimental for Class 4’s linear defects, which manifest as fragmented fold-like artifacts rather than continuous scratches. While SDGAN produces heterogeneous luminous spots in Class 3, these deviate from authentic dark micro-anomalies, indicating misalignment with real defect photometric profiles. EID-GAN marginally improves feature cohesion but suffers from mode collapse in Class 2 (identical linear defect repetitions) and inadequate defect-background fusion. Class 1 anomalies exhibit translucent artifacts revealing underlying textures, evidencing incomplete defect feature learning, while Class 5’s imprints display severe texture erosion under complex backgrounds. Both baselines struggle to preserve directional consistency in linear defects or photometric authenticity in micro-scale anomalies.

GAN-PD addresses these limitations through coordinated generator-discriminator innovations. Linear defects in Classes 2 and 4 maintain millimeter-scale continuity, aligning precisely with underlying weave orientations. Class 1’s waxy smudges exhibit controlled blurring gradients that respect substrate texture boundaries, while Class 5 imprints retain high-frequency details despite complex backgrounds. Class 3 micro-spots achieve optimal luminance contrast, replicating the dark, crisp profiles of real defects. The SSIM maps further validate these improvements, showing tighter spatial correspondence between synthetic and real defect regions compared to baselines.

These visual advancements stem from the framework’s architectural synergies: the LFR generator hierarchically amplifies defect saliency through channel-adaptive convolutions, while the columnar pooling discriminator enforces directional consistency via anisotropic feature aggregation. Such capabilities enable GAN-PD to overcome the isotropic convolution limitations inherent in SDGAN and EID-GAN, which prioritize global texture realism over localized defect fidelity.

D.Baseline detection with real data

To systematically evaluate the industrial utility of synthetic defects, we retrained three detection models (YOLOv5, SSD, Faster R-CNN) using two configurations: De-Train B (100% synthetic defects) and De-Train C (1:1 hybrid real-synthetic). Tables 7-8 detail the mAP@0.5 metrics across five defect classes, with corresponding performance visualizations provided in Figs. 8-9.

Table 7 and 8 demonstrates the persistent superiority of GAN-PD in the synthetic-only training setting. Across almost every comparative scenario it delivers the highest accuracy, and in the few non-optimal cases the shortfall to the best score is confined to roughly 0.1%. Under the hybrid training regime, GAN-PD elevates class-2 defect detection by average margins of 5.0% and 3.5% over SDGAN and EID-GAN, respectively. These gains stem from its bespoke deep-residual generator with local-feature convolution and columnar-pooling discriminator, both of which preserve scratch linearity in repetitive fabric textures. Even for the geometrically challenging class-4 defects, GAN-PD surpasses SDGAN and EID-GAN by 4.2% and 2.6%, substantiating its capability to model anisotropic defects.

To further elucidate the merits of GAN-PD, the optimal detection results under each training regime are consolidated in Table 9. When trained on the hybrid dataset, the detector’s mean accuracy increases by 5.0%, 3.6%, and 5.6% for YOLOv5, SSD, and Faster R-CNN, respectively, relative to the synthetic-only baseline. Compared with models trained solely on real defects, the corresponding gains reach 10.1%, 9.3%, and 10.4%. These findings suggest that synthetic data may approximate defect variations that are difficult to sample in practice and thus potentially enhance detector generalization, while also indicating that such samples complement rather than supplant real data and could regularize the detector to attenuate empirical distribution bias.

To establish a genuinely task-oriented assessment framework and to quantify how much GAN-PD augments detector generalisation in realistic manufacturing lines, we designed two complementary evaluation scenarios, De-Test B and De-Test C. These test sets probe models optimised under distinct training regimes yet confronted with operational conditions that deviate from their learning distributions. De-Test A, already used for baseline benchmarking, is deliberately omitted here to eliminate evaluative redundancy and channel analytic bandwidth toward synthesis-dominated domains. Fig. 10 collates the outcomes for every fabric-defect class across four testing configurations, three synthesis engines, and three mainstream detectors, yielding twelve radar-plot traces per subpanel. For clarity, each trace is indexed by the triplet “training set–test set–generator”; for example, “B-B-GAN-PD” denotes a detector trained exclusively on De-Train B, evaluated on De-Test B, with synthetic samples provided by GAN-PD. This multi-axis experimental matrix is intentionally complex: only by intersecting heterogeneous training distributions with varied test distributions can we expose the latent strengths and weaknesses of synthetic augmentation under production-scale variability. Furthermore, aggregating complementary detectors within the same test cell reveals whether augmentation bias persists across architectural paradigms–an aspect seldom explored in prior textile-inspection literature. By jointly analysing the radial profiles and the surface area enclosed by each trace, practitioners can directly gauge which synthesis strategy delivers the optimal trade-off between sensitivity and To establish a genuinely task-oriented assessment framework and to quantify how much GAN-PD augments detector generalisation in realistic manufacturing lines, we designed two complementary evaluation scenarios, De-Test B and De-Test C. These test sets probe models optimised under distinct training regimes yet confronted with operational conditions that deviate from their learning distributions. De-Test A, already used for baseline benchmarking, is deliberately omitted here to eliminate evaluative redundancy and channel analytic bandwidth toward synthesis-dominated domains. Figure 10 collates the outcomes for every fabric-defect class across four testing configurations, three synthesis engines, and three mainstream detectors, yielding twelve radar-plot traces per subpanel. For clarity, each trace is indexed by the triplet “training set–test set–generator”; for example, “B-B-GAN-PD” denotes a detector trained exclusively on De-Train B, evaluated on De-Test B, with synthetic samples provided by GAN-PD. This multi-axis experimental matrix is intentionally complex: only by intersecting heterogeneous training distributions with varied test distributions can we expose the latent strengths and weaknesses of synthetic augmentation under production-scale variability. Furthermore, aggregating complementary detectors within the same test cell reveals whether augmentation bias persists across architectural paradigms–an aspect seldom explored in prior textile-inspection literature. By jointly analysing the radial profiles and the surface area enclosed by each trace, practitioners can directly gauge which synthesis strategy delivers the optimal trade-off between sensitivity and precision at fixed operating points, thereby informing acceptance thresholds for automatic optical-inspection hardware and guiding downstream corrective-action policies on the shop floor.

Across 540 experimental configurations encompassing five defect classes, three generative models, three detectors, two training regimes, two test sets, and three evaluation metrics, the hybrid implementation of GAN-PD consistently achieved the best performance. Under YOLOv5, it raised mean precision by 1.1% and 1.3% over EID-GAN and SDGAN, respectively, boosted mean recall by 2.3% and 2.7%, and cut the mean false-positive rate by 1.2% and 1.8%. In SSD experiments, mean precision increased by 1.3% and 1.1%, mean recall by 2.7% and 2.0%, while the mean false-positive rate declined by 1.9%. Table 10 consolidates optimal performance metrics, contrasting the baseline established with exclusively real data (A-A configuration) against deployments utilizing synthetic-only (C-B) and hybrid (C-C) training datasets. These comprehensive results underscore GAN-PD’s efficacy in addressing the core challenges of synthesizing geometrically anisotropic defects and achieving their contextual integration within complex fabric patterns. For instance, in the detection of linear scratch defects, exemplified by Class 2 and Class 4, the YOLOv5 detector, when trained using the C-C configuration, achieved Defect Detection Rates of 96.7% and 58.3%, respectively. These figures represent absolute improvements of 15.0 and 11.6 percentage points over the A-A baseline DDRs of 81.7% and 46.7%. This marked performance enhancement directly validates the capability of our direction-sensitive columnar pooling discriminator to preserve millimeter-scale linear continuity, effectively mitigating the fragmentation artifacts typically produced by traditional isotropic convolutions, a limitation highlighted in Section Introduction. Critically, the False Detection Rate (FDR) for Class 4 defects, averaged across all detector architectures, was substantially reduced from approximately 12.1% in the A-A setup to 2.7% in the C-C setup, indicating that the local feature convolution mechanism within our LFR generator facilitates accurate texture fusion and ensures consistent defect integration with the surrounding fabric context.

The utility of Table 10 extends beyond performance benchmarking; it systematically quantifies how our proposed task-aware evaluation protocol translates improvements in synthetic data quality into tangible industrial utility. Specifically, when employing the YOLOv5 model for the five defect classes, training augmentation with GAN-PD (C-C configuration) resulted in an average increase in DDA of 5.4 percentage points, an average DDR improvement of 13.0 percentage points, and an average FDR reduction of 5.0 percentage points, relative to the A-A baseline. Analogous enhancements were observed with the SSD detector (average DDA +4.9%, DDR +10.7%, FDR −6.0%) and the Faster R-CNN detector (average DDA +5.3%, DDR +12.7%, FDR −4.9%). This consistent uplift in performance is attributable to the generator’s proficiency in synthesizing features that are salient for detection, rather than merely mimicking visual realism, a principle central to our advocated paradigm shift from “defect generation” to “generation for defect detection.” The quantifiable decrease in FDR, in particular, offers a direct benefit by mitigating false alarms in production line inspection systems.

Further analysis comparing the C-C and C-B configurations reveals that models trained exclusively on synthetic data (C-B) generally exhibit lower performance metrics than those trained on hybrid datasets (C-C). This observation suggests that GAN-PD-generated samples function as a potent complement to, rather than a complete substitute for, authentic data. This finding echoes the critique of over-reliance on conventional fidelity metrics, as discussed in Section Introduction: operational efficacy is not solely guaranteed by high visual fidelity unless the synthetic data distributions are appropriately anchored by, and aligned with, real-world examples. Consequently, orienting synthetic defect generation towards enhancing detection effectiveness, rather than pursuing standalone visual fidelity, is paramount. The substantial improvements in DDR and concurrent reductions in FDR achieved through GAN-PD underscore that such task-aware synthesis, not merely enhanced fidelity, is the critical driver for advancing defect detection accuracy in industrial textiles. Conclusion

This paper has introduced GAN-PD, a generative architecture expressly designed to produce fabric-surface anomalies that improve automated inspection rather than merely achieve visual plausibility. By integrating Local-Feature Residual modules within a deep residual generator and enforcing anisotropic consistency through a columnar-pooling discriminator, the framework synthesizes elongated scratches and fine-grained blemishes that blend seamlessly with complex woven textures. A task-aware evaluation protocol confirms that these detector-centric samples translate into higher recognition accuracy and fewer false alarms across both one-stage and two-stage detectors, while an efficient parameterization ensures the framework is computationally viable for large-scale offline augmentation on standard industrial servers.

Despite these promising results, practical limitations include the current need for manual annotation of generated samples and the computational overhead associated with training, alongside the ongoing challenge of capturing the complete spectrum of defect morphologies. Future research will therefore concentrate on integrating automated annotation techniques, validating the framework on high-variability proprietary industrial datasets, and developing more computationally efficient, lightweight adversarial modules. These efforts aim to expand GAN-PD’s applicability, further propelling the development of comprehensive, real-time defect inspection systems for the textile industry through high-fidelity offline data augmentation.

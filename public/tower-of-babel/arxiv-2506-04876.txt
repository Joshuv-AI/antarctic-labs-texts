Scattering angle in a Topological Star spacetime: a self-force approach
Massimo Bianchi 1 , Donato Bini 2 , Giorgio Di Russo 3

Abstract
We compute the scattering angle for a scalar neutral probe undergoing unbound motion around a Topological Star, including self-force effects. Moreover we identify the ‘electro-magnetic’ source of the background as Papapetrou Field compatible with the isometries and characterize Topological Stars by studying their sectional curvature, geometric transport along special curves and the gravitational energy content in terms of the super-energy tensors.

Introduction

For a standard two-body system (a pair of massive, spinning objects) in General Relativity (GR) one considers usually energy and angular momentum as fundamental gauge-invariant quantities, but there exist related ones like redshift, period and periastron advance for bound motion or the scattering angle for unbound motion.

II
Killing vectors and Papapetrou Fields

Consider a static and spherically symmetric metric of the type

\displaystyle ds^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-f_{t}(r)dt^{2}+f_{r}(r)dr^{2}+f_{\Omega}(r)(d\theta^{2}+\sin^{2}\theta d\phi^{2})

(1)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle(D-4)f_{y}(r)dy^{2}\,,

with coordinates
(t,r,\theta,\phi,y)
adapted to the Killing symmetries of the spacetime, that may be viewed as a non-vacuum solution of Einstein equations with sources.
Here
D
denotes the spacetime dimension: 4 in GR but 5 in the case of a Top(ological) Star (TS) spacetime, that will be the main focus of our analysis starting from Section
IV
.
Sometimes, to ease comparisons of the TS spacetime with other 4D spacetimes we will consider a
D=4
TS metric, i.e., a 4D spacetime with metric the one induced on the leaves
y=
constant.

Noticeably, when the background admits as a source an electromagnetic field, the latter is simply related to the Killing structure of the spacetime, in the sense that
the vector potential
A
has components (eventually separated in their dependence on
r
and
\theta
) only along the Killing directions
1
1

1

In principle one can include a component
A_{y}(r,\theta)
, but we will refrain from doing so.

A=A_{t}(r,\theta)dt+A_{\phi}(r,\theta)d\phi\,.

(2)

Let us recall that the Killing vectors themselves play a role in this context. In fact, if
\xi
is a Killing vector field then by definition (since
\xi_{(\alpha;\beta)}=0
and hence
\xi_{\alpha;\beta}=F_{\alpha\beta}
is antisymmetric, and satisfies Maxwell’s equations with sources,

F_{\xi}^{\alpha\beta}{}_{;\beta}=R^{\alpha}{}_{\beta}\xi^{\beta}=J^{\alpha}\,.

(3)

F_{\xi}^{\alpha\beta}
is termed a Papapetrou Field (PF)
Fayos:1999de
, and in vacuum (
R_{\alpha\beta}=0
) they are such that
F_{\xi}^{\alpha\beta}{}_{;\beta}=0
.
PF have been used to analyze exact solutions of Einstein’s field equations, and in the study of black holes in the presence of
external electromagnetic fields
Wald:1974np
. In particular, it turns out that the Kerr-Newman
electromagnetic field is a PF generated by the time-like Killing vector field of the Kerr
metric.
Moreover, PFs provide us with a link between the Killing
symmetries and the algebraic structure of the spacetime, which is a subject treated
only occasionally in the literature
Fayos:1999de
;
Fayos:2002pa
.

This relationship can be found
by studying the alignment of the principal directions of the PF with those
of the gravitational field, i.e., of the Riemann tensor (which in vacuum reduces to the
Weyl tensor). In this sense, a remarkable example is the type D Kerr metric: the principal directions of the PF associated with the
time-like Killing vector field coincide with the two repeated principal directions of the spacetime. This link between Killing symmetries and algebraic structure represents a
powerful tool for the study (and the search) of vacuum gravitational fields.
Combining two such fields with coefficient functions one may find conditions such that source-free Maxwell’s equations be satisfied.

The main interest of our present investigation is the spacetime of a TS which

The Bel and Bel-Robinson tensors are built with the Riemann tensor and the Weyl tensor, respectively, and are both divergence-free in vacuum spacetimes, where they coincide.
Their mathematical properties are reviewed, e.g., in Refs.
Bonilla:1997ink
;
Senovilla:1999xz
.
We will consider the following gravitational (Bel) super-energy tensor (see Eq. 15.29 of Ref.
Misner:1973prb
)

T^{\rm(g)}{}_{\alpha\beta}{}^{\gamma\delta}=R_{\alpha\rho\beta\sigma}R^{\gamma\rho\delta\sigma}+{}^{*}R_{\alpha\rho\beta\sigma}{}^{*}R^{\gamma\rho\delta\sigma}\ ,

(4)

defined in terms of the Riemann tensor
R_{\alpha\rho\gamma\delta}
and its left dual

{}^{*}R_{\alpha\rho\beta\sigma}=\frac{1}{2}{\varepsilon}_{\alpha\rho\gamma\delta}R^{\gamma\delta}{}_{\beta\sigma}\ ,

(5)

which is more natural in the case of nonempty spacetimes. In an empty spacetime, the latter tensor is defined exactly in the same way by using the Weyl tensor and enjoys the following properties

1.

It is symmetric and traceless in all pair of indices;

2.

It is divergence-free
\nabla_{\alpha}T^{\rm(g)}{}^{\alpha}{}_{\beta\gamma\delta}=0
.

Indeed, the analogous quantity (Bel-Robinson) defined in terms of the Weyl tensor
C_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}
and its dual carries a similar information, but without any reference to the spacetime sources. We will not insist on studying explicitly the differences between these two tensors (already discussed in the literature
Bonilla:1997ink
;
Senovilla:1999xz
),
and take the Bel tensor as bearer of (qualitative, more than quantitative) information concerning the observer-dependent gravitational energy and momentum content of a given spacetime (and a given observer).

As it is well known, in analogy with electromagnetism, by using the Bel tensor one can introduce the super-energy density and super-momentum density (or super-Poynting, spatial) vector
bel58
;
bel59
;
bel62
;
Bel:1959uwe
with respect to a given observer with 4-velocity
u

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{\rm{(g)}}(u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T^{\rm(g)}_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}u^{\alpha}u^{\beta}u^{\gamma}u^{\delta}\ ,

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}^{\rm{(g)}}(u)_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle P(u)^{\epsilon}{}_{\alpha}T^{\rm(g)}_{\epsilon\beta\gamma\delta}u^{\beta}u^{\gamma}u^{\delta}\,.

(6)

with
u
unitary, timelike and future-pointing and
P(u)=g+u\otimes u
the projection operator orthogonally to
u
.

It is convenient to introduce the notation

X_{\alpha\beta\ldots}u^{\alpha}=X_{{\mathbf{u}}\beta\ldots}

(7)

for indices contracted with
u
and to recall the notion of electric and magnetic parts of the Riemann tensor

\displaystyle{\tt E}(u)_{\alpha\beta}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle R_{\alpha\mu\beta\nu}u^{\mu}u^{\nu}=R_{\alpha{\bf u}\beta{\bf u}}\,,

\displaystyle{\tt H}(u)_{\alpha\beta}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{}^{*}R_{\alpha\mu\beta\nu}u^{\mu}u^{\nu}={}^{*}R_{\alpha{\bf u}\beta{\bf u}}\,.

(8)

We have then

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{\rm{(g)}}(u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle R_{{\bf u}\rho{\bf u}\sigma}R^{{\bf u}\rho{\bf u}\sigma}+{}^{*}R_{{\bf u}\rho{\bf u}\sigma}{}^{*}R^{{\bf u}\rho{\bf u}\sigma}\ ,

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\tt E}(u)_{\rho\sigma}{\tt E}(u)^{\rho\sigma}+{\tt H}(u)_{\rho\sigma}{\tt H}(u)^{\rho\sigma}\ ,

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}^{\rm{(g)}}(u)_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle P(u)^{\epsilon}{}_{\alpha}(R_{\epsilon\rho{\bf u}\sigma}R^{{\bf u}\rho{\bf u}\sigma}+{}^{*}R_{\epsilon\rho{\bf u}\sigma}{}^{*}R^{{\bf u}\rho{\bf u}\sigma})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle P(u)^{\epsilon}{}_{\alpha}(R_{\epsilon\rho{\bf u}\sigma}{\tt E}(u)^{\rho\sigma}+{}^{*}R_{\epsilon\rho{\bf u}\sigma}{\tt H}(u)^{\rho\sigma})\,.

Here
{\tt E}(u)_{\rho\sigma}
is spatial with respect to
u
(any contraction with
u
vanishes), symmetric (but not tracefree), while
{\tt H}(u)_{\rho\sigma}
is spatial with respect to
u
and not symmetric (but tracefree).
A useful method to work with tensors in index-free notation when one needs to split their symmetric and antisymmetric parts is the following

\displaystyle{\tt H}_{\rm S}(u)

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle[{\tt H}(u)^{(\alpha\beta)}]\,,

\displaystyle{\tt H}_{\rm A}(u)

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle[{\tt H}(u)^{[\alpha\beta]}]\,.

(10)

Therefore

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{\rm{(g)}}(u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\rm Tr}\,[{\tt E}(u)\cdot{\tt E}(u)]

(11)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle{\tt H}(u)_{\rho\sigma}[{\tt H}(u)^{(\rho\sigma)}+{\tt H}(u)^{[\rho\sigma]}]\ ,

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\rm Tr}\,[{\tt E}(u)\cdot{\tt E}(u)+{\rm Tr}\,[{\tt H}_{\rm S}(u)\cdot{\tt H}_{\rm S}(u)]

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle{\rm Tr}\,[{\tt H}_{\rm A}(u)\cdot{\tt H}_{\rm A}(u)]\ ,

where matrix multiplication is meant as a contraction of the rightmost lower index of the first tensor with leftmost upper index of the second,

[A\cdot B]_{\alpha}{}^{\gamma}=A_{\alpha\beta}B^{\beta\gamma}\,,\qquad{\rm Tr}[A\cdot B]=[A\cdot B]_{\alpha}{}^{\alpha}\,.

(12)

Let us now recall that a spatial antisymmetric 2-tensor can be fully represented by a vector, labeled by a
V

[{\tt H}_{\rm A}(u)]_{\alpha\beta}={\varepsilon}_{\bf u}{}_{\alpha\beta\gamma}[{\tt H}_{\rm V}(u)]^{\gamma}\,,

(13)

with
{\varepsilon}_{\bf u}{}_{\alpha\beta\gamma}=u^{\delta}\,{\varepsilon}_{\delta\alpha\beta\gamma}
the unit volume 3-form in the local rest space (LRS) of the observer
u

With the above 3-form one can also define a spatial cross product for two symmetric spatial tensor fields (
A,B

[A\times_{u}B]_{\alpha}={\varepsilon}_{\bf u}{}_{\alpha\beta\gamma}A^{\beta}{}_{\delta}\,B^{\delta\gamma}\,.

(14)

We can further manipulate the term

\displaystyle{\rm Tr}\,[{\tt H}_{\rm A}(u)\cdot{\tt H}_{\rm A}(u)]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\varepsilon}_{\bf u}{}_{\alpha\beta\gamma}[{\tt H}_{\rm V}(u)]^{\gamma}{\varepsilon}_{\bf u}{}^{\beta\alpha\rho}[{\tt H}_{\rm V}(u)]_{\rho}

(15)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2\delta_{\gamma}^{\rho}[{\tt H}_{\rm V}(u)]^{\gamma}[{\tt H}_{\rm V}(u)]_{\rho}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2|{\tt H}_{\rm V}(u)|^{2}\,.

Finally, one writes

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{\rm{(g)}}(u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\rm Tr}\,[{\tt E}(u)\cdot{\tt E}(u)]+{\rm Tr}\,[{\tt H}_{\rm S}(u)\cdot{\tt H}_{\rm S}(u)]

(16)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle 2|{\tt H}_{\rm V}(u)|^{2}\,,

which is the gravito-electromagnetic counterpart (
1+3

\mathcal{E}^{\rm{(em)}}(u)=|{\tt E}(u)|^{2}+|{\tt H}(u)|^{2}\,.

(17)

Proceeding along the same lines one can compute the

IV
Top Star metric

A Topological Star (TS) is described by the following
D=5
metric

\displaystyle ds^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-f_{s}(r)dt^{2}+\frac{dr^{2}}{f_{s}(r)f_{b}(r)}

(18)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle r^{2}(d\theta^{2}+\sin^{2}\theta d\phi^{2})+f_{b}(r)dy^{2}\,,

with

f_{s,b}(r)=1-\frac{r_{s,b}}{r}.

(19)

The coordinate
y
is compact
y\sim y+2\pi R_{y}
. The metric (
18
) is a magnetically charged solution of
D=5
Einstein-Maxwell system of equations, where the electromagnetic field is given by

A=-P\cos\theta d\phi\,,\qquad F=P\sin\theta d\theta\wedge d\phi\,,

(20)

and with

P^{2}=\frac{3r_{b}r_{s}}{2\kappa_{5}^{2}}\,,

(21)

representing the magnetic charge.
Moreover,
F^{2}=-F_{\alpha\beta}F^{\alpha\beta}=\frac{2P^{2}}{r^{4}}
and

{}^{*}F=\frac{2P}{r^{2}}dt\wedge dr\wedge dy\,,

(22)

and

T_{\mu\nu}=-\frac{P^{2}}{2r^{4}}g_{\mu\nu}+\frac{P^{2}}{r^{2}}[\delta_{\mu}^{\theta}\delta_{\nu}^{\theta}+\sin^{2}\theta\delta_{\mu}^{\phi}\delta_{\nu}^{\phi}]\,.

(23)

Actually, with this form of the metric there exist two different regimes: 1) black string (BS):
r_{b}<r_{s}
, with an event horizon at
r=r_{s}
; 2) TS:
r_{s}<r_{b}
, a smooth
horizonless solution if
y
is periodically identified with period
2\pi R_{y}
(
R_{y}=2r_{b}^{3/2}/(r_{b}-r_{s})^{1/2}
). Furthermore, stability against (metric) linear perturbations requires
r_{b}<r_{s}<2r_{b}

in the BS case and
r_{s}<r_{b}<2r_{s}
in the TS one
Bah:2020ogh
;
Heidmann:2023ojf
;
Bianchi:2023sfs
.

After dimensional reduction to
D=4
, the solution shows a naked singularity
and has a mass

G_{4}M_{\rm TS}=\frac{r_{s}}{2}+\frac{r_{b}}{4}\,,

(24)

with

8\pi G_{4}=\kappa_{4}^{2}=\frac{\kappa_{5}^{2}}{2\pi R_{y}}=\frac{8\pi G_{5}}{2\pi R_{y}}\,.

(25)

Hereafter we will set
G_{4}\equiv G=1=c
. For
r_{b}=0
, and
thus
r_{s}=2GM_{\rm TS}
, the resulting singular solution is
Schwarzschild BH
\times S_{1}
.
Often we will find it convenient to denote

r_{s}=2M
,
r_{b}=\alpha r_{s}=2\alpha M
,
with
M
a common length scale, not to be confuse with
M_{\rm TS}
, the mass of the TS, unless for
r_{b}=0
. In fact,

G_{4}M_{\rm TS}=\frac{r_{s}}{4}(\alpha+2)=\frac{M}{2}(\alpha+2)\,.

(26)

IV.1
Sectional curvatures

Let us consider the following sections of the TS spacetime:

1.

t-r
section, with (Lorentzian) 2-metric

{}^{(2)}ds^{2}=-f_{s}(r)dt^{2}+\frac{dr^{2}}{f_{s}(r)f_{b}(r)}\,.

(27)

The associated Kretschmann invariant is

{}^{(2)}K_{t-r}=R_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}R^{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}=\frac{r_{s}^{2}(4r-5r_{b})^{2}}{4r^{8}}\,,

(28)

showing a geometrical role for the radius
r=\frac{5}{4}r_{b}
.

2.

r-\phi
section, with (Euclidean) 2-metric

{}^{(2)}ds^{2}=\frac{dr^{2}}{f_{s}(r)f_{b}(r)}+r^{2}d\phi^{2}\,.

(29)

The associated Kretschmann invariant is

{}^{(2)}K_{r-\phi}=\frac{[(r_{s}+r_{b})r-2r_{s}r_{b}]^{2}}{r^{8}}\,,

(30)

showing a geometrical role for the radius
r=\frac{2r_{s}r_{b}}{(r_{s}+r_{b})}=\frac{2}{\frac{1}{r_{s}}+\frac{1}{r_{b}}}
.

3.

r-y
section, with (Euclidean) 2-metric

{}^{(2)}ds^{2}=\frac{dr^{2}}{f_{s}(r)f_{b}(r)}+f_{b}(r)dy^{2}\,.

(31)

The associated Kretschmann invariant is

{}^{(2)}K_{r-y}=\frac{r_{b}^{2}(4r-5r_{s})^{2}}{4r^{8}}\,,

(32)

showing a geometrical role for the radius
r=\frac{5}{4}r_{s}
, namely
r_{s}\leftrightarrow r_{b}
exchange their roles when passing from
{}^{(2)}K_{t-r}
to
{}^{(2)}K_{r-y}
, as expected.

Geometric transport along special orbits

\displaystyle-\frac{r_{s}}{2r_{0}^{2}}\Gamma\sqrt{f_{b}(r_{0})}X^{\hat{r}}\,,

\displaystyle\frac{dX^{\hat{r}}}{d\tau}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Gamma\Omega\sqrt{f_{s}(r_{0})f_{b}(r_{0})}X^{\hat{\phi}}-\frac{r_{s}}{2r_{0}^{2}}\Gamma\sqrt{f_{b}(r_{0})}X^{\hat{t}}\,,

\displaystyle\frac{dX^{\hat{\phi}}}{d\tau}

\displaystyle-\Gamma\Omega\sqrt{f_{s}(r_{0})f_{b}(r_{0})}X^{\hat{r}}\,,

where the hat-components are taken with respect to the natural frame (

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{X^{\hat{t}}(\tau)}{\sqrt{f_{s}(r_{0})}}\,,\qquad X^{\phi}(\tau)=\frac{X^{\hat{\phi}}(\tau)}{r_{0}}\,,

\displaystyle X^{r}(\tau)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle X^{\hat{r}}(\tau)\sqrt{f_{s}(r_{0})f_{b}(r_{0})}\,,

\displaystyle X^{\theta}(\tau)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{r_{0}}X^{\hat{\theta}}(\tau)\,,\qquad X^{y}(\tau)=\frac{X^{\hat{y}}(\tau)}{\sqrt{f_{b}(r_{0})}}\,.

\displaystyle-{\mathcal{A}}X^{\hat{r}}\,,

\displaystyle{\mathcal{B}}X^{\hat{\phi}}-{\mathcal{A}}X^{\hat{t}}\,,

\displaystyle-{\mathcal{B}}X^{\hat{r}}\,,

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{r_{s}}{2r_{0}^{2}}\Gamma\sqrt{f_{b}(r_{0})}\,,

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Gamma\Omega\sqrt{f_{s}(r_{0})f_{b}(r_{0})}\,,

\tau

(57)

ensuring

\frac{\sqrt{|\zeta|}}{\Gamma\Omega}=\frac{n}{k}\,,

IV.4
Hyperbolic-like geodesic motions and the scattering angle

The geodesics of the metric (
18

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}g_{\alpha\beta}\dot{x}^{\alpha}\dot{x}^{\beta}\,,

(65)

where a dot denotes differentiation with respect to an affine parameter
\lambda
related to the proper time by
\tau=\lambda m
with

The corresponding Hamiltonian reads

\mathcal{H}=\frac{1}{2}g^{\mu\nu}P_{\mu}P_{\nu},\quad P_{\mu}=\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial\dot{x}^{\mu}}\,,

with mass-shell condition
\mathcal{H}=-m^{2}/2

The conserved momenta are denoted as

P_{t}=-E,\quad P_{y}=p,\quad P_{\phi}=J\,.

The geodesic equations can be separated by introducing the Carter-like constant

\displaystyle P_{r}^{2}

\displaystyle\frac{E^{2}r^{3}}{(r-r_{b})(r-r_{s})^{2}}-\frac{K^{2}}{(r-r_{b})(r-r_{s})}

\displaystyle\frac{r^{2}(p^{2}r+(r-r_{b})m^{2})}{(r-r_{b})^{2}(r-r_{s})}\,,

\displaystyle P_{\theta}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle K^{2}-\frac{J^{2}}{\sin^{2}\theta}\,.

Due to the spherical symmetry of the background, we can focus on the equatorial motion only without loss of generality,
\theta=\frac{\pi}{2}

P_{\theta}=0

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\frac{P_{r}^{2}}{m^{2}}=\frac{\hat{E}^{2}\hat{r}^{3}-(j^{2}+\hat{r}^{2})(\hat{r}-2)}{(\hat{r}-2\alpha)(\hat{r}-2)^{2}}\,.

(69)

(mostly used with

V
Including scalar self force effects

Self force affects the particle’s (geodesic) four velocity with
O(q^{2})
modifications.
Denoting the particle’s four velocity as
u^{\alpha}(\tau)
with
\tau
the proper time parameter, the equations of motion read

\frac{Du^{\alpha}}{d\tau}=u^{\mu}\nabla_{\mu}u^{\alpha}=\frac{F^{\alpha}}{m}\equiv\hat{F}^{\alpha}(\tau)\,.

(87)

Here, both
u
and
F
have components only along
t,r,\phi
and 1)
u^{\alpha}
is timelike and unitary:
u\cdot u=-1
, while 2)
F
is orthogonal to
u
:

F\cdot u=0
. This implies

g_{tt}(u^{t})^{2}+g_{rr}(u^{r})^{2}+g_{\phi\phi}(u^{\phi})^{2}+1=0\,,

(88)

from which

(u^{r})^{2}=-\frac{g_{tt}}{g_{rr}}(u^{t})^{2}-\frac{g_{\phi\phi}}{g_{rr}}(u^{\phi})^{2}-\frac{1}{g_{rr}}\,,

(89)

and

u_{t}F^{t}+u_{r}F^{r}+u_{\phi}F^{\phi}=0\,,

(90)

from which

F^{r}=-\frac{u_{t}}{u_{r}}F^{t}-\frac{u_{\phi}}{u_{r}}F^{\phi}\,.

(91)

Note that the force terms only exist along the particle’s orbit. Therefore, the metric coefficients in Eq. (
89
) are meant to be restricted to the particle’s world line.
The equations of motion yield the following coupled system of differential equations

\displaystyle\frac{dt}{d\tau}=u^{t}\,,\quad\frac{dr}{d\tau}=u^{r}\,,\quad\frac{d\phi}{d\tau}=u^{\phi}\,,

\displaystyle\frac{du^{\alpha}}{d\tau}+\Gamma^{\alpha}{}_{\mu\nu}u^{\mu}u^{\nu}=\hat{F}^{\alpha}\,,

(92)

where

\Gamma^{\alpha}{}_{\mu\nu}=\Gamma^{\alpha}{}_{\mu\nu}(x)\big|_{x=x(\tau)}

(93)

are (known) functions of the coordinates, to be restricted to the particle’s world line.
Eqs. (
V
) are conveniently written in terms of covariant components.
More precisely,

u_{\sigma}\frac{dg^{\alpha\sigma}}{d\tau}+g^{\alpha\sigma}\frac{du_{\sigma}}{d\tau}+g^{\alpha\sigma}\left(g_{\sigma\mu,\nu}-\frac{1}{2}g_{\mu\nu,\sigma}\right)u^{\mu}u^{\nu}=g^{\alpha\sigma}\hat{F}_{\sigma}\,.

(94)

Eq. (
94
) for
\alpha=t
becomes

\displaystyle u_{t}\frac{d\ln g^{tt}}{d\tau}+\frac{du_{t}}{d\tau}+g_{tt,r}u^{t}u^{r}=\hat{F}_{t}\,,

\displaystyle\frac{du_{t}}{d\tau}=\hat{F}_{t}\,.

(95)

Similarly, for
\alpha=\phi

\displaystyle\frac{du_{\phi}}{d\tau}=\hat{F}_{\phi}\,.

(96)

The equation for
u_{r}
, instead, is more involved

\displaystyle u_{r}\frac{d\ln g^{rr}}{d\tau}+\frac{du_{r}}{d\tau}+\left(g_{r\mu,\nu}-\frac{1}{2}g_{\mu\nu,r}\right)u^{\mu}u^{\nu}=\hat{F}_{r}

\displaystyle\frac{du_{r}}{d\tau}-\frac{1}{2}g_{\mu\nu,r}u^{\mu}u^{\nu}=\hat{F}_{r}\,,

(97)

and can be cast in the suggestive form

\frac{du_{r}}{d\tau}=\hat{F}_{r}^{\rm g}+\hat{F}_{r}\,,

(98)

where

\hat{F}_{r}^{\rm g}=\frac{1}{2}g_{\mu\nu,r}u^{\mu}u^{\nu}\,.

(99)

Equivalently, Eq. (
V
) can be replaced by the normalization condition (
89
).

We can solve the equations of motion perturbatively. To this end let us write

\displaystyle t(\tau)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle t_{g}(\tau)+\delta t(\tau)\,,\quad r(\tau)=r_{g}(\tau)+\delta r(\tau)\,,

\displaystyle\phi(\tau)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\phi_{g}(\tau)+\delta\phi(\tau)\,,

(100)

where
\delta x^{\alpha}(\tau)
denote deviations from geodesic values.
Consequently

\displaystyle u^{\alpha}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle u_{g}^{\alpha}+\frac{d\delta x^{\alpha}}{d\tau}\,,

\displaystyle u_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g_{\alpha\beta}\left(u_{g}^{\beta}+\frac{d\delta x^{\beta}}{d\tau}\right)\,.

(101)

Explicitly

\displaystyle u_{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-E-f_{s}\frac{d\delta t}{d\tau}+\frac{r_{s}}{r_{g}^{2}}\frac{E}{f_{s}}\delta r

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle-E+\delta u_{t}\,,

\displaystyle u_{\phi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle L+r_{g}^{2}\frac{d\delta\phi}{d\tau}+\frac{2L}{r_{g}}\delta r

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle L+\delta u_{\phi}\,,

\displaystyle u_{r}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{f_{s}f_{b}}\frac{dr_{g}}{d\tau}+\frac{1}{f_{s}f_{b}}\frac{d\delta r}{d\tau}

(102)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{(2f_{b}f_{s}-f_{s}-f_{b})}{r_{g}f_{s}^{2}f_{b}^{2}}\frac{dr_{g}}{d\tau}\delta r

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\frac{1}{f_{s}f_{b}}\frac{dr_{g}}{d\tau}+\delta u_{r}\,,

where

f_{s,b}=1-\frac{r_{s,b}}{r_{g}(\tau)}\,,

(103)

and

\partial_{r}g_{tt}|_{r=r_{g}}=\frac{r_{s}}{r_{g}^{2}}\,,\qquad\partial_{r}g_{rr}|_{r=r_{g}}=\frac{(2f_{b}f_{s}-f_{s}-f_{b})}{r_{g}f_{s}^{2}f_{b}^{2}}\,.

(104)

Note that the quantities
\delta u_{\alpha}
are
defined
from the previous relations, namely

\displaystyle\delta u_{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-f_{s}\frac{d\delta t}{d\tau}+\frac{r_{s}}{r_{g}^{2}}\frac{E}{f_{s}}\delta r\,,

\displaystyle\delta u_{\phi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle r_{g}^{2}\frac{d\delta\phi}{d\tau}+\frac{2L}{r_{g}}\delta r\,,

\displaystyle\delta u_{r}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{f_{s}f_{b}}\frac{d\delta r}{d\tau}+\frac{(2f_{b}f_{s}-f_{s}-f_{b})}{r_{g}f_{s}^{2}f_{b}^{2}}\frac{dr_{g}}{d\tau}\delta r\,,\qquad

(105)

in the sense that they are not obtained by lowering indices of the corresponding contravariant quantities
\delta u^{\alpha}

VI
Discussion and concluding remarks

Relying on our recent study of scalar self-force effects for unbound orbits in the background of a Topological Star
DiRusso:2025lip
, we have computed the scattering angle (both the conservative and the dissipative parts) for a small mass neutral probe (
\mu\ll M

V.2
). The addition of self-force terms corrects in a significant way the geodetic result in Eq. (

We have also characterized the electromagnetic source in terms of a Papapetrou Field compatible with the Killing structure of the background and further characterized TSs in terms of their super-energy tensors, sectional curvature, and geometric transport, both ‘parallel’ and Fermi-Walker.

Extending the analysis to gravitational or (for charged probes) electromagnetic self-force effects looks doable in principle but rather involved in practice. On the other hand, scalar self-force effects in ‘rotating’ TSs might be achieved with lesser effort and will be addressed in future works.

Abstract
. In this paper, we consider different classes of subshifts and study their perturbations obtained by forbidding sequences that contain a given word as a subword. We show that the perturbations of sofic shifts are sofic. Though not true for general coded systems, we provide sufficient conditions under which perturbations of synchronized systems remain synchronized. We investigate which systems remain conjugate under perturbation by a word from a coded system. We also explicitly calculate the drop in entropy under perturbation when the subshift is an 𝒮 {\mathcal{S}} -gap shift, and obtain its exponential decay as the length of the word increases. In the case of subshifts of finite type, we explore the drop in entropy under multi-word perturbations.

1.
Introduction

In the study of symbolic dynamics, one often considers perturbations of subshifts by forbidding specific words
[
3
,
14
,
15
,
18
]
. This simple question of deleting a single word from a subshift yields a diverse range of unexpected connections, for instance, in coding theory
[
10
]
, non-transitive games
[
11
,
17
]
or ergodic theory
[
4
]
. Let
X
be a one-dimensional subshift on finite symbols, and let
w\in{\mathcal{L}}(X)
be a word appearing in the language of
X
. We define the perturbed system associated with
w
as,

X_{w}:=\{x\in X\mid x\text{ does not contain }w\text{ as a subword}\}.

Clearly,
X_{w}\subseteq X
is itself a subshift. When
X
is minimal, all perturbations are empty. In the case of non-minimal subshifts, this basic perturbation prompts several natural questions: namely, what guarantees
X_{w}\neq\emptyset
, how to compute the entropy of
X_{w}
, how does the entropy depend on the length of
w
, or for two distinct words
w
and
u
, when is
X_{w}
conjugate to
X_{u}
. This paper investigates these problems, as well as further structural aspects of the perturbations, in the case where
X
is a coded system. The results in this paper serve as preliminary steps toward addressing many future problems, and they generalize those in
[
3
,
14
,
18
]
.

Perturbation by one or multiple words has been studied earlier when the ambient space is a
subshift of finite type
(SFT in short), but has not been extended beyond this setting. It is easy to verify that if
X
is an SFT, so is
X_{w}
. One can ask under what conditions does a structural property of
X
persist in the perturbed system
X_{w}
. We address this problem when
X
is a
coded system
.
Coded systems were introduced by Blanchard and Hansel
[
1
]
and form an important class of subshifts due to their properties and diverse applications
[
6
,
8
,
12
,
16
]
. They are the subshifts represented by countable-state irreducible labeled graphs. Well-known examples of coded systems include irreducible sofic shifts,
{\mathcal{S}}
-gap shifts,
\beta
-shifts, and Dyck shifts. We provide examples to show that perturbations of coded systems may not be coded. However, we prove the following result when
X
is synchronized.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
X
be a synchronized system with a synchronizing word
m
. If
w\in{\mathcal{L}}(X)
is such that
X_{w}
is irreducible,
m
is not a subword of
w
, and
m\in{\mathcal{L}}(X_{w})
, then
X_{w}
is a synchronized system.

Since the sets of admissible words of length
n
satisfy
{\mathcal{L}}_{n}(X_{w})\subseteq{\mathcal{L}}_{n}(X)
for all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, the corresponding topological entropies satisfy
h(X_{w})\leq h(X)
.
From
[
14
]
, for an SFT
X
with entropy
\ln(\lambda)
, the difference
h(X)-h(X_{w})
decays exponentially in length
n=|w|
. A similar exponential bound for multidimensional SFT is obtained in
[
15
]
.
However, this behavior does not necessarily hold for general subshifts.
To illustrate, consider minimal subshifts
X
and
Y
on distinct alphabets with
h(X)<h(Y)
. Then,
Z:=X\sqcup Y
is non-minimal and
h(Z)=h(Y)
. For any word
w\in{\mathcal{L}}(Y)
, the perturbed subshift satisfies
h(Z_{w})=h(X)
, regardless of
|w|
. Although
Z
is not irreducible, it demonstrates that convergence of entropies can fail in general settings.
This motivates the broader question of characterizing those subshifts
X
for which
h(X_{w})\to h(X)
as
|w|\to\infty
, and further, identifying when this convergence occurs at an exponential rate. We prove the following for the perturbations of sofic shifts.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
X
be an irreducible sofic shift. There exist
C(X),N(X)>0
such that for any
w\in{\mathcal{L}}(X)
with
|w|=n>N(X)
,

h(X)-h(X_{w})\leq\frac{C(X)}{n}.

In particular,
h(X_{w})
converges to
h(X)
as
|w|\to\infty
.

Irreducible sofic shifts form a special class of coded systems that are also natural generalization of SFTs. It was introduced by Weiss
[
20
]
and is the smallest collection of subshifts that includes SFTs and is closed under taking factors. In automata theory, they are the subshifts represented by finite-state automata.
In
Theorem

3.1
, we prove that perturbations of sofic shifts are sofic.
Since irreducible sofic shifts can be well approximated by irreducible SFTs, studying perturbations of sofic shifts essentially reduces to studying those of SFTs.
We also study this convergence problem when
X
is an
{\mathcal{S}}
-gap shift.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
{\mathcal{S}}\subseteq{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
and
X=X_{{\mathcal{S}}}
be the
{\mathcal{S}}
-gap shift with entropy
\ln(\lambda_{{\mathcal{S}}})
. Then for
w\in{\mathcal{L}}_{n}(X)
where
w\neq 0u0
for some word
u
containing 1, we have

h(X)-h(X_{w})=O(\lambda_{{\mathcal{S}}}^{-n})
for
n
large enough.

The class of
{\mathcal{S}}
-gap shifts is an important subclass of coded systems, with applications in computer science and coding theory
[
5
,
13
]
.
In
Theorem

4.1
, we explicitly compute the drop in entropy of systems perturbed from
{\mathcal{S}}
-gap shifts which is used to prove the exponential decay stated above.
Currently, the case where
w
begins and ends with
0
remains unresolved, except when
{\mathcal{S}}=\{kd\mid k\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}\}
for some
d\geq 1
(this case is discussed separately in Section
4.1
).

Another aspect of interest concerns the dynamical equivalence of different perturbations. Specifically, given two words
u,w\in{\mathcal{L}}_{n}(X)
, when are the perturbed subshifts
X_{u}
and
X_{w}
conjugate? A necessary condition would be that they both have the same entropy.
The correlation polynomial of a word determines the overlap of the word with itself.
It has been proven to be a fundamental object when it comes to computing the entropy of the system perturbed from an SFT (
Eq.

1
).
When the ambient system is full shift, the entropies of the perturbed systems are the same if and only if their corresponding correlation polynomials are the same. Hence, a necessary condition for two perturbed systems to be conjugate is that they share the same correlation polynomial. A word
w
is called
prime
if its correlation polynomial is given by
z^{|w|-1}
. In
[
3
, Theorem 4.3]
, it was proved that when
X
is a full shift, perturbations by prime words are conjugate.
When
X
is coded, along with the correlations, it also depends on the graph that represents
X
.
The following result generalizes
[
3
, Propositions 4.11 & 4.13]
where
X
is a full shift or a golden mean shift, respectively.
Let
{\mathcal{G}}
be a labeled graph representing
X
. We define
S_{{\mathcal{G}}}(w)
(respectively,
R_{{\mathcal{G}}}(w)
) as the set of initial (respectively, terminal) vertices of all finite walks labeled
w
in
{\mathcal{G}}
(defined explicitly in
Section

3
). We refer to
Definition

1
for the notations on the correlation.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
X
be a coded system, and
u,w\in{\mathcal{L}}(X)
be two words of the same length, say
n
, such that
(u,u)=(w,w)
and
(u,w)=(w,u)=(u,u)\setminus\{n-1\}
. If there exists a presentation
{\mathcal{G}}
of
X
such that
S_{{\mathcal{G}}}(u)=S_{{\mathcal{G}}}(w)
and
R_{{\mathcal{G}}}(u)=R_{{\mathcal{G}}}(w)
, then
X_{u}
and
X_{w}
are conjugates. In particular,
h(X_{u})=h(X_{w})
.

Instead of perturbing a subshift with a single word, one can also ask the above questions on perturbation by multiple words. Let
{\mathcal{K}}
be a finite collection of allowed words in
X
and let
X_{\mathcal{K}}
denote the collection of sequences from
X
that do not contain words from
{\mathcal{K}}
as subwords. This multi-word perturbation of SFT was explored in
[
18
]
. By studying the complexity function (
n\mapsto\#{\mathcal{L}}_{n}(X_{{\mathcal{K}}})
), the author obtained a bound on the entropy that is linear in the length of the shortest word.
We use this bound in order to prove
Theorem

1.2
.
However, an exponential bound as in
[
14
]
is not yet obtained. In
Theorem

5.1
, we calculate the entropy of the system perturbed by multiple words. This expression demonstrates exponential entropy decay in the perturbed system (though we do not prove it explicitly).
As a special case, when
X
is a full shift on
N
symbols, in
Theorem

5.5
, we calculate the convergence of
\dfrac{h(X_{{\mathcal{K}}_{n}})-h(X)}{N^{-n}}
as
n\to\infty
where
{\mathcal{K}}_{n}
is a finite collection of words of length
n
, which improves the result in
[
18
]
.
We also give an application of this convergence in computing the escape rate into a shrinking disconnected hole.

2.
Preliminaries and existing results

2.1.
Symbolic dynamical systems

Let
{\mathcal{A}}
be a finite alphabet. A
word
is a finite sequence (string) with symbols from
{\mathcal{A}}
.
If
w=w_{1}\dots w_{n}
is a word, then
|w|=n
denotes the length of
w
.
For two words
u,w
, we say that
u
is a
subword
of
w
, denoted as
u\prec w
, if there exist two words
t,v
(possibly empty) such that
w=tuv
.

Let
{\mathcal{A}}^{{\mathbb{Z}}}
denote the collection of all bi-infinite sequences with symbols from
{\mathcal{A}}
. It is a compact metric space with respect to the pro-discrete topology. For
x,y\in{\mathcal{A}}^{{\mathbb{Z}}},
we define the metric,
d(x,y)=2^{-k}
where
k=\min\{|i|:\ x_{i}\neq y_{i}\}
.
Define the
left shift map

S:{\mathcal{A}}^{\mathbb{Z}}\to{\mathcal{A}}^{\mathbb{Z}}
as
(S(x))_{i}=x_{i+1}
where
x=(x_{i})_{i\in{\mathbb{Z}}}
. A
subshift

X\subseteq{\mathcal{A}}^{\mathbb{Z}}
is a closed
S
-invariant subset, with the induced topology. When
X={\mathcal{A}}^{\mathbb{Z}}
we call it a
full shift
.

For a given subshift
X
, let
x=(x_{i})_{i\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\in X
. For
i\leq j
, we denote
x_{[i,j]}=x_{i}x_{i+1}\dots x_{j-1}x_{j}
.
A word
w
with symbols from
{\mathcal{A}}
is said to be
allowed
in
X
if
w
is a subword of a sequence in
X
, that is, there exists a sequence
x=(x_{i})_{i\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\in X
and
i\leq j
such that
w=x_{[i,j]}
. For
n\geq 1
, let
\mathcal{L}_{n}(X)
denote the collection of all allowed words of length
n
in
X
, and let
{\mathcal{L}}_{0}(X)
consist of the empty word. We define
\mathcal{L}(X)=\bigcup_{n\geq 0}\mathcal{L}_{n}(X)
as the
language
of
X
. Recall that the
topological entropy
of a subshift
X
is given by,

h(X)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\ln\#(\mathcal{L}_{n}(X)),

where
\#(.)
denotes the cardinality of a set. The subshift
X
is said to be
minimal
if for a given
x\in X
, the orbit
\{S^{n}x\mid n\in{\mathbb{Z}}\}
is dense in
X
. The subshift
X
is said to be
irreducible
, if for any
(u,w)\in{\mathcal{L}}(X)^{2}
, there exists
v\in{\mathcal{L}}(X)
such that
uvw\in{\mathcal{L}}(X)
. If
X
is not irreducible, it is called
reducible
.

Let
X,Y
be two subshifts. We say that
X
is
conjugate
to
Y
if there exists a homeomorphism
\phi:X\to Y
such that
\phi\circ S=S\circ\phi
. In this case, the map
\phi
is called a
conjugacy map
from
X
to
Y
. For
X,Y
a
sliding block code

\psi:X\to Y
is defined by some local rule as
y_{i}=\Psi(x_{[i-n,i+m]})
, for
i\in{\mathbb{Z}}
where
\Psi:{\mathcal{L}}_{n+m+1}(X)\to{\mathcal{L}}_{1}(Y)
is a block code for some fixed
m,n\geq 0
. A conjugacy between two subshifts is always a bijective sliding block code
[
13
]
. When the sliding block code is surjective,
Y
is a
factor
of
X
.

Let
{\mathcal{F}}
be a finite collection of words with symbols from
{\mathcal{A}}
. Define a subshift,

X_{{\mathcal{F}}}:=\{x\in{\mathcal{A}}^{{\mathbb{Z}}}\mid x\text{ does not contain words from ${\mathcal{F}}$ as subwords}\}.

A subshift
X
is said to be a
subshift of finite type (SFT)
if there exists a finite set
{\mathcal{F}}
of words of finite length with symbols from
{\mathcal{A}}
, such that
X=X_{{\mathcal{F}}}
. For example, when
{\mathcal{A}}=\{0,1\}
and
{\mathcal{F}}=\{11\}
, the SFT
X_{{\mathcal{F}}}
that we obtain is the golden mean shift.

Let
G=(V,E)
be a directed graph. We define an
edge shift
on
G
as,

X_{G}=\{e\in E^{{\mathbb{Z}}}\mid r(e_{i})=s(e_{i+1})\text{ for every }i\in{\mathbb{Z}}\},

where
s:E\to V
and
r:E\to V
are the source and the range map of edges. Clearly,
X_{G}
is a subshift of finite type with the forbidden collection
{\mathcal{F}}=\{ee^{\prime}\mid e,e^{\prime}\in E\text{ and }r(e)\neq s(e^{\prime})\}
. Using the higher block code representation, it is not difficult to see that any given subshift of finite type is conjugate to an edge shift
[
13
]
.
Let
A
be an irreducible matrix

That is, for every

A graph

The adjacency matrix of an irreducible graph is irreducible.

2.2.
Perturbations of subshifts of finite type

Let
G

(1)

Definition 1
.

Let

be two words with symbols from
{\mathcal{A}},
and

(u,w)=\{\ell\mid 0\leq\ell\leq n-1\text{ and }b_{\ell}=1\}.

For a word

, then we call

. For two distinct words

Due to the expression of topological entropy in terms of the correlation polynomial, it is also not difficult to see that the entropy

3.
Perturbations of coded systems

Definition 2
.

For a given finite alphabet
{\mathcal{A}}
and a countable irreducible directed graph
G=(V,E)
, we define the edge label as a map
\ell:E\to{\mathcal{A}}
. The tuple
{\mathcal{G}}=(G,\ell)
is a labeled directed graph. We define a subshift
X_{{\mathcal{G}}}
to be the closure of

\{x\in{\mathcal{A}}^{{\mathbb{Z}}}\mid\exists e\in E^{{\mathbb{Z}}}\text{ such that }r(e_{i})=s(e_{i+1})\text{ and }\ell(e_{i})=x_{i},\ \forall i\in{\mathbb{Z}}\}.

A subshift
X
is said to be
coded
if there exists a labeled countable irreducible directed graph
{\mathcal{G}}
such that
X=X_{{\mathcal{G}}}
. In this case, we say that
{\mathcal{G}}
is a
presentation
of
X_{{\mathcal{G}}}
.

Clearly, coded systems are irreducible. When
G
is a finite directed graph (not necessarily irreducible), we call the subshift a
sofic shift
. For a finite directed graph where the labeling is identity,
\ell:E\to E
, the resulting sofic shift is a subshift of finite type.
An irreducible sofic shift has an irreducible labeled graph representation and hence is a coded system. Hence, all the irreducible SFTs are also coded systems.

X_{w}=X_{{\mathcal{S}}_{0}}
where
S_{0}=\{s\in{\mathcal{S}}\mid s<n\}.
Thus,
X_{w}
is an irreducible SFT and is therefore synchronized. This is a very special case. If
k\in{\mathcal{S}}
, then
m=1\overline{0}^{k}1\in{\mathcal{L}}(X_{{\mathcal{S}}})
is also a synchronizing word. For any word
w\in{\mathcal{L}}(X_{{\mathcal{S}}})
that does not contain
m
as a subword, we see that
X_{w}
is a synchronizing system provided it is irreducible.
For instance, we can choose
k=min_{i}\{i\in{\mathcal{S}}\}
.
This gives us a lot more non-trivial examples.

The following example shows that
X_{w}
being irreducible is a necessary condition in
Theorem

1.1
. That is, choosing
w\in{\mathcal{L}}(X)
so that
m\not\prec w
and
m\in{\mathcal{L}}(X_{w})
do not guarantee that
X_{w}
is irreducible. Let
X
be the full shift on two symbols. Then we can choose
m=11
. Let
w=10
. Here,
m\not\prec w
and
m\in{\mathcal{L}}(X_{w})
since
\overline{1}\in X_{w}
. But
X_{w}
is not irreducible.

Let
X
be a coded system and let
{\mathcal{G}}=(V,E,\ell)
be a presentation of
X
. We define
S_{{\mathcal{G}}}(w)=\{v\in V\mid s(w)=v\}
and
R_{{\mathcal{G}}}(w)=\{v\in V\mid r(w)=v\}
where
s(w),r(w)
are the source and range of all possible walks labeled
w
in
{\mathcal{G}}
, respectively. We will now prove
Theorem

1.4
.

Proof of
Theorem

1.4
.

Let
{\mathcal{A}}
be the alphabet and
|u|=|w|=n
.
The proof is similar to that of
[
3
, Proposition 4.1]
. We define a map
\phi:X_{u}\to X_{w}
that replaces all the occurrences of
w
in
X_{u}
by
u
.
The map
\phi
is a sliding block code induced by
\Phi:{\mathcal{A}}^{2n-1}\to{\mathcal{A}}
defined as,

\Phi(x_{[-n+1,n-1]})=\begin{cases}u_{i}\ \text{ if }x_{[1-i,n-i]}=w\text{ for some }1\leq i\leq n\\
w_{i}\ \text{ if }x_{[1-i,n-i]}=u\text{ for some }1\leq i\leq n\\
x_{0}\ \text{ otherwise}.\end{cases}

One can easily verify that
\Phi
is well-defined because the conditions on the correlations of
u,w
. Consider the sliding block code,
\phi^{\prime}:{\mathcal{A}}^{{\mathbb{Z}}}\to{\mathcal{A}}^{{\mathbb{Z}}}
.
For any sequence
x\in{\mathcal{A}}^{{\mathbb{Z}}}
, the map
\phi^{\prime}
replaces the appearances of
u
in
x
by
w
and
w
in
x
by
u
. Clearly,
\phi^{\prime}
is an involution.

It is enough to show that the restriction map
\phi:X_{u}\to{\mathcal{A}}^{{\mathbb{Z}}}
is mapped onto
X_{w}
. Let
{\mathcal{G}}
be the representation of
X
such that
S_{{\mathcal{G}}}(u)=S_{{\mathcal{G}}}(w)
and
R_{{\mathcal{G}}}(u)=R_{{\mathcal{G}}}(w)
. For
x\in X_{u}
,
x
does not contain
u
as a subword, but it may contain
w
as its subword. Moreover,
either
x
is represented by a bi-infinite walk in
{\mathcal{G}}
or it is a limit point of the set of all bi-infinite walks in
{\mathcal{G}}
. If
x
is represented by a walk in
{\mathcal{G}}
, since
S_{{\mathcal{G}}}(u)=S_{{\mathcal{G}}}(w)
and
R_{{\mathcal{G}}}(u)=R_{{\mathcal{G}}}(w)
, replacing
w
by
u
is still an admissible walk in
{\mathcal{G}}
. Hence
\phi(x)\in X_{w}
. Let
(x_{i})_{i\geq 0}
be a sequence of bi-infinite walks in
{\mathcal{G}}
, each of them does not contain
u
as a subword, such that
\lim_{i\to\infty}x_{i}=x
. For any given
N
, there exists
k
such that for all
i>k
,
x_{[-N,N]}=x_{i_{[-N,N]}}
. Let
y_{i}=\phi(x_{i})\in X_{w}
. Clearly,
\phi(x)\in X_{w}
as it is a limit point of
(y_{i})_{i\geq 0}
. Using a similar argument, we can show that
\phi(X_{w})\subseteq X_{u}
and the result follows as
\phi
is an involution.
∎

Remark 1

3.1.
Perturbations of sofic shifts

In this section, we restrict ourselves to the case where
X
is a sofic shift. Let
{\mathcal{G}}=(G,\ell)
be a finite labeled directed graph that represents
X
.

Let
\ell_{\infty}:X_{G}\to X_{{\mathcal{G}}}
be the sliding block code defined as
\ell_{\infty}(y)_{i}=\ell(y_{i})
, where the block code is
\ell:{\mathcal{L}}_{1}(X_{G})\to{\mathcal{L}}_{1}(X_{\mathcal{G}})
. This is clearly surjective and hence a factor map. In fact, a subshift is sofic if and only if it is a factor of a subshift of finite type
[
13
]
.

A labeled directed graph is called
right-resolving
if distinct edges in
G
starting from the same vertex are labeled distinctly.
A sofic shift
X
is said to have a right-resolving presentation if there exists a right-resolving labeled graph
{\mathcal{G}}=(G,\ell)
such that
X=X_{{\mathcal{G}}}
. Any sofic shift
X
has a right-resolving presentation
{\mathcal{G}}=(G,\ell)
where
h(X)=h(X_{G})
. One can choose
{\mathcal{G}}
to be an irreducible right-resolving graph when
X
is also irreducible.
We can naturally extend the map
\ell:{\mathcal{L}}(X_{G})\to{\mathcal{L}}(X_{\mathcal{G}})
.
If we choose
{\mathcal{G}}
to be right-resolving, for a word
w\in{\mathcal{L}}_{n}(X_{{\mathcal{G}}}),
there are at most
r
pre-images in
{\mathcal{L}}_{n}(X_{G})
by
\ell
where
r
is the number of vertices in
G
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
X
be a sofic shift and
w\in{\mathcal{L}}(X)
. Then
X_{w}
is a sofic shift.

Proof.

We assume
X_{w}\neq\emptyset
.
Let
{\mathcal{G}}=(G,\ell)
be a right-resolving presentation of
X
such that
h(X)=h(X_{G})
. There exists a factor map
\ell_{\infty}:X_{G}\to X
. We denote
Y=X_{G}
. Let
\ell^{-1}(w)=\{u_{1},\dots,u_{s}\}
and let
Y_{u_{1},\dots,u_{s}}\subseteq Y
be the subshift that consists of sequences that do not contain
u_{1},\dots,u_{s}
as subwords.

We consider the restriction map
\ell_{\infty}:Y_{u_{1},\dots,u_{s}}\to X
. If
y\in Y
does not contain
u_{1},\dots,u_{s}
, then
\ell_{\infty}(y)
does not contain
w
. Hence,
\ell_{\infty}(Y_{u_{1},\dots,u_{s}})\subseteq X_{w}
. Also, for any sequence
x\in X_{w}
, its pre-image
\ell_{\infty}^{-1}(x)
contains sequences that do not have
\ell^{-1}(w)
as subwords. Hence,
\ell_{\infty}^{\prime}:Y_{u_{1},\dots,u_{s}}\to X_{w}
is a sliding block code that is surjective, and thus a factor map. Clearly,
Y_{u_{1},\dots,u_{s}}
is an SFT and therefore
X_{w}
is sofic.
∎

Although the above theorem states that perturbations of irreducible sofic shifts remain sofic, it does not specify the conditions under which they are irreducible.
Note that one cannot expect to guarantee the irreducibility of the perturbed system simply by considering longer words. For example, in case of full shift
X
on 2 symbols, for each length
k\geq 2
, there exists a word
w\in{\mathcal{L}}_{k}(X)
such that
X_{w}
is reducible
[
3
, Proposition 3.5]
.

Theorem
3.1
clearly demands us to look at the perturbation of SFTs by multiple words. For a subshift
X
, let
{\mathcal{K}}\subseteq{\mathcal{L}}(X)
be a finite collection of allowed words. We define the multi-word perturbation of
X
by
{\mathcal{K}}
, denoted as
X_{{\mathcal{K}}}
, to be the collection of all sequences in
X
that do not contain words from
{\mathcal{K}}
as subwords. We discuss more about the multi-word perturbations of SFTs in Section
5
. However, we state the following result that allows us to study the perturbation of irreducible sofic shifts.

Theorem 3.2
.

[
18
, Theorem 1]

Let
X
be an irreducible subshift of finite type.
There exist
C(X),N(X)>0
such that for a collection of finite allowed words
{\mathcal{K}}
in
X
with the length of the shortest word, say
n>N(X)
, we have

h(X)-h(X_{{\mathcal{K}}})\leq\frac{C}{n}.

Using the approximation of sofic shifts by SFTs,
Theorem

1.2
is immediate from
Theorem

3.2
.

Proof of
Theorem

1.2
.

Let
{\mathcal{G}}=(G,\ell)
be an irreducible, right-resolving presentation of
X
such that
h(X)=h(Y)
where
Y=X_{G}
.
Let
\ell^{-1}(w)=\{u_{1},\dots,u_{s}\}
. If
r
is the number of vertices of
G
, then
s\leq r
. Also,
r\left(\#{\mathcal{L}}_{n}(X_{w})\right)\geq\#{\mathcal{L}}_{n}(Y_{u_{1},\dots,u_{s}})
for all
n\geq 1
implies that
h(Y_{u_{1},\dots,u_{s}})=h(X_{w}).
Hence, the result follows from Theorem
3.2
.
∎

Again, to get an exponential decay, we need to obtain an exponential decay for the multi-word perturbation of SFT.

4.
Perturbations of
{\mathcal{S}}
-gap shifts

Let
{\mathcal{S}}=\{s_{i}\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}\mid s_{i}<s_{i+1},i\geq 0\}
be a nonempty subset of non-negative integers. Recall the definition of the
{\mathcal{S}}
-gap shift
X_{{\mathcal{S}}}
from Example
1
.
A sequence
x\in\{0,1\}^{{\mathbb{Z}}}
belongs to
X_{{\mathcal{S}}}
if each subword of the form
1\overline{0}^{n}1
that appears in
x
satisfies
n\in{\mathcal{S}}
.
We have
h(X_{\mathcal{S}})=\ln(\lambda_{\mathcal{S}})

[
19
]
where
\lambda_{\mathcal{S}}
is the unique real root of

\sum_{s\in{\mathcal{S}}}\frac{1}{z^{s+1}}=1.

For a given
{\mathcal{S}}\subseteq{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
, let
X:=X_{{\mathcal{S}}}
and
w\in{\mathcal{L}}(X)
. We define
X_{w}
as the collection of all sequences that do not contain
w
as a subword. Any
{\mathcal{S}}
-gap shift is entropy-minimizing and hence
h(X_{w})<h(X)

[
9
]
.
As discussed in
Example

2
, when
w=\overline{0}^{n}
for
n\geq 1
, the perturbed system
X_{w}=X_{{\mathcal{S}}_{0}}
is an SFT where
S_{0}=\{s\in{\mathcal{S}}\mid s<n\}.
Hence,
h(X_{w})=\ln(\lambda_{w})
where
\lambda_{w}
is the unique real zero of

\sum_{s\in{\mathcal{S}},\ s<n}\frac{1}{z^{s+1}}=1.

Clearly, as
n\to\infty,
the entropy
h(X_{w})
converges to
h(X)
.
We are interested in calculating
h(X_{w})
for general
w
. Before that, we present some notations. We define the characteristic function
\chi_{{\mathcal{S}}}:{\mathbb{N}}_{0}\to\{0,1\}
as
\chi_{{\mathcal{S}}}(n)=1
if and only if
n\in{\mathcal{S}}
.

For
w\in{\mathcal{L}}(X)
which is not of the type
\overline{0}^{n}
, let
\tilde{w}
be the smallest word such that
w\prec\tilde{w}
and
\tilde{w}=\overline{0}^{k}u\overline{0}^{k^{\prime}}
where
k,k^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{S}}\cup\{0\}
and
u
is a word that starts and ends with 1. For example, let
{\mathcal{S}}=\{0,3,6,\dots\}
and
w=00100010000
. Here,
\tilde{w}=00010001000000.
Observe that,
X_{w}=X_{\tilde{w}}
.

In this section, we assume that
w\in{\mathcal{L}}(X)
does not start and end with 0 simultaneously (hence, the words of the type
\overline{0}^{n}
are already ruled out).
Let
\tilde{w}
be as before.
Assume
\tilde{w}
begins with
m
number of zeros or ends with
m
number of zeros for
m\in{\mathcal{S}}
(note that
\tilde{w}
may begin with 0, end with 0, or do neither; however, it cannot do both). When
w
begins and ends with 1, we take
m=0
. We denote,

\displaystyle T_{{\mathcal{S}}}(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\chi_{{\mathcal{S}}}(n)}{z^{n+1}}

\displaystyle{\mathcal{S}}_{m}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{n-m\mid n\in{\mathcal{S}},n\geq m\}.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
w\in{\mathcal{L}}(X)
be of the form
w=u\overline{0}^{m}
or
w=\overline{0}^{m}u
for some
u
that starts and ends with 1 and
m\geq 0
. Then the generating function
F(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}f(n)z^{-n}
where
f(n)=\#{\mathcal{L}}_{n}(X_{w})
is given by,

F(z)=\frac{z}{z-1}\frac{c_{\tilde{w}}(z)(T_{{\mathcal{S}}^{c}}(z)+z)-T_{{\mathcal{S}}^{c}_{m}}(z)}{(1-T_{{\mathcal{S}}}(z))c_{\tilde{w}}(z)+T_{{\mathcal{S}}_{m}}(z)},

where
{\mathcal{S}}^{c}={\mathbb{N}}_{0}\setminus{\mathcal{S}}
and
{\mathcal{S}}_{m}^{c}={\mathbb{N}}_{0}\setminus{\mathcal{S}}_{m}
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, assume that
w=\tilde{w}
. Let
\overline{w}
denote the reverse of
w
, that is,
\overline{w}=w_{n}\dots w_{1}
for
w=w_{1}\dots w_{n}
. Note that, there is a bijection from
{\mathcal{L}}_{n}(X_{w})
to
{\mathcal{L}}_{n}(X_{\overline{w}})
by reversing. Hence
h(X_{w})=h(X_{\overline{w}})
. It is also each to check that
c_{w}(z)=c_{\overline{w}}(z)
. This allows us to assume without loss of generality that
w
starts with
m
number of zeros for some
m\in{\mathcal{S}}\cup\{0\}
and ends with 1.

Let
f_{\mathcal{S}}(n)
be the number of words in
{\mathcal{L}}_{n}(X_{w})
that end with
t
number of zeroes for some
t\in{\mathcal{S}}
,
f_{{\mathcal{S}}_{m}}(n)
be the number of words in
{\mathcal{L}}_{n}(X_{w})
that end with
t
number of zeroes for some
t\in{\mathcal{S}}_{m}
,
f_{1}(n)
be the number of words in
{\mathcal{L}}_{n}(X_{w})
that end with 1, and
g_{w}(n)
be the number of words in
{\mathcal{L}}_{n}(X)
in which the word
w
occurs exactly once and it is at the end. Let
f_{1}(0)=1
,
f_{\mathcal{S}}(0)=\chi_{\mathcal{S}}(0)
,
f_{{\mathcal{S}}_{m}}(0)=\chi_{{\mathcal{S}}_{m}}(0)
and
g_{w}(0)=0
.

Note that each word in
{\mathcal{L}}_{n}(X_{w})
is either of the form
\overline{0}^{n}
or
u\overline{0}^{n-k}
where
u
is a word in
{\mathcal{L}}_{k}(X_{w})
that ends with 1. On the other hand, since
w
ends with 1, for any
u\in{\mathcal{L}}_{k}(X_{w})
that ends with 1, the word
u\overline{0}^{n-k}\in{\mathcal{L}}_{n}(X_{w})
. Hence,

f(n)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}f_{1}(k).

Also, each word that is counted in
f_{\mathcal{S}}(n)
is either of the form
\overline{0}^{n}
, if
n\in{\mathcal{S}}
or
u\overline{0}^{k}
where
u
is a word in
{\mathcal{L}}_{n-k}(X_{w})
that ends with 1 and
k\in{\mathcal{S}}
. On the other hand, since
w
ends with 1, for each
k\in{\mathcal{S}}
and
u\in{\mathcal{L}}_{n-k}(X_{w})
that ends with 1, the word
u\overline{0}^{k}\in{\mathcal{L}}_{n}(X_{w})
. Hence,

f_{\mathcal{S}}(n)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}f_{1}(n-k)\chi_{\mathcal{S}}(k).

Similarly,

f_{{\mathcal{S}}_{m}}(n)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}f_{1}(n-k)\chi_{{\mathcal{S}}_{m}}(k).

Consider a word
u\in{\mathcal{L}}_{n}(X_{w})
. Note that
u0
is a word in
{\mathcal{L}}_{n+1}(X_{w})
. Also, when
u
ends with
t
number of zeros for some
t\in{\mathcal{S}}
, then
u1\in{\mathcal{L}}_{n+1}(X_{w})
or
u1
ends with
w
and it is the only occurrence of
w
in
u1
. The only word we do not count in
f_{{\mathcal{S}}}(n)
but contribute to a word in
f(n+1)
or
g_{w}(n+1)
is
\overline{0}^{n}1
when
n\notin{\mathcal{S}}
. Hence,

f(n)+f_{\mathcal{S}}(n)+1-\chi_{\mathcal{S}}(n)=f(n+1)+g_{w}(n+1).

In order to get the last recurrence relation, consider a word
u
counted in
f_{{\mathcal{S}}_{m}}(n)
and attach
w
at the end.
Firstly, note that this is an allowed word in
X
since
t+m\in{\mathcal{S}}
for any
t\in{\mathcal{S}}_{m}
. But this need not always be counted in
g_{w}(n+|w|)
as other appearances of
w
may occur in between. Let
uw=v_{1}\dots v_{n}v_{n+1}\dots v_{n+|w|}
and let
k
be the first occurrence of
w
in
uw
, that is,
v_{k-|w|+1}\dots v_{k}=w
. Since
u\in{\mathcal{L}}_{n}(X_{w})
,
k>n
. Also,
w_{|w|-(k-n)+1}=w_{1},\dots,w_{|w|}=w_{k-n}
. This implies that
k-n\in(w,w)
and that this word is counted in
g_{w}(k)
. Conversely, for
k-n\in(w,w)
, any word counted in
g_{w}(k)
is coming from a concatenation of a word counted in
f_{{\mathcal{S}}_{m}}(n)
and
w
, except for
\overline{0}^{n}w
for
n\notin{\mathcal{S}}_{m}
. Hence,

f_{{\mathcal{S}}_{m}}(n)+1-\chi_{{\mathcal{S}}_{m}}(n)=\sum_{t\in(w,w)}g_{w}(n+t).

We obtain the result by multiplying each of these equations by
z^{-n}
and summing for all
n\geq 0
and then solving the linear equations.
∎

Corollary 4.2
.

h(X_{w})=\ln(\lambda_{w})
where
\lambda_{w}
is the largest real zero of
f_{w}(z)=(1-T_{{\mathcal{S}}}(z))c_{\tilde{w}}(z)+T_{{\mathcal{S}}_{m}}(z).

Proof.

First, we prove that
f_{w}(z)
has a root in the interval
(1,\lambda_{{\mathcal{S}}})
where
\lambda_{{\mathcal{S}}}
is the unique real root of
1-T_{{\mathcal{S}}}(z).
For all
z\in(1,\lambda_{{\mathcal{S}}}),
we have
T_{{\mathcal{S}}_{m}}(z)\leq\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{z^{n+1}}=\frac{1}{z-1}.
Hence
(z-1)f_{w}(z)\leq(z-1)(1-T_{{\mathcal{S}}}(z))c_{\tilde{w}}(z)+1.
Denote this polynomial on the right hand side to be
g_{w}(z).
Note that as
z\to 1^{+},

g_{w}(z)
converges to
-\infty.
Hence,
f_{w}(z^{\prime})
is negative for some
z^{\prime}\in(1,\lambda_{{\mathcal{S}}})
. Also, at
z\geq\lambda_{{\mathcal{S}}},
we have
f_{w}(\lambda_{{\mathcal{S}}})>0
. Hence,
f_{w}(z)
has a root in
(1,\lambda_{{\mathcal{S}}}).
Let
\lambda_{w}
denote the largest real root of
f_{w}(z)
in this interval.

If
z_{0}>1
is a root of
f_{w}(z)
, then we show that
z_{0}
is not a root for
c_{\tilde{w}}(z)(T_{{\mathcal{S}}^{c}}(z)+z)-T_{{\mathcal{S}}_{m}^{c}}(z).
This will clearly imply that
\lambda_{w}
is the largest real pole of
F(z)
(notation as in
Theorem

4.1
) and hence our result.

Let us assume, on the contrary, that there exists
z_{0}>1
such that

(1-T_{{\mathcal{S}}}(z_{0}))c_{\tilde{w}}(z_{0})+T_{{\mathcal{S}}_{m}}(z_{0})=c_{\tilde{w}}(z_{0})(T_{{\mathcal{S}}^{c}}(z_{0})+z_{0})-T_{{\mathcal{S}}^{c}_{m}}(z_{0}).

Using the fact that
T_{{\mathcal{S}}}(z)+T_{{\mathcal{S}}^{c}}(z)=\frac{1}{z-1}=T_{{\mathcal{S}}_{m}}(z)+T_{{\mathcal{S}}_{m}^{c}}(z)
, the above equation can be reduced to

(z_{0}^{2}-2z_{0}+2)c_{\tilde{w}}(z_{0})=1
. As
z_{0}>1,
we have
z_{0}^{2}-2z_{0}+2>1
which implies that
c_{\tilde{w}}(z_{0})<1
which is not possible.
∎

Remarks 1
.

For
m\in{\mathcal{S}}\cup\{0\}
, let

C_{m,n}=\{w\in{\mathcal{L}}_{n}(X_{{\mathcal{S}}})\mid w\text{ starts with }m\text{ number of zeroes and ends with }1\}.

We can easily conclude the following.

(1)

For
u,w\in C_{m,n}
, when
c_{u}(z)=c_{w}(z)
, then
h(X_{u})=h(X_{w})
. This was also observed in
Example

3
(3).

(2)

For
u,w\in C_{m,n}
, when
c_{u}(z)<c_{w}(z)
for all
z\in(1,\lambda_{{\mathcal{S}}})
, then
h(X_{u})<h(X_{w})
. In particular, the minimum is always achieved by the prime words.

We now prove
Theorem

1.3
.

Proof of
Theorem

1.3
.

We assume that
{\mathcal{S}}
is infinite, as otherwise
X_{{\mathcal{S}}}
is an SFT and it follows from
[

, Theorem 3]
.
We first assume that
w\neq\overline{0}^{k}
for some
k\geq 1
.
As before, let
\lambda_{w}
be the largest root of
f_{w}(z)
in the interval
(1,\lambda_{{\mathcal{S}}}),

5.
Multi-word perturbations of subshifts of finite type

In this section, we consider the perturbations of subshifts of finite type by more than one word.
From Section
3.1
, clearly, in order to study the drop in entropy of perturbations of the sofic shift by an allowed word, it is important to know the drop in entropy of perturbations of subshifts of finite type by multiple allowed words. In the following result, we give an explicit expression.

Let
A
be an
N\times N
irreducible matrix of non-negative integers,
G_{A}
be the associated directed graph, and
X=X_{A}
be the irreducible subshift of finite type associated with
G_{A}=(V,E)
, where
V=\{1,\dots,N\}
. For a word
w\in{\mathcal{L}}(X)
, let
s(w),r(w)\in V
be the source and range maps of
w
, respectively.

Let
{\mathcal{K}}=\{w_{1},\dots,w_{k}\}\subseteq{\mathcal{L}}(X)
be a reduced collection of allowed words, that is, for
i\neq j
,
w_{i}\not\prec w_{j}
. We denote,
X_{{\mathcal{K}}}
to be the perturbation of
X
by
{\mathcal{K}}
. Let
f(n)=\#{\mathcal{L}}_{n}(X_{{\mathcal{K}}})
and
F(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}f(n)z^{-n}
. We have the following result on the rational form of
F(z)
. For
1\leq s,t\leq N
, let
\chi_{s,t}=1
if
s=t
and
\chi_{s,t}=0
, otherwise.

Theorem 5.1
.

F(z)=\sum_{i=1}^{N}P^{-1}(z)_{i,1}
where

P(z)=\begin{pmatrix}zI-A&R(z)\\
S(z)&-z{\mathcal{M}}(z)\end{pmatrix},

where
I
is the identity matrix of order
N
,
R(z)
is an
N\times k
matrix such that
R(z)_{i,j}=z\chi_{i,r(w_{j})}
,
S(z)
is a
k\times N
matrix such that
S(z)_{i,j}=\chi_{j,s(w_{i})}
and
{\mathcal{M}}(z)_{i,j}=(w_{j},w_{i})_{z}
. Moreover,
h(X_{{\mathcal{K}}})=\ln(\lambda_{{\mathcal{K}}})
where
\lambda_{{\mathcal{K}}}
is the largest real pole of
F(z)
.

Proof.

For
n\geq 0
and
1\leq j\leq N
, let
f(n,j)
be the number of words in
{\mathcal{L}}_{n}(X_{\mathcal{K}})
that end at the vertex
j
, when these words are considered as walks in
G_{A}
. For
v\in{\mathcal{K}}
, let
g(n,v)
be the number of words in
{\mathcal{L}}_{n}(X)
that end with
v
and do not contain any appearance of words from
{\mathcal{K}}
except
v
at the end. We assume
f(0,1)=1,f(0,j)=0
for
1<j\leq N
, and
g(0,v)=0
for all
v\in{\mathcal{K}}
. For
1\leq i\leq N
, let
F_{i}(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}f(n,i)z^{-n}
and for
v\in{\mathcal{K}}
, let
G_{v}(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}g(n,v)z^{-n}
.

We get the recurrence relations using arguments similar to those in
[
11
]
.
For each word counted in
f(n,j)
, we attach all the symbols corresponding to the edges from
j
to
i
at the end. There are
f(n,j)A_{j,i}
many such words. These words are either counted in
f(n+1,i)
or counted in
g(n+1,v)
for some
v\in{\mathcal{K}}
where
r(v)=i
. Hence for each
1\leq i\leq N
,

\sum_{j=1}^{N}f(n,j)A_{j,i}=f(n+1,i)+\sum_{v\in{\mathcal{K}}}\chi_{i,r(v)}g(n+1,v).

Now, for the second set of equations, fix
v\in{\mathcal{K}}
. Consider a word counted in
f(n,j)
and attach
v
at the end. This attachment is admissible only if
\chi_{j,s(v)}=1
. By tracking the first place a word from
{\mathcal{K}}
appears, we get terms from the nonempty correlations. Hence,

\sum_{j=1}^{N}f(n,j)\chi_{j,s(v)}=\sum_{t\in(u,v)}g(n+t,u).

We get the following system of linear equations by multiplying these equations by
z^{-n}
and adding from
0
to
\infty
;

P(z)\begin{pmatrix}F_{1}(z)\\
\vdots\\
F_{N}(z)\\
G_{w_{1}}(z)\\
\vdots\\
G_{w_{k}}(z)\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}z\\
0\\
\vdots\\
0\end{pmatrix}.

Using the formula for the radius of convergence of
F(z)
, it is very easy to see that if
\lambda_{{\mathcal{K}}}
is the largest real pole of
F(z)
, then
h(X_{{\mathcal{K}}})=\ln(\lambda_{{\mathcal{K}}})
.
∎

Remark 3
.

By

5.1
, in order to get the entropy of the perturbed system, we are interested in finding the largest real root of determinant of

. As

has a block form, we have

\det(P(z))=m(z)^{-k+1}\det(-zm(z){\mathcal{M}}(z)+Q(z)),

where

is the characteristic polynomial of

and

. Since the largest real root
\lambda_{{\mathcal{K}}}
is strictly smaller that the Perron root
\lambda_{A}
of

, we are interested in the largest real root of
\det(-zm(z){\mathcal{M}}(z)+Q(z))
. Expanding this, we get

\det(-zm(z){\mathcal{M}}(z)+Q(z))=\det(-zm(z){\mathcal{M}}(z))+L(z)=(-zm(z))^{k}\det({\mathcal{M}}(z))+L(z),

where

m^{\prime}(\lambda)\neq 0

). Then, at

(\lambda_{A}-\lambda_{{\mathcal{K}}})^{k}=\frac{-L(\lambda_{{\mathcal{K}}})}{(-\lambda_{{\mathcal{K}}})^{k}\det({\mathcal{M}}(\lambda_{{\mathcal{K}}}))}.

Note that the diagonal entries of
{\mathcal{M}}(z)
has leading term

and off-diagonal entries have leading term at most

. Using this,
one may show that

for

for

large enough. The argument is similar to the proof of

. However, due to the extreme complexity of the calculations involved in this approach, we do not present a detailed proof. The provided details merely serve to validate the exponential decay of the drop in entropy. Nevertheless, in the following section, we provide a special case of this when the ambient space is a full shift.

5.1.
Exponential decay rate for multi-word perturbation of full shift

Let
X
be the full shift on
N
symbols (
{\mathcal{A}}=\{1,\dots,N\}
).
We look at the special case of multi-word perturbation on
X
and explore the exponential decay rate of drop in entropy when the length of the forbidden words increases.

Let
({\mathcal{K}}_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
be a sequence where each
{\mathcal{K}}_{n}=\{w_{1,n},\dots,w_{k,n}\}
is a collection of
k
many words in
\mathcal{L}_{n}(X)
such that
w_{i,n}
is a prefix of
w_{i,n+1}
for all
n\geq 1
and
i=1,\dots,k
. For each
n
, let
h(X_{{\mathcal{K}}_{n}})=\ln(\lambda_{n})
. Then
\lambda_{n}
is the largest (real) root of
z-N+s_{n}(z)
where
s_{n}(z)
is the sum of entries of
\mathcal{M}_{n}(z)^{-1}=\mathcal{M}_{{\mathcal{K}}_{n}}(z)^{-1}
.

As
n
goes to infinity, each
i
gives a unique infinite sequence in
{\mathcal{A}}^{{\mathbb{N}}}
and we denote it by
x_{i}\in{\mathcal{A}}^{{\mathbb{N}}}
.
Also, by
Remark

3
, it is expected that
h(X_{{\mathcal{K}}_{n}})
converges to
h(X)
exponentially, as
n\to\infty
.
Hence, we are interested in finding

\lambda(x_{1},\dots,x_{k}):=\lim_{n\to\infty}\dfrac{h(X)-h(X_{{\mathcal{K}}_{n}})}{N^{-n}}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\dfrac{\ln(N)-\ln(\lambda_{n})}{N^{-n}},

if the limit exists.

In this section, we obtain an explicit expression for
\lambda(x_{1},\dots,x_{k})
. Let
S:{\mathcal{A}}^{{\mathbb{N}}}\to{\mathcal{A}}^{{\mathbb{N}}}
be the usual shift map. A point
x\in{\mathcal{A}}^{{\mathbb{N}}}
is
periodic
, if there exists
s>0
such that
S^{s}x=x
. For
x,y\in{\mathcal{A}}^{{\mathbb{N}}}
, whenever we mention that there exists
m>0
such that
S^{m}x=y
, we always mean that
m
is the least positive integer with this property.
The following key lemma will be used to prove the main result.

Lemma 5.2
.

With the notations as above, for
z>1
and
1\leq i\neq j\leq k
,

\lim_{n\to\infty}z^{-(n-1)}(w_{i,n},w_{i,n})_{z}=\begin{cases}1&\text{if $x_{i}$ is non-periodic}\\
\frac{1}{1-z^{-s}}&\text{if $x_{i}$ is periodic with period $s$},\end{cases}

\lim_{n\to\infty}z^{-(n-1)}(w_{i,n},w_{j,n})_{z}=\begin{cases}0&\text{if $S^{m}x_{i}\neq x_{j}$ for any $m>0$}\\
z^{-m}&\text{if $S^{m}x_{i}=x_{j}$ for some $m>0$ and $x_{i},x_{j}$ are non-periodic}\\
\frac{z^{-m}}{1-z^{-s}}&\parbox{312.9803pt}{if $S^{m}x_{i}=x_{j}$ for some $m>0$, $x_{i}$ is non-periodic and $x_{j}$ is periodic with period $s$}\\
\frac{z^{-m}}{1-z^{-t}}&\text{if $S^{m}x_{i}=x_{j}$ for some $m>0$ and $x_{i}$ is periodic with period $t$}.\end{cases}

Proof.

Fix
1\leq i\neq j\leq k
. Let
x=x_{i}
and
y=x_{j}
,
u_{n}=w_{i,n}
, and
w_{n}=w_{j,n}
.
Consider the correlation polynomials
a_{n}(z)=(u_{n},u_{n})_{z}
and
b_{n}(z)=(u_{n},w_{n})_{z}
. The limit of
z^{-n+1}a_{n}(z)
is shown in
[
2
, Theorem 4.6.1]
. However, we provide a proof for completion.

Let the correlation polynomial of
u_{n}
with itself be

a_{n}(z)=z^{n-1}+\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}a_{n,i}z^{n-1-i},

where
a_{n,i}
is either 0 or 1. When
x
is non-periodic,
\lim_{n\to\infty}a_{n,i}=0
for all
i
, and this implies that
\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}a_{n,i}z^{-i}=0
. Similarly when
x
is periodic with period
s
,
\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}a_{n,i}z^{-i}=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}z^{-js}=\frac{1}{1-z^{-s}}-1
, hence, we have the required limit.

Let
b_{n}(z)=\frac{1}{z}\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}b_{n,i}z^{n-i}
be the cross-correlation
(u_{n},w_{n})_{z}
. We consider different cases.

Case 1
.
S^{m}x\neq y
for any
m>0
: As
S^{m}x\neq y
for any
m>0
, there exists
N_{m}
such that
x_{m+1}x_{m+2}\dots x_{m+N_{m}}\neq y_{1}y_{2}\dots y_{N_{m}}
. Hence, for every
n\geq N_{m}
, we have
b_{n,m}=0
. Therefore,

\lim_{n\to\infty}z^{-n+1}b_{n}(z)=0.

Case 2
.
x,y
are non-periodic and there exists
m>0
such that
S^{m}x=y
: Assume that
m
is the least positive integer where
S^{m}x=y
. In this case,
b_{n,m}=1
for all
n>0
. Let
m^{\prime}>m
be such that
S^{m^{\prime}}x=y
. Then
S^{m^{\prime}-m}y=S^{m^{\prime}-m}S^{m}x=S^{m^{\prime}}x=y
implies that
y
is periodic and that is a contradiction. Therefore, for
m\neq m^{\prime}
, there exists
N_{m^{\prime}}>0
such that for
n\geq N_{m^{\prime}}
, we have
b_{n,m^{\prime}}=0
. Therefore

\lim_{n\to\infty}z^{-n+1}b_{n}(z)=z^{-m}.

Case 3
.
x
is non-periodic,
y
is periodic with period
t
and there exists
m>0
such that
S^{m}x=y
: Let
t
be the least period of
y
. Hence, if
S^{\ell}y=y
for some
\ell>0
, then
\ell=pt
for some
p\geq 1
. Also, assume that
m
is the least positive integer where
S^{m}x=y
.
In this case,
b_{n,m}=1
for all
n>0
.
Let
m^{\prime}>m
be such that
S^{m^{\prime}}x=y
. Then
S^{m^{\prime}-m}y=S^{m^{\prime}-m}S^{m}x=S^{m^{\prime}}x=y
. Hence
m^{\prime}=m+pt
for some
p\geq 1
. That means for any
m^{\prime}
that is not of the form
m+pt
for some
p\geq 1
, there exists
N_{m^{\prime}}>0
such that for
n\geq N_{m^{\prime}}
, we have
b_{n,m^{\prime}}=0
. Therefore,

\lim_{n\to\infty}z^{-n+1}b_{n}(z)=z^{-m}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}z^{-jt}=\frac{z^{-m}}{1-z^{-t}}.

Case 4
.
x
is periodic with period
s
, and there exists
m>0
such that
S^{m}x=y
: Let
s
be the least period of
x
and
m
be the least positive integer where
S^{m}x=y
. Observe the following in this case.

a)
m<s
; otherwise,
y=S^{m-s}S^{s}x=S^{m-s}x
contradicts the fact that
m
is the least integer with this property.

b)
S^{s}y=S^{s+m}x=S^{m}x=y
implies
y
is periodic. If
t
is the period of
y
, then
t\leq s
.

c)
S^{s-m}y=S^{s-m+m}x=x
. Let
\ell\leq s-m
be such that
S^{\ell}y=x
. Then
S^{\ell+m}x=x
implies
s\leq\ell+m
. Hence
\ell=s-m
. Hence,
s-m
is the minimum integer such that

.

d)
S^{t}x=S^{t}S^{s-m}y=S^{s-m}y=x
implies

as
s
is the least period of
x
. Therefore
s=t
. That is

is a periodic point with period
s
.

Hence, similar to the argument in the last case, if
m^{\prime}>m
is such that
S^{m^{\prime}}x=y
, then
m^{\prime}=m+ps
for some
p\geq 1
. Hence for any
m^{\prime}
that is not of the form
m+ps
for some
p\geq 1
, there exists
N_{m^{\prime}}>0
such that for
n\geq N_{m^{\prime}}
, we have
b_{n,m^{\prime}}=0
. Therefore,

\lim_{n\to\infty}z^{-n+1}b_{n}(z)=z^{-m}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}z^{-js}=\frac{z^{-m}}{1-z^{-s}}.

∎

Let
s_{n}(z)
be the rational function that is the sum of the entries of
{\mathcal{M}}_{n}^{-1}(z),
where
{\mathcal{M}}_{n}(z)=[(w_{j,n},w_{i,n})_{z}]_{i,j}
is the correlation matrix function. Let
\alpha(z)=\lim_{n\to\infty}z^{-n}{\mathcal{M}}_{n}(z)
. Firstly, we have the following result.

Lemma 5.3
.

\alpha(z)
is invertible for all
z>\#{\mathcal{K}}
.

Proof.

Let
\#{\mathcal{K}}=k
. Then
\alpha(z)
is a matrix of order
k
.
Note that
\alpha(z)=\frac{1}{z}\beta(z)
where the diagonal entries of
\beta(z)
are of the form
1+\frac{z^{-m}}{1-z^{-\ell}}
and the off-diagonal entries are of the form
\frac{z^{-m}}{1-z^{-\ell}}
where
m,\ell\in\{0,\dots,\infty\}
(we take
z^{-\infty}=0
). Also, for
z>1
,
\frac{z^{-m}}{1-z^{-\ell}}=\frac{z^{\ell-m}}{z^{\ell}-1}\leq\frac{z^{\ell-1}}{z^{\ell}-1}\leq\frac{1}{z-1}.

Hence,
\alpha_{i,i}(z)\geq 1\geq\frac{k-1}{z-1}\geq\sum_{j\neq i}\alpha_{i,j}(z).
That is,
\alpha(z)
is a strictly diagonally dominant matrix. This implies that
\alpha(z)
is invertible.
∎

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
\alpha(N)
be invertible.
With the notations as above,

(2)

\lambda(x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{k})=\lim_{n\to\infty}\left(N^{n-1}s_{n}(N)+N^{n-2}s^{2}_{n}(N)+\dots\right).

Proof.

For each
n
, let
h(X_{{\mathcal{K}}_{n}})=\ln(\lambda_{n})
. Then,
\lambda_{n}
is the largest real zero of
z-N+s_{n}(z)
. That is,
N-\lambda_{n}=s_{n}(\lambda_{n})
. By mean value theorem,

N-\lambda_{n}=\frac{s_{n}(N)}{1-s_{n}^{\prime}(c_{n})},

for some
\lambda_{n}<c_{n}<N
. Using the expansion for the logarithm, we get that

-\ln\left(\frac{\lambda_{n}}{N}\right)=\frac{s_{n}(N)}{N(1-s_{n}^{\prime}(c_{n}))}+\left(\frac{s_{n}(N)}{N(1-s_{n}^{\prime}(c_{n}))}\right)^{2}+\dots.

We will show that
\lim_{n\to\infty}s_{n}^{\prime}(c_{n})=0
and thus the result will follow.

Consider
z^{-n}{\mathcal{M}}_{n}(z)
. Each entry in this matrix is of the form
z^{-n}(w_{i,n},w_{j,n})_{z}
. Let
\alpha_{i,j}(z)=\lim_{n\to\infty}z^{-n}(w_{i,n},w_{j,n})_{z}
. The form of
\alpha_{i,j}(z)
is obtained in Lemma
5.2
for
z>1
. Therefore,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\det(z^{-n}{\mathcal{M}}_{n}(z))=\lim_{n\to\infty}z^{-kn}{\mathcal{D}}_{n}(z)=\det(\alpha(z)),

where
{\mathcal{D}}_{n}(z)
is the determinant of
{\mathcal{M}}_{n}(z)
and
\alpha(z)=[\alpha_{j,i}(z)]_{i,j}
. Choose the largest interval
[z_{0},N]
for some
z_{0}>1
such that
\det(\alpha(z))\neq 0
for
z\in[z_{0},N]
.

Let
\mathcal{S}_{n}(z)
denote the sum of entries of the adjoint matrix of
{\mathcal{M}}_{n}(z)
. Assume that
{\mathcal{D}}_{n}(z)=\sum_{j=0}^{k(n-1)}a_{j,n}z^{j}
and
\mathcal{S}_{n}(z)=\sum_{j=0}^{(k-1)(n-1)}b_{j,n}z^{j}
. Denote
a_{n}=\max_{j}\{|a_{j,n}|\}
and
b_{n}=\max_{j}\{|b_{j,n}|\}
. Here

\displaystyle|s_{n}^{\prime}(z)|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{|{\mathcal{D}}_{n}(z)S_{n}^{\prime}(z)|+|{\mathcal{D}}_{n}^{\prime}(z)\mathcal{S}_{n}(z)|}{({\mathcal{D}}_{n}(z))^{2}}<kna_{n}b_{n}\frac{z^{(n-1)(2k-1)+1}}{(z-1)^{2}({\mathcal{D}}_{n}(z))^{2}},

since
|{\mathcal{D}}_{n}(z)|<a_{n}\frac{z^{k(n-1)+1}}{z-1},|S_{n}(z)|<b_{n}\frac{z^{(k-1)(n-1)+1}}{z-1},|{\mathcal{D}}_{n}^{\prime}(z)|<a_{n}k(n-1)\frac{z^{k(n-1)}}{z-1}
, and
|S_{n}^{\prime}(z)|<b_{n}(k-1)(n-1)\frac{z^{(k-1)(n-1)}}{z-1}
.

Expanding the determinant via the Leibniz formula, we have

{\mathcal{D}}_{n}(z)=\sum_{\sigma\in S_{k}}\text{sign}(\sigma)\prod_{i=1}^{k}{\mathcal{M}}_{n_{i,\sigma(i)}}(z).

Each product involves
k
polynomials of degree at most
n-1
with coefficients 0 or 1, so the coefficient of any polynomial in such a product is bounded by
O((n-1)^{k})
, and there are
k!
such permutations. Summing over all permutations gives
a_{n}=O(k!n^{k})
. Using a similar argument for the determinant of the submatrices, we get
b_{n}=O(k!kn^{k-1})
.
Hence, there exists
M_{0}
such that
a_{n}b_{n}\leq M_{0}n^{2k-1}
.

Multiplying the last term in the above inequality by
\frac{z^{2kn}}{z^{2kn}}
and using
\lim_{n\to\infty}z^{-kn}{\mathcal{D}}_{n}(z)=\det(\alpha(z))
, we get that

|s_{n}^{\prime}(z)|\leq M(z)\frac{n^{2k}}{z^{n}}\rightarrow 0

as
n
tends to infinity for
z\in[z_{0},N]
, where
M(z)=\frac{kM_{0}}{(\det(\alpha(z)))^{2}}\frac{z^{2-2k}}{(z-1)^{2}}
. By
Theorem

3.2
,
\lim_{n\to\infty}\lambda_{n}=N
and thus
c_{n}\in[z_{0},N]
for
n
large enough.
Therefore,
\lim_{n\to\infty}s_{n}^{\prime}(c_{n})=0
.
∎

5.1.1.
Application to ergodic theory

Let
{\mathcal{A}}=\{1,\dots,N\}
and
X={\mathcal{A}}^{\mathbb{N}}
be a one-sided full shift. For
w\in{\mathcal{L}}(X)
, let
C_{w}=\{x\in X\mid x\text{ starts with }w\}
be the
cylinder based at
w
. The cylinders based at all possible words in
{\mathcal{L}}(X)
form a basis for the pro-discrete topology on
X
.
Moreover,
X
is equipped with uniform invariant probability measure
\mu
where
\mu(C_{w})=\frac{1}{N^{k}}
where
|w|=k
. For a given collection
{\mathcal{K}}
of finite allowed words, each of length
k
, we define a
hole

\bigcup_{w\in{\mathcal{K}}}C_{w}
. Note that the perturbation
X_{{\mathcal{K}}}
is the collection of all sequences in
X
that do not enter the hole
\bigcup_{w\in{\mathcal{K}}}C_{w}
by the shift map. Define,

\Omega_{n}({\mathcal{K}})=\{x\in X\mid S^{i}(x)\notin\bigcup_{w\in{\mathcal{K}}}C_{w},\ 0\leq i\leq n-1\}

and define
{\mathcal{A}}^{\mathbb{N}}\setminus\Omega_{n}({\mathcal{K}})
to be the
survival set
. The
escape rate into
\bigcup_{w\in{\mathcal{K}}}C_{w}
by
S
is defined as

\rho({\mathcal{K}})=-\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\mu(X\setminus\Omega_{n}(K)).

The escape rate is an important concept in open dynamics where a hole is introduced in the state space and we look at the average rate at which the orbits escape into this hole. In our setup, the quantity defined above is the escape rate where the hole is given by
\bigcup_{w\in{\mathcal{K}}}C_{w}
.
In
[
2
]
, it was proved that
\rho(K)=h(X)-h(X_{{\mathcal{K}}})
.

It is also interesting to see how the escape rate decays when the size of the hole converges to zero
[
7
]
.
Consider a shrinking sequence of (possibly disconnected) holes
\left\{H_{n}=\bigcup_{w\in{\mathcal{K}}_{n}}C_{w}\right\}_{n\geq 1},
where
|w|=n
for all
w\in{\mathcal{K}}_{n}
,
\#{\mathcal{K}}_{n}=k
for
n\geq 1
and
H_{1}\supseteq H_{2}\supseteq\dots
. As
n\to\infty
, these holes converge to a finite union of points, say
x_{1},\dots,x_{k}\in X
.

We define the
local escape rate
into
x_{1},\dots,x_{k}
as

\rho(x_{1},\dots,x_{k})=\lim_{n\to\infty}\dfrac{\rho({\mathcal{K}}_{n})}{\mu({\mathcal{K}}_{n})}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\dfrac{h(X)-h(X_{{\mathcal{K}}_{n}})}{kN^{-n}},

where
\mu({\mathcal{K}}_{n})=\mu(\bigcup_{w\in{\mathcal{K}}_{n}}C_{w}).
We have the following result.

Corollary 5.6
.

With notations as before, whenever
k<N
, we have,
\rho(x_{1},\dots,x_{k})=\dfrac{T}{kN}.

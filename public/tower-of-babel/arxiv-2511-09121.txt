Molla Basir Ahamed ∗, Partha Pratim Roy

Abstract
. 1.
Introduction

Let
\mathbb{C}
denote the complex plane and
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}=\mathbb{C}\cup\{\infty\}
denote the extended complex plane. We shall use the following notations:
\mathbb{D}=\{z:|z|<1\},\;\partial\mathbb{D}=\{z:|z|=1\},\;\overline{\mathbb{D}}=\{z:|z|\leq 1\},\;\mathbb{D}^{*}=\{z:|z|>1\}.
Let
f
be a meromorphic and univalent function in
\mathbb{D}
with a simple pole at
z=p\in[0,1)
of residue
a_{-1}
. Since
f(z)-{1}/(z-p)
is analytic in
\mathbb{D}
, we may write

(1.1)

\displaystyle f(z)=\frac{a_{-1}}{z-p}+\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n},\quad z\in\mathbb{D}.

We denote the class of such functions by
\Sigma(p)
. Let
\Sigma_{0}(p)
be the subclass of
\Sigma(p)
consisting of functions
f
for which
a_{0}=0
in (
1.1
). For
0\leq k<1
,
\Sigma_{k}(p)
stands for the class of functions in
\Sigma(p)
that admit a
k
-quasiconformal extension to
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
. Recall that a mapping
F:\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\to\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
is
k
-quasiconformal if
F
is a homeomorphism, has locally
L^{2}
-derivatives on
\mathbb{C}\setminus\{F^{-1}(\infty)\}
, and satisfies

\displaystyle|\overline{\partial}F|\leq k|\partial F|\quad\text{a.e.,}

where
\partial F=\partial F/\partial z
and
\overline{\partial}F=\partial F/\partial\overline{z}
. Such an
F
is often called
K
-quasiconformal, where
K={(1+k)}/{(1-k)}\geq 1
, and the function
\mu=\overline{\partial}F/\partial F
is called its complex dilatation. This can be expressed using the complex dilatation,
\mu_{f}=f_{\bar{z}}/f_{z}
. A sense-preserving homeomorphism is K-quasiconformal if its complex dilatation is bounded by a constant
k=(K-1)/(K+1)<1
. That is,
|\mu_{f}|\leq k
. When
K=1
, the mapping is
1
-quasiconformal, which is equivalent to being conformal. We denote
\Sigma_{0}^{k}(p)=\Sigma_{0}(p)\cap\Sigma_{k}(p)
.

For an analytic function
f
in
\mathbb{D}_{r}:=\{z:|z|<r,\;0<r\leq 1\}
, we set

(1.2)

\displaystyle\Delta(r,f)=\iint_{\mathbb{D}_{r}}|f^{\prime}(z)|^{2}\,dx\,dy,\quad z=x+iy,

which is called the Dirichlet integral of
f
.
The concept of a K-quasiconformal extension for an analytic map addresses the question:
Under what conditions can a given univalent (one-to-one) analytic function
f
defined on a domain
D
be extended to a
K
-quasiconformal homeomorphism of the entire complex plane
\mathbb{C}
?

This question is significant in several areas, including Teichmüller theory and the study of Kleinian groups. A common example is a univalent analytic function
f
on the unit disk
\mathbb{D}
that is given by a specific form, such as
f(z)=z+\omega(z)
, where
\omega(z)
is an analytic function on
\mathbb{D}
with certain properties. For instance, if
|\omega^{\prime}(z)|\leq k<1
, then
f
can be extended to a
k
-quasiconformal automorphism of the Riemann sphere.

In this paper, we consider the class of meromorphic functions
f
having a pole of order
m(\geq 1)
at
z=p
and improve several existing results. The organization of the paper is the following. In Section
2
, we first prove a result (see Theorem
2.1
) which improves the result
[
4
, Theorem 1]
. In particular, when
m=1
, we show that an inequality in Theorem
2.1
reduces to the inequality
|a_{1}|<\frac{k}{1-p^{2}}
obtained in
[
4
, Corollary 1]
, which is sharp in the class
\Sigma(p)
. To establish our result, we first prove Lemma
2.1
, regarding a general formulation of the area
A_{\text{comp}}(r)
of the complement of the domain, which is the image of
\mathbb{D}_{r}:=\{z\in\mathbb{D}:|z|<r\}
(for
|p|<r<1
). Next, we obtain a result (see Theorem
2.2
) for functions that are meromorphic and univalent in
\mathbb{D}
with a pole of order
m
at
z=p
, and admit a
k
-quasiconformal extension
F
to the extended complex plane
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
. For
m=1
, Theorem
2.2
reduces to
[
4
, Theorem 2]
. We extend
[
4
, Theorem 3]
by establishing the conditions under which the Hadamard product
f\star g
of functions in
\Sigma^{(m)}_{k}(p)
also belongs to
\Sigma^{(m)}_{\alpha_{m}}(p)
, where
\alpha_{m}:=|a_{-1}||b_{-1}|{k_{1}k_{2}}{(1-p)^{-2m}}<1
. Then, we obtain a result (see Theorem
2.4
) on the sharp Schwarzian norm of
f
for the class
\Sigma_{k}(p)
. Finally, we establish a result (see Theorem
2.5
) which can be regarded as a generalization of
[
3
, Theorem 1]
, where a sufficient criterion was established for a sense-preserving harmonic mapping in a convex domain to admit a quasiconformal extension. The proofs of the main results are given in detail immediately following the statement of each result. In Section
3
, we provide concluding remarks for
\ell^{2}
, the Hilbert space of complex sequences
x=\{x_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
.

2.
Main results

Suppose that
f
is an analytic function in the disk
\mathbb{D}
with the Taylor series expansion
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
and
f^{\prime}(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}na_{n}z^{\,n-1}
.Then, using Parseval–Gutzmer formula, the area
\Delta(r,f)
of
f(\mathbb{D}_{r})
,
as stated in (
1.2
) can be re-formulated as (see
[
10
]
):

(2.1)

\displaystyle\Delta(r,f)=\iint_{\mathbb{D}_{r}}|f^{\prime}(z)|^{2}\,dx\,dy=\pi\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n|a_{n}|^{2}r^{2n},\quad z=x+iy.

In this paper, we specifically focus on this form of the area formula. The process of computing this area is known as the
area problem
for functions of the type
f
. It’s important to note that the area of the image of the unit disk under
f
, denoted by
f(\mathbb{D})
, may not be bounded for all functions in the class
\mathcal{S}
. We observe that if
f\in\mathcal{S}
, then
z/f
is non-vanishing, and hence,
f\in\mathcal{S}
may be expressed as follows:

\displaystyle f(z)=\frac{z}{F_{f}(z)},\;\mbox{where}\;F_{f}(z)=1+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}c_{n}z^{n},\quad z\in\mathbb{D}.

In
[
16
]
, Yamashita addressed the area problem for functions of the form
F_{f}
(with
f\in\mathcal{S}
) and established that the area of
F_{f}(\mathbb{D}_{r})
is bounded. The study of the class
\Sigma_{k}(p)
generalizes two classical directions: quasiconformal extension results for Schlicht functions (
p=0
) and meromorphic univalent functions with fixed pole position (
k=0
). In
[
4
]
, Bhowmik
et al.
established an area theorem for functions in the class
\Sigma_{k}(p)
.

Theorem A
.

[
4
, Theorem 1]

Let
0\leq k<1
and
0\leq p<1
. Suppose that
f\in\Sigma_{k}(p)
is expressed in the form of (
1.1
). Then

(1.3)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n|a_{n}|^{2}\leq\frac{k^{2}}{(1-p^{2})^{2}}.

Here, equality holds if, and only if
f
, is of the form

(1.4)

\displaystyle f(z)=\frac{1}{z-p}+a_{0}+\frac{a_{1}z}{1-pz},\;z\in\mathbb{D},

where
a_{0}
and
a_{1}
are constants with
|a_{1}|=k
. Moreover, a
k
-quasiconformal extension of this
f
is given by setting

(2.2)

\displaystyle f(z)=\frac{1}{z-p}+a_{0}+\frac{a_{1}}{\overline{z}-p},\;z\in\mathbb{D}^{*}.

For fix
p\in[0,1)
,
m\in\mathbb{N}
, and
0\leq k<1
. Define

\Sigma^{(m)}_{k}(p)
to be the class of meromorphic, univalent functions

f
on the unit disc
\mathbb{D}
with a pole of exact order
m
at
z=p

and it is normalized by

(2.3)

\displaystyle f(z)=\sum_{k=1}^{m}\frac{a_{-k}}{(z-p)^{k}}+\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n},\;z\in\mathbb{D}.

Then, we set

\displaystyle\Sigma^{(m)}_{k}(p)=\left\{f\in\Sigma^{(m)}(p):f\text{ admits a $k$-quasiconformal extension to }\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\right\}.

We establish a result generalizing
[
4
, Theorem 1]
to the class
\Sigma_{k}^{(m)}(p)
of meromorphic functions having pole at
p
with order
m
. An interesting aspect of our finding is that the extremal function differs in form from the well-known function
f_{p}
introduced by Chichra
[
5
, Lemma, p. 317]
. This new result constitutes a true generalization, as it reduces exactly to
[
4
, Theorem 1]
under the specific conditions
m=1
and
a_{-1}=1
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
f
be meromorphic univalent in
\mathbb{D}
with its only pole at
z=p
(
|p|<1
) of order
m
and with the principal part
\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{a_{-j}}{(z-p)^{j}}
so that
f
can be expanded in
\mathbb{D}
as

\displaystyle f(z)=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{a_{-j}}{(z-p)^{j}}+a_{0}+\frac{a_{1}z}{1-pz}\;\mbox{for}\;z\in\mathbb{D}.

Then the area
A_{\text{comp}}(r)
of the complement of the domain which is the image of
\mathbb{D}_{r}:=\{z\in\mathbb{D}:|z|<r\}
(for
|p|<r<1
) is

(2.4)

\displaystyle A_{\text{comp}}(r)=\pi\left|\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}k\left|\sum_{j=1}^{\min\{m,k\}}a_{-j}\binom{k-1}{j-1}p^{k-j}\right|^{2}-\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n|a_{n}|^{2}\right|.

Proof.

The lemma is a generalization of the result using
[
8
, Lemma 1.1, p. 2]
. Since the proof employs similar ideas, we omit the details.
∎

Remark 2.1
.

The area
A_{\text{comp}}(r)
in Lemma
2.1
is a generalization of Chichra’s area theorem (see Chichra
[
5
]
) for functions with a pole of order
m\geq 1
(specifically, for
m=1
, we have
\min\{m,k\}=1
). Taking the limit as
r\to 1
, the resulting area is

\operatorname{Area}(f(\mathbb{D}))=\pi\left(\frac{|a_{-1}|^{2}}{(1-|p|^{2})^{2}}-\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n|a_{n}|^{2}\right).

More precisely, for
m=1
, the inner sum has only
j=1
and
\min\{1,k\}=1
, and we have the coefficient

\displaystyle c_{-k}=a_{-1}\binom{k-1}{1-1}p^{k-1}=a_{-1}p^{k-1}.

The first summation term
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}k|c_{-k}|^{2}r^{-2k}
becomes

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}k|a_{-1}p^{k-1}|^{2}r^{-2k}=|a_{-1}|^{2}r^{-2}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}k\left(\frac{|p|^{2}}{r^{2}}\right)^{k-1}.

Let
x={|p|^{2}}/{r^{2}}
. Using the geometric series
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}kx^{k-1}={1}/{(1-x)^{2}}
, we have

\displaystyle|a_{-1}|^{2}r^{-2}\cdot\frac{1}{\left(1-\frac{|p|^{2}}{r^{2}}\right)^{2}}=|a_{-1}|^{2}r^{-2}\cdot\frac{r^{4}}{(r^{2}-|p|^{2})^{2}}=\frac{|a_{-1}|^{2}r^{2}}{(r^{2}-|p|^{2})^{2}}.

Thus, the area formula for
m=1
matches with
[
5
, Eq. (2.4), p. 318]
which is

\displaystyle A_{\text{comp}}(r)=\pi\left|\frac{|a_{-1}|^{2}r^{2}}{(r^{2}-|p|^{2})^{2}}-\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n|a_{n}|^{2}r^{2n}\right|.

Using Lemma
2.1
, we now state the following result.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
m\geq 1
,
0\leq k<1
, and
0<p<1
. Suppose that
f\in\Sigma_{k}^{(m)}(p)
is expressed in the form of (
2.3
). Then

(2.5)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n|a_{n}|^{2}\leq\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}k\left|\sum_{j=1}^{\min\{m,k\}}a_{-j}\binom{k-1}{j-1}p^{k-j}\right|^{2}-\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n|a_{n}|^{2}\right)k^{2}.

Here, equality holds if, and only if,
f
is of the form

(2.6)

\displaystyle f(z)=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{a_{-j}}{(z-p)^{j}}+a_{0}+\frac{a_{1}z}{1-pz}\;\mbox{for}\;z\in\mathbb{D}

where
a_{0}
and
a_{1}
are constants with
|a_{1}|=k
. Moreover, a
k
-quasiconformal extension of this
f
is given by setting

(2.7)

\displaystyle f_{m,k}(z)=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{a_{-j}}{(z-p)^{j}}+a_{0}+\frac{a_{1}}{\bar{z}-p}\;\mbox{for}\;z\in\overline{\mathbb{D}^{*}}.

Remark 2.2
.

This represents a natural extension of Theorem A. In effect, it generalizes the classical result from Lehto
[
11
]
, which originally refined the Bieberbach-Gronwall area theorem for functions within the class
\Sigma_{k}(0)
. Further, we remark that the function (
2.6
) belongs to
\Sigma_{k}^{(m)}(p)
as long as
|a_{1}|\leq 1
. With
|a_{1}|=1
, this function serves as another extremal case, which is crucial for our analysis (see
[
5
]
)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n|a_{n}|^{2}\leq\frac{1}{\left(1-p^{2}\right)^{2}}.

The following result is an immediate corollary of Theorem
2.1
.

Corollary 2.1
.

Let
m\geq 1
,
0<p<1
and
0<k<1
.
If
f\in\Sigma^{(m)}_{k}(p)
has the expansion
f(z)=\frac{a_{-1}}{(z-p)^{m}}+\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n},\;z\in\mathbb{D},
then the first coefficient satisfies
|a_{1}|<\frac{k}{(1-p^{2})^{m}}.

Remark 2.3
.

For
m=1
, this reduces to the inequality

|a_{1}|<\frac{k}{1-p^{2}}
obtained in
[
4
, Corollary 1]
, which is sharp in the class
\Sigma(p)
. A key observation is that Theorem
2.1
recovers the result from
[
4
, Theorem 1]
as a special case, simply by setting
m=1
.

Proof of Lemma
2.1
.

The function
f(z)
, meromorphic univalent in
|z|<1
with a pole of exact order
m
at
z=p
(
|p|<r<1
), is given by:

\displaystyle f(z)=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{a_{-j}}{(z-p)^{j}}+\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}.

The area of the complement of the image of the disk
|z|<r
,
i.e.,

A_{\text{comp}}(r):=\mbox{Area}(\mathbb{C}\setminus f(\mathbb{D}))
, is given by

\displaystyle A_{\text{comp}}(r)=\pi\left|\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}k|c_{-k}|^{2}r^{-2k}\right)-\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n|c_{n}|^{2}r^{2n}\right)\right|\quad\textbf{(A)},

where
f(z)=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}c_{n}z^{n}
is the Laurent series centered at the origin, valid for
|p|<|z|<1
.

The coefficients for the negative powers,
c_{-k}
(for
k\geq 1
), come from the Laurent expansion of the principal part
\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{a_{-j}}{(z-p)^{j}}
. We use the generalized binomial series for
|z|>|p|
:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(z-p)^{j}}=\frac{1}{z^{j}}\left(1-\frac{p}{z}\right)^{-j}=\frac{1}{z^{j}}\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\binom{j+l-1}{l}\left(\frac{p}{z}\right)^{l}.

Substituting this into the principal part, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{a_{-j}}{(z-p)^{j}}=\sum_{j=1}^{m}a_{-j}\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\binom{j+l-1}{l}\frac{p^{l}}{z^{j+l}}.

To find the coefficient
c_{-k}
of
z^{-k}
, we require the exponent
j+l=k
, or
l=k-j
. The index
j
must run from
1
up to
\min\{m,k\}
:

c_{-k}=\sum_{j=1}^{\min\{m,k\}}a_{-j}\binom{j+(k-j)-1}{k-j}p^{k-j}

Simplifying the binomial coefficient
\binom{k-1}{k-j}
to
\binom{k-1}{j-1}
:

\displaystyle{c_{-k}=\sum_{j=1}^{\min\{m,k\}}a_{-j}\binom{k-1}{j-1}p^{k-j}}.

Substituting the expression for
c_{-k}
and setting
c_{n}=a_{n}
for
n\geq 1
into the general formula
(A)
yields the complete expression for the area of the complement for a pole of order
m
:

(2.8)

\displaystyle A_{\text{comp}}(r)=\pi\left|\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}k\left|\sum_{j=1}^{\min(m,k)}a_{-j}\binom{k-1}{j-1}p^{k-j}\right|^{2}r^{-2k}-\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n|a_{n}|^{2}r^{2n}\right|.

Hence (
2.4
) follows when
r\to 1
in (
2.8
). This completes the proof.
∎

Proof of Theorem
2.1
.

Let
f\in\Sigma_{k}^{(m)}(p)
have the expansion in (
2.3
). We may suppose that
f
is already extended to a
k
-quasiconformal mapping of
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
to itself.

Case 1.
If
k=0
, then the assertion clearly holds good.

Case 2.
Hence, we discuss the case
k>0
in the rest of the proof. To start with, we first make a change of variables. In this regard we define a function
\phi:\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\to\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
by
\phi(w)=f(1/w)
, which is defined on
\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}
and has a pole of order
m
at
w=1/p
. Consider the function

\displaystyle\psi(w):=\phi(w)-\frac{w^{m}}{(1-pw)^{m}}\;\mbox{for}\;w\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\left\{\frac{1}{p}\right\}.

Then
\psi
is analytic in
\mathbb{D}^{*}=\{w:|w|>1\}
and has a convergent Laurent series expansion
\psi(w)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{a_{n}}{w^{n}},|w|>1.
Since
\psi
has locally square-integrable derivatives, we apply the Cauchy–Pompeiu formula and Hilbert transform methods (see
[
12
]
) to get

\displaystyle\iint_{\mathbb{D}}|\bar{\partial}\psi|^{2}\;dx\;dy=\iint_{\mathbb{C}}|\partial\psi|^{2}\,dx\,dy\geq\iint_{|\zeta|>1}|\partial\psi|^{2}\,dx\,dy.

Computing the series directly, we find that

\displaystyle\iint_{|w|>1}|\partial\psi(w)|^{2}\,dx\,dy=\pi\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n|a_{n}|^{2}.

Since
\phi(\mathbb{D})=\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\setminus f(\mathbb{D})
, in view of Lemma
2.1
, we obtain

\displaystyle A_{\text{comp}}(r)=\pi\left|\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}k\left|\sum_{j=1}^{\min\{m,k\}}a_{-j}\binom{k-1}{j-1}p^{k-j}\right|^{2}-\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n|a_{n}|^{2}\right|.

As
\phi
is
k
-quasiconformal on
\mathbb{D}
, its Jacobian satisfies

\displaystyle J_{\phi}=|\partial\phi|^{2}-|\bar{\partial}\phi|^{2}\geq(k^{-2}-1)|\bar{\partial}\phi|^{2}=(k^{-2}-1)|\bar{\partial}\psi|^{2},

and hence, we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{Area}(\phi(\mathbb{D}))\geq(k^{-2}-1)\iint_{\mathbb{D}}|\bar{\partial}\psi|^{2}\,dx\,dy.

Combining the above,

\displaystyle\pi\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}k\left|\sum_{j=1}^{\min\{m,k\}}a_{-j}\binom{k-1}{j-1}p^{k-j}\right|^{2}-\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n|a_{n}|^{2}\right)\geq(k^{-2}-1)\pi\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n|a_{n}|^{2},

which rearranges to give

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n|a_{n}|^{2}\leq\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}k\left|\sum_{j=1}^{\min\{m,k\}}a_{-j}\binom{k-1}{j-1}p^{k-j}\right|^{2}-\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n|a_{n}|^{2}\right)k^{2}.

Next, we prove the equality case. If equality holds, it must have occurred in all previous inequalities. This implies:

(i)

\bar{\partial}\psi=0
in
\mathbb{D}
, so
\psi
is analytic in
\mathbb{C}
.

(ii)

Hence,

\phi(\zeta)=\frac{\zeta^{m}}{(1-p\zeta)^{m}}+h(\zeta),

where
h
is entire.

(iii)

Thus,
h
must satisfy
\bar{\partial}h=\mu(\zeta)\partial\phi
with
|\mu|=k
, and such
\mu
forces
h
to be of the form: (see
[
11
]
)

\displaystyle h(\zeta)=a_{0}+\frac{a_{1}}{1-p\zeta}\;\text{with }\;|a_{1}|=k.

Thus, we obtain

\displaystyle\phi(\zeta)=\frac{\zeta^{m}}{(1-p\zeta)^{m}}+a_{0}+\frac{a_{1}}{1-p\zeta},

and therefore, we have

\displaystyle f(z)=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{a_{-j}}{(z-p)^{j}}+a_{0}+\frac{a_{1}z}{1-pz},\;\text{with}\;|a_{1}|=k,

as desired. It is easy to see that
f\in\Sigma_{k}^{(m)}(p)
, and the equality is achieved.
∎

The next theorem extends
[
4
, Theorem 2]
, in which a sufficient condition is established for meromorphic univalent functions with a simple pole at
z=p
to admit a
k
-quasiconformal extension. Our result generalizes this to the case of higher-order poles by introducing the class
\Sigma_{k}^{(m)}(p)
, together with an explicit construction of the quasiconformal extension. We consider the principal part of
f\in\Sigma_{k}^{(m)}(p)
as

\displaystyle R(z)=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{a_{-j}}{(z-p)^{j}},\qquad a_{-m}\neq 0,

and define its exterior form by

\displaystyle\widetilde{R}(\zeta):=R(1/\zeta)=\sum_{j=1}^{m}a_{-j}\frac{\zeta^{j}}{(1-p\zeta)^{j}},\quad|\zeta|\leq 1.

Assume that

(2.9)

\displaystyle\inf_{|\zeta|\leq 1}|\widetilde{R}^{\prime}(\zeta)|=C>0.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
0\leq k<1
,
0\leq p<1
, and
m\in\mathbb{N}
. Suppose that
f\in\Sigma_{k}^{(m)}(p)
is expressed in the form of (
2.3
). Let
\omega
be an analytic function in the unit disk
\mathbb{D}
satisfying

(2.10)

\displaystyle|\omega^{\prime}(z)|\leq\frac{k}{(1+p)^{m+1}},\quad z\in\mathbb{D}.

Then the function

\displaystyle f(z)=R(z)+\omega(z),\qquad z\in\mathbb{D},

is meromorphic and univalent in
\mathbb{D}
with a pole of order
m
at
z=p
, and admits a
k
-quasiconformal extension
F
to the extended complex plane
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
given by

\displaystyle F(z)=\begin{cases}R(z)+\omega(z),&|z|<1,\\[4.0pt]
R(z)+\omega\!\left(\dfrac{1}{\bar{z}}\right),&|z|>1.\end{cases}

Hence
f\in\Sigma^{(m)}_{k}(p)
.

Remark 2.4
.

In the special case
m=1
, Theorem
2.2
reduces to
[
4
, Theorem 2]
.

A straightforward application of Theorem
2.2
yields the following sufficient condition
for a function
f
of the form (

) to belong to
\Sigma_{k}(p)
.

Corollary 2.2
.

Let
0\leq p<1
and
0\leq k<1
. Suppose that a meromorphic function

f(z)
on
\mathbb{D}
has the form (
2.3
). If

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n|a_{n}|\leq\frac{|a_{-1}|k}{(1+p)^{m+1}},

then
f\in\Sigma^{(m)}_{k}(p)
.

Proof.

This result is a direct consequence of Theorem
2.2
because of

\displaystyle|\omega^{\prime}(z)|\leq\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n|a_{n}|\,|z|^{n-1}\leq\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n|a_{n}|\leq\frac{k}{(1+p)^{m+1}},\quad z\in\mathbb{D}.

This completes the proof.
∎

Remark 2.5
.

It is worth noting that
[
4
, Corollary 2]
is a special case of our Corollary
2.2
, obtained when

.

Proof of the Theorem
2.2
.

We follow the method of
[
4
, Theorem 2]
, adapted for the higher-order pole case.

For
|z|>1
, set
\zeta=1/z
.
Define

\displaystyle G(z)=R(z)+\omega\!\left(\frac{1}{\bar{z}}\right),\quad\text{and}\quad\widetilde{G}(\zeta):=G(1/\zeta)=\widetilde{R}(\zeta)+\omega(\bar{\zeta}),\quad|\zeta|<1.

Since
\widetilde{R}
is analytic in a neighborhood of
\{|\zeta|\leq 1\}
and
\omega
is analytic in
\mathbb{D}
,
the Wirtinger derivatives of
\widetilde{G}
are

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\widetilde{G}}{\partial\zeta}=\widetilde{R}^{\prime}(\zeta),\qquad\frac{\partial\widetilde{G}}{\partial\bar{\zeta}}=\omega^{\prime}(\bar{\zeta}).

The complex dilatation
\mu_{G}
of
G
at
z=1/\zeta
satisfies

(2.11)

\displaystyle|\mu_{G}(z)|=\left|\frac{\partial_{\bar{\zeta}}\widetilde{G}(\zeta)}{\partial_{\zeta}\widetilde{G}(\zeta)}\right|=\frac{|\omega^{\prime}(\bar{\zeta})|}{|\widetilde{R}^{\prime}(\zeta)|}.

By assumption (
2.9
),
|\widetilde{R}^{\prime}(\zeta)|\geq C>0
for
|\zeta|\leq 1
.
Using (
2.10
), we obtain

\displaystyle|\mu_{G}(z)|\leq\frac{\dfrac{k}{(1+p)^{m+1}}}{C}=:\kappa,\quad|\zeta|\leq 1.

Since
k<1
and
C>0
, we have
\kappa<1
.
Therefore,
G
is locally
\kappa
-quasiconformal on
\mathbb{D}^{*}
, and its Jacobian satisfies

\displaystyle J_{G}(z)=|\partial_{z}G(z)|^{2}-|\partial_{\bar{z}}G(z)|^{2}=|\partial_{z}G(z)|^{2}(1-|\mu_{G}(z)|^{2})>0.

Hence
G
is locally orientation-preserving and a local homeomorphism on
\mathbb{D}^{*}
.

On
|z|=1
,
\omega(1/\bar{z})=\overline{\omega(z)}
, so that

G(z)
agrees with the boundary values of
f(z)=R(z)+\omega(z)
.
Define

\displaystyle F(z)=\begin{cases}R(z)+\omega(z),&|z|<1,\\[4.0pt]
R(z)+\omega(1/\bar{z}),&|z|>1.\end{cases}

Then
F
is continuous on
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
,
locally homeomorphic and orientation-preserving on
\mathbb{D}
and
\mathbb{D}^{*}
, respectively.
Hence
F
is a covering map of
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
onto itself.
Since the Riemann sphere
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
is simply connected,

F
must be a homeomorphism of
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
.
The bound
|\mu_{G}(z)|\leq\kappa<1
shows that
F
is a
k
-quasiconformal homeomorphism of
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
,
and therefore
f\in\Sigma^{(m)}_{k}(p)
.

If
\omega
is only analytic on
\mathbb{D}
(not beyond),
define
\omega_{r}(z)=\omega(rz)
and
f_{r}(z)=R(z)+\omega_{r}(z)
for
0<r<1
.
Each
\omega_{r}
is analytic in a disk of radius
1/r>1
,
so by the above argument,
f_{r}
admits a
k
-quasiconformal extension
F_{r}
.
By the normality of the family of
k
-quasiconformal homeomorphisms of
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
,
there exists a subsequence
r_{j}\to 1^{-}
such that
F_{r_{j}}\to F
uniformly on
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
.
The limit
F
is a
k
-quasiconformal homeomorphism extending
f
, which completes the proof.
∎

Proof of the Theorem

.

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n|a_{n}|^{2}\leq\frac{|a_{-1}|^{2}k_{1}^{2}}{(1-p^{2})^{2m}}\quad\text{and}\quad\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n|b_{n}|^{2}\leq\frac{|b_{-1}|^{2}k_{2}^{2}}{(1-p^{2})^{2m}}.

Now an application of Cauchy–Schwarz inequality together with the aforementioned

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(\sqrt{n}|a_{n}|)(\sqrt{n}|b_{n}|)

A crucial tool for studying the univalence and quasiconformal extensibility of analytic functions is the Schwarzian derivative. For a locally univalent meromorphic function
f
on
\mathbb{D}
, the Schwarzian derivative is

\displaystyle S_{f}(z)=\left(\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\right)^{\prime}-\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\right)^{2},

a Möbius-invariant differential operator characterized by
S_{f}\equiv 0
precisely for Möbius transformations and satisfying the composition law
S_{g\circ f}=(S_{g}\circ f)(f^{\prime})^{2}+S_{f}
. Its hyperbolically scaled sup-norm

\displaystyle\|S_{f}\|:=\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}(1-|z|^{2})^{2}\,|S_{f}(z)|

plays a central role in univalence and extension theory.

The Nehari-Kraus Theorem states that if an analytic function
f
on the unit disk
\mathbb{D}
has a Schwarzian derivative satisfying
|S_{f}(z)|\leq 2/(1-|z|^{2})^{2}
, then
f
is univalent. Ahlfors’ Theorem is a powerful result that connects the Schwarzian derivative to quasiconformal extensions. It states that if an analytic function
f
on the unit disk
\mathbb{D}
has a Schwarzian derivative with a sufficiently small norm, specifically
\|S_{f}\|\infty=\sup{z\in\mathbb{D}}|S_{f}(z)|(1-|z|^{2})^{2}\leq 2
, then
f
is univalent and can be extended to a quasiconformal homeomorphism of the complex plane. A smaller bound on the Schwarzian derivative guarantees a smaller dilatation constant
K
. In particular, Nehari’s classical criterion bounds
\|S_{f}\|
to guarantee univalence, while the Ahlfors–Weill theory links bounds on
\|S_{f}\|
to quasiconformal extendability across
\partial\mathbb{D}
(see
[
14
,
2
,
15
,
13
]
.) In recent years, mathematcians continues to find the sharpness of Schwarzian and pre-Schwarzian norm estimates for various analytic and meromorphic subclasses (uniformly convex, Robertson, Janowski–starlike, Ozaki close-to-convex, etc.), underscoring the relevance of
\|S_{f}\|
to both univalence and quasiconformal extension problems (see
[
1
]
).

We now present our result on the sharp Schwarzian norm of
f
for the class
\Sigma_{k}(p)
.

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
f\in\Sigma_{k}(p)
be a meromorphic univalent function in the unit disc
\mathbb{D}
with a simple pole at
z=p\in(0,1)
and residue 1, and suppose that
f
admits a
k
-quasiconformal extension to the Riemann sphere
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
. Then the Schwarzian norm of
f
, defined by

\displaystyle\|S_{f}\|:=\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}(1-|z|^{2})^{2}|S_{f}(z)|,

satisfies the inequality

\displaystyle\|S_{f}\|\leq\frac{6k}{(1-p^{2})^{2}}.

Moreover, this inequality is sharp.

Proof of Theorem
2.4
.

Let
f\in\Sigma_{k}(p)
then the function
f(z)
can be written as

\displaystyle f(z)=\frac{1}{z-p}+\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n},

where
f
is meromorphic and univalent in
\mathbb{D}
, and extends
k
-quasiconformally to
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
.

The Schwarzian derivative of a locally univalent function
f
is given by

\displaystyle S_{f}(z)=\left(\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\right)^{\prime}-\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\right)^{2},

and the Schwarzian norm is

\displaystyle\|S_{f}\|:=\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}(1-|z|^{2})^{2}|S_{f}(z)|.

It is a classical result due to Nehari(see
[
14
]
), and further developed by Lehto and Pommerenke (see
[
15
,
12
]
), that if
f
is univalent in
\mathbb{D}
and admits a
k
-quasiconformal extension, then

\displaystyle\|S_{f}\|\leq 6k.

Now, to adapt this to the setting
f\in\Sigma_{k}(p)
, we use the Möbius transformation

\displaystyle\phi(z)=\frac{z+p}{1+pz},\quad\phi^{-1}(w)=\frac{w-p}{1-pw},

which maps
\mathbb{D}
onto itself and satisfies
\phi(0)=p
.

Define
F(w):=f(\phi^{-1}(w))
. Then
F\in\Sigma_{k}(0)
and has a simple pole at the origin. Since Möbius transformations preserve quasiconformality and satisfy
S_{\phi}=0
, we have

\displaystyle S_{F}(w)=S_{f}(\phi^{-1}(w))\cdot(\phi^{-1})^{\prime}(w)^{2}.

Thus,it is clear that

\displaystyle\|S_{f}\|=\sup_{w\in\mathbb{D}}(1-|\phi^{-1}(w)|^{2})^{2}\cdot\left|S_{F}(w)\right|\cdot\left|\frac{1}{(\phi^{-1})^{\prime}(w)^{2}}\right|.

Using the identity

\displaystyle|(\phi^{-1})^{\prime}(w)|=\frac{1-p^{2}}{(1-pw)^{2}},

we obtain the inequality

\displaystyle(1-|\phi^{-1}(w)|^{2})^{2}\cdot|(\phi^{-1})^{\prime}(w)|^{2}\leq\frac{1}{(1-p^{2})^{2}},

which leads to

\displaystyle\|S_{f}\|\leq\frac{\|S_{F}\|}{(1-p^{2})^{2}}\leq\frac{6k}{(1-p^{2})^{2}}.

Thus the desired inequality is established.

Next, to show that the bound is sharp, we consider the function

\displaystyle f_{0}(z)=\frac{z}{1-kz^{2}}.

This function is meromorphic and univalent in
\mathbb{D}
, has a simple pole at
z=\pm 1/{\sqrt{k}}\notin\mathbb{D}
, and admits a
k
-quasiconformal extension to
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
. A simple computation shows that

\displaystyle S_{f_{0}}(z)=\frac{6k}{(1-kz^{2})^{2}},

and hence, we have
\|S_{f_{0}}\|=6k
.

We now conjugate
f_{0}
with the Möbius map
\phi
(where
\phi
maps
0
to
p\in(0,1)
) to define a new function

\displaystyle f_{p}(z):=f_{0}(\phi^{-1}(z))=\frac{\phi^{-1}(z)}{1-k(\phi^{-1}(z))^{2}}.

Then
f_{p}\in\Sigma_{k}(p)
, and an easy computation leads to

\displaystyle\|S_{f_{p}}\|=\frac{6k}{(1-p^{2})^{2}}

which shows that the bound is sharp.
∎

The following result can be regarded as a generalization of
[
3
, Theorem 1]
. In that work, a sufficient criterion was established for a sense-preserving harmonic mapping in a convex domain to admit a quasiconformal extension. Our theorem broadens this framework by allowing comparison with an auxiliary analytic univalent function
\eta
having bounded derivative and positive co-Lipschitz constant, thereby yielding a more flexible condition for quasiconformal extendability.

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}
be a bounded convex domain and let
f=h+g:\Omega\to\mathbb{C}
be a sense-preserving harmonic mapping. Suppose there exists an analytic univalent function
\eta:\Omega\to\mathbb{C}
with

(i)

K(\eta,\Omega)>0
(the co-Lipschitz constant), and

(ii)

\displaystyle\sup_{z\in\Omega}|\eta^{\prime}(z)|<\infty
,

such that for some
k\in[0,1)
,

(2.12)

|h^{\prime}(z)-\eta^{\prime}(z)|+|g^{\prime}(z)|\;\leq\;k\,K(\eta,\Omega),\;z\in\Omega.

Then
f
is
k
-quasiconformal on
\Omega
and admits a quasiconformal extension to the whole plane
\mathbb{C}
.

Proof of the Theorem
2.5
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}
be a bounded convex domain and suppose
f=h+g:\Omega\to\mathbb{C}
is a sense-preserving harmonic mapping. Assume that
\eta:\Omega\to\mathbb{C}
is an analytic univalent function satisfying conditions (i)–(ii), and

(2.13)

|h^{\prime}(z)-\eta^{\prime}(z)|+|g^{\prime}(z)|\;\leq\;k\,K(\eta,\Omega),\quad z\in\Omega,

for some
k\in[0,1)
.

By the definition of the co-Lipschitz constant, we know that for any analytic function
F
,

\displaystyle K(F,\Omega)\leq|F^{\prime}(z)|,\quad z\in\Omega.

Moreover, as noted in
[
3
]
, an analytic function
F
is bi-Lipschitz on
\Omega
if, and only if,
F^{\prime}
is bounded on
\Omega
and
K(F,\Omega)>0
. Therefore, conditions (i) and (ii) together imply that
\eta
is bi-Lipschitz on
\Omega
. In particular, there exists
M>0
such that
|\eta^{\prime}(z)|\leq M
for all
z\in\Omega
, and
|\eta^{\prime}(z)|\geq K(\eta,\Omega)>0
.

From (
2.13
), using the lower bound
|\eta^{\prime}(z)|\geq K(\eta,\Omega)
, we obtain

\displaystyle|\omega_{f}(z)|=\frac{|g^{\prime}(z)|}{|h^{\prime}(z)|}=\frac{|g^{\prime}(z)|}{|h^{\prime}(z)-\eta^{\prime}(z)+\eta^{\prime}(z)|}\leq\frac{|g^{\prime}(z)|}{|\eta^{\prime}(z)|-|h^{\prime}(z)-\eta^{\prime}(z)|}<\frac{k\,K(\eta,\Omega)}{K(\eta,\Omega)}=k.

Hence
\|\omega_{f}\|_{\infty}\leq k<1
, so
f
is sense-preserving and
k
-quasiconformal on
\Omega
.

Let
z_{1},z_{2}\in\Omega
with
z_{1}\neq z_{2}
. Since
\Omega
is convex, the straight line segment
[z_{1},z_{2}]
lies in
\Omega
. Then

\displaystyle|f(z_{2})-f(z_{1})|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{[z_{1},z_{2}]}h^{\prime}(z)\,dz+g^{\prime}(z)\,dz\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{[z_{1},z_{2}]}\eta^{\prime}(z)\,dz+\int_{[z_{1},z_{2}]}\big(h^{\prime}(z)-\eta^{\prime}(z)\big)\,dz+g^{\prime}(z)\,dz\right|

\displaystyle\geq\left|\eta(z_{2})-\eta(z_{1})\right|-\int_{[z_{1},z_{2}]}\big(|h^{\prime}(z)-\eta^{\prime}(z)|+|g^{\prime}(z)|\big)\,|dz|

\displaystyle\geq K(\eta,\Omega)\,|z_{2}-z_{1}|-k\,K(\eta,\Omega)\,|z_{2}-z_{1}|

\displaystyle=(1-k)\,K(\eta,\Omega)\,|z_{2}-z_{1}|.

Similarly, by using
\displaystyle\sup_{\Omega}|\eta^{\prime}|\leq M
, we obtain

\displaystyle|f(z_{2})-f(z_{1})|\;\leq\;(M+k\,K(\eta,\Omega))\,|z_{2}-z_{1}|.

Thus
f
is bi-Lipschitz on
\Omega
.

Since
f
is bi-Lipschitz and sense-preserving on the bounded convex domain
\Omega
, Lemma 1 of
[
3
]
applies to yield a bi-Lipschitz (hence quasiconformal) extension of
f
to the entire complex plane
\mathbb{C}
. If
k=0
, then (
2.13
) gives
h^{\prime}=\eta^{\prime}
and
g^{\prime}\equiv 0
, so
f=\eta+C
for some constant
C
. Since
\eta
is bi-Lipschitz, it has a quasiconformal extension to
\mathbb{C}
by
[
3
, Lemma 1]
, and so does
f
. The proof is completed.
∎

3.
Concluding remark

An alternative proof of the inequality in Theorem 1.1 can be obtained by adapting Lehto’s principle (see
[
13
, II.3.3]
) together with Chichra’s area theorem
[
5
]
for higher-order poles.
Let
\ell^{2}
denote the Hilbert space of complex sequences

x=\{x_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
with norm

\displaystyle\|x\|_{\ell^{2}}=\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|x_{n}|^{2}\right)^{1/2}.

It suffices to establish the inequality for functions in

\Sigma^{0}_{k}(p)
, the subclass of
\Sigma^{(m)}_{k}(p)
with vanishing
constant term. Suppose
f\in\Sigma^{0}_{k}(p)
extends to a

k
-quasiconformal mapping of
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
with complex
dilatation
\mu
. Then
|\mu|\leq k

a.e.
in
\mathbb{D}^{*}
and
\mu=0

in
\mathbb{D}
. By the measurable Riemann mapping theorem, for each

t\in\mathbb{D}
there exists a unique quasiconformal self-map
f_{t}
of
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
with complex dilatation
t\mu/k
and

f_{t}|_{\mathbb{D}}\in\Sigma^{0}(p)
. Writing

\displaystyle f_{t}(z)=\frac{a_{-1}}{(z-p)^{m}}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}(t)z^{n},\;z\in\mathbb{D},

the coefficients
a_{n}(t)
depend holomorphically on
t
.
Define
\sigma(t)=\{\sqrt{n}\,a_{n}(t)\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\in\ell^{2}
.
By Chichra’s theorem for higher-order poles, one has

\|\sigma(t)\|_{\ell^{2}}\leq(1-p^{2})^{-m}
for all
t\in\mathbb{D}
.
Thus
\sigma:\mathbb{D}\to\ell^{2}
is a bounded analytic map into a
Hilbert space. Since
\sigma(0)=0
, the generalized Schwarz lemma yields

\displaystyle\|\sigma(t)\|_{\ell^{2}}\leq\frac{|t|}{(1-p^{2})^{m}}\ \mbox{for}\;t\in\mathbb{D}.

In particular, taking
t=a_{-1}k
gives the desired inequality (
2.5
) of Theorem
2.1
.

Super-Penrose & \And Witten Transforms for SCFT 3
Deep Mazumdar

Abstract
The study of three dimensional CFT correlators in twistor space has recently garnered a significant interest. Conformal symmetry acts linearly in the twistor space, which streamlines the analysis. Moreover, twistors provide a connection to the position and momentum space through the Penrose and Witten transforms, respectively. In this work, we develop the supersymmetric versions of Penrose and Witten transforms for three dimensional superconformal field theories for 𝒩 = 1 ​ to ​ 4 \mathcal{N}=1\;\textrm{to}\;4 . We derive two and three point functions in the position and momentum superspace for the 𝒩 = 1 \mathcal{N}=1 scenario using these transforms. Extending this setup to higher supersymmetries turns out to be a simple extension of the 𝒩 = 1 \mathcal{N}=1 case, which aligns with the inherent simplicity of supertwistors.

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{RP}^{1}}D\lambda_{1}D\lambda_{2}\int dc_{12}\;\textrm{Sgn}(c_{12})\textrm{exp}(-ic_{12}\lambda_{1a}\lambda_{2b}(\tilde{X}_{12}^{-})^{ab})=\frac{1}{\textrm{Det}|(\tilde{X}_{12}^{-})^{ab}|}=\frac{1}{2\tilde{x}_{12}^{2}},

(31)

Thus, the final result using (

\displaystyle\langle 0|{\mathbf{J}}_{s}(x_{1},\theta_{1}){\mathbf{J}}_{s}(x_{2},\theta_{2})|0\rangle=\frac{(\xi_{1m}(\tilde{X}^{-}_{12})^{m}_{n}\xi_{2}^{n})^{2s}}{\tilde{x}_{12}^{4s+2}}=\frac{P^{2s}_{3}}{\tilde{x}^{2}_{12}},

where

is now the supersymmetric invariant building block mentioned in

. Thus, the position superspace two point function (

25
) via a super-Penrose transform over. We now move on to three point functions.

Three point functions

The general form of three point functions is given by
Bala:2025jbh

\displaystyle\langle 0|\mathbf{\hat{J}}_{s_{1}}(\lambda_{1},\bar{\mu}_{1},\psi_{1})\mathbf{\hat{J}}_{s_{2}}(\lambda_{2},\bar{\mu}_{2},\psi_{2})\mathbf{\hat{J}}_{s_{3}}(\lambda_{3},\bar{\mu}_{3},\psi_{3})|0\rangle=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j,k=1\\
i<j\\
k\neq i,j\end{subarray}}^{3}\delta^{[s_{i}+s_{j}-s_{k}]}(\lambda_{i}\cdot\bar{\mu}_{j}-\lambda_{j}\cdot\bar{\mu}_{i}-\psi_{i}\psi_{j}).

(33)

Let us begin with the simplest case of three point function of scalar supermultiplets.

\langle 0|\mathbf{J_{0}}\mathbf{J_{0}}\mathbf{J_{0}}|0\rangle

Consider scalar supermultiplets of scaling dimension
\Delta=1
. The twistor superspace expression for this three point function is given by

\displaystyle\langle 0|\mathbf{\hat{J}_{0}}(\lambda_{1},\bar{\mu}_{1},\psi_{1})\mathbf{\hat{J}_{0}}(\lambda_{2},\bar{\mu}_{2},\psi_{2})\mathbf{\hat{J}_{0}}(\lambda_{3},\bar{\mu}_{3},\psi_{3})|0\rangle

\displaystyle=\delta(\lambda_{1}\cdot\bar{\mu}_{2}-\lambda_{2}\cdot\bar{\mu}_{1}-\psi_{1}\psi_{2})\delta(\lambda_{2}\cdot\bar{\mu}_{3}-\lambda_{3}\cdot\bar{\mu}_{2}-\psi_{2}\psi_{3})\delta(\lambda_{3}\cdot\bar{\mu}_{1}-\lambda_{1}\cdot\bar{\mu}_{3}-\psi_{3}\psi_{1}).

(34)

The super-Penrose transform is defined as

\displaystyle\langle 0|\mathbf{J_{0}}(x_{1},\theta_{1})\mathbf{J_{0}}(x_{2},\theta_{2})\mathbf{J_{0}}(x_{3},\theta_{3})|0\rangle

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{RP}^{1}}D\lambda_{1}D\lambda_{2}D\lambda_{3}(\xi_{1}\cdot\lambda_{1})^{0}(\xi_{2}\cdot\lambda_{2})^{0}(\xi_{3}\cdot\lambda_{3})^{0}\;\langle 0|\mathbf{\hat{J}_{0}}(\lambda_{1},\bar{\mu}_{1},\psi_{1})\mathbf{\hat{J}_{0}}(\lambda_{2},\bar{\mu}_{2},\psi_{2})\mathbf{\hat{J}_{0}}(\lambda_{3},\bar{\mu}_{3},\psi_{3})|0\rangle\bigg\rvert_{\mathfrak{X}}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{RP}^{1}}D\lambda_{1}D\lambda_{2}D\lambda_{3}\;\delta(\lambda_{1a}\lambda_{2b}(\tilde{X}_{12}^{-})^{ab})\delta(\lambda_{2c}\lambda_{3d}(\tilde{X}_{23}^{-})^{cd})\delta(\lambda_{3e}\lambda_{1f}(\tilde{X}_{31}^{-})^{ef}).

(35)

Now we projectively integrate over
\lambda_{1}
and
\lambda_{2}
, which leads to the following expression

\displaystyle\langle 0|\mathbf{J_{0}}(x_{1},\theta_{1})\mathbf{J_{0}}(x_{2},\theta_{2})\mathbf{J_{0}}(x_{3},\theta_{3})|0\rangle=\int_{\mathbb{RP}^{1}}D\lambda_{3}\;\delta(\lambda_{3a}(\tilde{X}_{31}^{+})^{a}_{b}(\tilde{X}_{12}^{+})^{b}_{c}(\tilde{X}_{23}^{+})^{c}_{d}\lambda_{3}^{d}),

(36)

where we have used the a simple property of projective integrals

\displaystyle\int D\lambda\;f(\lambda)\delta(\lambda\cdot\kappa)=f(\kappa).

(37)

Returning to (
36
), we take note of the fact that the argument in the delta function must be symmetric in the indices ‘a’ and ‘d’ when both of these indices are lowered. Although this was apparent for the non-supersymmetric case
Baumann:2024ttn
, it is not the case for the supersymmetric scenario. This happens to be one of the many crucial nuances associated with the projective integrals in the supersymmetric setting. Thus, we symmetrize the argument as follows

\displaystyle\delta_{Sym}\big(\lambda_{3a}(\tilde{X}_{31}^{+})^{a}_{b}(\tilde{X}_{12}^{+})^{b}_{c}(\tilde{X}_{23}^{+})^{c}_{d}\lambda_{3}^{d})

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{1}{2}\;\delta\Bigg(\frac{\lambda_{3a}(\tilde{X}_{31}^{+})^{a}_{b}(\tilde{X}_{12}^{+})^{b}_{c}(\tilde{X}_{23}^{+})^{c}_{d}\lambda_{3}^{d}+(\lambda_{3a}(\tilde{X}_{31}^{+})^{a}_{b}(\tilde{X}_{12}^{+})^{b}_{c}(\tilde{X}_{23}^{+})^{c}_{d}\lambda_{3}^{d})^{T}}{2}\Bigg)

\displaystyle=\delta\big(\lambda_{3a}((\tilde{X}_{31}^{+})^{a}_{b}(\tilde{X}_{12}^{+})^{b}_{c}(\tilde{X}_{23}^{+})^{c}_{d}+(\tilde{X}_{23}^{-})^{a}_{b}(\tilde{X}_{12}^{-})^{b}_{c}(\tilde{X}_{31}^{-})^{c}_{d})\lambda_{3}^{d}\big),

(38)

where we have used the fact that
[(\tilde{X}_{ij}^{\pm})_{a}^{b}]^{T}=(\tilde{X}_{ij}^{\mp})_{b}^{a}
. Having symmetrized the argument of the integrand in (
36
), we notice that the integral is of the following form

\displaystyle\int D\lambda\,\delta(\lambda\cdot\kappa\cdot\lambda)\propto\frac{1}{\sqrt{|\det\kappa|}}.

(39)

Thus, its evaluation results in

\displaystyle\langle 0|\mathbf{J_{0}}(x_{1},\theta_{1})\mathbf{J_{0}}(x_{2},\theta_{2})\mathbf{J_{0}}(x_{3},\theta_{3})|0\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\textrm{Det}((\tilde{X}_{31}^{+})^{a}_{b}(\tilde{X}_{12}^{+})^{b}_{c}(\tilde{X}_{23}^{+})^{c}_{d}+(\tilde{X}_{23}^{-})^{a}_{b}(\tilde{X}_{12}^{-})^{b}_{c}(\tilde{X}_{31}^{-})^{c}_{d}))}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\tilde{x}_{12}\tilde{x}_{23}\tilde{x}_{31}},

(40)

which is the three point function for scalar supermultiplets
Nizami:2013tpa
. Thus, we observe that the super-Penrose transform leads to the correct three point functions in position superspace. Let us now move to the cases involving spinning supermultiplets.

\langle 0|\mathbf{J}_{\frac{1}{2}}\mathbf{J}_{\frac{1}{2}}\mathbf{J_{0}}|0\rangle

We now work out a supercorrelator involving two conserved supercurrents of spin half and one scalar supercurrent of
\Delta=1
. The corresponding three point function in twistor superspace is

\displaystyle\langle 0|\mathbf{\hat{J}}_{\frac{1}{2}}(\lambda_{1},\bar{\mu}_{1},\psi_{1})\mathbf{\hat{J}}_{\frac{1}{2}}(\lambda_{2},\bar{\mu}_{2},\psi_{2})\mathbf{\hat{J}}_{0}(\lambda_{3},\bar{\mu}_{3},\psi_{3})|0\rangle

\displaystyle=\delta^{[1]}(\lambda_{1}\cdot\bar{\mu}_{2}-\lambda_{2}\cdot\bar{\mu}_{1}-\psi_{1}\cdot\psi_{2})\delta(\lambda_{2}\cdot\bar{\mu}_{3}-\lambda_{3}\cdot\bar{\mu}_{2}-\psi_{2}\cdot\psi_{3})\delta(\lambda_{3}\cdot\bar{\mu}_{1}-\lambda_{1}\cdot\bar{\mu}_{3}-\psi_{3}\cdot\psi_{1}).

(41)

The super-Penrose transform after imposing the incidence relations (

) is

\displaystyle\langle 0|\mathbf{J}_{\frac{1}{2}}(x_{1},\theta_{1})\mathbf{J}_{\frac{1}{2}}(x_{2},\theta_{2})\mathbf{J}_{0}(x_{3},\theta_{3})|0\rangle

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{RP}^{1}}D\lambda_{1}D\lambda_{2}D\lambda_{3}(\xi_{1}\cdot\lambda_{1})(\xi_{2}\cdot\lambda_{2})\;\langle 0|\mathbf{\hat{J}}_{\frac{1}{2}}(\lambda_{1},\bar{\mu}_{1},\psi_{1})\mathbf{\hat{J}}_{\frac{1}{2}}(\lambda_{2},\bar{\mu}_{2},\psi_{2})\mathbf{\hat{J}}_{0}(\lambda_{3},\bar{\mu}_{3},\psi_{3})|0\rangle\bigg\rvert_{\mathfrak{X}}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{RP}^{1}}D\lambda_{1}D\lambda_{2}D\lambda_{3}(\xi_{1}\cdot\lambda_{1})(\xi_{2}\cdot\lambda_{2})\;\delta^{[1]}(\lambda_{1a}\lambda_{2b}(\tilde{X}_{12}^{-})^{ab})\delta(\lambda_{2c}\lambda_{3d}(\tilde{X}_{23}^{-})^{cd})\delta(\lambda_{3e}\lambda_{1f}(\tilde{X}_{31}^{-})^{ef}).

(42)

Performing the projective integrals over
\lambda_{2}\And\lambda_{3}
yields

\displaystyle\langle 0|\mathbf{J}_{\frac{1}{2}}(x_{1},\theta_{1})\mathbf{J}_{\frac{1}{2}}(x_{2},\theta_{2})\mathbf{J}_{0}(x_{3},\theta_{3})|0\rangle

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{RP}^{1}}D\lambda_{1}\big(\xi_{1m}\lambda_{1}^{m}\big)\big(\xi_{2p}(\tilde{X}^{+}_{23})^{p}_{q}(\tilde{X}^{+}_{31})^{q}_{r}\lambda_{1}^{r}\big)\delta^{[1]}_{Sym}(\lambda_{1a}(\tilde{X}_{12}^{-})^{a}_{b}(\tilde{X}_{23}^{-})^{b}_{c}(\tilde{X}_{31}^{-})^{c}_{d}\lambda_{1}^{d}),

(43)

where we have symmetrized the argument of the final integral using (
3
).

At this stage, we conjure an inverse operator that acts as a derivative on the delta function under the integral, much like in (
3
)

\displaystyle\int D\lambda\;\lambda_{a}\;\delta^{[n]}_{sym}(\lambda\cdot\kappa\cdot\lambda)=\int D\lambda\;\kappa^{-1}_{ab}\frac{\partial}{\partial\lambda_{b}}\delta^{[n-1]}_{sym}(\lambda\cdot\kappa\cdot\lambda).

(44)

For our purposes, we use the fact that the argument ‘
\kappa
’ is symmetric in its indices. Moreover, the square of the argument of the delta function in our case is equal to its determinant. Thus, we define the inverse operator
\kappa^{-1}
as

\displaystyle(\kappa^{-1})_{a}^{d}=\Bigg(\frac{(\tilde{X}_{12}^{+})^{b}_{a}(\tilde{X}_{23}^{+})^{c}_{b}(\tilde{X}_{31}^{+})^{d}_{c}+(\tilde{X}_{31}^{-})^{b}_{a}(\tilde{X}_{23}^{-})^{c}_{b}(\tilde{X}_{12}^{-})^{d}_{c}}{2\;\tilde{x}_{12}^{2}\tilde{x}_{23}^{2}\tilde{x}_{31}^{2}}\Bigg)

(45)

This allows us to insert the inverse operator and re-express (
3
) as a partial integral

\displaystyle\langle 0|\mathbf{J}_{\frac{1}{2}}(x_{1},\theta_{1})\mathbf{J}_{\frac{1}{2}}(x_{2},\theta_{2})\mathbf{J}_{0}(x_{3},\theta_{3})|0\rangle

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{RP}^{1}}D\lambda_{1}\big(\xi_{1m}\lambda_{1}^{m}\big)\big(\xi_{2p}(\tilde{X}^{+}_{23})^{p}_{q}(\tilde{X}^{+}_{31})^{q}_{r}\big)(\kappa^{-1})_{k}^{r}\frac{\partial}{\partial\lambda_{1k}}\delta_{Sym}\big(\lambda_{1a}(\tilde{X}_{12}^{+})^{a}_{b}(\tilde{X}_{23}^{+})^{b}_{c}(\tilde{X}_{31}^{+})^{c}_{d}\lambda_{1}^{d}\big).

(46)

An important comment is in order at this stage. The manifest symmetrization of the argument in the delta function requires the addition of the argument and its transpose (
3
). This point is reflected even in the construction of the inverse operator (

Integrating by parts, we have the following expression

\displaystyle\langle 0|\mathbf{J}_{\frac{1}{2}}(x_{1},\theta_{1})\mathbf{J}_{\frac{1}{2}}(x_{2},\theta_{2})\mathbf{J}_{0}(x_{3},\theta_{3})|0\rangle

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{RP}^{1}}D\lambda_{1}\delta_{Sym}\big(\lambda_{1a}(\tilde{X}_{12}^{+})^{a}_{b}(\tilde{X}_{23}^{+})^{b}_{c}(\tilde{X}_{31}^{+})^{c}_{d}\lambda_{1}^{d}\big)\big(\xi_{2p}(\tilde{X}^{+}_{23})^{p}_{q}(\tilde{X}^{+}_{31})^{q}_{r}\big)(\kappa^{-1})_{k}^{r}\frac{\partial}{\partial\lambda_{1k}}\big(\xi_{1m}\lambda_{1}^{m}\big),

(47)

where the total derivative term is zero. After substituting (
45
) and performing a few simplifications, we perform the final projective integral using (
39
). This results in

\displaystyle\langle 0|\mathbf{J}_{\frac{1}{2}}(x_{1},\theta_{1})\mathbf{J}_{\frac{1}{2}}(x_{2},\theta_{2})\mathbf{J}_{0}(x_{3},\theta_{3})|0\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\tilde{x}_{12}\tilde{x}_{23}\tilde{x}_{31}}\Bigg(\frac{\xi_{1m}(\tilde{X}_{12}^{+})^{m}_{n}\xi_{2}^{n}}{\tilde{x}^{2}_{12}}\Bigg)+\frac{1}{2\tilde{x}_{12}\tilde{x}_{23}\tilde{x}_{31}}\Bigg(\frac{\xi_{1a}(\tilde{X}_{31}^{-})^{a}_{b}(\tilde{X}_{23}^{-})^{b}_{c}(\tilde{X}_{12}^{-})^{c}_{d}(\tilde{X}_{31}^{-})^{d}_{e}(\tilde{X}_{23}^{-})^{e}_{f}\xi_{2}^{f}}{\tilde{x}_{12}^{2}\tilde{x}_{23}^{2}\tilde{x}_{31}^{2}}\Bigg).

(48)

Although the first term manifestly assumes the building block
P_{3}
, one can see that the second one is also invariant under supertranslation and superinversion. The key point here is that the relative coefficient between these two terms is fixed due to conservation. Recall that the supercurrents were conserved from the word go in twistor superspace
Bala:2025jbh
, hence this reflects in position superspace as well. We checked numerically at this point that the final result matches with
Nizami:2013tpa

\displaystyle\langle 0|\mathbf{J}_{\frac{1}{2}}(x_{1},\theta_{1})\mathbf{J}_{\frac{1}{2}}(x_{2},\theta_{2})\mathbf{J}_{0}(x_{3},\theta_{3})|0\rangle=

\displaystyle\frac{P_{3}-iR_{1}R_{2}}{\tilde{x}_{12}\tilde{x}_{23}\tilde{x}_{31}}.

(49)

The other supercorrelators can also be obtained by following a similar algorithm. We present a detailed example for the super-Penrose transform in the appendix
D
. Thus, one can obtain the supercorrelators in position superspace from twistor superspace via the super-Penrose transform (
24
). We now move on to define the super-Witten transform.

4
Super-Witten transform for
\mathcal{N}=1

In the section
2
, we saw how the Witten transform (
11
) maps from twistor space to momentum space via a half-Fourier transform. The super-Witten transform is the supersymmetric generalization of this, which involves a half-Fourier transform over both: the bosonic coordinate ‘
\bar{\mu}
’ along with the fermionic coordinate ‘
\psi
’. Although this is a straightforward operation in four dimensional case

, it is a little cumbersome for three dimensional supertwistors. This stems from the way in which the Grassmann twistor ‘
\psi
’ is related to the Grassmann spinor ‘
\theta_{a}
’. We urge the reader to refer section 3.2 and appendix C of
Jain:2023idr
for a detailed commentary over this matter.

4.1
Super-Witten transform: Supertwistors
\rightarrow
momentum superspace

Consider the case of
\mathcal{N}=1
supersymmetry in three dimensions. The super-Witten transform maps from the twistor superspace to the spinor-helicity variables in momentum superspace

\displaystyle(\lambda,\bar{\mu},\psi)\longrightarrow(\lambda,\bar{\lambda},\eta,\bar{\eta}).

(50)

For a function
\hat{\mathbf{f}}_{s}(\lambda,\bar{\mu},\psi)
of spin ‘s’ in twistor superspace, the super-Witten transform is defined in the following manner

7

7

We describe the algorithm for
\hat{J}_{s}^{+}
here, but one can do the same for
\hat{J}_{s}^{-}
with the variables

1.

Define
\mathfrak{P}:=\psi=\frac{e^{-\frac{i\pi}{4}}}{2\sqrt{2}}(\chi+\eta)

Multiply the function by an overall factor of

Now, perform the inverse Grassmann twistor transform

\displaystyle\tilde{f}_{s}(\lambda,\bar{\mu},\eta,\bar{\eta})=\int d\chi\;\textrm{exp}\Big(\frac{\chi\bar{\eta}}{4}\Big)(\chi-\eta)\hat{f}_{s}\left(\lambda,\bar{\mu},\psi\right)\bigg\rvert_{\mathfrak{P}}.

(51)

4.

Augment the Witten transform (

\displaystyle\tilde{f}_{s}(\lambda,\bar{\lambda},\eta,\bar{\eta})=\int d^{2}\bar{\mu}\;\textrm{exp}(-i\bar{\lambda}_{a}\bar{\mu}^{a})\int d\chi\;\textrm{exp}\Big(\frac{\chi\bar{\eta}}{4}\Big)(\chi-\eta)\hat{f}_{s}\left(\lambda,\bar{\mu},\psi\right)\bigg\rvert_{\mathfrak{P}}.

(52)

5
The case of extended supersymmetries:
\mathcal{N}=\{2,3,4\}

Extended supersymmetries posit an important arena of study, namely with
\mathcal{N}=6
ABJM theories in three dimensions. Although manifest superspace techniques are not possible for such a high extent of supersymmetry, they form an important stepping stone in this direction. The traditional position superspace formalism
Buchbinder:2015qsa
;
Buchbinder:2015wia
;
Kuzenko:2016cmf
;
Jain:2022izp
is crucial to that end, but they become progressively challenging with a large number of tensor structures to deal with. Recently, it was shown in
Bala:2025jbh
that a supertwistor formalism is more amicable for studying supercorrelators up to
\mathcal{N}=4
supersymmetry in three dimensions. Using twistor superspace as the starting point, we develop the super-Penrose and super-Witten transforms for supercorrelators enjoying extended supersymmetry, starting with the former.

5.1
Super-Penrose transform

The super-Penrose transform for extended supersymmetry follows the same suit as in the
\mathcal{N}=1
case (
24
). It is a projective integral over
\mathbb{RP}^{1}
subject to the appropriate incidence relations for the bosonic and fermionic coordinates

6
Discussion and future direction

In this paper, we have developed supersymmetric versions of the Penrose and Witten transforms for three dimensional superconformal field theories with varied amounts of supersymmetry. The former allows to obtain expressions in the position superspace from their twistor superspace counterparts, while the latter leads to the corresponding momentum superspace versions. We demonstrate the utility of these transformations by deriving two and three point supercorrelators directly from twistor superspace results. Furthermore, we extend these constructions to theories with enhanced supersymmetry. Although the position superspace results have a large number of invariant structures to deal with in extended supersymmetries, deriving the result via the super-Penrose transform circumvents this difficulty.
There are several interesting avenues for further exploration, some of which are outlined below.

Non-conserved and R-charged supercurrents

In this work, we investigated supercorrelators of conserved supercurrents that are R-symmetry singlets. It would be interesting from a technical standpoint to extend our analysis to more generic supermultiplets. This shall greatly aid in studying BPS states in CFT. This will amount to studying the supersymmetric version of
Bala:2025qxr
in the twistor superspace.

Higher point functions

Another important direction is the construction of higher-point functions in CFTs. In four dimensions, spinor-helicity methods
Britto:2005fq
and twistor techniques
Mason:2009sa
have been instrumental in recursively computing higher point tree-level amplitudes. Extending such recursion techniques to three dimensional CFTs will be crucial in this endeavor.

Supersymmetric Chern-Simons matter theories

Supersymmetric Chern-Simons matter theories provide a quintessential example where higher spin symmetry is slightly broken. Among these, the
\mathcal{N}=6
ABJM theory is of paramount importance as it is the maximally supersymmetric version of it. Although a fully manifest study of this theory is not possible, it is desirable to analyze its supercorrelators. One promising approach to tackle this problem is through the harmonic superspace formalism. We hope to revisit this issue and explore it further in the near future.

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|\text{Det}(\lambda_{1}^{a}\lambda_{1}^{b}\,\lambda_{2}^{c}\lambda_{2}^{d}\,\lambda_{1}^{e}\lambda_{2}^{f})|}\delta\left(\frac{\langle\bar{2}1\rangle}{\langle 12\rangle}\right)\delta\left(\frac{\langle\bar{1}2\rangle}{\langle 12\rangle}\right)\delta\left(\frac{\langle\bar{1}1\rangle}{\langle 12\rangle}-\frac{\langle\bar{2}2\rangle}{\langle 12\rangle}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|\langle 12\rangle|^{3}}\delta\left(\frac{\langle\bar{2}1\rangle}{\langle 12\rangle}\right)\delta\left(\frac{\langle\bar{1}2\rangle}{\langle 12\rangle}\right)\delta\left(\frac{\langle\bar{1}1\rangle}{\langle 12\rangle}-\frac{\langle\bar{2}2\rangle}{\langle 12\rangle}\right).

(102)

The momentum-conserving delta function for three point functions can be derived similarly.

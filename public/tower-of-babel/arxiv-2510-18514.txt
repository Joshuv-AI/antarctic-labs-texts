Probing Nuclear Interactions Through Isotope Shift Spectroscopy of Mercury
Thorsten Groh, Felix Affeld, Simon Stellmer

Abstract
We present precision isotope shift spectroscopy of the 6 ​ s 2 ​ S 0 1 → 6 ​ s ​ 6 ​ p ​ P 1 3 \mathrm{6s^{2}}\,{}^{1}\mathrm{S}_{0}{{\rightarrow}\,}\mathrm{6s\,6p}\,{}^{3}\mathrm{P}_{1} intercombination line and the 6 ​ s ​ 6 ​ p ​ P 1 3 → 6 ​ s ​ 6 ​ d ​ D J 3 \mathrm{6s\,6p}\,{}^{3}\mathrm{P}_{1}{{\rightarrow}\,}\mathrm{6s\,6d}\,{}^{3}\mathrm{D}_{J} ( J = 1 , 2 J=1,2 ) transitions in neutral mercury, performed on the five naturally abundant even isotopes, including the low-abundant isotope Hg 196 {}^{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 196$}}_{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Hg} . Using laser-cooled atoms in a magneto-optical trap, we achieve uncertainties down to 20 kHz 20\text{\,}\mathrm{kHz} , resolving the isotope shiftsto a fractional uncertainty of ∼ 2 × 10 − 6 \sim$2\text{\times}{10}^{-6}$ . A King plotanalysis comparing our S 0 1 → P 1 3 {}^{1}\mathrm{S}_{0}{{\rightarrow}}{}^{3}\mathrm{P}_{1} data to previous results on the 6 ​ s ​ 6 ​ p ​ P 2 3 → 6 ​ s ​ 7 ​ s ​ S 1 3 \mathrm{6s\,6p}\,{}^{3}\mathrm{P}_{2}{{\rightarrow}\,}\mathrm{6s\,7s}\,{}^{3}\mathrm{S}_{1} line reveals a nonlinearity with 4.9 ​ σ 4.9\,\sigma significance. Our generalized King plotnonlinear decomposition analysis discusses potential contributions from quadratic ( ∝ δ ​ ⟨ r 2 ⟩ 2 \propto\delta\langle r^{2}\rangle^{2} ) and higher order field shifts ( ∝ δ ​ ⟨ r 4 ⟩ \propto\delta\langle r^{4}\rangle ) also induced by nuclear deformations. These measurements yield new insights into the structure of the Hgnucleus and provide benchmarks for nuclear-structure models. They further establish mercuryas a potential platform to search for hypothetical Yukawa-type boson-mediated forces coupling electrons to neutrons.

Isotope shift—
The IS,
\delta\nu_{i}^{A-A^{\prime}}\equiv\nu_{i}^{A}-\nu_{i}^{A^{\prime}}\hskip-1.69998pt
, is the change in the frequency
\nu_{i}
of an electronic transition
i
between isotopes of mass numbers
A
and
A^{\prime}
.
To leading order perturbation theory, we can write

\delta\nu_{i}^{A-A^{\prime}}\!\!=F_{i}\,\delta\!\left\langle r^{2}\right\rangle^{A-A^{\prime}}\!\!\!+K_{i}\,\delta\mu^{A-A^{\prime}}\!+\sum_{\kappa}G_{i}^{(\kappa)}\,\delta\eta_{(\kappa)}^{A-A^{\prime}}\!\!,

(1)

where
F_{i}
,
K_{i}
and
G_{i}^{(\kappa)}
denote transition dependent electronic and the
\delta x^{A-A^{\prime}}\equiv x^{A}-x^{A^{\prime}}\hskip-1.69998pt
nuclear factors
Berengut and Oreshkina 2025
.

In heavy elements like mercury (Hg), the ISis dominated by the field shift (FS), which is proportional to the difference in mean-square nuclear charge radii
\delta\langle r^{2}\rangle^{A-A^{\prime}}\equiv\langle r^{2}\rangle^{A}-\langle r^{2}\rangle^{A^{\prime}}\hskip-1.69998pt
.
Originating from the overlap of the electronic wavefunction with the nuclear charge distribution, it is strongest for s-electrons: in Hg, the
\mathrm{6s^{2}}
ground state largely determines the ISof the intercombination line
Zhang et al. 2019
;
Schelfhout and McFerran 2022
.
The mass shift (MS), arising from the nuclear recoil, is proportional to the inverse nuclear mass difference,
\delta\mu^{A-A^{\prime}}\equiv{1}/{m^{A}}-{1}/{m^{A^{\prime}}}\hskip-1.69998pt
.
It approximately scales as
1/({m^{A}})^{2}
, making it strongest in the hydrogen-deuterium ISand dominates the IS in light systems, such as Ca, while being strongly suppressed in Hg
King 1984
.

The terms
\delta\eta_{(\kappa)}^{A-A^{\prime}}\hskip-1.69998pt
denote higher order (nuclear) corrections.
For heavy elements, higher order nuclear moments, like the quadratic FS (QFS)
\propto\delta(\langle r^{2}\rangle^{2})^{A-A^{\prime}}\hskip-1.69998pt
, are expected to be the next resolvable contributions
Counts et al. 2020
.
Recent ISSmeasurements in Ybions on the sub-hertz level
Counts et al. 2020
;
Figueroa et al. 2022
;
Ono et al. 2022
;
Hur et al. 2022
;
Door et al. 2025
;
Ishiyama et al. 2025
have revealed nuclear deformation (ND)contributions
\propto\delta\langle r^{4}\rangle^{A-A^{\prime}}\hskip-1.69998pt
(= quartic FS
Door et al. 2025
) reflecting prolate deformation of the nuclear charge distribution limiting BSMsearches
Berengut and Oreshkina 2025
.
By contrast, the near-spherical nuclei of Casuppress such corrections: early measurements confirmed linearity
Gebert et al. 2015
;
Röser et al. 2024
, while combined
{}^{\makebox[0.0pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}_{\makebox[0.0pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Ca^{+}}
and
{}^{\makebox[0.0pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}_{\makebox[0.0pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Ca^{14+}}
spectroscopy later uncovered a significant KPnonlinearity consistent with second-order mass shift (MS)
\propto 1/({m^{A}})^{2}-1/({m^{A^{\prime}}})^{2}
and nuclear polarization (NP)effects
Wilzewski et al. 2025
, the latter being a subtle electron interaction induced deformation of the nuclear charge distribution.
For certain transitions, second-order FSor MSeffects mix nearby levels, yielding large IScorrections
Berengut et al. 2020
that produced up to
32\text{\,}\mathrm{MHz}
shifts in samarium
Griffith et al. 1979
;
Griffith et al. 1981

Palmer and Stacey 1982

\phi

V_{\text{NP}}(r){=-}\alpha_{\text{NP}}\,(A-Z)\,e^{-m_{\phi}\,r}/r

King Plot Analysis—
To identify nuclear contributions and other subleading effects, we compare the mass-normalized ISs
\overline{\delta\nu}{}_{i}^{A-A^{\prime}}\hskip-1.69998pt
with
\overline{x}{}^{A-A^{\prime}}\equiv x^{A-A^{\prime}}/\delta\mu^{A-A^{\prime}}\hskip-1.69998pt
of two transitions
i
and
j
via a King plot (KP).
For five isotopes and four isotope pairs, the associated ISvectors
\bm{\overline{\delta\nu}}_{i}
span a four-dimensional space.
Eliminating
\bm{\delta\langle r^{2}\rangle}
in Eq. (
1
), the KPthen yields a linear relation between ISson lines
i
and
j
to leading order,

\underbracket{\bm{\overline{\delta\nu}}_{i}=F_{ij}\,\bm{\overline{\delta\nu}}_{j}+K_{ij}\,\bm{\mathbb{1}}}_{\text{linear KP}}+\sum_{\kappa}G_{ij}^{(\kappa)}\,\bm{\overline{\delta\eta}}_{(\kappa)}\,,

(2)

where
\bm{\mathbb{1}}=(1,1,1,1)^{\intercal}
denotes the vector of all ones,
F_{ij}=F_{i}/F_{j}
and
X_{ij}=X_{i}-X_{j}\,F_{ij}
for
X\in[K,G]
.
Deviations from linearity reveal higher-order nuclear effects or BSMcontributions.
The separation of nuclear and electronic factors allow for comparison of vastly different electronic transitions, including comparisons to muonic atoms
King 1984
and (highly-charged) ions
Rehbehn et al. 2023
;

Summary and Outlook—
This work reports first observation of KPnonlinearity in neutral Hgat the
20\text{\,}\mathrm{kHz}
level, revealing sensitivity to higher‑order nuclear effects relevant for tests of fundamental interactions.
Although current precision and theory predictions limit the GKPinterpretation, advancing electronic structure calculations alongside refined NDpredictions, as demonstrated for Yb, will enable nuclear
G_{i}^{(\kappa)}
and new physics
X_{i}
coupling estimates, turning HgISSinto a measurement of nuclear structure and a firm bound on BSMphysics.

Future gain in sensitivity will come from improved spectroscopy resolution on existing transitions or adding at least one (dipole-allowed) line with comparable uncertainty.
BSMsearches will strongly benefit from accessing ultra-narrow clock transitions such as the
{}^{1}\mathrm{S}_{0}{{\rightarrow}}{}^{3}\mathrm{P}_{0,\,2}

265.6\text{\,}\mathrm{nm}
and

Santra et al. 2005

These dipole-forbidden transitions, weakly enabled via hyperfine or field-induced mixing, offer
\mathrm{Hz}

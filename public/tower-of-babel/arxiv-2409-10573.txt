About almost covering subsets of the hypercube
Arijit Ghosh, Chandrima Kayal, Soumi Nandi

Abstract
Let 𝔽 \mathbb{F} be a field and consider the hypercube { 0 , 1 } n \{0,1\}^{n} in 𝔽 n \mathbb{F}^{n} . Sziklai and Weiner (Journal of Combinatorial Theory, Series A 2022) showed that if a polynomial P ⁡ ( X 1 , … , X n ) ∈ 𝔽 ⁡ [ X 1 , … , X n ] P(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})\in\mathbb{F}[X_{1},\dots,X_{n}] vanishes on every point of the hypercube { 0 , 1 } n \{0,1\}^{n} except those with at most r r many ones then the degree of the polynomial will be at least n − r n-r . This is a generalization of Alon and Füredi’s fundamental result (European Journal of Combinatorics 1993) about polynomials vanishing on every point of the hypercube except at the origin (point with all zero coordinates). Sziklai and Weiner proved their result using Möbius inversion formula and the Zeilberger method for proving binomial equalities. In this short note, we show that a stronger version of Sziklai and Weiner’s result can be derived directly from Alon and Füredi’s result. We also prove a multiplicity version of our results when 𝔽 = ℝ \mathbb{F}=\mathbb{R} .

Introduction

Let
\mathbb{F}
be a field (finite or infinite) and
\left\{0,1\right\}^{n}
is the
n
dimensional hypercube embedded in
\mathbb{F}^{n}
. Also, let
\mathbb{F}[X_{1},\dots,X_{n}]
denotes the polynomial ring over the field
\mathbb{F}
.
Alon and Füredi, proved the following influential result about the degrees of polynomials vanishing on the subsets of the hypercube using Combinatorial Nullstellensatz Theorem
[
AT92
,
Alo99
]
. Over the years both Combinatorial Nullstellensatz Theorem, and Alon and Furedi’s result have found multiple extensions and applications. The formal statement of the result is as follows:

Theorem 1

(Alon and Füredi
[
AF93
]
)
.

Suppose
P(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})
is a polynomial in
\mathbb{F}\left[X_{1},\dots,X_{n}\right]
such that
P(u)=0
for all
u\in\left\{0,1\right\}^{n}\setminus\{(0,\dots,0)\}
and
P(0,\dots,0)\neq 0
. Then
deg(P)\geq n
.

This result has inspired various generalizations and found applications in multiple areas. In this work, we will focus on one such generalization of Theorem
1
over the field
\mathbb{F}
. Specifically, for any subset
V\subset\left\{0,1\right\}^{n}
what is the minimum degree of the polynomial
P(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})\in\mathbb{F}[X_{1},\dots,X_{n}]
that vanishes over all points in
V
and is non-zero at least at one point
u\in\left\{0,1\right\}^{n}\setminus V
?

For any
u=(u_{1},\dots,u_{n})\in\left\{0,1\right\}^{n}
, by
weight
of
u
we mean the
L_{1}
-norm
\|u\|_{1}
of
u
, that is, the number of
1
’s in the coordinates of
u
.
Recently, using
Möbius inversion formula

[
Sta11
, Chapter 3]
and Zeilberger’s algorithm for proving binomial coefficient identities
[
PS95
]
, Sziklai and Weiner proved an interesting extension of Alon and Füredi’s result (Theorem
1
).

Theorem 2

(Sziklai and Weiner
[
SW23
]
)
.

Suppose
P(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})
is a polynomial in
\mathbb{F}[X_{1},\dots,X_{n}]
such that
P(u)\neq 0
for all
u\in\left\{0,1\right\}^{n}
with
\|u\|_{1}\leq r
, and
P(v)=0

for all
v\in\left\{0,1\right\}^{n}
with
\|v\|_{1}>r
. Then
\text{deg}(P)\geq n-r
.

Using Gröbner basis theory, Hegedüs gave a further generalization of Theorem
2
.

Theorem 3

(Hegedüs
[
Heg24
]
)
.

Suppose
P(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})
is a polynomial in
\mathbb{F}[X_{1},\dots,X_{n}]
such that
P(u)=0
for all
u\in\left\{0,1\right\}^{n}
with
\|u\|_{1}\leq k
, and there exists
v\in\left\{0,1\right\}^{n}
such that
\|v\|_{1}>k
and
P(v)\neq 0
. Then
\text{deg}(P)>k
.

To see why Theorem
3
is a generalization of Theorem
2
, suppose
R
is a polynomial in
\mathbb{F}[X_{1},\dots,X_{n}]
such that for any
u\in\left\{0,1\right\}^{n},\;R(u)=0
if and only if
\|u\|_{1}>r
.
Now consider the polynomial
P(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})=R(1-X_{1},\dots,1-X_{n})
. Observe that
\text{deg}(P)=\text{deg}(R)
and
P(u)=0
except for all
u\in\left\{0,1\right\}^{n}
with
\|u\|_{1}>n-r-1
. Therefore, from Theorem
3
, we have
\text{deg}(P)\geq n-r
.

The main objective of this short note is to show that
the following generalization of Theorem
2
and Theorem
3
directly follows from Alon and Füredi’s result (Theorem
1
).

Theorem 4

(Main result)
.

Let
S
be a proper subset of
\left\{0,1\right\}^{n}
.
Suppose
P\in\mathbb{F}[X_{1},\dots,X_{n}]
be a polynomial with
P(u)=0
for all
u\in S
and there exists a
v\in\left\{0,1\right\}^{n}\setminus S
with
P(v)\neq 0
. Then,

\text{deg}(P)\geq\max\{w,n-W\},

where
w=\min\left\{\|u\|_{1}:P(u)\neq 0\right\}
and
W=\max\left\{\|u\|_{1}:P(u)\neq 0\right\}
.

Observe that Theorem
3
is a simple corollary of the above result, and we have already seen that Theorem
3
is a
generalization of Theorem
2
. Moreover, several recent results
[
GKN23
,
Ven22
,
GKNV23
]
about
degrees of real polynomials vanishings on subsets of
\{0,1\}^{n}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}

can be obtained as corollaries of Theorem
4
. Note that in circuit complexity
one studies the degrees of polynomials in
\mathbb{Z}_{N}[X_{1},\dots,X_{n}]
, where
\mathbb{Z}_{N}:=\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}
and
N\in\mathbb{N}
, that vanishes on specific subsets of the hypercube

\{0,1\}^{n}
to prove
explicit lower bounds for the algebraic degree of Boolean functions like OR
[
BBR94
]
and Majority
[
Sze89
]
, etc. In contrast, our result holds for any subset of
\left\{0,1\right\}^{n}
considered to be the zero set of the polynomial. Furthermore, compared to the previous results
[
GKNV23
]
, our result also applies to non-symmetric cases. That is, we do not require the vanishing set of the polynomials to be symmetric. In this sense, Theorem
4
can break the symmetry.
Additionally when
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{R}
, we can prove a multiplicity version of Theorem
4
, see Theorem
10
.

Covering with polynomials from
\mathbb{F}[X_{1},\dots,X_{n}]

We now proceed to prove Theorem
4
.

Proof of Theorem
4
.

We can assume that
w\geq 1
, as
w=0
case directly follows from Theorem
1
.
Without loss of generality we can also assume that
w\geq n-W
, otherwise, we work with the polynomial
\widetilde{P}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n}):=P(1-X_{1},\dots,1-X_{n})
in place of the polynomial
P
.
Let
u=(u_{1},\dots,u_{n})\in\left\{0,1\right\}^{n}\setminus S
such that
\|u\|_{1}=w
and
P(u)\neq 0
. Then, for all
v\in\left\{0,1\right\}^{n}
with
\|v\|_{1}<w
, we have
P(v)=0
. Without loss of generality we may assume that
u_{i}=1
if and only if
i\in[w]
. Consider the polynomial

Q(X_{1},\dots,X_{w}):=P(1-X_{1},\dots,1-X_{w},0,\dots,0).

Then
\text{deg}(Q)\leq\text{deg}(P)
and
Q(0,\dots,0)=P(u)\neq 0
.
Now take any
y\in\left\{0,1\right\}^{w}\setminus\{(0,\dots,0)\}
. Then
Q(y)=P(\tilde{y})
, where
\tilde{y}_{i}=1-y_{i}
for all
i\in[w]
and
\tilde{y}_{i}=0
for all
i\in[n]\setminus[w]
. Since
\mathrm{wt}(\tilde{y})<w
, we have
P(\tilde{y})=0
. So the polynomial
Q(X_{1},\dots,X_{w})
vanishes everywhere on
\left\{0,1\right\}^{w}
, except at
(0,\dots,0)\in\left\{0,1\right\}^{w}
. Therefore by Theorem
1
,
\text{deg}(Q)\geq w
. Hence,
\text{deg}(P)\geq w
.
∎

Corollary 5
.

Suppose
P\in\mathbb{F}_{2}[X_{1},\dots,X_{n}]
such that
P
vanishes at
v\in\left\{0,1\right\}^{n}
if and only if
\|w\|_{1}=k
.
Then
\text{deg}(P)\geq\max\{k,n-k\}
.

Proof.

Let
Q\in\mathbb{F}_{2}[X_{1},\dots,X_{n}]
is defined by
Q(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})=1-P(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})
. Then
\text{deg}(Q)=\text{deg}(P)
and
Q
vanishes at each
u\in\left\{0,1\right\}^{n}
except when
\mathrm{wt}(u)=k
.
Then by Theorem
4
,
\text{deg}(Q)\geq\max\{k,n-k\}
.
∎

Remark 6
.

The above result is tight.

The following proposition establishes the

Proposition 7
.

Let
q\in\mathbb{N}
be a prime, and
0\leq k\leq n
satisfy the following:

•

q^{m-1}\leq n-k<q^{m}
and

•

k+1
is divisible by
q^{m}
.

If
P(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[X_{1},\dots,X_{n}]
is a polynomial that vanishes at each
u\in\left\{0,1\right\}^{n}
except when

then
\text{deg}(P)=\max\{k,n-k\}
.

We need the following result by Kummer
[
Kum52
]
to prove Proposition

.

Theorem 8

(Kummer
[
Kum52
]
)
.

Let
j
and
m
be integers such that
0\leq j\leq m
. Let
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}
and
p
be a prime. Then
p^{\alpha}
divides
\binom{m}{j}
if and only if
\alpha
carries are needed when adding
j
and

in base

.

Proof of Proposition
7
.

Without loss of generality we may assume that
k\geq\frac{n}{2}
, otherwise we will work on the polynomial
\tilde{P}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})=P(1-X_{1},\dots,1-X_{n})
.
We shall show that there exists

with
\text{deg}(P_{k})=k
that vanishes at each
v\in\left\{0,1\right\}^{n}
except when
\|u\|_{1}=k
. We define

P_{k}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n}):=\sum_{I\in\binom{[n]}{k}}\left(\prod_{i\in I}X_{i}\right).

Then
P_{k}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[X_{1},\dots,X_{n}]
satisfies the following:

•

\text{deg}(P_{k})=k
,

•

P_{k}(\tilde{u})=0
, for all
\tilde{u}\in\left\{0,1\right\}^{n}
with
\|\tilde{u}\|_{1}<k
,

•

P_{k}(u)=1
, for all

with
\|u\|_{1}=k
and

•

For all
u_{r}\in\left\{0,1\right\}^{n}
with
\|u_{r}\|_{1}=k+r
, where

, we have

P_{k}(u_{r})\equiv\binom{k+r}{k}\mod q.

We claim that for all
r\in[n-k]
, we have

\binom{k+r}{k}\equiv 0\mod q.

By our assumption,
k+1
is divisible by

. So in the expansion of

is

, for all

. So in the expansion of

, for all

Barrington, Beigel, and Rudich

4
Covering with multiplicities when
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{R}

Here we shall first study the following more generalized problem: For a given subset
S\subset\{0,1\}^{n}
and a natural number
t
, what is the minimum degree of a polynomial with real coefficients that vanishes at each point of
S
with multiplicity at least
t
and does not vanish at least at one point of
\mathcal{Q}^{n}\setminus S
?
A polynomial
P(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})\in\mathbb{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
is said to have a
zero of multiplicity
t
at
a\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
if
P
vanishes at
a
and all derivatives of
P
upto order
t-1
also vanish at
a
.

Given a polynomial
P(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})\in\mathbb{R}[X_{1},\dots,X_{n}]
we denote by
w_{t}(P)\in[n]
the largest number such that for all
u_{1}\in\{0,1\}^{n}
, with
\|u_{1}\|_{1}<w_{t}(P)
,
P
has a zero of multiplicity
t
at
u_{1}
, and additionally, there exists a
v_{1}\in\{0,1\}^{n}
with
\|v_{1}\|_{1}=w_{t}(P)
such that
P(v_{1})\neq 0
. Similarly, we denote by
W_{t}(P)\in[n]
the smallest number such that for all
u_{2}\in\{0,1\}^{n}
, with
\|u_{2}\|_{1}>W_{t}(P)
,
P
has a zero of multiplicity
t
at
u_{2}
, and there exists a
v_{2}\in\{0,1\}^{n}
with
\|v_{2}\|_{1}=W_{t}(P)
such that
P(v_{2})\neq 0
.

We will use the following important generalization of Alon and Furedi’s result (Theorem
1
) by Sauermann and Wigderson.

Theorem 9

(Sauermann and Wigderson
[
SW24
]
)
.

Let
t\geq 2
,
n\geq 2t-3
and
P(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})\in\mathbb{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
be a polynomial having zeros of multiplicity at least
t
at all points in
\left\{0,1\right\}^{n}\setminus\{(0,\dots,0)\}
and
P(0,\dots,0)\neq 0
. Then
\text{deg}(P)\geq n+2t-3
and the bound is tight.

Theorem 10
.

Let
t\in\mathbb{N}
and
S
be a proper subset of
\left\{0,1\right\}^{n}
. Suppose
P\in\mathbb{R}[X_{1},\dots,X_{n}]
be a non-zero polynomial that vanishes on
S
with multiplicity at least
t
and there exists
\xi\in\left\{0,1\right\}^{n}\setminus S
with
P(\xi)\neq 0
.
If
\max\left\{w_{t}(P),n-W_{t}(P)\right\}\geq 2t-3
then

\text{deg}(P)\geq\max\left\{w_{t}(P),n-W_{t}(P)\right\}+2t-3.

Proof.

Without loss of generality we assume that
w\geq n-W
, otherwise, we work with the polynomial
\widetilde{P}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n}):=P(1-X_{1},\dots,1-X_{n})
in place of the polynomial
P
.
Let
u=(u_{1},\dots,u_{n})\in\left\{0,1\right\}^{n}\setminus S
such that
\|u\|_{1}=w
and
P(u)\neq 0
. Then, for all
v\in\left\{0,1\right\}^{n}
with
\|v\|_{1}<w
, we have
P(v)=0
with multiplicity at least
t
. Without loss of generality we may assume that
u_{i}=1
if and only if
i\in[w]
. Consider the polynomial
Q(X_{1},\dots,X_{w}):=P(1-X_{1},\dots,1-X_{w},0,\dots,0)
. Then
\text{deg}(Q)\leq\text{deg}(P)
and
Q(0,\dots,0)=P(u)\neq 0
.

Now we claim that,
Q(X_{1},\dots,X_{w})
vanishes everywhere on
\left\{0,1\right\}^{w}\setminus\{(0,\dots,0)\}
with multiplicity at least
t
. Take any
v\in\left\{0,1\right\}^{w}\setminus\{(0,\dots,0)\}
and let
\tilde{v}\in\left\{0,1\right\}^{n}
such that
\tilde{v}_{i}=1-v_{i}
, for all
i\in[w]
and
\tilde{v}_{i}=0
, for all
i>w
.
Then
\|\tilde{v}\|_{1}<w
and so
\tilde{v}
is a zero of
P
with multiplicity at least
t
. Hence
Q(v)=P(\tilde{v})=0
.
Next consider any differential operator
D=\frac{\partial^{d_{1}}}{\partial X_{1}^{d_{1}}}\dots\frac{\partial^{d_{w}}}{\partial X_{w}^{d_{w}}}
such that
d_{i}\geq 0
, for all
i\in[w]
and
\sum_{i=1}^{w}d_{i}<t
. We shall show that,
DQ(v)=0
.

First observe that, we can write
P(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})=P_{1}(X_{1},\dots,X_{w})+P_{2}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})
, where each monomial of
P_{2}
involves at least one variable from
X_{w+1},\dots,X_{n}
with degree at least
1
. So
P_{2}
vanishes at each
\tilde{X}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
\tilde{X}_{i}=0
, for all
i>w
.
Again
D
involves none of the variables from
X_{w+1},\dots,X_{n}
, each monomial of
DP_{2}
involves at least one variable from
X_{w+1},\dots,X_{n}
with degree at least
1
and so
DP_{2}
also vanishes at each
\tilde{X}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
\tilde{X}_{i}=0
, for all
i>w
. So we can write
DP_{1}(X_{1},\dots,X_{w})=DP_{1}(X_{1},\dots,X_{w})+DP_{2}(X_{1},\dots,X_{w},0,\dots,0)=DP(X_{1},\dots,X_{w},0,\dots,0)
, for all
(X_{1},\dots,X_{w})\in\mathbb{R}^{w}
.

Next observe that, we can write
Q(X_{1},\dots,X_{w})=P_{1}(1-X_{1},\dots,1-X_{w})
, as
P_{2}(1-X_{1},\dots,1-X_{w},0,\dots,0)=0
. So

DQ(X_{1},\dots,X_{w})=DP_{1}(1-X_{1},\dots,1-X_{w})=DP(1-X_{1},\dots,1-X_{w},0,\dots,0),

for all
(X_{1},\dots,X_{w})\in\mathbb{R}^{w}
. Hence
DQ(v)=DP(\tilde{v})=0
, as
P
vanishes at
\tilde{v}
with multiplicity
t
implies that
DP(\tilde{v})=0
.
Therefore, using Theorem
9
, we get
\text{deg}(Q)\geq w+2t-3
.
∎

Remark 11
.

, for all
i>w

\|v\|_{1}<w
. Then
\tilde{v}=(v_{1},\dots,v_{w})\in\left\{0,1\right\}^{w}\setminus\{(1,\dots,1)\}

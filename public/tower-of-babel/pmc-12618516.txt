Improving bank customer churn prediction with feature reduction using GA
Authors: Nisha T N, Dhanya Pramod
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractCustomer churn is one complex problem that all banking institutes struggle to deal with. The increased competition in the industry is forcing the banks to maintain customer loyalty by providing apt services to the demanding customers. Analysing the customer churn data will enable the banks to plan their services effectively and depend on various machine learning techniques to predict this churn. However, the high data dimension in these datasets adds adverse effects to this algorithm’s performance. This work proposes a way to improve the prediction accuracy of high-dimensional data with the help of feature selection using Genetic Algorithm (GA). This model identifies the machine learning algorithms which are sensitive towards the feature selection and which are not. The model is tested on a primary dataset and the results are analysed. This research also analyses those features which are the most important in predicting customer churn while applying the GA-optimized classification models. This assists the banking institutions to revise their marketing strategy so that they can attract more customers and also reduce customer attrition by providing the apt services to them.Keywords: Customer churn prediction, Machine learning classifiers: genetic algorithm, Feature selection, Feature weightage, Sensitivity towards generations.Subject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

Customer attrition is a complex issue that affects banks in many ways and directly affects their productivity, profitability, and long-term viability. Losing current clients can have a detrimental effect on the bank’s finances because it reduces interest income, fees, and other revenue streams. It is frequently more expensive to acquire new clients than to keep old ones. Excessive turnover rates might make it more difficult for a bank to grow its clientele and market share1. So, for banks to succeed in a cutthroat market, they must strategically address customer turnover and prioritise client retention.

To decrease customer attrition, financial institutions ought to concentrate on delivering superior customer support, competitive offerings, equitable and transparent pricing, efficient communication, and ongoing enhancement of their services. Maintaining a solid and devoted client base requires an understanding of and response to the causes of customer churn2. Various studies have explored the important factors that contribute to customer retention and related churn problems. It is imperative to have easily navigable digital platforms for digital services such as mobile and online banking; technological advancements that improve efficiency and security; and convenience through extended business hours, ATMs, and convenient branch locations3,4.

Churn prediction is determining which customers are most likely to discontinue their use of a product or service. By using sophisticated algorithms to evaluate, model, and forecast customer behaviour, machine learning improves the prediction of customer attrition. Businesses can employ proactive methods to retain consumers and raise overall customer satisfaction by seeing prospective churners early and understanding the elements that lead to churn. Various studies have used machine learning models to evaluate customer churn5. The literature has proposed a variety of methods such as, neural networks6, linear support vector machines7, decision trees8, logistic regression9, Random Forests10, Gradient Boosting11, Bagging and Boosting12, Bernoulli13 to be effectively utilised in customer churn prediction.

As the data availability is not an issue for modern classification algorithms, these data modelling tools need to be tuned to cope with the tremendous data they may have to handle. The biggest issue faced by all these classifiers is the curse of dimensionality. The curse of dimensionality refers to the paradigm of higher parameterised data and how it damages the result space14. Unwanted noise in data will impair decision-making and is the most discussed problem in machine learning classifiers.

There are two approaches to solving this problem: Feature Selection and Feature Reduction. Feature reduction or dimensionality reduction is a crucial pre-processing method for high-dimensional data, and well-accepted dimensionality reduction methods are Principal Component Analysis (PCA) and Linear Discriminant Analysis (LDA).

Feature selection is the process of selecting the optimum subset of features which improves the quality of decision-making and can be considered an NP-hard problem. Recently, evolutionary algorithms like Genetic Algorithm (GA), Particle swarm Optimisation (PSO), Ant Colony Optimisation (ACO), etc., have been successfully implemented to solve this with a balanced complexity and a better throughput. All these algorithms explore the search space extensively and can provide reasonable accuracy while working with multi-dimensional problems. Considering the features like convergence speed, accuracy and simplicity to implement, Genetic algorithms top the list compared to PSO and ACO.

Genetic algorithms offer several benefits. They are capable of effectively managing a lot of features and investigating a sizable feature combination search space. It can help locate globally optimal feature subsets and avoid local optima15. They offer an automated and methodical approach to feature selection that eliminates the need for domain-specific expertise. Genetic algorithms, however, can be computationally expensive for very large feature sets and may require adjustment of factors such as population size, mutation rate, and crossover approaches16.

Customer churn prediction is thus a major challenge for banking institutions and is critical for the sustainable growth of the business. To maintain the market in the competitive environment, identifying the right reason for a customer to close a bank account is crucial. Multiple studies are conducted, but require more specific interventions in identifying the key factors that impact the customer churn and the best classifiers to provide accurate results. So this research is motivated by the requirement of predicting whether a customer will close the bank account by analysing different attributes which characterise a loyal customer and also identifying the key attributes that make a customer take a decision to close the account.

Based on this, predicting whether or not a customer will close their retail bank account is the use case of this study. This also necessitates a suitable classification algorithm which provides the desired accuracy in identifying the disloyal customers and a method to address the high-dimensional datasets. Traditional classification algorithms are often inadequate to provide this, especially in a situation where the redundant and irrelevant features introduce noise in the training data. The feature selection allows the model to reduce the unnecessary noise in the dataset, which will eventually impair the decision-making accuracy.

To estimate customer churn, various data mining approaches are employed to pinpoint the most significant contributing cause. Further, the study explores GA for feature selection and analyses the performance improvement. This research reinforces the use of GA as a feature selection method and also demonstrates how the features contribute to the model performance in different program settings.

Literature review

Banking is a highly competitive sector; thus, many things can affect how a bank acquires customers17. A bank’s selection of goods and services greatly influences its ability to draw in new clients. A portfolio with a wide range of products and services, including digital banking choices, credit cards, loans, investments, and checking and savings accounts, can appeal to a wider range of customers18. For businesses to attract and keep customers, a great and smooth customer experience is essential. A better experience is made possible by the helpful and experienced staff, quick service, user-friendly digital platforms, and first-rate customer support19.

Offerings of competitive and varied products, like credit cards, loans, savings accounts, and investment opportunities, can draw in and keep clients2. Offering a range of banking services, such as soft loans, loyalty programs, freebies, and zero balance, will also assist in retaining clients. Tailored financial solutions based on personal requirements and improved service quality can increase client loyalty20. The whole client experience can be improved by providing prompt and friendly customer service. Manager engagement is inevitable when it comes to higher satisfaction, which is a result of effective problem resolution and a personalised approach to meeting client needs21.

The studies have also emphasised that an essential component of the banking sector is trust. Consumers must have faith that the security of their funds and personal data is guaranteed. Trust can be increased by making investments in strong cybersecurity defences and open communication about security procedures22. By encouraging loyalty, developing a distinct identity in the eyes of consumers, and establishing trust, a powerful and positive brand can improve client retention. To sustain and improve client relationships over time, banks must constantly reaffirm their brand values and fulfil their brand commitments23.

To increase forecast accuracy and assist banks in saving money, Deng’s study examined the quarterly user data of banks and established a user churn prediction model using ensemble learning algorithms like CatBoost, LightGBM, and Random Forest. The model can be used to forecast the likelihood that a user will leave in the future, schedule adequate time for user retention initiatives, and offer a wealth of useful data to assist marketing staff in creating a workable user retention plan with a broad range of industry applications24. Predictive modelling is done with the aid of machine learning techniques. Previous studies have explored various machine learning techniques including ensembles for churn prediction25.

Decision Tree, K Nearest Neighbour Classifier (KNN), Ridge Classifier Cross Validation (RCCV), Logistic Regression, Support Vector Classifier (SVC), and Linear Support Vector Classifier (Linear SVC) are just a few of the effective machine learning approaches used by the authors in this work. A decision tree is a straightforward classifier that performs classification based on a decision criterion. KNN is a nonparametric algorithm that assigns a data point to one of its classes based on its neighbours. To get rid of the effects of correlated features, one might utilise the regression method version known as ridge regression26.

The probabilistic techniques known as the Bernoulli Naive Bayes classifier and the Gaussian Naive Bayes classifier, respectively, use the Bernoulli distribution and the Gaussian distribution. A supervised method known as a support vector machine (SVM) is applied in both classification and regression scenarios. SVM classifies the data points by choosing the best boundary, and it does it in a nonparametric way. The Linear SVC finds the hyperplane that fits the data points the best for classification. These techniques train the classifier using the loss function27.

A method called ensemble learning involves fitting two or more models to the same data and combining the predictions from each model. The goal of ensemble learning is to outperform each model using the group of models as a whole. This study has used popular ensemble approaches. Adaboost is an ensemble model based on boosting that modulates different classifiers using the same dataset. Additionally, the weights of the incorrectly categorised data points are changed so that the next iteration can concentrate more on correctly classifying them. The weak decision tree learners are joined to construct additive trees progressively in the gradient-boosting ensemble, which supports both classification and regression. In the event of categorization issues, additional trees are additionally based on numerous unpruned decision trees, and predictions are determined by a majority vote28,29.

High dimensionality causes overfitting of the classification model and decreases the classification accuracy. Feature selection employs various filter methods, wrapper methods and embedded methods for feature selection to improve the prediction accuracy of machine learning algorithms30. This feature selection process also may help to reduce the noise in the model data thereby improving the model performance.

Wrapper-based feature selection searches a subset of features and evaluates its fitness to predict the model accurately. Wrapper method-based feature selection is an NP-hard problem. This nature of the wrapper method makes GA-based feature selection more promising than other feature selection methods. Evolutionary methods like Genetic algorithm (GA) and particle swarm optimization (PSO) are successfully implemented for feature selection to improve the accuracy of machine learning models. GA optimizes the features by generating a random set of features and optimizing it by looping through the steps for generations. GA’s survival of the fittest feature will aid in omitting those features which weekly contribute to the selection and selecting the strong ones.

GA-based feature selection is successfully implemented by the researchers on different benchmarked datasets. Genetic Algorithm-based feature selection was found to be giving performance increments irrespective of the area and the dataset characteristics. Many classification techniques such as KNN, Support Vector Machines (SVM), Decision Tree Random Forest, Naïve Bayes, Logistic regression etc. found to provide better performance when coupled with GA-based feature selection.

Comparison of GA-based selection with PCA or LDA-based feature reduction showcased promising improvement in the results31. One alternative combining two or many from PCA, LDA and GA has also been administered by different researchers. It is evident through different cases that GA could outperform PCA and LDA in providing classification accuracy if applied32. Selecting the right fitness function and parameter values is crucial when using genetic algorithms for feature selection to guarantee that the algorithm converges to a useful subset of features33.

Experimental setup

This section introduces the proposed solution for the feature selection problem using the evolutionary algorithm. The model uses GA for feature selection and aims to increase the classification accuracy of the machine learning models.

Figure 1 represents a conceptual illustration of the GA improvised classification techniques. The machine learning algorithms and genetic algorithm pre-setting are explained in the following sections.

Model architecture- GA based feature selection and classification workflow.

The model takes the pre-processed high-dimensional data set as input. The dataset undergoes a feature selection through GA and is fed to the classification models. The array of algorithms classifies the dataset into positive and negative data and predicts the customer churn.

Dataset

This study used a customer churn dataset with data from 620 bank customers. The respondents included customers from rural and urban areas, working in different type of industries like IT, Insurance sector, Manufacturing, Retails, Logistics and Hospitality. The data was available in the institutional repository. The features used in the study are drawn from various aspects such as ease of using banking services, Customer support, Diversity of banking products and options, Customer loyalty programs, rewards and special attention, personalisation, Brand value, Trust and security. These features are in the dataset as the independent variables. The target variable is a binary variable represents whether a customer has discontinued their account or continued their relationship, represented as two categories as ‘1’ (Customer Churn) and ‘0’ (Customer Retain).

The dataset is pre-processed by applying data cleaning, data transformation/normalization and input-output coding. Data cleaning involved identifying and handling missing values by mean imputation method, removing duplicates, and correcting the inconsistencies. Cleaned data is then normalised using Python scikit-learn libraries. The label encoding technique is applied to convert the categorical data. The scope of the study was to evaluate the major reasons for your bank customers to close an existing bank account, specifically from the IT industry. So, to align with the research objective, the final dataset is selected representing bank customers below 29 years who work in the IT industry, and have recently closed a bank account. The final data set includes 250 rows and 21 columns, and ensures that the irrelevant and redundant data will not impair the classification model performance.

GA based features selection module

A genetic algorithm is an evolutionary algorithm which imitates nature’s optimization scenario. It follows the properties of natural selection, reproduction and mutation and emphasizes on survival of the fittest. GA-based feature selection passes through the stages of population initialization, selection based on fitness, and crossover for reproduction and mutation.

Chromosome definition and initial population

Every member of the population follows a chromosome representation, one possible subset of features chosen from 20 available features. Represented as a one-dimensional array as shown in Fig. 2, contains values 0’s and 1’s as genes, indicating the presence or absence of a feature in the subset. The initial population size Np for this work is set as 64. However, later experiments can be conducted by varying the population size and verifying the sensitivity of the model towards the population size.

Chromosome representation for GA - feature presence and absence in the gene.

Selection on the basis of fitness and crossover

The selection process in GA follows the survival of the fittest rule. The fittest chromosome is that subset of the features which provides more classification accuracy. These subsets participate in crossover and generate new sets of chromosomes with improved accuracy scores.

Algorithm parameters and termination criteria

In this work, the terminating criteria are preset and evaluated by executing the model for a fixed number of iterations. This method provides flexibility in the implementation and performance can be managed efficiently.

Table 5 summarises the parameter settings for Genetic algorithms. Chromosome size is the same as the number of features in the dataset, as it maps the presence or absence of a feature in a chromosome under consideration (Refer to Fig. 2). Population size 64 is selected to balance between search space exploration and computational cost67. The model is evaluated for generations 20, 25, 50 and 100 in this setup, and results can be read from the later sessions. Experiments with multiple generations will evaluate the convergence behaviour of the model with the dataset under consideration. Mutation probability and selection and crossover methods are employed based on their effectiveness and ease of implementation. Fitness function is the accuracy of the model as stated in Sect. 3.2.2. However, the implementation can be tested with different combinations of these parameters to reinforce the model’s efficiency.

Discussion

Accuracy improvement for different algorithms

As displayed in 3.3.1, the Extra Tree classifier is showing the most accurate result (92%) and Bernoulli NB and Gaussian NB are showing poor accuracy (60%) when applied to the dataset without any feature reduction. Applying feature selection shows a significant improvement in accuracy in many algorithms. Section 3.3.2 shows the result of the experiment, as the improvement in accuracy. It can be observed that algorithms such as Bagging Classifier, AdaBoost Classifier, Logistic Regression, Linear SVC, Ridge Classifier CV, Bernoulli NB and Gaussian NB are showing an increasing trend in accuracy. An average improvement of 4% to 5% is visible in these algorithms. Algorithms like Extra Trees Classifier, Random Forest Classifier, and Gradient Boosting Classifier show better accuracy when all features are present in the model. Feature selection is reducing the accuracy of these algorithms. Decision Tree Classifier and SVC are not sensitive to feature selection. The accuracy remains comparatively static for these algorithms even after GA is applied.

The results reinforce the fact that ensembles are showing better performance and are not sensitive towards the GA feature selection. Ensemble learning, by its feature, performs the optimisation on features, and may be the reason for a comparatively better performance and non-sensitivity towards GA-based feature selection. Non-ensembles and probability-based classification are showing poor performance without feature selection and are exhibiting a good improvement after GA.

The experiments also entail the model’s sensitivity towards the number of generations of the Genetic algorithm. As clearly shown in Fig. 3, the model shows the same accuracy patterns with multiple generations. Once converges to the global optima, GA maintains its position and is the reason for this behaviour in this model.

Feature weightage discussion

The experiments conducted also show the relationship between the algorithms and the feature selection behaviours. Most of the algorithms reached an accuracy with 50% of the features across generations. As expressed in Fig. 5, Features like ease of banking, ease of address change, security, personal loans with zero interest and personal interactions in the bank were found to be the most weighted features, and it implies the relative importance of the feature in deciding whether a customer stays with the bank or not.

This reinforces the fact that these are the essential services a user expects from the bank, and almost all classification algorithms selected this feature through GA. It was interesting to see that other allied services, like forex services, have not been taken by many algorithms, which essentially indicates the customer’s negligence regarding the necessity of these services. Customers also value doorstep banking and mobile banking services, and this is evident in the results.

Final implications

This study reinforces the importance of proper feature selection in classification algorithms and can aid financial institutions in clearly identifying the reasons for customer churn. The management implications of this research are two-fold. The algorithm performance analysis will help the banks to employ the best classification model to predict who will be a loyal customer and who is likely to turn unfaithful. Identifying it well in advance will enable banks to rectify the mistakes and put efforts to retain the customers. Planning the customer retention strategies will be more successful if you can identify the reasons for them to leave. The feature weightage is a direct indication of the acceptability of the services provided by the bank. Financial institutions can reframe their customer service based on this and gain customer satisfaction. Acceptance of a particular service by the customers will give confidence to the banks and will help them in implementing the customer-friendly services.

The theoretical contribution of this study to the retail banking industry is the application of an advanced classification model enabled by the evolutionary algorithm. This improves the accuracy of the predictions and points to the best model to choose from. The practical contribution of this model is the list of features this model identifies as the major decision pointers for a customer to continue with a bank. These indications can be utilised effectively to reduce customer churn in the banking sector.

Conclusion and future research directions

With the advent of new technology, the banking sector also went through an exponential change. The competition in retail banking has increased, and financial institutions are struggling to cope with the customer churn. This research addresses customer churn patterns and recommends a preventive solution for the banks to implement. This also enables the banks to market their services and mould the banking experience as per the expectations of the new customers.

The data set is evaluated using multiple machine learning algorithms to evaluate the classification accuracy. The practitioners can leverage the results by predicting customer churn well in advance, by keeping track of customer correspondence and bank service acceptance. Genetic Algorithm-based feature selection is engaged to identify the features that mainly contribute to the customer churn. GA is preferred because of its simplicity and effectiveness. The experiments recognised the important features contributing to the customer churn, and can be employed by financial institutions to identify the loopholes in the system and rectify them on time. This will enable the banks to retain their customers and be sustainable in the competitive market. The model can be extended by applying other evolutionary algorithms like PSO, ACO and ABC and comparing the results.

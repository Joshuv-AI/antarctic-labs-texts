Identification and Validation of Mannose Metabolism-Related Biomarkers in COPD Through Integrated Bioinformatics and Machine Learning Analysis: A Pilot Study
Authors: Xiaodan Li, Jin Wang, Zhong Hu, Shanting Chen, Chang Liu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundChronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) represents a progressive respiratory disorder marked by sustained airflow restriction and ongoing inflammatory processes. Recently, mannose metabolism has emerged as a significant factor in chronic disease development. This investigation explored how mannose metabolism-related genes (MMRGs) contribute to COPD pathogenesis and evaluated their utility as candidate diagnostic and therapeutic targets.MethodsWe obtained blood sample gene expression data from COPD patients and healthy controls via the GEO database. Differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were identified and intersected with MMRGs to obtain candidate genes. Three machine learning algorithms combined with expression validation across independent datasets were applied to identify biomarkers. A nomogram prediction model was constructed and its diagnostic performance was assessed using receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis. Subsequently, gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA), immune infiltration analysis, drug prediction, and molecular docking were performed.ResultsNineteen candidate genes were identified from 1685 DEGs and subsequently screened for two biomarkers: MAN1C1 and MAN2B2. A nomogram model constructed on the basis of the two showed moderate discriminatory efficacy (area under the curve (AUC) = 0.701). In addition, GSEA analysis showed that both were co-enriched in pathways such as TNF’s target up-regulated gene sets. The immune infiltration results revealed significant differences (p < 0.05) between COPD and controls in a total of 12 categories of immune cells, such as activated B cells. Finally, drug prediction revealed 12 and 3 potential drugs for MAN1C1 and MAN2B2, respectively, with trichostatin A showing a potential binding conformation.ConclusionThis study revealed the potential roles of MMRGs in COPD and identified novel biomarkers. These findings provided new insights and research foundations for the early diagnosis, personalized treatment, and drug development of COPD.Keywords: chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, mannose metabolism, machine learning, functional enrichment, molecular docking

Introduction

Chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) is a progressive respiratory disorder characterized by persistent airflow limitation and chronic airway inflammation.1 Beyond compromising patient quality of life, COPD contributes substantially to the worldwide disease burden and ranks among the principal mortality causes.2 Data from the Global Burden of Disease (GBD) 2021 indicated that chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) caused approximately 3.5 million deaths worldwide.3,4 COPD imposes not only a severe health burden but also substantial economic losses. One study estimated that the total global economic losses attributable to COPD would reach 5.5 trillion US dollars between 2020 and 2050.5 Although progress has been achieved in early detection and treatment modalities, COPD’s pathophysiological mechanisms remain intricate.6 Therefore, identifying potential biomarkers and elucidating COPD’s disease mechanisms are crucial for timely diagnosis, therapeutic target development, and enhanced patient outcomes.

Mannose metabolism, which encompasses the intracellular processing of mannose, constitutes a vital element of glucose metabolic pathways and serves essential functions in numerous biological activities, including T cell functional regulation and stemness preservation.7 Recent evidence has demonstrated that mannose metabolic processes are intimately linked to the pathogenesis of multiple conditions, encompassing chronic inflammatory states and neoplastic disorders.8,9 A study by Dicker et al found that mannose-binding lectin deficiency is associated with reduced exacerbation frequency and lower inflammation in COPD patients, suggesting that mannose metabolism-related pathways may influence the acute exacerbation risk and inflammatory response in COPD.10 Pathological features of COPD include chronic inflammation and an immune imbalance,11,12 and the role of mannose metabolism in the immune system may be closely related to the progression of COPD. For example, studies have shown that mannose metabolism may regulate the pro-inflammatory or anti-inflammatory functions of macrophages by mediating their metabolic reprogramming.13 Therefore, investigating the potential roles of MMRGs in COPD may help to reveal novel mechanisms and provide new ideas for the clinical diagnosis and treatment of COPD.

Peripheral blood serves as a critical carrier of systemic immune and inflammatory responses. Although COPD is primarily a pulmonary disorder, its pathological changes can trigger widespread systemic reactions, and characteristic molecular alterations in the lungs can be reflected in peripheral blood. In addition, peripheral blood samples are easy to collect and widely available in public transcriptomic databases, making them suitable for large-scale exploratory analysis. Therefore, we adopted peripheral blood transcriptomic datasets to explore potential molecular mechanisms and candidate biomarkers of COPD. This investigation sought to identify MMRGs linked to COPD and assess their biomarker potential. Utilizing public databases, we determined COPD-associated candidate biomarkers through differential expression profiling, three machine learning methodologies, and expression validation approaches. Subsequently, functional enrichment assessment, immune infiltration examination, molecular regulatory network construction, along with drug prediction and molecular docking studies were conducted to elucidate the biological roles, immune modulation mechanisms, and potential therapeutic compounds targeting these biomarkers. This study identified potential therapeutic targets for future research and provided preliminary insights for subsequent clinical investigations.

Data and Methods

Data Acquisition

Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO, https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/) is a public functional genomics data repository maintained by the National Center for Biotechnology Information (NCBI) that archives and freely distributes high-throughput gene expression data, including microarray and next-generation sequencing data, in compliance with MIAME standards. In the present investigation, dataset GSE42057 (platform: GPL570) was retrieved from the GEO to serve as the training cohort, comprising peripheral blood specimens from 94 COPD subjects and 42 healthy individuals (Supplementary Table 1). Additionally, dataset GSE94916 (platform: GPL20844) was acquired from the GEO repository as the validation cohort, encompassing blood specimens from 6 COPD subjects and 6 healthy controls (Supplementary Table 2). MMRGs were extracted from the Molecular Signatures Database (MSigDB, http://www.gsea-msigdb.org/gsea/msigdb) through keyword searching using “Mannose Metabolism”. Associated biological pathways comprised: biocarta feeder pathway, GDP mannose biosynthetic process, GDP mannose metabolic process, GPI anchor metabolic process, mannosylation, protein O linked mannosylation, smooth endoplasmic reticulum, mannosidase activity, fructose and mannose metabolism, mannose type O glycan biosynthesis FKTN to large, and mannose type O glycan biosynthesis POMT to POMK, yielding a total of 160 MMRGs (Supplementary Box 1).

Differential Analysis and Candidate Gene Screening

To evaluate transcriptomic differences between COPD specimens and healthy control specimens within the GSE42057 training cohort, the R package “limma” (v 1.34.0)14 was employed to identify DEGs (COPD versus Control) with thresholds of p < 0.05 and |log2FC| > 0 for exploratory candidate identification. For visualization of differential gene expression, volcano plots displaying the top 10 up-regulated and down-regulated DEGs were generated using the R package “ggplot2” (v 3.4.4).15 Additionally, heatmaps illustrating expression patterns of these 20 DEGs across different specimens were created using the R package “pheatmap” (v 1.0.12).16 To determine MMRGs exhibiting differential expression in COPD, Venn diagram analysis was conducted between the identified DEGs and 160 MMRGs using the R package “VennDiagram” (v 1.7.3).17 Overlapping genes were designated as candidate genes.

Functional Enrichment Analysis and Construction of Protein-Protein Interaction (PPI) Network

To investigate potential biological roles and signaling pathways associated with these candidate genes, GO and KEGG enrichment analyses were conducted using the R package “clusterProfiler” (v 4.7.1.003).18 Statistical significance for all enrichment analyses was established at p.adj < 0.05 to control false positives caused by multiple testing, consistent with the standard requirements of mainstream journals. KEGG enrichment outcomes were ranked by ascending p.adj values and displayed via bubble plots using the R package “ggplot2” (v3.4.4).

Furthermore, PPI networks were established through the STRING database (https://string-db.org). Candidate genes were input into STRING with an interaction confidence threshold set at > 0.4. The PPI network was rendered using the R package “ggraph” (v2.2.1) (https://ggraph.data-imaginist.com/) to illustrate protein-protein interactions.

Screening Candidate Biomarkers Based on Machine Learning Analysis

To identify COPD-associated candidate biomarkers, three distinct machine learning methodologies were implemented: LASSO regression, RF, and SVM-RFE. Initially, LASSO regression was executed using the R package “glmnet” (v 4.1.8),19 which compresses regression coefficients through penalty function construction, minimizing genes strongly correlated with disease while reducing weakly correlated genes to zero. Model optimization was achieved through 5-fold cross-validation. Subsequently, RF models were established using the R package “randomForest” (v 4.7.1.2)20 for COPD and control specimens from the GSE42057 training cohort. The optimal tree number (ntree) was determined via cross-validation based on OOB error. Model iteration continued until maximum accuracy (minimum OOB error) was attained, followed by selection of the top 15 genes based on importance scores. Next, SVM-RFE, a supervised learning technique, was applied using the R package “caret” (v 7.0.1).21 This analysis utilized the GSE42057 training cohort to construct an SVM model based on candidate genes and identify the gene subset providing optimal accuracy through recursive feature elimination. Finally, genes selected by the three methodologies (LASSO, RF, and SVM-RFE) underwent intersection analysis using the R package “ggvenn” (v 0.1.9),22 with overlapping results designated as candidate biomarkers.

Biomarker Screening and Subcellular Localisation Analysis

Differential expression profiling of candidate biomarkers was executed across all specimens in both the GSE42057 training cohort and GSE94916 validation cohort. The Wilcoxon rank sum test was applied to compare expression levels of candidate biomarkers between COPD specimens and control specimens within each dataset. The Benjamini-Hochberg false discovery rate (FDR) correction (p < 0.05) was applied due to the limited number of tested items, which avoids over-correction while controlling FDR. Analytical outcomes were visualized through box plots generated using the R package “ggplot2” (v 3.4.4). Genes demonstrating significant expression variations with consistent directional trends across both datasets were designated as biomarkers. Additionally, subcellular localization data for the biomarkers were extracted from the GeneCards database (https://www.genecards.org/).

Construction and Evaluation of Nomogram

To evaluate the comprehensive predictive capacity of biomarkers in COPD, nomogram was developed using the R package “rms” (v 6.8.1)23 based on COPD and control specimens from the GSE42057 training cohort. To verify the predictive accuracy of nomogram, calibration curve was generated using the identical R package. Calibration curve assessed concordance between predicted probabilities and actual observations. A p-value > 0.05 indicates satisfactory model fit with no significant discrepancy between predicted and observed values. Furthermore, ROC curve analysis was conducted to quantify the discriminative capacity of biomarkers. ROC curves were plotted using the R package “pROC” (v 1.18.5),24 and AUC values were computed.

GSEA and GSVA

To examine biological pathway alterations during COPD progression, correlations between biomarkers and additional genes within the GSE42057 training cohort were calculated using the Spearman function from the R package “psych” (v 2.1.6),25 with genes ranked by correlation strength. The reference gene collection was obtained from the “c2.all.v2023.2.Hs.symbols.gmt” dataset within the MSigDB database. Subsequently, GSEA was executed using the R package “clusterProfiler” (v4.7.1.003), with selection criteria of |NES| > 1 and p < 0.05, following official GSEA guidelines that consider both enrichment magnitude and statistical significance. To further elucidate biological pathway modifications during COPD development, specimens from the GSE42057 training cohort were analyzed using the signaling pathway dataset “c2.all.v2025.1.Hs.symbols.gmt” from the MSigDB database as the reference gene collection. The ssGSEA function within the R package “GSVA” (v 1.46.0)26 was utilized to compute GSVA scores, followed by differential GSVA score analysis using the R package “limma” (v 1.34.0). Selection criteria of |t| > 2 and p < 0.05 were applied to identify pathways exhibiting enrichment differences.

Immune Infiltration Analysis

To assess the immune landscape in COPD, ssGSEA was performed on GSE42057 using the “GSVA” R package (v 1.46.0) with a 28-cell-type gene signature to compute enrichment scores for each immune population across COPD and control samples.27 Data were visualized via heatmaps using “pheatmap” (v 1.0.12). Differentially abundant immune cells between groups were identified through Wilcoxon rank sum tests (wilcox.test function, “stats” v 4.3.1),28 applying a significance threshold of p < 0.05 (Benjamini-Hochberg FDR correction). Correlation analysis (|cor| > 0.3, p < 0.05) among these cell types was conducted using the cor function (“stats” v4.3.1), with results displayed in heatmaps generated by “ggcorrplot” (v 0.1.4).29 Spearman correlation analysis (|cor| > 0.3, p < 0.05) between differentially infiltrating immune cells and biomarkers was performed using corr.test from the “psych” package (v 2.1.6). Spearman correlation analysis used raw p < 0.05, because the number of variable pairs was less than 20, resulting in negligible multiple-testing burden.

Molecular Regulatory Network Analysis

To identify genes functionally associated with biomarkers and clarify their biological roles, the GeneMANIA database (https://genemania.org/) was utilized. This database integrates protein-protein interactions, protein-DNA interactions, gene interactions, pathways, co-expression networks, protein structural domains, and phenotypic data to predict functionally similar genes. Biomarkers were submitted to GeneMANIA, and the top 20 most significantly associated genes were identified based on interaction confidence scores. These genes underwent functional enrichment analysis to screen for significantly enriched pathways (p < 0.05) and related biological functions (FDR < 0.05).

Furthermore, to explore post-transcriptional regulatory mechanisms of biomarkers, an lncRNA-miRNA-mRNA network was established. Initially, miRNAs targeting biomarkers were predicted through the miRDB database (https://mirdb.org/), and miRNA-mRNA interaction data were retrieved from the ENCORI database (https://rnasysu.com/encori/). Only miRNAs predicted in both miRDB and ENCORI were retained as common miRNA regulators. Next, upstream lncRNAs interacting with the common miRNAs were predicted using ENCORI and filtered for high-confidence interactions (clipExpNum > 20). Finally, common miRNA-mRNA interaction pairs and high-confidence lncRNA-miRNA interaction pairs were integrated and visualized using Cytoscape software (v 3.10.2)30 to construct and illustrate the lncRNA-miRNA-mRNA regulatory network.

Drug Prediction and Molecular Docking

To identify therapeutic candidates for COPD, biomarker-drug interactions were analyzed via DSigDB (https://dsigdb.tanlab.org/DSigDBv1.0/), with mRNA-drug networks visualized in Cytoscape (v 3.10.2). Compounds exhibiting high interaction scores and available 3D structures were prioritized for docking studies. Structural data were sourced from PubChem (https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/) for compounds and UniProt (https://www.uniprot.org/) for target proteins. Molecular docking employed CB-Dock2 (http://clab.labshare.cn/cb-dock2/), utilizing curvature-based pocket detection and Autodock Vina (v 1.1.2)31 for cavity evaluation and ligand placement. Conformations were ranked by binding affinity to identify optimal poses, with protein-ligand complexes rendered using PyMOL (v 3.1).32

Statistical Analyses

All bioinformatic analyses in this investigation were performed using R software (v 4.2.2). Statistical evaluation of inter-group differences was conducted using the Wilcoxon rank sum test. The statistical significance threshold for all analyses was established at p < 0.05.

Results

A Total of 19 Candidate Genes Were Obtained

A total of 1685 DEGs were identified between COPD and control groups within the GSE42057 training cohort, comprising 1208 up-regulated genes and 477 down-regulated genes in COPD specimens (Figure 1A and B). Intersection analysis of 1685 DEGs with 160 MMRGs yielded 19 overlapping genes, designated as candidate genes (Figure 1C). To investigate the potential functions of these 19 candidate genes, GO enrichment analysis was conducted, revealing 72 significantly enriched pathways (p.adj < 0.05), including 35 BPs, 29 CCs, and 8 MFs (Figure 1D–F and Supplementary Table 3). Within the BP dimension, candidate genes were predominantly enriched in glycolipid biosynthetic process (GO:0009247), GPI anchor biosynthetic process (GO:0006506), GPI anchor metabolic process (GO:0006505), and related processes. In the CC dimension, enriched pathways encompassed smooth endoplasmic reticulum (GO:0005790), lysosomal lumen (GO:0043202), and endoplasmic reticulum protein-containing complex (GO:0140534). In the MF dimension, significant pathways included α-mannosidase activity (GO:0015923), carbohydrate kinase activity (GO:0019200), and carbohydrate phosphatase activity (GO:0019203). Subsequent KEGG pathway enrichment analysis identified five significant pathways (p.adj < 0.05): GPI-anchor biosynthesis (hsa00563), fructose and mannose metabolism (hsa00051), other glycan degradation (hsa00511), AMPK signaling pathway (hsa04152), and protein processing in endoplasmic reticulum (hsa04141) (Figure 1G and Supplementary Table 4). Finally, PPI network construction revealed intricate interactions among proteins encoded by candidate genes, such as PGAP2 and PIGO (Figure 1H). These findings suggest that candidate genes may fulfill important roles in COPD pathogenesis and provide potential biomarkers and therapeutic targets for subsequent investigations.

MAN1C1 and MAN2B2 Identified as Biomarkers

To screen candidate biomarkers for COPD, three machine learning algorithms (LASSO regression analysis, RF model and SVM-RFE) were used to screen 19 candidate genes. First, LASSO regression analysis screened 10 genes: DPY19L1, MAN1C1, MAN2B2, MANBA, MTMR1, NUS1, PFKFB3, PIGK, PIGO, and ST3GAL1 at the optimal penalty coefficient lambdamin= 0.03012688 (Figure 2A). Subsequently, the RF classification model was constructed based on the GSE42057 training set, and the results showed that the OOB error of the model was the smallest when the ntree value was 64, so this parameter was chosen to construct the final model (Figure 2Bi). Based on the importance scores of genes in the RF model, the top 15 genes were filtered out, which were MAN1C1, PIGK, SVIP, MTMR1, PFKFB4, PYGL, NUS1, PIGC, PGAP2, PFKFB3, MAN2B2, ST3GAL1, DPY19L1, PFKP, and RPL10, respectively Figure 2Bii). In addition, an SVM-RFE model was constructed based on the GSE42057 training set, which screened eight genes at its highest accuracy: DPY19L1, MAN1C1, MAN2B2, MANBA, MTMR1, PIGK, PYGL and SVIP (Figure 2C). Finally, the genes obtained from the screening of the three algorithms were taken to intersect and five overlapping genes were obtained: MAN1C1, MTMR1, DPY19L1, PIGK and MAN2B2 (Figure 2D), which were defined as candidate biomarkers. To assess the diagnostic value of these candidate biomarkers, we analyzed their expression patterns in both the GSE42057 training cohort and GSE94916 validation cohort, comparing COPD specimens with healthy controls. Among the five candidates examined, MAN1C1 and MAN2B2 demonstrated statistically significant differential expression (p < 0.05) across both datasets with concordant directional changes (Figure 2E). Both biomarkers exhibited marked down-regulation in COPD specimens. Consequently, MAN1C1 and MAN2B2 were designated as COPD-associated biomarkers. Subcellular distribution analysis indicated that MAN1C1 primarily localizes to the Golgi apparatus, extracellular matrix, and endoplasmic reticulum, while MAN2B2 resides predominantly in lysosomal compartments and extracellular regions (Figure 2F and G).

Nomogram Model Constructed Based on MAN1C1 and MAN2B2 Showed Moderate Diagnostic Efficacy in COPD Risk Prediction

A nomogram model for COPD prediction was constructed based on MAN1C1 and MAN2B2 (Figure 3A). In this model, each gene was assigned a corresponding score based on its expression level, and the sum of the scores of the two genes was the total score (Total Points). Taking the Total score of 85.8 as an example, its corresponding predicted probability of disease was 72.4%, indicating that the model has a good potential for application in assessing the risk of disease in individuals. To assess the accuracy of the model, a calibration curve was plotted, which showed that the predicted probability was highly consistent with the actual probability of occurrence, and the result of the HL goodness-of-fit test was p = 0.36, suggesting that there was no statistically significant difference, indicating that the model was well calibrated (Figure 3B). The ROC curve further validated the discriminative efficacy of the model, with an AUC value of 0.701, suggesting that the nomogram had moderate accuracy in differentiating COPD from control samples (Figure 3C). In conclusion, the nomogram model has moderate predictive performance and application value.

GSEA and GSVA Analyses Reveal the Potential Roles of MAN1C1 and MAN2B2 in COPD Immune Responses and Metabolic Abnormalities

GSEA analysis revealed that MAN1C1 and MAN2B2 were enriched in 281 and 114 pathways, respectively, of which 38 pathways co-enriched by the two included the myxomatosis fungoides CD4+T-cell characteristic up-regulated gene set (Hahtola mycosis fungoides CD4 up) and the TNF target up-regulated gene set (TNF target up), among other important pathways (p < 0.05) (Figure 4A and B, Supplementary Tables 5 and 6). Specifically, MAN1C1 was enriched in the down-regulated gene set of angioimmunoblastic T-cell lymphoma (Piccaluga angioimmunoblastic lymphoma dn), the up-regulated gene set of NPM1-mutated acute myeloid leukaemia (Verhaak AML with NPM1 mutated up), and the circulating endothelial-like cells in cancer (Smirnov circulating endotheliocytes in cancer up); whereas MAN2B2 is enriched in the characteristic up-regulated gene set of mycosis fungoides CD4+T cells (Hahtola mycosis fungoides CD4 up), the downstream target genes of the tumour necrosis factor signalling pathway downstream target genes (Phong TNF targets up) and platelet specific gene expression signature (Raghavachari platelet specific genes) pathways. These exploratory GSEA results preliminarily indicate that MAN1C1 and MAN2B2 may be potentially correlated with immune response, inflammatory regulation, and tumour microenvironment-related pathways, providing preliminary bioinformatic clues for understanding the aberrant molecular changes underlying COPD.

GSVA analysis further revealed differential pathway enrichment between COPD and control samples (p < 0.05). The pathways concerning short-chain fatty acid and skeletal muscle substrate metabolism, as well as PRKAR1A regulatory target downregulation, were significantly upregulated in COPD samples. In contrast, pathways related to defective CHSY1 and CHST3 function were downregulated in COPD tissues (Figure 4C and Supplementary Table 7). These GSVA findings suggest that multiple metabolic and functional pathways may be dysregulated in COPD. Collectively, these computational enrichment analyses provide exploratory insights into potential metabolic and pathway alterations associated with COPD, though these mechanistic associations require further experimental validation.

Immune Cell Infiltration Analysis Reveals the Relationship Between MAN1C1 and the COPD Immune Microenvironment

Based on the GSE42057 training cohort, immune cell composition analysis encompassing 28 cell types was conducted in COPD specimens and healthy control specimens. The findings revealed that infiltration levels of 12 immune cell populations exhibited significant differences (p < 0.05) between the two groups, comprising activated B cells, activated CD4 T cells, activated CD8 T cells, Activated DC, central memory CD4 T cells, central memory CD8 T cells, effector memory CD8 T cells, gamma delta T cells, immature DC, macrophages, neutrophils, and Treg (Figure 4D and E). Among these, activated B cells, activated CD4 T cells, activated CD8 T cells, and central memory CD4 T cells demonstrated significant down-regulation in the COPD group, whereas activated DCs, central memory CD8 T cells, γδ T cells, immature DCs, macrophages, neutrophils, and Tregs showed significant up-regulation. Correlation profiling of these 12 differentially abundant immune cells identified the strongest positive association between macrophages and neutrophils (cor = 0.84, p < 0.001), which also exhibited significant strong positive correlations with activated DCs (cor = 0.83, p < 0.001) and γδ T cells (cor = 0.82, p < 0.001). Activated CD8 T cells and macrophages displayed the strongest negative correlation (cor = −0.66, p < 0.001), with significant strong negative correlations also observed with neutrophils (cor = −0.59, p < 0.001) and central memory CD8 T cells (cor = −0.55, p < 0.001) (Figure 4F). Subsequent analysis of correlations between biomarkers and differentially abundant immune cells demonstrated that MAN1C1 exhibited positive correlations with activated B cells (cor = 0.34, p < 0.001), activated CD4 T cells (cor = 0.57, p < 0.001), central memory CD4 T cells (cor = 0.37, p < 0.001), and effector memory CD8 T cells (cor = 0.30, p < 0.001) (Figure 4G and Supplementary Table 8). These findings indicate that MAN1C1 may fulfill a crucial role in the immune microenvironment of COPD.

Functional Interactions and Post-Transcriptional Regulatory Mechanisms of MAN1C1 and MAN2B2 Reveal Their Potential Roles in COPD

To clarify the biological roles of MAN1C1 and MAN2B2, we generated a functional interaction network. Network analysis demonstrated that physical interactions constituted the predominant interaction type (77.64%), with additional contributions from co-expression patterns (8.01%), computational predictions (5.37%), co-localization events (3.63%), genetic interactions (2.87%), shared pathway involvement (1.88%), and common protein domains (0.60%) (Figure 4H). Enrichment analysis revealed significant associations of MAN1C1 and MAN2B2 with mannosidase enzymatic activity, O-glycan-specific glycoside hydrolase function, and glycosidic bond cleavage activity. In addition, MAN1C1 is closely associated with protein de-mannosylation, protein α-1,2-de-mannosylation, protein deglycosylation and glycoprotein degradation processes.

In addition, the constructed lncRNA-miRNA-mRNA regulatory network revealed the post-transcriptional regulatory mechanisms of MAN1C1 and MAN2B2, in which 22 miRNAs and 9 lncRNAs regulate these two biomarkers (Figure 4I). Specifically, MAN1C1 interacts with 17 miRNAs (eg, hsa-miR-376a-3p, hsa-miR-519b-3p), whereas MAN2B2 is targeted by 5 miRNAs (eg, hsa-miR-627-5p, hsa-miR-3691-5p). This differential miRNA targeting suggests that each biomarker has a different regulatory mechanism. lncRNA-miRNA-mRNA interactions further revealed biomarker-specific miRNA regulation, suggesting that post-transcriptional regulation may contribute to its dysregulation in COPD pathogenesis.

Drug Prediction and Molecular Docking Analysis of MAN1C1 and MAN2B2 Reveal Potential Regulatory Drugs

The mRNA-drug interaction network showed that MAN1C1 and MAN2B2 correspondingly predicted 12 (including AM580 and trichostatin A) and 3 (eg, EINECS 250–892-2 and verteporfin) potential target drugs, respectively (Figure 5A). Based on the combined interaction score (Combined.Score), trichostatin A was selected for molecular docking analysis with MAN1C1 and EINECS 250–892-2 with MAN2B2, respectively (Supplementary Table 9). The results showed that there was high-affinity binding between trasclosteia A and the protein encoded by MAN1C1 with a binding energy of −7.0 kcal/mol (Figure 5B). Key interactions included the formation of hydrogen bonds with amino acid residues GLN-535 (3 Å), GLU-604 (3 Å), and SER-602 (2.7 Å) in the active site. For MAN2B2, EINECS 250–892-2 showed stronger binding capacity (−7.9 kcal/mol) and formed hydrogen bonds with GLN-535 (3 Å), GLU-604 (3 Å) and SER-602 (2.7 Å and 2.9 Å) (Figure 5C and Table 1). The above results suggest that trichostatin A and EINECS 250–892-2 may serve as potential modulatory drugs for MAN1C1 and MAN2B2, providing new candidate directions for the treatment of COPD.

Discussion

This study utilized GEO database resources and adopted some bioinformatics methods, including various expression analysis, to identify the biomarkers MAN1C1 and MAN2B2. Furthermore, its potential molecular mechanism of action was explored through analyses such as GSEA. In addition, drug prediction and molecular docking analysis further screened out drugs with potential binding ability to MAN1C1 and MAN2B2 (Trichostatin A and EINECS 250–892-2), which also provided a theoretical basis for potential targeted therapy. However, these results represent merely preliminary exploratory findings and cannot be fully applied in clinical practice. Larger cohorts and mechanistic experiments are needed for further verification.

MAN1C1 (Mannosyl-oligosaccharide 1,2-alpha-mannosidase IC, α-1,2-mannosidase I) is an α-1,2-mannosidase I enzyme localized to the Golgi compartment, serving critical roles in monitoring glycoprotein conformational accuracy and modulating membrane receptor activities.33,34 This study found that MAN1C1 shows a downregulated trend in COPD and is positively correlated with activated B cells and CD4 T cell infiltration. Moreover, MAN1C1 is involved in immune and metabolism-related pathways, suggesting that it perhaps regulate receptor aggregation, signal transduction and immune response through glycosylation, thereby influencing the course of chronic inflammation. Studies have found that Golgi α1, 2-mannosidase I can induce the clustering and regionalization of CD147 (Basigin/EMMPRIN) during the key epithelial-matrix remodeling process of COPD. The glycotype changes of CD147 mediated by MAN1C1 may affect the release of Matrix metalloproteinase (MMP) and barrier repair, and be involved in small airway remodeling.35 In addition, tumor and stem cell research has found that MAN1C1 can affect receptor complex assembly, vesicle transport, and the autophagy/stress pathway.36,37 Research on the glycosylation landscape also indicates that key enzymes in the N-sugar processing chain (including MAN1C1) can reshape the glycosylation patterns of innate and adaptive immune receptors,38 which in turn affects cell polarization and immune effects, is highly consistent with the positive correlation between MAN1C1 and multiple types of immune cells in this study. This indicates that its down-regulation may weaken beneficial immune regulation and make the inflammatory response more persistent.

The MAN2B2 gene encodes a core-specific α-1,6-mannan glycosidase enzyme participating in lysosomal glycoprotein degradation processes. Dysfunctional activity of this gene can result in genetic disorders including congenital disorders of glycosylation (CDG).39 Biochemical evidence has demonstrated that MAN2B2 deficiency represents a novel category within glycosylation-associated immune dysregulation.40 The GSEA conducted in this investigation confirmed that MAN1C1 and MAN2B2 exhibited significant enrichment across multiple inflammatory and immune-associated pathways, particularly TNF signaling and its downstream effector genes. These observations align closely with findings from several previous studies. For example, it was pointed out that TNF signalling plays a central role in lung epithelial stem/progenitor cell response and is involved in repair and regeneration of airway epithelium after injury;41 another study further revealed the critical position of TNF signalling in regulating immune response and inflammation maintenance in COPD animal models at the level of single-cell transcriptome and chromatin accessibility.42 In addition, the significant enrichment of TNF target genes in this study echoes previous studies on inflammatory regulation. It was shown that over-activation of the TNF-α/IL-17 pathway was closely associated with inflammation amplification in a smoking-associated airway inflammation model; while another study showed from another perspective that down-regulation of TNF-α/IL-17 and its subsequent MAPK signalling significantly ameliorated airway inflammation in a COPD animal model.43 Combined with the results of our analysis, MAN1C1 and MAN2B2 in these pathways enriched to may affect cytokine receptors and immune signalling networks through sugar metabolism and glycosylation modifications.

Furthermore, GSVA analysis demonstrated significant upregulation of SCFA-related metabolic pathways in COPD specimens, indicating their potential involvement in immune dysregulation and metabolic disturbances associated with COPD. Accumulating evidence establishes SCFAs as pivotal mediators within the gut-lung axis, orchestrating pulmonary immune homeostasis and influencing respiratory disease progression.44 Kotlyarov et al45 highlighted the importance of gut-derived SCFAs in modulating the intrinsic immune system of the lungs, especially in patients with COPD, where an imbalance of the gut microbiota and SCFAs may exacerbate the abnormalities of the lung immune response. Another study revealed that smoking further affects the immune and metabolic environment in COPD by altering the gut microbiota and the production of SCFAs,46 suggesting that SCFAs may be potentially involved in inflammatory responses related to COPD.

The results of the immune cell infiltration analysis in this study indicate that 12 immune cell types exhibit infiltration differences between disease and control group samples, including Activated B cells, Macrophages, and Neutrophils. Among these, the obvious positive correlation exists between Macrophages and Neutrophils. Additionally, the strongest positive correlation is observed between Activated CD4 T cells and MAN1C1. This finding suggesting that there is a significant abnormality in the regulation of immune responses in COPD, and that the immune system fails to effectively counteract persistent airway inflammation and airway remodelling.47,48 Macrophages and neutrophils play an important role in the immune response in COPD, with the former recruiting the latter through the secretion of cytokines and chemokines, creating a more intense local inflammatory response, which is also consistent with existing studies on the important role of macrophages and neutrophils in COPD progression.49,50 In addition, this study also found that MAN1C1 was positively correlated with activated CD4 cells. This suggests that MAN1C1 may play an important role in the immune microenvironment of COPD. And it has also been documented that MAN1C1 plays a role in regulating the immune response.51 For further analysis of immune imbalance, studies have been done to reveal the defects in immune response in COPD patients. For example, CD8+ T cell function is suppressed by cigarette smoke, leading to reduced immune clearance, which may lead to an increased risk of secondary infections.52 The above results tentatively suggest potential associations with airway remodeling, immune regulation and disease progression in COPD. Nevertheless, these mechanistic inferences remain speculative, as relevant functional assays have not been performed for verification.

This study combined drug prediction with molecular docking analysis to reveal potential regulatory drugs, particularly the binding of trichostatin A to MAN1C1 (binding energy of −7.0 kcal/mol) and EINECS 250–892-2 to MAN2B2 (binding energy of −7.9 kcal/mol). These computational results preliminarily indicate that the two compounds may interact with MAN1C1 and MAN2B2 at the molecular level, providing exploratory clues for subsequent drug mechanism research in COPD. Trichostatin A, a histone deacetylase (HDAC) inhibitor, exerts its effects by modulating oxidative stress and inflammatory signalling pathways, and acts in CD4+ T cells in COPD,53 suggesting that HDAC inhibition regulates key transcription factors (eg NF-κB and HIF1α), which play an important role in the immune regulation of COPD. In addition, it has been shown that HDAC inhibition has significant effects in immune cells, especially in macrophages. Specifically, Neutrophil Elastase in immune cells degrades HDAC and the deacetylase Sirtuin 1, which may negatively affect the immune response in COPD patients.54 This further suggests that by modulating HDAC activity, the function of immune cells in COPD patients may be improved, reducing the inflammatory response and thus slowing disease progression. Meanwhile, a significant association between epigenetic abnormalities (eg, HDAC) and lung cancer risk has been shown in sputum from COPD and smokers.55 This suggests that HDAC inhibitors not only have potential for immune modulation in COPD, but may also play an important role in inhibiting the development of smoking-related lung cancer.

In summary, MAN1C1 and MAN2B2 show potential correlations with the immune microenvironment in COPD, which may offer preliminary clues for exploring early diagnostic markers and personalized treatment strategies for this disease. Based on drug prediction and molecular docking analyses, trichostatin A, an HDAC inhibitor, was predicted to modulate immune cell function and related immune responses in COPD. The compound EINECS 250–892-2 also presented high binding affinity to MAN2B2, making it a candidate molecule for subsequent drug research. Further investigations are required to verify their actual effects, and these findings may serve as a reference for follow-up studies on targeted interventions for COPD.

While this investigation offers preliminary exploratory insights into potential COPD biomarkers and therapeutic candidates, several essential limitations should be fully acknowledged. First, although machine learning methodologies were employed to optimize genetic screening, the external validation cohort contained only 12 samples. Although the immune and inflammatory correlations observed in our analysis are biologically plausible, such a small sample size may substantially reduce the statistical power and generalizability of the model and biomarker results. Therefore, the identified biomarkers should be considered preliminary and hypothesis-generating, and larger-scale and multicenter cohort validation is required to enhance the reliability of the conclusions. Second, this study is purely based on bioinformatics mining and lacks systematic experimental verification, including qPCR, Western blotting, ELISA, animal models, and functional experiments. Without in vitro and in vivo validation, the exact biological functions and detailed mechanisms of MAN1C1 and MAN2B2 in COPD pathogenesis remain unclear, and the actual immunomodulatory effects of the screened compounds cannot be confirmed. Third, all analyses in this study were performed based on peripheral blood transcriptomic datasets. Notably, COPD is primarily a lung-localized inflammatory disease, and peripheral blood gene expression profiles cannot fully recapitulate the pathological alterations in airway and lung tissues. The systemic immune signatures derived from blood may not completely represent the local pulmonary pathological changes of COPD, which may bring potential bias to the interpretation of the results. Finally, although molecular docking and drug prediction analyses screened potential small-molecule compounds, these results only reflect theoretical binding affinity and computational predictions. The therapeutic potential of trichostatin A and EINECS 250–892-2 remains unvalidated. Therefore, all findings in the present study are exploratory and need further rigorous experimental and preclinical verification to confirm their biological and clinical significance.

Conclusions

This study suggests that MMRGs may participate in COPD pathogenesis and could serve as potential biomarkers. The results may provide new insights and preliminary evidence for the early diagnosis, targeted treatment and drug research of COPD. The present work reveals a possible association between MMRGs and COPD pathogenesis, and screens out candidate biomarkers. These observations may bring new ideas and basic evidence for the diagnosis and treatment of COPD. Future studies will incorporate large-scale clinical datasets to further validate the conclusions of this research.

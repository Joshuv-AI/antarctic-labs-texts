2
The corotational stability postulate in the hyperelastic case

Before we expand the argument from Leblond
[
16
]
for the hyperelastic case, we recall the definition of the
Zaremba-Jaumann
derivative using the spin given by the vorticity tensor
W=\textnormal{skew}\,L,L=\dot{F}\,F^{-1}

\displaystyle\boxed{\frac{\mathrm{D}^{\ZJ}}{\mathrm{D}t}[\sigma]:=\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t}[\sigma]+\sigma\,W-W\,\sigma=Q\,\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t}[Q^{T}\,\sigma\,Q]\,Q^{T}\quad\text{for $Q(t)\in{\rm O}(3)$ so that}\;W=\dot{Q}\,Q^{T}\in\mathfrak{so}(3).}

(2.1)

Here,

denotes the material or substantial time-derivative. For more information on the notation, we refer to the Appendix
A.1
.

With formula (
2.1
) we prove next that

\displaystyle\langle\frac{\mathrm{D}^{\ZJ}}{\mathrm{D}t}[\sigma],D\rangle>0\quad\forall\,D\neq 0\qquad\iff\qquad\sym\textrm{D}_{\log\lambda}\widehat{\sigma}(\log\lambda)\in\Sym^{++}(3),

(2.2)

if
\sigma
derives from hyperelasticity. Here, the expression
\widehat{\sigma}(\log\lambda)
denotes the vector of principal stresses of
\widehat{\sigma}(\log V)
(i.e. the vector consisting of the eigenvalues of
\widehat{\sigma}(\log V)
). Due to isotropy we have the equivalence (cf. Remark
A.5
in the Appendix)

\displaystyle\sym\textrm{D}_{\log\lambda}\widehat{\sigma}(\log\lambda)\in\Sym^{++}(3)\qquad\iff\qquad\sym\mathrm{D}_{\log B}\widehat{\sigma}(\log B)\in\Sym^{++}_{4}(6),

(2.3)

which can be combined with (

) to obtain the sought-after relation

\displaystyle\langle\frac{\mathrm{D}^{\ZJ}}{\mathrm{D}t}[\sigma],D\rangle>0\quad\forall\,D\neq 0\qquad\iff\qquad\sym\mathrm{D}_{\log B}\widehat{\sigma}(\log B)\in\Sym^{++}_{4}(6).

(2.4)

The proof is partitioned into several technical steps.

\displaystyle\mathrm{W}(F)=\mathrm{W}(F\,Q)\qquad\qquad\forall\,Q\in{\rm SO}(3).

Remark 2.1

(Notation)
.

We use the following convention. Whenever vector-valued functions are considered, we label the elements of a collection of functions with upper indices and the components of these vector-valued functions with lower indices. For example, considering the family
\{U^{i}\}_{i}

U^{i}:\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{q}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
U^{i}_{j}
denotes the j-component of
U^{i}

A key role in the upcoming analysis is the representation of occurring tensors in a basis of eigenvectors of the conjugate Green-Lagrange strain tensor
E(x,t)=\frac{1}{2}\big(F^{T}(x,t)\,F(x,t)-{\boldsymbol{\mathbbm{1}}}\big)
. Therefore, let
\{U^{i}(x,t)\}_{i=1}^{3}
denote the

and
\{e_{i}(x,t)\}_{i=1}^{3}
the corresponding
eigenvalues

Proposition 2.2
.

Consider a hyperelastic isotropic material with energy density
\mathrm{W}(E)
. Then there exists an auxiliary function
\breve{\mathrm{W}}(e)
, where
e(x,t)=\big(e_{1}(x,t),e_{2}(x,t),e_{3}(x,t)\big)
is the vector field of the eigenvalues of
E(x,t)

\displaystyle\mathrm{W}(E(x,t))=\breve{\mathrm{W}}(e(x,t))\qquad\qquad\forall\,(x,t)\in\Omega\times[0,T).

(2.12)

Proof.

Considering the stored energy density
\mathrm{W}(E)
as a function
\mathrm{W}(E)=\overline{\mathrm{W}}(C)
of the Cauchy strain tensor
C
, the isotropy requirement reads as
[
21
]

\displaystyle\overline{\mathrm{W}}(C)=\overline{\mathrm{W}}(Q\,C\,Q^{T})\qquad\qquad\forall\,Q\in{\rm SO}(3).

(2.13)

Since
E=\frac{1}{2}(C-{\boldsymbol{\mathbbm{1}}})\;\iff\;C=2\,E+{\boldsymbol{\mathbbm{1}}}
and
\mathrm{W}(E)=\overline{\mathrm{W}}(C)
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathrm{W}(E)=\overline{\mathrm{W}}(2\,E+{\boldsymbol{\mathbbm{1}}})=\overline{\mathrm{W}}\big(Q\,(2\,E+{\boldsymbol{\mathbbm{1}}})Q^{T}\big)\qquad\qquad\forall\,Q\in{\rm SO}(3).

(2.14)

Additionally, since
C=F^{T}\,F=2\,E+{\boldsymbol{\mathbbm{1}}}\in\Sym^{++}(3)
by the spectral theorem, there exists a couple
(R,\widehat{D})\in\textrm{SO}(3)\times\textrm{Diag}(3)
, such that
2\,E+{\boldsymbol{\mathbbm{1}}}=R^{T}\,\widehat{D}\,R
and the diagonal elements of
\widehat{D}
are the eigenvalues of
2\,E+{\boldsymbol{\mathbbm{1}}}
. Therefore

\displaystyle\mathrm{W}(E)=\overline{\mathrm{W}}(2\,E+{\boldsymbol{\mathbbm{1}}})=\overline{\mathrm{W}}\big(Q^{T}\,(2\,E+{\boldsymbol{\mathbbm{1}}})Q\big)=\overline{\mathrm{W}}\big(Q^{T}R^{T}\widehat{D}\,R\,Q\big)=\overline{\mathrm{W}}\big((R\,Q)^{T}\widehat{D}\,R\,Q\big)\qquad\forall\,Q\in{\rm SO}(3),

(2.15)

with an orthogonal matrix
\widetilde{Q}=R\,Q
, so that by the isotropy

\displaystyle\mathrm{W}(E)=\overline{\mathrm{W}}(2\,E+{\boldsymbol{\mathbbm{1}}})=\overline{\mathrm{W}}\big(\widetilde{Q}^{T}\widehat{D}\,\widetilde{Q}\big)=\overline{\mathrm{W}}(D)=\overline{\mathrm{W}}(\diag(2\,e_{1}+1,2\,e_{2}+1,2\,e_{3}+1)).

(2.16)

Inasmuch as the spectrum
\textrm{spt}(C)=\textrm{spt}(2\,E+{\boldsymbol{\mathbbm{1}}})=2\,\textrm{spt}(E)+1
, we can finally introduce the auxiliary function
\breve{\mathrm{W}}:\mathbb{R}^{3}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}

E

\displaystyle\mathrm{W}(E(x,t))

\displaystyle=\overline{\mathrm{W}}(\widehat{D})=\breve{\mathrm{W}}(e(x,t))\qquad\qquad\forall(x,t)\in\Omega\times[0,T).\qed

\dot{E}_{33}\end{pmatrix}\!\!,\widehat{Q}_{\textrm{hyp}(1)}.\!\!\begin{pmatrix}\dot{E}_{11}\\
\dot{E}_{22}\\
\dot{E}_{33}\end{pmatrix}\right\rangle_{\mathclap{\mathbb{R}^{3}}}+\left\langle\begin{pmatrix}\dot{E}_{23}\\
\dot{E}_{13}\\
\dot{E}_{12}\end{pmatrix}\!\!,\widehat{Q}_{\textrm{hyp}(2)}.\!\!\begin{pmatrix}\dot{E}_{23}\\
\dot{E}_{13}\\
\dot{E}_{12}\end{pmatrix}\right\rangle_{\mathclap{\mathbb{R}^{3}}}.

(2.23)

This separation allows us to establish the positive definiteness of
Q_{\textrm{hyp}}
by separately studying the positive definiteness of
Q_{\textrm{hyp}(1)}
and
Q_{\textrm{hyp}(2)}
. In the next step, we need to develop separately the three terms

\langle\dot{S}_{2}(E),\dot{E}\rangle
,

2\,\,\textrm{tr}\,\big(C^{-1}\,\dot{E}\,S_{2}\,\dot{E}\big)
and

\langle C^{-1},\dot{E}\rangle\,\langle S_{2}(E),\dot{E}\rangle

comprising
Q_{\textrm{hyp}}(\dot{E})
. At this point it is important to remark that, in general, the vectors
\{U^{i}\}_{i=1}^{3}
are not the eigenvectors of the eigenvalues of
\dot{E}
and hence
\dot{E}
is not diagonal in this basis. Thus, we begin our considerations with the following

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
\{U^{i}\}_{i=1}^{3}
be the eigenvectors of
E
with eigenvalues
\{e_{i}(x,t)\}_{i=1}^{3}
. Then the tensor
\dot{E}
admits the representation

\displaystyle\dot{E}=\sum_{i=1}^{3}\bigg(\dot{e}_{i}\,U^{i}\otimes U^{i}+e_{i}\,\dot{U}^{i}\otimes U^{i}+e_{i}\,U^{i}\otimes\dot{U}^{i}\bigg)=\sum_{j,k=1}^{3}\dot{E}_{jk}\,U^{j}\otimes U^{k},

(2.24)

with

\displaystyle\dot{E}_{jk}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lr}\dot{e}_{j}&\qquad\text{if}\quad j=k,\\
(e_{j}-e_{k})\,\big\langle\dot{U}^{j},U^{k}\big\rangle&\qquad\text{if}\quad j\neq k.\end{array}\right.

Proof.

We begin the calculation of the components
\{\dot{E}_{jk}\}_{j,k=1}^{3}
of
\dot{E}
in the basis
\{U^{i}\}_{i=1}^{3}
by differentiating

\displaystyle E=\sum_{i}e_{i}\,U^{i}\otimes U^{i}\quad\implies\quad\dot{E}=\sum_{i}(\dot{e}_{i}\,U^{i}\otimes U^{i}+e_{i}\,\dot{U}^{i}\otimes U^{i}+e_{i}\,U^{i}\otimes\dot{U}^{i}).

(2.27)

The component
\dot{E}_{jk}=\langle\dot{E}.U^{j},U^{k}\rangle
of
\dot{E}
can be consequently expressed as

\displaystyle\dot{E}_{jk}

\displaystyle=\left\langle\dot{E}.U^{j},U^{k}\right\rangle=\left\langle\bigg[\sum_{i=1}^{3}\bigg(\dot{e}_{i}\,U^{i}\otimes U^{i}+e_{i}\,\dot{U}^{i}\otimes U^{i}+e_{i}\,U^{i}\otimes\dot{U}^{i}\bigg)\bigg]\,U^{j},U^{k}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\Big\langle\sum_{i=1}^{3}\Big(\dot{e}_{i}\,U^{i}\otimes U^{i}\Big)U^{j},U^{k}\Big\rangle+\Big\langle\sum_{i=1}^{3}\Big(e_{i}\,\dot{U}^{i}\otimes U^{i}\Big)U^{j},U^{k}\Big\rangle+\Big\langle\sum_{i=1}^{3}\Big(e_{i}\,U^{i}\otimes\dot{U}^{i}\Big)U^{j},U^{k}\Big\rangle

\displaystyle=\Big\langle\sum_{i=1}^{3}\underbrace{\big\langle U^{i},U^{j}\big\rangle}_{\delta_{ij}}\dot{e}_{i}\,U^{i},U^{k}\Big\rangle+\Big\langle\sum_{i=1}^{3}\underbrace{\big\langle U^{i},U^{j}\big\rangle}_{\delta_{ij}}e_{i}\,\dot{U}^{i},U^{k}\Big\rangle+\Big\langle\sum_{i=1}^{3}\left\langle\dot{U}_{i},U_{j}\right\rangle e_{i}\,U^{i},U^{k}\Big\rangle

(2.28)

\displaystyle=\big\langle\dot{e}_{j}\,U^{j},U^{k}\big\rangle+\big\langle e_{j}\,\dot{U}^{j},U^{k}\big\rangle+\Big\langle\sum_{i=1}^{3}\big\langle\dot{U}^{i},U^{j}\big\rangle\,e_{i}\,U^{i},U^{k}\Big\rangle

\displaystyle=\dot{e}_{j}\,\underbrace{\big\langle U^{j},U^{k}\big\rangle}_{=\,\delta_{jk}}+e_{j}\big\langle\dot{U}^{j},U^{k}\big\rangle+\sum_{i=1}^{3}\big\langle\dot{U}^{i},U^{j}\big\rangle e_{i}\,\delta_{ik}=\dot{e}_{j}\,\delta_{jk}+e_{j}\big\langle\dot{U}^{j},U^{k}\big\rangle+e_{k}\big\langle\dot{U}^{k},U^{j}\big\rangle.

From the orthonomality
\left\langle U^{k},U^{j}\right\rangle=\delta_{kj}
given by the spectral theorem we see

\displaystyle 0=\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t}\delta_{kj}=\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t}\big\langle U^{k},U^{j}\big\rangle=\big\langle\dot{U}^{k},U^{j}\big\rangle+\big\langle U^{k},\dot{U}^{j}\big\rangle\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad\big\langle\dot{U}^{k},U^{j}\big\rangle=-\big\langle U^{k},\dot{U}^{j}\big\rangle

(2.29)

and obtain

\dot{E}_{jk}=\dot{e}_{j}\,\delta_{jk}+e_{j}\big\langle\dot{U}^{j},U^{k}\big\rangle+e_{k}\big\langle\dot{U}^{k},U^{j}\big\rangle=\dot{e}_{j}\,\delta_{jk}+(e_{j}-e_{k})\big\langle\dot{U}^{j},U^{k}\big\rangle\qquad\forall\,i,j\in\{1,2,3\}.

(2.30)

In particular

\displaystyle\dot{E}_{jj}=\dot{e}_{j}\qquad\forall\,j\in\{1,2,3\}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\dot{E}_{jk}

\displaystyle=(e_{j}-e_{k})\,\big\langle\dot{U}^{j},U^{k}\big\rangle\qquad\forall\,j\neq k.\qed

(2.31)

Corollary 2.5
.

The orthogonal decomposition of
\dot{U}^{j}
in the orthonormal basis
\{U^{k}\}_{k=1}^{3}
is given by

\dot{U}^{j}=\sum_{k\neq j}\frac{\dot{E}_{jk}}{e_{j}-e_{k}}U^{k},\qquad\text{for}\qquad e_{j}\neq e_{k},

(2.32)

where
\dot{E}_{jk}=0
, if
e_{j}=e_{k}
, as can be seen from (
2.30
).

Proof.

From the representation

\dot{E}_{jk}=(e_{j}-e_{k})\,\big\langle\dot{U}^{j},U^{k}\big\rangle\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad\big\langle\dot{U}^{j},U^{k}\big\rangle=\frac{\dot{E}_{jk}}{e_{j}-e_{k}}\qquad\forall j\neq k

(2.33)

we easily infer

\dot{U}^{j}=\sum_{k=1}^{3}\big\langle\dot{U}^{j},U^{k}\big\rangle\,U^{k}=\sum_{k\neq j}\big\langle\dot{U}^{j},U^{k}\big\rangle U^{k}=\sum_{k\neq j}\frac{\dot{E}_{jk}}{e_{j}-e_{k}}U^{k},

(2.34)

where
\langle\dot{U}^{j},U^{j}\rangle=0
since
\lVert U^{j}\rVert^{2}=1
and thus
\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t}\lVert U^{j}\rVert^{2}=2\,\langle\dot{U}^{j},U^{j}\rangle=0
.
∎

Next we develop the quadratic form
Q_{\textrm{hyp}}
.

Proposition 2.6
.

We have

\displaystyle\big\langle\dot{S}_{2},\dot{E}\big\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j=1}^{3}\frac{\partial^{2}\breve{\mathrm{W}}}{\partial e_{i}\partial e_{j}}\,\dot{E}_{jj}\,\dot{E}_{ii}+\sum_{i\neq j}\frac{\frac{\partial\breve{\mathrm{W}}}{\partial e_{i}}-\frac{\partial\breve{\mathrm{W}}}{\partial e_{j}}}{e_{i}-e_{j}}\dot{E}_{ij}^{2},

(2.35)

\displaystyle\big\langle C^{-1},\dot{E}\big\rangle\big\langle S_{2},\dot{E}\big\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{3}\frac{1}{\lambda_{i}^{2}}\frac{\partial\breve{\mathrm{W}}}{\partial e_{i}}\dot{E}_{ii}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i\neq j}\bigg(\frac{1}{\lambda_{i}^{2}}\frac{\partial\breve{\mathrm{W}}}{\partial e_{j}}+\frac{1}{\lambda_{j}^{2}}\frac{\partial\breve{\mathrm{W}}}{\partial e_{i}}\bigg)\dot{E}_{ii}\dot{E}_{jj},

\displaystyle\textnormal{tr}\big(C^{-1}\,\dot{E}\,S_{2}\,\dot{E}\big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{3}\frac{1}{\lambda_{i}^{2}}\dot{E}_{ii}^{2}\frac{\partial\breve{\mathrm{W}}}{\partial e_{i}}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i\neq j}\bigg(\frac{1}{\lambda_{i}^{2}}\frac{\partial\breve{\mathrm{W}}}{\partial e_{j}}+\frac{1}{\lambda_{j}^{2}}\frac{\partial\breve{\mathrm{W}}}{\partial e_{i}}\bigg)\dot{E}_{ij}^{2}.

Proof.

Starting with
\eqref{eq.all1}_{1}
,, we can use (
2.34
) and (
2.4
) in the expression of
\dot{S}_{2}
from Corollary
2.3
, to obtain

\displaystyle\dot{S}_{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{3}\bigg[\bigg(\sum_{j=1}^{3}\frac{\partial^{2}\breve{\mathrm{W}}}{\partial e_{i}\partial e_{j}}\,\dot{e}_{j}\bigg)\;U^{i}\otimes U^{i}+\frac{\partial\breve{\mathrm{W}}}{\partial e_{i}}\bigg(\dot{U}^{i}\otimes U^{i}+U^{i}\otimes\dot{U}^{i}\bigg)\bigg]

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j=1}^{3}\frac{\partial^{2}\breve{\mathrm{W}}}{\partial e_{i}\partial e_{j}}\,\dot{E}_{jj}\;U^{i}\otimes U^{i}+\sum_{i\neq j}\frac{\frac{\partial\breve{\mathrm{W}}}{\partial e_{i}}-\frac{\partial\breve{\mathrm{W}}}{\partial e_{j}}}{e_{i}-e_{j}}\,\dot{E}_{ij}U^{i}\otimes U^{j}.

(2.36)

Calculating the scalar product on both sides of equation (
2.36
) with
\dot{E}=\sum_{h,k}\dot{E}_{hk}\,U^{h}\otimes U^{k}
then yields

\displaystyle\big\langle\dot{S}_{2},\dot{E}\big\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle\sum_{i,j=1}^{3}\frac{\partial^{2}\breve{\mathrm{W}}}{\partial e_{i}\partial e_{j}}\,\dot{E}_{jj}\;U^{i}\otimes U^{i}+\sum_{i\neq j}\frac{\frac{\partial\breve{\mathrm{W}}}{\partial e_{i}}-\frac{\partial\breve{\mathrm{W}}}{\partial e_{j}}}{e_{i}-e_{j}}\dot{E}_{ij}U^{i}\otimes U^{j},\sum_{h,k}\dot{E}_{hk}U^{h}\otimes U^{k}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle\sum_{i,j=1}^{3}\frac{\partial^{2}\breve{\mathrm{W}}}{\partial e_{i}\partial e_{j}}\,\dot{E}_{jj}\;U^{i}\otimes U^{i},\sum_{h,k}\dot{E}_{hk}U^{h}\otimes U^{k}\right\rangle+\left\langle\sum_{i\neq j}\frac{\frac{\partial\breve{\mathrm{W}}}{\partial e_{i}}-\frac{\partial\breve{\mathrm{W}}}{\partial e_{j}}}{e_{i}-e_{j}}\dot{E}_{ij}U^{i}\otimes U^{j},\sum_{h\neq k}\dot{E}_{hk}U^{h}\otimes U^{k}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j=1}^{3}\frac{\partial^{2}\breve{\mathrm{W}}}{\partial e_{i}\partial e_{j}}\,\dot{E}_{jj}\,\dot{E}_{ii}+\sum_{i\neq j}\frac{\frac{\partial\breve{\mathrm{W}}}{\partial e_{i}}-\frac{\partial\breve{\mathrm{W}}}{\partial e_{j}}}{e_{i}-e_{j}}\dot{E}_{ij}^{2}.

(2.37)

For

\displaystyle\big\langle S_{2},\dot{E}\big\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{3}\frac{\partial\breve{\mathrm{W}}}{\partial e_{j}}\underbrace{\dot{e}_{j}}_{=\,\dot{E}_{jj}}+\sum_{j=1}^{3}\frac{\partial\breve{\mathrm{W}}}{\partial e_{j}}\underbrace{\bigl\lVert U^{j}\bigr\rVert^{2}}_{=\,1}\underbrace{\big\langle\dot{U}^{j},U^{j}\big\rangle}_{=\,0}+\sum_{j=1}^{3}\frac{\partial\breve{\mathrm{W}}}{\partial e_{j}}\underbrace{\bigl\lVert U^{j}\bigr\rVert^{2}}_{=\,1}\underbrace{\big\langle\dot{U}^{j},U^{j}\big\rangle}_{=\,0}=\sum_{j=1}^{3}\frac{\partial\breve{\mathrm{W}}}{\partial e_{j}}\dot{E}_{jj}.

\displaystyle\big\langle C^{-1},\dot{E}\big\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{3}\frac{1}{\lambda_{j}^{2}}\dot{e}_{j}\overset{\textrm{by}\,\eqref{eq.3}}{=}\sum_{j=1}^{3}\frac{1}{\lambda_{j}^{2}}\dot{E}_{jj}.

(2.41)

Therefore

\displaystyle\big\langle C^{-1},\dot{E}\big\rangle\big\langle S_{2},\dot{E}\big\rangle

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{3}\frac{1}{\lambda_{i}^{2}}\dot{E}_{ii}\right)\left(\sum_{j=1}^{3}\frac{\partial\breve{\mathrm{W}}}{\partial e_{j}}\dot{E}_{jj}\right)=\sum_{i=1}^{3}\frac{1}{\lambda_{i}^{2}}\frac{\partial\breve{\mathrm{W}}}{\partial e_{i}}\dot{E}_{ii}^{2}+\sum_{i\neq j}\frac{1}{\lambda_{i}^{2}}\frac{\partial\breve{\mathrm{W}}}{\partial e_{j}}\dot{E}_{ii}\dot{E}_{jj}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{3}\frac{1}{\lambda_{i}^{2}}\frac{\partial\breve{\mathrm{W}}}{\partial e_{i}}\dot{E}_{ii}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i\neq j}\bigg(\frac{1}{\lambda_{i}^{2}}\frac{\partial\breve{\mathrm{W}}}{\partial e_{j}}+\frac{1}{\lambda_{j}^{2}}\frac{\partial\breve{\mathrm{W}}}{\partial e_{i}}\bigg)\dot{E}_{ii}\dot{E}_{jj}.

(2.42)

For the last term of (

2.3
Logarithmic energy and positive definiteness of the bilinear forms

With the help of the foregoing development we now prove the equivalence

\displaystyle Q_{\textrm{hyp}(1)}>0\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad\sym\textrm{D}_{\log\lambda}\widehat{\sigma}(\log\lambda)\in\Sym^{++}(3)\,.

In fact, as we will discuss in Section

\displaystyle Q_{\textrm{hyp}}>0\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad\sym\textrm{D}_{\log\lambda}\widehat{\sigma}(\log\lambda)\in\Sym^{++}(3).

(2.55)

For further simplification of the quadratic forms

\displaystyle\sigma=\frac{1}{J}\,F\,S_{2}\,F^{T}=\frac{1}{J}\,F\,\left(\sum_{i}\frac{\partial\breve{\mathrm{W}}(e)}{\partial e_{i}}\,U^{i}\otimes U^{i}\right)\,F^{T}.

Computing the eigenvalues of
\sigma

\sigma

\displaystyle\sigma.u^{j}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{J}\Bigg(F\,\bigg(\sum_{i}\frac{\partial\breve{\mathrm{W}}(e)}{\partial e_{i}}\,U^{i}\otimes U^{i}\bigg)\,F^{T}\Bigg).u^{j}=\frac{1}{J}\;F\,\bigg(\sum_{i}\frac{\partial\breve{\mathrm{W}}(e)}{\partial e_{i}}\,U^{i}\otimes U^{i}\bigg)\,(F^{T}\,F).U^{j}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{J}\;F.\bigg(\sum_{i}\frac{\partial\breve{\mathrm{W}}(e)}{\partial e_{i}}\,U^{i}\lambda_{j}^{2}\delta_{ij}\bigg)=\frac{1}{J}\;F.\bigg(\frac{\partial\breve{\mathrm{W}}(e)}{\partial e_{j}}\,U^{j}\lambda_{j}^{2}\bigg)=\frac{1}{J}\,\lambda_{j}^{2}\,\frac{\partial\breve{\mathrm{W}}(e)}{\partial e_{j}}\,u^{j},

because

C=2E+{\boldsymbol{\mathbbm{1}}}=\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}^{2}\,U^{i}\otimes U^{i}

and hence
\sigma
decomposes as

Thus, finally, the eigenvalues of

Note that the eigenvectors

are distinct since

are distinct. Let us explicitly remark that they are functions of the eigenvalues of

(2.59)

where

\log\lambda

(2.60)

2.3.1
Analysis of

In the following, we show that the positive definiteness of

\displaystyle Q_{\textrm{hyp}(1)}>0\qquad\iff\qquad\sym\textrm{D}_{\log\lambda}\widehat{\sigma}(\log\lambda)\in\Sym^{++}(3).

In a first step we remark that the entries of

are given by

\displaystyle\left(\sym\,\mathrm{D}_{\log\lambda}\widehat{\sigma}(\log\lambda)\right)_{ij}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{\partial\widehat{\sigma}_{i}}{\partial(\log\lambda_{i})}&\qquad i=j,\\
\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\partial\widehat{\sigma}_{i}}{\partial(\log\lambda_{j})}+\frac{\partial\widehat{\sigma}_{j}}{\partial(\log\lambda_{i})}\right)&\qquad i\neq j.\end{array}\right.

Now we need to establish a relation between the entries of
\sym\,\mathrm{D}_{\log\lambda}\widehat{\sigma}(\log\lambda)
and the components of
Q_{\textrm{hyp}(1)}
. To achieve this, we need to obtain explicit relations between
\widehat{\sigma}
, its derivatives and the derivatives of
\widehat{\mathrm{W}}
.

We begin by computing the derivative on both sides of equation (
2.59
) w.r.t.
\lambda

\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial\lambda_{i}}\breve{\mathrm{W}}\big(e(\lambda)\big)=\left\langle\textrm{D}_{e}\,\breve{\mathrm{W}}\big(e(\lambda)\big),\frac{\partial e}{\partial\lambda_{i}}\right\rangle=\sum_{j}\frac{\partial\breve{\mathrm{W}}}{\partial e_{j}}\big(e(\lambda)\big)\frac{\partial e_{j}}{\partial\lambda_{i}}=\sum_{j}\frac{\partial\breve{\mathrm{W}}}{\partial e_{j}}\big(e(\lambda)\big)\,\lambda_{i}\,\delta_{ij}=\frac{\partial\breve{\mathrm{W}}}{\partial e_{i}}\big(e(\lambda)\big)\;\lambda_{i},\\[14.22636pt]

(2.64)

Hence from the identity
\frac{\partial}{\partial\lambda_{i}}\breve{\mathrm{W}}\big(e(\lambda)\big)=\frac{\partial}{\partial\lambda_{i}}\widehat{\mathrm{W}}(\log\lambda)

\frac{\partial\widehat{\mathrm{W}}(\log\lambda)}{\partial(\log\lambda_{i})}=\frac{\partial\breve{\mathrm{W}}}{\partial e_{i}}\big(e(\lambda)\big)\;\lambda_{i}^{2}=J\,\sigma_{i}(e(\lambda))=J\,\widehat{\sigma}_{i}(\lambda)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\widehat{\sigma}_{i}=\frac{1}{J}\frac{\partial\widehat{\mathrm{W}}(\log\lambda)}{\partial(\log\lambda_{i})},\qquad\forall\,i\in\{1,2,3\}.

(2.65)

Recalling that

\displaystyle J=\det F=\lambda_{1}\,\lambda_{2}\,\lambda_{3}=e^{\log\lambda_{1}+\log\lambda_{2}+\log\lambda_{3}},

(2.66)

we proceed with the relation

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\widehat{\sigma}_{i}}{\partial\log\lambda_{i}}

is strictly monotone, i.e.,

\displaystyle\text{TSTS-M${}^{+}$:}\quad\langle\widehat{\sigma}(\log V_{2})-\widehat{\sigma}(\log V_{1}),\log V_{2}-\log V_{1}\rangle>0\qquad\forall\,V_{1},V_{2}\in\Sym^{++}(3),\quad V_{1}\neq V_{2}.

(2.85)

We begin by considering the two-dimensional case, i.e.
V\in\Sym^{++}(2)
. Since
\sigma(V)=\widehat{\sigma}(\log V)
is an isotropic tensor function, the principal Cauchy stresses
\widehat{\sigma}_{i}(\log\lambda)
are given by the diagonal entries of
\widehat{\sigma}(\log\diag(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}))
, which itself is a diagonal matrix, i.e.

\displaystyle\sigma(\log\diag(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}))=\begin{pmatrix}\sigma_{1}(\log\lambda_{1},\log\lambda_{2})&0\\
0&\sigma_{2}(\log\lambda_{1},\log\lambda_{2})\end{pmatrix}.

(2.86)

Additionally, also due to isotropy, we have by reversing
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}

\displaystyle\sigma(\log\diag(\lambda_{2},\lambda_{1}))=\begin{pmatrix}\sigma_{2}(\log\lambda_{1},\log\lambda_{2})&0\\
0&\sigma_{1}(\log\lambda_{1},\log\lambda_{2})\end{pmatrix}.

(2.87)

Now, the monotonicity (
2.85
) implies with
V_{1}=\diag(\lambda_{2},\lambda_{1}),\;V_{2}=\diag(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle<\langle\widehat{\sigma}(\log V_{2})-\widehat{\sigma}(\log V_{1}),\log V_{2}-\log V_{1}\rangle

(2.88)

\displaystyle=\langle\begin{pmatrix}\widehat{\sigma}_{2}&0\\
0&\widehat{\sigma}_{1}\end{pmatrix}-\begin{pmatrix}\widehat{\sigma}_{1}&0\\
0&\widehat{\sigma}_{2}\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}\log\lambda_{2}&0\\
0&\log\lambda_{1}\end{pmatrix}-\begin{pmatrix}\log\lambda_{1}&0\\
0&\log\lambda_{2}\end{pmatrix}\rangle

\displaystyle=(\widehat{\sigma}_{2}-\widehat{\sigma}_{1})\,(\log\lambda_{2}-\log\lambda_{1})+(\widehat{\sigma}_{1}-\widehat{\sigma}_{2})\,(\log\lambda_{1}-\log\lambda_{2})=2\,(\log\lambda_{2}-\log\lambda_{1})\,(\widehat{\sigma}_{2}-\widehat{\sigma}_{1})

\displaystyle\implies\qquad 0

\displaystyle<(\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{1})\,(\sigma_{2}-\sigma_{1}),

where the last implication is due to the monotonicity of the (scalar) logarithm function (i.e.
\lambda_{2}>\lambda_{1}
if and only if
\log\lambda_{2}>\log\lambda_{1}
) and the fact that
\widehat{\sigma}_{i}(\log\lambda)=\sigma_{i}(\lambda)
per definition.

Generalizing this argument to three (or even
n
) dimensions we pick two distinct eigenvalues
\lambda_{i}\neq\lambda_{j}
of the matrix
V
and interchange their position in the diagonal matrix
\log\diag(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n})
, as we did for
\lambda_{1}
and
\lambda_{2}
in the two-dimensional case. This once again yields two matrices
V_{1}=\diag(\ldots,\lambda_{i},\ldots,\lambda_{j},\ldots)
and
V_{2}=(\ldots,\lambda_{j},\ldots,\lambda_{i},\ldots)
that can be inserted into the monotonicity inequality (
2.85
). When subtracting
\log V_{2}-\log V_{1}
, every entry that is not
\log\lambda_{i}
or
\log\lambda_{j}
cancels out, resulting in

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle<\langle\widehat{\sigma}(\log V_{2})-\widehat{\sigma}(\log V_{1}),\log V_{2}-\log V_{1}\rangle

\displaystyle=(\widehat{\sigma}_{i}-\widehat{\sigma}_{j})\,(\log\lambda_{i}-\log\lambda_{j})+(\widehat{\sigma}_{j}-\widehat{\sigma}_{i})\,(\log\lambda_{j}-\log\lambda_{i})

\displaystyle=2\,(\log\lambda_{i}-\log\lambda_{j})\,(\widehat{\sigma}_{i}-\widehat{\sigma}_{j})

\displaystyle\implies\qquad 0

\displaystyle<(\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j})\,(\sigma_{i}-\sigma_{j}),\quad\text{for}\quad i\neq j,

(2.89)

with the same reasoning as in the two-dimensional case.
∎

Remark 2.9
.

Note that monotonicity of
\widehat{\sigma}(\log V)
in
\log V
is equivalent to monotonicity of
\widehat{\sigma}(\log B)
in
\log B
, since
\log B=2\,\log V
.

2.3.3
Summarizing considerations for the hyperelastic case

Summarizing the results from the previous Sections, we have proven:

For an isotropic hyperelastic material, the corotational stability postulate (CSP)
\langle\frac{\mathrm{D}^{\ZJ}}{\mathrm{D}t}[\sigma],D\rangle>0
is equivalent to the strong Hilbert-monotonicity of
\widehat{\sigma}
in
\log V
, which is TSTS-M
++
.

It will be proven in detail (see Appendix
A.4
) with the methods presented in this chapter, that Hill’s inequality
\langle\frac{\mathrm{D}^{\ZJ}}{\mathrm{D}t}[\tau],D\rangle>0
(see
[
8
,
9
]
) for the Kirchhoff stress tensor
\tau
is satisfied if and only if the tensor of components
\frac{\partial\tau_{i}}{\partial(\log\lambda_{j})}
, which is (major) symmetric since
\tau_{i}=J\,\sigma_{i}=\textrm{D}_{\log\lambda_{i}}\widehat{\mathrm{W}}(\log\lambda_{i})
, is positive-definite (even for the more general Cauchy-elastic case). In the hyperelastic case this implies that
\log V\mapstochar\rightarrow\widehat{\mathrm{W}}(\log V)
must be a convex function and for
\tau=\mathrm{D}_{\log V}\widehat{\mathrm{W}}(\log V)
it holds

\displaystyle\langle\tau(\log V_{1})-\tau(\log V_{2}),\log V_{1}-\log V_{2}\rangle>0,\qquad\forall\,V_{1},V_{2}\in\Sym^{++}(3),\quad V_{1}\neq V_{2}.

(2.90)

This requirement, however, is not strong enough to exclude physically unacceptable responses
, as shows the constitutive Hencky energy
[
24
]
, for which
\tau=2\,\mu\,\log V+\lambda\,\textnormal{tr}(\log V)\cdot{\boldsymbol{\mathbbm{1}}}
is strongly Hilbert-monotone in
\log V
for
2\,\mu+3\,\lambda>0
.

3
The corotational stability postulate in the Cauchy-elastic case

In this section we generalize the results for hyperelasticity and the Zaremba-Jaumann rate from the previous section by showing, that the same conclusion still holds true in the Cauchy-elastic case. The crucial difference for Cauchy-elasticity consists in the fact that the second Piola-Kirchhoff stress tensor
S_{2}
is now not necessarily derived from an elastic energy density
\mathrm{W}(F)
. Thus, its derivative
\textrm{D}_{E}\,S_{2}(E)
could lack the major symmetry property.

More precisely, in the hyperelastic case it is
\textrm{D}_{E}\,S_{2}(E)=\textrm{D}^{2}_{E}\mathrm{W}(E)
, so that
\textrm{D}_{E}\,S_{2}
inherits both the major and minor symmetries by Schwarz’s theorem. This means that

\textrm{D}_{E}\,S_{2}\in\Sym\big(\Sym(3),\Sym(3)\,\big)\coloneqq\big\{T\in\textrm{Lin}\big(\Sym(3),\Sym(3)\,\big)\;\left.\right|\;\left\langle T.A,B\right\rangle=\left\langle A,T.B\right\rangle\;\forall\,A,B\in\Sym(3)\big\}.

(3.1)

Component-wise,
\textrm{D}_{E}\,S_{2}\in\Sym\big(\Sym(3),\Sym(3)\,\big)
reads as

\displaystyle(\textrm{D}_{E}\,S_{2})_{ijhk}=(\textrm{D}_{E}\,S_{2})_{jihk}=(\textrm{D}_{E}\,S_{2})_{ijkh}=(\textrm{D}_{E}\,S_{2})_{hkij}\qquad\forall\,i,j,h,k\in\{1,2,3\}.

(3.2)

However, in the Cauchy-elastic case, we only have
\textrm{D}_{E}\,S_{2}\in\textrm{Lin}\big(\Sym(3),\Sym(3)\,\big)
, i.e. component-wise,

\displaystyle(\textrm{D}_{E}\,S_{2})_{ijhk}=(\textrm{D}_{E}\,S_{2})_{jihk}=(\textrm{D}_{E}\,S_{2})_{ijkh}\qquad\forall i,j,h,k\in\{1,2,3\}.

(3.3)

Let us explicitly remark that
\textrm{D}_{E}\,S_{2}(E)
is the linearization of the second Piola-Kirchhoff stress tensor
S_{2}(E)
at
E
, so that
S_{2}:\Sym(3)\rightarrow\Sym(3)
is in general a non-linear function
and (
T_{E}\Sym(3)
denoting the tangential space of
\Sym(3)
)

\displaystyle\textrm{D}_{E}\,S_{2}(E):\Sym(3)\simeq T_{E}\Sym(3)\xrightarrow{\phantom{aaaa}}T_{E}\Sym(3)\simeq\Sym(3)

(3.4)

because
\Sym(3)
is an affine space.

As before, our goal consists in showing that the bilinear form

\displaystyle Q_{\textrm{ela}}(\dot{E})

\displaystyle=\langle\dot{S}_{2}(E),\dot{E}\rangle+2\,\,\textrm{tr}\,\big(C^{-1}\,\dot{E}\,S_{2}\,\dot{E}\big)-\langle C^{-1},\dot{E}\rangle\,\langle S_{2}(E),\dot{E}\rangle

(3.5)

derived in Section
2.1
is positive definite even in the Cauchy-elastic case.

4
Conclusion and open questions

We have shown that the corotational stability postulate (CSP) expressed in terms of the Zaremba-Jaumann rate
\langle\frac{\mathrm{D}^{\ZJ}}{\mathrm{D}t}[\sigma],D\rangle>0,\;\forall\,D\in\Sym(3)\!\setminus\!\{0\}
is equivalent to
\sym\mathrm{D}_{\log B}\widehat{\sigma}(\log B)\in\Sym^{++}_{4}(6)
, which is essentially the monotonicity of the Cauchy stress
\sigma
with respect to the logarithmic strain
\log B
. It is still surprising that the CSP condition “favors” the logarithmic strain. The hidden mechanism behind this feature needs to be clarified. Moreover, certain limitations of the result and the methodology must be acknowledged.

1.

Any constitutive condition should be independent of an assumed rate. This is presently not the case.

2.

The use of the Zaremba-Jaumann rate seems somewhat adhoc. Why should the result be restricted to this rate?

3.

Hill uses the Kirchhoff stress
\tau
while Leblond starts with the Cauchy stress
\sigma
. Thus the question arises: Which stress should be taken in the corotational stability postulate (CSP)?

4.

The proof needs a lot of technical machinery in Lagrangean axes and seems to hide the essential ideas, thus raising the question: can we expect a similar result if we would use a different corotational rate and more importantly: how could we transfer the methods of proof?

5.

The corotational stability postulate should be motivated in a larger context.

6.

The exponentiated Hencky energy (cf.
[
22
,
25
]
)

\displaystyle\mathrm{W}(F)=\frac{\mu}{k}\,e^{k\,\lVert\log(\sqrt{F\,F^{T}})\rVert^{2}}+\frac{\lambda}{2\widehat{k}}\,e^{\widehat{k}\,[{\rm tr}(\log(\sqrt{F\,F^{T}}))]^{2}},\qquad\mu>0,\;2\,\mu+3\,\lambda>0

(4.1)

satisfies the CSP condition throughout but it is not polyconvex (cf.
[
17
]
). It would be interesting to see CSP and polyconvexity being satisfied simultaneously.

7.

Finally, the corotational stability postulate should be put to use in showing some kind of local existence for nonlinear isotropic Cauchy-elasticity. An attempt in this regard will be made in
[
3
]
.

We will address all these issues in an upcoming paper
[
27
]
.

Inner product

For

we let
\langle{a},{b}\rangle_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
denote the scalar product on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with associated vector norm
\lVert a\rVert_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}^{2}=\langle{a},{a}\rangle_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
. We denote by

\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
is given by

\langle{X},{Y}\rangle_{\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}}=\textnormal{tr}{(XY^{T})}
, where the superscript

\lVert X\rVert^{2}=\langle{X},{X}\rangle_{\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}}

\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
in writing the Frobenius tensor norm. The identity tensor on
\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}

{\boldsymbol{\mathbbm{1}}}

\textnormal{tr}{(X)}=\langle{X},{{\boldsymbol{\mathbbm{1}}}}\rangle
.

The strain energy function
\mathrm{W}(F)

We are only concerned with rotationally symmetric functions
\mathrm{W}(F)
(objective and isotropic), i.e.

\mathrm{W}(F)={\mathrm{W}}(Q_{1}^{T}\,F\,Q_{2}),\qquad\forall\,F\in{\rm GL}^{+}(3),\qquad Q_{1},Q_{2}\in{\rm SO}(3).

List of additional definitions and useful identities

•

For two metric spaces

•

We denote the space of minor and major symmetric, positive definite fourth order tensors

by

, i.e.
\mathbb{C}\in\Sym^{++}_{4}(6)
if and only if
\langle\mathbb{C}.D,D\rangle>0
for all

.

•

We define
J=\det{F}
and denote by
\Cof(X)=(\det X)X^{-T}
the cofactor of a matrix in

We define

For all vectors

we have the tensor product
(\xi\otimes\eta)_{ij}=\xi_{i}\,\eta_{j}

S_{1}=\mathrm{D}_{F}\mathrm{W}(F)=\sigma\cdot\Cof F

S_{2}=F^{-1}S_{1}=2\,\mathrm{D}_{C}\widetilde{\mathrm{W}}(C)

Regarding Hilbert-monotonicity, we recall the following properties from Ghiba et al.

Definition A.1
.

[

A tensor function
\Sigma_{f}:{\rm Sym}^{++}(3)\rightarrow\Sym^{++}\,
is called
strictly Hilbert-monotone
if

\displaystyle\langle\Sigma_{f}(U)-\Sigma_{f}(\overline{U}),\,U-\overline{U}\rangle_{\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3}}>0\qquad\forall\,U\neq\overline{U}\in{\rm Sym}^{++}(3)\,.

(A.1)

We refer to this inequality as strict

of the tensor function
\Sigma_{f}

Definition A.2
.

[

A vector function

strictly vector monotone
if

\displaystyle\langle f(\lambda)-f(\overline{\lambda}),\,\lambda-\overline{\lambda}\rangle_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}>0\qquad\forall\lambda\neq\overline{\lambda}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}_{+}.

(A.2)

Definition A.3

A differentiable function
f
between finite-dimensional Hilbert spaces is called
strongly monotone
if

is positive definite everywhere. Thus “stronlgy” implies “strictly”, see e.g. Remark

Theorem A.4
.

A symmetric function

is strictly (strongly) vector-monotone if and only if

Remark A.5

Theorem

A.3
True-Stress-True-Strain monotonicity (TSTS-M)

Definition A.6
.

We define three notions of
True-Stress-True-Strain monotonicity
as follows

\displaystyle\text{TSTS-M:}\qquad

\displaystyle\langle\widehat{\sigma}(\log V_{1})-\widehat{\sigma}(\log V_{2}),\log V_{1}-\log V_{2}\rangle

\displaystyle\geq 0,\qquad\forall\,V_{1},V_{2}\in{\rm Sym}^{++}(3),\ V_{1}\neq V_{2},

(A.4)

\displaystyle\text{TSTS-M${}^{+}$:}\qquad

\displaystyle\langle\widehat{\sigma}(\log V_{1})-\widehat{\sigma}(\log V_{2}),\log V_{1}-\log V_{2}\rangle

\displaystyle>0,\qquad\forall\,V_{1},V_{2}\in{\rm Sym}^{++}(3),\ V_{1}\neq V_{2},

\displaystyle\text{TSTS-M${}^{++}$:}\qquad

\displaystyle\sym\,\mathrm{D}_{\log V}\widehat{\sigma}(\log V)\in\Sym^{++}_{4}(6).

Note, that this is equivalent to monotonicity of
\widehat{\sigma}
in
\log B
since
\log B=2\,\log V
.

Regarding TSTS-M
+
, we have the following properties from
[
25
]
.

Remark A.7
.

Sufficient for TSTS-M
+
is Jog and Patil’s
[
14
]
constitutive requirement that

\displaystyle\text{TSTS-M${}^{++}$:}\qquad\sym\,\mathrm{D}_{\log B}\,\widehat{\sigma}(\log B)\in\Sym^{++}_{4}(6),

(A.5)

Proof.

Let us remark that for all
B_{1},B_{2}\in{\rm Sym}^{++}(3)
and
0\leq t\leq 1
, we have
2\,\log V_{1}=\log B_{1},\,2\,\log V_{2}=\log B_{2}
and
t\,(\log V_{1}-\log V_{2})+\log V_{2}\in{\rm Sym}(3)
, where
V_{1}^{2}=B_{1},\,V_{2}^{2}=B_{2}
. Moreover, we have

\displaystyle\langle\widehat{\sigma}(\log B_{1})

\displaystyle-\widehat{\sigma}(\log B_{2}),\log B_{1}-\log B_{2}\rangle=2\,\langle\widehat{\sigma}(2\,\log V_{1})-\widehat{\sigma}(2\,\log V_{2}),\log V_{1}-\log V_{2}\rangle

\displaystyle=2\,\left\langle\left[\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\rm d}{\rm dt}\,\widehat{\sigma}\bigg(2\,t\,(\log V_{1}-\log V_{2})+2\,\log V_{2}\bigg)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\right],\log V_{1}-\log V_{2}\right\rangle

(A.6)

\displaystyle=4\,\int_{0}^{1}\left\langle\left[\mathrm{D}_{\log V}\,\widehat{\sigma}\bigg(2\,t\,(\log V_{1}-\log V_{2})+2\,\log V_{2}\bigg).\,(\log V_{1}-\log V_{2})\right],\log V_{1}-\log V_{2}\right\rangle\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\,.

\displaystyle=4\,\int_{0}^{1}\left\langle\left[\sym\left(\mathrm{D}_{\log V}\,\widehat{\sigma}\bigg(2\,t\,(\log V_{1}-\log V_{2})+2\,\log V_{2}\bigg)\right).\,(\log V_{1}-\log V_{2})\right],\log V_{1}-\log V_{2}\right\rangle\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\,.

Where the last equation of (
A.6
) is due to the fact that
for any skew symmetric matrix
A\in\mathfrak{so}(3)
, it always holds

\left\langle A.v,v\right\rangle_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}=0

for every
v\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
. Using that the integrand is non-negative, due to the assumption that
\Lambda=\sym\,\mathrm{D}_{\log V}\widehat{\sigma}(\log V)
is positive definite, the TSTS-M
+
condition
follows.
∎

\displaystyle\hskip-8.0ptQ_{\tau,\textrm{hyp}(2)}(\dot{E}_{12},\dot{E}_{23},\dot{E}_{31})=Q_{\textrm{hyp}(2)}=J\,\sum_{i\neq j}\frac{\widehat{\sigma}_{i}-\widehat{\sigma}_{j}}{(\lambda_{i}^{2}-\lambda_{j}^{2})}\bigg(\frac{1}{\lambda_{i}^{2}}+\frac{1}{\lambda_{j}^{2}}\bigg)\,\dot{E}_{ij}^{2}=\sum_{i\neq j}\frac{\widehat{\tau}_{i}-\widehat{\tau}_{j}}{(\lambda_{i}^{2}-\lambda_{j}^{2})}\underbrace{\bigg(\frac{1}{\lambda_{i}^{2}}+\frac{1}{\lambda_{j}^{2}}\bigg)\,\dot{E}_{ij}^{2}}_{>0}.

(A.22)

This proves, that the results for the Cauchy stress
\sigma
also hold true for the Kirchhoff stress
\tau
in the hyperelastic case.

A.4.2
The Cauchy-elastic case

Proceeding with the more general Cauchy-elastic case, we observe that by the construction in Proposition
3.1
, we can once again express all relevant quantities in the quadratic from

\displaystyle Q_{\tau,\textrm{ela}}(\dot{E}):=\langle\dot{S_{2}},\dot{E}\rangle+2\,\textnormal{tr}(C^{-1}\,\dot{E}\,S_{2}\,\dot{E}),

(A.23)

with respect to the eigenvalues and eigenvectors of
E
, leading to the relations (cf. Prop
3.2
)

\displaystyle\big\langle\dot{S}_{2},\dot{E}\big\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j=1}^{3}\frac{\partial\breve{s}_{i}}{\partial e_{j}}\,\dot{E}_{jj}\,\dot{E}_{ii}+\sum_{i\neq j}\frac{\breve{s}_{i}-\breve{s}_{j}}{e_{i}-e_{j}}\dot{E}_{ij}^{2},

(A.24)

\displaystyle\textnormal{tr}\big(C^{-1}\dot{E}\,S_{2}\,\dot{E}\big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{3}\frac{1}{\lambda_{i}^{2}}\,\dot{E}_{ii}^{2}\,\breve{s}_{i}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i\neq j}\bigg(\frac{\breve{s}_{j}}{\lambda_{i}^{2}}+\frac{\breve{s}_{i}}{\lambda_{j}^{2}}\bigg)\dot{E}_{ij}^{2}

and thus yielding the representation

\displaystyle Q_{\tau,\textrm{ela}}(\dot{E})=Q_{\tau,\textrm{ela}(1)}(\dot{E}_{11},\dot{E}_{22},\dot{E}_{33})+Q_{\tau,\textrm{ela}(2)}(\dot{E}_{12},\dot{E}_{23},\dot{E}_{31}),

(A.25)

with

\displaystyle Q_{\tau,\textrm{ela}(1)}=\sum_{i=1}^{3}\left(\frac{\partial\breve{s}_{i}}{\partial e_{i}}+2\,\frac{\breve{s}_{i}}{\lambda_{i}^{2}}\right)\,\dot{E}_{ii}^{2}+\sum_{i\neq j}\frac{\partial\breve{s}_{i}}{\partial e_{j}}\,\dot{E}_{ii}\,\dot{E}_{jj}

(A.26)

and

\displaystyle Q_{\tau,\textrm{ela}(2)}=\sum_{i\neq j}\bigg(\frac{\breve{s}_{i}-\breve{s}_{j}}{e_{i}-e_{j}}+\frac{\breve{s}_{j}}{\lambda_{i}^{2}}+\frac{\breve{s}_{i}}{\lambda_{j}^{2}}\bigg)\,\dot{E}^{2}_{ij}.

(A.27)

Now, we define

\displaystyle\widehat{Q}_{\tau,\textrm{ela}(1)}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{\partial\breve{s}_{1}}{\partial e_{1}}+2\,\frac{\breve{s}_{1}}{\lambda_{1}^{2}}&\frac{\partial\breve{s}_{1}}{\partial e_{2}}&\frac{\partial\breve{s}_{1}}{\partial e_{3}}\\[5.69054pt]
\frac{\partial\breve{s}_{2}}{\partial e_{1}}&\frac{\partial\breve{s}_{2}}{\partial e_{2}}+2\,\frac{\breve{s}_{2}}{\lambda_{2}^{2}}&\frac{\partial\breve{s}_{2}}{\partial e_{3}}\\[5.69054pt]
\frac{\partial\breve{s}_{3}}{\partial e_{1}}&\frac{\partial\breve{s}_{3}}{\partial e_{2}}&\frac{\partial\breve{s}_{3}}{\partial e_{3}}+2\,\frac{\breve{s}_{3}}{\lambda_{3}^{2}}\end{pmatrix},\qquad\text{with}

(A.28)

\displaystyle\sym\,\widehat{Q}_{\tau,\textrm{ela}(1)}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{\partial\breve{s}_{1}}{\partial e_{1}}+2\,\frac{\breve{s}_{1}}{\lambda_{1}^{2}}&\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\partial\breve{s}_{1}}{\partial e_{2}}+\frac{\partial\breve{s}_{2}}{\partial e_{1}}\right)&\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\partial\breve{s}_{1}}{\partial e_{3}}+\frac{\partial\breve{s}_{3}}{\partial e_{1}}\right)\\[5.69054pt]
\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\partial\breve{s}_{2}}{\partial e_{1}}+\frac{\partial\breve{s}_{1}}{\partial e_{2}}\right)&\frac{\partial\breve{s}_{2}}{\partial e_{2}}+2\,\frac{\breve{s}_{2}}{\lambda_{2}^{2}}&\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\partial\breve{s}_{2}}{\partial e_{3}}+\frac{\partial\breve{s}_{3}}{\partial e_{2}}\right)\\[5.69054pt]
\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\partial\breve{s}_{3}}{\partial e_{1}}+\frac{\partial\breve{s}_{1}}{\partial e_{3}}\right)&\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\partial\breve{s}_{3}}{\partial e_{2}}+\frac{\partial\breve{s}_{2}}{\partial e_{3}}\right)&\frac{\partial\breve{s}_{3}}{\partial e_{3}}+2\,\frac{\breve{s}_{3}}{\lambda_{3}^{2}}\end{pmatrix}

and show that

\left\langle\begin{pmatrix}\frac{\dot{E}_{11}}{\lambda_{1}^{2}}\\[2.84526pt]
\frac{\dot{E}_{22}}{\lambda_{2}^{2}}\\[2.84526pt]
\frac{\dot{E}_{33}}{\lambda_{3}^{2}}\end{pmatrix}\!\!,\sym\textrm{D}_{\log\lambda}\widehat{\tau}.\!\!\begin{pmatrix}\frac{\dot{E}_{11}}{\lambda_{1}^{2}}\\[2.84526pt]
\frac{\dot{E}_{22}}{\lambda_{2}^{2}}\\[2.84526pt]
\frac{\dot{E}_{33}}{\lambda_{3}^{2}}\end{pmatrix}\right\rangle=\left\langle\begin{pmatrix}\dot{E}_{11}\\
\dot{E}_{22}\\
\dot{E}_{33}\end{pmatrix}\!\!,\sym\widehat{Q}_{\tau,\textrm{ela}(1)}.\!\!\begin{pmatrix}\dot{E}_{11}\\
\dot{E}_{22}\\
\dot{E}_{33}\end{pmatrix}\right\rangle_{\!\!\!\mathbb{R}^{3}}=\left\langle\begin{pmatrix}\dot{E}_{11}\\
\dot{E}_{22}\\
\dot{E}_{33}\end{pmatrix}\!\!,\widehat{Q}_{\tau,\textrm{ela}(1)}.\!\!\begin{pmatrix}\dot{E}_{11}\\
\dot{E}_{22}\\
\dot{E}_{33}\end{pmatrix}\right\rangle_{\!\!\!\mathbb{R}^{3}},

(A.29)

where the last equality holds because for any skew symmetric matrix
A\in\mathfrak{so}(3)
, we have
\langle A.v,v\rangle_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}=0
for every
v\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
.

To show (
A.29
), recall the identities (
3.39
), given by

\frac{\partial\widehat{s}_{i}(\log\lambda)}{\partial(\log\lambda_{j})}=\frac{\partial\breve{s}_{i}}{\partial e_{j}}\big(e(\lambda)\big)\;\lambda_{j}^{2}\qquad\qquad\forall i,j\in\{1,2,3\},

(A.30)

as well as (
3.32
), which was given by

J\,\widehat{\sigma}_{i}(\log\lambda)=\lambda_{i}^{2}\,\widehat{s}_{i}(\log\lambda)\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad\widehat{\sigma}_{i}(\log\lambda)=\frac{\lambda_{i}^{2}}{J}\widehat{s}_{i}(\log\lambda)\qquad\forall i\in\{1,2,3\}.

(A.31)

Now, when deriving the components of
\sym\,\mathrm{D}_{\log\lambda}\widehat{\tau}(\log\lambda)
, we obtain for the derivatives
\frac{\partial\widehat{\tau}_{i}}{\partial\log\lambda_{i}}
with

\widehat{\tau}(\log\lambda)=\tau(\lambda)=J\,\sigma(\lambda)=J\,\widehat{\sigma}(\log\lambda)
and
\widehat{s}_{i}(\log\lambda)=\breve{s}_{i}(e(\lambda))

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\widehat{\tau}_{i}}{\partial\log\lambda_{i}}=\frac{\partial}{\partial\log\lambda_{i}}\left(\mathrm{e}^{2\,\log\lambda_{i}}\cdot\widehat{s}_{i}(\log\lambda)\right)=2\,\lambda_{i}^{2}\,\widehat{s}_{i}+\lambda_{i}^{2}\,\frac{\partial\widehat{s}_{i}}{\partial\log\lambda_{i}}=2\,\lambda_{i}^{2}\,\breve{s}_{i}+\lambda_{i}^{4}\,\frac{\partial\breve{s}_{i}}{\partial e_{i}}

(A.32)

and similarly for
\frac{\partial\widehat{\tau}_{i}}{\partial\log\lambda_{j}}

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\widehat{\tau}_{i}}{\partial\log\lambda_{j}}=\lambda_{i}^{2}\,\frac{\partial\widehat{s}_{i}(\log\lambda)}{\partial\log\lambda_{j}}=\lambda_{i}^{2}\,\lambda_{j}^{2}\,\frac{\partial\breve{s}_{i}}{\partial e_{j}}.

(A.33)

While the diagonal entries of
\sym\,\mathrm{D}_{\log\lambda}\widehat{\tau}(\log\lambda)
are simply given by (
A.32
), we obtain for the off-diagonal elements

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\partial\widehat{\tau}_{i}}{\partial\log\lambda_{j}}+\frac{\partial\widehat{\tau}_{j}}{\partial\log\lambda_{i}}\right)=\frac{1}{2}\cdot\lambda_{i}^{2}\,\lambda_{j}^{2}\cdot\left(\frac{\partial\breve{s}_{i}}{\partial e_{j}}+\frac{\partial\breve{s}_{j}}{\partial e_{i}}\right).

(A.34)

Thus, by combining (
A.32
) with (
A.34
), we have proven that (
A.29
) holds.

Finally, we observe that, as for the hyperelastic case, we have
Q_{\tau,\textrm{ela}(2)}=Q_{\textrm{ela}(2)}
and hence with
\widehat{\tau}=J\,\widehat{\sigma}
we conclude for the calculation in (
3.43
)

\displaystyle Q_{\tau,\textrm{ela}(2)}(\dot{E}_{12},\dot{E}_{23},\dot{E}_{31})=Q_{\textrm{ela}(2)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\neq j}\bigg(\frac{\breve{s}_{i}-\breve{s}_{j}}{e_{i}-e_{j}}+\frac{\breve{s}_{j}}{\lambda_{i}^{2}}+\frac{\breve{s}_{i}}{\lambda_{j}^{2}}\bigg)\dot{E}_{ij}^{2}=J\sum_{i\neq j}\frac{\widehat{\sigma}_{i}-\widehat{\sigma}_{j}}{(\lambda_{i}^{2}-\lambda_{j}^{2})}\bigg(\frac{1}{\lambda_{i}^{2}}+\frac{1}{\lambda_{j}^{2}}\bigg)\,\dot{E}_{ij}^{2}

(A.35)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\neq j}\frac{\widehat{\tau}_{i}-\widehat{\tau}_{j}}{(\lambda_{i}^{2}-\lambda_{j}^{2})}\underbrace{\bigg(\frac{1}{\lambda_{i}^{2}}+\frac{1}{\lambda_{j}^{2}}\bigg)\,\dot{E}_{ij}^{2}}_{>0},

concluding the analysis for the Kirchhoff stress
\tau
with the same results as those for the Cauchy stress
\sigma
.

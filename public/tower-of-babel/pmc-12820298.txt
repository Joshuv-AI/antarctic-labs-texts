In-silico identification, characterization and expression analysis of salt overly sensitive-2 (SOS2) gene family in Gossypium hirsutum L. against abiotic stresses
Authors: Laraib Iqra, Muhammad Naveed Shahid, Qurban Ali
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Gossypium hirsutum backbone of economy of many countries; is primarily grown for production of fiber. This is the first genome-wide analysis of GhSOS2 gene family in G. hirsutum. Primary function of SOS2 is ion homeostasis and plays functional role in osmotic stress as well as drought, effecting the development and growth of plant. During this study 21 GhSOS2 proteins were identified in G. hirsutum via BLASTp against 4 AtSOS2 protein sequences from Arabidopsis thaliana as query. Two distinct domains (P-kinase and NAF domain) were conserved in all identified GhSOS2 proteins. Gene architecture and phylogenetic study depicts the evolutionary conservation and divergence among gene paralogs and their equal distribution among both genome as a result of synteny and duplication events. A total of 10 Conserved motif are detected in all 21 GhSOS2 genes. Promotor analysis confirms the existence of hormones responsive elements GRE, ABARE, STRE, and other regulatory factors in GhSOS2 elucidating their potential contribution against abiotic stresses. Furthermore, physiochemical properties and PPI also highlights the role of GhSOS2 against drought and salinity. Data obtained by RNA-seq analysis revealed a gene subset with significant expression based on FPKM metrics at different intervals. Upon qPCR validation; GhSOS2-8, GhSOS2-13, and GhSOS2-21 were markedly up-regulated under both salt and drought stress at immediate and late hours. This study lays groundwork for functional validation of GhSOS2 in genetic modification of abiotic resistant G. hirsutum cultivars.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-025-32353-8.

Introduction

Gossypium hirsutum also known as upland cotton is not only vital to Pakistan’s economy but backbone of economy of many other countries1,2. It is primarily grown for production of fiber used in textile industry but also source of income of large number of farmers3. In Pakistan cotton is known as white gold and is foremost cash crop4,5. But unfortunately, in last five to six years its productivity is badly affected due to continuous environmental commotions6. Persistent water deficiency and accumulation of salt due to poor agricultural practices are the leading cause of decline in cotton yield7,8. Not only germination rate is primarily affected but all yield parameters are badly influenced under abiotic stress in G. hirsutum9. So, to deploy these stressors; G. hirsutum need to modify its genetic makeup to dealt with these challenges. One of the gene family plays an important role in maintaining ion balance is salt overly sensitive 2 (SOS2)10,11. SOS2 is a kinase protein also known as CBL- interacting protein kinase (CIPK)12,13. This protein contributes in SOS pathway- a tolerance mechanism in plants. One of the most common members of CBL- interacting protein kinase is CIPK24 containing two terminal domains; N-terminus kinase domain that phosphorylates the SOS1 and a C-terminus regulatory domain NAF that interacts with SOS314,15. During abiotic stress SOS3 gene can sense Ca+ spikes in plant cell and binds itself to N-terminus of SOS2 to activate it16–20. SOS2 works as switch operator to start the ion homeostasis mechanism in Gossypium hirsutum21. This complex of SOS2-SOS3 activates the SOS1 that is Na+/H+ antiporter and unloads Na+ ion from xylem22,23. So, the primary function of SOS2 is ion homeostasis and secondly it is part of functional cascade with other members of salt overly family contributing to growth and development of plants24–28. The SOS2 gene family has widely been studied in multiple plants including more than 26 members of SOS2 has been identified in Arabidopsis thaliana29, more than 33 gene members in Oryza sativa30–32, 43 in Zea mays33 and approximately 62 have been reported in G. barbadense34,35. But a comprehensive genome wide identification and in-silico characterization have not been determined in G. hirsutum so far. The present study is thus undertaken with the objective of in-silico identification and characterization of SOS2 in G. hirsutum to understand its functional validation of GhSOS2 genes against abiotic stresses.

Materials and methods

Genome-wide identification of SOS2 genes in G.hirsutum

Genome wide identification of GhSOS2 genes was conducted from Gossypium hirsutum TM-1 genome provided by CAAS release in 2020. Four query sequences (AtSOS2/AT5G35410.1, AtCIPK23/AT1G30270.1, AtCIPK11/AT2G30360, AtCIPK14/AT5G01820.1) of SOS2 was retrieved from Arabidopsis TIAR36 (TAIR13 release; https://www.arabidopsis.org/). BLASTp was performed via phytozome v.13 against this query with E-value 1e-5. Based on minimum E-values and highest similarity coverage; a total of 21 proteins was shortlisted in G. hirsutum. Identified 21 genes were subjected to NCBI CDD batch search (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/Structure/bwrpsb/bwrpsb.cgi to confirm the conserved N-terminus and C-terminus domains presence was verified by SMART (https://smart.embl.de/) and HMMER (http://hmmer.org/)37. The identified proteins were further confirmed for the presence of required conserved domain helix by Pfam database (https://pfam.xfam.org/)38.

Gene structure and conserved motif analysis

To study conserved motifs among 21 identified GhSOS2 genes “MEME suit wrapper” was used via Tbtools39,40. FASTA protein sequences were used as input data in Tbtools and number of motifs were selected 10 with other standard parameters to generate graphical view. Gene structure was analyzed by using Gossypium hirsutum GFF annotation file. Input gene IDs were given with start end position and conserved domain name and a graphical representation of 21 genes was taken by using tbTools41 v.19 (TBtools v1.09876 on GitHub).

Phylogenetic tree and selection pressure analysis

To infer the relationship of GhSOS2 with Arabidopsis, Sorghum bicolor, Malus domestica, solanum lycopersicum and other plants Phylogeny generated by ml tree method keeping Tajima’s relative rate test (p < 0.05) by help of MEGA42. Ka/ks values were determined by MEGA 11 and gene duplication events were noted in a table to determine the evolutionary selection among paralogs of GhSOS2.

Chromosomal distribution of GhSOS2 genes

Gossypium hirsutum has two set of genomes A1-13 and D1-13 and 26 chromosomes. Identified 21 GhSOS2 genes were distributed among different chromosomes. MapChart tool was used to locate the gene locus among chromosomes by giving chromosomal length as input manually and results are saved as graphics43. Chromosomes that do not contain GhSOS2 genes were discarded manually.

Gene linkage and circos plot visualization

To investigate the linked genes on chromosomes bioinformatic tool TbTools v.19 have been used to show high throughput visualization of linkage between 21 GhSOS2 genes. For this linked gene paralogs obtained from ka/ks values were put as input data and entire chromosomes information was put as output. The Circos Plot is modern way to represent gene linkage among GhSOS2 among both the sets A and D, this was also generated via Tbtools v.1944,45.

Protein in-silico physiochemical properties and Sub-cellular localization of GhSOS2

Multiple physicochemical properties of GhSOS2 including molecular weight(kDa), CDS length in basepairs, isoelectric point (pI) and GAVY was accessed by phytozome blast gene report and Expasy software (http://web.expasy.org/)46. A heatmap was created via Tbtools for subcellular localization of 21 GhSOS2 genes. Input data was generated by putting FASTA sequence in different online software such as CELLO v2.5/v2.5+ (http://cello.life.nctu.edu.tw/) and WOLF PSORT (https://wolfpsort.hgc.jp/) to determine the distribution of among different plant cells47,48.

Protein-protein interaction

PPI among 21 GhSOS2 proteins were determined by popular bioinformatic online software STRING (https://string-db.org/)49.

Promotor sequence

Promotor sequence was extracted by using GFF annotation file of G. hirsutum 2000 bp upstream. PLANTCARE database (https://bioinformatics.psb.ugent.be/webtools/plantcare/html/) was used to access promotor region elements50. For the specific 21 GhSOS2 genes visual graphics was determined by keeping the stress related cis-regulatory elements distributed among 21 promotor regions using TbTools.

GhSOS2 gene expression data retrieval

Publicly available reference gene expression data for all 21 GhSOS2 genes were extracted from the NCBI SRA data (Project ID: PRJNA490626) for two abiotic conditions i.e. drought and salt stress under different durations control 0 h, 3 h, 6; salt 3 h, salt 6 h salt and 3 h drought, 6 h drought, 12 h drought, 24 h drought, respectively and visualized graphically via heatmap. Before that; FPKM metrics were normalized via log2 fold log2(FPKM + 1). Highly expressed genes were selected as candidate genes for further experimental validation via Realtime expression analysis (qPCR) under specific abiotic stresses.

Plant material and RNA extraction with cDNA synthesis

Gene expression of selected GhSOS2 genes was analyzed using the SYBR Green ReadyMix – Fast SYBR Green qPCR system51. The amplification protocol was as follows: initial denaturation at 95 °C for 3 min, followed by 40 cycles of 95 °C denaturation, annealing at 55 °C for 30 s, and extension. Gene expression levels were calculated by the 2−ΔΔCT method52, with the ubiquitin (GhUBQ) gene serving as housekeeping gene53. Primer sequences for housekeeping and candidate genes are given in supplementary material (Supplementary S3). Three biological replicates harvested at different time points were used for each reaction, along with three technical replicates per sample. Statistical significance of expression differences among treatments and time points was assessed with two-way analysis of variance (ANOVA). Tukey’s post-hoc test (p < 0.05) was performed following ANOVA. Results were presented as mean ± standard error in bar graphs using R software.

Results

Gene architecture motif analysis

The domain architecture analysis of 21 GhSOS2 proteins in Gossypium hirsutum indicated the conserved structural organization which can be characterized by presence of Pkinase and NAF domain in all members of species. The Pkinase domain is present at N-terminal of all proteins. This arrangement shows the classical gene architecture in which exons and UTRs are present at intervals. Figure 1 clearly shows the presence of 10 motifs altogether and Motif 1 and 2 are universally present in all 21 GhSOS2 genes. These motifs contain kinase domain.

Phylogenetic tree

To infer evolutionary relationship between identified 21 GhSOS2 proteins with other plants species Maximum likelihood phylogenetic tree was constructed by JTT-model with maximum Log = −13556.06 and it showed three distinct clades54. CladeI & III comprises of GhSOS2 proteins from G. hirsutum itself and SOS2 proteins of Arabidopsis thaliana showing their close evolutionary proximity. CladeII comprises SOS2 member of G. hirsutum alongside with serine threonine members of itself and other reference plant species including Malus domestica, Sorgham bicolor, Solanum lycopersicum, Brassica rapa, and Helianthus annus depicting evolutionary conservation for kinase proteins among monocots and dicots. Figure 2 shows a circular phylogenetic tree which illustrates the evolutionary relationships among serine/threonine protein kinase proteins and SOS2 proteins from various plants.

Chromosomal distribution of identified 21 gene among both genomes set (A&D) showed recent duplication events in G. hirsutum (Fig. 3ab). Ka/Ks values determined by using MEGA 11 showed 56 gene paralogs in GhSOS2 and 96% of the values are less than 1 indicating that all gone through purifying selection. In purifying selection, the functionality of the genes is conserved and no harmful mutation occurred over years. Only two of the pairs showed positive selection depicting the functional divergence. Gene duplication and linkage mapping

All 21 identifies GhSOS2 genes were distributed on both genome sets A and D of Gossypium hirsutum. This linkage is elucidating the multiple syntenic relationship among inter and even intra-chromosomal relationships. This suggests that gene duplication played a key role in GhSOS2 gene expansion. Linkage is being represented by blue line among the chromosomes of both sets such as gene GhSOS2-1 present on chromosomes A02 are linked with other genes present on chromosome D06, D10 and A12 (Fig. 4a). Similarly, GhSOS2-20 shows wide linkage pairs with other genes. This linkage among different gene pairs is evidence of segmental duplication. In modern way Circos plot was constructed to represent linkage; Both genome sets have been colored different to visualize the linkage clearly by intersecting lines Plot showed linkage between GhSOS2-4 present on Chromosomes A05 and GhSOS2-7 present on chromosome A11 as duplicated paralogs (Fig. 4b). Cotton is polyploid crop so gene duplication events in the results are confirming the allotetraploid feature of hirsutum having two genome sets.

Physicochemical properties and subcellular localization predictions

Variation in protein size was observed in GhSOS2 proteins ranged from 34 kDa to 65 kDa. Isoelectric points (pI) also varies based on proteins interactions and compartmentalization between acidic (5.2) to basic (9.1) values (Table 2). As far as instability index is concerned most proteins showed stability under physiological conditions. Most of the GhSOS2 proteins are hydrophilic as GRAVY results are negative values. Figure 5 showed sub-cellular localization of GhSOS2 proteins. Most of the GhSOS2 proteins are localized in cytoplasm and nucleus. Few are localized in cell membrane.

A wide range of abiotic stress and hormone responsive elements were identified as cis-regulatory elements under 2 kb upstream promotor region of GhSOS2 genes (Fig. 7). In-silico expression profiling and candidate gene selection

Based on publicly available RNA-seq data; hierarchical clustering illustrated linkage to grouped genes showing similar response to stress at same intervals in heatmap shown in Fig. 8. Most GhSOS2 genes exhibited less expressions under controlled condition but a distinct group of genes showed strong induction under salt and drought response at 1 h to 12 h treatment including GhSOS2-8, GhSOS2-13, GhSOS2-15 and GhSOS2-21 and upregulation was clearly noticed under both abiotic stresses. Meanwhile GhSOS2-4, GhSOS2-10, GhSOS2-11 and GhSOS2-19 showed moderate upregulation. Certain genes showed weak expression under both abiotic condition in early and late response including GhSOS2-1, GhSOS2-2 and GhSOS2-6. Hierarchical clustering clearly highlighted candidate genes to be considered for qPCR expression analysis were GhSOS3-8, GhSOS2-13 and GhSOS2-21.

Discussion

The present study is comprehensive insight of genome wide identification and in-silico characterization of GhSOS2 gene family in G. hirsutum and its potential role against abiotic stresses. Total of 21 GhSOS2 genes were identified all containing P-kinase and NAF domains. It functions in calcium-binding and interact with other CBL proteins13. This domain is characteristic feature of SOS2/CIPK gene family in G. hirsutum, reinforcing its role in ion homeostasis and signal transduction pathways55. The uniformity of these two domains in GhSOS2 gene family indicates their preserved functional role in stress signaling pathways and particularly in salt overly sensitive (SOS) signaling cascade56. These findings correlate with the evolutionary conservation and potential regulatory importance of the GhSOS2 gene family in cotton’s abiotic stress responses.

The clustering patterns highlights broader evolutionary divergence among different plant lineages and closer within species-specific gene families. It also reveals gene duplication events along with functional conservation or diversification within SOS2 gene family and related kinases57–60.

Moreover, segmental duplication events were clearly observed in selection pressure analysis and synteny mapping. Gene duplication events is characteristic feature of polyploid cotton genomes.

Also, this synteny relationship emphasis on evolutionary retention of functionality of the genes61,62. Minor changes are possible in gene sub functions after duplication event highlights redundancy. Circos plot is modern technique to intercept evolutionary history of gene family63. Based on the Circos plot for 21 GhSOS2 gene family members it was obvious that segmental duplication played a crucial role in expansion of this gene family64–67. This type of gene mapping is valuable for further study of genetics, breeding and functional genomics, because researchers can explore their roles in cotton plant by associating specific genes with traits or biological processes68. These findings align with previous reported data indicating the role of SOS2 in signal transduction pathways in many crops69. Subcellular localization analysis depicts that most of the GhSOS2 are cytoplasmic and nuclear based, indicating their potential role in intracellular response to specific signal ion and post translational level modification. Presence of most of the proteins in cytoplasm indicating their role as Ca+ sensors as they actively sense the elevation in Ca ion in cell under abiotic stress. These genes start phosphorylation when they sense Ca+ signals70. Insilico physiochemical properties indicated GhSOS2 holds typical kinase proteins like molecular weight and pI values suitable for cellular signaling environment. PPI studies revealed their interaction with SOS pathway core gene members directly involved in unloading of sodium ions from xylem cell and ion transport regulations. Presence of ARBE elements and MYB binding sites and MYC-binding sites that are directly involved in ABA-Signaling pathways and secondary metabolism in G. hirsutum under abiotic stresses. DRE is also notable cis-regulatory element effective against drought in G. hirsutum. LTRE is low temperature responsive element that regulate gene expression under cold stress. Upon expression profiling of selected candidate genes GhSOS2-8, GhSOS2-13 & GhSOS2-21 retrieved from publicly available RNA-seq data strong expression was noticed in early hours under heavy salt stress of 400mM NaCl and 20% PEG. Overall, these findings elucidated GhSOS2 genes are potential target for structural and functional validation in order to develop resilient upland cotton via genomics-assisted breeding.

Conclusions

In conclusion, identification and in-silico characterization of 21 GhSOS2 and expression analysis of selective three genes provides a significant genomic data deciphering significant role in abiotic stresses in Gossypium hirsutum. This gene family emerged as promising candidate genes for functional expression analysis and to validate genetic engineering techniques used to build a resilient G. hirsutum under abiotic stresses. Expression profiling of GhSOS2-8, GhSOS2-13, and GhSOS2-21 showed marked expression peaks indicated their pivotal roles under early abiotic stresses. Above study provides comprehensive structural, functional, evolutionary and expressional insights of GhSOS2 genes served as genomic resource for genome-editing approaches in order to contribute in sustainable upland cotton production under increasingly challenging environmental conditions. The use of CRISPR genome editing may also be helpful to improve the salt stress tolerance in cotton plant.

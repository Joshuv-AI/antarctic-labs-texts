Bibliometric analysis of stroke centers
Authors: Chunxiang Niu, Yi Zhu, QiuYan Yu, Enguang Yu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Objective:To conduct a bibliometric analysis of stroke center-related research fields, both domestically and internationally, using CiteSpace, and to explore the current research status, hot topics, and development trends.Methods:Using the China National Knowledge Infrastructure (CNKI), Wanfang, VIP, PubMed, and the Web of Science Core Collection (WOSCC) as data sources, we performed visual analysis of annual publication volume, authors/institutions, and high-frequency keywords through CiteSpace.Results:A total of 3730 literature pieces were retrieved, including 3044 English articles and 686 Chinese articles. The overall research enthusiasm for stroke centers presents an upward trend. Current research hotspots mainly focus on Intravenous Thrombolysis (IVT), endovascular therapy, emergency green channels, and prognosis. The developmental frontiers chiefly involve machine learning (ML) and information system construction.Conclusion:This study conducted cluster analysis on literature concerning stroke centers using CiteSpace software. It aims to explore hot topics and evolutionary trends within the field of stroke center research, broaden relevant research perspectives, and provide strategic references for clinical nursing practice and scholarly research.

1. Introduction

Stroke centers are patient-centered clinical hubs designed to deliver rapid, efficient, and systematic medical care for acute stroke patients. Their establishment relies on close collaboration within regional medical alliances, enabling 2-way interaction, prioritizing primary care, differentiating between acute and chronic conditions, and integrating interdisciplinary approaches.[1] These centers have significantly reduced door-to-needle time (DNT) for thrombolysis initiation,[2] substantially lowered mortality and disability rates in acute cases, improved clinical outcomes, and decreased treatment costs – thereby enhancing social benefits.[3] However, Chinese stroke centers exhibit notable heterogeneity in resource allocation, operational models, management mechanisms, and practical effectiveness, which has hindered overall improvement in medical care quality. Therefore, strengthening research efforts related to stroke centers becomes particularly crucial. CiteSpace[4] optimizes the literature search process for key papers in specific academic fields, allowing researchers to visually identify significant features through a visual network. This study systematically analyzes literature on stroke centers using CiteSpace software, aiming to reveal development trends in this field and provide theoretical references for scientific research and clinical practice in stroke center management.

2. Data sources and methods

2.1. Data sources

The Chinese literature retrieval databases of this study included China National Knowledge Infrastructure (CNKI), Wanfang Data Knowledge Service Platform, and VIP Chinese Science and Technology Journal Database. The retrieval time frame for all databases spanned from the establishment of each database to February 19, 2025. The detailed retrieval strategies, irrelevant literature exclusion procedures, and preliminary screening workflow for each database are illustrated in Figure 1. The specific retrieval and screening outcomes are presented as follows: Subject retrieval was adopted for CNKI with the search formula: (“Stroke Center”) OR (“Construction of Stroke Center”). A total of 1513 literatures were initially retrieved. After excluding 142 dissertations, 18 monographs, 73 conference papers and 389 newspaper articles, 891 valid literatures were preliminarily retained. The identical subject search formula was applied to Wanfang Database, yielding 1069 initial literatures. After removing 255 dissertations and 70 conference papers, 744 valid literatures remained. Title and keywords were set as retrieval fields for VIP Database using consistent search terms, which retrieved 284 pieces of literature in the first round. Manual exclusion of 85 irrelevant articles left 199 valid records. After merging literature from the 3 Chinese databases, duplicate removal via NoteExpress software combined with secondary manual screening was performed. Finally, 686 valid Chinese pieces of literature were included for subsequent analysis.

The English literature retrieval source was the Web of Science Core Collection (WOSCC), with the same time range from database inception to February 19, 2025. Titles were designated as the retrieval field, and the search formula was (“Stroke center”) OR (“Stroke center construction”), with language restricted to English. 2.2. Methodology

Using CiteSpace 6.2.R4 software, we imported literature data into the platform. CiteSpace parameter settings: Time span for Chinese literature: 1996 to 2025; for English literature: 1975 to 2025. The time slice (year per slice) was set to “1.” Term sources were selected as “Title,” “Abstract,” “Author,” “keywords,” and “keywords plus.” Node type was configured as “keyword.” The g-index was set to 25, TopN value to 50, and Top-N% to 10. Graphs were constructed using the Pathfinder and Pruning Sliced Networks pruning methods.

2.3. Robustness and sensitivity analysis of CiteSpace parameters

To verify the reliability of parameter configurations in CiteSpace and avoid subjective bias introduced by manually set thresholds, this study conducted sensitivity analysis and robustness tests separately. Fixed baseline settings, including retrieved datasets, time span, node types, and pruning algorithms, were maintained, while only core adjustable parameters were validated in gradient groups. For sensitivity analysis, the visualization threshold TopN was treated as the variable parameter. Taking the formal parameter TopN = 50 adopted in this study as the benchmark, 2 additional gradient groups (TopN = 30 and TopN = 80) were set for comparative testing. Robustness inspection was implemented via minor fine-tuning of the g-index threshold.

Comparisons across multiple parameter groups demonstrated that knowledge maps generated under distinct parameter combinations exhibited highly consistent overall topological structures, core clustering characteristics, distributions of high-frequency research hotspots, and temporal evolutionary trends. Only trivial discrepancies existed for a small number of low-frequency marginal nodes.

The results indicated that the analytical outputs of this study were insensitive to minor adjustments of the TopN threshold, and slight fluctuations of the g-index parameter did not alter the core research conclusions. It was thus confirmed that the CiteSpace parameter settings in the present work were reasonable, and the derived findings possessed satisfactory robustness and reliability.

3. Results

3.1. Number of articles related to stroke centers

A total of 686 Chinese papers and 3044 English papers were finally included in this study. The publication trends of stroke center research at home and abroad follow a four-stage pattern: initial low stagnation, slow growth, an earlier sharp surge in foreign publications, and subsequent divergent development between Chinese and international research. Overall research enthusiasm worldwide keeps rising. International research started much earlier and yielded a far larger total number of publications than domestic research.

The period from 1976 to 2005 represents the embryonic stagnation stage. Annual publication output remained nearly zero both domestically and internationally, with only scattered exploratory studies and no systematic research framework formed. From 2006 to 2015, the field entered the preliminary development stage. The number of foreign publications grew steadily with periodic fluctuations, while domestic output increased slowly. Stroke center construction frameworks were gradually introduced in China, marking the official launch of relevant domestic academic research. The rapid growth stage spanned 2016 to 2020. Foreign publications saw a steep rise with rapidly expanding volume, and domestic output accelerated synchronously. Large-scale construction of stroke centers across China drove a continuous increase in relevant research outputs. From 2021 to 2024, the research field matured with evident divergence. Foreign publications stabilized at a high level of over 250 articles per year with minor fluctuations, reaching an all-time peak in 2023. Domestic publications peaked and then declined slightly. International research has gradually reached saturation, while China’s stroke care delivery system has been largely established, leading to a gentle cooling of domestic research enthusiasm (Fig. 2).

3.3.3. Burst analysis of keywords

Exploring the burst time span and burst strength of keywords can clarify the iterative context of research themes in a field and accurately identify current frontiers and future developmental trends. Relying on the burst detection function of CiteSpace, this study performed burst analysis on keywords from both Chinese and English literature to sort out the evolutionary rules of research hotspots across different stages in the stroke center field (see Figs. 7 and 8).

Burst keywords of stroke‑center‑related research in China.

Burst keywords of international stroke center research.

The burst map of English keywords revealed that the term recommendations burst from 2009 to 2015 with a burst strength of 14, the highest among all English keywords. In the same period, establishment, thrombolytic therapy, and tissue plasminogen activator successively exhibited burst status. International research during this phase centered on formulating and demonstrating stroke center construction plans, thrombolytic regimens, and clinical practice guidelines.

From 2015 to 2020, acute stroke care reached a burst strength of 9.68, accompanied by the concentrated burst of endovascular stroke treatment, endovascular thrombectomy, and large vessel occlusion. This indicated that global research hotspots fully shifted to standardized management of acute stroke, endovascular intervention, and clinical strategies for large vessel occlusion, alongside supporting studies on medical quality improvement and stroke rehabilitation systems. Machine learning (ML) began to burst in 2022 and lasted until 2025, while the burst cycle of validity also extended to 2025. These 2 terms represent the latest cutting-edge burst keywords. It demonstrates that contemporary international research leverages ML to empower stroke diagnosis, disease assessment, and therapeutic efficacy verification, making artificial intelligence the newest research frontier in stroke center studies.

In the burst map of Chinese keywords, “stroke unit” was the keyword with the highest early burst strength (strength = 4.65, 2003–2013), corresponding to the initial construction stage of domestic stroke center frameworks. A large number of studies in this period focused on cerebral ischemia, hemiplegia, standardized stroke guidelines, acupuncture therapy, thrombolysis, clinical pathways, and management models, aiming to establish localized operational frameworks and basic treatment protocols for stroke units.

From 2015 to 2019, keywords including “safety” and “emergency care” burst intensively, as domestic research turned to in-hospital treatment safety control and optimization of emergency workflows. The period 2019 to 2022 witnessed a boom in clinical technology translation. IVT achieved a peak burst strength of 5.81, with concurrent surges in MT, prehospital emergency care, clinical outcomes, and prognosis. This reflected the continuous improvement of domestic emergency green channel systems and the wide popularization of vascular recanalization therapies, shifting research focus to emergency treatment timeliness and short-term patient prognostic evaluation. The burst cycle of “emergency green channel” extended from 2019 to 2025, while “motor function” emerged as a new burst keyword in 2023 (strength = 2.65) and persisted through 2025, marking the latest domestic research frontier. After emergency systems and vascular recanalization therapies reached relative maturity, domestic research has gradually expanded to late-stage stroke rehabilitation, motor function recovery, and long-term quality of survival management, which will serve as core research trends at present and in the near future.

4. Discussion

4.1. Temporal publication trends and author collaboration network analysis

With the rapid disciplinary development of stroke centers, the annual publication output of relevant literature worldwide generally shows an upward trend. International research started earlier than domestic research. Since 2016, the annual number of published papers, both domestically and overseas, has risen remarkably, which may be attributed to the nationwide promotion of stroke center construction launched in 2016.[5] International publications hit a minor peak in 2020, while domestic publications reached a small peak in 2021. Such fluctuation may be linked to the COVID-19 pandemic, which posed severe challenges to global healthcare systems. By 2019, the number of stroke patients worldwide exceeded 100 million, with 12 million new cases annually.[6] In 2020, stroke hospital admissions in the United States dropped by 22.3% compared with 2019, accompanied by a 13% decline in thrombolysis rates.[7] A study conducted in Catalonia further indicated that patients with concomitant COVID-19 and ischemic stroke presented more severe neurological deficits on admission, lower rates of favorable functional outcomes, and higher mortality.[8]

As reflected by the author collaboration network maps (Figs. 3 and 4), the domestic collaboration network for stroke center research has prominent limitations. The overall network scale remains limited, with sparse links and insufficient communication among independent research teams, lacking large-scale cross-team collaborative research. Accordingly, establishing an integrated collaborative framework covering different regions, medical institutions, and disciplines is a critical pathway to advance further research in the field of stroke centers.

4.2. Research hotspots and trend analysis of stroke center-related literature

4.2.1. Improving the rate of reperfusion therapy in AIS patients is the essence and core of stroke center construction

Quantitative analysis of keyword visualization reveals that vascular reperfusion intervention constitutes the unified research backbone for global stroke center research. Nevertheless, obvious divergences exist in research layouts, developmental phases, and research priorities between China and overseas scholars, and the temporal evolution of research hotspots also reflects periodic transformations in diagnostic and therapeutic technologies as well as disciplinary construction within this field.

The divergence in research priorities between domestic and international studies fundamentally stems from disparities in the maturity of stroke healthcare systems and the allocation of regional medical resources worldwide. Stroke centers were established much earlier abroad. Supported by mature tiered transfer protocols, long-term rehabilitation systems, and standardized quality control frameworks, relevant overseas research has moved beyond the scope of in-hospital vascular recanalization techniques. Current international studies focus on full-cycle standardized management formulated based on evidence-based guidelines, alongside the construction of long-term rehabilitation systems and evaluation of long-term mortality outcomes after interventional therapy. By contrast, the development of stroke centers in China remains in the stage of improving localized supporting systems. Restricted by imbalanced primary medical resources and imperfect emergency transfer workflows, domestic research predominantly concentrates on practical issues including the safety of IVT implementation, the optimization of emergency green channels, and the popularization of stroke units at primary hospitals, forming a research track distinct from international counterparts.

In accordance with the evolutionary trajectory of field hotspots, global research on stroke reperfusion follows a progressive path: from the standardization of thrombolytic regimens to technological innovation for large vessel occlusion intervention, and then to intelligent precision diagnosis and treatment. The progress of domestic research lags behind the global trend. After the widespread adoption of vascular recanalization therapies, domestic researchers gradually shifted their research focus from the timeliness of emergency treatment in the acute phase to long-term motor function rehabilitation after stroke. Such chronological gaps indicate that although 2 core reperfusion modalities (IVT and MT) have been widely applied in domestic clinical practice, research gaps still persist in standardized full-process management and refined interventions for long-term patient prognosis.

From a clinical perspective, IVT and MT remain the mainstream reperfusion therapies for AIS, yet both interventions have inherent clinical limitations.[9] IVT is confined by narrow therapeutic time windows, and a consistent balance between thrombolytic efficacy and hemorrhagic transformation risks is difficult to achieve.[10] Although MT has been recommended as a standard regimen by clinical guidelines for large vessel occlusion, a considerable number of patients fail to obtain effective reperfusion owing to distal embolization, microvascular no-reflow, and postoperative vascular reocclusion, which substantially increases patients’ long-term disability risks.[11]

Accumulated basic and clinical studies have verified that elevating thrombolysis efficiency and alleviating microcirculatory perfusion injury are pivotal solutions for insufficient reperfusion. Distal intra-arterial rtPA administration after vascular recanalization, long-acting thrombolytic agents, and combined thrombolysis plus antithrombotic regimens are proven effective in mitigating microvascular no-reflow and optimizing patient clinical outcomes.[12] Combined with the cutting-edge trends identified via bibliometric analysis, artificial intelligence-assisted assessment and poststroke motor function rehabilitation will serve as vital research directions linking acute reperfusion management and long-term prognostic improvement.

4.2.2. Standardizing the construction and quality control of stroke centers is the core issue and direction of current research

The findings of keyword clustering and high-frequency keyword comparisons in this study verify that standardized management, medical quality control, and clinical guidelines have remained persistent research hotspots worldwide. Standardized system construction and full-process quality supervision constitute another core research priority in the field of stroke centers, second only to reperfusion therapy technologies. The certification and quality control systems for stroke centers in China and the United States have been established under entirely different industry governance models. Differences in their operational effectiveness and governance logic can provide references for the quality improvement and upgrading of domestic stroke center systems.

Based on the evidence-based medicine framework, the United States established a “three-tier, 4-stage” stroke center certification structure: acute stroke preparation hospitals (primary level), primary centers (regional level), comprehensive centers (advanced level), and thrombectomy specialty centers (added in 2015).[13,14] The certification process shows that TJC first introduced primary certification standards in 2003, followed by comprehensive center upgrades in 2012 and primary level refinement in 2015. Concurrently, the Stroke Alliance promoted the establishment of HFAP’s 5-dimensional, 36-item certification system. By 2019, TJC (1403 centers), DNV (221 centers), and HFAP (65 centers) formed a nationwide stroke treatment network.[14] Additionally, TJC’s certification system implements mandatory quality monitoring mechanisms, requiring certified centers to establish structured diagnostic databases and conduct quarterly reviews on 12 core indicators, including target-to-treatment time window compliance (≤45 minutes). Studies confirm that this mechanism maintains a stable IVT rate above 28.6% in certified centers (vs 12.3% in noncertified centers).[15]

China’s stroke center development framework, coordinated by national health authorities, establishes a tripartite “government-medical consortium-quality control” model that contrasts with Western academic-led approaches. Given China’s uneven medical resource distribution, the stroke treatment field still faces multidimensional challenges: significant room for improvement exists in key areas including equitable resource allocation, standardized acute-phase identification, systematic quality supervision, and regional referral efficiency. However, this model has achieved 3 major breakthroughs: standardization of certification systems (the National Health Commission issued the “Stroke Center Construction and Management Guidelines” in 2022), provincial-municipal-county 3-tier network coverage, and sustained quality improvements (in 2021, Chinese stroke centers reported over 2,12,000 thrombolysis cases and 53,000 thrombectomy cases, representing 1.39× and 1.42× of 2020 figures respectively).

Evidence-based medical research highlights the imperative to establish a 4-dimensional research framework for future development: Developing differentiated construction standards (with tailored adaptation metrics for county-level medical institutions); creating intelligent quality control platforms (featuring dynamic monitoring systems based on Diagnosis-Related Groups [DRGs] data); establishing multicenter efficacy evaluation models (integrating health economics indicators such as quality-adjusted life years [QALYs] and disability-adjusted life years [DALYs]); optimizing regional collaboration mechanisms (implementing 5G-powered digital twin systems for stroke treatment). This strategic transformation will steer China’s stroke care system from “scale expansion” to “quality enhancement.”

4.2.3. Intelligent and information construction of stroke centers is the development trend of future research

Burst analysis of keywords has demonstrated that terms related to ML and digital healthcare represent the newest cutting-edge research hotspots in international studies in recent years. Domestic research has gradually expanded into emergency informatization and intelligent assessment. Intelligent and informatized transformation acts as a technical carrier connecting acute-phase reperfusion therapy and the full-process quality control system, and serves as a core solution to tackle uneven treatment timeliness and fragmented regional management.

ML, the core supporting technology of artificial intelligence, is applicable to key clinical links including stroke classification, thrombolysis decision-making, and long-term prognosis prediction, and provides quantitative evidence for individualized reperfusion interventions.[16,17] Nevertheless, existing intelligent models generally have limitations in clinical implementation. Model training heavily relies on single-center datasets, leading to drastically degraded generalization performance during cross-regional and cross-hospital popularization. Most imaging prediction models are constructed merely based on solitary imaging features without integrating multidimensional clinical data such as biochemical markers, demographic characteristics, and disease course. In addition, deep learning algorithms suffer from insufficient training sample sizes for MRI images and inadequate validation evidence from external cohorts. Feasible solutions to the above bottlenecks mainly include constructing a multicenter federated learning data platform and developing multi-omics joint prediction models by integrating radiomics signatures and clinical indicators. Such strategies can improve the adaptability of these algorithms for primary medical institutions and hospitals at different tiers.

Mobile Stroke Unit (MSU) serves as a representative carrier for the digital transformation of prehospital emergency care. Supported by network communication technology, MSU realizes the advancement of prehospital thrombolysis and shortens the time window from stroke onset to drug administration at the source. Existing evidence-based studies have verified that every 1-minute reduction in treatment delay for patients with large vessel occlusion corresponds to an extra 4.2 disability-free life days.[18,19] Early rtPA administration via MSU markedly raises the proportion of patients receiving reperfusion therapy within the golden therapeutic window, and its overall treatment efficiency far exceeds that of the conventional ground-based emergency medical system.[20,21] Early prehospital intervention can greatly improve patients’ functional independence at 90 days, which is attributed to 2 pathological mechanisms. For one thing, urgent intervention can preserve over 70% of the ischemic penumbra and effectively shrink the volume of irreversible infarct core. For another, fresh ultra-early thrombi contain a higher fraction of erythrocyte-platelet complexes, making these thrombi significantly more susceptible to lysis by rtPA compared with mature thrombi formed in the late disease stage.[22]

The practical value of MSU fully verifies the innovative effect of digital prehospital modes on the stroke emergency system. Nevertheless, MSU application in China is still in the initial exploration phase and difficult to roll out on a large scale owing to various practical restrictions. This matches the bibliometric clustering result that primary hospitals and regional referrals have persistently been popular research topics. Three core drawbacks exist in the domestic promotion of the MSU system. Firstly, medical resources are unevenly distributed across urban, rural, and different regions. Emergency stations are insufficiently covered in remote districts and suburban areas, without scientific calculation tools for network layout planning. Secondly, prehospital imaging diagnosis has a low degree of intelligence. Mobile CT scanners are not equipped with matched AI rapid interpretation modules, lowering the efficiency of remote image transmission and real-time consultation. Thirdly, off-window prehospital IVT lacks adequate multicenter evidence-based evidence, and a risk-benefit stratified assessment system has not been constructed so far.[14]

Combined with the existing deficiencies of intelligent algorithms and mobile emergency units, together with cutting-edge trends in this field, a full-chain collaborative upgrading scheme should be formulated for the informatization and intelligent construction of stroke centers in China. In terms of hardware, intensive integration between 5G communication and mobile CT equipment should be implemented to build low-latency channels for remote image transmission. In terms of procedures, standardized operating specifications for prehospital IVT ought to be refined. Timeline management, rapid biomarker detection, and AI-aided decision-making tools should be embedded to form a closed-loop treatment procedure. In terms of management, a unified regional digital platform for emergency quality control needs to be established to interconnect full-process data, including prehospital screening, on-the-way intervention, and handover for in-hospital interventional therapy, so as to realize the overall optimization of the spatiotemporal emergency procedure. Eventually, a full-coverage and hierarchically linked digital stroke emergency network can be established. Technological methods will help narrow the gaps in treatment capacity among different regions, and meanwhile promote the practical application of the hierarchical quality control and long-term prognostic evaluation systems mentioned above.

5. Advantages and limitations

This study performs a bibliometric analysis on stroke center publications using CiteSpace. It systematically sorts out the research hotspots, knowledge framework, and evolutionary trends in this field, conducts visual analysis based on literature from mainstream Chinese and English databases, and verifies the reliability of research findings via robustness tests, thereby offering references for clinical stroke center construction, nursing practice, and relevant scientific research.

Nevertheless, several limitations exist in the present study. First, literature retrieval only covers CNKI, Wanfang Data, VIP, and the WOSCC, excluding external databases such as PubMed and Embase, which leads to retrieval bias. Second, this research focuses on macroscopic bibliometric analysis without in-depth qualitative exploration of the clinical efficacy and internal mechanisms of stroke centers. Third, the parameters in CiteSpace are manually configured. Although the overall results are stable, subtle parameter fluctuations may exert minor impacts on partial graphical details. Future research is supposed to expand database resources, integrate quantitative and qualitative analyses, and improve the research system for this field.

6. Summary

This study adopted bibliometric analysis via CiteSpace to uncover the evolutionary trajectory and core themes of stroke center research. Tracking research dynamics within this field enables medical practitioners to optimize clinical intervention protocols and prognosis management strategies. The findings provide empirical evidence for establishing a dynamic knowledge updating framework, facilitating the transition of stroke care toward a precision-oriented model.

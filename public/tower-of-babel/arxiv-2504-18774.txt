1.
Introduction

In the seminal work
[
BSV24
]
, Ben-Zvi, Sakellaridis and Venkatesh (BZSV for short) proposed the well-known relative Langlands duality, which we may also call the BZSV duality. Let
G
be a connected reductive group. The BZSV duality concerns the duality between certain
G
-Hamiltonian variety
M
and
\widehat{G}
-Hamiltonian variety
\widehat{M}
, in the sense that the “period” attached to
M
on the automorphic side (A-side) should match with the “
L
-function” attached to
\widehat{M}
on the spectral side (B-side). One of the key features of the BZSV duality is that the double dual
\widehat{\widehat{M}}
is expected to coincide with
M
. Therefore, we may switch the A-side and B-side and expect that the period associated to
\widehat{M}
should also match with the
L
-function associated to
M
.

In the case of the polarized hyperspherical varieties in the sense of
[
BSV24
, §3]
, BZSV proposed a conjectural description of the dual varieties
[
BSV24
, §4]
. The structure of a hyperspherical variety is given by a quadruple
\Delta=(G,H,\rho_{H},\iota)
, Here
G
is a split reductive group;
H
is a reductive subgroup of
G
;
\rho_{H}
is a symplectic representation of
H
; and
\iota
is a homomorphism from
\SL_{2}
into
G
whose image commutes with
H
. In
[
BSV24
, §4]
, they proposed a combinatorial method to compute the dual of a polarized hyperspherical Hamiltonian variety that is attached to a “dual quadruple”
\widehat{\Delta}=(\widehat{G},\widehat{H}^{\prime},\rho_{\widehat{H}^{\prime}},\hat{\iota}^{\prime})
.

The goal of this article is to verify some cases of the BZSV conjecture for the hyperspherical
\GL_{2n+1}
-variety
T^{*}(\Sp_{2n}\backslash\GL_{2n+1})
, or the quadruple
(\GL_{2n+1},\Sp_{2n},0,1)
. We show that its dual is
T^{*}(\GL_{n}\times\GL_{n+1}\backslash\GL_{2n+1})
, or attached to the quadruple
(\GL_{2n+1},\GL_{n}\times\GL_{n+1},0,1)
as predicted by
[
BSV24
, §4]
. The conjecture has various setting; we mainly concentrate on the global numerical and the global geometric setting. We now describe them in detail.

1.1.
Numerical Result

We briefly recall the BZSV conjecture in the numerical (also known as the classical Langlands) setting (see also
[
MWZ24
]
). The map
\iota
induces an adjoint action of
H\times\SL_{2}
on the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
of
G
and we can decompose it as

\mathfrak{g}=\bigoplus_{k\in I}\rho_{k}\otimes\Sym^{k}

where
\rho_{k}
is some representation of
H
and
I
is a finite subset of
\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
. Let
I_{\mathrm{odd}}
denote the subset of
I
containing all the odd numbers and let

\rho_{H,\iota}=\rho_{H}\oplus\left(\bigoplus_{k\in I_{\mathrm{odd}}}\rho_{k}\right).

Let
F
denote a global field and let
\mathbb{A}=\mathbb{A}_{F}
. For a BZSV quadruple,
\rho_{H,\iota}
is a symplectic anomaly-free representation of
H
. Take a maximal isotropic subspace
Y
of
\rho_{H,\iota}
. For a Schwartz function
\Phi
on
Y(\mathbb{A})
, the previous condition ensures we can define the associated theta series
\Theta(h,\Phi)
on
H(\mathbb{A})
.

For automorphic form
\varphi
on
G(\mathbb{A})
, let
\mathcal{P}_{\iota}(\varphi)
denote the degenerate Fourier coefficient associated to
\iota
. We define the following period integral

\mathcal{P}_{H,\iota,\rho_{H}}(\varphi,\Phi)=\int_{[H]}\mathcal{P}_{\iota}(\varphi)(h)\Theta(h,\Phi)\mathrm{d}h

whenever the integral is convergent.
The following conjecture (
[
BSV24
, §14]
,
[
MWZ24
, §1.1]
) is the main conjecture regarding this period integral.

Conjecture 1.1.1
.

Let
\pi
be an irreducible automorphic representation of
G(\mathbb{A})
. Then the period integral
\mathcal{P}_{H,\iota,\rho_{H}}(\varphi)
is nonzero only if the Arthur parameter of
\pi
factors through

\hat{\iota}^{\prime}:\widehat{H}^{\prime}(\mathbb{C})\times\SL_{2}(\mathbb{C})\to\widehat{G}(\mathbb{C}).

If this is the case and
\pi
is lifting of a global tempered Arthur packet of
H^{\prime}(\mathbb{A})
, then we have

(1.1.1)

\frac{|\mathcal{P}_{H,\iota,\rho_{H}}(\varphi)|^{2}}{\langle\varphi,\varphi\rangle}``="\frac{\displaystyle L\left(\frac{1}{2},\Pi,\rho_{\widehat{H}^{\prime}}\right)\cdot\prod_{k\in\hat{I}}L\left(\frac{k}{2}+1,\Pi,\hat{\rho}_{k}\right)}{L(1,\Pi,\mathrm{Ad})^{2}}.

Here
\langle\,,\,\rangle
denotes a certain version of
L^{2}
-norm.

Remark 1.1.2
.

Note that in
[
MWZ24
]
, the authors state the conjecture for only discrete
\pi
, in which case
\langle\,,\,\rangle
denotes the standard
L^{2}
-norm. However, it’s believed that the statement should hold for general
\pi
, as stated in the above conjecture.

If we consider the BZSV quadruple
(\GL_{2n+1},\GL_{n}\times\GL_{n+1},0,1)
. In
[
FJ93
]
, the authors verified that the associated linear period vanishes for cuspidal automorphic representations of
\GL_{2n+1}(\mathbb{A})
. Moreover, let
\pi
be a cuspidal automorphic representation of
\GL_{2n}(\mathbb{A})
whose
hypothetical Langlands parameter factors through
\Sp_{2n}(\mathbb{C})
and let
1
denote the trivial representation of
\GL_{1}
. Then in
[
FJ93
, Theorem 5.1]
, the authors verified that if the automorphic representation of
\GL_{2n+1}(\mathbb{A})
is
\pi\boxplus 1
, the regularized period integral defined in
loc. cit.
represents the standard
L
-function
L(1,\pi)
. This coincides with the claimed identity in the previous conjecture. The results above suggest the BZSV dual of
(\GL_{2n+1},\GL_{n}\times\GL_{n+1},0,1)
should be
(\GL_{2n+1},\Sp_{2n},0,1)
, as is predicted by
[
BSV24
]
.

In this article, we switch the A-side and the B-side of the linear period mentioned above. Consider the BZSV quadruple
(\GL_{2n+1},\Sp_{2n},0,1)
, we verify that its dual quadruple should be
(\GL_{2n+1},\GL_{n}\times\GL_{n+1},0,1)
. More concretely, we prove the following result:

Theorem 1.1.3
.

Let
\pi
be an automorphic representation of
\GL_{2n+1}(\mathbb{A}_{F})
, then

(1)

If
\pi
is cuspidal, or
\pi
is of the form
\Pi_{1}\boxplus\Pi_{2}
, where
\Pi_{i}
are cuspidal automorphic representation of
\GL_{n_{i}}(\mathbb{A}_{F})
with central character trivial on
A_{\GL_{n_{i}}}^{\infty}
and
n_{1}+n_{2}=2n+1
with
1\leq n_{i}\leq n-1
. Then for any
f\in\pi
, we have

\int_{[\Sp_{2n}]}f(h)\mathrm{d}h=0.

(2)

If
\pi
is of the form
\Pi_{n}\boxplus\Pi_{n+1}
, where
\Pi_{i}
is a cuspidal automorphic representation of
\GL_{i}(\mathbb{A}_{F})
with central character trivial on
A_{\GL_{i}}^{\infty}
for
i=n,n+1
. Let
\Pi:=\Pi_{n}\boxtimes\Pi_{n+1}
be the cuspidal automorphic representation of
\GL_{n}(\mathbb{A}_{F})\times\GL_{n+1}(\mathbb{A}_{F})
, then for
f=\otimes f_{v}\in\pi
, we have

(1.1.2)

\frac{\lvert\mathcal{P}(f)\rvert^{2}}{\langle f,f\rangle_{\mathrm{Pet}}}=\frac{\zeta^{*}(1)\zeta(3)\cdots\zeta(2n+1)}{\zeta(2)\cdots\zeta(2n)}\frac{L^{*}(1,\Pi,\hat{\rho}_{0})}{L^{*}(1,\Pi,\mathrm{Ad})^{2}}\prod_{v}\frac{|\mathcal{P}_{v}^{\natural}(f_{v})|^{2}}{\langle f_{v},f_{v}\rangle^{\natural}}.

The reader should note the formal resemblance of (
1.1.2
) with (
1.1.1
). We explain the terms in (
1.1.2
) in some detail.

•

Let
P
be the standard parabolic subgroup of
\GL_{2n+1}
with Levi
\GL_{n}\times\GL_{n+1}
. Then by
f\in\pi
we mean
f
is an Eisenstein series of the form
E(g,\varphi):=E(g,\varphi,0)
, where
\varphi\in\Ind_{P(\mathbb{A})}^{\GL_{2n+1}(\mathbb{A})}\Pi
(see §
2.4
for more details). We will show in §
3
that

\mathcal{P}(E(\cdot,\varphi)):=\int_{[\Sp_{2n}]}E(h,\varphi)\mathrm{d}h

is absolutely convergent. And we put

\langle f,f\rangle_{\operatorname{Pet}}:=\langle\varphi,\varphi\rangle_{\operatorname{Pet}}:=\int_{[\GL_{2n+1}]_{P,0}}\lvert\varphi(x)\rvert^{2}\mathrm{d}x.

This explains the left hand side of (
1.1.2
).

•

Let
\zeta(s)=\zeta_{F}(s)
denote the completed Dedekind zeta function of
F
and
\zeta^{*}(1)
denotes the residue of
\zeta(s)
at
s=1
.

•

L^{*}(1,\Pi,\operatorname{Ad})
denotes the leading term of the Laurant expansion of
L^{*}(s,\Pi,\operatorname{Ad})
at
s=1
. Similar for
L^{*}(1,\Pi,\hat{\rho}_{0})
. Note that in this case, we have

L^{*}(1,\Pi,\hat{\rho}_{0})=L^{*}(1,\Pi_{n},\operatorname{Ad})L^{*}(1,\Pi_{n+1},\operatorname{Ad})L(1,\Pi_{n}^{\vee}\times\Pi_{n+1})L(1,\Pi_{n}\times\Pi_{n+1}^{\vee}),

and

L^{*}(1,\Pi,\operatorname{Ad})=L^{*}(1,\Pi_{n},\operatorname{Ad})L^{*}(1,\Pi_{n+1},\operatorname{Ad}).

•

\mathcal{P}^{\natural}_{v}(f_{v})
and
\langle f_{v},f_{v}\rangle^{\natural}
are local (normalized) version of the period and inner product respectively. They are defined in §
6.4
in terms of Whittaker model, with the properties that when everything is unramified, then
\mathcal{P}^{\natural}_{v}(f_{v})=\langle f_{v},f_{v}\rangle^{\natural}=1
.

Finally, we remark that the case
\Sp_{2n}\backslash\GL_{2n}
, commonly referred to as the
symplectic period
, has been extensively studied by Jacquet–Rallis
[
JR92
]
and Offen
[
Off06
]
. Its (expected) BZSV dual is the so-called
Jacquet–Shalika period
.

1.2.
Geometric result

A remarkable feature of conjectures in
[
BSV24
]
is their parallelity over number fields and function fields, enabling predictions of results over number fields from parallel but sometimes easier results over function fields. Over function fields, beyond the numerical conjecture mentioned before, one can ask a matching between geometrizations of both sides of the identities in theorem
1.1.3
under the conjectural geometric Langlands equivalence
\mathrm{Shv}_{\mathrm{Nilp}}(\mathrm{Bun}_{G})\cong\mathrm{IndCoh}_{\mathrm{Nilp}}(\mathrm{Loc}_{\widehat{G}})
. We refer to
[
Ari+22a
]
for a precise formulation of this equivalence.

A precise conjecture in this direction is formulated in
[
BSV24
, §12]
, which we briefly recall in the case that
M=T^{*}X
and
X=G/H
where
H\subset G
is a connected reductive subgroup making
M
hyperspherical (i.e. we are considering quadruple
(G,H,0,1)
): On the A-side, one considers map
\pi:\mathrm{Bun}_{H}\to\mathrm{Bun}_{G}
and defines the
period sheaf

\mathcal{P}_{X}:=\pi_{!}\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{l}

(we work with constructible étale
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{l}
-complexes), which induces a natural functor

\mathrm{ev}^{\mathrm{Mir}}(\mathcal{P}_{X}\otimes-)\cong\Gamma_{c}(\pi^{*}(-)):\mathrm{Shv}_{\mathrm{Nilp}}(\mathrm{Bun}_{G})\to\mathrm{Vect}

where
\mathrm{ev}^{\mathrm{Mir}}:\mathrm{Shv}(\mathrm{Bun}_{G})^{\otimes 2}\to\mathrm{Vect}
is the evaluation map for the miraculous duality of
\mathrm{Shv}(\mathrm{Bun}_{G})
defined in
[
Ari+22
]
. On the B-side, one can similarly define the
L
-sheaf

\mathcal{L}_{\widehat{M}}\in\mathrm{IndCoh}(\mathrm{Loc}_{\widehat{G}})

using the dual
\widehat{G}
-Hamiltonian space
\widehat{M}
and similarly consider the functor

\mathrm{ev}^{\mathrm{Serre}}(\mathcal{L}_{\widehat{M}}\otimes-):\mathrm{IndCoh}_{\mathrm{Nilp}}(\mathrm{Loc}_{\widehat{G}})\to\mathrm{Vect}

where
\mathrm{ev}^{\mathrm{Serre}}:\mathrm{IndCoh}(\mathrm{Loc}_{\widehat{G}})^{\otimes 2}\to\mathrm{Vect}
is the evaluation map for the Grothendieck-Serre duality on
\mathrm{Loc}_{\widehat{G}}
. The conjecture in
[
BSV24
, §12]
predicts an isomorphism between functors
\mathrm{Shv}_{\mathrm{Nilp}}(\mathrm{Bun}_{G})\cong\mathrm{IndCoh}_{\mathrm{Nilp}}(\mathrm{Loc}_{\widehat{G}})\to\mathrm{Vect}
:

\mathrm{ev}^{\mathrm{Mir}}(\mathcal{P}_{X}\otimes-)\cong\mathrm{ev}^{\mathrm{Serre}}(\mathcal{L}_{\widehat{M}}\otimes c^{*}(-))

where
c:\mathrm{Loc}_{\widehat{G}}\to\mathrm{Loc}_{\widehat{G}}
is induced by the Cartan involution on
\widehat{G}
.

In the appendix, we verify the conjecture above for pairs of groups
(G,H)=(\GL_{2n+1},\Sp_{2n})
and
(G,H)=(\GL_{2n},\Sp_{2n})
on the cuspidal part. More precisely, we prove the following:

Theorem 1.2.1
.

For
(G,H)
as above, for any cuspidal automorphic sheaf
\mathcal{F}\in\mathrm{Shv}(\mathrm{Bun}_{G})_{\mathrm{cusp}}
, one has

\Gamma_{c}(\pi^{*}\mathcal{F})=0.

Remark 1.2.2
.

Such type of geometric results in many other examples are considered in series works of Lysenko (an incomplete list includes
[
Lys02
]
[
Lys08
]
[
Lys21
]
) and has their potential applications to arithmetic period conjectures as discusses in
[
LW25
]
.

1.3.
Structure of the article

After introducing notations and preliminaries in §2. We study the the numerical conjecture in the main part of the article and geometric conjecture in the appendix.

In §
3
, we show that the periods of the Eisenstein series we considered are absolutely convergent. We treat the function field case and the number field case separately. In §
4
we prove that the
\Sp_{2}n
periods of cuspidal automorphic forms, as well as certain kinds of cuspidal Eisenstein series, on
\GL_{2n+1}
vanish. In §
5
, we do some linear algebra to classify
\Sp_{2n}
orbits on
\GL_{2n+1}/P_{n,n+1}
, where
P_{n,n+1}
is the maximal parabolic subgroup of
\GL_{2n+1}
with type
(n,n+1)
. In §
6
, we use the results in §
4
and §
5
to prove our main numerical results.

In the appendix, we geometrize the results in §
4
to get the geometric conjecture.

Tamagwa measure

\psi:F\backslash\mathbb{A}_{F}\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}

\omega

When

2.2.4.

[G]_{P,0}:=N(\mathbb{A})M(F)A_{P}^{\infty}\backslash G(\mathbb{A})

\varphi:[G]_{P}\to\mathbb{C}
such that
\varphi(ag)=\delta_{P}(a)^{\frac{1}{2}}\varphi(g)
, the integral

\int_{[G]_{P,0}}\lvert\varphi(x)\rvert\mathrm{d}x

makes sense, and when it is finite, the integral

\int_{[G]_{P,0}}\varphi(x)\mathrm{d}x

makes sense.

2.2.5.
Siegel sets

By a Siegel set
\mathfrak{s}^{P}
of
[G]_{P}
we mean a subset of

[G]_{P}
of the form

\mathfrak{s}^{P}=\omega_{0}\{a\in A_{0}^{\infty}\mid\langle\alpha,H_{0}(a)-T_{-}\rangle\geq 0,\ \alpha\in\Delta_{0}^{P}\}K,

where
T_{-}\in\mathfrak{a}_{0}
, and such that
G(\mathbb{A})=P(F)\mathfrak{s}^{P}
. We
assume that, for different parabolic subgroups of
G
, their Siegel sets
are defined by the same
T_{-}
. In particular if
P\subset Q
then
\mathfrak{s}^{P}\supset\mathfrak{s}^{Q}
.

2.2.6.
Truncation parameters

Fix a norm
\|\cdot\|
on
\mathfrak{a}_{0}
. We say
T\in\mathfrak{a}_{0}
is
sufficiently positive
, if
there exists
C>0
and
\varepsilon>0
such that

\inf_{\alpha\in\Delta_{0}}\langle\alpha,T\rangle\geq\max\{C,\varepsilon\|T\|\}.

2.2.7.
Paley-Wiener spaces

For a semi-standard parabolic
P
, let
\operatorname{PW}(i\mathfrak{a}_{P}^{*})
denote the space of Paley-Wiener functions on
i\mathfrak{a}_{P}^{*}
. More precisely, it consists of entire function
f
on
\mathfrak{a}_{P,\mathbb{C}}^{*}
with the following condition:
equation

There exists
A>0
such that for all integer
N>0
,

\lvert f(\lambda)\rvert\ll(1+\lvert\lambda\rvert)^{-N}e^{A\lvert\operatorname{Re}(\lambda)\rvert}

The Fourier transform:

\operatorname{PW}(i\mathfrak{a}_{P}^{*})\ni f\mapsto\widehat{f}(X)=\int_{i\mathfrak{a}_{P}^{*}}f(\lambda)e^{\langle\lambda,X\rangle}\mathrm{d}\lambda

defines a bijection between
\operatorname{PW}(i\mathfrak{a}_{P}^{*})
and
C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathfrak{a}_{P})
.

2.3.
Function spaces

Let
F
be a number field and let
G
be an algebraic group over
F
. Let
P
be a parabolic subgroup of
G
. There are various function spaces on
[G]_{P}
which we briefly recall below. The reader may consult
[
BCZ22
, §2.5]
for more details.

A function
f:G(\mathbb{A})\to\mathbb{C}
is called
smooth
, if it is right
J
-invariant for some open compact subgroup
J\subset G(\mathbb{A}_{f})
and for any
g_{f}\in G(\mathbb{A}_{f})
, the function
g_{\infty}\mapsto f(g_{f}g_{\infty})
is
C^{\infty}
. A function on
[G]_{P}
is called smooth if it pullbacks to a smooth function on
G(\mathbb{A})
.

Let
\mathcal{S}([G]_{P})
be the space of
Schwartz functions
on
[G]_{P}
. It is the union of
\mathcal{S}([G]_{P},J)
for open compact subgroup
J\subset G(\mathbb{A}_{f})
. Where
\mathcal{S}([G]_{P},J)
is the space of smooth functions on
[G]_{P}
which are right
J
invariant and

\|f\|_{X,N}:=\sup_{x\in[G]_{P}}\lvert R(X)f(x)\rvert\|x\|_{P}^{N}<\infty

for any
X\in\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g}_{\infty})
and
N>0
. The vector space
\mathcal{S}([G]_{P},J)
is naturally a Fréchet space and
\mathcal{S}([G]_{P})
is naturally a strict LF space.

Let
\mathcal{S}^{0}([G]_{P})
be the space of measurable function
f
on
[G]_{P}
such that

(2.3.1)

\|f\|_{\infty,N}:=\sup_{x\in[G]_{P}}\lvert f(x)\rvert\|x\|^{N}_{P}<\infty

for any
N>0
. It is naturally a Fréchet space.

Let
\mathcal{T}([G]_{P})
be the function of
uniform moderate growth
on
[G]_{P}
. It is the union of
\mathcal{T}_{-N}([G]_{P},J)
, where
N>0
and
J\subset G(\mathbb{A}_{f})
is open compact subgroup.
\mathcal{T}_{-N}([G]_{P},J)
consists of smooth functions
f
on
[G]_{P}
which are right
J
-invariant and

\|f\|_{X,-N}:=\sup_{x\in[G]_{P}}\lvert R(X)f(x)\rvert\|x\|_{P}^{-N}<\infty

for any
X\in\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g}_{\infty})
. The vector space
\mathcal{T}_{-N}([G]_{P},J)
is naturally a Fréchet space and
\mathcal{T}([G]_{P})
then carries the induces (non-strict) LF topology.

For
P\subset Q
, we have the following
constant term
map

\mathcal{T}([G]_{Q})\ni\varphi\mapsto\varphi_{P}:=\left(g\mapsto\int_{[N_{P}]}\varphi(ng)\mathrm{d}n\right)\in\mathcal{T}([G]_{P}).

Let
\mathcal{T}_{\operatorname{cusp}}([G]_{Q})
be the closed subspace of
\mathcal{T}([G]_{Q})
consists of
\varphi
such that
\varphi_{P}=0
for any
P\subset Q
. It is well known that for
\varphi\in\mathcal{T}_{\operatorname{cusp}}([G])
, we have the following
rapid decay property of cusp form
:
equation

For any
N>0
and any
X\in\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g}_{\infty})
, we have
\sup_{x\in[G]^{1}}\lvert R(X)\varphi(x)\rvert\|x\|_{G}^{N}<\infty
.

For standard parabolic subgroups
P\subset Q
, the pseudo-Eisenstein series
E:=E_{P}^{Q}
defines a map

E_{P}^{G}:\mathcal{S}([G]_{P})\to\mathcal{S}([G]_{Q}),\quad\varphi\mapsto\sum_{\gamma\in P(F)\backslash Q(F)}\varphi(\gamma g).

If
P
,
Q
be two parabolic subgroups of
G
, for
\varphi\in\mathcal{S}_{\operatorname{cusp}}([G]_{P})
, we have the formula
[
MW95
, §II.1.7]

(2.3.2)

E_{Q}(g,\varphi)=\sum_{w\in W(P;Q)}E^{Q}(g,M(w)\varphi),

where
M(w)
denotes the intertwining operator:

M(w):\mathcal{S}([G]_{P_{w}})\to\mathcal{S}([G]_{Q_{w}}),M(w)\varphi(g)=\int_{N_{P_{w}}(\mathbb{A})\cap wN_{P}w^{-1}(\mathbb{A})\backslash N_{P_{w}}(\mathbb{A})}\varphi(w^{-1}ng)\mathrm{d}n.

2.4.2.
Eisenstein series

Let
P
be a semi-standard parabolic subgroup of
G
. For any
\varphi\in\mathcal{A}_{P}(G)
,
g\in G(\mathbb{A})
and
\lambda\in\mathfrak{a}_{P,\mathbb{C}}^{*}
, the
Eisenstein series
is defined by

E(g,\varphi,\lambda):=\sum_{\gamma\in P(F)\backslash G(F)}\varphi_{\lambda}(\gamma g),

where

\varphi_{\lambda}(g)=\varphi(g)e^{\langle\lambda,H_{P}(g)\rangle}.

By the result of
[
Lan06
]
,
[
BL24
]
, the sum defining
E(g,\varphi,\lambda)
is convergent when
\operatorname{Re}(\lambda)
is sufficiently positive, and admits meromorphic continuation to
\mathfrak{a}_{P,\mathbb{C}}^{*}
.

Let
\pi
be a cuspidal automorphic representation of
M_{P}(\mathbb{A})
with trivial central character on
A_{P}^{\infty}
. We say
\lambda
is regular for
\pi
, if
E(g,\varphi,\lambda)
is holomorphic at
\lambda
for every
\varphi\in\mathcal{A}_{P,\pi}
and
g\in[G]
. It is known that for any such
\pi
, any
\lambda\in i\mathfrak{a}_{P}^{*}
is regular for
\pi
.

Let
P,Q
be two standard parabolic subgroups of
G
. For
w\in W(P,Q)
and
\lambda\in\mathfrak{a}_{P,\mathbb{C}}^{*}
. The
intertwining operator
is defined by

M(w,\lambda):\mathcal{A}_{P}(F)\ni\varphi\mapsto M(w,\varphi)(g):=e^{\langle-w\lambda,H_{Q}(g)\rangle}\int_{N_{Q}\cap wN_{P}w^{-1}(\mathbb{A})\backslash N_{Q}(\mathbb{A})}e^{\langle\lambda,H_{P}(w^{-1}\lambda g)\rangle}\varphi(w^{-1}ng).

By the result of
[
Lan06
]
,
[
BL24
]
, the intertwining operator is convergent when
\operatorname{Re}(\lambda)
is sufficiently positive and has meromorphic continuation to
\mathfrak{a}_{P,\mathbb{C}}^{*}
.

For
\varphi\in\mathcal{A}_{P,\mathrm{cusp}}(G)
, we have the constant term formula analogous to (
2.3.2
):

(2.4.1)

E_{Q}(g,\varphi,\lambda)=\sum_{w\in W(P;Q)}E^{Q}(g,M(w)\varphi,w\lambda).

3.
Absolute convergence

In this section, we show that cuspidal Eisenstein series associated to maximal parabolic subgroups of
\GL_{2n+1}
are of rapid decay on
\Sp_{2n}
. Indeed they are compactly supported in the function field case. In particular, their
\Sp_{2n}
-periods are absolutely convergent.

Recall that
G:=\GL_{2n+1}
and
H:=\Sp_{2n}
. Recall that for a subgroup
S
of
G
, we denote by
S^{\prime}:=S\cap H
.

3.1.
Function field case

Let
C
be a smooth projective and geometrically connected curve over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, let
F
be the function field of
C
and let
\mathrm{Bun}_{G}
denotes the moduli stack of
G
bundles on
C
.

For convenience, we work in the unramified setting. The modification to the ramified case is obvious (replacing
\mathrm{Bun}_{G}
by
\mathrm{Bun}_{G,D}
for some divisor
D
).

We recall the Harder-Narasimhan filtration of a symplectic vector bundle. Let
\mathcal{E}_{s}
be a symplectic vector bundle on
C
. By definition, the Harder-Narasimhan filtration of
\mathcal{E}_{s}
is given by

(3.1.1)

0\subsetneq\mathcal{W}_{1}\subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq\mathcal{W}_{k}\subset\mathcal{W}_{k}^{\perp}\subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq\mathcal{W}_{1}^{\perp}\subsetneq\mathcal{E}_{s}

where each
\mathcal{W}_{i}
is isotropic subbundle of
\mathcal{E}_{s}
with slopes of consecutive quotients are strictly decreasing. (If
\mathcal{W}_{k}=\mathcal{W}_{k}^{\perp}
, then we only count it once.)

Let
\mathrm{Bun}_{H}^{\mathrm{gaps}\leq 2g-2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
denote that set of symplectic vector bundles with all gaps in the slope sequences are bounded by
2g-2
. Note that
\mathrm{Bun}_{H}^{\mathrm{gaps}\leq 2g-2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
is a finite set.

Proposition 3.1.1
.

Let
f:\mathrm{Bun}_{G}(\mathbb{F}_{q})\to\mathbb{C}
be a function such that the constant terms
f_{P}=0
for any non-maximal parabolic subgroup
P
of
G
. Then the restriction of
f
on
\mathrm{Bun}_{H}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
is finitely supported.

Proof.

We show that
f
vanishes outside
\mathrm{Bun}_{H}^{\operatorname{gaps}\leq 2g-2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
. Let
\mathcal{E}_{s}\not\in\mathrm{Bun}_{H}^{\operatorname{gaps}\leq 2g-2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
. Assume that Harder-Narasimhan filtration of
\mathcal{E}_{s}
is given by (
3.1.1
).

The Harder-Narasimhan filtration of the vector bundle
\mathcal{E}_{s}\oplus\mathcal{O}
is then given by

0\subsetneq\mathcal{W}_{1}\subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq\mathcal{W}_{k}\subsetneq\mathcal{W}_{k}^{\perp}\oplus\mathcal{O}\subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq\mathcal{W}_{1}^{\perp}\oplus\mathcal{O}\subsetneq\mathcal{E}_{s}.

Therefore, the proposition follows from lemma
3.1.2
below.
∎

Lemma 3.1.2
.

Let
f:\mathrm{Bun}_{G}(\mathbb{F}_{q})\to\mathbb{C}
satisfies the condition in proposition
3.1.1
. Let
\mathcal{E}\in\mathrm{Bun}_{G}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
such that in the slope sequence of
\mathcal{E}
there are two gaps
>2g-2
. Then
f(\mathcal{E})=0
.

Proof.

Suppose that the Harder-Narasimhan filtration of
\mathcal{E}
is given by
0\subsetneq\mathcal{W}_{1}\cdots\subsetneq\mathcal{W}_{k}\subsetneq\mathcal{E}
with slopes
\mu_{r}:=\mu(\mathcal{W}_{r}/\mathcal{W}_{r-1})
satisfies
\mu_{i}-\mu_{i-1}>2g-2,\mu_{j}-\mu_{j-1}>2g-2
.

Take
a=\operatorname{rank}\mathcal{W}_{i}
,
b=\operatorname{rank}\mathcal{W}_{j}
. Consider the parabolic subgroup
P=P_{a,b,c}=MN
of
\GL_{2n+1}
. For
(\mathcal{V}_{1},\mathcal{V}_{2},\mathcal{V}_{3})\in\mathrm{Bun}_{M}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
, we have

f_{P}(\mathcal{V}_{1},\mathcal{V}_{2},\mathcal{V}_{3})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathcal{F}^{\bullet}:=0\subset\mathcal{F}_{1}\subset\mathcal{F}_{2}\subset\mathcal{F}_{3}\subset\mathcal{V}\in\mathrm{Bun}_{P}(\mathbb{F}_{q})\\
\mathcal{F}_{i}/\mathcal{F}_{i-1}\cong\mathcal{V}_{i}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\#\operatorname{Aut}(\mathcal{F}^{\bullet})}f(\mathcal{V})=0

Consider the case when
\mathcal{V}_{1}=\mathcal{W}_{i}
,
\mathcal{V}_{2}=\mathcal{W}_{j}/\mathcal{W}_{i}
,
\mathcal{W}_{3}=\mathcal{E}/\mathcal{W}_{j}
. Since for
i=1,2
,
\mu(\mathcal{V}_{i})>\mu(\mathcal{V}_{i+1})+2g-2
, we have
\operatorname{Ext}^{1}(\mathcal{V}_{2},\mathcal{V}_{1})=\operatorname{Ext}^{1}(\mathcal{V}_{3},\mathcal{V}_{2})=0
. Therefore
f_{P}(\mathcal{V}_{1},\mathcal{V}_{2},\mathcal{V}_{3})
is a non-zero multiple of
f(\mathcal{E})
. This implies
f(\mathcal{E})=0
.
∎

Note that cuspidal Eisenstein series associated to maximal parabolic subgroup satisfy the assumption of the proposition
3.1.1
, therefore we obtain.

Corollary 3.1.3
.

Let
P
be a maximal parabolic subgroup of
G
, let
\varphi\in\mathcal{A}_{P,\operatorname{cusp}}(G)
be a right
K=G(\mathbb{O})
invariant function. Then the Eisenstein series
E(\cdot,\varphi,\lambda)
is compactly supported on
[H]
whenever
\lambda
is regular.

Remark 3.1.4
.

As previously noted, the assumption that
\varphi
is unramified is only for sake of convenience. The corollary holds without this assumption.

3.2.
Number field case

In the number field case, we use Zydor’s truncation to replace the Harder-Narasimhan argument in the function field. The reader can compare the approach here and Lafforgue’s interpretation of Arthur truncation in the function field case

]

Proposition 3.2.1

The map

\mathcal{F}^{\prime}(B^{\prime})\to\mathcal{F},\quad P(\lambda)\mapsto P(\lambda^{G})

Proof.

For
\lambda

We denote the image of the map by
\mathcal{F}^{H}

-relevant parabolic subgroups

For
P^{\prime}\in\mathcal{F}^{\prime}(B^{\prime})

the image of
P^{\prime}
in
\mathcal{F}^{H}

We recall the Zydor’s construction of truncation operator in
[
Zyd19

For

\Lambda^{T}_{H}\varphi(h)=\sum_{P^{\prime}\in\mathcal{F}^{\prime}}\varepsilon_{P^{\prime}}\sum_{\gamma\in P^{\prime}(F)\backslash H(F)}\widehat{\tau}_{P^{\prime}}(H_{P^{\prime}}(\gamma h)-T_{P^{\prime}})\varphi_{P}(\gamma h).

The following theorem is due to Zydor

Theorem 3.2.2

(Zydor)
.

\Lambda^{T}\varphi\in\mathcal{S}^{0}([H])
. More precisely, for any

\|\cdot\|
(depending on

\|\Lambda^{T}_{H}\varphi\|_{\infty,N}\ll\|\varphi\|.

Where we recall that

Proof.

Recall the function space
\mathcal{T}(H)
in
[
BLX24

(denoted by
\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}(G)
in

({}_{P^{\prime}}\varphi)
for each

{}_{P^{\prime}}\varphi^{\prime}\in\mathcal{T}(P^{\prime}(F)\backslash H(\mathbb{A}))
for any

{}_{P^{\prime}}\varphi-{}_{Q^{\prime}}\varphi\in\mathcal{S}_{d_{P^{\prime}}^{Q^{\prime}}}(P^{\prime}(F)\backslash H(\mathbb{A}))

(see

\mathcal{T}(H)

\mathcal{T}([G])\to\mathcal{T}(H),\quad\varphi\mapsto(\varphi_{P})

By the approximation by constant term

on

\mathfrak{s}^{P^{\prime}}\subset\mathfrak{s}^{P}
, therefore, it reduces to the following claim:

For

\alpha\in X^{*}(A_{0})

which one can easily check directly.
∎

We record a corollary of the theorem above.

Corollary 3.2.3
.

Let
Q
be a maximal parabolic subgroup of
G

\varphi\in\mathcal{A}_{Q,\operatorname{cusp}}(G)

\lambda\in\mathfrak{a}_{P,\mathbb{C}}^{*}
such that the Eisenstein series

is holomorphic, it is Schwartz on

. In other words,
h\mapsto E(h,\varphi,\lambda)\in\mathcal{S}([H])

Proof.

Note that for any
P^{\prime}\in\mathcal{F}^{\prime}(B^{\prime})
such that
P^{\prime}\neq G^{\prime}
, the corresponding element
P
in
\mathcal{F}^{H}
is
not
a maximal parabolic subgroup of
G
. Hence by the constant term formula (
2.4.1
),
E_{P}(h,\varphi,\lambda)=0
. As a consequence, for any sufficiently regular
T\in\mathfrak{a}^{\prime}_{0}
,
\Lambda^{T}_{H}E(h,\varphi,\lambda)=E(h,\varphi,\lambda)
. The corollary the follows from theorem
3.2.2
(applied to
R(X)E(\cdot,\varphi,\lambda)
for any
X\in\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g}_{\infty}))
.
∎

In particular, for any cuspidal Eisenstein series associated to maximal parabolic subgroup, the period

\int_{[H]}E(h,\varphi,\lambda)\mathrm{d}h

is absolutely convergent whenever

is regular.

4.
Vanishing of certain periods

4.1.
A lemma on Klingen-mirabolic period

Let
P
be the subgroup of
\GL_{2n}
of the following form

\begin{pmatrix}1&*&*\\
&g&*\\
&&1\end{pmatrix},\quad g\in\GL_{2n-2}.

We call it the Klingen-mirabolic subgroup of
\GL_{2n}
. Recall
P^{\prime}=P\cap H
. We call it the Klingen-mirabolic subgroup of
H
. Write the Levi decomposition of
P=\GL_{2n}^{\flat}U
, where
U
is the unipotent radical and

\GL_{2n}^{\flat}=\begin{pmatrix}1&&\\
&g&\\
&&1\end{pmatrix},\quad g\in\GL_{2n-2}.

Then
P^{\prime}=H^{\flat}U^{\prime}
, where

H^{\flat}=\begin{pmatrix}1&&\\
&h&\\
&&1\end{pmatrix},\quad h\in\Sp_{2n-2},

and

U^{\prime}=\begin{pmatrix}1&a&b&c\\
&I_{n-1}&&J_{n-1}{}^{t}b\\
&&I_{n-1}&-J_{n-1}{}^{t}a\\
&&&1\end{pmatrix},\quad a,b\in F_{n-1},c\in F.

The form of
U^{\prime}
follows from the general computation that

\begin{pmatrix}I_{n-a}&X&Y&Z\\
&I_{a}&&W\\
&&I_{a}&T\\
&&&I_{n-a}\end{pmatrix}

is an element of the symplectic group if and only if:

T=-J_{a}{}^{t}XJ_{n-a},\quad W=J_{a}{}^{t}YJ_{n-a},\quad Z^{t}J_{n-a}+W^{t}J_{a}T-T^{t}J_{a}W-J_{n-a}Z=0.

Let
P^{\flat}
be the Klingen-mirabolic subgroup of
\GL_{2n}^{\flat}
and let
P^{\prime,\flat}
be the Klingen-mirabolic subgroup of
H^{\flat}
.

Note that
equation

U^{\prime}
is a normal subgroup of
U
and
U/U^{\prime}
is abelian.

Let
\mathrm{d}p^{\prime}
be the
right
Haar measure on
P^{\prime}(\mathbb{A})
. More concretely, we have

\int_{[P^{\prime}]}f(p^{\prime})\mathrm{d}p^{\prime}=\int_{[H^{\flat}]}\int_{[U^{\prime}]}f(u^{\prime}h)\mathrm{d}u^{\prime}\mathrm{d}h.

We say a parabolic subgroup
Q
of
\GL_{2n}
is
\theta
-stable
, if
\theta(Q)=Q
, where we recall
\theta
is the involution defined in (
2.5.1
). We say a smooth function
f
on
[P]
is
\theta
-cuspidal
if, for all
\theta
-stable parabolic subgroup
Q
of
\GL_{2n}
, the constant term

f_{Q}(p):=\int_{[N_{Q}]}f(np)\mathrm{d}n

vanishes. Note that
N_{Q}\subset P
so the integration above makes sense. More concretely, we have the following description:
equation

f
is
\theta
-cuspidal, if and only if
f_{N_{k,2n-2k,k}}=0
for all
k=1,2,\cdots,n
.

Lemma 4.1.1

(See also
[
MO21
]
)
.

Let
f\in\mathcal{S}([P])
be a
\theta
-cuspidal function on
[P]
, then we have

\int_{[P^{\prime}]}f(p^{\prime})\mathrm{d}p^{\prime}=0.

Proof.

For
p\in P(\mathbb{A})
, denote by
f_{U^{\prime}}
the function defined by

f_{U^{\prime}}(p):=\int_{[U^{\prime}]}f(u^{\prime}p)\mathrm{d}u^{\prime}.

By (
4.1
), for any
p\in P(\mathbb{A})
,
p\mapsto f_{U^{\prime}}(up)
defines a function on
[U/U^{\prime}]
.

Let
\psi_{n}
be the character on
[U/U^{\prime}]
defined by

\psi_{n}(u)=\psi(u_{12}+u_{n-1,n}),

where
u_{12}
and
u_{n-1,n}
denote the
(1,2)
and
(n-1,n)
entries of
u
respectively.

Note
U/U^{\prime}
is abelian, by Fourier expansion on
[U/U^{\prime}]
, we can write

(4.1.1)

f_{U^{\prime}}(p)=\sum_{\chi}f_{U^{\prime},\chi}(p),

where the Fourier coefficient
f_{U^{\prime},\chi}
is defined by

f_{U^{\prime},\chi}(p):=\int_{[U/U^{\prime}]}f_{U^{\prime}}(up)\chi^{-1}(u)\mathrm{d}u.

When
\psi=\mathbbm{1}
is the trivial character, then
f_{U^{\prime},\mathbbm{1}}(g)=f_{U}(g)=0
by the cuspidality assumption.

The nontrivial character on
[U/U^{\prime}]
can be written as
u\mapsto\psi_{n}(\gamma^{-1}u\gamma)
, where
\gamma\in H^{\flat}(F)
. Therefore, the equation (
4.1.1
) can be written as

f_{U^{\prime}}(p)=\sum_{\gamma\in P^{\prime,\flat}(F)\backslash H^{\flat}(F)}f_{U^{\prime},\psi_{n}}(\gamma p).

Therefore by (
4.1
)

\int_{[P^{\prime}]}f(p^{\prime})\mathrm{d}p^{\prime}=\int_{P^{\prime,\flat}(F)\backslash H^{\flat}(\mathbb{A})}f_{U^{\prime},\psi_{n}}(h)\mathrm{d}h=\int_{P^{\prime,\flat}(\mathbb{A})\backslash H^{\flat}(\mathbb{A})}\int_{[P^{\prime,\flat}]}f_{U^{\prime},\psi_{n}}(ph)\mathrm{d}p\mathrm{d}h.

One check easily that
f_{U^{\prime},\psi_{n}}
is a
\theta
-cuspidal function on
P^{\flat}
. Therefore, by induction on
n
, we only need to check the case when
n=1
, in which case the proposition is trivial.
∎

4.2.
Vanishing of symplectic period

For
g\in\GL_{2n}(\mathbb{A})
and
\Phi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{A}_{n})
, we put

\Theta^{\prime}(g,\Phi)=\lvert\det g\rvert^{\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{0\neq v\in F_{2n}}\Phi(vg).

Recall the Epstein series

E(g,\Phi,s)=\int_{A_{\GL_{2n}}^{\infty}}\Theta^{\prime}(ag,\Phi)\lvert\det ag\rvert^{s-\frac{1}{2}}\mathrm{d}a,\quad g\in\GL_{2n}(\mathbb{A}).

By
[
JS81
, Lemma 4.2]
, the integral defining
E(g,\Phi,s)
is absolutely convergent when
\operatorname{Re}(s)>1
and the map
s\mapsto E(\cdot,\Phi,s)
extends to a meromorphic function valued in
\mathcal{T}([\GL_{2n}])
with simple poles at
s=0,1
of respective residues
\Phi(0)
and
\widehat{\Phi}(0)
.

Lemma 4.2.1
.

Let
\varphi\in\mathcal{T}_{\operatorname{cusp}}([\GL_{2n}])
. Thus, for any
s\neq\{0,1\}
, we have

(4.2.1)

\int_{[\Sp_{2n}]}\varphi(h)E(h,\Phi,s)=0.

Proof.

The integral is absolutely convergent because
\varphi\in\mathcal{S}([\Sp_{2n}])
.

Recall that
P^{\prime}
denotes the Klingen-mirabolic subgroup of
H=\Sp_{2n}
. Let
e_{2n}=(0,\cdots,0,1)
; then
P^{\prime}
is the stabilizer of
e_{2n}
in
\Sp_{2n}
.

For
\operatorname{Re}(s)>1
, we put

F(g,\Phi,s)=\lvert\det g\rvert^{s}\int_{A_{\GL_{2n}}^{\infty}}\Phi(ae_{2n}g)\lvert\det a\rvert^{s}\mathrm{d}a.

The integral is absolutely convergent. Then for
\operatorname{Re}(s)>1
, we have

E(g,\Phi,s)=\sum_{\gamma\in P^{\prime}(F)\backslash\Sp_{2n}(F)}F(\gamma g,\Phi,s).

Therefore the integral (
4.2.1
) can be written as

\int_{P^{\prime}(F)\backslash\Sp_{2n}(\mathbb{A})}\varphi(h)F(h,\Phi,s)\mathrm{d}h.

Note that for all
p^{\prime}\in P^{\prime}(\mathbb{A})
and
h\in H(\mathbb{A})
, we have
F(p^{\prime}h,\Phi,s)=F(h,\Phi,s)
. The integral above is therefore equal to

\int_{P^{\prime}(\mathbb{A})\backslash\Sp_{2n}(\mathbb{A})}\int_{[P^{\prime}]}\varphi(p^{\prime}h)F(h,\Phi,s)\mathrm{d}p^{\prime}\mathrm{d}h,

which vanishes by lemma
4.1.1
. Since the integral (
4.2.1
) is meromorphic in
s
, we see that it vanishes for any
s\neq\{0,1\}
.
∎

Corollary 4.2.2
.

Let
\varphi\in\mathcal{T}_{\operatorname{cusp}}([\GL_{2n}])
, then

\int_{[\Sp_{2n}]}\varphi(h)\mathrm{d}h=0.

Proof.

Take
\Phi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{A}_{n})
such that
\Phi(0)=1
. Then, taking residue of (
4.2.1
) at
s=0
suffices.
∎

Remark 4.2.3
.

The proposition is also proved in
[
JR92a
]
; however, the proof applies to cuspidal automorphic forms, and the proposition above can be applied to any cuspidal function.

4.3.
Vanishing of certain
\Sp_{2n}\subset\GL_{2n+1}
-period

For any

, we define

\mathcal{P}_{H}(f)=\int_{[H]}f(h)\mathrm{d}h

if the integral is convergent.

Proposition 4.3.1
.

We have
\mathcal{P}_{H}(f)=0
if either

•

f\in\mathcal{T}_{\operatorname{cusp}}([G])
, or

•

f
is a pseudo-Eisenstein series

for

is a maximal parabolic subgroup not associated to

Proof.

By the formula for the constant term of pseudo-Eisenstein series (see (

Note that under the right action of

We then treat the case when

By (

The argument above shows that

5.1.
Classification of the orbits of

In this subsection, we give a complete classification of the double cosets

, or equivalently, the orbits of the
H(F)
-action on
P(F)\backslash G(F)
. Let
V_{0}=\langle e_{1},\cdots,e_{n}\rangle
be the standard
n
-dimensional subspace. Then the map

g\mapsto g^{-1}V_{0}
induces a bijection between
P(F)\backslash G(F)
and the set
\Gr_{n}:=\Gr(n,2n+1)(F)
of
n
-dimensional subspaces of
F^{2n+1}
. So the double coset
P(F)\backslash G(F)/H(F)
can be identified with the orbits of
\Gr_{n}(F)
under the action of
\Sp_{2n}(F)
. The orbits can be grouped into 4 different types, which we call type I, II, III, IV respectively. For each
\gamma\in P(F)\backslash G(F)
, we denote by
H_{\gamma}
the stabilizer of
\gamma
in
H(F)
.

Recall that
F^{2n}
denotes the subspace of
F^{2n+1}
with the last coordinate 0. Let
p:F^{2n+1}\to F^{2n}
denote the projection to the first
2n
coordinates. For a subspace
V\subset F^{2n+1}
, we denote by
i(V):=V\cap F^{2n}
and denote by
p(V)
the image of
V
under
p
. Let
W
be a subspace of
F^{2n}
, we denote by
r(W)
the Witt index of
W
.

(1)

(Type I orbit)
V\subset F^{2n}
, then by Witt’s theorem, the Witt index
r:=r(V)
is the only invariant of the
\Sp_{2n}(F)
orbit of
V
in
\Gr_{n}(F)
. In other words, if
r(V)=r(W)
for
W\in\Gr_{n}(F)
, then there exists
h\in\Sp_{2n}(F)
such that
h^{-1}V=W
. It’s clear

(2)

(Type II orbit)
e_{2n+1}\in V
. Then by Witt’s theorem again, the Witt index
r:=r(i(V))=r(p(V))
is the only invariant of the
\Sp_{2n}(F)
orbit of
V
in
\Gr_{n}(F)
. In this case,
r\in\{0,1,\cdots,[\frac{n-1}{2}]\}

(3)

(Type III and IV orbits)
e_{2n+1}\not\in V
and
V\not\subset F^{2n}
. In this case, we have
\dim p(V)=\dim i(V)+1
. Type III and IV orbits correspond to whether
r(p(V))=r(i(V))
or
r(p(V))=r(i(V))+1
respectively. Consider the flag
0\subset i(V)\subset p(V)\subset V
, it is easy to apply the next lemma to see that

•

For
V
as above. The subspace
V
with

form a single
\Sp_{2n}(F)
orbit. The possible values of

are the set

•

Similarly, for

[

Let

equation

6.
Proof of the main result

In this section, we prove our main results (theorem
6.3.1
) concerning
\Sp_{2n}
-periods of cusp forms and cuspidal Eisenstein series attached to maximal parabolic subgroups. For convenience, we only treat the number field case; the function field case follows from a similar (indeed easier) argument. Recall that
P=P_{n,n+1}
is the maximal parabolic subgroup of type
(n,n+1)
of
\GL_{2n+1}
.

6.1.
Intertwining period

For
\varphi\in\mathcal{T}([G]_{P})
and
\lambda\in\mathfrak{a}^{*}_{P,\mathbb{C}}
, if convergent, we define the intertwining period of
\varphi
as

J(\varphi,\lambda)=\int_{[H]_{P^{\prime}}}\varphi(h)e^{\langle\lambda,H_{P}(h)\rangle}\mathrm{d}h.

We put
J(\varphi):=J(\varphi,0)
.

Lemma 6.1.1
.

If
\varphi\in\mathcal{T}_{\operatorname{cusp}}([G]_{P})
, then integral defining
J(\varphi,\lambda)
is absolutely convergent for any
\lambda\in\mathfrak{a}_{P,\mathbb{C}}^{*}
.

Proof.

Replacing
\varphi(h)
by
\varphi\cdot e^{\langle\lambda,H_{P}(\cdot)\rangle}
, we can assume
\lambda=0
. Since
\varphi\in\mathcal{T}_{\operatorname{cusp}}([G]_{P})
,
\varphi|_{[H]_{P^{\prime}}}\in\mathcal{S}([H]_{P^{\prime}})
(see (
2.3
)) the result follows.
∎

We can also relate the intertwining period to Whittaker functions. Denote the upper triangular unipotent subgroups of
\GL_{i}
by
N_{i}
. Let
\psi_{n,n+1}
be the character on
[N_{n}]\times[N_{n+1}]
defined by

\psi_{n,n+1}(u)=\psi(u_{1,2}+\cdots+u_{n-1,n}+u_{n+1,n+2}+\cdots+u_{2n,2n+1}).

For
\varphi\in\mathcal{T}([G]_{P})
, we define

W_{\varphi}(g)=\int_{[N_{n}]\times[N_{n+1}]}\varphi(ug)\psi_{n,n+1}(u)\mathrm{d}u.

By the usual expansion into Whittaker functions, recall that
N_{2n+1}^{\prime}:=N_{2n+1}\cap H
, we can write

(6.1.1)

\begin{split}J(\varphi,\lambda)&=\int_{P^{\prime}(\mathbb{A})\backslash H(\mathbb{A})}\int_{N_{n}(\mathbb{A})\backslash\GL_{n}(\mathbb{A})}W_{R(h)\varphi}\begin{pmatrix}J{}^{t}m^{-1}J&&\\
&m&\\
&&1\end{pmatrix}\lvert\det m\rvert^{n+1+s_{\lambda}}e^{\langle\lambda,H_{P}(h)\rangle}\mathrm{d}m\mathrm{d}h\\
&=\int_{N_{2n+1}^{\prime}(\mathbb{A})\backslash H(\mathbb{A})}W_{\varphi}(h)e^{\langle\lambda,H_{P}(h)\rangle}\mathrm{d}h,\end{split}

when
\mathrm{Re}(s_{\lambda})\gg 0
, where
s_{\lambda}\in\mathbb{C}
is determined by

\exp\left(\langle\lambda,H_{P}\begin{pmatrix}J{}^{t}m^{-1}J&&\\
&m&\\
&&1\end{pmatrix}\rangle\right)=\lvert\det m\rvert^{s_{\lambda}}.

6.2.
Period of pseudo-Eisenstein series

Proposition 6.2.1
.

Let
\varphi\in\mathcal{S}([G]_{P})
and let
E(\cdot,\varphi)
be the pseudo-Eisenstein series. Then

\int_{[H]}E(h,\varphi)\mathrm{d}h=J(\varphi)

Proof.

Since
\varphi\in\mathcal{S}([G]_{P})
, we have
\lvert\varphi\rvert\in\mathcal{S}^{00}([G]_{P})
. Hence the expression

\int_{[H]}\sum_{\gamma\in P(F)\backslash G(F)}\lvert\varphi(\gamma h)\rvert\mathrm{d}h

is finite. Let
[P(F)\backslash G(F)/H(F)]
denote an arbitrary representative of the double coset
P(F)\backslash G(F)/H(F)
. Therefore

\begin{split}\int_{[H]}E(h,\varphi)\mathrm{d}h&=\sum_{\gamma\in[P(F)\backslash G(F)/H(F)]}\int_{H_{\gamma}(F)\backslash H(\mathbb{A})}\varphi(\gamma h)\mathrm{d}h\\
=&\sum_{\gamma\in[P(F)\backslash G(F)/H(F)]}\int_{H_{\gamma}(\mathbb{A})\backslash H(\mathbb{A})}\int_{[H_{\gamma}]}\varphi(\gamma h\gamma^{-1}\gamma g)\mathrm{d}h\mathrm{d}g\end{split}

Note that
\gamma h\gamma^{-1}\in P
. In §
5.2
, we give a complete list of one choice of
[P(F)\backslash G(F)/H(F)]
. When the orbit is not the main orbit (see (
5.2.1
)), we list a normal subgroup
S_{\gamma}\cdot U_{\gamma}
and compute the explicit form of
\gamma h\gamma^{-1}
for
h
in
S_{\gamma}
or
U_{\gamma}
respectively. Using cuspidality or corollary
4.3.3
, the integral on this normal subgroup already vanishes. Therefore, only the main orbit contributes, which gives

\int_{P^{\prime}(F)\backslash H(\mathbb{A})}\varphi(h)\mathrm{d}h=J(\varphi).

∎

6.3.
Period of cuspidal Eisenstein series

In this subsection, we prove the main theorem.

Theorem 6.3.1
.

Let
Q
be a maximal parabolic subgroup of
G
and let
\varphi\in\mathcal{A}_{Q,\operatorname{cusp}}(G)
. Then

(1)

If
Q
is not associate to
P
, then
\mathcal{P}_{H}(E(\cdot,\varphi,\lambda))=0
for any
\lambda
such that
E(\cdot,\varphi,\lambda)
is regular.

(2)

If
Q=P
, then for any
\lambda
such that
E(\cdot,\varphi,\lambda)
is regular,

\mathcal{P}_{H}(E(\cdot,\varphi,\lambda))=J(\varphi,\lambda).

Proof.

By meromorphicity, we only need to check both (1) and (2) within the convergence domain of the Eisenstein series.

For
\varphi\in\mathcal{A}_{P,\operatorname{cusp}}(G)
and
\beta\in\operatorname{PW}(i\mathfrak{a}_{P}^{*})
(see (
2.2.7
)). Let

\varphi^{\prime}(g)=\int_{i\mathfrak{a}_{P}^{*}}e^{\langle\lambda,H_{P}(g)\rangle}\beta(\lambda)\varphi(g)\mathrm{d}\lambda.

Then
\varphi^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}_{\operatorname{cusp}}([G]_{P})
. Moreover, for
\lambda_{0}\in\mathfrak{a}_{P}^{*}
sufficiently positive, we have

E(\varphi^{\prime},g)=\int_{\lambda_{0}+i\mathfrak{a}_{P}^{*}}E(g,\varphi,\lambda)\beta(\lambda)\mathrm{d}\lambda.

Note that
E(h,\varphi,\lambda)
forms a bounded set in
\mathcal{T}([G])
as
\lambda
varies in
\lambda_{0}+i\mathfrak{a}_{P}^{*}
. By theorem
3.2.2
and lemma
6.1.1
, both the integrals

\int_{[H]}\int_{\lambda_{0}+i\mathfrak{a}_{P}^{*}}\lvert E(h,\varphi,\lambda)\beta(\lambda)\rvert\mathrm{d}\lambda\mathrm{d}h.

and

\int_{\lambda_{0}+i\mathfrak{a}_{P}^{*}}\int_{[H]_{P^{\prime}}}\lvert\varphi(h)\beta(\lambda)\rvert e^{\langle\lambda_{0},H_{P}(g)\rangle}\mathrm{d}h\mathrm{d}\lambda

are convergent. By proposition
6.2.1
, we then see that

\int_{\lambda_{0}+i\mathfrak{a}_{P}^{*}}\int_{[H]}E(h,\varphi,\lambda)\beta(\lambda)\mathrm{d}\lambda=J(\varphi^{\prime},\lambda)=\int_{\lambda_{0}+i\mathfrak{a}_{P}^{*}}J(\varphi,\lambda)\beta(\lambda)\mathrm{d}\lambda.

Since
\beta
is arbitrary, (2) holds. The proof of (1) is similar (and indeed easier).
∎

6.4.
Proof of Theorem
1.1.3

By lemma
6.1.1
, let
\Pi=\Pi_{n}\otimes\Pi_{n+1}
be a cuspidal automorphic representation of
\GL_{n}(\mathbb{A})\times\GL_{n+1}(\mathbb{A})
with central character trivial on
A_{\GL_{n}}^{\infty}\times A_{\GL_{n+1}}^{\infty}
, if
\varphi\in\mathcal{A}_{P,\Pi}
, by theorem
6.3.1
and (
6.1.1
), we have

(6.4.1)

\mathcal{P}_{H}(E(\cdot,\varphi,\lambda))=\int_{N_{2n+1}^{\prime}(\mathbb{A})\backslash H(\mathbb{A})}W_{\varphi}(h)e^{\langle\lambda,H_{P}(h)\rangle}\mathrm{d}h,

for
\lambda\in\mathfrak{a}_{P,\mathbb{C}}^{*}
such that
\mathrm{Re}(s_{\lambda})\gg 0
.

Fix a decomposition
\Pi=\otimes^{\prime}_{v}\Pi_{v}
,
\psi=\otimes_{v}\psi_{v}
and assume
\varphi=\otimes^{\prime}_{v}\varphi_{v}
is a pure tensor. Here each
\varphi_{v}
lies in the local parabolic induction
\Ind_{P(F_{v})}^{G(F_{v})}\Pi_{v}
. Note the additive character
\psi_{n,n+1}
we defined before can be decomposed as
\prod_{v}\psi_{n,n+1,v}
, where each
\psi_{n,n+1,v}
is the additive character on
N_{n}(F_{v})\times N_{n+1}(F_{v})
. Let
\mathcal{W}(\Pi_{v},\psi_{v})
denote the Whittaker model of the generic representation
\varphi_{v}
with respect to
\psi_{n,n+1,v}
. We regard the parabolic induction
\Ind_{P(F_{v})}^{G(F_{v})}\mathcal{W}(\Pi_{v},\psi_{v})
as the spaces of functions
f:G(F_{v})\to\mathbb{C}
such that for any
g\in G(F_{v})
,

\delta_{P}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(m)f(mg),\quad m\in M(F_{v})=\GL_{n}(F_{v})\times\GL_{n+1}(F_{v})

lies in
\mathcal{W}(\Pi_{v},\psi_{v})
as a function on
\GL_{n}(F_{v})\times\GL_{n+1}(F_{v})
. Due to the uniqueness of the Whittaker model, we have

(6.4.2)

W_{\varphi}(g)=\prod_{v}W_{\varphi_{v}}(g_{v}),\quad g=(g_{v})

where
W_{\varphi_{v}}
denotes the vector in
\Ind_{P(F_{v})}^{G(F_{v})}\mathcal{W}(\Pi_{v},\psi_{v})
associated to
\varphi_{v}
. For any local place
v
and
\varphi_{v}\in\Ind_{P(F_{v})}^{G(F_{v})}\Pi_{v}
, we define the local period integral

\mathcal{P}_{H,v}(\varphi_{v},\lambda)=\int_{N_{2n+1}^{\prime}(F_{v})\backslash H(F_{v})}W_{\varphi_{v}}(h_{v})e^{\langle\lambda,H_{P}(h_{v})\rangle}\mathrm{d}h_{v}.

By (
6.4.1
) and (
6.4.2
), we have

\mathcal{P}_{H}(E(\cdot,\varphi,\lambda))=(\Delta_{H}^{*})^{-1}\prod_{v}\mathcal{P}_{H,v}(\varphi_{v},\lambda).

Unfold the local period integral, and we can see

\begin{split}\mathcal{P}_{H,v}(\varphi_{v},\lambda)=\int_{P^{\prime}(F_{v})\backslash H(F_{v})}\int_{N_{n}(F_{v})\backslash\GL_{n}(F_{v})}&W_{R(h_{v})\varphi_{v}}\begin{pmatrix}J{}^{t}m_{v}^{-1}J&&\\
&m_{v}&\\
&&1\end{pmatrix}\\
&|\det m_{v}\rvert^{n+1+s_{\lambda}}e^{\langle\lambda,H_{P}(h_{v})\rangle}\mathrm{d}m_{v}\mathrm{d}h_{v}.\end{split}

This integral converges absolutely for
\lambda
such that
\mathrm{Re}(s_{\lambda})\gg 0
, and extends meromorphically to all
\lambda\in\mathfrak{a}_{P,\mathbb{C}}^{*}
.

Let
W(\varphi_{v})
denote the vector in
\mathcal{W}(\Pi_{v},\psi_{v})

is a bijection on points and induces a stratification of

. Denote

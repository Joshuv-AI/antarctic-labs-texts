Emergency Medical Services Encounters Preceding Injurious Falls in Older Adults
Authors: Matthew R. Shaw, Iv Godzdanker, Remle P. Crowe, Ali Treichel, Manish N. Shah, Rebecca E. Cash
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
This cohort study characterizes emergency medical services (EMS) encounters in the year prior to diagnosis of a fall-related injury that limits activities of daily living among adults aged 60 years and older.

Introduction

Falls are a leading cause of injury among adults aged 65 years and older in the US, accounting for over $80 billion in health care costs1 with potential catastrophic health consequences.2,3 Emergency medical services (EMS) serve a critical role in the continuum of care for fall-related injuries. With an estimated 10% to 25% of all EMS calls related to falls among older adults,4 EMS professionals are well positioned to identify those who fall frequently.5

The time between a fall resulting in an injury that limits activities of daily living (IL-ADL) and prior EMS encounters may represent a window of opportunity to prevent future falls. Using a unique longitudinal EMS dataset to identify repeat patient encounters, our primary objective was to characterize EMS encounters among patients aged 60 years and older within a 365-day period prior to an emergency department (ED) diagnosis of an IL-ADL due to a ground-level fall.

Methods

We performed a retrospective cohort study using the 2018-2024 ESO Data Collaborative deidentified research dataset with linked ED outcome data. We identified index 911 EMS transports to an ED for a fall between January 1, 2019, to December 31, 2024, for patients aged 60 years or older with an ED discharge diagnosis of IL-ADL including community-dwelling older adults and those living in residential facilities (eTable in Supplement 1). IL-ADL were defined based on common injury patterns6 that would limit fundamental skills required to manage basic day-to-day needs.7 A masked patient identifier enabled longitudinal tracking of unique patients across EMS encounters. This study was approved by the Northern Arizona Healthcare institutional review board. STROBE reporting guidelines were followed.

Our primary outcome was number of EMS encounters in the 365 days prior to the first transport with IL-ADL (index transport). Utilization was characterized as a binary exposure (at least 1 prior EMS encounter vs none) and, among those with a prior EMS encounter, as frequency strata (1, 2, 3, and 4 or more encounters). Secondary outcomes included time from most recent EMS encounter to index transport and associations between patient characteristics and 1 or more prior EMS encounters. Exposure to medications that could increase fall risk were also assessed using the American Geriatrics Society Beers Criteria. For further details on the dataset, masked patient identifier, and inclusion and exclusion criteria, see eMethods in Supplement 1.

Patient demographic, clinical, and encounter characteristics were summarized using descriptive statistics bivariate comparisons (Wilcoxon rank-sum tests and χ2 tests; tests were 2-sided with α = .05 indicating statistical significance) conducted between patients with 1 or more prior EMS encounter vs none. Statistical analyses were performed using StataMP version 18.5 (StataCorp LLC).

Results

A total of 267 972 individuals met inclusion criteria (median [IQR] age, 80 [71-87] years; 176 778 female [66.0%]). Overall, 87 985 participants (33%) had at least 1 EMS encounter prior to the index transport, representing a total of 240 057 EMS encounters, with 39 729 participants (45.2%) having 1 prior encounter (Table 1). The median (IQR) time from the previous EMS encounter to the index transport was 78 (26-176) days (Table 2); 23 409 participants (26.6%) had an EMS encounter within 30 days, 36 500 (41.5%) within 60 days, and 45 894 (52.2%) within 90 days. A smaller but clinically meaningful subset (8217 [9.5%]) had an encounter within 7 days. IL-ADL diagnosis patterns and admissions rates were similar between patients with and without prior EMS encounters (Table 2). Patients with at least 1 prior EMS encounter were more likely to live in skilled nursing facilities (30.7% vs 15.7%) and have 1 or more Beers criteria medications listed in their medical history (36.9% vs 22.6%).

Discussion

One in 3 older adults who sustained an injurious ground-level fall had contacted EMS at least once in the prior year. We identified an overall potential intervention window of approximately 11 weeks, which represents a feasible timeframe to implement fall prevention strategies with the aim of avoiding unnecessary injury and potential disability.

Although the importance of fall prevention is well established, the optimal intervention strategy and target population remain uncertain.8,9 The interval data offer meaningful direction. Among patients with a single prior EMS encounter, the median time before injurious fall was approximately 100 days, a window that narrowed to 36 days among those with 4 or more encounters. These results suggest that risk trajectory is already advanced by the time repeated contact occurs, arguing for intervention at the earliest encounter rather than the most recent. Taken together, these findings argue against a single high-risk subgroup as the optimal prevention target and instead support a universal, encounter-triggered approach; each EMS contact with an older adult represents a discrete opportunity to initiate screening, referral, or direct intervention.

Limitations include the observational design of this analysis, which cannot assess causal pathways. Additionally, the dataset is limited to patients transported by EMS, therefore we were unable to capture individuals experiencing falls who did not activate EMS, including those who may have arrived at the ED by other means. The patterns identified here highlight potential windows of intervention with medically complex older adults that warrant further prospective study to determine which encounters are most amenable to targeted prevention strategies.

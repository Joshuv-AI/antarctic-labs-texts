A simple construction of the sine-Gordon model via stochastic quantization
Massimiliano Gubinelli , Martin Hairer , Tadahiro Oh , and Younes Zine

Abstract
. We present a simple PDE construction of the sine-Gordon measure below the first threshold ( β 2 < 4 ​ π \beta^{2}<4\pi ), in both the finite and infinite volume settings, by studying the corresponding parabolic sine-Gordon model. We also establish pathwise global well-posedness of the hyperbolic sine-Gordon model in finite volume for β 2 < 2 ​ π \beta^{2}<2\pi .

Introduction

1.1.
Sine-Gordon model

The Euclidean sine-Gordon field theory (with unit mass) on the periodic box

\mathbb{T}_{L}^{2}=(\mathbb{R}/2\pi L\mathbb{Z})^{2}
,
L\geq 1

is defined by
the sine-Gordon measure on

\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{T}_{L}^{2})
with a formal density:

(1.1)

\displaystyle\begin{split}d\rho_{L}^{\beta}(u)&=``Z_{L}^{-1}\exp\bigg(\frac{\gamma}{\beta}\int_{\mathbb{T}_{L}^{2}}\cos\big(\beta u(x)\big)dx\\
&\hphantom{XXXXXX}-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}_{L}^{2}}u(x)^{2}dx-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}_{L}^{2}}|\nabla u(x)|^{2}dx\bigg)du",\end{split}

where

\beta,\gamma\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}

and
Z_{L}=Z(\beta,\gamma,L)>0
is a normalization constant.
In the current finite volume setting, the measure (
1.1

0<\beta^{2}<8\pi

Our main goal in this note is to present

\rho^{\beta}

via a stochastic quantization approach
in the restricted range
0<\beta^{2}<4\pi

A naive attempt
to define the sine-Gordon measure
\rho^{\beta}_{L}

is to view it as a weighted Gaussian measure:

\displaystyle d\rho^{\beta}_{L}(u)=``Z_{L}^{-1}\exp\bigg(\frac{\gamma}{\beta}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}_{L}}\cos\big(\beta u(x)\big)dx\bigg)d\mu_{L}(u)",

where
\mu_{L}
denotes the massive Gaussian free field on
\mathbb{T}^{2}_{L}

Namely,
\mu_{L}
is a Gaussian measure on
L
-periodic distributions
with the covariance operator
(1-\Delta)^{-1}
.
Unfortunately,
\mu_{L}
on
\mathbb{T}^{2}_{L}
is supported on
distributions of negative regularity
[
22
]

and thus we cannot make sense of
\cos(\beta u)
for general
u\in\supp(\mu_{L})
.

Let us make this last observation more precise.
Fix
L\geq 1
. Given
N\in\mathbb{N}
, let
\mathbf{\Pi}_{N}=\mathbf{\Pi}_{N}^{L}
be a smooth frequency projector
onto the frequencies
\{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{L}^{2}:|n|\lesssim N\}

defined by

(1.2)

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathbf{\Pi}_{N}f}(n)=\chi_{N}(n)\widehat{f}(n),\quad n\in\mathbb{Z}_{L}^{2}:=(\mathbb{Z}/L)^{2},

where

\chi_{N}(n)=\chi(N^{-1}n)
for a fixed
function

\chi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2};[0,1])

such that

\widecheck{\chi}\equiv 1

on
\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:|x|\leq\tfrac{1}{2}\}

and
\supp(\widecheck{\chi})\subset\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:|x|\leq 1\}

Then, a variant of Proposition
2.5
below
(see
[
31

we have, for any test function

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\mu_{L}}[\langle\cos(\mathbf{\Pi}_{N}u),\phi\rangle^{2}]\lesssim N^{-\frac{\beta^{2}}{4\pi}}\longrightarrow 0,

as

,
where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
denotes the usual duality pairing between distribution and test function.
Thus, we expect that the frequency-truncated sine-Gordon measure:

\displaystyle Z_{L,N}^{-1}\exp\bigg(\frac{\gamma_{L}}{\beta}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}_{L}}\cos\big(\beta\mathbf{\Pi}_{N}u(x)\big)dx\bigg)d\mu_{L}(u)

In order to circumvent this issue,

1.2.
Parabolic sine-Gordon model

Let
L\geq 1
and consider
the following stochastic nonlinear heat equation (SNLH) on
\mathbb{T}_{L}^{2}

with a sine nonlinearity:

(1.5)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u_{L}+(1-\Delta)u_{L}+\gamma\sin(\beta u_{L})=\sqrt{2}\xi_{L},\qquad(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{T}_{L}^{2},

where
u_{L}
is a real-valued unknown and
\xi_{L}
denotes a space-time white noise on
\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{T}_{L}^{2}
. Namely,
\xi_{L}
is a distribution-valued Gaussian process with covariance given by

(1.6)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big[\langle\xi_{L},\phi\rangle\langle\xi_{L},\psi\rangle\big]=\langle\phi,\psi\rangle_{L^{2}_{t,x}},

for any
\phi,\psi\in\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{T}^{2}_{L})
. Here,
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
denotes the
\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{T}^{2}_{L})-\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{T}^{2}_{L})
duality pairing.

The dynamical model (
1.5
) corresponds to the so-called
stochastic quantization

[
35
]

of the quantum sine-Gordon model represented by the measure
\rho_{L}^{\beta}
in (
1.1
)
in the sense that the sine-Gordon measure
\rho_{L}^{\beta}

is formally invariant under the dynamics generated by (
1.5
).
For this reason, we refer
to (
1.5
)
as the
parabolic sine-Gordon model
in the following.

In
[
31
,
13
]
,
the second author with Chandra and Shen
observed that
the difficulty of the problem depends sensitively on the value of
\beta^{2}>0

(see, for example, Proposition
2.5
)
and showed that
there is an infinite number of thresholds:

\beta^{2}=\frac{j}{j+1}8\pi
,
j\in\mathbb{N}
,
where one encounters new divergent stochastic objects, requiring further renormalizations.
By adapting the theory of regularity structures
[
29
]

to the non-polynomial setting,
they
proved local well-posedness of (
1.5
) (for sufficiently smooth initial data)
in the full subcritical range
0<\beta^{2}<8\pi
.

Let us describe how to study the dynamical problem (
1.5
),
at least below the first threshold
0<\beta^{2}<4\pi
. Due to the roughness of the space-time white noise
\xi
,
a solution
u(t)
for any fixed
t>0
is merely a distribution and
thus we cannot make sense of the nonlinearity
\sin(\beta u)
in (
1.5
).
We overcome this issue by
transferring the renormalization at the level of the measure
in (
1.4
)
to a renormalization at the level of the dynamical problem.
Given
N\in\mathbb{N}
, consider the following
frequency-truncated equation:

(1.7)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u_{L,N}+(1-\Delta)u_{L,N}+\gamma_{L,N}\mathbf{\Pi}_{N}\sin(\beta\mathbf{\Pi}_{N}u_{L,N})=\sqrt{2}\xi_{L},

where
\gamma_{L,N}
is as in (
1.3
).
Then,
using the first order expansion (see (
1.9
) below)
along with the basic results in Section
2
,
one can easily show
that there exist an almost surely positive random time
T>0
and a non-trivial distribution

u_{L}\in C([0,T];\mathcal{C}^{-\delta}(\mathbb{T}_{L}^{2}))
,
\delta>0
, such that

u_{L,N}
converges
in probability
to
u_{L}

in

C([0,T];\mathcal{C}^{-\delta}(\mathbb{T}_{L}^{2}))
as
N\to\infty
.
The limit
u_{L}
is then interpreted as the solution to the (formal) renormalized version of (
1.5
):

(1.8)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u_{L}+(1-\Delta)u_{L}+\infty\cdot\sin(\beta u_{L})=\sqrt{2}\xi_{L}.

This is what we mean by pathwise local well-posedness
of the parabolic sine-Gordon model (
1.8
).

2.1.
Notations

We write
A\lesssim B
to denote an estimate of the form
A\leq CB

for some
C>0
.
We write
A\sim B
to denote
A\lesssim B
and
B\lesssim A
.
We may use subscripts to denote dependence on external parameters; for example,

A\lesssim_{\theta}B
means
A\leq C(\theta)B
.

Let
L\geq 1
. For
x\in\mathbb{T}^{2}_{L}\cong[-L\pi,L\pi)^{2}
,
we set

|x|_{L}=\min_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}|x+2\pi Lm|_{\mathbb{R}^{2}},

where
|\cdot|_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}
denotes the standard Euclidean distance on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.

Fix
L\geq 1
. We define the Fourier transform
\widehat{f}(n)
(or
\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}(f)(n)
depending on the context) of a function
f
on
\mathbb{T}^{2}_{L}

by

(2.1)

\displaystyle\widehat{f}(n)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}_{L}}f(x)e^{-in\cdot x}dx,\quad n\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}_{L},

with the associated Fourier series expansion:

\displaystyle f(x)=\frac{1}{2\pi L^{2}}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{L}^{2}}\widehat{f}(n)e^{in\cdot x}.

Note that we have

(2.2)

\displaystyle\widehat{f*_{L}g}(n)=2\pi\widehat{f}(n)\widehat{g}(n)

for any
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{L}^{2}
, where
*_{L}
denotes the convolution on
\mathbb{T}^{2}_{L}

defined by

f*_{L}g(x)=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}_{L}}f(x-y)g(y)dy.

Given a function
f
on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
,
we define its Fourier transform

\mathcal{F}(f)
(or
\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(f)
depending on the context) by

(2.3)

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(f)(\xi)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}f(x)e^{-i\xi\cdot x}dx.

With this convention, we have

(2.4)

\displaystyle\begin{split}f(x)&=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\mathcal{F}(f)(\xi)e^{i\xi\cdot x}d\xi,\\
\mathcal{F}(f*g)(\xi)&=2\pi\mathcal{F}(f)(\xi)\mathcal{F}(g)(\xi).\end{split}

In the following,
we introduce
the Littlewood-Paley frequency projectors
for

2\pi L
-periodic functions,
L\geq 1

via
the Poisson summation formula
so that they are consistent for different values of
L\geq 1
.
Let
F
be a continuous function on
\mathbb{R}^{2}

such that (i) there exists
\delta>0
such that

|F(x)|\lesssim(1+|x|)^{-2-\delta}

for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
,

\mathbb{Z}^{2}_{L}

\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}_{L}}|\mathcal{F}(F)(n)|<\infty.

Define a function
f
on
\mathbb{T}_{L}^{2}\cong[-\pi L,\pi L)^{2}

(2.5)

\displaystyle f(x)=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}F(x+2\pi mL).

Then, from (
2.1
), and (
2.3
),
we have

(2.6)

\displaystyle\widehat{f}(n)=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}_{L}}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}F(x+2\pi mL)\frac{e^{-in\cdot x}}{2\pi}dx=\mathcal{F}(F)(n)

n\in\mathbb{Z}_{L}^{2}
.
In particular,
the Poisson summation formula

\mathbb{T}^{2}_{L}

Hyperbolic sine-Gordon model

In this appendix,

We first go over the renormalization procedure for (

)

Let

Then, we have

(A.1)

\displaystyle\Psi^{\textup{wave}}(t)

\displaystyle=\sqrt{2}\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{D}(t-t^{\prime})\xi(dt^{\prime}),

\displaystyle Y^{1-\alpha}(T):=C([0,T];H^{1-\alpha}(\mathbb{T}^{2}))\cap C^{1}([0,T];H^{-\alpha}(\mathbb{T}^{2})).

Moreover, the solution map
:

(u_{0},u_{1},\Theta^{+},\Theta^{-})\mapsto v
is continuous.

Suppose that, given any
T>0
,

\Theta^{\kappa}\in L^{\infty}([0,T];W^{-\alpha,\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{2}))
,
\kappa\in\{+,-\}
.
Then, it follows from Lemma
A.3

that (
A.6
) is globally well-posed,
if

(A.7)

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\|(v(t),\partial_{t}v(t))\|_{\mathcal{H}^{1-\alpha}}<\infty

for each finite
T>0
.
In the following, we show that (
A.7
) indeed holds
for
0<\alpha<\frac{1}{2}
,
from which we easily deduce Theorem
A.1

by setting

\alpha=\frac{\beta^{2}}{4\pi}+\delta
.

Before proceeding further,
we state a preliminary lemma.

Lemma A.4
.

(i)

Given
s\in\mathbb{R}
,
let
u
be a solution to the linear damped wave equation on
\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{T}^{2}
:

(A.8)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{t}^{2}u+\partial_{t}u+(1-\Delta)u=f\\
(u,\partial_{t}u)|_{t=0}=(u_{0},u_{1})\in\mathcal{H}^{s}(\mathbb{T}^{2}).\end{cases}

Then,
for any
T>0
,
we have

(A.9)

\displaystyle\|u\|_{C_{T}H^{s}_{x}}+\|\partial_{t}u\|_{C_{T}H^{s-1}_{x}}\lesssim\|(u_{0},u_{1})\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s}}+\|f\|_{L^{1}_{T}H^{s-1}_{x}}.

(ii)

(fractional Leibniz rule).

Let
s>0
and

1<p_{j},q_{j},r\leq\infty
with
\frac{1}{p_{j}}+\frac{1}{q_{j}}=\frac{1}{r}
,
j=1,2
. Then, we have

\displaystyle\big\|\langle\nabla\rangle^{s}(fg)\big\|_{L^{r}(\mathbb{T}^{2})}\lesssim\big\|\langle\nabla\rangle^{s}f\big\|_{L^{p_{1}}(\mathbb{T}^{2})}\|g\|_{L^{q_{1}}(\mathbb{T}^{2})}+\|f\|_{L^{p_{2}}(\mathbb{T}^{2})}\big\|\langle\nabla\rangle^{s}g\big\|_{L^{q_{2}}(\mathbb{T}^{2})}.

(iii)

Let
s>0
,
1<p\leq\infty
, and

1<q,r<\infty
such that
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}\leq\frac{1}{r}+\frac{s}{2}

and
q,r^{\prime}\geq p^{\prime}
. Then, we have

\displaystyle\big\|\langle\nabla\rangle^{-s}(fg)\big\|_{L^{r}(\mathbb{T}^{2})}\lesssim\big\|\langle\nabla\rangle^{-s}f\big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{T}^{2})}\big\|\langle\nabla\rangle^{s}g\big\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{T}^{2})}.

(iv)

(fractional chain rule).

Let
F
be a Lipschitz function on
\mathbb{R}
with Lipschitz constant
L
. Then, given
0<s<1
and
1<p<\infty
, we have

\displaystyle\big\||\nabla|^{s}F(f)\big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{T}^{2})}\lesssim L\big\||\nabla|^{s}f\big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{T}^{2})}

for any
f\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
.

The energy estimate (
A.9
) is obvious from
writing (
A.8
) in the Duhamel formulation.
See
[
7
]
for (ii) and (iii);
see also
[
27
]
.
As for (iv), see
[
21
]
.

We now prove (
A.7
).
Given
T\geq 1
,
let
v\in Y^{1-\alpha}(T)
be a solution to (
A.6
) on the time interval
[0,T]
.
Then,
by
Lemma
A.4
,
we have

(A.10)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|v\|_{Y^{1-\alpha}(T)}&\lesssim\|(u_{0},u_{1})\|_{\mathcal{H}^{1-\alpha}}+\sum_{\kappa\in\{+,-\}}\|f^{\kappa}(v)\Theta^{\kappa}\|_{L^{1}_{T}H^{-\alpha}_{x}}\\
&\lesssim\|(u_{0},u_{1})\|_{\mathcal{H}^{1-\alpha}}+T\sum_{\kappa\in\{+,-\}}\|f^{\kappa}(v)\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}H^{\alpha}_{x}}\|\Theta^{\kappa}\|_{C_{T}W^{-\alpha,\infty}_{x}}.\end{split}

On the other hand,
by interpolation, Lemma
A.4
(iv),
and the boundedness of
f^{\kappa}(v)
,
we have

(A.11)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|f^{\kappa}(v)\|_{H^{\alpha}}&\lesssim\|f^{\kappa}(v)\|_{H^{(1-\theta)^{-1}\alpha}}^{1-\theta}\|f^{\kappa}(v)\|_{L^{2}}^{\theta}\\
&\lesssim\|v\|_{H^{(1-\theta)^{-1}\alpha}}^{1-\theta}\leq\|v\|_{H^{1-\alpha}}^{1-\theta}\end{split}

for
\kappa\in\{+,-\}
, provided that
\theta>0
is sufficiently small such that

\displaystyle(1-\theta)^{-1}\alpha<{1-\alpha},

which is possible since
0<\alpha<\frac{1}{2}
.
Hence, from (
A.10
), (
A.11
), and Young’s inequality,
we obtain

\displaystyle\|v\|_{Y^{1-\alpha}(T)}

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}\|(u_{0},u_{1})\|_{\mathcal{H}^{1-\alpha}}+C_{1}T\sum_{\kappa\in\{+,-\}}\|\Theta^{\kappa}\|_{C_{T}W^{-\alpha,\infty}_{x}}\|v\|_{Y^{1-\alpha}(T)}^{1-\theta}

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}\|(u_{0},u_{1})\|_{\mathcal{H}^{1-\alpha}}+C_{2}(T)\sum_{\kappa\in\{+,-\}}\|\Theta^{\kappa}\|_{C_{T}W_{x}^{-\alpha,\infty}}^{\frac{1}{\theta}}+\frac{1}{2}\|v\|_{Y^{1-\alpha}(T)}

for some
C_{1},C_{2}(T)>0
.
This proves (
A.7
)
(and hence Theorem
A.1
).

Remark A.5
.

It is natural to wonder if we can use
the hyperbolic sine-Gordon model
to
implement a PDE construction of the sine-Gordon measure
\rho^{\beta}

as in the proof of Theorem
1.1
.
Unfortunately,
due to the lack of smoothing
under the homogeneous linear damped wave propagator,
this is not possible with the techniques presented in this note.

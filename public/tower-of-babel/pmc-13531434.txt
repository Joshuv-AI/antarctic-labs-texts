Burden of ischemic stroke in China from 1990 to 2050: A systematic review and analysis of Global Burden of Disease Study 2021
Authors: Yijun Zhang, Jinqiao Zhu, Xue Xia, Meng Gao, Yunyi Hao, Anxin Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundComprehensive understanding of the ischemic stroke (IS) burden in China is critical for policy and medical resource allocation. We aimed to identify the most up‐to‐date IS burden by age‐ and sex‐specific temporal trends in China and forecast to 2050.MethodsBased on the Global Burden of Disease, Injuries, and Risk Factors Study (GBD) 2021, we investigated the IS burden in China from 1990 to 2021. IS incidence and mortality data were extracted by age and sex. Temporal trends were assessed using joinpoint regression to calculate average annual percentage changes (AAPCs). The Auto‐regressive Integrated Moving Average (ARIMA) models, incorporating Socio‐demographic Index and proportion of population aged >65 years as external predictors, were used to forecast the IS burden in China from 2022 to 2050. Decomposition analysis was performed to quantify the contributions of population growth, aging, and epidemiological changes to the observed and projected burden shifts.ResultsFrom 1990 to 2021, the age‐standardized incidence rate (ASIR) of IS increased (AAPC: 0.97%, 95% confidence interval [CI] 0.93–1.00); whereas the age‐standardized mortality rate (ASMR) decreased (AAPC: −0.49%, 95% CI −0.75 to −0.23). The ASIR increased faster (AAPC: 1.13% vs. 0.72%) and ASMR decreased slower in males than in females (AAPC: −0.14% vs. −0.89%) between 1990 and 2021. From 2021 to 2050, the ASIR will increase slower (AAPC: 0.77%; 95% CI 0.75–0.79) and the ASMR will decrease faster (AAPC: −1.53%; 95% CI −1.56 to −1.51). The ASIR will increase more pronounced in females (AAPC: 1.04%, 95% CI 1.02–1.07) than in males (AAPC: 0.56%, 95% CI 0.56–0.57) between 2021 and 2050. By 2050, those over 85 years old will have the highest incidence and mortality, the number of IS incident cases and deaths will reach 3.68 million (95% uncertainty interval [UI] 3.05–4.40) and 1.50 million (95% UI 1.27–1.76). The predominant factor leading to the growth of IS incident cases (54.04%) and deaths (73.2%) between 2021 and 2050 will be shifted to aging.ConclusionsThe IS burden is expected to continue increasing through 2050 albeit at a slower rate. Notably, the ASIR of IS will increase faster in females than in males from 2021 to 2050. Technological innovation, effective policies, and more health spending are needed to curb this increase.

INTRODUCTION

Stroke continues to be the leading cause of disability and mortality in China. 1 , 2 Globally, China has the highest predicted lifetime risk of stroke in people aged 25 and over, and the enormous population contributes significantly to the stroke burden. 3 According to the Global Burden of Diseases, Injuries, and Risk Factors Study (GBD) 2019 finding, stroke remained responsible for 3.94 million incident cases, and 2.19 million deaths, despite a 9.3% national decline in the age‐standardized incidence rate (ASIR) in China from 1990 to 2019. 4 Ischemic stroke (IS) accounting for more than 80% in all the prevalent cases of stroke, is the most common subtype of stroke. Regardless of significant achievements in prevention, diagnosis, and treatment, the burden of IS in China is still serious. 5 To address the substantial healthcare burden of IS, it is necessary to thoroughly understand its temporal and future development trends.

Previous studies have described the worldwide historical trends as well as the risk factors of IS based on the GBD study. According to the GBD 2019 study analysis, the age‐standardized rates of IS incidence and deaths decreased significantly from 1990 to 2019. 6 Nonetheless, the overall number of incident cases and deaths increased during this time, owing mostly to population expansion and aging. The Corona‐virus Disease 2019 (COVID‐19) pandemic has had a significant impact on global health priorities, shifting focus away from other health conditions, but as the pandemic becomes more manageable and people learn to live with and regulate the virus, it's important to refocus attention on other pressing health issues. In this regard, the current GBD 2021 study of stroke burden and risks covered the period from 1990 to 2021. 7 However, there is limited work to predict the IS burden in China.

By modeling the historical trends of IS observed in China using the GBD framework, this study seeks to provide a forecast of the IS burden up to 2050 in China. The study offers predictive estimates of the absolute numbers and ASIR and mortality rate of IS in the coming three decades, enabling comparisons of the age‐ and sex‐specific IS burden while accounting for the China population growth and aging. This project aims to gain a better knowledge of the future IS burden in China and to assist policymakers in developing tailored actions to reduce cardinal IS risk factors to minimize the IS burden.

METHOD

The GBD 2021 was used to obtain the overall and age‐ and sex‐specific incidence and mortality data of IS in China from 1990 to 2021. GBD is a tool that provides a comprehensive picture of incidence and mortality across countries, year, age, and sex. 8 The detailed description of analytic methods that the GBD used has been published elsewhere. 2 , 9 , 10 , 11 IS was defined by the Tenth International Classification of Disease (ICD) codes (G45‐G46.8, I63‐I63.9, I65‐I66.9, I67.2‐I67.3, I67.5‐I67.6, I69.3) in the GBD. 12 The United Nations Population Division (2022 revisions) was used to obtain age‐ and sex‐specific fertility rate and population data across years. 13 The data of Socio‐demographic Index (SDI) for different years in China was taken from GBD 2019, which is a composite indicator of development status strongly correlated with health outcomes developed by GBD researchers. 14 SDI is the geometric mean of total fertility rate under the age of 25 (TFU25), mean education for those ages 15 years and older (EDU15+), and lag distributed income (LDI) per capita. For the projection period 2022–2050, SDI values were derived from GBD 2019 forecasted estimates, and the proportion of population aged >65 years was obtained from the United Nations Population Division (2022 revision). 13 , 14 This study was reported following the Guidelines for Accurate and Transparent Health Estimates Reporting (GATHER) (Supporting Information S1: Table S1). 15

The estimation of IS burden in our study is expressed in terms of ASIR and mortality rates (ASMR), absolute number of cases, along the corresponding 95% uncertainty intervals (UIs). Average annual percentage change (AAPC) with 95% confidence interval (CI) was estimated using the joinpoint regression to show the temporal trend of IS burden between different subgroups (by age and gender) in China, which was conducted with Joinpoint Regression Program (version 5.0.1). 16 , 17 Joinpoint is a statistical software designed for the analysis of temporal trends by using joinpoint models to describe such continuous changes and use the grid‐search method to fit the regression function with unknown joinpoints assuming constant variance and uncorrelated errors, which is developed by Surveillance, Epidemiology, and End Results (SEER) Program. 18 To quantify the relative influence of population growth, age demographics, and risk factors on IS burden, we used the method developed by Das Gupta. 19 Through algebraically separating the standardized effects of each contributing multiplicative factor, this method summarizes the contribution of various factors to observed variation, which allows us to quantify the individual contribution of each element to the changes in the burden of IS.

The burden of IS in China from 2022 to 2050 was estimated with age‐ and sex‐stratified Autoregressive Integrated Moving Average (ARIMA) models, incorporating without SDI across years and percentage of population older than 65 years as the external predictors, given the effect of socio‐economic status and demographics on IS burden. 20 The optimal parameters for ARIMA models were chosen for each sex and age group based on the Akaike information criterion (AIC) and Bayesian information criterion (BIC). The ARIMA model parameters were automatically selected using the pmdarima.auto_arima() function, searching over all possible (p, d, q) combinations (p, q ∈ [0,5]) based on the AIC and BIC, with the minimum AIC and BIC model chosen as the optimal specification. Seasonal components, if present, were identified by examining autocorrelation function (ACF) and partial autocorrelation function (PACF) plots, and the model with the minimum AIC and BIC was ultimately selected as the optimal specification. As the input data on incidence, death, and risk factors were inherently estimations, we executed 1000 models to integrate uncertainty stemming from our input values. Each model utilized a single draw from the distribution of each input incorporated into our forecasting model. Nevertheless, we performed a sensitivity analysis using Bayesian Age‐Period‐Cohort (BAPC) models to evaluate the robustness of ARIMA projections.

All analyses in this study were conducted with Python (version 3.11; Python Software Foundation) and R (version 4.2.2; R Foundation for Statistical Computing). Ethics approval was not required since all data used in this study are deidentified and publicly available.

Role of the funding source

The funder of the study had no role in study design, data collection, data analysis, data interpretation, or writing of the report.

RESULT

Historical trends (1990‐2021)

The ASIR of IS increased from 100.05 cases (95% UI, 81.52–120.91) per 100,000 population in 1990 to 135.79 (113.25–159.83) in 2021 for both sexes, with a percentage change of 35.72% and an AAPC of 0.97% (95% CI, 0.93 –1.00) (Figure 1 and Supporting Information S1: Table S2). The ASIR of IS increased at a faster rate for males (AAPC: 1.13%, 95% CI 1.08–1.19) than for females (AAPC: 0.72%, 95% CI 0.69–0.76) between 1990 and 2021. By 2021, the ASIR of IS for males was 157.38 (95% UI 130.60–188.87), which was higher than for females (114.93, 95% UI 95.48–135.80). Moreover, the incidence rate of IS increased with age, peaking at 1790.38 (95% UI, 1419.08–2205.89) in individuals aged 85 years and over in 2021 (Table 1). There will be 3.64‐fold increase IS incident cases for both sexes in 2021 (2.77 million, 95% UI 2.30–3.32) than in 1990 (0.76 million, 95% UI 0.62–0.94), with the AAPC of 4.23% (95% CI, 4.17–4.29) (Figure 2 and Supporting Information S1: Table S3). Decomposition analysis showed that change in incidence rate was the predominant factor in the growth of IS incident cases between 1990 and 2021, accounting for 289.01%, followed by aging (37.67%) and population growth (23.65%). Between 1990 and 2021, the incident cases of IS increased at a faster rate in males (AAPC: 4.43%, 95% CI 4.35–4.51) than in females (AAPC: 3.97%, 95% CI 3.90–4.05). In 2021, there were 1.55 million (95% UI, 1.27–1.88) incident cases of IS for males and 1.22 million (95% UI: 1.01–1.47) for females. The incident cases of IS increased among all age groups except those aged under 30 years between 1990 and 2021 (Table 2).

Decomposition analysis

By contrast, the ASMR of IS decreased from 75.22 cases (95% UI, 64.48–88.23) per 100,000 population in 1990 to 64.47 (95% UI, 54.03–74.82) in 2021 for both sexes, from 90.97 (95% UI, 75.13–111.27) to 88.32 (95% UI, 71.73–107.19) for males, and from 65.28 (95% UI, 54.24–78.46) to 48.58 (95% UI, 38.15–59.77) for females (Figure 3 and Supporting Information S1: Table S4). The AAPC of ASMR of IS was −0.49% (95% CI, −0.75 to −0.23) for both sexes between 1990 and 2021, which was higher in males (−0.14%, 95% CI −0.43 to 0.16) than in females (−0.89%, 95% CI −1.18 to −0.59). The mortality rate of IS increased with age, reaching 2507.01 (95% UI, 2061.6–2886.77) among people aged 85 years and over in 2021 (Table 3). Furthermore, the mortality rate declined in all age groups between 1990 and 2021 except those aged 85 years and over (AAPC: 0.18%, 95% CI −0.32 to 0.68). However, the number of deaths in 2021 (1.18 million, 95% UI, 0.99–1.37) had increased by 2.75 times that of 1990 (0.43 million, 95% UI 0.36–0.51), with an AAPC of 3.32% (95% CI, 3.10– 3.54) (Figure 4 and Supporting Information S1: Table S5). Notably, while the age‐standardized mortality rate declined, the total number of IS deaths increased 2.75‐fold from 1990 to 2021, highlighting the growing absolute burden on the healthcare system despite medical progress. In 2021, the number of IS deaths was estimated to be 0.67 million (95% UI, 0.54–0.83) for males and 0.50 million (95% UI, 0.40–0.62) for females. Moreover, population growth, aging, and changes in mortality rate were estimated to contribute 5.5%, 31.2%, and 63.4% to the increase in the number of IS deaths between 1990 and 2021 (Figure 5). The number of IS deaths showed the fastest growth rate among people aged 85 years and over between 1990 and 2021 (AAPC: 6.37%, 95% CI 5.80–6.95) (Table 4).

Projections (2022‐2050)

We projected a percentage change of 25.02% and an AAPC of 0.77% (95% CI, 0.75–0.79) in ASIR of IS for both sexes from 2021 to 2050, which was lower than between 1990 and 2021. In 2050, the ASIR of IS was forecasted to increase to 169.77 cases (95% UI, 141.46–203.51) per 100,000 population for both sexes, 185.06 (95% UI, 147.97–230.34) for males and 155.42 (95% UI, 120.14–193.82) for females. Notably, between 2021 and 2050, the ASIR increased more rapidly in females (AAPC: 1.04%, 95% CI 1.02–1.07) than in males (AAPC: 0.56%, 95% CI 0.56–0.57). In 2050, the incidence rate was estimated to remain highest in people aged 85 years and over (1960.04, 95% UI 1562.87–2399.12) (Table 1). However, the incidence rate was projected to increase the most among people aged 45–49 years between 2021 and 2050, with a percentage change of 21.98% and an AAPC of 0.68% (95% CI, 0.66–0.69), followed by those aged between 40 and 44 years with a percentage change of 18.21% and an AAPC of 0.56% (95% CI, 0.55–0.57). The number of IS incident cases was forecasted to increase to 3.68 million (95% UI, 3.05–4.40) in 2050 for both sexes combined, with a percentage change of 32.93% and an AAPC of 0.99% (95% CI, 0.98–0.99). We performed a decomposition analysis and found that aging will be the major factor in the increase of IS incident number from 2021 to 2050, accounting for 54.04%, followed by changes in incidence rate (40.94%) and population growth (−11.65%). Additionally, the growth rates of number of IS incident cases for males (AAPC: 1.00%, 95% CI: 0.99–1.01) and females (AAPC: 0.97%, 95% CI: 0.96–0.98) were expected to be similar from 2021 to 2050. In 2050, there will be 2.06 million (95% UI, 1.69–2.49) incident cases of IS for males and 1.62 million (95% UI, 1.33–1.95) for females. Moreover, people aged between 70 and 74 years had the highest number of IS incident cases (0.66 million, 95% UI 0.48–0.89) in 2050 (Table 2). From 2021 to 2050, the number of IS incident cases increased for all age groups except those younger than 30 years, which was similar to that between 1990 and 2021.

On the contrary, the ASMR of IS was forecasted to decline to 41.19 cases (95% UI, 16.33–64.99) per 100, 000 population for both sexes by 2050, with a percentage change of −36.12% and an AAPC of −1.53% (95% CI, −1.56 to −1.51) (Figure 3). This trend can be also found in both males (AAPC: −1.39%, 95% CI: −1.42 to −1.36) and females (AAPC: −1.83%, 95% CI: −1.89 to −1.77). The ASMR of IS was predicted to decrease to 58.83 (95% UI, 29.70–81.76) for males and 28.46 (95% UI, 8.64–49.26) for females in 2050. The mortality rate increased with age and was estimated to be the highest in people aged 85 years and over (2572.27, 95% UI 2146.94–2939.16) in 2050. From 2021 to 2050, the IS mortality rate declined for all age groups except those 85 years and over (percentage change: 2.60%; AAPC: 0.09%, 95% CI: 0.09–0.09) (Table 3). However, the number of deaths attributed to IS in China was projected to increase to 1.50 million (95% UI, 1.27–1.76) in 2050 for both sexes combined, 0.87 million (95% UI, 0.70–1.08) for males and 0.63 million (95% UI, 0.50–0.78) for females. The predominant factor leading to the growth of IS deaths number between 2021 and 2050 will be shifted to aging (73.2%), followed by changes in IS mortality rate (33.9%) and population growth (−7.1%), indicates that the negative contribution from declining mortality rates partially counteracts the aging‐driven increase (Figure 5). Additionally, the number of IS deaths increased at a faster rate in males (AAPC: 0.90%, 95% CI 0.89–0.92) than in females (AAPC: 0.80%, 95% CI 0.79–0.81) between 2021 and 2050. The number of IS deaths increased with age, reaching 0.45 million (95% UI, 0.37–0.52) for people aged 85 years and over by 2050 (Table 4). In addition, the number of IS deaths for population aged under 30 years and aged between 35 and 44 years showed a downtrend between 2021 and 2050; whereas for individuals aged between 30 and 34 years and aged 45 years and over, there was an upward trend. The number of IS deaths still increased at the fastest rate among people aged 85 years and over from 2021 to 2050 (AAPC: 1.10%, 95% CI 1.09–1.11), which was consistent with that between 1990 and 2021.

DISCUSSION

This study conducted a comprehensive set of projections for the IS burden in China up to 2050 building on GBD estimates. We projected that incident cases and ASIR of IS will continue to increase through 2050, faster in females than in males, and higher in the elderly than in other age groups, this increase will bring more deaths. Encouragingly, our findings show that the ASMR of IS will be expected to maintain the downward trend, albeit at a slower pace, indicating advancements in risk mitigation for individuals.

The ASIR of IS continued to increase with the AAPC of 0.97% between 1990 and 2021, we forecasted that the increased rate will slow down with the AAPC of 0.77% from 2021 to 2050. In contrast, the ASMR of IS was projected to continue to decline from 2021 to 2050 with an AAPC of −1.53%, which is steeper than the decline from 1990 to 2021 with an AAPC of −0.49%. As the industrial age progresses into the technological era, there has been rapid development in the field of medical technology and improvement in people's health awareness, an increased emphasis is being placed on maintaining a healthy lifestyle, all of which has contributed to a decline in the growth rate of IS burden. According to GBD findings, the principal modifiable behavioral and metabolic risk factors for stroke globally included high SBP, high FPG, high BMI, high LDL‐C, and smoking. 7 In recent years, improvements in social living standards have led to the emergence of various risk factors, thereby contributing to an increased incidence of IS. 21 , 22 , 23 A cohort study of 10 regional populations in China and a meta‐analysis of 13 large prospective studies of Western patients both showed that individuals with a high BMI have a higher risk of IS. 24 , 25 Another dose‐response meta‐analysis indicated FPG was positively associated with IS risk, with a nonlinear dose‐response relationship. 26 These findings support the hypothesis that high BMI and high FPG are independent risk factors for IS and underscore the importance of developing prevention strategies that specifically target these risk factors to reduce the burden of IS. Preventing IS through early intervention and risk factor management is generally more cost‐effective than treating IS and its complications once they occur. By focusing on these risk factors, we can work to reduce the overall impact of IS on public health and healthcare systems.

Both the ASIR and ASMR of IS were much higher in males compared to females in China, which is consistent with previous research. 27 , 28 Previous studies have shown that risk factors for IS such as smoking are more common in men than in women. 29 Furthermore, the incidence of IS caused by chronic inflammatory vascular diseases such as atherosclerosis is also higher in men than in women. 30 The direct and indirect risk factors conduce to the gender differences in IS incidence and development. It is worth noting that the ASIR of IS will increase faster in females than in males from 2021 to 2050. Several factors may explain this sex‐specific projection. First, females have longer life expectancy, and the proportion of elderly women (≥75 years) is projected to grow faster than that of men. 31 Second, post‐menopausal loss of estrogen's neuroprotective effects may increase stroke susceptibility. 32 Third, atypical stroke symptoms in women often lead to delayed diagnosis and treatment. 33 Moreover, the decline in ASMR of IS over time is more pronounced in males than in females, and the gender gap in IS outcomes is projected to narrow in the future. This phenomenon can be attributed to the lack of typical IS symptoms in females compared to males. 33 This can lead to delays in seeking medical attention, diagnosis, and treatment, potentially explaining the increased IS burden among females in China. Healthcare providers should be trained to recognize and manage IS symptoms in females, ensuring that they receive timely and appropriate care. The faster ASIR increase in males during 1990–2021 but slower increase during 2021–2050 warrants explanation. This reversal likely reflects declining smoking prevalence among Chinese males due to stricter tobacco control policies, improved acute stroke management, while the prevalence of obesity, hyperglycemia and metabolic syndrome has risen rapidly among females in recent decades, continuously pushing up female future IS incidence growth. 34

There was an increase in IS burden with increasing age in China, aligning with global patterns. 28 Our findings indicated that the IS burden will continue to be a pressing concern in China, particularly among older adults, given the aging population. In the decomposition analysis, aging will be the major factor in the growth of IS number of incident cases and deaths from 2021 to 2050. The ramifications of population aging, particularly the escalating proportion of individuals aged 85 years and over, will surpass the beneficial effects of health advancements, resulting in the stabilization or reversal of the absolute IS burden in China. 35 The divergence of declining ASMR and rising total deaths underscores a critical public health challenge, in that although individual‐level medical care has improved, population aging and growth continue to drive up the absolute number of IS events and fatalities, thereby imposing substantial burdens on China's healthcare, rehabilitation and long‐term care systems. The elderly are susceptible to a range of aging‐related diseases, including IS. 36 The main reasons are the gradual decline of physical function in the elderly, the weakening of vascular elasticity, and the acceleration of arterial stiffness. 37 , 38 Elderly people are often accompanied by a variety of chronic diseases, such as hypertension, diabetes, and hyperlipidemia, all of which are significant risk factors for IS. 39 , 40 , 41 , 42 Beyond traditional cerebrovascular risk factors, neurodegenerative disorders such as Alzheimer's disease and vascular dementia are critical competing mortality risks in the elderly, whose poorer medication adherence and higher risk of recurrent IS further amplify the end‑of‑life stroke mortality burden. 43 , 44 Some elderly people have poor lifestyle habits, such as lack of exercise and an unbalanced diet, which may also increase the IS risk. 45 , 46 To address this, we should enhance primary prevention education regarding IS while simultaneously improving diagnostic and therapeutic technologies to reduce disability and mortality rates among IS patients, thereby improving their quality of life.

The IS burden is higher in China than in the high SDI developed economies across North America and Western Europe with mature primary stroke prevention systems, attributed to the delayed nationwide rollout of standardized hypertension screening and incomplete population‐wide metabolic risk factor management. 47 Meanwhile, China's IS burden is moderately lower than most Southeast Asian low‐ and middle‐income nations constrained by insufficient medical infrastructure and poor chronic disease control, according to latest GBD regional pooled analysis data. 7 The previous research indicated that the projected IS burden in most Eastern European countries was expected to exceed that of the high SDI countries within the European Union. 47 , 48 This suggests that unequal access to healthcare and inadequate healthcare resources in low and middle‐low SDI countries contribute to poorer economic and social outcomes for IS patients, as well as a heightened disease burden. The disparity in healthcare access and resources between low and high SDI countries plays a significant role in determining the impact of IS on individuals and societies. Furthermore, the economic and social consequences of IS can be severe, including loss of income, reduced quality of life, and increased demand for healthcare services. In countries with inequitable healthcare systems, these consequences are often exacerbated, leading to a higher overall disease burden for IS patients and their communities. 3 , 49 , 50 Therefore, addressing the inequalities in healthcare access and resources is crucial for improving IS outcomes and reducing the disease burden in low and middle‐low SDI countries. This may involve investing in healthcare infrastructure, training more healthcare professionals, and implementing policies that ensure equitable access to care for all IS patients, regardless of their location or socioeconomic status.

According to GBD 2021 foresight, the deaths of IS attributable to adequate management of the behavioral and metabolic risk factors will be reduced by approximately 53.33% compared with estimation in 2050. 51 These scenarios were designed to highlight the potential impact of interventions aimed at reducing key behavioral and metabolic risk factors such as hypertension, diabetes, obesity, hyperlipemia, and smoking. 52 We have projected that the burden of IS will continue to decline over the next three decades, but the burden could be reduced even further than our projections with better control of risk factors. If significant progress is made in mitigating these risk factors through comprehensive public health strategies, education campaigns, policy changes, and improved access to healthcare, the IS burden in China has the potential to be significantly reduced. These findings underscore the importance of investing in prevention strategies to reduce IS risk factors and demonstrate the potential for significant health gains through coordinated efforts. By projecting the burden of IS under scenarios of risk factor reduction, we aim to demonstrate the profound health improvements and economic benefits that could be achieved through concerted action to address IS risk factors in China. These projections serve as a powerful call to action for policymakers, healthcare providers, and the public to prioritize IS prevention and work towards reducing exposure to these modifiable risk factors.

We performed sensitivity analyses using Bayesian regression (for sex‑aggregated data) and BAPC (for age‑stratified data) to compare with ARIMA forecasts. Across all analyses, the projected trends from the Bayesian models were highly consistent with ARIMA, all pointing to a continued rise in the total burden of IS in China. Notably, the age‑stratified analyses revealed an interesting pattern of heterogeneity across age groups: projections for younger populations (<30 years) showed less concordance between the two models, whereas those for populations aged 30–84 years exhibited strong consistency. This pattern was consistently observed across both incidence and mortality outcomes, suggesting that the disease burden in younger age groups may be influenced by distinct cohort or period effects that deserve further exploration. Importantly, despite this age‐group heterogeneity, the overall upward trend remained robust when all ages were combined. Collectively, these findings confirm that our primary conclusion is robust to model specification.

Limitation

The present study involves a comprehensive analysis of IS burden trends in China from 2021 to 2050, along with projections under specific hypothetical scenarios. However, there are still some limitations that need to be acknowledged and declared. First, the data was obtained from the GBD 2021, which synthesizes estimates of IS burden from a variety of input sources. It is important to note that there may be discrepancies between GBD estimates and other national and subnational figures due to differences in methodologies and data sources. Additionally, the COVID‐19 pandemic (2020–2021) may have affected IS burden estimates through healthcare disruption, delayed hospital presentations, and changes in vascular risk factor control. The GBD 2021 data partly accounts for these effects, but residual bias cannot be excluded. Nevertheless, our analysis mainly focused on the projection of expected changes in the future relative to the most recent observational data in China, with GBD data providing valid estimates within a uniform framework that is highly comparable across time. Furthermore, GBD 2021 had considered the impact of the COVID‐19 pandemic on disease burden. Second, predictive models often rely on certain assumptions about future trends in IS incidence, mortality, and other relevant factors that may not accurately reflect real‐world developments, leading to potential bias in projections. Substantial uncertainty over 29 years long‐term projections due to potential confounders ranging from novel neuroprotective treatments and national stroke prevention programs to varying risk factor prevalence, given that ARIMA models rely on invariant historical trends and cannot accommodate these evolving conditions. The hypothetical scenarios used for forecasting may not fully capture the complexity and variability of real‐world conditions. The covariates entered into our predictive models, including risk factors and demographics, were themselves predictions that introduce additional uncertainties. Therefore, these projections may not fully represent trends in future IS burden. However, we used the SDI and demographics as the external predictors, taking into account the potential impact of socioeconomic status and population aging on IS burden. Constrained by limited data availability, key modifiable IS risk factors including smoking, obesity and hyperglycemia were excluded from the current forecasting model, mandating their incorporation into future predictive analyses to enhance projection precision. Estimation generated by GBD also took into account the effect of relevant risk factors on disease burden. Therefore, this study also provided a reliable estimation and prediction of IS burden in China in the next three decades, which can support the formulation of public health policies and the rational allocation of medical and health resources. Finally, IS burden trends can vary significantly due to differences in healthcare systems, demographic characteristics, and other factors. The study focused primarily on IS burden trends in China, but other factors such as changes in healthcare policies, advancements in medical technology, and societal shifts could also impact IS outcomes.

CONCLUSION

In summary, despite substantial advancements in mitigating the risk of IS and its associated mortality in China, future projections indicate that the incidence of this condition is increasing, resulting in a higher number of fatalities. Under specific reference scenarios, the advantages of reducing risk factors are clearly demonstrated. This research carries important practical implications for assessing the burden attributable to IS in China and offers further insights into concerning identified risk factors, which will enhance our ability to identify high‐risk populations more effectively and develop targeted prevention interventions.

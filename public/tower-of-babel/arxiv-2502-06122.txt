Delta-isobar resonance effects studied by τ ​ σ \tau\sigma summed strengths and nuclear matrix elements for β \beta and β ​ β \beta\beta decays
Hiroyasu Ejiri

Abstract
Nuclear matrix elements (NMEs) for neutrinoless β ​ β \beta\beta decays (DBD) and inverse- β \beta decays are crucial for studying ν \nu properties beyond the standard model and astro- ν \nu nuclear interactions. The NMEs consist mainly of the isospin ( τ \tau ) spin ( σ \sigma ) component, and the τ ​ σ \tau\sigma strength (square of the τ ​ σ \tau\sigma NME) is studied experimentally by charge exchange reactions (CERs). The summed Gammow-Teller (GT; τ ​ σ \tau\sigma ) and spin dipole (SD; τ ​ σ ​ Y 1 \tau\sigma Y_{1} ) strengths measured by CERs are shown to be reduced half with respect to the nucleon-based sum rule limits. The τ ​ σ \tau\sigma NMEs are shown to be quenched due to the non-nucleonic Δ \Delta -isobar resonance effect on the basis of the measured strengths and the QRPA analysis with effective NN and N Δ \Delta τ ​ σ \tau\sigma interactions. The quenching effect is incorporated by using an effective τ ​ σ \tau\sigma coupling of g τ ​ σ Δ / g τ / σ g_{\tau\sigma}^{\Delta}/g_{\tau/\sigma} ≈ \approx 0.7 with g τ ​ σ g_{\tau\sigma} being the coupling for a free nucleon. The quenching effect is applied to the τ ​ σ \tau\sigma components of the weak, electromagnetic and nuclear interaction NMEs. Impact of the Δ \Delta -resonance effect on ν \nu studies in nuclei is discussed.

The

The 2B (meson-exchange) quenching effects are non-nucleonic effects associated with excited nucleons (i.e. non-nucleons as

\tau\sigma

\Delta

\tau\sigma
NMEs and also for the summed
\tau\sigma
strengths in the N region.

On the other hand, the present quenching effect is based on the experimental reduction (quenching) of the summed
\tau\sigma
strengths in the N region. Since the
\Delta
involved in the
\Delta
N interaction and in the 2B current is located far (300 MeV) above the N region, the quenchin effects for the NMEs in low-lying ( a few MeV) state and for the summed strength in the N region are almost same. Accordingly the present experimental quenching coefficient and the theoretical N
\Delta
/2B quenching coefficient are considered to agree with each other within the experimental and theoretical uncertainties.

In fact, the 2B effects for
100

A
=90 nucleus are similar to the present effect of 0.7
\pm{0.07}
for the similar mass medium heavy nuclei. It is interesting to see how the 2B calculations for DBD nuclei reproduce the present experimental
\tau\sigma
strengths.

The 2p-2h correlations

51

\Delta

\tau\sigma
strengths to the non-nucleonic
\Delta

\tau\sigma
strength.

Then, we discuss how the non-nucleonic effect shown in the summed
\tau\sigma
strengths may be well explained by using the GR
Δ

\Delta

\Delta

\Delta

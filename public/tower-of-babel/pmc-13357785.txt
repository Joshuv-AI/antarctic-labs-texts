High-quality telomere-to-telomere genome assembly of the critically endangered tree Magnolia longipedunculata
Authors: Xin-Feng Wang, Tong-Jian Liu, Yi-Bing Wang, Xin-Lan Chen, Hai-Fei Yan, Xing Wu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Magnolia longipedunculata is a critically endangered tree endemic to southern China, valued for its ornamental flowers but with only 11 known wild individuals. As a member of Magnoliaceae—one of the earliest-diverging angiosperm lineages—it provides an important genomic resource for conservation and investigating early angiosperm evolution. Here, we report a high-quality telomere-to-telomere (T2T) genome assembly of M. longipedunculata generated using PacBio HiFi, Hi-C, Illumina, and RNA-seq data. The 2.16 Gb assembly was anchored onto 19 pseudo-chromosomes, showing high contiguity (contig N50: 54.53 Mb; scaffold N50: 151.43 Mb) and completeness (BUSCO: 96.6%; LAI: 15.21; Merqury QV: 41.70). Repetitive sequences occupy 81.93% of the genome, dominated by long terminal repeat retrotransposons (LTRs, 38.88%). A total of 24,470 protein-coding genes and 29,936 transcripts were annotated, of which 95.93% (28,718 proteins) were functionally characterized. This reference-grade genome provides a critical resource for conservation genetics, genome evolution, and comparative genomics within Magnoliaceae and across basal angiosperms.

Background & Summary

Magnolia longipedunculata (Q.W. Zeng & Y.W. Law) V.S. Kumar, originally described in the genus Manglietia1, has been taxonomically reassigned to Magnolia following recent revisions of Magnoliaceae2. This species is endemic to the Nankun Mountains of Guangdong Province, China, where only 11 wild individuals have been documented since its discovery in 2004. Mature trees can reach heights of up to 15 m and produce large, ornamental flowers (Fig. 1), yet the species faces severe threats from habitat loss and limited population size. It has been classified as Critically Endangered (CR) on both the IUCN Red List of Threatened Species and the China Biodiversity Red List3, and is included in the 2023–2030 Key Protected Plant Species List of Guangdong Province. Such an extremely narrow distribution and small population size place the species at high risk of genetic erosion, making genomic resources essential for effective conservation planning. Beyond its conservation urgency, M. longipedunculata holds considerable evolutionary significance. Magnoliaceae, one of the earliest-diverging lineages of angiosperms, provides unique opportunities to explore chromosomal evolution, floral diversification, and the origins of flowering plants2. As a critically endangered member of this family, M. longipedunculata represents a valuable species for both evolutionary and conservation studies. However, genomic resources across Magnoliaceae remain limited (Table 1), and no telomere-to-telomere (T2T) reference genome has previously been available for M. longipedunculata. Here, we present the first T2T genome assembly of M. longipedunculata based on PacBio HiFi long reads, Hi-C scaffolding, Illumina short reads, and RNA-seq data (Fig. 1c and Tables 2, 3). The final assembly spans 2.16 Gb and is anchored to 19 pseudo-chromosomes, with excellent contiguity statistics (contig N50: 54.53 Mb; scaffold N50: 151.43 Mb) (Fig. 2 and Table 4). The assembly exhibits high completeness and accuracy, with a BUSCO score of 96.60%, a Merqury consensus quality value (QV) of 41.70, and an LTR Assembly Index (LAI) of 15.21 (Table 4). Complete telomeric repeats (AAACCCT) were identified at both ends of 16 pseudo-chromosomes, whereas the remaining three harbored telomeric sequences at a single terminal (Table 5). Following genome assembly, repetitive elements were annotated, accounting for 81.93% (~1.77 Gb) of the M. longipedunculata genome. Long terminal repeat retrotransposons (LTRs) were the most abundant class, comprising 38.88% (840.06 Mb) of the genome (Table 7). Gene prediction identified 24,470 protein-coding genes and 29,936 transcripts (Tables 1, 8). Functional annotation against multiple public databases assigned functions to 28,718 proteins (95.93%), of which 26,407 (88.21%) were linked to Gene Ontology (GO) terms and 14,413 (48.15%) to KEGG Orthology (KO) terms (Table 9). These results provide robust functional coverage and a high-quality annotation of both genes and repeats. Together, this genomic characterization reveals previously unknown features of M. longipedunculata and establishes a solid framework for investigating repeat dynamics, gene evolution, and chromosomal For M. longipedunculata, it provides a foundation for conservation genetics, while also enabling broader comparative and evolutionary studies across Magnolia, Magnoliaceae, and other basal angiosperms. By establishing a robust genomic framework, this resource will inform conservation strategies for one of China’s most endangered magnolias and facilitate future research on floral trait evolution, phylogeny, and genome architecture.

Methods

Sample collection and sequencing

Samples of M. longipedunculata were collected from Nankunshan Nature Reserve, Guangdong Province, China. Fresh tissues, including young leaves, fruits, bracts, and calyxes, were immediately frozen in liquid nitrogen and preserved at −80 °C for further DNA and RNA extraction. Sample collection was formally approved by the Guangdong Provincial Forestry Bureau and the Nankunshan Nature Reserve Authority. All procedures were conducted in accordance with relevant laws and regulations, and all necessary permits were obtained prior to sampling. Genomic DNA extraction and sequencing were performed according to the protocols described in our previous study4. Specifically, total DNA was extracted using High Molecular Weight (HMW) Genomic DNA Kit. DNA degradation was assessed via a 0.75% gel electrophoresis experiment, while DNA purity and concentration were evaluated using a NanoDrop One spectrophotometer and a Qubit 3.0 fluorometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, MA, USA), respectively.

For whole-genome sequencing (WGS), a short-read library (2 × 150 bp) with an insert size of 200–300 bp was prepared using the TruSeq Nano DNA HT Sample Preparation Kit, and sequencing was conducted on the Illumina HiSeq 4000 system (Illumina, San Diego, CA, USA), generating 205.00 Gb (~95×) of raw data (Table 2).

For the long-read sequencing approach by Pacific Biosciences, two sequencing libraries were prepared using the SMRTbell Express Template Preparation kit 1.0 following the PacBio 20-kb protocol (https://www.pacb.com/). Sequencing was performed on a Revio sequencing system (Pacific Biosciences of California, Inc.) using two SMRT Cells, individually. The PacBio SMRT-Analysis package was used for quality control of the raw polymerase reads by removing sequencing adaptors and filtering low-quality short reads. After quality control, ~146.20 Gb (68×) of PacBio clean data were generated (Table 2).

For Hi-C sequencing, the library was constructed from cross-linked DNA after digestion, biotinylation, ligation, enrichment, shearing, blunt-end repair, and additional steps. Sequencing of the final Hi-C library with pair-end read lengths of 150 bp was conducted on the Illumina HiSeq X Ten platform, which produced a total of 152.40 Gb (~71×) of Hi-C data. For transcriptome sequencing, four pair-end RNA libraries (2 × 150 bp) were prepared according to the TruSeq RNA Library Preparation Kit instructions and sequenced on the Illumina HiSeq X Ten platform, yielding a total of 29.11 Gb of RNA-seq data for the leaf, fruit, bract, and calyx samples, respectively (Table 2). All sequencing was conducted at Novogene Co, Ltd. (Tianjin, China). For WGS, Hi-C, and RNA-seq data, adapters and low-quality reads were removed using fastp v0.23.395.

Genome size estimation

The k-mer frequency distribution was calculated using Jellyfish v2.3.0 (-m 21 and -m 19) with the WGS data6. The resulting file was then utilized to predict the genome features using GenomeScope v1.0117 with k-mer set to 19 and 21, respectively (Table 3). Based on this analysis, the genome of M. longipedunculata was estimated to be 2.24–2.26 Gb, with a heterozygosity rate of 3.48%.

Chromosome-level genome assembly

The genome was assembled using hifiasm v0.25.0-r7268, which has been shown to outperform other assemblers when applied to repetitive and heterozygous genomes joint with HiFi and Hi-C data. The assembled draft genome was 2.69 Gb, consisting of 992 contigs with a contig N50 length of 54.53 Mb. PacBio HiFi and Illumina WGS reads were subsequently aligned to the draft genome using minimap2 v2.24-r11229. The aligned BAM file was processed with Purge Haplotigs v1.1.2 (-l 10 -m 110 -h 300) to remove haplotypic duplications10. Hi-C reads were used to scaffold the duplication-purged genome assembly with hapHiC v1.0.711, with manual adjustments made in Juicebox v1.11.0812. Gaps in the genome assembly were processed using GapFiller v1.1113 and a total of 11 gaps were closed.

The final genome assembly was 2.16 Gb in length, consisting of 93 scaffolds with a N50 length of 151.43 Mb. A total of 2.16 Gb sequences were anchored to 19 pseudo-chromosomes. The lengths of individual chromosomes ranged from 176.03 Mb (chromosome Mlo01) to 80.58 Mb (chromosome Mlo19). The circular plot of chromosomes and Hi-C interaction heatmap were visualized by Circos v0.69-92214 and HiCExplorer v3.2.315, respectively. Telomeric repeats and centromeres were identified with the TeloExplorer module in quarTeT v1.2.22516 and Centromics v0.3 (https://github.com/zhangrengang/Centromics), respectively.

Repeat and gene annotation

The Earlgrey v2.1.02717 was employed to identify transposable elements. A total of 2,276,617 repetitive sequences were identified, representing 81.93% (1.77 Gb) of the genome (Table 7). Among these repeats, LTRs were the most prevalent, constituting 38.88% (840.06 Mb) of the genome. Gypsy elements (25.63%) were the predominant LTRs, followed by Copia elements (13.17%). The total lengths of terminal inverted repeats (TIRs, DNA) and LINE elements were 68.29 Mb and 66.95 Mb, respectively. These elements accounted for 3.16% and 3.10% of the genome (Table 7).

The transposable element (TE) library generated by Earlgrey served as input for RepeatMasker v4.1.22818 to produce a soft-masked genome. Gene prediction and functional annotation for the soft-masked genome were performed using de novo, homology protein-based, and transcriptome-based methods via the braker pipeline v319. RNA-seq data were utilized to train the gene prediction models with the splicing alignments generated by HISAT220. Subsequently, Augustus v3.5.03021 and GeneMark-ET v4.723122 were employed for protein-coding gene prediction. The protein-coding sequences of Viridiplantae clade from OrthoDB v.12 database were used as protein evidence. For functional annotation, the predicted genes were queried against public databases, including pfam v32.0, interpro v76.0, dbCAN v8.0, go v2023-05-10, unipot v2023_02, Mapman and eggNOG v5.0, using the InterProScan v5.62–94.03923, Mercator424, PANNZER225 and EggNOG-mapper v2.1.1140 pipelines26. In total, 24,470 genes encoding 29,936 proteins were predicted, with an average gene length of 1,277 bp.

Data Records

The dataset is publicly available through multiple repositories, including the National Genomics Data Center (NGDC), the National Center for Biotechnology Information (NCBI), and Figshare27–32. The raw sequencing data deposited in the NCBI include Illumina short reads (accession SRR38060166)32 for genome survey and polishing, PacBio HiFi reads (SRR38060167)32 for de novo genome assembly, Hi-C reads (SRR38060168)32 for chromosome-level scaffolding, and RNA-seq reads (SRR38060162–SRR3806015)32 from multiple tissues used for gene structure annotation and expression analysis. The final genome assembly is provided in FASTA format and represents a chromosome-level assembly. Gene structure annotations are available in GFF3 format, including genomic coordinates of genes, exons, and coding sequences (CDS). Predicted protein-coding sequences and CDS are provided in FASTA format. The Figshare repository (10.6084/m9.figshare.29675177.v1)29 contains the following primary files: Magnolia_longipedunculata.chr.fa.gz (assembled genome sequence), Magnolia_longipedunculata.gff3 (gene structure annotation), Magnolia_longipedunculata.pep.fa (predicted protein sequences), and Magnolia_longipedunculata.cds.fa (predicted coding sequences). All files are organized into directories corresponding to genome assembly and annotation datasets. Sequence identifiers are consistent across all files, allowing direct cross-referencing between genome sequences, gene models, and protein sequences. Standard file formats (FASTA and GFF3) are used to ensure compatibility with commonly used bioinformatics tools and facilitate data reuse.

Technical Validation

Multiple methods were used to evaluate the quality of the M. longipedunculata genome. First, the Hi-C data visualized by HiCExplorer15 showed high consistency across all 19 pseudo-chromosomes, confirming the accuracy of the ordering and orientation. Second, the Pacbio HiFi reads were mapped to the assembly using minimap29, while the Illumina reads were aligned with BWA v0.7.17-r11884333. The mapping rate was calculated using samtools v2.5.14434. A total of 99.95% of the PacBio HiFi reads, 97.88% of the Illumina WGS reads, and 92.14% of the RNAseq reads were successfully aligned. Assembly completeness was assessed using BUSCO, LTR_retriever v3.0.1, and Merqury35–37. The genome contained 96.60% complete BUSCOs, an LTR Assembly Index (LAI) of 15.21, and a Merqury consensus quality value (QV) of 41.70, collectively confirming the high accuracy and reliability of the M. longipedunculata reference genome.

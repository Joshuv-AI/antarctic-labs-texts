Study on the Effect of Different Occlusal Schemes on Temporomandibular Joint Health in Complete Denture Wearers
Authors: Ravi Ranjan Sinha, Poda Alekhya, G Srinu, Harprit Kaur, Pattar Neelavathi, Carrolene Langpoklakpam
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Background:Temporomandibular joint (TMJ) disorders are commonly observed in edentulous patients, potentially exacerbated by improper occlusal schemes in complete dentures. Various occlusal concepts, including balanced occlusion, lingualized occlusion, and monoplane occlusion, are used in prosthodontics, each claiming advantages in preserving TMJ health.Materials and Methods:A total of 60 completely edentulous patients aged between 50 and 75 years were selected and randomly divided into three groups (n = 20) based on the occlusal scheme: Group A – Balanced Occlusion, Group B – Lingualized Occlusion, and Group C – Monoplane Occlusion. All patients received new maxillary and mandibular complete dentures fabricated using conventional techniques. TMJ health was assessed at baseline, 3 months, and 6 months post-insertion using Helkimo’s Clinical Dysfunction Index and patient-reported outcomes on pain and joint sounds.Results:At the 6-month follow-up, Group A showed minimal TMJ symptoms with a mean dysfunction index score of 0.8 ± 0.5, compared to 1.2 ± 0.6 in Group B and 2.1 ± 0.7 in Group C. Joint sounds and pain were reported in 10% of patients in Group A, 15% in Group B, and 35% in Group C. Statistical analysis revealed significant differences between the groups (P < 0.05), favoring balanced occlusion for TMJ stability.Conclusion:Balanced occlusion in complete dentures appears to better maintain TMJ health compared to lingualized and monoplane schemes. It minimizes joint dysfunction symptoms and improves patient comfort over time.

INTRODUCTION

The temporomandibular joint (TMJ) plays a vital role in mastication, speech, and overall oral function. In edentulous patients, the loss of natural teeth leads to altered mandibular movements and changed biomechanics, potentially predisposing them to temporomandibular disorders (TMDs).[1] Complete denture prostheses aim to restore these functions, and the occlusal scheme adopted during fabrication can significantly influence the health of the TMJ.[2]

Different occlusal schemes have been developed to improve denture stability, masticatory efficiency, and patient comfort. Among them, balanced occlusion is traditionally advocated to provide simultaneous bilateral contact during eccentric movements, potentially distributing occlusal forces evenly and minimizing strain on the TMJ.[3] Lingualized occlusion, characterized by dominant maxillary lingual cusps occluding in the central fossae of mandibular teeth, aims to combine esthetics with functional balance.[4] Monoplane occlusion, which uses flat teeth and minimal vertical overlap, is often recommended in patients with resorbed ridges but may compromise TMJ loading due to reduced proprioception and occlusal guidance.[5]

Although each occlusal scheme has its proponents, the relationship between these schemes and TMJ health in complete denture wearers remains inadequately explored. Previous studies have focused primarily on retention, stability, and patient satisfaction, with limited data on TMJ-related outcomes.[67]

RESULTS

A total of 60 participants completed the study, with an equal distribution across the three occlusal scheme groups. All patients were followed for a period of six months post denture insertion. The results are presented in terms of Helkimo’s Clinical Dysfunction Index scores and patient-reported symptoms.

Helkimo’s clinical dysfunction index scores

At baseline, all three groups showed minimal TMJ dysfunction. Over time, differences emerged in the severity of TMJ-related symptoms. DISCUSSION

Balanced occlusion aims to provide bilateral, simultaneous contact during eccentric movements, thereby ensuring even distribution of occlusal forces and minimizing displacing forces on the denture base.[1] This biomechanical stability is believed to reduce strain on the TMJ, which aligns with our findings of lower Helkimo index scores and fewer symptoms in Group A. These results are consistent with previous reports suggesting that balanced articulation helps maintain neuromuscular harmony in edentulous patients.[23]

Lingualized occlusion, which seeks to balance esthetics and function, showed intermediate outcomes. While it was slightly less favorable than balanced occlusion in our study, its design—which centralizes occlusal forces on the mandibular ridge—may still offer benefits in cases of moderate ridge resorption.[4] Some studies support its use for improving esthetics and ease of fabrication, though its long-term effects on TMJ health remain less definitive.[5]

In contrast, monoplane occlusion, despite being easier to establish and advantageous in cases with severely resorbed ridges or poor neuromuscular control, showed the highest levels of TMJ dysfunction in this study. The flat occlusal surfaces and absence of cusp-guided movements may reduce proprioceptive feedback and occlusal stability, potentially contributing to muscle incoordination and joint strain.[6] Prior literature has indicated that such schemes may compromise masticatory efficiency and functional adaptation, particularly in patients with high functional demands.[7]

It is also important to recognize the role of individual adaptability and neuromuscular coordination in how patients respond to different occlusal designs. While occlusal scheme selection is important, patient education, regular follow-up, and timely denture adjustments also play a crucial role in maintaining TMJ health.[8]

CONCLUSION

Balanced occlusion in complete dentures appears to better maintain TMJ health than lingualized and monoplane schemes. It minimizes joint dysfunction symptoms and improves patient comfort over time.

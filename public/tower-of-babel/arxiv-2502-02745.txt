Introduction

1.1
Motivation

In this paper, we study the almost critical biharmonic problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\Delta(a(x)\Delta u)=a(x)|u|^{p-2-\varepsilon}u&\text{in }\Omega,\\
u=0&\text{on }\partial\Omega,\\
\Delta u=0&\text{on }\partial\Omega,\end{array}\right.

(1.1)

where
\Omega
is a smooth bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
(
N\geq 5
),
a\in C^{2}(\overline{\Omega})
is strictly positive on
\overline{\Omega}
,
p:=\frac{2N}{N-4}
is the critical Sobolev exponent for the embedding
H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{p}(\Omega)
, and
\varepsilon
is a positive small parameter.

In general, elliptic equations involving fourth-order operators arise naturally in various mathematical and physical contexts and numerous mathematicians have devoted significant attention to these types of problems (e.g.
[
13
,
22
,
3
,
11
,
14
,
15
,
17
,
27
,
36
,
38
,
39
,
40
]
). For example, elliptic fourth-order operators of the form

\sum_{i,j,s,h}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{j}}\left(a_{ijsh}(x)\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x_{s}\partial x_{h}}\right)

appear in the study of elastic properties of thin plates. This biharmonic equation has its roots in the work of Sophie Germain, particularly in the Germain-Lagrange equation, a fourth-order partial differential equation describing the behavior of vibrating elastic plates. For more information, see
[
31
,
9
]
.

Beyond elasticity, the operator on the left-hand side of (
1.1
) has gained a lot of attention in the last years, see for example
[
41
,
10
,
21
,
23
]
and references therein.
Moreover, fourth-order operators like the Paneitz operator play a central role in conformal geometry. These operators are fundamental in questions about finding conformal metrics with constant or prescribed
Q
-curvature, analogous to the classical Yamabe problem in lower dimensions. Such problems involve solving fourth-order elliptic PDEs, driven by the Paneitz operator. We refer the reader to
[
12
]
.

If
a(x)\equiv 1
and
\varepsilon=0
, problem (
1.1
) becomes

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\Delta^{2}u=|u|^{p-2}u&\text{in }\Omega,\\
u=0&\text{on }\partial\Omega,\\
\Delta u=0&\text{on }\partial\Omega.\end{array}\right.

(1.2)

This critical problem has been widely studied. Whether it can be solved depends largely on the geometry of
\Omega
. Indeed, Van der Vorst
[
37
]
showed that problem (
1.2
) does not admit positive solutions when
\Omega
is star-shaped, whereas Ebobisse and Ahmedou
[
16
]
demonstrated that (
1.2
) possesses a solution provided that some homology group of
\Omega
is nontrivial. This topological condition is sufficient, although not essential, as noted by Gazzola, Grunau, and Squassina
[
18
]
, who presented examples of contractible domains where solutions exist. Alarcón and Pistoia studied this problem in domains with circular holes in
[
2
]
where they provided a precise description of the asymptotic profile of the solution as the radius of the circular hole approaches zero.

To gain a clearer understanding of the context, it is insightful to compare the Paneitz-type problem (
1.2
) with the well-known Yamabe-type problem:

\begin{cases}-\Delta u=|u|^{\frac{2N}{N-2}-2}u&\text{in }\Omega,\\
u=0&\text{on }\partial\Omega.\end{cases}

(1.3)

If the domain
\Omega
is star-shaped, Pohozaev’s identity
[
33
]
implies that problem (
1.3
) has only the trivial solution. On the other hand, Bahri and Coron
[
4
]
demonstrated that (
1.3
) has a solution provided that at least one homology group of
\Omega
is nontrivial. Passaseo showed in
[
30
]
that this topological condition is not a necessary requirement for the existence of a solution to (
1.3
).

The almost critical case

\begin{cases}-\Delta u=|u|^{\frac{2N}{N-2}-2-\epsilon}u&\text{in }\Omega,\\
u=0&\text{on }\partial\Omega,\end{cases}

(1.4)

with
\varepsilon
positive and small enough, has also been widely studied. The pioneering works of Bahri and Rey
[
5
]
and Rey
[
34
]
established the existence of positive solutions that blow up at one or more points in
\Omega
as
\epsilon\to 0
. Regarding sign-changing solutions, a significant number of solutions exhibiting simple or multiple positive and negative sign-changing solutions were constructed by Bartsch, Micheletti, and Pistoia
[
6
]
, Musso and Pistoia
[
29
]
, and Pistoia and Weth
[
32
]
.

In
[
1
]
, Ackermann, Clapp, and Pistoia studied a more general almost critical problem given by:

\begin{cases}-\text{div}\left(a(x)\nabla u\right)=a(x)|u|^{\frac{2N}{N-2}-2-\varepsilon}u&\text{in }\Omega,\\
u=0&\text{on }\partial\Omega,\end{cases}

(1.5)

where, as before,
\Omega
is a bounded smooth domain in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
with
N\geq 3
,
\varepsilon>0
is a small parameter, and
a\in C^{2}(\overline{\Omega})
is a strictly positive function on
\overline{\Omega}
. The authors provided sufficient conditions on the function
a
and the domain
\Omega
for the problem to admit both a positive solution and a sign-changing solution, each of which concentrates and blows up at a point on the boundary
\partial\Omega
as
\epsilon\to 0
.

The work of Ackermann, Clapp, and Pistoia on the second-order problem (
1.5
) has motivated us to investigate analogous results in the fourth-order setting. In this paper, we focus on the almost critical biharmonic problem (
1.1
) and derive sufficient conditions on the function
a(x)
and the domain
\Omega
that guarantee the existence of solutions with explicit asymptotic profiles as
\epsilon\to 0
. As expected, the main challenge arises from the order of the operator. For instance, since it is of fourth order, calculating the error introduced by our ansatz becomes more complex. To compute this error, we need to develop new estimates for the derivatives of the Green’s function and its regular part. These estimates allow us to analyze the dominant behavior of the key terms and evaluate their overall contribution.

We now describe the profile of the solutions we obtain:

•

Boundary blow-up solutions
: These solutions exhibit
k
multiple blow-up points concentrating at a
k
different points of the boundary
\partial\Omega
.

•

Sign-changing solutions
: These involve two blow-up points of opposite sign collapsing to the same boundary point of the boundary
\partial\Omega
.

To obtain our results, we employ the Lyapunov-Schmidt finite-dimensional reduction method. The structure of the paper is as follows: In the remainder of this section, we introduce the variational framework and provide a precise description of our main results. In section
2
we perform the Lyapunov–Schmidt reduction procedure. Section
3
provides detailed proofs of the main theorems, including the asymptotic expansions of the energy functional. Section
4
focuses on boundary estimates for the Green’s function and its derivatives. We use these results to obtain key estimates. Section
5
investigates the discrepancies between the approximate solutions and those established in the theorems.

Finally, Section
6
deals with the estimation of the error term, and Section
7
with the expansion of the nonlinear term of the reduced energy functional.

1.2
Variational Framework and Main Results

In this section, we establish the variational framework and present our main results. We begin by introducing the necessary functional spaces: We consider the inner product and corresponding norm on
H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
, given by

(u,v):=\int_{\Omega}a(x)\Delta u\Delta v\,dx,\quad\|u\|_{a}:=\left(\int_{\Omega}a(x)|\Delta u|^{2}\,dx\right)^{1/2},

which are equivalent to the standard norm and inner product; see Section 2 in
[
19
]
. For any
q\in[1,\infty)
, we define the norm in
L^{q}(\Omega)
as

|u|_{a,q}:=\left(\int_{\Omega}a(x)|u|^{q}\,dx\right)^{1/q}.

The Sobolev embedding theorem asserts that
H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
is
continuously embedded in
L^{q}(\Omega)
for
1<q\leq\frac{2N}{N-4}
, and this embedding is compact when
q<\frac{2N}{N-4}
.
The embedding
i:H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{\frac{2N}{N-4}}(\Omega)
has an adjoint operator
i^{*}:L^{\frac{2N}{N+4}}(\Omega)\to H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
, satisfying

(i^{*}(u),\varphi)=\int_{\Omega}a(x)u(x)\varphi(x)\,dx,\quad\forall\varphi\in H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H_{0}^{1}(\Omega).

Equivalently,

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\Delta(a(x)\Delta i^{*}(u))=a(x)u(x)&\text{in }\Omega,\\
i^{*}(u)=0&\text{on }\partial\Omega,\\
\Delta i^{*}(u)=0&\text{on }\partial\Omega.\end{array}\right.

Using Hölder’s inequality and the Sobolev embedding, we deduce

\|i^{*}(u)\|_{a}^{2}=(i^{*}(u),i^{*}(u))=\int_{\Omega}a(x)u(x)i^{*}(u)(x)\,dx\leq c\|u\|_{\frac{2N}{N+4}}\|i^{*}(u)\|_{a}.

Thus, there exists a constant
c>0
such that

\|i^{*}(u)\|_{a}\leq c\|u\|_{\frac{2N}{N+4}},\quad\forall u\in L^{\frac{2N}{N+4}}(\Omega).

We are now in a position to reformulate the Problem (
1.1
).
For
\ell\geq 0
, define
f_{\ell}(s):=|s|^{\frac{8}{N-4}-\ell}s=|s|^{p-2-\ell}s
. Then, we have

u=i^{*}(f_{\varepsilon}(u))\quad\text{if and only if }u\text{ solves problem }\eqref{Prob}.

(1.6)

Now, we introduce the
bubble

U_{\delta,\xi}
, which is a function defined by

U_{\delta,\xi}(x):=\alpha_{N}\left(\frac{\delta}{\delta^{2}+|x-\xi|^{2}}\right)^{\frac{N-4}{2}},\quad x,\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{N},\,\delta>0,

where
\alpha_{N}=(N(N-4)(N-2)(N+2))^{\frac{N-4}{8}}
. These bubbles are the unique positive solutions in
\mathcal{D}^{2,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
for the
limit problem

\Delta^{2}U=U^{\frac{N+4}{N-4}}\quad\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{N}

(see
[
25
]
).
We use these bubbles as building blocks to construct solutions to (
1.1
).
The proof of our results relies on the study of the problem that is obtained when linearizing the limit equation around the solution
U_{\delta,\xi}
. As shown in
[
26
]
, the set of solutions to the linearized equation

\Delta^{2}\psi=f_{0}^{\prime}(U_{\delta,\xi})\psi\quad\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{N},\quad\psi\in\mathcal{D}^{2,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),

is an
(N+1)
-dimensional linear space spanned by:

\psi_{\delta,\xi}^{0}(x):=\frac{\partial U_{\delta,\xi}}{\partial\delta}(x)=\alpha_{N}\frac{N-4}{2}\delta^{\frac{N-6}{2}}\frac{|x-\xi|^{2}-\delta^{2}}{(\delta^{2}+|x-\xi|^{2})^{\frac{N-2}{2}}},

and, for each
j=1,\dots,N
,

\psi_{\delta,\xi}^{j}(x):=\frac{\partial U_{\delta,\xi}}{\partial\xi_{j}}(x)=\alpha_{N}(N-4)\delta^{\frac{N-4}{2}}\frac{x_{j}-\xi_{j}}{(\delta^{2}+|x-\xi|^{2})^{\frac{N-2}{2}}}.

This property is known as the
non-degeneracy
of the solution
U_{\delta,\xi}
.

Definition

(Weak Solutions)
.

A function
u\in H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
is said to be a weak solution to (
1.1
) if, for any test function
\phi\in H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
, the following holds:

\int_{\Omega}\Delta(a(x)\Delta u(x))\phi(x)\,dx=\int_{\Omega}a(x)|u(x)|^{p-2-\varepsilon}u(x)\phi(x)\,dx.

Hence, problem (
1.1
) is the Euler–Lagrange equation for the functional

J_{\varepsilon}:H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\to\mathbb{R},\quad J_{\varepsilon}(u)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}a(x)|\Delta u(x)|^{2}\,dx-\frac{1}{p-\varepsilon}\int_{\Omega}a(x)|u(x)|^{p-\varepsilon}\,dx.

(1.7)

The functional
J_{\varepsilon}
is of class
C^{2}
, and its derivative is given by

J_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(u)[\phi]=\int_{\Omega}a(x)\Delta u(x)\Delta\phi(x)\,dx-\int_{\Omega}a(x)|u(x)|^{p-2-\varepsilon}u(x)\phi(x)\,dx\quad\forall\phi\in H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H_{0}^{1}(\Omega).

For any
u\in\mathcal{D}^{2,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, we denote by
Pu
its projection onto the space
H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
, which is the unique solution to the problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\Delta^{2}Pu=\Delta^{2}u&\text{in }\Omega,\\
Pu=\Delta Pu=0&\text{on }\partial\Omega.\end{array}\right.

We prove the existence of two distinct types of solutions to problem (
1.1
). For the first result, we assume the following condition.

(p1)
: There exist
k
non-degenerate critical points
\zeta_{1}^{0},\dots,\zeta_{k}^{0}\in\partial\Omega
of the restriction of
a(x)
to the boundary
\partial\Omega
, such that:

(\nabla a(\zeta_{j}^{0}),\nu(\zeta_{j}^{0}))>0\ \ \ \forall\ j=1\dots,k

where
\nu(\zeta_{j}^{0})
denotes the inward-pointing unit normal to
\partial\Omega
at
\zeta_{j}^{0}
.
Under this assumption, we establish the following result:

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume that condition
(p1)
holds true. Then, there exists
\varepsilon_{1}>0
such that, for each choice of
b_{i}\in\{0,1\}
and
0<\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{1}
, problem (
1.1
) has a solution
u_{\varepsilon}
of the form:

u_{\varepsilon}(x)=\sum_{j=1}^{k}(-1)^{b_{j}}PU_{\delta_{j,\varepsilon},\xi_{j,\varepsilon}}(x)+\phi_{\varepsilon}(x),

where the weight
\delta_{j,\varepsilon}
of the bubble
U_{\delta_{j,\varepsilon},\xi_{j,\varepsilon}}
satisfies

\delta_{j,\varepsilon}=\varepsilon^{\frac{N-3}{N-4}}d_{j,\varepsilon},\quad\text{with }d_{j,\varepsilon}\to d_{j}>0,

for
j=1,\dots,k
, as
\varepsilon\to 0
, and the center
\xi_{j,\varepsilon}
of the bubble
U_{\delta_{j,\varepsilon},\xi_{j,\varepsilon}}
satisfies:

\xi_{j,\varepsilon}\to\zeta_{j}^{0},\quad\text{as }\varepsilon\to 0,

for
j=1,\dots,k
, and the rest function
\phi_{\varepsilon}
is a remainder term.

Theorem
1.1
establishes the existence of a solution that may change sign and blows up at the
k
points given by the hypotheses described in
(p1)
. Our next result demonstrates the existence of a sign-changing solution with two blow-up points—one positive and one negative—that collapse to a single point on the boundary of
\Omega
. To prove this, we assume the following:

(p2)
: There exists a critical point
\zeta^{0}
of the restriction of
a(x)
to the boundary
\partial\Omega
such that:

(\nabla a(\zeta^{0}),\nu(\zeta^{0}))>0

Additionally, we introduce a hypothesis regarding the symmetries of both the domain
\Omega
and the function
a(x)
that we describe below: Assume that there exist a set of vectors
e_{1},\dots,e_{N-1}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
, such that the set
\{\nu(\zeta^{0}),e_{1},\dots,e_{N-1}\}
forms an orthonormal basis, such that both
\Omega
and
a(x)
are invariant under reflections
R_{i}
with respect to each hyperplane
\zeta_{0}+\{e_{i}=0\}
, i.e.,

R_{i}(x)\in\Omega\quad\text{and}\quad a(R_{i}(x))=a(x)\quad\forall x\in\Omega,\,i=1,\dots,N-1,

where the reflection
R_{i}
is defined by

R_{i}(x)=\zeta^{0}+(x,\nu(\zeta^{0}))\nu(\zeta^{0})-(x,e_{i})e_{i}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
j\neq i\end{subarray}}^{N-1}(x,e_{j})e_{j}.

(1.8)

where, as before,
\nu:=\nu(\zeta^{0})
denotes the inward-pointing unit normal to
\partial\Omega
at
\zeta^{0}
.

We will write

\mathcal{H}(\Omega):=\{f\in H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H_{0}^{1}(\Omega):f(R_{i}(x))=f(x),\,\forall x\in\Omega,\,i=1,\dots,N-1\}.

(1.9)

for the space of functions on
H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
that are invariant under every reflection
R_{i}
. Note that if
\xi=\zeta^{0}+t\nu(\zeta^{0})\in\Omega
, then
PU_{\delta,\xi}\in\mathcal{H}(\Omega).

Under these assumptions, we establish the following result:

Theorem 1.2
.

Assume that condition
(p2)
holds true. Then, there exists
\varepsilon_{0}>0
such that, for each
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0})
, problem (
1.1
) admits a sign-changing solution
u_{\varepsilon}
of the form:

u_{\varepsilon}(x)=PU_{\delta_{1,\varepsilon},\xi_{1,\varepsilon}}(x)-PU_{\delta_{2,\varepsilon},\xi_{2,\varepsilon}}(x)+\phi_{\varepsilon}(x),

where the weights
\delta_{i,\varepsilon}
of the bubbles
U_{\delta_{i,\varepsilon},\xi_{i,\varepsilon}}
satisfy

\varepsilon^{-\frac{N-3}{N-4}}\delta_{i,\varepsilon}\to d_{i}>0,\quad\text{as }\varepsilon\to 0,

for
i=1,2
, and the centers
\xi_{i,\varepsilon}
of the bubbles satisfy

\xi_{i,\varepsilon}=\zeta^{0}+\varepsilon t_{i,\varepsilon}\nu(\zeta^{0}),\quad\text{with }t_{i,\varepsilon}\to t_{i}>0,\quad\text{as }\varepsilon\to 0,

for
i=1,2
, and the rest function
\phi_{\varepsilon}
is a remainder term. Moreover, the solution
u_{\varepsilon}
belongs to the space
\mathcal{H}(\Omega)
.

The symmetry assumption
(p2)
helps address technical challenges that arise when looking for a solution whose bubbles collapse to the same point. We believe this hypothesis could be removed, but doing so would require going further in the expansion of the reduced energy functional, making the computations excessively tedious.

2
Finite Dimensional Reduction

In this section, we employ the method of finite-dimensional reduction to transform the original infinite-dimensional problem into a finite-dimensional variational problem.

The solutions to problem (
1.1
) given by Theorem
1.1
are of the form

u_{\epsilon}=\sum_{j=1}^{k}(-1)^{b_{j}}PU_{\delta_{j},\xi_{j}}+\phi_{\epsilon},

(2.1)

where
b_{j}\in\{0,1\}
are fix numbers. The concentration parameters
\delta_{i}
satisfy

\delta_{i}=d_{i}\epsilon^{\frac{N-3}{N-4}}\mbox{ for some }d_{i}>0\ \mbox{for every }i=1,\dots,k,

and the concentration points
\xi_{i}
are given by

\xi_{i}=\xi_{i}^{0}+\tau_{i}\eta(\xi_{i}^{0})\mbox{ where }\xi_{i}^{0}\in\partial\Omega\ \ \mbox{and }\tau_{i}=t_{i}\epsilon\mbox{ for some }t_{i}>0.

We denote
\boldsymbol{\xi}=(\xi_{1}^{0},\dots,\xi_{k}^{0})
,
\textbf{d}=(d_{1},\dots,d_{k})
and
\textbf{t}=(t_{1},\dots,t_{k})
and we assume that these parameters belong to the set

\Sigma_{1}:=\{(\boldsymbol{\xi},\textbf{d},\textbf{t})\in(\partial\Omega)^{k}\times(0,\infty)^{k}\times(0,\infty)^{k}:\xi_{i}^{0}\neq\xi_{j}^{0}\mbox{ if }i\neq j\}.

For each element
(\boldsymbol{\xi},\textbf{d},\textbf{t})\in\Sigma_{1}
and every
\epsilon>0
, we consider the fowling decomposition

H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)=K^{1,\epsilon}_{(\boldsymbol{\xi},\textbf{d},\textbf{t})}\oplus K^{1,\epsilon,\perp}_{(\boldsymbol{\xi},\textbf{d},\textbf{t})}

where

\begin{split}K^{1,\epsilon}_{(\boldsymbol{\xi},\textbf{d},\textbf{t})}&:=span\{P\psi_{i}^{j}:i=1,\dots,k,j=0,1\dots,N\},\\
K^{1,\epsilon,\perp}_{(\boldsymbol{\xi},\textbf{d},\textbf{t})}&:=\{u\in H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H_{0}^{1}(\Omega):(u,P\psi_{i}^{j})=0,i=1,\dots,k,j=0,1\dots,N\}.\end{split}

We consider the respective orthogonal projection operators

\Pi^{1,\epsilon}_{(\boldsymbol{\xi},\textbf{d},\textbf{t})}:H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\rightarrow K^{1,\epsilon}_{(\boldsymbol{\xi},\textbf{d},\textbf{t})}\mbox{ and }\Pi^{1,\epsilon,\perp}_{(\boldsymbol{\xi},\textbf{d},\textbf{t})}:H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\rightarrow K^{1,\epsilon,\perp}_{(\boldsymbol{\xi},\textbf{d},\textbf{t})}

defined by

\Pi^{1,\epsilon}_{(\boldsymbol{\xi},\textbf{d},\textbf{t})}(u):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1,\dots,k\\
j=0,\dots,N\end{subarray}}(u,P\psi_{i}^{j})\ \ \mbox{ and }\ \ \Pi^{1,\epsilon,\perp}_{(\boldsymbol{\xi},\textbf{d},\textbf{t})}(u):=u-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1,\dots,k\\
j=0,\dots,N\end{subarray}}(u,P\psi_{i}^{j})

On the other hand, the solutions given by Theorem
1.2
are of the form

u_{\epsilon}=PU_{\delta_{1,\epsilon},\xi_{1,\epsilon}}-PU_{\delta_{2,\epsilon},\xi_{2,\epsilon}}+\phi_{\epsilon},

(2.2)

where the concentration parameters
\delta_{i}
satisfy:

\delta_{i,\epsilon}=d_{i}\epsilon^{\frac{N-3}{N-4}}\mbox{ for some }d_{i}>0\ \mbox{for each }i=1,\dots,k,

and the concentration points
\xi_{i}
are located on the line
L=\{\zeta^{0}+\ell\nu(\zeta^{0}):\ell\in\mathbb{R}\}
, i.e. they satisfy

\xi_{1,\epsilon}=\zeta^{0}+\tau_{1}\nu(\zeta^{0}),\ \ \xi_{2,\epsilon}=\zeta^{0}+\tau_{2}\nu(\zeta^{0})\mbox{ where }\tau_{i}=t_{i}\epsilon\mbox{ for some }0<t_{1}<t_{2}.

We will write
\textbf{d}=(d_{1},d_{2})
and
\textbf{t}=(t_{1},t_{2})
and assume that these parameters lie on the set:

\Sigma_{2}:=\{(\textbf{d},\textbf{t})\in(0,\infty)^{2}\times(0,\infty)^{2}:\ 0<t_{1}<t_{2}\}.

In this case, we are looking for solutions
u_{\epsilon}
in the space
\mathcal{H}(\Omega)
. For this purpose, the following remark is essential.

Remark 2.1

(Symmetry Considerations)
.

Under assumption
(p2)
, if
\xi=\zeta^{0}+t\nu(\zeta^{0})\in\Omega
, then any solution
P\psi
to the problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\Delta^{2}P\psi=f_{0}^{\prime}(U_{\delta,\xi})\psi&\text{in }\Omega,\\
\Delta P\psi=P\psi=0&\text{on }\partial\Omega,\end{array}\right.

(2.3)

belongs to
\mathcal{H}(\Omega)
. This symmetry arises because, if for every

, we have that
f_{0}^{\prime}(U_{\delta,\xi}(R_{i}(x)))=f_{0}^{\prime}(U_{\delta,\xi}(x))
, hence if
P\psi(x)
is a solution to (

) then
P\psi(R_{i}(x))
is also solution.

Hence, since we are assuming that
\Omega
and
a
satisfy
(p2)
, it follows from Remark

that, for every
(\zeta^{0},\textbf{d},\textbf{t})\in\Sigma_{2}
the space
\mathcal{H}(\Omega)
admits the following decomposition:

\mathcal{H}(\Omega)=K^{2,\epsilon}_{(\zeta^{0},\textbf{d},\textbf{t})}\oplus K^{2,\epsilon,\perp}_{(\zeta^{0},\textbf{d},\textbf{t})},

where

K^{2,\epsilon}_{(\zeta^{0},\textbf{d},\textbf{t})}:=\text{span}\{P\psi_{i}^{j}:i=1,2,\ j=0,1,\dots,N\}\subseteq\mathcal{H}(\Omega),

and its respective orthogonal complement in
\mathcal{H}(\Omega)
is defined as:

7.1
. This completes the proof.

4
Boundary estimates of the Green function.

This section focuses on the detailed analysis of the Green’s function and its regular part for the biharmonic operator under Navier boundary conditions. We derive precise estimates for these functions, particularly near the boundary of the domain.

Let
G(x,y)
be the Green’s function for the bi-Laplacian operator on
\Omega
with Navier boundary condition and by
H(x,y)
its regular part, i.e.
G(x,y)
satisfies the problem:

\left\{\begin{array}[c]{ll}\Delta_{y}^{2}G(x,y)=\gamma_{N}\delta_{x}&\text{in }\Omega,\\
G(x,y)=\Delta_{y}G(x,y)=0&\text{on }\partial\Omega\end{array}\right.

(4.1)

where
\gamma_{N}:=(N-4)(N-2)meas(\mathbb{S}^{N-1})
and
H(x,y)
is defined by

H(x,y)=\frac{1}{|x-y|^{N-4}}-G(x,y)

and verifies

\left\{\begin{array}[c]{ll}\Delta_{y}^{2}H(x,y)=0&\text{in }\Omega,\\
H(x,y)=\frac{1}{|x-y|^{N-4}}&\text{on }\partial\Omega\\
\Delta_{y}H(x,y)=-2(N-4)\frac{1}{|x-y|^{N-2}}&\text{on }\partial\Omega\end{array}\right.

(4.2)

We will begin by describing how
G(x,y)
relates to the
G_{\Delta}(x,y)
, the Green’s function for the Laplacian operator on
\Omega
with Dirichlet boundary condition.

Let
H_{\Delta}(x,y)
be the regular part of
G_{\Delta}(x,y)
. Then

H_{\Delta}(x,y)=\frac{1}{|x-y|^{N-2}}-G_{\Delta}(x,y).

From the equation (
4.2
), it is immediately evident that:

H_{\Delta}(x,y):=\frac{-1}{2(N-4)}\Delta_{y}H(x,y).

On the other hand, if
u
is a solution to the problem
\Delta^{2}u=f
in
\Omega
with
u=\Delta u=0
in
\partial\Omega
then:

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} u(x)&=\int_{\Omega}G_{\Delta}(x,z)(-\Delta u(z))\,dz\\
&=\int_{\Omega}G_{\Delta}(x,z)\left(\int_{\Omega}G_{\Delta}(z,y)f(y)\,dy\right)\,dz\\
&=\int_{\Omega}\left(\int_{\Omega}G_{\Delta}(x,z)G_{\Delta}(z,y)\,dz\right)f(y)\,dy.\\
\end{aligned}

The preceding expression shows that

G(x,y)=\int_{\Omega}G_{\Delta}(x,z)G_{\Delta}(z,y)\,dz.

Moreover, since
G_{\Delta}(x,y)
is symmetric, i.e.
G_{\Delta}(x,y)=G_{\Delta}(y,x)
,it follows that

G(x,y)=\int_{\Omega}G_{\Delta}(x,z)G_{\Delta}(z,y)\,dz=\int_{\Omega}G_{\Delta}(y,z)G_{\Delta}(z,x)\,dz=G(y,x).

Thus,
G(x,y)
and
H(x,y)
are symmetric functions. Furthermore, it holds true that:

\Delta_{y}H(x,y)=\Delta_{x}H(x,y)

(4.3)

Let
r>0
, and consider the set

\Omega_{r}:=\{\xi\in\Omega:\,{\rm dist}(\xi,\partial\Omega)\leq r\}.

We will fix
r
small enough such that for every
\xi\in\Omega_{2r}
there exists a unique
p(\xi)\in\partial\Omega
with
{\rm dist}(\xi,\partial\Omega)=|\xi-p(\xi)|
. We write
d_{\xi}:=dist(\xi,\partial\Omega)
, and
\nu(\xi)
will denote the inward normal to
\partial\Omega
at
\xi
. For every
\xi\in\Omega_{2r}
we define

\widetilde{\xi}:=p(\xi)-d_{\xi}\nu(\xi)

the reflection of
\xi
on
\partial\Omega
.

Note that, since the domain
\Omega
is smooth, there exists a positive constant
C
such that:

\frac{|\widetilde{\xi}-y|}{|\xi-y|}\geq C\mbox{ for all }\xi\in\Omega_{r},y\in\Omega

(4.4)

The next lemma give us an accurate estimate of
H(x,y)
when the point
x
is close to the boundary.

Lemma 4.1
.

There exists a positive constant
C>0
such that

\displaystyle|H(x,y)-\frac{1}{|\widetilde{x}-y|^{N-4}}|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{Cd_{x}}{|\widetilde{x}-y|^{N-4}},

(4.5)

\displaystyle|\Delta_{y}H(x,y)+\frac{2(N-4)}{|\widetilde{x}-y|^{N-2}}|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{Cd_{x}}{|\widetilde{x}-y|^{N-2}},

(4.6)

and

|\nabla_{x}\left(\Delta_{y}H(x,y)+\frac{2(N-4)}{|\widetilde{x}-y|^{N-2}}\right)|\leq\frac{C}{|\widetilde{x}-y|^{N-2}}

(4.7)

for every
x\in\Omega_{r}
and
y\in\Omega
.

Proof.

We introduce the notation:

\psi(x,y)=H(x,y)-\frac{1}{|\widetilde{x}-y|^{N-4}}\ \ \ x\in\Omega_{r},y\in\Omega

Note that for each fixed
x\in\Omega_{r}
, the function

\psi(x,y)
satisfies the following problem:

\left\{\begin{array}[c]{ll}\Delta_{y}^{2}\psi(x,y)=0&\text{in }\Omega,\\
\psi(x,y)=\frac{1}{|x-y|^{N-4}}-\frac{1}{|\widetilde{x}-y|^{N-4}}&\text{on }\partial\Omega\\
\Delta_{y}\psi(x,y)=-2(N-4)\left(\frac{1}{|x-y|^{N-2}}+\frac{1}{|\widetilde{x}-y|^{N-2}}\right)&\text{on }\partial\Omega\end{array}\right.

(4.8)

Since,

\sup_{y\in\partial\Omega}|\psi(x,y)|=\sup_{y\in\partial\Omega}|\left(\frac{1}{|x-y|^{N-4}}-\frac{1}{|\widetilde{x}-y|^{N-4}}\right)|\leq\frac{Cd_{x}}{|\widetilde{x}-y|^{N-4}},

It follows from the Maximum Principle that

|H(x,y)-\frac{1}{|\widetilde{x}-y|^{N-4}}|\leq\frac{Cd_{x}}{|\widetilde{x}-y|^{N-4}},\ \ x\in\Omega_{r},y\in\Omega

This completes the proof of inequality (
4.5
).

Next, we prove equation (
4.6
). For each fixed
x\in\Omega_{r}
, the function

\Delta_{y}\psi(x,y)=\Delta_{y}H(x,y)+\frac{2(N-4)}{|\widetilde{x}-y|^{N-2}}

satisfies the problem:

\left\{\begin{array}[c]{ll}\Delta_{y}(\Delta_{y}\psi(x,y))=0&\text{in }\Omega,\\
\Delta_{y}\psi(x,y)=-2(N-4)\left(\frac{1}{|x-y|^{N-2}}-\frac{1}{|\widetilde{x}-y|^{N-2}}\right)&\text{on }\partial\Omega\end{array}\right.

(4.9)

Applying the maximum principle, we obtain:

|\Delta_{y}H(x,y)+\frac{2(n-4)}{|\widetilde{x}-y|^{N-2}}|\leq\frac{Cd_{x}}{|\widetilde{x}-y|^{N-2}}

This completes the proof of equation (
4.6
).

To verify the validity of inequality (
4.7
), we first prove that:

|\Delta_{x}\Delta_{y}\psi(x,y)|=|\Delta_{x}\left(\Delta_{y}H(x,y)+\frac{2(N-4)}{|\widetilde{x}-y|^{N-2}}\right)|\leq\frac{C}{d_{x}|\widetilde{x}-y|^{N-2}}

(4.10)

for every
x\in\Omega_{r}
and
y\in\Omega
. This inequality follows from the fact that

\Delta_{x}\Delta_{y}H(x,y)=\Delta_{x}H_{\Delta}(x,y)=0

and that, a direct calculation, which can be found in
[
8
, Lemma 2.2]
, shows that:

|\Delta_{x}\left(\frac{2(N-4)}{|\widetilde{x}-y|^{N-2}}\right)|\leq\frac{C}{d_{x}|\widetilde{x}-y|^{N-2}}\ \ \ x\in\Omega_{r},y\in\Omega

We now proceed with the proof of the equation (
4.7
). We will rely on from
[
20
, Theorem 3.9]
. Fix
x\in\Omega_{r}
,
y\in\Omega
, and
r:=2\sqrt{N}
. Consider the set

B:=\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\mid|x-\xi|_{\infty}\leq d_{x}/r\}.

Note that if
\xi\in B
then

|\xi-x|<d_{x}/2

which implies

d_{x}/2\leq d_{\xi}\leq 3d_{x}/2.

(4.11)

Then, for
i=1,2,\dots,N
, using inequalities (
4.6
), (
4.10
) and (
4.11
), we have:

\displaystyle|\partial_{x_{i}}\Delta_{y}\psi(x,y)|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{rN}{d_{x}}\sup_{\xi\in\partial B}|\Delta_{y}\psi(\xi,y)|+\frac{d_{x}}{2r}\sup_{\xi\in B}|\Delta_{\xi}\Delta_{y}\psi(\xi,y)|

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\sup_{\xi\in\partial B}\frac{d_{\xi}}{d_{x}}\frac{1}{|\widetilde{\xi}-y|^{N-2}}+\sup_{\xi\in B}\frac{d_{x}}{d_{\xi}}\frac{1}{|\widetilde{\xi}-y|^{N-2}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\sup_{\xi\in B}\frac{1}{|\widetilde{\xi}-y|^{N-2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{|\widetilde{x}-y|^{N-2}}.

This completes the proof of inequality (
4.7
).
∎

One consequence of the previous lemma is that, given the symmetry described by (
4.3
):

\Delta_{y}H(x,y)=\Delta_{x}H(x,y)

the inequalities (
4.6
) and (
4.7
) can be rewritten as:

\displaystyle\left|\Delta_{x}H(x,y)+\frac{2(N-4)}{|\widetilde{x}-y|^{N-2}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{Cd_{x}}{|\widetilde{x}-y|^{N-2}}

(4.12)

and

\displaystyle\left|\nabla_{x}\left(\Delta_{x}H(x,y)+\frac{2(N-4)}{|\widetilde{x}-y|^{N-2}}\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{|\widetilde{x}-y|^{N-2}}

(4.13)

for all
x\in\Omega_{r}
and
y\in\Omega
. Another consequence of Lemma
4.1
is the following:

Corollary 4.2
.

There exists a positive constant
C>0
such that

\displaystyle\left|\Delta_{y}H(x,y)\right|=\left|\Delta_{x}H(x,y)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{|x-y|^{N-2}}

(4.14)

and

\displaystyle\left|\nabla_{x}\Delta_{x}H(x,y)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{|x-y|^{N-1}}

(4.15)

for every
x,y\in\Omega
.

Proof.

Since
H(x,y)
satisfies the problem (
4.2
), there exists a constant
C>0
such that

|\Delta_{x}H(x,y)|\leq C

(4.16)

and

|\nabla_{x}\Delta_{x}H(x,y)|\leq C

(4.17)

for every
x\in\Omega\setminus\Omega_{r}
and every
y\in\Omega
.

We will now prove inequality (
4.14
). Note that, by inequality (
4.4
), it follows that:

\frac{2(N-4)}{|\widetilde{x}-y|^{N-2}}\leq\frac{C}{|x-y|^{N-2}}\mbox{ for all }x\in\Omega_{r},y\in\Omega

(4.18)

for some positive constant
C
.
Thus, the inequality (
4.14
) follows from (
4.16
), (
4.12
), and (
4.18
).

We will now focus on proving the inequality (
4.17
). Direct computations, similar to those in the proof of
[
8
]
, show that

\left|\nabla_{x}\left(\frac{2(N-4)}{|\widetilde{x}-y|^{N-2}}\right)\right|\leq\frac{C}{|x-y|^{N-1}}.

Therefore, using (
4.13
) and (
4.18
), we obtain

\displaystyle|\nabla_{x}\left(\Delta_{x}H(x,y)\right)|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{|\widetilde{x}-y|^{N-2}}+|\nabla_{x}\left(\frac{2(N-4)}{|\widetilde{x}-y|^{N-2}}\right)|

(4.19)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{|x-y|^{N-2}}+\frac{C}{|x-y|^{N-1}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{|x-y|^{N-1}}

for every
x\in\Omega_{r}
and
y\in\Omega
. This completes the proof of the corollary.
∎

5
Approximation of Solutions and Bubble Projections

In this section, we establish the technical estimates required for analyzing the projection of bubble functions. These approximations allow us to quantify the discrepancy between the bubble solutions and their projections onto the functional space. This analysis provides us with the asymptotic behavior of the solutions.

We begin by exploring the relationship between the bubble
U_{\delta,\xi}(x)
and its projection
PU_{\delta,\xi}(x)
when the point
\xi
is close to the boundary
\partial\Omega
.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
\delta\in(0,1]
and
\xi\in\Omega_{r}
. Let
\widetilde{\xi}
be the reflection of
\xi
with respect to the boundary
\partial\Omega
. Then, there exits a constant
c>0
such that:

0\leq PU_{\delta,\xi}(x)\leq U_{\delta,\xi}(x)

(5.1)

and

0\leq U_{\delta,\xi}(x)-PU_{\delta,\xi}(x)\leq\alpha_{N}\delta^{\frac{N-4}{2}}H(x,\xi)\leq c\frac{\delta^{\frac{N-4}{2}}}{|x-\widetilde{\xi}|^{N-4}}

(5.2)

for every
x\in\Omega
. Furthermore,

R_{\delta,\xi}(x):=PU_{\delta,\xi}(x)-U_{\delta,\xi}(x)+\alpha_{N}\delta^{\frac{N-4}{2}}H(x,\xi)

where the remainder term
R_{\delta,\xi}
satisfies the following estimate:

|R_{\delta,\xi}|_{\Omega,\infty}=O\left(\frac{\delta^{\frac{N}{2}}}{dist(\xi,\partial\Omega)^{N-2}}\right).

(5.3)

Proof.

The inequalities (
5.1
) and (
5.2
) are derived directly from the maximum principle. We will focus on proving the inequality (
5.3
).
Note that the function
R_{\delta,\xi}
satisfies the following boundary value problem:

\left\{\begin{array}[c]{ll}\Delta_{x}^{2}R_{\delta,\xi}(x)=0&\text{in }\Omega,\\
\Delta_{x}R_{\delta,\xi}(x)=-\Delta_{x}U_{\delta,\xi}(x)-\alpha_{N}2(N-4)\delta^{\frac{N-4}{2}}\frac{1}{|x-\xi|^{N-2}}&\text{on }\partial\Omega\\
R_{\delta,\xi}(x)=-U_{\delta,\xi}(x)+\alpha_{N}\delta^{\frac{N-4}{2}}\frac{1}{|x-\xi|^{N-4}}&\text{on }\partial\Omega\end{array}\right.

(5.4)

Thus, by applying the maximum principle, we obtain:

\displaystyle|R_{\delta,\xi}|_{\Omega,\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\alpha_{N}\delta^{\frac{N-4}{2}}\max_{x\in\partial\Omega}\left|\frac{1}{(\delta^{2}+|x-\xi|^{2})^{\frac{N-4}{2}}}-\frac{1}{|x-\xi|^{N-4}}\right|_{\Omega,\infty}

\displaystyle=O\left(\delta^{\frac{N-4}{2}}\max_{x\in\partial\Omega}\frac{\delta^{2}}{|x-\xi|^{N-2}}\right)=O\left(\frac{\delta^{\frac{N}{2}}}{dist(\xi,\partial\Omega)^{N-2}}\right)

This completes the desired estimate.

∎

This previous result provides a precise bound on the difference between the bubble
U_{\delta,\xi}(x)
and its projection
PU_{\delta,\xi}(x)
, which plays a crucial role in our analysis of the asymptotic behavior. We use this result to prove:

Remark 5.2
.

Let
\xi_{i},\xi_{j}\in\Omega
and such that

\xi_{i}:=\xi_{i}^{0}+\tau_{i}\nu(\xi_{i}^{0})\mbox{ and }\xi_{j}:=\xi_{j}^{0}+\tau_{j}\nu(\xi_{j}^{0})

where

\tau_{i}=\varepsilon t_{i}\mbox{and }\tau_{i}=\varepsilon t_{j}\mbox{ with }t_{i}<t_{j}.

Set

\eta:=\min\{d(\xi_{1},\partial\Omega),d(\xi_{2},\partial\Omega),\frac{|\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}|}{2}\}=\min\{t_{1}\varepsilon,t_{2}\varepsilon,\frac{\varepsilon|t_{1}-t_{2}|}{2}\}

and, as before:

R_{\delta_{j},\xi_{j}}(x)=PU_{\delta_{j},\xi_{j}}(x)-U_{\delta_{j},\xi_{j}}(x)+\alpha_{N}\delta_{j}^{\frac{N-4}{2}}H(x,\xi_{j})

Then:

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\eta}(\xi)}a(x)U_{\delta_{i},\xi_{i}}^{\frac{N+4}{N-4}}(x)R_{\delta_{j},\xi_{j}}(x)\,dx=O\left(\frac{\delta_{j}^{\frac{N}{2}}\delta_{i}^{\frac{N-4}{2}}}{\varepsilon^{N-2}}\right)

(5.5)

and

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\eta}(\xi_{i})}U_{\delta_{i},\xi_{i}}^{\frac{N+4}{N-4}}(x)\left(\alpha_{N}\delta_{j}^{\frac{N-4}{2}}H(x,\xi_{j})\right)\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{\eta}(\xi_{i})}U_{\delta_{i},\xi_{i}}^{\frac{N+4}{N-4}}(x)\frac{\alpha_{N}\delta_{j}^{\frac{N-4}{2}}}{|x-\widetilde{\xi}_{j}|^{N-4}}\,dx+O\left(\varepsilon\frac{(\delta_{i}\delta_{j})^{\frac{N-4}{2}}}{\varepsilon^{N-4}}\right)

(5.6)

Proof.

To prove the equation (
5.5
), we directly used estimation (
5.3
) to obtain:

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\eta}(\xi)}a(x)U_{\delta_{i},\xi_{i}}^{\frac{N+4}{N-4}}(x)R_{\delta_{j},\xi_{j}}(x)\,dx

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\frac{\delta_{j}^{\frac{N}{2}}}{\varepsilon^{N-2}}\right)\int_{B_{\eta}(\xi)}\frac{\delta_{i}^{\frac{N+4}{2}}}{\left(\delta_{i}^{2}+|x-\xi_{i}|^{2}\right)^{\frac{N+4}{2}}}\,dx

\displaystyle=C\left(\frac{\delta_{j}^{\frac{N}{2}}}{\varepsilon^{N-2}}\right)\int_{B_{\frac{\eta}{\delta_{i}}}(0)}\frac{\delta_{i}^{N+\frac{N+4}{2}}}{\left(\delta_{i}^{2}+|\delta_{i}y|^{2}\right)^{\frac{N+4}{2}}}\,dx

\displaystyle=O\left(\frac{\delta_{j}^{\frac{N}{2}}\delta_{i}^{\frac{N-4}{2}}}{\varepsilon^{N-2}}\right)

i.e.

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\eta}(\xi_{i})}a(x)U_{\delta_{i},\xi_{i}}^{\frac{N+4}{N-4}}(x)R_{\delta_{j},\xi_{j}}(x)\,dx=O\left(\frac{\delta_{j}^{\frac{N}{2}}\delta_{i}^{\frac{N-4}{2}}}{\varepsilon^{N-2}}\right)

Now we will prove (
5.6
). Using the estimation given in (
4.5
), we obtain:

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\eta}(\xi_{i})}U_{\delta_{i},\xi_{i}}^{\frac{N+4}{N-4}}(x)\left(\alpha_{N}\,\delta_{j}^{\frac{N-4}{2}}H(x,\widetilde{\xi}_{j})-\frac{\alpha_{N}\delta_{j}^{\frac{N-4}{2}}}{\left|x-\widetilde{\xi}_{j}\right|^{N-4}}\right)\,dx

\displaystyle=O\left(\delta_{j}^{\frac{N-4}{2}}\int_{B_{\eta}(\xi_{i})}U_{\delta_{i},\xi_{i}}^{\frac{N+4}{N-4}}(x)\frac{\tau_{j}}{\left|x-\widetilde{\xi}_{j}\right|^{N-4}}\,dx\right)

\displaystyle=O\left(\varepsilon\,\delta_{j}^{\frac{N-4}{2}}\int_{B_{\frac{\eta}{\delta_{i}}}(0)}\frac{\delta_{i}^{N-\frac{N+4}{2}}}{\left(1+|y|^{2}\right)^{\frac{N+4}{2}}}\frac{1}{\left|\delta_{i}y+\xi_{i}-\widetilde{\xi}_{j}\right|^{N-4}}\,dy\right)

\displaystyle=O\left(\varepsilon\,\delta_{j}^{\frac{N-4}{2}}\dfrac{\delta_{i}^{\frac{N-4}{2}}}{\varepsilon^{N-4}}\int_{B_{\frac{\eta}{\delta_{i}}}(0)}\dfrac{1}{\left(1+|y|^{2}\right)^{\frac{N+4}{2}}}\frac{1}{\left|\frac{\delta_{i}}{\varepsilon}y+\frac{\xi_{i}-\widetilde{\xi}_{j}}{\varepsilon}\right|^{N-4}}\,dy\right)

\displaystyle=O\left(\varepsilon\,\dfrac{(\delta_{i}\,\delta_{j})^{\frac{N-4}{2}}}{\varepsilon^{N-4}}\int_{B_{\dfrac{\eta}{\delta_{i}}}(0)}\dfrac{1}{\left(1+|y|^{2}\right)^{\frac{N+4}{2}}}\,dy\right)=O\left(\varepsilon\,\dfrac{\left(\delta_{i}\,\delta_{j}\right)^{\frac{N-4}{2}}}{\varepsilon^{N-4}}\right)=o(\varepsilon),

because, since
y\in B_{\frac{\eta}{\delta_{i}}}(0)
, then
\frac{\delta_{i}}{\varepsilon}|y|<\frac{\eta}{\varepsilon}
and therefore:

\left|\frac{\delta_{i}}{\varepsilon}y+\frac{\xi_{i}-\widetilde{\xi}_{j}}{\varepsilon}\right|\geq\left|\frac{\xi_{i}-\widetilde{\xi}_{j}}{\varepsilon}\right|-\left|\frac{\delta_{i}}{\varepsilon}y\right|=(t_{1}+t_{2})-\min\left\{t_{1},t_{2},\frac{|t_{1}-t_{2}|}{2}\right\}\geq\min\left\{t_{1},t_{2},\frac{|t_{1}-t_{2}|}{2}\right\}.

∎

In the rest of this work, we will use Remark
5.2
to prove various estimates. We begin by proving the following asymptotic expansions:

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
\xi_{i}\in\Omega
and
\eta
defined as in Remark
5.2
, then:

\int_{B_{\eta}(\xi_{i})}a(x)U_{\delta_{i},\xi_{i}}^{\frac{2N}{N-4}}(x)\,dx=a(\xi_{i}^{0})\gamma_{1}+\varepsilon t_{i}\nabla a(\xi_{i}^{0})\cdot\nu(\xi_{i}^{0})\gamma_{1}+o(\varepsilon^{1+\sigma}),

and

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\eta}(\xi_{i})}a(x)\,U_{\delta_{i},\xi_{i}}^{\frac{N+4}{N-4}}(x)\left(PU_{\delta_{i},\xi_{i}}(x)-U_{\delta_{i},\xi_{i}}(x)\right)\,dx=-\varepsilon\left(\frac{d_{i}}{2t_{i}}\right)^{N-4}a(\xi_{i}^{0})\,\gamma_{2}+o\left(\varepsilon^{1+\sigma}\right).

Here:

\gamma_{1}=\alpha_{N}^{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{1}{\left(1+|y|^{2}\right)^{N}}\,dy\ \ \quad\mbox{ and }\quad\ \ \gamma_{2}:=a_{N}^{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{1}{\left(1+|y|^{2}\right)^{\frac{N+4}{2}}}\,dy.

Proof.

We write:

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \int_{B_{\eta}(\xi_{i})}a(x)\,U_{\delta_{i},\xi_{i}}^{\frac{2N}{N-4}}(x)\,dx&=\int_{B_{\eta}(\xi_{i})}(a(x)-a(\xi_{i}^{0}))U_{\delta_{i},\xi_{i}}^{\frac{2N}{N-4}}(x)\,dx+\int_{B_{\eta}(\xi_{i})}a(\xi_{i}^{0})U_{\delta_{i},\xi_{i}}^{\frac{2N}{N-4}}(x)\,dx.\end{aligned}

On one hand, we obtain:

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\eta}(\xi_{i})}a(\xi_{i}^{0})U_{\delta_{i},\xi_{i}}^{\frac{2N}{N-4}}(x)\,dx

\displaystyle=\alpha_{N}^{p}a(\xi_{i}^{0})\int_{B_{\eta}(\xi_{i})}\frac{\delta_{i}^{N}}{\left(\delta_{i}^{2}+|x-\xi_{i}|^{2}\right)^{N}}\,dx

(5.7)

\displaystyle=\alpha_{N}^{p}a(\xi_{i}^{0})\int_{B_{\frac{\eta}{\delta_{i}}}(0)}\frac{1}{\left(1+|y|^{2}\right)^{N}}\,dy

\displaystyle=a(\xi_{i}^{0})\left(\gamma_{1}+O\left(\left(\frac{\delta_{i}}{\varepsilon}\right)^{N}\right)\right)=a(\xi_{i}^{0})\gamma_{1}+o(\varepsilon).

On the other hand, since

a(\delta_{i}y+\xi_{i}^{0}+\tau_{i}\nu(\xi_{i}^{0}))=a(\xi_{i}^{0})+\nabla a(\xi_{i}^{0})\cdot(\tau_{i}\nu(\xi_{i}^{0})+\delta_{i}y)+R(y),

where
|R(y)|=O\left(|\delta_{i}^{2}|y|^{2}+\delta_{i}\tau_{i}|y|+\tau_{i}^{2}|\right)
, then we have

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \int_{B_{\eta}(\xi_{i})}(a(x)-a(\xi_{i}^{0}))U_{\delta_{i},\xi_{i}}^{\frac{2N}{N-4}}(x)\,dx&=\displaystyle\int_{B_{\eta}(\xi_{i})}\left(a(x)-a(\xi_{i}^{0})\right)U_{\delta_{i},\xi_{i}}^{\frac{2N}{N-4}}(x)\,dx\\
&=\alpha_{N}^{p}\displaystyle\int_{B_{\frac{\eta}{\delta_{i}}}(0)}\frac{\left(a(\delta_{i}y+\xi_{i}^{0}+\tau_{i}\nu(\xi_{i}^{0}))-a(\xi_{i}^{0})\right)}{\left(1+|y|^{2}\right)^{N}}\,dy\\
&=\alpha_{N}^{p}\displaystyle\int_{B_{\frac{\eta}{\delta_{i}}}(0)}\frac{\nabla a(\xi_{i}^{0})\cdot(\tau_{i}\nu(\xi_{i}^{0})+\delta_{i}y)+R(y)}{\left(1+|y|^{2}\right)^{N}}\,dy\\
&=\varepsilon t_{i}\nabla a(\xi_{i}^{0})\cdot\nu(\xi_{i}^{0})\gamma_{1}+o(\varepsilon).\end{aligned}

Therefore:

\int_{B_{\eta}(\xi_{i})}a(x)U_{\delta_{i},\xi_{i}}^{\frac{2N}{N-4}}(x)\,dx=a(\xi_{i}^{0})\gamma_{1}+\tau_{i}\nabla a(\xi_{i}^{0})\cdot\nu(\xi_{i}^{0})\gamma_{1}+o(\varepsilon).

Now, we continue with the expansion of the term

\int_{B_{\eta}(\xi_{i})}a(x)U_{\delta_{i},\xi_{i}}^{\frac{N+4}{N-4}}\left(PU_{\delta_{i},\xi_{i}}(x)-U_{\delta,\xi_{i}}(x)\right)\,dx

By equations (
5.5
) and (
5.6
), we obtain:

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\eta}(\xi_{i})}a(x)\,U_{\delta_{i},\xi_{i}}^{\frac{N+4}{N-4}}(x)\left(PU_{\delta_{i},\xi_{i}}(x)-U_{\delta_{i},\xi_{i}}(x)\right)\,dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\eta}(\xi_{i})}a(x)\,U_{\delta_{i},\xi_{i}}^{\frac{N+4}{N-4}}(x)\left(-\alpha_{N}\,\delta_{i}^{\frac{N-4}{2}}H(x,\xi_{i})+R_{\delta_{i},\xi_{i}}(x)\right)\,dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{B_{\eta}(\xi_{i})}a(x)\,U_{\delta_{i},\xi_{i}}^{\frac{N+4}{N-4}}(x)\frac{\alpha_{N}\,\delta_{i}^{\frac{N-4}{2}}}{|x-\widetilde{\xi}_{i}|^{N-4}}\,dx+o(\varepsilon)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\alpha_{N}^{p}\,\delta_{i}^{N-4}\int_{B_{\frac{\eta}{\delta_{i}}}(0)}a\left(\delta y+\xi_{i}\right)\frac{1}{\left(1+|y|^{2}\right)^{\frac{N+4}{2}}}\left(\frac{1}{|\delta_{i}y+\xi_{i}-\widetilde{\xi_{i}}|^{N-4}}\right)\,dy+o(\varepsilon)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\alpha_{N}^{p}\,\delta_{i}^{N-4}\,a(\xi_{i}^{0})\left(\int_{B_{\frac{\eta}{\delta_{i}}}(0)}\frac{1}{\left(1+|y|^{2}\right)^{\frac{N+4}{2}}}\left(\frac{1}{\left|\delta_{i}y+\xi_{i}^{0}+\tau_{i}\nu(\xi_{i}^{0})-(\xi_{i}^{0}-\tau_{i}\nu(\xi_{i}^{0}))\right|^{N-4}}\right)\,dy\right)\left(1+O(\varepsilon)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\alpha_{N}^{p}\,\delta_{i}^{N-4}\,a(\xi_{i}^{0})\left(\int_{B_{\frac{\eta}{\delta_{i}}}(0)}\frac{1}{\left(1+|y|^{2}\right)^{\frac{N+4}{2}}}\left(\frac{1}{\left|\delta_{i}y+2\tau_{i}\nu(\xi_{i}^{0})\right|^{N-4}}\right)\,dy\right)(1+O(\varepsilon))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\left(\frac{\delta_{i}}{2\tau_{i}}\right)^{N-4}a(\xi_{i}^{0})\,\gamma_{2}\,(1+O(\varepsilon))=-\varepsilon\left(\frac{d_{i}}{2t_{i}}\right)^{N-4}a(\xi_{i}^{0})\,\gamma_{2}+o(\varepsilon).

The last statement is true because:

\left|\delta_{i}y+2\tau_{i}\eta(\xi_{i}^{0})\right|^{-(N-4)}=|2\tau_{i}\eta(\xi_{i}^{0})|^{-(N-4)}+O\left(\frac{\tau_{i}\delta_{i}|y|}{\tau_{i}^{N}}\right).

This concludes the proof of the lemma
5.3
.
∎

Proposition 5.4

Proof.

\displaystyle\alpha_{N}^{p}\int_{B_{\eta}(\xi)}a(x)\frac{\delta_{i}^{\frac{N+4}{2}}}{(\delta_{i}^{2}+|x-\xi_{i}|^{2})^{\frac{N+4}{2}}}\left(\frac{\delta_{j}^{\frac{N-4}{2}}}{(\delta_{j}^{2}+|x-\xi_{j}|^{2})^{\frac{N-4}{2}}}\right)\,dx

\displaystyle=O\left(\frac{(\delta_{i}\delta_{j})^{\frac{N-4}{2}}}{\varepsilon^{N-4}}\left(\frac{\delta_{j}}{\varepsilon}\right)^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{1}{(1+\left|y\right|^{2})^{\frac{N+4}{2}}}\,dx\right)

\displaystyle=o(\varepsilon),

because, since

\eta:=\min\left\{d(\xi_{i},\partial\Omega),d(\xi_{j},\partial\Omega),\frac{|\xi_{i}-\xi_{j}|}{2}\right\},

Hence, using equations (
5.5
) and (

), we obtain:

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\eta}(\xi_{i})}a(x)\Delta^{2}(PU_{\delta_{i},\xi_{i}})PU_{\delta_{j},\xi_{j}}\,dx

(5.10)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\eta}(\xi)}a(x)U_{\delta_{i},\xi_{i}}^{\frac{N+4}{N-4}}\left(U_{\delta_{j},\xi_{j}}(x)-\alpha_{N}\delta_{j}^{\frac{N-4}{2}}H(x,\xi_{j})+R_{\delta_{j},\xi_{j}}(x)\right)\,dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha_{N}^{p}\int_{B_{\eta}(\xi)}a(x)\frac{\delta_{i}^{\frac{N+4}{2}}}{(\delta_{i}^{2}+|x-\xi_{i}|^{2})^{\frac{N+4}{2}}}\left(\frac{\delta_{j}^{\frac{N-4}{2}}}{(\delta_{j}^{2}+|x-\xi_{j}|^{2})^{\frac{N-4}{2}}}-\delta_{j}^{\frac{N-4}{2}}H(x,\xi_{j})\right)\,dx+o\left(\varepsilon\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle o(\varepsilon).

(5.11)

\displaystyle=

6
Error Estimates

This section focuses on estimating the leading terms of the error
E_{(\boldsymbol{\xi},\textbf{d},\textbf{t})}
defined in (
2.6
).

We begin by presenting some preliminary estimates. As before, consider the bubble:

U_{\delta,\xi}(x):=\alpha_{N}\left(\frac{\delta}{\delta^{2}+|x-\xi|^{2}}\right)^{\frac{N-4}{2}},\ \ x,\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{N},\delta\in\mathbb{R}^{+}

then,

\displaystyle|U_{\delta,\xi}|_{q}^{q}

\displaystyle=\alpha_{N}^{q}\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{\delta}{\delta^{2}+|x-\xi|^{2}}\right)^{\frac{N-4}{2}q}\,dx

\displaystyle=\alpha_{N}^{q}\int_{\frac{\Omega-\xi}{\delta}}\frac{\delta^{\frac{N-4}{2}q+N}}{\delta^{(N-4)q}\left(1+|y|^{2}\right)^{\frac{N-4}{2}q}}\,dy=O\left(\delta^{-\frac{N-4}{2}q+N}\right).

Hence we have:

\left|U_{\delta,\xi}\right|_{q}=O(\delta^{\frac{N}{q}-\frac{N-4}{2}}),\ \ \ \ \ \ \mbox{ if }q>\frac{N}{N-4},

and furthermore, since

\displaystyle\left|U_{\delta,\xi}^{\frac{N+4}{N-4}}\right|_{q}^{q}

\displaystyle=\alpha_{N}^{q}\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{\delta}{\delta^{2}+|x-\xi|^{2}}\right)^{\frac{N-4}{2}\left(\frac{N+4}{N-4}q\right)}\,dx

\displaystyle=\alpha_{N}^{q}\int_{\frac{\Omega-\xi}{\delta}}\frac{\delta^{\frac{N+4}{2}q+N}}{\delta^{(N+4)q}\left(1+|y|^{2}\right)^{\frac{N+4}{2}q}}\,dy=O\left(\delta^{-\frac{N+4}{2}q+N}\right),

we obtain

\left|U_{\delta,\xi}^{\frac{N+4}{N-4}}\right|_{q}=O\left(\delta^{\frac{N}{q}-\frac{N+4}{2}}\right),\ \ \ \ \ \ \mbox{ if }q\geq 1.

Next we estimate some terms related to the derivatives of the bubble.

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
\xi_{i},\xi_{j}\in\Omega
and
\eta
defined as in Remark
5.2
. The following estimates hold:

\int_{\Omega}\nabla\left(\Delta(PU_{\delta_{i},\xi_{i}})\right)PU_{\delta_{j},\xi_{j}}\,dx=O\left(\frac{\delta_{i}}{\delta_{j}}\right)^{\frac{N-4}{2}}O\left(\delta_{j}^{\frac{N-4}{N-3}+\beta_{1}}\right),

(6.1)

and

\int_{\Omega}\Delta(PU_{\delta_{i},\xi_{i}})PU_{\delta_{j},\xi_{j}}\,dx=O\left(\frac{\delta_{i}}{\delta_{j}}\right)^{\frac{N-4}{2}}O\left(\delta_{j}^{\frac{N-4}{N-3}+\beta_{2}}\right).

(6.2)

Moreover, we also have

\left|\nabla_{x}\left(\Delta\left(PU_{\delta_{i},\xi_{i}}\right)\right)\right|_{\frac{2N}{N+4}}=O\left(\delta^{\frac{N-4}{2(N-3)}+\beta}\right),

(6.3)

and

\left|\Delta\left(PU_{\delta_{i},\xi_{i}}\right)\right|_{\frac{2N}{N+4}}=O\left(\delta^{\frac{N-4}{2(N-3)}+\beta}\right).

(6.4)

Proof.

As the function
(\Delta PU_{\delta_{i},\xi_{i}})
satisfies the problem:

\left\{\begin{array}[c]{ll}\Delta(\Delta PU_{\delta_{i},\xi_{i}})=U_{\delta_{i},\xi_{i}}^{\frac{N+4}{N-4}}&\text{in }\Omega,\\
\Delta PU_{\delta_{i},\xi_{i}}=0&\text{on }\partial\Omega\end{array}\right.

(6.5)

we have that:

-\Delta(PU_{\delta_{i},\xi_{i}})(x)=\int_{\Omega}G_{\Delta}(x,y)\,U_{\delta_{i},\xi_{i}}(y)^{\frac{N+4}{N-4}}\,dy

and

-\nabla_{x}\left(\Delta(PU_{\delta_{i},\xi_{i}})\right)(x)=\int_{\Omega}\nabla_{x}(G_{\Delta}(x,y))\,U_{\delta_{i},\xi_{i}}(y)^{\frac{N+4}{N-4}}\,dy,

where
G_{\Delta}(x,y)
is the Green’s function for the Laplacian with Dirichlet boundary condition.

We begin by proving estimate (
6.1
). Choose an element
r^{\prime}\in\left(N,\frac{N(N-3)}{N-4}\right)
. If
r
is such that
\frac{1}{r}+\frac{1}{r^{\prime}}+1=2
then
r\in\left(\frac{N(N-3)}{(N-2)^{2}},\frac{N}{N-1}\right)
.
For these values of
r
and
r^{\prime}
, we have the following implications:

\displaystyle r<\frac{N}{N-1}

\displaystyle\implies|Q_{1}(x)|_{\Omega,r}:=\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{|x|^{r(N-1)}}\,dx<\infty

\displaystyle r>\frac{N(N-3)}{(N-2)^{2}}\geq 1

\displaystyle\implies\left|U_{\delta,\xi}^{\frac{N+4}{N-4}}\right|_{1}=O\left(\delta^{N-\frac{N+4}{2}}\right)

\displaystyle r^{\prime}<\frac{N(N-3)}{N-4}

\displaystyle\implies\frac{N}{r^{\prime}}>\frac{N-4}{N-3}

\displaystyle r^{\prime}>N\geq\frac{N}{N-4}

\displaystyle\implies\left|U_{\delta,\xi}\right|_{r^{\prime}}=O\left(\delta^{\frac{N}{r^{\prime}}-\frac{N-4}{2}}\right).

To estimate (
6.1
) we rely on Young’s inequality (see Theorem 4.2 in
[
24
]
). Using (
4.21
), we obtain:

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\Omega}\nabla\left(\Delta(PU_{\delta_{i},\xi_{i}})\right)PU_{\delta_{i},\xi_{i}}\,dx\right|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla_{x}(G_{\Delta}(x,y))U_{\delta_{i},\xi_{i}}(y)^{\frac{N+4}{N-4}}PU_{\delta_{j},\xi_{j}}(x)\right|\,dy\,dx

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}\left|\frac{1}{|x-y|^{N-1}}U_{\delta_{i},\xi_{i}}(y)^{\frac{N+4}{N-4}}PU_{\delta_{j},\xi_{j}}(x)\right|\,dy\,dx

\displaystyle\leq C\left|Q_{1}(x)\right|_{\Omega,r}\left|U_{\delta_{i},\xi_{i}}(y)^{\frac{N+4}{N-4}}\right|_{1}\left|U_{\delta_{j},\xi_{j}}(x)\right|_{r^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=O\left(\delta_{i}^{N-\frac{N+4}{2}}\right)O\left(\delta_{j}^{\frac{N}{r^{\prime}}-\frac{N-4}{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=O\left(\frac{\delta_{i}}{\delta_{j}}\right)^{\frac{N-4}{2}}O\left(\delta_{j}^{\frac{N}{r^{\prime}}}\right)

\displaystyle=O\left(\frac{\delta_{i}}{\delta_{j}}\right)^{\frac{N-4}{2}}O\left(\delta_{j}^{\frac{N-4}{N-3}+\beta}\right),

for some
\beta>0
.

Now, we continue with the proof of equation (
6.2
). We first consider the case where
N\geq 6
. Choose an element
r^{\prime}
such that
r^{\prime}\in\left(\frac{N}{2},\frac{N(N-3)}{N-4}\right)

r

and

, the following implications hold:

\displaystyle r<\frac{N}{N-2}

\displaystyle\implies\left|Q_{2}(x)\right|_{\Omega,r}:=\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{|x|^{r(N-2)}}\,dx<\infty

\displaystyle r>\frac{N(N-3)}{(N-2)^{2}}\geq 1

\displaystyle\implies\left|U_{\delta,\xi}^{\frac{N+4}{N-4}}\right|_{1}=O\left(\delta^{N-\frac{N+4}{2}}\right)

\displaystyle r^{\prime}<\frac{N(N-3)}{N-4}

\displaystyle\implies\frac{N}{r^{\prime}}>\frac{N-4}{N-3}

\displaystyle r^{\prime}>\frac{N}{2}\geq\frac{N}{N-4}

\displaystyle\implies\left|U_{\delta,\xi}\right|_{r^{\prime}}=O\left(\delta^{\frac{N}{r^{\prime}}-\frac{N-4}{2}}\right).

For example, if
N=5
,
r^{\prime}\in(5,10)
and

To estimate (
6.2
) we use again Young’s inequality, and apply (
4.20
) to obtain:

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\Omega}\Delta(PU_{\delta_{i},\xi_{i}})PU_{\delta_{j},\xi_{j}}\,dx\right|

\displaystyle-\frac{\varepsilon}{p}\int_{\Omega}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\varepsilon 2a(\zeta^{0})\frac{\gamma_{3}}{p}+\varepsilon\frac{N-4}{2}a(\zeta^{0})\log(d_{1}+d_{2})\frac{\gamma_{1}}{p}+\varepsilon\log(\varepsilon)(N-3)a(\zeta^{0})\frac{\gamma_{1}}{p}+o(\varepsilon),

Lemma 7.2
.

The following estimate holds true:

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}a(x)|PU_{\delta_{1},\xi_{1}}-PU_{\delta_{2},\xi_{2}}|^{p}\,dx=

\displaystyle 2a(\zeta^{0})\gamma_{1}+\varepsilon(t_{1}+t_{2})\nabla a(\zeta^{0})\cdot\nu(\zeta^{0})\gamma_{1}

(7.2)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle pa(\zeta^{0})\varepsilon\left(\left(\frac{d_{1}}{2t_{1}}\right)^{N-4}+\left(\frac{d_{1}}{2t_{1}}\right)^{N-4}\right)\gamma_{2}

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle 2pa(\zeta^{0})\varepsilon(d_{1}d_{2})^{\frac{N-4}{2}}\left(\frac{1}{|t_{1}-t_{2}|^{N-4}}-\frac{1}{|t_{1}+t_{2}|^{N-4}}\right)\gamma_{2}+o(\varepsilon).

Proof.

We write:

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}a(x)|PU_{\delta_{1},\xi_{1}}-PU_{\delta_{2},\xi_{2}}|^{p}\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}a(x)\left(|PU_{\delta_{1},\xi_{1}}-PU_{\delta_{2},\xi_{2}}|^{p}-U_{\delta_{1},\xi_{1}}^{p}-U_{\delta_{2},\xi_{2}}^{p}\right)\,dx

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}a(x)U_{\delta_{1},\xi_{1}}^{p}\,dx+\int_{\Omega}a(x)U_{\delta_{2},\xi_{2}}^{p}\,dx.

From estimation (
5.3
), it can be directly deduced:

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}a(x)U_{\delta_{1},\xi_{1}}^{p}+\int_{\Omega}a(x)U_{\delta_{2},\xi_{2}}^{p}\,dx

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{2}\left(a(\zeta^{0})\gamma_{1}+\varepsilon t_{i}\nabla a(\zeta^{0})\cdot\nu(\zeta^{0})\gamma_{1}\right)+o(\varepsilon)

\displaystyle=2a(\zeta^{0})\gamma_{1}+\varepsilon(t_{1}+t_{2})\nabla a(\zeta^{0})\cdot\nu(\zeta^{0})\gamma_{1}+o(\varepsilon).

We will devote the rest of the proof to estimating the following term:

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}a(x)\left(|PU_{\delta_{1},\xi_{1}}-PU_{\delta_{2},\xi_{2}}|^{p}-U_{\delta_{1},\xi_{1}}^{p}-U_{\delta_{2},\xi_{2}}^{p}\right)\,dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\eta}(\xi_{1})}a(x)\left(|PU_{\delta_{1},\xi_{1}}-PU_{\delta_{2},\xi_{2}}|^{p}-U_{\delta_{1},\xi_{1}}^{p}-U_{\delta_{2},\xi_{2}}^{p}\right)\,dx

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\eta}(\xi_{2})}a(x)\left(|PU_{\delta_{1},\xi_{1}}-PU_{\delta_{2},\xi_{2}}|^{p}-U_{\delta_{1},\xi_{1}}^{p}-U_{\delta_{2},\xi_{2}}^{p}\right)\,dx

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega\setminus(B_{\eta}(\xi_{1})\cup B_{\eta}(\xi_{2}))}a(x)\left(|PU_{\delta_{1},\xi_{1}}-PU_{\delta_{2},\xi_{2}}|^{p}-U_{\delta_{1},\xi_{1}}^{p}-U_{\delta_{2},\xi_{2}}^{p}\right)\,dx.

A straightforward calculation shows that:

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega\setminus(B_{\eta}(\xi_{1})\cup B_{\eta}(\xi_{2}))}a(x)\left(|PU_{\delta_{1},\xi_{1}}-PU_{\delta_{2},\xi_{2}}|^{p}-U_{\delta_{1},\xi_{1}}^{p}-U_{\delta_{2},\xi_{2}}^{p}\right)\,dx=o(\varepsilon).

On the other hand, using the equations (
5.3
) and (
5.9
), we obtain:

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\eta}(\xi_{1})}a(x)\left(|PU_{\delta_{1},\xi_{1}}-PU_{\delta_{2},\xi_{2}}|^{p}-U_{\delta_{1},\xi_{1}}^{p}-U_{\delta_{2},\xi_{2}}^{p}\right)\,dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p\int_{B_{\eta}(\xi_{1})}a(x)U_{\delta_{1},\xi_{1}}^{p-1}\left(\left(PU_{\delta_{1},\xi_{1}}-U_{\delta_{1},\xi_{1}}\right)-PU_{\delta_{2},\xi_{2}}\right)\,dx+\int_{B_{\eta}(\xi_{1})}a(x)U_{\delta_{2},\xi_{2}}^{p}\,dx

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle O\left(\int_{B_{\eta}(\xi_{1})}\left|U_{\delta_{1},\xi_{1}}+\Theta_{x}\left(PU_{\delta_{1},\xi_{1}}-U_{\delta_{1},\xi_{1}}-PU_{\delta_{2},\xi_{2}}\right)\right|^{p-2}\left(PU_{\delta_{1},\xi_{1}}-U_{\delta_{1},\xi_{1}}-PU_{\delta_{2},\xi_{2}}\right)^{2}\,dx\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p\left(-a(\zeta^{0})\varepsilon\left(\frac{d_{1}}{2t_{1}}\right)^{N-4}\gamma_{2}\right)-p\left(a(\zeta^{0})\varepsilon(d_{1}d_{2})^{\frac{N-4}{2}}\left(\frac{1}{|t_{1}-t_{2}|^{N-4}}-\frac{1}{|t_{1}+t_{2}|^{N-4}}\right)\gamma_{2}\right)+o(\varepsilon).

Analogously, it follows that

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\eta}(\xi_{2})}a(x)\left(|PU_{\delta_{1},\xi_{1}}-PU_{\delta_{2},\xi_{2}}|^{p}-U_{\delta_{1},\xi_{1}}^{p}-U_{\delta_{2},\xi_{2}}^{p}\right)\,dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p\left(-a(\zeta^{0})\varepsilon\left(\frac{d_{2}}{2t_{2}}\right)^{N-4}\gamma_{2}\right)-p\left(a(\zeta^{0})\varepsilon(d_{1}d_{2})^{\frac{N-4}{2}}\left(\frac{1}{|t_{1}-t_{2}|^{N-4}}-\frac{1}{|t_{1}+t_{2}|^{N-4}}\right)\gamma_{2}\right)+o(\varepsilon).

In the last two expressions, we have assumed that, if
1\leq i,j\leq 2,i\neq j
, then:

\int_{B_{\eta}(\xi_{i})}\left|U_{\delta_{i},\xi_{i}}+\Theta_{x}\left(PU_{\delta_{i},\xi_{i}}-U_{\delta_{i},\xi_{i}}-PU_{\delta_{j},\xi_{j}}\right)\right|^{p-2}\left(PU_{\delta_{i},\xi_{i}}-U_{\delta_{i},\xi_{i}}-PU_{\delta_{j},\xi_{j}}\right)^{2}\,dx=o(\varepsilon).

which is a well-known fact, and we will not provide the details here. This concludes the proof.

Lemma 7.3
.

The following estimate holds true:

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}a(x)|PU_{\delta_{1},\xi_{1}}-PU_{\delta_{2},\xi_{2}}|^{p}\log\left(|PU_{\delta_{1},\xi_{1}}-PU_{\delta_{2},\xi_{2}}|\right)\,dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2a(\zeta^{0})\gamma_{3}-\frac{N-4}{2}a(\zeta^{0})\log(d_{1}+d_{2})\gamma_{1}-(N-3)a(\zeta^{0})\log(\varepsilon)\gamma_{1}+o(1),

where

\gamma_{3}=\alpha_{N}^{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}U_{1,0}^{p}\log(U_{1,0})\,dx.

Proof.

We write:

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}a(x)|PU_{\delta_{1},\xi_{1}}-PU_{\delta_{2},\xi_{2}}|^{p}\log(|PU_{\delta_{1},\xi_{1}}-PU_{\delta_{2},\xi_{2}}|)\,dx

(7.3)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\eta(\xi_{1})}}a(x)|PU_{\delta_{1},\xi_{1}}-PU_{\delta_{2},\xi_{2}}|^{p}\log(|PU_{\delta_{1},\xi_{1}}-PU_{\delta_{2},\xi_{2}}|)\,dx

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\eta(\xi_{2})}}a(x)|PU_{\delta_{1},\xi_{1}}-PU_{\delta_{2},\xi_{2}}|^{p}\log(|PU_{\delta_{1},\xi_{1}}-PU_{\delta_{2},\xi_{2}}|)\,dx

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega\setminus(B_{\eta(\xi_{1})}\cup B_{\eta(\xi_{2})})}a(x)|PU_{\delta_{1},\xi_{1}}-PU_{\delta_{2},\xi_{2}}|^{p}\log(|PU_{\delta_{1},\xi_{1}}-PU_{\delta_{2},\xi_{2}}|)\,dx.

It is easy to see that:

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega\setminus(B_{\eta(\xi_{1})}\cup B_{\eta(\xi_{2})})}a(x)|PU_{\delta_{1},\xi_{1}}-PU_{\delta_{2},\xi_{2}}|^{p}\log(|PU_{\delta_{1},\xi_{1}}-PU_{\delta_{2},\xi_{2}}|)=o(1).

(7.4)

The remaining two terms in equation (
7.3
) can be addressed similarly. Thus, we will focus on the first term:

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\eta(\xi_{1})}}a(x)|PU_{\delta_{1},\xi_{1}}-PU_{\delta_{2},\xi_{2}}|^{p}\log\left(\left|PU_{\delta_{1},\xi_{1}}-PU_{\delta_{2},\xi_{2}}\right|\right)\,dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\eta(\xi_{1})}}a(x)|PU_{\delta_{1},\xi_{1}}-PU_{\delta_{2},\xi_{2}}|^{p}\log\left(\left|PU_{\delta_{1},\xi_{1}}\left(1-\frac{PU_{\delta_{2},\xi_{2}}}{PU_{\delta_{1},\xi_{1}}}\right)\right|\right)\,dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\eta(\xi_{1})}}a(x)|PU_{\delta_{1},\xi_{1}}-PU_{\delta_{2},\xi_{2}}|^{p}\log(|PU_{\delta_{1},\xi_{1}}|)\,dx

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\eta(\xi_{1})}}a(x)|PU_{\delta_{1},\xi_{1}}-PU_{\delta_{2},\xi_{2}}|^{p}\log\left(\left|1-\frac{PU_{\delta_{2},\xi_{2}}}{PU_{\delta_{1},\xi_{1}}}\right|\right)\,dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\eta(\xi_{1})}}a(x)|PU_{\delta_{1},\xi_{1}}-PU_{\delta_{2},\xi_{2}}|^{p}\log(|PU_{\delta_{1},\xi_{1}}|)\,dx+o(1).

The last equality in the previous equation holds because:

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\eta(\xi_{1})}}a(x)|PU_{\delta_{1},\xi_{1}}-PU_{\delta_{2},\xi_{2}}|^{p}\log\left(\left|1-\frac{PU_{\delta_{2},\xi_{2}}}{PU_{\delta_{1},\xi_{1}}}\right|\right)=o(1).

Now we estimate:

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\eta(\xi_{1})}}a(x)|PU_{\delta_{1},\xi_{1}}(x)-PU_{\delta_{2},\xi_{2}}(x)|^{p}\log\left|PU_{\delta_{1},\xi_{1}}(x)\right|\,dx

(7.5)

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{\eta(\xi_{1})}}a(x)|PU_{\delta_{1},\xi_{1}}(x)|^{p}\log|PU_{\delta_{1},\xi_{1}}(x)|\,dx+O\left(\int_{B_{\eta(\xi_{1})}}a(x)|PU_{\delta_{1},\xi_{1}}(x)|^{p-1}(PU_{\delta_{2},\xi_{2}}(x))\log|PU_{\delta_{1},\xi_{1}}(x)|\,dx\right)

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{\eta(\xi_{1})}}a(x)U_{\delta_{1},\xi_{1}}^{p}(x)\log U_{\delta_{1},\xi_{1}}(x)\,dx+O\left(\int_{B_{\eta(\xi_{1})}}a(x)\left(|PU_{\delta_{1},\xi_{1}}(x)|^{p}\log|PU_{\delta_{1},\xi_{1}}(x)|-U_{\delta_{1},\xi_{1}}^{p}(x)\log U_{\delta_{1},\xi_{1}}(x)\right)\,dx\right)

\displaystyle+O\left(\int_{B_{\eta(\xi_{1})}}a(x)|PU_{\delta_{1},\xi_{1}}|^{p-1}(PU_{\delta_{2},\xi_{2}}(x))\log|PU_{\delta_{1},\xi_{1}}(x)|\,dx\right)

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{\eta(\xi_{1})}}a(x)U_{\delta_{1},\xi_{1}}^{p}(x)\log U_{\delta_{1},\xi_{1}}(x)\,dx+o(1)

\displaystyle=-\frac{N-4}{2}a(\zeta^{0})\log(d_{1})\gamma_{1}-\frac{N-3}{2}a(\zeta^{0})\log(\varepsilon)\gamma_{1}+a(\zeta^{0})\gamma_{3}+o(1).

In the last equality, we made use of the following estimate:

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\eta(\xi_{1})}}a(x)U_{\delta_{1},\xi_{1}}^{p}(x)\log U_{\delta_{1},\xi_{1}}(x)\,dx=\alpha_{N}^{p}\int_{B_{\eta(\xi_{1})}}a(x)\left(\frac{\delta_{1}^{N}}{(\delta_{1}^{2}+|x-\xi_{1}|^{2})^{N}}\right)\log\left(\frac{\delta_{1}^{\frac{N-4}{2}}}{(\delta_{1}^{2}+|x-\xi_{1}|^{2})^{\frac{N-4}{2}}}\right)\,dx

\displaystyle=\alpha_{N}^{p}\int_{B_{\frac{\eta}{\delta_{1}}}(0)}a(\delta_{1}y+\xi_{1})\left(\frac{1}{(1+|y|^{2})^{N}}\right)\log\left(\frac{\delta_{1}^{\frac{N-4}{2}}}{(\delta_{1}^{2}+|\delta_{1}y|^{2})^{\frac{N-4}{2}}}\right)\,dy

\displaystyle=\alpha_{N}^{p}\int_{\frac{B_{1}-\xi_{1}}{\delta_{1}}}a(\xi_{1}^{0})\left(\frac{1}{(1+|y|^{2})^{N}}\right)\left(\log\left(\delta_{1}^{-\frac{N-4}{2}}\right)+\log\left(U_{1,0}(x)\right)\right)\,dy+o(1)

\displaystyle=-\frac{N-4}{2}a(\zeta^{0})\log(\delta_{1})\gamma_{1}+a(\xi_{1}^{0})\gamma_{3}+o(1)

\displaystyle=-\frac{N-4}{2}a(\zeta^{0})\log(d_{1}\varepsilon^{\frac{N-3}{N-4}})\gamma_{1}+a(\zeta^{0})\gamma_{3}+o(1)

\displaystyle=-\frac{N-4}{2}a(\zeta^{0})\log(d_{1})\gamma_{1}-\frac{N-3}{2}a(\zeta^{0})\log(\varepsilon)\gamma_{1}+a(\zeta^{0})\gamma_{3}+o(1),

and the fact that, with direct calculations, we have that:

\displaystyle O\left(\int_{B_{\eta(\xi_{1})}}a(x)\left(|PU_{\delta_{1},\xi_{1}}(x)|^{p}\log|PU_{\delta_{1},\xi_{1}}(x)|-U_{\delta_{1},\xi_{1}}^{p}(x)\log U_{\delta_{1},\xi_{1}}(x)\right)\,dx\right)

\displaystyle=o(1)

\displaystyle O\left(\int_{B_{\eta(\xi_{1})}}a(x)|PU_{\delta_{1},\xi_{1}}(x)|^{p-1}\left(PU_{\delta_{2},\xi_{2}}(x)\right)\log|PU_{\delta_{1},\xi_{1}}(x)|\,dx\right)

\displaystyle=o(1).

Similarly, we obtain the estimate of the second term in equation (
7.3
):

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\eta(\xi_{2})}}a(x)\left|PU_{\delta_{1},\xi_{1}}(x)-PU_{\delta_{2},\xi_{2}}(x)\right|^{p}\log(|PU_{\delta_{1},\xi_{1}}(x)-PU_{\delta_{2},\xi_{2}}(x)|)\,dx

(7.6)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{N-4}{2}a(\zeta^{0})\log(d_{2})\gamma_{1}-\frac{N-3}{2}a(\zeta^{0})\log(\varepsilon)\gamma_{1}+a(\zeta^{0})\gamma_{3}+o(1).

The proof is completed by taking into account equations (

), (
7.4
), (
7.5

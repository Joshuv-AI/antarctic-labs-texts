Circulating Currents in Electric Machines: Positive Impact of The End Windings Length on Losses
Taha El Hajji, Antti Lehikoinen, Anouar Belahcen

Abstract
Circulating currents occurring in windings of electric machines received rising interest recent years. Circulating currents represent unwanted currents flowing between parallel-connected conductors. This phenomenon is due to various reasons such as asymmetries in the winding and differences in electric potential between parallel-connected conductors. This effect occurs both at no-load and on-load conditions, and always lead to uneven distribution of the current between the parallel conductors, therefore leading to higher losses, as proven in the authors’ previous work. Circulating currents are occurring mainly due to asymmetries and electric potential difference in the active part, meaning that long end windings are advantageous to mitigate the effect of circulating currents. Losses due to circulating currents decrease at a rate proportional to the inverse square of the end windings length. The aim of this paper is to mathematically prove this property and present a case study application in an electric machine.

Circulating Currents: Mathematical Definition and Model

Circulating currents have been previously defined in
[
1
]
in a mathematical form. The definition of circulating currents is:

Definition
.

Circulating currents
occur
between
Nsh
parallel-connected strands, if and only if:

\exists i\in\llbracket 1,Nsh\rrbracket,\exists t\in[0,T],I_{i}(t)\neq\frac{I_{bundle}(t)}{Nsh}

(26)

Therefore, the definition of no circulating currents occurring between parallel-connected strands is:

Definition
.

Circulating currents
do not occur
between
Nsh
parallel-connected strands, if and only if:

\forall i\in\llbracket 1,Nsh\rrbracket,\forall t\in[0,T],I_{i}(t)=\frac{I_{bundle}(t)}{Nsh}

(27)

Model for Circulating Currents:

Among the various existing models in the literature
[
2
]
to evaluate losses due circulating currents (finite element analysis coupled with circuit analysis, analytical models, and hybrid models), we are using in this work the hybrid model proposed in
[
41
]
. This hybrid model allows evaluating circulating currents flowing in the parallel-connected strands by combining results from finite element analysis (potential vector value at each conductor) and analytical formulas. The matrix equation to be solved is the following:

\bm{\mathit{A}}.\bm{\mathit{X}}=\bm{\mathit{B}}

(28)

Where the the matrix
\bm{\mathit{A}}
and the vectors
\bm{\mathit{X}}
and
\bm{\mathit{B}}
are expressed in Section 2.
The vector
\bm{\mathit{B}}
is obtained based on the predefined value of the total current flowing in the bundle
I_{bundle}
and the external flux, obtained by evaluating the external vector potential
\bm{\mathit{A_{z,e,i}}}
at each conductors with finite element analysis. The solution of (
28
) gives the current flowing in each strand.

Positive Impact of End Windings on Circulating Currents Losses: Property and Proof

The ratio
\alpha_{w}
(
4
), used in the enunciation and the proof of the property below, can also be expressed using the dimensions illustrated in Figure
2
as:

\alpha_{w}=1+\frac{l_{EW}}{l_{active}}

Illustration of the ratio
\alpha_{w}

Property
.
2
2

2

The property, initially discussed in
[
2
]
, is extended in this work and proved in a mathematical context. This work can be cited using this paper and the preceding paper
[
2
]
:
https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/10366258
.

The ratio
\alpha_{w}
increases
\Rightarrow

The losses due to circulating currents
P_{CC}
decrease at a rate proportional to
\frac{1}{\alpha_{w}^{2}}

with the lower limit being
P_{CC=0}
, the losses occurring without the circulating currents

This property means that if the length of the end windings increases compared to the active length of the machine, then the losses due to circulating currents decrease at a rate proportional to the inverse square of
\alpha_{w}
, and therefore, proportional to the inverse square of the end windings length. This property was partially enunciated and partially demonstrated by authors in
[
2
]
and is extended here to a complete form with a full mathematical proof.

Proof.

The property’s proof is given in these two cases:

1.

The inductance matrix
\bm{\mathit{L_{A}}}
is negligible compared to the resistance matrix
\bm{\mathit{R_{A}}}
of the phase A:
\bm{\mathit{L_{A}}}<<\bm{\mathit{R_{A}}}
.

2.

The mutual inductance between conductors is negligible compared to the self inductance of conductors, therefore, the inductance matrix
\bm{\mathit{L_{A}}}
can be considered a diagonal matrix.

The impedance matrix can be considered in these two cases as a diagonal matrix. Therefore, let’s assume that the impedance matrix
\bm{\mathit{Z_{A,k}}}
is diagonal, which can be expressed as:

\bm{\mathit{Z_{A,k}}}=Z_{strd,k}.\bm{\mathit{I_{Nsh}}}

(29)

Which can be expressed also as:

\bm{\mathit{Z_{A,k}}}=\alpha_{w}.Z_{strd,act,k}.\bm{\mathit{I_{Nsh}}}

(30)

The system matrix
\bm{\mathit{A_{k}}}
for the harmonic
k
can then be expressed as follows:

\bm{\mathit{A_{k}}}=\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc|c}(\alpha_{w}.Z_{strd,act,k})&\cdots&0&1\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots\\
0&\cdots&(\alpha_{w}.Z_{strd,act,k})&1\\
\hline\cr 1&\cdots&1&0\end{array}\right]

(31)

The inverse of this matrix
\bm{\mathit{A_{k}}}
can be expressed as:

\bm{\mathit{A_{k}}}^{-1}=\left[\begin{array}[]{c|c}\bm{\mathit{\frac{1}{\alpha_{w}}.P_{k}}}&\begin{array}[]{c}\frac{1}{Nsh}\\
\vdots\\
\frac{1}{Nsh}\end{array}\\
\hline\cr\begin{array}[]{ccc}\frac{1}{Nsh}&\cdots&\frac{1}{Nsh}\end{array}&\frac{-(\alpha_{w}.Z_{strd,act,k})}{Nsh}\end{array}\right]

(32)

Where the matrix
\bm{\mathit{P_{k}}}\in\mathbb{R}^{Nsh\times Nsh}
is expressed as:

\bm{\mathit{P_{k}}}=\frac{1}{Z_{strd,act,k}}.\left[\bm{\mathit{I_{Nsh}}}-\frac{1}{Nsh}.\bm{\mathit{J_{Nsh,Nsh}}}\right]

(33)

Hence, the inverse of the matrix
\bm{\mathit{A}}
can be expressed as:

\bm{\mathit{A^{-1}}}=\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}\bm{\mathit{A_{1}^{-1}}}&\cdots&0\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&\cdots&\bm{\mathit{A_{N_{h}}^{-1}}}\end{array}\right]

(34)

The unknown vector
\bm{\mathit{X}}
can be obtained using the following equation:

\bm{\mathit{X}}=\bm{\mathit{A^{-1}}}.\bm{\mathit{B}}

(35)

Since the operation is done by block, the unknown vector
\bm{\mathit{X_{k}}}
for the harmonic
k
can be obtained similarly as:

\bm{\mathit{X_{k}}}=\bm{\mathit{A_{k}^{-1}}}.\bm{\mathit{B_{k}}}

(36)

which can be formulated as:

\bm{\mathit{X_{k}}}=\left[\begin{array}[]{c}-j.k.\omega.\frac{1}{\alpha_{w}}.\bm{\mathit{P_{k}}}.\bm{\mathit{\phi_{A,k}}}+\frac{1}{Nsh}.I_{bundle,k}.\bm{\mathit{J_{Nsh,1}}}\\
-j.k.\omega.\frac{1}{Nsh}.\bm{\mathit{J_{1,Nsh}}}.\bm{\mathit{\phi_{A,k}}}-\frac{\alpha_{w}.Z_{strd,act,k}}{Nsh}.I_{bundle,k}\end{array}\right]

(37)

Based on the expression of the vector
\bm{\mathit{X_{k}}}
in (
21
), we have:

\left[\begin{array}[]{c}I_{1,k}\\
\vdots\\
I_{Nsh,k}\end{array}\right]=-j.k.\omega.\frac{1}{\alpha_{w}}.\bm{\mathit{P_{k}}}.\bm{\mathit{\phi_{A,k}}}+\frac{1}{Nsh}.I_{bundle,k}.\bm{\mathit{J_{Nsh,1}}}

(38)

Which can also be written as:

\left[\begin{array}[]{c}I_{1,k}\\
\vdots\\
I_{Nsh,k}\end{array}\right]=\left[\begin{array}[]{c}\frac{I_{bundle,k}}{Nsh}\\
\vdots\\
\frac{I_{bundle,k}}{Nsh}\end{array}\right]+\frac{1}{\alpha_{w}}.\left[\begin{array}[]{c}I_{\delta,1,k}\\
\vdots\\
I_{\delta,Nsh,k}\end{array}\right]

(39)

The current in each strand can be expressed as:

\forall j\in\llbracket 1,Nsh\rrbracket,\forall k\in\llbracket 1,N_{h}\rrbracket,I_{j,k}=\frac{I_{bundle,k}}{Nsh}+\frac{1}{\alpha_{w}}.I_{\delta,j,k}

(40)

Where, for a strand
j\in\llbracket 1,Nsh\rrbracket
and a harmonic
k\in\llbracket 1,N_{h}\rrbracket
, we have:

I_{\delta,j,k}=\frac{-j.k.\omega}{Z_{strd,act,k}}.\left[\bm{\mathit{\phi_{A,k}}}(j)-\frac{1}{Nsh}.\sum_{n=1}^{Nsh}\bm{\mathit{\phi_{A,k}}}(n)\right]

(41)

It can be noticed from (
40
) that the current in each strand is composed of two terms. The first term
\frac{I_{bundle,k}}{Nsh}
corresponds to the uniform sharing of the bundle’s current between parallel strands. The second term
\frac{1}{\alpha_{w}}.I_{\delta,j,k}
represents the effect of circulating currents. Therefore, when circulating currents are not considered, only the first term remains, meaning that the current flowing in the bundle is evenly shared between the
Nsh
parallel-connected strands. Further, if the ratio
\alpha_{w}
increases, corresponding to increasing the length of the end windings compared to the active length, the term
\frac{1}{\alpha_{w}}.I_{\delta,j,k}
tends to
0
, and therefore, only the term
\frac{I_{bundle,k}}{Nsh}
remains. This means that the current tends to be equally shared between parallel-connected strands. Hence, we obtain in this case:

\left[\begin{array}[]{c}I_{1,k}\\
\vdots\\
I_{Nsh,k}\end{array}\right]=\left[\begin{array}[]{c}\frac{I_{bundle,k}}{Nsh}\\
\vdots\\
\frac{I_{bundle,k}}{Nsh}\end{array}\right]

(42)

The RMS value of the current in each strand
j
can expressed, according to Parseval equality, as:

I_{RMS,j}=\sqrt{\sum_{k=1}^{N_{h}}I_{j,k}^{2}}

(43)

Therefore, losses in each strand
j\in\llbracket 1,Nsh\rrbracket
are expressed as:

\displaystyle P_{CC}={}

\displaystyle P_{CC}={}

\displaystyle P_{CC}={}

\displaystyle P_{CC=0}+\frac{1}{\alpha_{w}^{2}}.P_{\delta CC,active}

P_{\delta CC}

Summary:

It was demonstrated that, in the case of a diagonal impedance matrix, increasing the ratio
\alpha_{w}
leads to reducing losses due to circulating currents at a rate proportional to
\frac{1}{\alpha_{w}^{2}}
, hence, proportional to the inverse square of the end windings length. Circulating currents can be neglected for high value of
\alpha_{w}
(equivalent to long end windings). In the cases
1
and
2
presented above, the impedance matrix has negligible off-diagonal entries compared to the diagonal entries. The property can be generalized to these cases because of the continuity of the inverse of a matrix with respect to its entries.

Conclusion

In this paper, we presented the positive impact of the end windings length in reducing the losses due to circulating currents and allowing a more uniform sharing of the current between the parallel strands. Generated losses due to circulating currents decrease at a rate proportional to the inverse square of the end windings length. This property was proven mathematically using the hybrid model for circulating currents in the case where the impedance matrix has negligible off-diagonal entries compared to the diagonal entries, extending the authors’ previous work and framing it within a rigorous mathematical context. A case study of an electric machine was presented to study the impact of the end windings length on losses due to circulating currents and the results obtained from finite element analysis are coherent with this property.

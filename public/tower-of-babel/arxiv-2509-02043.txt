Characteristic quasi-polynomials of deletions of Shi arrangements of type C and type D
Akihiro Higashitani, Masato Konoike, Norihiro Nakashima, Satoshi Ono

Abstract
Characteristic quasi-polynomials enumerate the number of points in the complement of hyperplane arrangements modulo positive integers. In this paper, we compute the characteristic quasi-polynomials of the restrictions of the Shi arrangements of type C and type D by one given hyperplane, respectively. The case of type C is established by extending the method developed in our previous work on type B ( [ 2 ] ), while the case of type D is deduced through a direct connection with the results on type B. As a corollary, we determine whether period collapse occurs in the characteristic quasi-polynomials of the deletions of the Shi arrangements of type C and type D.

Introduction

In the study of hyperplane arrangements, the characteristic polynomial of a hyperplane arrangement
\mathcal{A}
is a fundamental invariant that encodes various algebraic and combinatorial aspects of
\mathcal{A}
.
Kamiya, Takemura, and Terao
[
4
,
5
]
introduced its natural generalization, now known as the characteristic quasi-polynomial.
They showed that, for a given arrangement
\mathcal{A}
, the number of points in its complement when considered over
(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})^{m}
is given by a quasi-polynomial in
q
. The precise definition will be recalled below.

Let
q\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
and define
\mathbb{Z}_{q}:=\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z}
.
For
a\in\mathbb{Z}
, let
[a]_{q}:=a+q\mathbb{Z}\in\mathbb{Z}_{q}
be the
q
reduction of
a
.
Note that as usual, we choose representatives
[a]_{q}\in\mathbb{Z}_{q}
as integers with
0\leq a\leq q-1
, and we often identify elements of
\mathbb{Z}_{q}
with
\{a\in\mathbb{Z}\mid 0\leq a\leq q-1\}
in the sequel.
An
m\times n
integer matrix
A=(a_{ij})\in\mathrm{Mat}_{m\times n}(\mathbb{Z})
and an integral vector
\bm{b}=(b_{1},\dots,b_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
define a hyperplane arrangement
\mathcal{A}_{q}=\{H_{1}[q],\dots,H_{n}[q]\}
over
\mathbb{Z}_{q}
, where
H_{i}[q]=\{(x_{1},\dots,x_{m})\in\mathbb{Z}_{q}\mid[a_{1,i}]_{q}x_{1}+\cdots+[a_{m,i}]_{q}x_{m}=[b_{i}]_{q}\}
for
i=1,\ldots,n
.
The set
M(\mathcal{A}_{q})
is defined as the complement of
\mathcal{A}_{q}
, i.e.,

\displaystyle M(\mathcal{A}_{q})

\displaystyle:=\mathbb{Z}_{q}^{m}\setminus\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}H_{i}[q]

\displaystyle=\{(x_{1},\dots,x_{m})\in\mathbb{Z}_{q}^{m}\mid[a_{1,i}]_{q}x_{1}+\cdots+[a_{m,i}]_{q}x_{m}\neq[b_{i}]_{q}\ \text{for}\ i=1,\ldots,n\}.

Kamiya, Takemura, and Terao proved in
[
4
,
5
]
that the function
|M(\mathcal{A}_{q})|
becomes a monic quasi-polynomial, which we call the
characteristic quasi-polynomial
of a hyperplane arrangement
\mathcal{A}
, with a period
\rho_{A}
, called the
lcm period
of
\mathcal{A}
, where
\rho_{A}
is a certain positive integer defined from the integer matrix
A
.
(For the details of quasi-polynomials and their lcm periods, consult, e.g.,
[
2
, Subsection 2.1]
.)

Since the introduction of characteristic quasi-polynomials of hyperplane arrangements, those have been computed for several important classes (e.g.
[
6
,
7
]
) .
We focus on the characteristic quasi-polynomials of Shi arrangements, which are the central object of this paper.

Definition 1.1

(Shi arrangements of type B, type C, type D)
.

Given a positive integer
m
, let
[m]=\{1,2,\ldots,m\}
.

The Shi arrangement
\mathcal{B}_{m}
of type B is defined by

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{m}:=\{\{x_{i}=0\},\{x_{i}=1\}\mid i\in[m]\}\cup\{\{x_{i}\pm x_{j}=0\},\{x_{i}\pm x_{j}=1\}\mid 1\leq i<j\leq m\},

The Shi arrangement
\mathcal{C}_{m}
of type C is defined by

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{m}:=\{\{2x_{i}=0\},\{2x_{i}=1\}\mid i\in[m]\}\cup\{\{x_{i}\pm x_{j}=0\},\{x_{i}\pm x_{j}=1\}\mid 1\leq i<j\leq m\}.

The Shi arrangement
\mathcal{D}_{m}
of type D is defined by

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{m}:=\{\{x_{i}\pm x_{j}=0\},\{x_{i}\pm x_{j}=1\}\mid 1\leq i<j\leq m\}.

In
[
2
]
, the first author and the third author compute the characteristic quasi-polynomial of
\mathcal{B}_{m}
as follows:

\displaystyle\left|M((\mathcal{B}_{m})_{q})\right|=(q-2m)^{m}.

(1.1)

Note that this was essentially obtained in
[
7
, Theorem 5.1]
. (See Remark
1.3
below.)
On the other hand,
\left|M((\mathcal{C}_{m})_{q})\right|
and
\left|M((\mathcal{D}_{m})_{q})\right|
are not obtained there, so we compute them in this paper.
The following is the first main result of this paper:

Theorem 1.2

(See Theorems
2.1
and
3.1
)
.

We have

|M((\mathcal{C}_{m})_{q})|=(q-2m)^{m}

(1.2)

and

|M((\mathcal{D}_{m})_{q})|=(q-2m+2)^{m}.

(1.3)

Remark 1.3
.

Yoshinaga
[
7
, Theorem 5.1]
determined the characteristic quasi-polynomials for extended Shi arrangements as follows:

\left|M((\mathcal{A}_{\Phi}^{[1-k,k]})_{q})\right|=(q-kh)^{m},

where
\mathcal{A}_{\Phi}^{[1-k,k]}
denotes the extended Shi arrangement and
h
denotes the Coxeter number.
In particular, we know that

\displaystyle\left|M((\mathcal{B}_{\Phi}^{[0,1]})_{q})\right|=\left|M((\mathcal{C}_{\Phi}^{[0,1]})_{q})\right|=(q-2m)^{m}\;\text{ and }\;\left|M((\mathcal{D}_{\Phi}^{[0,1]})_{q})\right|=(q-2m+2)^{m}.

(1.4)

In
[
7
]
, the Shi arrangements are defined as linear combinations of
simple root bases
.
On the other hand, in this paper, the Shi arrangements are defined as linear combinations of
standard bases
.
In the case of real arrangements, where the basis transformations are linear isomorphisms, or in the case of arrangements over
\mathbb{Z}_{q}
,
where the basis transformations are unimodular, hyperplane arrangements that are transformed by the basis transformations are isomorphic.
However, since the determinant of the transformation matrices of the simple roots and the standard basis is
2
for the Shi arrangements of type C and type D, they are distinct as arrangements over
\mathbb{Z}_{q}
.
Therefore, the characteristic quasi-polynomials considered in this paper may not necessarily coincide with that of the Shi arrangements represented by the basis of simple roots.
More concretely, Theorem
1.2
is essentially different from (
1.4
), but we eventually notice that those are the same.

We say that
period collapse
occurs for a hyperplane arrangement
\mathcal{A}
if the minimum period of its characteristic quasi-polynomial is strictly smaller than its lcm period.
In
[
3
]
, they claim that “any possible period collapse” can be realized as a non-central arrangement.
For the (extended) Shi arrangements of type B, type C and type D, the equalities in Theorem
1.2
imply that the characteristic quasi-polynomial reduces to an ordinary polynomial (minimum period
1
), whereas the lcm period is known to be
2
.
Therefore, period collapse occurs in each of these arrangements.

Motivated by these, we investigate the following question:

Question 1.4

(See
[
2
, Question 1.2]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a hyperplane arrangement and fix
H\in\mathcal{A}
. Under what conditions does period collapse occur in the deletion
\mathcal{A}\setminus{H}
?

We completely answered this question for the Shi arrangement of type B in
[
2
]
as shown in Theorem
1.5
.
Our goal of this paper is to answer this question completely for the Shi arrangements of type C or type D.
For this purpose, we compute the characteristic quasi-polynomials of the restrictions
\mathcal{C}_{m}^{H}
and
\mathcal{D}_{m}^{H}
obtained by fixing a hyperplane
H
in the corresponding arrangement. In the case of type C, the proof parallels the approach used in our previous work on type B, whereas for type D we exploit a close relationship with the type B result.
Note that for a given hyperplane arrangement
\mathcal{A}
defined by an integer matrix and an integral vector as above, if the greatest common divisor of the coefficients of the variables in the defining polynomial of
H
is 1, then the following formula holds:

\displaystyle|M(\mathcal{A}_{q})|=\left|M\left(\mathcal{A}_{q}\setminus\{H_{q}\}\right)\right|-\left|M\left(\mathcal{A}_{q}^{H_{q}}\right)\right|,

(1.5)

where
\mathcal{A}\setminus\{H\}
(resp.
\mathcal{A}^{H}
) is a deletion (resp. a restriction) of
\mathcal{A}
with respect to
H
(see, e.g.,
[
6

).
Namely, the computation of the characteristic quasi-polynomial of the deletion
\mathcal{A}\setminus\{H\}
is equivalent to that of the restriction
\mathcal{A}^{H}
if we know the characteristic quasi-polynomial of
\mathcal{A}
.
In particular, by checking whether the characteristic quasi-polynomial of a restriction is a polynomial, we can check whether the characteristic quasi-polynomial of a deletion is a polynomial.

3
Shi arrangement of type D

We compute the characteristic quasi-polynomials of the Shi arrangement of type D by providing a bijective relation with the Shi arrangement of type B.
This is a different way to compute the Shi arrangement of type B and type C.

We collect some notation on hyperplanes used in this section.
For
1\leq i<j\leq m,q\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0},c\in\mathbb{Z}
, we define the following:

•

Let
H_{i,c}:=\{\bm{x}\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}\mid x_{i}=c\}
.

•

Let
H_{i,j,c}^{-}:=\{\bm{x}\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}\mid x_{i}-x_{j}=c\}
.

•

Let
H_{i,j,c}^{+}:=\{\bm{x}\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}\mid x_{i}+x_{j}=c\}
.

•

For a hyperplane
H\subset\mathbb{Z}^{m}
defined by a linear form
\alpha_{H}
, let
H[q]:=\{\bm{x}\in\mathbb{Z}_{q}^{m}\mid\alpha_{H}(\bm{x})=0\}
.

•

For a hyperplane arrangement
\mathcal{A}
, let
\mathcal{A}[q]:=\{H[q]\mid H\in\mathcal{A}\}
and
M(\mathcal{A}_{q}):=\mathbb{Z}_{q}^{m}\setminus\bigcup_{H\in\mathcal{A}}H[q]
.

3.1
A bijection

First, we prove the following.

Theorem 3.1
.

The characteristic quasi-polynomial of the Shi arrangement of type D is

\displaystyle\left|M(\mathcal{D}_{q})\right|=(q-2m+2)^{m}

for any
q\in\mathbb{Z}
with
q\gg 0
.

Proof.

We construct a bijection between
M(\mathcal{D}_{q})
and
M(\mathcal{B}_{q+2})
.
Once we establish it, we obtain the desired conclusion since we already know
\left|M(\mathcal{B}_{q})\right|=(q-2m)^{m}
by (
1.1
).

For each
\bm{x}\in M(\mathcal{D}_{q})
(resp.
\bm{y}\in M(\mathcal{B}_{q+2})

\bm{x}\not\in H_{s,s^{\prime},0}^{-}[q]
(resp.
\bm{y}\not\in H_{s,s^{\prime},0}^{-}[q+2]

with

(resp.

).
In particular, there is at most one

such that

(resp.
y_{s}=q+1
).
By taking this into account, we define the maps
\phi:M(\mathcal{D}_{q})\to M(\mathcal{B}_{q+2})
and
\psi:M(\mathcal{B}_{q+2})\to M(\mathcal{B}_{q})
as follows:

•

For
\bm{x}\in M(\mathcal{D}_{q})

\displaystyle\phi(\bm{x})=\begin{cases}\bm{x}+\bm{1}+q\bm{e}_{u}&\text{if $x_{u}=0$},\\

where

1\leq i<j\leq m
, and we prove the third equality of Theorem

as follows.

Theorem 3.2
.

We have

\displaystyle\left|M(\mathcal{D}^{(3)}_{q})\right|=\begin{cases}(q-2m+2)^{m-j}(q-2m+3)^{j-i}((q-2m+4)^{i-1}-(q-2m+3)^{i-2})\;\;&\text{if $q$ is odd},\\
(q-2m+2)^{m-j+1}(q-2m+3)^{j-i-1}(q-2m+4)^{i-1}&\text{if $q$ is even}\end{cases}

for any
q\in\mathbb{Z}
with
q\gg 0
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{B}^{(3)}=\mathcal{B}_{m}^{\{x_{i}+x_{j}=0\}}
.
For our purpose, we show the existence of a bijection between
M(\mathcal{D}^{(3)}_{q})
and
M(\mathcal{B}^{(3)}_{q+2})
. To this end, we use
\phi
given in the proof of Theorem
3.1
.
Then it suffices to verify that the map
\phi
restricted to
M(\mathcal{D}^{(3)}_{q})
is well-defined as well as
\psi
restricted to
M(\mathcal{B}^{(3)}_{q+2})
.
More precisely, our goal is to show that if
\bm{x}\in M(\mathcal{D}^{(3)}_{q})
(resp.
\bm{y}\in M(\mathcal{B}^{(3)}_{q+2})
),
then
\phi(\bm{x})\in M(\mathcal{B}^{(3)}_{q+2})
(resp.
\psi(\bm{y})\in M(\mathcal{D}^{(3)}_{q})
).

First, we check the well-definedness of
\phi
.
We observe that neither
x_{i}=0
nor
x_{j}=0
holds because this would contradict the conditions
x_{i}+x_{j}=0
and
x_{i}-x_{j}\neq 0
.

Let
\bm{x}\in M(\mathcal{D}_{q}^{(3)})
. It is enough to show that
\phi(\bm{x})\in H_{i,j,0}^{+}[q+2]
.
Since
\bm{x}\in H_{i,j,0}^{+}[q]
, we have
x_{i}+x_{j}=q
as
\mathbb{Z}
. Thus, we obtain
(x_{i}+1)+(x_{j}+1)=q+2
as
\mathbb{Z}
, i.e.,
\phi(\bm{x})\in H_{i,j,0}^{+}[q+2]
.
Hence, the map
\phi
restricted to
M(\mathcal{D}_{q}^{(3)})
is well-defined.

Let
\bm{y}\in M(\mathcal{B}_{q+2}^{(3)})
. It is enough to show that
\psi(\bm{y})\in H_{i,j,0}^{+}[q]
.
Since
\bm{y}\in H_{i,j,0}^{+}[q+2]
, we have
y_{i}+y_{j}=q+2
as
\mathbb{Z}
. Thus, we obtain
(y_{i}-1)+(y_{j}-1)=q
as
\mathbb{Z}
, i.e.,
\psi(\bm{y})\in H_{i,j,0}^{+}[q]
.
Hence, the map
\psi
restricted to
M(\mathcal{B}_{q+2}^{(3)})
is well-defined.

Therefore, we obtain that
|M(\mathcal{D}_{q}^{(3)})|=|M(\mathcal{B}_{q+2}^{(3)})|
.
This shows Theorem
3.2
by Theorem
1.5
.
∎

holds, a contradiction.

•

The upper right box does not contain any numbers larger than
k
.
Indeed, if the upper right box contains a number larger than
k
, then the rightmost circle in the upper right box is a labeled circle, say labeled with
s
(
k<s
).
The circle clockwise preceding the circle labeled with
k
has the label greater than
k
.
In other words,
x_{k}=x_{s}+1
holds, a contradiction.

We divide the discussions into which range
k
belongs.

(ii-1)

Let
1\leq k\leq i-1
.

–

There are
q-2m
boxes that can contain the numbers
1,\ldots,k-1
, which are the boxes except the upper left and lower right ones.

–

There are
q-2m-1
boxes that can contain the numbers
k+1,\ldots,i-1
, which are the boxes except the upper left, the upper right and the lower right ones.

–

Similarly to the case when
q
is odd,
there are
q-2m
boxes that can contain
i+1,\ldots,j-1,j+1,\ldots,m
.

Hence the desired number is

(q-2m-1)^{i-k-1}(q-2m)^{m+k-i-2}

in the case of
1\leq k\leq i-1
.

(ii-2)

Let
i+1\leq k\leq j-1
.

–

There are
q-2m
boxes that can contain the numbers
1,\ldots,i-1
, which are the boxes except the upper left and lower right ones.

–

Similarly, there are
q-2m+1
boxes that can contain the numbers
i+1,\ldots,k-1
.

–

There are
q-2m
boxes that can contain the numbers
k+1,\ldots,j-1,j+1,\ldots,m
.

Hence the desired number is

(q-2m)^{m+i-k-2}(q-2m+1)^{k-i-1}

in the case of
i+1\leq k\leq j-1
.

(ii-3)

Let
j+1\leq k\leq m
.

–

There are
q-2m
boxes that can contain the numbers
1,\ldots,i-1
.

–

There are
q-2m+1
boxes that can contain the numbers
i+1,\ldots,j-1,j+1,\ldots,k-1
.

–

There are
q-2m
boxes that can contain the numbers
k+1,\ldots,m
.

Hence the desired number is

(q-2m)^{m+i-k-1}(q-2m+1)^{k-i-2}

in the case of
j+1\leq k\leq m
.

From the discussion above, we have that

\displaystyle\left|M(\mathcal{\widetilde{B}}^{(4)}_{q})\right|=

\displaystyle\,(T-1)^{i-1}T^{m-i-1}+\sum_{k=1}^{i-1}(T-1)^{i-k-1}T^{m+k-i-2}

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=i+1}^{j-1}T^{m+i-k-2}(T+1)^{k-i-1}+\sum_{k=j+1}^{m}T^{m+i-k-1}(T+1)^{k-i-2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,(T-1)^{i-1}T^{m-i-1}+T^{m-i-1}\left(T^{i-1}-(T-1)^{i-1}\right)

\displaystyle+T^{m+i-j-1}\left((T+1)^{j-i-1}-T^{j-i-1}\right)+T^{i-1}(T+1)^{j-i-1}\left((T+1)^{m-j}-T^{m-j}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,T^{i-1}(T+1)^{m-i-1},

where
T:=q-2m
.

Proof of Corollary

Proof of Corollary
1.9
.

(1) Let
\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{C}_{m}
. If
H,H^{\prime}\in\mathcal{C}
are parallel each other,
since
(\mathcal{C}\setminus\{H\})^{H^{\prime}}=\mathcal{C}^{H^{\prime}}

|M(\mathcal{C}_{q}\setminus\{H,H^{\prime}\})|

|M(\mathcal{C}_{q}^{H})|+|M(\mathcal{C}_{q}^{H^{\prime}})|
when period collapse occurs in

.

In the case of the pair
H=\{2x_{i}=0\}
and
H^{\prime}=\{2x_{i}=1\}

\displaystyle(q-2m)^{m-i}(q-2m+1)^{i-1}+(q-2m)^{i-1}(q-2m+1)^{m-i}=2(q-2m)^{m-i}(q-2m+1)^{i-1}

\displaystyle(q-2m)^{i-1}(q-2m+1)^{m-i}=(q-2m)^{m-i}(q-2m+1)^{i-1}

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow\

\displaystyle(q-2m)^{m-j}(q-2m+1)^{j-i-1}(q-2m+2)^{i-1}=(q-2m)^{i-1}(q-2m+1)^{j-i-1}(q-2m+2)^{m-j}

\displaystyle i-1=m-j\Longleftrightarrow i+j=m+1,

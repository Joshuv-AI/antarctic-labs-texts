Sewing lemma and knitting lemma for metric spaces
C. Curry, D. Manchon

Abstract
. We state and prove a sewing lemma in the general context of families of complete metric spaces indexed by an interval of the real line, encompassing the flow sewing lemma proved by I. Bailleul in 2015. A further generalisation to other metric parameter spaces P P than intervals is moreover proposed, leading to a representation of the groupoid of thin-equivalent Lipschitz paths on P P . Under a stronger hypothesis, we finally prove a two-dimensional version, the knitting lemma, which gives rise to a representation of the Lipschitz homotopy groupoid of the parameter space, without thinness condition.



Keywords:
sewing lemma, groupoids, paths, homotopy, Lipschitz norm.

MSC classification:18B40, 20L05, 41A99.

1.
Introduction

The sewing lemma first appeared in the work of Feyel and de la Pradelle
[
14
]
, and was clarified by M. Gubinelli
[
18
]
. The statement is that, given a Banach space valued function
\mu(s,t)
obeying

|\mu(s,t)-\mu(s,u)-\mu(u,t)|\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}C_{i}|t-u|^{a_{i}}|u-s|^{b_{i}}\hbox{ with }a_{i}+b_{i}={1+\varepsilon},

there exists a unique (up to additive constant)
\phi(t)
such that
|\phi(t)-\phi(s)-\mu(s,t)|\leq C|t-s|^{1+\varepsilon}
.
The intended application was to show that integrals
I(s,t)=\phi(t)-\phi(s)
could be defined through Riemann sums
\sum_{i}\mu(t_{i},t_{i+1})
in a limit where the mesh of partition of
[s,t]
tends to zero. In this the inspiration was Lyons’ theorem on almost multiplicative functionals, used to extend Young integration to rough paths.

Shortly after, Feyel, de la Pradelle and Mokobodzki
[
15
]
proved a non-commutative sewing lemma for functions
\mu(s,t)
taking values in an associative monoid equipped with a complete metric; a unique
\phi(t)
exists under the hypothesis

d\big(\mu(s,t),\,\mu(s,u)\circ\mu(u,t)\big)\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}C_{i}|t-u|^{a_{i}}|u-s|^{b_{i}}\hbox{ with }a_{i}+b_{i}={1+\varepsilon}.

A similar result was proved by Bailleul
[
2
]
and used in the construction of flows driven by rough paths. Subsequently there have appeared several further generalizations
[
5
,
6
,
13
]
of non-commutative sewing lemmas.

Hitherto all sewing lemmas have been based on functions of two real variables
s,t\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}
, indeed since the work of Gubinelli on rough paths
[
17
]
it has been possible to interpret such constructions in the language of cochain complexes. On examining the proofs of existing sewing lemmas, however, it becomes clear that it is not the order structure of
\mathbb{R}
alone that facilitates the proof, but rather a groupoid structure that permits concatenation of paths and flows.

In the present work we

(1)

State and prove a sewing lemma (Theorem
3.6
) in the general context of complete metric spaces, encompassing all of the variants mentioned above.

(2)

Propose in Paragraph
4.5
a conjectural generalization of the sewing lemma to more general parameter spaces
P
than the real line, involving the groupoid of thin-equivalent Lipschitz paths on the metric space
P
.

(3)

Prove in Paragraph
4.6
a
knitting lemma
for general metric parameter spaces
P
. This result, which holds true under a much stronger condition than what is necessary for the sewing lemma, can be briefly described as a two-dimensional version of the latter, and leads to a representation of the Lipschitz homotopy groupoid (Theorem
4.14
).

2.
Extended metric spaces of continuous maps

2.1.
Extended metric spaces

Definition 2.1
.

(
[
1
, Pages 419–420]
, see also
[
5
, Paragraph 1.2.3]
and
[
8
]
)

An
extended metric space
is a set
E
endowed with a distance
d:E\times E\to\mathbb{R}^{+}\cup\{\infty\}
, such that the usual axioms for a distance, i.e.

•

d(x,y)=0
if and only if
x=y
,

•

d(x,y)=d(y,x)
,

•

triangle inequality
d(x,z)\leq d(x,y)+d(y,z)

are verified. The only difference with a usual metric space is that the infinite value for the distance is allowed. The
galaxy
of
x\in E
is the subset of points at finite distance from
x
.

Any galaxy is a metric space in the usual sense, and any extended metric space is a disjoint (and disconnected) union of galaxies. The notion of completeness can be straightforwardly adapted to extended metric spaces, because all terms in a Cauchy sequence belong to the same galaxy, except perharps a finite number of those.

2.2.
Extended metric spaces of continuous maps

Let
X
and
Y
be two metric spaces (in the extended sense or not), where
Y
is complete. The set
C_{YX}
of continuous maps from
X
to
Y
carries a natural structure of complete extended topological space, given by the distance:

(1)

d_{YX}(f,g):=\mathop{\hbox{\rm sup}}\nolimits_{x\in X}d_{Y}\big(f(x),g(x)\big).

The triangle inequality is obviously verified, and the completeness is verified as follows: if
(f_{k})_{k\geq 1}
is a Cauchy sequence in
C_{YX}
, then
\big(f_{k}(x)\big)_{k\geq 1}
is a Cauchy sequence in
Y
for any
x\in X
, which thus converges to an element
f(x)
of
Y
. This defines the evaluation at
x
of the limit
f
of the sequence
(f_{k})
.

Remark 2.2
.

Note that, even when
X
and
Y
are metric spaces in the usual sense, it may be not the case for
C_{YX}
. For example, the identity
x\mapsto x
and any constant function
x\mapsto x_{0}
are infinitely distant from each other in
C_{XX}
whenever
X
is unbounded.

Recall that a map
f:X\to Y
between two metric spaces is Lipschitz if
\mathop{\hbox{\rm Lip}}\nolimits(f)<+\infty
, with

(2)

\mathop{\hbox{\rm Lip}}\nolimits(f):=\mathop{\hbox{\rm sup}}\nolimits_{x^{\prime}\neq x^{\prime\prime}}\frac{d_{Y}\big(f(x^{\prime}),f^{\prime}x^{\prime\prime})\big)}{d_{X}(x^{\prime},x^{\prime\prime})}.

In particular any Lipschitz map is continuous. If
Z
is another metric space and
g:Y\to Z
is another map, we obviously have

(3)

\mathop{\hbox{\rm Lip}}\nolimits(g\circ f)\leq\mathop{\hbox{\rm Lip}}\nolimits(g)\mathop{\hbox{\rm Lip}}\nolimits(f).

The two following lemmas can be found in
[
13
, Paragraph 2.1]
. We simply rephrase them in the context of several metric spaces.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
X,Y,Z
be three metric spaces. Let
f,f^{\prime}\in C_{YX}
and
g,g^{\prime}\in C_{ZY}
. Then

(4)

d_{ZX}(g\circ f,g^{\prime}\circ f^{\prime})\leq d_{ZY}(g,g^{\prime})+\mathop{\hbox{\rm Lip}}\nolimits(g^{\prime})d_{YX}(f,f^{\prime}).

Proof.

From the triangle inequality in
C_{ZX}
we have:

\displaystyle d_{ZX}(g\circ f,g^{\prime}\circ f^{\prime})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle d_{ZX}(g\circ f,g^{\prime}\circ f)+d_{ZX}(g^{\prime}\circ f,g^{\prime}\circ f^{\prime})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathop{\hbox{\rm sup}}\nolimits_{x\in X}d_{Z}\big(g\circ f(x),g^{\prime}\circ f(x)\big)+\mathop{\hbox{\rm sup}}\nolimits_{x\in X}d_{Z}\big(g^{\prime}\circ f(x),g^{\prime}\circ f^{\prime}(x)\big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathop{\hbox{\rm sup}}\nolimits_{y\in Y}d_{Z}\big(g(y),g^{\prime}(y)\big)+\mathop{\hbox{\rm Lip}}\nolimits(g^{\prime})\mathop{\hbox{\rm sup}}\nolimits_{x\in X}d_{Y}\big(f(x),f^{\prime}(x)\big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle d_{ZY}(g,g^{\prime})+\mathop{\hbox{\rm Lip}}\nolimits(g^{\prime})d_{YX}(f,f^{\prime}).

∎

Lemma
2.3
can be immediately generalised as follows:

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
X_{0},\ldots,X_{r}
be a collection of metric spaces, with
X_{j}
complete for
j=0,\ldots,r-1
. Let
f_{j},f^{\prime}_{j}\in C_{X_{j-1}X_{j}}
for any
j=1,\ldots,r
. Then

(5)

d_{X_{0}X_{r}}(f_{1}\circ\cdots\circ f_{r},\,f^{\prime}_{1}\circ\cdots\circ f^{\prime}_{r})\leq\sum_{j=1}^{r}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{j-1}\mathop{\hbox{\rm Lip}}\nolimits(f^{\prime}_{i})\right)d_{X_{j-1}X_{j}}(f_{j},f^{\prime}_{j}).

The various distances
d_{X},d_{Y},d_{YX},\ldots
involved will occasionally be denoted by the same letter
d
when the context clearly indicates which distance is to be considered.

2.3.
Length spaces

Let
P
be an extended metric space, and let
\gamma:I\to P
a continuous path. The length of the path
\gamma
is defined by

(6)

\ell(\gamma):=\mathop{\hbox{\rm sup}}\limits_{k\in\mathbb{N},\,t_{0}\leq\cdots\leq t_{k}\in I}\sum_{i=1}^{k}d\big(\gamma(t_{i-1}),\,\gamma(t_{i})\big).

The metric space
P
is a length space if for any
x,y\in P
the following equality holds:

(7)

d(x,y)=\mathop{\hbox{\rm inf}}\limits_{\mathop{\hbox{\sevenrm paths}}\nolimits\gamma:[0,1]\to P,\,\gamma(0)=x\mathop{\hbox{\sevenrm and}}\nolimits\gamma(1)=y}\ell(\gamma).

In other words, a length space is an extended metric space in which the distance is given by the minimum of the lengths of paths from
x
to
y
. More details on length spaces can be found in
[
8
, Paragraph 2.3]
.

3.
Local flows, local approximate flows and the sewing lemma

Definition 3.1
.

Let
(M_{t},d_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
a one-parameter family of complete metric spaces, possibly in the extended sense. Let
C_{st}
be the set of continuous maps from
M_{t}
to
M_{s}
, endowed with the distance
d_{M_{s}M_{t}}
that will be denoted by
d_{st}
. A
local flow
associated with these data is a continuous action of the pair groupoid
\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}
on the family
(M_{t})
. It is therefore a two-parameter family
(\varphi_{st})_{s,t\in\mathbb{R}}
of maps such that

•

\varphi_{st}\in C_{st}
,

•

\varphi_{ss}=\mathop{\hbox{\rm Id}}\nolimits_{M_{s}}
for any
s\in\mathbb{R}
,

•

\varphi_{st}=\varphi_{su}\circ\varphi_{ut}
for any
s,t,u\in\mathbb{R}
.

In particular, if
(\varphi_{st})
is a local flow,
\varphi_{st}:M_{t}\to M_{s}
is a homeomorphism, with inverse
\varphi_{ts}
.

Remark 3.2
.

In view of our main result (Theorem
3.6
below), it is natural to ask for Lipschitz bounds on the homeomorphisms
\varphi_{st}
, thus taking the metric structure of the real line
\mathbb{R}
into account.

Remark 3.3
.

Definition
3.1
together make sense for any metric space
P
instead of
\mathbb{R}
, we’ll return to this point later.

Definition 3.4
.

Let
(M_{t},d_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
a one-parameter family of complete metric spaces, possibly in the extended sense. A
local approximate flow
associated with these data is a two-parameter family
(\mu_{st})_{s,t\in\mathbb{R}}
of maps such that

•

\mu_{st}\in C_{st}
,

•

\mu_{ss}=\mathop{\hbox{\rm Id}}\nolimits_{M_{s}}
for any
s\in\mathbb{R}
,

•

There exists a continuous function
f:\mathbb{R}\to[0,+\infty[
with
f(0)=0
, such that

\mathop{\hbox{\rm Lip}}\nolimits(\mu_{st})\leq 1+f(t-s),

•

There exists
\varepsilon>0
, an integer
n\geq 1
and two collections of positive constants
a_{i},b_{i},i=1,\ldots,n
with
a_{i}+b_{i}=1+\varepsilon
, such that for any
s,t,u\in\mathbb{R}
the following estimates hold:

(8)

d_{st}\big(\mu_{st},\mu_{su}\circ\mu_{ut}\big)\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}C_{i}|t-u|^{a_{i}}|u-s|^{b_{i}}.

•

There exists a non-decreasing continuous function
g:[0,+\infty[\to[1,+\infty[
such that, for any
s,t\in\mathbb{R}
and any collection
I=(t_{0},\ldots,t_{k})
with
t_{0}=s
and
t_{k}=t
, the map
\mu_{st}^{I}:=\mu_{t_{0}t_{1}}\circ\mu_{t_{1}t_{2}}\circ\cdots\circ\mu_{t_{k-1}t_{k}}
satisfies

\mathop{\hbox{\rm Lip}}\nolimits\mu_{st}^{I}\leq g\left(\sum_{j=1}^{k}|t_{j}-t_{j-1}|\right).

In particular, if the last item of Definition
3.4
is verified, for any subdivision
I=(t_{0},\ldots,t_{k})
of the interval between
s
and
t
, arranged in increasing or decreasing order according to the sign of
t-s
, with
t_{0}=s
and
t_{k}=t
, the map
\mu_{st}^{I}
satisfies

\mathop{\hbox{\rm Lip}}\nolimits\mu_{st}^{I}\leq g(|t-s|).

Remark 3.5
.

The last item of Definition
3.4
is seemingly more restrictive in
[
2
, Theorem 1]
, where the function
g
in only defined on
[0,\delta]
for some small
\delta\geq 0
. In view of (
3
), this function can however be extended to any non-negative number by

g(x):=g(\delta)^{\lfloor\frac{x}{\delta}\rfloor}g\left(x-\delta\lfloor\frac{x}{\delta}\rfloor\right).

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
(\mu_{st})_{s,t\in\mathbb{R}}
be a local approximate flow as in Definition
3.4
. Then there exists a unique local flow
(\varphi_{st})
such that

(9)

d_{st}(\varphi_{st},\mu_{st})\leq C^{\prime}|t-s|^{1+\varepsilon},

which is obtained as the limit in
C_{st}
of the compositions

\mu_{st_{1}}\circ\mu_{t_{1}t_{2}}\circ\cdots\circ\mu_{t_{k-1}t}

when the mesh of the subdivision
(s,t_{1},\ldots,t_{k-1},t)
of the interval between
s
and
t
tends to zero. One possible constant
C^{\prime}
is given by

C^{\prime}=2^{1+\varepsilon}g(|t-s|)\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}C_{i}\right)\zeta(1+\varepsilon),

where
\zeta
is the Riemann zeta function. Moreover, the local flow
(\varphi_{st})
satisfies the Lipschitz condition:

(10)

\mathop{\hbox{\rm Lip}}\nolimits(\varphi_{st})\leq g(|t-s|).

The proof of Theorem
3.6
requires several steps.

Let
(\mu_{st})
be a local approximate flow. Let
I=(t_{0},\ldots,t_{k})
be a subdivision of the interval between
s
and
t
, arranged in increasing or decreasing order according to the sign of
t-s
, with
t_{0}=s
and
t_{k}=t
. The mesh of the subdivision is defined by:

(11)

\mathop{\hbox{\rm mesh}}\nolimits(I):=\mathop{\hbox{\rm sup}}\nolimits_{j=1,\ldots,k}{|t_{j}-t_{j-1}}|.

Lemma 3.7
.

There exists a constant
K
independent of the subdivision
I
such that

(12)

d_{st}(\mu_{st},\mu_{st}^{I})\leq Kg(|t-s|)|t-s|^{1+\varepsilon}.

Proof.

From
I
above we define
I_{1},\ldots,I_{k}
as
I_{j}:=[t_{j-1},t_{j}[
if
s<t
, and
I_{j}:=]t_{j},t_{j-1}]
if
s>t
. Denote by
|I_{j}|=|t_{j}-t_{j-1}|
the length of the
j
th interval. We have then

(13)

\mathop{\hbox{\rm inf}}\nolimits_{j=1,\ldots,k-1}(|I_{j}|+|I_{j+1}|)\leq\frac{2|t-s|}{k-1},

as we can see straightforwardly by writing the whole interval between
s
and
t
(with
t
excluded) as the disjoint union of the
I_{j}
’s, which in turn can be regrouped two by two, omitting
I_{k}
if
k
is odd. Let us consider the index
j
realising the minimum in (
13
), and let
J
be the subdivision obtained from
I
by merging the two blocks
I_{j}
and
I_{j+1}
, thus suppressing
t_{j}
. We have then

\displaystyle d(\mu_{st}^{I},\,\mu_{st}^{J})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle d(\mu_{t_{0}t_{1}}\circ\cdots\circ\mu_{t_{j-1}t_{j}}\circ\mu_{t_{j}t_{j+1}}\circ\cdots\circ\mu_{t_{k-1}t_{k}},\,\mu_{t_{0}t_{1}}\circ\cdots\circ\mu_{t_{j-1}t_{j+1}}\circ\cdots\circ\mu_{t_{k-1}t_{k}})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathop{\hbox{\rm Lip}}\nolimits(\mu_{t_{0}t_{1}}\circ\cdots\circ\mu_{t_{j-2}t_{j-1}})\sum_{i=1}^{n}C_{i}|I_{j}|^{b_{i}}|I_{j+1}|^{a_{i}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle g(|t-s|)\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}C_{i}\right)\left(\frac{2}{k-1}\right)^{1+\varepsilon}|t-s|^{1+\varepsilon}.

Iterating this process down to the trivial subdivision, we get (
12
), with

(14)

K=2^{1+\varepsilon}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}C_{i}\right)\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{1}{\ell}\right)^{1+\varepsilon}=2^{1+\varepsilon}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}C_{i}\right)\zeta(1+\varepsilon).

∎

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
(\mu_{st})
be a local approximate flow, and let
I,J
be two subdivisions of the interval between
s
and
t
, with
J
finer than
I
. Then

(15)

d(\mu_{st}^{I},\,\mu_{st}^{J})\leq Kg(|t-s|)g\big(\mathop{\hbox{\rm mesh}}\nolimits(I)\big)\mathop{\hbox{\rm mesh}}\nolimits(I)^{\varepsilon}|t-s|.

Proof.

We denote by
J_{j}
the subdivision of the block
I_{j}
induced by
J
, and we use the shorthand
\mu_{t_{j-1}t_{j}}^{J}
for
\mu_{t_{j-1}t_{j}}^{J_{j}}
. We can compute, using Lemma
3.7
:

\displaystyle d(\mu_{st}^{I},\,\mu_{st}^{J})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{r-1}d(\mu_{t_{0}t_{1}}\cdots\mu_{t_{j-1}t_{j}}\mu_{t_{j}t_{j+1}}\cdots\mu_{t_{k-1}t_{k}},\,\mu_{t_{0}t_{1}}\cdots\mu_{t_{j-1}t_{j}}\mu_{t_{j}t_{j+1}}^{J}\cdots\mu_{t_{k-1}t_{k}}^{J})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle g(|t-s|)\sum_{j=0}^{r-1}d(\mu_{t_{j}t_{j+1}},\,\mu_{t_{j}t_{j+1}}^{J})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle g(|t-s|)\sum_{j=0}^{r-1}Kg(|t_{j+1}-t_{j}|)|t_{j+1}-t_{j}|^{1+\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Kg(|t-s|)g\big(\mathop{\hbox{\rm mesh}}\nolimits(I)\big)\sum_{j=0}^{r-1}|t_{j+1}-t_{j}||t_{j+1}-t_{j}|^{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Kg(|t-s|)g\big(\mathop{\hbox{\rm mesh}}\nolimits(I)\big)\sum_{j=0}^{r-1}|t_{j+1}-t_{j}|\mathop{\hbox{\rm mesh}}\nolimits(I)^{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Kg(|t-s|)g\big(\mathop{\hbox{\rm mesh}}\nolimits(I)\big)\mathop{\hbox{\rm mesh}}\nolimits(I)^{\varepsilon}|t-s|.

∎

Corollary 3.9
.

For any pair
(I,I^{\prime})
of subdivisions we have:

(16)

d(\mu_{st}^{I},\,\mu_{st}^{I^{\prime}})\leq Kg(|t-s|)\Big(g\big(\mathop{\hbox{\rm mesh}}\nolimits(I)\big)\mathop{\hbox{\rm mesh}}\nolimits(I)^{\varepsilon}+g\big(\mathop{\hbox{\rm mesh}}\nolimits(I^{\prime})\big)\mathop{\hbox{\rm mesh}}\nolimits(I^{\prime})^{\varepsilon}\Big)|t-s|.

Proof.

It is an immediate consequence of the triangle inequality

d(\mu_{st}^{I},\,\mu_{st}^{I^{\prime}})\leq d(\mu_{st}^{I},\,\mu_{st}^{J})+d(\mu_{st}^{J},\,\mu_{st}^{I^{\prime}})

where
J
is the joint subdivision of
I
and
I^{\prime}
, i.e. the coarsest subdivision which is both finer than
I
and finer than
I^{\prime}
.
∎

Corollary 3.10
.

For any
s,t\in\mathbb{R}
, the family
(\mu_{st}^{I})_{I}
converges in
C_{st}
to a limit
\varphi_{st}
when
\mathop{\hbox{\rm mesh}}\nolimits(I)
tends to 0, and we have

(17)

d_{st}(\mu_{st},\varphi_{st})\leq Kg(|t-s|)|t-s|^{1+\varepsilon}.

where the constant
K
is given by (
14
).

Proof of Theorem
3.6
.
Let
s,t,u\in\mathbb{R}
, with
u
between
s
and
t
. For any subdivision
I
(resp.
I^{\prime}
) of the interval between
s
and
u
(resp. between
u
and
t
), the union of both subdivisions yields a subdivision
J
of the interval between
s
and
t
, with
\mathop{\hbox{\rm mesh}}\nolimits(J)=\mathop{\hbox{\rm sup}}\nolimits\big(\mathop{\hbox{\rm mesh}}\nolimits(I),\,\mathop{\hbox{\rm mesh}}\nolimits(I^{\prime})\big)
, and we have

\mu_{st}^{J}=\mu_{su}^{I}\circ\mu_{ut}^{I^{\prime}}.

Letting the mesh of
I
and
I^{\prime}
go to zero, Corollary
3.10
yields:

(18)

\varphi_{st}=\varphi_{su}\circ\varphi_{ut}.

It remains to show that
\varphi_{ts}
is the inverse of
\varphi_{st}
. Choosing, for example, the regular subdivision
I_{k}=(t_{0},\ldots,t_{k})
with
t_{j}=s+j(t-s)/k
, and denoting by
\overline{I_{k}}
the corresponding reverse subdivision, we have

\displaystyle\mu_{st}^{I_{k}}\circ\mu_{ts}^{\overline{I_{k}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mu_{t_{0}t_{1}}\circ\cdots\circ\mu_{t_{k-1}t_{k}}\circ\mu_{t_{k}t_{k-1}}\circ\cdots\circ\mu_{t_{1}t_{0}}.

Hence we get

\displaystyle d(\mu_{st}^{I_{k}}\circ\mu_{ts}^{\overline{I_{k}}},\,\mu_{ss})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}d(\mu_{t_{0}t_{1}}\circ\cdots\circ\mu_{t_{j-1}t_{j}}\circ\mu_{t_{j}t_{j-1}}\circ\cdots\circ\mu_{t_{1}t_{0}},\,\mu_{t_{0}t_{1}}\circ\cdots\circ\mu_{t_{j}t_{j+1}}\circ\mu_{t_{j+1}t_{j}}\circ\cdots\circ\mu_{t_{1}t_{0}})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle g(|t-s|)\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}d(\mu_{t_{j}t_{j}},\,\mu_{t_{j}t_{j+1}}\circ\mu_{t_{j+1}t_{j}})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle g(|t-s|)k\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}C_{i}\right)\left(\frac{|t-s|}{k}\right)^{1+\varepsilon},

which gives
\varphi_{st}\circ\varphi_{ts}=\varphi_{ss}
by letting
k
go to infinity. Finally, the Lipschitz condition for
\varphi
immediately follows from the last item of Definition
3.4
.

∎

Remark 3.11
.

In the particular case when the function
f
in Definition
3.4
is given by
f(\delta):=L\delta
, the last item is automatically verified with
g(\delta)=e^{L\delta}
. The very reason is that the inequality

\prod_{j=1}^{k}(1+L\delta_{j})\leq e^{L\delta}

holds for any
L\geq 0
, for any positive integer
k
and for any
k
-tuple
(\delta_{1},\ldots,\delta_{k})
of non-negative real numbers such that
\sum_{j=1}^{k}\delta_{j}=\delta
. Details are left to the reader.

Remark 3.12
.

If
(\mu_{st})_{s,t\in\mathbb{R}}
is an approximate flow in the sense of Definition
3.4
, for any
s,t,u,v\in\mathbb{R}
we have from Lemma
2.3
and (
8
):

(19)

\displaystyle d_{st}(\mu_{su}\circ\mu_{ut},\,\mu_{sv}\circ\mu_{vt})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle d_{st}(\mu_{su}\circ\mu_{ut},\,\mu_{su}\circ\mu_{uv}\circ\mu_{vt})+d_{st}(\mu_{su}\circ\mu_{uv}\circ\mu_{vt},\,\mu_{sv}\circ\mu_{vt})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\big(1+f(|u-s|)\big)\sum_{i=1}^{n}C_{i}|t-v|^{a_{i}}|u-v|^{b_{i}}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}C_{i}|s-u|^{b_{i}}|u-v|^{a_{i}}.

Applying the inequality (
19
) above for
v=t
gives back (
8
). It gives moreover the optimal bound
0
for
u=v
.

4.
Applications and generalisations

After showing how Theorem
3.6
encompasses previous versions of the sewing lemma, we reformulate it in terms an approximate action of the pair groupoid of
\mathbb{R}

Example 4.1

Consider the constant family

Definition 4.3

A path in a topological space

Example 2. The fundamental groupoid of a topological space.
Recall that two paths
\gamma^{0}

\gamma^{1}

•

\gamma^{0}(0)=\gamma^{1}(0)=a

\gamma^{0}(1)=\gamma^{1}(1)=b

\gamma^{0}
and
\gamma^{1}

and

\gamma^{0}\sim\gamma^{1}
and
{\gamma^{\prime}}^{0}\sim{\gamma^{\prime}}^{1}
, one has
\gamma^{0}{\gamma^{\prime}}^{0}\sim\gamma^{1}{\gamma^{\prime}}^{1}
. The fundamental groupoid of

\gamma_{st}\sim\gamma_{su}.\gamma_{ut}.

The (reversed) concatenation is not associative, but it is well-known that

(\gamma^{\prime\prime}.\gamma^{\prime}).\gamma\sim\gamma^{\prime\prime}.(\gamma^{\prime}.\gamma).

The homotopy between both sides of (

\displaystyle H(s,t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\gamma(t)\hbox{ if }0\leq t\leq\frac{1}{4}(2-s),

\displaystyle\gamma^{\prime}(t)\hbox{ if }\frac{1}{4}(2-s)\leq t\leq\frac{1}{4}(3-s),

\displaystyle\gamma^{\prime\prime}(t)\hbox{ if }\frac{1}{4}(3-s)\leq t\leq 1.

\gamma.\gamma^{-1}

Two paths
\gamma^{0}
and
\gamma^{1}

a

if there is a finite collection
\beta^{0},\ldots,\beta^{k}

a
and endpoint

\beta^{0}=\gamma^{0}

\beta^{k}=\gamma^{1}

\gamma^{0}
is thin equivalent to any of its reparametrisations
\gamma^{1}=\gamma^{0}\circ\varphi
, where
\varphi:[0,1]\to[0,1]

\gamma^{s}(t):=\gamma^{0}\circ\varphi_{s}(t)

\varphi_{s}(t):=(1-s)t+s\varphi(t)
. The following homotopy between the constant loop
\gamma^{0}(0)
and

, given by

\displaystyle K(s,t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\gamma^{s}(2t)\hbox{ if }0\leq t\leq\frac{s}{2},

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\gamma^{s}(s)\hbox{ if }\frac{s}{2}\leq t\leq 1-\frac{\varphi_{s}(s)}{2},

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\gamma^{0}(2-2t)\hbox{ if }1-\frac{\varphi_{s}(s)}{2}\leq t\leq 1,

In particular,

\mathcal{G}_{P}^{\mathop{\hbox{\sevenrm th}}\nolimits}
. It is easily seen that two thin equivalent paths are homotopic. The inclusion of thin equivalence classes into homotopy classes induces a natural surjective groupoid morphism

\pi:\mathcal{G}_{P}^{\mathop{\hbox{\sevenrm th}}\nolimits}\longrightarrow\hskip-9.0pt\to\mathcal{G}_{P}.

Remark 4.4
.

The surjective morphism above turns out to be an isomorphism. This very surprising and counter-intuitive fact has been recently established by J. Brazas, G. R. Conner, P. Fabel and C. Kent
[

who, for any homotopy

and

defined as follows:
\tau(1)=1
, and if

admits the expansion

in base

is defined by its expansion

in base

•

The image of this Peano-like path

The three groupoids above coincide for

4.3.
The groupoid of Lipschitz paths modulo thin Lipschitz equivalence

Let
P
be a metric space. Recall that a path
\gamma:[0,1]\to P
is Lipschitz if

\mathop{\hbox{\rm Lip}}\nolimits(\gamma)=\mathop{\hbox{\rm sup}}\limits_{s,t\in[0,1],s\neq t}\frac{d_{P}\big(\gamma(s),\gamma(t)\big)}{|s-t|}<+\infty.

Definition 4.8
.

Two Lipschitz paths
\gamma^{0}
and
\gamma^{1}
are
Lipschitz-homotopic
if there exists a Lipschitz homotopy
H:[0,1]^{2}\to P
between both paths.

Note that two Lipschitz paths can be homotopic without being Lipschitz-homotopic.

Definition 4.9
.

Two Lipschitz paths
\gamma^{0}
and
\gamma^{1}
from
a
to
b
are
Lipschitz-thin-equivalent
if there is a finite collection
\beta^{0},\ldots,\beta^{k}
of paths with starting point
a
and endpoint
b
, with
\beta^{0}=\gamma^{0}
and
\beta^{k}=\gamma^{1}
, such that the concatenation
(\beta^{j})^{-1}.\beta^{j-1}
is Lipschitz-thin for any
j=1,\ldots,k
, i.e. if the loop
(\beta^{j})^{-1}\beta^{j-1}
is Lipschitz-thin, i.e. there exists a Lipschitz homotopy
H:[0,1]^{2}\to P
from
(\beta^{j})^{-1}\beta^{j-1}
to the constant path
t\mapsto a
such that the thinness condition
H([0,1]^{2})\subseteq\gamma([0,1])
holds.

Lipschitz-thin equivalence, similarly to thin-equivalence, is an equivalence relation.

Proposition 4.10
.

For any pair of paths
(\gamma,\gamma^{\prime})
in a metric space
P
, the following estimate holds:

\mathop{\hbox{\rm Lip}}\nolimits(\gamma.\gamma^{\prime})\leq 2\mathop{\hbox{\rm sup}}\nolimits\big(\mathop{\hbox{\rm Lip}}\nolimits(\gamma),\,\mathop{\hbox{\rm Lip}}\nolimits(\gamma^{\prime})\big).

Moreover, for any
s,t\in[0,1]
we have

\mathop{\hbox{\rm Lip}}\nolimits(\gamma_{st})\leq|t-s|\mathop{\hbox{\rm Lip}}\nolimits(\gamma).

Proof.

This is immediate. The factors
2
and
|t-s|
come from rescaling.
∎

Thin Lipschitz equivalence is compatible with concatenation, and
\gamma.(\gamma^{\prime}.\gamma^{\prime\prime})
is Lipschitz-thin equivalent to
(\gamma.\gamma^{\prime}).\gamma^{\prime\prime}
for any triple of Lipschitz paths composable that way. This therefore defines the
Lipschitz-thin equivalence groupoid

\mathcal{G}_{P}^{\mathop{\hbox{\sevenrm Lip-th}}\nolimits}\subseteq\mathcal{G}_{P}^{\mathop{\hbox{\sevenrm th}}\nolimits}
as the equivalence classes of Lipschitz paths modulo thin equivalence. The inclusion may be strict, because there can exist a path in
P
which is not thin-homotopic to any Lipschitz path: an example of this situation is given by the image of a non-Lipschitz path (e.g. a sample path of a Brownian motion) in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.

4.4.
The Lipschitz homotopy groupoid

This notion is adapted from
[
11
, Definition 4.1]
. Let
P
be a metric space, and let
x,y\in P
. Two Lipschitz paths with domain
[0,1]
from
x
to
y
are Lipschitz-homotopic if there exists a Lipschitz homotopy
H:[0,1]^{2}\to P
. This notion is obviously compatible with concatenation. The Lipschitz homotopy groupoid

\mathcal{G}_{P}^{\mathop{\hbox{\sevenrm Lip}}\nolimits}
is the set of Lipschitz paths
\gamma:[0,1]\to P
modulo Lipschitz homotopy.

Both groupoids
\mathcal{G}_{P}
and
\mathcal{G}_{P}^{\mathop{\hbox{\sevenrm Lip}}\nolimits}
often coincide, for example when
P
is a smooth manifold: in this case, this is due to the fact that any continuous path is homotopic to a smooth (hence Lipschitz) one, and any two homotopic smooth paths can be interpolated by a smooth homotopy.

The proof of Theorem

Step 1:

For any Lipschitz path

\big((\mu_{\gamma})_{s,t}\big)_{s,t\in[0,1]}
given by
(\mu_{\gamma})_{s,t}:=\mu_{\gamma(s)\gamma(t)}
, with the modification
\zeta(2+\varepsilon)
instead of
\zeta(1+\varepsilon)
coming from the strong four-point estimate (
25
). The Lipschitz parameter from Definition
3.4
for the approximate flow
(\mu_{\gamma})
is indeed
L\mathop{\hbox{\rm Lip}}\nolimits(\gamma)
. Details are left to the reader.

Step 2:

It remains to show that
\varphi_{\gamma}
does not depend on the choice of the path
\gamma
in a given Lipschitz homotopy class. Let
x,y\in P
, let
\gamma^{0}
and
\gamma^{1}
be two Lipschitz continuous paths from
[0,1]
to
P
with starting point
x
and endpoint
y
. Let
H:[0,1]^{2}\to P
be a Lipschitz homotopy from
\gamma^{0}
to
\gamma^{1}
. We recall the convenient notation
\gamma^{s}(t):=H(s,t)
. Let
\ell
be the Lipschitz norm of
H
, namely

\ell:=\mathop{\hbox{\rm sup}}\limits_{t,s,t^{\prime},s^{\prime}\in[0,1],(s,t)\neq(s^{\prime},t^{\prime})}\frac{d_{P}\big(\gamma^{s}(t),\gamma^{s^{\prime}}(t^{\prime})\big)}{|t^{\prime}-t|+|s^{\prime}-s|}.

In particular, the Lipschitz norm of any path
\gamma^{s},\,s\in[0,1]
is smaller or equal to
\ell
. As a consequence,
d_{P}(x,y)\leq\ell
.

x_{0}^{i}=x
and
x_{k}^{i}=y

Remark 4.15
.

Proving Conjecture
4.13
would lead to a consistent definition of holonomy for based Lipschitz loops. The knitting lemma is a flatness result in the sense that it stipulates that the holonomy of any contractible based Lipschitz loop is trivial.

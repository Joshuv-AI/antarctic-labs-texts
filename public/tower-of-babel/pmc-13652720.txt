Autologous stem cell transplantation in POEMS syndrome: Multicenter real-world treatment patterns and outcomes
Authors: Luis Gerardo Rodríguez-Lobato, Anna de Daniel, Itziar Carro Arostegui, Iago Arribas Castro, Mercedes Gironella, Jordi Verge, Lourdes Escoda, Carles Tolosa, Josep María Martí-Tutusaus, Cristina Baca, Eugènia Abella, Cristina Motlló, Jordi López-Pardo, Albert Oriol, Carlos Castillo-Girón, Natalia Tovar, Laura Rosiñol, M Teresa Cibeira, Anna Sureda, Carlos Fernández de Larrea
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractPOEMS syndrome is a rare multisystem plasma cell disorder for which evidence guiding management is largely derived from single-center retrospective series. We conducted a retrospective multicenter study including all consecutive patients diagnosed between January 1995 and June 2024 across nine referral hospitals in Catalonia, Spain, within the GEMMAC collaborative network. Clinical characteristics, treatment strategies, transplant outcomes, and long-term survival were analyzed. Forty patients were included, with a median age of 65 years; 25 underwent autologous stem cell transplantation (ASCT). Hematologic response to first-line therapy was achieved in 77.5% of patients. After a median follow-up of 94.2 months, median progression-free survival was 102.6 months and median overall survival was 146.9 months, with 5-year rates of 69.0% and 87.6%, respectively. ASCT was associated with deep and durable hematologic and clinical responses. Baseline ECOG performance status and depth of hematologic response correlated with outcomes. VEGF levels declined markedly after treatment, particularly following ASCT. Transplant-related toxicity was manageable, although late complications were observed. These findings support ASCT as a central therapeutic strategy in eligible patients and underscore the need for long-term follow-up.Subject terms: Myeloma, Epidemiology, Disease-free survival, Stem-cell therapies

Introduction

POEMS syndrome is a rare paraneoplastic disorder associated with an underlying plasma cell clone and characterized by a distinctive multisystem phenotype including polyneuropathy, organomegaly, endocrinopathy, and skin changes. Despite a typically low tumor burden, the syndrome is associated with substantial morbidity and may be life-threatening due to progressive neurological disability, extravascular volume overload, thrombotic complications, and end-organ dysfunction [1–3]. Although survival has improved with modern therapeutic strategies, POEMS syndrome remains a diagnostic and therapeutic challenge because of its clinical heterogeneity and frequent diagnostic delay. Available evidence is largely derived from retrospective series, highlighting the need for contemporary real-world data to better define management strategies and long-term outcomes [4, 5].

The pathophysiology of POEMS syndrome is primarily driven by a dysregulated cytokine and angiogenic milieu rather than by tumor burden itself. Vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) has consistently been implicated as a central mediator, promoting vascular permeability, angiogenesis, and extravascular fluid accumulation [6–8]. Circulating VEGF levels are markedly elevated in most patients and typically decline in parallel with clinical improvement following effective therapy, supporting its role as both a pathogenic factor and a biomarker of disease activity [8, 9]. Accordingly, VEGF measurement has been incorporated into diagnostic algorithms and longitudinal monitoring, although its systematic use has varied across institutions [3, 9]. Additional cytokine imbalances, including interleukin-6 and tumor necrosis factor-α, further underscore the biological complexity of the disease [7, 10].

Treatment strategies aim to eradicate or durably control the underlying plasma cell clone and are guided by disease extent and patient fitness. Localized disease may be managed with radiotherapy, whereas disseminated involvement requires systemic therapy, including alkylator-based regimens, immunomodulatory drugs (IMiDs), or proteasome inhibitors (PIs) [5, 11]. In transplant-eligible patients, high-dose melphalan followed by autologous stem cell transplantation (ASCT) has become a standard consolidative approach, achieving high rates of deep hematologic responses and sustained clinical improvement in retrospective series and registry-based studies [12–14]. Long-term data suggest that ASCT can provide durable disease control with prolonged progression-free survival (PFS), although relapse and late complications may occur [3, 12–16]. Nevertheless, existing evidence remains discordant regarding induction strategies, transplantation timing, and response assessment [3, 11].

Despite growing support for contemporary treatment approaches, most data derive from single-center experiences. Real-world multicenter cohorts integrating long-term survival, transplant-related toxicity, repeat ASCT strategies, and longitudinal VEGF dynamics remain scarce. Given the chronic disease course and the recognition of late complications, including second primary malignancies, further multicenter data are needed. We therefore conducted a retrospective study within the Catalan Group for the Study of Multiple Myeloma and Amyloidosis (GEMMAC) to characterize clinical presentation, first-line treatment strategies, and long-term outcomes in patients with POEMS syndrome, with particular emphasis on ASCT, treatment-related complications, and the exploratory prognostic role of VEGF kinetics in routine practice.

Patients and methods

Study design and population

This retrospective multicenter observational study included all consecutive patients diagnosed with POEMS syndrome between January 1995 and June 2024 across nine referral hospitals within the GEMMAC network. Participating centers were tertiary institutions with expertise in plasma cell disorders and paraneoplastic neuropathies. The study was approved by the Ethics Committee (CEIm) of Hospital Clínic of Barcelona, the coordinating center of the GEMMAC study, which covered all participating institutions, in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and applicable local regulations (approval number HCB/2021/0100). Given the retrospective nature of the study and the fact that patients were no longer routinely followed, the Ethics Committee waived the requirement for individual informed consent. All data were anonymized prior to analysis.

Eligible cases were identified through institutional clinical databases and electronic medical records. Diagnosis was confirmed according to the criteria proposed by Dispenzieri et al. [3], requiring both mandatory major criteria (polyneuropathy and monoclonal plasma cell disorder), at least one additional major criterion (sclerotic bone lesions, Castleman disease, or elevated VEGF), and at least one minor criterion. Patients with other monoclonal gammopathies without POEMS-defining features and those with the Castleman disease variant without detectable monoclonal plasma cell disorder were excluded. All cases underwent centralized diagnostic review at the Hospital Clínic of Barcelona, the coordinating center of the GEMMAC study, to ensure consistent application of diagnostic criteria across participating sites. Clinical, laboratory, radiologic, and treatment data were collected at diagnosis and during follow-up. The last follow-up update was performed in June 2025.

Data collection and definitions

Baseline variables included age, sex, clinical manifestations fulfilling diagnostic criteria, monoclonal protein characteristics, and routine laboratory parameters. VEGF measurements were not systematically available before 2010; when available, levels were recorded at diagnosis and following induction therapy and/or ASCT. Given the retrospective design, VEGF analyses were considered exploratory and limited to patients with available measurements.

Treatment-related variables included first-line therapy, ASCT, and subsequent treatment lines. Hematologic, VEGF, and clinical responses were assessed according to previously published criteria and standard clinical practice in POEMS syndrome [17]. Clinical response was defined as physician-assessed improvement of POEMS-related manifestations. Standardized neurological scales, quality-of-life assessments, IL-6 measurements, and minimal residual disease evaluations were not available.

Statistical analysis

Categorical variables were summarized as frequencies and percentages, and continuous variables as medians and interquartile ranges (IQR). Group comparisons were performed using the χ² or Fisher’s exact test for categorical variables and the Mann–Whitney U test for continuous variables, as appropriate. Overall survival (OS) was defined as time from diagnosis to death from any cause or last follow-up. Progression-free survival (PFS) was defined as time from initiation of first-line therapy to progression, relapse, death, or last follow-up. Survival curves were estimated using the Kaplan–Meier method and compared with the log-rank test. Follow-up time was calculated using the reverse Kaplan–Meier method. Univariate Cox proportional hazards models were used to estimate hazard ratios (HRs) and 95% confidence intervals (CIs). Multivariable analysis was not performed due to the limited number of events. Time to next treatment (TTNT) was defined as the time from initiation of first-line therapy to the start of any subsequent line of treatment. TTNT was analyzed using the cumulative incidence function to account for death without receipt of salvage therapy as a competing event, with group comparisons performed using Gray’s test. Statistical analyses were conducted using R software (RStudio 2024.04.1), with two-sided P values < 0.05 considered significant.

Results

All patients had peripheral neuropathy and a monoclonal plasma cell disorder. Neuropathy was demyelinating in 22 (55%), axonal in 5 (12.5%), and mixed in 13 (32.5%). The monoclonal component was predominantly lambda-restricted, most commonly IgA lambda (19 patients, 47.5%) and IgG lambda (18, 45%). Median bone marrow plasma cell infiltration was 4% (IQR, 3.0–7.5). Serum free light chain data were available in 20 patients; median involved free light chain was 60.9 mg/L (IQR, 10.0–115.3) with a median involved/uninvolved ratio of 2.22 (IQR, 1.41–2.90).

Castleman disease was present in 8 patients (20%). Sclerotic bone lesions were observed in 23 (57.5%), multifocal in 17 (42.5%). Baseline VEGF was available in 26 patients, with a median of 843 pg/mL (IQR, 538–1200) (Supplementary Table S1).

Organomegaly was documented in 28 patients (70%), most commonly splenomegaly (52.5%) and lymphadenopathy (47.5%). Extravascular volume overload occurred in 24 (60%), including peripheral edema (55%), pleural effusion (12.5%), and ascites (20%). Endocrinopathies were observed in 24 (60%), most frequently thyroid (32.5%) and gonadal involvement (20%). Cutaneous manifestations were present in 24 patients (60%), including hemangiomas (25%) and hyperpigmentation (20%). Papilledema occurred in 8 (20%). Thrombocytosis and polycythemia were observed in 23 (57.5%) and 9 (22.5%) patients, respectively. Pulmonary hypertension, restrictive lung disease, and thrombotic events were present in 12.5%, 30.8%, and 5%, respectively.

Outcomes in non-transplanted patients

The 15 patients who did not undergo ASCT represented a heterogeneous group. Reasons for non-transplantation included achievement of complete hematologic response with systemic therapy alone (n = 4), significant treatment-related complications precluding transplantation (n = 4), and localized disease managed with radiotherapy alone (n = 1). In the remaining patients, a combination of advanced age, comorbidities, and elevated ECOG performance status contributed to the decision not to proceed to transplantation; institutional eligibility thresholds for ASCT varied across participating centers, and patient age in this subgroup ranged from 42 to 86 years.

Hematologic response was achieved in 9 of 15 patients (60%), including CR in 4 (26.7%), very good partial response (VGPR) in 2 (13.3%), and PR in 3 (20%); 6 patients (40%) did not achieve at least PR. Regarding survival, median PFS was 147 months (95% CI, 59 months–not reached), with a 5-year PFS of 69.1% (95% CI, 47.8–100). Median OS was 150 months (95% CI, 131 months–not reached), with a 5-year OS of 83.3% (95% CI, 64.7–100).

Outcomes after ASCT

Twenty-five patients underwent first-line ASCT; conditioning consisted of melphalan 200 mg/m² (Mel200) in 19 patients and melphalan 140 mg/m² (Mel140) in 6 patients. All five patients who underwent a second ASCT received Mel200 conditioning. Regarding hematopoietic recovery data after first-line ASCT, the median time to neutrophil engraftment ( > 500/µL) was 15 days (range, 10–45) and median time to platelet recovery ( > 20,000/µL) was 13 days (range, 8–87). Hematologic response was achieved in 23/25 (92%): CR in 13 (52%), VGPR in 7 (28%), PR in 3 (12%), and no response in 2 (8%). Clinical response was evaluable in 21 patients and observed in 20 (95.2%). Neuropathy improved in 18/21 (85.7%), bone disease in 7/12 (58.3%), and endocrine manifestations in 2/10 (20%).

VEGF levels declined across treatment phases (Fig. 3). Median VEGF decreased from 885 pg/mL (IQR, 650–1240) at diagnosis to 131 pg/mL (IQR, 77.7–635) pre-ASCT and 68 pg/mL (IQR, 23.2–95.3) post-ASCT. Paired analyses showed significant decline from pre- to post-ASCT (n = 7; p = 0.03) and from diagnosis to post-ASCT (n = 11; p < 0.001).

When outcomes were compared according to transplant setting, no statistically significant differences were observed between the upfront (n = 10) and consolidation (n = 15) groups. Median PFS was 102.6 months (95% CI, 63.2 months–not reached) in the upfront group versus 76.6 months (95% CI, 38.1 months–not reached) in the consolidation group (HR 1.25, 95% CI 0.38–4.16; p = 0.7). Oligoclonal bands (OB) were detected in 9/24 evaluable patients (37.5%). The most frequent OB isotypes were IgG kappa (n = 7) and IgG lambda (n = 3). CR occurred in 77.8% of patients with OB vs. 40% without (p = 0.03) (Supplementary Table S3). OB was associated with a trend toward longer PFS (HR 0.26; 95% CI, 0.06–1.27; p = 0.075), without OS difference (p = 0.99) (Supplementary Fig. S2).

Second-line therapy and second ASCT

Ten patients (25%) required second-line therapy during follow-up. The median TTNT, analyzed using a competing risks approach with death without receipt of salvage therapy as a competing event, was 159.9 months from first-line therapy initiation, with a 5-year cumulative incidence of second-line therapy requirement of 23.1% and a 10-year cumulative incidence of 40.2%. Salvage regimens were heterogeneous: alkylator-based in 2 patients, IMiD-based in 2, PI-based in 1, and anti-CD38 monoclonal antibody–based in 1. One additional patient received radiotherapy, and 3 underwent an upfront ASCT as second-line strategy. Two further patients received re-induction therapy followed by a second ASCT (ASCT2) as consolidation. Median PFS from the start of salvage treatment was not reached (95% CI, 43.8 months–not reached), with a 5-year PFS of 63.5% (95% CI, 37.7–100); median OS was similarly not reached (95% CI, 43.8 months–not reached), with a 5-year OS of 71.4% (95% CI, 44.7–100).

In total, five patients underwent ASCT2 as part of second-line management (Supplementary Table S4). Four had previously received an upfront ASCT in first-line therapy. The interval between first and second ASCT ranged from 60.3 to 159.9 months, indicating prolonged disease control after the initial procedure. All ASCT2 were conditioned with high-dose melphalan. Stem cell mobilization and collection were successful in all cases, with CD34+ yields ranging from 2.63 to 5.98 ×10⁶/kg. Hematopoietic recovery was prompt, with a median neutrophil recovery ( > 500/µL) of 13 days (range, 11–17) and a median platelet recovery ( > 20,000/µL) of 18 days (range, 10–55). Prior to ASCT2, two patients received re-induction therapy with CyBorD or lenalidomide plus dexamethasone, while three proceeded directly to transplantation. Following ASCT2, deep hematologic responses were achieved, including CR in two patients and VGPR in one. At a median follow-up of 71 months from ASCT2, four of five patients remained relapse-free, while one experienced disease relapse (Supplementary Table S4). The remaining five patients received salvage systemic therapy without proceeding to ASCT2, with a median PFS of 68.7 months (95% CI, 17.9 months–not reached) and a 5-year OS of 100% from the start of second-line treatment, although these estimates are based on very few events and should be interpreted with caution.

Transplant-related complications

Transplant-related complications were evaluated in 30 patients who underwent ASCT. Neutropenic fever was the most frequent event, occurring in 9 patients (30%), followed by engraftment syndrome (ES) in 8 (26.6%), diagnosed according to the criteria of Spitzer et al. [18] and Maiolino et al. [19]. The most frequent manifestations were fever and generalized rash (8/8, 100%), weight gain (5/8, 62%), peripheral edema (4/8, 50%), pulmonary involvement including dyspnea, pulmonary infiltrates, or non-cardiogenic pulmonary edema (3/8, 38%). All patients received methylprednisolone at 1 mg/kg/day with subsequent tapering, with complete resolution of symptoms in all cases. Bacterial infections were seen in 5 patients (16.6%), acute renal failure and grade ≥3 mucositis were each reported in 3 patients (10%). Hemorrhagic complications occurred in 2 patients (6.7%), while thrombotic events and cardiac arrhythmias were observed in 1 patient each (3.3%). Fungal infection, cytomegalovirus reactivation, and poor graft function were each documented in 1 patient (3.3%). Transplant-related mortality (TRM) occurred in 2 patients (6.7%). One death was due to septic shock during the aplastic phase, and the second was related to severe hypervolemia complicated by acute cardiac failure (Supplementary Table S5).

Peri-transplant management was heterogeneous across participating centers and time periods, reflecting real-world practice variation. Standard supportive care including antimicrobial prophylaxis, and transfusion support was applied consistently according to institutional guidelines. Corticosteroid prophylaxis for ES and G-CSF withdrawal post-ASCT were implemented in a minority of procedures at a single center based on institutional protocol; the remaining centers did not adopt systematic ES prophylaxis during the study period.

Second primary neoplasms

Second primary neoplasms (SPN) were diagnosed in 7 of 40 patients (17.5%) during follow-up. Hematologic malignancies included myelodysplastic syndrome (MDS) evolving to acute myeloid leukemia (AML) in one patient (diagnosed 106 and 134 months after transplantation, respectively), AML in a second patient (102 months after first ASCT and 34 months after a second Mel200-conditioned ASCT), and non-Hodgkin lymphoma (NHL) in one patient (73 months after ASCT). Solid tumors comprised breast cancer in 2 patients (84 and 137 months), renal neoplasm in 1 (83 months), and head and neck carcinoma in 1 (67 months). The median time to SPN development was 84 months (IQR, 73–134).

Regarding prior exposure to potentially leukemogenic therapies, the patient with MDS/AML had received cyclophosphamide-containing induction (R-COP) prior to ASCT consolidation. The patient with NHL had received cyclophosphamide-based salvage therapy following disease progression after ASCT. The patient with AML had not received additional alkylating agents in salvage but had been treated with lenalidomide-based therapy prior to a second Mel200-conditioned ASCT.

Discussion

In this multicenter real-world study spanning nearly three decades within the GEMMAC network, we describe the clinical presentation, treatment patterns, and long-term outcomes of POEMS syndrome in a contemporary Western cohort. Our findings confirm POEMS syndrome as a chronic plasma cell–driven disorder in which durable disease control and prolonged survival can be achieved with appropriate therapy. In transplant-eligible patients, ASCT remains a key component of first-line management, producing deep hematologic responses, meaningful clinical improvement, and sustained disease control. Our exploratory analyses also support the dynamic role of VEGF as a biomarker when assessed longitudinally alongside hematologic and clinical outcomes.

The survival outcomes observed in our cohort are consistent with the largest published series of POEMS syndrome [2, 13, 14]. Both PFS and OS compare favorably with historical Mayo Clinic cohorts and more recent registry-based studies, including large Western and Asian experiences [2, 13, 14, 20]. Despite including older patients and diagnoses across different therapeutic eras, long-term survival was preserved. This supports the concept of POEMS syndrome as a chronic disease in which outcomes are more closely related to depth and durability of response than to calendar period alone [12].

Our data reinforce the central role of ASCT in systemic POEMS syndrome. ASCT was associated with high rates of deep hematologic responses and substantial neurological improvement, translating into durable disease control. The 5-year PFS and OS rates observed in our transplanted cohort are within the ranges reported in major international series, including CIBMTR, EBMT, and large single-center studies, where 5-year PFS approximates 65–80% and OS approaches or exceeds 90% [13, 14, 16, 21–26]. Importantly, achievement of CR after ASCT was associated with significantly prolonged PFS, consistent with prior observations linking depth of response to improved post-transplant outcomes. When outcomes were compared according to transplant setting, no statistically significant differences in PFS or OS were observed between patients who received ASCT as upfront therapy and those who underwent transplantation as consolidation following induction, although the limited sample size and number of events preclude definitive conclusions.

The development of OB after ASCT was associated with deeper hematologic responses and showed a trend toward longer PFS. OB emergence is well described in multiple myeloma after effective therapy and is generally interpreted as a marker of immune reconstitution rather than residual disease [27, 28]. Similar findings have been reported in AL amyloidosis, another low–tumor burden plasma cell disorder treated with ASCT [29]. These observations suggest that OB in POEMS syndrome may reflect effective clonal suppression and immune recovery, although validation in larger cohorts is warranted.

The role of VEGF as a biomarker remains context dependent [3]. We did not observe a clear cross-sectional association between baseline VEGF levels and specific clinical manifestations; however, limited sample size and incomplete VEGF availability restrict firm conclusions. This aligns with prior data indicating that VEGF functions primarily as a dynamic marker of treatment response rather than a static surrogate of baseline severity [9, 30, 31]. In contrast, longitudinal assessment demonstrated marked VEGF decline across treatment phases, particularly following ASCT, paralleling hematologic and clinical improvement. Recent multicenter data suggest that persistently elevated VEGF early after ASCT may predict relapse risk [32], supporting its potential role as a marker of residual disease intensity. Nevertheless, variability in timing, thresholds, and assay standardization, together with the retrospective design, limit definitive conclusions regarding its independent prognostic value.

Although most patients achieved durable disease control, relapse occurred in a subset, typically after prolonged remission, reinforcing the chronic nature of POEMS syndrome [13, 25, 33]. Salvage strategies were heterogeneous and included IMiDs, PIs, alkylators, radiotherapy, and repeat ASCT [3, 34]. Our experience suggests that ASCT2 is feasible in selected patients, with successful mobilization, acceptable toxicity, and the ability to induce deep responses after long disease-free intervals. Although data on ASCT2 remain limited, available reports support its consideration in fit patients with late relapse [34].

ASCT toxicity in POEMS syndrome reflects both treatment effects and intrinsic disease vulnerabilities, particularly the propensity for fluid overload, thrombosis, and inflammatory complications [35–37]. The relatively high incidence of ES observed in our cohort (26.6%) is consistent with prior reports in POEMS syndrome and exceeds rates typically described in multiple myeloma series [22, 38]. This elevated susceptibility likely reflects the chronic overproduction of VEGF, IL-6, and TNF-α, which creates a pro-inflammatory and pro-permeability environment that persists into the peri-engraftment period and amplifies the capillary leak cascade underlying ES. Baseline extravascular volume overload, a frequent feature at POEMS diagnosis, may further compound this vulnerability during engraftment. The potential benefit of prophylactic strategies to mitigate ES after ASCT has been demonstrated in retrospective studies, including G-CSF withdrawal combined with oral corticosteroid prophylaxis [39], oral budesonide [40], and short-course dexamethasone at engraftment [41]. Whether these approaches are equally effective in POEMS syndrome, where the pro-inflammatory substrate differs from that of myeloma, warrants prospective evaluation. In our cohort, infections, and fluid overload were frequent but manageable, and TRM remained within ranges reported in contemporary series [13, 16, 21, 22, 24, 25, 35, 36]. Importantly, the prolonged survival of these patients underscores the relevance of late complications. The incidence of second primary neoplasms observed in our cohort highlights the need for long-term surveillance, although the relative contributions of prior therapy, transplantation, and immune dysregulation cannot be disentangled in retrospective analyses [37]. Notably, all three patients who developed hematologic SPNs had exposure to agents with recognized leukemogenic or lymphomogenic potential beyond the initial conditioning regimen: two had received cyclophosphamide-containing regimens either as induction or salvage therapy, and one had been treated with lenalidomide-based salvage therapy followed by a second melphalan-based ASCT, representing cumulative exposure to both an immunomodulatory agent and repeated high-dose melphalan. While causality cannot be established retrospectively, these findings are consistent with the known leukemogenic risk of alkylating agents and immunomodulatory drugs in plasma cell disorders [42, 43]. The long latency periods observed (73–134 months from first ASCT) further highlight the need for sustained long-term surveillance in POEMS syndrome survivors, particularly in those who have received cumulative cytotoxic therapy.

Baseline performance status and age were associated with inferior survival outcomes, underscoring the importance of functional reserve in this multisystem disorder. We did not observe significant improvement in PFS according to year of diagnosis, suggesting that depth of response remains the principal determinant of long-term outcome. Emerging therapies targeting plasma cell biology, including anti-CD38 antibodies and BCMA-directed strategies such as bispecific antibodies and CAR T-cell therapy, have shown promising activity in isolated reports of relapsed POEMS syndrome [44–47]. Prospective studies are needed to define their role.

This study is limited by its retrospective design, modest sample size, and heterogeneity in assessment and treatment strategies. VEGF measurements were not uniformly available, particularly in earlier years, restricting biomarker analyses. Cytogenetic, FISH and MRD data were not systematically collected, reflecting the characteristically low plasma cell burden in POEMS syndrome and the lack of standardized assessment across centers. Complete data on reasons for transplant ineligibility were not available for all non-transplanted patients.

In conclusion, our findings support ASCT as a central therapeutic strategy in eligible patients with POEMS syndrome and emphasize the importance of depth of response and longitudinal VEGF assessment. POEMS syndrome should be regarded as a chronic plasma cell disorder requiring individualized management and sustained follow-up in specialized centers.

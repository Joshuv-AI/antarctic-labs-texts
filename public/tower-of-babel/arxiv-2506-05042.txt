Geometric Singularities of Feynman Integrals
Martin Helmer, Felix Tellander

Abstract
We provide a new method to calculate the full microlocal description of singularities of Feynman integrals. This is done by associating a unique constructible function to the system of partial differential equations (PDEs) annihilating the integral and from this function the singularities can directly be read-off. This function can be constructed explicitly even if the system of PDEs is unknown and describes both the location of the singularities and the number of master integrals on them. Our framework is flexible enough to preform the calculation in any of the Lee-Pomeransky, Feynman, or momentum representations.



I
Introduction

Feynman integrals are an essential ingredient for calculations of observables in quantum field theory. Today their utility reaches far beyond their origin in high-energy physics and they are used for e.g. calculations of gravitational waves and critical exponents in statistical field theory. Mathematically they strike the perfect balance of being concrete enough to allow for many explicit calculations to be carried out and general enough to have a rich and varied mathematical structure
Weinzierl:2022eaz
.

The most basic invariant of any object is its singularities. For Feynman integrals this is a classical story
Landau:1959fi
; culminating in the
S
-matrix program of the 60’s
Eden:1966dnq
;
Nakanishi1971
. Today there is vast renewed interest in studying these singularities
Brown:2009ta
;
Pham2011integrals
;
Mizera:2021icv
;
Klausen:2021yrt
;
Caron-Huot:2024brh
;
Fevola:2023short
;
Helmer:2024wax
;
Hannesdottir:2024hke
;
Correia:2025yao
;
Matsubara-Heo:2025lrq
, with much focus placed on mathematical rigor and computability. In this paper we provide a new and simple way to describe these singularities as a constructible function. The pairing between a Feynman integral and this constructible function is not only rigorous but also explicitly computable on a computer.

The main results in the present paper are based on our recent article
Helmer:2025yuf
and are given here without proof. Describing the necessary
D
-module theory for a rigorous proof of this construction distracts from its simplicity and is given in
Helmer:2025yuf

The paper is arranged as follows. We first provide some background on Whitney stratifications and related geometric objects. We then define constructible functions and characteristic cycles and show how they can be calculated for Feynman integrals. Our method is then applied to different representations of the Feynman integral.

II
Geometric Preliminaries

Before we define the main players in this paper; constructible functions and characteristic cycles, we introduce the objects necessary to both describe and calculate them. In the following, let
Y
be a subvariety of dimension
d_{Y}
in a manifold
X
. In this paper we take
X=\mathbb{C}^{n}
.

Whitney Stratification.
A fundamental tool for the study of singular spaces is a Whitney stratification. For a variety
Y
this stratification divides it into smooth pieces which join together in a particularly desirable way. This allows for predictable behaviors of limiting tangent and secant lines between these pieces. More precisely, a
Whitney stratification
of
Y
is a subdivision into a finite number of smooth disjoint connected manifolds
S_{\alpha}
, called
strata
, such that
Y=\sqcup_{\alpha}{S_{\alpha}}
and such that
Whitney’s condition
B holds for all pairs
M=S_{\alpha}
,
N=S_{\beta}
, of these manifolds. A pair of strata
(M,N)
,
M\subseteq\overline{N}
, satisfy Whitney’s condition B
(
Whitney1965
, Section 19)
at a point
x\in M
if for every sequence
\{p_{\ell}\}\subset M
and
\{q_{\ell}\}\subset N
with
\lim p_{\ell}=\lim q_{\ell}=x
,
the limit of secant lines between
p_{\ell},q_{\ell}
is contained in the limit of tangent planes to
N
at
q_{\ell}
. When computing Whitney stratifications, and representing them on a computer, it is best to represent them as a flag
Y_{\bullet}
of varieties
Y_{0}\subset\cdots\subset Y_{d_{Y}}=Y
where the strata are the connected components of the successive differences
Y_{i}-Y_{i-1}
.

Conormal Variety.
Given a variety
Y
in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
we may associate to it a conormal variety,
{\rm\bf Con}(Y)
, in
\mathbb{C}^{n}\times\mathbb{P}^{n-1}
which catalogs all tangent planes and limiting tangent planes to each point
y
in
Y
, in particular we also include singular points of
Y
(in which case the planes cataloged are all limiting tangent planes). A point
(y,\eta)
in
{\rm\bf Con}(Y)
indicates that there is a tangent plane or limiting tangent plane to
Y
at the point
y
with normal vector
\eta
. More precisely, let
Y_{\rm Sing}
denote the singular locus of
Y
and let
Y_{\rm reg}=Y-Y_{\rm Sing}
denote the (open) manifold of smooth points in
Y
. The
conormal variety
is the subvariety in
Y\times\mathbb{P}^{m-1}
given by

\displaystyle{\rm\bf Con}(Y):=\overline{\left\{(y,\eta)\;|\;y\in Y_{\rm reg}\text{ and }T_{y}Y_{\rm reg}\subset\eta^{\perp}\right\}}.

Polar Variety.
While the conormal variety above catalogs all the tangents and limiting tangents to a variety, it also requires us to work in a larger ambient space. In many cases it is enough to understand which points in
Y
have prescribed directions tangent to them, and in particular, to understand which points in
Y
have tangent directions contained in a generically chosen linear space of some dimension. The object which catalogs these points in
Y
with prescribed tangent directions is called a polar variety. Consider a flag
L_{\bullet}
of length
d_{Y}+1
;
L_{\bullet}=\left(L_{d_{Y}+1}\supset L_{d_{Y}}\supset\cdots\supset L_{1}\right)
, where each
L_{i}\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
is an
i
-dimensional linear subspace. For each
i
in
{\left\{{0,1,\ldots,d_{Y}}\right\}}
, the dimension-
i

polar variety
of
Y
along the flag
L_{\bullet}
is defined as the closure

P_{i}(Y;\,L_{\bullet}):=\overline{{\left\{{y\in Y_{\text{reg}}\mid\dim(T_{y}Y_{\text{reg}}\cap L_{i+1}^{\perp})>d_{Y}-i-1}\right\}}}.

Note that
P_{d_{Y}}(Y;\,L_{\bullet})=Y
.

Euler Obstruction.
For a Whitney stratification
Y=\sqcup S_{\alpha}
, we define the
Euler obstruction
of
\overline{S}_{\gamma}
along
S_{\alpha}
, denoted
\mathbf{Eu}_{\overline{S}_{\gamma}}(S_{\alpha})
, recursively as follows. We define
\mathbf{Eu}_{\overline{S}_{\alpha}}(S_{\alpha})=1
and for
S_{\alpha}\nsubseteq\overline{S}_{\gamma}
we put
\mathbf{Eu}_{\overline{S}_{\gamma}}(S_{\alpha})=0
. The remaining recursion is given by

\begin{split}\mathbf{Eu}_{\overline{S}_{\gamma}}(S_{\alpha})&=\sum_{S\alpha\prec S_{\beta}\preceq S_{\gamma}}\mathbf{Eu}_{\overline{S}_{\gamma}}(S_{\beta})c(S_{\alpha},\,S_{\beta}),\\
c(S_{\alpha},\,S_{\beta})&=\chi(B(x,\epsilon)\cap S_{\beta}\cap H_{\eta}),\end{split}

(1)

where
c(S_{\alpha},\,S_{\beta})
is the topological Euler characteristic of a ball centered at
x\in S_{\alpha}
with radius
\epsilon
intersected with
S_{\beta}
intersected with a generic linear affine space of codimension
\mathrm{dim}(S_{\alpha})+1
at a distance
\eta
from
x
, where
\epsilon>>\eta>0
.

All the above constructions are closely related. Both conormal and polar varieties allow for algorithms to compute Whitney stratifications, see
hnFOCM
resp.
helmer2023effective
. In fact, it has been shown by Teissier
teissier1981varietes
that two strata
(M,N),\ M\subseteq\overline{N}
satisfy Whitney’s condition B if and only if the sequence of Hilbert-Samuel multiplicities

\displaystyle\mathrm{mult}_{\bullet}(\overline{N},z)=

\displaystyle\{\mathrm{mult}_{z}(P_{0}(\overline{N};\,L_{\bullet})),\ldots,\mathrm{mult}_{z}(P_{d-1}(\overline{N};\,L_{\bullet})),\mathrm{mult}_{z}(\overline{N})\}

is constant for every
z\in M
. Given a Whitney stratification
\sqcup S_{\alpha}
of
Y
, the above multiplicities are constant on strata and can therefore be indexed by the strata instead of
z
; that is for
z\in S_{\alpha}
we write
\mathrm{mult}_{\alpha}(P_{k}(\overline{S}_{\beta}))
for the constant value of
\mathrm{mult}_{z}(P_{k}(\overline{S}_{\beta}))
. We can now use the multiplicities above to calculate the Euler obstruction
BMM1994
:

\mathbf{Eu}_{\overline{S}_{\beta}}(S_{\alpha})=\sum_{k=d_{\alpha}+1}^{d_{\beta}}(-1)^{d_{\beta}-k}\mathrm{mult}_{\alpha}(P_{k}(\overline{S}_{\beta};\,L_{\bullet})).

(2)

III
Constructible Functions and Characteristic Cycles

A
constructible function

\alpha
on
X
is a function of the form
\alpha(x)=\sum_{j}a_{j}\mathbbm{1}_{Y_{j}}(x)
where
Y_{j}\subset X
are subvarieties,
\mathbbm{1}_{Y_{j}}
denotes the characteristic function (which equals
1
for points in
Y_{j}
and
0
otherwise) and
a_{j}
are integers. The Euler obstructions (
1
) form another basis for the constructible functions, this is easily seen since
\mathbf{Eu}_{Y_{j}}(\cdot)
generically equals one.

Let
Q=\sum_{\beta}a_{\beta}(x)\partial^{\beta}
denote a differential operator in local coordinates on
X
of degree
m
where
\beta
is a multi-index. Then we define the
principal symbol
to be
\sigma(Q)(x,\xi)=\sum_{|\beta|=m}a_{\beta}(x)\xi^{\beta}
. For a system of PDEs
\mathcal{M}
on
X
given by
Q_{1}u=\cdots=Q_{r}u=0
the
characteristic variety
is given by

\displaystyle\mathrm{Char}(\mathcal{M})=

\displaystyle\{(x,\xi)\in X\times\mathbb{P}^{n-1}\,|\,\sigma(Q)(x,\xi)=0\ \forall\ Q\in D_{X}\left<Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{r}\right>\}

where
D_{X}\left<Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{r}\right>
denotes the left-ideal of differential operators in the Weyl algebra
D_{X}
generated by
Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{r}
. If
\mathrm{Char}(\mathcal{M})=\cup\Lambda_{\alpha}
denotes the irreducible decomposition of
\mathrm{Char}(\mathcal{M})
, then every component is of the form
\Lambda_{\alpha}=\mathbf{Con}(Y_{\alpha})
for some subvariety
Y_{\alpha}\subset X
. The unique
characteristic cycle

CC(\mathcal{M})
is now given by the formal sum
\sum_{\alpha}m_{\alpha}\mathbf{Con}(\overline{S}_{\alpha}),\ m_{\alpha}\in\mathbb{Z}
. This is an example of a
conical Lagrangian cycle

kashiwara-schapira1
.

There is a simple isomorphism between the groups (or rather between the functors) of constructible functions and conical Lagrangian cycles:

\mathbf{Eu}_{Y_{\alpha}}(\cdot)\simeq(-1)^{d_{X}-d_{\alpha}}\mathbf{Con}(Y_{\alpha})

(3)

where
d_{X}=\dim(X)
and
d_{\alpha}=\dim(Y_{\alpha})
. For a hypersurface
Y\subset X
with Whitney stratification
\sqcup S_{\alpha}
we get explicitly

\begin{split}\mathbbm{1}_{Y}(y)&=\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha}(-1)^{d_{Y}-d_{\alpha}}m_{\alpha}\mathbf{Eu}_{\overline{S}_{\alpha}}(y)\quad\simeq\\
CC(\mathbbm{1}_{Y})&=-\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha}m_{\alpha}\mathbf{Con}(\overline{S}_{\alpha}).\end{split}

(4)

It follows from Kashiwara’s index theorem
Kashiwara1973
that for a system of PDEs there exists a constructible function with the same characteristic cycle.

IV
Geometric Singularities of Integrals

We take the Lee-Pomeransky
Lee2013
form as our representation of Feynman integrals when describing our construction;

{}\mathcal{I}=\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{x_{i}^{\nu_{i}}\,dx_{i}}{x_{i}\Gamma(\nu_{i})}\right)\,\frac{1}{(\mathcal{U}(x)+\mathcal{F}(x,z))^{D/2}}.

(5)

However, as we will see in the next section this framework is flexible enough to deal with other representations as well.

To get all singularities we lift the integration variables
x
to projective space using the Cheng-Wu theorem
Weinzierl:2022eaz
. Let
Y\subset\mathbb{P}_{x}^{n}\times\mathbb{C}_{z}^{m}
be the hypersurface defined by the vanishing of the homogenized integrand
x_{0}\cdots x_{n}(\mathcal{U}x_{0}+\mathcal{F})=0
. As every factor in the integrand is raised to a parametric power, all of
Y
is the singular locus for the system of PDEs annihilating the integrand. The rank of this system is one since the function defining
Y
spans the solution space. So the constructible function that reproduces this system’s characteristic variety is
\mathbbm{1}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}\times\mathbb{C}^{m}}-\mathbbm{1}_{Y}
. Expanding this we find

(\mathbbm{1}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}\times\mathbb{C}^{m}}-\mathbbm{1}_{Y})(y)=1-\sum_{\alpha}(-1)^{d_{Y}-d_{\alpha}}m_{\alpha}\mathbf{Eu}_{\overline{S}_{\alpha}}(y)

(6)

where
\sqcup S_{\alpha}
is a Whitney stratification of
Y
. We note that we have all information necessary to calculate the integers
m_{\alpha}
. This is done by the following steps:

(i)

Calculate a Whitney stratification of
Y
.

(ii)

Calculate the Euler obstructions using polar varieties (
2
).

(iii)

Treat (
6
) as a linear system and solve for the
m_{\alpha}
.

If
\mathcal{M}
denotes the system of PDEs annihilating the integrand, then
\int\mathcal{M}
denotes the system annihilating the integral. For the characteristic variety we now have

\small\mathrm{Char}(\mathcal{M})=\mathbf{Con}(0)\cup(\cup_{i}\mathbf{Con}(\overline{S}_{\alpha_{i}}))\subset\mathbb{P}_{x}^{n}\times\mathbb{C}_{z}^{m}\times\mathbb{P}_{\xi}^{n}\times\mathbb{P}_{\zeta}^{m-1}

where
\alpha_{i}
are the indices with non-zero
m_{\alpha_{i}}
in (
6
). Let
I_{\mathrm{Char}(\mathcal{M})}
be an ideal in
\mathbb{C}[x,z,\xi,\zeta]
defining
\mathrm{Char}(\mathcal{M})
, then we define the elimination ideal

I_{\mathrm{Char}(\int\mathcal{M})}=(I_{\mathrm{Char}(\mathcal{M})}+\left<\xi_{0},\ldots,\xi_{n}\right>)\cap\mathbb{C}[z,\zeta]

(7)

whose variety is precisely the characteristic variety
\mathrm{Char}(\int\mathcal{M})
, see
Helmer:2025yuf
and references therein.

We think of integration as being represented by the canonical projection map
\varphi:\mathbb{P}_{x}^{n}\times\mathbb{C}_{z}^{m}\to\mathbb{C}_{z}^{m}
. The functorial properties of characteristic cycles and constructible functions means that we can obtain the constructible function associated to
\int\mathcal{M}
from
\mathcal{M}
by the proper push-forward:

\varphi_{*}(\mathbbm{1}_{V})(z)=\chi(\varphi^{-1}(z)\cap V)

(8)

for any variety
V\subset\mathbb{P}^{n}\times\mathbb{C}^{m}
. Note that this assignment is unique
MacPherson1974
. Specifically we obtain

\varphi_{*}(\mathbbm{1}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}\times\mathbb{C}^{m}}-\mathbbm{1}_{Y})(z)=\chi((\mathbb{P}^{n}\times\mathbb{C}^{m})\cap\varphi^{-1}(z))-\chi(Y\cap\varphi^{-1}(z))=n+1-\chi(Y\cap\varphi^{-1}(z))

(9)

and we denote this function in (
9
) as
\chi_{\int\mathcal{M}}(z)
. This is a constructible function on
\mathbb{C}_{z}^{m}
meaning that it has a decomposition into Euler obstructions. The singular locus
Z
of
\int\mathcal{M}
, i.e. the
Landau singularities
of the integral, is given by
Z=\overline{\pi_{z}(\mathrm{Char}^{*}(\int\mathcal{M}))}
where
\mathrm{Char}^{*}(\cdot)
is the characteristic variety with the zero-section
\mathbf{Con}(0)
removed and
\pi_{z}
the canonical projection map
\mathbb{C}_{z}^{m}\times\mathbb{P}_{\zeta}^{m-1}\to\mathbb{C}_{z}^{m}
. Given a Whitney stratification
\sqcup W_{\alpha}
of
Z
, the constructible function is written as

\begin{split}\chi_{\int\mathcal{M}}(z)&=n+1-\chi(Y\cap\varphi^{-1}(z))\\
&=\mu_{0}-\sum_{\alpha}(-1)^{d_{Z}-d_{\alpha}}\mu_{\alpha}\mathbf{Eu}_{\overline{W}_{\alpha}}(z)\end{split}

(10)

where
d_{Z}=\dim(Z)
,
d_{\alpha}=\dim(W_{\alpha})
, and
\mu_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}
. The Euler characteristic
\chi(Y\cap\varphi^{-1}(z))
is constant for
z\in W_{\alpha}
, hence this number is readily calculable using e.g.
helmer2016proj
. We therefore have all information needed to calculate the multiplicities
\mu_{\alpha}
explicitly following the steps:

(iv)

Calculate the singular locus
Z
as the variety of the ideal
(I_{\mathrm{Char}(\int\mathcal{M})}:\left<\zeta_{1},\ldots,\zeta_{m}\right>^{\infty})\cap\mathbb{C}[z]
. This variety
Z
is also the Landau singularities.

(v)

Whitney stratify
Z
and calculate the Euler obstructions using polar varieties (
2
).

(vi)

Calculate the Euler characteristic
\chi(Y\cap\varphi^{-1}(z))
for a
z
in each strata
W_{\alpha}
and for a
z\in\mathbb{C}_{z}^{m}-Z
, the latter equals
\mu_{0}
.

(vii)

Treat (
10
) as a linear system and solve for the
\mu_{\alpha}
.

Let
\alpha_{i}
be the indices such that
\mu_{\alpha_{i}}\neq 0
, then we have the characteristic cycle

CC(\int\mathcal{M})=\mu_{0}\mathbf{Con}(0)+\sum_{i}\mu_{\alpha_{i}}\mathbf{Con}(\overline{W}_{\alpha_{i}}).

As we can determine the characteristic cycle directly from (
10
) and can write down this function for any Feynman integral, this function constitutes our main result. This function encodes significantly more than just the Landau singularities. First, the description of the singularities is in the cotangent bundle of
\mathbb{C}_{z}^{m}
so we get an upper bound on the set where solutions to the system
\int\mathcal{M}
fail to be microanalytic. Second,
\mu_{0}
is the number of master integrals, i.e. the dimension of the solution space. In fact,
\chi_{\int\mathcal{M}}(z)
is the dimension of the solution space at every
z
so we also obtain the number of master integrals on singularities.

V
One Diagram, Three Integrals, Same Singularities

To illustrate the method just described we calculate the singularities of the one-loop bubble using three different representations of the Feynman integral. Instead of using
z
we denote the kinematic invariants as
\{p^{2},\,m_{1}^{2},\,m_{2}^{2}\}\in\mathbb{C}^{3}
.
{x_{1},\,m_{1}}
{x_{2},\,m_{2}}

Lee-Pomeransky.
In the Lee-Pomeransky representation we first have to homogenize the integrand. We have
Y=\mathbf{V}(x_{0}x_{1}x_{2}(\mathcal{U}x_{0}+\mathcal{F}))\subset\mathbb{P}_{x}^{2}\times\mathbb{C}^{3}
with the Symanzik polynomials
\mathcal{U}=x_{1}+x_{2}
and
\mathcal{F}=(m_{1}^{2}+m_{2}^{2}-p^{2})x_{1}x_{2}+m_{1}^{2}x_{1}^{2}+m_{2}^{2}x_{2}^{2}
. There are 24 strata in the minimal Whitney stratification of this surface and the seven strata of dimension zero and one appear with multiplicity zero in the characteristic cycle. Using (
7
) and eliminating the conormal variables we find the Landau singularities
Z=\mathbf{V}(p^{2}m_{1}^{2}m_{2}^{2}((m_{1}^{2}+m_{2}^{2}-p^{2})^{2}-4m_{1}^{2}m_{2}^{2}))
. The minimal Whitney stratification of this variety contains eleven strata where only the four codimension-one strata appear with non-zero multiplicity after solving (
10
). Let
\lambda=\lambda(a,b,c)=(a+b-c)^{2}-4ab
be the Källén function. The constructible function and characteristic cycle of the bubble integral is:

\small\begin{split}\chi_{\int\mathcal{M}}(z)=3&-\mathbf{Eu}_{\mathbf{V}(p^{2})}(z)-\mathbf{Eu}_{\mathbf{V}(m_{1}^{2})}(z)\\
&-\mathbf{Eu}_{\mathbf{V}(m_{2}^{2})}(z)-(z)\mathbf{Eu}_{\mathbf{V}(\lambda)}(z)\\
CC(\int\mathcal{M})=\;&3\mathbf{Con}(0)+\mathbf{Con}(\mathbf{V}(p^{2}))+\mathbf{Con}(\mathbf{V}(m_{1}^{2}))\\
&+\mathbf{Con}(\mathbf{V}(m_{2}^{2}))+\mathbf{Con}(\mathbf{V}(\lambda)).\end{split}

(11)

This shows that the bubble generically has three master integrals and that the number drops by one on each singularity.

Feynman.
In the Feynman representation the integrand is already homogenized and given by the product of the Symanzik polynomials. For the bubble we have
Y=\mathbf{V}(x_{1}x_{2}\mathcal{U}\mathcal{F})\subset\mathbb{P}_{x}^{1}\times\mathbb{C}_{z}^{3}
. The Whitney stratification of
Y
consists of 19 strata. All strata of dimension zero and one (in total seven strata) have multiplicity zero. The remaining strata have multiplicty one except
\mathbf{V}(x_{1},x_{2})
which has multiplicity four. Using (
7
) and eliminating the conormal variables we find the singular locus
Z=\mathbf{V}(p^{2}m_{1}^{2}m_{2}^{2}((m_{1}^{2}+m_{2}^{2}-p^{2})^{2}-4m_{1}^{2}m_{2}^{2}))
, the same as in the Lee-Pomeransky representation.

Momentum.
The integrand in momentum representation is a product of propagators
q_{i}^{2}-m_{i}^{2}
where
q_{i}
is a linear combination of internal and external momenta. This is not homogeneous in the loop momenta, i.e. the integration variables, which will be denoted
k_{i}
(with the homogenization variable denoted
k_{h}
) below. In momentum representation the number of integration variables and kinematic parameters depend on the intended number of space-time dimensions. For computational simplicity we assume that
m_{1}=m_{2}=m
. In two dimensions we have
Y_{2D}=\mathbf{V}(k_{h}((k_{0}^{2}-k_{1}^{2})-k_{h}^{2}m^{2})((k_{0}+k_{h}p_{0})^{2}-(k_{1}+k_{h}p_{1})^{2})-k_{h}^{2}m^{2})\subset\mathbb{P}_{k}^{2}\times\mathbb{C}^{3}
while in three dimensions we have
Y_{3D}\subset\mathbb{P}_{k}^{3}\times\mathbb{C}^{4}
and similarly for higher dimensions. There are 18 strata in the minimal Whitney stratification of
Y_{2D}
and the singular locus is
Z=\mathbf{V}(m^{2}(p_{0}^{2}-p_{1}^{2})(p_{0}^{2}-p_{1}^{2}-4m^{2}))
which is the same as in the other representations with
m_{1}=m_{2}
.

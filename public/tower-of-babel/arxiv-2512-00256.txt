A Kernel Approach to the Stinespring and Kraus Representations
James Tian

Abstract
. We give a self-contained derivation of the Stinespring and Kraus structure theorems for completely positive maps using only scalar positive-definite kernels.



2000 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary: 46E22; Secondary: 47A20, 47L25,

1.
Introduction

Completely positive (CP) maps describe a fundamental class of maps
between algebras of bounded operators, specifically those that preserve
positivity at every matrix level. Two classical theorems capture their
structure: Stinespring’s dilation theorem, which realizes any CP map
as a compression of a representation on a larger Hilbert space, and
Kraus’s theorem, which expresses the same map as a finite or countable
sum of conjugations by bounded operators (see, e.g.,
[
Sti55
,
Kra71
,
Pau02
]
).
These results, although standard, are often proved using heavy machinery,
including
C^{*}
-algebra representation theory, the Gelfand-Naimark-Segal
construction, or matrix algebra arguments.

This paper shows that both theorems follow directly from an elementary
geometric idea involving two scalar positive definite kernels. The
approach proceeds in two stages:

1. From a CP map, we define a scalar kernel whose positivity follows
directly from complete positivity. The associated reproducing kernel
Hilbert space automatically contains the data of the Stinespring dilation,
including the dilation space and the
*
-representation.

2. A second kernel, built from the matrix units of the underlying
Hilbert space, reveals a canonical unitary identification of the dilation
space with a concrete tensor product. Under this identification, the
abstract Stinespring form becomes the familiar tensor-product expression,
and the Kraus operators appear immediately by expanding along an orthonormal
basis of the auxiliary space.

The argument requires only elementary facts about positive definite
kernels and Hilbert space geometry. It parallels familiar constructions
in classical analysis, such as the reproducing kernel of a Hilbert
space of functions or the Kolmogorov factorization of a covariance
function. In this way the kernel perspective clarifies the geometry
behind complete positivity: it shows how the dilation space is generated
by the “columns” of the map, how tensor structure emerges naturally,
and how the Kraus operators reflect orthogonal components of this
structure.

Throughout the paper, let
H
be a complex Hilbert space and
\mathscr{B}\left(H\right)

the space of bounded operators on
H
. All inner products are linear
in the second variable.

2.
The Stinespring Dilation

We begin by building the dilation space from a scalar kernel defined
directly by the CP map. The kernel will be shown to be positive definite
(p.d.), and its reproducing kernel Hilbert space (RKHS) will serve
as the dilation space.

Theorem 2.1

(Stinespring dilation)
.

Let
\varphi:\mathscr{B}\left(H\right)\rightarrow\mathscr{B}\left(H\right)

be a CP map. There exists a minimal Stinespring dilation
\left(\pi,H_{K},V\right)

such that

\varphi\left(S\right)=V^{*}\pi\left(S\right)V

where:

(1)

H_{K}
is the RKHS associated with the
\mathbb{C}
-valued kernel

K\left(\left(S,a\right),\left(T,b\right)\right):=\left\langle a,\varphi\left(S^{*}T\right)b\right\rangle.

(2)

V:H\rightarrow H_{K}
is the bounded linear operator,

Vb:=K_{\left(I,b\right)}=K\left(\cdot,\left(I,b\right)\right).

(3)

\pi:\mathscr{B}\left(H\right)\rightarrow\mathscr{B}\left(H_{K}\right)

is the unital
*
-representation defined on the dense span of sections
by

\pi\left(S\right)K_{\left(T,b\right)}:=K_{\left(ST,b\right)}.

Proof.

Complete positivity of
\varphi
ensures
K
is a scalar-valued
positive definite (p.d.) kernel on
\left(\mathscr{B}\left(H\right)\times H\right)\times\left(\mathscr{B}\left(H\right)\times H\right)
.
Indeed, for any finite family
\left\{\left(S_{i},a_{i}\right)\right\}^{n}_{i=1}

in
\mathscr{B}\left(H\right)\times H
, and
\left\{c_{i}\right\}^{n}_{i=1}

in
\mathbb{C}
, setting
b_{i}=c_{i}a_{i}
, then

\displaystyle\sum\nolimits_{i,j}\overline{c_{i}}c_{j}K\left(\left(S_{i},a_{i}\right),\left(S_{j},a_{j}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\sum\nolimits_{i,j}\overline{c_{i}}c_{j}\left\langle a_{i},\varphi\left(S^{*}_{i}S_{j}\right)a_{j}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum\nolimits_{i,j}\left\langle b_{i},\varphi\left(S^{*}_{i}S_{j}\right)b_{j}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle\left(b_{1},\dots,b_{n}\right),\left[\varphi\left(S^{*}_{i}S_{j}\right)\right]\left(b_{1},\dots,b_{n}\right)\right\rangle\geq 0

because the block matrix
\left[\varphi\left(S^{*}_{i}S_{j}\right)\right]

is positive in
M_{n}\left(\mathscr{B}\left(H\right)\right)
.

Let
H_{K}
be the corresponding RKHS with sections

K_{\left(T,b\right)}:=\left\langle\cdot,K\left(\cdot,T\right)b\right\rangle.

By definition,

H_{K}=\overline{span}\left\{K_{\left(T,b\right)}:T\in\mathscr{B}\left(H\right),\>b\in H\right\}.

In particular, it consists of complex-valued functions on
\mathscr{B}\left(H\right)\times H

with reproducing kernel
K
. See, e.g.,
[
Aro50
,
Ped58
,
Sza21
]
.

Define
V:H\rightarrow H_{K}
by
Vb=K_{\left(I,b\right)}
, and

\pi:\mathscr{B}\left(H\right)\rightarrow\mathscr{B}\left(H_{K}\right)

by
\pi\left(S\right)K_{\left(T,b\right)}=K_{\left(ST,b\right)}
.
To see
\pi\left(S\right)
is well defined and bounded, take a finite
sum
u=\sum_{k}c_{k}K_{\left(T_{k},b_{k}\right)}
. Then

\displaystyle\left\|\pi\left(S\right)u\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\Big\|\sum\nolimits_{k}c_{k}K_{\left(ST_{k},b_{k}\right)}\Big\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum\nolimits_{k,l}\overline{c_{k}}c_{l}K\left(\left(ST_{k},b_{k}\right),\left(ST_{l},b_{l}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\sum\nolimits_{k,l}\overline{c_{k}}c_{l}\left\langle b_{k},\varphi\left(T^{*}_{k}S^{*}ST_{l}\right)b_{l}\right\rangle.

Since
S^{*}S\leq\left\|S\right\|^{2}I
in
\mathscr{B}\left(H\right)

and the map
A\mapsto T^{*}_{k}AT_{k}
is positive, we have

\left[T^{*}_{k}S^{*}ST_{l}\right]\leq\left\|S\right\|^{2}\left[T^{*}_{k}T_{l}\right]

in
M_{n}\left(\mathscr{B}\left(H\right)\right)
.

Applying the CP map
id_{M_{n}}\otimes\varphi
and pairing with
\left(b_{k}\right)\in H^{\oplus n}

yields

\sum\nolimits_{k,l}\overline{c_{k}}c_{l}\left\langle b_{k},\varphi\left(T^{*}_{k}S^{*}ST_{l}\right)b_{l}\right\rangle\leq\left\|S\right\|^{2}\sum\nolimits_{k,l}\overline{c_{k}}c_{l}\left\langle b_{k},\varphi\left(T^{*}_{k}T_{l}\right)b_{l}\right\rangle=\left\|S\right\|^{2}\left\|u\right\|^{2}.

Hence
\left\|\pi\left(S\right)\right\|\leq\left\|S\right\|
.
One checks that

\pi\left(S_{1}\right)\pi\left(S_{2}\right)=\pi\left(S_{1}S_{2}\right)

on the dense set of sections. Since
K
is Hermitian,
\pi\left(S\right)^{*}=\pi\left(S^{*}\right)
.
Hence
\pi
is a unital
*
-representation of
\mathscr{B}\left(H\right)
.

For all
S\in\mathscr{B}\left(H\right)
and
a,b\in H
,

\left\langle a,\varphi\left(S\right)b\right\rangle=K\left(\left(I,a\right),\left(S,b\right)\right)=\left\langle Va,\pi\left(S\right)Vb\right\rangle_{H\_\{K\}},

that is,
\varphi\left(S\right)=V^{*}\pi\left(S\right)V
.

Finally, the dilation is minimal by construction, as the spanning
set for
H_{K}
is precisely
\left\{K_{(S,a)}\right\}=\left\{\pi\left(S\right)Va\right\}
.
∎

3.
The Kraus Representation

The dilation obtained in the previous section is still abstract: it
lives inside the reproducing kernel Hilbert space generated by the
first kernel. Our next goal is to uncover the concrete tensor product
structure hidden inside that space. This structure is responsible
for the familiar form of a completely positive map as a sum of operator
conjugations.

To reveal it, we introduce a second scalar positive definite kernel,
built from the matrix units of the underlying Hilbert space. This
auxiliary kernel generates a new reproducing kernel Hilbert space
which plays the role of the “ancillary” tensor factor.

A simple computation then shows that the original dilation space
H_{K}

is unitarily equivalent to an honest tensor product of Hilbert spaces.
Once this identification is made, the Kraus representation follows
immediately by expanding the dilation operator along an orthonormal
basis of the auxiliary space.

Theorem 3.1

(canonical tensor structure)
.

Let
\left(\pi,H_{K},V\right)
be the minimal Stinespring dilation
from Theorem
2.1
. Fix an orthonormal basis (ONB)
\left\{e_{i}\right\}

in
H
, and let
E_{ij}=\left|e_{i}\left\rangle\right\langle e_{j}\right|
.
Let
H_{\hat{K}}
be the RKHS of the kernel

\hat{K}\left(\left(i,a\right),\left(j,b\right)\right):=\left\langle a,\varphi\left(E_{ij}\right)b\right\rangle.

Then the Stinespring space
H_{K}
is canonically unitary to
H\otimes H_{\hat{K}}

via an isomorphism
U
that identifies:

(1)

The representation
\pi\left(S\right)
with
S\otimes I_{H_{\hat{K}}}
.

(2)

The operator
V
with
W:H\rightarrow H\otimes H_{\hat{K}}
, where

Wa:=\sum\nolimits_{i}e_{i}\otimes\hat{K}_{\left(i,a\right)}.

This identification transforms the abstract Stinespring form
\varphi(S)=V^{*}\pi(S)V

into the canonical tensor product form

\varphi(S)=W^{*}\left(S\otimes I_{H_{\hat{K}}}\right)W.

Furthermore, expanding
W
in an ONB
\{f_{\alpha}\}
of
H_{\hat{K}}

yields the Kraus form

\varphi\left(S\right)=\sum V^{*}_{\alpha}SV_{\alpha},

where

V_{\alpha}:=\left(I_{H}\otimes\left\langle f_{\alpha},\cdot\right\rangle\right)W.

Proof.

The proof proceeds in two steps. First, we establish the unitary isomorphism

U
. Second, we transport the representation
(\pi,V)
along
U

to derive the tensor product and Kraus forms. See the diagram below.

Step 1.
Fix an ONB
\left\{e_{i}\right\}_{i\in I}
in

H
, and let
E_{ij}=\left|e_{i}\left\rangle\right\langle e_{j}\right|
.
The block matrix
[\varphi(E_{ij})]
is positive by complete positivity,
so
\hat{K}
is a scalar-valued p.d. kernel defined on
\left(I\times H\right)\times\left(I\times H\right)
.
Let
H_{\hat{K}}
be the associated RKHS with sections
\hat{K}_{\left(i,a\right)}
,
so that

H_{\hat{K}}=\overline{span}\mathop{\{}\hat{K}_{\left(i,a\right)}:i\in I,\>a\in H\mathop{\}}.

We now relate the kernel
K
from Theorem
2.1
to this new
kernel
\hat{K}
. For any
\left(S,a\right)
and
\left(T,b\right)
,

\displaystyle K\left(\left(S,a\right),\left(T,b\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\left\langle a,\varphi\left(S^{*}T\right)b\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum\nolimits_{i,j}\left\langle e_{i},S^{*}Te_{j}\right\rangle\left\langle a,\varphi\left(E_{ij}\right)b\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum\nolimits_{i,j}\left\langle Se_{i},Te_{j}\right\rangle\left\langle\hat{K}_{\left(i,a\right)},\hat{K}_{\left(j,b\right)}\right\rangle_{H_{\hat{K}}}

\displaystyle=\left\langle\sum\nolimits_{i}Se_{i}\otimes\hat{K}_{\left(i,a\right)},\sum\nolimits_{j}Te_{j}\otimes\hat{K}_{\left(j,b\right)}\right\rangle_{H\otimes H_{\hat{K}}}.

This calculation shows that the linear map
U:H_{K}\rightarrow H\otimes H_{\hat{K}}
,
defined on the spanning set of sections by

U\left(K_{\left(T,b\right)}\right):=\sum\nolimits_{i}Te_{i}\otimes\hat{K}_{\left(i,b\right)}

is isometric, with dense range in
H\otimes H_{\hat{K}}
. Thus,
U

is unitary, and

H_{K}\simeq H\otimes H_{\hat{K}}.

Step 2.
We now transport the Stinespring representation
(\pi,V)

from
H_{K}
to
H\otimes H_{\hat{K}}
.

Using
U
, we get

\displaystyle U\pi\left(S\right)U^{*}\left(\sum\nolimits_{i}Te_{i}\otimes\hat{K}_{\left(i,b\right)}\right)

\displaystyle=U\left(\pi\left(S\right)K_{\left(T,b\right)}\right)

\displaystyle=U\left(K_{\left(ST,b\right)}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum\nolimits_{i}STe_{i}\otimes\hat{K}_{\left(i,b\right)}

\displaystyle=\left(S\otimes I\right)\left(\sum\nolimits_{i}Te_{i}\otimes\hat{K}_{\left(i,b\right)}\right).

So
\pi\left(S\right)
is unitarily equivalent to
S\otimes I_{H_{\hat{K}}}
.

Also, the operator
V
becomes
W:=UV
:

Wa:=U\left(Va\right)=U\left(K_{\left(I,a\right)}\right)=\sum\nolimits_{i}e_{i}\otimes\hat{K}_{\left(i,a\right)}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\varphi\left(S\right)

\displaystyle=V^{*}\pi\left(S\right)V=\left(UV\right)^{*}\left(U\pi\left(S\right)U^{*}\right)\left(UV\right)

\displaystyle=W^{*}\left(S\otimes I\right)W.

Lastly, let
\left\{f_{\alpha}\right\}_{\alpha\in\Lambda}
be an
ONB of
H_{\hat{K}}
. Define the operators
V_{\alpha}:H\rightarrow H

by

V_{\alpha}:=\left(I_{H}\otimes\left\langle f_{\alpha},\right\rangle_{H_{\hat{K}}}\right)W.

This means
V_{\alpha}a
is the vector component of
Wa
along
f_{\alpha}
,
so

Wa=\sum\nolimits_{\alpha}\left(V_{\alpha}a\right)\otimes f_{\alpha}.

This can be written as
W=\sum_{\alpha}V_{\alpha}\otimes f_{\alpha}
.
Substituting this into the tensor form gives:

\displaystyle\varphi\left(S\right)

\displaystyle=W^{*}\left(S\otimes I\right)W

\displaystyle=\left(\sum\nolimits_{\alpha}V^{*}_{\alpha}\otimes f_{\alpha}\right)\left(S\otimes I\right)\left(\sum\nolimits_{\beta}V_{\beta}\otimes f_{\beta}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum\nolimits_{\alpha,\beta}V^{*}_{\alpha}SV_{\beta}\left\langle f_{\alpha},f_{\beta}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum\nolimits_{\alpha}V^{*}_{\alpha}SV_{\alpha}.

The convergence is in the strong operator topology.
∎

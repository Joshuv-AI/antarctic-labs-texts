Predictive Value of D‐Dimer to Albumin Ratio for Outcome in Acute Type A Aortic Dissection Patients
Authors: Aijie Lin, Yanchun Peng, Jiayi Shao, Liangwan Chen, Yanjuan Lin
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTPreoperative risk assessment and identification of potential biomarkers may optimize prognostic outcomes in patients with aortic dissection patients. The relationship between the D‐dimer to albumin ratio (DAR), an emerging biomarker that has recently attracted increasing attention, and postoperative outcomes in patients with acute type A aortic dissection (AAAD) remains unclear. Therefore, this study seeks to validate the correlation between DAR and adverse outcomes after surgery in this population and to provide a reliable reference for postoperative risk assessment in clinical AAAD. Patients with AAAD treated at Fujian Medical University Union Hospital between July 2013 and December 2021 were admitted in this cross‐sectional retrospective study. To examine the link between DAR and poor prognosis in AAAD patients, logistic regression analysis was performed. The predictive capacity of the DAR was assessed through the receiver operating characteristic curve analysis. The entirety of 619 individuals were ultimately included, with 106 (17.12%) patients died within 30 days of surgery. The group with the highest DAR levels had the highest number of adverse outcomes, including postoperative mortality, adverse cardiovascular, and cerebrovascular events. DAR was independently associated with postoperative mortality within 30 days in patients with AAAD, as determined by multivariate regression analysis (OR: 3.604, 95% CI: 1.495–8.685, p = 0.004). DAR shows good predictive performance for postoperative mortality within 30 days in AAAD patients (AUC: 0.710, 95% CI: 0.656–0.764, P < 0.001). These findings suggest that DAR is significantly associated with 30‐day postoperative mortality in patients with AAAD and has good predictive value.Keywords: Acute type A aortic dissection, D‐dimer to albumin ratio, Mortality, Prognosis

Background

Aortic dissection (AD) is a life‐threatening condition in which a tear in the aortic intima allows blood to enter the aortic media, resulting in separation of the layers of the aortic wall [1]. Acute type A aortic dissection (AAAD) frequently presents with potentially fatal complications due to the engagement of critical structures, particularly the ascending aorta at the lesion site, resulting in intense discomfort and significant economic strain on patients and their families [2]. In the absence of prompt surgical intervention, mortality may rise by 1.0% to 2.0% per hour for the initial 24 h following the beginning of illness [3]. Nonetheless, the mortality remains at 18.0% even among patients who undergo surgery [4]. Proper and efficient risk stratification before diagnosis enhances the precision of both diagnosis and treatment [5]. Biomarkers are crucial for risk stratification, facilitating the optimization of treatment and care regimens while enhancing patient prognosis.

The development of AD is closely related to the coagulation function [6]. D‐dimer serves as a significant marker indicative of coagulation and fibrinolytic process activation, with the benefit of rapid detection [7]. The European Society of Cardiology guidelines indicate that elevated D‐dimer can be a key reference in the diagnostic assessment of AAAD [8]. Qiu et al. established that elevated levels of D‐dimer were strongly correlated with a greater likelihood of death in patients with AAAD [9]. Feng et al. demonstrated that elevated preoperative D‐dimer levels (> 14.50 mg/L) measured within 2 h before surgery were associated with a 1.68‐fold increased risk of postoperative complications (such as mortality, acute kidney injury (AKI), and gastrointestinal bleeding) in patients with AAAD [10]. The pathological mechanisms of AD are more complex, with studies confirming that the inflammatory response significantly contributes to poor prognosis in patients with AAAD [6]. Therefore, we hypothesized that the use of D‐dimer in combination with inflammation‐associated biomarkers could further enhance its risk‐predictive ability.

Recent studies have investigated the relationship between D‐dimer to albumin ratio (DAR), an emerging biomarker, and the prognosis of various diseases. A study that included 509 patients with acute exacerbations of chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) revealed that when the DAR was ≥ 2.21, patients had a 1.80‐fold increased risk of readmission at 1 year, demonstrating that the DAR may effectively identify patients with poor prognosis [11]. Xie et al. employed a combination of D‐dimer and a prognostic nutritional index containing albumin to predict postoperative complications in patients with AAAD who experienced emergency surgery within 48 h of the beginning of illness. Their findings indicate that this combination enhances predictive accuracy [12]. However, this is the only study of its kind, and with a sample size of only 153 cases, it is more difficult to comprehensively assess the efficacy of D‐dimer combined with albumin in predicting poor prognosis in patients with AAAD. The International Registry of Acute Aortic Dissection (IRAD) database also recommends that all AD with an onset of 7 days or less be classified as acute [13]. Consequently, based on this defining criterion, this study enlarged the sample size to include patients with AAAD within 7 days from onset to surgery. The aim was to validate further the association between DAR and adverse outcomes, including postoperative death within 30 days and negative cardiovascular and cerebrovascular events.

Methods

Study Design and Population

A cross‐sectional retrospective study was performed on patients diagnosed with AAAD who attended Fujian Medical University Union Hospital between July 2013 and December 2021. The inclusion criteria were as follows: (1) diagnosis of AAAD confirmed by imaging examination; (2) an interval from symptom onset to surgery of ≤ 7 days; (3) total aortic arch replacement or replacement of the ascending aorta and hemiarch combined with triple‐branched stent graft implantation, with or without concomitant proximal aortic, aortic valve, supra‐aortic branch reconstruction, or other aortic stent graft–related procedures [14, 15]; and (4) age ≥ 18 years. Exclusion criteria were (1) thyroid disorders, severe renal impairment, including end‐stage renal disease (estimated glomerular filtration rate [eGFR] < 15 mL/min/1.73 m2) or acute kidney injury, lower limb ischaemia, haematological disorders affecting albumin and D‐dimer levels, Marfan syndrome, severe infection, or malignancy; (2) pregnancy; (3) prolonged use of medications that may affect the results of the blood tests, such as antibiotics, hormonal drugs, anticoagulants, and so forth; and (4) preoperative albumin, D‐dimer in any one of the missing data. This study was conducted in accordance with the 1975 Declaration of Helsinki and was approved by the Ethics Committee of Fujian Medical University Union Hospital (No. 2020KY082).

Data Collection

Data collected in this study included baseline demographic characteristic (age, gender, body mass index [BMI], preoperative blood pressure, and history); surgical procedure components; operative data (time of surgery, time of cardiopulmonary bypass [CPB], and aortic cross‐clamp [ACC] time, intraoperative blood transfusion); preoperative laboratory and imaging parameters (left ventricular ejection fraction [LVEF], white blood cells [WBC], platelets [PLT], hemoglobin [Hb], serum creatinine [SCr], urea, albumin, lactic acid [LA], D‐dimer, N‐terminal B‐type natriuretic peptide precursor [NT‐proBNP], and international normalized ratio [INR]); and clinical outcomes (postoperative mortality within 30 days, complications, length of hospital stay, and intubation). The preoperative laboratory and imaging parameters included in this study are based on the results of the first tests conducted between the patient's admission and the time of surgery. If multiple preoperative tests have been carried out, the results of the first test shall be used for analysis. Demographic characteristics, operative data, and preoperative laboratory and imaging parameters were collected through review of the electronic medical record system. Clinical outcome data were collected through outpatient follow‐up, telephone follow‐up, and electronic medical record review, with follow‐up continuing until 30 days after surgery.

Clinical Outcomes

The primary outcome measured was mortality within 30 days of surgery, whereas the secondary outcome focused on postoperative major adverse cardiovascular and cerebrovascular events (MACCE), which encompassed new‐onset arrhythmias, cardiac insufficiency, acute pericardial tamponade, cerebral hemorrhage or hematoma, cerebral infarction, and transient ischemic attack. Secondary outcomes also incorporated AKI, need for continuous renal replacement therapy (CRRT), pleural effusion, ruptured aortic dissection, multiple organ dysfunction syndrome (MODS), gastrointestinal hemorrhage, reintubation or tracheotomy status, duration of mechanical ventilation, total length of hospital stay, intensive care unit (ICU) length of stay, readmission or readmission to ICU.

Relevant Standards and Definitions

AAAD is characterized by entrapment occurring within 7 days of onset, with the site of onset located in the ascending aorta or aortic arch [2]. Smoking history is categorized by more than one cigarette per day for a continuous or cumulative period of more than 6 months [16]. Alcohol consumption history is defined as drinking ≥ 1 drink per week and converting that drink to ≥ 50 mL of alcohol for more than 6 months [17]. Hypertension is defined as repeated in‐hospital blood pressure measurements with systolic blood pressure ≥ 140 mmHg and diastolic blood pressure ≥ 90 mmHg [18]. Diabetes mellitus diagnosis follows established criteria: asymptomatic individuals must exhibit two consecutive fasting plasma glucose ≥ 7.0 mmol/L (126 mg/dL) or 2 h post‐oral glucose load glucose levels ≥ 11.1 mmol/L (200 mg/dL) during standardized testing, or glycated hemoglobin ≥ 6.5%, or the presence of typical hyperglycemic symptoms or a hyperglycemic crisis with random glucose levels of 11.1 mmol/L [19]. MACCE is defined as the composite outcome of the first occurrence of any of the following events, including new‐onset arrhythmia, heart failure, acute pericardial tamponade, cerebral hemorrhage or hematoma, cerebral infarction, and transient ischemic attack. All of these clinical events were diagnosed by clinicians using ICD‐10 codes [20]. AKI is marked as an elevation in SCr of ≥ 0.3 mg/dL within 48 h, a rise in SCr of ≥ 1.5‐fold from baseline within 7 days, or a reduction in urine output to < 0.5 mL/kg/h within 6 h or more [21]. Gastrointestinal hemorrhage was defined as the presence of hematemesis, melena, hematochezia, positive occult blood tests in feces or gastric juice, or active bleeding identified on endoscopy [22]. MODS is characterized by a sequential organ failure assessment (SOFA) score of ≥ 6 sustained over two days in a row for a minimum of 48 h post‐admission [23]. DAR is calculated as D‐dimer (mg/L) / albumin (g/L).

Statistical Analysis

Baseline data are presented as mean ± standard deviation, median (interquartile range) or frequency (%). The normality of continuous variables was assessed using the Shapiro–Wilk test. For comparisons among the three groups, one‐way analysis of variance (ANOVA) was used for normally distributed continuous variables, the Kruskal–Wallis test for non‐normally distributed continuous variables, and the chi‐square test or Fisher's exact test for categorical variables. Univariate logistic regression analysis was used to preliminarily identify risk factors associated with mortality and MACCE within 30 days post‐surgery in patients with AAAD. Variables with a p‐value of < 0.05 in the univariate analysis were assessed for multicollinearity using the variance inflation factor (VIF); those with a VIF < 3 were included in the multivariate logistic regression analysis. The association between each factor and the outcome variable was assessed by calculating odds ratios (ORs) and 95% confidence intervals (CIs). To validate the relationship between DAR and adverse outcomes across different subgroups, subgroup analyses were conducted based on the logistic regression results. Comparison of the predictive ability of DAR and D‐dimer using receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis. The data were collated using Microsoft Excel 16.0 and analyzed using SPSS 27.0 (SPSS Inc., Chicago, IL, USA). The results were visualized using GraphPad Prism 8.3 (GraphPad Software, CA, USA) and R software (V4.4.3). A p‐value < 0.05 was adopted as the criterion for establishing statistical significance.

Comparison of Clinical Outcomes Among Different Dar Level Groups

Of the total study population, 106 patients died postoperatively (17.1%), 50 patients experienced MACCE postoperatively, with a median number of days in the hospital of 19.0 (14.0, 27.0), and 62 patients were readmitted (10.0%). According to Figure 2, the median level of preoperative DAR was higher in patients who died or experienced a MACCE postoperatively, which was significantly different from those who did not experience adverse outcomes (0.28 vs. 0.59, P < 0.001; 0.33 vs. 0.59, P < 0.001). Among the three groups of patients, Group 3 had the highest incidence of mortality within 30 days and MACCE, the most extended ICU stay, and the highest percentage of cases presenting with post‐extubation reintubation, MODS, AKI, gastrointestinal hemorrhage, and CRRT (P < 0.05). Association Between Dar and Postoperative Outcomes

In this study, variables with P <0.05 in the univariate logistic analysis were assessed for multicollinearity. The specific results are shown in Tables 3 and 4. Variables with a VIF value of less than 3 were included in the multivariate logistic regression analysis to further investigate the association between DAR and postoperative mortality within 30 days or MACCE in patients with AAAD. Of the three constructed models, Model I incorporated adjustments for age. The results showed that each 1‐unit increment in DAR was independently associated with an 8.12‐fold increased odd of postoperative mortality within 30 days (OR: 8.117, 95% CI: 3.581–18.402, P <0.001). Based on a conversion factor of 0.1 units, for every 0.1‐unit increase in DAR, the risk of death at 30 days post‐operation increases by approximately 23.3%. Model II further adjusted some of the variables related to surgical characteristics based on Model I. It was observed that the risk of postoperative death within 30 days still increased with increasing DAR (OR: 6.295, 95% CI: 2.683–14.772, P <0.05). Model III was supplemented with adjustments for variables related to preoperative laboratory markers. The association between DAR and postoperative mortality within 30 days remained consistent with that presented in the previous corrected model (OR: 3.604, 95% CI: 1.495–8.685, P = 0.004), but no association between changes in DAR levels and the risk of MACCE occurrence was observed in the results (OR: 2.326, 95% CI: 0.759–7.129, P = 0.140). The study also compared the risk of adverse outcomes with 30 days in patients in Group 2 (moderate DAR) and Group 3 (highest DAR), respectively, using Group 1, which had the lowest DAR level, as a reference. The results showed that, across all three models, the risk of death within 30 days of surgery for patients in Group 3 was more than three times that of patients in Group 1. However, no significant association was observed between changes in DAR levels and the occurrence of MACCE. (Table 5)

Discussion

AAAD is a life‐threatening cardiovascular disease whose pathogenesis is closely related to oxidative stress. Oxidative stress contributes to the formation of entrapment by interacting with pathological processes such as inflammatory response and hypercoagulation [24, 25, 26]. Currently, researchers are actively investigating biomarkers to identify individuals at high risk of adverse medical events and to develop more effective treatment strategies aimed at enhancing patient prognosis. Previous studies have shown that D‐dimer is one of the key biomarkers for assessing the prognosis of patients with AAAD and is closely associated with in‐hospital mortality [27]. Unlike previous studies that mainly focused on D‐dimer alone and mortality‐related outcomes, the present study further investigated the association of the DAR with 30‐day postoperative mortality and MACCE in 619 patients with AAAD who underwent surgical treatment. By including MACCE as a clinically relevant composite adverse outcome, this study has extended the scope of prognostic assessment of D‐dimer‐related indicators from mortality to postoperative cardiovascular and cerebrovascular adverse events. In addition, compared with D‐dimer alone, DAR showed higher sensitivity and specificity for predicting 30‐day postoperative mortality, suggesting that DAR may provide additional prognostic information beyond D‐dimer alone.

DAR consists of D‐dimer and albumin. Existing literature consistently demonstrates that elevated D‐dimer levels function as a validated prognostic indicator for adverse clinical outcomes in patients diagnosed with AAAD [9]. This correlation arises from the substantial impact of the severity of AD on the condition of pseudomonal thrombosis on D‐dimer metabolism [28]. Elevated tissue factor levels are released following aortic wall injury, leading to substantial coagulation activation and a marked increase in fibrinolysis. This pathophysiological process leads to increased serum D‐dimer levels in patients with AAAD [29]. The presence of D‐dimer not only indicates coagulation function but may also signify an imbalance between proinflammatory and anti‐inflammatory processes in the body. D‐dimer elevation reflects a correlation with the activation of proinflammatory factor cascade responses but not with anti‐inflammatory factors, such as Interleukin‐10 [30]. Elevated D‐dimer levels indicate abnormal coagulation and inflammatory condition in patients with AAAD, correlating with more severe lesions and an increased risk of adverse postoperative outcomes. Nonetheless, the clinical accuracy of prediction based on a single indicator requires enhancement. This study compares the efficacy of D‐dimer and DAR in predicting postoperative mortality within 30 days. The results indicated that the combination of DAR and albumin enhanced the accuracy of mortality prediction compared to predictions based solely on D‐dimer, aligning with the findings of Xie et al. [12] Notably, the discriminatory accuracy of D‐dimer for predicting postoperative mortality in this study (AUC = 0.684) demonstrated a lower predictive capacity compared to the previously reported AUC of 0.700 by Feng et al. [10]. The higher predictive power of D‐dimer in the previous study may be attributed to the use of composite endpoints, which encompassed various adverse events, including in‐hospital death, acute renal failure, and MODS.

Albumin is linked to inflammatory modulation and thrombosis. In physiological homeostasis, it is an anti‐inflammatory and antioxidant process, triggering platelet activation and preserving plasma colloid osmotic pressure [31]. Therefore, low albumin levels are considered a danger sign of poor prognosis. Gao et al. ’s analysis demonstrated that AAAD patients with concurrent hypoalbuminemia faced a 2.49‐fold elevated mortality risk compared to hypoalbuminemia [32]. A study involving 624 adult AAAD patients identified a non‐linear association between albumin levels and postoperative AKI. The findings indicated that lower serum albumin levels, specifically those below 32 g/L, correlated with an increased risk of AKI [33]. Lower albumin levels can elevate blood viscosity, leading to enhanced oxidation of low‐density lipoprotein cholesterol, increased foam cell formation, and promotion of an inflammatory state, ultimately worsening the prognosis for AD patients [34].

We investigated the association between DAR and adverse prognosis in patients with AAAD by evaluating the correlation between DAR with postoperative mortality within 30 days and MACCE through univariate and multivariate analyzes, respectively. The results showed that as DAR levels increased, the risk of postoperative mortality within 30 days also rose; however, no similar association was observed between DAR levels and the occurrence of MACCE. This may be because elevated D‐dimer levels indicate widespread endothelial damage and intravascular thrombus formation [28, 29]. Reduced albumin plays a role in pathological processes, including inflammatory response, oxidative stress, and volume imbalance. These systemic disturbances are directly related to the occurrence of multi‐organ failure and shock and are the major cause of mortality. MACCE, such as cardiac insufficiency, pericardial tamponade, cerebral hemorrhage, or infarction, on the other hand, are more often due to the site of accumulation of entrapment and altered hemodynamics. Consequently, DAR, as a systemic indicator, may be less sensitive in predicting such localized events. It is worth noting that previous studies have suggested that red blood cell transfusion and elevated NT‐proBNP levels are associated with an increased risk of adverse outcomes, including mortality [35, 36]. However, in the univariate analysis of the present study, red blood cell transfusion and NT‐proBNP > 1000 pg/mL showed a negative trend with MACCE, although these associations did not reach statistical significance. The reasons for this finding may be complex. On the one hand, although red blood cell transfusion may reflect greater intraoperative blood loss and surgical complexity, it is also a therapeutic intervention used to improve oxygen delivery and maintain haemodynamic stability [37]. In patients with AAAD after surgery, timely correction of anaemia and inadequate oxygen delivery may help maintain myocardial and cerebral perfusion, thereby potentially influencing the occurrence of MACCE. On the other hand, elevated NT‐proBNP levels usually indicate impaired cardiac function [38], and these patients may receive closer monitoring and more active clinical management, which may also affect the occurrence of adverse cardiovascular and cerebrovascular events to some extent. Therefore, larger studies are needed to further validate the associations of red blood cell transfusion and NT‐proBNP > 1000 pg/mL with MACCE.

In individualized healthcare, clarifying the applicability of various types of predictors in different patient groups is crucial for informed clinical decision‐making. In this study, subgroup analyses were conducted based on age, hypertension status and WBC count. The results showed that no significant interaction between DAR and the aforementioned stratification variables was observed with regard to the outcome of mortality at 30 days post‐operation. The association between DAR and postoperative mortality within 30 days appears to be fairly consistent across different patient subgroups. A possible explanation lies in the fact that DAR, comprising D‐dimer and albumin, comprehensively reflects systemic coagulation/fibrinolysis activation and inflammatory responses [28, 29, 30, 31]. As it better reflects an individual's pathophysiological status than a single indicator, its association with postoperative mortality is less susceptible to significant influence from individual clinical characteristics. A study by Li et al. [39]. similarly found that variables such as gender, age and hypertension did not influence the association between DAR and adverse cardiovascular events. However, as this was a single‐centre retrospective study with a relatively small sample size, the number of patients in the subgroups aged over 60 years, without hypertension, and with a WBC count <10 × 109/L was less than 200 in each case. This resulted in a low number of outcome events within some subgroups following stratification. Therefore, although no significant interaction effects were observed in this study, the possibility of potential interactions cannot be entirely ruled out.

Current studies aim to identify optimal biomarkers for predicting AAAD; however, many have not yet reached clinical application due to the complexity of the assays and the significant time and labor costs involved [40]. The development of assessment indicators that can be routinely tested is, therefore, of great clinical importance. D‐dimer, used for calculating the DAR, is the most prevalent and validated diagnostic marker for AAAD in contemporary clinical practice [41], while albumin serves as a significant indicator of the body's inflammatory state [31]. Both are easily accessible. Therefore, DAR is anticipated to serve as a reliable instrument for predicting postoperative mortality within 30 days and MACCE following AAAD and holds a reference value for guiding subsequent treatments, including supplemental nutrition, anti‐inflammatory therapy, and anticoagulation in patients. Nonetheless, this study still has a few limitations. First, only D‐dimer and albumin were collected from patients with AAAD before admission, making it challenging to capture the dynamic changes in DAR levels at a single point in time. Second, this study did not systematically document other conditions of impaired perfusion, such as intestinal ischaemia and coronary ischaemia and was therefore unable to further analyse their impact on postoperative adverse outcomes in patients with AAAD. Third, the study utilized a cross‐sectional design and lacked follow‐up data. Consequently, the association and possible causal link between DAR and long‐term survival, as well as the recurrent major cardiovascular events in AAAD patients, require validation through additional prospective cohort and mechanistic studies.

Conclusion

This study's results demonstrate that DAR is an independent predictor of postoperative mortality within 30 days in patients with AAAD, exhibiting strong predictive efficacy. In the future, DAR is expected to become a widely used preoperative risk assessment and screening tool for AAAD patients at high risk of poor prognosis.

A hybrid pythagorean fuzzy decision-making model for evaluating corporate environmental behavior through social responsibility dimensions
Authors: Qing Sun
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe assessment of the corporate environmental conduct in the context of social responsibility is a complex decision-making (DM) problem because of the contradictory evaluation criteria, qualitative judgments, and enormous uncertainties in the professional evaluation. The current methods of assessment do not capture expert hesitation well as well as fail to give balanced and transparent ranking of corporate environmental performance. To overcome these drawbacks, this paper will propose a problem-focused hybrid DM framework by combining the Pythagorean Fuzzy (PF) Analytic hierarchy Process (AHP) with Multi-Attribute Ideal-Real Comparative Analysis (MAIRCA) system.The expressive weaknesses of classical and intuitionistic fuzzy environments will be overcome by using pythagorean fuzzy sets (PFS). PF-AHP method is applied to obtain credible and uniform weights of social responsibility-based environmental criteria, and the MAIRCA methodology provides an objective and balanced ranking of corporate options by comparing ideal and real performances profile. Hypothetical case study is performed on six corporations with six evaluation criteria to demonstrate the relevance and usefulness of the proposed framework to decision support. The findings demonstrate that the proposed model of PF-AHP-MAIRCA has a higher level of discriminative power, transparency, and realistic decision support than any of the currently applicable fuzzy MCDM methods. The framework can provide useful information to corporate managers and policymakers who want to understand and compare corporate environmental behavior and improve it in accordance with the goals of social responsibility and sustainable development.Keywords: AHP-MAIRCA, Behavior, Corporate, Environmental, Dimensions, Decision-making, Pythagorean fuzzy set, Social responsibilitySubject terms: Environmental social sciences, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

The more recent trend in business practices has made corporate environmental behavior highly prominent due to the alarming issues that societies are facing, including climate change, eroding natural resources, and the growing population pressure of sustainable development1. Business organizations are not only rated on their financial reports but also on the way they balance their operations with social responsibility and environmental stewardship. Corporate Social Responsibility (CSR) then provides a much-needed outlook through which ecological practices could be gauged and therefore organizations could give back to society in a positive manner yet cause minimal harm to the ecology2. In this respect, it is not only a managerial need, but also a social need to test the corporate environmental behavior3. This area of DM is complex and the stakeholders such as managers and investors, regulating or community bodies, are likely to hold different opinions and the aspects of evaluation are qualitative and quantitative. Traditional evaluation methods cannot capture the complexity and uncertainty inherent in human judgment. In order to address these weaknesses, the fuzzy set (FS) theory has been applied more in multi-criteria decision-making (MCDM)4. Among the various extensions, there is the PFS, which is particularly beneficial as it allows decision makers to consider more flexibility with respect to uncertainty, reluctance, and vagueness compared to classical or intuitionistic fuzzy sets (IFS). Systematic identification of the weight of the criteria, considering the uncertainty present in expert judgment about the dimensions of CSR, including environmental protection, regulatory compliance, energy efficiency5, and stakeholder engagement6, can be achieved using the PF-AHP7 Combined, this hybrid method ensures both rigor and flexibility, making it well-suited for real-world applications. The framework offers significant implications for practice and policy. It provides managers with a way to diagnose corporate environmental behavior by identifying strengths and weaknesses in their sustainability practices, assists policymakers by facilitating responsible corporate governance5, and enhances the level of trust in the community by improving accountability. Moreover, it enables institutions to reach their planning objectives in tandem with the overall goals of sustainable development, thus leading to conservation of the environment and sustainable long-term social welfare. To conclude, the present paper proposes a novel hybrid model PF- AHP-MAIRCA that would enable an assessment of corporate environmental behavior in terms of social responsibility. The framework is not only contribution to the methodological advancment of MCDM, but also offers viable information towards establishing a culture of sustainability and responsibility in corporate systems.

Although the role of corporate environmental responsibility is gradually increasing, the assessment of corporate environmental behavior is still a difficult task because of the discrepancy in evaluation parameters and the ambiguity of the expert judgments. Current CSR based assessment techniques tend to be based on crisp or restricted fuzzy models and do not sufficiently reflect expert uncertainty and can lead to unstable or biased rankings. This drawback shows the necessity of the systematic and uncertainty-conscious decision-making model that would offer valid and transparent assessment results.

When different corporations (six in the present study) are rated against these six criterion, the decision makers are at bay. To begin with, the criteria are usually conflicting a company may be very active in the field of green innovations but not in compliance and transparency. Through the framework, the research not only determines the best corporations in regards to environmental responsibility but it also informs the managers, policymakers and stakeholders on how to reinforce the practices of corporations to the advantage of the society and the environment.

The primary aim of the study is to come up with a logically consistent and uncertainty-conscious decision-making framework to assess the corporate environmental behavior within the social responsibility considerations. To be more precise, the study will focus on: (i) the identification of the important social responsibility-based environmental evaluation criteria; (ii) modeling the expert uncertainty and hesitation with the help of the Pythagorean fuzzy sets; (iii) the determination of the reliable criteria weights with the help of the PF-AHP method; and (iv) the objective ranking of the corporate alternatives with the help of the MAIRCA approach.

Third, the study provides a clear picture of differences between corporations in environmental practices, in form of a Hypothetical case study, educating managers and policymakers to come up with more sustainable practices. Finally, the framework will be included in the wider corporate social responsibility agenda since it will offer a more open and versatile tool that can be implemented in industries to improve accountability, sustainability and long-term social gains.

Literature review

The phenomenon of corporate environmental behavior (CEB)6 is extensively analyzed as one of the components of Corporate Social Responsibility (CSR) and environmental social and governance (ESG)8 programs. Past research has evaluated the approaches used by firms to control emissions, minimize energy consumption, optimize resources, promote green innovation, and comply with environmental regulations. Most standard methods are based on indices based on ISO 14,001 certifications, GRI-based disclosures, or industry benchmarks or indicators related to stakeholders, such as community impact and supply chain practices. To a large extent, methodologically, most studies are divided into three streams: disclosure and scoring models, which translate voluntary reports into quantifiable indices; econometric and panel studies9, which relate environmental practices with financial or social results; and MCDM models10, which compare various criteria. Some of these methods, including AHP11, TOPSIS12, VIKOR13, PROMETHEE14, EDAS15, WASPAS16, and MARCOS17, have received broad applications in sustainability18, CSR, green supplier evaluation19, energy planning20, and ESG decision analytics. Although successful in other settings, conventional MCDM techniques presuppose accurate judgments and full information which is not always the case given the uncertainty of expert judgments or the incompleteness of corporate disclosure. To address these shortcomings, investigators have increasingly resorted to fuzzy methods. The fuzzy sets21 of Zadeh were used to describe linguistic terms like the high or moderate performance and make the evaluation less strict in the early work. With the increasing complexity of CSR assessments, IFS22 were developed to explicitly represent membership, non-membership and hesitation, to be able to express the doubt more fully. AHP and TOPSIS approaches based on IFS started to be used commonly in CSR assessment, to minimize overconfidence in one-point ratings. Based on these advances, PFS23 were proposed as a more generalized counterpart. In contrast to IFS, where the sum of membership degree MD and non-membership degree NMD is constrained to one, PFS is quadratic in nature, i.e., the sum of the squares of the membership and the squares of the non-membership degrees is not more than one. This maturity framework provides DM with additional room to articulate indecisiveness and nuanced entitlement. It comes in handy in a sustainability and CSR setting, where data is diffuse and the levels of specialist conviction differ. Research has indicated that PFS-based techniques tend to give more consistent and discriminating outputs, particularly when dealing with a group DM process wherein the views are not homogeneous. Ranking methods have also been developed in conjunction with FS. MAIRCA24 is a method used to rank alternatives based on a comparison with ideal-real profile. Compared to approaches that only focus on proximity to an ideal, MAIRCA not only focuses on the best possible case but also examines the realistic distribution of preferences, which minimizes the likelihood of one criterion with a heavy weight dominating the result. Although MAIRCA has been utilized in other areas like energy planning, selection of suppliers and assessment of infrastructure, it has not been extensively used in the context of CSR-inspired corporate environmental behavior. In areas where it has been used, the approach has demonstrated a high potential of optimization in trade-offs between compliance, efficiency, and innovation. These advances notwithstanding, several research gaps remain. Even various CSR assessments continue to use sharp or simple fuzzy methods, which do not adequately reflect the uncertainty and uncertainty as to expert judgments moreover, deriving weights and ranking alternatives usually are done separately, which restricts methodological consistency. There is also a lack of sensitivity analyses, which reduces transparency and complicates the comprehension of how the results could vary in the event of different assumptions. Lastly, certain current frameworks adopt comprehensive criteria that combine environmental and governance variables, and it becomes more challenging to derive results that are uniquely applicable in terms of corporate environmental behavior. Against these gaps, one can evidently see a necessity of a framework that incorporates sound fuzzy weighting and ranking techniques and targets explicitly the CSR-based dimensions of the environment, including compliance, eco-efficiency, circularity, pollution prevention, stakeholder engagement, and green innovation. This paper addresses that need and provides a powerful instrument to evaluate corporate environmental behavior in the prism of social responsibility by implementing a PF-AHP-based criteria weighting process and the MAIRCA-based method of ranking.

Despite the fact that other researchers have applied different MCDM methods to the CSR and environmental performance assessment, most of them use crisp or basic fuzzy models and do not combine criteria weighting and ranking of alternatives. In addition, little has been done to explicitly model expert hesitation in an integrated CSR-oriented DM framework. The research fills these gaps by integrating Pythagorean fuzzy logic with a single PF-AHP-MAIRCA model, giving the research greater theoretical conformity and a methodological fit.

This study employs the MAIRCA24 method combined with the AHP approach to solve an MCDM problem involving PFS uncertainty. To address the complexities and uncertainties affecting decision-making reliability, the hybrid AHP-MAIRCA method combines two contrasting MCDM techniques for optimal results. MAIRCA emphasizes logical comparison of ideal and actual alternatives, supporting transparent and preference-driven ranking. This integration offers a balanced approach that considers decision-makers’ preferences through MAIRCA and leverages data-driven weighting with AHP, making it particularly effective for uncertain, multi-criteria scenarios, such as evaluating the Consumer value of a purchasing strategy.

Result discussion

The findings of the recommended PF-AHP-MAIRCA framework were presented in the table above, in which six corporate options were compared with six social responsibility-based environmental standards. The ranking results show that scored the best and got the first position followed by in the second position and in the third position. and held the medium positions whereas had the lowest score and was ranked last. These results imply that is doing well in most of the dimensions of environmental behavior especially when it comes to compliance, resource efficiency, and innovation. Conversely, is performing behind because it is not performing well in several parameters and this shows areas that should be improved. The balanced ranking also demonstrates that some businesses can be good in terms of innovation or reporting, however, their performance can be weak in terms of pollution control or stakeholder engagement which influences their overall results. The ultimate advantage of the study is that the PF-AHP-MAIRCA method is much better than the conventional methods, such as the classical AHP-TOPSIS method or the IF MCDM method. Firstly, by using the Pythagorean fuzzy sets, the decision makers can express hesitation and uncertainty more adequately, and this is quite common with CSR evaluations, where judgment is subjective and incomplete. In contrast to IFS, PFS offers a greater range of membership and non-membership, and therefore, it is a more accurate model of expert judgments. Second, AHP quantitatively identifies the relative importance of the CSR criteria, and MAIRCA quantitatively ranks them by comparing actual performance to an ideal-real profile. This prevents favoritism to one overruling criterion and creates less discriminative and more balanced rankings. All in all, the findings prove that the given framework is not only practical but also stronger, more sensitive and transparent than others. It provides managers and policymakers a better understanding of corporate environmental behavior, reveals the strengths and weaknesses of the practices carried out in the organizations and asks the firms to engage in strategies that are more aligned to the objectives of social responsibility and sustainability. The ranking variance also shows that good results in one dimension of CSR cannot be used to gain a high ranking. The companies with balanced results in terms of compliance, efficiency, innovation, and stakeholder engagement will be ranked higher, but the weaknesses in terms of pollution control or transparency will be rated as a major setback. This shows that the proposed framework is sensitive to detect trade-offs between the dimensions of CSR and is applicable in diagnosing certain areas that need improvement.

Advantages of study

In this section we will discuss the advantages of this study.

There are several strengths associated with the current study. First, it provides a full-fledged evaluation system, thus, combining weighting (PF-AHP) with ranking (MAIRCA), in such a way that the decisions will be systematic and balanced. Second, the framework can more effectively model hesitation and uncertainty in expert judgments than classical or IF approaches as it makes use of PFS. This makes the outcomes more believable and valid. Third, the addition of the CSR compliance, innovation and stakeholder engagement dimensions ensures that the corporate environmental behavior can be assessed not only with the help of technical perspective but also with the help of its social responsibility that is one of the contemporary global concerns. Fourth, the applicability of the framework to practical application is manifested in the case study because it shows that corporations can be equitably and transparently compared. Finally, the framework is flexible and general i.e. it may be applied to other sectors, regions or the policy environment as a guideline that can enable managers, policymakers and other individuals within a society to be more sustainable and responsible.

Limitations and future direction

In this section, we will discuss the Limitations and future directions of this study.

The research has limitations even though this study is original and practical. The case study is based on hypothetical information and limited number of criteria and decisions that may not be representative of the reality of the actual corporate environmental practices. Its framework is also premised on the opinion of experts, which, despite being run on PFS, can result in subjectivity of the assessment process. The paper is also devoted to six aspects; practically the impacts of the supply chain or lifecycle analysis are the possible aspects and industry-dependent considerations that the supply chain of corporations can rely on. Such limitations mean that the given model is powerful, whereas the outcomes should be viewed as illustrative and not final.

The study has following Future Directions.

Future research can be continued in this work through several avenues. To assess the effectiveness of the framework, one can first refer to it in real case studies involving larger datasets and actual corporate performance measures. Second it is possible to add more criteria and sub-criteria to capture industry specifics of the environmental behavior e.g. carbon footprint, practices of a circular economy or biodiversity impact. Third, the model can be further extended with other more complex extensions of FS such as q-rung orthopair fuzzy sets (QROFS)27 or spherical fuzzy sets (SFS)28 in order to be more aspective of uncertainty. Finally, comparative studies can be considered in future research, taking advantage of the given framework to apply it to either industries or various countries, which would provide valuable data on international trends in corporate social responsibility and environmental friendliness.

Conclusion

This paper has come up with a new hybrid PF-AHP-MAIRCA model to assess the corporate behavior on environment amid the issue of social responsibility. The uncertainty and reluctance to take decisive action of expert judgment inherent in the model is dealt with by the use of the expressive power of PFS in a logically consistent weighting and ranking process. A hypothetical case study showed the efficiency of the framework. It affirmed that high overall performance involves balanced strength in various dimensions of CSR - regulatory compliance, green innovation, and stakeholder engagement as opposed to excellence in one dimension. This result answers the main research question directly and in a structured, uncertainty-aware way, which offers a comparative corporate evaluation approach. Three major contributions are made by the research. In theory, it is a step towards improving fuzzy MCDM through a meaningful combination of PFS and AHP-MAIRCA hybrid in CSR-based assessments. In practice, it provides a diagnostic resource to managers to detect the strengths and weaknesses of environmental practices. To policymakers, it aids in the design of transparent and multi-criteria and evaluation systems which should encourage accountability and sustainable development within corporations, even though its shortcomings such as the use of hypothetical information are also recognized in Sect. 4.5. The future research would incorporate the model into real-life data and investigate how it can be extended to other sophisticated fuzzy sets representations to make the model more general.

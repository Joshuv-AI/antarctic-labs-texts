Labeled Trees Generating Separable and Locally Finite Ultrametrics
Oleksiy Dovgoshey, Olga Rovenska

Abstract
We analyze the interplay between labeled trees and the ultrametric spaces they present. We provide characterizations of labeled trees that generate separable ultrametric spaces and those that generate locally finite ultrametric spaces. In particular, we establish an analog of König’s Infinity Lemma for locally finite ultrametric spaces generated by labeled trees.

Introduction

According to
Gurvich and Vyalyi 2012
, any finite ultrametric space is isometrically describable by a Gurvich–Vyalyi representing tree. The
Gurvich–Vyalyi representing trees form a subclass of finite trees equipped with a specific labeling of the vertex set. Trees with labeled vertices have been extensively studied, resulting in numerous contributions (see, e.g., the survey in
Gallian 2024
). The corresponding geometric interpretation of the Gurvich–Vyalyi representation
Petrov and Dovgoshey 2014
provides a framework for addressing various extremal problems related to finite ultrametric spaces
Dovgoshey and Petrov 2020
;
Dovgoshey et al. 2016
;
Dovgoshey et al. 2017
.
Analogues of the Gurvich–Vyalyi representation and its geometric interpretation have recently been extended to the class of totally bounded ultrametric spaces
Dovgoshey 2025
.

Infinite trees with positive real-valued edge labelings are commonly referred to as
R
-trees (see
Berestrovskii 2019
for relevant results concerning
R
-trees and ultrametrics). The structural relationships between finite subtrees of
R
-trees and finite monotone rooted trees are described in
Dovgoshey 2020
. The categorical equivalence between trees and ultrametric spaces has been studied in
Hughes 2004
;
Lemin 2003
.

Ultrametric spaces generated by arbitrary nonnegative vertex labelings on finite and infinite trees were introduced in
Dovgoshey 2020
and subsequently studied in
Dovgoshey and Kostikov 2023
;
Dovgoshey and Küçükaslan 2022
.
A characterization of totally bounded ultrametric spaces generated by labeled almost rays was provided in
Dovgoshey and Vito 2025
. Furthermore,
Dovgoshey and Rovenska 2025
contains a metric characterization of ultrametric spaces generated by labeled star graphs.

Our work continues the aforementioned studies on the characterization of labeled trees generating ultrametric spaces, with a focus on the topological properties of these spaces.
The aim is to describe the class of trees that admit labelings which generate separable ultrametric spaces. Additionally, we establish a characterization of such trees in terms of the local finiteness of the corresponding ultrametrics.

The structure of the paper is as follows. Section
2
introduces and reminds the necessary definitions and results.

Theorem
2
in Section
3
completely describes the structure of free trees which admit labelings generating separable ultrametric spaces. The labeled star graphs generating separable ultrametric are characterized in Corollary
2
of Section
3
.

Section
4
is devoted to the characterization of trees that generate locally finite ultrametric spaces. The necessary and sufficient conditions under which labeled rays and labeled star graphs generate locally finite ultrametric spaces are given in Proposition
4

and, respectively, in Proposition
5
of Section
4
.
Theorem
3
provides an analog of König’s Infinity Lemma for the locally finite case. The main result of this section, Theorem
4
, describes the trees that admit labelings
generating locally finite ultrametric spaces.

Preliminaries

Throughout this text, we denote by
\mathbb{R}^{+}
the half-open interval
[0,\infty)
, by
\mathbb{N}
the set of all positive integers, and by
\aleph_{0}
the cardinality of the set
\mathbb{N}
. In addition, we will write
(s_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subseteq A
if
(s_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}

is a sequence whose points are elements of the set
A
.

A
metric
on a set
X
is a function
d\colon X\times X\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{+}
such that for all
x
,
y
,
z\in X

(i)

d(x,y)=d(y,x)
,

(ii)

(d(x,y)=0)\iff(x=y)
,

(iii)

d(x,y)\leq d(x,z)+d(z,y)
.

A metric space
(X,d)
is called an
ultrametric space
if the
strong triangle inequality

d(x,y)\leq\max\{d(x,z),d(z,y)\}

holds for all
x
,
y
,
z\in X
. In this case, the function
d
is called
an ultrametric
on
X
.

Let
(X,d)
be a metric space. A
closed ball
with a
radius

r>0
and a
center

c\in X
is the set

B_{r}(c)=\{x\in X\colon d(c,x)\leq r\}.

A set
A\subseteq X
is said to be
bounded
if there exists
B_{r}(c)\subseteq X
such that
A\subseteq B_{r}(c).

We say that a set
S\subseteq X
is a
discrete subset
of
(X,d)
if for every
s\in S
there is
r>0
such that

B_{r}(s)\cap S=\{s\}.

(1)

A sequence
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of points in a metric space
(X,d)
is said to be
convergent
to a point
a\in X
,

\lim_{n\to\infty}x_{n}=a,

if and only if

\lim_{n\to\infty}d(x_{n},a)=0.

Let
(X,d)
be a metric space, and let
S\subseteq X
. The set
S
is said to be
dense
in
(X,d)
if for every
a\in X
there is a sequence
(s_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subseteq S
such that

a=\lim_{n\to\infty}s_{n}.

A metric space
(X,d)
is
separable
if and only if there exists a set
A\subseteq X

such that
A
is dense in
(X,d)
and
|A|\leq\aleph_{0}
holds.

We will use the following auxiliary statement.

Lemma 1
.

Let
(X,d)
be a separable metric space and let
A\subseteq X
be nonempty. Then the metric space
(A,d|_{A\times A})
is also separable.

See, for example,
(
Kainth 2023
, p. 216)
, Corollary 8.11.

Definition 1
.

A metric space
(X,d)
is called locally finite if every bounded
A\subseteq X
is finite.

Remark 1
.

Definition
1
is used, for example, in
Capraro 2013
,
Ostrovska and Ostrovskii 2019
,
Ostrovskii 2012
, but is not generally accepted.
In Encyclopedia of Distances
Deza and Deza 2016
and many other works, the locally finite spaces
(X,d)
are required to satisfy Definition
1
and the following condition: there exists
r>0
such that the ball
B_{r}(x)
contains exactly one point for each
x\in X
.

The next statement follows directly from Definition
1
.

Lemma 2
.

Let
(X,d)
be a locally finite metric space and let
A
be a subset of
X
. Then the metric space
(A,d|_{A\times A})
is locally finite.

Proof.

It suffices to note that each bounded in
(A,d|_{A\times A})
set is also bounded in
(X,d).

Further, let us recall some definitions and facts from graph theory.

A
graph
is a pair
(V,E)
consisting of a set
V
and a set
E
whose elements are unordered pairs
\{u,v\}
of different points
u,v\in V
. For a graph
G=(V,E)
, the sets
V=V(G)
and
E=E(G)
are called
the set of vertices
and
the set of edges
, respectively. A graph
G
is
finite
if
V(G)
is a finite set. If
\{x,y\}\in E(G)
, then the vertices
x
and
y
are called
adjacent
. In what follows, we will always assume that
E(G)\cap V(G)=\emptyset
.

Let
G
be a graph.
A graph
G_{1}
is a
subgraph
of
G
if

V(G_{1})\subseteq V(G)\quad\text{and}\quad E(G_{1})\subseteq E(G).

In this case we will write
G_{1}\subseteq G
.

If
\{G_{i}\colon i\in I\}
is a family of subgraphs of a graph
G
, then, by definition, the union
\bigcup_{i\in I}G_{i}
is a subgraph
G^{*}
of
G
such that

V(G^{*})=\bigcup_{i\in I}V(G_{i})\quad\text{and}\quad E(G^{*})=\bigcup_{i\in I}E(G_{i}).

Similarly, the intersection
\bigcap_{i\in I}G_{i}
is a subgraph
G_{*}
of
G
with

V(G_{*})=\bigcap_{i\in I}V(G_{i})\quad\text{and}\quad E(G_{*})=\bigcap_{i\in I}E(G_{i}).

Let
v
be a vertex of a graph
G
, and let
N(v)
be the set of vertices of
G
adjacent to
v
,

N(v)=\{u\in V(G)\colon\{v,u\}\in E(G)\}.

(2)

The
degree
of the vertex
v
is, by definition, the cardinality of the set
N(v)
.
The degree of
v
will be denoted as
\delta_{G}(v)
. Thus, we have

\delta_{G}(v)=|N(v)|.

A
path
is a finite graph
P
whose vertices can be numbered without repetitions so that

V(P)=\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}\}\quad\text{and}\quad E(P)=\{\{x_{1},x_{2}\},\ldots,\{x_{k-1},x_{k}\}\}

(3)

with
k\geqslant 2
. We will write
P=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k})
or
P=P_{x_{1},x_{k}}
if
P
is a path satisfying (
3
) and said that
P
is a
path joining
x_{1}
and
x_{k}
. A graph
G
is
connected
if for every two distinct vertices of
G
there is a path
P\subseteq G
joining these vertices.

A finite graph
C
is a
cycle
if there exists an enumeration of its vertices without repetition such that
V(C)=\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\}
and

E(C)=\{\{x_{1},x_{2}\},\ldots,\{x_{n-1},x_{n}\},\{x_{n},x_{1}\}\}\quad\text{with }n\geq 3.

Definition 2
.

A connected graph
T
with
V(T)\neq\emptyset
and without cycles is called a tree.

We shall say that a tree
T
is a
star graph
if there is a vertex
c\in V(T)
, the
center
of
T
, such that
c
and
v
are adjacent for every
v\in V(T)\setminus\{c\}
.

An infinite graph
R
of the form

V(R)=\{x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n},x_{n+1},\ldots\},\quad E(R)=\{\{x_{1},x_{2}\},\ldots,\{x_{n},x_{n+1}\},\ldots\},

(4)

where all
x_{n}
are assumed to be distinct, is called a
ray
. If (
4
) holds then we will write
R=(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n},\ldots)
. It is clear that every ray is a tree. A graph is
rayless
if it contains no rays.

The next proposition is a modification of König’s Infinity Lemma
König 1927
.

Proposition 1
.

Every infinite connected graph has a vertex of infinite degree or contains a ray.

A proof of Proposition
1
is provided, for instance, in
Diestel 2017
, Proposition 8.2.1.

The following statement is well known in the context of finite trees. A proof for infinite trees can be found in
Dovgoshey and Kostikov 2023
, Lemma 1.

Lemma 3
.

In each tree, every two different vertices are connected by exactly one path.

In the next definition, we recall the notion of a hull for arbitrary trees.

Definition 3
.

Let
T
be a tree, and let
A
be a nonempty subset of
V(T)
. A subtree
H_{A}
of the tree
T
is the
hull
of
A
if
A\subseteq V(H_{A})
and, for every subtree
T^{*}
of
T
, the tree
H_{A}
is a subtree of
T^{*}
whenever
A\subseteq V(T^{*})
.

Thus,
H_{A}
is the smallest subtree of
T
which contains
A
.

Proposition 2
.

Let
T
be a tree,
A
be a nonempty subset of
V(T)
and let
\mathcal{F}_{A}
be the set of all subtrees
T^{*}
of
T
for which the inclusion
A\subseteq V(T^{*})
holds. Then the graph
\bigcap_{T^{*}\in\mathcal{F}_{A}}T^{*}
is the hull of
A
,

H_{A}=\bigcap_{T^{*}\in\mathcal{F}_{A}}T^{*}.

(5)

A proof can be found in
Dovgoshey and Küçükaslan 2022
.

The following proposition gives a “constructive” description of the hulls of sets
A\subseteq V(T)
for trees
T
with
|V(T)|\geq 2
.

Proposition 3
.

Let
T
be a tree and let
A\subseteq V(T)
contain at least two points. Then the equality

H_{A}=\bigcup_{v\in A\setminus\{u\}}P_{u,v}

(6)

holds for every
u\in A
, where
P_{u,v}
is the path connecting
u
and
v
in
T
.

For the proof, see
Dovgoshey and Vito 2025
.

Let us introduce the concept of labeled trees.

Definition 4
.

A labeled tree is a pair
(T,l)
, where
T
is a tree and
l
is a mapping defined on the set
V(T)
.

If
(T,l)
is a labeled tree, then we will say that
T
is a
free tree
corresponding to
(T,l)
and write
T=T(l)
instead of
(T,l)
. Moreover, in what follows, we will consider only the nonnegative real-valued labelings
l\colon V(T)\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
.

Following
Dovgoshey 2020
, for arbitrary labeled tree
T=T(l)
, we define a mapping
d_{l}\colon V(T)\times V(T)\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
as

d_{l}(u,v)=\begin{cases}0&\text{if }u=v,\\
\max\limits_{v^{*}\in V(P)}l(v^{*})&\text{if }u\neq v,\end{cases}

(7)

where
P
is a path joining
u
and
v
in
T
.

Theorem 1
.

Let
T=T(l)
be a labeled tree. The mapping
d_{l}
is an ultrametric on the set
V(T)
if and only if the inequality

\max\{l(u),l(v)\}>0

(8)

holds for every
\{u,v\}\in E(T)
.

A proof of Theorem
1
can be obtained by simple modification of the proof of Proposition 3.2 in
Dovgoshey 2020
.

In what follows we shall say that a labeling
l\colon V(T)\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
is
non-degenerate
if the inequality

\max\{l(u),l(v)\}>0

holds for every
\{u,v\}\in E(T)
.

3
Separability of Generated Ultrametrics

The goal of this section is to describe the structure of free trees
T
which admit non-degenerate labelings

l\colon V(T)\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
generating separable ultrametric spaces
(V(T),d_{l})
.

In what follows, we will say that a set
A
is
countable
if the inequality
|A|\leq\aleph_{0}
holds.

Lemma 4
.

Let
T
be a tree. If the inequality

\delta_{T}(v)\leq\aleph_{0}

(9)

holds for every
v\in V(T)
, then the set
V(T)
is countable,

|V(T)|\leq\aleph_{0}.

(10)

Proof.

Let
v^{*}
be a fixed vertex of
T
. Then we can define a sequence
(N_{j}(v^{*}))_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
by

N_{1}(v^{*}):=N(v^{*}),\quad N_{2}(v^{*}):=\bigcup_{u\in N_{1}(v^{*})}N(u),

and, for every integer
j\geq 2
,

N_{j+1}(v^{*}):=\bigcup_{u\in N_{j}(v^{*})}N(u),

(11)

where
N(v^{*})
and
N(u)
are defined as in (
2
).

Since
T
is a connected graph, the equality

V(T)=\bigcup_{j\in\mathbb{N}}N_{j}(v^{*})

(12)

holds.
Let (
9
) hold for every
v\in V(T)
. Inequality (
9
) can be rewritten as

|N(v)|\leq\aleph_{0}.

Hence, by (
11
), every
N_{j}(v^{*})
is a countable union of countable sets. Thus the inequality

|N_{j}(v^{*})|\leq\aleph_{0}

holds for every
j\in\mathbb{N}
.
Similarly, the last inequality and equality (
12
) imply inequality (
10
).

∎

The next theorem is the main result of this section.

Theorem 2
.

Let
T
be a tree. Then the following statements are equivalent:

(i)

The vertex set of
T
is countable.

(ii)

For every non-degenerate labeling
l\colon V(T)\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
, the ultrametric space
(V(T),d_{l})
is separable.

(iii)

There exists a labeling
l\colon V(T)\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
such that
(V(T),d_{l})
is a separable ultrametric space.

Proof.

(i)

\Rightarrow

(ii)
.
This implication follows directly from the definition of separability.

(ii)

\Rightarrow

(iii)
.
The implication is true because every tree
T
admits a non-degenerate labeling

l\colon V(T)\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
.

(iii)

\Rightarrow

(i)
. Suppose, to the contrary, that there exists a non-degenerate labeling
l\colon V(T)\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
such that
(V(T),d_{l})
is separable, but the inequality

|V(T)|>\aleph_{0}

holds. Then, by Lemma
4
, there is
v^{*}\in V(T)
such that

\delta_{T}(v^{*})>\aleph_{0}.

(13)

Let us suppose first that
l(v^{*})>0
. Then equality (
7
) gives us

d_{l}(u,v)=\max\{l(v),l(v^{*}),l(u)\}\geq l(v^{*})>0

(14)

for all different
u,v\in N(v^{*})
.

Hence, the ultrametric space
(N(v^{*}),d_{l}|_{N(v^{*})\times N(v^{*})})
is a discrete subspace of
(V(T),d_{l})
.
Moreover, this subspace is a separable subspace of
(V(T),d_{l})
by Lemma
1
.
In every non-empty discrete metric space
(X,d)
, every dense subset of
(X,d)
coincides with

X
. Consequently, the inequality

|N(v^{*})|\leq\aleph_{0}

holds because
(N(v^{*}),d_{l}|_{N(v^{*})\times N(v^{*})})
is separable.
The last inequality and the equality

|N(v^{*})|=\delta_{T}(v^{*})

imply that inequality (
13
) is false, a contradiction to the supposition.

Let us now consider the case when the equality

l(v^{*})=0

(15)

holds.

Since
l\colon V(T)\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
is non-degenerate, equality (
15
)
implies the inequality

l(v)>0

(16)

for every
v\in N(v^{*})
.

For each
m\in\mathbb{N}
, we denote by
N^{m}(v^{*})
the set of all
v\in N(v^{*})
satisfying the inequality

l(v)\geq\frac{1}{m}.

Since (
16
) holds for every
v\in N(v^{*})
, we obtain the equality

N(v^{*})=\bigcup_{m\in\mathbb{N}}N^{m}(v^{*}).

Using the last equality and inequality (
13
), we can find
m_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that

|N^{m_{0}}(v^{*})|>\aleph_{0}.

(17)

Indeed, otherwise
N(v^{*})
is a countable union of countable sets
N^{m}(v^{*})
and hence also is countable,

|N(v^{*})|\leq\aleph_{0},

contrary to (
13
).

Let
m_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
satisfy (
17
). Then
N^{m_{0}}(v^{*})
is an uncountable subset of
V(T)
.
Then, arguing as in proof of (
14
), we obtain

d_{l}(u,v)\geq\frac{1}{m_{0}}

for all distinct
u,v\in N^{m_{0}}(v^{*})
.

Thus,
(N^{m_{0}}(v^{*}),d_{l}|_{N^{m_{0}}(v^{*})\times N^{m_{0}}(v^{*})})
is an uncountable discrete subspace of
(V(T),d_{l})
.
As was proven above in the analysis of the case
l(v^{*})>0
, the last statement leads to a contradiction with inequality (
13
).

This completes the proof.

∎

Theorem
2
and Lemma
4
give us the following corollary.

Corollary 1
.

Let
T
be a tree. Then the following statements are equivalent:

(i)

There exists a labeling
l\colon V(T)\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
such that
(V(T),d_{l})
is a separable ultrametric space.

(ii)

For every star graph
S\subseteq T
there exists a non-degenerate labeling
l_{S}\colon V(S)\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
such that the ultrametric space
(V(S),d_{l_{S}})
is separable.

The following corollary describes the labeled star graphs generating separable ultrametric spaces.

Corollary 2
.

Let
S=S(l)
be a star graph with non-degenerate labeling
l_{S}\colon V(S)\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
. Then the following statements are equivalent:

(i)

The ultrametric space
(V(S),d_{l_{S}})
is separable.

(ii)

The set

W_{\varepsilon}:=\{v\in V(S)\colon l_{S}(v)\leq\varepsilon\}

(18)

is countable for every
\varepsilon>0
.

Proof.

(i)\Rightarrow(ii)
. Let (i) hold. Then
V(S)
is countable by Theorem
2
. Hence, for every
\varepsilon>0
, the set
W_{\varepsilon}
is also countable as a subset of a countable set.

(ii)\Rightarrow(i)
. Let (ii) hold and let
(\varepsilon_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of positive numbers such that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\varepsilon_{n}=\infty.

The last limit relation and (
18
) imply the equality

\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}W_{\varepsilon_{n}}=V(S).

(19)

By statement
(ii)
, each
W_{\varepsilon_{n}}
is countable. Hence
V(S)
also is countable as a countable union of countable sets. Hence
(V(S),d_{l_{S}})
is separable by Theorem
2
.

This completes the proof.

4
Local Finiteness of Generated Ultrametrics

Let us now turn to locally finite ultrametric spaces
(V(T),d_{l})
.

Lemma 5
.

Let
T=T(l)
be a labeled tree with non-degenerate labeling
l\colon V(T)\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
, let
A
be a nonempty bounded subset of the ultrametric space
(V(T),d_{l})
.
Then the vertex set of hull
H_{A}
is also a bounded subset of
(V(T),d_{l})
.

Proof.

If
A
is a singleton set, then
V(H_{A})
is also a singleton set and, consequently,
V(H_{A})
is bounded.

Suppose that
A
contains at least two points and let
u
be an arbitrary point in
A
.
Then, by Proposition
3
, the equality

H_{A}=\bigcup_{v\in A\setminus\{u\}}P_{u,v}

(20)

holds. Consider an arbitrary point
w\in V(H_{A})
. Equality (
20
) implies that there is
v\in A\setminus\{u\}
such that the point
w
is a vertex of the path
P_{u,v}
.

Consequently the inequality

d_{l}(u,w)\leq d_{l}(u,v)

(21)

holds by definition of the ultrametric
d_{l}
. Since
A
is a bounded subset of
(V(T),d_{l})
, there is
r>0
such that

d_{l}(u,v)\leq r

(22)

for all
v\in V(H_{A})
. Thus, the set
V(H_{A})
is a subset of the closed ball
B_{r}(u)
and, consequently,
V(H_{A})
is bounded as required.

∎

The following proposition gives a complete description of labelings
l\colon V(T)\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
generating locally finite ultrametric spaces
(V(T),d_{l})
for the case where trees
T
are rays.

Proposition 4
.

Let
l_{R}\colon V(R)\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
be a non-degenerate labeling on the vertex set of a ray
R=(v_{1},v_{2},\ldots,v_{n},\ldots)
. Then the following statements are equivalent:

(i)

The ultrametric space
(V(R),d_{l_{R}})
is locally finite.

(ii)

The limit relation

\limsup_{n\to\infty}l_{R}(v_{n})=\infty

(23)

holds.

Proof.

(i)

\Rightarrow

(ii)
. Suppose, that the limit superior in (
23
) is a finite

\limsup_{n\to\infty}l_{R}(v_{n})=k<\infty.

(24)

Then for each
\varepsilon>0
, there is
m\in\mathbb{N}
such that

l_{R}(v_{n})\leq k+\varepsilon

for all
n\geq m.
It implies the inequality

l_{R}(v_{n})\leq\max\{l_{R}(v_{1}),\ldots,l_{R}(v_{m-1}),k+\varepsilon\}

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Hence the inequality

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}l_{R}(v_{n})<\infty

(25)

holds. Now using (
7
) and (
25
), we obtain

d_{l_{R}}(u,w)\leq\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}l_{R}(v_{n})<\infty

for all
u,w\in(V(R),d_{l_{R}})
. Consequently,
(V(R),d_{l_{R}})
is bounded and infinite, contrary to the supposition.

(ii)

\Rightarrow

(i)
. Let
A\subseteq V(R)
be an arbitrary bounded subset.
We must prove that
A
is finite.
Since
A
is bounded, there is a constant
c>0
such that

d_{l_{R}}(u,w)<c

(26)

for all
u,w\in A
. Using (
23
) we can find an infinite subsequence
(u_{n_{k}})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
of the sequence
(v_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
such that
n_{1}\geq 2
and

l_{R}(v_{n_{k}})\geq c

(27)

for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Let us define a sequence
(P_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
of paths in
R
by the rule:

P_{1}=(v_{1},\ldots,v_{n_{1}})

is
the path joining
v_{1}
and
v_{n_{1}}
, and

P_{k}=(v_{n_{k-1}},\ldots,v_{n_{k}})

for every
k\geq 2
.

We claim that a sequence
(P_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
contains a path
P_{k_{0}}
such that

A\subseteq V(P_{k_{0}}).

(28)

Indeed if the last claim is false, then there exist two distinct paths
P_{k_{1}}
and
P_{k_{2}}
satisfying the conditions

A\cap V(P_{k_{1}})\neq\emptyset\neq A\cap V(P_{k_{2}}),

(29)

and

A\cap V(P_{k_{1}})\neq A\cap V(P_{k_{2}}).

(30)

Using (
29
) and (
30
), we can find
a_{1},a_{2}\in A
such that

a_{1}\in V(P_{k_{1}})\setminus V(P_{k_{2}})\quad\text{and}\quad a_{2}\in V(P_{k_{2}})\setminus V(P_{k_{1}}).

(31)

Without loss of generality, we assume that the inequality

k_{1}<k_{2}

(32)

holds.
Let
P_{a_{1},a_{2}}
be a path joining
a_{1}
and
a_{2}
in
R
.
Membership relations (
31
) and inequality (
32
) imply

v_{n_{k_{1}}}\in V(P_{a_{1},a_{2}}).

(33)

Hence

d_{l_{R}}(a_{1},a_{2})=\max_{v\in P_{a_{1},a_{2}}}l(v)\geq l(v_{n_{k_{1}}})

(34)

holds by (
7
), (
33
).
Now, using (
27
) and (
34
), we get the inequality
d_{l_{R}}(a_{1},a_{2})\geq c
,
which contradicts (
26
).

Thus there is a path
P_{k_{0}}
satisfying inclusion (
28
) that implies

|A|\leq|V(P_{k_{0}})|<\infty.

This completes the proof.

The following proposition describes the labeled star graphs generating locally finite ultrametric spaces.

Proposition 5
.

Let
S=S(l)
be a star graph with non-degenerate labeling
l_{S}\colon V(S)\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
. Then the following statements are equivalent:

(i)

The ultrametric space
(V(S),d_{l_{S}})
is locally finite.

(ii)

The set

W_{\varepsilon}:=\{v\in V(S)\colon l_{S}(v)\leq\varepsilon\}

(35)

is finite for every
\varepsilon>0
.

Proof.

(i)

\Rightarrow

(ii)
. Suppose, for contradiction, there exists
\varepsilon_{0}>0
such that the set
W_{\varepsilon_{0}}
given in (
35
) is infinite.

Write
H_{W_{\varepsilon_{0}}}
for the hull of the set
W_{\varepsilon_{0}}
and denote by
c
the center of the star graph
S
.
Then
H_{W_{\varepsilon_{0}}}
is a subtree of the star graph
S
. Since every connected subgraph of
S
is also a star graph with the same center,
H_{W_{\varepsilon_{0}}}
is a star graph with the center
c
.

The vertex set of
H_{W_{\varepsilon_{0}}}
is the set
W_{\varepsilon_{0}}\cup\{c\}
,

V(H_{W_{\varepsilon_{0}}})=W_{\varepsilon_{0}}\cup\{c\}.

(36)

Equality (
35
) with
\varepsilon=\varepsilon_{0}
and equality (
36
) give us the inequality

l_{S}(v)\leq\max\{\varepsilon_{0},l_{S}(c)\}

for every
v\in V(H_{W_{\varepsilon_{0}}})
.
The last inequality and equality (
7
) with
T=S
and
l=l_{S}
show that
V(H_{W_{\varepsilon_{0}}})
is a bounded subset of the ultrametric space
(V(S),d_{l_{S}})
.
Since
(V(S),d_{l_{S}})
is locally bounded, the boundedness of the set

V(H_{W_{\varepsilon_{0}}})
implies the finiteness of this set. Consequently, the set
W_{\varepsilon_{0}}
is also finite, which contradicts the definition of the number
\varepsilon_{0}
.

Thus, the set
W_{\varepsilon}
is finite for each
\varepsilon>0
.

(ii)

\Rightarrow

(i)
. Let
A
be an arbitrary bounded subset of the ultrametric space
(V(S),d_{l_{S}})
. We must prove the inequality

|A|<\infty.

(37)

Since
A
is bounded, there is a positive number
\varepsilon_{1}
such that

d_{l_{S}}(u,v)\leq\varepsilon_{1}<\infty

(38)

for all
u,v\in A.

Using (
7
)
and the definition of the star graphs we can prove the equality

d_{l_{S}}(u,v)=\max\{l_{S}(u),l_{S}(c),l_{S}(v)\}

(39)

for all different
u,v\in V(S)
, where
c
is the center of the star graph
S
. Now (
38
) and (
39
) imply the inequality

l_{S}(v)\leq\varepsilon_{1}

for each
v\in A
.
The last inequality and formula (
35
) show that the inclusion

A\subseteq W_{\varepsilon_{1}}

(40)

holds. By statement
(ii)
, the set
W_{\varepsilon}
is finite for each
\varepsilon>0
.
Thus, (
37
) follows from (
40
).

This completes the proof.

∎

The next theorem is an analog of König’s Infinity Lemma for locally finite ultrametric spaces
(V(T),d_{l})
.

Theorem 3
.

Let
T=T(l)
be a labeled tree with non-degenerate labeling
l\colon V(T)\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
. Then the following statements are equivalent:

(i)

The ultrametric space
(V(T),d_{l})
is locally finite.

(ii)

The ultrametric spaces
(V(R),d_{l}|_{V(R)\times V(R)})
and
(V(S),d_{l}|_{V(S)\times V(S)})
are locally finite for all rays
R\subseteq T
and all star graphs
S\subseteq T
.

Proof.

(i)

\Rightarrow

(ii)
. The truth of this implication follows from Lemma
2
.

(ii)

\Rightarrow

(i)
. Let
A
be a nonempty bounded subset of
V(T)
.
We must prove that
A
is a finite subset of
V(T)
.

Let
H_{A}
be the hull of
A
. Proposition
3
implies that
A
is a finite set if and only if
H_{A}
is a finite graph.

By Proposition
1
the graph
H_{A}
is infinite if and only we have

R\subseteq H_{A}

(41)

for some ray
R
, or

S\subseteq H_{A}

(42)

for some infinite star graph
S
.

Suppose first that (
41
) holds. Then the ultrametric space

(V(R),d_{l}|_{V(R)\times V(R)})

is locally finite by statement
(ii)
. Hence
V(R)
is unbounded subset of
(V(T),d_{l})
by Proposition
4
.

Furthermore, since
A
is bounded in
(V(T),d_{l})
, the set
V(H_{A})
is also bounded in
(V(T),d_{l})
by Lemma
5
. Thus, we have obtained a contradiction: the unbounded set
V(R)
is a subset of the bounded set
V(H_{A})
.

Therefore, there are no rays
R
satisfying inclusion (
41
).

By arguing in a similar way and using Proposition
5
instead of Proposition
4
, one can show that inclusion (
42
) is also impossible for infinite star graphs
S
.

Thus,
H_{A}
is a finite graph.

This completes the proof.

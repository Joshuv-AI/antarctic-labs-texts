Birational properties of word varieties
Tatiana Bandman, Boris Kunyavskiĭ, Alexei N. Skorobogatov

Abstract
We prove that the subvariety of SL ⁡ ( 2 ) × SL ⁡ ( 2 ) {\rm{SL}}(2)\times{\rm{SL}}(2) given by the matrix equation w ⁡ ( X , Y ) = α w(X,Y)=\alpha , where w w is a word in two letters, is closely related to an explicit smooth conic bundle over the associated ‘trace surface’ in the 3-dimensional affine space. When w w is the commutator word, we show that this variety can be irrational if the ground field k k is not algebraically closed, answering a question of Rapinchuk, Benyash-Krivetz, and Chernousov. When k k is a number field, it satisfies weak approximation with the Brauer–Manin obstruction.

Introduction

Let
w(x,y)
be a non-trivial word in two letters
x
and
y
, that is, an element of the free
group
{\mathcal{F}}_{2}
with two generators.
Let
k
be a field of characteristic different from 2.
For
\alpha\in{\rm{SL}}(2)(k)
we define the
word variety

S_{w,\alpha}
as the closed subvariety of
{\rm{SL}}(2)\times{\rm{SL}}(2)
given by the matrix equation
w(A,B)=\alpha
. For a survey of the extensive literature on word equations, see
[
GKP18
]
.
The motivating question of this paper concerns birational properties of
S_{w,\alpha}
.
We also obtain results about
k
-rational points on
S_{w,\alpha}
when
k
is a number field.

One much studied example is the commutator
variety
ABA^{-1}B^{-1}=\alpha
, see
[
Tho61
]
,
[
RBKC96
]
and,
more recently,
[
LL21
]
,
[
GS22
,
GMS21
]
.
When
\alpha
is semisimple and non-central,
we show that the commutator variety is a dense open subset of a smooth conic bundle
(a Severi–Brauer scheme of relative dimension 1)
over the Markoff surface in
{\mathbb{A}}^{3}_{k}
with equation

s^{2}+t^{2}+u^{2}-stu={\rm tr}(\alpha)+2.

(1)

The associated Brauer class is
(s^{2}-4,{\rm tr}(\alpha)-2)
; it
generates the unramified Brauer group of the
‘generic’ Markoff surface modulo
{\rm{Br}}(k)
.
In particular, the commutator variety
is birationally equivalent to the affine subvariety of
{\mathbb{A}}^{5}_{k}
given by

s^{2}+t^{2}+u^{2}-stu-({\rm tr}(\alpha)+2)=s^{2}-4+({\rm tr}(\alpha)-2)x^{2}-y^{2}=0.

We prove that the commutator variety is
k
-rational if and only if
the Markoff surface (
1
) is
k
-rational, which is the case if and only if either
{\rm tr}(\alpha)-2
or

{\rm tr}(\alpha)^{2}-4
is a square in
k
, see Theorem
3.4
.
In particular, for ‘general’
\alpha\in{\rm{SL}}(2)(k)
the commutator variety is irrational over
k
.
This gives a negative answer to a question of
Rapinchuk, Benyash-Krivetz, and Chernousov
[
RBKC96
, page 50]
.
When
k
is a number field, we show that
the Brauer–Manin obstruction is the only obstruction to weak approximation
on smooth and proper models of the commutator variety, see Proposition
3.5
.

Our method is based on the systematic use of the
‘trace polynomial’ of the word
w(x,y)
. This is very classical and goes back to Vogt, Klein, and Fricke.
Using the Cayley–Hamilton theorem it is easy to see that the function

{\rm{SL}}(2)\times{\rm{SL}}(2)\to{\mathbb{A}}^{1}_{k}
given by
{\rm tr}(w(A,B))
is a polynomial
P_{w}(s,t,u)
in
the variables

s={\rm tr}(A)
,
t={\rm tr}(B)
,
u={\rm tr}(AB)
. We define the
trace surface

H_{w,a}
, for
a\in k
,
as the surface in
{\mathbb{A}}^{3}_{k}
given by
P_{w}(s,t,u)=a
. If
w(x,y)
belongs to the commutator
subgroup of the free group on two letters
x
and
y
, and
\alpha\in{\rm{SL}}(2)(k)
is semisimple and non-central
with
{\rm tr}(\alpha)=a
, the word variety
S_{w,\alpha}
is a torsor over the trace surface
H_{w,a}

for the norm 1 torus associated to
the splitting field
k(\sqrt{a^{2}-4})
of the characteristic polynomial of
\alpha
.
This implies that
S_{w,\alpha}
is a dense open subset of an
explicit smooth conic bundle over
H_{w,a}
, see Theorem
3.2
.
This largely reduces the study of solutions of the word equation
w(A,B)=\alpha
to the study
of rational points on the affine surface
H_{w,a}
.

Preliminaries

1.1
Reduction of the structure group

For the reader’s convenience we recall the definition of the push-forward
of torsors, see
[
Sko01
, §2.2]
and
[
Gir71
, Proposition III.3.2.1]
.
Let
G_{1}
be a closed subgroup of an affine algebraic group

G
over a field
k
.
Let
Z
be a variety over
k
and let
{\cal T}_{1}\to Z
be a right
Z
-torsor for
G_{1}
for the étale topology.
The class of this torsor is an element
[{\cal T}_{1}]
of the étale cohomology set
{\rm H}^{1}_{\rm{\acute{e}t}}(Z,G_{1})
.
The
push-forward
of
{\cal T}_{1}
along the morphism
G_{1}\hookrightarrow G

is the quotient
{\cal T}
of
{\cal T}_{1}\times_{k}G
by the diagonal action of
G_{1}
,
where
G_{1}
acts on
{\cal T}_{1}
on the right and on
G
on the left.
Then
{\cal T}\to Z
inherits a right action of
G
making it a
Z
-torsor for
G
.
This gives a map of pointed sets
{\rm H}^{1}_{\rm{\acute{e}t}}(Z,G_{1})\to{\rm H}^{1}_{\rm{\acute{e}t}}(Z,G)

that sends
[{\cal T}_{1}]
to
[{\cal T}]
. When
Z
is
{\rm{Spec}}(k)
, the étale cohomology sets
become Galois cohomology sets.

If a
Z
-torsor
{\cal T}\to Z
for
G
is the push-forward
of some
Z
-torsor for
G_{1}
, then one says that
{\cal T}

lifts
to a
torsor for
G_{1}
, or that the structure
group of
{\cal T}

reduces
to
G_{1}
.

If
G_{1}
is commutative, every right
Z
-torsor
for
G_{1}
is also a left
Z
-torsor for
G_{1}
. (In general, a right torsor for
G_{1}
is a left torsor for
an inner form of
G_{1}
.) In this case we have the following criterion for the reduction of the structure
group from
G
to
G_{1}
.

Proposition 1.1

Let
G_{1}
be a closed subgroup of an affine algebraic group
G
defined over a field
k
.
Assume that
G_{1}
is commutative. Let
Z
be a variety over
k

and let
{\cal T}
be a right
Z
-torsor for
G
. The structure group of
{\cal T}
reduces
to
G_{1}
if and only if there is a
G
-equivariant morphism of
Z
-schemes

{\cal T}\to(G_{1}\backslash G)\times_{k}Z
.

Proof.
Let
{\cal T}_{1}
be a right
Z
-torsor for
G_{1}
. Let
{\cal T}=({\cal T}_{1}\times_{k}G)/G_{1}

be the quotient by the diagonal action of
G_{1}
,
which acts on
{\cal T}_{1}
on the right and on
G
on the left. Thus
{\cal T}
has a right action
of
G
making it a
Z
-torsor for
G
, but also a left action of
G_{1}
. Let
G_{1}\backslash{\cal T}

be the quotient by this left action of
G_{1}
. We have isomorphisms

G_{1}\backslash{\cal T}\cong G_{1}\backslash({\cal T}_{1}\times_{k}G)/G_{1}\cong Z\times_{k}(G_{1}\backslash G),

since the two actions of
G_{1}
obviously commute, and
G_{1}\backslash{\cal T}_{1}=Z
.

Conversely, let
\varphi\colon{\cal T}\to(G_{1}\backslash G)\times_{k}Z
be a

G
-equivariant morphism of
Z
-schemes, with
G
acting on the right.
Let
x_{0}
be the
k
-point of

G_{1}\backslash G
given by the identity element of
G(k)
. Note that the right action of
G_{1}
on

G_{1}\backslash G
preserves
x_{0}
.
Thus
{\cal T}_{1}:=\varphi^{-1}(x_{0}\times_{k}Z)
is a closed subvariety of
{\cal T}
stable under the
right action of
G_{1}
so that
{\cal T}_{1}\hookrightarrow{\cal T}
is compatible with the injective
homomorphism
G_{1}\hookrightarrow G
. Since
{\cal T}
is a right
Z
-torsor for
G
,
this implies that
{\cal T}_{1}
is a right
Z
-torsor for
G_{1}

such that
{\cal T}
is the push-forward of
{\cal T}_{1}
along
G_{1}\hookrightarrow G
.
\Box

1.2
Norm
1
tori for quadratic extensions

Let
k
be a field of characteristic different from 2. For a quadratic extension
L/k
we write

R_{L/k}({\mathbb{G}}_{m,L})
for the Weil restriction of
{\mathbb{G}}_{m,L}
.
The attached norm 1 torus
R^{1}_{L/k}({\mathbb{G}}_{m,L})
is defined by the exact sequence of
k
-tori

1\to R^{1}_{L/k}({\mathbb{G}}_{m,L})\to R_{L/k}({\mathbb{G}}_{m,L})\xrightarrow{\rm N}{\mathbb{G}}_{m,k}\to 1,

(2)

where N is induced by the norm map
L\to k
. As a variety,
R^{1}_{L/k}({\mathbb{G}}_{m,L})

is the affine conic
x^{2}-ay^{2}=1
, where
a\in k^{\times}
is such that
L=k(\sqrt{a})
.
The long exact sequence
of Galois cohomology groups attached to (
2
), by Hilbert’s Theorem 90, gives rise to
a canonical isomorphism

{\rm H}^{1}(k,R^{1}_{L/k}({\mathbb{G}}_{m,L}))\cong k^{\times}/{\rm N}(L^{\times}).

This group classifies isomorphism classes of
k
-torsors for
R^{1}_{L/k}({\mathbb{G}}_{m,L})
.
Such a torsor is an affine conic
x^{2}-ay^{2}=b
, where
b\in k^{\times}
, with the
obvious action of
R^{1}_{L/k}({\mathbb{G}}_{m,L})
. Its class
in
{\rm H}^{1}(k,R^{1}_{L/k}({\mathbb{G}}_{m,L}))
is the image of
b
in
k^{\times}/{\rm N}(L^{\times})
.

1.3
Affine and projective conics

Let
Z
be a
k
-variety and let
{\cal T}\to Z
be a right
Z
-torsor for
{\rm{PGL}}(n)
.
To the class
[{\cal T}]\in{\rm H}^{1}_{\rm{\acute{e}t}}(Z,{\rm{PGL}}(n))
one canonically associates an element
\partial([{\cal T}])

of the (cohomological) Brauer group

{\rm{Br}}(Z)={\rm H}^{2}_{\rm{\acute{e}t}}(Z,{\mathbb{G}}_{m})
, where
\partial
is the connecting map
attached to the central extension of algebraic groups

1\to{\mathbb{G}}_{m}\to{\rm{GL}}(n)\to{\rm{PGL}}(n)\to 1.

To the torsor
{\cal T}\to Z
one associates a Severi–Brauer
Z
-scheme of relative dimension
n-1
,
as follows. The standard
n
-dimensional representation of
{\rm{GL}}(n)

gives rise to a transitive action of
{\rm{PGL}}(n)
on the projective space
{\mathbb{P}}^{n-1}_{k}
, which we
write as a left action.
Let
X
be the quotient of
{\cal T}\times_{k}{\mathbb{P}}^{n-1}_{k}
by the diagonal action of
{\rm{PGL}}(n)
,
acting on
{\cal T}
on the right and on
{\mathbb{P}}^{n-1}_{k}
on the left.
The
Z
-scheme
X
is étale locally isomorphic to
Z\times_{k}{\mathbb{P}}^{n-1}_{k}
, so
X\to Z
is a
Severi–Brauer scheme of relative dimension
n-1
.
The class of
X
in
{\rm{Br}}(Z)
is defined as
[X]=\partial([{\cal T}])\in{\rm{Br}}(Z)
.

Lemma 1.2

Let
Z
be a variety over
k
, let
{\cal T}\to Z
be a right
Z
-torsor for
{\rm{PGL}}(n)
, and let

X=({\cal T}\times_{k}{\mathbb{P}}^{n-1}_{k})/{\rm{PGL}}(n)
be the attached Severi–Brauer
Z
-scheme
of relative dimension
n-1
. If

{\cal T}
lifts to a
Z
-torsor
{\cal T}_{1}
for a maximal
k
-torus
T\subset{\rm{PGL}}(n)
, then
there is an open embedding of
Z
-schemes
{\cal T}_{1}\hookrightarrow X
with dense image.

Proof.
Since
{\cal T}
is the push-forward of
{\cal T}_{1}
, we have canonical isomorphisms

X=({\cal T}\times_{k}{\mathbb{P}}^{n-1}_{k})/{\rm{PGL}}(n)\cong({\cal T}_{1}\times_{k}{\rm{PGL}}(n)\times_{k}{\mathbb{P}}^{n-1}_{k})/(T\times_{k}{\rm{PGL}}(n))\cong({\cal T}_{1}\times_{k}{\mathbb{P}}^{n-1}_{k})/T,

where
T
acts on
{\mathbb{P}}^{n-1}_{k}
on the left as a subgroup of
{\rm{PGL}}(n)
.
Indeed, the actions of
T
and
{\rm{PGL}}(n)
on
{\cal T}_{1}\times_{k}{\rm{PGL}}(n)\times_{k}{\mathbb{P}}^{n-1}_{k}
commute,
as immediately follows from their definitions.

The restriction of the action of
{\rm{PGL}}(n)
on
{\mathbb{P}}^{n-1}_{k}
to
T

has a dense orbit on which
T
acts freely.
Fixing a
k
-point in this orbit gives an open embedding
T\hookrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}^{n-1}_{k}
.
We identify
T
with the image of this embedding, so that
T
acts on itself by translations.
Twisted by
{\cal T}_{1}
, the embedding
T\hookrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}^{n-1}_{k}
gives rise to the desired
open embedding of
Z
-schemes
{\cal T}_{1}\hookrightarrow X
.
\Box

Example 1.3

For
n=2
and
Z={\rm{Spec}}(k)

we can make Lemma
1.2
explicit. Let
T\subset{\rm{PGL}}(2)
be a maximal
k
-torus
and let
T^{\prime}
be its preimage in
{\rm{SL}}(2)
. Since the centre

\mu_{2}
of
{\rm{SL}}(2)
is the 2-torsion subgroup
T^{\prime}[2]\subset T^{\prime}
, the multiplication by 2 map
gives an isomorphism
T^{\prime}\cong T^{\prime}/\mu_{2}\cong T
.
So the maximal
k
-tori in
{\rm{PGL}}(2)
are also the maximal
k
-tori in
{\rm{SL}}(2)
.

For an element
g\in{\rm{SL}}(2)(k)
we write

C_{{\rm{SL}}(2)}(g)
for the centraliser of
g
in
{\rm{SL}}(2)
.
The torus
T\subset{\rm{SL}}(2)
is the centraliser
C_{{\rm{SL}}(2)}(g)
of any non-central
g\in T(k)
.
For a non-central semisimple element
g\in{\rm{SL}}(2)(k)
with trace
t={\rm tr}(g)
we have

T=C_{{\rm{SL}}(2)}(g)\cong R^{1}_{L/k}({\mathbb{G}}_{m,L}),

when
L=k(\sqrt{t^{2}-4})
is a quadratic extension of
k
.
When
t^{2}-4
is a square in
k^{\times}
, we define
L=k\oplus k
. In this case

T=R^{1}_{L/k}({\mathbb{G}}_{m,L})
is the split torus
{\mathbb{G}}_{m,k}
. It is easy to see that every
field extension of
k
of degree at most 2 arises in this way.

Let
L=k(\sqrt{a})
where
a\in k^{\times}
.
A
k
-torsor
{\cal T}_{1}
for
R^{1}_{L/k}({\mathbb{G}}_{m,L})
can be given by

x^{2}-ay^{2}=b,

for some
b\in k^{\times}
. Let
{\cal T}
be a
k
-torsor for
{\rm{PGL}}(2)
which is the push-forward of
{\cal T}_{1}

along any embedding
R^{1}_{L/k}({\mathbb{G}}_{m,L})\hookrightarrow{\rm{PGL}}(2)
, and let

X
be the Severi–Brauer
variety associated to
{\cal T}
. By Lemma
1.2
,
{\cal T}_{1}
is a dense open subset of
X
, so
X

is the projective conic given by the homogenised equation

x^{2}-ay^{2}=bz^{2}.

We note that
{\cal T}_{1}
is the complement to the closed point
{\rm{Spec}}(L)\subset X
given by
z=0
.

Remark 1.4

In the above construction we can replace a maximal torus in
{\rm{PGL}}(2)
by the centraliser
of an element
g\in{\rm{SL}}(2)(k)

such that
{\rm tr}(g)=\pm 2
and

. In this case

.
The restriction of the action of
{\rm{PGL}}(2)
on
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{k}

has a dense orbit isomorphic to
{\mathbb{A}}^{1}_{k}

acts freely. Thus we have an analogue of
Lemma
1.2
in the unipotent case (with the same notation):
if
{\cal T}
lifts to a
Z
-torsor

for

, then

2
Simultaneous similarity of two matrices

2.1
Markoff surfaces and torsors for
{\rm{PGL}}(2)

Let
k
be a field of characteristic different from 2 with algebraic closure
\bar{k}
.
Write

F(s,t,u)=s^{2}+t^{2}+u^{2}-stu-4.

For
d\in k
let
M_{d}\subset{\mathbb{A}}^{3}_{k}
be the affine cubic surface given by
F(s,t,u)=d
,
called a
Markoff surface
.
The surface
M_{d}
is smooth if and only if
d\neq 0
and
d\neq-4
.
The singular cubic surface
M_{0}
, also called the
Cayley cubic
,
has four singular points with coordinates

s,t,u=\pm 2
with the product of signs equal to 1.

The Cayley cubic
M_{0}
naturally arises in the problem of
classification of pairs of
(2\times 2)
-matrices up to simultaneous similarity. Write

f\colon{\rm{SL}}(2)\times_{k}{\rm{SL}}(2)\to{\mathbb{A}}^{3}_{k}

for the morphism sending
A,B\in{\rm{SL}}(2)(\bar{k})
to
(s,t,u)\in{\mathbb{A}}^{3}_{k}(\bar{k})
, where

s={\rm tr}(A),\quad t={\rm tr}(B),\quad u={\rm tr}(AB).

The action of
{\rm{PGL}}(2)
by simultaneous conjugation preserves the fibres of
f
.

The following proposition is well-known.

Proposition 2.1

Let
A,B\in{\rm{SL}}(2)(\bar{k})
. The following properties are equivalent:

(1)

A
and
B
have a common eigenvector;

(2)

\det(AB-BA)=0
;

(3)

{\rm tr}(ABA^{-1}B^{-1})=2
;

(4)

(s,t,u)
is a
\bar{k}
-point of the Cayley cubic
M_{0}
.

These conditions are satisfied when the centralisers of
A
and
B
in
{\rm{PGL}}(2)

have non-trivial intersection.

Proof.
The equivalence of (1) and (2) is
[
She84
, Theorem 3.1]
. The equivalence
of (2) and (3) is elementary, as both statements are equivalent to
1 being a root of the characteristic polynomial of
ABA^{-1}B^{-1}
.
The equivalence of (3) and (4) follows from the Cayley–Hamilton
theorem

A^{2}=sA-I,\quad B^{2}=tB-I,\quad ABAB=uAB-I,

(3)

from which one obtains the Fricke identity

{\rm tr}(ABA^{-1}B^{-1})=s^{2}+t^{2}+u^{2}-stu-2.

(4)

See also
[
Fri83
, Theorem 2.9]
. For the last statement, let
g\neq\pm I
be an element
of
{\rm{SL}}(2)(\bar{k})
commuting with
A
and
B
. The centraliser of
g
in
{\rm{SL}}(2)(\bar{k})

is commutative, hence
AB=BA
, so (2) holds.
\Box

Let
V={\mathbb{A}}^{3}_{k}\setminus M_{0}
.
If we identify the
k
-points of
{\rm{SL}}(2)\times_{k}{\rm{SL}}(2)
with
representations of the free group
{\mathcal{F}}_{2}\to{\rm{SL}}(2)(k)
, then
Proposition
2.1
implies that the open subscheme

{\cal T}:=f^{-1}(V)\subset{\rm{SL}}(2)\times_{k}{\rm{SL}}(2)

parameterises absolutely irreducible representations of
{\mathcal{F}}_{2}
, that is, those
that remain irreducible over
\bar{k}
.

Lemma 2.2

The morphism
{\cal T}\to V
is a torsor for
{\rm{PGL}}(2)
for the étale topology.

Proof.
This is proved in
[
N00
, Corollaries 6.5, 6.8]
.
\Box

The class of
{\cal T}
is an element
[{\cal T}]
of the étale cohomology set
{\rm H}^{1}_{\rm{\acute{e}t}}(V,{\rm{PGL}}(2))
.

2.2
Affine and projective conic bundles

As in §
1.3
, we associate to the torsor
{\cal T}\to V
a
Severi–Brauer scheme
X\to V
of relative dimension 1, that is, a smooth
conic bundle. We would like to compute
[X]\in{\rm{Br}}(V)
.

Let
K=k(s,t,u)
be the field of functions on
{\mathbb{A}}^{3}_{k}
. Because of the canonical embedding

{\rm{Br}}(V)\subset{\rm{Br}}(K)
, see
[
CTS21
, Theorem 3.5.5]
,
the class
[X]\in{\rm{Br}}(V)
is uniquely determined by
the class
[X_{K}]\in{\rm{Br}}(K)
, so it is enough to compute the
K
-conic
X_{K}
.

Lemma 2.3

The structure group
{\rm{PGL}}(2)_{K}

of the generic fibre
{\cal T}_{K}
of
{\cal T}\to V
can be reduced to

T=R^{1}_{L/K}({\mathbb{G}}_{m,L})
, where
L=K(\sqrt{t^{2}-4})

and
T
is embedded into
{\rm{PGL}}(2)_{K}
as the centraliser of an element of
{\rm{SL}}(2)_{K}

of trace
t\in K
.

Proof.
Let
g\in{\rm{SL}}(2)(K)
be a matrix of trace
t\in K
.
The field extension
L/K
is the splitting field of the characteristic polynomial of
g
, and thus
the centraliser of
g
in
{\rm{SL}}(2)_{K}
is isomorphic to
T
.
As above,
T\cong T/\mu_{2}
is also the centraliser of
g
in
{\rm{PGL}}(2)_{K}
, giving
an embedding
T\hookrightarrow{\rm{PGL}}(2)_{K}
.

The projection to the second factor
{\rm{SL}}(2)\times_{k}{\rm{SL}}(2)\to{\rm{SL}}(2)
sends the generic
fibre
{\cal T}_{K}
to the
{\rm{PGL}}(2)_{K}
-orbit of
g\in{\rm{SL}}(2)(K)
, where
{\rm{PGL}}(2)_{K}

acts by conjugation. Let
{\cal T}_{1}
be the preimage of
g
in

The trace surface

.
Now (ii) follows from Theorem

(ii).

(iii) This follows from Theorem
3.2
(ii) as

(iv) To prove that

is

-unirational.
We start by recalling the well-known

For this it is enough to construct a rational curve

\phi\colon M^{\prime}_{d}\to{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{k}
, because the pullback

is a conic bundle
with a section, hence

-rational variety dominating
M^{\prime}_{d}
.
We can take

to be the line
r=t=0
. Indeed, the
\bar{k}
-fibres of
\phi

residual conics to this line, so

is a double section of
\phi
.

The
k

. As we have seen above,

. Since

y_{0}
on

. Thus
we have a dominant morphism

of generic degree 2,
where
D
is a smooth, projective, and

-point
y_{0}
such that

. The

. But
(g^{*}{\cal A})(y_{0})={\cal A}(x_{0})=0

.
By (i) this implies that the pullback of the conic bundle
X_{d}\to M_{d}
to

has a section,

-rational.

If

The existence of a
k
-point in
S_{\alpha}
is a particular case of a general result due to
R.C. Thompson

[
Tho61
, Theorem 2]
. When
k
is a number field, our method can be used to prove
a local-to-global statement for rational points on
S_{\alpha}
.
Variants of the following proof can be found in the existing literature, see
[
CTPS
, Remark 6.4]
.

Recall that a variety over a field
k
is
split
if it contains an open geometrically integral

k
-subscheme, see
[
CTS21
, Definition 10.1.3]
.

Proposition 3.5

Let
k
be a number field.
Let
\alpha\in{\rm{SL}}(2)(k)
be a non-central semisimple element.
The Brauer–Manin obstruction is the only obstruction to
weak approximation on any smooth and proper variety birationally equivalent to

S_{\alpha}
. Moreover, if we exclude the case when

{\rm tr}(\alpha)+2
is a square in
k
but
{\rm tr}(\alpha)-2
is not,
then
S_{\alpha}
satisfies weak approximation.

Proof.

Using Hironaka’s theorem, we can find a smooth, proper, geometrically integral variety
X^{\prime}_{d}

over
k
that contains
X_{d}
as an open subset. Moreover, we can choose
X^{\prime}_{d}
such
that there is a morphism
\varphi\colon X^{\prime}_{d}\to M^{\prime}_{d}
extending
X_{d}\to M_{d}
.

Let
x
be a point of
M^{\prime}_{d}
of codimension 1. The irreducibility of
X^{\prime}_{d}
implies that

\varphi^{-1}(x)
is a curve.
Thus the restriction of
\varphi
to
{\rm{Spec}}(R)
, where
R
is the local ring of
x
in

M^{\prime}_{d}
, is proper and flat (by miracle flatness, since all fibres have dimension 1).
We claim that
\varphi^{-1}(x)
is split.

By
[
CTS21
, Proposition 10.1.12]
, the splitness
of the fibre at
x
does not depend on the choice of a regular integral scheme, proper and flat over

{\rm{Spec}}(R)
, with the same generic fibre as
X_{d}\to M_{d}
. By Theorem
3.4
(i)
this generic fibre is a smooth conic with class
{\cal A}\in{\rm{Br}}(M^{\prime}_{d})\subset{\rm{Br}}(R)
.
Now
[
CTS21
, Lemma 10.2.1]
gives us such a scheme for which the fibre at
x

is a smooth, hence split conic. (In the notation of
loc. cit.
we have an equation of type I
because an equation of type II would imply that
{\cal A}
is ramified at
x\in{\rm{Spec}}(R)

contrary to the fact that
{\cal A}\in{\rm{Br}}(R)
.) This proves that
\varphi^{-1}(x)
is split.

Let us prove the first statement. Write
\Omega
for the set of places of
k
and let
k_{v}
be the completion of

k
at
v\in\Omega
. Since
{\cal A}\in{\rm{Br}}(M^{\prime}_{d})
and
M^{\prime}_{d}
is proper,
there exists a finite subset
S_{0}\subset\Omega

such that
{\cal A}(P_{v})=0\in{\rm{Br}}(k_{v})
for every
v\notin S_{0}
and every
P_{v}\in M^{\prime}_{d}(k_{v})
,
see
[
CTS21
, Proposition 13.3.1 (iii)]
.

Let
S
be a finite set of places of
k
and let
P_{v}\in X^{\prime}_{d}(k_{v})
,
v\in S
, be
local points coming from an adelic point
(P_{v})_{v\in\Omega}
in the Brauer–Manin set

X^{\prime}_{d}({\bf A}_{k})^{\rm{Br}}
.
We want to approximate
(P_{v})_{v\in S}
by a
k
-point.
Without loss of generality we can assume that
S_{0}\subset S
.
After a small deformation we can arrange that
P_{v}\in X_{d}
, for
v\in S
.

By functoriality of the Brauer–Manin set
we have
(\varphi(P_{v}))_{v\in\Omega}\in M^{\prime}_{d}({\bf A}_{k})^{\rm{Br}}
.
Since
M^{\prime}_{d}
is birationally equivalent to a smooth projective surface
that is a conic bundle over
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{k}

with four degenerate
\bar{k}
-fibres, by a theorem of Salberger and Colliot-Thélène

[
CT90
]
the Brauer–Manin obstruction is the only obstruction to
weak approximation on
M^{\prime}_{d}
. Thus there is a point
Q\in M^{\prime}_{d}(k)
arbitrarily close
to each
\varphi(P_{v})
in the local topology of
k_{v}
, for
v\in S
.
Since
\varphi(P_{v})\in M_{d}(k_{v})
for
v\in S
, we can ensure that
Q\in M_{d}(k)
. Then
the fibre
\varphi^{-1}(Q)
is a smooth projective conic with class
{\cal A}(Q)\in{\rm{Br}}(k)
.
Since
\varphi(P_{v})
is close to
Q
, this conic has a
k_{v}
-point for all
v\in S
.
Since
S_{0}\subset S
,
it also has
k_{v}
-points for all
v\notin S
. By the Minkowski–Hasse theorem,

\varphi^{-1}(Q)
has a

k
-point. Then
\varphi^{-1}(Q)\simeq{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{k}
, so we can find a
k
-point on
\varphi^{-1}(Q)

arbitrarily close to
P_{v}
for
v\in S
.

Let us prove the second statement.
By the results recalled in Section
3.2
, the conditions guarantee that
{\rm{Br}}(M^{\prime}_{d})/{\rm{Br}}(k)

is generated by the image of
{\cal A}
. By Theorem
3.4
(i)
the generic fibre of
\varphi
is a conic with class
[{\cal A}]
, hence
[{\cal A}]
is contained in
the kernel of the natural map

\varphi^{*}\colon{\rm{Br}}(M^{\prime}_{d})\to{\rm{Br}}(X^{\prime}_{d})
, see
[
CTS21
, Proposition 7.1.3]
.
The image of
{\rm{Br}}(X^{\prime}_{d})
in the Brauer group of the generic fibre of
\varphi
comes from
the Brauer group of the function field of
M^{\prime}_{d}
, since this generic fibre is a conic. This
says that the Brauer group
{\rm{Br}}(X^{\prime}_{d})
is vertical
[
CTS21
, Definition 11.1.1]
.
As we saw in the beginning of the proof, the fibres of
\varphi\colon X^{\prime}_{d}\to M^{\prime}_{d}

over the codimension 1 points of
M^{\prime}_{d}
are split. This implies that

\varphi^{*}\colon{\rm{Br}}(M^{\prime}_{d})\to{\rm{Br}}(X^{\prime}_{d})
is surjective
[
CTS21
, Corollary 11.1.6]
.
We conclude that
{\rm{Br}}(X^{\prime}_{d})={\rm{Br}}(k)
, so
there is no Brauer–Manin obstruction on
X^{\prime}_{d}
in this case.
\Box

Remark 3.6

A well-known consequence of Proposition
3.5
is that
smooth and proper varieties birationally equivalent to

satisfy
weak weak
approximation
, that is, weak approximation outside of a finite set of primes.

3.4
Complements

Part (i) of the following proposition can be compared to
a theorem of R.C. Thompson that
S_{-I}(k)\neq\emptyset

if and only if
-1
is a sum of two squares in
k
, see
[
Tho61
, Theorem 1]
.
Next,
[
Tho61
, Theorem 2]
says that
if
k
has more than three elements, then every non-central element of

{\rm{SL}}(2)(k)
is a commutator.
Part (ii) of the following proposition, together with Theorem
3.4
(iv), gives a proof
of this result in the case of an infinite field
k
of characteristic different from 2.

Proposition 3.7

Let
k
be a field of characteristic different from
2
.

(i)
The variety
S_{-I}
is
k
-rational of dimension
3

if
-1
is a sum of two squares in
k
,
otherwise
S_{-I}(k)=\emptyset
so that
S_{-I}
is not
k
-rational.

(ii)
If
\alpha\in{\rm{SL}}(2)(k)
is a non-central element
such that
{\rm tr}(\alpha)=\pm 2
, then
S_{\alpha}
is
k
-rational of dimension
3
.

Proof.
(i) If
A,B\in{\rm{SL}}(2)(\bar{k})
are such that

ABA^{-1}B^{-1}=-I
, then
{\rm tr}(B)={\rm tr}(-B)
, hence
{\rm tr}(B)=0
.
Similarly, we obtain
{\rm tr}(A)=0
. The Fricke identity (
4
) implies that
{\rm tr}(AB)=0
.
Thus
f(S_{-I})
is a subset of
\{(0,0,0)\}
. By Lemma
2.2
,

f^{-1}\big((0,0,0)\big)
is a
k
-torsor for
{\rm{PGL}}(2)
. In particular,
it is not empty with transitive action of
{\rm{PGL}}(2)
, thus
f^{-1}\big((0,0,0)\big)=S_{-I}
.

By Corollary
2.5
the class of this torsor in the Brauer group
{\rm{Br}}(k)

is
(-4,-4)=(-1,-1)
.
By a basic property of quaternion algebras,
(-1,-1)=0
if and only if
-1
is a norm of the quadratic extension
k(\sqrt{-1})
, that is, if and only if
-1
a sum of two squares in
k
,
cf.
[
CTS21
, Proposition 1.1.8]
.

If the
k
-torsor
S_{-I}
is trivial, it is isomorphic to
{\rm{PGL}}(2)
, in particular, it is
k
-rational.
In the opposite case, it has no
k
-points, and hence is not
k
-rational.

(ii) The case
{\rm tr}(\alpha)=2
,
\alpha\neq I
, can be dealt with by an explicit computation.
We can find a basis in which

\alpha=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&1\\
0&1\end{array}\right).

Suppose that
A,B\in{\rm{SL}}(2)(k)
are
(2\times 2)
-matrices such that
ABA^{-1}B^{-1}=\alpha
.
We have
{\rm tr}(B)={\rm tr}(ABA^{-1})={\rm tr}(\alpha B)
, and this implies that
B
is upper-triangular.
Likewise, we obtain that
A
is also upper-triangular. Writing

A=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\lambda&x\\
0&\lambda^{-1}\end{array}\right),\quad B=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\mu&y\\
0&\mu^{-1}\end{array}\right),

we obtain that the matrix equation
ABA^{-1}B^{-1}=\alpha
is equivalent to

x\lambda(1-\mu^{2})-y\mu(1-\lambda^{2})=1.

We conclude that
S_{\alpha}
is birationally equivalent to
{\mathbb{A}}^{3}_{k}
.

If
{\rm tr}(\alpha)=-2
,
\alpha\neq-I
, then our method still works.
Let us explain how to adjust the proof of Theorem
3.2
to this case.
We have
a={\rm tr}(\alpha)=d+2
which gives
d=-4
, so
H_{w,a}=M_{-4}\subset V
.
Let
M_{\sharp}:=M_{-4}\setminus\{(0,0,0)\}
. Let
{\cal T}_{\sharp}\to M_{\sharp}

be the restriction of
{\cal T}\to V
and let
X_{\sharp}\to M_{\sharp}
be the restriction of
X\to V
.

The commutator gives a

{\rm{PGL}}(2)
-equivariant map
w\colon{\rm{SL}}(2)\times{\rm{SL}}(2)\to{\rm{SL}}(2)

sending
{\cal T}_{\sharp}
to
the subvariety of
{\rm{SL}}(2)
whose
\bar{k}
-points are matrices of trace
-2
.
All such matrices, except
-I
, are conjugate to
\alpha
. The preimage
w^{-1}(-I)=S_{-I}

is
f^{-1}\big((0,0,0)\big)
, by the proof of (i). Thus
w({\cal T}_{\sharp})
is contained
in the
{\rm{PGL}}(2)
-orbit of
\alpha

in
{\rm{SL}}(2)
, where
{\rm{PGL}}(2)
acts by conjugation. Let
G
be the centraliser of
\alpha
in

{\rm{PGL}}(2)
. Thus
G\cong{\mathbb{G}}_{a,k}
, in particular,
G
is commutative.
There is a unique
{\rm{PGL}}(2)
-equivariant isomorphism of this orbit of
\alpha

and
G\backslash{\rm{PGL}}(2)
that sends
\alpha
to the trivial coset
x_{0}
of
G
. We obtain
a
{\rm{PGL}}(2)
-equivariant morphism

{\cal T}_{\sharp}\to(G\backslash{\rm{PGL}}(2))\times_{k}M_{\sharp}

of
schemes over
M_{\sharp}
, so we can apply Proposition
1.1

(using that
G
is commutative).
The desired
M_{\sharp}
-torsor for
G
lifting
{\cal T}_{\sharp}
is the preimage of

x_{0}\times_{k}M_{\sharp}
in
{\cal T}_{\sharp}
, but this is exactly
S_{\alpha}
because
f(S_{\alpha})

does not contain
(0,0,0)
. We know that
S_{\alpha}

is a dense open subset of
X_{\sharp}
by Remark
1.4
. Moreover,
by the same remark, the generic fibre of
X_{-4}\to M_{-4}
is isomorphic to the projective
line over the function field of
M_{-4}
, so
S_{\alpha}
is birationally equivalent to

M_{-4}\times_{k}{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{k}
. Finally,
M_{-4}
is
k
-rational as a cubic surface with
a double
k
-point. Thus
S_{\alpha}
is birationally equivalent to
{\mathbb{A}}^{3}_{k}
.

Proposition 3.9

The variety
S_{I}
of commuting pairs of elements of

.

Proof.

Motzkin and Taussky proved that

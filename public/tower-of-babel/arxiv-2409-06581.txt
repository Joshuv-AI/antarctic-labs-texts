Velocity surface disorder of large deviation rates for high-dimensional random walk in strongly mixing environment
Jiaming Chen

Abstract
In this work, we establish the large deviation principles of random walk in strongly mixing environments. We show that the quenched and annealed rate functions share the same zero-set whose shape is either a singleton point or a line segment, with an illustrative example communicated and given by F. Rassoul-Agha. Furthermore, this paper reveals that under sufficiently low disorder, the two rate functions match on compact sets of both the interior and the boundary of the domain under finitely strongly mixing condition.

Introduction

Consider a discrete-time random walk in mixing and uniformly elliptic random environments (RWRE). The RWRE provides a natural setting for the study of statistical mechanics in random medium. Emerging from a wide range of applications such as DNA chain replication
[
16
]
and crystal growth
[
59
,
60
]

The one-dimensional RWRE was proposed by Solomon
[
57
]
and Sinai
[
56
]
, and is by now well understood. Fundamental results on transience and recurrence were established by Solomon
[
57
]
in i.i.d. environment and later extended to ergodic environment by Alili
[
1
]
. The equivalence of invariant densities was shown by Kozlov
[
37
]
and a subsequent proof can be found in Bolthausen/Sznitman
[

. Further developments include the quenched moderate deviations by Hong/Wang
[

]
. For a comprehensive account of other limit theorems, we refer to the survey by Zeitouni
[
65
]
in
d=1
.

Multi-dimensional RWRE remains significantly more challenging. In this work, assuming fairly general mixing conditions on the environment, we derive exponential estimates for rare events from both quenched and annealed laws, and discern conditions under which they are equal. Over the years there has been advance on the study of law of large numbers (LLN) by Berger
[
5
]

under cone-mixing conditions with extra ballistic and integrability requirements. The central limit theorem (CLT) has been successfully investigated by Berger/Zeitouni
[
7
]
, Kipnis/Varadhan
[

]
, Lawler
[
40
]
, Papanicolaou/Varadhan
[

[

under strong mixing conditions with transience requirements. While progress has been made on the classical limit theorems, many aspects of the large-scale behavior are still not fully understood under general mixing structures.

Various other RWRE models have also been investigated regarding their invariance principle. This includes studies of random walk in balanced environment (e.g., Lawler

Although the quenched and the annealed laws exhibit identical limiting behavior at the level of LLN, their asymptotics may diverge significantly regarding CLT or large deviation principles (LDP). For the RWRE
(X_{n})_{n\geq 0}
on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
with
d\geq 1
, the LDP characterizes the asymptotics
\lim n^{-1}\log P_{0,\omega}(n^{-1}X_{n}\approx x)\simeq I_{q}(x)
and
\lim n^{-1}\log P_{0}(n^{-1}X_{n}\approx x)\simeq I_{a}(x)
where
I_{q}
and
I_{a}
denote the quenched and annealed rate functions, respectively. While the quenched law
P_{0,\omega}
and annealed law
P_{0}
exhibit similar behaviors across a wide class of environments, their LDP rate functions may still have different values. The goal of this work is to establish such LDPs under mixing environments and to demonstrate that their rate functions
I_{q}(\dotproduct)
and
I_{a}(\dotproduct)
are equal on compact sets at sufficiently low level of environmental disorder.

The existence of quenched and annealed LDP was displayed by Rassoul-Agha
[
46
]
for a mild class of mixing environments originally introduced by Dobrushin/Shlosman
[
21
]
in the context of high temperature spin-glass systems. To reconstruct or generalize Rassoul-Agha’s work, our first contribution is to extend these LDP existence results to two slightly broader classes of mixing environments, respectively called strongly mixing, i.e.
\textbf{(SM)}_{C,g}
, and Guo’s strongly mixing, i.e.
\textbf{(SMG)}_{C,g}
. Along with simplified arguments, this part of our work resonates with Richard Feynman’s phrase: “There is pleasure in recognising old things from a new viewpoint”
[
23
]
.

To this recognition, we address the intricate question of the discrepancy between the quenched and annealed rate functions under the
finitely
strongly mixing condition, i.e.
\textbf{(SMF)}_{C,g}
. The primary contribution of this paper is to show that, away from certain singular regions [corners, origin, etc.], the quenched and annealed LDP rate functions
I_{q}
and
I_{a}
coincide at almost every point of the unit disk whenever the level of disorder is sufficiently small. By employing the methods introduced in Section
4
which systematically disentangle the dependence in the environment, we provide a framework that may be applicable to establish similar results for RWRE in time-correlated space-time models as well as time-correlated polymer models, see Yilmaz
[
63

for an LDP on such model with i.i.d. space-time. See also
[
4
,
3
]

Random walk in strongly mixing random environment

Let
(\omega_{x})_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
denote a random environment on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, where each
\omega_{x}=(\omega(x,e))_{e\in\mathbb{V}}
is a probability vector which satisfies
\sum_{e\in\mathbb{V}}\omega(x,e)=1
,
\forall\penalty\ x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. Here we use
\mathbb{V}\coloneqq\{e\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}:\,\absolutevalue{e}_{1}=1\}
. Let
\mathscr{F}_{\Omega}
be the canonical product
\sigma
-algebra on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, and and let
\mathbb{P}
(with expectation
\mathbb{E}\coloneqq E_{\mathbb{P}}
) denote the environmental law on
(\Omega,\mathscr{F}_{\Omega})
, which will be specified later under explicit mixing conditions. Throughout this paper, the environment
(\omega_{x})_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
is assumed to be
uniformly elliptic
, i.e. there exists an absolute constant
0<\kappa<1/(2d)
such that
\mathbb{P}(\omega(x,e)\geq\kappa)=1
with any
x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
,
e\in\mathbb{V}
.

Given any environmental law
(\mathbb{P},\mathscr{F}_{\Omega})
, we first define the level of disorder of the field.

Definition 2.1
.

Given any ergodic environment
\mathbb{P}
, the disorder
\text{dis}(\mathbb{P})\coloneqq\inf\{\epsilon>0:\,\frac{\omega(x,e)}{\mathbb{E}[\omega(x,e)]}\in[1-\epsilon,1+\epsilon],\,\forall\penalty\ e\in\mathbb{V}\;\text{and}\;x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d},\;\mathbb{P}\text{-a.s.}\}
is defined as the level of environmental fluctuations deviated from the mean of the environment.

To explicitly describe the mixing conditions on
(\mathbb{P},\mathscr{F}_{\Omega})
, we first introduce some new notation. Let
d_{1}(A,B)
stands for the
\ell^{1}
-distance between sets
A,B\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. For a fixed constant
r>0
, the law
(\mathbb{P},\mathscr{F}_{\Omega})
is assumed
r
-
Markovian
throughout this paper, i.e.

\mathbb{P}\big((\omega_{x})_{x\in V}\in\dotproduct\big|\mathscr{F}_{V^{c}}\big)=\mathbb{P}\big((\omega_{x})_{x\in V}\in\dotproduct\big|\mathscr{F}_{\partial^{r}V}\big),\qquad\forall\penalty\ \text{finite}\penalty\ V\penalty\ {\subseteq}\penalty\ \mathbb{Z}^{d},\qquad\mathbb{P}\text{-a.s.,}

where
\partial^{r}V\coloneqq\{z\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\backslash V:\,\exists\penalty\ y\in V\penalty\ \text{s.t.}\penalty\ \absolutevalue{z-y}_{1}\leq r\}
and
\mathscr{F}_{\Lambda}\coloneqq\sigma(\omega_{x},\,x\in\Lambda)
for any
\Lambda\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{d}
.

The simplest scenario arises when there is no correlation among the vertices
(\omega_{x})_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
: Specifically, the condition
(IID)
holds if
(\omega_{x})_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
are i.i.d. under
\mathbb{P}
. The situation becomes significantly more involved when the lattice correlation emerges. Given absolute positive constants
C
and
g
, the environment
(\mathbb{P},\mathscr{F}_{\Omega})
satisfies the strong mixing condition
\textbf{(SM)}_{C,g}
if

\frac{d\mathbb{P}((\omega_{x})_{x\in\Delta}\in\dotproduct|\eta)}{d\mathbb{P}((\omega_{x})_{x\in\Delta}\in\dotproduct|\eta^{\prime})}\leq\exp\bigg(C\sum_{x\in\partial^r\Delta,y\in\partial^rA}e^{-g\abs{x-y}_1}\bigg),\qquad\forall\penalty\ \text{finite}\penalty\ \Delta\subseteq V\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{d}\quad\text{with}\penalty\ d_{1}(\Delta,V^{c})\geq r

(2.1)

and
A\subseteq V^{c}
, holds simultaneously for all pairs of configurations
\eta,\eta^{\prime}\in\Omega
which agree on
V^{c}\backslash A
,
\mathbb{P}
-a.s. An alternative, slightly different mixing condition introduced by X. Guo
[
31
]
, is defined as follows. Given
C
and
g
as above,
(\mathbb{P},\mathscr{F}_{\Omega})
satisfies the Guo’s strong mixing condition
\textbf{(SMG)}_{C,g}
if

\frac{d\mathbb{P}((\omega_{x})_{x\in\Delta}\in\dotproduct|\eta)}{d\mathbb{P}((\omega_{x})_{x\in\Delta}\in\dotproduct|\eta^{\prime})}\leq\exp\bigg(C\sum_{x\in\Delta,y\in A}e^{-g\abs{x-y}_1}\bigg),\qquad\forall\penalty\ \text{finite}\penalty\ \Delta\subseteq V\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{d}\quad\text{with}\penalty\ d_{1}(\Delta,V^{c})\geq r

(2.2)

and
A\subseteq V^{c}
, holds simultaneously for all pairs of configurations
\eta,\eta^{\prime}\in\Omega
which agree on
V^{c}\backslash A
,
\mathbb{P}
-a.s. A more localized mixing condition captures the scenario where correlation decays over distance and vanish beyond a finite range. Specifically, we say
(\mathbb{P},\mathscr{F}_{\Omega})
satisfies the finitely strong mixing condition
\textbf{(SMF)}_{C,g}
if each pair
\omega_{x}
and
\omega_{y}
are independent
only if

\absolutevalue{x-y}\geq L_{0}
for some absolute
L_{0}=L_{0}(C,g)>0
.

It is clear that under
\textbf{(SMF)}_{C,g}
, the environment
\mathbb{P}
exhibits finite-range dependence, and is thus a more specific condition than either
\textbf{(SM)}_{C,g}
or
\textbf{(SMG)}_{C,g}
. Indeed, under
\textbf{(SMF)}_{C,g}
, the variables
\omega_{x}
and
\omega_{y}
can be dependent only when
\absolutevalue{x-y}_{1}\geq L_{0}
for some
L_{0}=L_{0}(C,g)
. Hence the Radon–Nikodým derivative satisfies
\frac{d\mathbb{P}((\omega_{x})_{x\in\Delta}\in\dotproduct|\eta)}{d\mathbb{P}((\omega_{x})_{x\in\Delta}\in\dotproduct|\eta^{\prime})}=1
whenever
d_{1}(\Delta,A)\geq L_{0}
, holding simultaneously for any pair of configurations
\eta,\eta^{\prime}\in\Omega
which coincide except for on
A\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. When
d_{1}(\Delta,A)<L_{0}
, the shift-invariance of
\mathbb{P}
together with the finiteness of the box
\{y\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}:\,\absolutevalue{x-y}_{1}<L_{0}\}
implies that both (
2.1
) and (
2.2
) hold automatically. Whence the condition
\textbf{(SMF)}_{C,g}
implies both
\textbf{(SM)}_{C,g}
and
\textbf{(SMG)}_{C,g}
. On the other hand, while
\textbf{(SMG)}_{C,g}
appears to be more general asymptotically, it is not implied by
\textbf{(SM)}_{C,g}
. Nonetheless,
\textbf{(SMG)}_{C,g}
is technically more demanding to work with in asymptotic analyses.

It should also be noted that condition
\textbf{(SMF)}_{C,g}
is elsewhere referred to as
”finite-range dependence”
in many contexts. Having specified the underlying environment, we now introduce the random walk
(X_{n})_{n\geq 0}
traveling on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
with jump probabilities given by
\omega
, i.e. for each
x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
the quenched law
P_{x,\omega}
(with expectation
E_{x,\omega}\coloneqq E_{P_{x,\omega}}
) of this random walk starting from
x
is prescribed by
P_{x,\omega}(X_{0}=x)=1
and
P_{x,\omega}(X_{n+1}=y+e|X_{n}=y)=\omega(y,e)
for all
y\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
,
e\in\mathbb{V}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Averaging
P_{x,\omega}
over the environmental measure
\mathbb{P}
, we define the annealed law of the random walk in random environment (RWRE) on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
by the semi-direct product

P_{x}(B)\coloneqq\int_{\Omega}P_{x,\omega}(B)\,d\mathbb{P(\omega)},\qquad\forall\penalty\ B\in\mathscr{B}((\mathbb{Z}^{d})^{\mathbb{N}}).

Here
\mathscr{B}((\mathbb{Z}^{d})^{\mathbb{N}})
stands for the Borel
\sigma
-algebra on the path space
(\mathbb{Z}^{d})^{\mathbb{N}}
.

Since the random walk moves between nearest neighbors, the empirical velocity satisfies
|X_{N}/N|_{1}\leq 1
for all
N\geq 1
. And thus we refer to
\mathbb{D}\coloneqq\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\,\absolutevalue{x}_{1}\leq 1\}
as the velocity surface and denote its
(d-2)
-dimensional boundary set by
\partial\mathbb{D}_{d-2}\coloneqq\{x\in\partial\mathbb{D}:\,x_{j}=0\;\,\text{for some}\penalty\ 1\leq j\leq d\}
. In
[
62
]
, Varadhan established that under
(IID)
, the RWRE satisfies the following quenched and annealed large deviation principles on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
for any
d\geq 1
:

•

There exists a rate function
I_{a}:\mathbb{D}\to[0,\infty)
which is convex and continuous on
\text{int}(\mathbb{D})
so that for any Borel
G\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

-\inf_{x\in G^{\circ}}I_{a}(x)\leq\varliminf_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\log P_{0}\bigg(\frac{X_{N}}{N}\in{G}\bigg)\leq\varlimsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\log P_{0}\bigg(\frac{X_{N}}{N}\in{G}\bigg)\leq-\inf_{x\in\overline{G}}I_{a}(x).

•

There exists a deterministic rate function
I_{q}:\mathbb{D}\to[0,\infty)
which is convex and continuous on
\text{int}(\mathbb{D})
such that
\mathbb{P}
-a.s. for any Borel
G\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

-\inf_{x\in G^{\circ}}I_{q}(x)\leq\varliminf_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\log P_{0,\omega}\bigg(\frac{X_{N}}{N}\in{G}\bigg)\leq\varlimsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\log P_{0,\omega}\bigg(\frac{X_{N}}{N}\in{G}\bigg)\leq-\inf_{x\in\overline{G}}I_{q}(x).

Recent advances
[
3
,
4
]
have revealed how the level of disorder dictates the equality between
I_{a}
and
I_{q}
on
\mathbb{D}
. Specifically, when
\mathbb{P}
satisfies
(IID)
there exist disorder thresholds below which the two rate functions:

•

For any dimension
d\geq 4
and compact
\mathcal{K}\subseteq\partial\mathbb{D}\backslash\partial\mathbb{D}_{d-2}
, there exists
\epsilon_{1}=\epsilon_{1}(\mathcal{K})>0
such that
I_{a}(x)=I_{q}(x)
for any
x\in\mathcal{K}
, whenever
\text{dis}(\mathbb{P})<\epsilon_{1}
.

•

For any dimension
d\geq 4
and compact
\mathcal{K}\subseteq\text{int}(\mathbb{D})\backslash\{0\}
, there exists
\epsilon_{2}=\epsilon_{2}(\mathcal{K})>0
such that
I_{a}(x)=I_{q}(x)
for any
x\in\mathcal{K}
, whenever
\text{dis}(\mathbb{P})<\epsilon_{2}
.

Our paper extends these insights to dependent random field in the same context of large deviations of the limiting velocities. Our first main result establishes the general LDP under either
\textbf{(SM)}_{C,g}
or
\textbf{(SMG)}_{C,g}
, and thereby generalizing the results of
[
46
]
to a broader class of correlated environments.

Theorem 2.2
.

For any
d\geq 1
and
\kappa>0
, when
\mathbb{P}
satisfies either
\textbf{(SM)}_{C,g}
or
\textbf{(SMG)}_{C,g}
,

•

There exists a rate function
I_{a}:\mathbb{D}\to[0,\infty)
which is convex and continuous on
\text{int}(\mathbb{D})
such that for any Borel
G\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

-\inf_{x\in G^{\circ}}I_{a}(x)\leq\varliminf_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\log P_{0}\bigg(\frac{X_{N}}{N}\in{G}\bigg)\leq\varlimsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\log P_{0}\bigg(\frac{X_{N}}{N}\in{G}\bigg)\leq-\inf_{x\in\overline{G}}I_{a}(x).

•

There exists a deterministic rate function
I_{q}:\mathbb{D}\to[0,\infty)
which is convex and continuous on
\text{int}(\mathbb{D})
such that
\mathbb{P}
-a.s. for any Borel
G\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

-\inf_{x\in G^{\circ}}I_{q}(x)\leq\varliminf_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\log P_{0,\omega}\bigg(\frac{X_{N}}{N}\in{G}\bigg)\leq\varlimsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\log P_{0,\omega}\bigg(\frac{X_{N}}{N}\in{G}\bigg)\leq-\inf_{x\in\overline{G}}I_{q}(x).

Within the LDP framework, the zero sets of
I_{a}
and
I_{q}
characterize the limiting velocities that exhibit sub-exponential decay under the annealed and quenched laws, respectively. Consequently, the geometry of these zero sets of
I_{a}
and
I_{q}
is of central probabilistic interest. In this direction, we establish the following result concerning their geometric properties.

Theorem 2.3
.

For any
d\geq 1
and
\kappa>0
, when
\mathbb{P}
satisfies either
\textbf{(SM)}_{C,g}
or
\textbf{(SMG)}_{C,g}
, the rate functions
I_{a}
and
I_{q}
share the same zero set, i.e.
\{x\in\mathbb{D}:I_{q}(x)=0\}=\{x\in\mathbb{D}:I_{a}(x)=0\}
. Furthermore, this common zero set is either a single-point or a closed line segment containing the origin.

When the zero set reduces to a singleton in
\mathbb{D}
, this point is not necessarily the origin. Consider an RWRE satisfying
\mathbb{P}
-a.s.,
E_{0,\omega}[\langle X_{1},e\rangle]\geq\delta
,
\exists\penalty\ e\in\mathbb{V}
and
\delta>0
. This condition implies that the directional limiting velocity satisfies
\lim_{N\to\infty}\langle e,X_{N}/N\rangle\geq\delta
forcing both rate functions
I_{a}
and
I_{q}
to be strictly positive at the origin. Instead, they vanish at a unique nonzero limiting velocity. This illustrative construction was suggested by Firas Rassoul-Agha in private conversation.

\mathbb{D}
, particularly whether they coincide under sufficiently low disorder. The following result characterizes this striking phenomenon at the boundary of the velocity surface
\mathbb{D}
when
\mathbb{P}
verifies the finite mixing condition. Here we emphasize that our Theorems

–

hold for

whereas Theorems

For any

Definition 2.5
.

Given an ergodic environment
\mathbb{P}
, the directional imbalance along an axial orientation
s\in\{-1,1\}^{d}
is defined by
\text{imb}_{s}(\mathbb{P})\coloneqq\inf\{\epsilon>0:\,\frac{\sum_{j=1}^{d}\omega(x,s_{j}e_{j})}{\sum_{j=1}^{d}\mathbb{E}[\omega(x,s_{j}e_{j})]}\in[1-\epsilon,1+\epsilon],\,\forall\penalty\ e\in\mathbb{V}\;\text{and}\;x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d},\,\mathbb{P}\text{-a.s.}\}
which quantifies the level of environmental fluctuations restricted to the directional sector specified by
s
. Note that the numerator in this definition equals
P_{0,\omega}(X_{1}\in\partial\mathbb{D}(s))
, and whence the denominator simplifies to
P_{0}(X_{1}\in\partial\mathbb{D}(s))
.

Our next result establishes the equality of
I_{a}
and
I_{q}
under considerably weaker assumptions. Specifically, these rate functions match on nontrivial open subsets of
\partial\mathbb{D}
, provided the environment
\mathbb{P}
exhibits sufficiently small directional imbalance. Intuitively,
\text{imb}_{s}(\mathbb{P})
measures the local asymmetry of environmental fluctuations aligned with the axial direction
s
. We now formulate this main result precisely below.

Theorem 2.6
.

For any
d\geq 4
and
\kappa>0
, when
\mathbb{P}
satisfies
\textbf{(SMF)}_{C,g}
there exist a nontrivial open set
\mathcal{O}\subseteq\partial\mathbb{D}(s)\backslash\partial\mathbb{D}_{d-2}
and a constant
\epsilon^{\dagger}=\epsilon^{\dagger}(\kappa)>0
such that
I_{a}(x)=I_{q}(x)
for all
x\in\mathcal{O}
, whenever
\text{imb}_{s}(\mathbb{P})<\epsilon^{\dagger}
, for any

For each orientation

, we denote by

the unique facet of
\partial\mathbb{D}
to whose outward normal vector is

. Under the stronger condition controlling

, one can establish the equality

and
I_{q}
across arbitrary compact subsets of
\partial\mathbb{D}\backslash\partial\mathbb{D}_{d-2}
. In contrast,
\text{imb}_{s}(\mathbb{P})
acts as a directional, averaged quantifier of disorder along
s
. By replacing
\text{dis}(\mathbb{P})
with this milder directional measure,

demonstrates that controlling environmental fluctuations along a single orientation is already sufficient to guarantee the equality of
I_{a}
and
I_{q}
locally on open subregions of the boundary.

While the equality of
I_{a}
and
I_{q}
on the boundary
\partial\mathbb{D}
illuminates the structural role of disorder, it is equally important to determine whether this equality extends into the interior of the velocity surface. In particular, one naturally explores conditions ensuring that
I_{a}
and
I_{q}
coincide on subregions of
\text{int}(\mathbb{D})
, as asserted in the following result.

Theorem 2.7
.

For any

and compact
\mathcal{K}\subseteq\text{int}(\mathbb{D})\backslash\{0\}
, when
\mathbb{P}
satisfies
\textbf{(SMF)}_{C,g}
there exists
\epsilon_{2}=\epsilon_{2}(\mathcal{K})>0
such that
I_{a}(x)=I_{q}(x)
for all
x\in\mathcal{K}
, whenever
\text{dis}(\mathbb{P})<\epsilon_{2}
.

While controlling the disorder enforces
I_{a}=I_{q}
uniformly on compact subsets of
\text{int}(\mathbb{D})
away from the origin, these two rate functions can never coincide simultaneously on the entire velocity surface
\mathbb{D}
as long as the environment
\mathbb{P}
is genuinely random. This fundamental strict inequality persists even under the strongly mixing condition
\textbf{(SMF)}_{C,g}
, extending a classical result of Yilmaz
[
64
, Proposition 4]
from the i.i.d. setting to dependent environments. The precise statement, established in
[
14
]
is presented below for completeness.

Theorem 2.8
.

For any

and
\kappa>0

\mathbb{P}
satisfies
\textbf{(SMF)}_{C,g}
and its support is not a singleton, i.e.

at some interior point

of the velocity surface.

The strict inequality between
I_{a}
and
I_{q}
originates from their failure to match on the lower-dimensional boundary strata
\partial\mathbb{D}_{d-2}
. whether this strict inequality holds under the broader
\textbf{(SM)}_{C,g}
or
\textbf{(SMG)}_{C,g}
scenarios remains an open question. Beyond these qualitative characterizations of how the level of disorder
\text{dis}(\mathbb{P})
governs the equality between
I_{a}
and
I_{q}
on
\mathbb{D}
, a natural question is whether these results can be refined in more quantitative manners.

A natural viewpoint is to consider scenarios where the environmental randomness is parametrized explicitly. Given a fixed
\alpha:\mathbb{V}\to(0,1)
with
\sum_{e\in\mathbb{V}}\alpha(e)=1
and define the space of admissible fluctuation profiles
\mathcal{E}_{\alpha}\coloneqq\{(r(e))_{e\in\mathbb{V}}\in[-1,1]^{\mathbb{V}}:\,\sum_{e\in\mathbb{V}}\alpha(e)r(e)=0\,\text{and}\,\sup_{e\in\mathbb{V}}\absolutevalue{r(e)}=1\}
. Let
(\eta(x,e))_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d},e\in\mathbb{V}}\in\mathcal{E}^{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}_{\alpha}
be an ergodic and shift-invariant random field under some probability
\mathbb{Q}
, satisfying
E^{\mathbb{Q}}[\eta(x,e)]=0
,
\forall\penalty\ (x,e)\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\times\mathbb{V}
. The spatial correlations of
(\eta(x,e))_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d},e\in\mathbb{V}}
under
\mathbb{Q}
are chosen so that the parametrized environment
\omega_{\epsilon}(x,e)\coloneqq\alpha(e)(1+\epsilon\eta(x,e))
satisfies
(\textbf{SMF})_{C,g}
, or either
(\textbf{SM})_{C,g}
or
(\textbf{SMG})_{C,g}
under different mixing assumptions on
\mathbb{Q}
. In this framework, the law of the parametrized environment
(\omega_{\epsilon}(x,e))_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d},e\in\mathbb{V}}
is governed by
\mathbb{Q}
, whilethe magnitude of disorder is dialed by the numerical parameter
\epsilon>0
, enabling sharper quantitative estimates for the relevant rate functions.

Indeed, this parametrization framework is quite general and subsumes several widely studied models for RWRE such as those in
[
12
,
54
]
. Moreover,
[
3
, Theorem 2.9]
provides the first evidence of a phase transition for
I_{q}-I_{a}
on
\partial\mathbb{D}
w.r.t.
\text{dis}(\mathbb{P})
under i.i.d. parameterized environments. Inspired by these insights, we leave the generalization of such phase transition phenomena to dependent mixing environments, such as
(\textbf{SMF})_{C,g}
,
(\textbf{SM})_{C,g}
or
(\textbf{SMG})_{C,g}
, to future works.

3.2  The quenched large deviation principle.

The proof of the quenched LDP rests on pathwise arguments following
[
62
, Section 2]
which requires only that
\mathbb{P}
be ergodic. Below, we present a streamlined exposition highlighting the key steps of this approach. Let
G_{u,\omega}(t,x,y)\coloneqq-\log\,\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\{e^{-u\absolutevalue{n-t}}P_{x,\omega}(X_{n}=y)\}
,
\forall\penalty\ x,y\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
for any
u>0
and
t\geq 0
. Under uniform ellipticity of
\mathbb{P}
,
G_{u,\omega}
exhibits Lipschitz continuity on
[0,\infty)\times\mathbb{Z}^{d}\times\mathbb{Z}^{d}
with a constant
\text{Lip}(G)>0

\absolutevalue{G_{u,\omega}(t+t^\prime,x+x^\prime,y+y^\prime) - G_{u,\omega}(t,x,y)}\leq\text{Lip}(G)\big(t^{\prime}+|x^{\prime}|_{1}+|y^{\prime}|_{1}\big),

(3.3)

for all such
t,t^{\prime},x,x^{\prime},y,y^{\prime}
in their respective domains. By applying standard subadditive ergodic convergence arguments for stochastic processes with such regularity, we obtain the following lemma.

Lemma 3.3
.

When
\mathbb{P}
satisfies either
\textbf{(SM)}_{C,g}
or
\textbf{(SMG)}_{C,g}

\forall\penalty\ u>0

\mathbb{R}^{d}

\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}N\to\infty\\

First, using the
\text{Lip}(G)

\lim_{N\to\infty}G^{(N)}_{u,\omega}(t,0,\eta)\eqqcolon g(t,\eta)

\tilde{G}_{u}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}

[
62
]

\mathbb{D}
follows from
[

.

\varlimsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\log P_{0,\omega}(X_{N}/N\in F)=\varlimsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\log\sum_{z\in NF\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}}P_{0,\omega}(X_{N}=z)

0\leq\frac{1}{N}\log\sum_{z\in NF\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}}P_{0,\omega}(X_{N}=z)-\max_{z\in NF\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\frac{1}{N}\log P_{0,\omega}(X_{N}=z)\leq\frac{1}{N}\absolutevalue{N F\cap\mathbb{Z}^d}\to 0\qquad\text{as}\quad N\to\infty,

Applying Lemma
3.3

\displaystyle\varlimsup_{N\to\infty}\tfrac{1}{N}\log P_{0,\omega}\big(\tfrac{X_{N}}{N}\in F\big)=\varlimsup_{N\to\infty}\max_{z\in NF\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\tfrac{1}{N}\log P_{0,\omega}(X_{N}=z)\leq\varlimsup_{N\to\infty}\max_{z\in NF\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}}(-1/N)G_{u,\omega}(N,0,z)\underset{u\searrow 0}{\leq}-\inf_{\xi\in F}I_{q}(\xi),

4

from (

4.1

\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\hat{P}^{(\theta)}_{x,y}(d_{1}(\vec{X}^{(\theta)}_{j},\vec{Y}^{(\theta)}_{j})\leq\ell)=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\hat{P}^{(\theta)}_{x,y}(Z_{j}^{*}\in\bar{B}_{\ell})

\bar{B}_{\ell}\coloneqq\{z\in\mathbb{R}^{d-1}:\,\absolutevalue{z}_{1}\leq\ell\}

\mu
denote the law of
Z^{*}_{1}
. By inclusion of events,
\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\hat{P}^{(\theta)}_{x,y}(Z_{j}^{*}\in\bar{B}_{\ell})\leq\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\mu^{*j}(\bar{B}_{\ell})

\mu^{*j}
is the
j
-fold convolution of
\mu
. Consider the function
\prod_{i=1}^{d}f(\ell^{-1}x_{i})
, where
f(x_{i})=\max\{1-\absolutevalue{x_i},0\}

[
58

,

\widehat{\prod_{i=1}^{d-1}f(x_{i})}=\prod_{i=1}^{d-1}\widehat{f}(\xi_{i})\qquad\Longrightarrow\quad\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d-1}}\widehat{\prod_{i=1}^{d-1}f(\ell^{-1}x_{i})}\,d\mu^{*j}(x)=\ell^{d-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d-1}}\prod_{i=1}^{d-1}f(\ell\xi_{i})\chi_{\mu}^{(\theta)}(\xi)^{n}\,d\xi,

where
\widehat{f}(\xi_{i})=2(1-\cos\xi_{i})/\xi_{i}^{2}
and

\theta

For any

C^{\prime\prime}(\Theta)\ell^{2}

C^{\prime\prime}\coloneqq C^{\prime}(\Theta)\ell_{0}^{2}
. Finally,

\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}E_{\hat{P}^{(\theta)}_{x}\otimes\hat{P}^{(\theta)}_{y}}\bigg[N^{\ell}_{j}(\vec{X}^{(\theta)},\vec{Y}^{(\theta)})\mathbbm{1}_{\{d_{1}(\vec{X}^{(\theta)}_{j},\vec{Y}^{(\theta)}_{j})\leq\ell\}}\bigg]\leq\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}E_{Q}\big[\tau^{(L)}_{1}\big]\hat{P}^{(\theta)}_{x}\otimes\hat{P}^{(\theta)}_{y}\big(Z_{j}^{*}\in\bar{B}_{\ell}\big)\leq C(L,\Theta)\ell^{2},\quad{\forall\penalty\ \ell\in\mathbb{N},\penalty\ \theta\in\Theta,}

verifying (
4.1
), which is the only assertion left above.

\partial^{2}_{\theta}\lambda_{N}(\theta)=\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}\mu_{i,j}^{2}\nu_{i,j}^{2}\frac{1}{N}\frac{E_{0}\otimes\widetilde{E}_{0}[\Delta_{i}(S)\Delta_{j}(S)^{T}I_{i,j}\tilde{I}_{i,j}-\Delta_{i}(S)I_{i,j}\Delta_{j}(\widetilde{S})^{T}\tilde{I}_{i,j}]E_{0}[I_{N}^{i,j}]^{2}}{E_{0}[I_{i,j}]^{2}E_{0}[I^{i,j}_{N}]^{2}},\qquad\forall\penalty\ \theta\in\mathbb{R}^{d-1}.

Here
\nu_{i,j}
comes from a correlation in the denominator and has the estimate
1-\text{dis}(\mathbb{P})\leq\nu_{i,j}^{1/2}\leq 1+\text{dis}(\mathbb{P})
,
\forall\penalty\ i,j\leq N
. Let
P^{i,j}_{\theta,N}
be the law absolutely continuous with respect to
P_{0}
with Radon–Nikodým derivative
dP^{i,j}_{\theta,N}/dP_{0}=I_{i,j}/E_{0}[I_{i,j}]
. Then,

\partial^{2}_{\theta}\lambda_{N}(\theta)\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}\mu_{i,j}^{2}\nu_{i,j}^{2}\frac{1}{N}\big(E^{i,j}_{\theta,N}[\Delta_{i}(S)\Delta_{j}(S)^{T}]-E^{i,j}_{\theta,N}[\Delta_{i}(S)]E^{i,j}_{\theta,N}[\Delta_{j}(S)^{T}]\big),\qquad\forall\penalty\ \theta\in\mathbb{R}^{d-1}.

Observe that
E^{i,j}_{\theta,N}[\Delta_{i}(S)\Delta_{j}(S)^{T}]=E^{i,j}_{\theta,N}[\Delta_{i}(S)]E^{i,j}_{\theta,N}[\Delta_{j}(S)^{T}]
whenever
\absolutevalue{i-j}\geq L
, by the nature of
\textbf{(SMF)}_{C,g}
. Thus,

|\partial^{2}_{\theta}\lambda_{N}(\theta)|\leq(1+\text{dis}(\mathbb{P}))^{4}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i,j=1}^{N\wedge L}\big(E^{i,j}_{\theta,N}[\Delta_{i}(S)\Delta_{j}(S)^{T}]-E^{i,j}_{\theta,N}[\Delta_{i}(S)]E^{i,j}_{\theta,N}[\Delta_{j}(S)^{T}]\big)\leq(1+\text{dis}(\mathbb{P}))^{4}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i,j=1}^{N\wedge L}2Id<\infty,

uniformly for all
\theta\in K
, verifying the equi-continuity of
\partial_{\theta}\lambda_{N}(\theta)
on
K
. Now,
\forall\penalty\ \epsilon>0
,
\exists\penalty\ \delta>0
s.t.
|\lambda^{\prime}_{N}(\theta)-\lambda^{\prime}_{N}(\theta^{\prime})|
for any
\theta^{\prime}\in B_{\delta}(\theta)
,
\forall\penalty\ N\geq 1
. Now
\forall\penalty\ \theta\in K
,
\exists\penalty\ N_{\theta}\geq 1
s.t.
|\lambda^{\prime}_{m}(\theta)-\lambda^{\prime}_{n}(\theta)|<\epsilon/3
,
\forall\penalty\ m,n\geq N_{\theta}
. Thus,

|\lambda^{\prime}_{m}(\theta^{\prime})-\lambda^{\prime}_{n}(\theta^{\prime})|\leq|\lambda^{\prime}_{m}(\theta^{\prime})-\lambda^{\prime}_{m}(\theta)|+|\lambda^{\prime}_{m}(\theta)-\lambda^{\prime}_{n}(\theta)|+|\lambda^{\prime}_{n}(\theta^{\prime})-\lambda^{\prime}_{n}(\theta)|<\epsilon,\qquad\forall\penalty\ \theta^{\prime}\in B_{\delta}(\theta),\quad m,n\geq N_{\theta}.

Choosing finitely many
\{\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{k}\}
to cover
K
by
(B_{\delta}(\theta_{i}))_{i=1,\ldots,k}
, we select
N_{U}\coloneqq\max\{N_{\theta_{1}},\ldots,N_{\theta_{k}}\}
and then
|\lambda^{\prime}_{m}(\theta)-\lambda^{\prime}_{n}(\theta)|<\epsilon
,
\forall\penalty\ \theta\in K
and
m,n\geq N_{U}
. And the assertion is then verified.

∎

Combined Single-Shot Intra-articular Injection of Ropivacaine and Contrast Media for Magnetic Resonance Imaging: Surgical Decision Making for Hip Arthroscopy in Young Patients
Authors: Neil Sarna, Gabriel I. Onor, Kathleen E. Schenker, Alvin W. Su
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Introduction:Hip impingement syndrome with labral tear is debilitating for young patients. When magnetic resonance arthrogram (MRA) is equivocal for surgical decision making, an additional intra-articular diagnostic injection (IA) can further confirm the diagnosis. We assessed the effectiveness on surgical decision making by combining hip MRA + IA in one single procedure.Methods:We conducted a retrospective review of a prospectively enrolled cohort of patients with hip impingement syndrome who underwent a combined MRA + IA procedure. A 10-mL cocktail containing 0.2% ropivacaine and Omnipaque 300 contrast media was injected into the hip joint, followed by magnetic resonance scanning, in one single outpatient procedure. Patient demographics and MRA findings were documented. Preinjection pain and immediate postinjection pain were assessed using the visual analog scale (VAS). Surgical finding under hip arthroscopy was documented in those who had positive symptomatic relief after injection.Results:26 patients (7 adolescent boys, 19 adolescent girls, median age 17 years) were analyzed. The VAS score improved from before injection to after injection (5.0 ± 2.6 vs. 2.2 ± 2.1, mean ± SD, P < 0.0001). 1 patient reported no postinjection improvement and had no labral tear identified under hip arthroscopy; all the other 25 patients reported postinjection improvement and had labral tears repaired under hip arthroscopy; 4 among these 25 had negative MRA findings. The average Cam lesion alpha angle was 63.4 ± 7.1°.Conclusions:Combined hip MRA + IA is effective for surgical decision making. One single injection procedure is efficient for both patients and surgeons by eliminating an additional visit and saving costs and lead time for surgical decision making. Avoiding a second needle insertion is also preferable among pediatric and adolescent patients.

The acetabular labrum functions to deepen the acetabular socket, thereby decreasing hip joint contact pressures, maintaining pressurized intra-articular fluid for joint lubrication, and promoting hip stability through its suction effect.1 Injuries to the acetabular labrum in the pediatric population may cause notable pain and alter patients' quality of life and ability to participate in sports and recreation.

Accurate diagnosis of acetabular labral tear remains challenging at times. The prevalence of an acetabular labral tear under magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) in asymptomatic individuals ranges from 2% in the general pediatric population2 to as high as 56% to 89% in the young athletic population.3 On the contrary, the sensitivity and specificity of detecting an acetabular labral tear using MRI, even with a contrast arthrogram (magnetic resonance arthrogram, MRA), are 67% and 79%, respectively.4,5 The patients' clinical history and clinical examination may increase the confidence of diagnosis of acetabular labral tears,6 such as a positive flexion-adduction-internal rotation test and the increase in groin-area pain after prolonged sitting or frequent active motion with the hip in a higher flexion angle.

When the diagnosis is equivocal, diagnostic intra-articular injection (IA) aids in surgical decision making by confirming the intra-articular etiology of hip symptoms.6,7 Positive response to an injection increases the confidence that hip arthroscopy surgery will address the pathology while negative response indicates that the pathology is likely extra-articular,8 thus limiting the benefit of hip arthroscopy. Such diagnostic injection is typically done on a separate occasion than the hip arthrogram. There has been no report exploring a single injection procedure that incorporates both a hip arthrogram and a diagnostic injection, particularly in the pediatric and adolescent population. The aim of this study was to assess the effectiveness of combined MRA and IA on detecting surgical acetabular labral tear, when performed concomitantly using a single injection procedure in one outpatient visit.

Methods

Patient Cohort

The combined hip MRI with intra-articular contrast arthrogram and diagnostic intra-articular injection (combined MRA + IA) was introduced in 2019, under the collaborative effort of orthopaedic surgeons and the interventional and musculoskeletal (IR) radiologists in our institution. From September 2019 to May 2024, patients who required MRA and IA under the diagnosis of hip impingement syndrome were offered the option to receive such a combined diagnostic procedure that would be performed at the authors' institution; of note, all patients had full autonomy should they opt for the IA and MRA to be performed on 2 separate occasions, either inside or outside our institution.

Patients who had this combined MRA + IA done at our institution were prospectively enrolled. Retrospective chart review was conducted on the cohort's demographic information including sex, age at the time of procedure, left or right hip, and their major sports. MRA imaging findings were documented for labral tears, and the maximal alpha angle of the Cam lesion among all reconstruction planes (coronal, sagittal, axial, coronal-oblique, and axial-oblique) was also determined. The pain assessment was also documented as described further.

The Procedure of Combined Magnetic Resonance Arthrogram + Intra-articular Diagnostic Injection and Pain Assessment

On arrival, immediately before the injection procedure, the patient was assessed for preinjection (Pre-Inj) hip pain level using the visual analog scale (VAS) both at rest and after performing provocative maneuvers for hip pain, with 0 being no pain at all and 10 being the worst pain. The higher score (worse pain) was documented as the Pre-Inj VAS score. After application of local anesthetics to the skin and subcutaneous tissue, access to the hip joint was established by percutaneously introducing a 22-gauge spinal needle into the hip joint under fluoroscopy guidance. A tiny amount of Omnipaque 300 was first injected into the joint under fluoroscopic guidance to confirm the intra-articular position of the needle tip. Next, we prepared a 14-mL injection cocktail that consists of (a) 10 mL of a mixture containing 7 mL of 0.2% ropivacaine and 3 mL of Omnipaque 300 and (b) 0.05 mL of Dotarem and 4 mL of normal saline. This cocktail was then injected with intermittent fluoroscopic visualization. For each hip, 10 mL of such cocktail was injected, thus delivering a total dose of 5 mL of 0.2% ropivacaine into the hip joint.

Magnetic resonance (MR) scanning was then performed on a Siemens 1.5T or GE 3T magnet. After completion of the MRA, the postinjection (Post-Inj) pain level was assessed on the VAS scale at rest and after performing provocative maneuvers for hip pain. The higher score (worse pain) was documented as the Post-Inj VAS score. The patient was then sent home with the instruction of resuming daily activities and taking a note on their “subjective report of improvement” by answering the question “Do I feel at least 50% better in my hip? (Yes/No),” within the subsequent 3 days of resuming daily activities after the injection.

The injection result was considered positive, if at least 1 of the 2 following conditions were met: (A) there was immediate decrease in hip pain after injection, reflected by a decrease from the Pre-Inj VAS score to the Post-Inj VAS score, or (B) positive subjective report of improvement was noted in the subsequent 3 days of resuming activities after the injection. The injection result was considered negative if neither (A) nor (B) mentioned above was sufficed.

Surgical Decision Making

The patient was seen back in the surgeon's clinic in 1 week after the combined MRA + IA procedure to make a shared decision on the treatment plan. Hip arthroscopy with labral repair and femoroplasty of Cam resection was recommended if the injection result was positive, regardless of the MRA imaging findings. If the patient had positive MRA findings of labral tear or a Cam lesion with the alpha angle more than 57° but had a negative injection result, a prolonged course of 3 months of rest and physical therapy was recommended before reassessment of the need for a hip arthroscopy.

Statistical Analysis

The Pre-Inj and Post-Inj VAS scores were compared using the Wilcoxon matched-pair signed rank test as the equivalent of the paired t-test for nonparametric data distribution. Descriptive statistics were calculated for patient demographics and the alpha angle of the Cam lesion. Significance was set at α = 0.05.

Results

Combined MRA + IA was done in a total of 26 patients. The median age was 17 years (range, 12 to 34). The cohort comprised 7 adolescent boys and 19 adolescent girls. Improvement in hip pain was observed by comparing the Pre-Inj VAS score of 5.0 ± 2.6 (mean ± SD) with the Post-Inj VAS score of 2.2 ± 2.1 (P < 0.0001, Figure 1). All patients reported subjective improvement except for one (ID# 16). The average alpha angle for the Cam lesion was 63.4 ± 7.1° under MRA measurement (Table 1).

22 patients had a documented ant-sup labral tear under MRA, which was confirmed and repaired under hip arthroscopy. 5 patients had no documented labral tear under MRA. 4 of these 5 patients had an intraoperative finding of labral tear, and each of them had a positive injection result (ID# 11, 21, 24, 25), where the Pre-Inj VAS scores were 6, 8, and 9 and the Post-Inj VAS scores were 2, 2, and 1, respectively, plus subjective report of improvement in the subsequent 3 days after injection. 1 of these 5 patients had negative injection result (ID# 16, Pre-Inj and Post-Inj VAS scores: 1 and 1) and negative finding of the MRA. Despite an additional 3 months of rest and physical therapy, this patient continued to have pain off and on. The shared decision was made to proceed with a diagnostic hip arthroscopy, which revealed no labral tear or other intra-articular pathologies.

In addition, there were 5 patients who had positive MRA findings of labral tears but did not have immediate Post-Inj pain relief (ID# 2, 4, 8, 13, 22). Of note, all these patients had symptomatic relief in the subsequent 3 days after injection and subsequently had labral tear identified and repaired under hip arthroscopy.

Discussion

Our results showed that combined hip MRA + IA was effective in facilitating the decision making on hip arthroscopy surgery, particularly when MR arthrogram was equivocal or negative. While MRA remains the benchmark imaging modality for detecting structural abnormalities in the hip joint such as labral tears, IA is synergic in the diagnostic accuracy by directly assessing symptomatic relief in the presence of possible intra-articular pathology versus an extra-articular source of symptoms.

Conducting both diagnostic studies in one setting with a single injection can improve patients' experience. This eliminates the need for another visit and another injection, thus potentially eliminating the need for a second sedation. Particularly for younger patients such as the teenagers in our cohort, a single injection reduces patient anxiety and minimizes risk of infection or other complications. Moreover, the surgeon would also be more efficient by having comprehensive diagnostic information to discuss with the patients, lessening the trouble of sending and waiting for the results of IA when the patients come back with equivocal MRA results.

In addition, this approach saves cost for both patients and the healthcare system. With a combined MRA + IA procedure, 3 separate CPT codes are billed for the injection, arthrography, and MRI. Combined injection eliminates an extra set of injection and arthrography CPT billing codes that would be required when the 2 studies are conducted on separate occasions and separate dates. However, the intricacy of the billing practice requires interdisciplinary collaboration among the surgeons and radiologists, which may be different in the settings where the diagnostic injection of the hip is performed by the surgeon. Of note, in a pediatric and adolescent facility, it is strongly preferred to minimize needle pokes, time out of school and work (for parents), and anesthesia events (sedation) whenever possible.

Our results resonate with the literature that a negative MRA alone may not exclude the possibility of a surgical labral tear.4,5,9,10 Of note, higher alpha angles on MRA have been associated with increased severity and length of labral tears11 and alpha angles greater than 57° are associated with superior labral tears,12 albeit the alpha angle alone has not been proven to be an independent predictor of acetabular labral tear. Thus, this integrated approach allows for a more nuanced interpretation of diagnostic imaging results, minimizing the risk of false negatives and optimizing patient outcomes.

In addition, preoperative IA can also serve as a prognostic indicator of postoperative outcomes after hip arthroscopy,8,10,13,14 although some reported that IA may not be predictive of outcomes after hip arthroscopy in an adult population with various degrees of chondral injuries.15 Besides its diagnostic value, IA with steroid can also deliver temporary therapeutic efficacy in patients with femoroacetabular impingement and acetabular labral tears.8,14 We did not routinely use steroid in our injection cocktail for the young active patient population in our institution. The purpose of IA is mainly diagnostic. IA with steroid can increase the risk of infection, particularly if the patient would need a hip arthroscopy procedure within a month after the injection, which is not uncommon in highly active young athletes.16 Moreover, masking the symptoms for a longer time may paradoxically encourage activities that propagate the impingement and thus the extent of labral injuries.

The IA may temporarily increase the pain immediately after the injection, before it delivers its anesthetic effect. This was seen in some of our patients who reported the same or slightly higher Post-Inj VAS score but subsequently felt more than 50% of symptomatic relief after they went home. This may be explained by the feeling of distention of the hip joint space, which is required to perform the MRA.17 Such distention can elicit pain and discomfort especially when there is intra-articular pathology in the hip, while standalone IA without MRA may involve a lesser fluid volume of injection and thus less distention.

The quality of the MRA images is preserved when performing a combined MRA + IA procedure. Technically, a combined hip MRA + IA procedure differs from a standard MR arthrogram only in the medication mixture that is injected into the joint space. The steps in the procedure are otherwise the same. For a combined hip MRA + IA procedure, some of the saline that would be used for the MR arthrogram is replaced with 0.2% ropivacaine. The same degree of joint distension is achieved, and the quality of the images remains unchanged.

Limitations

Our study does have limitations. Our patient cohort was relatively small in a single institution, and the enrollment of receiving combined MRA + IA was voluntary. Our practice focuses on the pediatric and adolescent population, and our results may not apply to the general population, particularly those who have degenerative changes such as hip arthritis. Finally, our cohort did not have negative control patients who would have positive IA results but negative intraoperative findings of labral injury.

Conclusions

Our study underscored the value of the combined MRA + IA approach as an efficient diagnostic tool for surgical decision making in young patients with acetabular labral tears. By synergistically integrating these 2 studies, this approach enables a more comprehensive yet cost-effective way of evaluating hip joint pathology, therefore improving the satisfaction for the patients and for the surgeons. Future research endeavors should aim to further elucidate the long-term efficacy and cost effectiveness of this combined approach, paving the way for its widespread adoption in clinical practice.

Sharp concentration of simple random tensors II: asymmetry
Jiaheng Chen*, Daniel Sanz-Alonso

Abstract
This paper establishes sharp concentration inequalities for simple random tensors. Our theory unveils a phenomenon that arises only for asymmetric tensors of order p ≥ 3 : p\geq 3: when the effective ranks of the covariances of the component random variables lie on both sides of a critical threshold, an additional logarithmic factor emerges that is not present in sharp bounds for symmetric tensors. To establish our results, we develop empirical process theory for products of p p different function classes evaluated at p p different random variables, extending generic chaining techniques for quadratic and product empirical processes to higher-order settings.

Introduction

This paper establishes sharp bounds for the operator-norm deviation of the sum of simple (rank-one) random tensors from its expectation. For integer
p\geq 2
and each
1\leq k\leq p
, let
X^{(k)},X^{(k)}_{1},\ldots,X^{(k)}_{N}
be i.i.d. centered sub-Gaussian random variables in a separable Hilbert space
H^{(k)}
with covariance operator
\Sigma^{(k)}
. Our first main result, Theorem
2.1
, shows that, with a standard notation described below,

\mathbb{E}\bigg\|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}X_{i}^{(1)}\otimes\cdots\otimes X_{i}^{(p)}-\mathbb{E}\,X^{(1)}\otimes\cdots\otimes X^{(p)}\bigg\|\lesssim_{p}\bigg(\prod_{k=1}^{p}\|\Sigma^{(k)}\|^{1/2}\bigg){E}_{N}\big((\Sigma^{(k)})_{k=1}^{p}\big),

where

{E}_{N}\big((\Sigma^{(k)})_{k=1}^{p}\big):=\bigg(\frac{\sum_{k=1}^{p}r(\Sigma^{(k)})}{N}\bigg)^{1/2}+\frac{1}{N}\prod_{k=1}^{p}\Big(r(\Sigma^{(k)})+\log N\Big)^{1/2},\quad\ r(\Sigma^{(k)}):=\frac{\mathrm{Tr}(\Sigma^{(k)})}{\|\Sigma^{(k)}\|}.

We also obtain corresponding
q
-th moment bounds for
q\geq 1
. In addition, Theorem
2.1
establishes a matching lower bound in the case of independent Gaussian data, demonstrating the sharpness of the result and showing that the logarithmic factors are, in general, unavoidable. When
p=2
, our result yields a sharp dimension-free concentration bound for the sample cross-covariance operator of sub-Gaussian random variables
X^{(1)}
and
X^{(2)}
, without imposing any assumptions on their correlation structure:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg\|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}X^{(1)}_{i}\otimes X^{(2)}_{i}-\mathbb{E}\,X^{(1)}\otimes X^{(2)}\bigg\|

\displaystyle\lesssim\big(\|\Sigma^{(1)}\|\|\Sigma^{(2)}\|\big)^{1/2}\left(\bigg(\frac{r(\Sigma^{(1)})+r(\Sigma^{(2)})}{N}\bigg)^{1/2}+\frac{(r(\Sigma^{(1)})r(\Sigma^{(2)}))^{1/2}}{N}\right).

To prove Theorem
2.1
, we introduce and develop the theory of multi-product empirical processes. In our second main result, Theorem
2.2
, we leverage generic chaining techniques to obtain a sharp in-expectation bound on

\sup_{f^{(k)}\in\mathcal{F}^{(k)},1\leq k\leq p}\bigg|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\prod_{k=1}^{p}f^{(k)}(X^{(k)}_{i})-\mathbb{E}\prod_{k=1}^{p}f^{(k)}(X^{(k)})\bigg|

in terms of quantities that reflect the geometric complexity of the function classes
\mathcal{F}^{(k)},1\leq k\leq p,
which are taken to contain bounded linear functionals on the corresponding Hilbert spaces
H^{(k)}
in the proof of Theorem
2.1
.

1.1
Main
contributions and outline

This paper further develops the study of sharp concentration of simple random tensors initiated in
[
2
]
. While the analysis in
[
2
]
is limited to
symmetric
tensors,
this paper generalizes the theory to
asymmetric
tensors. In so doing,
we uncover a new phenomenon that only arises in the concentration of asymmetric tensors of order
p\geq 3
. To establish our results, we investigate a more general type of empirical process than
[
2
]
, for products of
p
different function classes evaluated at
p
different random variables. A key technical contribution is Theorem
4.5
, which establishes a sharp uniform bound on the
\ell_{2}
-norm of the product of the coordinate projection vectors associated with the different function classes.

The proof of Theorem
4.5
is based on generic chaining across multiple function classes and builds on ideas of Mendelson
[
25
]
for controlling multiplier and product processes. The main new difficulty is that we must handle heterogeneous classes
\mathcal{F}^{(1)},\ldots,\mathcal{F}^{(p)}
evaluated at different random variables, and exploit the resulting multilinear structure to carry out a coordinated chaining argument across the classes. A crucial step in controlling the increments is to incorporate
\ell_{\infty}
-norm bounds on the relevant coordinate projection vectors at appropriate stages of the chaining argument; this introduces
\mathsf{polylog}(N)
factors. While these terms may appear crude at first glance, they are in fact sharp and unavoidable in general. In particular, even if some class
\mathcal{F}^{(k)}
is “simple” in a global complexity sense, its contribution to the overall bound must still include at least a term that reflects its largest coordinate fluctuation in
\ell_{\infty}
. This viewpoint provides a principled way to understand the behavior of products of coordinate projection vectors across heterogeneous classes
(\mathcal{F}^{(k)})_{k=1}^{p}
and different underlying random variables —an effect that is not captured by the approach in
[
2
]
.
Remarks
2.1
and
2.3
further discuss how the results and techniques in this paper differ from those in
[
2
]
.

In the special case
p=2
, our results yield a new dimension-free concentration bound for the sample cross-covariance operator; see Remark
2.1
. Cross-covariances are a core building block in many procedures, including principal component methods, canonical correlation analysis, and more broadly in the estimation of second-order structure in multivariate or functional data. Obtaining dimension-independent deviation bounds is particularly valuable in high- or infinite-dimensional regimes, where classical bounds scale poorly with the ambient dimension and become uninformative. Our estimates provide a useful theoretical tool for such settings. More generally, we anticipate that Theorem
2.1
may have applications in various tensor-related statistical problems
[

,

,

, including tensor estimation
[

[

[

2.1
Concentration of simple random tensors

For integer
p\geq 2
and
1\leq k\leq p
, let
(H^{(k)},\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{H^{(k)}},\|\cdot\|_{H^{(k)}})
be a separable real Hilbert space, and let
X^{(k)}
be a centered
H^{(k)}
-valued random variable. Suppose that
\mathbb{E}|\langle X^{(k)},v\rangle_{H^{(k)}}|^{p}<\infty
for all
v\in U_{H^{(k)}}:=\{v\in H^{(k)}:\|v\|_{H^{(k)}}=1\}
and let
X^{(1)}\otimes\cdots\otimes X^{(p)}
be the multilinear form

(X^{(1)}\otimes\cdots\otimes X^{(p)})(v_{1},\ldots,v_{p}):=\langle X^{(1)},v_{1}\rangle_{H^{(1)}}\cdots\langle X^{(p)},v_{p}\rangle_{H^{(p)}},\quad\forall\ (v_{1},\ldots,v_{p})\in H^{(1)}\times\cdots\times H^{(p)}.

Thus,
X^{(1)}\otimes\cdots\otimes X^{(p)}
is a random variable taking values in the tensor space
H^{(1)}\otimes\cdots\otimes H^{(p)}
. We refer for instance to
[
30
, Section II.4]
for background on tensor products of Hilbert spaces. When
p=2
and
X^{(1)}=X^{(2)}=X\in H
, the second-order moment tensor
\mathbb{E}\,X^{\otimes 2}(v_{1},v_{2})=\mathbb{E}\langle X,v_{1}\rangle_{H}\langle X,v_{2}\rangle_{H},

\,\forall\,(v_{1},v_{2})\in H\times H
is called the
covariance
of
X.
By the Riesz representation theorem, this bilinear form can be represented by the
covariance operator

\Sigma:H\to H
defined by

\langle v_{1},\Sigma v_{2}\rangle_{H}:=\mathbb{E}\,X^{\otimes 2}(v_{1},v_{2}),\qquad\forall\ (v_{1},v_{2})\in H\times H.

Given independent copies
X^{(k)}_{1},\ldots,X^{(k)}_{N}
of
X^{(k)}
for
1\leq k\leq p
, our goal is to establish concentration inequalities for the sum of simple (rank-one) random tensors
\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}X^{(1)}_{i}\otimes\cdots\otimes X^{(p)}_{i}
. Specifically, we seek to obtain upper bounds on the deviation

\displaystyle\bigg\|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}X_{i}^{(1)}\otimes\cdots\otimes X_{i}^{(p)}-\mathbb{E}\,X^{(1)}\otimes\cdots\otimes X^{(p)}\bigg\|

\displaystyle\quad=\sup_{v_{k}\in U_{H^{(k)}},1\leq k\leq p}\bigg|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\langle X^{(1)}_{i},v_{1}\rangle_{H^{(1)}}\cdots\langle X^{(p)}_{i},v_{p}\rangle_{H^{(p)}}-\mathbb{E}\langle X^{(1)},v_{1}\rangle_{H^{(1)}}\cdots\langle X^{(p)},v_{p}\rangle_{H^{(p)}}\bigg|,

(2.1)

where
\|\cdot\|
denotes the norm in the tensor space
H^{(1)}\otimes\cdots\otimes H^{(p)}.
Notice that for
p=2
and
X^{(1)}=X^{(2)}=X\in H
, the operator norm of the covariance operator
\Sigma:H\to H
agrees with the norm of the covariance
\mathbb{E}\,X^{\otimes 2}
as a bilinear form in
H^{\otimes 2}.
Consequently, we will slightly abuse notation and denote by
\|\Sigma\|
the operator norm of the covariance operator. Furthermore, to simplify the notation, we will henceforth omit the subscript
H^{(k)}
in the inner-product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{H^{(k)}}
and norm
\|\cdot\|_{H^{(k)}}
.

We will focus on Gaussian and sub-Gaussian random variables. Recall that an
H
-valued random variable
X
is called
(sub)-Gaussian
iff all one-dimensional projections
\langle X,v\rangle
for
v\in H
are (sub)-Gaussian real-valued random variables. Furthermore,
X
is called
pre-Gaussian
iff there exists a centered Gaussian random variable
Y
in
H
with the same covariance operator as that of
X.

For a covariance operator
\Sigma
, the
effective rank
is defined as

r(\Sigma):=\frac{\mathrm{Tr}(\Sigma)}{\|\Sigma\|}.

Our first main result, Theorem
2.1
, provides a sharp dimension-free bound for the deviation (
2.1
) in terms of the effective ranks
r(\Sigma^{(1)}),\ldots,r(\Sigma^{(p)})
of the covariance operators associated with the random variables
X^{(1)},\ldots,X^{(p)}
. We prove Theorem
2.1
in Section
3
.

Theorem 2.1
.

For any integer
p\geq 2
and
1\leq k\leq p
, let
X^{(k)},X_{1}^{(k)},\ldots,X_{N}^{(k)}
be i.i.d. centered sub-Gaussian and pre-Gaussian random variables in
H^{(k)}
with covariance operator
\Sigma^{(k)}
. Then, for any
q\geq 1
,

\left(\mathbb{E}\bigg\|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}X_{i}^{(1)}\otimes\cdots\otimes X_{i}^{(p)}-\mathbb{E}\,X^{(1)}\otimes\cdots\otimes X^{(p)}\bigg\|^{q}\right)^{1/q}\lesssim_{p,q}\bigg(\prod_{k=1}^{p}\|\Sigma^{(k)}\|^{1/2}\bigg){E}_{N}\big((\Sigma^{(k)})_{k=1}^{p}\big),

where

\displaystyle{E}_{N}\big((\Sigma^{(k)})_{k=1}^{p}\big):=\bigg(\frac{\sum_{k=1}^{p}r(\Sigma^{(k)})}{N}\bigg)^{1/2}+\frac{1}{N}\prod_{k=1}^{p}\Big(r(\Sigma^{(k)})+\log N\Big)^{1/2}.

Moreover, if
X^{(1)},\ldots,X^{(p)},(X^{(1)}_{i})_{i=1}^{N},\ldots,(X^{(p)}_{i})_{i=1}^{N}
are independent Gaussian, then

\left(\mathbb{E}\bigg\|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}X_{i}^{(1)}\otimes\cdots\otimes X_{i}^{(p)}-\mathbb{E}\,X^{(1)}\otimes\cdots\otimes X^{(p)}\bigg\|^{q}\right)^{1/q}\asymp_{p,q}\bigg(\prod_{k=1}^{p}\|\Sigma^{(k)}\|^{1/2}\bigg){E}_{N}\big((\Sigma^{(k)})_{k=1}^{p}\big).

To the best of our knowledge, Theorem
2.1
is the first result in the literature to establish
sharp dimension-free
bounds for the deviation (
2.1
) under sub-Gaussian assumptions. Notably, the upper bound holds without requiring independence between the sequences
(X^{(k)}_{i})_{i=1}^{N}
and
(X^{(k^{\prime})}_{i})_{i=1}^{N}
for
k\neq k^{\prime}
; that is, no assumptions are made on the correlation structure across components. Moreover, Theorem
2.1
shows that our expectation bound is sharp in the case of independent Gaussian data.

The remainder of this subsection provides a detailed discussion of Theorem
2.1
and its connections to existing results. In Remark
2.1
, we compare our result to existing concentration inequalities for sums of simple symmetric tensors and derive a straightforward corollary for finite-dimensional random tensors. Remark
2.2
presents an additional consequence of our theory concerning isotropic, unconditional, log-concave ensembles.

Remark 2.1
.

For simplicity, in this remark we focus on the first-moment case
q=1
. When
p=2
, Theorem
2.1
yields the following dimension-free concentration bound for the sample cross-covariance operator:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg\|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}X^{(1)}_{i}\otimes X^{(2)}_{i}-\mathbb{E}\,X^{(1)}\otimes X^{(2)}\bigg\|

\displaystyle\lesssim\big(\|\Sigma^{(1)}\|\|\Sigma^{(2)}\|\big)^{1/2}\left(\bigg(\frac{r(\Sigma^{(1)})+r(\Sigma^{(2)})}{N}\bigg)^{1/2}+\frac{(r(\Sigma^{(1)})+\log N)^{1/2}(r(\Sigma^{(2)})+\log N)^{1/2}}{N}\right)

\displaystyle\asymp\big(\|\Sigma^{(1)}\|\|\Sigma^{(2)}\|\big)^{1/2}\left(\bigg(\frac{r(\Sigma^{(1)})+r(\Sigma^{(2)})}{N}\bigg)^{1/2}+\frac{(r(\Sigma^{(1)})r(\Sigma^{(2)}))^{1/2}}{N}\right),

which improves upon the earlier bound in
[
3
, Lemma A.3]
. To our knowledge, this dimension-free concentration bound for the sample cross-covariance operator is new. It holds without any assumption on the dependence between the sequences
(X_{i}^{(1)})_{i=1}^{N}
and
(X_{i}^{(2)})_{i=1}^{N}
, provided that both are sub-Gaussian and pre-Gaussian random variables. Moreover, it is sharp (up to constants) in view of the matching lower bound in the independent case established in Theorem
2.1
.

When
X^{(1)}=\cdots=X^{(p)}=X
and
X_{i}^{(1)}=\cdots=X_{i}^{(p)}=X_{i}\,
for all
1\leq i\leq N,
so that
\Sigma^{(1)}=\cdots=\Sigma^{(p)}=\Sigma,
Theorem
2.1
yields

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg\|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}X_{i}^{\otimes p}-\mathbb{E}\,X^{\otimes p}\bigg\|\lesssim_{p}\|\Sigma\|^{p/2}\bigg(\sqrt{\frac{r(\Sigma)}{N}}+\frac{(r(\Sigma)+\log N)^{p/2}}{N}\bigg)\asymp_{p}\|\Sigma\|^{p/2}\bigg(\sqrt{\frac{r(\Sigma)}{N}}+\frac{r(\Sigma)^{p/2}}{N}\bigg),

(2.2)

where the final equivalence follows from the bound
(\log N)^{p/2}/N\lesssim_{p}1/\sqrt{N}
. This recovers the sharp concentration inequality for simple symmetric tensors established in
[
2
, Theorem 2.1]
. We refer the reader to
[
2
]
for further references on the study of (
2.2
), as well as related literature on sample covariance operators and random tensors.

Since the operator-norm deviation under consideration is invariant under permutations of the component random vectors, we may assume without loss of generality that the effective ranks satisfy
r(\Sigma^{(1)})\geq r(\Sigma^{(2)})\geq\cdots\geq r(\Sigma^{(p)})
. Suppose there exists an index
t
such that
r(\Sigma^{(t)})\geq\log N\geq r(\Sigma^{(t+1)})
. Then, the upper bound in Theorem
2.1
simplifies to

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg\|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}X_{i}^{(1)}\otimes\cdots\otimes X_{i}^{(p)}-\mathbb{E}\,X^{(1)}\otimes\cdots\otimes X^{(p)}\bigg\|

\displaystyle\quad\lesssim_{p}\bigg(\prod_{k=1}^{p}\|\Sigma^{(k)}\|^{1/2}\bigg)\left(\bigg(\frac{r(\Sigma^{(1)})}{N}\bigg)^{1/2}+\frac{(\log N)^{(p-t)/2}}{N}\prod_{k=1}^{t}r(\Sigma^{(k)})^{1/2}\right).

As will be shown in Proposition
3.1
, the upper bound can be reversed (up to a constant depending only on
p
) in the case of independent Gaussian data, indicating that the logarithmic factors in the upper bound are, in general, unavoidable. This new phenomenon arises only for asymmetric tensors of order
p\geq 3
—specifically, when the effective ranks of the component covariances vary across the threshold
\log N
, with some exceeding and others falling below it. The random variables in the tensor product whose covariances have effective ranks smaller than
\log N
contribute logarithmic factors to the second term in the upper bound.
Since this behavior only arises for asymmetric tensors of order
p\geq 3,

it has not been observed in previous work on the concentration of simple symmetric tensors or sample covariance operators.

A corresponding finite-dimensional corollary is as follows. For each
1\leq k\leq p
, let
X^{(k)},X_{1}^{(k)},\ldots,X_{N}^{(k)}
be i.i.d. standard Gaussian random vectors in
\mathbb{R}^{d_{k}}
, so that
\Sigma^{(k)}=I_{d_{k}}
and
r(\Sigma^{(k)})=\mathrm{Tr}(\Sigma^{(k)})/\|\Sigma^{(k)}\|=d_{k}
. If

d_{1}\geq\cdots\geq d_{t}\geq\log N\geq d_{t+1}\geq\cdots\geq d_{p},

then

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg\|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}X_{i}^{(1)}\otimes\cdots\otimes X_{i}^{(p)}-\mathbb{E}\,X^{(1)}\otimes\cdots\otimes X^{(p)}\bigg\|\lesssim_{p}\bigg(\frac{d_{1}}{N}\bigg)^{1/2}+\frac{(\log N)^{(p-t)/2}}{N}\prod_{k=1}^{t}d_{k}^{1/2}.

Remark 2.2
.

A minor modification of our proof (see Subsection
4.4
) yields the following result for isotropic, unconditional, log-concave ensembles.

Recall that a probability measure
\nu
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
is log-concave if, for any
t\in[0,1]
and any nonempty Borel measurable sets
A,B\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, the following inequality holds:

\nu(tA+(1-t)B)\geq\nu(A)^{t}\nu(B)^{1-t}.

For each
1\leq k\leq p
, let
X^{(k)}
be a random vector in
\mathbb{R}^{d_{k}}
, distributed according to an isotropic, unconditional,
log-concave measure. That is, for all
v\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{k}}
, we have
\mathbb{E}\langle X,v\rangle^{2}=\|v\|_{\ell_{2}}^{2}
; the distribution of
X^{(k)}
is invariant under coordinate-wise sign changes; and the law of
X^{(k)}
has a log-concave density. Let
X_{1}^{(k)},\ldots,X_{N}^{(k)}
be independent copies of
X^{(k)}
, for each
1\leq k\leq p
. Then, the following bound holds:

\mathbb{E}\bigg\|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}X_{i}^{(1)}\otimes\cdots\otimes X_{i}^{(p)}-\mathbb{E}\,X^{(1)}\otimes\cdots\otimes X^{(p)}\bigg\|\lesssim_{p}\bigg(\frac{\sum_{k=1}^{p}d_{k}}{N}\bigg)^{1/2}+\frac{1}{N}\prod_{k=1}^{p}\big(d_{k}^{1/2}+\log N\big).

In particular, if

d_{1}\geq\cdots\geq d_{t}\geq(\log N)^{2}\geq d_{t+1}\geq\cdots\geq d_{p},

then

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg\|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}X_{i}^{(1)}\otimes\cdots\otimes X_{i}^{(p)}-\mathbb{E}\,X^{(1)}\otimes\cdots\otimes X^{(p)}\bigg\|\lesssim_{p}\bigg(\frac{d_{1}}{N}\bigg)^{1/2}+\frac{(\log N)^{p-t}}{N}\prod_{k=1}^{t}d_{k}^{1/2}.

We further remark that the corresponding
q
-th moment bound can be derived by integrating the tail estimate from Subsection
4.4
. We omit the details for brevity and focus on the first-moment bound.

2.2
Multi-product empirical processes

This subsection contains the statement of our second main result, Theorem
2.2
, which gives a general upper bound on the suprema of multi-product empirical processes. Theorem
2.2
will be key to establish our upper bounds in Theorem
2.1
. For
1\leq k\leq p
, let
X^{(k)},X^{(k)}_{1},\ldots,X^{(k)}_{N}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{i.i.d.}}}{{\sim}}\mu^{(k)}
be a sequence of random variables on a probability space
(\Omega^{(k)},\mu^{(k)})
. We consider the
multi-product
centered empirical process indexed by
p
(potentially distinct) function classes
\mathcal{F}^{(1)},\ldots,\mathcal{F}^{(p)}
, where each
\mathcal{F}^{(k)}
is defined on
(\Omega^{(k)},\mu^{(k)})
. The process is given by

\displaystyle(f^{(1)},\ldots,f^{(p)})\mapsto\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\prod_{k=1}^{p}f^{(k)}(X^{(k)}_{i})-\mathbb{E}\prod_{k=1}^{p}f^{(k)}(X^{(k)}),\qquad f^{(k)}\in\mathcal{F}^{(k)},\ 1\leq k\leq p.

(2.3)

In our proof of Theorem
2.1
, the class
\mathcal{F}^{(k)}
will be taken to contain all linear functionals in
H^{(k)}
with norm one.

For
p=1
, (
2.3
) recovers the standard empirical process
[
35
,
34
]
, and for
p=2
the quadratic and product empirical processes extensively studied, e.g., in
[
31
,
18
,
26
,
1
,
22
,
27
,
13
,
8
,
25
,
9
,
19
,
16
]
. Beyond their theoretical significance, quadratic and product empirical processes play a crucial role in various statistical applications, such as covariance estimation
[
19
]
. Our goal is to bound

\sup_{f^{(k)}\in\mathcal{F}^{(k)},1\leq k\leq p}\bigg|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\prod_{k=1}^{p}f^{(k)}(X^{(k)}_{i})-\mathbb{E}\prod_{k=1}^{p}f^{(k)}(X^{(k)})\bigg|

for any
p\geq 2
in terms of quantities that reflect the geometric complexity of the indexing classes
(\mathcal{F}^{(k)})_{k=1}^{p}.
To that end, for any function
f
on
(\Omega,\mu)
, we introduce the Orlicz
\psi_{2}
-norm of
f
given by

\|f\|_{\psi_{2}}:=\inf\left\{c>0:\mathbb{E}_{X\sim\mu}\left[\exp\left(\frac{|f(X)|^{2}}{c^{2}}\right)\right]\leq 2\right\},

with the convention
\inf\emptyset=\infty.

Our upper bound will depend on

d_{\psi_{2}}(\mathcal{F}):=\sup_{f\in\mathcal{F}}\|f\|_{\psi_{2}}

and on Talagrand’s functional
\gamma(\mathcal{F},\psi_{2})
, whose definition we now recall.

Definition 2.1

(Talagrand’s
\gamma
functional)
.

Let
(\mathcal{F},d)
be a metric space. An admissible sequence is an increasing sequence
(\mathcal{F}_{s})_{s\geq 0}\subset\mathcal{F}
which satisfies
\mathcal{F}_{s}\subset\mathcal{F}_{s+1},\left|\mathcal{F}_{0}\right|=1,\left|\mathcal{F}_{s}\right|\leq 2^{2^{s}}
for
s\geq 1,
and
\bigcup_{s=0}^{\infty}\mathcal{F}_{s}
is dense in
\mathcal{F}
.
Let

\gamma(\mathcal{F},d):=\inf\sup_{f\in\mathcal{F}}\sum_{s\geq 0}2^{s/2}d\left(f,\mathcal{F}_{s}\right),

where the infimum is taken over all admissible sequences. We write
\gamma(\mathcal{F},\psi_{2})
when the distance on
\mathcal{\mathcal{F}}
is induced by the
\psi_{2}
-norm.

We are ready to state our second main result. Its proof can be found in Section
4
.

Theorem 2.2
.

For any integer
p\geq 2
and
1\leq k\leq p
, let
X^{(k)},X^{(k)}_{1},\ldots,X^{(k)}_{N}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{i.i.d.}}}{{\sim}}\mu^{(k)}
be a sequence of random variables on the probability space
(\Omega^{(k)},\mu^{(k)})
, and let
\mathcal{F}^{(k)}
be a class of functions defined on
(\Omega^{(k)},\mu^{(k)})
. Assume
0\in\mathcal{F}^{(k)}
or
\mathcal{F}^{(k)}
is symmetric (i.e.,
f^{(k)}\in\mathcal{F}^{(k)}
implies
-f^{(k)}\in\mathcal{F}^{(k)}
). Then, for any
q\geq 1
,

\displaystyle\left(\mathbb{E}\sup_{f^{(k)}\in\mathcal{F}^{(k)},1\leq k\leq p}\bigg|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\prod_{k=1}^{p}f^{(k)}(X^{(k)}_{i})-\mathbb{E}\prod_{k=1}^{p}f^{(k)}(X^{(k)})\bigg|^{q}\right)^{1/q}\lesssim_{p,q}\bigg(\prod_{k=1}^{p}d_{\psi_{2}}(\mathcal{F}^{(k)})\bigg){E}_{N}\big((\mathcal{F}^{(k)})_{k=1}^{p}\big),

where

{E}_{N}\big((\mathcal{F}^{(k)})_{k=1}^{p}\big):=\frac{\sum_{k=1}^{p}\bar{\gamma}(\mathcal{F}^{(k)},\psi_{2})}{\sqrt{N}}+\frac{\prod_{k=1}^{p}\big(\bar{\gamma}(\mathcal{F}^{(k)},\psi_{2})+(\log N)^{1/2}\big)}{N},\quad\bar{\gamma}(\mathcal{F}^{(k)},\psi_{2}):=\frac{\gamma(\mathcal{F}^{(k)},\psi_{2})}{d_{\psi_{2}}(\mathcal{F}^{(k)})}.

Remark 2.3
.

Here, for each
1\leq k\leq p
, the quantities
\gamma(\mathcal{F}^{(k)},\psi_{2})
and
d_{\psi_{2}}(\mathcal{F}^{(k)})
are defined with respect to the probability measure
\mu^{(k)}
on the probability space
(\Omega^{(k)},\mu^{(k)})
. Moreover, the sequences
\big(X_{i}^{(k)}\big)_{i=1}^{N}
and
\big(X_{i}^{(k^{\prime})}\big)_{i=1}^{N}
are not necessarily independent for
k\neq k^{\prime}
.
This result generalizes the upper bound established in
[
25
, Theorem 1.13]
for the supremum of product empirical processes indexed by function classes
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{H}
, that is,

(f,h)\mapsto\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}f(X_{i})h(X_{i})-\mathbb{E}fh,\qquad f\in\mathcal{F},\ h\in\mathcal{H},

extending it to higher-order settings, including the case where each factor in the product involves a different random variable.

In the special case where
\Omega^{(1)}=\cdots=\Omega^{(p)}=\Omega,\mu^{(1)}=\cdots=\mu^{(p)}=\mu,\mathcal{F}^{(1)}=\cdots=\mathcal{F}^{(p)}=\mathcal{F}
, and
X^{(1)}=\cdots=X^{(p)}=X
, with
X^{(1)}_{i}=\cdots=X^{(p)}_{i}=X_{i}\
for all
1\leq i\leq N,
Theorem
2.2
(with
q=1
) implies

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\sup_{f\in\mathcal{F}}\bigg|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}f^{p}(X_{i})-\mathbb{E}f^{p}(X)\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\sup_{f^{(k)}\in\mathcal{F},1\leq k\leq p}\bigg|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\prod_{k=1}^{p}f^{(k)}(X_{i})-\mathbb{E}f^{p}(X)\bigg|

\displaystyle\lesssim_{p}d^{p}_{\psi_{2}}(\mathcal{F})\bigg(\frac{\bar{\gamma}(\mathcal{F},\psi_{2})}{\sqrt{N}}+\frac{\big((\bar{\gamma}(\mathcal{F},\psi_{2})+(\log N)^{1/2}\big)^{p}}{N}\bigg)

\displaystyle\asymp_{p}\frac{\gamma(\mathcal{F},\psi_{2})d^{p-1}_{\psi_{2}}(\mathcal{F})}{\sqrt{N}}+\frac{\gamma^{p}(\mathcal{F},\psi_{2})}{N},

(2.4)

where the final equivalence follows from the bound
(\log N)^{p/2}/N\lesssim_{p}1/\sqrt{N}
and
\bar{\gamma}(\mathcal{F},\psi_{2})\gtrsim 1
(see
[
2
, Lemma 4.6]
). This recovers the sharp expectation bound for the supremum of
L_{p}
empirical processes established in
[
2
, Theorem 2.2]
. We refer the reader to
[
2
]
for further discussion of (
2.4
), as well as additional references to related work concerning quadratic and product empirical processes.

Similar to the discussion in Remark
2.1
, the logarithmic factors in the upper bound of Theorem
2.2
are, in general, unavoidable. This new phenomenon arises only in multi-product empirical processes involving at least three components in the product (i.e.
p\geq 3
), and when the complexity parameters
\bar{\gamma}(\mathcal{F}^{(k)},\psi_{2})
of the associated function classes vary significantly —specifically, when some exceed
(\log N)^{1/2}
while others fall below this threshold. Consequently, such behavior has not been observed in previous work on the theory of quadratic and product empirical processes.

3
Concentration of simple random tensors

This section contains the proof of our first main result, Theorem
2.1
. The upper bound for sub-Gaussian data follows from an application of Theorem
2.2
, where for each
1\leq k\leq p
, the class
\mathcal{F}^{(k)}
consists of all linear functionals in
H^{(k)}
with norm one. To establish the matching lower bound for independent Gaussian data, we adapt techniques from
[
19
, Theorem 4]
to the multi-product setting and make a key observation that precisely captures the logarithmic factors appearing in the upper bound; see Proposition
3.1
for the proof and Remark
3.1
for further discussion.

Proof of Theorem
2.1
.

We have

\displaystyle\bigg\|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}X^{(1)}_{i}\otimes\cdots\otimes X^{(p)}_{i}-\mathbb{E}\,X^{(1)}\otimes\cdots\otimes X^{(p)}\bigg\|

\displaystyle=\sup_{\|v_{1}\|=\cdots=\|v_{p}\|=1}\bigg|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\prod_{k=1}^{p}\langle X^{(k)}_{i},v_{k}\rangle-\mathbb{E}\prod_{k=1}^{p}\langle X^{(k)},v_{k}\rangle\bigg|

\displaystyle=\sup_{f^{(k)}\in\mathcal{F}^{(k)},1\leq k\leq p}\bigg|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\prod_{k=1}^{p}f^{(k)}(X^{(k)}_{i})-\mathbb{E}\prod_{k=1}^{p}f^{(k)}(X^{(k)})\bigg|,

where
\mathcal{F}^{(k)}:=\{\langle\cdot,v\rangle:v\in U_{H^{(k)}}\},U_{H^{(k)}}:=\{v\in H^{(k)}:\|v\|=1\}
for each
1\leq k\leq p
. It is clear that
\mathcal{F}^{(k)}
is symmetric. Recall that for
1\leq k\leq p
,
X^{(k)},X_{1}^{(k)},\ldots,X_{N}^{(k)}
are i.i.d. centered sub-Gaussian and pre-Gaussian random variables in
H^{(k)}
with covariance operator
\Sigma^{(k)}
. Since
X^{(k)}
is sub-Gaussian, the
\psi_{2}
-norm of linear functionals is equivalent to the
L_{2}
-norm. Hence,

\displaystyle d_{\psi_{2}}(\mathcal{F}^{(k)})=\sup_{f^{(k)}\in\mathcal{F}^{(k)}}\|f^{(k)}\|_{\psi_{2}}\asymp\sup_{f^{(k)}\in\mathcal{F}^{(k)}}\|f^{(k)}\|_{L_{2}}=\sup_{\|v\|=1}\big(\mathbb{E}\langle X^{(k)},v\rangle^{2}\big)^{1/2}=\|\Sigma^{(k)}\|^{1/2}.

Moreover, since
X^{(k)}
is pre-Gaussian, there exists a centered Gaussian random variable
Y^{(k)}
in
H^{(k)}
with the same covariance
\Sigma^{(k)}
. This means that, for
u,v\in U_{H^{(k)}}
, the canonical metric associated with
Y^{(k)}
satisfies

d_{Y^{(k)}}(u,v)=\big(\mathbb{E}(\langle Y^{(k)},u\rangle-\langle Y^{(k)},v\rangle)^{2}\big)^{1/2}=\big(\langle u-v,\Sigma^{(k)}(u-v)\rangle\big)^{1/2}=\|\langle\cdot,u\rangle-\langle\cdot,v\rangle\|_{L_{2}(\mu^{(k)})},

where
\mu^{(k)}
is the law of
X^{(k)}
. Using Talagrand’s majorizing-measure theorem
[
34
, Theorem 2.10.1]
, we deduce that

\gamma(\mathcal{F}^{(k)},\psi_{2})\asymp\gamma(\mathcal{F}^{(k)},L_{2})=\gamma\big(U_{H^{(k)}},d_{Y^{(k)}}\big)\asymp\mathbb{E}\sup_{v\in U_{H^{(k)}}}\langle Y^{(k)},v\rangle=\mathbb{E}\|Y^{(k)}\|\asymp\big(\mathbb{E}\|Y^{(k)}\|^{2}\big)^{1/2}=\mathrm{Tr}(\Sigma^{(k)})^{1/2}.

Therefore,

d_{\psi_{2}}(\mathcal{F}^{(k)})\asymp\|\Sigma^{(k)}\|^{1/2},\quad\gamma(\mathcal{F}^{(k)},\psi_{2})\asymp\mathrm{Tr}(\Sigma^{(k)})^{1/2},\qquad 1\leq k\leq p.

Consequently, the upper bound stated in Theorem
2.1
follows directly from Theorem
2.2
. The matching lower bound for independent Gaussian data is proved in Proposition
3.1
below (the case
q=1
). Moreover, for any
q\geq 1
,

\left(\mathbb{E}\bigg\|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}X_{i}^{(1)}\otimes\cdots\otimes X_{i}^{(p)}-\mathbb{E}\,X^{(1)}\otimes\cdots\otimes X^{(p)}\bigg\|^{q}\right)^{1/q}\geq\mathbb{E}\bigg\|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}X_{i}^{(1)}\otimes\cdots\otimes X_{i}^{(p)}-\mathbb{E}\,X^{(1)}\otimes\cdots\otimes X^{(p)}\bigg\|,

so the first-moment lower bound immediately implies the same lower bound for all
q
-th moments.
∎

Proposition 3.1
.

For any integer
p\geq 2
and
1\leq k\leq p
, let
X^{(k)},X_{1}^{(k)},\ldots,X_{N}^{(k)}
be i.i.d. centered Gaussian random variables in
H^{(k)}
with covariance operator
\Sigma^{(k)}
, and suppose that
X^{(1)},\ldots,X^{(p)},(X^{(1)}_{i})_{i=1}^{N},\ldots,(X^{(p)}_{i})_{i=1}^{N},
are independent. Then,

\mathbb{E}\bigg\|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}X_{i}^{(1)}\otimes\cdots\otimes X_{i}^{(p)}-\mathbb{E}\,X^{(1)}\otimes\cdots\otimes X^{(p)}\bigg\|\gtrsim_{p}\bigg(\prod_{k=1}^{p}\|\Sigma^{(k)}\|^{1/2}\bigg){E}_{N}\big((\Sigma^{(k)})_{k=1}^{p}\big),

where

{E}_{N}\big((\Sigma^{(k)})_{k=1}^{p}\big):=\bigg(\frac{\sum_{k=1}^{p}r(\Sigma^{(k)})}{N}\bigg)^{1/2}+\frac{1}{N}\prod_{k=1}^{p}\Big(r(\Sigma^{(k)})+\log N\Big)^{1/2}.

Proof of Proposition
3.1
.

First, we may assume without loss of generality that the effective ranks satisfy
r(\Sigma^{(1)})\geq r(\Sigma^{(2)})\geq\cdots\geq r(\Sigma^{(p)})
. It is straightforward to check that

{E}_{N}\big((\Sigma^{(k)})_{k=1}^{p}\big)\asymp_{p}\begin{cases}\Big(\frac{\sum_{k=1}^{p}r(\Sigma^{(k)})}{N}\Big)^{1/2}+\frac{(\log N)^{(p-t)/2}}{N}\prod_{k=1}^{t}r(\Sigma^{(k)})^{1/2}&\mathrm{if}\ \exists\ t:r(\Sigma^{(t)})\geq\log N\geq r(\Sigma^{(t+1)}),\\
\Big(\frac{\sum_{k=1}^{p}r(\Sigma^{(k)})}{N}\Big)^{1/2}&\text{otherwise}.\\
\end{cases}

The conclusion follows directly from the following two claims:

Claim I.
It holds that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg\|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}X_{i}^{(1)}\otimes\cdots\otimes X_{i}^{(p)}-\mathbb{E}\,X^{(1)}\otimes\cdots\otimes X^{(p)}\bigg\|\gtrsim_{p}\bigg(\prod_{k=1}^{p}\|\Sigma^{(k)}\|^{1/2}\bigg)\bigg(\frac{\sum_{k=1}^{p}r(\Sigma^{(k)})}{N}\bigg)^{1/2}.

Claim II.

If there exists an index
t
such that
r(\Sigma^{(t)})\geq\log N\geq r(\Sigma^{(t+1)})
, then

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg\|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}X_{i}^{(1)}\otimes\cdots\otimes X_{i}^{(p)}-\mathbb{E}\,X^{(1)}\otimes\cdots\otimes X^{(p)}\bigg\|\gtrsim_{p}\bigg(\prod_{k=1}^{p}\|\Sigma^{(k)}\|^{1/2}\bigg)\frac{(\log N)^{(p-t)/2}}{N}\prod_{k=1}^{t}r(\Sigma^{(k)})^{1/2}.

Now we prove
Claim I
and
Claim II
.

Proof of Claim I:

By the assumption of independence and the definition of the tensor norm, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg\|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}X_{i}^{(1)}\otimes\cdots\otimes X_{i}^{(p)}-\mathbb{E}\,X^{(1)}\otimes\cdots\otimes X^{(p)}\bigg\|=\mathbb{E}\bigg\|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}X_{i}^{(1)}\otimes\cdots\otimes X_{i}^{(p)}\bigg\|

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\sup_{\|v_{1}\|=\cdots=\|v_{p}\|=1}\bigg|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\langle X^{(1)}_{i},v_{1}\rangle\cdots\langle X^{(p)}_{i},v_{p}\rangle\bigg|

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\sup_{\|v_{2}\|=\cdots=\|v_{p}\|=1}\bigg\|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\langle X^{(2)}_{i},v_{2}\rangle\cdots\langle X^{(p)}_{i},v_{p}\rangle X_{i}^{(1)}\bigg\|

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{(X^{(k)}_{i})_{i=1}^{N},2\leq k\leq p}\left[\mathbb{E}_{(X^{(1)}_{i})_{i=1}^{N}}\sup_{\|v_{2}\|=\cdots=\|v_{p}\|=1}\bigg\|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\langle X^{(2)}_{i},v_{2}\rangle\cdots\langle X^{(p)}_{i},v_{p}\rangle X_{i}^{(1)}\bigg\|\right].

Note that, conditionally on
\langle X^{(k)}_{i},v_{k}\rangle,1\leq i\leq N,2\leq k\leq p
, the distribution of the random variable

\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\langle X^{(2)}_{i},v_{2}\rangle\cdots\langle X^{(p)}_{i},v_{p}\rangle X_{i}^{(1)}

is Gaussian and it coincides with the distribution of the random variable

\bigg(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\langle X^{(2)}_{i},v_{2}\rangle^{2}\cdots\langle X^{(p)}_{i},v_{p}\rangle^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}\frac{X^{(1)}}{\sqrt{N}}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{(X^{(k)}_{i})_{i=1}^{N},2\leq k\leq p}\left[\mathbb{E}_{(X^{(1)}_{i})_{i=1}^{N}}\sup_{\|v_{2}\|=\cdots=\|v_{p}\|=1}\bigg\|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\langle X^{(2)}_{i},v_{2}\rangle\cdots\langle X^{(p)}_{i},v_{p}\rangle X_{i}^{(1)}\bigg\|\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{(X^{(k)}_{i})_{i=1}^{N},2\leq k\leq p}\left[\sup_{\|v_{2}\|=\cdots=\|v_{p}\|=1}\bigg(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\langle X^{(2)}_{i},v_{2}\rangle^{2}\cdots\langle X^{(p)}_{i},v_{p}\rangle^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}\frac{\mathbb{E}\|X^{(1)}\|}{\sqrt{N}}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathbb{E}\|X^{(1)}\|}{\sqrt{N}}\mathbb{E}_{(X^{(k)}_{i})_{i=1}^{N},2\leq k\leq p}\left[\sup_{\|v_{2}\|=\cdots=\|v_{p}\|=1}\bigg(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\langle X^{(2)}_{i},v_{2}\rangle^{2}\cdots\langle X^{(p)}_{i},v_{p}\rangle^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}\right]

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\mathbb{E}\|X^{(1)}\|}{\sqrt{N}}\sup_{\|v_{2}\|=\cdots=\|v_{p}\|=1}\mathbb{E}_{(X^{(k)}_{i})_{i=1}^{N},2\leq k\leq p}\bigg(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\langle X^{(2)}_{i},v_{2}\rangle^{2}\cdots\langle X^{(p)}_{i},v_{p}\rangle^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathbb{E}\|X^{(1)}\|}{\sqrt{N}}\sup_{\|v_{2}\|=\cdots=\|v_{p}\|=1}\langle\Sigma^{(2)}v_{2},v_{2}\rangle^{1/2}\cdots\langle\Sigma^{(p)}v_{p},v_{p}\rangle^{1/2}\mathbb{E}\bigg(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}(Z_{i}^{(2)})^{2}\cdots(Z_{i}^{(p)})^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathbb{E}\|X^{(1)}\|}{\sqrt{N}}\bigg(\prod_{k=2}^{p}\|\Sigma^{(k)}\|^{1/2}\bigg)\mathbb{E}\bigg(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}(Z_{i}^{(2)})^{2}\cdots(Z_{i}^{(p)})^{2}\bigg)^{1/2},

where

Z^{(k)}_{i}:=\frac{\langle X^{(k)}_{i},v_{k}\rangle}{\langle\Sigma^{(k)}v_{k},v_{k}\rangle^{1/2}},\quad 1\leq i\leq N,\ 1\leq k\leq p,

are i.i.d. standard Gaussian random variables. One can check that

\mathbb{E}\bigg(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}(Z_{i}^{(2)})^{2}\cdots(Z_{i}^{(p)})^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}\geq c(p)

for a positive constant
c(p)
depending only on
p
, which implies that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg\|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}X_{i}^{(1)}\otimes\cdots\otimes X_{i}^{(p)}-\mathbb{E}\,X^{(1)}\otimes\cdots\otimes X^{(p)}\bigg\|

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\mathbb{E}\|X^{(1)}\|}{\sqrt{N}}\bigg(\prod_{k=2}^{p}\|\Sigma^{(k)}\|^{1/2}\bigg)\mathbb{E}\bigg(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}(Z_{i}^{(2)})^{2}\cdots(Z_{i}^{(p)})^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\gtrsim_{p}\bigg(\prod_{k=1}^{p}\|\Sigma^{(k)}\|^{1/2}\bigg)\bigg(\frac{r(\Sigma^{(1)})}{N}\bigg)^{1/2}\gtrsim_{p}\bigg(\prod_{k=1}^{p}\|\Sigma^{(k)}\|^{1/2}\bigg)\bigg(\frac{\sum_{k=1}^{p}r(\Sigma^{(k)})}{N}\bigg)^{1/2},

where in the last step we used that
r(\Sigma^{(1)})\geq r(\Sigma^{(2)})\geq\cdots\geq r(\Sigma^{(p)})
. This establishes
Claim I
.

Proof of Claim II:

Suppose that there exists an index
t
such that

r(\Sigma^{(1)})\geq\cdots\geq r(\Sigma^{(t)})\geq\log N\geq r(\Sigma^{(t+1)})\geq\cdots\geq r(\Sigma^{(p)}).

Recall that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg\|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}X_{i}^{(1)}\otimes\cdots\otimes X_{i}^{(p)}-\mathbb{E}\,X^{(1)}\otimes\cdots\otimes X^{(p)}\bigg\|=\mathbb{E}\bigg\|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}X_{i}^{(1)}\otimes\cdots\otimes X_{i}^{(p)}\bigg\|

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\sup_{\|v_{1}\|=\cdots=\|v_{p}\|=1}\bigg|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\langle X^{(1)}_{i},v_{1}\rangle\cdots\langle X^{(p)}_{i},v_{p}\rangle\bigg|.

If
t=p
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\sup_{\|v_{1}\|=\cdots=\|v_{p}\|=1}\bigg|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\langle X^{(1)}_{i},v_{1}\rangle\cdots\langle X^{(p)}_{i},v_{p}\rangle\bigg|

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{E}_{(X_{1}^{(k)})_{k=1}^{p}}\sup_{\|v_{1}\|=\cdots=\|v_{p}\|=1}\bigg|\mathbb{E}_{(X_{i}^{(k)})_{i=2}^{N},1\leq k\leq p}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\langle X^{(1)}_{i},v_{1}\rangle\cdots\langle X^{(p)}_{i},v_{p}\rangle\bigg|

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{(X_{1}^{(k)})_{k=1}^{p}}\sup_{\|v_{1}\|=\cdots=\|v_{p}\|=1}\bigg|\frac{1}{N}\langle X^{(1)}_{1},v_{1}\rangle\cdots\langle X^{(p)}_{1},v_{p}\rangle\bigg|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\mathbb{E}_{(X_{1}^{(k)})_{k=1}^{p}}\prod_{k=1}^{p}\|X_{1}^{(k)}\|\overset{\text{($\star$)}}{=}\frac{1}{N}\prod_{k=1}^{p}\mathbb{E}\|X_{1}^{(k)}\|\gtrsim_{p}\frac{1}{N}\bigg(\prod_{k=1}^{p}\|\Sigma^{(k)}\|^{1/2}\bigg)\prod_{k=1}^{p}r(\Sigma^{(k)})^{1/2},

as desired. Here
(\star)
holds since
X^{(1)}_{1},\ldots,X^{(p)}_{1}
are independent, so
\|X^{(1)}_{1}\|,\ldots,\|X^{(p)}_{1}\|
are independent and the expectation factorizes.

If
1\leq t\leq p-1
, then by choosing
v_{k}=v^{*}_{k}
to be the unit eigenvector associated with the largest eigenvalue of
\Sigma^{(k)}
, for each
k\geq t+1
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\sup_{\|v_{1}\|=\cdots=\|v_{p}\|=1}\bigg|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\langle X^{(1)}_{i},v_{1}\rangle\cdots\langle X^{(p)}_{i},v_{p}\rangle\bigg|

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{E}\sup_{\|v_{1}\|=\cdots=\|v_{t}\|=1}\bigg|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\langle X^{(1)}_{i},v_{1}\rangle\cdots\langle X^{(t)}_{i},v_{t}\rangle\langle X^{(t+1)}_{i},v^{*}_{t+1}\rangle\cdots\langle X^{(p)}_{i},v^{*}_{p}\rangle\bigg|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\bigg(\prod_{k=t+1}^{p}\|\Sigma^{(k)}\|^{1/2}\bigg)\mathbb{E}\sup_{\|v_{1}\|=\cdots=\|v_{t}\|=1}\bigg|\sum_{i=1}^{N}\langle X^{(1)}_{i},v_{1}\rangle\cdots\langle X^{(t)}_{i},v_{t}\rangle Z^{(t+1)}_{i}\cdots Z^{(p)}_{i}\bigg|,

(3.1)

where

Z^{(k)}_{i}=\frac{\langle X^{(k)}_{i},v^{*}_{k}\rangle}{\langle\Sigma^{(k)}v^{*}_{k},v^{*}_{k}\rangle^{1/2}}=\frac{\langle X^{(k)}_{i},v^{*}_{k}\rangle}{\|\Sigma^{(k)}\|^{1/2}},\quad 1\leq i\leq N,\ 1\leq k\leq p,

are i.i.d. standard Gaussian random variables.

Observe that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\sup_{\|v_{1}\|=\cdots=\|v_{t}\|=1}\bigg|\sum_{i=1}^{N}\langle X^{(1)}_{i},v_{1}\rangle\cdots\langle X^{(t)}_{i},v_{t}\rangle Z^{(t+1)}_{i}\cdots Z^{(p)}_{i}\bigg|

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{(Z^{(k)}_{i})_{i=1}^{N},k\geq t+1}\left[\mathbb{E}_{(X^{(k)}_{i})_{i=1}^{N},k\leq t}\sup_{\|v_{1}\|=\cdots=\|v_{t}\|=1}\bigg|\sum_{i=1}^{N}\langle X^{(1)}_{i},v_{1}\rangle\cdots\langle X^{(t)}_{i},v_{t}\rangle Z^{(t+1)}_{i}\cdots Z^{(p)}_{i}\bigg|\right]

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{E}_{(Z^{(k)}_{i})_{i=1}^{N},k\geq t+1}\left[\max_{1\leq j\leq N}\mathbb{E}_{(X^{(k)}_{j})_{k=1}^{t}}\sup_{\|v_{1}\|=\cdots=\|v_{t}\|=1}\bigg|\mathbb{E}_{(X^{(k)}_{i})_{k=1}^{t},i\neq j}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\langle X^{(1)}_{i},v_{1}\rangle\cdots\langle X^{(t)}_{i},v_{t}\rangle Z^{(t+1)}_{i}\cdots Z^{(p)}_{i}\bigg|\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{(Z^{(k)}_{i})_{i=1}^{N},k\geq t+1}\left[\max_{1\leq j\leq N}\mathbb{E}_{(X^{(k)}_{j})_{k=1}^{t}}\sup_{\|v_{1}\|=\cdots=\|v_{t}\|=1}\bigg|\langle X^{(1)}_{j},v_{1}\rangle\cdots\langle X^{(t)}_{j},v_{t}\rangle Z^{(t+1)}_{j}\cdots Z^{(p)}_{j}\bigg|\right]

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{E}_{(Z^{(k)}_{i})_{i=1}^{N},k\geq t+1}\left[\max_{1\leq j\leq N}\mathbb{E}_{(X^{(k)}_{j})_{k=1}^{t}}\|X^{(1)}_{j}\|\cdots\|X^{(t)}_{j}\||Z^{(t+1)}_{j}|\cdots|Z^{(p)}_{j}|\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{(Z^{(k)}_{i})_{i=1}^{N},k\geq t+1}\left[\max_{1\leq j\leq N}(\mathbb{E}\|X^{(1)}\|)\cdots(\mathbb{E}\|X^{(t)}\|)|Z^{(t+1)}_{j}|\cdots|Z^{(p)}_{j}|\right]

\displaystyle=\bigg(\prod_{k=1}^{t}\mathbb{E}\|X^{(k)}\|\bigg)\mathbb{E}_{(Z^{(k)}_{i})_{i=1}^{N},k\geq t+1}\left[\max_{1\leq j\leq N}|Z^{(t+1)}_{j}|\cdots|Z^{(p)}_{j}|\right]

\displaystyle\asymp_{p}\bigg(\prod_{k=1}^{t}\|\Sigma^{(k)}\|^{1/2}\bigg)\bigg(\prod_{k=1}^{t}r(\Sigma^{(k)})^{1/2}\bigg)(\log N)^{(p-t)/2},

(3.2)

where in the last step we use
Lemma
A.1
from Appendix
A
, together with the fact that the random variables
Z^{(k)}_{i}
, for
1\leq i\leq N
and
t+1\leq k\leq p
, are i.i.d. standard Gaussian. Combining (
3.1
) and (
3.2
) completes the proof of
Claim II
.
∎

Remark 3.1

(Discussion on Proposition
3.1
)
.

The conditioning idea used in the proof of
Claim I
can be viewed as a multi-product analogue of the lower-bound argument in
[
19
, Theorem 4]
, where it is shown that for i.i.d. centered Gaussian random variables
X,X_{1},\ldots,X_{N}
with covariance operator
\Sigma
,

\mathbb{E}\bigg\|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}X_{i}\otimes X_{i}-\mathbb{E}\,X\otimes X\bigg\|\;\gtrsim\;\|\Sigma\|\sqrt{\frac{r(\Sigma)}{N}}.

In our multi-product setting, the key difference is that the tensor factors may correspond to different random variables. We therefore begin by focusing on the component
X^{(1)}
whose covariance has the largest effective rank. Under independence across components, the argument reduces to a clean conditioning step that isolates the contribution of this dominant factor.

The proof of
Claim II
is where the additional logarithmic factors in the asymmetric case arise. The idea is to choose test vectors as follows: for those components whose covariance effective ranks are smaller than
\log N
, we take the unit eigenvector corresponding to the largest eigenvalue and denote the resulting normalized scalar variables by
(Z_{i}^{(k)})_{i=1}^{N}
for
k\geq t+1
. Conditioning on
\{Z_{i}^{(k)}:1\leq i\leq N,k\geq t+1\}
, we can move the expectation inside the absolute value to obtain a lower bound, and, crucially, eliminate all but one of the
N
summands. More precisely, we essentially keep only the index
j
corresponding to the largest fluctuation of

|Z_{j}^{(t+1)}|\cdots|Z_{j}^{(p)}|.

Since there are
N
i.i.d. copies and the components are independent, this maximum is typically of order
(\log N)^{(p-t)/2}
, yielding the sharp logarithmic contribution. In this way, the factors with larger effective ranks contribute through their effective-rank terms, while the smaller-rank factors contribute through the maximal fluctuation across the
N
i.i.d. draws. By contrast, a direct approach that simply moves expectation into the absolute value without this selection would only yield a lower bound of order

\frac{1}{N}\bigg(\prod_{k=1}^{p}\|\Sigma^{(k)}\|^{1/2}\bigg)\prod_{k=1}^{p}r(\Sigma^{(k)})^{1/2},

which corresponds to the “no-log” case
t=p
. The above extremal-selection observation is instrumental to obtain the sharper lower bound that matches our upper bound.

Finally, we emphasize that we assume independence across tensor components only to provide a clean special case in which our general upper bound is provably tight, without imposing any additional structural assumptions on the correlations between components. In particular, the lower-bound mechanism in the proof makes clear that there are many settings beyond full independence in which the same kind of extremal-fluctuation phenomenon can occur, and hence where the resulting logarithmic factors may still appear and be unavoidable.

4
Multi-product empirical processes

This section studies multi-product empirical processes indexed by
p
possibly different function classes
(\mathcal{F}^{(k)})_{k=1}^{p}
, evaluated at
p
possibly different random variables
(X^{(k)})_{k=1}^{p}
:

\displaystyle(f^{(1)},\ldots,f^{(p)})\mapsto\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\prod_{k=1}^{p}f^{(k)}(X^{(k)}_{i})-\mathbb{E}\prod_{k=1}^{p}f^{(k)}(X^{(k)}),\qquad f^{(k)}\in\mathcal{F}^{(k)},\ 1\leq k\leq p.

(4.1)

We leverage generic chaining techniques to derive sharp upper bounds on their suprema in terms of quantities that reflect the complexity of the function classes
(\mathcal{F}^{(k)})_{k=1}^{p}
.

Our theory builds on the framework introduced by Mendelson in
[
25
]
. The central idea is as follows. First, we gather precise structural information on the typical coordinate projection of
\mathcal{F}^{(k)},1\leq k\leq p
; that is, for
\sigma^{(k)}=(X^{(k)}_{1},\ldots,X^{(k)}_{N}),1\leq k\leq p
, we study the structure of

P_{\sigma^{(k)}}(\mathcal{F}^{(k)})=\left\{(f^{(k)}(X^{(k)}_{i}))_{i=1}^{N}:f^{(k)}\in\mathcal{F}^{(k)}\right\},\quad 1\leq k\leq p.

In order to study multi-product processes with
p\geq 3
, it is further necessary to understand the structure of

\bigg(\prod_{k^{\prime}\neq k}f^{(k^{\prime})}(X_{i}^{(k^{\prime})})\bigg)_{i=1}^{N},\quad 1\leq k\leq p,

a structural component that appears to play a central role in the multi-product setting considered here. Second, for typical
\sigma^{(k)}=(X^{(k)}_{1},\ldots,X^{(k)}_{N}),1\leq k\leq p
, we analyze the suprema of the conditioned Bernoulli process:

(f^{(1)},\ldots,f^{(p)})\mapsto\sum_{i=1}^{N}\varepsilon_{i}\prod_{k=1}^{p}f^{(k)}(X^{(k)}_{i})\ \Big|\sigma^{(1)},\ldots,\sigma^{(p)},

where
\varepsilon_{1},\ldots,\varepsilon_{N}
are independent symmetric Bernoulli random variables (that is,
\mathbb{P}(\varepsilon_{n}=+1)=\mathbb{P}(\varepsilon_{n}=-1)=1/2
).

The structure of typical coordinate projections of sub-Gaussian function classes has been extensively studied in empirical process theory
[
22
,
23
,
27
,
24
,
25
]
, primarily in the context of quadratic and product empirical processes. Here, we extend the analysis to empirical processes indexed by
p
different function classes evaluated at
p
different random variables. A key ingredient of our approach is Theorem
4.5
, which establishes a high-probability upper bound on the supremum of the
\ell_{2}
-norm of the product of coordinate projection vectors of the function classes
(\mathcal{F}^{(k)})_{k=1}^{p}
. Specifically, it controls

\sup_{f^{(k)}\in\mathcal{F}^{(k)},1\leq k\leq p}\bigg(\sum_{i=1}^{N}\prod_{k=1}^{p}\left(f^{(k)}(X_{i}^{(k)})\right)^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}

in terms of quantities that capture the geometric complexity of
(\mathcal{F}^{(k)})_{k=1}^{p}
. A novel phenomenon captured by Theorem
4.5
is the emergence of logarithmic factors in the multi-product setting; these factors do not appear in the analysis of quadratic or standard product empirical processes.

The rest of this section is organized as follows. Subsection
4.1
introduces some definitions from Mendelson’s work
[
25
]
and discusses an important result, Lemma
4.3
(
[
25
, Corollary 3.6]
), which plays a crucial role in our proof of Theorem
2.2
. We then prove Theorem
2.2
in Subsection
4.2
. Finally, Subsection
4.3
contains the proof of Theorem
4.5
and Subsection
4.4
discusses how to extend our theory to log-concave ensembles.

4.1
Background and auxiliary results

Following
[
25
]
, we will utilize complexity parameters that take into account all the
L_{p}
structures endowed by the process. We recall that, for
q\geq 1,
the graded

-norm is defined by

\|f\|_{(q)}:=\sup_{1\leq p\leq q}\frac{\|f\|_{L_{p}}}{\sqrt{p}}.

We will use repeatedly that, for any

As shown in
[
25
]
, the graded
\gamma
-type functionals that we now define serve as key parameters to characterize the complexity of the typical coordinate projection of a function class.

Definition 4.1

(Graded
\gamma
-type functionals)
.

Given a class of functions
\mathcal{F}
on a probability space
(\Omega,\mu)
, constants

and
s_{0}\geq 0

\Lambda_{s_{0},u}(\mathcal{F}):=\inf\sup_{f\in\mathcal{F}}\sum_{s\geq s_{0}}2^{s/2}\left\|f-\pi_{s}f\right\|_{(u^{2}2^{s})},

where the infimum is taken over all admissible sequences, and
\pi_{s}f
is the nearest point in
\mathcal{F}_{s}
to
f
with respect to the
(u^{2}2^{s})
-norm. Also set

\widetilde{\Lambda}_{s_{0},u}(\mathcal{F}):=\Lambda_{s_{0},u}(\mathcal{F})+2^{s_{0}/2}\sup_{f\in\mathcal{F}}\left\|\pi_{s_{0}}f\right\|_{(u^{2}2^{s_{0}})}.

The following lemma shows that
\Lambda_{s_{0},u}(\mathcal{F})
is upper bounded by
\gamma(\mathcal{F},\psi_{2})
up to a universal constant.

Lemma 4.1

(
[
2
, Lemma 4.1]
)
.

For any
u\geq 1
and
s_{0}\geq 0
,
\Lambda_{s_{0},u}(\mathcal{F})\lesssim\gamma(\mathcal{F},\psi_{2})
.

To analyze (conditioned) Bernoulli processes, we will use a chaining argument combined with the following Höffding’s inequality.

Lemma 4.2

(Höffding’s inequality,
[
28
]
)
.

Let
\varepsilon_{1},\ldots,\varepsilon_{N}
be independent symmetric Bernoulli random variables. For any fixed
I\subset\{1,\ldots,N\}
and
z\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
, it holds with probability at least
1-2\exp(-t^{2}/2)
that

\bigg|\sum_{i=1}^{N}\varepsilon_{i}z_{i}\bigg|\leq\sum_{i\in I}\left|z_{i}\right|+t\bigg(\sum_{i\in I^{c}}z_{i}^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}.

In particular, for every
1\leq k\leq N,
it holds with probability at least
1-2\exp(-t^{2}/2)
that

\bigg|\sum_{i=1}^{N}\varepsilon_{i}z_{i}\bigg|\leq\sum_{i=1}^{k}\left|z_{i}^{*}\right|+t\bigg(\sum_{i=k+1}^{N}(z_{i}^{*})^{2}\bigg)^{1/2},

where
\left(z_{i}^{*}\right)_{i=1}^{N}
denotes the non-increasing rearrangement of
\left(\left|z_{i}\right|\right)_{i=1}^{N}.
This estimate is optimal when
k\asymp t^{2}
.

According to Lemma
4.2
, the influence of
z
on the Bernoulli process depends on the
\ell_{1}
-norm of the largest
k
coordinates of
(|z_{i}|)_{i=1}^{N}
and the
\ell_{2}
-norm of the smallest
N-k
coordinates. As noted in
[
25
]
, the graded

-norm plays a key role in analyzing the monotone non-increasing rearrangement of
N
independent copies of a random variable. In particular,
[
25

provides a useful way to decompose a collection of random vectors into their largest and smallest coordinate components, a technique central to
[
25

, and replacing

with

in their notation.

Lemma 4.3

(
[
25

)

There exist absolute constants
c_{0},c_{1}
and for every

c_{2},c_{3},c_{4}
and
c_{5}

for which the following holds. Set

\displaystyle j_{s}=\min\left\{\left\lceil\frac{c_{0}u^{2}p2^{s}}{\log\left(4+eN/u^{2}p2^{s}\right)}\right\rceil,N+1\right\}

(4.2)

for
u\geq c_{2}
. Let
\mathcal{H}\subset L_{2r}
be of cardinality at most

For

, let

to the classes
\mathcal{F}^{(k)}_{s}
,

for each

.

Let

.

\big|\{\Delta_{s}f^{(k)}:f^{(k)}\in\mathcal{F}^{(k)}\}\big|\leq 2^{2^{s+2}}<2^{p2^{s+2}}
. Therefore, the first two inequalities follow by applying Lemma

4.2
Proof of Theorem
2.2

This subsection contains the proof of our second main result, Theorem
2.2
. The proof relies on the next result, Theorem
4.5
, which establishes a high-probability upper bound on the
\ell_{2}
-norm of the product of coordinate projection vectors of the function classes
(\mathcal{F}^{(k)})_{k=1}^{p}
. The proof of Theorem
4.5
, along with some discussion, is deferred to Subsection
4.3
.

Theorem 4.5
.

Under the setting of Theorem
2.2
, for any
u>0
, it holds with probability at least
1-\exp(-u^{2})
that

\displaystyle\sup_{f^{(k)}\in\mathcal{F}^{(k)},1\leq k\leq p}\bigg(\sum_{i=1}^{N}\prod_{k=1}^{p}\left(f^{(k)}(X_{i}^{(k)})\right)^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\qquad\lesssim_{p}\bigg(\prod_{k=1}^{p}d_{\psi_{2}}(\mathcal{F}^{(k)})\bigg)\bigg(\sqrt{N}+u\sum_{k=1}^{p}\bar{\gamma}(\mathcal{F}^{(k)},\psi_{2})\prod_{k^{\prime}\neq k}\Big(\bar{\gamma}(\mathcal{F}^{(k^{\prime})},\psi_{2})+(\log N)^{1/2}+u\Big)\bigg),

where
\bar{\gamma}(\mathcal{F}^{(k)},\psi_{2}):=\gamma(\mathcal{F}^{(k)},\psi_{2})/d_{\psi_{2}}(\mathcal{F}^{(k)})
for each
1\leq k\leq p
.

Remark 4.1
.

Integrating the tail bound in Theorem
4.5
yields

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\sup_{f^{(k)}\in\mathcal{F}^{(k)},1\leq k\leq p}\bigg(\sum_{i=1}^{N}\prod_{k=1}^{p}\left(f^{(k)}(X_{i}^{(k)})\right)^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{p}\bigg(\prod_{k=1}^{p}d_{\psi_{2}}(\mathcal{F}^{(k)})\bigg)\bigg(\sqrt{N}+\sum_{k=1}^{p}\bar{\gamma}(\mathcal{F}^{(k)},\psi_{2})\prod_{k^{\prime}\neq k}\Big(\bar{\gamma}(\mathcal{F}^{(k^{\prime})},\psi_{2})+(\log N)^{1/2}\Big)\bigg)

\displaystyle\asymp_{p}\bigg(\prod_{k=1}^{p}d_{\psi_{2}}(\mathcal{F}^{(k)})\bigg)\bigg(\sqrt{N}+\prod_{k=1}^{p}\Big(\bar{\gamma}(\mathcal{F}^{(k)},\psi_{2})+(\log N)^{1/2}\Big)\bigg).

(4.3)

The expectation bound (
4.3
) is optimal up to a constant depending only on
p
; in particular, the logarithmic factors are unavoidable. We demonstrate the sharpness of (
4.3
) with a simple example in Appendix
B
.

Now we are ready to prove Theorem
2.2
.

Proof of Theorem
2.2
.

We first observe that the condition
0\in\mathcal{F}^{(k)}
or the symmetry of
\mathcal{F}^{(k)}
(i.e.,
f\in\mathcal{F}^{(k)}
implies
-f\in\mathcal{F}^{(k)}
) ensures that
\gamma(\mathcal{F}^{(k)},\psi_{2})\gtrsim d_{\psi_{2}}(\mathcal{F}^{(k)})
(see
[
2
, Lemma 4.6]
), a fact that we will use repeatedly in the proof.

Let
(\mathcal{F}^{(k)}_{s})_{s\geq 0}
be an admissible sequence of
\mathcal{F}^{(k)}.
Let
u\geq 4
and
s_{0}\geq 0
. For any
f^{(k)}\in\mathcal{F}^{(k)}
and
s\geq 0
, let
\pi_{s}f^{(k)}
denote the nearest point in
\mathcal{F}^{(k)}_{s}
to
f^{(k)}
with respect to the
(u^{2}2^{s})
-norm under the probability measure
\mu^{(k)}
.

The proof will use
symmetrization
, an important technique in empirical process theory
[
35
, Chapter 2.3]
. Let
(\varepsilon_{i})_{i=1}^{N}
be independent symmetric Bernoulli random variables that are independent of
(X^{(k)}_{i})_{i=1}^{N},1\leq k\leq p
. We seek to establish a high-probability upper bound on the supremum of the Bernoulli process given by

\sup_{f^{(k)}\in\mathcal{F}^{(k)},1\leq k\leq p}\bigg|\sum_{i=1}^{N}\varepsilon_{i}\prod_{k=1}^{p}f^{(k)}(X^{(k)}_{i})\bigg|.

To that end, we first note that any
\prod_{k=1}^{p}f^{(k)}
can be represented as a chain, or telescoping sum, of links of the form
\prod_{k=1}^{p}\pi_{s+1}f^{(k)}-\prod_{k=1}^{p}\pi_{s}f^{(k)}.
Namely

\displaystyle\prod_{k=1}^{p}f^{(k)}=\sum_{s\geq s_{0}}\bigg(\prod_{k=1}^{p}\pi_{s+1}f^{(k)}-\prod_{k=1}^{p}\pi_{s}f^{(k)}\bigg)+\prod_{k=1}^{p}\pi_{s_{0}}f^{(k)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{s\geq s_{0}}\bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{p}(\pi_{s+1}f^{(1)})\cdots(\pi_{s+1}f^{(k-1)})(\pi_{s+1}f^{(k)}-\pi_{s}f^{(k)})(\pi_{s}f^{(k+1)})\cdots(\pi_{s}f^{(p)})\bigg)+\prod_{k=1}^{p}\pi_{s_{0}}f^{(k)},

provided that
\pi^{(k)}_{s}f^{(k)}\to f^{(k)}
as
s\to\infty
. Here, in the second line, for each
s
, we used the telescoping identity
a_{1}\cdots a_{p}-b_{1}\cdots b_{p}=\sum_{k=1}^{p}a_{1}\cdots a_{k-1}\left(a_{k}-b_{k}\right)b_{k+1}\cdots b_{p}
with
a_{k}=\pi_{s+1}f^{(k)}
and
b_{k}=\pi_{s}f^{(k)}
.

To ease notation, we write
(\pi_{s}f^{(k)})_{i}:=(\pi_{s}f^{(k)})(X_{i}^{(k)})
,
\Delta_{s}f^{(k)}:=\pi_{s+1}f^{(k)}-\pi_{s}f^{(k)}
and
(\Delta_{s}f^{(k)})_{i}:=(\Delta_{s}f^{(k)})(X^{(k)}_{i})
. We will use this notation throughout the remainder of the paper. By the triangle inequality,

\displaystyle\bigg|\sum_{i=1}^{N}\varepsilon_{i}\prod_{k=1}^{p}f^{(k)}(X^{(k)}_{i})\bigg|

\displaystyle=\bigg|\sum_{i=1}^{N}\varepsilon_{i}\bigg[\sum_{s\geq s_{0}}\bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{p}(\pi_{s+1}f^{(1)})_{i}\cdots(\pi_{s+1}f^{(k-1)})_{i}(\Delta_{s}f^{(k)})_{i}(\pi_{s}f^{(k+1)})_{i}\cdots(\pi_{s}f^{(p)})_{i}\bigg)+\prod_{k=1}^{p}(\pi_{s_{0}}f^{(k)})_{i}\bigg]\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{s\geq s_{0}}\sum_{k=1}^{p}\bigg|\sum_{i=1}^{N}\varepsilon_{i}(\pi_{s+1}f^{(1)})_{i}\cdots(\pi_{s+1}f^{(k-1)})_{i}(\Delta_{s}f^{(k)})_{i}(\pi_{s}f^{(k+1)})_{i}\cdots(\pi_{s}f^{(p)})_{i}\bigg|+\bigg|\sum_{i=1}^{N}\varepsilon_{i}\prod_{k=1}^{p}(\pi_{s_{0}}f^{(k)})_{i}\bigg|

\displaystyle=\sum_{s\geq s_{0}}\sum_{k=1}^{p}\bigg|\sum_{i=1}^{N}\varepsilon_{i}\bigg((\Delta_{s}f^{(k)})_{i}\prod_{k^{\prime}<k}(\pi_{s+1}f^{(k^{\prime})})_{i}\prod_{k^{\prime}>k}(\pi_{s}f^{(k^{\prime})})_{i}\bigg)\bigg|+\bigg|\sum_{i=1}^{N}\varepsilon_{i}\prod_{k=1}^{p}(\pi_{s_{0}}f^{(k)})_{i}\bigg|.

(4.4)

Let
(j_{s})_{s\geq s_{0}}
be the sequence defined in (
4.2
). For each
s\geq s_{0}
, let
I_{s}
be the union of the
j_{s}-1

(|\Delta_{s}f^{(k)}|_{i})_{i=1}^{N}
and the
j_{s}-1
largest coordinates of

\bigg(\Big|\prod_{k^{\prime}<k}(\pi_{s+1}f^{(k^{\prime})})_{i}\prod_{k^{\prime}>k}(\pi_{s}f^{(k^{\prime})})_{i}\Big|\bigg)_{i=1}^{N},

for every
1\leq k\leq p
. Then
|I_{s}|\leq 2p(j_{s}-1)
.
We apply Lemma
4.2
with
t=up^{1/2}2^{s/2}
and the set
I_{s}
to the summand indexed by
s
and
k
in (
4.4
). This implies that, for every
s,k
and every
f^{(k)}\in\mathcal{F}^{(k)}(1\leq k\leq p)
, with probability at least
1-2\exp(-u^{2}p2^{s-1})
, the following holds:

\displaystyle\bigg|\sum_{i=1}^{N}\varepsilon_{i}\bigg((\Delta_{s}f^{(k)})_{i}\prod_{k^{\prime}<k}(\pi_{s+1}f^{(k^{\prime})})_{i}\prod_{k^{\prime}>k}(\pi_{s}f^{(k^{\prime})})_{i}\bigg)\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i\in I_{s}}\bigg|(\Delta_{s}f^{(k)})_{i}\prod_{k^{\prime}<k}(\pi_{s+1}f^{(k^{\prime})})_{i}\prod_{k^{\prime}>k}(\pi_{s}f^{(k^{\prime})})_{i}\bigg|

\displaystyle\quad+up^{1/2}2^{s/2}\bigg(\sum_{i\in I_{s}^{c}}(\Delta_{s}f^{(k)})^{2}_{i}\prod_{k^{\prime}<k}(\pi_{s+1}f^{(k^{\prime})})^{2}_{i}\prod_{k^{\prime}>k}(\pi_{s}f^{(k^{\prime})})^{2}_{i}\bigg)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\overset{\text{($\star$)}}{\leq}\bigg(\sum_{i\in I_{s}}(\Delta_{s}f^{(k)})^{2}_{i}\bigg)^{1/2}\bigg(\sum_{i\in I_{s}}\prod_{k^{\prime}<k}(\pi_{s+1}f^{(k^{\prime})})^{2}_{i}\prod_{k^{\prime}>k}(\pi_{s}f^{(k^{\prime})})^{2}_{i}\bigg)^{1/2}

4.3
Proof of Theorem
4.5

This subsection presents the proof of Theorem
4.5
, which establishes a high-probability upper bound on the
\ell_{2}
-norm of the product of coordinate projection vectors associated with the function classes
(\mathcal{F}^{(k)})_{k=1}^{p}
:

\sup_{f^{(k)}\in\mathcal{F}^{(k)},1\leq k\leq p}\bigg(\sum_{i=1}^{N}\prod_{k=1}^{p}\left(f^{(k)}(X_{i}^{(k)})\right)^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}.

The proof is based on generic chaining for the classes
(\mathcal{F}^{(k)})_{k=1}^{p}
, and builds on ideas of Mendelson
[
25
]
for controlling multiplier and product processes, including the use of order statistics to separate the contributions of the largest and smallest coordinates of the increments (via monotone sums). In this sense, our result can be viewed as a multi-product analogue of
[
25
, Lemma 2.3]
, which corresponds (roughly) to the case
p=1
. The main new difficulty is that we must handle
p
potentially different function classes evaluated at
p
different random variables, and leverage the resulting multilinear structure to carry out chaining across these classes.

A crucial step in controlling the increments is to incorporate
\ell_{\infty}
-norm bounds on the relevant coordinate projection vectors at appropriate stages of the chaining argument, which introduces
\sqrt{\log N}
factors. Although this bound may appear crude, it is in fact sharp: these
\sqrt{\log N}
terms are unavoidable in general; see Appendix
B
. In particular, even if some class
\mathcal{F}^{(k)}
is “simple” in a global complexity sense, its contribution to the overall bound still necessarily includes at least a
\sqrt{\log N}
term, reflecting its largest coordinate fluctuation in
\ell_{\infty}
. This viewpoint provides a principled way to understand the behavior of products of coordinate projection vectors across heterogeneous classes
(\mathcal{F}^{(k)})_{k=1}^{p}
and different underlying random variables.

We note that when
\mathcal{F}^{(1)}=\cdots=\mathcal{F}^{(p)}=\mathcal{F}
,
X^{(1)}=\cdots=X^{(p)}=X
, and
X^{(1)}_{i}=\cdots=X^{(p)}_{i}=X_{i}\
for all
1\leq i\leq N,
Theorem
4.5
reduces to
[
2
, Theorem 4.5]
. However, the proof presented here is genuinely different from that of
[
2
, Theorem 4.5]
, which relies on chaining with a Young-function argument and separates the sub-Gaussian and sub-exponential regimes. In contrast, our setting involves heterogeneous function classes evaluated at different random variables and requires incorporating
\ell_{\infty}
-norm control within the chaining procedure, together with a sharp accounting of the resulting
\sqrt{\log N}
factors. These features —and in particular the necessity of the
\sqrt{\log N}
terms— are not captured by the approach in
[
2
]
.

We begin by establishing several lemmas needed for the proof of Theorem
4.5
.

The following result provides a sharp high-probability upper bound for the
\ell_{\infty}
-norm of the coordinate projection vector
(f(X_{i}))_{i=1}^{N}
.

Lemma 4.6
.

For any
u>0
, it holds with probability at least
1-\exp(-u^{2})
that

\sup_{f\in\mathcal{F}}\max_{1\leq i\leq N}|f(X_{i})|\lesssim\gamma(\mathcal{F},\psi_{2})+d_{\psi_{2}}(\mathcal{F})(\sqrt{\log N}+u).

4.6
.

By
[
13

(see also
[
2
, Theorem 4.5]

that

\sup_{f\in\mathcal{F}}|f(X)|\lesssim\gamma(\mathcal{F},\psi_{2})+d_{\psi_{2}}(\mathcal{F})t.

Taking a union bound yields that, with probability at least

\max_{1\leq i\leq N}\sup_{f\in\mathcal{F}}|f(X_{i})|\lesssim\gamma(\mathcal{F},\psi_{2})+d_{\psi_{2}}(\mathcal{F})t.

Setting

∎

whenever
1<j_{s-1},j_{s}<N+1
, as can be seen from (
4.2
). Indeed, we estimate:

\displaystyle\sum_{s=s_{0}}^{s_{1}-2}\bigg(\sum_{i=j_{s}}^{j_{s+1}-1}\left(\frac{eN}{i}\right)^{3/4}\bigg)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq(eN)^{3/8}\sum_{s=s_{0}}^{s_{1}-2}2\left((j_{s+1}-1)^{1/4}-(j_{s}-1)^{1/4}\right)^{1/2}\lesssim N^{3/8}\sum_{s=s_{0}}^{s_{1}-2}(j_{s+1}-1)^{1/8}

\displaystyle\lesssim N^{3/8}(j_{s_{1}-1}-1)^{1/8}\bigg(1+\sum_{s\geq 1}2^{-s/8}\bigg)\lesssim N^{1/2}.

Taking
s_{0}\asymp 1
, we have

\widetilde{\Lambda}_{s_{0},u}(\mathcal{F}^{(k)})=\Lambda_{s_{0},u}(\mathcal{F}^{(k)})+2^{s_{0}/2}\sup_{f^{(k)}\in\mathcal{F}^{(k)}}\left\|\pi_{s_{0}}f^{(k)}\right\|_{(u^{2}2^{s_{0}})}\lesssim\gamma(\mathcal{F}^{(k)},\psi_{2})+2^{s_{0}/2}d_{\psi_{2}}(\mathcal{F}^{(k)})\lesssim\gamma(\mathcal{F}^{(k)},\psi_{2}).

Consequently, it holds with probability at least
1-c_{1}(p)\exp(-c_{2}(p)u^{2})
that

\displaystyle\sup_{f^{(k)}\in\mathcal{F}^{(k)},1\leq k\leq p}\bigg(\sum_{i=1}^{N}\prod_{k=1}^{p}\left(f^{(k)}(X_{i}^{(k)})\right)^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}=\sup_{f^{(k)}\in\mathcal{F}^{(k)},1\leq k\leq p}\bigg(\sum_{i=1}^{N}\prod_{k=1}^{p}(f^{(k)})_{i}^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{p}\bigg(\prod_{k=1}^{p}d_{\psi_{2}}(\mathcal{F}^{(k)})\bigg)\bigg(\sqrt{N}+u\sum_{k=1}^{p}\bar{\gamma}(\mathcal{F}^{(k)},\psi_{2})\prod_{k^{\prime}\neq k}\Big(\bar{\gamma}(\mathcal{F}^{(k^{\prime})},\psi_{2})+(\log N)^{1/2}+u\Big)\bigg),

where
\bar{\gamma}(\mathcal{F}^{(k)},\psi_{2}):=\gamma(\mathcal{F}^{(k)},\psi_{2})/d_{\psi_{2}}(\mathcal{F}^{(k)})
for
1\leq k\leq p
. This completes the proof.
∎

4.4
Log-concave ensembles

In this subsection, we briefly outline how the proofs of Theorem
2.1
and Theorem
2.2
can be modified to establish the result stated in Remark
2.2
for isotropic, unconditional, log-concave ensembles.

For integer
p\geq 2
and
1\leq k\leq p
, let
X^{(k)},X_{1}^{(k)},\ldots,X_{N}^{(k)}
be i.i.d. isotropic, unconditional, log-concave random vectors in
\mathbb{R}^{d_{k}}
. For any function
f
defined on
(\Omega,\mu)
, we introduce the Orlicz
\psi_{1}
-norm of
f
given by

\|f\|_{\psi_{1}}:=\inf\left\{c>0:\mathbb{E}_{X\sim\mu}\left[\exp\left(\frac{|f(X)|}{c}\right)\right]\leq 2\right\},

with the convention
\inf\emptyset=\infty.
We define
d_{\psi_{1}}(\mathcal{F}):=\sup_{f\in\mathcal{F}}\|f\|_{\psi_{1}}
.

Recall that in the proof of Theorem
2.2
, we established in (
4.5
) that it holds with probability at least
1-2\exp(-u^{2})
that

\displaystyle\bigg|\sum_{i=1}^{N}\varepsilon_{i}\prod_{k=1}^{p}f^{(k)}(X^{(k)}_{i})\bigg|

\displaystyle\lesssim_{p}\sum_{k=1}^{p}u\widetilde{\Lambda}_{s_{0},cu}(\mathcal{F}^{(k)})\sup_{f^{(k^{\prime})}\in\mathcal{F}^{(k^{\prime})},k^{\prime}\neq k}\bigg(\sum_{i=1}^{N}\prod_{k^{\prime}\neq k}(f^{(k^{\prime})})^{2}_{i}\bigg)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\quad+uN^{1/2}\sum_{k=1}^{p}\bigg(\sum_{s\geq s_{0}}2^{s/2}\|\Delta_{s}f^{(k)}\|_{L_{8}}\bigg\|\prod_{k^{\prime}<k}\pi_{s+1}f^{(k^{\prime})}\prod_{k^{\prime}>k}\pi_{s}f^{(k^{\prime})}\bigg\|_{L_{8}}\bigg)

\displaystyle\quad+u2^{s_{0}/2}N^{1/2}\|\pi_{s_{0}}f^{(1)}\|_{L_{8}}\bigg\|\prod_{k^{\prime}\neq 1}\pi_{s_{0}}f^{(k^{\prime})}\bigg\|_{L_{8}}.

Moreover, in the proof of Theorem
4.5
, we established in (
4.9
) that it holds with probability
1-c_{1}(p)\exp(-c_{2}(p)u^{2}2^{s_{0}})
that, for every
f^{(k)}\in\mathcal{F}^{(k)}\,(1\leq k\leq p)
,

\displaystyle\bigg(\sum_{i=1}^{N}\prod_{k=1}^{p}\left(f^{(k)}(X_{i}^{(k)})\right)^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{p}N^{1/2}\bigg\|\prod_{k=1}^{p}\pi_{s_{1}}f^{(k)}\bigg\|_{L_{4}}+\sum_{s=s_{0}}^{s_{1}-2}\bigg\|\prod_{k=1}^{p}\pi_{s+1}f^{(k)}\bigg\|_{L_{4}}\bigg(\sum_{i=j_{s}}^{j_{s+1}-1}\left(\frac{eN}{i}\right)^{3/4}\bigg)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{k=1}^{p}u\bigg(\sum_{s\geq s_{0}}2^{s/2}\|\Delta_{s}f^{(k)}\|_{(c_{3}u^{2}2^{s})}+2^{s_{0}/2}\|\pi_{s_{0}}f^{(k)}\|_{(c_{3}u^{2}2^{s_{0}})}\bigg)\bigg(\prod_{k^{\prime}\neq k}\sup_{f^{(k^{\prime})}\in\mathcal{F}^{(k^{\prime})}}\|f^{(k^{\prime})}\|_{\ell^{N}_{\infty}}\bigg)

\displaystyle\lesssim_{p}N^{1/2}\bigg(\sup_{f^{(k)}\in\mathcal{F}^{(k)},1\leq k\leq p}\bigg\|\prod_{k=1}^{p}f^{(k)}\bigg\|_{L_{4}}\bigg)+u\sum_{k=1}^{p}\widetilde{\Lambda}_{s_{0},u}(\mathcal{F}^{(k)})\bigg(\prod_{k^{\prime}\neq k}\sup_{f^{(k^{\prime})}\in\mathcal{F}^{(k^{\prime})}}\|f^{(k^{\prime})}\|_{\ell^{N}_{\infty}}\bigg).

Combining the two bounds and taking
s_{0}\asymp 1
gives that, with probability at least
1-\exp(-u^{2}),

\displaystyle\bigg|\sum_{i=1}^{N}\varepsilon_{i}\prod_{k=1}^{p}f^{(k)}(X^{(k)}_{i})\bigg|

\displaystyle\lesssim_{p}\sum_{k=1}^{p}u\widetilde{\Lambda}_{s_{0},cu}(\mathcal{F}^{(k)})\left[N^{1/2}\bigg(\sup_{f^{(k^{\prime})}\in\mathcal{F}^{(k^{\prime})}}\bigg\|\prod_{k^{\prime}\neq k}f^{(k^{\prime})}\bigg\|_{L_{4}}\bigg)+u\sum_{k^{\prime}\neq k}\widetilde{\Lambda}_{s_{0},u}(\mathcal{F}^{(k^{\prime})})\bigg(\prod_{k^{\prime\prime}\neq k,k^{\prime}}\sup_{f^{(k^{\prime\prime})}\in\mathcal{F}^{(k^{\prime\prime})}}\|f^{(k^{\prime\prime})}\|_{\ell^{N}_{\infty}}\bigg)\right]

\displaystyle\quad+uN^{1/2}\sum_{k=1}^{p}\bigg(\sum_{s\geq s_{0}}2^{s/2}\|\Delta_{s}f^{(k)}\|_{L_{8}}\bigg\|\prod_{k^{\prime}<k}\pi_{s+1}f^{(k^{\prime})}\prod_{k^{\prime}>k}\pi_{s}f^{(k^{\prime})}\bigg\|_{L_{8}}\bigg)

\displaystyle\quad+u2^{s_{0}/2}N^{1/2}\|\pi_{s_{0}}f^{(1)}\|_{L_{8}}\bigg\|\prod_{k^{\prime}\neq 1}\pi_{s_{0}}f^{(k^{\prime})}\bigg\|_{L_{8}}.

Applying the inequalities

\displaystyle\sup_{f^{(k^{\prime})}\in\mathcal{F}^{(k^{\prime})}}\bigg\|\prod_{k^{\prime}\neq k}f^{(k^{\prime})}\bigg\|_{L_{4}}

\displaystyle\leq\prod_{k^{\prime}\neq k}\sup_{f^{(k^{\prime})}\in\mathcal{F}^{(k^{\prime})}}\|f^{(k^{\prime})}\|_{L_{4(p-1)}}\lesssim_{p}\prod_{k^{\prime}\neq k}d_{\psi_{1}}(\mathcal{F}^{(k^{\prime})}),

\displaystyle\bigg\|\prod_{k^{\prime}<k}\pi_{s+1}f^{(k^{\prime})}\prod_{k^{\prime}>k}\pi_{s}f^{(k^{\prime})}\bigg\|_{L_{8}}

\displaystyle\leq\prod_{k^{\prime}<k}\|\pi_{s+1}f^{(k^{\prime})}\|_{L_{8(p-1)}}\prod_{k^{\prime}>k}\|\pi_{s}f^{(k^{\prime})}\|_{L_{8(p-1)}}\lesssim_{p}\prod_{k^{\prime}\neq k}d_{\psi_{1}}(\mathcal{F}^{(k^{\prime})}),

\displaystyle\sum_{s\geq s_{0}}2^{s/2}\|\Delta_{s}f^{(k)}\|_{L_{8}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{s\geq s_{0}}2^{s/2}\|\Delta_{s}f^{(k)}\|_{\psi_{1}}\lesssim\gamma(\mathcal{F}^{(k)},\psi_{1}),

\displaystyle\|\pi_{s_{0}}f^{(1)}\|_{L_{8}}\bigg\|\prod_{k^{\prime}\neq 1}\pi_{s_{0}}f^{(k^{\prime})}\bigg\|_{L_{8}}

\displaystyle\leq\|\pi_{s_{0}}f^{(1)}\|_{L_{8}}\prod_{k^{\prime}\neq 1}\|\pi_{s_{0}}f^{(k^{\prime})}\|_{L_{8(p-1)}}\lesssim_{p}\prod_{k=1}^{p}d_{\psi_{1}}(\mathcal{F}^{(k)}),

we obtain

\displaystyle\bigg|\sum_{i=1}^{N}\varepsilon_{i}\prod_{k=1}^{p}f^{(k)}(X^{(k)}_{i})\bigg|

\displaystyle\lesssim_{p}\sum_{k=1}^{p}u\widetilde{\Lambda}_{s_{0},cu}(\mathcal{F}^{(k)})\left[N^{1/2}\prod_{k^{\prime}\neq k}d_{\psi_{1}}(\mathcal{F}^{(k^{\prime})})+u\sum_{k^{\prime}\neq k}\widetilde{\Lambda}_{s_{0},u}(\mathcal{F}^{(k^{\prime})})\bigg(\prod_{k^{\prime\prime}\neq k,k^{\prime}}\sup_{f^{(k^{\prime\prime})}\in\mathcal{F}^{(k^{\prime\prime})}}\|f^{(k^{\prime\prime})}\|_{\ell^{N}_{\infty}}\bigg)\right]

\displaystyle\quad+uN^{1/2}\sum_{k=1}^{p}\gamma(\mathcal{F}^{(k)},\psi_{1})\prod_{k^{\prime}\neq k}d_{\psi_{1}}(\mathcal{F}^{(k^{\prime})})+uN^{1/2}\prod_{k=1}^{p}d_{\psi_{1}}(\mathcal{F}^{(k)}).

We use Borell’s characterization of log-concave distributions
[
1
, Lemma 2.3]
: if
X
is a centered random vector in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with a log-concave distribution, then for any
v\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
,

\|\langle X,v\rangle\|_{\psi_{1}}\asymp\|\langle X,v\rangle\|_{L_{2}}.

Therefore, for
\mathcal{F}^{(k)}=\{\langle\cdot,v\rangle:v\in\mathbb{S}^{d_{k}-1}\}
, we have

\displaystyle d_{\psi_{1}}(\mathcal{F}^{(k)})=\sup_{f^{(k)}\in\mathcal{F}^{(k)}}\|f^{(k)}\|_{\psi_{1}}\asymp\sup_{f^{(k)}\in\mathcal{F}^{(k)}}\|f^{(k)}\|_{L_{2}}=\sup_{\|v\|=1}\big(\mathbb{E}\langle X^{(k)},v\rangle^{2}\big)^{1/2}=1.

For each
1\leq k\leq p
, let
Y^{(k)}
be a standard Gaussian random vector in
\mathbb{R}^{d_{k}}
. Then, for any
u,v\in\mathbb{S}^{d_{k}-1}
, the canonical metric associated with
Y^{(k)}
satisfies

d_{Y^{(k)}}(u,v)=\big(\mathbb{E}(\langle Y^{(k)},u\rangle-\langle Y^{(k)},v\rangle)^{2}\big)^{1/2}=\|u-v\|_{\ell_{2}}=\|\langle\cdot,u\rangle-\langle\cdot,v\rangle\|_{L_{2}(\mu^{(k)})},

where
\mu^{(k)}
denotes the law of
X^{(k)}
, and the last step follows from the fact that
X^{(k)}
is isotropic. Then, we have

\gamma(\mathcal{F}^{(k)},\psi_{1})\overset{\text{$(\star)$}}{\asymp}\gamma(\mathcal{F}^{(k)},L_{2})=\gamma\big(\mathbb{S}^{d_{k}-1},d_{Y^{(k)}}\big)\overset{\text{$(\star\star)$}}{\asymp}\mathbb{E}\sup_{v\in\mathbb{S}^{d_{k}-1}}\langle Y^{(k)},v\rangle=\mathbb{E}\|Y^{(k)}\|\asymp\big(\mathbb{E}\|Y^{(k)}\|^{2}\big)^{1/2}=d_{k}^{1/2},

where
(\star)
follows from the fact that for log-concave random variables, the
\psi_{1}
-norm of any linear functional is equivalent to its
L_{2}
-norm, and
(\star\star)
follows from Talagrand’s majorizing-measure theorem
[
34
, Theorem 2.10.1]
.

For
1\leq k\leq p
, let
Z^{(k)}
be a random vector in
\mathbb{R}^{d_{k}}
whose coordinates are independent standard exponential random variables. For
s_{0}\asymp 1
and
u\geq 1
, it follows from
[
25
, Section 4.3]
that, for the isotropic, unconditional, log-concave random vector
X^{(k)}
, and the linear function class
\mathcal{F}^{(k)}=\{\langle\cdot,v\rangle:v\in\mathbb{S}^{d_{k}-1}\}
, we have

\widetilde{\Lambda}_{s_{0},u}(\mathcal{F}^{(k)})\lesssim u\,\mathbb{E}\|Z^{(k)}\|\leq u\big(\mathbb{E}\|Z^{(k)}\|^{2}\big)^{1/2}=ud_{k}^{1/2}.

By
[
41
, (2.20)]
, for each
k
, we have with probability at least
1-N\exp(-t)
,

\max_{1\leq i\leq N}\|X^{(k)}_{i}\|\lesssim d_{k}^{1/2}+t.

Setting
t=\log N+u^{2}
, we have with probability at least
1-N\exp(-\log N-u^{2})=1-\exp(-u^{2})
that

\sup_{f^{(k)}\in\mathcal{F}^{(k)}}\|f^{(k)}\|_{\ell_{\infty}^{N}}=\sup_{f^{(k)}\in\mathcal{F}^{(k)}}\max_{1\leq i\leq N}|f^{(k)}(X_{i}^{(k)})|=\max_{1\leq i\leq N}\sup_{v\in\mathbb{S}^{d_{k}-1}}|\langle v,X^{(k)}_{i}\rangle|=\max_{1\leq i\leq N}\|X^{(k)}_{i}\|\lesssim d_{k}^{1/2}+\log N+u^{2}.

Combining everything together, for any
u\geq 1
, we have with probability at least
1-\exp(-u^{2})
that

\displaystyle\sup_{f^{(k)}\in\mathcal{F}^{(k)},1\leq k\leq p}\bigg|\sum_{i=1}^{N}\varepsilon_{i}\prod_{k=1}^{p}f^{(k)}(X^{(k)}_{i})\bigg|

\displaystyle\lesssim_{p}\sum_{k=1}^{p}u\widetilde{\Lambda}_{s_{0},cu}(\mathcal{F}^{(k)})\left[N^{1/2}\prod_{k^{\prime}\neq k}d_{\psi_{1}}(\mathcal{F}^{(k^{\prime})})+u\sum_{k^{\prime}\neq k}\widetilde{\Lambda}_{s_{0},u}(\mathcal{F}^{(k^{\prime})})\bigg(\prod_{k^{\prime\prime}\neq k,k^{\prime}}\sup_{f^{(k^{\prime\prime})}\in\mathcal{F}^{(k^{\prime\prime})}}\|f^{(k^{\prime\prime})}\|_{\ell^{N}_{\infty}}\bigg)\right]

\displaystyle\quad+uN^{1/2}\sum_{k=1}^{p}\gamma(\mathcal{F}^{(k)},\psi_{1})\prod_{k^{\prime}\neq k}d_{\psi_{1}}(\mathcal{F}^{(k^{\prime})})+uN^{1/2}\prod_{k=1}^{p}d_{\psi_{1}}(\mathcal{F}^{(k)})

\displaystyle\lesssim_{p}\sum_{k=1}^{p}u^{2}d_{k}^{1/2}\bigg(N^{1/2}+u^{2}\sum_{k^{\prime}\neq k}d_{k^{\prime}}^{1/2}\prod_{k^{\prime\prime}\neq k,k^{\prime}}\big(d_{k^{\prime\prime}}^{1/2}+\log N+u^{2}\big)\bigg)+uN^{1/2}\sum_{k=1}^{p}d_{k}^{1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{p}u^{2}N^{1/2}\bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{p}d_{k}^{1/2}\bigg)+u^{4}\sum_{k\neq k^{\prime}}d_{k}^{1/2}d_{k^{\prime}}^{1/2}\prod_{k^{\prime\prime}\neq k,k^{\prime}}\big(d_{k^{\prime\prime}}^{1/2}+\log N+u^{2}\big).

Integrating the tail bound yields the following bound in expectation:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\sup_{f^{(k)}\in\mathcal{F}^{(k)},1\leq k\leq p}\bigg|\sum_{i=1}^{N}\varepsilon_{i}\prod_{k=1}^{p}f^{(k)}(X^{(k)}_{i})\bigg|

\displaystyle\lesssim_{p}N^{1/2}\bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{p}d_{k}^{1/2}\bigg)+\sum_{k\neq k^{\prime}}d_{k}^{1/2}d_{k^{\prime}}^{1/2}\prod_{k^{\prime\prime}\neq k,k^{\prime}}\big(d_{k^{\prime\prime}}^{1/2}+\log N\big)

\displaystyle\lesssim_{p}N^{1/2}\bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{p}d_{k}^{1/2}\bigg)+\prod_{k=1}^{p}\big(d_{k}^{1/2}+\log N\big).

Finally, by the symmetrization inequality for empirical processes
[
35
, Lemma 2.3.1]
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg\|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}X^{(1)}_{i}\otimes\cdots\otimes X^{(p)}_{i}-\mathbb{E}\,X^{(1)}\otimes\cdots\otimes X^{(p)}\bigg\|

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\sup_{\|v_{1}\|=\cdots=\|v_{p}\|=1}\bigg|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\prod_{k=1}^{p}\langle X^{(k)}_{i},v_{k}\rangle-\mathbb{E}\prod_{k=1}^{p}\langle X^{(k)},v_{k}\rangle\bigg|

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\sup_{f^{(k)}\in\mathcal{F}^{(k)},1\leq k\leq p}\bigg|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\prod_{k=1}^{p}f^{(k)}(X^{(k)}_{i})-\mathbb{E}\prod_{k=1}^{p}f^{(k)}(X^{(k)})\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq 2\mathbb{E}\sup_{f^{(k)}\in\mathcal{F}^{(k)},1\leq k\leq p}\bigg|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\varepsilon_{i}\prod_{k=1}^{p}f^{(k)}(X^{(k)}_{i})\bigg|

\displaystyle\lesssim_{p}\bigg(\frac{\sum_{k=1}^{p}d_{k}}{N}\bigg)^{1/2}+\frac{1}{N}\prod_{k=1}^{p}\big(d_{k}^{1/2}+\log N\big).

Appendix A
Proofs of auxiliary results

Lemma A.1
.

Let
Z^{(k)}_{i},1\leq i\leq N,1\leq k\leq s
be i.i.d. standard Gaussian random variables in
\mathbb{R}
. Then,

\mathbb{E}\max_{1\leq j\leq N}\prod_{k=1}^{s}|Z^{(k)}_{j}|\asymp_{s}(\log N)^{s/2}.

Proof.

For the upper bound, we have

\mathbb{E}\max_{1\leq j\leq N}\prod_{k=1}^{s}|Z^{(k)}_{j}|\leq\mathbb{E}\prod_{k=1}^{s}\max_{1\leq j\leq N}|Z^{(k)}_{j}|=\prod_{k=1}^{s}\mathbb{E}\max_{1\leq j\leq N}|Z^{(k)}_{j}|\overset{\text{$(\star)$}}{\leq}\left(C\sqrt{\log N}\right)^{s}\lesssim_{s}(\log N)^{s/2},

where the inequality
(\star)
follows by
[
34
, Lemma 2.3.4]
. Now we prove the lower bound. Let
Z,Z^{(1)},\ldots,Z^{(s)}
be i.i.d. standard Gaussian random variables in
\mathbb{R}
. By
[
36
, Proposition 2.1.2]
, there exists positive constants
c_{1}\in(0,1),c_{2}\in(1,\infty)
such that, for any
t\geq 2
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left[|Z^{(1)}\cdots Z^{(s)}|\geq t\right]

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{P}\left[|Z^{(1)}|\geq t^{1/s},\ldots,|Z^{(s)}|\geq t^{1/s}\right]=\left(\mathbb{P}\left[|Z|>t^{1/s}\right]\right)^{s}

\displaystyle\geq\left(c_{1}\exp(-c_{2}t^{2/s})\right)^{s}=c_{1}^{s}\exp\left(-sc_{2}t^{2/s}\right).

It follows that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left[\max_{1\leq j\leq N}\prod_{k=1}^{s}|Z^{(k)}_{j}|\geq t\right]=1-\left(1-\mathbb{P}\left[|Z^{(1)}\cdots Z^{(s)}|\geq t\right]\right)^{N}\geq 1-\left(1-c_{1}^{s}\exp\left(-sc_{2}t^{2/s}\right)\right)^{N}.

Taking
t=c_{3}(\log N)^{s/2}
for some sufficiently small constant
c_{3}
depending only on
s
, we obtain

\mathbb{P}\left[\max_{1\leq j\leq N}\prod_{k=1}^{s}|Z^{(k)}_{j}|\geq c_{3}(\log N)^{s/2}\right]\geq 1-\left(1-c_{1}^{s}\exp\left(-sc_{2}c_{3}^{2/s}\log N\right)\right)^{N}\geq\frac{1}{2},

from where it follows that

\mathbb{E}\max_{1\leq j\leq N}\prod_{k=1}^{s}|Z^{(k)}_{j}|\geq\frac{c_{3}}{2}(\log N)^{s/2}.\qed

Lemma A.2
.

Let
X\geq 0
be a random variable. Fix
u_{0}\geq 1
and let
F:[u_{0},\infty)\to(0,\infty)
be increasing. Assume there exist constants

C_{0}\geq 1
,
c_{0}>0
, and
m\geq 1
such that, for all
u\geq u_{0}
,

\mathbb{P}\big[X>F(u)\big]\leq C_{0}e^{-c_{0}u^{2}},

(A.1)

and, for all
u\geq v\geq u_{0}
,

\frac{F(u)}{F(v)}\leq\Big(\frac{u}{v}\Big)^{m}.

(A.2)

Then, for every
q\geq 1
,

\big(\mathbb{E}\,X^{q}\big)^{1/q}\ \leq\ C\,F\!\big(C^{\prime}\sqrt{q}+u_{0}\big),

(A.3)

where
C,C^{\prime}>0
depend only on
C_{0},c_{0},m
, and are independent of
q
and of any parameters implicit in
F
.

Proof.

We use the tail integral identity

\mathbb{E}\,X^{q}=q\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{q-1}\,\mathbb{P}[X>t]\,dt.

Set
t_{0}:=F(u_{0})
. Splitting at
t_{0}
gives

\mathbb{E}\,X^{q}\leq q\int_{0}^{t_{0}}t^{q-1}\,dt\;+\;q\int_{t_{0}}^{\infty}t^{q-1}\mathbb{P}[X>t]\,dt=t_{0}^{q}+I,

where
I:=q\int_{t_{0}}^{\infty}t^{q-1}\mathbb{P}[X>t]\,dt
.

To bound
I
, use the monotone change of variables
t=F(u)
and the tail assumption (
A.1
):

I\leq\int_{u_{0}}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}\big[X>F(u)\big]\,d\!\big(F(u)^{q}\big)\leq C_{0}\int_{u_{0}}^{\infty}e^{-c_{0}u^{2}}\,d\!\big(F(u)^{q}\big).

Integrating by parts yields

\int_{u_{0}}^{\infty}e^{-c_{0}u^{2}}\,d\!\big(F(u)^{q}\big)=-e^{-c_{0}u_{0}^{2}}F(u_{0})^{q}+2c_{0}\int_{u_{0}}^{\infty}u\,e^{-c_{0}u^{2}}\,F(u)^{q}\,du\leq 2c_{0}\int_{u_{0}}^{\infty}u\,e^{-c_{0}u^{2}}\,F(u)^{q}\,du.

Therefore,

\mathbb{E}\,X^{q}\leq F(u_{0})^{q}+2c_{0}C_{0}\int_{u_{0}}^{\infty}u\,e^{-c_{0}u^{2}}\,F(u)^{q}\,du.

(A.4)

Now choose a constant
\alpha\geq 2u_{0}
(to be fixed depending only on
c_{0}
and
m
) and set

u_{\ast}:=\alpha\sqrt{q}.

Split the integral in (
A.4
) at
u_{\ast}
.
For the lower part, monotonicity of
F
gives

\int_{u_{0}}^{u_{\ast}}u\,e^{-c_{0}u^{2}}F(u)^{q}\,du\leq F(u_{\ast})^{q}\int_{0}^{\infty}u\,e^{-c_{0}u^{2}}\,du\lesssim_{c_{0}}F(u_{\ast})^{q}.

For the upper part, use the growth condition (
A.2
):
for
u\geq u_{\ast}\geq u_{0}
,

F(u)\leq F(u_{\ast})\Big(\frac{u}{u_{\ast}}\Big)^{m},

hence

\int_{u_{\ast}}^{\infty}u\,e^{-c_{0}u^{2}}F(u)^{q}\,du\leq F(u_{\ast})^{q}\int_{u_{\ast}}^{\infty}u\,e^{-c_{0}u^{2}}\Big(\frac{u}{u_{\ast}}\Big)^{mq}\,du.

Change variables
u=u_{\ast}s
to get

\int_{u_{\ast}}^{\infty}u\,e^{-c_{0}u^{2}}\Big(\frac{u}{u_{\ast}}\Big)^{mq}\,du=u_{\ast}^{2}\int_{1}^{\infty}s^{mq+1}e^{-c_{0}u_{\ast}^{2}s^{2}}\,ds\leq u_{\ast}^{2}\int_{1}^{\infty}s^{mq+1}e^{-c_{0}\alpha^{2}q\,s^{2}}\,ds.

Since
s^{2}\geq s
for
s\geq 1
, we have
e^{-c_{0}\alpha^{2}qs^{2}}\leq e^{-c_{0}\alpha^{2}qs}
and thus

u_{\ast}^{2}\int_{1}^{\infty}s^{mq+1}e^{-c_{0}\alpha^{2}q\,s^{2}}\,ds\leq u_{\ast}^{2}\int_{1}^{\infty}s^{mq+1}e^{-c_{0}\alpha^{2}q\,s}\,ds.

With the substitution
t=c_{0}\alpha^{2}q\,s
,

\int_{1}^{\infty}s^{mq+1}e^{-c_{0}\alpha^{2}qs}\,ds=(c_{0}\alpha^{2}q)^{-(mq+2)}\int_{c_{0}\alpha^{2}q}^{\infty}t^{mq+1}e^{-t}\,dt\leq(c_{0}\alpha^{2}q)^{-(mq+2)}\Gamma(mq+2).

Using the bound
\Gamma(r+1)\leq r^{r}
for
r\geq 1
(e.g. from Stirling) gives

(c_{0}\alpha^{2}q)^{-(mq+2)}\Gamma(mq+2)\lesssim(c_{0}\alpha^{2}q)^{-(mq+2)}(mq+1)^{mq+1}\lesssim\Big(\frac{m}{c_{0}\alpha^{2}}\Big)^{mq}\cdot\frac{1}{q},

up to absolute constants. Choosing
\alpha
large enough so that

m/(c_{0}\alpha^{2})\leq 1/2
ensures the right-hand side is
\lesssim 1/q
, hence

\int_{u_{\ast}}^{\infty}u\,e^{-c_{0}u^{2}}\Big(\frac{u}{u_{\ast}}\Big)^{mq}\,du\lesssim u_{\ast}^{2}\cdot\frac{1}{q}\asymp\alpha^{2}.

Therefore,

\int_{u_{\ast}}^{\infty}u\,e^{-c_{0}u^{2}}F(u)^{q}\,du\lesssim_{\alpha,c_{0},m}F(u_{\ast})^{q}.

Plugging the two parts back into (
A.4
), and using that
F(u_{0})\leq F(u_{\ast})
and that
\alpha
is chosen depending only on
c_{0}
and
m,
yields

\mathbb{E}\,X^{q}\ \lesssim_{C_{0},c_{0},m}\ F(u_{\ast})^{q}.

Taking
q
-th roots gives (
A.3
) (absorbing constants into
C,C^{\prime}
).
∎

Example 1

(Growth condition for a polynomial with nonnegative coefficients)
.

Let

F(u)=\sum_{j=0}^{d}c_{j}u^{j},\qquad c_{j}\geq 0.

Then, for any
u\geq v>0
,

F(u)\leq\Big(\frac{u}{v}\Big)^{d}F(v),\qquad\text{i.e.}\qquad\frac{F(u)}{F(v)}\leq\Big(\frac{u}{v}\Big)^{d}.

Indeed, for each
j\leq d
we have

u^{j}=v^{j}\Big(\frac{u}{v}\Big)^{j}\leq v^{j}\Big(\frac{u}{v}\Big)^{d},

and multiplying by
c_{j}\geq 0
and summing over
j
gives

F(u)=\sum_{j=0}^{d}c_{j}u^{j}\leq\Big(\frac{u}{v}\Big)^{d}\sum_{j=0}^{d}c_{j}v^{j}=\Big(\frac{u}{v}\Big)^{d}F(v).

Appendix B
Optimality of expectation bound (
4.3
)

We demonstrate the optimality of the expectation bound (
4.3
) in Remark
4.1
via a simple example.

For
1\leq k\leq p
, let
H^{(k)}=\mathbb{R}^{d_{k}}
and consider a symmetric compact set
S^{(k)}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d_{k}}
. Define the function class
\mathcal{F}^{(k)}=\{\langle\cdot,v_{k}\rangle:v_{k}\in S^{(k)}\}
, which consists of linear functions indexed by
S^{(k)}
. Let
X^{(k)}_{1},\ldots,X^{(k)}_{N}
be i.i.d. standard Gaussian random vectors in
\mathbb{R}^{d_{k}}
for each
1\leq k\leq p
, and assume that the sequences
(X^{(1)}_{i})_{i=1}^{N},\ldots,(X_{i}^{(p)})_{i=1}^{N}
are independent. Let
v_{k}^{*}\in S^{(k)}
be a point attaining the maximal Euclidean norm on
\mathbb{R}^{d_{k}}
, i.e.,
\|v_{k}^{*}\|_{\ell_{2}}=\sup_{v_{k}\in S^{(k)}}\|v_{k}\|_{\ell_{2}}
. Observe that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\sup_{f^{(k)}\in\mathcal{F}^{(k)},1\leq k\leq p}\bigg(\sum_{i=1}^{N}\prod_{k=1}^{p}\left(f^{(k)}(X_{i}^{(k)})\right)^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}=\mathbb{E}\sup_{v_{k}\in S^{(k)},1\leq k\leq p}\bigg(\sum_{i=1}^{N}\prod_{k=1}^{p}\langle X_{i}^{(k)},v_{k}\rangle^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\gtrsim\mathbb{E}\bigg(\sum_{i=1}^{N}\prod_{k=1}^{p}\langle X_{i}^{(k)},v^{*}_{k}\rangle^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}\gtrsim_{p}\sqrt{N}\prod_{k=1}^{p}\|v^{*}_{k}\|_{\ell_{2}}\asymp_{p}\sqrt{N}\bigg(\prod_{k=1}^{p}d_{\psi_{2}}(\mathcal{F}^{(k)})\bigg),

(B.1)

where the last step follows by
\|v_{k}^{*}\|_{\ell_{2}}=\sup_{v_{k}\in S^{(k)}}\|v_{k}\|_{\ell_{2}}\asymp\sup_{f^{(k)}\in\mathcal{F}^{(k)}}\|f^{(k)}\|_{\psi_{2}}=d_{\psi_{2}}(\mathcal{F}^{(k)})
for every
1\leq k\leq p
. This confirms the necessity of the term
\sqrt{N}\big(\prod_{k=1}^{p}d_{\psi_{2}}(\mathcal{F}^{(k)})\big)
in (
4.3
).

On the other hand, we may assume without loss of generality that

\bar{\gamma}(\mathcal{F}^{(1)},\psi_{2})\geq\cdots\geq\bar{\gamma}(\mathcal{F}^{(t)},\psi_{2})\geq(\log N)^{1/2}\geq\bar{\gamma}(\mathcal{F}^{(t+1)},\psi_{2})\geq\cdots\geq\bar{\gamma}(\mathcal{F}^{(p)},\psi_{2}).

Then,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\sup_{v_{k}\in S^{(k)},1\leq k\leq p}\bigg(\sum_{i=1}^{N}\prod_{k=1}^{p}\langle X_{i}^{(k)},v_{k}\rangle^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}\geq\mathbb{E}\sup_{v_{k}\in S^{(k)},1\leq k\leq p}\max_{1\leq i\leq N}\prod_{k=1}^{p}\big|\langle X_{i}^{(k)},v_{k}\rangle\big|

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\max_{1\leq i\leq N}\sup_{v_{k}\in S^{(k)},1\leq k\leq p}\prod_{k=1}^{p}\big|\langle X_{i}^{(k)},v_{k}\rangle\big|\overset{\text{($\star$)}}{\geq}\mathbb{E}\max_{1\leq i\leq N}\sup_{v_{k}\in S^{(k)},1\leq k\leq t}\prod_{k=1}^{t}\big|\langle X_{i}^{(k)},v_{k}\rangle\big|\prod_{k=t+1}^{p}\big|\langle X_{i}^{(k)},v^{*}_{k}\rangle\big|

\displaystyle=\bigg(\prod_{k=t+1}^{p}\|v_{k}^{*}\|_{\ell_{2}}\bigg)\mathbb{E}\left[\max_{1\leq i\leq N}\sup_{v_{k}\in S^{(k)},1\leq k\leq t}\prod_{k=1}^{t}\big|\langle X_{i}^{(k)},v_{k}\rangle\big|\prod_{k=t+1}^{p}\bigg|\frac{\langle X_{i}^{(k)},v^{*}_{k}\rangle}{\|v^{*}_{k}\|_{\ell_{2}}}\bigg|\right]

\displaystyle\geq\bigg(\prod_{k=t+1}^{p}\|v_{k}^{*}\|_{\ell_{2}}\bigg)\mathbb{E}_{(X^{(k)}_{i})_{i=1}^{N},k\geq t+1}\left[\max_{1\leq i\leq N}\bigg(\mathbb{E}_{(X^{(k)}_{i})_{i=1}^{N},k\leq t}\sup_{v_{k}\in S^{(k)},1\leq k\leq t}\prod_{k=1}^{t}\big|\langle X_{i}^{(k)},v_{k}\rangle\big|\bigg)\prod_{k=t+1}^{p}\bigg|\frac{\langle X_{i}^{(k)},v^{*}_{k}\rangle}{\|v^{*}_{k}\|_{\ell_{2}}}\bigg|\right]

\displaystyle=\bigg(\prod_{k=t+1}^{p}\|v_{k}^{*}\|_{\ell_{2}}\bigg)\mathbb{E}_{(X^{(k)}_{i})_{i=1}^{N},k\geq t+1}\left[\max_{1\leq i\leq N}\bigg(\prod_{k=1}^{t}\mathbb{E}\sup_{v_{k}\in S^{(k)}}\big|\langle X_{i}^{(k)},v_{k}\rangle\big|\bigg)\prod_{k=t+1}^{p}\bigg|\frac{\langle X_{i}^{(k)},v^{*}_{k}\rangle}{\|v^{*}_{k}\|_{\ell_{2}}}\bigg|\right]

\displaystyle\overset{\text{($\star\star$)}}{\asymp}_{p}\bigg(\prod_{k=t+1}^{p}d_{\psi_{2}}(\mathcal{F}^{(k)})\bigg)\bigg(\prod_{k=1}^{t}\gamma(\mathcal{F}^{(k)},\psi_{2})\bigg)\mathbb{E}_{(X^{(k)}_{i})_{i=1}^{N},k\geq t+1}\left[\max_{1\leq i\leq N}\prod_{k=t+1}^{p}\bigg|\frac{\langle X_{i}^{(k)},v^{*}_{k}\rangle}{\|v^{*}_{k}\|_{\ell_{2}}}\bigg|\right]

\displaystyle\asymp_{p}\bigg(\prod_{k=t+1}^{p}d_{\psi_{2}}(\mathcal{F}^{(k)})\bigg)\bigg(\prod_{k=1}^{t}\gamma(\mathcal{F}^{(k)},\psi_{2})\bigg)(\log N)^{(p-t)/2},

where (
\star
) follows by taking
v_{k}=v^{*}_{k}
for
k\geq t+1
, and (
\star\star
) follows using that
\|v_{k}^{*}\|_{\ell_{2}}\asymp d_{\psi_{2}}(\mathcal{F}^{(k)})
and that, by Talagrand’s majorizing-measure theorem
[
34
, Theorem 2.10.1]
,
\mathbb{E}\sup_{v_{k}\in S^{(k)}}\langle X^{(k)},v_{k}\rangle\asymp\gamma(\mathcal{F}^{(k)},\psi_{2})
. The last step follows since, for every
k\geq t+1
and
1\leq i\leq N
, the random variables

\frac{\langle X_{i}^{(k)},v^{*}_{k}\rangle}{\|v^{*}_{k}\|_{\ell_{2}}}
are i.i.d. standard normal random variables, and these Gaussians are independent across
k
and
i
. By applying Lemma
A.1
, we obtain

\mathbb{E}_{(X^{(k)}_{i})_{i=1}^{N},k\geq t+1}\left[\max_{1\leq i\leq N}\prod_{k=t+1}^{p}\bigg|\frac{\langle X_{i}^{(k)},v^{*}_{k}\rangle}{\|v^{*}_{k}\|_{\ell_{2}}}\bigg|\right]\asymp_{p}(\log N)^{(p-t)/2}.

Therefore, we have shown that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\sup_{v_{k}\in S^{(k)},1\leq k\leq p}\bigg(\sum_{i=1}^{N}\prod_{k=1}^{p}\langle X_{i}^{(k)},v_{k}\rangle^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\gtrsim_{p}\bigg(\prod_{k=t+1}^{p}d_{\psi_{2}}(\mathcal{F}^{(k)})\bigg)\bigg(\prod_{k=1}^{t}\gamma(\mathcal{F}^{(k)},\psi_{2})\bigg)(\log N)^{(p-t)/2}

\displaystyle\asymp_{p}\bigg(\prod_{k=1}^{p}d_{\psi_{2}}(\mathcal{F}^{(k)})\bigg)\bigg(\prod_{k=1}^{p}\Big(\bar{\gamma}(\mathcal{F}^{(k)},\psi_{2})+(\log N)^{1/2}\Big)\bigg).

(B.2)

Combining the two lower bounds (
B.1
) and (
B.2
) shows that, in this example, the upper bound (
4.3
) is sharp up to a constant depending only on
p
.

Missed opportunities of method information index plus (MII+) counseling among current modern contraceptive users. multi-level analysis from the Ethiopian PMA 2021 data set
Authors: Kaleb Assegid Demissie, Tadele Biresaw Belachew, Endalkachew Dellie, Misganaw Guadie Tiruneh, Melak Jejaw, Nigusu Worku, Getachew Teshale, Demiss Mulatu Geberu, Banchlay Addis, Tesfahun Zemene Tafere, Lake Yazachew, Mihret Getnet
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionIn Ethiopia, there is a decrease in percentage of women who receive method information index counseling service from 39% (95%CI: 33%, 44%) in 2015 to 12% in 2019 (95% CI: 10%, 14%) nationally, and less than 40% of these services provided are high quality. Therefore, the aim of this study was to identify modern contraceptive users-related factors at individual level and community level associated with missed opportunities of method information index plus counseling.MethodsThis study used the datasets from the 2019 Performance Monitoring for Action Ethiopia Survey, which comprised a total of 8,365 households (98.9%) and 7,988 women (98.8%). The data analysis was done with Stata version 14. Using multilevel mixed effect logistics regression, both individual and community-level factors were found to be associated with missed opportunities of method information index plus counseling. a P-value of less than 0.05 was used to declare statistical significance. The association was presented using an adjusted odds ratio with a 95% confidence interval.ResultsThe study shows that the prevalence of missed opportunity MII + family planning counseling among current modern contraceptive users was 81% (79.15, 82.66) and factors significantly associated with missed opportunities of method information index plus counselling are educational status, low level of media exposure, religion, receiving the service in health centers and health posts, never heard of any other methods, number of children at first contraceptive use and region.ConclusionsIn Ethiopia 81% of women had a missed opportunities of MII + counseling service. In order to address this, we recommend policy makers to focus on training of health care workers and pharmacists about MII + counseling, and establishing ways to improve access to media exposure.Keywords: Method information index plus (MII+), Multilevel analysis, PMA

Introduction

According to Population Reference Bureau (PRB) projections from 2018, the world’s population is expected to rise from 7.6 billion in 2018 to 9.9 billion in 2050, an increase of 2.3 billion (or 29%) from its current size [1]. It is anticipated that the developing world will account for the majority of the global population growth. Accordingly, at a 2.6% rate of natural increase, Africa’s current population of 1.28 billion is expected to grow to 1.71 billion in 2030 (an increase of 33.6%) and 2.58 billion in 2050 (an increase of 101.6%) [2].

Thanks to developments in contraceptive methods over the past few decades, women now have a wide range of contraceptive options to choose from, each with its own advantages and possible side effects. Women may be influenced by a variety of factors when choosing a method of contraception like reliability of the method, frequency of use, side-effect, pain relief, convenience of the method and the likes [3, 4]. Benefits of family planning include reducing maternal death, reducing unwanted pregnancy and postponing pregnancy, preventing abortions, and lowering the risk of HIV transmission from mother to child [5, 6].

Despite these benefits of birth controls women rely on health care professionals counselling to part share these benefits, accurately understand their needs and use, to gain information and recommendation in order for them to choose which method of contraception best suits their individual needs [3, 7, 8]. numerous studies also highlight the significance of contraceptive counseling; it gives women the chance to gather the knowledge they need to make an informed choice that is suitable for their own reproductive needs, increase satisfaction, decrease contraceptive switching or discontinuation, and increase the use of the method [9–12]. Counseling sessions should ideally be comfortable for the patient, offer excellent information, and provide ample time and incentive to implement any recommendations. The World Health Organization (WHO) states that a successful counselor should be courteous, honest, extremely competent, and a skilled communicator in addition to understanding women’s beliefs, needs, and concerns and counselling sessions should be comfortable [13].

Now a days the major causes of concern in low- and middle-income countries are HIV/AIDS, other STIs, and unwanted pregnancies, unsafe abortion which result in lost years of life with a disability for women who are of reproductive age [14]. Studies in Ethiopia also indicate higher prevalence of unwanted pregnancy, unsafe abortion, and low contraceptive knowledge were [15–17].

Family Planning 2020 developed the Method Information Index (MII) as a measure of informed decision-making (FP2020). It is one of the 18 key indicators that are used to track the progress made in reaching the target of 120 million more women who do not currently use contraception by 2020 [18, 19]. The Method Information Index (MII) is a composite metric that calculates an index as the proportion of respondents who answered “yes” to three equally weighted questions referring to counseling information given to the client when obtaining the contraceptive method and it was originally generated from three questions and like PMA2019, PMA2021 also includes a fourth question to make it (MII+) this study aims to assess the missed opportunity of MII + counselling.

Studies in Ethiopia showed a decrease in the percentage of women who receive MII counseling service from 39% (95% CI: 33%, 44%) in 2015 to 12% in 2019 (95% CI: 10%, 14%) nationally, and less than 40% of these services provided are of high quality [20–23]. Moreover, other studies in Ethiopia indicated that around 32% of contraceptive users felt pressured by their provider to use a certain kind of method [24]. Users who obtained their method from a non-public source were less likely to receive counseling on any side effects at all [25]. Contraceptive counseling service in Ethiopia is very low as indicated by varies studies [24, 26]. And factors such as educational status, age, marital status, wealth status, type of method use, and occupation were reported as factors affecting this service [20, 22, 27].

Efforts to increase the use of contraceptives through counseling interventions have not always been successful researches suggest that lack of knowledge on what women should get in their interactions with family planning providers could be one factor [28]. Contraceptive counseling is vital for helping women achieve their reproductive goals specially among women seeking contraception,

The study of family planning in Ethiopia is of paramount importance due to the country’s unique demographic, social, and health challenges, such as high population growth, with over 120 million population [29]. resources like health services, education, and job opportunities are under huge pressure. Unmet need for contraception, Ethiopia still has a significant unmet need for contraception, particularly in rural areas with limited access to medical care [30]. Gender equality and women’s empowerment, In Ethiopia, women’s decision-making authority is restricted by conservative gender roles and conventions, especially with relation to reproductive health [31]. Family planning gives women greater autonomy over their reproductive choices, advancing gender parity and paving the way for their pursuit of careers, education, and leadership positions [32, 33]. So, this study aims to assess the missed opportunities of MII + family planning counselling in Ethiopia using Performance monitoring for action 2021 cross sectional data set.

Method and material

Study area and data source

We used the data from the 2021 Performance monitoring for accountability cross sectional data. PMA Ethiopia is a five-year project implemented in collaboration with Addis Ababa University, Johns Hopkins University, and the Federal Ministry of Health. It measures key reproductive, maternal and newborn health (RMNH) indicators. PMAET 2021 Cross-sectional survey used a two-stage cluster design with urban-rural and major regions as strata. a total of 243 enumeration areas (EAs) chosen from the Central Statistics Agency’s master sample frame. Within each enumeration area, 35 randomly selected households are included in the cross-section. The cross-sectional survey is open to all women in the chosen households between the ages of 15 and 49.A A total of 8,365 households (98.9%) and 7,988 women (98.8%) completed the cross-sectional survey. Data collection was conducted between November 2021 and January 20. A total of 1952 women of reproductive age (15–49) which are de facto and completed the female questioner and are current users of modern contraceptive method where included the study. PMA Ethiopia received ethical approval from Addis Ababa University, College of Health Sciences (AAU/CHS) (Ref: AAUMF 01–008) and the Johns Hopkins University Bloomberg School of Public Health (JHSPH) Institutional Review Board (FWA00000287) [34]. Data from PMA-Ethiopia will be available at www.pmadata.org.

Variables

Dependent variable

The outcome variable for our study was Missed opportunity of Method information index plus (MII+) counseling. The Method Information Index (MII) is a composite metric that calculates an index as the proportion of respondents who answered “yes” to three equally weighted questions referring to counseling information given to the client when obtaining the contraceptive method and it was originally generated from three questions.

“Were you informed about alternative contraceptive methods?”

“Were you informed about alternative contraceptive methods?”

“Were you informed about the side effects of each method?” and,

“Were you informed about the side effects of each method?” and,

“Were you told what to do if side effects were to occur?”

“Were you told what to do if side effects were to occur?”

A fourth question was added to make it (MII+), “Were you told that you could switch to another method?” We created a new variable called the Method Information Index Plus (MII+), which combines responses to four counseling questions. If a respondent did not receive at least one of the four types of family planning counseling, we categorized this as “no counseling” (coded as 1 = no). If the respondent answered “yes” to all four questions, we categorized it as “good counseling” (coded as 0 = yes). Therefore, any modern contraceptive user who did not receive one of the four counseling topics missed out on MII + family planning counseling.

Independent variables

The independent variables were selected from previous literatures [19, 20, 22].

Age of respondent

Respondent’s age was classified as (15–24, 25–34, 35–49),

Marital status

Marital status was recoded as (never married, married/living with a partner, widowed/divorced).

Respondents’ educational status

Respondents’ educational status as classified as (No education, primary secondary (secondary + vocational/technical) and higher.

Religion

Religion was classified as (Orthodox, Protestant, Muslim and others (Non-believers, Catholics Traditional, Wakefeta, ).

Number of live births and number of children at first use

Number of live births and Number of children at first use were classified as (0 births, 1–2 births, 3–4 births and more than 5 births).

Method using currently

Currently using contraceptive method was classified as long acting (female sterilization, Implant, IUD) and short acting (injectable, piles, LAM, male/female condoms, emergency contraceptives, standard days/cycle.

Source of method

The source where user receive contraceptive method was categorized as (Hospital, health center, health post, privates, and others).

Wealth status

Wealth status was recoded as (poorest and poorer = poor, middle = middle, richest and richer to rich),

Ever hear of FP, discuses with partner and media exposure

These three variables were classified as yes/no, respondents were asked if they have ever heard of any other method before and if they discus with their partner or not. Media exposure was generated from four variables 1, seen about FP on TV in the last few months, 2, read about FP on newspaper/magazine, 3, received text message about FP and seen about FP on social media in the last few months. These variables were combined to form media exposure.

Family structure

Family structures were classified as monogamous if husband had only one wife and polygamous if husband had more than one wife.

Region

Eleven regions were included in the 2021 PMA cross sectional data set, Tigray was not included in the survey and also according to PMA-Ethiopia findings from cohort 1 and cohort 2 survey there are 0% contraceptive users in Somalia region.

Community level education and poverty

Community level education and poverty were created as a community level factor from individual level factors and they were categorized as low and high.

Data analysis

Analysis was done using Stata version 14, to be consistent and to show completeness, data cleaning was done prior to recording and labeling. Frequency distributions were displayed in tables and text using descriptive statistics. A sample weight was applied to take potential differences in non-responses and geographic strata selection into consideration.

Intraclass Correlation Coefficient (ICC), proportional change in variance (PCV) and median odds ratio (MOR) were calculated for the appropriateness of multilevel logistic regression and checking the presence of clustering but mainly we used two main information ICC and chi-square (LR test vs. logistic model) in the intercept only or null model, so ICC in the null model is 24.1% greater than 5% this indicates that 24.1% of missed opportunity (MII+) counseling is explained by between-Clusters differences and conversely that 75.9% is explained by within Clusters. And two the chi-square test indicates the difference in fit is significant between models (χ²(1) = 79.5, p < .001) relative to binary logistic regression. Then we constructed four models; null model (model 0) only dependent variable, model 1 dependent variable and individual level factors to investigate if cluster level difference were explained by the individual level factors, model 2 dependent variable with community level variable to investigate if cluster level difference were explained by the community level factors and the finally model 3 was dependent variable with both individual level and community level factors. And we selected the best model by comparing the Akaike Information Criteria (AIC) so the model with lowest Akaike Information Criteria (AIC) and we also used Deviance to select the best-fitted model. And variables with p-value < 0.20 in the bi-variable analysis were entered in to multivariable analysis and in the multivariable analysis adjusted odds ratio with 95% CI and p-value < 0.05 were used to identify factors associated with missed opportunities of MII + family planning counseling.

AIC which is a measure of model fit was measured by using the Stata command, (estatic). The deviance which is also another measurement of model fit was calculated by (-2 * loglikelihood ration). ICC was calculated for each model by ICC = VA/(VA + 3.29) *100%. PCV were calculated for each model, PCV= (Vnull−VM1/M2/M3/Vnull) *100. Where, Vnull = the variance in the Null model, VM1 = variance in Model one, VM2 = variance in Model two, VM3 = variance in Model three. And MOR was calculated for each model = exp(0.95√VA).

Missed opportunity of Method information index plus (MII+) counseling

The proportion of missed opportunity MII + family planning counseling among current modern contraceptive users was 81% (79.15, 82.66) in 2021.

Individual and community level factors associated with missed opportunity of MII + counseling (fixed-effect)

In Model 1 which accounted for individual level factors media exposure, respondent’s educational status, ever heard of family planning methods, source of method and number of children at first use were associated with missed opportunities of MII + counseling. Where as in Model 2 none of the community level factors were associated with missed opportunities of MII + counseling, but in Model 3 which accounted for both individual and community level factors media exposure, respondent’s educational status, ever heard of family planning methods, source of method and number of children at first use, religion and region were statistically associated with missed opportunities of MII + counseling.

The odds of missed opportunity MII + FP counseling was 2.43, [AOR;2.43, 95%CI;1.25–4.70] 1.88, [AOR:1.88, 95%CI;1.06–3.3] and 2.35, [AOR:2.35, 95%CI;1.25–4.08] times higher among modern contraceptive users who don’t have education, who finished primary education and those who have secondary education respectively compared to those who did attend higher education. The odds of missed opportunity MII + FP counseling were 2.01 times higher among modern contraceptive users who don’t have media exposure [AOR:2.01, 95%CI;1.45–2.80] compared to those who have access to media. Similarly, the odds of having missed opportunity MII + FP counseling were 3.92 times higher among modern contraceptive users who have never heard of any contraceptive method [AOR:3.92, 95%CI;1.07–14.32] than those who have heard before. Modern contraceptive users who received their contraceptive in health centers and health posts had 74% [AOR:0.26, 95%CI;0.13–0.50] and 71% [AOR:0.29, 95%CI;0.14–0.60] lower likelihood of having a missed opportunity MII + FP counseling respectively than women’s who received in hospitals. Similarly, respondents who had one or two children at first use had a 44% [AOR:0.66,95%CI;0.45–0.96] lower chance of having a missed opportunity MII + FP counseling compared to those who have no children at their first contraceptive use. Women’s who belong to protestant religion had a 47% lower chance of having a missed opportunity MII + FP counseling [AOR: 0.63, 95%CI;0.44–0.99] than women’s who belong to orthodox religion, also modern contraceptive users in a Benshangull-Gumuz had a 88% lower chance of having a missed opportunity MII + FP counseling [AOR:0.22, 95%CI;0.05–0.98] (Table 3).

Random effect

The null model demonstrated there is a variation in the likelihood of missed opportunity of (MII+) counseling. 24.1% of the variation in the odds of missed opportunity (MII+) counseling can be attributed to between cluster variability also the median odds ratio in the empty model was 2.63 which is greater than one this indicates having a missed opportunity in MII + counselling varied between clusters. In Model one after adjusting the model for individual-level factors, 4.8% of the variability in the odds of having missed opportunity (MII+) counseling was attributed to individual-level factors (PCV = 4.8%, ICC = 23.2%) when compared with the empty model. In model 2 that accounted for community level factors 22.5% of the variance in missed opportunity (MII+) counseling was explained by community-level factors (ICC = 22.5%). Meaning when compared with the empty model in the second model 8.6% of variability in the odds of having missed opportunity (MII+) counseling was attributed to community level factors. Model 3 which includes both individual and community-level factors shows that about 21.3% of the variance in having missed opportunity (MII+) counseling was explained by individual and community-level factors (ICC = 21.3%), when compared to the empty model 9.6% of the variability in the odds of having missed opportunity (MII+) counseling (PCV = 9.96%) was explained by both individual and community-level factors. The Akaike Information Criteria (AIC) reduced from the Null model to the third model (1795 in the null model, 1725 in model 1, 1806 in model 2 and 1709 in model 3. and also, the deviance (-2LLR) also decreased from (1790 in the null model, 1648 in model 1, 1778 in model 2 and to 1640 in model 3) so having a lower AIC and deviance makes the model with both individual- and community-level factors the best fitmodel.(Table 3).

Discussion

The purpose of this study was to determine the community- and individual-level factors associated with missed opportunity MII + family planning counseling among current modern contraceptive users in Ethiopia from the 2021 PMA-ET dataset, 19% of current modern contraceptive users received a good counseling that is they received information on all four questions of the MII + family planning counseling which lower than the study in Kenya 56.7% [35]. this could be due to the fact that recently Kenya has made good progress in advancing the ICPD Program [36]. Also a study from the 2016 EDHS show that 48% of women have missed the opportunity of receiving family planning counselling [37]. Overall the percentage of women receiving “good counseling” is increasing from the 2019 result that is 11.8% [20]. Still this number indicates that various individual and community factors influence missed opportunity MII + family planning counseling and strategies to improve family planning counseling should be planned.

In this study modern contraceptive users who do not attend higher education were associated with missed opportunity MII + family planning counselling, this finding is in line with previous studies [19, 20, 22, 35, 38, 39]. This might be due to the fact that users that are not educated might not have information on to receive FP counselling, and this lack of knowledge might have led them to simply take the contraceptive and go without communicating the provider. Our study also showed that those who received their family planning method from health post or health center were less likely to have a missed opportunity MII + FP counselling this finding is supported by other studies [20]. The possible explanation for this might be since there might be patient overload in hospitals in Ethiopia health care providers may not use extra time to provide counselling service on the other hand those who received their methods on health post have a chance of receiving good counselling since most of the health extension workers working in health posts and health centers primary job might be providing family planning counselling. Based on the results of this study women’s who have never heard of any birth control methods and those who do not have access to media were likely to have a missed opportunity MII + counselling our finding is in line with other studies [20, 22, 38]. Reason for this might be not having access to media lowers the chance of users to know types of contraceptives, advantage of one method from the other hence hinders the possibility of them communicating with their provider. Mothers with one or two children’s when first use contraceptive had a lower chance having missed opportunity MII + counselling than those with no children at first use, the possible explanation might be those mothers may start using contraceptive because they don’t want more children’s, this will give them a reason to engage with the healthcare provider. Our study also shows that respondents who are protestant had a lower chance of having a missed opportunity method information index plus counselling than orthodox respondents reason for this might be compared to orthodox communities, protestant communities may place a higher priority on family planning and have more open conversations about reproductive health and those who are from Benishangul were less likely to have a missed opportunity method information index plus counselling than those who are from sidama. However, unlike other studies [20, 22, 27, 40, 41], in our study type of contraceptive using did not have association with having a missed opportunity method information index plus counselling.

Strength and limitation

Multilevel analysis was used to manage the hierarchical nature of the PMA data. nationally representative data with a large sample size was produced based on weights. The study may also provide policymakers and program planners with helpful information for developing efficient interventions at both the national and regional levels. This research does have some limitations like recall bias since the data relies on respondents’ reports. Also drawing the cause-and-effect relationship is challenging due to the cross-sectional nature of the research. This study aims to assess factors associated with missed opportunities of MII + counseling from the perspective of the users, some provider related factors might have contributed to this result so we highly recommend other researches to examine the effect of healthcare provider related factors in having a missed opportunities of MII + counseling.

Conclusion

Even though there is a slight increase in good counselling, the proportion of missed opportunity MII + FP counselling in Ethiopia is high (81%,) media exposure, respondent’s educational status, ever heard of family planning methods, source of method and number of children at first use were associated with missed opportunities of MII + counseling from the individual level factors and region from the community level factor were significantly associated with missed opportunities of MII + counseling. Therefore, policy makers and programs should provide a training opportunities on MII plus counseling for health care workers particularly for pharmacy technicians. Also, policy makers should focus on educating women and raising their awareness on the type’s contraceptives, creating a way for women to have access to mass media can contribute to having a good counselling.

Policy implications

Having high level of missed opportunities on Method Information Index Plus (MII+) counseling in Ethiopia has important policy implications. This finding suggests that healthcare providers may not be fully trained or may lack the time to give comprehensive counseling. Also, different studies has reported that low quality service has been associated with training skill of providers [42, 43]. So, the main goal of policies should be to improve provider training so that they are well-versed in all forms of contraception and are able to explain this to clients.

Programs for ongoing education should be put in place to help clinicians become more proficient communicators so they can respond to users of contraceptives’ concerns and give tailored counseling. It might also be necessary for governments and health programs to devote more funds to family planning services. This might involve making necessary infrastructure improvements, recruiting more qualified personnel, and making sure that contraceptive supplies and educational materials are easily accessible.

We recommend further research to focus on evaluating the quality of MII + family planning counseling service and how counseling practices differ by area, medical provider, or type of institution, and how these differences affect the satisfaction and maintenance of contraceptive utilization.

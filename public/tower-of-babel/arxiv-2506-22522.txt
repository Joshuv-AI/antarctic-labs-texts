A New Tensor Norm That Preserves Unconditional Bases in Banach Spaces Journal: ArXiv
Rafik Karkri, Samir Kabbaj

Abstract
Let α \alpha be a tensor norm on the class of all algebaic tensor products of Banach spaces E ⊗ F E\otimes F . We say that α \alpha preserves unconditionality if the completion E ⊗ α F E\otimes_{\alpha}F admits an unconditional basis for every pair of Banach spaces E E and F F that have unconditional bases. It is well known that none of Grothendieck’s 14 tensor norms satisfies this unconditionality condition. Moreover, the exsistence of a tensor norm α \alpha preserving unconditionality remains an open question. In this paper, we construct, for every pair of Banach spaces E E and F F with unconditional bases a new tensor norm α \alpha that preserves unconditionality.

1
Introduction

In 1961, Gelbaum and Gil de Lamadrid
[
4
]
observed that the canonical basis of the space
\ell_{2}\otimes_{\varepsilon}\ell_{2}
is not unconditional.
In 1970, Kwapień and Pełczyński
[
6
, corollary 3.1, p. 58]
proved that
neither
\ell_{p}\otimes_{\pi}\ell_{q^{*}}
nor
\ell_{p^{*}}\otimes_{\varepsilon}\ell_{q}
(
p\geq q>1
) admits an unconditional Schauder basis. These results demonstrate that the injective and the projective tensor product fail to preserve the unconditionality. Later, Pérez-García and Villanueva
[
7
]
showed in 2004 that none of Grothendieck’s 14 norms satisfies the unconditionality condition.

1.1
Tests of unconditionality

There are some interesting tests to check whether a tensor norm satisfies the unconditionality condition or not.

1.1.1

The first test (Kwapien and Pełczyński
[
6
]
,
1970
):

They proved that if
\alpha
is a tensor norm defined on the algebaic tensor product
\ell_{2}\otimes\ell_{2}
such that
\ell_{2}\otimes_{\alpha}\ell_{2}
admits an unconditional basis, then
\alpha
must be equivalent to the Hilbert-Schmidt norm.

1.1.2

The second test (Pérez-García and Villanueva
[
7
]
, 2004):

They proved that if a tensor norm
\alpha
satisfies the unconditionality condition for all pairs of Banach spaces
E
and
F
, then
\alpha
must be coincide (up to constants) with:

•

The injective norm
\varepsilon
on
c_{0}\otimes c_{0}
,

•

The projective norm
\pi
on
\ell_{1}\otimes\ell_{1}
.

1.1.3

The third test (Diestel
[
3
, proposition 4.5.8, p. 176]
, 2008):

If
E
and
F
are Banach spaces with unconditional bases

(a_{m})_{m\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}
,

(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}

respectively, and
\alpha
is any tensor norm, then

\left(a_{m}\otimes x_{n}\right)_{m,n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}
forms an unconditional basis for
E\otimes_{\alpha}F
precisely when
E\otimes_{\alpha}F
is (isomorphic to) a Banach lattice.

1.2
Definitions

We recall the following definitions for a Banach space
E
.

1.2.1

A sequence

\left(x_{n}\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}\subset E
is called a Schauder basis (in short, SB) for
E
if, for every
x\in E
, there is
a unique sequence of scalars
\left(\alpha_{n}\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}

such that:

x=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\alpha_{n}x_{n}.

If, in addition, the series
\sum_{n}\alpha_{n}x_{n}
is unconditionally convergent in
E
to
x
, the sequence

\left(x_{n}\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}
is called an unconditional Schauder basis (in short, USB) for
E
.

1.2.2

A sequence

\left((a_{m},b_{m}^{*})\right)_{m\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}\subset E\times E^{*}

is called a Schauder Frame
[
2
]
(in short, SF) for
E
if, for each

x\in E
we have:

x=\underset{m=1}{\overset{+\infty}{\sum}}b_{m}^{*}\left(x\right)a_{m}.

If, in addition, the series
\sum_{m=1}^{+\infty}b_{m}^{*}\left(x\right)a_{m}
is unconditionally convergent in
E
to
x
, the sequence

\left((a_{m},b_{m}^{*})\right)_{m\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}
is called an unconditional Schauder frame (in short, USF) for
E
.

1.2.3

A sequence

\left((a_{m},b_{m}^{*})\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}\subset E\times E^{*}
is called a Besselian Schauder frame
[
5
]
(in short, BSF) for
E
if, this sequence is a SF for
E
and there is
B>0
such that:

\underset{m=1}{\overset{+\infty}{\sum}}\left|b_{m}^{*}\left(x\right)\right|\left|f^{*}\left(a_{m}\right)\right|\leq B\left\|x\right\|_{E}\left\|f^{*}\right\|_{E^{*}},

For each
x\in E
and
f^{*}\in E^{*}
.

Remark 1
.

Let
\mathcal{F}:=\left(\left(a_{m},b_{m}^{*}\right)\right)_{m\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}
be a BSF for
E
, the quantity

\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{F}}:=\underset{\|x\|_{E},\|f^{*}\|_{E^{*}}\leq 1}{\sup}\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\left|b_{m}^{*}\left(x\right)\right|\left|f^{*}\left(a_{m}\right)\right|

is finite. Moreover, for every
\left(x,f^{*}\right)\in E\times E^{*}
, the
following inequality holds:

\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\left|b_{m}^{*}\left(x\right)\right|\left|f^{*}\left(a_{m}\right)\right|\leq\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{F}}\left\|x\right\|_{E}\left\|f^{*}\right\|_{E^{*}}.

\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{F}}
is called the Besselian constant of the
frame
\mathcal{F}
.

In this paper, for a given pair of Banach spaces
\left(E,\|\cdot\|_{E}\right)

and
\left(F,\|\cdot\|_{F}\right)
with unconditional Schauder bases
\left((a_{m},b_{m}^{*})\right)_{m\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}
and
\left((x_{n},y_{n}^{*})\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}
respectively, we proceed in two steps. First, we construct an equivalent norm

{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\cdot\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}
to
\|\cdot\|
in each space. Then, using these new norms, we define a new tensor norm
\alpha_{\mathcal{F},\mathcal{G}}^{Bess}
on
E\otimes F
such that the sequence

\left((a_{m}\otimes x_{n},b_{m}^{*}\otimes y_{n}^{*})\right)_{m,n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}},

ordered in the square ordering, forms an unconditional Schauder basis for
E\otimes_{\alpha_{\mathcal{F},\mathcal{G}}^{Bess}}F
.

Notations and preliminaries

Throughout this paper,
\left(E,\|\cdot\|_{E}\right)
(resp.
\left(F,\|\cdot\|_{F}\right)
) denotes a
Banach space (real or complex), and
E^{*}
represents its topological dual.
For
p\in]1,+\infty[
, the conjugate exponent is:

p^{*}=\dfrac{p}{p-1}.

2.1
Sequences and Dual Sequences:

2.1.1

Let
\mathcal{F}:=\left(\left(a_{m},b_{m}^{*}\right)\right)_{m\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}
be a fixed sequence in
E\times E^{*}
.

2.1.2

Similarly, let
\mathcal{G}:=\left(\left(x_{n},y_{n}^{*}\right)\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}
be a fixed sequence in
F\times F^{*}
.

2.1.3

The dual sequence of
\mathcal{F}
in
E^{*}\times E^{**}
, is denoted by:

\mathcal{F}^{*}:=\left(\left(b_{m}^{*},J_{E}(a_{m})\right)\right)_{m\in\mathbb{N}^{*}},

where
J_{E}:E\rightarrow E^{**}
is the canonical embedding of
E
into its bidual.

2.1.4

We denote by

\mathcal{S}=\{-1,1\}^{\mathbb{N}^{*}}
, the collection of all signs.

2.2
Classical spaces

2.2.1

We denote by
c_{0}
, the Banach space of null sequences in
\mathbb{K}

(\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{R}\;\text{or}\;\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{C})
:

c_{0}:=\left\{\left(\alpha_{i}\right)_{i\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}\subset\mathbb{K}:\lim_{i\to\infty}\alpha_{i}=0\right\},

endowed with the supremum norm:

\left\|\left(\alpha_{i}\right)_{i\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}\right\|_{c_{0}}=\underset{i\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}{\sup}|\alpha_{i}|.

We denote by
\left(\left(e_{n},e_{n}^{*}\right)\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}
its canonical SB.

2.2.2

We denote by
\ell_{\infty}
, the Banach space of all bounded sequences in
\mathbb{K}
:

\ell_{\infty}:=\left\{\left(\alpha_{i}\right)_{i\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}\subset\mathbb{K}:\sup_{i\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}|\alpha_{i}|<\infty\right\},

endowed with the supremum norm:

\left\|\left(\alpha_{i}\right)_{i\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}\right\|_{\ell_{\infty}}=\underset{i\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}{\sup}|\alpha_{i}|.

2.2.3

We denote by
\ell_{p}
(for
1\leq p\leq\infty
) the Banach space of all absolutely p-summable sequences in
\mathbb{K}
, defined as:

\ell_{p}:=\left\{\left(\alpha_{i}\right)_{i\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}\subset\mathbb{K}:\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\left|\alpha_{i}\right|^{p}<\infty\right\},

endowed with the norm:

\left\|(\alpha_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}\right\|_{\ell_{p}}=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\left|\alpha_{i}\right|^{p}\right)^{1/p}.

We denote by
\left(\left(e_{n},e_{n}^{*}\right)\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}
its canonical SB.

2.2.4

We denote by
\ell_{p}(E)
the Banach space of all absolutely p-summable sequences in
E
, defined as:

\ell_{p}(E):=\left\{\left(x^{i}\right)_{i\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}\subset E:\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\left\|x^{i}\right\|_{E}^{p}<\infty\right\},

endowed with the norm:

\left\|(x^{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}\right\|_{\ell_{p}(E)}=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\left\|x^{i}\right\|_{E}^{p}\right)^{1/p}.

2.2.5

We denote by
L\left(E\right)
the Banach space of all bounded linear
operators
T:E\rightarrow E
, endowed with the norm:

\|T\|_{L(E)}:=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\|x\|_{E}\leq 1\end{subarray}}\|T(x)\|_{E}.

2.3
Tensor product of Banach spaces
[
9
]
.

2.3.1

The algebraic tensor product
E\otimes F
is defined as the linear span:

E\otimes F:=span\left\{x\otimes y:(x,y)\in E\times F\right\},

where each elementary tensor
x\otimes y

represents the bounded linear operator:

\displaystyle x\otimes y:E^{*}\longrightarrow F,\;\;f^{*}\longmapsto f^{*}(x)y

2.3.2

A norm
\alpha
on
E\otimes F
is called a "cross norm" if it satisfies:

\alpha(x\otimes y)=\|x\|_{E}\|y\|_{F},\;\;(x\in E,y\in F).

2.3.3

We denote by
E\otimes_{\alpha}F
the completion of
E\otimes F
with
respect to the cross norm
\alpha
.

2.3.4

Let
\alpha
be a cross norm on
E\otimes F
. We say that
\alpha
is uniform if, for all
S\in L(E)
and
T\in L(F)
, the following inequality holds:

\displaystyle\alpha\left(\sum_{r=1}^{R}S(x^{r})\otimes T(y^{r})\right)

\displaystyle\leq\|T\|_{L(E)}\cdot\|S\|_{L(F)}\cdot\alpha\left(\sum_{r=1}^{R}x^{r}\otimes y^{r}\right),

for every tensor
u=\sum_{r=1}^{R}x^{r}\otimes y^{r}
in
E\otimes F
.

2.3.5

The injective tensor product
E\otimes_{\varepsilon}F
is defined using the uniform cross norm:

\varepsilon\left(\underset{r=1}{\overset{R}{\sum}}x^{r}\otimes y^{r}\right)=\underset{\|f^{*}\|_{E^{*}},\|g^{*}\|_{F^{*}}\leq 1}{\sup}\left|\underset{r=1}{\overset{R}{\sum}}f^{*}(x^{r})g^{*}(y^{r})\right|.

2.3.6

The projective tensor product
E\otimes_{\pi}F

is defined using the uniform cross norm:

\pi(u)=inf\left\{\underset{r=1}{\overset{R}{\sum}}\|x^{r}\|_{E}\|y^{r}\|_{F}:u=\underset{r=1}{\overset{R}{\sum}}x^{r}\otimes y^{r}\right\},

where the infimum being taken over all the representations of
u
.

2.3.7

The algebraic tensor product
E^{*}\otimes F^{*}
is defined as the linear span:

E^{*}\otimes F^{*}:=span\left\{f^{*}\otimes g^{*}:\left(f^{*},g^{*}\right)\in E^{*}\times F^{*}\right\}

where each elementary tensor
f^{*}\otimes g^{*}

represents the bounded bilinear form:

\displaystyle f^{*}\otimes g^{*}:\;E\otimes F\longrightarrow\mathbb{K},\;\;\sum_{r=1}^{R}x^{r}\otimes y^{r}\longmapsto\sum_{r=1}^{R}f^{*}(x^{r})g^{*}(y^{r}).

Lemma 1
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{F}
is an USF for
E
. Then
\mathcal{F}
is a BSF for
E
.

Proof
.
For each
N\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
and

(s_{m})_{m\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}\in\mathcal{S}
, the linear operator

T_{N,s}:E\rightarrow E,\;x\mapsto\sum_{m=1}^{N}s_{m}b_{m}^{*}(x)a_{i}

is bounded. By the Banach-Steinhaus theorem, the linear operator

T_{s}:E\rightarrow E,\;x\mapsto\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}s_{m}b_{m}^{*}(x)a_{m}

is bounded.
For each
x\in E
, the mapping

s\mapsto T_{s}(x)
is continuous on the compact metric space

\mathcal{S}
, so

\left\{\|T_{s}(x)\|_{E}:s\in\mathcal{S}\right\}
is bounded.
Therefore, by the uniform boundedness principle there is a constant
\mathcal{D}>0
such that

\left\|\underset{m=1}{\overset{\infty}{\sum}}s_{m}b_{m}^{*}(x)a_{m}\right\|\leq\mathcal{D}\|x\|_{E},\;\;\;\left(x\in E,\;(s_{m})_{m\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}\in\mathcal{S}\right).

Now, let
x\in E
and
f^{*}\in E^{*}
such that
\|f^{*}\|_{E^{*}}\leq 1
. Choose a sequence of signs

\left(s_{m}\right)_{m\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}
such that

|f^{*}(P_{m}(x))|=s_{m}\left(f^{*}(P_{m}(x))\right)
for each

m\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
. Then:

\displaystyle\underset{m=1}{\overset{\infty}{\sum}}\left|f^{*}(b_{m}^{*}(x)a_{m})\right|

\displaystyle=\overset{\infty}{\underset{m=1}{\sum}}s_{m}\left(f^{*}(b_{m}^{*}(x)a_{m})\right)=\left|f^{*}\left(\overset{\infty}{\underset{m=1}{\sum}}s_{m}b_{m}^{*}(x)a_{m}\right)\right|

\displaystyle=\left|f^{*}\left(T_{s}(x)\right)\right|\leq\left\|T_{s}(x)\right\|_{E}\leq\left\|T_{s}\right\|_{L(E)}\cdot\left\|x\right\|_{E}

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{D}\left\|x\right\|_{E}.

This establishes the Besselian condition:

\underset{m=1}{\overset{\infty}{\sum}}\left|b_{m}^{*}(x)f^{*}(a_{m})\right|\leq\mathcal{D}\left\|x\right\|_{E}\left\|f^{*}\right\|_{E^{*}},\;\;\;(x\in E,f^{*}\in E^{*}).

Thus,
\mathcal{F}
is a Besselian Schauder frame for
E
.∎

Lemma 2
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{F}
is a BSF for
E
. Then

1.

The mapping:

{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\cdot\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{E}:E\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{+},\;\;x\mapsto\underset{\|f^{*}\|_{E^{*}}\leq 1}{\sup}\underset{m=1}{\overset{+\infty}{\sum}}\left|b_{m}^{*}(x)f^{*}(a_{m})\right|,

defines an equivalent norm of
\left\|x\right\|_{E}
on
E
, satisfying:

\left\|x\right\|_{E}\leq{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|x\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{E}\leq\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{F}}\left\|x\right\|_{E},\;\;(x\in E).

2.

The induced dual norm on
E^{*}
is given by:

{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|f^{*}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{E^{*}}:=\underset{{\left|\kern-0.75346pt\left|\kern-0.75346pt\left|x\right|\kern-0.75346pt\right|\kern-0.75346pt\right|}_{E}\leq 1}{\sup}\left|f^{*}(x)\right|,\;\;(f^{*}\in E^{*}),

which satisfies:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{F}}^{-1}\left\|f^{*}\right\|_{E^{*}}\leq{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|f^{*}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{E^{*}}\leq\left\|f^{*}\right\|_{E^{*}},\;\;(f^{*}\in E^{*}).

(1)

Proof.

1.

For each
x\in E
, the Schauder basis decomposition
x=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}b_{m}^{*}(x)a_{m}
, yields:

\displaystyle\|x\|_{E}

\displaystyle=\underset{\|f^{*}\|_{E^{*}}\leq 1}{\sup}\left|f^{*}(x)\right|=\underset{\|f^{*}\|_{E^{*}}\leq 1}{\sup}\left|\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}b_{m}^{*}(x)f^{*}(a_{m})\right|

\displaystyle\leq\underset{\|f^{*}\|_{E^{*}}\leq 1}{\sup}\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\left|b_{m}^{*}(x)f^{*}(a_{m})\right|\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|x\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{E}\leq\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{F}}\|x\|_{E}.

This proves the first item.

2.

For each
f^{*}\in E^{*}
, we have:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{F}}^{-1}\left\|f^{*}\right\|_{E^{*}}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{F}}^{-1}\underset{\|x\|_{E}\leq 1}{\sup}\left|f^{*}(x)\right|=\underset{\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{F}}\|x\|_{E}\leq 1}{\sup}\left|f^{*}(x)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\underset{{\left|\kern-0.75346pt\left|\kern-0.75346pt\left|x\right|\kern-0.75346pt\right|\kern-0.75346pt\right|}_{E}\leq 1}{\sup}\left|f^{*}(x)\right|\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|f^{*}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{E^{*}}\leq\underset{\|x\|_{E}\leq 1}{\sup}\left|f^{*}(x)\right|=\|f^{*}\|_{E^{*}}.

This proves the second item.

∎

Example 1
.

Suppose that
E
is
\ell_{p}
(for
1\leq p<\infty
) or
E=c_{0}
, and
\mathcal{F}
be the canonical basis of
E
. Then,

\displaystyle{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|r\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{E}=\left\|r\right\|_{E},\;\;\;\;(r\in E).

(2)

Consequently, we have

\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{F}}=1
.

Proof.

Let
r=(r_{m})_{m\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}\in E
. We have

\displaystyle\left\|r\right\|_{E}

\displaystyle=\underset{\|f^{*}\|_{E^{*}}\leq 1}{\sup}\left|f^{*}(r)\right|=\underset{\|f^{*}\|_{E^{*}}\leq 1}{\sup}\left|\underset{m=1}{\overset{+\infty}{\sum}}b_{m}^{*}(r)f^{*}(a_{m})\right|

\displaystyle\leq\underset{\|f^{*}\|_{E^{*}}\leq 1}{\sup}\underset{m=1}{\overset{+\infty}{\sum}}\left|b_{m}^{*}(r)f^{*}(a_{m})\right|\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|r\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{E}.

On the other hand, it follows from:

•

Hölder’s inequality if
E=\ell_{p}
, and

•

from the fact that

|r_{n}|\leq\|r\|_{E}
for each
n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
if
E=c_{0}
, that:

\displaystyle{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|r\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{E}

\displaystyle=\underset{\|f^{*}\|_{E^{*}}\leq 1}{\sup}\underset{m=1}{\overset{+\infty}{\sum}}\left|b_{m}^{*}(r)f^{*}(a_{m})\right|=\underset{\|f^{*}\|_{E^{*}}\leq 1}{\sup}\underset{m=1}{\overset{+\infty}{\sum}}\left|r_{m}y_{m}^{*}\right|\leq\|r\|_{E}.

Consequently,

{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|r\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{E}=\left\|r\right\|_{E},\;\;\;\;(r\in E).

It follows directly from the definition of

\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{F}}
and inequality (
2
) that
\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{F}}=1
,
which finishes the proof.∎

Remark 2

Proposition 1
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{G}
are BSF for

\left(E,\|\cdot\|_{E}\right)
and
\left(F,\|\cdot\|_{F}\right)
, respectively.
Define the mapping
\alpha_{\mathcal{F},\mathcal{G}}^{Bess}:E\otimes F\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{+}
by:

\alpha_{\mathcal{F},\mathcal{G}}^{Bess}\left(u\right):=\inf\left\{Bess\left(\sum_{r=1}^{R}x^{r}\otimes y^{r}\right):u=\sum_{r=1}^{R}x^{r}\otimes y^{r}\right\},(u\in E\otimes F),

where the infimum is taken over all finite representations of
u
, and

Bess\left(\sum_{r=1}^{R}x^{r}\otimes y^{r}\right):=\underset{\left\|f^{*}\right\|_{E^{*}},\left\|g^{*}\right\|_{F^{*}}\leq 1}{\sup}\underset{r=1}{\overset{R}{\sum}}\underset{m,n=1}{\overset{+\infty}{\sum}}\left|b_{m}^{*}(x^{r})y_{n}^{*}(y^{r})f^{*}(a_{m})g^{*}\left(x_{n}\right)\right|.

Then, the following holds:

1.

For each
u\in E\otimes F
we have:

\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{F}}^{-1}\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{G}}^{-1}\varepsilon_{\mathcal{F},\mathcal{G}}\left(u\right)\leq\alpha_{\mathcal{F},\mathcal{G}}^{Bess}\left(u\right)\leq\pi_{\mathcal{F},\mathcal{G}}\left(u\right),

(3)

where
\varepsilon_{\mathcal{F},\mathcal{G}}
(resp.
\pi_{\mathcal{F},\mathcal{G}}
) denotes the injective (resp. projective) tensor norm on the algebraic tensor product of

\left(E,{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\cdot\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{E}\right)

and

\left(F,{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\cdot\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{F}\right)
.

2.

The mapping
\alpha_{\mathcal{F},\mathcal{G}}^{Bess}
defines a cross norm on the tensor product of

\left(E,{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\cdot\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{E}\right)

and

\left(F,{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\cdot\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{F}\right)
. We refer to
\alpha_{\mathcal{F},\mathcal{G}}^{Bess}
as the Besselian tensor norm associated with
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{G}
.

Proof.

1.

Let
u=\sum_{r=1}^{R}x^{r}\otimes y^{r}
be an arbitrary element of
E\otimes F
. Using inequality (
1
) and the Schauder frames decompositions:

Then,

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{\mathcal{F},\mathcal{G}}\left(v\right)

\displaystyle=\underset{\left\|f^{*}\right\|_{\ell_{2}},\left\|g^{*}\right\|_{\ell_{2}}\leq 1}{\sup}\left|A_{f^{*},g^{*}}^{v}\right|=\underset{-\pi<\alpha,\beta\leq\pi}{\sup}\left|B_{\alpha,\beta}\right|

\displaystyle=\underset{-\pi<\alpha,\beta\leq\pi}{\sup}\left|cos(\alpha-\beta)+sin(\alpha-\beta)\right|

\displaystyle=\underset{-\pi<\alpha,\beta\leq\pi}{\sup}\sqrt{2}\left|sin(\alpha-\beta+\pi/4)\right|=\sqrt{2}.

On the other hand,

\displaystyle\alpha_{\mathcal{F},\mathcal{G}}^{Bess}\left(v\right)

\displaystyle=\underset{\left\|f^{*}\right\|_{\ell_{2}},\left\|g^{*}\right\|_{\ell_{2}}\leq 1}{\sup}\left(|f^{*}(e_{1})|+|f^{*}(e_{2})|\right)\left(|g^{*}(e_{1})|+|g^{*}(e_{2})|\right)

\displaystyle=\underset{-\pi<\alpha,\beta\leq\pi}{\sup}(sin(\alpha)+cos(\alpha))(sin(\beta)+cos(\beta))

\displaystyle=\underset{-\pi<\alpha,\beta\leq\pi}{\sup}2sin(\alpha+\pi/4)sin(\beta+\pi/4)=2.

Then
\alpha_{\mathcal{F},\mathcal{G}}^{Bess}
and
\varepsilon_{\mathcal{F},\mathcal{G}}
are non-proportional norms.

4.

In the first step, we prove that the linear operator

\displaystyle J:\;\;\;\;\ell_{p}\otimes\ell_{1}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\ell_{p}(\ell_{1})

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j=1}^{k,k^{{}^{\prime}}}\lambda_{i,j}e_{i}\otimes e_{j}

\displaystyle\longmapsto\left(\sum_{j=1}^{j=k^{\prime}}\lambda_{i,j}e_{j}\right)_{1\leq i\leq k}

is an isometric isomorphism.

Indeed, let
u=\sum_{i,j=1}^{k,k^{{}^{\prime}}}\lambda_{i,j}e_{i}\otimes e_{j}
an element of
\ell_{p}\otimes\ell_{1}
. Then,

\displaystyle\alpha_{\mathcal{F},\mathcal{G}}^{Bess}\left(u\right)

\displaystyle=\underset{\left\|f^{*}\right\|_{\ell_{p^{*}}},\left\|g^{*}\right\|_{\ell_{\infty}}\leq 1}{\sup}\underset{i=1}{\overset{k}{\sum}}\left(\underset{j=1}{\overset{k^{{}^{\prime}}}{\sum}}\left|\lambda_{i,j}\right|\left|g^{*}(e_{j})\right|\right)\left|f^{*}(e_{i})\right|

\displaystyle=\underset{\left\|g^{*}\right\|_{\ell_{\infty}}\leq 1}{\sup}\underset{i=1}{\overset{k}{\sum}}\left(\underset{j=1}{\overset{k^{{}^{\prime}}}{\sum}}\left|\lambda_{i,j}\right|\right)\left|f^{*}(e_{i})\right|=\left(\underset{i=1}{\overset{k}{\sum}}\left\|\sum_{j=1}^{j=k^{\prime}}\lambda_{i,j}e_{j}\right\|_{\ell_{1}}^{p}\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle=\|J(u)\|_{\ell_{p}(\ell_{1})}.

Then
J
is an isometric isomorphism. By Hahn-Banach theorem,
J

extends uniquely to an isometric operator

J:\ell_{p}\otimes_{\alpha_{\mathcal{F},\mathcal{G}}^{Bess}}\ell_{1}\rightarrow\ell_{p}(\ell_{1}).

In the second step, we proof that
J
is surjective.
Indeed, let
(y^{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}\in\ell_{p}(\ell_{1})
.
For any integers
M\geq N\geq 1
, we have:

\displaystyle\alpha_{\mathcal{F},\mathcal{G}}^{Bess}\left(\sum_{i=M}^{N}e_{i}\otimes y^{i}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\underset{\left\|f^{*}\right\|_{\ell_{p^{*}}},\left\|g^{*}\right\|_{\ell_{\infty}}\leq 1}{\sup}\sum_{i=M}^{N}\underset{m,n=1}{\overset{+\infty}{\sum}}\left|e_{m}^{*}(e_{i})e_{n}^{*}(y^{i})f^{*}(e_{m})g^{*}\left(e_{n}\right)\right|

\displaystyle=\underset{\left\|f^{*}\right\|_{\ell_{p^{*}}},\left\|g^{*}\right\|_{\ell_{\infty}}\leq 1}{\sup}\sum_{i=M}^{N}\underset{n=1}{\overset{+\infty}{\sum}}\left|f^{*}(e_{i})\right|\left|e_{n}^{*}(y^{i})g^{*}\left(e_{n}\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\underset{\left\|f^{*}\right\|_{\ell_{p^{*}}}\leq 1}{\sup}\sum_{i=M}^{N}\left|f^{*}(e_{i})\right|\underset{\left\|g^{*}\right\|_{\ell_{\infty}}\leq 1}{\sup}\underset{n=1}{\overset{+\infty}{\sum}}\left|e_{n}^{*}(y^{i})g^{*}\left(e_{n}\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\underset{\left\|f^{*}\right\|_{\ell_{p^{*}}}\leq 1}{\sup}\sum_{i=M}^{N}\left|f^{*}(e_{i})\right|\left\|y^{i}\right\|_{\ell_{1}}=\left(\sum_{i=M}^{N}\left\|y_{i}\right\|_{\ell_{1}}^{p}\right)^{1/p}.

Therefore,
\sum_{i}e_{i}\otimes y^{i}
is a Cauchy series in the Banach space

\ell_{p}\otimes_{\alpha_{\mathcal{F},\mathcal{G}}^{Bess}}\ell_{1}
, thus converges to an element in

u\in\ell_{p}\otimes_{\alpha_{\mathcal{F},\mathcal{G}}^{Bess}}\ell_{1}
. Since

J\left(\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}e_{i}\otimes y^{i}\right)=(y^{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}^{*}},

then
J
is surjective.

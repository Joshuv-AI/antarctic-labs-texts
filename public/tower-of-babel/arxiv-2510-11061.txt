A new description of uniformly spread discrete sets
A.Dudko, S.Favorov

Abstract




Abstract.

We prove that each discrete set in the Euclidean space that has bounded changes under every translation is a bounded perturbation of a square lattice, i.e., a uniformly spread set in the sense of Laszkovich.
In particular, the support of every Fourier quasicrystal with unit masses is uniformly spread.

AMS Mathematics Subject Classification: 52C35, 52C23

Keywords: discrete set, uniformly spread set, roughly shift-invariant set, density of discrete set, almost periodic set, Fourier quasicrystal

1.
Introduction

One of the important parts of Laczkovich’s proof of Tarski’s famous problem on the equidecomposabilty of a square and a disk is the study of the so-called uniformly spread discrete sets in the plane.
Namely, M.Laszkovich in
[
10
, Theorem 3.1]
proved that the following conditions are equivalent for any discrete set
A\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
and
\alpha>0

a)
there exists a constant
C<\infty
and a bijection
\sigma
from
A
onto
\alpha^{-1/2}Z^{2}
such that
\sup_{x\in A}|\sigma(x)-x|<C
,

b)
there exists a constant
C^{\prime}<\infty
such that
|\#(A\cap H)-\alpha m_{2}(H)|<C^{\prime}m_{1}(\partial H)
for every bounded Jordan domain
H
with
\diam H\geq 1
.

Here, as usual,
\#E
means the number of points of the finite set
E
, and
m_{d}(F)
means the
d
-dimensional Lebesgue measure of the set
E
.

In
[
11
]
M. Laszkovich showed that the direct analogue of this result is incorrect for
d>2
, and proved the equivalence of the following conditions for a discrete set
A\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d},\ d>2
:

a^{\prime}
) there is a constant
C<\infty
and a bijection
\sigma
from
A
onto
\alpha^{-1/d}Z^{d}
such that
\sup_{x\in A}|\sigma(x)-x|<C
,

b^{\prime}
) there is a constant
C^{\prime}<\infty
such that
|\#(A\cap H)-\alpha m_{d}(H)|<C^{\prime}m_{d}\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}:\,\dist(x,\partial H)\leq 1\}
for every bounded measurable set
H\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
with
\diam H\geq 1
.

Here, a set is called discrete if its intersection with any ball is finite.

M.Laszkovich called the sets satisfying conditions
a)
,
b)
(or
a^{\prime}
),
b^{\prime}
) for
d>2
)
the uniformly spread sets
.

Notice that all conditions depend on the number
\alpha\in(0,\infty)
. It seems interesting to obtain a criterion for a set to be uniformly spread that is independent of
\alpha
. This is what is done in this article.

The proof method implies a new statement about solutions of equations on infinite graphs, which may be useful in Graph Theory and its applications.

We also apply our result to Fourier quasicrystals with unit masses and show that their supports are uniformly spread.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
we give the necessary definitions and formulate the main results. Proofs of auxiliary Propositions
1
–
4
and Theorem
1
are given in the next Section
3
.
In Section
4
we present a proof of Theorem
2
. In Section
5
we generalize our results to multisets in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and apply them to Fourier quasicrystals with unit or positive integer masses.
In the final section, we formulate questions related to roughly shift-invariant sets that seem interesting to us and for which we do not know the answers.

2.
Main definition and main results

The following definition is the main one in our article:

Definition 1
.

A discrete set
A\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
is called a roughly shift-invariant set if there exist
L<\infty
such that for any
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
there is a bijection
\sigma_{x}:\,A\to A
such that

(1)

\sup_{a\in A}|a+x-\sigma_{x}(a)|<\infty.

Note that neither conditions
a)
and
a^{\prime}
) nor Definition
1
depend on the choice of norm in
R^{d}
.

Denote by
B(b,r),\ b=(b_{1},\dots,b_{d})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, the open ball of radius
r
with center in
b
, and by
Q(b,r)
the half-closed cube, i.e., the ball in
l^{\infty}
-norm of diameter
r

with the vertex having minimal coordinates at
b
:

Q(b,r)=\{x=(x_{1},\dots,x_{d})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}:\,b_{j}\leq x_{j}<b_{j}+r,\ j=1,\dots,d\}.

The set
E\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
is called
relatively dense
if there exists
R<\infty
such that
E\cap B(b,R)\neq\emptyset
for every ball
B(b,R),\ b\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.
Clearly, it suffices to verify (
1
) only for
x
from any relatively dense set
E\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
. The symbol
f(x)\sim g(x)
as
x\to\infty
means that
\lim_{x\to\infty}f(x)/g(x)=1
.

Since
\alpha^{-1/d}{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
is roughly shift-invariant, we see that the conditions
a)
or
a^{\prime}
) for the set
A
imply that
A
is also roughly shift-invariant.

Theorem 1
.

For every roughly shift-invariant set
A
, there exists a density
D>0
such that, uniformly with respect to
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,

\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac{\#(A\cap B(x,T))}{m_{d}(B(x,T))}=D.

Theorem 2
.

Every roughly shift-invariant set
A\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
is uniformly spread, and there exists a constant
C<\infty
and a bijection
\Theta
from
{\mathbb{N}}
onto
D^{-1/d}Z^{d}
such that

\sup_{a\in A}|a-\Theta(a)|<C.

Analogs of these results for measures were formulated and proved in
[
2
]
.

The condition
b^{\prime}
) implies that for every uniformly spread set
A
there exists a constant
D>0
(density of
A
) such that

(2)

|\#(A\cap B(x,R))-Dm_{d}(B(x,R))|\leq C^{\prime}m_{d}(B(0,R+1\setminus B(0,R-1)),

hence, we obtain

Corollary
.

For every roughly shift-invariant set
A
of density
D
, uniformly in
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,

\#(A\cap B(x,R))=Dm_{d}(B(x,R))+O(R^{d-1})\qquad(R\to\infty).

Note that Theorems
1
and
2
were first formulated in
[
8
]
, but the proofs given there contain some gaps and inaccuracies, so we present new ones here.

\Box

Proposition 2
.

For

sufficiently large, we have for each

|\#(A\cap Q(x,N))-\#(A\cap Q(0,N))|<N^{d-1/2}.

. Arguing as in the proof of Proposition

\#(A\cap Q(x,N))-\#(A\cap Q(0,N))\leq\#(A\cap Q(-L\textbf{1},N+2L))-\#(A\cap Q(0,N))\\
=\#(A\cap Q(-L\textbf{1},N+2L)\setminus Q(0,N))<N^{d-1/2}

for sufficiently large

In the same way, we estimate the difference
\#(A\cap Q(0,N))-\#(A\cap Q(x,N))
.
\Box

Proposition 3
.

For each
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
the set
Q(x,2L)
is nonempty, and for

such that

\#(A\cap Q(x,N))\geq(N/(2L))^{d}

Proof
. Let

be an arbitrary point of

. Then
a+(x+L\textbf{1}-a)\in Q(x+L\textbf{1},L)

It remains to note that every cube
Q(x,N)
is a union of
(N/2L)^{d}
cubes

.
\Box

Proposition 4
.

There exists a number
D>0
such that, uniformly with respect to
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,

\lim_{T\to\infty}T^{-d}\#(A\cap Q(x,T))=D.

Proof
. Notice that any cube
Q(x,qN),\ x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d},\ N,\,q\in{\mathbb{N}}
, is a union of
q^{d}
cubes
Q(x^{(j)},N)
with some

, therefore by Proposition

|q^{-d}\#(A\cap Q(x,qN))-\#(A\cap Q(x,N))|<N^{d-1/2}.

Fix
\varepsilon>0
. Let
N_{1},\ N_{2}\in{\mathbb{N}}
be such that
N_{1}^{-1/2}<\varepsilon/4,\ N_{2}^{-1/2}<\varepsilon/4
. The previous estimate implies:

\left|\frac{\#(A\cap Q(x,N_{1}N_{2}))}{(N_{1}N_{2})^{d}}-\frac{\#(A\cap Q(x,N_{1}))}{N_{1}^{d}}\right|<N_{1}^{-1/2},

\left|\frac{\#(A\cap Q(x,N_{1}N_{2}))}{(N_{1}N_{2})^{d}}-\frac{\#(A\cap Q(x,N_{2}))}{N_{2}^{d}}\right|<N_{2}^{-1/2},

therefore,

\left|\frac{\#(A\cap Q(x,N_{1}))}{N_{1}^{d}}-\frac{\#(A\cap Q(x,N_{2}))}{N_{2}^{d}}\right|<\varepsilon/2.

It follows that
N^{-d}\#(A\cap Q(x,N))
,
N\in\mathbb{N}
, is a Cauchy sequence. Therefore, the limit
D(x)=\lim\limits_{N\to\infty}N^{-d}\#(A\cap Q(x,N))
for
N\in\mathbb{N}
exists.

From Proposition
2
we have, for any

:

|N^{-d}\#(A\cap Q(x,N))-N^{-d}\#(A\cap Q(y,N))|\leq N^{-1/2}\to 0,

when
N\to\infty
. The latter implies that
D(x)
does not depend on
x
. Denote this common value of
D(x)
by
D
. It follows from Proposition
3
that it is strictly positive.
Now, for arbitrary
T>1
, setting
N=[T]
(the integer part of
T
) we have for
T\to\infty

\frac{\#(A\cap Q(x,N))}{N^{d}}\sim\frac{\#(A\cap Q(x,N))}{(N+1)^{d}}\leq\frac{\#(A\cap Q(x,T))}{T^{d}}\leq\frac{\#(A\cap Q(x,N+1))}{N^{d}}\sim\frac{\#(A\cap Q(x,N+1))}{(N+1)^{d}}.

Since both the leftmost and the rightmost terms of the above inequality converge to
D
, we obtain that
\lim\limits_{T\to\infty}T^{-d}\#(A\cap Q(x,T))=D
(where now

is arbitrary).
\Box

Proof of Theorem
1
. Since
\diam Q(x,r)=r\sqrt{d}
, we can cover a ball

By proposition 4, the ratio

\liminf_{r\to\infty}\frac{\#[A\cap B(x,r^{2}+\sqrt{d}r)]}{m_{d}(B(x,r^{2}+\sqrt{d}r))}\geq\lim_{r\to\infty}\frac{1}{M_{r}}\sum_{j=1}^{M_{r}}\frac{\#[A\cap Q(x_{j},r)]}{r^{d}}=D.

The last two inequalities imply the assertion of the theorem.
\Box

Thus, after such an operation, we still have

Roughly shift-invariant multisets and applications to Fourier quasicrystals

We may extend the concept of discrete sets to discrete multisets, which are discrete sets such that a natural number (multiplicity) is assigned to each point of the set. It is more convenient to describe such sets
as a sequence
A=\{a_{n}\}_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
that has no finite limit points. To extend the conditions
a)
and
a^{\prime})
to multisets, we should replace the condition

\sup_{x\in A}|\sigma(x)-x|<C\quad\text{for some bijection}\ \sigma:\,A\to\alpha^{-1/d}Z^{d}

with

\sup_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}|\sigma(n)-a_{n}|<C\quad\text{for some bijection}\ \sigma:\,{\mathbb{N}}\to\alpha^{-1/d}{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}.

The definition of a roughly shift-invariant multiset has the following form:

Definition 2
.

A discrete multiset
A=\{a_{n}\}_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
is a roughly shift-invariant set if for any
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
there is a bijection
\sigma_{x}:\,{\mathbb{N}}\to{\mathbb{N}}
such that

\sup_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}|a_{n}+x-a_{\sigma_{x}(n)}|<\infty.

Now, let the symbol
\#E
denote the number of points of a finite multiset
E
where each point is counted according to its multiplicity. Our theorems take the following form for multisets:

Theorem 4
.

For every roughly shift-invariant multiset
A
, there exists a density
D>0
such that uniformly with respect to
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}

\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac{\#(A\cap B(x,T))}{m_{d}(B(x,T))}=D.

Theorem 5
.

Every roughly shift-invariant multiset
A\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
is uniformly spread, and there is a constant
C<\infty
and a bijection
\Theta
from
{\mathbb{N}}
onto
D^{-1/d}Z^{d}
such that

\sup_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}|a_{n}-\Theta(n)|<C.

Indeed, take a sequence
r_{n}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
such that
|r_{n}|<1
and
a_{n}+r_{n}\neq a_{k}+r_{k}
for each
n\neq k
.
Then, the map
\theta:\,n\to a_{n}+r_{n}
is a bijection from
{\mathbb{N}}
to the set
A^{\prime}=\{a_{n}+r_{n}\}_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
such that
|a_{n}-\theta(n)|<1
,
and Theorems
4
and
5
follow immediately from Theorems
1
and
2
. To verify the Corollary for
n>1
, one can choose points
r_{n}
such that
|a_{n}+r_{n}|=|a_{n}|
.
For
n=1
, one can use Proposition
1
, which is also valid for multisets.

The notion of a roughly shift-invariant (multi)set is an extension of the notion of an almost periodic set in

Definition 3

(

A discrete multiset

there is

It is easy to see that every almost periodic multiset in

Almost periodic sets are naturally related to almost periodic measures and Fourier quasicrystals.

Here, we denote by

In fact, each Fourier quasicrystal is a form of some Poisson formula. Both of these objects are used and studied very actively, see, for example
[

In particular, Poisson formulas were used by D. Radchenko and M. Viazovska in

Abstract
In networked systems, enforcing a global configuration from a central authority against local peer influence is a foundational challenge. We investigate a model of influence dynamics on a finite weighted digraph where a distinguished hub node seeks to impose a desirable state, Glory , upon the entire network. Our central objective is to identify a sharp, local, and efficiently verifiable graph-theoretic condition that guarantees global convergence to Glory in a single synchronous update, irrespective of the initial network state. Our primary contribution is an exact necessary-and-sufficient condition based on the relative weights of incoming edges from the hub versus all other nodes. We specialise this to derive a sharp threshold for systems with a uniform-hub broadcast weight. The analysis is extended to a biased update rule and to asynchronous (Gauss–Seidel) updates, where we show one pass suffices under the same domination hypothesis. Every theoretical result is formally verified in the Coq proof assistant.

Introduction

In networked systems, achieving global consensus or enforcing a desired configuration is a foundational challenge, particularly when nodes are subject to conflicting local influences. We consider a scenario where a designated control node, or
hub
, attempts to steer the entire system towards a specific state (termed
Glory
) against the potentially opposing influence of other nodes, which may promote an alternative state,
Gnash
. This model’s diverse phenomena range from a trusted server disseminating correct software versions in a peer-to-peer network to a central bank managing inflation expectations among regional actors.

Our central objective is to identify a sharp, local, and efficiently verifiable graph-theoretic condition that guarantees global convergence to
Glory
in a single synchronous update, irrespective of the initial network state. Such a one-step guarantee is crucial for applications demanding rapid and reliable configuration control.

Classical binary-state dynamics include voter/majority models
[
1
,
2
]
, linear-threshold processes
[
3
]
, and Glauber-type updates on Ising graphs
[
4
]
. Those lines typically study asymptotic behaviour, spectral or energy-based criteria, or average-case initialisations. Control-theoretic work on distributed consensus (and Byzantine-robust variants) focuses on linear averaging or fault bounds
[
5
,
6
]
, again with multi-step convergence. Our setting differs in three respects: a non-linear, score-based update; an adversarial (worst-case) initial state; and an exact one-step criterion. The condition is vertex-local (hub-vs-rest inequality on in-weights), needs no spectral computation, and applies to arbitrary non-negative weights.

This perspective is also tied to influence and seeding problems
[
3
]
. Linear-threshold cascades and influence maximisation address reachability over time under budgeted interventions; here, the
budget
is concentrated at a single hub, whether one step suffices for universal adoption. The failure mode without a hub is immediate (two cycles under parity on a 4-cycle; see
Figure 2

matches the predicted threshold
\mathrm{max\_rest}
.

Our contributions are as follows:

(i)

We establish an exact necessary-and-sufficient condition for one-step convergence, expressed as a simple inequality between hub and non-hub influence at each vertex (

(ii)

This general result is specialised to the practical case of a uniform-hub broadcast, yielding a sharp threshold characterised by a single global graph parameter,

(iii)

We extend our analysis to include a biased (

(iv)

We demonstrate that the same hub domination hypothesis guarantees convergence in one pass under asynchronous (Gauss–Seidel) updates (

).

(v)

Finally, every theoretical result presented is backed by a formal Coq development, aligning each theorem with a mechanised proof and ensuring its correctness.

Methodology

Model and Update Rules.

We consider a finite vertex set
\mathcal{V}
with a distinguished hub
g\in\mathcal{V}
. The network is a weighted directed graph where edge weights are given by a function
w:\mathcal{V}\times\mathcal{V}\to\mathbb{N}
. Each vertex
v\in\mathcal{V}
can be in one of two states from the set
\{\textsc{Glory},\textsc{Gnash}\}
. The system’s configuration is a state function
s:\mathcal{V}\to\{\textsc{Glory},\textsc{Gnash}\}
.

For any vertex
v
, its incoming influence from peers in a state
x
is quantified by a score function,
\mathrm{score}_{x}(s,v)\coloneqq\sum_{u\in\mathcal{V}}w(u,v)\cdot\mathbb{I}(s(u)=x)
, where
\mathbb{I}(\cdot)
is the indicator function.

The update rule prioritises the hub’s state. We define a hub-forced state
s_{g}
, where
s_{g}(v)=\textsc{Glory}
if
v=g
and
s_{g}(v)=s(v)
otherwise. A vertex
v
updates its state based on a majority vote of incoming influence in this hub-forced state. The unbiased update rule,
\mathrm{next}_{\mathrm{heaven}}
, is defined as:

\mathrm{next}_{\mathrm{heaven}}(s,v)\coloneqq\begin{cases}\textsc{Glory}&\text{if }v=g\\
\textsc{Glory}&\text{if }v\neq g\text{ and }\mathrm{score}_{\textsc{Glory}}(s_{g},v)\geq\mathrm{score}_{\textsc{Gnash}}(s_{g},v)\\
\textsc{Gnash}&\text{if }v\neq g\text{ and }\mathrm{score}_{\textsc{Glory}}(s_{g},v)<\mathrm{score}_{\textsc{Gnash}}(s_{g},v)\end{cases}

The
\tau
-biased variant,
\mathrm{next}_{\mathrm{heaven}}^{\tau}
, introduces a non-negative bias
\tau(v)
to the
Glory
score, thereby comparing
\mathrm{score}_{\textsc{Glory}}(s_{g},v)+\tau(v)
against
\mathrm{score}_{\textsc{Gnash}}(s_{g},v)
.

For analysis, we partition the total incoming weight at any vertex
v
into that from the hub,
\mathrm{hub\_weight}(v)\coloneqq w(g,v)
, and that from all other nodes,
\mathrm{rest\_weight}(v)\coloneqq\sum_{u\neq g}w(u,v)
. The foundational identities and bounds relating these weights to the score functions are derived in
Appendix A
.

2.1
Main Theorems

We present our primary results concerning the conditions for guaranteed one-step convergence. The proofs are based on the lemmas and score properties established in
Appendix A
.

Theorem 2.1

(One-step Convergence, Unbiased)
.

Global convergence to
Glory
in one synchronous step, from any initial state, is guaranteed if and only if the hub’s influence dominates the collective non-hub influence at every other vertex:

(\forall s,v,\ \mathrm{next}_{\mathrm{heaven}}(s,v)=\textsc{Glory})\iff(\forall v\neq g,\ \mathrm{hub\_weight}(v)\geq\mathrm{rest\_weight}(v))

Proof.

The proof is deferred to the appendix, see
Theorem A.2
.
∎

Theorem 2.2

(Uniform Hub Threshold)
.

If the hub influence is uniform, i.e.,
\mathrm{hub\_weight}(v)=W
for all
v\neq g
, and we define
\mathrm{max\_rest}\coloneqq\max_{v\neq g}\mathrm{rest\_weight}(v)
, then one-step convergence holds if and only if
W\geq\mathrm{max\_rest}
.

Proof.

By
Theorem 2.1
, the system converges if and only if
(\forall v\neq g,\mathrm{hub\_weight}(v)\geq\mathrm{rest\_weight}(v))
. Substituting the uniform hub weight, this becomes
(\forall v\neq g,W\geq\mathrm{rest\_weight}(v))
. By
A.8
, this universal condition is equivalent to
W
being greater than or equal to the maximum of all such rest weights, which is precisely
W\geq\mathrm{max\_rest}
.
∎

Theorem 2.3

(Sharpness of the Threshold)
.

If the uniform hub weight
W
is below the threshold,
W<\mathrm{max\_rest}
, there exists a non-hub vertex
v
for which the update from the
all_gnash
state results in
Gnash
.

Proof.

Assume
W<\mathrm{max\_rest}
. By the property of the maximum function (
A.10
), there must exist a vertex
v_{0}\neq g
such that
\mathrm{rest\_weight}(v_{0})>W
. Given the uniform hub weight, this implies
\mathrm{hub\_weight}(v_{0})<\mathrm{rest\_weight}(v_{0})
. By
A.9
, this is the precise condition under which
\mathrm{next}_{\mathrm{heaven}}(\texttt{all\_gnash},v_{0})=\textsc{Gnash}
.
∎

Theorem 2.4

(One-step Convergence,
\tau
-Biased)
.

For the biased update rule, one-step convergence holds if and only if the sum of the hub’s influence and the local bias dominates the rest-influence:

(\forall s,v,\ \mathrm{next}_{\mathrm{heaven}}^{\tau}(s,v)=\textsc{Glory})\iff(\forall v\neq g,\ \mathrm{hub\_weight}(v)+\tau(v)\geq\mathrm{rest\_weight}(v))

Proof.

The proof follows the same structure as that of
Theorem 2.1
and is presented in the appendix as part of
Theorem A.2
.
∎

3
Algorithmic Certification and Threshold Extraction

The theoretical conditions translate into efficient algorithms for certifying a network’s stability or extracting the minimum required hub strength.

Linear-time certification.

The domination condition can be checked in
O(|E|)
time and
O(|\mathcal{V}|)
space. A single pass over the edges is sufficient: initialise arrays for hub and rest weights, iterate through edges to populate them, and finally verify the inequality for all non-hub vertices. This procedure is formalised in
algorithm 1
.

Initialise arrays
\text{hub\_weight}[\cdot]\leftarrow 0
,
\text{rest\_weight}[\cdot]\leftarrow 0
on
\mathcal{V}
;

1

foreach

edge
(u,v)\in E
with weight
w

do

2

if

u=g

then

3

\text{hub\_weight}[v]\leftarrow\text{hub\_weight}[v]+w
;

4

else

5

\text{rest\_weight}[v]\leftarrow\text{rest\_weight}[v]+w
;

6

return

Pass
iff
\text{hub\_weight}[v]\geq\text{rest\_weight}[v]
for all
v\neq g

Algorithm 1

Certify one-step consensus and compute thresholds

Uniform-hub threshold.

If the hub broadcasts a uniform weight
W
to all
v\neq g
, the minimal one-step convergence weight
W^{\*}
is
W^{\*}=\max_{v\neq g}\mathrm{rest\_weight}(v)
. This value is a natural by-product of the certification process in
algorithm 1
.

Tie-bias
\tau
.

For the
\tau
-biased rule, the necessary and sufficient condition is
\forall v\neq g:\mathrm{hub\_weight}(v)+\tau(v)\geq\mathrm{rest\_weight}(v)
. With a uniform hub weight
W
, the minimal broadcast weight is
W^{\*}_{\tau}=\max_{v\neq g}(\mathrm{rest\_weight}(v)-\tau(v))_{+}
, where
(\cdot)_{+}
denotes truncation at zero. This requires a single pass to compute rest weights, followed by a pass to find the maximum of the needed values.

Seeding in two steps.

If a seed set
S\subseteq\mathcal{V}\setminus\{g\}
of vertices is preset to
Glory
, a sufficient condition for these vertices to remain in
Glory
is
\mathrm{hub\_weight}(v)+w_{\text{from}}(S,v)\geq\text{rest\_outside}(S,v)
for all
v\in S
, where
w_{\text{from}}(S,v)=\sum_{u\in S}w(u,v)
and
\text{rest\_outside}(S,v)=\sum_{u\notin S\cup\{g\}}w(u,v)
. Both sums are computable in
O(|E|)
time.

Asynchronous schedule.

Under the standard domination condition, any asynchronous update schedule that includes each non-hub vertex at least once guarantees convergence to
Glory
after a single pass (
Theorem A.3
).

Global coarse bound.

A quick a priori upper bound on the required uniform weight
W^{\*}
can be derived without a full weight scan. Let
\deg^{\text{in}}(v)
be the number of non-hub in-neighbours of
v
and
w_{\max}
be the maximum non-hub in-edge weight. Then
\mathrm{rest\_weight}(v)\leq\deg^{\text{in}}(v)\cdot w_{\max}
, which implies
W^{\*}\leq(\max_{v\neq g}\deg^{\text{in}}(v))\cdot w_{\max}
.

5
Empirical Validation

To validate the sharpness of the uniform hub threshold predicted by
Theorem 2.2
, we conducted numerical simulations. The procedure involved generating random directed graphs of 50 vertices with a designated hub. Non-hub vertices were connected with an edge probability of 0.1, and edge weights were drawn uniformly from
\{1,\dots,10\}
.

For each graph, we computed its theoretical threshold,
\mathrm{max\_rest}
. We then swept the uniform hub weight,
W
, across a range of values. For each
W
, we simulated one synchronous update step from the
all_gnash
configuration and measured the fraction of non-hub vertices converging to
Glory
. We also evaluated the success rate of one-pass asynchronous updates over 100 trials with randomised schedules.

The results, exemplified in
Figure 1
, confirm the theoretical predictions. The synchronous update exhibits a stark phase transition: the convergence rate is partial or zero for all
W<\mathrm{max\_rest}
and jumps to 100% precisely at
W=\mathrm{max\_rest}
. For the instance shown, the calculated threshold was
\mathrm{max\_rest}=57
, which is exactly where the transition occurs. The asynchronous updates also achieve a 100% success rate for all
W\geq\mathrm{max\_rest}
, validating the one-pass guarantee of
Theorem A.3
. This evidence supports the sharpness and correctness of the derived domination conditions.

Figure 1
:
Empirical sweep of uniform hub weight
W
versus one-step convergence rate for a randomly generated 50-vertex graph. The observed phase transition occurs precisely at the calculated theoretical threshold of
W=\mathrm{max\_rest}=57
.

6
Conclusion

We have established a sharp, necessary and sufficient graph-theoretic condition for a central hub to enforce a global state of
Glory
in a single synchronous update. This condition,
\mathrm{hub\_weight}(v)\geq\mathrm{rest\_weight}(v)
for all non-hubs
v
, is intuitive, analytically powerful, and algorithmically efficient to verify. We characterised its implications for uniform-hub systems, biased updates, and asynchronous dynamics, demonstrating the robustness of the core domination principle. All claims are supported by mechanically-checked proofs in Coq, providing the highest standard of formal assurance. Future work could explore multi-step convergence guarantees when the one-step condition is not met, or investigate the impact of stochastic noise on the update rule.

Appendix A
Auxiliary Proofs and Derivations

A.1
Score and Weight Structure

This section establishes the core relationships between total weight, hub weight, rest weight, and the resulting scores under the hub-forced state
s_{g}
.

Lemma A.1

(Total Weight Decomposition)
.

For any vertex
v
, the total incoming weight decomposes as:

\sum_{u\in\mathcal{V}}w(u,v)=\text{hub\_weight}(v)+\text{rest\_weight}(v)

Proof.

The sum over all vertices
\mathcal{V}
can be partitioned into the term for
u=g
and the sum over all
u\neq g
. The former is
w(g,v)\equiv\text{hub\_weight}(v)
and the latter is
\sum_{u\neq g}w(u,v)\equiv\text{rest\_weight}(v)
by definition.
∎

Lemma A.2

(Glory Score Decomposition)
.

For any state
s
and vertex
v
, the
Glory
score under
s_{g}
decomposes as:

\text{score}_{\textsc{Glory}}(s_{g},v)=\text{hub\_weight}(v)+\sum_{u\neq g}w(u,v)\cdot\mathbb{I}(s(u)=\textsc{Glory})

Proof.

The sum for
\text{score}_{\textsc{Glory}}(s_{g},v)
is split into the term for
u=g
and the sum over
u\neq g
. For
u=g
, the term is
w(g,v)\cdot\mathbb{I}(s_{g}(g)=\textsc{Glory})=w(g,v)=\text{hub\_weight}(v)
. For all
u\neq g
,
s_{g}(u)=s(u)
, which yields the second term.
∎

Lemma A.3

(Fundamental Score Bounds)
.

For any state
s
and vertex
v
:

(i)

\text{score}_{\textsc{Gnash}}(s_{g},v)\leq\text{rest\_weight}(v)
.

(ii)

\text{hub\_weight}(v)\leq\text{score}_{\textsc{Glory}}(s_{g},v)
.

Proof.

(i)

The sum for
\text{score}_{\textsc{Gnash}}(s_{g},v)
is
\sum_{u\in\mathcal{V}}w(u,v)\cdot\mathbb{I}(s_{g}(u)=\textsc{Gnash})
. The term for
u=g
is zero as
s_{g}(g)=\textsc{Glory}
. For
u\neq g
, the indicator is at most 1, so the sum is bounded by
\sum_{u\neq g}w(u,v)=\text{rest\_weight}(v)
.

(ii)

The sum for
\text{score}_{\textsc{Glory}}(s_{g},v)
includes the term for
u=g
, which is
w(g,v)\cdot\mathbb{I}(\textsc{Glory}=\textsc{Glory})=\text{hub\_weight}(v)
. All other terms are non-negative.

∎

Lemma A.4

(Scores from the
all_gnash
State)
.

For any vertex
v
, let
s_{ag}=\text{force\_g}(\text{all\_gnash})
. Then:

(i)

\text{score}_{\textsc{Glory}}(s_{ag},v)=\text{hub\_weight}(v)
.

(ii)

\text{score}_{\textsc{Gnash}}(s_{ag},v)=\text{rest\_weight}(v)
.

Proof.

(i)

Using
A.2
, the non-hub term is
\sum_{u\neq g}w(u,v)\cdot\mathbb{I}(\text{all\_gnash}(u)=\textsc{Glory})
. As
\text{all\_gnash}(u)=\textsc{Gnash}
, this sum is zero.

(ii)

The sum for
\text{score}_{\textsc{Gnash}}(s_{ag},v)
has a zero term for
u=g
. For all
u\neq g
,
s_{ag}(u)=\text{all\_gnash}(u)=\textsc{Gnash}
, so the indicator is 1. The sum becomes
\sum_{u\neq g}w(u,v)=\text{rest\_weight}(v)
.

∎

A.2
Update Rule Characterisations

Lemma A.5

(Fixed State of the Hub)
.

For any state
s
,
\text{next}_{\text{heaven}}(s,g)=\textsc{Glory}
and
\text{next}_{\text{heaven}}^{\tau}(s,g)=\textsc{Glory}
.

Proof.

This is by definition of the update rules.
∎

Lemma A.6

(Update Rule Equivalence for Non-Hubs)
.

For any state
s
and vertex
v\neq g
:

(i)

\text{next}_{\text{heaven}}(s,v)=\textsc{Glory}\iff\text{score}_{\textsc{Gnash}}(s_{g},v)\leq\text{score}_{\textsc{Glory}}(s_{g},v)
.

(ii)

\text{next}_{\text{heaven}}^{\tau}(s,v)=\textsc{Glory}\iff\text{score}_{\textsc{Gnash}}(s_{g},v)\leq\text{score}_{\textsc{Glory}}(s_{g},v)+\tau(v)
.

Proof.

A direct translation of the update rule’s logic. A vertex becomes
Gnash
only if the strict inequality holds against
Glory
’s score (plus bias). This lemma states the condition for the complementary case.
∎

Theorem A.1

(Domination Sufficiency)
.

For any state
s
and vertex
v\neq g
, if
\text{rest\_weight}(v)\leq\text{hub\_weight}(v)
, then
\text{next}_{\text{heaven}}(s,v)=\textsc{Glory}
. The same holds for the
\tau
-biased rule if
\text{rest\_weight}(v)\leq\text{hub\_weight}(v)+\tau(v)
.

Proof.

Using the bounds from
A.3
and the domination hypothesis, we form the chain:

\text{score}_{\textsc{Gnash}}(s_{g},v)\leq\text{rest\_weight}(v)\leq\text{hub\_weight}(v)\leq\text{score}_{\textsc{Glory}}(s_{g},v).

The result follows from the update rule equivalence. The proof for the
\tau
-biased case is identical, carrying the
+\tau(v)
term through the inequalities.
∎

Lemma A.7

(Domination Condition from
all_gnash
)
.

For
v\neq g
,
\text{next}_{\text{heaven}}(\text{all\_gnash},v)=\textsc{Glory}\iff\text{hub\_weight}(v)\geq\text{rest\_weight}(v)
. For the
\tau
-biased rule, the equivalence is

\text{next}_{\text{heaven}}^{\tau}(\text{all\_gnash},v)=\textsc{Glory}\iff\text{hub\_weight}(v)+\tau(v)\geq\text{rest\_weight}(v)
.

Proof.

This follows by substituting the exact scores for the
all_gnash
case from
A.4
into the update rule characterisation.
∎

Theorem A.2

(Global Convergence Equivalence)
.

Global one-step convergence to
Glory
from any state occurs if and only if the hub domination condition holds for all non-hub vertices.

(\forall s,v,\text{next}_{\text{heaven}}(s,v)=\textsc{Glory})\iff(\forall v\neq g,\text{hub\_weight}(v)\geq\text{rest\_weight}(v))

The same equivalence holds for the
\tau
-biased system with its corresponding domination condition.

Proof.

(
\Leftarrow
) If the domination condition holds for all non-hubs, then by
Theorem A.1
, every non-hub vertex updates to
Glory
. The hub updates to
Glory
by definition.
(
\Rightarrow
) If convergence happens from any state, it must happen from the worst-case state
all_gnash
. By
A.7
, this implies the domination condition must hold for every non-hub vertex.
∎

A.3
Uniform-Hub Thresholds

Lemma A.8

(Global vs Local Rest Weight Bounds)
.

For any threshold
W
,
\mathrm{max\_rest}\leq W\iff(\forall v\neq g,\text{rest\_weight}(v)\leq W)
.

Proof.

(
\Rightarrow
) If
\mathrm{max\_rest}\leq W
, then for any
v\neq g
, we have
\text{rest\_weight}(v)\leq\mathrm{max\_rest}\leq W
.
(
\Leftarrow
) If
\text{rest\_weight}(v)\leq W
for all non-hubs
v
, then
W
is an upper bound for all elements in the set whose maximum is
\mathrm{max\_rest}
. The maximum itself must therefore be at most
W
.
∎

Lemma A.9

(Instability from a Weak Hub)
.

For any
v\neq g
, if
\text{hub\_weight}(v)<\text{rest\_weight}(v)
, then
\text{next}_{\text{heaven}}(\text{all\_gnash},v)=\textsc{Gnash}
.

Proof.

This is the contrapositive of the forward direction of
A.7
.
∎

Lemma A.10

(Existence of a Dominating Rest Weight)
.

If
W<\mathrm{max\_rest}
, there exists a non-hub vertex
v
such that
\text{rest\_weight}(v)>W
.

Proof.

This is a property of the maximum function. If the maximum of a set of values is greater than
W
, at least one value in that set must be greater than
W
.
∎

A.4
Asynchronous Schedule

Theorem A.3

(One-Pass Asynchronous Convergence)
.

If the hub domination condition
(\forall v\neq g,\text{hub\_weight}(v)\geq\text{rest\_weight}(v))
holds, then any asynchronous update schedule that includes each non-hub vertex at least once will result in all non-hub vertices being in state
Glory
after one pass.

Proof.

Let
sched
be such a schedule. Consider any non-hub vertex
v
. Since
v\in\texttt{sched}
, at some point in the schedule,
v
is updated. The state of
v
becomes
\text{next}_{\text{heaven}}(s^{\prime},v)
for some intermediate state
s^{\prime}
. By the domination condition and
Theorem A.1
, this update always results in
Glory
, because the condition is independent of the current state
s^{\prime}
. If
v
is updated multiple times, each update will result in
Glory
. Thus, after the pass is complete, the state of
v
must be
Glory
. This holds for all non-hub vertices.
∎

A.5
Fixed Points

Finally, we confirm that the state of unanimous
Glory
is a fixed point of the dynamics.

Theorem A.4

(Fixed Point at Unanimous Glory)
.

The state
all_glory
is a fixed point for both the unbiased and
\tau
-biased update rules.

Proof.

For the state
s=\text{all\_glory}
, the hub-forced state is also
all_glory
. For any vertex
v
, the score for
Gnash
is
\text{score}_{\textsc{Gnash}}(s_{g},v)=0
, as no vertex is in state
Gnash
. The score for
Glory
is non-negative. Thus, the condition for updating to
Glory
is always met. The hub also remains
Glory
by definition.
∎

(Two-Cycle Oscillation)

For state
s_{0}
:

v_{1}
: Neighbours
v_{2},v_{4}
are
Gnash
. Score vector is
(\text{score}_{\textsc{Glory}},\text{score}_{\textsc{Gnash}})=(0,2)
. Next state:
Gnash
.

•

At
v_{2}
: Neighbours
v_{1},v_{3}
are
Glory
. Score vector is
(2,0)
. Next state:
Glory
.

By symmetry, the next state for

(Hub and Rest Weights)

For any non-hub vertex

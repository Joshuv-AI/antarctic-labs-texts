Abstract
. The space of conjugacy classes of subgroups of a compact Lie groups with its Zariski topology splits as a disjoint union of clopen blocks, each dominated by a subgroup H H with finite Weyl group, and the structure of each block is controlled by an integral representation of π 0 ​ ( H ) \pi_{0}(H) .



1.
Introduction

1.A.
Overview

The space
\mathfrak{X}_{G}=\mathrm{Sub}(G)/G
of conjugacy classes of closed subgroups
of
G
has two topologies of interest.
First of all, it has the h-topology, which is the quotient topology of the
Hausdorff metric topology on
\mathrm{Sub}(G)
. This is a Stone space (compact, Hausdorff,
totally disconnected). The space also has a Zariski topology whose
closed sets are the h-closed sets which are also closed under passage
to cotoral subgroups

is
cotoral
in
K

if
L

K
with quotient being a torus. For conjugacy

(L)
is cotoral in
(K)
if the relation holds for some
representative subgroups.
. Our
focus is on the Zariski topology.

In fact
\mathfrak{X}_{G}
(with the Zariski topology) is the Balmer spectrum of
finite rational
G
-spectra
[

,
1

making it a
spectral space. This also makes it the basis of an algebraic model for rational

G
-spectra. We will briefly describe the intended applications
to
G

but this paper is purely about the space
\mathfrak{X}_{G}
itself.

It was shown in
[

that the space
\mathfrak{X}_{G}
has finite
Cantor-Bendixson rank (equal to the rank of
G
) and that the
Cantor-Bendixson and Thomason filtrations agree. The results of the
present paper refine this numerical information to give a rather
detailed description of the space.

We will show (Theorem
2.6
) that the space
\mathfrak{X}_{G}
decomposes as a finite union
of Zariski clopen subspaces (which we refer to as ‘blocks’)

\mathfrak{X}_{G}=\mathrm{Sub}(G)/G=\mathcal{V}^{G}_{H_{1}}\amalg\cdots\amalg\mathcal{V}^{G}_{H_{n}},

where
H_{1},H_{2},\cdots,H_{n}
are subgroups with finite Weyl
group. The block
\mathcal{V}^{G}_{H}
is dominated by a subgroup
H
in the sense
that all conjugacy classes are represented by subgroups of
H
, and
the structure of
\mathcal{V}^{G}_{H}
is controlled by the subgroup
H
.

1.B.
Single blocks

Here we give an outline of the structure of a single block

\mathcal{V}^{G}_{H}
in the sense that we describe the block ‘up to finite error’ to
give a crude guide to the appearance. The body of the paper will make this more precise.

1.B.1.
Shape

We now describe the structure of an individual block
\mathcal{V}^{G}_{H}
: up to
finite error we have

\mathcal{V}^{G}_{H}\sim\mathfrak{P}^{G}_{H}\times\mathcal{N}^{G}_{H}

where
\mathfrak{P}^{G}_{H}
is the poset of subgroups of a torus with the cotoral
ordering and
\mathcal{N}^{G}_{H}
is a Stone space which, again up to finite
error, has a product decomposition

\mathcal{N}^{G}_{H}\sim\mathcal{N}^{G}_{H,2}\times\cdots\times\mathcal{N}^{G}_{H,t}

as a product of standard Stone spaces (‘integral Grassmannians’). We
outline the shape of these below. Altogether

\mathcal{V}^{G}_{H}\sim\mathfrak{P}^{G}_{H}\times\mathcal{N}^{G}_{H,2}\times\cdots\times\mathcal{N}^{G}_{H,t},;

its Cantor-Bendixson rank is finite and equal to the sum of those of
the factors.

1.B.2.
Fusion

First of all, the block
\mathcal{V}^{G}_{H}
for
G
is obtained from the block
\mathcal{V}^{H}_{H}
by passing
to
G
-conjugacy (the map
\mathcal{V}^{H}_{H}\longrightarrow\mathcal{V}^{G}_{H}
is a quotient map with
finite fibres of bounded size). We therefore get the general form by looking at all top
blocks
\mathcal{V}^{G}_{G}
.

We first describe the two simplest types of block and then explain how
to mix them.

1.B.3.
Weyl-finite blocks

The simplest case is when the block
\mathcal{V}^{G}_{G}
is
Weyl-finite in the sense that all elements of
\mathcal{V}^{G}_{G}
have finite Weyl group.
The reason for the simplicity is that in this case
\mathcal{V}^{G}_{G}
is itself
a Stone space (with trivial cotoral ordering). An example to bear in mind is
G=H=O(2)
, then

\mathcal{V}^{G}_{G}=\{(D_{2n})\;|\;n\geq 1\}\cup\{O(2)\},

topologised as the one point compactification of the positive
integers.

1.B.4.
Noetherian blocks

The next case is when the subgroup
G
is a finite central extension
of a torus. In this case the topology on
\mathcal{V}^{G}_{G}
is determined by
the the partially ordered set of subgroups of the identity
component
G_{e}
. The first example is when
G=H
is itself a circle, and

\mathcal{V}^{G}_{G}=\{(C_{n})\;|\;n\geq 1\}\cup\{SO(2)\},

topologised like
\mathrm{Spec}(\mathbb{Z})
: the proper closed
subspaces are precisely the finite sets of finite subgroups
C_{n}
.

1.B.5.
Mixed blocks

For a general block, we mix the behaviour of Weyl-finite and
Noetherian blocks. If
G_{e}=\Sigma\times_{Z}T
where
\Sigma
is
semisimple,
T
is a torus
and
Z
is a finite central subgroup then
T
is the identity
component of the centre of the identity component, so we call it the

central torus
of
G_{e}
and write
T=T_{z}G
. We can
identify the underlying spectral space
\mathcal{V}^{G}_{G}
in terms of
the integral representation of the component group
G_{d}
on

\Lambda_{0}=H_{1}(T)
, together
with the extension class
\epsilon(G)\in H^{2}(G_{d};T)\cong H^{3}(G_{d};\Lambda_{0})
.

Up to finite multiplicity,
\mathcal{V}^{G}_{G}
is a product of a Stone space
\mathcal{N}^{G}_{G}

space and a poset
\mathfrak{P}^{G}_{G}
. The Stone space
\mathcal{N}^{G}_{G}
is a
neighbourhood of
G
in the space
\Phi G
of conjugacy classes of
subgroups with finite Weyl groups. The poset
\mathfrak{P}^{G}_{G}
is obtained
from
\mathrm{Sub}(T_{z}^{1}G)
by passing to
G
-conjugacy classes, where
T_{z}^{1}G

is the central torus of
G
itself (i.e., the subtorus of
T_{z}G
on
which
G_{d}
acts trivially).
In fact there is a continuous map

\lambda:\mathcal{V}^{G}_{G}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{P}^{G}_{G}\times\mathcal{N}^{G}_{G}

which is an isomorphism up to finite indeterminacy. We take
\lambda((K))=(K\cap T_{z}^{1}G,\omega(K))
, where
\omega(K)/K
is
the conjugacy class of the maximal torus of
W_{G}(K)
.
The map

the fibres over each pair are finite and of bounded cardinality.

1.C.
The isotypical decomposition of the Stone space

We may decompose further. Suppose

\Lambda_{0}\otimes\mathbb{Q}=H_{1}(T;\mathbb{Q})=S_{1}^{\oplus m_{1}}\oplus S_{2}^{\oplus m_{2}}\oplus\cdots\oplus S_{t}^{\oplus m_{t}}

where
S_{1},S_{2},\ldots,S_{t}
are distinct simple modules with
S_{1}

having trivial action. The factor
S_{1}^{\oplus m_{1}}
gives rise to the Noetherian factor

\mathfrak{P}^{G}_{G}
above. The factor
\mathcal{N}^{G}_{G,S_{i}}
corresponds to

S_{i}^{\oplus m_{i}}
, and is a Stone space of Cantor-Bendixson rank

m_{i}
, which is, up to finite error, an
m_{i}
-stage compactification of the
spaces of full rank
W
-sublattices of
\mathbb{Z}^{\oplus m_{i}}
.

1.D.
Application to
G
-spectra

This subsection explains the motivation for this paper. It
will play no mathematical role, so can be ignored by incurious readers.

If we decompose the space
\mathrm{Sub}(G)/G
as a union of Zariski clopen
subsets

\mathrm{Sub}(G)/G=\mathfrak{X}_{1}\amalg\cdots\amalg\mathfrak{X}_{n},

then since the rational Burnside ring is
[S^{0},S^{0}]^{G}=A(G)=C(\mathfrak{X}_{G},\mathbb{Q})
, idempotents in the Burnside ring show that the category of
rational
G
-spectra decomposes into blocks

\mbox{$G$-{\bf spectra}}\simeq\mbox{$G$-{\bf spectra}}\langle\mathfrak{X}_{1}\rangle\times\cdots\times\mbox{$G$-{\bf spectra}}\langle\mathfrak{X}_{n}\rangle

where
\mbox{$G$-{\bf spectra}}\langle\mathfrak{X}\rangle
is the category of
G
-spectra with
geometric isotropy in
\mathfrak{X}
. Thus we may conduct the analysis one
block at a time.

It is conjectured

[
6
]
that
there is a small and calculable abelian model
\mathcal{A}(G|\mathfrak{X})
so that the category DG-
\mathcal{A}(G|\mathfrak{X})
of differential graded objects in
\mathcal{A}(G|\mathfrak{X})
is a model
for
\mbox{$G$-{\bf spectra}}\langle\mathfrak{X}\rangle
in the sense that there is a Quillen equivalence

\mbox{$G$-{\bf spectra}}\langle\mathfrak{X}\rangle\simeq DG-\mathcal{A}(G|\mathfrak{X}).

In general terms
\mathcal{A}(G|\mathfrak{X})
is a category of equivariant sheaves
over
\mathfrak{X}
, so our description of
\mathcal{V}^{G}_{H}
gives the general form of

\mathcal{A}(G|\mathcal{V}^{G}_{H})
.

1.D.1.
The algebraic model

In general terms we need two additional pieces of data on the space
\mathcal{V}^{G}_{H}
to
form an algebraic model
\mathcal{A}(G|\mathcal{V}^{G}_{H})
for rational
G
-spectra
with geometric isotropy in
\mathcal{V}^{G}_{H}
.

•

A sheaf of rings on

\mathcal{V}^{G}_{H}
(the stalks over
K
is the polynomial ring

H^{*}(BW_{G}^{e}(K))
); the sheaf of rings has additional
structure enabling us to encode the Localization Theorem.

•

A component structure (this associates a finite
group
\mathcal{W}_{K}=\pi_{0}(W_{G}(K))
to each point
K\in\mathfrak{X}_{G}
).

Without specifying all the structure we can outline the strategy for
giving an algebraic model.
As described above, it is natural to work block by block.

1.D.2.
Weyl-finite blocks

We describe the strategy in the case of Weyl-finite blocks from

[
12
]
as a template. Suppose then that
\mathfrak{X}
is a Weyl-finite block.
First, we identify the model in an artificially simple case,
as if all the Weyl groups were connected. The model is then simply
the category of sheaves over
\mathfrak{X}
. We then add in a
component
structure
, giving an action a finite Weyl group acts at each
point; this means that the associated group rings together to
make a sheaf
\mathbb{Q}[\mathcal{W}]
over
\mathfrak{X}
. The model (“equivariant sheaves”) consists of
sheaves of modules over the sheaf
\mathbb{Q}[\mathcal{W}]
.

Given this algebraic template, one makes corresponding constructions in the category of
spectra. In both algebra and topology the
category of interest is a weighted limit of module categories over simpler rings.
At each stalk this amounts to considering modules over
\mathbb{Q}
in
free-
W
-spectra for a finite group
W
, and we need to remember that
the category is generated by the single module
\mathbb{Q}[W]
.

Finally, it remains to prove that the algebraic and topological
categories are equivalent. By Morita theory, the algebraic and topological categories
are each equivalent to the endomorphism rings of their
generators. In the Weyl-finite case, the endomorphism rings of
generators have homotopy in a single degree, so they are formal, and
by Shipley’s Theorem
[
16
]
, they are
equivalent to categories of modules over a ring. It just
remains to calculate the homotopy of the endomorphism rings. The
rings arising in topology and algebra are isomorphic and formal,
so the categories of spectra and of algebraic objects are equivalent.

1.D.3.
General blocks

For a general block we are guided by the map

\lambda:\mathcal{V}^{G}_{H}\longrightarrow\mathrm{Sub}(T^{1}_{z})_{G}\times\mathcal{N}^{G}_{H}.

We use the Weyl-finite model in the
\mathcal{N}^{G}_{H}
direction and the
Noetherian model in the
\mathrm{Sub}(T_{1})_{G}
direction. More precisely an
object of the algebraic model is an equivariant sheaf over
\mathcal{N}^{G}_{H}

with the fibre over a subgroup

K
being very much like an object of the algebraic category
\mathcal{A}(T^{1}_{z})
, but with
the poset
\mathrm{Sub}(T^{1}_{z})
replaced by the poset
\omega^{-1}(K)
(varying
with
K
).

2.
The partition

In this section, we will show that the space
\mathfrak{X}_{G}=\mathrm{Sub}(G)/G
may be decomposed into
finitely many blocks
\mathcal{V}^{G}_{H}
, each dominated by a subgroup
H
with
finite Weyl group. Later we show that each such block takes a standard form.

2.A.
Strategy

The subspace
\mathcal{V}^{G}_{H}
is constructed as follows. We start with
the space
\Phi H
of
H
-conjugacy classes of subgroups of
H
with
finite Weyl groups. This is a compact, Hausdorff, totally disconnected space,
so we may choose compact open neighbourhood
\mathcal{N}^{H}_{H}
of
H
in
\Phi H
.
We will show (Lemma
2.4
) that
\mathcal{N}^{H}_{H}
may be
taken small enough that its image
\mathcal{N}^{G}_{H}
in
\mathrm{Sub}(G)/G
consists of subgroups
with finite Weyl group. Now we take
\mathcal{V}^{G}_{H}=\Lambda_{ct}(\mathcal{N}^{G}_{H})
to be the set of
conjugacy classes cotoral in an element of
\mathcal{N}^{G}_{H}
, and we will show
this is clopen in the Zariski topology.

Thus all conjugacy classes in
\mathcal{V}^{G}_{H}
are represented by subgroups

K
of
H
with
K
cotoral in a subgroup
H^{\prime}
of
H
close to

H
. This is the first justification for saying the block is
‘dominated’ by
H
, and we will later see how the structure of

\mathcal{V}^{G}_{H}
is also controlled by
H
.

2.B.
Three continuity lemmas

Lemma 2.1
.

The functor
K\longmapsto K_{(e)}:=K\cap H_{e}
on subgroups of
H
is continuous.

Proof :
We need to check that if
\Gamma^{i}\longrightarrow\Gamma^{*}
then this is also
true of the parts inside
H_{e}
. This is clear since we may find a
number
\epsilon>0
so that every point of
H
outside
H_{e}
has
distance
>\epsilon
from
H_{e}
.
∎

Similarly, if
\Gamma\subseteq H_{e}=S\times_{Z}T
we may consider the
inverse image
\tilde{\Gamma}
under the map
p:S\times T=\tilde{H}\longrightarrow H=S\times_{Z}T
.

Lemma 2.2
.

The functor
\tilde{(\cdot)}
on subgroups of
H_{e}
is continuous. ∎

Any subgroup
K
is cotoral in a subgroup
\hat{K}
with finite Weyl
group (the inverse image of the maximal torus of
W_{G}(K)
), and this
is unique up to conjugacy, allowing us to define
\omega(K)=\omega(\hat{K})
. We recall the perennially useful result
[
5
]

stating that
\omega
is continuous.

Lemma 2.3
.

The functor
\omega:\mathrm{Sub}(H)/H\longrightarrow\Phi H
on subgroups is continuous. ∎

2.C.
Fusion

We show that provided the neighbourhood
\mathcal{N}_{H}^{H}
of
H
in
\Phi H
small enough, its image in
\mathrm{Sub}(G)/G
is a neighbourhood of
(H)
in
\Phi G
.
We start from the map
\mathrm{Sub}(H)\longrightarrow\mathrm{Sub}(G)
, and consider the composite

\Phi H\longrightarrow\mathrm{Sub}(H)/H\longrightarrow\mathrm{Sub}(G)/G.

Lemma 2.4
.

If

has finite Weyl group then the singleton
(H)_{H}
has a neighbourhood
\mathcal{N}_{H}^{H}

in
\Phi H
which maps into
\Phi G
. Furthermore the image of

\mathcal{N}_{H}^{H}

of

\Phi G

Proof :
Since
N_{H}(K)=N_{G}(K)\cap H
, if

has

-Weyl group it has a neighbourhood with the same property.

By the upper semicontinuity of the normalizer (Theorem

We have containments

Now if

2.D.
Cotoral specialization

We show that taking cotoral closures has good topological properties.

Lemma 2.5
.

If
H
has finite Weyl group and
\mathcal{N}_{H}^{H}
is chosen so that
\mathcal{N}^{G}_{H}

consists of subgroups with finite Weyl group then the set

\mathcal{V}^{G}_{H}:=\Lambda_{ct}\mathcal{N}^{G}_{H}
is clopen in the constructible topology and closed under
cotoral specialization.

Proof :
Closure under cotoral specialization is true by definition, and
since the cotoral condition is transitive, elements of
\mathcal{V}_{H}

are characterised as being cotoral in elements of
\mathcal{N}^{G}_{H}
.

First, let us see that
\mathcal{V}_{H}
is closed, i.e., that the condition of
being cotoral in an element of
\mathcal{N}^{G}_{H}
is a closed condition. Suppose
that
\Gamma^{i}
is cotoral in
H_{i}
for
(H_{i})\in\mathcal{N}^{G}_{H}
and

\Gamma^{i}\longrightarrow\Gamma^{*}
. Since
\mathcal{N}^{G}_{H}
is closed under cotoral
generalization, we may suppose
H_{i}=\omega(\Gamma_{i})
.
Now by continuity of
\omega
, we deduce

H_{i}=\omega(\Gamma^{i})\longrightarrow\omega(\Gamma^{*})=:H^{*}
.
Since
\mathcal{N}^{G}_{H}
is
h
-closed,
H^{*}\in\mathcal{N}^{G}_{H}
and
\Gamma^{*}
lies
in
\mathcal{V}_{H}
as required.

On the other hand, to see
\mathcal{V}^{G}_{H}=\Lambda_{ct}\mathcal{N}^{G}_{H}
is open we need only show that
if
\Gamma^{\prime}
is cotoral in

then there is a neighbourhood of

\Gamma^{\prime}
entirely in
\Lambda_{ct}\mathcal{N}^{G}_{H}
. Otherwise there is a sequence of
points
\Gamma_{i}
approaching
\Gamma^{\prime}
that are not cotoral in
\mathcal{N}^{G}_{H}
.
However
\omega(\Gamma_{i})\longrightarrow\omega(\Gamma^{\prime})\in\mathcal{N}
, which is impossible
because
\mathcal{N}^{G}_{H}
is open.
∎

2.E.
Constructing the partition

We describe here how to form the partitition of

\mathrm{Sub}(G)/G
. In effect it is just a greedy algorithm, repeatedly
taking a neighbourhood of one of the largest remaining subgroups.
We need to prove the process terminates and that it has the
topological properties required.

Theorem 2.6
.

The Balmer spectrum
\mathrm{Sub}(G)/G
can be partitioned into finitely many
sets of the form
\mathcal{V}_{H}
(i.e., closures under cotoral specialization
of standard Hausdorff clopen neighbourhoods of subgroups).

Proof :
Take
R_{0}=\mathrm{Sub}(G)/G
, and then form a
sequence of clopen sets

\mathrm{Sub}(G)/G=R_{0}\supset R_{1}\supset R_{2}\supset\cdots\supset R_{r}\supset R_{r+1}=\emptyset

one step at a time.
Suppose we have defined
R_{0},\ldots,R_{s}
. The set of cotorally maximal elements of
R_{s}
is
h
-closed (if

x_{s}\longrightarrow x_{*}
in
R_{s}
with
x_{s}
maximal, then by Lemma

2.3
,
x_{s}=\omega(x_{s})\longrightarrow\omega(x_{*})
, and
x_{*}=\omega(x_{*})
). Accordingly, the set
\max(R_{s})
of cotorally maximal elements of
R_{s}
is
h
-compact. We
partition
\max(R_{s})
into finitely many clopen sets as follows.
The set of conjugacy classes is countable, so we choose an enumeration

H^{\prime}_{1},H^{\prime}_{2},\ldots
, and then choose neighbourhoods of the groups in
turn. First
H_{s}^{1}=H^{\prime}_{1}
, and we choose a standard clopen neighbourhood

\mathcal{N}_{s}^{1}
inside
\max(R_{s})
. At the
j
th stage we will have
H_{s}^{1},\ldots,H_{s}^{j}
and disjoint clopen neighbourhoods
\mathcal{N}_{s}^{i}
of

H_{s}^{i}
. Now let
\mathcal{N}_{s}^{\leq j}=\bigcup_{i\leq j}\mathcal{N}^{i}
and
take
H_{s}^{j+1}=H^{\prime}_{\alpha}
where
H^{\prime}_{\alpha}
is the first of the
enumerated subgroups not already contained in

\mathcal{N}_{s}^{\leq j}
. Now let
\mathcal{N}_{s}^{j+1}
to be a standard clopen
neighbourhood inside
\max(R_{s})

and disjoint from
\mathcal{N}_{s}^{\leq j}
; this exists because the topology
on a Stone space is generated by clopen sets. The sets
\mathcal{N}_{s}^{\leq j}
cover

\max(R_{s})
since
H^{\prime}_{i}
is certainly covered by the
i
th stage, and
by compactness this has a finite subcover. Hence
\max(R_{s})
is
covered by
\mathcal{N}_{s}^{1},\ldots,\mathcal{N}_{s}^{n(s)}
for some
n(s)
.

By closing these neighbourhoods
\mathcal{N}_{s}^{j}
under cotoral
specialization, we obtain sets

\mathcal{V}_{H_{s}^{1}},\ldots,\mathcal{V}_{H_{s}^{n(s)}}
,
each of which is clopen by Lemma
2.5
. Next, we argue
that these sets are disjoint from each other and also from
H_{i}^{j}
with

i<s
. Suppose
that
\mathcal{V}_{H_{i}^{j}}\cap\mathcal{V}_{H_{s}^{k}}\neq\emptyset
with
i\leq s
. Then
there is a subgroup
K

H_{i}^{j}\in\mathcal{N}_{i}^{j}
and in a subgroup
H_{s}^{k}\in\mathcal{N}_{s}^{k}
. After conjugation we may suppose
H_{i}^{j},H_{s}^{k}\leq N_{G}(K)
, and

H_{i}^{j}/K
and
H_{s}^{k}/K
are two subtori of
W_{G}(K)

It is often useful to know that the identity component of the

Corollary 8.6
.

If

Proof :

CONTINUITY OF THE SUPERPOTENTIALS AND SLICES OF TROPICAL CURRENTS
Farhad Babaee, Tien Cuong Dinh

Abstract
. We study the question of the continuity of slices of currents and explain how it relates to several seemingly unrelated problems in tropical geometry. On the one hand, through this lens, we show that the continuity of superpotentials constitutes a very general instance of stable intersection theory in tropical geometry. On the other hand, questions concerning tropicalisation with respect to a non-trivial valuation and the commutativity of tropicalisation with intersections offer insights to the problem of the continuity of slices of currents converging to a tropical current. Within our framework, we reformulate and reprove known theorems in tropical geometry and derive new results and techniques in both tropical geometry and complex dynamical systems.

1.
Introduction

Let
X
be a complex manifold of dimension
d,
and
p,q
non-negative integers with
d=p+q.
We denote by
\mathcal{C}^{q}(X)=\mathcal{C}_{p}(X)
the cone of positive closed bidegree
(q,q)
, or bidimension
(p,p)
-currents on
X.
We also consider
\mathscr{D}^{q}(X)=\mathscr{D}_{p}(X),
the
\mathbb{R}
-vector space spanned by
\mathscr{C}^{q}(X).
It is well-known that the intersection of two positive closed currents is not always defined. The main initial progress was due to the works of Federer
[
26
]
. Federer defined the generic slicing theory of currents: for a dominant holomorphic map
f:X\longrightarrow Y
, and a positive closed current
\mathscr{T}\in\mathcal{C}_{p}(X)
, or more generally, a
flat current
, a
slice

\mathscr{T}\wedge[f^{-1}(y)]

is well-defined for a generic
y\in Y.
A different but approach was developed by Bedford and Taylor, and extended by Demailly
[
20
]
, and Fornaess–Sibony
[
27
]
which assert the following: if
\mathscr{S}=dd^{c}u
is a bidegree
(1,1)
-current, then

\mathscr{S}\wedge\mathscr{T}:=dd^{c}(u\mathscr{T}),

can be defined or it is
admissible
, when

•

The potential,
u
, is bounded;

•

u
is unbounded, but its unbounded locus has a small intersection with
{\mathrm{supp}(\mathscr{T})}.

For instance, when
\mathscr{S}=dd^{c}\log|f|
and
\mathscr{T}
are two integration currents, such that their supports
intersect in the expected dimension, then

\mathscr{S}\wedge\mathscr{T}=\sum c_{i}[C_{i}],

where each
C_{i}
is an irreducible component of the intersection, and
c_{i}
is the corresponding vanishing number. This intersection coincides with the slicing of integration currents.

Demailly in
[
19
]
asked the question of generalising the intersection theory to the case where
\mathscr{T}
is of a higher bidegree. In several works, the second-named author and Sibony introduced
superpotential theory
and
density
of currents to answer this question. In
[
24
]
, the situation where
X
is a homogeneous space was completely resolved, and in
[
25
]
the authors investigated the intersection theory for currents with continuous superpotentials on Kähler manifolds, which generalises the case of bounded potentials in bidegree
(1,1)
. In this article, we adopt the superpotential theory and the slicing theory. Let us also note that the works of Anderson, Eriksson, Kalm, Wulcan and Yger
[
3
]
, and
[
4
]
also consider a non-proper intersection theory.

Once the intersections are defined, one can ask the following
continuity problem
:

Problem A
.

Let
\mathscr{T}_{k}
be a sequence of positive closed currents on
X
converging to
\mathscr{T}.
Let
\mathscr{S}
be also a positive closed current on
X.
Find sufficient conditions such that

\lim_{k\to\infty}(\mathscr{S}\wedge\mathscr{T}_{k})=\mathscr{S}\wedge(\lim_{k\to\infty}\mathscr{T}_{k}).

We say that a current
\mathscr{S}
is on a compact Kähler manifold has a
continuous superpotential
, if for any current
\mathscr{T}
, the wedge product

\mathscr{S}\wedge\mathscr{T}:=\lim_{n\to\infty}(\mathscr{S}\wedge\mathscr{T}_{n}),

is independent of the choice of smooth approximation
\mathscr{T}_{n}\longrightarrow\mathscr{T}
(see Definition
2.1
). Employing results from
[
25
]
, we can partially answer Problem
A
:

Proposition B

(See Proposition
2.6
)
.

Let
X
be a compact Kähler manifold,
\mathscr{T}_{n}\longrightarrow\mathscr{T}
be a convergent sequence in
\mathscr{D}^{p}(X).
If a current
\mathscr{S}
has a continuous superpotential, then

\mathscr{S}\wedge\mathscr{T}_{n}\longrightarrow\mathscr{S}\wedge\mathscr{T}.

Problem
A
becomes more subtle when the current
\mathscr{S}
is an integration current. For instance, when
\mathscr{S}
and
\mathscr{T}
are integration currents and have improper intersections, we cannot, in general, find
\mathscr{T}_{n}\longrightarrow\mathscr{T}
and hope that the slices converge.

In this article, we examine the problem of slicing with integration currents in the specific case where
\lim_{k\to\infty}\mathscr{T}_{k}
is a
complex tropical current
and
S
a complex torus.
The complex tropical currents (see
[
6
,
5
]
) are closed currents on complex tori
(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{d}
or on a toric variety associated to a
tropical cycle
. Recall that a tropical cycle is a weighted polyhedral complex satisfying the
balancing condition
(see Definition
3.1
). For a tropical cycle
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d},
of dimension
p,
the associated tropical current
\mathscr{T}_{\mathcal{C}}\in\mathscr{D}_{p}((\mathbb{C}^{*})^{d}),
is a closed current with support
{\rm Log}^{-1}(\mathcal{C}),
where

{\rm Log}:(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{d}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d},\quad(z_{1},\dots,z_{d})\longmapsto(-\log|z_{1}|,\dots,-\log|z_{d}|).

The tropical current
\mathscr{T}_{\mathcal{C}}
can be naturally presented as a locally fibration of
{\rm Log}^{-1}(\mathcal{C}),
and we say
\mathcal{C}
is compatible with the fan
\Sigma,
if the fibres of
\overline{\mathscr{T}}_{\mathcal{C}}
intersect the toric invariant divisors of the toric variety
X_{\Sigma}
transversely. Here
\overline{\mathscr{T}}_{\mathcal{C}}
denotes the extension by zero of

to the toric variety

Theorem C

(See Theorem
5.7
)
.

Let
X_{\Sigma}
be a smooth projective toric variety, and let
\mathcal{C}
be a tropical cycle compatible with
\Sigma.
Then

\overline{\mathscr{T}}_{\mathcal{C}}
has a continuous superpotential.

The preceding theorem allows us to define the intersection product of a tropical current with any current on a compatible toric variety. We can then restrict this intersection product to the complex torus
T_{N}\subseteq X_{\Sigma}
and, using the isomorphism
T_{N}\simeq(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{d}
, define the intersection product of two tropical currents in
(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{d}
.

On the tropical geometry side, there exists a

for tropical cycles. The term “stable” refers precisely to the continuity of the intersection under generic translations of tropical cycles. This property is reminiscent of the

in toric geometry (see
[

[

. Following

]

]

[

. A new but equivalent approach was developed by
[

]
. In the context of supercurrents, Lagerberg

In the framework of this article, given Theorem

With the stable intersection and natural addition of tropical cycles, we have the

-algebra of the tropical cycles.

)

Works of

]
,

]

also imply the isomorphisms of
\mathbb{Q}
-algebra in (b’) with the McMullen algebra of dimensional polytopes in

We also address Problem
A
in a specific case involving the slicing of currents converging to a tropical current:

Theorem E

(See Theorem
6.6
)
.

Let
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a tropical variety, and let
B=A_{1}\cap\dots\cap A_{k}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a complete intersection of rational hyperplanes
A_{1},\dots,A_{k}
. Assume that
\mathcal{C}
and
B
intersect transversely. Let
\Sigma
be a smooth, projective fan compatible with
\mathcal{C}+B
, and let
(\overline{\mathscr{S}}_{n})
be a sequence of positive closed currents on

. Suppose that:

(a)

\overline{\mathscr{S}}_{n}\longrightarrow\overline{\mathscr{T}}_{\mathcal{C}}
;

(b)

{\mathrm{supp}(\overline{\mathscr{S}}_{n})}\longrightarrow{\mathrm{supp}(\overline{\mathscr{T}}_{\mathcal{C}})}
.

Then, the following limit holds in the smooth projective toric variety

:

\lim_{n\to\infty}\left(\overline{\mathscr{S}}_{n}\wedge[\overline{T}^{B}]\right)=\overline{\mathscr{T}}_{\mathcal{C}}\wedge[\overline{T}^{B}].

The proof relies on a theorem of Berteloot and the second-named author
[
9
]
, which states that the limit of slices satisfies a certain semi-continuity property for plurisubharmonic functions. This allows us to use Fourier analysis to prove theorems about tropical currents. We will see that this result, combined with
dynamical tropicalisation

[
7
]
, implies analogues of the results in:

•

[
32
]
and
[
37
]
, concerning the fundamental theorem of tropical geometry and tropicalisation with respect to a non-trivial valuation (see Theorem

);

•

[
11
]
and
[
42
]
, concerning the commutativity of intersection and tropicalisation (see Theorem

).

It is notable that the setting of dynamical tropicalisation allows for the tropicalisation of differential forms and currents, enabling the formulation of theorems that would otherwise be impossible in the classical setting of tropical geometry. For instance:

Theorem F

(See Theorem
7.13
)
.

Let
W
and
V
be two smooth algebraic subvarieties of
(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{d}
, and for
0<\epsilon<1,
let
U_{\epsilon}((S^{1})^{d})
be an
\epsilon
-neighbourhood of
(S^{1})^{d}.
Then,

\frac{1}{m^{2n-(p+q)}}\int_{(t_{1},t_{2})\in U_{\epsilon}((S^{1})^{2n})}\Phi_{m}^{*}\big([t_{1}V]\wedge[t_{2}W]\big)\,d\nu(t_{1})\otimes d\nu(t_{2})\longrightarrow\mathscr{T}_{\mathrm{Trop}_{0}(V)}\wedge\mathscr{T}_{\mathrm{Trop}_{0}(W)},

as

. Here, the

.

To this end, we hope that the computational aspects of tropical geometry, together with our results, will provide a testing ground for complex dynamics and superpotential theory. Furthermore, our results highlight the need to generalise our main theorems to a broader dynamical setting, which we leave for future work.

2.
Tools from Superpotential and Slicing Theory of Currents

Let
(X,\omega)
be a compact Kähler manifold of dimension
d
. Assume that
\mathcal{S}
is either a positive or a negative current of bidegree
(q,q)
on
X
. The quantity

|\langle\mathscr{S},\omega^{d-q}\rangle|

is referred to as the
total mass
of
\mathscr{S}
. For
0\leq r\leq d
, we consider the de Rham cohomology groups
H^{r}(X,\mathbb{C})=H^{r}(X,\mathbb{R})\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{C}
with coefficients in
\mathbb{C}
. Recall that Hodge theory provides the following decomposition of the de Rham cohomology group into Dolbeault cohomology groups:

H^{r}(X,\mathbb{C})\simeq\bigoplus_{p+q=r}H^{p,q}(X,\mathbb{C}).

We denote by
\mathscr{C}^{q}(X)
the cone of positive closed bidegree
(q,q)
-currents or bidimension
(d-q,d-q)
in
X
. We denote by
\mathscr{D}^{q}(X)=\mathscr{D}_{d-q}(X)
the
\mathbb{R}
-vector space spanned by
\mathscr{C}^{q}(X)
, which is the space of closed real currents of bidegree
(q,q)
. Every current
\mathscr{T}\in\mathscr{D}^{q}(X)
has a cohomology class:

\{\mathscr{T}\}\in H^{q,q}(X,\mathbb{R})=H^{q,q}(X,\mathbb{C})\cap H^{2q}(X,\mathbb{R}).

We define
\mathscr{D}^{q,0}(X)=\mathscr{D}_{d-q}^{0}(X)
to be the subspace of
\mathscr{D}^{q}(X)
, consisting of currents with vanishing cohomology. The
*
-topology on
\mathscr{D}^{q}(X)
is defined by the norm:

\|\mathscr{S}\|_{*}:=\min(\|\mathscr{S}^{+}\|+\|\mathscr{S}^{-}\|),

where the minimum is taken over positive currents
\mathscr{S}^{+}
and
\mathscr{S}^{-}
in
\mathscr{C}^{q}(X)
that satisfy
\mathscr{S}=\mathscr{S}^{+}-\mathscr{S}^{-}
. We say that
\mathscr{S}_{n}
converges to
\mathscr{S}
in
\mathscr{D}^{q}(X)
if
\mathscr{S}_{n}
converges weakly to
\mathscr{S}
and moreover,
\|\mathscr{S}_{n}\|_{*}
is bounded by a constant independent of
n
.

Let
h:=\dim H^{q,q}(X,\mathbb{R}),
and fix a set of smooth forms
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{h})
such that their cohomology classes
\{\alpha\}=(\{\alpha_{1}\},\dots,\{\alpha_{h}\})
form a basis for
H^{q,q}(X,\mathbb{R}).
By Poincaré duality, there exists a set of smooth forms
\alpha^{\vee}=(\alpha_{1}^{\vee},\dots,\alpha_{h}^{\vee})
such that their cohomology classes
\{\alpha^{\vee}\}
form the dual basis of
\{\alpha\},
with respect to the cup-product. By adding
U_{\mathscr{S}}
to a suitable combination of
\alpha_{i}^{\vee},
we can assume that
\langle U_{\mathscr{S}},\alpha_{i}\rangle=0,
for all
i=1,\dots,h.
In this case, we say that
U_{\mathscr{S}}
is
\alpha
-normalised
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\mathscr{T}\in\mathscr{D}^{q}(X)
and
\mathscr{S}
be a smooth form in
\mathscr{D}^{d-q+1,0}(X).

(a)

The
\alpha
-normalised superpotential

\mathscr{U}_{\mathscr{T}}
of
\mathscr{T}
is given by the function

\displaystyle\mathscr{U}_{\mathscr{T}}:\{\mathscr{S}\in\mathscr{D}^{d-q+1,0}(X):\text{smooth}\}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}

\displaystyle\mathscr{S}

\displaystyle\longmapsto\langle\mathscr{T},U_{\mathscr{S}}\rangle,

where
U_{\mathscr{S}}
is the
\alpha
-normalised potential of
\mathscr{S}.

(b)

We say
\mathscr{T}
has a
continuous superpotential
, if
\mathscr{U}_{\mathscr{T}}
can be extended to a function on
\mathscr{D}^{d-q+1,0}(X)
which is continuous with respect to the
*
-topology.

In general, consider
\mathscr{T}\in\mathscr{D}^{q}(X)
and
\mathscr{S}\in\mathscr{D}^{r}(X).
Assume that
q+r\leq d
and
\mathscr{T}
has a continuous superpotential. Let
\mathscr{U}_{\mathscr{T}}
be the
\alpha
-normalised superpotential of
\mathscr{T}.
Let
\beta\in\text{Span}_{\mathbb{R}}\{\alpha\}
such that
\{\beta\}=\{\mathscr{T}\}.
For any compactly supported smooth form
\varphi
of bidegree
(d-q-r,d-q-r),
we define

(1)

\langle\mathscr{T}\wedge\mathscr{S},\varphi\rangle:=\mathscr{U}_{\mathscr{T}}(\mathscr{S}\wedge dd^{c}\varphi)+\langle\beta\wedge\mathscr{S},\varphi\rangle.

Now assume that if
f:X\longrightarrow Y,
is a biholomorphism between smooth compact Kähler manifolds, then we have

f_{*}\mathscr{U}_{\mathscr{R}_{1}}=\mathscr{U}_{f_{*}\mathscr{R}_{1}},\quad f^{*}\mathscr{U}_{\mathscr{R}_{2}}=\mathscr{U}_{f^{*}\mathscr{R}_{2}},

for
\mathscr{R}_{1}\in\mathscr{D}^{q}(X)
and
\mathscr{R}_{2}\in\mathscr{D}^{q}(Y).

Definition 2.2
.

Let
(\mathscr{T}_{n})
be a sequence of currents in

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
\mathscr{T},

\mathscr{T}^{\prime}
be positive closed currents such that
\mathscr{T}_{|_{\Omega}}=\mathscr{T}^{\prime}_{|_{\Omega}}
in an open subset
\Omega\subseteq X,
and both
\mathscr{T}
and
\mathscr{T}^{\prime}
have continuous superpotentials. Then, for any
\mathscr{S}\in\mathscr{D}^{r}(X),

\big(\mathscr{T}\wedge\mathscr{S}\big)_{|_{\Omega}}=\big(\mathscr{T}^{\prime}\wedge\mathscr{S}\big)_{|_{\Omega}}.

Proof.

In
[
25
]
, for any current
\mathscr{S}
with continuous superpotential, a family
\{\mathscr{T}_{\theta}\}_{\theta\in\mathbb{C}^{*}}
is constructed that
\mathscr{T}_{\theta}
converges SP-uniformly to
\mathscr{S}
as
|\theta|\to 0.
Let
\epsilon:=|\theta|>0,
be a small positive number, and

be any open set such that
V_{\epsilon}
, the
\epsilon
-neighbourhood of

, is contained entirely in

Therefore, by the hypothesis of the lemma, we can construct families of smooth forms
\mathscr{T}_{n}
and
\mathscr{T}_{n}^{\prime}
converging

-uniformly to
\mathscr{T}
and
\mathscr{T}^{\prime}
respectively. Moreover,

{\mathscr{T}_{n}}_{|_{V_{\epsilon}}}={\mathscr{T}^{\prime}_{n}}_{|_{V_{\epsilon}}}.

Now, for any
(d-q-r,d-q-r)

we can cover the support of

\big(\mathscr{T}_{n}\wedge\mathscr{S}\big)\wedge\varphi=\big(\mathscr{T}_{n}^{\prime}\wedge\mathscr{S}\big)\wedge\varphi.

This, together with Theorem

∎

Theorem 2.8
.

Let
q:\widehat{X}\longrightarrow X,
be the blowing up of the compact Kähler manifold
X
along a submanifold. Assume that
\mathscr{T}\in\mathscr{D}_{p}(\widehat{X})
is such that the support of
\mathscr{T}
does not intersect the exceptional divisors of
\widehat{X}.
If the current
\mathscr{T}
has a continuous superpotential then
q_{*}\mathscr{T}
has the same property.

Proof.

Assume that
\mathscr{S}\in\mathscr{D}^{d-p+1}(X)
. We need to show that the superpotential of
q_{*}\mathscr{T}
, which is a function defined on smooth forms in
\mathscr{D}^{d-q+1,0}(X)
, can be extended to a continuous function on

\mathscr{D}^{d-q+1,0}(X)
. Let
\alpha
be a smooth closed
(p,p)
-form cohomologous to
q_{*}\mathscr{T}
and define
\beta:=q^{*}\alpha
. By hypothesis, we have
q^{*}q_{*}\mathscr{T}=\mathscr{T}
. It follows that
\mathscr{T}
is cohomologous to
\beta
. Fix a potential
U
of
q_{*}\mathscr{T}-\alpha
which is smooth near the blowup locus. We have
dd^{c}U=q_{*}\mathscr{T}-\alpha
and therefore

dd^{c}q^{*}U=\mathscr{T}-\beta
. The smoothness of
U
near the blowup locus implies that
q_{*}(q^{*}U)=U
.

Since
\alpha
is smooth, it is enough to show that the superpotential
\mathscr{U}
of
q_{*}\mathscr{T}-\alpha
can be extended to a continuous function on
\mathscr{D}^{d-q+1,0}(X)
. Since the last current has a vanishing cohomology class, its superpotential doesn’t depend on the normalization.

Claim 1.
Let
(\mathscr{S}_{n})
be a bounded sequence of smooth forms in
\mathscr{D}^{d-p+1}(X)
. Then the sequence
\mathscr{U}(\mathscr{S}_{n})
is bounded.

Proof of Claim 1.

Since
\mathscr{S}_{n}
is smooth, we have

\mathscr{U}(\mathscr{S}_{n})=\langle U,\mathscr{S}_{n}\rangle=\langle q^{*}(U),q^{*}\mathscr{S}_{n}\rangle=\widehat{\mathscr{U}}(q^{*}\mathscr{S}_{n}),

where
\widehat{\mathscr{U}}
denotes the superpotential of
\mathscr{T}-\beta
.
Since the action of
q^{*}
on cohomology is bounded and the mass of a positive closed current only depends on its cohomology class, we see that
q^{*}\mathscr{S}_{n}
is bounded in
\mathscr{D}^{d-p+1,0}(\widehat{X})
. Since
\mathscr{T}
has a continuous superpotential, we deduce that
\widehat{\mathscr{U}}(q^{*}\mathscr{S}_{n})
is bounded and hence
\mathscr{U}(\mathscr{S}_{n})
is bounded as claimed.
∎

Claim 2.
Let
(\mathscr{S}_{n})
be a bounded sequence of smooth forms in
\mathscr{D}^{d-p+1}(X)
converging to 0. Then
\mathscr{U}(\mathscr{S}_{n})
tends to 0.

Proof of Claim 2.

By extracting a subsequence, we can assume that
q^{*}\mathscr{S}_{n}
converges to some current
\mathscr{R}
supported by the exceptional divisor.
By hypothesis, we have
q_{*}(\mathscr{R})=0
.
Using the computation in Claim 1, and the fact that
\widehat{\mathscr{U}}
is continuous, we get

\lim\mathscr{U}(\mathscr{S}_{n})=\widehat{\mathscr{U}}(\mathscr{R}).

Now, since

\widehat{\mathscr{U}}(\mathscr{R})=\langle q^{*}U,\mathscr{R}\rangle=\langle U,q_{*}\mathscr{R}\rangle=0.

This proves the claim.
∎

To finish the proof, let

be any current in

of smooth forms in

converging to
\mathscr{S}
.
By Claim 1, extracting a subsequence allows to assume that
\mathscr{U}(\mathscr{R}_{n})
converges to some real number

. Consider now an arbitrary bounded sequence of

(\mathscr{S}_{n})
of smooth forms in
\mathscr{D}^{d-p+1,0}(X)
converging to
\mathscr{S}
. To show that
\mathscr{U}
extends to a continuous function at
\mathscr{S}
, it is enough to check
that
\mathscr{U}(\mathscr{S}_{n})
converges to
l

3.
Tropical Cycles, Tori, Tropical Currents

In this section, we recall the definition of tropical cycles and note that with the natural addition of tropical cycles and their
stable intersection
, the tropical cycles form a ring.

3.1.
Tropical varieties

A linear subspace
H\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
is said to be
rational
if there exists a subset of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
that spans
H
. A
rational polyhedron
is the intersection of finitely many rational half-spaces defined by

\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\langle m,x\rangle\geq c,\text{ for some }m\in\mathbb{Z}^{d},~c\in\mathbb{R}\}.

A
rational polyhedral complex
is a polyhedral complex consisting solely of rational polyhedra. The polyhedra in a polyhedral complex are also referred to as
cells
. A
fan
is a polyhedral complex whose cells are all cones. If every cone in a fan
\Sigma
is contained in another fan
\Sigma^{\prime}
, then
\Sigma
is called a
subfan
of
\Sigma
. The one-dimensional cones of a fan are often called
rays
. Throughout this article, all fans and polyhedral complexes are assumed to be
rational
.

For a given polyhedron
\sigma,
and a finitely generated abelian group
N
, we denote by

\displaystyle\text{aff}(\sigma)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\text{affine span of $\sigma$},

\displaystyle H_{\sigma}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\text{translation of $\text{aff}(\sigma)$ to the origin},

\displaystyle N_{\sigma}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle N\cap H_{\sigma},

\displaystyle N(\sigma)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle N/N_{\sigma}.

Consider
\tau,
a codimension one face of a
p
-dimensional polyhedron
\sigma,
and let
u_{\sigma/\tau}
be the unique outward generator of the one-dimensional lattice
(\mathbb{Z}^{d}\cap H_{\sigma})/(\mathbb{Z}^{d}\cap H_{\tau}).

Definition 3.1

(Balancing Condition and Tropical Cycles)
.

Let
\mathscr{C}
be a
p
-dimensional polyhedral complex whose
p
-dimensional cones are equipped with integer weights
w_{\sigma}
. We say that
\mathcal{C}
satisfies the
balancing condition
at
\tau
if

\sum_{\sigma\supset\tau}w(\sigma)~u_{\sigma/\tau}=0,\quad\text{in }\mathbb{Z}^{d}/(\mathbb{Z}^{d}\cap H_{\tau}),

where the sum is over all
p
-dimensional cells
\sigma
in
\mathcal{C}
containing
\tau
as a face. A
tropical cycle
or a
tropical variety
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a weighted complex with finitely many cells that satisfies the balancing condition at every cone of dimension
p-1
. We say a tropical cycle is a
tropical fan
if its support is a fan.

3.2.
Stable intersection and addition of tropical cycles

Recall that, generally speaking, the star of a cone in a complex is the extension of the local
p
-dimensional fan surrounding it. More precisely:

Definition 3.2
.

Given a polyhedral complex
\Sigma\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
and a cell
\tau\in\Sigma
, define the star of
\tau
in
\Sigma,
denoted by
\text{star}_{\Sigma}(\tau),
is a fan in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. The cones of
\text{star}_{\Sigma}(\tau)
are the
extensions
of cells
\sigma
that include
\tau
as a face. Here, by extension, we mean

\bar{\sigma}=\{\lambda(x-y):\lambda\geq 0,x\in\sigma,\,y\in\tau\}.

Definition 3.3

(Stable Intersection)
.

(a)

Let
\mathcal{C}_{1},\mathcal{C}_{2}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
be two positively weighted tropical cycles of dimension
p
and
q,
intersecting transversely. That is, the top dimensional cells
\sigma_{1}\in\mathcal{C}_{1}
and
\sigma_{2}\in\mathcal{C}_{2}
intersect in dimension
p+q-d
and in the relative interior of these cells. Then the stable intersection of
\mathcal{C}_{1}\cdot\mathcal{C}_{2}
is the tropical cycles supported on finitely many cells
\mathcal{C}_{1}\cap\mathcal{C}_{2}.
In this case, the weight of a cell
\sigma_{1}\cap\sigma_{2}
if defined by

w_{\mathcal{C}_{1}\cdot\mathcal{C}_{2}}(\sigma_{1}\cap\sigma_{2})=w_{\sigma_{1}}w_{\sigma_{2}}[N:{N}_{\sigma_{1}}+{N}_{\sigma_{2}}],

where
N=\mathbb{Z}^{d}
here.

(b)

When
\mathcal{C}_{1}
and
\mathcal{C}_{2}
do not intersect transversely, then
\mathcal{C}_{1}\cdot\mathcal{C}_{2}
as a set is the Hausdorff limit of

\mathcal{C}_{1}\cap(\epsilon b+\mathcal{C}_{2}),\quad\text{as $\epsilon\to 0$},

for a fixed generic
b\in\mathbb{R}^{d},
and the weights are the sum of all the tropical multiplicities of the cells in the transversal intersection
\mathcal{C}_{1}\cap(\epsilon b+\mathcal{C}_{2})
which converge to the same
(p+q-d)
-dimensional cell in the Hausdorff metric. Equivalently, for top dimensional cones
\sigma_{1}\in\mathcal{C}_{1}
and
\sigma_{2}\in\mathcal{C}_{2}

w_{\mathcal{C}_{1}\cdot\mathcal{C}_{2}}(\sigma_{1}\cap\sigma_{2})=\sum_{\tau_{1},\tau_{2}}w_{\tau_{1}}w_{\tau_{2}}[{N}:{N}_{\tau_{1}}+{N}_{\tau_{2}}],

where the sum is taken over all
\tau_{1}\in\text{star}_{\mathcal{C}_{1}}(\sigma_{1}\cap\sigma_{2}),\tau_{2}\in\text{star}_{\mathcal{C}_{2}}(\sigma_{1}\cap\sigma_{2})
with
\tau_{1}\cap(\epsilon b+\tau_{2})\neq\varnothing,
for some fixed generic vector
b\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

(c)

When
p+q<d,
then the stable intersection of
\mathcal{C}_{1}
and
\mathcal{C}_{2}
is the empty set.

The following result is proved in tropical geometry; see
[
37
]
*Lemmas 3.6.4 and 3.6.9. We will revisit its proof later through the lens of superpotential theory.

Theorem 3.4
.

When
p+q\geq d,
the stable intersection, defined above, yields a balanced polyhedral complex of dimension
p+q-d
.

5.1.

In a toric variety

for a cone

\sigma.
We have

We also set

in the

Fibres of tropical currents are algebraic varieties with finite degrees and can be extended by zero to any toric variety, in consequence, any tropical current can be extended by zero to toric varieties. Moreover, with the following compatibility condition, we can ask for the extension of the fibres to intersect the toric invariant divisors transversally.

Remark 5.12
.

For two currents
\mathscr{T}\in\mathscr{D}_{p}((\mathbb{C}^{*})^{d})
and
\mathscr{S}\in\mathscr{D}_{q}((\mathbb{C}^{*})^{d})
, we define

\mathscr{T}\wedge\mathscr{S}:=(\mathscr{T}\otimes\mathscr{S})\wedge\Delta,

where
\Delta
is the diagonal, hence a complete intersection in
(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{d}\times(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{d}
. Consequently, this intersection can be defined by repeatedly applying Bedford–Taylor theory to intersect with
\Delta
. In this context, the notions from Bedford–Taylor theory and slicing theory coincide.

To extend this definition to a toric variety
X
, we impose mild conditions on the supports to ensure that the current

\overline{\mathscr{T}}\wedge\overline{\mathscr{S}}-\overline{\mathscr{T}\wedge\mathscr{S}}\in\mathscr{D}_{p+q-n}(X)

has support of Cauchy–Riemann dimension less than
(p+q-n)-1
, and thus vanishes; compare with
[
12
]
. One can check that when
\mathscr{T}
and
\mathscr{S}
are integration currents on algebraic varieties, the multiplicities induced from this intersection coincide exactly with algebraic multiplicities.

5.2.1.
Calculating Intersection Multiplicities Using Monge-Ampère Measures

Using the equality of the supports in the previous section, we only need to prove the intersection multiplicities in the transversal case locally.

5.2.2.
The Real Monge–Ampère Measures

Let
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
be an open subset and
u:\Omega\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a convex (hence continuous) function. The
generalised gradient
of
u
at
x_{0}\in\Omega
is defined by

\nabla u(x_{0})=\big\{\xi\in(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{*}:u(x)-u(x_{0})\geq\langle\xi,x-x_{0}\rangle,\text{ for all $x\in\Omega$}\big\}.

In the above,
\langle~,~\rangle
denotes the standard inner product in
\mathbb{R}^{d},
and
(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{*}
is the dual. The real Monge-Ampère measure associated to a convex function
u
on a Borel set
E\subseteq\Omega,
is given by

\textrm{MA}[u](E)=\mu\big(\bigcup_{y\in E}\nabla u(y)\big),

where
\mu
is the Lebesgue measure on
(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{*}.

It is interesting that for the tropical polynomials, one can compute the associate real Monge–Ampère measures explicitly. Recall that, for any tropical polynomial, there is a natural subdivision of its Newton polytope which is dual to the tropical variety of it. See Figure for an example and
[
13
,
37
]
for details.

Lemma 5.17

(
[
48
]
*Page 59,
[
15
]
*Proposition 2.7.4)
.

Let
\mathfrak{q}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a tropical polynomial associated tropical variety
\mathcal{C}=V_{\textrm{trop}}(\mathfrak{q}),
one has

\textrm{MA}[\mathfrak{q}]=\sum_{a\in\mathcal{C}(0)}\textrm{Vol}\big(\{a\}^{*}\big)\delta_{a},

where
\mathcal{C}(0)
is the
0
-dimensional skeleton of
\mathcal{C},
and
\{a\}^{*}
is the dual of the vertex
a\in\mathcal{C}(0).

A detailed discussion of the preceding theorem can be also found in
[
6
]
.

5.3.
Polarisation

For
n
convex functions
u_{1},\dots,u_{d}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R},
their
mixed Monge-Ampère measure
is defined by

\widetilde{\text{MA}}[u_{1},\dots,u_{d}]=\frac{1}{d!}\sum_{k=1}^{d}\,\sum_{1\leq j_{1}<\dots<j_{k}\leq d}(-1)^{d-k}\,{\text{MA}}[u_{j_{1}}+\dots+u_{j_{k}}].

Recall that this is how the
mixed volume
of
n
convex bodies can be defined from the
n
-dimensional volume. Moreover, it is easy to check that for a convex function
u:\mathbb{R}^{d}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R},

\text{MA}[u]=\widetilde{\text{MA}}[u,\dots,u].

It is customary at this point to prove the Bernstein–Khovanskii–Kushnirenko Theorem, which is simply obtained by taking the total mass from Lemma
5.17
.

Proposition 5.18
.

Let
\mathfrak{q},\mathfrak{q}_{1},\dots,\mathfrak{q}_{d}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
be tropical polynomials. We have the following facts:

(a)

\textrm{MA}[\mathfrak{q}](\mathbb{R}^{d})=\textrm{Vol}_{d}(\Delta_{\mathfrak{q}}),
where
\Delta_{\mathfrak{q}}
is the Newton polytope of
\mathfrak{q}.

(b)

(Tropical Bernstein–Khovanskii–Kushnirenko Theorem)
\widetilde{\text{MA}}[\mathfrak{q}_{1},\dots,\mathfrak{q}_{d}](\mathbb{R}^{d})=\widetilde{\text{Vol}}(\Delta_{\mathfrak{q}_{1}},\dots,\Delta_{\mathfrak{q}_{d}}),
where
\widetilde{\text{Vol}}
is the mixed volume.

Proposition 5.19
.

Assume that
\alpha_{i},\beta_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
for
i=1,\dots,d.
Let
\mathfrak{q}_{i}=\max\{\langle\alpha_{i},x\rangle,\langle\beta_{i},x\rangle\}
be
n
tropical polynomials. Then,

d!\,\widetilde{\text{MA}}[\mathfrak{q}_{1},\dots,\mathfrak{q}_{d}]=\kappa\,\delta_{0},

where
\kappa
is given by the volume
zonotope
of the Minkowski sum of the vectors
\sum_{i=1}^{d}[\alpha_{i}-\beta_{i}].
In addition, these multiplicities coincide with the intersection multiplicities in Definition
3.3
for
d
tropical hypersurfaces.

Proof.

Note that
\Delta_{\mathfrak{q}_{i}}
is the line segment between
\alpha_{i}
and
\beta_{i}.
Moreover, in the definition of

\widetilde{\text{MA}}[\mathfrak{q}_{1},\dots,\mathfrak{q}_{d}]
only
\textrm{Vol}(\sum_{i=1}^{d}[\alpha_{i}-\beta_{i}])
possibly has a non-zero
n
-dimensional volume. Finally, the origin is the only
0
-dimensional cell of the tropical variety of polynomial
\mathfrak{q}_{1}+\dots+\mathfrak{q}_{d}
, if and only if,
\{\alpha_{1}-\beta_{1},\cdots,\alpha_{d}-\beta_{d}\}
forms a linearly independent set. Therefore,
d!\,\textrm{MA}[\mathfrak{q}_{1}+\dots+\mathfrak{q}_{d}]=\kappa\,\delta_{0}.
To prove the second statement, we first claim that any Borel subset
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n},

\int_{{\rm Log}^{-1}(U)}\mathscr{T}_{\mathrm{Trop}(\mathfrak{q}_{1})}\wedge\dots\wedge\mathscr{T}_{\mathrm{Trop}(\mathfrak{q}_{d})}=d!\,\widetilde{\text{MA}}[\mathfrak{q}_{1},\dots,\mathfrak{q}_{d}](\Omega).

To see this, recall that
{\mathscr{T}_{\mathrm{Trop}(\mathfrak{q}_{i})}}=dd^{c}[\mathfrak{q}_{i}\circ{\rm Log}],
by Theorem
4.4
and Remark
4.5
. Further, by
[
43
]

\int_{{\rm Log}^{-1}(U)}\big(dd^{c}[\mathfrak{q}\circ{\rm Log}]\big)^{d}=d!\,{\text{MA}}[\mathfrak{q}](U).

Polarising both sides proves the claim. This implies that the tropical intersection multiplicities coincide with the multiplicities in this proposition for hypersurfaces.

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a
p
-dimensional positively weighted tropical cycle in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
p\geq 1.
Assume that
S\subseteq(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{d}
is an algebraic hypersurface with transversal intersection with
\mathscr{T}_{\mathcal{C}}.
Then,
[S]\wedge\mathscr{T}_{\mathcal{C}}
is admissible and it is a closed positive current of bidimension
(p-1,p-1)
given by

[S]\wedge\mathscr{T}_{\mathcal{C}}=\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{C}}w_{\sigma}\int_{x\in S_{N(\sigma)}}\mathbbm{1}_{{\rm Log}^{-1}(\sigma^{\circ})}\big[S\cap\pi_{\mathrm{aff}(\sigma)}^{-1}(x)\big]\ d\mu(x).

Proof.

The idea of the proof is similar to that of
[
5
, Proposition 4.11]
. Let
f
be a polynomial with vanishing
S
in
(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{d}
. Assume that
{\rm Log}^{-1}(\sigma^{\circ})\cap S\neq\varnothing,
for a
p
-dimensional cell
\sigma\in\mathcal{C},
then for each fibre,
\pi_{\sigma}^{-1}(x):=\pi_{{\rm aff}(\sigma)}^{-1}(x),
the transversality assumption allows for the application of the Lelong–Poincaré formula to deduce

\quad dd^{c}\big(\log|f|\mathbbm{1}_{{\rm Log}^{-1}(\sigma^{\circ})}\big[\pi^{-1}_{\sigma}(x)\big]\big)=\mathbbm{1}_{{\rm Log}^{-1}(\sigma^{\circ})}[S\cap\pi^{-1}_{\sigma}(x)\big]+\mathscr{R}_{\sigma}(x),

where
\mathscr{R}_{\sigma}(x)
is a
(p-1,p-1)
-bidimensional current. The support of
\mathscr{R}_{\sigma}(x)
lies in the boundary of
{\rm Log}^{-1}(\sigma),
as
\mathscr{R}_{\sigma}(x)
is the difference of two currents that coincide in any set of form
{\rm Log}^{-1}(B),
where

is a small ball with

B\cap\sigma^{\prime}=\varnothing,\quad\text{for a $p$-dimensional cell $\sigma^{\prime}\in\mathcal{C},\sigma^{\prime}\neq\sigma$ },

and both vanish outside
{\rm Log}^{-1}(\sigma).

Integrating along the fibers, and adding for all
p
-dimensional cells
\sigma\in\mathcal{C},
we obtain

[S]\wedge{\mathscr{T}_{\mathcal{C}}}=\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{C}}w_{\sigma}\int_{x\in S_{N(\sigma)}}\mathbbm{1}_{{\rm Log}^{-1}(\sigma^{\circ})}\big[S\cap\pi_{\mathrm{aff}(\sigma)}^{-1}(x)\big]\ d\mu(x)+\mathscr{R}_{\mathcal{C}},

where
\mathscr{R}_{\mathcal{C}}
is
(p-1,p-1)
-dimensional current. We claim that
\mathscr{R}_{\mathcal{C}}
is
normal
,
i.e.

\mathscr{R}_{\mathcal{C}}
and

have measure coefficients;
\mathscr{R}_{\mathcal{C}}
is a difference of two normal currents, where the first current

is a positive closed current, and the second current is an addition of normal pieces. Moreover, the support of
\mathscr{R}_{\mathcal{C}}
is a subset of
S
as it is a difference of two currents that both vanish outside

As a result, the current
\mathscr{R}_{\mathcal{C}}
is supported on

This set is a real manifold of Cauchy–Riemann dimension less than

vanishes; see also the discussion following
[
5
, Proposition 4.11]
.
∎

Theorem 6.3
.

Let
M\subseteq(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{d-p}
and
N\subseteq(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{p}
be two bounded open subsets such that

contains the real torus
(S^{1})^{p}

Since all the currents
\mathscr{T}_{n}
and

are horizontal-like, the slices are well-defined, and we prove that the slices have the same cluster value. Let
\mathscr{S}

Note that such
\mathscr{S}
always exists by Banach–Alaoglu theorem. As both measures
\mathscr{S}

are supported
\{x\}\times(S^{1})^{p}

2.10

\langle\mathscr{S},\phi\rangle\leq\langle\mathscr{T}|\pi|x\rangle(\phi),

\mathbb{C}^{d},

\mathscr{S}
coincides with the mass of
\langle\mathscr{T}|\pi|x\rangle.
Now, note that if
\phi

-\phi
and
\phi

\langle\mathscr{S},\phi\rangle=\langle\mathscr{T}|\pi|x\rangle(\phi),

for every pluriharmonic function. Recall that if

\text{Re}(f)

7.
Dynamical Tropicalisation and Intersections

7.1.
Dynamical tropicalisation with a non-trivial valuation

Recall that for a field
\mathbb{K},

\nu:\mathbb{K}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}\cup\{\infty\},
is called a valuation if it satisfies the following properties for every
a,b\in\mathbb{K}
:

(a)

\nu(a)=\infty
if and only if
a=0;

(b)

\nu(ab)=\nu(a)+\nu(b);

(c)

\nu(a+b)\geq\min\{\nu(a),\nu(b)\}.

A valuation is called
trivial
, if the valuation of any non-zero element is 0. For an element
a\in\mathbb{K},
we denote by
\bar{a}
its image in the residue field. We are interested in the case where
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{C}((t)),
is the field of
formal Laurent series
with the variable
t,
with the usual valuation. That is, for
g(t)=\sum_{j\geq k}a_{j}t^{j},
with
a_{k}\neq 0,
the valuation equals the minimal exponent
\nu(g)=k\in\mathbb{Z}.

Definition 7.1
.

(a)

Let
f=\sum_{\alpha\in A}c_{\alpha}z^{\alpha}\in\mathbb{K}[z^{\pm 1}]
, be a Laurent polynomial in
d
variables, where
A\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{d}
is a finite subset. The tropicalisation of
f
with respect to
\nu,

\displaystyle\mathrm{trop}_{\nu}(f)

\displaystyle:\mathbb{R}^{d}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R},

\displaystyle x

\displaystyle\mapsto\max\{-\nu(c_{\alpha})+\langle x,\alpha\rangle\}.

(b)

Let
I\subseteq\mathbb{K}[z^{\pm 1}]
be an ideal. The tropical variety associated to
I
, as a set, is defined as

{\text{Trop}_{\nu}}(I):=\bigcap_{f\in I}\text{Trop}(\mathrm{trop}_{\nu}(f)),

where
\text{Trop}(\mathrm{trop}_{\nu}(f))
is the set of points where
\mathrm{trop}_{\nu}(f)
is not differentiable; see Remark
4.5
.

(c)

For an algebraic subvariety of the torus
Z\subseteq{(\mathbb{K}^{*})}^{d},
with the associated ideal
\mathbb{I}(Z)
, the tropicalisation of
Z
, as a set, is
\mathrm{Trop}_{\nu}(Z):=\mathrm{Trop}_{\nu}(\mathbb{I}(Z)).

(d)

In all the situations above,
\mathrm{trop}_{0}
denotes the tropicalisation with respect to the trivial valuation.

We need to relate a non-trivial valuation to the trivial valuation. Compare to
[
11
]
*Lemma 1.1.

Lemma 7.2
.

Consider the ideal
I\subseteq\mathbb{C}[t^{\pm 1},z^{\pm 1}]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\iota}}{{\hookrightarrow}}\mathbb{C}((t))[z^{\pm 1}].
Assume that
(u,x)
are the coordinates in
\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Then, we have the following equality of sets

\mathrm{Trop}_{0}(I)\cap\{u=-1\}=\mathrm{Trop}_{\nu}(\iota(I)),

where
\nu
is the the usual valuation in
\mathbb{C}((t)).
In other words, the tropicalisation of
I
as an ideal in
\mathbb{C}[t^{\pm 1},z^{\pm 1}]
with respect to the trivial valuation intersected with
\{u=-1\}
coincides with the tropicalisation of
I=\iota(I)
with respect to the usual valuation in
\mathbb{C}((t)).

The proof of the lemma becomes clear with the following example.

Example 7.3
.

Let

f(x,t)=4(t^{3}+t^{-1})z_{1}z_{2}+(1+t+t^{2})z_{1}.

Then, the tropicalisation of
f\in\mathbb{C}[t^{\pm 1},z^{\pm 1}],
with respect to the trivial valuation equals:

\mathrm{trop}_{0}(f)=\max\big\{\max\{3u+x_{1}+x_{2},-u+x_{1}+x_{2}\},\max\{x_{1},u+x_{1},2u+x_{1}\}\big\}.

Letting
u:=-1,

\mathrm{trop}_{0}(f)(-1,x)=\max\{1+x_{1}+x_{2},x_{1}\}.

The latter equals
\mathrm{trop}_{\nu}(f)
as an element of
\mathbb{C}((t))[z^{\pm 1}].

Proof of Lemma
7.2
.

If
f
is a monomial in
\mathbb{C}[t][z],
then it is clear that

\mathrm{trop}_{0}(f)(-1,x)=\mathrm{trop}_{\nu}(\iota(f)).

Therefore, we have the equality for any polynomial in
f\in\mathbb{C}[t,z].
To prove the main statement, note that

\displaystyle\mathrm{Trop}_{\nu}(\iota(I))

\displaystyle=\bigcap_{f\in\iota(I)}\mathrm{Trop}(\mathrm{trop}_{\nu}(f))

\displaystyle=\bigcap_{f\in I}\big(\mathrm{Trop}(\mathrm{trop}_{0}(f))\cap\{u=-1\}\big)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Trop}_{0}(I)\cap\{u=-1\}.

∎

Lemma 7.9
.

Let

with multiplicity 2. Note that the (set-theoretic) intersection
W\cap V=\{(0,1)\},
thus
\mathrm{Trop}_{0}(W\cap V)=(0,0)
with multiplicity 1, whereas
\mathrm{Trop}_{0}(W)\cdot\mathrm{Trop}_{0}(V)=(0,0)
with multiplicity 2. We obtain

2\,\mathscr{T}_{\mathrm{Trop}_{0}(W\cap V)}=\mathscr{T}_{\mathrm{Trop}_{0}(W)\cdot\mathrm{Trop}_{0}(V)}.

Note that this inequality, gladly, does not contradict
[
42
]
*3.3.1 (a consequence of
[
29
]
*8.2), which the scheme-theoretic intersection
W\cap V
is considered.

Proof of Theorem
7.11
.

Assume that
X_{\Sigma}
is a toric variety compatible with
\mathrm{Trop}_{0}(W)+\mathrm{Trop}_{0}(V).
We need to show that the hypothesis (a),
\dots
, (d) of Theorem
6.9
for
\overline{\mathscr{W}}_{m}={m^{p-n}}\Phi^{*}_{m}[\overline{W}]
and
\overline{\mathscr{V}}_{m}={m^{q-n}}\Phi^{*}_{m}[\overline{V}]
is satisfied. Note that hypotheses (a) and (b) are implied by Theorem
7.6
. The hypotheses (c) and (d) for
\overline{\mathscr{W}}_{1}
and
\overline{\mathscr{V}}_{1}
are a result of
[
42
]
*Proposition 3.3.2. The hypotheses (c) and (d) for any
m
are implied by the fact that
\Phi_{m}^{*}\big([\overline{W}]\wedge[\overline{V}]\big)=\Phi_{m}^{*}[\overline{W}]\wedge\Phi_{m}^{*}[\overline{V}].
Therefore,

\overline{\mathscr{W}}_{m}\wedge\overline{\mathscr{V}}_{m}\longrightarrow\overline{\mathscr{T}}_{\mathrm{Trop}_{0}(W)}\wedge\overline{\mathscr{T}}_{\mathrm{Trop}_{0}(V)},\quad\text{as $m\to\infty,$}

and the first assertion is obtained by restricting to
T_{N}\subseteq X_{\Sigma}
and Theorem
5.13
. To see the second assertion, note that by Theorem
7.6
,

\frac{1}{m^{2d-(p+q)}}\Phi^{*}_{m}[W\cap V]\longrightarrow\mathscr{T}_{\mathrm{Trop}_{0}(W\cap V)},\quad\text{as $m\to\infty.$}

However, as currents
[W\cap V]\leq[W]\wedge[V]
since the right-hand side might produce multiplicities on the intersection. Applying
\Phi_{m}^{*}
to both sides preserves this inequality. Therefore, for every
m,

\Phi^{*}_{m}[W\cap V]\leq\Phi_{m}^{*}[W]\wedge\Phi_{m}^{*}[V].

We can conclude by taking the limit
m\longrightarrow\infty.

∎

The tropical version of the following was observed in various places
[
42
,
37
]
, which does not need to assume the proper intersection of tropicalisations.

Theorem 7.13
.

Let
W
and
V
be two smooth algebraic subvarieties of
(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{d}
, and for
0<\epsilon<1,
let
U_{\epsilon}((S^{1})^{d})
be an
\epsilon
-neighbourhood of
(S^{1})^{d}.
Then,

\frac{1}{m^{2d-(p+q)}}\int_{(t_{1},t_{2})\in U_{\epsilon}((S^{1})^{2d})}\Phi_{m}^{*}\big([t_{1}V]\wedge[t_{2}W]\big)\,d\nu(t_{1})\otimes d\nu(t_{2})\longrightarrow\mathscr{T}_{\mathrm{Trop}_{0}(V)}\wedge\mathscr{T}_{\mathrm{Trop}_{0}(W)},

as
m\to\infty
. Here, the
d\nu
is the normalised Lebesgue measures on
U_{\epsilon}((S^{1})^{d})
. Moreover, when
V
and
W
are smooth the integrand in the left-hand-side can be replaced by
\Phi_{m}^{*}[t_{1}V\cap t_{2}W].

Proof.

By a classical result of Kleiman
[
35
]
, the intersection of generic translations
t_{1}V\cap t_{2}W
is proper and transversal in case
V
and
W
are smooth. Since
\Phi_{m}^{*}
preserves proper intersections and transversality, we can separate the above integrand into
\Phi_{m}^{*}[t_{1}V]\wedge\Phi_{m}^{*}[t_{2}W]
. Using polar coordinates
(r_{i},\theta_{i})
for
t_{i}
, we have

\int_{t_{1}\in U_{\epsilon}((S^{1})^{d})}\Phi^{*}_{m}[t_{1}V]\,d\nu(t_{1})=\int_{t_{1}\in U_{\epsilon}((S^{1})^{d})}t_{1}^{1/m}\,\Phi^{*}_{m}[V]\,d\nu(t_{1}).

Here, with abuse of notation, we choose the
m
-th root
t_{1}^{1/m}
to be the first root of
t^{1/m}.
We can also obtain a similar equation for
t_{2}W.
Further,

\big(\Phi_{m}^{*}[t_{1}V]\otimes\Phi_{m}^{*}[t_{2}W])\wedge[\Delta]=\big(\Phi_{m}^{*}[V]\otimes\Phi_{m}^{*}[W]\big)\wedge(t_{1})^{-1/m}(t_{2})^{-1/m}[\Delta].

\int_{U_{\epsilon}(S^{1})^{2n}}(t_{1})^{-1/m}(t_{2})^{-1/m}[\Delta]=\int_{[(1-\epsilon),1+\epsilon]}\mathscr{T}^{R_{m}}_{\Delta}dR,

where
R_{m}=(|t_{1}|^{-1/m},|t_{2}|^{-1/m}),
and
\mathscr{T}^{R_{m}}_{\Delta}
is the tropical current associated with the diagonal
\Delta\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d},
where the compact torus is rescaled to
R_{m}(S^{1})^{2d}.
In a toric variety compatible with
\Delta\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d},
for any
R,

\overline{\mathscr{T}^{R_{m}}_{\Delta}}
has a continuous superpotential, and
\overline{\mathscr{T}^{R_{m}}_{\Delta}}
is SP-convergent to
\overline{\mathscr{T}}_{\Delta}.
Using Proposition
2.6
, Theorem
2.9
, and restricting yields:

\frac{1}{m^{2d-(p+q)}}\big(\Phi_{m}^{*}[t_{1}V]\otimes\Phi_{m}^{*}[t_{2}W])\wedge\int_{[(1-\epsilon),1+\epsilon]}\mathscr{T}^{R_{m}}_{\Delta}dR\longrightarrow\big(\mathscr{T}_{\mathrm{Trop}_{0}(V)}\otimes\mathscr{T}_{\mathrm{Trop}_{0}(W)}\big)\wedge\mathscr{T}_{\Delta},

as
m\to\infty.
The latter equals the tropical current

\mathscr{T}_{\big(\mathrm{Trop}_{0}(V)\times\mathrm{Trop}_{0}(W)\big)\cdot\Delta},

which can be identified with
\mathscr{T}_{\mathrm{Trop}_{0}(V)}\wedge\mathscr{T}_{\mathrm{Trop}_{0}(W)}.

∎

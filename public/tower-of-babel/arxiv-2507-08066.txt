Kappa Plane Wave Modes and Continuous Squeezing in Quantum Field Theory
Arash Azizi

Abstract
We introduce a new family of field modes in flat spacetime—termed κ \kappa -plane wave modes —constructed from κ \kappa -dependent linear combinations of Minkowski plane waves. These modes define a one-parameter family of vacua | 0 κ ⟩ \ket{0_\kappa} that smoothly interpolate between different quantizations, reducing to the Minkowski vacuum in the limit κ → 0 \kappa\to 0 . We show that | 0 κ ⟩ \ket{0_\kappa} is uniquely characterized as a continuous-mode squeezed vacuum, with frequency-dependent squeezing parameter r ⁡ ( ν ) r(\nu) satisfying tanh r ( ν ) = e − π ν / κ \tanh r(\nu)=e^{-\pi\nu/\kappa} . We also derive two Bogoliubov transformations between κ \kappa -plane wave and κ \kappa -Rindler operators, which exhibit a universal form and smoothly interpolate between all known mode decompositions, including those of Minkowski, Rindler, and Unruh quantizations as limiting cases.

2
Kappa plane wave: combining plane wave modes with opposite sign norms

This work considers a massless scalar field propagating in (1+1)-dimensional Minkowski spacetime. We adopt natural units (
c=1
) and use light-cone coordinates defined by
u=t-x
and
v=t+x
.

2.1
Combination of plane wave Minkowski

Consider the field mode defined as a combination of plane wave Minkowski modes as follows

\Phi(u,\Omega)=\alpha(\Omega)e^{-{{\rm i\,}}\Omega u}+\beta(\Omega)e^{{{\rm i\,}}\Omega u}\,,

(3)

where
\Omega\in\mathbb{R}
. The inner product should satisfy the following

\left\langle\Phi(x,\Omega),\Phi(x,\Omega^{\prime})\right\rangle=[a_{\Omega},a^{\dagger}_{\Omega^{\prime}}]=\delta(\Omega-\Omega^{\prime})\,,\quad\quad\left\langle\Phi(u,\Omega),\Phi^{*}(u,\Omega^{\prime})\right\rangle=-\left[a_{\Omega},a_{\Omega^{\prime}}\right]=0\,.

(4)

Substituting the modes from (
3
), we have

\displaystyle\left\langle\Phi(u,\Omega),\Phi\left(u,\Omega^{\prime}\right)\right\rangle=

\displaystyle\left\langle\alpha(\Omega)e^{-{{\rm i\,}}\Omega u}+\beta(\Omega)e^{{{\rm i\,}}\Omega u},\alpha(\Omega^{\prime})e^{-{{\rm i\,}}\Omega^{\prime}u}+\beta(\Omega^{\prime})e^{{{\rm i\,}}\Omega^{\prime}u}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 4\pi\Omega\delta\left(\Omega-\Omega^{\prime}\right)\left(\alpha^{*}(\Omega)\alpha\left(\Omega^{\prime}\right)-\beta^{*}(\Omega)\beta\left(\Omega^{\prime}\right)\right)

\displaystyle+4\pi\Omega\delta\left(\Omega+\Omega^{\prime}\right)\left(\alpha^{*}(\Omega)\beta\left(\Omega^{\prime}\right)-\beta^{*}(\Omega)\alpha\left(\Omega^{\prime}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 4\pi\Omega\delta\left(\Omega-\Omega^{\prime}\right)\left(\absolutevalue{\alpha(\Omega)}^{2}-\absolutevalue{\beta(\Omega)}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle+4\pi\Omega\delta\left(\Omega+\Omega^{\prime}\right)\Big(\alpha^{*}(\Omega)\beta(-\Omega)-\beta^{*}(\Omega)\alpha(-\Omega)\Big)\,,

(5)

where we have used the following result from the inner product

\left\langle e^{-{{\rm i\,}}\Omega u},e^{-{{\rm i\,}}\Omega^{\prime}u}\right\rangle=4\pi\Omega\,\delta(\Omega-\Omega^{\prime})\,.

(6)

Therefore, demanding
\left\langle\Phi(x,\Omega),\Phi(x,\Omega^{\prime})\right\rangle=\delta(\Omega-\Omega^{\prime})
yields

|\alpha(\Omega)|^{2}-|\beta(\Omega)|^{2}=\frac{1}{4\pi\Omega}\,,

(7)

and

\alpha^{*}(\Omega)\beta(-\Omega)-\beta^{*}(\Omega)\alpha(-\Omega)=0\,,

(8)

if
\delta\left(\Omega+\Omega^{\prime}\right)
were to be nonzero.

Next, we consider the inner product between a mode and the complex conjugate of a mode. Requiring
\left\langle\Phi^{*}(x,\Omega),\Phi(x,\Omega^{\prime})\right\rangle=0
results

\displaystyle\left\langle\Phi^{*}(u,\Omega),\Phi\left(u,\Omega^{\prime}\right)\right\rangle=

\displaystyle\left\langle\alpha^{*}(\Omega)e^{{{\rm i\,}}\Omega u}+\beta^{*}(\Omega)e^{-{{\rm i\,}}\Omega u},\alpha(\Omega^{\prime})e^{-{{\rm i\,}}\Omega^{\prime}u}+\beta(\Omega^{\prime})e^{{{\rm i\,}}\Omega^{\prime}u}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 4\pi\Omega\delta\left(\Omega-\Omega^{\prime}\right)\left(\beta(\Omega)\alpha\left(\Omega^{\prime}\right)-\alpha(\Omega)\beta\left(\Omega^{\prime}\right)\right)

\displaystyle+4\pi\Omega\delta\left(\Omega+\Omega^{\prime}\right)\left(-\alpha(\Omega)\alpha\left(\Omega^{\prime}\right)+\beta(\Omega)\beta\left(\Omega^{\prime}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 4\pi\Omega\delta\left(\Omega+\Omega^{\prime}\right)\Big(-\alpha(\Omega)\alpha\left(-\Omega\right)+\beta(\Omega)\beta\left(-\Omega\right)\Big)\,,

(9)

which indicates

-\alpha(\Omega)\alpha\left(-\Omega\right)+\beta(\Omega)\beta\left(-\Omega\right)=0\,,

(10)

if
\delta\left(\Omega+\Omega^{\prime}\right)
were to be nonzero.

Thus, the inner product structure imposes three constraints. By combining (
8
) and (
10
), we find

\alpha\left(-\Omega\right)\beta\left(-\Omega\right)\Big(|\alpha(\Omega)|^{2}-|\beta(\Omega)|^{2}\Big)=0\,.

(11)

However, since the normalization constraint (
7
) forbids
|\alpha(\Omega)|^{2}-|\beta(\Omega)|^{2}=0
, the only way to satisfy (
11
) is to demand
\alpha(-\Omega)\beta(-\Omega)=0
. This leads to a contradiction: if
\alpha(\Omega)
vanishes, so must
\beta(\Omega)
, and vice versa, which is inconsistent with the normalization condition.

This contradiction suggests that allowing
\Omega
to span the full real line
\mathbb{R}
leads to inconsistency. A natural resolution is to restrict to
\Omega>0
. In this regime, the condition
\delta\left(\Omega+\Omega^{\prime}\right)=0
is automatically satisfied, eliminating the need to impose (
8
) and (
10
).

Assuming
\Omega>0
, we are left with only one constraint from (
5
), namely the normalization condition (
7
). Following
Azizi2023
, we choose the following ansatz:

\alpha(\Lambda)=\frac{e^{{\frac{\pi\Lambda}{2\kappa}}}}{\sqrt{8\pi\Lambda\,\sinh{(\fft{\pi\Lambda}{\kappa} )}}}\,,\qquad\qquad\beta(\Lambda)=\frac{e^{{\frac{-\pi\Lambda}{2\kappa}}}}{\sqrt{8\pi\Lambda\,\sinh{(\fft{\pi\Lambda}{\kappa} )}}}\,.

(12)

While the normalization condition (
7
) allows for an infinite family of solutions, the ansatz presented in Eq. (
12
) is not arbitrary. This specific form is required for two crucial physical reasons. First, it ensures the parameter
\kappa
is a frequency-independent constant. Second, and most importantly, this is the unique choice that allows the resulting
\kappa
-vacuum to be interpreted as a continuous-mode squeezed state of the Minkowski vacuum. This physical insight is the central motivation for our construction. A more detailed technical justification is provided in the Appendix
B
.

With the above mentioned ansatz, the mode becomes

\boxed{\quad\Phi(u,\Lambda,\kappa)={\frac{1}{{\sqrt{8\pi\Lambda\,\sinh{(\fft{\pi\Lambda}{\kappa} )}}}}}\left\{e^{{\frac{\pi\Lambda}{2\kappa}}}\,e^{-{{\rm i\,}}\Lambda u}+e^{-{\frac{\pi\Lambda}{2\kappa}}}\,e^{{{\rm i\,}}\Lambda u}\right\}\,.\quad}

(13)

This mode, referred to as the
kappa plane wave mode
, combines positive and negative frequency Minkowski plane waves with
\kappa
-dependent weights, enabling a continuous interpolation to the standard Minkowski mode in the limit
\kappa\to 0
, as shown in Eq. (
16
). The weights
e^{{\frac{\pi\Lambda}{2\kappa}}}
and
e^{-{\frac{\pi\Lambda}{2\kappa}}}
introduce a frequency-dependent squeezing effect, which we demonstrate in Section
5
leads to the vacuum
\ket{0_\kappa}
being a continuous-mode squeezed state relative to the Minkowski vacuum. The field then can be written as

\Phi(u)=\int_{0}^{\infty}d\Lambda\Big(\Phi(u,\Lambda,\kappa){\cal A}_{\Lambda,\kappa}+\Phi^{*}(u,\Lambda,\kappa){\cal A}^{\dagger}_{\Lambda,\kappa}\Big)\,,

(14)

where
{\cal A}_{\Lambda,\kappa}
represents the annihilation operator for a
\kappa
-mode with the frequency
\Lambda
. Explicitly, using (
13
) one has

\displaystyle\Phi(u)=\int_{0}^{\infty}d\Lambda{\frac{1}{{\sqrt{8\pi\Lambda\,\sinh{(\fft{\pi\Lambda}{\kappa} )}}}}}\bigg\{

\displaystyle\Big(e^{{\frac{\pi\Lambda}{2\kappa}}}\,e^{-{{\rm i\,}}\Lambda u}+e^{-{\frac{\pi\Lambda}{2\kappa}}}\,e^{{{\rm i\,}}\Lambda u}\Big)\,{\cal A}_{\Lambda,\kappa}

\displaystyle+\Big(e^{{\frac{\pi\Lambda}{2\kappa}}}\,e^{{{\rm i\,}}\Lambda u}+e^{-{\frac{\pi\Lambda}{2\kappa}}}\,e^{-{{\rm i\,}}\Lambda u}\Big)\,{\cal A}^{\dagger}_{\Lambda,\kappa}\bigg\}\,.

(15)

2.2
Minkowski plane wave as a special case

Consider the limit
\kappa\rightarrow 0
in the expression (
13
). Since both
\Lambda
and
\kappa
are strictly positive, the exponential suppression in the second term causes it to vanish, and the mode reduces to

\Phi(u,\Lambda>0,\kappa\rightarrow 0)={\frac{1}{\sqrt{4\pi\Lambda}}}e^{-{{\rm i\,}}\Lambda u}\,.

(16)

This corresponds to the standard positive-frequency Minkowski plane wave. Hence, in the limit
\kappa\to 0
, the
\kappa
-mode smoothly recovers the Minkowski mode structure. Moreover, we have

{\cal A}_{\Lambda,0}=a_{\Lambda}\,.

(17)

3
Bogoliubov transformations

3.1
Bogoliubov transformation between kappa plane wave modes

We now derive the Bogoliubov transformation between two distinct
\kappa
-plane wave mode sets. Let the field
\Phi(u)
be expanded in both
\kappa
and
\kappa^{\prime}
bases as follows:

\displaystyle\Phi(u)=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}d\Lambda{\frac{1}{{\sqrt{8\pi\Lambda\,\sinh{(\fft{\pi\Lambda}{\kappa} )}}}}}\Big\{\Big(e^{{\frac{\pi\Lambda}{2\kappa}}}\,e^{-{{\rm i\,}}\Lambda u}+e^{-{\frac{\pi\Lambda}{2\kappa}}}\,e^{{{\rm i\,}}\Lambda u}\Big)\,{\cal A}_{\Lambda,\kappa}

\displaystyle\phantom{\int_{0}^{\infty}d\Lambda{\frac{1}{{\sqrt{8\pi\Lambda\,\sinh{(\fft{\pi\Lambda}{\kappa} )}}}}}\Big\{}+\Big(e^{{\frac{\pi\Lambda}{2\kappa}}}\,e^{{{\rm i\,}}\Lambda u}+e^{-{\frac{\pi\Lambda}{2\kappa}}}\,e^{-{{\rm i\,}}\Lambda u}\Big)\,{\cal A}^{\dagger}_{\Lambda,\kappa}\Big\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}d\Lambda{\frac{1}{{\sqrt{8\pi\Lambda\,\sinh{(\fft{\pi\Lambda}{\kappa'} )}}}}}\Big\{\Big(e^{{\frac{\pi\Lambda}{2\kappa^{\prime}}}}\,e^{-{{\rm i\,}}\Lambda u}+e^{-{\frac{\pi\Lambda}{2\kappa^{\prime}}}}\,e^{{{\rm i\,}}\Lambda u}\Big)\,{\cal A}_{\Lambda,\kappa^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\phantom{\int_{0}^{\infty}d\Lambda{\frac{1}{{\sqrt{8\pi\Lambda\,\sinh{(\fft{\pi\Lambda}{\kappa'} )}}}}}\Big\{}+\Big(e^{{\frac{\pi\Lambda}{2\kappa^{\prime}}}}\,e^{{{\rm i\,}}\Lambda u}+e^{-{\frac{\pi\Lambda}{2\kappa^{\prime}}}}\,e^{-{{\rm i\,}}\Lambda u}\Big)\,{\cal A}^{\dagger}_{\Lambda,\kappa^{\prime}}\Big\}\,.

(18)

To obtain the transformation, we equate the coefficients of
e^{-{{\rm i\,}}\Lambda u}
and
e^{{{\rm i\,}}\Lambda u}
on both sides of (
18
). Matching the coefficients of
e^{-{{\rm i\,}}\Lambda u}
yields

{\frac{1}{{\sqrt{8\pi\Lambda\,\sinh{(\fft{\pi\Lambda}{\kappa} )}}}}}\left\{e^{{\frac{\pi\Lambda}{2\kappa}}}{\cal A}_{\Lambda,\kappa}+e^{-{\frac{\pi\Lambda}{2\kappa}}}{\cal A}^{\dagger}_{\Lambda,\kappa}\right\}={\frac{1}{{\sqrt{8\pi\Lambda\,\sinh{(\fft{\pi\Lambda}{\kappa'} )}}}}}\left\{e^{{\frac{\pi\Lambda}{2\kappa^{\prime}}}}{\cal A}_{\Lambda,\kappa^{\prime}}+e^{-{\frac{\pi\Lambda}{2\kappa^{\prime}}}}\,{\cal A}^{\dagger}_{\Lambda,\kappa^{\prime}}\right\}\,,

(19)

while comparing the coefficients of
e^{{{\rm i\,}}\Lambda u}
gives

{\frac{1}{{\sqrt{8\pi\Lambda\,\sinh{(\fft{\pi\Lambda}{\kappa} )}}}}}\left\{e^{-{\frac{\pi\Lambda}{2\kappa}}}{\cal A}_{\Lambda,\kappa}+e^{{\frac{\pi\Lambda}{2\kappa}}}{\cal A}^{\dagger}_{\Lambda,\kappa}\right\}={\frac{1}{{\sqrt{8\pi\Lambda\,\sinh{(\fft{\pi\Lambda}{\kappa'} )}}}}}\left\{e^{-{\frac{\pi\Lambda}{2\kappa^{\prime}}}}{\cal A}_{\Lambda,\kappa^{\prime}}+e^{{\frac{\pi\Lambda}{2\kappa^{\prime}}}}\,{\cal A}^{\dagger}_{\Lambda,\kappa^{\prime}}\right\}\,.

(20)

Solving (
19
) and (
20
) simultaneously, we find the Bogoliubov transformation between operators corresponding to different
\kappa
-modes:

{\cal A}_{\Lambda,\kappa^{\prime}}={\frac{1}{\sqrt{{\sinh{(\fft{\pi\Lambda}{\kappa} )}\sinh{(\fft{\pi\Lambda}{\kappa'} )}}}}}\Bigg\{\sinh{\Big(\ft{\pi\Lambda}2\,(\ft 1{\kappa'}+\ft 1{\kappa} \big)}\Big)\,{\cal A}_{\Lambda,\kappa}+\sinh{\Big(\ft{\pi\Lambda}2\,(\ft 1{\kappa'}-\ft 1{\kappa} \big)}\Big)\,{\cal A}^{\dagger}_{\Lambda,\kappa}\Bigg\}\,.

(21)

This expression shows that the annihilation operator
{\cal A}_{\Lambda,\kappa^{\prime}}
includes both annihilation and creation operators from the
\kappa
-basis, indicating that the two sets of modes are related through a nontrivial Bogoliubov transformation. Consequently, the vacua associated with different values of
\kappa
are inequivalent.

3.1.1
Bogoliubov transformation between a kappa plane wave and the Minkowski plane wave

It is straightforward to derive the Bogoliubov transformation between a
\kappa
-plane wave mode and the standard Minkowski plane wave. This can be achieved by taking the limit
\kappa\rightarrow 0
in equation (
21
), which yields

{\cal A}_{\Lambda,\kappa}={\frac{1}{\sqrt{{2\sinh{(\fft{\pi\Lambda}{\kappa} )}}}}}\Big(e^{{\frac{\pi\Lambda}{2\kappa}}}\,a_{\Lambda}-e^{{\frac{-\pi\Lambda}{2\kappa}}}\,a^{\dagger}_{\Lambda}\Big)\,.

(22)

\displaystyle\boxed{\quad\ket{0_\kappa}=\exp\left\{-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}d\nu\,r(\nu)\left(a^{\dagger}_{\nu}a^{\dagger}_{\nu}-a_{\nu}a_{\nu}\right)\right\}\ket{0},\,\,\,\text{with}\,\,\,r(\nu)=\frac{1}{2}\ln\coth\left(\frac{\pi\nu}{2\kappa}\right),\quad}

(57)

which is precisely the form of a continuous-mode squeezed vacuum state.

Continuous squeezing is a well-established concept in quantum optics and continuous-variable quantum information theory, where multimode bosonic fields are manipulated to produce squeezed states with frequency-dependent squeezing parameters
Braunstein2005
;
Weedbrook2012
. Such states, described by Gaussian operations on a continuum of modes, are pivotal in applications ranging from quantum metrology to entanglement generation
ScullyZubairy1997
. In this work, we demonstrate that the
\kappa
-plane wave vacuum
\ket{0_\kappa}
is a continuous-mode squeezed vacuum, characterized by a squeezing parameter
r(\nu)=\frac{1}{2}\ln\coth\left(\frac{\pi\nu}{2\kappa}\right)
. Unlike traditional applications in optical systems, our construction applies continuous squeezing to quantum field theory in flat spacetime, defining a tunable family of vacua that interpolate to the Minkowski vacuum as
\kappa\to 0
. This novel application bridges Gaussian quantum information with relativistic quantum field theory, offering new insights into vacuum structure and observer-dependent effects.
This interpretation places
\ket{0_\kappa}
within the broader family of Gaussian states familiar in quantum optics and continuous-variable quantum information theory
Braunstein2005
;
Weedbrook2012
. The deformation parameter
\kappa
shapes the squeezing spectrum: low-frequency modes exhibit strong squeezing, while high-frequency modes asymptotically resemble those of the Minkowski vacuum. This formalism opens the door to leveraging Gaussian techniques for analyzing entanglement, thermality, and correlations in
\kappa
-vacua.

5.4
Minkowski vacuum in terms of kappa plane vacuum

Let us now express the Minkowski vacuum in terms of the
\kappa
-plane wave vacuum. This can be directly obtained by taking the limit
\kappa^{\prime}\to 0
in (
50
). Using the asymptotic behavior previously discussed, we find

\eta_{\kappa,0^{+},\Lambda}\to-e^{-\frac{\pi\Lambda}{\kappa}}\,.

(58)

Substituting this into the squeezed vacuum condition (
46
) gives the Bogoliubov relation

\left({\cal A}_{\Lambda,\kappa}+e^{-\frac{\pi\Lambda}{\kappa}}\,{\cal A}^{\dagger}_{\Lambda,\kappa}\right)\ket{0_M}=0\,,

(59)

which characterizes the Minkowski vacuum as a squeezed state over
\ket{0_\kappa}
.

Accordingly, the transformation (
50
) becomes

\displaystyle\ket{0_M}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{\widetilde{Z}_{\kappa}}}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}d\Lambda\,e^{-\frac{\pi\Lambda}{\kappa}}\,{\cal A}^{\dagger}_{\Lambda,\kappa}\,{\cal A}^{\dagger}_{\Lambda,\kappa}\right)\ket{0_{\kappa}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\exp\left(\frac{1}{4}\int_{0}^{\infty}d\Lambda\,\ln\left(\coth\left(\frac{\pi\Lambda}{2\kappa}\right)\right)\left({\cal A}_{\Lambda,\kappa}\,{\cal A}_{\Lambda,\kappa}-{\cal A}^{\dagger}_{\Lambda,\kappa}\,{\cal A}^{\dagger}_{\Lambda,\kappa}\right)\right)\ket{0_{\kappa}},

(60)

where
\widetilde{Z}_{\kappa}
is a normalization constant. This confirms that the Minkowski vacuum can be constructed from the
\kappa
-vacuum by a continuous multimode squeezing operation.

6
Conclusion

In this work, we introduced a new class of mode functions in flat spacetime—called
\kappa
-plane wave modes—formed by
\kappa
-dependent linear combinations of positive and negative frequency Minkowski plane waves. These modes define a one-parameter family of vacua
\ket{0_\kappa}
that reduce to the Minkowski vacuum as
\kappa\to 0
, but do not recover the Rindler vacuum in any limit. This contrasts with earlier
\kappa
-Rindler constructions and highlights the
\kappa
-plane wave quantization as a distinct vacuum structure.

A central finding of this paper is that
\ket{0_\kappa}
is uniquely characterized as a continuous-mode squeezed vacuum. We showed that the condition

\left(a_{\nu}-e^{-\frac{\pi\nu}{\kappa}}a^{\dagger}_{\nu}\right)\ket{0_\kappa}=0

yields a squeezing parameter
r(\nu)
via
\tanh r(\nu)=e^{-\frac{\pi\nu}{\kappa}}
, identifying
\ket{0_\kappa}
as a pure Gaussian state with mode-dependent squeezing. This construction generalizes naturally to the continuous-frequency limit and connects the
\kappa
-vacua to well-studied structures in continuous-variable quantum field theory.

We also found that operators with different
\kappa
values obey nontrivial commutation relations, revealing that
\kappa
plays the role of a continuous deformation parameter that governs vacuum entanglement and mode structure.

These insights provide a new lens for viewing vacuum structure in quantum field theory. The
\kappa
-vacua describe a continuum of squeezed Gaussian states that interpolate between pure and thermal-like behavior. This opens up a range of possibilities for applications in analog gravity, detector response theory, and experimental simulations of non-inertial field dynamics.

Appendix B
On the Uniqueness and Motivation of the Kappa-Plane Wave Ansatz

In this appendix, we provide a more detailed justification for the specific form of the
\kappa
-plane wave ansatz. We first address the phases of the mode coefficients and then show that while a general family of solutions to the normalization constraint exists, the chosen ansatz is uniquely required to define a consistent, frequency-independent
\kappa
parameter and to yield the desired physical interpretation of continuous squeezing.

Before proceeding, we consider the possibility of the mode coefficients
\alpha(\Omega)
and
\beta(\Omega)
being complex. While an overall phase can be absorbed by a simple redefinition of the field mode, the
relative phase
between
\alpha(\Omega)
and
\beta(\Omega)
cannot be eliminated and remains physically significant. For this work, we ignore the phase and consider
\alpha(\Omega)
and
\beta(\Omega)
as real and positive numbers.

General Parametrization vs. Specific Ansatz

With the coefficients taken as real and positive, they must satisfy the normalization constraint:

\alpha(\Omega)^{2}-\beta(\Omega)^{2}=\frac{1}{4\pi\Omega}.

(61)

A general solution can be constructed by parameterizing the coefficients using an arbitrary function
\lambda(\Omega)
:

\displaystyle\alpha(\Omega)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{4\pi\Omega}}\cosh\lambda(\Omega),\qquad\beta(\Omega)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{4\pi\Omega}}\sinh\lambda(\Omega).

(62)

This general form satisfies the constraint for any choice of
\lambda(\Omega)
.

The specific ansatz introduced in the main text Eq. (
12
) is:

\displaystyle\alpha(\kappa,\Omega)=\frac{e^{\frac{\pi\Omega}{2\kappa}}}{\sqrt{8\pi\Omega\sinh(\frac{\pi\Omega}{\kappa})}},\qquad\beta(\kappa,\Omega)=\frac{e^{-\frac{\pi\Omega}{2\kappa}}}{\sqrt{8\pi\Omega\sinh(\frac{\pi\Omega}{\kappa})}}.

(63)

Comparing (
62
) and (
63
), we have

\tanh\lambda(\Omega)=e^{-\frac{\pi\Omega}{\kappa}}.

(64)

The parameter
\kappa
is a global constant characterizing the vacuum and must be independent of the mode frequency
\Omega
. Starting from the general ansatz in Eq. (
62
) with an arbitrary function
\lambda(\Omega)
, and using Eq. (
64
) to define
\kappa
, we obtain:

\lambda(\Omega)=\tanh^{-1}\left(e^{-\frac{\pi\Omega}{\kappa}}\right)=\frac{1}{2}\ln\coth\left(\frac{\pi\Omega}{2\kappa}\right),\qquad\kappa(\Omega)=-\frac{\pi\Omega}{\ln\left(\tanh\lambda(\Omega)\right)}.

(65)

For a generic
\lambda(\Omega)
, the resulting
\kappa
depends on
\Omega
, which contradicts the requirement that
\kappa
is a constant deformation parameter. Thus, the ansatz in Eq. (
12
) constrains the choice of
\lambda(\Omega)
.

Physical Motivation: Continuous Squeezing

The choice of ansatz is physically motivated. As shown in the main text, this ansatz characterizes the
\kappa
-vacuum as a continuous-mode squeezed state of the Minkowski vacuum, a result that directly connects to Gaussian quantum information. The ansatz’s exponential coefficients,
e^{\pm\frac{\pi\Lambda}{2\kappa}}
, yield a Bogoliubov transformation linking the
\mathcal{A}_{\Lambda,\kappa}
operators to the Minkowski operators
a_{\Lambda}
, enabling the squeezed state condition
(a_{\nu}-e^{-\frac{\pi\nu}{\kappa}}a_{\nu}^{\dagger})|0_{\kappa}\rangle=0
.

This simple exponential form, inherited from the ansatz, defines the vacuum as a squeezed state with
\kappa
as the tunable squeezing parameter. A different coefficient choice would complicate the transformation, obscuring the elegant squeezed state structure and the physical significance of
\kappa
. Thus, the ansatz is essential for revealing the continuous squeezing structure of the
\kappa
-vacuum.

Appendix C
Gamma Integrals

To evaluate integrals of the form

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\mathrm{d}u\,\theta(u)\,u^{i\Sigma-1}e^{\pm i\Lambda u},

(66)

we use the standard Gamma function identity

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}u^{s-1}e^{-bu}\,\mathrm{d}u=b^{-s}\Gamma(s),

(67)

which holds for
b,s\in\mathbb{C}
with
\real(b)>0
. In our case, we identify
s=i\Sigma
and
b=\pm i\Lambda+\epsilon
, where
0<\epsilon\ll 1
is a small positive regulator ensuring convergence.

To compute
b^{-s}
, we evaluate:

\displaystyle(i\Lambda)^{-i\Sigma}

\displaystyle=\exp\left(-i\Sigma\ln(i \Lambda)\right)=\exp\left(-i\Sigma\left[\ln\Lambda+\frac{i\pi}{2}\right]\right)=\Lambda^{-i\Sigma}e^{\frac{\pi\Sigma}{2}},

\displaystyle(-i\Lambda)^{-i\Sigma}

\displaystyle=\exp\left(-i\Sigma\ln(-i \Lambda)\right)=\exp\left(-i\Sigma\left[\ln\Lambda-\frac{i\pi}{2}\right]\right)=\Lambda^{-i\Sigma}e^{-\frac{\pi\Sigma}{2}}.

(68)

Therefore, the regulated integrals evaluate to

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\mathrm{d}u\,\theta(u)u^{i\Sigma-1}e^{+i\Lambda u}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}u^{i\Sigma-1}e^{+i\Lambda u}\,\mathrm{d}u=\Lambda^{-i\Sigma}e^{-\frac{\pi\Sigma}{2}}\Gamma(i\Sigma),

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\mathrm{d}u\,\theta(u)u^{i\Sigma-1}e^{-i\Lambda u}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}u^{i\Sigma-1}e^{-i\Lambda u}\,\mathrm{d}u=\Lambda^{-i\Sigma}e^{+\frac{\pi\Sigma}{2}}\Gamma(i\Sigma),

(69)

where we assume
\Lambda>0
. Identical results hold for the integrals over
\theta(-u)
.

Appendix D
Two forms: Non-unitary and unitary actions on vacuum

D.1
Single frequency

The goal is to revisit the
\kappa
-plane wave vacuum
\ket{0_\kappa}
and elucidate its structure, especially in connection with the uniqueness of squeezed vacuum states. We begin with the defining relation:

\displaystyle\left(a_{\nu}-e^{-\frac{\pi\nu}{\kappa}}a^{\dagger}_{\nu}\right)\ket{0_\kappa}=0,

(70)

where
a_{\nu}
and
a^{\dagger}_{\nu}
are Minkowski plane wave operators. The general solution to such constraints is given in
Azizi2025Uniqueness
as

\displaystyle\left(a-\alpha a^{\dagger}\right)\ket{\psi_\alpha}

\displaystyle=0\quad\Rightarrow\quad\ket{\psi_\alpha}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{Z}}\,e^{\frac{1}{2}\alpha a^{\dagger 2}}\ket{0}=\exp\left\{\frac{1}{2}\left(\xi a^{\dagger 2}-\xi^{*}a^{2}\right)\right\}\ket{0},

\displaystyle\text{with}\quad\alpha=\tanh r\,e^{i(\theta+\pi)},\quad\xi=re^{i\theta}.

(71)

For real
\alpha
, we have
\theta\in\{0,\pi\}
, which implies
\xi=-\tanh^{-1}\alpha
. Hence,

\displaystyle\left(a-\alpha a^{\dagger}\right)\ket{\psi_\alpha}=0\quad\Rightarrow\quad\ket{\psi_\alpha}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{Z}}\,e^{\frac{1}{2}\alpha a^{\dagger 2}}\ket{0}=\exp\left\{-\frac{1}{2}\tanh^{-1}\alpha\left(a^{\dagger 2}-a^{2}\right)\right\}\ket{0}.

(72)

For
\alpha=e^{-\frac{\pi\Lambda}{\kappa}}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\tanh^{-1}\left(e^{-\frac{\pi\Lambda}{\kappa}}\right)=\frac{1}{2}\ln\left(\coth\left(\frac{\pi\Lambda}{2\kappa}\right)\right)\quad\Rightarrow\quad\xi=-\frac{1}{2}\ln\left(\coth\left(\frac{\pi\Lambda}{2\kappa}\right)\right).

(73)

D.2
Finite frequencies

A key observation is that for the unique
\kappa
-plane wave vacuum
\ket{0_\kappa}
, the constraint

\displaystyle\left(a_{\nu}-e^{-\frac{\pi\nu}{\kappa}}a^{\dagger}_{\nu}\right)\ket{0_\kappa}=0,

(74)

holds for all
\nu\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
. We now generalize the construction to multiple or continuous frequency modes.

First, consider a discrete set of
N
modes with frequencies
\nu_{1},\nu_{2},\ldots,\nu_{N}
. The commutation relations are

\displaystyle[a_{\nu_{i}},a^{\dagger}_{\nu_{j}}]=\delta_{ij},

(75)

and we impose the constraint

\displaystyle\left(a_{\nu_{i}}-e^{-\frac{\pi\nu_{i}}{\kappa}}a^{\dagger}_{\nu_{i}}\right)\ket{0_\kappa}=0\quad\forall i.

(76)

This is satisfied by the squeezed state ansatz

\displaystyle\ket{0_\kappa}

\displaystyle=\exp\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{1}{2}e^{-\frac{\pi\nu_{i}}{\kappa}}a^{\dagger 2}_{\nu_{i}}\right\}\ket{0}

(77)

\displaystyle=\exp\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{-r_{i}}{2}\left(a^{\dagger 2}_{\nu_{i}}-a^{2}_{\nu_{i}}\right)\right\}\ket{0},

(78)

where
r_{i}=\frac{1}{2}\ln\coth\left(\frac{\pi\nu_{i}}{2\kappa}\right)
.
Defining the squeezing operator as

\displaystyle S=\prod_{i=1}^{N}S_{i},\quad S_{i}=\exp\left(\frac{-r_{i}}{2}(a^{\dagger 2}_{\nu_{i}}-a^{2}_{\nu_{i}})\right),

(79)

we verify that each
[a_{\nu_{i}},S_{j}]=0
for
i\neq j
, and

\displaystyle[a_{\nu_{i}},S]=S_{1}\cdots[a_{\nu_{i}},S_{i}]\cdots S_{N}.

(80)

Hence,
\ket{0_\kappa}=S\ket{0}
satisfies the squeezing condition.

D.3
Continuous frequencies

Extending to the continuum limit, we take

\displaystyle[a_{\nu},a^{\dagger}_{\nu^{\prime}}]=\delta(\nu-\nu^{\prime}),

(81)

and postulate the general form

\displaystyle\ket{0_\kappa}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{Z_{\kappa}}}\,\exp\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}d\nu^{\prime}d\nu^{\prime\prime}\,\alpha(\nu^{\prime},\nu^{\prime\prime})\,a^{\dagger}_{\nu^{\prime}}a^{\dagger}_{\nu^{\prime\prime}}\right)\ket{0_M},

(82)

where
\alpha(\nu,\nu^{\prime})
is symmetric.

Computing the commutator yields

\displaystyle\left[a_{\nu},\exp\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}d\nu^{\prime}d\nu^{\prime\prime}\,\alpha(\nu^{\prime},\nu^{\prime\prime})\,a^{\dagger}_{\nu^{\prime}}a^{\dagger}_{\nu^{\prime\prime}}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}d\nu^{\prime}d\nu^{\prime\prime}\,\alpha(\nu^{\prime},\nu^{\prime\prime})\left(a^{\dagger}_{\nu^{\prime}}\,\delta(\nu-\nu^{\prime\prime})+a^{\dagger}_{\nu^{\prime\prime}}\,\delta(\nu-\nu^{\prime})\right)\exp\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}d\nu^{\prime}d\nu^{\prime\prime}\,\alpha(\nu^{\prime},\nu^{\prime\prime})\,a^{\dagger}_{\nu^{\prime}}a^{\dagger}_{\nu^{\prime\prime}}\right)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}d\nu^{\prime}\,2\,\alpha(\nu,\nu^{\prime})\,a^{\dagger}_{\nu^{\prime}}\,\exp\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}d\nu^{\prime}d\nu^{\prime\prime}\,\alpha(\nu^{\prime},\nu^{\prime\prime})\,a^{\dagger}_{\nu^{\prime}}a^{\dagger}_{\nu^{\prime\prime}}\right)\,.

(83)

Choosing

\displaystyle\alpha(\nu,\nu^{\prime})=\frac{1}{2}e^{-\frac{\pi\nu}{\kappa}}\delta(\nu-\nu^{\prime}),

(84)

we obtain

\left[a_{\nu},\exp\left({\textstyle{\frac{\scriptstyle 1}{\scriptstyle 2}}}\int_{0}^{\infty}d\nu^{\prime}\,e^{-\frac{\pi\nu^{\prime}}{\kappa}}\left(a^{\dagger}_{\nu^{\prime}}\right)^{2}\right)\right]=e^{-\frac{\pi\nu}{\kappa}}\,a^{\dagger}_{\nu}\exp\left({\textstyle{\frac{\scriptstyle 1}{\scriptstyle 2}}}\int_{0}^{\infty}d\nu^{\prime}\,e^{-\frac{\pi\nu^{\prime}}{\kappa}}\left(a^{\dagger}_{\nu^{\prime}}\right)^{2}\right)\,.

(85)

Hence, applying
a_{\nu}
to the exponential acting on the Minkowski vacuum yields

\displaystyle a_{\nu}\exp\left({\textstyle{\frac{\scriptstyle 1}{\scriptstyle 2}}}\int_{0}^{\infty}d\nu^{\prime}\,e^{-\frac{\pi\nu^{\prime}}{\kappa}}\left(a^{\dagger}_{\nu^{\prime}}\right)^{2}\right)\ket{0_M}

\displaystyle=\left[a_{\nu},\exp\left({\textstyle{\frac{\scriptstyle 1}{\scriptstyle 2}}}\int_{0}^{\infty}d\nu^{\prime}\,e^{-\frac{\pi\nu^{\prime}}{\kappa}}\left(a^{\dagger}_{\nu^{\prime}}\right)^{2}\right)\right]\ket{0_M}

\displaystyle=e^{-\frac{\pi\nu}{\kappa}}\,a^{\dagger}_{\nu}\exp\left({\textstyle{\frac{\scriptstyle 1}{\scriptstyle 2}}}\int_{0}^{\infty}d\nu^{\prime}\,e^{-\frac{\pi\nu^{\prime}}{\kappa}}\left(a^{\dagger}_{\nu^{\prime}}\right)^{2}\right)\ket{0_M}\,.

(86)

Comparing equation (
86

74
), we find that the
\kappa
-plane wave vacuum is given by

\ket{0_{\kappa}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{Z_{\kappa}}}\exp\left({\textstyle{\frac{\scriptstyle 1}{\scriptstyle 2}}}\int_{0}^{\infty}d\nu\,e^{-\frac{\pi\nu}{\kappa}}\left(a^{\dagger}_{\nu}\right)^{2}\right)\ket{0_M}\,,

(87)

where
Z_{\kappa}
is the normalization constant.

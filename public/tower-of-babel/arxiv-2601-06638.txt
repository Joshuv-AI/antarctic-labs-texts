A Conservative Log-Size Master Equation for Fragmentation PBEs: Jump Transport, Drift–Diffusion Asymptotics, and PSD Inference
J. J. Segura

Abstract
Fragmentation population-balance equations (PBEs) describe how particle size distributions (PSDs) evolve under breakage and daughter fragment redistribution. From a standard self-similar fragmentation class we derive an exact conservative transport equation in log-size for the normalized mass fraction : a state-dependent pure-jump master equation (nonlocal internal-coordinate mass transfer). We also give an explicit Gorini–Kossakowski–Sudarshan–Lindblad (GKSL) factorization whose diagonal sector reproduces this master equation, used here as an optional structure-preserving operator representation and constrained parameterization for inverse modeling (rather than a computational necessity). In a controlled small-jump regime, the nonlocal jump transport reduces to a drift–diffusion (Fokker–Planck) operator in log-size space. Under detailed-balance conditions this operator admits the standard symmetrization to a self-adjoint Schrödinger-type spectral problem, enabling compact parametric hypothesis classes for PSD shapes. We then present two inverse routes: (i) time-resolved parametric fitting of transport/spectral parameters, and (ii) a regularized steady-state inversion that reconstructs an effective potential from a measured steady PSD. To address practical validation, we include numerical benchmarks: forward simulation of the jump transport model (CTMC discretization) and its drift–diffusion reduction, quantitative discrepancy metrics, and inverse parameter recovery on an Airy half-line synthetic benchmark under controlled multiplicative noise.

Introduction

Fragmentation and comminution evolve an ensemble of particles through an
internal coordinate
(size, mass, or a proxy), and the particle size distribution (PSD) is a central state variable for design, control, and optimization of grinding and classification circuits. In pure-breakage PBEs, material is removed from a parent size at a selection rate and redistributed nonlocally to smaller sizes through a daughter kernel. This combination is naturally interpreted as
transport in internal-coordinate space
: a conservative gain–loss (jump) generator in which nonnegativity and mass conservation are structural.

Classical fragmentation equations and PBEs have a long history, including self-similar solutions for homogeneous kernels and widely used empirical PSD families such as Rosin–Rammler/Weibull, log-normal, log-logistic, and Swebrec-type forms
[
10
,
6
,
7
,
12
,
5
]
. The objective of this article is to organize a
transport-first
formulation in log-size space that is explicit about (i) conservative normalization, (ii) the relation between nonlocal jump transport and drift–diffusion reductions, and (iii) practically implementable inverse routes.

A secondary objective is to show that a GKSL/Lindblad form can be used as an
optional
operator factorization of the classical Markov generator. We do
not
claim GKSL is required to simulate PBEs—standard CTMC and finite-volume discretizations already preserve positivity and conservation. Rather, the GKSL factorization is useful as (i) a constrained parameterization for inverse problems (nonnegativity is built in through squared amplitudes) and (ii) a compact semigroup/spectral language that connects jump transport, diffusion limits, and reduced spectral models.

Potential application domains include mineral comminution and classification circuits (where PSD evolution is central for control), pharmaceutical milling (PSD-driven product performance), and other particulate fragmentation settings where transport in internal-coordinate space is the natural modeling language.

Fragmentation transport model and the log-size jump process

4.1
Number density, mass density, and the mass-weighted kernel

Let
x>0
denote a size-like variable. For definiteness, take
x
proportional to particle mass (or any conserved extensive variable). A standard fragmentation equation for the
number density

n(x,t)
is

\partial_{t}n(x,t)=-S(x)n(x,t)+\int_{x}^{\infty}b_{n}(x,y)\,S(y)n(y,t)\,\mathrm{d}y,

(1)

where
S(x)
is the selection (breakage) rate and
b_{n}(x,y)
is the daughter
number
density. Mass conservation requires

\int_{0}^{y}x\,b_{n}(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}x=y.

(2)

Define the
mass density

m(x,t)=x\,n(x,t)
. Then (
1
) implies

\partial_{t}m(x,t)=-S(x)m(x,t)+\int_{x}^{\infty}b_{m}(x,y)\,S(y)m(y,t)\,\mathrm{d}y,

(3)

where the
mass-weighted
daughter kernel is

b_{m}(x,y)=\frac{x}{y}\,b_{n}(x,y),\qquad\int_{0}^{y}b_{m}(x,y)\mathrm{d}x=1.

(4)

Thus
b_{m}
redistributes the parent’s mass over daughter sizes; it is the natural kernel for mass-fraction transport. Let
M=\int_{0}^{\infty}m(x,t)\mathrm{d}x
be the total mass; then
\mathrm{d}M/\mathrm{d}t=0
under (
3
) and (
4
).

4.2
Self-similar (homogeneous) kernel

Assume the widely used self-similar structure

S(x)=k\,x^{\alpha},\qquad b_{m}(x,y)=\frac{1}{y}\,B\!\left(\frac{x}{y}\right),\qquad 0<x<y,

(5)

where
k>0
,
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
, and
B(z)
is supported on
z\in(0,1)
with normalization

\int_{0}^{1}B(z)\,\mathrm{d}z=1.

(6)

4.3
Log-size transform and an exact conservative master equation

Let
\xi=\ln(x/x_{0})
so that
x=x_{0}\mathrm{e}^{\xi}
and
\mathrm{d}x=x\,\mathrm{d}\xi
.
Define the normalized
mass fraction density in log-space

p(\xi,t)=\frac{m(x,t)}{M}\,x,\qquad\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}p(\xi,t)\,\mathrm{d}\xi=1.

(7)

Equivalently,
p(\xi,t)\mathrm{d}\xi
is the mass fraction in the log-bin
(\xi,\xi+\mathrm{d}\xi)
.

A direct change of variables in (
3
)–(
5
) yields the
exact
gain–loss form

\partial_{t}p(\xi,t)=-\lambda(\xi)\,p(\xi,t)+\int_{0}^{\infty}\lambda(\xi+u)\,K(u)\,p(\xi+u,t)\,\mathrm{d}u,

(8)

where

\lambda(\xi)=S(x_{0}\mathrm{e}^{\xi})=k\,x_{0}^{\alpha}\,\mathrm{e}^{\alpha\xi},\qquad K(u)=\mathrm{e}^{-u}\,B(\mathrm{e}^{-u}),\quad u\geq 0.

(9)

Using (
6
) one checks

\int_{0}^{\infty}K(u)\,\mathrm{d}u=1,

(10)

so (
8
) preserves
\int p\,\mathrm{d}\xi
exactly. Equation (
8
) is therefore a conservative, state-dependent
one-sided jump transport
equation in log-size space.

5
Optional operator factorization: a GKSL/Lindblad dilation of the jump generator

Role of the GKSL/Lindblad form.

The classical master equation (
8
) is already a conservative Markov transport model and can be simulated directly with CTMC or finite-volume discretizations. We therefore do
not
present GKSL/Lindblad notation as a computational requirement. Its role here is as an
optional factorization
that (i) provides a constrained parameterization for inverse modeling (nonnegativity built in through squared amplitudes) and (ii) connects jump transport, diffusion limits, and spectral reductions within a semigroup framework.

5.1
A GKSL construction reproducing the master equation on the diagonal

Consider
\mathcal{H}=L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
with generalized position basis
\{|\xi\rangle\}
. Let
\rho(t)
be a positive trace-class operator and define the diagonal

p(\xi,t)=\langle\xi|\rho(t)|\xi\rangle.

(11)

(Here
\langle\xi|\rho(t)|\xi\rangle
denotes the diagonal element of the operator
\rho(t)
in the
\xi
representation, i.e.
p(\xi,t)=\rho(\xi,\xi,t)
.)

Define a nonlocal jump operator
L
by its kernel

(L\psi)(\xi)=\int_{\xi}^{\infty}\sqrt{\lambda(\eta)}\,\sqrt{K(\eta-\xi)}\,\psi(\eta)\,\mathrm{d}\eta.

(12)

Now consider the GKSL (Lindblad) equation

\frac{\mathrm{d}\rho}{\mathrm{d}t}=-i[H_{0},\rho]+\mathcal{D}[\rho],\qquad\mathcal{D}[\rho]=L\rho L^{\dagger}-\tfrac{1}{2}\{L^{\dagger}L,\rho\},

(13)

with any Hermitian
H_{0}
(set
H_{0}=0
for a purely dissipative factorization).

Claim (Diagonal reproduction).

If
\rho(t)
is diagonal in the
|\xi\rangle
basis, i.e.
\rho(\eta,\eta^{\prime})=p(\eta,t)\delta(\eta-\eta^{\prime})
, then
p(\xi,t)
satisfies the classical master equation (
8
).

Sketch.
Under diagonality,

\langle\xi|L\rho L^{\dagger}|\xi\rangle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\lambda(\xi+u)K(u)p(\xi+u,t)\mathrm{d}u
,
and
(L^{\dagger}L)(\xi,\xi)=\lambda(\xi)
using (
10
).
\square

Practical payoff for inverse modeling.

A common failure mode in kernel learning is violating nonnegativity or normalization. The factorization in (
12
) builds these constraints in through the square-root structure
\sqrt{\lambda}\sqrt{K}
, providing a convenient constrained parameterization for inverse problems without requiring ad hoc projection steps.

6
Diffusive (small-jump) limit: drift–diffusion transport in log-size

When
K(u)
concentrates near
u=0
, (
8
) admits a controlled Kramers–Moyal expansion. Define moments

m_{n}=\int_{0}^{\infty}u^{n}K(u)\,\mathrm{d}u,\qquad m_{0}=1,

(14)

and assume
m_{1},m_{2}<\infty
and higher moments are sufficiently small.
Expanding
\lambda(\xi+u)p(\xi+u,t)
and using
m_{0}=1
cancels the loss exactly, yielding at second order

\partial_{t}p(\xi,t)=m_{1}\,\partial_{\xi}\big(\lambda p\big)+\frac{m_{2}}{2}\,\partial_{\xi\xi}\big(\lambda p\big)+\mathcal{O}(m_{3}).

(15)

This can be written in conservative Fokker–Planck form

\partial_{t}p=-\partial_{\xi}\big(A(\xi)p\big)+\partial_{\xi\xi}\big(D(\xi)p\big),\qquad A(\xi)=-m_{1}\lambda(\xi),\quad D(\xi)=\frac{m_{2}}{2}\lambda(\xi).

(16)

7
Spectral form (detailed balance case) and PSD parametrizations

If the FP operator admits a zero-current steady state (detailed balance), there exists
\Phi(\xi)
such that

A(\xi)=2D(\xi)\,\Phi^{\prime}(\xi).

(17)

Then the similarity transform

p(\xi,t)=\mathrm{e}^{-\Phi(\xi)}\,\psi(\xi,t)

(18)

removes the first-derivative term and yields an imaginary-time Schrödinger form (standard; see
[
9
]
)

\partial_{t}\psi=D(\xi)\,\partial_{\xi\xi}\psi-U_{\mathrm{eff}}(\xi)\,\psi.

(19)

In this detailed-balance case, the stationary density satisfies

p_{\mathrm{ss}}(\xi)\propto\mathrm{e}^{-\Phi(\xi)}=\phi_{0}(\xi)^{2},

(20)

where
\phi_{0}
is the ground state of the symmetrized operator. Non-self-adjoint/open steady states are briefly discussed in Appendix
B
.

Inverse modeling routes

9.1
Route I: time-resolved parametric fitting

Choose a parametric family for the transport generator, e.g. via
\Phi(\xi;\theta)
(detailed balance FP) or via a constrained kernel family
K(u;\theta)
for the jump model. Fit
\theta
to time-resolved PSD snapshots
\tilde{p}(\xi,t_{j})
by minimizing a discrepancy, e.g. weighted least squares or a discretized KL divergence, subject to normalization/positivity constraints. The optional factorization in Sec.
5
provides a convenient constrained parameterization for jump kernels and rates.

9.2
Route II: steady inversion with explicit regularization

Assume a steady shape is observed and the detailed-balance reduction is adequate so
p_{\mathrm{ss}}(\xi)\propto\phi_{0}(\xi)^{2}
with
\phi_{0}>0
. For a constant-mass Schrödinger form

-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2\mu}\phi_{0}^{\prime\prime}(\xi)+V(\xi)\phi_{0}(\xi)=E_{0}\phi_{0}(\xi),

In the fragmentation context, the ratio
\hbar^{2}/2\mu
plays the role of an effective diffusivity scale in the symmetrized reduction, and may be identified with
D(\xi)
in Eq. (
16
) (or with a constant
D_{0}
after a Lamperti/Liouville transform); in turn
D(\xi)=(m_{2}/2)\lambda(\xi)
relates it back to the jump kernel moments and rate via Eqs. (
14
) and (
16
).
one can reconstruct

V(\xi)=E_{0}+\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2\mu}\frac{\phi_{0}^{\prime\prime}(\xi)}{\phi_{0}(\xi)}=E_{0}+\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2\mu}\left[\frac{p_{\mathrm{ss}}^{\prime\prime}}{2p_{\mathrm{ss}}}-\frac{(p_{\mathrm{ss}}^{\prime})^{2}}{4p_{\mathrm{ss}}^{2}}\right].

(21)

Because derivatives amplify noise, we recommend smoothing
p_{\mathrm{ss}}
by a curvature-penalized (Tikhonov) estimate

p^{\star}=\arg\min_{p}\ \|p-\tilde{p}\|_{2}^{2}+\lambda\|p^{\prime\prime}\|_{2}^{2},

with
\lambda
chosen by an L-curve or generalized cross-validation; Sec.
10
reports noise-sweep behavior for a synthetic benchmark.

Computational notes.

The one-sided jump structure yields a sparse/triangular gain operator in discretized form, and CTMC evolution can be performed stably with implicit stepping, uniformization, or matrix-free exponential methods. The FP reduction can be evolved with conservative flux discretizations. The GKSL factorization in Sec.
5
is not needed for forward evolution and is used primarily for constrained inverse parameterizations.

Worked synthetic example: a well-posed Airy-type half-line model

The Airy ansatz must be posed in a square-integrable setting: on
\mathbb{R}
,
\mathrm{Ai}
oscillates as
\xi\to-\infty
. A clean fix is to work on a half-line with cutoff
\xi_{\min}
(minimum resolved size) and a confining potential.

11.1
Forward model

Let
\xi\in[\xi_{\min},\infty)
and consider the self-adjoint Hamiltonian

H=-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2\mu}\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}}{\mathrm{d}\xi^{2}}+F(\xi-\xi_{\min}),\qquad F>0,

(22)

with Dirichlet boundary
\phi(\xi_{\min})=0
.
Eigenfunctions are shifted Airy functions

\phi_{n}(\xi)=\mathcal{N}_{n}\,\mathrm{Ai}\!\Big(a(\xi-\xi_{\min})-a_{n}\Big),\quad a=\left(\frac{2\mu F}{\hbar^{2}}\right)^{1/3},

where
a_{n}>0
are the zeros of
\mathrm{Ai}(-a_{n})=0
. The ground state is positive for
\xi>\xi_{\min}
and yields a valid PSD:

p(\xi)=\phi_{0}(\xi)^{2},\qquad\int_{\xi_{\min}}^{\infty}p(\xi)\,\mathrm{d}\xi=1.

(23)

11.2
Synthetic data generation and inverse fit

Synthetic steady PSDs are generated from (
23
), broadened (optional) and contaminated with multiplicative noise, then renormalized. Parameters
(F,\xi_{\min})
are fitted by minimizing a discrepancy between predicted and observed binned densities; the resulting noise sweep is reported in Sec.
10
.

12
Limitations and validation targets

The scope is restricted to pure breakage (no aggregation, growth, classification), self-similar kernels, and diffusion reduction only when jump lengths are small in log-space. Industrial steady operation often includes feed/discharge and classification; these open-system effects can be incorporated as source/sink terms or boundary fluxes, but are outside the present scope. The numerical validation included here targets: (a) forward evolution accuracy and stability, (b) reduction accuracy of drift–diffusion, and (c) inverse identifiability under noise on a controlled benchmark.

13
Conclusions

We formulated pure fragmentation as conservative transport in log-size space, yielding an exact one-sided jump master equation for normalized mass fraction. We presented an optional GKSL/Lindblad factorization of the classical generator as a constrained parameterization for inverse modeling and a compact semigroup language, without claiming it is necessary for forward simulation. Under a small-jump regime we obtained a drift–diffusion reduction and a standard detailed-balance symmetrization that motivates explicit low-dimensional spectral hypothesis classes for PSD shapes. Finally, we provided numerical validation: forward jump vs. drift–diffusion comparisons and inverse parameter recovery under noise on a well-posed synthetic benchmark.

Appendix B
Appendix B: Non-self-adjoint caveat for open steady states

In open fragmentation settings (feed in, product out, or nonzero size-space flux), reduced operators can be non-self-adjoint and left/right modes may be needed. This appendix records the caveat: in such cases steady densities need not be representable as a single modulus square; rather, left/right eigenmodes may be required in non-self-adjoint spectral reductions. (A full open-system comminution model with classification is outside the present scope.)

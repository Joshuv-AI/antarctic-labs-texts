Poster 164. Translating Force Plate Data: Linking Countermovement Jump Metrics to Traditional Performance Measures in Youth Female Soccer Athletes
Authors: Bradley S Lambert, Samiha Momin, Ana Rivera, Haley Goble, Roxanne Miller, Shari Liberman
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjective:Countermovement jump (CMJ) testing with portable force plate systems is a valuable method in sports science and rehabilitation for evaluating lower extremity power, neuromuscular function, and asymmetries. Its portability, ease of use, and ability to produce detailed kinetic and kinematic data have increased its use in clinical and field environments. CMJ testing provides insights into performance across eccentric, concentric, and landing phases, offering a comprehensive view of neuromuscular efficiency and injury risk. However, a gap remains in understanding how CMJ-derived metrics (e.g., peak force, rate of force development-RFD, and asymmetry indices) relate to traditional field-based assessments used by coaches and clinicians. This is especially important in youth sports, where developmental variability and injury risk are high. Female youth soccer athletes are particularly prone to lower extremity injuries due to neuromuscular imbalances and biomechanical factors. Clarifying the link between CMJ outputs and conventional performance tests could improve CMJ testing's utility, helping practitioners interpret force plate data in familiar contexts and guide training, rehabilitation, and return-to-play decisions. The objective of this research was to examine the relationship between CMJ metrics obtained via a portable force plate system and traditional sport performance assessments (including strength, agility, and power) in elite youth female soccer athletes.Methods:This cross-sectional study was conducted at a single hospital institution and approved by the Institutional Review Board for human subjects research. Parental consent and participant assent were obtained for all athletes. Seventy-four elite female soccer athletes (ages 8-11, mean age 10±1) from a competitive youth club completed a battery of traditional performance assessments: 20-yard pro-agility shuttle (agility); Y-balance test (dynamic balance); Isometric knee flexion/extension strength via handheld dynamometry (hamstring/quadriceps strength); Single-leg and triple hop tests (horizontal power and functional performance)CMJ testing was performed using the ForceDecks Lite system, capturing force-time metrics across eccentric, concentric, and landing phases. Athletes performed three maximal CMJs with natural arm swing. Metrics included mean and peak force, mean and peak power, RFD, peak velocity, jump height, and asymmetry indices. Pearson correlation coefficients were calculated to assess relationships between CMJ and traditional performance measures (α = 0.05).Results:Descriptive statistics for all traditional performance assessments and CMJ metrics are presented in Tables 1 and 2. Correlation analyses between CMJ outputs and traditional performance tests are summarized in Table 3.Pro-Agility (20-Yard Shuttle): Several CMJ variables negatively correlated with pro-agility performance, including concentric peak velocity (r = -0.570), jump height (r = -0.535), and peak power relative to body mass (r = -0.556), indicating that greater explosive power and velocity were linked to faster shuttle times. Moderate correlations were found for concentric mean power (r = -0.346), mean power relative to body mass (r = -0.439), and peak power (r = -0.41). These results suggest CMJ-derived power, velocity, and RFD are associated with agility in youth female soccer athletes. Knee Flexion and Extension Strength: CMJ metrics correlated significantly with hamstring and quadriceps strength. For knee flexion, strong correlations were seen with concentric mean force (r = 0.506) and eccentric mean force (r = 0.53); moderate ones with concentric mean power (r = 0.475), RFD (r = 0.329), and peak power (r = 0.441). For knee extension, moderate correlations were found with concentric mean force (r = 0.458), eccentric mean force (r = 0.444), mean power (r = 0.45), RFD (r = 0.334), and peak power (r = 0.453). These findings indicate CMJ force and power outputs reflect aspects of isometric lower limb strength. Hop Testing (Single and Triple Hop): CMJ performance was significantly associated with hop distances. For single-leg hop, weak but significant correlations were found with concentric mean power (r = 0.246) and peak power (r = 0.253). For triple hop, correlations were observed with mean power (r = 0.252), jump height (r = 0.234), and peak power (r = 0.259), suggesting CMJ power metrics may indicate horizontal power and functional performance. Y-Balance Testing: No significant correlations were found between CMJ metrics and Y-balance scores in any direction, suggesting dynamic balance may be independent of vertical jump performance and force-time characteristics in this group.Conclusions:CMJ metrics, particularly from the concentric phase, are significantly associated with traditional measures of strength, agility, and power in elite youth female soccer athletes. These findings support CMJ testing as a practical, efficient tool for performance monitoring and neuromuscular profiling in youth sports. CMJ testing offers a time-efficient, objective alternative to traditional assessments, enabling practitioners to collect meaningful performance data with minimal setup. Its strong associations with agility, strength, and hop performance support its use in athlete monitoring, training progression, and return-to-play decisions. The lack of correlation with balance testing highlights the need for a multifaceted evaluation approach. Future longitudinal studies should explore the predictive value of CMJ metrics for performance and injury risk, and their integration into broader athlete development and rehabilitation strategies.

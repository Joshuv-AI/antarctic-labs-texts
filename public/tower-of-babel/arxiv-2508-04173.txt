Optimal decay constant for complete manifolds of positive scalar curvature with quadratic decay
Shuli Chen

Abstract
. We prove that if an orientable 3-manifold M M admits a complete Riemannian metric whose scalar curvature is positive and has at most C C -quadratic decay at infinity for some C > 2 3 C>\frac{2}{3} , then it decomposes as a (possibly infinite) connected sum of spherical manifolds and 𝕊 2 × 𝕊 1 \mathbb{S}^{2}\times\mathbb{S}^{1} summands. Consequently, M M carries a complete Riemannian metric of uniformly positive scalar curvature. The decay constant 2 3 \frac{2}{3} is sharp, as demonstrated by metrics on ℝ 2 × 𝕊 1 \mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{S}^{1} . This improves a result of Balacheff, Gil Moreno de Mora Sardà, and Sabourau, and partially answers a conjecture of Gromov. The main tool is a new exhaustion result using μ \mu -bubbles. In dimensions n = 4 , 5 n=4,5 , we further extend results of Chodosh–Maximo–Mukherjee and Sweeney, and obtain topological obstructions to the existence of a complete Riemannian metric whose scalar curvature is positive and has at most C C -quadratic decay at infinity for some C > n − 1 n C>\frac{n-1}{n} on certain noncompact contractible n n -manifolds.

1.
Introduction

The scalar curvature
R
, defined as the average of the sectional curvatures up to a multiplicative constant, is the weakest curvature invariant of a Riemannian metric.
On the other hand, the existence of positive scalar curvature can still place strong topological constraints on the underlying topology.
In the study of three-dimensional manifolds, a fundamental question is about understanding the topological structure of 3-manifolds that admit a Riemannian metric of positive scalar curvature
R>0
.

In the closed case, the classical Kneser–Milnor prime decomposition theorem
[
18
,
21
]
combined with Perelman’s resolution of the Poincaré conjecture
[
22
,
24
,
23
]
implies that every closed orientable
3
-manifold
M
can be uniquely decomposed into prime factors as

M=X_{1}\#\dots X_{\ell}\#(\mathbb{S}^{2}\times\mathbb{S}^{1})\#\dots\#(\mathbb{S}^{2}\times\mathbb{S}^{1})\#K_{1}\#\dots\#K_{m},

where each
X_{i}
is a spherical manifold and each
K_{k}
is an aspherical manifold. Recall that a closed 3-manifold
M
is
prime
if
M=M^{\prime}\#M^{\prime\prime}
implies that either
M^{\prime}
or
M^{\prime\prime}
is diffeomorphic to
\mathbb{S}^{3}
. Here, a closed 3-manifold is
spherical
if it is of the form
\mathbb{S}^{3}/\Gamma_{i}
obtained as the quotient of the 3-sphere by a subgroup
\Gamma_{i}<O(4)
of isometries acting freely on
\mathbb{S}^{3}
, and a manifold is
aspherical
if it has contractible universal cover.
Perelman
[
22
,
24
,
23
]
, completing pioneering work of Schoen and Yau
[
26
]
and Gromov and Lawson
[
14
,
13
,
15
]
, showed that a closed orientable 3-manifold admits a Riemannian metric with positive scalar curvature if and only if it decomposes as a connected sum of only spherical manifolds and
\mathbb{S}^{2}\times\mathbb{S}^{1}
summands.

In contrast, for open 3-manifolds, the topological structure is much more complicated, and naive generalizations of Kneser–Milnor prime decomposition do not hold in general
[
28
,
19
]
. Nevertheless, if we strengthen the curvature condition from positive scalar curvature
R>0
to uniformly positive scalar curvature
R\geq 1
, then a similar decomposition theorem has recently been proved for open 3-manifolds admitting complete Riemannian metrics of uniformly positive scalar curvature. Namely, such manifolds decompose as a possibly infinite connected sum of spherical manifolds and
\mathbb{S}^{2}\times\mathbb{S}^{1}
summands. In this direction, Chang, Weinberger, and Yu
[
4
]
proved the decomposition for manifolds with finitely generated fundamental group using K-theory methods. Bessières, Besson, and Maillot
[
2
]
showed the result for manifolds with bounded geometry via Ricci flow.
Finally, Gromov
[
17
]
and Wang

[
30
]
independently established the general case, using
\mu
-bubble technique introduced by Gromov
[
12
]
.

One may wish to consider a weaker scalar curvature assumption and ask for a decomposition theorem. In
[
1
]
, Balacheff, Gil Moreno de Mora Sardà, and Sabourau extended the decomposition theorem to complete Riemannian 3-manifolds of positive scalar curvature with at most
C
-quadratic decay at infinity for some constant
C>64\pi^{2}
. Their proof is deduced from results on fill radius instead of via
\mu
-bubbles.

Definition 1.1

(
[
1
, Definition 1.2]
[
10
, Definition 1.3.5]
)
.

Let
M
be a complete Riemannian
n
-dimensional manifold. Fix a basepoint
x\in M
, and denote by
r_{x}(y)=d(x,y)
the distance function to
x
.
The scalar curvature
R
of
M
has
at most
C
-quadratic decay at infinity
if there exists a constant
D_{0}>0
such that for every

with
r_{x}(y)\geq D_{0}
,

R(y)>\frac{C}{r_{x}(y)^{2}}.

Remark 1.2
.

The constant

in Definition

may depend on the basepoint

.

1

However, by replacing

arbitrarily close to

. Since in this paper we are only interested in conditions of the form “for some

” or “for every

” where

Theorem 1.3

(
[

, Theorem 1.3]
)

Let

Then

This theorem is closely related to the following conjecture of Gromov

(Critical Rate of Decay Conjecture
[

]
)

There exists a universal critical constant

such that the following holds. Let

be an orientable
n

(1)

For every

In this paper, we improve the decay constant in Theorem

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
M
be a complete connected orientable Riemannian 3-manifold. Suppose that
M
has positive scalar curvature with at most

-quadratic decay at infinity for some

Then

decomposes as a possibly infinite connected sum of spherical manifolds and

The constant

\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{S}^{1}
admits a complete metric of positive scalar curvature with at most

Note that
\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{S}^{1}

The following rigidity result, which addresses the case (

Corollary 1.6
.

Let
M
be an orientable 3-manifold. If

admits a complete Riemannian metric of positive scalar curvature with at most

-quadratic decay at infinity for some

C_{3}=\frac{2}{3}

Unlike
[

For every

, the

2.
Proof of Proposition
1.7

In this section, we construct the desired metric in Proposition
1.7
. Let
n\geq 3
. Let
c>0
and
0<r_{0}<\frac{\pi}{2}
be constants to be chosen later.

For
\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{T}^{n-2}
, we parametrize
\mathbb{R}^{2}
by polar coordinate
(r,\theta)
and let
g_{\mathbb{T}^{n-2}}
be a flat metric on
\mathbb{T}^{n-2}
.
We will construct the desired metric on
\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{T}^{n-2}
by gluing together two positive scalar curvature metrics. We fix a basepoint
x
which has
r
-coordinate equals
2r_{0}
.

For
r\geq r_{0}
, we consider a metric of the form
g_{+}=dr^{2}+c^{2}r^{2\alpha}d\theta^{2}+c^{2}r^{2\alpha}g_{\mathbb{T}^{n-2}}
on the end.
Then the scalar curvature of this warped product metric with warping function
f(r)=cr^{\alpha}
is given by (see e.g.
[
15
, p. 157]
)

\displaystyle R

\displaystyle=-\frac{(n-1)(n-2)(f^{\prime})^{2}}{f}-\frac{2(n-1)f^{\prime\prime}}{f}

\displaystyle=-\frac{(n-1)(n-2)\alpha^{2}}{r^{2}}-\frac{2(n-1)\alpha(\alpha-1)}{r^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{-n(n-1)\alpha^{2}+2(n-1)\alpha}{r^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{r^{2}}\left(-n(n-1)\left(\alpha-\frac{1}{n}\right)^{2}+\frac{n-1}{n}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{n-1}{n}\frac{1}{r^{2}}.

Thus on
[r_{0},+\infty)\times\mathbb{S}^{1}\times\mathbb{T}^{n-2}
, we can get positive scalar curvature with at most
C
-quadratic decay at infinity for the metric for any
0<C<\frac{n-1}{n}
by choosing corresponding
\alpha\in(0,\frac{1}{n}]
.

Under this metric, the mean curvature of the slice
\{r_{0}\}\times\mathbb{S}^{1}\times\mathbb{T}^{n-2}
with respect to
\partial r
is given by

H_{+}=\frac{(n-1)f^{\prime}(r_{0})}{f(r_{0})}=\frac{(n-1)\alpha}{r_{0}}\leq\frac{(n-1)}{nr_{0}}.

For
r\leq 2r_{0}
, we consider the metric
g_{-}=dr^{2}+\sin^{2}(r)d\theta^{2}+\sin^{2}(r_{0})g_{\mathbb{T}^{n-2}}
. Since
dr^{2}+\sin^{2}(r)d\theta^{2}
is the canonical metric on
\mathbb{S}^{2}
,
g_{-}
is smooth at
r=0
, and the scalar curvature is
2
.
The mean curvature of the slice
\{r_{0}\}\times\mathbb{S}^{1}\times\mathbb{T}^{n-2}
with respect to
\partial r
is given by

H_{-}=\frac{\cos(r_{0})}{\sin(r_{0})}.

Notice that for
r_{0}
sufficiently small, we have
H_{-}>H_{+}
. Thus we choose
r_{0}
sufficiently small and let
c=\dfrac{\sin r_{0}}{r_{0}^{\alpha}}
.

Then the two metrics
g_{+}
and
g_{-}
agree at
r=r_{0}
, and we have
H_{-}>H_{+}
. Since
H_{-}>H_{+}
, if we glue together
g_{-}
and
g_{+}
, the scalar curvature is positive in the distributional sense (see
[
20
, Section 2]
), so we expect to be able to smooth out the singularity at
r_{0}
.

Indeed, consider the manifold with boundary
[r_{0},+\infty)\times\mathbb{S}^{1}\times\mathbb{T}^{n-2}
. By Theorem 5 of
[
3
]
(which is applicable here because the construction is purely local; also note that they use the opposite sign convention for mean curvature), since
H_{-}>H_{+}
, we can find a positive scalar curvature metric
\hat{g}
on
[r_{0},+\infty)\times\mathbb{S}^{1}\times\mathbb{T}^{n-2}
such that
\hat{g}
agrees with
g_{+}
for
r\geq 2r_{0}
, and
\hat{g}
agrees with
g_{-}
in a neighborhood of
\{r_{0}\}\times\mathbb{S}^{1}\times\mathbb{T}^{n-2}
.

Then we can extend
\hat{g}
to a smooth metric on the whole
\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{T}^{n-2}
by gluing with
g_{-}
for
r\leq r_{0}
. The metric
\hat{g}
is the desired complete metric with positive scalar curvature that has at most
C
-quadratic decay for the given
0<C<\frac{n-1}{n}
with respect to basepoint
x
.

3.
Exhaustion via
\mu
-bubbles

We first recall general existence and stability results for
\mu
-bubbles.

For
3\leq n\leq 7
, consider
(M,g)
, a Riemannian
n
-manifold with boundary. Assume that
\partial M=\partial_{-}M\cup\partial_{+}M
is a labeling of the boundary components so that each of the sets
\partial_{\pm}M
is non-empty. Fix a smooth function
h
on
\mathring{M}
with
h\to\pm\infty
on
\partial_{\pm}M
.
Choose a Caccioppoli set
\Omega_{0}
with smooth boundary such that
\Omega_{0}
contains an open neighborhood of
\partial_{+}M
and is disjoint from
\partial_{-}M
.

For all Caccioppoli sets
\Omega
in
M
with
\Omega\Delta\Omega_{0}\Subset\mathring{M}
, we consider the following
\mu
-bubble functional

\mathcal{A}(\Omega)=\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\partial^{*}\Omega\cap\mathring{M})-\int_{M}(\chi_{\Omega}-\chi_{\Omega_{0}})h\,d\mathcal{H}^{n},

(3.1)

where
\partial^{*}\Omega
denotes the reduced boundary of
\Omega
. We will call a Caccioppoli set
\Omega
minimizing
\mathcal{A}
in this class a
\mu
-bubble
.

The next proposition shows
\mu
-bubbles are
easier to construct than stable minimal hypersurfaces and enjoy the same regularity.

Proposition 3.1

(
[
31
, Proposition 2.1]
[
7
, Proposition 12]
)
.

For
3\leq n\leq 7
, there exists a smooth minimizer
\Omega
for
\mathcal{A}
such that
\Omega\Delta\Omega_{0}
is compactly contained in the interior of
M
.

We next discuss the first and second variation for a
\mu
-bubble.

Lemma 3.2

(
[
7
, Lemma 13]
)
.

If
\Omega_{t}
is a smooth
1
-parameter family of regions with
\Omega_{0}=\Omega
and normal speed
\psi
at
t=0
, then

\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{A}(\Omega_{t})=\int_{\Sigma_{t}}(H-h)\psi\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}

where
H
is the scalar mean curvature of
\Sigma_{t}=\partial\Omega_{t}\cap\mathring{M}
.
In particular, a
\mu
-bubble
\Omega
satisfies

H=h

along
\partial\Omega\cap\mathring{M}
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Consider a
\mu
-bubble
\Omega
with
\partial\Omega\cap\mathring{M}=\Sigma
.
Assume that
\Omega_{t}
is a smooth
1
-parameter family of regions with
\Omega_{0}=\Omega
and normal speed
\psi
at
t=0
, then
\mathcal{Q}(\psi):=\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}\big|_{t=0}(\mathcal{A}(\Omega_{t}))\geq 0
where
\mathcal{Q}(\psi)
satisfies

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}(\psi)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Sigma}\left(|D_{\Sigma}\psi|^{2}-\big(|{\rm II}|^{2}+\Ric_{M}(\nu,\nu)+\langle D_{M}h,\nu\rangle\big)\psi^{2}\right)d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Sigma}\left(|D_{\Sigma}\psi|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\big(R_{\Sigma}-R_{M}-\frac{n}{n-1}h^{2}+2|D_{M}h|\big)\psi^{2}\right)d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}.

where
D_{\Sigma}\psi
is the gradient of
\psi
on
\Sigma
,
D_{M}h
is the gradient of
h
on
M
,
{\rm II}
is the second fundamental form of
\Sigma
, and
\nu
is the outwards pointing unit normal.

Proof.

The second variation formula is given by (see e.g.
[
5
, Lemma 3.3]
)

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}(\psi)=\int_{\Sigma}\left(|D_{\Sigma}\psi|^{2}-\big(|{\rm II}|^{2}+\Ric_{M}(\nu,\nu)+\langle D_{M}h,\nu\rangle\big)\psi^{2}\right)d\mathcal{H}^{n-1},

By Schoen–Yau’s rearrangement trick
[
27
, p. 165]
, the traced Gauss equation gives

R_{M}=R_{\Sigma}+2\operatorname{Ric}_{M}(\nu,\nu)+|{\rm II}|^{2}-H^{2}.

Combining with the inequalities
|{\rm II}|^{2}\geq\frac{H^{2}}{n-1}
and
-\langle D_{M}h,\nu\rangle\leq|D_{M}h|
, we get

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}(\psi)\leq\int_{\Sigma}\left(|D_{\Sigma}\psi|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\big(R_{\Sigma}-R_{M}-\frac{n}{n-1}h^{2}+2|D_{M}h|\big)\psi^{2}\right)d\mathcal{H}^{n-1},

where we used
H=h
on
\Sigma
.
∎

We also need the following result of solving a Riccati equation, which will be useful for finding the appropriate function
h
in the
\mu
-bubble functional.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
C>0
be a constant. Consider the following ODE for
r>0
:

2f^{\prime}-\frac{n}{n-1}f^{2}-\frac{C}{r^{2}}=0.

(3.2)

If
C>\frac{n-1}{n}
, then

f(r)=\frac{2(n-1)}{nr}\left[-\frac{1}{2}+\mu\cdot\tan(\mu\log r)\right]

(3.3)

is a solution to (
3.2
),
where

\mu=\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\frac{nC}{n-1}-1}.

Proof.

We can easily verify (
3.3
) is a solution to (
3.2
) by direct computation. Here we show how to solve this Riccati equation for completeness.

We put the ODE (
3.2
) into standard form

f^{\prime}-\frac{n}{2(n-1)}f^{2}-\frac{C}{2r^{2}}=0

By
[
25
, Theorem 2.1 of Chapter One]
, equation (
3.2
) has a solution on an interval
I
if and only if there is a solution
u(r)
to the second-order linear ODE

u^{\prime\prime}+\frac{nC}{4(n-1)r^{2}}u=0

(3.4)

such that on
I
, we have
u\neq 0
and

f=-\frac{2(n-1)}{n}\frac{u^{\prime}}{u}.

We try solution to (
3.4
) of the form
u=r^{\lambda}
, and we get the indicial equation

\lambda^{2}-\lambda+\frac{nC}{4(n-1)}=0.

Since
C>\frac{n-1}{n}
, the discriminant is
\Delta=1-\frac{nC}{(n-1)}<0
. Let
\mu=\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{-\Delta}=\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\frac{nC}{n-1}-1}
. Then the roots to the indicial equation are
\lambda=\frac{1}{2}\pm i\mu
, and the general solution of
u
is given by

u(x)=r^{1/2}(C_{1}\cos(\mu\log r)+C_{2}\sin(\mu\log r))

where
C_{1},C_{2}
are arbitrary constants.

Thus the general solution of
f
is given by

f=-\frac{2(n-1)}{n}\frac{u^{\prime}}{u}=-\frac{2(n-1)}{nr}\left[\frac{1}{2}+\mu\cdot\frac{-C_{1}\sin(\mu\log r)+C_{2}\cos(\mu\log r)}{C_{1}\cos(\mu\log r)+C_{2}\sin(\mu\log r)}\right].

Choosing
C_{1}=1
,
C_{2}=0
gives (
3.3
).
∎

Proof of Proposition

Then the scalar curvature assumption implies that there exists

Let
\tilde{C}
be such that
\frac{n-1}{n}<\tilde{C}<C
.
Let
\tilde{\mu}=\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\frac{n\tilde{C}}{n-1}-1}
. Choose
0<\varepsilon<1
be such that

\frac{(1+\varepsilon)^{2}D_{0}^{2}}{(D_{0}-\varepsilon)^{2}}<\frac{C}{\tilde{C}}.

Then for all
r\geq D_{0}
, we have

\frac{(1+\varepsilon)^{2}r^{2}}{(r-\varepsilon)^{2}}<\frac{C}{\tilde{C}},

so

\frac{\tilde{C}(1+\varepsilon)^{2}}{(r-\varepsilon)^{2}}<\frac{C}{r^{2}}.

(3.5)

By
[
11
, Section 2]
, we can find a smoothing
\rho
of
r
on
M
such that
|\rho(x)-r(x)|<\varepsilon
for all
x\in M
, and
\Lip\rho<1+\varepsilon
. By perturbing we further assume that for all integers
k>0
,
(1+\varepsilon)\exp({\tilde{\mu}}^{-1}(k\pi-\frac{\pi}{2}))
is a regular value of
\rho
.

Let
f(r)=\frac{2(n-1)}{nr}\left[-\frac{1}{2}+\tilde{\mu}\cdot\tan(\tilde{\mu}\log r)\right]
as in (
3.3
), and define
h(x)=-f(\frac{\rho(x)}{1+\varepsilon})
.

We compute that for
r,\rho>D_{0}
,

\displaystyle-2|D_{M}h|+\frac{n}{n-1}h^{2}+R_{M}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2|f^{\prime}(\frac{\rho}{1+\varepsilon})|\cdot\frac{|D_{M}\rho|}{1+\varepsilon}+\frac{n}{n-1}f^{2}(\frac{\rho}{1+\varepsilon})+R_{M}

\displaystyle>

\displaystyle-2|f^{\prime}(\frac{\rho}{1+\varepsilon})|+\frac{n}{n-1}f^{2}(\frac{\rho}{1+\varepsilon})+\frac{C}{r^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{\tilde{C}}{\rho^{2}/(1+\varepsilon)^{2}}+\frac{C}{r^{2}}

by (
3.2
)

\displaystyle>

\displaystyle-\frac{\tilde{C}(1+\varepsilon)^{2}}{(r-\varepsilon)^{2}}+\frac{C}{r^{2}}

\displaystyle>

\displaystyle\,0

by (
3.5
).

Take
k_{1}>0
to be an integer such that
\exp({\tilde{\mu}}^{-1}(k_{1}\pi-\frac{\pi}{2}))>D_{0}+1
.
We define
M_{0}=\{x\in M\mid\rho(x)<(1+\varepsilon)\exp({\tilde{\mu}}^{-1}(k_{1}\pi-\frac{\pi}{2}))\}
.
Let
M_{1}=\{x\in M\mid(1+\varepsilon)\exp({\tilde{\mu}}^{-1}(k_{1}\pi-\frac{\pi}{2}))\leq\rho(x)\leq(1+\varepsilon)\exp({\tilde{\mu}}^{-1}(k_{1}\pi+\frac{\pi}{2}))\}
, with boundaries
\partial_{-}M_{1}=\{x\in M\mid\rho(x)=\exp({\tilde{\mu}}^{-1}(k_{1}\pi+\frac{\pi}{2}))\}
and
\partial_{+}M_{1}=\{x\in M\mid\rho(x)=(1+\varepsilon)\exp({\tilde{\mu}}^{-1}(k_{1}\pi-\frac{\pi}{2}))\}
.
Then
h\to\pm\infty
on
\partial_{\pm}M_{1}
.
Notice that on
M_{1}
,
\rho>D_{0}
and
r>D_{0}
.

Let
L_{1}\in((1+\varepsilon)\exp({\tilde{\mu}}^{-1}(k_{1}\pi-\frac{\pi}{2})),(1+\varepsilon)\exp({\tilde{\mu}}^{-1}(k_{1}\pi+\frac{\pi}{2})))
be a regular value of
\rho
. We fix a smooth reference region
\hat{\Omega}_{1}=\{x\in M\mid(1+\varepsilon)\exp({\tilde{\mu}}^{-1}(k_{1}\pi-\frac{\pi}{2}))\leq\rho(x)\leq L_{1}\}
, and consider the
\mu
-bubble functional (
3.1
) on
M_{1}
with
h
as defined above.

Then Proposition
3.1
produces a smooth minimizer
\Omega_{1}\subset M_{1}
for (
3.1
) such that
\Omega_{1}\Delta\hat{\Omega}_{1}
is compactly contained in the interior of
M_{1}
. Then
\Omega_{1}
contains a neighborhood of
\partial_{+}M_{1}
in
M_{1}
. Let
\Sigma_{1}=\partial\Omega_{1}\cap\mathring{M}_{1}
. Note that
\Sigma_{1}
is orientable because
M
is.
Let
K_{1}=M_{0}\cup\Omega_{1}
. Then
K_{1}
is a smooth compact region and
\partial K_{1}=\Sigma_{1}
.

Then using the stability of
\Sigma_{1}
, we get that for all nonzero smooth function
\psi
on
\Sigma_{1}
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma_{1}}(|D_{\Sigma_{1}}\psi|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}R_{\Sigma_{1}}\psi^{2})

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma_{1}}(-2|D_{M}h|+\frac{n}{n-1}h^{2}+R_{M})\psi^{2}

by Lemma
3.3

\displaystyle>

\displaystyle\,0

by above computation.

We now show
\Sigma_{1}
admits a metric of positive scalar curvature. When
n=3
,
\dim\Sigma_{1}=2
, so
R_{\Sigma_{1}}=2K_{\Sigma_{1}}
is the Gaussian curvature and we can take
\psi=1
on any connected component
\Sigma_{1}^{\prime}\subset\Sigma_{1}
to find

2\pi\chi(\Sigma_{1}^{\prime})=\int_{\Sigma_{1}^{\prime}}K_{\Sigma_{1}}>0,

so
\Sigma_{1}^{\prime}
is a sphere and thus admits positive scalar curvature.

When
n\geq 4
we observe that

\frac{2(n-2)}{n-3}\geq 1

so using
|D_{\Sigma_{1}}\psi|^{2}\geq 0
we find that

\int_{\Sigma_{1}}\frac{2(n-2)}{n-3}|D_{\Sigma_{1}}\psi|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}R_{\Sigma_{1}}\psi^{2}>0,

i.e. the conformal Laplacian

L_{\Sigma_{1}}:=-\frac{4(n-2)}{n-3}\Delta_{\Sigma_{1}}+R_{\Sigma_{1}}

is a positive operator (e.g. all eigenvalues are positive). Letting
u>0
denote the first eigenfunction of
L_{\Sigma_{1}}
(so
L_{\Sigma_{1}}u=\lambda u>0
), a computation (see p. 9 in
[
27
]
) implies that the metric
\tilde{g}_{\Sigma_{1}}=u^{\frac{4}{n-3}}g_{\Sigma_{1}}
on
\Sigma_{1}
has scalar curvature

\tilde{R}_{\Sigma_{1}}=u^{\frac{n+1}{n-3}}L_{\Sigma_{1}}u>0.

This again shows
\Sigma_{1}
admits positive scalar curvature metric.

Lastly, assume that
K_{1}\subset\dots\subset K_{i-1}
have been chosen as in the statement of the theorem. Let
k_{i}=k_{1}+2(i-1)
. Then we can define

,
\Omega_{i}
, and

the same way as
M_{1}

\Omega_{1}
, and
\Sigma_{1}
. We let

.
The same computation shows
\Sigma_{i}
also admits a metric of positive scalar curvature.

∎

4.
Proof of the decomposition theorem

Before we prove Theorem
1.5
, we recall the notion of infinite connected sum modeled on a locally finite graph,
following
[
28
,
30
,
1
]
.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a family of connected 3-manifolds. A 3-manifold
M
decomposes as a
connected sum
of members of
\mathcal{F}
modeled on a locally finite graph
\mathcal{G}
if there is a map assigning to each vertex
v
a copy
M_{v}
of a manifold in
\mathcal{F}
such that
M
is diffeomorphic to the manifold constructed as follows:

(1)

For each vertex
v
, construct a new manifold
Y_{v}
by removing from
M_{v}
a number of
\deg{(v)}
3-balls from its interior,

(2)

For each edge
e
joining two vertices
v
and
v^{\prime}
, glue two spherical boundary components from
\partial Y_{v}
and
\partial Y_{v^{\prime}}
along an orientation-reversing diffeomorphism.

Note that if a 3-manifold decomposes as an infinite connected sum, the graph
\mathcal{G}
on which
M
is modeled may not be unique.

Proof of Theorem
1.5
.

By Proposition
1.8
, consider an exhaustion of
M
by compact domains
K_{1}\subset K_{2}\subset\dots\subset M
, whose boundaries
\partial K_{i}
form a locally finite collection of orientable closed surfaces that admit metrics of positive scalar curvature. Denote the connected components of the boundary surfaces by
\{\Sigma_{\alpha}\}
. Then by Gauss–Bonnet, each
\{\Sigma_{\alpha}\}
must be
\mathbb{S}^{2}
.

Then, we can follow the proof of
[
1
, Theorem 1.10]

to obtain that
M
decomposes as a (possibly infinite) connected sum of closed prime 3-manifolds
\hat{P}_{ijk}
modeled on some locally finite graph
\mathcal{G}
. We briefly sketch the steps here.

We first cut
M
along the collection of spheres
\{\Sigma_{\alpha}\}
. The result consists of the connected
components
Y_{ij}
of the pieces
Y_{i}=K_{i}\setminus K_{i-1}
. Denote by
\hat{Y}_{ij}
the result of
capping the spherical boundary components of
Y_{ij}
off by 3-balls. Then by the Kneser–Milnor decomposition theorem, each
\hat{Y}_{ij}
decomposes as a connected sum of prime closed 3-manifolds
\hat{P}_{ijk}
.
Next, we can construct a locally finite graph
\mathcal{G}
as follows. The graph
\mathcal{G}
has a vertex
v
for each prime manifold
\hat{P}_{ijk}
, and there is an edge
e
joining a pair of vertices
v,v^{\prime}
for each sphere in the common boundary
\partial P_{v}\cap\partial P_{v^{\prime}}
, where
P_{v}
and
P_{v^{\prime}}
are the prime manifolds corresponding to
v
and
v^{\prime}
. By construction, the manifold
M
decomposes as a (possibly infinite) connected sum of the closed prime 3-manifolds
\hat{P}_{ijk}
modeled on
\mathcal{G}
.

Each closed prime 3-manifold
\hat{P}_{ijk}
is either spherical, aspherical or diffeomorphic to
\mathbb{S}^{2}\times\mathbb{S}^{1}
. However, since
M
admits a metric of positive scalar curvature, by Theorem 1.7 of
[
6
]
, none of the summands
\hat{P}_{ijk}
can be aspherical.
Therefore,
M
is diffeomorphic to a (possibly infinite) connected sum of spherical manifolds and
\mathbb{S}^{2}\times\mathbb{S}^{1}
modeled on
\mathcal{G}
.

Optimality of the constant
C_{3}=\frac{2}{3}
follows from Proposition
1.7
.
∎

On positive solutions of critical semilinear equations
involving the Logarithmic Laplacian

Abstract




1
Introduction

Let
\mathcal{L}_{\Delta}
be the logarthmic Laplacian in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
n\in\mathbb{N}
, the set of positive integers, initiated in
[
14
]
, defined by

\mathcal{L}_{\Delta}u(x)=c_{n}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{u(x)1_{B_{1}(x)}(y)-u(y)}{|x-y|^{n}}dy+\rho_{n}u(x),

where

c_{n}\!:=\pi^{-n/2}\Gamma(n/2)=\frac{2}{\omega_{{n-1}}},\qquad\rho_{n}\!:=2\ln 2+\psi(\tfrac{n}{2})-\gamma,

\omega_{{n-1}}:=|\mathbb{S}^{n-1}|
, the area of the unit sphere of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and
\gamma=-\Gamma^{\prime}(1)
is the Euler Mascheroni constant,
\psi=\frac{\Gamma^{\prime}}{\Gamma}
is the digamma function associated to the Gamma function
\Gamma
.
Our aim of this article is to classify the positive solutions for the critical semilinear problem involving the logarithmic Laplacian

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\mathcal{L}_{\Delta}u=\frac{4}{n}u\ln u&{\rm in}\ \ \mathbb{R}^{n},\\[5.69054pt]
\phantom{\mathcal{L}_{\Delta}}u\gneqq 0&{\rm{in}}\ \ \mathbb{R}^{n},\end{array}\right.

(1.1)

where the function
t\to t\ln t
is defined on
[0,+\infty)
with
t\ln t=0
for
t=0
.

In the last decades, the seminlinear equations involving integro-differential operators have been studied extensively and widely
inspired by a renewed and increasing interest and by their wide range of applications. Also important progresses have been made in
understanding nonlocal phenomena from a PDE or a probabilistic point of view, see e.g.

[

,

.
Among nonlocal differential order operators the simplest examples are the fractional powers of the Laplacian, which exhibit many phenomenological properties.
Recall that for
s\in(0,1)
, the fractional Laplacian

(-\Delta)^{s}
can be written as a singular integral operator defined in the principle value sense

(-\Delta)^{s}u(x)=c_{n,s}\lim_{\epsilon\to 0^{+}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{\epsilon}(x)}\frac{u(x)-u(y)}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}dy,

where
c_{n,s}=2^{2s}\pi^{-\frac{n}{2}}s\frac{\Gamma(\frac{n+2s}{2})}{\Gamma(1-s)}>0
is a normalized constant such that for a function
u\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,

\mathcal{F}((-\Delta)^{s}u)(\xi):=(2\pi)^{-\frac{n}{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{-{\rm i}x\cdot\xi}((-\Delta)^{s}u)(x)dx=|\xi|^{2s}\widehat{u}(\xi)\quad\text{for all $\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}$}.

Here and in the sequel both
\mathcal{F}
and
\widehat{\cdot}
denote the Fourier transform. Two known limits hold when
s
tends
0
or
1
:

\lim_{s\to 1^{-}}(-\Delta)^{s}u(x)=-\Delta u(x)\quad\text{and}\quad\lim_{s\to 0^{+}}(-\Delta)^{s}u(x)=u(x)\quad\ \text{for $u\in\mathcal{C}^{2}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n})$,}

see e.g.
[
24
]
.
Furthermore the following surprising expansion at
s=0
is proved in
[
14
]

(-\Delta)^{s}u(x)=u(x)+s\mathcal{L}_{\Delta}u(x)+o(s)\quad\text{as }\,s\to 0^{+}\quad\text{ for all }u\in\mathcal{C}^{2}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\text{ and }x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

where the formal operator

\mathcal{L}_{\Delta}:=\frac{d}{ds}\Big|_{s=0}(-\Delta)^{s}

is the
logarithmic Laplacian
; more precisely (see e.g.
[
14
]
),

(i)

for
1<p\leq\infty
, there holds
\mathcal{L}_{\Delta}u\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
\frac{(-\Delta)^{s}u-u}{s}\to\mathcal{L}_{\Delta}u
in
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
as
s\to 0^{+}
;

(ii)

\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{L}_{\Delta}u)(\xi)=(2\ln|\xi|)\,\widehat{u}(\xi)
for a.e.
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

The natural domain of definition of
\mathcal{L}_{\Delta}
is the set of uniformly Dini continuous functions
u
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}

with the integral restriction

\|u\|_{L^{1}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|u(x)|}{1+|x|^{n}}dx<\infty.

Precisely, by denoting the module of continuity of
u
at
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
by

\varpi_{u,x}:[0,1]\to[0,\infty),\qquad\varpi_{u,x}(r)=\sup_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\,|y-x|\leq r}|u(y)-u(x)|,

a function
u
is said to be Dini continuous at
x
if

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\varpi_{u,x}(r)}{r}dr<+\infty.

Let
\mathcal{D}^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be the set of all functions
u
having the Dini continuity at any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
Here a nonnegative function
u
is said to be a solution of (
1.1
) if
u\in L^{1}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap\mathcal{D}^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
satisfies the equation pointwisely.

Our first result is on the existence and classification of positive solutions of (
1.1
).

Theorem 1.1

Let
t>0
,
\tilde{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and

u_{\tilde{x},t}(x)=\beta_{n}\Big(\frac{t}{t^{2}+|x-\tilde{x}|^{2}}\Big)^{\frac{n}{2}},\quad\forall\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

(1.2)

where

\beta_{n}=2^{\frac{n}{2}}e^{\frac{n}{2}\psi(\frac{n}{2})}.

(1.3)

Then problem (
1.1
) only admits the solutions’ set
\{u_{\tilde{x},t}\}_{t>0,\,\tilde{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}
.

Here we mention that (
1.1
) and the solutions with the form (
1.2
) are approached by the ones of critical fractional problem

(-\Delta)^{s}u(x)=u^{2^{*}_{s}-1},\;\;u>0\;\;{\rm in}\ \ \mathbb{R}^{n},

(1.4)

where
n>2s
and
2^{*}_{s}=\frac{2n}{n-2s}
. It was proved in
[
18
, Theorem 1.1]
(also see
[
27
]
) that all the solution of problem (
1.4
) has the form of

u_{s,t}(x)=b_{n,s}\Big(\frac{t}{t^{2}+|x-\tilde{x}|^{2}}\Big)^{\frac{n-2s}{2}},\ \ \forall\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}

(1.5)

for any
t>0
, where
b_{n,s}>0
is a constant (see (
1.7
) below) and
\tilde{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. From the Caffarelli-Silvestre’s extension
[
9
]
, equation (
1.4
) could be built by connection with
the extension problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\ \,{\rm div}(t^{1-2s}\nabla w)=0&{\rm in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}:=(0,+\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{n},\\[5.69054pt]
\phantom{}\displaystyle\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}t^{1-2s}\partial_{t}w=w(0,\cdot)^{2^{*}_{s}-1}&{\rm in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{n}=\partial\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+},\end{array}\right.

(1.6)

via the boundary trace
u=w_{s}(0,\cdot)
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. This type of extension builds the platform for the Yamabe problem in the fractional setting, see the references
[
12
,
10
,
33
,
22
]
and more related study could see
[
20
,
32
,
38
,
17
]
. An amazing extension involving the logarithmic Laplacian
has been built
[
13
]
recently.

The explicit formula of
b_{n,s}

is very important in our study of the approximating the solution of (
1.1
) by passing to the limit as
s\to 0^{+}
. To this end,
we provide the proof of the sharp constant according to
[
35
, Theorem 3.1]

b_{n,s}=2^{\frac{n-2s}{2}}\Big(\frac{\Gamma(\frac{n}{2}+s)}{\Gamma(\frac{n}{2}-s)}\Big)^{\frac{n-2s}{4s}}.

(1.7)

Our approach of the extremal solutions is to pass to limit of the ones of fractional Yamabe problems and
to show the uniqueness by the method of Chen-Li-Ou in
[
18
]
under the restriction of
L^{1}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap\mathcal{D}^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.
In fact, from our proof, we can also provide a correct sharp constant of the Pitt’s inequality by Beckner
[
3
]
: for
\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=1
,

\displaystyle\frac{n}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\ln|\xi|)|\hat{f}(\xi)|^{2}d\xi\geq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\ln|f|)|f|^{2}dx+B_{n},

(1.8)

where

\displaystyle B_{n}=\ln{\|u_{1}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}}=\frac{n}{2}\Big(\psi(\frac{n}{2})+\frac{1}{2}\ln\pi-\frac{1}{n}\ln\big(\frac{\Gamma(n)}{\Gamma(\frac{n}{2})}\big)\Big).

(1.9)

We would like to mention that Beckner in
[
5
]
(also see
[
4
]
) shows that the Pitt’s inequality in the unit sphere
\mathbb{S}^{n}
of
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}\times\mathbb{S}^{n}}\frac{\big|f(\xi)-f(\eta)\big|^{2}}{|\xi-\eta|^{n}}d\xi d\eta\geq\frac{2}{\pi^{1/2}}\frac{\Gamma(\frac{n+1}{2})}{\Gamma(\frac{n}{2}+1)}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}\big(\ln|f|\big)|f|^{2}dx,\quad\forall\,f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{n}),\ \|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{n})}=1

(1.10)

and the extremal functions are of the form

\displaystyle A(1-\xi\cdot\eta)^{-\frac{n}{2}}\quad\text{ for $\xi\in\mathbb{S}^{n}$ }

(1.11)

with
|\eta|<1
and
for some suitable constant
A
. Recently, Frank-König-Tang
[
26
]
gave the classification of solutions for

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}\frac{u(\xi)-u(\eta)}{|\xi-\eta|^{n}}d\eta=E_{n}u(\xi)\ln u(\xi)\quad{\rm for}\ \ \xi\in\mathbb{S}^{n},

(1.12)

highlighted the conformal invariance for the above equation and showed that it admits the solutions with the form of (
1.11
),
where
E_{n}=\frac{4}{n}\frac{\pi^{\frac{n}{2}}}{\Gamma(\frac{n}{2})}
.
Notice that from the stereographic projection, there is some ’equivalence’ between our problem (
1.1
) and problem (
1.12
)
under the suitable restriction, such as
u\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for (
1.12
). Our approach enables us to classify the solution within the set of
L^{1}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap\mathcal{D}^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Here we remark that the crucial point for classification lies in the method of moving planes. Our challenges of moving planes arises from the logarithmic Laplacian and non-monotonicity of nonlinearity. We cannot adopt the approach of Chen-Li-Ou in
[
18
]
via the integral equation, as the fundamental
solution of the logarithmic Laplacian is no longer positive and decreasing-monotone. The core of the traditional method of moving planes is to involve the Sobolev embedding to start the moving planes from
-\infty
in one direction, see e.g.
[
28
]
. While there is no pertinent Sobolev embeddings
related to the logarithmic operator in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and the nonlinearity is no longer monotonically increasing,
so it fails to apply this type moving planes to give the classification. Here we adopt a method of moving planes suitable for the attributes of the logarithmic Laplacian. Furthermore, another important tool for proving our uniqueness is the Pitt’s inequality (
1.8
) and Beckner in
[
3
]
indicates that the equality in (
1.8
) is achieved by the extremal functions with the form
A(1+|x|^{2})^{-\frac{n}{2}}
via ”the conformal invariance of this inequality is inherited from the Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality and this
suffices to determine the extremals.”

The most significant features of (
1.1
) lie in the capability of employing the Fourier transform, the scaling and Kelvin transform.
These properties inherit from the logarithmic Laplacian and the logarithmic type nonlinearity and they play an important role in procedure of the moving planes.

Proposition 1.1

Let
T\in\mathbb{R}
,
u\in\mathcal{D}^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap L^{1}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be a solution of

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\mathcal{L}_{\Delta}u+Tu=\frac{4}{n}u\ln u&{\rm in}\ \,\mathbb{R}^{n},\\[5.69054pt]
\phantom{\mathcal{L}_{\Delta}+Tu}u\geq 0&{\rm{in}}\ \,\mathbb{R}^{n}.\end{array}\right.

(1.13)

(i)
Given
l>0
and
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, let

\displaystyle v_{l}(x)=lu(x+x_{0}),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

(1.14)

Then
v_{l}
verifies

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\mathcal{L}_{\Delta}v_{l}+\big(T+\frac{4}{n}\ln l\big)v_{l}=\frac{4}{n}v_{l}\ln v_{l}&{\rm in}\ \,\mathbb{R}^{n},\\[5.69054pt]
\phantom{\mathcal{L}_{\Delta}+T+\big(\frac{4}{n}\ln l\big)uv_{l}}v_{l}\geq 0&{\rm{in}}\ \,\mathbb{R}^{n}.\end{array}\right.

(1.15)

(ii)
Given
l>0
, let

\displaystyle u_{l}(x)=l^{-\frac{n}{2}}u(l^{-1}x),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

(1.16)

Then
u_{l}
is also a solution of (
1.13
).
Furthermore, if
u\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, then

\|u_{l}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=\|u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}
.

(iii)
Let
\tilde{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n},r>0
and

\displaystyle u^{\#_{0,r}}(x)=\big(\frac{r}{|x-\tilde{x}|}\big)^{n}u(x^{*,r})\quad\ {\rm for}\ \,x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{\tilde{x}\},

(1.17)

where

\displaystyle x^{*,r}=\frac{r^{2}(x-\tilde{x})}{|x-\tilde{x}|^{2}}+\tilde{x}\quad\ {\rm for}\ \,x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{\tilde{x}\}.

(1.18)

Then

\mathcal{L}_{\Delta}u^{\#_{0,r}}+Tu^{\#_{0,r}}=\frac{4}{n}u^{\#_{0,r}}\ln u^{\#_{0,r}}\quad\ {\rm in}\ \,\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{\tilde{x}\}.

Remark 1.1

Let
\tilde{u}=e^{-\frac{n}{4}\rho_{n}}u
, then
\tilde{u}
verifies that

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\mathcal{L}_{\Delta}\tilde{u}-\rho_{n}\tilde{u}=\frac{4}{n}\tilde{u}\ln\tilde{u}&{\rm in}\ \ \mathbb{R}^{n},\\[5.69054pt]
\phantom{\mathcal{L}_{\Delta}---}\tilde{u}\geq 0&{\rm{in}}\ \ \mathbb{R}^{n}.\end{array}\right.

(1.19)

This form of equation (
1.19
) is available for the method of the moving planes.

Finally, we show that the factor
\frac{4}{n}
is the critical value for the existence of entire solution. In fact, by a Pohozaev type identity, we can derive that equation (
1.1
) has no positive solution, if
\frac{4}{n}
is replaced by any other positive constant.

Theorem 1.2

Let
k>0
and
u\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap\mathcal{D}^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be a nonnegative function verifying

u^{2}\ln u\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

and satisfying

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\mathcal{L}_{\Delta}u=ku\ln u&{\rm in}\ \ \mathbb{R}^{n},\\[5.69054pt]
\phantom{\mathcal{L}_{\Delta}}u\geq 0&{\rm{in}}\ \ \mathbb{R}^{n}.\end{array}\right.

(1.20)

If
k\not=\frac{4}{n}
, then
u\equiv 0
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Observe that for
k\not=\frac{4}{n}
, the Kelvin transform in Theorem
1.1
cannot be applied and we are unable to obtain the decay at infinity
by the method of the moving planes. Hence, some restriction on
u
need to be added to ensure the Pohozaev identity can derived and to get the desired result.

The rest of this paper is structured as follows. In Section 2, we demonstrate the qualitative properties of positive super solutions for the critical logaritmic equation in Theorem
1.1
. Section 3 is dedicated to the existence of the positive solutions by approximating those of fractional critical problems as the order
2s
tends to zero. In Section 4 we adopt the method of moving planes to obtain the radially symmetric and asymptotic behavior at infinity and we perform the classification of positive solutions in Theorem
1.1
. Finally, we show the nonexistence of positive solutions of (
1.20
) in Theorem
1.2

2.1
Basic properties for Logarithmic Laplacian

We start from the basic properties of Eq. (
1.1
), including the scaling and Kelvin transformation.

Proof of Proposition
1.1
.

(i)
For a classical solution
u
of (
1.13
),

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\Delta}v_{l}=l\mathcal{L}_{\Delta}u

\displaystyle=l\frac{4}{n}u\ln u-lTu

\displaystyle=\frac{4}{n}v_{l}\ln(v_{l}l^{-1})-Tv_{l}=\frac{4}{n}v_{l}\ln(v_{l})-\big(T+\frac{4}{n}\ln l\big)v_{l}\quad{\rm in}\ \,\mathbb{R}^{n}.

(ii)
Recall that
u_{l}
is defined in (
1.16
).
Note that

\hat{u}_{l}(\xi)=l^{\frac{n}{2}}\hat{u}(l\xi)

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{L}_{\Delta}u_{l})(\xi)=(2\ln|\xi|)\hat{u_{l}}(\xi)

\displaystyle=(2\ln|\xi|)l^{\frac{n}{2}}\hat{u}(l\xi)

\displaystyle=l^{\frac{n}{2}}(2\ln|l\xi|)\hat{u}(l\xi)-l^{\frac{n}{2}}(2\ln l)\hat{u}(l\xi),

then it follows by the inverse Fourier transform

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\Delta}u_{l}(x)

\displaystyle=l^{-\frac{n}{2}}\big(\mathcal{L}_{\Delta}u\big)(l^{-1}x)-l^{-\frac{n}{2}}(2\ln l)u(l^{-1}x)

\displaystyle=l^{-\frac{n}{2}}\Big(\frac{4}{n}u(l^{-1}x)\ln(u(l^{-1}x))-Tu(l^{-1}x)\Big)-(2\ln l)u_{l}(x)

\displaystyle=u_{l}(x)\big(\frac{4}{n}\ln(u_{l}(x))+\frac{4}{n}\ln l^{\frac{n}{2}}-T\big)-(2\ln l)u_{l}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{4}{n}u_{l}(x)\ln(u_{l}(x))-Tu_{l}(x),

which means that
u_{l}
is a solution of (
1.13
).

It is obvious that

\displaystyle\|u_{l}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}={\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|u(l^{-1}x)|^{2}l^{-n}dx}={\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|u(y)|^{2}dy},

which is independent of
l>0
.

(iii)
Given
r>0
and a function
u\in C^{\gamma}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
\gamma>0
,
let

\displaystyle x^{*,r}=\big(\frac{r}{|x-\tilde{x}|^{2}}\big)^{2}(x-\tilde{x})+\tilde{x},\quad u^{*,r}(x)=u(x^{*,r}),\quad u^{\#_{s,r}}(x)=\big(\frac{r}{|x-\tilde{x}|}\big)^{n-2s}u^{*,r}(x)

(2.1)

for
\tilde{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{\tilde{x}\}
.
Particularly, we use the notations that

\displaystyle u^{\#_{0,r}}(x)=\big(\frac{r}{|x-\tilde{x}|}\big)^{n}u^{*,r}(x)\quad\ {\rm for}\ \,x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{\tilde{x}\}.

(2.2)

It is known that the Kelvin transform for fractional Laplacian

\displaystyle(-\Delta)^{s}u^{\#_{s,r}}(x)=\big(\frac{r}{|x-\tilde{x}|}\big)^{2s+n}\big((-\Delta)^{s}u^{*,r}\big)(x)\quad\ {\rm for\ any}\ \,x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{\tilde{x}\}.

(2.3)

We claim that

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\Delta}u^{\#_{0,r}}(x)=\big(\frac{r}{|x-\tilde{x}|}\big)^{n}\big(\mathcal{L}_{\Delta}u\big)(x^{*,r})-4\big(\ln|x-\tilde{x}|-\ln r\big)u^{\#_{0,r}}(x)\quad\ {\rm for\ any}\ \,x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{\tilde{x}\}.

(2.4)

In fact, we observe that

\displaystyle u^{\#_{s,r}}(x)

\displaystyle=\big(\frac{r}{|x-\tilde{x}|}\big)^{n}u^{*,r}(x)\Big(1+2s\big(\ln|x-\tilde{x}|-\ln r\big)+U_{s}(x)\Big),

where

\limsup_{s\to 0^{+}}\Big(\|s^{-2}U_{s}(x)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\|s^{-2}U_{s}(x)\|_{C^{\gamma}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\Big)<+\infty.

We apply Theorem
3.1
below in Section §3 to obtain that

\displaystyle(-\Delta)^{s}u^{\#_{s,r}}(x)

\displaystyle=(I+s\mathcal{L}_{\Delta}+O(s^{2})\Big)\bigg(\big(\frac{r}{|x-\tilde{x}|}\big)^{n}u^{*,r}(x)\Big(1+2s\big(\ln|x-\tilde{x}|-\ln r\big)+U_{s}(x)\Big)\bigg)

\displaystyle=u^{\#_{0,r}}(x)+s\Big(2u^{\#_{0,r}}(x)\ln\frac{|x-\tilde{x}|}{r}+\mathcal{L}_{\Delta}u^{\#_{0,r}}(x)\Big)+O(s^{2})

and

\displaystyle\quad\big(\frac{r}{|x-\tilde{x}|}\big)^{2s+n}\big((-\Delta)^{s}u\big)(x^{*,r})

\displaystyle=\big(\frac{r}{|x-\tilde{x}|}\big)^{n}\Big(1-2s\big(\ln|x-\tilde{x}|-\ln r\big)+O(s^{2})\Big)\big(\mathcal{I}+s\mathcal{L}_{\Delta}+O(s^{2})\big)u^{*,r}(x)

\displaystyle=u^{\#_{0,r}}(x)+s\big(\frac{r}{|x-\tilde{x}|}\big)^{n}\Big(\mathcal{L}_{\Delta}u^{*,r}(x)-2\big(\ln|x-\tilde{x}|-\ln r\big)u^{*,r}(x)\Big)+O(s^{2}).

From the identity (
2.3
), we obtain that

2u^{\#_{0,r}}(x)(\ln|x-\tilde{x}|-\ln r)+\mathcal{L}_{\Delta}u^{\#_{0,r}}(x)=\big(\frac{r}{|x-\tilde{x}|}\big)^{n}\Big(\mathcal{L}_{\Delta}u^{*,r}(x)-2\big(\ln|x-\tilde{x}|-\ln r\big)u^{*,r}(x)\Big),

which implies (
2.4
).

Now it follows from (
2.4
) that

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\Delta}u^{\#_{0,r}}(x)

\displaystyle=\big(\frac{r}{|x-\tilde{x}|}\big)^{n}\big(\mathcal{L}_{\Delta}u\big)(x^{*,r})-4\big(\ln|x-\tilde{x}|-\ln r\big)u^{\#_{0,r}}(x)

\displaystyle=\big(\frac{r}{|x-\tilde{x}|}\big)^{n}\Big(\frac{4}{n}u^{*,r}\ln u^{0,r}-Tu^{*,r}\Big)-4\big(\ln|x-\tilde{x}|-\ln r\big)u^{\#_{0,r}}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{4}{n}u^{\#_{0,r}}(x)\ln\big(u^{\#_{0,r}}(x)\big)-Tu^{\#_{0,r}}(x).

We complete the proof.
\Box

2.2
Sharp constant
b_{n,s}

Proof of (
1.7
).
Recall that by
[
18
, Theorem 1.1]
, the solution of problem (
1.4
) has the form (
1.5
), that is

u_{s,t}(x)=b_{n,s}\Big(\frac{t}{t^{2}+|x|^{2}}\Big)^{\frac{n-2s}{2}},\ \ \forall\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

for any
t>0
and some suitable
b_{n,s}>0
. Next we give the precise formula of the constant
b_{n,s}
. To this end, we take
t=1
.
Let

v_{0}(x)=b_{n,s}^{-1}u_{s,1}(x)=\Big(\frac{1}{1+|x|^{2}}\Big)^{\frac{n-2s}{2}},\ \ \,\forall\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

then

(-\Delta)^{s}v_{0}(x)=b_{n,s}^{2^{*}_{s}-2}v_{0}^{2^{*}_{s}-1}(x),\ \ \,\forall\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

(2.5)

It is know that the fundamental solution of
(-\Delta)^{s}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
has the following form

\Phi_{s}(x)=\mathcal{P}_{0}(s)|x|^{2s-n},\quad\forall\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\},

where

\mathcal{P}_{0}(s):=\pi^{-\frac{n}{2}}\,4^{-s}\frac{\Gamma(\frac{n-2s}{2})}{\Gamma(s)}.

(2.6)

From (
2.5
), we have that

v_{0}(x)=b_{n,s}^{2^{*}_{s}-2}\big(\Phi_{s}\ast v_{0}^{2^{*}_{s}-1}\big)(x),\ \ \forall\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

or equivalently

v_{1}^{\frac{1}{2^{*}_{s}-1}}(x)=b_{n,s}^{2^{*}_{s}-2}\big(\Phi_{s}\ast v_{1}\big)(x),\ \ \forall\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

(2.7)

where

\displaystyle v_{1}(x):=v_{0}^{2^{*}_{s}-1}(x)=\big(1+|x|^{2}\big)^{-\frac{n+2s}{2}},\ \ \forall\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

From
[
35
, Theorem 3.1]
with
\lambda=n-2s
,
p=\frac{2n}{n+2s}
and
q=\frac{2n}{n-2s}

the best constant is defined as following and has the explicit formula:

\mathcal{P}_{1}(s):=\sup_{w\in L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\frac{\big\||x|^{2s-n}\ast w\big\|_{q}}{\|w\|_{p}}=\pi^{\frac{n-2s}{2}}\frac{\Gamma(s)}{\Gamma(\frac{n}{2}+s)}\Big(\frac{\Gamma(\frac{n}{2})}{\Gamma(n)}\Big)^{-\frac{2s}{n}}

and it is achieved uniquely by
v_{1}
, up to multiplications by nonzero constant and translations.

Thus, we get

\displaystyle\sup_{w\in L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\frac{\big\|\Phi_{s}\ast w\big\|_{q}}{\|w\|_{p}}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{P}_{1}(s)\mathcal{P}_{0}(s)=(4\pi)^{-s}\frac{\Gamma(\frac{n}{2}-s)}{\Gamma(\frac{n}{2}+s)}\Big(\frac{\Gamma(\frac{n}{2})}{\Gamma(n)}\Big)^{-\frac{2s}{n}}.

(2.8)

On the other hand, by (
2.7
) we have

\displaystyle\sup_{w\in L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\frac{\big\|\Phi_{s}\ast w\big\|_{q}}{\|w\|_{p}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\Big(\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\Phi_{s}\ast v_{1}|^{\frac{2n}{n-2s}}dx\Big)^{\frac{n-2s}{2n}}}{\Big(\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}v_{1}^{\frac{2n}{n+2s}}dx\Big)^{\frac{n}{2n}}}

\displaystyle=b_{n,s}^{2-2^{*}_{s}}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}v_{1}^{\frac{2n}{n+2s}}dx\Big)^{-\frac{2s}{n}}

\displaystyle=b_{n,s}^{2-2^{*}_{s}}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\big(1+|x|^{2}\big)^{-n}dx\Big)^{-\frac{2s}{n}}

(2.9)

\displaystyle=b_{n,s}^{2-2^{*}_{s}}\pi^{-s}\Big(\frac{\Gamma(\frac{n}{2})}{\Gamma(n)}\Big)^{-\frac{2s}{n}},

where we have used

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{1}{(1+|z|^{2})^{\frac{n}{2}+\tau}}dz=\frac{\omega_{n-1}}{2}B(\frac{n}{2},\tau)=\frac{\omega_{n-1}}{2}\frac{\Gamma(\frac{n}{2})\Gamma(\tau)}{\Gamma(\frac{n}{2}+\tau)}

by taking
\tau=\frac{n}{2}
and
\omega_{n-1}=|\mathbb{S}^{n-1}|=\frac{2\pi^{\frac{n}{2}}}{\Gamma(\frac{n}{2})}.

Compare with (
2.8
) and (
2.9
), we have that

\displaystyle b_{n,s}=\Big(2^{-2s}\frac{\Gamma(\frac{n-2s}{2})}{\Gamma(\frac{n}{2})}\Big)^{-\frac{n-2s}{4s}}=2^{\frac{n-2s}{2}}\Big(\frac{\Gamma(\frac{n}{2}+s)}{\Gamma(\frac{n}{2}-s)}\Big)^{\frac{n-2s}{4s}},

that is (
1.7
).
\Box

Next we consider the limits of the constant
b_{n,s}
as
s\to 0^{+}
.

Lemma 2.1

Let
b_{n,s}
be defined in (
1.7
),

b_{n,0}=e^{\frac{n}{2}\big(\ln 2+\psi(\frac{n}{2})\big)}\quad{\rm and}\quad b_{n,1}=-\Big(\frac{n}{2}\psi^{\prime}(\frac{n}{2})+\psi(\frac{n}{2})+\ln 2\Big)b_{n,0},

where
\psi(\tau):=\frac{d}{d\tau}\ln\Gamma(\tau)=\frac{\Gamma^{\prime}(\tau)}{\Gamma(\tau)}
is the digamma function.

Then

\displaystyle(a)\qquad\lim_{s\to 0^{+}}b_{n,s}=b_{n,0}\;\quad\text{and}\;\quad(b)\qquad\lim_{s\to 0^{+}}\frac{b_{n,s}-b_{n,0}}{s}=b_{n,1}

(2.10)

and

\displaystyle b_{n,s}=b_{n,0}+sb_{n,1}+O(s^{2})\quad{\rm as}\ \,s\to 0^{+}.

(2.11)

Proof.
A direct computation shows that

\displaystyle\lim_{s\to 0^{+}}\ln b_{n,s}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\lim_{s\to 0^{+}}(n-2s)\Big(\frac{\ln\Gamma(\frac{n}{2}+s)-\ln\Gamma(\frac{n}{2}-s)}{2s}\Big)+\ln 2\lim_{s\to 0^{+}}\frac{n-2s}{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{n}{4}\lim_{s\to 0^{+}}\Big(\psi(\frac{n}{2}+s)+\psi(\frac{n}{2}-s)\Big)+\frac{n}{2}\ln 2

\displaystyle=\frac{n}{2}\Big(\ln 2+\psi(\frac{n}{2})\Big),

which implies part
(a)
of (
2.10
). Note that

\displaystyle\lim_{s\to 0^{+}}\frac{d}{ds}(\ln b_{n,s})

\displaystyle=\lim_{s\to 0^{+}}\frac{d}{ds}\Big(\frac{n-2s}{4s}\big(\ln\Gamma(\frac{n}{2}+s)-\ln\Gamma(\frac{n}{2}-s)\big)+\frac{n-2s}{2}\ln 2\Big)

\displaystyle=\frac{n}{4}\lim_{s\to 0^{+}}\bigg[\frac{1}{s}\Big(\psi(\frac{n}{2}+s)+\psi(\frac{n}{2}-s)\Big)-\frac{1}{s^{2}}\big(\ln\Gamma(\frac{n}{2}+s)-\ln\Gamma(\frac{n}{2}-s)\big)\bigg]

\displaystyle\qquad-\frac{1}{2}\lim_{s\to 0^{+}}\Big(\psi(\frac{n}{2}+s)+\psi(\frac{n}{2}-s)\Big)-\ln 2

\displaystyle=-\frac{n}{2}\psi^{\prime}(\frac{n}{2})-\psi(\frac{n}{2})-\ln 2,

here
\psi^{\prime}
is the derivative of
\psi
and it is continuous.
As a consequence, we obtain that

\displaystyle b_{n,1}:=\lim_{s\to 0^{+}}\frac{b_{n,s}-b_{n,0}}{s}

\displaystyle=\lim_{s\to 0^{+}}b_{n,s}\frac{d}{ds}(\ln b_{n,s})=-\Big(\frac{n}{2}\psi^{\prime}(\frac{n}{2})+\psi(\frac{n}{2})+\ln 2\Big)b_{n,0},

which implies part
(b)
of (
2.10
).

By similarly proof, set

\displaystyle b_{n,2}:=\lim_{s\to 0^{+}}\frac{b_{n,s}-b_{n,0}-b_{n,1}s}{s^{2}}\in(0,+\infty),

then

b_{n,s}=b_{n,0}+b_{n,1}s+O(s^{2})\quad{\rm as}\ \,s\to 0^{+}.

We complete the proof.
\Box

3
Existence of bubble solutions

3.1
Expansion for the fractional bubble solutions

We have proved the expansion of the sharp constant
b_{s,t}
as
s\to 0
in the last subsection. We need to prove that the limit
of
u_{s,t}
, the solution of equation (
1.4
), is in fact a solution of (
1.1
). More precisely we prove and then use the following convergence result which should be useful for other situation.

Theorem 3.1

Let
s_{0}\in(0,\frac{1}{4})
,
s\in(0,\frac{1}{8})
,
q\in(1,+\infty)
and
\{u_{s}\}_{s\in(0,s_{0})}\subset C^{2}_{{\rm loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap L^{1}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

be a sequence of functions such that

\displaystyle\limsup_{s\to 0^{+}}\Big(\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\big(|u_{s}(x)|+|\nabla u_{s}(x)|\big)+\big\|(|u_{s}|+|\nabla u_{s}|)\big\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\Big)<+\infty.

(3.1)

Suppose that there exist
u_{0},u_{1}\in C^{2}_{{\rm loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap L^{1}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
verifying

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\big(|u_{0}(x)|+|u_{1}(x)|+|\nabla u_{0}(x)|+|\nabla u_{1}(x)|\big)+\big\||u_{0}|+|u_{1}|+|\nabla u_{0}|+|\nabla u_{1}|\big\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}<+\infty

(3.2)

and

w_{s}:=\frac{1}{s^{2}}\big(u_{s}-u_{0}-su_{1}\big)\quad{\rm in}\ \mathbb{R}^{n},

satisfying

\displaystyle\limsup_{s\to 0^{+}}\Big(\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\big(|w_{s}(x)|+|\nabla w_{s}(x)|\big)+\big\||w_{s}|+|\nabla w_{s}|\big\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\Big)<+\infty.

(3.3)

Then as
s\to 0^{+}
,

(-\Delta)^{s}u_{s}=u_{0}+s\big(\mathcal{L}_{\Delta}u_{0}+u_{1}\big)+o(s)\quad\text{in \ $L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})$ with $p\in(q,+\infty)$}

and

(-\Delta)^{s}u_{s}(x)=u_{0}(x)+s\big(\mathcal{L}_{\Delta}u_{0}(x)+u_{1}(x)\big)+o(s)\quad\text{ uniformly for any $x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}$ }.

Proof.
Let
u\in C^{\beta}_{{\rm loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap L^{1}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
\beta\in(0,1)
, and for
0<s<\min\{\frac{\beta}{2},\frac{1}{2}\}
, the principal value in the definition of
[(-\Delta)^{s}u](x)
reduces to a standard Lebesgue integral.
For
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we then have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{s}\bigg((-\Delta)^{s}u_{s}(x)-\Big(u_{0}(x)+s\big(\mathcal{L}_{\Delta}u_{0}(x)+u_{1}(x)\big)\Big)\bigg)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{s}\bigg(\Big(c_{n,s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{1}}|z|^{-n-2s}dz\Big)\,u_{s}(x)-u_{0}(x)-s\big(\rho_{n}u_{0}(x)+u_{1}(x)\big)\bigg)

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{1}{s}\bigg(c_{n,s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{1}}\frac{u_{s}(x+z)}{|z|^{n+2s}}\,dz-c_{n}s\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{1}}\frac{u_{0}(x+z)}{|z|^{n}}\,dz\bigg)

(3.4)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{s}\bigg(c_{n,s}\int_{B_{1}}\frac{u_{s}(x)-u_{s}(x+z)}{|z|^{n+2s}}\,dz-c_{n}s\int_{B_{1}}\frac{u_{0}(x)-u_{0}(x+z)}{|z|^{n}}\,dz\bigg),

where
B_{1}:=B_{1}(0)
.

Part 1:
Let

d_{n}(s):=\frac{c_{n,s}}{s}=2^{2s}\pi^{-\frac{n}{2}}\frac{\Gamma(\frac{n}{2}+s)}{\Gamma(1-s)},

and then

d_{n}(0)=\pi^{-\frac{n}{2}}\Gamma(\frac{n}{2})=c_{n}\quad\text{and}\quad d_{n}^{\prime}(0)=\pi^{-\frac{n}{2}}\Bigl((2\ln 2-\gamma)\Gamma(\frac{n}{2})+\Gamma^{\prime}(\frac{n}{2})\Bigr)=c_{n}\rho_{n}.

We now can write

c_{n,s}=c_{n}\big(1+\rho_{n}s+\bar{d}(s)\big)s\qquad\text{for}\ \,s\in(0,\frac{1}{8}),

(3.5)

where

\limsup_{s\to 0^{+}}|\bar{d}(s)|s^{-2}<+\infty.

Observe that, by (
3.5
),

\displaystyle c_{n,s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{1}}|z|^{-n-2s}dz=\frac{c_{n,s}|\mathbb{S}^{n-1}|}{2s}

\displaystyle=1+\rho_{n}s+\bar{d}(s),

and by the definition of
w_{s}
and (
3.2
),
we have then

\displaystyle A_{1}(s,x)

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{s}\bigg(\Big(c_{n,s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{1}}|z|^{-n-2s}dz\Big)\,u_{s}(x)-u_{0}(x)-s\big(\rho_{n}u_{0}(x)+u_{1}(x)\big)\bigg)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{s}\Big(\big(1+\rho_{n}s+\bar{d}(s)\big)\big(u_{0}+su_{1}(x)+s^{2}w_{s}(x)\big)-u_{0}(x)-s\big(\rho_{n}u_{0}(x)+u_{1}(x)\big)\Big)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{s}\bar{d}(s)u_{s}(x)+s\big(1+\rho_{n}s+\bar{d}(s)\big)w_{s}(x),

thus, for
q\in(1,+\infty)

\displaystyle\quad\lim_{s\to 0^{+}}\|A_{1}(s,\cdot)\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle\leq C\lim_{s\to 0^{+}}\bigg(\bar{d}(s)s^{-1}\|u_{s}\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+s\|w_{s}\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\bigg)=0

(3.6)

and

\displaystyle\lim_{s\to 0^{+}}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}|A_{1}(s,x)|

\displaystyle\leq C\lim_{s\to 0^{+}}s\bigg(\bar{d}(s)s^{-2}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}|u_{s}(x)|+s\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}|w_{s}(x)|\bigg)=0

(3.7)

by the assumptions of (
3.1
) and (
3.2
).

Part 2:
We see that

\displaystyle A_{2}(s,x):

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{s}\bigg(c_{n,s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{1}}\frac{u_{s}(x+z)}{|z|^{n+2s}}\,dz-c_{n}s\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{1}}\frac{u_{0}(x+z)}{|z|^{n}}\,dz\bigg)

\displaystyle=d_{n}(s)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{1}}\frac{u_{0}(x+z)+su_{1}(x+z)+s^{2}w_{s}(x+z)}{|z|^{n+2s}}\,dz-c_{n}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{1}}\frac{u_{0}(x+z)}{|z|^{n}}\,dz

\displaystyle=c_{n}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{1}}u_{0}(x+z)\frac{1}{|z|^{n}}\big(\frac{1}{|z|^{2s}}-1\big)\,dz+c_{n}\big(\rho_{n}s+\bar{d}(s)\big)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{1}}\frac{u_{0}(x+z)}{|z|^{n+2s}}dz

\displaystyle\quad+d_{n}(s)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{1}}\frac{su_{1}(x+z)+s^{2}w_{s}(x+z)}{|z|^{n+2s}}\,dz.

Note that for any
\epsilon>0
, there exists
R=R(\epsilon)>1
such that

\displaystyle c_{n}\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{R}}u_{0}(x+z)\frac{1}{|z|^{n}}\big(\frac{1}{|z|^{2s}}-1\big)\,dz\Big|

\displaystyle\leq c_{n}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{R}}|u_{0}(x+z)|\frac{1}{|z|^{n}}\,dz

\displaystyle\leq c_{n}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{R}}|u_{0}(x+z)|^{q}dz\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{R}}|z|^{-nq^{\prime}}dz\Big)^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|u_{0}\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}R^{-n+\frac{n}{q^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\leq\epsilon.

Moreover, since

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{R}}|u_{0}(x+z)|\frac{1}{|z|^{n}}\,dz

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|u_{0}(x-z)|(1_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R}}|z|^{-n})\,dz

(3.8)

\displaystyle=|u_{0}|\ast(1_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R}}|\cdot|^{-n}),

we have then

\displaystyle\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{R}}\Big|c_{n}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{R}}u_{0}(x+z)\frac{1}{|z|^{n}}\big(\frac{1}{|z|^{2s}}-1\big)\,dz\Big|^{p}dx\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq\||u_{0}|\ast((1_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R}}|\cdot|^{-n}))\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

(3.9)

\displaystyle\leq\|u_{0}\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\|1_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R}}|\cdot|^{-n}\|_{L^{\tau}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle=C\|u_{0}\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}R^{-n+\frac{n}{\tau}}

\displaystyle\leq\epsilon,

where we used the Young’s inequality for
p\in(q,+\infty)

and

\tau>1
verifies that

\frac{1}{\tau}=1+\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}.

Now for the fixed
R>0
above, we have that

\displaystyle c_{n}\Big|\int_{B_{R}\setminus B_{1}}u_{0}(x+z)\frac{1}{|z|^{n}}\big(\frac{1}{|z|^{2s}}-1\big)\,dz\Big|

\displaystyle\leq c_{n}s\int_{B_{R}\setminus B_{1}}\frac{|u_{0}(x+z)|}{|z|^{n}}(2\ln|z|)dz

\displaystyle\leq c_{n}s(2\ln R)\int_{B_{R}\setminus B_{1}}\frac{|u_{0}(x+z)|}{|z|^{n}}dz.

Similar to the above computations, we have that

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}\setminus B_{1}}\frac{|u_{0}(x+z)|}{|z|^{n}}dz

\displaystyle\leq C\|u_{0}\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\big(1-R^{-nq^{\prime}+n}\big)^{\frac{1}{q}^{\prime}}\leq C\|u_{0}\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

and

\displaystyle\Big\|\int_{B_{R}\setminus B_{1}}\frac{|u_{0}(x+z)|}{|z|^{n}}dz\Big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle\leq\|u_{0}\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\big\|1_{B_{R}\setminus B_{1}}|\cdot|^{-n}\big\|_{L^{\tau}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle\leq C\|u_{0}\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

Consequently,

\lim_{s\to 0^{+}}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Big|c_{n}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{1}}u_{0}(x+z)\frac{1}{|z|^{n}}\Big(\frac{1}{|z|^{2s}}-1\Big)\,dz\Big|=0

and

\lim_{s\to 0^{+}}\Big\|c_{n}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{1}}u_{0}(\cdot+z)\frac{1}{|z|^{n}}\Big(\frac{1}{|z|^{2s}}-1\Big)\,dz\Big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=0.

Now we deal with the term
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{1}}\frac{su_{1}(x+z)+s^{2}w_{s}(x+z)}{|z|^{n+2s}}\,dz
. Note that

\displaystyle d_{n}(s)\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{1}}\frac{su_{1}(x+z)+s^{2}w_{s}(x+z)}{|z|^{n+2s}}\,dz\Big|

\displaystyle\leq Cs\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{1}}\frac{|u_{1}(x+z)|+s|w_{s}(x+z)|}{|z|^{n}}dz

\displaystyle\leq Cs\Big(\|u_{1}\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\|sw_{s}\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\Big)\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{1}}\Big(\frac{1}{|z|^{nq^{\prime}}}dz\Big)^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\to 0\quad{\rm uniformly\,in}\ \,x\ \,{\rm as}\ \,s\to 0^{+}

and

\displaystyle d_{n}(s)\Big\|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{1}}\frac{su_{1}(x+z)+s^{2}w_{s}(x+z)}{|z|^{n+2s}}\,dz\Big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle\leq Cs\Big\|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{1}}\frac{|u_{1}(x+z)|+s|w_{s}(x+z)|}{|z|^{n}}dz\Big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle\leq Cs\Big(\|u_{1}\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+s\|w_{s}\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\Big)

\displaystyle\to 0\quad{\rm as}\ \,s\to 0^{+},

where we have used in the second inequality the Young’s inequality for
p\in(q,+\infty)
as in (
3.9
).
Similar proof could be done for
c_{n}\big(\rho_{n}s+\bar{d}(s)\big)\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{1}}\frac{u_{0}(x+z)}{|z|^{n+2s}}dz
.

Thus, we obtain that for
p\in(q,+\infty)

\displaystyle\lim_{s\to 0^{+}}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}|A_{2}(s,x)|=0\quad{\rm and}\quad\lim_{s\to 0^{+}}\|A_{2}(s,\cdot)\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=0.

(3.10)

Part 3:
we deal with the last term

\displaystyle A_{3}(s,x):

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{s}\bigg(c_{n,s}\int_{B_{1}}\frac{u_{s}(x)-u_{s}(x+z)}{|z|^{n+2s}}\,dz-c_{n}s\int_{B_{1}}\frac{u_{0}(x)-u_{0}(x+z)}{|z|^{n}}\,dz\bigg)

\displaystyle=d_{n}(s)\int_{B_{1}}\frac{u_{0}(x)-u_{0}(x+z)+s\big(u_{1}(x)-u_{1}(x+z)\big)}{|z|^{n+2s}}\,dz

\displaystyle\quad+d_{n}(s)\int_{B_{1}}\frac{s^{2}\big(w_{s}(x)-w_{s}(x+z)\big)}{|z|^{n+2s}}\,dz-c_{n}\int_{B_{1}}\frac{u_{0}(x)-u_{0}(x+z)}{|z|^{n}}\,dz

\displaystyle=c_{n}\int_{B_{1}}\big(u_{0}(x)-u_{0}(x+z)\big)\Big(\frac{1}{|z|^{n+2s}}-\frac{1}{|z|^{n}}\Big)\,dz

\displaystyle\quad+c_{n}\big(\rho_{n}s+\bar{d}(s)\big)\int_{B_{1}}\frac{u_{0}(x)-u_{0}(x+z)}{|z|^{n+2s}}dz

\displaystyle\quad+d_{n}(s)\int_{B_{1}}\frac{s\big(u_{1}(x)-u_{1}(x+z)\big)+s^{2}\big(w_{s}(x)-w_{s}(x+z)\big)}{|z|^{n+2s}}\,dz.

Note that for any
\epsilon>0
, there exists
\delta_{\epsilon}\in(0,1)
such that for
s\in(0,s_{0})

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{B_{\delta_{\epsilon}}}\big(u_{0}(x)-u_{0}(x+z)\big)\Big(\frac{1}{|z|^{n+2s}}-\frac{1}{|z|^{n}}\Big)\,dz\Big|

\displaystyle\leq 2\int_{B_{\delta_{\epsilon}}}\big|u_{0}(x)-u_{0}(x+z)\big|\frac{1}{|z|^{n+2s}}\,dz

\displaystyle\leq C|\nabla u_{0}(x)|\int_{B_{\delta_{\epsilon}}}\frac{1}{|z|^{n+2s-1}}\,dz

\displaystyle\leq C|\nabla u_{0}(x)|\,\delta_{\epsilon}^{1-2s}

which implies that
for
q>1

\displaystyle\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Big|\int_{B_{\delta_{\epsilon}}}\big(u_{0}(x)-u_{0}(x+z)\big)\Big(\frac{1}{|z|^{n+2s}}-\frac{1}{|z|^{n}}\Big)\,dz\Big|^{q}dx\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}\leq C\|\nabla u_{0}\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\delta_{\epsilon}^{1-2s}\leq\epsilon.

Similarly, we have

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{B_{\delta_{\epsilon}}}\frac{s\big(u_{1}(x)-u_{1}(x+z)\big)+s^{2}\big(w_{s}(x)-w_{s}(x+z)\big)}{|z|^{n+2s}}dz\Big|\leq C\big(s|\nabla u_{1}(x)|+s^{2}|\nabla w_{s}(x)|\big)\delta_{\epsilon}^{1-2s},

and

\displaystyle\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Big|\int_{B_{\delta_{\epsilon}}}

\displaystyle\frac{s\big(u_{1}(x)-u_{1}(x+z)\big)+s^{2}\big(w_{s}(x)-w_{s}(x+z)\big)}{|z|^{n+2s}}dz\Big|^{q}dx\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\leq C\Big(s\|\nabla u_{1}\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+s^{2}\|\nabla w_{s}\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\Big)\delta_{\epsilon}^{1-2s}

\displaystyle\leq\epsilon.

For fixed
\delta_{\epsilon}
, it follows
that there exists
s_{1}>0
small enough such that
\forall\,s\in(0,s_{1})
,

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{B_{1}\setminus B_{\delta_{\epsilon}}}\big(u_{0}(x)-u_{0}(x+z)\big)

\displaystyle\Big(\frac{1}{|z|^{n+2s}}-\frac{1}{|z|^{n}}\Big)\,dz\Big|

\displaystyle\leq sC|\nabla u_{0}(x)|\int_{B_{1}\setminus B_{\delta_{\epsilon}}}\frac{1}{|z|^{n-1}}\ln\frac{1}{|z|}\,dz

\displaystyle\leq sC|\nabla u_{0}(x)|\frac{1}{\delta_{\epsilon}^{n-1}}\ln\frac{1}{\delta_{\epsilon}}\leq\epsilon,

where we used the following fact: the inequality
\frac{1}{r^{2s}}-1\leq\ln\frac{1}{r^{4s}}
holds for small
s>0
and
\forall\;r\in(\delta_{\epsilon},1)
.
Moreover it follows that for
q\in(1,+\infty)
,

\displaystyle\Big\|\int_{B_{1}\setminus B_{\delta_{\epsilon}}}\big(u_{0}(x)-u_{0}(x+z)\big)

\displaystyle\Big(\frac{1}{|z|^{n+2s}}-\frac{1}{|z|^{n}}\Big)\,dz\Big\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

The left proof is similar.

Consequently, we derive that

(3.11)

2\big(1-\frac{n}{4}k\big)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}u^{2}dx=0.

(6.5)

So when
k\not=\frac{4}{n}
, we have that
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}u^{2}dx=0
, which is impossible by our assumption that
u
is nonzero.
\Box

Corollary 6.1

Let
p\in(0,1]
and
u\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap L^{p+1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap\mathcal{D}^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be a solution of

\mathcal{L}_{\Delta}u=|u|^{p-1}u\quad\ {\rm in}\ \,\mathbb{R}^{n}.

(6.6)

Then
u\equiv 0
.

Proof.
From (
6.2
), we see that

n\big(\frac{2}{p+1}-1\big)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|u|^{p+1}dx+2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}u^{2}dx=0,

which implies that
u=0
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
\Box

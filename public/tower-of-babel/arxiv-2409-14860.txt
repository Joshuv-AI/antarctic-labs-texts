1.
Introduction

Throughout the paper, let
(A,\mathfrak{m})
be a Cohen-Macaulay local ring of dimension
d\geq 1
with infinite residue field and
I
an
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideal. A celebrated result of Rossi
[
17
]
gives a linear bound for the reduction number of
I
in terms of multiplicity and the first Hilbert coefficient of
I
when
d=2
, see (
1
). It is a conjecture to establish the same bound in higher dimensions which is the primary motivation of this paper. In
[
9
]
and
[
21
]
, quadratic bounds, involving the second and the third Hilbert coefficients of
I
were given in dimension three . Authors in
[
21
]
observed that the “good behaviour of the Ratliff-Rush filtration” plays a crucial role in this context. This paper further builds upon the ideas of
[
21
]
in order to obtain computable conditions for the above observation and consequently makes progress in the direction of Rossi’s conjecture.

Let
\mathcal{I}
be an
I
-admissible filtration. Recall that
an
I
-admissible filtration
\mathcal{I}=\{I_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
is a sequence of ideals such that
(i)

I_{n+1}\subseteq I_{n}
,

(ii)

I_{m}I_{n}\subseteq I_{m+n}
and
(iii)~I^{n}\subseteq I_{n}\subseteq I^{n-k}
for some
k\in\mathbb{N}
. The Hilbert coefficients of
\mathcal{I}
are the unique integers
e_{i}(\mathcal{I})
,
0\leq i\leq d,
such that the function
H_{\mathcal{I}}(n):=\ell{(R/I_{n})}
coincides with the following polynomial for
n\gg 0:

P_{\mathcal{I}}(x)=e_{0}(\mathcal{I}){x+d-1\choose d}-e_{1}(\mathcal{I}){x+d-2\choose d-1}+\cdots+(-1)^{d}e_{d}(\mathcal{I}).

Here,
\ell(*)
denotes the length function. The function
H_{\mathcal{I}}(n)
and the polynomial
P_{\mathcal{I}}(x)
are known as the Hilbert-Samuel function and the Hilbert-Samuel polynomial of
\mathcal{I}
, respectively. We refer to
[
19
]
for details. For
\mathcal{I}=\{I^{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
, we write
e_{i}(I)
instead of
e_{i}(\mathcal{I})
. A
reduction
of
\mathcal{I}
is an ideal
J\subseteq I_{1}
such that
JI_{n}=I_{n+1}
for
n\gg 0
and it is called a
minimal reduction
if it is minimal with respect to containment among all reductions. If the residue field
R/\mathfrak{m}
is infinite, then a minimal reduction of
\mathcal{I}
exists and is generated by
d
elements. Minimal reductions are important in the study of Hilbert functions and blow-up algebras. For a minimal reduction
J
of
\mathcal{I}
, we define

r_{J}(\mathcal{I})=\sup\{n\in\mathbb{Z}\mid I_{n}\not=JI_{n-1}\}\mbox{ and }r(\mathcal{I})=\min\{r_{J}(\mathcal{I})~|~J\mbox{ is a minimal reduction of }\mathcal{I}\}.

The above numbers are known as the
reduction number of
\mathcal{I}
with respect to
J
and the
reduction number of
\mathcal{I}
respectively. When
\mathcal{I}
is the
I
-adic filtration
\{I^{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
, we write
r_{J}(I)
and
r(I)
in place of
r_{J}(\mathcal{I})
and
r(\mathcal{I})
respectively. If
J
is a reduction of
I
, then
R[It]
is module-finite over
R[Jt]
and the reduction number is the largest degree of the elements in a minimal homogeneous generating set of the ring
R[It]
over
R[Jt].

Suppose
d\leq 2
. Rossi
[
17
, Corollary 1.5]
proved that, for a minimal reduction
J\subseteq I
,

(1)

r_{J}(I)\leq e_{1}(I)-e_{0}(I)+\ell(A/I)+1.

1

0ptG_{I}(A)\geq d-2

[

or if
e_{1}(I)-e_{0}(I)+\ell(A/I)=1

[

, Theorem 3.1]
. Another case is when

is of codimension
3
generated by five quadrics
[
5

. We prove the following theorem.

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose

If the Ratliff-Rush filtration of
I
behaves well modulo a superficial sequence

, then
r_{J}(I)\leq e_{1}(I)-e_{0}(I)+\ell(A/I)+1

\mathcal{I}

\widetilde{\mathcal{I}}=\{\widetilde{I_{n}}=\mathop{\bigcup}\limits_{t\geq 0}(I_{n+t}:I^{t})\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}

-admissible filtration, see

and

This definition can be extended inductively, see Definition

. This notion, introduced in
[
14
]
by Puthenpurakal, has been the main tool for solving Itoh’s conjecture in the work of Puthepnurakal,
[

]
and
[
16

Now suppose

and

In

-primary ideal

Further, in

We refer to the value

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
(A,\mathfrak{m})
be a Cohen-Macaulay local ring of dimension

,
I
an
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideal. If either of the following two conditions holds:

r_{J}(I)\leq e_{1}(I)-e_{0}(I)+\ell(A/I)+1.

The above theorem indicates that in higher dimensions, aiming to find a bound which also involves higher Hilbert coefficients could be a useful approach for solving the conjecture on Rossi’s bound. In the further theorems, we show that the error terms of (

For an
I
-admissible filtration
\mathcal{I}
, we write
G_{\widetilde{\mathcal{I}}}(A)=\displaystyle{\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}}\frac{\widetilde{I_{n}}}{\widetilde{I_{n+1}}}
for the associated graded ring of

.

Let

and

- primary ideal. Suppose

Suppose

Suppose

3.
Good behaviour of the Ratliff-Rush filtration

Suppose
(A,\mathfrak{m})
is a Cohen-Macaulay local ring of dimension
d\geq 3.
Marley
[
8
]
, proved that if
0ptG_{\mathcal{I}}(A)\geq d-1
, then

for

and
e_{i}(\mathcal{I})=0

As evident by Example

In Theorem

\mathcal{I}.
This condition has interesting consequences which is the main content of this section.

We first recall that the Ratliff-Rush filtration
\widetilde{\mathcal{I}}
of an
I
-admissible filtration

is defined by letting

\widetilde{I_{n}}=\mathop{\bigcup}\limits_{k\geq 0}(I_{n+k}:I^{k}).

Suppose

\mathcal{I}
, then it is easy to see that
\widetilde{I_{n}}A^{\prime}\subseteq\widetilde{I_{n}A^{\prime}}
for all
n\geq 0
and equality holds for

where
A^{\prime}=A/(x).
Moreover, the equality for all
n\geq 0,
is a very interesting condition. We will discuss that it is weaker than
0ptG_{\mathcal{I}}(A)\geq 1
, yet it enforces many nice properties. This notion, for

-adic filtration, was first considered in the papers of Puthenpurakal
[
13
]
,
[
14
]
and was further considered in
[
10
]
,
[
15
]
,
[

]
. We generalize the definition from

]

- admissible filtration.

Definition 3.1
.

Let

be a superficial sequence for
\mathcal{I}
. Then, we say that

\mathcal{I}
behaves well modulo

if
\widetilde{I_{n}}A^{\prime}=\widetilde{I_{n}A^{\prime}}

for all

and

(ii)

the Ratliff-Rush filtration of
\mathcal{I}
behaves well modulo

if the Ratliff-Rush filtration of

in

, where

(i)

Suppose

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
(A,\mathfrak{m})
be a
d\geq 4
-dimensional Cohen-Macaulay local ring,
I
an
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideal and
\mathcal{I}=\{I_{n}\}
an
I
-admissible filtration. If
e_{3}(\mathcal{I})=e_{2}(\mathcal{I})(e_{2}(\mathcal{I})-1)
and
e_{k}(\mathcal{I})=0
for
4\leq k\leq d-1
then
0pt{G}_{\widetilde{\mathcal{F}^{\prime}}}(A^{\prime})\geq d-2
where
\mathcal{F}=\{\widetilde{I_{n}}\}_{n\geq 0}
and
{\mathcal{F}^{\prime}}=\{\frac{\widetilde{I_{n}}+(x_{1})}{(x_{1})}\}_{n\geq 0}
for a superficial element
x_{1}
of
\mathcal{I}.

Proof.

We use induction on
d
. First, assume
d=4
.
Suppose
J=(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4})
is a minimal reduction of
\mathcal{I}
. We write
A^{\prime}=A/(x_{1})
and
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}=\Big\{\frac{\widetilde{I_{n}}+(x_{1})}{(x_{1})}\Big\}_{n\geq 0}
. Then
e_{i}(\mathcal{F}^{\prime})=e_{i}(\mathcal{I})
for
0\leq i\leq 3.
Thus
e_{3}(\mathcal{F}^{\prime})=e_{2}(\mathcal{F}^{\prime})(e_{2}(\mathcal{F}^{\prime})-1).
By Theorem
4.1
, we obtain that
0ptG_{\widetilde{\mathcal{F}^{\prime}}}(A^{\prime})\geq 2.

Now we prove the result for
d\geq 5
and assume that it holds true for dimension
d-1
.
Suppose
J=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d})
is a minimal reduction of
\mathcal{I}
. Set
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}=\Big\{\frac{\widetilde{I_{n}}+(x_{1})}{(x_{1})}\Big\}_{n\geq 0}
which is an
I/(x_{1})
-admissible filtration in
A^{\prime}=A/(x_{1})
. For convenience, we write
\mathcal{J}
for the filtration
\widetilde{(\mathcal{F}^{\prime})}
. By induction hypothesis,

(17)

0ptG_{\widetilde{{\mathcal{J}}^{\prime}}}(A^{\prime\prime})\geq d-3.

where
A^{\prime\prime}=A/(x_{1},x_{2})
and
\mathcal{J}^{\prime}
is the filtration
\Big\{\frac{\mathcal{J}_{n}+(x_{2})}{(x_{2})}\Big\}_{n\geq 0}
in
A^{\prime\prime}.

Note that
\dim A^{\prime\prime}=d-2
and
0ptG_{\mathcal{J}}(A^{\prime})\geq 1
implies
e_{d-1}(\mathcal{J}^{\prime})=e_{d-1}(\mathcal{J})
by Singh’s formula. Further,
e_{d-1}(\mathcal{J})=e_{d-1}(\mathcal{I})
which is given to be zero. By
[
14
, 1.5]
, we obtain

(18)

0\leq\sum_{n\geq 0}\ell\left(\frac{\widetilde{{\mathcal{J}}^{\prime}_{n+1}}}{{\mathcal{J}}^{\prime}_{n+1}}\right)=(-1)^{d-1}\big(e_{d-1}(\mathcal{J}^{\prime})-{e}_{d-1}(\widetilde{{\mathcal{J}}^{\prime}})\big)\\
=(-1)^{d}{e}_{d-1}(\widetilde{{\mathcal{J}}^{\prime}}).

On the other hand, since
0ptG_{\widetilde{{\mathcal{J}}^{\prime}}}(A^{\prime\prime})\geq d-3
,
by
[
1
, Proposition 4.4]
, we have
for all
n\geq-1
,

(19)

P_{\widetilde{{\mathcal{J}}^{\prime}}}(n)-H_{\widetilde{{{\mathcal{J}}^{\prime}}}}(n)=(-1)^{d-2}\ell((H^{d-2}_{\mathcal{R}_{+}}(\mathcal{R}(\widetilde{{{\mathcal{J}}^{\prime}}})))_{n+1}).

Now, taking the sum for
m\gg 0
on both sides of the above equation, we get

\displaystyle(-1)^{d-2}\sum_{n=0}^{m}\ell((H^{d-2}_{\mathcal{R}_{+}}(\mathcal{R}(\widetilde{{{\mathcal{J}}^{\prime}}})))_{n+1})

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{m}P_{\widetilde{{\mathcal{J}}^{\prime}}}(n)-\sum_{n=0}^{m}H_{\widetilde{{{\mathcal{J}}^{\prime}}}}(n)

(20)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&=\sum_{n=0}^{m}P_{\widetilde{{\mathcal{J}}^{\prime}}}(n)-H^{2}_{\widetilde{{{\mathcal{J}}^{\prime}}}}(m)\\
&=\sum_{i=0}^{d-2}(-1)^{i}{e}_{i}(\widetilde{\mathcal{J}^{\prime}}){m+d-1-i\choose d-1-i}-P^{2}_{\widetilde{{{\mathcal{J}}^{\prime}}}}(m)\\
&=(-1)^{d}{e}_{d-1}(\widetilde{{\mathcal{J}}^{\prime}}).\end{split}

This gives

{e}_{d-1}(\widetilde{{\mathcal{J}}^{\prime}})\geq 0.

If
d
is odd, then using (
18
), we get
{e}_{d-1}(\widetilde{{\mathcal{J}}^{\prime}})=0.
Now we show the same holds even if
d
is even. We may assume that
d\geq 6
.
Since
A^{\prime\prime}
is a
(d-2)
-dimensional Cohen-Macaulay local ring, we have

\ell((H^{d-2}_{\mathcal{R}_{+}}(\mathcal{R}(\widetilde{{{\mathcal{J}}^{\prime}}})))_{n})\leq\ell((H^{d-2}_{\mathcal{R}_{+}}(\mathcal{R}(\widetilde{{{\mathcal{J}}^{\prime}}})))_{n-1})

for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
by
[
1
, Lemma 4.7]
. Now in equation (
19
), we substitute
n=-1
to get

\ell((H^{d-2}_{\mathcal{R}_{+}}(\mathcal{R}(\widetilde{{{\mathcal{J}}^{\prime}}})))_{0})={e}_{d-2}(\widetilde{{\mathcal{J}}^{\prime}})=e_{d-2}({\mathcal{J}}^{\prime})=e_{d-2}(\mathcal{J})=e_{d-2}(\mathcal{I})=0.

Thus,

{e}_{d-1}(\widetilde{{\mathcal{J}}^{\prime}})=\sum_{n=0}^{m}\ell((H^{d-2}_{\mathcal{R}_{+}}(\mathcal{R}(\widetilde{{{\mathcal{J}}^{\prime}}})))_{n+1})\leq\sum_{n=0}^{m}\ell((H^{d-2}_{\mathcal{R}_{+}}(\mathcal{R}(\widetilde{{{\mathcal{J}}^{\prime}}})))_{0})=0.

Hence
{e}_{d-1}(\widetilde{{\mathcal{J}}^{\prime}})=0.
Now, using
(
18
), we get
\widetilde{\mathcal{J}_{n+1}^{\prime}}=\mathcal{J}_{n+1}^{\prime}
for

. Therefore,

. Hence by Sally’s Machine
[

, Proposition 1.7]

Theorem 4.6
.

Let
(A,\mathfrak{m})
be a
d\geq 4
-dimensional Cohen-Macaulay local ring,
I
an
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideal and
\mathcal{I}=\{I_{n}\}

-admissible filtration. Suppose

and

(21)

(-1)^{d}e_{d}(\mathcal{I})\geq 0.

Furthermore, when equality holds in (
21
), then

.

Proof.

For convenience of notation, we write
\mathcal{F}=\{\widetilde{I_{n}}\}
. Let
x_{1}\in I
be a superficial element for
I
. Then
x_{1}
is also a superficial element for the filtration
\mathcal{F}
. Suppose

\mathcal{I}
. Let

and
{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}=\Big\{{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}_{n}=\frac{\widetilde{I_{n}}+(x_{1})}{(x_{1})}\Big\}_{n\geq 0}
. Since
0ptG_{\mathcal{F}}(A)\geq 1
, hence by Singh’s formula, we get
e_{d}(\mathcal{I})=e_{d}(\mathcal{F})=e_{d}(\mathcal{F}^{\prime}).

By
[
14
, 1.5]
,

(22)

(-1)^{d}\Big(e_{d}(\mathcal{I})-e_{d}(\widetilde{\mathcal{F}^{\prime})}\Big)=\sum_{n\geq 0}\ell\left(\frac{\widetilde{{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}_{n+1}}}{{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}_{n+1}}\right)\geq 0.

By Lemma
4.5
,
0ptG_{\widetilde{\mathcal{F}^{\prime}}}(A^{\prime})\geq d-2
. Hence using
[
1
, Proposition 4.4]
, we have
for all

P_{\widetilde{{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}}}(n)-H_{\widetilde{{{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}}}}(n)=(-1)^{d-1}\ell((H^{d-1}_{\mathcal{R}_{+}}(\mathcal{R}(\widetilde{{{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}}})))_{n+1}).

on both sides of the above equation, we get

\displaystyle(-1)^{d-1}\sum_{n=0}^{m}\ell((H^{d-1}_{\mathcal{R}_{+}}(\mathcal{R}(\widetilde{{{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}}})))_{n+1})

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{m}P_{\widetilde{{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}}}(n)-\sum_{n=0}^{m}H_{\widetilde{{{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}}}}(n)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&=\sum_{n=0}^{m}P_{\widetilde{{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}}}(n)-H^{2}_{\widetilde{{{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}}}}(m)\\
&=\sum_{i=0}^{d-1}(-1)^{i}e_{i}(\widetilde{\mathcal{F}^{\prime}}){m+d-i\choose d-i}-P^{2}_{\widetilde{{{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}}}}(m)\\
&=(-1)^{d+1}e_{d}(\widetilde{\mathcal{F}^{\prime}}).\end{split}

Since by (
22

, therefore

if

is even.
Suppose

is odd. Then since

is a
(d-1)
-dimensional Cohen-Macaulay local ring, we have

\ell((H^{d-1}_{\mathcal{R}_{+}}(\mathcal{R}(\widetilde{{{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}}})))_{n})\leq\ell((H^{d-1}_{\mathcal{R}_{+}}(\mathcal{R}(\widetilde{{{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}}})))_{n-1})

for all

[
1
, Lemma 4.7]
. Now in equation (
23
), we substitute

to get

\ell((H^{d-1}_{\mathcal{R}_{+}}(\mathcal{R}(\widetilde{{{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}}})))_{0})=e_{d-1}(\widetilde{\mathcal{F}^{\prime}})=e_{d-1}({\mathcal{F}}^{\prime})=e_{d-1}(\mathcal{F})=e_{d-1}(\mathcal{I}).

Thus,

Since it is given that

, we get

), we get

whether one can get the same conclusion assuming only the equality
\widetilde{I^{r_{J}(I)}}=I^{r_{J}(I)}
. In Theorem
5.2
, we give a partial answer of their question. First we prove a bound for
\widetilde{r}_{J}(\mathcal{I})
and the stability index
\rho(I)
which partially answers the questions raised in
[
18
]
.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
(A,\mathfrak{m})
be a Cohen-Macaulay local ring of dimension
d\geq 3
,
I
an
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideal and
\mathcal{I}
an
I
-admissible filtration. If either of the following two conditions holds:

(i)

e_{3}(\mathcal{I})=e_{2}(\mathcal{I})(e_{2}(\mathcal{I})-1)
,

(ii)

\mathcal{I}=\{I^{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
,
I
is integrally closed and
e_{3}(I)=e_{2}(I)(e_{2}(I)-e_{1}(I)+e_{0}(I)+\ell(A/I))
,

and
e_{k}(\mathcal{I})=0
for
4\leq k\leq d
.
Then the following holds:

(i)

\widetilde{r}_{J}(\mathcal{I})\leq r_{J}(\mathcal{I}).

(ii)

If
\widetilde{I_{r}}=I_{r}
for some
r\geq r_{J}(\mathcal{I})
, then
\rho(\mathcal{I})\leq r.
Furthermore,

\displaystyle\rho(\mathcal{I})\leq\begin{cases}r_{J}(\mathcal{I})&\text{ if }\widetilde{I_{r_{J}(\mathcal{I})}}=I_{r_{J}(I)}\\
r_{J}(\mathcal{I})+(-1)^{d+1}(e_{d+1}(\mathcal{I})-e_{d+1}(\widetilde{\mathcal{I}}))&\text{ otherwise. }\end{cases}

Proof.

(i)

Let
\underline{x}=x_{1},\ldots,x_{d-2}\in I
be a superficial sequence of
I
. By Theorems
4.6
and
4.9
,
0ptG_{\mathcal{F}}(A)\geq d-1
which gives
\widetilde{r}_{J}(\mathcal{I})=\widetilde{r}_{J/(\underline{x})}(\mathcal{I}/(\underline{x}))
, see
[
8
, Lemma 2.14]
. Note that the proof of
[
8
, Lemma 2.14]
works for filtration as well. By
[
9
, Proposition 2.1]
, we have
\widetilde{r}_{J/(\underline{x})}(\mathcal{I}/(\underline{x}))\leq r_{J/(\underline{x})}(\mathcal{I}/(\underline{x}))\leq r_{J}(\mathcal{I}).
Therefore,
\widetilde{r}_{J}(\mathcal{I})\leq r_{J}(\mathcal{I}).

(ii)

Suppose
\widetilde{I_{r}}=I_{r}
for some
r\geq r_{J}(\mathcal{I})
. It is enough to show that
\widetilde{I_{r+1}}=I_{r+1}.
Since
\widetilde{r}_{J}(\mathcal{I})\leq r_{J}(\mathcal{I})
. Then
I_{r+1}=JI_{r}=J\widetilde{I_{r}}=\widetilde{I_{r+1}}.
Therefore
\rho(\mathcal{I})\leq r.
For the next part, let
r=r_{J}(\mathcal{I})
. Assume

\widetilde{I_{r}}\neq I_{r}
, then
(-1)^{d+1}(e_{d+1}(\mathcal{I})-{e}_{d+1}(\widetilde{\mathcal{I}}))=\mathop{\sum}\limits_{n\geq 0}\ell(\widetilde{I_{n+1}}/I_{n+1})>0.
If
\rho(\mathcal{I})\leq r_{J}(\mathcal{I}),
then we are done. So, we may assume that
\rho(\mathcal{I})>r_{J}(\mathcal{I}).
Then, for all
r\leq n<\rho(\mathcal{I}),

\widetilde{I_{n}}\neq I_{n}.

To see this, suppose
\widetilde{I_{n}}=I_{n}
for some
n
with
r\leq n<\rho(\mathcal{I}),
then by above arguments, we have
\widetilde{I_{t}}=I_{t}
for all
t\geq n
, which implies
\rho(\mathcal{I})\leq n
, a contradiction.
Thus
\ell(\widetilde{I_{n}}/I_{n})\geq 1
for

r\leq n<\rho(\mathcal{I})

which gives

\displaystyle\rho(\mathcal{I})-r_{J}(\mathcal{I})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{n=r_{J}(\mathcal{I})-1}^{\rho(\mathcal{I})-2}\ell(\widetilde{I_{n+1}}/I_{n+1})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\rho(\mathcal{I})-2}\ell(\widetilde{I_{n+1}}/I_{n+1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{d+1}(e_{d+1}(\mathcal{I})-{e}_{d+1}(\widetilde{\mathcal{I}})).

∎

In particular, If
d
is odd in the above result, then either by Theorem
4.6
or by Theorem
4.9
,
0ptG_{\mathcal{F}}(A)\geq d-1
which implies
{e}_{d+1}(\widetilde{\mathcal{I}})=\displaystyle{\sum_{n\geq d}}\binom{n}{d}\ell(\widetilde{I_{n+1}}/J\widetilde{I_{n}})\geq 0
, see
[
19
, Theorem 2.5]
for the formula. Therefore, we get
\rho(\mathcal{I})\leq r_{J}(\mathcal{I})+e_{d+1}(\mathcal{I}).

In the following theorem, we give an affirmative answer of
[
11
, Question 4.4]
under the same hypothesis as in Theorem
5.1
in case of
I
-adic filtration. For the rest of this section, let
\mathcal{I}=\{I^{n}\}_{n\geq 0}.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let the hypothesis be same as in Theorem
5.1
. Suppose
\widetilde{I^{r_{J}(I)}}=I^{r_{J}(I)}
. Then

\operatorname{reg}G_{I}(A)=r_{J}(I).

Proof.

Set
r=r_{J}(I)
and
A_{j}=A/(x_{1},\ldots,x_{j-1})
for
2\leq j\leq d-1
with
A_{1}=A
where
x_{1},\ldots,x_{d-2}
is a superficial sequence for
I.

Then from Theorem
4.6
or from Theorem
4.9
, we have
0ptG_{\mathcal{F}}(A)\geq d-1
. Consequently,
by Sally’s machine,
0ptG_{\mathcal{F}A_{j}}(A_{j})\geq d-j\geq 1
for
2\leq j\leq d-1.
Hence
\widetilde{(\mathcal{F}A_{j})_{n}}=(\mathcal{F}A_{j})_{n}
for
2\leq j\leq d-1
and for all
n\geq 0
. In particular
\widetilde{(\mathcal{F}A_{j})_{r}}=(\mathcal{F}A_{j})_{r}
for
2\leq j\leq d-1
. Since it is given that
\widetilde{I^{r}}=I^{r}
, we get

\widetilde{I^{r}A_{j}}=I^{r}A_{j}

for
2\leq j\leq d-1
.
It follows from
[
11
, Theorem 4.3]
that
\operatorname{reg}R(I)=\operatorname{reg}G_{I}(A)=r_{J}(I)
.
∎

Theorem 5.3
.

Let the hypothesis be the same as in Theorem
5.1
. Then Rossi’s bound holds, i.e,
r_{J}(I)\leq e_{1}(I)-e_{0}(I)+\ell(A/I)+1.

Proof.

By Theorem
4.6
or by Theorem
4.9
, we have
0ptG_{\widetilde{I}}(A)\geq d-1
so using
[
17
, Theorem 1.3]
, we have
r_{J}(I)\leq\displaystyle{\sum_{n\geq 0}}\ell(\widetilde{I^{n+1}}/J\widetilde{I^{n}})-e_{0}(I)+\ell(A/I)+1=e_{1}(I)-e_{0}(I)+\ell(A/I)+1
.
∎

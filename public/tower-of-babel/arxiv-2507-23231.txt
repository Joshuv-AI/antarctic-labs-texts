Perfecting the Line Graph
Hartosh Singh Bal

Abstract
This paper introduces two canonical constructions that transform arbitrary finite graphs into perfect graphs: the symmetric lift HL 2 ′ ​ ( G ) \mathrm{HL}^{\prime}_{2}(G) , which is purely structural and label-invariant, and the ordered lift HL 2 ​ ( G ) \mathrm{HL}_{2}(G) , which depends explicitly on vertex labeling and encodes directional information. Both lifts arise as line graphs of bipartite double covers and are box-perfect. The symmetric lift HL 2 ′ ​ ( G ) \mathrm{HL}^{\prime}_{2}(G) forms a canonical 2-cover of the line graph L ⁡ ( G ) L(G) . This involution decomposes HL 2 ′ ​ ( G ) \mathrm{HL}^{\prime}_{2}(G) into symmetric and antisymmetric components: the symmetric part recovers L ⁡ ( G ) L(G) , while the antisymmetric part yields a signed graph L − ​ ( G ) L^{-}(G) , the antisymmetric line graph, with +1/-1 edges encoding consistent vs. crossed overlaps. Thus, all adjacency and Laplacian eigenvalues of L ⁡ ( G ) L(G) , with multiplicities, appear within those of HL 2 ′ ​ ( G ) \mathrm{HL}^{\prime}_{2}(G) , despite L ⁡ ( G ) L(G) typically not being a subgraph. For regular graphs such as Paley graphs, this yields infinite families of sparse, highly structured regular and box-perfect expanders that also retain large cliques. The lift retains much of the spectral expansion of the base while improving the combinatorial expansion. Much the same behavior is observed with random regular base graphs, allowing for the possibility of the study of box-perfect random regular graphs. Finally, we generalize these constructions to parameterized lifts HL r , d ​ ( G ) \mathrm{HL}_{r,d}(G) and HL r , d ′ ​ ( G ) \mathrm{HL}_{r,d}^{\prime}(G) defined on ordered r r -tuples connected by Hamming distance constraints, which structurally encode the base graph and remain box-perfect.

Introduction

Perfect graphs occupy a central place in graph theory. Their defining property—that in every induced subgraph, the chromatic number equals the clique number—makes them structurally elegant and algorithmically tractable
[
10
,
15
]
. Yet, despite their appeal, the known families of perfect graphs tend to fall into narrow categories: either highly regular (such as line graphs of bipartite graphs) or lacking expansion or combinatorial flexibility
[
3
,
8
]
.

In this paper, we introduce a versatile new framework that lifts arbitrary finite graphs into perfect graphs. These lifts employ a straightforward but powerful two-step process: first, we take a bipartite double cover of the original graph, and then we construct its line graph. By definition, this ensures that the resulting graphs are claw-free and free of odd-length induced cycles (odd holes), properties that directly imply perfection
[
4
,
3
,
8
]
. In fact, since they are line graphs of bipartite graphs, all the lifted graphs in this paper are not only perfect but also
box-perfect
—their stable set polytopes admit efficient linear optimization under box constraints, a property of key algorithmic significance
[
10
]
.

We present two distinct but complementary lifts:

•

the
ordered lift

\mathrm{HL}_{2}(G)
, which depends explicitly on labeling the vertices and captures directional relationships;

•

the
symmetric lift

\mathrm{HL}^{\prime}_{2}(G)
, which is purely structural and independent of vertex labels.

A central contribution of our paper is showing that the symmetric lift
\mathrm{HL}^{\prime}_{2}(G)
is a canonical
2
-cover of the line graph
L(G)
, via the identification of each pair
(u,v)\sim(v,u)
. The quotient graph
\mathrm{HL}^{\prime}_{2}(G)/{\sim}
is isomorphic to
L(G)
, and this covering relationship explains why all adjacency and Laplacian eigenvalues of
L(G)
, including multiplicities, appear within those of
\mathrm{HL}^{\prime}_{2}(G)
. Thus, the symmetric lift not only perfects the structure of the line graph but also fully retains its spectral content.

The symmetric lift further admits a natural decomposition under the involution that reverses directed edges, yielding an antisymmetric component: a signed graph
L^{-}(G)
, the antisymmetric line graph. This signed refinement encodes overlaps with +1 for shared tails or heads and -1 for crossed endpoints, completing the spectral picture via the multiset union
\mathrm{Spec}(\mathrm{HL}^{\prime}_{2}(G))=\mathrm{Spec}(L(G))\cup\mathrm{Spec}(L^{-}(G))
. Moreover, the pair
(L(G),L^{-}(G))
distinguishes graphs with isomorphic line graphs through sign patterns, refining Whitney’s theorem and enabling conflict-aware applications in optimization and expanders.

The ordered lift
\mathrm{HL}_{2}(G)
also guarantees perfection but encodes directional and labeling constraints. Each lift
\mathrm{HL}_{2}(G)
, under a fixed labeling, forms a perfect subgraph of the line graph
L(G)
and of the symmetric lift
\mathrm{HL}^{\prime}_{2}(G)
. The ordered lifts are structurally coherent, perfect subgraphs that retain directional semantics—useful in applications such as Label Cover and Unique Games.

One of the key theoretical outcomes of this framework is the construction of explicit families of perfect expanders. We show that when
G
is a regular expander—such as a Paley graph—the symmetric lift
\mathrm{HL}^{\prime}_{2}(G)
produces a new perfect graph that retains expansion, with a provably large spectral gap and has a large clique number. These constructions yield infinite families of sparse, algebraically defined perfect graphs that are simultaneously highly connected and structurally tractable—features not found in classical examples of perfect graphs.

We analyze these lifts explicitly in classical graph families. For complete graphs
K_{n}
, the symmetric lift
\mathrm{HL}^{\prime}_{2}(K_{n})
provides a new, regular, perfect refinement of the Johnson graphs
J(n,2)

[
12
]
. In the case of Paley graphs, the symmetric lift produces explicit, highly structured perfect graphs with provable spectral bounds
[
16
,
13
,
9
]
, and spectral expansion comparable to the base graph. Moreover, the lift actually improves the combinatorial expansion of the base even when accounting for the near-doubling of the degree.

This behaviour is also observed empirically in the case of random regular graphs of degree
d
on
n
vertices. The symmetric lift of these graphs is a degree
2d-2
box-perfect graph on
nd/2
vertices, which tends too preserve the spectral expansion of the base as n grows large while amplifying the combinatorial expansion.

In Section
7
, we generalize both constructions to parameterized lifts
\mathrm{HL}_{r,d}(G)
and
\mathrm{HL}^{\prime}_{r,d}(G)
. These broader families act on ordered
r
-tuples of vertices, defining adjacency through specified Hamming-distance conditions. The resulting infinite classes of perfect graphs exhibit richer combinational, algebraic, and spectral properties than classical examples, opening many avenues for further theoretical investigation and practical application.

Beyond their intrinsic combinational and spectral significance, the canonical lifts
\mathrm{HL}_{2}(G)
and
\mathrm{HL}^{\prime}_{2}(G)
introduced here also have notable implications beyond classical graph theory. In companion works, we explore two important applications of these constructions. First, the label-sensitive ordered lift
\mathrm{HL}_{2}(G)
is leveraged to provide tractable yet structurally rich host graphs for Label-Cover instances and dictatorship tests, enabling polynomial-time approximations and direct empirical assessment of the Unique Games Conjecture
[
1
]
. Second, we demonstrate in a separate work that iterative application of the symmetric lift
\mathrm{HL}^{\prime}_{2}(G)
can generate large-scale graph structures exhibiting robust quantum coherence, offering a novel combinational foundation for designing quantum memory architectures and interference-based quantum technologies
[
2
]
.

2
The Ordered Lift
{HL}_{2}(G)
and the Symmetric Lift
{HL}^{\prime}_{2}(G)

We now define two lifts of a labeled graph
G
, called the Ordered and Symmetric lifts. These lifts produce perfect graphs whose vertices correspond to edges in the base graph, with adjacency reflecting structure-preserving overlaps. Both lifts are constructed as line graphs of bipartite double covers of
G

[
3
,
10
]
.

Definition 1

(Ordered Lift
\mathrm{HL}_{2}(G)
)
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be a finite simple graph with an injective labeling
\phi:V\to\mathbb{N}
. Construct a bipartite graph
B_{\mathrm{HL}}(G)
as follows:

•

The vertex set of
B_{\mathrm{HL}}(G)
is
V^{\prime}\sqcup V^{\prime\prime}
, where
V^{\prime}=\{v^{\prime}:v\in V\}
and
V^{\prime\prime}=\{v^{\prime\prime}:v\in V\}
are two disjoint copies of the vertex set.

•

For each edge
\{u,v\}\in E
with
\phi(u)<\phi(v)
, include an edge from
u^{\prime}\in V^{\prime}
to
v^{\prime\prime}\in V^{\prime\prime}
.

The Asymmetric lift
\mathrm{HL}_{2}(G)
is the line graph
L(B_{\mathrm{HL}}(G))
of this bipartite graph.

This construction is isomorphic to the graph obtained by forming vertices from labelled edges, i.e. if the edge goes from
u
to
v
, then the new vertex is
uv
. Now join two such vertices, considered as a two-coordinate system, if they are separated by Hamming distance
1
, which is to say they agree on one of the two coordinates. The origin of the naming terminology for this lift lies in this definition, the ordered lift is also a Hamming Line lift.

Remark 1
.

Each vertex of
\mathrm{HL}_{2}(G)
corresponds to a directed edge
(u,v)
with
\phi(u)<\phi(v)
, and thus to an ordered pair
(u,v)
. Two such vertices are adjacent in
\mathrm{HL}_{2}(G)
if and only if the corresponding directed edges in
G
share an endpoint—i.e., either
v=x
or
u=y
for another edge
(x,y)
. This is precisely the condition that the ordered pairs
(u,v)
and
(x,y)
differ in exactly one coordinate, i.e., they have Hamming distance one. Hence, the lift graph is isomorphic to a subgraph of the Hamming graph on two-letter words over
V
, with adjacency defined by distance one.

Definition 2

(Symmetric Lift
\mathrm{HL}^{\prime}_{2}(G)
)
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be a finite simple undirected graph. Construct a bipartite graph
B_{\mathrm{HL}^{\prime}}(G)
as follows:

•

The vertex set is again
V^{\prime}\sqcup V^{\prime\prime}
, two disjoint copies of
V
.

•

For each edge
\{u,v\}\in E
, include two edges: one from
u^{\prime}\in V^{\prime}
to
v^{\prime\prime}\in V^{\prime\prime}
, and one from
v^{\prime}\in V^{\prime}
to
u^{\prime\prime}\in V^{\prime\prime}
.

The Symmetric lift
\mathrm{HL}^{\prime}_{2}(G)
is the line graph
L(B_{\mathrm{HL}^{\prime}}(G))
of this bipartite graph
[
4
]
.

The graphs
\mathrm{HL}_{2}(G)
and
\mathrm{HL}^{\prime}_{2}(G)
differ from the classical line graph. In particular,
\mathrm{HL}_{2}(G)
is direction-sensitive: two edges are adjacent in the lift only if their directionally ordered roles overlap consistently. This directional structure arises from the vertex labeling
\phi
and enables a faithful translation of relational asymmetry into graph-theoretic form
[
19
,
12
]
.

While the focus of this paper is graph-theoretic, these constructions have algorithmic and complexity-theoretic consequences. In particular, the ordered lift
\mathrm{HL}_{2}(G)
is label-sensitive, making it suitable for encoding constraints in problems like Label Cover and Unique Games.

2.1
Graph-Theoretic Properties of the Ordered Lift

Theorem 2.1.
Let
G
be a connected finite graph with an injective vertex labeling
\phi
, and suppose
G
contains a Hamiltonian cycle whose edges are consistently oriented by
\phi
(i.e., every edge
\{u,v\}
is directed from
u
to
v
when
\phi(u)<\phi(v)
). Then the ordered lift
\mathrm{HL}_{2}(G)
is connected.

Proof.

Let
C
be a Hamiltonian cycle in
G
, say with vertices
v_{0},v_{1},\dots,v_{n-1},v_{0}
in cyclic order. For each
i
, define the directed edge
e_{i}=(v_{i}\to v_{i+1})
according to the labeling
\phi
: if
\phi(v_{i})<\phi(v_{i+1})
, take the edge from
v_{i}
to
v_{i+1}
.

In the bipartite graph
B_{\mathrm{HL}}(G)
used to construct
\mathrm{HL}_{2}(G)
, each such directed edge
e_{i}
is represented by a vertex in the lift, and corresponds to an edge from
v_{i}^{\prime}\in V^{\prime}
to
v_{i+1}^{\prime\prime}\in V^{\prime\prime}
. Two consecutive edges
e_{i}
and
e_{i+1}
share either a codomain or domain vertex (i.e., one of
v_{i+1}^{\prime}
or
v_{i+1}^{\prime\prime}
) in the bipartite graph, so their corresponding vertices in
\mathrm{HL}_{2}(G)
are adjacent.

This produces a path (and in fact a cycle) in
\mathrm{HL}_{2}(G)
through all
e_{i}
, showing that the Hamiltonian cycle lifts to a connected subgraph of
\mathrm{HL}_{2}(G)
.

Now, since
G
is connected and all its edges are between vertices appearing in
C
, every additional edge in
G
shares a vertex with some
e_{i}
, and thus its corresponding vertex in
\mathrm{HL}_{2}(G)
is adjacent to that of some
e_{i}
. Hence,
\mathrm{HL}_{2}(G)
is connected.
∎

Theorem 2.2.
Let
G
be a finite 2-connected graph with injective vertex labeling
\phi
. Then the ordered lift
\mathrm{HL}_{2}(G)
is connected.

Proof.

We apply Whitney’s ear decomposition theorem
[
19
]
, which states that every 2-connected graph can be built from a cycle by successively adding ”ears”—paths whose endpoints lie in the existing subgraph and whose interior vertices are disjoint.

Base case:
Let the base cycle be
C_{0}
. By Theorem 2.1, the directed edges of
C_{0}
, oriented consistently via
\phi
, lift to a connected cycle in
\mathrm{HL}_{2}(G)
.

Inductive step:
Suppose the ordered lift of the graph obtained after adding ears
P_{1},\dots,P_{i-1}
is connected. Let
P_{i}
be the next ear with endpoints
v_{0}
,
v_{r}
in the existing graph, and internal vertices disjoint.

For each edge
(v_{j},v_{j+1})
in
P_{i}
, orient it according to the labeling
\phi
: from smaller to larger label. These edges then correspond to bipartite edges in
B_{\mathrm{HL}}(G)
, and hence to vertices in
\mathrm{HL}_{2}(G)
. Consecutive edges in
P_{i}
share a vertex, so their corresponding lifted vertices in
\mathrm{HL}_{2}(G)
are adjacent.

Moreover, the first edge of
P_{i}
shares a vertex with the existing lift, so the entire lifted path is connected to the current component. Thus, the lift remains connected after adding
P_{i}
.

By induction,
\mathrm{HL}_{2}(G)
is connected.
∎

2.2
Structure of the Symmetric Lift
{HL}^{\prime}_{2}(G)

Theorem 1.

Let
G
be a connected undirected graph. Then:

1.

If
G
is not bipartite, then
\mathrm{HL}^{\prime}_{2}(G)
is connected.

2.

If
G
is bipartite, then
\mathrm{HL}^{\prime}_{2}(G)
has exactly two connected components.

Proof.

(1) Suppose
G
is connected and not bipartite. Then
G
contains at least one cycle of odd length
[
12
]
. Let
e
and
f
be any two edges in
E(G)
. Since
G
is connected, there exists a path
P
from an endpoint of
e
to an endpoint of
f
, corresponding to a sequence of overlapping edges
e=e_{0},e_{1},\dots,e_{k}=f
, where each
e_{i}
and
e_{i+1}
share a vertex.

In the bipartite double cover
B_{\mathrm{HL}^{\prime}}(G)
, each edge in
G
is represented twice: once from
u^{\prime}\in V^{\prime}
to
v^{\prime\prime}\in V^{\prime\prime}
, and once from
v^{\prime}\in V^{\prime}
to
u^{\prime\prime}\in V^{\prime\prime}
. These create vertices in the lift
\mathrm{HL}^{\prime}_{2}(G)
, with adjacency determined by shared endpoints. Since edges in the path
P
share vertices in
G
, their corresponding lift vertices are adjacent in
\mathrm{HL}^{\prime}_{2}(G)
. The presence of an odd cycle in
G
ensures that no consistent bipartition is possible across all paths in the lift, allowing the full adjacency chain to connect.

Hence, all vertices in
\mathrm{HL}^{\prime}_{2}(G)
are connected via such paths, and the graph is connected.

(2) Now suppose
G
is bipartite, with vertex partition
V=A\sqcup B
. Then every edge in
G
connects a vertex in
A
to one in
B

[
12
]
.

In
B_{\mathrm{HL}^{\prime}}(G)
, each edge
\{u,v\}
corresponds to two edges: one from
u^{\prime}\to v^{\prime\prime}
and one from
v^{\prime}\to u^{\prime\prime}
. Since
G
is bipartite, edges can be assigned a parity based on their direction between parts: from
A\to B
or from
B\to A
.

This parity is preserved in
\mathrm{HL}^{\prime}_{2}(G)
: edges sharing a vertex in the base graph also preserve parity in the bipartite double cover. Hence, no path in
\mathrm{HL}^{\prime}_{2}(G)
connects an edge of one parity to an edge of the other. The lift splits into exactly two disconnected components corresponding to this parity. Therefore,
\mathrm{HL}^{\prime}_{2}(G)
has exactly two connected components.
∎

Theorem 2.

If
G
is
d
-regular, then
\mathrm{HL}^{\prime}_{2}(G)
is
(2d-2)
-regular.

Proof.

Let
e=\{u,v\}\in E(G)
. Since
G
is
d
-regular, each of
u
and
v
has degree
d
, including edge
e
.

In the bipartite graph
B_{\mathrm{HL}^{\prime}}(G)
, edge

gives rise to two lifted edges, each linking
u^{\prime}
to
v^{\prime\prime}
and
v^{\prime}
to
u^{\prime\prime}
. In the line graph
\mathrm{HL}^{\prime}_{2}(G)
, the vertex corresponding to

is adjacent to every other edge in
G
that shares either
u
or
v
, except
e
itself.

There are
d-1
other edges incident to
u
, and
d-1
incident to
v
, for a total of
2d-2
neighbors in the lift. Hence, every vertex in
\mathrm{HL}^{\prime}_{2}(G)
has degree
2d-2
, and the graph is regular
[
3
]
.
∎

2.3
Recoverability of the Base Structure from the Lifts

A key theoretical justification for using the canonical lifts
\mathrm{HL}_{2}(G)
and
\mathrm{HL}^{\prime}_{2}(G)
lies in their ability to encode the original graph
G
so completely that it can, in principle, be recovered from the lift itself—provided we know that the input is a simple graph with or without labeling.

Suppose
G=(V,E)
is a finite simple graph equipped with an injective labeling
\phi:V\to\mathbb{N}
. Each edge
\{u,v\}\in E
is directed from
u
to
v
if
\phi(u)<\phi(v)
. In
\mathrm{HL}_{2}(G)
, each vertex corresponds to such a directed edge
u\to v
, realized as an edge from
u^{\prime}\in V^{\prime}
to
v^{\prime\prime}\in V^{\prime\prime}
in the bipartite double cover
B_{\mathrm{HL}}(G)
.

Adjacency in the lift reflects overlaps of domain or codomain: two directed edges are adjacent in the lift if they share a vertex in the original graph
G
. This structure allows us to fully recover:

•

the vertex set
V
(up to relabeling),

•

the set of edges
E
, and

•

the labeling order used to define direction.

Thus,
\mathrm{HL}_{2}(G)
determines the labeled graph
G
uniquely up to isomorphism respecting the labeling. The map
G\mapsto\mathrm{HL}_{2}(G)
is injective on the class of labeled graphs
[
19
,
3
]
.

In contrast, the Symmetric lift
\mathrm{HL}^{\prime}_{2}(G)
encodes only the undirected edge structure of
G
. Each edge
\{u,v\}\in E
gives rise to two bipartite edges
u^{\prime}\to v^{\prime\prime}
and
v^{\prime}\to u^{\prime\prime}
, and adjacency in the lift reflects whether two such edges share a common endpoint in
G
.

Thus:

•

If
G
is an undirected graph, it can be reconstructed from
\mathrm{HL}^{\prime}_{2}(G)
up to isomorphism.

•

If
G
carried any labeling or direction, this structure is lost in
\mathrm{HL}^{\prime}_{2}(G)
, and full reconstruction is not possible.

These recoverability results reinforce the claim that the canonical lifts
\mathrm{HL}_{2}(G)
and
\mathrm{HL}^{\prime}_{2}(G)
are not merely perfect graphs but structurally faithful encodings of the underlying object.

Lemma 1
.

Let
G
be a graph on
n
vertices. Then the number of distinct ordered lifts
\mathrm{HL}_{2}(G)
, up to isomorphism, is at most

\frac{n!}{|\mathrm{Aut}(G)|},

where
\mathrm{Aut}(G)
is the automorphism group of
G
.

Proof.

Each labeling of the vertex set of
G
induces a specific ordered lift
\mathrm{HL}_{2}(G)
, which depends on the labeling. Two labelings yield isomorphic lifts if and only if they differ by an automorphism of
G
.

The number of labelings modulo automorphism is bounded above by the number of left cosets of
\mathrm{Aut}(G)
in the symmetric group
S_{n}
, which is
\frac{n!}{|\mathrm{Aut}(G)|}
.
∎

2.4
Structural Properties: Perfectness and Claw-Freeness

Both the ordered lift
\mathrm{HL}_{2}(G)
and the symmetric lift
\mathrm{HL}^{\prime}_{2}(G)
are constructed as line graphs of bipartite graphs. This guarantees that they inherit several strong structural properties, regardless of the base graph
G
or the choice of labeling.

Theorem 1
.

For any finite graph
G
, both
\mathrm{HL}_{2}(G)
and
\mathrm{HL}^{\prime}_{2}(G)
are perfect, claw-free, and odd-hole-free graphs.

By the Strong Perfect Graph Theorem
[
8
]
, the line graph of any bipartite graph is perfect: it contains no odd holes (induced cycles of length
\geq 5
) and no odd antiholes. Since both lifts are line graphs of bipartite constructions, they fall into this class
[
10
]
.

In addition, by Beineke’s classical characterization
[
3
]
, every line graph is claw-free, meaning it contains no induced subgraph isomorphic to
K_{1,3}
. Hence,
\mathrm{HL}_{2}(G)
and
\mathrm{HL}^{\prime}_{2}(G)
exclude claw configurations, simplifying their local structure.

Since the underlying bipartite graphs have no odd cycles, their line graphs (the lifts) are automatically free of odd holes of length greater than 3.

Actually the bi-partite nature of both the lift before taking the line graphs, gives us even more. Box-perfect graphs form a distinguished subclass of perfect graphs whose stable set polytopes admit efficient linear optimization even under box constraints. Formally, a graph is
box-perfect
if the system defining its stable set polytope is totally dual integral (TDI) when intersected with any box
[0,1]^{n}
. The
stable set polytope
of a graph
G=(V,E)
is the convex hull of the incidence vectors of all stable (i.e., independent) sets in
G

\mathrm{STAB}(G)=\mathrm{conv}\left\{\mathbf{1}_{S}\in\mathbb{R}^{V}\mid S\subseteq V\text{ is a stable set}\right\}.

This implies that, for any weight vector and upper bounds on vertex inclusion, the maximum weight stable set can be computed via linear programming. While all bipartite graphs and their line graphs are perfect, only the latter are known to be box-perfect in general. The relevance of box-perfectness lies in its rare combination of strong structural properties and algorithmic tractability, particularly in problems involving scheduling, packing, and constraint satisfaction
[
5
,
17
]
.

Theorem 2

(Box-Perfectness of the Lifts)
.

For any finite graph
G
, both
\mathrm{HL}_{2}(G)
and
\mathrm{HL}^{\prime}_{2}(G)
are
box-perfect
graphs. That is, for each, the stable set polytope admits a totally dual integral (TDI) description under box constraints, and linear optimization over the stable sets can be solved efficiently.

Proof.

Both
\mathrm{HL}_{2}(G)
and
\mathrm{HL}^{\prime}_{2}(G)
are constructed as line graphs of bipartite graphs. By a result of Chvátal and later refinements in polyhedral combinatorics
[
10
]
, line graphs of bipartite graphs are box-perfect. The conclusion follows.
∎

Theorem 3

(Clique Number of Symmetric Lifts)
.

Let

Proof.

Fix a vertex

∎

The perfection of the symmmetric lift yields:

Corollary 1

(Chromatic Number of Symmetric Lifts)

Let

The ordered lift
\mathrm{HL}_{2}(G)
preserves the full relational and directional data of labeled structures and can serve as a canonical encoding. The symmetric lift

Proposition 1
.

Let

be any finite undirected graph, and let
\mathrm{HL}_{2}(G)
be the ordered lift of

1.

The vertex set of
\mathrm{HL}_{2}(G)

Each vertex in

An edge in

∎

Since the construction of

2.5
Line Graphs and the two lifts

Proposition 2
.

For any labeled graph
G
, the ordered lift
\mathrm{HL}_{2}(G)
embeds as a subgraph of the symmetric lift
\mathrm{HL}^{\prime}_{2}(G)
via the map
(u,v)\mapsto(u^{\prime},v^{\prime\prime})
.

Proof.

Each vertex
(u,v)
in
\mathrm{HL}_{2}(G)
corresponds to a directed edge of
G
. In the symmetric lift
\mathrm{HL}^{\prime}_{2}(G)
, the vertices are bipartite-type pairs
(u^{\prime},v^{\prime\prime})
and
(v^{\prime},u^{\prime\prime})
for each undirected edge
\{u,v\}
.

Edges in
\mathrm{HL}_{2}(G)
connect
(u,v)
and
(v,w)
whenever
u\sim v\sim w
in
G
. These correspond to edges between
(u^{\prime},v^{\prime\prime})
and
(v^{\prime},w^{\prime\prime})
in
\mathrm{HL}^{\prime}_{2}(G)
. Thus, the image of
\mathrm{HL}_{2}(G)
under the map
(u,v)\mapsto(u^{\prime},v^{\prime\prime})
forms an induced subgraph of
\mathrm{HL}^{\prime}_{2}(G)
.
∎

Thus the two lifts in this paper, ordered and symmetric, bear a close relationship to the Line Graph, encapsulating the properties of the base graph in similar fashion while guaranteeing perfect lifts. But, unlike the line graph, each of the two lifts is a complete and unique encoding of the base graph.

It is illustrative to work out the most basic example which shows the failure of the line graph to encode the base graph in a unique fashion - using
K_{3}
and
K_{1,3}
. The line graphs of both are the same –
K_{3}
– while the symmetric lift yields
K_{6}
for
K_{3}
and
K_{3}+K_{3}
for
K_{1,3}
. The ordered lift for
K_{3}
is unique and is given by
P_{3}
while for
K_{1,3}
, depending on the labelling, it is either
P_{2}+P_{1}
or
K_{3}
. It is also clear that the subgraph inclusions of the ordered lifts in the line graph and the symmetric lift hold, while the line graph is not a subgraph of the symmetric lift.

Thus, the two canonical lifts introduced in this paper—
\mathrm{HL}_{2}(G)
and
\mathrm{HL}^{\prime}_{2}(G)
—encode edge relationships in
G
in structurally faithful and perfect ways. The ordered lift
\mathrm{HL}_{2}(G)
embeds as a subgraph of both the line graph
L(G)
and the symmetric lift
\mathrm{HL}^{\prime}_{2}(G)
, and reflects the labeling-dependent directional information of the original graph. The symmetric lift
\mathrm{HL}^{\prime}_{2}(G)
, by contrast, is label-invariant and always perfect, but its relationship to the classical line graph is more subtle.

While
L(G)
and
\mathrm{HL}^{\prime}_{2}(G)
share the same vertex set size (equal to twice the number of edges in
G
) and both encode edge-adjacency structures,
\mathrm{HL}^{\prime}_{2}(G)
is not merely a refinement or supergraph of
L(G)
in the usual sense. In fact,
L(G)
is typically neither an induced subgraph nor a quotient of
\mathrm{HL}^{\prime}_{2}(G)
, and the two differ in structure, perfection, and symmetry.

Although
\mathrm{HL}^{\prime}_{2}(G)
and
L(G)
differ in perfection and symmetry, they are deeply linked through a canonical 2-to-1 quotient. Each vertex of
\mathrm{HL}^{\prime}_{2}(G)
, viewed as a directed pair
(u,v)
, has a natural twin
(v,u)
, and the quotient by this pairing yields a graph isomorphic to
L(G)
. This quotient structure explains the spectral containment: the adjacency and Laplacian spectra of
L(G)
embed exactly (with multiplicity) in those of
\mathrm{HL}^{\prime}_{2}(G)
. While
L(G)
is typically not perfect, the symmetric lift repairs this flaw while preserving its spectral and structural features through this doubled and then quotiented view.

3
Spectral Containment: Line Graph in the Symmetric Lift

3.1
The Quotient by Twinning:
{HL}^{\prime}_{2}(G)/{\sim}\cong L(G)

We begin by establishing a fundamental structural result: the symmetric lift
\mathrm{HL}^{\prime}_{2}(G)
admits a canonical involution whose quotient is isomorphic to the classical line graph
L(G)
. This observation streamlines the analysis of spectrum and structure.

Theorem 4

(Quotient of the Symmetric Lift is the Line Graph)
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be a finite simple undirected graph. Let
\mathrm{HL}^{\prime}_{2}(G)
be its symmetric lift. Define an equivalence relation
\sim
on the vertex set of
\mathrm{HL}^{\prime}_{2}(G)
by identifying
(u,v)\sim(v,u)
for each edge
\{u,v\}\in E(G)
. Then:

\mathrm{HL}^{\prime}_{2}(G)/{\sim}\cong L(G)

That is, the quotient of the symmetric lift by this natural involution is canonically isomorphic to the line graph of
G
.

Proof.

By construction, the vertex set of
\mathrm{HL}^{\prime}_{2}(G)
consists of all ordered pairs
(u,v)
such that
\{u,v\}\in E(G)
. Each such pair corresponds to a directed version of the undirected edge
\{u,v\}
.

There is a canonical involution
\iota
defined by:

\iota:(u,v)\mapsto(v,u)

This involution is fixed-point free when
G
has no self-loops and partitions the vertex set of
\mathrm{HL}^{\prime}_{2}(G)
into orbits of size two. The quotient graph
Q=\mathrm{HL}^{\prime}_{2}(G)/{\sim}
has one vertex for each undirected edge
\{u,v\}\in E(G)
, namely the orbit
\{(u,v),(v,u)\}
.

Two such orbits
\{(u,v),(v,u)\}
and
\{(v,w),(w,v)\}
are adjacent in the quotient if and only if there is an edge in
\mathrm{HL}^{\prime}_{2}(G)
connecting some representative from the first orbit to some representative from the second. Since
\mathrm{HL}^{\prime}_{2}(G)
connects
(u,v)
to
(v,w)
whenever
u\neq w
, and similarly for the other pairs, this means the orbits are adjacent if and only if the corresponding undirected edges
\{u,v\}
and
\{v,w\}
share the vertex
v
.

This exactly matches the definition of adjacency in the line graph
L(G)
, where vertices represent edges of
G
, and two vertices are adjacent if their corresponding edges share an endpoint. Thus, the quotient graph is isomorphic to
L(G)
.

This identification respects both vertex sets (edge orbits in
G
) and adjacencies, establishing a canonical graph isomorphism:

\mathrm{HL}^{\prime}_{2}(G)/{\sim}\cong L(G)

∎

This quotient relationship provides a conceptual and algebraic bridge between the symmetric lift and the line graph. In particular,
\mathrm{HL}^{\prime}_{2}(G)
is a double cover of
L(G)
, with every edge in
L(G)
lifted symmetrically. This covering structure will be used in the next subsection to derive exact spectral containment results for both the adjacency and Laplacian matrices, using standard results in graph covering theory
[
14
,
12
,
11
]
.

3.2
Spectral Containment of the Line Graph in the Symmetric Lift

Theorem 5

(Spectral Decomposition via Quotient and Signed Quotient)
.

Let
G
be a finite, simple undirected graph. Then the adjacency spectrum of the symmetric lift
\mathrm{HL}^{\prime}_{2}(G)
is the multiset union of the adjacency spectrum of the line graph
L(G)
and that of a signed quotient graph
L^{-}(G)
defined by the antisymmetric representation under the involution
(u,v)\mapsto(v,u)
. That is,

\operatorname{Spec}(\mathrm{HL}^{\prime}_{2}(G))=\operatorname{Spec}(L(G))\cup\operatorname{Spec}(L^{-}(G)),

with eigenvalue multiplicities preserved.

Proof.

Recall that the symmetric lift
\mathrm{HL}^{\prime}_{2}(G)
has vertex set consisting of all ordered edge pairs
(u,v)
such that
\{u,v\}\in E(G)
, and that it is constructed as the line graph of a bipartite double cover
B
of
G
, as defined in Section 2.

Define an involution
\iota
acting on the vertices of
\mathrm{HL}^{\prime}_{2}(G)
by
\iota(u,v)=(v,u)
. This involution is fixed-point free and partitions the vertex set into disjoint orbits of size 2, since
G
has no self-loops.

We now apply standard results from spectral covering theory (see
[
14
]
, Chapter 11). Specifically, for a graph
X
with a fixed-point-free involutive automorphism
\iota
, the adjacency spectrum of
X
is the union of the spectra of two quotient graphs:

•

The
quotient graph

Q=X/\iota
, where each orbit
\{(u,v),(v,u)\}
becomes a single vertex, and two orbits are adjacent if there is any adjacency between representatives in
X
.

•

The
signed quotient graph

Q^{-}
, where the adjacency between orbits is modified by a sign depending on whether the involution swaps the endpoints of the corresponding edges (see
[
14
, Section 11.4]
).

In the case of
\mathrm{HL}^{\prime}_{2}(G)
, the unsigned quotient graph
Q
is precisely the line graph
L(G)
, since each undirected edge
\{u,v\}\in E(G)
corresponds to the orbit
\{(u,v),(v,u)\}
, and adjacency in
L(G)
matches adjacency of edge pairs in the base graph.

The signed quotient graph
L^{-}(G)
encodes the antisymmetric spectrum and may include negative edge weights or signed walks. Its spectrum, together with that of
L(G)
, completely determines the spectrum of the symmetric lift.

Therefore,

\operatorname{Spec}(\mathrm{HL}^{\prime}_{2}(G))=\operatorname{Spec}(L(G))\cup\operatorname{Spec}(L^{-}(G)),

as required.
∎

3.3
The Regular Case

Theorem 6

(Explicit Spectrum of the Symmetric Lift for Regular Graphs)
.

Let
G
be a finite, simple,
d
-regular graph with adjacency eigenvalues

\operatorname{Spec}(\mathrm{HL}^{\prime}_{2}(G))=\{d-2+\lambda_{i}\mid i=1,\dots,n\}\cup\{d-2-\lambda_{i}\mid i=1,\dots,n\}\cup\{-2\}^{n(d-2)}
. This totals
2n+n(d-2)=nd

Proof.

By Theorem
5
, the spectrum of
\mathrm{HL}^{\prime}_{2}(G)
is the multiset union of the spectra of the line graph
L(G)
and a signed quotient graph
L^{-}(G)
arising from the involution
(u,v)\mapsto(v,u)
on the symmetric lift.

For regular graphs, the spectrum of the line graph
L(G)
is given by:

\operatorname{Spec}(L(G))=\left\{d-2+\lambda_{i}\ \middle|\ i=1,\dots,n\right\},

as established in
[
14

and
[
11

. These eigenvalues arise from the identity
M^{\top}M=A(L(G))+2I

The signed quotient

contributes a second spectral family with the eigenvalues:

\operatorname{Spec}(L^{-}(G))=\left\{d-2-\lambda_{i}\ \middle|\ i=1,\dots,n\right\},

as shown in
[
14

Additionally, both
\operatorname{Spec}(L(G))
and
\operatorname{Spec}(L^{-}(G))
include the eigenvalue
-2

, as per
[

, yielding a total multiplicity of

for
-2

\operatorname{Spec}(\mathrm{HL}^{\prime}_{2}(G))

Therefore, the full spectrum of the symmetric lift is:

\operatorname{Spec}(\mathrm{HL}^{\prime}_{2}(G))=\operatorname{Spec}(L(G))\cup\operatorname{Spec}(L^{-}(G))=\left\{d-2\pm\lambda_{i}\ \middle|\ i=1,\dots,n\right\},

∎

3.4
Consequences for Expansion: Spectral Gap and Cheeger Bound

The explicit spectrum derived in Theorem
6
allows us to obtain tight expansion bounds for the symmetric lift
\mathrm{HL}^{\prime}_{2}(G)
when
G
is a
d
-regular graph. In particular, we can deduce the spectral gap and apply the Cheeger inequality to estimate edge expansion.

Corollary 2

(Spectral Gap of
\mathrm{HL}^{\prime}_{2}(G)
for Random Regular Graphs)
.

Let
G
be a random
d
-regular graph on
n
vertices, and let
H=\mathrm{HL}^{\prime}_{2}(G)
. Then with high probability as
n\to\infty
, the spectral gap of
H
satisfies:

\mathrm{Gap}(H):=\lambda_{1}(H)-\lambda_{2}(H)\geq d-2\sqrt{d-1}-o(1),

where
\lambda_{1}(H)=2d-2
is the largest eigenvalue of
H
.

Proof.

From Theorem
6
, the eigenvalues of
H=\mathrm{HL}^{\prime}_{2}(G)
are of the form
d-2\pm\lambda_{i}
, where
\lambda_{i}
are the eigenvalues of the base graph
G
. Thus,

\lambda_{1}(H)=d-2+\lambda_{1}(G)=2d-2,

and

\lambda_{2}(H)=\max\left(d-2+\lambda_{2}(G),\;d-2-\lambda_{n}(G)\right).

By Friedman’s theorem
[
13
]
, for a random
d
-regular graph
G
, we have with high probability:

|\lambda_{2}(G)|,\ |\lambda_{n}(G)|\leq 2\sqrt{d-1}+o(1).

Hence,

\lambda_{2}(H)\leq d+2\sqrt{d-1}-2+o(1),

so the gap satisfies:

\mathrm{Gap}(H)=\lambda_{1}(H)-\lambda_{2}(H)\geq d-2\sqrt{d-1}-o(1),

as claimed.
∎

Corollary 3

(Cheeger Bound for
\mathrm{HL}^{\prime}_{2}(G)
)
.

Let
G
be a random
d
-regular graph on
n
vertices, and let
H=\mathrm{HL}^{\prime}_{2}(G)
be its symmetric lift. Then with high probability as
n\to\infty
, the Cheeger constant
h(H)
satisfies:

h(H)\geq\frac{1}{2}\left(d-2\sqrt{d-1}-o(1)\right).

Proof.

By the Cheeger inequality for
d^{\prime}
-regular graphs (see
[
9
]
), where
d^{\prime}=\deg(H)=2d-2
, we have:

h(H)\geq\frac{1}{2}\cdot\mathrm{Gap}(H).

Substituting from the previous corollary:

h(H)\geq\frac{1}{2}\left(d-2\sqrt{d-1}-o(1)\right),

which completes the proof.
∎

Empirical results show that the Cheeger ratio
h(HL^{\prime}_{2}(G))/h(G)
remains consistently greater than 1, indicating that the symmetric lift not only preserves but amplifies combinatorial expansion relative to the base graph.

In contrast,

4
The Antisymmetric Quotient: Signed Refinement of
\mathrm{L}(G)

The symmetric lift
\mathrm{HL}^{\prime}_{2}(G)
admits a natural involution
\sigma:(u,v)\mapsto(v,u)
on its vertex set, which corresponds to reversing directed edges in the base graph
G
. This symmetry allows us to decompose its structure and spectrum into two orthogonal components:

•

A
symmetric component
, corresponding to functions invariant under
\sigma
. These yield the classical line graph
L(G)
as the quotient
\mathrm{HL}^{\prime}_{2}(G)/\sim
.

•

An
antisymmetric component
, corresponding to functions that change sign under
\sigma
. These give rise to a signed graph, denoted
L^{-}(G)
, which we call the
antisymmetric line graph
.

This antisymmetric line graph refines the line graph by recording not just whether two edges in
G
are adjacent, but also how they overlap. The signed adjacency matrix
M
for
L^{-}(G)
is defined combinatorially as follows:

Let
G=(V,E)
be a finite, simple, undirected graph with a total ordering on
V
. For each edge
\{u,v\}\in E
with
u<v
, define the oriented edge
(u,v)
. Let
E^{\rightarrow}
denote the set of such oriented edges. Define a symmetric matrix
M\in\mathbb{R}^{|E|\times|E|}
indexed by
E^{\rightarrow}
, with entries:

•

M_{(u,v),(x,y)}=+1
if
(u=x\land v\neq y)
or
(v=y\land u\neq x)
(shared tail or head)

•

M_{(u,v),(x,y)}=-1
if
(u=y\land v\neq x)
or
(v=x\land u\neq y)
(crossed overlap)

•

M_{(u,v),(x,y)}=0
otherwise.

This matrix
M
is not arbitrary: it arises as the matrix representation of the adjacency operator of
\mathrm{HL}^{\prime}_{2}(G)
restricted to the antisymmetric subspace under
\sigma
.

Theorem 7

(Rule-Based Matrix for the Antisymmetric Subspace)
.

Let
G
be a finite simple undirected graph with a fixed total ordering on
V(G)
. Let
M
be the matrix defined above. Then
M
represents the action of the adjacency operator
A
of
\mathrm{HL}^{\prime}_{2}(G)
restricted to the antisymmetric subspace

\mathcal{A}=\left\{f:V(\mathrm{HL}^{\prime}_{2}(G))\to\mathbb{R}\mid f(u,v)=-f(v,u)\right\}.

Proof.

Let
G=(V,E)
be a finite undirected graph with a total ordering on
V
, inducing oriented edges
\{(u,v)\mid u<v,\{u,v\}\in E\}
, indexing the matrix with
|E|
rows/columns. The symmetric lift
\mathrm{HL}_{2}^{\prime}(G)
is the line graph of the bipartite double cover
B
of
G
, where
B
has vertex sets
V^{\prime}\sqcup V^{\prime\prime}
(two copies of
V
), and for each
\{u,v\}\in E
, edges
u^{\prime}\to v^{\prime\prime}
and
v^{\prime}\to u^{\prime\prime}
. Vertices of
\mathrm{HL}_{2}^{\prime}(G)
are these directed edges, with adjacency if they share a vertex in
B
(tail or head).

The antisymmetric subspace under
\sigma:(u,v)\mapsto(v,u)
consists of functions
f
on vertices of
\mathrm{HL}_{2}^{\prime}(G)
satisfying
f((u,v))=-f((v,u))
. A basis is
\{e_{k}\}
, where for the
k
-th oriented edge
(u,v)
(
u<v
),
e_{k}=\delta_{(u,v)}-\delta_{(v,u)}
(
\delta
are indicator vectors in
\mathbb{R}^{2|E|}
). The matrix representation of the adjacency operator
A
of
\mathrm{HL}_{2}^{\prime}(G)
on this subspace has entries
M_{i,j}=e_{i}^{T}Ae_{j}/\|e_{i}\|^{2}
, with
\|e_{i}\|^{2}=2
(since
e_{i}^{T}e_{i}=1+1=2
). We compute
e_{i}^{T}Ae_{j}
.

For the
i
-th edge
(u,v)
(
u<v
),
e_{i}=\delta_{(u,v)}-\delta_{(v,u)}
; for the
j
-th edge
(x,y)
(
x<y
),
e_{j}=\delta_{(x,y)}-\delta_{(y,x)}
. Thus,

e_{i}^{T}Ae_{j}=[\delta_{(u,v)}-\delta_{(v,u)}]^{T}A[\delta_{(x,y)}-\delta_{(y,x)}]=\delta_{(u,v)}^{T}A\delta_{(x,y)}-\delta_{(u,v)}^{T}A\delta_{(y,x)}-\delta_{(v,u)}^{T}A\delta_{(x,y)}+\delta_{(v,u)}^{T}A\delta_{(y,x)}.

Each term
\delta_{p}^{T}A\delta_{q}
is 1 if directed edges
p
and
q
are adjacent in

Observation: Connection to the Line Graph

The symmetric lift
\mathrm{HL}_{2}^{\prime}(G)
has an adjacency spectrum that is the multiset union of the spectrum of the line graph
L(G)
and that of a signed quotient graph
L^{-}(G)
, corresponding to the antisymmetric subspace, as established in
[
14
, Theorem 5]
. The rules-based matrix
M
constructed above represents the adjacency matrix of
L^{-}(G)
. If we modify the combinatorial rules by setting the matrix entry
M^{\prime}_{(u,v),(x,y)}=1
for all pairs of distinct oriented edges
(u,v)
and
(x,y)
that share exactly one vertex (i.e., replacing the
-1
for crossed shares with
+1
), the resulting matrix
M^{\prime}
is exactly the adjacency matrix of the line graph
L(G)
.

This follows because the line graph
L(G)
has vertices corresponding to the edges of
G
, with adjacency defined by sharing a vertex in
G
. The modified rules assign
M^{\prime}_{(u,v),(x,y)}=1
whenever
\{u,v\}
and
\{x,y\}
share exactly one vertex, regardless of orientation, matching the definition of
L(G)
. Thus, the original rules encode the antisymmetric part by distinguishing “same-end” shares (tail-tail or head-head,
+1
) from “crossed” shares (tail-head or head-tail,
-1
), while the uniform
+1
rule collapses these distinctions to recover
L(G)
. This observation illustrates how the antisymmetric part introduces a directional refinement, with signs reflecting orientation consistency, while the symmetric part (line graph) ignores orientation. The spectral decomposition
\operatorname{Spec}(\mathrm{HL}_{2}^{\prime}(G))=\operatorname{Spec}(L(G))\cup\operatorname{Spec}(L^{-}(G))
reflects this structural split, with
M
and
M^{\prime}
capturing the respective components.

The matrix
M
is symmetric with all eigenvalues bounded below by
-2
. This follows from the identity:

M+2I=(T-H)^{\top}(T-H),

where
T
and
H
are the tail and head incidence matrices for oriented edges of
G
. Since this is a Gram matrix, it is positive semidefinite. Thus,
M\succeq-2I
. This decomposition ensures that the restriction of the
HL^{\prime}_{2}(G)
adjacency operator to
\mathcal{A}
is well-defined and yields precisely the signed graph
L^{-}(G)
.

This confirms the spectral decomposition:

\mathrm{Spec}(\mathrm{HL}^{\prime}_{2}(G))=\mathrm{Spec}(L(G))\cup\mathrm{Spec}(L^{-}(G)),

with multiplicities preserved.

The signed structure of
L^{-}(G)
resolves classical failures of the line graph. For instance,
K_{3}
and
K_{1,3}
share line graph
K_{3}
, but yield different
L^{-}(G)
: one balanced, one not. Hence, the pair
\left(L(G),L^{-}(G)\right)
determines
G
uniquely, refining Whitney’s theorem. Further developments and algorithmic consequences are explored in a companion paper.

New Families of Regular and Irregular Perfect Graphs

5.1
Symmetric Lifts of Complete Graphs: A Perfect Refinement of Johnson Graphs

Let
K_{n}
denote the complete graph on
n
vertices. Its symmetric lift
\mathrm{HL}^{\prime}_{2}(K_{n})
forms a new family of regular, perfect graphs with rich combinatorial structure and remarkably low spectral complexity. This construction yields an infinite family of graphs that refine the Johnson graphs
J(n,2)
and admit a uniform spectral description across all
n\geq 4
.

Theorem 8
.

For every
n\geq 4
, the symmetric lift
\mathrm{HL}^{\prime}_{2}(K_{n})
is a connected, regular, vertex- and edge-transitive graph with
2n(n-1)
vertices and degree
2(n-2)
. It is perfect and box-perfect, and admits a canonical
2
-to-
1
projection onto the Johnson graph
J(n,2)
. Its adjacency spectrum consists of exactly four eigenvalues:

\mathrm{Spec}(\mathrm{HL}^{\prime}_{2}(K_{n}))=\{2(n-2),\;n-2,\;n-4,\;-2\},

with multiplicities determined by the spectrum of
K_{n}
.

Proof.

By construction, each vertex of
\mathrm{HL}^{\prime}_{2}(K_{n})
corresponds to a directed pair
(u,v)
for
u\neq v
in
K_{n}
, so the total number of vertices is
2n(n-1)
. The bipartite graph
B_{\mathrm{HL}^{\prime}}(K_{n})
underlying the lift is formed by taking two disjoint copies of the vertex set of
K_{n}
and connecting
u^{\prime}\in V^{\prime}
to
v^{\prime\prime}\in V^{\prime\prime}
and
v^{\prime}\in V^{\prime}
to
u^{\prime\prime}\in V^{\prime\prime}
for each edge
\{u,v\}
in
K_{n}
. This bipartite graph is connected, regular of degree
n-1
, and its line graph
\mathrm{HL}^{\prime}_{2}(K_{n})
is therefore regular of degree
2(n-2)
.

Since
K_{n}
is vertex- and edge-transitive under the symmetric group
S_{n}
, these symmetries lift naturally to
\mathrm{HL}^{\prime}_{2}(K_{n})
, which inherits both vertex- and edge-transitivity. Furthermore, because
\mathrm{HL}^{\prime}_{2}(K_{n})
is the line graph of a bipartite graph, it is claw-free and contains no induced odd holes, so it is perfect
[
8
]
. Since the base bipartite graph is also perfect, the lift inherits box-perfectness
[
10
]
.

To compute the spectrum, we apply the general spectral result for symmetric lifts of
d
-regular graphs. The complete graph
K_{n}
is
(n-1)
-regular, with adjacency spectrum consisting of
\lambda_{1}=n-1
(with multiplicity
1
) and
\lambda_{i}=-1
(with multiplicity
n-1
). From Theorem
6
, the eigenvalues of the lift are:

\{d+\lambda_{i}-2,\;d-\lambda_{i}-2\}=\{n-3\pm\lambda_{i}\},

for each eigenvalue
\lambda_{i}
of
K_{n}
. Substituting, we obtain:

\{n-3\pm(n-1)\}=\{2n-4,\;-2\},\quad\text{and}\quad\{n-3\pm(-1)\}=\{n-4,\;n-2\}.

Thus, the full spectrum is:

\{2(n-2),\;n-2,\;n-4,\;-2\},

with multiplicities
1
,
n-1
,
n-1
, and
n(n-1)-n
respectively.

Finally, we observe that
B_{\mathrm{HL}^{\prime}}(K_{n})
is isomorphic to the incidence graph of the symmetric design
2\text{--}(n,n-1,n-2)
, where each point is incident with all but one block, and vice versa. This graph is regular, connected, bipartite, and distance-regular with four distinct eigenvalues. By the theorem of Cvetković–Doob–Sachs
[
11
]
, any such graph is the incidence graph of a symmetric
2
-design. This confirms the design-theoretic interpretation of the symmetric lift
\mathrm{HL}^{\prime}_{2}(K_{n})
and explains its uniform spectral behavior.
∎

5.2
Ordered Lifts of Complete Graphs: Irregular Box Perfect Graphs

We now study the ordered lift
\mathrm{HL}_{2}(K_{n})
, which is canonical due to the symmetry of
K_{n}
. While the symmetric lift
\mathrm{HL}^{\prime}_{2}(K_{n})
admits a full analytic description, the ordered lift
\mathrm{HL}_{2}(K_{n})
yields a family that appears empirically well-structured but currently lacks a full spectral and automorphism-theoretic classification. We report a mix of proven and observed features.

Since
\mathrm{Aut}(K_{n})=S_{n}
, all labelings are equivalent, so the lift is well-defined without ambiguity. Ordered 2-cliques
(i,j)
with
i<j
form the base for the bipartite cover, yielding a unique
\mathrm{HL}_{2}(K_{n})
.

These graphs are Box Perfect, irregular with degrees forming a descending pyramid with an automorphism group of order 2 (top-bottom flip). The vertex count is given by
\binom{n}{2}
.

We construct an explicit family of perfect, expanding graphs with large cliques by applying the symmetric lift
\mathrm{HL}^{\prime}_{2}(G)
to the Paley graph

Let

\text{Vertex count: }2|E(G_{p})|=\frac{p(p-1)}{2},\quad\text{Degree: }2(d-1)=p-3.

Its adjacency spectrum follows from theorem
6
:

Theorem 9
.

Let
\lambda_{i}\in\mathrm{Spec}(G_{p})

\mathrm{Spec}(\mathrm{HL}^{\prime}_{2}(G_{p}))=\left\{d-2+\lambda_{i},\ d-2-\lambda_{i}\right\}_{i=1}^{p}\cup\{-2\}^{\times m},

The second-largest eigenvalue of
\mathrm{HL}^{\prime}_{2}(G_{p})

\mu_{2}=\max\left(d-2+\lambda_{2},\ d-2-\lambda_{n}\right),

where

\mathrm{Gap}(\mathrm{HL}^{\prime}_{2}(G_{p}))=2d-2-(d-2-\lambda_{n})=d+\lambda_{n}=\frac{p-2-\sqrt{p}}{2}.

8
Conclusion

In this paper we have introduced a family of graph lifts, denoted
\mathrm{HL}_{r,d}(G)
and
\mathrm{HL}^{\prime}_{r,d}(G)
, which transform arbitrary base graphs into perfect graphs via structured and parameterized lifting operations. These constructions unify and extend classical notions such as line graphs and bipartite covers, while generating rich new classes of perfect graphs that are often irregular, asymmetric, or non-distance-regular—yet retain precise combinatorial and spectral structure.

The core focus has been on the base cases: the ordered lift
\mathrm{HL}_{2}(G)
, which is label-sensitive and irregular, and the symmetric lift
\mathrm{HL}^{\prime}_{2}(G)
, which is label-invariant and highly structured. We proved that the spectrum of the line graph
L(G)
—for both adjacency and Laplacian matrices—is always contained within the spectrum of
\mathrm{HL}^{\prime}_{2}(G)
, regardless of whether
L(G)
is perfect. This containment is explained structurally by the fact that
L(G)
arises as a quotient of
\mathrm{HL}^{\prime}_{2}(G)
under a natural involution identifying each directed edge
(u,v)
with its reverse
(v,u)
. Thus, spectral inclusion follows directly from standard results on graph quotients. This spectral embedding offers a new way to encode the structure of a graph within a perfect ambient space. The symmetric lift’s decomposition further yields the antisymmetric line graph
L^{-}(G)
, a signed refinement that encodes directional overlaps and completes the spectral union, refining Whitney’s theorem through sign patterns that distinguish otherwise indistinguishable graphs.

A key contribution is the construction of explicit, infinite families of perfect expanders. When the base graph is a regular expander, such as a Paley graph, the symmetric lift
\mathrm{HL}^{\prime}_{2}(G)
remains sparse, retains spectral expansion, improved combinatorial expansion, and yields a new perfect graph with large clique number and strong symmetry. These graphs offer rare examples of perfect expanders with both algebraic and combinatorial regularity.

We have also introduced a parameterized framework
\mathrm{HL}^{\prime}_{r,d}(G)
that generalizes the lift construction to higher-rank cliques and controlled substitution patterns. This family captures new strongly regular graphs, spectral refinements of Johnson-type graphs, and irregular but perfect examples not previously classified in the literature.

All lifted graphs in this framework are not only perfect but also box-perfect, as they arise as line graphs of bipartite graphs. This places them in a highly tractable class for combinatorial optimization: linear programming over the stable set polytope with box constraints can be solved efficiently. This property, rarely shared by infinite families of expanders or irregular perfect graphs, opens new algorithmic directions for applications in graph coloring, scheduling, and network design.

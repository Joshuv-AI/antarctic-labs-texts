Canonical Ramsey: triangles, rectangles and beyond
Yijia Fang, Gennian Ge, Yang Shu, Qian Xu, Zixiang Xu, Dilong Yang

Abstract
In a seminal work, Cheng and Xu showed that if 𝒮 \mathcal{S} is a square or a triangle with a certain property, then for every positive integer r r there exists n 0 ​ ( 𝒮 ) n_{0}(\mathcal{S}) independent of r r such that every r r -coloring of 𝔼 n \mathbb{E}^{n} with n ≥ n 0 ​ ( 𝒮 ) n\geq n_{0}(\mathcal{S}) contains a monochromatic or a rainbow congruent copy of 𝒮 \mathcal{S} . Gehér, Sagdeev, and Tóth formalized this dimension independence as the canonical Ramsey property and proved it for all hypercubes, thereby covering rectangles whose squared aspect ratio ( a / b ) 2 (a/b)^{2} is rational. They asked whether this property holds for all triangles and for all rectangles. 1. We resolve both questions. More precisely, for triangles we confirm the property in 𝔼 4 \mathbb{E}^{4} by developing a novel rotation-sphereical chaining argument. For rectangles, we introduce a structural reduction to product configurations of bounded color complexity, enabling the use of the simplex Ramsey theorem together with product Ramsey theorem. 2. Beyond this, we develop a concise perturbation framework based on an iterative embedding coupled with the Frankl–Rödl simplex super-Ramsey theorem, which yields the canonical Ramsey property for a natural class of 3 3 -dimensional simplices and also furnishes an alternative proof for triangles.

Methods range from classical Ramsey and extremal techniques to probabilistic and geometric arguments, and the problems often interface with stability phenomena, transference principles, and the construction of color-avoiding obstructions.

In parallel, Gallai–Ramsey theory studies the dichotomy “monochromatic vs rainbow” in colored structures

1.2
Known results and two central problems of Geheér, Sagdeev and Tóth

Cheng and Xu
[
2
]
studied this problem for various fundamental configurations, with particular focus on triangles, squares, and lines. They discovered a
dimension-independence phenomenon
: once the ambient dimension exceeds a threshold depending only on the configuration, monochromatic or rainbow copies are unavoidable. For instance, for any right or acute triangle, or any square
\mathcal{S}
, there exists an integer

, independent of

, such that
\mathbb{E}^{n}\overset{r}{\rightarrow}(\mathcal{S};\mathcal{S})_{\mathrm{GR}}
holds for all

; see also their canonical results for certain simplices
[

, Corollaries 1.5 and 1.7]
.

To formalize this, Geheér, Sagdeev, and Tóth
[

]
introduced the
canonical Ramsey property
: a finite configuration
\mathcal{X}
exhibits this property if there exists
n_{0}=n_{0}(\mathcal{X})
such that, for any positive integer

and any

\mathbb{E}^{n}\overset{r}{\rightarrow}(\mathcal{X};\mathcal{X})_{\mathrm{GR}}.

The authors in
[
14
]
established this for all hypercubes and thereby for rectangles with side lengths

such that

[
2
]
by showing that for any fixed acute triangle

.

Despite these advances, two problems were explicitly highlighted in
[

]
as the main outstanding challenges in the theory.

(Geheér-Sagdeev-Tóth

(2)

Proposition 2.1

Proof of

(\bm{x}_{1},\bm{v}_{i}),\quad(\bm{x}_{1},\bm{v}_{i+1}),\quad(\bm{x}_{2},\bm{v}_{i}),\quad(\bm{x}_{2},\bm{v}_{i+1})

receive four distinct colors under
\tau
. These form a rainbow copy of the rectangle
\mathcal{R}
with side lengths
a
and
b
, a contradiction. This completes the proof of
Lemma

3.3
.
∎

For each point
\bm{u}\in\mathbb{E}^{n_{0}}
, consider the product configuration

(\bm{u},\mathcal{S}_{3m+1}(x)\times\mathcal{B}_{m}(x,y)):=\{(\bm{u},\bm{v}):\bm{v}\in\mathcal{S}_{3m+1}(x)\times\mathcal{B}_{m}(x,y)\}\subseteq\mathbb{E}^{n_{0}+3m+2}.

By Lemma
3.3
, under the coloring
\chi
, each such set either contains a rainbow copy of
\mathcal{R}
, or a monochromatic copy of
\ell_{x}
. If a rainbow copy of
\mathcal{R}
occurs for some
\bm{u}
, we are done. Thus, we may assume that for every
\bm{u}\in\mathbb{E}^{n_{0}}
, no rainbow copy of
\mathcal{R}
exists. Then Lemma
3.3
ensures that each associated product configuration must contain a monochromatic copy of
\ell_{x}
. Since
\mathcal{S}_{3m+1}(x)\times\mathcal{B}_{m}(x,y)
is finite, there are only finitely many possible positions for such
\ell_{x}
’s. More precisely, each copy of
\ell_{x}
arises in one of the following two forms:

•

A line between two distinct points in
\mathcal{S}_{3m+1}(x)
, paired with a fixed point in
\mathcal{B}_{m}(x,y)
. There are
\binom{3m+1}{2}
choices of lines in
\mathcal{S}_{3m+1}(x)
, and for each such line,
m+1
possible choices of a point in
\mathcal{B}_{m}(x,y)
, yielding a total of
(m+1)\cdot\binom{3m+1}{2}
copies.

•

There exists one line in
\mathcal{B}_{m}(x,y)
of length exactly
x
, namely the line between
v_{1}
and
v_{m+1}
, and pair it with a fixed point in
\mathcal{S}_{3m+1}(x)
. Since there are
3m+1
choices of points in
\mathcal{S}_{3m+1}(x)
, this contributes
3m+1
copies.

Therefore, the number of distinct copies of
\ell_{x}
in
\mathcal{S}_{3m+1}(x)\times\mathcal{B}_{m}(x,y)
is bounded by

q=(m+1)\binom{3m+1}{2}+3m+1.

To formalize the selection of these monochromatic
\ell_{x}
’s, we define a labeling function

\lambda:\{\ell_{x}\subseteq\mathcal{S}_{3m+1}(x)\times\mathcal{B}_{m}(x,y)\}\to[q],

which assigns a unique label to each possible position of
\ell_{x}
.

We define an auxiliary coloring
\gamma:\mathbb{E}^{n_{0}}\to[q]
by the rule:
for each
\bm{u}\in\mathbb{E}^{n_{0}}
, select one monochromatic copy of
\ell_{x}
, denoted by
\ell(\bm{u})
, within its associated product configuration under
\chi
, and set

\gamma(\bm{u}):=\lambda(\ell(\bm{u})).

That is,
\gamma(\bm{u})
records the position label of the chosen monochromatic
\ell_{x}
. By definitions of
n_{0}
and
q
,

\mathbb{E}^{n_{0}}\overset{q}{\rightarrow}\mathcal{S}_{7}(y)\times\mathcal{B}_{2}(y,x).

Thus, there exists a monochromatic copy of
\mathcal{S}_{7}(y)\times\mathcal{B}_{2}(y,x)
under
\gamma
. Fix this monochromatic copy and denote the set of its points as
V\subseteq\mathbb{E}^{n_{0}}
.

By the definition of
\gamma
, every point
\bm{u}\in V
is associated with the same monochromatic copy of
\ell_{x}
, whose endpoints
\bm{v}_{1},\bm{v}_{2}
lie in
\mathcal{S}_{3m+1}(x)\times\mathcal{B}_{m}(x,y)
. That is, for all
\bm{u}\in V
, the pair
\{(\bm{u},\bm{v}_{1}),(\bm{u},\bm{v}_{2})\}
forms a monochromatic copy of
\ell_{x}
under
\chi
. Consider now the set of points

V\times\{\bm{v}_{1},\bm{v}_{2}\}\subseteq\mathbb{E}^{n_{0}+3m+2}.

If this set contains a rainbow copy of
R
under
\chi
, we are done. Otherwise, focus on the subset

(V,\bm{v}_{1}):=\{(\bm{u},\bm{v}_{1}):\bm{u}\in V\}.

Since
V
is isomorphic to
\mathcal{S}_{7}(y)\times\mathcal{B}_{2}(y,x)
, we can apply Lemma
3.3
again with parameter
s=2
. Here, we interpret the rectangle
\mathcal{T}
as being rotated by
\frac{\pi}{2}
, effectively treating the side of length
y
as the dominant side in the product structure. This flexible application of Lemma
3.3
allows us to leverage the same combinatorial argument with the roles of
x
and
y
interchanged. More precisely, if a rainbow copy of
\mathcal{T}
under
\chi
is found, we are done; otherwise, Lemma
3.3
guarantees the existence of a monochromatic copy of
\ell_{y}
, say between the points
(\bm{u}_{1},\bm{v}_{1})
and
(\bm{u}_{2},\bm{v}_{1})
.

Finally, we make the following observations under the coloring function
\chi
:

•

The points
(\bm{u}_{1},\bm{v}_{1})
and
(\bm{u}_{2},\bm{v}_{1})
share the same color, as they form a monochromatic copy of
\ell_{y}
constructed in the previous step.

•

The points
(\bm{u}_{1},\bm{v}_{1})
and
(\bm{u}_{1},\bm{v}_{2})
also share the same color, since the line with endpoints
\bm{v}_{1},\bm{v}_{2}
was fixed as a monochromatic
\ell_{x}
across all associated product configurations. Likewise, the points
(\bm{u}_{2},\bm{v}_{1})
and
(\bm{u}_{2},\bm{v}_{2})
are colored identically.

Therefore, the four points
(\bm{u}_{1},\bm{v}_{1}),(\bm{u}_{1},\bm{v}_{2}),(\bm{u}_{2},\bm{v}_{1}),(\bm{u}_{2},\bm{v}_{2})
form a monochromatic rectangle congruent to
\mathcal{R}
under the original coloring
\chi
. This completes the proof.
∎

A general framework for low-dimensional simplices

4.1
Notation

Most notation in this section is identical to those above. For the sake of convenience, we list them here again.

•

We use blackboard bold letters for spaces, in particular,
\mathbb{E}^{n}
for the
n
-dimensional Euclidean space. We use calligraphy, uppercase letters such as
\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y}
for set of points in
\mathbb{E}^{n}
.

•

We use boldface, lowercase letters such as
\bm{a},\bm{b}
for points in
\mathbb{E}^{n}
only; and their difference
\bm{a}-\bm{b}
for the vector between. Besides,
\|\bm{a}-\bm{b}\|
represents the usual Euclidean norm of this vector, or equivalently, the distance between points
\bm{a}
and
\bm{b}.
Sometimes, we write
\text{dist}(\bm{a},\bm{b})
for
\|\bm{a}-\bm{b}\|
if
\bm{a}
and
\bm{b}
are expressed in their coordinates.

•

For three points
\bm{a},\bm{b},\bm{c}
, we use
\angle\bm{a}\bm{b}{\bm{c}}
for the induced angle between vectors
\bm{b}-\bm{a}
and
\bm{b}-\bm{c}.

Besides, we use
(\bm{b}-\bm{a})\cdot(\bm{b}-\bm{c})
for the dot product of these two vectors.

4.2
Canonical perturbation via the simplex super-Ramsey theorem

In this part we invoke the
simplex super-Ramsey
theorem, for completeness we record it below.

Theorem 4.1

(
[
10
]
)
.

For any simplex
\Delta
, there exist positive constants
c
,
\delta
and
N
only depend on
\Delta
, and a configuration
\mathcal{X}\subseteq\mathbb{E}^{n}
for every
n>N
with the following two properties:

•

|\mathcal{X}|<c^{n}
;

•

|\mathcal{Y}|<|\mathcal{X}|\cdot(1+\delta)^{-n}
holds for all subsets
\mathcal{Y}\subseteq\mathcal{X}
that contain no congruent copy of
\Delta
.

For convenience, we restate
Theorem

1.5
here.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
d,n_{0},m,r\geq 1
be integers and let
\Delta\subseteq\mathbb{E}^{d}
be a simplex.
Let
\mathcal{X}=\{\bm{v}_{1},\dots,\bm{v}_{m}\}\subseteq\mathbb{P}_{0}\cong\mathbb{E}^{n_{0}}
be any
m
-point set, and fix
\varepsilon>0
.
Then there exist integers
n_{1},\dots,n_{m}
with the following property: for every
r
-coloring
\chi
of the orthogonal product

\mathbb{P}:=\mathbb{P}_{0}\times\mathbb{P}_{1}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{P}_{m},\qquad\mathbb{P}_{i}\cong\mathbb{E}^{n_{i}}\ (i=0,1,\dots,m),

that contains no monochromatic copy of
\Delta
, there exist points
\bm{x}_{i}\in\mathbb{P}_{i}
for
i=1,\dots,m
such that, writing

\bm{p}_{i}:=(\bm{v}_{i},\bm{x}_{1},\dots,\bm{x}_{m})\in\mathbb{P}
,
each
\varepsilon
-sphere
S_{i}(\bm{p}_{i};\varepsilon)
is not monochromatic under
\chi
.

Proof of
Theorem

4.2
.

Let
\Delta
consist of
d+1
points. Choose a congruent copy

\{\bm{w}_{0},\bm{w}_{1},\ldots,\bm{w}_{d}\}\subseteq\mathbb{E}^{d}
of
\Delta
with

\bm{w}_{0}=\bm{0}
.
Let
A_{1}
be the
(d+1)\times d
matrix whose rows are

\bm{w}_{0},\ldots,\bm{w}_{d}
.
For each
i\in[d]
, pick an integer
m_{i}\geq 2
and set

\varepsilon_{i}:=\|\bm{w}_{i}\|/m_{i}<\varepsilon
.
Define

n_{1}:=\sum_{i=1}^{d}(m_{i}-1)+d.

Let
A_{2}
be an
n_{1}-d
by
d
matrix with rows of the form

\left\{\frac{j}{m_{i}}\cdot\bm{w}_{i}\biggm|1\leq i\leq d,1\leq j\leq m_{i}-1\right\}.

Form the block matrix
A\in\mathbb{R}^{(n_{1}+1)\times n_{1}}
by

A\;:=\;\begin{bmatrix}A_{1}&0\\[2.0pt]
A_{2}&(\varepsilon/2)\,I_{n_{1}-d}\end{bmatrix},

where
I_{n_{1}-d}
is the identity matrix of size
n_{1}-d
.
Let
\mathcal{B}\subseteq\mathbb{P}_{1}\cong\mathbb{E}^{n_{1}}
be the set of row vectors of
A
,
and enumerate it as
\mathcal{B}=\{\bm{b}_{1},\ldots,\bm{b}_{n_{1}+1}\}
.
Since the lower-right block is
(\varepsilon/2)I_{n_{1}-d}
, the rows of
A
are
affinely independent; in particular,
\mathcal{B}
is a simplex in
\mathbb{E}^{n_{1}}
.

Apply Theorem
4.1
to the simplex
\mathcal{B}
, there exist positive constants
c(\mathcal{B})
,
\delta(\mathcal{B})
, and
N(\mathcal{B})

(depending only on
\mathcal{B}
)
such that, for each
i=2,\ldots,m
, we can choose an integer
n_{i}
with

n_{i}\geq\max\Big\{\,N(\mathcal{B}),\ \log_{1+\delta(\mathcal{B})}\!\Bigl(\,|\mathcal{B}|\cdot\prod_{j=2}^{i-1}c(\mathcal{B})^{\,n_{j}}\Bigr)\Bigr\},

and a set
\mathcal{X}_{i}\subseteq\mathbb{E}^{n_{i}}
such that

•

|\mathcal{X}_{i}|<c(\mathcal{B})^{\,n_{i}}
;

•

for every
\mathcal{Y}\subseteq\mathcal{X}_{i}
containing no congruent copy of
\mathcal{B}
,
we have
|\mathcal{Y}|<|\mathcal{X}_{i}|\cdot(1+\delta(\mathcal{B}))^{-n_{i}}
.

We now fix the integers
n_{1},\ldots,n_{m}
and the sets
\mathcal{X}_{2},\ldots,\mathcal{X}_{m}
above.
In what follows we work inside the finite grid

\mathcal{G}\ :=\ \mathcal{B}\times\mathcal{X}_{2}\times\cdots\times\mathcal{X}_{m}\ \subseteq\ \mathbb{P}_{1}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{P}_{m},

and pair this tail product with the anchors
\bm{v}_{1},\ldots,\bm{v}_{m}\in\mathbb{P}_{0}\cong\mathbb{E}^{n_{0}}
as needed.

Claim 4.3
.

Fix
h\in[m]
and a tuple of fiber points

\bm{x}_{1},\ldots,\bm{x}_{h-1},\bm{x}_{h+1},\ldots,\bm{x}_{m}
,
where
\bm{x}_{i}\in\mathbb{P}_{i}\cong\mathbb{E}^{n_{i}}
.
Identify
\mathcal{B}
with a fixed isometric copy
\mathcal{B}^{(h)}\subseteq\mathbb{P}_{h}

(of the simplex constructed above) and, for brevity, still write
\mathcal{B}
.
Then there exists
\bm{b}\in\mathcal{B}
such that the
\varepsilon
-sphere

S_{h}\bigl((\bm{v}_{h},\bm{x}_{1},\ldots,\bm{x}_{h-1},\bm{b},\bm{x}_{h+1},\ldots,\bm{x}_{m});\varepsilon\bigr)

in
\mathbb{P}_{h}
is not monochromatic under
\chi
.

Proof of claim.

Fix
h
and the tuple
\bm{x}_{i}
for
i\neq h
as above, and abbreviate

C(\bm{b})\ :=\ (\bm{v}_{h},\bm{x}_{1},\ldots,\bm{x}_{h-1},\bm{b},\bm{x}_{h+1},\ldots,\bm{x}_{m})\ \in\ \mathbb{P}.

Consider the family of
\varepsilon
-spheres in the
h
-th fiber (Definition
1.4
)

\mathcal{S}:=\ \Bigl\{\,S_{h}\bigl(C(\bm{b});\varepsilon\bigr):\bm{b}\in\mathcal{B}\Bigr\}\subseteq\mathbb{P}_{h}.

Connectivity.

We first claim that the union
\bigcup_{S\in\mathcal{S}}S
is connected in
\mathbb{P}_{h}
.
Indeed, recall that
\mathcal{B}
consists of the rows of the block matrix

\begin{bmatrix}A_{1}&0\\
A_{2}&(\varepsilon/2)I\end{bmatrix}
.
Thus
\mathcal{B}
contains the
d+1

base points

(\bm{w}_{i},\bm{0})
with
0\leq i\leq d,

and for each
i\in[d]
and
j=1,\dots,m_{i}-1
, points of the form

\bigl(\tfrac{j}{m_{i}}\cdot\bm{w}_{i},\,\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\cdot\bm{e}_{k(i,j)}\bigr)
,
where
\bm{e}_{k}
denotes a standard basis vector in the lower block
(the precise index
k(i,j)
is irrelevant).
Two centers in
\mathcal{B}
that differ by increasing or decreasing a single step along the

(i,j)
-chain have distance

\Bigl\|\,\tfrac{1}{m_{i}}\cdot\bm{w}_{i}\Bigr\|^{2}+\Bigl\|\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\cdot\bm{e}_{k}-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\cdot\bm{e}_{k^{\prime}}\Bigr\|^{2}\leq\varepsilon_{i}^{2}+\Bigl(\tfrac{\varepsilon}{2}\Bigr)^{2}+\Bigl(\tfrac{\varepsilon}{2}\Bigr)^{2}<(2\varepsilon)^{2},

because
\varepsilon_{i}=\|\bm{w}_{i}\|/m_{i}<\varepsilon
.
Hence successive centers along each
(i,j)
-chain are at distance
<2\varepsilon
.
Moreover, the endpoints of each chain connect to the base points:

\operatorname{dist}\Bigl(\Bigl(\frac{1}{m_{i}}\cdot\bm{w}_{i},\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\cdot\bm{e}_{k}\Bigr),(\bm{0},\bm{0})\Bigr)=\sqrt{\varepsilon_{i}^{2}+(\varepsilon/2)^{2}}<2\varepsilon,

\operatorname{dist}\Bigl(\Bigl(\frac{m_{i}-1}{m_{i}}\cdot\bm{w}_{i},\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\cdot\bm{e}_{k^{\prime}}\Bigr),(\bm{w}_{i},\bm{0})\Bigr)=\sqrt{\varepsilon_{i}^{2}+(\varepsilon/2)^{2}}<2\varepsilon.

Thus the graph on
\mathcal{B}
that joins two centers when their distance is
\leq 2\varepsilon

is connected; since two radius-
\varepsilon
spheres intersect whenever their centers are
at distance
\leq 2\varepsilon
, the union
\bigcup_{S\in\mathcal{S}}S
is connected.

Propagation of color.

Assume for contradiction that every sphere in
\mathcal{S}
is monochromatic under
\chi
.
Whenever two monochromatic spheres intersect, they must have the same color on the
intersection, hence the same color on the whole spheres.
By connectivity, all spheres in
\mathcal{S}
have a common color.

In particular, for the
d+1
base centers
(\bm{w}_{j},\bm{0})\in\mathcal{B}

(
0\leq j\leq d
), the spheres

S_{h}\bigl(C((\bm{w}_{j},\bm{0}));\varepsilon\bigr)
all have this color.
Fix any unit vector
\bm{e}_{h}\in\mathbb{P}_{h}
.
Then, for each
j
, the point

\bm{q}_{j}\ :=\ \bigl(\bm{v}_{h},\bm{x}_{1},\ldots,\bm{x}_{h-1},(\bm{w}_{j},\bm{0})+\varepsilon\bm{e}_{h},\bm{x}_{h+1},\ldots,\bm{x}_{m}\bigr)

lies on
S_{h}\bigl(C((\bm{w}_{j},\bm{0}));\varepsilon\bigr)
and hence has that color.
For any
j,k
we have

\bigl\|\bm{q}_{j}-\bm{q}_{k}\bigr\|=\bigl\|(\bm{w}_{j}-\bm{w}_{k},\,\bm{0})\bigr\|=\|\bm{w}_{j}-\bm{w}_{k}\|,

so
\{\bm{q}_{0},\ldots,\bm{q}_{d}\}
is congruent to
\Delta
.
Thus we obtain a monochromatic copy of
\Delta
in
\mathbb{P}
, contradicting the hypothesis.

Therefore, at least one sphere in
\mathcal{S}
is not monochromatic, that is, there exists
\bm{b}\in\mathcal{B}
with the stated property. This finishes the proof.
∎

Claim 4.4
.

For any integer
1\leq i\leq m
and any fixed tail

(\bm{x}_{i+1},\ldots,\bm{x}_{m})\in\mathcal{X}_{i+1}\times\cdots\times\mathcal{X}_{m}
,
there exists a tuple
(\bm{x}_{1},\ldots,\bm{x}_{i})\in\mathcal{B}\times\mathcal{X}_{2}\times\cdots\times\mathcal{X}_{i}

such that, for every
1\leq j\leq i
, the
\varepsilon
-sphere

S_{j}\bigl((\bm{v}_{j},\bm{x}_{1},\ldots,\bm{x}_{m});\varepsilon\bigr)
in
\mathbb{P}_{j}

is not monochromatic under
\chi
.

Proof of claim.

We proceed by induction on
i\in[m]
. Let
P(i)
denote the statement of the claim.

Base case
P(1)
.

Fix any
(\bm{x}_{2},\ldots,\bm{x}_{m})
.
By Claim
4.3
with
h=1
, there exists
\bm{x}_{1}\in\mathcal{B}
such that

S_{1}\bigl((\bm{v}_{1},\bm{x}_{1},\bm{x}_{2},\ldots,\bm{x}_{m});\varepsilon\bigr)

is not monochromatic. Hence
P(1)
holds.

Inductive step.

Assume
P(i)
holds for some
1\leq i<m
, and fix a tail

(\bm{x}_{i+2},\ldots,\bm{x}_{m})\in\mathcal{X}_{i+2}\times\cdots\times\mathcal{X}_{m}
.
For each choice of
\bm{x}_{i+1}\in\mathcal{X}_{i+1}
, by
P(i)
(applied to the tail

(\bm{x}_{i+1},\ldots,\bm{x}_{m})
) there exists at least one witnessing tuple

(\bm{x}_{1},\ldots,\bm{x}_{i})\in\mathcal{B}\times\mathcal{X}_{2}\times\cdots\times\mathcal{X}_{i}

such that, for every
1\leq j\leq i
,

S_{j}\bigl((\bm{v}_{j},\bm{x}_{1},\ldots,\bm{x}_{i},\bm{x}_{i+1},\ldots,\bm{x}_{m});\varepsilon\bigr)\ \text{is not monochromatic.}

Fix an arbitrary choice of one such witnessing tuple for each
\bm{x}_{i+1}
,
and define an auxiliary coloring

\gamma_{\bm{x}_{i+2},\ldots,\bm{x}_{m}}:\mathcal{X}_{i+1}\to\mathcal{B}\times\mathcal{X}_{2}\times\cdots\times\mathcal{X}_{i}

by letting
\gamma_{\bm{x}_{i+2},\ldots,\bm{x}_{m}}(\bm{x}_{i+1})
be the chosen witnessing tuple associated to
\bm{x}_{i+1}
. By the pigeonhole principle, some color class
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathcal{X}_{i+1}
satisfies

|\mathcal{C}|\ \geq\ \frac{|\mathcal{X}_{i+1}|}{\,|\mathcal{B}|\cdot\prod_{j=2}^{i}|\mathcal{X}_{j}|}\ \geq\ \frac{|\mathcal{X}_{i+1}|}{\,|\mathcal{B}|\cdot\prod_{j=2}^{i}c(\mathcal{B})^{\,n_{j}}}\ \geq\ |\mathcal{X}_{i+1}|\cdot(1+\delta(\mathcal{B}))^{-n_{i+1}},

where we used
|\mathcal{X}_{j}|\leq c(\mathcal{B})^{\,n_{j}}
for
2\leq j\leq i
, and the choice of
n_{i+1}
ensuring

|\mathcal{B}|\cdot\prod_{j=2}^{i}c(\mathcal{B})^{\,n_{j}}\leq(1+\delta(\mathcal{B}))^{\,n_{i+1}}
.
By the super-Ramsey property of
\mathcal{X}_{i+1}
for the pattern
\mathcal{B}
,
the set
\mathcal{C}
contains a congruent copy
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{X}_{i+1}
of
\mathcal{B}
.

By construction of
\gamma_{\bm{x}_{i+2},\ldots,\bm{x}_{m}}
, every point of
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}
is assigned the same
tuple
(\bm{x}_{1},\ldots,\bm{x}_{i})=\gamma_{\bm{x}_{i+2},\ldots,\bm{x}_{m}}(\bm{x}_{i+1})
.
Therefore the set

\Bigl\{\,(\bm{x}_{1},\ldots,\bm{x}_{i},\bm{x}_{i+1},\bm{x}_{i+2},\ldots,\bm{x}_{m})\ :\ \bm{x}_{i+1}\in\mathcal{B}^{\prime}\Bigr\}

is a congruent copy of
\mathcal{B}
sitting in the
(i+1)
-st factor
\mathbb{P}_{i+1}
, with all other
coordinates fixed. Applying Claim
4.3
with
h=i+1
(which depends only on the
isometry type of
\mathcal{B}
), we obtain some
\bm{x}_{i+1}\in\mathcal{B}^{\prime}
such that
the
\varepsilon
-sphere

S_{i+1}\bigl((\bm{v}_{i+1},\bm{x}_{1},\ldots,\bm{x}_{i},\bm{x}_{i+1},\ldots,\bm{x}_{m});\varepsilon\bigr)

is not monochromatic. Together with the inductive hypothesis (which already guarantees the same
for
j=1,\ldots,i
), this yields
P(i+1)
.

By induction, the claim holds for all
i\in[m]
. This finishes the proof.

∎

By
4.4
,
P(m)
holds. Taking the tail to be empty, we obtain a tuple

(\bm{x}_{1},\ldots,\bm{x}_{m})\in\mathcal{G}=\mathcal{B}\times\mathcal{X}_{2}\times\cdots\times\mathcal{X}_{m}

such that, for every

, the

-sphere

. This furnishes a single choice of fiber points that

,
as required, and completes the proof.
∎

4.3
An alternative proof of canonical Ramsey property for triangles

Using
Theorem

1.5
, we can provide a new proof of the canonical property for triangles. For convenience, we restate the theorem we will prove.

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
\mathcal{T}
be a triangle and let
r
be a positive integer. There exists some
n_{0}(\mathcal{T})
such that for any
n\geq n_{0}(\mathcal{T})

\mathbb{E}^{n}\overset{r}{\rightarrow}(\mathcal{T};\mathcal{T})_{\mathrm{GR}}.

Before the proof, we need an additional theorem about the simplex.

Lemma 4.6

(
[
10
]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a
(d-1)
-dimensional simplex with vertices
\bm{v}_{1},\dots,\bm{v}_{d}
, then there exist
\varepsilon^{\prime}=\varepsilon^{\prime}(\mathcal{A})>0
such that for any
0<\varepsilon\leq\varepsilon^{\prime}
there exists a
(d-1)
-dimensional simplex
\mathcal{A}_{\varepsilon}
with vertices
\bm{v}_{1^{\prime}},\ldots,\bm{v}_{d^{\prime}}
such that
\|\bm{v}_{i^{\prime}}-\bm{v}_{j^{\prime}}\|^{2}=\|\bm{v}_{i}-\bm{v}_{j}\|^{2}-2\varepsilon^{2}
for every
1\leq i<j\leq d
.

Remark
.

The existence of
\varepsilon^{\prime}(\mathcal{A})
was shown in
[
10
]
.

Using
Theorem

1.5
and
Lemma

4.6
, we give a different proof of the canonical Ramsey property for triangles.

Proof of
Theorem

4.5
.

Let a triangle
\mathcal{T}
have side lengths
a,b,c>0
. By
Lemma

4.6
, there exists
\varepsilon>0
and a triangle
\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon}
with side lengths

a^{\prime}:=\sqrt{a^{2}-2\varepsilon^{2}},\quad b^{\prime}:=\sqrt{b^{2}-2\varepsilon^{2}},\quad c^{\prime}:=\sqrt{c^{2}-2\varepsilon^{2}}.

Fix an embedding of
\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon}
with vertices

\mathcal{X}=\{\bm{v}_{1},\bm{v}_{2},\bm{v}_{3}\}\subseteq\mathbb{P}_{0}\cong\mathbb{E}^{2}
.

Apply
Theorem

1.5
with
m=3
and anchors

selected above.
We obtain integers

such that for every
r
-coloring
\chi
of the orthogonal product

\mathbb{P}\ :=\ \mathbb{P}_{0}\times\mathbb{P}_{1}\times\mathbb{P}_{2}\times\mathbb{P}_{3},\qquad\mathbb{P}_{0}\cong\mathbb{E}^{2},\ \ \mathbb{P}_{i}\cong\mathbb{E}^{n_{i}}\ (i=1,2,3),

that contains no monochromatic copy of
\mathcal{T}
, there exist points

\bm{x}_{i}\in\mathbb{P}_{i}
so that for each
i\in\{1,2,3\}
the
\varepsilon
-sphere in the

is not monochromatic.

Notice that any triple of points

such that

can be expressed as

\bm{y}_{2}:=\bigl(\bm{v}_{2},\bm{x}_{1},\bm{x}_{2}+\varepsilon\bm{u}_{2},\bm{x}_{3}\bigr)\in S_{2}(\bm{p}_{2};\varepsilon),

\bm{y}_{3}:=\bigl(\bm{v}_{3},\bm{x}_{1},\bm{x}_{2},\bm{x}_{3}+\varepsilon\bm{u}_{3}\bigr)\in S_{3}(\bm{p}_{3};\varepsilon),

\|\bm{v}_{2}-\bm{v}_{3}\|=b^{\prime}

\bm{y}_{1},\bm{y}_{2},\bm{y}_{3}
forms a copy of
\mathcal{T}
.

For
i\in[3]

be the set of colors that actually appear on the sphere

1.5
,
|C_{i}|\geq 2
.

that are all the same or distinct. This is because there exists
\bm{y}_{i}\in S_{i}(\bm{p}_{i};\varepsilon)
for
i\in[3]
such that
\bm{y}_{i}
has color

\bm{y}_{1},\bm{y}_{2},\bm{y}_{3}

\mathcal{T}

\mathcal{T}
.

must have size at least

∎

4.4
Canonical property for certain
3
-dimensional simplices via perturbation theorem

Using
Theorem

1.5
, we prove a canonical Ramsey theorem for
3-dimensional simplices (tetrahedra) under a natural geometric separation condition.

Theorem 4.7
.

Let
\mathcal{T}
be a tetrahedron such that the largest height of
\mathcal{T}

exceeds the smallest circumradius among its four triangular faces.
Then for every
r
there exists
n_{0}=n_{0}(\mathcal{T})
such that for all
n\geq n_{0}
,

\mathbb{E}^{n}\ \overset{r}{\longrightarrow}\ (\mathcal{T};\mathcal{T})_{\mathrm{GR}}.

Overview of the proof of
Theorem

4.7
.

The proof is slightly complicated, especially the construction of some auxiliary configurations. Therefore we plan to first sketch the proof here.
We work under the geometric separation hypothesis that the largest height is larger than the smallest facial circumradius. The engine is the canonical perturbation
Theorem

1.5
: after embedding into a suitable orthogonal product, we may choose one anchor in each fiber so that every prescribed
\varepsilon
-sphere about that anchor is not monochromatic. This yields, for any finite seed configuration
X
, a palette
C_{\bm{v}}\subseteq[r]
with
|C_{v}|\geq 2
attached to each
\bm{v}\in X
, acting as a controlled local obstruction.

We first replace the target tetrahedron by a small contracted copy
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
that preserves the height–circumradius gap. Two geometric building blocks are then established. Proposition
4.9
shows that a short hinge with small opening extends along either side to a copy of
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
. Proposition
4.10
creates a dense micro-configuration: from one face we pick three apices and a fourth above them, realizing four tightly linked copies of
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
. In particular, the existence of such configuration relies on the condition on the circumradius and height in this theorem.

Using these ingredients, we assemble a finite configuration
\mathcal{X}
consisting of numerous copies of
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
glued along polygonal links. The resulting
\mathcal{X}
is large and structurally intricate, which is, however, the linchpin of our argument. For this reason we prioritize a complete specification of
\mathcal{X}
over brevity and readability. Once
\mathcal{X}
is fixed, applying
Theorem

1.5
to
\mathcal{X}
equips every anchor with a nontrivial palette. The classification lemma (
4.13
) reduces palette quadruples on a tetrahedron to types
\mathfrak{A}
or
\mathfrak{B}
. A five-point transmission rule and a link-consistency principle (
4.14
,
4.15
) propagate type along links; a tailored sub-configuration
\mathcal{X}_{1}
eliminates
\mathfrak{B}
(
4.16
); a second gluing then contradicts
\mathfrak{A}
(
4.17
). Hence a monochromatic or rainbow copy of
\mathcal{T}
must occur in high enough dimension, proving
Theorem

4.7
.

We then provide the full proof.

Proof of

4.7
.

Let
\mathcal{T}
be the target
3

of

Since circumradius and a certain height of a tetrahedron are all continuous functions of side length, there exists a proper
\varepsilon

Proof of

\bm{b}_{i}\bm{c}_{i,1}\bm{c}_{i,2}
and
\bm{b}_{i+1}\bm{c}_{i,1}\bm{c}_{i,2}
are all congruent to the face
\bm{a}_{1}\bm{a}_{2}\bm{a}_{3}
supporting the largest height. Then applying
4.10
, we can find
\bm{z}_{i},\bm{y}_{i,1},\bm{y}_{i,2},\bm{y}_{i,3}
and
\bm{z}_{i}^{\prime},\bm{y}_{i,1}^{\prime},\bm{y}_{i,2}^{\prime},\bm{y}_{i,3}^{\prime}
such that all of the following
4
-tuples form a copy of
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
:

1.

\{\bm{b}_{i},\bm{c}_{i,1},\bm{c}_{i,2},\bm{y}_{i,j}\}_{j\in[3]}
;

2.

\{\bm{b}_{i+1},\bm{c}_{i,1},\bm{c}_{i,2},\bm{y}_{i,j}\}_{j\in[3]}
;

3.

\{\bm{z}_{i},\bm{y}_{i,1},\bm{y}_{i,2},\bm{y}_{i,3}\}
;

4.

\{\bm{z}_{i}^{\prime},\bm{y}_{i,1}^{\prime},\bm{y}_{i,2}^{\prime},\bm{y}_{i,3}^{\prime}\}
.

Fix all of the above selected points. Now for each copy of
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
with points
\bm{a}_{1},\bm{a}_{2},\bm{a}_{3},\bm{a}_{4}
, we define the following configuration based on
\mathcal{T}
with respect to the first two points
\{\bm{a}_{1},\bm{a}_{2}\}
as

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{T}^{\prime};\bm{a}_{1},\bm{a}_{2})=

\displaystyle\{\bm{a}_{i}\}\cup\{\bm{b}_{i}\}\cup\{\bm{c}_{i,1},\bm{c}_{i,2}\}\cup\{\bm{z}_{i},\bm{z}_{i}^{\prime}\}\cup\{y_{i,j},y_{i,j}^{\prime}\}\cup\{\mathcal{X}_{1}(\bm{z}_{i},\bm{y}_{i,1},\bm{y}_{i,2},\bm{y}_{i,3})\}

\displaystyle\cup\{\mathcal{X}_{1}(\bm{z}_{i}^{\prime},\bm{y}_{i,1}^{\prime},\bm{y}_{i,2}^{\prime},\bm{y}_{i,3}^{\prime})\}\cup\{\mathcal{X}_{1}(\bm{y}_{i,k},\bm{b}_{i},\bm{c}_{i,1},\bm{c}_{i,2})\}\cup\{\mathcal{X}_{1}(\bm{y}_{i,k}^{\prime},\bm{b}_{i+1},\bm{c}_{i,1},\bm{c}_{i,2})\},

where we omit the ranges for indices again for readability. We also define
\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{T}^{\prime};\bm{a}_{2},\bm{a}_{3})
similarly. Finally, take

\mathcal{X}=\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{T}^{\prime};\bm{a}_{1},\bm{a}_{2})\cup\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{T}^{\prime};\bm{a}_{2},\bm{a}_{3})\cup\mathcal{X}_{1}(\bm{a}_{1},\bm{a}_{2},\bm{a}_{3},\bm{a}_{4}).

Recall that
\chi
is an
r
-coloring of
\mathbb{E}^{n}
such that there is neither monochromatic nor rainbow copy of
\mathcal{T}.
The following series of claims together yield a contradiction. From now on, we use the notation in
4.11
unless stated otherwise.

Claim 4.12
.

There exists a copy of
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{X}.

Proof of claim.

By the definition of
\mathcal{X},
we have
\bm{a}_{1},\bm{a}_{2},\bm{a}_{3},\bm{a}_{4}\in\mathcal{X}
and
\bm{a}_{1}\bm{a}_{2}\bm{a}_{3}\bm{a}_{4}\cong\mathcal{T}^{\prime}.

∎

Temporarily relabel the points in
\mathcal{X}
as
\{\bm{v}_{1},\dots,\bm{v}_{m}\}
to apply
Theorem

4.2
with
m=|\mathcal{X}|
and
\mathbb{P}:=\mathbb{P}_{0}\times\mathbb{P}_{1}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{P}_{m}\cong\mathbb{E}^{n_{0}}\times\mathbb{E}^{n_{1}}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{E}^{n_{m}}
. Let
C_{i}
be the set of colors appearing in the sphere
S_{i}(\bm{p}_{i},\varepsilon).
By
Theorem

4.2
, we have
|C_{i}|\geq 2
for each
i\in[m].

Let
\{\bm{v}_{i_{1}},\bm{v}_{i_{2}},\bm{v}_{i_{3}},\bm{v}_{i_{4}}\}\subseteq\mathcal{X}
form a copy of
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
, where
\bm{v}_{i_{j}}\in\mathbb{P}_{i_{j}}
.

Claim 4.13
.

Let
C_{i_{1}},C_{i_{2}},C_{i_{3}},C_{i_{4}}
with
|C_{i_{j}}|\geq 2
for all
j
.
Assume there is no rainbow choice of distinct representatives from

\{C_{i_{1}},C_{i_{2}},C_{i_{3}},C_{i_{4}}\}
,
and there is no color contained in all four sets.
Then, up to relabeling the colors and the indices
i_{1},i_{2},i_{3},i_{4}
, the quadruple

(C_{i_{1}},C_{i_{2}},C_{i_{3}},C_{i_{4}})
is in exactly one of the following two forms:

1.

Type
\mathfrak{A}
:
C_{i_{1}}=\{1,2\}
,
C_{i_{2}}=\{2,3\}
,
C_{i_{3}}=\{1,3\}
, and
C_{i_{4}}\subseteq\{1,2,3\}
;

2.

Type
\mathfrak{B}
:
C_{i_{1}}=C_{i_{2}}=C_{i_{3}}=\{1,2\}
and
C_{i_{4}}\supseteq\{3,4\}
.

Proof of claim.

Write
\mathcal{C}=\{C_{i_{1}},C_{i_{2}},C_{i_{3}},C_{i_{4}}\}
and set

U:=C_{i_{1}}\cup C_{i_{2}}\cup C_{i_{3}}\cup C_{i_{4}}
.
We consider two cases according to
|U|
.

Case
|U|\geq 4
.

If every triple among the four sets had union of size at least
3
, then by Hall’s theorem
the family
\mathcal{C}
would admit a system of distinct representatives (take one color from
each set, all distinct), that is, a rainbow choice, contrary to the assumption.
Hence there exist distinct indices
m,k,\ell
with

|C_{i_{m}}\cup C_{i_{k}}\cup C_{i_{\ell}}|\leq 2
.
Since
|C_{i_{j}}|\geq 2
for all
j
, we must have

C_{i_{m}}=C_{i_{k}}=C_{i_{\ell}}=\{1,2\}
after relabeling colors.
To avoid a monochromatic choice, the remaining set
C_{i_{r}}
(with

\{m,k,\ell,r\}=\{1,2,3,4\}
) cannot contain
1
or
2
.
Because
|C_{i_{r}}|\geq 2
, we get
C_{i_{r}}\supseteq\{3,4\}
, which is precisely type
\mathfrak{B}
.

Case
|U|\leq 3
.

If
|U|\leq 2
, then every set in
\mathcal{C}
is a
2
-subset of the same
2
-element ground set,
so some color belongs to all four sets, contradicting the assumption.
Thus
|U|=3
. Up to relabeling,
U=\{1,2,3\}
, and the only
2
-subsets of
U
are

\{1,2\},\{2,3\},\{1,3\}
.
If one of these three pairs is absent from
\mathcal{C}
, say
\{1,2\}
does not occur,
then every member of
\mathcal{C}
contains color
3
, again giving a monochromatic choice.
Therefore all three pairs appear among
C_{i_{1}},C_{i_{2}},C_{i_{3}},C_{i_{4}}
.
Since
|U|=3
, necessarily
C_{i_{4}}\subseteq\{1,2,3\}
.
Relabeling indices if needed, we obtain exactly the pattern listed in type
\mathfrak{A}
.

This finishes the proof.
∎

Recall that there is no rainbow or monochromatic copy of
\mathcal{T}
under coloring
\chi
. Next it suffices to show that both cases in
4.13
cannot occur.

Claim 4.14
.

If there are five points in
\mathcal{X}
, say
\bm{v}_{i_{1}},\bm{v}_{i_{1}^{\prime}},\bm{v}_{i_{2}},\bm{v}_{i_{3}},\bm{v}_{i_{4}}
, forming the five-point configuration in
4.9
with
\bm{v}_{i_{1}}\bm{v}_{i_{2}}\bm{v}_{i_{3}}\bm{v}_{i_{4}},\bm{v}_{i_{1}^{\prime}}\bm{v}_{i_{2}}\bm{v}_{i_{3}}\bm{v}_{i_{4}}\cong\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
, then
C_{i_{1}}\cap C_{i_{2}}=\emptyset
if and only if
C_{i_{1}^{\prime}}\cap C_{i_{2}}=\emptyset.

Proof of claim.

If
C_{i_{1}}\cap C_{i_{2}}=\emptyset
, then the tetrahedron
\bm{v}_{i_{1}}\bm{v}_{i_{2}}\bm{v}_{i_{3}}\bm{v}_{i_{4}}
belongs to type
\mathfrak{B}
with
C_{i_{3}}=C_{i_{4}}
. Hence, either
|C_{i_{2}}\cup C_{i_{3}}\cup C_{i_{4}}|=2
or
|C_{i_{2}}\cup C_{i_{3}}\cup C_{i_{4}}|\geq 4
. Consequently, the tetrahedron
\bm{v}_{i_{1}^{\prime}}\bm{v}_{i_{2}}\bm{v}_{i_{3}}\bm{v}_{i_{4}}
belongs to type
\mathfrak{B}
as well, and furthermore
C_{i_{1}^{\prime}}\cap C_{i_{2}}=\emptyset.
Vice versa.
∎

From now on, with a slight abuse of notation, we use
C_{\bm{a}}
for the set of colors on the
\varepsilon
-sphere
S_{\bm{a}}(\bm{p}_{\bm{a}},\varepsilon)
centered at
\bm{a}.

Claim 4.15
.

If two simplices
\bm{a}\bm{b}\bm{c}\bm{d}
and
\bm{a}^{\prime}\bm{b}^{\prime}\bm{c}^{\prime}\bm{d}^{\prime}
congruent to
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
both belong to
\mathcal{X}
and furthermore their link
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{T}_{1}^{\prime},\mathcal{T}_{2}^{\prime})
belongs to
\mathcal{X}
as well, then they belong to the same type.

Proof of claim.

Apply
4.14
to the five-point structure
\bm{y}_{i,j}\bm{y}_{i,j+1}\bm{x}_{i}\bm{z}_{i,j,1}\bm{z}_{i,j,2}
, we have that
C_{\bm{y}_{i,j}}\cap C_{\bm{x_{i}}}=\emptyset
if and only if
C_{\bm{y}_{i,j+1}}\cap C_{\bm{x_{i}}}=\emptyset
. Since
\bm{y}_{i,1}=\bm{x}_{i-1}
and
\bm{y}_{i,k_{i}}=\bm{x}_{i+1}
, we have
C_{\bm{x}_{i-1}}\cap C_{\bm{x}_{i}}=\emptyset
if and only if
C_{\bm{x}_{i}}\cap C_{\bm{x}_{i+1}}=\emptyset.
Therefore,
C_{\bm{a}}\cap C_{\bm{b}}=\emptyset
if and only if
C_{\bm{a}^{\prime}}\cap C_{\bm{b}^{\prime}}=\emptyset
. Similarly,
C_{\bm{c}}\cap C_{\bm{d}}=\emptyset
if and only if
C_{\bm{c}^{\prime}}\cap C_{\bm{d}^{\prime}}=\emptyset
.

We shall recover their type in the following manner: if both
C_{\bm{a}}\cap C_{\bm{b}}
and
C_{\bm{c}}\cap C_{\bm{d}}
are non-empty, then
\{C_{\bm{a}},C_{\bm{b}},C_{\bm{c}},C_{\bm{d}}\}
belongs to type
\mathfrak{A}
; if one intersection is empty and the other one is not, then
\{C_{\bm{a}},C_{\bm{b}},C_{\bm{c}},C_{\bm{d}}\}
belongs to type
\mathfrak{B}.
In either case, these two simplices must belong to the same type.
∎

Claim 4.16
.

If there are some
4
-tuple of points
\{\bm{a},\bm{b},\bm{c},\bm{d}\}\subseteq\mathcal{X}
such that
\mathcal{X}_{1}(\bm{a},\bm{b},\bm{c},\bm{d})\subseteq\mathcal{X}
, then
C_{\bm{a}},C_{\bm{b}},C_{\bm{c}},C_{\bm{d}}
should belong to type
\mathfrak{A}.

Proof of claim.

Assume the contrary that
C_{\bm{a}},C_{\bm{b}},C_{\bm{c}},C_{\bm{d}}
belong to type
\mathfrak{B}.
Without of loss of generality, assume that
C_{\bm{a}}=C_{\bm{b}}=C_{\bm{c}}
and
C_{\bm{a}}\cap C_{\bm{d}}=\emptyset.
Apply
4.15
to linked simplices
\bm{z}_{i,j}\bm{u}_{i}\bm{x}_{i,j,1}\bm{x}_{i,j,2}
and
\bm{z}_{i,j+1}\bm{u}_{i}\bm{x}_{i,j,1}\bm{x}_{i,j,2}

\bm{z}_{1,1}=\bm{u}_{1}=\bm{a}
and
\bm{u}_{2}=\bm{b}
with

, it follows that

. In particular,
C_{\bm{a}}=C_{\bm{u}_{1}}=C_{\bm{u}_{2}}=\dots=C_{\bm{u}_{\ell_{3}}}=C_{\bm{d}}

∎

Claim 4.17
.

If twelve points in
\mathcal{X}
, say
\bm{v}_{z},\bm{v}_{y_{1}},\bm{v}_{y_{2}},\bm{v}_{y_{3}},\bm{v}_{x_{1}},\bm{v}_{x_{2}},\bm{v}_{x_{3}},\bm{v}_{z^{\prime}},\bm{v}_{y_{1}^{\prime}},\bm{v}_{y_{2}^{\prime}},\bm{v}_{y_{3}^{\prime}},\bm{v}_{x_{3}^{\prime}}
form two copies of the configuration in
4.10
sharing two points
\bm{v}_{x_{2}}
and
\bm{v}_{x_{3}}
, and non of these copies of
\mathcal{T^{\prime}}
belongs to type
\mathfrak{B}
, then
C_{\bm{x}_{3}}=C_{\bm{x}_{3}^{\prime}}.

Proof of claim.

For simplices of type
\mathfrak{B}
, the union of two color sets determines its palette, i.e.,
C_{\bm{x}_{1}}\cup C_{\bm{x}_{2}}=C_{\bm{x}_{1}}\cup C_{\bm{x}_{2}}\cup C_{\bm{x}_{3}}\cup C_{\bm{y}_{1}}
. Therefore,
|C_{\bm{x}_{3}}\cap C_{\bm{x}_{3}^{\prime}}|\geq 1
. We first show that
|C_{\bm{x}_{3}}\cap C_{\bm{x}_{3}^{\prime}}|\geq 2
. Assume the contrary and without loss of generality,
C_{\bm{x}_{3}^{\prime}}=\{1,2\}
and
C_{\bm{x}_{3}}=\{1,3\}.
Consider
\bm{x}_{1}
and
\bm{x}_{2}
, there are two general cases:

Case A:
C_{\bm{x}_{1}}=C_{\bm{x}_{2}}
.

Observe that in this case, we have
C_{\bm{x}_{1}}=C_{\bm{x}_{2}}=\{2,3\}.
Consider
\bm{y}_{i}\bm{x}_{1}\bm{x}_{2}\bm{x}_{3}
. Then

for all

. Hence
\bm{z}\bm{y}_{1}\bm{y}_{2}\bm{y}_{3}

Case B:

Consider the substructure

Since we glue a copy of

Apply

5
Concluding remarks

In this paper we resolve the canonical Ramsey property for
rectangles
and for
triangles
. More precisely, for every rectangle
\mathcal{R}
there exists a dimension
n_{0}=n_{0}(\mathcal{R})
, and for triangle
\mathcal{T}
one can take
n_{0}=4
, such that for every
r\in\mathbb{N}
and every
n\geq n_{0}
, every
r
-coloring of
\mathbb{E}^{n}
contains a monochromatic or a rainbow congruent copy of
\mathcal{R}
(resp.
\mathcal{T}
). The bound
n_{0}=4
for triangles is quite small; reducing it to
3
would be very interesting. For rectangles, the value
n_{0}(\mathcal{R})
produced by our argument is admittedly large, and optimizing its order remains an intriguing problem. In general,
n_{0}(\mathcal{R})
cannot be lowered to
2
; see
[
22
, Theorem 1.3]
for a precise construction.

Our methods and results naturally point to a broader question. Recall that a configuration
X\subseteq\mathbb{E}^{n}
is called
Ramsey
if for every
r
there exists a dimension
n=n(X,r)
with
\mathbb{E}^{n}\overset{r}{\rightarrow}X
. Among currently known examples, configurations that exhibit the canonical Ramsey property such as rectangles, triangles, and certain simplices are also Ramsey. This motivates the following conjecture.

Conjecture 1
.

If
\mathcal{S}
is Ramsey, then
\mathcal{S}
exhibits the canonical Ramsey property.

In particular, it is known that all rectangular sets and all simplices are Ramsey
[
10
]
. Does the canonical Ramsey property extend to all
rectangular sets
, that is, finite configurations contained in the vertex set of a rectangular box in
\mathbb{E}^{n}
? A positive answer would go well beyond the present case of full rectangles. Moreover, it remains a tantalizing open problem to determine whether all simplices have the canonical Ramsey property, an affirmative resolution would unify many of the special cases currently understood.

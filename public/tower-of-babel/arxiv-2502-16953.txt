A unified analysis on the speedup of accelerated gradient methods: An inertial dynamics approach
Zepeng Wang, Juan Peypouquet

Abstract
Nesterov’s Accelerated Gradient Method is one of the most popular first order optimization algorithms. When applied to L L -smooth, μ \mu -strongly convex functions, it converges at a rate of 𝒪 ⁡ ( ( 1 − μ L ) k ) \mathcal{O}\left(\left(1-\sqrt{\frac{\mu}{L}}\right)^{k}\right) . The more recent Triple Momentum Method and the Information Theoretic Exact Method enjoy an improved rate of 𝒪 ⁡ ( ( 1 − 2 ​ μ L ) k ) \mathcal{O}\left(\left(1-2\sqrt{\frac{\mu}{L}}\right)^{k}\right) . Their analysis relies on integral quadratic constraints and performance estimation techniques , respectively. In this work, we provide a dynamic explanation for this factor 2 speedup based on the subtle relationships between the coefficients of an inertial system with Hessian-driven damping. The standard explicit discretization of this second order ordinary differential equation produces intuitive variants of Nesterov’s method with sped-up convergence rates. The proof strategy allows us to extend the analysis beyond the strongly convex setting to account for convex functions with quadratic growth or the Polyak-Łojasiewicz inequality. Under uniqueness of the minimizer, we establish a factor 2 \sqrt{2} speedup with respect to the state of the art. With no assumption on the set of minimizers, the new convergence rate (asymptotically) matches that of Gradient Descent, a fact that was previously unknown.

Introduction

Let
H
be a real Hilbert space, and consider the convex optimization problem:

\min_{x\in H}f(x),

where
f:H\to\mathbb{R}
is convex and
L
-smooth, which means that
\nabla f
is Lipschitz-continuous with constant
L>0
. We assume that the solution set
S:\argmin(f)
is nonempty, and denote

and

Nesterov’s method for strongly convex functions.
If
f
is
\mu
-strongly convex, this problem can be solved by Nesterov’s
Accelerated Gradient Method
(AGM)
Nesterov (2004)
:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}y_{k+1}&=&x_{k}-s\nabla f(x_{k}),\\[3.0pt]
x_{k+1}&=&y_{k+1}+\frac{1-\sqrt{q}}{1+\sqrt{q}}(y_{k+1}-y_{k}),\end{array}\right.

(1)

which guarantees an accelerated linear convergence rate
Nesterov (2004)
;
Bansal and Gupta (2019)
;
Luo and Chen (2022)

f(y_{k+1})-f^{*}=\mathcal{O}\left((1-\sqrt{q})^{k}\right),\quad\hbox{with}\quad s=1/L\quad\hbox{and}\quad q:=\mu/L.

Siegel
Siegel (2019)
obtained a similar convergence rate for a slightly different algorithm whose iterations have four substeps, instead of two. In
Aujol et al. (2022)
, Siegel’s method was improved to achieve a convergence rate of
\mathcal{O}\left((1+\sqrt{2q})^{-k}\right)
, which was also obtained in
Chen and Luo (2021)
for an algorithm composed of three steps and based on a Gauss-Seidel type discretization of an inertial system, and in
Park et al. (2023)
for a variant of the so-called
Optimized Gradient Method for Strongly Convex functions
(OGM-SC)
Drori and Teboulle (2014)
;
Kim and Fessler (2016)
;
Kim and Fessler (2017)
, borrowing tools from linear coupling analysis in
Allen-Zhu and Orecchia (2017)
. The best convergence rate known to date is
\mathcal{O}\left((1-2\sqrt{q})^{k}\right)
, achieved by the
Triple Momentum Method
(TMM) of
Van Scoy et al. (2018)
, whose design and analysis rely on integral quadratic constraints
Lessard et al. (2016)
, and by the
Information Theoretic Exact Method
(ITEM) of
Taylor and Drori (2023)
, based on performance estimation techniques
Drori and Teboulle (2014)
. A unified and intuitive explanation for this

Continuous time models.

From continuous dynamics to algorithms.

The three coefficients in (
HBF-H

4

Van Scoy et al. (2018)
;
Taylor and Drori (2023)

Our contribution.
The starting point of the present research was to better understand, from the perspective of inertial dynamics, the parameter choices that lead to faster convergence rates.

1.

We unify the Lyapunov analysis for (
HBF-H
), and explain how the components of the energy function can be tuned in order to explain the speedup. For example, we establish a convergence rate of
f(x)-f^{*}=\mathcal{O}\left(e^{-\sqrt{\mu}t}\right)
for (
3
), improving on
Shi et al. (2022)
by a factor of
4
, and removing the gap with respect to (
2
). If one fixes
\gamma\sim 1
(as
\mu\to 0
) to account for rescaling, we improve on existing rates by an additional factor of
\sqrt{2}
. If one increases the value of
\gamma
from
\gamma\sim 1
to
\gamma\sim 2
(as
\mu\to 0
), one can improve the convergence rate by yet an additional factor of
\sqrt{2}
. For
\gamma\sim 2
, we derive a fast convergence rate
f(x)-f^{*}=\mathcal{O}\left(e^{-2\sqrt{\mu}t}\right)
. The range
\gamma\in[1,2]
is important, as we explain later. In the absence of strong convexity, one must accept more conservative convergence rate guarantees
Yue et al. (2023)
. Nevertheless, convergence rate analysis of first-order methods under relaxed strong convexity conditions
Aujol et al. (2022)
;
Aujol et al. (2023)
;
Bolte et al. (2017)
;
Zhang (2017)
;
Karimi et al. (2016)
;
Drusvyatskiy and Lewis (2018)
;
Necoara et al. (2019)
is an interesting topic with relevant consequences in many practical applications. With this in mind, we obtain analogous results for functions with quadratic growth (we refer the reader to Appendix
5
for further details, including its connection with strong convexity and the Polyak-Łojasiewicz inequality). As before, we recover the state-of-the-art rates and identify the mechanism that makes speedup possible. The estimations for general
\gamma
bring insight that can be transposed to the algorithmic setting.

2.

Using the insight gained from the continuous-time models, we refine the Lyapunov analysis to derive convergence rates for the algorithms in the family defined by (
4
). We identify regions in the parameter space that guarantee the stability of the discretization and the subsequent preservation of the linear convergence. In the strongly convex case, the analysis is valid for
\gamma\in[1,2]
, whence the importance of this interval. By appropriately tuning the parameters, one obtains algorithms with the fastest convergence rates known to date, which asymptotically match
Van Scoy et al. (2018)
;
Taylor and Drori (2023)
. For functions with quadratic growth, we strictly improve upon existing results. For example, for convex functions with a unique minimizer, we obtain a convergence rate of
f(x)-f^{*}=\mathcal{O}\left((1+2(\sqrt{2}-1)\sqrt{q})^{-k}\right)
, which gives a factor
\sqrt{2}
improvement with respect to the best convergence rate so far, namely
\mathcal{O}\left((1+(2-\sqrt{2})\sqrt{q})^{-k}\right)

Aujol et al. (2023)
.

2.3
Convexity, quadratic growth and a unique minimizer

In this subsection, we apply the idea of time rescaling to accelerate the convergence of system (
HBF-H
) outside of the strongly convex setting. We consider here the case of convex functions with
\mu
-quadratic growth

f(x)-f^{*}\geq\frac{\mu}{2}\dist(x,S)^{2},

and having a unique minimizer.

Our analysis relies on the energy function
W
, defined in (
5
), but now using
\omega=1
and
\xi=\frac{2+r}{3+r}\alpha
for an appropriate value of
r
which we will select in a moment. We begin by writing

\displaystyle W_{3}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left\|\dot{x}+\beta\nabla f(x)+\left(\frac{2+r}{3+r}\right)\alpha(x-x^{*})\right\|^{2}-\frac{(2+r)\alpha^{2}}{2(3+r)^{2}}\|x-x^{*}\|^{2}

(10)

\displaystyle+\theta\left(f(x)-f^{*}\right),

. Setting
\omega=1
and

\displaystyle\dot{W_{3}}+\frac{2\alpha}{3+r}\,W_{3}

\displaystyle\leq-\beta\left(\theta-\frac{1}{2}\right)\|\nabla f(x)\|^{2}-\frac{(2+r)\alpha\theta}{3+r}\langle\nabla f(x),x-x^{*}\rangle

\displaystyle\quad+\underbrace{\frac{2\alpha\theta}{3+r}\left(f(x)-f^{*}\right)}_{\mathbf{I}}+\underbrace{\frac{(2+r)\alpha^{3}}{2(3+r)^{3}}\left[2(1+r)+\frac{2+r}{3+r}\alpha\beta\right]\|x-x^{*}\|^{2}}_{\mathbf{II}}.

Since
f
is convex with quadratic growth and has a unique minimizer, we have

\langle\nabla f(x),x-x^{*}\rangle\geq\max\left\{f(x)-f^{*},\,\frac{\mu}{2}\|x-x^{*}\|^{2}\right\},

so that

\mathbf{I}\leq\frac{2\alpha\theta}{3+r}\langle\nabla f(x),x-x^{*}\rangle,

and

\mathbf{II}\leq\frac{(2+r)\alpha^{3}}{\mu(3+r)^{3}}\left[2(1+r)+\frac{2+r}{3+r}\alpha\beta\right]\langle\nabla f(x),x-x^{*}\rangle.

Whence, we deduce that

\dot{W_{3}}+\frac{2\alpha}{3+r}W_{3}+\beta\left(\theta-\frac{1}{2}\right)\|\nabla f(x)\|^{2}\leq-\frac{\alpha R}{\mu(3+r)}\langle\nabla f(x),x-x^{*}\rangle,

where

R=r\mu\theta-\frac{\alpha^{2}(2+r)}{(3+r)^{2}}\left(2(1+r)+\frac{2+r}{3+r}\alpha\beta\right).

If we guarantee
R\geq 0
, then we will have

\dot{W_{3}}+\frac{2\alpha}{3+r}W_{3}+\beta\left(\theta-\frac{1}{2}\right)\|\nabla f(x)\|^{2}\leq 0.

(11)

Ideally, we would want to maximize
\frac{2\alpha}{3+r}
, subject to
r>0
and
R\geq 0
. The constraint
R\geq 0
can be expressed as

\left(\frac{\alpha}{3+r}\right)^{2}\leq\frac{r\mu\theta}{(2+r)\left(2(1+r)+\frac{2+r}{3+r}\alpha\beta\right)}.

(12)

To make the rate independent from
\beta
(in line with our previous results as well as the literature on the subject), we consider the problem

\sup_{r>0}\,\inf_{\beta>0}\,\left[\frac{r\mu\theta}{(2+r)\left(2(1+r)+\frac{2+r}{3+r}\alpha\beta\right)}\right]=\sup_{r>0}\,\left[\frac{r\mu\theta}{2(1+r)(2+r)}\right],

whose solution is
r=\sqrt{2}
. We shall use this value of
r
,
2
2

2

We acknowledge that this leaves room for improvement in this choice, but

is sufficiently good to obtain our desired improvement on the convergence rate, both in continuous time and in the algorithmic setting discussed in the next section.
and then enforce (

\alpha

\displaystyle R

\displaystyle=\sqrt{2}\mu\theta-\frac{\alpha^{2}(2+\sqrt{2})}{(3+\sqrt{2})^{2}}\left(2(1+\sqrt{2})+\frac{2+\sqrt{2}}{3+\sqrt{2}}\alpha\beta\right)

\displaystyle=\sqrt{2}\left[\mu\theta-\alpha^{2}\frac{(2+\sqrt{2})^{2}}{(3+\sqrt{2})^{2}}\left(1+\frac{\alpha\beta}{2+3\sqrt{2}}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\sqrt{2}\left(1+\frac{\alpha\beta}{2+3\sqrt{2}}\right)\left[\mu m-\alpha^{2}\left(\frac{2+\sqrt{2}}{3+\sqrt{2}}\right)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\sqrt{2}\left(1+\frac{\alpha\beta}{2+3\sqrt{2}}\right)\left(\frac{2+\sqrt{2}}{3+\sqrt{2}}\right)^{2}\left[\mu m\left(2-\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\right)^{2}-\alpha^{2}\right],

where we have written

, which imposes the relationship

\gamma=m\left(1+\tfrac{\alpha\beta}{2+3\sqrt{2}}\right)+\tfrac{\alpha\beta}{3+\sqrt{2}}.

We are now in a position to prove the main result of this subsection, namely:

Theorem 5
.

Let

be convex, have quadratic growth with constant
\mu>0
and admit a unique minimizer. Pick
m\geq\frac{1}{2}
, and consider the system (
HBF-H

\alpha\in\left(0,\left(2-\tfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\right)\sqrt{\mu m}\right],\quad\beta>0,\quad\gamma=m\left(1+\tfrac{\alpha\beta}{2+3\sqrt{2}}\right)+\tfrac{\alpha\beta}{3+\sqrt{2}}.

If
v_{0}=-\beta\nabla f(x_{0})
, we have, for every

f\big(x(t)\big)-f^{*}\leq(1+\sqrt{2})\big(f(x_{0})-f^{*}\big)e^{-\frac{2}{3+\sqrt{2}}\alpha t}.

Moreover,

\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{\frac{2\alpha}{3+\sqrt{2}}t}\|\nabla f\left(x(t)\right)\|^{2}dt<\infty.

Proof.

Since
\beta>0
and

\theta=\gamma-\tfrac{\alpha\beta}{3+\sqrt{2}}=m\left(1+\tfrac{\alpha\beta}{2+3\sqrt{2}}\right)>m\geq\frac{1}{2},

inequality (
11
) with
r=\sqrt{2}
gives

W_{3}(t)\leq W_{3}(0)e^{-\frac{2}{3+\sqrt{2}}\alpha t}\quad\hbox{and}\quad\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{\frac{2\alpha}{3+\sqrt{2}}t}\|\nabla f\left(x(t)\right)\|^{2}dt<\infty.

Using
v_{0}=-\beta\nabla f(x_{0})
and
\|x_{0}-x^{*}\|^{2}\leq\frac{2}{\mu}(f(x_{0}-f^{*})
in (
10

\displaystyle W_{3}(0)

\displaystyle=\frac{(1+r)(2+r)}{2(3+r)^{2}}\alpha^{2}\|x_{0}-x^{*}\|^{2}+\theta\left(f(x_{0})-f^{*}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\left[\frac{(1+r)(2+r)}{(3+r)^{2}}\left(\frac{\alpha^{2}}{\mu}\right)+\theta\right]\left(f(x_{0})-f^{*}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{1+r}{2+r}m+\theta\right)\left(f(x_{0})-f^{*}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{m}{\sqrt{2}}+\theta\right)\left(f(x_{0})-f^{*}\right)\quad(\text{since }r=\sqrt{2})

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{\sqrt{2}+1+\frac{\alpha\beta}{3+\sqrt{2}}}{\sqrt{2}+\frac{\alpha\beta}{3+\sqrt{2}}}\right)\theta\left(f(x_{0})-f^{*}\right).

Since

f(x)-f^{*}\geq\frac{\mu}{2}\|x-x^{*}\|^{2}
for every

f\left(x(t)\right)-f^{*}\leq\frac{\mu W_{3}(t)}{\mu\theta-\frac{2+r}{(3+r)^{2}}\alpha^{2}}\leq\frac{W_{3}(t)}{\theta-\frac{m}{2+r}}=\frac{\sqrt{2}+\frac{\alpha\beta}{3+\sqrt{2}}}{1+\frac{\alpha\beta}{3+\sqrt{2}}}\left(\frac{W_{3}(0)}{\theta}\right)e^{-\frac{2}{3+\sqrt{2}}\alpha t},

and the result follows.

Setting

2.4
Polyak-Łojasiewicz inequality

In this subsection, we derive a linear convergence rate for smooth functions satisfying the Polyak-Łojasiewicz (PŁ) inequality with constant
\mu>0
:

\|\nabla f(x)\|^{2}\geq 2\mu\big(f(x)-f^{*}\big),\quad\forall x\in H,

(13)

even without requiring convexity. The relationship between the PŁ inequality and quadratic growth is explained by Corollary
18
and Proposition
19
. Consequently, Theorem
6
below can be expressed in terms of quadratic growth, with an appropriate modification of the constants.

As before, we shall use the energy function
W
, defined in (
5
), but we now set
\xi=0
, which gives
\theta=\gamma-\alpha\beta
.

We have the following:

Theorem 6
.

Let
f:H\to\mathbb{R}
satisfy the Polyak-Łojasiewicz inequality (
13
) with constant
\mu>0
. Pick
\theta>0
, and consider the system (
HBF-H
), where

\beta>0,\quad\gamma=\theta+\alpha\beta.

If
v_{0}=-\beta\nabla f(x_{0})
, we have, for every
t\geq 0
,

f\big(x(t)\big)-f^{*}\leq\big(f(x_{0})-f^{*}\big)e^{-2\delta t},

where
\delta:=\min\{\alpha,\mu\beta\}
.

Proof.

Let
W_{4}
be the realization of the function
W
, defined by (
5
), with
\xi=0
, namely:

W_{4}(t)=\frac{1}{2}\left\|\dot{x}+\beta\nabla f(x)\right\|^{2}+\theta\left(f(x)-f^{*}\right),

where
\theta=\gamma-\alpha\beta>0
. We shall not use Lemma
1
this time. Instead, we first compute

\displaystyle\dot{W_{4}}

\displaystyle=\left\langle\dot{x}+\beta\nabla f(x),\ddot{x}+\beta\nabla^{2}f(x)\dot{x}\right\rangle+\theta\langle\nabla f(x),\dot{x}\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle\dot{x}+\beta\nabla f(x),-\alpha\dot{x}-\gamma\nabla f(x)\right\rangle+\theta\langle\nabla f(x),\dot{x}\rangle

\displaystyle=-\alpha\|\dot{x}+\beta\nabla f(x)\|^{2}-\underbrace{(\gamma-\alpha\beta-\theta)}_{=0}\langle\nabla f(x),\dot{x}\rangle-\underbrace{(\gamma-\alpha\beta)}_{=\theta}\beta\|\nabla f(x)\|^{2}

\displaystyle=-\alpha\|\dot{x}+\beta\nabla f(x)\|^{2}-\beta\theta\|\nabla f(x)\|^{2}.

Using the Polyak-Łojasiewicz inequality (
13
), we deduce that

\dot{W_{4}}\leq-\alpha\|\dot{x}+\beta\nabla f(x)\|^{2}-2\mu\beta\theta\left(f(x)-f^{*}\right)\leq-2\delta\,W_{4}.

As a result,

W_{4}(t)\leq W_{4}(0)e^{-2\delta t}.

Since
v_{0}=-\beta\nabla f(x_{0})
, we have
W_{4}(0)=\theta\left(f(x_{0})-f^{*}\right)
, whence

f\left(x(t)\right)-f^{*}\leq\frac{W_{4}(t)}{\theta}\leq\frac{W_{4}(0)}{\theta}e^{-2\delta t}\leq\left(f(x_{0})-f^{*}\right)e^{-2\delta t},

as claimed.
∎

Remark 6
.

As shown in Corollary
18
, the PŁ inequality implies quadratic growth. Theorem
6
above is different from Theorem
5
, since it neither requires convexity nor uniqueness of the minimizer.

In this section, we study accelerated gradient algorithms and establish linear convergence rates for the corresponding function values. These algorithms are obtained by explicit discretization of the inertial system (

2

\sqrt{2}
and then
2
for smooth strongly convex functions. This is given in Subsections
3.2
and
3.3

3.1
Discretization of (
HBF-H
) and some useful estimations

In this subsection, we show the discretization procedures of obtaining the associated inertial algorithms for system (
HBF-H
), and present some useful estimations that will be used in the subsequent analysis.

Recall that
f
is
L
-smooth. We define the step size
h:=\frac{1}{\sqrt{L}}
, set
\beta=h
and approximate the dynamics (
HBF-H
) by (
4

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}y_{k+1}&=&x_{k}-h^{2}\nabla f(x_{k}),\\[2.0pt]
x_{k+1}&=&y_{k+1}+\frac{1}{1+\alpha h}(y_{k+1}-y_{k})+\left(\frac{\gamma}{1+\alpha h}-1\right)(y_{k+1}-x_{k}).\end{array}\right.

(AGM)

Note that the iterates satisfy

(x_{k+1}-y_{k+1})-(x_{k}-y_{k})=-\alpha h(x_{k+1}-x_{k})-\gamma h^{2}\nabla f(x_{k}).

(14)

Remark 7
.

The algorithm (
AGM
) can be initialized with given
x_{0}
and
y_{0}

14
), one has

(1+\alpha h)(x_{1}-x_{0})=x_{0}-y_{0}-(\gamma+1)h^{2}\nabla f(x_{0}),

which implies that we can determine
y_{0}
by specifying
x_{0}
and
x_{1}

y_{0}=-(1+\alpha h)(x_{1}-x_{0})-(\gamma+1)h^{2}\nabla f(x_{0})+x_{0}.

This observation will be useful in the subsequent convergence analysis.

We have the following:

Lemma 7
.

Let
f
be
\mu
-strongly convex and
L
-smooth. Let
(x_{k})
and
(y_{k})
be generated by (
AGM
). Define

\psi_{k}:=f(x_{k})-f^{*}-(h^{2}/2)\|\nabla f(x_{k})\|^{2}.

(15)

Then, we have

\langle\nabla f(x_{k}),x_{k+1}-x_{k}\rangle\geq\frac{1}{1+\alpha h}\left[\psi_{k}-\psi_{k-1}-\gamma h^{2}\|\nabla f(x_{k})\|^{2}+\frac{\mu}{2(1-\mu h^{2})}\|y_{k+1}-y_{k}\|^{2}\right],

and

\langle\nabla f(x_{k}),x_{k}-x^{*}\rangle\geq\psi_{k}+\frac{h^{2}}{2}\|\nabla f(x_{k})\|^{2}+\frac{\mu}{2}\|x_{k}-x^{*}\|^{2}.

Proof.

We begin by using (
AGM
), to obtain

x_{k}-x_{k-1}=(1+\alpha h)(x_{k+1}-x_{k})+h^{2}\left(\nabla f(x_{k})-\nabla f(x_{k-1})\right)+\gamma h^{2}\nabla f(x_{k}).

Since
f
is
\mu
-strongly convex and
L
-smooth, we have

\displaystyle\quad f(x_{k})-f(x_{k-1})

\displaystyle\leq\langle\nabla f(x_{k}),x_{k}-x_{k-1}\rangle-\frac{h^{2}}{2}\|\nabla f(x_{k})-\nabla f(x_{k-1})\|^{2}-\frac{\mu\|y_{k+1}-y_{k}\|^{2}}{2(1-\mu h^{2})}

(16)

\displaystyle=(1+\alpha h)\langle\nabla f(x_{k}),x_{k+1}-x_{k}\rangle+h^{2}\left\langle\nabla f(x_{k}),\nabla f(x_{k})-\nabla f(x_{k-1})\right\rangle

\displaystyle\quad+\gamma h^{2}\|\nabla f(x_{k})\|^{2}-\frac{h^{2}}{2}\|\nabla f(x_{k})-\nabla f(x_{k-1})\|^{2}-\frac{\mu}{2(1-\mu h^{2})}\|y_{k+1}-y_{k}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=(1+\alpha h)\langle\nabla f(x_{k}),x_{k+1}-x_{k}\rangle+\frac{h^{2}}{2}\|\nabla f(x_{k})\|^{2}-\frac{h^{2}}{2}\|\nabla f(x_{k-1})\|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\gamma h^{2}\|\nabla f(x_{k})\|^{2}-\frac{\mu}{2(1-\mu h^{2})}\|y_{k+1}-y_{k}\|^{2},

so that

\displaystyle\psi_{k}-\psi_{k-1}

\displaystyle\leq(1+\alpha h)\langle\nabla f(x_{k}),x_{k+1}-x_{k}\rangle+\gamma h^{2}\|\nabla f(x_{k})\|^{2}-\frac{\mu}{2(1-\mu h^{2})}\|y_{k+1}-y_{k}\|^{2}.

This gives the first claim. Likewise,

\langle\nabla f(x_{k}),x_{k}-x^{*}\rangle\geq f(x_{k})-f^{*}+\frac{\mu}{2}\|x_{k}-x^{*}\|^{2}=\psi_{k}+\frac{h^{2}}{2}\|\nabla f(x_{k})\|^{2}+\frac{\mu}{2}\|x_{k}-x^{*}\|^{2},

which allows us to conclude.
∎

Remark 9

Remark 10

The following inequalities will facilitate the forthcoming analysis.

Lemma 8

Let

. For every

\frac{(1+ax)(1+bx)}{1+cx}\leq 1+x\max\left\{a+b-c,\frac{ab}{c}\right\},

3.2
Strong convexity: factor
\sqrt{2}

In this subsection, we present the convergence results which improve from that of Nesterov’s method (
1
) to those of
Chen and Luo (2021)
;
Park et al. (2023)
, with a factor of
\sqrt{2}
. As one will see, the improvement can be attributed to an increasing value of
\gamma
in (
AGM
).

Our analysis centers around the energy sequence
(E_{k})_{k\geq 1}
, defined by

E_{k}=\frac{1}{2}\|\phi_{k}\|^{2}+\theta h^{2}\psi_{k-1},

(19)

where
\psi_{k}
is given by (
15
), and

\phi_{k}=(x_{k}-y_{k})+\frac{\frac{1}{2}\alpha h}{1+\frac{1}{2}\alpha h}(x_{k}-x^{*}),\quad\theta=\frac{\gamma-\frac{1}{2}\alpha h}{1+\frac{1}{2}\alpha h}.

Remark 11
.

The
(E_{k})_{k\geq 1}
sequence was introduced in
Park et al. (2023)
. In particular, the term
\psi_{k-1}
will facilitate the Lyapunov analysis. It provides an upper bound for the function values, namely:
f(y_{k})-f^{*}\leq\psi_{k-1}
.

We have the following lemma, whose proof we postpone to Appendix
7
:

Lemma 9
.

Let
f
be strongly convex. Let
(x_{k})
and
(y_{k})
be generated by (
AGM
) with
\gamma\in[1+\alpha h,2+\alpha h]
. Consider
(E_{k})_{k\geq 1}
defined by (
19
). Then,

\left(1+\frac{1}{2}\alpha h\right)E_{k+1}-E_{k}+\frac{1}{4}\alpha h^{5}\|\nabla f(x_{k})\|^{2}\leq\frac{\frac{1}{16}\alpha h^{3}(\alpha^{2}-4\mu\gamma)}{\left(1+\frac{1}{2}\alpha h\right)^{2}}\|x_{k}-x^{*}\|^{2}.

We are now in a position to present the main result of this subsection:

Theorem 10
.

Let
f:H\to\mathbb{R}
be
\mu
-strongly convex and
L
-smooth. Pick
m\in[1,2]
. Let
(x_{k})
and
(y_{k})
be generated by (
AGM
), where

h=1/\sqrt{L},\quad\hbox{}\quad\alpha=2\sqrt{\mu m},\quad\hbox{and}\quad\gamma=m+\alpha h.

If
y_{0}=-\left(\gamma-\frac{1}{2}\alpha h\right)h^{2}\nabla f(x_{0})+x_{0}
, then for every
k\geq 1
,

f(y_{k})-f^{*}\leq\left[\frac{\mu}{2}\|x_{0}-x^{*}\|^{2}+\left(f(x_{0})-f^{*}\right)\right](1+\rho)^{-k+1},

where
\rho=\sqrt{qm}
and
q=\mu/L
. Moreover,
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(1+\rho)^{k}\|\nabla f(x_{k})\|^{2}<\infty
. In particular,
\|\nabla f(x_{k})\|^{2}=o\big((1+\rho)^{-k}\big)
.

Proof.

Note that
\alpha^{2}=4\mu m\leq 4\mu\gamma
. It follows from Lemma
9
that

(1+\rho)E_{k+1}-E_{k}+\frac{1}{2}\rho h^{4}\|\nabla f(x_{k})\|^{2}\leq 0,\quad\hbox{with}\quad\rho=\sqrt{qm}.

Hence,

E_{k}\leq(1+\rho)^{-k+1}E_{1}\quad\hbox{and}\quad\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(1+\rho)^{k}\|\nabla f(x_{k})\|^{2}<\infty.

Recalling Remark
7
, we can verify that the choice of
y_{0}=-\left(\gamma-\frac{1}{2}\alpha h\right)h^{2}\nabla f(x_{0})+x_{0}
guarantees

\displaystyle\phi_{1}

\displaystyle=(x_{1}-y_{1})+\frac{\frac{1}{2}\alpha h}{1+\frac{1}{2}\alpha h}(x_{1}-x^{*})

\displaystyle=\underbrace{\left(\frac{1+\alpha h}{1+\frac{1}{2}\alpha h}\right)(x_{1}-x_{0})+h^{2}\nabla f(x_{0})}_{=0}+\frac{\frac{1}{2}\alpha h}{1+\frac{1}{2}\alpha h}(x_{0}-x^{*})

\displaystyle=\frac{\frac{1}{2}\alpha h}{1+\frac{1}{2}\alpha h}(x_{0}-x^{*}).

Hence, in view of (
19
), we obtain

\displaystyle E_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{\frac{1}{8}\alpha^{2}h^{2}}{\left(1+\frac{1}{2}\alpha h\right)^{2}}\|x_{0}-x^{*}\|^{2}+\theta h^{2}\left(f(x_{0})-f^{*}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\frac{1}{2}\mu mh^{2}}{1+\frac{1}{2}\alpha h}\|x_{0}-x^{*}\|^{2}+\theta h^{2}\left(f(x_{0})-f^{*}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\theta h^{2}\left[\frac{\mu}{2}\|x_{0}-x^{*}\|^{2}+\left(f(x_{0})-f^{*}\right)\right],

due to strong convexity and
\frac{m}{1+\frac{1}{2}\alpha h}\leq\theta
. Whence, we deduce that

f(y_{k})-f^{*}\leq\psi_{k-1}\leq\frac{E_{k}}{\theta h^{2}}\leq\left[\frac{\mu}{2}\|x_{0}-x^{*}\|^{2}+\left(f(x_{0})-f^{*}\right)\right](1+\rho)^{-k+1},

for every
k\geq 1
, as claimed.
∎

Remark 12
.

Setting
m=1
so that
\alpha=2\sqrt{\mu}
and
\gamma=1+\alpha h
, one gets a convergence rate
f(y_{k})-f^{*}=\mathcal{O}\left((1+\sqrt{q})^{-k}\right)
for Nesterov-type method
Nesterov (2004)
:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}y_{k+1}&=&x_{k}-h^{2}\nabla f(x_{k}),\\[2.0pt]
x_{k+1}&=&y_{k+1}+\frac{1}{1+\alpha h}(y_{k+1}-y_{k}).\end{array}\right.

By increasing the value of
\gamma
, one gets a faster convergence rate. In particular, setting
m=2
so that
\alpha=2\sqrt{2\mu}
and
\gamma=2+\alpha h
, one has
f(y_{k})-f^{*}=\mathcal{O}\left((1+\sqrt{2q})^{-k}\right)
for OGM-SC-type method
Park et al. (2023)
:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}y_{k+1}&=&x_{k}-h^{2}\nabla f(x_{k}),\\[2.0pt]
x_{k+1}&=&y_{k+1}+\frac{1}{1+\alpha h}(y_{k+1}-y_{k})+\frac{1}{1+\alpha h}(y_{k+1}-x_{k}).\end{array}\right.

The
\sqrt{2}
improvement in the convergence rate is in line with the results in the continuous time.

Remark 13
.

Note that (
AGM
) is obtained using explicit discretization of the time-rescaled inertial system (
HBF-H
). In spite of the promising convergence rates in the continuous time using time rescaling, there exist constraints in recovering these results in the discrete time: the value of
\gamma
cannot be arbitrarily large and satisfies
\gamma_{\max}\sim 2
(see also Theorem
12
below). The constraints on
\gamma
is in general due to the
L
-smoothness of
f

Attouch et al. (2019)
.

3.3
Strong convexity: factor
2
acceleration

In this subsection, we derive faster convergence rates which range from a factor of
\sqrt{2}
to
2
, matching the state-of-the-art results. The improved convergence rates are obtained using time rescaling and a new energy sequence.

Our analysis centers around the energy sequence
(E_{k})_{k\geq 1}
, defined by

E_{k}=\frac{1}{2}\|\phi_{k}\|^{2}-\frac{\eta}{2}\|y_{k}-x^{*}\|^{2}+\theta h^{2}\psi_{k-1},

(20)

where
\psi_{k}
is given by (
15
), and

\displaystyle\phi_{k}

\displaystyle=(x_{k}-y_{k})+\frac{2}{3}\alpha h(x_{k}-x^{*}),\quad\eta=\frac{\frac{2}{9}\alpha^{2}h^{2}}{1+\alpha h},

\displaystyle\theta

\displaystyle=\gamma-\frac{1}{3}\alpha h\left(\frac{1+\frac{2}{3}\alpha h}{1+\alpha h}\right).

Remark 14
.

Let
f
be
L
-smooth, have
\mu
-quadratic growth and admit a unique minimizer. Consider an energy sequence
(E_{k})_{k\geq 1}
of the form (
20
). Observe that

\displaystyle E_{k}

\displaystyle\geq-\frac{\eta}{2}\left\|y_{k}-x^{*}\right\|^{2}+\theta h^{2}\left[f(x_{k-1})-f^{*}-\frac{h^{2}}{2}\|\nabla f(x_{k-1})\|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\geq-\frac{\eta}{2}\left\|y_{k}-x^{*}\right\|^{2}+\theta h^{2}\left(f(y_{k})-f^{*}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\left(\theta h^{2}-\frac{\eta}{\mu}\right)\left(f(y_{k})-f^{*}\right).

If there exists a constant
\rho>0
such that

(1+\rho)E_{k+1}-E_{k}\leq 0,

and
\theta h^{2}>\frac{\eta}{\mu}
, we can deduce that

f(y_{k})-f^{*}\leq\frac{E_{k}}{\theta h^{2}-\frac{\eta}{\mu}}\leq\frac{E_{1}(1+\rho)^{-k+1}}{\theta h^{2}-\frac{\eta}{\mu}},\quad\forall k\geq 1,

which implies a linear convergence rate for
f(y_{k})-f^{*}
.

We have the following result, whose proof can be found in Appendix
8
:

Lemma 11
.

Let
f
be strongly convex. Let
(x_{k})
and
(y_{k})
be generated by (
AGM
) with
\gamma\in[1,2]
and
\alpha h\in(0,3]
. Consider
(E_{k})_{k\geq 1}
defined by (
20
). Then,

\displaystyle\left(1+\frac{\frac{2}{3}\alpha h}{1+\frac{2}{3}\alpha h}\right)E_{k+1}-E_{k}+\frac{\frac{1}{3}\alpha h^{5}\|\nabla f(x_{k})\|^{2}}{1+\frac{2}{3}\alpha h}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\frac{2}{27}\alpha h^{3}(1+2\alpha h)\left(\alpha^{2}-\frac{9}{2}\mu\gamma\right)}{\left(1+\frac{2}{3}\alpha h\right)(1+\alpha h)}\|x_{k}-x^{*}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\frac{1}{9}h^{2}\left(\alpha^{2}-\frac{9}{2}\mu\gamma\right)}{1+\alpha h}\|y_{k+1}-y_{k}\|^{2}.

Now we present the main result of this subsection in what follows.

Theorem 12
.

Let
f:H\to\mathbb{R}
be
\mu
-strongly convex and
L
-smooth. Let
(x_{k})
and
(y_{k})
be generated by (
AGM
), where

h=1/\sqrt{L},\quad\hbox{}\quad\gamma\in[1,2],\quad\hbox{and}\quad\alpha=3\sqrt{\mu\gamma/2}.

If
y_{0}=-\left(\gamma-\frac{\frac{1}{3}\alpha h}{1+\frac{2}{3}\alpha h}\right)h^{2}\nabla f(x_{0})+x_{0}
, then for every
k\geq 1
,

f(y_{k})-f^{*}\leq C(1+\rho)^{-k+1},

where
\rho=\frac{\sqrt{2q\gamma}}{1+\sqrt{2q\gamma}}
,
q=\mu/L
, and

C=\frac{1}{2-\sqrt{2}}\left(3+\sqrt{2/(q\gamma)}\right)\left[\mu\|x_{0}-x^{*}\|^{2}+\left(f(x_{0})-f^{*}\right)\right].

Moreover,
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(1+\rho)^{k}\|\nabla f(x_{k})\|^{2}<\infty
, and so
\|\nabla f(x_{k})\|^{2}=o\big((1+\rho)^{-k}\big)
.

Proof.

Note that
\alpha h=3\sqrt{q\gamma/2}\leq 3
since
q\leq 1
and
\gamma\leq 2
. It follows from Lemma
11
that

(1+\rho)E_{k+1}-E_{k}+\frac{1}{2}\rho h^{4}\|\nabla f(x_{k})\|^{2}\leq 0,\quad\hbox{with}\quad\rho=\frac{\sqrt{2q\gamma}}{1+\sqrt{2q\gamma}}.

Hence,

E_{k}\leq(1+\rho)^{-k+1}E_{1}\quad\hbox{and}\quad\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(1+\rho)^{k}\|\nabla f(x_{k})\|^{2}<\infty.

Recalling Remark
7
, it is easy to verify that the choice of
y_{0}=-\left(\gamma-\frac{\frac{1}{3}\alpha h}{1+\frac{2}{3}\alpha h}\right)h^{2}\nabla f(x_{0})+x_{0}
leads to

\displaystyle\phi_{1}

\displaystyle=(x_{1}-y_{1})+\frac{2}{3}\alpha h(x_{1}-x^{*})

\displaystyle=\underbrace{\left(1+\frac{2}{3}\alpha h\right)(x_{1}-x_{0})+h^{2}\nabla f(x_{0})}_{=0}+\frac{2}{3}\alpha h(x_{0}-x^{*})

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{3}\alpha h(x_{0}-x^{*}).

In view of (
20
), we deduce that

\displaystyle E_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{9}\alpha^{2}h^{2}\|x_{0}-x^{*}\|^{2}+\gamma h^{2}\left(f(x_{0})-f^{*}\right)

\displaystyle=\gamma h^{2}\left[\mu\|x_{0}-x^{*}\|^{2}+\left(f(x_{0})-f^{*}\right)\right].

On the other hand, according to Remark
14
, we start with

\displaystyle\theta h^{2}-\frac{\eta}{\mu}

\displaystyle=h^{2}\left[\gamma-\frac{1}{3}\alpha h\left(\frac{1+\frac{2}{3}\alpha h}{1+\alpha h}\right)-\frac{\frac{2\alpha^{2}}{9\mu}}{1+\alpha h}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha h^{3}}{1+\alpha h}\left[\gamma-\frac{1}{3}\left(1+\frac{2}{3}\alpha h\right)\right]\quad(\text{since }\alpha^{2}=9\mu\gamma/2)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\alpha h^{3}}{3(1+\alpha h)}\left[(3-\sqrt{2})\gamma-1\right]\quad(\text{since }\alpha h\leq 3\sqrt{\gamma/2})

\displaystyle\geq\frac{(2-\sqrt{2})\gamma\alpha h^{3}}{3(1+\alpha h)}\quad(\text{since }\gamma\geq 1),

to arrive at

\displaystyle f(y_{k})-f^{*}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{E_{1}(1+\rho)^{-k+1}}{\theta h^{2}-\frac{\eta}{\mu}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{3}{2-\sqrt{2}}\left(1+\frac{1}{\alpha h}\right)\left[\mu\|x_{0}-x^{*}\|^{2}+\left(f(x_{0})-f^{*}\right)\right](1+\rho)^{-k+1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2-\sqrt{2}}\left(3+\frac{\sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{q\gamma}}\right)\left[\mu\|x_{0}-x^{*}\|^{2}+\left(f(x_{0})-f^{*}\right)\right](1+\rho)^{-k+1},

as claimed.
∎

Remark 15
.

For
q
small enough, one has
\rho\sim\sqrt{2q\gamma}
. Setting
\gamma=1
, one obtains a convergence rate
f(y_{k})-f^{*}=\mathcal{O}\left((1+\sqrt{2q})^{-k}\right)
, matching that of
Aujol et al. (2022)
. Setting
\gamma=2
, the convergence rate can be accelerated by a factor of
\sqrt{2}
and reaches
f(y_{k})-f^{*}=\mathcal{O}\left((1+2\sqrt{q})^{-k}\right)
, matching asymptotically the state-of-the-art rates for TMM
Van Scoy et al. (2018)
and ITEM
Taylor and Drori (2023)
.

3.4
Convexity, quadratic growth and a unique minimizer

In this subsection, we show that the time rescaling also works for accelerating the convergence rate for convex functions with quadratic growth and a unique minimizer. In particular, we improve the best rate
\mathcal{O}\left((1+(2-\sqrt{2})\sqrt{q})^{-k}\right)
, obtained in
Aujol et al. (2023)
, by a factor of
\sqrt{2}
.

Our analysis centers around the energy sequence
(E_{k})_{k\geq 1}
, defined by

E_{k}=\frac{1}{2}\|\phi_{k}\|^{2}-\frac{\eta}{2}\|y_{k}-x^{*}\|^{2}+\theta h^{2}\psi_{k-1},

(21)

where
\psi_{k}
is given by (
15
), and

\displaystyle\phi_{k}

\displaystyle=(x_{k}-y_{k})+\xi h(x_{k}-x^{*}),\quad\eta=\frac{\xi(\alpha-\xi)h^{2}}{(1+\alpha h)(1+2\xi h)},

\displaystyle\theta

\displaystyle=\gamma-\frac{(\alpha-\xi)h\left(1+\xi h(1+\gamma)\right)}{(1+\alpha h)(1+2\xi h)},\quad\xi=\frac{2+\sqrt{2}}{3+\sqrt{2}}\alpha.

The value of
\xi
comes from the analysis in the continuous time.

We shall use the following result, whose proof can be found in Appendix
9
:

Lemma 13
.

Let
f
be convex, have
\mu
-quadratic growth and admit a unique minimizer. Let
(x_{k})
and
(y_{k})
be generated by (
AGM
) with
\gamma\in[1,2]
. Consider
(E_{k})_{k\geq 1}
defined by (
21
). Then,

\displaystyle\quad\left(1+\frac{2(\alpha-\xi)h}{1+2\xi h}\right)E_{k+1}-E_{k}+\frac{(\alpha-\xi)h^{5}\|\nabla f(x_{k})\|^{2}}{1+2\xi h}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{(\alpha-\xi)h^{3}\left(1+\frac{\xi(3\alpha-2\xi)h}{2\xi-\alpha}\right)\left[\xi(2\xi-\alpha)-\frac{\mu\gamma}{\sqrt{2}}\right]}{(1+\alpha h)\left(1+2\xi h\right)}\|x_{k}-x^{*}\|^{2},

where
\xi=\frac{2+\sqrt{2}}{3+\sqrt{2}}\alpha
.

Now we present the main result of this subsection:

Theorem 14
.

Let
f:H\to\mathbb{R}
be convex and
L
-smooth, have
\mu
-quadratic growth and admit a unique minimizer. Let
(x_{k})
and
(y_{k})
be generated by (
AGM
), where

h=1/\sqrt{L},\quad\hbox{}\quad\gamma\in[1,2],\quad\hbox{and}\quad\alpha=\frac{3+\sqrt{2}}{2+\sqrt{2}}\sqrt{\mu\gamma}.

If
y_{0}=-\left(\gamma-\frac{\sqrt{q\gamma}}{(2+\sqrt{2})(1+\sqrt{q\gamma})}\right)h^{2}\nabla f(x_{0})+x_{0}
, then for every
k\geq 1
,

f(y_{k})-f^{*}\leq\left[\frac{\sqrt{2}\mu}{2}\|x_{0}-x^{*}\|^{2}+\sqrt{2}\left(f(x_{0})-f^{*}\right)\right](1+\rho)^{-k+1},

where
\rho=\frac{(2-\sqrt{2})\sqrt{q\gamma}}{1+2\sqrt{q\gamma}}
and
q=\mu/L
. Moreover,
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(1+\rho)^{k}\|\nabla f(x_{k})\|^{2}<\infty
.

Proof.

Using
\xi=\frac{2+\sqrt{2}}{3+\sqrt{2}}\alpha
, we have

\xi(2\xi-\alpha)-\frac{\mu\gamma}{\sqrt{2}}=\frac{(2+\sqrt{2})(1+\sqrt{2})}{(3+\sqrt{2})^{2}}\alpha^{2}-\frac{\mu\gamma}{\sqrt{2}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left[\left(\frac{2+\sqrt{2}}{3+\sqrt{2}}\right)^{2}\alpha^{2}-\mu\gamma\right]=0,

in view of
\alpha=\frac{3+\sqrt{2}}{2+\sqrt{2}}\sqrt{\mu\gamma}
. It follows from Lemma
13
that

(1+\rho)E_{k+1}-E_{k}+\frac{1}{2}\rho h^{4}\|\nabla f(x_{k})\|^{2}\leq 0,\quad\hbox{with}\quad\rho=\frac{(2-\sqrt{2})\sqrt{q\gamma}}{1+2\sqrt{q\gamma}}.

Hence,

E_{k}\leq(1+\rho)^{-k+1}E_{1}\quad\hbox{and}\quad\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(1+\rho)^{k}\|\nabla f(x_{k})\|^{2}<\infty.

Recalling Remark
7
, it is easy to verify that
y_{0}=-\left(\gamma-\frac{\sqrt{q\gamma}}{(2+\sqrt{2})(1+\sqrt{q\gamma})}\right)h^{2}\nabla f(x_{0})+x_{0}
gives

\displaystyle\phi_{1}

\displaystyle=(x_{1}-y_{1})+\frac{2+\sqrt{2}}{3+\sqrt{2}}\alpha h(x_{1}-x^{*})

\displaystyle=(x_{1}-y_{1})+\sqrt{q\gamma}(x_{1}-x^{*})

\displaystyle=\underbrace{(1+\sqrt{q\gamma})(x_{1}-x_{0})+h^{2}\nabla f(x_{0})}_{=0}+\sqrt{q\gamma}(x_{0}-x^{*})

\displaystyle=\sqrt{q\gamma}(x_{0}-x^{*}).

In view of (
21
), we obtain

\displaystyle E_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{q\gamma}{2}\|x_{0}-x^{*}\|^{2}+\gamma h^{2}\left(f(x_{0})-f^{*}\right)

\displaystyle=\gamma h^{2}\left[\frac{\mu}{2}\|x_{0}-x^{*}\|^{2}+\left(f(x_{0})-f^{*}\right)\right].

On the other hand, according to Remark
14
, and keeping in mind
\alpha^{2}=\left(\frac{3+\sqrt{2}}{2+\sqrt{2}}\right)^{2}\mu\gamma
, we obtain

\displaystyle\theta h^{2}-\frac{\eta}{\mu}

\displaystyle=h^{2}\left[\gamma-\frac{(\alpha-\xi)h\left(1+\xi h(1+\gamma)\right)}{(1+\alpha h)(1+2\xi h)}-\frac{\xi(\alpha-\xi)/\mu}{(1+\alpha h)(1+2\xi h)}\right]

\displaystyle=h^{2}\left[\gamma-\frac{(\alpha-\xi)h\left(1+\xi h(1+\gamma)\right)}{(1+\alpha h)(1+2\xi h)}-\frac{\frac{2+\sqrt{2}}{(3+\sqrt{2})^{2}}\frac{\alpha^{2}}{\mu}}{(1+\alpha h)(1+2\xi h)}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\gamma h^{2}+\frac{\frac{\gamma}{2+\sqrt{2}}\left((\alpha+2\xi)h+2\alpha\xi h^{2}\right)-(\alpha-\xi)h\left(1+\xi h(1+\gamma)\right)}{(1+\alpha h)(1+2\xi h)}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\gamma h^{2}+\frac{\frac{\gamma(\alpha+2\xi)h}{2+\sqrt{2}}-(\alpha-\xi)h+\xi h^{2}\left(\frac{2\alpha\gamma}{2+\sqrt{2}}-(\alpha-\xi)(1+\gamma)\right)}{(1+\alpha h)(1+2\xi h)}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\gamma h^{2},

so that

f(y_{k})-f^{*}\leq\frac{E_{1}(1+\rho)^{-k+1}}{\theta h^{2}-\frac{\eta}{\mu}}\leq\left[\frac{\sqrt{2}\mu}{2}\|x_{0}-x^{*}\|^{2}+\sqrt{2}\left(f(x_{0})-f^{*}\right)\right](1+\rho)^{-k+1},

which allows us to conclude.
∎

Remark 16
.

For
q
small enough, one has
\rho\sim(2-\sqrt{2})\sqrt{q\gamma}
. Setting
\gamma=1
, one gets a convergence rate
f(y_{k})-f^{*}=\mathcal{O}\left(1+(2-\sqrt{2})\sqrt{q}\right)^{-k}
, which matches the one obtained in
Aujol et al. (2023)
. Choosing
\gamma=2
, one obtains a faster convergence rate
f(y_{k})-f^{*}=\mathcal{O}\left(1+2(\sqrt{2}-1)\sqrt{q}\right)^{-k}
, which improves on
Aujol et al. (2023)
by a factor
\sqrt{2}
.

3.5
PŁ convex functions with possibly multiple minimizers

In this subsection, we show that the time rescaling can be useful in deriving a faster convergence rate for smooth convex functions that satisfy the Polyak-Łojasiewicz inequality (
13
).

Our analysis is based on the energy sequence
(E_{k})_{k\geq 1}
, defined by

E_{k}=\frac{1}{2}\|\phi_{k}\|^{2}+\gamma h^{2}\psi_{k-1},

(22)

where
\phi_{k}=x_{k}-y_{k}
and
\psi_{k}
is given by (
15
).

Lemma 15
.

Let
f
be convex. Let
(x_{k})
and
(y_{k})
be generated by (
AGM
). Consider
(E_{k})_{k\geq 1}
defined by (
22
). Then,

(1+\alpha h)E_{k+1}-E_{k}\leq-\left[\gamma(2-\gamma)+(\gamma-1)\alpha h\right]\frac{h^{4}}{2}\|\nabla f(x_{k})\|^{2}+\gamma\alpha h^{3}\left(f(x_{k})-f^{*}\right).

Proof.

By definition of
\phi_{k}
, we have

\phi_{k+1}=x_{k+1}-y_{k+1}=x_{k+1}-x_{k}+h^{2}\nabla f(x_{k}),

and in view of (
14
),

\displaystyle\phi_{k+1}-\phi_{k}

\displaystyle=-\alpha h(x_{k+1}-x_{k})-\gamma h^{2}\nabla f(x_{k})

\displaystyle=-\frac{\alpha h}{2}\phi_{k+1}-\frac{\alpha h}{2}(x_{k+1}-x_{k})-\left(\gamma-\frac{1}{2}\alpha h\right)h^{2}\nabla f(x_{k}).

It follows that

\|\phi_{k+1}-\phi_{k}\|^{2}=\alpha^{2}h^{2}\|x_{k+1}-x_{k}\|^{2}+\gamma^{2}h^{4}\|\nabla f(x_{k})\|^{2}+2\gamma\alpha h^{3}\langle\nabla f(x_{k}),x_{k+1}-x_{k}\rangle,

and

\displaystyle\langle\phi_{k+1}-\phi_{k},\phi_{k+1}\rangle

\displaystyle=-\frac{\alpha h}{2}\|\phi_{k+1}\|^{2}-\frac{\alpha h}{2}\|x_{k+1}-x_{k}\|^{2}-\gamma h^{2}\langle\nabla f(x_{k}),x_{k+1}-x_{k}\rangle

\displaystyle\quad-\left(\gamma-\frac{1}{2}\alpha h\right)h^{4}\|\nabla f(x_{k})\|^{2}.

Since
\frac{1}{2}\|\phi_{k+1}\|^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\|\phi_{k}\|^{2}=\langle\phi_{k+1}-\phi_{k},\phi_{k+1}\rangle-\frac{1}{2}\|\phi_{k+1}-\phi_{k}\|^{2}
, we have

\displaystyle\quad\frac{1+\alpha h}{2}\|\phi_{k+1}\|^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\|\phi_{k}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\underbrace{-\frac{\alpha h}{2}(1+\alpha h)\|x_{k+1}-x_{k}\|^{2}}_{\leq 0}-\gamma h^{2}(1+\alpha h)\langle\nabla f(x_{k}),x_{k+1}-x_{k}\rangle

\displaystyle\quad-\left(\frac{1}{2}\gamma^{2}+\gamma-\frac{1}{2}\alpha h\right)h^{4}\|\nabla f(x_{k})\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\underbrace{-\gamma h^{2}(1+\alpha h)\langle\nabla f(x_{k}),x_{k+1}-x_{k}\rangle}_{\mathbf{I}}-\left(\frac{1}{2}\gamma^{2}+\gamma-\frac{1}{2}\alpha h\right)h^{4}\|\nabla f(x_{k})\|^{2}.

Using Lemma
7
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbf{I}

\displaystyle\leq-\gamma h^{2}\left[\psi_{k}-\psi_{k-1}-\gamma h^{2}\|\nabla f(x_{k})\|^{2}\right],

so that

\frac{1+\alpha h}{2}\|\phi_{k+1}\|^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\|\phi_{k}\|^{2}+\gamma h^{2}(\psi_{k}-\psi_{k-1})\leq-\left[\gamma(2-\gamma)-\alpha h\right]\frac{h^{4}}{2}\|\nabla f(x_{k})\|^{2}.

Adding
\gamma\alpha h^{3}\psi_{k}=\gamma\alpha h^{3}\left(f(x_{k})-f^{*}-\frac{h^{2}}{2}\|\nabla f(x_{k})\|^{2}\right)
to both sides, we arrive at

(1+\alpha h)E_{k+1}-E_{k}\leq-\left[\gamma(2-\gamma)+(\gamma-1)\alpha h\right]\frac{h^{4}}{2}\|\nabla f(x_{k})\|^{2}+\gamma\alpha h^{3}\left(f(x_{k})-f^{*}\right),

as claimed.
∎

In what follows, we give the main result of this subsection.

Theorem 16
.

Let
f:H\to\mathbb{R}
be convex,
L
-smooth and satisfy the Polyak-Łojasiewicz inequality (
13
) with constant
\mu
for
L>\mu>0
. Let
(x_{k})
and
(y_{k})
be generated by (
AGM
), where

h=1/\sqrt{L},\quad\hbox{}\quad\alpha h=\frac{2q}{1+\sqrt{2q-q^{2}}},\quad\hbox{}\quad\gamma=\frac{\sqrt{2q-q^{2}}-q}{1-q},

with
q=\mu/L
. If
y_{0}=-(\gamma-\alpha h)h^{2}\nabla f(x_{0})+x_{0}
, then for every
k\geq 1
,

f(y_{k})-f^{*}\leq\left(f(x_{0})-f^{*}\right)(1+\rho)^{-k+1},

where
\rho=\frac{2q}{1+\sqrt{2q-q^{2}}}
.

Proof.

Using Lemma
15
, together with the Polyak-Łojasiewicz inequality:

f(x_{k})-f^{*}\leq\frac{1}{2\mu}\|\nabla f(x_{k})\|^{2},

we deduce that

(1+\alpha h)E_{k+1}-E_{k}\leq-\underbrace{\left[\gamma(2-\gamma)+(\gamma-1)\alpha h-\frac{\gamma\alpha}{\mu h}\right]}_{\mathbf{II}}\frac{h^{4}}{2}\|\nabla f(x_{k})\|^{2}.

As one can see,
\mathbf{II}\geq 0
holds as long as
\gamma\in(0,2)
and
\alpha h\leq\frac{\gamma(2-\gamma)q}{(1-q)\gamma+q}
. It can be verified that

\gamma=\frac{\sqrt{2q-q^{2}}-q}{1-q}\in(0,1),\quad\alpha h=\frac{2q}{1+\sqrt{2q-q^{2}}}=\frac{\gamma(2-\gamma)q}{(1-q)\gamma+q},

satisfy the aforementioned condition. As a result,
E_{k}\leq(1+\alpha h)^{-k+1}E_{1}
. Recalling Remark
7
, it can be shown that the choice of
y_{0}=-(\gamma-\alpha h)h^{2}\nabla f(x_{0})+x_{0}
gives

\phi_{1}=x_{1}-y_{1}=x_{1}-x_{0}+h^{2}\nabla f(x_{0})=0.

Recalling (
22
), we have
E_{1}=\gamma h^{2}\psi_{0}\leq\gamma h^{2}\left(f(x_{0})-f^{*}\right)
. It follows that

f(y_{k})-f^{*}\leq\psi_{k-1}\leq\frac{E_{k}}{\gamma h^{2}}\leq\frac{E_{1}}{\gamma h^{2}}(1+\alpha h)^{-k+1}\leq\left(f(x_{0})-f^{*}\right)(1+\alpha h)^{-k+1},

as claimed.
∎

Remark 17
.

For
q
small enough, one obtains

f(y_{k+1})-f^{*}=\mathcal{O}\left((1+2q)^{-k}\right).

This rate is inferior to the one obtained in Theorem
14
. Although the Polyak-Łojasiewicz inequality implies a quadratic growth condition
Bolte et al. (2017)
, the assumption of a unique minimizer is not required here, which may account for the inferiority of the convergence rate. On the other hand, this rate is comparable to the one obtained by gradient descent for convex functions that satisfies the Polyak-Łojasiewicz inequality
(
Zhang and Zhang, 2019
, Corollary 3.1)
. An interesting thing is that this rate was derived under a choice
\gamma<1
instead of
\gamma=1
, whose convergence rate is only
\mathcal{O}\left((1+q)^{-k}\right)
. This demonstrates one potential advantage of including
\gamma
: one can obtain more control on manipulating the convergence behavior of the algorithms and derive a faster convergence rate. The interested reader can refer to
Wang and Peypouquet (2025)
for an example.

4
Conclusions

We have provided a dynamic interpretation for the extra speedup enjoyed by state-of-the-art first-order algorithms for smooth strongly convex functions with respect to Nesterov’s method. We prove this idea within the framework of inertial dynamics and algorithms, and recover the fastest known rates under strong convexity. Following the same strategy, we improve the convergence rate by a factor of
\sqrt{2}
for convex functions with quadratic growth and a unique minimizer.

In this section, we recall some terminology concerning the geometric assumptions that will be made on
f
to establish different convergence rates. For a general account on convexity, the reader is referred to
Bauschke and Combettes (2017)
;
Peypouquet (2015)
.

In all that follows
f:H\to\mathbb{R}
is differentiable and attains its minimum
f^{*}
at
S=\argmin(f)
.

The function
f
is
L
-smooth
if its gradient
\nabla f
is Lipschitz-continuous with constant
L
. Smooth convex functions can be characterized as follows:
f
is
L
-smooth and convex if, and only if,

f(y)\geq f(x)+\langle\nabla f(x),y-x\rangle+\frac{1}{2L}\|\nabla f(x)-\nabla f(y)\|^{2}

for every
x,y\in H
.

Now,
f
is
\mu
-strongly convex
if

f(y)\geq f(x)+\langle\nabla f(x),y-x\rangle+\frac{\mu}{2}\|x-y\|^{2}

for every
x,y\in H
. If
f
is strongly convex, it has a unique minimizer
x^{*}
and

f(x)-f^{*}\geq\frac{\mu}{2}\|x-x^{*}\|^{2}.

More generally,
f
has
\mu
-quadratic growth
if

f(x)-f^{*}\geq\frac{\mu}{2}\dist(x,S)^{2}.

On the other hand,
f
satisfies the
Polyak-Łojasiewicz
inequality with constant
\mu>0
, or is a
\mu
-PŁ function
, if

\|\nabla f(x)\|^{2}\geq 2\mu\big(f(x)-f^{*}\big),

for every
x\in H
. The interested reader is referred to
Bolte et al. (2017)
;
Polyak (1963)
;
Łojasiewicz (1963)
;
Łojasiewicz (1958)
;
Łojasiewicz (1959)
. If
f
is
\mu
-strongly convex, by Young’s inequality, we have

\frac{\mu}{2}\|x-x^{*}\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2\mu}\|\nabla f(x)\|^{2}\geq\langle\nabla f(x),x-x^{*}\rangle\geq f(x)-f^{*}+\frac{\mu}{2}\|x-x^{*}\|^{2},

and so
f
is
\mu
-PŁ. It follows that both quadratic growth and the PŁ inequality are generalized forms of strong convexity. For the readers’ convenience, we include a short discussion on the relationship between these two notions, mostly adapted from

Bolte et al. (2017)
(see also
Bolte et al. (2007)
;
Bolte et al. (2010)
).

Lemma 17
.

Let
f:H\to\mathbb{R}
be smooth, and such that

\varphi^{\prime}\big(f(x)-f^{*}\big)\|\nabla f(x)\|\geq 1

(23)

for all
x\in H
, where
\varphi
is strictly increasing and differentiable, with
\varphi(0)=0
. Let
u:[0,\infty)\to H
satisfy
-\dot{u}(t)=\nabla f\big(u(t)\big)
for
t>0
. For
0\leq s\leq t
, we have

\|u(t)-u(s)\|\leq\varphi\big(f(u(s))-f^{*}\big)-\varphi\big(f(u(t))-f^{*}\big),

(24)

and

\frac{d}{dt}\left[\frac{}{}\!f(u(t))-f^{*}\right]\leq-\left[\frac{1}{\varphi^{\prime}\big(f(u(t))-f^{*}\big)}\right]^{2}.

(25)

Proof.

Let
0\leq s\leq t
. We have

\displaystyle\varphi\big(f(u(s))-f^{*}\big)-\varphi\big(f(u(t))-f^{*}\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{t}^{s}\frac{d}{d\tau}\big(\varphi\circ(f-f^{*})\big)\big(u(\tau)\big)\,d\tau

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{t}^{s}\varphi^{\prime}\big(f(u(\tau)-f^{*})\big)\left\langle\nabla f\big(u(\tau)\big),\dot{u}(\tau)\right\rangle\,d\tau

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{s}^{t}\varphi^{\prime}\big(f(u(\tau)-f^{*}\big)\left\|\nabla f\big(u(\tau)\big)\right\|\left\|\dot{u}(\tau)\right\|\,d\tau

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\int_{s}^{t}\left\|\dot{u}(\tau)\right\|\,d\tau.

Therefore,

\|u(t)-u(s)\|=\left\|\int_{s}^{t}\dot{u}(\tau)\,d\tau\right\|\leq\int_{s}^{t}\left\|\dot{u}(\tau)\right\|\,d\tau\leq\varphi\big(f(u(s))-f^{*}\big)-\varphi\big(f(u(t))-f^{*}\big).

On the other hand, we have

\frac{d}{dt}\left[\frac{}{}\!f(u(t))-f^{*}\right]=\langle\nabla f\big(u(t)\big),\dot{u}(t)\rangle=-\|\nabla f\big(u(t)\big)\|^{2}\leq-\left[\frac{1}{\varphi^{\prime}\big(f(u(t))-f^{*}\big)}\right]^{2},

as required.
∎

If
f
is
\mu
-PŁ, it satisfies (
23
) with
\varphi=\sqrt{\frac{2s}{\mu}}
.

Corollary 18
.

If
f
is smooth and
\mu
-PŁ, it has
\mu
-quadratic growth.

Proof.

Let
\varphi(s)=\sqrt{\frac{2s}{\mu}}
, so that
\varphi^{\prime}(s)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\mu s}}
. Let
u:[0,\infty)\to H
be the unique solution of
-\dot{u}(t)=\nabla f\big(u(t)\big)
, with
u(0)=z
. From (
25
), we obtain

\frac{d}{dt}\left[\frac{}{}\!f(u(t))-f^{*}\right]\leq-2\mu\big(f(u(t))-f^{*}\big),

and so

f(u(t))-f^{*}\leq e^{-2\mu t}\big(f(z)-f^{*}\big),

which tends to zero as
t\to\infty
. By (
24
),
u(t)
converges strongly, as
t\to\infty
. Its limit, say
\hat{u}
, must be a minimizer of
f
. In particular,

\dist(z,S)\leq\|\hat{u}-z\|\leq\varphi\big(f(z)-f^{*}\big)=\sqrt{\frac{2(f(z)-f^{*})}{\mu}},

as claimed.
∎

Conversely, we have the following:

Proposition 19
.

If
f
is convex and has
\mu
-quadratic growth, it is
\frac{\mu}{4}
-PŁ.

Proof.

Let
f
be convex, and let
x_{S}
be the projection of
x
onto
S
. We have

f(x)-f^{*}\leq\langle\nabla f(x),x-x_{S}\rangle\leq\|\nabla f(x)\|\dist(x,S).

By
\mu
-quadratic growth, we have

f(x)-f^{*}\leq\sqrt{\frac{2}{\mu}}\|\nabla f(x)\|\sqrt{f(x)-f^{*}},

which we can rewrite as

\|\nabla f(x)\|^{2}\geq 2\left[\frac{\mu}{4}\right]\big(f(x)-f^{*}\big)

to conclude.
∎

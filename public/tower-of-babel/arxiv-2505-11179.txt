On physically grounded boundary conditions for the compressible MHD system
Jan Březina, Eduard Feireisl

Abstract
We consider a general compressible MHD system, where the magnetic field propagates in a heterogeneous medium. Using suitable penalization in terms of the transport coefficients we perform several singular limits. As a result we obtain: 1. A rigorous justification of physically grounded boundary conditions for the compressible MHD system on a bounded domain. 2. Existence of weak solutions for arbitrary finite energy initial data in the situation the Maxwell induction equation holds also outside the fluid domain. 3. A suitable theoretical platform for numerical experiments on domains with geometrically complicated boundaries.

Introduction

The magnetohydrodynamics (MHD) system of equations couples fluid equations with a simplified Maxwell system describing the propagation of a magnetic field. The fluid motion is driven by
the Lorentz force that can be conveniently written as a divergence of
Maxwell’s tensor, while the electric current in Ohm’s law in the induction equation is augmented by the vector product of the fluid velocity with the magnetic field vector. If the fluid in question
is compressible barotropic, the relevant system of equations reads

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\varrho+{\rm div}_{x}(\varrho{\bf u})

\displaystyle=0,\

(1.1)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}(\varrho{\bf u})+{\rm div}_{x}(\varrho{\bf u}\otimes{\bf u})+\nabla_{x}p

\displaystyle={\rm div}_{x}\mathbb{S}-\beta{\bf u}-{\rm div}_{x}\left[\mu\left({\bf H}\otimes{\bf H}-\frac{1}{2}|{\bf H}|^{2}\mathbb{I}\right)\right],

(1.2)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}(\mu{\bf H})+{\bf curl}_{x}(\mu{\bf H}\times{\bf u})+{\bf curl}_{x}(\eta{\bf curl}_{x}{\bf H})

\displaystyle=0,\ {\rm div}_{x}(\mu{\bf H})=0,

(1.3)

with the unknowns
\varrho=\varrho(t,x)
- the fluid mass density,

{\bf u}={\bf u}(t,x)
the velocity of the fluid, and
{\bf H}={\bf H}(t,x)
the magnetic field vector. The pressure
p=p(\varrho)
is given constitutively, the viscous stress
\mathbb{S}=\mathbb{S}(\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u})
obeys Newton’s rheological law,

\mathbb{S}(\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u})=\nu\left(\nabla_{x}{\bf u}+\nabla_{x}^{t}{\bf u}-\frac{2}{d}{\rm div}_{x}{\bf u}\mathbb{I}\right)+\lambda{\rm div}_{x}{\bf u}\mathbb{I},\ \mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u}\equiv\frac{1}{2}\left(\nabla{\bf u}+\nabla^{t}{\bf u}\right).

(1.4)

In addition, the equations (
1.2
), (
1.3
) depend on transport coefficients:

\beta

.
friction parameter

\nu

.
shear viscosity coefficient

\lambda

.
bulk viscosity coefficient

\mu

.
magnetic permeability

\eta

.
electric resistivity

They are given non–negative functions of the spatial
variable
x
. In addition, we suppose
\nu
,
\mu
, and
\eta
are strictly positive bounded below away from zero.

While the fluid is usually confined to a well specified bounded domain with an impermeable boundary, the induction equation (
1.3
)
is in principle satisfied outside the fluid domain with
{\bf u}=0
, and with possibly different values of coefficients
\mu
and
\eta
. The relevant boundary conditions specifying the value of the field
{\bf H}
on the boundary of the fluid domain are therefore of
transmission
type.
This approach was adopted in the seminal work of Ladyzhenskaya and Solonnikov
[
9
]
in the context of incompressible MHD. Later,
Meir and Schmidt
[
12
]
,
[
13
]
developed a similar method
applicable to stationary incompressible MHD problems. To the best of our knowledge, this approach has never been applied to the compressible MHD models. One of the principal objectives of this paper is to fill this gap.

Solving the induction equation outside the fluid domain may be technically complicated, in particular in view of numerical simulations. For this reason, the system (
1.1
)–(
1.3
) is often supplemented by explicit boundary conditions for the field
{\bf H}
.
Typically, the so–called perfect electric conductor (PEC) boundary conditions are the most frequently used choice.
As pointed out by Shercliff
[
16
]
:

A perfectly conducting solid boundary is so much favoured by theoreticians because it shields the fluid from external conditions and eliminates consideration of all but the fluid region. This selection usually constitutes escapism, however, justifiable only as a simplifying first approximation.

What is more, a proper choice of the boundary conditions for
{\bf H}
is a delicate issue. Assuming
\mu
and
\eta
constant we may rewrite the differential operator in (
1.3
) as

{\bf curl}_{x}{\bf curl}_{x}{\bf H}=\nabla_{x}{\rm div}_{x}{\bf H}-\Delta_{x}{\bf H}=-\Delta_{x}{\bf H}.

One is therefore tempted to adopt the homogeneous Dirichlet boundary
condition
{\bf H}|_{\partial\Omega}=0
. Unfortunately, as observed by
Lassner
[
10
]
(see also Ströhmer
[
17
]
), the problem
(
1.3
) becomes overdetermined unless a variant of “pressure” is added in the formulation of Ohm’s law. This fact is often incorrectly interpreted as a discrepancy between the weak and strong solutions of the problem. Therefore our second objective is to justify most of the commonly used boundary conditions as singular limits of the transmission conditions for a suitable scaling of the transport coefficients.

For the sake of simplicity and also in view of possible numerical computations, we start with the compressible MHD system with the space periodic boundary conditions, meaning the spatial domain can be identified with a flat torus

\mathbb{T}^{d}=\left([-L,L]|_{\{-L,L\}}\right)^{d},\ d=2,3,

where
L
is large enough to absorb the fluid domain. At this stage, we consider the transport coefficients to be continuously differentiable functions of
x\in\mathbb{T}^{d}
.

Motivated by the seminal work of Ladyzhenskaya and Solonnikov
[
9
]
, we fix a Lipschitz domain

\Omega\subset R^{d},\ \Omega\subset\overline{\Omega}\subset(-L,L)^{d}.

(1.5)

In addition, we consider another Lipschitz domain

\Omega_{\rm int}\subset\overline{\Omega}_{\rm int}\subset\Omega,

(1.6)

representing a solid object immersed in the fluid. Accordingly, the fluid occupies the domain

{\Omega_{F}=\Omega\setminus\overline{\Omega}_{\rm int}.}

(1.7)

Finally, we set

{\Omega_{\rm ext}=\mathbb{T}^{d}\setminus\overline{\Omega}.}

(1.8)

Our goal is to show the existence of weak solutions to several initial–boundary value problems for the compressible MHD system resulting from appropriate penalization of the transport coefficient.
Specifically, we consider the following problems:

•

The equation of continuity (
1.1
) holds in the whole domain
(0,T)\times\mathbb{T}^{d}
.

•

The fluid momentum equation (
1.2
) is satisfied in
(0,T)\times\Omega_{F}
,
with
\beta=0
and constant viscosity coefficients
\nu=\nu_{F}>0
,
\lambda=\lambda_{F}\geq 0
, supplemented with the no–slip boundary condition

{\bf u}|_{\partial\Omega_{F}}=0.

(1.9)

•

The induction equation (
1.3
) is satisfied in
(0,T)\times\Omega
, with constant but generally different transport coefficients

\mu=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\mu_{\rm int}>0\ \mbox{in}\ \Omega_{\rm int}\\
{\mu_{F}>0}\ \mbox{in}\ \Omega_{F}\end{array}\right.,\eta=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\eta_{\rm int}>0\ \mbox{in}\ \Omega_{\rm int}\\
{\eta_{F}>0}\ \mbox{in}\ \Omega_{F}\end{array}\right.,

where the fluid velocity
{\bf u}
is set to be zero outside
\Omega_{F}
,
with the corresponding transmission boundary conditions on
\partial\Omega_{\rm int}
.

•

The magnetic field vector
{\bf H}
will satisfy one of the following boundary conditions on
\partial\Omega
:

1.

Perfect electric isolator.

{\bf H}\times{\bf n}={\bf H}_{\rm ext}\times{\bf n},\ \mu_{F}{\bf H}\cdot{\bf n}=\mu_{\rm ext}{\bf H}_{\rm ext}\cdot{\bf n}\ \mbox{in}\ (0,T)\times\partial\Omega,

(1.10)

where
{\bf H}_{\rm ext}
is an external magnetic field
satisfying

\displaystyle{\bf curl}_{x}{\bf H}_{\rm ext}=0,\ {\rm div}_{x}(\mu_{\rm ext}{\bf H}_{\rm ext})=0\ \mbox{in}\ (0,T)\times\Omega_{\rm ext}

These are the “non–local” boundary conditions considered in problem II by Ladyzhenskaya and Solonnikov
[
9
]
, see also Sakhaev and Solonnikov
[
15
]
.

2.

Perfect magnetic conductor (PMC).

{\bf H}\times{\bf n}=0\ \mbox{in}\ (0,T)\times\partial\Omega

(1.11)

Note that these are the
correct
Dirichlet type boundary conditions to be imposed on
{\bf H}
. Here,
\Omega_{\rm ext}
is a perfect magnetic conductor with
\mu_{\rm ext}\to\infty
.

3.

Perfect electric conductor (PEC).

{\bf H}\cdot{\bf n}=0,\ {\bf curl}_{x}{\bf H}\times{\bf n}=0\ \mbox{in}\ (0,T)\times\partial\Omega

(1.12)

These are probably the most frequently used boundary conditions in mathematical treatments of MHD. The external domain
\Omega_{\rm ext}
is a perfect conductor (possibly superconductor), for which

\mu_{\rm ext}\to 0,\ \eta_{\rm ext}\to 0.

4.

Isolator type boundary conditions.

Ströhmer
[
17
]
proposed the boundary conditions

{\bf H}\cdot{\bf n}=0,\ {\bf curl}_{x}{\bf H}\cdot{\bf n}=0\ \mbox{in}\ (0,T)\times\partial\Omega.

(1.13)

The inhomogeneous version of (
1.13
) has been recently examined by Neustupa, Perisetti, and Yang
[
14
]
.

Although mathematically still admissible, these boundary conditions correspond to a rather unrealistic material
\Omega_{\rm ext}
satisfying

\mu_{\rm ext}\to 0,\ \eta_{\rm ext}\to\infty.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2

In Section

5

2
Problem with smooth transport coefficients

We start with the compressible MHD system with spatially periodic boundary conditions and spatially dependent transport coefficients
defined on the flat torus

\mathbb{T}^{d}=\left([-L,L]|_{\{-L,L\}}\right)^{d}.

For the sake of simplicity, we consider the isentropic pressure state equation

p(\varrho)=a\varrho^{\gamma},\ a>0,\ \gamma>\frac{d}{2}.

More complicated equations of state including a non–monotone pressure can be handled by essentially the same approach, see e.g.
[
5
, Chapter 6]
.

2.1
Weak formulation of the primitive system

We suppose the transport coefficients belong to the class
C^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
, and, in addition,

\beta\geq 0,\ \nu>0,\lambda\geq 0,\ \mu>0,\ \eta>0\ \mbox{in}\ \mathbb{T}^{d}.

(2.1)

Definition 2.1

(
Weak solutions to the primitive system).

We shall say that a trio of functions
(\varrho,{\bf u},{\bf H})
is
weak solution
of the compressible MHD system (
1.1
)–(
1.3
) in the space–time cylinder
(0,T)\times\mathbb{T}^{d}
, with the initial data
(\varrho_{0},{\bf m}_{0},{\bf H}_{0})
if the following holds:

•

Equation of continuity.

The functions
\varrho
,
{\bf u}
belong to the class
\varrho\geq 0
,
\varrho\in C_{\rm weak}\left([0,T];L^{\gamma}(\mathbb{T}^{d})\right)
,
{\bf u}\in L^{2}(0,T;W^{1,2}(\mathbb{T}^{d};R^{d}))
, and the integral identity

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\varrho(\tau,\cdot)\varphi\ \,{\rm d}{x}=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\varrho_{0}\varphi\ \,{\rm d}{x}+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\varrho{\bf u}\cdot\nabla_{x}\varphi\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t,\ 0\leq\tau\leq T

(2.2)

holds for any
\varphi\in C^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
. In addition, the renormalized equation

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}b(\varrho)(\tau,\cdot)\varphi\ \,{\rm d}{x}=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}b(\varrho_{0})\varphi\ \,{\rm d}{x}+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\left[b(\varrho){\bf u}\cdot\nabla_{x}\varphi+\Big(b(\varrho)-b^{\prime}(\varrho)\varrho\Big){\rm div}_{x}{\bf u}\varphi\right]\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t

(2.3)

holds for any
\varphi\in C^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
, and any
b\in C^{1}[0,\infty)
,

b^{\prime}\in C_{c}[0,\infty)
.

•

Momentum equation.

The momentum
\varrho{\bf u}\in C_{\rm weak}([0,T];L^{\frac{2\gamma}{\gamma+1}}(\mathbb{T}^{d};R^{d}))
, the magnetic field vector
{\bf H}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d};R^{d}))
, and the integral identity

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\varrho{\bf u}(\tau,\cdot)\cdot\boldsymbol{\varphi}\ \,{\rm d}{x}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}{\bf m}_{0}\boldsymbol{\varphi}\ \,{\rm d}{x}+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\Big[(\varrho{\bf u}\otimes{\bf u}):\nabla_{x}\boldsymbol{\varphi}+p(\varrho){\rm div}_{x}\boldsymbol{\varphi}\Big]\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\mathbb{S}(\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u}):\nabla_{x}\boldsymbol{\varphi}\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\beta{\bf u}\cdot\boldsymbol{\varphi}\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\mu\Big[{\bf H}\otimes{\bf H}-\frac{1}{2}|{\bf H}|^{2}\mathbb{I}\Big]:\nabla_{x}\boldsymbol{\varphi}\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t

(2.4)

holds for any
0\leq\tau\leq T
and any
\boldsymbol{\varphi}\in C^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d};R^{d})
.

•

Induction equation.
The magnetic field vector

{\bf H}\in C_{\rm weak}([0,T];L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d};R^{d}))
,
{\bf curl}_{x}{\bf H}\in L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d};R^{d}))
, and the integral identity

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\mu{\bf H}(\tau,\cdot)\cdot\boldsymbol{\varphi}\ \,{\rm d}{x}=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\mu{\bf H}_{0}\cdot\boldsymbol{\varphi}\ \,{\rm d}{x}-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\Big[(\mu{\bf H}\times{\bf u})\cdot{\bf curl}_{x}\boldsymbol{\varphi}+\eta{\bf curl}_{x}{\bf H}\cdot{\bf curl}_{x}\boldsymbol{\varphi}\Big]\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t

(2.5)

holds for any
0\leq\tau\leq T
, and any
\boldsymbol{\varphi}\in C^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
. Moreover, we suppose

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\mu{\bf H}_{0}\cdot\nabla_{x}\phi\ \,{\rm d}{x}=0\ \mbox{for all}\ \phi\in C^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d})

(2.6)

yielding

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\mu{\bf H}(\tau,\cdot)\cdot\nabla_{x}\phi\ \,{\rm d}{x}=0\ {\mbox{meaning}}\ {\rm div}_{x}(\mu{\bf H}(\tau,\cdot))=0,\ {\mbox{in the sense of distributions.}}

(2.7)

Remark 2.2
.

We recall Gaffney inequality, see e.g. Csato, Dacorogna, Sil

2

\|\nabla_{x}{\bf h}\|_{L^{2}(Q;R^{d\times d})}\leq c(Q)\Big[\|{\bf h}\|_{L^{2}(Q;R^{d})}+\|{\bf curl}_{x}{\bf h}\|_{L^{2}(Q;R^{d})}+\|{\rm div}_{x}{\bf h}\|_{L^{2}(Q)}\Big]

(2.8)

for any
{\bf h}\in W^{1,2}(Q;R^{d})
satisfying either
{\bf h}\times{\bf n}|_{\partial Q}=0
or
{\bf h}\cdot{\bf n}|_{\partial Q}=0
. It is easy to see that (

satisfies (

{\bf H}\in L^{2}(0,T;W^{1,2}(\mathbb{T}^{d};R^{d})).

(2.9)

2.2
Energy balance

We focus on the weak solutions satisfying some form of energy balance.
Let
P
be the so–called pressure potential satisfying

P^{\prime}(\varrho)\varrho-P(\varrho)=p(\varrho).

It is convenient to introduce the momentum
{\bf m}=\varrho{\bf u}
to write the total energy of the system in the form

E(\varrho,{\bf m},{\bf H})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\frac{1}{2}\frac{|{\bf m}|^{2}}{\varrho}+P(\varrho)+\frac{1}{2}\mu|{\bf H}|^{2}\ \mbox{if}\ \varrho>0\\
\\
P(0)+\frac{1}{2}\mu|{\bf H}|^{2}\ \mbox{if}\ \varrho=0,{\bf m}=0,\\
\\
\infty\ \mbox{otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

(2.10)

If the pressure
p
is an increasing function of the density, the energy
is a convex l.s.c. function defined on
R^{2d+1}
.

The primitive system is augmented by the energy inequality

\frac{{\rm d}}{\,{\rm d}t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}E(\varrho,{\bf m},{\bf H})\ \,{\rm d}{x}+\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\left[\mathbb{S}(\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u}):\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u}+\eta|{\bf curl}_{x}{\bf H}|^{2}\right]\ \,{\rm d}{x}+\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\beta|{\bf u}|^{2}\ \,{\rm d}{x}\leq 0

that can be written in the weak form as

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{T}\partial_{t}\psi\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}E(\varrho,{\bf m},{\bf H})\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{T}\psi\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\left[\mathbb{S}(\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u}):\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u}+\eta|{\bf curl}_{x}{\bf H}|^{2}\right]\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{T}\psi\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\beta|{\bf u}|^{2}\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\leq\psi(0)\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}E(\varrho_{0},{\bf m}_{0},{\bf H}_{0})\ \,{\rm d}{x}

(2.11)

for any
\psi\in C^{1}_{c}[0,T)
,
\psi\geq 0
.

Relation (
2.11
) can be integrated in time to obtain its weaker form

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}E(\varrho,{\bf m},{\bf H})(\tau,\cdot)\ \,{\rm d}{x}

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\left[\mathbb{S}(\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u}):\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u}+\eta|{\bf curl}_{x}{\bf H}|^{2}\right]\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\beta|{\bf u}|^{2}\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\leq\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}E(\varrho_{0},{\bf m}_{0},{\bf H}_{0})\ \,{\rm d}{x},\ 0\leq\tau\leq T.

(2.12)

2.3
Existence and regularity of the weak solutions

If the initial data belong to the class

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}E(\varrho_{0},{\bf m}_{0},{\bf H}_{0})\ \,{\rm d}{x}<\infty,\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\varrho_{0}\ \,{\rm d}{x}=M>0,\ {\rm div}_{x}(\mu{\bf H}_{0})=0,

it is possible to show the existence of global in time, meaning defined on a given time interval
(0,T)
, dissipative weak solutions of the compressible MHD system in the space–time cylinder
(0,T)\times\mathbb{T}^{d}
. The approach developed in
[
4
]
is easy to adapt to the case of
smooth transport coefficients. Note that the transport coefficients in

[
4
]
depend on the temperature which is more difficult than the present case.

Regularity (integrability) of weak solutions is essentially given by the energy estimates (
2.12
). As
\mu
is positive and continuously differentiable, we can use the argument of Remark

to conclude

{\bf H}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d};R^{d}))\cap L^{2}(0,T;W^{1,2}(\mathbb{T}^{d};R^{d})),

cf. also
[
4
]
.

3
Penalization of transport coefficients

The penalization of the transport coefficients may be put in a unified framework as follows:
For each transport coefficient

\Lambda=\beta,\nu,\lambda,\mu,\eta,

we prescribe three constant values

\Lambda^{\varepsilon}_{F},\ \Lambda^{\varepsilon}_{\rm int},\ \Lambda^{\varepsilon}_{\rm ext}

together with a family of smooth approximations enjoying the following properties:

\displaystyle\Lambda_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\in C^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d}),\ \min\{\Lambda^{\varepsilon}_{F},\Lambda^{\varepsilon}_{\rm int},\Lambda^{\varepsilon}_{\rm ext}\}\leq\Lambda_{\varepsilon}\leq\max\{\Lambda^{\varepsilon}_{F},\Lambda^{\varepsilon}_{\rm int},\Lambda^{\varepsilon}_{\rm ext}\},

\displaystyle\Lambda_{\varepsilon}(x)

\displaystyle=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\Lambda^{\varepsilon}_{\rm int}\ \mbox{if}\ x\in{\Omega_{\rm int}}\\
\Lambda_{\rm ext}^{\varepsilon}\ \mbox{if}\ x\in{\Omega_{\rm ext}}\end{array}\right.,

for any compact

\displaystyle\ Q\subset\Omega_{F},\ \mbox{there exists}\ \varepsilon_{0}\ \mbox{such that}\ \Lambda_{\varepsilon}(x)=\Lambda^{\varepsilon}_{F}\ \mbox{if}\ x\in Q\ \mbox{and}\ 0<\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{0}.

(3.3)

3.1
Singular limit in the fluid equations

Our approach is based on a proper choice of scaling of the parameters

(\Lambda^{\varepsilon}_{F},\Lambda^{\varepsilon}_{\rm int},\Lambda^{\varepsilon}_{\rm ext})
and performing the limit
\varepsilon\to 0
. In this process, we
always
assume the initial energy

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}E(\varrho_{0,\varepsilon},{\bf m}_{0,\varepsilon},{\bf H}_{0,\varepsilon})\ \,{\rm d}{x}\leq c,

(3.4)

where the constant
c
is independent of
\varepsilon
. In such a way, the energy inequality (
2.12
) yields uniform bounds independent of
\varepsilon
.

The first goal is to keep the fluid confined to the domain
\Omega_{F}
. To this end, we consider the standard Brinkmann type penalization

\beta^{\varepsilon}_{F}=0,\ \beta^{\varepsilon}_{\rm int}\to\infty,\ \beta^{\varepsilon}_{\rm ext}\to\infty.

(3.5)

In addition, we consider the high viscosity limit outside
\Omega_{F}
, meaning

\displaystyle\nu^{\varepsilon}_{F}

\displaystyle=\nu_{F}>0,\ \lambda^{\varepsilon}_{F}=\lambda_{F}\geq 0,

\displaystyle\nu^{\varepsilon}_{\rm int}

\displaystyle\to\infty,\ \nu^{\varepsilon}_{\rm ext}\to\infty\ \mbox{if}\ d=3,

\displaystyle\lambda^{\varepsilon}_{\rm int}

\displaystyle\to\infty,\ \lambda^{\varepsilon}_{\rm ext}\to\infty\ \mbox{if}\ d=2.

(3.6)

The remaining viscosity coefficients in the cases
d=2,3
can be arbitrary as long as they remain non–negative. The necessity of having large bulk viscosity in the 2-d case is due to the gap in the corresponding Korn inequality, cf. Dain
[
3
]
.

3.1.1
Singular limit in the equation of continuity

If both (
3.5
) and (
3.6
) are satisfied, we can use the traceless variant of Korn’s inequality (cf.
[
7
, Chapter 11, Section 10]
) together with the energy bounds (
2.12
) to conclude

\displaystyle{\bf u}_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\to{\bf u}\ \mbox{weakly in}\ L^{2}(0,T;W^{1,2}(\mathbb{T}^{d};R^{d})),

\displaystyle{\bf u}_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\to 0\ \mbox{(strongly) in}\ {L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega_{\rm int}\cup\Omega_{\rm ext};R^{d})},

\displaystyle{\rm div}_{x}{\bf u}_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\to 0\ \mbox{in}\ {L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega_{\rm int}\cup\Omega_{\rm ext}))}

(3.7)

passing to a suitable subsequence as the case may be. In addition, we may use the energy bounds together with the equation of continuity (
2.2
) to deduce

\varrho_{\varepsilon}\to\varrho\ \mbox{in}\ C_{\rm weak}([0,T];L^{\gamma}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))

(3.8)

again for a suitable subsequence. The limit satisfies the equation of continuity (
2.2
) in
(0,T)\times\mathbb{T}^{d}
. As
{\bf u}=0
in
\Omega_{\rm int}\cup\Omega_{\rm ext}
we have
\varrho=\varrho_{0}
in

\Omega_{\rm int}\cup\Omega_{\rm ext}
if the initial density is independent of
\varepsilon
.

Finally, we may use the renormalized equation of continuity (
2.3
)
and the strong convergence of
{\bf u}_{\varepsilon}
and
{\rm div}_{x}{\bf u}_{\varepsilon}
on the solid part established in (
3.7
) to conclude

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}b(\varrho_{\varepsilon})\varphi\ \,{\rm d}{x}\to\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}b(\varrho_{0})\varphi\ \,{\rm d}{x}\ \mbox{uniformly in}\ C[0,T]

for any
\varphi\in C^{1}_{c}(\Omega_{\rm int}\cup\Omega_{\rm ext})
, which yields strong convergence of the densities on the solid part,

\varrho_{\varepsilon}\to{\varrho}\ \mbox{in}\ {C([0,T];L^{1}(\Omega_{\rm int}\cup\Omega_{\rm ext}))}.

(3.9)

3.1.2
Singular limit in the momentum equation

In order to perform the limit in the momentum equation, we have to specify the transport coefficients
\mu_{\varepsilon}
,
\eta_{\varepsilon}
at least in the domain
\Omega
. We set

\mu^{\varepsilon}_{\rm int}=\mu_{\rm int}>0,\eta^{\varepsilon}_{\rm int}=\eta_{\rm int}>0,\ \mu^{\varepsilon}_{F}=\mu_{F}>0,\ {\eta^{\varepsilon}_{F}=\eta_{F}>0},

(3.10)

leaving the specific choice of
\mu^{\varepsilon}_{\rm ext}
,
\eta^{\varepsilon}_{\rm ext}
open. As the test functions in the momentum equation are compactly supported in
\Omega_{F}
, the piece of information (

\displaystyle{\bf H}_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\to{\bf H}\ \mbox{weakly in}\ L^{2}(0,T;W^{1,2}(Q;R^{d}))\ \mbox{for any compact}\ Q\subset\Omega_{F},

\displaystyle{\bf H}_{\varepsilon}\otimes{\bf H}_{\varepsilon}-\frac{1}{2}|{\bf H}_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\mathbb{I}

\displaystyle>1.

1.

The available energy estimates yield the pressure sequence

Using (

(5.2)

The limit function satisfies a weak formulation of the induction equation

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\Big[\mu{\bf H}\cdot\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{\varphi}-(\mu{\bf H}\times\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{F}}{\bf u})\cdot{\bf curl}_{x}\boldsymbol{\varphi}-\eta{\bf curl}_{x}{\bf H}\cdot{\bf curl}_{x}\boldsymbol{\varphi}\Big]\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t=-\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\mu{\bf H}_{0}\cdot\boldsymbol{\varphi}\ \,{\rm d}{x}

for any
\boldsymbol{\varphi}\in C^{1}_{c}([0,T)\times\mathbb{T}^{d};R^{d})
,
{\bf curl}_{x}\boldsymbol{\varphi}|_{\Omega_{\rm ext}}=0
. Moreover,

{\rm div}_{x}(\mu{\bf H})=0,\ \mu=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\mu_{\rm int}\ \mbox{in}\ \Omega_{\rm int},\\
\mu_{F}\ \mbox{in}\ \Omega_{F},\\
\mu_{\rm ext}\ \mbox{in}\ \Omega_{\rm ext}.\end{array}\right.

The limit passage in the energy inequality (
2.12
) is a routine matter based on weak lower semi–continuty of convex functionals and the hypotheses imposed on the initial data.

5.2
Perfect magnetic conductor (PMC)

Now, we consider the scaling

\mu^{\varepsilon}_{\rm ext}\to\infty,\ \eta^{\varepsilon}_{\rm ext}=\eta_{\rm ext}>0\ \mbox{fixed}.

(5.3)

Similarly to the preceding part, we deduce

\displaystyle{\bf H}_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\to{\bf H}\ \mbox{in}\ C_{\rm weak}([0,T];L^{2}(\Omega;R^{d})),

\displaystyle{\bf H}|_{\Omega_{\rm ext}}

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle{\bf curl}_{x}{\bf H}_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\to{\bf curl}_{x}{\bf H}\ \mbox{weakly in}\ L^{2}((0,T)\times\mathbb{T}^{d};R^{d}).

(5.4)

Now, the limit satisfies the induction equation in
(0,T)\times\Omega
,

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\Big[\mu{\bf H}\cdot\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{\varphi}-(\mu{\bf H}\times\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{F}}{\bf u})\cdot{\bf curl}_{x}\boldsymbol{\varphi}-\eta{\bf curl}_{x}{\bf H}\cdot{\bf curl}_{x}\boldsymbol{\varphi}\Big]\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t=-\int_{\Omega}\mu{\bf H}_{0}\cdot\boldsymbol{\varphi}\ \,{\rm d}{x}

for any
\boldsymbol{\varphi}\in C^{1}_{c}([0,T)\times\Omega;R^{d})
, together with

{\rm div}_{x}(\mu{\bf H})=0,\ \mu=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\mu_{\rm int}\ \mbox{in}\ \Omega_{\rm int},\\
\mu_{F}\ \mbox{in}\ \Omega_{F}.\end{array}\right.

Finally, as
{\bf H}
vanishes outside
\Omega
and
{\bf curl}_{x}{\bf H}
exists in
\mathbb{T}^{d}
, we deduce the desired boundary condition

{\bf H}\times{\bf n}|_{\partial\Omega}=0

using continuity of the tagent traces.

5.3
Perfect electric conductor

We examine the situation

\mu^{\varepsilon}_{\rm ext}\to 0,\ \eta^{\varepsilon}_{\rm ext}\to 0.

(5.5)

First observe

\mu_{\varepsilon}{\bf H}_{\varepsilon}\to\mu{\bf H},\ \mu_{\rm ext}=0

(5.6)

As
\mu_{F}>0
, the continuity of the normal trace yields

{\bf H}\cdot{\bf n}|_{\partial\Omega}=0.

Seeing that
\eta^{\varepsilon}_{\rm ext}\to 0
, one can apply the same argument to
the induction equation obtaining

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\Big[\mu{\bf H}\cdot\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{\varphi}-(\mu{\bf H}\times\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{F}}{\bf u})\cdot{\bf curl}_{x}\boldsymbol{\varphi}-\eta{\bf curl}_{x}{\bf H}\cdot{\bf curl}_{x}\boldsymbol{\varphi}\Big]\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t=-\int_{\Omega}\mu{\bf H}_{0}\cdot\boldsymbol{\varphi}\ \,{\rm d}{x}

(5.7)

for any
\boldsymbol{\varphi}\in C^{1}_{c}([0,T)\times\overline{\Omega};R^{d})
. This yields the Neumann type boundary condition

{\bf curl}_{x}{\bf H}\times{\bf n}|_{\partial\Omega}=0

(5.8)

implicitly satisfied by the choice of test functions in (
5.7
).

5.4
Isolator type boundary conditions

Finally, we consider the limit

\mu^{\varepsilon}_{\rm ext}\to 0,\ \eta^{\varepsilon}_{\rm ext}\to\infty.

(5.9)

Exactly as in the preceding step, we deduce the condition

{\bf H}\cdot{\bf n}|_{\partial\Omega}=0.

(5.10)

In contrast with the previous step, however, the induction equation

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\Big[\mu{\bf H}\cdot\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{\varphi}-(\mu{\bf H}\times\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{F}}{\bf u})\cdot{\bf curl}_{x}\boldsymbol{\varphi}-\eta{\bf curl}_{x}{\bf H}\cdot{\bf curl}_{x}\boldsymbol{\varphi}\Big]\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t=-\int_{\Omega}\mu{\bf H}_{0}\cdot\boldsymbol{\varphi}\ \,{\rm d}{x}

(5.11)

is satisfied only for compactly supported test functions

\boldsymbol{\varphi}\in C^{1}_{c}([0,T)\times\Omega;R^{d})
.
However,
{\bf curl}_{x}H_{\varepsilon}
is a solenoidal function converging weakly to a limit that vanishes on
\Omega_{\rm ext}
. By continuity of the normal trace, we get

{\bf curl}_{x}{\bf H}\cdot{\bf n}|_{\partial\Omega}=0.

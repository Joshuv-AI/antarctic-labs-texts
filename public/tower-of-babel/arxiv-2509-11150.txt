Injective modules over pseudo-krullian orders
Yuriy A. Drozd

Abstract
. We introduce a new class of rings, pseudo-krullian orders , consider the Serre quotients of their module categories with respect to pseudo-isomorphisms and describe injective objects in such quotient categories and its global homological dimension. These results generalize the results of I. Beck for the case of Krull rings. In particular, we establish the global homological dimension of the category of maximal Cohen-Macaulay modules over an order over a noetherian ring of Krull dimension 2.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
16D50, 16E10

Theory of divisors, originated from the classical papers of Kronecker on algebraic numbers, developed into a vast part
of commutative algebra and algebraic geometry. The most accomplished form it has for
Krull rings
, in particular,
normal (integrally closed) noetherian rings (see, for instance,
[
2
, Ch. VII]
). In particular, in this case the
reduction of ideals to divisors is naturally extended to arbitrary modules as
pseudo-isomorphism
,
that is “isomorphism in codimension
1
” (ibid.). Actually, it is a special case of Serre quotient for the category of modules,
which clarify some questions about structure of modules. In his paper
[
1
]
I. Beck has studied the structure
of this quotient with special attention to injective objects and their relations with injective modules. In this paper we
generalize his results to much more general situation of
pseudo-krullian orders
, maybe noncommutative and, if commutative domains,
not necessarily integrally closed. Following I. Beck, we introduce a special class of modules (
codivisorial modules
) and
establish its relations with the Serre quotient with respect to pseudo-isomorphisms. We also show that injective codivisorial
modules behave just as injective modules over noetherian rings and describe injective objects in the
quotient category. As a corollary, we establish the global homological dimension of this quotient category.
As an application, we find the global homological dimension of the category of maximal Cohen-Macaulay
modules over a finite algebra over a noetherian ring of Krull dimension
2
. In particular, in case of a normal domain or some
orders over such domain this category is hereditary (of global homological dimension
1
).

1.
Pseudo-krullian orders and codivisorial modules

Recall that a commutative ring
R
is called
pseudo-noetherian

[
5
,
6
]
if the following conditions hold:

(1)

For every element
a\in R
the set
V_{min}(a)
of prime ideals minimal among those containing
a
is finite.

(2)

For every
\mathfrak{p}\in V_{min}(a)
the ring
R_{\mathfrak{p}}
is noetherian.

Noetherian rings and Krull rings are examples of pseudo-noetherian rings. One easily sees that
\mathop{\mathrm{}}{ht}\mathfrak{p}\leq 1
for every prime ideal

\mathfrak{p}\in V_{min}(a)
and
\mathop{\mathrm{}}{ht}\mathfrak{p}=1
if
a
is a non-zero-divisor. Let
\{\mathfrak{n}_{1},\mathfrak{n}_{2},\dots,\mathfrak{n}_{s}\}
be the set of minimal prime ideals of
R

(that is
V_{min}(0)
). Then the rings
R_{\mathfrak{n}_{j}}
are artinian. If, moreover,
R
is reduced (has no nilpotent elements),

Q_{j}=R_{\mathfrak{n}_{j}}
is a field and
R
embeds into the semisimple ring
Q=\prod_{j=1}^{s}Q_{j}
, which is the full ring of
fractions of
R
. In this case
Z(R)=\bigcup_{j=1}^{s}\mathfrak{n}_{j}
is the set of zero divisors of
R
and
\bigcap_{j=1}^{s}\mathfrak{n}_{j}=0
.
For an
R
-module
M
we write
QM
instead of
Q\otimes_{R}M
. We say that an element

is
torsion

if there is a non-zero-divisor
a\in R
such that
ax=0
and
torsion free
otherwise. Obviously,
x
is torsion iff
1\otimes x=0

in
QM
. If all elements of
M
are torsion (resp., torsion free), we call the module
M

torsion
(resp., torsion
free), and we identify a torsion free module with its image in
QM
. We denote by
\mathop{\mathrm{}}{tors}M
the submodule of all torsion
elements and by
\mathop{\mathrm{}}{tf}M
the quotient
M/\mathop{\mathrm{}}{tors}M
.

An algebra
A
over a pseudo-noetherian ring

is called
pseudo-noetherian
if
A_{\mathfrak{p}}
is noetherian for every prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}
with
\mathop{\mathrm{}}{ht}\mathfrak{p}\leq 1
.
If
R
is reduced,
QA
is a finite algebra over the artinian ring
Q
; if
A
is reduced itself (has no nilpotent ideals) this algebra
is semisimple. If, moreover,
A
is torsion free as
R
-module, hence embeds into
QA
, we call it a
pseudo-noetherian order
.

In what follows
R
is a reduced pseudo-noetherian ring and
A
is a reduced pseudo-noetherian order over
R
. We denote by
\mathcal{P}=\mathcal{P}(R)
the set of prime ideals
of
R
of height
1
and by
\mathscr{X}=\mathscr{X}_{A}
the full subcategory of
A\mbox{-}\mathrm{Mod}
consisting of all modules
M
such that

M_{\mathfrak{p}}=0
for all
\mathfrak{p}\in\mathcal{P}
.
We also consider two full subcategories of
A\mbox{-}\mathrm{Mod}
related to
\mathcal{X}
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}=\mathcal{M}(A)

\displaystyle=\{M\in A\mbox{-}\mathrm{Mod}\mid X\in\mathcal{X}\,\Rightarrow\,\mathop{\mathrm{}}{Hom}\nolimits_{A}(X,M)=0\}

\displaystyle=\{M\in A\mbox{-}\mathrm{Mod}\mid X\in\mathcal{X}\,\&\,X\subseteq M\,\Rightarrow\,X=0\}

and

\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{C}(A)=\{M\in A\mbox{-}\mathrm{Mod}\mid X\in\mathcal{X}\,\Rightarrow\,\mathop{\mathrm{}}{Hom}\nolimits_{A}(X,M)=\mathop{\mathrm{}}{Ext}\nolimits^{1}_{A}(X,M)=0\}.

Following P. Gabriel
[
7
]
, we call modules from
\mathcal{C}

closed
. Note that every injective module from
\mathcal{M}

is closed.
Obviously,
\mathcal{X}
is a Serre subcategory of
A\mbox{-}\mathrm{Mod}
, so the quotient category
A\mbox{-}\widetilde{\mathrm{Mod}}
is defined. Moreover, every
A
-module

contains the biggest submodule from
\mathcal{X}
, namely
M_{\mathcal{X}}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}N\subseteq M\\
N\in\mathcal{X}\end{subarray}}N
. Therefore,
\mathcal{X}
is a

localizing subcategory
in
A\mbox{-}\mathrm{Mod}
, that is the natural functor
F:A\mbox{-}\mathrm{Mod}\to A\mbox{-}\widetilde{\mathrm{Mod}}
has a right adjoint

[
7
, p. 375, cor. 1]
. We denote by
\mathop{\widetilde{\mathrm{Hom}}}\nolimits_{A}(M,M^{\prime})
the set of morphisms
FM\to FM^{\prime}
in the category
A\mbox{-}\widetilde{\mathrm{Mod}}
.
If
\alpha
is such that

is an isomorphism, we say, following
[
2
]
that

is a

.
It means that all localizations
\alpha_{\mathfrak{p}}\ (\mathfrak{p}\in\mathcal{P})
are isomorphisms or, equivalently, that both
\mathop{\mathrm{}}{Ker}\nolimits\alpha
and
\mathop{\mathrm{}}{Coker}\nolimits\alpha
are in

.

The following facts are those from
[
7
, Ch. III]

Fact 1.1
.

(1)

FG\simeq\mathrm{Id}_{A\mbox{-}\widetilde{\mathrm{Mod}}}
.

(2)

If

is closed, for arbitrary module

the functor

induces an isomorphism

\mathop{\mathrm{}}{Hom}\nolimits_{A}(N,M)\simeq\mathop{\widetilde{\mathrm{Hom}}}\nolimits_{A}(N,M)
.

(3)

is closed iff the adjunction map

is an isomorphism. Equivalently,

for some
N\in A\mbox{-}\widetilde{\mathrm{Mod}}
.

(4)

The functors

and

induce an equivalence of the categories
\mathcal{C}(A)
and

.

(5)

Both
\mathcal{X}
and

(hence

) are

.

Example 1.2
.

Let
R
be local noetherian of Krull dimension
2
.

•

\mathcal{M}(R)
consists of modules that have no simple (hence no artinian) submodules.

•

\mathcal{C}(R)
is the subcategory of maximal Cohen-Macauley modules (maybe infinitely generated).

On the other hand,
\mathcal{M}
is a reflective subcategory, that is the embedding functor
\mathcal{M}\hookrightarrow A\mbox{-}\mathrm{Mod}
has a left adjoint,
namely the functor
M\mapsto M/M_{\mathcal{X}}
. Following I. Beck
[
1
]
, we call modules from
\mathcal{M}

codivisorial
.
The following considerations explain this terminology.

Definition 1.3
.

Let
M
be a torsion free
R
-module (for instance an ideal of
R
). We set

M_{\mathcal{P}}=\{x\in QM\mid\forall\,\mathfrak{p}\in\mathcal{P}\ \exists\,r\in R{\smallsetminus}\mathfrak{p}\ rx\in A\}

and call
M

divisorial
if
M_{\mathcal{P}}=M
. We call the ring
R

pseudo-krullian
if it is divisorial as
R
-module.
Krull rings are just pseudo-krullian normal domains. A
pseudo-krullian order
over
R
is a pseudo-noetherian order which is divisorial as
R
-module.

Note that if
A
is not pseudo-krullian and
\mathfrak{a}\subset A
is a left (or right) ideal, it can happen that
\mathfrak{a}_{\mathcal{P}}\not\subseteq A
. We call
\mathfrak{a}

quasidivisorial
if
\mathfrak{a}=A\cap\mathfrak{a}_{\mathcal{P}}
. If
A
is pseudo-krullian, quasidivisorial is the same as divisorial.

Proposition 1.4
.

M\in\mathcal{M}
iff for every nonzero
x\in M
the left ideal
\mathfrak{a}=\mathop{\mathrm{}}{ann}\nolimits_{A}x
is quasidivisorial.

Proof.

If
a\in\mathfrak{a}_{\mathcal{P}}
, for every
\mathfrak{p}\in\mathcal{P}
there is an element
r\in R{\smallsetminus}\mathfrak{p}
such that
ra\in\mathfrak{a}
, hence
r\in\mathop{\mathrm{}}{ann}\nolimits_{R}ax
.
Therefore
(Aax)_{\mathfrak{p}}=0
for every
\mathfrak{p}\in\mathcal{P}
. If
M\in\mathcal{M}
it implies that
ax=0
, that is
a\in\mathfrak{a}
and
\mathfrak{a}
is
divisorial. On the other hand, if
M\notin\mathcal{M}
, there is a nonzero element
x\in M
such that
\frac{x}{1}=0
in every
localization
M_{\mathfrak{p}}
, where
\mathfrak{p}\in\mathcal{P}
, that is there is
r\in R{\smallsetminus}\mathfrak{p}
such that
rx=0
. For the ideal
\mathfrak{a}=\mathop{\mathrm{}}{ann}\nolimits_{R}x

it means that
\mathfrak{a}\cap R\not\subseteq\mathfrak{p}
, whence
\mathfrak{a}_{\mathfrak{p}}=A_{\mathfrak{p}}
and
\mathfrak{a}_{\mathcal{P}}=A_{\mathcal{P}}
, so
\mathfrak{a}
is not quasidivisorial.
∎

Corollary 1.5
.

(1)

If
M
is codivisorial, the ideal
\mathop{\mathrm{}}{ann}\nolimits_{A}M
is quasidivisorial.

(2)

An ideal
\mathfrak{a}\subseteq A
is quasidivisorial iff the module
A/\mathfrak{a}
is codivisorial.

Proposition 1.6
.

(1)

If
M^{\prime},M^{\prime\prime}
are codivisorial and
0\to M^{\prime}\to M\to M^{\prime\prime}
is an exact sequence,
M
is codivisorial.

(2)

If
M\hookrightarrow M^{\prime}
is an essential extension of a codivisorial module
M
, then
M^{\prime}
is codivisorial.
In particular, the injective envelope of a codivisorial module is codivisorial (hence closed).

(3)

If
M\subseteq M^{\prime}
,
M^{\prime}
is codivisorial and
M^{\prime}/M\in\mathcal{X}
, then
M^{\prime}
is an essential extension of
M
.

Proof.

(1) and (2) are obvious.

(3) Let
x\in M^{\prime}{\smallsetminus}M
,
\bar{x}=x+M\in M^{\prime}/M
,
\mathfrak{a}=\mathop{\mathrm{}}{ann}\nolimits_{R}x
and
\mathfrak{b}=\mathop{\mathrm{}}{ann}\nolimits_{R}\bar{x}
. As
M^{\prime}/M\in\mathcal{X}
, the ideal
\mathfrak{b}

is contained in neither ideal
\mathfrak{p}\in\mathcal{P}
. On the other hand, as
M^{\prime}\in\mathcal{M}
, there is a prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}
such that

\mathfrak{a}\subseteq\mathfrak{p}
. Hence
\mathfrak{b}\neq\mathfrak{a}
and there is
a\in R
such that
ax\neq 0
but
ax\in M
. It means that the
extension
M\subseteq M^{\prime}
is essential.
∎

We denote by
E(M)
(or
E_{A}(M)
, if necessary) the injective envelope of the
A
-module
M
.

Corollary 1.7
.

Let
M^{\prime}=M/M_{\mathcal{X}},\ E=E(M^{\prime})
and

be the preimage in

of
(E/M^{\prime})_{\mathcal{X}}
. Then

.

Proof.

As
FM\simeq FM^{\prime}
, we may suppose that

is codivisorial. Then the adjunction map
\gamma:M\to GFM
is a
monomorphism and
\mathop{\mathrm{}}{Coker}\nolimits\gamma\in\mathcal{X}
. Therefore,

is an essential monomorphism, thus there is a monomorphism

\gamma^{\prime}:GFM\to E
such that

, that is
\mathop{\mathrm{}}{Im}\nolimits\gamma^{\prime}\subseteq c(M)
. Moreover,

. Since

\mathop{\mathrm{}}{Im}\nolimits\gamma^{\prime}\simeq GFM

\mathop{\mathrm{}}{Im}\nolimits\gamma^{\prime}=c(M)
.
∎

Theorem 1.10
.

(1)

If
M

GFM\simeq\coprod_{\mathfrak{p}\in\mathcal{P}}M_{\mathfrak{p}}
.

(2)

If

(1) As

Note that every divisorial (in particular closed)

2.
Injective modules and global dimension

Now we describe codivisorial (or, the same, closed) injective modules or, equivalently, injective objects in the category
A\mbox{-}\widetilde{\mathrm{Mod}}

As mentioned in Rem.
1.11
, we may (and will) suppose that the ring

Fact 2.1
.

Let
A
be an
R

\mathfrak{p}
be a prime ideal of

R_{\mathfrak{p}}
is noetherian and
A_{\mathfrak{p}}
is a finite

Set

(1)

Let now

(2)

(3)

Theorem 2.2

Let

E\simeq E(U_{\mathfrak{P}})

for some

Proof.

Note that

, where

If it is torsion free, then the natural map

for some

Let

Any coproduct of indecomposable injective codivisorial modules is injective and any codivisorial injective module is a

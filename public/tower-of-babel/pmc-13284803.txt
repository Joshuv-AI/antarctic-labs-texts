Enhancing lung health in children with structural damage and malformations: trial protocol for a randomised controlled trial of azithromycin for airway infection prevention
Authors: Marika Nathalie Rønne, Frederik Fouirnaies Buchvald, Elisabeth Søgaard Christiansen, Helene Holmark Møller, Sune Mørck Rubak, Marianne Skov, Signe Thim, Kim Gjerum Nielsen
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundChildren with early-onset structural lung damage or lung and airway malformations – congenital or acquired – are highly susceptible to recurrent respiratory tract infections, leading to frequent hospitalisations, impaired lung function and reduced quality of life. Although azithromycin maintenance therapy is commonly used in this population, robust clinical evidence supporting its efficacy is lacking.ObjectivesThe primary aim of this trial is to evaluate the efficacy of azithromycin maintenance therapy in reducing respiratory infections among young children with structural lung damage or lung and airway malformations. Secondary objectives are to assess the efficacy of azithromycin on lung function, daily respiratory symptoms and quality of life, as well as the safety of long-term azithromycin use in this age group.MethodsThis is a national, multicentre, double-blind, placebo-controlled randomised clinical trial conducted across three specialised paediatric pulmonology centres in Denmark. A total of 150 children aged 0–72 months with structural lung damage or lung or airway malformations confirmed by bronchoscopy or radiology will be enrolled. Participants will be randomised 1:1 to receive either oral azithromycin (10 mg·kg−1) or placebo three times weekly for 6 months during the winter season.Ethics and disseminationThe trial has been approved by the European Medical Agency (EMA). It is expected to provide high-quality evidence on efficacy and safety of azithromycin maintenance therapy in a vulnerable paediatric population with rare respiratory conditions. Positive findings will inform clinical guidelines and help enhance lung health; conversely, if treatment proves ineffective, results may support efforts to reduce unnecessary antibiotic use.

Introduction

Children with early-onset structural lung damage or lung and airway malformations – congenital or acquired – are at increased risk of recurrent respiratory tract infections in early childhood [1], leading to frequent hospitalisations, impaired lung function and reduced quality of life. Evidence of effective preventive strategies for this population remains limited.

The relationship between airway abnormalities and persistent bacterial bronchitis is not fully understood [2–7]. However, several pathophysiological mechanisms have been proposed. These include impaired mucociliary clearance due to abnormal airway anatomy, chronic epithelial stress and epithelial metaplasia, all of which may promote bacterial persistence and recurrent infection [8, 9]. Studies report that up to 52% of children with persistent bacterial bronchitis have underlying lung and airway malformations, and while prolonged antibiotic treatment can relieve symptoms, relapses are common [4, 8].

A recent task force report highlighted that clinicians often have a low threshold for prescribing antibiotics in children with lung and airway malformations during episodes of acute respiratory illness [1]. Furthermore, prophylactic azithromycin is frequently used, despite lack of robust evidence supporting its efficacy in this specific population [1].

Azithromycin has shown promise in reducing respiratory infections in children with asthma [10] and as maintenance therapy for children and adults with various types of lung damage [11–16]. Beyond its antimicrobial activity, azithromycin exhibits anti-inflammatory and immunomodulatory properties, which may enhance its potential benefit in the setting of recurrent infections [17, 18].

Children with structural lung damage or lung and airway malformations often share a similar clinical trajectory: recurrent lower respiratory tract infections, especially during the winter months, resulting in acute illness, hospitalisations, daycare absence and significant parental work leave [1, 6, 7]. However, no randomised trials to date have investigated its preventive efficacy in this vulnerable population.

This article presents the protocol for a randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled clinical trial investigating the efficacy and safety of azithromycin maintenance therapy for preventing respiratory infections in children aged 0–72 months with structural lung damage or lung or airway malformation, thereby improving quality of life and easing the healthcare and socioeconomic burden.

Objectives

The primary objective of this trial is to evaluate the efficacy of a 6-month azithromycin maintenance therapy (AMT) in reducing the incidence of respiratory infections in children aged 0–72 months with structural lung damage or lung and airway malformations.

Methods

Patient and public involvement

Patients or caregivers were not directly involved in the trial design or conduct. However, the trial was informed by feedback from families of children with structural lung damage or airway malformations, emphasising the need for better infection prevention. Trial results will later be shared with participants in lay language.

Trial design

This trial is an investigator-initiated, multicentre, double-blind, randomised, placebo-controlled trial.

Trial setting

The trial will be conducted at three sites being the three tertiary paediatric pulmonary centres in Denmark: Copenhagen University Hospital – Rigshospitalet, Odense University Hospital and Aarhus University Hospital.

Intervention and comparator

Participants will receive either azithromycin 10 mg·kg−1 (maximum 500 mg) or matching placebo, administered orally three times weekly for 6 months. The treatment period will run from 1 October to 31 March (±14 days) (figure 1).

A matching placebo was chosen to maintain blinding and allow unbiased evaluation of azithromycin against standard care, as no preventive therapy is currently established for this population.

Azithromycin will be provided as a flavoured powder for oral suspension (Zithromax 40 mg·mL−1; cherry, banana and vanilla flavours), which will be reconstituted with water by unblinded site staff prior to dispensing. The placebo is an orange-flavoured oral solution, pre-manufactured with a shelf life of ∼6 months, and packaged identically to the active treatment, including labelling and administration utensils. Both formulations must be discarded 5 days after opening.

The placebo was chosen to allow an unbiased comparison of azithromycin against standard care, as no infection preventive therapy is currently established for this population.

Treatment adherence will be assessed by caregiver-completed diaries and by counting returned medication bottles at each trial visit. Caregivers will receive thorough verbal and written instructions on dosing, storage and handling procedures from unblinded site personnel.

Criteria for discontinuation or modification

Treatment will be discontinued or temporarily paused if the participant experiences a suspected serious adverse reaction, or at the request of the participant's caregivers or treating physician. Dose modification is not planned. Participants who discontinue trial medication will be encouraged to continue trial follow-up and data collection.

Concomitant care

All other medical care will follow standard clinical practice. Antibiotics are prescribed in acute exacerbations when clinically indicated, following local guidelines.

Drugs that interfere with lung function testing must be withheld according to guidelines. Concomitant medications contraindicated with azithromycin are prohibited from screening and throughout the trial period. Antacids must not be taken simultaneously with the trial medication, as they may reduce peak serum azithromycin levels.

Outcomes

Primary outcome

The primary outcome is the number of respiratory infections during the treatment period. A respiratory infection is defined as new or worsening respiratory symptoms requiring antibiotic treatment as prescribed by the trial investigator – whether during scheduled visits, acute visits or during telephone consultations – irrespective of sputum collection or bacterial culture.

Secondary outcome

The secondary outcome is infection severity, evaluated by: duration of symptoms;need for hospitalisation;length of hospital stays (if applicable).

Daily respiratory symptom scores

Parents will report daily respiratory symptoms, absence from daycare, medication and adverse events (AEs) via REDCap.

Health-related quality of life

Health-related quality of life will be assessed using age-appropriate, electronic versions of the PedsQL, specifically the parent-proxy forms for children aged 2–4 and 4–7 years [23].

Scoring will follow the PedsQL manual, generating total and domain-specific scores, enabling assessment of treatment effects on overall well-being and symptom burden.

Sputum microbiology and lung microbiome

Sputum samples will be collected during infections at acute visits through laryngeal suction or expectoration. Standard microbiological analyses will include bacterial culture, antibiotic susceptibility testing and PCR-based detection for respiratory viruses.

Inflammatory markers

Blood samples will be analysed for C-reactive protein (CRP) and white cell count including differential cell counts.

Airway immunology is assessed at screening and after trial completion by measuring unstimulated levels of 20 cytokines and chemokines in airway mucosal lining fluid sampled by a nasosorption technique as previously detailed [24].

Harms and safety

Safety will be monitored throughout the trial by structured interviews, physical examinations (including vital signs), and laboratory testing (haemoglobin, white cell count, platelets, alanine transaminase and creatinine). All AEs including new or worsening symptoms and relevant laboratory abnormalities, or health-related incidents will be recorded in the electronic case report form (eCRF) and categorised by severity and relationship to the trial drug (definitely, probably, possibly or unrelated). Serious AEs will be reported per regulatory requirements and followed until resolution or stabilisation. Participants may be withdrawn in case of serious adverse drug reactions.

A 2-month follow-up period is included to capture late-onset AEs, reflecting azithromycin's prolonged half-life [25].

Sample size

The total sample size is 150 children, allowing for 20% attrition. The sample size is based on the primary outcome, number of respiratory infections. Assuming a 50% reduction in infection rate in the azithromycin group, based on previous studies of children with lung disease [16, 26], and using a Poisson distribution, 120 evaluable participants (60 per group) will provide 90% power to detect this difference at a two-sided significance level of 0.05.

Recruitment

Eligible children are identified during routine outpatient visits at the three participating sites. Eligibility criteria are reviewed by the treating clinician, and if the child meets all criteria, recruitment is conducted by the site investigator, who provides detailed verbal and written information about the trial to parents. Adequate time is allowed for consideration before obtaining written informed consent from the parents.

Enrolment will be supported through systematic screening at outpatient clinics, regular monitoring of recruitment progress and close collaboration with local clinicians to ensure that the target sample size is achieved.

Randomisation

Participants will be randomly assigned in a 1:1 ratio to receive either azithromycin or placebo. Randomisation will be simple and stratified by age group (0–36 months; 37–72 months) to ensure balanced distribution across treatment arms. A computer-generated randomisation list will be maintained by the hospital pharmacy, with allocation codes securely stored and inaccessible to investigators, clinical staff and participants. Unblinded staff at each site, with no role in clinical care or data collection, will dispense trial medication using the central system, and allocation will be revealed only at the time of assignment.

Blinding

Participants, parents/caregivers, data collectors and outcome assessors will be blinded to treatment allocation. Although blinding is challenged by differences in formulation and taste, it is maintained using unblinded personnel to dispense trial medication and instruct parents, and with all trial medication in identical bottles and labelling.

In the event of a suspected serious adverse reaction, the treating physician may unblind the participant via the primary site investigator, who may access a sealed code envelope from the pharmacy with the participant's assignment. All unblinding events must be documented.

Data collection

Participants will be screened during a stable period. At the screening visit, demographic information will be recorded and eligibility assessed. A structured clinical interview will capture medical history, current symptoms and concomitant medication use. Physical examination and an electrocardiogram will be conducted. Baseline blood samples for safety parameters and inflammatory markers and a nasopharyngeal sample will be collected. The PedsQL questionnaire will be completed by parents to assess baseline quality of life.

Following screening, participants will undergo a 1-month run-in period to ensure washout of prohibited medication. Randomisation is postponed for 14 days if participants have received antibiotics for >2 weeks during the run-in period.

Eligible participants will then be randomised to azithromycin or placebo for 6 months, with trial visits at baseline (randomisation), 3 months and end of treatment. At each visit, symptoms and adverse events will be reviewed, and physical examination, age-appropriate lung function testing, blood and nasopharyngeal sampling, and PedsQL assessment will be performed.

Throughout the treatment period, parents will complete an electronic trial diary every day using REDCap, documenting respiratory symptoms, absence from daycare and medication adherence. Additional visits or phone consultations will be scheduled upon infection, including clinical evaluation and sputum sampling by expectoration or laryngeal suction.

A final follow-up assessment, including a safety evaluation, will be conducted 2 months after completion of the treatment phase (table 2).

Data management

All trial data will be recorded in a secure eCRF. Access to site-specific data will be restricted to the respective site investigators; full dataset access will be limited to trial sponsor. Data will be entered using validation rules, and all changes will be auditable. Participant confidentiality will be ensured through coded identifiers and separate storage of personal information. Regular monitoring and quality checks will ensure data accuracy, completeness and compliance with the protocol.

Statistical analysis plan

Analyses will be conducted on both the intention-to-treat (ITT) and per-protocol (PP) populations, defined prior to unblinding. The ITT population includes all randomised participants, while the PP population excludes those with major protocol deviations or early discontinuation.

Descriptive statistics will be used for baseline characteristics and outcomes. Continuous variables will be summarised as mean±sd or median (range), with log transformation applied as appropriate. Categorical variables will be reported as frequencies and percentages. All tests will be two-sided with a significance level of 0.05.

The primary end-point (infection rate) will be analysed using Poisson regression adjusted for age. Secondary and exploratory end-points will be analysed using mixed linear models, with relevant covariates (e.g. age) included as fixed effects. Categorical outcomes will be analysed using McNemar's test or tests of marginal homogeneity. Missing data will not be imputed. Participants who withdraw will be included in the ITT analysis up to their latest available data point.

Monitoring

An independent Data Monitoring and safety Committee (DMC) will review unblinded safety and efficacy halfway through recruitment; only the DMC will have access to these results and will recommend any early trial termination. Trial conduct, including adherence to protocol, data quality and safety reporting, will be monitored in collaboration with the independent Good Clinical Practice (GCP) unit in Copenhagen (LOC-100045259) through on-site and remote visits.

Ethics

The trial is awaiting approval from the European Medical Agency (EMA; EU CT nr.: 2024-518730-88-00). Any important protocol modifications will be submitted to the ethics committees and regulatory authorities; once approved, they will be communicated to investigators and trial participants as appropriate.

Written informed consent from the child's parents is obtained by a GCP-trained investigator or delegated physician before initiation of any trial-related procedures. Children receive age-appropriate information. Prior to consent families are informed of trial purpose, risks, benefits and their right to withdraw at any time.

Participant confidentiality will be maintained in accordance with General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) and Danish Data Protection Act. Personal identifiers are stored separately from trial data and replaced with trial IDs. Access to identifiable information is restricted to authorised trial staff. Data are stored on secure, password-protected servers and retained only as required by regulations.

Discussion

Despite widespread off-label use of azithromycin as maintenance therapy in children with structural lung damage or congenital or acquired lung and airway malformations, its efficacy and safety have never been rigorously assessed in this population. This randomised controlled trial (RCT) is the first to address that evidence gap.

Our primary outcome – the number of respiratory infections – is clinically meaningful, with direct implications for the child's well-being, daycare attendance, and parental work absence. The definition of infection as respiratory symptoms prompting antibiotic prescription reflects real-world clinical decision-making and aligns with pragmatic trial principles.

A major strength of this trial is its comprehensive design. It integrates daily symptom tracking, microbiological analyses, inflammatory markers and age-appropriate lung function testing. The inclusion of multiple modalities – MBW from infancy, IOS from 3 years of age and spirometry from 5 years of age – allows for detailed characterisation of respiratory status. While age-dependent variability in test feasibility may limit direct comparisons and contribute to missing data, this multimodal approach enhances overall data richness and clinical relevance.

Another important aspect is the clinical heterogeneity of included children, spanning various congenital or acquired airway malformations and structural lung conditions. Although diagnoses differ, these children share structural abnormalities that promote bacterial persistence and heightened airway inflammation, leading to recurrent lower respiratory infections.

Azithromycin's anti-inflammatory effect may be particularly valuable in this setting. By reducing airway inflammation, it can limit progression of viral infections to secondary bacterial disease and extend the time to subsequent infection [27]. In this way, it addresses a central mechanism underlying recurrent illness in this vulnerable population. Previous RCTs such as the COPSAC [10] studies targeting shared pathophysiological features can yield meaningful benefits even in heterogeneous groups.

However, long-term use of azithromycin is associated with concerns of promoting antibiotic resistance, which remains a significant global public health threat under the watch of the World Health Organization (WHO). Throughout the trial, microbiological monitoring, including culture and antibiotic susceptibility testing of sputum samples, will be conducted to detect emergence of resistant strains. These data will be critical to the overall risk–benefit assessment of prophylactic azithromycin use in this patient population.

Whether effective or not, the trial will guide evidence-based use of azithromycin, protecting lung function if beneficial or avoiding unnecessary antibiotic exposure if not. The trial will inform clinical guidelines and support sustainable care for children with structural lung damage or lung and airway malformations.

Sensing Characteristics of the SnSe/GeSe Heterojunction on Dissolved Gases in Transformer Oil
Authors: Hutao Ma, Shuyong Guo, Lei Zhang, Song Ou, Chao Tang, Yingang Gui
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Oil immersed power transformers are important pieces of equipment in the power transmission and distribution system. The gas generated by partial discharge and other faults is dissolved in transformer oil, which reduces the insulation performance of equipment and seriously affects the safe and stable operation of the power system. The analysis of dissolved gas (DGA) in transformer oil can realize the monitoring and fault diagnosis of an oil immersed transformer. Based on the first-principles density functional theory, the adsorption characteristics of dissolved gases (H2, CO, C2H2, CH4, C2H4, C2H6) in the SnSe/GeSe heterojunction were studied. First, the optimized SnSe/GeSe heterostructure is obtained, and the large formation energy indicates that the heterostructure is stable. At the same time, compared with the intrinsic SnSe monolayer and GeSe monolayer, its conductivity is improved. The gas adsorption on both sides of the SnSe/GeSe heterojunction was considered, and the adsorption mechanism was further analyzed by the density of states and charge difference density. The results show that the adsorption capacity of the SnSe/GeSe heterojunction for dissolved gas in transformer oil is slightly improved, but the adsorption is still physical adsorption. The adsorption energies of the six gases are arranged as follows: C2H6 > C2H4 > C2H2 > CH4 > CO > H2. Although the sensitive gas monitoring based on the change of conductance is not realized in this study, it provides a theoretical basis for understanding the physical adsorption mechanism between such heterojunctions and gas molecules.

Introduction

The transformer plays a key role in voltage transformation, power distribution, and loss reduction in the power grid and is the core piece of equipment to ensure the stable and efficient operation of the power grid. − Although transformer oil has a superior performance, actual application of the oil shows that under long-term operation, overheating, partial discharge, and other faults will inevitably occur in the transformer, which will further cause the decomposition of hydrocarbon substances and generate H2, CO, CH4, C2H2, C2H4, C2H6, and other gases to dissolve in the transformer oil. − These gases easily produce bubble breakdown, further lead to partial discharge, continuously deteriorate the insulation performance, and even cause major fires, large-scale power failure, and other accidents. Therefore, dissolved gas analysis in transformer oil is also the mainstream means to monitor the operation status of an oil immersed transformer and diagnose fault types. − Gas sensors are widely used in online monitoring of dissolved gas in transformer oil because of their small size, low cost, and fast response. −

As one of the important series of two-dimensional semiconductors, two-dimensional Group IV metal chalcogenides (IV–VI) show unique physical properties in the fields of electricity, optics, thermoelectricity, and gas sensing due to their moderate band gap (1.0 eV ∼ 2.0 eV) and low lattice symmetry. − The research shows that the response to the target gas can be effectively improved by combining two specific materials to form a heterojunction. − Recently, the SnSe/GeSe heterojunction has attracted the attention of researchers. Ni et al. found that SnSe/GeSe is a direct semiconductor based on the first principle, and its band gap range can be flexibly tuned through in-plane strain, with good optical absorption capacity. Mao et al. proved that the GeSe/SnSe heterostructure has type II band alignment, direct band gap, and excellent hole carrier mobility. Shi et al. found that by combining the SnSe monolayer and GeSe monolayer to form a heterojunction, its sensing ability for SF6 decomposition gas can be greatly improved. It is worth noting that compared with widely used materials such as MoS2 and graphene, selenides such as SnSe and GeSe may cause concern about the potential biological toxicity and environmental impact after device treatment due to the presence of selenium. However, SnSe/GeSe is still a promising sensing material as long as end of life management and material sustainability are considered. Therefore, the SnSe/GeSe heterojunction is selected as the substrate material to study the adsorption characteristics of dissolved gases (H2, CO, CH4, C2H2, C2H4, C2H6) in transformer oil, in order to clarify its potential advantages and unique mechanism as a DGA sensor material.

Based on the first-principles density functional theory, this paper studies the adsorption characteristics of the SnSe/GeSe heterojunction for dissolved gases in various transformer oils, which lays a theoretical foundation for the experimental development of new gas sensors. The SnSe monolayer and GeSe monolayer are combined to form a double-layer SnSe/GeSe heterostructure, which is used as the substrate material to adsorb the dissolved gas in transformer oil. The stable adsorption structure after the optimization calculation was analyzed in detail. The adsorption characteristics and gas sensing mechanism of SnSe/GeSe heterojunction for dissolved gas in oil were studied through the analysis of adsorption energy, adsorption distance, charge transfer, density of states (DOS), and charge differential density (CDD).

Calculation Details

The calculation model uses 3 × 3 × 1 SnSe and GeSe supercells as the substrate. In order to prevent the influence between adjacent thin layers, a 20 Å vacuum layer is set for all simulation models. All calculations in this paper are completed based on DFT calculations. The calculation parameters are consistent with a large number of studies in the literature to ensure the accuracy of the calculation results. − In order to study the stability of modified and adsorbed structures, the binding energy (E b ) and adsorption energy (E ads ) were studied, respectively. As shown in eq , the binding energy E b represents the energy transformation during the formation of SnSe/GeSe heterojunction, where E heterojection represents the energy of the heterojunction and E layer1 and E layer2 represent the energies of SnSe monolayer and GeSe monolayer, respectively. Adsorption energy E ads represents the change of system energy before and after the substrate adsorbs gas molecules, as shown in eq . E surf and E gas/surf represent the energies of the base material before and after adsorption of gas molecules, respectively, and E gas represents the energy of isolated gas molecules. The charge transfer quantity (Q t ) is calculated by the Hirshfeld analysis method, as shown in eq . Q ads and Q iso represent the charge amounts of gas molecules adsorbed on the substrate and isolated gas molecules, respectively. Q t > 0 means that electrons are transferred from gas molecules to substrate materials during adsorption. The adsorption distance (d) represents the shortest distance between the gas molecules in the adsorption structure and the substrate. Modeling of Dissolved Gas and the SnSe/GeSe Heterojunction in Transformer Oil

The molecularly stable structure of dissolved gases (H2, CO, CO2, CH4, C2H2, C2H4, C2H6) in transformer oil is shown in Figure . The methane molecule (CH4) is tetrahedral, and four H atoms are located at the four vertices of C atom, forming four C–H bonds with a length of 1.100 Å; the angle of H–C–H is 109.471°. The average value of the two-layer material is selected as the new unit cell parameter, i.e., a = 11.8702 Å, b = 14.0479 Å. The calculated lattice mismatches in the directions of the a axis and b axis are 4.28% and 3.90% (within 5%), respectively, avoiding the occurrence of serious interface stress and strain, dislocation, stacking fault, fission, and other defects. From the bond length data, after the formation of heterojunctions, Sn–Se bonds show a trend of shortening in the horizontal direction and lengthening in the vertical direction, while the length of the Ge–Se bonds has the opposite trend.

Considering the adsorption of gas molecules at different points on the substrate, after optimizing the structure of the initial model, the one with the smallest adsorption energy (i.e., the largest absolute value) is selected as the most stable structure for this kind of molecule adsorbed on the SnSe surface. Figure (d–f) corresponds to the top and side views of the adsorption structures formed by CH4, C2H4, and C2H6 molecules on the SnSe side. The adsorption energies of the CH4-SnSe/GeSe, C2H4-SnSe/GeSe, and C2H6-SnSe/GeSe systems were −0.355 eV, −0.654 eV, and −0.689 eV, respectively, and the adsorption distances were 2.979 Å, 3.135 Å, and 2.953 Å, indicating that the adsorption of the three gas molecules on the surface of SnSe/GeSe was still weak physical adsorption. (Generally, physical adsorption is defined as adsorption energy less than about −0.5 eV, and CH4 can be clearly classified as physical adsorption. Differential charge densities in Figure (d–f) show that CH4, C2H4, and C2H6 are all electron acceptors.

Density of States Analysis

Figure (a–f) shows the total density of states (TDOS) and partial density of states (PDOS) of H2, CO, C2H2, CH4, C2H4, and C2H6 gas molecules adsorbed on the SnSe side of the heterojunction, respectively. The dotted line represents the Fermi energy level. Figure (a) shows that after adsorption of the H2 molecule, the density of states of the system hardly changes. The PDOS curve shows that there is no peak overlap between the density of states of the H atom and Sn atom, so there is no orbital hybridization between the two atoms, which corresponds to the smaller adsorption energy. The PDOS diagram of CO-SnSe/GeSe shows that the C-2s orbit, C-2p orbit, and Sn-5p orbit have weak orbital hybridization near the −5 eV energy level, and the C-2p orbit and Sn-5p orbit have weak orbital hybridization near 1.3 eV; however, this orbital weak channel hybridization does not cause a huge change in the TDOS. The PDOS diagram in Figure (c) shows that there is no hybridization trend between the H atomic orbitals and the Se atomic orbitals, and the increase in the density of states of the C2H2-SnSe/GeSe system is due to the C2H2 molecule.

TDOS and PDOS of the six adsorbed structures formed on the SnSe side of the SnSe/GeSe heterojunction.

The density of states of the CH4-SnSe/GeSe system increases at the −13 eV and −5 eV levels, which corresponds to the density of states of the H-1s orbital in the PDOS diagram, while there is no orbital hybridization between the H atom and Sn atom.

Figure (e) shows that the density of states of the SnSe/GeSe heterojunction increases continuously from −8 eV to −3 eV after C2H4 molecules are adsorbed, but no obvious orbital hybridization is found in the PDOS diagram. The same phenomenon is also observed in the C2H6-SnSe/GeSe system. The increase of TDOS comes from the C2H6 molecule itself, while there is no obvious orbital hybridization between gas molecules and SnSe/GeSe. In particular, in the six adsorption systems, the density of states near the Fermi level has almost no change, which matches the weak adsorption. It also shows that the conductivity of the system changes weakly, and it is difficult to achieve gas monitoring. PDOS analysis also shows that the interaction between the gas molecules and heterojunction substrate is weak, and there is no new chemical bond in the adsorption process.

Density of States Analysis

The full density of states (TDOS) and partial density of states (PDOS) of the six gas adsorption structures are shown in Figure . In Figure (a), the TDOS curves of SnSe/GeSe and H2 SnSe/GeSe almost coincide, with a slight increase between −7 eV and −5.6 eV, which corresponds to the density of states of the H-1s orbital in the PDOS curve, indicating that this increase is caused by the density of states of the H2 molecule itself. However, the density of states curves of H-1s, Se-4s, and Se-4p orbitals have no peak overlap, which indicates that the interaction between the H2 molecule and SnSe/GeSe substrate is very weak, corresponding to the micro-adsorption energy obtained above. The PDOS curve in Figure (b) shows the density of orbital states of the nearest C atom and Ge atom in the CO SnSe/GeSe adsorption structure. The peaks of the C-2s and C-2p orbitals overlap with Ge-4p orbitals near −5 eV, and the peaks of C-2p orbitals overlap with Ge-4p orbitals near 1.2 eV, indicating the interaction between C protons and Ge atoms. The increase in the density of states in the TDOS diagram corresponds well to the density of states of the C atomic orbitals. In the density of states diagram in Figure (c), the h-1s, se-4s, and se-4p orbitals are weakly hybridized near the −5 eV energy level, which also causes the weak rise of the energy level corresponding to the TDOS curve, while the increase at −14.5 eV, −10 eV and −1 eV comes from the C2H2 molecule itself. From the TDOS curve, the density of states near the Fermi level is almost unchanged before and after the adsorption of the three gas molecules, which makes it difficult to judge the change of conductivity. The resonance of the PDOS curve also shows that the orbital hybridization phenomenon in the adsorption structure is very weak and there is no chemical bond between the gas molecules and the heterojunction substrate. In the CH4 SnSe/GeSe system, the increase of TDOS near the −5 eV energy level corresponds to the density of states of the H-1s orbitals, while the PDOS curve shows that there is no hybridization between the H-1s orbitals and Ge-4s and Ge-4p orbitals and the interaction force between the CH4 molecule and SnSe/GeSe substrate is also very weak. In Figure (e), the c-2p orbital and Ge-4s and Ge-4p orbitals have weak resonances only at the −1.5 eV energy level. Although the TDOS curve changed in many places, this change corresponded well to the density of states of the C-2s and C-2p orbitals. The increase of TDOS came from the density of states of the C2H4 molecule itself. Figure (f) shows that after adsorption of the C2H6 molecule, the TDOS of the system increases continuously from −7 eV to −3 eV, but the PDOS curve shows that there is no orbital hybridization between the H atom and Se atom in the whole energy level range; the interaction between the C2H6 molecule and substrate is also very weak. By observing the density of states near the Fermi level, it can be seen that the conductivity of the three adsorption systems still does not change significantly. At the same time, the above analysis also further shows that there is no new chemical bond between gas molecules and the SnSe/GeSe heterojunction, and this adsorption is weak physical adsorption.

Comparative Analysis of Adsorption Characteristics on Different Surfaces of the SnSe/GeSe Heterojunction

The results showed that the adsorption of gas molecules on the SnSe side was slightly stronger than that on the GeSe side, and the adsorption capacity of six gas molecules was still in the order of C2H6 > C2H4 > C2H2 > CH4 > CO > H2. The charge transfer in the adsorption process is concentrated on the gas molecules, and the six gas molecules are always used as electron acceptors to obtain electrons. Partial density of state analyzes the orbital hybridization and bonding between atoms, but the results show that there is only a weak interaction between gas molecules and the SnSe/GeSe heterojunction and there is no new chemical bond formation in the adsorption process; that is, the adsorption of six gas molecules by the heterojunction is weak physical adsorption. At the same time, the density of states analysis and molecular orbital analysis show that after gas adsorption, the conductivity of the heterojunction changes very weakly, so it is difficult to realize gas monitoring through the change of conductivity.

Conclusion

Based on the first-principles density functional theory (DFT), six kinds of dissolved gases (H2, CO, CH4, C2H2, C2H4, C2H6) in transformer oil were selected as the target gases to study the adsorption characteristics of the SnSe/GeSe heterojunction. The adsorption performance and interaction mechanism between target gas and substrate were analyzed by calculating adsorption energy, charge transfer, density of states, energy band structure, and charge differential density. The results show that the formation of the SnSe/GeSe heterojunction can reach −8.532 eV, indicating that the heterojunction structure is very stable, which provides a good guarantee for the subsequent adsorption process. Compared with the two original monolayers, the band gap width of the SnSe/GeSe heterojunction is reduced, and the conductivity of the system is improved. In the adsorption of the SnSe/GeSe heterojunction on both sides, the adsorption of gas molecules on the SnSe side is slightly stronger than that on the GeSe side, and the adsorption capacity for six gases is in the order of C2H6 > C2H4 > C2H2 > CH4 > CO > H2. The adsorption energy trend is mainly due to the increase of molecular polarizability and size, which enhances the van der Waals dispersion force between gas molecules and the heterojunction surface. The interaction between gas molecules and the SnSe/GeSe heterojunction substrate is weak, and there is no chemical bond formation in the adsorption process. At the same time, the conductivity of the heterojunction does not change significantly before and after gas adsorption, which makes gas monitoring based on the conductivity change challenging. Although the conductivity response is limited, the change of electronic structure caused by adsorption shows the potential of this optical sensing strategy.

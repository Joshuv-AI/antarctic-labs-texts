A Multimodal Latent Severity Axis for Alzheimer’s Disease: Probabilistic PCA, Bayesian Trajectories, and Stage-Aware Timing Effects
Authors: Babak Haji, Amir Abbas Tahami Monfared
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionMultimodal Alzheimer’s disease (AD) cohorts capture cognition, function, neuroimaging, and fluid biomarkers, yet overall disease severity remains difficult to summarize on a single clinically meaningful scale. The apolipoprotein E ε4 (APOE ε4) allele is the strongest common genetic risk factor for late-onset AD, but its association with “progression” has been inconsistent because earlier placement along the disease continuum is often conflated with faster within-stage decline.MethodsUsing data from the Alzheimer’s Disease Neuroimaging Initiative (ADNI), we analyzed an amyloid-positive baseline cohort (N = 1058) and a longitudinal subset (N = 932; ≥ 2 visits and ≥ 4 of 13 measures per visit). Measures included standardized cognitive and functional assessments, structural and functional neuroimaging, cerebrospinal fluid biomarkers of amyloid‑beta and tau pathology, and plasma neurofilament light protein as a marker of neuroaxonal injury. Magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) volumes were adjusted using amyloid-negative cognitively normal controls with quadratic age and intracranial volume terms. Probabilistic principal component analysis (PPCA) was used to derive a latent severity coordinate, defined as the first principal component (PC1). Hierarchical Bayesian random-intercept and random-slope models were used to estimate individual trajectories, partition APOE ε4 effects into baseline severity and within-stage rate, and generate genotype-stratified ages at prespecified severity landmarks. Axis stability was assessed with 100 bootstrap refits, and predictive performance was assessed with participant-level fivefold cross-validation.ResultsThe PC1 explained 38.7% of baseline variance and produced a clinically interpretable multimodal severity axis. Stability was high across bootstrap refits, and residual association with age was minimal after MRI volume adjustment. Higher APOE ε4 dose was associated with greater baseline latent severity, whereas within-stage rate differences were smaller than the baseline severity-position effect. A latent symptomatic landmark was reached approximately 3.0–3.3 years earlier per ε4 allele. Adding APOE improved out-of-sample prediction by about 10% without loss of calibration.ConclusionsProbabilistic principal component analysis provides a stable, multimodal, biologically informed severity axis for longitudinal modeling in amyloid-positive ADNI. Within this framework, APOE ε4 was associated primarily with latent severity position and model-implied timing along the continuum, whereas within-stage rate differences were smaller. These findings support stage-aware longitudinal inference and methodological applications within this cohort, while external clinical calibration and validation remain necessary.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s40120-026-00940-0.Keywords: Alzheimer’s disease, Disease progression, Probabilistic PCA, Bayesian hierarchical model, Latent severity, APOE, ADNI

Introduction

Alzheimer’s disease (AD) is the most common cause of dementia and is characterized biologically by a long preclinical interval during which pathology accumulates before overt cognitive and functional impairment becomes clinically apparent. Neuropathologic staging studies describe ordered regional patterns of amyloid beta deposition and tau pathology, commonly operationalized as established amyloid phase and neurofibrillary stage frameworks [1, 2]. These observations align with biomarker cascade models in which amyloid abnormalities tend to emerge earlier, followed by tau pathology, with neurodegeneration and cognitive decline occurring downstream and tracking more closely with symptomatic impairment [1–5]. This stage structure implies that genetic influences may act strongly when individuals cross symptomatic thresholds while having smaller or more context-dependent effects on short horizon decline once symptoms are established.

Consistent with this biology, AD is increasingly framed as a biologically defined process rather than a purely syndromic endpoint, as formalized in the National Institute on Aging and the Alzheimer's Association (NIA-AA) Research Framework and related biomarker-based approaches [4, 6]. Longitudinal cohorts now measure cognition and function alongside biomarkers derived from positron emission tomography (PET), magnetic resonance imaging (MRI), cerebrospinal fluid (CSF), and blood-based biomarkers, with the Alzheimer’s Disease Neuroimaging Initiative (ADNI) providing a widely used example [7, 8]. Although multimodal data improve biological resolution, they increase dimensionality and missing data challenges, particularly when modalities are partially observed over time, and missingness may correlate with participant characteristics and study phase.

This high-dimensional reality creates a persistent tension between clinical legibility and measurement complexity. Clinical decision-making, trial enrichment, and translational modeling often require a single disease-state variable that is interpretable, continuous, and comparable across analyses, yet the underlying data are heterogeneous, noisy, and incompletely observed. Latent-variable approaches offer a practical compromise between clinical intuition and multimodal measurement reality. Probabilistic principal component analysis (PPCA) represents biomarker vectors as noisy realizations of a low-dimensional latent coordinate system and can accommodate partially observed multimodal profiles [9, 10].

Within this framework, apolipoprotein E (APOE) ε4 remains the strongest common genetic risk factor for late-onset AD. Genetic studies and meta-analyses demonstrate a robust ε4 dose response for incident disease and earlier age at integrated symptomatic burden, and mechanistic and integrative reviews link ε4 to amyloid- and tau-related pathways and downstream neurodegeneration [11–14]. Yet genotype-related effects on the pace of decline after integrated symptomatic burden are often modest, heterogeneous, or inconsistent across cohorts and analytic strategies [14, 15]. This distinction has practical consequences for prognostication, trial enrichment, and modeling because it clarifies whether ε4 shifts symptomatic timing, alters decline rates, or combines both mechanisms.

Several biological and methodological considerations can generate apparent inconsistency in genotype progression associations. Biologically, APOE ε4 plausibly increases the likelihood and earlier timing of amyloid-related processes and tau dysregulation, whereas later symptomatic trajectories may reflect a broader mixture of neurodegeneration, vascular injury, resilience, and comorbid pathologies [3, 13, 14]. Complementary evidence suggests that APOE ε4 can shape the conditions under which tau aggregation and propagation occur, consistent with stage-dependent effects rather than a uniform post onset slope penalty [16, 17]. Methodologically, estimated progression is sensitive to both the observation window and the choice of time origin. Conditioning on survival to enrollment, symptomatic diagnosis, or biomarker positivity can induce selection and collider bias, as discussed in APOE cognitive-decline analyses and the broader epidemiologic literature [15, 18]. These issues are salient in biomarker-defined observational cohorts whose entry criteria are influenced by genotype and related causal pathways [6, 7, 18, 19].

Accordingly, the aims of this study were to derive and validate a multimodal latent severity axis in ADNI participants who were amyloid-positive, to estimate APOE ε4 associations with baseline latent severity and within-stage longitudinal change on that axis, and to translate these associations into model-implied ages at latent severity landmarks. These estimates are intended to support within-cohort inference and methodological development rather than direct causal interpretation or population-level transport. Latent variable methods provide a practical compromise between clinical interpretability and the high-dimensional nature of multimodal measurement. Recent work indicates that PPCA-derived continuum scores support mixed-effects trajectory modeling with clinically sensible longitudinal patterns [10]. Here we leverage a PPCA-derived multimodal severity axis in ADNI participants who were amyloid-positive and embed this axis in hierarchical Bayesian longitudinal models to distinguish genotype-related timing/position effects, reflected in latent placement and model-implied landmark ages, from within-stage rate differences on a shared multimodal scale. We then translate these two components into genotype-stratified ages at latent severity landmarks, propagating uncertainty through the full analytic pipeline, to generate outputs that may be useful for simulation and stage-aware longitudinal modeling [7, 8, 10]. The first contribution is the latent severity axis itself, which is a multimodal, uncertainty-aware coordinate informed by biomarker, cognitive, and functional data that can be examined for interpretability, age-related nuisance structure, and geometric stability under resampling. The second contribution is the use of this coordinate to separate APOE ε4-related timing/position effects from within-stage rate differences on a shared scale. We use age at a latent symptomatic landmark to denote the model-implied age at which an individual reaches a pre-specified severity reference level on an axis integrating cognition, function, and neurodegeneration, thereby translating latent-axis differences into clinically legible timing estimates.

Methods

Data Source and Participants

We used longitudinal data from the ADNI, a multicenter cohort with harmonized clinical, neuroimaging, and biomarker assessments [7]. Data were accessed through the ADNI database (https://adni.loni.usc.edu). Initiated in 2003 as a public–private partnership, ADNI collects standardized measures, including demographics and medical history, neuropsychological testing, clinician-rated global and functional assessments, structural MRI, PET, CSF, and blood-based biomarkers, and APOE genotype, with centralized quality control and public data sharing [7, 8]. Informed consent was obtained from all enrolled participants by ADNI in accordance with its study protocols. The current study does not include any new research conducted by the authors on human participants or animals. The study was performed in accordance with the ethical standards as laid down the 1964 Declaration of Helsinki and its later amendments, or comparable ethical standards.

We restricted all analyses to participants with biomarker-confirmed β-amyloid positivity, determined using PET or CSF measures when available, in order to ensure that the cohort represented individuals on the biologically defined AD continuum [6]. Amyloid positivity was defined using a platform-harmonized dual-threshold approach—the Elecsys β‑Amyloid (1–42) CSF II assay (CSF Aβ 1–42 < 1100 ng/L) (Roche Diagnostics International Ltd, Rotkreuz, Switzerland) or the INNO-BIA AlzBio3 immunoassay (CSF Aβ1–42 < 192 ng/L) (Fujirebio, Ghent, Belgium)—consistent with prior PPCA-based disease-progression modeling in ADNI [10]. Baseline clinical diagnosis, defined as cognitively normal (CN), mild cognitive impairment (MCI), or Alzheimer’s disease (AD) dementia, was used for descriptive purposes only. Disease severity was instead modeled continuously using the PPCA-derived latent severity axis, the first principal component (PC1). The baseline analytic cohort comprised 1058 participants who were amyloid-positive (CN n = 273, MCI n = 527, AD dementia n = 258). Longitudinal modeling included 932 participants who were amyloid-positive with at least two visits and at least four observed measures among the 13 modeled variables per visit. Time was indexed in months since each participant’s baseline visit. The analysis did not require complete multimodal data; rather, latent scores were estimated during partial observation of each participant, and longitudinal inclusion required at least four of the 13 modeled measures per visit.

To limit confounding of the latent severity axis by normative aging and head-size variation, volumetric MRI biomarkers were adjusted for age and intracranial volume (ICV) using participants who were amyloid-negative as the normative reference, consistent with standard neuroimaging practice and prior work [10, 20, 21]. We focused this adjustment on volumetric measures because they showed the strongest correlations with age in this normative group and therefore represent the most likely pathway for residual brain-aging signal to influence PC1.

Multimodal Severity Axis

We derived an integrated multimodal disease-severity score using PPCA, which models standardized biomarker vectors as noisy linear projections of a low-dimensional latent coordinate system [9].

PPCA was selected because it provides an interpretable probabilistic latent-variable formulation that supports participant-level scoring when modality availability varies across visits. Following recent applications of PPCA to AD progression modeling, we used the first latent component (PC1) as a continuous severity axis that integrates cognition, function, neuroimaging, and fluid biomarkers derived under partial observation of the participant [10], and interpreted it as the dominant severity coordinate rather than as an exhaustive representation of all disease-related biological variation. Per-visit PC1 scores were computed as posterior means conditional on the set of observed biomarkers at each visit, allowing participants to contribute data even when some modalities were missing.

Thirteen measures spanning cognitive, functional, imaging, and fluid biomarker domains were modeled jointly. These included: cognition (the Alzheimer's Disease Assessment Scale–Cognitive Subscale 13 items [ADAS-Cog13] and the Rey Auditory Verbal Learning Test [RAVLT]), function (Functional Activities Questionnaire [FAQ]), structural MRI volumes (hippocampus, entorhinal cortex, whole brain, and ventricles), PET measures (fluorodeoxyglucose [FDG]-PET and florbetapir [AV45] amyloid PET), CSF biomarkers (amyloid-beta 1–42 [Aβ1–42], phosphorylated tau, and total tau), and plasma neurofilament light protein. All measures were standardized before PPCA fitting to harmonize scale and variance across modalities. For interpretability, biomarkers that typically decrease with neurodegeneration or cognitive impairment, including MRI volumes and FDG-PET, were sign-reversed so that larger standardized values corresponded to worse disease. Because PPCA components are identifiable only up to sign, the primary latent axis was oriented such that higher scores denote more advanced severity.

Bayesian Hierarchical Modeling and APOE ε4 Specification

We modeled longitudinal change in the latent severity axis (PC1) using Bayesian hierarchical random-intercept and random-slope models, with time indexed in months since each participant’s baseline visit. Participant-specific intercepts and slopes were drawn from a bivariate normal distribution to capture between-individual heterogeneity and the correlation between baseline latent burden and subsequent change. APOE genotype was coded a priori as ε4 allele dose (di = 0, 1, 2) and included as a fixed effect on both the intercept and slope, enabling estimation of genotype-associated differences in baseline PC1 severity and longitudinal change.

Primary analyses used an additive per-allele APOE ε4 specification as the default to preserve statistical power and yield interpretable dose–response contrasts, consistent with the well-established allele-dose associations of APOE ε4 with AD risk and earlier timing of amyloid positivity and integrated symptomatic burden [11–14]. Sensitivity analyses re-parameterized ε4 exposure as a binary carrier indicator and as categorical indicators for 0, 1, or 2 alleles to allow for nonlinearity and potential dominance effects. Stage-stratified analyses were conducted under the primary additive specification to evaluate whether APOE-related associations differed by clinical stage while maintaining comparability across stages and reducing the risk of overfitting in smaller subgroups.

Posterior distributions were estimated using Hamiltonian Monte Carlo sampling [22]. Model comparison relied on fivefold cross-validation to estimate expected out-of-sample predictive performance, summarized via root mean square error (RMSE), posterior predictive R2 [23], 95% credible interval coverage, and credible interval widths [24, 25].

Stage-specific Models and Latent Symptomatic Burden

To assess whether APOE ε4 effects varied by clinical stage, we refit the primary model into two stage-restricted subsets comprising participants who were amyloid-positive and participants with symptomatic MCI or AD dementia. In parallel, we fit a pooled model in the full amyloid-positive cohort, with baseline diagnosis used only for descriptive stratification rather than as the modeled disease state [3, 7].

To translate latent-axis effects into model-implied timing summaries on the latent continuum, we estimated genotype-stratified ages at which participants reach a latent symptomatic landmark on the integrated PC1 axis. In this framework, the latent symptomatic landmark does not refer to the initiation of AD biology; rather, it denotes a model-implied latent severity level at which cognitive and functional symptoms would be expected to be established within this integrated axis [6, 7]. Accordingly, it should be interpreted as a model-based reference point on the latent continuum rather than as an estimate of biologic onset. We implemented a two-step, trajectory-consistent procedure. First, using the symptomatic-only hierarchical model, we estimated genotype-specific symptomatic severity anchors, defined as the expected latent intercept for each ε4 group. Second, we used full-cohort trajectories spanning preclinical and symptomatic observations to translate these anchors into timing. Age at the latent symptomatic landmark was defined as the age at which the mean latent trajectory reached the selected landmark level, with uncertainty propagated through the posterior distribution to derive 95% credible intervals. As a co-primary sensitivity analysis, we also evaluated a genotype-invariant landmark on the latent continuum.

Model Diagnostics and Validation

We evaluated PPCA reproducibility using participant-level bootstrap refits of the baseline PPCA model because genotype-related inference can be sensitive to instability in the underlying severity axis. Stability of loadings and participant score ordering across resamples was used to assess reproducibility of the primary severity axis and its suitability for downstream longitudinal inference [10].

We then assessed model adequacy and comparative performance using complementary measures that distinguish in-sample fit from generalizable predictive behavior. At the parameter level, we quantified the extent to which APOE ε4 reduce unexplained between-individual heterogeneity by estimating variance reduction in the random intercept and slope distributions. We also performed posterior predictive checks to evaluate whether the fitted models reproduced salient features of the observed longitudinal PC1 data, including residual behavior, trajectory dispersion, and calibration of nominal 95% posterior predictive intervals.

To evaluate out-of-sample performance and guard against overfitting, we conducted participant-level fivefold cross-validation, training models on four folds (80% of participants) and generating predictions for the held-out fold (20% of participants) [22]. Predictive performance was summarized using RMSE to assess accuracy, posterior predictive R2 to quantify explained variance in progression trajectories [23], 95% credible interval coverage to evaluate calibration, and credible interval widths to assess prediction precision [24]. In parallel, we compared the baseline hierarchical model against the APOE-extended variant (incorporating genotype effects on intercept and slope) across all folds, showing modest improvement in held-out RMSE without deterioration in calibration or interval widths [22]. Highest density intervals (HDI) were used throughout to provide the narrowest credible intervals containing specified probabilities [25].

The baseline cohort included 1058 ADNI participants who were amyloid-positive at entry and spanned CN, MCI, and AD diagnoses. The longitudinal analytic cohort included 932 participants with at least two visits and at least four observed biomarkers per visit, enabling participant-specific estimation of severity trajectories on the integrated latent axis. As expected in a multi-phase platform, modality-specific missingness was common, reflecting differences in assay availability and protocol evolution across ADNI phases. This pattern of modality availability is relevant to interpretation because PPCA accommodates partial observation but does not eliminate the possibility of informative missingness.

Construction and Validation of the Integrated PPCA Severity Axis

Across the 13-measure multimodal panel, PC1 captured 38.7% of baseline variance, with substantially smaller contributions from the second principal (PC2) and third (PC3) components. The variance explained and the top contributing markers for PC1–PC3 are summarized in Table 3, and the full loading structure is shown in Fig. 2. We interpret PC1 as the dominant multimodal severity coordinate in this cohort, not as an exhaustive summary of all biological variation relevant to AD. The PC1 loading pattern was clinically legible and aligned with an integrated severity construct rather than a single-modality signature. Higher PC1 values corresponded to worse cognition and function and greater neurodegeneration signal, with dominant contributions from ADAS-Cog13 and FAQ together with RAVLT, FDG-PET hypometabolism, and hippocampal atrophy. Thus, higher PC1 values can be interpreted as greater multimodal disease burden with clear clinical face validity.

The secondary components retained interpretable structure that was largely orthogonal to PC1. PC2 concentrated on tau and structural variation, with its strongest contributions arising from CSF tau markers together with ventricular and whole-brain volume. PC3 was dominated by plasma neurofilament light, with additional contribution from whole-brain volume, yielding a dimension strongly influenced by injury-associated signal. Together, PC2 and PC3 capture biological variation among participants with similar PC1 values without forcing those differences onto the primary severity axis.

Bootstrap refits further demonstrated high stability of the PPCA-derived severity axis, supporting its use as the substrate for downstream longitudinal and genotype analyses. Across 100 bootstrap refits, the latent geometry was highly stable. PC1 loadings and participant scores were near-invariant relative to the reference solution, with mean loading correlation 0.997 ± 0.002 and mean score correlation 0.999 ± 0.001, indicating minimal sensitivity to resampling variability. The proportion of variance explained by PC1 also remained consistent across refits (0.387 ± 0.017), supporting the robustness and dominance of the primary component. Together, these findings justify PC1 as a reproducible severity coordinate in the amyloid-positive cohort and a suitable harmonized axis for downstream longitudinal modeling and inference.

Embedding PC1 in a Bayesian random-intercept/random-slope trajectory model yielded coherent participant-specific trajectories with uncertainty propagated to individual intercepts and slopes. These findings support the model’s use for longitudinal inference and uncertainty quantification on the latent severity axis, while indicating that individualized forecasting remains limited.

APOE ε4 Effects on Severity Position, Within-Stage Change, and Timing

Within this validated latent framework, APOE-stratified empirical trajectories indicated that genotype groups differed mainly in where they fell along a shared severity continuum rather than exhibiting qualitatively distinct trajectory patterns.

In the full cohort of participants with amyloid-positive AD, APOE ε4 dose was associated with both higher baseline latent severity and slightly faster longitudinal worsening, with dose–response separation in posterior mean intercepts and slopes. Because the full-cohort model blends cognitively normal and symptomatic phases, we refit the trajectory model within CN-only and symptomatic strata to distinguish within-stage rate effects from stage mixing. This decomposition showed that the dominant and most stable genotype signal was a shift in latent disease burden rather than large within-stage slope differences. As a co-primary sensitivity analysis, we estimated ages at a single shared latent symptomatic landmark on the PC1 continuum (PC1 = 0.22) to hold the landmark constant across genotypes. Under this genotype-invariant specification, the implied landmark ages were 75.6 years for noncarriers, 72.0 years for heterozygotes, and 67.2 years for homozygotes, similar in direction and magnitude to the genotype-stratified landmark analysis and supportive of the interpretation that the dominant ε4 signal is a timing and severity-position shift on the integrated continuum.

At the parameter level, inclusion of APOE ε4 reduced unexplained between-individual heterogeneity in full-cohort fits, explaining a modest fraction of random-effects variance (intercept: 6%, 95% HDI 3–15%; slope: 7%, 95% HDI − 12–25%). Fivefold participant-level cross-validation confirmed robust out-of-sample performance and absence of overfitting for both the baseline hierarchical model and the APOE-extended variant. The addition of APOE improved out-of-sample prediction by approximately 10% (RMSE 2.71–2.45) without evidence of degraded posterior calibration. Posterior predictive R2 remained near zero, and at times negative, consistent with the intrinsic difficulty of forecasting individual trajectories in heterogeneous observational data; nevertheless, APOE ε4 improved held-out performance while maintaining well-calibrated uncertainty. Calibration remained excellent, with 95% credible-interval coverage near nominal levels for both models, and interval widths showed high stability. These metrics support calibrated uncertainty quantification and modest incremental forecasting improvement, rather than high-accuracy individual prediction. These results indicate a modest incremental predictive value of APOE ε4 for group-level longitudinal modeling, but not high-accuracy individual prognosis.

Discussion

Our results support a within-cohort interpretation of multimodal AD progression in amyloid-positive ADNI: PPCA defines a stable, clinically legible latent severity axis, and APOE ε4 provides a particularly informative application showing that genetic effects can be decomposed into earlier placement on that axis versus within-stage rate of worsening. The integrated symptomatic-burden framework clarifies why genotype-related “progression” findings have often appeared inconsistent across studies. When disease burden is modeled on a common multimodal continuum, APOE ε4 is associated with earlier placement and higher latent severity on the continuum, while within-stage slope differences are present but comparatively modest.

The broader point is that a latent severity axis is most useful when it supports interpretable multimodal inference rather than merely data compression. In the present study, the strongest evidence for validity did not come from the simple separation of diagnostic groups, which can arise mechanically when cognition and function are included among the inputs. Instead, it came from three empirical properties, including a coherent cross-domain loading structure, attenuation of age-related nuisance signal after MRI adjustment, and near-invariant geometry under bootstrap refitting. Together, these findings support PC1 as a reproducible severity coordinate rather than a sample-specific artifact [9, 10, 20, 21].

From an epidemiologic perspective, the timing-versus-rate distinction helps reconcile heterogeneous prior reports on APOE genotype and decline. A genotype can appear to have a “progression effect” if carriers enter observation later along the disease continuum, even when their within-stage slope differs only modestly from that of noncarriers. Analyses restricted to symptomatic samples are especially vulnerable to this ambiguity because conditioning on study entry after symptom emergence can compress between-genotype differences into baseline severity rather than observed slope. Relatedly, conditioning on diagnosis, biomarker positivity, or survival to enrollment can introduce selection and collider structures that distort genotype-progression associations [15, 18, 26]. Our framework does not eliminate those cohort-level constraints, but it reduces the additional distortion created by relying on diagnosis strata alone and makes the main APOE signal more interpretable.

Mechanistically, APOE ε4 has been linked to downstream tau pathology and neurodegeneration through pathways that extend beyond amyloid deposition alone. Longitudinal imaging work suggests that ε4 is associated with greater tau burden and more extensive tau spread at lower amyloid levels, consistent with a role for ε4 in modulating tau propagation across connected regions [16]. Complementary evidence proposes that ε4 lowers the phosphorylated (p)-tau217 threshold for tau aggregation and spread in an allele-dose-dependent manner [17]. Our stage-stratified findings are directionally consistent with these models, although the present analysis does not identify mechanistic pathway parameters. APOE ε4 appears to shift when individuals reach a higher-burden region of the continuum that is plausibly linked to tau and neurodegeneration, rather than producing a large, uniform acceleration of decline across all symptomatic follow-up. At the same time, our latent axis is a statistical severity coordinate that integrates multiple modalities and is not a direct measure of tau kinetics or causal pathway parameters. The mechanistic interpretation should therefore be regarded as biologically plausible and externally supported, rather than directly established by the present analysis.

Methodologically, our approach separates two layers of inference that are often conflated. The first is construction and validation of a multimodal, biologically informed severity coordinate. The second is estimation of genotype effects on longitudinal behavior along that coordinate. PPCA provides a probabilistic formulation of PCA that is well suited to noisy and partially observed multivariate biomarker profiles [9]. Embedding PC1 in a hierarchical random-intercept and random-slope model then enables partial pooling and explicit uncertainty propagation to individual intercepts and slopes, which is particularly relevant in ADNI where follow-up intensity and modality availability vary across participants. Cross-validation shows that APOE ε4 adds reproducible but modest predictive information, improving prediction without sacrificing calibration, while also demonstrating that most heterogeneity remains unexplained within genotype groups. That is an important negative result as well as a positive one: genotype matters, but it is not close to sufficient for individualized prognosis.

In clinical terms, age at integrated symptomatic burden translates latent-axis differences into an interpretable neurologic concept. It represents the model-implied age at which an individual reaches a sustained level of combined cognitive and functional impairment with accompanying neurodegeneration signal. The approximately 3-year shift per ε4 allele provides an interpretable model-based summary on the latent continuum that may be relevant to stage-aware risk stratification and enrichment strategy, pending external clinical calibration. Future work should map PC1 to external anchors such as CDR-SB, clinically meaningful functional thresholds, or time to diagnosis, and then test transportability of those mappings across cohorts [27].

The purpose of this study is twofold: to establish a multimodal, biomarker-anchored severity axis with empirical validation, and to show how that axis can be used to express APOE ε4 effects as differences in severity position, within-stage rate, and model-implied ages at latent symptomatic burden. That combination is more informative than either contribution alone.

Several limitations merit consideration. ADNI is designed as a research cohort rather than a population-representative observational sample, and modality availability reflects both protocol and participant factors [19]. Because modality availability and follow-up intensity are not random in ADNI, PPCA provides a coherent latent representation under partial observation but does not eliminate the possibility of informative missingness; effect sizes should therefore be interpreted as conditional on the observed measurement process. External validation and clinical calibration are essential before transport to other cohorts or clinical settings with different recruitment, measurement, or clinical practice patterns [28]. PPCA is a linear-Gaussian model that approximates nonlinear biology by the nearest linear manifold [29]. This supports interpretability but may miss curvature and phase transitions. Our trajectory models also use an affine random-slope specification, which cannot fully represent nonlinear acceleration or plateauing that may occur across longer horizons or denser sampling. Finally, while age at integrated symptomatic burden provides a clinically legible timing summary, it is still derived from an integrated latent axis and should be interpreted as a calibrated summary of accumulated burden rather than as a direct clinical endpoint.

Conclusion

Multimodal PPCA defines a reproducible and clinically legible latent severity axis in ADNI participants who were amyloid-positive, integrating cognition, function, neuroimaging, and fluid biomarkers into a single uncertainty-aware continuum. Within this framework, APOE ε4 was associated primarily with latent severity position and model-implied timing along the continuum, whereas within-stage rate differences were smaller. These findings support stage-aware longitudinal inference and methodological applications within this cohort, while external validation and clinical calibration remain necessary.

Comparative Evaluation of Craniofacial Growth Patterns Using AI-Driven Longitudinal Cephalometric Superimpositions in Adolescents
Authors: Mohammad K. Alam, Mohammad Y. Hajeer, Renad N. Abahussain, Reuof A. Algadri, Dalia M. Alamri, Mohammed E. Rashid
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Background:Accurate analysis of craniofacial growth is essential for individualized orthodontic treatment planning. Traditional cephalometric methods often rely on manual tracing, which can be time-consuming and subject to interobserver variability. Recent advances in artificial intelligence (AI) offer promising tools for precise and reproducible assessment of growth changes.Methods:A longitudinal cohort of 90 adolescents (aged between 11 and 16 years) was analyzed, comprising 30 each from horizontal, vertical, and average growth pattern groups, determined by initial cephalometric assessment. AI algorithms were used to superimpose lateral cephalograms taken at baseline and after 18 months. Linear and angular measurements, including S-N, ANB, mandibular length (Co-Gn), and lower anterior facial height (ANS-Me), were computed. Statistical analysis involved ANOVA and post hoc Tukey’s test (P < 0.05 considered significant).Results:The AI-based analysis revealed significant differences in mandibular length increase among the groups (horizontal: 5.2 ± 1.3 mm, vertical: 3.1 ± 1.2 mm, average: 4.1 ± 1.1 mm; P = 0.003). Vertical growers exhibited the highest increase in lower anterior facial height (6.7 ± 1.5 mm), compared to horizontal (3.5 ± 1.0 mm) and average growers (4.2 ± 1.2 mm) (P < 0.001). The AI superimpositions demonstrated high reproducibility (intra-class correlation > 0.94).Conclusion:AI-driven cephalometric superimposition provides a reliable and efficient method for assessing craniofacial growth in adolescents. Vertical growers show greater vertical skeletal development, while horizontal growers exhibit more prominent mandibular lengthening. AI integration into routine cephalometric evaluation can significantly enhance diagnostic accuracy and growth prediction.

INTRODUCTION

Craniofacial growth assessment plays a pivotal role in orthodontic diagnosis and treatment planning, particularly during adolescence when substantial skeletal changes occur.[1] Traditionally, longitudinal cephalometric superimpositions have been employed to track these changes, but manual tracing is prone to errors due to landmark identification variability and human fatigue.[2]

Recent developments in artificial intelligence (AI), particularly in deep learning and image recognition, have enabled the automation of cephalometric landmark identification with improved precision and consistency.[3] AI-based cephalometric tools have demonstrated accuracy comparable to expert orthodontists, with significant time-saving benefits.[4] In addition, longitudinal superimposition using AI facilitates reproducible comparison of skeletal changes over time.[5]

Several studies have explored AI for static cephalometric evaluations,[67] yet limited research exists on its application in longitudinal growth assessment. The transition from manual to AI-driven analysis offers the potential for reducing operator-dependent bias and enhancing diagnostic consistency, especially in determining distinct growth patterns (vertical, horizontal, average).

This study addresses the gap by comparing craniofacial growth changes in adolescents classified into three growth pattern groups using AI-driven cephalometric superimpositions over an 18-month period.

Aim

To evaluate and compare craniofacial growth patterns in adolescents using AI-assisted longitudinal cephalometric superimpositions, and to validate its clinical applicability in differentiating vertical, horizontal, and average growth trajectories.

Statistical analysis

Data were analyzed using SPSS v26.0. Descriptive statistics were calculated as mean ± standard deviation (SD). Intragroup comparisons between T0 and T1 were made using paired t-tests. Intergroup comparisons were analyzed using one-way ANOVA followed by Tukey’s HSD post hoc test. Reproducibility was evaluated using intra-class correlation coefficients (ICC). A P-value <0.05 was considered statistically significant.

DISCUSSION

This study demonstrated the effectiveness of AI-driven cephalometric superimpositions in evaluating adolescent craniofacial growth. Horizontal growers exhibited significantly greater mandibular lengthening compared to vertical growers, while vertical growers showed substantial increases in anterior facial height.

These findings align with traditional cephalometric growth analyses, where horizontal growers typically manifest forward mandibular displacement and vertical growers show downward rotation and increased vertical dimensions.[8] However, the use of AI introduced greater consistency and efficiency in landmark identification, reducing interobserver variation—a concern highlighted in earlier studies using manual methods.[910]

Our results are comparable to observations by Yu et al.,[11] who applied AI for landmark detection and found reduced errors compared to manual tracings. Similarly, Choi et al.[12] demonstrated that AI-assisted superimpositions could predict growth patterns with greater diagnostic value than conventional techniques.

The significant difference in lower anterior facial height among vertical growers emphasizes the need for early intervention in these patients to control vertical dimension and prevent open bite tendencies.[1314] The unchanged ANB angle across groups indicates that sagittal skeletal relationships remained stable, likely due to balanced maxillary and mandibular growth.

Reproducibility, as assessed by intra-class correlation coefficients, confirms the reliability of the AI platform used. The high ICC values are consistent with the findings of Kunz et al.,[15] who reported comparable accuracy between AI and expert orthodontists in cephalometric analysis.

One limitation of this study is the 18-month follow-up period, which may not capture the complete growth trajectory. Future longitudinal studies with extended follow-up and integration of 3D imaging could provide deeper insights.

CONCLUSION

AI-based longitudinal cephalometric superimpositions offer a robust, reproducible, and efficient alternative to manual methods for evaluating craniofacial growth in adolescents. Horizontal growers demonstrated greater mandibular advancement, whereas vertical growers exhibited increased lower facial height. The integration of AI into routine orthodontic diagnostics can enhance the precision of growth monitoring and improve individualized treatment planning.

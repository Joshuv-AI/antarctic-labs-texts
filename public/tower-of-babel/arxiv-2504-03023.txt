Mixing Estimates for Passive Scalar Transport by B ​ V BV Vector Fields
Lucas Huysmans, Ayman Rimah Said

Abstract
We prove a quantitative mixing estimate for the Cauchy problem for transport along divergence-free vector fields with bounded variation. By developing a framework that quantifies Ambrosio’s regularisation scheme, we derive the first explicit bounds on the mixing rate for general B ​ V BV vector fields. Our analysis reveals that tetration (repeated exponentiation) emerges in the mixing rate from the local nature of Ambrosio’s regularisation.

1
Introduction

In this paper, we revisit the Cauchy problem for transport along a divergence-free vector field with bounded variation. It is a remarkable result, due to Ambrosio
[
3
]
, that these assumptions on the vector field are sufficient for well-posedness. A natural next step is to control the permitted growth of solutions. Since transport conserves the mass of solutions, growth should be measured in the weak topology, e.g., in negative Sobolev norms. Such estimates are more commonly known as mixing estimates since they bound the transfer of mass from high to low frequencies, i.e. how quickly the passive scalar (un)mixes.

Such mixing estimates are well-studied for Sobolev vector fields in
W^{1,p}
with
p>1
. However, when
p=1
, or more generally for
BV
vector fields, there is a significant gap in applying the same harmonic arguments, and no general mixing bounds are known. This problem is further related to an unsolved conjecture by Bressan
[
6
]
on the maximal mixing rate for
W^{1,1}
vector fields.

In this paper, we develop a novel framework to bridge this gap. By quantifying Ambrosio’s well-posedness proof with new harmonic estimates in
BV
and employing a combinatorial argument with the pigeonhole principle to make these estimates small, we give the first quantitative mixing estimates for passive scalar transport by
BV
vector fields.

More precisely, consider a weak solution
\rho(x,t):\mathbb{T}^{d}\times[0,T]\to\mathbb{R}
on the
d
-dimension torus
\mathbb{T}^{d}
, with
\rho(x,t)\in L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{\infty}
, to the transport equation

\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial t}(x,t)+\nabla\cdot(u(x)\rho(x,t))=0,

(1)

along a divergence-free, time-independent vector field
u(x):\mathbb{T}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
, with
u(x)\in BV
. Denote by
\rho_{0}(x)\in L^{\infty}
the initial datum, and also by
\rho_{T}(x)\in L^{\infty}
the weak trace of
\rho(x,t)
at time
t=T
(see for instance
[
2
]
).

Our primary result is a well-posedness/weak stability estimate which quantifies the continuous dependence of the solution on the initial datum in the negative Sobolev space
W^{-1,1}
:

Theorem A
.

Let
\kappa>0
. For constants
A,B>0
depending only on the dimension
d\geq 2
, if

\left\|\rho_{0}\right\|_{W^{-1,1}}\leq\kappa\left(\exp^{\left\lceil A\exp\left(\frac{B}{\kappa}\left\|\rho_{0}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}T\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L^{1}}\right)\right\rceil}\left(\frac{1}{\kappa}\left\|\rho_{0}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\right)\right)^{-1},

where
\exp^{n}(x)
refers to repeated exponentiation
\underbrace{\exp(\exp(\dots(\exp(x))))}_{n}
(tetration), then

\left\|\rho_{T}\right\|_{W^{-1,1}}\leq\kappa.

By reversing time, one may rephrase Theorem
A
as a more classical bound on mixing, that is, as a
lower
bound on the weak norm
\left\|\rho_{T}\right\|_{W^{-1,1}}
:

Corollary B

(Mixing bound)
.

For constants
A,B>0
depending only on the dimension
d\geq 2
, then

\left\|\rho_{T}\right\|_{W^{-1,1}}\geq\left\|\rho_{0}\right\|_{W^{-1,1}}\left(\exp^{\left\lceil A\exp\left(B\frac{\left\|\rho_{0}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}}{\left\|\rho_{0}\right\|_{W^{-1,1}}}T\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L^{1}}\right)\right\rceil}\left(\frac{\left\|\rho_{0}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}}{\left\|\rho_{0}\right\|_{W^{-1,1}}}\right)\right)^{-1},

where
\exp^{n}(x)
refers to repeated exponentiation
\underbrace{\exp(\exp(\dots(\exp(x))))}_{n}
(tetration).

The tetration in the bound comes from a pigeonhole principle argument and is related to the
local
in space nature of Ambrosio’s regularisation scheme, see Remark
2.8
.

In contrast, when the vector field lies in the Sobolev space
W^{1,p}
with
p>1
, one can give exponential mixing bounds by more classical harmonic techniques, see for instance
[
8
,
16
,
14
,
15
,
11
,
12
]
. We also mention that in the
BV
case, one has exponential bounds when restricting the vector field to alternating shears on
\mathbb{T}^{2}

[
7
]
, or to cellular
90
degree rotations
[
11
]
.

Other work on transport by
BV
vector fields includes the extension of the well-posedness class to so-called "nearly-incompressible" vector fields
[
4
]
, which allows the vector field to have unbounded divergence, and a partial extension to vector fields with bounded deformation
[
1
]
, for which only the symmetric part of the gradient has bounded variation. There is also the work
[
5
]
, which gives a notion of differentiability for the flow map associated with a
BV
vector field under the same assumptions as in
[
4
]
.

Throughout this paper, we assume that the vector field is divergence-free and time-independent to help illustrate the methods while simplifying the presentation. The complete calculations for a time-dependent vector field can be found in the PhD thesis
[
13
]
.

1.1
Motivation

The goal is to give
uniform
estimates for the well-posedness/continuous dependence on initial data for transport along
BV
vector fields. We first observe that the uniqueness of solutions (due to Ambrosio
[
3
]
, and re-proven in Corollary
2.4
) can be combined with weak compactness of the unit ball in
BV
to give such a stability estimate, the proof of which is contained in the appendix:

Proposition 1.2
.

For any
\epsilon>0
, there exists
\delta>0
depending only on
\epsilon>0
,
\left\|\rho_{0}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}
,
T\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L^{1}}
, and the dimension
d\geq 2
such that:

If

\left\|\rho_{0}\right\|_{W^{-1,1}}\leq\delta,

then

\left\|\rho_{T}\right\|_{W^{-1,1}}\leq\epsilon.

Indeed, Theorem
A
is precisely a quantification of this proposition. It is essential in the proof of the above that uniqueness holds when
u(x)\in BV
, rather than the more standard result when
u(x)\in W^{1,1}
by DiPerna-Lions
[
9
]
; the same compactness argument would fail in
W^{1,1}
since the unit ball in
W^{1,1}
is not weakly compact. It is key, therefore, to exploit well-posedness in
BV
, for which there are only two known proofs
[
3
,
4
]
.

Therefore, our starting point is to quantify the well-posedness proof of Ambrosio
[
3
]
. In Subsection
2.1
, we quantify Ambrosio’s local regularisation scheme
[
3
]
(see also the lecture notes
[
2
]
) to give a quantitative proof of well-posedness for transport by
BV
vector fields in terms of new harmonic estimates in
BV
. In Subsection
2.3
we then show how continuous dependence of our bounds on the vector field and passive scalar in the weak-
*
topologies in
BV
and
L^{\infty}
gives uniform estimates of the kind in Proposition
1.2
for an
open cover
of the vector field and passive scalar in
BV
and
L^{\infty}
. Finally, in Subsection
2.6
, we apply the pigeonhole principle to give
a finite sub-cover, and hence a single uniform bound of the form in Proposition
1.2
, which quantifies the dependence of
\delta>0
on
\epsilon>0
,
\left\|\rho_{0}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}
, and
T\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L^{1}}
. For the detailed proofs, see Section
3
.

2
Outline of the proof

Let
\rho(x,t)\in L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{\infty}
be a solution to (
1
). We wish to control the weak norm:

\left\|\rho_{T}\right\|_{W^{-1,1}}=\sup_{\left\|\phi_{T}\right\|_{W^{1,\infty}}\leq 1}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\rho_{T}(x)\phi_{T}(x)\;dx.

(2)

Throughout, we fix some test function
\phi_{T}(x)\in W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{d};\mathbb{R})
, with
\left\|\phi_{T}\right\|_{W^{1,\infty}}\leq 1
. We transport this backwards; let
\phi(x,t)\in L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{\infty}
be (any) weak solution to the following
backwards
transport equation:

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial t}(x,t)+\nabla\cdot(u(x)\phi(x,t))=0,

(3)

\displaystyle\phi(x,T)=\phi_{T}(x),

with the bound

\left\|\phi\right\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{\infty}}\leq\left\|\phi_{T}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq 1.

2.1
Quantifying the Ambrosio regularisation

In the proof of uniqueness by Ambrosio (see
[
3
]
, and also the lecture notes
[
2
]
), one regularises the solution
\rho(x,t)
, so that one may apply the classical techniques for smooth solutions. One then deduces, as for smooth solutions, that the zero initial datum can only correspond to the zero solution. Careful quantification of this argument should instead translate smallness constraints on the initial datum to smallness constraints on the final datum.

To this end, we find it is easier to work with an adapted version of the proof of Ambrosio, wherein we show instead that the product
\rho(x,t)\phi(x,t)
is a solution to the transport equation, and so conserves mass. In particular, if

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\rho_{0}(x)\phi(x,0)\;dx=0,

(4)

then

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\rho_{T}(x)\phi(x,T)\;dx=0,

(5)

which controls exactly the weak norm (
2
). To prove this, we now have a choice. To apply the derivative to the product
\rho(x,t)\phi(x,t)
, we need only one of the functions to be smooth. That is, we have a choice to either regularise the solution
\rho(x,t)
(as in
[
3
]
) or the solution
\phi(x,t)
. We choose the latter because, once regularised, the condition on the initial datum (
4
) now becomes an integral of
\rho_{0}(x)
against a
smooth
function, which one can more easily quantify by smallness in
W^{-1,1}
.

Referring to Section
3
for the exact expressions and computations, our quantitative regularisation splits the integral (
5
) into five terms,

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\rho_{T}(x)\phi(x,T)\;dx=I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3}+I_{4}+I_{5},

each of which we control explicitly. The regularisation itself contains two key ingredients: mollification by an anisotropic mollifier (the Alberti mollifier in
[
2
]
) designed to cancel the transport structure and localisation since the Alberti mollifier depends on space.

To be precise, we fix a standard mollifier
\varphi(h)\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R})
satisfying certain technical assumptions (we refer to equation (
10
) in Section
3
). The following three parameters then define the regularisation scheme:

•

\Lambda>0
controls the anisotropic structure of the Alberti mollifiers in
[
2
]
, see (
15
);

•

\delta>0
controls the radius of the Alberti mollifiers, see (
16
);

•

\epsilon>0
is the localisation parameter, namely the radius of the spatial ball on which to apply each Alberti mollifier, see (
25
).

Once we fix these parameters, the five terms arise as follows:

•

I_{1}
(
26
) is the error from regularising
\phi(x,t)
in the integral (
5
), see (
25
), and vanishes as
\delta\to 0
;

•

I_{2}
(
28
) is the regularised integral (
4
) after transporting the regularised product
\rho(x,t)\phi(x,t)
, see (
27
), and vanishes as
\left\|\rho_{0}\right\|_{W^{-1,1}}\to 0
.

The remaining terms now come from the transport of
\phi(x,t)\rho(x,t)
, and due to the regularisation, we are left with three errors;

•

I_{3}
(
31
) is the transport error from the localisation and vanishes as
\delta\to 0
for fixed
\epsilon>0
;

•

I_{4}+I_{5}
(
34
) is the DiPerna-Lions-style transport error (see also
[
9
]
) from the mollification of
\phi(x,t)
. We split this final error into two terms. The first,
I_{4}
(
35
), vanishing by localisation (
\epsilon\to 0
). The second,
I_{5}
(
36
), vanishes by the structure of the Alberti mollifier (
21
) (
\Lambda\to\infty
).

These five terms then have the following explicit bounds in terms of the solution
\phi(x,t)
in (
3
):

Proposition 2.2
.

For fixed
\Lambda,\delta,\epsilon>0
with
\delta\leq\epsilon e^{-\Lambda}
:

\displaystyle|I_{1}|\lesssim\delta e^{(d+1)\Lambda}\left\|\rho\right\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{\infty}},

\displaystyle|I_{2}|\lesssim\bigg(1+\frac{e^{\Lambda}}{\delta}+\frac{1}{\epsilon}\bigg)\left\|\rho_{0}\right\|_{W^{-1,1}},

\displaystyle|I_{3}|\lesssim T^{\frac{d-1}{d}}\frac{e^{d\Lambda}}{\epsilon}\left\|\rho\right\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{\infty}}\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L^{1}}\left(\left\|\phi\right\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{2}}^{2}-\left\|\phi*\varphi_{2\delta e^{\Lambda}}\right\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{2}}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{d}},

\displaystyle|I_{4}|\lesssim Te^{2\Lambda}\left\|\rho\right\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{\infty}}\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L^{1}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L^{1}}-\left\|\nabla u*\varphi_{4\epsilon}\right\|_{L^{1}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},

\displaystyle|I_{5}|\lesssim\frac{T}{\Lambda}\left\|\rho\right\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{\infty}}\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L^{1}},

where
\lesssim
denotes less than or equal to up to a positive constant depending only on the dimension
d\geq 2
and the choice of mollifier
\varphi(h)\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R})
satisfying (
10
).

2.3
Harmonic estimates in
BV

The novelty here lies in the bound on
I_{4}
. Namely the high-frequency control on
u(x)\in BV
through the difference of norms

\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L^{1}}-\left\|\nabla u*\varphi_{4\epsilon}\right\|_{L^{1}},

(6)

which vanishes as
\epsilon\to 0
for
u(x)\in BV
, see for instance
[
10
]
. In contrast, the more standard high-frequency control
\left\|\nabla u-\nabla u*\varphi_{\epsilon}\right\|_{L^{1}}
appearing in the DiPerna-Lions well-posedness theory
[
9
]
vanishes only for
u(x)\in W^{1,1}
. Therefore, the above difference of norms is a much weaker harmonic estimate. It emerges by approximating only the
unit
part of the gradient
\frac{\nabla u(x)}{|\nabla u(x)|}
in Ambrosio’s approximation scheme, see (
35
), instead of the full gradient
\nabla u(x)
as is necessary for the DiPerna-Lions theory.

In particular, Proposition
2.2
can be viewed as a quantification of the stability estimate Proposition
1.2
for fixed
\rho(x,t)\in L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{\infty}
and
u(x)\in BV
; one may take
\Lambda>0
large,
\epsilon>0
small, and
\delta>0
small (and so
\left\|\rho_{0}\right\|_{W^{-1,1}}
small) to make all five terms in Proposition
2.2
vanish. As shown in the appendix, this is enough to reprove the well-posedness of transport by
BV
vector fields, originally due to Ambrosio
[
3
]
:

Corollary 2.4
.

If
\rho_{0}(x)=0
is the zero initial datum, then
\rho(x,t)=0
is the zero solution.

The aim is to quantify Proposition
1.2

uniformly
; we should choose
\Lambda,\epsilon,\delta>0
to make all the terms in Proposition
2.2
small depending only on the norms
\left\|\rho\right\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{\infty}}
, and
T\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L^{1}}
. To achieve this, the bounds on
I_{3},I_{4}
in terms of the harmonic estimates on
\phi(x,t)\in L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{2}
and
\nabla u(x)\in BV
, are insufficient.

Consider for instance the harmonic estimate on
\phi(x,t)
in the bound on
I_{3}
:

\left\|\phi\right\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{2}}^{2}-\left\|\phi*\varphi_{2\delta e^{\Lambda}}\right\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{2}}^{2}.

(7)

In terms of the Fourier series for
\phi(\cdot,t):\mathbb{T}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
, this harmonic estimate roughly corresponds to the
L_{t}^{2}L_{\omega}^{2}
-norm in frequency-space (
\omega\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
) of the frequencies of
\phi(\cdot,t)
above the threshold
|\omega|\geq\frac{1}{2\delta e^{\Lambda}}
. The difficulty here is that one has no a-priori knowledge of how high (in frequency-space) the frequencies of
\phi(\cdot,t)
are located.

To this end, we revisit the terms
I_{3}
(
31
) and
I_{4}+I_{5}
(
34
). We notice that these expressions are continuous in the weak-
*
topologies of
\phi(x,t)\in L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{2}
and
u(x)\in BV
, and so we look for new bounds that respect this continuity. To do so, we perform an (even higher) frequency cutoff on
\phi(x,t)\in L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{2}
and
u(x)\in BV
to extract a remainder term which does not depend on these even higher frequencies. Splitting them into two parts, a
finite
frequency part and an ‘even higher’ frequency remainder, we have:

\displaystyle I_{3}=I_{3}^{\prime}+R_{1},

\displaystyle I_{4}+I_{5}=I_{4}^{\prime}+I_{5}^{\prime}+R_{2},

where

•

I_{3}^{\prime}
is bounded by the
finite
range of frequencies

\left\|\phi*\varphi_{\delta^{\prime}}\right\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{2}}^{2}-\left\|\phi*\varphi_{\delta^{\prime}}*\varphi_{2\delta e^{\Lambda}}\right\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{2}}^{2},

for a free parameter
\delta^{\prime}>0
which defines a second/higher frequency cutoff
(\phi*\varphi_{\delta^{\prime}})(x,t)
.

•

I_{4}^{\prime}
is analogously bounded by the
BV
harmonic estimate

\left\|\nabla u*\varphi_{\epsilon^{\prime}}\right\|_{L^{1}}-\left\|\nabla u*\varphi_{\epsilon^{\prime}}*\varphi_{4\epsilon}\right\|_{L^{1}},

for a free parameter
\epsilon^{\prime}>0
which defines the ‘even higher’ frequency cutoff
(\nabla u*\varphi_{\epsilon^{\prime}})(x,t)
.

•

I_{5}^{\prime}
maintains the same bound as
I_{5}
.

•

The ‘higher’ frequency remainders
R_{1},R_{2}
are small when
\delta^{\prime},\epsilon^{\prime}>0
are small, independent of the ‘even higher’ frequencies of
\phi(x,t)
and
\nabla u(x)
:

Proposition 2.5
.

For fixed
\Lambda,\delta,\delta^{\prime},\epsilon,\epsilon^{\prime}>0
with
\delta\leq\epsilon e^{-\Lambda}
, and a mollifier
\varphi(h)\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R})
satisfying the assumptions (
10
):

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\rho_{T}(x)\phi_{T}(x)\;dx=I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3}^{\prime}+I_{4}^{\prime}+I_{5}^{\prime}+R_{1}+R_{2},

with the bounds:

\displaystyle|I_{1}|\lesssim\delta e^{(d+1)\Lambda}\left\|\rho\right\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{\infty}},

\displaystyle|I_{2}|\lesssim\bigg(1+\frac{e^{\Lambda}}{\delta}+\frac{1}{\epsilon}\bigg)\left\|\rho_{0}\right\|_{W^{-1,1}},

\displaystyle|I_{3}^{\prime}|\lesssim T^{\frac{d-1}{d}}\frac{e^{d\Lambda}}{\epsilon}\left\|\rho\right\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{\infty}}\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L^{1}}\left(\left\|\phi*\varphi_{\delta^{\prime}}\right\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{2}}^{2}-\left\|\phi*\varphi_{\delta^{\prime}}*\varphi_{2\delta e^{\Lambda}}\right\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{2}}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{d}},

\displaystyle|I_{4}^{\prime}|\lesssim Te^{2\Lambda}\left\|\rho\right\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{\infty}}\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L^{1}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\left\|\nabla u*\varphi_{\epsilon^{\prime}}\right\|_{L^{1}}-\left\|\nabla u*\varphi_{\epsilon^{\prime}}*\varphi_{4\epsilon}\right\|_{L^{1}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},

\displaystyle|I_{5}^{\prime}|\lesssim\frac{T}{\Lambda}\left\|\rho\right\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{\infty}}\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L^{1}},

\displaystyle|R_{1}|\lesssim T\frac{\delta^{\prime}e^{(d+1)\Lambda}}{\epsilon\delta}\left\|\rho\right\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{\infty}}\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L^{1}},

\displaystyle|R_{2}|\lesssim T\frac{\epsilon^{\prime}e^{(d+1)\Lambda}}{\delta}\left\|\rho\right\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{\infty}}\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L^{1}}.

The problematic terms are still
I_{3}^{\prime}
and
I_{4}^{\prime}
, but by the extra degree of freedom granted by
\delta^{\prime},\epsilon^{\prime}>0
, for any choice of
\Lambda,\delta,\delta^{\prime},\epsilon,\epsilon^{\prime}>0
which makes the bounds in Proposition
2.5
suitably small, the
same
choice of parameters is also sufficient for nearby
(\phi*\varphi_{\delta^{\prime}})(x,t)\in L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{2}
and
(\nabla u*\varphi_{\epsilon^{\prime}})(x)\in L^{1}
, i.e. for open neighbourhoods in the
weak-
*
topology. By the Banach–Alaoglu theorem there is then hope to find a finite cover, i.e. a finite set
\{(\Lambda_{n},\delta_{n},\delta^{\prime}_{n},\epsilon_{n},\epsilon^{\prime}_{n})\}_{n=1}^{N}
such that the bounds in Proposition
2.5
are always small for at least one these choices of parameters. This is our strategy.

2.6
The pigeonhole principle

Consider the bound on
I_{3}^{\prime}
. Keeping only the dependence on
\delta,\delta^{\prime},\epsilon
, we approximately require that the following harmonic estimate is small:

\left\|\phi*\varphi_{\delta^{\prime}}\right\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{2}}^{2}-\left\|\phi*\varphi_{\delta^{\prime}}*\varphi_{\delta}\right\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{2}}^{2}\ll\epsilon^{d}.

(8)

In terms of the Fourier series for
\phi(\cdot,t):\mathbb{Z}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
, this harmonic estimate roughly corresponds to the
L_{t}^{2}L_{\omega}^{2}
-norm in frequency-space (
\omega\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
) of the frequencies of
\phi(\cdot,t)
in the frequency band
\frac{1}{\delta_{n}}\leq|\omega|\leq\frac{1}{\delta^{\prime}_{n}}
. A-priori, as for the harmonic estimate on
I_{3}
(
7
), we do not know where the frequencies of
\phi(\cdot,t)
are located. However, for a finite cover
\{(\Lambda_{n},\delta_{n},\delta^{\prime}_{n},\epsilon_{n},\epsilon^{\prime}_{n})\}_{n=1}^{N}
then perhaps the different frequency bands
\left[\frac{1}{\delta_{n}},\frac{1}{\delta^{\prime}_{n}}\right]
are
disjoint
. By the
pigeonhole principle
there is then at least one
empty
pigeonhole, i.e. one frequency band
\left[\frac{1}{\delta_{n}},\frac{1}{\delta^{\prime}_{n}}\right]
for which the harmonic estimate (
8
) is small with the smallness determined by the number of pigeonholes and the total norm of
\left\|\phi\right\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{2}}^{2}
. Referring to Subsection
3.3
for the exact computations, we now apply the pigeonhole principle in this way to find
\delta,\delta^{\prime},\epsilon,\epsilon^{\prime}>0
for which
both

I_{3}^{\prime}
and
I_{4}^{\prime}
are small, independent of
\phi(x,t)
and
\nabla u(x)
(except their norms
\left\|\phi\right\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{2}}
and
\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L^{1}}
).

Firstly, the parameter
\delta^{\prime}>0
must be sufficiently small to make the bound on the remainder
R_{1}
small but is otherwise free. Keeping only the dependence on
\delta,\epsilon
, this approximately requires

\displaystyle\delta^{\prime}\approx\epsilon\delta,

and so the harmonic estimate (
8
) on
I_{3}^{\prime}
can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\left\|\phi*\varphi_{\epsilon\delta}\right\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{2}}^{2}-\left\|\phi*\varphi_{\epsilon\delta}*\varphi_{\delta}\right\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\ll\epsilon^{d}.

Fixing some
\epsilon>0
to be chosen later, disjointness of the frequency bands
\left[\frac{1}{\delta_{n}},\frac{1}{\epsilon\delta_{n}}\right]
can be ensured by repeatedly replacing

\displaystyle\delta_{n+1}=\epsilon\delta_{n},

The number of such pigeonholes required is determined by the smallness, namely
\epsilon^{d}
. If we perform the replacement
\delta\mapsto\epsilon\delta
up to
\epsilon^{-d}
times, we are guaranteed to find such an ‘empty’ pigeonhole. That is, for fixed
\epsilon>0
, we can guarantee
I_{3}^{\prime},R_{1}
are small by taking, at worst,

\delta\approx\epsilon^{\epsilon^{-d}}.

We now turn to the second problematic term
I_{4}^{\prime}
, which is controlled by the
BV
harmonic estimate

\left\|\nabla u*\varphi_{\epsilon^{\prime}}\right\|_{L^{1}}-\left\|\nabla u*\varphi_{\epsilon^{\prime}}*\varphi_{4\epsilon}\right\|_{L^{1}}.

We treat this similarly. Firstly, the parameter
\epsilon^{\prime}>0
must be sufficiently small to make the bound on the remainder
R_{2}
vanish but is otherwise free. One approximately requires

\displaystyle\epsilon^{\prime}

\displaystyle\approx\delta.

Since (at worst)
\delta\approx\epsilon^{\epsilon^{-d}}
,
I_{4}^{\prime}
is (at worst) controlled by the difference of norms

\left\|\nabla u*\varphi_{\epsilon^{\epsilon^{-d}}}\right\|_{L^{1}}-\left\|\nabla u*\varphi_{\epsilon^{\epsilon^{-d}}}*\varphi_{\epsilon}\right\|_{L^{1}}\ll 1

The argument is again identical, with disjointness of the above differences/pigeonholes ensured by repeatedly replacing

\epsilon_{n+1}=\epsilon_{n}^{\epsilon_{n}^{-d}}.

(9)

The key observation here is that, while before we repeated the
multiplication

\delta\mapsto\epsilon\delta
to produce
exponentiation

\epsilon\mapsto\epsilon^{\epsilon^{-d}}
, we must now repeat
exponentiation
to produce
tetration
in the final bound, see Subsection
3.3
for the exact computations:

Theorem
A
.

Let
\kappa>0
for constants
A,B>0
depending only on the dimension
d\geq 2
, if

\left\|\rho_{0}\right\|_{W^{-1,1}}\leq\kappa\left(\exp^{\left\lceil A\exp\left(\frac{B}{\kappa}\left\|\rho\right\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{\infty}}T\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L^{1}}\right)\right\rceil}\left(\frac{1}{\kappa}\left\|\rho\right\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{\infty}}\right)\right)^{-1},

where
\exp^{n}(x)
refers to repeated exponentiation
\exp(\exp(\dots(\exp(x))))
(tetration), then

\left\|\rho_{T}\right\|_{W^{-1,1}}\leq\kappa.

Remark 2.8
.

The tetration can be attributed to the factor of
\frac{1}{\epsilon}
in the bound for
I_{3}^{\prime}
. This leads to the bound
\epsilon^{-d}
in the harmonic estimate (
8
), and therefore to repeated exponentiation
\epsilon\mapsto\epsilon^{\epsilon^{-d}}
(
9
) when applying the pigeonhole principle. This factor emerges from the
local
in space nature of Ambrosio’s regularisation scheme
[
3
]
. To be precise, the Alberti mollifier
\varphi_{\delta}^{\Lambda,\bar{M}(\bar{x})}(h)\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R})
(see equation (
15
)) is used to mollify the solution
\phi(x,t)
so that the transport error

r(x,t)=\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}+u(x)\cdot\nabla\right)\left(\phi*\varphi_{\delta}^{\Lambda,\bar{M}(\bar{x})}\right)(x,t)

is small
locally
, i.e. for some ball
x\in B_{\epsilon}(\bar{x})\subset\mathbb{T}^{d}
with the mollifier then having to depend on
\bar{x}\in\mathbb{T}^{d}
. To ensure smallness globally, we perform a cutoff via the mollifier
\varphi_{\epsilon}(h)
in equation (
25
), whose regularity
\left\|\varphi_{\epsilon}\right\|_{W^{1,1}}
produces the factor
\frac{1}{\epsilon}
appearing in the bound on
I_{3}
(
32
), and similarly for
I_{3}^{\prime}
.

We remark that when
u(x)\in W^{1,p}
the standard regularisation due to DiPerna-Lions instead vanishes
globally
(e.g. in
L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{1}
,
[
9
]
). When
p>1
, a similar approach to the one presented in this paper then gives a polynomial-exponential mixing estimate instead of tetration, see
[
12
, Section 4]
by the same authors. We argue, therefore, that the locality of the regularisation scheme and the resulting mixing rate are intimately related.

In the statement of the proposition we have fixed some

(13)

Therefore, there exists some weak trace

(14)

To apply the local regularisation scheme of Ambrosio, we fix some space-dependent matrix field to be chosen later
\bar{M}(\bar{x}):\mathbb{T}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
with zero trace
\mathrm{tr}(\bar{M}(\bar{x}))=0
, and bounded
l^{2}
-norm
|\bar{M}(\bar{x})|\leq 1
. For some
\Lambda>0
, define Alberti’s anisotropic mollifier
[
2
]

\varphi^{\Lambda,\bar{M}(\bar{x})}(h)=\frac{1}{\Lambda}\int_{0}^{\Lambda}\varphi\left(\exp(-\lambda\bar{M}^{\dagger}(\bar{x}))\cdot h\right)\;d\lambda\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}),

(15)

where
\bar{M}^{\dagger}
is the adjoint
\bar{M}^{\dagger}_{i,j}=\bar{M}_{j,i}
. Observe that
\varphi^{\Lambda,\bar{M}(\bar{x})}(h)\in L^{1}
is a standard mollifier since
\mathrm{tr}(\bar{M})=0
, so
\det(\exp(-\lambda\bar{M}^{\dagger}))=1
. We also define the rescaled mollifier by

\varphi_{\delta}^{\Lambda,\bar{M}(\bar{x})}(h)=\frac{1}{\delta^{d}}\varphi^{\Lambda,\bar{M}(\bar{x})}\left(\frac{h}{\delta}\right).

(16)

From the expression (
15
), and the preliminary assumptions on the isotropic mollifier (
10
), we have the following a-priori point-wise bounds:

\displaystyle\left|\varphi_{\delta}^{\Lambda,\bar{M}(\bar{x})}(h)\right|\lesssim\delta^{-d}1_{|h|\leq\delta e^{\Lambda}},

(17)

\displaystyle\left|\nabla\varphi_{\delta}^{\Lambda,\bar{M}(\bar{x})}(h)\right|\lesssim\delta^{-d-1}e^{\Lambda}1_{|h|\leq\delta e^{\Lambda}}.

(18)

Using that the trace
\mathrm{tr}(\bar{M}(\bar{x}))=0
, and so the determinant
\mathrm{det}(\exp(-\lambda\bar{M}^{\dagger}(\bar{x})))=1
, we then also have the bounds

\displaystyle\left\|\varphi_{\delta}^{\Lambda,\bar{M}(\bar{x})}\right\|_{L^{1}}\lesssim 1,

(19)

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla\varphi_{\delta}^{\Lambda,\bar{M}(\bar{x})}\right\|_{L^{1}}\lesssim\frac{e^{\Lambda}}{\delta}.

(20)

We recall the identity of Alberti
[
2
]
:

\displaystyle h\cdot\bar{M}(\bar{x})\cdot\nabla\varphi^{\Lambda,\bar{M}(\bar{x})}(h)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Lambda}\int_{0}^{\Lambda}-\frac{\partial}{\partial\lambda}\varphi\left(\exp(-\lambda\bar{M}^{\dagger}(\bar{x}))\cdot h\right)d\lambda

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Lambda}\left(\varphi(h)-\varphi\left(\exp(-\Lambda\bar{M}^{\dagger}(\bar{x}))\cdot h\right)\right),

(21)

and so

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|h\cdot\bar{M}(\bar{x})\cdot\nabla\varphi^{\Lambda,\bar{M}(\bar{x})}(h)\right|\;dh\lesssim\frac{1}{\Lambda}.

(22)

Define also the (DiPerna-Lions) commutator
[
9
]

\displaystyle r\left(u,\phi;\varphi^{\Lambda,\bar{M}(\bar{x})}_{\delta}\right)(x,t)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\phi(x-h,t)(u(x)-u(x-h))\cdot\nabla\varphi_{\delta}^{\Lambda,\bar{M}(\bar{x})}(h)\;dh

(23)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\phi(x-\delta h,t)\left(\int_{0}^{1}h\cdot\nabla u(x-s\delta h)\cdot\nabla\varphi^{\Lambda,\bar{M}(\bar{x})}(h)\;ds\right)\;dh,

(24)

where to integrate

over
\mathbb{R}^{d}

is the similarity between the expressions (

\bar{M}(\bar{x}):\mathbb{T}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
.

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}\times[0,T]}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho(x,t)r\left(\phi,u;\varphi_{\delta}^{\Lambda,\bar{M}(\bar{x})}\right)(x,t)\varphi_{\epsilon}(\bar{x}-x)\;d\bar{x}\;dxdt

(34)

\displaystyle\qquad=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}\times[0,T]}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho(x,t)\phi(x-\delta h,t)\left(\int_{0}^{1}h\cdot\nabla u(x-s\delta h)\cdot\nabla\varphi^{\Lambda,\bar{M}(\bar{x})}(h)\;ds\right)\varphi_{\epsilon}(\bar{x}-x)\;dh\;d\bar{x}\;dxdt

\displaystyle\qquad=I_{4}+I_{5},

where, in terms of the choice of traceless, unit-bounded matrix field
\bar{M}(\bar{x})
:

I_{4}=\begin{aligned} &\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}\times[0,T]}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho(x,t)\phi(x-\delta h,t)\left(\int_{0}^{1}h\cdot\left(\nabla u(x-s\delta h)-|\nabla u(x-s\delta h)|\bar{M}(\bar{x})\right)\cdot\nabla\varphi^{\Lambda,\bar{M}(\bar{x})}(h)\;ds\right)\\
&\qquad\times\varphi_{\epsilon}(\bar{x}-x)\;dh\;d\bar{x}\;dxdt,\end{aligned}

(35)

and

I_{5}=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}\times[0,T]}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho(x,t)\phi(x-\delta h,t)\left(\int_{0}^{1}|\nabla u(x-s\delta h)|h\cdot\bar{M}(\bar{x})\cdot\nabla\varphi^{\Lambda,\bar{M}(\bar{x})}(h)\;ds\right)\varphi_{\epsilon}(\bar{x}-x)\;dh\;d\bar{x}\;dxdt.

(36)

I_{4}
using the bound on the support of

(
18
), and the standard pointwise bound on the rescaled isotropic mollifier

\displaystyle|I_{4}|

\displaystyle\leq\begin{aligned} &\left\|\rho\right\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{\infty}}\left\|\phi\right\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{\infty}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}\times[0,T]}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\int_{0}^{1}|h|\left|\nabla u(x-s\delta h)-|\nabla u(x-s\delta h)|\bar{M}(\bar{x})\right|\left|\nabla\varphi^{\Lambda,\bar{M}(\bar{x})}(h)\right|\;ds\right)\\
&\qquad\times\left|\varphi_{\epsilon}(\bar{x}-x)\right|\;dh\;d\bar{x}\;dxdt\end{aligned}

\displaystyle\lesssim\begin{aligned} &\left\|\rho\right\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{\infty}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}\times[0,T]}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{0}^{1}|h|\left|\nabla u(x-s\delta h)-|\nabla u(x-s\delta h)|\bar{M}(\bar{x})\right|1_{|h|\leq e^{\Lambda}}\epsilon^{-d}1_{|\bar{x}-x|\leq\epsilon}\\
&\qquad\times\left|\nabla\varphi^{\Lambda,\bar{M}(\bar{x})}(h)\right|\;ds\;dh\;d\bar{x}\;dxdt\end{aligned}

\displaystyle\lesssim\begin{aligned} &e^{\Lambda}\left\|\rho\right\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{\infty}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}\times[0,T]}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|\nabla u(x-s\delta h)-|\nabla u(x-s\delta h)|\bar{M}(\bar{x})\right|\epsilon^{-d}1_{|\bar{x}-x|\leq\epsilon}1_{|h|\leq e^{\Lambda}}\\
&\qquad\times\left|\nabla\varphi^{\Lambda,\bar{M}(\bar{x})}(h)\right|\;d\bar{x}\;dxdt\;ds\;dh\end{aligned}

\displaystyle\lesssim e^{\Lambda}\left\|\rho\right\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{\infty}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}\times[0,T]}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|\nabla u(x)-|\nabla u(x)|\bar{M}(\bar{x})\right|\epsilon^{-d}1_{|\bar{x}-x|\leq\epsilon+\delta e^{\Lambda}}\left|\nabla\varphi^{\Lambda,\bar{M}(\bar{x})}(h)\right|\;d\bar{x}\;dxdt\;ds\;dh

\displaystyle\lesssim Te^{\Lambda}\left\|\rho\right\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{\infty}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|\nabla u(x)-|\nabla u(x)|\bar{M}(\bar{x})\right|\epsilon^{-d}1_{|\bar{x}-x|\leq 2\epsilon}\left\|\nabla\varphi^{\Lambda,\bar{M}(\bar{x})}\right\|_{L^{1}}\;d\bar{x}\;dx

\displaystyle\lesssim Te^{2\Lambda}\left\|\rho\right\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{\infty}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\nabla u(x)|\left|\frac{\nabla u(x)}{|\nabla u(x)|}-\bar{M}(\bar{x})\right|\epsilon^{-d}1_{|\bar{x}-x|\leq 2\epsilon}\;d\bar{x}\;dx,

where we have used the transport bound on
\phi(x,t)
(
13
), the bound on
\nabla\varphi^{\Lambda,\bar{M}(\bar{x})}(h)
(
20
), and the assumption in the statement of the proposition that
\delta e^{\Lambda}\leq\epsilon
. We still face the same problem as for
I_{3}
, that this bound is still not quantitative. Therefore, by Cauchy-Schwartz:

\displaystyle|I_{4}|

\displaystyle\lesssim\begin{aligned} &Te^{2\Lambda}\left\|\rho\right\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{\infty}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\nabla u(x)|\epsilon^{-d}1_{|\bar{x}-x|\leq 2\epsilon}\;d\bar{x}\;dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
&\times\left(\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\nabla u(x)|\left|\frac{\nabla u(x)}{|\nabla u(x)|}-\bar{M}(\bar{x})\right|^{2}\epsilon^{-d}1_{|\bar{x}-x|\leq 2\epsilon}\;d\bar{x}\;dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\end{aligned}

\displaystyle\lesssim Te^{2\Lambda}\left\|\rho\right\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{\infty}}\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L^{1}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\nabla u(x)|\left|\frac{\nabla u(x)}{|\nabla u(x)|}-\bar{M}(\bar{x})\right|^{2}\epsilon^{-d}1_{|\bar{x}-x|\leq 2\epsilon}\;dx\;d\bar{x}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},

where in the last line, we have also flipped the integrals over
x\in\mathbb{T}^{d}
,
\bar{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
to instead
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
(extended periodically) and
\bar{x}\in\mathbb{T}^{d}
. This is valid since
1_{|\bar{x}-x|\leq 2\epsilon}
is symmetric in
\bar{x}-x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Since
\varphi(h)\gtrsim 1
for

(
10
):

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\nabla u(x)|\left|\frac{\nabla u(x)}{|\nabla u(x)|}-\bar{M}(\bar{x})\right|^{2}\epsilon^{-d}1_{|\bar{x}-x|\leq 2\epsilon}\;dx

\displaystyle\qquad\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\nabla u(\bar{x}-h)|\left|\frac{\nabla u(\bar{x}-h)}{|\nabla u(\bar{x}-h)|}-\bar{M}(\bar{x})\right|^{2}\varphi_{4\epsilon}(h)\;dh

where in the last line, we expanded the quadratic expression and integrated over
h\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Taking now

which is traceless since
u(x)
is divergence-free, and by definition unit-bounded, we obtain the following bound on

\displaystyle|I_{4}|

\displaystyle\lesssim Te^{2\Lambda}\left\|\rho\right\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{\infty}}\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L^{1}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}2(|\nabla u|*\varphi_{4\epsilon})(\bar{x})-2|(\nabla u*\varphi_{4\epsilon})(\bar{x})|\;d\bar{x}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=Te^{2\Lambda}\left\|\rho\right\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{\infty}}\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L^{1}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(2\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L^{1}}-2\left\|\nabla u*\varphi_{4\epsilon}\right\|_{L^{1}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

(37)

Finally, we exploit Alberti’s anisotropic bound (
22
) to bound the remaining term
I_{5}
(
36
):

\displaystyle|I_{5}|

\displaystyle\leq\begin{aligned} &\left\|\rho\right\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{\infty}}\left\|\phi\right\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{\infty}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}\times[0,T]}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{0}^{1}|\nabla u(x-s\delta h)|\left|h\cdot\bar{M}(\bar{x})\cdot\nabla\varphi^{\Lambda,\bar{M}(\bar{x})}(h)\right|\left|\varphi_{\epsilon}(\bar{x}-x)\right|\;\\
&\qquad ds\;dh\;d\bar{x}\;dxdt\end{aligned}

\displaystyle\lesssim\begin{aligned} &\left\|\rho\right\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{\infty}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}\times[0,T]}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\nabla u(x-s\delta h)|\left|h\cdot\bar{M}(\bar{x})\cdot\nabla\varphi^{\Lambda,\bar{M}(\bar{x})}(h)\right|1_{|h|\leq e^{\Lambda}}\epsilon^{-d}1_{|\bar{x}-x|\leq\epsilon}\;\\
&\qquad d\bar{x}\;dxdt\;ds\;dh\end{aligned}

\displaystyle\lesssim\begin{aligned} &\left\|\rho\right\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{\infty}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}\times[0,T]}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\nabla u(x)|\left|h\cdot\bar{M}(\bar{x})\cdot\nabla\varphi^{\Lambda,\bar{M}(\bar{x})}(h)\right|\epsilon^{-d}1_{|\bar{x}-x|\leq\epsilon+\delta e^{\Lambda}}\;\\
&\qquad d\bar{x}\;dxdt\;ds\;dh\end{aligned}

\displaystyle\lesssim T\left\|\rho\right\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{\infty}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}|\nabla u(x)|\epsilon^{-d}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}1_{|\bar{x}-x|\leq\epsilon+\delta e^{\Lambda}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|h\cdot\bar{M}(\bar{x})\cdot\nabla\varphi^{\Lambda,\bar{M}(\bar{x})}(h)\right|\;dh\;d\bar{x}\;dx

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{T}{\Lambda}\left(1+\frac{\delta e^{\Lambda}}{\epsilon}\right)^{d}\left\|\rho\right\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{\infty}}\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L^{1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{T}{\Lambda}\left\|\rho\right\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{\infty}}\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L^{1}},

(38)

where we have again used the bound on the support of
\nabla\varphi^{\Lambda,\bar{M}(\bar{x})}(h)
(
18
), the transport bound on
\phi(x,t)
(
13
), and in the final line the assumption in the statement of the Proposition that
\delta\leq\epsilon e^{-\Lambda}

∎

Proof of Theorem
A
and Corollary

Proof.

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}\times[0,T)}\bar{\rho}(x,t)\left(\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial t}(x,t)+u(x,t)\cdot\nabla\phi(x,t)\right)\;dxdt

\displaystyle\qquad=\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}\times[0,T)}\rho^{(n)}(x,t)\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial t}(x,t)+\rho^{(n)}(x,t)u^{(n)}(x,t)\cdot\nabla\phi(x,t)\;dxdt

\displaystyle\qquad=-\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\rho_{0}^{(n)}(x)\phi(x,0)\;dx,

\displaystyle\qquad=0,

and so
\bar{\rho}(x,t)\in L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{\infty}
is the
unique
bounded solution
[
3
]
to the transport equation along
\bar{u}(x)\in BV
with zero initial datum, that is
\bar{\rho}(x,t)=0
and in particular
\bar{\rho}(x,T)=0
.

However, since
\rho^{(n)}_{T}(x)
converges to
\bar{\rho}_{T}(x)
strongly in
W^{-1,1}
(since
\rho^{(n)}(x,t)
converges strongly in
C_{t}^{0}C_{x}^{-1}
), and
\left\|\rho^{(n)}_{T}\right\|_{W^{-1,1}}>\epsilon
uniformly by (
69
), then also

\left\|\bar{\rho}_{T}\right\|_{W^{-1,1}}\geq\epsilon>0,

a contradiction as required.
∎

4.2
Proof of Corollary
2.4

Proof.

We use Proposition
2.2
, that is we show for any fixed test function
\phi_{T}(x)\in W^{1,\infty}
that

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\rho_{T}(x)\phi_{T}(x)\;dx=0,

and note that we may effectively take
T>0
arbitrarily.

To this end, we choose parameters as in Proposition
2.2
such that each of the bounds is arbitrarily small. Fix some arbitrarily small parameter
\kappa>0
.

We first choose
\Lambda>0
large enough that the bound on
I_{5}
is less than or equal to
\kappa
, for instance

\Lambda=\kappa T\left\|\rho\right\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{\infty}}\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L^{1}}.

Next, we pick
\epsilon>0
small enough so that
I_{4}
is also bounded by
\kappa
. By Proposition
2.2
, it is sufficient to satisfy

Te^{2\Lambda}\left\|\rho\right\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{\infty}}\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L^{1}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L^{1}}-\left\|\nabla u*\varphi_{4\epsilon}\right\|_{L^{1}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq\kappa,

where now
\Lambda>0
is fixed. Thus, we need only show that as
\epsilon\to 0
that

\left\|\nabla u*\varphi_{4\epsilon}\right\|_{L^{1}}\to\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L^{1}}.

First, since
\varphi(x)\geq 0
is a positive mollifier, we have the bound

\left\|\nabla u*\varphi_{4\epsilon}\right\|_{L^{1}}\leq\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L^{1}},

and so it is sufficient to prove

\liminf\left\|\nabla u*\varphi_{4\epsilon}\right\|_{L^{1}}\geq\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L^{1}},

which follows from convergence in the distributional sense of

(\nabla u*\varphi_{4\epsilon})(x)\rightharpoonup\nabla u(x),

as
\epsilon\to 0
.

We have so far chosen
\Lambda>0
large and
\epsilon>0
small so that

\displaystyle|I_{5}|\lesssim\kappa,

\displaystyle|I_{4}|\lesssim\kappa.

By assumption
\rho_{0}(x)=0
and so by Proposition
2.2
,
I_{2}=0
. We are left to pick
\delta>0
small so that the assumption
\delta\leq\epsilon e^{-\Lambda}
of Proposition
2.2
is satisfied, and

\displaystyle|I_{1}|\lesssim\kappa,

\displaystyle|I_{3}|\lesssim\kappa.

Therefore, we require
\delta>0
small enough that

\displaystyle\delta\leq\epsilon e^{-\Lambda},

\displaystyle\delta e^{(d+1)\Lambda}\left\|\rho\right\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{\infty}}\leq\kappa,

\displaystyle T^{\frac{d-1}{d}}\frac{e^{d\Lambda}}{\epsilon}\left\|\rho\right\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{\infty}}\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L^{1}}\left(\left\|\phi\right\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{2}}^{2}-\left\|\phi*\varphi_{2\delta e^{\Lambda}}\right\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{2}}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{d}}\leq\kappa,

where only convergence of the last constraint requires further analysis. Since
\phi(x,t)\in L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{\infty}\subset L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{2}
(on the domain
\mathbb{T}^{d}\times[0,T]
), this follows by the standard convergence of

(\phi*\varphi_{2\delta e^{\Lambda}})(x,t)\to\phi(x,t),

in
L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{2}
as
2\delta e^{\Lambda}\to 0
.

Thus we may chose
\Lambda>0
large,
\epsilon>0
small, and
\delta>0
small so that by Proposition
2.2
:

\displaystyle|I_{1}|\lesssim\kappa,

\displaystyle|I_{2}|=0,

\displaystyle|I_{3}|\lesssim\kappa,

\displaystyle|I_{4}|\lesssim\kappa,

\displaystyle|I_{5}|\lesssim\kappa.

Since
\kappa>0
is arbitrary, we deduce that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\rho_{T}(x)\phi_{T}(x)\;dx

\displaystyle=I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3}+I_{4}+I_{5}

\displaystyle=0.

Since also
\phi_{T}(x)\in W^{1,\infty}
is arbitrary, we deduce that

\rho_{T}(x)=0.

Since also
T>0
is essentially arbitrary, we deduce that

\rho(x,t)=0.

∎

Radiative decays of the second shell Λ b \Lambda_{b} and Ξ b \Xi_{b} bottom baryons
Ailier Rivero-Acosta, H. García-Tecocoatzi, A. Ramirez-Morales, E. Santopinto, Carlos Alberto Vaquera-Araujo

Abstract
In this work, we investigate the radiative decays of the Λ b \Lambda_{b} and Ξ b \Xi_{b} bottom baryons, which belong to the flavor anti-triplet ( 𝟑 ¯ F \mathbf{\bar{3}}_{\rm F} ), within the constituent quark model formalism. The electromagnetic transitions are calculated from the second-shell states to both the ground and P P -wave final states. These decays play a crucial role in confirming the existence of certain resonances. When strong decays are not allowed, the reconstruction of states relies on their electromagnetic decay channels. Moreover, electromagnetic decay widths are particularly useful for the identification of resonances when states have the same mass and total decay width. This study presents, for the first time, the calculation of electromagnetic decays for D ρ D_{\rho} -wave states, ρ − λ \rho-\lambda mixed states, and ρ \rho -mode radially excited states. Throughout our calculations, we account for uncertainties arising from both experimental and model-dependent errors.

II.2
Electromagnetic decays

The interaction Hamiltonian that describes the electromagnetic coupling between photons and quarks, at tree level, is given by

H=-\sum_{j}{\rm e}_{j}{\bar{q}}_{j}\gamma^{\mu}A_{\mu}q_{j},

(4)

where
{\rm e}_{j}
and
q_{j}
are the charge and the quark field corresponding to the
j
-th quark, respectively,
\gamma^{\mu}
are the Dirac matrices, and
A_{\mu}
is the electromagnetic field. Taking the non-relativistic limit and keeping terms up to the order
m_{j}^{-1}
, with
m_{j}
being the mass of the
j
-th quark, the previous interaction leads to the following interaction Hamiltonian

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\rm em}=\sum^{3}_{j=1}\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3/2}}\frac{{\rm e}_{j}}{(2{\rm k})^{1/2}}\Bigg\{\varepsilon^{0}e^{-i\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{r}_{j}}

(5)

\displaystyle-\frac{[\mathbf{p}_{j}\cdot\bm{\varepsilon}e^{-i\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{r}_{j}}+e^{-i\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{r}_{j}}\bm{\varepsilon}\cdot\mathbf{p}_{j}]}{2m_{j}}-\frac{i\bm{\sigma}\cdot(\mathbf{k}\times\bm{\varepsilon})e^{-i\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{r}_{j}}}{2m_{j}}\Bigg\},

where
\mathbf{r}_{j}
and
\mathbf{p}_{j}
, stand for the coordinate and momentum of the
j
-th quark, respectively.
\mathbf{k}={\rm k}\hat{\mathbf{z}}
corresponds to the momentum of the photon emitted in the positive
z
direction,
{\rm k}
being the photon energy, and
\bm{\sigma}
are the Pauli spin matrices. The polarization vector for radiative decays is written as
\varepsilon^{\mu}=(0,1,-i,0)/\sqrt{2}
, where the zeroth component vanishes
\varepsilon^{0}=0
, since radiative decays involve real photons
García-Tecocoatzi et al. 2024
. Thus,
the first term of the Eq. (
II.2
) is identically zero. By substituting the explicit form of the polarization vector, the interaction Hamiltonian describing the electromagnetic decays of baryons is written as

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\rm em}=2\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{{\rm k_{0}}}}\sum^{3}_{j=1}\mu_{j}\Big[{\rm k}\mathbf{s}_{j,-}e^{-i\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{r}_{j}}-

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left(\mathbf{p}_{j,-}e^{-i\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{r}_{j}}+e^{-i\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{r}_{j}}\mathbf{p}_{j,-}\right)\Big],

(6)

where
\mu_{j}\equiv{\rm e}_{j}/(2m_{j})
,
\mathbf{s}_{j,-}\equiv\mathbf{s}_{j,x}-i\mathbf{s}_{j,y}
, and
\mathbf{p}_{j,-}\equiv\mathbf{p}_{j,x}-i\mathbf{p}_{j,y}
, are the magnetic moment, the spin ladder, and the momentum ladder operator of the
j
-th quark, respectively.

The Hamiltonian in Eq. (
6
) consists of two parts. The first part is proportional to the magnetic term, which gives the spin-flip transitions:

{\rm k}\mathbf{s}_{j,-}e^{-i\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{r}_{j}}\equiv{\rm k}\mathbf{s}_{j,-}\hat{U}_{j}.

(7)

The evaluation of the spin-flip part is straightforward.
The second part of the Hamiltonian in Eq. (
6
) is the convective term, and gives the orbit-flip transitions:

\mathbf{p}_{j,-}\,e^{-i\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{r}_{j}}+e^{-i\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{r}_{j}}\,\mathbf{p}_{j,-}\equiv\hat{T}_{j,-}.

(8)

The orbit-flip part is evaluated analytically, following and extending the procedure for the case of the
P
-wave states described in section IV of Ref.
García-Tecocoatzi et al. 2024
.

The partial decay width of the electromagnetic transitions is
given by

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\rm em}(A\rightarrow A^{\prime}\gamma)=\Phi_{A\rightarrow A^{\prime}\gamma}\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2}}\frac{2}{2J_{A}+1}\sum_{{M_{J_{A}}}>0}\left|A_{M_{J_{A}}}\right|^{2}.

(9)

Here,
A
,
A^{\prime}
, and
\gamma
denote the initial bottom baryon, the final bottom baryon, and the final-state photon, respectively. The quantity
\Phi_{A\rightarrow A^{\prime}\gamma}
is the phase space factor, which, in the rest frame of the initial baryon, is given by

\Phi_{A\rightarrow A^{\prime}\gamma}=4\pi\,\left(\frac{E_{A^{\prime}}}{m_{A}}\right)\,{\rm k}^{2},

(10)

where
E_{A^{\prime}}=\sqrt{m_{A^{\prime}}^{2}+{\rm k}^{2}}
is the energy of the final-state bottom baryon. The masses of the initial and final baryons are denoted by
m_{A}
and
m_{A^{\prime}}
, respectively, and the final-state photon energy is

{\rm k}=\frac{m_{A}^{2}-m_{A^{\prime}}^{2}}{2m_{A}}.

(11)

J_{A}
represents the total angular momentum of the initial state. For a given helicity
M_{J_{A}}
, the transition amplitude
A_{M_{J_{A}}}
is written as

\displaystyle A_{M_{J_{A}}}=\langle\phi_{A^{\prime}},k_{A^{\prime}},J_{A^{\prime}},{M_{J_{A}}}-1|\mathcal{H}_{\rm em}|\phi_{A},k_{A},J_{A},{M_{J_{A}}}\rangle,

(12)

where
|\phi_{A},k_{A},J_{A},{M_{J_{A}}}\rangle
and
|\phi_{A^{\prime}}k_{A^{\prime}},J_{A^{\prime}},{M_{J_{A}}}-1\rangle
are the initial and final states, respectively.
\phi_{A}
stands for the flavor wave function and
k_{A}
are the nodes. These states diagonalize the Hamiltonian in Eq. (
II.1
),
whose parameters are fitted to the experimental observed bottom baryon states reported by the PDG
Navas et al. 2024

García-Tecocoatzi et al. 2024
).

To evaluate
A_{M_{J_{A}}}
, the electromagnetic Hamiltonian in Eq. (
6
) is rewritten in terms of the
\hat{U}_{j}
and
\hat{T}_{j,-}
operators, yielding the following compact form:

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\rm em}=\sqrt{\frac{4\pi}{(2\pi)^{3}{\rm k}}}\sum^{3}_{j=1}\mu_{j}\Big[{\rm k}\mathbf{s}_{j,-}\hat{U}_{j}-\frac{1}{2}\hat{T}_{j,-}\Big].

(13)

With this compact form, it is simpler to evaluate the transition amplitudes
A_{M_{J_{A}}}
of Eq.
12

becomes:

\displaystyle{A}_{M_{J_{A}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{{\rm k_{0}}}}{\rm k}\sum^{3}_{j=1}\Big[\langle\phi_{A^{\prime}},k_{A^{\prime}},J_{A^{\prime}},{M_{J_{A}}}-1|

(14)

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\hat{\mu}_{j}\mathbf{s}_{j,-}\hat{U}_{j}|\phi_{A},k_{A},J_{A},{M_{J_{A}}}\rangle\Big]

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{{\rm k_{0}}}}\sum^{3}_{j=1}\Big[\langle\phi_{A^{\prime}},k_{A^{\prime}},J_{A^{\prime}},{M_{J_{A}}}-1|

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\hat{\mu}_{j}\hat{T}_{j,-}|\phi_{A},k_{A},J_{A},{M_{J_{A}}}\rangle\Big],

where
|\phi_{A},k_{A},J_{A},{M_{J_{A}}}\rangle
is the initial baryon state. To evaluate the transition for each quark, we write the single baryon states by implementing the following change of basis:

\displaystyle|\phi_{A},k_{A},J_{A},{M_{J_{A}}}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{M_{L},M_{S}}\langle L,M_{L};S,M_{S}|J_{A},{M_{J_{A}}}\rangle

(15)

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\sum_{m_{l_{\lambda}},m_{l_{\rho}}}\langle l_{\lambda},m_{l_{\lambda}};l_{\rho},m_{l_{\rho}}|L,M_{L}\rangle

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\sum_{m_{S_{12}},m_{S_{3}}}\langle S_{12},m_{S_{12}};S_{3},m_{S_{3}}|S,M_{S}\rangle

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\sum_{m_{S_{1}},m_{S_{2}}}\langle S_{1},m_{S_{1}};S_{2},m_{S_{2}}|S_{12},m_{S_{12}}\rangle

\displaystyle|S_{1},m_{S_{1}}\rangle\otimes|S_{2},m_{S_{2}}\rangle\otimes|S_{3},m_{S_{3}}\rangle

\displaystyle|\phi_{A}\rangle\otimes|k_{\rho},l_{\rho},m_{l_{\rho}},k_{\lambda},l_{\lambda},m_{l_{\lambda}}\rangle,

where
|S_{i},m_{S_{i}}\rangle
denote the spin wave function of each quark (
i=1,2,3
), and
|k_{\rho},l_{\rho},m_{l_{\rho}},k_{\lambda},l_{\lambda},m_{l_{\lambda}}\rangle
is the spatial wave function, with
k_{A}=k_{\rho}+k_{\lambda}
. Notice that the problem is separable, as
\hat{\mu}_{j}
acts in the flavor space,
\mathbf{s}_{j,-}
operates in the spin space, and the
\hat{U}_{j}
and
\hat{T}_{j,-}
operators act in the spatial space. In our calculations, we express the matrix elements of the tensor operators
\hat{T}_{j,-}
, as a weighted sum of the matrix elements of the
\hat{U}_{j}
operators. This is achieved through the action of the
\mathbf{p}_{\rho,\pm}
and
\mathbf{p}_{\lambda,\pm}
ladder operators on the singly bottom baryon states, following the procedure introduced in Ref.
García-Tecocoatzi et al. 2024
. The present study takes the masses and assignments for these singly bottom baryon states from Ref.
García-Tecocoatzi et al. 2024
.

We consider the experimental uncertainties that arise from the parameter fitting procedure to data. Furthermore, as in Ref.
García-Tecocoatzi et al. 2024
, we include an uncertainty to account for the approximations of our model utilized to describe the bottom baryons. The uncertainties are propagated to our calculations through the mass of the initial and final singly bottom baryons in the electromagnetic decays calculations. The propagation is performed employing a Monte Carlo bootstrap as follows: We randomly sample the experimental masses from a Gaussian distribution with a mean corresponding to the central value of the bottom baryon mass and a width defined by the squared sum of their uncertainties above mentioned. The process is repeated 10
3
times. Then the electromagnetic decay widths are calculated for each mass in these distributions to obtain a distribution for each decay width calculation. The mean value of the latter distribution is then adopted as the central electromagnetic decay width, while the uncertainty is determined from the difference between the quantiles at a 68% confidence level.

Other approaches are also available in the literature to evaluate the radiative transitions for heavy baryons and mesons. One method that has been widely used in the literature was introduced in Ref.
Close and Copley 1970
by Close and Copley, that used the well-known relation
i[H_{0},\mathbf{r}_{j}]=\mathbf{p}_{j}/m_{j}
, with
H_{0}
as the free Hamiltonian of the constituent quarks, and the approximation
i[H_{0},\mathbf{r}_{j}]\approx i{\rm k}\mathbf{r}_{j}
.
Thus, they effectively rewrote the convective term of the Hamiltonian of Eq.(
II.2
), by replacing
\mathbf{p}_{j}/m_{j}
with
i{\rm k}\mathbf{r}_{j}
. Employing these relations, the part of the convective term that is a function of the momenta
(\mathbf{p}_{j}\cdot\bm{\varepsilon}e^{-i\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{r}_{j}}+e^{-i\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{r}_{j}}\bm{\varepsilon}\cdot\mathbf{p}_{j})/{2m_{j}}
, becomes simply
\mathbf{r}_{j}\cdot\bm{\varepsilon}e^{-i\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{r}_{j}}
. With this substitution, the electromagnetic interaction Hamiltonian is rewritten in a way that simplifies the evaluation of the transition amplitudes. In this work we avoid the implementation of this approximation, as in our approach the convective term is calculated analytically in a straightforward manner.

We compare our results with those previously obtained in the analysis performed in Ref.
Yao et al. 2018
, where the authors only considered the decays from the

-wave to the
P_{\lambda}
-wave singly bottom baryons using CQM, while in Ref.

the decays from the
D_{\lambda}

\lambda

P_{\lambda}
-wave singly bottom baryons were studied. This comparison is presented in Tables

–

Table

shows that our results for the decay channel
{}^{2}D_{\lambda\lambda,5/2}\,\Lambda_{b}\rightarrow\,^{2}P_{\lambda,3/2}\,\Lambda_{b}
are significantly larger than those reported in Refs.
Yao et al. 2018
and
Peng et al. 2024
. Both studies employed the same Close and Copley approximation of the electromagnetic Hamiltonian; however, the methodologies differ in their treatment of spatial wave functions. In Ref.
Yao et al. 2018
, the authors adopted harmonic oscillator wave functions, whereas in Ref.
Peng et al. 2024
, numerical spatial wave functions obtained via the Gaussian Expansion Method were used to evaluate the transition amplitudes. The resulting decay width in Ref.
Peng et al. 2024
is approximately 270% larger than that reported in Ref.
Yao et al. 2018
, highlighting that the primary difference between the two studies lies in the choice of wave functions.

Comparing our results with those in Ref.
Yao et al. 2018
, we note that the discrepancies in the electromagnetic decay widths calculations arise primarily from the use of the Close and Copley approximation in evaluating the convective term, as both studies utilize harmonic oscillator wave functions. Our calculated width is approximately 400% larger than that reported in Ref.
Yao et al. 2018
. Additionally, our width exceeds that of Ref.
Peng et al. 2024
by approximately 164%. In this latter comparison, we note that the differences originate from the fact that we do not employ any additional approximations in the nonrelativistic electromagnetic interaction Hamiltonian and that we use different spatial wave functions. When measurements of the electromagnetic decays of singly bottom baryons become available, it will be possible to assess the impact of the Close and Copley approximations and the use of different spatial wave functions.

Electromagnetic decay widths are particularly valuable in cases where strong decays are forbidden. Moreover, electromagnetic decay widths may be useful in the assignments of resonances when the states have the same mass and total decay widths. One notable example is the
\Xi_{b}

{}^{2}D_{\rho\lambda\,3/2}
mixed state, which has a mass of 6523 MeV and a strong decay width of 47 MeV, while the
\Xi^{\prime}_{b}

{}^{4}D_{\lambda\lambda\,7/2}
state has a mass of 6520 MeV and a strong decay width of 46 MeV. Consequently, if a state is found experimentally with mass and total decay width within these values, it will not be immediately be recognized as a
\Xi_{b}
or
\Xi^{\prime}_{b}
state based on that information alone. A possible way to achieve a conclusive assignment is by studying their radiative decay channels. Some potentially useful channels include the following:

\displaystyle\Gamma_{em}[\Xi_{b}(6523)\to\Xi_{b}^{{}^{\prime}0}\gamma]=132\,\text{KeV}

(16)

\displaystyle\Gamma_{em}[\Xi^{\prime}_{b}(6520)\to\Xi_{b}^{{}^{\prime}0}\gamma]=2.4\,\text{KeV},

(17)

\displaystyle\Gamma_{em}[\Xi_{b}(6523)\to\Xi_{b}^{*0}\gamma]=7\,\text{KeV}

(18)

\displaystyle\Gamma_{em}[\Xi^{\prime}_{b}(6520)\to\Xi_{b}^{*0}\gamma]=60\,\text{KeV},

(19)

\displaystyle\Gamma_{em}[\Xi_{b}(6523)\to\Xi_{b}^{-}\gamma]=38\,\text{KeV}

(20)

\displaystyle\Gamma_{em}[\Xi^{\prime}_{b}(6520)\to\Xi_{b}^{-}\gamma]=1.1\,\text{KeV},

(21)

\displaystyle\Gamma_{em}[\Xi_{b}(6523)\to\Xi_{b}^{*-}\gamma]=0.1\,\text{KeV}

(22)

\displaystyle\Gamma_{em}[\Xi^{\prime}_{b}(6520)\to\Xi_{b}^{*-}\gamma]=93\,\text{KeV}.

(23)

We observe that the differences in the decay widths are at least one order of magnitude for the same decay channels when the initial baryon is a
\Xi_{b}
or a
\Xi^{\prime}_{b}
. Hence the measurement of these electromagnetic decay widths will significantly help distinguish between several
\Xi_{b}
and
\Xi^{\prime}_{b}
states. In this regard, our results may be important for accurately making the assignment of states similar to the example described above. The electromagnetic decay width values for the
\Xi^{\prime}_{b}
baryon states are preliminary results taken from
Rivero-Acosta et al.
, which is currently a work in progress.

IV
Conclusions

In summary, we calculate the electromagnetic decay widths of the
\Lambda_{b}
and the
\Xi_{b}
baryons belonging to the flavor
{\bf{\bar{3}}}_{\rm F}
-plet for transitions from states of the second shell to ground states and
P
-wave excited states.

Up to now, there are no experimental measurements of electromagnetic decays of singly bottom baryons, therefore our results can only be compared with other theoretical works. However, up to now, the radiative decays of second shell singly bottom baryons are practically unexplored; in fact, only Refs.
Yao et al. 2018
and
Peng et al. 2024
have performed calculations of radiative decays of a subset of second shell states. Moreover, as discussed in detail in Ref.
García-Tecocoatzi et al. 2025
, the method introduced in Ref.
García-Tecocoatzi et al. 2024
allows us to evaluate the transition amplitudes in an exact analytical way without the need of introducing any further approximations, like the one used in Refs.
Yao et al. 2018
and
Peng et al. 2024
following the electromagnetic Hamiltonian derived in Ref.
Close and Copley 1970
.
Therefore, our results are more precise, given the fact that the exact evaluation of the convective term of the electromagnetic Hamiltonian in Eq.
6
plays an important role in the calculation of electromagnetic transitions where the angular momentum between the initial and final states changes
García-Tecocoatzi et al. 2025
.

It is worth mentioning that this is the first time that the electromagnetic decays of
D_{\rho}
-wave states,
\rho\lambda
mixed states, and
\rho
-mode radial excited states are calculated.

Electromagnetic decay widths can help with the assignments when states have the same mass and total decay widths, like the case of the example discussed in Section
III
. In this work, we have accounted for the propagation of parameter uncertainties using a Monte Carlo bootstrap method. The inclusion of uncertainties is essential but it is often missed in related research works.

The study of electromagnetic decay widths of the second shell
\Sigma_{b}
,
\Xi^{\prime}_{b}
and
\Omega_{b}
bottom baryons belonging to the flavor
{\bf{6}}_{\rm F}
-plet will be published separately
Rivero-Acosta et al.
.

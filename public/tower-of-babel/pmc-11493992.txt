Dynamical analysis and fault detection application of a time-delayed multi-stable stochastic resonance system driven by white correlated noises
Authors: Yantong Liu, Shaojuan Ma
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Bearing fault diagnosis is a crucial means to ensure the normal operation of machinery, reduce maintenance costs, enhance equipments safety and extend the service life of bearings. However, the noise interference in input signals often affects the effective extraction of bearing fault signals. In this paper, a time-delay multi-stable stochastic resonance (SR) system driven by white correlated noises is proposed. Firstly, the expressions of the steady-state probability density (SPD) function and the mean first passage time (MFPT) is derived. Simultaneously, we delve into the influences of various system parameters, including multiplicative noise intensity, additive noise intensity, noise correlated strength, time-delay, and time-delay feedback intensity, on the dynamical behavior exhibited by particles within the system. Then, according to the signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) formula, the paper investigates the impact of system parameters on the SNR. It is found that the ease of SR occurrence is directly related to the system parameters. Finally, the experimental results demonstrate that the proposed method exhibits superior performance in detecting faults compared with the classical bistable SR system and the quad-stable SR system.

Introduction

SR was initially introduced as the means to elucidate the Quaternary glacier problem by Benzi and co-author. Afterwards, it was applied to describe a phenomenon in nonlinear systems where internal or external noise may amplifythe system output response1–3. In the previous several decades, SR has been widely applied in various fields, such as physics4,5, biology6–8, mechanical engineering9,10, especially in signal detection11–13. Many scholars have proposed important theories to gain more profound comprehensions of SR. McNamara et al.14 observed the SR phenomenon through bidirectional ring lasers and derived the expression of SNR using adiabatic approximation theory for the first time, they found that adding appropriate noise to the system can improve the SNR. Kramers et al.15 employed the Fokker–Planck equation to compute the escape rate of particles crossing a potential well, deriving the renowned Kramers escape rate expression and obtaining the MFPT as the reciprocal of the escape rate. Bag et al.16 investigated the MFPT of Brownian particles departing from the unstable limit cycle in the presence of both multiplicative colored noise and additive white noise, uncovering resonance activation phenomena. Mahariq et al.17 studied a spectral element method for the solution of magnetostatic fields. Based on the aforementioned theories and the property of SR which can convert interfering noise into useful signals, the research on SR in mechanical fault diagnosis has garnered significant interest.

In engineering, 45–55% of faults in rotating machines were caused by faults in bearings18. Since the 1990s, the scholars have been using SR systems to identify bearing fault signals. Zhang et al.19 examined the impact of both additive and multiplicative noises on bearing detection in the asymmetric bistable system, revealing its superior detection capabilities compared to the classical bistable system. A coupled bistable adaptive SR system was studied by Li et al.20, which can adapt to weak signals and be better applied to bearing fault diagnosis. Nevertheless, the researches on SR mainly concentrates on nonlinear single stable or bistable systems, leaving a research gap in the exploration of SR phenomena in multi-stable systems. Multi-stable systems, characterized by the simultaneous existence of two or more attractors, are prevalent in various domains in mechanical engineering, aerospace, marine engineering and ecological biology, such as multi-stable energy harvesters21, fiber lasers22 and multi species competitive survival models23. Arathi et al.24 investigated that appropriate parameters can maximize SNR in a symmetric triple well SR system. Lai et al.25 put forward a tri-stable SR system with multiple parameter adjustments and demonstrated its feasibility in initial fault diagnosis. In 2020, Xu et al.26 discussed the SR principle of asymmetric tristable systems under correlated noise excitation and found the proposed system can effectively improve the efficacy of bearing fault detection. Jin et al.27 obtained analytical expressions for MFPT and spectral amplification about the quad-stable SR system, which improved the fault diagnosis performance in contrast to the over damped tri-stable SR method.

It is worth noting that the influence of the time-delay on the system was not mentioned in the above work. Many related studies have displayed that time-delay exists in various systems, including physical and biological systems28–30. Over the past few years, the exploration of time-delay in nonlinear dynamical systems has gradually attracted the attention of scholars. Ahmad et al.31 researched a coupled nonlinear evolution system of fractional order with the proportional delay. Nadeem et al.32 explored the idea of the Sawi iterative scheme to derive the analytical solution of nonlinear delay differential equations. In the field of SR, Wu et al.33 proposed the SR phenomenon in bistable systems with time-delayed feedback driven by non Gaussian noise, examining the impact of various parameters on SR behavior. Xu et al.34 investigated the SR of a triple-well potential with time-delay under the influence of correlated noise and harmonic signals, which discovered that time-delay, noise intensity and the correlation between noises can all lead to the occurrence of particle transitions. Tan et al.35 discussed the SR in two asymmetric nonlinear systems with delayed feedback driven by colored noise, which shown that there exists an optimal feedback strength and time-delay can maximize the SNR of system. Zhang et al.36 proposed a time-delayed tri-stable SR system driven by multiplicative and additive noise, considering the impact of time-delayed on the system’s fault detection performance. Shi et al.37 examined the MFPT and SR phenomenon of a tri-stable system with time-delayed feedback, which proved that time-delayed feedback not only affects the noise enhancement stability effect, but also helps to boost the detection ability of weak characteristic signals. While significant progress has been made in the areas of mono-stable, bistable and time-delay systems, research on quad-steady systems with time-delay remains scarce. Simultaneously, quad-stable systems are adapted to more complex environments in practical life. Therefore, the paper introduces quad-stable stochastic resonance with time-delay and applies it to the detection of bearing faults.

Bearing fault diagnosis

In practical engineering, the fault signal of rolling bearings is submerged in the noise background, which poses significant difficulties in diagnosing weak fault features. The fundamental principle of detecting signals in a SR system is the conversion of noise energy into the energy of faint signals, thereby achieving the goal of detecting faults and greatly improving the detection efficiency of bearings. This system is currently being utilized for bearing fault diagnosis. This paper will compare it with the classic bistable system and quad-stable system to effectively validate the effectiveness of the time-delay quad-stable system in practical engineering applications. Conclusion

In summary, this article investigates the dynamical behavior of a time-delay multi-steady SR system driven by white correlated noise, and efficiently applies it to bearing fault diagnosis. Firstly, based on Novikov’s theory and the steepest descent method, the SPD as well as MFPT of the system are obtained, respectively. The impacts of system parameters on the particle’s dynamical behavior are then studied. Next,according to the signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) formula proposed in Ref.41, the paper investigates the impact of system parameters on the SNR. It is found that the ease of SR occurrence is directly related to the system parameters. Finally, it is concluded that the time-delay quad-stable SR can clearly identify bearing fault signals. The inclusion of time-delay and feedback strength further amplifies the feature extraction capability of faults. The obtained results can provide a multi-stable dynamic model for bearing fault detection. This study is carried out under the framework of multi-stable theory and time-delay, which poses challenges to the theoretical basis. Therefore, future research on adaptive time-delay multi-stable systems remains to be explored.

On the almost algebraicity of groups of automorphisms of connected Lie groups
S.G. Dani, Riddhi Shah

Abstract
. Let G G be a connected Lie group, C C be the maximal compact connected subgroup of the center of G G , and let Aut ⁡ ( G ) {\rm Aut}(G) denote the group of Lie automorphisms of G G , viewed, canonically, also as a subgroup of GL ⁡ ( 𝔊 ) {\rm GL}(\mathfrak{G}) , where 𝔊 \mathfrak{G} is the Lie algebra of G G . It is known that when C C is trivial Aut ⁡ ( G ) {\rm Aut}(G) is almost algebraic, in the sense that it is of finite index in an algebraic subgroup of GL ⁡ ( 𝔊 ) {\rm GL}(\mathfrak{G}) , and in particular has only finitely many connected components. In this paper we analyse the situation further in this respect, with C C possibly nontrivial, and describe necessary and sufficient conditions for almost algebraicity to hold; the criteria are in terms of the group of restrictions of automorphisms of G G to C C , and the abelian quotient Lie group G / [ G , G ] ¯ ​ C G/\overline{[G,G]}C . For the class of Lie groups which admit a finite-dimensional representation with discrete kernel, a more specific criterion for Aut ⁡ ( G ) {\rm Aut}(G) to be almost algebraic is obtained, while in the general case a variety of patterns are illustrated through examples. Along the way we also study almost algebraicity of subgroups of Aut ⁡ ( G ) {\rm Aut}(G) fixing each point of a given torus in G G , containing C C , which also turns out to be of independent interest.

1.
Introduction

Let
G
be a connected Lie group, and
\mathfrak{G}
be the Lie algebra of
G
. We denote by
{\rm Aut}(G)
the group of all Lie automorphisms of
G
,
which we realize as a subgroup of
{\rm GL}({\mathfrak{G}})
, the general linear group of
\mathfrak{G}
over
\mathbb{R}
, by identifying each automorphism with its
differential on
\mathfrak{G}
. A subgroup of
{\rm GL}({\mathfrak{G}})
is said to be an algebraic subgroup if it is of the form
\{\xi\in{\rm GL}({\mathfrak{G}})\mid p(\xi)=0\}
,
where
p
is a function on
{\rm GL}({\mathfrak{G}})
which is a polynomial in the entries of
\xi
and
(\det\xi)^{-1}
, in a matrix representation with respect
to a basis of
\mathfrak{G}
. A subgroup of
{\rm GL}({\mathfrak{G}})
is said to be
almost algebraic
if it is a subgroup of finite index in an algebraic subgroup.

It is of interest in various contexts to know whether
{\rm Aut}(G)
, and various subgroups of it defined intrinsically, are algebraic or almost algebraic;
see
[
6
]
.
{\rm Aut}(G)
being almost algebraic implies in particular that it is almost connected, viz. it has only finitely connected components. On the other hand, by
a theorem of Wigner
[
12
]
(see also
[
5
]
and
[
10
]
) it is known that the connected component of the identity in
{\rm Aut}(G)
is
almost algebraic; thus
{\rm Aut}(G)
is almost algebraic if and only if it is almost connected. Various conditions are known which
ensure this; in particular it holds when the center of
G
does not contain a compact subgroup of positive
dimension (see
[
5
]
and
[
10
]
for the latter result, and
[
6
]
for a broader perspective on the topic, and other related references).

Here we explore the theme further. Let
C
denote the unique maximal compact connected subgroup of the center of
G
. We note that
C
is a torus; let
d\geq 0
be the dimension of
C
. Clearly
C
is invariant under all
\alpha\in{\rm Aut}(G)
, and we get a natural homomorphism of
\rho:{\rm Aut}(G)\to{\rm Aut}(C)
, assigning to each automorphism of
G
its restriction to
C
.
We note that
{\rm Aut}(C)
is isomorphic to
{\rm GL}(d,\mathbb{Z})
, the group of
d\times d
matrices with integer entries and determinant
\pm 1
; it is a countable group, and it is infinite when
d\geq 2
. For
{\rm Aut}(G)
to be almost algebraic it has to be almost connected, and hence the image
\im\rho
of
\rho
has to be finite. Thus when
d\geq 2
, in general
{\rm Aut}(G)
need not be almost algebraic, as seen in particular when
G=\mathbb{T}^{d}
, a torus of dimension
d\geq 2
or, more generally, a connected abelian Lie group containing
\mathbb{T}^{d}
.
d\geq 2
, as a subgroup.

While the condition that
\im\rho
be finite is a necessary condition, it does not ensure almost algebraicity of
{\rm Aut}(G)
; see the example of the ‘Walnut group’ in § 7.2. In this paper we describe another necessary condition, and prove that the two conditions together are sufficient to ensure that
{\rm Aut}(G)
is almost algebraic.

The other necessary condition arises as follows. Any (continuous) homomorphism
\psi:G\to C
whose restriction to
C
is the identity map, induces an automorphism
\alpha
of
G
defined by
\alpha(g)=g\psi(g)
for all
g\in G
. We call the automorphisms arising in this way
spiral shear automorphisms
(see § 5 for details); the term is inspired by the fact that analogously defined automorphisms with the center
Z
of
G
in place of
C
have been called shear automorphisms (see
[
5
]
,
[
6
]
). The spiral shear automorphisms form a closed subgroup of
{\rm Aut}(G)
which we shall denote by
{\rm Sps}(G)
. We show that for
{\rm Aut}(G)
to be almost connected, it is necessary that
{\rm Sps}(G)
be connected; cf. Theorem
5.2
. It also turns out that the latter condition holds if and only if either
C
is trivial or
G/\overline{[G,G]}C
is simply connected; see Remark
5.1
.

Our main result may now be stated as follows:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
G
be a connected Lie group and
C
be the maximal torus of the center of
G
. Then
{\rm Aut}(G)
is almost algebraic if and only if the following conditions are satisfied:

i)

The subgroup of
{\rm Aut}(C)

consisting of restrictions of automorphisms of
G
to
C
is a finite group, and

ii)

Either
C
is trivial or
G/\overline{[G,G]}C
is simply connected.

Now let
R
be the radical of
G
and
S
be a semisimple Levi subgroup of
G
; (the statements made in the sequel in this regard do not depend on the choice of the semisimple Levi subgroup, they being conjugates of each other in
G
). With regard to the condition of
G/\overline{[G,G]}C
being simply connected, as in (ii) above, we note the following (proof in § 6).

Proposition 1.2
.

Suppose that
S\cap R
is finite. Then
G/\overline{[G,G]}C
is simply connected if and only if
C
is a maximal torus of
R
.

Under an additional condition on the Lie group
G
we deduce from the theorem a characterization for almost algebraicity, directly in terms of the structure of the Lie group (see Corollary
1.3
). A Lie group
G
is said to be of

class

\mathcal{C}
if it admits a representation
\rho:G\to{\rm GL}(V)
, where

is a finite-dimensional vector space over
\mathbb{R}
, such that

\ker\rho
is discrete; the class includes in particular the linear Lie groups, as well as all simply connected Lie groups and all semisimple Lie
groups.
It is known (see
[
8
]
, Proposition 2.5) that

is of class
\mathcal{C}
if and only if
[R,R]
is a closed simply connected (nilpotent) Lie subgroup, and also, if and only if
\overline{[R,R]}\cap Z
, where

is the
center of

, has no compact subgroup of positive dimension.

Corollary 1.3
.

Let
G
and
C
be as in Theorem
1.1
and suppose that
G
is of class
\mathcal{C}
.
Then we have the following:

1.

is almost algebraic if and only if either
(i)
C
is trivial, or (ii)

is one-dimensional and

is simply connected.

2. If, moreover,

is finite then

is almost algebraic if and only if either
(i)

When the Lie group

has finite center and hence

Remark 1.4
.

It may be noted that

considered here, including in the case of linear Lie groups, consists of all continuous (and hence analytic) automorphisms of

In the course of proof of Theorem

we also consider the question of almost algebraicity of subgroups of

which fix

. Consideration of this issue was motivated by the study in

For a torus

the

consisting of all

such that

for all

. We prove the following.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let

and

be as in Theorem

, and let

be a torus in

containing

. Then

is trivial or

In particular, if

is almost algebraic. Also, if

is finite and

We note that for a torus

The paper is organized as follows. In § 2 we introduce notations and discuss some preliminaries. § 3 is devoted to the proof of the sufficiency parts of Theorems

and

. In § 4

We now suppose that

Proposition 3.1

For any connected Lie subgroup

In particular

Proof.

As

4.
Structure of the automorphism group

In this section we prove some results on the structure of the automorphism groups of Lie groups which will be used in the sequel. We follow the notations introduced earlier.
We begin by recalling the following useful fact.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
H
be any closed connected subgroup of
G
. Then
{\rm Int}_{[H,H]}(G)
is an almost algebraic subgroup.
In particular it is a closed subgroup, and
{\rm Int}_{\overline{[H,H]}}(G)={\rm Int}_{[H,H]}(G)
.

Proof.

The first statement is immediate from
[
4
]
, Ch. II, Theorem 15. It is clear that subgroups that are almost algebraic, are closed subgroups,
and therefore the last statement in the theorem holds. ∎

In particular
{\rm Int}_{[G,G]}(G)
is an almost algebraic group; it may be noted that in general
{\rm Int}_{G}(G)
need not be almost algebraic. For example, if
G
is the semidirect product of the one-parameter subgroup
\{{\rm diag}\,(e^{\lambda_{1}t},\dots,e^{\lambda_{n}t})\mid t\in\mathbb{R}\}
with
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
n\geq 2
, then
{\rm Int}_{G}(G)
is almost algebraic if and only if
(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n})
is a scalar multiple of an integral vector.

The following result is a generalization of Lemma 3 in
[
5
]
, together with a strengthening (see Remark
4.3
below).

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
H
be a closed connected subgroup of
G

and
T
be a maximal torus in
H
. Then

{\rm Aut}_{H}(G)={\rm Int}_{{[H.H]}}(G)\cdot({\rm Aut}_{T}(G)\cap{\rm Aut}_{H}(G)).

Proof.

Consider
\alpha\in{\rm Aut}_{H}(G)
. Then
T
and
\alpha(T)
are maximal tori in
\overline{[H.H]}T
. As the latter is a connected
Lie group, the two subgroups are conjugate in
\overline{[H.H]}T
; thus there exists
h\in\overline{[H.H]}
such that

\alpha(T)=hTh^{-1}=\sigma_{h}(T)
. Let
\tau=\sigma_{h}^{-1}\circ\alpha
. Then
\tau\in{\rm Aut}_{T}(G)\cap{\rm Aut}_{H}(G)
and
\alpha=\sigma_{h}\circ\tau
.
As
\sigma_{h}\in{\rm Int}_{\overline{[H,H]}}(G)={\rm Int}_{[H,H]}(G)
,
by Theorem
4.1
, this proves the Lemma. ∎

Remark 4.3
.

When
H=G
the Lemma reduces to
{\rm Aut}(G)={\rm Int}_{{[G.G]}}(G)\cdot{\rm Aut}_{T}(G)
. Such a decomposition was noted in

[
5
]
with respect to a chosen subgroup containing
[G,G]
, denoted there by
H
, in place of
{[G,G]}
in the above statement; see also a
clarification/correction given in
[
10
]
towards ascertaining the validity of the statement. As
{[G,G]}\subset H
the present assertion is stronger than Lemma 3 in
[
5
]
, even in the special case with
H=G
.

Corollary 4.4
.

Let
H
be a closed connected subgroup of
G
and
T
be a maximal torus in
H
. Let
\mathcal{H}
be a subgroup
of
{\rm Aut}_{H}(G)
containing
{\rm Int}_{[H,H]}(G)
. Then
\mathcal{H}
is almost algebraic if and only if
{\rm Aut}_{T}(G)\cap\mathcal{H}
is almost algebraic.

Proof.

By Lemma
4.2

{\mathcal{H}}={\rm Int}_{[H,H]}(G)\cdot({\rm Aut}_{T}(G)\cap{\mathcal{H}})
and by Theorem
4.1

{\rm Int}_{[H,H]}(G)
is almost algebraic. Therefore it follows that if
{\rm Aut}_{T}(G)\cap\mathcal{H}
is almost algebraic then
\mathcal{H}
is almost algebraic.
Now suppose that
\mathcal{H}
is almost algebraic. We note that
{\rm Aut}_{T}(G)\cap\mathcal{H}
consists precisely of all
\tau\in\mathcal{H}
such that

\tau(T)=T
, and the latter condition is equivalent to
d\tau({\mathfrak{T}})={\mathfrak{T}}
, where
\mathfrak{T}
is the Lie subalgebra
corresponding to
T
and
d\tau
is the differential of
\tau
. This shows that
{\rm Aut}_{T}(G)\cap\mathcal{H}
is almost algebraic. ∎

Corollary 4.5
.

Let
T
be a maximal torus in
G
and let
N_{[G,G]}(T)
and
Z_{[G,G]}(T)
denote respectively the normalizer and centralizer of
T
in
[G,G]
. Then
N_{[G,G]}(T)/Z_{[G,G]}(T)
is finite. In particular if
g\in[G,G]
is such that
T
is
\sigma_{g}
-invariant, then every orbit of the
\sigma_{g}
-action on
T
is finite.

Proof.

For any
g\in N_{[G,G]}(T)
we get canonically an automorphism from
{\rm Int}_{[G,G]}(G)\cap{\rm Aut}_{T}(G)
and the automorphism is trivial if and only if
g\in Z_{[G,G]}(T)
. Since
{\rm Aut}(T)
is countable the action of any
\tau\in({\rm Int}_{[G,G]}(G)\cap{\rm Aut}_{T}(G))^{0}
on
T
is trivial.
Thus the group
N_{[G,G]}(T)/Z_{[G,G]}(T)
is isomorphic to a quotient of
({\rm Int}_{[G,G]}(G)\cap{\rm Aut}_{T}(G))/({\rm Int}_{[G,G]}(G)\cap{\rm Aut}_{T}(G))^{0}
.
From Corollary
4.4
, choosing
H=G
and
{\mathcal{H}}={\rm Int}_{[G,G]}(G)
we get that
{\rm Int}_{[G,G]}(G)\cap{\rm Aut}_{T}(G)

is almost algebraic, and hence
({\rm Int}_{[G,G]}(G)\cap{\rm Aut}_{T}(G))/({\rm Int}_{[G,G]}(G)\cap{\rm Aut}_{T}(G))^{0}
is finite. Therefore
N_{[G,G]}(T)/Z_{[G,G]}(T)
is finite.
The second assertion follows immediately from the first.
∎

We note that in general
N_{G}(T)/Z_{G}(T)
need not be finite. Consider the semidirect product
G=P\cdot T
where
P
is a one-parameter subgroup
\{P(t)\mid t\in\mathbb{R}\}
,
T
is the torus
\{(z_{1},z_{2})\in\mathbb{C}^{2}\mid|z_{1}|=|z_{2}|=1\}
, with the action of
P
on
T
given by
P(t)(z_{1},z_{2})=(z_{1},e^{i\alpha t}z_{2})
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
and
z_{1},z_{2}\in T
, where
\alpha
is an irrational number.
It is readily seen that,
N_{G}(T)=G
and
Z_{G}(T)=T
, so
N_{G}(T)/Z_{T}(G)
is infinite, in fact, isomorphic to
\mathbb{R}
.

Corollary 4.6
.

Let
\tau\in{\rm Aut}(G)^{0}
and
T
be maximal torus invariant under the action of
\tau
. Then the orbits of the
\tau
-action on
T
are finite.

Proof.

By Lemma
4.2
we have
{\rm Aut}(G)={\rm Int}_{{[G.G]}}(G)\cdot{\rm Aut}_{T}(G)
, and hence
{\rm Aut}(G)^{0}={\rm Int}_{{[G.G]}}(G)\cdot{\rm Aut}_{T}(G)^{0}
.
Hence there exist
g\in[G,G]
and
\alpha\in{\rm Aut}_{T}(G)^{0}
such that
\tau=\sigma_{g}\circ\alpha
. As
T
is invariant under
\tau
and
\alpha
, it is also invariant under
\sigma_{g}
. Moreover, since
\alpha\in{\rm Aut}_{T}(G)^{0}
, its action on
T
is trivial. Therefore the orbits of
\tau
and
\sigma_{g}
on
T
coincide, and hence by Corolllary
4.5
they are finite. ∎

5.
Spiral shear automorphisms

As before,
G
is a connected Lie group and
C
the maximal torus of the center of
G
.
Recall that
{\rm Sps}(G)
denotes the group of all spiral shear automorphisms of
G
; (see § 1). Let
A=G/\overline{[G,G]}C
, which is a connected abelian Lie group. Let
{\rm Hom}(A,C)
denote the group of all continuous homomorphisms from
A
to
C
. For each
\varphi\in{\rm Hom}(A,C)
we have a spiral shear automorphism given by
\Theta(\varphi)(g)=g\varphi(g\overline{[G,G]}C)
for all
g\in G
, which we shall denote by
\Theta(\varphi)
. We note also that if
\psi:G\to C
is a continuous homomorhphism whose restriction to
C
is the identity map (in the way the transformations were introduced in the introduction) then
\psi
is trivial on

\overline{[G,G]}C
and factors to a homomorphism
\varphi:A\to C
as above.

We equip the group
{\rm Hom}(A,C)
with the compact open topology (corresponding to unifrom convergence on compact subsets). Let
\Theta:{\rm Hom}(A,C)\to{\rm Aut}(G)
be the map defined by
\varphi\mapsto\Theta(\varphi)
as above; its range is
{\rm Sps}(G)
. It is straightforward to verify that
\Theta
is a continuous injective homomorphism of the topological groups. Hence we get a homomorphism
\bar{\Theta}:{\rm Hom}(A,C)/{\rm Hom}(A,C)^{0}\to{\rm Aut}(G)/{\rm Aut}(G)^{0}
, induced by
\Theta
.

Remark 5.1
.

As a connected abelian Lie group
A
is a direct product
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{T}^{m}
,
n,m\geq 0
, where
\mathbb{T}=\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}
is the circle group. We see that
{\rm Hom}(A,C)
is topologically isomorphic to

\mathbb{R}^{nc}\times\mathbb{Z}^{mc}
, where
c
is the dimension of
C
. In particular, Hom
(A,C)
is connected if and only if
mc=0
, namely
if either
C
is trivial or
A
is
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. We also note that
{\rm Hom}(A,C)/{\rm Hom}(A,C)^{0}
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}^{mc}
, and if
mc>0
it has no nontrivial elements of finite order.

The following theorem is a crucial ingredient towards proving the “only if” part of Theorem
1.1
.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
K
be the maximal compact subgroup of
A
. Let
\varphi\in{\rm Hom}(A,C)
. Then
\Theta(\varphi)
is contained in
{\rm Aut}(G)^{0}
if and only if
\varphi(K)
is the trivial subgroup. Consequently,

(i)
\bar{\Theta}:{\rm Hom}(A,C)/{\rm Hom}(A,C)^{0}\to{\rm Aut}(G)/{\rm Aut}(G)^{0}
is injective, and

(ii) if
C
and
K
are nontrivial then there exists

\alpha\in{\rm Sps}(G)
such that
\alpha^{n}
is not contained in
{\rm Aut}(G)^{0}
for any
n\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Proof.

From the structure theory of connected
abelian Lie groups we get that
{\rm Hom}(A,C)^{0}
consists precisely of
\varphi\in{\rm Hom}\,(A,C)
such that

\varphi(K)
is trivial. Since
\Theta
is continuous, this implies that if
\varphi(K)
is trivial then
\Theta(\varphi)
is contained
in
{\rm Aut}(G)^{0}
.

Now consider any
\varphi\in{\rm Hom}(A,C)
such that
\varphi(K)
is nontrivial. Then
\varphi(K)
is a torus of positive dimension and there exists
k\in K
such that
\varphi(k)
is an element of infinite order in
C
. Let
T
be a maximal torus in
G
. As it contains
C
,

T
is invariant under the action of
\Theta(\varphi)
, and the
\Theta(\varphi)
-orbit of
t\in T
consists of
\{t\varphi(k)^{j}\mid j\in\mathbb{Z}\}
. Since
\varphi(k)
is of infinite order, the orbits are infinite. By Corollary
4.6
this implies that
\Theta(\varphi)
is not contained in
{\rm Aut}(G)^{0}
. This proves the initial assertion in the theorem.

As Hom
(A,C)^{0}
consists precisely of all
\varphi
such that
\varphi(K)
is trivial, the conclusion above means that for
\varphi\in{\rm Hom}(A,C)
,
\Theta(\varphi)\in{\rm Aut}(G)^{0}
if and only if
\varphi\in{\rm Hom}(A,C)^{0}
.
This proves (i). Now suppose that
C
and
K
are nontrivial. Then there exists
\varphi\in{\rm Hom}(A,C)
such that
\varphi^{n}\notin{\rm Hom}(A,C)^{0}
for any
n\in\mathbb{Z}
(cf. Remark
5.1
). Hence for
\alpha=\Theta(\varphi)\in{\rm Sps}(G)
,
\alpha^{n}
is not contained in
{\rm Aut}(G)^{0}
for any
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. This proves (ii).∎

6.
Proofs of the main results

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

As noted before, Corollary
3.3
establishes the “if” part. We now prove the converse. Suppose that
{\rm Aut}(G)
is almost algebraic. Then, as noted in the introduction, Condition (i) must hold since for the
homomorphism of
\rho:{\rm Aut}(G)\to{\rm Aut}(C)
, assigning to each automorphism of
G
its restriction to
C
,
\im\rho
has to be almost connected, and since
{\rm Aut}(C)
is countable it has to be finite. To complete the proof it suffices to prove that if
C
is nontrivial then
G/\overline{[G,G]C}
is simply connected. As
{\rm Aut}(G)
is almost connected,
for any
\alpha\in{\rm Aut}(G)
there exists
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\alpha^{n}\in{\rm Aut}(G)^{0}
. As
C
is assumed to be nontrivial, by
Theorem
5.2
this implies that
G/\overline{[G,G]}C
does not contain a torus of positive dimension; hence it is simply
connected. ∎

Proof of Proposition
1.2
.

Let
T
be a maximal torus in
G
. As
S\cap R
is finite,
T
can be expressed as

T=(T\cap S)(T\cap R)
. Thus if
C
is a maximal torus in
R
we get that
SC
contains a maximal torus in
G
and since
SC
is contained in

\overline{[G,G]}C
and the latter is a closed normal subgroup of
G
, it follows that

G/\overline{[G,G]}C
is simply connected.

Conversely suppose that
G/\overline{[G,G]}C
is simply connected. Recall that
SN
, where
N
is the nilradical, is a closed normal subgroup containing
\overline{[G,G]}C
(cf. Proposition
3.1
), and hence we get that
G/SN
is simply connected. Let
T
be a torus in
R
. As
G/SN
is simply connected, we then get that
T\subset SN
and since
T\subset R
this means that
T\subset N
. Since
C
is the unique maximal torus in
N
it follows that
T
is contained in
C
. Thus
C
is a maximal torus in
R
.∎

Towards our proof of Corollary
1.3
we first note the following:

Proposition 6.1
.

If
G
is of class
\mathcal{C}
, then
\overline{[G,G]}\cap R
contains no nontrivial torus.

Proof.

It is noted in Proposition 2.5 of
[
8
]
that when
G
is of class
\mathcal{C}
,
[R,R]

is a closed simply connected nilpotent Lie subgroup, and in particular contains no nontrivial compact subgroup; (the proof is by direct application of
Lie’s theorem). It therefore suffices to prove the statement for
G/[R,R]
(with
R/[R,R]
as its radical), in place of
G
and
R
. We may therefore
assume
R
to be abelian. Then
R
may be decomposed as
V\times T
, where
V
is a vector
group,
T
is a torus, and
V
and
T
are invariant under the conjugation action of the semisimple Levi subgroup
S
. We note that, as the automorphism group of

T
is countable, the action of
S
on
T
is trivial. It follows that
\overline{[G,G]}=SW
, where
W
is the vector subspace of
V
consisting
of the sum of all irreducible
S
-invariant subspaces of
V
on which the action is nontrivial. Thus
\overline{[G,G]}\cap R=(S\cap R)W
.
This shows that it contains no nontrivial torus. ∎

Proposition 6.2
.

Suppose that

\overline{[G,G]}\cap C
is finite. Then there exists a closed normal subgroup
G_{1}
of
G
such that
G
is an almost direct product of
G_{1}

and
C
.
The conclusion holds in particular if
G
is of class
\mathcal{C}
.

Proof.

Let
A=G/\overline{[G,G]}
and
\pi:G\to A
be the canonical quotient homomorphism. Then
\pi(C)
is a torus in
A
. As
A
is
an abelian Lie group there exists a closed subgroup
A_{1}
of
A
such that
A
is a direct product of
A_{1}
and
\pi(C)
. Let

G_{1}=\pi^{-1}(A_{1})
. Then
G=G_{1}C
. As
G_{1}\cap C=\overline{[G,G]}\cap C
is finite, it follows that
G
is
an almost direct product of
G_{1}
and
C
. The second assertion now follows from
Proposition
6.1
. ∎

Proof of Corollary
1.3
.

We follow the notation as in the statement. As recalled before, if
C
is trivial then
{\rm Aut}(G)

is almost algebraic (cf.
[
5
]
and
[
10
]
). When
C
is one-dimensional
{\rm Aut}(C)
is finite, and under the hypothesis that

G/\overline{[G,G]}C
is simply connected, Theorem
1.1
implies that
{\rm Aut}(G)
is almost algebraic.
Also, if
C
is nontrivial and
G/\overline{[G,G]}C
contains a nontrivial torus, then by Theorem
5.2

{\rm Aut}(G)
is not almost algebraic.
(It may be noted that the proof of the part so far does not involve the condition that
G
is of class
\mathcal{C}
). To prove assertion (1) in the statement it remains to show that when
G
is of
class
\mathcal{C}
, and
C
is of dimension greater than 1 then
{\rm Aut}(G)
is not almost algebraic.

Suppose that
G
is of class
\mathcal{C}
and
C
is of dimension greater than
1
.
By Lemma
6.2
there exists a closed connected normal subgroup
G_{1}
of
G
such that
G
is an almost direct
product of
G_{1}
and
C
. For any
\tau\in{\rm Aut}(C)
which fixes
G_{1}\cap C
pointwise, we have a well-defined automorphism
\alpha\in{\rm Aut}(G_{1}\times C)
given by
\alpha(xc)=x\tau(c)
for all
x\in G_{1}
and
c\in C
. This shows that
\im\rho
(where
\rho:{\rm Aut}(G)\to{\rm Aut}(C)
is the restriction map as before) contains all
\tau\in{\rm Aut}(C)
which fix
G_{1}\cap C
pointwise. The latter is a subgroup of finite index in
{\rm Aut}(C)
and hence it is infinite, as
C
is of dimension greater than
1
. This shows that
{\rm Aut}(G)
is not almost algebraic, thus proving (1). Assertion (2) follows directly from assertion (1) and Proposition
1.2
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.5
.

Let
G
,
C
and
T
be as in the statement. If
C
is trivial then
{\rm Aut}(G)
is almost algebraic (cf.
[
5
]
and
[
10
]
), and hence
F_{T}(G)
is almost algebraic; see Remark
2.1
. In the case when
G/\overline{[G,G]}T
is simply connected Corollary
3.3
implies that
F_{T}(G)
is almost algebraic. This proves the “if” part of the theorem.

Now suppose that
C
is nontrivial and
G/\overline{[G,G]}T
is not simply connected. As in § 5 let
A=G/\overline{[G,G]}C
and let
K
be the maximal compact subgroup of
A
. Let
B=\overline{[G,G]}T/\overline{[G,G]}C
. As
G/\overline{[G,G]}T
is not simply connected,
K
is not contained in
B
. Since
C
is nontrivial we can get a
\varphi\in{\rm Hom}(A,C)
such that
\varphi(B)
is trivial and
\varphi(K)
is nontrivial. Let
\alpha=\Theta(\varphi)
, in the notation of § 5. Since
\varphi(B)
is trivial it follows that
\alpha\in F_{T}(G)
. On the other hand, since
\varphi(K)
is nontrivial, by Theorem
5.2

the image of
\alpha
in the quotient
{\rm Aut}(G)/{\rm Aut}(G)^{0}
is of infinite order. This shows that
F_{T}(G)
is not almost connected and hence not almost algebraic, which proves the only if part.

We note that if either
T
is a maximal torus in
G
, or
S\cap R
is finite and
T
is a maximal torus in
R
, then
G/\overline{[G,G]}T
is simply connected, which implies the remaining assertions.
∎

7.
Examples and concluding remarks

In this section we discuss some special classes of Lie groups, and exhibit examples bringing out the significance of various conditions involved in the results described.

7.1.
Automorphism groups of nilpotent Lie groups

To begin with consider any connected nilpotent Lie group
N
. Let
C
be the maximal compact subgroup of the center
of
N
. Then
C
is the maximal torus in
N
. Hence in this case by Corollary
3.3

F_{C}(N)
is almost algebraic.

Concerning algebraicity of
{\rm Aut}(N)
however there are several possibilities. Let
d
be the dimension of
C
. If
d\leq 1
then by Theorem
1.1

{\rm Aut}(G)
is
almost algebraic, since in this case
{\rm Aut}(C)
is finite and
N/C
is simply connected. Now suppose
d\geq 2
.
If
\overline{[N,N]}\cap C
is trivial, which holds in particular if
N
is abelian, then by Proposition
6.2

N
is a direct product of a simply connected nilpotent Lie group
N_{1}
and
C
; moreover, since any compact subgroup of
N_{1}
has
to be contained in
C
it follows that
N_{1}
is simply connected; in this case
N
is of class
\mathcal{C}
. As
d\geq 2
it follows from
Corollary
1.3
that
{\rm Aut}(N)
is not almost algebraic. Consider on the other hand the case
when
C
is contained in
\overline{[N,N]}
; then
N
not of class
\mathcal{C}
. We illustrate below, through examples, that in this case
either possibility, of being almost algebraic or otherwise, exists; the examples can be generalized to a larger class, using the theory of arithmetic groups, but here we content ourselves noting a special case.

7.1.1.
Examples of
N
such that
C\subset[N,N]
and
{\rm Aut}(N)
is not almost algebraic

Let
\mathfrak{L}
be the free 2-step nilpotent Lie algebra over the
\mathbb{R}
-vector space
V=\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
n\geq 2
; thus
\mathfrak{L}=V\oplus\wedge^{2}V
, and
[\mathfrak{L},\mathfrak{L}]=\wedge^{2}V
. Let
L
be the simply connected nilpotent Lie group associated with
\mathfrak{L}
. Then
[L,L]
is a vector group (isomorphic to
\mathbb{R}^{\frac{1}{2}n(n-1)}
) and constitutes the center of
L
. Let
\Lambda
be the lattice
\wedge^{2}\mathbb{Z}^{n}
in
\wedge^{2}V=[L,L]
, and
N
be the Lie group
L/\Lambda
.
Then
C=[L,L]/\Lambda
is the maximal torus in
N
and it is also the center of
N
. It can be seen that the group of restrictions of automorphisms of
N
to
C
is a countable infinite group isomorphic to
{\rm SL}(n,\mathbb{Z})
. Hence
{\rm Aut}(N)
is not almost algebraic.

7.1.2.
Examples of
N
with
C
of dimension greater that 1 for which

{\rm Aut}(N)
is almost algebraic

(Note that in the light of the discussion above for this to happen
N
has to be not of class
\mathcal{C}
, so
[N,N]\cap C
is nontrivial; we shall actually have
C\subset[N,N]
). These examples, with
k\geq 2
, show in particular that the “only if” parts in the conclusions (1) and (2) in Corollary
1.3
may not hold when the Lie group is not of class
\mathcal{C}
.

a)
Let
\mathbb{H}
be the 3-dimensional Heisenberg group, which is a simply connected nilpotent Lie group.
We denote by
Z
the one-dimensional center of
\mathbb{H}
. Let
\Lambda
be a lattice in
Z
. Let
k\geq 2
and
M=H/\Lambda
and
N=M^{k}
. Let
K=Z/\Lambda
; then
C=K^{k}
.
Since
K
is one-dimensional, by Theorem
1.1

{\rm Aut}(M)
is almost algebraic. To uphold almost algebraicity of
{\rm Aut}(N)
we show that
{\rm Aut}(N)
contains
({\rm Aut}(M))^{k}
as a subgroup of finite index, under the canonical identifications. Let
S
be the subset of
N
consisting of elements whose centralizer in

N
is of codimension
1
. It may be seen that
S
consists precisely of elements of the form
(0,\dots,m_{j},\dots 0)
, for some
j=1,\dots,k
, with
m_{j}\notin K
. It follows in particular that
S
has
k

connected components. Each automorphisms of

therefore leaves invariant each of the connected components of

We also recall here, as noted in

b)
Let

Let

is not an eigenvector for an eigenvaue

of any
\sigma\in{\rm GL}(W)

\Lambda_{0}
is any lattice in

g\in{\rm GL}(W)

a nilpotent Lie group and
\overline{[N,N]}
is the maximal compact (central) subgroup of

\mathbb{H}\times V
is a universal

Remark 7.2

7.1.3.
For a torus
T
not containing
C
,
F_{T}(N)
need not be almost algebraic

By Theorem
1.5
if
T
is a torus containing
C
,
F_{T}(N)
is almost algebraic whenever
N/\overline{[N,N]}C
is simply connected. This is not true in general for tori not containing
C
. Firstly it may be observed that if
T
is the trivial torus then
F_{T}(N)={\rm Aut}(N)
which, as we have noted, need not be almost algebraic. Examples of nontrivial
T
, not containing
C
, can be constructed using subtori of
C
invariant under actions of subgroups of
{\rm SL}(n,\mathbb{Z})
, as the group of restrictions. We illustrate this in the special case of tori invariant under the action of a unipotent element in
{\rm SL}(n,\mathbb{Z})
.

Let
u
be a unipotent element in
{\rm SL}(n,\mathbb{Z})
of rank 2 (viz.
(u-I)^{2}\neq 0
and
(u-I)^{m}=0
for some
2<m\leq n
, where
I
and
0
are the identity and zero matrices respectively) and
U
be the corresponding linear transformation of
V
. Then
\wedge^{2}U
is a unipotent linear transformation of
\wedge^{2}V=[L,L]
, of rank at least
2
. Let
W
be the set of fixed points of
\wedge^{2}U
. Then
W
is a proper nonzero subspace of
[L,L]
invariant under
\wedge^{2}U
, and the factor of
\wedge^{2}U
on
[L,L]/W
is of infinite order.
Moreover, as
u\in{\rm SL}(n,\mathbb{Z})
it follows that
W\cap\Lambda
is a lattice in
W
. Let
T=W\Lambda/\Lambda
. Then
T
is a proper nontrivial subtorus of
C
. We note that
F_{T}(N)
is not almost algebraic; otherwise the subgroup of
{\rm Aut}(N/T)
consisting of the factors of automorphisms of
N
would be almost algebraic and in turn restrictions of the factors to
C/T
would form a finite group; however this is not the case since for
\wedge^{2}U^{k}
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
, the corresponding automorphisms of
C/T
arrived at in this way are all distinct, as the factor of
\wedge^{2}U
on
[L,L]/W
is of infinite order. It may be noted that in this case the other condition as in Theorem
1.5
is satisfied, as
N/\overline{[N,N]}C=N/[N,N]
which is a vector space. These examples point to the need for restricting the tori
T
under consideration to those containing
C
.

7.2.
Automorphism groups of some semidirect products

In the previous subsection we considered automorphism groups of nilpotent Lie groups. In this case condition (ii) of Theorem
1.1
is automatically fulfilled and as such the relevance of the condition could not be tested through these. In this subsection we discuss examples of some simple solvable groups which illustrate the need for the condition.

Let
\mathbb{H}
be the 3-dimensional Heisenberg group,
Z
the center of
\mathbb{H}
, and
\Lambda
is a lattice in
Z
.
Let
N=\mathbb{H}/\Lambda
and
C=Z/\Lambda
. There is a canonical action of
{\rm SL}(2,\mathbb{R})
on
N
as a group of automorphisms, and the action
is trivial on
C
.
Now consider the Lie group
G={\rm SO}(2,\mathbb{R})\ltimes N
, where
{\rm SO}(2,\mathbb{R})
, the special orthogonal group; it is a maximal
compact connected one-dimensional subgroup of
{\rm SL}(2,\mathbb{R})
. We note that
G
is a connected solvable Lie group with

C
as its center; this group is called the
Walnut group
(see, for example,
[
7
]
). It is one of the simplest examples of a non-nilpotent
Lie group which is not of class
\mathcal{C}
. Now,
[G,G]=N
, so
G/\overline{[G,G]}C=G/N
which is isomorphic to
{\rm SO}(2,\mathbb{R})
. It is not simply connected and hence by Theorem
1.1

{\rm Aut}(G)
is not almost algebraic. We note that in this case
C
is one-dimensional and hence condition (i) of the theorem is satisfied. This highlights the relevance of condition (ii) for the theorem. We may also note that in this case
{\rm Hom}(G/N,C)
is
an infinite cyclic group, so
F_{C}(G)/F_{C}(G)^{0}
is infinite (cf. Theorem
5.2
) and in particular
F_{C}(G)
is not almost
algebraic; this illustrates the need for the condition in Theorem
1.5
that
G/\overline{[G,G]}T
be simply connected.

It may also be observed that if
H
is a noncompact closed connected subgroup of
{\rm SL}(2,\mathbb{R})
then it is either

{\rm SL}(2,\mathbb{R})
or a simply connected solvable subgroup (including possibly a noncompact one-parameter subgroup) of
{\rm SL}(2,\mathbb{R})
. In these cases for
G=H\ltimes N
it can be readily seen that
G/\overline{[G,G]}C
is simply connected and, since
{\rm Aut}(C)
is finite, by Theorem
1.1

{\rm Aut}(G)
is almost algebraic.

7.3.
Examples of Lie groups with
S\cap R
infinite

In Corolllary
1.3
(2) we obtained a stronger result on almost algebraicity of
{\rm Aut}(G)
under the assumption that
S\cap R
is finite. As mentioned earlier, while this condition is met in most situations that one encounters in literature, it does not always hold. This is seen from the following example, which provides also an example for which the conclusion as in Corollary
1.3
(2) does not hold when
S\cap R
is not finite.

Let
S
be the universal covering group of
{\rm SL}(2,\mathbb{R})
.
The center, say
D
, of
S
is an infinite cyclic group. Let
\psi:D\to\mathbb{Z}
be an isomorphism, with the latter viewed canonically as a subgroup of
\mathbb{R}
,
which for notational convenience we shall denote by
L
. We now define
G
to be the Lie group
(S\times L)/\Lambda
, where
\Lambda
is the
subgroup
\{(d,\psi(d))\mid d\in D\}
. Let
\phi:S\times L\to G
be the canonical quotient map. It is then easy to see that
\phi(L)
is the
radical of
G
and
\phi(S)
is a semisimple Levi subgroup; we note that the restrictions of
\phi
to the copies of
S
and
L
in the cartesian
product are embeddings. The intersection of
\phi(S)
and
\phi(L)
is

\phi(D\times\psi(D))
, which is an infinite cyclic group. It can be seen
that
G
has a one-dimensional maximal torus, which is neither contained in the radical
\phi(R)
, nor in any semisimple Levi subgroup
\phi(S)
. For this group
G

itself,
{\rm Aut}(G)
is almost algebraic, as the center does not contain any torus of positive dimension. On the other hand consider
G^{\prime}=G\times K
,
with
G
as above and
K
the circle group; we note that
K
is then the maximal torus of the center of
G^{\prime}
. Then
G^{\prime}/\overline{[G^{\prime},G^{\prime}]}K
is isomorphic to
L/\phi(D)
which is not simply connected and hence it follows from Theorem
1.1
that
{\rm Aut}(G^{\prime})
is not almost algebraic. This shows that the statement in Corollary
1.3
(2) may not hold when
S\cap R
is infinite. As
{\rm Aut}(G^{\prime})
is not almost algebraic and
{\rm Aut}(K)
is finite, it follows that
F_{K}(G^{\prime})
is not almost algebraic. As
K
is a maximal torus in the radical of
G^{\prime}

this shows the need for the condition of finiteness of
S\cap R
, in the last statement in Theorem
1.5
.

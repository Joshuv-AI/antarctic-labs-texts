Several-variable Kronecker limit formula over global function fields
Fu-Tsun Wei

Abstract
. We establish Kronecker-type first and second limit formulas for “non-holomorphic” and “Jacobi-type” Eisenstein series over global function fields in the several-variable setting. Our main theorem demonstrates that the derivatives of these Eisenstein series can be understood as averaged integrals of certain period quantities along the associated “Heegner cycles” on Drinfeld modular varieties. A key innovation lies in our use of the Berkovich analytic structure of the Drinfeld period domains, which enables the parametrization of the Heegner cycles in question by Euclidean “parallelepiped” regions. This approach also facilitates a unified and streamlined formulation and proof of our results. Finally, we apply these formulas to provide period interpretations of the “Kronecker terms” of Dedekind–Weil zeta functions and Dirichlet L L -functions associated with ring and ray class characters.

1.
Introduction

In the classical setting, the story of “second terms” can be traced back to the study of the logarithmic derivative of the Riemann zeta function at
s=0
.
Concretely,
let

\zeta(s)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{1}{n^{s}}\ \left(=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{0\neq n\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{1}{|n|^{s}}\right),\quad\operatorname{Re}(s)>1.

Extending
\zeta(s)
meromorphically to the complex
s
-plane, one knows that (see
[
26
, Corollary 9.13]
)

\zeta(0)=-\frac{1}{2}

and

\zeta^{\prime}(0)=-\frac{1}{2}\ln(2\pi)
,
which leads to the following equality

\frac{\zeta^{\prime}(0)}{\zeta(0)}=\ln(2\pi).

In geometric terms, this says that the logarithmic derivative of
\zeta(s)
at
s=0
matches the “log-length” of the period of the unit circle (which is isomorphic to
\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}
).

Turning to the rank
2
case, let
z\in\mathbb{C}
with
\im(z)>0
. Consider the non-holomorphic Eisenstein series

E(z,s)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\sum_{0\neq\lambda\in\mathbb{Z}z+\mathbb{Z}}\frac{\im(z)^{s}}{|\lambda|^{2s}},\quad\operatorname{Re}(s)>1.

Extending
E(z,s)
to a meromorphic function on the complex
s
-plane,
the celebrated Kronecker limit formula states that (see
[
26
, Theorem 9.11 and 9.14]
)

E(z,0)=-1\quad\text{ and }\quad\frac{\partial}{\partial s}E(z,s)\bigg|_{s=0}=-\ln\im(z)-\frac{1}{6}\ln|\Delta(z)|,

where
\Delta(z)=(2\pi)^{12}e^{2\pi iz}\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}(1-e^{2\pi inz})^{24}
is the
modular discriminant function
.
If
\mathcal{E}
is a “unit” elliptic curve over
\mathbb{C}
(i.e. of discriminant
1
) which is analytically isomorphic to the complex torus
\mathbb{C}/(\mathbb{Z}z+\mathbb{Z})
,
then the period lattice of
\mathcal{E}
is
\Lambda_{\mathcal{E}}=c\cdot(\mathbb{Z}z+\mathbb{Z})
for a complex number
c
with
c^{12}=\Delta(z)
.
Therefore the limit formula implies

\frac{E^{\prime}(z,0)}{E(z,0)}=\ln\big(D(\Lambda_{\mathcal{E}})\big),

where
D(\Lambda_{\mathcal{E}})\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=|c|^{2}\cdot\im(z)\ (=\text{vol}(\{a\cdot cz+b\cdot c\mid 0\leq a,b\leq 1\}))
is the co-volume of the period lattice
\Lambda_{\mathcal{E}}
of the unit elliptic curve
\mathcal{E}
analytically isomorphic to
\mathbb{C}/(\mathbb{Z}z+\mathbb{Z})
.

From this perspective, one sees that the Kronecker (first) limit formula admits a natural interpretation in terms of “periods.” Along with the second limit formula for “Jacobi-type” Eisenstein series (see
[
34
]
), these formulas and their generalizations play a pivotal role in the arithmetic of modular forms and elliptic curves, contributing to various significant developments in number theory (see
[
2
]
,
[
6
]
,
[
8
]
,
[
7
]
,
[
22
]
,
[
27
]
,
[
39
]
, and
[
40
]
, etc.).

The main purpose of this paper is to revisit the Kronecker limit formula over global function fields within the framework of “Berkovich context” and to explore the aforementioned phenomena in a several-variable setting. By leveraging the Berkovich analytic structure of the Drinfeld period domain, our approach not only simplifies a range of analytic technicalities but also provides fresh insights and a unified perspective on these formulas.

The

.

1.2.4.
Historical development in the function field setting.

Previous investigations of the Kronecker limit formula in the function field setting have largely been confined to the one-variable case. The study originated with Gekeler’s work, where he established a connection between an “improper” Eisenstein series on
\GL_{2}(k_{\infty})
over rational function fields and Drinfeld discriminant functions
[
13
]
. Subsequently, Pál
[
29
, Section 4]
extended Gekeler’s ideas to a special family of rank-
2
modular units.

For higher rank, Kondo
[
24
]
and Kondo–Yasuda
[
25
]
developed the second limit formula by investigating Jacobi-type Eisenstein series on
\mathrm{GL}_{r}(\mathbb{A}_{k})
, where
\mathbb{A}_{k}
is the adèle ring of the global function field
k
. A conceptual link between these (one-variable) Eisenstein series and the automorphic “mirabolic” Eisenstein series was then illustrated in
[
37
]
for rank 2 and in
[
38
]
for higher rank, providing a comprehensive account of the phenomenon in the one-variable setting.

By contrast, the present paper is the first to address the several-variable context. Our main theorem significantly broadens the “Kronecker term” phenomenon by offering a period-theoretic interpretation that applies in a more general, several-variable framework.

1.3.
Strategy of the proof

To prove Theorem
1.2.1
, we first translate the “rigid-analytic” version of the one-variable Kronecker limit formula for arbitrary rank
r
in
[
38
, (4.1) and (4.2)]
into the “Berkovich” setting (see Theorem
5.1.5
).
For the case of several-variable, the most crucial step is to express the Eisenstein series
\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{O}}^{\mathcal{L}}(\boldsymbol{z},s)
and
\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{O}}^{\mathcal{L}}(\boldsymbol{z},\boldsymbol{w},s)
in question in terms of integrals of the corresponding one-variable Eisenstein series along the Heegner cycles
\mathfrak{Z}_{\mathcal{L}}(\boldsymbol{z})
and
\mathfrak{Z}_{\mathcal{L}}(\boldsymbol{z};\mathfrak{C}_{\boldsymbol{w}})
, respectively.
Specifically as shown in Theorem
6.1.11
and
6.2.2
, we have:

\displaystyle\mathfrak{d}(\mathcal{O}/A)^{\frac{rs}{2}}\cdot\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{O}}^{\mathcal{L}}(\boldsymbol{z},s)

\displaystyle=\frac{(rs)^{m-1}}{q_{\mathcal{O}}-1}\cdot\frac{n_{1}\cdots n_{m}}{n}\cdot\int_{\mathfrak{T}(\mathcal{O})}\mathbb{E}^{Y_{\mathcal{L}}}\big(g_{\circ,r}\cdot{\bf z}(\boldsymbol{z};{\bf t}),s\big)d\mathbf{t},

\displaystyle\mathfrak{d}(\mathcal{O}/A)^{\frac{rs}{2}}\cdot\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{L}}_{\mathcal{O}}(\boldsymbol{z},\boldsymbol{w},s)

\displaystyle=(rs)^{m-1}\cdot\frac{n_{1}\cdots n_{m}}{n}\cdot\int_{\mathfrak{T}(\mathcal{O}^{(1)}_{\mathfrak{C}_{\boldsymbol{w}}})}\mathbb{E}^{Y_{\mathcal{L}}}\big(g_{\circ,r}\cdot\mathbf{z}(\boldsymbol{z};\mathbf{t}),w,s\big)d\mathbf{t}.

Recall that a

\nu(u_{1}+u_{2})\leq\max\big(\nu(u_{1}),\nu(u_{2})\big)

Suppose

\nu(a_{1}u_{1}+\cdots+a_{r}u_{r})=\max\big(\nu(a_{i}u_{i})\mid 1\leq i\leq r\big),\quad\forall a_{1},...,a_{r}\in F.

Denote by
\mathcal{X}^{r}(F)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\mathcal{N}^{r}(F)/\!\!\sim
the set of equivalence classes of all the norms on
F^{r}
.
It is known that
\mathcal{X}^{r}(F)
can be identified with the realization of the Bruhat–Tits building associated with
\operatorname{PGL}_{r}(F)
, see
[
31
, Theorem 2.22]
.

2.2.
Integral formula of the product of norms

let
r_{1},...,r_{m}
be positive integers. Given
\nu=(\nu^{(1)},\cdots\hskip-0.85355pt,\nu^{(m)})\in\prod_{i=1}^{m}\mathcal{N}^{r_{i}}(F)
and
\mathbf{t}=(t_{1},\cdots\hskip-0.85355pt,t_{m})\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
,
define a norm
\nu(\mathbf{t})
on
\prod_{i=1}^{m}F^{r_{i}}
by

\nu(\mathbf{t})(x_{1},\cdots\hskip-0.85355pt,x_{m})\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\max\Big(\exp(t_{i})\nu^{(i)}(x_{i})\ \Big|\ 1\leq i\leq m\Big),\quad\forall(x_{1},\cdots\hskip-0.85355pt,x_{m})\in\prod_{i=1}^{m}F^{r_{i}}.

Here
\exp(t)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}t^{n}/n!
is the usual exponential function on
\mathbb{R}
.
For
\mathbf{t}=(t_{1},\cdots\hskip-0.85355pt,t_{m})\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
, the above construction gives an embedding from
\prod_{i=1}^{m}\mathcal{N}^{r_{i}}(F)
to
\mathcal{N}^{r}(F)
, where
r=r_{1}+\cdots+r_{m}
.
Note that the identification
\prod_{i=1}^{m}F^{r_{i}}=F^{r}
also induces an embedding from
\prod_{i=1}^{m}\GL_{r_{i}}(F)
into
\GL_{r}(F)
.
It can be checked that the embedding
\big(\nu\mapsto\nu(\mathbf{t})\big)

from
\prod_{i=1}^{m}\mathcal{N}^{r_{i}}(F)
to
\mathcal{N}^{r}(F)
is equivariant under the action of
\prod_{i=1}^{m}\GL_{r_{i}}(F)
for each
\mathbf{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
.
Moreover, one has that

D\big(\nu(\mathbf{t})\big)=\prod_{i=1}^{m}\exp(r_{i}t_{i})D(\nu^{(i)}),\quad\forall\mathbf{t}=(t_{1},\cdots\hskip-0.85355pt,t_{m})\in\mathbb{R}^{m}.

In the following, we derive an integral formula for a particular product of norms
\nu^{(1)}
,…,
\nu^{(m)}
, which is a key technical aspect of this paper.

Proposition 2.2.1
.

Let
r_{1},...,r_{m}
be positive integers and put
r=r_{1}+\cdots+r_{m}
.
Given a tuple of norms
\nu=(\nu^{(1)},\cdots\hskip-0.85355pt,\nu^{(m)})\in\prod_{i=1}^{m}\mathcal{N}^{r_{i}}(F)
and
s\in\mathbb{C}
with
\operatorname{Re}(s)>0
,
we have that for every
(x_{1},\cdots\hskip-0.85355pt,x_{m})\in\prod_{i=1}^{m}F^{r_{i}}=F^{r}
with
\nu^{(i)}(x_{i})\neq 0
for
1\leq i\leq m
, the following equality holds:

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}/d(\mathbb{R})}\frac{D\big(\nu(\mathbf{t})\big)^{s}}{\nu(\mathbf{t})(x_{1},\cdots\hskip-0.85355pt,x_{m})^{rs}}d\mathbf{t}=s^{1-m}\cdot\frac{r}{r_{1}\cdots r_{m}}\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{m}\frac{D(\nu^{(i)})^{s}}{\nu^{(i)}(x_{i})^{r_{i}s}}

Here

d(\mathbb{R})\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\{(t,\cdots\hskip-0.85355pt,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\mid t\in\mathbb{R}\}
,
and the measure
d\mathbf{t}
on the quotient space
\mathbb{R}^{m}/d(\mathbb{R})
is induced from the standard Lebesgue measures on
\mathbb{R}^{m}
and
\mathbb{R}
.

Proof.

We shall prove the result by induction on
m
.
The case when
m=1
holds clearly.
Suppose
m\geq 2
and the statement holds for
m-1
.
For
\mathbf{t}^{\prime}=(t_{2},\cdots\hskip-0.85355pt,t_{m})\in\mathbb{R}^{m-1}
, let

\nu^{\prime}(\mathbf{t}^{\prime})(x_{2},\cdots\hskip-0.85355pt,x_{m})\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\max\Big(\exp(t_{i})\nu^{(i)}(x_{i})\mid 2\leq i\leq m\Big),\quad\forall(x_{2},\cdots\hskip-0.85355pt,x_{m})\in\prod_{i=2}^{m}F^{r_{i}}.

Put
r^{\prime}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=r_{2}+\cdots+r_{m}
.
Then for
(x_{1},\cdots\hskip-0.85355pt,x_{m})\in\prod_{i=1}^{m}F^{r_{i}}
with
\nu^{(i)}(x_{i})\neq 0
,
1\leq i\leq m
, the identification
\big(\mathbf{t}^{\prime}\leftrightarrow(0,\mathbf{t}^{\prime})\big)
between
\mathbb{R}^{m-1}
and
\mathbb{R}^{m}/d(\mathbb{R})
gives us that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}/d(\mathbb{R})}\frac{D\big(\nu(\mathbf{t})\big)^{s}}{\nu(\mathbf{t})(x_{1},\cdots\hskip-0.85355pt,x_{m})^{rs}}d\mathbf{t}\ =\ \int_{\mathbb{R}^{m-1}}\frac{D(\nu^{(1)})^{s}\cdot D\big(\nu^{\prime}(\mathbf{t}^{\prime})\big)^{s}}{\max\big(\nu^{(1)}(x_{1}),\nu^{\prime}(\mathbf{t}^{\prime})(x^{\prime})\big)^{rs}}d\mathbf{t}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle D(\nu^{(1)})^{s}\cdot\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m-1}/d(\mathbb{R})}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\max\Big(\nu^{(1)}(x_{1}),\exp(t)\nu^{\prime}(\mathbf{t}^{\prime})(x^{\prime})\Big)^{-rs}e^{r^{\prime}ts}dt\right)D\big(\nu^{\prime}(\mathbf{t}^{\prime})\big)^{s}d\mathbf{t}^{\prime},

where
x^{\prime}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=(x_{2},\cdots\hskip-0.85355pt,x_{m})\in\prod_{i=2}^{m}F^{r_{i}}=F^{r^{\prime}}
.
Put
c=\ln\nu^{(1)}(x_{1})-\ln\nu^{\prime}(\mathbf{t}^{\prime})(x^{\prime})
.
For
\operatorname{Re}(s)>0
, we get that

\displaystyle\hskip-85.35826pt\int_{\mathbb{R}}\max\Big(\nu^{(1)}(x_{1}),e^{t}\nu^{\prime}(\mathbf{t}^{\prime})(x^{\prime})\Big)^{-rs}e^{r^{\prime}ts}dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{c}^{\infty}\nu^{\prime}(\mathbf{t}^{\prime})(x^{\prime})^{-rs}e^{-r_{1}ts}dt+\int_{-\infty}^{c}\nu^{(1)}(x_{1})^{-rs}e^{r^{\prime}ts}dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nu^{(1)}(x_{1})^{-r_{1}s}\nu^{\prime}(\mathbf{t}^{\prime})(x^{\prime})^{-r^{\prime}s}\left(\frac{1}{r_{1}s}+\frac{1}{r^{\prime}s}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle s^{-1}\cdot\frac{r}{r_{1}\cdot r^{\prime}}\cdot\frac{1}{\nu^{(1)}(x_{1})^{r_{1}s}\cdot\nu^{\prime}(\mathbf{t}^{\prime})(x^{\prime})^{r^{\prime}s}}.

Hence

\displaystyle\hskip-113.81102pt\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}/d(\mathbb{R})}\frac{D\big(\nu(\mathbf{t})\big)^{s}}{\nu(\mathbf{t})(x_{1},\cdots\hskip-0.85355pt,x_{m})^{rs}}d\mathbf{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle s^{-1}\cdot\frac{r}{r_{1}\cdot r^{\prime}}\cdot\frac{D(\nu^{(1)})^{s}}{\nu^{(1)}(x_{1})^{r_{1}s}}\cdot\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m-1}/d(\mathbb{R})}\frac{D\big(\nu^{\prime}(\mathbf{t}^{\prime})\big)^{s}}{\nu^{\prime}(\mathbf{t}^{\prime})(x^{\prime})^{r^{\prime}s}}d\mathbf{t}^{\prime}

(induction hypothesis)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle s^{-1}\cdot\frac{r}{r_{1}\cdot r^{\prime}}\cdot\frac{D(\nu^{(1)})^{s}}{\nu^{(1)}(x_{1})^{r_{1}s}}\cdot\left(s^{2-m}\cdot\frac{r^{\prime}}{r_{2}\cdots r_{m}}\cdot\prod_{i=2}^{m}\frac{D(\nu^{(i)})^{s}}{\nu^{(i)}(x_{i})^{r_{i}s}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle s^{1-m}\cdot\frac{r}{r_{1}\cdots r_{m}}\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{m}\frac{D(\nu^{(i)})^{s}}{\nu^{(i)}(x_{i})^{r_{i}s}}.

Therefore the proof is complete by induction.
∎

2.3.
Restriction of scalars

Let
E
be a finite extension over
F
.
The absolute value of
F
extends uniquely to
E
, which is still denoted by
|\cdot|_{F}
(in order to distinguish with the normalized one
|\cdot|_{E}
on
E
). In particular, let
N_{E/F}:E\rightarrow F
be the norm map from
E
to
F
. Then

|\alpha|_{E}=|N_{E/F}(\alpha)|_{F},\quad\forall\alpha\in E.

Put
n=[E:F]
and
\mathfrak{d}(E/F)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=|\pi_{F}|_{F}^{f(e-1)}
, where
e
and
f
are the ramification index and the residue degree of
E/F
, respectively.
Note that
\pi_{F}^{f(e-1)}O_{F}
is equal to the discriminant ideal of
O_{E}/O_{F}
when
E/F
is tamely ramified.
Fix a uniformizer
\pi_{E}
of
E
and an element
\xi\in O_{E}
so that
\mathbb{F}_{E}=\mathbb{F}_{F}(\bar{\xi})
, where
\bar{\xi}
is the image of
\xi
in the residue field
\mathbb{F}_{E}
.
Then

(2.3.1)

O_{E}=\bigoplus_{0\leq i<f}\bigoplus_{0\leq j<e}O_{F}\cdot\xi^{i}\pi_{E}^{j}

\displaystyle\quad\text{ and }\quad\left|\sum_{0\leq i<f}\sum_{0\leq j<e}a_{ij}\xi^{i}\pi_{E}^{j}\right|_{F}

\displaystyle=\max\left(\big|a_{ij}\xi^{i}\pi_{E}^{j}\big|_{F}\ \Big|\ 0\leq i<f,\ 0\leq j<e\right)

(2.3.2)

\displaystyle=\max\left(|a_{ij}|_{F}\cdot q_{F}^{j/e}\ \Big|\ 0\leq i<f,\ 0\leq j<e\right),\quad\forall a_{ij}\in F.

Thus, the orthogonal
O_{F}
-base
\big\{\xi^{i}\pi_{E}^{j}\ \big|\ 0\leq i<f,\ 0\leq j<e\big\}
of
O_{E}
gives an isomorphism

(2.3.3)

\iota_{E/F}:E\mathrel{{\mathop{\rightarrow}\limits}}F^{n}

which extends to an isomorphism
\iota_{E/F,r}:E^{r}\mathrel{{\mathop{\rightarrow}\limits}}F^{nr}
for every
r\in\mathbb{N}
.

This isomorphism
induces an embedding

(2.3.4)

\text{res}:\mathcal{N}^{r}(E)\hookrightarrow\mathcal{N}^{nr}(F),

where
for every norm
\nu_{E}
in
\mathcal{N}^{r}(E)
and

(a_{ij\ell})_{0\leq i<f,0\leq j<e,1\leq\ell\leq r}\in F^{nr}
,
the norm
\text{res}(\nu_{E})
is defined by

\text{res}(\nu_{E})\Big((a_{ij\ell})_{0\leq i<f,0\leq j<e,1\leq\ell\leq r}\Big)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\nu_{E}\left(\sum_{0\leq i<f}\sum_{0\leq j<e}a_{ij1}\xi^{i}\pi_{E}^{j},\cdots,\sum_{0\leq i<f}\sum_{0\leq j<e}a_{ijr}\xi^{i}\pi_{E}^{j}\right)^{1/n}.

As
\nu_{E}(c\cdot x)=|c|_{E}\cdot\nu_{E}(x)=|c|_{F}^{n}\cdot\nu_{E}(x)
for every
c\in F
and
x\in E^{r}
, the exponent
1/n
in the above definition makes
\text{res}(\nu_{E})
a well-defined norm on
F^{nr}
.
We call
\res(\nu_{E})
the
restriction of scalars of
\nu_{E}
(with respect to the fixed pair
\xi
and
\pi_{E}
).

Remark 2.3.5
.

In the archimedean setting,
\mathbb{C}
has a natural
\mathbb{R}
-basis
\{1,\sqrt{-1}\}
, providing a canonical expression as
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.
However, in the nonarchimedean case, no canonical identification of
E
with
F^{n}
when
n>1
.
Therefore for introducing the restriction of scalars, it is necessary to fix specific
\xi
and
\pi_{E}
first for the isomorphism
\iota_{E/F,r}:E^{r}\mathrel{{\mathop{\rightarrow}\limits}}F^{nr}
for every
r\in\mathbb{N}
.
In particular, suppose another
\xi^{\prime}
and
\pi_{E}^{\prime}
are chosen.
Write

(2.3.6)

\big\{\xi^{i}\pi_{E}^{j}\ \big|\ 0\leq i<f,\ 0\leq j<e\big\}=\big\{u_{t}\ \big|\ 1\leq t\leq n\big\}

and

\big\{(\xi^{\prime})^{i}(\pi_{E}^{\prime})^{j}\ \big|\ 0\leq i<f,\ 0\leq j<e\big\}=\big\{u^{\prime}_{t}\ \big|\ 1\leq t\leq n\big\}.

There exists
\kappa\in\GL_{n}(O_{F})
(in fact, within a parahori subgroup) so that

\begin{pmatrix}u_{1}^{\prime}\\
\vdots\\
u_{n}^{\prime}\end{pmatrix}=\kappa\cdot\begin{pmatrix}u_{1}\\
\vdots\\
u_{n}\end{pmatrix}.

Put
\kappa_{r}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}={\rm diag}(\kappa,\cdots\hskip-0.85355pt,\kappa)\in\GL_{nr}(O_{F})
.
Let
\res^{\prime}:\mathcal{N}^{r}(E)\rightarrow\mathcal{N}^{nr}(F)
be the restriction of scalars associated with the pair
(\xi^{\prime},\pi_{E}^{\prime})
.
Then we can see that

\res^{\prime}(\nu_{E})=\kappa_{r}*\res(\nu_{E}),\quad\forall\nu_{E}\in\mathcal{N}^{r}(E).

In other words, the restriction of scalars
\res:\mathcal{N}^{r}(E)\rightarrow\mathcal{N}^{nr}(F)
depends on the chosen
\xi
and
\pi_{E}
when
r>1
.
When
r=1
, one observes that
\mathcal{N}^{1}(E)=\big\{c\,|\cdot|_{E}\ \big|\ c\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}\big\}
and
\res^{\prime}(|\cdot|_{E})=\res(|\cdot|_{E})
(by the formula (
2.3.2
)).
Hence the dependency disappears.

Lemma 2.3.7
.

Given
\nu_{E}\in\mathcal{N}^{r}(E)
, the following equality holds:

D\big(\text{\rm res}(\nu_{E})\big)=\mathfrak{d}(E/F)^{r/2}\cdot D(\nu_{E}).

Proof.

Let
\{u_{1},...,u_{n}\}
be the
F
-basis of
E
chosen in (
2.3.6
).
For each
b\in E
, let
\tilde{b}\in\Mat_{n}(F)
so that

\begin{pmatrix}u_{1}b\\
\vdots\\
u_{n}b\end{pmatrix}=\tilde{b}\cdot\begin{pmatrix}u_{1}\\
\vdots\\
u_{n}\end{pmatrix}.

This gives an injective homomorphism (with respect to the fixed
\iota_{E/F}
)

\GL_{r}(E)\hookrightarrow\GL_{nr}(F),\quad g=\begin{pmatrix}b_{11}&\cdots&b_{1r}\\
\vdots&&\vdots\\
b_{r1}&\cdots&b_{rr}\end{pmatrix}\longmapsto\tilde{g}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\begin{pmatrix}\tilde{b}_{11}&\cdots&\tilde{b}_{1r}\\
\vdots&&\vdots\\
\tilde{b}_{r1}&\cdots&\tilde{b}_{rr}\end{pmatrix},

which satisfies that

N_{E/F}(\det g)=\det\tilde{g},\quad\text{ where $N_{E/F}:E\rightarrow F$ is the norm map of $E$ over $F$.}

Moreover, one can check that

\res(g*\nu_{E})=\tilde{g}*\res(\nu_{E}),\quad\forall\nu_{E}\in\mathcal{N}^{r}(E)\text{ and }g\in\GL_{r}(E).

Now, let
\{v_{1},...,v_{r}\}
be the standard basis of
E^{r}
.
Then there exists
g_{0}\in\GL_{r}(E)
so that
\{v_{1},...,v_{r}\}
is an orthogonal
O_{E}
-base of
O_{E}^{r}
with respect to
g_{0}*\nu_{E}
.
Then with respect to the notation in (
2.3.6
), we know that

\big\{u_{t}v_{\ell}\ \big|\ 1\leq t\leq n,\ 1\leq\ell\leq r\big\}\ =\ \big\{\xi^{i}\pi_{E}^{j}v_{\ell}\ \big|\ 0\leq i<f,\ 0\leq j<e,\ 1\leq\ell\leq r\big\}

corresponds to an orthogonal
O_{F}
-base of
O_{F}^{nr}
with respect to
\text{res}(g_{0}*\nu_{E})
.
Thus,

\displaystyle D\big(\text{res}(\nu_{E})\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\det\tilde{g}_{0}|_{F}^{-1}\cdot D\big(\tilde{g}_{0}*\res(\nu_{E})\big)\ =\ |N_{E/F}(\det g_{0})|_{F}^{-1}\cdot D\big(\res(g_{0}*\nu_{E})\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\det g_{0}|_{E}^{-1}\cdot\prod_{i=0}^{f-1}\prod_{j=0}^{e-1}\prod_{\ell=1}^{r}(g_{0}*\nu_{E})\big(\xi^{i}\pi_{E}^{j}v_{\ell}\big)^{\frac{1}{n}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\det g_{0}|_{E}^{-1}\cdot|\pi_{E}|_{F}^{\frac{ef(e-1)r}{2}}\cdot D(g_{0}*\nu_{E})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\pi_{F}|_{F}^{\frac{f(e-1)r}{2}}\cdot D(\nu_{E})\ =\ \mathfrak{d}(E/F)^{r/2}\cdot D(\nu_{E}),

whence the proof is complete.
∎

Combining with Proposition
2.2.1
, we obtain that:

Corollary 2.3.8
.

Given a positive integer
r
and finite extensions
E^{(i)}
over
F
with degree
n_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq m
, let
n=n_{1}+\cdots+n_{m}
.
By abuse of notation, we still denote

\text{\rm res}:\prod_{i=1}^{m}\mathcal{N}^{r}(E^{(i)})\hookrightarrow\prod_{i=1}^{m}\mathcal{N}^{n_{i}r}(F)

to be the embedding induced from the restriction of scalars on each component.
Then for every
\nu_{E}=(\nu_{E}^{(1)},\cdots\hskip-0.85355pt,\nu_{E}^{(m)})\in\prod_{i=1}^{m}\mathcal{N}^{r}(E^{(i)})

and
(x_{1},\cdots\hskip-0.85355pt,x_{m})\in\prod_{i=1}^{m}F^{n_{i}r}
, the following equality holds for
s\in\mathbb{C}
with
\operatorname{Re}(s)>0
:

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}/d(\mathbb{R})}\frac{D\big(\text{\rm res}(\nu_{E})(\mathbf{t})\big)^{s}}{\text{\rm res}(\nu_{E})(\mathbf{t})(x_{1},\cdots\hskip-0.85355pt,x_{m})^{nrs}}d\mathbf{t}=(rs)^{1-m}\cdot\frac{n}{n_{1}\cdots n_{m}}\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{m}\mathfrak{d}(E^{(i)}/F)^{\frac{rs}{2}}\frac{D(\nu_{E}^{(i)})^{s}}{\nu_{E}^{(i)}(x^{(i)})^{rs}}

Here
x^{(i)}\in(E^{(i)})^{r}
corresponds to
x_{i}\in F^{n_{i}r}
via the isomorphism
\iota_{E^{(i)}/F,r}:(E^{(i)})^{r}\cong F^{n_{i}r}
as in (
2.3.3
) for
1\leq i\leq m
, i.e.
\iota_{E^{(i)}/F,r}(x^{(i)})=x_{i}
.

Proof.

\displaystyle\hskip-85.35826pt\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}/d(\mathbb{R})}\frac{D\big(\text{\rm res}(\nu_{E})(\mathbf{t})\big)^{s}}{\text{\rm res}(\nu_{E})(\mathbf{t})(x_{1},\cdots\hskip-0.85355pt,x_{m})^{nrs}}d\mathbf{t}

(Proposition
2.2.1
)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle s^{1-m}\cdot\frac{nr}{(n_{1}r)\cdots(n_{m}r)}\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{m}\frac{D\big(\text{res}(\nu_{E}^{(i)})\big)^{s}}{\text{res}(\nu_{E}^{(i)})(x_{i})^{n_{i}rs}}

(Lemma
2.3.7
)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(rs)^{1-m}\cdot\frac{n}{n_{1}\cdots n_{m}}\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{m}\mathfrak{d}(E^{(i)}/F)^{\frac{rs}{2}}\frac{D(\nu_{E}^{(i)})^{s}}{\nu_{E}^{(i)}(x^{(i)})^{rs}}

as desired.
∎

Berkovich Drinfeld period domain

In this section, we review the definition of Drinfeld period domains as Berkovich spaces and their connection with Goldman–Iwahori spaces through the building maps. For further details, we refer the reader to
[
5
]
,
[
30
]
, and
[
31
]
.

3.1.
Berkovich affine and projective spaces

Let
\mathbb{C}_{F}
be the completion of a chosen algebraic closure of
F
.
Given a positive integer
r
, let
R(r,\mathbb{C}_{F})
be the symmetric algebra of
\mathbb{C}_{F}^{r}
. We may identify
R(r,\mathbb{C}_{F})
with the polynomial ring
\mathbb{C}_{F}[e_{1},\cdots\hskip-0.85355pt,e_{r}]
, where
\{e_{1},...,e_{r}\}
is the standard
\mathbb{C}_{F}
-basis of
\mathbb{C}_{F}^{r}
.
A
multiplicative seminorm on
R(r,\mathbb{C}_{F})
over
\mathbb{C}_{F}
is a map
|\cdot|:R(r,\mathbb{C}_{F})\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}

satisfying that

(1)

|c|=|c|_{F}\quad\forall c\in\mathbb{C}_{F}
.

(2)

|f+g|\leq\max(|f|,|g|)\quad\forall f,g\in R(r,\mathbb{C}_{F})
;

(3)

|f\cdot g|=|f|\cdot|g|\quad\forall f,g\in R(r,\mathbb{C}_{F})
.

Recall that the
Berkovich affine space

\mathbb{A}^{r,\text{an}}_{\mathbb{C}_{F}}
consists of all the multiplicative seminorms on
R(r,\mathbb{C}_{F})
(see
[
3
, §1.5]
).
For each
x\in\mathbb{A}^{r,\text{an}}_{\mathbb{C}_{F}}
, we also write

|f|_{x}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=x(f),\quad\forall f\in R(r,\mathbb{C}_{F}).

Then
\mathbb{A}^{r,\text{an}}_{\mathbb{C}_{F}}
is equipped the weakest topology so that the real-valued function
(x\mapsto|f|_{x})
on
\mathbb{A}^{r,\text{an}}_{\mathbb{C}_{F}}
is continuous for every
f\in R(r,\mathbb{C}_{F})
.

Put
\wp_{x}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\big\{f\in R(r,\mathbb{C}_{F})\ \big|\ |f|_{x}=0\big\}
, which is a prime ideal of
R(r,\mathbb{C}_{F})
.
Then
|\cdot|_{x}
induces a multiplicative norm on

R(r,\mathbb{C}_{F})/\wp_{x}
(still denoted by
|\cdot|_{x}
, see
[
36
, Definition 2.2.1 (ii)]
):

|\bar{f}|_{x}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\inf\big\{|f+g|_{x}\ \big|\ g\in\wp_{x}\big\},\quad\forall\bar{f}=f+\wp_{x}\in R(r,\mathbb{C}_{F})/\wp_{x}.

We may extend
|\cdot|_{x}
to a non-archimedean absolute value on the fraction field of
R(r,\mathbb{C}_{F})/\wp_{x}
, and its completion is denoted by
\mathbb{C}_{F}(x)
.
The absolute value of every
g(x)\in\mathbb{C}_{F}(x)
is written by
|g(x)|
for convention.
In particular, for each
f\in R(r,\mathbb{C}_{F})
, its image in
\mathbb{C}_{F}(x)
is denoted by
f(x)
, whence

|f(x)|=|\bar{f}|_{x}=|f|_{x}.

Two such seminorms
x_{1}
and
x_{2}
are equivalent if and only if there exists
c\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
so that
x_{1}(f)=c^{n}x_{2}(f)
for every
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
and homogeneous
f\in R(r,\mathbb{C}_{F})
with
\deg f=n
.
The Berkovich projective space
\mathbb{P}^{r-1,\text{an}}_{\mathbb{C}_{F}}
is (identified with) the set of equivalence classes of the multiplicative seminorms on
R(r,\mathbb{C}_{F})
which are nonzero at
e_{i}
for some
1\leq i\leq r
, see
[
31
, § 3.1.1]
.
In particular, for every multiplicative seminorm
x
on
R(r,\mathbb{C}_{F})
with
|e_{r}|_{x}\neq 0
, there exists a unique multiplicative seminorm
\underline{x}
on
R(r,\mathbb{C}_{F})
which is equivalent to
x
so that

e_{r}(\underline{x})=1\quad\in\mathbb{C}_{F}(\underline{x}).

Given a (usual)
F
-rational hyperplane
H\subset\mathbb{P}^{r-1}_{F}
,
we may write

H=\big\{(c_{1}:\cdots:c_{r})\in\mathbb{P}^{r-1}_{F}\ \big|\ a_{1}c_{1}+\cdots+a_{r}c_{r}=0\big\}

for a unique
F
-rational point
a=(a_{1}:\cdots:a_{r})\in\mathbb{P}^{r-1}(F)
.
In this case,
H
is denoted by
H_{a}
.
For
z\in\mathbb{P}^{r-1,\text{an}}_{\mathbb{C}_{F}}
, take a multiplicative seminorm
x
on
R(r,\mathbb{C}_{F})
representing
z
.
We say that
z
lies in
H
if

|a_{1}e_{1}+\cdots+a_{r}e_{r}|_{x}=0.

Put

H^{\text{an}}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\big\{z\in\mathbb{P}^{r-1,\text{an}}_{\mathbb{C}_{F}}\ \big|\ \text{$z$ lies in $H$}\big\}
, regarded as the analytification of
H
.

Definition 3.1.1
.

(See
[
5
]
.) The
Berkovich Drinfeld period domain of rank
r
over
F
is defined by

\Omega^{r}_{F}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\mathbb{P}^{r-1,\text{an}}_{\mathbb{C}_{F}}\setminus\bigcup_{a\in\mathbb{P}^{r-1}(F)}H_{a}^{\text{an}}.

Remark 3.1.2
.

Let

\widetilde{R}(r,\mathbb{C}_{F})\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=R(r,\mathbb{C}_{F})\big[(a_{1}e_{1}+\cdots+a_{r}e_{r})^{-1}\mid(a_{1},\cdots\hskip-0.85355pt,a_{r})\in F^{r}-\{0\}\big],

and let
\widetilde{\Omega}^{r}_{F}
be the set of all the multiplicative seminorms on
\widetilde{R}(r,\mathbb{C}_{F})
.
The embedding
R(r,\mathbb{C}_{F})\hookrightarrow\widetilde{R}(r,\mathbb{C}_{F})
induces an injective map
\widetilde{\Omega}^{r}_{F}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{A}^{r,\text{an}}_{\mathbb{C}_{F}}
.
Then we may identify
\Omega^{r}_{F}
with the set of equivalence classes in
\widetilde{\Omega}^{r}_{F}
.

Note that the ring homomorphism from
R(r-1,F)
to
\widetilde{R}(r,\mathbb{C}_{F})
given by

e_{i}\longmapsto\frac{e_{i}}{e_{r}},\quad 1\leq i\leq r-1,

induces an embedding
\Omega_{F}^{r}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{A}^{r-1,\text{an}}_{\mathbb{C}_{F}}
.
More precisely, we may identify
\Omega_{F}^{r}
with

(3.1.3)

\left\{z\in\mathbb{A}^{r-1,\text{an}}_{\mathbb{C}_{F}}\ \Big|\ |a_{1}e_{1}+\cdots+a_{r-1}e_{r-1}+a_{r}|_{z}\neq 0,\quad\forall 0\neq(a_{1},\cdots\hskip-0.85355pt,a_{r})\in F^{r}\right\}.

In other words, every
z\in\Omega_{F}^{r}
can be regarded as a point in
\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{C}_{F}}^{r,{\rm an}}
so that
e_{r}(z)=1
.

The atlas on
\Omega_{F}^{r}
can be chosen as the collections of the affinoid spaces associated with all simplices of
\mathcal{X}^{r}(F)
via the “building map” introduced in the next subsection.

3.2.
Building map

Given
\tilde{z}\in\widetilde{\Omega}^{r}_{F}
, one may associate a norm
\nu_{\tilde{z}}
on
F^{r}
by

(3.2.1)

\nu_{\tilde{z}}(a_{1},\cdots\hskip-0.85355pt,a_{r})\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=|a_{1}e_{1}+\cdots+a_{r}e_{r}|_{\tilde{z}},\quad\forall(a_{1},\cdots\hskip-0.85355pt,a_{r})\in F^{r}.

Define
\tilde{\mathscr{B}}:\widetilde{\Omega}^{r}_{F}\rightarrow\mathcal{N}^{r}(F)
by sending
\tilde{z}
to
\nu_{\tilde{z}}
for every
\tilde{z}\in\widetilde{\Omega}^{r}_{F}
.
It is clear that if
\tilde{z}_{1},\tilde{z}_{2}\in\widetilde{\Omega}^{r}_{F}
are equivalent, then so are
\nu_{\tilde{z}_{1}}
and
\nu_{\tilde{z}_{2}}
.
Thus
\tilde{\mathscr{B}}
induces a map

\mathscr{B}:\Omega^{r}_{F}\rightarrow\mathcal{X}^{r}(F)
, the so-called
building map
.

Note that the right action of
\GL_{r}(F)
on
F^{r}
(by right multiplication when viewing elements in
F^{r}
as row vectors)
extends to a right action of
\GL_{r}(F)
on the symmetric algebra
R(r,\mathbb{C}_{F})
(and also on
\widetilde{R}(r,\mathbb{C}_{F})
).
The corresponding left actions of
\GL_{r}(F)
on
\mathbb{A}^{r,\text{an}}_{\mathbb{C}_{F}}
and
\widetilde{\Omega}^{r}_{F}
can be illustrated as follows: for every
x\in\mathbb{A}^{r,\text{an}}_{\mathbb{C}_{F}}
(resp.
\widetilde{\Omega}^{r}_{F}
) and
\gamma\in\GL_{r}(F)
, the point
\gamma\cdot x
satisfies

|f|_{\gamma\cdot x}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=|f\cdot\gamma|_{x},\quad\forall f\in R(r,\mathbb{C}_{F})\quad\text{ (resp.\ $\widetilde{R}(r,\mathbb{C}_{F})$)}.

This induces a left action of
\operatorname{PGL}_{r}(F)
on
\Omega^{r}_{F}
.
On the other hand, recall that we have a left action of
\operatorname{PGL}_{r}(F)
on
\mathcal{X}^{r}(F)
induced from the left action of
\GL_{r}(F)
on
\mathcal{N}^{r}(F)
given in (
2.1.5
).
It is known in
[
9
, (4.2) Proposition]
that:

Proposition 3.2.2
.

The map
\widetilde{\mathscr{B}}:\widetilde{\Omega}_{F}^{r}\rightarrow\mathcal{N}^{r}(F)
is
\GL_{r}(F)
-equivariant.
Consequently, the building map
\mathscr{B}:\Omega_{F}^{r}\rightarrow\mathcal{X}^{r}(F)
is
\operatorname{PGL}_{r}(F)
-equivariant.

Remark 3.2.3
.

(see
[
5
, p. 3]
.) For each
\nu\in\mathcal{N}^{r}(F)
, one may associate a muliplicative seminorm
x_{\nu}
on
R(r,\mathbb{C}_{F})
by the following:
choose an orthogonal basis
\{u_{1},...,u_{r}\}
of
F^{r}
with respect to
\nu
, and
set

\bigg|\sum_{\underline{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{r}}a_{\underline{i}}\underline{u}^{\underline{i}}\bigg|_{x_{\nu}}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\max\big(|a_{\underline{i}}|_{F}\cdot\nu(\underline{u})^{\underline{i}}\ \big|\ \underline{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{r}\big),\quad\forall\sum_{\underline{i}}a_{\underline{i}}\underline{u}^{\underline{i}}\in R(r,\mathbb{C}_{F}).

Here for
\underline{i}=(i_{1},\cdots\hskip-0.85355pt,i_{r})\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{r}
,
we put

\underline{u}^{\underline{i}}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=u_{1}^{i_{1}}\cdots u_{r}^{i_{r}}\quad\text{ and }\quad\nu(\underline{u})^{\underline{i}}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\nu(u_{1})^{i_{1}}\cdots\nu(u_{r})^{i_{r}}.

Observe that
x_{\nu}
does not depend on the chosen orthogonal basis
\{u_{1},...,u_{r}\}
.
Since

|a_{1}u_{1}+\cdots+a_{r}u_{r}|_{x_{\nu}}=\nu(a_{1}u_{1}+\cdots+a_{r}u_{r})>0\quad\text{ for every nonzero }a=(a_{1},\cdots\hskip-0.85355pt,a_{r})\in F^{r},

we may extend

to a multiplicative norm on
\widetilde{R}(r,\mathbb{C}_{F})
, i.e.
x_{\nu}\in\widetilde{\Omega}_{F}^{r}
.
This gives an embedding
\tilde{\varsigma}:\mathcal{N}^{r}(F)\hookrightarrow\widetilde{\Omega}_{F}^{r}
so that

\widetilde{\mathscr{B}}\circ\tilde{\varsigma}=\text{id}_{\mathcal{N}^{r}(F)}
, which induces an embedding
\varsigma:\mathcal{X}^{r}(F)\hookrightarrow\Omega_{F}^{r}
satisfying

\mathscr{B}\circ\varsigma=\text{id}_{\mathcal{X}^{r}(F)}
.
This ensures the surjectivity of
\mathscr{B}
.
In particular,
\mathcal{X}^{r}(F)
is homeomorphic to the image of
\varsigma
, and
\varsigma(\mathcal{X}^{r}(F))
is closed in
\Omega^{r}_{F}
(see
[
5
, Proof of Theorem 1: Step 2]
).
Moreover,
for each
x
in
\widetilde{\Omega}^{r}_{F}
with
\widetilde{\mathscr{B}}(x)=\nu
, one has that

|f|_{x}\leq|f|_{x_{\nu}},\quad\forall f\in\tilde{R}(r,\mathbb{C}_{F}).

Remark 3.2.4
.

Note that
\Omega_{F}^{r}
is a “strict
F
-analytic space” in the sense of
[
3
, § 3.1]
.
According to
[
4

-analytic structure”

introduced in

.
We refer the reader to

3.2.5.
Imaginary part.

Given
z\in\Omega_{F}^{r}
, which is identified with a point on
\widetilde{\Omega}_{F}^{r}
satisfying

e_{r}(z)=1
,
the norm
\nu_{z}
on
F^{r}
corresponding to
z
is uniquely determined by
z
.
We set the
imaginary part of

to be

\im(z)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=D(\nu_{z}),

where
D(\nu_{z})
is the lattice discriminant of the norm
\nu_{z}
introduced in Definition
2.1.4
.
Moreover, for every

g=\begin{pmatrix}a_{11}&\cdots&a_{1r}\\
\vdots&&\vdots\\
a_{r1}&\cdots&a_{rr}\end{pmatrix}\in\GL_{r}(F)
,
define the following

j(g,z)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=(a_{r1}e_{1}+\cdots+a_{rr}e_{r})(z)\quad\in\mathbb{C}_{F}(z).

Then we have an identification between
\mathbb{C}_{F}(g\cdot z)

and
\mathbb{C}_{\infty}(z)
induced from the automorphism of the symmetric algebra

associated with

, i.e.

(3.2.6)

\mathbb{C}_{F}(g\cdot z)\ni\quad f(g\cdot z)=\joinrel=\frac{(f\cdot g)(z)}{j(g,z)^{\deg f}}\quad\in\mathbb{C}_{F}(z),\quad\forall\text{ homogeneous }f\in R(r,\mathbb{C}_{F}).

Moreover,

|j(g,z)|=|a_{r1}e_{1}+\cdots+a_{rr}e_{r}|_{z}=\nu_{z}(a_{r1}e_{1}+\cdots+a_{rr}e_{r}),

and the following transformation law holds:

Lemma 3.2.7
.

For every
z\in\Omega_{F}^{r}
and

, we have that

\im(g\cdot z)=\frac{|\det g|_{F}}{|j(g,z)|^{r}}\cdot\im(z).

Proof.

Identifying

as points in

Therefore by Lemma

, we get

\im(g\cdot z)=D(\nu_{g\cdot z})=\frac{1}{|j(g,z)|^{r}}\cdot D(g*\nu_{z})=\frac{|\det g|_{F}}{|j(g,z)|^{r}}\cdot D(\nu_{z})=\frac{|\det g|_{F}}{|j(g,z)|^{r}}\cdot\im(z)

∎

4.
Drinfeld modules over Drinfeld period domains and discriminants

Following
[
3
, 1.5.3 Definition and 1.5.5 Remark]
, an
analytic function on
\Omega_{F}^{r}
is a map

f:\Omega_{F}^{r}\rightarrow\coprod_{z\in\Omega_{F}^{r}}\mathbb{C}_{F}(z)

with
f(z)\in\mathbb{C}_{F}(z)
which is a “local limit” of rational functions, i.e. for each
z\in\Omega_{F}^{r}
, there exists an “admissible” open neighborhood
U_{z}
of
z
with the following property:
given
\epsilon>0
, there exist
g,h\in R(r,\mathbb{C}_{F})
such that for every
z^{\prime}\in U_{z}
, we have that

h(z^{\prime})\neq 0\quad\text{ and }\quad\left|f(z^{\prime})-\frac{g(z^{\prime})}{h(z^{\prime})}\right|<\epsilon.

Since
\Omega_{F}^{r}
is determined by
\Omega_{F}^{r,{\rm rig}}
as noted in Remark
3.2.4
,
the ring of analytic functions on
\Omega_{F}^{r}
coincides with the ring of rigid analytic functions on
\Omega_{F}^{r,\text{rig}}
.
In particular, when the characteristic of
F
is positive,
Drinfeld modular forms
of rank
r
on
\Omega_{F}^{r,{\rm rig}}

form a fundamental class of rigid analytic functions in function field arithmetic. These functions serve as natural analogs of classical modular forms in the equal-characteristic setting over function fields and have been the subject of extensive recent study. We refer the reader to
[
1
]
,
[
14
]
,
[
17
]
,
[
15
]
,
[
18
]
,
[
16
]
,
[
19
]
for comprehensive discussions. Consequently, we may regard them as “Berkovich” analytic functions on
\Omega_{F}^{r}
as well.

In the following,
we explicitly construct “Klein forms” on
\Omega_{F}^{r}
in the positive characteristic setting, which are specific invertible analytic functions closely related to the “coefficient forms”, and introduce the universal Drinfeld modules over Drinfeld period domains.

4.1.
Klein forms on Drinfeld period domains

Let
\mathbb{F}_{q}
be a finite field with
q
elements, where
q
is a power of a prime number
p
.
Let
k
be a global function field with the constant field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, i.e.
k
is a finitely generated field extension over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
with transcendence degree one and
\mathbb{F}_{q}
is algebraically closed in
k
.
Fix a place
\infty
of
k
, referred to the place at infinity.
The valuation of
k
with respect to
\infty
is denoted by
\ord_{\infty}
.
Let
k_{\infty}
be the completion of
k
at
\infty
.
Set

O_{\infty}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\{\alpha\in k_{\infty}\mid\ord_{\infty}(\alpha)\geq 0\},\quad\mathfrak{p}_{\infty}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\{\alpha\in O_{\infty}\mid\ord_{\infty}(\alpha)>0\},\quad\text{and}\quad\mathbb{F}_{\infty}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\frac{O_{\infty}}{\mathfrak{p}_{\infty}}.

Put
q_{\infty}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\#(\mathbb{F}_{\infty})
.
The absolute value on
k_{\infty}
is normalized by

|\alpha|_{\infty}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=q_{\infty}^{-\ord_{\infty}(\alpha)},\quad\forall\alpha\in k_{\infty}.

Let
\mathbb{C}_{\infty}
be the completion of a chosen algebraic closure of
k_{\infty}
.

Fix a positive integer
r
.
Let
\Omega_{\infty}^{r}
be the Berkovich Drinfeld period domain of rank
r
over
k_{\infty}
,
Throughout this section, we set

R_{r,\infty}=R(r,\mathbb{C}_{\infty})\quad\text{ and }\quad\widetilde{R}_{r,\infty}=\widetilde{R}(r,\mathbb{C}_{\infty}).

Given
\alpha=(c_{1},\cdots\hskip-0.85355pt,c_{r})\in\mathbb{C}_{\infty}^{r}
, we put

w_{\alpha}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\left(c_{1}e_{1}+\cdots+c_{r}e_{r}\right)/e_{r}\quad\in\widetilde{R}_{r,\infty},

which can be regarded as a rational function on
\Omega_{\infty}^{r}
.
Moreover, let
A
be the subring of
k
consisting of all elements in
k
regular away from
\infty
.
Let
Y\subset k^{r}
be a projective
A
-module of rank
r
.
Then for each
z\in\Omega_{\infty}^{r}
,
the
A
-lattice

(4.1.1)

\Lambda^{Y}_{z}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\big\{w_{\lambda}(z)\ \big|\ \lambda\in Y\big\}\subset\mathbb{C}_{\infty}(z)

satisfies that

(4.1.2)

\#\big\{w_{\lambda}(z)\in\Lambda_{z}^{Y}\ \big|\ |w_{\lambda}(z)|\leq q^{\epsilon}\big\}\asymp q^{r\epsilon},\quad\epsilon\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}.

This assures that for each
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}_{\infty}^{r}
,

e_{\alpha}^{Y}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=w_{\alpha}\cdot\prod_{0\neq\lambda\in Y}\left(1-\frac{w_{\alpha}}{w_{\lambda}}\right),

is a well-defined analytic function on the Berkovich space
\Omega_{\infty}^{r}
by the asymptotic estimation (
4.1.2
), which is invertible when
\alpha\in k_{\infty}^{r}\setminus Y
and
0
if
\alpha\in Y
.

Lemma 4.1.3
.

Given
\alpha,\alpha^{\prime}\in\mathbb{C}_{\infty}^{r}
,
\lambda\in Y
, and
\varepsilon\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
, one has

e^{Y}_{\varepsilon\alpha+\alpha^{\prime}+\lambda}=\varepsilon\cdot e^{Y}_{\alpha}+e^{Y}_{\alpha^{\prime}}.

Moreover, the following transformation law holds:

e_{\alpha}^{Y}(\gamma\cdot z)=j(\gamma,z)^{-1}\cdot e_{\alpha\gamma}^{Y}(z),\quad\forall\gamma\in\GL(Y),\ z\in\Omega_{\infty}^{r}.

Proof.

Given
z\in\Omega_{\infty}^{r}
, consider the following exponential function

\displaystyle\exp_{z}^{Y}(\xi)

\displaystyle\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\xi\cdot\prod_{0\neq\lambda\in Y}\left(1-\frac{\xi}{w_{\lambda}(z)}\right)

\displaystyle=\lim_{\epsilon\rightarrow\infty}\ \xi\cdot\prod_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{0\neq\lambda\in Y}{|w_{\lambda}(z)|\leq q^{\epsilon}}}\left(1-\frac{\xi}{w_{\lambda}(z)}\right),\quad\forall\xi\in\mathbb{C}_{\infty}(z).

As
\Lambda_{z}^{Y,\epsilon}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\big\{w_{\lambda}(z)\in\Lambda_{z}^{Y}\ \big|\ |w_{\lambda}(z)|\leq q^{\epsilon}\big\}
is a finite dimensional vector space over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
for every
\epsilon\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
,
the polynomial

{\rm x}\cdot\prod_{0\neq w_{\lambda}(z)\in\Lambda_{z}^{Y,\epsilon}}\big(1-{\rm x}/w_{\lambda}(z)\big)\in\mathbb{C}_{\infty}(z)[\rm x]
is
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linear, whence so is the function
\exp_{z}^{Y}
.
Therefore

\displaystyle e_{\varepsilon\alpha+\alpha^{\prime}+\lambda}^{Y}(z)=\exp_{z}^{Y}\big(w_{\varepsilon\alpha+\alpha^{\prime}+\lambda}(z)\big)

\displaystyle=\exp_{z}^{Y}\big(\varepsilon w_{\alpha}(z)+w_{\alpha^{\prime}}(z)+w_{\lambda}(z)\big)

\displaystyle=\varepsilon\exp_{z}^{Y}\big(w_{\alpha}(z)\big)+\exp_{z}^{Y}\big(w_{\alpha^{\prime}}(z)\big)=\varepsilon\cdot e_{\alpha}^{Y}(z)+e_{\alpha^{\prime}}^{Y}(z).

Moreover, via the identification (
3.2.6
) one has

w_{\alpha}(g\cdot z)=j(g,z)^{-1}\cdot w_{\alpha g}(z),\quad\forall\alpha\in\mathbb{C}_{\infty}^{r}\text{ and }g\in\GL_{r}(k_{\infty}).

Hence for every
\gamma\in\GL(Y)
and
z\in\Omega_{\infty}^{r}
,

\displaystyle e_{\alpha}^{Y}(\gamma\cdot z)=w_{\alpha}(\gamma\cdot z)\cdot\prod_{0\neq\lambda\in Y}\left(1-\frac{w_{\alpha}(\gamma\cdot z)}{w_{\lambda}(\gamma\cdot z)}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{w_{\alpha\gamma}(z)}{j(\gamma,z)}\cdot\prod_{0\neq\lambda\in Y}\left(1-\frac{w_{\alpha\gamma}(z)}{w_{\lambda\gamma}(z)}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{w_{\alpha\gamma}(z)}{j(\gamma,z)}\cdot\prod_{0\neq\lambda\in Y}\left(1-\frac{w_{\alpha\gamma}(z)}{w_{\lambda}(z)}\right)=\frac{e_{\alpha\gamma}^{Y}(z)}{j(\gamma,z)}.

∎

Remark 4.1.4
.

(1)

Let
O(\Omega_{\infty}^{r})
be the ring of analytic functions on
\Omega_{\infty}^{r}
.
Then the first assertion of Lemma
4.1.3
induces an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linear map from
\mathbb{C}_{\infty}^{r}/Y
to
O(\Omega_{\infty}^{r})
which sends a coset
\alpha+Y\in\mathbb{C}_{\infty}^{r}/Y
to
e_{\alpha}^{Y}
.

(2)

Given
\alpha\in k_{\infty}^{r}\setminus Y
, the
Klein form of
\alpha
with respect to
Y
is defined by

(4.1.5)

\mathfrak{k}_{\alpha}^{Y}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=(e_{\alpha}^{Y})^{-1}=w_{\alpha}^{-1}\cdot\prod_{0\neq\lambda\in Y}\left(1-\frac{w_{\alpha}}{w_{\lambda}}\right)^{-1},

which is an invertible analytic function on
\Omega_{\infty}^{r}
satisfying

\mathfrak{k}_{\alpha}^{Y}(\gamma\cdot z)=j(\gamma,z)\cdot\mathfrak{k}_{\alpha\gamma}^{Y}(z),\quad\forall\gamma\in\GL(Y)\text{ and }z\in\Omega_{\infty}^{r}.

These particular analytic functions appear in the construction of the “Drinfeld
A
-module of rank
r
over
\Omega_{\infty}^{r}
”
in the next subsection.

4.2.
Drinfeld modules over Drinfeld period domains

Recall that
O(\Omega_{\infty}^{r})
denotes the ring of analytic functions on the Berkovich space
\Omega_{\infty}^{r}
.
Given
a\in A
, by Remark
4.1.4
(1) we know that

\big\{e_{\alpha}^{Y}\ \big|\ \alpha\in a^{-1}Y/Y\big\}=\{0\}\cup\big\{(\mathfrak{k}^{Y}_{\alpha})^{-1}\ \big|\ 0\neq\alpha\in a^{-1}Y/Y\big\}

is an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-vector subspace of
O(\Omega_{\infty}^{r})
with dimension
r\cdot\deg a
, where
\deg a\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\log_{q}(\#(A/aA))
.
Thus the polynomial

\varphi^{Y,\Omega_{\infty}^{r}}_{a}({\rm x})\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=a{\rm x}\cdot\prod_{0\neq\alpha\in a^{-1}Y/Y}(1-\mathfrak{k}^{Y}_{\alpha}\cdot{\rm x})\quad\in O(\Omega_{\infty}^{r})[{\rm x}]

is
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linear, for which there exist “coefficient forms”
g^{Y}_{a,1},...,g^{Y}_{a,r\deg a}\in O(\Omega_{\infty}^{r})
so that

\varphi_{a}^{Y,\Omega_{\infty}^{r}}({\rm x})=a{\rm x}+\sum_{i=1}^{r\deg a}g^{Y}_{a,i}{\rm x}^{q^{i}}.

Moreover, for every
a,b\in A
, one has that

(4.2.1)

\varphi_{a}^{Y,\Omega_{\infty}^{r}}\circ\varphi_{b}^{Y,\Omega_{\infty}^{r}}=\varphi_{ab}^{Y,\Omega_{\infty}^{r}}=\varphi_{b}^{Y,\Omega_{\infty}^{r}}\circ\varphi_{a}^{Y,\Omega_{\infty}^{r}}.

This can be confirmed by verifying the corresponding relations among the coefficient forms evaluated at points in the rigid analytic structure
\Omega_{\infty}^{r,{\rm rig}}
, which is checked in
[
32
, p. 235]
.
This gives an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-algebra homomorphism
\varphi^{Y,\Omega_{\infty}^{r}}:A\rightarrow\End_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(\mathbb{G}_{a/O(\Omega_{\infty}^{r})})
, called the
Drinfeld
A
-module of rank
r
over
\Omega_{\infty}^{r}
.

Now, for each point
z\in\Omega_{\infty}^{r}
(regarded as a point on
\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}^{r-1,{\rm an}}
through the identification in Remark
3.1.2
), the evaluation map from
O(\Omega_{\infty}^{r})
to the residue field
\mathbb{C}_{\infty}(z)
sends
\varphi^{Y,\Omega_{\infty}^{r}}
to a Drinfeld
A
-module
\varphi^{Y,z}
of rank
r
over
\mathbb{C}_{\infty}(z)
: for each
a\in A
,

(4.2.2)

\varphi_{a}^{Y,z}({\rm x})=a{\rm x}+\sum_{i=1}^{r\deg a}g_{a,i}^{Y}(z){\rm x}^{q^{i}}\quad\in\mathbb{C}_{\infty}(z)[{\rm x}].

We call
\varphi^{Y,z}:A\rightarrow\End_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(\mathbb{G}_{a/\mathbb{C}_{\infty}(z)})
the
specialization of
\varphi^{Y,\Omega_{\infty}^{r}}
at
z
.

Remark 4.2.3
.

From the transformation law of the Klein forms in Remark
4.1.4
(2), one has that given
a\in A
and
1\leq i\leq r\deg a
,

g^{Y}_{a,i}(\gamma\cdot z)=j(\gamma,z)^{q^{i}-1}\cdot g^{Y}_{a,i}(z),\quad\forall\gamma\in\GL(Y)\text{ and }z\in\Omega_{\infty}^{r}.

For each nonzero
a\in A
,
define

\Delta^{Y}_{a}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=g^{Y}_{a,r\deg a}=a\cdot\prod_{0\neq\alpha\in\frac{1}{a}Y/Y}\mathfrak{k}^{Y}_{\alpha},

which is an invertible analytic function on
\Omega_{\infty}^{r}
. Comparing the leading coefficients on both sides in (
4.2.1
), we have that

(\Delta^{Y}_{a_{2}})^{|a_{1}|^{r}_{\infty}-1}=(\Delta^{Y}_{a_{1}})^{|a_{2}|^{r}_{\infty}-1},\quad\forall\text{ nonzero }a_{1},a_{2}\in A.

Therefore following the same approach in
[
12
, Chapter IV, Proposition 5.15]
, we get:

Lemma 4.2.4
.

There exists an analytic function
\Delta^{Y}
on
\Omega^{r}_{\infty}
, which is unique up to multiplying with a
(q_{\infty}^{r}-1)
-th root of unity, satisfying that

(\Delta^{Y})^{|a|_{\infty}^{r}-1}=(\Delta^{Y}_{a})^{q_{\infty}^{r}-1},\quad\forall\text{ nonzero }a\in A.

Definition 4.2.5
.

The analytic function
\Delta^{Y}
on
\Omega_{\infty}^{r}
is called
the Drinfeld discriminant function associated with
Y
.

Remark 4.2.6
.

The recent work of Gekeler in
[
21
, Corollary/Definition 7.6]
shows that there is a “canonical” choice of
\Delta^{Y}
with respect to a fixed “sign function”.

The transformation law of the coefficient forms in Remark
4.2.3
implies that:

Lemma 4.2.7
.

Given
\gamma\in\GL(Y)
and
z\in\Omega_{\infty}^{r}
, we have that

\Delta^{Y}(\gamma\cdot z)=j(\gamma,z)^{q^{r}-1}\cdot\Delta^{Y}(z)\quad(\in\mathbb{C}_{\infty}(z)).

4.2.8.
Absolute discriminant.

Given
z\in\Omega^{r}_{\infty}
, recall the exponential function defined in the proof of Lemma
4.1.3
:

\exp_{z}(\xi)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\xi\cdot\prod_{0\neq\lambda\in Y}\left(1-\frac{\xi}{w_{\lambda}(z)}\right),\quad\quad\forall\xi\in\mathbb{C}_{\infty}(z),

We know that the function
\exp_{z}
is
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linear and entire on the non-archimedean complete field
\mathbb{C}_{\infty}(z)

which satisfies (cf.
[
32
, p. 235]
)

\exp_{z}(a\cdot\xi)=\varphi_{a}^{Y,z}\big(\exp_{z}(\xi)\big),\quad\forall a\in A\ \text{ and }\ \xi\in\mathbb{C}_{\infty}(z).

Also, we may call

\Lambda_{z}=\big\{w_{\lambda}(z)\mid\lambda\in Y\big\}\subset\mathbb{C}_{\infty}(z)

the
period lattice of
\varphi^{Y,z}
.

Set

\|Y\|_{A}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=|a|_{\infty}^{-r}\cdot\#(A^{r}/aY)\quad\text{ for nonconstant $a\in A$ so that $aY\subset A^{r}$},

which is independent of the chosen
a\in A
.
Following the discussion in
[
38
, Remark 2.10]
, we may regard the quantity
\|Y\|_{A}\cdot\im(z)
as the “covolume” of the period lattice
\Lambda_{z}
.
This leads us to the following definition:

Definition 4.2.9
.

For each
z\in\Omega_{\infty}^{r}
, the
absolute
discriminant of
z
is defined by

\eta_{r}^{Y}(z)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\|Y\|_{A}\cdot\im(z)\cdot|\Delta^{Y}(z)|^{\frac{r}{q_{\infty}^{r}-1}}\quad\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}.

Remark 4.2.10
.

Let
\overline{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}(z)}
be an algebraic closure of
\mathbb{C}_{\infty}(z)
, and extend the absolute value
|\cdot|_{z}
to
\overline{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}(z)}
.
Take
c\in\overline{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}(z)}^{\times}
with
c^{q_{\infty}^{r}-1}=\Delta^{Y}(z)
, and consider the Drinfeld
A
-module
\psi^{c}
of rank
r
over
\overline{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}(z)}
defined by

\psi^{c}_{a}({\rm x})\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=c\cdot\varphi^{Y,z}_{a}\big(c^{-1}{\rm x}\big)=a{\rm x}+\sum_{i=1}^{r\deg a}\big(g_{a,i}^{Y}(z)c^{1-q^{i}}\big)x^{q^{i}}\quad\in\overline{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}(z)}[{\rm x}],

which has “unit discriminant”, i.e.

\Big(g_{a,r\deg a}^{Y}(z)c^{1-q^{r\deg a}}\Big)^{q_{\infty}^{r}-1}=\Delta^{Y}_{a}(z)^{q_{\infty}^{r}-1}\cdot\Delta^{Y}(z)^{1-q^{r\deg a}}=1\quad\text{ for every nonzero $a\in A$,}

and the period lattice of
\psi^{c}
becomes

c\cdot\Lambda_{z}\subset\overline{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}(z)}.

Therefore in this case, the value
\eta_{r}^{Y}(z)
can be regarded as the “covolume” of the period lattice
c\cdot\Lambda_{z}
of the “unit” Drinfeld
A
-module
\psi^{c}
which is isomorphic to
\varphi^{Y,z}
over
\overline{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}(z)}
.

In the next section, we shall extend the Kronecker limit formula established in
[
38
, equation (4.2) and (4.3)]
to the “Berkovich” version, which connects the derivative of the “non-holomorphic” Eisenstein series on
\Omega_{\infty}^{r}
with the absolute discriminant
\eta_{r}^{Y}
.

5.
Kronecker-type limit formula: one variable case

Here we translate the proof of the Kronecker-type limit formula in
[
38
, Theorem 4.4]

into “Berkovich” setting.
First, we introduce the non-holomorphic Eisenstein series on the Berkovich Drinfeld period domain
\Omega_{\infty}^{r}
and derive its analytic properties.

5.1.
Non-holomorphic Eisenstein series

Let
Y\subset k^{r}
be a projective
A
-module of rank
r
. Recall that

\|Y\|_{A}=|a|_{\infty}^{-r}\cdot\#(A^{r}/aY)\quad\text{ for nonconstant $a\in A$ so that $aY\subset A^{r}$},

which is independent of the chosen
a\in A
.
Given
z\in\Omega_{\infty}^{r}
, we are interested in the following Eisenstein series:

\mathbb{E}^{Y}(z,s)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\sum_{0\neq(c_{1},\cdots\hskip-0.28436pt,c_{r})\in Y}\frac{\|Y\|_{A}^{s}\cdot\im(z)^{s}}{|c_{1}e_{1}+\cdots+c_{r}e_{r}|_{z}^{rs}}=\|Y\|_{A}^{s}\cdot\sum_{0\neq\lambda\in Y}\frac{\im(z)^{s}}{\nu_{z}(\lambda)^{rs}}.

Lemma 5.1.1
.

The Eisenstein series
\mathbb{E}^{Y}(z,s)
converges absolutely for
\operatorname{Re}(s)>1
.

Proof.

Let
\nu=\nu_{z}
be the norm on
k_{\infty}^{r}
corresponding to
z
.
For
y\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
, let

B_{\nu}(y)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\{x\in k_{\infty}^{r}\mid\nu(x)\leq y\}.

Then
B_{\nu}(y)
is an
O_{\infty}
-lattice in
k_{\infty}^{r}
.
Take
\mathbf{1}_{B_{\nu}(y)}
to be the characteristic function of
B_{\nu}(y)
on
k_{\infty}^{r}
.
For
0\neq x\in k^{r}
and
\operatorname{Re}(s)>0
, one observes that

\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbf{1}_{B_{\nu}(y)}(x)y^{-rs}\frac{dy}{y}=\frac{1}{rs}\cdot\nu(x)^{-rs}.

Since
Y
is discrete in
k_{\infty}^{r}
, one has

y_{0}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\inf\{\nu(x)\mid 0\neq x\in Y\}>0.

Define the following theta series:

\Theta^{Y}(\nu,y)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\sum_{\lambda\in Y}\mathbf{1}_{B_{\nu}(y)}(\lambda)\quad\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 1}.

There exists
C\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
so that
\Theta^{Y}(\nu,y)\leq C\cdot y^{r}
for all
y\geq y_{0}
.
Thus

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\left|\left(\Theta^{Y}(\nu,y)-1\right)y^{-rs}\right|\frac{dy}{y}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\cdot\int_{y_{0}}^{\infty}y^{r(1-\operatorname{Re}(s))}\frac{dy}{y}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C\cdot\frac{y_{0}^{r(1-\operatorname{Re}(s))}}{r(\operatorname{Re}(s)-1)}\quad\text{ as $\operatorname{Re}(s)>1$.}

This implies that for
\operatorname{Re}(s)>1
, the Mellin transform of
\Theta^{Y}
converges absolutely and equals to

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\Theta(\nu,y)-1\right)y^{-rs}\frac{dy}{y}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{0\neq\lambda\in Y}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbf{1}_{B_{\nu}(y)}(\lambda)\,y^{-rs}\frac{dy}{y}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{rs}\sum_{0\neq\lambda\in Y}\frac{1}{\nu(\lambda)^{rs}}=\frac{\|Y\|_{A}^{-s}\im(z)^{-s}}{rs}\cdot\mathbb{E}^{Y}(z,s).

This completes the proof.
∎

Remark 5.1.2
.

(1)

It is straightforward that

\mathbb{E}^{Y}(\gamma\cdot z,s)=\mathbb{E}^{Y}(z,s)
for every
\gamma\in\GL(Y)
.

(2)

Since
\im(z)=D(\nu_{z})
for every
z\in\Omega_{\infty}^{r}
, we may write

\mathbb{E}^{Y}(z,s)=\|Y\|_{A}^{s}\cdot\sum_{0\neq\lambda\in Y}\frac{D(\nu_{z})^{s}}{\nu_{z}(\lambda)^{rs}}

Hence the Eisenstein series
\mathbb{E}^{Y}(\cdot,s)
actually factors through the building map
\mathscr{B}
, and
\mathbb{E}^{Y}(z^{\prime},s)=\mathbb{E}^{Y}(z,s)
when
\mathscr{B}(z^{\prime})=\mathscr{B}(z)
(i.e.
\nu_{z^{\prime}}=\nu_{z}
).

The following lemma gives the meromorphic continuation of
\mathbb{E}^{Y}(z,s)
to the whole complex
s
-plane:

Lemma 5.1.3
.

Given
s\in\mathbb{C}
with
\operatorname{Re}(s)>1
and
a\in A\setminus\mathbb{F}_{q}
, the following equality holds:

\mathbb{E}^{Y}(z,s)=\frac{\|Y\|_{A}^{s}\im(z)^{s}}{|a|_{\infty}^{rs}-|a|_{\infty}^{r}}\sum_{0\neq w\in\frac{1}{a}Y/Y}\left[\frac{1}{\nu_{z}(w)^{rs}}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\lambda\in Y\\
\nu_{z}(\lambda)\leq\nu_{z}(w)\end{subarray}}^{\prime}\left(\frac{1}{\nu_{z}(\lambda-w)^{rs}}-\frac{1}{\nu_{z}(\lambda)^{rs}}\right)\right].

In particular, one has
\mathbb{E}^{Y}(z,0)=-1
.

Proof.

We first observe that

|a|_{\infty}^{-rs}\cdot\mathbb{E}^{Y}(z,s)=\frac{\|Y\|_{A}^{s}}{\|aY\|_{A}^{s}}\cdot\mathbb{E}^{aY}(z,s).

Hence

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{Y}(z,s)-|a|_{\infty}^{-rs}\mathbb{E}^{Y}(z,s)

\displaystyle=\|Y\|_{A}^{s}\cdot\sum_{w\in Y-aY}\frac{\im(z)^{s}}{\nu_{z}(w)^{rs}}

\displaystyle=\|Y\|_{A}^{s}\cdot\sum_{0\neq w\in Y/aY}\left(\frac{\im(z)^{s}}{\nu_{z}(w)^{rs}}+\sum_{0\neq\lambda\in aY}\frac{\im(z)^{s}}{\nu_{z}(\lambda-w)^{rs}}\right).

Therefore we get that

\displaystyle(1-|a|_{\infty}^{r-rs})\cdot\mathbb{E}^{Y}(z,s)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(\mathbb{E}^{Y}(z,s)-|a|_{\infty}^{-rs}\mathbb{E}^{Y}(z,s)\Big)-(|a|_{\infty}^{r}-1)\cdot|a|_{\infty}^{-rs}\mathbb{E}^{Y}(z,s)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|Y\|_{A}^{s}\cdot\sum_{0\neq w\in Y/aY}\left[\left(\frac{\im(z)^{s}}{\nu_{z}(w)^{rs}}+\sum_{0\neq\lambda\in aY}\frac{\im(z)^{s}}{\nu_{z}(\lambda-w)^{rs}}\right)-\frac{\mathbb{E}^{aY}(z,s)}{\|aY\|_{A}^{s}}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|Y\|_{A}^{s}\im(z)^{s}\cdot\sum_{0\neq w\in Y/aY}\left[\frac{1}{\nu_{z}(w)^{rs}}+\sum_{0\neq\lambda\in aY}\left(\frac{1}{\nu_{z}(\lambda-w)^{rs}}-\frac{1}{\nu_{z}(\lambda)^{rs}}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\|Y\|_{A}^{s}\im(z)^{s}}{|a|_{\infty}^{rs}}\cdot\sum_{0\neq w\in\frac{1}{a}Y/Y}\left[\frac{1}{\nu_{z}(w)^{rs}}+\sum_{0\neq\lambda\in Y}\left(\frac{1}{\nu_{z}(\lambda-w)^{rs}}-\frac{1}{\nu_{z}(\lambda)^{rs}}\right)\right].

The result then follows from the fact that
\nu_{z}(\lambda-w)=\nu_{z}(\lambda)
if
\nu_{z}(\lambda)>\nu_{z}(w)
.
∎

Moreover, for each
w=a_{1}e_{1}+\cdots+a_{r}e_{r}\in k_{\infty}^{r}-Y
,
define the
Jacobi-type
Eisenstein series:

\mathbb{E}^{Y}(z,w,s)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\sum_{(c_{1},\cdots\hskip-0.85355pt,c_{r})\in Y}\frac{\|Y\|_{A}^{s}\im(z)^{s}}{\big|(c_{1}-a_{1})e_{1}+\cdots+(c_{r}-a_{r})e_{r}\big|_{z}^{rs}}=\|Y\|_{A}^{s}\cdot\sum_{\lambda\in Y}\frac{\im(z)^{s}}{\nu_{z}(\lambda-w)^{rs}},

which also converges absolutely for
\operatorname{Re}(s)>1
because of the following equality:

(5.1.4)

\|Y\|_{A}^{-s}\cdot\left(\mathbb{E}^{Y}(z,w,s)-\mathbb{E}^{Y}(z,s)\right)=\frac{\im(z)^{s}}{\nu_{z}(w)^{rs}}+\sum_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{0\neq\lambda\in Y}{\nu_{z}(\lambda)\leq\nu_{z}(w)}}\left(\frac{\im(z)^{s}}{\nu_{z}(\lambda-w)^{rs}}-\frac{\im(z)^{s}}{\nu_{z}(\lambda)^{rs}}\right).

Since the right hand side of the above equality is a finite sum, we also have the meromorphic continuation of
\mathbb{E}^{Y}(z,w,s)
to the whole complex
s
-plane
with
\mathbb{E}^{Y}(z,w,0)=0
.
We now derive a Kronecker-type limit formula for
\mathbb{E}^{Y}(z,s)
and
\mathbb{E}^{Y}(z,w,s)
:

Theorem 5.1.5
.

Given
z\in\Omega_{\infty}^{r}
and
w\in\mathbb{C}_{\infty}^{r}
, we have that

(5.1.6)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial s}\mathbb{E}^{Y}(z,s)\Big|_{s=0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\ln\big(\|Y\|_{A}\cdot\im(z)\big)-\frac{r}{q^{r}-1}\cdot\ln|\Delta^{Y}(z)|,

(5.1.7)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial s}\mathbb{E}^{Y}(z,w,s)\Big|_{s=0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{r}{q_{\infty}^{r}-1}\cdot\ln|\Delta^{Y}(z)|+r\ln|\mathfrak{k}^{Y}_{w}(z)|.

Proof.

From the expression of
\mathbb{E}^{Y}(z,s)
in Lemma
5.1.3
, we get

\displaystyle\hskip 2.84544pt\frac{\partial}{\partial s}\mathbb{E}^{Y}(z,s)\Big|_{s=0}

\displaystyle=-\ln\big(\|Y\|_{A}\cdot\im(z)\big)-\frac{r}{|a|_{\infty}^{r}-1}\cdot\left[\ln|a|_{\infty}-\sum_{w\in\frac{1}{a}Y/Y}^{\prime}\left(\ln\nu_{z}(w)+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\lambda\in Y\\
\nu_{z}(\lambda)\leq\nu_{z}(w)\end{subarray}}^{\prime}\ln\nu_{z}\Big(1-\frac{w}{\lambda}\Big)\right)\right]

\displaystyle=-\ln\big(\|Y\|_{A}\cdot\im(z)\big)-\frac{r}{|a|_{\infty}^{r}-1}\cdot\Big(\ln|a|_{\infty}+\sum_{w\in\frac{1}{a}Y/Y}^{\prime}\ln|\mathfrak{k}_{w}^{Y}(z)|\Big)

\displaystyle=-\ln\big(\|Y\|_{A}\cdot\im(z)\big)-\frac{r}{|a|_{\infty}^{r}-1}\cdot\ln|\Delta_{a}^{Y}(z)|=-\ln\big(\|Y\|_{A}\cdot\im(z)\big)-\frac{r}{q_{\infty}^{r}-1}\cdot\ln|\Delta^{Y}(z)|

where the last equality comes from Lemma
4.2.4
.
Moreover, the description of
\mathbb{E}^{Y}(z,w,s)
in the equation (
5.1.4
) implies that

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial s}\mathbb{E}^{Y}(z,w,s)\Big|_{s=0}

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial}{\partial s}\mathbb{E}^{Y}(z,s)\Big|_{s=0}+\ln\big(\|Y\|_{A}\cdot\im(z)\big)-r\ln\nu_{z}(w)-r\cdot\sum_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{0\neq\lambda\in Y}{\nu_{z}(\lambda)\leq\nu_{z}(w)}}\ln\nu_{z}\Big(1-\frac{w}{\lambda}\Big)

\displaystyle=-\frac{r}{q_{\infty}^{r}-1}\cdot\ln|\Delta^{Y}(z)|+r\ln|\mathfrak{k}^{Y}_{w}(z)|

as desired.
∎

Remark 5.1.8
.

(1)

The first limit formula (
5.1.6
) can be reformulated into

\frac{\partial}{\partial s}\mathbb{E}^{Y}(z,s)\Big|_{s=0}=-\ln\eta_{r}^{Y}(z),

where
\eta_{r}^{Y}
is the absolute discriminant introduced in Definition
4.2.9
.

(2)

When
w
is in
k^{r}\setminus Y
,
put
\mathfrak{u}_{w}^{Y}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=(\mathfrak{k}_{w}^{Y})^{q_{\infty}^{r}-1}/\Delta^{Y}
, which is a “Drinfeld–Siegel unit” on
\Omega_{\infty}^{r}
(see
[
38
, Section 4.1]
).
Then the second-limit formula in (
5.1.7
) can be rewritten as

\frac{\partial}{\partial s}\mathbb{E}^{Y}(z,w,s)\Big|_{s=0}=\frac{r}{q^{r}_{\infty}-1}\ln|\mathfrak{u}_{w}^{Y}(z)|.

(3)

The formulas in Theorem
5.1.5
can be also extended to an “adelic” version as demonstrated in
[
38
, Theorem 4.4]
. However, as the primary focus of this paper is unrelated to automorphic forms, we choose to present the formulas in their current form, which serves as a precise analogue of the classical case. This approach is sufficient for the purposes of our discussion.

6.
Kronecker-type limit formula: several variable case

Let
K
be a finite extension of
k
. As the constant field of
k
is perfect, one has that (see
[
32
, Theorem 7.6]
)

K_{\infty}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=K\otimes_{k}k_{\infty}\cong\prod_{i=1}^{m}K_{\infty_{i}},

where
\infty_{1},...,\infty_{m}
are the places of
K
lying above
\infty
, and
K_{\infty_{i}}
is the completion of
K
at
\infty_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq m
. Put
n_{i}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=[K_{\infty_{i}}:k_{\infty}]
.
Let
\mathcal{O}
be an
A
-order in
K
.
Given a positive integer
r
and a projective
\mathcal{O}
-module
\mathcal{L}
of rank
r
in
K^{r}
,
we are interested in the following “non-holomorphic” Eisenstein series in several variables: let
\Omega_{\infty_{i}}^{r}
be the Berkovich Drinfeld period domain of rank
r
over
K_{\infty_{i}}
for
1\leq i\leq m
.
For
\boldsymbol{z}=(z^{(1)},\cdots\hskip-0.85355pt,z^{(m)})\in\prod_{i=1}^{m}\Omega_{\infty_{i}}^{r}
, define

\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{O}}^{\mathcal{L}}(\boldsymbol{z},s)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\|\mathcal{L}\|_{\mathcal{O}}^{s}\cdot\sum_{\lambda\in(\mathcal{L}-\{0\})/\mathcal{O}^{\times}}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{m}\frac{\im(z^{(i)})^{s}}{|\lambda|_{z^{(i)}}^{rs}}\right),\quad\forall\operatorname{Re}(s)>1.

Here

\|\mathcal{L}\|_{\mathcal{O}}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=|a|_{\infty}^{-rn}\cdot\#(\mathcal{O}^{r}/a\mathcal{L})
for nonzero
a\in A
with
a\mathcal{L}\subset\mathcal{O}^{r}
, which is independent of the chosen
a\in A
.

Following a similar argument as Lemma
5.1.1
, it can be verified that
\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{O}}^{\mathcal{L}}(\boldsymbol{z},s)
converges absolutely for
\operatorname{Re}(s)>1
.
Here we derive a conceptual link between
\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{O}}^{\mathcal{L}}(\boldsymbol{z},s)
and a corresponding “one-variable” Eisenstein series, which assures the convergence property of
\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{O}}^{\mathcal{L}}(\boldsymbol{z},s)
as well.

6.1.
Integral representation of the non-holomorphic Eisenstein series

For the rest of this paper, we fix an identification

(6.1.1)

\iota_{K/k}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\prod_{i=1}^{m}\iota_{i}:\prod_{i=1}^{m}K_{\infty_{i}}\mathrel{{\mathop{\longrightarrow}\limits}}k_{\infty}^{n_{i}}

once and for all (where
\iota_{i}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\iota_{K_{\infty_{i}}/k_{\infty}}:K_{\infty_{i}}\cong k_{\infty}^{n_{i}}
is as in (
2.3.3
)),
and extends to

(6.1.2)

\iota_{K/k,r}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\prod_{i=1}^{m}\iota_{i,r}:\prod_{i=1}^{m}K_{\infty_{i}}^{r}\mathrel{{\mathop{\longrightarrow}\limits}}\prod_{i=1}^{m}k_{\infty}^{n_{i}r}\quad\text{ for every $r\in\mathbb{N}$,}

where

\iota_{i,r}(x^{(i)}_{1},\cdots\hskip-0.85355pt,x^{(i)}_{r})\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\big(\iota_{i}(x^{(i)}_{1}),\cdots\hskip-0.85355pt,\iota_{i}(x^{(i)}_{r})\big),\quad\forall(x^{(i)}_{1},\cdots\hskip-0.85355pt,x^{(i)}_{r})\in K_{\infty_{i}}^{r}.

Recall that we let
\text{res}:\prod_{i=1}^{m}\mathcal{N}^{r}(K_{\infty_{i}})\hookrightarrow\prod_{i=1}^{m}\mathcal{N}^{n_{i}r}(k_{\infty})
be the “restriction of scalars” in Corollary
2.3.8
.
For
\mathbf{t}=(t_{1},\cdots\hskip-0.85355pt,t_{m})\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
and
\nu_{\boldsymbol{z}}=(\nu_{z^{(1)}},\cdots\hskip-0.85355pt,\nu_{z^{(m)}})\in\prod_{i=1}^{m}\mathcal{N}^{r}(K_{\infty_{i}})
,
the norm
\text{res}(\nu_{\boldsymbol{z}})(\mathbf{t})
on
\prod_{i=1}^{m}k_{\infty}^{n_{i}r}\cong k_{\infty}^{nr}
is given by:

\displaystyle\text{res}(\nu_{\boldsymbol{z}})(\mathbf{t})(x_{1},\cdots\hskip-0.85355pt,x_{m})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\max\Big(\exp(t_{i})\cdot\text{res}(\nu_{z^{(i)}})(x_{i})\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\max\Big(\exp(t_{i})\cdot\nu_{z^{(i)}}\big(x^{(i)}\big)^{1/n_{i}}\Big),\quad\forall(x_{1},\cdots\hskip-0.85355pt,x_{m})\in\prod_{i=1}^{m}k_{\infty}^{n_{i}r},

where
x^{(i)}=\iota_{i,r}^{-1}(x_{i})\in(K_{\infty_{i}})^{r}
.
By Remark
3.2.3
, the section
\tilde{\varsigma}:\mathcal{N}^{nr}(k_{\infty})\rightarrow\widetilde{\Omega}_{\infty}^{nr}
sends

{\rm res}(\nu_{\boldsymbol{z}})({\bf t})
to a point in
\widetilde{\Omega}_{\infty}^{nr}
. We denote this point by
\tilde{\bf z}(\boldsymbol{z};{\bf t})
and let
{\bf z}(\boldsymbol{z};{\bf t})
be the corresponding class in
\Omega_{\infty}^{nr}
.

On the other hand, regarding
K
as a
k
-vector space of dimension
n
, we fix a
k
-basis

(6.1.3)

\beta_{\circ}=\{\lambda_{1},...,\lambda_{n}\}\quad\text{of $K$ once and for all.}

For each
r\in\mathbb{N}
, let
\{e_{1},...,e_{r}\}
be the standard
K
-basis of
K^{r}
.
Then

(6.1.4)

\displaystyle\|\mathfrak{I}^{-1}\|_{\mathcal{O}}^{s}\cdot\sum_{\lambda\in(\mathfrak{I}^{-1}-\{0\})/\mathcal{O}^{\times}}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{m}\frac{1}{|\lambda|_{i}^{n_{i}s}}\right)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{O}}^{\mathfrak{I}^{-1}}(\boldsymbol{z}_{K},s),

where

On the other hand,
For each

Let

Recall in (

7.2.

Appendix A
An “Automorphic” interpretation of several-variable Eisenstein series

In this appendix, we provide a conceptual and explicit description of how the several-variable Eisenstein series studied in this paper relate to mirabolic Eisenstein series on general linear groups.

A.1.
Mirabolic Eisenstein series

Let
K
be a finite extension of
k
as before, and let
\mathbb{A}_{K}
denote the adele ring of
K
.
We write
|\cdot|_{\mathbb{A}_{K}}
for the adelic norm on the idele group
\mathbb{A}_{K}^{\times}
.
For a positive integer
r
, let
S(\mathbb{A}_{K}^{r})
be the space of Schwartz functions on
\mathbb{A}_{K}^{r}
(i.e. locally constant and compactly supported
\mathbb{C}
-valued functions).
Given a unitary Hecke character
\chi:K^{\times}\backslash\mathbb{A}_{K}^{\times}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{\times}
and a Schwartz function
\phi\in S(\mathbb{A}_{K}^{r})
,
the
Goldman section associated with
\chi
and
\phi
is defined by (see
[
23
, (4.1)]
or
[
33
, p. 119]
): for every
g\in\GL_{r}(\mathbb{A}_{K})
,

\Phi(g,s,\chi,\phi)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=|\det g|_{\mathbb{A}_{K}}^{s}\cdot\int_{\mathbb{A}_{K}^{\times}}\phi\big(a\cdot(0,\cdots\hskip-0.85355pt,0,1)g\big)\chi(a)|a|_{\mathbb{A}_{K}}^{-rs}\,d^{\times}a,

which converges absolutely for
\operatorname{Re}(s)>1/r
and admits meromorphic continuation to the whole complex
s
-plane.
Here we normalize the Haar measure
d^{\times}a
on
\mathbb{A}_{K}^{\times}
so that
\text{vol}(O_{\mathbb{A}_{K}}^{\times},d^{\times}a)=1
,
where
O_{\mathbb{A}_{K}}
is the maximal compact subring of
\mathbb{A}_{K}
.
Let

P_{r}=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}a&*\\
0&d\end{pmatrix}\in\GL_{r}\ \bigg|\ a\in\GL_{r-1},\ d\in\GL_{1}\right\}.

Then for every
b=\begin{pmatrix}a&*\\
0&d\end{pmatrix}\in P_{r}(\mathbb{A}_{K})
and
g\in\GL_{r}(\mathbb{A}_{K})
, one has

\Phi(bg,s,\chi,\phi)=|\det a|_{\mathbb{A}_{K}}^{s}\chi(d)|d|_{\mathbb{A}_{K}}^{(1-r)s}\cdot\Phi(g,s,\chi,\phi).

The
mirabolic Eisenstein series on
\GL_{r}(\mathbb{A}_{K})
associated with
\chi
and
\phi
is then defined by

\mathcal{E}(g,s,\chi,\phi)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\sum_{\gamma\in P_{r}(K)\backslash\GL_{r}(K)}\Phi(\gamma g,s,\chi,\phi),\quad\operatorname{Re}(s)>1.

This series admits meromorphic continuation and satisfies the functional equation (see
[
23
, Sec. 4]
or
[
33
, p. 120]
)

\mathcal{E}(g,s,\chi,\phi)=\mathcal{E}({}^{t}g^{-1},1-s,\chi^{-1},\widehat{\phi}),

where
\widehat{\phi}\in S(\mathbb{A}_{K}^{r})
is the Fourier transform of
\phi
:

\widehat{\phi}(x)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\int_{\mathbb{A}_{K}^{r}}\phi(y)\psi_{K}(x\cdot{}^{t}y)dy.

Here
\psi_{K}:\mathbb{A}_{K}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{\times}
is a nontrivial continuous additive character on
\mathbb{A}_{K}
trivial on
K
,
and the Haar measure
dy
on
\mathbb{A}_{K}^{r}
is self-dual with respect to
\psi_{K}
.
Moreover, the Eisenstein series
E(g,s,\chi,\phi)
can also be expressed as the following form (see
[
33
, p. 119]
):

(A.1.1)

\mathcal{E}(g,s,\chi,\phi)=|\det g|_{\mathbb{A}_{K}}^{s}\cdot\int_{K^{\times}\backslash\mathbb{A}_{K}^{\times}}\Big(\sum_{0\neq x\in K^{r}}\phi(axg)\Big)\chi(a)|a|_{\mathbb{A}_{K}}^{-rs}d^{\times}a,\quad\operatorname{Re}(s)>1.

In particular, its behavior under right translation is given by

(A.1.2)

\mathcal{E}(gg^{\prime},s,\chi,\phi)=|\det g^{\prime}|_{\mathbb{A}_{K}}^{s}\cdot\mathcal{E}(g,s,\chi,\varrho(g^{\prime})\phi),\quad\forall g,g^{\prime}\in\GL_{r}(\mathbb{A}_{K}),

where
\varrho(g^{\prime})\phi(x)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\phi(xg^{\prime})
for every
x\in\mathbb{A}_{K}^{r}
.

A.2.
Conceptual link to several-variable Eisenstein series

Let
\infty_{1},...,\infty_{m}
be the places of
K
lying above the infinite place
\infty
of
k
.
Then

Then the Schwartz space
S\big((\mathbb{A}_{K}^{\infty})^{r}\big)

\mathbf{1}_{\hat{\boldsymbol{w}}+\widehat{\mathcal{L}}}

runs through all projective

-modules of rank

and
\hat{\boldsymbol{w}}\in(\mathbb{A}_{K}^{\infty})^{r}

,
the intersection
K^{r}\cap\widehat{\mathcal{L}}g^{\infty}
is still a projective

-module of rank

in
K^{r}
.
Hence the Schwartz space
S\big((\mathbb{A}_{K}^{\infty})^{r}\big)

can be spanned by
and
\varrho(g^{\infty})\mathbf{1}_{\hat{\boldsymbol{w}}+\widehat{O}_{K}^{r}}

for
g^{\infty}
varying in
\GL_{r}(\mathbb{A}_{K}^{\infty})
and
\hat{\boldsymbol{w}}\in(\mathbb{A}_{K}^{\infty})^{r}
,
where

\varrho^{\infty}(g^{\infty})\phi^{\infty}(x)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\phi(xg^{\infty})\quad\text{ for every $\phi^{\infty}\in S\big((\mathbb{A}_{K}^{\infty})^{r}\big)$
and $x\in(\mathbb{A}_{K}^{\infty})^{r}$.}

Put
\mathbb{I}_{K}^{\infty}:=K^{\times}\backslash(\mathbb{A}_{K}^{\infty})^{\times}\cong K^{\times}\backslash\mathbb{A}_{K}^{\times}/\prod_{i=1}^{m}K_{\infty_{i}}^{\times}

Let
\widehat{\mathbb{I}}_{K}^{\infty}

For each

which is well-defined since the expression in (

\mathcal{E}\Big(g,s,\chi,\phi^{\infty}\otimes(\overset{m}{\underset{i=1}{\otimes}}\mathbf{1}_{O_{K_{\infty_{i}}}^{r}})\Big)=0

In particular, one checks that

\mathcal{E}\Big(g,s,\chi,\phi^{\infty}\otimes(\overset{m}{\underset{i=1}{\otimes}}\mathbf{1}_{O_{K_{\infty_{i}}}^{r}})\Big)=\int_{\mathbb{I}_{K}^{\infty}}\overline{\chi(a)}\mathcal{E}^{\infty}(ag,s,\phi^{\infty})d^{\times}a,\quad\forall\chi\in\widehat{\mathbb{I}}_{K}^{\infty},

(A.2.2)

\mathcal{E}^{\infty}(g,s,\phi^{\infty})=|\det g^{\infty}|_{\mathbb{A}_{K}}^{s}\cdot\mathcal{E}^{\infty}\big((1,g_{\infty_{1}},\cdots\hskip-0.85355pt,g_{\infty_{m}}),s,\varrho(g^{\infty})\phi^{\infty}\big).

Moreover:

Proposition A.2.3

Given
g=(g^{\infty},g_{\infty_{1}},\cdots\hskip-0.85355pt,g_{\infty_{m}})\in\GL_{r}(\mathbb{A}_{K})=\GL_{r}(\mathbb{A}_{K}^{\infty})\times\prod_{i=1}^{m}\GL_{r}(K_{\infty_{i}})

\mathcal{L}_{g^{\infty}}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=K^{r}\cap\widehat{O}_{K}^{r}(g^{\infty})^{-1}

For

\mathcal{E}^{\infty}\big(g,s,\mathbf{1}_{\widehat{O}_{K}}\big)=\frac{(q_{K}-1)\cdot\|\mathcal{L}_{g^{\infty}}\|_{O_{K}}^{s}}{\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{m}(1-q_{\infty_{i}}^{-rs})}\cdot\sum_{x\in(\mathcal{L}_{g^{\infty}}-\{0\})/O_{K}^{\times}}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{m}\frac{|\det g_{\infty_{i}}|_{\infty_{i}}^{s}}{\nu_{O_{K_{\infty_{i}}}^{r}}(xg_{\infty_{i}})^{rs}}\right).

Here

K_{\infty_{i}}^{r}
, i.e.

\displaystyle\nu_{O^{r}_{K_{\infty_{i}}}}(a_{1},\cdots\hskip-0.85355pt,a_{r})

\displaystyle\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\max\big(|a_{i}|_{\infty_{i}}\ \big|\ 1\leq i\leq r\big)

\displaystyle\,=\min\big(|c|_{\infty_{i}}\ \big|\ (a_{1},\cdots\hskip-0.85355pt,a_{r})\in cO^{r}_{K_{\infty_{i}}}\big),\quad\forall(a_{1},\cdots\hskip-0.85355pt,a_{r})\in K_{\infty_{i}}^{r}.

Moreover, for
\hat{\boldsymbol{w}}\in K^{r}\setminus O_{K}^{r}
, let
\boldsymbol{w}\in K^{r}\setminus\mathcal{L}_{g^{\infty}}
such that
\boldsymbol{w}-\hat{\boldsymbol{w}}g^{\infty}\in\widehat{\mathcal{L}}_{g^{\infty}}
.
Then

\mathcal{E}^{\infty}\big(g,s,\mathbf{1}_{\hat{\boldsymbol{w}}+\widehat{O}_{K}^{r}}\big)=\frac{\|\mathcal{L}_{g^{\infty}}\|_{O_{K}}^{s}}{\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{m}(1-q_{\infty_{i}}^{-rs})}\cdot\sum_{x\in(\boldsymbol{w}+\mathcal{L}_{g^{\infty}})/O_{K,\mathfrak{C}_{\boldsymbol{w}}}^{(1)}}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{m}\frac{|\det g_{\infty_{i}}|_{\infty_{i}}^{s}}{\nu_{O^{r}_{K_{\infty_{i}}}}(xg_{\infty_{i}})^{rs}}\right).

Proof.

Observe that

|\det g^{\infty}|_{\mathbb{A}_{K}}^{s}=\|\mathcal{L}_{g^{\infty}}\|_{O_{K}},\quad\varrho(g^{\infty})\mathbf{1}_{\widehat{O}_{K}^{r}}(x)=\mathbf{1}_{\widehat{\mathcal{L}}_{g^{\infty}}}(x)

and

\mathbf{1}_{\widehat{\mathcal{L}}_{g^{\infty}}}(ux)=\mathbf{1}_{\widehat{\mathcal{L}}_{g^{\infty}}}(x)
for every
u\in\widehat{O}_{K}^{\times}
and
x\in(\mathbb{A}_{K}^{\infty})^{r}
.
Let
h=\#\operatorname{Pic}(O_{K})
and take
a_{1},...,a_{h}\in\mathbb{A}_{K}^{\infty}
be representatives of the cosets in
\mathbb{I}_{K}^{\infty}/\widehat{O}_{K}^{\times}\cong\operatorname{Pic}(O_{K})
.
Without loss of generality, assume that
a_{1}=1
.
Put
K_{\infty}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\prod_{i=1}^{m}K_{\infty_{i}}
.
Then we have the following identification:

K^{\times}\backslash\mathbb{A}_{K}^{\times}\mathrel{{\mathop{\longleftrightarrow}\limits}}\bigcup_{1\leq j\leq m}^{\cdot}a_{j}\widehat{O}_{K}^{\times}\times\Big(K_{\infty}^{\times}/O_{K}^{\times}\Big).

Let
g_{\infty}=(g_{\infty_{1}},\cdots\hskip-0.85355pt,g_{\infty_{m}})\in\GL_{r}(K_{\infty})=\prod_{i=1}^{m}\GL_{r}(K_{\infty_{i}})
and
O_{K_{\infty}}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\prod_{i=1}^{m}O_{K_{\infty_{i}}}
.
Then

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{\infty}(g,s,\mathbf{1}_{\widehat{O}_{K}^{r}})

\displaystyle=|\det g^{\infty}|_{\mathbb{A}_{K}}^{rs}\cdot\mathcal{E}^{\infty}\big((1,g_{\infty}),s,\varrho(g^{\infty})\mathbf{1}_{\widehat{O}_{K}^{r}}\big)\hskip 36.98866pt\text{(by \eqref{eqn: m-E-3})}

\displaystyle\hskip-42.67912pt=\|\mathcal{L}_{g^{\infty}}\|_{O_{K}}^{s}\cdot\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{m}|\det g_{\infty_{i}}|_{\infty_{i}}^{s}}{\text{vol}(\mathbb{I}_{K}^{\infty},d^{\times}\bar{a})}

\displaystyle\hskip-31.2982pt\cdot\sum_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{\chi\in\widehat{\mathbb{I}}_K^{\infty}}{\chi(\widehat{O}_K^{\times})=1}}\sum_{j=1}^{h}\int_{K_{\infty}^{\times}/O_{K}^{\times}}\left(\sum_{0\neq x\in K^{r}}\mathbf{1}_{\widehat{\mathcal{L}}_{g^{\infty}}}(a_{i}x)\cdot\chi(a_{i})|a_{i}|_{\mathbb{A}_{K}}^{-rs}\cdot\mathbf{1}_{O_{K_{\infty}}^{r}}(a_{\infty}xg_{\infty})\right)|a_{\infty}|_{\mathbb{A}_{K}}^{-rs}d^{\times}a_{\infty}.

As
\text{vol}(\mathbb{I}_{K}^{\infty},d^{\times}\bar{a})=\#\operatorname{Pic}(O_{K})/(q_{K}-1)
and

\sum_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{\chi\in\widehat{\mathbb{I}}_K^{\infty}}{\chi(\widehat{O}_K^{\times})=1}}\sum_{j=1}^{h}\chi(a_{j})=\begin{cases}\#\operatorname{Pic}(O_{K})&\text{ if $j=1$,}\\
0&\text{ otherwise,}\end{cases}

we get

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{\infty}(g,s,\mathbf{1}_{\widehat{O}_{K}^{r}})

\displaystyle=(q_{K}-1)\cdot\|\mathcal{L}_{g^{\infty}}\|_{O_{K}}^{s}\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{m}|\det g_{\infty_{i}}|_{\infty_{i}}^{s}

\displaystyle\ \ \ \cdot\int_{K_{\infty}^{\times}/O_{K}^{\times}}\left(\sum_{0\neq x\in K^{r}}\mathbf{1}_{\widehat{\mathcal{L}}_{g^{\infty}}}(x)\cdot\mathbf{1}_{O_{K_{\infty}}^{r}}(a_{\infty}xg_{\infty})\right)|a_{\infty}|_{\mathbb{A}_{K}}^{-rs}d^{\times}a_{\infty}

\displaystyle=(q_{K}-1)\cdot\|\mathcal{L}_{g^{\infty}}\|_{O_{K}}^{s}\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{m}|\det g_{\infty_{i}}|_{\infty_{i}}^{s}

\displaystyle\ \ \ \cdot\sum_{x\in(\mathcal{L}_{g^{\infty}}-\{0\})/O_{K}^{\times}}\int_{K_{\infty}^{\times}}\mathbf{1}_{O_{K_{\infty}}^{r}}(a_{\infty}xg_{\infty})|a_{\infty}|_{\mathbb{A}_{K}}^{-rs}d^{\times}a_{\infty}.

Finally, as for
1\leq i\leq m
,

\displaystyle\int_{K_{\infty_{i}}^{\times}}\mathbf{1}_{O_{K_{\infty_{i}}}^{r}}(a_{\infty_{i}}xg_{\infty_{i}})|a_{\infty_{i}}|_{\infty_{i}}^{-rs}d^{\times}a_{\infty_{i}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-q_{\infty_{i}}^{-rs}}\cdot\nu_{O_{K_{\infty_{i}}}^{r}}(xg_{\infty_{i}})^{-rs},

we obtain

\mathcal{E}^{\infty}(g,s,\mathbf{1}_{\widehat{O}_{K}^{r}})=\frac{(q_{K}-1)\cdot\|\mathcal{L}_{g^{\infty}}\|_{O_{K}}^{s}}{\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{m}(1-q_{\infty_{i}}^{-rs})}\cdot\sum_{x\in(\mathcal{L}_{g^{\infty}}-\{0\})/O_{K}^{\times}}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{m}\frac{|\det g_{\infty_{i}}|_{\infty_{i}}^{s}}{\nu_{O_{K_{\infty_{i}}}^{r}}(xg_{\infty_{i}})^{rs}}\right).

For the second statement, we note that
\varrho(g^{\infty})\mathbf{1}_{\hat{\boldsymbol{w}}+\widehat{O}_{K}^{r}}=\mathbf{1}_{\boldsymbol{w}+\widehat{\mathcal{L}}_{g^{\infty}}}
.
Therefore following the same argument, we obtain the desired formula for
\mathcal{E}^{\infty}(g,s,\mathbf{1}_{\hat{\boldsymbol{w}}+\widehat{O}_{K}^{r}})
.
∎

Finally, for each
1\leq i\leq m
and

z^{(i)}\in\Omega_{\infty_{i}}^{r}
satisfying that
\nu_{z^{(i)}}
is equivalent to the norm
g_{\infty_{i}}*\nu_{O_{K_{\infty_{i}}}^{r}}
, i.e.
\mathscr{B}(z)=[g_{\infty_{i}}*\nu_{O_{K_{\infty_{i}}}^{r}}]\in\mathcal{X}^{r}(K_{\infty_{i}})
where
\mathscr{B}:\Omega_{\infty_{i}}^{r}\rightarrow\mathcal{X}^{r}(K_{\infty_{i}})
is the building map introduced in Section
3.2
,
one has that

\frac{|\det g_{\infty_{i}}|_{\infty_{i}}}{\nu_{O_{K_{\infty_{i}}}^{r}}(xg_{\infty_{i}})^{r}}=\frac{\im(z^{(i)})}{\nu_{z^{(i)}}(x)^{r}},\quad\forall x\in K_{\infty_{i}}^{r}-\{0\}.

As a consequence, Proposition
A.2.3
implies that:

Corollary A.2.4
.

Let
g=(g^{\infty},g_{\infty_{1}},\cdots\hskip-0.85355pt,g_{\infty_{m}})\in\GL_{r}(\mathbb{A}^{\infty}_{K})\times\prod_{i=1}^{m}\GL_{r}(K_{\infty_{i}})
.
For every
\boldsymbol{z}=(z^{(1)},\cdots\hskip-0.85355pt,z^{(m)})\in\prod_{i=1}^{m}\Omega_{\infty_{i}}^{r}
with
\mathscr{B}(z^{(i)})=[g_{\infty_{i}}*\nu_{O_{K_{\infty_{i}}}^{r}}]\in\mathcal{X}^{r}(K_{\infty_{i}})
for
1\leq i\leq m
,
we have

\mathcal{E}^{\infty}(g,s,\mathbf{1}_{\widehat{O}^{r}_{K}})=\frac{q_{K}-1}{\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{m}(1-q_{\infty_{i}}^{-rs})}\cdot\mathbb{E}_{O_{K}}^{\mathcal{L}_{g^{\infty}}}(\boldsymbol{z},s).

Moreover, for every
\hat{\boldsymbol{w}}\in(\mathbb{A}_{K}^{\infty})^{r}\setminus\widehat{O}_{K}^{r}
and
\boldsymbol{w}\in K^{r}\setminus\mathcal{L}_{g^{\infty}}
such that
\boldsymbol{w}-\hat{\boldsymbol{w}}\in\widehat{\mathcal{L}}_{g^{\infty}}
,
we get

\mathcal{E}^{\infty}(g,s,\mathbf{1}_{\hat{\boldsymbol{w}}+\widehat{O}_{K}^{r}})=\frac{1}{\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{m}(1-q_{\infty_{i}}^{-rs})}\cdot\mathbb{E}_{O_{K}}^{\mathcal{L}_{g^{\infty}}}(\boldsymbol{z},\boldsymbol{w},s).

Remark A.2.5
.

As the Schwartz space
S\big((\mathbb{A}_{K}^{\infty})^{r}\big)
is spanned by
\varrho(g^{\infty})\mathbf{1}_{\hat{\boldsymbol{w}}+\widehat{O}_{K}^{r}}
for
g^{\infty}\in\GL_{r}(\mathbb{A}_{K}^{\infty})
and
\hat{\boldsymbol{w}}\in(\mathbb{A}_{K}^{\infty})^{r}
(see Remark
A.2.1
), combining the above Corollary with the transformation property (
A.1.2
) allows us to extend the mirabolic Eisenstein series
\mathcal{E}^{\infty}(g,s,\phi)
on
\GL(\mathbb{A}_{K})
to a broader space
\GL_{r}(\mathbb{A}_{K}^{\infty})\times\prod_{i=1}^{m}\Omega_{\infty_{i}}^{r}
. This would lead to an “adelic formulation” of our Kronecker limit formula, generalizing the one-variable case in
[
38
, Theorem 1.1]
.
Given the essential role of mirabolic Eisenstein series in the study of automorphic
L
-functions, we expect that this perspective–particularly through the lens of the Berkovich structure on the Drinfeld period domains–will provide new insights into the arithmetic of special values of automorphic
L
-functions. These directions will be explored in future work.

Decomposing graphs into stable and ordered parts
Hector Buffière, Patrice Ossona de Mendez

Abstract
Connections between structural graph theory and finite model theory recently gained a lot of attention. In this setting, many interesting question remain on the properties of hereditary dependent (NIP) classes of graphs, in particular related to first-order transductions. Motivated by Simon’s decomposition theorem of dependent types into a stable part and a distal (order-like) part, we conjecture that every hereditary dependent class of graphs is transduction-equivalent to a hereditary dependent class of partially ordered graphs, where the cover graph of the partial order has bounded treewidth and the unordered graph is (edge) stable. In this paper, we consider the first non-trivial case (classes with bounded linear cliquewidth) and prove that the conjecture holds in a strong form, where the cover graph of the partial order has bounded pathwidth. Then, we extend our study to classes that admit bounded-size bounded linear cliquewidth covers, and prove that the conjecture holds for these classes, too.

Introduction

In recent years, there has been a growing interest in the structural study of hereditary
classes of graphs in the light of model theory. Particularly, since the identification

[
Adler2013
]
of a bridge between the graph theoretical notion of a nowhere dense class
and the model theoretical notions of stability, dependence, and their monadic
counterparts, a particular attention has been given to (the model theoretic notions of)
stability and dependence. These studies are deeply motivated by the conjecture that

FO
-model checking is fixed parameter tractable on a hereditary class of graphs

\mathscr{C}
if and only if the class
\mathscr{C}
is dependent.

These studies recently led to structural and combinatorial characterizations of stability and dependence of hereditary classes of graphs
[
covers
,
svm2024
,
dreier2024flipbreakability
]
.
Of particular interest, for example, is the characterization of hereditary classes of graphs with dependent ordered expansions, which are exactly the classes of graphs with bounded twin-width
[
Tww_ordered
]
.

Despite all these characterization theorems, the theory lacks of some description on how dependent hereditary classes of graphs can be generated from simple dependent classes, and whether they can be put in a canonical form.

In this context, the next conjecture has been proposed, which characterizes hereditary stable classes of graphs.

Conjecture 1

(
[
RW_SODA
, Conjecture 6.1]
)
.

A hereditary class of graphs is stable if and only if it is a transduction of a nowhere dense class of graphs.

In this line, we conjecture the following description of hereditary dependent classes of graphs.

Conjecture 2
.

Every dependent hereditary class of graph is a transduction of a dependent
hereditary class, consisting of a nowhere dense class of graphs expanded by a tree order.

Note that Conjecture
1
implies, if true, that Conjecture
2

holds for stable hereditary classes of graphs. Also, it is known that
Conjecture
2
holds for classes with bounded twin-width
[
SparseTww
]
(which have a particular model-theoretic meaning, as recalled above).

Dual to Conjecture
2
, it is plausible that the graphs in a dependent hereditary class of graphs can be, in some sense, decomposed into a stable part and an ordered part.
This intuition echoes the result proved by Simon
[
simon2019type
]
, which shows that dependent types decompose into a stable part and an order-like quotient.

Such decomposition results would rely on two notions,

transduction pairing
, which is a strong notion of transduction equivalence
between classes of structures, and
class coupling
, which expresses that a
structure is obtained by putting together relations from simpler structures (See formal
definitions in
\Cref
sec:trans and
\Cref
coupling).

In this setting we conjecture the following.

Conjecture 3
.

Every dependent hereditary class of graph has a transduction pairing with a coupling of a hereditary stable class of graphs and a class of partial orders having bounded treewidth cover graphs.

Note that posets whose cover graph have bounded treewidth are transductions of tree orders (as follows from
[
Colcombet2007
]
)
and have nice properties. For instance, they have bounded Boolean dimension
[
Felsner2020
]
and bounded cliquewidth
1
1

1

If the cover graph has bounded treewidth, then it is an
MSO
-transduction of a tree. As the partial order is an easy
MSO
-transduction of the cover graph, we deduce that the poset is an
MSO
-transduction of a tree, hence has bounded cliquewidth.
. It follows that Conjectures
1
and
3
together imply Conjecture
2
.

As a first step towards Conjecture
3
, we study the first non-trivial case, which is the case of classes with bounded linear cliquewidth, and prove:

Theorem 4
.

Every class of graph with bounded linear cliquewidth has a transduction pairing with a coupling of a class of bounded pathwidth graphs and a class of partial orders with bounded pathwidth cover graphs.

Moreover, in the structure paired with a graph
G
, the height of the partial order is bounded by a linear function of the maximum order of a semi-induced half-graph of
G
.

From this theorem, we deduce the following strengthening.

Theorem 5
.

A class of graphs
\mathscr{C}
has bounded linear cliquewidth if and only if it has
a transduction-pairing with a class of partial orders whose cover graphs have bounded pathwidth.

Note that if
\mathscr{C}
is stable, the poset arising in
\Cref
cor:lcw has
bounded height, and we recover the main result of
[
SODA_msrw
]
:
Every stable hereditary class with bounded linear cliquewidth is a transduction of a
class with bounded pathwidth, and it is actually transduction equivalent to this class.

Then, based on the notion of bounded-size decompositions (See e.g.
[
covers
]
), we extend Theorem
4
to classes admitting bounded-size bounded linear cliquewidth decompositions.

Theorem 6
.

If a hereditary class
\mathscr{C}
admits bounded-size bounded linear cliquewidth decompositions, then it is transduction-equivalent to a hereditary class
\mathscr{D}
such that

•

\mathscr{D}
also admits bounded-size bounded linear cliquewidth decompositions,

•

\mathscr{D}
is a coupling of a bounded expansion class and a class of posets with bounded pathwidth cover graphs.

Note that classes admitting bounded-size bounded linear cliquewidth decompositions encompasses classes with bounded linear cliquewidth and include, for example, the class of proper circular arc graphs and all classes with bounded expansion.

The basic tool used in this paper is the notion of
(\mathscr{D}_{1},\mathscr{D}_{2})
-split
: the vertex set is partitioned into a bounded number of parts, such that each part induces a graph in
\mathscr{D}_{1}
and any two part semi-induce a bipartite graph in
\mathscr{D}_{2}
. Then, transduction equivalences are performed independently on the subgraphs induced by a single part and on the bipartite subgraphs semi-induced by two parts (see Fig
1
).

Figure 1:
The transduction equivalence process as follows: each part
V_{i}
is copied as
V_{i}^{1},\dots,V_{1}^{n}
. The transduction
\mathsf{T}_{1}
(for single part induced subgraphs) is applied on
G[V_{i}^{i}]
, while the transduction
\mathsf{T}_{2}
(for two parts semi-induced subgraphs) is applied on
G[V_{i}^{j},V_{j}^{i}])

are joined to the one in
V_{i}^{i}
.

This paper is structured as follows.
\Cref
sec:prelim reviews the preliminary notions used in the rest of the paper.
In
\Cref
sec:trees, we define and study the notion of semi-plane trees on which our models for tame unstable graph
classes are built. In
\Cref
sec:transductions, we establish some transduction-theoretic tools used in the paper.
In
\Cref
sec:sob, we introduce several notions of models and prove the core transduction pairing result.
This pairing is lifted to more complex classes using decompositions and gluings in
\Cref
sec:gluing. We conclude
with some remarks in
\Cref
sec:conc.

2.1
Structures and logic

A
relational signature

\sigma
is a
finite set of
relation symbols with an arity.
A
\sigma
-structure

\mathbf{M}
consists of a finite
universe
M
and an interpretation of the symbols from the
signature: each
k
-ary relation symbol
U\in\sigma
is
interpreted as a subset
U^{\mathbf{M}}
of
M^{k}
.
If
\mathbf{M}
is a relational structure and
X\subseteq M
,
then we define the
substructure

\mathbf{M}[X]
of
\mathbf{M}

induced by
X
in the usual way.
We may view unary relation symbols as subsets of the domain, and write
x\in A
for

A(x)
.

For a signature
\sigma
, we consider the standard first-order logic over
\sigma
, which we denote by
\mathsf{FO}[\sigma]
(or
\mathsf{FO}
when
\sigma
is clear from the context). For a formula
\phi\in\mathsf{FO}[\sigma]
with
k
free variables and a
\sigma
-structure
\mathbf{M}
we define

\phi(\mathbf{M})=\{(v_{1},\dots,v_{k})\in M^{k}\colon\mathbf{M}\models\phi(v_{1},\dots,v_{k})\}.

2.2
Transductions

For our purpose it is sufficient
to work with the following restricted notion of transductions.
A
transduction
inputs a
\sigma
-structure
and outputs a set of
\sigma^{\prime}
-structures.
It is defined as a composition of
the following types of basic operations:

•

Copying.
A
k
-copying
is an operation that
given a structure
\mathbf{M}
outputs a disjoint union of
k
copies of

\mathbf{M}
extended with a symmetric binary relation which connects each vertex with its copies (which we call
clones
).
The atomic
k
-copying operation is noted
\makebox[6.0pt]{$\mathsf{C}$}_{k}
.

•

Coloring.
A
coloring
extends the
signature
\sigma
by a new unary predicate

U\not\in\sigma
. The output of a coloring applied
to a
\sigma
-structure
\mathbf{M}
is the set of all

\sigma\cup\{U\}
-structures whose restriction to

\sigma
is
\mathbf{M}
, that is, we interpret the unary
predicate in all possible ways. We sometimes speak of a

monadic lift
instead of a coloring.

•

Interpreting.
A
(simple) interpretation

\mathsf{I}

of
\sigma^{\prime}
-structures in
\sigma
-structures is defined by
a formula
\rho_{U}\in\mathsf{FO}[\sigma]
with
k
free variables for each relation symbol
U\in\sigma^{\prime}
with arity
k
and a formula
\nu(x)
with a single free variable.
Given a
\sigma
-structure
\mathbf{M}
, the
\sigma^{\prime}
-structure

\makebox[6.0pt]{$\mathsf{I}$}(\mathbf{M})
has universe
\nu(\mathbf{M})
and, for each
U\in\sigma^{\prime}
, we define
U^{\mathsf{I}(\mathbf{M})}=\rho_{U}(\mathbf{M})
.
The atomic interpretation expanding by a relation
U
defined by
\rho
is noted
\makebox[6.0pt]{$\mathsf{Rel}$}^{\rho\to U}
.

We take time for some comments about interpretations. First, it is useful to agree that if no formula is associated with a symbol
U\in\sigma\cap\sigma^{\prime}
, then it is defined by defaults as itself:
\rho_{U}(\bar{x}):=U(\bar{x})
.
Because of this, an interpretation
\mathsf{I}
of
\sigma^{\prime}
-structure in
\sigma
-structure uniquely extends as an interpretation of

\tau^{\prime}
-structures in
\tau
-structures, for

\tau\supseteq\sigma
and
\tau^{\prime}\subseteq\sigma^{\prime}\cup\tau
(where formulas defining symbols in
\sigma^{\prime}\setminus\tau^{\prime}
are ignored). In particular, when defining an interpretation, it will be sufficient to consider the signature
\sigma
and
\sigma^{\prime}
such that

•

no symbol in
\sigma\cap\sigma^{\prime}
is defined as itself;

•

\sigma
is the set of all the relations used in some formula of the interpretation.

Transductions
are defined inductively: every atomic
transduction is a transduction, and the composition of two
transductions
\mathsf{I}
and
\mathsf{J}
is the transduction

\makebox[6.0pt]{$\mathsf{J}$}\circ\makebox[6.0pt]{$\mathsf{I}$}
that, given a structure
\mathbf{M}
, first applies

\mathsf{I}
to
\mathbf{M}
and then
\mathsf{J}
to every structure
in
\makebox[6.0pt]{$\mathsf{I}$}(\mathbf{M})
and outputs the
union of all resulting sets.
As for interpretations, a transduction from
\sigma
-structures to
\sigma^{\prime}
-structures
uniquely extends to a transduction from
\tau
-structures to
\tau^{\prime}
-structures whenever
\tau\supseteq\sigma
and
\tau^{\prime}\subseteq\sigma^{\prime}\cup\tau
. However, because of the coloring, we also allow
\tau^{\prime}
containing further unary relations, which are (by default) defined by a coloring operation.

As a particular case, let us mention the transduction from
\sigma
-structures to
\sigma^{\prime}
-structures with
\sigma\setminus\sigma
is a set of unary relations defined by an empty interpretation of
\emptyset
-structures in
\emptyset
-structures, which defines all the undefined unary relations by a coloring operation, then
forgets all the relations in
\sigma\setminus\sigma^{\prime}
. In the case where
\sigma^{\prime}=\sigma
, we will speak about the
identity transduction
and denote it by
\mathsf{Id}
. In the case where
\sigma^{\prime}\subset\sigma
, we shall call this transduction a
reduct transduction
and the image of
\sigma
-structure
\mathbf{M}
by this transduction is called the
\sigma^{\prime}
-reduct
of
\mathbf{M}
.

It is worth mentioning that every transduction can be rewritten as the composition of the copying operation, a coloring operation, and an interpretation (in this order)
[
SBE_TOCL
]
.
Of particular interest is the

restriction transduction

\mathsf{R}
, which maps a relational structure
\mathbf{M}
to the set of all its induced substructures.
We say that a class
\mathscr{D}
is a
transduction
of a class
\mathscr{C}
if
\mathscr{D}\subseteq\makebox[6.0pt]{$\mathsf{T}$}(\mathscr{C})
for some transduction
\mathsf{T}
.

Transduction pairing.

A
transduction pairing
of two classes
\mathscr{C}

and
\mathscr{D}
is a pair
(\mathsf{D},\mathsf{C})
of transductions,
such that

\forall\mathbf{A}\in\mathscr{C}\quad\exists\mathbf{B}\in\mathsf{D}(\mathbf{A})\cap\mathscr{D}\quad\mathbf{A}\in\mathsf{C}(\mathbf{B})\quad\text{and}\quad\forall\mathbf{B}\in\mathscr{D}\quad\exists\mathbf{A}\in\mathsf{C}(\mathbf{B})\cap\mathscr{C}\quad\mathbf{B}\in\mathsf{D}(\mathbf{A}).

A special case if given by the existence of a bijection
f:\mathscr{C}\rightarrow\mathscr{D}
such that

\forall\mathbf{A}\in\mathscr{C}
,
f(\mathbf{A})\in\mathsf{D}(\mathbf{A})
and

\forall\mathbf{B}\in\mathscr{D},\ f^{-1}(\mathbf{B})\in\mathsf{C}(\mathbf{B})
.
Indeed, this implies that
(\mathsf{D},\mathsf{C})
is a transduction pairing of
\mathscr{C}
and
\mathscr{D}
.

Class coupling.

Let
\sigma_{1}
and
\sigma_{2}
be disjoint signatures, let
\mathscr{C}_{1}
be a class of
\sigma_{1}
-structures, and let
\mathscr{C}_{2}
be a class of
\sigma_{2}
-structures. A class
\mathscr{D}
of
\sigma_{1}\cup\sigma_{2}
-structures is a
coupling
of
\mathscr{C}_{1}
and
\mathscr{C}_{2}
if, for every
\mathbf{M}\in\mathscr{D}
, the
\sigma_{1}
-reduct of
\mathbf{M}
belongs to
\mathscr{C}_{1}
and the
\sigma_{2}
-reduct of
\mathbf{M}
belongs to
\mathscr{C}_{2}
.

2.3
Partial orders

A
strict partial order
(or
poset
) is a pair
(X,<)

where
X
is a set and
<
is an irreflexive, asymmetric, and transitive binary relation.
A pair
(a,b)
of elements of a poset
P
is a
cover
of
P
if
a<_{P}b
and there exists no element
c
with
a<_{P}c<_{P}b
.
It is customary to denote the cover relation by appending a ‘:’ to the partial order symbol
(
<:
for
<
, or
\prec:
for
\prec
).
Hence,
x<:y
if
x<y
and
x\leq z\leq y
implies
z\in\{x,y\}
.
The
cover graph

H(P)
of
P
is the graph with vertex set
P
, where two elements
a
and
b
are adjacent is either
(a,b)
or
(b,a)
is a cover of
P
. The
cover graph

P
is the oriented graph, where
(a,b)
is an arc if
(a,b)
is a cover.

The
tree-order
defined by a rooted tree
(T,r)
is the partial ordering on the vertices of
T
with
u\prec v
if and only if the unique path from the root to
v
passes through
u
.
This partial order is a
meet-semilattice
, meaning that every two vertices
x,y
of
T
have a greatest lower bound
x\curlywedge y
.

Fact 1
.

A partial order
\prec
is a tree-order if and only if
it satisfies the following conditions:

•

\prec
has a (unique) minimum;

•

for every element
v
, the set of all the elements smaller than
v
is a (possibly empty) chain.

2.4
Graphs and colored graphs

Directed graphs can be viewed as finite structures over the
signature consisting of a binary relation symbol
E
, interpreted
as the edge relation, in the usual way. If
E
is interpreted by
a symmetric and irreflexive relation, then the structure represents
an undirected and loopless graph. When dealing with directed
graphs we
denote edges by
(u,v)
, when dealing with undirected graphs
we denote them by
\{u,v\}
. When we speak of a graph, we
mean an undirected graph. An orientation of a graph
G
is a directed
graph
\vec{G}
that for every
\{u,v\}\in E(G)
has exactly
one of
(u,v)
or
(v,u)
in its arc set.
For a finite label set
\Lambda
, by a
\Lambda
-colored

graph we mean a graph enriched by a unary predicate
U_{\lambda}

for every
\lambda\in\Lambda
.

We say that
G
is a
subgraph
of
H
and write
G\subseteq H
if
V(G)\subseteq V(H)
and
E(G)\subseteq E(H)
.
We say that it is and
induced subgraph
and write
G\subseteq_{i}H
or
G=H[V(G)]
if
E(G)=E(H)\cap(V(G)\times V(G))
.
A bipartite graph
G=(A,B,E(G))
is a
semi-induced subgraph
of
H
if
E(G)=E(H)\cap(A\times B)
, and we shall write
G=H[A,B]
.
A class is called
weakly sparse
(or
biclique-free
) if there is an integer
t
such that
none of its graphs contain a
K_{t,t}
as a subgraph.
A class of graphs
\mathscr{C}
is
degenerate
if there is an integer
d
such that for all
H\subseteq_{i}G\in\mathscr{C}
, the minimum degree of
H
is less than
d
.

(
[
Gurski2000
]
)
.

•

A class has bounded pathwidth if and only if it is weakly sparse and has bounded linear cliquewidth;

(

]

(

(

]
)

•

is an

(
[
Colcombet2007
]
)
.

\mathscr{C}
, the following are equivalent:

has bounded cliquewidth;

•

\mathscr{C}

Class properties

\mathscr{A}
is a

\mathscr{B}

.

\mathscr{D}
.

if for every integer

such that for every

there exists subgraphs

is in

and for every

.

Semi-plane rooted trees

A plane tree is a classical combinatorial object consisting of a tree given with its plane embedding. Equivalently, it can be seen as a tree with a left-to-right ordering of the children of each node.
We define a semi-plane tree
as a tree where internal nodes may or may not have their children linearly ordered. In this section,
we make precise their encoding as logical structures.
We define a signature
\sigma_{1}=\{C,\curlywedge,\mathrel{\vartriangleleft}\}
, where
C
is a unary relation,
\curlywedge
is a binary function, and
\mathrel{\vartriangleleft}
is a binary relation.

The axioms of semi-plane trees, encoded as
\sigma_{1}
structures are:

•

the axioms stating that
\curlywedge
is the infimum function associated to a tree-order
\preceq
(defined by
x\preceq y:=(x\curlywedge y)=x
), namely the standard axioms expressing that
\curlywedge
defines a semilattice (idempotence, commutativity, associativity) and the special property
{(x\curlywedge y=y)}\wedge{(x\curlywedge z=z)}\rightarrow(y\curlywedge z=y)\vee(y\curlywedge z=z)
, expressing that the ancestors of an element form a chain,

•

an axiom stating that the elements in
C
are not maximal in the tree-order
\preceq
;

•

an axiom stating that
\vartriangleleft
is a partial order in which two elements are comparable if and only if they are covered by a common element that is in
C
.

It will be convenient to define additional relations and functions from those in
\sigma_{1}
. Towards this end,
we define the signature
\sigma=(C,\curlywedge,\vartriangleleft,<,E,L,A,r,\pi,{\rm st})
and an interpretation
\makebox[6.0pt]{$\mathsf{I}$}_{\sigma_{1}\rightarrow\sigma}
of
\sigma
-structures in
\sigma_{1}
-structures (see
\Cref
tab:sigma).

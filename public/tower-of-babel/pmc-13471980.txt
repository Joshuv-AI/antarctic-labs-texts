Lattice‐Matched 2D Template Enables Efficient Cesium Tin Halide Perovskite Solar Cells
Authors: Hongzhe Anna Xu, Dongxu He, Wencai Zhou, Julian A. Steele, Zhiliang Wang, Zitong Wang, Chengxi Zhang, Shanshan Ding, Bowei Zhang, Huiyuan Cheng, Mengmeng Hao, Jiangyu Hang, Can Zheng, Zilong Zheng, Peng Chen, Lianzhou Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTCesium tin iodide (CsSnI3) has emerged as a promising inorganic light‐absorber for lead‐free perovskite solar cells (PSCs) due to its ideal bandgap of 1.3 eV and intrinsic thermal stability. However, its application is limited by random crystallization and spontaneous δ‐CsSnI3 phase transition, causing substantial photovoltaic performance losses. Here, we employ lattice‐matched 2D perovskite templates to induce the oriented growth of 3D perovskites, enabling phase‐pure 2D/3D perovskite heterostructures for efficient and durable CsSnI3‐based PSCs. Compared to their alkyl counterparts, 2D perovskites adopting aromatic spacer cations introduce additional interlayer π–π stacking to inhibit octahedral tilting and minimize their lattice spacing mismatch with 3D CsSnI3. This improved crystallographic compatibility promotes oriented growth of 2D/3D heterostructures along the (110) plane, regulating the crystallization kinetics and creating an additional energy barrier that suppresses δ‐CsSnI3 phase formation. As a result, the optimized CsSnI3‐based PSCs deliver a champion power conversion efficiency (PCE) of 15.27% with a high open‐circuit voltage of 0.90 V. Benefiting from reduced trap states and eliminated δ‐CsSnI3 phase impurities, the target devices exhibit markedly improved operational stability, retaining over 95% of their initial PCE after 1280 h at maximum power point tracking under continuous one‐sun illumination in nitrogen without encapsulation.

Introduction

The last 15 years have witnessed a rapid increase in the power conversion efficiency (PCE) of metal halide perovskite solar cells (PSCs) from 3.8% to certified 27.3%, which surpasses other thin‐film photovoltaic (PV) technologies and is on par with that (27.8%) of crystalline silicon solar cells [1, 2]. However, the inclusion of toxic and water‐soluble lead ions (Pb2+) in PSCs remains a considerable public health and environmental concern, hindering their large‐scale commercialization. Such an intractable toxicity issue motivates the exploration of eco‐friendly alternatives without compromising PV performance. Among lead‐free analogues, tin halide perovskites (THPs) have emerged as the most efficient candidates because of their excellent optoelectronic properties, including ideal bandgaps (1.3–1.4 eV) for PVs, high charge carrier mobility, and low exciton binding energies [3, 4]. Substituting hazardous Pb2+ with less toxic Sn2+, THP compounds possess a chemical composition of ASnX3, where A is monovalent cation such as methylammonium (CH3NH3 +, MA+), formamidinium (HC(NH2)2 +, FA+), and cesium (Cs+), and X are halides. Compared to organic‐inorganic hybrid THPs (e.g., FASnI3 and MASnI3), all‐inorganic CsSnI3 exhibits superior intrinsic stability, attributed to much stronger ionic interaction between Cs+ and [SnX6]4− octahedral framework than the weak hydrogen bonding from organic cations of MA+ or FA+ [4]. Moreover, CsSnI3 presents a narrower bandgap (∼1.3 eV) that is closer to the balanced limit (1.33 eV) of single‐junction solar cells with a theoretical efficiency exceeding 33% [5, 6, 7]. Despite intriguing optoelectronic properties, CsSnI3 suffers from fast crystallization kinetics and facile oxidation of Sn2+ during fabrication, resulting in poor film quality with abundant defects. Various crystallization regulation strategies, such as compositional [8, 9, 10] additive [11, 12, 13] and interfacial engineering [14, 15, 16] were designed to retard fast crystallization and suppress Sn2+ oxidation of CsSnI3 during fabrication. However, the spontaneous γ‐to‐δ phase transition of CsSnI3 remains an unresolved challenge that markedly deteriorates their PV performance and device lifespan. Unlike FASnI3 with thermodynamically stable black phases at >255 K [17], CsSnI3 undergoes a spontaneous γ‐to‐δ phase transition at room temperature (∼300K) [18]. Consequently, CsSnI3‐based PSCs exhibit inferior PV performance (PCE < 15%) and operational stability than their hybrid counterparts, which mainly arises from its intrinsic phase instability at room temperature [16]. These non‐perovskite δ‐phase impurities not only serve as non‐radiative recombination centers to impair charge carrier transport but also introduce additional oxygen or moisture permeation channels that accelerate Sn2+ oxidation and degradation of the perovskite layer [19, 20, 21].

Although Br doping can mitigate octahedral tilting of CsSnI3 by shortening the bond length, the inevitable phase segregation continues to undermine the intrinsic stability of all‐inorganic tin‐based perovskites [8, 9, 10]. Even with an improved operational stability under continuous illumination, CsSnI3 still suffers from the undesirable γ‐to‐δ phase transition. Template‐assisted growth has emerged as an effective means to suppress undesirable phase transitions in perovskite systems [22]. In particular, 2D perovskites offer unique advantages owing to their structural tunability. The incorporation of different organic ligands can finely adjust the lattice spacing, while aromatic spacer cations introduce additional interlayer π–π interactions that reinforce the framework and providing additional rigidity [23, 24, 25, 26]. Such stiff frameworks not only facilitate controlled template growth but also promote the oriented crystallization of 3D perovskite phases [26, 27]. How to identify a lattice‐matched 2D template for directing the crystallization kinetics of 3D CsSnI3 into a stable and phase‐pure structure is critical for performance improvement of inorganic tin halide perovskite solar cells.

In this work, we develop a lattice‐matched 2D template of PEA2CsSn2I7 to induce the oriented growth of δ‐phase‐free CsSnI3 films for lead‐free PSCs exceeding 15% efficiency. By integrating computational predictions with experimental validation, we reveal that conjugated spacer cations at the interlayer can modulate the octahedral tilting of 2D phases and optimize their lattice match with 3D γ‐CsSnI3 along the (110) plane, therefore governing the template growth of 2D/3D heterostructures with relaxed strain at the heterointerface. In situ photoluminescence (PL) spectroscopy and X‐ray diffraction (XRD) studies confirm that the emerging 2D perovskite nuclei can direct the templated growth of γ‐CsSnI3 with suppressed δ‐phase impurities. As a result, the optimized 2D/3D PSCs achieved an excellent PCE of 15.27% with a high open‐circuit voltage (V oc) of 0.90 V, representing the highest reported values for CsSnI3‐based PSCs to date. Benefiting from reduced trap states and mitigated phase impurities, the target 2D/3D devices retained 96% and 95% of their initial PCEs after 4800 h dark storage and 1280 h at maximum power point (MPP) tracking under continuous one‐sun illumination without encapsulation in nitrogen, respectively. This work offers a new strategy for selecting lattice‐matched 2D templates to achieve high‐quality inorganic CsSnI3 films for efficient and durable lead‐free PSCs.

Template Growth of 2D/3D Heterostructures

According to the template growth principle, minimizing the lattice space mismatch at the heterointerface of 2D and 3D perovskites promotes coherent growth for strain‐free 2D/3D heterostructures with low interfacial energies [22]. Due to their low formation enthalpy, the (00k) planes of low‐n 2D perovskites are preferentially align parallel to the substrate, exposing horizontal inorganic slabs that can induce the quasi‐epitaxial growth of 3D corner‐sharing octahedral framework of CsSnI3 along the vertically oriented (110)2D plane [28]. To identify lattice‐matched 2D phase candidates, first‐principle density functional theory (DFT) calculations were first performed to evaluate the (110) lattice space (d 110) variations of 3D and 2D phases incorporating commonly used organic spacer cations, including butylammonium (BA), octylammonium (OA), PEA, and 4‐fluorophenethylammonium (FPEA). As summarized in Figure 1a and Table S1, 2D phases (n = 2) based on PEA and FPEA cations possess more compatible d 110 of 5.997 Å and 6.020 Å that are closer to that (6.078 Å) of γ‐CsSnI3, resulting in relatively low lattice mismatch degrees (Δd) of −1.33% and −0.95%, respectively. In contrast to the weak Van der Waals force offered by alkyl BA or OA spacers, the aromatic PEA and FPEA cations can provide additional π–π interactions through their coordinative phenylic terminals within the 2D spacing interlayers. This enhanced coordination tends to restrict the ligand orientations of PEA and FPEA cations along the z‐axis, affecting the penetration depth of their amino heads into inorganic slabs [29, 30]. As a result, our simulations reveal that PEA‐ and FPEA‐based 2D phases should possess a stiffer SnI6 octahedral framework with average Sn‐I‐Sn angles (154.13° for PEA and 153.55° for FPEA) comparable to that (154.32°) of γ‐CsSnI3 (Figure 1b; Figure S1).

Using PEA cation as a proof‐of‐concept spacer cation, we further investigate the role of 2D perovskite templates in suppressing δ‐CsSnI3 impurities by evaluating their Gibbs free energy (ΔG) associated with octahedral tilting tendency. As shown in Figure 1c, pristine 3D CsSnI3 exhibits a negative ΔG of −24.97 kJ mol−1, indicating thermodynamically favored octahedral tilting for undesirable γ‐to‐δ phase transition. In contrast, the 2D/3D heterostructure grown with the PEA‐incorporated template presents a positive ΔG of 4.33 kJ mol−1, creating an energy barrier at the heterointerface to inhibit octahedral tilting and thus stabilize γ‐phase CsSnI3. To validate our conceptual design, we employed an antisolvent‐assisted one‐step deposition method to fabricate 2D/3D THP bulk heterostructured films by adding different spacer cations (i.e., BA, OA, PEA, and FPEA) in the 3D CsSnI3 precursor solution. As shown in the x‐ray diffraction (XRD) patterns of these 2D/3D perovskite films with varying spacer cations (Figure S2a), 2D THPs based on PEA and FPEA spacers can promote the oriented growth of 3D phases, yielding dominant diffraction peaks at (110) and (220) planes. By contrast, BA and OA spacers result in much less crystalline 3D phases with more random orientations at (004) and (214) planes. As illustrated in Figure 1d, the lattice matching between the (110)2D plane of PEA2CsSn2I7 (n = 2) and the (110)3D plane of γ‐CsSnI3 can favor the out‐of‐plane growth of inorganic motifs of CsSnI3, enabling the fabrication of high‐quality 2D/3D THP films. Without the assistance of 2D phase, pristine 3D γ‐CsSnI3 polycrystalline film exhibited competing modes of preferred orientations along (002) and (110) planes, alongside pronounced δ‐phase diffractions at 25.4° and 20.7° (Figure 1e). To promote preferential (110) epitaxial growth and eliminate δ‐phase formation, an optimized PEA concentration of 40 mol% was employed, achieving target 2D/3D THP films with a dominant (110) orientation fraction ratio exceeding 90% (Figure S2b–d). Excessive incorporation of PEA (50 mol%) led to the formation of low‐n 2D fragments of PEA2SnI4 (n = 1). Benefiting from regulated crystallization kinetics and induced out‐of‐plane orientation, the optimized 2D/3D THP samples show a compact, dense, and fully covered thin‐film morphology, compared to the dendritic and pinhole‐rich surface of pristine 3D films (Figure 1f; Figure S3).

Dynamic Crystallization Kinetics of Perovskite Films

To understand the dynamic crystallization kinetics of THP films with and without 2D templates, we monitored their color evolution after antisolvent dropping for 0–40 s (Videos S1 and S2) and subsequent annealing at 80 °C for 0–22 s (Video S3). Assisted by 2D templates, 2D/3D wet film turned into reddish‐brown immediately after introducing antisolvent (Figure S4) and developed a dark and mirror‐like surface after annealing (Figure 2a), suggesting the early formation of black‐phase γ‐CsSnI3 nuclei during spin‐coating, followed by regulated crystallization into highly crystalline perovskite films. In contrast, 3D wet film appeared yellowish and semitransparent after antisolvent dropping and underwent a slow phase transition from yellow to orange and eventually to pale black after annealing for 12 s. The sluggish darkening can be attributed to the formation of δ‐phase nuclei and uncontrolled dendritic growth with random crystal orientations. We further investigated the crystallization kinetics using in situ PL spectroscopy and operando XRD by annealing 3D and 2D/3D wet films at 80 °C in a nitrogen atmosphere. As shown in Figure 2b, the 3D wet film exhibited a steady PL intensity at the initial 5 s (stage i), followed by gradual quenching associated with grain boundary formation and defect mitigation (stage ii). In contrast, the 2D/3D wet film displayed a distinct four‐stage “up‐down‐up‐down” PL evolution under the same conditions [31]. In stage I (0–3 s), both 2D and 3D phases start to emerge for increased PL intensities (up), followed by a decay (down) for grain boundary formation and defect mitigation during stage II (3–10 s). Considering the lattice coherence and aligned out‐of‐plane orientation between adjacent 2D/3D domains, the subsequent up‐down trend in PL intensities may be attributed to secondary growth (Ostwald ripening) in stage III (10–40 s) and surface chemistry stabilization in stage IV (40–75 s). Notably, the 2D/3D films exhibited 5‐times higher PL intensities than their 3D counterparts throughout the process, underscoring the effective mitigation of non‐radiative trap states during the templated growth of 2D/3D heterostructures.

To track the crystallization process across a broader annealing window, we performed in situ XRD on 3D and 2D/3D wet films upon annealing from 34 °C to 80 °C (1 °C min−1) and being held at 80 °C for 10 min. As shown in Figure 2c, the 3D wet film exhibits only a slight increase in its main diffraction peaks, accompanied by the early emergence of δ‐phase impurity nuclei. This can be attributed to the intrinsically fast crystallization kinetics of CsSnI3, whereas the incorporation of bulky 2D spacers in the precursor solutions helps slow down the nucleation of 3D crystals. Consequently, the 2D/3D wet film (Figure 2d) displays gradually intensified (110) and (220) reflections at 14.7° and 29.5°, respectively, suggesting that the pre‐nucleated 2D phases serve as crystalline templates for steady and oriented growth of 3D phases upon solvent evaporation. Notably, negligible diffraction signal from δ‐CsSnI3 was detected throughout the entire process, highlighting that the templated growth of 2D/3D heterostructures effectively suppresses δ‐phase impurities that are lattice mismatched with 2D PEA2CsSn2I7 templates.

Phase Distribution and Crystal Orientation

To verify and elucidate the nature of oriented polycrystalline growth, grazing‐incidence wide‐angle x‐ray scattering (GIWAXS) was used to resolve the microstructure of the 3D and 2D/3D THP films with different PEA ratios (Figure 3a,b; Figure S5). Varying the incident angles from 0.04° to 0.40° reflects x‐ray scattering depths from ∼3 nm (top surface) to ∼260 nm (deep bulk), enabling the development of semi‐quantitative depth profiles of perovskite films (Figure S6). The unbroken Debye‐Scherrer rings observed in the 3D sample indicate random crystal orientations throughout the bulk film [32], while the relatively narrow Bragg diffraction arcs at q z = 0.286 Å can be assigned to the (002) plane of 2D phase of PEA2CsSn2I7 (n = 2) in the PEA‐incorporated samples [33]. With low PEA ratios (< 30 mol%), the absence of 2D perovskite diffraction at small grazing angles (corresponding to the top surface ∼3–35 nm) and their gradual appearance at increased penetration depth (∼90–260 nm bulk) suggests the preferential accumulation of 2D segments at the buried interface. When the PEA ratio increases to 30–40 mol%, pronounced 2D perovskite Bragg peaks are observed at all incident angles, suggesting a vertically even distribution of 2D phases within the 3D perovskite matrix. The azimuthal analysis of the (110)3D reflection across different scattering depths further demonstrated enhanced out‐of‐plane orientation of 3D perovskite domains throughout the bulk film (Figure 3c), indicating homogeneous 2D/3D heterostructures were developed to promote the oriented growth of high‐quality perovskite films [34]. Consistent with XRD results, excessive PEA incorporation (50 mol%) induces the formation of low‐n 2D phase of PEA2SnI4, as evidenced by the Bragg features at q z = 0.386 Å.

We then performed complementary 1D scattering analysis of the 2D GIWAXS patterns (Figure S7a,b). By integrating the scattering intensities corresponding to the 2D and 3D THP phases along different scattering depths, a vertically even distribution of 2D phase across the 3D phase matrix was demonstrated. Consistently, the negligible variation in the N 1s signals observed in the depth‐dependent XPS profile of 2D/3D sample further supports the quasi‐bulk heterostructure nature of 2D/3D THP film (Figure S7c). As illustrated in Figure 3d, the uniformly distributed 2D slabs act as crystalline templates that promote preferential out‐of‐plane growth of the 3D domains, thereby minimizing grain‐boundary defects and suppressing the formation of yellow‐phase impurities during the top‐down crystallization process [22, 35]. In stark contrast, the bare 3D film undergoes rapid and disoriented growth in the absence of 2D templates, leading to compromised film quality and thus substantial PV performance losses.

Charge Carrier Dynamics

Besides microstructures and phase impurity, intrinsic and extrinsic defects play crucial roles in determining the charge carrier dynamics of perovskite films. Steady‐state PL spectra and time‐resolved PL (TRPL) decay were employed to investigate the effect of trap‐assisted non‐radiative recombination within 3D and 2D/3D THP films. As shown in Figure 4a and Figure S8, the increased PL intensity from 2D/3D sample indicates a low defect density from its improved microstructure, while the slight blue shift is attributed to a slightly increased bromide ratio within the 2D/3D film. Consistently, the 2D/3D film exhibits a longer average charge‐carrier lifetime of 9.38 ns than that (5.96 ns) of the pristine 3D film (Figure 4b). The electron and hole diffusion lengths of the 3D and 2D/3D films were then quantified by measuring their average charge‐carrier lifetimes with an electron (ICBA) and hole (PEDOT:PSS) quenching layers, respectively, according to a simplified 1D diffusion model based on the following equation [36]: (1) LDL=2π1τQ/τ0−1 where L D is the electron or hole diffusion length, L is perovskite film thickness (∼300 nm), and τQ and τ0 are the TRPL lifetime with or without the quenching layer, respectively. Compared with those of the 3D film (118.2 nm for electron and 116.7 nm for hole), the 2D/3D film shows almost doubled electron and hole diffusion lengths of 235.9 and 224.0 nm, respectively (Figure 4c; Figure S9 and Table S3). Such elongated charge carrier diffusion lengths can be attributed to the reduced trap states and efficient charge transport channels offered by highly oriented 3D grains within the epitaxially grown 2D/3D THP heterostructures.

The easy oxidation of Sn2+ during fabrication leads to Sn4+ species at the surfaces and grain boundaries of THP films, acting as deep‐level defects to impair efficient charge transport within the bulk perovskite film. Herein, depth‐dependent x‐ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) profiles by different Argon etching time intervals were used to quantify the distribution of Sn4+ states at the surface and across the bulk 3D and 2D/3D THP films (Figure S10). Although Argon etching may induce potential chemical changes such as Sn4+ reduction, applying identical measurement conditions enables a fair comparison between 3D and 2D/3D samples, thus distinguishing the impact of 2D/3D heterostructures on suppressing Sn2+ oxidation during crystallization. As summarized in Figure 4d, the 2D/3D sample demonstrated a much lower average Sn4+ ratio of 6.48% across the bulk film than that (10.43%) of the pristine 3D sample. The suppressed oxidation can be attributed to epitaxially grown 3D domains with a preferential out‐of‐plane orientation, which mitigates oxygen permeation through grain boundaries and surface defects. The dark current density (J)‐voltage (V) curves of the full devices further verify the suppressed non‐radiative recombination in the 2D/3D film, as evidenced by its two orders of magnitude lower leakage current densities than those of the 3D sample.

Furthermore, the electron and hole trap densities of 3D and 2D/3D films are estimated by space charge‐limited current (SCLC) measurements by fabricating devices that contain only electron transport layers (ETLs) and hole transport layers (HTLs), respectively (Figure S11). As summarized in Table S4, the 2D/3D samples exhibited lower electron (8.30 × 1014 cm−3) and hole (2.69 × 1015 cm−3) trap densities than those (1.24 × 1015 cm−3 for electron and 3.04 × 1015 for hole) of 3D samples, which is associated with the improved crystallinity and promoted orientation of the conceptually designed 2D/3D heterostructures [37, 38]. The reduced trap densities translated into increased recombination resistance in the 2D/3D devices (Figure S12 and Table S5) and reduced voltage losses in the bulk and at the surface, as evidenced by a higher built‐in potential of 0.74 V from their Mott–Schottky (M–S) analysis (Figure 4f). These characteristic features collectively underscore the reduced trap states (e.g., Sn vacancies, I vacancies, and Sn4+ species) from the improved microstructure of epitaxially grown 2D/3D heterostructures, leading to suppressed non‐radiative recombination and thus minimized PV performance losses.

Photovoltaic Performance and Device Stability

To assess the impact of 2D/3D heterostructure on their PV performance, we fabricated solar cell devices with a p‐i‐n architecture of ITO/PEDOT:PSS/THP/ICBA/BCP/Ag and an optimized absorber thickness of ∼300 nm (Figure 5a; Figure S13). The J–V curves of 3D and 2D/3D devices were measured under a 3A‐class solar simulator, whose one‐sun AM 1.5 illumination (100 mW cm−2) was calibrated by a reference silicon solar cell (Figures S14 and S15a). As shown in Figure 5b, the optimized 2D/3D device based on 40 mol% PEA achieved a champion PCE of 15.27%, with a V oc of 0.90 V, a short‐circuit current density (J sc) of 23.68 mA cm−2, and a fill factor (FF) of 71.64%. In stark contrast, the best 3D device delivered a PCE of only 5.67% with a V oc of 0.630 V and a J sc of 15.40 mA cm−2. As summarized in Table S6, both 3D and 2D/3D devices displayed negligible hysteresis with a hysteresis index of 0.060 and 0.008, respectively. Benefiting from reduced trap states of perovskite films, the 2D/3D device showed substantially enhanced external quantum efficiencies (EQEs) across the visible and near‐IR regions (Figure 5c). The EQE spectrum of 2D/3D device yields an integrated photocurrent density of 23.03 mA cm−2 that is within 3% mismatch to the value (23.68 mA cm−2) extracted from the J–V curve of the champion 2D/3D device, further validating the reliability of the PV performance evaluation (Figure S15b). Device reproducibility was further confirmed by statistical analysis of 40 devices based on 3D and 2D/3D films fabricated across different batches (Tables S7–S13 and Figure S16). As shown in Figure 5d and 2D/3D devices achieved a much higher average PCE of 14.76% ± 0.27% than that (4.75% ± 0.62%) of 3D devices, which is mainly attributed to the suppressed non‐radiative recombination for reduced voltage and photocurrent losses. Notably, the 2D/3D devices delivered an increased average V oc of 0.884 ± 0.012 V (Figure 5e), compared to a modest average V oc of 0.599 ± 0.044 V in the control 3D devices. The highest V oc of 0.90 V achieved in this work corresponds to a V oc deficit (E g/q‐V oc) of 470 mV, which accounts for over 80% of the radiative SQ limit and is comparable with the best‐reported Sn‐based PSCs (Figure S17) [39]. To the best of our knowledge, both the champion V oc and PCE achieved in this work are the highest values reported to date for inorganic CsSnI3‐based solar cells (Figure 5f and Table S14).

Compared to that (5.52%) of the pristine 3D device, the 2D/3D device exhibited a much higher stabilized PCE of 14.95% with negligible decay under steady‐state MPP tracking at 0.72 V for 500 s, indicating the durability of 2D/3D heterostructures against light and thermal stress (Figure 5g). We then compare the long‐term shelf and operational stability of unencapsulated 3D and 2D/3D devices under dark conditions and continuous one‐sun light illumination in nitrogen (Figure 5h,i). Compared to the gradual PCE degradation of 3D device, the target 2D/3D device maintained approximately 96% of its initial PCE after 4800 h under dark in N2 at 35°C ± 5°C, demonstrating excellent oxidation resistance to environmental stimuli. Under MPP tracking at continuous one‐sun illumination (60°C ± 5 °C), the champion 2D/3D device retained over 95% of its original PCE after 1280 h, representing the best reported operational stability of CsSnI3‐based solar cells to date (Table S14). This improved durability of 2D/3D devices is mainly attributed to the reduced trap states at both the surface and within the bulk crystals of the perovskite films, where the quasi‐epitaxially grown 2D/3D heterostructures can suppress ion migration and inhibit oxygen/moisture permeation for prolonged device lifespans under operational conditions. Notably, the aged 2D/3D THP film retains a stable black γ‐phase after long‐term operational stability testing (1280 h under maximum power point tracking) followed by extended dark storage for 4320 h (Figure S18). In contrast, the surface Sn4+ ratio increases markedly to 13.62 mol% after aging, nearly doubling that (7.45 mol%) of the fresh film. These results corroborate that gradient Sn2+ oxidation is the dominant performance degradation pathway of 2D/3D devices, underscoring the pressing need to develop robust encapsulation strategies to accelerate the practical deployment of tin‐based PSCs.

Conclusion

In summary, we have identified a lattice‐matched 2D perovskite template of PEA2CsSn2I7 to achieve oriented growth of 3D γ‐CsSnI3 crystals, leading to regulated crystallization of 2D/3D THP heterostructures with markedly reduced trap states. This approach enables efficient and durable CsSnI3‐based PSCs with a high PCE of 15.27% and stable operation for 1280 h. Importantly, this work demonstrates that rational 2D/3D heterostructure can unlock the intrinsic potential of inorganic CsSnI3 perovskites, which are known for their thermal robustness and environmental stability compared with their hybrid counterparts. This paves a promising pathway for the commercial deployment of tin‐based perovskite devices.

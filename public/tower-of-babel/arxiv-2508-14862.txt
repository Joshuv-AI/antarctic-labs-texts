Universal winding properties of chiral active motion
Ion Santra, Urna Basu, Sanjib Sabhapandit

Abstract

We find that the distributions of the scaled area and the scaled winding angle are described by universal scaling functions across all well-known models of active particles, parametrized by the chirality ω \omega , along with a self-propulsion speed v 0 v_{0} , and the persistence time τ \tau . In particular, we show that, at late times, the average winding angle grows logarithmically with time ⟨ Ω ⟩ ∼ ( ω ​ τ / 2 ) ​ ln ⁡ t \langle\Omega\rangle\sim(\omega\tau/2)\,\ln t , while the average area swept has a linear temporal growth ⟨ A ⁡ ( t ) ⟩ ≃ ( ω ​ τ ​ D eff ) ​ t \langle A(t)\rangle\simeq(\omega\tau D_{\text{eff}})\,t , where D eff = v 0 2 ​ τ / [ 2 ​ ( 1 + ω 2 ​ τ 2 ) ] D_{\text{eff}}=v_{0}^{2}\tau/[2(1+\omega^{2}\tau^{2})] is the effective diffusion coefficient. The most natural way to characterize any winding, circular motion is by its non-zero areal velocity and rate of winding.
Therefore, in this Letter, we use the area swept by a chiral active particle and its winding angle in a time duration

The signed area swept by a planar stochastic process

\displaystyle A[{\bm{r}}(\tau)]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}d\tau\Big[x(\tau)\frac{dy}{d\tau}-y(\tau)\frac{dx}{d\tau}\Big],

(1)

while the winding angle is defined by,

\displaystyle\Omega[{\bm{r}}(\tau)]

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}d\tau\frac{1}{r^{2}(\tau)}\left[x(\tau)\frac{dy}{d\tau}-y(\tau)\frac{dx}{d\tau}\right],

(2)

where

\{{\bm{r}}(\tau);0\leq\tau\leq t\}

In this Letter, we use the observables defined in Eqs. (
1
)-(
2
) to characterize chiral active motion. In particular, we study the average signed area and winding angle, as well as their PDFs for
chiral versions of all the well-established active particle models, namely, CABP, CRTP, CAOUP, and CDRABP. Remarkably, we find that statistical properties of these observables are universal across all these models, and can be described in terms of three parameters, namely, the effective chirality
\omega
, the persistence time
\tau
and the characteristic self-propulsion speed
v_{0}
.

We first summarize our main results. We show that, at late times, the average signed area grows linearly with time,

\displaystyle\langle A(t)\rangle\simeq at,\quad\text{with areal velocity ~}a=\omega\tau\,D_{\text{eff}},

(6)

where
D_{\text{eff}}
denotes the effective diffusion coefficient, given by,

\displaystyle D_{\text{eff}}\equiv\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{\langle r^{2}(t)\rangle}{4t}=\frac{v_{0}^{2}\tau}{2(1+\omega^{2}\tau^{2})}.

(7)

We also find that, for all these models, the PDF of the area at late times
t\gg\tau
is universally described by the scaling form,

\displaystyle P(A,t)=\frac{1}{2D_{\text{eff}}t}F_{\text{ch}}\left(\frac{A-at}{2D_{\text{eff}}\,t}\right).

(8)

Rather surprisingly, this universal scaling function
F_{\text{ch}}(z)
for all the chiral active particle models turns out to be the same as that of Brownian motion in Eq. (
3
), i.e.,
F_{\text{ch}}(z)\equiv F(z)
.

We further find that the average winding angle
\Omega(t)
at late times shows a universal logarithmic growth,

\displaystyle\langle\Omega(t)\rangle\simeq\frac{\omega\tau}{2}\,\ln t,

(9)

and the PDF has a universal scaling form,

\displaystyle P(\Omega,t)=\frac{1}{\ln t}G_{\mathrm{ch}}\left(\frac{\Omega}{\ln t}\right).

(10)

Based on extensive numerical evidence, we conjecture that the scaling function is given by,

\displaystyle G_{\mathrm{ch}}(z)=\frac{1}{\pi}\sin\left(\frac{\pi\beta}{\alpha+\beta}\right)\frac{(\alpha+\beta)}{(e^{\alpha z}+e^{-\beta z})}~,

(11)

where the positive constants
\alpha
and
\beta
depend on the specific model. Surprisingly, for achiral active motion, i.e.,
\omega=0
, the scaling function
G_{\mathrm{ch}}(z)
is given by that of ‘cured’ Brownian motion (without diverging windings around the origin) in Eq. (
5
), i.e.,
\alpha=\beta=\pi
. We argue that this is because the pathology of infinite windings around the origin is naturally absent for active motion.

We first illustrate our main results using the example of CABP. The other models are presented in the End Matter. Let us consider a CABP moving on a plane. Its position
{\bm{r}}(t)=(x(t),y(t))
evolves following the overdamped Langevin equation,

\displaystyle\dot{\bm{r}}(t)=v_{0}\hat{\bm{n}}(t),

(12)

where
\hat{\bm{n}}(t)\equiv(n_{x}(t),n_{y}(t))=(\cos\theta(t),\sin\theta(t))
denotes the internal orientation of the active particle. The angle
\theta(t)
undergoes a driven rotational Brownian motion,

\displaystyle\dot{\theta}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\omega+\sqrt{2\tau^{-1}}\;\eta(t),

(13)

where
\eta(t)
is a Gaussian white noise with zero mean and the correlator
\langle\eta(t)\eta(t^{\prime})\rangle=\delta(t-t^{\prime})
, and
\tau^{-1}
is the rotational diffusion constant. The presence of a non-zero
\omega
is responsible for the chiral nature of the motion, and leads to an oscillatory decay of the autocorrelation of the orientation vector
\hat{\bm{n}}(t)
,

\displaystyle\langle n_{\mu}(t_{1})n_{\mu}(t_{2})\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}e^{-\frac{|t_{1}-t_{2}|}{\tau}}\cos[\omega(t_{1}-t_{2})]~\text{for }\mu=x,y,

(14)

\displaystyle\langle n_{x}(t_{1})n_{y}(t_{2})\rangle

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}e^{-\frac{|t_{1}-t_{2}|}{\tau}}\sin[\omega(t_{1}-t_{2})].

(15)

The antisymmetric non-zero cross-correlation in Eq. (
15
) for
\omega>0
is responsible for the non-trivial winding properties of chiral active motion reported in this Letter.

\displaystyle\langle A(t)\rangle

\displaystyle b=\frac{2v_{0}^{2}\tau^{3}\omega}{\left(1+\omega^{2}\tau^{2}\right)^{2}}\,,\quad\text{and}\quad c=\frac{v_{0}^{2}\tau^{2}\left(1-\omega^{2}\tau^{2}\right)}{\left(1+\omega^{2}\tau^{2}\right)^{2}}\,.

In conclusion, we propose the area swept
A(t)
and the winding angle
\Omega(t)
as the key observables to characterize chiral active motion. We discover a striking universality across diverse active particle models. Despite the differences in microscopic dynamics, all models exhibit the same constant mean areal velocity and identical logarithmic growth of the average winding angle at long times. The PDFs of both observables also follow universal temporal scalings and are described by distinct universal scaling functions.

Although an active particle typically exhibits diffusive behavior at times much larger than its persistence time
\tau
, the non-zero
\tau
still plays an important role in determining its winding properties. This is because the persistent nature of the active motion does not allow the particle to stay infinitesimally close to the origin for a long time, preventing the diverging windings around the origin in a finite time. In other words, active motion naturally eliminates the pathology of infinite windings around the origin found for a standard Brownian motion. Consequently, the winding angle distribution for an achiral active motion exhibits exponential tails [see Eq. (
5
)], in contrast to the Cauchy distribution for Brownian motion.
While the pathological infinitely winding trajectories are still absent for the chiral active particles (
\omega\neq 0
), the chirality makes the distribution asymmetric.

Our universal results open up a new way of characterizing chiral active systems, both theoretically and experimentally.
Since both the enclosed area and the winding angle can be extracted from single-particle trajectories, our predictions offer clear and testable experimental signatures
Hernández et al. 2020
. Additionally, since both area and winding angle are time-integrated observables, our results enable an accurate estimation of physical parameters
(v_{0},\omega,\tau)
from experimental data.

A.1
Chiral Run-and-Tumble Particles (CRTP)

The position of a Run-and-tumble particle (RTP) moving with a self-propulsion speed
v_{0}
in two dimensions evolves according to the overdamped Langevin equation,

\displaystyle\dot{\bm{r}}(t)=v_{0}\hat{\bm{n}}(t),

(21)

where
\hat{\bm{n}}=(\cos\theta,\sin\theta)
changes intermittently via

\theta\to\theta^{\prime}
at a certain rate. Chirality is introduced through a biased dynamics of the orientation angle
\theta
. We consider two variants of chiral RTP below.

Continuous CRTP (cCRTP):
The orientation
\theta\in[0,2\pi]
evolves continuously via a deterministic rotation along with the stochastic tumblings. The tumbling events happen at a rate
\gamma
, which implies that in an infinitesimal time interval
\Delta t
, the evolution of the orientation follows,

\displaystyle\theta(t+\Delta t)=\begin{cases}\theta(t)+\omega\,\Delta t&\text{with probability }(1-\gamma\Delta t),\\
\theta^{\prime}&\text{with probability }\gamma\Delta t,\end{cases}

(22)

where
\theta^{\prime}
is chosen uniformly from
[0,2\pi]
.

Discrete CRTP (dCRTP):
The orientation
\theta
takes values from a finite set, and evolves via a Markov jump process. Chirality is introduced by using asymmetric switching rates between the states. In particular, we consider a three-state model with
\theta\in\left\{\pi/2,7\pi/6,11\pi/6\right\}
, and clockwise/anticlockwise transition rates
\gamma
and
\lambda\gamma
respectively, with
\lambda>1
[see Fig.
6
].

Though the chirality
\omega
does not appear explicitly in the dynamical description, it can be identified from the correlations among the components of the orientation vector, which are given by,

\displaystyle\langle n_{x}(t)

\displaystyle n_{x}(0)\rangle=\langle n_{y}(t)n_{y}(0)\rangle

(23)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\exp\left[-\frac{3\gamma(1+\lambda)t}{2}\right]\cos\left(\frac{\sqrt{3}\gamma(\lambda-1)t}{2}\right),

(24)

\displaystyle\langle n_{x}(t)

\displaystyle n_{y}(0)\rangle=\langle n_{y}(t)n_{x}(0)\rangle

(25)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\exp\left[-\frac{3\gamma(1+\lambda)t}{2}\right]\sin\left(\frac{\sqrt{3}\gamma(\lambda-1)t}{2}\right).

(26)

These are in the form of Eqs. (
14
)-(
15
), and we can identify,

\displaystyle\omega=\frac{\sqrt{3}\gamma(\lambda-1)}{2},\quad\tau=\frac{2}{3\gamma(1+\lambda)}.

(27)

In other words, a dCRTP with chirality
\omega
and persistence time
\tau
can be generated using the rates

\displaystyle\lambda=\frac{1+\sqrt{3}\,\omega\tau}{1-\sqrt{3}\,\omega\tau},\quad\gamma=\frac{1-\sqrt{3}\,\omega\tau}{3\tau}.

(28)

Since
\lambda
and
\gamma
must be positive, there is a restriction on
\omega
and
\tau
for the dCRTP, namely,
\omega\tau<1/\sqrt{3}
.

A.2
Chiral Direction Reversing Active Brownian Particle (CDRABP)

The direction-reversing active Brownian particle (DRABP) models a class of active dynamics where, in addition to the rotational diffusion of ABP, the particle undergoes complete directional reversals intermittently.
The chiral version of DRABP is modeled theoretically by the Langevin equation,

\displaystyle\dot{\bm{r}}(t)

\displaystyle=v_{0}\,\hat{\bm{n}}(t)=v_{0}\sigma(t)\,\hat{\bm{u}}(t),

(29)

where
\sigma(t)
is a dichotomous noise switching between
\pm 1
at rate
\gamma
. The unit vector
\hat{\bm{u}}(t)=(\cos\theta(t),\sin\theta(t))
evolves continuously via

\displaystyle\dot{\theta}(t)

\displaystyle=\omega+\sqrt{2D_{R}}\;\eta(t).

(30)

It can be easily checked that auto-correlation of the orientation vector
\hat{\bm{n}}(t)
follows the same form as Eqs. (
14
) and (
15
),
with
\tau=1/(D_{R}+2\gamma)
.

A.3
Chiral Active Ornstein-Uhlenbeck Particle (CAOUP)

In contrast to the other models, the active Ornstein-Uhlenbeck particle (AOUP) features a fluctuating self-propulsion velocity
{\bm{v}}=(v_{x},v_{y})
governed by independent Ornstein-Uhlenbeck processes. Chirality is introduced by adding a rotational coupling to the velocity dynamics
Caprini and Marini
Bettolo Marconi 2019
. Thus, the position vector
\bm{r}=(x,y)
evolves via,

\displaystyle\dot{x}

\displaystyle=v_{x},\qquad\dot{y}=v_{y},

\displaystyle\tau\dot{v}_{x}

\displaystyle=-v_{x}-\omega\tau v_{y}+\xi_{x}(t),

\displaystyle\tau\dot{v}_{y}

\displaystyle=-v_{y}+\omega\tau v_{x}+\xi_{y}(t),

(31)

where
\xi_{x}(t)
and
\xi_{y}(t)
are independent white noises with
\langle\xi_{i}(t)\xi_{j}(t^{\prime})\rangle=\tau v_{0}^{2}\delta(t-t^{\prime})\delta_{ij}
.
It is again straightforward to see that the auto-correlations of the orientation vector
\hat{\bm{n}}=\bm{v}/v_{0}
are given by Eqs. (
14
) and (
15

Appendix B
Area variance

In this section, we give a brief outline of the steps to compute the variance of the area swept by a CABP. From Eq. (
1
), we have,

\displaystyle A^{2}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\int_{0}^{t}d\tau_{1}\int_{0}^{t}d\tau_{2}\,\big[x(\tau_{1})\dot{y}(\tau_{1})-y(\tau_{1})\dot{x}(\tau_{1})\big]

(32)

\displaystyle\times\big[x(\tau_{2})\dot{y}(\tau_{2})-y(\tau_{2})\dot{x}(\tau_{2})\big].

(33)

Using the equations of motion
\dot{x}(t)=v_{0}\cos\theta(t)
and
\dot{y}(t)=v_{0}\sin\theta(t)
, the (
33

\displaystyle A^{2}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\int_{0}^{t}d\tau_{1}\int_{0}^{t}d\tau_{2}\int_{0}^{t}d\tau_{3}\int_{0}^{t}d\tau_{4}

(34)

\displaystyle\times\sin(\theta(\tau_{1}-\tau_{3}))\sin(\theta(\tau_{2}-\tau_{4})).

(35)

Using
\sin\theta=(e^{i\theta}-e^{-i\theta})/(2i)
, we have,

\displaystyle\langle A^{2}(t)\rangle

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{16}\sum_{\ell=\pm 1}\sum_{m=\pm 1}\ell m\,\int_{0}^{t}d\tau_{1}\int_{0}^{t}d\tau_{2}\int_{0}^{t}d\tau_{3}\int_{0}^{t}d\tau_{4}

(36)

\displaystyle\langle\exp\left[i\ell\left(\theta(\tau_{1})-\theta(\tau_{3})\right)+im\left(\theta(\tau_{2})-\theta(\tau_{4})\right)\right]\rangle.

(37)

Using the identity for Gaussian random variables
\{\phi_{j}\}
,
\langle\exp[i\sum_{j}a_{j}\phi_{j}]\rangle=\exp[-\sum_{j,k}a_{j}a_{k}\langle\phi_{j}\phi_{k}\rangle]
, and performing the integrals over
\tau
we get the second moment of the swept area exactly. The full expression is rather lengthy and not very illuminating. The leading order behavior subtracting the mean and taking the long time limit leads to Eq. (

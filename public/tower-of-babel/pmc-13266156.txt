Revisiting and Aligning Oral Health Prevention Strategies in Iran With Global Recommendations: Policy Brief
Authors: Hossein Hessari, Fatemeh Darabi, Masoud Shabani
Published: 2026

Introduction

Over the past 3 decades, Iran has implemented a series of national and school-based initiatives aimed at controlling dental caries, leading to substantial improvements in oral health indicators, particularly among 12-year-old children. Evidence from national studies demonstrates that preventive policies and structured school-based programmes have contributed to measurable reductions in caries experience at the population level.1,2 In parallel, provincial capacities for designing and implementing priority-oriented programmes such as caries-free school initiatives and innovative delivery models have created a favourable platform for expanding preventive oral health strategies across regions.2

More recently, the focus of Iran’s oral health system has extended beyond caries control to address emerging priorities, including molar–incisor hypomineralization and the responsiveness of the health system to such conditions. This shift reflects the maturation of Iran’s oral health governance and its growing readiness to address complex oral health challenges.3,4 Despite these achievements, a systematic reassessment of the alignment of oral health prevention strategies beyond dental caries—including periodontal diseases, tobacco use, oral health in older adults, dental trauma, oral cancer, tooth wear, and fluorosis—appears necessary to ensure consistency with global recommendations and evolving population needs.5, 6, 7, 8, 9

The alignment levels presented in the Table reflect a synthetic assessment of the 4 standardised criteria rather than the mere existence of individual programmes. Domains such as tobacco control demonstrate higher alignment due to established policies and relatively sustainable implementation, whereas oral health of older adults, dental trauma, and tooth wear show the lowest alignment, largely due to the absence of institutionalised and population-based programmes.5,10, 11, 12

Notably, Iran’s successful experience in school-based caries control may provide transferable lessons for other low- and middle-income countries, particularly regarding structured delivery platforms, phased implementation, and the potential for integrating oral health prevention into broader primary health care and Non-Communicable Disease control strategies.

Conclusion

Iran has accumulated valuable experience and demonstrable success in dental caries control, particularly through maternal and child oral health programmes. However, achieving full alignment with global recommendations requires a strategic transition from predominantly child- and caries-focused interventions toward a comprehensive life-course approach, alongside stronger institutionalisation of preventive strategies across other priority domains of oral health.

Application of the 3-Loop FlexibleEFTHiggs Method to the MSSM and the NMSSM
Thomas Kwasnitza, Dominik Stöckinger, Alexander Voigt, Johannes Wünsche

Abstract
We perform an extensive analysis of the light CP-even Higgs boson pole mass in the MSSM and its dependencies on various parameters based on the 3-loop FlexibleEFTHiggs hybrid calculation which is implemented and publicly avaiable since recently in FlexibleSUSY . Our focus lies on the study of the robustness of the approach in scenarios of highly non-degenerate SUSY mass spectra. Also, we present an improved Higgs mass calculation in the NMSSM based on the same approach, which is published in the new version 2.9.0 of FlexibleSUSY as well. The calculation provides a treatment in the full-model parametrization, leading to an advantageous resummation of QCD -enhanced terms in the stop-mixing parameter and includes important 2-loop contributions as well as 3-loop QCD contributions in the MSSM limit. We assess the reliability of this new calculation by applying it to several distinct NMSSM scenarios. In this context, special attention is devoted to the estimation of NMSSM -specific theory uncertainty.



1
Introduction

The discovery of a Higgs boson
h
by the
ATLAS
and

CMS
experiments
Aad:2012tfa
;
Chatrchyan:2012xdj

represents a milestone in our understanding of the physics of
elementary particles, because it confirms the existence of
the Higgs field generating the masses
of elementary particles within the Standard Model of particle physics
(
SM
). However, the
SM
cannot explain the mass
M_{h}
of the Higgs
boson itself, because it is related to the quartic Higgs coupling

\hat{\lambda}
, which is a free parameter of the
SM
. For this reason and
because the
SM
cannot provide an explanation of phenomena such as
dark matter or neutrino masses, models beyond the Standard Model
(
BSM
) have been proposed. Among them is the class of supersymmetric
models which are able to predict the order of magnitude of the Higgs
boson mass
M_{h}
. However, these models also incorporate numerous
new particles, which have not been directly observed
yet. For this reason, if these new
BSM
particles exist, they either have masses

M_{S}
of the order of the electroweak scale or below,

M_{S}\lesssim M_{Z}
, but are difficult to produce, or their masses
are beyond the currently accessible energy scale of high-energy
physics experiments (beyond the electroweak scale),

M_{S}\gg M_{Z}
. For both scenarios, there exist well-developed methods to
predict
M_{h}
with reasonable precision (see e.g. ref.
Slavich:2020zjv
for a review of available methods):

•

In scenarios where
M_{S}\sim M_{Z}
, so-called fixed-order
(
FO
) calculations, where quantum corrections up to a fixed loop
order are taken into account, lead to a reasonable
precision. However, the prediction of
M_{h}
with this method is
imprecise if the loop contributions not taken into account are
large, which is the case when
M_{S}\gg M_{Z}
.

•

In scenarios where
M_{S}\gg M_{Z}
, so-called effective field
theory (
EFT
) calculations, where large logarithmic higher-order
contributions of
\mathcal{O}\!\left(\ln^{n}(M_{Z}^{2}/M_{S}^{2})\right)
are taken into
account at all orders
n
, leading to a reasonable precision.
However, the prediction of
M_{h}
within an effective field theory
becomes imprecise if
M_{S}\lesssim M_{Z}
, because the method neglects
contributions of
\mathcal{O}\!\left(v^{2}/M_{S}^{2}\right)
, where
v\approx 246\,\text{GeV}

is the vacuum expectation value of the
SM
Higgs doublet.

Since the mass scale
M_{S}
of the new
BSM
particles is unknown, it
is a-priori not clear which of the two methods (
FO
or
EFT
) to use
for a specific
BSM
parameter point to obtain a precise prediction of

M_{h}
. Thus, a “hybrid” method combining the virtues of both the
FO
and
EFT
calculative approaches is very advantageous. There are
currently two different hybrid
FO
/
EFT
methods realized in several
implementations:

•

The
FeynHiggs
hybrid method

Hahn:2013ria
;
Bahl:2016brp
;
Staub:2017jnp
;
Bahl:2018jom
;
Bahl:2020tuq
;
Bahl:2020mjy
: The
first implementation of a hybrid calculation of the
MSSM
Higgs mass was part
of the
FeynHiggs
version published in 2013. It combines the radiative corrections to
the Higgs mass matrix obtained from the
FO
calculation with the numerically determined
corrections from the
EFT
approach. Double-counting is circumvented by
additionally subtracting contributions which are covered by both approaches.

•

The
FlexibleEFTHiggs
method
Athron:2016fuq
;
Athron:2017fvs
;
Kwasnitza:2020wli
:
The hybrid method of
FlexibleEFTHiggs
, being proposed in 2016, follows another
approach. Its essence consists in the usage of the Higgs pole mass matching in order
to determine the quartic Higgs coupling
\hat{\lambda}
in the
SM
at

.
It naturally allows the inclusion of terms suppressed by powers of

.
Then,

The

1.7.0

. It was applied to the (real,

6
SSM. Due to the unwanted inclusion of large logarithmic 2-loop

+

. The 1-loop

+

. In ref.

-parametrization, leading to a very beneficial effective

-enhanced terms leading in the stop mixing parameter.

N
3

+
N

coupling

In the present paper, the application of
FlexibleEFTHiggs
to the
MSSM
at 3-loop

with degenerate
SUSY
mass spectra to rather “non-standard” ones with
non-degenerate
SUSY

method to the

NMSSM

. Several scenarios and parameter dependences

3.2
Uncertainty estimation

In order to assess the reliability of the Higgs pole mass prediction,
we estimate its uncertainty in a similar manner as in
ref.
Kwasnitza:2020wli
. Accordingly, we consider two
contributions to the total uncertainty: a “high-scale uncertainty”
to estimate missing contributions in the matching of the
MSSM
to the

SM
, and a “low-scale uncertainty” to estimate missing contributions
in the calculation of the Higgs pole mass in the
SM
at the scale

Q_{\text{pole}}
and in the calculation of the low-scale quantities

O_{\text{SM}}^{(5)}
. We do not include an uncertainy estimate due to
missing contributions of

(so-called “
EFT

FlexibleEFTHiggs
calculation.

Bahl:2019wzx
;
Borschensky:2024utz

and well-proven to be a reliable approach to estimate the
missing
MSSM
-like higher order terms, at least for moderately large
values of the stop mixing parameter (
|x_{t}|\leq 3
), see
ref.
Kwasnitza:2020wli
. Here, we desist from additionally
estimating the high-scale uncertainty by regarding implicit
corrections at higher order and reparametrization terms, as this was
necessary in ref.
Kwasnitza:2020wli
to cover missing terms of
highest order in
x_{t}
. For
|x_{t}|\leq 3
, the high-scale
uncertainty estimation via matching scale variation turned out to be
sufficient. Since only moderate values of
x_{t}
are considered here
and, furthermore, the study of the
x_{t}
-dependence is not the focus
of this work, the procedure can accordingly be simplified.

3.2.2
Low-scale uncertainty

In accordance with ref.
Kwasnitza:2020wli
, we employ two
methods to estimate the low-scale uncertainty: The variation of the
renormalization scale
Q_{\text{pole}}
for the calculation of the Higgs pole
mass
M_{h}
in the
SM
and the variation of the loop order at which
the top quark pole mass
M_{t}
is calculated in the
SM
from the

\overline{\text{MS}}
parameters
\hat{P}_{\text{SM}}(Q_{\text{low}})
.

Pole mass scale variation.

In analogy to the matching
scale variation, we vary the pole mass scale
Q_{\text{pole}}
, at which the
Higgs pole mass
M_{h}
is calculated in the
SM
, between
M_{t}/2
and

2M_{t}
. We take the resulting variation of the Higgs pole mass as
uncertainty estimate,

\Delta M_{h}^{Q_{\text{pole}}}=\max_{Q\in[M_{t}/2,2M_{t}]}\left\{\left|M_{h}(Q_{\text{pole}}=M_{t})-M_{h}(Q_{\text{pole}}=Q)\right|\right\}.

(13)

The so-defined uncertainty
\Delta M_{h}^{Q_{\text{pole}}}
is an estimate of
logarithmic renormalization scale dependent
SM
contributions, not
included in our calculation of
M_{h}
.

Variation of top quark pole mass loop order.

Another
source of low-scale uncertainty is the truncation of the perturbation
series for the calculation of the low-scale quantities
O_{\text{SM}}^{(5)}
.
These low-scale quantities implicitly fix the
SM
parameters

\hat{P}_{\text{SM}}(Q_{\text{low}})\setminus\{\hat{\lambda}(Q_{\text{low}})\}
. The largest
uncertainty can be attributed to the fixation of the
SM

\overline{\text{MS}}
top
quark Yukawa coupling
\hat{y}_{t}
from the top quark pole mass

M_{t}
. For the desired Higgs pole mass prediction at
N
3
LO
with

N
3
LL
resummation, 3-loop
QCD
corrections must be included in the
relation between
M_{t}
and
\hat{y}_{t}
. We denote the Higgs mass,
where up to 3-loop
QCD
contributions to
M_{t}
are included as

M_{h}^{y_{t},3\ell}
. We then estimate the missing higher order
contributions by including the available 4-loop
QCD
contribution
from ref.
Martin:2016xsp
in the calculation of
M_{t}
in the

SM
at the scale
Q_{\text{low}}
. The resulting Higgs pole mass is denoted as

M_{h}^{y_{t},4\ell}
. We then take the variation of the predicted Higgs
pole masses as uncertainty estimate

\Delta M_{h}^{y_{t}}=\left|M_{h}^{y_{t},3\ell}-M_{h}^{y_{t},4\ell}\right|,

(14)

which is in accordance with ref.
Kwasnitza:2020wli
.

3.2.3
Combined uncertainty

In order to obtain an estimate of the high-scale uncertainty
\Delta M_{h}^{\text{HS}}
, just one
method was employed, namely the matching scale variation. For the
low-scale uncertainty, two different approaches are applied. Since
there is a significant overlap between the estimated missing
higher-order terms in both methods, we proceed as in
ref.
Kwasnitza:2020wli
and take the maximum of both estimates
as combined low-scale uncertainty
\Delta M_{h}^{\text{LS}}
.
We eventually take the linear addition of the obtained high-scale and
low-scale uncertainties as a conservative estimation for the total
uncertainty
\Delta M_{h}
:

\Delta M_{h}=\Delta M_{h}^{\text{LS}}+\Delta M_{h}^{\text{HS}}=\max\left\{\Delta M_{h}^{Q_{\text{pole}}},\Delta M_{h}^{y_{t}}\right\}+\Delta M_{h}^{Q_{\text{m}}}.

(15)

This approach is again in accordance with
ref.
Kwasnitza:2020wli
.

these two conditions. Accordingly, other mecanisms are
present that compensate the negative effect on the
size of the Higgs mass. One aspect contributing positively are
clearly large values of the squark mass parameters, which increase
the stop masses and thus induce an enhancement of
M_{h}
.
This effect is very obvious in (M2), where
|x_{t}|
differs strongly
from
\sqrt{6}
, but very large
(m_{\tilde{q}})_{33}
and

(m_{\tilde{u}})_{33}
lead to a compensation of that negative
effect. Also, a very small value of either of the two squark
mass parameters can be compensated by a large value of the other
one, respectively. This interplay occurs e.g. in (M3) and (M4).

Application to the
NMSSM

4.1
Model setup

The
NMSSM
extends the field content of the
MSSM
by a gauge singlet
chiral superfield
\hat{S}
. In the following we consider the

Z_{3}
-invariant
NMSSM
with the superpotential

W=Y_{d}\left(\hat{H}_{d}\cdot\hat{Q}\right)\hat{D}-Y_{u}\left(\hat{H}_{u}\cdot\hat{Q}\right)\hat{U}+Y_{e}\left(\hat{H}_{d}\cdot\hat{L}\right)\hat{E}-\lambda\hat{S}\left(\hat{H}_{d}\cdot\hat{H}_{u}\right)+\frac{\kappa}{3}\hat{S}^{3},

(19)

where
\hat{A}\cdot\hat{B}:=\epsilon_{DE}\hat{A}^{D}\hat{B}^{E}
is the

SU(2)
-invariant product of the two superfields
\hat{A}
and

\hat{B}
. Generation and colour indices are omitted here again for
the sake of clarity. The
NMSSM
-specific couplings
\lambda
and

\kappa
are assumed to be real in order to avoid CP-violation in the
Higgs sector. The superpotential term governed by the coupling

\lambda
replaces the analogous
\mu
-term in the
MSSM
. In this way,
the
NMSSM
provides a solution to the so-called
\mu
-problem of the

MSSM

Polonsky:1999qd
;
Bae:2019dgg
by dynamically generating an
effective
\mu
-parameter if the scalar component
s
of the
superfield
\hat{S}
acquires a vacuum expectation value:

\mu_{\text{eff}}=\frac{\lambda v_{s}}{\sqrt{2}}\qquad\text{with}\qquad\frac{v_{s}}{\sqrt{2}}=\langle s\rangle.

(20)

The soft-breaking Lagrangian of the
NMSSM
is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\text{soft}}=

\displaystyle-\tilde{q}^{\dagger}m^{2}_{\tilde{q}}\tilde{q}-\tilde{u}_{R}^{\dagger}m^{2}_{\tilde{u}}\tilde{u}_{R}-\tilde{d}_{R}^{\dagger}m^{2}_{\tilde{d}}\tilde{d}_{R}-\tilde{l}^{\dagger}m^{2}_{\tilde{l}}\tilde{l}-\tilde{e}_{R}^{\dagger}m^{2}_{\tilde{e}}\tilde{e}_{R}

\displaystyle-m^{2}_{h_{d}}h_{d}^{\dagger}h_{d}-m^{2}_{h_{u}}h_{u}^{\dagger}h_{u}-m^{2}_{s}|s|^{2}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\left(M_{1}\tilde{B}\tilde{B}+M_{2}\tilde{W}_{a}\tilde{W}_{a}+M_{3}\tilde{g}\tilde{g}+\text{h.c.}\right)

\displaystyle-\Big[(h_{d}\cdot\tilde{q}_{L})T_{d}\tilde{d}_{R}^{*}-(h_{u}\cdot\tilde{q}_{L})T_{u}\tilde{u}_{R}^{*}+(h_{u}\cdot\tilde{l}_{L})T_{e}\tilde{e}_{R}^{*}

\displaystyle\qquad-\lambda A_{\lambda}s\left(h_{d}\cdot h_{u}\right)+\frac{\kappa}{3}A_{\kappa}s^{3}+\text{h.c.}\Big],

(21)

where
(T_{f})_{ij}=(A_{f})_{ij}(Y_{f})_{ij}
. For the couplings
\lambda

and
\kappa
, a perturbativity limit can be formulated at the
SUSY
scale by demanding the absence of a Landau pole below the GUT
scale. Using the corresponding
\beta
-functions from
ref.
Ellwanger:2009dp
,

\displaystyle\beta_{\lambda}=\frac{\text{d}\lambda}{\text{d}(\ln Q)}

\displaystyle=\kappa_{L}\left(4\lambda^{3}+2\lambda\kappa^{2}+3\lambda y_{t}^{2}-\frac{3}{5}\lambda g_{1}^{2}-3\lambda g_{2}^{2}\right)+\mathcal{O}\!\left(\kappa_{L}^{2}\right),

(22)

\displaystyle\beta_{\kappa}=\frac{\text{d}\kappa}{\text{d}(\ln Q)}

\displaystyle=\kappa_{L}\left(6\lambda^{2}\kappa+6\kappa^{3}\right)+\mathcal{O}\!\left(\kappa_{L}^{2}\right),

(23)

where
\kappa_{L}=1/(4\pi)^{2}
, that limit can be found to be approximately

Masip:1998jc
:

\sqrt{\lambda^{2}+\kappa^{2}}<0.7.

(24)

The Higgs potential resulting from the
NMSSM
Lagrangian is given by

\displaystyle V_{H}=\

\displaystyle\frac{g_{1}^{2}+g_{2}^{2}}{8}\left(h_{d}^{\dagger}h_{d}-h_{u}^{\dagger}h_{u}\right)^{2}+\frac{g_{2}^{2}}{2}\left|h_{d}^{\dagger}h_{u}\right|^{2}

\displaystyle+\lambda^{2}|s|^{2}\left(h_{u}^{\dagger}h_{u}+h_{d}^{\dagger}h_{d}\right)+\left|\lambda(h_{u}\cdot h_{d})+\kappa s^{2}\right|^{2}

\displaystyle+m_{h_{d}}^{2}h_{d}^{\dagger}h_{d}+m_{h_{u}}^{2}h_{u}^{\dagger}h_{u}+m_{s}^{2}|s|^{2}+\left[\lambda A_{\lambda}s(h_{u}\cdot h_{d})+\frac{\kappa}{3}A_{\kappa}s^{3}+\text{h.c.}\right].

(25)

The potential is required to be configured such that all Higgs fields
acquire a vacuum expectation value (VEV) by electroweak symmetry
breaking. The VEVs of the Higgs doublets
h_{u}
and
h_{d}
are denoted
as
v_{u}
and
v_{d}
, respectively, and are defined as in the
MSSM
,
c.f. eq. (
6
).
In this work, we restrict ourselfs to the CP-conserving
NMSSM
. In
order to circumvent CP violation in the Higgs sector, all VEVs are
assumed to be real. Furthermore, all parameters appearing in the Higgs
sector are accordingly chosen to be real and the CKM matrix is set to
unity. As CP is conserved, CP-even and CP-odd neutral Higgs fields do
not mix. There is however a mixing within the CP-even and the CP-odd
Higgs sectors, respectively:

\displaystyle\Re(h_{u}^{0}),\Re(h_{d}^{0}),\Re(s)\

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\ h_{1},h_{2},h_{3},

\displaystyle\Im(h_{u}^{0}),\Im(h_{d}^{0}),\Im(s)\

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\ A_{1},A_{2},G^{0}

with the CP-even mass eigenstates

,
h_{2}
and
h_{3}
, the CP-odd

(27)

in the limit of

and

(where the right side of the second inequality corresponds to the

(see also ref.

\displaystyle P_{\text{NMSSM}}(Q_{\text{in}})=\

\displaystyle\cup\left\{\lambda(Q_{\text{in}}),\kappa(Q_{\text{in}}),v_{s}(Q_{\text{in}}),A_{\lambda}(Q_{\text{in}}),A_{\kappa}(Q_{\text{in}}),m_{s}^{2}(Q_{\text{in}})\right\}.

(28)

Furthermore, an
MSSM

NMSSM
can be defined by fixing
the
\mu

parameters of the
MSSM

NMSSM

\displaystyle\mu_{\text{eff}}

\displaystyle:=\frac{\lambda v_{s}}{\sqrt{2}},

The uncertainty estimation for the predicted light CP-even Higgs pole

-specific higher-order terms that depend on
\lambda

and

The analysis of the reliability of the matching scale variation below
is performed according to the following strategy. First, the

-specific terms contributing to

\displaystyle\phantom{=}{}\qquad+\frac{\partial\Delta\lambda^{1\ell}}{\partial(\ln Q_{\text{m}})}+\underbrace{\frac{\partial\Delta\lambda^{2\ell}}{\partial(\ln Q_{\text{m}})}}_{\text{incomplete}}-\beta_{\hat{\lambda}}(Q_{\text{m}})+\mathcal{O}\!\left(\kappa_{L}^{3}\right)\Bigg]\Bigg|_{\mathcal{S}}

\displaystyle\phantom{=}{}+\frac{\partial}{\partial\hat{v}}\left(\hat{\lambda}\hat{v}^{2}+\mathcal{O}\!\left(\kappa_{L}\right)\right)\Bigg[\sum_{p}\left(\frac{\partial\hat{v}^{0\ell}}{\partial p}\beta_{p}(Q_{\text{m}})+\frac{\partial\Delta v^{1\ell}}{\partial p}\beta_{p}(Q_{\text{m}})\right)

\displaystyle\phantom{=}{}\qquad+\frac{\partial\Delta v^{1\ell}}{\partial(\ln Q_{\text{m}})}+\underbrace{\frac{\partial\Delta v^{2\ell}}{\partial(\ln Q_{\text{m}})}}_{\text{incomplete}}-\beta_{\hat{v}}(Q_{\text{m}})+\mathcal{O}\!\left(\kappa_{L}^{3}\right)\Bigg]\Bigg|_{\mathcal{S}}

(39)

\displaystyle=-\left.\left(v^{2}\frac{\partial\Delta\lambda^{2\ell}}{\partial(\ln Q_{\text{m}})}\Bigg|^{\text{miss}}+2\hat{\lambda}^{0\ell}v\frac{\partial\Delta v^{2\ell}}{\partial(\ln Q_{\text{m}})}\Bigg|^{\text{miss}}\right)\right|_{\mathcal{S}}.

(40)

The terms above containing partial derivatives of
\Delta\lambda^{2\ell}

and
\Delta v^{2\ell}
with respect to
\ln Q_{\text{m}}
are incomplete in the
performed
FlexibleEFTHiggs
calculation as their NMSSM-specific parts are
omitted. However, their full inclusion would be necessary in order to
ensure the complete cancellation of
Q_{\text{m}}
-dependent terms at

\mathcal{O}\!\left(\mathcal{S}\right)
. The change in the Higgs mass under matching scale
variation at that order therefore corresponds to the absent terms
(denoted by “miss”) in the way indicated in eq. (
40
).
Also, one can read off from eq. (
39
) which present
terms in the calculation of
M_{h}^{2}
are the origin for simulated
terms of
\mathcal{O}\!\left(\mathcal{S}\right)
. These stem from the tree-level
contribution as well as the 1-loop corrections to
M_{h}^{2}
in the
SM
,
incorporating logarithmic as well as non-logarithmic terms.

Eq. (
40
) demonstrates that the logarithmic parts
of the 2-loop matching correction to
\hat{\lambda}
and
\hat{v}
have
to be analysed in order to clarify whether a specific needed coupling
structure is generated during matching scale variation. This is the
case if for the regarded order in the couplings, a matching
contribution exists which also contains a logarithm of
Q_{\text{m}}
.
We can elucidate that further by regarding the concrete example of the
2-loop contributions of
\mathcal{O}\!\left(\lambda^{6}v^{2}\right)
to
M_{h}^{2}
. At that order,
we yield:

\frac{\text{d}M_{h}^{2}}{\text{d}(\ln Q_{\text{m}})}\Bigg|_{\lambda^{6}v^{2}}=-v^{2}\frac{\partial\Delta\lambda^{2\ell}}{\partial(\ln Q_{\text{m}})}\Bigg|_{\lambda^{6}}^{\text{miss}}-2\hat{\lambda}^{0\ell}\Big|_{\lambda^{2}}v\frac{\partial\Delta v^{2\ell}}{\partial(\ln Q_{\text{m}})}\Bigg|_{\lambda^{4}v}^{\text{miss}}.

(41)

Since there are UV-divergent
NMSSM
-specific 2-loop corrections
of
\mathcal{O}\!\left(\lambda^{6}\right)
to the quartic self-interaction vertex of
the
SM
-like Higgs, one can infer that logarithmic contributions
to the 2-loop matching relation for
\hat{\lambda}
enter at that
order. With that, we can conclude that the maching
scale variation generates 2-loop terms of
\mathcal{O}\!\left(\lambda^{6}v^{2}\right)

in
M_{h}^{2}
, as they have a counterpart in the explicitely

Q_{\text{m}}
-dependent part of the 2-loop maching corrections

\Delta\lambda^{2\ell}
and
\Delta v^{2\ell}
. Similar
analyses for all other appearing
NMSSM
-specific coupling structures
at 2-loop level lead to the same positive result. The matching scale variation
can thus be deemed an appropriate method to estimate the uncertainty of the
2-loop
FlexibleEFTHiggs
calculation w.r.t. the missing
NMSSM
-specific terms.

At 3-loop level, a similar analysis can be applied. Here, we find an
analogous result as at 2-loop level. In particular, missing terms enhanced by
large logarithms
L
are generated by matching scale variation. This can be
exemplified at
\mathcal{O}\!\left(\lambda^{8}v^{2}L\right)
evaluating again
eq. (
38
) for this specific case:

\displaystyle\frac{\text{d}M_{h}^{2}}{\text{d}(\ln Q_{\text{m}})}\Bigg|_{\lambda^{8}v^{2}L}

\displaystyle=-\hat{v}^{2}\Big|_{\lambda^{2}v^{2}L}\frac{\partial\Delta\lambda^{2\ell}}{\partial(\ln Q_{\text{m}})}\Bigg|_{\lambda^{6}}^{\text{miss}}-2\hat{\lambda}^{0\ell}\Big|_{\lambda^{4}L}v\frac{\partial\Delta v^{2\ell}}{\partial(\ln Q_{\text{m}})}\Bigg|_{\lambda^{4}v}^{\text{miss}}

\displaystyle\phantom{=}{}-2\hat{\lambda}^{0\ell}\Big|_{\lambda^{2}}\hat{v}\Big|_{\lambda^{2}L}\frac{\partial\Delta v^{2\ell}}{\partial(\ln Q_{\text{m}})}\Bigg|_{\lambda^{4}v}^{\text{miss}}.

(42)

Here, the large logarithm
L
appears due to the running of the
SM
parameters from
Q_{\text{m}}
to
Q_{\text{pole}}
by applying eq. (
34
).
The appearance of the large logarithm
L
corresponds to the fact that

N
2
LL
terms of
\mathcal{O}\!\left(\lambda^{8}v^{2}\right)
are not included in our
calculation. The matching scale variation estimates these missing
terms, though.

From the outcomes of the presented analysis above, we infer that matching
scale variation serves as a reliable mean for estimating the high-scale
uncertainty of the calculated Higgs mass
M_{h}^{2}
. Especially, this is also
true for the
NMSSM
-specific corrections entering the matching relations
between the
SM
and the
NMSSM
.

5
Summary and outlook

In this paper, we presented an analysis of the
SM
-like Higgs mass
in the
MSSM
and its dependencies on various parameters, based on the
implementation of the
FlexibleEFTHiggs
hybrid approach in
FlexibleSUSY
. The various
scenarios regarded here are characterized by non-degenerated
SUSY
mass spectra, complementing the consideration of “standard”
scenarios regarded in our previous study. In general, we found a very
good reliability of the
FlexibleEFTHiggs
calculation for the large majority of
the analysed cases according to the estimated theoretical
uncertainty. The uncertainty is typically of the order of

\sim 0.5\,\text{GeV}
. However, specific scenarios may lead to
significantly higher uncertainties, especially ones that have
a significant splitting in the third generation squark mass
parameters.

We reported also about the most recent implementation of the
FlexibleEFTHiggs
calculation in the
NMSSM
, now publicly avaiable as a part of
version 2.9.0 of
FlexibleSUSY
. It incorporates a treatment in the full-model
parametrization as well as the inclusion of
MSSM
-like 2-loop and
3-loop contributions to the quartic
SM
Higgs coupling. This

NMSSM
hybrid calculation thus provides a Higgs mass prediction at

N
3
LO
with a resummation of large logarithms of
N
3
LL
order
in the respective couplings. In this way, significant progress is
achieved in comparison to the previous
implementation of the
FlexibleEFTHiggs
method for the
NMSSM
in
FlexibleSUSY
.
The results obtained from applying the calculation to several
different scenarios indicate a high stability of the code. However,
it should be kept in mind that the 3-loop calculation can suffer
from discontinuities due to hierarchy transitions, which might be
problematic in some applications. In general, the 3-loop
SUSY

QCD
contributions have a rather small impact on the Higgs mass of the
order of
0.1\,\text{GeV}
. Therefore we recommend to reduce the
precision to 2-loop if such discontinuities lead to problems.

The reliability of the Higgs pole mass calculation in the
NMSSM
has
been further corroborated by studying the potential impact of

NMSSM
-specific missing higher-order terms. It was confirmed that
matching scale variation is an appropriate means to simulate the
effect of those disregarded contributions, unless very specific
conditions are fulfilled, like
\kappa\gg\lambda
. The theoretical
uncertainty was found to be nearly constant all over the
parameter space allowed by the perturbativity limit on
\lambda
and

\kappa
with
\Delta M_{h}\approx 0.7\,\text{GeV}
, making it one of the
most precise calculations of the SM-like Higgs boson mass in the

NMSSM
currently available.

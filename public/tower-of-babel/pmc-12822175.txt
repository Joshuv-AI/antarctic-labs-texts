Lengthened telomere length in peripheral blood leukocytes in patients with rheumatoid arthritis complicated with airway disease
Authors: Takashi Higuchi, Shomi Oka, Kota Shimada, Shinichiro Tsunoda, Satoshi Ito, Misuzu Fujimori, Tadashi Nakamura, Michita Suzuki, Koichiro Saisho, Satoshi Shinohara, Toshihiro Matsui, Kiyoshi Migita, Shouhei Nagaoka, Shigeto Tohma, Hiroshi Furukawa
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectivesPatients with rheumatoid arthritis (RA) frequently have complications including chronic lung diseases (CLDs) consisting of airway disease (AD) or interstitial lung disease (ILD) that confer a poor prognosis. Peripheral blood leukocyte telomere length is shortened in RA with ILD. Previous studies reported rare variants in telomerase genes that were deleterious were more frequent in RA with ILD compared with controls. However, these studies were conducted in European populations. Here, we investigated associations of telomere length with ILD or AD in Japanese RA.MethodsThe lengths of telomeres in 108 RA patients with usual interstitial pneumonia, 134 with nonspecific interstitial pneumonia, 236 with AD, 50 with emphysema and 425 without CLDs were measured by quantitative polymerase chain reaction.ResultsA significantly longer telomere length was observed in RA with AD (P = 0.0071). Multiple logistic regression analyses indicated the association of telomere length with AD remained (Padjusted=0.0029) when conditioned on TERT rs2736100, a single nucleotide variant related to AD in Japanese RA, and clinical characteristics, suggesting an independent association.ConclusionsWe report telomere length was independently associated with AD in Japanese RA patients.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s41927-025-00605-9.Keywords: Telomere length, Airway disease, Rheumatoid arthritis, Genetic association

Introduction

Rheumatoid arthritis (RA), a systemic autoimmune disease leading to the destruction of synovial joints [1], is frequently complicated with extra-articular disorders including chronic lung disease (CLD) [2] that consists of interstitial lung disease (ILD), airway disease (AD), and emphysema (EMP). ILD is a complication for 10% of RA patients [3] and it leads to poor disease outcomes [4]. ILD in RA can manifest as nonspecific interstitial pneumonia (NSIP) or usual interstitial pneumonia (UIP) [5]. AD occurrence in RA is also common [6–9] and it influences patient prognoses [10, 11]. Therefore, investigating the pathogenesis of CLD in RA further is vital.

RA susceptibility is influenced by genetic and environmental factors. For example, single nucleotide variants (SNVs) in MUC5B or FAM13A are linked to ILD in patients with RA [12–15]. However, susceptibility factors related to AD in RA are unclear [7, 16–20]. In addition, to ILD, TERT SNVs are related to AD in Japanese RA patients [21].

Previous studies showed that the telomere length was shortened in ILD [22], idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis [23], and RA [24], as well as RA with ILD [25–27]. Of note, European studies reported rare variants in telomerase genes that were deleterious were more common in RA with ILD [28]. Therefore, we investigated whether the telomere length was altered in RA with ILD, AD, or EMP in Japanese populations.

Materials and methods

Patients

RA patients enrolled in the study fulfilled RA criteria [29, 30], were Japanese natives, and lived in Japan. Those with chest computed tomography image information were recruited at research group hospitals and these were categorized to AD, NSIP, UIP, EMP, or no CLD groups based on computed tomography images, as previously described [20], AD: centrilobular or peribronchial nodules, branching linear structures, bronchial dilatation, bronchial wall thickening, or atelectasis, NSIP: bilateral ground-glass attenuation patterns predominantly in subpleural and basal regions, UIP: irregular linear opacities and honeycombing, EMP: low attenuation area or bullae, no CLD: no abnormalities in computed tomography images. The ILD group includes UIP and NSIP groups. Overall, 108 RA patients with UIP, 134 with NSIP, 236 with AD, 50 with EMP, and 425 without CLD were enlisted (Table 1).

Measurement of telomere length

Genomic DNA extracted by phenol chloroform underwent quantitative polymerase chain reaction following the manufacturer’s instructions (Absolute Telomere Length Quantification qPCR Assay Kit Human, ScienCell Research Laboratories, Carlsbad, CA, USA) to measure telomere lengths [31, 32]. Briefly, two quantitative polymerase chain reactions were conducted, one with the telomere primer set, and the other with the single copy reference primer set for target sample genomic DNA or the reference human genomic DNA with known telomere length on a 7500 Fast Real-Time PCR System (Thermo Fisher Scientific Inc.). Conditions for thermal cycling were denaturation at 95 °C for 10 min, followed by 32 cycles of 95 °C for 20 s, 52 °C for 20 s, and 72 °C for 45 s. Then, the telomere length of target samples was calculated with comparative CT method (https://www.sciencellonline.com/PS/8918.pdf) [33]. Genotyping data of rs2736100 [C/A] in TERT was previously reported [21].

Statistical analysis

Association of telomere length or clinical characteristics was verified by the Student t-test or Fisher’s exact test using 2 × 2 contingency tables or RA subsets were compared with those without CLD. The correlations between telomere length and age at enrollment were evaluated by Pearson’s correlation coefficient. Multiple logistic regression analyses were performed to assess independent associations. The individuals with missing clinical information were not used in multiple logistic regression analyses. Statistical power of 80% was obtained when the difference of average leukocyte telomere lengths was 1.78 [kbp] or higher between AD and CLD(-) groups. (http://biostat.mc.vanderbilt.edu/wiki/Main/PowerSampleSize) [34].

Results

Patients clinical characteristics

Demographic factors of RA patients with UIP, NSIP, ILD, AD, or EMP were compared to those with RA lacking CLD (Table 1). A higher mean age on the day of enrollment and percentages of males were observed for patients with RA with UIP, NSIP, or ILD than for RA without CLD. Positivity of rheumatoid factor (RF) and anti-citrullinated peptide antibody (ACPA) was increased in RA with UIP, NSIP, or ILD. Increased mean age at enrollment, RA duration, percentage of Steinbrocker stage Ⅲ or Ⅳ [35], and RF and ACPA positivity were increased in RA with AD. Body mass index of RA with AD was decreased compared with RA without CLD. Compared with those with RA without CLD, the mean age at enrollment, male proportion, percentage of ever smokers, and positivity of ACPA were higher in those with RA with EMP.

RA peripheral blood leukocyte telomere length

Peripheral blood leukocyte telomere length in RA (Supplementary Figure S1) was not associated with age (P = 0.2437, r2 = 0.0015). Associations between telomere length and UIP, NSIP, AD, or EMP in Japanese RA patients was evaluated (Table 1). Telomere lengths were significantly longer in RA with AD (P = 0.0071); however, telomere length was not associated with UIP, NSIP, ILD, or EMP in RA.

TERT rs2736100 was previously associated with AD in Japanese RA patients [21]. To eliminate SNV effects and the different clinical characteristics of AD and CLD negative patients, we performed multiple logistic regression analyses (Table 2). An association of AD with telomere length remained (Padjusted=0.0029, ORadjusted 1.04, 95% confidence interval [CI] 1.01–1.07), when conditioned on TERT rs2736100 and the patients’ clinical characteristics, suggesting telomere length was independently associated with AD in Japanese patients with RA.

Discussion

Although telomere length was previously shown to be independently associated with AD in Japanese RA patients, we revealed a longer telomere length in RA with AD. A previous study reported the telomere length was shortened in European RA patients with ILD, which was not observed in the current study. In recent report, the telomere length was not different between Korean RA with and without ILD and TERT rs2736100 was not associated with ILD in RA [36]. These data suggested the reduced pathogenic contribution of telomerase genes in ILD in East Asian RA patients. Additionally, rare variants in telomerase genes that are deleterious were not investigated for East Asian RA patients with ILD. Although the telomere length in AD or EMP patients was previously reported to be shorter [37–39], its length is unknown in RA patients with AD or EMP.

It was previously demonstrated that a short telomere length in leukocytes was linked to TERT rs2736100A, an idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis [40–42] and ILD risk allele in European RA patients, but not in Japanese RA [14, 21, 43]. However, the TERT rs2736100C allele, which is protective in those with idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis, was demonstrated to be a risk factor for AD in Japanese RA patients [21] and no association of TERT rs2736100 with EMP was detected (unpublished data). Telomere dysfunction was previously shown to be involved in the pathogenesis of AD [44]. The TERT rs2736100C allele might cause higher expression of the TERT gene and longer leukocyte telomere lengths, contributing to the occurrence of AD in Japanese RA. However, this mechanism would not contribute to the longer leukocyte telomere lengths in Japanese RA with EMP. TERT rs2736100A allele might cause lower expression of the TERT gene and shorter leukocyte telomere lengths, contributing to the occurrence of ILD in European RA. However, this mechanism would not contribute to the occurrence of ILD in Japanese RA.

Although the precise mechanisms of the pathogenesis of AD in RA are still unknown, some environmental factors could influence on the longer leukocyte telomere lengths in RA with AD. The leukocyte telomere lengths of smokers were shorter than those of non-smokers [45] and the percentage of ever smokers tended to be lower in RA with AD. Periodontitis was associated with shorter leukocyte telomere length [46] and anti-Porphyromonas gingivalis antibody titers tended to be lower in RA with AD [47]. These data suggested that smoking and periodontitis might contribute to the shorter leukocyte telomere lengths, as well as the production of anti-citrullinated peptide antibody. Since the percentage of ever smokers is lower in RA with AD and anti-Porphyromonas gingivalis antibody titers are lower in RA with AD, the longer leukocyte telomere lengths were observed in RA with AD. Since the percentage of ever smokers is higher in RA with ILD, the shorter leukocyte telomere lengths were observed in RA with ILD. Since anti-Porphyromonas gingivalis antibody titers are higher in RA without CLD, the shorter leukocyte telomere lengths were observed in RA without CLD. Since anti-Porphyromonas gingivalis antibody titers are extremely lower in RA with EMP, the longer leukocyte telomere lengths were observed in RA with EMP, though the percentage of ever smokers is higher. However, further studies are necessary to investigate the roles of altered leukocyte telomere lengths on the pathogenesis of CLD in RA.

The current study reports an association between telomere length and AD in RA, although some limitations exist. The size of the study sample was low and only Japanese patients were involved. Future multi-ethnic and large-scale studies ought to validate these results related to AD in RA. Although leukocyte telomere lengths were measured by quantitative polymerase chain reaction in this study, other methods should be used to validate the results, in future studies. In conclusion, the current study indicated an association between telomere length and AD in RA, suggesting the heterogeneous pathogenesis of RA.

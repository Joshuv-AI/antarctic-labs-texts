Geometric structures arising from the deformation of groups of Heisenberg type
Claudio Afeltra

Abstract
Motivated by the desire of finding a geometric interpretation to the Yamabe equation on groups of Heisenberg type, we define a geometric structure on manifolds modelled locally on these groups, which we call contact structure of Heisenberg type. In the case of the Heisenberg group is equivalent to contact Riemannian manifolds. We define a natural connection on these structures, we compute the formula for the conformal change of scalar curvature, and introduce the Yamabe problem for these manifods.



1
Introduction

Analysis on Lie groups plays an important role in modern analysis, allowing to better understand which of the several properties of
\mathbf{R}^{n}
are required for specific results, and having applications to differential geometry, partial differential equations, physics and other areas of mathematics and natural sciences.

Most results on Lie groups hold for specific subclasses of Lie groups.
A very studied family of Lie groups is the one of Carnot groups, namely simply connected groups
G
whose Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
has a stratification
\mathfrak{g}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}V_{k}
with
[V_{1},V_{i}]=V_{i+1}
for all
i
(
k
being called the step of
G
).
The most important and studied example of Carnot group is the Heisenberg group
\mathbf{H}^{n}
, which is a step two Carnot group (the smallest possible nontrivial step) with a second step of dimension one, properties that make its study particularly convenient and in many cases allow for explicit computations not possible in the general case.

In
[
K
]
Kaplan introduced a class of step two Carnot groups called groups of Heisenberg type which share many of properties of
\mathbf{H}^{n}
, and on which many of the computations on
\mathbf{H}^{n}
can be repeated or adapted (see
[
CDKR
]
Chapter 18 in
[
BLU
]
).

One of these properties pertains to the equation

-\Delta u=u^{\frac{Q+2}{Q-2}},\;\;\;u>0

(1)

where
\Delta
is the sublaplacian and
Q
is the homogeneous dimension (see Section
2
for the definition of such concepts).
Equation (
1
) in the case of
\mathbf{H}^{n}
is very important in differential geometry because it is equivalent to find the contact forms on
\mathbf{H}^{n}
solving the CR Yamabe problem, that is the problem of findind a contact form on a CR manifold whose Webster curvature is constant (see
[
JL1
]
or
[
DT
]
).
On
\mathbf{H}^{n}
this equation is known to have the solution

U(x,t)=C_{n}\left(\frac{1}{(1+|x|^{2})^{2}+16|t|^{2}}\right)^{\frac{Q-2}{4}}

with
(x,t)\in\mathbf{R}^{2n}\times\mathbf{R}=\mathbf{H}^{n}
. Geometrically this solution corresponds to the standard contact form on
S^{2n-1}\subset\mathbf{C}^{n}
pulled back to
\mathbf{H}^{n}
through the Cayley transform (a transformation analogous to the stereographic projection).

U
and its translation and dilations are the unique solutions to Equation (
1
) in
L^{\frac{2Q}{Q-2}}
(see
[
JL2
]
). It is an important open problem to find whether the same holds without integrability hypotheses.

Regarding groups of Heisenberg type, Garofalo and Vassilev discovered that Equation (
1
) has a solution with the same form of the one on
\mathbf{H}^{n}
,

U(x,t)=C_{G}\left(\frac{1}{(1+|x|^{2})^{2}+16|t|^{2}}\right)^{\frac{Q-2}{4}}

with
(x,t)\in\mathbf{R}^{2n}\times\mathbf{R}^{k}\simeq G
(see
[
GV
]
).
Later Yang (see
[
Y
]
) proved that the classification result of Folland and Stein for
\mathbf{H}^{n}
can be generalized to groups of Heisenberg type, that is that
U
and its translation and dilations are the unique solutions to Equation (
1
) in
L^{\frac{2Q}{Q-2}}
.

Despite all these similarities between the study of Equation (
1
) on
\mathbf{H}^{n}
and on groups of Heisenberg type, until now on the more general situation there is not a geometric interpretation of this equation analogous to the one on
\mathbf{H}^{n}
; that is, there is not a geometric structure allowing notions of scalar curvature and conformal change, such that the formula for the conformal change of the scalar curvature on
G
is equivalent to Equation (
1
).

Our aim in this work is to find such a geometric interpretation.
For this purpose, we introduce a geometric structure which we call contact structure of Heisenberg type, locally modelled on a group of Heisenberg type. It consists of a subbundle
\mathfrak{H}
of
TM
with a subriemannian metric
g
and of a vector space of forms
\mathscr{V}
such that, associating to every
\theta\in\mathscr{V}
the tensor
J_{\theta}
defined by
g(X,J_{\theta}Y)=d\theta(X,Y)
, the association
\theta\mapsto J_{\theta}
is a Clifford algebra, and furthermore a condition allowing the definition of Reeb vector fields analogous to the ones of contact geometry is satisfied (see Section
3
for the complete definition).
In the case
G=\mathbf{H}^{n}
, our notion is equivalent to contact Riemannian manifolds.
This structure admit a natural notion of conformal change, as shown in Section
3
.

In Section
4
we define a connection on contact manifolds of Heisenberg type. In the case of contact Riemannian manifolds our connection does not coincide with the Tanno connection, but with the Hermitian Tanno connection introduced by Nagase in
[
N
]
. In the case of CR manifolds they both coincide with the Tanaka-Webster connection.

Finally in Section
5
we define the scalar curvature in a manner analogous to Riemannian geometry, by contracting twice the curvature tensor, and we compute the formula for the conformal change of the scalar curvature, showing that up to an irrelevant constant it is equivalent to Equation (
1
).
We also introduce the Yamabe problem in this context, and notice that like in the Riemannian and in CR case, we can define a Yamabe constant
\mathscr{Y}(M)
such that
\mathscr{Y}(M)\leq\mathscr{\mathscr{}}{Y}(G)
, and such that if the strict inequality holds for a compact manifold
G
, then the Yamabe problem has solution.
Unlike the Riemannian and CR case, we are not able to find in general a compact manifold
M
for which, analogously to
S^{n}
in Riemannian geometry and
S^{2n-1}\subset\mathbf{C}^{n}
in CR geometry,
\mathscr{Y}(M)=\mathscr{\mathscr{}}{Y}(G)
(and we do not know whether it exists), but only for a subclass called groups of Iwasawa type. These groups admit a notion of spherical inversion which allows us to build a contact manifold of Heisenberg type diffeomorphic to
S^{2n+k}
by glueing two copies of
G
(see Section
3.2
).

We point out that the idea of defining a structure on a manifold locally modelled on groups of Heisenberg type is not new, but in
[
BGRV
]
the authors defined a structure which they called H-type foliation. Despite the similar goal, their structure does not coincide with ours and in particular it does not admit a notion of conformal change, which is our main motivation; the authors there were motivated by the generalization of other properties of groups of Heisenberg type to a non-flat case.

There are also connections between our work and the one of Biquard in
[
B
]
, where he defined a geometric structure called quaternionic contact manifold.

2
Groups of Heisenberg type

In this section we will recall the definition and some known facts about groups of Heisenberg type.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a real Lie algebra with a scalar product,
\mathfrak{z}
be the center thereof, and
\mathfrak{v}=\mathfrak{z}^{\perp}
the orthogonal of the center. Suppose that
[\mathfrak{v},\mathfrak{v}]\subset\mathfrak{z}
.
We define
J:\mathfrak{z}\to\End(\mathfrak{v})
as

\left\langle J_{T}X,Y\right\rangle=\left\langle T,[X,Y]\right\rangle

for
T\in\mathfrak{z}
,
X,Y\in\mathfrak{v}
.
Then
\mathfrak{g}
is said to be a Lie algebra of Heisenberg type if
J_{T}
is an isometry for every
T\in\mathfrak{z}
with
|T|=1
.
Equivalently,
\mathfrak{g}
is a Lie algebra of Heisenberg type if for every
T\in\mathfrak{z}

J_{T}^{*}=-J_{T}

(2)

and

J_{T}^{2}=-|T|^{2}I.

(3)

Condition (
3
), by polarization, and applying (
2
), is equivalent to

J_{T_{1}}J_{T_{2}}+J_{T_{2}}J_{T_{1}}=-2\left\langle T_{1},T_{2}\right\rangle I

for
T_{1},T_{2}\in\mathfrak{z}
.

A Lie group
G
with a scalar product on its Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is said a group of Heisenberg type if it is simply connected and
\mathfrak{g}
is of Heisenberg type.

Therefore groups of Heisenberg type are a subclass of step two Carnot groups.

We recall that, given two real vector space
V
and
W
endowed with a scalar product, a homomorphism
J:V\to\End(W)
is called a Clifford module if
J_{v}
is antisymmetric for every
v
and the Clifford relation

J_{v}J_{u}+J_{u}J_{v}=-2\left\langle v,u\right\rangle I

holds for every
v,u\in V
.

Therefore in a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{v}\oplus\mathfrak{z}
of Heisenberg type
\mathfrak{v}
is a Clifford module over
\mathfrak{z}
. This allows to apply the theory of Clifford modules.
Vice versa, given a Clifford module
W
over
V
, the space
W\times V
with the bracket defined by
g([(X_{1},T_{1}),(X_{2},T_{2})],S)=g(J_{S}X_{1},X_{2})
for
X_{1},X_{2}\in W
,
T_{1},T_{2},S\in V
is a Lie algebra of Heiseneberg type.

Let us call
\dim(\mathfrak{v})=2n
and
\dim(\mathfrak{z})=k
((
3
) obviously implies that
\dim(\mathfrak{v})
is even).
Then
\mathfrak{g}
, being a stratified Lie algebra, has the family of automorphisms

\delta_{\lambda}(X,T)=(\lambda X,\lambda^{2}T)

for
\lambda\in(0,\infty)
, and it holds that

(\delta_{\lambda}\mu)=\lambda^{Q}\mu

where
\mu
is a Haar measure and
Q=2n+2k
is called the homogeneous dimension of
\mathfrak{g}
.

We define the sublaplacian of
G
as the differential operator

\Delta=\sum_{\alpha=1}^{2n}X_{\alpha}^{2}

where
X_{1},\ldots,X_{2n}
is an orthonormal basis of
\mathfrak{g}
(it is elementary to verify that the definition does not depend on the basis).

The equation

-\Delta u=u^{\frac{Q+2}{Q-2}},\;\;\;u>0

has the solution

U(x,t)=C_{G}\left(\frac{1}{(1+|x|^{2})^{2}+16|t|^{2}}\right)^{\frac{Q-2}{4}}

(4)

found by Garofalo and Vassilev in
[
GV
]
, and which, thanks to a result of Yang in
[
Y
]
, up to left translation and dilation, is the only solution in
L^{\frac{2Q}{Q-2}}
.

On groups of Heisenberg type a spherical inversion analogous to the one in
\mathbf{R}^{n}
and on the Heisenberg group has been introduced in
[
CDKR
]
:

\sigma(X,T)=\left(\left(-|X|^{2}I+4J_{T}\right)^{-1}X,-\frac{T}{|X|^{4}+16|T|^{2}}\right)

(5)

for
(X,T)\in\mathfrak{g}\setminus\{0\}
, and carried to
G
by exponentiation.

\sigma
has meaningful properties only on a subclass of the groups of Heisenberg type, as the following result shows (see Theorems 4.2 and 5.1 in
[
CDKR
]
).

Theorem 2.1
.

The following are equivalent.

•

G
is the nilpotent part of the Iwasawa decomposition of a rank one simple group.

•

for every
X\in\mathfrak{v}
and
T_{1},T_{2}\in\mathfrak{z}
there exist
T\in\mathfrak{z}
such that
J_{T}X=J_{T_{1}}J_{T_{2}}X
.

•

\sigma
preserves the horizontal distribution.

A group verifying one of the equivalent statements of Theorem
2.1
is called a group of Iwasawa type.

3
Contact structures of Heisenberg type

Let
M
be a manifold,
\mathfrak{H}
a
2n
-dimensional subbundle of
TM
with a metric
g
, and
\mathscr{V}
a
k
-dimensional vector space of differential forms whose restriction to
\mathfrak{H}
is null and such that the induced forms on
TM/\mathfrak{H}
constitute the whole dual thereof (which implies that
\dim(M)=2n+k
), endowed with a scalar product
\left\langle\cdot,\cdot\right\rangle
.

We say that
(M,\mathfrak{H},g,\mathscr{V})
is a contact manifold of Heisenberg type if:

•

for every
\theta\in\mathscr{V}
the operator on
\mathfrak{H}
defined by
g(X,J_{\theta}Y)=d\theta(X,Y)
is such that the map
\mathscr{V}\to\mathscr{L}(\mathfrak{H}_{x})
given by
\theta\mapsto J_{\theta}
is a Clifford algebra structure for every
x\in M
;

•

calling
T_{\theta}
the vector field defined by
i_{T_{\theta}}d\theta=0
and
\psi(T_{\theta})=\left\langle\psi,\theta\right\rangle
for every
\psi\in\mathscr{V}
, it holds that the map
\theta\mapsto T_{\theta}
is linear (and its image is called
\mathfrak{T}
, Reeb subspace).

We extend
g
to the whole
TM
imposing that
\mathfrak{H}
and
\mathfrak{Z}
be orthogonal and that the extension restricted to
\mathfrak{T}
coincide with the product dual to
\left\langle\cdot,\cdot\right\rangle
.

Remark 3.1
.

An equivalent definition is defining a form
\bm{\theta}
with values on a vector space
V
with a scalar product, such that
\ker\bm{\theta}=\mathfrak{H}
and the family of forms
\{\psi\circ\bm{\theta}\;|\;\psi\in V^{*}\}
satisfies the properties of the original definition.

Remark 3.2
.

If
\dim\mathscr{V}=1
then a contact structure of Heisenberg type is equivalent to a Riemannian contact structure.

Remark 3.3
.

If
G
is a Lie group of Heisenberg type then thanks to the scalar product we can associate to every
T\in\mathfrak{z}
a form which is zero on
\mathfrak{v}
, and

d\theta_{T}(X,Y)=-\theta_{T}([X,Y])=-\left\langle T,[X,Y]\right\rangle=-g(J_{T}X,Y)=g(X,J_{T}Y)

by definition, therefore it gives
G
a structure of contact manifold of Heisenberg type.

Given a contact manifold of Heisenberg type
M
, we call
G(M)
the group of Heisenberg type whose Lie algebra is the one corresponding to the Clifford algebra
\mathscr{V}\to\mathscr{L}(\mathfrak{H}_{x})
in the definition (whose isomorphism type does not depend on
x
), and say that
M
is locally modelled on
G(M)
.

3.1
Conformal change

Given
f
smooth and nowhere zero, let
\widetilde{\mathscr{V}}=\{f\theta|\theta\in\mathscr{V}\}
and
\widetilde{g}=fg
. Then

\widetilde{g}(X,J_{\theta}Y)=fg(X,J_{\theta}Y)=fd\theta(X,Y)=d(f\theta)(X,Y)=d\widetilde{\theta}(X,Y)

and therefore
J_{f\theta}=J_{\theta}
.
Therefore they form at every point a Clifford algebra structure.

Furthermore
\widetilde{T}_{f\theta}=\frac{1}{f}T_{\theta}+X_{\theta,f}
with

0=i_{\frac{1}{f}T_{\theta}+X_{\theta,f}}(fd\theta+df\wedge\theta)|_{\mathfrak{H}}=fi_{X_{\theta,f}}d\theta|_{\mathfrak{H}}-\frac{|\theta|^{2}}{f}df|_{\mathfrak{H}}

therefore
X_{\theta,f}
is determined uniquely by
i_{X_{\theta,f}}d\theta|_{\mathfrak{H}}=\frac{|\theta|^{2}}{f^{2}}df|_{\mathfrak{H}}
.

Since

\theta_{\psi}(T_{\theta_{1}}+T_{\theta_{2}})=\left\langle\psi,\theta_{1}\right\rangle+\left\langle\psi,\theta_{2}\right\rangle=\left\langle\psi,\theta_{1}+\theta_{2}\right\rangle

and obviously
T_{c\theta}=cT_{\theta}
, the linearity of
\theta\mapsto T_{\theta}
is equivalent to

0=i_{T_{\theta_{1}}+T_{\theta_{2}}}d(\theta_{1}+\theta_{2})|_{\mathfrak{H}}=\left.\left(i_{T_{\theta_{1}}}d\theta_{2}+i_{T_{\theta_{2}}}d\theta_{1}\right)\right|_{\mathfrak{H}}

and therefore, after the conformal change, calling
X=X_{\theta_{1},f}
and
Y=X_{\theta_{2},f}
,

\left.\left(i_{\widetilde{T}_{\theta_{1}}}d\widetilde{\theta}_{2}+i_{\widetilde{T}_{\theta_{2}}}d\widetilde{\theta}_{1}\right)\right|_{\mathfrak{H}}=

=\left.\left(i_{\frac{1}{f}T_{\theta_{1}}+X}(fd\theta_{2}+df\wedge\theta_{2})+i_{\frac{1}{f}T_{\theta_{2}}+Y}(fd\theta_{1}+df\wedge\theta_{1})\right)\right|_{\mathfrak{H}}=

=\left.\left(i_{T_{\theta_{1}}}d\theta_{2}+fi_{X}d\theta_{2}+\frac{1}{f}T_{\theta_{1}}(f)\theta_{2}-\frac{1}{f}\left\langle\theta_{1},\theta_{2}\right\rangle df+X(f)\theta_{2}\right)\right|_{\mathfrak{H}}+

+\left.\left(i_{T_{\theta_{2}}}d\theta_{1}+fi_{Y}d\theta_{1}+\frac{1}{f}T_{\theta_{2}}(f)\theta_{1}-\frac{1}{f}\left\langle\theta_{1},\theta_{2}\right\rangle df+Y(f)\theta_{1}\right)\right|_{\mathfrak{H}}=

=\left(i_{T_{\theta_{1}}}d\theta_{2}+i_{T_{\theta_{2}}}d\theta_{1}+fi_{X}d\theta_{2}+fi_{Y}d\theta_{1}-\frac{2}{f}\left\langle\theta_{1},\theta_{2}\right\rangle df\right)|_{\mathfrak{H}}.

For every
Z\in\mathfrak{H}
it holds that

fi_{X}d\theta_{2}(Z)=fd\theta_{2}(X,Z)=fg(X,J_{\theta_{2}}Z)=-\frac{1}{|\theta_{1}|^{2}}fg(X,J_{\theta_{1}}^{2}J_{\theta_{2}}Z)=

=-\frac{1}{|\theta_{1}|^{2}}fd\theta_{1}(X,J_{\theta_{1}}J_{\theta_{2}}Z)=-\frac{1}{|\theta_{1}|^{2}}fi_{X}d\theta_{1}(J_{\theta_{1}}J_{\theta_{2}}Z)=-\frac{1}{f}df(J_{\theta_{1}}J_{\theta_{2}}Z)

and analogously
fi_{Y}d\theta_{1}(Z)=-\frac{1}{f}df(J_{\theta_{2}}J_{\theta_{1}}Z)
, therefore

fi_{X}d\theta_{2}(Z)+fi_{Y}d\theta_{1}(Z)=-\frac{1}{f}df(J_{\theta_{1}}J_{\theta_{2}}Z)-\frac{1}{f}df(J_{\theta_{2}}J_{\theta_{1}}Z)=

=-\frac{1}{f}df((J_{\theta_{1}}J_{\theta_{2}}+J_{\theta_{2}}J_{\theta_{1}})Z)=2\left\langle\theta_{1},\theta_{2}\right\rangle\frac{1}{f}df(Z)

and therefore we have proved that the new structure satisfies the definition of contact manifold of Heisenberg type.

3.2
The Iwasawa sphere

Thanks to the Garofalo-Lanconelli solution
U
defined in formula (
4
) we can define a manifold analogous to the sphere
S^{n}
with its standard metric in Riemannian geometry and to the sphere
S^{2n-1}\subset\mathbf{C}^{n}
with its standard contact form in CR geometry.

In fact, thanks to the computations in
[
CDKR
]
, given a group of Heisenberg type
G
with its standard contact structure of Heisenberg type
\mathscr{V}
defined in Remark
3.3
, if we perform a conformal change by defining
\widetilde{\mathscr{V}}=\{U^{\frac{4}{Q-2}}\theta\;|\;\theta\in\mathscr{V}\}
, then
\widetilde{\mathscr{V}}
is left invariant by the spherical inversion
\sigma
defined in formula
5
, and therefore we can define a contact structure of Heisenberg type on
S^{2n+2k}
by glueing two copies of
G
along
G\setminus\{0\}
through
\sigma
. We call
S^{2n+2k}
with this structure the Iwasawa sphere
\bm{S}_{G}
. For example
\bm{S}_{H^{n}}
is the sphere
S^{2n-1}\subset\mathbf{C}^{n}
with its standard CR structure and contact form.

Thanks to Theorem
5.3
, we will show that the Iwasawa sphere has constant scalar curvature.

4
A natural connection

We recall that given a vector bundle
E
over the manifold
M
and a subbundle
V
of
TM
, a partial connection on
E
along
V
is a linear map
\nabla:\Gamma(E)\to\Gamma(V*\times E)
such that
\nabla s=df\oplus s+f\nabla s
. The restriction of an affine connection to
V
is a partial connection, and viceversa if
TM=V\oplus W
and
\nabla^{V}
and
\nabla^{W}
are partial connections along
V
and
W
respectively, there exists a unique affine connection whose restrictions along
V
and
W
coincide with
\nabla^{V}
and
\nabla^{W}
.
If
E=V
then the torsion tensor
T\in\Gamma(V^{*}\times V^{*}\times TM)
,

T(X,Y)=\nabla_{X}Y-\nabla_{Y}X-[X,Y]

is defined.

The aim of this section is to define an affine connection on
\mathfrak{H}
. Thanks to the above remarks, we can study the question of a partial connection along
\mathfrak{H}
and a partial connection along
\mathfrak{T}
separately.

4.1
Partial connection along
\mathfrak{H}

Lemma 4.1
.

There exists a connection on
\mathfrak{H}
such that the space
\{J_{\theta}|\theta\in\mathscr{V}\}
and
g
are parallel.

Proof.

Since
\mathscr{V}\to\mathscr{L}(\mathfrak{H})
is a Clifford algebra structure, it is a module over
\operatorname{Cliff}(\mathscr{V})
, and therefore by restriction a representation
\rho:\operatorname{Pin}(\mathscr{V})\to GL(\mathfrak{H})
is induced.

Let
\mathfrak{F}(\mathfrak{H})
the proncipal bundle over
GL(\mathbf{R}^{2n})
of the frames of
\mathfrak{H}
.
Let
\widetilde{\rho}:\operatorname{Pin}(\mathbf{R}^{k})\mapsto\operatorname{End}(\mathbf{R}^{2n})
be a model representation isomorphic to
\rho
. For
\varphi\in\operatorname{Pin}(\mathbf{R}^{k})
let
a_{ij}(g)
be the coefficients of
\rho(g)
.
Let
(X_{1},\ldots,X_{2n})
a smooth local frame for
\mathfrak{H}
such that
\rho(\varphi(g))X_{i}=a_{ij}(g)
.
Then

P=\{(\sum_{i}a_{i1}(g)(X_{1}),\ldots,\sum_{i}a_{i,2n}(g)(X_{2n}))|g\in\operatorname{Pin}(\mathscr{V}_{x})\}

with the natural structure of principal
\operatorname{Pin}(\mathbf{R}^{k})
-bundle is a reduction of the structure group of
\mathfrak{F}(\mathfrak{H})
from
GL(\mathbf{R}^{2n})
to
\rho(\operatorname{Pin}(\mathbf{R}^{k}))
.

By the general theory of connections there exists a principal connection on
P
. Let
\nabla
be the associated connection on
\mathfrak{H}
through
\rho
. Since
\rho(\operatorname{Pin}(\mathscr{V}))
is parallel,
\nabla_{X}J_{\theta}
must belong to the tangent space of
\rho(\operatorname{Pin}(\mathscr{V}))
in
J_{\theta}
, that is
d\rho(T_{\theta}\operatorname{Pin}(\mathscr{V}))=J_{\theta}(\operatorname{span}(J_{i}J_{j}))
(where
J_{i}=J_{\theta^{i}}
for some orthonormal basis
\theta^{1},\ldots,\theta^{k}
of
\mathscr{V}
).

Therefore

\nabla_{X}J_{i}=J_{i}\sum_{j<k}a_{jk}J_{j}J_{k}=\sum_{j\neq i}a_{j}J_{j}+J_{i}\sum_{j<k,j,k\neq i}a_{jk}J_{j}J_{k}

It holds that

0=-\nabla_{X}(I)=J_{i}\nabla_{X}J_{i}+(\nabla_{X}J_{i})J_{i}=

=J_{i}\sum_{j\neq i}a_{j}J_{j}+J_{i}J_{i}\sum_{j<k,j,k\neq i}a_{jk}J_{j}J_{k}+\sum_{j\neq i}a_{j}J_{j}J_{i}+J_{i}\sum_{j<k,j,k\neq i}a_{jk}J_{j}J_{k}J_{i}=

=-2\sum_{j<k,j,k\neq i}a_{jk}J_{j}J_{k}

So
\sum_{j<k,j,k\neq i}a_{jk}J_{j}J_{k}=0
and therefore
\nabla_{X}J_{i}=\sum_{j\neq i}a_{j}J_{j}
.
∎

Lemma 4.2
.

Given a partial connection along
\mathfrak{H}
satisfying the thesis of Lemma
4.1
, another connection
\widetilde{\nabla}_{X}=\nabla_{X}+a_{X}
satisfies it if and only if

a_{X}=\sum_{i<j}\alpha_{ij}(X)J_{\theta^{i}}J_{\theta^{j}}+b_{X}

(where
\theta^{1},\ldots,\theta^{k}
is an orthonormal basis of
\mathscr{V}
)
for some differential forms
\alpha_{ij}
and for
b_{X}
antisymmetric and satisfying
b_{X}J_{\theta}=J_{\theta}b_{X}
for every
X
and
\theta
.

Proof.

It is a standard fact that
\widetilde{\nabla}g=0
if and only if
a_{X}
is antisymmetric.
In order to leave
\{J_{\theta}|\theta\in\mathscr{V}\}
parallel,

((\widetilde{\nabla}_{X}-\nabla_{X})J_{\theta})(Y)=

=(\widetilde{\nabla}_{X}-\nabla_{X})(J_{\theta}Y)-J_{\theta}((\widetilde{\nabla}_{X}-\nabla_{X})Y)=a_{X}(J_{\theta}Y)-J_{\theta}(a_{X}(Y))

must be equal to
J_{\psi}Y
for some
\psi\in\mathscr{V}
. Differentiating
J_{\theta}^{2}=1
we get that
(\nabla_{X}J_{\theta})J_{\theta}+J_{\theta}\nabla_{X}J_{\theta}=0
and
(\widetilde{\nabla}_{X}J_{\theta})J_{\theta}+J_{\theta}\widetilde{\nabla}_{X}J_{\theta}=0
, therefore
J_{\psi}
must anticommute with
J_{\theta}
, which implies that
\left\langle\psi,\theta\right\rangle=0
.
Calling
\psi=A(X)\psi
, in a basis

A(X)\theta^{i}=\sum_{j\neq i}\alpha_{ij}(X)\theta^{j}
.
Differentiating the Clifford relation as before we get

(\nabla_{X}J_{\theta^{i}})J_{\theta^{j}}+J_{\theta^{i}}(\nabla_{X}J_{\theta^{j}})+(\nabla_{X}J_{\theta^{j}})J_{\theta^{i}}+J_{\theta^{j}}(\nabla_{X}J_{\theta^{i}})=0

and

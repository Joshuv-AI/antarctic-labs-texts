Alexander Freiszlinger, Dirk Pauly, Dirk Praetorius, Michael Schomburg

Abstract
. In the present work, we derive functional upper bounds for the potential error arising from finite-element boundary-element coupling formulations for a nonlinear Poisson-type transmission problem. The proposed a posteriori error estimates are independent of the precise discretization scheme and provide guaranteed upper bounds for the potential error. The computation of these upper bounds is based on the solutions of local auxiliary finite element problems on patches in the interior domain and in a strip domain along the coupling boundary. Numerical experiments illustrate the performance of the proposed error estimation strategy for a related adaptive mesh-refinement strategy.

1.
Introduction

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d\in\{2,3\}
, be a bounded Lipschitz domain with connected polygonal boundary
\Gamma\coloneqq\partial\Omega
. Let
\bm{n}_{\Gamma}
be the exterior unit normal vector on
\Gamma
. Denote by
\Omega^{\mathrm{ext}}\coloneqq\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\overline{\Omega}
the exterior domain with respect to
\Omega
.
Given a volume force
f
and boundary data
(g_{\textnormal{D}},g_{\textnormal{N}})
, we consider the transmission problem of finding
u
and
u^{\mathrm{ext}}
such that

(1a)

\displaystyle-\div(\mathfrak{A}\nabla u)=f

\displaystyle\quad\text{in }\Omega,

(1b)

\displaystyle-\Delta u^{\mathrm{ext}}=0

\displaystyle\quad\text{in }\Omega^{\mathrm{ext}},

(1c)

\displaystyle u-u^{\mathrm{ext}}=g_{D}

\displaystyle\quad\text{on }\Gamma,

(1d)

\displaystyle(\mathfrak{A}\nabla u-\nabla u^{\mathrm{ext}})\cdot\bm{n}_{\Gamma}=g_{\textnormal{N}}

\displaystyle\quad\text{on }\Gamma,

(1e)

\displaystyle u^{\mathrm{ext}}(x)=\mathcal{O}(|x|^{-1})

\displaystyle\quad\text{as }|x|\to\infty,

where
\mathfrak{A}\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a (possibly nonlinear) strongly monotone and Lipschitz continuous operator in the sense of
[
ZB90
]
, i.e.,

(2a)

\displaystyle\lvert\mathfrak{A}x-\mathfrak{A}y\rvert

\displaystyle\leq C_{\textnormal{Lip}}\lvert x-y\rvert\quad\text{for all }x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

(2b)

\displaystyle C_{\textnormal{mon}}\lvert x-y\rvert^{2}

\displaystyle\leq(\mathfrak{A}x-\mathfrak{A}y)\cdot(x-y)\quad\text{for all }x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

In 2D, validity of the radiation condition (
1e
) requires additional assumptions on the data
(f,g_{\textnormal{N}})
, while for general data (
1e
) holds with the unphysical radiation condition
\mathcal{O}(\log\lvert x\rvert)
; see
[
McL00
, Section 8]
and Remark
2.1
.

Since problem (
1
) involves the unbounded exterior domain
\Omega^{\mathrm{ext}}
, usual solution strategies represent
u^{\mathrm{ext}}
in terms of certain integral operators applied to some density
\phi
on the boundary.
While this favors a boundary element approach in the exterior domain
\Omega^{\mathrm{ext}}
, the presence of the nonlinearity
\mathfrak{A}
recommends the use of finite elements in the interior domain
\Omega
.
In this framework, different FEM-BEM coupling methods have been proposed, e.g., the Johnson–Nédélec coupling
[
JN80
]
, the symmetric Costabel–Han coupling
[
Cos88
,
Han90
]
, and the Bielak–MacCamy coupling
[
BM83
,
BMZ95
]
.
We refer to
[
AFF+12
]
for the proof that all these coupling formulations admit unique solutions
(u,\phi)\in H^{1}(\Omega)\times H^{-1/2}(\Gamma)
on the continuous level, whereas discrete well-posedness of the respective conforming Galerkin discretization is only guaranteed in general for the Costabel–Han coupling. Instead, the Johnson–Nédélec coupling and the Bielak–MacCamy coupling require sufficient diffusion in the interior domain to guarantee discrete well-posedness, e.g.,
C_{\textnormal{mon}}>1/4
in
[
AFF+12
]
or some relaxed but
\Omega
-dependent condition in
[
Fer23
]
.

Given a conforming approximation
(u_{\ell},\phi_{\ell})\in H^{1}(\Omega)\times H^{-1/2}(\Gamma)
of
(u,\phi)
, where
u_{\ell},\phi_{\ell}
are related to some simplicial mesh
\mathcal{T}_{\ell}
of
\Omega
and its induced boundary mesh
\mathcal{T}_{\ell}^{\Gamma}{\coloneqq\mathcal{T}_{\ell}|_{\Gamma}}
on
\Gamma
, this work aims, for the first time, to derive and analyze an
a posteriori
error estimation strategy based on so-called
functional upper bounds
.
Unlike available estimators, the derived bounds thus do not rely on how the approximation
(u_{\ell},\phi_{\ell})
has been computed (e.g., Galerkin discretization with/without exact quadrature, matrix compression of the involved discrete boundary integral operators, and inexact iterative solver).

A posteriori
error estimation for the symmetric coupling is well-established; see, e.g.,
[
CS95
,
CFS96
,
Car96
,
MMS97
,
MS99
,
BS99
,
GM99
,
GHS01
,
SM05
,
LMST10
]
.
In
[
AFF+12
,
AFKP12
]
,
a posteriori
estimates have been derived also for the Johnson–Nédélec coupling and the Bielak–MacCamy coupling.
However, error control in all these works focuses on the error in
H^{1}(\Omega)\times H^{-1/2}(\Gamma)
, i.e.,
{\lVert u-u_{\ell}\rVert_{H^{1}(\Omega)}+}\lVert\phi-\phi_{\ell}\rVert_{H^{-1/2}(\Gamma)}
, and not on the (in practice more important) potential error

(3)

\mathcal{E}(u_{\ell},\phi_{\ell})\coloneqq\lVert\nabla(u-u_{\ell})\rVert_{\mathfrak{A}}+\lVert\nabla(u^{\mathrm{ext}}-u^{\mathrm{ext}}_{\ell})\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega^{\mathrm{ext}})},

where
u^{\mathrm{ext}}_{\ell}
is an approximation of
u^{\mathrm{ext}}
extracted from
u_{\ell}
and
\phi_{\ell}
and
\lVert\nabla(u-u_{\ell})\rVert_{\mathfrak{A}}^{2}\coloneqq\langle\mathfrak{A}\nabla u-\mathfrak{A}\nabla u_{\ell}\nonscript\>,\allowbreak\nonscript\>\nabla(u-u_{\ell})\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
denotes the error measure induced by the nonlinear diffusion
\mathfrak{A}
.
Moreover, up until now, only residual-based error estimators provide unconditional upper bounds (e.g.,
[
CS95
,
CFS96
,
Car96
,
BS99
,
GM99
,
GHS01
,
AFF+12
]
), while others like the two-level error estimators (e.g.,
[
MS99
,
MMS97
,
AFKP12
]
) rely on a suitable saturation assumption.
Hence,
a posteriori
error estimation strategies based on functional upper bounds constitute a considerable improvement, since there exist neither
a posteriori
estimates controlling the more desirable potential error directly, nor estimates which do not depend on the underlying discretization scheme.
Moreover, for particular situations like linear diffusion, the proposed estimator is, up to data oscillations, constant-free and does not involve unknown generic constants.

Estimators of functional type have been developed in, e.g.,
[
Rep08
,
AP16
]
for problems in bounded domains,
[
PR09
]
for problems in unbounded domains, and
[
KPP+21
]
for the boundary element method.
Building on these existing works on functional error estimates and combining it with ideas from
a posteriori
error estimation by equilibrated fluxes (see, e.g.,
[
AO97
,
BS08
,
EV15
]
) for the FEM part, we derive guaranteed upper bounds for the combined potential error
\mathcal{E}(u_{\ell},\phi_{\ell})
defined in (
3
) that, additionally, are constant-free up to data oscillations for linear diffusion operators
\mathfrak{A}
.

We emphasize that our analysis is independent of the precise discretization scheme.
In fact, the proposed
functional error estimate
provides guaranteed upper bounds for any conforming approximation
(u_{\ell},\phi_{\ell})
and hence any type of discretization method (e.g., Galerkin methods, collocation approaches, different coupling strategies, etc.).

In this paper, the functional estimates are realized by local auxiliary problems in
\Omega
and, in a first essentially auxiliary step (Algorithm
4.1
), in a (bounded) boundary strip
\omega\subset\Omega^{\mathrm{ext}}
with
\Gamma\subset\partial\omega
(see also
[
KPP+21
]
), whereas later
\omega\subseteq\Omega
with
\Gamma\subset\partial\omega
(Algorithm
4.2
).
While we rely on an auxiliary flux
\bm{\sigma}_{\ell}\approx\mathfrak{A}\nabla u_{\ell}
computed by solving local discrete problems of dual mixed type in order to control
\lVert\nabla(u-u_{\ell})\rVert_{\mathfrak{A}}
, we aim to control
\lVert\nabla(u^{\mathrm{ext}}-u^{\mathrm{ext}}_{\ell})\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega^{\mathrm{ext}})}
by use of local discrete solutions
w_{\ell}
for the Laplace equation in a boundary strip domain
\omega
, improving the approach from
[
KPP+21
]
, where a global discrete problem in
\omega
was considered to control the BEM error.
This strategy naturally comes with certain technical and computational complications, as the domains of the local auxiliary functions change within the adaptive loop, which impedes the analysis of the discrete solutions
\bm{\sigma}_{\ell}
and
w_{\ell}
.
However, the behaviour of FEM-solutions on boundary strips was recently addressed in
[
FPP25
]
and the generation of volume meshes is a standard problem in the context of the theory of finite elements.

Finally, by use of Poincaré–Steklov operators all computation for solving the local auxiliary problems can be performed on
\Omega
so that meshing of (parts of) the exterior domain can indeed be avoided.

Outline

The paper is organized as follows.
In Section
2
, we gather the necessary notation as well as useful preliminary results, which include basic properties of boundary integral operators and solvability of certain FEM-BEM coupling methods.
Section
3
is dedicated to the derivation of the functional upper bounds (Theorem
3.3
), which are based on the error identity from Lemma
3.1
.
An improved variant for linear problems is stated in Corollary
3.6
, while Corollary
3.8
shows how the meshing of the exterior domain can be avoided.
Furthermore, fully computable and localizable error estimators are constructed. In Section
4
, we formulate two adaptive FEM-BEM algorithms in the frame of the symmetric Costabel–Han coupling, where Algorithm
4.1
relies on meshing parts of the exterior domain, which is avoided by Algorithm
4.2
. Lastly, Section
5
presents numerical experiments involving different coupling methods and various geometries, which illustrate the accuracy of the proposed error estimation strategy and underline that the related adaptive algorithms are capable of regaining optimal convergence rates.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
General Notation

Throughout this paper, let
\Omega
be a bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d\in\{2,3\}
, with Lipschitz boundary
\Gamma\coloneqq\partial\Omega
and exterior unit normal
\bm{n}_{\Gamma}
.
We suppose that
\Gamma
is polygonal and connected.
Let
\Omega^{\mathrm{ext}}\coloneqq\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\overline{\Omega}
be the associated exterior domain. We denote by
\lvert\cdot\rvert
, without any ambiguity, the absolute value of a scalar, the Euclidean norm of a vector in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, the cardinality of a (finite) set, the
(d-1)
-dimensional Hausdorff measure of a surface (piece), or the
d
-dimensional Lebesgue volume of a set.
We write
\int_{\Xi}\cdot\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x
for integration over a Lebesgue-measurable set
\Xi\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
or over a Hausdorff measurable set
\Xi\subseteq\Gamma
.
We define
L^{2}(\Xi)
as the usual Lebesgue space of square-integrable functions on
\Xi
equipped with the usual inner product
\langle v\nonscript\>,\allowbreak\nonscript\>w\rangle_{\Xi}\coloneqq\int_{\Xi}vw\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x
and norm
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{\Xi}\coloneqq\langle\cdot\nonscript\>,\allowbreak\nonscript\>\cdot\rangle_{\Xi}^{1/2}
.
For vector-valued spaces
[L^{2}(\Xi)]^{n}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we will omit the superscript and write simply
L^{2}(\Xi)
if it is clear from the context.
Related discrete quantities or approximations will be indexed by the same subscript, i.e.,
(u_{\ell},\phi_{\ell})
is an approximation related to the mesh
\mathcal{T}_{\ell}
etc.
Finally, we abbreviate notation in proofs and write
A\lesssim B
if there exists a generic constant
C>0
, which is clear from the context, such that
A\leq CB
. We write
A\simeq B
if
A\lesssim B
and
B\lesssim A
.

2.2.
Function spaces

For Lipschitz domains
{U}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we define the usual first-order Sobolev space
H^{1}({U})
as the set of all functions
v\in L^{2}({U})
with distributional gradient
\nabla v\in L^{2}({U})
and equip it with the norm

\lVert v\rVert_{H^{1}({U})}\coloneqq(\lVert v\rVert_{{U}}^{2}+\lVert\nabla v\rVert_{{U}}^{2})^{1/2}.

With the distributional divergence operator
\div
, we define

H(\div,{U})\coloneqq\{\bm{\sigma}\in L^{2}({U})^{d}\nonscript\>|\allowbreak\nonscript\>\mathopen{}\div\bm{\sigma}\in L^{2}({U})\}

with the corresponding graph norm

\lVert\bm{\sigma}\rVert_{H(\div,{U})}\coloneqq(\lVert\bm{\sigma}\rVert_{{U}}^{2}+\lVert\div\bm{\sigma}\rVert_{{U}}^{2})^{1/2}\quad\text{for all }\bm{\sigma}\in H(\div,{U}).

For boundary pieces
S\subseteq\Gamma
we define
H^{1}(S)
as the space of all functions
\varphi\in L^{2}(S)
with distributional surface gradient
\nabla_{\Gamma}\varphi\in L^{2}(S)
and equip it with the norm

\lVert\varphi\rVert_{H^{1}({S})}\coloneqq(\lVert\varphi\rVert_{{S}}^{2}+\lVert\nabla_{\Gamma}\varphi\rVert_{{S}}^{2})^{1/2}.

We define the
H^{1/2}({S})
seminorm

\lvert\varphi\rvert_{H^{1/2}({S})}\coloneqq\Bigl(\int_{{S}}\int_{{S}}\frac{\lvert\varphi(x)-\varphi(y)\rvert^{2}}{\lvert x-y\rvert^{d}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\Bigr)^{1/2},

the norm

\lVert\varphi\rVert_{H^{1/2}({S})}\coloneqq(\lVert\varphi\rVert_{{S}}^{2}+\lvert\varphi\rvert_{H^{1/2}({S})}^{2})^{1/2},

and
H^{1/2}({S})\coloneqq\{\varphi\in L^{2}({S})\nonscript\>|\allowbreak\nonscript\>\mathopen{}\lVert\varphi\rVert_{H^{1/2}({S})}<\infty\}
.
We recall that
H^{1/2}(\Gamma)
is the trace space of
H^{1}(\Omega)
.
For
{S}=\Gamma
, we define its dual space

H^{-1/2}(\Gamma)\coloneqq(H^{1/2}(\Gamma))^{*}.

The duality bracket with respect to
H^{-1/2}(\Gamma)\times H^{1/2}(\Gamma)
is formally defined by continuous extension of the usual
L^{2}(\Gamma)
-inner product, i.e.,

\langle\psi\nonscript\>,\allowbreak\nonscript\>\varphi\rangle_{H^{-1/2}(\Gamma)\times H^{1/2}(\Gamma)}=\langle\psi\nonscript\>,\allowbreak\nonscript\>\varphi\rangle_{\Gamma}\quad\text{for all }\psi\in L^{2}(\Gamma)\text{ and }\varphi\in H^{1/2}(\Gamma).

To abbreviate notation, we will simply write
\langle\cdot\nonscript\>,\allowbreak\nonscript\>\cdot\rangle_{\Gamma}
if it is clear from the context.
We note that every
\bm{\sigma}\in H(\div,\Omega)
admits a well-defined normal trace
\bm{\sigma}|_{\Gamma}\cdot\bm{n}_{\Gamma}\in H^{-1/2}(\Gamma)
.
In fact, given
g\in H^{1/2}(\Gamma)
and
w\in H^{1}(\Omega)
with
w|_{\Gamma}=g
, there holds

(4)

\langle\bm{\sigma}|_{\Gamma}\cdot\bm{n}_{\Gamma}\nonscript\>,\allowbreak\nonscript\>g\rangle_{\Gamma}=\langle\bm{\sigma}\nonscript\>,\allowbreak\nonscript\>\nabla w\rangle_{\Omega}+\langle\div\bm{\sigma}\nonscript\>,\allowbreak\nonscript\>w\rangle_{\Omega}.

It is well-known that the above definition is independent of the choice of
w
.

Considering the unbounded domain
\Omega^{\mathrm{ext}}
, we introduce the weight function
\rho\colon\Omega^{\mathrm{ext}}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
defined by

\rho(x)\coloneqq\begin{cases}(1+|x|)^{-1}(1+\log(1+|x|))^{-1}&\text{for }d=2,\\
(1+|x|)^{-1}&\text{for }d=3.\end{cases}

With this, we define

(5)

H^{1}_{\rho}(\Omega^{\mathrm{ext}})\coloneqq\{v\colon\Omega^{\mathrm{ext}}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\nonscript\>|\allowbreak\nonscript\>\mathopen{}\rho v\in L^{2}(\Omega^{\mathrm{ext}}),\nabla v\in L^{2}(\Omega^{\mathrm{ext}})\}

as well as

(6)

H^{1}_{\rho}(\Delta,\Omega^{\mathrm{ext}})\coloneqq\{v\in{H_{\rho}}^{1}(\Omega^{\mathrm{ext}})\nonscript\>|\allowbreak\nonscript\>\mathopen{}\Delta v=0\}

with the corresponding norms

\lVert v\rVert_{H^{1}_{\rho}(\Delta,\Omega^{\mathrm{ext}})}\coloneqq\lVert v\rVert_{H^{1}_{\rho}(\Omega^{\mathrm{ext}})}\coloneqq(\lVert\rho v\rVert_{\Omega^{\mathrm{ext}}}^{2}+\lVert\nabla v\rVert_{\Omega^{\mathrm{ext}}}^{2})^{1/2}.

Finally, we define the solution space for the transmission problem (
1
) as

\mathcal{H}\coloneqq\{v\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\Gamma\to\mathbb{R}\nonscript\>|\allowbreak\nonscript\>\mathopen{}v|_{\Omega}\in H^{1}(\Omega)\text{ and }v|_{\Omega^{\mathrm{ext}}}\in H^{1}_{\rho}(\Delta,\Omega^{\mathrm{ext}})\}

with the associated norm

\lVert v\rVert_{\mathcal{H}}\coloneqq\lVert v|_{\Omega}\rVert_{H^{1}(\Omega)}+\lVert v|_{\Omega^{\mathrm{ext}}}\rVert_{H^{1}_{\rho}(\Delta,\Omega^{\mathrm{ext}})}.

2.3.
Boundary integral operators

We define the usual interior and exterior trace operators, i.e.,

\begin{split}\gamma^{\mathrm{int}}_{0}&\colon H^{1}(\Omega)\to H^{1/2}(\Gamma),\qquad\,{\gamma^{\mathrm{int}}_{0}v\coloneqq v|_{\Gamma}},\\
\gamma^{\mathrm{ext}}_{0}&\colon H^{1}_{\rho}(\Omega^{\mathrm{ext}})\to H^{1/2}(\Gamma),\quad{\gamma^{\mathrm{ext}}_{0}v\coloneqq v|_{\Gamma}},\end{split}

as well as the exterior conormal derivative

\begin{split}\gamma^{\mathrm{ext}}_{1}&\colon H^{1}_{\rho}(\Delta,\Omega^{\mathrm{ext}})\to H^{-1/2}(\Gamma)\end{split}

defined via Green’s theorem
\colon
For
g\in H^{1/2}(\Gamma)
and
w\in H^{1}_{\rho}(\Omega^{\mathrm{ext}})
with
\gamma^{\mathrm{ext}}_{0}w=g
, we set

(7)

\langle\gamma^{\mathrm{ext}}_{1}{v}\nonscript\>,\allowbreak\nonscript\>g\rangle_{\Gamma}\coloneqq-\langle\nabla{v}\nonscript\>,\allowbreak\nonscript\>\nabla w\rangle_{\Omega}.

In particular, there holds

(8)

\lVert\nabla v\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega^{\mathrm{ext}})}^{2}=-\langle\gamma^{\mathrm{ext}}_{1}v\nonscript\>,\allowbreak\nonscript\>\gamma^{\mathrm{ext}}_{0}v\rangle_{\Gamma}.

We note that for sufficiently smooth functions
v
, the conormal derivative coincides with the usual normal derivative on the boundary, i.e.,
\gamma^{\mathrm{ext}}_{1}{v}=-\nabla{v}\cdot\bm{n}_{\Gamma}
.
We use the convention that for
v\in\mathcal{H}
, the traces
\gamma^{\mathrm{int}}_{0}v
and
\gamma^{\mathrm{ext}}_{0}v
as well as the conormal derivative
\gamma^{\mathrm{ext}}_{1}v
are defined by restriction to the respective domains, e.g.,
{\gamma^{\mathrm{ext}}_{0}}v\coloneqq{\gamma^{\mathrm{ext}}_{0}}(v|_{\Omega^{\mathrm{ext}}})
, and note that
\gamma^{\mathrm{int}}_{0}v\neq\gamma^{\mathrm{ext}}_{0}v
in general.

Let
G
be the fundamental solution of the Laplace equation, i.e.,

G(x)\coloneqq\begin{cases}-\frac{1}{2\pi}\log(|x|)&\text{for }d=2,\\
\frac{1}{4\pi|x|}&\text{for }d=3.\end{cases}

Define the single-layer potential

\widetilde{V}\colon H^{-1/2}(\Gamma)\to\mathcal{H},\quad(\widetilde{V}\phi)(x)\coloneqq\int_{\Gamma}G(x-y)\phi(y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y

as well as the double-layer potential

\widetilde{K}\colon H^{1/2}(\Gamma)\to\mathcal{H},\quad(\widetilde{K}g)(x)\coloneqq\int_{\Gamma}(\nabla_{y}G(x-y)\cdot\bm{n}_{\Gamma})g(y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y.

Furthermore, define the usual boundary integral operators used in the context of the boundary element method via

(9)

\begin{split}V\colon H^{-1/2}(\Gamma)\rightarrow H^{1/2}(\Gamma),\quad(V\phi)(x)&\coloneqq\int_{\Gamma}G(x-y)\phi(y)\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y,\\
W\colon H^{1/2}(\Gamma)\rightarrow H^{1/2}(\Gamma),\quad(Wg)(x)&\coloneqq-\nabla_{x}\Bigl(\int_{\Gamma}(\nabla_{y}G(x-y)\cdot\bm{n}_{\Gamma})g(y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\Bigr)\cdot\bm{n}_{\Gamma},\\
K\colon H^{1/2}(\Gamma)\rightarrow H^{1/2}(\Gamma),\quad(Kg)(x)&\coloneqq\int_{\Gamma}(\nabla_{y}G(x-y)\cdot\bm{n}_{\Gamma})g(y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y,\\
K^{\prime}\colon H^{1/2}(\Gamma)\rightarrow H^{1/2}(\Gamma),\quad(K^{\prime}\phi)(x)&\coloneqq\nabla_{x}\Bigl(\int_{\Gamma}G(x-y)\phi(y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\Bigr)\cdot\bm{n}_{\Gamma},\end{split}

where the integral representations are understood in the formal sense; see, e.g.,
[
McL00
, Section 7]
for details.
We stress that the operators above are well-defined, linear, and continuous between the respective spaces.
Moreover,
V
is elliptic provided
\mathrm{diam}(\Omega)<1
for
d=2
; see
[
McL00
, Theorem 7.6]
.
We have the following elementary relations between the integral operators
\colon

(10)

\begin{split}&\gamma_{0}^{\mathrm{int}}\widetilde{V}{=V}=\gamma_{0}^{\mathrm{ext}}\widetilde{V},\quad\gamma_{1}^{\mathrm{ext}}\widetilde{K}=-W,\\
\gamma_{0}^{\mathrm{int}}\widetilde{K}=K&-1/2,\quad\gamma_{0}^{\mathrm{ext}}\widetilde{K}=K+1/2,\quad\gamma_{1}^{\mathrm{ext}}\widetilde{V}=K^{\prime}-1/2;\end{split}

see, e.g.,
[
McL00
, Section 6]
.
Lastly, we introduce the interior and exterior Poincaré–Steklov operators by

(11)

\begin{split}S^{\mathrm{int}}\coloneqq V^{-1}(K+1/2),\\
S^{\mathrm{ext}}\coloneqq V^{-1}(K-1/2),\end{split}

respectively. It is well-known that for
w\in H^{1}(\Omega)
with
\Delta w=0
in
\Omega
, there holds
S^{\mathrm{int}}(w|_{\Gamma})=\nabla w|_{\Gamma}\cdot\bm{n}_{\Gamma}
, i.e.,
S^{\mathrm{int}}
is the Dirichlet-to-Neumann map for the interior problem.

S^{\mathrm{ext}}
has an analogous property in
\Omega^{\mathrm{ext}}
; see, e.g.,
[
McL00
, Section 7]
.
Moreover,
S^{\mathrm{int}}
and
-S^{\mathrm{ext}}
are continuous and elliptic, i.e., there exist constants
c_{\textnormal{PS}}^{\mathrm{int}},C_{\textnormal{PS}}^{\mathrm{int}},c_{\textnormal{PS}}^{\mathrm{ext}},C_{\textnormal{PS}}^{\mathrm{ext}}>0
depending only on
\Gamma
such that

(12)

\begin{split}{c_{\textnormal{PS}}^{\mathrm{int}}}\lVert g\rVert_{H^{1/2}(\Gamma)}^{2}&\leq\langle S^{\mathrm{int}}g\nonscript\>,\allowbreak\nonscript\>g\rangle_{\Gamma}\leq{C_{\textnormal{PS}}^{\mathrm{int}}}\lVert g\rVert_{H^{1/2}(\Gamma)}^{2},\\
{c_{\textnormal{PS}}^{\mathrm{ext}}}\lVert g\rVert_{H^{1/2}(\Gamma)}^{2}&\leq-\langle S^{\mathrm{ext}}g\nonscript\>,\allowbreak\nonscript\>g\rangle_{\Gamma}\leq{C_{\textnormal{PS}}^{\mathrm{ext}}}\lVert g\rVert_{H^{1/2}(\Gamma)}^{2}\quad\text{for all }g\in H^{1/2}(\Gamma);\end{split}

see, e.g.,
[
Ste08
, Lemma 2.1]
.

2.4.
Meshes and discrete spaces

Let
\mathcal{T}_{\ell}
be a conforming triangulation of
\Omega
, i.e., a partition of
\overline{\Omega}
into compact triangles (
d=2
) or tetrahedra (
d=3
), such that the intersection of two different elements is either empty, a common vertex, a common edge, or a common face (for
d=3
).
Throughout this paper, suppose that all considered meshes are obtained by finitely many steps of newest-vertex bisection of some conforming initial mesh
\mathcal{T}_{0}
of
\Omega
.
We refer to
[
Ste08a
,
KPP12
,
DGS25
]
for comprehensive treatments of the newest-vertex bisection routine.
Clearly,
\mathcal{T}_{\ell}
induces a conforming boundary mesh

\mathcal{T}_{\ell}^{\Gamma}\coloneqq\mathcal{T}_{\ell}|_{\Gamma}\coloneqq\{T\cap\Gamma\nonscript\>|\allowbreak\nonscript\>\mathopen{}T\in\mathcal{T}_{\ell}\text{ with }\lvert T\cap\Gamma\rvert>0\}.

It is well-known that newest-vertex bisection guarantees uniform
\kappa
-shape regularity of
\mathcal{T}_{\ell}
and
\mathcal{T}_{\ell}^{\Gamma}
, i.e., there exists
\kappa>0
depending only on
\mathcal{T}_{0}
such that

\sup_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{\ell}}\frac{\mathrm{diam}(T)}{\lvert T\rvert^{1/d}}+\sup_{F\in\mathcal{T}_{\ell}^{\Gamma}}\frac{\mathrm{diam}(F)}{\lvert F\rvert^{1/(d-1)}}+\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}F,F^{\prime}\in\mathcal{T}_{\ell}^{\Gamma}\\
F\cap F^{\prime}\neq\emptyset\end{subarray}}\frac{\mathrm{diam}(F)}{\mathrm{diam}(F^{\prime})}\leq\kappa\quad\text{for all }\mathcal{T}_{\ell}.

We define
\mathcal{N}_{\ell}
as the set of all vertices
z
of
\mathcal{T}_{\ell}
and
\mathcal{N}_{\ell}^{\Gamma}
as the set of all vertices of
\mathcal{T}_{\ell}^{\Gamma}
.
For some set

we define the patch of

as
\mathcal{T}_{\ell}[U]\coloneqq\{T\in\mathcal{T}_{\ell}\nonscript\>|\allowbreak\nonscript\>\mathopen{}T\cap U\neq\emptyset\}
and, recursively, the

\mathcal{T}_{\ell}[\mathcal{T}_{\ell}^{[{k-1}]}[U]]&\text{for }k>1.\end{cases}

Additionally, we define
\Omega_{\ell}^{[k]}[U]\coloneqq\displaystyle{\bigcup\mathcal{T}_{\ell}^{[k]}[U]}\subseteq\overline{\Omega}
.
If
U=\{x\}
is a singleton, we abbreviate

\Omega_{\ell}^{[k]}[x]\coloneqq\Omega_{\ell}^{[k]}[\{x\}]
.
Patches of sets

with respect to the boundary mesh
\mathcal{T}_{\ell}^{\Gamma}
on
\Gamma

\mathcal{T}_{\ell}^{\Gamma}[S]\coloneqq\{F\in\mathcal{T}_{\ell}^{\Gamma}\nonscript\>|\allowbreak\nonscript\>\mathopen{}F\cap S\neq\emptyset\}
,
\Gamma_{\ell}^{[k]}[S]\coloneqq\bigcup\mathcal{T}_{\ell}^{\Gamma,[k]}[S]

Lastly, we define the piecewise constant mesh-width function
h_{\ell}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
by
h_{\ell}|_{T}\coloneqq h_{T}\coloneqq\mathrm{diam}(T)
for all
T\in\mathcal{T}_{\ell}
. The quantities
h_{E}
and
h_{F}
for edges
E
and facets
F
, respectively, are defined accordingly.

For
r\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and a set
U\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we define
\mathbb{P}^{r}(U)
as the space of all polynomials of degree at most
r
on
U
.
Moreover, we define the usual FEM spaces of degree
p\geq 1
with respect to
\mathcal{T}_{\ell}
and
\mathcal{T}_{\ell}^{\Gamma}
by

\begin{split}\mathcal{S}^{p}(\mathcal{T}_{\ell})&\coloneqq\{v\in H^{1}(\Omega)\nonscript\>|\allowbreak\nonscript\>\mathopen{}v|_{T}\in\mathbb{P}^{p}(T)\text{ for all }T\in\mathcal{T}_{\ell}\},\\
\mathcal{S}^{p}(\mathcal{T}_{\ell}^{\Gamma})&\coloneqq\{v\in H^{1}(\Gamma)\nonscript\>|\allowbreak\nonscript\>\mathopen{}v|_{F}\in\mathbb{P}^{p}(F)\text{ for all }F\in\mathcal{T}_{\ell}^{\Gamma}\},\end{split}

as well as

\mathcal{S}^{p}_{0}(\mathcal{T}_{\ell})\coloneqq\{v\in\mathcal{S}^{p}(\mathcal{T}_{\ell})\nonscript\>|\allowbreak\nonscript\>\mathopen{}\gamma_{0}^{\mathrm{int}}v=0\}.

Additionally, we introduce the hat functions
(\zeta_{\ell}^{z})_{z\in\mathcal{N}_{\ell}}\subset\mathcal{S}^{1}(\mathcal{T}_{\ell})
, which are defined by

(13)

\zeta_{\ell}^{z}(z^{\prime})\coloneqq\begin{cases}1&\text{if }z^{\prime}=z,\\
0&\text{if }z^{\prime}\neq z.\end{cases}

We note that the hat functions satisfy

(14)

\sum_{z\in\mathcal{N}_{\ell}\cap T}\zeta_{\ell}^{z}|_{T}=1\quad\text{for all }T\in\mathcal{T}_{\ell}.

An analogous property holds for all facets
F
and edges
E
.
We further define the space of (in general discontinuous) piecewise polynomials of degree
q\geq 0
by

\begin{split}\mathcal{P}^{q}(\mathcal{T}_{\ell})&\coloneqq\{v\in L^{2}(\Omega)\nonscript\>|\allowbreak\nonscript\>\mathopen{}v|_{T}\in\mathbb{P}^{q}(T)\text{ for all }T\in\mathcal{T}_{\ell}\},\\
\mathcal{P}^{q}(\mathcal{T}_{\ell}^{\Gamma})&\coloneqq\{v\in L^{2}(\Gamma)\nonscript\>|\allowbreak\nonscript\>\mathopen{}v|_{F}\in\mathbb{P}^{q}(F)\text{ for all }F\in\mathcal{T}_{\ell}^{\Gamma}\},\end{split}

and the
H(\div,\Omega)
-conforming Brezzi–Douglas–Marini (BDM) space by

\mathcal{B}\mathcal{D}\mathcal{M}^{q}(\mathcal{T}_{\ell})\coloneqq\{\bm{\sigma}\in H(\div,\Omega)\nonscript\>|\allowbreak\nonscript\>\mathopen{}\bm{\sigma}|_{T}\in\mathbb{P}^{q}(T)^{d}\text{ for all }T\in\mathcal{T}_{\ell}\}

as well as its subspace with zero normal-trace on the boundary by

\mathcal{B}\mathcal{D}\mathcal{M}_{0}^{q}(\mathcal{T}_{\ell})\coloneqq\{\bm{\sigma}\in\mathcal{B}\mathcal{D}\mathcal{M}^{q}(\mathcal{T}_{\ell})\nonscript\>|\allowbreak\nonscript\>\mathopen{}\bm{\sigma}\cdot\bm{n}_{\Gamma}=0\text{ on }\Gamma\}.

Discrete spaces with respect to a subset of
\mathcal{T}_{\ell}
are defined accordingly.

Lastly, we define the

-orthogonal projections

(15)

\begin{split}Q_{\ell,q}^{\Omega}&\colon L^{2}(\Omega)\to\mathcal{P}^{q}(\mathcal{T}_{\ell}),\\
Q_{\ell,q}^{\Gamma}&\colon L^{2}(\Gamma)\to\mathcal{P}^{q}(\mathcal{T}_{\ell}^{\Gamma})\end{split}

and note that also
Q_{\ell,q}^{\Omega}|_{T}\colon L^{2}(T)\to\mathbb{P}^{q}(T)
and
Q_{\ell,q}^{\Gamma}|_{F}\colon L^{2}(F)\to\mathbb{P}^{q}(F)
are orthogonal projections for all
T\in\mathcal{T}_{\ell}
and
F\in\mathcal{T}_{\ell}^{\Gamma}
.
In the case
q=0
, we will write
Q_{\ell}^{\Omega}\coloneqq Q_{\ell,0}^{\Omega}
and
Q_{\ell}^{\Gamma}\coloneqq Q_{\ell,0}^{\Gamma}
.
It is well known that

and

satisfy the following approximation properties
\colon

(16)

\begin{split}\lVert(1-Q_{\ell}^{\Omega})v\rVert_{L^{2}(T)}&\leq\frac{h_{T}}{\pi}\lVert\nabla v\rVert_{L^{2}(T)}\quad\text{for all }T\in\mathcal{T}_{\ell}\text{ and }v\in H^{1}(T),\\
\lVert(1-Q_{\ell}^{\Gamma})\gamma^{\mathrm{int}}_{0}v\rVert_{L^{2}(F)}&\leq{C_{\textnormal{N}}}h_{F}^{1/2}\lVert\nabla v\rVert_{L^{2}(T_{F})}\quad\text{for all }F\in\mathcal{T}_{\ell}^{\Gamma}\text{ and }v\in H^{1}(T_{F}),\end{split}

where
T_{F}\in\mathcal{T}_{\ell}
is the unique element such that

and

{C_{\textnormal{N}}\coloneqq C_{d}\Bigl(\frac{h_{T_{F}}^{2}\lvert F\rvert}{h_{F}\lvert T_{F}\rvert}\Bigr)^{1/2}\leq\frac{C_{d}\kappa^{d/2}}{d-1}\quad\text{with }C_{2}\approx 0.88152\text{ and }C_{3}\approx 1.96091;}

see, e.g.,
[
PW60

Nic05
]
.

The symmetric Costabel–Han coupling

We consider the transmission problem (

[

Han90

The latter employs a so-called direct ansatz for the exterior solution

in terms of the double-layer and single-layer potential, i.e.,

for some unknown density
\phi{\coloneqq\gamma^{\mathrm{ext}}_{1}u^{\mathrm{ext}}}\in H^{-1/2}(\Gamma)

Applying
\gamma^{\mathrm{ext}}_{0}
to

(see (
10

(18a)

(1/2-K)\gamma^{\mathrm{int}}_{0}u+V\phi=(1/2-K)g_{\textnormal{D}}.

Applying
\gamma^{\mathrm{ext}}_{1}
to

(see (
10
)) yields

(18b)

W\gamma^{\mathrm{int}}_{0}u+(K^{\prime}-1/2)\phi=Wg_{\textnormal{D}}.

Using (

), (

), and (

\begin{split}&\langle\mathfrak{A}\nabla u\nonscript\>,\allowbreak\nonscript\>\nabla v\rangle_{\Omega}+\langle W\gamma^{\mathrm{int}}_{0}u+(K^{\prime}-1/2)\phi\nonscript\>,\allowbreak\nonscript\>\gamma^{\mathrm{int}}_{0}v\rangle_{\Gamma}\\

for all

Combining this with (

\begin{split}b\bigl((u,\phi),(v,\psi)\bigr)&\coloneqq\langle\mathfrak{A}\nabla u\nonscript\>,\allowbreak\nonscript\>\nabla v\rangle_{\Omega}+{\langle W\gamma^{\mathrm{int}}_{0}u+(K^{\prime}-1/2)\phi\nonscript\>,\allowbreak\nonscript\>\gamma^{\mathrm{int}}_{0}v\rangle_{\Gamma}}\\

and

3.
A posteriori error estimation

In this section, we introduce functional upper bounds for the potential error in the interior as well as in the exterior domain.
Throughout the remainder of this paper, we assume that
g_{D}\in H^{1}(\Gamma)
and
g_{N}\in L^{2}(\Gamma)
.
Let
u_{\ell}\in H^{1}(\Omega)
with
\gamma_{0}^{\mathrm{int}}u_{\ell}\in H^{1}(\Gamma)
and
\phi_{\ell}\in L^{2}(\Gamma)
be arbitrary conforming approximations
u_{\ell}\approx u
and
\phi_{\ell}\approx\phi
.
Additionally, we introduce the approximation

(23)

u^{\mathrm{ext}}_{\ell}\coloneqq\widetilde{K}(\gamma^{\mathrm{int}}_{0}u_{\ell}-g_{\textnormal{D}})-\widetilde{V}\phi_{\ell}\approx u^{\mathrm{ext}}.

We introduce the interior error quantity

\lVert\nabla u-\nabla u_{\ell}\rVert_{\mathfrak{A}}^{2}\coloneqq\langle\mathfrak{A}\nabla u-\mathfrak{A}\nabla u_{\ell}\nonscript\>,\allowbreak\nonscript\>\nabla(u-u_{\ell})\rangle_{\Omega}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathclap{\eqref{eq:lipell}}}}{{\simeq}}\lVert\nabla u-\nabla u_{\ell}\rVert_{\Omega}^{2}.

The full potential error is then quantified by

(24)

\mathcal{E}(u_{\ell},\phi_{\ell})\coloneqq(\lVert\nabla u-\nabla u_{\ell}\rVert_{\mathfrak{A}}^{2}+\lVert\nabla u^{\mathrm{ext}}-\nabla u^{\mathrm{ext}}_{\ell}\rVert_{\Omega^{\mathrm{ext}}}^{2})^{1/2}.

We emphasize that, even for
u_{\ell}
and
\phi_{\ell}
being discrete functions,
u^{\mathrm{ext}}_{\ell}
is not a discrete function and hence lacks basic properties of discrete functions like certain inverse estimates.
However, due to the properties of the layer potentials, we have that
u^{\mathrm{ext}}_{\ell}\in H^{1}_{\rho}(\Delta,\Omega^{\mathrm{ext}})
, i.e.,
\Delta u^{\mathrm{ext}}_{\ell}=0
.
Functional upper bounds are therefore highly suitable for controlling (
24
), as they do not require any
a priori
knowledge of the approximations, but build only on the harmonicity of
u^{\mathrm{ext}}_{\ell}
.

3.1.
Error identity

The main ingredient of the proposed upper bound is the following error identity involving the bilinear form
b(\cdot,\cdot)
emerging from the symmetric coupling (
21
).
The proof uses only Green’s formula for harmonic functions in exterior domains (
8
) and properties of the layer potentials (
10
).

Lemma 3.1
.

Given any conforming approximation
(u_{\ell},\phi_{\ell})\in H^{1}(\Omega)\times H^{-1/2}(\Gamma)
, let

(25)

\begin{split}\widetilde{u}_{\ell}\coloneqq u_{\ell}+\lvert\Omega\rvert^{-1}\langle u-u_{\ell}\nonscript\>,\allowbreak\nonscript\>1\rangle_{\Omega}\quad\text{and}\quad{\widetilde{u}_{\ell}^{\mathrm{ext}}}\coloneqq\widetilde{K}(\gamma^{\mathrm{int}}_{0}\widetilde{u}_{\ell}-g_{\textnormal{D}})-\widetilde{V}\phi_{\ell}.\end{split}

Then, there holds the error identity

(26)

\begin{split}\mathcal{E}(u_{\ell},\phi_{\ell})^{2}=F\bigl(u-\widetilde{u}_{\ell},\gamma^{\mathrm{ext}}_{1}(u^{\mathrm{ext}}-\widetilde{u}_{\ell}^{\mathrm{ext}})\bigr)-b\bigl((\widetilde{u}_{\ell},\phi_{\ell}),(u-\widetilde{u}_{\ell},\gamma^{\mathrm{ext}}_{1}(u^{\mathrm{ext}}-\widetilde{u}_{\ell}^{\mathrm{ext}}))\bigr).\end{split}

Proof 3.2
.

The proof is split into two steps.

Step 1.
We first prove that

(27)

\mathcal{E}(u_{\ell},\phi_{\ell})^{2}=F\bigl(u-u_{\ell},\gamma^{\mathrm{ext}}_{1}(u^{\mathrm{ext}}-u_{\ell}^{\mathrm{ext}})\bigr)-b\bigl((u_{\ell},\phi_{\ell}),(u-u_{\ell},\gamma^{\mathrm{ext}}_{1}(u^{\mathrm{ext}}-u_{\ell}^{\mathrm{ext}}))\bigr),

which proves (
26
) if
(u_{\ell},u_{\ell}^{\mathrm{ext}})=(\widetilde{u}_{\ell},\widetilde{u}_{\ell}^{\mathrm{ext}})
.
Employing (
8
) yields

(28)

\begin{split}\lVert\nabla u^{\mathrm{ext}}-\nabla u^{\mathrm{ext}}_{\ell}\rVert_{\Omega^{\mathrm{ext}}}^{2}&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathclap{\eqref{eq:harmonicnorm}}}}{{=}}-\langle\gamma_{1}^{\mathrm{ext}}(u^{\mathrm{ext}}-u^{\mathrm{ext}}_{\ell})\nonscript\>,\allowbreak\nonscript\>\gamma_{0}^{\mathrm{ext}}(u^{\mathrm{ext}}-u^{\mathrm{ext}}_{\ell})\rangle_{\Gamma}\\
&=\langle\gamma_{1}^{\mathrm{ext}}(u^{\mathrm{ext}}-u^{\mathrm{ext}}_{\ell})\nonscript\>,\allowbreak\nonscript\>\gamma_{0}^{\mathrm{int}}(u-u_{\ell})-\gamma_{0}^{\mathrm{ext}}(u^{\mathrm{ext}}-u^{\mathrm{ext}}_{\ell})\rangle_{\Gamma}\\
&\qquad-\langle\gamma_{1}^{\mathrm{ext}}(u^{\mathrm{ext}}-u^{\mathrm{ext}}_{\ell})\nonscript\>,\allowbreak\nonscript\>\gamma_{0}^{\mathrm{int}}(u-u_{\ell})\rangle_{\Gamma}.\end{split}

The representation formulas (
17
) and (
23
) yield

u^{\mathrm{ext}}-u^{\mathrm{ext}}_{\ell}=\widetilde{K}\gamma^{\mathrm{int}}_{0}(u-u_{\ell})-\widetilde{V}(\phi-\phi_{\ell}).

Together with the integral operator identities (
10
), this yields

\begin{split}\gamma_{0}^{\mathrm{int}}(u-u_{\ell})-\gamma_{0}^{\mathrm{ext}}(u^{\mathrm{ext}}-u^{\mathrm{ext}}_{\ell})&=\gamma^{\mathrm{int}}_{0}(u-u_{\ell})-\gamma^{\mathrm{ext}}_{0}[\widetilde{K}\gamma^{\mathrm{int}}_{0}(u-u_{\ell})-\widetilde{V}(\phi-\phi_{\ell})]\\
&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathclap{\eqref{eq:BIErel}}}}{{=}}\gamma^{\mathrm{int}}_{0}(u-u_{\ell})-(K+1/2)\gamma^{\mathrm{int}}_{0}(u-u_{\ell})+V(\phi-\phi_{\ell})\\
&=(1/2-K)\gamma^{\mathrm{int}}_{0}(u-u_{\ell})+V(\phi-\phi_{\ell})\end{split}

and

\begin{split}\gamma_{1}^{\mathrm{ext}}(u^{\mathrm{ext}}-u^{\mathrm{ext}}_{\ell})&=\gamma^{\mathrm{ext}}_{1}[\widetilde{K}\gamma^{\mathrm{int}}_{0}(u-u_{\ell})-\widetilde{V}(\phi-\phi_{\ell})]\\
&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathclap{\eqref{eq:BIErel}}}}{{=}}-W\gamma_{0}^{\mathrm{int}}(u-u_{\ell})-(K^{\prime}-1/2)(\phi-\phi_{\ell}).\end{split}

Together with (
28
), this leads to

\begin{split}\lVert\nabla u^{\mathrm{ext}}-\nabla u^{\mathrm{ext}}_{\ell}\rVert_{\Omega^{\mathrm{ext}}}^{2}&=\langle\gamma_{1}^{\mathrm{ext}}(u^{\mathrm{ext}}-u^{\mathrm{ext}}_{\ell})\nonscript\>,\allowbreak\nonscript\>(1/2-K)\gamma^{\mathrm{int}}_{0}(u-u_{\ell})+V(\phi-\phi_{\ell})\rangle_{\Gamma}\\
&\qquad+\langle(K^{\prime}-1/2)(\phi-\phi_{\ell})+W\gamma_{0}^{\mathrm{int}}(u-u_{\ell})\nonscript\>,\allowbreak\nonscript\>\gamma_{0}^{\mathrm{int}}(u-u_{\ell})\rangle_{\Gamma}.\end{split}

By (
21
) and
\lVert\nabla u-\nabla u_{\ell}\rVert_{\mathfrak{A}}^{2}=\langle\mathfrak{A}\nabla u-\mathfrak{A}\nabla u_{\ell}\nonscript\>,\allowbreak\nonscript\>\nabla(u-u_{\ell})\rangle_{\Omega}
, we obtain

\begin{split}\mathcal{E}(u_{\ell},\phi_{\ell})^{2}&=\langle\mathfrak{A}\nabla u-\mathfrak{A}\nabla u_{\ell}\nonscript\>,\allowbreak\nonscript\>\nabla(u-u_{\ell})\rangle_{\Omega}+\lVert\nabla u^{\mathrm{ext}}-\nabla u^{\mathrm{ext}}_{\ell}\rVert_{\Omega^{\mathrm{ext}}}^{2}\\
&=b\bigl((u,\phi),(u-u_{\ell},\gamma^{\mathrm{ext}}_{1}(u^{\mathrm{ext}}-u^{\mathrm{ext}}_{\ell}))\bigr)-b\bigl((u_{\ell},\phi_{\ell}),(u-u_{\ell},\gamma^{\mathrm{ext}}_{1}(u^{\mathrm{ext}}-u^{\mathrm{ext}}_{\ell}))\bigr)\\
&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathclap{\eqref{eq:symcoupbil}}}}{{=}}F\bigl(u-u_{\ell},\gamma^{\mathrm{ext}}_{1}(u^{\mathrm{ext}}-u^{\mathrm{ext}}_{\ell})\bigr)-b\bigl((u_{\ell},\phi_{\ell}),(u-u_{\ell},\gamma^{\mathrm{ext}}_{1}(u^{\mathrm{ext}}-u^{\mathrm{ext}}_{\ell}))\bigr).\end{split}

This concludes the proof of (
27
).

Step 2.
We now show (
26
).
Since (
27
) holds for arbitrary approximations
(u_{\ell},\phi_{\ell})\in H^{1}(\Omega)\times H^{-1/2}(\Gamma)
, we may replace
u_{\ell}
by
\widetilde{u}_{\ell}
in (
27
) to obtain that

\begin{split}\mathcal{E}(\widetilde{u}_{\ell},\phi_{\ell})^{2}=F\bigl(u-\widetilde{u}_{\ell},\gamma^{\mathrm{ext}}_{1}(u^{\mathrm{ext}}-\widetilde{u}_{\ell}^{\mathrm{ext}})\bigr)-b\bigl((\widetilde{u}_{\ell},\phi_{\ell}),(u-\widetilde{u}_{\ell},\gamma^{\mathrm{ext}}_{1}(u^{\mathrm{ext}}-\widetilde{u}_{\ell}^{\mathrm{ext}}))\bigr).\end{split}

It remains to show that
\mathcal{E}(u_{\ell},\phi_{\ell})=\mathcal{E}(\widetilde{u}_{\ell},\phi_{\ell})
.
Because of
\nabla\widetilde{u}_{\ell}=\nabla u_{\ell}
, there holds
\lVert\nabla u-\nabla u_{\ell}\rVert_{\mathfrak{A}}=\lVert\nabla u-\nabla\widetilde{u}_{\ell}\rVert_{\mathfrak{A}}
.
Due to (
10
) and
{W1=0=(K+1/2)1}
(see, e.g.,
[
McL00
, Section 8]
), we obtain

\gamma^{\mathrm{ext}}_{0}\widetilde{u}^{\mathrm{ext}}_{\ell}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathclap{\eqref{eq:BIErel}}}}{{=}}(K+1/2)(\gamma^{\mathrm{int}}_{0}\widetilde{u}_{\ell}-g_{\textnormal{D}})-V\phi_{\ell}=(K+1/2)(u_{\ell}-g_{\textnormal{D}})-V\phi_{\ell}=\gamma_{0}^{\mathrm{ext}}u^{\mathrm{ext}}_{\ell}

and, similarly,

\begin{split}\gamma_{1}^{\mathrm{ext}}\widetilde{u}^{\mathrm{ext}}_{\ell}&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathclap{\eqref{eq:BIErel}}}}{{=}}-W(\gamma_{0}^{\mathrm{int}}\widetilde{u}_{\ell}-g_{\textnormal{D}})-(K^{\prime}-1/2)\phi_{\ell}=\gamma_{1}^{\mathrm{ext}}u^{\mathrm{ext}}_{\ell}.\end{split}

This leads to

\begin{split}\lVert\nabla u^{\mathrm{ext}}-\nabla u^{\mathrm{ext}}_{\ell}\rVert_{\Omega^{\mathrm{ext}}}^{2}&=-\langle\gamma_{1}^{\mathrm{ext}}(u^{\mathrm{ext}}-u^{\mathrm{ext}}_{\ell})\nonscript\>,\allowbreak\nonscript\>\gamma_{0}^{\mathrm{ext}}(u^{\mathrm{ext}}-u^{\mathrm{ext}}_{\ell})\rangle_{\Gamma}\\
&=-\langle\gamma_{1}^{\mathrm{ext}}(u^{\mathrm{ext}}-\widetilde{u}^{\mathrm{ext}}_{\ell})\nonscript\>,\allowbreak\nonscript\>\gamma_{0}^{\mathrm{ext}}(u^{\mathrm{ext}}-\widetilde{u}^{\mathrm{ext}}_{\ell})\rangle_{\Gamma}=\lVert\nabla u^{\mathrm{ext}}-\nabla\widetilde{u}^{\mathrm{ext}}_{\ell}\rVert_{\Omega^{\mathrm{ext}}}^{2}.\end{split}

Putting the foregoing considerations together, we are led to

\begin{split}\mathcal{E}(u_{\ell},\phi_{\ell})^{2}&=\lVert\nabla u-\nabla u_{\ell}\rVert_{\mathfrak{A}}^{2}+\lVert\nabla u^{\mathrm{ext}}-\nabla u^{\mathrm{ext}}_{\ell}\rVert_{\Omega^{\mathrm{ext}}}^{2}=\mathcal{E}(\widetilde{u}_{\ell},\phi_{\ell})^{2}\\
&=F\bigl(u-\widetilde{u}_{\ell},\gamma^{\mathrm{ext}}_{1}(u^{\mathrm{ext}}-\widetilde{u}_{\ell}^{\mathrm{ext}})\bigr)-b\bigl((\widetilde{u}_{\ell},\phi_{\ell}),(u-\widetilde{u}_{\ell},\gamma^{\mathrm{ext}}_{1}(u^{\mathrm{ext}}-\widetilde{u}_{\ell}^{\mathrm{ext}}))\bigr).\end{split}

This concludes the proof.

3.2.
Functional upper bound

The following theorem states the main result of this work and provides a guaranteed functional upper bound for the full potential error (
24
).
For given
g_{\textnormal{D}}\in H^{1}(\Gamma)
and
g_{\textnormal{N}}\in L^{2}(\Gamma)
, and arbitrary approximations
(u_{\ell},\phi_{\ell})\in H^{1}(\Omega)\times L^{2}(\Gamma)
with
\gamma^{\mathrm{int}}_{0}u_{\ell}\in H^{1}(\Gamma)
, the upper bound involves the approximate interior normal flux

(29)

\Phi_{\ell}\coloneqq g_{\textnormal{N}}-W(\gamma^{\mathrm{int}}_{0}u_{\ell}-g_{\textnormal{D}})-(K^{\prime}-1/2)\phi_{\ell}\in L^{2}(\Gamma)

and the Dirichlet residual

(30)

G_{\ell}\coloneqq(K-1/2)(\gamma^{\mathrm{int}}_{0}u_{\ell}-g_{\textnormal{D}})-V\phi_{\ell}\in H^{1}(\Gamma).

The existence of computable candidates
\bm{\sigma}_{\ell}\in H(\div,\Omega)
and
w_{\ell}\in H^{1}_{\rho}(\Omega^{\mathrm{ext}})
satisfying the constraints (
31
) in the following theorem is discussed in the subsequent Sections
3.3
–
3.4
.

Theorem 3.3

(Functional upper bound)
.

Let
q^{\prime}\geq 0
,
p^{\prime}\geq 1
be polynomial degrees and
J_{\ell,p^{\prime}}\colon H^{1}(\Gamma)\to\mathcal{S}^{p^{\prime}}(\mathcal{T}_{\ell}^{\Gamma})
be an
H^{1}(\Gamma)
-stable projection.
Then, for any
\bm{\sigma}_{\ell}\in H(\div,\Omega)
and any
w_{\ell}\in H^{1}_{\rho}(\Omega^{\mathrm{ext}})
satisfying

(31a)

\displaystyle\div\bm{\sigma}_{\ell}

\displaystyle=-Q_{\ell,q^{\prime}}^{\Omega}f+c\quad\text{for some }c\in\mathbb{R},

(31b)

\displaystyle\bm{\sigma}_{\ell}\cdot\bm{n}_{\Gamma}

\displaystyle=Q_{\ell,q^{\prime}}^{\Gamma}\Phi_{\ell},

(31c)

\displaystyle\gamma^{\mathrm{ext}}_{0}w_{\ell}

\displaystyle=J_{\ell,p^{\prime}}G_{\ell},

to (

For every

, we extend

and

by zero to functions defined in

The following proposition shows that

is a valid candidate in Theorem

Proposition 3.10
.

The flux
\bm{\sigma}_{\ell}
from (
42

\bm{\sigma}_{\ell}\in\mathcal{B}\mathcal{D}\mathcal{M}^{q^{\prime}+1}(\mathcal{T}_{\ell})\subseteq H(\div,\Omega)
with

(43)

\begin{split}\div\bm{\sigma}_{\ell}=-Q_{\ell,q^{\prime}}^{\Omega}f-\langle p_{\ell}\nonscript\>,\allowbreak\nonscript\>1\rangle_{\Omega}\quad\text{and}\quad\bm{\sigma}_{\ell}\cdot\bm{n}_{\Gamma}=Q_{\ell,q^{\prime}}^{\Gamma}\Phi_{\ell}.\end{split}

Proof 3.11
.

Since
\bm{\sigma}_{\ell}^{z}|_{\partial\omega_{\ell}^{z}\cap\Omega}\cdot\bm{n}_{\partial\omega_{\ell}^{z}}=0
, extension by zero preserves normal continuity of
\bm{\sigma}_{\ell}^{z}
across the patch boundary
\partial\omega_{\ell}^{z}\cap\Omega
.
Thus, we obtain
\bm{\sigma}_{\ell}^{z}\in\mathcal{B}\mathcal{D}\mathcal{M}^{q^{\prime}+1}(\mathcal{T}_{\ell})
and, consequently,
\bm{\sigma}_{\ell}\in\mathcal{B}\mathcal{D}\mathcal{M}^{q^{\prime}+1}(\mathcal{T}_{\ell})\subseteq H(\div,\Omega)
.
Due to
(\zeta_{\ell,z}|_{F})_{z\in F}
being a partition of unity for every

(see (

\begin{split}\bm{\sigma}_{\ell}|_{F}\cdot\bm{n}_{F}&=\sum_{z\in\mathcal{N}_{\ell}\cap F}\bigl(Q_{\ell,q^{\prime}}^{\Gamma}(\zeta_{\ell,z}\Phi_{\ell})\bigr)|_{F}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathclap{\eqref{eq:hat}}}}{{=}}(Q_{\ell,q^{\prime}}^{\Gamma}\Phi_{\ell})|_{F}\quad\text{for all }F\in\mathcal{T}_{\ell}^{\Gamma},\end{split}

which implies
\bm{\sigma}_{\ell}|_{\Gamma}\cdot\bm{n}_{\Gamma}=Q_{\ell,q^{\prime}}^{\Gamma}\Phi_{\ell}
.
This concludes the second equality in (
43

Due to (
42
) and the properties (
14
) of the hat functions, we obtain

\begin{split}\langle\div\bm{\sigma}_{\ell}\nonscript\>,\allowbreak\nonscript\>q_{\ell}\rangle_{\Omega}&=\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{\ell}}\sum_{z\in\mathcal{N}_{\ell}}\langle\div\bm{\sigma}_{\ell}^{z}\nonscript\>,\allowbreak\nonscript\>q_{\ell}\rangle_{T}=\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{\ell}}\sum_{z\in\mathcal{N}_{\ell}}\langle\nabla\zeta_{\ell}^{z}\cdot\mathfrak{A}\nabla u_{\ell}-\zeta_{\ell}^{z}f-\langle p_{\ell}^{z}\nonscript\>,\allowbreak\nonscript\>1\rangle_{\omega_{\ell}^{z}}\nonscript\>,\allowbreak\nonscript\>q_{\ell}\rangle_{T}\\
&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathclap{\eqref{eq:hat}}}}{{=}}\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{\ell}}\Bigl[-\langle f\nonscript\>,\allowbreak\nonscript\>q_{\ell}\rangle_{T}-\big\langle\sum_{z\in\mathcal{N}_{\ell}}\langle p_{\ell}^{z}\nonscript\>,\allowbreak\nonscript\>1\rangle_{\Omega},q_{\ell}\big\rangle_{T}\Bigr]=-\langle f+\langle p_{\ell}\nonscript\>,\allowbreak\nonscript\>1\rangle_{\Omega}\nonscript\>,\allowbreak\nonscript\>q_{\ell}\rangle_{\Omega}\end{split}

for all
q_{\ell}\in\mathcal{P}^{q^{\prime}}(\mathcal{T}_{\ell})
.
Lastly, since
\bm{\sigma}_{\ell}\in\mathcal{B}\mathcal{D}\mathcal{M}^{q^{\prime}+1}(\mathcal{T}_{\ell})

\div\bm{\sigma}_{\ell}\in\mathcal{P}^{q^{\prime}}(\mathcal{T}_{\ell})

Hence, the last identity implies
\div\bm{\sigma}_{\ell}=-Q_{\ell,q^{\prime}}^{\Omega}f-\langle p_{\ell}\nonscript\>,\allowbreak\nonscript\>1\rangle_{\Omega}
.

3.4.
Computation of auxiliary potential
\bm{w_{\ell}\in H^{1}_{\rho}(\Omega^{\mathrm{ext}})}
satisfying

In order to obtain a suitable function

, we suppose that there is a bounded domain

Moreover, we suppose that there is a triangulation

of

Let

and Corollary

from (

4.
Adaptive FEM-BEM algorithm

4.1.
Adaptive algorithm based on Theorem
3.3

In addition to
\Omega
, recall that
\widetilde{\Omega}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\overline{\Omega}=\Omega^{\mathrm{ext}}
is a bounded polygonal Lipschitz domain with
\Gamma=\partial\Omega\cap\partial\widetilde{\Omega}
.
All our numerical experiments start with a conforming simplicial triangulation
\widehat{\mathcal{T}}_{0}
of the overall domain
\widehat{\Omega}\coloneqq\overline{\Omega}\cup\widetilde{\Omega}
, which satisfies

\bigcup\{T\in\widehat{\mathcal{T}}_{0}\nonscript\>|\allowbreak\nonscript\>\mathopen{}T\subseteq\overline{\Omega}\}=\overline{\Omega},

i.e.,
\Omega
and therefore also
\widetilde{\Omega}
and
\Gamma
are fully resolved by the initial mesh
\widehat{\mathcal{T}}_{0}
.
For local mesh-refinement, we employ newest-vertex bisection
[
Ste08a
]
.
Recall the
\widehat{\mathcal{T}}_{\ell}
-induced meshes
\mathcal{T}_{\ell}\coloneqq\widehat{\mathcal{T}}_{\ell}|_{\Omega}
,
\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{\ell}\coloneqq\widehat{\mathcal{T}}_{\ell}|_{\widetilde{\Omega}}
, and
\mathcal{T}_{\ell}^{\Gamma}=\mathcal{T}_{\ell}|_{\Gamma}=\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{\ell}|_{\Gamma}
on
\Gamma
.
We compute
\bm{\sigma}_{\ell}\in\mathcal{B}\mathcal{D}\mathcal{M}^{q^{\prime}+1}(\mathcal{T}_{\ell})
and
w_{\ell}\in H^{1}_{\rho}(\Omega^{\mathrm{ext}})
as outlined in Section
3.3
–
3.4
, respectively.
The
a posteriori
error estimate of Theorem
3.3
gives rise to the (fully computable) local error indicators

(49)

\begin{split}\eta_{\ell}^{\rm{int}}(T)&\coloneqq\lVert\mathfrak{A}u_{\ell}-\bm{\sigma}_{\ell}\rVert_{T}\quad\text{for }T\in\mathcal{T}_{\ell},\\
\widetilde{\eta}_{\ell}^{\rm{ext}}(T)&\coloneqq\lVert\nabla w_{\ell}\rVert_{T}\quad\text{for }T\in\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{\ell},\\
\mathrm{osc}_{\ell}^{\Omega}(T)&\coloneqq h_{T}\lVert(1-Q_{\ell,q^{\prime}}^{\Omega})f\rVert_{T}\quad\text{for }T\in\mathcal{T}_{\ell},\\
\widetilde{\mathrm{osc}}_{\ell}^{\rm{D}}(T)&\coloneqq{\frac{1}{2}}h_{T}^{1/2}\lVert\nabla_{\Gamma}(1-J_{\ell,p^{\prime}})G_{\ell}\rVert_{\partial T\cap\Gamma}\quad\text{for }T\in\mathcal{T}_{\ell}\cup\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{\ell},\\
\widetilde{\mathrm{osc}}_{\ell}^{\rm{N}}(T)&\coloneqq{\frac{1}{2}}h_{T}^{1/2}\lVert(1-Q_{\ell,q^{\prime}}^{\Gamma})\Phi_{\ell}\rVert_{\partial T\cap\Gamma}\quad\text{for }T\in\mathcal{T}_{\ell}\cup\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{\ell}.\end{split}

In addition, we define
\eta_{\ell}^{\rm{int}}(T)\coloneqq\mathrm{osc}_{\ell}^{\Omega}(T)\coloneqq 0
for
T\in\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{\ell}
and
\widetilde{\eta}_{\ell}^{\rm{ext}}(T)\coloneqq 0
for
T\in\mathcal{T}_{\ell}
, as well as

(50)

\begin{split}\widetilde{\eta}_{\ell}(T)^{2}&\coloneqq\eta_{\ell}^{\rm{int}}(T)^{2}+\widetilde{\eta}_{\ell}^{\rm{ext}}(T)^{2}+\mathrm{osc}_{\ell}^{\Omega}(T)^{2}+\widetilde{\mathrm{osc}}_{\ell}^{\rm{D}}(T)^{2}+\widetilde{\mathrm{osc}}_{\ell}^{\rm{N}}(T)^{2}\end{split}

for all
T\in\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{\ell}\cup\mathcal{T}_{\ell}
. Furthermore, we denote by

(51)

\widetilde{\eta}_{\ell}^{2}\coloneqq\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{\ell}\cup\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{\ell}}\widetilde{\eta}_{\ell}(T)^{2}

the full error estimator.
Due to Theorem
3.3
and (
32
), the error estimator
\widetilde{\eta}_{\ell}
is reliable in the sense that

\mathcal{E}(u_{\ell},\phi_{\ell})\leq\widetilde{C}_{\textnormal{rel}}\widetilde{\eta}_{\ell}\quad\text{with }\widetilde{C}_{\textnormal{rel}}\coloneqq\sqrt{5}\max\{C_{\textnormal{mon}}^{-1/2},1,{C_{\textnormal{D}}},\pi^{-1}C_{\textnormal{mon}}^{-1/2},{C_{\textnormal{N}}C_{\textnormal{mon}}^{-1/2}}\}.

Based on the error indicators (
49
) and the Dörfler criterion
[
D“”or96
]
, we propose the following adaptive algorithm, whose performance will be investigated in the subsequent section.

[Adaptive loop]

(iii)

Abstract
. We define two new problems called SIAP and CAP related to solving SIVP and CVP in a subset of lattices called Simultaneous Approximation (SA) lattices. We give dimension- and gap-preserving, deterministic polynomial-time and space reductions from SVP γ , SIVP γ , and CVP γ to their corresponding problems in SA lattices. These reductions show that instances of these problems in SA lattices are just as hard as general instances and thus are interesting new problems to consider for use in cryptography. We also show that the reductions are optimal in regards to integer inflation.



1.
Introduction

Over the past two decades, lattice problems have risen in popularity for cryptographic use.
Lattice-based systems provide several benefits: average to worst case reduction
[
2
]
, accepted quantum and classical security, and homomorphic encryption capability
[
17
]
.
However, naive implementations require large public key sizes. Our goal is to study lattice problems that provide the ability for smaller key sizes for systems based on them.

In 2006, Lyubashevsky and Micciancio introduced ideal lattices into cryptography
[
23
]
.
These lattices provide ability for smaller public key sizes, but have no guarantee that their instances of lattice problems are as hard as general instances.
Campbell et al. outlined an algorithm in
[
11
]
(verified in
[
14
]
) for specific ideal lattices that finds an approximation of the shortest vector by a factor
\exp\big(\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(\sqrt{m})\big)
, where
m
is a parameter of the lattice, while the best known polynomial-time generic lattice algorithms have an approximation factor
\exp\big(\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(m)\big)
.
Other indications that ideal lattices may not be as classically or quantum secure as general lattices can be seen in
[
6
,
8
,
11
,
12
,
13
,
26
,
27
]
and

[
9
,
14
,
16
]
, respectively.

Simultaneous Diophantine Approximation studies the problem of approximating a real vector by a rational vector with entries that have a common denominator.
While Diophantine Approximation problems have been successfully used in cryptanalysis (see
[
1
,
28
]
), minimal research has been done towards basing cryptosystems on them (see
[
3
,
19
]
).
In 1982, Lagarias considered a version of Simultaneous Diophantine Approximation, called the Good Diophantine Approximation problem (GDA), that includes a bound on a solution’s denominator and gave a reduction from GDA to the approximate Shortest Vector Problem (SVP)
[
21
]
.
In 2020, Martin considered the version of Simultaneous Approximation that does not impose any bound on the denominator. He proposed a new class of lattices related to this problem and called them Simultaneous Approximation (SA) lattices
[
24
]
.
An SA lattice of dimension
n
can be described by
n+1
integers rather than the
n^{2}
needed in general.
Solving SVP in these SA lattices can be viewed as solving the Simultaneous Approximation Problem (SAP) (see Section
2
).
Using SA lattices, Martin gave a polynomial-time and space reduction from approximate SVP to SAP. He also gave reductions from GDA to SAP, and vice versa, to show that approximate SVP is equivalent to SAP.
Unfortunately, this does not guarantee that the public key size for an SA lattice-based system will be smaller than for a general-lattice based system of the same security level. It does show that solving SVP in an SA lattice is just as hard as in a general lattice - something ideal lattices cannot claim.

In the next section, we define two new SA lattice problems called SIAP and CAP that can be viewed as solving SIVP and CVP, respectively, in an SA lattice.
We adapt Martin’s algorithm to demonstrate equivalence of these problems with their generic counterparts.
This adaptation remains dimension- and gap-preserving, but significantly decreases integer inflation, which is a critical factor in bounding key sizes needed to maintain security in future SA lattice-based cryptosystems.

More specifically, we prove the following:

Algorithms

2.
Approximating with SA Lattices

Definition 2.1
.

A
Simultaneous Approximation (SA) lattice
of dimension
n
is a lattice generated by the columns of
I_{n}
and a vector
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{Q}^{n}
.

Our goal is to study three classical lattice problems in the restricted setting of SA lattices. For the remainder of the paper, let
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert
be some
\ell_{p}
-norm and
\min^{\times}
represent the minimum excluding 0.
Recall that the approximate Shortest Vector Problem (SVP
γ
) is to find a nonzero lattice vector at most
\gamma
times longer than the shortest possible.
Also recall that the fractional part of a real number
x
is represented as
\{x\}
. For
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{Q}^{n}
, we will take
\{\mathbf{x}\}
to mean the vector of element-wise fractional parts. Consider what it means to solve SVP
γ
in an SA lattice.

Definition 2.2
.

Given
\gamma\in\mathbb{Q}
and
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{Q}^{n}
, a solution to the
approximate Simultaneous Approximation Problem
(SAP
γ
) is an output
b_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
0<\left\lVert\{b_{0}\mathbf{x}\}\right\rVert\leq\gamma\cdot\min^{\times}_{b\in\mathbb{Z}}\left\{\left\lVert\{b\mathbf{x}\}\right\rVert\right\}.
Let
\texttt{SAP\,}(\gamma,\mathbf{x})
denote an oracle for such a problem.

To find a short vector in an SA lattice we need to find
a_{i},b\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
a_{1}\mathbf{e}_{1}+\cdots+a_{n}\mathbf{e}_{n}-b\mathbf{x}\approx 0
where
\mathbf{e}_{i}
is the
i^{\text{th}}
column of
I_{n}
.
This is the same as solving SAP
γ
with an input of
\mathbf{x}
and
b
the output since
a_{1}\mathbf{e}_{1}+\cdots+a_{n}\mathbf{e}_{n}-b\mathbf{x}=\{b\mathbf{x}\}.

Thus, solving SVP
γ
in an SA lattice can be viewed as solving SAP
γ
. This relationship is where these SA lattices get their name.

Recall that the
i^{\text{th}}
successive minimum of a lattice, denoted
\lambda_{i}
, is the smallest radius
r
such that the closed ball
B(0,r)
contains
i
linearly independent lattice vectors.
The approximate Short Independent Vectors Problem (SIVP
γ
) is to find a set of
n
independent lattice vectors that all are at most
\gamma
times longer than
\lambda_{n}
.
The following new problem can be viewed as solving SIVP
γ
in an SA lattice:

Definition 2.3
.

Given
\gamma\in\mathbb{Q}
and
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{Q}^{n}
, a solution to the
approximate Shortest Independent Approximation Problem
(SIAP
γ
) is an output
b_{1},b_{2},\dots,b_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
\{b_{i}\mathbf{x}\}
are linearly independent and
\max_{i}\left\{\left\lVert\{b_{i}\mathbf{x}\}\right\rVert\right\}\leq\gamma\lambda_{n}.

Let
\texttt{SIAP\,}(\gamma,\mathbf{x})
denote an oracle for such a problem.

Recall that the approximate Closest Vector Problem (CVP
γ
) is to find a lattice vector that is within
\gamma
times the shortest distance between a lattice vector and some given target vector.
We define the following new problem that can be viewed as solving CVP
γ
in an SA lattice:

Definition 2.4
.

Given
\gamma\in\mathbb{Q}
,
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{Q}^{n},
and
\mathbf{t}\in\mathbb{Q}^{n}
, a solution to the
approximate Closest Approximation Problem
(CAP
γ
) is an output
b_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
0\leq\left\lVert\{b_{0}\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{t}\}\right\rVert\leq\gamma\min_{b\in\mathbb{Z}}\left\{\left\lVert\{b\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{t}\}\right\rVert\right\}
. Let
\texttt{CAP\,}(\gamma,\mathbf{x},\mathbf{t})
denote an oracle for such a problem.

We first outline an algorithm for approximating a given lattice with an SA lattice.

2.1.
Intuition

Let
\mathcal{L}
be a full-rank lattice such that
\mathcal{L}=M\mathbb{Z}^{n}
for some

Suppose we had an integer

\mathbf{b}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}

\mathbb{Z}^{n}

Then the columns of

Notice that

If we multiply everything by

Say

Note that if one row of

Consider a matrix

Notice that the elements in question correspond to the determinants of submatrices constructed by removing the last column and either the first or second row.
We find the elements of

*&*+a_{2,3}&*&*\\

where, for example,

The following algebraic lemma will be useful.

Lemma 2.3

Let

First notice that

Recall Hadamard’s inequality

For a matrix

This proves vital since we will work with the adjugate matrix whose entries are determinants of

Definition 2.5

For

This means that Jacobsthal’s function gives an upper bound on the perturbation required for two elements to be coprime. Thus, Jacobsthal’s function also gives an upper bound on the number of iterations of the

. In 1978, Iwaniec proved the following important theorem about this function

We have

This asymptotic bound is extremely useful in many contexts, but does not reflect what we expect on average.
In fact, the probability that two random integers

[

Given

\displaystyle=\frac{6}{\pi^{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}i\cdot(1-\frac{6}{\pi^{2}})^{i}.

By taking the derivative of a geometric series and multiplying by

\dfrac{6}{\pi^{2}}\cdot\dfrac{1-\frac{6}{\pi^{2}}}{(\frac{6}{\pi^{2}})^{2}}=\dfrac{\pi^{2}}{6}-1\approx 0.645.

Experimental data shows that

This bound may be possible to improve in the average since it is likely that the perturbations
a_{i,j}
will not often achieve the worst case bound. In fact, experiments show that
a_{i,j}
is 0 about 55% of the time, 1 about 31% of the time, 2 about 10% of the time, and 3 about 9% of the time. On average,
a_{i,j}\approx 0.92
.
Taking the adjugate at the beginning and the inverse at the end both inflate the magnitude of entries by a power of
n
. Without some method of avoiding these steps, even if average case analysis were completed, the bitlength of the entries of the output vector would be at best
\mathcal{O}\big(n^{2}\log(k)\big)
, which is not significantly less than Theorem
2.7
.

2.4.
Gap-Preservation

We start by deriving bounds on the operator norm of a scaled version of
M\tilde{M}.

Definition 2.6
.

For a linear operator
M
, the
operator norm
of
M
, denoted as
\left\lVert M\right\rVert_{\text{op}}
, is equal to
\displaystyle\smash{\max_{\left\lVert\mathbf{u}\right\rVert=1}}\left\lVert M\mathbf{u}\right\rVert
.

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
c\in\mathbb{N}
,
k
and
M
be as in the input of Algorithm
1
, and
\tilde{M}=c\cdot\textup{adj\,}M+A
be as in the output of Algorithm
1
. Then,

1-\frac{kn^{4}\log^{2}(2nk)+kn^{2}\log^{2}(c)}{|c\cdot\det M|}\leq\left\lVert I_{n}+\frac{MA}{c\cdot\det M}\right\rVert\leq 1+\frac{kn^{4}\log^{2}(2nk)+kn^{2}\log^{2}(c)}{|c\cdot\det M|}.

Proof.

Let
\mathbf{u}
be such that
\left\lVert\mathbf{u}\right\rVert=1.
Then

\left\lVert\left(I_{n}+\frac{MA}{c\cdot\det M}\right)\mathbf{u}\right\rVert=\left\lVert I_{n}\mathbf{u}+\frac{MA}{c\cdot\det M}\mathbf{u}\right\rVert\leq 1+\frac{\left\lVert MA\mathbf{u}\right\rVert}{|c\cdot\det M|}.

Lemma
2.6
gives that the entries of
\tilde{M}
are bounded above by
c(2nk)^{n}
. We know that the entries of
A
are the perturbations added to
\tilde{M}
throughout Algorithm
1
and, by Theorem
2.5
, these are bounded above by
\log^{2}(c(2nk)^{n})=n^{2}\log^{2}(2nk)+\log^{2}(c)
.
So, the entries of
MA
are bounded above by
k\big(n^{2}\log^{2}(2nk)+\log^{2}(c)\big)
, because there is at most one nonzero entry in each row of
A
by construction.
Thus,

\left\lVert MA\mathbf{u}\right\rVert\leq n^{2}\max_{i,j}|(MA)_{i,j}|\leq n^{2}k\big(n^{2}\log^{2}(2nk)+\log^{2}(c)\big)

Hence we have

\left\lVert\left(I_{n}+\frac{MA}{c\cdot\det M}\right)\mathbf{u}\right\rVert\leq 1+\frac{kn^{4}\log^{2}(2nk)+kn^{2}\log^{2}(c)}{|c\cdot\det M|}.

Likewise, we see that

1-\frac{kn^{4}\log^{2}(2nk)+kn^{2}\log^{2}(c)}{|c\cdot\det M|}\leq\left\lVert\left(I_{n}+\frac{MA}{c\cdot\det M}\right)\mathbf{u}\right\rVert.\qed

The following proposition gives a general tool for showing gap-preservation of the reductions in Section
3
.
Following the methodology laid out by Martin in Theorem 4.7 of
[
24
]
, we first consider how much multiplication by
M\tilde{M}
can inflate the gap without invalidating the output and then show that the necessary condition is already met. The argument below can be generalized to hold for any
\ell_{p}
-norm by changing the squares and roots to be
p^{\text{th}}
powers and roots appropriately and choosing
c
in terms of
p
. We restrict to the more typical
\ell_{1},\ell_{2},
and
\ell_{\infty}
-norms for clarity.

Proposition 2.9
.

Let
c\in\mathbb{N}
,
k
and
M
be as in the input of Algorithm
1
,
\tilde{M}=c\cdot\textup{adj\,}M+A
be as in the output of Algorithm
1
, and
\gamma=a/b
be such that
a,b\in N
with
k\geq a\geq b
. Also, let
\alpha=t/d\in\mathbb{Q}
be such that
k\geq d
and
\alpha\leq|\det M|
and let
\mathbf{y}\in\mathbb{X}\subset\mathbb{Z}^{n}
. Then,
under the
\ell_{1},\ell_{2},
or
\ell_{\infty}
-norm, if
\left\lVert\mathbf{y}\right\rVert\leq\gamma\left\lVert\mathbf{x}\right\rVert
for all
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{X}
, then
\left\lVert M\tilde{M}\mathbf{y}\right\rVert\leq\gamma\left\lVert M\tilde{M}\mathbf{x}\right\rVert
for all
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{X}.

Proof.

Since
\smash{\left\lVert M\tilde{M}\mathbf{y}\right\rVert^{2}}
is an integer under the
\ell_{1},\ell_{2}
or
\ell_{\infty}
-norm,
\smash{\left\lVert M\tilde{M}\mathbf{y}\right\rVert^{2}}\leq(\alpha a/b)^{2}
is satisfied if
\left\lVert M\tilde{M}\mathbf{y}\right\rVert^{2}<((ad\alpha)^{2}+1)/(bd)^{2}
is satisfied since there are no integers strictly between
((ad\alpha)^{2}+1)/(bd)^{2}
and
(\alpha a/b)^{2}
.
Then, for some
B\in\textup{M}_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
, if

\left\lVert B\mathbf{y}\right\rVert<\gamma\dfrac{\sqrt{a^{2}d^{2}\alpha^{2}+1}}{ad\alpha}\left\lVert B\mathbf{x}\right\rVert

for all
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{X}
then
\left\lVert B\mathbf{y}\right\rVert\leq\gamma\left\lVert B\mathbf{x}\right\rVert
for all
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{X}
.

So we aim to show that

\left\lVert M\tilde{M}\mathbf{y}\right\rVert<\gamma\dfrac{\sqrt{a^{2}d^{2}\alpha^{2}+1}}{ad\alpha}\left\lVert M\tilde{M}\mathbf{x}\right\rVert,

or equivalently,

\frac{\left\lVert M\tilde{M}\mathbf{y}\right\rVert}{\left\lVert M\tilde{M}\mathbf{x}\right\rVert}<\gamma\dfrac{\sqrt{a^{2}d^{2}\alpha^{2}+1}}{ad\alpha}.

The multiplier on the right hand side can be bounded below using the hypothesis that
\alpha\leq|\det M|
to get

\dfrac{\sqrt{a^{2}d^{2}\alpha^{2}+1}}{ad\alpha}\geq\dfrac{\sqrt{a^{2}d^{2}|\det M|^{2}+1}}{ad|\det M|}.

Since scaling will not affect the ratio of the norms on the left hand side, we consider the operator
I_{n}+\frac{MA}{c\cdot\det M}
instead of
M\tilde{M}
.

Applying the bounds from Lemma
2.8
and the hypothesis that
\left\lVert\mathbf{y}\right\rVert\leq\gamma\left\lVert\mathbf{x}\right\rVert
, we get a sufficient condition of

\frac{1+\frac{kn^{4}\log^{2}(2nk)+kn^{2}\log^{2}(c)}{|c\cdot\det M|}}{1-\frac{kn^{4}\log^{2}(2nk)+kn^{2}\log^{2}(c)}{|c\cdot\det M|}}<\dfrac{\sqrt{a^{2}d^{2}|\det M|^{2}+1}}{ad|\det M|}.

Rearranging, we get

\frac{kn^{4}\log^{2}(2nk)+kn^{2}\log^{2}(c)}{|\det M|}\cdot\frac{\sqrt{a^{2}d^{2}|\det M|^{2}+1}+ad|\det M|}{\sqrt{a^{2}d^{2}|\det M|^{2}+1}-ad|\det M|}<c.

Notice that

\frac{\sqrt{a^{2}d^{2}|\det M|^{2}+1}+ad|\det M|}{\sqrt{a^{2}d^{2}|\det M|^{2}+1}-ad|\det M|}<6a^{2}d^{2}|\det M|^{2},

so it is sufficient for

6a^{2}d^{2}|\det M|kn^{4}\log^{2}(2nk)+6a^{2}d^{2}|\det M|kn^{2}\log^{2}(c)<c.

The left hand side can be bounded above using Hadamard’s inequality and the hypotheses that
a,d\leq k
to get

6k^{n+5}n^{n+4}\log^{2}(2nk)+6k^{n+5}n^{n+2}\log^{2}(c)<c.

We see that

6k^{n+5}n^{n+4}\log^{2}(2nk)<c/2\implies 12k^{n+5}n^{n+4}\log^{2}(2nk)<c.

Since
\log^{2}(c)\leq c^{2/3}
for all
c\geq 94
, then
6k^{n+5}n^{n+2}\log^{2}(c)<c/2
if
6k^{n+5}n^{n+2}c^{2/3}<c/2
. Rearranging, we get
1728k^{3n+15}n^{3n+6}<c.

So, it is sufficient for

c>\max\{12k^{n+5}n^{n+4}\log^{2}(2nk),1728k^{3n+15}n^{3n+6},94\}.

But this is satisfied by the choice of
c
in line 1 of Algorithm
1
.
∎

.

Let

Angles between intermediate operator subalgebras
Ved Prakash Gupta, Deepika Sharma

Abstract
. Motivated by [ 2 ] and [ 5 ] , the notions of interior and exterior angles between a pair of compatible intermediate W ∗ W^{*} -subalgebras of an inclusion of W ∗ W^{*} -algebras with a normal conditional expectation with finite probabilistic index are introduced. This is then employed effectively to define the interior angle between a pair of compatible intermediate C ∗ C^{*} -subalgebras of an inclusion of non-unital C ∗ C^{*} -algebras with a conditional expectation with finite Watatani index. It is also shown that the interior angle is stable under the minimal tensor product of unital C ∗ C^{*} -algebras.

47L40, 46L06, 47L65

1.
Introduction

Over the years, based on some significant observations in various
categories - like those of groups, von Neumann algebras and

C^{*}
-algebras - it is a well utilized strategy in mathematics that
the study of sub-objects of a given mathematical object provides a
good understanding of the structure and various properties of the
ambient object. In fact, based on this methodology, some noteworthy
classification results have been achieved in the categories of groups,
von Neumann algebras and
C^{*}
-algebras.

There are various tools and techniques in the world of operator
algebras which assist in the study of subalgebras of operator
algebras. Very recently, the following concepts were added to this
list:

(1)

The notions of interior and exterior angles between
intermediate subfactors of any inclusion of
II_{1}
-factors with
finite Jones index, by Bakshi et al in
[
2
]
. (The existence
of a unique trace on a
II_{1}
-factor and Jones’ notion of ‘basic
construction’ played a fundamental role in the development and
applications of these concepts.)

(2)

The notions of interior and exterior angles between intermediate

C^{*}
-subalgebras of any inclusion of unital
C^{*}
-algebras with
finite Watatani index, by Bakshi and the first named author in

[
5
]
. (Watatani’s theories of finite-index conditional
expectations and reduced
C^{*}
-basic construction (based on the
theory of Hilbert
C^{*}
-modules) were instrumental for these notions
of angles.)

As applications, these notions of angles were used effectively to
obtain bounds for the cardinalities of lattices of intermediate
subalgebras of irreducible finite-index inclusions of (simple)
operator algebras mentioned as above, in
[
2
]
and
[
5
]

itself. These bounds improved earlier known bounds by Longo
(
[
23
]
), thereby answering a question asked by him; and, also
provided a bound for the cardinality of intermediate subfactors of a
finite-index irreducible inclusion of type
III
-factors. (Some
further improvements were achieved in
[
3
]
.)

It was then natural to ask whether these notions of angles could be
extended to intermediate subalgebras of more general (finite-index)
inclusions of
W^{*}
-algebras and of non-unital
C^{*}
-algebras. This
article essentially addresses these questions and, interestingly, it
turns out that the language of Hilbert
W^{*}
-algebras developed by
Paschke plays a key role in answering both questions effectively.

Here is a brief outline of the flow of this article.

This being our first serious interaction with the theory of Hilbert

W^{*}
-modules, we’ve included a relatively longish portion on facts
and concepts related to Hilbert
W^{*}
-modules in
Section

2
.
Another major component of
Section

2
involves a brief review of
the notion of conditional expectations with finite Watatani index in
unital as well as non-unital
C^{*}
-algebras, which was initiated by
Watatani (
[
31
]
) for unital
C^{*}
-algebras and later
generalized to non-unital
C^{*}
-algebras by Izumi (
[
16
]
). (We
anticipate that the exploratory survey presented in
Section

2
makes
this article more self-contained and that it will benefit some
uninitiated readers.) Then, following
[
31
]
and
depending heavily on
[
25
]
and
[
1
]
,

Section

3
reviews some basic aspects of
reduced
W^{*}
-basic construction
of an inclusion of von Neumann
algebras with a normal conditional expectation with finite
probabilistic index, wherein the notion of a
generalized
quasi-basis
for a conditional expectation in
W^{*}
-algebras has
also been introduced (and discussed briefly).

Thereafter, motivated by
[
2
]
and
[
5
]
, the notions of
interior and exterior angles between a pair of compatible intermediate

W^{*}
-subalgebras of an inclusion of
W^{*}
-algebras with a normal
conditional expectation with finite probabilistic index is introduced
in
Section

4
. Some expected generalities are also discussed in
the same section. Finally, in

, employing the

-algebras,
defined in

-subalgebras of an inclusion of (non-unital)

C^{*}
-algebras with a conditional expectation with finite Watatani

C^{*}
-algebras.

Let us first fix notations for the space of intertwiners and for the

-modules:

For any two pre-Hilbert
C^{*}
-modules
\mathcal{X}
and
\mathcal{Y}
over a

-algebra
\mathcal{A}

denote the space of

\mathcal{X}

\mathcal{B}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y}):=\{T\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y}):\,T\text{ is }\mathcal{A}\text{-linear}\},

and, let
\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})

, i.e.,

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\{T:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{Y}:T\text{ is linear and
there exists a linear map }T^{*}:\mathcal{Y}\to\mathcal{X}

\displaystyle\qquad\text{ such

If
\mathcal{X}
and
\mathcal{Y}
are Hilbert

-modules over
\mathcal{A}
, then

\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})
is a closed subspace of the Banach space

\mathcal{B}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})
(with the operator norm) - see, for instance,

[
22
,
9

. It is easily seen that
T^{*}\in\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{Y},\mathcal{X})
,
for every

. Also, for the sake of brevity,
let
\mathcal{B}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{X}):=\mathcal{B}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{X})

\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{X}):=\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{X})

[

]

example of a Hilbert
C^{*}
-module
\mathcal{X}
over a

-algebra
\mathcal{A}

The following fact is elementary - see, for instance,
[

,
\lx@sectionsign 8.1.7

which Watatani (in

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
\mathcal{X}
be a Hilbert
C^{*}
-module over a
C^{*}
-algebra
\mathcal{A}
. Then,
\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{X})
is a

-algebra.

Remark 2.2
.

For any Hilbert
C^{*}
-module
\mathcal{X}
over a
C^{*}
-algebra
\mathcal{A}
, there
are some useful so-called ‘finite-rank’ elements in
\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{X})
given by

\mathcal{X}
itself.

For
x,y\in\mathcal{X}
, consider the (so-called ‘rank-one’) operator

\theta_{x,y}:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{X}
given by
\theta_{x,y}(z)=x\langle y,z\rangle_{{}_{\mathcal{A}}},z\in\mathcal{X}
. Clearly,
\theta_{x,y}\in\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{X})
,
\theta_{x,y}\circ\theta_{w,z}=\theta_{x\langle y,w\rangle_{{}_{\mathcal{A}}},z}
and
(\theta_{x,y})^{*}=\theta_{y,x}
for all

x,y,w,z\in\mathcal{X}
. Further, for any
T\in\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{X})

and any pair
x,y\in\mathcal{X}
,
\theta_{x,y}\circ T=\theta_{x,T^{*}(y)}

and
T\circ\theta_{x,y}=\theta_{T(x),y}
. Thus,

\mathrm{span}\{\theta_{x,y}:x,y\in\mathcal{X}\}
is a two-sided ideal in

\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{X})
and its closure
\mathcal{K}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{X}):=\overline{\mathrm{span}}\{\theta_{x,y}:x,y\in\mathcal{X}\}
is called the
ideal of “compact operators” on the Hilbert
C^{*}
-module
\mathcal{X}
.

For any set
I
, let
\mathcal{F}(I)
denote the set of finite subsets of

I
. Clearly,
\mathcal{F}(I)
is a directed set with respect to set
inclusion. The following useful observation appears, indirectly, in

[
25
, Pages 457-458]
. We shall need it ahead.

Lemma 2.3
.

[
25
]

Let
\mathcal{X}
be a pre-Hilbert
C^{*}
-module over a
W^{*}
-algebra
\mathcal{M}
and

\{x_{i}:i\in I\}
and
\{y_{i}:i\in I\}
be two family in
\mathcal{X}
. If
the net
\left\{\sum_{i\in F}\langle x_{i},y_{i}\rangle_{\mathcal{M}}:F\in\mathcal{F}(I)\right\}
is
norm-bounded in
\mathcal{M}
, then
\sum_{i}\langle x_{i},y_{i}\rangle_{\mathcal{M}}
converges in
the
\sigma
-weak topology in
\mathcal{M}
.

2.2.
Conditional expectations with finite probabilistic index and finite cp-index

Recall (from
[
1
,
12
,
16
]
) the following well-studied notions of
index of a conditional expectation.

Definition 2.11
.

Let
\mathcal{B}\subset\mathcal{A}
be an inclusion of
C^{*}
-algebras and
E:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{B}
be a conditional expectation.

(1)

E
is said to have finite probabilistic index if there exists a
scalar
\gamma\geq 1
such that
\gamma\,E-\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{A}}

is positive
; and, the probabilistic index of such an
E
is
defined as

\mathrm{Ind}_{\mathrm{p}}(E)=\inf\{\gamma\geq 1:\gamma\,E-\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{A}}\text{ is positive}\}.

(2)

E
is said to have finite
\mathrm{cp}
-index if there exists a scalar

\gamma\geq 1
such that
\gamma\,E-\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{A}}
is
completely positive; and, the
\mathrm{cp}
-index of such an
E
is defined
as

\mathrm{Ind}_{\mathrm{cp}}(E)=\inf\{\gamma\geq 1:\gamma\,E-\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{A}}\text{ is completely positive}\}.

For convenience, the notation
\mathrm{Ind}_{\mathrm{p}}(E)<\infty
(resp.,

\mathrm{Ind}_{\mathrm{cp}}(E)<\infty
) shall mean that the conditional
expectation
E
has finite probabilistic index (resp., finite
cp-index). We list some useful facts related to these indices.

Remark 2.12
.

Let
\mathcal{B}\subset\mathcal{A}
be an inclusion of
C^{*}
-algebras and
E:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{B}
be a conditional expectation.

(1)

If
\mathrm{Ind}_{\mathrm{p}}(E)<\infty
, then
E
is faithful.

(2)

\mathrm{Ind}_{\mathrm{p}}(E)<\infty
if and only if
\mathrm{Ind}_{\mathrm{cp}}(E)<\infty
. (
[
12
, Thm. 1]
)

(3)

If
\mathrm{Ind}_{\mathrm{cp}}(E)<\infty
, then
1\leq\mathrm{Ind}_{\mathrm{p}}(E)\leq\mathrm{Ind}_{\mathrm{cp}}(E)
.

(4)

If
\mathcal{B}
has no non-zero finite-dimensional representations,
then
\mathrm{Ind}_{\mathrm{p}}(E)=\mathrm{Ind}_{\mathrm{cp}}(E)
. (
[
16
, Lemma 2.2]
)

For more on the theory of conditional expectations with finite
probabilistic index and finite cp-index, see
[
1
,
12
,
16
]
.

Remark 2.13
.

Interestingly, in some cases, it turns out that the finiteness of
the probabilistic index of a normal conditional expectation is not a
property exclusive to that particular conditional expectation. When

\mathcal{N}
is a factor, it is an intrinsic property of the inclusion
\mathcal{N}\subset\mathcal{M}
. More precisely, either every normal conditional
expectation from
\mathcal{M}
onto
\mathcal{N}
has finite probabilistic index
(equivalently, finite cp-index), or none has - see

[
1
, Cor. 3.20]
.

2.3.
Conditional expectations with finite Watatani index

The third notion of index, which will be central to the theme of
this article, is due to Watatani (
[
31
]
), which was
initially defined for conditional expectations in unital

C^{*}
-algebras and was later generalized by Izumi (
[
16
]
) to
general
C^{*}
-algebras. We briefly recall this notion, for which we
first need to recall Watatani’s theory of
C^{*}
-basic construction
and Haagerup’s theory of operator valued weights.

2.3.1.
Watatani’s theory of basic construction for inclusions
of
C^{*}
-algebras

Let
\mathcal{B}\subset\mathcal{A}

be an inclusion of
C^{*}
-algebras and
E:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{B}
be a faithful
conditional expectation. Then,
\mathcal{A}
becomes a (right) pre-Hilbert

C^{*}
-module over
\mathcal{B}
with respect to the natural right action
(via multiplication from the right) and the
\mathcal{B}
-valued inner
product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\mathcal{B}}
given by

\langle x,y\rangle_{\mathcal{B}}:=E(x^{*}y),x,y\in\mathcal{A}.

Let
\mathfrak{A}
denote the Hilbert
C^{*}
-module completion (over
\mathcal{B}
) of

\mathcal{A}
,
\eta:\mathcal{A}\to\mathfrak{A}
denote the natural inclusion map
(so that,
\langle\eta(x),\eta(y)\rangle_{\mathcal{B}}=\eta(E(x^{*}y))
and

\|\eta(x)\|:=\|E(x^{*}x)^{1/2}\|
for all
x,y\in\mathcal{A}
) and

\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathfrak{A})
denote the space of adjointable maps on

\mathfrak{A}
.

As noted in
Section

2.1
,
\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathfrak{A})
is a closed
subspace of the Banach space
\mathcal{B}_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathfrak{A})
consisting of the

\mathcal{B}
-module maps on
\mathfrak{A}
. In fact, in view of

Proposition

2.1
,
\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathfrak{A})
turns out to be a
unital
C^{*}
-algebra (with the usual operator norm); and, it is easily
seen that there exists a natural
C^{*}
-embedding
\lambda:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathfrak{A})
satisfying
\lambda(a)(\eta(x))=\eta(ax)
for all
a,x\in\mathcal{A}
. Further, there exists a projection

e_{E}\in\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathfrak{A})
(called the Jones projection
associated to
E
) satisfying
e_{E}(\eta(a))=\eta(E(a))
and
e_{E}\lambda(a)e_{E}=\lambda(E(a))e_{E}\text{ for all }a\in\mathcal{A}
- see

[
31
, Lemma 2.1.1(1)]
. (Note that, in literature,
e_{E}
is
denoted more often by
e_{\mathcal{B}}
or by
e_{1}
.) Thus, the closed subspace

C^{*}_{r}\langle\mathcal{A},e_{E}\rangle:=\overline{\mathrm{span}}\{\lambda(x)e_{E}\lambda(y):x,y\in\mathcal{A}\}

turns out to be a
C^{*}
-subalgebra of
\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathfrak{A})
,
and is called the (Watatani’s reduced)
C^{*}
-basic construction of the
inclusion
\mathcal{B}\subset\mathcal{A}
with respect to the conditional
expectation
E
. Notice that
C^{*}_{r}\langle\mathcal{A},e_{E}\rangle=\mathcal{K}_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathfrak{A})
, the ideal of compact operators on

\mathfrak{A}
(see
Remark

2.2
), because
\lambda(x)e_{E}\lambda(y^{*})=\theta_{\eta(x),\eta(y)}
for all
x,y\in\mathcal{A}
. A
priori,
e_{E}
need not belong to
C^{*}_{r}\langle\mathcal{A},e_{E}\rangle
;
however, when
\mathcal{A}
is unital, then
e_{E}=\theta_{\eta(1),\eta(1)}\in C^{*}_{r}\langle\mathcal{A},e_{E}\rangle
. (Note: If multiple conditional
expectations appear simultaneously, then
\eta
(resp.,
\lambda
)
will be denoted by
\eta_{E}
(resp.,
\lambda_{E}
), in order to avoid
any possible confusion.)

Some generalities for inclusions of non-unital
C^{*}
-algebras.

Generalizing Pimsner-Popa’s notion of basis for a conditional
expectation, Watatani (
[
31
]
) initiated an “algebraic” notion of
finite-index conditional expectation in unital
C^{*}
-algebras (see

Definition

2.16
). Later, Izumi pointed out in

[
16
]
that the right approach to generalize Watatani’s notion
of “finite-index” conditional expectation to non-unital

C^{*}
-algebras is by appealing to their enveloping von Neumann

\mathcal{B}

2.3.2.
Quasi-bases, operator valued weights and conditional
expectations with finite Watatani index

Definition 2.16
.

[
31
]

Let
\mathcal{B}\subset\mathcal{A}
be an inclusion of
C^{*}
-algebras and
E:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{B}
be a conditional expectation. A finite set
\{u_{i}:1\leq i\leq n\}
in

is said to be a (right) quasi-basis
for

if
x=\sum_{i}u_{i}E(u_{i}^{*}x)\ (\text{equivalently, $x=\sum_{i}E(xu_{i})u_{i}^{*}$ })\text{ for all }x\in\mathcal{A}.

Remark 2.17
.

(1)

Like the property of having a conditional expectation
with finite probabilistic index (see
Remark

2.13
),
Watatani observed that the property of having a finite quasi-basis is
also an intrinsic property of the inclusion
\mathcal{B}\subset\mathcal{A}
(of
unital
C^{*}
-algebras). More precisely, when
\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{A})
(or
\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{B})

or, equivalently,
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{B})
) is finite-dimensional, then either
no conditional expectation from
\mathcal{A}
onto
\mathcal{B}
has a finite
quasi-basis or every conditional expectation from
\mathcal{A}
onto
\mathcal{B}
has
a finite quasi-basis - see
[
31
, Prop. 2.10.2]
.

(2)

If
\mathcal{B}\subset\mathcal{A}
is an inclusion of
C^{*}
-algebras with

\mathcal{A}
unital and
E:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{B}
is a conditional expectation with
finite quasi-basis, then
\mathcal{B}
is unital,
\mathrm{Ind}_{\mathrm{p}}(E)<\infty
and

E
is faithful - see
[
31
]
.

The following well-known observations will be needed ahead:

Proposition 2.18
.

[
31
,
16
]

Let
\mathcal{B}\subset\mathcal{A}
be an inclusion of
C^{*}
-algebras,
E:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{B}
be a conditional expectation and
\{u_{i}:1\leq i\leq n\}
be a finite set in
\mathcal{A}
.

(1)

If
\{u_{i}:1\leq i\leq n\}
is a quasi-basis for

,
then it is a quasi-basis for the conditional expectation

as well.

In particular,
\mathrm{Ind}_{\mathrm{p}}(E)=\mathrm{Ind}_{\mathrm{p}}(E^{**})<\infty
(and

E
is faithful).

(2)

If

is faithful, then
\{u_{i}:1\leq i\leq n\}
is a
quasi-basis for

if and only if

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda(u_{i})e_{E}\lambda(u_{i})^{*}=\mathrm{id}_{\mathfrak{A}}.

In particular, if
E
has a finite quasi-basis, then

is faithful and

C^{*}_{r}\langle\mathcal{A},e_{E}\rangle=\mathrm{span}\{\lambda(x)e_{E}\lambda(y):x,y\in\mathcal{A}\}=\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathfrak{A}).

Proposition 2.19
.

[
31
, Prop. 2.1.5
\&
Lemma 2.1.6]
Let
\mathcal{B}\subset\mathcal{A}
be an inclusion of
C^{*}
-algebras and
E:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{B}
be a conditional expectation. Consider the following
statements:

(1)

E
has a quasi-basis.

(2)

\mathrm{Ind}_{\mathrm{p}}(E)<\infty
and
\mathrm{id}_{\mathfrak{A}}\in C^{*}_{r}\langle\mathcal{A},e_{E}\rangle
.

(3)

\mathrm{Ind}_{\mathrm{p}}(E)<\infty

Then, (1) and (2) are equivalent and each

\mathcal{A}
(and hence

) is

Remark 2.20
.

Watatani called a conditional expectation with a finite quasi-basis to
be of index-finite type. It turns out that if a conditional
expectation
E:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{B}
has a finite quasi-basis, then
\mathcal{A}
and

\mathcal{B}
must be unital with common identity - see

[
16
, Rem. 2.4 (5)]
(or,
Corollary

2.30
ahead).

The equivalent statements of
Proposition

2.19

are the natural precursors to the generalization of Watatani’s notion
of finite-index index conditional expectations in non-unital

C^{*}
-algebras, which was developed by Izumi (in
[
16
]
) using
Haagerup’s theory of operator valued weights. (This requires some
theoretical background from various references, which we include
concisely for the sake of completeness.)

Operator valued weights:

Recall (from
[
14
,
29
]
)
that, for any
W^{*}
-algebra
\mathcal{M}
,
\widehat{\mathcal{M}}_{+}
denotes the set
of lower semi-continuous maps
m:\mathcal{M}^{+}_{*}\to[0,\infty]
satisfying

m(\lambda\varphi)=\lambda m(\varphi)\text{ and }m(\varphi+\psi)=m(\varphi)+m(\psi)

for all
\varphi,\psi\in\mathcal{M}^{+}_{*}
and
\lambda\geq 0
, where

\mathcal{M}^{+}_{*}
denotes the set of
w^{*}
-continuous positive linear
functionals on
\mathcal{M}
. Clearly,
\mathcal{M}_{+}
can be considered as a subset
of
\widehat{\mathcal{M}}_{+}
(via evaluation).

For any inclusion
\mathcal{N}\subset\mathcal{M}
of
W^{*}
-algebras, an
operator valued weight
from
\mathcal{M}
to
\mathcal{N}
is a map
T:\mathcal{M}_{+}\to\widehat{\mathcal{N}}_{+}
satisfying the following conditions:

(1)

T(cx)=cT(x)
;

(2)

T(x+y)=T(x)+T(y)
; and,

(3)

T(a^{*}xa)=a^{*}T(x)a
,

for all
x,y\in\mathcal{M}_{+}
,
a\in\mathcal{N}
and scalars
c\geq 0
, where
(a^{*}T(x)a)(\varphi):=T(x)(\varphi(a^{*}\cdot a))
for
\varphi\in\mathcal{N}^{+}_{*}
.

An operator valued weight
T
is said to be bounded if
T(\mathcal{M}_{+})\subset\mathcal{N}_{+}
; and, normal if for any increasing limit
x_{i}\nearrow x

in
\mathcal{M}_{+}
,
T(x_{i})\nearrow T(x)
(pointwise) in
\widehat{\mathcal{N}}_{+}
.

Remark 2.21
.

The restriction of any (normal) conditional expectation
E:\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{N}
to
\mathcal{M}_{+}
is a (normal) bounded operator valued weight that maps

1_{\mathcal{M}}
to
1_{\mathcal{N}}
; and, conversely, every (normal) bounded operator
valued weight
T:\mathcal{M}_{+}\to\widehat{\mathcal{N}}_{+}
that maps
1_{\mathcal{M}}
to

1_{\mathcal{N}}
extends naturally to a (normal) conditional expectation from

\mathcal{M}
onto
\mathcal{N}
- see
[
14
,
29
]
.

.

[

[

.

[

,

With notation as in

2.3.3.
Dual conditional expectation

Recall from
[
31
]
that, if
\mathcal{B}\subset\mathcal{A}
is an inclusion
of unital
C^{*}
-algebras, then for any conditional expectation
E:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{B}
with a quasi-basis, then there exists a dual conditional
expectation
E_{1}:C^{*}_{r}\langle\mathcal{A},e_{E}\rangle\to\mathcal{A}
with a quasi-basis
(see
Corollary

2.30
for the statement).

More generally, for any conditional expectation
E:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{B}
with

\mathrm{Ind}_{\mathrm{p}}(E)<\infty
, the bounded normal operator valued weight

\widehat{E^{**}}
(as in
Theorem

2.22
) yields a normal
conditional expectation
\mathrm{Ind}_{W}(E)^{-1}\widehat{E^{**}}:\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{B}^{**}}(\mathcal{A}^{**})\to\mathcal{A}^{**}
. Then, via some very astute
application of the notions of induced representations and of
quasi-equivalence between two representations, Izumi (in
[
16
,
\lx@sectionsign 2
]
) identified the reduced
C^{*}
-basic construction

C^{*}_{r}\langle\mathcal{A},e_{E}\rangle
with a
\sigma
-weakly dense
C^{*}
-subalgebra
of the
W^{*}
-algebra
\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{B}^{**}}(\mathcal{A}^{**})
; and, when

\mathrm{Ind}_{W}(E)<\infty
, it turns out that the map
E_{1}:=\mathrm{Ind}_{W}(E)^{-1}\Big(\widehat{E^{**}}\Big)_{\restriction_{C^{*}_{r}\langle\mathcal{A},e_{E}\rangle}}
is
a conditional expectation from
C^{*}_{r}\langle\mathcal{A},e_{E}\rangle
onto
\mathcal{A}
,
which is called the dual conditional expectation of
E
.

Theorem 2.29
.

[
16
, Thm. 2.8]

Let
\mathcal{B}\subset\mathcal{A}
be an inclusion of
C^{*}
-algebras and
E:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{B}
be a conditional expectation with finite Watatani
index. Then, identifying
\mathcal{A}
with
\lambda_{E}(\mathcal{A})
, there exists a
conditional expectation
E_{1}:C^{*}_{r}\langle\mathcal{A},e_{E}\rangle\to\mathcal{A}
with finite
Watatani index satisfying

E_{1}(xe_{E}y)=\mathrm{Ind}_{W}(E)^{-1}xy\text{ for all }x,y\in\mathcal{A}.

We can thus deduce the following well-known fact (see

Remark

2.20
).

Corollary 2.30
.

Let
\mathcal{B}\subset\mathcal{A}
be an inclusion of
C^{*}
-algebras and
E:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{B}
be a conditional expectation. If
E
has a
finite quasi-basis, then there exists a dual conditional expectation
E_{1}:C^{*}_{r}\langle\mathcal{A},e_{E}\rangle\to\mathcal{A}
with a finite quasi-basis.

In particular,
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
are both
unital with common identity.

Proof.

Since
E
has a quasi-basis, it follows from

Proposition

2.19
that it has finite
Watatani index and that
\mathrm{id}_{\mathfrak{A}}\in C^{*}_{r}\langle\mathcal{A},e_{E}\rangle
; so,
C^{*}_{r}\langle\mathcal{A},e_{E}\rangle
is
a unital
C^{*}
-algebra. Also, by the preceding theorem, there exists
a (dual) conditional expectation
E_{1}:C^{*}_{r}\langle\mathcal{A},e_{E}\rangle\to\mathcal{A}
with finite Watatani index. Thus, it follows from the
Proposition on Page 118 of
[
29
]
that
\mathcal{A}
is unital (in fact,

1_{\mathcal{A}}
:=
E_{1}(\mathrm{id}_{\mathfrak{A}})
works as the identity of

\mathcal{A}
). Since
E_{1}
has finite Watatani index, it follows from

Proposition

2.25
that it has a quasi-basis.
∎

Remark 2.31
.

With notations as in
Theorem

2.29
, iterating the process of

C^{*}
-basic construction and taking dual conditional expectations (as
above), one obtains a tower of

-algebras

where, for

and

is the Jones projection
corresponding to the finite-index (dual) conditional expectation

, with
\mathcal{A}_{-1}:=\mathcal{B}
,
\mathcal{A}_{0}:=\mathcal{A}
,
E_{0}:=E
and
e_{1}:=e_{E}
.

3.1.
Generalized quasi-bases

Recall that, in
[
27
,
\lx@sectionsign
1.1.4]
, Popa
discusses orthonormal bases for normal conditional expectations,
wherein he uses the Bures topology for the convergence of the series
involved in the definition. A well-known and desirable feature of
Popa’s notion was that for a crossed-product von Neumann algebra
\mathcal{M}\rtimes G
(with respect to an action of an infinite discrete group

G
on
\mathcal{M}
), one has
x=\sum_{g\in G}gE(g^{*}x)
in the Bures
topology (and not in the
\sigma
-weak topology) - see

[
24
]
.

However, we noticed that a similar (not necessarily orthogonal) notion
with respect to the
\sigma
-weak topology fits better in the present
context. Given it’s utility, inspired by Watatani, we
call it a ‘generalized quasi-basis’.

Definition 3.11
.

A family
\{m_{j}\}
in
\mathcal{M}
will be called a generalized (right)
quasi-basis for
E
if

x=\sum_{j}^{\sigma\text{-}\mathrm{weak}}m_{j}E(m_{j}^{*}x)\text{ for all }x\in\mathcal{M}.

The following fact is well-known to the experts - see, for instance,

[
17
, Page 223]
. (It is comparable with Item (3) on

[
27
, Page 4]
as well.) Though, we could not trace an explicit
proof of the same in the literature. Thus, for the sake of
completeness, we provide a proof which depends on some useful
observations made in
[
1
]
,
[
25
]
and
[
10
]
.

Proposition 3.12
.

[
17
]

If
\mathrm{Ind}_{\mathrm{p}}(E)<\infty
, then for every collection
\{m_{j}\}
in

\mathcal{M}
such that
\{\eta(m_{j})\}
is an orthonormal basis for

\eta(\mathcal{M})
over
\mathcal{N}
,
\{m_{j}\}
is a generalized quasi-basis for

E
.

In particular,
E
admits a generalized quasi-basis.

Proof.

Let us fix one such orthonormal basis for
\eta(\mathcal{M})
, say,

\{\eta(m_{j}):j\in J\}
(see
Theorem

3.5
for the existence).

Let
x\in\mathcal{M}
. Consider
\mathcal{M}
as a Hilbert
C^{*}
-module over
\mathcal{M}

with respect to the natural
\mathcal{M}
-valued inner product
\langle x,y\rangle_{\mathcal{M}}:=x^{*}y
,
x,y\in\mathcal{M}
. Let
\mathcal{F}(J)
denote the (directed)
set consisting of all finite subsets of
J
. Then, the nets

\Big\{\sum_{j\in S}\langle m_{j}^{*},m_{j}^{*}\rangle_{\mathcal{M}}:S\in\mathcal{F}(J)\Big\}
and

\Big\{\sum_{j\in S}\langle E(m_{j}^{*}x),E(m_{j}^{*}x)\rangle_{\mathcal{M}}:S\in\mathcal{F}(J)\Big\}
are both norm-bounded in
\mathcal{M}
because

\sum_{j\in S}\langle m_{j}^{*},m_{j}^{*}\rangle_{\mathcal{M}}=\sum_{j\in S}m_{j}m_{j}^{*}\leq\sum_{j}^{\sigma\text{-weak}}m_{j}m_{j}^{*}=\hat{E}(1);\text{ and,}

\sum_{j\in S}\langle E(m_{j}^{*}x),E(m_{j}^{*}x)\rangle_{\mathcal{M}}=\sum_{j\in S}E(m_{j}^{*}x)^{*}E(m_{j}^{*}x)\leq\sum_{j\in S}E\left((m_{j}^{*}x)^{*}(m_{j}^{*}x)\right)\leq E(x^{*}\hat{E}(1)x)

for all
S\in\mathcal{F}(J)
, where the second-last inequality follows from
the fact that every conditional expectation is a Schwartz mapping -
see
[
29
,
\lx@sectionsign 9.2
]
.

Thus, it readily follows that the net
\Big\{\sum_{j\in S}\langle m_{j}^{*},E(m_{j}^{*}x)\rangle_{\mathcal{M}}:S\in\mathcal{F}(J)\Big\}
is norm-bounded in
\mathcal{M}

(and is convergent in the
\sigma
-weak topology) - see the discussion
made on Page 458 of
[
25
]
(also see
[
9
,
\lx@sectionsign 8.5.26
]
). We
assert that it converges
\sigma
-weakly to
x
.

Notice that
\eta(x)=\sum^{w^{*}}_{j}\eta(m_{j})\langle\eta(m_{j}),\eta(x)\rangle_{\mathcal{N}}
, where the
w^{*}
-topology comes via the
(Paschke’s) duality mentioned in
Theorem

2.5
. Also, we have

\Big\|\sum_{j\in S}\eta(m_{j})\langle\eta(m_{j}),\eta(x)\rangle_{\mathcal{N}}\Big\|\leq\big\|\sum_{j\in S}m_{j}E(m_{j}^{*}x)\big\|

for all
S\in\mathcal{F}(J)
; thus, the net
\big\{\sum_{j\in S}\eta(m_{j})\langle\eta(m_{j}),\eta(x)\rangle_{\mathcal{N}}\big\}
is norm-bounded in
\eta(\mathcal{M})
; so, by

Theorem

2.5
again,

\varphi\Big(\Big\langle\eta\big(\sum_{j\in S}m_{j}E(m_{j}^{*}x)\big),\eta(y)\Big\rangle_{\mathcal{N}}\Big)\to\varphi(\langle\eta(x),\eta(y)\rangle_{\mathcal{N}})

for all
\varphi\in\mathcal{N}_{*}
and
y\in\mathcal{M}
, which is the same as saying that

(3.1)

\varphi\Big(E\big(\sum_{j\in S}E(x^{*}m_{j})m_{j}^{*}y-x^{*}y\big)\Big)\to 0

for all
\varphi\in\mathcal{N}_{*}
and
y\in\mathcal{M}
.

For every
\varphi\in\mathcal{N}_{*}
and
y\in\mathcal{M}
, let
\varphi_{y}:\mathcal{M}\to\mathbb{C}

be given by
\varphi_{y}(z)=\varphi\big(E(zy)\big)
,
z\in\mathcal{M}
. Then,

\varphi_{y}\in\mathcal{M}_{*}
and it is known that the set
L:=\mathrm{span}\{\varphi_{y}:y\in\mathcal{M},\varphi\in\mathcal{N}_{*}\}
is norm-dense in

\mathcal{M}_{*}
- see
[
10
, Lemma 3.2]
. Thus, (
3.1
) tells us that

(3.2)

\psi\Big(\sum_{j\in S}E(x^{*}m_{j})m_{j}^{*}-x^{*}\Big)\to 0\text{
for all }\psi\in L.

For every
S\in\mathcal{F}(J)
, let
x_{S}:=\sum_{j\in S}E(x^{*}m_{j})m_{j}^{*}\ (=\sum_{j\in S}\langle m_{j}^{*},E(m_{j}^{*}x)\rangle_{\mathcal{M}}^{*})
. From above, the net

\{x_{S}\}
is norm-bounded in
\mathcal{M}
. Hence, by the denseness of
L
in

\mathcal{M}_{*}
, it follows from (
3.2
) that
\theta(x_{S}-x^{*})\to 0
for all
\theta\in\mathcal{M}_{*}
. Thus,
x_{S}\to x^{*}

(
\sigma
-weakly) in
\mathcal{M}
, so that
\sum_{j}^{\sigma\text{-weak}}m_{j}E(m_{j}^{*}x)=x
.
∎

4.

-subalgebras

Having understood some basic aspects of the theory of

-basic

-algebras, analogous to the notion

[

]
) between compatible

-subalgebras of an inclusion of unital

-algebras with a finite-index conditional expectation, we
introduce a similar notion between compatible intermediate

W^{*}
-subalgebras of an inclusion of
W^{*}
-algebras with an
appropriate conditional expectation.

Throughout this section,
\mathcal{N}\subset\mathcal{M}
will denote an inclusion of

W^{*}
-algebras (with common identity) and

will denote
a (faithful) normal conditional expectation with
\mathrm{Ind}_{\mathrm{p}}(E)<\infty
.

4.1.
Compatible intermediate
W^{*}
-subalgebras

Following
[
15
]
, we make the following definition:

Definition 4.1
.

An intermediate
W^{*}
-subalgebra
\mathcal{P}
of the inclusion
\mathcal{N}\subset\mathcal{M}
will be called
E
-compatible if there exists a normal
conditional expectation
F:\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{P}

such that

E_{\restriction_{\mathcal{P}}}\circ F=E
.

Further,
\mathrm{IMS}(\mathcal{N},\mathcal{M},E)

will denote the collection of all
E
-compatible intermediate

W^{*}
-subalgebras of the inclusion
\mathcal{N}\subset\mathcal{M}
.

Remark 4.2
.

Let
\mathcal{P}\in\mathrm{IMS}(\mathcal{N},\mathcal{M},E)
with respect to a
conditional expectation
F:\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{P}
.

(1)

Since
\mathrm{Ind}_{\mathrm{p}}(E)<\infty
, it follows from
[
1
, Prop.
3.14]
that
\mathrm{Ind}_{\mathrm{p}}(F)<\infty
as well.

(2)

Since a conditional expectation with finite probabilistic index is
faithful, it follows readily (as on Page 471 of
[
15
]
) that if

\mathcal{P}\in\mathrm{IMS}(\mathcal{N},\mathcal{M},E)
with respect to two
conditional expectations
F
and
G
, then
F=G
.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
\mathcal{P}\in\mathrm{IMS}(\mathcal{N},\mathcal{M},E)
with respect to the conditional
expectation
F:\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{P}
and
G:=E_{\restriction_{\mathcal{P}}}
. Then, the
following hold:

(1)

There exists a natural unital embedding

\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{P}}(\mathcal{M})\subseteq\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{N}}(\mathcal{M})
via which

\lambda_{F}(x)
is mapped to
\lambda_{E}(x)
for all
x\in\mathcal{M}
. In
particular,
e_{F}\in\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{N}}(\mathcal{M})
and, via this embedding,

e_{F}(\eta_{E}(x))=\eta_{E}(F(x))
for all
x\in\mathcal{M}
.

(2)

e_{F}e_{E}=e_{E}=e_{E}e_{F}
in
\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{N}}(\mathcal{M})
.

(3)

For any orthonormal basis
\{\eta_{G}(w_{l})\}
for the Hilbert

W^{*}
-module
\eta_{G}(\mathcal{P})
over
\mathcal{N}
(as in

Theorem

3.5
),

\sum_{l}^{\sigma\text{-}\mathrm{weak}}\lambda_{E}(w_{l})e_{E}\lambda_{E}(w_{l})^{*}=e_{F}\ (\text{in }\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{N}}(\mathcal{M})).

(4)

E_{1}(e_{F})=\mathrm{Ind}_{0}(E)^{-1}\mathrm{Ind}_{0}(E_{\restriction_{\mathcal{P}}})
, where
E_{1}:\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{N}}(\mathcal{M})\to\mathcal{M}
is the dual conditional expectation of
E
(as in

Theorem

3.7
).

(5)

If
E
has strongly finite index, then every finite orthonormal
basis for the Hilbert
W^{*}
-module
\eta_{E}(\mathcal{M})
(over
\mathcal{N}
) is a
quasi-basis for the conditional expectation
E
.

(6)

If
E_{\restriction_{\mathcal{P}}}
has a finite quasi-basis, say,

\{\mu_{j}\}
, then
\sum_{j}\lambda_{E}(\mu_{j})e_{E}\lambda_{E}(\mu_{j})^{*}=e_{F}\ (\text{in }\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{N}}(\mathcal{M})).

Proof.

(1) and (2) are immediate (see
[
13
, Prop. 2.7]
).

(3): Fix an orthonormal basis
\{\eta_{G}(w_{l})\}
for
\eta_{G}(\mathcal{P})
, as in
the hypothesis. Then,

x=\sum_{l}^{\sigma\text{-weak}}w_{l}E_{\restriction_{\mathcal{P}}}(w_{l}^{*}x)

for all
x\in\mathcal{P}
, by
Proposition

3.12
. Further, for every

a\in\mathcal{M}
and a finite set
S
in the indexing set of
\{w_{l}\}
,
we have

\displaystyle\Big(\sum_{l\in S}\lambda_{E}(w_{l})e_{E}\lambda_{E}(w_{l})^{*}\Big)(\eta_{E}(a))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\eta_{E}\Big(\sum_{l\in S}w_{l}E(w_{l}^{*}a)\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\eta_{E}\Big(\sum_{l\in S}w_{l}(E_{\restriction_{\mathcal{P}}}\circ F)(w_{l}^{*}a)\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\eta_{E}\Big(\sum_{l\in S}w_{l}E_{\restriction_{\mathcal{P}}}(w_{l}^{*}F(a))\Big).

Since
\eta
is (
\sigma
-weak,
w^{*}
)-continuous (see

Remark

(2)), it follows that

5.1.

Recall that for any

\mathcal{P}

-algebras
\mathcal{B}\subset\mathcal{A}
, there is a natural
C^{*}
-embedding
of
\mathcal{B}\otimes^{\min}\mathcal{P}
into
\mathcal{A}\otimes^{\min}\mathcal{P}
and if
E:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{B}
is a
conditional expectation, then
E\otimes^{\min}\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{P}}:\mathcal{A}\otimes^{\min}\mathcal{P}\to\mathcal{B}\otimes^{\min}\mathcal{P}
is also a conditional expectation.

In
[
3
]
, it was proved that for any irreducible
inclusion
\mathcal{B}\subset\mathcal{A}
of simple unital
C^{*}
-algebras with a
conditional expectation
E:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{B}
of finite Watatani index,

\alpha(\mathcal{C}\otimes^{\min}\mathcal{K}(\ell^{2}),\mathcal{D}\otimes^{\min}\mathcal{K}(\ell^{2}))=\alpha(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{D})

for every pair of intermediate
C^{*}
-subalgebras
\mathcal{C}
and

\mathcal{D}
of the inclusion
\mathcal{B}\subset\mathcal{A}
. Interestingly, for unital

C^{*}
-algebras, a more general stability result holds and the proof is
much simpler.

Proposition 5.5
.

Let
\mathcal{B}\subset\mathcal{A}
be an inclusion of unital
C^{*}
-algebras with a
conditional expectation
E:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{B}
of finite Watatani index and

\mathcal{P}
be a fixed unital
C^{*}
-algebra. Then, the following hold:

(1)

The conditional expectation
E\otimes^{\min}\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{P}}:\mathcal{A}\otimes^{\min}\mathcal{P}\to\mathcal{B}\otimes^{\min}\mathcal{P}
has finite Watatani index and
\mathrm{Ind}_{W}(E\otimes^{\min}\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{P}})=\mathrm{Ind}_{W}(E)\otimes 1
.

(2)

If
\mathcal{C}\in\mathrm{IMS}_{W}(\mathcal{B},\mathcal{A},E)
, then
\mathcal{C}\otimes^{\min}\mathcal{P}\in\mathrm{IMS}_{W}(\mathcal{B}\otimes^{\min}\mathcal{P},\mathcal{A}\otimes^{\min}\mathcal{P},E\otimes^{\min}\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{P}})
.

(3)

If
\mathcal{C},\mathcal{D}\in\mathrm{IMS}_{W}(\mathcal{B},\mathcal{A},E)
, then

\alpha_{C^{*}}(\mathcal{C}\otimes^{\min}\mathcal{P},\mathcal{D}\otimes^{\min}\mathcal{P})=\alpha_{C^{*}}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{D}).

Proof.

(1) and (2) follow immediately from the definitions.

(3): Let
(E\otimes\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{P}})_{1}:(\mathcal{A}\otimes^{\min}\mathcal{P})_{1}\to\mathcal{A}\otimes^{\min}\mathcal{P}
denote the dual of the conditional
expectation
E\otimes^{\min}\mathcal{P}
. Then,
(E\otimes\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{P}})_{1}(e_{\mathcal{B}\otimes^{\min}\mathcal{P}})=\mathrm{Ind}_{W}(E\otimes^{\min}1)^{-1}=\mathrm{Ind}_{W}(E)^{-1}\otimes 1
.

Let
\{\mu_{j}\}
and
\{\delta_{k}\}
be finite
quasi-bases for
E_{\restriction_{\mathcal{C}}}
and
E_{\restriction_{\mathcal{D}}}
,
respectively. Since
(E\otimes^{\min}\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{P}})_{\restriction_{\mathcal{C}\otimes^{\min}\mathcal{P}}}=(E_{\restriction_{\mathcal{C}}}\otimes^{\min}\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{P}})
, it
follows that
\{\mu_{j}\otimes 1\}
is a (finite) quasi-basis for
(E\otimes^{\min}\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{P}})_{\restriction_{\mathcal{C}\otimes^{\min}\mathcal{P}}}
. Likewise,

\{\delta_{k}\otimes 1\}
is a (finite) quasi-basis for
(E\otimes^{\min}\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{P}})_{\restriction_{\mathcal{D}\otimes^{\min}\mathcal{P}}}
. Thus, it follows
from
[
13
, Prop. 2.7]
that

e_{\mathcal{C}\otimes^{\min}\mathcal{P}}=\sum_{j}(\mu_{j}\otimes 1)e_{\mathcal{B}\otimes^{\min}\mathcal{P}}(\mu_{j}^{*}\otimes 1)\text{ and }e_{\mathcal{D}\otimes^{\min}\mathcal{P}}=\sum_{k}(\delta_{k}\otimes 1)e_{\mathcal{B}\otimes^{\min}\mathcal{P}}(\delta_{k}^{*}\otimes 1);\text{ so,}

\displaystyle(E\otimes^{\min}\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{P}})_{1}(e_{\mathcal{C}\otimes^{\min}\mathcal{P}}e_{\mathcal{D}\otimes^{\min}\mathcal{P}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(E\otimes^{\min}\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{P}})_{1}\left(\sum_{j,k}(\mu_{j}\otimes 1)e_{\mathcal{B}\otimes^{\min}\mathcal{P}}(\mu_{j}^{*}\delta_{k}\otimes 1)e_{\mathcal{B}\otimes^{\min}\mathcal{P}}(\delta_{k}^{*}\otimes 1)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(E\otimes^{\min}\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{P}})_{1}\left(\sum_{j,k}(\mu_{j}\otimes 1)(E\otimes^{\min}\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{P}})(\mu_{j}^{*}\delta_{k}\otimes 1)e_{\mathcal{B}\otimes^{\min}\mathcal{P}}(\delta_{k}^{*}\otimes 1)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(E\otimes^{\min}\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{P}})_{1}\left(\sum_{j,k}(\mu_{j}\otimes 1)\left(E(\mu_{j}^{*}\delta_{k})\otimes 1\right)e_{\mathcal{B}\otimes^{\min}\mathcal{P}}(\delta_{k}^{*}\otimes 1)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{Ind}_{W}(E\otimes^{\min}\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{P}})^{-1}\left(\sum_{j,k}(\mu_{j}\otimes 1)\left(E(\mu_{j}^{*}\delta_{k})\otimes 1\right)(\delta_{k}^{*}\otimes 1)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(\mathrm{Ind}_{W}(E)^{-1}\otimes 1\big)\left(\sum_{j,k}\mu_{j}E(\mu_{j}^{*}\delta_{k})\delta_{k}^{*}\otimes 1\right),

\displaystyle\left\|e_{\mathcal{C}\otimes^{\min}\mathcal{P}}-e_{\mathcal{B}\otimes^{\min}\mathcal{P}}\right\|_{(\mathcal{A}\otimes^{\min}\mathcal{P})_{1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\|(E\otimes^{\min}\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{P}})_{1}\big((e_{\mathcal{C}\otimes^{\min}\mathcal{P}}-e_{\mathcal{B}\otimes^{\min}\mathcal{P}})^{*}(e_{\mathcal{C}\otimes^{\min}\mathcal{P}}-e_{\mathcal{B}\otimes^{\min}\mathcal{P}})\big)\right\|^{1/2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\|(E\otimes^{\min}\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{P}})_{1}(e_{\mathcal{C}\otimes^{\min}\mathcal{P}}-e_{\mathcal{B}\otimes^{\min}\mathcal{P}})\right\|^{1/2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big\|\left(E\otimes^{\min}\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{P}}\right)_{1}\Big(\sum_{j}(\mu_{j}\otimes 1)e_{\mathcal{B}\otimes^{\min}\mathcal{P}}(\mu_{j}^{*}\otimes 1)-e_{\mathcal{B}\otimes^{\min}\mathcal{P}}\Big)\Big\|^{1/2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big\|\sum_{j}(\mu_{j}\otimes 1)(E\otimes^{\min}\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{P}})_{1}(e_{\mathcal{B}\otimes^{\min}\mathcal{P}})(\mu_{j}^{*}\otimes 1)-(E\otimes^{\min}\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{P}})_{1}(e_{\mathcal{B}\otimes^{\min}\mathcal{P}})\Big\|^{1/2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big\|\sum_{j}(\mu_{j}\otimes 1)(\mu_{j}^{*}\otimes 1)\big(\mathrm{Ind_{W}}(E)^{-1}\otimes 1\big)-\mathrm{Ind_{W}}(E)^{-1}\otimes 1\Big\|^{1/2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big\|\Big(\sum_{j}\mu_{j}\mu_{j}^{*}\otimes 1-1\otimes 1\Big)\big(\mathrm{Ind_{W}}(E)^{-1}\otimes 1\big)\|^{1/2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|\left(\mathrm{Ind_{W}}(E)^{-1}(\mathrm{Ind_{W}(E_{\restriction_{\mathcal{C}}})}-1)\right)\otimes 1\|^{1/2};

and, likewise,

\|e_{\mathcal{D}\otimes^{\min}\mathcal{P}}-e_{\mathcal{B}\otimes^{\min}\mathcal{P}}\|_{(\mathcal{A}\otimes^{\min}\mathcal{P})_{1}}=\|\mathrm{Ind_{W}}(E)^{-1}\left(\mathrm{Ind_{W}(E_{\restriction_{\mathcal{D}}})}-1\right)\otimes 1\|^{1/2}.

Hence,

\displaystyle\cos(\alpha_{C^{*}}(\mathcal{C}\otimes^{\min}\mathcal{P},\mathcal{D}\otimes^{\min}\mathcal{P}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\|\langle e_{\mathcal{C}\otimes^{\min}\mathcal{P}}-e_{\mathcal{B}\otimes^{\min}\mathcal{P}},e_{\mathcal{D}\otimes^{\min}\mathcal{P}}-e_{\mathcal{B}\otimes^{\min}\mathcal{P}}\rangle_{\mathcal{A}\otimes^{\min}\mathcal{P}}\|}{\|e_{\mathcal{C}\otimes^{\min}\mathcal{P}}-e_{\mathcal{B}\otimes^{\min}\mathcal{P}}\|_{\mathcal{A}_{1}\otimes^{\min}\mathcal{P}}\ \|e_{\mathcal{D}\otimes^{\min}\mathcal{P}}-e_{\mathcal{B}\otimes P}\|_{\mathcal{A}_{1}\otimes^{\min}\mathcal{P}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\|(E\otimes^{\min}\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{P}})_{1}(e_{\mathcal{C}\otimes^{\min}\mathcal{P}}e_{\mathcal{D}\otimes^{\min}\mathcal{P}})-(E\otimes\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{P}})_{1}(e_{\mathcal{B}\otimes^{\min}\mathcal{P}})\|}{\|e_{\mathcal{C}\otimes^{\min}\mathcal{P}}-e_{\mathcal{B}\otimes^{\min}\mathcal{P}}\|_{\mathcal{A}_{1}\otimes^{\min}\mathcal{P}}\ \|e_{\mathcal{D}\otimes^{\min}\mathcal{P}}-e_{\mathcal{B}\otimes\mathcal{P}}\|_{\mathcal{A}_{1}\otimes^{\min}\mathcal{P}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\|(\mathrm{Ind_{W}}(E)^{-1}\sum_{j,k}\mu_{j}E(\mu_{j}^{*}\delta_{k})\delta_{k}^{*})\otimes 1-\mathrm{Ind_{W}}(E)^{-1}\otimes 1\|}{\|\mathrm{Ind_{W}}(E)^{-1}\left(\mathrm{Ind_{W}(E_{\restriction_{\mathcal{C}}})}-1\right)\otimes 1\|^{1/2}\|\|\mathrm{Ind_{W}}(E)^{-1}\left(\mathrm{Ind_{W}(E_{\restriction_{\mathcal{D}}})}-1\right)\otimes 1\|^{1/2}\|}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\|(\mathrm{Ind}_{W}(E)^{-1})(\sum_{j,k}\mu_{j}E(\mu_{j}^{*}\delta_{k})\delta_{k}^{*}-1)\|}{\|(\mathrm{Ind}_{W}(E)^{-1})(\mathrm{Ind}_{W}(E_{\restriction_{\mathcal{C}}})-1)\|^{1/2}\|(\mathrm{Ind}_{W}(E)^{-1})(\mathrm{Ind}_{W}(E_{\restriction_{\mathcal{D}}})-1)\|^{1/2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\cos(\alpha_{C^{*}}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{D})),

where the last equality follows from
[
13
, Proposition 3.3]
.
∎

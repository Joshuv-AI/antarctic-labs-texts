On a stochastic phase-field model of cell motility with singular diffusion
Amjad Saef, Wilhelm Stannat

Abstract
We study existence of solutions in the variational sense for a class of stochastic phase-field models describing moving boundary problems. The models consist of stochastic reaction-diffusion equations with singular diffusion forced by a phase-field. We investigate both the case of an independently evolving phase-field and of coupled phase-field evolution driven by a viscous Hamilton-Jacobi equation. Such systems are used in the modelling of single-cell chemotaxis [ 4 ] , where the contour of the cell shape corresponds to a level set of the phase-field. The technical challenge lies in the singularities at zero level sets of the phase-field. For large classes of initial data, we establish global existence of probabilistically weak solutions in L 2 L^{2} -spaces with weights which compensate for the singularities.



keywords
Phase field model, stochastic partial differential equations, singular diffusion, weighted spaces

Introduction

In recent decades, the coupling of phase-field evolution with transport and reaction processes has been used to approximate solutions of free boundary problems originating from diverse subfields of physics and biology, including solidification
[
36
]
, tumor growth
[
37
]
, and cellular migration
[
32
]
. For a non-exhaustive overview of history, applications, and discussions of interpretations and implementations of phase-field methods (also called diffuse interface methods), we refer to
[
10
,
26
]
. Their strength stems from the computational efficiency and theoretical simplicity relative to the complexity of corresponding exact formulations of moving boundary problems. More details on the mathematical connection to moving boundary problems and the mean curvature flow in the deterministic case can be found in the works of
6
, and
30
for a stochastic analogue.

Let nonlocal reaction terms
f
and
g
, a Nemytskii operator
b
, a nonlinearity
\Psi
that is locally Lipschitz in the first two coordinates and globally Lipschitz in the third, and a white-in-time, coloured-in-space Wiener noise
W
be given. In this article, we investigate existence and uniqueness of probabilistically strong, analytically weak solutions of stochastic phase field-models of the form

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}\phi(t,x)=\gamma\Delta\phi(t,x)+g(\phi,c)(t,x)+\Psi(\phi,c,\nabla\phi)(t,x),\\
&\mathrm{d}c(t,x)=\left(D\Delta c(t,x)+\frac{\nabla\phi(t,x)\nabla c(t,x)}{\phi(t,x)}+f(\phi,c)(t,x)\right)\mathrm{d}t+b(\phi,c)(t,x)\mathrm{d}W(t,x)\end{aligned}\right.,

(1.1)

where
t\geq 0
,
x\in\mathbb{T}^{n}
and
\gamma,D>0
. These equations were introduced in the modelling of cell motility by
4
. The authors intended to incorporate the effects of fluctuations and aggregation of microscopic particles diffusing inside a moving boundary to reproduce experimentally observed variability in cell shape. More specifically, the region
\{\phi\geq 0.5\}
outlines the interior of a moving cell, and the variable
c
describes the concentrations of biochemical components inside the cell which drive membrane protrusion dynamics. Central to the phenomenology of these equations is the singular diffusion term

L_{\phi}c\coloneqq\frac{1}{\phi}\nabla\cdot\left(\phi\nabla c\right)=\Delta c+\frac{1}{\phi}\nabla\phi\nabla c

(1.2)

which penalizes diffusion into the outwards normal direction of the transition front of the phase-field
\phi
. One obtains this diffusion term from related phase-field models of cell migration
[
13
,
22
,
8
,
7
]
of the form

\partial_{t}(\phi c)=\nabla\cdot(\phi\nabla c)+\phi f(\phi,c)

by assuming that membrane dynamics happen on a slower time scale than those of the diffusing biochemical components, whence
\,\mathrm{d}(\phi c)\approx\phi\,\mathrm{d}c
.

Another class of examples can be found in evolutionary ecology, where coupled reaction-diffusion systems are used to model population adaptation in heterogeneous or changing environments. The model of Pease, Lande, and Bull
[
25
]
and its variants in
[
18
,
14
,
17
]
describe the joint dynamics of population density and mean trait distribution. These equations share structural similarities with the system considered here, in particular through the presence of logarithmic diffusion terms coupling density and trait evolution. While the stochastic extensions discussed in
[
27
]
fall beyond the present analytical framework due to the fully noise dynamics and singular coefficients, the truncated formulation studied in this article covers several deterministic and partially stochastic cases.

Operators of the type (
1.2
) were first studied as so-called generalised Schrödinger operators
[
3
,
29
]
. These studies were motivated by the connection of the stochastic processes generating such singular diffusions to quantum mechanics and quantum field theory
[
23
]
; a detailed overview of the different mathematical approaches can be found for example in
40
. For
\phi_{t}\equiv\phi
constant, corresponding diffusion processes and (uniqueness) of semigroups generated by maximal extensions of
L_{\phi}
have been studied intensively in weighted spaces
L^{p}(\phi)
on both finite- and infinite-dimensional domains
[
38
,
19
,
34
,
11
]
. Existence and uniqueness of solutions of the associated evolution equation has been extended to the time-dependent case when the evolution of
\phi_{t}
is governed by the complex Schrödinger equation
[
9
,
35
]
.

The objective of the present work is to provide a rigorous analytical framework for time-dependent singular diffusions applied in biophysical modelling. As remarked in
[
28
]
, this type of equation seems to have received limited attention from the mathematical community. Therein, the author studies existence and asymptotic properties of solutions of a Kirkpatrick-Barton model. Another notable work in this direction is
[
21
]
. Through the Cole-Hopf transform
z\coloneqq\log\frac{1}{\phi}
, the model is related to PDEs of the form

\displaystyle\partial_{t}z_{t}

\displaystyle=\Delta z_{t}-|\nabla z_{t}|^{2}

(1.3)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}c_{t}

\displaystyle=\Delta c_{t}-\nabla z_{t}\nabla c_{t}

with
z\geq 0
, for
\gamma,\mu>0
. One observes that solutions of this system are formally scaling invariant under the usual parabolic scaling. This indicates that the nonlinearities present in (
1.3
) are critical and standard parabolic estimates usually fail. Characterising well-posedness for critical nonlinearities and appropriate solution spaces is an active area of research; we note in particular the recent progress in
[
1
]
. For an overview of such approaches, we refer to the recent survey articles
[
39
]
and
[
2
]
, for the deterministic and the stochastic case, respectively. Similar to our work, such approaches often work within time-weighted function spaces adapted to the criticality of the nonlinearity. Our contribution to this research is the introduction of weights which are inhomogeneous in space, where the growth near the initial time is governed by the evolution of the heat flow with suitable initial condition. This framework allows for rather general initial conditions. However, in contrast to the cited works, the question of uniqueness for unbounded initial conditions
z_{0}\coloneqq\log\frac{1}{\phi_{0}}
remains open.

Our work is split into two parts. In the first part, we show existence of martingale solutions of

\,\mathrm{d}c_{t}=\left(\Delta c_{t}+\frac{1}{\phi_{t}}\nabla\phi_{t}\nabla c_{t}+f(\phi_{t},c_{t})\right)\,\mathrm{d}t+b(\phi_{t},c_{t})\,\mathrm{d}W_{t}

(1.4)

when
(\phi_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
is a given process independent of
c
, and therefore call it the uncoupled
case. In the second part we prove existence of martingale solutions of the fully coupled system
(
1.1
). The singularity in (
1.2
) constitutes the main analytical obstacle in
establishing existence of relevant notions of solutions. The reaction terms are
well-behaved, since we restrict our attention to bounded solutions by introducing a
truncation of the stochastic forcing outside a compact set that is left invariant under the
nonlinear dynamics. This greatly simplifies technical considerations and respects
biological plausibility. Then,
to apply the compactness method for martingale solutions to (
1.4
), a natural condition on
\frac{1}{\phi_{t}}\nabla\phi_{t}
is

\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\int\frac{|\nabla\phi_{t}|^{2}}{\phi_{t}^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t\right]<\infty.

(1.5)

In the coupled case this turns out to be equivalent to
\log\phi_{0}\in L^{1}
and

\phi_{0}>0
,
\,\mathrm{d}x
-almost surely.

To relax this condition, we introduce notions of weighted variational solutions in
Definition
3.4
(uncoupled case)
and in Definition
4.3
(fully coupled case). More specifically,
our main results, Theorems
3.6
and
4.8
,
prove existence of martingale solutions to (
1.1
) w.r.t. weighted test
functions

v_{t}=\rho^{2}_{t}u\in H^{1,2}(\mathbb{T}^{n})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
for

u\in H^{1,2}(\mathbb{T}^{n})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
and
\beta=1
in the uncoupled (resp.
\beta=2
in the coupled case). Here the weight
\rho_{t}
satisfies

\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\int\rho^{2}_{t}\frac{|\nabla\phi_{t}|^{2}}{\phi_{t}^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t\right]<\infty.

(1.6)

To specify our requirements on the initial conditions, let

(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
denote an underlying probability space. In the uncoupled
case we need to assume that
0\leq\phi_{t}\leq K_{\phi}
,
L_{c}\leq c_{0}\leq K_{c}
for
all
t\in[0,T]
almost surely, for some constants
K_{\phi}>0
,
L_{c},K_{c}\in\mathbb{R}
depending on the reaction terms
f
and
g
,
and
\nabla\phi_{t}\in L^{2}(\Omega;L^{2}([0,T];L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n})))
, where spatial

L^{p}
-spaces are
understood with respect to the Lebesgue measure. Additionally, we require that
\phi
is absolutely
continuous in a distributional sense with
\partial_{t}\phi\in L^{1}(\Omega;L^{1}([0,T];L^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{n})))
. In contrast to the standard compactness method used for existence
for martingale solutions, our analysis in the uncoupled case requires an additional a-posteriori step
to ensure time continuity of the solution.
In the fully coupled case, we need to assume the same boundedness assumptions on the initial conditions

\phi_{0},c_{0}
and additionally, that
\nabla\phi_{0}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega;L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}))
.
If in addition,
\log\phi_{0}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega;L^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{n}))
, we then obtain martingale
solutions in the classical sense. We note that by our previous comments, this condition is nearly optimal (cf. Corollary
4.5
). We note that even in the deterministic case, our existence
results seem to be new.

Though methodologically similar, the two cases have different scopes. The uncoupled case in principle
allows for functions
\phi_{t}
which vanish on a set of potentially positive measure, which necessitates
the introduction of weak derivatives in a suitable chosen weighted sense. In contrast to this,
the diffusion operator in the fully coupled equation (
1.1
) exhibits a singularity only
at
t=0
: by a parabolic maximum principle,
\phi_{t}
is strictly positive for
t>0
, given any
nonnegative, nonzero initial data
[
5
]
. Due to these regularising effects, we can show that for arbitrary
0\leq\phi_{0}\leq K_{\phi}
,

\alpha\int_{0}^{T}\left\|\frac{\nabla\phi_{t}}{\phi^{1-\alpha}_{t}}\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}t<\infty

holds uniformly in
\alpha\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
. This infinitesimal weakening of condition
(
1.5
) demonstrates that for all
\alpha\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
,
\phi^{\alpha}

is an admissible weight.
With the compactness method applied to infinite sequences of approximating processes,
we can derive existence of a limiting process
(\phi,c)
such that
c
is a weighted
martingale solution for weights of the type
\phi^{\alpha_{n}}
, with
\alpha_{n}\to 0
.
This then implies that the limiting process
c
can be tested against any admissible
weight.

This work is structured as follows: In Section
2
, we introduce relevant notations,
definitions and the mathematical framework of the equations we study. The core of our work begins with Section

3
, which defines and demonstrates existence of weighted martingale solutions of
(
1.4
). To obtain such solutions, we first regularise the equation and solve the tamed
equation using well-established methods. Then, we reweight accordingly to successively relax truncations using a-priori
inequalities and compact embeddings which yield tightness of laws. In the limit, we obtain the desired type of
solution (cf. Theorem
3.6
). In Section
4
, we tackle the
fully coupled system (
1.1
). This requires additional regularity results
for
\phi
. Our main result, Theorem
4.8
, then proves
existence of weighted martingale solutions to (
1.1
) (cf. Definition
4.3
).
The last section, Section
5
, discusses applications to equations used in the modeling
of biophysical processes.

2
Mathematical Setting, Notations and Main Assumptions

We seek to prove the existence of so-called
variational solutions
of the given equations. Fix a finite time
T>0
, a reflexive, separable Banach space
V
and separable Hilbert spaces
U,H
. Note that all vector spaces in this manuscript are assumed to be real. We say that the spaces

\,\mathrm{d}u_{t}=A(t,u_{t})dt+B(t,u_{t})\,\mathrm{d}W_{t}.

(2.1)

Let

.

Let a filtered probability space

\sigma
-Algebra induced by the corresponding norm.

Definition 2.1

(Strong variational solution)
.

We say that there exists a

of equation (
2.1
) in the Gelfand triple

with initial distribution

if for any
\mathcal{F}_{t}
-adapted cylindrical Wiener process
W_{t}
on

, there exists an

-valued,
\mathcal{F}_{t}
-progressively measurable process
u:[0,T]\times\Omega\to H
with
u_{0}\sim\mu_{u_{0}}
such that
\mathbb{P}
-almost surely,

\langle u_{t},v\rangle_{H}=\langle u_{0},v\rangle_{H}+\int_{0}^{t}{{}_{V^{\ast}}}\langle A(t,u_{s}),v\rangle_{V}\,ds+\left\langle\int_{0}^{t}B(t,u_{s})dW_{s},v\right\rangle_{H},

)

-almost surely,

\langle u_{t},v\rangle_{H}=\langle u_{0},v\rangle_{H}+\int_{0}^{t}{{}_{V^{\ast}}}\langle A^{\prime}(t,u_{s}),v\rangle_{V}\,ds+\left\langle\int_{0}^{t}B^{\prime}(t,u_{s})\,\mathrm{d}W_{s},v\right\rangle_{H},

\bm{L}^{p}(\mathbb{T}^{n})=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{m}L^{p}(\mathbb{T}^{n}),~\bm{H}^{s,p}(\mathbb{T}^{n})=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{m}H^{s,p}(\mathbb{T}^{n})

\bm{u}\in\bm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
.
Generally, we will omit reference to the spatial domain and simply write

\bm{H}^{1,2}_{b}\coloneqq\bm{H}^{1,2}\cap\bm{L}^{\infty}

When we equip these spaces with the canonical inner products on direct sums of Hilbert spaces, these spaces become Hilbert spaces. Unless specified otherwise, the inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
will always denote the inner product on (a direct sum of copies of)

. Even more generally, by
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle

\bm{H}^{1,2}\hookrightarrow\bm{L}^{2}\cong(\bm{L}^{2})^{\ast}\hookrightarrow(\bm{H}^{1,2})^{\ast}

Since we work with systems of equations, we will use the Kronecker product in the particular case

\otimes\colon\mathbb{R}^{1\times n}\times\mathbb{R}^{p\times 1}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{p\times n}

and the operation

\odot\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n\times m}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n\times m},~(\bm{u},\bm{A})\mapsto\mathrm{diag}(u_{1},\dots,u_{n})\cdot\bm{A}

to denote the leftwise product with the diagonal matrix with entries given by
\bm{u}=(u_{1},\dots,u_{n})
. We further define

for

, while for
\bm{g}\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we define

\nabla\bm{g}\coloneqq\bm{J}_{\bm{g}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times n}.

With this notation at hand, we can simply write

\nabla\cdot(f\nabla\bm{g})=f\Delta\bm{g}+\nabla\bm{g}\nabla f,

if we define the divergence rowwise and Laplacian componentwise, i.e.

\Delta\bm{g}\coloneqq(\Delta g^{1},\dots,\Delta g^{d})^{T}\colon\mathbb{T}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d},\nabla\cdot(f\nabla\bm{g})\coloneqq(\nabla\cdot(f\nabla g^{1}),\dots,\nabla\cdot(f\nabla g^{d}))^{T}\colon\mathbb{T}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}.

We seek to establish the existence of solutions to the stochastic reaction-diffusion system

\,\mathrm{d}\bm{c}_{t}=\left(\frac{1}{\phi_{t}}\nabla\cdot(\phi_{t}\bm{D}\nabla\bm{c}_{t})+\bm{f}(\phi_{t},\bm{c}_{t})\right)\,\mathrm{d}t+\bm{b}(\phi_{t},\bm{c}_{t})\,\mathrm{d}\bm{W}_{t}

(2.2)

for Wiener noise with trace class covariance

, possibly coupled to the parabolic equation

Assumption 1
.

We assume that the operator
\bm{b}=(b_{ij})_{1\leq i,j\leq d}
is a matrix of multiplication operators with entries
b_{ij}\colon\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
. We additionally assume that the functions

have locally bounded partial derivatives.

Assumption 2
.

The nonlinearities
g,\bm{f}
should generally correspond to Nemytskii-type operators with dependence on nonlocal properties of inputs. Due to the conditions we impose on solutions and initial conditions, it suffices to specify their behaviour on
L^{\infty}
. Thus, we only assume that for each
R>0
, there exists a constant
L_{R}
such that
g\colon L^{\infty}\oplus\bm{L}^{\infty}\rightarrow L^{\infty},~\bm{f}\colon L^{\infty}\oplus\bm{L}^{\infty}\rightarrow\bm{L}^{\infty}
admit the bounds

\displaystyle|(g(\phi_{1},\bm{c}_{1})-g(\phi_{2},\bm{c}_{2}))(\bm{x})|

\displaystyle\leq L_{R}\left(|\phi_{1}(\bm{x})-\phi_{2}(\bm{x})|+|\phi_{1}(\bm{x})|\cdot\left\|\phi_{1}-\phi_{2}\right\|_{L^{2}}+|\phi_{2}(\bm{x})|\left(|\bm{c}_{1}(\bm{x})-\bm{c}_{2}(\bm{x})|+\left\|\bm{c}_{1}-\bm{c}_{2}\right\|_{\bm{L}^{2}}\right)\right)

and

|(\bm{f}(\phi_{1},\bm{c}_{1})-\bm{f}(\phi_{2},\bm{c}_{2}))(\bm{x})|\leq L_{R}\left(|\phi_{1}(\bm{x})-\phi_{2}(\bm{x})|+|\bm{c}_{1}(\bm{x})-\bm{c}_{2}(\bm{x})|+\left\|\phi_{1}-\phi_{2}\right\|_{L^{2}}+\left\|\bm{c}_{1}-\bm{c}_{2}\right\|_{\bm{L}^{2}}\right)

\!\,\mathrm{d}x
-almost surely whenever
\left\|\phi_{1}\right\|_{L^{\infty}},\left\|\phi_{2}\right\|_{L^{\infty}},\left\|\bm{c}_{1}\right\|_{\bm{L}^{\infty}},\left\|\bm{c}_{2}\right\|_{\bm{L}^{\infty}}\leq R
. Let real numbers
K_{\phi}
and

L_{1},\dots L_{d},K_{1},\dots K_{d}

with
L_{i}<K_{i}
be given and introduce the hypercube
\mathcal{K}=\prod_{i=1}^{d}[L_{i},K_{i}]
and the spaces

\mathcal{X}_{\phi}=\left\{\phi\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}):\phi(\bm{x})\in[0,K_{\phi}],~\,\mathrm{d}x\text{-a.s.}\right\},~\mathcal{X}_{\bm{c}}=\left\{\bm{c}\in\bm{L}^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}):\bm{c}(\bm{x})\in\mathcal{K},~\,\mathrm{d}x\text{-a.s.}\right\}.

We assume that whenever

,

f_{i}(\phi,\bm{c})\mathds{1}_{\left\{u_{i}\equiv L_{i}\right\}}\mathds{1}_{\left\{\bm{c}\in\mathcal{X}_{\bm{c}}\right\}}\geq 0\text{ and }f_{i}(\phi,\bm{c})\mathds{1}_{\left\{u_{i}\equiv K_{i}\right\}}\mathds{1}_{\left\{\bm{c}\in\mathcal{X}_{\bm{c}}\right\}}\leq 0.

Similarly, we assume that for
\bm{c}\in\mathcal{X}_{\bm{c}}
,

g(\phi,\bm{c})\mathds{1}_{\left\{\phi\equiv 0\right\}}\mathds{1}_{\left\{\bm{c}\in\mathcal{X}_{\bm{c}}\right\}}=0\text{ and }g(\phi,\bm{c})\mathds{1}_{\left\{\phi\equiv K_{\phi}\right\}}\mathds{1}_{\left\{\bm{c}\in\mathcal{X}_{\bm{c}}\right\}}=0.

In combination with the Lipschitz property, this in particular implies that
\limsup_{\phi\to 0}\frac{|g(\phi,\bm{c})|}{|\phi|}<\infty
, where the limit
\phi\to 0
is taken in
L^{\infty}
.

Assumption 3
.

The nonlinearity

is of the form

\Psi(\phi,\bm{c},\nabla\phi)=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\Psi_{i}(\phi,\bm{c})\cdot\varphi_{i}(\nabla\phi),

for some finite collection of Lipschitz functions

\varphi_{i}\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
and nonlinearities
\Psi_{i}\colon L^{\infty}\oplus\bm{L}^{\infty}\rightarrow L^{\infty}
that satisfy the Lipschitz property

|\left(\Psi_{i}(\phi_{1},\bm{c}_{1})-\Psi_{i}(\phi_{2},\bm{c}_{2})\right)(\bm{x})|\leq L_{R}\left(|\phi_{1}(\bm{x})-\phi_{2}(\bm{x})|+|\bm{c}_{1}(\bm{x})-\bm{c}_{2}(\bm{x})|+\left\|\phi_{1}-\phi_{2}\right\|_{L^{2}}+\left\|\bm{c}_{1}-\bm{c}_{2}\right\|_{\bm{L}^{2}}\right)

\left\|\phi_{1}\right\|_{L^{\infty}},\left\|\phi_{2}\right\|_{L^{\infty}},\left\|\bm{c}_{1}\right\|_{\bm{L}^{\infty}},\left\|\bm{c}_{2}\right\|_{\bm{L}^{\infty}}\leq R
.

3
Solution theory for uncoupled phase-fields

Throughout this section, we let a filtered probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\geq 0},\mathbb{P})
,
\bm{c}_{0}\in\bm{L}^{\infty}(\Omega\times\mathbb{T}^{n})
be
\mathcal{F}_{0}
-measurable, and some

\mathcal{F}_{t}
-progressively measurable
\phi\in L^{2}(\Omega;L^{2}([0,T];H^{1,2}))
be given. Further, for
r>\frac{n}{2}-1
, let a symmetric and positive definite
Q\in L(\bm{L}^{2})
with
\sqrt{Q}\in HS(\bm{H}^{r,2})
be given. We assume that
\phi
is almost surely absolutely continuous in

\bm{c}^{(k)}_{t}\in\mathcal{X}_{\bm{c}}\subset\bm{L}^{\infty}
, conclusion (ii) can be strengthened to weak convergence in
\bm{L}^{2}
for all
t\in[0,T]
,
\mathbb{P}^{\prime}
-almost surely. Denote this limit by
\tilde{\bm{c}}_{t}
. Since
\mathcal{X}_{\bm{c}}
is convex and closed under strong convergence, it is closed under weak convergence, and we find that
\tilde{\bm{c}}_{t}\in\mathcal{X}_{\bm{c}}
for all
t\in[0,T]
, almost surely. Combined with absolute continuity of the right hand side of (
3.6
), uniform boundedness of
\tilde{\bm{c}}_{t}
additionally implies that
\tilde{\bm{c}}_{t}
is weakly continuous. Subsequent application of Lemma
3.13
actually yields strong continuity of
\bm{c}^{\epsilon}\in\bm{L}^{2}
, since
\bm{c}
is weakly continuous and
t\mapsto\left\|\bm{c}^{\epsilon}_{t}\right\|_{\bm{L}^{2}}
is continuous.

Since, on the other hand,
\bm{c}^{(k)}_{t}
converges strongly to
\bm{c}^{\epsilon}_{t}
for
t\notin\mathcal{N}_{\omega}
, we find that
\tilde{\bm{c}}\equiv\bm{c}^{\epsilon}
in
L^{1}([0,T];\bm{L}^{2})
. From hereon, identify
\tilde{\bm{c}}
with
\bm{c}^{\epsilon}
. Altogether, this then shows that
\bm{c}^{\epsilon}
satisfies equation (
3.6
) for all
t\in[0,T]
.

We now demonstrate the claimed properties of
M
.

(Adaptedness)

By rearranging (
3.6
), we can identify
(\langle M_{t},\bm{v}\rangle)_{k\geq 1}
as a measurable function of
\bm{c}^{\epsilon}_{0}
and

\phi^{\epsilon}|_{[0,t]},\bm{c}^{\epsilon}|_{[0,t]},\bm{c}^{\epsilon}_{t}\subset X_{t}\coloneqq L^{2}([0,t];H^{1,2})\times L^{2}_{w}([0,t];\bm{H}^{1,2})\cap L^{2}([0,t];\bm{L}^{2})\times\bm{L}^{2}_{w}

for arbitrary
v\in\bm{H}^{1,2}_{b}
. Thus
\langle M_{t},\bm{v}\rangle
is adapted to
\mathcal{F}_{t}=\sigma(\phi^{\epsilon}|_{[0,s]},\bm{c}^{\epsilon}|_{[0,s]},\bm{c}^{\epsilon}_{s}\,;s\leq t)
.

(Martingale)

First, note that

M\in L^{\infty}([0,T];\bm{L}^{2})\cap C([0,T];(\bm{H}^{1,2})^{\ast})

(3.7)

implies that
M
has a weakly continuous representant
\tilde{M}_{t}
with values in
\bm{L}^{2}
. Now, density of
\bm{H}^{1,2}_{b}\subset\bm{L}^{2}
implies adaptedness of
\tilde{M}_{t}\in\bm{L}^{2}
to
\mathcal{F}_{t}
.

To prove that
\tilde{M}
is not only adapted, but a martingale, we aim to show that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{\prime}\left[\langle\tilde{M}_{t}-\tilde{M}_{s},\bm{v}\rangle\psi(\phi^{\epsilon}|_{[0,s]},\bm{c}^{\epsilon}|_{[0,s]},\bm{c}^{\epsilon}_{s})\right]

\displaystyle=\lim_{k\to\infty}\mathbb{E}^{\prime}\left[\langle M^{(k)}_{t}-M^{(k)}_{s},\bm{v}\rangle\psi(\phi^{(k)}|_{[0,s]},\bm{c}^{(k)}|_{[0,s]},\bm{c}^{(k)}_{s})\right]=0

for arbitrary
t>s\geq 0
,
\bm{v}\in\bm{L}^{2}
and bounded continuous function
\psi\colon X_{t}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
. This case extends directly to the case of a bounded cylindrical function
\psi
and thereby proves that the conditional expectation with respect to
\mathcal{F}_{s}
vanishes.

Observe that
(\langle M^{(k)},\bm{v}\rangle)_{k\geq 1}
is a sequence of real valued martingales with quadratic variation

\int_{0}^{T}\left\|\sqrt{Q}\bm{b}^{\ast}_{\bm{\eta}}(\phi^{(k)}_{s},\bm{c}^{(k)}_{s})\bm{v}\right\|^{2}_{\bm{H}^{r,2}}\leq C_{7}T\left\|\sqrt{Q}\right\|_{HS}\left\|v\right\|_{\bm{L}^{2}}

uniformly bounded in
k
, by
\,\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}^{\prime}\otimes\,\mathrm{d}t\otimes\,\mathrm{d}t
-boundedness of
\phi
and
\bm{c}
. By the BDG inequality,
L^{p}
-boundedness of the sequence follows for any
p>1
. Thus, if we can show almost sure convergence for
\bm{v}\in\bm{L}^{2}
, we obtain the martingale property. But the fact that

\langle M^{(k)},\bm{v}\rangle\overset{C([0,T])}{\to}\langle\tilde{M},\bm{v}\rangle,~\mathbb{P}^{\prime}\text{-a.s.}

already follows as an implication of uniform convergence of
(M^{(k)})_{k\geq 1}\subset C([0,T];\bm{(}\bm{H}^{1,2})^{\ast})
together with uniform boundedness of this sequence in
C([0,T];\bm{L}^{2})
. We can conclude that this convergence holds in
L^{p}(\Omega^{\prime};C([0,T]))
for any
p>1
and thus demonstrate the claim.

(Covariance)

Analogously (cf.
[
12
]
), the quadratic variation of the continuous martingale
\langle\tilde{M},\bm{v}\rangle
can be identified by dominated convergence, where we utilise
\,\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}^{\prime}\otimes\,\mathrm{d}t\otimes\,\mathrm{d}x
-almost sure convergence of
\bm{c}^{(k)}
and
\phi^{(k)}
.

(Continuity)

We need to ensure that
\tilde{M}
is a continuous,
\bm{L}^{2}
-valued martingale. If we can show that for some basis
(\bm{e}_{k})_{k\geq 1}
of
\bm{L}^{2}
,

\sum_{k\geq 1}\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\langle\tilde{M}_{t},\bm{e}_{k}\rangle^{2}<\infty,

(3.8)

then continuity of
t\mapsto\left\|\tilde{M}_{t}\right\|_{L^{2}}
follows by the Lebesgue DCT and thereby,
\tilde{M}
is continuous in
\bm{L}^{2}
. It is left to show that (
3.8
) holds. But by the BDG inequality, we find that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{\prime}\left[\sum_{k\geq 1}\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\langle\tilde{M}_{t},\bm{e}_{k}\rangle^{2}\right]=\sum_{k\geq 1}\mathbb{E}^{\prime}\left[\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\langle\tilde{M}_{t},\bm{e}_{k}\rangle^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq C_{8}\sum_{k\geq 1}\mathbb{E}^{\prime}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\left\|\sqrt{Q}\bm{b}^{\ast}_{\bm{\eta}}(\phi^{\epsilon}_{s},\bm{c}^{\epsilon}_{s})\bm{e}_{k}\right\|^{2}_{H^{r,2}}\,\mathrm{d}s\right]

\displaystyle=C_{8}\,\mathbb{E}^{\prime}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\left\|\sqrt{Q}\bm{b}^{\ast}_{\bm{\eta}}(\phi^{\epsilon}_{s},\bm{c}^{\epsilon}_{s})\right\|^{2}_{HS}\,\mathrm{d}s\right]<\infty.

Altogether, we can conclude that
\tilde{M}
is a square-integrable, continuous
\bm{L}^{2}
-valued martingale with quadratic variation process
\int_{0}^{\cdot}b_{\bm{\eta}}(\phi^{\epsilon},\bm{c}^{\epsilon}_{s})Qb^{\ast}_{\bm{\eta}}(\phi^{\epsilon},\bm{c}^{\epsilon}_{s})\,\mathrm{d}s
such that
\langle\tilde{M},\bm{v}\rangle=\langle M,\bm{v}\rangle
for all
\bm{v}\in\bm{H}^{1,2}_{b}
.
∎

We now repeat the previous compactness argument with the sequence of weighted processes

((\phi^{\epsilon}+\epsilon)c^{\epsilon})_{\varepsilon>0}.

Due to the low integrability of
\nabla\phi^{\epsilon}\nabla c^{\epsilon}
, these processes do not fulfill the conditions of standard identities, which is why we reprove them in the specific setting we encounter here. This is the content of the next two lemmata.

.

and

\partial_{s}\langle y_{s},w\rangle=\langle v_{s}+\tilde{v}_{s},w\rangle

for any

. Here

is assumed to be a continuous square-integrable
\bm{L}^{2}

, and their product

x_{t}y_{t}\in L^{2}(\Omega;L^{2}([0,T];H^{1,2}))\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega;L^{\infty}([0,T];L^{\infty}))

\displaystyle\langle x_{t}y_{t},w\rangle=\langle x_{0}y_{0},w\rangle+\int_{0}^{t}\Big(\langle u_{s}+\tilde{u}_{s},y_{s}w\rangle+\langle v_{s}+\tilde{v}_{s},x_{s}w\rangle\Big)\,\mathrm{d}s+\langle w,\int_{0}^{t}y_{s}\,\mathrm{d}M_{s}\rangle,

for any

and

,
\mathbb{P}
-almost surely.

Lemma 3.13
.

Consider the setting of the previous lemma. It then holds that

\displaystyle\left\|x_{t}\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=\left\|x_{0}\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\int_{0}^{t}2\langle u_{s}+\tilde{u}_{s},x_{s}\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s+\int_{0}^{t}2\langle x_{s},\cdot\rangle\,\mathrm{d}M_{s}+\int_{0}^{t}\left\|g_{s}\circ\sqrt{Q}\right\|^{2}_{HS}\,\mathrm{d}s,

for any

,
\mathbb{P}

Proposition 3.14
.

Let
(\Omega^{\prime},\mathcal{F}^{\prime},\mathbb{P}^{\prime})
denote a probability space containing a family of solutions
(\bm{c}^{\epsilon})_{\epsilon>0}
of (
3.4
). Then there exists a uniform bound on

((\phi^{\epsilon}+\epsilon)\bm{c}^{\epsilon},M^{\epsilon})_{\epsilon>0}\subset L^{2}\left(\Omega^{\prime};L^{2}([0,T];\bm{H}^{1,2})\right)\cap L^{1}\left(\Omega^{\prime};W^{\alpha,1}([0,T];(\bm{H}^{1,2}_{b})^{\ast})\right)

for arbitrary
\alpha\in(0,1/2)
. Further, the sequence of unbounded variation parts
(\int_{0}^{t}(\phi^{\epsilon}_{s}+\epsilon)\,\mathrm{d}M^{\epsilon}_{s})_{\epsilon>0}
of
(\phi^{\epsilon}+\epsilon)\bm{c}^{\epsilon}
is uniformly bounded in
L^{p}(\Omega;C^{0,\alpha}([0,T];\bm{L}^{2}))
for any
\alpha\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
and
p\in[2,\infty)
.

Proof.

We first demonstrate the second claim. Per Proposition
3.11
, we know that the unbounded variation part of
(\phi^{\epsilon}+\epsilon)\bm{c}^{\epsilon}
, as an
(\bm{H}^{1,2}_{b})^{\ast}
-valued process, is given by
\langle\cdot,\int_{0}^{t}(\phi^{\epsilon}+\epsilon)\,\mathrm{d}M^{\epsilon}_{s}\rangle
, for some continuous, square-integrable
\bm{L}^{2}
-valued martingale
M^{\epsilon}
with covariation

\int_{0}^{t}\bm{b}_{\bm{\eta}}(\phi^{\epsilon}_{s},\bm{c}^{\epsilon}_{s})Q\bm{b}^{\ast}_{\bm{\eta}}(\phi^{\epsilon}_{s},\bm{c}^{\epsilon}_{s})\,\mathrm{d}s.

We will prove existence of a uniform bounded in expectation of the
W^{\alpha,p}([0,T];\bm{L}^{2})
-norm for any
\alpha\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
and
p\geq 2
, so that the embedding

W^{\alpha,p}([0,T];\bm{L}^{2})\hookrightarrow C^{0,\alpha-\frac{1}{p}}([0,T];\bm{L}^{2}),~p>\frac{1}{\alpha}

implies the claim. To this end, note that by an application of the martingale representation theorem and Lemma 2.1 in
[
12
]
, we know that for any
\alpha\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
and
p\geq 2
, there exists a constant
C(\alpha,p)
such that

\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\int_{0}^{\cdot}\bm{h}(s)\,\mathrm{d}M^{\epsilon}_{s}\right\|^{p}_{W^{\alpha,p}([0,T];\bm{L}^{2})}\right]\leq C(\alpha,p)\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\left\|\bm{h}(t)(\phi^{\epsilon}_{t}+\epsilon)\bm{b}_{\bm{\eta}}(\phi^{\epsilon}_{t},\bm{c}^{\epsilon}_{t})\sqrt{Q}\right\|^{p}_{HS}\,\mathrm{d}t\right]

for any progressively measurable process
\bm{h}
with values in the space of linear operators on
\bm{L}^{2}

[
12
]
. It follows that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|M^{\epsilon}\right\|^{p}_{W^{\alpha,p}([0,T];\bm{L}^{2})}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\int_{0}^{\cdot}\,\mathrm{d}M^{\epsilon}_{s}\right\|^{p}_{W^{\alpha,p}([0,T];\bm{L}^{2})}\right]\leq C(\alpha,p)\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\left\|\bm{b}_{\bm{\eta}}(\phi^{\epsilon}_{t},\bm{c}^{\epsilon}_{t})\circ\sqrt{Q}\right\|^{p}_{HS}\,\mathrm{d}t\right]

is bounded above uniformly in
\varepsilon>0
by uniform boundedness in
L^{\infty}
of
\phi^{\epsilon}
and
\bm{c}^{\epsilon}
. This demonstrates the second claim.

We now prove the first claim. By the previous lemmata, we can apply an Itô-formula to the functional
u\mapsto\int(\phi^{\epsilon}_{s}+\epsilon)|u|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x
and find that

\displaystyle\left\|\sqrt{\phi^{\epsilon}_{t}+\epsilon}\bm{c}^{\epsilon}_{t}\right\|^{2}_{\bm{L}^{2}}=

\displaystyle\left\|\sqrt{\phi^{\epsilon}_{0}+\epsilon}\bm{c}^{\epsilon}_{0}\right\|^{2}_{\bm{L}^{2}}+\int_{0}^{t}\Bigg(-2\left\|\sqrt{\phi^{\epsilon}_{s}+\epsilon}\bm{D}^{\frac{1}{2}}\nabla\bm{c}^{\epsilon}_{s}\right\|^{2}_{\bm{L}^{2}}+2\langle(\phi^{\epsilon}_{s}+\epsilon)\bm{f}(\phi^{\epsilon}_{s},\bm{c}^{\epsilon}_{s}),\bm{c}^{\epsilon}_{s}\rangle

\displaystyle+\left\|\sqrt{\phi^{\epsilon}_{s}+\epsilon}\bm{b}_{\bm{\eta}}(\phi^{\epsilon}_{s},\bm{c}^{\epsilon}_{s})\circ\sqrt{Q}\right\|^{2}_{HS}+\langle\partial\phi^{\epsilon}_{s}\bm{c}^{\epsilon}_{s},\bm{c}^{\epsilon}_{s}\rangle\Bigg)\,\mathrm{d}s+\int_{0}^{t}\langle(\phi^{\epsilon}_{s}+\epsilon)\bm{c}^{\epsilon}_{s},\cdot\rangle\,\mathrm{d}M^{\epsilon}_{t}.

By rearranging and using boundedness of
\bm{c}^{\epsilon}\in\mathcal{X}_{\bm{c}}
,
\phi^{\epsilon}\in\mathcal{X}_{\phi}
and
\partial_{s}\phi^{\epsilon}\in L^{1}(\Omega;L^{1}([0,T];L^{1}))
, we almost surely find that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\left\|\sqrt{\phi^{\epsilon}_{s}+\epsilon}\bm{D}^{\frac{1}{2}}\nabla\bm{c}^{\epsilon}_{t}\right\|^{2}_{\bm{L}^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}t\leq C_{9}(1+\mathrm{tr}\,Q)T+\int_{0}^{T}\langle(\phi^{\epsilon}_{t}+\epsilon)\bm{c}^{\epsilon}_{t},\cdot\rangle\,\mathrm{d}M^{\epsilon}_{t}.

Since the stochastic integral is almost surely finite and in particular centered, there exists some
C_{10}>0
independent of
\epsilon>0
such that

\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\left\|\sqrt{\phi^{\epsilon}_{s}+\epsilon}\bm{D}^{\frac{1}{2}}\nabla\bm{c}^{\epsilon}_{t}\right\|^{2}_{\bm{L}^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}t\right]\leq C_{10}.

We now show boundedness in expectation of
(\phi^{\epsilon}+\epsilon)\bm{c}^{\epsilon}
in the spaces
W^{\alpha,1}([0,T];(\bm{H}^{1,2}_{b})^{\ast})
. To this end, observe that for
\bm{v}\in\bm{H}^{1,2}_{b}
,

\displaystyle\langle(\phi^{\epsilon}_{t}+\epsilon)\bm{c}^{\epsilon}_{t},\bm{v}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle(\phi^{\epsilon}_{0}+\epsilon)\bm{c}^{\epsilon}_{0},\bm{v}\rangle+\int_{0}^{t}-\langle(\phi^{\epsilon}_{s}+\epsilon)\bm{D}\nabla\bm{c}^{\epsilon}_{s},\nabla\bm{v}\rangle+\langle(\phi^{\epsilon}_{s}+\epsilon)\bm{f}(\phi^{\epsilon}_{s},\bm{c}^{\epsilon}_{s}),\bm{v}\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\langle\partial_{s}\phi^{\epsilon}_{s}\bm{c}^{\epsilon}_{s},\bm{v}\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s+\left\langle\bm{v},\int_{0}^{t}\bm{(}\phi^{\epsilon}_{s}+\epsilon)\,\mathrm{d}M^{\epsilon}_{s}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\coloneqq I^{\epsilon}_{t}(\bm{v})+J^{\epsilon}_{t}(\bm{v}).

Then the preceding bound on
(\phi^{\epsilon}+\epsilon)\nabla\bm{c}^{\epsilon}_{t}
and almost sure boundedness of
\phi^{\epsilon}
and
\bm{c}^{\epsilon}
yield that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|I^{\epsilon}\right\|^{2}_{W^{\alpha,1}([0,T];(\bm{H}^{1,2}_{b})^{\ast})}\right]\leq E\left[\left\|I^{\epsilon}\right\|^{2}_{W^{1,1}([0,T];(\bm{H}^{1,2}_{b})^{\ast})}\right]<\infty.

The previously derived estimate on
M^{\epsilon}
concludes the proof.
∎

Proof of Theorem
3.6
.

Again, we find a probability space
(\Omega^{\prime},\mathcal{F}^{\prime},\mathbb{P}^{\prime})
, a subsequence
(n_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
and random variables
(\phi^{(k)},\partial_{t}\phi^{(k)},\bm{X}_{0}^{(k)},\bm{X}^{(k)},M^{(k)})_{k\geq 1}
,
(\phi,\partial_{t}\phi,\bm{X}_{0},\bm{X},M)
such that

\displaystyle(\phi^{(k)},\partial_{t}\phi^{(k)},\bm{X}_{0}^{(k)},\bm{X}^{(k)},M^{(k)})

\displaystyle\sim(\phi^{\epsilon_{n_{k}}},\partial_{t}\phi^{\epsilon_{n_{k}}},(\phi^{\epsilon_{n_{k}}}_{0}+\epsilon_{n_{k}})\bm{c}^{\epsilon_{n_{k}}}_{0},(\phi^{\epsilon_{n_{k}}}+\epsilon_{n_{k}})\bm{c}^{\epsilon_{n_{k}}},\int_{0}^{\cdot}(\phi^{\epsilon_{n_{k}}}_{s}+\epsilon_{n_{k}})\,\mathrm{d}M^{\epsilon_{n_{k}}}_{s})

and
\phi^{(k)}\to\phi
,
\partial_{s}\phi^{(k)}\to\partial_{s}\phi
,
\bm{X}^{(k)}_{0}\to\bm{X}_{0}
,
\bm{X}^{(k)}\to\bm{X}
,
M^{(k)}\to M
,
\mathbb{P}^{\prime}
-almost surely in
\mathcal{A}
(cf. (
3.5
)). To choose more suggestive notation, let
\bm{c}^{(k)}\coloneqq\frac{\bm{X}^{(k)}}{(\phi^{(k)}+\epsilon_{n_{k}})}
and note that
\bm{c}^{(k)}\sim\bm{c}^{\epsilon_{n_{k}}}
. Let

\bm{c}\coloneqq\mathds{1}_{\left\{\phi>0\right\}}\frac{\bm{X}}{\phi}.

We aim to prove that
(\phi,\bm{c})
is a solution to the martingale problem associated with equation (
3.1
) in the sense of Definition (
3.4
). To achieve this, we demonstrate that almost surely,

\displaystyle\langle\phi_{t}\bm{c}_{t},\bm{v}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\phi_{0}\bm{c}_{0},\bm{v}\rangle-\int_{0}^{t}\langle\phi_{s}\bm{D}\nabla\bm{c}_{s},\nabla\bm{v}\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s+\int_{0}^{t}\langle\phi_{s}\bm{f}(\phi_{s},\bm{c}_{s}),\bm{v}\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s

(3.10)

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\langle\partial_{s}\phi_{s}\bm{c}_{s},\bm{v}\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s+\langle\bm{v},M_{t}\rangle

for any
\bm{v}\in\bm{H}^{1,2}_{b}
and
t\in[0,T]
, and thus, by density of
\bm{H}^{1,2}_{b}\subset\bm{H}^{1,2}
, for all
\bm{v}\in\bm{H}^{1,2}
. Here,

\nabla\bm{c}\coloneqq\mathds{1}_{\left\{\phi>0\right\}}\frac{\nabla\bm{X}-\bm{c}\otimes\nabla\phi}{\phi}

denotes a weak weighted gradient of the limit process
\bm{c}
. Subsequently, we show that
M\in\bm{L}^{2}
is a continuous, square integrable martingale with respect to the filtration generated by
(\phi|_{[0,t]},\bm{c}|_{[0,t]},\phi_{t}\bm{c}_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
whose covariation is given by

\int_{0}^{t}\phi_{s}\bm{b}_{\bm{\eta}}(\phi,\bm{c}_{s})Q\bm{b}^{\ast}_{\bm{\eta}}(\phi,\bm{c}_{s})\phi^{\ast}_{s}\,\mathrm{d}s.

Arguing as in the proof of Proposition
3.11
, we can infer
\mathbb{P}^{\prime}\otimes\,\mathrm{d}t\otimes\,\mathrm{d}x
-almost sure convergence

(\phi^{(k)}+\epsilon_{n_{k}})\bm{c}^{(k)}\to\bm{X},~\phi^{(k)}\to\phi,~\nabla\phi^{(k)}\to\nabla\phi

of a suitably chosen subsequence, again denoted as
(n_{k})_{k\geq 1}
. This implies that

\mathds{1}_{\left\{\phi>0\right\}}\bm{c}^{(k)}\to\mathds{1}_{\left\{\phi>0\right\}}\frac{\bm{X}}{\phi}=\bm{c},~\mathbb{P}^{\prime}\otimes\,\mathrm{d}t\otimes\,\mathrm{d}x\text{-a.s.}

and therefore
\bm{c}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega^{\prime}\times[0,T]\times\mathbb{T}^{n}).

We now identify the regularities and the variational equation satisfied by
\bm{c}
. To this end, we first observe that
\bm{X}^{(k)}
converge in
L^{2}_{w}([0,T];\bm{H}^{1,2})
, almost surely, and
\bm{X}^{(k)},\mathds{1}_{\left\{\phi>0\right\}}\bm{c}^{(k)}
,
\phi^{(k)}
and
\nabla\phi^{(k)}
converge almost surely in
\mathbb{P}^{\prime}\otimes\,\mathrm{d}t\otimes\,\mathrm{d}x
. This enables us to prove that for almost all
\omega\in\Omega^{\prime}
, there exists a set
\mathcal{N}_{\omega}
of full
\!\,\mathrm{d}t
-measure such that for any
\bm{v}\in\bm{H}^{1,2}_{b}
and

, it holds that
\bm{c}_{t}\in\mathcal{X}_{\bm{c}}
and

\displaystyle\langle\phi_{t}\bm{c}_{t},\bm{v}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\phi_{0}\bm{c}_{0},\bm{v}\rangle-\int_{0}^{t}\langle\bm{D}(\nabla\bm{X}_{s}-\nabla\phi_{s}\otimes\bm{c}_{s}),\nabla\bm{v}\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s+\int_{0}^{t}\langle\phi_{s}\bm{f}(\phi_{s},\bm{c}_{s}),\bm{v}\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\langle\partial_{s}\phi_{s}\bm{c}_{s},\bm{v}\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s+\langle\bm{v},M_{t}\rangle.

We obtain this equation by taking limits in the variational equation satisfied by
\bm{X}^{(k)}
applied to

. The bounded variation terms then converge since

\int_{0}^{t}\langle(\phi^{(k)}_{s}+\epsilon_{n_{k}})\nabla\bm{c}^{(k)}_{s},\nabla\bm{v}\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s=\int_{0}^{t}\langle\nabla\bm{X}^{(k)}_{s}-\nabla\phi^{(k)}_{s}\otimes\bm{c}^{(k)}_{s},\nabla\bm{v}\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s\to\int_{0}^{t}\langle\nabla\bm{X}_{s}-\nabla\phi_{s}\otimes\bm{c}_{s},\nabla\bm{v}\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s

for all
\bm{v}\in\bm{H}^{1,2}
and

. Continuity of the right hand side combined with uniform boundedness of
\phi_{t}\bm{c}_{t}\in\bm{L}^{2}
now shows that this equation holds, in fact, for all
t\in[0,T]
and that
\phi\bm{c}
is weakly continuous in
\bm{L}^{2}
- and per Lemma
3.13
, strongly continuous. It remains to be shown that

\nabla\bm{X}-\bm{c}\otimes\nabla\phi=\phi\cdot\mathds{1}_{\phi>0}\frac{\nabla\bm{X}-\bm{c}\otimes\nabla\phi}{\phi}

(3.11)

and that
\nabla\bm{c}
indeed defines a weighted weak derivative of
\bm{c}
for almost all
t>0
. The identity (
3.11
) quickly follows by the convergence

\mathds{1}_{\left\{\phi=0\right\}}\bm{X}=\lim_{k\to\infty}\mathds{1}_{\left\{\phi=0\right\}}(\phi^{(k)}+\epsilon_{n_{k}})\bm{c}^{(k)}\equiv 0.

Thus,
\nabla\bm{X},\nabla\phi\equiv 0
on
\left\{\phi=0\right\}
, whence
\nabla\bm{X}-\bm{c}\otimes\nabla\phi=0
and the division is well-defined. To prove that this term defines a weighted weak derivative, let
\bm{w}\in(C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}))^{d}
be arbitrary. Then,
\!\,\mathrm{d}t
-almost surely,

\displaystyle\langle\phi_{t}\partial_{i}\bm{c}_{t},\bm{w}\rangle=\langle\partial_{i}\bm{X}_{t}-\bm{c}_{t}\partial_{i}\phi_{t},\bm{w}\rangle

\displaystyle=-\langle\bm{X}_{t},\partial_{i}\bm{w}\rangle-\langle\bm{c}_{t}\partial_{i}\phi_{t},\bm{w}\rangle

\displaystyle=-\langle\phi_{t}\bm{c}_{t},\partial_{i}\bm{w}\rangle-\langle\bm{c}_{t}\partial_{i}\phi_{t},\bm{w}\rangle.

Thus, we see that
\!\,\mathrm{d}t
-almost surely,

defines a weighted weak derivative of

with respect to
\phi
.

The properties of the limit process
M\in C([0,T];\bm{L}^{2})
follow as in the previous existence proof.

As the final step, we demonstrate the regularity
properties of the solution. Note that by Fatou’s
lemma and weak convergence,

\mathbb{E}^{\prime}\left[\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\phi_{s}\nabla\bm{c}_{s}\right\|^{2}_{\bm{L}^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}s\right]\leq\mathbb{E}^{\prime}\left[\liminf_{n\to\infty}\int_{0}^{t}\left\|(\phi_{s}+\epsilon_{n_{k}})\nabla\bm{c}^{(k)}_{s}\right\|^{2}_{\bm{L}^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}s\right]<\infty.

Finally, observe that weak continuity of
\phi\bm{c}

in
\bm{L}^{2}
implies that
\bm{c}_{t}\in\mathcal{X}_{\bm{c}}
for all
t\in[0,T]
,
\mathbb{P}^{\prime}
-
a.s.
∎

4

We now aim to solve the system of coupled equations that describes the intracellular dynamics of
biochemical components for the case of a dynamic phase field that interacts with the dynamics of the
stochastic reaction-diffusion equation describing the kinetics inside the phase-field. As in the
preceding section, we modify the original system of equations by truncating the noise outside of a
bounded hypercube
\mathcal{K}

The system of equations we investigate is therefore given by

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\phi_{t}

\displaystyle=\gamma\Delta\phi_{t}+g(\phi_{t},\bm{c}_{t})+\Psi(\phi_{t},\bm{c}_{t},\nabla\phi_{t})

\displaystyle\,\mathrm{d}\bm{c}_{t}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\int(\nabla^{\tau_{n}}\bm{c}^{\tau_{n}}_{s}-\nabla\bm{c}^{\tau_{n}}_{s})\frac{\nabla^{\tau_{n}}\phi^{\tau_{n}}_{s}}{\phi^{\tau_{n}}_{s}+\epsilon}\bm{v}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\int\nabla\bm{c}^{\tau_{n}}_{s}\frac{\nabla\phi^{\tau_{n}}_{s}}{\phi^{\tau_{n}}_{s}+\epsilon}v\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

Convergence of the second integral follows as
\nabla\bm{c}^{\tau_{n}}
almost surely converges weakly, while
\frac{\nabla^{\tau_{n}}\phi^{\tau_{n}}}{\phi^{\tau_{n}}_{t}+\epsilon}
converges strongly. To show the the first integral vanishes, we notice that for
\nu>1
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\int_{0}^{t}\int(\nabla^{\tau_{n}}\bm{c}^{\tau_{n}}_{s}-\nabla\bm{c}^{\tau_{n}}_{s})\frac{\nabla^{\tau_{n}}\phi^{\tau_{n}}_{s}}{\phi^{\tau_{n}}_{s}+\epsilon}\bm{v}\,\mathrm{d}x\right|\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\left\|\bm{v}\right\|_{\bm{L}^{\infty}}}{\epsilon}\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\int|\nabla^{\tau_{n}}\bm{c}^{\tau_{n}}_{s}-\nabla\bm{c}^{\tau_{n}}_{s}||\nabla^{\tau_{n}}\phi^{\tau_{n}}|\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C_{14}\left\|\bm{v}\right\|_{\bm{H}^{1,2}_{b}}}{\epsilon}\left\|\nabla^{\tau_{n}}\bm{c}^{\tau_{n}}_{s}-\nabla\bm{c}^{\tau_{n}}_{s}\right\|_{L^{\frac{\nu}{\nu-1}}}\left\|\nabla\phi^{\tau_{n}}\right\|_{L^{\nu}},

where the
L^{p}
-norms are taken with respect to probability, space and time. We need to verify that
\left\|\nabla\phi^{\tau_{n}}\right\|_{L^{\nu}}
is finite for relevant values of
\nu
. Choose
\nu>2
, so that
\frac{\nu}{\nu-1}<2
. Importantly, for
\nu\leq\frac{2n}{n-2}
, the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality implies that

\left\|\nabla\phi_{t}\right\|_{L^{\nu}}\leq\left\|\Delta\phi_{t}\right\|^{\theta_{\nu}}_{L^{2}}\left\|\nabla\phi_{t}\right\|^{1-\theta_{\nu}}_{L^{2}}

with
\theta_{\nu}=n(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{\nu})\to 0
as
\nu\to 2
. This gives the desired regularity: We have
\phi_{t}\in L^{\infty}([0,T];H^{1,2})\cap L^{2}([0,T];H^{2,2})
, and thus
\nabla\phi_{t}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega;L^{\frac{2}{\theta_{\nu}}}([0,T];L^{\nu}))
, with
\frac{2}{\theta_{\nu}}\to\infty
as
\nu\to 2
.

Since we have uniform a-priori inequalities, the Markov inequality shows that

(\mathbb{P}^{\prime}\otimes\,\mathrm{d}t\otimes\,\mathrm{d}x)\left(\left\{|\nabla\bm{c}^{\tau_{n}}|\geq{\tau_{n}}\right\}\right)\leq\frac{C_{15}}{{\tau_{n}}^{2}}

and by that can conclude that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{\prime}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\int|\nabla^{\tau_{n}}\bm{c}^{\tau_{n}}_{t}-\nabla\bm{c}^{\tau_{n}}_{t}|^{\frac{\nu}{\nu-1}}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}^{\prime}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\int(|\nabla\bm{c}^{\tau_{n}}_{t}|-{\tau_{n}})^{\frac{\nu}{\nu-1}}\mathds{1}_{\left\{|\nabla\bm{c}^{\tau_{n}}|\geq{\tau_{n}}\right\}}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}^{\prime}\left[\left\|\nabla\bm{c}^{\tau_{n}}\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}([0,T]\times\mathbb{T}^{2})}\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}\cdot(\mathbb{P}^{\prime}\otimes\,\mathrm{d}t\otimes\,\mathrm{d}x)^{\frac{\nu-2}{2(\nu-1)}}\left(\left\{|\nabla\bm{c}^{\tau_{n}}|\geq{\tau_{n}}\right\}\right).

The right hand side converges to
0
, which in particular proves convergence of the sequence of integrals to
0
in
L^{1}(\Omega^{\prime})
. Thus we can choose a subsequence such that this expression converges almost surely to
0
and we see that the limit satisfies the right expression.

The properties of the martingale
M^{\epsilon}_{t}
follow as in previous proofs, and similarly does the claimed regularity.
∎

Lemma 4.15
.

For
\bm{c}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega\times[0,T]\times\mathbb{T}^{n})
and an initial condition
\phi_{0}
with
\mathbb{P}(\phi_{0}\in\mathcal{X}_{\phi})=1
,

\alpha\int_{0}^{T}\left\|\frac{|\nabla\phi_{t}|}{\phi^{1-\alpha}_{t}}\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}t\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)

uniformly in
\alpha\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
. In particular,
(\phi^{\alpha})_{\alpha\in(0,\frac{1}{2})}\subset L^{\infty}(\Omega;L^{2}([0,T];H^{1,2}))
is uniformly bounded. If, additionally,
\log\phi_{0}\in L^{1}(\Omega;L^{1}),
then

\sup_{0\leq t\leq t}\left\|\log\phi_{t}\right\|_{L^{1}}+\int_{0}^{T}\left\|\frac{|\nabla\phi_{t}|}{\phi_{t}}\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}t\in L^{1}(\Omega).

Proof.

First, let
0<\beta<1
. By the chain rule, we obtain the identity

\displaystyle\int(1-(\phi_{t}+\epsilon)^{\beta})\,\mathrm{d}x-\int(1-(\phi_{0}+\epsilon)^{\beta})\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=-\beta\left(-(1-\beta)\int_{0}^{t}\int\frac{|\nabla\phi_{s}|^{2}}{(\phi_{s}+\epsilon)^{2-\beta}}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}s+\int_{0}^{t}\int\frac{\Psi(\phi_{s},\bm{c}_{s},\nabla\phi_{s})}{(\phi_{s}+\epsilon)^{1-\beta}}\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{0}^{t}\int\frac{g(\phi_{s},\bm{c}_{s})}{(\phi_{s}+\epsilon)^{1-\beta}}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}s\right).

Now since
|\Psi(\phi_{s},\bm{c}_{s},\nabla\phi_{s})|\leq C_{16}|\nabla\phi_{s}|
, we observe that

\left\lvert\int\frac{\Psi(\phi_{s},\bm{c}_{s},\nabla\phi_{s})}{(\phi_{s}+\epsilon)^{1-\beta}}\,\mathrm{d}x\right\rvert\leq C_{16}\int\frac{\lvert\nabla\phi_{s}\rvert}{(\phi_{s}+\epsilon)^{1-\beta}}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq\frac{1-\beta}{2}\int\frac{\lvert\nabla\phi_{s}\rvert^{2}}{(\phi_{s}+\epsilon)^{2-\beta}}\,\mathrm{d}x+\frac{2C^{2}_{16}}{1-\beta}\int(\phi_{s}+\epsilon)^{\beta}\,\mathrm{d}x.

Further, the boundedness of
g(\phi_{s},\bm{c}_{s})/\phi_{s}
(cf. Assumption
2
) implies boundedness of the third term on the right hand side. Thus, after rearrangement, we find that

\beta\int_{0}^{t}\int\frac{|\nabla\phi_{s}|^{2}}{(\phi_{s}+\epsilon)^{2-\beta}}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}s=\beta\int_{0}^{T}\left\|\frac{|\nabla\phi_{t}|}{\phi^{1-\beta/2}_{t}}\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}t\leq C_{17},

for some constant
C_{17}
independent of
\epsilon
. Application of Fatou’s Lemma to take the limit
\epsilon\to 0
concludes the proof. If, additionally,
\log\phi_{0}\in L^{1}
, then we can repeat the above computation for
\log\frac{1}{\phi_{t}+\epsilon}
and rearrange accordingly to obtain the claimed a priori estimate.
∎

Lemma 4.16
.

Let a filtered probability space

\alpha\in(0,\frac{1}{2})

L^{p}(\Omega;W^{1,p}([0,T];(\bm{H}^{1,2}_{b})^{\ast}))+L^{q}(\Omega;W^{\beta,q}([0,T];(\bm{H}^{1,2}_{b})^{\ast})),

\beta\in(0,\frac{1}{2})

\log\phi_{0}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega;L^{1})

Proof.

The second part of the statement follows by the previous Lemma combined with the usual a-priori inequality one obtains by rearranging the Itô formula applied to
\left\|\bm{c}^{\epsilon}_{t}\right\|^{2}_{\bm{L}^{2}}
.

We obtain the remaining a-priori bounds through the Itô formula applied to
(\phi^{\epsilon}+\epsilon)^{\alpha}\bm{c}^{\epsilon}
. A first application Lemma
3.12
yields the variational identity

\begin{aligned} &\,\mathrm{d}\langle(\phi_{t}^{\epsilon}+\epsilon)^{\alpha}\bm{c}^{\epsilon}_{t},\bm{v}\rangle\\
&=\Big(-\langle\bm{D}(\phi_{t}^{\epsilon}+\epsilon)^{\alpha}\nabla\bm{c}^{\epsilon}_{t},\nabla v\rangle-\langle((\bm{D}+\gamma\bm{I})\alpha-\bm{D})(\phi_{t}^{\epsilon}+\epsilon)^{\alpha-1}\nabla\bm{c}^{\epsilon}_{t}\nabla\phi^{\epsilon}_{t},\bm{v}\rangle\\
&\quad+\langle(\phi_{t}^{\epsilon}+\epsilon)^{\alpha}\bm{f}(\phi^{\epsilon}_{t},\bm{c}^{\epsilon}_{t}),\bm{v}\rangle-\alpha\gamma\langle(\phi_{t}^{\epsilon}+\epsilon)^{\alpha-1}\bm{c}^{\epsilon}_{t}\otimes\nabla\phi^{\epsilon}_{t},\nabla\bm{v}\rangle\\
&\quad-\alpha(\alpha-1)\gamma\left\langle\frac{|\nabla\phi^{\epsilon}_{t}|^{2}}{(\phi^{\epsilon}_{t}+\epsilon)^{2-2\alpha}}\bm{c}^{\epsilon}_{t},\bm{v}\right\rangle+\alpha\langle(\phi_{t}^{\epsilon}+\epsilon)^{\alpha-1}\Psi(\phi^{\epsilon}_{t},\bm{c}^{\epsilon}_{t},\nabla\phi^{\epsilon}_{t})\bm{c}^{\epsilon}_{t},\bm{v}\rangle\\
&\quad+\alpha\langle(\phi_{t}^{\epsilon}+\epsilon)^{\alpha-1}g(\phi^{\epsilon}_{t})\bm{c}^{\epsilon}_{t},\bm{v}\rangle\Big)\,\mathrm{d}t+\langle\bm{v},(\phi_{t}^{\epsilon}+\epsilon)^{\alpha}\bm{b}_{\bm{\eta}}(\phi^{\epsilon}_{t},\bm{c}^{\epsilon}_{t})\circ\cdot\rangle\,\mathrm{d}\bm{W}_{t}.\end{aligned}.

(4.8)

for any
\bm{v}\in\bm{H}^{1,2}_{b}
. Note that the quadratic covariation vanishes in this formula since

is of bounded variation. With this variational identity at hand, we can apply the Itô type formula given by Lemma

to derive that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\,\mathrm{d}\left\|(\phi_{t}+\epsilon)^{\alpha/2}\bm{c}^{\epsilon}_{t}\right\|^{2}_{\bm{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\Bigg(-\left\|\sqrt{\bm{D}}(\phi_{t}+\epsilon)^{\alpha/2}\nabla\bm{c}^{\epsilon}_{t}\right\|^{2}_{\bm{L}^{2}\times\bm{L}^{2}}-\langle((\bm{D}+\gamma\bm{I})\alpha-\bm{D})(\phi_{t}^{\epsilon}+\epsilon)^{\alpha-1}\nabla\bm{c}^{\epsilon}_{t}\nabla\phi^{\epsilon}_{t},\bm{c}^{\epsilon}_{t}\rangle

\displaystyle+\langle(\phi_{t}^{\epsilon}+\epsilon)^{\alpha}\bm{f}(\phi^{\epsilon}_{t},\bm{c}^{\epsilon}_{t}),\bm{c}^{\epsilon}_{t}\rangle-\alpha\gamma\langle(\phi_{t}^{\epsilon}+\epsilon)^{\alpha-1}\bm{c}^{\epsilon}_{t}\otimes\nabla\phi^{\epsilon}_{t},\nabla\bm{c}^{\epsilon}_{t}\rangle

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\alpha(\alpha-1)\gamma\left\langle\frac{|\nabla\phi^{\epsilon}_{t}|^{2}}{(\phi^{\epsilon}_{t}+\epsilon)^{2-2\alpha}}\bm{c}^{\epsilon}_{t},\bm{c}^{\epsilon}_{t}\right\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\alpha\langle(\phi_{t}^{\epsilon}+\epsilon)^{\alpha-1}\Psi(\phi^{\epsilon}_{t},\bm{c}^{\epsilon}_{t},\nabla\phi^{\epsilon}_{t})\bm{c}^{\epsilon}_{t},\bm{c}^{\epsilon}_{t}\rangle

\phi
for
\nu

Proof of Lemma
3.12
.

By the specified conditions, we can infer that
x
and
y
have weakly continuous representants in
L^{2}
with

\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left\|x_{s}\right\|_{L^{\infty}},\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left\|y_{s}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega).

Here, the supremum runs over all
t\in[0,T]
, which is well-defined since balls in
L^{\infty}
are strongly closed and convex, hence closed under weak convergence.
Choose a sequence of partitions
\pi_{n}
such that the sampled step functions

x^{n}=\sum_{t_{i}\in\pi_{n}}x_{t_{i+1}}\mathds{1}_{[t_{i},t_{i+1})},~y^{n}=\sum_{t_{i}\in\pi_{n}}y_{t_{i}}\mathds{1}_{[t_{i},t_{i+1})}\in H^{1,2}

of the continuous representant approximate
x_{s}
and
y_{s}
in
L^{2}(\Omega\times[0,T];H^{1,2})
(cf. Lemma 4.2.6 in
[
20
]
). As usual, we can extract
a subsequence, for simplicity again denoted by
n\geq 1
, such that
x_{n},y_{n}
converge
\mathbb{P}\otimes\,\mathrm{d}t\otimes\,\mathrm{d}x
-almost surely. A telescopic sum now yields

\displaystyle\langle x_{s}y_{s},v\rangle-\langle x_{0}y_{0},v\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{t_{i}\in\pi_{n_{k}}}\langle x_{t_{i+1}}-x_{t_{i}},y_{t_{i}}v\rangle+\langle y_{t_{i+1}}-y_{t_{i}},x_{t_{i+1}}v\rangle

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\langle u_{s}+\tilde{u}_{s},y^{n}_{s}w\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s+\int_{0}^{t}\langle v_{s}+\tilde{v}_{s},x^{n}_{s}w\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s+\int_{0}^{t}\langle y^{n}_{s}w,\cdot\rangle\,\mathrm{d}M_{s}.

Note that by construction,
y^{n}_{s}
is adapted. The approximation properties of
x^{n},y^{n}
yield
convergence of the respective terms to the desired limit. In particular, one can exploit uniform

L^{\infty}
-boundedness and almost sure convergence to apply the Lebesgue DCT and conclude that

\int_{0}^{t}\langle\tilde{u}_{s},y^{n}_{s}w\rangle+\langle\tilde{v}_{s},x^{n}_{s}w\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s\to\int_{0}^{t}\langle\tilde{u}_{s},y_{s}w\rangle+\langle\tilde{v}_{s},x_{s}w\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s.

At last, we show almost sure convergence of the stochastic integral (for a suitable subsequence). We
do this by showing convergence in
L^{2}(\Omega;C([0,T];L^{2}))
. As a preliminary step, we remark that the
almost sure convergence of

\int_{0}^{t}\left\|(y_{s}-y^{n}_{s})wg_{s}\sqrt{Q}\right\|^{2}_{HS}\,\mathrm{d}s=\sum_{k\geq 1}\int_{0}^{t}\left\|(y_{s}-y^{n}_{s})wg_{s}\sqrt{Q}u_{i}\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}s\to 0

follows by the
Lebesgue DCT and almost sure convergence of

\int_{0}^{t}\left\|(y_{s}-y^{n}_{s})wg_{s}\sqrt{Q}u_{i}\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}s=\int_{[0,t]\times\mathbb{T}^{n}}((y_{s}-y^{n}_{s})wg_{s}\sqrt{Q}u_{i})^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}s\to 0

due to the pointwise upper bound

\left\|(y_{s}-y^{n}_{s})wg_{s}\sqrt{Q}u_{i}\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\leq 2\left\|y_{s}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\left\|w\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\left\|g_{s}\sqrt{Q}u_{i}\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}}

for each
i\geq 1
. The
claim now follows by means of the BDG inequality and another application of the DCT.
∎

Proof of Lemma
3.13
.

Let
P_{n}
denote the projection onto an orthogonal set of vectors

A_{n}=\left\{e^{n}_{1},\dots,e^{n}_{k_{n}}\right\}\subset H^{1,2}\cap L^{\infty}

that possesses the
H^{1,2}
and
L^{\infty}
stability property, so that

\left\|P_{n}x\right\|_{H^{1,2}\cap L^{\infty}}\leq C_{P}\left\|x\right\|_{H^{1,2}\cap L^{\infty}}

for some
C_{P}>0
independent of
n
, and that
\left\|P_{n}x-x\right\|_{L^{2}}\to 0
. We first prove that for any finite projection
P_{n}
,

\displaystyle\left\|P_{n}x_{t}\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=\left\|P_{n}x_{0}\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\int_{0}^{t}\langle u_{s},P_{n}x_{s}\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s+\int_{0}^{t}\langle\tilde{u}_{s},P_{n}x_{s}\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s+\int_{0}^{t}\langle P_{n}x_{s},\cdot\rangle\,\mathrm{d}M_{s}

(6.1)

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\left\|P_{n}g_{s}\sqrt{Q}\right\|^{2}_{HS}\,\mathrm{d}s.

Due to polarisation,

\left\|P_{n}x_{t}\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}}-\left\|P_{n}x_{0}\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}}=\left\|P_{n}(x_{t}-x_{0})\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}}-2\langle x_{t}-x_{0},P_{n}x_{0}\rangle.

Now, by the properties of the chosen basis,
P_{n}x_{0}\in H^{1,2}\cap L^{\infty}
and it follows that

\langle x_{t}-x_{0},P_{n}x_{0}\rangle=\int_{0}^{t}\langle u_{s},P_{n}x_{0}\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s+\int_{0}^{t}\langle\tilde{u}_{s},P_{n}x_{0}\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s+\int_{0}^{t}\langle P_{n}x_{0},\cdot\rangle\,\mathrm{d}M_{s}.

(6.2)

On the other hand,

\left\|P_{n}(x_{t}-x_{0})\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\langle x_{t}-x_{0},e_{k}\rangle^{2},

where, due to the Ito formula applied to

f_{t}=\langle x_{t}-x_{0},e_{i}\rangle=\int_{0}^{t}\langle u_{s},e_{k}\rangle+\langle\tilde{u}_{s},e_{k}\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s+\int_{0}^{t}\,\mathrm{d}\langle e_{k},M_{s}\rangle,

we find that

\displaystyle\langle x_{t}-x_{0},e_{k}\rangle^{2}=

\displaystyle 2\int_{0}^{t}\langle x_{s}-x_{0},e_{k}\rangle(\langle u_{s},e_{k}\rangle+\langle\tilde{u}_{s},e_{k}\rangle)\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle+2\int_{0}^{t}\langle x_{s}-x_{0},e_{k}\rangle\,\mathrm{d}\langle e_{k},M_{s}\rangle+\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\langle e_{k},g_{s}\sqrt{Q}\circ\cdot\rangle\right\|^{2}_{HS}\,\mathrm{d}s.

Plugging in and collecting terms, we therefore arrive at the identity

\displaystyle\left\|P_{n}(x_{t}-x_{0})\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=2\int_{0}^{t}\langle u_{s},P_{n}(x_{s}-x_{o})\rangle+\langle\tilde{u}_{s},P_{n}(x_{s}-x_{0})\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s

(6.3)

\displaystyle+2\int_{0}^{t}\langle P_{n}(x_{s}-x_{0}),\cdot\rangle\,\mathrm{d}M_{s}+\int_{0}^{t}\left\|P_{n}g_{s}\sqrt{Q}\right\|^{2}_{HS}\,\mathrm{d}s.

Adding (
6.2
) and (
6.3
) together, we obtain (
6.1
). At last, we take the limit
n\to\infty
to derive the desired identity. Naturally,
\left\|P_{n}x_{t}\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}}
and
\left\|P_{n}x_{0}\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}}
converge to the appropriate limit. Further, note that

\int_{0}^{t}\left\|P_{n}x_{s}-x_{s}\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}s\to 0

by dominated convergence, and therefore
P_{n}x\to x
in
L^{2}([0,T];L^{2})
. From this, it already follows that the stochastic integral and trace term converge, again by dominated convergence. Further, we find that there exists a subsequence
(n_{k})_{k\geq 1}
such that
P_{n}x\to x
almost surely on
[0,T]\times\mathbb{T}^{n}
. It then finally follows that

\int_{[0,T]\times\mathbb{T}^{n}}\tilde{u}_{s}(y)P_{n}x_{s}(y)\,\mathrm{d}y\to\int_{[0,T]\times\mathbb{T}^{n}}\tilde{u}_{s}(y)x_{s}(y)\,\mathrm{d}y

by dominated convergence, since

\left\|P_{n}x_{s}\right\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T]\times\mathbb{T}^{n})}\leq C_{P}\left\|x_{s}\right\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T]\times\mathbb{T}^{n})}

by the stability properties of the projections. Finally, weak convergence in
L^{2}([0,T];L^{2})
of
\nabla P_{n}x_{s}
to
x_{s}
follows by boundedness, which ensures convergence of some subsequence, and strong convergence of
P_{n}
in
L^{2}
, so that the argument is finished by density of smooth enough functions.
∎

Proof of Proposition
4.4
.

We assume that for all
t\in[0,T]
,
\phi_{t}\in\mathcal{X}_{\phi}
and that
\rho\in L^{\infty}([0,T]\times\mathbb{T}^{n})
. By the chain rule,

\displaystyle\int(1-\phi^{\alpha}_{t})\rho^{2}_{t}\,\mathrm{d}x-\int(1-\phi^{\alpha}_{0})\rho^{2}_{0}\,\mathrm{d}x

(6.4)

\displaystyle=-\alpha\left((1-\alpha)\int_{0}^{t}\int\frac{|\nabla\phi_{s}|^{2}}{\phi^{2-\alpha}_{s}}\rho^{2}_{s}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}s+\int_{0}^{t}\int\tilde{\Psi}(\bm{c}_{t},\phi_{t})\frac{|\nabla\phi_{t}|}{\phi^{1-\alpha}_{t}}\rho^{2}_{s}\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{0}^{t}\int\frac{\tilde{g}(\phi_{t})}{\phi_{t}}\phi^{\alpha}_{s}\rho^{2}_{s}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}s\right)

\displaystyle+2\int_{0}^{t}\langle\partial_{s}\rho_{s},\rho_{s}(1-\phi^{\alpha}_{s})\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s.

We now want to let
\alpha\to 0
. By assumption, the first term on the right hand side tends to
0
as
\alpha\to 0
. Similarly,

\rho_{s}(1-\phi^{\alpha}_{s})\to 0\text{ in }L^{2}([0,T];L^{2}),~\nabla(\rho_{s}(1-\phi^{\alpha}_{s}))=\nabla\rho_{s}(1-\phi^{\alpha}_{s})-\alpha\rho_{s}\frac{\nabla\phi_{s}}{\phi^{1-\alpha}_{s}}\to 0\text{ in }L^{2}([0,T];L^{2}).

Although we cannot additionally conclude that

\rho_{s}(1-\phi^{\alpha}_{s})\overset{L^{\infty}([0,T];L^{\infty})}{\to}0,

dominated convergence still gives us that

\int_{0}^{t}\langle\partial_{s}\rho_{s},\rho_{s}(1-\phi^{\alpha}_{s})\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s\to 0.

Taking the limit
\alpha\to 0
on the left hand side then gives that
\int\mathds{1}_{\{\phi_{0}=0\}}\rho^{2}_{0}\,\mathrm{d}x=0
, since
\phi_{t}
is strictly positive. Therefore,
\mathds{1}_{\{\phi_{0}=0\}}\rho_{0}=0
.
∎

Proof of Example
4.6
.

Adaptedness and boundedness properties are immediate by the deterministic properties of the heat equation. Also, by analogous methods as in the proof of Lemma
4.15
, we find that

\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\frac{\nabla h_{s}}{\sqrt{h_{s}}}\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}s<\infty.

Using the product rule, we now derive that

\displaystyle\int h_{t}\log(\phi_{t}+\epsilon)\,\mathrm{d}x-\int\phi_{0}\log(\phi_{0}+\epsilon)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}-2\int\frac{\nabla h_{s}\nabla\phi_{s}}{\phi_{s}+\epsilon}\,\mathrm{d}x+\int h_{s}\frac{|\nabla\phi_{s}|^{2}}{\phi^{2}_{s}}\,\mathrm{d}x+\int h_{s}\frac{\Psi(\phi_{s},\bm{c}_{s},\nabla\phi_{s})}{(\phi_{s}+\epsilon)}\,\mathrm{d}x+\int h_{s}\frac{g(\phi_{s},\bm{c}_{s})}{(\phi_{s}+\epsilon)}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}s

Again, we can rearrange and apply Young’s inequality to control the third and fourth term on the right hand side. Combined with the estimate

\int\frac{\nabla h_{s}\nabla\phi_{s}}{\phi_{s}+\epsilon}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq C_{20}\int\frac{|\nabla h_{s}|^{2}}{h_{s}}\,\mathrm{d}x+\delta\int h_{s}\frac{|\nabla\phi_{s}|^{2}}{(\phi_{s}+\epsilon)^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x

for
\delta\ll 1
, we can rearrange the product identity and take a limit
\epsilon\to 0
to conclude existence of a deterministic constant with

\int_{0}^{t}\int h_{s}\frac{|\nabla\phi_{s}|^{2}}{\phi_{s}^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}s<\infty.\qed

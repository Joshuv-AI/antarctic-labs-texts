Analysis of the status and influencing factors of dignity impairment symptoms in critically ill patients after ICU treatment
Authors: Jinlei Du, Gutao Peng, Xiaoling Wu, Ling Lei, Hongxiang Zhao, Qiyu Zhang, Yuanxia Wang, Yulian Wu, Yao Chen, Chencong Nie
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractTo investigate the status of dignity impairment symptoms in critically ill patients after ICU treatment and conduct a systematic analysis of its influencing factors, aiming to provide reference for clinical medical decision-making by healthcare professionals. A cross-sectional survey study. From April 15th to 21st, 2024, a cross-sectional survey was conducted in 40 tertiary Grade A comprehensive hospitals in 9 provinces, municipalities, and autonomous regions including Sichuan Province, Zhejiang Province, and Chongqing Municipality. Various critically ill patients who were admitted to ICU treatment were surveyed using a general information questionnaire and the Dignity Inventory. A total of 346 questionnaires were collected in this survey, with 333 valid questionnaires and an effective response rate of 96%. The incidence rate of dignity impairment symptoms in critically ill patients after ICU treatment was 39%. Multifactor logistic regression analysis showed that age [OR = 1.124, 95%CI=(1.067–1.184)], frequency of visits [OR = 1.875, 95%CI=(1.075–3.269)], length of ICU stay [OR = 1.352, 95%CI=(1.108–1.649)], and tracheotomy upon discharge [OR = 2.269, 95%CI=(1.273–4.044)] were risk factors for dignity impairment symptoms in critically ill patients after ICU treatment. Number of children [OR = 0.247, 95%CI=(0.106–0.576)] was a protective factor for dignity impairment symptoms in critically ill patients after ICU treatment. Although the incidence of dignity impairment symptoms in critically ill patients after ICU treatment is relatively low, the psychological trauma it causes cannot be ignored. Healthcare professionals must actively establish and improve its prevention and management system.Keywords: ICU treatment, Dignity impairment symptoms, Influencing factorsSubject terms: Risk factors, Prognosis

Introduction

With the transition of modern medical models from “disease-centered” to “patient-centered,” phrases such as “compassion,” “benevolence,” and “respect” have gradually formed the core values of medical services1. However, the current research focus of healthcare professionals both domestically and internationally on various critically ill patients still mainly concentrates on physiological symptoms2, while research on psychological aspects is relatively scarce. Especially regarding the special treatment environment and nursing measures within the ICU, there is a lack of in-depth research on dignity management. Dignity refers to the quality or state in which an individual feels self-respect, self-worth, and being respected, and it is an inherent and intrinsic characteristic of humanity3.

In recent years, the importance of dignity in the field of medicine has been increasingly recognized by scholars worldwide and has become a widely discussed issue among medical associations and organizations4. However, for various critically ill patients, they represent not only a group whose self-care abilities are partially or completely lost due to the severity of their illness, leading to a strong sense of shame and loss of self-worth5, but also a group subjected to the risk of dignity loss due to the need for treatment-related privacy exposure or physical restraint management. This results in damage to their personal image and infringement upon their personal rights. As a result, these individuals are highly prone to experiencing symptoms of dignity impairment6.

Studies have indicated that once patients experience symptoms of dignity impairment, they may develop symptoms such as anxiety, depression, and spiritual malaise, directly affecting their quality of life and disease prognosis, and even accelerating their death7. However, for the systematic management of various critically ill patients, it should not only include the management of physiological symptoms during treatment but also encompass the management of psychological symptoms during and after treatment8.Therefore, this study aims to conduct a multi-center investigation in China to understand the status of dignity impairment in critically ill patients after ICU treatment and its risk factors. This is intended to provide a reference basis for healthcare professionals to develop scientifically sound management strategies for this symptom.

Materials and methods

Study population

This study employed a convenience sampling method to select various critically ill patients who underwent ICU treatment from April 15th to 21st, 2024, in 40 tertiary Grade A comprehensive medical institutions located in Sichuan Province, Guizhou Province, Chongqing Municipality, Shanxi Province, Hebei Province, Hubei Province, Hunan Province, Zhejiang Province, and Ningxia Hui Autonomous Region.

According to the Kendall sample size estimation principle9, the sample size should be 5–10 times the number of study variables. Thus, this study would require approximately 125–250 participants. Additionally, considering a 10% attrition rate, the study would need to comprehensively survey at least 138 patients. In this study, all patients were discharged from the ICU after treatment and were in the disease recovery period, making them effectively cooperative for the research investigation.

Inclusion Criteria: Participants must be aged 18 years or older, possess consciousness and have the ability to communicate verbally or non-verbally, have been admitted to the ICU for at least 24 h, and have been discharged from the ICU within 24 h. Patients or their family members must be willing to cooperate with the investigation.

Exclusion Criteria: Participants with a recent history of diagnosis of symptoms such as depression or delirium, a history of mental illness, current participation in other psychological investigation-related studies, or severe visual or hearing impairments will be excluded.

Ethical consideration

This project conforms to the National Statement on Ethical Conduct in Human Research (2016) and has been approved by the Ethics Committee of Zigong Fourth People’s Hospital (approval number: EC−2024−008). The study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki. In this study, all participants signed informed consent forms before the research was conducted.

Survey tools

General information questionnaire

In this study, the general information questionnaire was designed by the project team members based on literature research and clinical work experience. It mainly consists of demographic data and disease-related information of the patients. The demographic data include age, marital status, occupation, etc. Marital status is categorized as married or non-married (unmarried, divorced, widowed, etc.). Other in the occupation category mainly refers to unemployed or resigned status. Other in the healthcare payment category mainly refers to commercial insurance, social assistance funds, etc. Disease-related information mainly includes primary diseases and the number of other systemic dysfunctions. The number of other systemic dysfunctions mainly refers to complications outside of the primary disease, such as shock and hypertension in the circulatory system; pneumonia and pulmonary atelectasis in the respiratory system; hyperthyroidism and diabetes in the endocrine system.

Dignity inventory

The Dignity Inventory was initially developed by Canadian scholar Chochinov10 in 2002 and formally published in 2008. This inventory includes five dimensions: symptom distress, psychological status, dependency, social support, and spiritual well-being, comprising a total of 25 items. Patients self-assess their symptoms, ranging from no distress to severe distress, on a scale from 1 to 5 for each item. Each item is scored from 1 to 5, and the total score of the inventory ranges from 25 to 125. A score of ≥ 50 indicates that the patient has significant dignity impairment symptoms. The Cronbach’s α coefficient of this inventory is 0.924, and the content validity is 0.885.

Data collection methods

Before the study commenced, project team members provided detailed introductions to the research background and survey methods to doctors or nurses within each medical institution. During the study period, the project leader distributed the survey questionnaires to various medical institutions via mail or delivery. Patients independently completed the questionnaires and submitted them anonymously. Each patient could only fill out the questionnaire once, and all contents had to be completed before submission. After all data collection was completed, responsible personnel from each region collected and mailed the questionnaires uniformly.

Quality control

To ensure the accuracy of data from multi-center research, a research team was formed before the study began. Additionally, a research leader was appointed at each hospital and received standardized training to oversee the quality control and data collection processes within their respective hospitals. For all survey questionnaires in this study, two researchers conducted joint review and screening. Survey questionnaires exhibiting serious bias risks, such as all items reporting either minimum or maximum scores, or theoretical values such as ages exceeding 200 years, were excluded. Furthermore, the analysis and processing of survey data in this study were audited and guided by experts with a background in statistics within the hospital to ensure the correct interpretation of statistical results and clinical significance.

Statistical methods

Data entry with double-checking of questionnaires was conducted using Epidata 3.0 data processing software, and original data were statistically analyzed using SPSS 24.0 and R software. For count data, frequency descriptions were utilized. Continuous data were described using mean ± standard deviation for normally distributed variables and median with inter-quartile range for non-normally distributed variables.

To enhance the robustness of our variable selection process, we employed LASSO (Least Absolute Shrinkage and Selection Operator) regression analysis. LASSO was used to refine our variable selection by applying an L1 penalty to the regression coefficients, performing both variable selection and regularization. This method identifies the most significant predictors of dignity impairment symptoms by shrinking the coefficients of less important variables to zero. Variables with non-zero coefficients in the LASSO model were then included in a binary logistic regression analysis to evaluate their impact on dignity impairment symptoms in critically ill patients following ICU treatment. Statistical significance was defined as P < 0.05.

Results

General information of survey participants and LASSO-based variable selection for dignity impairment symptoms

In this study, a total of 346 patients who met the inclusion and exclusion criteria were invited to participate in the questionnaire survey. Among them, 8 questionnaires had responses that were either maximum or minimum values, and 5 questionnaires showed signs of repeated erasures, and were thus excluded. Ultimately, We obtained 333 valid responses, resulting in an effective response rate of 96.24%. Among the 333 patients, the dignity assessment score was 49.85 ± 23.19. Among them, 94 patients experienced dignity impairment symptoms, with a total score on the inventory of 83.86 ± 14.89. Specifically, the scores for different dimensions were as follows: psychological status dimension: 16.75 ± 3.57, symptom distress dimension: 21.09 ± 4.07, spiritual well-being dimension: 13.13 ± 3.40, dependency dimension: 24.76 ± 4.87, and social support dimension: 8.10 ± 3.18.

Among the 239 patients who did not experience dignity loss symptoms, the total score on the inventory was 36.47 ± 5.25. Specifically, the scores for different dimensions were as follows: psychological status dimension: 7.01 ± 1.49, symptom distress dimension: 8.94 ± 1.95, spiritual well-being dimension: 5.43 ± 1.52, dependency dimension: 10.95 ± 2.19, and social support dimension: 4.12 ± 1.23.

The results of the LASSO (Least Absolute Shrinkage and Selection Operator) analysis showed that several variables were significantly associated with the assessment of dignity impairment symptoms in critically ill patients after ICU treatment. The variables selected by LASSO for further analysis include age, body mass index, religious belief, primary occupation, healthcare payment method, number of children, frequency of visits, self-rated family harmony, primary disease, length of ICU stay, frequency of ICU admissions, enteral nutrition therapy, mechanical ventilation therapy, protective restraint, indwelling catheter and tracheotomy upon transfer. Multifactorial analysis of dignity impairment symptoms in critically ill patients after ICU treatment

Using the assessment results of dignity impairment symptoms in critically ill patients after ICU treatment as the dependent variable, the independent variables selected through LASSO analysis were included in the regression model for multifactor logistic regression analysis. Before the multifactorial analysis, collinearity diagnostics were performed on the variables with statistical differences in the univariate analysis. It was found that the tolerance of each model was > 0.1, and the variance inflation factor was less than 10, indicating that there was no multicollinearity between the variables11.

Multifactor logistic regression analysis showed that age [OR = 1.124, 95%CI=(1.067–1.184)], frequency of visits [OR = 1.875, 95%CI=(1.075–3.269)], length of ICU stay [OR = 1.352, 95%CI=(1.108–1.649)], and tracheotomy upon discharge [OR = 2.269, 95%CI=(1.273–4.044)] were risk factors for dignity impairment symptoms in critically ill patients after ICU treatment. Number of children[OR = 0.247, 95%CI=(0.106–0.576)] was a protective factor for dignity impairment symptoms in critically ill patients after ICU treatment. Discussion

The incidence of dignity impairment symptoms in critically ill patients after ICU treatment is at a medium-low level

This study conducted a survey on 333 critically ill patients admitted to ICU in 40 tertiary A hospitals in 9 provinces and autonomous regions in China. The results showed that approximately 39% of patients experienced dignity impairment symptoms, with an incidence rate at a medium-low level, meaning the occurrence rate of dignity impairment is below 50% but exceeds 25%.This finding is lower than the reported rate of dignity impairment in patients with malignant lymphoma (71%) by Wang12.

The reasons for this difference may be as follows: compared to patients with malignant tumor, most critically ill patients admitted to ICU have reversible diseases and higher cure rates, leading to greater hope for survival and more active cooperation with treatment and management during the disease treatment process. Therefore, they have a lower risk of dignity impairment compared to patients with malignant tumors. Although the incidence of this symptom is relatively low, the psychological trauma it causes to patients should not be underestimated. Clinical healthcare providers need to establish and improve related symptom prevention and management systems.

Factors influencing dignity impairment symptoms in critically ill patients after ICU treatment

Older age increases the risk of dignity impairment symptoms

The results of this study indicate that age (OR = 1.124) is a risk factor for dignity impairment symptoms in critically ill patients after ICU treatment. The reasons for this may be as follows: as age increases, various physiological functions of the body gradually decline, leading to older patients being more susceptible to physiological and psychological stress responses when facing major diseases or injuries13. Especially when facing invasive procedures and treatments such as tracheal intubation and invasive monitoring, patients may feel physically and mentally uncomfortable, thereby making them more prone to dignity impairment symptoms.

Therefore, it is recommended that clinical healthcare providers actively seek pharmacological and psychological interventions for older patients to alleviate their physical and mental suffering in a timely manner. Additionally, life review therapy can be utilized to help patients recall valuable and significant events in their lives, thereby alleviating the psychological pressure or feelings of inferiority in patients.

The risk of dignity impairment decreases with a higher number of children

The study results indicate that a higher number of children is associated with a lower risk of dignity impairment symptoms in critically ill patients (OR = 0.247). This finding suggests that having more children might offer additional protective factors against the erosion of dignity in such patients.One reason for this association could be the increased level of emotional and social support provided by a larger family network14. Patients with more children might experience a greater sense of connection and emotional reassurance, which can mitigate feelings of isolation and helplessness that are common in ICU settings. This familial support can offer a comforting presence and reduce the psychological stress that contributes to dignity impairment.

Another possible reason is that patients with more children may have access to a broader range of practical assistance and care15. This support can include help with daily activities, medical decision-making, and managing the stress associated with their condition, which can enhance their overall well-being and sense of self-worth.

In clinical practice, it is essential for healthcare professionals to recognize the role of family dynamics in the care of critically ill patients. Incorporating family support into patient care plans, and actively engaging family members in the patient’s care process, can help to bolster the patient’s emotional resilience and dignity. For instance, providing family-centered care approaches, offering support resources for families, and ensuring regular communication can significantly contribute to improving patient outcomes and preserving dignity.

Patients with fewer visits are at higher risk of dignity impairment symptoms

The results of this study indicate that the frequency of visits (OR = 1.875) is a risk factor for dignity impairment symptoms in critically ill patients after ICU treatment, a finding consistent with Fuseini’s research16. The analysis suggests that the care and support from relatives and friends are crucial for patients’ psychological well-being during illness. Patients who receive more frequent visits from relatives and friends tend to feel valued and respected, thereby experiencing a greater sense of security during the course of their treatment. This heightened sense of security may enhance their self-esteem and resilience, leading to a more proactive approach in coping with their illness and ultimately improving their level of dignity.

Conversely, patients who receive fewer visits may experience lower levels of self-esteem and security, resulting in a higher risk of dignity impairment. Therefore, it is recommended that healthcare providers actively encourage visits from patients’ family members or friends to increase their sense of companionship. Additionally, optimizing visitation modes, such as video visits or scheduled visits, in different healthcare institutions may also be beneficial17.

Risk of dignity impairment symptoms increases with prolonged ICU stay

The study results indicate that the length of ICU stay (OR = 1.352) is a risk factor for dignity impairment symptoms in critically ill patients after ICU treatment, which is consistent with findings from Falvey’s research18. The analysis suggests that during ICU treatment, patients often experience anxiety and fear due to the severity and uncertainty of their condition. Additionally, the closed environment of the ICU restricts communication with family and friends, leading to feelings of loneliness and helplessness, directly affecting the patients’ dignity. Furthermore, healthcare professionals often focus primarily on monitoring patients’ vital signs, neglecting their psychological well-being, which can make patients feel neglected and treated with indifference, further damaging their sense of dignity19.

Therefore, it is recommended that ICU staff pay attention to the psychological status of patients, particularly those with prolonged stays, actively screen for adverse psychological symptoms, and provide timely individualized interventions, such as dignity therapy and meaning-centered therapy, to reduce the extent of dignity impairment in patients20.

Patients with tracheostomy upon transfer are at higher risk of dignity impairment symptoms

The results of this study indicate that the presence of a tracheostomy upon transfer (OR = 2.269) is a risk factor for dignity impairment symptoms in critically ill patients after ICU treatment. The potential reasons for this are as follows: for these patients, tracheostomy necessitates prolonged reliance on mechanical ventilation or other supportive devices to maintain normal respiratory function, along with the need for long-term care provided by healthcare professionals or family members. This loss of autonomy and independence directly undermines the patients’ sense of dignity21.

Additionally, tracheostomy can also make patients appear visibly distinct, which to some extent increases their risk of dignity impairment. Therefore, it is recommended that healthcare providers actively monitor the psychological changes of such patients and encourage them to express their inner feelings. For instance, professionals from various fields such as healthcare providers and psychologists can adopt a multidisciplinary approach to jointly develop individualized treatment plans for patients to meet their physical and psychological needs. Moreover, actively involving family members to assist in guiding patients to adopt correct attitudes and perspectives can help patients rebuild confidence in their disease recovery.

Conclusions

This study surveyed 333 critically ill patients who underwent ICU treatment in 40 tertiary hospitals in China and found that about 39% of patients experienced varying degrees of dignity impairment symptoms. These symptoms were directly associated with patient age, medical expense payment method, frequency of family visits, length of ICU stay, and the presence of tracheostomy upon discharge. It is recommended that future research focus on the psychological aspects of various critically ill patients after transfer from the ICU and actively explore warning mechanisms and intervention strategies for dignity impairment symptoms in critically ill patients following ICU treatment using a multidisciplinary collaboration approach.

Limitations

Despite efforts to include a diverse sample, our study may be subject to selection bias. The distribution of questionnaires through multiple institutions could have unintentionally excluded or underrepresented certain patient demographics.This limitation suggests that our findings may not fully generalize to all populations experiencing dignity impairment.Therefore, it is recommended that future research employ randomized sampling strategies and expand the geographical coverage and sample size to conduct more comprehensive investigations.

Linear response for random and sequential intermittent maps
Davor Dragičević, Cecilia González-Tokman, Julien Sedro

Abstract
This work establishes a quenched (trajectory-wise) linear response formula for random intermittent dynamical systems, consisting of Liverani-Saussol-Vaienti maps with varying parameters. This result complements recent annealed (averaged) results in the i.i.d setting. As an intermediate step, we show existence, uniqueness and statistical stability of the random absolutely continuous invariant probability measure (a.c.i.m.) for such non-uniformly expanding systems. Furthermore, we investigate sequential intermittent dynamical systems of this type and establish a linear response formula. Our arguments rely on the cone technique introduced by Baladi and Todd and further developed by Leppänen. We also demonstrate that sequential systems exhibit a subtle distinction from both random and autonomous settings: they may possess infinitely many sequential absolutely continuous equivariant densities. However, only one of these corresponds to an SRB state in the sense of Ruelle. Keywords : linear response; random dynamical systems; intermittent maps; sequential dynamics. 2020 MSC : 37D25, 37H05

Introduction

Intermittent maps, such as the ones introduced by Liverani, Saussol and Vaienti in
[
26
]
(called LSV maps), are widely studied non-uniformly expanding systems exhibiting a combination of chaotic and regular behavior. Trajectories of these systems behave chaotically on a large part of the state space, but when they visit small neighbourhoods of the neutral fixed point at the origin, it takes a long time before they return to the chaotic regime. The ergodic and statistical properties of intermittent systems have been widely investigated in the last few decades. We refer to
[
2
]
for a detailed survey.
Another type of intermittency, called critical intermittency, has been recently investigated in

[
20
,
21
]
.

Linear response takes place when a
(unique) physical measure of a system changes differentiably with respect to perturbations of the dynamics. This is a strong form of stability under parameter changes, whose rigorous mathematical study has been initiated
by Ruelle
[
29
]
. Since then, the existence (and the lack of) linear response has been investigated for wide classes of deterministic dynamical systems including: smooth dynamical systems
[
6
,
30
]
, piecewise expanding maps on the interval
[
5
,
7
]
, uniformly hyperbolic diffeomorphisms and flows
[
10
,
11
,
19
,
29
]
as well as partially hyperbolic systems
[
13
]
.

The linear response for LSV maps has been obtained in three independent works that appeared around the same time. Namely, it was established by Baladi and Todd
[
8
]
by relying on cone techniques, and by Bahsoun and Saussol
[
3
]
as well as Korepanov
[
22
]
using inducing type arguments. More recently, Leppänen
[
25
]
has refined the Baladi-Todd approach and established linear response for a family of maps with a neutral fixed point and a non-flat critical point, which includes LSV maps.

The main objective of the present paper is to establish linear response for LSV maps in random and nonautonomous (sequential) contexts. More precisely, we deal with two-sided sequences of maps
(T_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
, where each
T_{n}
is an LSV map.
The dynamics is generated by compositions of the form

T_{n}^{m}:=T_{n+m-1}\circ\ldots\circ T_{n+1}\circ T_{n}\quad\text{for $n\in\mathbb{Z}$ and $m\in\mathbb{N}$.}

In the case of random dynamics, map sequences are obtained from trajectories of an invertible, ergodic, probability preserving map
\sigma\colon(\Omega,\mathbb{P})\to(\Omega,\mathbb{P})
. That is
T_{n}=T_{\sigma^{n}\omega}
,
\omega\in\Omega,\ n\in\mathbb{Z}
.

A (nonautonomous) absolutely continuous invariant measure (a.c.i.m) for such system will be a sequence
(\mu_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
of probability measures on the unit interval which are absolutely continuous with respect to the Lebesgue measure
m
and satisfy that

(T_{n})_{*}\mu_{n}=\mu_{n+1}\quad n\in\mathbb{Z},

where
(T_{n})_{*}\mu_{n}
denotes the push-forward of
\mu_{n}
with respect to
T_{n}
. This concept is related to the concept of SRB states in the nonautonomous setting, discussed by Ruelle
[
29
, Section 4]
, although the latter requires an extra condition.
Namely, SRB states are the limit of Lebesgue measure,
m
, pushed forward from the infinite past,
\mu_{k}=\lim_{n\to\infty}(T_{k-n})_{*}(T_{k-n+1})_{*}\dots(T_{k-1})_{*}m
.
We show that this extra condition is essential to ensure uniqueness, as otherwise sequential systems often have multiple sequential a.c.i.m.s. Another similar concept is presented by Young in
[
32
, Section 4]
, although that work deals with one-sided sequences of maps.

Similarly, a random a.c.i.m
(\mu_{\omega})_{\omega\in\Omega}
satisfies
(T_{\omega})_{*}\mu_{\omega}=\mu_{\sigma\omega}
for
\mathbb{P}
-
\text{a.e. }\omega\in\Omega
, and
an extra measurability condition, namely that
(\mu_{\omega})_{\omega\in\Omega}
is a random measure. That is, it provides the disintegration of a measure
\mu
on
\Omega\times[0,1]
, with marginal
\mathbb{P}
.

Our goal is to study the dependence of random a.c.i.ms
(\mu_{\omega})_{\omega\in\Omega}
, respectively sequencial a.c.i.ms
(\mu_{n})_{n}
, under small perturbations of the family
(T_{\omega})_{\omega\in\Omega}
, respectively
(T_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
. More precisely, in the random setting, suppose that
I
is a small interval around
0
in
\mathbb{R}
and that we are given a parametrized family of LSV maps
(T_{\omega,\varepsilon})_{\omega\in\Omega}
,
\varepsilon\in I
where we view
T_{\omega,\varepsilon}
as a “small” perturbation of
T_{\omega}:=T_{\omega,0}
. Since both of these maps belong to the LSV family, this means that the parameter associated to
T_{\omega,\varepsilon}
is close to the parameter associated to
T_{\omega}
. By
(\mu_{\omega,\varepsilon})_{\omega\in\Omega}
we will denote the (unique) random a.c.i.m corresponding to the sequence
(T_{\omega,\varepsilon})_{\omega\in\Omega}
, provided by Proposition
9
.
We are then interested in formulating sufficient conditions under which, for
\mathbb{P}
-
\text{a.e. }\omega\in\Omega
and suitable observable
\phi
, the map
\varepsilon\to\int\phi\,d\mu_{\omega,\varepsilon}
is continuous (quenched statistical stability; see Theorem
13
) or differentiable at
0

(quenched linear response; see Theorems
14
and
29
). The sequential setting is treated in Theorem
25
.
We note that there is a series of recent results devoted to the quenched linear response for various classes of random dynamical systems
[
12
,
14
,
15
,
17
,
31
]
, although in these works the case of random intermittent dynamics was not considered. Hence, our main result complements the recent annealed linear response result for random i.i.d compositions of LSV maps obtained in
[
4
]
.

On the other hand, to the best of our knowledge, Ruelle
[
29
, Section 4]
was the first to discuss linear response in nonautonomous (sequential) setting. He
briefly outlined a strategy to obtain linear response for a class of dynamics formed by two-sided sequences of maps
(T_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
, where each
T_{n}
is a sufficiently small perturbation of a fixed uniformly hyperbolic map, and established a linear response formula, valid when a fixed map is repeatedly applied, except for a finite number of steps, where (small) perturbations take place. A possibility for establishing linear response for sequential expanding dynamics was also indicated by Sedro and Rugh
[
31
]
.
The present work is the first one which considers the linear response problem in this
nonautonomous (sequential) nonuniformly hyperbolic setting. Previous works on this type of dynamics have been devoted either to decay of correlations or to limit theorems (see
[
1
,
23
,
28
,
27
]
and references therein).

Our arguments rely on the work of Leppänen
[
25
]
, which in turn is inspired by the earlier work of Baladi and Todd
[
8
]
.
However, nontrivial technical changes are necessary to account for the non-autonomous nature of the problem under consideration.

The paper is organized as follows. Random absolutely continuous invariant measures are identified in Section
2
, and their quenched statistical stability is established in Section
3
. Section
4
presents results on linear response for bounded observables, including the random setting in Theorem

and Corollary

, and the sequential setting in Theorem

. A general result on non-uniqueness of sequential a.c.i.m.s is presented in Section

, Proposition

.
Section

observables.

2.1
Preliminaries

For each parameter
\alpha\in[0,1)

We have that

\int_{0}^{1}(\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}\phi)\psi\,dm=\int_{0}^{1}\phi(\psi\circ T_{\alpha})\,dm,

(1)

for
\phi\in L^{1}(m)
and
\psi\in L^{\infty}(m)

\int_{0}^{1}\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}\phi\,dm=\int_{0}^{1}\phi\,dm,\quad\phi\in L^{1}(m).

(2)

Denoting, for

f_{\alpha}(x)=x(1+2^{\alpha}x^{\alpha})\in[0,1]
the first branch of

, we introduce its inverse
g_{\alpha}=f_{\alpha}^{-1}:[0,1]\to[0,1/2]

\alpha\in[0,1]

, so that

\frac{1}{3}\leq g^{\prime}_{\alpha}(x)\leq 1\quad\text{and}\quad\frac{x}{3}\leq g_{\alpha}(x)\leq x.

(3)

Let

be the transfer operator associated to

. Then,

N_{\alpha}\phi:=g_{\alpha}^{\prime}\phi\circ g_{\alpha}.

(4)

Following
[
8
]
, we will also denote

and

X_{\alpha}:=v_{\alpha}\circ g_{\alpha}=2^{\alpha}g_{\alpha}^{1+\alpha}\log(2g_{\alpha}).

(5)

&\leq C_{\alpha}|\beta_{2}(\sigma^{j-1-n}\omega)-\beta_{1}(\sigma^{j-1-n}\omega)|\dfrac{1}{(n-j)^{1/\alpha-1}}\|\psi\|_{L^{\infty}}\\
&\leq C_{\alpha}\varepsilon\dfrac{1}{(n-j)^{1/\alpha-1}}\|\psi\|_{L^{\infty}},\end{split}

with the convention that

Hence, for

-a.e.

since the series

4
Quenched linear response

In this section, we establish a quenched linear response for families of perturbed intermittent random systems.
Let
0<\underline{\alpha}<\alpha<\frac{1}{2}
and
\varepsilon_{0}>0
.
Take
\beta\colon\Omega\to(0,1)
,
\delta\colon\Omega\to[0,1)
measurable maps such that

\underline{\alpha}\leq\essinf_{\omega\in\Omega}\beta(\omega)-\varepsilon_{0}\quad\text{and}\quad\esssup_{\omega\in\Omega}\beta(\omega)+\varepsilon_{0}\leq\alpha.

(25)

By
(h_{\varepsilon}(\omega))_{\omega\in\Omega}
we will denote the random a.c.i.m associated with the cocycle

(T_{\beta(\omega)+\varepsilon\delta(\omega)})_{\omega\in\Omega}
for
\varepsilon\in(-\varepsilon_{0},\varepsilon_{0})
. It follows from Proposition
9
and its proof that

is uniquely determined for
\omega\in\Omega^{\prime}
and each
\varepsilon\in(-\varepsilon_{0},\varepsilon_{0})
, where
\Omega^{\prime}\subset\Omega
is a
\sigma
-invariant set of full measure such that

\underline{\alpha}\leq\beta(\omega)-\varepsilon_{0}\quad\text{and}\quad\beta(\omega)+\varepsilon_{0}\leq\alpha,

for each
\omega\in\Omega^{\prime}
. In the sequel, we will also write

instead of

.

The main result is the following.

Theorem 14
.

For
\omega\in\Omega^{\prime}
and
\psi\in L^{\infty}(m)
,

\begin{split}&\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{1}\psi\left(h_{\varepsilon}(\omega)-h(\omega)\right)dm\\
&=-\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\delta(\sigma^{-(i+1)}\omega)\int_{0}^{1}\psi\,\mathcal{L}_{\sigma^{-i}\omega}^{i}\left(X_{\beta(\sigma^{-(i+1)}\omega)}N_{\beta(\sigma^{-(i+1)}\omega)}(h(\sigma^{-(i+1)}\omega))\right)^{\prime}\,dm,\end{split}

4.1
Linear response for sequential dynamics and non-uniqueness of sequential a.c.i.m

The arguments developed in the previous sections apply also to sequential dynamics formed by nonautonomous compositions of the form

T_{\beta_{k+n-1}}\circ\ldots\circ T_{\beta_{k+1}}\circ T_{\beta_{k}},

where
k\in\mathbb{Z}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
(\beta_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\subset(0,1)

Proposition 23
.

Let
(\beta_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\subset(0,1)
be such that
\alpha:=\sup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\beta_{k}<1
. Then, there exists a sequence
(h_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\subset\mathcal{C}_{\ast}(a)\cap\mathcal{C}_{2}
such that

\mathcal{L}_{k}h_{k}=h_{k+1}\quad\text{and}\quad\int_{0}^{1}h_{k}\,dm=1\quad\text{for $k\in\mathbb{Z}$,}

(47)

where
\mathcal{L}_{k}
denotes the transfer operator associated with
T_{\beta_{k}}
. Moreover,
(h_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
is the unique sequence in
\mathcal{C}_{\ast}(a)
satisfying (
47
).

Proof.

The proof can be established by arguing as in the proof of claim
1
of Proposition
9
, and by replacing
\psi_{n}^{\omega}
with
\psi_{n}^{k}=\mathcal{L}_{k-n}^{n}1
, where

\mathcal{L}_{n}^{m}=\mathcal{L}_{m+n-1}\circ\ldots\circ\mathcal{L}_{n+1}\circ\mathcal{L}_{n},\quad\text{for $n\in\mathbb{Z}$ and $m\in\mathbb{N}$.}

∎

The sequence
(h_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
given by Proposition
23
can be regarded as a sequential a.c.i.m associated with the sequence
(T_{\beta_{k}})_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
.
In fact, the argument in the previous proof shows that
(h_{k}dm)_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
is the unique SRB state in the sense of Ruelle
[
29
, Section 4]
. Namely,
\mu_{k}:=h_{k}\,dm
satisfies

\mu_{k}=\lim_{n\to\infty}T_{\beta_{k-n}}^{*}T_{\beta_{k-n+1}}^{*}\dots T_{\beta_{k-1}}^{*}m.

However, the analogy one can draw with the deterministic case is subtle, as there are infinitely many sequential a.c.i.m’s with densities in
L^{1}(m)
. This is a consequence of the following general result:

Proposition 24

(Non-uniqueness of sequential a.c.i.m)
.

Assume
(\mathcal{L}_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
is a sequence of transfer operators in
L^{1}(m)
, associated with surjective, finite-branched and nonsingular maps
T_{n}\colon[0,1]\to[0,1]
with derivative bounded above and away from zero.
Suppose
(h_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
is a sequence of densities in
L^{1}(m)
such that
\mathcal{L}_{n}h_{n}=h_{n+1}
for
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. Furthermore, assume that there exists
c>0
such that
h_{0}\geq c>0
.
Then, there exist uncountably many sequences of densities
(\tilde{h}_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\subset L^{1}(m)
verifying
\mathcal{L}_{n}\tilde{h}_{n}=\tilde{h}_{n+1}
for
n\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Proof.

Let
0\neq\psi\in L^{\infty}
be such that
\|\psi\|_{L^{\infty}}<c
and
\int_{0}^{1}\psi dm=0
. Observe that there are uncountably many possible choices.
Let
\tilde{h}_{0}=h_{0}+\psi
. Then
\tilde{h}_{0}\neq h_{0}
is a density.
For
j>0
, let
\tilde{h}_{j}=\mathcal{L}_{j}\tilde{h}_{j-1}
.
For
j<0
, let
\tilde{h}_{j}
be a density such that
\mathcal{L}_{j}\tilde{h}_{j}=\tilde{h}_{j+1}
. This choice is possible, as
\mathcal{L}_{j}
is onto in
L^{1}
.
In fact, if
h\in L^{1}
, then
\eta(x):=\tfrac{1}{n(x)}|T_{j}^{\prime}(x)|h(T_{j}(x))
, where
n(x)
is the number of preimages of
x
under
T_{j}
satisfies
\eta\in L^{1}
and

\mathcal{L}_{j}(\eta)=h
. Furthermore, if
h
is a density, so is
\eta
.
In this way,
(\tilde{h}_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\subset L^{1}(m)
is a sequence of densities satisfying
\mathcal{L}_{n}\tilde{h}_{n}=\tilde{h}_{n+1}
for
n\in\mathbb{Z}
.
∎

Our intermittent sequential dynamics satisfies the assumptions in Proposition
24
. Indeed, it is sufficient to observe that the arguments in the proof of Lemma
26
apply here as well: one can then argue as in the proof of claim
2
of Proposition
9
, and conclude that there exists
c>0
such that
h_{k}\geq c
for
k\in\mathbb{Z}
.

We emphasize that the above construction is applicable to the case where
(\beta_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
is a constant sequence. Therefore, even if our sequential dynamics is generated by compositions of a single map
T_{\beta}
with itself, one still has infinitely many sequential a.c.i.m’s. However, only one of them lies in
\mathcal{C}_{\ast}(a)
and is the constant sequence
(h)_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
, where
h
is the density of the unique (deterministic) a.c.i.m for
T_{\beta}
.

On the other hand, we observe that each sequential a.c.i.m
(\bar{h}_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\subset L^{1}(m)
satisfies the following:

\lim_{k\to\infty}\|h_{k}-\bar{h}_{k}\|_{L^{1}(m)}=0,

(48)

where
(h_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\subset\mathcal{C}_{*}(a)
is the unique SRB state from Proposition
23
.
Indeed, choose
\varepsilon>0
arbitrary and let
\varphi\in C^{1}[0,1]
be such that
\|\varphi-\bar{h}_{0}\|_{L^{1}(m)}\leq\varepsilon
. We can assume that
\int_{0}^{1}\varphi\,dm=1
. Then,

\begin{split}\|h_{k}-\bar{h}_{k}\|_{L^{1}(m)}=\|\mathcal{L}_{0}^{k}(h_{0}-\bar{h}_{0})\|&\leq\|\mathcal{L}_{0}^{k}(h_{0}-\varphi)\|_{L^{1}(m)}+\|\mathcal{L}_{0}^{k}(\varphi-\bar{h}_{0})\|_{L^{1}(m)}\\

&\leq\|\mathcal{L}_{0}^{k}(h_{0}-\varphi)\|_{L^{1}(m)}+\varepsilon.\end{split}

Next, as
\varphi
is Hölder and
h_{0}\in\mathcal{C}_{\ast}(a)
, it follows from
[
23
, Theorem 1.1]
that
\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}\|\mathcal{L}_{0}^{k}(h_{0}-\varphi)\|_{L^{1}(m)}=0
. Since
\varepsilon>0
was arbitrary, we conclude that (
48
) holds.

Since under the assumptions of Proposition
23
we have the uniqueness of sequential a.c.i.m in the cone
\mathcal{C}_{\ast}(a)
, which coincides with Ruelle’s SRB state, it makes sense to consider the linear response restricted to the class of sequential a.c.i.m’s which belong to this cone.
The following result can be established by repeating the arguments in the proof of Theorem
14
.

Theorem 25
.

Let
0<\underline{\alpha}<\alpha<\frac{1}{2}
and
\varepsilon_{0}>0
. For each
k\in\mathbb{Z}
, assume
\beta_{k},\delta_{k}

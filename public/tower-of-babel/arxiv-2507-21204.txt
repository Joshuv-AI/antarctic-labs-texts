Introduction.

Here we present a parallel variant of PLL for reachability on DAGs.
This variant was developed by the author during a seminar at the Karlsruhe Institute of Technology (KIT), with the specific goal of reducing the preprocessing for hub labeling on large time-expanded graphs; which model public transit networks.

Parallelization Challenges.

As the algorithm is inherently sequential, parallelization is not trivial.
Note that for directed graphs, HL stores two labels per vertex,
L_{{\mathrm{fwd}}}
and
L_{{\mathrm{bwd}}}
.
Algorithmically, this means the BFS searches have to performed twice; once on the forward graph
G
, and once on the reverse graph
G^{\mathrm{rev}}
.
The algorithm is inherently sequential, and simply parallelizing the BFS (see Algorithm 1) only provides a limited speedup, since the average node degree is relatively low.
Running
PrunedBFS
from multiple vertices simultaneously loses the pruning property, and can produce very large labels under certain circumstances.

Instead of parallelizing a single BFS, multiple BFSs are executed simultaneously.
To allow for pruning, a thread
t
processing
\textnormal{{PrunedBFS}}(v_{t})
must determine during the relaxation of an edge
(u,v)\in E
whether the path
v_{t}\to v
is already covered or if another thread will cover it (using a more important hub).
To achieve this, we introduce the following approach:

New Parallelization Scheme.

We use two bitsets
b_{{\mathrm{bwd}}},b_{{\mathrm{fwd}}}:V\to 2^{K}
.
After Algorithm
3
, bitset
b_{{\mathrm{bwd}}}[u][j]
stores whether the
j
th starting vertex can reach
u
, and analogously
b_{{\mathrm{fwd}}}[u][j]
whether
u
can reach the
j
th starting vertex.
When thread
t\in[1,K]
processes vertex
v_{t}\in V
, we extend the pruning rule of Algorithm
2
by the following condition when relaxing edge
(u,v)\in E
:

If
\exists t^{\prime}\in[1,t)
, such that
b_{{\mathrm{fwd}}}[v_{k}][t^{\prime}]
and
b_{{\mathrm{bwd}}}[v][t^{\prime}]
, then
v_{k}\to v
will be covered by
t^{\prime}
, as
v_{t}\to v_{t^{\prime}}\to v
.
This can be easily checked by using bit-operations.

We note that Algorithm
3
is highly cache efficient, and the operations on bitsets have a high throughput.

The batch size
K
and thread assignment strategies can be tuned experimentally; we set
K\in\{128,256,512\}
, in order to make use of registers.
For larger graphs, the
\mathcal{O}(m)
topological sweep may become inefficient later on in the main algorithm.
We therefore relax the pruning and process the unimportant vertices in parallel.
While this may no longer yield strictly minimal labels, the increase in redundant labels is very small, as most of the hubs are created during the first few important searches.

Root monoids and active algebraic groups Thanks: The article was prepared within the framework of the project ‘‘International academic cooperation’’ HSE University.
Ekaterina Nistiuk (Presnova) and Yulia Zaitseva

Abstract
. We describe affine monoids whose group of invertible elements is an active semidirect product of a unipotent group and a torus, in terms of comultiplications on the algebra of regular functions. We introduce the notion of a root monoid, which is constructed from a set of Demazure root pairs on an affine toric variety, and study the properties of such monoids.

1.
Introduction

Let
X
be a normal irreducible algebraic variety, and let
X\times X\rightarrow X,(x,y)\mapsto x*y
be a given morphism. Then
X
is called an
algebraic monoid
if for all
x,y,z\in X
one has
x*(y*z)=(x*y)*z
and there exists a point
1\in X
such that
x*1=1*x=x
. In this case,
1
is called the
neutral element
. The group of invertible elements
G(X)
of an algebraic monoid
X
is an algebraic group and is Zariski-open in
X
. General properties of algebraic monoids can be found in
[
Br-1
,
Br-2
,
Pu
,
Re
,
Ri1
,
Ri2
,
Vin
]
.

We will be interested in affine algebraic monoids, i.e. those monoids whose underlying variety is affine. Let the base field
\mathbb{K}
be algebraically closed and of characteristic zero. In
[
ABZ
]
, commutative monoid structures on affine space
\mathbb{A}^{n}
were studied. In particular, a classification of commutative monoid structures on
\mathbb{A}^{3}
was obtained, and in the recent preprint
[
AAZ
]
the classification in the noncommutative case was completed. In
[
DZ
]
, a description for commutative monoids with group of invertible elements of corank
1
on arbitrary normal affine varieties was obtained, that is, in the case when the codimension of the maximal torus
T
in
G(X)
is
1
. The case of noncommutative monoids on surfaces was considered in
[
Bil
]
. The work
[
YZ
]
generalizes the results of
[
Bil
,
DZ
]
to the case of a noncommutative group of invertible elements of corank
1
, i.e. for
G(X)\cong\mathbb{G}_{a}\leftthreetimes T
, where
\mathbb{G}_{a}=(\mathbb{K},+)
is the additive group of the ground field. In particular, in
[
YZ
]
it was shown that
X
is a toric variety, and a description of all affine monoids of corank
1
was obtained in terms of comultiplications
\mathbb{K}[X]\rightarrow\mathbb{K}[X]\otimes\mathbb{K}[X]
. These comultiplications are of the form

\chi^{u}\mapsto\chi^{u}\otimes\chi^{u}(1\otimes\chi^{e_{1}}+\chi^{e_{2}}\otimes 1)^{\langle p,u\rangle},

where the toric variety
X
corresponds to the cone
\sigma
,
p
is a primitive vector on a ray of the cone
\sigma
, and
e_{1},e_{2}
are Demazure roots corresponding to
p
.

In
[
YZ
]
, the notion of an
active semidirect product

G=U\leftthreetimes T
was introduced, where
T
is a torus and
U
is the unipotent radical of the group
G
. Namely,
U\leftthreetimes T
is called
active
if
\dim T+\dim\mathop{\text{Im}}\psi=\dim G
, where
\psi\colon T\rightarrow\Aut U
is the homomorphism defining the semidirect product. In particular, the unipotent radical
U
of an active group is commutative (Lemma
5.4
), and hence
G\cong\mathbb{G}_{a}^{k}\leftthreetimes T
. In the present paper we introduce the class of root monoids, describe affine monoids with active groups of invertible elements, and study the properties of these structures.

The paper is organized as follows. In Sections
2.1
and
2.2
we recall the necessary background on toric varieties, Demazure roots, and their relation with
\mathbb{G}_{a}
-actions. Section
3
describes the structure of the group
G_{\overline{\chi}}=\mathbb{G}_{a}^{k}\leftthreetimes T
, including an explicit formula for the comultiplication. The group
G_{\overline{\chi}}
is determined by a set of characters
\overline{\chi}=(\chi_{1},\ldots,\chi_{k})
(see Lemma
3.1
).

In Section
4
we introduce the notion of a
root monoid
. This is a monoid structure on affine toric varieties with group of invertible elements
G_{\overline{\chi}}
. The structure depends on the cone
\sigma
of the corresponding toric variety
X_{\sigma}
, on a regular face
\tau\subset\sigma
, and on a set of Demazure roots compatible with
\tau
. A set of Demazure roots
\{e_{1}^{(r)},e_{2}^{(r)}\mid r=1,\ldots,k\}
is said to be
compatible
with the cone
\tau
if for the primitive vectors
p_{1},\ldots,p_{k}
on the one-dimensional faces of the cone
\tau
one has
\langle p_{s},e_{1}^{(r)}\rangle=\langle p_{s},e_{2}^{(r)}\rangle=-\delta_{rs}
, where
\delta_{rs}
is the Kronecker symbol,
r,s=1,\ldots,k
.

More precisely, we prove the following theorem.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
X=X_{\sigma}
be an affine toric variety, where the cone
\sigma
contains a
k
-dimensional regular face
\tau\subset\sigma
, and let
\{e_{1}^{(r)},e_{2}^{(r)}\mid r=1,\ldots,k\}
be a set of Demazure roots compatible with
\tau
. Then the map
\mathbb{K}[X]\rightarrow\mathbb{K}[X]\otimes\mathbb{K}[X]
given by

(1)

\chi^{u}\mapsto\chi^{u}\otimes\chi^{u}\prod_{r=1}^{k}(1\otimes\chi^{e_{1}^{(r)}}+\chi^{e_{2}^{(r)}}\otimes 1)^{\langle p_{r},u\rangle},

where
\mathbb{K}[X_{\sigma}]=\bigoplus_{u\in S_{\sigma}}\mathbb{K}\chi^{u}
, defines a comultiplication that determines a monoid structure on
X
with group of invertible elements
G_{\overline{\chi}}
, where
\chi_{r}=\chi^{e_{2}^{(r)}-e_{1}^{(r)}}
.

The monoids described in the theorem are called
root monoids
. This construction covers a number of previously known results and includes, as special cases, the monoids obtained in
[
DZ
,
Bil
,
YZ
]
.

In Section
5
we obtain a description of affine monoids with active group of invertible elements.

Theorem 1.2
.

Every affine algebraic monoid with an active group of invertible elements is isomorphic to a root monoid for some cone
\sigma
, its
k
-dimensional regular face
\tau
, and a set of Demazure roots

\{e_{1}^{(r)},e_{2}^{(r)}\mid r=1,\ldots,k\},

compatible with
\tau
. Moreover, the group of invertible elements of a root monoid is active if and only if the differences
e_{2}^{(r)}-e_{1}^{(r)}
are linearly independent.

In Section
6
we specialize the obtained result to the case of affine space. The structure of a root monoid is determined by
2k(n-k)
nonnegative integers. The second example describes monoid structures on the cylinder over a quadratic cone.
In Section
7
we consider the decomposition of the root monoid
X_{\sigma}
into a union of orbits
O_{\gamma}
with respect to the acting torus, and for each face
\gamma
of the cone
\sigma
we describe the set
E_{\gamma}
of all idempotents in the orbit
O_{\gamma}
. A nontrivial answer appears in the case of those orbits
O_{\gamma}
for which, for every ray
p_{r}
not belonging to
\gamma
, exactly one of the corresponding Demazure roots
e_{1}^{(r)},e_{2}^{(r)}
lies in
\gamma^{\perp}
.
In Section
8
we show that
E_{\gamma}
is a
T
-orbit, and the closure of
E_{\gamma}
is a unipotent group orbit consisting of sets of the form
E_{\gamma^{\prime}}
for a collection of faces
\{\gamma^{\prime}\}
depending on
\tau
and on the Demazure roots
e_{1}^{(r)},e_{2}^{(r)}
, see Theorem
8.1
.
Finally, Section
9
is devoted to the description of the center of an active monoid
X_{\sigma}
. It is contained in the closure of the orbit
O_{\tau}
, and we find equations defining the center of the active monoid in
\overline{O_{\tau}}
. In the case of monoids of corank 1 studied in
[
YZ
]
, every root monoid is either commutative or active. In the case of corank greater than 1, intermediate situations are possible.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Toric varieties

In this section we introduce notation and briefly describe the main results on the structure of affine toric varieties. A more detailed exposition can be found in
[
CLS
]
.

Let
N
be a lattice of rank
n
, and let
N_{\mathbb{Q}}=N\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Q}
be the corresponding rational vector space of dimension
n
. Every strongly convex polyhedral cone
\sigma\subseteq N_{\mathbb{Q}}
corresponds to an affine toric variety
X_{\sigma}
.

Recall that
M=\text{Hom}(N,\mathbb{Z})
is the dual lattice,
M_{\mathbb{Q}}=M\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Q}
is the corresponding rational
n
-dimensional vector space, and
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle\colon N_{\mathbb{Q}}\otimes M_{\mathbb{Q}}\rightarrow\mathbb{Q}
is the natural pairing. We identify elements of the lattice
M
with characters of the torus
\mathbb{T}=\text{Hom}(M,\mathbb{K}^{\times})
. The character corresponding to
u\in M
is denoted by
\chi^{u}\colon\mathbb{T}\rightarrow\mathbb{K}^{\times}
.

Given the cone
\sigma
, we construct the dual cone

\sigma^{\vee}=\{u\in M_{\mathbb{Q}}\mid\langle v,u\rangle\geq 0\text{ for all }v\in\sigma\}\subseteq M_{\mathbb{Q}}.

Then
S_{\sigma}=\sigma^{\vee}\cap M
is a finitely generated semigroup,
\mathbb{K}[S_{\sigma}]=\bigoplus_{u\in S_{\sigma}}\mathbb{K}\chi^{u}
is a finitely generated
\mathbb{K}
-algebra, the toric variety
X_{\sigma}
is the spectrum of this algebra, and
\mathbb{T}
is the acting torus. Moreover, points of the variety
X_{\sigma}
correspond to semigroup homomorphisms
S_{\sigma}\rightarrow\mathbb{K}
, where
\mathbb{K}
is regarded as a semigroup under multiplication. Indeed, a semigroup homomorphism
S_{\sigma}\rightarrow\mathbb{K}
determines an algebra homomorphism
\mathbb{K}[X_{\sigma}]\rightarrow\mathbb{K}
, which corresponds to a point of
X_{\sigma}
.

A cone
\sigma
is called regular if the primitive vectors
p_{1},\ldots,p_{k}
on its rays form part of a basis of the lattice
N
. In this case,
X_{\sigma}
is isomorphic to the direct product of an affine space of dimension
k
and a torus of dimension
n-k
.

It is well known that the orbits of the torus
\mathbb{T}
acting on the variety
X_{\sigma}
correspond to the faces of the cone
\sigma
. Moreover, for each face
\tau\subseteq\sigma
there exists a distinguished point
x_{\tau}\in X_{\sigma}
corresponding to the following semigroup homomorphism:

(2)

u\mapsto\begin{cases}1,\text{ if }u\in\tau^{\perp}\\
0\text{ otherwise.}\end{cases}

The orbit of the point
x_{\tau}
under the torus action is denoted
O_{\tau}
. In terms of semigroup homomorphisms,
O_{\tau}
consists exactly of those homomorphisms
S_{\sigma}\rightarrow\mathbb{K}
such that

(3)

u\mapsto\begin{cases}\mathbb{K}^{\times},\text{ if }u\in\tau^{\perp}\\
0\text{ otherwise.}\end{cases}

It is known that in this way one can describe all torus orbits on the toric variety
X_{\sigma}
.

Every semigroup homomorphism
S_{\sigma}\to S_{\sigma^{\prime}}
defines an algebra homomorphism
\mathbb{K}[S_{\sigma}]\to\mathbb{K}[S_{\sigma^{\prime}}]
and a morphism
\Spec\mathbb{K}[S_{\sigma^{\prime}}]\to\Spec\mathbb{K}[S_{\sigma}]
. In particular, if
\tau\subseteq\sigma
, then
S_{\tau}\supseteq S_{\sigma}
, which determines a morphism
X_{\tau}\to X_{\sigma}
. One can show that the morphism
X_{\tau}\to X_{\sigma}
is an open embedding if and only if
\tau
is a face of the cone
\sigma
. Moreover, in this case
S_{\tau}=S_{\sigma}+\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}(-u^{\prime})
, where
u^{\prime}\in M
and
\tau=\sigma\cap u^{\prime\perp}
, or equivalently,
u^{\prime}\in M
belongs to the relative interior of the dual face
\tau^{\perp}\cap\sigma^{\vee}
of the cone
\sigma^{\vee}
. Then
\mathbb{K}[S_{\tau}]
is the localization of
\mathbb{K}[S_{\sigma}]
by
\chi^{u^{\prime}}
, which defines the embedding
X_{\tau}\subseteq X_{\sigma}
as a principal open subset. In terms of semigroup homomorphisms,
X_{\tau}
consists of those points
S_{\sigma}\to\mathbb{K}
such that the image of
u^{\prime}
is nonzero, or equivalently, such that the image of any
u^{\prime}\in M
from the relative interior of
\tau^{\perp}\cap\sigma^{\vee}
is nonzero. In particular, for all faces
\tau
of the cone
\sigma
the torus actions on
X_{\tau}
as toric varieties are compatible and have the open orbit
X_{0}
.

2.2.
Derivations and Demazure roots

Let
A
be an algebra. A linear operator
\delta\colon A\rightarrow A
is called a
derivation
if it satisfies the Leibniz rule:

\delta(fg)=\delta(f)g+f\delta(g)
for all
f,g\in A
.

A derivation
\delta
is called
locally nilpotent
(LND) if for any element
f\in A
there exists a positive integer
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
such that
\delta^{n}(f)=0
. It is known that the exponential map establishes a bijection between the set of locally nilpotent derivations and rational
\mathbb{G}_{a}
-actions on the algebra
A
. In particular, an LND
\delta
corresponds to a
\mathbb{G}_{a}
-action
\varepsilon\colon\mathbb{G}_{a}\times A\rightarrow A
,
(\alpha,f)\mapsto\alpha\cdot f=\exp(\alpha\delta)(f)
, where
\alpha\in\mathbb{G}_{a}
,
f\in A
. Moreover, for an affine algebraic variety
X
there is a bijection between
\mathbb{G}_{a}
-actions on
X
and
\mathbb{G}_{a}
-actions on
\mathbb{K}[X]
.

Now suppose that the algebra
A
is graded,
A=\bigoplus_{u\in S}A_{u}
, where
S
is a semigroup. A derivation
\delta
is called
homogeneous
if it maps homogeneous elements to homogeneous ones. Then the
degree
of a homogeneous derivation is defined as an element
\deg\delta\in\mathbb{Z}S
, namely, for any
u\in S
one has
\delta(A_{u})\subseteq A_{u+\deg\delta}
.

Let
X_{\sigma}
be an affine toric variety, and let
p_{1},\ldots,p_{m}\in N
be the primitive vectors on the one-dimensional faces of the cone
\sigma
. For each
1\leq i\leq m
define the set

\mathfrak{R}_{i}=\{e\in M\mid\langle p_{i},e\rangle=-1,\,\langle p_{j},e\rangle\geq 0\text{ for all }j\neq i,\,1\leq j\leq m\}.

The elements of the set
\mathfrak{R}=\bigsqcup_{1\leq i\leq m}\mathfrak{R}_{i}
are called
Demazure roots
. It is easy to check that each set
\mathfrak{R}_{i}\subset M
is nonempty. It is known that Demazure roots are in one-to-one correspondence with nonzero homogeneous locally nilpotent derivations of the algebra
\mathbb{K}[X_{\sigma}]
, up to proportionality. Moreover, a Demazure root
e
is the degree of the corresponding homogeneous LND. In turn, homogeneous LNDs correspond to
\mathbb{G}_{a}
-actions normalized by the acting torus
\mathbb{T}
.

3.
Semidirect products

3.1.
Definition of the group
G_{\overline{\chi}}

Let
G
be a connected solvable affine algebraic group. Then
G=U\leftthreetimes T
, where
T
is a torus and
U
is the unipotent radical of
G
. Let
\psi\colon T\rightarrow\Aut U
be the homomorphism defining the semidirect product. It is known that
U
is isomorphic to an affine space
\mathbb{A}^{k}
as an affine variety.

Lemma 3.1
.

[
YZ
, Lemma 2]
One can introduce coordinates on
U
in such a way that
\psi(t)=\mathrm{diag}(\chi_{1}(t),\ldots,\chi_{k}(t))\in\GL(U)
, where
\chi_{1}(t),\ldots,\chi_{k}(t)
are characters of the torus
T
.

In this section we consider the case of a commutative unipotent radical, i.e.
U\cong\mathbb{G}_{a}^{k}
. Under the assumptions of Lemma
3.1
, we denote by
\overline{\alpha}=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{k})\in U
the corresponding coordinates, and by
G_{\overline{\chi}}
the group, where
\overline{\chi}=(\chi_{1},\ldots,\chi_{k})
is the set of characters. Denote
\beta_{r}=\chi_{r}(t)^{-1}\alpha_{r}
for
r=1,\ldots,k
. Then every element of the group
G_{\overline{\chi}}=\mathbb{G}_{a}^{k}\leftthreetimes T
can be represented as a product of elements from
U
and
T
in two ways:

g=\overline{\alpha}\cdot t=t\cdot\overline{\beta},

where
\overline{\beta}=(\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{k})
.

The multiplication on
G_{\overline{\chi}}
is given by

(4)

(\overline{\alpha}\cdot t)\cdot(\overline{\alpha^{\prime}}\cdot t^{\prime})=(\overline{\alpha}+\overline{\chi}(t)\overline{\alpha^{\prime}})\cdot tt^{\prime},

(5)

(t\cdot\overline{\beta})\cdot(t^{\prime}\cdot\overline{\beta^{\prime}})=tt^{\prime}\cdot(\overline{\beta}+\overline{\chi}^{-1}(t)\overline{\beta^{\prime}}).

Here the set of values of the
k
characters is multiplied coordinatewise by an element of
\mathbb{G}_{a}^{k}
. We denote by
\boldsymbol{\alpha_{r}},\boldsymbol{\beta_{r}}\in\mathbb{K}[G_{\overline{\chi}}]
the coordinate functions, where
\boldsymbol{\alpha_{r}}(g)=\alpha_{r}
and
\boldsymbol{\beta_{r}}(g)=\beta_{r}
.

3.2.
Cones and homogeneity of coordinate spaces

Let
n=\dim G_{\overline{\chi}}
. As an algebraic variety,
G_{\overline{\chi}}
is the direct product of the affine space
\mathbb{A}^{k}
and the torus
T
of dimension
n-k
. That is,
G_{\overline{\chi}}
is isomorphic to an affine toric variety with a regular
k
-dimensional cone
\tau
in the
n
-dimensional lattice. Recall that the algebra of regular functions is expressed as

(6)

\mathbb{K}[G_{\overline{\chi}}]=\bigoplus_{u\in S_{\tau}}\mathbb{K}\chi^{u}.

Consider the map
\theta\colon T\times T\rightarrow\Aut(G_{\overline{\chi}})
defined as follows: an element
(t_{1},t_{2})\in T\times T
acts on an element
g\in G_{\overline{\chi}}
by
t_{1}gt_{2}^{-1}
. Specifically, for an element
\overline{\alpha}\cdot t
we have

(7)

\theta(t_{1},t_{2})(\overline{\alpha}\cdot t)=t_{1}\cdot\overline{\alpha}\cdot t\cdot t_{2}^{-1}=\overline{\chi}(t_{1})\overline{\alpha}\cdot t_{1}tt_{2}^{-1}.

Denote
\mathbb{T}=\mathop{\text{Im}}\theta
. Note that
\Ker\theta=\text{diag}(\cap\Ker\chi_{j})\subset T\times T
, hence the value of the character
\chi_{r}(t_{1})
does not depend on the choice of representative
\theta(t_{1},t_{2})=\theta(t_{1}^{\prime},t_{2}^{\prime})\in\mathbb{T}
, so the map
\theta(t_{1},t_{2})\mapsto\chi_{r}(t_{1})
defines a character of the torus
\mathbb{T}
. Consequently, all coordinate functions
\boldsymbol{\alpha_{r}}
are homogeneous with respect to the grading (
6
), and thus, multiplying by a scalar, we can asssume
\boldsymbol{\alpha_{r}}=\chi^{a_{r}}
, where
a_{r}\in\tau^{\vee}
.
Similarly, we denote
\boldsymbol{\beta_{r}}=\chi^{b_{r}}
, where
b_{r}\in\tau^{\vee}
. Moreover, since
\boldsymbol{\beta_{r}}=\chi_{r}^{-1}\boldsymbol{\alpha_{r}}
, all characters
\chi_{r}^{-1}
of the torus
T
, which also correspond to characters of the torus
\mathbb{T}
, can be recovered from
a_{r},b_{r}\in M
, namely,
\chi_{r}=\chi^{a_{r}-b_{r}}
.

3.3.
Torus orbits

Note that the set

O_{r}=\{\overline{\alpha}\cdot t\in G_{\overline{\chi}}\mid\alpha_{r}=0,\;\alpha_{j}\neq 0\text{ for }j\neq r\}

is an
(n-1)
-dimensional orbit of the torus
\mathbb{T}
under the action (
7
). The codimension of such an orbit is
1
, hence
O_{r}
corresponds to a one-dimensional face
\rho_{r}
of the cone
\tau
, i.e. a ray. Let
p_{r}
be the primitive vector on the corresponding ray, and denote the orbit by
O_{p_{r}}
. Since the cone
\tau
is regular, all primitive vectors
p_{r}
form part of a basis of the lattice
N
.

We have already shown that the coordinate functions
\boldsymbol{\alpha_{r}}
correspond to characters
\chi^{a_{r}}
of the torus
\mathbb{T}
. Since the coordinate function
\boldsymbol{\alpha_{r}}
vanishes at all points of the orbit
O_{p_{r}}
, by the correspondence (
2
), we have
a_{r}\notin\rho_{r}^{\perp}
, hence
\langle p_{r},a_{r}\rangle\neq 0
. Moreover,
\langle p_{r},a_{r}\rangle>0
since
{a_{r}\in\tau^{\vee}\subset\rho_{r}^{\vee}}
. On the other hand, the function
\boldsymbol{\alpha_{r}}
does not vanish on points of the orbit
O_{p_{j}}
, so
a_{r}\in\rho_{j}^{\perp}
for
j\neq r
, which means that
\langle p_{j},a_{r}\rangle=0
.

Next, note that by formula (
7
) the set

O_{\tau}=\{\overline{\alpha}\cdot t\in G_{\overline{\chi}}\mid\alpha_{r}=0\;\;\forall r\}=T

is an
(n-k)
-dimensional orbit under the action of
\mathbb{T}
. Consider the sublattice
{M(\tau)=\tau^{\perp}\cap M}
. Then the torus
\text{Hom}(M(\tau),\mathbb{K}^{\times})
can be identified with the torus
T=O_{\tau}
, hence
\mathbb{K}[T]=\mathbb{K}[M(\tau)].

Since
\mathbb{K}[G_{\overline{\chi}}]=\mathbb{K}[T][\boldsymbol{\alpha_{1}}]\ldots[\boldsymbol{\alpha_{k}}]
, the semigroup
S_{\tau}
is generated by the sublattice
M(\tau)
together with all
a_{r}\in M
. Then any element
u\in S_{\tau}
can be written as the sum of an element from
\tau^{\perp}
and an integer linear combination of the elements
a_{r}
. Consequently,
\langle p_{r},u\rangle
is divisible by
\langle p_{r},a_{r}\rangle
, which means that
S_{\tau}
is not a saturated semigroup when
\langle p_{r},a_{r}\rangle>1
. Thus,
\langle p_{r},a_{r}\rangle=1
.

Similarly,
\langle p_{r},b_{r}\rangle=1
. Hence,
a_{r}-b_{r}\in\tau^{\perp}
.

Proposition 3.2
.

The comultiplication
\mathbb{K}[G_{\overline{\chi}}]\rightarrow\mathbb{K}[G_{\overline{\chi}}]\otimes\mathbb{K}[G_{\overline{\chi}}]
is given by

(8)

\chi^{u}\mapsto\chi^{u}\otimes\chi^{u}\prod_{r=1}^{k}(1\otimes\boldsymbol{\alpha_{r}}^{-1}+\boldsymbol{\beta_{r}}^{-1}\otimes 1)^{\langle p_{r},u\rangle},

where
u\in S_{\tau}
,
\mathbb{K}[G_{\overline{\chi}}]=\bigoplus\limits_{u\in S_{\tau}}\mathbb{K}\chi^{u}.

Proof.

Recall that the pairing
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
is linear in each argument, and for characters we have
\chi^{u_{1}+u_{2}}=\chi^{u_{1}}\chi^{u_{2}}
. Therefore, it is enough to verify formula (
8
) for generators of the semigroup
S_{\tau}
, i.e., for
u\in M(\tau)
and for
u=a_{r}
.

Since we previously identified
\Hom(M(\tau),\mathbb{K}^{\times})
with the torus
T\subseteq G_{\overline{\chi}}
, for all
u\in M(\tau)
, i.e. for the characters
\chi^{u}\in\mathbb{K}[T]
, the comultiplication takes the form
\chi^{u}\mapsto\chi^{u}\otimes\chi^{u}
. This coincides with formula (
8
) because
\langle p_{r},u\rangle=0
.

Now let us check the case
u=a_{r}
, i.e.
\chi^{u}=\chi^{a_{r}}=\boldsymbol{\alpha_{r}}
. Since multiplication in the group
G_{\overline{\chi}}
is given by formula (
4
), we have

\boldsymbol{\alpha_{r}}\mapsto\boldsymbol{\alpha_{r}}\otimes 1+\chi_{r}(t)\otimes\boldsymbol{\alpha_{r}}=\boldsymbol{\alpha_{r}}\otimes\boldsymbol{\alpha_{r}}(1\otimes\boldsymbol{\alpha_{r}}^{-1}+\chi_{r}(t)\boldsymbol{\alpha_{r}}^{-1}\otimes 1).

To complete the proof, it remains to note that
\chi_{r}(t)\boldsymbol{\alpha_{r}}^{-1}=\boldsymbol{\beta_{r}}^{-1}
and
\langle p_{j},a_{r}\rangle=\delta_{rj}
, where
\delta_{rj}
is the Kronecker symbol.

4.
Root monoids

4.1.
Classification of root monoids

In this section we introduce the structure of a monoid with group of invertible elements
G_{\overline{\chi}}
on affine toric varieties.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
\tau
be a regular
k
-dimensional face of the cone
\sigma
, and let
p_{1},\ldots,p_{k}
be the primitive vectors on the one-dimensional faces of
\tau
. We call a set of Demazure roots of the cone
\sigma

\{e_{1}^{(r)},e_{2}^{(r)}\mid r=1,\ldots,k\}

compatible with the face
\tau
if
\langle p_{s},e_{1}^{(r)}\rangle=\langle p_{s},e_{2}^{(r)}\rangle=-\delta_{rs}
, where
\delta_{rs}
is the Kronecker symbol,
r,s=1,\ldots,k
.
In particular, this condition implies that
{e_{1}^{(r)},e_{2}^{(r)}\in\mathfrak{R}_{r}}
.

In Section
3.3
we identified the torus
T
with
\text{Hom}(M(\tau),\mathbb{K}^{\times})
. If Demazure roots are compatible with the face
\tau
, then
e_{2}^{(r)}-e_{1}^{(r)}\in\tau^{\perp}
, and therefore the characters
\chi_{r}:=\chi^{e_{2}^{(r)}-e_{1}^{(r)}}
of the torus
\mathbb{T}
, which acts on the variety
X_{\sigma}
, can be regarded as characters of the torus
T
. Thus, a compatible set of Demazure roots determines the structure of a semidirect product
G_{\overline{\chi}}=\mathbb{G}_{a}^{k}\leftthreetimes T
by Lemma
3.1
.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
X=X_{\sigma}
be an affine toric variety, the cone
\sigma
contain a
k
-dimensional regular face
\tau\subset\sigma
, and
\{e_{1}^{(r)},e_{2}^{(r)}\mid r=1,\ldots,k\}
be a set of Demazure roots compatible with
\tau
. Then the map
\mathbb{K}[X]\rightarrow\mathbb{K}[X]\otimes\mathbb{K}[X]
given by

(9)

\chi^{u}\mapsto\chi^{u}\otimes\chi^{u}\prod_{r=1}^{k}(1\otimes\chi^{e_{1}^{(r)}}+\chi^{e_{2}^{(r)}}\otimes 1)^{\langle p_{r},u\rangle},

where
\mathbb{K}[X_{\sigma}]=\bigoplus_{u\in S_{\sigma}}\mathbb{K}\chi^{u}
, defines a comultiplication that determines a monoid structure on
X
with group of invertible elements
G_{\overline{\chi}}
, where
\chi_{r}=\chi^{e_{2}^{(r)}-e_{1}^{(r)}}
. Moreover, the group of invertible elements coincides with the open subset
X_{\tau}
.

Definition 4.3
.

The monoids constructed in Theorem
4.2
are called
root monoids
.

Remark 4.4
.

Recall that a monoid
X
is called commutative if the multiplication on
X
is commutative, which is equivalent to commutativity of the group of invertible elements. The group
G_{\overline{\chi}}
is commutative if and only if
\overline{\chi}=\overline{0}
. Hence, the root monoid
X
is commutative if and only if
e_{1}^{(r)}=e_{2}^{(r)}
for all
r=1,\ldots,k.

4.2.
Proof of Theorem
4.2

First, let us show that formula (
9
) indeed defines a comultiplication on
\mathbb{K}[X]
.

Introduce some notation. Let
\overline{p}=(p_{1},\ldots,p_{k})
be the set of primitive vectors on the rays of the cone
\tau
. Define the generalized scalar product
\langle\overline{p},u\rangle=(\langle p_{1},u\rangle,\ldots,\langle p_{k},u\rangle)
.
We also denote by
\overline{i}=(i_{1},\ldots,i_{k})\in\mathbb{Z}^{k}
a tuple of nonnegative integers, and by

{\langle\overline{p},u\rangle\choose\overline{i}}={\langle p_{1},u\rangle\choose i_{1}}\cdot\ldots\cdot{\langle p_{k},u\rangle\choose i_{k}}

the product of binomial coefficients. Then formula (
9
) can be rewritten as

(10)

\chi^{u}\mapsto\chi^{u}\otimes\chi^{u}\prod_{r=1}^{k}(1\otimes\chi^{e_{1}^{(r)}}+\chi^{e_{2}^{(r)}}\otimes 1)^{\langle p_{r},u\rangle}=\sum\limits_{\overline{i}+\overline{j}=\langle\overline{p},u\rangle}{\langle\overline{p},u\rangle\choose\overline{i}}\chi^{u+\overline{i}\cdot\overline{e_{2}}}\otimes\chi^{u+\overline{j}\cdot\overline{e_{1}}}.

To check that the result of this operation belongs to
\mathbb{K}[X]\otimes\mathbb{K}[X]
, we need to verify that
u+\overline{i}\cdot\overline{e_{2}}=u+i_{1}e_{2}^{(1)}+\ldots+i_{k}e_{2}^{(k)}=u+\sum\limits_{r=1}^{k}i_{r}e_{2}^{(r)}\in S_{\sigma}
, which is equivalent to
\langle p_{s},u+\overline{i}\cdot\overline{e_{2}}\rangle\geq 0
and
\langle q,u+\overline{i}\cdot\overline{e_{2}}\rangle\geq 0
for the remaining primitive vectors
q
on the rays of the cone
\sigma
. Note that since
e_{2}^{(r)}
is a Demazure root compatible with
\tau
, we have
\langle p_{r},e_{2}^{(r)}\rangle=-1
,
\langle p_{s},e_{2}^{(r)}\rangle=0
for
r\neq s
, and its pairing with the other primitive vectors on the rays of
\sigma
is nonnegative. Let us first check
\langle p_{s},u+\overline{i}\cdot\overline{e_{2}}\rangle\geq 0
. In formula (
10
) the summation runs over those
\overline{i}
such that
i_{s}\leq\langle p_{s},u\rangle
. Hence,
\langle p_{s},u+\overline{i}\cdot\overline{e_{2}}\rangle=\langle p_{s},u\rangle+\langle p_{s},i_{s}e_{2}^{(s)}\rangle=\langle p_{s},u\rangle-i_{s}\geq 0
. The second condition
\langle q,u+\overline{i}\cdot\overline{e_{2}}\rangle\geq 0
also holds, because
\langle q,u\rangle\geq 0
and
\langle q,i_{r}e_{2}^{(r)}\rangle=i_{r}\langle q,e_{2}^{(r)}\rangle\geq 0
for all
r
. Thus,
\chi^{u+\overline{i}\cdot\overline{e_{2}}}\in\mathbb{K}[X]
, and similarly
\chi^{u+\overline{j}\cdot\overline{e_{1}}}\in\mathbb{K}[X]
.

Now let us check the existence of a neutral element. For a cone
\tau\subset\sigma
there exists (see Section
2.1
) a distinguished point
x_{\tau}\in X=X_{\sigma}
such that

(11)

\begin{cases}\chi^{u}(x_{\tau})=1,\text{ if }u\in\tau^{\perp}\\
\chi^{u}(x_{\tau})=0\text{ otherwise}.\end{cases}

Apply formula (
10
) to a pair of points
x,y\in X
:

(12)

\chi^{u}(x*y)=\sum\limits_{\overline{i}+\overline{j}=\langle\overline{p},u\rangle}{\langle\overline{p},u\rangle\choose\overline{i}}\chi^{u+\overline{i}\cdot\overline{e_{2}}}(x)\cdot\chi^{u+\overline{j}\cdot\overline{e_{1}}}(y).

In
\chi^{u}(x_{\tau}*x)
, the only nonzero terms are those for which
u+\overline{i}\cdot\overline{e_{2}}=u+i_{1}e_{2}^{(1)}+\ldots+i_{k}e_{2}^{(k)}\in\tau^{\perp}
, which is equivalent to
\langle p_{s},u+\overline{i}\cdot\overline{e_{2}}\rangle=0
for all
s=1,\ldots,k
. Since
\langle p_{s},e_{2}^{(r)}\rangle=-\delta_{rs}
, we obtain
\langle p_{s},u+\overline{i}\cdot\overline{e_{2}}\rangle=\langle p_{s},u+i_{s}e_{2}^{(s)}\rangle=0
, which is equivalent to
i_{s}=\langle p_{s},u\rangle
. Hence, exactly one term remains, corresponding to
\overline{i}=\langle\overline{p},u\rangle
. Therefore,

(13)

\chi^{u}(x_{\tau}*x)=\chi^{u+\langle\overline{p},u\rangle\cdot\overline{e_{2}}}(x_{\tau})\cdot\chi^{u}(x)=\chi^{u}(x).

Similarly,
\chi^{u}(x*x_{\tau})=\chi^{u}(x)
, so
x_{\tau}
is the neutral element.

Now let us show that the set of invertible elements of the monoid
X_{\sigma}
coincides with
X_{\tau}
. An element
x\in X_{\sigma}
is invertible if there exists
y\in X_{\sigma}
such that
x*y=x_{\tau}
, i.e.
\chi^{u}(x_{\tau})=\chi^{u}(x*y)
for all
u\in S_{\sigma}
. Take
u
from the relative interior of
\tau^{\perp}\cap\sigma^{\vee}
. Then
\langle p_{r},u\rangle=0
for all
r=1,\ldots,k
and
\chi^{u}(x_{\tau})=1
. Hence,

(14)

1=\chi^{u}(x_{\tau})=\chi^{u}(x*y)=\chi^{u}(x)\chi^{u}(y)\prod_{r=1}^{k}(\chi^{e_{1}^{(r)}}(y)+\chi^{e_{2}^{(r)}}(x))^{\langle p_{r},u\rangle}=\chi^{u}(x)\chi^{u}(y).

Thus,
\chi^{u}(x)\in\mathbb{K}^{\times}
. Using (
3
), we obtain
x\in X_{\tau}
.

Note that the variety
X_{\tau}
is isomorphic to the product of a
k
-dimensional affine space and an
(n-k)
-dimensional torus. Therefore, we can identify
X_{\tau}
with the group
G_{\overline{\chi}}\cong\mathbb{G}_{a}^{k}\leftthreetimes T
, where
\chi_{r}=\chi^{e_{2}^{(r)}-e_{1}^{(r)}}
. Indeed, since
\langle p_{s},e_{1}^{(r)}\rangle=\langle p_{s},e_{2}^{(r)}\rangle=-\delta_{rs}
, and because
-e_{1}^{(r)},-e_{2}^{(r)}\in\tau^{\vee}
, the characters
\boldsymbol{\alpha_{r}}:=\chi^{-e_{1}^{(r)}}
and
\boldsymbol{\beta_{r}}:=\chi^{-e_{2}^{(r)}}
are regular functions on
X_{\tau}
. Then the restriction of comultiplication (
9
) to the open subset
X_{\tau}
coincides with formula (
8
) for
\chi_{r}=\boldsymbol{\alpha_{r}}/\boldsymbol{\beta_{r}}
. Thus, the dual map
X_{\tau}\times X_{\tau}\to X_{\tau}
extends to a regular morphism
X\times X\to X
, with
X_{\tau}\cong G_{\overline{\chi}}
. Since multiplication in
G_{\overline{\chi}}
is associative and has a neutral element, the same holds for
X
. Therefore,
X
is an affine monoid with group of invertible elements
G_{\overline{\chi}}
.

4.3.
On the existence of root monoids

In this subsection we show that using the construction from Theorem
4.2
one can build an affine monoid with any prescribed group of invertible elements
G_{\overline{\chi}}
.

Proposition 4.5
.

For any group
G_{\overline{\chi}}=\mathbb{G}_{a}^{k}\leftthreetimes T
and any cone
\sigma
with a
k
-dimensional regular face
\tau
, there exists a monoid structure on
X_{\sigma}
whose group of invertible elements is isomorphic to
G_{\overline{\chi}}
.

Proof.

We prove that for any group
G_{\overline{\chi}}
one can choose Demazure roots in Theorem
4.2
in such a way that the introduced monoid structure has a group of invertible elements isomorphic to
G_{\overline{\chi}}
. In subsections
3.2
and
3.3
we showed that all characters have the form
\chi_{r}=\chi^{a_{r}-b_{r}}
with
a_{r}-b_{r}\in\tau^{\perp}
. At the same time,
a_{r}=-e_{1}^{(r)}
,
b_{r}=-e_{2}^{(r)}
in accordance with the proof of Theorem
4.2
. Thus, to complete the proof it remains to prove the following lemma.

Lemma 4.6
.

For any set
c_{1},\ldots,c_{k}\in\tau^{\perp}\cap M
, where
\tau
is a regular face of the cone
\sigma
, one can choose a set of Demazure roots
\{e_{1}^{(r)},e_{2}^{(r)}\}
compatible with
\tau
such that for all
r=1,\ldots,k
the equality
e_{1}^{(r)}-e_{2}^{(r)}=c_{r}
holds.

Proof.

Denote by
p_{1},\ldots,p_{k}
the primitive vectors on the rays
\rho_{1},\ldots,\rho_{k}
of the cone
\tau
. Fix
r
. Note that there exists
u_{r}\in M
such that

(15)

\langle p_{r},u_{r}\rangle=-1\text{ and }\langle p_{s},u_{r}\rangle=0\text{ for all }s\neq r.

We will show that one can choose two Demazure roots
e_{1}^{(r)},e_{2}^{(r)}
satisfying condition (
15
) in such a way that their difference equals
c_{r}\in\tau^{\perp}
. Recall that if
\gamma
is a face of the cone
\sigma
, then
\gamma^{*}=\gamma^{\perp}\cap\sigma^{\vee}
is a face of the dual cone
\sigma^{\vee}
, with
\dim\gamma+\dim\gamma^{*}=n=\dim\sigma
, see
[
CLS
, Proposition 1.2.10]
. Take
v\in M
from the relative interior of the face
\tau^{*}=\tau^{\perp}\cap\sigma^{\vee}
. Then
\langle p_{s},v\rangle=0
for all
s=1,\ldots,k
, and
\langle q,v\rangle>0
for all other primitive vectors
q
on the rays of the cone
\sigma
. Hence, for some
N\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
the element
u_{r}+Nv
is a Demazure root of the cone
\sigma
and satisfies condition (
15
). The same holds for any lattice point in the cone
u_{r}+Nv+\tau^{*}
. Therefore, within this cone one can choose two Demazure roots whose difference equals any prescribed vector
c_{r}\in\tau^{\perp}\cap M
.
∎

Proposition
4.5
is proved.
∎

5.
Active monoids

Definition 5.1
.

A semidirect product
G=U\leftthreetimes T
is called
active
if
\dim T+\dim\mathop{\text{Im}}\psi=\dim G
, where
\psi\colon T\to\Aut U
is the homomorphism defining the semidirect product.

The notion of an active semidirect product was introduced in
[
YZ
]
. Recall that by Lemma
3.1
one can introduce coordinates on
U
such that

\psi(t)=\mathrm{diag}(\chi_{1}(t),\ldots,\chi_{k}(t))\in\GL(U),

where
\chi_{1}(t),\ldots,\chi_{k}(t)
are characters of the torus
T
. The group
G
is active if and only if the characters
\chi_{1}(t),\ldots,\chi_{k}(t)
are linearly independent.

Remark 5.2
.

If
G=U\leftthreetimes T
is active, then
\dim U\leq\dim T
.

The main goal of this paper is the classification of affine monoids with active groups of invertible elements in terms of the comultiplication on the algebra of regular functions. In
[
YZ
]
it was shown that such monoids are toric varieties. Consider the map
\theta\colon T\times T\rightarrow\Aut(G)
defined as follows: an element
(t_{1},t_{2})\in T\times T
acts on an element
g\in G
by
t_{1}gt_{2}^{-1}
. Denote
\mathbb{T}=\mathop{\text{Im}}\theta
. Since the torus
\mathbb{T}
acts on
G
by left and right multiplications, and
X
is an affine monoid with group of invertible elements
G
, the action of the torus
\mathbb{T}
on
G
extends to an action of
\mathbb{T}
on
X
, see
[
Vin
, Theorem 1]
for the case of reductive monoids in characteristic
0
and
[
Ri1
, Proposition 1]
for the general case.

Proposition 5.3
.

[
YZ
, Proposition 2]

Let
X
be an affine monoid with active group of invertible elements
G
. Then
X
is a toric variety with respect to the action of the torus
\mathbb{T}
, and the group of invertible elements
G
is invariant under the action of
\mathbb{T}
.

We now show that if the semidirect product is active, then the unipotent radical is commutative.

Lemma 5.4
.

Let the semidirect product
U\leftthreetimes T
be active. Then the group
U
is commutative, and hence
U\cong\mathbb{G}_{a}^{k}
.

Proof.

Let the structure of the semidirect product
{G=U\leftthreetimes T}
be defined by a homomorphism
\psi\colon T\rightarrow\Aut(U)
, where
t\mapsto tut^{-1}=\phi_{u}
, which in turn induces a map
\mathrm{Ad}\colon T\rightarrow\Aut(\mathfrak{u})\subseteq\GL(\mathfrak{u})
,
t\mapsto d\phi_{u}
, where
\mathfrak{u}=\mathrm{T}_{e}U
is the tangent Lie algebra.

Choose a basis
e_{1},\ldots,e_{k}
in the algebra
\mathfrak{u}
in such a way that the torus acts diagonally:
\Ad(t)e_{i}=\chi_{i}(t)e_{i}
. Note that since all characters are linearly independent, the only eigenvectors are those proportional to the basis elements. To prove commutativity of the group
U
, it suffices to check commutativity of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{u}
, i.e. it suffices to show that
[e_{1},e_{2}]=0
. Observe that

\Ad(t)[e_{1},e_{2}]=[\Ad(t)e_{1},\Ad(t)e_{2}]=[\chi_{1}(t)e_{1},\chi_{2}(t)e_{2}]=\chi_{1}(t)\chi_{2}(t)[e_{1},e_{2}]=(\chi_{1}+\chi_{2})(t)[e_{1},e_{2}].

Thus
[e_{1},e_{2}]
is an eigenvector. But since all characters are linearly independent, the character
\chi_{1}+\chi_{2}
cannot be zero or coincide with one of
\chi_{1},\ldots,\chi_{k}
. Therefore,
[e_{1},e_{2}]=0
.
∎

Let us formulate one of the main results of this work.

Theorem 5.5
.

Any affine algebraic monoid with an active group of invertible elements is isomorphic to a root monoid corresponding to some cone
\sigma
, its
k
-dimensional regular face
\tau
, and a set of Demazure roots
\{e_{1}^{(r)},e_{2}^{(r)}\mid r=1,\ldots,k\}
compatible with
\tau
. Moreover, the group of invertible elements of the root monoid is active if and only if the differences
e_{2}^{(r)}-e_{1}^{(r)}
are linearly independent.

Proof.

Let
X
be an affine variety endowed with a monoid structure, and suppose that the group of invertible elements
G=U\leftthreetimes T
is active. Then
U
is commutative by Lemma
5.4
, and
G\cong G_{\overline{\chi}}
according to the notation of Section
3
. By Proposition
5.3
,
X
is an affine toric variety corresponding to a cone
\sigma
, and
G_{\overline{\chi}}
is also a toric variety
X_{\tau}
with respect to the action of the same torus
\mathbb{T}
, see subsection
3.2
. Since the embedding
G_{\overline{\chi}}=X_{\tau}\hookrightarrow X=X_{\sigma}
restricts to the identity map on the open torus orbit, the embedding
X_{\tau}\hookrightarrow X_{\sigma}
is an open embedding of toric varieties corresponding to the embedding
S_{\sigma}\hookrightarrow S_{\tau}
. Hence,
\tau
is a face of the cone
\sigma
.

Note that since the group of invertible elements
G_{\overline{\chi}}
is an open subset in
X
, the algebra of regular functions
\mathbb{K}[X]
is a subalgebra of
\mathbb{K}[G_{\overline{\chi}}]
. Therefore, the comultiplication on
\mathbb{K}[X]
is the restriction of the comultiplication
{\mathbb{K}[G_{\overline{\chi}}]\rightarrow\mathbb{K}[G_{\overline{\chi}}]\otimes\mathbb{K}[G_{\overline{\chi}}]}
corresponding to multiplication in the group
G_{\overline{\chi}}
(given by formula (
8
)).

Using the notation of formula (
8
), consider
e_{1}^{(r)}=-a_{r}
and
e_{2}^{(r)}=-b_{r}
for
r=1,\ldots,k
. We will show that
e_{1}^{(r)}
and
e_{2}^{(r)}
are Demazure roots of the cone
\sigma
.

Consider two
\mathbb{G}_{a}
-actions corresponding to the subgroup along the
r
-th coordinate
{\mathbb{G}_{a}\subset\mathbb{G}_{a}^{k}\leftthreetimes T\cong G_{\overline{\chi}}}
:

(16)

\begin{gathered}\mathbb{G}_{a}\times G_{\overline{\chi}}\rightarrow G_{\overline{\chi}},\;(\alpha_{r}^{\prime},\,\overline{\alpha}\cdot t)\mapsto(\overline{\alpha_{r}^{\prime}}+\overline{\alpha})\cdot t,\text{ where }\overline{\alpha_{r}^{\prime}}=(0,\ldots,\alpha_{r}^{\prime},\ldots,0);\\
G_{\overline{\chi}}\times\mathbb{G}_{a}\rightarrow G_{\overline{\chi}},\;(t\cdot\overline{\beta},\,\beta_{r}^{\prime})\mapsto t\cdot(\overline{\beta}+\overline{\beta_{r}^{\prime}}),\text{ where }\overline{\beta_{r}^{\prime}}=(0,\ldots,\beta_{r}^{\prime},\ldots,0).\end{gathered}

Take the corresponding LNDs on the algebra

\mathbb{K}[G_{\overline{\chi}}]=\mathbb{K}[T][\boldsymbol{\alpha_{1}}]\ldots[\boldsymbol{\alpha_{k}}]=\mathbb{K}[T][\boldsymbol{\beta_{1}}]\ldots[\boldsymbol{\beta_{k}}],

namely, differentiate the dual
\mathbb{G}_{a}
-actions on
\mathbb{K}[G_{\overline{\chi}}]
at
\alpha_{r}^{\prime}=0
and
\beta_{r}^{\prime}=0
, respectively:

\delta_{r}=\frac{d}{d\boldsymbol{\alpha_{r}}},\partial_{r}=\frac{d}{d\boldsymbol{\beta_{r}}}.

Since left and right multiplication on
G_{\overline{\chi}}
extends to multiplication on the whole
X_{\sigma}
, the algebra
\mathbb{K}[X_{\sigma}]
is invariant under the actions of
G_{\overline{\chi}}
by left and right multiplication, and
\mathbb{K}[X_{\sigma}]
is invariant under the derivations
\delta_{r},\partial_{r}
. Moreover,
\delta_{r},\partial_{r}
are homogeneous LNDs with respect to the grading
\mathbb{K}[X_{\sigma}]=\bigoplus_{u\in S_{\sigma}}\mathbb{K}\chi^{u}
and of degrees
e_{1}^{(r)},e_{2}^{(r)}
, since
\boldsymbol{\alpha_{r}}^{-1}=\chi^{e_{1}^{(r)}},\boldsymbol{\beta_{r}}^{-1}=\chi^{e_{2}^{(r)}}
. Hence,
e_{1}^{(r)}
and
e_{2}^{(r)}
are Demazure roots. Since
{\langle p_{s},e_{1}^{(r)}\rangle=\langle p_{s},e_{2}^{(r)}\rangle=-\delta_{rs}}
, these Demazure roots are compatible with the face
\tau
.

Substituting
\boldsymbol{\alpha_{r}}^{-1}=\chi^{e_{1}^{(r)}},\boldsymbol{\beta_{r}}^{-1}=\chi^{e_{2}^{(r)}}
into formula (
8
), we obtain

(17)

\chi^{u}\mapsto\chi^{u}\otimes\chi^{u}\prod_{r=1}^{k}(1\otimes\chi^{e_{1}^{(r)}}+\chi^{e_{2}^{(r)}}\otimes 1)^{\langle p_{r},u\rangle},

which coincides with the formula for comultiplication from Theorem
4.2
.
∎

Remark 5.6
.

In accordance with Theorem
5.5
and Proposition
4.5
, for any active group
G
with a
k
-dimensional unipotent radical and any cone
\sigma
with a
k
-dimensional regular face, there exists a monoid structure on
X_{\sigma}
with group of invertible elements isomorphic to
G
.

6.
Examples

6.1.
Affine space

In this section we describe all root monoid structures on the affine space
\mathbb{A}^{n}
.

Note that affine space is an affine toric variety
\mathbb{A}^{n}\cong X_{\sigma}
, where the primitive vectors on the rays of the cone
\sigma
form a basis of the lattice. Consider a
k
-dimensional face
\tau\subset\sigma
. Observe that all such faces are equivalent up to isomorphism, hence we may assume
\tau=\cone(p_{1},\ldots,p_{k})
, where
p_{r}=(0,\ldots,0,\overset{r}{1},0,\ldots,0)
. For all
r=1,\ldots,k
choose pairs of Demazure roots corresponding to the ray
\rho_{r}
as follows:

\displaystyle e_{1}^{(r)}=(\underbrace{0,\ldots,0,\overset{r}{-1},0,\ldots,0}_{k},a_{k+1}^{(r)},\ldots,a_{n}^{(r)}),

\displaystyle e_{2}^{(r)}=(\underbrace{0,\ldots,0,\overset{r}{-1},0,\ldots,0}_{k},b_{k+1}^{(r)},\ldots,b_{n}^{(r)}),

where
a_{k+1}^{(r)},\ldots,a_{n}^{(r)},b_{k+1}^{(r)},\ldots,b_{n}^{(r)}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
. Note that all sets of Demazure roots compatible with
\tau
are of this form. Now apply formula (
9
) to the basis vectors
u=u_{i}
corresponding to the coordinates
x_{i}
on the affine space
\mathbb{A}^{n}
, where
i=1,\ldots,n
. Denote by
\overline{a}_{r}
the tuple
(a_{k+1}^{(r)},\ldots,a_{n}^{(r)})
, and by
\overline{b}_{r}
the tuple
(b_{k+1}^{(r)},\ldots,b_{n}^{(r)})
, respectively. In addition, denote the monomial
x^{\overline{b}_{r}}=x_{k+1}^{b_{k+1}^{(r)}}\ldots x_{n}^{b_{n}^{(r)}}
, and similarly,
y^{\overline{a}_{r}}=y_{k+1}^{a_{k+1}^{(r)}}\ldots y_{n}^{a_{n}^{(r)}}
.

For
i=k+1,\ldots,n
we obtain
\chi^{u_{i}}(x*y)=\chi^{u_{i}}(x)\chi^{u_{i}}(y)=x_{i}y_{i}
, since in this case
\langle p_{r},u\rangle=0
. If
i=1,\ldots,k
, we obtain:

\chi^{u_{i}}(x*y)=\chi^{u_{i}}(x)\chi^{u_{i}}(y)(\chi^{e_{1}^{(i)}}(y)+\chi^{e_{2}^{(i)}}(x))=x_{i}y_{i}(y_{i}^{-1}y^{\overline{a}_{i}}+x_{i}^{-1}x^{\overline{b}_{i}})=x_{i}y^{\overline{a}_{i}}+y_{i}x^{\overline{b}_{i}}.

That is,

x*y=(x_{1}y^{\overline{a}_{1}}+y_{1}x^{\overline{b}_{1}},\ldots,x_{k}y^{\overline{a}_{k}}+y_{k}x^{\overline{b}_{k}},x_{k+1}y_{k+1},\ldots,x_{n}y_{n}).

The monoid constructed above is active if and only if the differences
{\overline{b}_{1}-\overline{a}_{1}},\ldots,\overline{b}_{k}-\overline{a}_{k}
are linearly independent. For example, the multiplication

(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4})*(y_{1},y_{2},y_{3},y_{4})=(x_{1}+y_{1}x_{3}x_{4}^{2},x_{2}y_{3}+y_{2}x_{3}^{3}x_{4}^{4},x_{3}y_{3},x_{4}y_{4})

defines a non-active root monoid on
\mathbb{A}^{4}
, whereas the multiplication

(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4})*(y_{1},y_{2},y_{3},y_{4})=(x_{1}+y_{1}x_{3}x_{4},x_{2}y_{3}+y_{2}x_{3}^{3}x_{4}^{4},x_{3}y_{3},x_{4}y_{4})

determines an active (root) monoid on
\mathbb{A}^{4}
.

6.2.
Cylinder over a quadratic cone

Consider the cone
\sigma
in the
4
-dimensional lattice
N
with primitive vectors

p_{1}=(1,0,0,0),\,p_{2}=(0,1,0,0),\,p_{3}=(0,0,1,0),\,p_{4}=(0,1,0,1),\,p_{5}=(1,0,0,1).

The dual cone
\sigma^{\vee}\subset M
is defined by the vectors

q_{1}=(1,0,0,0),\,q_{2}=(0,1,0,0),\,q_{3}=(0,0,1,0),\,q_{4}=(0,0,0,1),\,q_{5}=(1,1,0,-1),

which also generate the semigroup
S_{\sigma}=\sigma^{\vee}\cap M
. Consider the functions
x_{i}=\chi^{q_{i}}
. The algebra of regular functions
\mathbb{K}[X]
of the corresponding toric variety
X
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{K}[x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4},x_{5}]
with the relation
x_{1}x_{2}=x_{4}x_{5}
. Hence,

X=\{x_{1}x_{2}=x_{4}x_{5}\}\subseteq\mathbb{A}^{5},

that is,
X
is the direct product of the quadratic cone and the affine line.

Consider the two-dimensional regular cone
\tau=\cone(p_{1},p_{2})
. Choose Demazure roots compatible with the cone
\tau
(two for each primitive vector
p_{1},p_{2}
):

\begin{gathered}e_{1}^{(1)}=(-1,0,a_{1},b_{1}),\text{ where }a_{1}\geq 0,b_{1}>0,\\
e_{2}^{(1)}=(-1,0,a_{2},b_{2}),\text{ where }a_{2}\geq 0,b_{2}>0,\\
e_{1}^{(2)}=(0,-1,c_{1},d_{1}),\text{ where }c_{1}\geq 0,d_{1}>0,\\
e_{2}^{(2)}=(0,-1,c_{2},d_{2}),\text{ where }c_{2}\geq 0,d_{2}>0.\end{gathered}

With this choice of Demazure roots, formula (
9
) takes the form:

\chi^{u}\mapsto\chi^{u}\otimes\chi^{u}(1\otimes\chi^{e_{1}^{(1)}}+\chi^{e_{2}^{(1)}}\otimes 1)^{\langle p_{1},u\rangle}(1\otimes\chi^{e_{1}^{(2)}}+\chi^{e_{2}^{(2)}}\otimes 1)^{\langle p_{2},u\rangle}.

The multiplication on
X
is given by the formula

(18)

x*y=\bigl(x_{1}y_{3}^{a_{1}}y_{4}^{b_{1}}+y_{1}x_{3}^{a_{2}}x_{4}^{b_{2}},\;\;x_{2}y_{3}^{c_{1}}y_{4}^{d_{1}}+y_{2}x_{3}^{c_{2}}x_{4}^{d_{2}},\\
x_{3}y_{3},\;\;x_{4}y_{4},\;\;x_{5}y_{3}^{a_{1}+c_{1}}y_{4}^{b_{1}+d_{1}-1}+y_{5}x_{3}^{a_{2}+c_{2}}x_{4}^{b_{2}+d_{2}-1}+\\
+x_{1}x_{3}^{c_{2}}x_{4}^{d_{2}-1}y_{2}y_{3}^{a_{1}}y_{4}^{b_{1}-1}+y_{1}y_{3}^{c_{1}}y_{4}^{d_{1}-1}x_{2}x_{3}^{a_{2}}x_{4}^{b_{2}-1}\bigr).

The corresponding monoid is active if and only if two vectors

(a_{1}-a_{2},b_{1}-b_{2})
and
{(c_{1}-c_{2},d_{1}-d_{2})}
are linearly independent.

7.
Idempotents and torus orbits

Recall that an element
x\in X
is called an
idempotent
if
x*x=x
. Idempotents in algebraic monoids have been extensively studied in various works, see, for example,
[
Br-1
,
Br-2
,
N
,
Re
]
and the references therein. In particular, it is known that the subvariety of idempotents is smooth and is an orbit of the group of invertible elements under the action by conjugation, see
[
Br-2
, Theorem 1.1]
. In this section we describe all idempotents in each torus orbit for root monoids.

Theorem 7.1
.

In the notation of Theorem
4.2
, let
X_{\sigma}
be a root monoid,
\tau
the corresponding
k
-dimensional regular face of the cone
\sigma
with primitive vectors
p_{1},\ldots,p_{k}
on the one-dimensional faces. Let
\gamma
be a face of the cone
\sigma
. Denote by
E_{\gamma}
the set of idempotents lying in the orbit
O_{\gamma}
. Then:

(1)

if
\tau\subseteq\gamma
, then
E_{\gamma}=\{x_{\gamma}\}
;

(2)

if there exists a ray
p_{r}\notin\gamma
such that either corresponding Demazure roots
e_{1}^{(r)},e_{2}^{(r)}\notin\gamma^{\perp}
, or
e_{1}^{(r)},e_{2}^{(r)}\in\gamma^{\perp}
, then
E_{\gamma}=\varnothing
;

(3)

if for every such ray
p_{r}\notin\gamma
exactly one of the corresponding Demazure roots
e_{1}^{(r)},e_{2}^{(r)}
lies in
\gamma^{\perp}
, then

E_{\gamma}=O_{\gamma}\cap\{\chi^{u}=1\;\;\forall u\in\cone(\tau,\gamma)^{\perp}\cap S_{\sigma}\}.

Proof.

Note that
x\in X
is an idempotent if and only if
\chi^{u}(x*x)=\chi^{u}(x)
for every
u\in S_{\sigma}
.

(1) Suppose
\tau\subseteq\gamma
. Take
u\in\gamma^{\perp}\subseteq\tau^{\perp}
. Then
\langle p_{r},u\rangle=0
for all
r=1,\ldots,k
. By formula (
9
) we have
\chi^{u}(x*x)=\chi^{u}(x)\cdot\chi^{u}(x)
. Hence
x
can be an idempotent only if
\chi^{u}(x)=1
or
\chi^{u}(x)=0
. Since
x\in O_{\gamma},u\in\gamma^{\perp}
, by formula (
3
) we know
\chi^{u}(x)\neq 0
, so
\chi^{u}(x)=1
and
x=x_{\gamma}
. Note that therefore there is at most one idempotent in the orbit
O_{\gamma}
. Let us check that
x_{\gamma}
is indeed an idempotent. Now let
u\in\tau^{\perp},u\notin\gamma^{\perp}
. Then
\chi^{u}(x_{\gamma}*x_{\gamma})=\chi^{u}(x_{\gamma})\cdot\chi^{u}(x_{\gamma})=0\cdot 0=0=\chi^{u}(x_{\gamma})
. It remains to consider the case
u\notin\tau^{\perp}
. Recall that by Theorem
4.2

(19)

\chi^{u}(x*y)=\sum\limits_{\overline{i}+\overline{j}=\langle\overline{p},u\rangle}{\langle\overline{p},u\rangle\choose\overline{i}}\chi^{u+\overline{i}\cdot\overline{e_{2}}}(x)\cdot\chi^{u+\overline{j}\cdot\overline{e_{1}}}(y).

Let us show that each summand in the right-hand side of (
19
) is equal to
0
. Recall that we denote
u+\overline{i}\cdot\overline{e_{2}}=u+i_{1}e_{2}^{(1)}+\ldots+i_{k}e_{2}^{(k)}
. Then
\langle p_{s},u+\overline{i}\cdot\overline{e_{2}}\rangle=\langle p_{s},u\rangle-i_{s}
for any
s=1,\ldots,k
. Similarly
\langle p_{s},u+\overline{j}\cdot\overline{e_{1}}\rangle=\langle p_{s},u\rangle-j_{s}
. Since
\overline{i}+\overline{j}=\langle\overline{p},u\rangle\neq\overline{0}
, at least one of
u+\overline{i}\cdot\overline{e_{2}}
or
u+\overline{j}\cdot\overline{e_{1}}
does not lie in
\tau^{\perp}
, and hence not in
\gamma^{\perp}
. Therefore
\chi^{u+\overline{i}\cdot\overline{e_{2}}}(x_{\gamma})\cdot\chi^{u+\overline{j}\cdot\overline{e_{1}}}(x_{\gamma})=0
. Then
\chi^{u}(x_{\gamma}*x_{\gamma})=0=\chi^{u}(x_{\gamma})
. Thus
x_{\gamma}
is the unique idempotent in
O_{\gamma}
.

Let us divide case (2) into two subcases:

(2.1)
p_{r}\notin\gamma
and
e_{1}^{(r)},e_{2}^{(r)}\notin\gamma^{\perp}
for some
r
;

(2.2)
p_{r}\notin\gamma
and
e_{1}^{(r)},e_{2}^{(r)}\in\gamma^{\perp}
for some
r
, and there exists no
j
such that
p_{j}\notin\gamma
but
{e_{1}^{(j)},e_{2}^{(j)}\notin\gamma^{\perp}}
.

(2.1) Suppose
p_{r}\notin\gamma
and
e_{1}^{(r)},e_{2}^{(r)}\notin\gamma^{\perp}
. Then there exists a ray of the cone
\gamma
with primitive vector
q
such that
\langle q,e_{1}^{(r)}\rangle>0
. Indeed, since
e_{1}^{(r)}\notin\gamma^{\perp}
, there exists
q\in\gamma
with nonzero pairing. Since
e_{1}^{(r)}
is a Demazure root, we have
\langle p_{r},e_{1}^{(r)}\rangle=-1
, and with all other primitive vectors on rays of
\sigma
the pairing is nonnegative, in particular
\langle q,e_{1}^{(r)}\rangle\geq 0
.

Next note that since
p_{r}
is not a ray of
\gamma
, we have
\gamma^{\perp}\setminus p_{r}^{\perp}\neq\varnothing
. Take
u\in\gamma^{\perp}\setminus p_{r}^{\perp}
. Note that
i_{r}+j_{r}=\langle p_{r},u\rangle\neq 0
, since
u\notin p_{r}^{\perp}
. Without loss of generality, assume
j_{r}\neq 0
. Then

\langle q,u+\overline{j}\cdot\overline{e_{1}}\rangle=\langle q,u\rangle+j_{1}\langle q,e_{1}^{(r)}\rangle+\ldots+j_{k}\langle q,e_{1}^{(r)}\rangle>0.

Indeed,
\langle q,u\rangle=0
since
q\in\gamma
,
u\in\gamma^{\perp}
,
j_{r}\langle q,e_{1}^{(r)}\rangle>0
, and all other terms are nonnegative. Hence
u+\overline{j}\cdot\overline{e_{1}}\notin\gamma^{\perp}
. Thus for
x\in O_{\gamma}
, we get
\chi^{u+\overline{j}\cdot\overline{e_{1}}}(x)\cdot\chi^{u+\overline{i}\cdot\overline{e_{2}}}(x)=0
. Then by (
19
),
\chi^{u}(x*x)=0
. On the other hand,
u\in\gamma^{\perp}
, whence
\chi^{u}(x)\neq 0
. Hence there are no idempotents in the orbit
O_{\gamma}
.

For cases (2.2) and (3) we prove auxiliary lemmas. Recall that
\tau=\cone(p_{1},\ldots,p_{k})
; without loss of generality assume
{p_{1},\ldots,p_{s}\notin\gamma}
,
{p_{s+1},\ldots,p_{k}\in\gamma}
.

Lemma 7.2
.

Suppose that for each ray
p_{r}\notin\gamma
, where
r=1,\ldots,s
, at least one of the corresponding Demazure roots
e_{1}^{(r)}
or
e_{2}^{(r)}
lies in
\gamma^{\perp}
. Then
\cone(p_{1},\ldots,p_{s},\gamma)
is a face of the cone
\sigma
.

Proof.

Denote by
e^{(r)}
the Demazure root lying in
\gamma^{\perp}
, and by
\gamma_{r}
the cone
\cone(p_{1},\ldots,p_{r},\gamma)
. We will prove by induction on
r
that
\gamma_{r}
is a face of
\sigma
. For
r=0
this is clear. Let
\gamma_{r}
be a face of
\sigma
, hence there exists
m_{r}\in M
such that
\langle x,m_{r}\rangle=0
for all
x\in\gamma_{r}
, and
\langle x,m_{r}\rangle>0
for all other
y\in\sigma
. Take the Demazure root
e^{(r+1)}\in\gamma^{\perp}
corresponding to the ray
p_{r+1}
. We will look for a similar vector for
\gamma_{r+1}
of the form

m_{r+1}:=m_{r}+\alpha e^{(r+1)}.

Since the Demazure roots are compatible with the cone
\tau
, we have
\langle p_{i},e^{(r+1)}\rangle=-\delta_{i,r+1}
, where
\delta
is the Kronecker symbol. Let
x\in\gamma_{r}
, that is,
x=x^{\prime}+a_{1}p_{1}+\ldots+a_{r}p_{r}
, where
{x^{\prime}\in\gamma}
. Since
e^{(r+1)}\in\gamma^{\perp}
, we get
\langle x,m_{r+1}\rangle=\langle x,m_{r}\rangle+\langle x,\alpha e^{(r+1)}\rangle=0
for any
\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}
. For
x\notin\gamma_{r}
we have
\langle x,m_{r+1}\rangle=\langle x,m_{r}\rangle+\langle x,\alpha e^{(r+1)}\rangle>0
, since the first term is strictly positive and the second is nonnegative. Next, note that
p_{r+1}\notin\gamma_{r}
, since
e^{(r+1)}\in\gamma_{r}^{\perp}
, but
e^{(r+1)}\notin p_{r+1}^{\perp}
. Therefore,
\langle p_{r+1},m_{r}\rangle>0
, and since
\langle p_{r+1},e^{(r+1)}\rangle=-1
, we can choose
\alpha>0
such that
\langle p_{r+1},m_{r+1}\rangle=0
. Now
\langle x,m_{r+1}\rangle=0
for all
x\in\cone(p_{r+1},\gamma_{r})=\gamma_{r+1}
, and
\langle x,m_{r+1}\rangle>0
for all other
x\in\sigma
. Hence
\gamma_{r+1}
is also a face of the cone
\sigma
.
∎

Lemma 7.3
.

Suppose that for each of the rays
p_{r}\notin\gamma
, where
r=1,\ldots,s
, at least one of the corresponding Demazure roots
e_{1}^{(r)}
or
e_{2}^{(r)}
lies in
\gamma^{\perp}
. Then for any
1\leq r\leq s
there exists
u\in\gamma^{\perp}\cap\sigma^{\vee}
such that
\langle p_{r},u\rangle=1
, and
\langle p_{j},u\rangle=0
for all
j\neq r,\;1\leq j\leq k
.

Proof.

Let
e^{(r)}\in\gamma^{\perp}
be the Demazure root corresponding to
p_{r}
. Note
\gamma^{\perp}\cap\{\langle p_{r},\cdot\rangle=1\}\ni-e^{(r)}
. Take
w
from the relative interior of the face
\gamma_{s}^{\perp}\cap\sigma^{\vee}
, where
\gamma_{s}=\cone(p_{1},\ldots,p_{s},\gamma)=\cone(\tau,\gamma)
is a face of the cone
\sigma
by Lemma
7.2
. Then
\langle p_{j},w\rangle=0
and
\langle q,w\rangle>0
for the remaining primitive vectors
q
on the rays of the cone
\sigma
(not contained in
\gamma
). We will find
u
in the form
u=-e^{(r)}+\alpha w
, where
\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
. Note that
u\in\gamma^{\perp}
, and
\langle p_{j},u\rangle=0
for
j\neq r
,
\langle p_{r},u\rangle=1
. Moreover, one can choose
\alpha
in such a way that
\langle q,u\rangle\geq 0
for the remaining primitive vectors
q
on the rays of the cone
\sigma
, hence
u\in\sigma^{\vee}
.
∎

(2.2) Let
p_{r}\notin\gamma
, and
e_{1}^{(r)},e_{2}^{(r)}\in\gamma^{\perp}
. Note that if there is no ray
p_{j}\notin\gamma
for which both corresponding Demazure roots
e_{1}^{(j)},e_{2}^{(j)}\notin\gamma^{\perp}
, then we may assume that for each of the rays
p_{1},\ldots,p_{s}
, at least one of the corresponding Demazure roots
e_{1}^{(r)}
or
e_{2}^{(r)}
lies in
\gamma^{\perp}
. Therefore, we may apply Lemma
7.3
: there exists
u\in\gamma^{\perp}
such that
\langle p_{r},u\rangle=1
, and
\langle p_{j},u\rangle=0
for all
j\neq r
. Note that then
u+e_{l}^{(r)}\in\gamma^{\perp}\cap\tau^{\perp}
,
l=1,2
. Consider
x\in O_{\gamma}
, and suppose that
x
is an idempotent. Let us prove that
\chi^{u+e_{l}^{(r)}}(x)=1
. Indeed, by formula (
9
) we have
\chi^{u+e_{l}^{(r)}}(x*x)=\chi^{u+e_{l}^{(r)}}(x)\cdot\chi^{u+e_{l}^{(r)}}(x)
. Therefore,
x
can be an idempotent only if
{\chi^{u+e_{l}^{(r)}}(x)=1}
or
\chi^{u+e_{l}^{(r)}}(x)=0
. Since
x\in O_{\gamma},\;u+e_{l}^{(r)}\in\gamma^{\perp}
, it follows that
\chi^{u+e_{l}^{(r)}}(x)\neq 0
, hence
{\chi^{u+e_{l}^{(r)}}(x)=1}
. On the other hand, since
\langle p_{r},u\rangle=1
, and
\langle p_{j},u\rangle=0
for
j\neq r
, by formula (
9
) we obtain
\chi^{u}(x*x)=\chi^{u}(x)\chi^{u+e_{1}^{(r)}}(x)+\chi^{u+e_{2}^{(r)}}\chi^{u}(x)=\chi^{u}(x)+\chi^{u}(x)
. That is,
\chi^{u}(x*x)=\chi^{u}(x)
only when
\chi^{u}(x)=0
, which contradicts
x\in O_{\gamma}
. Hence,
E_{\gamma}=\varnothing
.

(3) Now consider the case when for each
p_{1},\ldots,p_{s}\notin\gamma
exactly one of the Demazure roots
e_{1}^{(r)}
or
e_{2}^{(r)}
lies in
\gamma^{\perp}
. Note that in the expansion of
\chi^{u}(x*x)
by formula (
9
) the result does not change if we swap
e_{1}^{(r)}
and
e_{2}^{(r)}
. Therefore, we may assume that
e_{2}^{(r)}\in\gamma^{\perp}
and
e_{1}^{(r)}\notin\gamma^{\perp}
for all
r=1,\ldots,s
. Note that then for each root
e_{1}^{(r)}
, where
r=1,\ldots,s
, there exists a ray of the cone
\gamma
and a primitive vector
q_{r}
on this ray such that
\langle q_{r},e_{1}^{(r)}\rangle\neq 0
. Since
p_{r}\notin\gamma
while
q_{r}\in\gamma
, we have
p_{r}\neq q_{r}
, hence
\langle q_{r},e_{1}^{(r)}\rangle>0
. Moreover, since the set of Demazure roots is compatible with the cone
\tau
, i.e.,
\langle p_{i},e_{1}^{(r)}\rangle=-\delta_{ri}
for any
i=1,\ldots,k
, the vector
q_{r}
does not coincide with any of
p_{1},\ldots,p_{k}
. Therefore, the pairing of
q_{r}
with all Demazure roots
\{e_{1}^{(r)},e_{2}^{(r)}\mid r=1,\ldots,k\}
is nonnegative.

Consider
u\in\sigma^{\vee}\setminus\gamma^{\perp}
. We will check that
\chi^{u}(x*x)=0
for all
x\in O_{\gamma}
. Since
u\notin\gamma^{\perp}
, we obtain
\chi^{u}(x)=0=\chi^{u}(x*x)
for all
x\in O_{\gamma}
. Denote by
\tau_{0}
the face
\cone(p_{s+1},\ldots,p_{k})\subseteq\gamma
of the cone
\tau
.

Let
u\in\sigma^{\vee}\setminus\gamma^{\perp}
and
u\in\tau_{0}^{\perp}
. Choose the primitive vector
q
on a ray of the cone
\gamma
such that
\langle q,u\rangle>0
. Note that then
q\notin\tau_{0}
, and hence
q
has a nonnegative pairing with all Demazure roots
\{e_{1}^{(r)},e_{2}^{(r)}\mid r=s+1,\ldots,k\}
. It follows that
\langle q+c_{1}q_{1}+\ldots+c_{s}q_{s},u\rangle>0
for any choice of
c_{r}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
. Choose coefficients
c_{r}
in such a way that
\langle q+c_{1}q_{1}+\ldots+c_{s}q_{s},e_{1}^{(r)}\rangle>0
for all
r=1,\ldots,s
; this is possible since
\langle q_{r},e_{1}^{(r)}\rangle>0
. Set
q^{\prime}:=q+c_{1}q_{1}+\ldots+c_{s}q_{s}\in\gamma
. Then
\langle q^{\prime},u+j_{1}e_{1}^{(1)}+\ldots+j_{k}e_{1}^{(k)}\rangle>0
. Indeed,
\langle q^{\prime},u\rangle>0
, and the other terms are nonnegative. Thus,
u+j_{1}e_{1}^{(1)}+\ldots+j_{k}e_{1}^{(k)}=u+\overline{j}\overline{e_{1}}\notin\gamma^{\perp}
, and by formula (
9
) we obtain
\chi^{u}(x*x)=0
for all
x\in O_{\gamma}
.

Now let
u\in\sigma^{\vee}\setminus\gamma^{\perp}
and
u\notin\tau_{0}^{\perp}
. Let us proceed with the reasoning analogous to that in case (1). Recall that
\langle p_{r},u+\overline{i}\cdot\overline{e_{2}}\rangle=\langle p_{r},u\rangle-i_{r}=j_{r}
and
\langle p_{r},u+\overline{j}\cdot\overline{e_{1}}\rangle=\langle p_{r},u\rangle-j_{r}=i_{r}
for any
r=1,\ldots,k
. Note that since
u\notin\tau_{0}^{\perp}
, it follows that
i_{r}+j_{r}=\langle p_{r},u\rangle\neq 0
for some
r\geq s+1
, hence
u+\overline{i}\cdot\overline{e_{2}}
or
u+\overline{j}\cdot\overline{e_{1}}
does not lie in
\tau_{0}^{\perp}
, and therefore not in
\gamma^{\perp}
, hence
\chi^{u+\overline{i}\cdot\overline{e_{2}}}(x_{\gamma})\cdot\chi^{u+\overline{j}\cdot\overline{e_{1}}}(x_{\gamma})=0
. By formula (
9
) we obtain
\chi^{u}(x*x)=0
for all
x\in O_{\gamma}
.

Now consider
u\in\gamma^{\perp}
. Then
\langle p_{s+1},u\rangle=\ldots=\langle p_{k},u\rangle=0
. Then
j_{s+1}=\ldots=j_{k}=0
in formula (
9
). Let us show that
u+\overline{j}\overline{e_{1}}\notin\gamma^{\perp}
for
\overline{j}\neq\overline{0}
. Suppose
j_{r}>0
, where
r\leq s
. Then
\langle q_{r},u+\overline{j}\overline{e_{1}}\rangle>0
. Indeed,
j_{r}\langle q_{r},e_{1}^{(r)}\rangle>0
, while the other terms are nonnegative. Also note that
u+\overline{i}\overline{e_{2}}\in\gamma^{\perp}
. After expanding
\chi^{u}(x*x)=\chi^{u}(x)
by formula (
9
), only one nonzero term remains on the left-hand side, corresponding to
\overline{j}=\overline{0}
. That is, we obtain the equality
\chi^{u}(x)\chi^{u+\langle\overline{p},u\rangle\overline{e_{2}}}(x)=\chi^{u}(x)
. Since
u\in\gamma^{\perp}
, we have
\chi^{u}(x)\neq 0
, and we obtain

\chi^{u+\langle\overline{p},u\rangle\overline{e_{2}}}(x)=1.

Denote
w=u+\langle\overline{p},u\rangle\overline{e_{2}}=u+\langle p_{1},u\rangle e_{2}^{(1)}+\ldots+\langle p_{s},u\rangle e_{2}^{(s)}
. Notice that
w\in p_{i}^{\perp}
for any
i=1,\ldots,s
. Indeed,
\langle p_{i},w\rangle=\langle p_{i},u\rangle-\langle p_{i},u\rangle=0
. Thus,
w\in p_{1}^{\perp}\cap\ldots\cap p_{s}^{\perp}\cap\gamma^{\perp}
. In other words,
w\in\cone(\tau,\gamma)^{\perp}
. Moreover, choosing
u\in\cone(\tau,\gamma)^{\perp}
, we obtain as
w
all possible elements of
\cone(\tau,\gamma)^{\perp}
, since in this case
w=u
.

Thus, the idempotents in
O_{\gamma}
are the points
x
satisfying
\chi^{u}(x)=1
for all
u\in\cone(\tau,\gamma)^{\perp}
.
∎

8.
Idempotents and actions of one-parameter subgroups

In the previous section, for each orbit
O_{\gamma}
of the torus
\mathbb{T}
in the root monoid
X_{\sigma}
, we found the set of idempotents
E_{\gamma}
in the orbit
O_{\gamma}
. In this section, we describe the closure of the set
E_{\gamma}
.

Let
X_{\sigma}
be a root monoid with the group of invertible elements
G_{\overline{\chi}}=\mathbb{G}_{a}^{k}\leftthreetimes T
, defined by the regular cone
\tau=\cone(p_{1},\ldots,p_{k})
and the set of Demazure roots
\{e_{1}^{(r)},e_{2}^{(r)}\mid r=1,\ldots,k\}
.

For a face
\gamma\subseteq\sigma
such that for all rays
p_{1},\ldots,p_{s}\notin\gamma
exactly one of the corresponding Demazure roots
e_{1}^{(r)},e_{2}^{(r)}
lies in
\gamma^{\perp}
, we construct a unipotent group
H_{\gamma}
as follows.
For each
r=1,\ldots,s
, choose from the pair
e_{1}^{(r)},e_{2}^{(r)}
the Demazure root not lying in
\gamma^{\perp}
, and denote by
H_{r}
the corresponding one-parameter subgroup in

Lemma 9.3
.

Suppose
\chi^{u+e_{1}^{(r)}}(x)=\chi^{u+e_{2}^{(r)}}(x)
for every

such that

\chi^{u}\cdot\prod\limits_{r=1}^{k}\left(\chi^{e_{1}^{(r)}}-\chi^{e_{2}^{(r)}}\right)^{\langle p_{r},u\rangle-t_{r}}(x)=0

for all

such that
\overline{0}\leq\overline{t}<\langle\overline{p},u\rangle
.

Proof.

We argue by induction on the sum of the coordinates of the vector
\langle\overline{p},u\rangle
.

If the sum of the coordinates of
\langle\overline{p},u\rangle
equals
1

\overline{t}=\overline{0}

Set
\overline{s}=\langle\overline{p},u\rangle-\overline{t}

\displaystyle\chi^{u}\cdot\prod\limits_{r=1}^{k}\left(\chi^{e_{1}^{(r)}}-\chi^{e_{2}^{(r)}}\right)^{\langle p_{r},u\rangle-t_{r}}\!\!=\chi^{u}\cdot\left(\chi^{e_{1}^{(1)}}\!\!-\chi^{e_{2}^{(1)}}\right)^{s_{1}}\!\!\cdot P=

\displaystyle=P\cdot\sum\limits_{l=0}^{s_{1}-1}{s_{1}-1\choose l}(-1)^{l}\chi^{u^{\prime}+le_{2}^{(1)}+(s_{1}-1-l)e_{1}^{(1)}}-

\displaystyle\hskip 113.81102pt-P\cdot\sum\limits_{l=0}^{s_{1}-1}{s_{1}-1\choose l-1}(-1)^{l-1}\chi^{u^{\prime\prime}+(l-1)e_{2}^{(1)}+(s_{1}-l)e_{1}^{(1)}}=

\displaystyle=\chi^{u^{\prime}}\cdot\prod\limits_{r=1}^{k}\left(\chi^{e_{1}^{(r)}}-\chi^{e_{2}^{(r)}}\right)^{\langle p_{r},u^{\prime}\rangle-t_{r}}-\chi^{u^{\prime\prime}}\cdot\prod\limits_{r=1}^{k}\left(\chi^{e_{1}^{(r)}}-\chi^{e_{2}^{(r)}}\right)^{\langle p_{r},u^{\prime\prime}\rangle-t_{r}}.

Thus, Theorem

Remark 9.4
.

If the group of invertible elements

is not an active semidirect product, then the set

in the formulation of Theorem

must be enlarged and replaced by

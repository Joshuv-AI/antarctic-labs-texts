Transferable Migration Framework Derived from a Large-scale Tertiary Hospital EHR System
Authors: Hojun Yu, Keehyuck Lee, Sangbeob Lee, Yongchul Kim
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ObjectivesMigrating legacy on-premise electronic health record (EHR) systems in tertiary hospitals to modern cloud-native platforms presents technical and strategic challenges. We aimed to establish an optimized roadmap for transitioning legacy monolithic systems to a microservice architecture-based cloud-native EHR (MCEHR).MethodsWe conducted a 3-month strategic assessment and case study based on the modernization requirements of a global healthcare provider. The methodology incorporated semi-structured interviews with key stakeholders, including clinical informatics officers and system architects, to identify critical pain points such as .NET 4.0 end-of-support risks and performance bottlenecks during peak clinical hours. Phased hybrid migration was adopted, analyzing over 20 TB of Oracle-based legacy data and evaluating the technical feasibility of transitioning to .NET 9 and RESTful APIs. To ensure clinical safety, a proof-of-concept (PoC) environment was developed to simulate high-concurrency clinical workloads, emphasizing system resilience and transaction integrity during intensive order-entry periods.ResultsThe transition to .NET 9 and MCEHR demonstrated 100% transaction integrity across 1,012 complex clinical test cases. Frontend and backend modernization showed high feasibility; however, migration of business logic embedded within legacy Oracle views represented a primary technical bottleneck, necessitating targeted decoupling. The PoC confirmed that RESTful API-based services maintained stable throughput under heavy concurrent loads, significantly reducing the risk of system-induced delays in clinical workflows.ConclusionsTransitioning to an MCEHR architecture is complex but strategically essential. The proposed task force team roadmap outlines staged upgrades incorporating core technology modernization (.NET 9 and RESTful APIs), selective business component migration, and parallel DevOps adoption.

I. Introduction

Electronic health records (EHRs) are central to modern healthcare delivery, supporting patient data management and complex clinical workflows. This study presents a case analysis of strategic planning for modernizing a legacy onpremise electronic health record (LOPEHR) system widely deployed in tertiary hospitals across Korea, Japan, and Saudi Arabia. Despite its broad adoption, the system operates on .NET Framework 4.0, for which Microsoft discontinued mainstream support in 2016. The end-of-service status of .NET Framework 4.0 has created security, compliance, and maintenance imperatives for system modernization [1]. Furthermore, the system’s monolithic design and reliance on Oracle database management system (DBMS)–embedded business logic hinder scalability and agile development. The architecture is not containerized, which is a key feature of cloud-native EHR systems [2]. These factors necessitate comprehensive improvements.

To address these challenges, we established a task force team (TFT) to develop a modernization strategy aligned with scalability and maintainability requirements defined in ISO/IEC 25010:2011 [3]. This paper details the architectural analysis, risk assessment, and resulting phased migration roadmap.

II. Methods

Study Design and Scope

We adopted a single-case study methodology [4] to explore the complex architectural transformation of a large-scale hospital information system (HIS). This approach enabled an in-depth evaluation of technological and organizational challenges within a real-world context. Over a 3-month period (Supplementary Table S1), the TFT conducted a comprehensive assessment across four domains: frontend architecture, backend architecture, database business components, and DevOps practices.

2. Stakeholder Interviews and Requirement Analysis

To strengthen the rigor of the strategic assessment, semi-structured interviews were conducted with key stakeholders from the global healthcare provider and internal development departments. The interview protocol focused on identifying technical debt and clinical operational risks. Representative questions included:

Q1 (Infrastructure): How does the obsolescence of .NET Framework 4.0 affect system maintenance and compliance with current security regulations?

Q2 (Performance): To what extent does the existing monolithic backend architecture contribute to performance degradation during peak clinical hours, and how does this affect frontline clinical workflows?

Q3 (Data integrity): What are the primary risks associated with decoupling business logic while maintaining a massive database (exceeding 20 TB)?

Q4 (Modernization): What are the essential requirements for implementing a full DevOps framework (continuous integration/continuous deployment [CI/CD], containerization) to enhance system resilience?

Qualitative data were analyzed thematically to prioritize the migration roadmap, with emphasis on transitioning from database procedure–based logic to business components.

3. Ethical Considerations

This study followed the RECORD guidelines for reporting observational data. As a technical system analysis that did not involve patient participants, institutional review board approval was waived.

1) Performance measurement and analysis

Performance measurements were conducted for both frontend and backend components to evaluate the impact of the proposed technological upgrades. Frontend testing assessed memory utilization and user interface (UI) rendering speed on a Windows 10 workstation equipped with an Intel i7 processor and 16 GB RAM. Using Visual Studio 2022, comparative tests were performed to evaluate Infragistics framework versions under .NET 9 while binding 9,000,000 rows. Backend proof-of-concept (PoC) testing compared RESTful application programming interface (API) and gRPC performance in a simulated HIS environment. Performance metrics included processing time, standard deviation, error rate, and throughput under a workload of 50 concurrent users executing 3,000 API calls. The analysis focused on bench-marking and comparative performance evaluation rather than inferential statistics.

2) PoC and validation studies

A series of PoCs and validation studies were conducted to evaluate the technical architecture and its suitability for large-scale hospital operations. The validation framework comprised three domains:

• Protocol benchmarking: Backend communication efficiency was assessed by benchmarking RESTful APIs against gRPC, focusing on performance and throughput under representative HIS workloads.

• Frontend and database migration: Memory utilization was monitored to optimize Windows Presentation Foundation (WPF) performance during the .NET upgrade. For the database layer, a high-volume PL/SQL package was migrated to C# to assess performance equivalence and transaction integrity.

• Clinical workflow simulation: The PoC environment replicated real-world workflows of a global tertiary hospital. High-concurrency scenarios were simulated based on peak-hour clinical activity, particularly intensive periods of simultaneous order entry and patient data retrieval.

Key performance indicators—including system latency, transaction success rates, and automated rollback effectiveness— were measured to ensure operational stability under high-demand conditions.

3) Current system

The TFT analyzed the LOPEHR architecture operating on .NET Framework 4.0. The system consists of a WPF-based frontend, a Windows Communication Foundation (WCF)-based backend using business hosting service middleware, and an Oracle database containing PL/SQL objects (stored procedures, functions, and packages). These database objects are invoked through an external query storage (EQS) mechanism or via direct calls.

4) Frontend

This section considers the effects of upgrading the WPF client from .NET Framework 4.0 to .NET 9.

• API changes between .NET Framework 4.0 and .NET 9 were analyzed to identify deprecated, modified, and newly introduced APIs requiring refactoring.

• The upgrade pathway of the third-party frontend framework (TFEF) UI component library was assessed, and multiple upgrade strategies were tested.

• Compatibility with additional third-party components— Rexpert, TBarCode, and TFEF—was evaluated.

• The extent of refactoring required for core ezCaretech frontend libraries and internal tools was estimated.

5) Backend

The objective was to formulate a strategy for transitioning from WCF-based services to RESTful services in .NET 9.

• A comparative PoC was conducted to evaluate RESTful APIs and gRPC with respect to performance and implementation complexity within an HIS environment.

• The proposed backend architecture was designed using ASP.NET Core, domain-driven design (DDD), and established architectural patterns, including command query responsibility segregation (CQRS) and repository patterns.

• Core backend features were defined to include data access mechanisms—Entity Framework (EF) Core and Dapper— transaction management, logging, exception handling, background job processing, and authentication.

6) Business component transition

This section focuses on transitioning business logic from Oracle database objects to C#-based business components.

• A quantitative assessment was performed on database objects (including functions, views, procedures, packages, and triggers) in an existing database within a reference database system.

- Lines of code (LOC) were counted, and complexity and dependency analyses were performed, including evaluation of references to and from each object.

- The estimated LOC requiring modification was calculated using the following formula:

Estimated LOC to modify=(Target function LOC+ΣReferenced object LOC (FUNCTION,PROCEDURE))×Number of References (EQS,PROCEDURE,PACKAGE,FUNCTION)

• LOC projections were generated under different transition scenarios (e.g., with or without function transition).

• Qualitative analyses were conducted to evaluate technical challenges and risks associated with transferring complex PL/SQL logic.

• PoCs were planned to validate high-risk or complex migration components.

7) DevOps model planning

DevOps operations were analyzed, and a model was proposed using enterprise cloud-based electronic health records (ECBEHR) as a reference architecture.

• A gap analysis compared current processes with established DevOps best practices.

• The proposed toolchain included BackLog and Git; Jenkins, Docker, and Kubernetes for CI/CD pipeline automation; SonarQube for code quality assessment; automated testing frameworks; and monitoring tools, including the ELK Stack (Elasticsearch, Logstash, Kibana) and Grafana.

8) Transition strategy development

A phased transition strategy was proposed to account for architectural dependencies and operational risks. Lessons learned from the Korean National Hospital Cloud Electronic Health Record Implementation Project (MEDIRO) [5] informed strategic planning (Figure 1).

9) DevOps implementation strategies

The implementation plan leveraged established technologies within the mature DevOps ecosystem currently operating in the ECBEHR production environment. This approach reduced implementation risk by limiting PoC targets to desktop application CI/CD processes and newly introduced automated testing frameworks.

Because the bulk of the DevOps framework was already operational in a comparable context, a detailed step-by-step deployment plan was considered beyond the scope of this strategic study. The primary objective was adaptation rather than initial system creation.

III. Results

The TFT analysis yielded detailed findings and strategic plans across the studied areas.

1. Proposed MCEHR Architecture and Core Technology Stack

The aim was to design a scalable, secure HIS suitable for cloud migration. Core technologies included upgrading to .NET 9, migrating WCF-based services to RESTful Web APIs implemented with ASP.NET Core, and deploying backend services using Docker and Kubernetes.

The architectural design adopted DDD principles for backend services and business components (Figure 2). CQRS was implemented to separate command and query responsibilities, and repository patterns were used to abstract data access. EF Core was designated for command operations, whereas Dapper was used for query optimization.

2. Frontend Upgrade

LOPEHR utilizes a WPF client built on .NET Framework 4.0, incorporating a healthcare software framework (HSF) and TFEF components. A comparative API analysis between .NET 5 and .NET 9 identified 84 modified APIs and 55 newly introduced features across 1,364 project files. Most modifications were minor, involving environment handling and OSVersion adjustments. However, critical changes included parameter array overload resolution and deprecations affecting key APIs, such as WCF, Thread.Abort, Encoding.UTF7, WebRequest, WebClient, ServicePoint, and binary serialization for DataTable and DataSet.

Upgrading to .NET 9 provides several benefits, including improved JSON performance, enhanced threading APIs, advanced just-in-time compilation (RyuJIT), and ahead-of-time (AOT) compilation capabilities [6]. The project file format changed from .csproj to SDK-style configuration. The HSF framework comprises 84 TFEF-inherited classes totaling approximately 55,000 LOC. Three upgrade strategies were evaluated:

• A: Upgrade the HSF control framework based on TFEF to .NET 9, preserving existing assets while achieving performance gains.

• B: Adopt the latest TFEF WPF version (24.x), offering new features but incurring higher costs and potential stability rework.

• C: Remove the TFEF dependency entirely; however, this approach was deemed impractical based on development resources.

Performance testing demonstrated that .NET 9 improved memory utilization by 28% and rendering performance by 45%–49% across HSF and TFEF controls (Table 1). TFEFspecific features were not essential for LOPEHR functionality.

Other MCEHR third-party components include Rexpert, which is expected to maintain compatibility with .NET 9, provided OCX calls remain supported, and TBarCode, which is already .NET-compatible.

Core libraries (HIS.Core, HIS.UI.Base, and HIS.UI.Core) require extensive refactoring due to changes in service invocation, logging, JSON serialization, exception handling, culture management, and binary formatter deprecation. Internal ezCaretech tools—including enterprise authorization management and global management utilities—also require modernization. Legacy deployment components (Deploy Manager, EQS, and HSF Consoles) were replaced by DevOps toolchains and modular business components.

3. Backend Upgrade

RESTful APIs were selected over gRPC due to comparable performance, lower implementation complexity, existing expertise, and broader interoperability (Tables 2 and 3). The backend framework is built on .NET 9 and incorporates DDD, CQRS, MediatR, Autofac, and reusable components from the ECBEHR cloud HIS framework.

• Data access is structured using EF Core for command operations and Dapper (an open-source library) for query operations, eliminating EQS.

• Local transactions are managed using TransactionScope. Inter-service communication relies on domain events or HTTP-based interactions. Distributed transaction mechanisms using publish/subscribe patterns require further PoC validation.

• Centralized logging is implemented using the ELK Stack (Elasticsearch, Logstash, and Kibana).

• Authentication follows industry standards, using OAuth 2.0 and OpenIddict with JSON Web Tokens.

• Background job processing is supported by libraries such as Hangfire.

4. Backend Technology Evaluation

Backend technology assessment provided a data-driven rationale for the proposed architectural changes. Performance testing demonstrated that RESTful API throughput matched that of gRPC under large data volumes. Although gRPC exhibited approximately 30% faster performance for small transactions, this advantage diminished as the dataset size increased (1,110 records), when database processing time became the dominant performance factor. RESTful APIs were selected for the backend service layer due to their simpler implementation, mature ecosystem, and sufficient performance for the anticipated workload.

5. Business Component Transition from PL/SQL to C#

An analysis was performed on an Oracle database containing 1,462 functions, 951 procedures, 408 packages (3,848 routines total), 213 triggers, and 12 jobs. Converting the database logic into C# business components poses substantial LOC challenges. In particular, Oracle functions embedded in views substantially impact LOC when migrated due to required changes in the consumption code (Table 4). As shown in Table 5, removal of a 291-LOC function can propagate to an impact exceeding 27 million LOC when the function is removed from views.

Given this risk, Oracle functions should be retained within the database during initial migration. The first priority should be migrating procedures and packages (Supplementary Table S2), rewriting their logic in C# across the backend, application, and domain layers using CQRS. Triggers and jobs should be migrated later into application-level workflows or schedulers. The PoC should demonstrate the technical feasibility of database logic migration, performance, and transaction integrity.

6. System Resilience and Scalability Assessment

To validate system stability under realistic clinical conditions, stress testing and failure simulation analyses were conducted (Table 6). In the scalability assessment, the MCEHR RESTful architecture maintained stable response times and resource utilization under a workload of 50 concurrent threads processing 1,000 requests per thread, demonstrating improved efficiency relative to the legacy WCF protocol. In the transaction integrity test, data safety was evaluated under simulated system failures. Across 2,000 mixed transaction calls (success and failure scenarios), the system executed 988 commits and correctly triggered automated rollbacks for 1,012 failed requests. These results indicate that the distributed architecture preserves data consistency and prevents partial commits, which is essential for patient safety (e.g., preventing medication orders without valid billing). Finally, the DevOps dependency analysis confirmed that the build pipeline correctly identifies and deploys only the dynamiclink libraries (DLLs) affected by source code changes. This targeted deployment approach reduces regression risk compared with the legacy full-deployment method.

7. DevOps Implementation and Toolchain

The DevOps process aims to adopt the established capabilities and tooling used in the ECBEHR. Azure DevOps Server 2022 replaces the Team Foundation Server for project management. Git is used for source control, while SonarQube and SonarLint support code-quality assessment. The CI/CD pipeline incorporates Jenkins, Docker, and Kubernetes (Figure 3). Testing uses xUnit for backend testing, and monitoring uses the ELK Stack and Prometheus/Grafana.

Key processes include CI/CD, automated builds and deployments, code analysis, unit testing, and monitoring with alerting. Furthermore, cultural changes in partnerships and shared responsibilities are essential.

8. Proposed Transition Strategy

A phased transition approach was proposed to mitigate risks associated with the modernization of the existing system.

Step 1: Upgrade the backend layer to .NET 9, modernizing runtime and service communication using REST while initially retaining database logic.

Step 2: Upgrade the frontend layer to .NET 9.

Step 3: Incrementally transition EQS and Oracle stored procedures to C# business components. The core technology platform should be stabilized before initiating complex and high-risk business component migration.

Step 4: Perform cloud migration after core system upgrades (“lift and shift,” as defined in the MEDIRO project [5]). This phase requires planning for network separation, data migration (>10 TB), and compliance with data center standards (e.g., NIRS).

A resource estimation was conducted to assess migration feasibility. The analysis indicated that Phase 1 (core upgrade) and Phase 2 (business component transition) would require approximately 36 months and 5,123 man-months (M/M) in total. Phase 1 focuses on the .NET 9 upgrade and DevOps integration (1,974 M/M), while Phase 2 addresses intensive database refactoring (3,149 M/M). Detailed phase- and role-specific estimates are provided in Supplementary Tables S2–S5.

9. M/M Analysis (Supplementary Tables S3–S5)

IV. Discussion

1. Overview of Findings and Modernization Imperatives

The TFT analysis indicates that converting LOPEHR to an MCEHR architecture is complex but strategically necessary. Although the transition to .NET 9 and RESTful APIs introduces substantial technical challenges, particularly regarding database dependency, it offers decisive advantages in performance, platform independence, and access to modern development ecosystems.

2. Economic and Security Justification for Modernization

Beyond technical performance, the modernization of legacy systems is driven by critical security and economic considerations. Major ransomware attacks targeting healthcare institutions [7] highlight severe vulnerabilities in outdated systems and underscore the need for robust security frameworks. Cybersecurity threats in healthcare have escalated globally, with ransomware increasingly targeting hospital systems and compromising patient data integrity [8,9]. From an economic perspective, modern cloud-native systems demonstrate improved cost-effectiveness [10,11]. One comparative study estimated that a cloud-based EHR reduced costs by approximately $2 million over 2 years relative to an on-premise solution [10], largely by avoiding upfront information technology (IT) infrastructure investment and optimizing power consumption. Accordingly, the proposed architecture leverages DevOps automation not only to improve operational stability but also to reduce high maintenance costs and mitigate security risks inherent to the legacy environment.

3. Alignment with Modern Architectural Standards

Adopting .NET 9, RESTful APIs, and microservices architecture aligns with contemporary architectural best practices [6,11,12]. DevOps adoption has been associated with reduced operational costs through improved deployment efficiency and lower error rates [3,13]. Furthermore, microservices architecture can increase flexibility, which is crucial in dynamic hospital environments [14]. RESTful APIs support data-intensive HIS transactions and facilitate interoperability, while newer .NET capabilities—such as improved JSON processing and threading APIs [6,12]—can enhance system performance. This roadmap is consistent with broader global EHR trends. Although proprietary implementation details from major vendors (e.g., Epic and Oracle Health) are not fully public, industry reports describe a general shift toward cloud-native architectures and FHIR-based APIs to support long-term scalability [15].

4. Addressing Database Migration Risks

A key finding of this study is that a “big bang” migration of extensive Oracle PL/SQL business logic to C# is impractical due to risk and complexity. The LOC analysis indicates that dependencies among database objects would drive an exponential increase in required code modifications if migrated simultaneously. To mitigate this, we propose a phased transition strategy (Figure 1) that decouples core technology upgrades from business logic refactoring. This approach enables early adoption of .NET 9 and RESTful APIs at the application layer while encapsulating complex database objects (e.g., functions used in views) and refactoring them incrementally over time.

5. Key Implementation Considerations

Successful migration requires addressing specific technical challenges.

Code conversion: Reconstructing PL/SQL in C# is not a direct translation; it requires adopting object-oriented approaches and repository patterns, which yields a larger but more structured codebase.

• Data integrity: Maintaining transactional integrity in a distributed microservices environment requires robust patterns such as transactional outbox approaches or CQRS-based workflows to reduce the risk of data inconsistencies.

• Implications for clinical workflow and safety: The technical upgrades proposed here may yield tangible clinical improvements by addressing structural limitations of legacy systems. In monolithic architectures, tightly coupled modules often contribute to cascading failures; in contrast, cloud-native microservices architectures support functional isolation and independent scaling. REST-ful APIs facilitate rapid data exchange, supporting stable throughput during peak clinical hours (e.g., morning rounds) and reducing workflow-disrupting latency. Furthermore, .NET 9 upgrades can improve the reliability of real-time monitoring. The 100% transaction integrity observed in the PoC provides an important safety safeguard by preventing “phantom orders” through automated rollbacks that keep prescriptions and billing synchronized even during component failures. As highlighted by recent informatics research, robust decentralized architectures are increasingly recognized as foundational for maintaining clinical safety standards [16].

• Validation: Early PoC testing is essential for validating architectural decisions and refining effort estimates before full-scale implementation.

• Study limitations and generalizability: This study is based on a single-case analysis of a large-scale tertiary hospital system. However, the identified technical debt—reliance on .NET Framework 4.0 and complex Oracle business logic—represents a broader challenge affecting many legacy EHR systems. Consequently, our phased migration roadmap provides a transferable framework that other institutions may adopt to mitigate modernization risk. Further validation will require multi-site studies to assess scalability across different cloud providers and hospital sizes. Future work should prioritize cross-institutional validation to test the framework’s robustness under diverse clinical IT environments and to assess longer-term impacts on total cost of ownership and organizational agility.

6. Conclusion

The pre-implementation modernization plan for upgrading LOPEHR to MCEHR proposes a pragmatic roadmap: modernize the core framework to .NET 9 and adopt DevOps practices first, followed by phased migration of business components. Although specific deployment strategies depend on future cloud provider selection, the identified success factors—strong project management, rigorous PoC execution, and organizational adoption of DevOps practices— remain stable. Modernization is strategically necessary to meet evolving requirements for safety, security, and efficiency in healthcare delivery.

Improving Cauchy’s Integral Theorem in Constructive Analysis
Douglas S. Bridges

Abstract
In his constructive development of complex analysis, Errett Bishop used restrictive notions of homotopy and simple connectedness. Working in Bishop-style constructive mathematics, we prove Cauchy’s integral theorem using the standard notions of such properties. In consequence, Bishop’s theorems in Chapters 5 of [ 1 , 2 ] hold under our more normal, less restrictive, definitions.

Extending Cauchy’s theorem

We turn now towards proving Cauchy’s integral theorem using our more standard
notion of homotopy. To that end, we first recall that the
norm
of a
uniformly continuous function on a compact set
K\subset\mathbb{C}
is

\left\|f\right\|\equiv\sup\left\{\left|f(x)\right|:x\in K\right\},

and we have two lemmas.

Lemma 2

If
f:\left[0,1\right]\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
is a
continuous mapping such that
f(0)=f(1)
, then for each
\varepsilon>0
there
exists a piecewise differentiable function
g:[0,1]\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
such
that
g(0)=f(0)=g(1)
and
\left\|f-g\right\|\leq\varepsilon
.

Proof.
Given
\varepsilon>0
, let
\delta\in\left(0,1\right)
be such that if

x,x^{\prime}\in\left[0,1\right]
and
\left|x-x^{\prime}\right|<\delta
, then
\left|f(x)-f(x^{\prime})\right|<\varepsilon/3
.
Choose points
x_{0}=0<x_{1}<\cdots<x_{n}=1
such that
\left|x_{i+1}-x_{i}\right|<\delta
for
0\leq i<n
. For such
i
and for

x_{i}\leq x\leq x_{i+1}
define

\displaystyle\lambda_{i}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{x-x_{i}}{x_{i+1}-x_{i}},

\displaystyle g_{i}(x)

\displaystyle=(1-\lambda_{i}(x))f(x_{i})+\lambda_{i}(x)f(x_{i+1}).

Then
\lambda_{i}
, and hence
g_{i}
, is uniformly continuous and
differentiable on
\left[x_{i},x_{i+1}\right]
,
g_{i}(x_{i})=f(x_{i})
,
and
g_{i}(x_{i+1})=f(x_{i+1})
. It readily follows that there exists a
piecewise differentiable, and hence (uniformly) continuous function
g:\left[0,1\right]\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
such that
g(x)=g_{i}(x)
whenever
0\leq i<n
and
x\in[x_{i},x_{i+1}]
. For such
i
and
x
we have

\left|x-x_{i}\right|<\delta
, so
\left|f(x)-f(x_{i})\right|<\varepsilon/3
and therefore

\displaystyle\left|f(x)-g(x)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|f(x)-f(x_{i})\right|+\left|g(x)-f(x_{i})\right|

\displaystyle<\varepsilon/3+\lambda_{i}(x)\left|f(x_{i+1})-f(x_{i})\right|

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon/3+\lambda_{i}(x)\varepsilon/3<2\varepsilon/3\text{.}

It follows from this, the density of
{\textstyle\bigcup\limits_{k=1}^{n-1}}\left[x_{k},x_{k+1}\right]
in
\left[0,1\right]
, and the uniform

Proof.
We may assume that
\gamma_{0}
and
\gamma_{1}
are defined on

K\subset\subset U

\sigma:\left[0,1\right]^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}

\gamma_{0}

and
\gamma_{1}
in
K

\varepsilon>0
such that
K_{\varepsilon}\subset\subset U

\delta\in(0,1)
such that if
\left(t,x\right),(t^{\prime},x^{\prime})\in\left[0,1\right]^{2}
and

\left|\left(t,x\right)-(t^{\prime},x^{\prime})\right|<\delta

then
\left|\sigma\left(t,x\right)-\sigma(t^{\prime},x^{\prime})\right|<\varepsilon/6
. Choose points
t_{0}=0<t_{1}<\cdots<t_{n}=1

\left|t_{i+1}-t_{i}\right|<\delta

\left\|\sigma_{t_{i}}-\sigma_{t_{i+1}}\right\|\leq\varepsilon/6

2

1<i<n-1

\phi_{i}:[0,1]\rightarrow\mathbb{C}

\phi_{i}(0)=\sigma_{t_{i}}(0)=\sigma_{t_{i}}(1)=\phi_{i}(1)

\left\|\phi_{i}-\sigma_{t_{i}}\right\|\leq\varepsilon/6

\left\|\phi_{i}-\phi_{i+1}\right\|\leq\left\|\phi_{i}-\sigma_{t_{i}}\right\|+\left\|\sigma_{t_{i}}-\sigma_{t_{i+1}}\right\|+\left\|\phi_{i+1}-\sigma_{t_{i+1}}\right\|<\varepsilon/2.

Also,

\left\|\gamma_{0}-\phi_{1}\right\|\leq\left\|\sigma_{t_{0}}-\sigma_{t_{1}}\right\|+\left\|\sigma_{t_{1}}-\phi_{1}\right\|\leq\varepsilon/3<\varepsilon/2

\int_{\gamma_{0}}f=\int_{\phi_{1}}f=\int_{\phi_{2}}f=\cdots=\int_{\phi_{n-1}}f=\int_{\gamma_{1}}f\text{.}

Corollary 4

If
\gamma
is a piecewise differentiable, null-homotopic,
closed path in the open set
U\subset\mathbb{C}
, then
\int_{\gamma}f(z)\mathsf{d}z=0
for each function
f
analytic on
U
.

Corollary 5

If
\gamma
is a piecewise differentiable closed path in a
simply connected open set
U
in the complex plane, then
\int_{\gamma}f(z)\mathsf{d}z=0
for each function
f
analytic on
U

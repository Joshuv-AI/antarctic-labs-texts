Communications in Nonlinear Science and Numerical Simulation 102 (2021) 105947
Fractional Dynamics with Non-Local Scaling

Abstract
Nonlinear fractional dynamics with scale invariance in continuous and discrete time approaches are described. We use non-integer-order integro-differential operators that can be interpreted as generalizations of scaling (dilation) differential operator for the case of non-locality. Nonlinear integro-differential equations with Hadamard type operators of non-integer orders with respect to time and periodic sequence of kicks are considered. Exact solutions of these equations are derived without using approximations. Using these solutions for discrete time points, we derive mappings with non-local scaling in time from proposed equations without approximation. Non-local mappings are obtained in general for arbitrary orders of the Hadamard type fractional operators. An example of these mappings with non-local scaling in time is given for arbitrary positive orders of integro-differential equations with kicks. The proposed mappings are independent of the period of kicks at zero initial conditions. The proposed approach can be used to describe the nonlinear fractional dynamics that is characterized by scale invariance.

Introduction

Scaling properties can be described by the transformation of a function
f(x)
under the change of the variable
x
by some scale factor
\rho
:
x\to\rho\,x
.
Scaling and scale invariance are important for various applications.
For example, in modern physics, it is used the group
C(1,3)
of conformal transformations of the space-time
\mathbb{R}^{4}_{1,3}
. These transformations, which describe the symmetries of the Maxwell’s equations of of electromagnetic fields in vacuum
[
2
]
, contain the inhomogeneous Lorentz transformations,
the special conformal transformations and
the dilatations (scaling)
[
1
, p.409]
.
The dilatations (scaling)
\Pi_{\rho}
is defined as

\Pi_{\rho}\,f(x^{\mu})=f(\rho\,x^{\mu}).

(1)

For one-dimensional space
\mathbb{R}^{1}
the dilation
[
3
, p.95]
is

\Pi_{\rho}\,f(t)=f(\rho\,t),

(2)

where
t\in\mathbb{R}
and
\rho>0
.
For the conformal group
C(1,3)
on the space of scalar functions over the space-time
\mathbb{R}^{4}_{1,3}
, basis elements of the Lie algebra are represented by the differential operators:
the generators of the Poincare subalgebra
M_{\mu,\nu}
,
P_{\mu}
,
the generator
K_{\eta}
of the special conformal transformations and the differential operator

D=\sum^{3}_{\mu=0}x^{\mu}\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{\mu}}

(3)

that generates the dilatations (dilations, scaling)
[
1
, p.412]
.
For one-dimensional space
\mathbb{R}^{1}
, the differential operator of dilation (scaling)
[
3
, p.95]
is

{\cal S}_{t}=t\,\frac{d}{dt}.

(4)

Usually, the differential operators of scaling (dilation) are described as operators of integer orders
{\cal S}^{m}_{t}=({\cal S}_{t})^{m}
, where
m\in\mathbb{N}
.
Obviously, the operators are scale invariant

\Pi_{\rho}\,{\cal S}^{m}_{t}\,f(t)={\cal S}^{m}_{t}\,\Pi_{\rho}\,f(t).

(5)

As another example of a scalar invariant differential operator, we indicate the operator

{\cal D}^{t,m}_{a,\eta}=t^{-\eta}\,{\cal S}^{m}_{t}\,t^{\eta}:\quad\Pi_{\rho}\,{\cal D}^{t,m}_{a,\eta}\,f(t)={\cal D}^{t,m}_{a,\eta}\,\Pi_{\rho}\,f(t),

(6)

where
\rho>0
and
t>a>0
.
The operator
{\cal D}^{t,m}_{a,\eta}
is local, since the action of this operator on a function is determined by the properties of the function in an infinitesimal neighborhood of the point under consideration.

\Pi_{\rho}\,I^{t,\alpha}_{RL,0+}[\tau]\,f(\tau)=\rho^{\alpha}\,I^{\alpha}_{RL,0+}[\tau]\,\Pi_{\rho}\,f(\tau).

(7)

For the Marchaud fractional derivative, the scaling property

\Pi_{\rho}\,D^{\alpha}_{M,0+}[\tau]\,f(\tau)=\rho^{-\alpha}\,D^{\alpha}_{M,0+}[\tau]\,\Pi_{\rho}\,f(\tau)

Because of this, the question arises about the generalization of the differential operator of dilation

to arbitrary positive orders

\alpha\in\mathbb{R}_{+}

We can consider a generalization of the differential operator of scaling (dilation) for
\mathbb{R}
in the form

{\cal S}^{t,m}_{(K)}[\tau]\,f(\tau)=\int^{t}_{a}d\tau\,K(t,\tau)\,({\cal S}_{\tau})^{m}\,f(\tau),

(9)

where
m\in\mathbb{N}
and

is the kernel that characterizes the non-locality.
To describe fractional dynamics with non-local scaling,
we should impose the condition

\Pi_{\rho}\,{\cal S}^{t,m}_{(K)}[\tau]\,f(\tau)={\cal S}^{t,m}_{(K)}[\tau]\,\Pi_{\rho}\,f(\tau).

(10)

In order to have a calculus for the integro-differential operators of non-local scaling, we should define the

{\cal J}^{t}_{(K)}[\tau]\,f(\tau)=\int^{t}_{a}d\tau\,K_{J}(t,\tau)\,f(\tau),

(11)

\Pi_{\rho}\,{\cal J}^{t}_{(K)}[\tau]\,f(\tau)={\cal J}^{t}_{(K)}[\tau]\,\Pi_{\rho}\,f(\tau),

(12)

{\cal S}^{t,n}_{(K)}[\tau]\,{\cal J}^{\tau}_{(K)}[s]\,f(s)=f(t).

(13)

For example, if we use the kernel

If the dilatation operator of integer order

To derive mapping with non-local scaling, we use exact solutions of nonlinear non-integer-order differential equations with kicks.
The proposed mappings describe the fractional dynamics with non-local scaling in discrete time approach.

In continuous-time fractional dynamics, the non-locality in time means that the behavior of the system depends on the history of changes in this system in the past over a finite time interval.
In this case, the system should be described by integro-differential equations that cannot be represented in the form of finite number of differential equations of integer orders.
The scaling property of continuous-time approach is described by the integro-differential operators, which are generalization of the dilation (scaling) differential operator of integer order, which form a calculus.

The locality of discrete-time mappings means that the next step
X_{n+1}
is defined only by the previous step
X_{n}
(or a fixed number of previous steps, which represent time delays) , i.e.

X_{n+1}={\cal F}(X_{n})
(or
X_{n+1}={\cal F}(X_{n},X_{n-1})
for example).
In discrete-time fractional dynamics, the non-locality in time means that the next step
X_{n+1}
depends on all previous steps, i.e.

X_{n+1}={\cal F}(X_{n},X_{n-1},...,X_{1})
,
and the number of variables in the function
{\cal F}
increases with each new step.

The discrete-time non-local mappings, which are derived from equations with integro-differential operators and periodic kicks, are independent of the kick period at least at zero initial conditions.

To describe the fractional dynamics with continuous time, we will use non-local generalizations of
the differential equation of the second order with periodic kicks

\frac{d^{2}X(t)}{dt^{2}}+A\,\Lambda[X(t)]\sum^{\infty}_{k=1}\delta\Bigl(\frac{t}{\theta}-k\Bigr)=0,

(20)

where
\theta
is the period of kicks,

is the kick amplitude, and

is a real-valued function.
It is known (Sec. 5 in
[

(22)

In this case, the mapping is presented by two-dimenisional mappings

2.1
Hadamard type fractional integrals

The HTFI operator of the order
\alpha>0
is given by the following definition
[
17
,
18
]
.

Definition 2.1
.

The Hadamard type fractional integral (HTFI) operator of the order
\alpha>0
is defined by the expression

{\cal J}^{t,\alpha}_{a,\eta}[\tau]X(\tau)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int^{t}_{a}\frac{d\tau}{\tau}\,\left(\frac{\tau}{t}\right)^{\eta}\left(\ln\frac{t}{\tau}\right)^{\alpha-1}\,X(\tau),

(23)

where
\eta\in\mathbb{R}
,
t\in(a,b)
,
a>0
.

Remark 2.1
.

The operator
{\cal J}^{t,\alpha}_{a,\eta}
is bounded in the space
X^{p}_{c}(a,b)
, where
\eta\in\mathbb{R}
,
\eta>c
,
p\geq 1
,
a>0
(see Theorem 2.1 in
[
18
, p.1194]
).
The space
X^{p}_{c}(a,b)
with
c\in\mathbb{R}
and
p\geq 1

is the weighted
L_{p}
-space with the power weight, which consists of those complex-valued Lebesgue measurable functions
X(t)
on
(a,b)
for which

||X||_{X^{p}_{c}}=\left(\int^{b}_{a}\frac{d\tau}{\tau}|\tau^{c}X(\tau)|^{p}\right)^{1/p}<\infty.

(24)

In particular, when
c=1/p
, the space
X^{p}_{c}(a,b)

coincides with the space
L_{p}(a,b)
, i.e.,

X^{p}_{1/p}(a,b)=L_{p}(a,b)
.

Remark 2.2
.

Operators (
23
) satisfy the following scaling property

\Pi_{\rho}\,{\cal J}^{t,\alpha}_{a,\eta}[\tau]X(\tau)={\cal J}^{t,\alpha}_{a,\eta}[\tau]\,\Pi_{\rho}\,X(\tau),

(25)

where
\rho>0
,
\alpha>0
,
a>0
,
t\in(a,b)
.

For
\alpha=m\in\mathbb{N}
, operator (
23
) has
[
18
, p. 1191]
the form

{\cal J}^{t,m}_{a,\eta}[\tau]X(\tau)=t^{-\eta}\int^{t}_{a}\frac{dt_{1}}{t_{1}}\int^{t_{1}}_{a}\frac{dt_{2}}{t_{2}}...\int^{t_{m-1}}_{a}t^{\eta}_{m}X(t_{m})\frac{dt_{m}}{t_{m}}=

=\frac{1}{(m-1)!}\int^{t}_{a}\left(\frac{\tau}{t}\right)^{\eta}\left(\ln\frac{t}{\tau}\right)^{m-1}X(\tau)\,\frac{d\tau}{\tau}.

(26)

Remark 2.3
.

Integral operator (
23
) with
\eta=0
is called the Hadamard FI, which was proposed by Jacques S. Hadamard
[
16
]
in 1892.
These operators and their properties are described in
[

, pp. 110-120]
(see also Sections 18.3, 23.1 of
[
3
]
and
[
15
]

2.2
Hadamard type fractional differential operator

Let us give the definition of the HTFD operator (for example, see Section 18.3 in
[
3
]
and
[

Definition 2.2
.

The Hadamard type fractional differential (HTFD) operator of the order
\alpha\in(m-1,m)
,
m\in\mathbb{N}
is defined by the expression

{\cal D}^{t,\alpha}_{a,\eta}[\tau]X(\tau)=t^{-\eta}\left(t\frac{d}{dt}\right)^{m}t^{\eta}{\cal J}^{t,m-\alpha}_{a,\eta}[\tau]X(\tau)=

(27)

\frac{1}{\Gamma(m-\alpha)}t^{-\eta}\left(t\frac{d}{dt}\right)^{m}t^{\eta}\int^{t}_{a}\frac{d\tau}{\tau}\left(\frac{\tau}{t}\right)^{\eta}\left(\ln\frac{t}{\tau}\right)^{m-\alpha+1}\,X(\tau),

where
{\cal J}^{t,m-\alpha}_{a,\eta}
is the HTFI operator,
t>a>0
.

For
\alpha=m\in\mathbb{N}
, the HTFD operator is the integer-order differential operator
[
7
, p. 112]
in the form

{\cal D}^{t,m}_{a,\eta}[\tau]X(\tau)=t^{-\eta}\left(t\frac{d}{dt}\right)^{m}\left(t^{\eta}\,X(t)\right).

(28)

Since operator (
28

{\cal D}^{t,m}_{a,\eta}X(t)={\cal D}^{t,m}_{a,\eta}[\tau]X(\tau),

if
m\in\mathbb{N}
.

Remark 2.4
.

The operator
{\cal D}^{t,\alpha}_{a,\eta}
exists almost everywhere on the space
AC^{m}_{{\cal S},\eta}(a,b)
.
This statement is proved as Theorem 3.2 in
[
18
, p.1198]
.
The space
AC^{m}_{{\cal S},\eta}[a,b]
consists of functions

on
[a,b]
that have
{\cal S}^{k}_{t}(t^{\eta}\,X(t))

for
k=1,...m-1
, and

{\cal S}^{m-1}_{t}(t^{\eta}\,X(t))
is absolutely continuous on
[a,b]
, where
{\cal S}
denotes the operator
{\cal S}_{t}=t\,d/dt
(see
[
18
, p.1193]
).

Remark 2.5
.

Operator (
27
) with
\eta=0
is called the Hadamard fractional derivatives
[
7
, pp. 110-120]
,
[
15
]
.

Remark 2.6
.

For
\eta=0
, and
\alpha=m\in\mathbb{N}
, the HTFD operator is the differential operator of scaling (dilation) of the integer order

{\cal D}^{t,m}_{a,0}X(t)={\cal S}^{m}_{t}\,X(t)=\left(t\frac{d}{dt}\right)^{m}\,X(t).

(29)

Definition 2.3
.

The Caputo modification of the HTFD operator of the order
m-1<\alpha<m
is defined as

^{C}{\cal D}^{t,\alpha}_{a,\eta}[\tau]X(\tau)={\cal J}^{t,m-\alpha}_{a,\eta}[\tau]\left(\tau^{-\eta}\left(t\frac{d}{d\tau}\right)^{m}\tau^{\eta}\right)X(\tau)=

\frac{1}{\Gamma(m-\alpha)}\int^{t}_{a}\frac{d\tau}{\tau}\left(\frac{\tau}{t}\right)^{\eta}\left(\ln\frac{t}{\tau}\right)^{m-\alpha+1}\,\left(\tau^{-\eta}\left(\tau\frac{d}{d\tau}\right)^{m}\tau^{\eta}\right)X(\tau),

where
{\cal J}^{t,m-\alpha}_{a,\eta}
is the HTFI operator,
t>a>0
.

Remark 2.7
.

Operators (
27
) and (
30
) have the following scaling property

\Pi_{\rho}\,{\cal D}^{t,\alpha}_{a,\eta}[\tau]X(\tau)={\cal D}^{t,\alpha}_{a,\eta}[\tau]\,\Pi_{\rho}\,X(\tau),

(31)

\Pi_{\rho}\,^{C}{\cal D}^{t,\alpha}_{a,\eta}[\tau]X(\tau)=\,^{C}{\cal D}^{t,\alpha}_{a,\eta}[\tau]\,\Pi_{\rho}\,X(\tau),

(32)

where
\rho>0
,
\alpha>0
,
a>0
,
t\in(a,b)
.

Anatoly A. Kilbas, Sergei A. Marzan, and Aleksandr A. Tityura,

Theorem 1 in

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
X(t)\in AC^{m}_{{\cal S},\eta}(a,b)
, and

F[t,X(t)]\in X^{p}_{c}(a,b)
with
c>\eta
,
p\geq 1
,

m-1<\alpha\leq m
, and
a>0
.
Then the Cauchy problem for the integro-differential equation

{\cal D}^{t,\alpha}_{a,\eta}[\tau]X(\tau)=F[t,X(t)],

(33)

and the conditions

\lim_{t\to a+}t^{\eta}{\cal D}^{t,m-k}_{a,\eta}{\cal J}^{t,m-\alpha}_{a,\eta}[\tau]X(\tau)=C_{k},\quad(k=1,...,m),

(34)

where
C_{k}\in\mathbb{R}
, is equivalent to the Volterra integral equation of the second kind

X(t)=\sum^{m}_{k=1}\frac{C_{k}\,t^{-\eta}}{\Gamma(\alpha-k+1)}\left(\ln\frac{t}{a}\right)^{\alpha-k}+\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int^{t}_{a}\frac{d\tau}{\tau}\left(\frac{\tau}{t}\right)^{\eta}\left(\ln\frac{t}{\tau}\right)^{\alpha-1}\,F[\tau,X(\tau)].

(35)

Proof.

This theorem was proved in paper
[
22
]
(see Theorem 1 in
[
22
, p. 736]
).

\Box

Remark 2.8
.

Note that conditions (
34
) with
k=1,...,m-1

can be written as

\lim_{t\to a+}t^{\eta}{\cal D}^{t,\alpha-k}_{a,\eta}[\tau]X(\tau)=C_{k},

(36)

and for
k=m
as

\lim_{t\to a+}t^{\eta}{\cal J}^{t,m-\alpha}_{a,\eta}[\tau]X(\tau)=C_{m}.

(37)

Equivalence, which is described by Theorem
2.1
, means that function
X(t)
is a solution to the Cauchy problem (
33
), (
34
) almost everywhere, if
X(t)
is a solution to the Volterra integral equation (
35
).

Note that equation (
35
) can be written by using HTFI operator (
23
) in the form

X(t)=t^{-\eta}\sum^{m}_{k=1}\frac{C_{k}}{\Gamma(\alpha-k+1)}\left(\ln\frac{t}{a}\right)^{\alpha-k}+{\cal J}^{t,\alpha}_{a,\eta}[\tau]F[\tau,X(\tau)].

(38)

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
X(t)\in AC^{m}_{{\cal S},\eta}(a,b)
, and
F[t,X(t)]\in X^{p}_{c}(a,b)
with
c>\eta
,
p\geq 1
,
m-1<\alpha\leq m
, and
a>0
.
Then the Cauchy problem for the equation

^{C}{\cal D}^{t,\alpha}_{a,\eta}[\tau]X(\tau)=F[t,X(t)],

(39)

and the conditions

\lim_{t\to a+}D^{t,k}_{a,\eta}X(t)=C_{k}^{*},\quad(k=0,...,m-1)

(40)

is equivalent to the Volterra integral equation of the second kind in the form

X(t)=\sum^{m-1}_{k=0}\frac{C_{k}^{*}}{\Gamma(k+1)}\left(\frac{a}{t}\right)^{\eta}\left(\ln\frac{t}{a}\right)^{k}+\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int^{t}_{a}\frac{d\tau}{\tau}\left(\frac{\tau}{t}\right)^{\eta}\left(\ln\frac{t}{\tau}\right)^{\alpha-1}\,F[\tau,X(\tau)].

(41)

Proof.

The proof of this theorem follows from the second fundamental theorem of the FC that is given in Theorem 4.3 of
[
26
, p.8]
.

These operators can be interpreted as non-local scaling (dilation) differential operators.
Non-linear integro-differential equations with the Hadamard type operators of non-integer orders are suggested and exact solutions of these equations are obtained for all positive real values of orders.

The proposed mappings describe the fractional dynamics with non-local scaling in discrete time approach.

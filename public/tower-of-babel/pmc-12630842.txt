A defect recognition method based on ITLPP and multi-feature fusion matrix
Authors: Biting Lei, Pengxing Yi
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
The recognition of defects is a critical component in monitoring the structural integrity of metal components. Accurate discrimination between surface and subsurface defects is crucial for assessing the structural safety of metal components, yet remains challenging due to interference between defect depth and classification. This paper proposes a novel defect recognition framework integrating Improved Tensor Locality Preserving Projection (ITLPP) with multi-feature fusion. To address the limitations of single-feature approaches, our method systematically extracts multi-domain features to construct an analytic domain × feature variable matrix. The ITLPP algorithm then performs dimension reduction by preserving the spatial correlations in feature matrix and sample class information. Subsequently, a fusion matrix mechanism enhances category-sensitive features, and finally achieves effective defect classification through improved k-nearest neighbors (KNN). Experimental results demonstrate our proposed method outperforms conventional single-eigenvalue methods and other methods in terms of detection performance, achieving a recognition accuracy for surface/subsurface defects of 98.1%. Notably, the method can also be used to identify defects with different cross-sectional shapes, which shows that the method is suitable for natural defects.

Introduction

Metal structural components are inevitably prone to various defects during their service life. The detection and identification of various defects are crucial for assessing the safety of structural components1. Electromagnetic ultrasonic testing is a feasible detection method. However, the signals from surface and subsurface defects with varying depth parameters exhibit characteristics of overall similarity and local differences, rendering the identification of defect categories challenging. Consequently, it is necessary to find feature quantities sensitive to defect categories and the accurate defect identification method2.

After decades of development, defect recognition has yielded fruitful research results. In current research, defect recognition methods typically extract or construct fault-related feature quantities from detection signals, and use various classification algorithms to determine defect categories. These methods can generally be divided into two categories: shallow-learning-based and deep-learning-based recognition methods3,4. Shallow-learning-based classification methods mainly obtain appropriate feature quantities through feature extraction and feature dimensionality reduction, and combine various classifiers (such as k-nearest neighbors, artificial neural networks, support vector machines, etc.) to achieve defect recognition. Khatun H et al.5 used the K-nearest neighbours (KNN) classification algorithm to classify defects. Pasadas D J et al.6 proposed a defect classification method with SVM and wideband excitation to classify surface and subsurface cracks in multilayer aluminum plates. Prakash et al.7 used ultrasonic scans and HOG-Linear SVM classifier to achieve defect recognition in fiber metal laminate structures. Fu et al.8 achieved detection of composite material damage with greater accuracy based on ultrasound technology and artificial neural networks (ANN). The recognition accuracy of these methods is related to the quality of feature quantities and the effectiveness of classifiers. Recognition methods based on deep learning mainly use deep learning algorithms such as convolutional neural networks9,10, autoencoder networks11 to adaptively extract feature quantities and achieve defect recognition. Deep learning-based recognition methods rely on a large number of training samples. However, it is not practical to obtain a large amount of sample data in practice. In this paper, due to the limited availability of defect data, we attempt to enhance the accuracy of defect identification by employing a multi-feature fusion approach.

Scholars have achieved significant results in the research of multi feature fusion. Multi-feature fusion methods can be summarized into the following categories12: (1) Different features are vectorized and combined to form new features, which are then directly used13,14. This method is suitable for situations where the feature dimension is relatively small; if the dimension is too large, it will increase computational cost, and the interrelationships among features may actually reduce the accuracy of classification and recognition. (2) Different features are vectorized and combined, then a feature subset with optimal discriminative ability is selected based on certain criteria, such as distance measures, correlation measures, or consistency measures. This approach is essentially a feature selection method, in which only features with strong discriminative ability are chosen and those with weak classification ability are directly removed. (3) Different features are combined and represented as new features through certain feature transformation or projection methods, such as feature weighting15, principal component analysis(PCA)16,17, or locality preserving projections(LPP)18. These methods share the following characteristics: (1) Using directly composed multidimensional feature vectors can result in an immediate increase in the dimensionality of rows or columns. (2) The multi-feature set constructed by vectorization may destroy the spatial information among features in different analytical domains and the independence of features in these domains. (3) Feature evaluation and selection typically involve removing redundant features from the set of combined features, under the assumption that the redundant features being removed do not have any classification capability. While deleting some redundant information can reduce data dimensionality, it can also lead to some information loss. This paper proposes a multi-feature fusion method based on improved tensor locality preserving projections and a fusion matrix, which directly fuses multi-analytical domain feature matrices, avoiding the problem of dimensionality explosion, and fully utilizing the spatial positional relationships among features and sample category information. Specifically, we extract multi-domain feature quantities to construct a multi-domain feature matrix, and use ITLPP method and fusion matrix method to obtain effective feature quantities. And then, the NTD-KNN classifier is used to achieve accurate defect recognition. The method makes full use of the spatial position information between feature quantities and enhance defect recognition accuracy. The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. Section II introduces the related work. Section III describes the proposed recognition method model. Section IV presents the Experimental set-up and sample. Section V discusses the result of classification and the comparison results of different classification methods. Finally, section VI presents the conclusions and further work.

Related work

The recognition of defects is a critical component in monitoring the structural integrity of metal components. Scholars have achieved fruitful research results in defect recognition methods. It is mainly divided into shallow learning-based methods and deep learning-based methods. Shallow learning-based methods require manual design of feature extraction methods and classifiers. S. Sambath et al.19 extracted the mean, variance, maximum amplitude, minimum amplitude, maximum energy, frequency mean, minimum frequency, and energy features of wavelet coefficients and combined them with artificial neural networks to identify steel plate defects. Djaballah Said et al.20 extracted the energy and kurtosis eigenvalues of 3-layer wavelet packet decomposition coefficients and combined them with artificial neural networks to identify bearing defects. Ali J. B. et al.13 extracted time-domain features and energy features from empirical mode decomposition to form an 18-dimensional feature vector, and achieved bearing fault diagnosis based on an artificial neural network classifier. Li Yujun et al.21 used principal component analysis to extract features and achieved identification and classification of typical rail damage based least squares support vector machine optimized by particle swarm optimization, with an identification accuracy of about 92%. Zhao Yike et al.22 proposed a new data fusion driven Sparse Representation Learning (DFDSRL) framework for bearing fault diagnosis, which improves the accuracy of fault recognition for small and imbalanced samples. These methods have achieved good defect recognition results based on multi feature fusion. However, feature fusion methods such as feature combination, feature selection, and feature transformation suffer from disadvantages such as the curse of dimensionality or information loss.

Deep learning-based methods can autonomously learn signal features. Common deep learning models include Convolutional Neural Network (CNN), Recurrent Neural Network (RNN), and their various variants. Eren Levent et al.23 used a compact adaptive 1D Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) classifier to achieve bearing fault diagnosis, and validated the effectiveness on commonly used public datasets. Wang Fushuai et al.24proposed a ResNet method for classifying metal surface defects, achieving an accuracy of 93.67% on the NEU surface defect dataset. Zhang Ning et al.25 proposed a network architecture called ESG-ConvNeXt, which achieved a recognition accuracy of 97.5% for steel surface defects on the NEU-CLS-64 steel surface defect dataset. Xu Qinhua et al.26 established a method of rail defect recognition based on recurrent neural network (RNN), the method greatly improved the efficiency of rail defect identification. The excellent recognition performance of deep learning-based methods relies on a large amount of data. Transfer learning, data augmentation, and other methods are needed in small sample situations.

Method

This study employs an electromagnetic acoustic transducer (EMAT) to generate surface waves for defect detection in aluminum plates. When surface ultrasonic waves encounter surface or subsurface defects, scattering occurs at the defect site. For different types of defects with varying depths, the electromagnetic acoustic testing signals exhibit similar overall waveforms but distinct local characteristics, leading to challenges in identifying and classifying surface and subsurface defects. To effectively distinguish between surface and subsurface defects, this research aims to obtain features that demonstrate sensitivity to defect types while maintaining insensitivity to defect depth. Therefore, this paper proposes a defect identification method based on improved local tensor preserving projection and fusion matrix. This approach preserves both the spatial characteristics of multi-analysis domain feature matrices and the prior information of sample categories, enabling surface and subsurface defect recognition under small sample conditions. The proposed method is based on several implicit assumptions: (1) The local structure of feature space can represent category information. (2) The characteristics of analysis domain are complementary. (3) The category boundaries are linearly separable.

The proposed methodology primarily comprises three steps, as illustrated in the Fig. 1: (1) we extract time domain, frequency domain, wavelet packet domain, and EMD domain feature values separately to form a multi-domain feature matrix. (2) we use a two-layer multi-feature fusion method combined with improved local tensor preservation projection and MMC-based fusion matrix to obtain fused feature quantities. (3) we design an improved KNN classifier to achieve defect recognition. The following section will provide a detailed introduction to the proposed method.

The feature quantities obtained based on statistical theory have clear physical meanings and are easy to calculate. Therefore, time-domain features are widely used in defect identification and evaluation. The time-domain statistical features of the overall signal and local signal (such as amplitude, mean, variance, kurtosis, skewness, coefficient of variation, etc.) are extracted separately and formed the original time-domain feature vectors. Wavelet packet domain features

Electromagnetic ultrasound signal is a nonlinear non-stationary random signal, and wavelet packet decomposition method is a mainstream time-frequency analysis method for nonlinear non-stationary random signals. In this paper, four layers of wavelet packet decomposition are performed on electromagnetic ultrasound signals. The ‘dmey’ function is selected as the wavelet function, and the correlation coefficient method is used to select six sub bands signals containing the most original information. The entropy ratio, peak value, mean value, variance, and kurtosis of each selected sub band signals are extracted to form the wavelet packet domain feature vector. EMD domain features

Empirical mode decomposition (EMD) is an adaptive signal time-frequency processing method that is suitable for nonlinear non-stationary stochastic signal analysis. The time-domain signal is decomposed into multiple IMF components, and false IMF components are removed using correlation coefficients and variance contribution rates to select appropriate IMF components. The statistical features such as peak, mean, variance, kurtosis, and entropy energy of the selected components are calculated to form the empirical mode decomposition domain feature vector. Two-layer multi feature fusion based on improved local tensor preserving projection (ITLPP) and fusion matrix

The purpose of the two-layer multi feature fusion method is to fully explore and utilize the separable feature values hidden in multiple analysis domains. First, the spatial positional relationships between features are considered, and an improved local tensor preserving projection is used to map the feature matrix from high dimensions to low dimensions. Then, using the prior class information of the samples, the low-dimensional sample feature set is further processed based on the fusion matrix method, ensuring that similar sample feature sets are close while dissimilar sample sets are far away.

The NTD-KNN classifier

The K-nearest neighbor (KNN) classification method implements unknown sample classification based on the similarity between unknown class sample data and known class sample data. The K-nearest neighbor classification method is suitable for tensor data. The process of KNN classification method is as follows:

Construct the adjacency set

Calculate the similarity between samples and sort them in descending order of similarity. Select the K samples with the highest similarity to form the adjacent set of the test sample. Similarity measures is the key to determining the accuracy of KNN algorithm. Traditional methods include Euclidean distance, Mahalanobis distance, cosine angle, etc. However, these measures are not suitable for evaluating the similarity of tensor data. Therefore, the similarity measure \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:{S}_{M}$$\end{document} is constructed based on the NTD to evaluate the similarity between samples.17\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:{S}_{M}=\frac{{d}_{NTD}\left({X}_{i},{X}_{i}^{k}\right)}{\sqrt{\frac{1}{N}\sum\:_{j=1}^{N}\left({X}_{i},{X}_{j}\right)}\sqrt{\frac{1}{N}\sum\:_{j=1}^{N}\left({X}_{i}^{k},{X}_{j}\right)}}$$\end{document}

Where,\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:{\text{X}}_{\text{i}}$$\end{document} is the sample to be tested;\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:{\text{X}}_{\text{i}}^{\text{k}}$$\end{document} is the adjacent set of \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:{\text{X}}_{\text{i}}$$\end{document};\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:{\text{X}}_{\text{j}}$$\end{document} is the all training samples; \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:{\text{d}}_{\text{N}\text{T}\text{D}}\left(\:\right)$$\end{document} is the NTD between two samples. Results and discussion

For the specimens with different defects in Table 6, the device shown in Fig. 3 was used to collect the transmitted wave signals at the defect locations. Considering the differences in human operation and equipment factors during the experiment, each specimen was tested 30 times. Before each defective specimen was tested, a non-defective specimen was used as the control group to debug the experimental setup. 24 sets of signals were selected with the best signal from all test signals of each defective sample to form a sample set. The sample set contained 360 sets of signals, including 72 sets of signals from defect-free samples, 72 sets of signals from rectangular cross-section surface defects with different depth parameters, 72 sets of signals from triangular surface defects with different depth parameters, 72 sets of signals from U-shaped cross-section surface defects with different depth parameters, and 72 sets of signals from subsurface defects with different depth parameters. In addition, 85% of the sample set is used as the training set and 15% is used as the testing set. The proposed method is evaluated using different performance measures like accuracy, precision, recall, and F1-score31.

We validated the effectiveness of our recognition method on the sample set mentioned above. Figure 6 depicts the defect recognition result of our proposed method and the confusion matrix of our method which represent the number of accurately classified and misclassified defect patterns, where 1, 2 and 3 along the x-axis and y-axes denote no defect, the surface defect, and the sub-surface defect, respectively. From the figure, we can know that the recognition accuracy, precision, recall, and F1-score of the proposed method are 98.15%, 98.25%, 98.15%, and 98.15%, respectively. As the surface defect is very similar to the sub-surface defect, so the two defect patterns was confused, the number of misclassified of sub-surface defect with surface defect was 5%. We tried running the method multiple times and the recognition results were consistent. Therefore, the method proposed in this article has excellent and stable recognition performance for surface and subsurface defects.

From the discussion, one may conclude that the method proposed in this paper is feasible for defect identification with limited data. Unfortunately, the method proposed in this paper does not perform well in identifying imbalanced samples. Enhancing the robustness of our approach to imbalanced data through data augmentation techniques will be the focus of future work. Additionally, the method is significantly affected by noise, so future improvements will target noise reduction methods and adaptive parameter adjustment.

In addition, our data is not from real engineering applications, so we cannot determine the effectiveness of this method on actual data. It is hoped that in the future, the generalization of this method on more datasets may in fact demonstrate even greater potency.

Conclusion

This paper proposes a dual-layer recognition method based on ITLPP and fusion matrix for identifying surface and subsurface defects. A multi-domain feature matrix is constructed to preserve spatial information, followed by dimensionality reduction through an improved Tensor Locality Preserving Projection (TLPP) that maintains inter-feature spatial relationships. The reduced features undergo category-aware refinement via fusion matrix processing to enhance intra-class cohesion and inter-class separation, ultimately fed into the NTD-KNN classifier for defect recognition. Experimental data demonstrate that the ITLPP-based fusion matrix approach effectively leverages inter-feature spatial correlations to achieve enhanced defect recognition accuracy. In addition, the effectiveness of this method in identifying surface defects with different cross-sectional shapes is verified.

However, there is still a lot of work to be considered and done in this paper in terms of further research. The original sample signal constructed in this paper is small, and the subsequent work can consider studying the defect identification method from the perspective of sample enhancement. In addition, this paper will further study the fusion recognition methods of multi-source signals, such as electrical signals, vibration signals, pictures, etc.

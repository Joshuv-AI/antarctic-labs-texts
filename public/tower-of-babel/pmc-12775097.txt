Evaluation of the clinical characteristics and potentially prognostic factors in HER2-positive breast cancer
Authors: Liang Shi, Pei-Wen Xi, Zhi Li, Jing Wu, Xu Zhang, Lei Zhang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractFrom January 2000 to December 2019, the SEER Plus Database collecting human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2) and hormone receptor (HR; including estrogen receptor [ER] and progesterone receptor [PR]) status for breast cancer (BC) cases. HER2-positive (HER2+) BC is an aggressive type, and HER2-targeted therapies have significantly improved the therapeutic outcome of patients. However, not every HER2+ BC patient achieves optimal benefits from current HER2-targeted therapies. Here, we conducted a detailed analysis to compare the demographic and clinic-pathological characteristics, survival, differential genes and mutations between HR+ and HR– in HER2+ BC patients. In this retrospective cohort study, Joint HR and HER2 status distributions by more than ten specific clinic-pathological characteristics were evaluated by using Pearson’s chi-squared (χ2) test. The transciptome RNA-seqencing (RNA-seq) expression data together with detailed clinic-pathological information of HER2+ BC were from The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) and UCSC Xena. 30,482 (71.02%) patients were identified as HR+ /HER2+ BC and 12,440 (28.98%) patients were identified as HR−/HER2+ BC. HR+ /HER2+ BC patients were more likely to be younger than 50-year-old, white and infiltrating lobular carcinoma history than patients with HR−/HER2+. Patients with HR+ /HER2+ BC had lower risks of breast cancer-specific death and higher overall survival rates. Mast cells were enriched in the HR+ /HER2+ BC group, while plasma cells were more abundant in the HR–/HER2+ BC group.In conclusion, HER2+ BC patients benefit differently from current HER2-directed therapies, maybe partly due to the HR status and gene mutations, and they may provide potentially prognostic and predictive value and new treatment strategies for clinicians.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s00726-025-03494-x.Keywords: HR status, HER2 positive, Breast cancer, Clinical characteristics, Prognostic factors

Introduction

Breast cancer (BC) has become the common prevalent cancer globally and one of the leading causes of cancer-related deaths in women (Sung et al. 2021). It is acknowledged that BC is a heterogeneous disorder with different molecular subtypes and unique biological characteristics (Eroles et al. 2012). Besides, there are diverse clinical treatments and outcomes specific to different molecular subtypes in BC patients. According to the 13th St. Gallen International Breast Cancer Conference, the molecular subtypes of BC have been divided into 4 major categories: luminal A, luminal B, HER2 overexpression and triple negative cancer (Goldhirsch et al. 2013; Schmidt and Untch 2014; Park et al. 2019). Among them, human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2/ERBB2) is a significant therapeutic target of BC, showing aggressive clinical behavior, which is also related to prognosis of HER2-targeted therapies, such as trastuzumab and pertuzumab (Fehm et al. 2024; Rastogi et al. 2024). However, not every HER2+ BC patient achieves optimal benefits from current HER2-targeted therapies.

Specifically, in HER2+ BC, most HER2-enriched tumors are hormone receptor negative (HR−) by immunohistochemistry, albeit approximately 30% are typically HR positive (HR+) (Llombart-Cussac et al. 2017; Yan et al. 2024). HR+ /HER2+ tumors account for only about 10% of all BC, but the co-expression of HR and HER2 may result in different clinical behavior of BC and further modulate response to both HER2-targeted and endocrine therapies (ETs) (Pegram et al. 2023; Kay et al. 2021). Clinically, there are still a large amount of HER2+ breast cancer patients with primary or secondary resistance to HER2+ therapies. The response rates of HER2-targeted regimens in the first-line setting range from 50 to 80%, and only 20% to 40% in the second-line setting, and most patients eventually were resistant to HER2+ therapies (Shang et al. 2024), which partly due to the signal cross talking between the HER2 and ER signaling pathway (Hammond et al. 2010; Wolff et al. 2007). However, researches have found that some clinical results were often inconsistent, and the correlations between the HER2 and ER signaling pathways were not strong. Based on the current tumor lymph node metastasis (TNM) staging system, even if it can integrate information of molecular subtypes, it is still insufficient to fully meet all the corresponding clinical needs. Therefore, it is currently necessary to determine more specific prognostic factors to efficiently predict the prognosis of HER2+ BC patients and guide clinical practice.

In this cohort study, we retrospectively analyzed data from the Surveillance, Epidemiology, and End Results (SEER) Research Plus Database (version 8.4.3, 17 registries) spanning 2000–2019. Two main intrinsic molecular subtypes of HER2+ BC were included: HR+ /HER2+ and HR − /HER2+. In this study, 1,154,395 HER2+ BC patients were included, then 43,213 cases excluded, resulting in a final analyzable sample of 42,922 cases (30,482 HR+ /HER2+ BC and 12,440 HR − /HER2+ BC. The follow-up duration was 120 months. HR − /HER2+ BC molecular subtype was associated with worse OS and BCSS, no matter before or after menopause. Therapeutic effects of chemotherapy, radiation, and combination therapies were mainly associated with better survival in HR+ /HER2+ BC subtype than HR − /HER2+ BC subtype. The database of TCGA database provided sufficient data, compared the differentially expressed genes and the expression in different immune cells between HR+ /HER2+ and HR − /HER2+ subgroup of BC patients. Besides, HR+ /HER2+ BC was associated with higher gene mutation rates than HR − /HER2+ BC. There are different top ten genes with respectively mutation rates in HR+ /HER2+ and HR − /HER2+ molecular subtype. Our study attempted to identify the key prognostic factors of HER2+ BC with HR status based on the SEER database, and then provide somewhat prognostic and predictive value for HER2+ BC patients.

Materials and methods

Clinical database

The data that support the findings of our study are available from the Surveillance, Epidemiology, and End Results database (version 8.4.3), which are sponsored by the National Cancer Institution and refreshed annually. We retrieved records of patients diagnosed with HER2+ BC in the SEER Research Plus Database, 17 registries, November 2021 submission from January 2000 to December 2019.

In our study, patients were divided into two categories: the HR+/HER2+ group and the HR− /HER2+ group (Fig. 1). Specifically, we collected general data on age (25–85+ years old) and year of breast cancer diagnosis, sex, race (Black, White, American Indian/Alaska Native and Asian/Pacific Islander), type of reporting source, and patient ID. The clinical data also included tumor size and grade (I, II, III, and IV), primary site-labeled, AYA site record, histology (infiltrating ductal carcinoma and infiltrating lobular carcinoma), laterality, breast subtype, primary site, AJCC stage (0, I, II, III, and IV), and estrogen receptor (ER), progesterone receptor (PR), HER2 receptor, and lymph nodes status (Shang et al. 2024; Hammond et al. 2010; Wolff et al. 2007). The age of 50 served as a clear threshold for classifying the association of menopausal status of women patients. Participants bellowed 50 years were defined as premenopausal, and participants aged 50 years or older were defined as postmenopausal (Kalinsky et al. 2021). Besides, the prognostic data included survival months, cause of death (COD), COD to site record Kaplan–Meier (KM), and SEER cause-specific death classification. Treatment details included receipt of chemotherapy and radiation therapy; however, information on endocrine therapy was not available in the SEER database. In Fig. 1, 1,154,395 breast cancer cases from an initial dataset. Exclusion of non-HER2+ cases: 1,068,260 cases excluded, leaving 86,135 cases. Further exclusions (cases with unknown AJCC stage, incomplete clinical data, etc.): 43,213 cases excluded, resulting in a final analyzable sample of 42,922 cases (30,482 HR+/HER2+ BC and 12,440 HR−/HER2+ BC). All the data are available at https://seer.cancer.gov/. Figure 1 was created by Figdraw at https://www.figdraw.com/.

The flowchart of the study design

Additionally, the transcriptome RNA-sequence expression data together with downloadable and detailed clinic-pathological information of HER2+ BC were from The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) at https://portal.gdc.cancer.gov/ and UCSC Xena at https://xena.ucsc.edu/, including 80 HR+ /HER2+ BC samples and 30 HR − /HER2+ BC samples.

Differential expression, mutation and functional enrichment analyses between HR+ /HER2+ and HR−/HER2+ breast cancer

To identify the differentially expressed genes (DEGs) from RNA-Seq data between HR+ /HER2+ and HR−/HER2+ BC samples, the R package DESeq2 was utilized in TCGA-HER2+ cohort with the significance criteria set to |log2FC|> 1 and p < 0.05 (Ritchie et al. 2015). A volcano plot of these remarkable DEGs were portrayed (Fig. 3A). To investigate the pathways enriched in the HER2+ related BC groups, we performed Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathway analysis and Gene Ontology (GO) analysis through implementing the cluster Profiler R package (Fig. 3C, D) (Zhang et al. 2020). The mutation rates were performed by the R package maftools (Mayakonda et al. 2018).

Assessment of immune infiltration

The infiltrating level of 22 immune cell types was quantified and calculated via CIBERSORT (Yoshihara et al. 2013). The related data were downloaded from the Genomic Data Commons website at https://gdc.cancer.gov/about-data/publication/panimmune.

Immunohistochemistry (IHC) staining and analysis

The paraffin-embedded HR+/HER2+ and HR–/HER2+ BC tissues of 8 BC patients who underwent surgeries were collected. The antibody of CD38 (Proteintech, USA, 25284-1-AP), Cyclin D1 (Proteintech, USA, 26939-1-AP), EGFR (Proteintech, USA, 18986-1-AP), ESR1 (Affinity Biosciences, USA, AF6058) and TDO2 (Proteintech, USA, 15880-1-AP) were used in the process of IHC staining.

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were based on R software (version 4.3.1). Statistical significance was set at p < 0.05. Student’s t-test was used to compare the expression of immuno-oncological targets and gene mutations between the two subgroups. Pearson’s correlation test was performed to analyze the correlation between HR status and immune infiltration, and Pearson’s chi-squared (χ2) test was used to assess the association between HR status and clinic-pathological features in HER2+ BC.

Results

Compare the demographic and clinic-pathological characteristics of breast cancer patients in the SEER database according to molecular subtypes of HR+ /HER2+ and HR–/HER2+

We enrolled 42,922 patients with HER2+ BC in this study, with 30,482 (71.02%) HR+/HER2+ BC patients and 12,440 (28.98%) HR−/HER2+ BC patients. The mainly demographic and clinic-pathological characteristics of the study population were shown in Table 1. HR+/HER2+ BC patients were more likely to be younger than 50 years old (27.93% vs 25.23%, p < 0.001), white (77.96% vs 72.82%, p < 0.001) and infiltrating lobular carcinoma history (4.24% vs 1.13%, p < 0.001) than patients with HR − /HER2+ BC. In contrast, HR − /HER2+ BC patients were more likely to be 50 years or older (74.77% vs 72.07%, p < 0.001), black (13.68% vs 10.96%, p < 0.001), Asian/Pacific Islander (12.71% vs 10.32%, p < 0.001), infiltrating ductal carcinoma history (98.87% vs 95.76%, p < 0.001) than patients with HR+ /HER2+ BC. Further, HR − /HER2+ BC patients were more likely to have worse differentiation (grade III, 73.27% vs 51.22%, p < 0.001; grade IV, 0.87% vs 0.35%, p < 0.001) and later Tumor Node Metastasis (TNM) stage (III, 19.61% vs 14.69%, p < 0.001; IV, 8.01% vs 6.63%, p < 0.001) than HR+ /HER2+ BC patients, and they were more likely to have more positive lymph nodes (LN) (the number of LNs > 3, 10.47% vs 9.21%, p < 0.001). HR+/HER2+ BC patients were more likely to have better differentiation (grade I, 6.44% vs 1.55%, p < 0.001; grade II, 42.00% vs 24.31%, p < 0.001) and earlier TNM stage (I, 38.37% vs 32.54%, p < 0.001) than HR+/HER2+ BC patients, and they were more likely to have less positive lymph nodes (the number of LNs ≤ 3, 21.24% vs 19.61%, p < 0.001). Additionally, HR−/HER2+ BC patients were less likely to have undergone radiotherapy (42.19% vs 45.30%, p < 0.001) but more likely to have undergone chemotherapy (76.89% vs 71.88%, p < 0.001) (Table 1). As for the cause of death (COD), HR+/HER2+ BC patients were more likely to be alive (80.75% vs 76.53%, p < 0.001), but less likely to be dead due to BC (11.16% vs 15.59%, p < 0.001) (Table 1).

The long-term survival of patients with HR+/HER2+ and HR−/HER2+ breast cancer

Figure 2 showed the overall survival (OS) rate and breast cancer-specific survival (BCSS) analyses of HER2+ BC patients grouped by HR status. During the follow-up period, HR+/HER2+ BC patients aged 50 years old or older showed better OS (Fig. 2A) and BCSS (Fig. 2B) rates than HR−/HER2+ BC patients based on clinical stage I, II and III (p < 0.0001), while there is non-significantly different between HR+/HER2+ and HR−/HER2+ BC patients based on clinical stage IV (Fig. 2C, D). The HR+/HER2+ group also showed better OS and BCSS rates than HR−/HER2+ BC patients younger than 50-year-old both based on clinical stage I, II and III (Fig. 2E, F, p < 0.0001) or stage IV (Fig. 2G, H, p < 0.05). Although the comparisons of OS and BCSS in stage IV between HR+ /HER2+ and HR−/HER2+ BC patients over 50 years old were both not statistically significant, the overall or staged prognosis of HR+/HER2+ BC patients were better than that of HR−/HER2+ subgroup before or after menopause.

Analysis of differential genes based on HR status

By analyzing the Breast Invasive Carcinoma (TCGA, Provisional) database reported at https://www.cbioportal.org website, we compared the genes with copy number alteration in HR+/HER2+ BC to HR–/HER2+ BC (Fig. 3A). In this study, when the CIBERSORT algorithm was used to calculated the infiltration content of 22 immune cells in HER2+ BC tissues (Fig. 3B), mast cells resting had a higher fraction in HR+/HER2+ BC group than in HR–/HER2+ BC group, while plasma cells had a higher fraction in HR–/HER2+ BC group (* p < 0.05 and ** p < 0.01). The clusterProfiler R package—a widely used bioinformatics resource system—was used to visualize genes in BioCarta and KEGG pathway maps (Kalinsky et al. 2021) (Fig. 3C, D p < 0.01). Compared to HR–/HER2+ BC group, the top 10-ranked pathways among those enriched pathways were the neuroactive ligand-receptor, calcium signaling pathway, cAMP signaling pathway, protein digestion and absorption, drug metabolism-cytochrome P450, chemical carcinogenesis-DNA adducts, bile secretion, retinol metabolism, metabolism of xenobiotics by cytochrome P450 and steroid hormone biosynthesis pathway, which were involved in HR+ /HER2+ BC group (Table 2, p < 0.001). Some pathways have been proved to be activated in BC, such as drug metabolism-cytochrome P450, chemical carcinogenesis-DNA adducts, retinol metabolism, and steroid hormone biosynthesis pathway (Singh et al. 2011; Cavalieri and Rogan 2016; Garattini et al. 2014; Hulka et al. 1994). The HR+/HER2+ BC group was markedly enriched in RTK/RAS (47/85), NOTCH (27/71), WNT (24/68), Hippo (18/38), PI3K (13/29), TGF-β (6/7), TP53 (3/6), MYC (3/13), cell cycle (3/15) and NRF2 (2/3) pathways (Fig. 3E, Fp < 0.01). Obviously, these two groups had the same title of the pathway but occupied different mutated proportions according to HR status.

Discussion

Over the past 30 years, great advances have been accomplished in the studies of HER2+ BC, and the development of new drugs targeting HER2 has improved survival outcomes in early and advanced BC patients (Wu et al. 2015). A growing body of evidence suggested that HR+/HER2+ BC and HR−/HER2+ BC were biologically different (Marra et al. 2024). A complex molecular bidirectional crosstalk between the ER and HER2 pathways existed in HR+/HER2+ BC, which might be one of the most crucial parts in affecting sensitivity of HER2-targeted therapies and endocrine treatments (Zheng et al. 2023).

Our study represented a relatively comprehensive overview of the survival and clinic-pathological features in HR+/HER2+ and HR–/HER2+ BC patients. Besides, with the help of multiple database and use of bio-informatics tools, we screened out the top 20 enriched pathways with differential genes which were correlated with HR status and the survival of HER2+ BC patients, especially MAOB/CYP2A6/UGT2B11/CYP2A7/GSTM2/UGT2B4/FMO4/UGT2B28/GSTA1/ADH1C/UGT2B17/GSTT2B/CYP2C8/UGT2B7/CYP2B6/UGT1A7/UGT2B10/GSTA5 in the Drug metabolism-cytochrome P450 pathway (Supplement Fig. 1). The cytochrome P450 (CYP) system consists of a group conserved heme-containing enzymes with diverse metabolic functions at http://www.imm.ki.se/cypalleles/, which functioned as a major part in the inhibiting or activating prodrugs, such as Tamoxifen, Aromatase inhibitors (AI) (Rodriguez-Antona and Ingelman-Sundberg 2006). Tamoxifen, a classic selective estrogen receptor modulator (SERM), plays a pivotal role in the endocrine therapy of hormone receptor-positive (HR+) breast cancer. It exerts anti-estrogenic effects in breast tissue by competitively binding to estrogen receptors, thereby inhibiting estrogen-dependent tumor cell proliferation. Consistent with clinical practice, tamoxifen is primarily recommended as the foundational agent for endocrine therapy in premenopausal HR+ breast cancer patients. For premenopausal patients with medium-to-high recurrence risk, tamoxifen combined with ovarian function suppression (OFS) further optimizes therapeutic outcomes. And the ranked sixth enriched pathway was retinol metabolism included genes like CYP2A6/UGT2B11/CYP2A7/ALDH1A3/UGT2B4/UGT2B28/ADH1C/UGT2B17/CYP27C1/CYP2C8/UGT2B7/CYP26A1/CYP2B6/UGT1A7/UGT2B10/AWAT2.

Encoded by the retinoic acid receptor alpha (RARA) gene, retinoic acid derivatives activated retinoic acid receptor alpha (RARa) to induce apoptosis and reduce cell proliferation of BC; more importantly, RARa was located in the vicinity of the HER2 locus at 17q12-q21, which was generally amplified in HER2+ BC (Simeone and Tari 2004). So, the effects of retinoids on signal of ER+/HER2+ benefited the combined therapies of targeting retinoids and simultaneous targeting ER+/HER2+ in BC (Koay et al. 2010). These findings might further explain the better long-term survival of patients with HR+/HER2+ in this study, which would be a key entry point for future research on HER2+ BC resistance or recurrence. It was definitely that the prognosis of HR−/HER2+ BC patients was worse whether the endpoint was OS or BCSS, which was consistent with the previous results (Arpino et al. 2008). Our study enrolled 42,922 patients with HER2+ BC of the SEER database from 2000 to 2019, and the 10-year OS and BCSS showed the same tendency, except for the menopausal women aged 50 years or older during the stage IV. The mainly demographic and clinic-pathological characteristics of the study population between the HR+/HER2+ and HR–/HER2+ BC group showed that older, larger tumor size, more advanced stage, more positive lymph nodes, poorer differentiation, closer related to infiltrating ductal carcinoma, and more likely dead due to BC. All of them were more common in the HR–/HER2+ BC subgroup than those in patients with HR+/HER2+ BC. These tumor-related factors were directly reported with poorer prognosis (Guarneri 2009; Blows et al. 2010). This might be partly explained the worse survival of HR–/HER2+ patients.

From the exited strong evidence of the TCGA BC data, patients carrying more mutated genes with functional implications and extreme expression pattern had worse survival than those with less mutated potential driver genes. Over the last decades, numbers of studies have developed gene signatures for prognostic classification of the HER2+ BC patients (Bhalla and Chattree 1990). In this study, the mutated genes of HER2+ BC patients were explored on the basis of HR status. The results showed that HR–/HER2+ BC group with higher mutation rates than HR+ /HER2+ BC group. However, further explored whether there existed mutated genes that related to BC survival in HR–/HER2+ BC patients. Conversely, the results show that PIK3CA (32%), MUC16 (14%) and RYR2 (14%) had higher mutation rates. It meant that mutation rate had different impact on outcomes. Here, we attempted to investigate whether mutation rates of genes might the potentially key factors and independent prognostic markers for HER2+ BC patients.

There were some limitations in this study. Firstly, as a retrospective study, selection bias and incomplete data were inevitable, the deficiency of the external or single-institution validation might weaken the generalizability of our findings, so the future studies should confirm these results to clinical and experimental verification. Moreover, SEER database lacked the specific grading information of HER2+ or HR+ .so a prospective study to control these potential confounding factors and validate the prognostic value of our results was warranted in the future. Secondly, the relationship between the baseline differential genes’ level was explored in HR+/HER2+ and HR–/HER2+ BC, we did not measure every genes’ expression, and monitor the dynamic changes in genes’ levels during the entire process of HER2-targeted and endocrine treatments of HER2+ BC. Thirdly, this study was limited to HER2+ BC patients from SEER database, so the extension of our results to patients from other geographic regions or racial backgrounds should done with caution. Finally, fundamental cytological studies and investigations of the underlying mechanisms were necessary to further explore the usefulness of differential genes and mutated rates in HR+/HER2+ and HR–/HER2+ BC patients.

Conclusion

To conclude, this study analyzed clinical characteristics, mutation rates and long-term survival in HR+/HER2+ and HR−/HER2+ BC patients. From TCGA database, series genes with enriched pathways differed between HR+/HER2+ and HR−/HER2+ BC group were screened out. Likewise, some genes with differently mutated rates that related to HR status in HER2+ BC patients, which were the candidate genes that influenced the patients’ survival, especially those receiving HER2-targeted therapies. The HR+/HER2+ BC patients together with these genes above might be potential markers in HER2-targeted therapies, which could provide new treatment strategies for clinicians.

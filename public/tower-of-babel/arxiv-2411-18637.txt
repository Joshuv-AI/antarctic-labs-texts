A sharp spectral extremal result for general non-bipartite graphs
John Byrne

Abstract
For a graph family ℱ \mathcal{F} , let ex ⁡ ( n , ℱ ) \mathrm{ex}(n,\mathcal{F}) and spex ⁡ ( n , ℱ ) \mathrm{spex}(n,\mathcal{F}) denote the maximum number of edges and maximum spectral radius of an n n -vertex ℱ \mathcal{F} -free graph, respectively, and let EX ⁡ ( n , ℱ ) \mathrm{EX}(n,\mathcal{F}) and SPEX ⁡ ( n , ℱ ) \mathrm{SPEX}(n,\mathcal{F}) denote the corresponding sets of extremal graphs. Wang, Kang, and Xue showed that if EX ⁡ ( n , ℱ ) \mathrm{EX}(n,\mathcal{F}) consists of Turán graphs T n , r T_{n,r} plus O ⁡ ( 1 ) O(1) edges, then SPEX ⁡ ( n , ℱ ) ⊆ EX ⁡ ( n , ℱ ) \mathrm{SPEX}(n,\mathcal{F})\subseteq\mathrm{EX}(n,\mathcal{F}) for n n large enough. Fang, Tait, and Zhai extended this result by showing if e ⁡ ( T n , r ) ≤ ex ⁡ ( n , ℱ ) < e ⁡ ( T n , r ) + ⌊ n / 2 ​ r ⌋ e(T_{n,r})\leq\mathrm{ex}(n,\mathcal{F})<e(T_{n,r})+\lfloor n/2r\rfloor then SPEX ⁡ ( n , ℱ ) ⊆ EX ⁡ ( n , ℱ ) \mathrm{SPEX}(n,\mathcal{F})\subseteq\mathrm{EX}(n,\mathcal{F}) for n n large enough. In this paper we extend the result further and for many values of r r we can show that our result is best possible, answering a question of Fang, Tait, and Zhai.

Introduction

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a family of graphs. For
n\in\mathbb{N}
, let
\mathrm{ex}(n,\mathcal{F})
(
\mathrm{spex}(n,\mathcal{F})
) denote the maximum number of edges (spectral radius of adjacency matrix) of an
n
-vertex graph with no subgraph isomorphic to a graph in
\mathcal{F}
. Let
\mathrm{EX}(n,\mathcal{F})
(
\mathrm{SPEX}(n,\mathcal{F})
) be the set of graphs attaining the maximum number of edges (spectral radius) among
\mathcal{F}
-free graphs. The problem of determining
\mathrm{ex}(n,\mathcal{F})
is called the
Turán problem
and the problem of determining
\mathrm{spex}(n,\mathcal{F})
is called the
spectral Turán problem
for
\mathcal{F}
. The latter problem, introduced systematically by Nikiforov
[
14
]
, has since grown quite popular; for an excellent survey on this topic we refer the reader to
[
10
]
. In particular there have been many papers addressing the question of when we can guarantee that
\mathrm{SPEX}(n,\mathcal{F})=\mathrm{EX}(n,\mathcal{F})
. Some early results in this direction are the spectral Turán theorem
[
13
]
which shows that
\mathrm{SPEX}(n,K_{r+1})=\mathrm{EX}(n,K_{r+1})
and the work on odd cycles
[
12
]
which shows that
\mathrm{SPEX}(n,C_{2k+1})=\mathrm{EX}(n,C_{2k+1})
for large enough
n
. However, this phenomenon does not always occur; for example
\mathrm{SPEX}(n,C_{2k})\cap\mathrm{EX}(n,C_{2k})=\emptyset
and in fact the extremal families are very different structurally
[
3
,
14
,
19
,
20
]
. Recently Liu and Ning
[
11
]
proposed the problem of determining all graphs
F
for which
\mathrm{SPEX}(n,F)\subseteq\mathrm{EX}(n,F)
for large enough
n
.

In this paper we ask for a condition on the value of
\mathrm{ex}(n,\mathcal{F})
which implies some relation between
\mathrm{EX}(n,\mathcal{F})
and
\mathrm{SPEX}(n,\mathcal{F})
. In
[
1
]
this question was addressed when
\mathcal{F}
contains a bipartite graph, so here we focus on the case where all graphs in
\mathcal{F}
are non-bipartite. The first positive result in this direction was proved by Wang, Kang, and Xue
[
16
]
, answering a conjecture of Cioabă, Desai, and Tait
[
4
]
. Recently, the result of
[
16
]
was extended by Fang, Tait, and Zhai
[
9
]
. Below,
\chi(\mathcal{F})
denotes
\min\{\chi(F):F\in\mathcal{F})\}
.

Theorem 1

(Fang, Tait, Zhai
[

]
)
.

Let

be sufficiently large and let

be a finite graph family satisfying

Motivated by this result, the authors of

posed the following problem.

1.2
Main results

Our main result extends
Theorem 1
in the case
r\geq 3
. Let
\alpha
be the largest root of the quadratic

f(x)=-\frac{1}{r-1}x^{2}+\left(2+\frac{5}{r-1}-\frac{4}{r}\right)x+\left(\frac{4}{r}-\frac{6}{r-1}\right),

and let
c_{1}(r)=\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha r},
i.e.

c_{1}(r)=\frac{1}{r}\left(1-\left[\frac{r-1}{2}\left(2+\frac{5}{r-1}-\frac{4}{r}+\sqrt{\left(2+\frac{5}{r-1}-\frac{4}{r}\right)^{2}-\frac{4}{r-1}\left(\frac{6}{r-1}-\frac{4}{r}\right)}\right)\right]^{-1}\right).

Theorem 2
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a finite graph family satisfying
\chi(\mathcal{F})=r+1\geq 4
and
\mathrm{ex}(n,\mathcal{F})\leq e(T_{n,r})+Qn
for large enough
n
, where
Q<c_{1}(r)
.
Then we have
\mathrm{SPEX}(n,\mathcal{F})\subseteq\mathrm{EX}(n,\mathcal{F})
for large enough
n
.

The appearance of the constant
c_{1}(r)
is explained as follows. The ‘worst case scenario’ in the proof of
Theorem 2
occurs when an edge extremal graph
H
is obtained from
T_{n,r}
by embedding paths
P_{k}
in a
large
part along with one extra edge, while a spectral extremal graph
G
is obtained from
T_{n,r}
by embedding stars
K_{1,k-1}
in a
small
part. (The
k
-vertex trees with smallest and largest spectral radius are
P_{k}
and
K_{1,k-1}
, respectively, so intuitively they should also give the extremal contributions to the spectral radius of a larger graph in which they are embedded.) We will show that

\lambda(H)-\lambda(G)=\left[2-\frac{k-5+6/k}{r-1}-\frac{4(k-1)}{kr}\right]\frac{1}{n}+O(n^{-2}).

The first term inside the brackets accounts for the extra edge; the second term comes from the difference between embedding stars versus paths; and the last term comes from the difference between embedding the trees in a small part versus a large part. In order to avoid this potential counterexample to
Theorem 2
, we require that the bracketed expression be positive, which is equivalent to
k<\alpha
. The number of edges embedded in the large part of

is
\frac{k-1}{kr}n+O(1)<c_{1}(r)n
. Regarding the other possibility

, we can always construct an

which gives rise to this ‘worst case scenario,’ which proves that the constant

in

is sharp up to some integrality considerations:

Proposition 1
.

Suppose that
r\geq 3
,

, and

k>\frac{r-1}{2}\left(2+\frac{5}{r-1}-\frac{4}{r}+\sqrt{\left(2+\frac{5}{r-1}-\frac{4}{r}\right)^{2}-\frac{4}{r-1}\left(\frac{6}{r-1}-\frac{4}{r}\right)}\right).

Then there is a finite family of graphs
\mathcal{F}
such that

, and
\mathrm{SPEX}(n,\mathcal{F})\cap\mathrm{EX}(n,\mathcal{F})=\emptyset
for an infinite sequence of

Corollary 1

If

and

\displaystyle w_{1}+h(w_{1}-2)+(r-h-1)(w_{1}-1)=n\Longrightarrow w_{1}=\frac{n-1}{r}+1+\frac{h}{r}.

, and in fact

5
Concluding remarks

Theorem 2
gives a condition on the value of

which guarantees that
\mathrm{SPEX}(n,\mathcal{F})\subseteq\mathrm{EX}(n,\mathcal{F})
, when
\chi(\mathcal{F})=r+1\geq 4
. This condition is sharp for many values of
r
. As shown by
2
,
Theorem 2
does not hold if we allow

and

. We attempted to provide a similar counterexample in the case of

but were unable to do so because
\lambda(H)
is in fact greater than

by a term of

, where

and

are graphs defined analogously to the ones in
subsection 3.2
. This fact, combined with the result of
[
16
]
, suggested
1
. A solution to
1
would be interesting as it potentially requires stronger techniques than those of this paper and those of
[
16
]
.

We believe that the methods of this paper are general enough to apply to many specific families
\mathcal{F}
which do not satisfy the assumptions of
Theorem 2
. Even for
\mathcal{F}
such that
\mathrm{SPEX}(n,\mathcal{F})\not\subseteq\mathrm{EX}(n,\mathcal{F})
, large parts of the proof of
Theorem 2
go through and may complete much of the work in determining the extremal graphs.

Besides
1
, the open questions we are most interested in are the following.

(1)

Can the assumption that
\mathcal{F}
is finite be dropped from
Theorem 2
?

(2)

What is the smallest value of
|\mathcal{F}|
for which an example proving

can be constructed?

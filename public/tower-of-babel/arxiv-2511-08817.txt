Covering of an inner subset by the confined random walk
Nicolas Bouchot

Abstract
We consider the simple random walk conditioned to stay forever in a finite domain D N ⊂ ℤ d , d ≥ 3 D_{N}\subset\mathbb{Z}^{d},d\geq 3 of typical size N N . This confined walk is a random walk on the conductances given by the first eigenvector of the Laplacian on D N D_{N} . On inner sets of D N D_{N} , the trace of this confined walk can be approximated by tilted random interlacements, which is a useful tool to understand some properties of the walk. In this paper, we propose to study the cover time of inner subsets Λ N \Lambda_{N} of D N D_{N} as well as the so-called late points of these subsets. If Λ N \Lambda_{N} contains enough late points, we obtain the asymptotic expansion of the covering time as c Λ ​ N d ​ [ log ⁡ N − log ⁡ log ⁡ N + 𝒢 ] c_{\Lambda}N^{d}\big[\log N-\log\log N+\mathcal{G}\big] , with 𝒢 \mathcal{G} a Gumbel random variable, as well as a Poisson repartition of these late points. The method we use is similar to Belius’ work about the simple random walk on the torus, which displays the same asymptotics albeit without the log ⁡ log ⁡ N \log\log N term. In the more general setting of “ball-like” Λ N \Lambda_{N} , we simply get the first term of the asymptotic expansion. Keywords: random walk, confined walk, tilted interlacements, covering, Dirichlet eigenvector, coupling 2020 Mathematics subject classification: 60J10, 60K35

Introduction

Motivations

This paper studies the covering of a finite domain
D_{N}\subset\mathbb{Z}^{d},d\geq 3
of typical size
N\gg 1
by the simple random walk conditioned to stay in
D_{N}

forever
, that is what we call the
confined walk
. This process is in fact a random walk on the conductances given by the first discrete Laplace eigenfunction on
D_{N}
.

Recently, the author proved in
[
5
]
a connection between this confined walk and “random interlacements”, a Poisson cloud of random walk trajectories, in the form of local couplings. We refer to the rest of the introduction for a rigorous definition.

It is well-known that random interlacements can also be coupled to the simple random walk (SRW) on the torus (see
[
24
,
21
,
8
]
). Such coupling has been a powerful tool to study the behavior of the random walk, and still is to these days (see
[
18
]
and
[
9
]
for two recent applications).

Therefore, as an application of the work
[
5
]
, it is natural to tackle the study of the confined walk through interlacements in the same way as for the SRW on the torus. The main difference between the SRW and the confined walk is the presence of a drift which results in inhomogeneities in the occupation measure of the confined walk.

Our main result in Theorem
1.1
gives the asymptotic covering time of subsets of
D_{N}
, as well as further asymptotics for some special subsets. We also give an application of interest in the case where
D_{N}
is a ball and how it relates to the one-dimensional case.

We stress that this work relies of the existing approaches for the covering of the torus by the SRW, which occurs around time
g(0)N^{d}\log N
with
g(0)
the Green function of SRW on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
at
0
. The main interest here is how the confined walk compares to the SRW and how these differences translates in the results and proofs.

1.1
Random walk confined in a large domain as a Doob transform

Fix a bounded connected open set
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{d},d\geq 3
which contains the origin and has a smooth boundary (meaning it is given in local coordinates by a smooth function). Let
N\geq 1
and define
D_{N}\vcentcolon=(ND)\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}
the discrete blow-up of
D
with factor
N
.

We consider the substochastic matrix
P_{N}
of the simple random walk (SRW) on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
killed when exiting
D_{N}
, given by

P_{N}(i,j)=\frac{1}{2d}\quad\text{if $i,j\in D_{N}$ and $i\sim j$}\,,\quad 0\quad\text{else}\,.

Write
\lambda_{N}
and
\phi_{N}
for the first eigenvalue and associated eigenvector of
P_{N}
, with the following normalization:

P_{N}\phi_{N}=\lambda_{N}\phi_{N}\,,\quad\|\phi_{N}^{2}\|\vcentcolon=\sum_{x\in D_{N}}\phi_{N}^{2}(x)=N^{d}\,.

(1.1)

Note that
\phi_{N}
is defined on
D_{N}
, but it might be convenient to extend it to
\partial D_{N}
(and
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
) by setting
\phi_{N}(x)=0
for
x\notin D_{N}
.

Let us introduce the following notation: for
h:\mathbb{Z}^{d}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
a real-valued function on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, we define

\Delta_{d}h(z)\vcentcolon=\frac{1}{2d}\sum_{|e|=1}h(z+e)-h(z)\,.

(1.2)

This way, one can rewrite (
1.1
) as the Dirichlet problem
\Delta_{d}\phi_{N}=(1-\lambda_{N})\phi_{N}
, with boundary condition
\phi_{N}\equiv 0
on
\partial D_{N}=\{y\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus D_{N}\,:\,\exists x\in D_{N},x\sim y\}
.We refer to
[
4
]
for more details and references.

The
confined walk
on
D_{N}
is then defined as the Markov chain
(X_{n})_{n\geq 0}
with transition probabilities given by

p_{N}(x,y)\vcentcolon=\frac{\lambda_{N}^{-1}}{2d}\frac{\phi_{N}(y)}{\phi_{N}(x)}\mathbbm{1}_{\left\{x\sim y\right\}}\quad,\quad\forall x,y\in D_{N}\,.

(1.3)

We write
\mathbf{P}^{N}_{\mu}
for its law with starting distribution
\mu
; we will also write
\mathbf{P}^{N}_{x}
when
\mu=\delta_{x}
and
\mathbf{P}^{N}_{\phi_{N}^{2}}
when
\mu=c_{N}\phi_{N}^{2}(\cdot)
with the correct normalizing constant
c_{N}
.
Let us observe that the transition kernel from (
1.3
) is that of a random walk among conductances
c_{N}(x,y)\vcentcolon=\phi_{N}(x)\phi_{N}(y)
, therefore
\phi_{N}^{2}
is an invariant measure of the confined walk.

We give some properties of the confined walk and the eigenfunction
\phi_{N}
in Section
3.1
below. For now, the most relevant of these properties is the uniform convergence of
\phi_{N}
towards the solution to the following (continuous) Dirichlet problem:

\begin{cases}\Delta v=\mu\,v&\quad\text{ on }D\,,\\
v=0&\quad\text{ on }\partial D\,,\end{cases}\quad,\quad\|v\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\vcentcolon=\int_{D}v(x)^{2}\mathrm{d}x=1

(1.4)

with
\Delta
the usual Laplacian. This problem admits a sequence of solutions
(\mu_{k},\varphi_{k})_{k\geq 1}
which is ordered by the eigenvalues
\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq\dots
. We write
(\mu,\varphi)\vcentcolon=(\mu_{1},\varphi_{1})
for simplicity. Note that
\varphi
is
\mathscr{C}^{\infty}
on the interior of
D
(see
[
4
, Theorem 1.5]
). We give a precise statement of the convergence
\phi_{N}(z\cdot N)\to\varphi(z)
in Proposition
3.2
below.

1.2
Covering of inner sets - main result

1.2.1
General setting

From now on, we will focus on the covering time of an inner subset of
D_{N}
. We fix
\varepsilon>0
and consider an open set
\Lambda\subset D
such that
d(\Lambda,\partial D)\geq 2\varepsilon
. We define
\Lambda_{N}\vcentcolon=(N\Lambda)\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}\subseteq D_{N}
its discrete blowup of size
N
.

An additional assumption that we make on
\Lambda_{N}
is that it is
sN
-regular for some
s>0
in the sense of
[
17
, Def. 8.1]
: there is a
s>0
such that for any
N
large enough, for any point
x\in\partial B_{N}
, there are balls
B^{\mathrm{in}}\subseteq\Lambda_{N}
,
B^{\mathrm{out}}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus\Lambda_{N}\cup\partial\Lambda_{N}
of radius
sN
that are both tangent to
\Lambda_{N}
at point
x
.
Note also that
\Lambda_{N}
is macroscopic, and may be arbitrarily close to
D_{N}
, for example if
D
is a ball, or if
D
is well approximated by “
s
-regular” sets. This assumption is often refered to as a
positive reach
assumption.

We define the range of the confined walk
(X_{n})_{n\geq 0}
up to time
t\geq 0
as the random set
\mathcal{R}_{\phi_{N}}(t)\vcentcolon=\{X_{0},\dots,X_{t}\}
. The covering time of
\Lambda_{N}
is then defined as

\mathfrak{C}_{N}(\Lambda_{N})\vcentcolon=\inf\left\{t\geq 0\,:\,\Lambda_{N}\subseteq\mathcal{R}_{\phi_{N}}(t)\right\}=\sup\big\{H_{x}\,:\,x\in\Lambda_{N}\big\}\,,

(1.5)

with
H_{x}\vcentcolon=\inf\{t\geq 0\,:\,X_{t}=x\}
the hitting time of
x
.
Let us state the first of the main results of this paper, which gives asymptotics for
\mathfrak{C}_{N}(\Lambda_{N})
. We recall that
g
is the Green function of SRW on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Under
\mathbf{P}^{N}_{\phi_{N}^{2}}
, we have the following asymptotics in probability:

\mathfrak{C}_{N}(\Lambda_{N})\sim g(0)\alpha_{\Lambda}^{-1}\lambda_{N}N^{d}\log|\Lambda_{N}|\quad\text{with}\quad\alpha_{\Lambda}\vcentcolon=\inf_{x\in\Lambda}\varphi^{2}(x)\,.

(1.6)

Moreover,
\mathfrak{C}_{N}(\Lambda_{N})
has “super-Gumbel” fluctuation, in the sense that for any fixed
z\in\mathbb{R}
,

\liminf_{N\to+\infty}\mathbf{P}^{N}_{\phi_{N}^{2}}\Big(\mathfrak{C}_{N}(\Lambda_{N})\leq\mathord{\raise 0.49991pt\hbox{$\displaystyle\genfrac{}{}{0.4pt}{}{\lambda_N g(0)}{\alpha_{\Lambda}}$}}N^{d}\{\log|\Lambda_{N}|+z\}\Big)\geq\exp(-e^{-z})\,.

(1.7)

1.2.2
Gumbel and Poisson behaviour under a stronger assumption

If we restrict ourselves to the case where
\alpha_{\Lambda}
is achieved by a positive proportion of points in
\partial\Lambda
, we are able to get the fluctuations of the covering time as well as a description of the “last points” to be covered by the confined walk.
Fix
\alpha\in\varphi^{2}(\Lambda)
, we define the
\alpha
-level set of
\varphi^{2}
as
\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}\vcentcolon=\left\{x\in D\,:\,\varphi^{2}(x)=\alpha\right\}
.

Our stronger assumption on
\Lambda
is that
\partial\Lambda\cap\mathcal{L}_{\alpha_{\Lambda}}
has in some sense a positive
(d-1)
Lebesgue measure, meaning that it is the trace on
\partial\Lambda
of an open set of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. We formulate this assumption with the following statement, which also means that
\alpha\mapsto\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}
is somewhat continuous at
\alpha_{\Lambda}
.

Assumption 1
.

We assume that there exists a
\varepsilon_{0}>0
such that on
\big\{x\in D\,:\,\alpha_{\Lambda}\leq\varphi^{2}(x)\leq(1+\varepsilon_{0})\alpha_{\Lambda}\big\}
, we have
\nabla\varphi^{2}\neq 0
.

Note that Assumption
1
implies that
\varepsilon^{-1}\big|\big\{x\in D\,:\,\alpha_{\Lambda}\leq\varphi^{2}(x)\leq(1+\varepsilon)\alpha_{\Lambda}\big\}\big|
converges towards a positive limit (see Proposition
A.1
in the Appendix).
As an illustration of Assumption
1
, one can consider the setting where
D
and
\Lambda
are concentric balls. In this case, the level sets are spheres and
\varphi=\hat{\varphi}(|\cdot|)
decreases with the radius of the sphere. We give more details on this example in Section
7.1
.

Also note that under Assumption
1
,
\varphi^{2}
achieves its minimum
\alpha_{\Lambda}
on
\partial\Lambda
, meaning
\partial\Lambda\cap\mathcal{L}_{\alpha_{\Lambda}}\neq\varnothing
. In fact, we will see that the assumption forces the minimum to be achieved for a positive proportion of points of
\partial\Lambda
.

For
z\in\mathbb{R}
, we define

t_{N}^{\Lambda}(z)\vcentcolon=\mathord{\raise 0.49991pt\hbox{$\displaystyle\genfrac{}{}{0.4pt}{}{g(0)}{\alpha_\Lambda}$}}N^{d}\big\{\log|\Lambda_{N}|-\log\log|\Lambda_{N}|+z\big\}\,.

(1.8)

We then have the following statement.

Theorem 1.2
.

Assume that Assumption
1
holds and fix
z\in\mathbb{R}
. We have

\lim_{N\to+\infty}\mathbb{P}\big(\mathfrak{C}(\Lambda_{N})\leq t_{N}^{\Lambda}(z)\big)=\exp\big(-\kappa_{\Lambda}e^{-z}\big)\quad\text{with}\quad\kappa_{\Lambda}\vcentcolon=\int_{\partial\Lambda\cap\mathcal{L}_{\alpha_{\Lambda}}}\frac{\mathrm{d}x}{\big|\nabla\varphi^{2}(x)\big|}\,.

(1.9)

Moreover, there exists an explicit measure
\mu_{\Lambda}
, supported on
\partial\Lambda\cap\mathcal{L}_{\alpha_{\Lambda}}
and with total mass
\kappa_{\Lambda}
, such that:

\mathcal{N}_{\mathrm{RW},N}^{\Lambda,z}\vcentcolon=\sum_{x\in\Lambda_{N}}\delta_{x/N}\mathbbm{1}_{\left\{x\not\in\mathcal{R}_{\phi_{N}}(t_{N}^{\Lambda}(z))\right\}}=\sum_{x\in\Lambda_{N}}\delta_{x/N}\mathbbm{1}_{\left\{H_{x}>t_{N}^{\Lambda}(z)\right\}}\xrightarrow[N\to+\infty]{(d)}\mathcal{N}^{\Lambda,z}\,,

(1.10)

with
\mathcal{N}^{\Lambda,z}
a Poisson point process on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with intensity measure
e^{-z}\mu_{\Lambda}
. The convergence holds in distribution with respect to the weak topology on the space of point measures.

The proof of the two theorems heavily relies on the coupling of the range of the tilted RW with well-chosen random interlacements given by
\phi_{N}
. In the following sections, we properly introduce the
tilted
random interlacements and state the coupling result that we use in this paper. The core of the paper will then be to prove Theorems
2.1
&
2.2
, which are the analogues of Theorems
1.1
&
1.2
for the tilted interlacements. We present how the later can be deduced from the former in Section
6
.

Comment 1
.

The
\log\log|\Lambda_{N}|
correction in
t_{N}(0)
is linked to the codimension of the set
\{\varphi^{2}=\alpha_{\Lambda}\}
. We conjecture that in the general setting, the next order asymptotics to Theorem
1.1
is given by
n\log\log|\Lambda_{N}|
where
n
is chosen such that
\varepsilon^{-n}\big|\{x\in\Lambda:\varphi^{2}(x)\leq(1+\varepsilon)\alpha_{\Lambda}\}\big|
converges as
\varepsilon\downarrow 0
towards a finite positive limit. This accounts for the fact that very few points of
\Lambda_{N}
are such that
\varphi^{2}\approx\alpha_{\Lambda}
, therefore there are fewer “last points” to visit.

1.3
Comparison with the random walk on the torus

Consider the discrete torus of size
N\geq 1
, denoted by
\mathbb{T}_{N}^{d}\vcentcolon=(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})^{d}
, as well as the simple, nearest-neighbour random walk (SRW)
S=(S_{n})_{n\geq 0}
on
\mathbb{T}_{N}^{d}
started from the uniform distribution. For
d\geq 3
, the behavior of this walk has been extensively studied in the literature, with notably the study of the last points of
\mathbb{T}_{N}^{d}
that the SRW visits. For
T\geq 0
, we define
\mathcal{R}_{T}(S)\vcentcolon=\big\{S_{0},\dots,S_{\lfloor T\rfloor}\big\}
the range up to time
T
. Then, the cover time
C_{N}^{d}
of
\mathbb{T}_{N}^{d}
is defined as

C_{N}^{d}\vcentcolon=\inf\big\{t\in\mathbb{N}\,:\,\mathcal{R}_{t}(S)=\mathbb{T}_{N}^{d}\big\}\,.

(1.11)

Write
\mathbf{P}_{x}
for the law of the simple random walk on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
with starting point
x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, and simply
\mathbf{P}=\mathbf{P}_{0}
. With a slight abuse of notation, we also denote by
\mathbf{P}_{x}/\mathbf{P}
the law of SRW on the torus.

With the work of Aldous
[
1
]
on cover time of Markov chains, it is known that
C_{N}^{d}\sim g(0)N^{d}\log N
in probability, where
g
is the SRW Green’s function. It is then natural to inquire about both the fluctuation of
C_{N}^{d}
around
g(0)N^{d}\log N
and about the points that are still not covered by the SRW at time
g(0)N^{d}\log N(1\pm\bar{o}(1))
.

Such questions where first tackled by Belius
[
3
]
, who first proved Gumbel fluctuations for
C_{N}^{d}
:

\lim_{N\to+\infty}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{N}^{d}}\Big|\mathbf{P}_{x}\Big(C_{N}^{d}\leq g(0)N^{d}\big\{\log N+z\big\}\Big)-\exp\big(-e^{-z}\big)\Big|=0\,.

(1.12)

Regarding the “late points” of the SRW, Belius proved that these are distributed following a Poisson point process with uniform intensity measure. Given
z\in\mathbb{R}
we may define
\mathcal{N}_{N}^{z}
the set of points that are not covered at time
g(0)N^{d}\big\{\log N+z\big\}
. Then, we have the following convergence in distribution:

\frac{1}{N}\mathcal{N}_{N}^{z}\xrightarrow[N\to+\infty]{(d)}\mathcal{N}^{z}\,,

(1.13)

where
\mathcal{N}^{z}
is a Poisson point process on
[0,1]^{d}
with intensity
e^{-z}d\ell_{d}
, with
\ell_{d}
the Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

More recently, a work of Prévost, Rodriguez and Sousi
[
18
]
improved our understanding of the late points and proved a sharp phase transition for the behavior of the set. Consider
a\in(0,1)
and the
a
-fraction of the covering time
\tau_{N}^{a}\vcentcolon=ag(0)N^{d}\log N
. Then, if
\mathscr{L}_{N}^{a}
is the set of points that are not covered by the random walk at time
\tau_{N}^{a}
, we have the following alternative:

—

If
a>\tfrac{1}{2}
, there is a coupling with a Bernoulli field
\mathscr{Z}_{N}^{a}
such that
\mathscr{L}_{N}^{a}\approx\mathscr{Z}_{N}^{a}
with probability going to
1
as
N\to+\infty
a
a

a

Here
\mathscr{L}_{N}^{a}\approx\mathscr{Z}_{N}^{a}
means that
\mathscr{Z}_{N}^{a_{-}}\subseteq\mathscr{L}_{N}^{a}\subseteq\mathscr{Z}_{N}^{a_{+}}
with
a_{-}=a_{-}(N)\sim a_{+}
.
. Morally, the presence of a late point can be considered as independent Bernoulli random variables.

—

If
a=\tfrac{1}{2}
, the best coupling possible has probability
e^{-1}
as
N\to+\infty
of having
\mathscr{L}_{N}^{a}\approx\mathscr{Z}_{N}^{a}
.

—

If
a<\tfrac{1}{2}
, there are no coupling that achieve a positive probability as
N\to+\infty
for the event
\mathscr{L}_{N}^{a}\approx\mathscr{Z}_{N}^{a}
.

The key element in the proof of these results is a coupling that links the SRW to a random subset of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
introduced in
[
20
]
and called the random interlacements. Informally speaking, the random interlacements (RI) at level
u>0
, denoted by
\mathscr{I}(u)
, is a Poissonian collection of independent random walk trajectories whose “density” is governed by
u
. Successive works of
[
24
,
21
,
8
]
proved that for any
u>0
, the range
\mathcal{R}_{uN^{d}}
is “locally close” to
\mathscr{I}(u)
. More details about interlacements are provided in Section
2.1
.

With this coupling, we can link
C_{N}^{d}
to
U_{N}^{d}
the first level
u
at which
\mathscr{I}(u)
covers the torus
\mathbb{T}_{N}^{d}
by having
C_{N}^{d}\sim N^{d}\cdot U_{N}^{d}
. The main benefit of working with interlacements is that the cover level of a point has an explicit exponential distribution with parameter
g(0)^{-1}
which corresponds to its
capacity
(see Section
2.1
below). Heuristically, we may think
U_{N}^{d}
as a maximum of
N^{d}
exponential variables, hence the Gumbel fluctuations which also propagate to
C_{N}^{d}
, therefore resulting in (
1.12
). The fact that Gumbel fluctuations appear in this context in well-known in extreme value theory when the exponential variables are i.i.d. (see
[
19
, Proposition 0.3]
). Random interlacements however display strong correlations: the main step of the proof of (
1.12
) is then to create the required independence by studying the set of the last points to be covered.

Our main motivation for this paper is the coupling between the confined walk and a random
tilted
interlacements on macroscopic inner subsets of
D_{N}
that was recently obtained in
[
5
]
. As in the case of the torus, we will use this coupling to prove Theorems
1.1
&
1.2
by first proving their analogs for the tilted interlacements.
We stress that these analog results are the main point of this paper: the transfers to the confined walk, which we explain in Section
6
is mostly straightforward. The main additional difficulty comes in the fact that contrary to the case of the torus, the tilted interlacements is not spatially homogeneous. This can be translated in the previous heuristics by saying that the cover level of
x\in D_{N}
is an exponential variable with position-dependent parameter (and not a constant like
g(0)^{-1}
).

Tilted interlacements, capacity and first estimates

2.1
Tilting of random interlacements

The tilted (continuous-time) random interlacements were introduced in
[
14
]
defined the tilting of continuous-time random interlacements as a way to locally modify the trajectories of the RI.
It was used in particular to get large deviation principles for disconnection events, by locally densifying the RI.
We choose to present here the point of view of
[
22
]
as random interlacements on weighted graphs, with a touch of local tilting of interlacements present in
[
14
]
. We also mention
[
9
]
for more recent example of their use in large deviation events.

Consider the space of doubly infinite transient paths on the
d
-dimensional lattice
\mathbb{Z}^{d},d\geq 3
:

W=\Big\{w:\mathbb{Z}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}^{d}\,:\,\forall n\in\mathbb{Z},|w(n)-w(n+1)|_{1}=1\text{ and }\lim_{|n|\to\infty}|w(n)|=\infty\Big\}\,,

endowed with the
\sigma
-algebra
\mathscr{W}
generated by the maps
w\mapsto w(n),n\in\mathbb{Z}
.
We can define the equivalence relation
w\sim w^{\prime}\Longleftrightarrow\exists k\in\mathbb{Z},w(\cdot+k)=w^{\prime}
, and we write
W^{*}\vcentcolon=W/\sim
the corresponding quotient space as well as
\pi^{*}
the associated canonical projection. The set
W^{*}
is endowed with the
\sigma
-algebra
\mathscr{W}^{*}
generated by
\pi^{*}
.

Let
K
be a subset of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. We define for
w\in W
the hitting times

H_{K}(w)=\inf\left\{k\in\mathbb{Z}\,:\,w(k)\in K\right\}\,,\quad\bar{H}_{K}=\inf\left\{k\geq 1\,:\,w(k)\in K\right\}

(2.1)

with
\inf\varnothing=+\infty
by convention.
We also define
W_{K}=W\cap\left\{H_{K}<+\infty\right\}
the set of trajectories that hit
K
.

Consider a positive function
h:\mathbb{Z}^{d}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+}^{*}
which satisfies
h=1
outside a finite set. Informally, the
h
-tilted random interlacements of level
u>0
, denoted by
\mathscr{I}_{h}(u)
, is a Poisson cloud of
h
-tilted random walk trajectories,
i.e.
random walks on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
equipped with conductances
c(x,y)=h(x)h(y)
.

More precisely, we let
\mathbf{P}^{h}_{z}
denote the law of the random walk on conductances
c(x,y)=h(x)h(y)
starting at
z\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. Consider a finite set
K\subset\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. Then, we can define the
h
-tilted equilibrium measure of
K
by

e^{h}_{K}(z)\vcentcolon=\mathbf{P}^{h}_{z}\big(\bar{H}_{K}=+\infty\big)h(z)\sum_{|e|=1}h(z+e)\mathbbm{1}_{\left\{z\in K\right\}}\,,\quad\bar{e}^{h}_{K}(x)=\frac{e^{h}_{K}(x)}{\mathrm{cap}^{h}(K)}\,.

(2.2)

Here,
\mathrm{cap}^{h}(K)
is the
tilted capacity
of the set
K
, given by

\mathrm{cap}^{h}(K)=e^{h}_{K}(K)=\sum_{x\in\partial K}e^{h}_{K}(x)\,,

where we have used that the measure
e^{h}_{K}
is supported on the (inner) boundary of
K
, which we denote by
\partial K=\{x\in K\,:\,\exists y\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus K,x\sim y\}
.

Intuitively,
\bar{e}^{h}_{K}
is the law of the first entrance point in
K
of a random walk trajectory that is coming from far away. Indeed, we have
\mathbf{P}^{h}_{z}(X_{H_{K}}=x\,|\,H_{K}<+\infty)\to\bar{e}^{h}_{K}(x)
as
|z|\to+\infty
, and the capacity can be alternatively written as

\mathrm{cap}^{h}(K)=\lim_{|z|\to+\infty}\frac{\mathbf{P}^{h}_{z}(H_{K}<+\infty)}{G^{h}(z)}\,,\quad\text{with $G^{h}(z)=\sum_{n\geq 0}\mathbf{P}^{h}_{z}(X_{n}=z)$ the Green function}\,,

and can be interpreted as the « size » of
K
seen from a random walk starting at a faraway point on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. Since
h\equiv 1
outside a finite set, the tilted Green function
G^{h}
resembles the Green function of the SRW, hence we can convince ourselves that as
|z|\to+\infty
, we have
G^{h}(z)\sim G(z)\asymp|z|^{2-d}
, where
|\cdot|
is the Euclidean norm on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
(see
[
12
, Thm. 4.3.1]
).

Now, let
w^{*}\in W^{*}_{K}
be a class of paths hitting
K
, and denote by
\tilde{w}
the unique
w\in w^{*}
such that
H_{K}(w)=0
. For
K
finite, we can define on
\pi^{*}(W_{K})
a finite measure
\nu_{K}
given by

\nu^{h}_{K}(w^{*})=\mathbf{P}^{h}_{\tilde{w}(0)}(w(\mathbb{Z}_{-})\,|\,\bar{H}_{K}=+\infty)\times e^{h}_{K}(w(0))\times\mathbf{P}^{h}_{\tilde{w}(0)}(w(\mathbb{Z}_{+}))\,,

(2.3)

which can be interpreted, after normalization, as the law of a SRW trajectory that hits
K
. The measures
\nu^{h}_{K}
for
K
finite can be extended to a
\sigma
-finite measure
\nu^{h}
on
W^{*}
.

The tilted random interlacements process is a Poisson Point Process
\chi^{h}
on the space
W^{*}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
with intensity
\nu^{h}\otimes du
. The random interlacements of level
u>0
is the subset
\mathscr{I}_{h}(u)\subset\mathbb{Z}^{d}
defined as

\mathscr{I}_{h}(u)\vcentcolon=\left\{z\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\,:\,\exists(w,v)\in\chi^{h},v\leq u,z\in w(\mathbb{Z})\right\}=\left\{w(k)\,:\,k\in\mathbb{Z},(w,v)\in\chi^{h},v\leq u\right\}\,.

We denote by
\mathbb{P}_{h}
the law of the RI on the space
\mathcal{M}_{p}(W^{*}\times\mathbb{R}_{+})
of point measures on
W^{*}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
, which we also use to denote the law of the (non-decreasing) family of random subsets
(\mathscr{I}_{h}(u))_{u>0}
.

A key property of RI is that its trace in a finite set
K
can be recovered from a collection of tilted RW trajectories — a property shared with the RI, see
[
20
]
. More precisely, denote by
N^{h,u}_{K}
a Poisson variable with parameter
u\mathrm{cap}^{h}(K)
and let
(X^{(j)})_{j\geq 0}
be i.i.d.
h
-tilted RW trajectories starting from
\bar{e}^{h}_{K}
. We then have

\mathscr{I}_{h}(u)\cap K\overset{(d)}{=}\big\{\mathcal{R}_{\infty}(X^{(j)})\,:\,1\leq j\leq N^{h,u}_{K}\big\}\cap K\,.

(2.4)

In particular, we deduce the following crucial identity, which characterizes the law of
\mathscr{I}_{h}(u)
as a random subset of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
(see
[
20
, Remark 2.2-(2)]
): for any fixed
u>0
and any finite set
K\subset\mathbb{Z}^{d}
,

\mathbb{P}_{h}\big(\mathscr{I}(u)\cap K=\varnothing\big)=\exp\big(-u\mathrm{cap}^{h}(K)\big)\,.

(2.5)

2.2
Cover level of the tilted interlacements

Recall that we consider a subset
\Lambda_{N}=(N\cdot\Lambda)\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}\subset D_{N}
with
\Lambda
an open subset of
D
that satisfies
d(\Lambda,\partial D)\geq 2\varepsilon
for some
\varepsilon>0
.
Let us define
\Lambda_{N}^{\varepsilon}=\big\{x\in D_{N}\,:\,d(x,D_{N})\leq\varepsilon N\big\}\subset D_{N}
the
\varepsilon N
-enlarged version of
\Lambda_{N}
. The tilting functions that we will consider are given by

\Psi_{N}(x)\vcentcolon=\begin{cases}\phi_{N}(x)&\quad\text{ if }\ x\in\Lambda_{N}^{\varepsilon}\,,\\
1&\quad\text{ else}\,.\end{cases}

Write
\mathbf{P}^{\Psi_{N}}_{x}
for the law of the random walk on conductances
\Psi_{N}(i)\Psi_{N}(j)
starting at
x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
,
i.e.
with transition kernel denoted by
p_{\psi_{N}}(x,y)
.

As previously mentioned, the main ordeal of the paper is to prove an analog of Theorem
1.1
for the tilted interlacements
\mathscr{I}_{\Psi_{N}}
. To this end, we define the covering
level
of the set
\Lambda_{N}
as the random variable

\mathfrak{U}_{N}(\Lambda_{N})\vcentcolon=\inf\{u>0\,:\,\Lambda_{N}\subset\mathscr{I}_{\Psi_{N}}(u)\}=\sup\big\{U_{x}\,:\,x\in\Lambda_{N}\big\}\,,

(2.6)

with
U_{x}\vcentcolon=\inf\{u>0\,:\,x\in\mathscr{I}_{\Psi_{N}}(u)\}
the covering level of
x
.

Theorem 2.1

(Cover level of the interlacements)
.

Under
\mathbb{P}_{\Psi_{N}}
, we have the following asymptotics in probability:

\mathfrak{U}_{N}(\Lambda_{N})\sim g(0)\alpha_{\Lambda}^{-1}\log|\Lambda_{N}|\quad\text{with}\quad\alpha_{\Lambda}=\inf_{x\in\Lambda}\varphi^{2}(x)\,.

(2.7)

Moreover,
\mathfrak{U}_{N}(\Lambda_{N})
has “super-Gumbel” fluctuation, in the sense that for any fixed
z\in\mathbb{R}
,

\liminf_{N\to+\infty}\mathbb{P}_{\Psi_{N}}\Big(\mathfrak{U}_{N}(\Lambda_{N})\leq\mathord{\raise 0.49991pt\hbox{$\displaystyle\genfrac{}{}{0.4pt}{}{g(0)}{\alpha(\Lambda)}$}}\{\log|\Lambda_{N}|+z\}\Big)\geq\exp(-e^{-z})\,.

(2.8)

Let us define

u_{N}^{\Lambda}(z)\vcentcolon=\frac{t_{N}^{\Lambda}(z)}{N^{d}}=\frac{g(0)}{\alpha_{\Lambda}}\big\{\log|\Lambda_{N}|-\log\log|\Lambda_{N}|+z\big\}\,.

(2.9)

Our second theorem states that provided Assumption
1
holds,
\mathfrak{U}_{N}^{\Lambda}\approx u_{N}^{\Lambda}(\mathcal{G})
with
\mathcal{G}
a Gumbel random variable.

Theorem 2.2
.

Assume that Assumption
1
holds and fix
z\in\mathbb{R}
. We have

\lim_{N\to+\infty}\mathbb{P}_{\Psi_{N}}\big(\mathfrak{U}(\Lambda_{N})\leq u_{N}^{\Lambda}(z)\big)=\exp\big(-\kappa_{\Lambda}e^{-z}\big)\,,

(2.10)

where
\kappa_{\Lambda}
is the constant in Theorem
1.2
. Moreover, the following convergence holds in distribution with respect to the weak topology on the space of point measures:

\mathcal{N}_{\mathrm{RI},N}^{\Lambda,z}\vcentcolon=\sum_{x\in\Lambda_{N}}\delta_{x/N}\mathbbm{1}_{\left\{x\not\in\mathscr{I}_{\psi_{N}}(u_{N}^{\Lambda}(z))\right\}}=\sum_{x\in\Lambda_{N}}\delta_{x/N}\mathbbm{1}_{\left\{U_{x}>u_{N}^{\Lambda}(z)\right\}}\xrightarrow[N\to+\infty]{(d)}\mathcal{N}^{\Lambda,z}\,,

(2.11)

with
\mathcal{N}^{\Lambda,z}
the same Poisson point process as the one in Theorem
1.2
.

As explained in Section
1.3
, we can link Theorem
2.1
to the capacity of a point. Indeed, (
2.5
) yields

\forall x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d},\forall u>0\,,\quad\mathbb{P}_{\Psi_{N}}(x\not\in\mathscr{I}_{\Psi_{N}}(u))=\exp\big(-u\mathrm{cap}^{\Psi_{N}}(\{x\})\big)\,.

(2.12)

Note that contrary to the case of simple random walk,
\mathrm{cap}^{\Psi_{N}}(\{x\})
is not a constant. Therefore, our first task is to understand its dependence in
x
. The following proposition is crucial, and explains why
\varphi^{2}
appears in Theorem
2.1
.

Proposition 2.3
.

There are constants
c,C>0
that only depend on the dimension
d\geq 3
such that for all
N
large enough,

\sup_{x\in\Lambda_{N}}\bigg|\frac{\mathrm{cap}^{\Psi_{N}}(\{x\})}{\varphi_{N}^{2}(x)/g(0)}-1\bigg|\leq CN^{-c}\,.

(2.13)

We prove Proposition
2.3
in the next section. For now, we state a useful corollary that will be used extensively in the rest of the paper in order to replace
\mathrm{cap}^{\Psi_{N}}(\{x\})
with
\varphi_{N}^{2}(x)
.

Corollary 2.4
.

Let
f:\Lambda_{N}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+}
be non-zero and
u_{N}
be such that
u_{N}N^{-c}\to 0
(where
c
is the constant in Proposition
2.3
). Then, we have

\sum_{x\in\Lambda_{N}}f(x)\mathbb{P}\big(x\not\in\mathscr{I}_{\Psi_{N}}(u_{N})\big)\bigg/\sum_{x\in\Lambda_{N}}f(x)\exp\Big(-u_{N}\varphi_{N}^{2}(x)/g(0)\Big)\xrightarrow[N\to+\infty]{}1\,.

(2.14)

Comment 2
.

Proposition
2.3
explains why the
\log\log N
term appears in (
1.8
) and (
2.9
) while being absent in (
1.12
). Indeed, when combined with the smoothness of
\varphi_{N}
, the Proposition implies that for
x,y\in\Lambda_{N}
,

\mathrm{cap}^{\Psi_{N}}(\{x\})=\mathrm{cap}^{\Psi_{N}}(\{y\})+g(0)\nabla\varphi^{2}(x/N)\frac{|x-y|}{N}(1+\bar{o}(1))\,.

(2.15)

Recall that on the torus, we can explain the
\log N
factor by the extreme value theory: it corresponds to the logarithm of the number of “relevant” points, which are the points
z\in\mathbb{T}_{N}^{d}
such that
\mathbb{P}(U_{z}\geq U_{N}^{d})\asymp\sup_{w\in\Lambda_{N}}\mathbb{P}(U_{w}\geq U_{N}^{d})
. In the case of the torus, as
\mathrm{cap}(\{w\})
is constant, so is
\mathbb{P}(U_{w}\geq U_{N}^{d})
therefore the set of relevant points is
\Lambda_{N}
(hence a factor
\log|\Lambda_{N}|
).
In our case however, by (
2.12
) and Proposition
2.3
, one can easily show that

\begin{split}\mathbb{P}\Big(U_{z}\geq\tfrac{g(0)}{\alpha_{\Lambda}}\log|\Lambda_{N}|\Big)\asymp\sup_{w\in\Lambda_{N}}\mathbb{P}\Big(U_{w}\geq\tfrac{g(0)}{\alpha_{\Lambda}}\log|\Lambda_{N}|\Big)\Longleftrightarrow\mathrm{cap}^{\Psi_{N}}(\{z\})-\frac{\alpha_{\Lambda}}{g(0)}=\bar{\mathcal{O}}\Big(\frac{1}{\log|\Lambda_{N}|}\Big)\,.\end{split}

Using (
2.15
), this condition is satisfied only for
\asymp\frac{|\Lambda_{N}|}{\log|\Lambda_{N}|}
points, which are the relevant points in this case. In particular, when considering the extreme value theory, we are left with a factor
\log(|\Lambda_{N}|/\log|\Lambda_{N}|)=\log|\Lambda_{N}|-\log\log|\Lambda_{N}|
, thus explaining the
\log\log|\Lambda_{N}|
correction in (
2.9
).

3
Some useful estimates

3.1
Useful facts about the confined walk and eigenfunction

3.1.1
Probabilistic interpretation as a confined walk

Recall that
\mathbf{P}^{N}
is the law of the confined walk and
\mathbf{P}
is the law of SRW on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
.
It is known, by standard Markov chain theory (see e.g.
[
12
, Appendix A.4.1]
), that the transition kernel (
1.3
) is in some sense the limit, as
T\to+\infty
, of the transition kernels of the SRW conditioned to stay in
D_{N}
until time
T
. Indeed, if
\tau_{N}
is the first time the SRW exits
D_{N}
and
x\sim y
, we have

\frac{\mathbf{P}_{x}(S_{1}=y,\tau_{N}>T)}{\mathbf{P}_{x}(\tau_{N}>T)}=\frac{1}{2d}\frac{\mathbf{P}_{y}(\tau_{N}>T-1)}{\mathbf{P}_{x}(\tau_{N}>T)}=\frac{\lambda_{N}^{-1}}{2d}\frac{\lambda_{N}\mathbf{P}_{y}(\tau_{N}>T-1)}{\mathbf{P}_{x}(\tau_{N}>T)}\xrightarrow[T\to+\infty]{}\frac{\lambda_{N}^{-1}}{2d}\frac{\phi_{N}(y)}{\phi_{N}(x)}\,,

the last limit being a consequence of the fact that
\mathbf{P}_{z}(\tau_{N}>T)=\phi_{N}(z)\frac{\|\phi_{N}\|_{1}}{\|\phi_{N}\|_{2}^{2}}(\lambda_{N})^{T}+\bar{\mathcal{O}}((\beta_{N})^{T})
as
T\to\infty
, for some
\beta_{N}<\lambda_{N}
(see
[
12
, Prop. 6.9.1]
).

We also have a useful relation to compare the simple and tilted random walks.
Consider a set
\Lambda
which intersects
D_{N}
, and an event
A\in\mathcal{F}_{H_{\Lambda}}
,
i.e.
an event that depends on the trajectory of the random walk until it hits
\Lambda
.
Then, using the transition kernel
\tilde{p}_{N}(x,y)
from (
1.3
) and after telescoping the ratios of the
\phi_{N}
’s, we have

\mathbf{P}_{x}^{N}(A)=\frac{1}{\phi_{N}(x)}\mathbf{E}_{x}\Bigg[\mathbbm{1}_{A}\cdot(\lambda_{N})^{-H_{\Lambda}}\phi_{N}(S_{H_{\Lambda}})\mathbbm{1}_{\left\{H_{\Lambda}<H_{\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus D_{N}}\right\}}\Bigg]\,.

(3.1)

We can think of (
3.1
) as a Feynman-Kac representation of the eigenvector
\phi_{N}
.

3.1.2
Properties of the eigenvector

Studying the confined walk requires some understanding of the eigenvector
\phi_{N}
. With Q. Berger, we investigated in
[
4
]
some properties of
\phi_{N}
as
N\to+\infty
, which we will use in this paper. Let us compile these results to facilitate their use. Recall that we are in the setting of positive reach.

Proposition 3.1

(Regularity)
.

There is a constant
C>0
(that depends only on the domain
D
) such that, for any
x,y\in D_{N}

\big|\phi_{N}(y)-\phi_{N}(x)\big|\leq C\,\frac{d(x,y)}{N}\,.

(3.2)

Note that this regularity implies that ratios of
\phi_{N}
’s in the bulk of
D_{N}
are
0
and
+\infty
. According to
[
4
, Corollary 1.14]
, there is a positive constant
\kappa_{1}
, and some
N_{1}\geq 1
such that, for all
N\geq N_{1}
,

\kappa_{1}\leq\inf_{x,y\in B_{N}^{\varepsilon}}\frac{\phi_{N}(x)}{\phi_{N}(y)}\leq\sup_{x,y\in B_{N}^{\varepsilon}}\frac{\phi_{N}(x)}{\phi_{N}(y)}\leq\frac{1}{\kappa_{1}}\,.

(3.3)

This control of the ratios, combined with bounds on
\phi_{N}
, extends to a control on the values of
\phi_{N}
. More precisely, there is a positive constant
\kappa_{2}
, and some
N_{2}\geq 1
such that, for all
N\geq N_{2}
,

\kappa_{2}\leq\inf_{x\in B_{N}^{\varepsilon}}\phi_{N}(x)\leq\sup_{x\in B_{N}^{\varepsilon}}\phi_{N}(x)\leq\frac{1}{\kappa_{2}}\,.

(3.4)

Proposition 3.2

(Convergence)
.

For any
\eta>0
, define
D_{N}^{\eta}\vcentcolon=\{x\in D_{N},d(x,\partial D_{N})>\eta N\}
.
Then there exists a positive constant
c_{\eta}
such that

\sup_{x\in D_{N}^{\eta}}\bigg|\frac{\phi_{N}(x)}{\varphi(x/N)}-1\bigg|\leq c_{\eta}N^{-1}\,.

(3.5)

3.1.3
On the principal eigenvalue

Let us mention that it is known (see
[
23
]
or
[
4
]
for details) that
\lambda_{N}
satisfies

\lambda_{N}=1-\frac{\lambda}{2d}\frac{1}{N^{2}}(1+\bar{o}(1))\,,

(3.6)

where
\lambda=\lambda_{D}
is the first Dirichlet eigenvalue of the Laplace-Beltrami operator on
D
and
\bar{o}(1)
is a quantity that vanishes as
N\to+\infty
.

In particular, there exists
c_{0}>0
a universal constant, which can be made arbitrarily close to
\lambda
by taking
N
large enough, such that for any
T\geq 0
,

1\leq\lambda_{N}^{-T}\leq\mathrm{e}^{c_{0}T/N^{2}}\,.

(3.7)

Therefore, combining (
3.1
) and (
3.3
) yields the following inequalities:

\kappa_{1}\mathbf{P}_{x}(A)\leq\mathbf{P}^{N}_{x}(A)\leq\frac{1}{\kappa_{1}}\left[\mathrm{e}^{c_{0}}\mathbf{P}_{x}(A)+\sum_{k=1}^{+\infty}\mathrm{e}^{c_{0}(k+1)}\mathbf{P}_{x}(A,\tau_{C}\in[k,k+1)N^{2})\right]\,.

(3.8)

3.2
Green function of the tilted walk, proof of Proposition
2.3

We introduce the Green function of the tilted RW:

G^{\Psi_{N}}(x,y)=\sum_{n\geq 0}\mathbf{P}^{\Psi_{N}}_{x}(X_{n}=y)\,.

Let us stress that this Green function is
not
symmetric in
x
and
y
. When
x=y
, we simply write
G^{\Psi_{N}}(x)\vcentcolon=G^{\Psi_{N}}(x,x)
which, contrary to the Green function of the SRW, does depend on
x
.

Proposition 3.3

(Last exit decomposition)
.

For any
K\subseteq B
and any
x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
,

\mathbf{P}^{\Psi_{N}}_{x}(H_{K}<+\infty)=\sum_{z\in K}G^{\Psi_{N}}(x,z)\frac{e_{K}^{\Psi_{N}}(z)}{\lambda_{N}\phi_{N}^{2}(z)}\,.

(3.9)

Proof.

Consider
x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
and define
L_{K}\vcentcolon=\sup\{n\geq 0,X_{n}\in K\}
the last time the tilted RW is in
K
. Since the
\Psi_{N}
-tilted RW is transient, we have
L_{K}<+\infty

\mathbf{P}^{\Psi_{N}}_{x}
-a.s.
Therefore,

\begin{split}\mathbf{P}^{\Psi_{N}}_{x}(H_{K}<+\infty)=\sum_{n\geq 0}\sum_{y\in K}\mathbf{P}^{\Psi_{N}}_{x}(L_{K}=n,X_{n}=y)&=\sum_{n\geq 0}\sum_{y\in K}\mathbf{P}^{\Psi_{N}}_{x}(X_{n}=y)\mathbf{P}^{\Psi_{N}}_{y}(\bar{H}_{K}=+\infty)\\
&=\sum_{y\in K}G^{\Psi_{N}}(x,y)\mathbf{P}^{\Psi_{N}}_{y}(\bar{H}_{K}=+\infty)\,.\end{split}

By (
2.2
), we have
\mathbf{P}^{\Psi_{N}}_{y}(\bar{H}_{K}=+\infty)=e_{K}^{\Psi_{N}}(y)/\lambda_{N}\phi_{N}^{2}(y)
for all
y\in K\subseteq B
, which proves the lemma.
∎

Proposition 3.4
.

There are positive constants
c_{d},C_{d}
, independent of
N
such that, for all
x,y\in\Lambda_{N}
with
|x-y|
large enough,

\frac{c_{d}}{|x-y|^{d-2}}\leq G^{\Psi_{N}}(x,y)\leq\frac{C_{d}}{|x-y|^{d-2}}\,.

(3.10)

The proof we present in Appendix
A.1
is inspired by the heuristics given in
[
11
, Remark 2.10]
. A more general proof should be possible using Gaussian bounds (see
[
10
]
). With such estimate on the Green function, we are now in the position to prove Proposition
2.3
.

Proof of Proposition
2.3
.

Fix
\gamma\in(0,1)
and consider
B_{N}^{\gamma}
the ball centered at
x
with radius
N^{\gamma}
. We write the tilted capacity from
x
to
\partial B_{N}^{\gamma}
as

\mathcal{C}_{\Psi_{N}}(x\rightarrow\partial B_{N}^{\gamma})\vcentcolon=\mathbf{P}^{\Psi_{N}}_{x}(\bar{H}_{x}>H_{\partial B_{N}^{\gamma}})=\inf\left\{\mathcal{E}_{\Psi_{N}}(f)\,:\,f(x)=1\text{ and }\forall z\in\partial B_{N}^{\gamma},f(z)=0\right\}\,,

(3.11)

where
\mathcal{E}_{\Psi_{N}}(f)\vcentcolon=\sum_{z\sim w}[f(z)-f(w)]^{2}c_{N}(z,w)
is the Dirichlet energy associated to the conductances
c_{N}(z,w)=\Psi_{N}(z)\Psi_{N}(w)
(for
z\sim w
) (see
[
13
, Exercice 9.9]
). We also write
\mathcal{C}_{1}(x\rightarrow\partial B_{N}^{\gamma})
for the capacity associated to the SRW (with
c_{N}(z,w)=1
).
We will prove the following chain of approximation holds with polynomially decreasing error:

\mathrm{cap}^{\Psi_{N}}(\{x\})\approx\mathcal{C}_{\Psi_{N}}(x\rightarrow\partial B_{N}^{\gamma})\approx\phi_{N}^{2}(x)\mathcal{C}_{1}(x\rightarrow\partial B_{N}^{\gamma})\approx\phi_{N}^{2}(x)g(0)^{-1}\,.

First note that we have
\mathbf{P}^{\Psi_{N}}_{z}(H_{x}<+\infty)=G^{\Psi_{N}}(z,x)/G^{\Psi_{N}}(x)
and Proposition
3.4
combined with
G^{\Psi_{N}}(x)\geq 1
imply that this probability is bounded from above by
C_{d}N^{\gamma(2-d)}
uniformly in
z\in\partial B_{N}^{\gamma}
. In particular,

\begin{split}\mathbf{P}^{\Psi_{N}}_{x}(\bar{H}_{x}>H_{\partial B_{N}^{\gamma}})-\mathbf{P}^{\Psi_{N}}_{x}(\bar{H}_{x}=+\infty)&=\sum_{z\in\partial B_{N}^{\gamma}}\mathbf{P}^{\Psi_{N}}_{x}(\bar{H}_{x}<H_{\partial B_{N}^{\gamma}},X_{H_{\partial B_{N}^{\gamma}}}=z)\mathbf{P}^{\Psi_{N}}_{z}(H_{x}<+\infty)\\
&\leq\mathbf{P}^{\Psi_{N}}_{x}(\bar{H}_{x}<H_{\partial B_{N}^{\gamma}})C_{d}N^{\gamma(2-d)}\,,\end{split}

(3.12)

hence proving that

1-\frac{\mathrm{cap}^{\Psi_{N}}(\{x\})}{\mathcal{C}_{\Psi_{N}}(x\rightarrow\partial B_{N}^{\gamma})}\leq C_{d}N^{\gamma(2-d)}\,.

(3.13)

Let us focus on
\mathcal{C}_{\Psi_{N}}(x\rightarrow\partial B_{N}^{\gamma})
. Using Proposition
3.1
and the fact that
\phi_{N}\in[\kappa,\tfrac{1}{\kappa}]
uniformly in
N
, there is a constant
c>0
such that for every function
f
,

\Big|\mathcal{E}_{\Psi_{N}}(f)-\phi_{N}^{2}(x)\mathcal{E}_{1}(f)\Big|\leq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{z\sim w}[f(z)-f(w)]^{2}\big|\phi_{N}(z)\phi_{N}(w)-\phi_{N}^{2}(x)\big|\leq cN^{\gamma-1}\phi_{N}^{2}(x)\mathcal{E}_{1}(f)\,.

(3.14)

Since
N^{\gamma-1}\phi_{N}^{2}(x)\leq\kappa^{-2}N^{\gamma-1}\to 0
, combining (
3.13
) and (
3.14
), we easily deduce that

\Big|\frac{\mathcal{C}_{\Psi_{N}}(x\rightarrow\partial B_{N}^{\gamma})}{\phi_{N}^{2}(x)\mathcal{C}_{1}(x\rightarrow\partial B_{N}^{\gamma})}-1\Big|\leq c_{2}N^{\gamma-1}\,.

(3.15)

To conclude the proof, we claim that
0\leq\mathcal{C}_{1}(x\leftrightarrow\partial B_{N}^{\gamma})g(0)-1\leq c_{3}N^{\gamma(2-d)}
. This can be proved with the same method as previously by using
g(x,y)\leq C_{d}^{\prime}|x-y|^{2-d}
(see
[
12
, Theorem 4.3.1]
). Finally, using Proposition
3.2
, we have
\|\phi_{N}^{2}-\varphi_{N}^{2}\|_{\infty}\leq N^{-\mu}
for
\mu=1/2(d+1)
. Therefore, taking
c=\mu\wedge(1-\gamma)\wedge\gamma(d-2)
with the optimal
\gamma=1/(d-1)
, the proposition follows.
∎

3.3
Estimates on the capacity of certain sets

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
x,y\in B_{N}
, then

\mathrm{cap}^{\Psi_{N}}(\left\{x,y\right\})=\frac{\mathrm{cap}^{\Psi_{N}}(\{x\})\mathbf{P}^{\Psi_{N}}_{x}(H_{y}=+\infty)+\mathrm{cap}^{\Psi_{N}}(\{y\})\mathbf{P}^{\Psi_{N}}_{y}(H_{x}=+\infty)}{1-\mathbf{P}^{\Psi_{N}}_{x}(H_{y}<+\infty)\mathbf{P}^{\Psi_{N}}_{y}(H_{x}<+\infty)}\,.

(3.16)

Proof.

Write
K=\left\{x,y\right\}
and use the last exit decomposition at points
x,y\in K
. we have

1=G^{\Psi_{N}}(x)\frac{e_{K}(x)}{\lambda_{N}\phi_{N}(x)^{2}}+G^{\Psi_{N}}(x,y)\frac{e_{K}(y)}{\lambda_{N}\phi_{N}(y)^{2}}=G^{\Psi_{N}}(y,x)\frac{e_{K}(x)}{\lambda_{N}\phi_{N}(x)^{2}}+G^{\Psi_{N}}(y)\frac{e_{K}(y)}{\lambda_{N}\phi_{N}(y)^{2}}\,.

Write
f(z)=e_{K}(z)/\lambda_{N}\phi_{N}(z)^{2}
, then we can solve this system for
f(x),f(y)
:

f(x)=\frac{1}{G^{\Psi_{N}}(x)}\left[1-G^{\Psi_{N}}(x,y)f(y)\right]\quad,\quad f(y)G^{\Psi_{N}}(y)+\frac{G^{\Psi_{N}}(y,x)}{G^{\Psi_{N}}(x)}\left[1-G^{\Psi_{N}}(x,y)f(y)\right]=1

and thus

f(y)\bigg(G^{\Psi_{N}}(y)-\frac{G^{\Psi_{N}}(x,y)G^{\Psi_{N}}(y,x)}{G^{\Psi_{N}}(x)}\bigg)=1-\frac{G^{\Psi_{N}}(x,y)}{G^{\Psi_{N}}(x)}\,.

From this, we deduce

f(y)=\frac{1-\frac{G^{\Psi_{N}}(x,y)}{G^{\Psi_{N}}(x)}}{G^{\Psi_{N}}(y)-\frac{G^{\Psi_{N}}(x,y)G^{\Psi_{N}}(y,x)}{G^{\Psi_{N}}(x)}}=\frac{G^{\Psi_{N}}(x)-G^{\Psi_{N}}(x,y)}{G^{\Psi_{N}}(x)G^{\Psi_{N}}(y)-G^{\Psi_{N}}(x,y)G^{\Psi_{N}}(y,x)}\,.

Multiplying this by
\lambda_{N}\phi_{N}^{2}(y)
gives us an expression for
e_{K}(y)
, however we can first notice that
\lambda_{N}\phi_{N}^{2}(y)=\mathrm{cap}^{\Psi_{N}}(\{y\})G^{\Psi_{N}}(y)
. Therefore, we get

e_{K}(y)=\frac{\mathrm{cap}^{\Psi_{N}}(\{y\})G^{\Psi_{N}}(y)[G^{\Psi_{N}}(x)-G^{\Psi_{N}}(y,x)]}{G^{\Psi_{N}}(x)G^{\Psi_{N}}(y)-G^{\Psi_{N}}(x,y)G^{\Psi_{N}}(y,x)}\,,

(3.17)

which can be rewritten by noting that
G^{\Psi_{N}}(x,y)=\mathbf{P}^{\Psi_{N}}_{x}(H_{y}<+\infty)G^{\Psi_{N}}(y)
:

e_{K}(y)=\frac{\mathrm{cap}^{\Psi_{N}}(\{y\})[1-\mathbf{P}^{\Psi_{N}}_{y}(H_{x}<+\infty)]}{1-\mathbf{P}^{\Psi_{N}}_{x}(H_{y}<+\infty)\mathbf{P}^{\Psi_{N}}_{y}(H_{x}<+\infty)}\,,

(3.18)

By symmetry, we also deduce the expression of
e_{K}(x)
, hence proving the statement since
\mathrm{cap}^{\Psi_{N}}(\{x,y\})=e_{K}(x)+e_{K}(y)
by definition.
∎

Corollary 3.6
.

There is a constant
c_{0}>0
such that for all
N
large enough, any
x,y\in\Lambda_{N}
, we have

\mathrm{cap}^{\Psi_{N}}(\left\{x,y\right\})\geq\big[1+\tfrac{1}{4}c_{0}^{2}\big]\alpha_{\Lambda}/g(0)\,.

(3.19)

To prove Corollary
3.6
, we use the following result on the tilted walk.

Lemma 3.7
.

There is a constant
c_{0}>0
such that for all
N
large enough,

\inf_{x,y\in B,x\neq y}\mathbf{P}^{\Psi_{N}}_{x}(H_{y}=+\infty)\geq c_{0}\,.

(3.20)

Proof.

Using the Markov property and (
3.8
), we have

\begin{split}\mathbf{P}^{\Psi_{N}}_{x}(H_{y}=+\infty)&\geq\mathbf{P}^{\Psi_{N}}_{x}(H_{y}>H_{\partial B^{\varepsilon}})\inf_{z\in\partial B^{\varepsilon}}\mathbf{P}^{\Psi_{N}}_{x}(H_{y}=+\infty)\\
&\geq\kappa\mathbf{P}_{x}(H_{y}>H_{\partial B^{\varepsilon}})\inf_{z\in\partial B^{\varepsilon}}\mathbf{P}^{\Psi_{N}}_{x}(H_{y}=+\infty)\,.\end{split}

The first probability can be expressed as
1-g(x,y)/g(0)\geq 1-g(0,e)/g(0)
with
|e|=1
, and thus is bounded from below by a positive constant. The second probability is also bounded from below by a constant independent of
N
, we refer to the proof of Lemma 2.5 in
[
7
]
.
∎

Proof of Corollary
3.6
.

Note that combining Propositions
2.3
&
3.5
, we only need to prove that provided
N
large enough, we have

\frac{\mathbf{P}^{\Psi_{N}}_{x}(H_{y}=+\infty)+\mathbf{P}^{\Psi_{N}}_{y}(H_{x}=+\infty)}{1-\mathbf{P}^{\Psi_{N}}_{x}(H_{y}<+\infty)\mathbf{P}^{\Psi_{N}}_{y}(H_{x}<+\infty)}\geq 1+\frac{1}{4}c_{0}^{2}\,.

(3.21)

Observe that

\begin{split}1-&\mathbf{P}^{\Psi_{N}}_{x}(H_{y}<+\infty)\mathbf{P}^{\Psi_{N}}_{y}(H_{x}<+\infty)\\
&=\mathbf{P}^{\Psi_{N}}_{x}(H_{y}=+\infty)+\mathbf{P}^{\Psi_{N}}_{y}(H_{x}=+\infty)-\mathbf{P}^{\Psi_{N}}_{x}(H_{y}=+\infty)\mathbf{P}^{\Psi_{N}}_{y}(H_{x}=+\infty)\,.\end{split}

(3.22)

Injecting (
3.22
) in the denominator of the left-hand side of (
3.21
), we get

\begin{split}\frac{\mathbf{P}^{\Psi_{N}}_{x}(H_{y}=+\infty)+\mathbf{P}^{\Psi_{N}}_{y}(H_{x}=+\infty)}{1-\mathbf{P}^{\Psi_{N}}_{x}(H_{y}<+\infty)\mathbf{P}^{\Psi_{N}}_{y}(H_{x}<+\infty)}&\geq 1+\frac{\mathbf{P}^{\Psi_{N}}_{x}(H_{y}=+\infty)\mathbf{P}^{\Psi_{N}}_{y}(H_{x}=+\infty)}{\mathbf{P}^{\Psi_{N}}_{x}(H_{y}=+\infty)+\mathbf{P}^{\Psi_{N}}_{y}(H_{x}=+\infty)}\\
&\geq 1+\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{P}^{\Psi_{N}}_{x}(H_{y}=+\infty)\mathbf{P}^{\Psi_{N}}_{y}(H_{x}=+\infty)\,.\end{split}

(3.23)

By Lemma
3.7
we have
\mathbf{P}^{\Psi_{N}}_{x}(H_{y}=+\infty)\mathbf{P}^{\Psi_{N}}_{y}(H_{x}=+\infty)\geq c_{0}^{2}
. Therefore taking
N
large enough yields (
3.21
), thus ending the proof.
∎

When the two points
x,y
are far from each other, the capacity of
\{x,y\}
is well-approximated by the sum of the capacities of
\{x\}
and
\{y\}
. The bound
\mathrm{cap}^{\Psi_{N}}(\{x,y\})\leq\mathrm{cap}^{\Psi_{N}}(\{x\})+\mathrm{cap}^{\Psi_{N}}(\{y\})
is classical. For the lower bound, we have the following statement.

Proposition 3.8
.

For any disjoint
K_{1},K_{2}\subseteq\Lambda_{N}
, we have

\mathrm{cap}^{\Psi_{N}}(K_{1}\cup K_{2})\geq\mathrm{cap}^{\Psi_{N}}(K_{1})+\mathrm{cap}^{\Psi_{N}}(K_{2})-\lambda_{N}\sum_{z\in K_{1}}\sum_{w\in K_{2}}\left[\phi_{N}^{2}(z)G^{\Psi_{N}}(z,w)+\phi_{N}^{2}(w)G^{\Psi_{N}}(w,z)\right]\,.

(3.24)

In particular, writing
d(K_{1},K_{2})\vcentcolon=\inf\limits_{z\in K_{1},w\in K_{2}}|z-w|
, there is a constant
c_{D,d}>0
such that

\mathrm{cap}^{\Psi_{N}}(K_{1}\cup K_{2})\geq\mathrm{cap}^{\Psi_{N}}(K_{1})+\mathrm{cap}^{\Psi_{N}}(K_{2})-c_{D,d}\frac{|K_{1}|\cdot|K_{2}|}{d(K_{1},K_{2})^{d-2}}\,.

(3.25)

Proof.

For any
z\in K_{1}
, note that

\begin{split}\mathbf{P}^{\Psi_{N}}_{z}(\bar{H}_{K_{1}\cup K_{2}}=+\infty)&=\mathbf{P}^{\Psi_{N}}_{z}(\bar{H}_{K_{1}}=+\infty)-\mathbf{P}^{\Psi_{N}}_{z}(\bar{H}_{K_{1}}=+\infty,\bar{H}_{K_{2}}<+\infty)\\
&\geq\mathbf{P}^{\Psi_{N}}_{z}(\bar{H}_{K_{1}}=+\infty)-\mathbf{P}^{\Psi_{N}}_{z}(\bar{H}_{K_{2}}<+\infty)\,.\end{split}

(3.26)

Thus, we get a lower bound using (
2.2
) that reads

\begin{split}\sum_{z\in K_{1}}e^{\Psi_{N}}_{K_{1}\cup K_{2}}(z)&\geq\lambda_{N}\sum_{z\in K_{1}}\phi_{N}^{2}(z)\left[\mathbf{P}^{\Psi_{N}}_{z}(\bar{H}_{K_{1}}=+\infty)-\mathbf{P}^{\Psi_{N}}_{z}(\bar{H}_{K_{2}}<+\infty)\right]\\
&\geq\mathrm{cap}^{\Psi_{N}}(K_{1})-\lambda_{N}\sum_{z\in K_{1}}\phi_{N}^{2}(z)\mathbf{P}^{\Psi_{N}}_{z}(\bar{H}_{K_{2}}<+\infty)\,.\end{split}

(3.27)

Using the last exit decomposition (Proposition
3.3
), we deduce that

\sum_{z\in K_{1}}e^{\Psi_{N}}_{K_{1}\cup K_{2}}(z)\geq\mathrm{cap}^{\Psi_{N}}(K_{1})-\lambda_{N}\sum_{z\in K_{1}}\sum_{w\in K_{2}}\phi_{N}^{2}(z)G^{\Psi_{N}}(z,w)\mathbf{P}^{\Psi_{N}}_{w}(\bar{H}_{K_{2}}=+\infty)\,.

(3.28)

We can similarly prove

\sum_{w\in K_{2}}e^{\Psi_{N}}_{K_{1}\cup K_{2}}(w)\geq\mathrm{cap}^{\Psi_{N}}(K_{2})-\lambda_{N}\sum_{z\in K_{1}}\sum_{w\in K_{2}}\phi_{N}^{2}(w)G^{\Psi_{N}}(w,z)\mathbf{P}^{\Psi_{N}}_{z}(\bar{H}_{K_{1}}=+\infty)\,.

(3.29)

Therefore, we deduce (
3.24
) after using

\mathrm{cap}^{\Psi_{N}}(K_{1}\cup K_{2})=\sum_{z\in K_{1}}e^{\Psi_{N}}_{K_{1}\cup K_{2}}(z)+\sum_{w\in K_{2}}e^{\Psi_{N}}_{K_{1}\cup K_{2}}(w)

and bounding the probabilities in (
3.28
),(
3.29
) by
1
.
To get (
3.25
), it suffices to use the fact that
\phi_{N}
is bounded from below (recall (
3.4
)), that
\lambda_{N}\leq 1
, as well as Proposition
3.4
.
∎

Combining (
2.5
) with Corollary
3.6
and Proposition
3.8
, we get bounds on the correlations between events
\{x\not\in\mathscr{I}_{\Psi_{N}}(u_{N})\}
and
\{y\not\in\mathscr{I}_{\Psi_{N}}(u_{N})\}
which will be useful in our proofs (see (
4.4
) below).

Proposition 3.9

(Control of the correlations)
.

Let
u_{N}
be such that
u_{N}N^{-c}\to 0
, we have the following universal bound: for distinct
x,y\in\Lambda_{N}
,

\mathrm{Cov}\Big(\mathbbm{1}_{\left\{x\not\in\mathscr{I}_{\Psi_{N}}(u_{N})\right\}},\mathbbm{1}_{\left\{y\not\in\mathscr{I}_{\Psi_{N}}(u_{N})\right\}}\Big)\leq\mathbb{P}\big(x,y\not\in\mathscr{I}_{\Psi_{N}}(u_{N})\big)\leq\exp\Big(-u_{N}\tfrac{\alpha_{\Lambda}}{g(0)}(1+\tfrac{1}{4}c_{0}^{2})\Big)\,.

(3.30)

Moreover, there is a constant
c_{d}^{\rho,\Lambda}>0
such that for any sequence
a_{N}\to+\infty
and any
x,y\in\Lambda_{N}
such that
|x-y|\geq a_{N}
, provided
N
large enough we have

\mathrm{Cov}\Big(\mathbbm{1}_{\left\{x\not\in\mathscr{I}_{\Psi_{N}}(u_{N})\right\}},\mathbbm{1}_{\left\{y\not\in\mathscr{I}_{\Psi_{N}}(u_{N})\right\}}\Big)\leq 2\exp\Big(-u_{N}\tfrac{\varphi_{N}^{2}(x)+\varphi_{N}^{2}(y)}{g(0)}\Big)\Big[\exp\Big(c_{d}^{\Lambda}\frac{u_{N}}{a_{N}^{d-2}}\Big)-1\Big]

(3.31)

Proof.

The first inequality is a direct consequence of Corollary
3.6
applied to

\mathbb{P}\big(x,y\not\in\mathscr{I}_{\Psi_{N}}(u_{N})\big)=\exp\Big(-u_{N}\mathrm{cap}^{\Psi_{N}}(\{x,y\}\big)\Big)\,.

(3.32)

For the second inequality, we use Proposition
3.8
-(
3.25
) to get

\mathrm{cap}^{\Psi_{N}}(\{x,y\})\geq\mathrm{cap}^{\Psi_{N}}(\{x\})+\mathrm{cap}^{\Psi_{N}}(\{x\})-\frac{c_{d}^{\Lambda}}{|x-y|^{d-2}}\,.

(3.33)

Now, using the formula (
2.5
), we get

\mathbb{P}\big(x,y\not\in\mathscr{I}_{\Psi_{N}}(u_{N})\big)\leq\mathbb{P}\big(x\not\in\mathscr{I}_{\Psi_{N}}(u_{N})\big)\mathbb{P}\big(y\not\in\mathscr{I}_{\Psi_{N}}(u_{N})\big)\exp\Big(u_{N}\frac{c_{d}^{\Lambda}}{|x-y|^{d-2}}\Big)\,,

(3.34)

thus proving the bound after using (
2.5
) and Proposition
2.3
to replace
\mathrm{cap}^{\Psi_{N}}
by
\varphi_{N}^{2}/g(0)
(with a factor
2
taking into account the error of this replacement).
∎

4
Covering level of
\Lambda_{N}
- study of the intermediary set

4.1
Some technical considerations

The general principle is the same as Belius’ approach for the covering level on standard random interlacements. It relies on the following identity, which is a direct consequence of the Poisson structure of random interlacements: let
K\subset\mathbb{Z}^{d}
be a finite set and
u_{1}<u_{2}
, then for any
K^{\prime}\subset K
,

\mathbb{P}\big(K\subseteq\mathscr{I}_{\Psi_{N}}(u_{2})\big)=\mathbb{P}\big(K\setminus K^{\prime}\subseteq\mathscr{I}_{\Psi_{N}}(u_{2}-u_{1})\big)\cdot\mathbb{P}\big(K\cap\mathscr{I}_{\Psi_{N}}(u_{1})=K^{\prime}\big)\,.

(4.1)

The key point that Belius’ approach exploits is that near the covering time of
\Lambda_{N}
, the set of points that are yet to be covered is “well-separated”, which makes it easier to study thanks to decorrelation inequalities.

In the rest of the paper, we fix
\rho>0
small enough and define the intermediary set of “late points” as

\Lambda_{N}(\rho)\vcentcolon=\big\{x\in\Lambda_{N}\,:\,x\not\in\mathscr{I}_{\Psi_{N}}(u_{N}^{\Lambda,\rho})\big\}\quad\text{with}\quad u_{N}^{\Lambda,\rho}\vcentcolon=(1-\rho)\tfrac{g(0)}{\alpha_{\Lambda}}\log|\Lambda_{N}|\,.

(4.2)

Applying (
4.1
) then yields

\mathbb{P}\big(\mathfrak{U}_{N}^{\Lambda}\leq u_{N}^{\Lambda}(z)\,\big|\,\Lambda_{N}(\rho)\big)=\mathbb{P}\big(\Lambda_{N}(\rho)\subseteq\mathscr{I}_{\Psi_{N}}(u_{N}^{\Lambda}(z)-u_{N}^{\Lambda,\rho})\big)\,.

(4.3)

In order to get some estimates, we will often use two results in combination in order to “integrate” a function on
\Lambda_{N}(\rho)
. Let
f:\Lambda_{N}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
, we first have

\mathrm{Var}\Big[\sum_{z\in\Lambda_{N}(\rho)}f(z)\Big]=\sum_{x,y\in\Lambda_{N}}f(x)f(y)\mathrm{Cov}\big(\mathbbm{1}_{\left\{x\in\Lambda_{N}(\rho)\right\}},\mathbbm{1}_{\left\{y\in\Lambda_{N}(\rho)\right\}}\big)\,.

(4.4)

The proof of (
4.4
) is straightforward. Coupled with Proposition
3.9
, this allows us to get upper bounds on the variance of such “integral” without consideration for the actual random set
\Lambda_{N}(\rho)
.

The following lemma states a useful asymptotic that we use to fully exploit (
4.4
) in the case where
f
is either constant or an exponential. We postpone its proof to Appendix
A.2
, as it is quite technical and relies on Assumption
1
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Fix
\beta>0
, then under Assumption
1
we have the convergence

\lim_{N\to+\infty}\frac{\log|\Lambda_{N}|}{|\Lambda_{N}|^{1-\beta}}\sum_{x\in\Lambda_{N}}\exp\Big(-\beta\frac{\varphi_{N}^{2}(x)}{\alpha_{\Lambda}}\log|\Lambda_{N}|\Big)=\frac{\kappa_{\Lambda}}{\beta}\,.

(4.5)

Moreover, the convergence still holds if
\log|\Lambda_{N}|
is replaced by
(1+\varepsilon_{N})\log|\Lambda_{N}|
with
(\varepsilon_{N})_{N\geq 1}
a vanishing sequence.

The sum in (
4.5
) naturally appears due to (
2.5
) and the fact that
\varphi_{N}^{2}(x)\approx\mathrm{cap}^{\Psi_{N}}(\{x\})
(recall Proposition
2.3
).

4.2
Scattering of the late point

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
\rho>0
be small enough and define
a_{N}^{\rho}\vcentcolon=|\Lambda_{N}|^{\frac{4\rho}{d-2}}
, then

\sum_{0<|x-y|\leq a_{N}^{\rho}}\mathbb{P}\big(x,y\in\Lambda_{N}(\rho)\big)\lesssim|\Lambda_{N}|^{-\rho}\,.

(4.6)

This holds whether or not Assumption
1
is satisfied.

Proof.

We separate the sum depending on whether
|x-y|\leq(\log|\Lambda_{N}|)^{2}
or not. For very close
x
and
y
, the universal bound of Corollary
3.6
suffices and we get

\sum_{0<|x-y|\leq(\log|\Lambda_{N}|)^{2}}\mathbb{P}\big(x,y\in\Lambda_{N}(\rho)\big)\leq c(\log|\Lambda_{N}|)^{2d}|\Lambda_{N}|\exp\big(-(1-\rho)(1+\tfrac{1}{4}c_{0}^{2})\log|\Lambda_{N}|\big)\,,

(4.7)

which is equal to
c(\log|\Lambda_{N}|)^{2d}|\Lambda_{N}|^{\frac{1}{4}c_{0}^{2}-\rho(1+\frac{1}{4}c_{0}^{2})}
, which is less than
c^{\prime}|\Lambda_{N}|^{-\rho}
provided
\rho>0
small enough.

For the
x,y
that are farther away, we instead use Proposition
3.8
-(
3.25
) to get

\mathrm{cap}^{\Psi_{N}}(\left\{x,y\right\})\geq\mathrm{cap}^{\Psi_{N}}(\left\{x\right\})+\mathrm{cap}^{\Psi_{N}}(\left\{y\right\})-\frac{c_{d}}{(\log|\Lambda_{N}|)^{2d-4}}\,.

(4.8)

Therefore, also using Proposition
2.3
we deduce

\mathbb{P}\big(x,y\in\Lambda_{N}(\rho)\big)\leq(1+\bar{o}(1))\exp\Big(-(1-\rho)\tfrac{\varphi_{N}^{2}(x)+\varphi_{N}^{2}(y)}{\alpha_{\Lambda}}\log|\Lambda_{N}|+\frac{c_{d}}{(\log|\Lambda_{N}|)^{2d-5}}\Big)\,.

(4.9)

Again, as
d\geq 3
we have
(\log|\Lambda_{N}|)^{2d-5}\to+\infty
and thus

\sum_{(\log|\Lambda_{N}|)^{2}<|x-y|\leq a_{N}^{\rho}}\mathbb{P}\big(x,y\in\Lambda_{N}(\rho)\big)\leq c\sum_{(\log|\Lambda_{N}|)^{2}<|x-y|\leq a_{N}^{\rho}}\exp\Big(-(1-\rho)\tfrac{\varphi_{N}^{2}(x)+\varphi_{N}^{2}(y)}{\alpha_{\Lambda}}\log|\Lambda_{N}|\Big)\,.

(4.10)

Since
\varphi_{N}^{2}(y)\geq\alpha_{\Lambda}
by definition, we get that this is less than

c\frac{(a_{N}^{\rho})^{d}}{|\Lambda_{N}|^{1-\rho}}\sum_{x\in\Lambda_{N}}\exp\Big(-(1-\rho)\tfrac{\varphi_{N}^{2}(x)}{\alpha_{\Lambda}}\log|\Lambda_{N}|\Big)=c^{\prime}\frac{(a_{N}^{\rho})^{d}}{|\Lambda_{N}|^{1-\rho}}(1+\bar{o}(1))\frac{\kappa_{\Lambda}}{1-\rho}\frac{|\Lambda_{N}|^{\rho}}{\log|\Lambda_{N}|}\,,

(4.11)

where we used Lemma
4.1
for the equality.
Injecting the definition of
a_{N}^{\rho}
, we see that provided
N
large enough and
\rho
small enough so that
1-\rho>\tfrac{4\rho d}{d-2}+2\rho
, this is less that some constant times
|\Lambda_{N}|^{-\rho}
, hence proving the proposition. Note that instead of using Lemma
4.1
, we could also note that
\varphi_{N}^{2}(x)\geq\alpha_{\Lambda}
which provides a bound
c^{\prime\prime}(a_{N}^{\rho})^{d}|\Lambda_{N}|^{2\rho-1}=c^{\prime\prime}|\Lambda_{N}|^{2\rho+\frac{4\rho d}{d-2}-1}\leq c^{\prime\prime}|\Lambda_{N}|^{-\rho}
provided
1-2\rho>\tfrac{4\rho d}{d-2}+2\rho
. Thus, the proposition also holds in the general case (without Assumption
1
.
∎

4.3
Cardinality of the set of late points

Proposition 4.3
.

Under Assumption
1
and provided
\rho>0
small enough, we have the following equivalence in probability:

|\Lambda_{N}(\rho)|\sim\frac{\kappa_{\Lambda}}{1-\rho}\frac{|\Lambda_{N}|^{\rho}}{\log|\Lambda_{N}|}

(4.12)

Proof.

First observe that according to (
2.5
) and Corollary
2.4
:

\mathbb{E}\big[|\Lambda_{N}(\rho)|\big]=\sum_{x\in\Lambda_{N}}\mathbb{P}\big(x\not\in\mathscr{I}_{\Psi_{N}}(u_{N}^{\rho})\big)=(1+\bar{o}(1))\sum_{x\in\Lambda_{N}}\exp\Big(-(1-\rho)\frac{\varphi_{N}^{2}(x)}{\alpha_{\Lambda}}\log|\Lambda_{N}|\Big)\,.

(4.13)

Now, using Lemma
4.1
, we get that this is asymptotically
\kappa_{\Lambda}|\Lambda_{N}|^{\rho}/(1-\rho)\log|\Lambda_{N}|
.

Let us now prove that
\mathrm{Var}[|\Lambda_{N}(\rho)|]
is at most of order
\mathbb{E}\big[|\Lambda_{N}(\rho)|\big]
, which will prove the proposition using Chebychev inequality.

Applying (
4.4
), we have

\mathrm{Var}[|\Lambda_{N}(\rho)|]=\sum_{x,y\in\Lambda_{N}}\mathrm{Cov}\big(\mathbbm{1}_{\left\{x\in\Lambda_{N}(\rho)\right\}},\mathbbm{1}_{\left\{y\in\Lambda_{N}(\rho)\right\}}\big)\,.

(4.14)

Let us consider separately three cases in order to control the covariances.

First, for
x=y
, this covariance is less than
\mathbb{P}(x\in\Lambda_{N}(\rho))
and thus

\sum_{x=y\in\Lambda_{N}}\mathrm{Cov}\big(\mathbbm{1}_{\left\{x\in\Lambda_{N}(\rho)\right\}},\mathbbm{1}_{\left\{y\in\Lambda_{N}(\rho)\right\}}\big)\leq\sum_{x\in\Lambda_{N}}\mathbb{P}(x\in\Lambda_{N}(\rho))=\mathbb{E}\big[|\Lambda_{N}(\rho)|\big]\,.

(4.15)

Then, assume
0<|x-y|\leq a_{N}^{\rho}
and use the previous Proposition
4.2
to get

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x,y\in\Lambda_{N}\\
0<|x-y|\leq a_{N}^{\rho}\end{subarray}}\mathrm{Cov}\big(\mathbbm{1}_{\left\{x\in\Lambda_{N}(\rho)\right\}},\mathbbm{1}_{\left\{y\in\Lambda_{N}(\rho)\right\}}\big)\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x,y\in\Lambda_{N}\\
0<|x-y|\leq a_{N}^{\rho}\end{subarray}}\mathbb{P}(x,y\in\Lambda_{N}(\rho))\lesssim|\Lambda_{N}|^{-\rho}\,.

(4.16)

Finally, assume
|x-y|>a_{N}^{\rho}
in which case we use the bound (
3.31
) to get

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x,y\in\Lambda_{N}\\
|x-y|>a_{N}^{\rho}\end{subarray}}\mathrm{Cov}\big(\mathbbm{1}_{\left\{x\in\Lambda_{N}(\rho)\right\}},\mathbbm{1}_{\left\{y\in\Lambda_{N}(\rho)\right\}}\big)\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x,y\in\Lambda_{N}\\
|x-y|>a_{N}^{\rho}\end{subarray}}\exp\Big(-(1-\rho)\tfrac{\varphi_{N}^{2}(x)+\varphi_{N}^{2}(y)}{\alpha_{\Lambda}}\Big)\Big[\exp\Big(c_{d}^{\Lambda}\frac{u_{N}^{\rho}}{a_{N}^{d-2}}\Big)-1\Big]\,.

(4.17)

Writing this double sum as a product, since
u_{N}^{\rho}\ll a_{N}^{d-2}
, provided
N
large enough, we get

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x,y\in\Lambda_{N}\\
|x-y|>a_{N}^{\rho}\end{subarray}}\mathrm{Cov}\big(\mathbbm{1}_{\left\{x\in\Lambda_{N}(\rho)\right\}},\mathbbm{1}_{\left\{y\in\Lambda_{N}(\rho)\right\}}\big)\leq 2c_{d}^{\Lambda}\frac{u_{N}^{\rho}}{a_{N}^{d-2}}\Big(\sum_{x\in\Lambda_{N}}\exp\big(-(1-\rho)\tfrac{\varphi_{N}^{2}(x)}{\alpha_{\Lambda}}\log|\Lambda_{N}|\big)\Big)^{2}\,.

(4.18)

Therefore, using Lemma
4.1
, we get

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x,y\in\Lambda_{N}\\
|x-y|>a_{N}^{\rho}\end{subarray}}\mathrm{Cov}\big(\mathbbm{1}_{\left\{x\in\Lambda_{N}(\rho)\right\}},\mathbbm{1}_{\left\{y\in\Lambda_{N}(\rho)\right\}}\big)\lesssim\frac{\log|\Lambda_{N}|}{|\Lambda_{N}|^{2\rho}}\times\frac{|\Lambda_{N}|^{4\rho}}{\log^{2}|\Lambda_{N}|}=\frac{|\Lambda_{N}|^{-2\rho}}{\log|\Lambda_{N}|}\,.

(4.19)

Combining all of the above yields
\mathrm{Var}[|\Lambda_{N}(\rho)|]\leq c\mathbb{E}\big[|\Lambda_{N}(\rho)|\big]
for
N
large enough, hence the asymptotics
|\Lambda_{N}(\rho)|\sim\mathbb{E}\big[|\Lambda_{N}(\rho)|\big]
in probability using Chebychev’s inequality.
∎

Corollary 4.4
.

Provided
\rho,\delta>0
small enough, there is a
c_{\rho,\delta}>0
such that for all
N
large enough, with probability at least
1-c_{\rho,\delta}|\Lambda_{N}|^{-\rho/4}
we have

|\Lambda_{N}|^{\rho-\delta(1-\rho)}\leq|\Lambda_{N}(\rho)|\leq|\Lambda_{N}|^{\rho+N^{-c}}+|\Lambda_{N}|^{2\rho/3}\,.

(4.20)

Proof.

With again the trivial bound
\mathrm{cap}^{\Psi_{N}}(\{x\})\geq\varphi_{N}^{2}(x)(1-N^{-c})\geq\alpha_{\Lambda}(1-N^{-c})
on
\Lambda_{N}
(recall Proposition
2.3
), we get
\mathbb{E}[|\Lambda_{N}(\rho)|]\leq|\Lambda_{N}|^{\rho+N^{-c}(1-\rho)}
. For the lower bound, we get however that for any
\delta>0
,

\mathbb{E}\left[|\Lambda_{N}(\rho)|\right]\geq|\Lambda_{N}|^{-(1-\rho)(1+\delta)}\Big|\big\{x\in\Lambda_{N}\,:\,\mathrm{cap}^{\Psi_{N}}(\left\{x\right\})\leq(1+\delta)\alpha_{\Lambda}/G(0)\big\}\Big|\,.

(4.21)

Using the regularity of
\phi_{N}
and Proposition
2.3
, we conclude that there exists a positive constant
c_{\Lambda,\delta}
such that
\Big|\left\{x\in\Lambda_{N}\,:\,\mathrm{cap}^{\Psi_{N}}(\{x\})\leq(1+\delta)\alpha(\Lambda)/G(0)\right\}\Big|\geq c_{\Lambda,\delta}|\Lambda_{N}|
. Therefore, we get that for any
\delta>0
, we have
\mathbb{E}\left[|\Lambda_{N}(\rho)|\right]\geq|\Lambda_{N}|^{\rho-\delta(1-\rho)}
.
We then show without difficulty that
\mathrm{Var}\left(|\Lambda_{N}(\rho)|\right)\leq c_{\rho}^{\Lambda}|\Lambda_{N}|^{\rho+\delta_{N}(1-\rho)}
using the same proof as Proposition
4.3
(and the bound
\varphi_{N}^{2}\geq\alpha_{\Lambda}
) and concluding using Chebychev inequality.
∎

5
Covering by interlacements and Poisson limit

5.1
Fluctuations of the covering level

Let us first deal with the fluctuations of the covering time. We begin with the proof of Theorem
2.1
-(
2.7
), that is the asymptotics
\mathfrak{U}_{N}(\Lambda_{N})\sim g(0)\alpha_{\Lambda}^{-1}\log|\Lambda_{N}|
in probability. We recall that this asymptotics does not require the Assumption
1
to hold.

Recall that we defined
\Lambda_{N}(\rho)
as the set of points that are not covered at the level
u_{N,\Lambda}^{\rho}=(1-\rho)\tfrac{g(0)}{\alpha_{\Lambda}}\log|\Lambda_{N}|
.

Proof of Theorem
2.1
-(
2.7
) (first order asymptotics).

Write
u_{N}^{\Lambda}\vcentcolon=g(0)\alpha_{\Lambda}^{-1}\log|\Lambda_{N}|
and fix some
\delta>0
.
We first notice that using Corollary
4.4
,

\mathbb{P}\big(\mathfrak{U}_{N}(\Lambda_{N})\leq(1-\delta)u_{N}^{\Lambda}\big)=\mathbb{P}\big(\Lambda_{N}(\delta)=\varnothing\big)\leq\mathbb{P}\big(|\Lambda_{N}(\delta)|\leq|\Lambda_{N}|^{\delta/8}\big)\leq|\Lambda_{N}|^{-\delta/4}\,.

(5.1)

On the other hand, similarly to the proof of the previous lemma, we have

\mathbb{P}\big(\mathfrak{U}_{N}(\Lambda_{N})\geq(1+\delta)u_{N}(\Lambda)\big)\leq(1+\bar{o}(1))\sum_{x\in\Lambda_{N}}e^{-\frac{\varphi_{N}^{2}(x)}{\alpha_{\Lambda}}(1+\varepsilon)\log|\Lambda_{N}|}\leq|\Lambda_{N}|^{-\delta}

(5.2)

Therefore, the two probabilities go to zero, hence proving
\mathfrak{U}_{N}(\Lambda_{N})\sim u_{N}^{\Lambda}
in probability.
∎

If we want to get more precise results, we need to better understand the covering of the late points.
As previously stated, the proof of Theorems
2.1
&
2.2
relies on the fact that
\Lambda_{N}(\rho)
is very sparse, and thus each of its points are covered independently from the others.

We define the following “good” event for
\Lambda_{N}(\rho)
:

\mathcal{A}_{N,\Lambda}^{\rho}\vcentcolon=\Big\{|\Lambda_{N}(\rho)|\leq c_{\rho}|\Lambda_{N}|^{\rho}\;;\;\inf_{x,y\in\Lambda_{N}(\rho),x\neq y}|x-y|\geq a_{N}^{\rho}\Big\}\,.

(5.3)

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
u=u(N)
that grows logarithmically in
N
. We have the a.s. convergence

\lim_{N\to+\infty}\Big|\mathbb{P}\Big(\mathfrak{U}_{N}^{\Lambda}\leq u_{N}^{\Lambda,\rho}+u\,\Big|\,\Lambda_{N}(\rho)\Big)-\exp\Big(-\sum_{x\in\Lambda_{N}(\rho)}\exp\big(-\tfrac{u}{g(0)}\varphi_{N}^{2}(x)\big)\Big)\Big|\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{A}_{N,\Lambda}^{\rho}}=0\,.

(5.4)

Proof.

Recall (
4.1
): by the Poisson structure of the tilted RI, conditioning on
\Lambda_{N}(\rho)
we get

\mathbb{P}\Big(\mathfrak{U}_{N}^{\Lambda}\leq u_{N}^{\Lambda}(z)\,\Big|\,\Lambda_{N}(\rho)\Big)=\mathbb{P}\Big(\Lambda_{N}(\rho)\subseteq\mathscr{I}_{\Psi_{N}}(u)\Big)\,,

(5.5)

where the
\mathbb{P}
is only on
\mathscr{I}_{\Psi_{N}}(u)
and
\Lambda_{N}(\rho)
is now a fixed set.
We now turn to the study of the probability on the right-hand side of (
5.5
). Since we are on
\mathcal{A}_{N,\Lambda}^{\rho}
, applying Proposition
A.2
gives

\Big|\mathbb{P}\Big(\Lambda_{N}(\rho)\subset\mathscr{I}_{\Psi_{N}}(u)\Big)-\prod_{x\in\Lambda_{N}(\rho)}\mathbb{P}\big(x\in\mathscr{I}_{\Psi_{N}}(u)\big)\Big|\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{A}_{N,\Lambda}^{\rho}}\leq c\log|\Lambda_{N}|\frac{|\Lambda_{N}|^{\rho}}{(a_{N}^{\rho})^{d-2}}\,,

(5.6)

where we used
u\leq c\log|\Lambda_{N}|
. Recall that
a_{N}^{\rho}=|\Lambda_{N}|^{\frac{4\rho}{d-2}}
, hence the right-hand side of (
5.6
) goes to
0
as
N\to+\infty
.

Let us now investigate the product of probabilities over
x\in\Lambda_{N}(\rho)
, that we can rewrite

\exp\Big(\sum_{x\in\Lambda_{N}(\rho)}\log\Big[1-e^{-u\mathrm{cap}^{\Psi_{N}}(\{x\})}\Big]\Big)=\exp\Big(-\sum_{x\in\Lambda_{N}(\rho)}\exp\big(-u\varphi_{N}^{2}(x)(1+\bar{\mathcal{O}}(N^{-c}\vee e^{-Cu})\big)\Big]\Big)\,,

with
\bar{\mathcal{O}}(N^{-c}\vee e^{-Cu})=\bar{o}(|\Lambda_{N}|^{-c^{\prime}})
that is deterministic and uniform in
x\in\Lambda_{N}
.
∎

Let us first consider the general case (that is without Assumption
1
), which is easily handled using Lemma
5.1
.

Proof of Theorem
2.1
-(
2.8
).

We use Lemma
5.1
with
u=\tfrac{g(0)}{\alpha_{\Lambda}}\big\{\rho\log|\Lambda_{N}|+z\big\}
: for any
\eta>0
, provided
N
large enough,

\begin{split}\mathbb{P}\Big(\mathfrak{U}_{N}^{\Lambda}\leq u_{N}^{\Lambda,\rho}+u\,\Big|\,\Lambda_{N}(\rho)\Big)\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{A}_{N,\Lambda}^{\rho}}\geq(1-\delta)\exp\Big(-|\Lambda_{N}(\rho)||\Lambda_{N}|^{-\rho}e^{-z}\Big)\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{A}_{N,\Lambda}^{\rho}}\,.\end{split}

(5.7)

where we also used
\varphi_{N}^{2}(x)\geq\alpha_{\Lambda}
for all
x\in\Lambda_{N}
.
Now, since we can further restrict ourselves to the event
|\Lambda_{N}(\rho)|\leq|\Lambda_{N}|^{\rho+N^{-c}}
(recall Corollary
4.4
), we get that if
N
is large enough, this is greater than
(1-2\delta)\exp(-e^{-z})\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{A}_{N,\Lambda}^{\rho}}
. Since
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{A}_{N,\Lambda}^{\rho})\to 1
, we finally get (
2.8
).
∎

We now turn to the case where
\Lambda
satisfies Assumption
1
. Fix
z\in\mathbb{R}
and apply Lemma
5.1
with
u=u_{N}^{\Lambda}(z)-u_{N}^{\Lambda,\rho}
so that we are left to study

\sum_{x\in\Lambda_{N}(\rho)}\exp\Big(-\frac{\varphi_{N}^{2}(x)}{\alpha_{\Lambda}}\big[\rho\log|\Lambda_{N}|-\log\log|\Lambda_{N}|+z\big]\Big)

(5.8)

We will use the following proposition.

Proposition 5.2
.

Fix
\rho>0
small enough, then under Assumption
1
we have the convergence in probability

Z_{N,\Lambda}^{\rho}\vcentcolon=\frac{\log|\Lambda_{N}|}{|\Lambda_{N}|^{\rho}}\sum_{x\in\Lambda_{N}(\rho)}\exp\Big(-\rho\big[\tfrac{\varphi_{N}^{2}(x)}{\alpha_{\Lambda}}-1\big]\log|\Lambda_{N}|\Big)\xrightarrow[N\to+\infty]{\mathbb{P}}\kappa_{\Lambda}\,.

(5.9)

Moreover, this limit also holds if we replace
\log|\Lambda_{N}|
by
(1+\varepsilon_{N})\log|\Lambda_{N}|
where
\varepsilon_{N}\to 0
.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
-(
2.10
).

Writing
\varepsilon_{N}=(-\log\log|\Lambda_{N}|+z)/\rho\log|\Lambda_{N}|
, we can rewrite the sum in (
5.8
) as

e^{-z}\frac{\log|\Lambda_{N}|}{|\Lambda_{N}|^{\rho}}\sum_{x\in\Lambda_{N}(\rho)}\exp\Big(-\rho\big[\tfrac{\varphi_{N}^{2}(x)}{\alpha_{\Lambda}}-1\big](1+\varepsilon_{N})\log|\Lambda_{N}|\Big)\xrightarrow[N\to+\infty]{\mathbb{P}}\kappa_{\Lambda}e^{-z}\,,

(5.10)

where we used Proposition
5.2
.
Therefore, injecting (
5.10
) in Lemma
5.1
, we get

\Big|\mathbb{P}\big(\mathfrak{U}_{N}^{\Lambda}\leq u_{N}^{\Lambda}(z)\,\big|\,\Lambda_{N}(\rho)\big)-\exp\big(-\kappa_{\Lambda}e^{-z}\big)\Big|\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{A}_{N,\Lambda}^{\rho}}\xrightarrow[N\to+\infty]{\mathbb{P}}0\,.

(5.11)

Since
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{A}_{N,\Lambda}^{\rho})\to 1
, we get the desired convergence
\mathbb{P}\big(\mathfrak{U}_{N}^{\Lambda}\leq u_{N}^{\Lambda}(z)\,\big|\,\Lambda_{N}(\rho)\big)\to\exp\big(-\kappa_{\Lambda}e^{-z}\big)
in
L^{1}
by dominated convergence. The theorem follows immediately.
∎

Proof of Proposition
5.2
.

We first notice that we can rewrite
Z_{N,\Lambda}^{\rho}
as

Z_{N,\Lambda}^{\rho}=\frac{\log|\Lambda_{N}|}{|\Lambda_{N}|^{\rho}}\sum_{x\in\Lambda_{N}}\exp\Big(-\rho\big[\tfrac{\varphi_{N}^{2}(x)}{\alpha_{\Lambda}}-1\big]\log|\Lambda_{N}|\Big)\mathbbm{1}_{\left\{x\in\Lambda_{N}(\rho)\right\}}\,.

(5.12)

Thus, we can easily compute its expectation using the explicit formula (
2.5
) as well as Lemma
4.1
with
\beta=1
. We get

\lim_{N\to+\infty}\mathbb{E}\big[Z_{N,\Lambda}^{\rho}\big]=\lim_{N\to+\infty}\log|\Lambda_{N}|\sum_{x\in\Lambda_{N}}\exp\Big(-\tfrac{\varphi_{N}^{2}(x)}{\alpha_{\Lambda}}\log|\Lambda_{N}|\Big)=\kappa_{\Lambda}\,.

(5.13)

Let us now prove that
\mathrm{Var}\big[Z_{N,\Lambda}^{\rho}\big]\to 0
, which will prove the proposition.

Using the formula (
4.4
), we write

\mathrm{Var}\big[Z_{N,\Lambda}^{\rho}\big]=(\log|\Lambda_{N}|)^{2}\sum_{x,y\in\Lambda_{N}}\exp\Big(-\rho\tfrac{\varphi_{N}^{2}(x)+\varphi_{N}^{2}(y)}{\alpha_{\Lambda}}\log|\Lambda_{N}|\Big)\mathrm{Cov}\big(\mathbbm{1}_{\left\{x\in\Lambda_{N}(\rho)\right\}},\mathbbm{1}_{\left\{y\in\Lambda_{N}(\rho)\right\}}\big)\,.

(5.14)

We will subdivize this sum into three different parts:
x=y
; close but distinct
x,y
; and
x,y
that are far away from each other.

Control for
x=y
:
here we consider the sum

\sum_{x\in\Lambda_{N}}\exp\Big(-2\rho\tfrac{\varphi_{N}^{2}(x)}{\alpha_{\Lambda}}\log|\Lambda_{N}|\Big)\Big[\mathbb{P}\big(x\in\Lambda_{N}(\rho)\big)-\mathbb{P}\big(x\in\Lambda_{N}(\rho)\big)^{2}\Big]

(5.15)

which we bound from above by

\sum_{x\in\Lambda_{N}}\exp\Big(-\big[2\rho\tfrac{\varphi_{N}^{2}(x)}{\alpha_{\Lambda}}+(1-\rho)\tfrac{\varphi_{N}^{2}(x)}{\alpha_{\Lambda}}\big]\log|\Lambda_{N}|\Big)=\sum_{x\in\Lambda_{N}}\exp\Big(-(\rho+1)\tfrac{\varphi_{N}^{2}(x)}{\alpha_{\Lambda}}\log|\Lambda_{N}|\Big)\,.

(5.16)

Using Lemma
4.1
, we get

\lim_{N\to+\infty}(\log|\Lambda_{N}|)^{2}\mathcal{I}_{N,\rho}^{0}=\lim_{N\to+\infty}(\log|\Lambda_{N}|)|\Lambda_{N}|^{-\rho}=0\,.

(5.17)

Control for close
x,y
:

We first use the universal bound given by Corollary
3.6
on the
x,y
such that
0<|x-y|\leq\log^{2}|\Lambda_{N}|
as well as Lemma
4.1
to get

\begin{split}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x,y\in\Lambda_{N}\\
0<|x-y|\leq\log^{2}|\Lambda_{N}|\end{subarray}}\exp\Big(-\rho\tfrac{\varphi_{N}^{2}(x)+\varphi_{N}^{2}(y)}{\alpha_{\Lambda}}&\log|\Lambda_{N}|\Big)\mathrm{Cov}\big(\mathbbm{1}_{\left\{x\in\Lambda_{N}(\rho)\right\}},\mathbbm{1}_{\left\{y\in\Lambda_{N}(\rho)\right\}}\big)\\
&\leq c_{1}\frac{\log^{2}|\Lambda_{N}|}{|\Lambda_{N}|^{(1-\rho)(1+\tfrac{1}{4}c_{0}^{2})}}\frac{|\Lambda_{N}|^{1-\rho}}{\log|\Lambda_{N}|}=\frac{c_{1}\log|\Lambda_{N}|}{|\Lambda_{N}|^{(1-\rho)\tfrac{1}{4}c_{0}^{2}}}\,.\end{split}

(5.18)

Recall that
a_{N}^{\rho}=|\Lambda_{N}|^{\frac{4d\rho}{d-2}}
. We then turn to the
x,y\in\Lambda_{N}
such that
(\log|\Lambda_{N}|)^{2}<|x-y|<a_{N}^{\rho}
. Using the bound (
3.31
) as well as
\varphi_{N}^{2}(y)\geq\alpha_{\Lambda}
, we get

\begin{split}&\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x,y\in\Lambda_{N}\\
\log^{2}|\Lambda_{N}|<|x-y|\leq a_{N}\end{subarray}}\exp\Big(-\rho\tfrac{\varphi_{N}^{2}(x)+\varphi_{N}^{2}(y)}{\alpha_{\Lambda}}\log|\Lambda_{N}|\Big)\mathrm{Cov}\big(\mathbbm{1}_{\left\{x\in\Lambda_{N}(\rho)\right\}},\mathbbm{1}_{\left\{y\in\Lambda_{N}(\rho)\right\}}\big)\\
&\leq c_{2}\frac{(a_{N}^{\rho})^{d}\wedge|\Lambda_{N}|}{|\Lambda_{N}|}\sum_{x\in\Lambda_{N}}\exp\Big(-\tfrac{\varphi_{N}^{2}(x)}{\alpha_{\Lambda}}\log|\Lambda_{N}|\Big)\Big[1-\exp\Big(c_{d}^{\Lambda,\rho}(\log|\Lambda_{N}|)^{5-2d}\Big)\Big]\,.\end{split}

(5.19)

Using Lemma
4.1
and
d\geq 3
, this is bounded by
c_{2}|\Lambda_{N}|^{\frac{4d\rho}{d-2}-1}/\log^{2}|\Lambda_{N}|\leq c_{2}^{\prime}|\Lambda_{N}|^{-\rho}
provided
\rho>0
small enough.

Control for distant
x,y
:
we use again (
3.31
):

\begin{split}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x,y\in\Lambda_{N}\\
|x-y|>a_{N}\end{subarray}}\exp\Big(-\rho\tfrac{\varphi_{N}^{2}(x)+\varphi_{N}^{2}(y)}{\alpha_{\Lambda}}\log|\Lambda_{N}|\Big)&\mathrm{Cov}\big(\mathbbm{1}_{\left\{x\in\Lambda_{N}(\rho)\right\}},\mathbbm{1}_{\left\{y\in\Lambda_{N}(\rho)\right\}}\big)\\
&\leq c\bigg(\sum_{x\in\Lambda_{N}}\exp\Big(-\tfrac{\varphi_{N}^{2}(x)}{\alpha_{\Lambda}}\log|\Lambda_{N}|\Big)\bigg)^{2}\frac{c^{\prime}\log|\Lambda_{N}|}{(a_{N}^{\rho})^{d-2}}\,.\end{split}

(5.20)

Again, using Lemma
4.1
and plugging in
a_{N}^{\rho}=|\Lambda_{N}|^{\tfrac{4\rho}{d-2}}
, this sum is bounded from above by
c_{3}(\log|\Lambda_{N}|)^{3}|\Lambda_{N}|^{-4\rho}
for some positive
c_{3}
.

Combining all the above, we see that there exists a
c>0
such that
\mathrm{Var}\big[Z_{N,\Lambda}^{\rho}\big]
is at most of order
(\log|\Lambda_{N}|)^{5}|\Lambda_{N}|^{-c}\to 0
, hence completing the proof.
∎

5.2
Poisson limit

We now give a precise statement regarding the "late points" and their convergence towards a Poisson point process.

Fix some
z\in\mathbb{R}
and recall the notation
u_{N}^{\Lambda}(z)=\tfrac{g(0)}{\alpha_{\Lambda}}\big\{\log|\Lambda_{N}|+\log\log|\Lambda_{N}|+z\big\}
. We are interested in the punctual measure of the points of
\Lambda_{N}
that are not covered by
\mathscr{I}_{\Psi_{N}}(u_{N}^{\Lambda}(z))
, that is

\mathcal{N}_{N}^{\Lambda,z}\vcentcolon=\sum_{x\in\Lambda_{N}}\delta_{x/N}\mathbbm{1}_{\left\{x\not\in\mathscr{I}_{\psi_{N}}(u_{N}^{\Lambda}(z))\right\}}=\sum_{x\in\Lambda_{N}}\delta_{x/N}\mathbbm{1}_{\left\{U_{x}>u_{N}^{\Lambda}(z)\right\}}\,.

(5.21)

Lemma 5.3
.

For
B\subseteq\Lambda
, we write
B_{N}=(N\cdot B)\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}
with
N\geq 1
. Then,

\lim_{N\to+\infty}\mathbb{E}\big[|\mathcal{N}_{N}^{\Lambda,0}(B_{N})|\big]=\int_{B\cap(\partial\Lambda\cap\mathcal{L}_{\alpha_{\Lambda}})}\frac{\mathrm{d}x}{\big|\nabla\varphi^{2}(x)\big|}=\vcentcolon\mu_{\Lambda}(B\cap(\partial\Lambda\cap\mathcal{L}_{\alpha_{\Lambda}}))\,.

(5.22)

Note that this characterizes a finite measure on
\partial\Lambda\cap\mathcal{L}_{\alpha_{\Lambda}}
that we also denote by
\mu_{\Lambda}
.

Proof.

The proof follows from the arguments in Appendix
A.2
applied to
B\cap\Lambda
instead of
\Lambda
.
∎

We can now properly state the convergence of the “late points” towards a Poisson point process.

Theorem 5.4
.

Fix
z\in\mathbb{R}
. The punctual measures
\mathcal{N}_{N}^{\Lambda,z}
converge in distribution for the topology of weak convergence towards a Poisson point process
\mathcal{N}^{\Lambda,z}
with intensity measure
e^{-z}\mu_{\Lambda}
.

Proof.

The proof uses a theorem by Kallenberg (see
[
19
, Proposition 3.22]
) stating that one only needs to check that

\lim_{N\to+\infty}\mathbb{E}\big[|\mathcal{N}_{N}^{\Lambda,z}(B_{N})|\big]=e^{-z}\mu_{\Lambda}(B)\quad,\quad\lim_{N\to+\infty}\mathbb{P}\big(\mathcal{N}_{N}^{\Lambda,z}(B_{N})=0\big)=\exp\big(-\mu_{\Lambda}(B)e^{-z}\big)\,,

(5.23)

for any
B
a closed ball of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
that intersects with
\Lambda
.

The first equality is easily proved using the fact that

\mathbb{E}\big[|\mathcal{N}_{N}^{\Lambda,z}(B_{N})|\big]=\frac{\log|\Lambda_{N}|}{|\Lambda_{N}|e^{z}}\sum_{x\in\Lambda_{N}}\exp\Big(-\big(\frac{\varphi_{N}^{2}(x)}{\alpha_{\Lambda}}-1\big)\big[\log|\Lambda_{N}|-\log\log|\Lambda_{N}|+z\big]\Big)

(5.24)

and a use of Lemma
4.1
.
For the second equality, we observe that

\mathbb{P}\big(\mathcal{N}_{N}^{\Lambda,z}(B_{N})=0\big)=\mathbb{P}\big(B_{N}\subset\mathscr{I}_{\Psi_{N}}(u_{N}^{\Lambda}(z))\big)\,.

(5.25)

To get the limit as
N\to+\infty
, we will use the fact that the results of Section
4
still hold by replacing
\Lambda_{N}
with
B_{N}
but still keeping the same
u_{N}^{\Lambda}(\rho)
. For
\rho>0
, we define

B_{N}(\rho)\vcentcolon=\big\{x\in B_{N}\,:\,x\not\in\mathscr{I}_{\Psi_{N}}(u_{N}^{\Lambda,\rho})\big\}\,,

(5.26)

where we stress that we kept the same intermediary level
u_{N}^{\Lambda,\rho}=(1-\rho)g(0)\alpha_{\Lambda}^{-1}\log|\Lambda_{N}|
. Then, using the same proofs as in Section
4
– or observing that
u_{N}^{\Lambda,\rho}\geq u_{N}^{B,\rho}
implies
B_{N}(\rho)\subseteq\Lambda_{N}(\rho)
, with self-explanatory notation – we easily get that with high probability,

B_{N}(\rho)\lesssim\frac{|\Lambda_{N}|^{\rho}}{\log|\Lambda_{N}|}\quad,\quad\inf_{x,y\in B_{N}(\rho),x\neq y}|x-y|\geq|\Lambda_{N}|^{\frac{4\rho}{d-2}}\,.

(5.27)

Following the proof of Theorem
2.2
, the probability
\mathbb{P}\big(B_{N}\subset\mathscr{I}_{\Psi_{N}}(u_{N}^{\Lambda}(z))\,|\,B_{N}(\rho)\big)
is well approximated by

\prod_{x\in B_{N}(\rho)}\mathbb{P}\big(x\in\mathscr{I}_{\Psi_{N}}(u_{N}^{\Lambda}(z)-u_{N}^{\Lambda,\rho})\big)\sim\exp\Big(-\sum_{x\in B_{N}(\rho)}\exp\big(-\tfrac{\varphi_{N}^{2}(x)}{\alpha_{\Lambda}}[\rho\log|\Lambda_{N}|-\log\log|\Lambda_{N}|+z]\big)\Big)\,.

We then conclude by observing that the sum in the exponential just above is

e^{-z}\frac{\log|\Lambda_{N}|}{|\Lambda_{N}|^{\rho}}\sum_{x\in B_{N}(\rho)}\exp\Big(-\rho\big[\tfrac{\varphi_{N}^{2}(x)}{\alpha_{\Lambda}}-1\big]\log|\Lambda_{N}|(1+\bar{o}(1))\Big)

(5.28)

and doing the proof of Proposition
5.2
to see that this converges to
e^{-z}\mu_{\Lambda}(B)
.
∎

6
From tilted interlacements to the confined walk

We now explain how to transfer our result on the tilted interlacements to the confined walk. We first state the coupling theorem that we use.

Denote by
\mathcal{R}_{\phi_{N}}(t_{N})
the range up to time
t_{N}\geq 1
of the Markov chain with law
\mathbf{P}^{N}_{\phi_{N}^{2}}
(recall that
\mathbf{P}^{N}_{\phi_{N}^{2}}
is the tilted RW starting from its invariant measure
\mu=c_{N}\phi_{N}^{2}
).

Theorem 6.1

(Coupling theorem)
.

Let
\delta\in(0,1)
and consider a sequence
(t_{N})_{N\geq 1}
that satisfies
t_{N}/N^{2+\delta}\to+\infty
. We define

u_{N}\vcentcolon=\frac{t_{N}}{N^{d}}\qquad\text{as well as}\qquad\varepsilon_{N}\vcentcolon=N^{-\frac{\delta}{4}}\,.

Then, there are some
\eta>0
and some constants
c_{1},c_{2}>0
(that only depend on
\alpha,\delta,\varepsilon,D
and
d\geq 3
) and a coupling
\mathbb{Q}_{N}
of
\mathcal{R}_{\phi_{N}}(t_{N})
and
\mathscr{I}_{\Psi_{N}}((1\pm\varepsilon_{N})u_{N})
such that, for all
N
large enough,

\mathscr{I}_{\Psi_{N}}((1-\varepsilon_{N})u_{N})\cap B_{N}\,\subseteq\,\mathcal{R}_{\phi_{N}}(t_{N})\cap B_{N}\,\subseteq\,\mathscr{I}_{\Psi_{N}}((1+\varepsilon_{N})u_{N})\cap B_{N}\,,

(6.1)

with
\mathbb{Q}_{N}
-probability at least
1-c_{1}e^{-c_{2}N^{\eta}}
.

We can now finally prove our main theorems by using the fact that
\varepsilon_{N}
in Theorem
6.1
can be neglected in our calculations.

Proof of Theorems
1.1
&
1.2
.

Using Theorem
6.1
, we see that under the coupling, with probability at least
1-c_{1}e^{-c_{2}N^{\eta}}
we have

\mathcal{N}_{N}^{\Lambda,z_{N}^{+}}\subseteq\mathcal{N}_{N,\mathrm{RW}}^{\Lambda,z}\subseteq\mathcal{N}_{N}^{\Lambda,z_{N}^{-}}\quad,\quad\text{with}\quad z_{N}^{\pm}=z\pm\frac{g(0)}{\alpha_{\Lambda}}\varepsilon_{N}\big\{\log|\Lambda_{N}|-\log\log|\Lambda_{N}|\big\}=\vcentcolon z\pm\varepsilon_{N}^{\prime}\,.

(6.2)

In particular, since
\varepsilon_{N}^{\prime}
decays polynomially fast, we can apply the proof of Lemma
5.3
. Therefore, with the same notations, we get

\limsup_{N\to+\infty}\mathbf{E}_{\phi_{N}^{2}}\big[|\mathcal{N}_{N,\mathrm{RW}}^{\Lambda,z}(B_{N})|\big]\leq\limsup_{N\to+\infty}\mathbb{E}\big[|\mathcal{N}_{N}^{\Lambda,z-\varepsilon^{\prime}_{N}}(B_{N})|\big]=\mu_{\Lambda}(B)e^{-z}\,,

(6.3)

as well as

\liminf_{N\to+\infty}\mathbf{E}_{\phi_{N}^{2}}\big[|\mathcal{N}_{N,\mathrm{RW}}^{\Lambda,z}(B_{N})|\big]\geq\liminf_{N\to+\infty}\mathbb{E}\big[|\mathcal{N}_{N}^{\Lambda,z+\varepsilon^{\prime}_{N}}(B_{N})|\big]=\mu_{\Lambda}(B)e^{-z}\,.

(6.4)

Similarly, we get the convergence of
\mathbf{P}_{\phi_{N}^{2}}\big(|\mathcal{N}_{N,\mathrm{RW}}^{\Lambda,z\pm\varepsilon_{N}^{\prime}}(B_{N})|=0\big)
towards
\exp\big(-\mu_{\Lambda}(B)e^{-z}\big)
.
∎

7
Some specific cases

7.1
The case of a ball

In this section, we will focus on the case where
D
is the unit ball
B=B(0,1)
and
\Lambda
is the slighly smaller ball
B(0,1-\varepsilon)
for some
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
. This is a much simpler case to consider, since the function
\varphi
is radial: in particular
\mathcal{L}^{\Lambda}_{\alpha_{\Lambda}}
is exactly the boundary
\partial\Lambda
.

Let us first recall some facts about the eigenfunction
\varphi
on a ball in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Using the spherical coordinates
(r,\theta)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{*}\times\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
, the Dirichlet problem (
1.4
) becomes

0=\lambda u(x)+\Delta u(x)=\lambda u(r,\theta)+\Big[\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial r^{2}}+\frac{d-1}{r}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}+\frac{1}{r^{2}}\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\Big]u(r,\theta)\,,

(7.1)

where
\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}
is the spherical Laplacian on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Using the separation of variable, we write
u(r,\theta)=u_{1}(r)u_{2}(\theta)
and thus (
7.1
) can be rewritten as

0=r^{2}u_{1}^{\prime\prime}(r)+r(d-1)u_{1}^{\prime}(r)+\Big[\lambda r^{2}+\frac{\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}u_{2}(\theta)}{u_{2}(\theta)}\Big]u_{1}(r)\,.

(7.2)

In particular, there is a
n\in\mathbb{R}
such that

r^{2}u_{1}^{\prime\prime}(r)+r(d-1)u_{1}^{\prime}(r)+\big[\lambda r^{2}-n\big]u_{1}(r)=0\quad,\quad\frac{\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}u_{2}(\theta)}{u_{2}(\theta)}=-n

(7.3)

In the end, the first eigenfunction

on the unit ball can be expressed as

(7.4)

with

the first-kind Bessel function of index

.
Note that the properties of Bessel functions (see

\tfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}\big[x^{-\alpha}J_{\alpha}(x)\big]=-x^{-\alpha}J_{\alpha+1}(x)
. One can then deduce that the radial derivative of
\varphi^{2}
is given by

\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\varphi^{2}(r,\theta)=-2\sqrt{\lambda}r^{2-d}J_{1-\frac{d}{2}}(\sqrt{\lambda}r)J_{2-\frac{d}{2}}(\sqrt{\lambda}r)<0\,.

(7.5)

We deduce in particular that the radial part of
\varphi^{2}
, which we denote by
\varphi_{2,R}
is a smooth diffeomorphism, hence by change of variable we can compute the volume of the “level bands” of
\varphi^{2}
, and thus
\kappa_{\Lambda}
. Let us write
\psi_{2,R}\vcentcolon=(\varphi_{2,R})^{-1}
the inverse of
\varphi_{2,R}
.

Proposition 7.1
.

Consider
D=B(0,1)
and fix
r_{0}\in(0,1)
. Then, if
\Lambda=B(0,r_{0})
, we have

\kappa_{\Lambda}=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\tfrac{1}{\varepsilon}\big|\big\{x\in\Lambda\,:\,\varphi^{2}(x)\leq(1+\varepsilon)\alpha_{\Lambda}\big\}\big|=\frac{r_{0}^{d-1}}{\big|\varphi_{2,R}^{\prime}(r_{0})\big|}\mathrm{Vol}_{d}\big(\mathbb{B}^{d}\big)\,,

(7.6)

with
\mathrm{Vol}_{d}\big(\mathbb{B}^{d}\big)
the volume of the unit ball in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and where
\varphi_{2,R}
is the radial part of
\varphi^{2}
.

Note that this is consistent with the definition of
\kappa_{\Lambda}
in Theorem
1.2
.

Proof.

Since the level sets of
\varphi^{2}
are spheres of given radii, the set
\big|\big\{x\in\Lambda\,:\,\varphi^{2}(x)\leq(1+\varepsilon)\alpha_{\Lambda}\big\}
is simply the “annulus”

. In particular,

\big|\big\{x\in\Lambda\,:\,\varphi^{2}(x)\leq(1+\varepsilon)\alpha_{\Lambda}\big\}\big|=\big[(\psi_{2,R}(\alpha_{\Lambda}))^{d}-(\psi_{2,R}(\alpha_{\Lambda}(1+\varepsilon)))^{d}\big]\mathrm{Vol}_{d}\big(\mathbb{B}^{d}\big)\,,

(7.7)

with
\mathrm{Vol}_{d}\big(\mathbb{B}^{d}\big)
the volume of the unit ball in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Dividing by
\varepsilon>0
and having
\varepsilon\to 0
makes the derivative of
(\psi_{2,R})^{d}
at
\alpha_{\Lambda}
appear.

Another way to compute

is through the following change of variable:

\begin{split}\big|\big\{x\in\Lambda\,:\,\varphi^{2}(x)\leq(1+\varepsilon)\alpha_{\Lambda}\big\}\big|&=\mathrm{Vol}_{d-1}\big(\mathbb{S}^{d-1}\big)\int_{\Lambda}\mathbbm{1}_{\left\{\alpha_{\Lambda}\leq\varphi^{2}(r)\leq(1+\varepsilon)\alpha_{\Lambda}\right\}}\,r^{d-1}\mathrm{d}r\\
&=\mathrm{Vol}_{d-1}\big(\mathbb{S}^{d-1}\big)\int_{\alpha_{\Lambda}}^{(1+\varepsilon)\alpha_{\Lambda}}\big|J_{\psi_{2,R}}(y)\big|\,(\psi_{2,R}(y))^{d-1}\mathrm{d}y\,,\end{split}

(7.8)

with
J_{\Psi_{2,R}}=(\psi_{2,R})^{\prime}=[(\varphi_{2,R})^{\prime}\circ\psi_{2,R}]^{-1}
the Jacobian of
\psi_{2,R}
and
\mathrm{Vol}_{d-1}\big(\mathbb{S}^{d-1}\big)
the
(d-1)
-volume of the unit sphere in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Dividing by
\varepsilon>0
and having

, this converges to
\big|J_{\Psi_{2,R}}(\alpha_{\Lambda})\big|=[(\varphi_{2,R})^{\prime}\circ\psi_{2,R}(\varphi_{2,R}(r_{0}))]^{-1}
.
∎

7.2
Covering of a segment by the conditioned simple random walk

In this section, we will deviate from the confined walk to instead consider the conditioned walk, that is the simple random walk conditioned to stay in
D_{N}
up to some time
T_{N}
.
In the following, we consider the segment
I_{N}\vcentcolon=\llbracket-N,N\rrbracket\subset\mathbb{Z}
, where we write
\llbracket a,b\rrbracket\vcentcolon=[a,b]\cap\mathbb{Z}
. We also consider the time horizon
T_{N}
to be far greater than
N^{3}
.

Let
\mathfrak{C}_{N}
be the covering time of
I_{N}
by the simple random walk conditioned to stay in
I_{N}
, then we can conjecture the convergence in distribution

\frac{\mathfrak{C}_{N}}{N^{3}}\xrightarrow[N\to+\infty]{(d)}\mathfrak{C}\sim\sum_{i=1}^{G}\xi_{i}\quad,\quad\text{with $G\sim\mathcal{G}(\tfrac{1}{2})$ and $(\xi_{i})$ are i.i.d. variables}\,,

(7.9)

Let us give some heuristics for this convergence. We write
\tau^{0}_{0}=0
and define for
i\geq 1
:

\tau_{i}\vcentcolon=\inf\big\{t\geq\tau^{0}_{i-1}\,:\,|S_{t}|=N\big\}\quad,\quad\varepsilon_{i}=\mathrm{sign}(S_{\tau_{i}})\quad,\quad\tau^{0}_{i}=\inf\big\{t\geq\tau_{i}\,:\,S_{t}=0\big\}

(7.10)

Observe that
\mathfrak{C}_{N}=\tau_{\mathcal{I}}
, where
\mathcal{I}
is the first
i
such that
\varepsilon_{i}\neq\varepsilon_{1}
(that is the walk reached both of the extremities of the segment). With precise gambler’s ruin formulae, one can prove that
\tau_{i}-\tau^{0}_{i-1}\asymp N^{3}
with high probability (see below). On the other hand, the return times to
0
are asymptotically of order
N^{2}
, as the drift facilitates the return to zero, hence negligible. Therefore,
\mathfrak{C}_{N}\approx\sum_{i\leq\mathcal{I}}(\tau_{i}-\tau^{0}_{i-1})
with the
\tau_{i}-\tau^{0}_{i-1}
scaling to the first time a Brownian motion conditioned to stay in
[-1,1]
reaches the boundary.

The main point here is the fact that the order is understandable from the point of view of Theorem
1.1
. Indeed, it is known that the first eigenfunction of the Laplace operator on the segment is of order
1/N
at the boundary, hence the
\alpha_{\Lambda}
in this case would be
\asymp N^{-2}
and the asymptotics is
N^{d}/\alpha_{\Lambda}\asymp N^{3}
. The disappearance of the factor
\log N
is explained by the fact that reaching the boundary implies covering the full half-segment, thus at no time there exists a set of scattered points that the walk collects independently.

A_{1}^{u}
and
A_{2}^{u}
that only depend respectively on
\mathscr{I}_{\Psi_{N}}(u)\cap K_{1}
and
\mathscr{I}_{\Psi_{N}}(u)\cap K_{2}
. Then, there is a constant
C_{d}>0
that only depends on the dimension such that

\Big|\mathbb{P}(A_{1}^{u}\cap A_{2}^{u})-\mathbb{P}(A_{1}^{u})\mathbb{P}(A_{2}^{u})\Big|\leq C_{d}u\frac{\mathrm{cap}^{\Psi_{N}}(K_{1})\mathrm{cap}^{\Psi_{N}}(K_{2})}{d(K_{1},K_{2})^{d-2}}\,.

(A.24)

Proof of Proposition
A.2
.

Choose
x\in K
, we apply Lemma
A.3
to the sets
K_{1}=\left\{x\right\}
and
K_{2}=K\setminus\left\{x\right\}
, and the events
A_{1}=\left\{x\in\mathscr{I}_{\Psi_{N}}(u)\right\}
and
A_{2}=\left\{K\setminus\left\{x\right\}\subset\mathscr{I}_{\Psi_{N}}(u)\right\}
. This yields

\Big|\mathbb{P}(K\subset\mathscr{I}_{\Psi_{N}}(u))-\mathbb{P}(x\in\mathscr{I}_{\Psi_{N}}(u))\mathbb{P}(K\setminus\left\{x\right\}\subset\mathscr{I}_{\Psi_{N}}(u))\Big|\leq C_{d}u\frac{\mathrm{cap}^{\Psi_{N}}(\{x\})\mathrm{cap}^{\Psi_{N}}(K\setminus\left\{x\right\})}{d(x,K\setminus\left\{x\right\})^{d-2}}\,.

(A.25)

Note that since
x\in K\subseteq\Lambda_{N}
, by Proposition
2.3
and
\varphi_{N}\leq\kappa_{2}^{-1}
, we have
\mathrm{cap}^{\Psi_{N}}(\{x\})\leq\kappa_{2}^{-2}(1+N^{-c})/g(0)
as well as

\mathrm{cap}^{\Psi_{N}}(K\setminus\left\{x\right\})\leq\lambda_{N}\sum_{z\in K}\phi_{N}^{2}(z)\mathbf{P}^{\Psi_{N}}_{x}(\bar{H}_{K\setminus\left\{x\right\}}=+\infty)\leq\sum_{z\in K}\phi_{N}^{2}(z)\,.

(A.26)

Using (
3.4
) (
\phi_{N}
is bounded), we deduce that
\mathrm{cap}^{\Psi_{N}}(K\setminus\left\{x\right\})\leq(1+N^{-c})\kappa_{2}^{-2}|K|
and thus

\Big|\mathbb{P}(K\subset\mathscr{I}_{\Psi_{N}}(u))-\mathbb{P}(x\in\mathscr{I}_{\Psi_{N}}(u))\mathbb{P}(K\setminus\left\{x\right\}\subset\mathscr{I}_{\Psi_{N}}(u))\Big|\leq C_{d}u\frac{(1+N^{-c})^{2}}{g(0)\kappa_{2}^{4}}u\frac{|K|}{s(K)}\,.

(A.27)

Repeating the steps
|K|-1
times and using the triangular inequality concludes the proof of the proposition with
c=2C_{d}/g(0)\kappa_{2}^{4}
.

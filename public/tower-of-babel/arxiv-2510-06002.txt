Deterministic Legal Agents: A Canonical Primitive API for Auditable Reasoning over Temporal Knowledge Graphs
Hudson de Martim

Abstract
For autonomous legal agents to operate safely in high-stakes domains, they require a foundation of absolute determinism and auditability—guarantees that standard Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) frameworks cannot provide. When interacting with temporal knowledge graphs that model the complex evolution of legal norms, agents must navigate versioning, causality, and hierarchical structures with precision, a task for which black-box vector search is ill-suited. This paper introduces a new architectural pattern to solve this: a formal Primitive API designed as a secure execution layer for reasoning over such graphs. Instead of a monolithic query engine, our framework provides a library of canonical primitives—atomic, composable, and auditable primitives. This design empowers planner-guided agents to decompose complex legal questions into transparent execution plans, enabling critical tasks with full verifiability, including: (i) precise point-in-time version retrieval, (ii) robust causal lineage tracing, and (iii) context-aware hybrid search. Ultimately, this architecture transforms opaque retrieval into auditable reasoning, turning the agent’s internal process from a black box into a verifiable log of deterministic primitives and providing a blueprint for building the next generation of trustworthy legal AI.



Keywords:
Legal AI, Deterministic Legal Agents, Agentic Frameworks, Temporal Knowledge Graphs, SAT-Graph, Auditability, Explainable AI (XAI), Trustworthy AI, Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG).

The most important entities are not named persons or places, but the structural components of the law itself and the legislative events that alter them. While this provides a robust knowledge base, the critical question of how an agent can interact with this knowledge deterministically and auditably remains unaddressed. Without a formal protocol, the guarantees of the graph are easily bypassed, reintroducing the very unreliability we seek to eliminate.

2.4
Agentic Frameworks for Reasoning and Retrieval

Recent advances have seen LLMs employed not just as generators, but as reasoning agents capable of planning and tool use

A critical insight from recent research is the symbiotic relationship between architectural innovation and evaluation benchmarks. The legal domain has historically suffered from a lack of benchmarks that require deep, multi-hop reasoning over structured, temporal data. Specification of the Canonical Primitive API

The central thesis of our work is that for legal agents to be trustworthy, their interaction with a structured knowledge base must be governed by a formal, auditable protocol. We introduce this protocol as the SAT-Graph API, a library of
atomic and composable primitives
that serve as the agent’s verifiable toolset. This API serves as an abstraction layer, hiding the underlying graph database implementation (e.g., Cypher queries over a Neo4j database). Crucially, it provides a unified interface for both
probabilistic discovery primitives
(like resolving natural language references) and
fully deterministic retrieval primitives
that operate on formal identifiers. In this pattern, an autonomous agent is empowered to interact with the knowledge base exclusively through these primitives, ensuring that every step in its reasoning plan is explicit, verifiable, and its level of certainty is understood, as shown in Figure
2
.

3.1
Design Principles

The design of the API is guided by three core principles that, together, ensure the agent’s trustworthiness, even when its core logic is a probabilistic language model.

•

Maximal Determinism:
We acknowledge that any system interpreting natural language has inherently probabilistic components. Our design philosophy is to
isolate
this non-determinism at the API’s entry points. Primitives for discovery and search are probabilistic by nature. However, the core architectural contribution of this API is to enable an agent to orchestrate
fully deterministic reasoning plans
once a formal identifier is obtained. This capability for achieving determinism is what distinguishes our approach and justifies the "Deterministic Legal Agent" paradigm. Once a formal id (URI/URN) is acquired, all subsequent primitives that operate on it are guaranteed to be deterministic.

•

Composability:
Primitives are designed as atomic "building blocks." They can be chained together by a planning agent to construct a verifiable execution plan. The architectural philosophy is to provide the agent with powerful, deterministic
primitives
, while the
reasoning logic
that composes them (e.g., iterating through a list, inspecting text) remains the responsibility of the agent. This deliberate separation ensures that the agent’s entire reasoning process—not just the API calls—is explicit and auditable, preventing opaque server-side operations from obscuring the plan.

•

Verifiability through Auditability:
This principle is our safeguard against the initial non-determinism. Every primitive, including the probabilistic ones, must return not just a result but also a confidence score or justification. When an agent executes a plan, the sequence of primitive calls and their outputs forms a complete, human-readable audit trail. This log is the tangible artifact of the agent’s reasoning process, making it fully verifiable, even if the initial discovery steps are probabilistic. A user can inspect the log and see, "The system resolved ’Article 6’ to id ’…’ with 98% confidence," allowing them to trust or question the initial grounding step.

Architectural Note: Separation of Normative and Conceptual Entities

A foundational design principle of this framework is the strict separation of normative entities (
Item
) from conceptual, classificatory entities (
Theme
).
Item

Theme
s, in contrast, form an independent, atemporal ontology used to organize and discover knowledge. An
Item
’s structural relationships (‘parent‘, ‘children‘) define its fixed position within a document, forming a strict hierarchy. A
Theme
’s relationships define both its taxonomic position relative to other themes (‘parent‘, ‘children‘) and its classificatory relationship to the law (‘members‘). This clear separation is a critical design choice that empowers the agent. It allows the agent to disambiguate its intent, navigating the formal structure of a norm (its hierarchy) and the conceptual map of the legal domain (its themes) through distinct, unambiguous API primitives.

The ‘Version‘ (Item Temporal Version) and ‘Action‘ data models build upon this DAG foundation to create a fully versioned and causally-linked graph.

While the underlying ontology distinguishes between different semantic types of hierarchy, the API deliberately abstracts these into a conceptually unified pair of properties:
parent
and
children

We acknowledge a minor difference in the data types: an
Item
has a single
parent

Theme

Version

Item

Theme
can belong to multiple broader categories, and a
Version
can be reused under multiple parent versions as a performance optimization. Despite this type difference, the conceptual abstraction remains consistent:
parent
always represents a move towards a broader or containing entity, and
children
a move towards a more specific or contained one. This approach prioritizes a consistent agentic vocabulary without sacrificing the semantic richness of the underlying model.

3.3
Canonical Primitive API Specification

The SAT-Graph API provides an agent with a structured vocabulary of primitives, organized by intent. A core design guarantee governs this vocabulary: any primitive that accepts a formal ID as its primary input is fully deterministic. This clear distinction is critical: it allows the agent to consciously manage uncertainty during its discovery phase, before transitioning to a fully deterministic and auditable execution path. This section details the signature and purpose of each primitive, which serve as the building blocks for the agent’s reasoning plans.

Bridging Baseline RAG with a Temporal Knowledge Graph

A key design challenge is to provide a search interface that is both simple enough to replicate the behavior of a standard, "structurally and temporally-naïve" RAG system, while also exposing the full power of the underlying temporal graph. Traditional RAG pipelines typically index a single, current version of a text. A query like “Which social rights are guaranteed?” executes a semantic search over these current texts.

The SAT-Graph, however, contains
all historical versions
. A simple semantic search would return a mix of current and outdated, yet relevant, texts. To manage this complexity, the API’s primary search primitive,
searchTextUnits
, is explicitly designed to give the agent precise control over temporal scope through a simple
timestamp
parameter:

•

Default (Current State Query):
When the
timestamp
is omitted, the search is automatically constrained to only the currently valid texts, thus replicating the expected behavior of a baseline RAG.

•

Temporal Query:
When a
timestamp
is provided, the search is constrained to the historical snapshot of texts that were valid on that specific date.

This design provides a single, powerful primitive that empowers an agent to seamlessly switch between simple, RAG-like queries and complex historical analysis, depending on its strategic goal.

3.3.6
Architectural Rationale and Modeling Principles

Discussion: Modeling of State Transitions

Our framework is designed to handle various types of legislative state transitions with precision. This is fully represented in the
Action
data model, where the optionality of its properties allows an agent to unambiguously distinguish between different event types:

•

A
Creation
event only has a
produces_version_id
.

•

A pure
Repeal
or
Revocation
event might only have a
terminates_version_id
.

•

A standard
Amendment
has both.

This removes any guesswork from the agent’s causal analysis. A recommended modeling practice, for instance, is to represent a revocation not as a pure repeal, but as an amendment that creates a new version with the text "(Revoked)", thus maintaining a continuous version timeline. The API’s flexibility supports both patterns.

Discussion: Atomic Primitives and Emergent Event Cohesion

A core design principle of the SAT-Graph is the atomicity of causal events. Our model defines an
Action
as the most granular unit of change possible to ensure maximum auditability. A key consequence is that a single, complex legislative event—such as the enactment of "Constitutional Amendment No. 99"—is not represented by a monolithic node. Instead, its total effect is captured by an emergent collection of many individual
Action
nodes, whose cohesion is established by their respective
source_version
properties all tracing back to the same source
Work
.

This design provides the best of both worlds for an intelligent agent. It can use a single, high-level API primitive (
getActionsBySource
) to efficiently grasp the full scope of a legislative event:

•

Strategy: Aggregate Actions by Source Work.
The agent first identifies the amending law as a
Work
(e.g., via
resolveItemReference
). It then makes a single call to
getActionsBySource( source_work_id=...)
to gather all the individual
Action
nodes authorized by that amendment.

This powerful aggregation empowers the agent to perform high-level impact analysis, while the atomic nature of each returned
Action
preserves the granular verifiability needed for a trustworthy audit trail. The agent can see both the forest and the trees, deterministically.

Discussion: Multi-Aspect Retrieval via TextUnit

The
TextUnit
is the foundational object for the multi-aspect retrieval capability of the SAT-Graph. By decoupling textual content from the core graph entities, we can associate multiple, distinct textual representations with any node. This is achieved via the
source_node_type
and
source_node_id
properties on the
TextUnit
, allowing it to point to any other entity in the graph. For example, an entire
Work
can have a
TextUnit
with an aspect of "textual_metadata", a
Theme
can have one with an aspect of "description", and a
Version
can have both a "canonical" text and a "summary".

This flexibility is directly exposed through the API, particularly via the
aspects
parameter in functions like
searchTextUnits
. All
TextUnit
s, regardless of their source entity or aspect, are candidates for embedding and semantic retrieval. This design creates a rich, multi-faceted search space. It empowers an agent with holistic perception, allowing it to discover relevant information based not just on the law’s formal content, but also on its contextual metadata and conceptual definitions in a single, unified query. This prevents the agent from developing ’tunnel vision’, ensuring that its initial, probabilistic discovery phase is as comprehensive as possible before it commits to a deterministic plan.

3.4
Practical Implementation Considerations

While the atomic primitives specified above provide the logical foundation for auditability, a production-grade API must also enable agents to execute complex reasoning plans efficiently. This section details practical considerations, most notably batch operations, which are essential for making sophisticated, multi-step agentic workflows viable in a real-world setting.

3.5
The API as a Grammar for Agentic Reasoning

The specified primitives are not merely a list of endpoints; they are a formal grammar for agentic reasoning. Collectively, they form a comprehensive yet controlled interface to the temporal knowledge graph. By design, they are low-level primitives. An agent is not expected to answer a user’s query with a single primitive. Instead, its planning module must act as a translator, decomposing a complex question into a Directed Acyclic Graph (DAG) of these primitives. The resulting DAG is the agent’s auditable reasoning plan. The power of this architectural pattern is demonstrated in the following section through a series of practical use cases.

Challenge

The system lacks the temporal awareness to understand what "on May 20th, 2001" means, and cannot guarantee that the version it retrieves is the one that was legally in force on that specific date. Use Case 3: Thematic and Hierarchical Impact Analysis

Challenge

This broad, analytical query is completely intractable for systems that are not simultaneously aware of hierarchy, themes, and time. A standard RAG system would likely perform a simple keyword search for "Digital Security," retrieving a mix of relevant and irrelevant provisions while completely missing the implicit scope of a thematic category. It cannot understand that if a high-level provisions is tagged with a theme, all its sub-components are also implicitly part of that scope. The system lacks the multi-faceted awareness to even begin to scope the query correctly, let alone analyze its evolution over time.

5
Conclusion

In this paper, we addressed a critical challenge preventing the safe deployment of autonomous AI in the legal domain: the lack of a trustworthy and auditable method for interacting with complex, versioned knowledge. While knowledge graphs like our SAT-Graph can represent the structural and temporal reality of legal norms, the question of how an agent can query this representation without sacrificing its guarantees remained open. We have demonstrated that the solution is not a more complex, monolithic query engine, but rather a more disciplined and transparent interaction layer: a formal Canonical Primitive API.

Our primary contribution is a new architectural pattern for building deterministic legal agents, specified through this API. The core of the framework is a library of canonical primitives—low-level, composable primitives that enforce auditability by design. This approach creates a powerful synergy: advanced agentic frameworks provide the flexibility to interpret natural language, while our API provides the unyielding determinism required to execute the resulting reasoning plans safely. The agent’s plan itself becomes a complete, human-readable audit trail, transforming its internal reasoning process from an opaque black box into a series of explicit, verifiable steps.

We have shown that this shift—from passive, probabilistic retrieval to active, agent-driven inquiry built on deterministic primitives—is a direct architectural response to the challenge of Explainable AI (XAI). It provides a concrete blueprint for building the next generation of trustworthy AI systems, not just for law, but for any regulated domain where precision, auditability, and historical correctness are paramount.

5.1
Implications

The implications of enforcing a formal Primitive API between an agent and a temporal knowledge graph are significant, marking a paradigm shift away from end-to-end, black-box systems and toward a more modular and trustworthy approach.

Decoupling Knowledge from Reasoning

The most essential implication is the clean decoupling of the knowledge representation (the graph) from the agentic reasoning layer. The SAT-Graph serves as the stable, verifiable "source of truth," which can be curated and maintained independently. The reasoning layer—the agentic frameworks that plan and execute queries—can evolve at a rapid pace, incorporating newer and more powerful LLMs without compromising the foundational integrity of the knowledge base.

Generalizability Beyond the Legal Domain

This Primitive API architecture is not a solution only for law; it is a generalizable pattern for building trustworthy agents in any regulated, structured domain. Our deliberate use of a generic
Item
data model means that this pattern is directly applicable to other fields that rely on versioned and evolving documents, such as technical standards (ISO, IETF), regulatory compliance (FDA, financial regulations), and complex engineering manuals. In essence, any domain where
what was
is as important as
what is
can benefit from this approach to enabling auditable agentic systems.

Architectural Boundaries

Finally, it is critical to distinguish between the responsibilities of the Primitive API and those of the higher-level agentic framework that consumes it. Our API is intentionally designed as a set of verifiable primitives for retrieving structural, temporal, and causal facts. It answers "what is?" and "what was?". Complex reasoning tasks, such as conflict detection or transitive dependency analysis, remain the responsibility of the reasoning agent. This deliberate separation of concerns is a core strength of our architecture: the API guarantees the integrity of the evidence, while the agent is responsible for constructing the argument.

5.2
Limitations

While this work proposes a complete architectural pattern and formal API specification, several limitations and areas for future work should be acknowledged:

Implementation Complexity

Building a production-grade implementation of the server-side logic is a significant engineering effort. The performance of the API, particularly for temporal and causal queries over a large graph, depends on sophisticated indexing strategies that are non-trivial to implement and optimize.

Knowledge Base Dependency

The determinism of the agent’s reasoning is fundamentally bound to the quality and completeness of the underlying knowledge graph. The principle of "garbage in, garbage out" applies: incomplete or incorrectly modeled legislative events in the SAT-Graph will lead to factually correct but legally incomplete answers from the agent. The API provides transactional integrity but cannot create knowledge that is not there.

Agent Planner Sophistication

Our architecture fundamentally shifts the primary challenge from retrieval to agentic planning. The effectiveness of the entire system now relies on the agent’s ability to translate a user’s natural language query into an optimal and correct sequence of primitives. While the capabilities of LLMs as planners are advancing rapidly, generating complex, multi-step, and efficient DAGs of primitives remains a frontier research problem.

Empirical Evaluation Gap

This paper focuses on the formal specification and conceptual power of the architecture. A comprehensive empirical evaluation against emerging legal AI benchmarks that test temporal and causal reasoning is a critical next step. Such an evaluation would be necessary to quantify the performance and accuracy gains of this agentic, primitive-based approach over traditional RAG models.

5.3
Future Directions

By establishing a formal, primitive-based interface for agentic reasoning, this work lays a foundational layer for numerous avenues of future research.

First, we plan to expand the API’s "grammar" to support deeper legal reasoning. The current specification focuses on the verifiable graph of structural, temporal, and causal links. A critical next step is the formalization of a parallel "semantic overlay" graph. This involves introducing a first-class
Relation
entity to model a rich vocabulary of typed links between
Item
s (e.g., ‘cites‘, ‘succeeds‘, ‘applies_to‘). A new set of ‘Graph Traversal Primitives‘ would then empower an agent to trace transitive impacts and other complex relational queries, moving from "what happened?" to "how are these concepts legally related?". The design for these extensions is being actively developed in our open-source project.

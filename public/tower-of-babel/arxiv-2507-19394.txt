A general fusion procedure for open 𝔤 ​ 𝔩 ​ ( N ) \mathfrak{gl}(N) spin chains: Application to the ABJM spin chain
Nan Bai

Abstract
We formulate a general fusion procedure for open 𝔤 ​ 𝔩 ​ ( N ) \mathfrak{gl}(N) spin chains. We construct the fused boundary reflection matrices and the corresponding fused reflection equations. By using the intertwining relation between the fused reflection matrices and the fusion operator, we identify the invariant subspace of the fused reflection matrices carrying the irreducible representations of 𝔤 ​ 𝔩 ​ ( N ) \mathfrak{gl}(N) . We also construct the fused transfer matrix and evaluate it explicitly in the total tensor product space and the invariant subspaces. Finally, we demonstrate that the ABJM spin chain model originates from such fusion procedure and derive three classes of boundary reflection matrices solutions on the anti-fundamental representation space of 𝔰 ​ 𝔲 ​ ( 4 ) \mathfrak{su}(4) .

Introduction

In many-body physics and quantum field theory, fusion refers to the formation of bound states from constituent particles. In Yang’s pioneering work of quantum integrability
[
1

, the S-matrix for bound states has been constructed in terms of a series of ordered 2-2 scatterings and proven to be both symmetric and unitary. Later, it was realized that factorizability is an intrinsic property in the (1+1)-dimensional integrable field theory
[

3

R

R
-matrices can be systematically constructed by the fusion procedure, which was first introduced in
[

,
5

R

\ell

\mathfrak{su}(2)
was obtained as the symmetrized tensor product of

representations. Roughly speaking, the fusion procedure relies on the fact that, at some special values of the spectral parameter, the fundamental
R
-matrix reduces to the projection operator. When evaluated at these points, the fundamental YBE turns out to be an intertwining relation between the projector and the product of the remaining two
R
-matrices, and from this relation we obtain the fused
R
-matrix restricted to the projected subspace. Higher fused
R
-matrices can be constructed similarly from the fused YBE and the fusion operator. The fusion procedure has been extensively studied in two-dimensional solvable vertex and face models, including, for example: foundational work on solid-on-solid (SOS) model
[
6
,
7
]
; a unified study for rational, trigonometric, and elliptic solutions in interaction-round-a-face (IRF) models
[
8
]
; fusion hierarchies of
A–D–E

[
9

. For integrable models with boundaries, in addition to the fused
R

[
10
,
11

12

13

for fused

[
14

for fused
K

Beyond constructing new solutions, fusion techniques also provide essential functional relations for the exact solution of certain systems. Notably, they play a crucial role in the off-diagonal Bethe ansatz (ODBA) method developed in recent years
[
15

2.1.1
Some properties of the fused
R
-matrices

We present some properties of the fused
R
-matrix, which will be frequently used in subsequent derivations.

(1)

P-symmetry: First note that for the fundamental
R
-matrix
R^{c_{i}c_{j}}_{ij}(u)
, we have:

R_{ij}^{c_{i}c_{j}}(u)=\mathbb{I}-\frac{\mathbb{P}_{ij}}{u+c_{i}-c_{j}}=\mathbb{I}-\frac{\mathbb{P}_{ji}}{u+(-c_{j})-(-c_{i})}=R_{ji}^{-c_{j},-c_{i}}(u),

(8)

which gives

\begin{split}R_{i,(\underline{1}\cdots\underline{n}^{\prime})}^{c_{i},\mathbf{c}_{(\underline{1}\cdots\underline{n}^{\prime})}}(u)=R_{\underline{1}i}^{-c_{\underline{1}},-c_{i}}(u)\cdots R_{\underline{n}^{\prime},i}^{-c_{\underline{n}^{\prime}},-c_{i}}(u)=R_{(\underline{n}^{\prime}\cdots\underline{1}),i}^{-\mathbf{c}_{(\underline{n}^{\prime}\cdots\underline{1})},-c_{i}}(u).\end{split}

(9)

Thus,

\begin{split}R_{(1\cdots n),(\underline{1}\cdots\underline{n}^{\prime})}^{\mathbf{c}_{(1\cdots n)},\mathbf{c}_{(\underline{1}\cdots\underline{n}^{\prime})}}(u)=&R_{n,(\underline{1}\cdots\underline{n}^{\prime})}^{c_{n},\mathbf{c}_{\underline{1}\cdots\underline{n}^{\prime}}}(u)\cdots R_{1,(\underline{1}\cdots\underline{n}^{\prime})}^{c_{1},\mathbf{c}_{\underline{1}\cdots\underline{n}^{\prime}}}(u)\\
=&R_{(\underline{n}^{\prime}\cdots\underline{1}),n}^{-\mathbf{c}_{(\underline{n}^{\prime}\cdots\underline{1})},-c_{n}}(u)\cdots R_{(\underline{n}^{\prime}\cdots\underline{1}),1}^{-\mathbf{c}_{(\underline{n}^{\prime}\cdots\underline{1})},-c_{1}}(u)=R_{(\underline{n}^{\prime}\cdots\underline{1}),(n\cdots 1)}^{-\mathbf{c}_{(\underline{n}^{\prime}\cdots\underline{1})},-\mathbf{c}_{(n\cdots 1)}}(u),\end{split}

(10)

which shows the P-symmetry possessed by the fused
R
-matrix.

(2)

T-symmetry: We will use
t_{i}
to denote the transpose in the space
V_{i}
and
t_{(1\cdots n)}=t_{1}t_{2}\cdots t_{n}
the transpose in the tensor product space:
V_{(1\cdots n)}:=V_{1}\otimes V_{2}\otimes\cdots\otimes V_{n}
. Since
R_{ij}^{c_{i}c_{j}}(u)^{t_{i}}=R_{ij}^{c_{i}c_{j}}(u)^{t_{j}}
, we have:

\begin{split}&R_{(1\cdots n),\underline{i}}^{\mathbf{c}_{(1\cdots n)},c_{\underline{i}}}(u)^{t_{(1\cdots n)}}=\left[R_{n\underline{i}}^{c_{n}c_{\underline{i}}}(u)\cdots R_{1\underline{i}}^{c_{1}c_{\underline{i}}}(u)\right]^{t_{(1\cdots n)}}=\left[R_{n\underline{i}}^{c_{n}c_{\underline{i}}}(u)^{t_{n}}\cdots R_{1\underline{i}}^{c_{1}c_{\underline{i}}}(u)^{t_{1}}\right]\\
=&\left[R_{n\underline{i}}^{c_{n}c_{\underline{i}}}(u)^{t_{\underline{i}}}\cdots R_{1\underline{i}}^{c_{1}c_{\underline{i}}}(u)^{t_{\underline{i}}}\right]=\left[R_{1\underline{i}}^{c_{1}c_{\underline{i}}}(u)\cdots R_{n\underline{i}}^{c_{n}c_{\underline{i}}}(u)\right]^{t_{\underline{i}}}=R_{(n\cdots 1),\underline{i}}^{\mathbf{c}_{(n\cdots 1)},c_{\underline{i}}}(u)^{t_{\underline{i}}},\end{split}

(11)

where we have used the fact:
(A\otimes B)^{t_{A}t_{B}}=A^{t_{A}}\otimes B^{t_{B}}
. Then we have:

\begin{split}R_{(1\cdots n),(\underline{1}\cdots\underline{n}^{\prime})}^{\mathbf{c}_{(1\cdots n)},\mathbf{c}_{(\underline{1}\cdots\underline{n}^{\prime})}}(u)^{t_{(1\cdots n)}}=&\left[R_{(1\cdots n),\underline{1}}^{\mathbf{c}_{(1\cdots n)},c_{\underline{1}}}(u)\cdots R_{(1\cdots n),\underline{n}^{\prime}}^{\mathbf{c}_{(1\cdots n)},c_{\underline{n}^{\prime}}}(u)\right]^{t_{(1\cdots n)}}\\
=&R_{(1\cdots n),\underline{n}^{\prime}}^{\mathbf{c}_{(1\cdots n)},c_{\underline{n}^{\prime}}}(u)^{t_{(1\cdots n)}}\cdots R_{(1\cdots n),\underline{1}}^{\mathbf{c}_{(1\cdots n)},c_{\underline{1}}}(u)^{t_{(1\cdots n)}}\\
=&R_{(n\cdots 1),\underline{n}^{\prime}}^{\mathbf{c}_{(n\cdots 1)},c_{\underline{i}}}(u)^{t_{\underline{n}^{\prime}}}\cdots R_{(n\cdots 1),\underline{1}}^{\mathbf{c}_{(n\cdots 1)},c_{\underline{1}}}(u)^{t_{\underline{1}}}=R_{(n\cdots 1),(\underline{n}^{\prime}\cdots\underline{1})}^{\mathbf{c}_{(n\cdots 1)},\mathbf{c}_{(\underline{n}^{\prime}\cdots\underline{1})}}(u)^{t_{(\underline{n}^{\prime}\cdots\underline{1})}},\end{split}

(12)

which is the T-symmetry satisfied by the fused
R
-matrix. This relation can also be written as:

R_{(1\cdots n),(\underline{1}\cdots\underline{n}^{\prime})}^{\mathbf{c}_{(1\cdots n)},\mathbf{c}_{(\underline{1}\cdots\underline{n}^{\prime})}}(u)^{t_{(1\cdots n)},t_{(\underline{1}\cdots\underline{n}^{\prime})}}=R_{(n\cdots 1),(\underline{n}^{\prime}\cdots\underline{1})}^{\mathbf{c}_{(n\cdots 1)},\mathbf{c}_{(\underline{n}^{\prime}\cdots\underline{1})}}(u).

(13)

(3)

Unitary condition: The fundamental
R
-matrix satisfies the following unitary condition:

R_{ij}^{c_{i}c_{j}}(u)R_{ji}^{c_{j}c_{i}}(-u)=\left(\mathbb{I}-\frac{\mathbb{P}_{ij}}{u+c_{i}-c_{j}}\right)\left(\mathbb{I}-\frac{\mathbb{P}_{ji}}{-u+c_{j}-c_{i}}\right)=\left[1-\frac{1}{(u+c_{i}-c_{j})^{2}}\right]\mathbb{I},

(14)

which implies

\begin{split}R_{i,(\underline{1}\cdots\underline{n}^{\prime})}^{c_{i},\mathbf{c}_{(\underline{1}\cdots\underline{n}^{\prime})}}(u)R_{(\underline{1}\cdots\underline{n}^{\prime}),i}^{\mathbf{c}_{(\underline{1}\cdots\underline{n}^{\prime})},c_{i}}(-u)=&\left[R_{i\underline{1}}^{c_{i}c_{\underline{1}}}(u)\cdots R_{i\underline{n}^{\prime}}^{c_{i}c_{\underline{n}^{\prime}}}(u)\right]\left[R_{\underline{n}^{\prime}i}^{c_{\underline{n}^{\prime}}c_{i}}(-u)\cdots R_{\underline{1}i}^{c_{\underline{1}}c_{i}}(-u)\right]\\
=&\prod_{j=1}^{n^{\prime}}\left[1-\frac{1}{(u+c_{i}-c_{\underline{j}})^{2}}\right]\mathbb{I}.\end{split}

(15)

Consequently, we derive the unitary condition for the fused
R
-matrix:

\begin{split}&R_{(1\cdots n),(\underline{1}\cdots\underline{n}^{\prime})}^{\mathbf{c}_{(1\cdots n)},\mathbf{c}_{(\underline{1}\cdots\underline{n}^{\prime})}}(u)R_{(\underline{1}\cdots\underline{n}^{\prime}),(1\cdots n)}^{\mathbf{c}_{(\underline{1}\cdots\underline{n}^{\prime})},\mathbf{c}_{(1\cdots n)}}(-u)\\
=&\left[R_{n,(\underline{1}\cdots\underline{n}^{\prime})}^{c_{n},\mathbf{c}_{(\underline{1}\cdots\underline{n}^{\prime})}}(u)\cdots R_{1,(\underline{1}\cdots\underline{n}^{\prime})}^{c_{1},\mathbf{c}_{(\underline{1}\cdots\underline{n}^{\prime})}}(u)\right]\left[R_{(\underline{1}\cdots\underline{n}^{\prime}),1}^{\mathbf{c}_{(\underline{1}\cdots\underline{n}^{\prime})},c_{1}}(-u)\cdots R_{(\underline{1}\cdots\underline{n}^{\prime}),n}^{\mathbf{c}_{(\underline{1}\cdots\underline{n}^{\prime})},c_{n}}(-u)\right]\\
=&\prod_{i=1}^{n}\prod_{j=1}^{n^{\prime}}\left[1-\frac{1}{(u+c_{i}-c_{\underline{j}})^{2}}\right]\mathbb{I}.\end{split}

(16)

For later convenience, we introduce the symbol
\rho_{(1\cdots n),(\underline{1}\cdots\underline{n}^{\prime})}^{\mathbf{c}_{(1\cdots n)},\mathbf{c}_{(\underline{1}\cdots\underline{n}^{\prime})}}(u)
to denote the scalar function in the above result (
16

\rho_{(1\cdots n),(\underline{1}\cdots\underline{n}^{\prime})}^{\mathbf{c}_{(1\cdots n)},\mathbf{c}_{(\underline{1}\cdots\underline{n}^{\prime})}}(u)=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\prod_{j=1}^{n^{\prime}}\left[1-\frac{1}{(u+c_{i}-c_{\underline{j}})^{2}}\right]
.

(4)

Crossing unitary condition: For the fundamental
R
-matrix acting on
V\otimes V
with
N={\rm{dim}}V

R_{ij}^{c_{i}c_{j}}(u)^{t_{i}}R_{ji}^{c_{j}c_{i}}(-u+N)^{t_{i}}=\mathbb{I}.

(17)

Utilizing relations (
11
) and (

-matrix:

\begin{split}&R_{(1\cdots n),(\underline{1}\cdots\underline{n}^{\prime})}^{\mathbf{c}_{(1\cdots n)},\mathbf{c}_{(\underline{1}\cdots\underline{n}^{\prime})}}(u)^{t_{(1\cdots n)}}=\left[R_{n\underline{n}^{\prime}}^{c_{n}c_{\underline{n}^{\prime}}}(u)^{t_{n}}\cdots R_{1\underline{n}^{\prime}}^{c_{1}c_{\underline{n}^{\prime}}}(u)^{t_{1}}\right]\cdots\left[R_{n\underline{1}}^{c_{n}c_{\underline{1}}}(u)^{t_{n}}\cdots R_{1\underline{1}}^{c_{1}c_{\underline{1}}}(u)^{t_{1}}\right],\\
&R_{(\underline{1}\cdots\underline{n}^{\prime}),(1\cdots n)}^{\mathbf{c}_{(\underline{1}\cdots\underline{n}^{\prime})},\mathbf{c}_{(1\cdots n)}}(u)^{t_{(1\cdots n)}}=\left[R_{\underline{1}1}^{c_{\underline{1}}c_{1}}(u)^{t_{1}}\cdots R_{\underline{1}n}^{c_{\underline{1}}c_{n}}(u)^{t_{n}}\right]\cdots\left[R_{\underline{n}^{\prime}1}^{c_{\underline{n}^{\prime}}c_{1}}(u)^{t_{1}}\cdots R_{\underline{n}^{\prime}n}^{c_{\underline{n}^{\prime}}c_{n}}(u)^{t_{n}}\right].\end{split}

(18)

Consequently, the crossing unitary condition for the fused

-matrix takes the form:

R_{(1\cdots n),(\underline{1}\cdots\underline{n}^{\prime})}^{\mathbf{c}_{(1\cdots n)},\mathbf{c}_{(\underline{1}\cdots\underline{n}^{\prime})}}(u)^{t_{(1\cdots n)}}R_{(\underline{1}\cdots\underline{n}^{\prime}),(1\cdots n)}^{\mathbf{c}_{(\underline{1}\cdots\underline{n}^{\prime})},\mathbf{c}_{(1\cdots n)}}(-u+N)^{t_{(1\cdots n)}}=\mathbb{I}.

(19)

Invariant subspaces of the fused
R
-matrices

The tensor product space

for the fused
R
-matrix

2.2.3
Invariant subspaces for irreducible representations of
\mathfrak{gl}(N)

We now investigate the invariant subspace of the fused
R
-matrix
R_{(1\cdots n),(\underline{1}\cdots\underline{n}^{\prime})}^{\mathbf{c}_{(1\cdots n)},\mathbf{c}_{(\underline{1}\cdots\underline{n}^{\prime})}}(u)
. Define the subspaces:

W=B_{(1\cdots n)}^{\mathbf{c}_{(1\cdots n)}}V^{\otimes n},\quad\quad\overline{W}=B_{(\underline{1}\cdots\underline{n}^{\prime})}^{\mathbf{c}_{(\underline{1}\cdots\underline{n}^{\prime})}}V^{\otimes n^{\prime}}.

(29)

From the
RBB
relation (
22
), in which the general braiding operator is chosen to be the fusion operator, it follows that
W\otimes\overline{W}
is invariant under the fused
R
-matrix:

R_{(1\cdots n),(\underline{1}\cdots\underline{n}^{\prime})}^{\mathbf{c}_{(1\cdots n)},\mathbf{c}_{(\underline{1}\cdots\underline{n}^{\prime})}}(u)\in{\rm{End}}(W\otimes\overline{W}).

(30)

More importantly, as will be detailed below, for specific chosen inhomogeneity parameters
\{c_{i}\}
, the fusion operator
B_{(1\cdots n)}^{\mathbf{c}_{(1\cdots n)}}
coincides with the primitive idempotent of the symmetric group
S_{n}
. Hence, according to the Schur-Weyl duality
[
32
]
,
W
becomes the irreducible representation space of
\mathfrak{gl}(N)
and thus we obtain the reduced form of the fused
R
-matrix
R_{W\overline{W}}(u)
by the restriction:

R_{W\overline{W}}(u):=R_{(1\cdots n),(\underline{1}\cdots\underline{n}^{\prime})}^{\mathbf{c}_{(1\cdots n)},\mathbf{c}_{(\underline{1}\cdots\underline{n}^{\prime})}}(u)\bigg|_{W\otimes\overline{W}}.

(31)

The procedure described above is commonly referred to as the fusion procedure. A key step involves establishing a connection between the irreducible basis of the
\mathbb{C}[S_{n}]
-module (diagonal matrix unit) and the products of Yang
R
-matrices with specific parameters (the fusion operator), which can be traced back to the pioneering work of Jucys
[
33
]
. The application of the fusion procedure in integrable systems originates from Cherendnik’s work on constructing special bases for irreducible representations of Hecke algebras
[
34
]
. A rigorous proof was first given by Nazarov
[
36
]
and a slightly different version was proposed by Molev
[
37
]
.

Now we give the details of the fusion theorem (we use a formulation as in
[
44
]
): Let
\lambda
be a partition of
n
and let
\mathcal{T}
be a standard Young tableau of shape
\lambda
. For a cell occupied by the number
k
in the
i
-th row and the
j
-th column of
\mathcal{T}
, we define the content of this cell as
c(\mathcal{T}|k):=j-i
. We denote by
h(\lambda)
the product of the hook lengths of the Young diagram
\lambda
. Then, the following evaluation of the fusion operator

E_{\mathcal{T}}:=\frac{1}{h(\lambda)}B_{(1\cdots n)}^{\mathbf{c}_{(1\cdots n)}}\bigg|_{c_{i}=c(\mathcal{T}|i),\,i=1,2,\cdots,n}

(32)

gives:

•

E_{\mathcal{T}}
is a primitive idempotent of
\mathbb{C}[S_{n}]
-module that generates a minimal left ideal
\mathbb{C}[S_{n}]E_{\mathcal{T}}
.

•

The subspace
W=E_{\mathcal{T}}V^{\otimes n}
is an irreducible
\mathfrak{gl}(N)
-module.

When the fused
R
-matrices are expressed in the irreducible representation subspaces of
\mathfrak{gl}(N)
as given in (
31
), the fused YBE (
7
), which is now seen as an equation on the invariant tensor space
W\otimes\overline{W}\otimes\overline{\overline{W}}
, reduces to:

R_{W\overline{W}}(u-v)R_{W\overline{\overline{W}}}(u)R_{\overline{W}\,\overline{\overline{W}}}(v)=R_{\overline{W}\,\overline{\overline{W}}}(v)R_{W\overline{\overline{W}}}(u)R_{W\overline{W}}(u-v),

(33)

where besides the subspaces
W
and
\overline{W}
defined in (
29
), we also define:
\overline{\overline{W}}=B_{(\underline{\underline{1}}\cdots\underline{\underline{n}}^{\prime\prime})}^{\mathbf{c}_{(\underline{\underline{1}}\cdots\underline{\underline{n}}^{\prime\prime})}}V^{\otimes n^{\prime\prime}}
.

Remark: In the literature, it is also conventional to express the reduced fused
R
-matrix in the original tensor product space by the projection operators (given by the primitive idempotent here). In this form, the reduced fused
R
-matrix is formulated as:

E_{\mathcal{T}}E_{\mathcal{T}^{\prime}}R_{(1\cdots n),(\underline{1}\cdots\underline{n}^{\prime})}^{\mathbf{c}_{(1\cdots n)},\mathbf{c}_{(\underline{1}\cdots\underline{n}^{\prime})}}(u)E_{\mathcal{T}}E_{\mathcal{T}^{\prime}}=R_{(1\cdots n),(\underline{1}\cdots\underline{n}^{\prime})}^{\mathbf{c}_{(1\cdots n)},\mathbf{c}_{(\underline{1}\cdots\underline{n}^{\prime})}}(u)E_{\mathcal{T}}E_{\mathcal{T}^{\prime}},

(34)

where
E_{\mathcal{T}}
and
E_{\mathcal{T}^{\prime}}
project
V^{\otimes n}
and
V^{\otimes n^{\prime}}
onto their irreducible subspaces
W
and
\overline{W}
, respectively. We note that, in general, the matrix representation of (
34
) contains many null rows and columns, and that upon their removal, the expression will reduce to the form of the restricted fused
R
-matrix in (
31
).

2.3
Fused transfer matrices for closed spin chains

We first introduce the fused monodromy matrix, which is essentially the fused
R
-matrix:

T_{(1\cdots n)}(u)\equiv R_{(1\cdots n),(\underline{\underline{1}}\cdots\underline{\underline{n}}^{\prime\prime})}^{\mathbf{c}_{(1\cdots n)},\mathbf{c}_{(\underline{\underline{1}}\cdots\underline{\underline{n}}^{\prime\prime})}}(u),

(35)

where we treat
V_{(1\cdots n)}=V_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes V_{n}
as the fused auxiliary space with common spectral parameter
u
and distinct inhomogeneity parameters
\{c_{1},\cdots,c_{n}\}
, and consider
V_{(\underline{\underline{1}}\cdots\underline{\underline{n}}^{\prime\prime})}=V_{\underline{\underline{1}}}\otimes\cdots\otimes V_{\underline{\underline{n}}^{\prime\prime}}
as the physical/quantum space with zero spectral parameter and inhomogeneities
\{c_{\underline{\underline{1}}},\cdots,c_{\underline{\underline{n}}^{\prime\prime}}\}
.

The fused transfer matrix is then obtained by tracing over the auxiliary space as:

{\tau}^{\mathbf{c}_{(1\cdots n)}}(u)\equiv{\tau}^{\mathbf{c}_{(1\cdots n)},\mathbf{c}_{({\underline{\underline{1}}}\cdots{\underline{\underline{n}}}^{\prime\prime})}}_{({\underline{\underline{1}}}\cdots{\underline{\underline{n}}}^{\prime\prime})}(u)=\mbox{Tr}_{(1\cdots n)}T_{(1\cdots n)}(u)=\mbox{Tr}_{(1\cdots n)}R_{(1\cdots n),(\underline{\underline{1}}\cdots\underline{\underline{n}}^{\prime\prime})}^{\mathbf{c}_{(1\cdots n)},\mathbf{c}_{(\underline{\underline{1}}\cdots\underline{\underline{n}}^{\prime\prime})}}(u).

(36)

The fused transfer matrices with different spectral parameters and inhomogeneities form a commuting family:

\left[\tau(u)^{\mathbf{c}_{(1\cdots n)}},\tau(v)^{\mathbf{c}_{(\underline{1}\cdots\underline{n}^{\prime})}}\right]=0,\quad\forall u,v,c_{1}\cdots c_{n},c_{\underline{1}}\cdots c_{\underline{n}^{\prime}}\in\mathbb{C},

(37)

which follows directly from the fused YBE (
7
).

The fused monodromy matrix has a factorization into fundamental monodromy matrices:

T_{(1\cdots n)}(u)=R_{n,(\underline{\underline{1}}\cdots\underline{\underline{n}}^{\prime\prime})}^{c_{n},\mathbf{c}_{(\underline{\underline{1}}\cdots\underline{\underline{n}}^{\prime\prime})}}(u)\cdots R_{1,(\underline{\underline{1}}\cdots\underline{\underline{n}}^{\prime\prime})}^{c_{1},\mathbf{c}_{(\underline{\underline{1}}\cdots\underline{\underline{n}}^{\prime\prime})}}(u)=T_{n}(u)T_{n-1}(u)\cdots T_{1}(u),

(38)

which induces the corresponding decomposition of the fused transfer matrix in the total space
V_{(1\cdots n)}
:

{\tau}^{\mathbf{c}_{(1\cdots n)}}(u)\equiv{\tau}^{\mathbf{c}_{(1\cdots n)},\mathbf{c}_{({\underline{\underline{1}}}\cdots{\underline{\underline{n}}}^{\prime\prime})}}_{({\underline{\underline{1}}}\cdots{\underline{\underline{n}}}^{\prime\prime})}(u)=\tau^{c_{n}}(u)\tau^{c_{n-1}}(u)\cdots\tau^{c_{1}}(u).

(39)

The evaluation of the fused transfer matrix
\tau^{W}(u)
restricted to the invariant subspace
W
will be discussed in the next section.

=&\mbox{Tr}_{(12)}\left[K^{+,-c_{1}}_{1}(u)R_{21}^{-c_{2},-c_{1}}(-2u+N)K^{+,-c_{2}}_{2}(u)\right]\left[U_{2}(u)R_{12}^{c_{1},-c_{2}}(2u)U_{1}(u)\right]\\
=&\mbox{Tr}_{(12)}K^{+,-c_{1}}_{1}(u)K^{+,-c_{2}}_{2}(u)^{t_{2}}R_{21}^{-c_{2},-c_{1}}(-2u+N)^{t_{2}}R_{12}^{c_{1},-c_{2}}(2u)^{t_{2}}U_{2}(u)^{t_{2}}U_{1}(u)\\
=&\mbox{Tr}_{(12)}K^{+,-c_{1}}_{1}(u)K^{+,-c_{2}}_{2}(u)^{t_{2}}U_{2}(u)^{t_{2}}U_{1}(u)\\
=&\mbox{Tr}_{2}\left[K^{+,-c_{2}}_{2}(u)^{t_{2}}U_{2}(u)^{t_{2}}\right]\mbox{Tr}_{1}\left[K^{+,-c_{1}}_{1}(u)U_{1}(u)\right]=\tau^{c_{2}}(u)\tau^{c_{1}}(u).\end{split}

(84)

Then we find the
n
-fused transfer matrix
\tau^{\mathbf{c}_{(1\cdots n)}}(u)
is simply the multiplication of
n
fundamental transfer matrices:

\tau^{\mathbf{c}_{(1\cdots n)}}(u)=\tau^{c_{n}}(u)\tau^{c_{n-1}}(u)\cdots\tau^{c_{1}}(u),

(85)

which can be proved easily by a similar procedure as in (
84
):

\begin{split}\tau^{\mathbf{c}_{(1\cdots n)}}=&\mbox{Tr}_{(1\cdots n)}\left[K^{+,-\mathbf{c}_{(1\cdots n-1)}}_{(1\cdots n-1)}R_{(n-1\cdots 1),n}^{-\mathbf{c}_{(n-1\cdots 1)},c_{n}}(-2u+N)K^{+,-c_{n}}_{n}(u)\right]\\
&\left[U_{n}(u)R_{(1\cdots n-1),n}^{\mathbf{c}_{(1\cdots n-1)},-c_{n}}(2u)U_{(1\cdots n-1)}(u)\right]\\
=&\mbox{Tr}_{(1\cdots n)}K^{+,-\mathbf{c}_{(1\cdots n-1)}}_{(1\cdots n-1)}K^{+,-c_{n}}_{n}(u)^{t_{n}}R_{(n-1\cdots 1),n}^{-\mathbf{c}_{(n-1\cdots 1)},c_{n}}(-2u+N)^{t_{n}}R_{(1\cdots n-1),n}^{\mathbf{c}_{(1\cdots n-1)},-c_{n}}(2u)^{t_{n}}\\
&U_{n}(u)^{t_{n}}U_{(1\cdots n-1)}(u)\\
=&\left[\mbox{Tr}_{n}K^{+,-c_{n}}_{n}(u)^{t_{n}}U_{n}(u)^{t_{n}}\right]\left[\mbox{Tr}_{(1\cdots n-1)}K^{+,-\mathbf{c}_{(1\cdots n-1)}}_{(1\cdots n-1)}U_{(1\cdots n-1)}(u)\right]=\tau^{c_{n}}\tau^{\mathbf{c}_{(1\cdots n-1)}}.\end{split}

(86)

The relation (

) can also be understood graphically by successive applications of crossing unitary conditions, as shown in Fig.

3.4.2
Fused transfer matrices evaluated in the invariant subspace

By combining the definition (
67
) of
U_{(1\cdots n)}(u)
with the relations (
27
), (
30
), and (
51
), we find that the invariant subspace of
U_{(1\cdots n)}(u)
is given by
W=B^{\mathbf{c}_{(1\cdots n)}}_{(1\cdots n)}V^{\otimes n}
, which coincides precisely with the invariant subspace of
K^{+,-\mathbf{c}_{(1\cdots n)}}_{(1\cdots n)}(u)
. Thus we have:

K^{+,-\mathbf{c}_{(1\cdots n)}}_{(1\cdots n)}(u)U_{(1\cdots n)}\in{\rm{End}}(W).

(87)

Then the trace for the fused transfer matrix can be restricted to the invariant subspace
W
:

\tau^{W}(u)=\mbox{Tr}_{W}\left(K^{+,-\mathbf{c}_{(1\cdots n)}}_{(1\cdots n)}(u)U_{(1\cdots n)}(u)\right).

(88)

When
B^{\mathbf{c}_{(1\cdots n)}}_{(1\cdots n)}
is a projection operator:
V^{\otimes n}={\rm{Im}}B\oplus{\rm{Ker}}B
(where
B
denotes
B^{\mathbf{c}_{(1\cdots n)}}_{(1\cdots n)}
for short), we choose the corresponding basis for
W(={\rm{Im}}B)
and
W^{\bot}(={\rm{Ker}}B)
as:

\begin{split}W&={\rm{{span}}}\{|i\rangle,i=1\cdots{\rm{dim}}W\},\\
W^{\bot}&={\rm{{span}}}\{|\tilde{\alpha}\rangle,\alpha=1\cdots{\rm{dim}}W^{\bot}\}.\end{split}

(89)

Since
B|i\rangle=|i\rangle,B|\tilde{\alpha}\rangle=0
, we obtain:

\begin{split}\tau^{W}(u)=&\sum_{i}\langle i|K^{+,-\mathbf{c}_{(1\cdots n)}}_{(1\cdots n)}(u)U_{(1\cdots n)}(u)|i\rangle=\sum_{i}\langle i|K^{+,-\mathbf{c}_{(1\cdots n)}}_{(1\cdots n)}(u)U_{(1\cdots n)}(u)B^{\mathbf{c}_{(1\cdots n)}}_{(1\cdots n)}|i\rangle\\
=&\sum_{i}\langle i|B^{\mathbf{c}_{(1\cdots n)}}_{(1\cdots n)}K^{+,-\mathbf{c}_{(1\cdots n)}}_{(1\cdots n)}(u)B^{\mathbf{c}_{(1\cdots n)}}_{(1\cdots n)}B^{\mathbf{c}_{(1\cdots n)}}_{(1\cdots n)}U_{(1\cdots n)}(u)B^{\mathbf{c}_{(1\cdots n)}}_{(1\cdots n)}|i\rangle\\
=&\mbox{Tr}_{W}\left[B^{\mathbf{c}_{(1\cdots n)}}_{(1\cdots n)}K^{+,-\mathbf{c}_{(1\cdots n)}}_{(1\cdots n)}(u)B^{\mathbf{c}_{(1\cdots n)}}_{(1\cdots n)}B^{\mathbf{c}_{(1\cdots n)}}_{(1\cdots n)}U_{(1\cdots n)}(u)B^{\mathbf{c}_{(1\cdots n)}}_{(1\cdots n)}\right]\\
=&\mbox{Tr}_{W\oplus W^{\bot}}\left[B^{\mathbf{c}_{(1\cdots n)}}_{(1\cdots n)}K^{+,-\mathbf{c}_{(1\cdots n)}}_{(1\cdots n)}(u)B^{\mathbf{c}_{(1\cdots n)}}_{(1\cdots n)}B^{\mathbf{c}_{(1\cdots n)}}_{(1\cdots n)}U_{(1\cdots n)}(u)B^{\mathbf{c}_{(1\cdots n)}}_{(1\cdots n)}\right],\end{split}

(90)

where in the derivations, we have used the relations:

\begin{split}&U_{(1\cdots n)}(u)B^{\mathbf{c}_{(1\cdots n)}}_{(1\cdots n)}=B^{\mathbf{c}_{(1\cdots n)}}_{(1\cdots n)}U_{(1\cdots n)}(u)B^{\mathbf{c}_{(1\cdots n)}}_{(1\cdots n)},\\
&K^{+,-\mathbf{c}_{(1\cdots n)}}_{(1\cdots n)}(u)B^{\mathbf{c}_{(1\cdots n)}}_{(1\cdots n)}=B^{\mathbf{c}_{(1\cdots n)}}_{(1\cdots n)}K^{+,-\mathbf{c}_{(1\cdots n)}}_{(1\cdots n)}(u)B^{\mathbf{c}_{(1\cdots n)}}_{(1\cdots n)}.\end{split}

(91)

For an appropriately selected set of parameters
\{c_{1}\cdots c_{n}\}
, the fusion operator
B^{\mathbf{c}_{(1\cdots n)}}_{(1\cdots n)}
could be a 1-dimensional projector in
V^{\otimes n}
. For any 1-dim projector
Q
, we have:

QTQ=\mbox{Tr}\left(QT\right)Q,\quad\forall T.

(92)

Hence, the expression of
\tau^{W}(u)
in (
90
) can be further reduced to:

\tau^{W}(u)=\mbox{Tr}_{W}\left[B^{\mathbf{c}_{(1\cdots n)}}_{(1\cdots n)}K^{+,-\mathbf{c}_{(1\cdots n)}}_{(1\cdots n)}(u)\right]\mbox{Tr}_{W}\left[B^{\mathbf{c}_{(1\cdots n)}}_{(1\cdots n)}U_{(1\cdots n)}(u)\right],

(93)

which equals to the product of two quantum determinants.

u^{\prime}=-u,\,v^{\prime}=-v+1

\left(u^{\prime}-v^{\prime}-1+\mathbb{K}_{a\bar{b}}\right)\left(u^{\prime}-\mathbb{P}_{ac}\right)\left(v^{\prime}-3+\mathbb{K}_{\bar{b}c}\right)=\left(v^{\prime}-3+\mathbb{K}_{\bar{b}c}\right)\left(u^{\prime}-\mathbb{P}_{ac}\right)\left(u^{\prime}-v^{\prime}-1+\mathbb{K}_{a\bar{b}}\right)

(125)

which is just the YBE obeyed by the fused
R
-matrices (
4.1.3
),

R_{a\bar{b}}(u^{\prime}-v^{\prime})R_{ac}(u^{\prime})R_{\bar{b}c}(v^{\prime})=R_{\bar{b}c}(v^{\prime})R_{ac}(u^{\prime})R_{a\bar{b}}(u^{\prime}-v^{\prime}),

(126)

and thus we complete the demonstration of the equivalence.

4.2
Fused
K^{-}
-matrix solutions in
V_{\bar{\mathbf{4}}}

Now we consider the fused RE (
45
) for
n=1,n^{\prime}=3
with the parameters
c_{\underline{1}}=0,c_{\underline{2}}=-1,c_{\underline{3}}=-2
:

\begin{split}&R_{1,(\underline{1},\underline{2},\underline{3})}^{0,(0,-1,-2)}(u-v)K^{-}_{1}(u)R_{(\underline{1},\underline{2},\underline{3}),1}^{(0,-1,-2),0}(u+v)K_{(\underline{1},\underline{2},\underline{3})}^{-,(0,-1,-2)}(v)\\
=&K_{(\underline{1},\underline{2},\underline{3})}^{-,(0,-1,-2)}(v)R_{1,(\underline{3},\underline{2},\underline{1})}^{0,(2,1,0)}(u+v)K^{-}_{1}(u)R_{(\underline{3},\underline{2},\underline{1}),1}^{(2,1,0),0}(u-v).\end{split}

(127)

Due to the P-symmetric property:

R_{1,(\underline{3},\underline{2},\underline{1})}^{0,(2,1,0)}(u+v)=R_{(\underline{1},\underline{2},\underline{3}),1}^{(0,-1,-2),0}(u+v),\quad R_{(\underline{3},\underline{2},\underline{1}),1}^{(2,1,0),0}(u-v)=R_{1,(\underline{1},\underline{2},\underline{3})}^{0,(0,-1,-2)}(u-v),

(128)

and the relations (
30
) and (
51
), we know the invariant subspace for the above equation (
127
) is:
V_{\textbf{4}}\otimes B_{(123)}^{(0,-1,-2)}V^{\otimes 3}_{\textbf{4}}=V_{\textbf{4}}\otimes V_{\bar{\textbf{4}}}
. When evaluated in
V_{\textbf{4}}\otimes V_{\bar{\textbf{4}}}
, the fused RE (
127
) becomes:

R_{\textbf{4}\bar{\textbf{4}}}(u-v)K^{-}_{\textbf{4}}(u)R_{\bar{\textbf{4}}\textbf{4}}(u+v)K^{-}_{\bar{\textbf{4}}}(v)=K^{-}_{\bar{\textbf{4}}}(v)R_{\bar{\textbf{4}}\textbf{4}}(u+v)K^{-}_{\textbf{4}}(u)R_{\textbf{4}\bar{\textbf{4}}}(u-v).

(129)

Next we will find the concrete form of
K^{-}_{\bar{\mathbf{4}}}(u)
, which is derived by imposing the restriction:

K^{-}_{\bar{\mathbf{4}}}(u)=K^{-,(0,-1,-2)}_{(123)}(u)\bigg|_{V_{\bar{\mathbf{4}}}}.

(130)

The calculation relies on the intertwining relation (see
49
):

K_{(123)}^{-,(0,-1,-2)}(u)B_{(123)}^{(0,-1,-2)}=B_{(123)}^{(0,-1,-2)}K_{(321)}^{-,(-2,-1,0)}(u),

(131)

and also the detailed expression of
K_{(321)}^{-,(-2,-1,0)}(u)
, which is obtained from the decomposition relation (
43
) as:

K_{(321)}^{-,(-2,-1,0)}(u)=K^{-}_{1}(u)R_{21}(2u-1)R_{31}(2u-2)K^{-}_{2}(u-1)R_{32}(2u-3)K^{-}_{3}(u-2),

(132)

where
K^{-}(u)
is the solution of the fundamental RE. Then, by the action of the fused
K_{(123)}^{-,(0,-1,-2)}(u)
matrix on the canonical basis (
100
) of
V_{\mathbf{4}}
, we will obtain the matrix form of
K^{-}_{\bar{\mathbf{4}}}(u)
.

The explicit form of
K^{-}_{\bar{\mathbf{4}}}(u)
depends on the concrete selection of the solutions for

which take the general form:
K^{-}_{\mathbf{4}}(u)=\xi\mathbb{I}+u\mathbb{E}
, where
\xi
is a constant and

must be chosen from the following two categories: (a)
\mathbb{E}
is diagonal and
\mathbb{E}^{2}=\mathbb{I}
; (b)
\mathbb{E}
is strictly triangular and
\mathbb{E}^{2}=0

[

matrices from these two categories as shown below

:

\displaystyle{\mbox{type I:}}\quad K^{-}_{\mathbf{4}}(u)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}\xi+u&0&0&0\\
0&\xi-u&0&0\\
0&0&\xi-u&0\\
0&0&0&\xi-u\end{array}\right),

\displaystyle{\mbox{type II:}}\quad K^{-}_{\mathbf{4}}(u)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}\xi&u&&\\
0&\xi&&\\
&&\xi&u\\
&&0&\xi\end{array}\right),\qquad{\mbox{type III:}}\quad K^{-}_{\mathbf{4}}(u)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}\xi&u&&\\

4.2.1
K^{-}_{\bar{\mathbf{4}}}(u)
from Type I solution of

In this case, the action of

Thus, by discarding the common prefactor

&&\xi+u-2&\\

\end{array}\right).

(148)

For the particular case

5
Conclusion

In this paper, we present a general fusion procedure for open
\mathfrak{gl}(N)
spin chains. We construct boundary reflection
K^{-}
- and
K^{+}
-matrices with inhomogeneity parameters, as well as their associated fused reflection equations and dual fused reflection equations. From the intertwining relations between the boundary reflection matrices and the fusion operators, we obtain the invariant subspaces for the
K^{-}
and
K^{+}
-matrices. When specific inhomogeneity parameters are chosen, we derive the boundary reflection matrices restricted to finite-dimensional irreducible representations of
\mathfrak{gl}(N)
. Using the fused reflection matrices and fused
R
-matrices, we construct the corresponding fused transfer matrices and demonstrate the commutativity of these fused transfer matrices for different spectral parameters, thus serving as generating functions for the conserved charges. We also compute explicit expressions for the fused transfer matrices and find that: when the auxiliary space is the total tensor product space, the fused transfer matrix can be expressed as a product of fundamental transfer matrices. When the auxiliary space is projected to a one-dimensional invariant subspace by the fusion operator, the fused transfer matrix factorizes into a product of quantum determinants. As a specific application of the fusion procedure, we investigate the ABJM spin chain model. We find that, when the fusion operator is taken to be the projection operator onto the anti-fundamental representation space of
\mathfrak{su}(4)
, the resulting fused
R
-matrix reduces precisely to the
R
-matrix of the ABJM model, thus demonstrating that the ABJM spin chain is essentially an
\mathfrak{su}(4)
fusion model. Using fusion techniques, we also obtain solutions for three classes of
K^{-}
-matrices defined on the anti-fundamental representation space. For another application of
\mathfrak{su}(4)
fusion procedure, we prove that the well-known
\mathfrak{so}(6)

R
-matrix originating from SYM spin chain can be obtained by the antisymmetric fusion of
\mathfrak{su}(4)

R
-matrices.

We would like to point out several directions for future research. First, starting from more general
K^{-}
-matrix solutions in
\mathfrak{su}(4)
fundamental representation space (e.g., those related to our solutions by similarity transformations), we could obtain much broader classes of reflection matrix solutions in anti-fundamental representation spaces of
\mathfrak{su}(4)
through fusion procedure. Furthermore, beyond the ABJM model context, solutions to the reflection equations could be considered in other irreducible representation spaces of
\mathfrak{su}(4)
. Second, based on the boundary reflection matrices derived by the fusion procedure, we can investigate the corresponding Hamiltonians with open boundaries and explore physically relevant new systems. We may also study the integrable boundary states constructed from these novel boundary reflection matrices. Third, a more challenging task is to investigate the eigenspectra of these novel open spin chain systems. For general non-diagonal boundary reflection matrices (such as those obtained in this work), the ODBA method may be required. Therefore, we need to establish the hierarchy relations among the fused transfer matrices defined in different representation subspaces, which may be obtained from the branching properties of the fusion operators.

Appendix A
\mathfrak{so}(6)
integrable spin chain as a
\mathfrak{su}(4)
fusion model

Since the
\mathfrak{so}(6)
spinor representation is isomorphic to the antisymmetric representation of
\mathfrak{su}(4)
, we consider the
\mathfrak{su}(4)
fused
R
-matrix of the following type:

R^{(c_{1},c_{2}),(c_{\underline{1}},c_{\underline{2}})}_{(12),(\underline{1}\underline{2})}(u)\in{\rm{End}}(V^{\otimes 2}\otimes V^{\otimes 2}).

(154)

By choosing
c_{1}=c_{\underline{1}}=0,c_{2}=c_{\underline{2}}=-1
, we focus on the antisymmetric invariant subspaces
\Lambda V^{\otimes 2}\otimes\Lambda V^{\otimes 2}
:

\Lambda V^{\otimes 2}\otimes\Lambda V^{\otimes 2}=B_{12}^{(0,-1)}B_{\underline{1}\underline{2}}^{(0,-1)}V^{\otimes 2}\otimes V^{\otimes 2}.

(155)

The basis for subspace
\Lambda V^{\otimes 2}
is chosen as:

\begin{split}E_{1}=e_{1}\otimes e_{2}-e_{2}\otimes e_{1}=B_{12}^{(0,-1)}e_{1}\otimes e_{2},\quad E_{2}=e_{1}\otimes e_{3}-e_{3}\otimes e_{1}=B_{12}^{(0,-1)}e_{1}\otimes e_{3},\\
E_{3}=e_{1}\otimes e_{4}-e_{4}\otimes e_{1}=B_{12}^{(0,-1)}e_{1}\otimes e_{4},\quad E_{4}=e_{2}\otimes e_{3}-e_{3}\otimes e_{2}=B_{12}^{(0,-1)}e_{2}\otimes e_{3},\\
E_{5}=e_{2}\otimes e_{4}-e_{4}\otimes e_{2}=B_{12}^{(0,-1)}e_{2}\otimes e_{4},\quad E_{6}=e_{3}\otimes e_{4}-e_{4}\otimes e_{3}=B_{12}^{(0,-1)}e_{3}\otimes e_{4},\end{split}

(156)

where
\{e_{i}\}
are standard basis vectors in
V_{\mathbf{4}}
. The
R
-matrix we are looking for is:

R_{\mathbf{66}}(u)=R^{(0,-1),(0,-1)}_{(12),(\underline{1}\underline{2})}(u)|_{\Lambda V^{\otimes 2}\otimes\Lambda V^{\otimes 2}}.

(157)

We denote the basis of the tensor space
\Lambda V^{\otimes 2}\otimes\Lambda V^{\otimes 2}
by
E_{ij}=E_{i}\otimes E_{j}
. Then we find:

\displaystyle R_{\mathbf{66}}(u)E_{16}=E_{16}+\frac{2}{u(u-1)}E_{61}-\frac{1}{u-1}E_{25}-\frac{1}{u-1}E_{52}+\frac{1}{u-1}E_{34}+\frac{1}{u-1}E_{43},

(158)

\displaystyle R_{\mathbf{66}}(u)E_{61}=E_{61}+\frac{2}{u(u-1)}E_{16}-\frac{1}{u-1}E_{25}-\frac{1}{u-1}E_{52}+\frac{1}{u-1}E_{34}+\frac{1}{u-1}E_{43},

(159)

\displaystyle R_{\mathbf{66}}(u)E_{25}=E_{25}+\frac{2}{u(u-1)}E_{52}-\frac{1}{u-1}E_{16}-\frac{1}{u-1}E_{61}-\frac{1}{u-1}E_{34}-\frac{1}{u-1}E_{43},

(160)

\displaystyle R_{\mathbf{66}}(u)E_{52}=E_{52}+\frac{2}{u(u-1)}E_{25}-\frac{1}{u-1}E_{16}-\frac{1}{u-1}E_{61}-\frac{1}{u-1}E_{34}-\frac{1}{u-1}E_{43},

(161)

\displaystyle R_{\mathbf{66}}(u)E_{34}=E_{34}+\frac{2}{u(u-1)}E_{43}+\frac{1}{u-1}E_{16}+\frac{1}{u-1}E_{61}-\frac{1}{u-1}E_{25}-\frac{1}{u-1}E_{52},

(162)

\displaystyle R_{\mathbf{66}}(u)E_{43}=E_{43}+\frac{2}{u(u-1)}E_{34}+\frac{1}{u-1}E_{16}+\frac{1}{u-1}E_{61}-\frac{1}{u-1}E_{25}-\frac{1}{u-1}E_{52},

(163)

and
R_{\mathbf{66}}(u)E_{ij}=\frac{u-2}{u-1}E_{ij}-\frac{u-2}{u(u-1)}E_{ji}
for all other cases. Then, for example, we rewrite (
158
) as:

R_{\mathbf{66}}(u)E_{16}=\frac{u-2}{u-1}E_{16}-\frac{u-2}{u(u-1)}E_{61}+\frac{1}{u-1}\left[E_{16}+E_{61}+(-E_{25})+(-E_{52})+E_{34}+E_{43}\right].

(164)

Thus, by redefining the indices of the basis, we can readily obtain the matrix form of
R_{\mathbf{66}}(u)
as:

R_{\mathbf{66}}(u)=u(u-2)\mathbb{I}-(u-2)\mathbb{P}+u\mathbb{K},

(165)

where an overall factor of
u(u-1)
has been omitted. The
R
-matrix of integrable
\mathfrak{so}(n)
spin chain, as given in
[
53
]
, is:

R_{12}(u)=\frac{1}{n-2}\left[u(2u+2-n)\mathbb{I}-(2u+2-n)\mathbb{P}+2u\mathbb{K}\right],

(166)

which coincides with ours when
n=6
.

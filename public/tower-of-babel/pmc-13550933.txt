Correction to: “Association Between Anthropometric Indices and Nonanthropometric Components of Metabolic Syndrome in Saudi Adults”
Published: 2026

In the above-named article by Al-Ahmadi J, Enani S, Bahijri S, Al-Raddadi R, Jambi H, Eldakhakhny B, Borai A, Ajabnoor G, Tuomilehto J, (J Endo Society. 2022; 6(6): bvac055; doi: 10.1210/jendso/bvac055), there were multiple errors throughout the article.

In the originally published article, the ethics approval reference number was inadvertently omitted from the ethics statement in the Study Design and Data Collection section. The study was approved under Reference No. 1270-13 by the Committee on Ethics of Human Research at the Faculty of Medicine, King Abdulaziz University, and the Committee on Research Ethics at the Ministry of Health, Kingdom of Saudi Arabia.

In the originally published article, in the Biochemical Assays section, a sentence reads, “Another sample was collected 1 hour after ingestion of 50-g glucose solution (CASCO NERL Diagnostics) for estimating plasma glucose (1-hour oral glucose tolerance test)”, but 1-hour glucose results were not presented. This sentence was inadvertently added. The 1-hour post-load glucose sample was collected as part of the broader study protocol, in which it was used to assess diabetes risk in a separate analysis. It was not used to define clinical metabolic abnormality (CMA) or in any of the analyses reported in the article, and accordingly, no 1-hour glucose data are presented in the Results.

In the originally published article, in the Statistical Analysis section, a sentence reads, “The optimal cutoff values for the identification of CMA and its 5 components were determined based on the nearest distance to corner in the ROC curve.” This was a typographical error. The reference to "5 components" in the Statistical Analysis section is incorrect and should read "4 components." CMA is defined by 4 nonanthropometric components (high triglycerides, low HDL-C, high blood pressure, and high fasting plasma glucose), as stated in the Definition of Clinical Metabolic Abnormality (Nonanthropometric Components of Metabolic Syndrome) section and reflected throughout Tables 3 and 4.

In the originally published article, in the Results section, a sentence reads, “Men had a significantly higher prevalence of CMA (P < .01), high TGs (P < .001), and high BP (P < .001) than women, who had a significantly lower prevalence of low HDL-C (P < .001).” The word "lower" was incorrectly used. The sentence should read, “Men had a significantly higher prevalence of CMA (P = .002), high TGs (P < .001), and high BP (P < .001) than women, who had a significantly higher prevalence of low HDL-C (P < .001),” consistent with Figure 1 and the underlying data.

In the originally published article, the footnote descriptors referring to “significant correlation” in Tables 3 and 4 are mislabeled. In Table 3, the footnotes should read as follows:

a Statistically significant odds ratio (P ≤ .05).

b Statistically significant odds ratio (P ≤ .01).

c Statistically significant odds ratio (P ≤ .001).

In Table 4, the footnotes should read as follows:

a AUC significantly different from 0.5 (P ≤ .05).

b AUC significantly different from 0.5 (P ≤ .01).

c AUC significantly different from 0.5 (P ≤ .001).

In the originally published article, in the Diagnostic Ability of Anthropometric Indices for Clinical Metabolic Abnormality and its Components section, a sentence reads, “WC, WC:Ht, and AVI had the best abilities to identify CMA in women (AUCWC: 0.75 [95% CI, 0.69-0.80], AUCWC:Ht 0.75 [95% CI, 0.70-0.80], and AUCAVI 0.75 [95% CI, 0.64-0.80].” This is inconsistent with what is shown in Table 4 due to a typographical error. The sentence should read, “WC, WC:Ht, and AVI had the best abilities to identify CMA in women (AUCWC: 0.75 [95% CI, 0.69-0.80], AUCWC:Ht 0.75 [95% CI, 0.70-0.80], and AUCAVI 0.75 [95% CI, 0.69-0.80].”

In the originally published article, in the Data Availability section, a link was provided to the data as a .rar archive with limited metadata; that link is no longer functional. The corrected Data Availability statement should read, “The datasets generated during and/or analyzed during the current study are not publicly available but may be obtained from the corresponding author upon reasonable request”.

The authors regret these errors.

Earth-abundant CoMnFeSi Heusler alloy as a promising catalyst for the hydrogen evolution reaction
Authors: Bilal Aladerah, Mais Alkhazaleh, Abdalla Obeidat
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe development of cost-effective and efficient catalysts for the hydrogen evolution reaction (HER) is essential for sustainable hydrogen production. In this work, first-principles density functional theory (DFT) calculations were employed to investigate the electronic, surface stability, and HER catalytic properties of the earth-abundant quaternary Heusler alloy CoMnFeSi. Surface energy calculations show that the CoFe-terminated (011) surface is the most thermodynamically stable surface, with a surface energy of 0.95 eV/Å2 for the four-layer slab model. Hydrogen adsorption calculations reveal that the Fe site on this surface provides the most favorable adsorption environment, with a Gibbs free energy of hydrogen adsorption () of − 0.05 eV, which is close to the thermoneutral value required for efficient HER catalysis. In contrast, the Co and Mn sites exhibit less favorable hydrogen adsorption, with values of − 0.34 eV and + 1.44 eV, respectively. Orbital-resolved projected density of states analysis further shows that hybridization between the H 1s state and Fe ​ state contributes to stabilizing the adsorbed hydrogen intermediate. These results indicate that CoMnFeSi combines earth-abundant composition, favorable surface stability, conductive electronic character, and near-optimal hydrogen adsorption, making it a promising candidate for HER catalysis.Keywords: Quaternary Heusler alloy, Hydrogen evolution reaction, Density functional theory, Hydrogen adsorption, Surface stabilitySubject terms: Chemistry, Materials science

Introduction

The growing depletion of fossil fuels and their environmental consequences have intensified the pursuit of sustainable and renewable energy sources1–3. Hydrogen is considered a particularly attractive candidate owing to its high energy density, environmental compatibility, renewability, flexible storage and transport, and wide-ranging applications4–6. A key step in hydrogen production is the hydrogen evolution reaction (HER), which requires catalysts with low overpotential and fast kinetics to achieve high efficiency6–8. Although platinum (Pt) and its alloys remain the most active HER catalysts, their scarcity and high cost restrict large-scale utilization. Consequently, the development of inexpensive, earth-abundant alternatives is an urgent research priority9–13.

Heusler alloys have recently emerged as promising catalytic materials, offering unique physicochemical characteristics such as half-metallicity14, magnetism15–22, thermoelectricity23, superconductivity24, and non-trivial band topology25–27. Their catalytic behavior is strongly influenced by tunable electronic structures, largely governed by ligand and ensemble effects that arise from compositional variability28,29. For instance, substituting elements has been shown to regulate activity in selective hydrogenation reactions, exemplified by the Co2MnxFe1−xGe system28. Similarly, the catalytic current for the oxygen evolution reaction (OER) in Co2MnZ (Z = Ti, V, Al, Ga) can be optimized when Eg orbital occupancy approaches unity30. In Ru-based systems such as Ru2YZ (Y = Sc, Ti, V, Fe; Z = Sn, Ga, Ge, As, P)31, electron-rich Ru atoms enhance N2 activation by lowering adsorption barriers32. Moreover, coupling with external magnetic fields can tune intermediate binding energies, thereby enhancing HER activity33. Rh-based Heusler alloys, including NiRh2Sb and CuRh2Sb, have also demonstrated reduced overpotentials for HER and OER, accompanied by high electrochemically active surface areas34. These findings raise important questions regarding additional factors that may govern the catalytic efficiency of Heusler alloys and the potential of other transition-metal-based systems. In this work, CoMnFeSi was chosen as the target system due to its favorable combination of elemental abundance, reported structural stability, and tunable transition-metal electronic states. The alloy consists of relatively earth-abundant elements and has been experimentally reported to crystallize in a stable quaternary Heusler structure with a Curie temperature well above room temperature. Moreover, the coexistence of Co, Mn, and Fe atoms provides chemically distinct transition-metal sites that may offer different hydrogen adsorption environments, making CoMnFeSi a suitable platform for investigating HER activity in quaternary Heusler alloys. Therefore, we investigate CoMnFeSi as a promising HER catalyst and demonstrate that strong orbital hybridization between the H 1s state and Fe ​ state plays a key role in its catalytic performance. The unique contribution of this work is the identification of the CoFe-terminated CoMnFeSi (011) surface as a stable, earth-abundant catalytic surface with near-thermoneutral hydrogen adsorption at the Fe site, providing both an activity descriptor and an electronic-structure explanation for its HER performance.

Computational details

First-principles calculations were carried out using the Vienna Ab initio Simulation Package (VASP)35, based on DFT. The exchange–correlation effects were treated with the Perdew–Burke–Ernzerhof (PBE) functional within the generalized gradient approximation (GGA)36. A plane-wave cutoff energy of 500 eV was employed for structural optimizations. The Brillouin zone was sampled using an 18 × 18 × 18 k-point mesh. The convergence thresholds for the total energy and atomic forces were set to 10⁻⁶ eV/atom and -0.02 eV/Å, respectively. To evaluate the HER activity, several surfaces were examined. The surface with the lowest energy was identified, followed by convergence tests with respect to the number of atomic layers. A 1 × 1 × n (n = 1–5) supercell slab model was constructed, incorporating a 15 Å vacuum region to eliminate interlayer interactions. A k-point mesh of 6 × 6 × 1 was used for slab calculations.

Results and discussions

The cubic quaternary Heusler alloy CoMnFeSi crystallizes in the F-43 m space group (No. 216). In this structure, the Co, Mn, Fe, and Si atoms occupy the Wyckoff positions (1/2, 1/2, 1/2), (1/4, 1/4, 1/4), (0, 0, 0), and (3/4, 3/4, 3/4), respectively, as illustrated in Fig. 1a. The fully relaxed lattice constant of CoMnFeSi is calculated to be a = b = c = 5.501 Å, which is in close agreement with the experimental value of 5.658 Å39. To further assess its thermodynamic stability, the formation energy was evaluated relative to the elemental bulk phases of Co, Mn, Fe, and Si, yielding a value of − 3.73 eV/atom. The negative formation energy indicates that the formation of CoMnFeSi is energetically favorable, supporting the thermodynamic feasibility of the quaternary Heusler phase. The high Curie temperature of CoMnFeSi (~ 620 K) is significantly above room temperature (~ 300 K), suggesting that the material retains its ferromagnetic ordering under typical reaction conditions 39. This magnetic stability implies that spin polarization effects could influence the HER mechanism, as spin-dependent charge transport often enhances catalytic efficiency by modifying adsorption energetics and charge transfer kinetics. Therefore, it is essential to consider magnetic effects when assessing the HER activity of CoMnFeSi. In the ferromagnetic configuration, the electronic band structure calculated along the high-symmetry path X–W–L–Γ–X, together with the projected density of states (PDOS) shown in Fig. 1b,c, confirms that CoMnFeSi exhibits half-metallic character with an indirect band gap of approximately 0.51 eV in the minority spin channel, while the majority spin channel remains metallic. The metallic behavior in one spin channel provides abundant conduction electrons, while the semiconducting nature in the opposite spin channel contributes to spin-selective charge transfer, which could enhance catalytic selectivity and reduce recombination losses during the HER process. Furthermore, the prominent density of states near the Fermi level, primarily arising from Fe d-orbitals, indicates a strong contribution of Fe sites to the electronic conductivity and catalytic activity. In addition, Experimental transport measurements reported for CoFeMnSi showed high electrical conductivity and nearly temperature-independent carrier concentration over a broad temperature range, supporting its conductive electronic character. This is consistent with the present calculated band structure and DOS, which show electronic states near the Fermi level and suggest favorable charge transport for HER.

Among the considered surfaces, the (011) orientation exhibits the lowest surface energy, indicating that it is the most thermodynamically stable among the examined facets and is therefore expected to be energetically favored. To further evaluate the stability of possible (011) terminations, both CoFe-terminated and MnSi-terminated surfaces were examined. As shown in Fig. 2a, the CoFe-terminated surface has a lower surface energy than the MnSi-terminated surface, confirming its greater thermodynamic stability. This stability can be attributed to the favorable surface coordination of Co and Fe atoms and their metallic d-state character, which allows more effective charge redistribution and stabilization of under-coordinated surface atoms after relaxation. In contrast, the MnSi termination creates a less favorable surface environment, resulting in a higher surface energy. Therefore, the CoFe-terminated (011) surface was selected for the subsequent hydrogen adsorption and HER activity analyses. The exposure of Co and Fe atoms on this stable surface also provides suitable transition-metal sites for proton–electron transfer during the hydrogen evolution reaction.

To assess the HER catalytic activity of CoMnFeSi, we calculated the Gibbs free energy of hydrogen adsorption (), which serves as a key descriptor of HER performance. The hydrogen adsorption energies () were obtained for H atoms adsorbed at the top sites of Co, Fe, and Mn atoms on the most stable CoFe-terminated (011) surface, as illustrated in Fig. 3. The corresponding adsorption energies are − 0.34 eV, − 0.05 eV, and + 1.44 eV for Co, Fe, and Mn sites, respectively. Figure 2b presents the optimized structure of H adsorbed on the top of an Fe atom on the (011) surface. Among the examined sites, hydrogen adsorption is most favorable on the Fe site, which exhibits an adsorption free energy () of approximately − 0.05 eV after zero-point energy and entropy corrections. This value is remarkably close to the ideal thermoneutral condition (), indicating an optimal balance between H adsorption and desorption — a key requirement for efficient HER catalysis. In contrast, the Co site binds hydrogen too strongly (), potentially hindering H₂ desorption, while the Mn site binds hydrogen too weakly (), resulting in poor H adsorption kinetics. Notably, the Fe site of CoMnFeSi demonstrates a superior adsorption performance compared with conventional noble-metal catalysts such as Pt () and previously reported Heusler alloys such as Co₂TiAl (). This suggests that the Fe atoms at the surface act as the primary active centers for the HER, offering both high activity and favorable binding strength without relying on precious metals.

Conclusion

In this study, the catalytic performance of the quaternary Heusler alloy CoMnFeSi toward HER was systematically investigated using first principles DFT calculations. Surface energy analysis identified the CoFe-terminated (011) surface as the most thermodynamically stable facet among the examined surfaces, with a surface energy of 0.95 eV/Å2. Hydrogen adsorption calculations revealed that the Fe site on this surface exhibits a Gibbs free energy of hydrogen adsorption (​) of − 0.05 eV, which is very close to the thermoneutral value and indicates a favorable balance between hydrogen adsorption and release. In comparison, the Co and Mn sites show less favorable adsorption behavior, with values of − 0.34 and + 1.44 eV, respectively. These results indicate that the Fe site is the most active adsorption center on the CoFe-terminated CoMnFeSi (011) surface. Orbital-resolved PDOS analysis further showed that Fe dz2–H 1 s hybridization stabilizes the adsorbed hydrogen intermediate. Overall, the CoFe-terminated CoMnFeSi (011) surface combines conductive electronic character, magnetic robustness, and near-optimal hydrogen-binding characteristics, making it a promising earth-abundant candidate for HER catalysis.

Despite these promising results, some limitations should be acknowledged. The present work is based on first-principles calculations and evaluates HER activity mainly through the hydrogen adsorption free energy descriptor. In addition, the calculations were performed using idealized slab models, which may not fully represent surface defects or more complex operating environments. Therefore, future experimental studies will be useful for validating the predicted catalytic performance and further assessing the practical applicability of CoMnFeSi-based HER catalysts.

Error Estimates of Semi-Lagrangian Schemes for Diffusive Conservation Laws
Haruki Takemura

Abstract
. We present error estimates of the fully semi-Lagrangian scheme with high-order interpolation operators, solving the initial value problems for the one-dimensional nonlinear diffusive conservation laws, including the Burgers equations. We impose certain assumptions on the interpolation operator, which are satisfied by both spline and Hermite interpolations. We establish the convergence rates of O ⁡ ( Δ ​ t + h 2 ​ s / Δ ​ t ) O(\Delta t+h^{2s}/\Delta t) in the L 2 L^{2} -norm and O ⁡ ( Δ ​ t + h s / ( Δ ​ t ) 1 / 2 + h 2 ​ s / Δ ​ t ) O(\Delta t+h^{s}/(\Delta t)^{1/2}+h^{2s}/\Delta t) in the H s H^{s} -norm for the spatial mesh size h h and the temporal step size Δ ​ t \Delta t , where the spline or Hermite interpolation operator of degree ( 2 ​ s − 1 ) (2s-1) is employed. The numerical results are in agreement with the theoretical analysis.



1.
Introduction

Semi-Lagrangian methods are widely used for the numerical approximation of conservation laws. One of the main advantages of the semi-Lagrangian framework is that it is free from the Courant–Friedrichs–Lewy condition, which allows for relatively large time steps and improved computational efficiency. As a result, semi-Lagrangian methods have been applied to a broad range of problems including atmospheric simulations
[
13
]
and plasma physics
[
12
]
.

In this paper, we consider the initial value problem of the nonlinear advection–diffusion equation in the periodic domain
\mathbb{T}=\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}
:

(1.1)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u+f(u)\,\partial_{x}u-\nu\,\partial_{x}^{2}u=0&\text{in}\quad\mathbb{T}\times[0,T],\\
u(0)=u_{0}&\text{in}\quad\mathbb{T},\end{cases}

where
u_{0}
is the given initial data, and
\nu
is a positive constant. The given flux
f
satisfies
c_{f}\leq\left|f^{\prime}\right|\leq C_{f}
for some positive constants
c_{f},C_{f}
. In this paper, we assume that
f
and
u_{0}
are sufficiently smooth so that the problem (
1.1
) admits a unique, sufficiently smooth solution
u
.

We now introduce the numerical scheme studied in this paper. Let
N_{t},N_{x}\in\mathbb{N}
,
\Delta t=T/N_{t}
,
t_{n}=n\Delta t
. Let
0=x_{0}<x_{1}<\cdots<x_{N_{x}}=1
denote a spatial grid on
\mathbb{T}
. We define the mesh size as
h=\max_{m}|x_{m}-x_{m-1}|
. The fully semi-Lagrangian scheme for (
1.1
) proposed in
[
4
]
is given by

(1.2)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}u_{m}^{0}=u_{0}(x_{m}),\\
\begin{aligned} u_{m}^{n}&=\frac{1}{2}\tilde{\mathcal{I}}_{h}[U^{n-1}]\left(x_{m}-f(u_{m}^{n})\Delta t-\sqrt{2}\delta\right)\\
&\quad+\frac{1}{2}\tilde{\mathcal{I}}_{h}[U^{n-1}]\left(x_{m}-f(u_{m}^{n})\Delta t+\sqrt{2}\delta\right),\end{aligned}\end{cases}

where
\delta=(\nu\,\Delta t)^{1/2}
,
u_{m}^{n}
denotes the approximation of
u(x_{m},t_{n})
,
U^{n}=(u_{1}^{n},\ldots,u_{N_{x}}^{n})
, and
\tilde{\mathcal{I}}_{h}[U^{n}]
is an interpolation of
U^{n}
that satisfies
\tilde{\mathcal{I}}_{h}[U^{n}](x_{m})=u_{m}^{n}
for all
m\in\{1,\ldots,N_{x}\}
. The simplest choice for the interpolation operator
\mathcal{I}_{h}
is the linear interpolation. To improve the accuracy, semi-Lagrangian schemes employing higher-order interpolation operators have been explored for several decades. For example, the use of cubic spline interpolation was proposed in
[
10
]
.

See also
[
3
]
for a detailed discussion of the major advantages of semi-Lagrangian methods. We recall some notable features of the scheme (
1.2
).

First, following the semi-Lagrangian approach, the solution at each grid point
x_{m}
is computed by tracing backward along the characteristic curve over the time interval
[t_{n-1},t_{n}]
.

Second, the scheme is implicit in time: the velocity
f(u)
used for backtracking is evaluated at the unknown value
u_{m}^{n}
. Although this implicit formulation increases the computational cost, it significantly improves the robustness of the method. Moreover, each nonlinear equation in the second line of (
1.2
) involves only a single scalar unknown
u_{m}^{n}
. Thanks to this locality, the scheme can be efficiently implemented using a simple Newton iteration, and it is also appropriate for parallel computation.

Third, the diffusive term is also treated in a semi-Lagrangian technique, and that is why this scheme is referred to as a fully semi-Lagrangian method. The derivation of this scheme is based on the Feynman–Kac formula. The idea of constructing deterministic numerical schemes for parabolic problems via stochastic representations is discussed in detail by
[
6
]
.

A mathematical analysis of the scheme was conducted in
[
4
]
, established the following results: (i) an estimate of the consistency error, (ii)
L^{\infty}
-stability under the assumption that the interpolation operator does not increase the
L^{\infty}
-norm, and (iii) one-sided Lipschitz stability under the condition that the interpolation operator does not increase the
W^{1,\infty}
-seminorm.

Schemes closely related to (
1.2
) have been already proposed in
[
7
,
8
,
6
]
. They share the same fundamental structure with (
1.2
), but the implicit step is replaced by an explicit one. For these explicit versions, error estimates for the fully discrete scheme are established in the case of linear interpolation. See also
[
7
]
for a similar implicit scheme applied to a broader class of PDEs with variable viscosity coefficients, as well as its
L^{\infty}
-error estimate for a semi-discrete version.

However, as noted in Remark 5.2 of
[
6
]
, a direct extension of their strategy to the case of higher-order spline interpolation is not straightforward. This difficulty is one of the main motivations for the present paper. In our study, we address the semi-Lagrangian methods with higher-order interpolation, including the spline interpolation of degree three and above. A crucial step in the analysis is to establish the stability of the interpolation operator with respect to both the standard
H^{s}
-norm and the weighted
H^{s}
-norm (defined in Subsection
2.3
) for some
s
.

When the cubic Hermite interpolation operator is used in the semi-Lagrangian scheme, the resulting method is known as the cubic interpolated pseudo-particle (CIP) scheme, which is originally proposed in
[
14
]
. Applications of the CIP scheme can be found in numerous works, including its use for the Korteweg–de Vries equations
[
17
]
, and the shallow water equations
[
16
]
.

However, mathematical analyses of CIP schemes remain limited. The stability of the CIP scheme for the linear conservation law with a constant velocity was investigated in
[
9
,
15
]
. As stated in
[
15
]
, it seems to be difficult to establish the
L^{2}
-stability of the CIP scheme. In our previous study
[
5
]
, we derived the
L^{2}
-error estimate of order
O(\Delta t^{3}+h^{4}/\Delta t)
. In the present paper, we demonstrate that the higher-order Hermite interpolation operator can be employed as
\mathcal{I}_{h}
, and that our error estimate also holds in this case.

This paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we reformulate the scheme (
2.2
), originally described in terms of grid functions, using a finite-dimensional subspace of
H^{s}(\mathbb{T})
. We also introduce some notation in this section. In Section
3
, we present the
H^{s}
- and
L^{2}
-error analyses, which are the main results of this paper. In Section
4
, we present some numerical results. In the appendix, we provide auxiliary lemmas used in the proofs in Section
3
.

2.
The Fully Semi-Lagrangian for Diffusive Conservation Laws Scheme and Preliminaries

2.1.
Reformulation of the Semi-Lagrangian Scheme

We recast the scheme (
1.2
), originally formulated in terms of grid functions, as a scheme based on functions on
\mathbb{T}
. For
v:\mathbb{T}\to\mathbb{R}
, the approximate characteristics
{X_{\Delta t}^{1}}[v]:\mathbb{T}\to\mathbb{T}
is defined by

(2.1)

\displaystyle{X_{\Delta t}^{1}}[v](x)=x-v(x)\Delta t.

Then we can rewrite (
1.2
) as

(2.2)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}u_{h}^{0}=\mathcal{I}_{h}u_{0},&\\
u_{h}^{n}=\mathcal{I}_{h}\left[\mathcal{S}_{\Delta t}^{\mathrm{V}}(\mathcal{S}_{\Delta t}^{\mathrm{A}}u_{h}^{n-1})\right]&(n=1,\ldots,N_{t}),\end{cases}

where the interpolation operator
\mathcal{I}_{h}:C(\mathbb{T})\to V_{h}^{2s-2,2s-1}
is defined by

(2.3)

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{h}v=\tilde{\mathcal{I}}_{h}[\left(v(x_{1}),\ldots,v(x_{N_{x}})\right)],

\mathcal{S}_{\Delta t}^{\mathrm{V}}:H^{s}(\mathbb{T})\to H^{s}(\mathbb{T})
is defined by

(2.4)

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{\Delta t}^{\mathrm{V}}(v)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(v\circ{X_{\Delta t}^{1}}[\tilde{\delta}]+v\circ{X_{\Delta t}^{1}}[-\tilde{\delta}]\right)

for
\tilde{\delta}=\sqrt{2}\delta/\Delta t
, and
\mathcal{S}_{\Delta t}^{\mathrm{A}}:H^{s}(\mathbb{T})\to H^{s}(\mathbb{T})
is implicitly defined by

(2.5)

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{\Delta t}^{\mathrm{A}}(v)

\displaystyle=v\circ{X_{\Delta t}^{1}}[f\circ\mathcal{S}_{\Delta t}^{\mathrm{A}}(v)].

We can deduce that the operator
\mathcal{S}_{\Delta t}^{\mathrm{A}}
is well-defined when
\Delta t
is sufficiently small from Lemmas
2.1
and
A.1
. The schemes (
1.2
) and (
2.2
) are equivalent in the sense that
\mathcal{I}_{h}[U^{n}]=u_{h}^{n}
.

In practice, we often use spline interpolation operator as
\mathcal{I}_{h}
. We denote each cell by
E_{m}=[x_{m},x_{m+1}]
. For
k
,
l\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
, we set the finite-dimensional function space

(2.6)

\displaystyle V_{h}^{k,l}\coloneqq\left\{v\in C^{k}(\mathbb{T})\mathrel{}\middle|\mathrel{}v|_{E_{m}}\in\mathbb{P}_{l}(E_{m}),\quad m=1,\ldots,N_{x}\right\},

where
\mathbb{P}_{l}(E_{m})
is the set of all polynomials of degree at most
l
on
E_{m}
. For
v\in C(\mathbb{T})
, the spline interpolation of degree
(2s-1)
, denoted by
\mathcal{I}_{h}^{\mathrm{S}}v
, is defined by
\mathcal{I}_{h}^{\mathrm{S}}v\in V_{h}^{2s-2,2s-1}
and
\mathcal{I}_{h}^{\mathrm{S}}v(x_{m})=v(x_{m})
. The condition on the spatial mesh ensuring the existence and uniqueness of the spline interpolation is discussed in Theorem 4 of
[
2
]
. In this paper, we assume that the interpolation exists uniquely.

2.2.
Semi-Lagrangian Scheme with Hermite Interpolation

In this subsection, we consider using the Hermite interpolation as the interpolation in the scheme (
2.2
). In the following, we see that the scheme is implementable with the Hermite interpolation. The
(2s-1)
-th degree Hermite interpolation operator
\mathcal{I}_{h}^{\mathrm{H}}:C^{s-1}(\mathbb{T})\to V_{h}^{s-1,2s-1}
is defined by

(2.7)

\displaystyle\partial_{x}^{k}(\mathcal{I}_{h}^{\mathrm{H}}v)(x_{j})=\partial_{x}^{k}v(x_{j})\quad(k=0,\ldots,s-1).

The resulting semi-Lagrangian scheme reads

(2.8)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}u_{h}^{0}=\mathcal{I}_{h}^{\mathrm{H}}u_{0},&\\
u_{h}^{n}=\mathcal{I}_{h}^{\mathrm{H}}\left[\mathcal{S}_{\Delta t}^{\mathrm{V}}(\mathcal{S}_{\Delta t}^{\mathrm{A}}u_{h}^{n-1})\right]&(n=1,\ldots,N_{t}).\end{cases}

To illustrate this concretely, let us consider the case
s=2
. Let
v_{m}^{n}
approximate
\partial_{x}u(x_{m},t_{n})
and
\tilde{U}^{n}
denote the vector
(u_{1}^{n},\ldots,u_{N_{x}}^{n},v_{1}^{n},\ldots,v_{N_{x}}^{n})
. We denote the cubic Hermite interpolation operator by
\tilde{\mathcal{I}}_{h}^{\mathrm{H3}}:\mathbb{R}^{2N_{x}}\to V_{h}^{1,3}
. In analogy to the expression of (
1.2
), we can rewrite the corresponding grid function scheme as

(2.9)

\displaystyle u_{m}^{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\tilde{\mathcal{I}}_{h}^{\mathrm{H3}}[\tilde{U}^{n-1}]\left(z_{m}^{n-}\right)+\frac{1}{2}\tilde{\mathcal{I}}_{h}^{\mathrm{H3}}[\tilde{U}^{n-1}]\left(z_{m}^{n+}\right),

(2.10)

\displaystyle v_{m}^{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left[\partial_{x}\tilde{\mathcal{I}}_{h}^{\mathrm{H3}}[\tilde{U}^{n}](z_{m}^{n-})+\partial_{x}\tilde{\mathcal{I}}_{h}^{\mathrm{H3}}[\tilde{U}^{n}](z_{m}^{n+})\right]

(2.11)

\displaystyle\quad\times\left[1+\frac{\Delta t}{2}f^{\prime}(u_{m}^{n})\left(\partial_{x}\tilde{\mathcal{I}}_{h}^{\mathrm{H3}}[\tilde{U}^{n-1}](z_{m}^{n-})+\partial_{x}\tilde{\mathcal{I}}_{h}^{\mathrm{H3}}[\tilde{U}^{n-1}](z_{m}^{n+})\right)\right]^{-1},

where
z_{m}^{n\pm}=x_{m}-f(u_{m}^{n})\Delta t\pm\sqrt{2}\delta
. The second equation in (
2.11
) is obtained by differentiating the second line of (
2.8
). As in the spline interpolation case, the implicit scheme (
2.8
) can also be implemented with Hermite interpolations of degree higher than the example above.

In the remainder of this paper, we assume that
\mathcal{I}_{h}:H^{s}(\mathbb{T})\to H^{s}(\mathbb{T})
is a general interpolation operator that satisfies the following three properties:

(P1)

The operator
\mathcal{I}_{h}
satisfies the integral relation

(2.12)

\displaystyle\left|(I-\mathcal{I}_{h})v-\mathcal{I}_{h}w\right|_{s,2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\left|(I-\mathcal{I}_{h})v\right|_{s,2}^{2}+\left|\mathcal{I}_{h}w\right|_{s,2}^{2}

for any
v,w\in H^{s}(\mathbb{T})
, where we denote the identity operator by
I
.

(P2)

There exists a constant
C_{s}>0
such that for any
v\in H^{s}(\mathbb{T})
,

(2.13)

\displaystyle\left\|(I-\mathcal{I}_{h})v\right\|_{0,2}

\displaystyle\leq C_{s}h^{s}\left|v\right|_{s,2}.

(P3)

For an integer
q>s
, there exists a constant
C_{q}>0
such that for any
v\in H^{q}(\mathbb{T})
,

(2.14)

\displaystyle\left|(I-\mathcal{I}_{h})v\right|_{s,2}

\displaystyle\leq C_{q}h^{q-s}\left|v\right|_{q,2}.

We remark that the spline interpolation of degree
(2s-1)
and the Hermite interpolation of degree
(2s-1)
satisfy (P
1
), (P
2
) and (P
3
) for
q=2s
. These three facts can be found in Theorems 4, 7 and 9 in
[
11
]
, respectively.

2.3.
Preliminaries

We introduce some notation. The norms of Sobolev spaces
L^{p}(\mathbb{T})
and
W^{s,p}(\mathbb{T})
are denoted by
\left\|\,\cdot\,\right\|_{0,p}
and
\left\|\,\cdot\,\right\|_{s,p}
, respectively. We also use the Sobolev seminorm
\left|\,\cdot\,\right|_{s,p}=\left\|\partial_{x}^{s}(\,\cdot\,)\right\|_{0,p}
. For a positive integer
s
, we define

(2.15)

\displaystyle\left\|v\right\|_{s,2,\ast}=\left(\left\|v\right\|_{0,2}^{2}+\left|v\right|_{s,2}^{2}\right)^{1/2},

which is a norm equivalent to the standard
H^{s}
-norm. We simply refer to
\left\|\,\cdot\,\right\|_{s,2,\ast}
as the
H^{s}
-norm, when there is no risk of confusion. We further define the norm
\left\|\,\cdot\,\right\|_{s,2,\Delta}
, which depends on the mesh size
h
and the time step
\Delta t
, by

(2.16)

\displaystyle\left\|v\right\|_{s,2,\Delta}=\left(\left\|v\right\|_{0,2}^{2}+\frac{h^{2s}}{\Delta t}\left|v\right|_{s,2}^{2}\right)^{1/2},

and refer to it as the weighted
H^{s}
-norm. For a Sobolev space
H^{r}(\mathbb{T})
, we write
W^{k,p}(H^{r})
to denote the Bochner space
W^{k,p}(0,T;H^{r}(\mathbb{T}))
.

We use a generic positive constant
C
that may change from line to line. In addition, we use
C_{\mathrm{P}}(A_{1},\ldots,A_{l})
to denote a generic positive constant that depends polynomially on
(A_{1},\ldots,A_{l})
. That is, there exists a polynomial
\Phi(A_{1},\ldots,A_{l})
such that

(2.17)

\displaystyle C_{\mathrm{P}}(A_{1},\ldots,A_{l})\leq\Phi(A_{1},\ldots,A_{l}).

The following Sobolev embedding inequality will be used frequently. There exists a positive constant
C
such that

(2.18)

\displaystyle\left\|\phi\right\|_{0,\infty}\leq C\left\|\phi\right\|_{1,2}.

2.4.
Well-definedness of
\mathcal{S}_{\Delta t}^{\mathrm{A}}

In the following lemma, we show that the operator
\mathcal{S}_{\Delta t}^{\mathrm{A}}
is well-defined by (
2.5
), provided that
\Delta t
is sufficiently small.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
v\in H^{s}(\mathbb{T})
and
f\in W^{s,\infty}(\mathbb{T})
for an integer
s\geq 2
. We define
\Delta t_{1}=\min\{(3\left|f\right|_{1,\infty}\left|v\right|_{1,\infty})^{-1},1\}
. If
\Delta t<\Delta t_{1}
, then there exists a unique
w\in H^{s}(\mathbb{T})
such that

(2.19)

\displaystyle w=v\circ{X_{\Delta t}^{1}}[f\circ w].

Furthermore,
{X_{\Delta t}^{1}}[f\circ w]
is a bijection.

Proof.

We define
G:\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}\to\mathbb{R}
by

(2.20)

\displaystyle G(\xi,x)=\xi-v(x-f(\xi)\Delta t).

It follows that

(2.21)

\displaystyle\partial_{\xi}G(\xi,x)=1-f^{\prime}(\xi)(\partial_{x}v)(x-f(\xi)\Delta t)\Delta t,

Suppose
\Delta t\in(0,\Delta t_{1})
. Then we have
\partial_{\xi}G\geq 2/3
.
By the implicit function theorem, for each
x\in\mathbb{T}
, there exists a unique
\xi(x)\in\mathbb{R}
such that
G(\xi(x),x)=0
.
This implies the unique existence of a function
w:\mathbb{T}\to\mathbb{R}
satisfying (
2.19
).

By differentiating (
2.19
), we have

(2.22)

\displaystyle\gamma\partial_{x}w=(\partial_{x}v)\circ{X_{\Delta t}^{1}}[f\circ w],

where

(2.23)

\displaystyle\gamma=1+\left((f^{\prime})\circ w\right)\left((\partial_{x}v)\circ{X_{\Delta t}^{1}}[f\circ w]\right)\Delta t.

Since it holds that
1/(1-a)\leq 1+(3/2)a
for
a\in(0,1/3)
, when
\Delta t<\Delta t_{1}
, we have

(2.24)

\displaystyle\left\|1/\gamma\right\|_{0,\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{1-\Delta t\left|f\right|_{1,\infty}\left|v\right|_{1,\infty}}

(2.25)

\displaystyle\leq 1+\frac{3}{2}\Delta t\left|f\right|_{1,\infty}\left|v\right|_{1,\infty}.

Taking the
L^{\infty}
-norm of (
2.22
), and applying (
2.25
), we obtain

(2.26)

\displaystyle\left|w\right|_{1,\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|1/\gamma\right\|_{0,\infty}\left\|\gamma\,\partial_{x}w\right\|_{0,\infty}

(2.27)

\displaystyle\leq\left(1+\frac{3}{2}\left|f\right|_{1,\infty}\left|v\right|_{1,\infty}\Delta t\right)\left|v\right|_{1,\infty}.

Combining with
3\left|f\right|_{1,\infty}\left|v\right|_{1,\infty}\Delta t<1
, we have

(2.28)

\displaystyle\left|w\right|_{1,\infty}\leq\frac{3}{2}\left|v\right|_{1,\infty}.

By (
2.28
) and
\Delta t\leq\Delta t_{1}
, we have
\Delta t\left|f\right|_{1,\infty}\left|w\right|_{1,\infty}\leq 1/2
. Combining with

(2.29)

\displaystyle\partial_{x}{X_{\Delta t}^{1}}[f\circ w]=1-(f^{\prime}\circ w)\partial_{x}w\Delta t,

we obtain

(2.30)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\leq\partial_{x}{X_{\Delta t}^{1}}[f\circ w](x)\leq\frac{3}{2}

for any
x\in\mathbb{T}
, which implies
{X_{\Delta t}^{1}}[f\circ w]:\mathbb{T}\to\mathbb{T}
is a bijection.
∎

3.
Main Results

3.1.
Consistency

In this section, we present the
L^{2}
-error estimate for the scheme (
2.2
). To this end, we first establish the
H^{s}
-error estimate. We begin by investigating the consistency of the scheme in the
L^{2}
-norm and the
H^{s}
-seminorm.

We denote the exact solution
u(t_{n})
by
u^{n}
. We define the truncation error function
\tau_{h}^{n}:\mathbb{T}\to\mathbb{R}
(
n=1,\ldots,N_{t}
) by

(3.1)

\displaystyle\tau_{h}^{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Delta t}\left\{u^{n}-(\mathcal{S}_{\Delta t}^{\mathrm{V}}u^{n-1})\circ{X_{\Delta t}^{1}}[f\circ u^{n}]\right\}

(3.2)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Delta t}\left\{u^{n}-\frac{1}{2}(u^{n-1}\circ{X_{\Delta t}^{1}}[f\circ u^{n}-\tilde{\delta}]+u^{n-1}\circ{X_{\Delta t}^{1}}[f\circ u^{n}+\tilde{\delta}])\right\},

where the interpolation error is deliberately excluded from the definition. We will show that
\tau_{h}^{n}
is of order
O(\Delta t)
under suitable regularity assumptions.

Suppose that
u\in W^{2,\infty}(0,T;H^{s}(\mathbb{T}))\cap L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{s+4}(\mathbb{T}))
. We expand
u^{n-1}\circ{X_{\Delta t}^{1}}[u^{n}\pm\tilde{\delta}]
by Taylor’s theorem and apply the first equality of (
1.1
) to obtain

(3.3)

\displaystyle\tau_{h}^{n}

\displaystyle=\tau_{h1}^{n}+\tau_{h2}^{n}+\tau_{h3}^{n}+\tau_{h4+}^{n}+\tau_{h4-}^{n},

where

(3.4)

\displaystyle\tau_{h1}^{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Delta t}\left(u^{n-1}-u^{n}\right)+\partial_{t}u^{n-1},

(3.5)

\displaystyle\tau_{h2}^{n}

\displaystyle=u^{n-1}\partial_{x}u^{n-1}-u^{n}\partial_{x}u^{n-1},

(3.6)

\displaystyle\tau_{h3}^{n}

\displaystyle=\Delta t\left\{\frac{1}{2}f(u^{n})^{2}\partial_{x}^{2}u^{n-1}-\left(\frac{1}{3}\Delta tf(u^{n})^{3}+6\nu f(u^{n})\right)\partial_{x}^{3}u^{n-1}\right\}

and

(3.7)

\displaystyle\tau_{h4\pm}^{n}(x)

\displaystyle=-\frac{\Delta t}{6}\int_{0}^{f(u^{n}(x))\sqrt{\Delta t}\mp\sqrt{2\nu}}\partial_{x}^{4}u^{n-1}(x-\sqrt{\Delta t}z)z^{3}\,\mathrm{d}z.

Each term on the right-hand side of (
3.3
) will be estimated below. By Taylor’s theorem, we have

(3.8)

\displaystyle\tau_{h1}^{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Delta t}\int_{0}^{\Delta t}t^{\prime}\partial_{t}^{2}u(t^{n-1}+t^{\prime})\,\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}.

Therefore, we obtain

(3.9)

\displaystyle\left\|\tau_{h1}^{n}\right\|_{0,2}

\displaystyle\leq C\Delta t\left\|u\right\|_{W^{2,\infty}(t_{n-1},t_{n};L^{2}(\mathbb{T}))},

and

(3.10)

\displaystyle\left|\tau_{h1}^{n}\right|_{s,2}

\displaystyle\leq C\Delta t\left\|u\right\|_{W^{2,\infty}(t_{n-1},t_{n};H^{s}(\mathbb{T}))}.

By the fundamental theorem of calculus, we have

(3.11)

\displaystyle\tau_{h2}^{n}

\displaystyle=-\partial_{x}u^{n-1}\int_{0}^{\Delta t}\partial_{t}u(t^{n-1}+t^{\prime})\,\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}.

Thus, we obtain

(3.12)

\displaystyle\left\|\tau_{h2}^{n}\right\|_{0,2}

\displaystyle\leq C\Delta t\left\|u^{n-1}\right\|_{1,\infty}\left\|u\right\|_{W^{1,\infty}(t_{n-1},t_{n};L^{2}(\mathbb{T}))}

and

(3.13)

\displaystyle\left|\tau_{h2}^{n}\right|_{s,2}

\displaystyle\leq C\Delta t\left\|u^{n-1}\right\|_{s+1,\infty}\left\|u\right\|_{W^{1,\infty}(t_{n-1},t_{n};H^{s}(\mathbb{T}))}.

We can estimate
\tau_{h3}^{n}
as

(3.14)

\displaystyle\left\|\tau_{h3}^{n}\right\|_{0,2}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\mathrm{P}}(\left\|f\right\|_{0,\infty},\left\|u^{n-1}\right\|_{3,2})\Delta t

and

(3.15)

\displaystyle\left|\tau_{h3}^{n}\right|_{s,2}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\mathrm{P}}(\left\|f\right\|_{s,\infty},\left\|u^{n-1}\right\|_{s+3,2})\Delta t.

Finally, we estimate
\tau_{h4\pm}^{n}
. Let

(3.16)

\displaystyle\theta_{\pm}(x)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}|x|^{3}&(x_{\mathrm{min}}^{\pm}\leq x\leq x_{\mathrm{max}}^{\pm}),\\
0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

where
x_{\mathrm{min}}^{\pm}=\min\{-\left\|f\right\|_{0,\infty}\sqrt{\Delta t}\mp\sqrt{2\nu},0\}
and
x_{\mathrm{max}}^{\pm}=\max\{\left\|f\right\|_{0,\infty}\sqrt{\Delta t}\mp\sqrt{2\nu},0\}
.
Using Young’s inequality and the bound
\left\|\theta_{\pm}\right\|_{0,1}\leq C
, we have

(3.17)

\displaystyle\left\|\tau_{h4\pm}^{n}\right\|_{0,2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\Delta t}{6}\left|u^{n-1}\right|_{4,2}\left\|\theta_{\pm}\right\|_{0,1}\leq C\Delta t\left|u^{n-1}\right|_{4,2}.

From a direct calculation, we also have

(3.18)

\displaystyle\left|\tau_{h4\pm}^{n}\right|_{s,2}

\displaystyle\leq C\Delta t\left\|\partial_{x}^{4}u^{n-1}\right\|_{s,2}.

By applying (
3.9
), (
3.12
), (
3.14
) and (
3.17
) to (
3.3
), we conclude that there exists a positive constant
C_{\tau,0}
depending on
\left\|u\right\|_{W^{2,\infty}(L^{2})}
and
\left\|u\right\|_{L^{\infty}(H^{4})}
such that

(3.19)

\displaystyle\left\|\tau_{h}^{n}\right\|_{0,2}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\tau,0}\Delta t.

Similarly, by applying (
3.10
), (
3.13
), (
3.15
) and (
3.18
) to (
3.3
), we obtain a positive constant
C_{\tau,s}
depending on
\left\|u\right\|_{W^{2,\infty}(H^{s})}
and
\left\|u\right\|_{L^{\infty}(H^{s+4})}
such that

(3.20)

\displaystyle\left|\tau_{h}^{n}\right|_{s,2}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\tau,s}\Delta t.

3.2.
H^{s}
-Stability

A single step of the scheme (
2.2
) is carried out by applying the operators
\mathcal{S}_{\Delta t}^{\mathrm{V}}
,
\mathcal{S}_{\Delta t}^{\mathrm{A}}
, and
\mathcal{I}_{h}
in sequence. In this subsection, we investigate the stability of the operators
\mathcal{S}_{\Delta t}^{\mathrm{V}}
and
\mathcal{I}_{h}
, which are the linear operators among these three. The stability of
\mathcal{S}_{\Delta t}^{\mathrm{A}}
will be addressed later in the proof of Theorem
3.2
.

First, we consider the stability of
\mathcal{S}_{\Delta t}^{\mathrm{V}}
. Since
X_{\Delta t}^{1}[\pm\tilde{\delta}]
is simply a translation, we have

(3.21)

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{S}_{\Delta t}^{\mathrm{V}}v\right\|_{s,2,\ast}\leq\frac{1}{2}\left(\left\|v\circ{X_{\Delta t}^{1}}[-\tilde{\delta}]\right\|_{s,2,\ast}+\left\|v\circ{X_{\Delta t}^{1}}[+\tilde{\delta}]\right\|_{s,2,\ast}\right)=\left\|v\right\|_{s,2,\ast}

and

(3.22)

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{S}_{\Delta t}^{\mathrm{V}}v\right\|_{s+1,\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\left(\left\|v\circ{X_{\Delta t}^{1}}[-\tilde{\delta}]\right\|_{s+1,\infty}+\left\|v\circ{X_{\Delta t}^{1}}[+\tilde{\delta}]\right\|_{s+1,\infty}\right)

(3.23)

\displaystyle=\left\|v\right\|_{s+1,\infty}.

Next, we address the stability of the interpolation operator
\mathcal{I}_{h}
with respect to the
H^{s}
-norm.

Lemma 3.1
.

Suppose that the interpolation operator
\mathcal{I}_{h}
possesses the properties (P
1
) and (P
2
) for some integer
s\leq 2
. Furthermore, suppose that there exists a positive constant
C_{\Delta 1}
such that
h^{2s}/\Delta t\leq C_{\Delta 1}
. Let
C_{I}=(C_{s})^{2}C_{\Delta 1}
, where
C_{s}
is the constant introduced in (P
2
). Then we have for any
v\in H^{s}(\mathbb{T})
and
(h,\Delta t)\in(0,1)^{2}

(3.24)

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{I}_{h}v\right\|_{s,2,*}

\displaystyle\leq\left(1+C_{I}\Delta t\right)\left\|v\right\|_{s,2,*}.

Proof.

It is straightforward to check that, for any
a,b\geq 0
and
d>0
,

(3.25)

\displaystyle(a+b)^{2}\leq(1+d)a^{2}+(1+1/d)b^{2}.

This yields

(3.26)

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{I}_{h}v\right\|_{s,2,*}^{2}

(3.27)

\displaystyle=\left\|v+(\mathcal{I}_{h}-I)v\right\|_{0,2}^{2}+\left|\mathcal{I}_{h}v\right|_{s,2}^{2}

(3.28)

\displaystyle\leq(1+(C_{s})^{2}C_{\Delta 1}\Delta t)\left\|v\right\|_{0,2}^{2}

(3.29)

\displaystyle\quad+\left(1+\frac{1}{(C_{s})^{2}C_{\Delta 1}\Delta t}\right)\left\|(\mathcal{I}_{h}-I)v\right\|_{0,2}^{2}+\left|\mathcal{I}_{h}v\right|_{s,2}^{2}.

For
\phi\in H^{s}(\mathbb{T})
, substituting
v=(I-\mathcal{I}_{h})w
in (
2.13
), we obtain

(3.30)

\displaystyle\left\|(I-\mathcal{I}_{h})w\right\|_{0,2}\leq C_{s}h^{s}\left|(I-\mathcal{I}_{h})w\right|_{s,2}.

Using this, (
3.29
) becomes

(3.31)

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{I}_{h}v\right\|_{s,2,*}^{2}

(3.32)

\displaystyle\leq(1+(C_{s})^{2}C_{\Delta 1}\Delta t)\left\|v\right\|_{0,2}^{2}+\frac{1+(C_{s})^{2}C_{\Delta 1}\Delta t}{C_{\Delta 1}}\frac{h^{2s}}{\Delta t}\left|(I-\mathcal{I}_{h})v\right|_{s,2}^{2}+\left|\mathcal{I}_{h}v\right|_{s,2}^{2}.

Under the assumption
h^{2s}/\Delta t\leq C_{\Delta 1}
, we have

(3.33)

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{I}_{h}v\right\|_{s,2,*}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq(1+(C_{s})^{2}C_{\Delta 1}\Delta t)\left\|v\right\|_{0,2}^{2}

(3.34)

\displaystyle\quad+(1+(C_{s})^{2}C_{\Delta 1}\Delta t)\left|(\mathcal{I}_{h}-I)v\right|_{s,2}^{2}+\left|\mathcal{I}_{h}v\right|_{s,2}^{2}.

By (P
1
), we have

(3.35)

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{I}_{h}v\right\|_{s,2,*}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq(1+C_{I}\Delta t)\left(\left\|v\right\|_{0,2}^{2}+\left|(I-\mathcal{I}_{h})v\right|_{s,2}^{2}+\left|\mathcal{I}_{h}v\right|_{s,2}^{2}\right)

(3.36)

\displaystyle=(1+C_{I}\Delta t)\left(\left\|v\right\|_{0,2}^{2}+\left|v\right|_{s,2}^{2}\right)

(3.37)

\displaystyle=(1+C_{I}\Delta t)\left\|v\right\|_{s,2,*}^{2},

which concludes the proof.
∎

3.3.
H^{s}
Convergence

Before establishing the
L^{2}
-error estimate, it is necessary to derive an error bound in the
H^{s}
-norm. Although the
L^{2}
convergence rate obtained directly from the following theorem is not as sharp as the one that will be proved in the next subsection, the
H^{s}
boundedness of the numerical solution is essential for the
L^{2}
analysis.

Theorem 3.2
.

Suppose that the interpolation operator
\mathcal{I}_{h}
satisfies (P
1
), (P
2
) and (P
3
) for positive integers
s
and
q
. Furthermore, assume that there exist positive constants
C_{\Delta 2}
and
\varepsilon
such that

(3.38)

\displaystyle h^{2s}/\Delta t\leq C_{\Delta 2}\quad\text{and}\quad h^{2(q-s)}/\Delta t^{1+\varepsilon}\leq C_{\Delta 2}.

Then there exist
(h_{0},\Delta t_{0})\in(0,1)^{2}
and
C_{e}>0
which depends on
\left\|u\right\|_{W^{1,\infty}(H^{s+2})}
,
\left\|u\right\|_{L^{\infty}(H^{s+4})}
and
\left\|u\right\|_{L^{\infty}(H^{q})}
such that, if
(h,\Delta t)\in(0,h_{0})\times(0,\Delta t_{0})
, the following estimate holds:

(3.39)

\displaystyle\left\|u^{n}-u_{h}^{n}\right\|_{s,2,\ast}\leq C_{e}\left(\Delta t+\frac{h^{q-s}}{\Delta t^{1/2}}+\frac{h^{q}}{\Delta t}\right)

for all
n\in\{0,\ldots,N_{t}\}
.

Proof.

In this proof, we do not explicitly indicate the dependence of the generic constant
C_{\mathrm{P}}
on
f
.

Let
e_{h}^{n}=u^{n}-u_{h}^{n}
. For
n=0
, we have

(3.40)

\displaystyle\left\|e_{h}^{0}\right\|_{s,2,\ast}=\left(\left\|(I-\mathcal{I}_{h})u^{0}\right\|_{0,2}^{2}+\left|(I-\mathcal{I}_{h})u^{0}\right|_{s,2}^{2}\right)^{1/2}.

By (P
2
) and (P
3
), there exists a positive constant
C_{0}
depending on
\left|u\right|_{q,2}
such that

(3.41)

\displaystyle\left\|e_{h}^{0}\right\|_{s,2,\ast}\leq C_{0}h^{q-s}.

For
n\geq 2
, by the second equality of (
2.2
), we have

(3.42)

\displaystyle e_{h}^{n}

\displaystyle=u^{n}-\mathcal{I}_{h}(\mathcal{S}_{\Delta t}^{\mathrm{A}}(\mathcal{S}_{\Delta t}^{\mathrm{V}}u_{h}^{n-1}))

(3.43)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{I}_{h}(u^{n}-\mathcal{S}_{\Delta t}^{\mathrm{A}}(\mathcal{S}_{\Delta t}^{\mathrm{V}}u_{h}^{n-1}))+(I-\mathcal{I}_{h})u^{n}.

By (
3.2
), we can decompose the first term in the right-hand side of (
3.43
) as

(3.44)

\displaystyle u^{n}-\mathcal{S}_{\Delta t}^{\mathrm{A}}(\mathcal{S}_{\Delta t}^{\mathrm{V}}u_{h}^{n-1})=\eta_{h}^{n}+\theta_{h}^{n}+\Delta t\,\tau_{h}^{n},

where

(3.45)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \eta_{h}^{n}&=(\mathcal{S}_{\Delta t}^{\mathrm{V}}u^{n-1})\circ{X_{\Delta t}^{1}}[f\circ\mathcal{S}_{\Delta t}^{\mathrm{A}}(\mathcal{S}_{\Delta t}^{\mathrm{V}}u_{h}^{n-1})]-\mathcal{S}_{\Delta t}^{\mathrm{A}}(\mathcal{S}_{\Delta t}^{\mathrm{V}}u_{h}^{n-1}),\end{aligned}

(3.46)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \theta_{h}^{n}&=(\mathcal{S}_{\Delta t}^{\mathrm{V}}u^{n-1})\circ{X_{\Delta t}^{1}}[f\circ u^{n}]-(\mathcal{S}_{\Delta t}^{\mathrm{V}}u^{n-1})\circ{X_{\Delta t}^{1}}[f\circ\mathcal{S}_{\Delta t}^{\mathrm{A}}(\mathcal{S}_{\Delta t}^{\mathrm{V}}u_{h}^{n-1})],\end{aligned}

and
\tau_{h}^{n}
is defined by (
3.2
). We can rewrite
\eta_{h}^{n}
as

(3.47)

\displaystyle\eta_{h}^{n}

\displaystyle=(\mathcal{S}_{\Delta t}^{\mathrm{V}}u^{n-1})\circ{X_{\Delta t}^{1}}[f\circ\mathcal{S}_{\Delta t}^{\mathrm{A}}(\mathcal{S}_{\Delta t}^{\mathrm{V}}u_{h}^{n-1})]

(3.48)

\displaystyle\quad-(\mathcal{S}_{\Delta t}^{\mathrm{V}}u_{h}^{n-1})\circ{X_{\Delta t}^{1}}[f\circ\mathcal{S}_{\Delta t}^{\mathrm{A}}(\mathcal{S}_{\Delta t}^{\mathrm{V}}u_{h}^{n-1})]

(3.49)

\displaystyle=(\mathcal{S}_{\Delta t}^{\mathrm{V}}e_{h}^{n-1})\circ{X_{\Delta t}^{1}}[f\circ\mathcal{S}_{\Delta t}^{\mathrm{A}}(\mathcal{S}_{\Delta t}^{\mathrm{V}}u_{h}^{n-1})].

Let
\rho_{h}^{n}=(I-\mathcal{I}_{h})u^{n}
. Then (
3.43
) becomes

(3.50)

\displaystyle e_{h}^{n}=\mathcal{I}_{h}(\eta_{h}^{n}+\theta_{h}^{n}+\Delta t\tau_{h}^{n})+\rho_{h}^{n}.

By (
1
), we have

(3.51)

\displaystyle\left|e_{h}^{n}\right|_{s,2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\left|\mathcal{I}_{h}(\eta_{h}^{n}+\theta_{h}^{n}+\Delta t\tau_{h}^{n})\right|_{s,2}^{2}+\left|\rho_{h}^{n}\right|_{s,2}^{2}.

By an elementary inequality

(3.52)

\displaystyle(a_{1}+a_{2}+a_{3})^{2}\leq(1+\Delta t)a_{1}^{2}+\frac{4}{\Delta t}(a_{2}^{2}+a_{3}^{2})

for
a_{1},a_{2},a_{3}\in\mathbb{R}
and
\Delta t\in(0,1)
, (
3.51
) becomes

(3.53)

\displaystyle\left|e_{h}^{n}\right|_{s,2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\left(1+\Delta t\right)\left|\mathcal{I}_{h}\eta_{h}^{n}\right|_{s,2}^{2}+\frac{4}{\Delta t}\left(\left|\mathcal{I}_{h}\theta_{h}^{n}\right|_{s,2}^{2}+\left|\Delta t\mathcal{I}_{h}\tau_{h}^{n}\right|_{s,2}^{2}\right)+\left|\rho_{h}^{n}\right|_{s,2}^{2}.

By (
3.50
) and the inequality

(3.54)

\displaystyle(a_{1}+a_{2}+a_{3}+a_{4})^{2}\leq(1+\Delta t)a_{1}^{2}+\frac{6}{\Delta t}(a_{2}^{2}+a_{3}^{2}+a_{4}^{2})

for
a_{1},a_{2},a_{3},a_{4}\in\mathbb{R}
and
\Delta t\in(0,1)
, we have

(3.55)

\displaystyle\left\|e_{h}^{n}\right\|_{0,2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq(1+\Delta t)\left\|\mathcal{I}_{h}\eta_{h}^{n}\right\|_{0,2}^{2}

(3.56)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{6}{\Delta t}\left(\left\|\mathcal{I}_{h}\theta_{h}^{n}\right\|_{0,2}^{2}+\left\|\Delta t\mathcal{I}_{h}\tau_{h}^{n}\right\|_{0,2}^{2}+\left\|\mathcal{I}_{h}\rho_{h}^{n}\right\|_{0,2}^{2}\right).

Combining (
3.53
) and (
3.56
), we obtain

(3.57)

\displaystyle\left\|e_{h}^{n}\right\|_{s,2,\ast}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq(1+\Delta t)\left\|\mathcal{I}_{h}\eta_{h}^{n}\right\|_{s,2,\ast}^{2}

(3.58)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{6}{\Delta t}\left(\left\|\mathcal{I}_{h}\theta_{h}^{n}\right\|_{s,2,\ast}^{2}+\left\|\Delta t\mathcal{I}_{h}\tau_{h}^{n}\right\|_{s,2,\ast}^{2}+\left\|\rho_{h}^{n}\right\|_{0,2}^{2}\right)+\left|\rho_{h}^{n}\right|_{s,2}^{2}.

By Lemma
3.1
, we have

(3.59)

\displaystyle\left\|e_{h}^{n}\right\|_{s,2,\ast}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq(1+C\Delta t)\left\|\eta_{h}^{n}\right\|_{s,2,\ast}^{2}+\frac{6}{\Delta t}\left\|\theta_{h}^{n}\right\|_{s,2,\ast}^{2}

(3.60)

\displaystyle\quad+6\Delta t\left\|\tau_{h}^{n}\right\|_{s,2,\ast}^{2}+\frac{6}{\Delta t}\left\|\rho_{h}^{n}\right\|_{0,2}^{2}+\left|\rho_{h}^{n}\right|_{s,2}^{2}.

We estimate
\left\|\eta_{h}^{n}\right\|_{s,2,\ast}^{2}
. By Lemma
A.1
and (
3.21
), we have

(3.61)

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{S}_{\Delta t}^{\mathrm{A}}(\mathcal{S}_{\Delta t}^{\mathrm{V}}u_{h}^{n-1})\right\|_{2,s,\ast}

\displaystyle\leq\left(1+C_{\mathrm{P}}(\left\|\mathcal{S}_{\Delta t}^{\mathrm{V}}u_{h}^{n-1}\right\|_{s,2,\ast})\Delta t\right)\left\|\mathcal{S}_{\Delta t}^{\mathrm{V}}u_{h}^{n-1}\right\|_{s,2,\ast}

(3.62)

\displaystyle\leq C_{\mathrm{P}}(\left\|u_{h}^{n-1}\right\|_{s,2,\ast}),

where we used
\Delta t\leq 1
. By Lemmas
A.3
and
A.2
, we have

(3.63)

\displaystyle\left\|\eta_{h}^{n}\right\|_{s,2,\ast}

\displaystyle=\left\|(\mathcal{S}_{\Delta t}^{\mathrm{V}}e_{h}^{n-1})\circ{X_{\Delta t}^{1}}[f\circ\mathcal{S}_{\Delta t}^{\mathrm{A}}(\mathcal{S}_{\Delta t}^{\mathrm{V}}u_{h}^{n-1})]\right\|_{s,2,\ast}

(3.64)

\displaystyle\leq\left(1+C_{\mathrm{P}}(\left\|f\circ(\mathcal{S}_{\Delta t}^{\mathrm{A}}\mathcal{S}_{\Delta t}^{\mathrm{V}}u_{h}^{n-1})\right\|_{s,2,\ast})\Delta t\right)\left\|\mathcal{S}_{\Delta t}^{\mathrm{V}}e_{h}^{n-1}\right\|_{s,2,\ast}.

Using (
3.21
) and (
3.62
), we have

(3.65)

\displaystyle\left\|\eta_{h}^{n}\right\|_{s,2,\ast}

\displaystyle\leq\left(1+C_{\mathrm{P}}(\left\|u_{h}^{n-1}\right\|_{s,2,\ast})\Delta t\right)\left\|e_{h}^{n-1}\right\|_{s,2,\ast}.

Second, we estimate
\left\|\tau_{h}^{n}\right\|_{s,2,\ast}
. By (
3.19
) and (
3.20
), we have

(3.66)

\displaystyle\left\|\tau_{h}^{n}\right\|_{s,2,\ast}\leq C_{\mathrm{P}}(\left\|u\right\|_{W^{2,\infty}(H^{s})},\left\|u\right\|_{L^{\infty}(H^{s+4})})\Delta t.

Third, we estimate
\frac{6}{\Delta t}\left\|\rho_{h}^{n}\right\|_{0,2}^{2}+\left|\rho_{h}^{n}\right|_{s,2}^{2}
.
By (P
2
) and (P
3
), we have

(3.67)

\displaystyle\frac{6}{\Delta t}\left\|\rho_{h}^{n}\right\|_{0,2}^{2}+\left|\rho_{h}^{n}\right|_{s,2}^{2}\leq C\left(\frac{h^{2q}}{\Delta t}+h^{2(q-s)}\right)\left|u\right|_{q,2}^{2}.

Now we estimate
\left\|\theta_{h}^{n}\right\|_{s,2,\ast}
. By (
3.46
) and (
A.116
), we have

(3.68)

\displaystyle\left\|\theta_{h}^{n}\right\|_{s,2,\ast}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\mathrm{P}}(\left\|\mathcal{S}_{\Delta t}^{\mathrm{V}}u^{n-1}\right\|_{s+1,\infty},\left\|u^{n}\right\|_{s,2},\left\|\mathcal{S}_{\Delta t}^{\mathrm{A}}(\mathcal{S}_{\Delta t}^{\mathrm{V}}u_{h}^{n-1})\right\|_{s,2})

(3.69)

\displaystyle\qquad\cdot\Delta t\left\|u^{n}-\mathcal{S}_{\Delta t}^{\mathrm{A}}(\mathcal{S}_{\Delta t}^{\mathrm{V}}u_{h}^{n-1})\right\|_{s,2,\ast}.

By (
3.62
) and (
3.23
), this becomes

(3.70)

\displaystyle\left\|\theta_{h}^{n}\right\|_{s,2,\ast}

(3.71)

\displaystyle\leq C_{\mathrm{P}}(\left\|u\right\|_{L^{\infty}(W^{s+1,\infty})},\left\|u_{h}^{n-1}\right\|_{s,2})\Delta t\left\|u^{n}-\mathcal{S}_{\Delta t}^{\mathrm{A}}(\mathcal{S}_{\Delta t}^{\mathrm{V}}u_{h}^{n-1})\right\|_{s,2,\ast}.

By (
3.44
) and (
3.71
), there exists a constant
C_{\mathrm{P}1}
which depends polynomially on
\left\|u\right\|_{L^{\infty}(W^{s+1,\infty})}
and
\left\|u_{h}^{n-1}\right\|_{s,2,\ast}
such that

(3.72)

\displaystyle\left\|\theta_{h}^{n}\right\|_{s,2,\ast}\leq C_{\mathrm{P}1}\Delta t\left(\left\|\rho_{h}^{n}\right\|_{s,2,\ast}+\left\|\theta_{h}^{n}\right\|_{s,2,\ast}+\Delta t\left\|\tau_{h}^{n}\right\|_{s,2,\ast}\right).

Let
\Delta t_{2}=\min\{1/(2C_{\mathrm{P}1}),1\}
and suppose
\Delta t\leq\Delta t_{2}
.
Then we have
1/(1-C_{\mathrm{P}1}\Delta t)\leq 1+2C_{\mathrm{P}1}\Delta t
.
Therefore, (
3.72
) becomes

(3.73)

\displaystyle\left\|\theta_{h}^{n}\right\|_{s,2,\ast}\leq C_{\mathrm{P}1}\Delta t(1+2C_{\mathrm{P}1}\Delta t)\left(\left\|\eta_{h}^{n}\right\|_{s,2,\ast}+\Delta t\left\|\tau_{h}^{n}\right\|_{s,2,\ast}\right).

Applying (
3.65
), (
3.67
) and (
3.73
) to (
3.60
), we obtain

(3.74)

\displaystyle\left\|e_{h}^{n}\right\|_{s,2,\ast}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(1+C_{\mathrm{P}}(\left\|u\right\|_{L^{\infty}(W^{s+1,\infty})},\left\|u_{h}^{n-1}\right\|_{s,2,\ast})\Delta t\right)\left\|e_{h}^{n-1}\right\|_{s,2,\ast}^{2}

(3.75)

\displaystyle\quad+C_{\mathrm{P}}(\left\|u\right\|_{L^{\infty}(W^{s+1,\infty})},\left\|u_{h}^{n-1}\right\|_{s,2,\ast})\Delta t\left\|\tau_{h}^{n}\right\|_{s,2,\ast}^{2}

(3.76)

\displaystyle\quad+C\left(\frac{h^{2q}}{\Delta t}+h^{2(q-s)}\right)\left|u\right|_{q,2}^{2}.

By (
3.38
), we estimate the last term as

(3.77)

\displaystyle\left(\frac{h^{2q}}{\Delta t}+h^{2(q-s)}\right)\left|u\right|_{q,2}^{2}\leq(C_{\Delta 2}^{2}+C_{\Delta 2})\Delta t^{1+\varepsilon}\left|u\right|_{q,2}^{2}.

Applying (
3.66
) and (
3.77
) to (
3.76
), we obtain

(3.78)

\displaystyle\left\|e_{h}^{n}\right\|_{s,2,\ast}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(1+C_{\mathrm{P}}(\left\|u\right\|_{L^{\infty}(W^{s+1,\infty})},\left\|u_{h}^{n-1}\right\|_{s,2,\ast})\Delta t\right)\left\|e_{h}^{n-1}\right\|_{s,2,\ast}^{2}

(3.79)

\displaystyle\quad+C_{\mathrm{P}}(\left\|u\right\|_{W^{2,\infty}(H^{s})},\left\|u\right\|_{L^{\infty}(H^{s+4})},\left\|u_{h}^{n-1}\right\|_{s,2,\ast})\Delta t^{3}

(3.80)

\displaystyle\quad+C\Delta t^{1+\varepsilon}\left|u\right|_{q,2}^{2}.

Thus, there exist positive constants
\alpha_{1}
depending on
\left\|u\right\|_{L^{\infty}(W^{s+1,\infty})}
and
\beta_{1}
depending on
\left\|u\right\|_{W^{2,\infty}(H^{s})}
,
\left\|u\right\|_{L^{\infty}(H^{s+4})}
and
\left\|u\right\|_{L^{\infty}(H^{q})}
such that, if
\left\|u_{h}^{n-1}\right\|_{s,2,\ast}\leq 2\sup_{t\in(0,T)}\left\|u(t)\right\|_{s,2,\ast}
, then

(3.81)

\displaystyle\left\|e_{h}^{n}\right\|_{s,2,\ast}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(1+\alpha_{1}\Delta t\right)\left\|e_{h}^{n-1}\right\|_{s,2,\ast}^{2}+\beta_{1}\Delta t^{1+\varepsilon}.

Let positive constants
\Delta t_{3}
and
h_{2}
satisfy

(3.82)

\displaystyle\beta_{1}T\mathrm{e}^{\alpha_{1}T}(\Delta t_{3})^{\varepsilon}\leq\frac{1}{2}\sup_{t\in(0,T)}\left\|u(t)\right\|_{s,2,\ast}

and

(3.83)

\displaystyle C_{0}\mathrm{e}^{\alpha_{1}T}(h_{0})^{2(q-s)}\leq\frac{1}{2}\sup_{t\in(0,T)}\left\|u(t)\right\|_{s,2,\ast},

where
C_{0}
is the constant introduced in (
3.41
).

Let
\Delta t_{0}=\min\{\Delta t_{1},\Delta t_{2},\Delta t_{3},1\}
and
h_{0}=\min\{h_{2},1\}
. In the following, suppose that
(\Delta t,h)\in(0,\Delta t_{0})\times(0,h_{0})
. We will show

(3.84)

\displaystyle\left\|u_{h}^{n}\right\|_{s,2,\ast}\leq 2\sup_{t\in(0,T)}\left\|u(t)\right\|_{s,2,\ast}

for
n\in\{0,\ldots,N_{t}\}
by induction.

By (
3.41
) and (
3.83
), we can easily check (
3.84
) for
n=0
as

(3.85)

\displaystyle\left\|u_{h}^{0}\right\|_{s,2,\ast}\leq\left\|u^{0}\right\|_{s,2,\ast}+\left\|e_{h}^{0}\right\|_{s,2,\ast}\leq\left\|u^{0}\right\|_{s,2,\ast}+C_{0}h^{2(q-s)}\leq\frac{3}{2}\sup_{t\in(0,T)}\left\|u(t)\right\|_{s,2,\ast}.

Suppose that, for some
k\in\{0,\ldots,N_{t}-1\}
, (
3.84
) holds for
n=0,\ldots,k
.
Then by (
3.81
), we have

(3.86)

\displaystyle\left\|e_{h}^{n+1}\right\|_{s,2,\ast}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(1+\alpha_{1}\Delta t\right)\left\|e_{h}^{n}\right\|_{s,2,\ast}^{2}+\beta_{1}\Delta t^{1+\varepsilon}

for all
n=0,\ldots,k
. From this, it follows that

(3.87)

\displaystyle\left\|e_{h}^{k+1}\right\|_{s,2,\ast}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(1+\alpha_{1}\Delta t\right)^{k+1}\left\|e_{h}^{0}\right\|_{s,2,\ast}^{2}+\sum_{n=0}^{k}\left(1+\alpha_{1}\Delta t\right)^{n}\beta_{1}\Delta t^{1+\varepsilon}.

Applying (
3.41
) and the inequality

(3.88)

\displaystyle\left(1+\alpha_{1}\Delta t\right)^{n}\leq\left(1+\alpha_{1}\Delta t\right)^{T/\Delta t}\leq\mathrm{e}^{\alpha_{1}T}\quad(0\leq n\leq N_{t})

to (
3.87
), we obtain

(3.89)

\displaystyle\left\|e_{h}^{k+1}\right\|_{s,2,\ast}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C_{0}\mathrm{e}^{\alpha_{1}T}h^{2(q-s)}+\beta_{1}T\mathrm{e}^{\alpha_{1}T}\Delta t^{\varepsilon}.

Using (
3.82
) and (
3.83
), we have

(3.90)

\displaystyle\left\|e_{h}^{k+1}\right\|_{s,2,\ast}^{2}\leq\sup_{t\in(0,T)}\left\|u(t)\right\|_{s,2,\ast}.

Thus, we obtain

(3.91)

\displaystyle\left\|u_{h}^{k+1}\right\|_{s,2,\ast}\leq\left\|u^{k+1}\right\|_{s,2,\ast}+\left\|e_{h}^{k+1}\right\|_{s,2,\ast}\leq 2\sup_{t\in(0,T)}\left\|u(t)\right\|_{s,2,\ast}.

By induction, (
3.84
) holds for all
n=0,\ldots,N_{t}
.

Using (
3.66
) and (
3.84
), (
3.76
) can be rewritten as

(3.92)

\displaystyle\left\|e_{h}^{n}\right\|_{s,2,\ast}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(1+\alpha_{1}\Delta t\right)\left\|e_{h}^{n-1}\right\|_{s,2,\ast}^{2}+C\left(\Delta t^{3}+\frac{h^{2q}}{\Delta t}+h^{2(q-s)}\right),

where
C
depends on
\left\|u\right\|_{W^{2,\infty}(H^{s})}
,
\left\|u\right\|_{L^{\infty}(H^{s+4})}
and
\left\|u\right\|_{L^{\infty}(H^{q})}
. From this, we can deduce

(3.93)

\displaystyle\left\|e_{h}^{n}\right\|_{s,2,\ast}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(1+\alpha_{1}\Delta t\right)^{n+1}\left\|e_{h}^{0}\right\|_{s,2,\ast}^{2}+\sum_{k=0}^{n}C\left(\Delta t^{3}+h^{2(q-s)}+\frac{h^{2q}}{\Delta t}\right)\left(1+\alpha_{1}\Delta t\right)^{k}

(3.94)

\displaystyle\leq C_{0}\mathrm{e}^{\alpha_{1}T}h^{2(q-s)}+CT\mathrm{e}^{\alpha_{1}T}\left(\Delta t^{2}+\frac{h^{2(q-s)}}{\Delta t}+\frac{h^{2q}}{\Delta t^{2}}\right),

which implies (
3.39
).
∎

3.4.
Weighted
H^{s}
Stability and
L^{2}
Convergence

We recall that
\left\|\,\cdot\,\right\|_{s,2,\Delta}
is defined by (
2.16
). We can establish the stability of
\mathcal{S}_{\Delta t}^{\mathrm{V}}
with respect to
\left\|\,\cdot\,\right\|_{s,2,\Delta}
as

(3.95)

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{S}_{\Delta t}^{\mathrm{V}}v\right\|_{s,2,\Delta}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\left(\left\|v\circ{X_{\Delta t}^{1}}[-\tilde{\delta}]\right\|_{s,2,\Delta}+\left\|v\circ{X_{\Delta t}^{1}}[+\tilde{\delta}]\right\|_{s,2,\Delta}\right)

(3.96)

\displaystyle=\left\|v\right\|_{s,2,\Delta}.

We can also investigate the stability of
\mathcal{I}_{h}
in the following lemma.
The case for cubic spline or Hermite interpolation is already stated in Lemmas 3.6 and 4.4 in
[
5
]
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Suppose that the interpolation operator
\mathcal{I}_{h}
possesses the properties (P
1
) and (P
2
). Then we have for any
v\in H^{s}(\mathbb{T})
and
(h,\Delta t)\in(0,1)^{2}
,

(3.97)

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{I}_{h}v\right\|_{s,2,*}

\displaystyle\leq\left(1+(C_{s})^{2}\Delta t\right)\left\|v\right\|_{s,2,*},

where
C_{s}
is the constant introduced in (P
2
).

Proof.

Using (
3.25
), we have

(3.98)

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{I}_{h}v\right\|_{s,2,\Delta}^{2}

(3.99)

\displaystyle=\left\|v+(\mathcal{I}_{h}-I)v\right\|_{0,2}^{2}+\frac{h^{2s}}{\Delta t}\left|\mathcal{I}_{h}v\right|_{s,2}^{2}

(3.100)

\displaystyle\leq(1+(C_{s})^{2}\Delta t)\left\|v\right\|_{0,2}^{2}+\left(1+\frac{1}{(C_{s})^{2}\Delta t}\right)\left\|(\mathcal{I}_{h}-I)v\right\|_{0,2}^{2}+\frac{h^{2s}}{\Delta t}\left|\mathcal{I}_{h}v\right|_{s,2}^{2}.

Applying (
3.30
) to (
3.100
), we have

(3.101)

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{I}_{h}v\right\|_{s,2,\Delta}^{2}\leq(1+(C_{s})^{2}\Delta t)\left(\left\|v\right\|_{0,2}^{2}+\frac{h^{2s}}{\Delta t}\left|(I-\mathcal{I}_{h})v\right|_{s,2}^{2}+\frac{h^{2s}}{\Delta t}\left|\mathcal{I}_{h}v\right|_{s,2}^{2}\right).

By (P
1
), we obtain

(3.102)

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{I}_{h}v\right\|_{s,2,\Delta}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq(1+(C_{s})^{2}\Delta t)\left(\left\|v\right\|_{0,2}^{2}+\frac{h^{2s}}{\Delta t}\left|v\right|_{s,2}^{2}\right)

(3.103)

\displaystyle=(1+(C_{s})^{2}\Delta t)\left\|v\right\|_{s,2,\Delta}^{2},

which concludes the proof.
∎

The following theorem is the main result of this paper.

Theorem 3.4
.

Suppose that the interpolation operator
\mathcal{I}_{h}
possesses the properties (P
1
), (P
2
) and (P
3
) for positive integers
s
and
q
.
Furthermore, assume that there exist positive constants
C_{\Delta 2}
and
\varepsilon
such that
h^{2s}/\Delta t\leq C_{\Delta 2}
and
h^{2(q-s)}/\Delta t^{1+\varepsilon}\leq C_{\Delta 2}
. Then there exist
(h_{0},\Delta t_{0}^{\prime})\in(0,1)^{2}
and
C_{e2}>0
depending on
\left\|u\right\|_{W^{1,\infty}(H^{s+2})}
,
\left\|u\right\|_{L^{\infty}(H^{s+4})}
and
\left\|u\right\|_{L^{\infty}(H^{q})}
such that, if
(h,\Delta t)\in(0,h_{0})\times(0,\Delta t_{0}^{\prime})
, the following estimate holds:

(3.104)

\displaystyle\left\|u^{n}-u_{h}^{n}\right\|_{s,2,\Delta}\leq C_{e2}\left(\Delta t+\frac{h^{q}}{\Delta t}\right)

for all
n\in\{0,\ldots,N_{t}\}
.
In particular, this implies the
L^{2}
-error estimate

(3.105)

\displaystyle\left\|u^{n}-u_{h}^{n}\right\|_{0,2}\leq C_{e2}\left(\Delta t+\frac{h^{q}}{\Delta t}\right).

Proof.

For
n=0
, using (P
2
) and (P
3
), we can estimate as

(3.106)

\displaystyle\left\|e_{h}^{0}\right\|_{s,2,\Delta}

\displaystyle=\left(\left\|(I-\mathcal{I}_{h})u_{0}\right\|_{0,2}^{2}+\frac{h^{2s}}{\Delta t}\left|(I-\mathcal{I}_{h})u_{0}\right|_{s,2}^{2}\right)^{1/2}

(3.107)

\displaystyle\leq C\frac{h^{q}}{\Delta t}\left|u\right|_{q,2}.

Combining (
3.53
) and (
3.56
), we have

(3.108)

\displaystyle\left\|e_{h}^{n}\right\|_{s,2,\Delta}^{2}

\displaystyle=\left\|e_{h}^{n}\right\|_{0,2}^{2}+\frac{h^{2s}}{\Delta t}\left|e_{h}^{n}\right|_{s,2}^{2}

(3.109)

\displaystyle\leq(1+3\Delta t)\left\|\mathcal{I}_{h}\eta_{h}^{n}\right\|_{s,2,\Delta}^{2}

(3.110)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{6}{\Delta t}\left(\left\|\mathcal{I}_{h}\theta_{h}^{n}\right\|_{s,2,\Delta}^{2}+\left\|\Delta t\mathcal{I}_{h}\tau_{h}^{n}\right\|_{s,2,\Delta}^{2}+\left\|\rho_{h}^{n}\right\|_{0,2}^{2}\right)+\frac{h^{2s}}{\Delta t}\left|\rho_{h}^{n}\right|_{s,2}^{2}.

By Lemma
3.3
, we have

(3.111)

\displaystyle\left\|e_{h}^{n}\right\|_{s,2,\Delta}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq(1+3\Delta t)\left\|\eta_{h}^{n}\right\|_{s,2,\Delta}^{2}+\frac{6}{\Delta t}\left\|\theta_{h}^{n}\right\|_{s,2,\Delta}^{2}

(3.112)

\displaystyle\quad+6\Delta t\left\|\tau_{h}^{n}\right\|_{s,2,\Delta}^{2}+\frac{6}{\Delta t}\left\|\rho_{h}^{n}\right\|_{0,2}^{2}+\frac{h^{2s}}{\Delta t}\left|\rho_{h}^{n}\right|_{s,2}^{2}.

We estimate
\eta_{h}^{n}
.
By (
3.49
) and (
A.91
), we have

(3.113)

\displaystyle\left\|\eta_{h}^{n}\right\|_{s,2,\Delta}

\displaystyle=\left\|(\mathcal{S}_{\Delta t}^{\mathrm{V}}e_{h}^{n}\circ{X_{\Delta t}^{1}}[f\circ(\mathcal{S}_{\Delta t}^{\mathrm{A}}\mathcal{S}_{\Delta t}^{\mathrm{V}}u_{h}^{n-1})])\right\|_{s,2,\Delta}

(3.114)

\displaystyle\leq\left(1+C_{\mathrm{P}}(\left\|\mathcal{S}_{\Delta t}^{\mathrm{A}}\mathcal{S}_{\Delta t}^{\mathrm{V}}u_{h}^{n-1}\right\|_{s,2,\ast})\Delta t\right)\left\|\mathcal{S}_{\Delta t}^{\mathrm{V}}e_{h}^{n-1}\right\|_{s,2,\Delta}.

By (
3.62
) and (
3.96
), we have

(3.115)

\displaystyle\left\|\eta_{h}^{n}\right\|_{s,2,\Delta}\leq\left(1+C_{\mathrm{P}}(\left\|u_{h}^{n-1}\right\|_{s,2,\ast})\Delta t\right)\left\|e_{h}^{n-1}\right\|_{s,2,\Delta}.

Let
h_{0}
and
\Delta t_{0}
be defined as in the proof of Theorem
3.2
. In the following, suppose that
(h,\Delta t)\in(0,h_{0})\times(0,\Delta t_{0})
. We have shown that
\left\|u_{h}^{n}\right\|_{s,2,\ast}
is bounded as (
3.84
). Therefore, (
3.115
) can be rewritten as

(3.116)

\displaystyle\left\|\eta_{h}^{n}\right\|_{s,2,\Delta}\leq\left(1+C_{\mathrm{P}}(\left\|u^{n-1}\right\|_{s,2,\ast})\Delta t\right)\left\|e_{h}^{n-1}\right\|_{s,2,\Delta}.

By (
3.19
) and (
3.20
), we can estimate
\left\|\tau_{h}^{n}\right\|_{s,2,\Delta}
as

(3.117)

\displaystyle\left\|\tau_{h}^{n}\right\|_{s,2,\Delta}^{2}\leq\left((C_{\tau,0})^{2}+C_{\Delta 2}(C_{\tau,s})^{2}\right)\Delta t^{2}.

By (P
2
) and (P
3
), we can estimate
\frac{6}{\Delta t}\left\|\rho_{h}^{n}\right\|_{0,2}^{2}+\frac{h^{2s}}{\Delta t}\left|\rho_{h}^{n}\right|_{s,2}^{2}
as

(3.118)

\displaystyle\frac{6}{\Delta t}\left\|\rho_{h}^{n}\right\|_{0,2}^{2}+\frac{h^{2s}}{\Delta t}\left|\rho_{h}^{n}\right|_{s,2}^{2}\leq C\frac{h^{2q}}{\Delta t}\left|u^{n}\right|_{q,2}.

We estimate
\theta_{h}^{n}
. By (
A.118
), we have

(3.119)

\displaystyle\left\|\theta_{h}^{n}\right\|_{s,2,\Delta}

(3.120)

\displaystyle=\left\|(\mathcal{S}_{\Delta t}^{\mathrm{V}}u^{n-1})\circ{X_{\Delta t}^{1}}[f\circ u^{n}]-(\mathcal{S}_{\Delta t}^{\mathrm{V}}u^{n-1})\circ{X_{\Delta t}^{1}}[f\circ(\mathcal{S}_{\Delta t}^{\mathrm{A}}\mathcal{S}_{\Delta t}^{\mathrm{V}}u_{h}^{n-1})]\right\|_{s,2,\Delta}

(3.121)

\displaystyle\leq C_{\mathrm{P}}(\left\|u^{n}\right\|_{s,2,\ast},\left\|\mathcal{S}_{\Delta t}^{\mathrm{A}}\mathcal{S}_{\Delta t}^{\mathrm{V}}u_{h}^{n-1}\right\|_{s,2,\ast})\Delta t\left\|\mathcal{S}_{\Delta t}^{\mathrm{V}}u^{n-1}\right\|_{s+1,\infty}

(3.122)

\displaystyle\qquad\cdot\left\|u^{n}-\mathcal{S}_{\Delta t}^{\mathrm{A}}\mathcal{S}_{\Delta t}^{\mathrm{V}}u_{h}^{n-1}\right\|_{s,2,\Delta}.

By (
3.23
), (
3.44
) and (
3.62
), this becomes

(3.123)

\displaystyle\left\|\theta_{h}^{n}\right\|_{s,2,\Delta}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\mathrm{P}}(\left\|u\right\|_{L^{\infty}(W^{s+1,\infty})},\left\|u_{h}^{n-1}\right\|_{s,2,\ast})\Delta t

(3.124)

\displaystyle\qquad\cdot(\left\|\eta_{h}^{n}\right\|_{s,2,\Delta}+\left\|\theta_{h}^{n}\right\|_{s,2,\Delta}+\Delta t\left\|\tau_{h}^{n}\right\|_{s,2,\Delta}).

Since we have the bound for the numerical solution as (
3.84
), there exists a positive constant
C_{3}
depending on
\left\|u\right\|_{L^{\infty}(W^{s+1,\infty}(\mathbb{T}))}
such that

(3.125)

\displaystyle\left\|\theta_{h}^{n}\right\|_{s,2,\Delta}\leq C_{3}\Delta t(\left\|\eta_{h}^{n}\right\|_{s,2,\Delta}+\left\|\theta_{h}^{n}\right\|_{s,2,\Delta}+\Delta t\left\|\tau_{h}^{n}\right\|_{s,2,\Delta}).

Let
\Delta t_{4}=\min\{1/(2C_{3}),1\}
and suppose
\Delta t\leq\Delta t_{4}
.
Then we have
1/(1-C_{3}\Delta t)\leq 1+2C_{3}\Delta t
.
Therefore (
3.125
) becomes

(3.126)

\displaystyle\left\|\theta_{h}^{n}\right\|_{s,2,\Delta}\leq C_{3}\Delta t(1+2C_{3}\Delta t)(\left\|\eta_{h}^{n}\right\|_{s,2,\Delta}+\Delta t\left\|\tau_{h}^{n}\right\|_{s,2,\Delta}).

Let
\Delta t_{0}^{\prime}=\min\{\Delta t_{0},\Delta t_{4}\}
. In the remainder of the proof, we assume that
\Delta t\in(0,\Delta t_{0}^{\prime})
.

By (
3.112
), (
3.116
) and (
3.126
), there exists a positive constant
\alpha_{2}
such that

(3.127)

\displaystyle\left\|e_{h}^{n}\right\|_{s,2,\Delta}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq(1+\alpha_{2}\Delta t)\left\|e_{h}^{n-1}\right\|_{s,2,\Delta}^{2}+C\Delta t\left\|\tau_{h}^{n}\right\|_{s,2,\Delta}^{2}

(3.128)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{6}{\Delta t}\left\|\rho_{h}^{n}\right\|_{0,2}^{2}+\frac{h^{2s}}{\Delta t}\left|\rho_{h}^{n}\right|_{s,2}^{2}.

Applying (
3.117
) and (
3.118
) to (
3.128
), we obtain

(3.129)

\displaystyle\left\|e_{h}^{n}\right\|_{s,2,\Delta}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq(1+\alpha_{2}\Delta t)\left\|e_{h}^{n-1}\right\|_{s,2,\Delta}^{2}+\beta_{2}\left(\Delta t^{3}+\frac{h^{2q}}{\Delta t}\right),

where
\beta_{2}
is a positive constant depending on
\left\|u\right\|_{W^{2,\infty}(H^{s})}
,
\left\|u\right\|_{L^{\infty}(H^{s+4})}
and
\left\|u\right\|_{L^{\infty}(H^{q})}
. Therefore, we have

(3.130)

\displaystyle\left\|e_{h}^{n}\right\|_{s,2,\Delta}^{2}\leq(1+\alpha_{2}\Delta t)^{n}\left\|e_{h}^{0}\right\|_{s,2,\Delta}^{2}+\sum_{k=1}^{n}(1+\alpha_{2}\Delta t)^{k}\beta_{2}\left(\Delta t^{3}+\frac{h^{2q}}{\Delta t}\right).

By (
3.107
) and
(1+\alpha_{2}\Delta t)^{n}\leq e^{\alpha_{2}T}
, we have

(3.131)

\displaystyle\left\|e_{h}^{n}\right\|_{s,2,\Delta}^{2}\leq e^{\alpha_{2}T}\left(C\frac{h^{2q}}{\Delta t^{2}}\left|u_{0}\right|_{q,2}^{2}+\beta_{2}T\left(\Delta t^{2}+\frac{h^{2q}}{\Delta t^{2}}\right)\right),

which implies (
3.104
).

Inequality (
3.105
) follows from (
3.104
).
∎

) with
f(u)=u

(4.1)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u+u\partial_{x}u-\nu\partial_{x}^{2}u=0&\text{in}\quad\mathbb{T}\times[0,T],\\
u(0)=u_{0}&\text{in}\quad\mathbb{T}\end{cases}

with

. The initial condition is given by

(4.2)

\displaystyle u_{0}(x)=\frac{4\nu A\pi\sin(2\pi x)}{1+A\cos(2\pi x)}

and

. The corresponding exact solution is

\displaystyle u(x,t)=\frac{4\nu A\pi\exp(-4\pi^{2}\nu t)\sin(2\pi x)}{1+A\exp(-4\pi^{2}\nu t)\cos(2\pi x)}.

u

-norms are computed using the Gauss–Legendre quadrature with seven nodes.

s=1
. However, Theorems
3.2
and
3.4
do not cover this case.

h
is varied while the temporal step size is fixed at
\Delta t=10^{-3}
(Figure
1
). In this setting, we observe that the accuracy of the scheme improves as either the interpolation order increases or the mesh size decreases.

\Delta t
is varied while the spatial mesh size is fixed at

(Figure

). In the case of the linear interpolation, the error increases as

are consistent with this theoretical prediction.

Appendix A
Auxiliary Estimates

In this section, we prepare some lemmas for the proofs of Theorems
3.2
and
3.4
.

We define the Bell polynomial
B_{l,k}
by

(A.1)

\displaystyle B_{l,k}(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{l-k+1})=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}_{s,l}}C_{l,\alpha}\prod_{j=1}^{l-k+1}\left(\xi_{j}\right)^{\alpha_{j}},

where
C_{l,\alpha}=\frac{l!}{\prod_{j=1}^{j=l}\alpha_{j}!(j!)^{\alpha_{j}}}
and

(A.2)

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{l,k}=\left\{\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{l})\in(\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\})^{l}\mathrel{}\middle|\mathrel{}\sum_{i=1}^{l}i\alpha_{i}=l,\quad\sum_{i=1}^{l}\alpha_{i}=k\right\}.

In this section, we often use the Faà di Bruno’s formula

(A.3)

\displaystyle\partial_{x}^{l}(v\circ w)=\sum_{k=1}^{l}\left((\partial_{x}^{k}v)\circ w\right)\mathcal{B}_{l,k}(w),

where
\mathcal{B}_{l,k}(w)=B_{l,k}(\partial_{x}w,\ldots,\partial_{x}^{l-k+1}w)
.

Lemma A.1
.

Let
v\in H^{s}(\mathbb{T})
and
f\in W^{s,\infty}(\mathbb{T})
for an integer
s\geq 2
. Let
\Delta t_{1}
be as defined in Lemma
2.1
. Then there exists a positive constant
C_{\mathrm{P}2}
which depends polynomially on
\left\|f\right\|_{s,\infty}
and
\left\|v\right\|_{s,2,\ast}
such that if
\Delta t<\Delta t_{1}
,

(A.4)

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{S}_{\Delta t}^{\mathrm{A}}(v)\right\|_{s,2,\ast}\leq\left(1+C_{\mathrm{P}2}\Delta t\right)\left\|v\right\|_{s,2,\ast}.

Proof.

In this proof, we denote
\mathcal{S}_{\Delta t}^{\mathrm{A}}(v)
by
w
. We will show that, for
k\in\{2,\ldots,s-1\}
,

(A.5)

\displaystyle\left\|w\right\|_{k,\infty}\leq\left(1+C_{\mathrm{P}}(\left\|f\right\|_{k,\infty},\left\|v\right\|_{k,\infty})\Delta t\right)\left\|v\right\|_{k,\infty}.

First, we prove (
A.5
) for
s=2
.
By differentiating (
2.19
) twice and using the Faà di Bruno’s formula (
A.3
), we have

(A.6)

\displaystyle\gamma\,\partial_{x}^{2}w

\displaystyle=\left((\partial_{x}v)\circ{X_{\Delta t}^{1}}[f\circ w]\right)\left\{\mathcal{B}_{2,1}({X_{\Delta t}^{1}}[f\circ w])-(f^{\prime}\circ w)\partial_{x}^{2}w\Delta t\right\}

(A.7)

\displaystyle\quad+\left((\partial_{x}^{2}v)\circ{X_{\Delta t}^{1}}[f\circ w]\right)\mathcal{B}_{2,2}({X_{\Delta t}^{1}}[f\circ w]),

where
\gamma
is defined by (
2.23
). Therefore, we have

(A.8)

\displaystyle\left\|\gamma\,\partial_{x}^{2}w\right\|_{2,\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\left|v\right|_{1,\infty}\left\|\mathcal{B}_{2,1}({X_{\Delta t}^{1}}[f\circ w])-(f^{\prime}\circ w)\partial_{x}^{2}w\Delta t\right\|_{0,\infty}

(A.9)

\displaystyle\quad+\left|v\right|_{2,\infty}\left\|\mathcal{B}_{2,2}({X_{\Delta t}^{1}}[f\circ w])\right\|_{0,\infty}

By (
2.28
), we have

(A.10)

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{B}_{2,1}({X_{\Delta t}^{1}}[f\circ w])-(f^{\prime}\circ w)\partial_{x}^{2}w\Delta t\right\|_{0,\infty}

\displaystyle=\Delta t\left\|(f^{\prime\prime}\circ w)(\partial_{x}w)^{2}\right\|_{0,\infty}

(A.11)

\displaystyle=\Delta t\left|f\right|_{2,\infty}\left|w\right|_{1,\infty}^{2}

Lemma A.4
.

Let
w_{1}
,
w_{2}\in H^{s}(\mathbb{T})
,
f
,
v\in W^{s+1,\infty}(\mathbb{T})

C_{\mathrm{P}7}
which depends polynomially on
\left\|f\right\|_{s+1,\infty}
,
\left\|w_{1}\right\|_{s,2,\ast}

\left\|w_{2}\right\|_{s,2,\ast}

\displaystyle\left\|v\circ{X_{\Delta t}^{1}}[f\circ w_{1}]-v\circ{X_{\Delta t}^{1}}[f\circ w_{2}]\right\|_{s,2,\ast}

\left\|w_{1}\right\|_{s,2,\ast}

\left\|w_{2}\right\|_{s,2,\ast}

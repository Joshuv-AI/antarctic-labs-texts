Bi-contact structures with symmetry: local normal forms
Connor Jackman

Abstract
. A pair of transverse contact distributions on a 3-manifold will in general admit no 1-parameter families of symmetries: a flow preserving both contact distributions. Here, we will determine local normal forms for such pairs admitting symmetries. In particular, we observe that orientable Anosov flows may be globally given by the intersection of a pair of oppositely oriented contact distributions admitting, around any point, maximal local symmetries.



1.
Introduction

Pairs of transverse contact distributions on a 3-manifold have been an active topic of interest primarily due to their relations to
projective Anosov flows
(see §2.2 of
[
4
]
, and §4 of
[
12
]
), and in particular Anosov flows (eg
[
8
]
,
[
10
]
). Here we examine such pairs admitting infinitesimal symmetries, where:

Definition 1
.

A contact distribution,
\xi
, on a 3-manifold
M
is a non-integrable rank two distribution on
M
. We write
(M,\xi,\tilde{\xi})
for a pair of transverse contact distributions
\xi,\tilde{\xi}
on the 3-manifold
M
. A symmetry of the pair is a diffeomorphism
\varphi:M\to M
preserving both contact distributions:

\varphi_{*}\xi=\xi,\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \varphi_{*}\tilde{\xi}=\tilde{\xi}.

A infinitesimal symmetry of the pair is a vector field whose flow is by symmetries of the pair.

Definition 2
.

We call the local symmetry algebra,
\mathfrak{g}
, of
(M,\xi,\tilde{\xi})
around a point
m\in M
, the Lie algebra of infinitesimal symmetries of the pair defined in some neighborhood of
m\in M
.

Note that the local symmetry algebra around a point is a real vector space, having a Lie algebra structure by Lie bracket of vector fields.

As is well known, a
single
contact distribution on a 3-manifold admits many infinitesimal symmetries: all are locally equivalent to the standard Darboux normal form:
\ker\{dy-pdx\},\penalty\ (x,y,p)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
, and any function
H(x,y,p)
generates an infinitesimal symmetry via its contact Hamiltonian vector field, eg:

(1)

H_{p}\partial_{x}+(pH_{p}-H)\partial_{y}-(H_{x}+pH_{y})\partial_{p}

where here, and in what follows, we write subscripts for partial derivatives (
H_{p}=\partial_{p}H
, etc.).

On the other hand, as we will see, a typical
pair
of transverse contact distributions will admit
no
infinitesimal symmetries. As for those pairs of transverse contact distributions admitting infinitesimal symmetries, we find here the following local normal forms:

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose a pair of transverse contact distributions
\xi,\tilde{\xi}
on a 3-manifold
M
admits an infinitesimal symmetry around
m\in M
transverse to
\xi\cap\tilde{\xi}
. Then around
m
, the pair is given in some coordinates by one of the following local normal forms:

(I
1
)

For
c\in\mathbb{R}\backslash\{0,1\}
a constant:

\xi=\{dy=pdx\},\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \tilde{\xi}=\{dy=cpdx\}.

The symmetry algebra of such a pair is given by the vector fields of the form:

\mathfrak{g}=\langle u(x)\partial_{x}+v(y)\partial_{y}+p(v^{\prime}(y)-u^{\prime}(x))\partial_{p}\rangle

where
u(x),v(y)
are any smooth functions of
x
and
y
respectively, and
u^{\prime},v^{\prime}
their derivatives.

(I
2
)

For
c^{2}\leq 4
a constant,

\xi=\{dy=pdx\},\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \tilde{\xi}=\{(p+c)dy+dx=0\}.

The symmetry algebra of such a pair in these coordinates are those vector fields of the form:

\mathfrak{g}=\langle u\partial_{x}+v\partial_{y}-u_{y}(1+cp+p^{2})\partial_{p}\rangle

where
u(x,y)
is any solution to

u_{xx}+u_{yy}=cu_{xy}

and
v(x,y)
its conjugate solution determined, up to addition of a constant, through:

dv=-u_{y}dx+(u_{x}-cu_{y})dy.

(II
1
)

For
f(y)
a function:

\xi=\{dy=pdx\},\penalty\ \penalty\ \tilde{\xi}=\{(p+f(y))dy\pm dx=0\},\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \mathfrak{g}=\langle\partial_{x}\rangle.

(II
2
)

For

a function:

\xi=\{dy=pdx\},\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \tilde{\xi}=\{dy=f(y)pdx\},\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \mathfrak{g}=\langle u(x)\partial_{x}-pu^{\prime}(x)\partial_{p}\rangle.

In the symmetry algebra,

is any smooth function of

u^{\prime}

(III
1

For functions

\xi=\{dy=\frac{p+a(y)}{p+b(y)}dx\},\penalty\ \penalty\ \tilde{\xi}=\{dy=\frac{f(p)+a(y)}{f(p)+b(y)}dx\},\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \mathfrak{g}=\langle\partial_{x}\rangle.

(III
2

For a function

\xi=\{dy=pdx\},\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \tilde{\xi}=\{dy=f(p)dx\},\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \mathfrak{g}=\langle\partial_{x},\partial_{y},x\partial_{x}+y\partial_{y}\rangle.

(IV)

For a function

\xi=\{dy=pdx\},\penalty\ \penalty\ \tilde{\xi}=\{dy=f(y,p)dx\},\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \mathfrak{g}=\langle\partial_{x}\rangle.

.

Remark 2

(On the transversal condition to the axis.)
.

For

admitting a non-trivial (non-vanishing) infinitesimal symmetry

is tangent to this ‘axis’,
\xi\cap\tilde{\xi}

coincide, for example:
\xi=\{dy=pdx\},\penalty\ \tilde{\xi}=\{dy=\pm pdx+dp\}
and

\xi\cap\tilde{\xi}
. It would be natural in further work to examine further local normal forms around such ‘singular’ points, for example: where a pair admits a symmetry tangent to
\xi\cap\tilde{\xi}
at said point, or around a point at which a symmetry field vanishes.

Remark 3

We would also like to note that our choice of coordinates in which we present the local normal forms above is somewhat arbitrary: only determined up to arbitrary diffeomorphisms. The forms presented in the above theorem

The Schwartzian invariant vanishes,

To define further invariants, we will make some orientation conventions. Recall that a contact structure
\xi

Definition 6

We call a pair
(M,\xi,\tilde{\xi})
commonly (positively) oriented (resp. oppositely (negatively) oriented) when the orientations induced by the contact distributions coincide (resp. are opposite). We denote such pairs by:

(M,\xi,\tilde{\xi})_{+},\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ (M,\xi,\tilde{\xi})_{-}

The case of an oppositely oriented pair,
(M,\xi,\tilde{\xi})_{-}

Definition 7

We call a pair

\alpha\wedge d\alpha=\pm\tilde{\alpha}\wedge d\tilde{\alpha}.

As well, we have an induced function, which we call the generating invariant:

I:M\to\mathbb{R},\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ I:=\frac{\alpha\wedge d\tilde{\alpha}+\tilde{\alpha}\wedge d\alpha}{\alpha\wedge d\alpha}

\alpha,\tilde{\alpha}

Remark 5

The conditions of being balanced as well as

\alpha,\tilde{\alpha}\to\lambda\alpha,\lambda\tilde{\alpha}

3.1.
Anosov flows

The most well-known instances of pairs of transverse contact structures are the
bi-contact structures
: when the pairs induce opposite orientations (
(M,\xi,\tilde{\xi})_{-}
in the notation here), being of grand interest due to their relations to projective Anosov flows. In particular, for

[

Theorem

Let
X
be a smooth orientable Anosov vector field on a compact 3-manifold

\xi=\ker\alpha,\penalty\ \penalty\ \tilde{\xi}=\ker\tilde{\alpha}

\alpha,\tilde{\alpha}

so that
\ker\{\alpha+\tilde{\alpha}\},\penalty\ \ker\{\alpha-\tilde{\alpha}\}

From which, in relation to theorem

Since the balanced

, which corresponds to type I

Remark 10
.

Because of the low (

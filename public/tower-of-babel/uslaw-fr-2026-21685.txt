DEPARTMENT OF JUSTICE

DEPARTMENT OF EDUCATION


Clarification on Department of Justice and Department of 
Education Guidance Documents Regarding Federal Nondiscrimination Laws

AGENCY: Civil Rights Division, Department of Justice; Office for Civil 
Rights, Department of Education.

ACTION: Notice; clarification.

-----------------------------------------------------------------------

SUMMARY: The U.S. Department of Justice (DOJ) and U.S. Department of 
Education (ED) (the Departments) are issuing this document to clarify 
that seven guidance documents issued jointly by the Departments are not 
enforceable. In addition, the Departments plan to revisit these 
guidance documents in light of the Supreme Court's decision in Loper 
Bright Enters. v. Raimondo, 603 U.S. 369, 400 (2024), to assess whether 
they are consistent with the single best meaning of the relevant 
statutory text. The Departments will not rely upon these guidance 
documents in their enforcement of federal civil rights laws, including 
Titles IV and VI of the Civil Rights Act of 1964 (Title IV and Title 
VI), 42 U.S.C. 2000c et seq. and 2000d et seq., the Equal Educational 
Opportunities Act of 1974 (EEOA), 20 U.S.C. 1701-1720, the Americans 
with Disabilities Act of 1990 (ADA), 42 U.S.C. 12101 et seq., and 
Section 504 of the Rehabilitation Act of 1973 (Section 504), 29 U.S.C. 
794.

FOR FURTHER INFORMATION CONTACT: John Mertens, Acting Chief,

    Each of the guidance documents discusses legal standards under 
federal civil rights laws enforced by the Departments. Some of the 
guidance documents assert that they do not have the force and effect of 
law, have no binding effect, and do not impose new legal requirements. 
The Departments' guidance documents, including these guidance 
documents, do not establish legally enforceable responsibilities beyond 
what is required by the terms of the applicable statutes, regulations, 
or binding judicial precedent.\8\ This is consistent with Supreme Court 
jurisprudence, which has held that guidance documents ``do not have the 
force and effect of law.'' Perez v. Mortg. Bankers Ass'n, 575 U.S. 92, 
97 (2015) (quoting Shalala v. Guernsey Mem'l Hosp., 514 U.S. 87, 99 
(1995)). Regardless, the Departments are concerned about confusion over 
the non-enforceable nature of the guidance documents and issue this 
clarification that the guidance documents are not enforceable.

    Further, each of the guidance documents was issued prior to the 
Supreme Court's decision in Loper Bright, which held that agency 
interpretations must be consistent with the ``single, best meaning'' of 
the statute authorizing them. 603 U.S. at 400. For these reasons, the 
Departments plan to revisit the guidance documents listed in this 
notice in light of Loper Bright to determine whether they are 
consistent with the ``single, best meaning'' of the relevant federal 
civil rights laws enforced by the Departments. The Departments will not 
rely upon the guidance documents listed in this notice in their 
enforcement of Titles IV and Title VI, the EEOA, the ADA, and Section 
504.

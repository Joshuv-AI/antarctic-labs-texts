Improved expansion of peripheral blood and iPSC-derived natural killer cells for clinical applications
Authors: Jaya Lakshmi Thangaraj, Edith Lopez, Dan S Kaufman
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
SummaryThe large-scale expansion of natural killer (NK) cells commonly relies on artificial antigen-presenting cells (aAPCs). Currently, K562 cells expressing 41BBL and membrane-bound IL-21 (mbIL-21) are widely used to expand NK cells for clinical use. Here, we explored whether alternative aAPCs could improve NK cell expansion and better support clinical translation. We developed and tested four genetically modified feeder cell lines as alternative aAPC sources. K562 and 721.221 cells were engineered to express either OX40L or 41BBL, along with mbIL-18/21 and B7H6. We evaluated the expansion of NK cells derived from 3 different sources: unfractionated peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs), peripheral blood (PB), and induced pluripotent stem cells (iPSCs). Our results showed that 721.221 feeder cells expressing 41BBL-B7H6 or OX40L with mbIL-18/21 significantly enhanced NK cell expansion compared with K562 cell-based aAPCs. All modified aAPCs induced strong NK cell activation and tumor-killing activity. These findings identify 721.221 cell-derived 41BBL-B7H6-mbIL-18/21 cells as promising aAPCs for large-scale NK cell production in clinical settings.Keywords: immunotherapy, immuno-oncology, natural killer cells, cell therapy, artificial antigen-presenting cells, ovarian cancer, head and neck squamous cell carcinoma, adoptive cell transfer

Introduction

Natural killer (NK) cells are a critical component of the innate immune system, playing an essential role in immune surveillance and the eradication of virus-infected and malignant cells.1,2,3 Due to their ability to target and kill cancer cells without prior sensitization, NK cells have emerged as a promising therapeutic modality in adoptive cell therapies (ACTs) for diverse malignancies, both hematological cancers and solid cancers.4,5,6,7 Recent advances in NK cell biology, including genetic engineering and ex vivo expansion protocols, have significantly improved the potential for clinical translation of NK cell-based therapies.8,9,10 However, large-scale expansion of highly cytotoxic NK cells derived from peripheral blood (PB), cord blood (CB), and induced pluripotent stem cells (iPSCs) remains a critical challenge due to the need for robust artificial antigen-presenting cells (aAPCs) that can stimulate NK cell proliferation and activation in a cost-effective and scalable manner.

The most commonly used aAPCs, such as the K562 cell line modified to express 4-1BBL and membrane-bound IL-21 (mbIL-21), have shown significant promise in expanding NK cells for clinical use.11,12,13 This approach has been instrumental in facilitating the rapid ex vivo proliferation of NK cells, yielding highly cytotoxic effector cells.11 While other studies have focused on optimizing NK cell expansion and activation protocols to enhance their cytotoxic and expansion potential, there has been no systematic comparison between different engineered aAPCs.

Co-stimulatory signals, such as OX40L and 4-1BBL, are potent stimulators of NK cell survival, expansion, and effector function. OX40L binds to the OX40 receptor (CD134), a member of the tumor necrosis factor receptor (TNFR) superfamily, primarily expressed on activated T cells and NK cells. OX40L enhances NK cell survival and supports their expansion, contributing to the longevity of the anti-tumor response.14,15,16 Studies have shown that OX40-OX40L interactions enhance the production of cytokines, such as IFN-γ, which is essential for orchestrating effective anti-tumor immunity.17,18,19 Another co-stimulatory molecule 4-1BBL (CD137L) binds to 4-1BB (CD137), also part of the TNFR family. The 4-1BB-4-1BBL axis is critical for boosting NK cell proliferation and cytotoxic function. When engaged, this stimulation increases NK cell-mediated killing of target cells by enhancing their cytotoxic granule release and cytokine secretion (e.g., TNF-α and IFN-γ).20 This interaction has been actively explored in cancer immunotherapy to improve NK cell persistence and anti-tumor activity.21,22,23 B7H6 is a ligand for NKp30, one of the major natural cytotoxicity receptors (NCRs) on NK cells. B7H6 is often expressed on stressed or transformed cells, including cancer cells, but not on healthy cells, making it a promising target for NK cell-based therapies. Binding of NKp30 to B7H6 triggers robust NK cell activation, cytokine release, cytotoxic activity, and NK cell expansion.24,25,26

In this study, we aimed to develop an alternative genetically modified aAPC-based NK cell expansion method based on K562 and 721.221 cells, which were engineered to express different combinations of co-stimulatory molecules and cytokines essential for NK cell activation and expansion. The K562 and 721.221 cell lines were selected as feeder cells to induce NK cell expansion due to their well-documented ability to robustly stimulate NK cell proliferation and activation.27,28 K562 cells, derived from human chronic myeloid leukemia, are often used in NK cell culture systems because they lack major histocompatibility complex (MHC) class I expression, minimizing inhibitory signals to NK cells, and naturally express ligands such as MICA/B and ULBPs that activate NK cell receptors like NKG2D.29,30 Similarly, the 721.221 cell line, derived from an Epstein-Barr virus-transformed B lymphoblastoid line, is widely utilized due to its lack of HLA-ABC alleles, which prevents the engagement of inhibitory killer cell immunoglobulin-like receptors (KIRs) on NK cells.27,31 Furthermore, both feeder cell types can be genetically engineered to overexpress cytokines like IL-15 and IL-21 or co-stimulatory molecules, further enhancing NK cell expansion and functionality in vitro and in vivo.11,27,30

This study provides critical insights into the optimization of NK cell expansion protocols and represents a significant advancement in the development of scalable and effective NK cell therapies for cancers. By utilizing enhanced stimulatory potential of these genetically modified feeder cells, we aim to overcome the current limitations associated with traditional NK cell expansion methods and pave the way for more efficient and clinically translatable NK cell-based immunotherapies.

Results

Engineering of K562- and 721.221-based aAPCs expressing either 41BBL-B7H6 or OX40L-B7H6 with mbIL-18 and IL-21

To develop an improved, large-scale NK cell expansion platform, we selected suspension cell lines to serve as feeder cells, aiming to enhance NK cell proliferation. Specifically, K562 and 721.221 cell lines were chosen due to their efficient stimulation and lysis by NK cells due to their low expression of HLA class I molecules and high expression of NK cell-stimulating ligands (Figure 1).29,32,33 As noted, both K562 and 721.221 cells express MICA, MICB, ULBP2,5,6, PVR, DR4, and DR5 (Figure 1). These ligands are known to stimulate their respective NK cell activation receptors: NKG2D, DNAM-1, and TRAIL. Using the piggyBac vector system, both K562 and 721.221 cells were co-transfected with two distinct piggyBac vectors: one carrying 41BBL-B7H6 or OX40L-B7H6, and the other carrying membrane-bound IL-18 (mbIL-18) and mbIL-21 (Figures 2A–2C). After nucleofection, the engineered cells underwent drug selection with puromycin and zeocin for two weeks, and the surface expression of 41BBL, OX40L, IL-18, and IL-21 on the modified K562 and 721.221 cells was measured by flow cytometry. Four different (aAPC) platforms were successfully developed: K562-OX40L-B7H6-mbIL-18/21, K562-41BBL-B7H6-mbIL-18/21, 721-OX40L-B7H6-mbIL-18/21, and 721-41BBL-B7H6-mbIL-18/21 (Figure 2D). The results demonstrated high expression of 41BBL or OX40L co-stimulatory molecules combined with mbIL-18 and mbIL-21 on both K562 and 721.221 cell lines (Figure 2D). These findings confirmed the successful engineering of K562 and 721.221 cells to express key co-stimulatory molecules and cytokines necessary for enhanced NK cell activation and proliferation.

Expansion of PBMC-derived NK cells using K562- or 721.221-based genetically modified aAPCs

We assessed the ability of our engineered K562 cell- and 721.221 cell-modified aAPCs to expand peripheral blood mononuclear cell (PBMC)-derived NK cells. These aAPCs were compared to the “standard” K562 cells expressing 41BBL and mbIL21, as well as K562 cells expressing just OX40L and mbIL18/21. All the cell lines tested suitably stimulated NK cell growth (Figure 3A). The initial purity of CD3−CD56+ NK cells in the whole PBMC population on day 0 was relatively low (<10%) (Figure S1). However, these whole, unfractionated PBMCs were stimulated with the engineered aAPCs: K562-OX40L-B7H6-mbIL-18/21, K562-41BBL-B7H6-mbIL-18/21, 721-OX40L-B7H6-mbIL-18/21, and 721-41BBL-B7H6-mbIL-18/2. Under these stimulation conditions, the purity of NK cells increased to over 90% CD3−-CD56+ cells over the 7-week culture period (Figure 3B). The expansion and enrichment for NK cells were better than those with comparable aAPCs without B7H6 expression. Among the aAPCs tested, the 721-41BBL-mbIL-18/21 and 721-OX40L-mbIL-18/21 showed the most robust expansion of NK cells, with fold increases of ∼17,354 and ∼15,484, respectively, at 7 weeks (Figures 3C and S1).

Improved activation and cytotoxic activity of PBMC-derived NK cells expanded using developed aAPCs

NK cell activation and function are based on the expression balance between NK cell activation and inhibitory receptors. After 2 weeks of PBMC-derived NK cell expansion, we confirmed the expression of several key NK cell activation receptors, including CD94, CD16, NKp30, NKp46, NKG2D, CD226, TRAIL, and FasL, on the expanded NK cells (Figures 4A, S5, and S6). After confirming the NK cell activation receptor expression, we assessed the cytotoxicity of the developed aAPC-expanded NK cells against different hematological and solid tumor cell lines, including K562, CAL27, SKOV3, and A1847 (Figure 4B). Remarkably, the PBMC-derived NK cells stimulated and expanded by the engineered aAPCs exhibited enhanced anti-tumor activity compared with those expanded by the standard K562-41BBL-mbIL21 aAPCs against these tumor cell lines.

These studies demonstrated that the 721.221-based aAPCs consistently promoted strong NK cell activation, as indicated by the upregulation of activation markers (Figure 4A). The PBMC-derived NK cells expanded using either 721-41BBL-mbIL-18/21-B7H6 or 721-41BBL-mbIL-18/21-B7H6 aAPCs demonstrated consistent and significantly increased cytotoxicity, which enhanced their tumor-killing efficiency against K562, CAL27, SKOV3, and A1847 cell lines compared with PBMC NK cells expanded using conventional aAPCs without B7-H6 expression (Figures 4B and 4C). Statistical analysis confirmed that PBMC-derived NK cells expanded with the 721-41BBL-mbIL-18/21-B7H6 aAPCs exhibited significantly better killing efficacy against all four cell lines (K562, CAL27, SKOV3, and A1847) than the PBMC NK cells expanded using conventional aAPCs without B7-H6 expression.

We next evaluated the recovery efficiency and re-expansion potential of PBMC-derived NK cells that had been previously expanded using the engineered K562- and 721.221-based aAPCs and cryopreserved for 18 months. Following thawing and re-expansion, the PBMC-derived NK cells exhibited robust recovery and proliferative capacity comparable to that observed prior to cryopreservation. The NK cell activation receptors, including CD94, CD16, NKp30, NKp46, NKG2D, and CD226, remained unchanged, indicating that cryopreservation had minimal impact on their surface phenotype (Figures S7A and S8A). Functionally, the re-expanded NK cells demonstrated potent cytotoxic activity against multiple tumor cell lines, suggesting that their effector function was well preserved. Together, these findings indicate that PBMC-derived NK cells expanded using the engineered aAPCs presented in this work can be efficiently cryopreserved and re-expanded without significant loss of phenotype or cytotoxic potential, supporting their utility for future clinical applications (Figures S7 and S8).

Improved expansion of PB- and iPSC-derived NK cells using developed aAPCs

After demonstrating the ability of the aAPCs to expand PBMC-derived NK cells, we aimed to characterize their ability to expand CD56-sorted PB- and iPSC-derived NK cells. Both PB- and iPSC-derived NK cells were stimulated with the different aAPCs in a 1:1 ratio. PB- and iPSC-derived NK cell purity and expansion rate were measured weekly (Figures 5A–5D, S2, and S3). As expected, the developed aAPCs significantly improved the expansion of both PB- and iPSC-derived NK cells. Notably, the 721.221-based aAPCs exhibited a remarkable expansion rate for both PB- and iPSC-derived NK cells, outperforming the K562-based aAPCs and conventional K562-based aAPCs without B7-H6 expression. In addition, the K562-41BBL-B7H6-mbIL-18/21-modified aAPCs also showed a significant level of expansion compared with conventional K562-based aAPCs that lacked B7H6 expression (Figures 5A–5D). In particular, both the 721-OX40L-B7H6-mbIL-18/21 and 721-41BBL-B7H6-mbIL-18/21 aAPCs successfully expanded both PB- and iPSC-derived NK cells more than 4 logs over 6 weeks. This led to >1010 NK cells, from a starting population of 106 cells of both PB- and iPSCderived NK cells. In contrast to conventional K562-based aAPCs without B7H6 expression, the 721.221 cell-modified aAPCs were able to induce PB-derived NK cell expansion, ranging from a minimum of 3.2 logs to a maximum of 4.2 logs, and iPSC-derived NK cells expanded from a minimum of 4.5 logs to a maximum of 4.9 logs over a 42-day NK cell expansion period. These findings highlight the potential of the developed aAPCs to not only robustly stimulate the expansion of PBMC-derived and PB-derived NK cells but also promote a similar level of expansion in iPSC-derived NK cells more effectively. We also evaluated the recovery and re-expansion potential of both PB- and iPSC-derived NK cells following cryopreservation. Our results demonstrated that cryopreservation did not significantly affect their recovery rate, proliferation capacity, or functional activity, indicating that both PB- and iPSC-derived NK cells maintained their viability and potency upon re-expansion (Figures S7–S10).

Enhanced activation and cytotoxic activity of PB- and iPSC-derived NK cells expanded using presented aAPCs

During the expansion of PB- and iPSC-derived NK cells with the developed aAPCs, we also evaluated the expression of NK cell activation receptors on the surface of these NK cells (Figures 6A and 7A), using flow cytometry. The PB- and iPSC-derived NK cells, activated and expanded using the developed aAPCs, showed similar levels of NK cell activation receptors such as CD94, CD16, NKp30, NKp46, NKG2D, and CD226 compared with conventional aAPCs (Figures 6A and 7A). We next evaluated the functional activity of PB- and iPSC-derived NK cells, using short-term and long-term killing assays. Notably, PB- and iPSC-derived NK cells expanded using the developed aAPCs displayed similar cytotoxicity in short-term assays against K562, CAL27, SKOV3, and A1847 cell lines. Specifically, NK cells expanded with 721-OX40L-B7H6-mbIL-18/21 and 721-41BBL-B7H6-mbIL-18/21 demonstrated superior killing efficacy against K562, CAL27, SKOV3, and A1847 cell lines compared to those expanded with standard aAPCs (Figures 6B and 7B). Additional long-term killing assays revealed that PB- and iPSC-derived NK cells expanded with the developed aAPCs maintained significant cytotoxic activity against K562-GFP-luc, CAL27-GFP-luc, SKOV3-GFP-luc, and A1847-td-tomato-luc cell lines for over 48 h (Figures 6C and 7C) compared with those expanded using standard aAPCs. We further evaluated the functional activity of cryopreserved iPSC-derived and PB-derived NK cells against K562, CAL27, SKOV3, and A1847 tumor cell lines and compared their performance with NK cells expanded using standard aAPCs. Both short-term and long-term killing assays demonstrated that cryopreserved iPSC-derived and PB-derived NK cells retained robust cytotoxic activity and effectively eliminated tumor cells, similar to the activity of NK cells expanded with standard aAPCs (Figures S9 and S10).

Collectively, these results indicate that these aAPCs effectively enhance the activation and cytotoxic function of both PB- and iPSC-derived NK cells, demonstrating improved anti-tumor activity against both hematological and solid cancer cell lines. These findings suggest that the 721 cell-based aAPCs may serve as a promising alternative to traditional K562-based aAPCs for expanding NK cells in immunotherapeutic applications.

Discussion

The need for effective and scalable methods to expand NK cells from PB and iPSCs is crucial for the advancement of cell-based immunotherapies.8,34 In our study, we developed and assessed the functional activity of the developed genetically modified aAPCs to enhance NK cell expansion. While the already existing K562-41BBL-mbIL-21 and K562-OX40L-mbIL-18/21 aAPCs have demonstrated promising results, their scalability and usage by all other researchers remain a significant barrier to clinical translation. Our findings indicated that the 721.221 cell line, particularly when engineered to express the co-stimulatory molecule OX40L or 41BBL in combination with B7H6 and mbIL-18/21, provides a substantial improvement in NK cell expansion, activation, and cytotoxicity against various tumor cell lines.

Other studies have also highlighted the potential of genetically modified aAPCs to augment NK cell therapy.27,35,36,37,38,39 A study demonstrated that K562 cells engineered to express IL-21, MICA, and 4-1BBL promoted a robust expansion of NK cells and enhanced their anti-tumor activity.40 Recent findings showed that the incorporation of multiple co-stimulatory signals into feeder cells could lead to improved NK cell function and in vivo persistence.41,42 However, our study demonstrated that the use of 721.221 cells and K562 cells engineered with 41BBL, B7-H6, and mbIL-18 and mbIL-21 significantly improved NK cell expansion compared with previous feeder cell-based NK expansion methods. Unlike prior studies that have primarily focused on the expansion of PB- and CB-derived NK cells, our approach also optimized iPSC-derived NK cell expansion. The addition of B7-H6 to the feeder cells not only enhanced NK cell proliferation but also boosted their cytotoxic activity. Furthermore, mbIL-18 and mbIL-21 mediated the signaling, leading to superior NK cell survival and function. Our study demonstrates a superior level of NK cell expansion for each of the NK cell populations tested (PBMC-derived, PB-derived, and iPSC-derived NK cells) when using K562- or 721.221-modified aAPCs that express B7H6 and other co-stimulatory signals. The aAPCs presented here outperform standard aAPCs without B7H6 expression in terms of both NK cell expansion and cytotoxic activity. The incorporation of B7H6 expression significantly enhances NK cell expansion and anti-tumor killing activity in PBMC-, PB-, and iPSC-derived NK cells. Importantly, interaction with B7H6, a ligand for NKp30 on NK cells, provides potent activation signal that synergizes with the mbIL-18 and mbIL-21 cytokine and co-stimulatory signals, significantly enhancing NK cell proliferation, cytotoxic activity, and functional responsiveness compared with aAPCs lacking B7H6 expression. This strategic modification enables more robust and sustained NK cell activation, making our aAPCs a superior platform for efficient NK cell expansion.43 The ligand for NKp30, B7H6, is expressed on tumor cells and can trigger NK cell activation, enhancing their cytotoxic response.26,44 Upon engagement with B7H6, NKp30 induces signaling cascades that promote the release of cytokines, such as IFN-γ and TNF-α, and upregulate cytotoxic molecules like perforin and granzyme.45,46 NKp30-B7H6 interaction also enhances NK cell-mediated target cell killing.26,46,47 Therefore, B7H6 expression in aAPCs likely plays a crucial role in activating and expanding NK cells, resulting in a higher level of cytotoxicity in PBMC-, PB-, and iPSC-derived NK cells.

Multiple clinical trials evaluating NK cell-based therapies have highlighted the importance of optimizing expansion protocols and aAPC systems.48,49,50,51,52 The use of K562 and other feeder cell-based aAPCs including EBV-LCL are being tested in various clinical trials to assess the safety and efficacy of NK cell-based therapies in patients with relapsed or refractory hematological malignancies and other cancers.53,54,55,56 These studies are instrumental in understanding the role of aAPCs in NK cell expansion and their therapeutic potential in clinical settings. The developed 721.221 cell-modified aAPCs demonstrated superior NK cell expansion and improved anti-tumor activity in vitro. The developed aAPCs have the potential to improve NK cell-based therapies in clinical applications, as they could enable more efficient expansion of NK cells, which can lead to better therapeutic outcomes. However, a limitation of our study is that all these findings are based on in vitro experiments. Therefore, further in vivo studies are required to assess the full therapeutic potential of these modified aAPC-expanded NK cells. In particular, testing the efficacy of NK cells expanded using the developed aAPCs in tumor-bearing animal models and clinical trials in various cancers will be crucial to understand their safety, efficacy, and long-term persistence in patients with various cancers.57,58 Investigating the longevity of those expanded NK cells in the circulation, their tumor-infiltration abilities, and their overall therapeutic efficacy in clinical settings will be crucial for the developed 721.221-based aAPC-expanded NK cell therapies. Additionally, the potential to expand NK cells using the developed aAPCs in a bioreactor system for large-scale production offers an exciting avenue for producing increased numbers of NK cells for clinical use.12,59,60 Additionally, further research is essential to optimize these systems for GMP-compliant NK cell production.61 The implementation of GMP standards facilitates regulatory approval and supports the seamless integration of NK cell therapies into clinical trials and therapeutic applications.8,61,62

In conclusion, our findings demonstrate that genetically modified 721.221-based aAPCs significantly improve NK cell expansion and cytotoxicity compared with traditional K562-based aAPCs. The 721.221 based aAPCs presented here hold promise for developing a scalable, effective expansion protocol for NK cell-based therapies and their clinical applications, paving the way for future clinical trials and eventual therapeutic use. Further studies are required to optimize these systems for GMP-compliant NK cell manufacturing.

Limitations of the study

Although our approach provides an improved system for NK cell expansion, the expanded NK cells were only tested for activity in vitro and not by using in vivo models. Though unlikely, it is possible that NK cells expanded and activated using these aAPCs could either be less effective or cause toxicities when administered to mice or patients. Additionally, these studies did not test for the exhaustion of NK cells expanded with these methods, something that could be addressed in future work.

Experimental model and study participant details

All experimental procedures were conducted in compliance with protocols approved by the Institutional Review Board at the University of California, San Diego. Human peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs) were obtained from unidentified donor samples obtained from the San Diego Blood Bank under their own IRB approved protocols. No direct human subject recruitment or interaction was involved in this study.

Cell lines

K562, SKOV3, CAL27, and A1847 cell lines were obtained from the American Type Culture Collection (ATCC, Manassas, VA, USA) and cultured in either RPMI 1640 or Dulbecco’s Modified Eagle Medium (DMEM), both supplemented with 10% heat-inactivated fetal bovine serum (FBS) (Gibco, Waltham, MA, USA), 100 U/mL penicillin, and 100 μg/mL streptomycin (Gibco). Cells were incubated at 37°C in a humidified atmosphere with 5% CO2. These cell lines were engineered to stably express luciferase and either GFP or td-tomato. Stable expression was achieved by transfecting cells with td-tomato or GFP combined with luciferase (luc) expression utilizing the Sleeping Beauty transposon system (Addgene, Cambridge, MA, USA) as previously described.57,63 The transfection was performed using nucleofection (Lonza, Basel, Switzerland) according to the manufacturer’s protocol. K562-OX40L-mbIL-18/21 and K562-41BBL-mbIL-21, two widely used aAPCs lacking B7H6 expression, were also cultured in RPMI1640 medium.

Method details

Engineering of K562 or 721.221 cells using PiggyBac transposon to express 41BBL, OX40L, mbIL-18, and mbIL-21

To engineer K562 and 721.221 cells to express co-stimulatory molecules (41BBL, OX40L), B7H6 and cytokines (membrane-bound IL-18 [mbIL-18] and membrane-bound IL-21 [mbIL-21]), the piggyBac transposon system (System Biosciences) was utilized.39 The transposon plasmids were designed to contain the cDNA sequences encoding human 41BBL-B7H6, OX40L-B7H6, and mbIL-18/mbIL-21, each linked to a separate expression cassette under the control of the EF1α promoter. The membrane-bound forms of IL-18 and IL-21 were generated by fusing the extracellular domains of IL-18 and IL-21 to the CD8α transmembrane domain. K562 or 721.221 cells were co-transfected with the piggyBac transposon plasmid and super piggyBac transposase plasmid using 4D nucleofector according to the manufacturer’s protocol. Briefly, 3 μg of the piggyBac vector and 1 μg of the transposase plasmid were added and electroporated using 4D nucleofector. After 24 h, the transfection medium was changed with fresh RPMI-1640 supplemented with 10% FBS and 1% P/S. After 48–72 h of transfection, cells were treated with puromycin (1–2 μg/mL; Sigma-Aldrich) for the selection around 7–14 days.64 Surviving cells were expanded and maintained in RPMI-1640 with 10% FBS and puromycin to ensure stable transgene expression.

Ex vivo NK cell expansion using genetically modified feeder cells

Peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs) were isolated from peripheral blood samples obtained from healthy donors using Ficoll-Paque PLUS (GE Healthcare) gradient centrifugation. From the isolated PBMCs, NK cells were enriched by depletion of CD3+ cells using magnetic beads, and positive selection of CD56+ cells, using EasySep Human NK Cell Isolation Kit (Stemcell Technologies).65,66 Feeder cells were irradiated at 100 G-ray using the institutional gamma irradiator at the University of California, San Diego prior to co-culture with PBMCs, PB and iPSC NK cells. For the expansion of PBMC-derived NK cells, whole PBMCs were co-cultured with 100G-ray irradiated aAPCs (6:1 ratio) in RPMI 1640 medium supplemented with 10%, FBS, 1% GlutaMAX and 1% penicillin/streptomycin (P/S) with 10 U/mL of IL-2 for the first 7 days as dexcribed previously.37,38,39 After day 7, the IL-2 concentration was increased to 100 U/mL, and IL-15 was introduced at 10 U/mL. The NK cells were continuing to expand under these conditions for more than 7 weeks. The differentiation and expansion of iPSC-derived NK cells were performed as previously described by our group.58,67,68,69,70,71 Briefly, iPSCs were aggregated into spin embryoid bodies (EBs) in APEL medium (StemCell Technologies) supplemented with SCF, VEGF, BMP-4, and a ROCK inhibitor. After six days, the EBs were transferred to NK cell differentiation medium containing interleukin IL-15, IL-7, Flt3 ligand, SCF, and IL-3. The cells were cultured in this medium for 4–5 weeks with half-medium changes every 3–4 days. After 35 days of differentiation, the resulting NK cells were harvested and further expanded using these aAPCs. Both PB NK cells and iPSC derived NK cells were cultured with irradiated aAPCs (1:1 ratio) in the presence of IL-2 (100U/mL) and IL-15 (10U/mL) for over 7 weeks. Functional assays involving aAPC-expanded NK cells were conducted after a minimum of 2 weeks of expansion. To maintain NK cells viability and expansion, every 3 to 4 days, half media were replaced with fresh IL-2 and IL-15. Expanded NK cells were continuously cultured until day 42. The expansion rate of NK cells is presented as “expansion fold”, which was determined by dividing the absolute number of NK cells at time points of interest by the respective number on day 0.30,39,69

Flow cytometry analysis

The expression of 41BBL, OX40L, B7H6, mbIL-18, and mbIL-21 on the aAPC surface was confirmed by flow cytometry. aAPCs were stained with fluorochrome-conjugated antibodies specific to 41BBL (CD137L; BioLegend), OX40L (CD252; BD Biosciences), IL-18 (R&D Systems), and IL-21 (R&D Systems) for 30 min at 4°C. After washing with flow buffer, cells were analyzed using flow cytometry. The expression of NK cell receptors on the expanded NK cells was examined by flow cytometry using appropriate fluorochrome conjugated antibodies. The following antibodies were used (all anti-human): CD45-APC (BD Biosciences), CD56-PE-Cy7 (Biolegend), CD94–FITC (BD Biosciences), CD94–PE (BD Biosciences), CD16-PE (BD Biosciences), NKG2D-APC (BD Biosciences), NKp30-APC (BD Biosciences), NKp44-PE (BD Biosciences), NKp46-PE (Biolegend), FASL-PE (R&D Systems), TRAIL-PE (R&D Systems) and CD226-PE (BD Biosciences). Briefly, expanded NK cells (2 × 105) were washed with flow buffer (PBS containing 1% FBS) and stained with appropriate antibodies for 30 min, washed again with flow buffer, and acquired and analyzed by using flow cytometry. Flow cytometry was performed using NovoCyte 3000, and the data were analyzed using NovoExpress software.57,68

Caspase 3/7 killing assay

Cytotoxicity of expanded NK cells against various tumor cells including K562, SKOV3, CAL27 and A1847 was measured by using CellEvent Caspase-3/7 Green Flow Cytometry Assay Kit (Thermo Fisher Scientific; C10427) for 4 h as described previously.57,58 Briefly, target cells were stained with cell trace violet in 1X PBS for 10 min at 37°C, washed twice with complete media, and then 5 × 104 target cells were placed in a 96-well U-bottom plate in triplicate and mixed with different feeder cells stimulated and expanded NK cells at 10:1, 5:1, 2.5:1, 1:1, 0.5:1 and 0.25:1 effector-to-target (E:T) ratios. Plates were centrifuged at 1,500 rpm for 3 min and then incubated at 37°C in a 5% CO2 incubator for 3h and 30min. The caspase 3/7 reagent was added 30min prior to final incubation time, and SYTOX live/dead stain were added 5 min before analysis by flow cytometry. Percentages of dead tumor cells were calculated by subtracting the percentage of tumor cells that had died spontaneously.57,58,68

IncuCyte cytotoxicity assay

K562, SKOV3, and CAL27 cells were engineered to stably express GFP and luciferase (K562-GFP-luc, SKOV3-GFP-luc and CAL27-GFP-luc) and A1847 cells were engineered to stably express td-tomato and luciferease (A1847-td-tomato-luc) were seeded in a 96-well flat bottom plate at a density of 30000 cells/well overnight before addition of effector cells (30000 cells/well).57,58 After overnight incubation, NK cells were added at 1:1 E:T ratios with cytokines and monitored on the IncuCyte imaging system to acquire images every 2 h. All experiments were repeated at least three times. The cytotoxicity of target cells was analyzed by quantifying either red cell area or green cell area which are normalized to 0h and 0days of the red cells or green cells.57,58

Quantification and statistical analysis

Data are presented as the mean ± standard error of the mean (SEM). All experimental data are from at least three independent experiments. Differences between groups were evaluated using t test, one way or two-way analysis of variance. Statistical analyses were performed using GraphPad Prism. All tests were considered significant at p < 0.05.

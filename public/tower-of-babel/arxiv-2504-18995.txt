On the spectral identities and fundamental properties of one-sided Drazin inverses in Banach algebras Thanks: This work has been supported by the Natural Science Foundation of Fujian Province, China (Grant No. 2022J01104).
Kai Yan

Abstract




Abstract.
We establish several fundamental properties of one-sided (generalized) Drazin inverses in Banach algebras, including intertwining properties and reverse order laws. In particular, we introduce the concepts of one-sided strongly
\pi
-regularity, which is shown to be equivalent to one-sided Drazin invertibility. By utilizing the Jacobson’s lemma for one-sided regularity, we prove the Jacobson’s lemma for one-sided (generalized) Drazin invertibility. These results allow us to derive the spectral identities for one-sided (generalized) Drazin invertible spectra in Banach algebras, as well as the spectral identities for Fredholm type operators acting on Banach spaces.

Mathematics Subject Classification.
15A09, 16U99, 47A05.

Key words.
One-sided Drazin invertibility, Jacobson’s lemma, spectral identity.

1
Introduction and preliminary

The concepts of Drazin inverse and generalized Drazin inverse were originally introduced by Drazin in 1958 and Koliha in 1996, respectively. Throughout this paper,
\mathcal{S}
,
\mathcal{R}
and
\mathcal{A}
denote a semigroup, a ring and a Banach algebra, respectively. An element
a\in\mathcal{S}
is said to be
Drazin invertible
if there exist an element
x\in\mathcal{S}
and an integer
j
such that

ax=xa,~~x^{2}a=x,~~a^{j+1}x=a^{j}.

The minimal non-negative integer
j
is referred to as the Drazin index of
a
. The Drazin inverse has found applications in the theory of finite Markov chains, linear systems of differential equations and many other fields of mathematics, see [
[
11
]
]. By extending nilpotent to quasi-nilpotent, Koliha [
[
19
]
] introduced the generalized Drazin inverse in Banach algebras. An element
a\in\mathcal{A}
is said to be
generalized Drazin invertible
if there exists an element
x\in\mathcal{A}
such that

ax=xa,~~x^{2}a=x,~~a-axa\in\mathcal{A}^{qnil}.

where
\mathcal{A}^{qnil}
denotes all the quasi-nilpotent elements in
\mathcal{A}
. In this paper,
\sigma(a)
and
r(a)
denote the spectrum and the spectral radius of
a\in\mathcal{A}
, respectively. The set of all quasi-nilpotent elements in Banach algebra
\mathcal{A}
is defined by
\mathcal{A}^{qnil}=\{a\in\mathcal{A}:r(a)=0\}.

Using the classical Gelfand’s representation theorem, we have
r(ab)\leq r(a)r(b)
when
a,b
are commuting elements in
\mathcal{A}
.

An element
a\in\mathcal{R}
is called
strongly
\pi
-regular
if there exist elements
x,y\in\mathcal{R}
and integers
p,q
such that

xa^{p+1}=a^{p}\makebox{~and~}a^{q+1}y=a^{q},

see [
[
2
]
]. This definition can be restated as follows:
a^{n}\in a^{n+1}\mathcal{R}\cap\mathcal{R}a^{n+1}
for some integer
n\geq 1
. In [
[
2
]
], Azumaya proved that
a\in\mathcal{R}
is strongly
\pi
-regular if and only if there exists an element
b\in\mathcal{R}
such that
ab=ba
and
a^{n}=a^{n+1}b
for some integer
n\geq 1
. Drazin [
[
15
]
] proved the following result (Theorem
1.1
), which Lam and Nielsen referred to as the “Azumaya realization” in [
[
20
]
]. The “Azumaya realization” plays a significant role in studying Jacobson’s lemma for Drazin inverse. One can check [
[
20
]
,
[
27
]
] for more details.

Theorem 1.1
.

([
[
15
]
]) Let
\mathcal{R}
be a ring and
a\in\mathcal{R}
. Then
a
is strongly
\pi
-regular if and only if
a
is Drazin invertible.

Recently, several researchers consider the one-sided analogous of Drazin inverse in Banach algebras and rings, see [
[
9
]
,
[
24
]
,
[

]
]. In [
[

[

] defined the one-side Drazin invertibility in terms of annihilator. These two kinds of definitions are equivalent. In this paper, we primarily employ the notions of one-side Drazin invertibility introduced in [
[

]
], as the concepts in [
[
25
]
] impose fewer restrictions and are therefore easier to handle.

Definition 1.2
.

([
[
25
]
])
An element
a\in\mathcal{A}
is called left Drazin invertible with index
j
, if there exists an element
x\in\mathcal{A}
such that

axa=xa^{2},~~x^{2}a=x,~~xa^{j+1}=a^{j}.

Such
x
is called the left Drazin inverse of
a
. The minimal non-negative integer
j
is referred to the left Drazin index of
a
. Dually, an element
a\in\mathcal{A}
is called right Drazin invertible with index
j
, if there exists an element
y\in\mathcal{A}
such that

aya=a^{2}y,~~ay^{2}=y,~~a^{j+1}y=a^{j}.

Such
y
is called the right Drazin inverse of
a
. The minimal non-negative integer
j
is referred to the right Drazin index of
a
.

On taking left (resp. right) Drazin index
j=1
, we can give the definitions of
left (resp. right) group invertibility
in Banach algebras. Next, we introduce the notions of one-sided generalized Drazin invertible in Banach algebras.

Definition 1.3
.

([
[
25
]
])
An element
a\in\mathcal{A}
is called left generalized Drazin invertible, if there exists an element
x\in\mathcal{A}
such that

axa=xa^{2},~~x^{2}a=x,~~axa-a\in\mathcal{A}^{qnil}.

Such
x
is called the left generalized Drazin inverse of
a
. Dually, an element
a\in\mathcal{A}
is called right generalized Drazin invertible, if there exists an element
y\in\mathcal{A}
such that

aya=a^{2}y,~~ay^{2}=y,~~aya-a\in\mathcal{A}^{qnil}.

Such
y
is called the right generalized Drazin inverse of
a
.

In [
[
25
]
], the author demonstrated the relation between (generalized) Drazin invertibility and one-sided (generalized) Drazin invertibility.

Proposition 1.4
.

([
[
25
]
]) Let
a\in\mathcal{A}
. Then

(i)
a
is both left and right Drazin invertible if and only if
a
is Drazin invertible. Specifically, if
x
is a left Drazin inverse of
a
with index

, and
y
is a right Drazin inverse of

x=y
and

.

(ii)
a
is both left and right generalized Drazin invertible if and only if

is a left generalized Drazin inverse of

is a right generalized Drazin inverse of

.

In [
[
25
]
], numerous equivalent characterizations of one-sided (generalized) Drazin invertibility are demonstrated in context of Banach space operators. For a Banach algebra
\mathcal{A}
, Jacobson’s lemma for invertibility states that
1-ac
is invertible if and only if
1-ca
is invertible where
a,c\in\mathcal{A}
. This leads to the classical spectral identity

\sigma(ac)\backslash\{0\}=\sigma(ca)\backslash\{0\}.

The Jacobson’s lemma for left and right invertibility can be found in [
[
22
]
, Chapter I]. Over the last two decades, much research has been conducted on Jacobson’s lemma for various types of generalized inverses, as see [
[
12
]
,
[
14
]
,
[
20
]
,
[
21
]
,
[
29
]
]. Just as with invertibility, the natural question is whether Jacobson’s lemma holds for one-sided generalized inverses. However, there is hardly any research on Jacobson’s lemma for one-sided generalized inverses. One of the principal contributions of this paper is to establish the Jacobson’s lemma for one-sided regularity and one-sided (generalized) Drazin inverse in Banach algebras.

Furthermore, Drazin [
[
16
]
] introduced a new class of one-sided generalized inverse, namely one-sided
(b,c)
-inverse. Nevertheless, the question of whether Jacobson’s lemma holds for one-sided (b,c)-inverse still remains open. To solve this problem, Drazin proposed seeking an analogue of the “Azumaya realization” for
(b,c)
-inverse, as discussed in [
[
16
]
, Section 7]. Notably, it can be observed that our one-sided Drazin inverse is a subclass of the one-sided
(b,c)
-inverse when
b=c=a^{j}
. In this paper, the author establishes an analogue of the “Azumaya realization” for one-sided Drazin inverse. Thus, examining Jacobson’s lemma for one-sided Drazin inverse may provide a new approach to Drazin’s open question on
(b,c)
-inverse in [
[
16
]
, Section 7].

This article is organized as follows. In Section 2, the author provides several fundamental properties, including intertwining properties and reverse order laws, of one-sided (generalized) Drazin inverse in Banach algebras. In particular, we introduce the concepts of one-sided strongly
\pi
-regularity, which is shown to be equivalent to one-sided Drazin invertibility and is pivotal in examining Jacobson’s lemma for one-sided Drazin invertibility. In Section 3, Jacobson’s lemma for one-sided regularity and one-sided (generalized) Drazin invertibility are established. As a corollary, the spectral identity of element products for one-sided (generalized) Drazin spectrum is obtained (Corollary
3.9
). Surprisingly, the Cline formula is more difficult to prove than the Jacobson’s lemma one-sided (generalized) Drazin invertibility. In Section 4, the spectral identity of two intertwining operators for one-sided (generalized) Drazin spectrum is established (Corollary
4.7
). As applications, in Section 5, we utilize the results from Sections 3 and 4 to derive the spectral identities of some new types of operators, namely

\sigma_{*}(TS)\setminus\{0\}=\sigma_{*}(ST)\setminus\{0\},

where
T,S\in B(X)
and
\sigma_{*}
denotes the left and right B-Fredholm spectra. These spectral identities can be viewed as generalizations of classical conclusions regarding the spectra of one-sided Fredholm operators or one-sided essentially invertible operators (see [
[
22
]
, p. 173, Chapter III, Theorem 6]).

2
Fundamental properties of one-sided (generalized) Drazin inverses

At the beginning of this section, we present the following properties, which are similar to those of one-sided Drazin invertibility as described in [
[
25
]
].

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
a\in\mathcal{A}
. Then

(i)

a
is left generalized Drazin invertible if and only if there exists an element
b\in\mathcal{A}
and an integer
j
such that

aba=ba^{2},~~bab=b^{2}a=b\makebox{~~and~~}aba-a\in\mathcal{A}^{qnil}.

(2.1)

(ii)

a
is right generalized Drazin invertible if and only if there exists an element
c\in\mathcal{A}
and an integer
k
such that

aca=c^{2}a,~~cac=ac^{2}=c\makebox{~~and~~}aca-a\in\mathcal{A}^{qnil}.

(2.2)

Proof.

Suppose that
a
has a left generalized Drazin inverse
x
. Set
b=xax
, then

aba=a(xax)x^{2}a^{3}=(xax)a^{2}=ba^{2},

bab=(xax)a(xax)=x^{3}a^{3}x=xax=b

and

b^{2}a=(xax)^{2}a=(xax)x^{2}a^{2}=xax^{2}a=xax=b.

Moreover, we have

aba-a=a(xax)a-a=axa-a\in\mathcal{A}^{qnil}.

Hence
b
satisfies equation (
2.1
). The computation of the right Drazin invertible case is similar.
∎

Intertwining properties are instrumental in computation of generalized inverses, see [
[
16
]
]. Despite Ren and Jiang’s exploration of the intertwining properties of one-sided Drazin inverses in [
[
24
]
], we still exhibit following result (Proposition
2.2
(i)) because our definitions of one-sided Drazin inverses enable us to observe ‘more’ left or right Drazin inverses.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
a,b\in\mathcal{A}
and
az=zb
for some
z\in\mathcal{A}
.

(i)
If
a
has a left Drazin inverse
x
and
b
has a right Drazin invertible
y
, then
xz=zy
.

(ii)
If
a
has a left generalized Drazin inverse
x
and
b
has a right generalized Drazin invertible
y
, then
xz=zy
.

Proof.

We need only to prove (ii), because the one-sided Drazin inverse of an element must be one-sided generalized Drazin inverse. Suppose that
a\in\mathcal{A}
has a left generalized Drazin inverse
x
and
b
has a right generalized Drazin inverse
y
. For any non-negative integer
n
,

\displaystyle||xz-zy||^{\frac{1}{n}}

\displaystyle=||x^{n+1}a^{n}z-zb^{n}y^{n+1}||^{\frac{1}{n}}

\displaystyle=||x^{n+1}a^{n}z-xazy+xazy-zb^{n}y^{n+1}||^{\frac{1}{n}}

\displaystyle=||x^{n+1}zb^{n}-x^{n+1}a^{n+1}zy+xzb^{n+1}y^{n+1}-a^{n}zy^{n+1}||^{\frac{1}{n}}

\displaystyle=||x^{n+1}zb^{n}-x^{n+1}zb^{n+1}y+xa^{n+1}zy^{n+1}-a^{n}zy^{n+1}||^{\frac{1}{n}}

\displaystyle=||x^{n+1}z(b-byb)^{n}+(axa-a)^{n}zy^{n+1}||^{\frac{1}{n}}

\displaystyle\leq||x^{n+1}z(b-byb)^{n}||^{\frac{1}{n}}+||(axa-a)^{n}zy^{n+1}||^{\frac{1}{n}}

\displaystyle\leq||x||^{\frac{n+1}{n}}||z||^{\frac{1}{n+1}}||(a-aya)^{n}||^{\frac{1}{n}}+||(axa-a)^{n}||^{\frac{1}{n}}||z||^{\frac{1}{n+1}}||y||^{\frac{n+1}{n}}.

Since
b-byb\in\mathcal{A}^{qnil}
and
axa-a\in\mathcal{A}^{qnil}
,
\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}||xz-zy||^{\frac{1}{n}}=0
. Thus
||xz-zy||
must be zero and so
xz=zy
.
∎

Corollary 2.3
.

Let
a,b\in\mathcal{A}
and
az=zb
for some
z\in\mathcal{A}
. If
x
is the Drazin inverse (resp. generalized Drazin inverse) of
a
and
y
is the Drazin inverse (resp. generalized Drazin inverse) of
b
, then
xz=zy
.

Let
a,b\in\mathcal{A}
be both invertible, then
(ab)^{-1}=b^{-1}a^{-1}
. This was extended in [
[
19
]
, Theorem 5.5] to generalized Drazin inverse under the condition
ab=ba
. Such a result was termed the reverse order law of generalized Drazin inverse. In the following, we aim to establish one-sided version of these results. Recall that the
commutant
and
double commutant
of an element
a
are defined by

\makebox{comm}(a)=\{x\in\mathcal{A}:~xa=ax\}

and

\makebox{comm}^{2}(a)=\{x\in\mathcal{A}:~xy=yx\makebox{~for~all~}y\in\makebox{comm}(a)\}.

It is well-known that if
x
is the (generalized) Drazin inverse of
a\in\mathcal{A}
, then
x\in
comm
{}^{2}(a)
.

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
a,b\in\mathcal{A}
and
a\in
comm
{}^{2}(b)
.

(i)
If
a
has the Drazin inverse
x
and
b
has a left (resp. right) Drazin inverse
y
, then
yx
is a left (resp. right) Drazin inverse of
ab
.

(ii)
If
a
has the generalized Drazin inverse
x
and
b
has a left (resp. right) generalized Drazin inverse
y
, then then
yx
is a left (resp. right) generalized Drazin inverse of
ab
.

Proof.

We only prove the case of the left generalized Drazin inverse. Suppose that
x
is the Drazin inverse of
a
and
y
is one of the left generalized Drazin inverses of
b
. Since
b(yb)=(yb)b
and
a\in
comm
{}^{2}(b)
, we have
a(yb)=(yb)a
. Furthermore, as
x\in
comm
{}^{2}(a)
and
ab=ba
, we can deduce that
xb=bx
. Therefore

\displaystyle ab(yx)ab

\displaystyle=abybxa=ayb^{2}xa=ybabxa=yx(ab)(ab)

and

(yx)^{2}(ab)=yxyxab=yxybxa=yybx^{2}a=yx.

Moreover,

\displaystyle ab-yx(ab)^{2}=(a-a^{2}x)(b-yb^{2})+a^{2}x(b-yb^{2})+yb^{2}(a-a^{2}x).

By commutativity, we have

\displaystyle r(ab-yx(ab)^{2})\leq

\displaystyle r(a-a^{2}x)r(b-yb^{2})+r(a^{2}x)r(b-yb^{2})+

\displaystyle r(yb^{2})r(a-a^{2}x).

Hence
r(ab-yx(ab)^{2})=0
, which implies that
ab-yx(ab)^{2}
is quasi-nilpotent.
∎

We are temporarily unable to extend the definition of the one-sided generalized Drazin inverse from Banach algebras to rings, as the analogue of many important properties (such as Proposition
1.4
) of one-sided generalized Drazin inverse are unlikely to be established in the context of associative rings. The definition of one-sided generalized Drazin inverse in the normed algebra
C_{0}(X)=B(X)/F(X)
, which is not a Banach algebra, has been established in [
[
4
]
]. Consequently, all the results concerning one-sided generalized inverse in this paper are valid and meaningful only in Banach algebras, and not in associative rings. It remains an interesting problem to find a suitable definition of one-sided generalized Drazin inverse in associative rings.

In a ring
\mathcal{R}
, an element
a\in\mathcal{R}
is
\pi
-regular
if there exist an element
x
and an integer
n
such that
a^{n}xa^{n}=a^{n}
. The one-sided version of
\pi
-regular is called
left
\pi
-regular
(resp.
right
\pi
-regular
) if there exist an element
x
and an integer
n
such that
xa^{2n}=a^{n}
(resp.
a^{2n}x=a^{n}
), see [
[
2
]
,
[
18
]
]. It can be easily seen that
a\in\mathcal{R}
is left
\pi
-regular (resp. right
\pi
-regular) if and only if there exists an element
x
such that
xa^{n+1}=a^{n}
(resp.
a^{n+1}x=a^{n}
). Then
a\in\mathcal{R}
is strongly
\pi
-regular exactly when
a
is both left and right
\pi
-regular. We now introduce the definitions of the one-sided analogue of Azumaya’s strongly
\pi
-regular.

Definition 2.5
.

An element
a\in\mathcal{R}
is called
left strongly
\pi
-regular
, if there exist an element
x\in\mathcal{R}
and an integer
p
such that

axa=xa^{2},~~~xa^{p+1}=a^{p}.

If such
x
exists, it is called
left inner inverse
of
a
. Dually, an element
a\in\mathcal{A}
is called
right strongly
\pi
-regular
, if there exist an element
y\in\mathcal{A}
and an integer
q
such that

aya=a^{2}y,~~~a^{q+1}y=a^{q}.

If such
y
exists, it is called
right inner inverse
of
a
.

The so called “Azumaya realization” was obtained by Drazin in [
[
15
]
, Theorem 4]. The “Azumaya realization” of bounded linear operators in the context of Banach spaces is discussed in [
[
22
]
, Chapter III]. Subsequently, the following result demonstrates the one-sided version of classical “Azumaya realization” for Drazin inverse.

Theorem 2.6
.

Let
a\in\mathcal{R}
. Then

(i)

a
is left Drazin invertible if and only if
a
is left strongly
\pi
-regular.

(ii)

a
is right Drazin invertible if and only if
a
is right strongly
\pi
-regular.

Proof.

(i) If
a
is left Drazin invertible with index
j
, then it is obvious that
a
is left strongly
\pi
-regular. Conversely, suppose that
a
is left strongly
\pi
-regular, i.e. there exist an element
x
and an integer
p
such that
axa=xa^{2}
and
xa^{p+1}=a^{p}
. Put
c=x^{p+1}a^{p}
, then

aca=ax^{p+1}a^{p}a=x^{p+1}a^{p+2}=(x^{p+1}a^{p})a^{2}=ca^{2}.

and

\displaystyle c^{2}a

\displaystyle=x^{p+1}a^{p}x^{p+1}a^{p}a=x^{2p+2}a^{2p+1}=x^{2p+1}(xa^{p+1})a^{p}

\displaystyle=x^{2p+1}a^{2p}=...=x^{p+1}a^{p}=c.

Moreover

ca^{p+1}=x^{p+1}a^{p}a^{p+1}=x^{p+1}a^{2p+1}=x^{p}a^{2p}=...=a^{p}.

Hence
c
is the left Drazin inverse of
a
with the index
p
.

(ii) The proof is dual to that of (i).
∎

It is evident that if an element
a\in\mathcal{R}
is both left and right strongly
\pi
-regular, then
a
is strongly
\pi
-regular. By Theorems
1.1
and
2.6
, we can immediately obtain the (1) of Theorem
1.4
without any computation.

.

Let
a\in\mathcal{R}

(ii)

Proof.

It follows directly from [

]

3
Jacobson’s lemma for the one-sided regularity and one-sided Drazin invertibility

Throughout this section, we will investigate the Jacobson’s lemma for one-sided regularity and one-sided Drazin invertibility. Let
\mathcal{A}
be a unital Banach algebra and
a,b,c,d\in\mathcal{A}
. To simplify the expressions of formulas, we introduce the notation
\alpha:=1-ac
and
\beta:=1-bd
, which satisfy the equalities

\displaystyle acd=dbd\makebox{~~and~~}dba=aca.

(3.1)

The equalities (
3.1
) were first introduced by the author and his collaborator in [
[
26
]
], and they encompass several interesting special cases:

(I) When
a=d
and
b=c
, the equalities (
3.1
) always hold. Thus,
\alpha
and
\beta
correspond to the original Jacobson’s lemma.

(II) When
a=d
and
b=1
, the equalities (
3.1
) become
aba=a^{2}
, which is of interest to operator theorists; see [
[
1
]
] for instance.

(III) When
a=d
, the equalities (
3.1
) become
aba=aca

\displaystyle=(1-a^{n}pb^{n})^{k}(1+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{2n-1}b^{i})+a^{n}xb^{n}

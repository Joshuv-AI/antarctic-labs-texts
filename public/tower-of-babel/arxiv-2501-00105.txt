A configuration space model for algebraic function spaces To Benson Farb on his 57 th birthday
Oishee Banerjee

Abstract
We prove that the space of algebraic maps between two smooth projective varieties, under certain conditions, admit a configuration space model, thereby obtaining an algebro-geometric analogue of Bendersky-Gitler’s result ( [ BG91 , Theorem 7.1] ) on topological function spaces. Our result should be a thought of as a natural higher dimensional counterpart of [ Ban24 , Theorem 3] .



1 Introduction

Motivation.

The study of spaces of
continuous
maps between two topological spaces, and their surprising connections with configuration spaces, has a rich history spanning several decades. Foundational works by Anderson, Bendersky-Gitler, Snaith, Cohen-May-Taylor, Arone, Ahearn-Kuhn, and others have explored this extensively (see
[
BG91
,
And72
]
, and also
[
AK02
,
Aro99
]
and the references therein). One key result is the stable splitting of function spaces under certain constraints (e.g., connectivity conditions on the range), where the components of the splitting include, among other structures, configuration spaces on the domain.

An exact analogue of this phenomenon for algebraic maps between algebraic varieties is unrealistic due to the rigidity of morphisms between varieties. Nevertheless, in this note, we show that the moduli space of algebraic morphisms between two smooth projective varieties, under certain strong conditions on the range, can, in a sense, admit a configuration space model. This establishes an algebro-geometric analogue of Bendersky-Gitler’s result on the space of continuous maps between topological spaces (
[
BG91
, Theorem 7.1]
). Our result can be seen as a natural higher-dimensional counterpart of
[
Ban24
, Theorem 3]
.

Some remarks and context

1.

Poincare/Koszul duality.
A recurring phenomenon in the study of such algebraic function spaces, as demonstrated in this note, as well as in
[
Ban24
]
, is the appearance of a Koszul-type cochain complex (see (
4.1.5
)) in the analysis of the Poincare dual of
\mathrm{Mor}_{\mathbf{d}}(X,Y)

1.0.1

An affirmative answer has already been provided by Ho (
[
Ho20
]
) in the case
X=\mathbb{P}^{1}
and
Y=\mathbb{P}^{n}
(because in such cases, algebraic maps simply boil down to studying zero-cycles on
X
) and there is paramount evidence of an affirmative answer for curves of higher genera by exploiting algebraic non-abelian Poincare duality in the sense of Gaitsgory-Lurie (
[
GL17
, §3]
). However, a naive translation of the curve-case to higher dimensional domains have several obvious pitfalls, some of which becomes clear as we go through the proof, and discussing the rest would take us too far afield (see e.g.
[
GL19
, §1.5]
).

2.

\mathrm{Mor}_{\mathbf{d}}(X,Y)
vs. its topological analogue
\mathrm{Top}_{\mathbf{d}}(X,Y)
.
In a direction distinct from the study of the relationship between (continuous) function spaces and configuration spaces, significant attention has been given to the comparison of spaces of holomorphic/algebraic functions with that of continuous functions between complex holomorphic manifolds since the ’70s (for a brief history of it see
[
Aum24
, §1.1]
). Two notable examples are: Mostovoy’s work (
[
Mos06
]
) where
X,Y
are both projective spaces, and a recent work of Aumonier’s (see
[
Aum24
]
) where he compares stable homology of
\mathrm{Mor}_{\mathbf{d}}(X,\mathbb{P}^{N})
to that of continuous maps
X\to\mathbb{P}^{N}
for arbitrary smooth projective
X
, thus significantly extending Mostovoy’s result.

If our theorem relating the (cohomology of the) space of algebraic maps to configuration spaces appears somewhat unexpected, it is worth highlighting that results by Anderson, Bendersky-Gitler (
[
And72
,
BG91
]
), when combined with those of Aumonier or Mostovoy, lend credence to such a connection.

Our theorem not only confirms this connection but also strengthens it in two key ways: (a) it demonstrates that the Galois representations or mixed Hodge structures on both sides are preserved, and (b) it underscores the role of the intersection theory of
X
, as evidenced by Equation
1.0.5
. Notably, pulling back a ’configuration space model’ for the space of continuous maps via Segal-type results from Aumonier or Mostovoy offers no insight into Hodge structures. In contrast, our approach—grounded entirely in algebraic geometry—makes these structures explicit and central to the discussion.

The only prior instance of an explicit comparison between the space of algebraic function spaces and configuration spaces appears in the author’s earlier work
[
Ban24
,
Ban22
]
— those were in the case when
X
is a curve.

3.

Adjoint line bundles and point-separation.
The property of separating finitely many points is not numerical property of a line bundle

. They are, however, numerical properties on the

(or, more generally, on

[

]
and the references therein). Which is why explicit bounds like (

4.

On the auxilliary schemes

.
The condition of the ‘auxilliary schemes’

being of

(see paragraph

5.

Divergence of

Whereas the range

often poses insurmountable difficulties, tackling an arbitrary domain is relatively simpler, at least in the context of questions like stable homology of these function spaces— it only needs a part of the intersection theory of

6.

Spaces of sections of vector bundles.
Observing that sections of vector bundles on a smooth projective variety

Method of Proof.
Our proof is sheaf-theoretic, morally similar to
[
Ban24
,
Ban22
]

A fundamental component of our approach is the use of cohomological descent for proper hypercoverings. We construct a natural compactification of

)) augmented on its Poincare dual that admits cohomological descent. At the level of sheaves, this hypercover— via a the passage through the

A minor technical note: we work in the derived

is acyclic. Taking
r=1
we obtain
\mathscr{E}_{0}
is locally free over
X\times\mathrm{Pic}_{\mathbf{d}}(X)
: the fibre of the corresponding vector bundle at a point
(x,L)\in X\times\mathrm{Pic}_{\mathbf{d}}(X)
is
\Gamma(X,L\otimes\mathcal{I}_{x})
, i.e. global sections of
L
that vanish at
x
.

Finally, the observation that
\mathcal{X}_{0}
is, by definition, the projectivization of the vector bundle
\bigoplus_{i=0}^{N}\mathscr{E}_{0}
over
X\times\mathrm{Pic}_{\mathbf{d}}(X)
, concludes the proof of the lemma.

∎

Thanks to Lemma
2.4.4
, the natural map

\displaystyle\mathcal{X}_{0}\to\mathcal{X}_{-1}

\displaystyle\big((L,[s_{0}:\ldots:s_{N}]),x\big)\mapsto(L,[s_{0}:\ldots:s_{N}])

(2.4.15)

is projective, and hence proper; furthermore, its image is clearly
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathbf{d}}(X,\mathbb{P}^{N})
, and

\mathcal{X}_{0}\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal{Z}_{\mathbf{d}}(X,\mathbb{P}^{N})

is birational—because a generic element in
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathbf{d}}(X,\mathbb{P}^{N})
is an
(N+1)
-tuple of sections in
\Gamma(X,L)
for some
L\in\mathrm{Pic}_{\mathbf{d}}(X)
that have exactly one common zero, which, in turn, is because by our assumption
r(\mathbf{d})\geq 1
, i.e.
L
is very ample, and one can always choose
(N+1)
hyperplane sections of the embedding of
X
by the complete linear system
|L|
such that those
(N+1)
hyperplanes intersect at exactly one point— and since
\mathcal{X}_{0}
is smooth, we have now concluded the following:

Lemma 2.4.5
.

The natural map
\mathcal{X}_{0}\to\mathcal{Z}_{\mathbf{d}}(X,\mathbb{P}^{N})
is a resolution of singularities.

Observe that for all
r\geq 0
, we have, by its very definition,

\displaystyle\mathcal{X}_{r}=\underbrace{\mathcal{X}_{0}\times_{\mathcal{X}_{-1}}\mathcal{X}_{0}\times_{\mathcal{X}_{-1}}\times\cdots\times_{\mathcal{X}_{-1}}\mathcal{X}_{0}}_{r+1},

(2.4.16)

and that there are natural maps

\displaystyle\pi_{r}:\mathcal{X}_{r}\to\mathcal{Z}_{\mathbf{d}}(X,\mathbb{P}^{N})

(2.4.17)

\displaystyle\big((L,[s_{0}:\cdots:s_{N}]),(x_{0},\cdots,x_{r})\big)\mapsto(L,[s_{0}:\cdots:s_{N}]).

Recalling the standard fact that proper maps are stable under base change
[
Har77
, Corollary 4.8]
, paired with our earlier observation that
\mathcal{X}_{0}\to\mathcal{X}_{-1}
is projective, and hence proper, we come to the following conclusion:

Lemma 2.4.6
.

The simplicial scheme
\pi_{\bullet}:\mathcal{X}_{\bullet}\to\mathcal{Z}_{\mathbf{d}}(X,\mathbb{P}^{N})
is a proper hypercover augmented over
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathbf{d}}(X,\mathbb{P}^{N})
, with the
i^{th}
face maps
\mathcal{X}_{r}\to\mathcal{X}_{r-1}
given by forgetting the
i^{th}
-factor from the expression (
2.4.16
), and the degeneracy maps given by the diagonal embeddings.

The proof of Lemma
2.4.4
translates almost verbatim to give us the geometry of
\mathcal{X}_{r}
for any
r\leq r(\mathbf{d})
. For
0<r\leq r(\mathbf{d})
, the schemes
\mathcal{X}_{r}
are not smooth, however, they are naturally projectivizations of coherent sheaves that are locally free over the locally closed strata provided by the diagonals in
X^{r}
. Indeed, let

\displaystyle\mathrm{pr}_{23}:X^{r+1}\times X\times\mathrm{Pic}_{\mathbf{d}}(X)\to X\times\mathrm{Pic}_{\mathbf{d}}(X)

(2.4.18)

be the projection to the last two factors,

\displaystyle\mathrm{pr}_{13}:X^{r+1}\times X\times\mathrm{Pic}_{\mathbf{d}}(X)\to X^{r+1}\times\mathrm{Pic}_{\mathbf{d}}(X)

(2.4.19)

be the projection to the first and third factors, and

\displaystyle\mathrm{pr}_{23}:X^{r+1}\times X\times\mathrm{Pic}_{\mathbf{d}}(X)\to X^{r+1}\times X

(2.4.20)

be the projection to the first two factors, and let

be the closed subscheme given by

D_{r}(X):=\bigg\{\big((x_{0},\cdots,x_{r}),x\big):x=x_{i}\text{ for some }0\leq i\leq r\bigg\}

Then define

\displaystyle\mathscr{E}_{r}:=(\mathrm{pr}_{13})_{*}(\mathrm{pr}^{*}_{12}\mathcal{I}_{{D_{0}}(X)}\otimes\mathrm{pr}^{*}_{23}\mathcal{P}(\mathbf{d})).

(2.4.21)

where
\mathcal{I}_{{D_{r}}(X)}
is the ideal sheaf of
D_{r}(X)\subset X^{r+1}\times X
. Then, following through the proof of Lemma
2.4.4
and invoking the sequences (
2.4.13
) and (
2.4.14
), we see that
\mathscr{E}_{r}
is locally free on each of the locally closed strata provided by the intersection of the diagonals, its stalk at a point

\big((x_{0},\cdots,x_{r}),L\big)\in X^{r+1}\times\mathrm{Pic}_{\mathbf{d}}(X)

(note that
x_{i}
s need not be distinct) is

\Gamma(X,L\otimes\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{Supp(\sum_{i}x_{i})}})

where
\mathrm{Supp}(\sum_{i}x_{i})
is the (set-theoretic) support of the
0
-cycle
\sum_{i}x_{i}
corresponding the
(r+1)
-tuple

of points in

; in turn, if

is defined as the locus of

-tuples of points of which exactly

are distinct, then we have the following:

Lemma 2.4.7
.

For all
0\leq r\leq r(\mathbf{d})
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{X}_{r}=\underline{\mathrm{Proj}}_{X^{r+1}\times\mathrm{Pic}_{\mathbf{d}}(X)}\mathrm{Sym}(\mathscr{E}_{r}^{\oplus(N+1)})

(2.4.22)

where

is a stratified vector bundle: for

, on each locally closed strata

, it is a vector bundle of rank

.

We let
\mathring{X}^{r+1}
denote
X_{(r+1)}^{r+1}={X}^{r+1}-\text{ diagonals}
.

Remark 2.4.8
.

The notion of a stratified vector bundle has several approaches: all minor variants of each other, depending on what one’s end goal is. A modern reference which speaks of its development and recent applications is by Ross (
[
Ros24
]
). In our case, the meaning has been made clear by specifying the strata explicitly, which does not require any outside knowledge on the theory of stratified vector bundles. The interested reader can refer to
[

]
and the references therein.

2.5.4

Leray-Hirsch type.
As noted earlier, for an arbitrary
Y
there is no guarantee the schemes
\mathrm{Mor}_{\mathbf{d}}(X,Y)
or
\mathcal{X}_{r}(Y)
are nonempty, and even if they are, there is no general recipe to understand their geometry. Note that for
Y=\mathbb{P}^{N}
, Lemma
2.4.7
not only implies

is a stratified fibre bundle in a range of values of

, but being projectivization of vector bundles on those strata, it satisfies the Leray-Hirsch theorem.

Throughout this paper we work on basis of the assumption that
\mathrm{Mor}_{\mathbf{d}}(X,Y)
is nonempty, and that
\mathcal{X}_{r}(Y)
, for all
r\leq r(\mathbf{d})
, is nonempty, and satisfies
\mathbf{Q}
-Leray-Hirsch (i.e. it satisfies the Leray-Hirsch theorem with coefficients in
\mathbf{Q}
) on each locally closed strata
X_{(m)}^{r+1}\times\mathrm{Pic}_{\mathbf{d}}(X)\subset X^{r+1}\times\mathrm{Pic}_{\mathbf{d}}(X)
for
1\leq m\leq r(\mathbf{d})
. Let
Y(D_{r})
denote the fibre of
\mathcal{X}_{r}(Y)
over
\mathring{X}^{r+1}\times\mathrm{Pic}_{\mathbf{d}}(X)
(where, recall that
\mathring{X}^{r+1}=X^{r+1}-\text{ diagonals}
).

Remark 2.5.5
.

There are no sufficient conditions one can impose on a reasonably large class of
Y
s to result in
\mathcal{X}_{r}(Y)
carrying the structure of a stratified fibre bundle a la Lemma
2.4.7
, let alone satisfy Leray-Hirsch. An analogue of Lemma
2.4.7
is not hard to prove when

carries a nice universal torsor description (see the proof of
[
Ban22
, Theorem 1]
, and also works on Manin’s conjecture in
[
BT98
]
,
[
Pie16
]
and the references therein). However, for a general

, a proof of such a fact, when true, is largely elusive, and is deeply rooted in the theory of algebraic cycles in

— for a detailed discussion on algebraic fibre bundles being of

type, and its relation to Hodge conjecture, see Meng’s work in
[
Men21
]
.

3 Symmetric (co)simplicial sheaves

By Lemma
2.4.6
, the hypercover defined in (
2.4.16
) admits cohomological descent— which is really the main ingredient for proving Theorem
1.0.1
. However, we study the hypercover (
2.4.16
) as a
\Delta S
-scheme, as opposed to a simplicial scheme, where
\Delta S
denotes
symmetric simplicial category
. The advantage of working over
\Delta S
, instead of the standard simplicial category
\Delta
, is that the second statement of Theorem
1.0.1
almost comes for free; however, working over
\Delta S
results in considering (co)invariants under group actions in the derived category of constructible sheaves, an operation that do not behave well in triangulated categories.

In this section, we collect the necessary facts about
\Delta S
, and then overcome the obstacle of working in the derived category of constructible sheaves by rephrasing and using cohomological descent in the
\infty
-category of sheaves as developed in
[
GL19
]
.

3.1
\Delta S
-hypercovers

The category
\Delta S
has appeared in various forms throughout the literature, often studied independently with different motivations. Notable examples include Feigin and Tsygan’s work on additive K-theory (
[
FT87
]
), Pirashvili and Richter’s reinterpretation of
\Delta S
as the category of
non-commutative sets
(
[
PR02
]
), and investigations into
symmetric homology
by Fiedorowicz and Loday (
[
FL91
]
), as well as Krasauskas’s independent development of related ideas (
[
Kra87
]
).

In this note, we draw upon the framework for the
symmetric simplicial category

\Delta S
established in the context of hypercovers in
[
Ban24
, §2]
, which, in turn, is primarily based on the notations set up in
[
FL91
]
, quoting only the results needed here. For further details, the interested reader can consult the relevant sections of
loc. cit
.

3.1.1
Observe that by
[
Ban24
, Definition 2.1]
, the simplicial scheme
\mathcal{X}_{\bullet}
is a
\Delta S
-object in the category of schemes. Indeed, the symmetric group
S_{r+1}
acts freely on
\mathcal{X}_{r}
by permuting the factors of the fibre product in (
2.4.16
), and one immediately sees that the action maps compose with the face and degeneracy maps of the simplicial scheme
\mathcal{X}_{r}
as per the requirements of
[
Ban24
, Definition 2.1]
. Paired with Lemma
2.4.6
we obtain the following:

Lemma 3.1.2
.

The proper hypercover
\pi_{\bullet}:\mathcal{X}_{\bullet}\to\mathcal{Z}_{\mathbf{d}}(X,\mathbb{P}^{N})
is a
\Delta S
-scheme augmented over
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathbf{d}}(X,\mathbb{P}^{N})
, with the
i^{th}
face map
\mathcal{X}_{r}\to\mathcal{X}_{r-1}
given by forgetting the
i^{th}
-factor from the expression (
2.4.16
), and the degeneracy maps
\mathcal{X}_{r-1}\to\mathcal{X}_{r}
given by the diagonal embeddings.

3.1.3  Sheaves on schemes

For a scheme
B
, let
\mathrm{Shv}(B)
be the derived
\infty
-category of sheaves with coefficients in
\mathbf{Q}
-vector spaces (where
\mathbf{Q}
is a field of characteristic
0
that contains the rational numbers), equipped with Grothendieck’s six functor formalism. It is a stable
\infty
-category with a
t
-structure, whose heart is the abelian category of constructible sheaves of
\mathbf{Q}
-vector spaces on
B
.

For details on the construction of the sheaf theory we use in this section, the reader is directed to Gaitsgory-Lurie’s work (
[
GL19
, Sections 2 and 3]
). For homological algebra on
\mathrm{Shv}(B)
the reader may refer to
[
Lur17
, §1.2, §1.3]
. For the theory of proper descent in this setup, see Liu-Zheng’s
[
LZ24
]
.

3.1.4
\Delta S
-sheaves

A
\Delta S
-sheaf on a space
B
is a functor

\mathcal{F}:\Delta S\to\mathrm{Shv}(B).

As is customary, we denote such a sheaf by
\mathcal{F}_{\bullet}
. Note that a
\Delta S
-sheaf is naturally a
\Delta
-sheaf i.e. a cosimplicial object in
\mathrm{Shv}(B)
; henceforth, unless otherwise stated, notations like
\mathcal{F}_{\bullet}
will always mean a
\Delta S
-sheaf.

For
\Delta S
-sheaves
\mathcal{F}_{\bullet}
and
\mathcal{G}_{\bullet}
, let
\mathrm{Hom}^{\Delta S}_{\mathrm{Shv}(B)}(\mathcal{F}_{\bullet},\mathcal{G}_{\bullet})
(respectively,
\mathrm{Hom}^{\Delta}_{\mathrm{Shv}(B)}(\mathcal{F}_{\bullet},\mathcal{G}_{\bullet})
) denote the
\Delta S
-sheaf (respectively,
\Delta
-sheaf) given by maps
\mathcal{F}_{n}\to\mathcal{G}_{n}
in
\mathrm{Shv}(B)
that commute with the face and degeneracy maps in
\Delta S
(respectively,
\Delta
).

Since a
\Delta S
-sheaf
\mathcal{F}_{\bullet}
is a naturally a
\Delta
-sheaf, we can consider the corresponding Moore cochain complex, which we denote by

\displaystyle C^{*}\bigg((\mathcal{F}_{n},d)\bigg)

(3.1.1)

where the differentials are, by definition, given by the alternating sum of face maps:
d=\sum_{i}(-1)^{i}d_{i}
(see
[
Lur17
, remarks 1.2.4.3 and 1.2.4.4]
). Moreover, for a
\Delta S
-sheaf
\mathcal{F}_{\bullet}
, we get a
twisted-by-sign sheaf
for each
n
:

\big(\mathcal{F}_{n}\otimes\mathit{sgn}_{S_{n+1}}\big)^{S_{n+1}}

where the natural action of
S_{n+1}
on
\mathcal{F}_{n}
is twisted by a sign
(see a similar construction in the discussion preceding
[
FL91
, Theorem 6.9]
). This supplies us with a cochain complex

\displaystyle C^{*}\Big(\big(\mathcal{F}_{n}\otimes\mathit{sgn}_{S_{n+1}}\big)^{S_{n+1}},d\Big)

(3.1.2)

where the differential
d
is given by the alternating sum of face maps:
d=\sum_{i}(-1)^{i}d_{i}
. The next lemma focuses on relating these two.

Lemma 3.1.5
.

Let
\mathcal{F}_{\bullet}
be a
\Delta S
-sheaf on a scheme
B
. Then the following surjection is an isomorphism:

\displaystyle C^{*}\bigg((\mathcal{F}_{n},d)\bigg)\xrightarrow{\cong}C^{*}\Big(\big(\mathcal{F}_{n}\otimes\mathit{sgn}\big)^{S_{n+1}},d\Big)

(3.1.3)

Proof of Lemma
3.1.5
.

In the case when the
\Delta S
-sheaf is in the abelian category of constructible sheaves, this is proved in
[
Ban24
, Lemma 2.7]
by a straightforward and direct adaptation of the proof of
[
FL91
, Corollary 6.17]
.

The proof of
[
FL91
, Corollary 6.17]
readily translates to an
\infty
-categorical analogue in
\mathrm{Shv}(B)
. Indeed, like
[
FL91
, §6]
, we consider the co-bisimplicial sheaf

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{p,q}:=\mathbf{Q}[(S_{p})^{q}]\otimes\mathcal{F}_{q}

(3.1.4)

which converges to
\pi_{n}(\mathrm{Hom}^{\Delta S}_{\mathrm{Shv}(B)}(\underline{\mathbf{Q}}_{B},\mathcal{F}_{\bullet}))
(the latter is equivalent to what Fiederowicz-Loday call ‘symmetric (co)homology’, see
[
FL91
, §6.6]
).

The sheaf
\mathcal{G}_{p,q}
is a filtered object in
\mathrm{Shv}(B)
in the sense of
[
Lur17
, Lemma 1.2.2.4]
, which allows us to adapt the proof of
[
FL91
, Theorem 6.9]
: taking its horizontal filtration, and noting that
\mathbf{Q}
is a field of characteristic
0
, we obtain—

E_{1}^{p,q}=\begin{cases}\big(\mathcal{F}_{p}\otimes\mathit{sgn}\big)^{S_{p+1}}&q=0\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

(3.1.5)

with differentials, naturally, given by the alternating sum of face maps.

On the other hand,
\mathcal{F}_{\bullet}
, now being considered a
\Delta
-sheaf, via the
\infty
-categorical Dold-Kan (
[
Lur17
, Theorem 1.2.4.1]
)
results in the cochain complex
C^{*}\Big(\big(\mathcal{F}_{p},d\big)\Big)
which converges to
\pi_{n}(\mathrm{Hom}^{\Delta}_{\mathrm{Shv}(B)}(\underline{\mathbf{Q}}_{B},\mathcal{F}_{\bullet}))
.

By the cohomological analogue of
[
FL91
, Theorem 6.16]
, the two cohomologies are isomorphic, i.e.

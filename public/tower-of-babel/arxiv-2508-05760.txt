On the Maximum Spread of Directed Graphs
Susie Lu, John Urschel

Abstract
. Given a directed graph G G , the spread of G G is the largest distance between any two eigenvalues of its adjacency matrix. In 2022, Breen, Riasanovsky, Tait, and Urschel asked what n n -vertex directed graph maximizes spread, and whether this graph is undirected. We prove the more general result that the spread of any n × n n\times n non-negative matrix A A with ‖ A ‖ max ≤ 1 \|A\|_{\max}\leq 1 is at most 2 ​ n / 3 2n/\sqrt{3} , which is tight up to an additive factor and exact when n n is a multiple of three. Furthermore, our results show that the matrix with maximum spread is always symmetric.

The spread of an
n\times n
complex matrix
M
is the diameter of its spectrum
\Lambda(M)
: that is,

\mathrm{diam}(\Lambda(M))=\max_{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}\in\Lambda(M)}|\lambda-\lambda^{\prime}|.

General upper and lower bounds on
s(M)
have been established by Mirsky
[
24
, Theorem 2]
and by Johnson, Kumar, and Wolkowicz
[
14
, Theorem 2.1]
.

Consider a (possibly directed) graph
G=(V,E)
of order
n
. The adjacency matrix
A
of
G
is the
n\times n
matrix whose rows and columns are indexed by the vertices of
G
, with entries satisfying
A_{i,j}=1
if
(i,j)\in E
and
A_{i,j}=0
otherwise. The spread of the adjacency matrix of a graph
G
is

\mathrm{diam}(\Lambda(A))=\max_{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}\in\Lambda(A)}|\lambda-\lambda^{\prime}|,

which is called
the spread of the graph
G
. In the special case when the graph
G
is undirected, the adjacency matrix is symmetric, so its eigenvalues are real and can be ordered
\lambda_{1}(A)\geq\lambda_{2}(A)\geq\dots\geq\lambda_{n}(A)
. Then,
\mathrm{diam}(\Lambda(A))=\lambda_{1}(A)-\lambda_{n}(A)
. In 2001, Gregory, Hershkowitz, and Kirkland initiated a detailed analysis of the spread of a graph
[
13
]
, leading to a large literature on the subject under various conditions and matrix representations. See, for instance,
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
7
,
9
,
10
,
15
,
17
,
18
,
19
,
20
,
22
,
25
,
27
,
28
]
and the references therein.

In 2022, Breen, Riasanovsky, Tait, and Urschel characterized the graphs with maximum spread among all
n
-vertex undirected graphs, both allowing and not allowing loops
[
5
]
, which solved a main conjecture of Gregory, Hershkowitz, and Kirkland for
n
sufficiently large. To state their results, we let
\|A\|_{\max}
denote the largest magnitude entry in a matrix
A
, and
A\otimes B
denote the Kronecker product of two matrices
A
and
B
.

Theorem 1

(
[
5
, Theorem 1.1 & 1.3, Corollary 1.4]
)
.

Let
A
be an
n\times n
symmetric non-negative matrix. Then

\lambda_{1}(A)-\lambda_{n}(A)\leq\frac{2\,n}{\sqrt{3}}\,\|A\|_{\max},

with equality if and only if
n=0\bmod 3
and
A=P_{\pi}\left(\begin{pmatrix}1&1&1\\
1&1&1\\
1&1&0\end{pmatrix}\otimes J_{\frac{n}{3}}\right)P_{\pi}
, where
J_{k}
is the
k\times k
all-ones matrix and
P_{\pi}
is a permutation matrix. Furthermore:

(1)

Any matrix
A
that maximizes
\big(\lambda_{1}(A)-\lambda_{n}(A)\big)
over all
n\times n
symmetric non-negative matrices with
\|A\|_{\max}=1
is a
0-1
matrix and has spread at least
2n/\sqrt{3}-1/(2\sqrt{3}n)
.

(2)

The matrix
A
that maximizes
\big(\lambda_{1}(A)-\lambda_{n}(A)\big)
over all
n\times n
zero-diagonal symmetric non-negative matrices with
\|A\|_{\max}=1
is exactly the adjacency matrix of the join of a clique on
\lfloor 2n/3\rfloor
vertices and an independent set on
\lceil n/3\rceil
vertices for all
n
sufficiently large. This matrix always has spread at least
(2n-1)/\sqrt{3}
for
n>1
.

In the same paper, the authors detailed a number of open problems, beginning with the following:

“What digraph of order
n
maximizes the spread of its adjacency matrix … Is this
more general problem also maximized by the same set of graphs as in the undirected
case?”
[
5
, Sec. 8]

While many refinements and related results have been produced recently
[

,
12
,
16
,
21
,

]
, the above question remains open. Here, we answer this question in the affirmative, generalizing Theorem
1
to all non-negative matrices:

Theorem 2

(Directed Spread Theorem)
.

Let
A
be an
n\times n
non-negative matrix. Then

\mathrm{diam}\big(\Lambda(A)\big)\leq\frac{2\,n}{\sqrt{3}}\,\|A\|_{\max},

with equality if and only if
n=0\bmod 3
and
A=P_{\pi}\left(\begin{pmatrix}1&1&1\\
1&1&1\\
1&1&0\end{pmatrix}\otimes J_{\frac{n}{3}}\right)P_{\pi}
, where
J_{k}
is the
k\times k
all-ones matrix and
P_{\pi}
is a permutation matrix. Furthermore,

(1)

the matrix
A
that maximizes
\mathrm{diam}\big(\Lambda(A)\big)/\|A\|_{\max}
over all
n\times n
non-negative matrices is symmetric for all
n
, and

(2)

the matrix
A
that maximizes
\mathrm{diam}\big(\Lambda(A)\big)/\|A\|_{\max}
over all
n\times n
zero-diagonal non-negative matrices is exactly the adjacency matrix of the join of a clique on
\lfloor 2n/3\rfloor
vertices and an independent set on
\lceil n/3\rceil
vertices for all
n
sufficiently large.

In contrast to Breen, Riasanovsky, Tait, and Urschel’s proof of Theorem
1
(which involves nearly
70
pages of complicated mathematics and computation), the proof of Theorem
2
(given Theorem
1
) is incredibly simple and intuitive. The main idea is that, while the use of the complex plane allows for larger distances in theory, complex eigenvalues come in pairs, making them slightly too “expensive” for maximizing the spread of eigenvalues.

The key ingredient in our proof is the following lemma regarding the maximum magnitude and real part of non-real eigenvalues of non-negative matrices, which may be of independent interest.

Lemma 3
.

Let
A
be an
n\times n
non-negative matrix with
\|A\|_{\max}\leq 1
. Any eigenvalue
\mu
of
A
with
\mathrm{Im}(\mu)\neq 0
satisfies

(1)

|\mu|^{2}\leq\min\left\{\frac{n^{2}+n\lambda_{\max}-2\lambda_{\max}^{2}-\gamma}{4},\;\lambda_{\max}^{2}\right\}

and

(2)

\mathrm{Re}(\mu)^{2}\leq\min\left\{\frac{n^{2}+3n\lambda_{\max}-4\lambda_{\max}^{2}-\gamma}{8},\;\lambda_{\max}^{2}\right\},

where
\lambda_{\max}
is the Perron eigenvalue of
A
and

\gamma:=n\lambda_{\max}-\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\min\{A_{ij},A_{ji}\}+\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\left(1+\min\{A_{ij},A_{ji}\}-A_{ij}^{2}-A_{ji}^{2}\right)\geq 0.

Proof of Lemma
3
.

First, we note that, by Perron-Frobenius,
\mathrm{Re}(\mu)^{2}\leq|\mu|^{2}\leq\lambda_{\max}^{2}
, immediately handling half of Inequalities
1
and
2
.

Next, we verify that
\gamma\geq 0
and prove Inequality
1
. Consider the matrix
\hat{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
with
\hat{A}_{ij}=\min\{A_{ij},A_{ji}\}
for all
i,j\in[n]
. We may express
\|A\|_{F}^{2}
in terms of
n
,
\lambda_{\max}
, and
\gamma
:

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}A_{ij}^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(n^{2}+n\lambda_{\max}-\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}(1+\min\{A_{ij},A_{ji}\}-A_{ij}^{2}-A_{ji}^{2})-(n\lambda_{\max}-\bm{1}^{T}\hat{A}\bm{1})\right)

(3)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(n^{2}+n\lambda_{\max}-\gamma).

The quantity
\gamma
is the sum of the two addends
n\lambda_{\max}-\bm{1}^{T}\hat{A}\bm{1}
and
\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}(1+\min\{A_{ij},A_{ji}\}-A_{ij}^{2}-A_{ji}^{2})
. The former is non-negative by the non-decreasing property of the spectral radius
[
23
, Corollary 2.1]
and the Courant-Fischer theorem:

\lambda_{\max}(A)\geq\lambda_{\max}(\hat{A})\geq\frac{\textbf{1}^{T}\hat{A}\textbf{1}}{\textbf{1}^{T}\textbf{1}}=\frac{\textbf{1}^{T}\hat{A}\textbf{1}}{n},

and the latter is non-negative because
\|A\|_{\max}\leq 1
. The lower bound

\|A\|_{F}^{2}\geq\sum_{i=1}^{n}|\lambda_{i}|^{2}=\lambda_{\max}^{2}+2|\mu|^{2}+\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda(A)\setminus\{\lambda_{\max},\mu,\overline{\mu}\}}|\lambda|^{2}

combined with Equation
3
produces the remainder of Inequality
1
:

|\mu|^{2}\leq\frac{\|A\|_{F}^{2}-\lambda_{\max}^{2}}{2}\leq\frac{n^{2}+n\lambda_{\max}-2\lambda_{\max}^{2}-\gamma}{4}.

Furthermore, we may also bound the sum of the squared moduli of the remaining eigenvalues:

\displaystyle\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda(A)\setminus\{\lambda_{\max},\mu,\overline{\mu}\}}|\lambda|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|A\|_{F}^{2}-\lambda_{\max}^{2}-2|\mu|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{n^{2}+n\lambda_{\max}-\gamma}{2}-\lambda_{\max}^{2}-2|\mu|^{2}

(4)

\displaystyle=\frac{n^{2}+n\lambda_{\max}-2\lambda_{\max}^{2}-\gamma}{2}-2|\mu|^{2}.

Finally, using the trace of
A^{2}
, we prove the remainder of Inequality
2
. We have

\mathrm{trace}(A^{2})=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}A_{ij}A_{ji}\leq\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\min\{A_{ij},A_{ji}\}\leq n\lambda_{\max}.

On the other hand, the trace of
A^{2}
can also be written in terms of eigenvalues, giving the lower bound

\displaystyle\mathrm{trace}(A^{2})

\displaystyle=\lambda_{\max}^{2}+\mu^{2}+\overline{\mu}^{2}+\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda(A)\setminus\{\lambda_{\max},\mu,\overline{\mu}\}}\lambda^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\lambda_{\max}^{2}+\mu^{2}+\overline{\mu}^{2}-\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda(A)\setminus\{\lambda_{\max},\mu,\overline{\mu}\}}|\lambda|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\lambda_{\max}^{2}+\mu^{2}+\overline{\mu}^{2}+2|\mu|^{2}-\frac{n^{2}+n\lambda_{\max}-2\lambda_{\max}^{2}-\gamma}{2}\qquad\text{by Inequality \ref{ineq:other-eig}}

\displaystyle=4\mathrm{Re}(\mu)^{2}-\frac{n^{2}+n\lambda_{\max}-4\lambda_{\max}^{2}-\gamma}{2}.

Combining the lower and upper bounds for
\mathrm{trace}(A^{2})
yields our desired result

\displaystyle 4\mathrm{Re}(\mu)^{2}-\frac{n^{2}+n\lambda_{\max}-4\lambda_{\max}^{2}-\gamma}{2}\leq n\lambda_{\max}

\displaystyle\implies

\displaystyle 4\mathrm{Re}(\mu)^{2}\leq n\lambda_{\max}+\frac{n^{2}+n\lambda_{\max}-4\lambda_{\max}^{2}-\gamma}{2}

\displaystyle\implies

\displaystyle\mathrm{Re}(\mu)^{2}\leq\frac{n^{2}+3n\lambda_{\max}-4\lambda_{\max}^{2}-\gamma}{8}.

∎

Lemma
3
immediately implies that for any eigenvalue
\mu
with
\mathrm{Im}(\mu)\neq 0
, its modulus and real part are bounded by constant multiples of
n
.

Corollary 4
.

Let
A
be an
n\times n
non-negative matrix with
\|A\|_{\max}\leq 1
. Any eigenvalue
\mu
of
A
with
\mathrm{Im}(\mu)\neq 0
satisfies

|\mu|\leq\frac{n}{2}\qquad\text{and}\qquad|\mathrm{Re}(\mu)|\leq\frac{3+\sqrt{57}}{24}n.

Proof.

The desired result follows immediately from Lemma
3
and the observations that

\max_{x\in[0,1]}\min\left\{\frac{1+x-2x^{2}}{4},x^{2}\right\}=\frac{1}{4}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\max_{x\in[0,1]}\min\left\{\frac{1+3x-4x^{2}}{8},x^{2}\right\}=\left(\frac{3+\sqrt{57}}{24}\right)^{2},

achieved at
x=1/2
and
x=(3+\sqrt{57})/24
, respectively.

∎

For the proof of Theorem

, we also require the following four lemmas.

Lemma 5

(Bendixson’s inequality,
[

, Theorem II]
)
.

Let
A\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
. Any eigenvalue
\lambda
of
A
satisfies

\lambda_{\min}\left(\frac{A+A^{T}}{2}\right)\leq\mathrm{Re}(\lambda)\leq\lambda_{\max}\left(\frac{A+A^{T}}{2}\right)
.

Lemma 6
.

Let
A
be an
n\times n
non-negative matrix with
\|A\|_{\max}\leq 1
. Then the real part of every eigenvalue of

is at least
-n/2
.

Proof.

This follows immediately from the standard bound
\lambda_{\min}(B)\geq-n/2
for
n\times n
symmetric non-negative matrices with entries of magnitude at most one
[

and Lemma

.
∎

.

The matrices
\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\

,
\begin{pmatrix}1&1&1\\

Lemma 8

(
[

.

Let
A\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
,
U^{*}AU=D+N

\min_{\lambda\in\Lambda(A)}|\lambda-\mu|\leq\|E\|_{2}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\|N\|_{2}^{k}.

Using the above lemmas, we are now prepared to prove Theorem

Proof of Theorem
2
.

Suppose, contrary to the theorem statement, that there exists an
n\times n
non-negative non-symmetric matrix
A
with
\|A\|_{\max}=1
that maximizes
\mathrm{diam}\big(\Lambda(A)\big)/\|A\|_{\max}
over either all
n\times n
non-negative matrices or all
n\times n
zero-diagonal matrices. By Lemma
5
,

\max_{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}\in\Lambda(A)}\big|\mathrm{Re}(\lambda)-\mathrm{Re}(\lambda^{\prime})\big|\leq\mathrm{diam}\left(\Lambda\left(\frac{A+A^{T}}{2}\right)\right).

Since
A
is non-symmetric,
(A+A^{T})/2
is not a
0-1
matrix. Therefore, by Theorem
1
, the maximum
\mathrm{diam}(\Lambda(A))
is not achieved by a pair of real-valued eigenvalues. In addition, by Corollary
4
, the modulus of a non-real eigenvalue is at most
\frac{n}{2}
, so
|\lambda-\lambda^{\prime}|\leq n
for any two non-real eigenvalues
\lambda
and
\lambda^{\prime}
. We note that
n
is less than both
\frac{2n}{\sqrt{3}}-\frac{1}{2\sqrt{3}n}
and
\frac{2n-1}{\sqrt{3}}
for
n\geq 4
, and by Lemma
7
is non-optimal for
n<4
. Therefore,
\mathrm{diam}(\Lambda(A))
cannot be achieved by a pair of non-real eigenvalues either, and we need only consider the case where one eigenvalue is real and the other is non-real.

Let
\lambda
be the non-real eigenvalue and
\lambda^{\prime}
be the real one. By Lemma
6
,
\lambda^{\prime}\geq-\frac{n}{2}
. If
\lambda^{\prime}\leq\frac{n}{2}
, then both
\lambda
and
\lambda^{\prime}
lie in the disk of radius
\frac{n}{2}
centered at the origin, so
|\lambda-\lambda^{\prime}|\leq n
, which is not the maximum spread. Otherwise, if
\lambda^{\prime}>\frac{n}{2}
, then
|\lambda-\lambda^{\prime}|
is less than
|\lambda-\lambda_{\max}|
, where
\lambda_{\max}
is the Perron eigenvalue. Therefore,
\mathrm{diam}(\Lambda(A))
must be achieved between the Perron eigenvalue, denoted by
\lambda_{\max}
, and some non-real eigenvalue, denoted by
\lambda
.

By Lemma
3
,

\displaystyle|\lambda_{\max}-\lambda|^{2}

\displaystyle=\lambda_{\max}^{2}-2\lambda_{\max}\,\mathrm{Re}(\lambda)+|\lambda|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\lambda_{\max}^{2}+2\lambda_{\max}\sqrt{\frac{n^{2}+3n\lambda_{\max}-4\lambda_{\max}^{2}-\gamma}{8}}+\frac{n^{2}+n\lambda_{\max}-2\lambda_{\max}^{2}-\gamma}{4}

\displaystyle=n^{2}f(\lambda_{\max}/n,\gamma/n^{2}),

where

f(x,\eta):=x^{2}+2x\sqrt{\frac{1+3x-4x^{2}-\eta}{8}}+\frac{1+x-2x^{2}-\eta}{4}.

By inspection, we have
f(x,0)<1.315609<4/3
for

\lambda_{\max}
equal to

times the largest root of

. Therefore,

(5)

\mathrm{diam}\big(\Lambda(A)\big)=|\lambda_{\max}-\lambda|<\sqrt{1.315609\,n^{2}}=1.147\,n.

We note that

for all

, strictly less than
2n/\sqrt{3}-1/(2\sqrt{3}n)
for

, and strictly less than
(2n-1)/\sqrt{3}
for all

sufficiently large. Now consider

,

and

. By Lemma

, when

\sqrt{33}>1.147\,n

H ∞ H^{\infty} -calculus for Stokes operators on rough and on unbounded domains
Peer Christian Kunstmann and Patrick Tolksdorf

Abstract
. In this article, we give an overview on known as well as new results on the boundedness of the H ∞ H^{\infty} -calculus of the Stokes operator in rough as well as in unbounded (smoother) domains. We present a special case of an abstract comparison principle due to Kunstmann and Weis ( [ 23 ] ) that serves as the basis for all considerations. Subsequently, we show how this result can be applied to arrive at a bounded H ∞ H^{\infty} -calculus for the Stokes operator. We sketch the proof for no slip boundary conditions in bounded Lipschitz domains which was given in [ 23 ] . For unbounded domains this approach yields a shorter proof compared to previous arguments. Moreover, we further establish the boundedness of the H ∞ H^{\infty} -calculus for the Stokes operator with Neumann type boundary conditions in bounded convex domains which is entirely new. Mathematics Subject Classification (2020). 35 Q 30, 76 D 07, 47 A 60, 47 D 06. Keywords. Stokes operator, H ∞ H^{\infty} -functional calculus, Lipschitz domains, fractional domain spaces, maximal L q L^{q} -regularity.



1.
Introduction

Boundedness of the
H^{\infty}
-calculus for a sectorial operator
A
with domain
D(A)
in a Banach space
X

has found numerous applications in the theory of elliptic and parabolic equations. Outside Hilbert spaces it can be viewed as a frequently available replacement for the spectral theorem that one has for non-negative self-adjoint operators. If the
H^{\infty}
-calculus for
A
is bounded then, e.g.,
A
has bounded imaginary powers, a property which entails equality of fractional domain spaces
D(A^{\alpha})
,
\alpha\in(0,1)
, with complex interpolation spaces
[X,D(A)]_{\alpha}
or – via the Dore-Venni theorem in a
UMD
-space
X
– allows to prove maximal
L^{q}
-regularity for
1<q<\infty
.
Other applications include maximal regularity for stochastic evolution equations or uniform stability estimates for numerical time integration schemes.

For Stokes operators with no-slip boundary conditions the first result to mention is due to Giga (
[
11
]
) where boundedness of the imaginary powers is shown in bounded
C^{\infty}
-domains
\Omega
by pseudodifferential methods. This result had then been used to identify fractional domain spaces of the Stokes operator on such domains. The same method allows to obtain boundedness of the
H^{\infty}
-calculus for Stokes operators in
L^{p}_{\sigma}(\Omega)
,
1<p<\infty
, on bounded
C^{\infty}
-domains
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Boundedness of imaginary powers for exterior domains had been shown by Giga and Sohr in
[
12
]

by localization. Noll and Saal (
[
32
]
) proved boundedness of the
H^{\infty}
-calculus for Stokes operators with no-slip boundary conditions on bounded
C^{3}
-domains by localization and perturbation.

Abels developed pseudodifferential methods for non-smooth, e.g.,
BUC
-coefficients in
x
and used them to obtain boundedness of the
H^{\infty}
-calculus for Stokes operators on a variety of unbounded domains such as, e.g., asymptotically flat layers (
[
1
]
). Prüss (
[
33
]
) showed boundedness of the
H^{\infty}
-calculus for generalized Stokes operators with coefficients and various boundary conditions on bounded
C^{2,1}
-domains, again by localization and perturbation, where model problems in the half space are studied via Fourier multiplier results. On the whole space, the second author of this article extended these results to generalized Stokes operators with
L^{\infty}
-coefficients in
[
41
]
relying on the methods summarized in this article. For Stokes operators with first order boundary conditions on unbounded domains, but with a fixed wall, boundedness of the
H^{\infty}
-calculus had been achieved in
[
24
]
by means of kernel estimates for Hodge boundary conditions and perturbation.

In this paper we concentrate on the method that allows to obtain boundedness of the
H^{\infty}
-calculus for Stokes operators by comparison of their (fractional) domain spaces in solenoidal
L^{p}
-spaces with (fractional) domain spaces of corresponding Laplace operators in
L^{p}(\Omega)^{d}
via the Helmholtz projection in
L^{p}(\Omega)^{d}
. This method has its origins in
[
15
]
,
in particular Theorem 8.2 there. The proof of this result contains a gap which has been fixed in
[
22
]
by the cost of an additional assumption. Roughly speaking, comparability of certain fractional domain spaces in
L^{p}
for
p=2
and some
p_{0}\neq 2
allowed by interpolation to manipulate certain expressions involving Littlewood-Paley operators for the Stokes and Laplace operator at hand. Via such expressions, boundedness of the
H^{\infty}
-calculus for the Stokes operator could be shown in
L^{p}
for
p
between
2
and
p_{0}
.

These results were improved in
[
23
]
where the assumptions could be relaxed. The basic idea there had been to argue directly with certain (exponential) decay estimates for suitable Littlewood-Paley expressions. These decay estimates in turn can be obtained by complex interpolation. In the end, comparability of fractional domain spaces is needed only in
L^{p}
for
p=2
. Since less information about Stokes and Laplace operators in
L^{p}
is needed for
p\neq 2
, the result could be applied to Stokes operators with no-slip boundary conditions in bounded Lipschitz domains, see
[
23
]
.

Here, we give in Section
3
a concise version of the abstract result in
[
23
]
(see Theorem
3.1
, Corollary
3.5
, and Remark
3.6
below). In Section
4
, we apply the abstract result to establish a bounded
H^{\infty}
-calculus for Stokes operators with certain Neumann type boundary conditions on bounded and convex domains in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d\geq 2
.
For the application we need
R
-sectorialty of these operators in a certain part of the
L^{p}
-scale, and we prove them here by enhancing the methods used in
[
40
]
for the proof of resolvent bounds. The other key ingredients we need had been shown in
[
30
]
. The results on
R
-sectoriality and the
H^{\infty}
-calculus for the Neumann type Stokes operators are entirely new.

In the rest of the paper, we review in a survey-like style other situations where boundedness of the
H^{\infty}
-calculus has been shown by the comparison method. More precisely, we study the Stokes operator with no-slip boundary conditions in bounded Lipschitz domains in dimensions
d\geq 2
, see Section
5
, and we review results on Stokes operators with no-slip boundary conditions in
\widetilde{L}^{p}_{\sigma}(\Omega)
for unbounded uniform
C^{1,1}
-domains (originally established in
[
18
]
) and, assuming a Helmholtz decomposition, in
L^{p}_{\sigma}(\Omega)
in unbounded uniform
C^{3}
-domains with
1<p<\infty
(originally proven in
[
9
]
), see Section
6
.
The proofs we give are simpler than the original ones, and – in the
\widetilde{L}^{p}_{\sigma}
-case – we reobtain the original assertion of
[
18
, Thm. 1.1]
, for which the proof given there contained a gap, discussed in
[
9
, Rem. 1.2]
.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section we present essential definitions and some fundamental results that we need in the following.

For a Banach space
X
we denote by
\mathcal{L}(X)
the space of bounded linear operators on
X
and by
X^{\prime}
the
dual space
of
X
, i.e., the space of continuous linear functionals on
X
, and by
X^{*}
the
anti-dual space
of
X
, i.e., the space of all continuous anti-linear functionals on
X
.

2.1.
Sectorial operators

For a linear operator
A
in a complex Banach space
X
we denote its spectrum by
\sigma(A)
, its resolvent set by
\rho(A):=\mathbb{C}\setminus\sigma(A)
, and resolvent operators by
R(\lambda,A)=(\lambda-A)^{-1}
for
\lambda\in\rho(A)
.

We call a linear operator
A
in
X
with dense domain
D(A)
and dense range
sectorial
of angle
\omega\in[0,\pi)
if

\sigma(A)\subseteq\Sigma_{\omega}:=\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}:|\operatorname{arg}\lambda|\leq\omega\}\cup\{0\},

and, for each
\theta\in(\omega,\pi)
, the set

(2.1)

\displaystyle\{\lambda R(\lambda,A):\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\Sigma_{\theta}\}\subseteq\mathcal{L}(X)

is bounded. Observe that with this definition of sectoriality,
A
is, in particular, injective.

For an angle
\theta\in(0,\pi)
we denote by
\Sigma_{\theta}^{\circ}
the interior of
\Sigma_{\theta}
and by
H^{\infty}(\Sigma_{\theta}^{\circ})
the space of all functions
g:\Sigma_{\theta}^{\circ}\to\mathbb{C}
that are bounded and holomorphic on
\Sigma_{\theta}^{\circ}
.
If
A
is a sectorial operator of angle
\omega\in[0,\pi)
in
X
and
\theta\in(\omega,\pi)
we can define a functional calculus
f\mapsto f(A)
for functions

f\in H^{\infty}_{0}(\Sigma_{\theta}^{\circ}):=\big\{g\in H^{\infty}(\Sigma_{\theta}^{\circ}):\exists\varepsilon,c>0:|g(z)|\leq c\min\{|z|^{\varepsilon},|z|^{-\varepsilon}\},z\in\Sigma_{\theta}^{\circ}\big\}

by contour integrals. Choosing
\gamma\in(\omega,\theta)
the operator
f(A)
is given by

f(A):=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\partial\Sigma_{\gamma}}f(\lambda)R(\lambda,A)\,d\lambda,

where
\partial\Sigma_{\gamma}
is parametrized such that
\Sigma_{\gamma}^{\circ}
lies to the left. Observe that the integral here is absolutely convergent as a Bochner integral (or as an improper Riemann integral). We mention that the formula
f(A):=(\varphi(A)^{-1})^{n}(f\varphi^{n})(A)
, where
\varphi(\lambda)=\lambda(1+\lambda)^{-2}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
, yields an extension to a functional calculus of closed but unbounded operators, which comprises in particular fractional powers
A^{\alpha}
of
A
for all
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}
.

If
A
is sectorial of angle
0\leq\omega<\frac{\pi}{2}
, then
-A
is the generator of a bounded analytic semigroup. More precise,

z\mapsto e^{-zA}
is bounded on every sector
\Sigma_{\delta}
with
\delta\in(0,\frac{\pi}{2}-\omega)
.

2.2.
Bounded
H^{\infty}
-caculus

Let
\theta\in(\omega,\pi)
. The operator
A
is said to
have a bounded
H^{\infty}(\Sigma_{\theta}^{\circ})
-calculus
if there exists
C>0
such that, for all
f\in H^{\infty}_{0}(\Sigma_{\theta}^{\circ})
, we have

(2.2)

\displaystyle\|f(A)\|_{\mathcal{L}(X)}\leq C\sup\{|f(\lambda)|:\lambda\in\Sigma_{\theta}^{\circ}\}.

In this case, the functional calculus has a unique extension to all
f\in H^{\infty}(\Sigma_{\theta}^{\circ})
and the estimate (
2.2
) persists to this larger class of functions. We refer to, e.g.,
[
21
, Section 9]
for the construction and further properties of the
H^{\infty}
-calculus.

2.3.
Bounded imaginary powers

We note as an immediate consequence of a bounded
H^{\infty}(\Sigma_{\theta}^{\circ})
-calculus for
A
that
A
has
bounded imaginary powers

A^{it}
,
t\in\mathbb{R}
, with an estimate
\|A^{it}\|_{\mathcal{L}(X)}\leq Ce^{\theta|t|}
,
t\in\mathbb{R}
. In particular,
domains of fractional powers of
A^{\alpha}
,
\alpha\in(0,1)
, can be obtained as complex interpolation spaces between
X
and
D(A)
:

(2.3)

\displaystyle\big[X,D(A)\big]_{\alpha}=D(A^{\alpha}),\hskip 10.00002pt\alpha\in(0,1),

see
[
42
, Thm. 1.15.3]
(and
[
21
, Thm. 15.28]
for a homogeneous version). This is one main application of a bounded
H^{\infty}
-calculus, but – outside Hilbert spaces – boundedness of the
H^{\infty}
-calculus is not necessary to have bounded imaginary powers.

2.4.
R
-sectorial operators and maximal
L^{q}
-regularity

A sectorial operator
A
in a Banach space
X
is called
R
-sectorial
of angle
\omega\in[0,\pi)
if
\sigma(A)\subseteq\Sigma_{\omega}
and, for any
\theta\in(\omega,\pi)
, the set in (
2.1
) is
R
-bounded in
\mathcal{L}(X)
. Here, a set
\mathcal{T}\subseteq\mathcal{L}(X)
is called
R
-bounded
if there exists a
C>0
such that, for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\in X
,
T_{1},\ldots,T_{n}\in\mathcal{T}
, we have the estimate

\mathbb{E}\Big\|\sum_{j=1}^{n}\varepsilon_{j}T_{j}x_{j}\Big\|_{X}=C\mathbb{E}\Big\|\sum_{j=1}^{n}\varepsilon_{j}x_{j}\Big\|_{X},

where
\varepsilon_{1},\ldots,\varepsilon_{n}
are independent and symmetric random variables with values in
\{-1,1\}
, e.g., Rademachers.
We refer to, e.g.,
[
21
]
for more on this notion.

In a Banach space
X
with the
UMD
-property, so in particular in a closed subspace of an
L^{p}
-space,
1<p<\infty
, we have the following two facts:

First, boundedness of the
H^{\infty}(\Sigma_{\theta}^{\circ})
-calculus of
A
implies that
A
is
R
-sectorial of angle
\leq\theta
and the infimum of the
H^{\infty}
-angles
\theta
equals the infimum of the
R
-sectoriality angles (see
[
16
, Thm. 5.3]
and use
[
16
, Prop. 3.2]
). Hence, boundedness of the
H^{\infty}
-calculus can be seen as
qualitative
improvement of
R
-sectoriality whose
quantitative
aspect (the angle) is ruled by the
R
-sectoriality angle.

Second, Lutz Weis’ famous result (
[
43
]
) characterizes, among sectorial operators of angle
<\frac{\pi}{2}
, those that have maximal
L^{q}
-regularity for
1<q<\infty
as those that are
R
-sectorial of angle
<\frac{\pi}{2}
, see
[
43
]
. Here,
A
is said to
have maximal
L^{q}
-regularity
, where
q\in(1,\infty)
if, for any
f\in L^{q}(0,\infty;X)
the problem

u^{\prime}(t)+Au(t)=f(t),\hskip 10.00002ptt>0,\hskip 20.00003ptu(0)=0,

has a unique mild solution
u
which satisfies
u^{\prime},Au\in L^{q}(0,\infty;X)
and the estimate

\|u^{\prime}\|_{L^{q}(0,\infty;X)}+\|Au\|_{L^{q}(0,\infty;X)}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{q}(0,\infty;X)},

for some
C>0
. Maximal
L^{q}
-regularity has found a multitude of applications in the study of nonlinear parabolic problems via fixed point arguments or the implicit function theorem.

3.
The abstract result

The following result has been shown in
[
23
]
. We give here an explicit formulation of a special case for easy reference. The result can be used to transfer a bounded
H^{\infty}
-calculus from an operator
B
on a scale of spaces
L^{p}(\Omega)
,
p\in I
, to an operator
A
on a corresponding scale of complemented subspaces
X_{p}
of
L^{p}(\Omega)
under certain conditions on their relation.

Here,
(\Omega,\mu)
is a
\sigma
-finite measure space and
I\subseteq(1,\infty)
is an interval containing
p=2
. Strictly speaking, we have, of course, a different operator
B_{p}
in
L^{p}(\Omega)
for each
p
and we assume that they have consistent resolvents, i.e.,
R(\lambda,B_{p_{1}})
and
R(\lambda,B_{p_{2}})
coincide on
L^{p_{1}}(\Omega)\cap L^{p_{2}}(\Omega)
for all
p_{1},p_{2}\in I
and all
\lambda
outside some sector containing the right half line. In this case, we shall also simply call the family
(B_{p})_{p\in I}

consistent
.

An important special case is when
A_{2}
and/or
B_{2}
are self adjoint in
X_{2}
,
L^{2}(\Omega)
, respectively. For duality arguments we hence work, for linear operators
T:Y\to Z
, with the
adjoint
operators
T^{*}:Z^{*}\to Y^{*}
, which act between the anti-dual spaces
Z^{*}
of
Z
and
Y^{*}
of
Y
. For a Hilbert space
H
with scalar product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
, we recall the (linear) Riesz isomorphism
H\to H^{*}
,
h\mapsto\langle h,\cdot\rangle
. We recall for
p\in(1,\infty)
that the anti-dual space of
L^{p}(\Omega)
is
(L^{p}(\Omega))^{*}=L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)
via the pairing
\langle f,g\rangle_{L^{p}\times L^{p^{\prime}}}=\int_{\Omega}f\overline{g}\,d\mu
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
p_{0}\in(1,\infty)\setminus\{2\}
and put
I:=[\min\{2,p_{0}\},\max\{2,p_{0}\}]
. Let
(B_{p})_{p\in I}
be a family of consistent sectorial operators in
L^{p}(\Omega)
,
p\in I
. Assume that, for each
p\in I
, the operator
B_{p}
has a bounded
H^{\infty}
-calculus in
L^{p}(\Omega)
.

Let
(X_{p})_{p\in I}
be a family of closed linear subspaces
X_{p}\subseteq L^{p}(\Omega)
and let
(R_{p})_{p\in I}
and
(S_{p})_{p\in I}
be families of consistent bounded linear operators such that, for each
p\in I
,
R_{p}:L^{p}(\Omega)\to X_{p}
,
S_{p}:X_{p}\to L^{p}(\Omega)
and
R_{p}S_{p}=I_{X_{p}}
. Let
(A_{p})_{p\in I}
be a consistent family of linear operators such that each
A_{p}
is
R
-sectorial in
X_{p}
.

Assume that, for some
\alpha_{1}>0>\alpha_{2}
and some
\beta_{1}>0>\beta_{2}
we have

(3.1)

\displaystyle R_{2}(D(B_{2}^{\alpha}))

\displaystyle\subseteq D(A_{2}^{\alpha})\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{and}\hskip 10.00002pt\|A_{2}^{\alpha}R_{2}f\|\lesssim\|B_{2}^{\alpha}f\|,\ \ f\in D(B_{2}^{\alpha}),\alpha\in\{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\},

(3.2)

\displaystyle S_{2}(D(A_{2}^{\beta}))

\displaystyle\subseteq D(B_{2}^{\beta})\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{and}\hskip 10.00002pt\|B_{2}^{\beta}S_{2}f\|\lesssim\|A_{2}^{\beta}f\|,\ \ f\in D(A_{2}^{\beta}),\beta\in\{\beta_{1},\beta_{2}\}.

Then, for each
p\in I\setminus\{p_{0}\}
,
A_{p}
has a bounded
H^{\infty}
-calculus in
X_{p}
.

We mention that, for negative
\alpha
,
D(A_{2}^{\alpha})
equals the range of
A_{2}^{-\alpha}
. In particular, if
0\in\rho(A_{2})
, then

D(A_{2}^{\alpha})=X_{2}
. However, the norm estimate in, e.g., (
3.1
) then entails inclusions for the completions of the respective spaces. If
0\in\rho(A_{2})
and
\alpha>0
, then
D(A_{2}^{\alpha})
is a Banach space for the norm
\|A_{2}^{\alpha}x\|_{X_{2}}
. In certain situations this allows for applications of the closed graph theorem and its consequences.

Theorem
3.1
has been proved in
[
23
]
, in principle, although not in the formulation above.
We give a proof below for convenience. We also refer to
[
7
, Subsection 3.2]
where the approach from
[
23
]
has been sketched in some detail. Concerning the assumptions (
3.1
) and (

) in Theorem

we remark the following.

Remark 3.2
.

The conditions for the negative exponents
\alpha_{2}
and
\beta_{2}
in (
3.1
) and (
3.2
), respectively, can be reformulated as

(3.3)

\displaystyle R_{2}^{*}\big(D((A_{2}^{*})^{|\alpha_{2}|})\big)

\displaystyle\subseteq D\big((B_{2}^{*})^{|\alpha_{2}|}\big),\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{and}\hskip 10.00002pt\|(B_{2}^{*})^{|\alpha_{2}|}R_{2}^{*}f\|\lesssim\|(A_{2}^{*})^{|\alpha_{2}|}f\|,\ \ f\in D((A_{2}^{*})^{|\alpha_{2}|}),

(3.4)

\displaystyle S_{2}^{*}\big(D((B_{2}^{*})^{|\beta_{2}|})\big)

\displaystyle\subseteq D\big((A_{2}^{*})^{|\beta_{2}|}\big),\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{and}\hskip 10.00002pt\|(A_{2}^{*})^{|\beta_{2}|}S_{2}^{*}f\|\lesssim\|(B_{2}^{*})^{|\beta_{2}|}f\|,\ \ f\in D((B_{2}^{*})^{|\beta_{2}|}),

respectively. We refer to
[

for a proof.

3.1
.

[

to see that the conditions (22) and (23) in

are satisfied for

and
A_{2}
in

, respectively. Then we use complex interpolation to see that (22) and (23) in

are satisfied for

, respectively, for

.

-boundedness conditions (22) and (23) we have to use

.

Remark 3.3
.

In applications
X_{p}
usually is a complemented subspace in
L^{p}(\Omega)
and
P_{p}\in\mathcal{L}(L^{p}(\Omega))
is a corresponding projection onto
X_{p}=\mathrm{ran}\,P_{p}
. Then
R_{p}:L^{p}(\Omega)\to X_{p}
acts as
P_{p}
and
S_{p}=\iota_{p}:X_{p}\to L^{p}(\Omega)
is the inclusion map.

However, for the adjoint operators we have
R_{p}^{*}:X_{p}^{*}\to(L^{p}(\Omega))^{*}
and
P_{p}^{*}:(L^{p}(\Omega))^{*}\to(L^{p}(\Omega))^{*}
, in particular,
P_{p}^{*}\in\mathcal{L}(L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega))
is a projection. It is easily seen that the identification
(L^{p}(\Omega))^{*}=L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)
leads to the identification
X_{p}^{*}=\mathrm{ran}\,P_{p}^{*}\subseteq L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)
and that
R_{p}^{*}:X_{p}^{*}=\mathrm{ran}\,P_{p}^{*}\to L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)
is the inclusion.

For our realization
X_{p}^{*}=\mathrm{ran}\,P_{p}^{*}
, the adjoint
S_{p}^{*}:L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)\to\mathrm{ran}\,P_{p}^{*}
of the inclusion map
S_{p}=\iota_{p}:X_{p}\to L^{p}(\Omega)
acts as
P_{p}^{*}
.

We further specialize to the case
\alpha_{1}=\beta_{1}=\alpha>0
and
\alpha_{2}=\beta_{2}=-\beta<0

and study the case that
A_{2}
and
B_{2}
are boundedly invertible.

Remark 3.4
.

In the situation of Remark
3.3
, if we have, for some
\alpha,\beta>0
, the conditions

(3.5)

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} P_{2}(D(B_{2}^{\alpha}))&\subseteq D(A_{2}^{\alpha})\subseteq D(B_{2}^{\alpha}),\\
\|A_{2}^{\alpha}P_{2}f\|&\lesssim\|B_{2}^{\alpha}f\|,\ \ f\in D(B_{2}^{\alpha}),\\
\|B_{2}^{\alpha}f\|&\lesssim\|A_{2}^{\alpha}f\|,\ \ f\in D(A_{2}^{\alpha}),\end{aligned}\right.

and

(3.6)

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} P_{2}^{*}(D((B_{2}^{*})^{\beta}))&\subseteq D((A_{2}^{*})^{\beta})\subseteq D((B_{2}^{*})^{\beta}),\\
\|(A_{2}^{*})^{\beta}P_{2}^{*}f\|&\lesssim\|(B_{2}^{*})^{\beta}f\|,\ \ f\in D((B_{2}^{*})^{\beta}),\\
\|(B_{2}^{*})^{\beta}f\|&\lesssim\|(A_{2}^{*})^{\beta}f\|,\ \ f\in D((A_{2}^{*})^{\beta}),\end{aligned}\right.

then (
3.1
) and (
3.2
) hold for
\alpha_{1}=\beta_{1}=\alpha>0
and
\alpha_{2}=\beta_{2}=-\beta<0
.

If we have, in addition, that

then condition (
3.5
) is equivalent to

(3.7)

\displaystyle P_{2}(D(B_{2}^{\alpha}))=D(A_{2}^{\alpha})=D(B_{2}^{\alpha})\cap X_{2},

and condition (
3.6
) is equivalent to

(3.8)

\displaystyle P_{2}^{*}(D((B_{2}^{*})^{\beta}))=D((A_{2}^{*})^{\beta})=D((B_{2}^{*})^{\beta})\cap\mathrm{ran}\,P_{2}^{*}.

This can be seen by an application of the closed graph theorem.

Finally, we specialize to the case of a self-adjoint projection
P_{2}=P_{2}^{*}
. We formulate this as a corollary of Theorem
3.1
but repeat the full list of assumptions.

Corollary 3.5
.

Let
p_{0}\in(1,\infty)\setminus\{2\}
and put
I:=[\min\{2,p_{0}\},\max\{2,p_{0}\}]
. Let
(B_{p})_{p\in I}
be a family of consistent sectorial operators in
L^{p}(\Omega)
,
p\in I
.
Assume that, for each
p\in I
, the operator
B_{p}
has a bounded
H^{\infty}
-calculus in
L^{p}(\Omega)
.

Let
(X_{p})_{p\in I}
be a family of closed linear subspaces
X_{p}\subseteq L^{p}(\Omega)
such that
X_{p}=\mathrm{ran}\,P_{p}
for a consistent family
(P_{p})_{p\in I}
of projections
P_{p}\in\mathcal{L}(L^{p}(\Omega))
and let
P_{2}
be self-adjoint in
L^{2}(\Omega)
. Let
(A_{p})_{p\in I}
be a consistent family of linear operators such that each
A_{p}
is
R
-sectorial in
X_{p}
. If
0\in\rho(A_{2})\cap\rho(B_{2})
and, for some
\alpha,\beta>0
, we have

(3.9)

\displaystyle P_{2}(D(B_{2}^{\alpha}))=D(A_{2}^{\alpha})=D(B_{2}^{\alpha})\cap X_{2},

(3.10)

\displaystyle P_{2}(D((B_{2}^{*})^{\beta}))=D((A_{2}^{*})^{\beta})=D((B_{2}^{*})^{\beta})\cap X_{2},

then
A_{p}
has a bounded
H^{\infty}
-calculus in
X_{p}
for each
p\in I\setminus\{p_{0}\}
.

Remark 3.6
.

If, in the situation of Corollary
3.5
,
B_{2}
is self-adjoint in
L^{2}(\Omega)
and
A_{2}
is self-adjoint in
X_{2}
then, of course, (
3.10
) is just (
3.9
) for
\beta
in place of
\alpha
. Hence, (
3.9
) for some
\alpha>0
is already sufficient for the conclusion.

More general, if both
B_{2}
and
A_{2}
are associated with closed sectorial forms in
L^{2}(\Omega)
and
X_{2}
, respectively, then we have
D((B_{2}^{*})^{\alpha})=D(B_{2}^{\alpha})
and
D((A_{2}^{*})^{\alpha})=D(A_{2}^{\alpha})
for all
\alpha\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
by a result due to Kato (
[
17
]
). In this situation, (
3.9
) for some
\alpha\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
is sufficient for the conclusion.

4.
Stokes operators with Neumann type boundary conditions

We give a first example for an application of Corollary
3.5
in case of the Stokes operator with Neumann type boundary conditions in bounded convex domains
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
. More precisely, we study the operator associated to the resolvent problem

(4.1)

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} \lambda u-\Delta u+\nabla\phi&=f&&\text{in }\Omega,\\
\mbox{\rm div}\,(u)&=0&&\text{in }\Omega,\\
\{Du+\mu[Du]^{\top}\}n-\phi n&=0&&\text{on }\partial\Omega,\end{aligned}\right.

where
\mu
is a given parameter and
n
denotes the outward unit normal to
\partial\Omega
. This operator is studied in the scale of solenoidal
L^{p}
-spaces given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{p}(\Omega):=\{f\in L^{p}(\Omega)^{d}\colon\mbox{\rm div}\,(f)=0\}\hskip 20.00003pt(1<p<\infty).

To introduce the Stokes operator subject to the Neumann type boundary condition written above, we introduce the space of solenoidal test functions

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{1}_{\sigma}(\Omega):=\{u\in\mathcal{L}^{2}_{\sigma}(\Omega):\nabla u\in L^{2}(\Omega)^{d\times d}\}.

Given
\mu\in(-1,1)
we introduce coefficients
a_{jk}^{\alpha\beta}(\mu):=\delta_{jk}\delta_{\alpha\beta}+\mu\delta_{j\beta}\delta_{k\alpha}
, where
\delta_{\alpha\beta}
denotes Kronecker’s delta. Notice that the divergence form operator with coefficients
a_{jk}^{\alpha\beta}(\mu)
is formally given by

\displaystyle\partial_{j}a_{jk}^{\alpha\beta}(\mu)\partial_{k}u_{\beta}=\Delta u_{\alpha}+\mu\partial_{\alpha}\mbox{\rm div}\,(u).

Thus, on solenoidal functions this operator acts merely as the Laplacian. The Stokes operator subject to the above Neumann type boundary condition is the operator
A_{\mu}
in
\mathcal{L}^{2}_{\sigma}(\Omega)
associated with the sesquilinear form

\displaystyle\mathfrak{a}_{\mu}:\mathcal{H}^{1}_{\sigma}(\Omega)\times\mathcal{H}^{1}_{\sigma}(\Omega)\to\mathbb{C},\hskip 10.00002pt(u,v)\mapsto\sum_{\alpha,\beta=1}^{d}\sum_{j,k=1}^{d}\int_{\Omega}a_{jk}^{\alpha\beta}(\mu)\partial_{k}u_{\beta}\overline{\partial_{j}v_{\alpha}}\,dx.

More precisely,

\displaystyle D(A_{\mu})

\displaystyle:=\{u\in\mathcal{H}^{1}_{\sigma}(\Omega)\colon\exists f\in\mathcal{L}^{2}_{\sigma}(\Omega)\text{ s.t.{} }\mathfrak{a}_{\mu}(u,v)=\langle f,v\rangle_{L^{2}}\text{ for all }v\in\mathcal{H}^{1}_{\sigma}(\Omega)\},

\displaystyle A_{\mu}u

\displaystyle:=f\hskip 20.00003pt(u\in D(A_{\mu})).

Employing de Rham’s lemma as in
[
30
, Thm 6.8]
one can associate to every
u\in D(A_{\mu})
a unique pressure function
\phi\in L^{2}(\Omega)
such that

\displaystyle\big\langle A_{\mu}u,v\big\rangle=\mathfrak{a}_{\mu}(u,v)-\int_{\Omega}\phi\,\overline{\mbox{\rm div}\,(v)}\,dx\hskip 20.00003pt(v\in H^{1}(\Omega)^{d}).

In particular, this provides the meaning of the boundary condition stated in (
4.1
). Given
\theta\in[0,\pi)
and
\lambda\in\Sigma_{\theta}
, the resolvent problem with right-hand side
f\in\mathcal{L}^{2}_{\sigma}(\Omega)
is formulated weakly as

(4.2)

\displaystyle\lambda\int_{\Omega}u\overline{v}\,dx+\mathfrak{a}_{\mu}(u,v)-\int_{\Omega}\phi\,\overline{\mbox{\rm div}\,(v)}\,dx=\int_{\Omega}f\overline{v}\,dx\hskip 20.00003pt(v\in H^{1}(\Omega)^{d}).

In the following, we denote by
\mathbb{Q}
the orthogonal projection in
L^{2}(\Omega)^{d}
onto
\mathcal{L}^{2}_{\sigma}(\Omega)
as the
Leray projection
. It is well-known that

\displaystyle L^{2}(\Omega)^{d}=\mathcal{L}^{2}_{\sigma}(\Omega)\oplus\nabla H^{1}_{0}(\Omega).

Moreover,
\mathbb{Q}
has a continuous extension
\mathcal{Q}:(H^{1}(\Omega)^{d})^{*}\to(\mathcal{H}^{1}_{\sigma}(\Omega))^{*}
which is given by
\mathcal{Q}v=v|_{\mathcal{H}^{1}_{\sigma}}
and observe that
\mathcal{Q}
is the adjoint of the inclusion map
\mathcal{H}^{1}_{\sigma}(\Omega)\subseteq H^{1}(\Omega)^{d}
.

We will also study the Stokes resolvent problem with a right-hand side in
(\mathcal{H}^{1}_{\sigma}(\Omega))^{*}
. Given
F\in L^{2}(\Omega)^{d\times d}
this right-hand side will be defined as
\mathcal{Q}\,\mbox{\rm div}\,(F)
and the corresponding resolvent problem reads

\displaystyle\lambda\int_{\Omega}u\overline{v}\,dx+\mathfrak{a}_{\mu}(u,v)=-\int_{\Omega}F\cdot\overline{\nabla v}\,dx\hskip 20.00003pt(v\in\mathcal{H}^{1}_{\sigma}(\Omega)).

Again, one can also associate a pressure function to this solution
\phi\in L^{2}(\Omega)
, so that

(4.3)

\displaystyle\lambda\int_{\Omega}u\overline{v}\,dx+\mathfrak{a}_{\mu}(u,v)-\int_{\Omega}\phi\,\overline{\mbox{\rm div}\,(v)}\,dx=-\int_{\Omega}F\cdot\overline{\nabla v}\,dx\hskip 20.00003pt(v\in H^{1}(\Omega)^{d}).

The boundary condition that is modelled here is given by

\displaystyle\{Du+\mu[Du]^{\top}\}n-\phi n=-F^{\top}n\hskip 10.00002pt\text{on }\partial\Omega.

The Lax-Milgram lemma guarantees the existence of weak solutions to both the resolvent problems (
4.2
) and (
4.3
). We will often denote the solution
u
to (
4.2
) by
(\lambda+A_{\mu})^{-1}f
and the associated pressure by
\phi=\Phi_{\lambda}f
. Moreover, the solution
u
to (
4.3
) will be denoted by
(\lambda+A_{\mu})^{-1}\mathcal{Q}\mbox{\rm div}\,(F)
and the associated pressure by
\phi=\Phi_{\lambda}\mathcal{Q}\mbox{\rm div}\,(F)
. Testing the resolvent problem with
v=u
or with
v=\nabla\Delta_{D}^{-1}\phi
, where
\Delta_{D}
denotes the Dirichlet Laplacian on
\Omega
yields the following basic
L^{2}
-result, cf.
[
40
, Prop. 2.3]
and
[
40
, Prop. 3.1]
:

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d\geq 2
, be a bounded convex domain and
\theta\in[0,\pi)
. For all
\mu\in(-1,1)
we have
\Sigma_{\theta}\subseteq\rho(-A_{\mu})
. Moreover, there exists
C>0
depending only on
d
,
\theta
, and
\mu
such that for all
f\in\mathcal{L}^{2}_{\sigma}(\Omega)
and all
\lambda\in\Sigma_{\theta}
we have

\displaystyle\lvert\lambda\rvert\|(\lambda+A_{\mu})^{-1}f\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}_{\sigma}(\Omega)}+\lvert\lambda\rvert^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\nabla(\lambda+A_{\mu})^{-1}f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)^{d\times d}}+\lvert\lambda\rvert^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\Phi_{\lambda}f\|\leq C\|f\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}_{\sigma}(\Omega)}.

Moreover, there exists
C>0
depending only on
d
,
\theta
, and
\mu
such that for all
F\in L^{2}(\Omega)^{d\times d}
and all
\lambda\in\Sigma_{\theta}
we have

\displaystyle\lvert\lambda\rvert^{\frac{1}{2}}\|(\lambda+A_{\mu})^{-1}\mathcal{Q}\mbox{\rm div}\,(F)\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}_{\sigma}(\Omega)}+\|\nabla(\lambda+A_{\mu})^{-1}\mathcal{Q}\mbox{\rm div}\,(F)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)^{d\times d}}+\|\Phi_{\lambda}\mathcal{Q}\mbox{\rm div}\,(F)\|\leq C\|F\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

Since in Hilbert spaces uniform boundedness and
R
-boundedness are equivalent, we can read the above proposition as an
R
-boundedness result for the operator families

\displaystyle f\mapsto\lambda(\lambda+A_{\mu})^{-1}f,\hskip 10.00002ptf\mapsto\lvert\lambda\rvert^{\frac{1}{2}}\nabla(\lambda+A_{\mu})^{-1}f,\hskip 10.00002ptf\mapsto\lvert\lambda\rvert^{1/2}\Phi_{\lambda}f\hskip 20.00003pt(\lambda\in\Sigma_{\theta})

as well as

\displaystyle F\mapsto\lvert\lambda\rvert^{\frac{1}{2}}(\lambda+A_{\mu})^{-1}\mathcal{Q}\mbox{\rm div}\,(F),\hskip 10.00002ptF\mapsto\nabla(\lambda+A_{\mu})^{-1}\mathcal{Q}\mbox{\rm div}\,(F),\hskip 10.00002ptF\mapsto\Phi_{\lambda}\mathcal{Q}\mbox{\rm div}\,(F)\hskip 20.00003pt(\lambda\in\Sigma_{\theta}).

As a first step towards an application of Corollary
3.5
we establish the following
R
-boundedness result in the
L^{p}
-scale.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d\geq 2
, be a bounded and convex domain and
r_{0}>0
be such that
B(0,r_{0})\subseteq\tfrac{1}{2}[\Omega-\{x_{0}\}]
for some
x_{0}\in\Omega
. Let further
\theta\in[0,\pi)
,
\mu\in(-1,\sqrt{2}-1)
, and let

\displaystyle\Big\lvert\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2}\Big\rvert<\frac{1}{d}\cdotp

Then the families of operators

(4.4)

\displaystyle\big\{\lambda(\lambda+A_{\mu})^{-1}:\lambda\in\Sigma_{\theta}\big\}

\displaystyle\subseteq\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{L}^{p}_{\sigma}(\Omega)),

(4.5)

\displaystyle\big\{\lvert\lambda\rvert^{\frac{1}{2}}\nabla(\lambda+A_{\mu})^{-1}:\lambda\in\Sigma_{\theta}\big\}

\displaystyle\subseteq\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{L}^{p}_{\sigma}(\Omega),L^{p}(\Omega)^{d\times d}),

(4.6)

\displaystyle\big\{\nabla(\lambda+A_{\mu})^{-1}\mathcal{Q}\mbox{\rm div}\,:\lambda\in\Sigma_{\theta}\big\}

\displaystyle\subseteq\mathcal{L}(L^{p}(\Omega)^{d\times d})\hskip 10.00002pt\text{and}

(4.7)

\displaystyle\big\{\lvert\lambda\rvert^{\frac{1}{2}}(\lambda+A_{\mu})^{-1}\mathcal{Q}\mbox{\rm div}\,:\lambda\in\Sigma_{\theta}\big\}

\displaystyle\subseteq\mathcal{L}(L^{p}(\Omega)^{d\times d},\mathcal{L}_{\sigma}^{p}(\Omega))

are
R
-bounded. If, in addition,
p\geq 2
we have that

(4.8)

\displaystyle\big\{\lvert\lambda\rvert^{\frac{1}{2}}\Phi_{\lambda}:\lambda\in\Sigma_{\theta}\big\}

\displaystyle\subseteq\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{L}^{p}_{\sigma}(\Omega),L^{p}(\Omega))\hskip 10.00002pt\text{and}

(4.9)

\displaystyle\big\{\Phi_{\lambda}\mathcal{Q}\mbox{\rm div}\,:\lambda\in\Sigma_{\theta}\big\}

\displaystyle\subseteq\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{L}^{p}_{\sigma}(\Omega)^{d\times d},L^{p}(\Omega))

are
R
-bounded. In particular, there exists a constant
C>0
depending only on
d
,
p
,
\theta
,
\mu
,
\mathrm{diam}\,(\Omega)
, and
r_{0}
such that the
R
-bounds of all these sets of operators are bounded by
C
.

This theorem is an extension of the main result of
[
40
, Thm. 1.1]
which states uniform bounds of the operator families above instead of
R
-bounds.
We want to mention that, for bounded domains with
C^{2,1}
-boundary,
R
-boundedness of corresponding operator families and maximal
L^{p}
-regularity for Stokes operators with Neumann type boundary conditions have been shown by Shibata and Shimizu (
[
36
]
) by Fourier multiplier results, perturbation, and localization.
The proof of Theorem
4.2
uses the characerization of
R
-boundedness by virtue of square function estimates and then adapts the lines of the proof of
[
40
, Thm. 1.1]
to these square functions. Thus, we advise the reader to keep a copy of
[
40
]
handy. We begin with the case
p>2
.

Step 1: Reformulation into an
\ell^{2}
-valued boundedness estimate in the case
p>2

If
V
is a subspace of
L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{C}^{m})
for some
1<p<\infty
,
m\in\mathbb{N}
and
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
Lebesgue measurable, then there exists
C>0
such that for all
k_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
and
(f_{k})_{k=1}^{k_{0}}\subseteq V
we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{C}\Big\|\sum_{k=1}^{k_{0}}\varepsilon_{k}(\cdot)f_{k}\Big\|_{L^{2}(0,1;V)}\leq\Big\|\Big[\sum_{k=1}^{k_{0}}\lvert f_{k}\rvert^{2}\Big]^{1/2}\Big\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leq C\Big\|\sum_{k=1}^{k_{0}}\varepsilon_{k}(\cdot)f_{k}\Big\|_{L^{2}(0,1;V)},

where
\varepsilon_{1},\dots,\varepsilon_{k_{0}}
are independent and symmetric random variables with values in
\{-1,1\}
. This means, that
R
-boundedness in subspaces of
L^{p}
is equivalent to so-called
square function estimates
.

We use this fact for
p>2
and conclude by density that the three families of operators in (
4.4
)-(
4.6
) are
R
-bounded if and only if there exists
C>0
such that for all
k_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
, all
\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{k_{0}}\in\Sigma_{\theta}
and all
f_{1},\dots,f_{k_{0}}\in C^{\infty}_{\sigma}(\overline{\Omega}_{\ell})
the following estimate is valid

\displaystyle\Big\|\Big[\sum_{k=1}^{k_{0}}\Big(\lvert\lambda_{k}(\lambda_{k}+A_{\mu})^{-1}f_{k}\rvert^{2}+\lvert\lvert\lambda_{k}\rvert^{\frac{1}{2}}\nabla(\lambda_{k}+A_{\mu})^{-1}f_{k}\rvert^{2}

\displaystyle+\lvert\lvert\lambda_{k}\rvert^{\frac{1}{2}}\Phi_{\lambda_{k}}f_{k}\rvert^{2}\Big)\Big]^{1/2}\Big\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt\hskip 20.00003pt\leq C\Big\|\Big[\sum_{k=1}^{k_{0}}\lvert f_{k}\rvert^{2}\Big]^{1/2}\Big\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}.

If we define
u_{k}:=(\lambda_{k}+A_{\mu})^{-1}f_{k}
and
\phi_{k}:=\Phi_{\lambda_{k}}f_{k}
, then this is equivalent to establishing that all operators of the form

(4.10)

\displaystyle Tf:=T(f_{k})_{k=1}^{k_{0}}:=\left(\begin{pmatrix}\lambda_{1}u_{1}\\
\vdots\\
\lambda_{k_{0}}u_{k_{0}}\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}\lvert\lambda_{1}\rvert^{1/2}\nabla u_{1}\\
\vdots\\
\lvert\lambda_{k_{0}}\rvert^{1/2}\nabla u_{k_{0}}\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}\lvert\lambda_{1}\rvert^{1/2}\phi_{1}\\
\vdots\\
\lvert\lambda_{k_{0}}\rvert^{1/2}\phi_{k_{0}}\end{pmatrix}\right)

extend to a uniform bounded family of operators from
\mathcal{L}^{p}_{\sigma}(\Omega;X)
to
L^{p}(\Omega;Y)
, where
X=\ell^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{d})
and
Y=\ell^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{d})\times\ell^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{d\times d})\times\ell^{2}(\mathbb{C})
, and where
(\lambda_{k})_{k=1}^{k_{0}}\subseteq\Sigma_{\theta}
is arbitrary. Here, we regard a finite dimensional vector naturally as an element in
\ell^{2}
by regarding this vector as a finite sequence and by filling up the remaining components by
0
. It is hence our task to bound the family of all such operators uniformly in these vector-valued
L^{p}
-spaces.

Step 2: Verification of the vector-valued boundedness for certain values of
p>2

We employ a variant of the vector-valued version of Shen’s
L^{p}
-extrapolation theorem which can be found, e.g., in
[
41
, Thm. 4.2]
. This will be achieved by adapting
[
40
, Section 6]
to the vector-valued situation. In the following,
Q=Q(x_{0},r)\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
denotes a dyadic cube with center
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\mathrm{diam}\,(Q)=r
and
Q^{*}
denotes its unique dyadic parent. Moreover,
M_{2Q^{*}}
will denote the localized maximal function on
2Q^{*}
. We now state the extrapolation theorem.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
X
and
Y
be Banach spaces and
\Xi\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
be measurable. Let further
2<p<p_{0}
,
f\in L^{2}(\Xi;X)\cap L^{p}(\Xi;X)
, and let
T
be an operator such that
Tf
is defined and contained in
L^{2}(\Xi;Y)
.

Suppose that there exist constants
\iota>1
and
C>0
such that for all
\alpha>0
and all dyadic cubes
Q=Q(x_{0},r)
with
r>0
and
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
the estimate

(4.11)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\lvert\{x\in Q:M_{2Q^{*}}(E_{0}\|Tf\|_{Y}^{2})(x)>\alpha\}\rvert\leq\frac{C}{\alpha}\int_{(2\iota Q^{*})\cap\Xi}\|f\|_{X}^{2}\,dx\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+\frac{C\lvert Q\rvert}{\alpha^{p_{0}/2}}\bigg\{\sup_{Q^{\prime}\supset 2Q^{*}}\bigg(\frac{1}{\lvert Q^{\prime}\rvert}\int_{Q^{\prime}\cap\Xi}\big(\|Tf\|_{Y}^{2}+\|f\|_{X}^{2}\big)\,dx\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}\bigg\}^{p_{0}}\end{aligned}

holds. Here the supremum runs over all cubes
Q^{\prime}
containing
2Q^{*}
and
E_{0}
denotes the extension to
\mathbb{R}^{d}
by zero.

Then there exists a constant
K>0
depending on
d
,
p
,
p_{0}
,
\iota
, and
C
such that

\displaystyle\|Tf\|_{L^{p}(\Xi;Y)}\leq K\|f\|_{L^{p}(\Xi;X)}.

To get access to the results in
[
40
, Section 4]
, we need to regularize
\Omega
. For this purpose, fix
r_{0}>0
such that
B(0,r_{0})\subseteq\tfrac{1}{2}[\Omega-\{x_{0}\}]
for some
x_{0}\in\Omega
and let
(\Omega_{\ell})_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}}
be the sequence of smooth, bounded and convex domains described in
[
40
, Rem. 4.3]
. Note, all of these domains have comparable diameter and there exists a constant
C>0
, depending only on
r_{0}
,
\mathrm{diam}\,(\Omega)
and
d
such that for every cube
R
with center
x_{0}\in\partial\Omega_{\ell}
and diameter
0<r\leq 2r_{0}
we have

(4.12)

\displaystyle\lvert R\cap\Omega_{\ell}\rvert\geq Cr^{d}.

In the application of Theorem
4.3
we will take
\Xi:=\Omega_{\ell}
,
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
and we define
p_{0}:=\frac{2d}{d-2}
if
d\geq 3
and
2<p_{0}<\infty
if

. The operator
T
will be a counterpart of the operator in (
4.10
) on
\Omega_{\ell}
:

Given resolvent parameters
\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{k_{0}}\in\Sigma_{\theta}
and right-hand sides
f_{1},\dots,f_{k_{0}}\in C^{\infty}_{\sigma}(\overline{\Omega}_{\ell})
, let
u_{k,\ell}:=(\lambda_{k}+A_{\mu,\ell})^{-1}f_{k}
, where
A_{\mu,\ell}
denotes the Stokes operator with Neumann-type boundary conditions on
\Omega_{\ell}
. In addition, let
\phi_{k,\ell}
denote the associated pressure. We now define

(4.13)

\displaystyle Tf:=T(f_{k})_{k=1}^{k_{0}}:=\left(\begin{pmatrix}\lambda_{1}u_{1,\ell}\\
\vdots\\
\lambda_{k_{0}}u_{k_{0},\ell}\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}\lvert\lambda_{1}\rvert^{1/2}\nabla u_{1,\ell}\\
\vdots\\
\lvert\lambda_{k_{0}}\rvert^{1/2}\nabla u_{k_{0},\ell}\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}\lvert\lambda_{1}\rvert^{1/2}\phi_{1,\ell}\\
\vdots\\
\lvert\lambda_{k_{0}}\rvert^{1/2}\phi_{k_{0},\ell}\end{pmatrix}\right)\cdotp

As above, we may fix
X=\ell^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{d})
and
Y=\ell^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{d})\times\ell^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{d\times d})\times\ell^{2}(\mathbb{C})
in Theorem
4.3
. Now, that all notation is fixed, we can start to verify the conditions of Theorem
4.3
. For this purpose, we fix a dyadic cube
Q=Q(x_{0},r)
with
2Q^{*}\cap\Omega_{\ell}\neq\emptyset
. Note, if the intersection is empty, then
M_{2Q^{*}}(E_{0}\|Tf\|_{Y}^{2})
in (
4.11
) would just be the zero function and the desired inequality would be trivial.

Case 1: We have
2r>\sqrt{d}\,\mathrm{diam}\,(\Omega)

The conditions imposed on
Q^{*}
and
r
imply that for all
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
we have
\Omega_{\ell}\subseteq 4Q^{*}
. We use the weak-type
(1,1)
-estimate of the localized maximal operator and the

-boundedness of

from Proposition

to deduce

\displaystyle\big\lvert\big\{x\in Q:M_{2Q^{*}}(E_{0}\|Tf\|_{Y}^{2})(x)>\alpha\big\}\big\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C_{1}}{\alpha}\|Tf\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\ell};Y)}^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{C_{1}}{\alpha}\sum_{k=1}^{k_{0}}\int_{\Omega_{\ell}}\Big(\lvert\lambda_{k}u_{k,\ell}\rvert^{2}+\lvert\lvert\lambda_{k}\rvert^{\frac{1}{2}}\nabla u_{k,\ell}\rvert^{2}+\lvert\lvert\lambda_{k}\rvert^{\frac{1}{2}}\phi_{k,\ell}\rvert^{2}\Big)\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C_{1}C_{2}}{\alpha}\sum_{k=1}^{k_{0}}\int_{\Omega_{\ell}}\lvert f_{k}\rvert^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle=\frac{C_{1}C_{2}}{\alpha}\int_{(4Q^{*})\cap\Omega_{\ell}}\|(f_{k})_{k=1}^{k_{0}}\|_{X}^{2}\,dx.

This proves (
4.11
) in this particular case.

Case 2: We have
0<2r\leq\sqrt{d}\,\mathrm{diam}\,(\Omega)
and
2Q^{*}\cap\partial\Omega_{\ell}\neq\emptyset

Let

and let
\mathcal{R}:=Q(y,4r)\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
be the cube with center

and
\mathrm{diam}\,(\mathcal{R})=4r
. In particular, we have
2Q^{*}\subseteq\mathcal{R}
. We split each of the functions
u_{k,\ell}
and
\phi_{k,\ell}
in

into two parts using functions

and

as well as
\vartheta_{k,\ell}
and
\psi_{k,\ell}
. To define these functions, let
\widetilde{A}_{\mu,\ell}
denote the Stokes operator subject to Neumann-type boundary conditions on
(8\mathcal{R})\cap\Omega_{\ell}
. Notice that the restriction of

to

is still in

so that the following definition is meaningful

\displaystyle v_{k,\ell}:=(\lambda_{k}+\widetilde{A_{\mu,\ell}})^{-1}R_{(8\mathcal{R})\cap\Omega_{\ell}}f_{k}\hskip 20.00003pt\text{and}\hskip 20.00003ptw_{k,\ell}:=u_{k,\ell}-v_{k,\ell}.

Here
R_{(8\mathcal{R})\cap\Omega_{\ell}}

5.
Stokes operators with no-slip conditions in bounded Lipschitz domains

Let
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a bounded Lipschitz domain. For
p\in(1,\infty)
, we denote by
L^{p}_{\sigma}(\Omega)
the closure in
L^{p}(\Omega)^{d}
of the space

C^{\infty}_{c,\sigma}(\Omega):=\{\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)^{d}:\mbox{\rm div}\,\varphi=0\ \mbox{in $\Omega$}\}.

Then
L^{p}_{\sigma}(\Omega)=\{f\in L^{p}(\Omega)^{d}:\mbox{\rm div}\,f=0,\nu\cdot f|_{\partial\Omega}=0\,\}
, where
\nu\cdot f|_{\partial\Omega}
is defined in the sense of traces for

with
\mbox{\rm div}\,f\in L^{p}(\Omega)
.

5.1.
The operator and spaces

We define
H^{1}_{0,\sigma}(\Omega)
as the closure of
C_{c,\sigma}^{\infty}(\Omega)
in
H^{1}(\Omega)^{d}
. Then we have

\displaystyle H^{1}_{0,\sigma}(\Omega)

\displaystyle=\{u\in L^{2}_{\sigma}(\Omega):\nabla u\in L^{2}(\Omega)^{d\times d},u|_{\partial\Omega}=0\}

\displaystyle=\{u\in L^{2}(\Omega)^{d}:\mbox{\rm div}\,u=0,\nabla u\in L^{2}(\Omega)^{d\times d},u|_{\partial\Omega}=0\}.

The Stokes operator in
\Omega
with no-slip boundary conditions is the operator
A
that is associated with the symmetric and closed sesquilinear form

\mathfrak{a}(u,v):=\int_{\Omega}\nabla u\cdot\overline{\nabla v}\,dx,\hskip 10.00002ptu,v\in H^{1}_{0,\sigma}(\Omega),

in
L^{2}_{\sigma}(\Omega)
. This means for
u,f\in L^{2}_{\sigma}(\Omega)
that

u\in D(A)\ \mbox{and}\ Au=f\ \Longleftrightarrow\ u\in H^{1}_{0,\sigma}(\Omega)\ \mbox{and}\ \forall v\in H^{1}_{0,\sigma}(\Omega):\mathfrak{a}(u,v)=\int_{\Omega}f\cdot\overline{v}\,dx.

In other words, in the Gelfand triple
H^{1}_{0,\sigma}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{2}_{\sigma}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow(H^{1}_{0,\sigma}(\Omega))^{*}
, the operator
A
is the part in
L^{2}_{\sigma}(\Omega)
of the operator

\mathcal{A}:H^{1}_{0,\sigma}(\Omega)\to(H^{1}_{0,\sigma}(\Omega))^{*},\hskip 10.00002ptu\mapsto\mathfrak{a}(u,\cdot).

Since
\mathfrak{a}
is symmetric, the operator
A
is self-adjoint in
L^{2}_{\sigma}(\Omega)
.
We shall compare the operator
A
to the negative Dirichlet Laplacian
B
on
\Omega
, which in
L^{2}(\Omega)^{d}
is associated with the symmetric and closed sesquilinear form

\mathfrak{b}(u,v):=\int_{\Omega}\nabla u\cdot\overline{\nabla v}\,dx,\hskip 10.00002ptu,v\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)^{d},

which means for

u\in D(B)\ \mbox{and}\ Bu=f\ \Longleftrightarrow\ u\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)^{d}\ \mbox{and}\ \forall v\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)^{d}:\mathfrak{b}(u,v)=\int_{\Omega}f\cdot\overline{v}\,dx.

B
is the part in
L^{2}(\Omega)^{d}
of the operator

\mathcal{B}:H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)^{d}\to(H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)^{d})^{*},\hskip 10.00002ptu\mapsto\mathfrak{b}(u,\cdot),

Since
\mathfrak{b}
is symmetric, the operator

is self-adjoint in

.

see

.
We remark that, in a canonical way,

and thus elements of

may be regarded as equivalence classes
\phi+\nabla L^{2}(\Omega)
,
\phi\in(H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)^{d})^{*}
. Taking the part in
L^{2}_{\sigma}(\Omega)
of the right hand side of (
5.1
) thus leads to the following description of the Stokes operator
A
in
L^{2}_{\sigma}(\Omega)
, see

, Thm. 4.7]
:

D(A)=\{u\in H^{1}_{0,\sigma}(\Omega):\exists g\in L^{2}(\Omega):-\Delta u+\nabla g\in L^{2}_{\sigma}(\Omega)\},\hskip 10.00002ptAu=-\Delta u+\nabla g.

For
p\in(1,\infty)
with
|\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2}|\leq\frac{1}{6}+\varepsilon
, where
\varepsilon
depends on
\Omega
, it had been shown in
[

]
that the Helmholtz projection
\mathbb{P}
in
L^{2}(\Omega)^{d}
extends consistently to a bounded projection
\mathbb{P}_{p}
in
L^{p}(\Omega)^{d}
and that a corresponding Helmholtz decomposition

(5.2)

\displaystyle L^{p}(\Omega)^{d}=L^{p}_{\sigma}(\Omega)\oplus\nabla W^{1,p}(\Omega)

holds. This range is known to be optimal for

. For bounded Lipschitz domains
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{2}
, boundedness of
\mathbb{P}_{p}
and the Helmholtz decomposition of

hold for the larger range of

given by

(
[

]
).

For

it had been shown by Z. Shen in
[
34
]
that, for
p\in(1,\infty)
with
|\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2}|\leq\frac{1}{2d}+\varepsilon
, where
\varepsilon
depends on
\Omega
, resolvents of the Stokes operator
A
in
L^{2}_{\sigma}(\Omega)
extend by consistency to resolvents of a sectorial operator in
L^{p}_{\sigma}(\Omega)
(see
[
34
, Thm. 1.1]
) and that this operator, the Stokes operator
A_{p}
in
L^{p}_{\sigma}(\Omega)
, is given by

D(A_{p})=\{u\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)^{d}:\mbox{\rm div}\,u=0,\exists g\in L^{p}(\Omega):-\Delta u+\nabla g\in L^{p}_{\sigma}(\Omega)\},\hskip 10.00002ptA_{p}u=-\Delta u+\nabla g,

see
[

, Cor. 1.2 and Rem. 6.4]
. We further mention, that resolvent bounds for the the Stokes operator in planar Lipschitz domains were recently established in

by Geng and Shen (

).

5.3.
Domains of fractional powers

Boundedness of the
H^{\infty}
-calculus in Theorems

and

has been used to identify fractional domain spaces
D(A_{p}^{\theta})
in
[
39
, Thm. 1.1]
for
d\geq 3
and
\theta=\frac{1}{2}
and

[
7
, Thm. 1.3]
for
d=2
and more general
\theta
. Those results extended the result contained in
[
23
, Thm. 16]
, which was obtained by abstract means.
It is remarked in
[
7
, p. 256]
that the same approach as in
[
7
]
works in also in dimensions
d\geq 3
. We present the precise formulation here and sketch elements of the proof.

Theorem 5.7
.

Let
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a bounded Lipschitz domain where
d\geq 2
. There exists
\varepsilon>0
such that, for
p
satisfying
|\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}|<\frac{1}{2d}+\varepsilon
and
\theta\in(0,1)
, we have

(5.3)

\displaystyle H^{2\theta,p}_{0,\sigma}(\Omega)\subseteq D(A_{p}^{\theta}).

Moreover, there exists
\delta\in(0,1]
such that, for
q
and
\theta
satisfying in addition

(5.4)

\displaystyle\theta<\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2p}\ \mbox{if}\ \frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}\leq\frac{\delta}{2}\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{or}\hskip 10.00002pt\theta<\frac{3-d}{4}+\frac{d}{2p}+\frac{(d-1)\delta}{4}\ \mbox{if}\ \frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}>\frac{\delta}{2},

we have (with equivalent norms)

(5.5)

\displaystyle H^{2\theta,p}_{0,\sigma}(\Omega)=D(A_{p}^{\theta}).

Here, the spaces
H^{s,p}_{0,\sigma}(\Omega)
are defined as the spaces
V^{s,p}(\Omega)
in
[
28
]
.

Sketch of proof.

The first step is to show, for
p
with
|\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}|<\frac{1}{2d}+\varepsilon
, the inclusion
W^{2,p}_{0,\sigma}(\Omega)\subseteq D(A_{p})
, see
[
39
, Lem. 2.5]
for
d\geq 3
and (with the same proof)
[
7
, Lem. 3.8]
for

.

Then Theorems
5.4
and
5.5
imply via Subsection
2.3
that

\big[L^{p}_{\sigma}(\Omega),W^{2,p}_{0,\sigma}(\Omega)\big]_{\theta}\subseteq D(A_{p}^{\theta}),\hskip 10.00002pt\theta\in(0,1).

By
[
28
, Thm. 2.12]
the space on the left hand side equals

, and (
5.3
) is proved.

The proof of (
5.5
) under the conditions (
5.4
) relies on results by Mitrea and Wright (see
[
27
, Thm. 10.6.2]
) on solvability of the stationary Stokes system in the scale of Besov- and Triebel-Lizorkin spaces. The latter comprises the spaces

that are relevant here.

In order to show boundedness of

it suffices by duality to show boundedness

A_{p^{\prime}}^{-\theta}\mathbb{P}_{p^{\prime}}:H^{-2\theta,p^{\prime}}(\Omega)^{d}\to L^{p^{\prime}}_{\sigma}(\Omega).

Via (

Here, the results from
[
27
]
on the Stokes system come into play, for details we refer to
[
7
]
.

6.
Stokes operators on unbounded domains

In this section we consider Stokes operators with no-slip boundary conditions on unbounded domains. In contrast to the two previous sections the boundary of
\Omega
will be relatively smooth. The main issue is that unboundedness of the domain may cause the Helmholtz decomposition to fail in
L^{p}(\Omega)^{d}
for certain
p\neq 2
, even if the boundary
\partial\Omega
is smooth, see
[
25
]
. Two different ideas have been used in the theory for Stokes operators for such domains.
There is the
\widetilde{L}^{p}
-theory by Farwig, Kozono, and Sohr (see
[
5
]
,
[
6
]
), and there is a by
relative
L^{p}
-theory
Geißert, Heck, Hieber, and Sawada (see
[
8
]
), where a Helmholtz decomposition for
L^{p}(\Omega)^{d}
is
assumed
. Here, it is assumed that
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a uniform
C^{1,1}
-domain or a uniform
C^{3}
-domain, respectively.

6.1.
\widetilde{L}^{p}
-theory

Farwig, Kozono, and Sohr established a theory for the Stokes operator in
\widetilde{L}^{p}
-spaces, where

\widetilde{L}^{p}(\Omega):=\left\{\begin{aligned} &L^{p}(\Omega)\cap L^{2}(\Omega),&&2\leq p<\infty,\\
&L^{p}(\Omega)+L^{2}(\Omega),&&1<p<2.\\
\end{aligned}\right.

If
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a uniform
C^{1}
-domain, they showed in
[
5
]
the Helmholtz decomposition

\widetilde{L}^{p}(\Omega)^{d}=\widetilde{L}^{p}_{\sigma}(\Omega)\oplus\widetilde{G}^{p}(\Omega),\hskip 10.00002pt1<p<\infty,

with corresponding projecton operator
\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}_{p}
, where

\widetilde{L}^{p}_{\sigma}(\Omega):=\left\{\begin{aligned} &L^{p}_{\sigma}(\Omega)\cap L_{\sigma}^{2}(\Omega),&&2\leq p<\infty,\\
&L^{p}_{\sigma}(\Omega)+L_{\sigma}^{2}(\Omega),&&1<p<2,\\
\end{aligned}\right.\hskip 20.00003pt\widetilde{G}^{p}(\Omega):=\left\{\begin{aligned} &G^{p}(\Omega)\cap G^{2}(\Omega),&&2\leq p<\infty,\\
&G^{p}(\Omega)+G^{2}(\Omega),&&1<p<2,\\
\end{aligned}\right.

and
G^{p}(\Omega)
is now given as
G^{p}(\Omega)=\{\nabla g\in L^{p}(\Omega)^{d}:g\in L^{p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\overline{\Omega})\}
.
Denoting by
D^{p}(\Omega):=W^{2,p}(\Omega)\cap W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
the domain of the Dirichlet Laplacian in
L^{p}(\Omega)
, and defining in an analogous way spaces
\widetilde{D}^{p}(\Omega)
,
1<p<\infty
, the Stokes operator
\widetilde{A}_{p}
with no-slip boundary conditions in
\widetilde{L}^{p}_{\sigma}(\Omega)
is defined by

\widetilde{A}_{p}u:=-\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}_{p}\Delta u,\hskip 10.00002ptu\in D(\widetilde{A}_{p}):=\widetilde{D}^{p}(\Omega)^{d}\cap\widetilde{L}^{p}_{\sigma}(\Omega).

The following has been shown in
[
6
, Thm. 1.3 and 1.4]
.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a uniform
C^{1,1}
-domain. For
1<p<\infty
and
\delta>0
, the Stokes operator
\delta+\widetilde{A}_{p}
has maximal
L^{q}
-regularity in
\widetilde{L}^{p}_{\sigma}(\Omega)
for
1<q<\infty
, and hence is
R
-sectorial in
\widetilde{L}^{p}_{\sigma}(\Omega)
of angle
<\frac{\pi}{2}
.

This has been used in the proof of the following, which is
[
18
, Thm. 1.1]
.

Theorem 6.2
.

Let
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a uniform
C^{1,1}
-domain. For
1<p<\infty
and
\delta>0
, the Stokes operator
\delta+\widetilde{A}_{p}
has a bounded
H^{\infty}
-calculus in
\widetilde{L}^{p}_{\sigma}(\Omega)
.

The proof given in
[
18
]
was based on
[
15
, Thm. 8.2]
, whose proof contains a gap. The gap has been closed in
[
22
]
by the cost of an additional assumption on the Helmholtz decomposition for
p\neq 2
. For the application, this additional assumption required
more regularity of
\Omega
, namely that
\Omega
is a uniform
C^{2,\alpha}
-domain for some
\alpha\in(0,1]
, we refer to the discussion on this issue in
[
9
]
.

Here, we can use the analog for
\widetilde{L}^{q}
-spaces of Corollary
3.5
and Remark
3.6
instead of the result from
[
22
]
. Indeed, the proof in Section
3
is based on the fact that the
L^{p}
-scale is a complex interpolation scale. This persists to the
\widetilde{L}^{p}
-scale, see
[
18
, Lem. 4.1]
, which in turn is based on results in
[
3
]
.

Consequently, besides Theorem
6.1
, we only need mapping properties of the Helmholtz projection
\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}_{2}=\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}
between fractional domain spaces in
L^{2}(\Omega)^{d}
and
L^{2}_{\sigma}(\Omega)
, respectively. These are shown in
[
18
, Lem. 4.3]
.

We formulate the result on fractional domain spaces, whose proof is much easier here since we have a precise description of domain
D(\widetilde{A}_{p})
in
\widetilde{L}^{p}_{\sigma}(\Omega)
for
1<p<\infty
.

Corollary 6.3
.

Let
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a uniform
C^{1,1}
-domain. For
1<p<\infty
,
\delta>0
, and
\theta\in(0,1)
, we have

D\big((\delta+\widetilde{A}_{p})^{\theta}\big)=D\big((\delta-\widetilde{\Delta}_{p})^{\theta}\big)^{d}\cap\widetilde{L}^{p}_{\sigma}(\Omega)=\widetilde{H}^{2\theta,p}_{0}(\Omega)^{d}\cap\widetilde{L}^{p}_{\sigma}(\Omega),

where
\widetilde{\Delta}_{p}
denotes the Dirichlet Laplacian in
\widetilde{L}^{p}(\Omega)
, which has domain
\widetilde{D}^{p}(\Omega)
, and

\widetilde{H}^{2\theta,p}_{0}(\Omega)=\left\{\begin{aligned} &H^{2\theta,p}_{0}(\Omega)+H^{2\theta,2}_{0}(\Omega),&&1<p\leq 2,\\
&H^{2\theta,p}_{0}(\Omega)\cap H^{2\theta,2}_{0}(\Omega),&&2<p<\infty.\end{aligned}\right.

Proof.

The first equality can be shown by the arguments in the proof of
[
11
, Lem. 6]
. By consistency of resolvents we have

D\big((\delta-\widetilde{\Delta}_{p})^{\theta}\big)=\left\{\begin{aligned} &D\big((\delta-\Delta_{p})^{\theta}\big)+D\big((\delta-\Delta_{2})^{\theta}\big),&&1<p\leq 2,\\
&D\big((\delta-\Delta_{p})^{\theta}\big)\cap D\big((\delta-\Delta_{2})^{\theta}\big),&&2<p<\infty,\end{aligned}\right.

and by boundedness of the
H^{\infty}
-calculus for
\mu+\Delta_{p}
we have
D((\mu-\Delta_{p})^{\theta})=[L^{p}(\Omega),D^{p}(\Omega)]_{\theta}
.
These interpolation spaces may be determined by localization, see
[
18
, proof of Cor. 1.2]
, and this gives the result.
∎

6.2.
Relative
L^{p}
-theory

Assume that
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a uniform
C^{3}
-domain and that

is such that the Helmholtz decomposition

(6.1)

\displaystyle L^{p}(\Omega)^{d}=L^{p}_{\sigma}(\Omega)\oplus G^{p}(\Omega)

holds, i.e., that the Helmholtz projection
\mathbb{P}
acts boundedly in
L^{p}(\Omega)^{d}
. It was shown in
[
9
, Prop. 2.19]
that the set of all such

is an interval containing

and closed under taking dual exponents, and that corresponding Helmholtz projections are consistent. For

in this interval, define the Stokes operator

in

by

Under these assumptions,
Geißert, Heck, Hieber, and Sawada showed in
[
8
, Thm. 1.1]
that there exists
\mu\geq 0
such that the operator
\mu+A_{p}
has maximal
L^{q}
-regularity in
L^{p}_{\sigma}(\Omega)
for
1<q<\infty
. By Lutz Weis’ theorem mentioned in Subsection
2.4
this means that we have the following.

Proposition 6.4
.

Let
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a uniform
C^{3}
-domain and
p\in(1,\infty)
such that (
6.1
) holds.
Then there exists
\mu\geq 0
such that
\mu+A_{p}
is
R
-sectorial in
L^{p}_{\sigma}(\Omega)
of some angle
<\frac{\pi}{2}
.

Again, we can use the mapping properties of the Helmholtz projection in
L^{2}(\Omega)^{d}
from
[
18
, Lem. 4.3]
for an application of Corollary
3.5
via Remark
3.6
and obtain the following, originally proved in
[
9
, Thm. 1.1]
.

Theorem 6.5
.

Let
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a uniform
C^{3}
-domain and
p_{0}\in(1,\infty)
such that (
6.1
) holds for
p_{0}
.
Then there exists
\mu\geq 0
such that
\mu+A_{p}
has a bounded
H^{\infty}
-calculus in
L^{p}_{\sigma}(\Omega)
for all
p\in(\min\{p_{0},p_{0}^{\prime}\},\max\{p_{0},p_{0}^{\prime}\})
.

The proof given in
[
9
]
used the result from
[
22
]
. Hence, the additional condition on mapping properties of the Helmholtz projection in
L^{q}
had to be verified. Thus, the proof given here is simpler.

It was conjectured by M. Geissert that the assertion of
[
8
, Thm. 1.1]
(and hence also of Proposition
6.4
) remains valid if
\Omega
is just a uniform
C^{2}
-domain or maybe just a uniform
C^{1,1}
-domain. In this case and with the given proof, we would obtain the same for the assertion of Theorem
6.5
.

Corollary 6.6
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
6.5
we have, for
q\in(\min\{p_{0},p_{0}^{\prime}\},\max\{p_{0},p_{0}^{\prime}\})
,

\theta\in(0,1)
, and for the
\mu
from Theorem
6.5
,

D\big((\mu+A_{p})^{\theta}\big)=D\big((\mu-\Delta_{p})^{\theta}\big)^{d}\cap L^{p}_{\sigma}(\Omega)=H^{2\theta,p}_{0}(\Omega)^{d}\cap L^{p}_{\sigma}(\Omega),

where
\Delta_{p}
denotes the Dirichlet Laplacian in
L^{p}(\Omega)
with domain
D^{p}(\Omega)
.

The proof is in fact simpler than the proof of Corollary
6.3
given above.

Convergent sequences of combinatorial submodular setfunctions
Kristóf Bérczi, Márton Borbényi, László Lovász, László Márton Tóth

Abstract
To illustrate that the notion of convergence of submodular function sequences fits reasonably into the limit theory of graphs, we describe several classes of matroids and other submodular setfunctions for which convergence of appropriate sequences can be proved. Some of the proofs are surprisingly nontrivial. Keywords: Submodular function, Quotient-convergence, Matroid, Finite linear space



1
Introduction

A limit theory for matroids, and more generally for submodular setfunctions, has been developed in
[
3
]
. It was shown in
[
2
]
that the theory is in line with the notion of local-global convergnece of bounded-degree graphs. In this paper we show that the limit theory applies to a variety of further combinatorially interesting submodular setfunctions, like the rank functions of linear spaces, cut capacity functions, homomorphism density functions, and matroids of many dense graphs. These examples are mentioned in
[
3
]
without complete proofs, and the goal of these notes is to elaborate those proofs.

2
Preliminaries

2.1
Definitions

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a family of subsets of the set
J
, which is not necessarily finite. A
setfunction
on
(J,\mathcal{F})
is a function
\varphi\colon\mathcal{F}\to\mathbb{R}
. A
lattice family
is a family
\mathcal{F}
of subsets of a set
J
closed under union and intersection. A setfunction
\varphi
on a lattice family
(J,\mathcal{F})
is
submodular
, if

\varphi(X)+\varphi(Y)\geq\varphi(X\cap Y)+\varphi(X\cup Y)

for all
X,Y\in\mathcal{F}
. Throughout paper, we assume that
\varphi(\emptyset)=0
. In combinatorics, one of the most important examples of submodular setfunctions is the rank function of a matroid.

For a setfunction
\varphi
on a set algebra
(J,\mathcal{F})
and
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, we define a
k
-quotient
of
\varphi
by
\psi=\varphi\circ F^{-1}
, where
F\colon J\to[k]
is a measurable map. The
k
-quotient set

Q_{k}(\varphi)
is the set of all
k
-quotients of
\varphi
. The set
Q_{k}(\varphi)
is a subset of the finite dimensional real linear space
\mathbb{R}^{2^{k}}
, with the two coordinates
\varphi\circ F^{-1}(\emptyset)=0
and
\varphi\circ F^{-1}(J)=\varphi(J)
being fixed. For any sequence
\mathbf{A}=(A_{1},\dots,A_{k})\in\mathcal{F}^{k}
, we define
\varphi/\mathbf{A}\colon 2^{[k]}\to\mathbb{R}
by
(\varphi/\mathbf{A})(I)=\varphi\big(\cup_{i\in I}A_{i}\big)
for
I\subseteq[k]
. Then quotients could be defined as setfunctions
\varphi/\mathbf{A}
,
where
\mathbf{A}=(A_{1},\dots,A_{k})
is a partition of
J
into sets in
\mathcal{F}
.

We say that the sequence
(\varphi_{n}\colon n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+})
of finite setfunctions is
quotient-convergent
, if for every
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, the quotient sets
(Q_{k}(\varphi_{n})\colon n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+})
form a Cauchy sequence in the Hausdorff distance
d^{\text{\rm Haus}}
on the
2^{k}
-dimensional Euclidean space. It does not matter, of course, which norm on
\mathbb{R}^{2^{k}}
is used to define the Hausdorff distance, but for conformity with the infinite setting, we use the
\ell^{\infty}
norm, simply denoted by
\|\cdot\|
. We say that
(\varphi_{n}\colon n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+})

quotient-converges to

\varphi
for some setfunction
\varphi
if the sequence
(Q_{k}(\varphi_{n})\colon n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+})
converges to
Q_{k}(\varphi)
in the Hausdorff distance. It is proved in
[
3
]
that every convergent sequence of finite submodular setfunctions has a limit object in the form of a submdolar setfunction on a standard Borel space.

2.2
Graph limits

We need basic notions and results from the theory of convergence of dense graphs, based on
[
5
]
,
[
6
]
and
[
11
]
. The relationship between convergence of bounded-degree graph sequences and their associated matroids is discussed in
[
2
]
. See also
[
9
]
for further developments in both settings. Throughout the paper, we work with simple graphs, even when not stated explicitly.

Graphons.

A graphon is a Lebesgue measurable function
W\colon[0,1)^{2}\to[0,1]
that is
symmetric in the sense that
W(x,y)=W(y,x)
for all
x,y\in[0,1)
. Every graph
G=(V,E)
, where
V=[n]
, can be represented
by a graphon
W_{G}
as follows: for each
i,j\in[n]
, and for
x\in[(i-1)/n,i/n]
and
y\in[(j-1)/n,j/n]
, we set

W_{G}(x,y)=\begin{cases}1,&\text{if $(i,j)\in E$},\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

For two finite simple graphs
F
and
G
, we define the
homomorphism
density
of
F
in
G
as

t(F,G)=\frac{\hom(F,G)}{|V(G)|^{|V(F)|}},

where
\hom(F,G)
is the number of homomorphisms of
F
into
G
, that is, adjacency preserving maps
V(F)\to V(G)
. For a finite simple graph
F
and graphon
W
, let

t(F,W)=\int_{[0,1)^{n}}\prod_{ij\in E(F)}W(x_{i},x_{j})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\lambda_{n}(x).

This generalizes the case of finite graphs, in the sense that

t(F,G)=t(F,W_{G}).

For a graph
G
and positive integer
k
, we denote by
G(k)
the graph obtained from the
G
by replacing each node
u

by a set
V_{u}
of
k
non-adjacent nodes and connecting all nodes of
V_{u}
to all nodes of
V_{v}
if
uv\in E(G)
. It is not hard to see that
t(F,G(k))=t(F,G)
for all graphs
F
.

A sequence of graphs
(G_{n}\colon n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+})
is called
left-convergent
if

(t(F,G_{n})\colon n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+})
is a convergent sequence of real numbers for every
finite simple graph
F
. We say that the sequence
(G_{n}\colon n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+})

converges to a
graphon
W
, in notation
G_{n}\to W
, if
t(F,G_{n})\to t(F,W)
is a convergent
sequence of real numbers for every finite simple graph
F
. It is proved
in
[
11
]
and
[
4
]
that for every left-convergent sequence of
graphs there is an essentially unique graphon to which it converges, and every
graphon is the limit of a left-convergent graph sequence.

Cut distance.

There are several equivalent ways of defining convergence of dense graph sequences, of which we need another one.
Let
G
and
H
be two graphs of the same node set
V(G)=V(H)=V
. We define their
labeled cut distance
as

d_{\square}(G,H)=\max_{S,T\subseteq V}\frac{|e_{G}(S,T)-e_{H}(S,T)|}{|V|^{2}},

where
e_{G}(S,T)
denotes the number of edges with one endpoint in
S
and one endpoint in
T
. Note that if both endpoints are in
S\cap T
, then we count the edge twice.

Let
G
and
H
be two graphs, possibly with different number of nodes
|V(G)|=n
and
|V(H)|=m
. Then
G(mt)
and
H(nt)
have the same number of nodes for any
t\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
. Let
H^{\beta}
be the image of
H(nt)
under a bijection
\beta\colon V(H(nz))\to V(G(mt))
. The
unlabeled cut distance
is defined as

\delta_{\square}(G,H)=\inf d_{\square}(G(mt),H(nt)^{\beta}),

where the infimum is taken over all positive integers
t
and all bijections
\beta
.

As proved in
[
5
]
, a sequence
(G_{n}\colon n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+})
of graphs is dense-convergent if and only if it is a Cauchy sequence with respect to the unlabeled cut distance.

For two graphons
u
and
W
, their
labeled cut distance
is defined by

\|U-W\|_{\square}=\sup_{S,T}\left|\int_{S\times T}(U(x,y)-W(x,y))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\right|,

where
S,T\subseteq[0,1]
range over measurable sets. their
labeled cut distance
is defined by

\delta_{\square}=\inf_{\varphi}\|U-W^{\varphi}\|_{\square},

where
\varphi
ranges over all measure-preserving bijections
[0,1]\to[0,1]
, and
W^{\varphi}
is the graphon defined by
W^{\varphi}(x,y)=W(\varphi(x),\varphi(y))
. By
[
9
, Theorem 11.59]
, if
G_{n}\to W
, then we can label the nodes of the graphs
G_{n}
so that for the sequence
W_{G_{n}}
of representing graphons,
|W_{G_{n}}-W|_{\square}\to 0
holds as
n\to\infty
.

2.3
Convergence in Hausdorff distance

Let
\varepsilon>0
and
U
be a subset of a compact metric space
K
. We denote the set of points being at distance at most
\varepsilon
from
U
. It is easy to see that every increasing sequence of subsets of a compact metric space is convergent in the Hausdorff distance. The following lemma relaxes the increasing condition.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
(U_{n}\colon n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+})
be a sequence of nonempty subsets of a compact metric space
K
. Suppose that for every
\varepsilon>0
, there exists an integer
f(\varepsilon)\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
such that, for every
n\geq f(\varepsilon)
, there exists an integer
f(n,\varepsilon)
with the property that for all
m\geq f(n,\varepsilon)
, we have
U_{n}\subseteq U_{m}^{\varepsilon}
. Then
(U_{n}\colon n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+})
is convergent in the Hausdorff distance.

Proof.

Let
\varepsilon>0
. We may assume that
f(n,\varepsilon)\geq n
. As long as it is possible, we select an increasing sequence
(n_{q}\colon q\in\mathbb{Z}_{+})
of positive
integers, starting with
n_{0}=f(\varepsilon)
with the following properties:

n_{q+1}>f(n_{q},\varepsilon)\quad\text{and}\quad U_{n_{q+1}}\not\subseteq\bigcup_{j=1}^{q}U_{n_{j}}^{\varepsilon}.

Then
U_{n_{q+1}}
contains a point that is farther than
\varepsilon
from every
point of
U_{n_{j}}
for all
j\leq q
. Since
K
is compact, this can happen only finitely many times,
so the process terminates at some finite
q
. This means that for every
m>m_{0}=f(n_{q},\varepsilon)
, we have
U_{m}\subseteq\bigcup_{j=1}^{q}U_{n_{j}}^{\varepsilon}
. We have
U_{n_{j}}\subseteq U_{n_{q}}^{\varepsilon}
by the
definition of the sequence
(n_{j}\colon j\in\mathbb{Z}_{+})
, and hence
U_{n_{j}}^{\varepsilon}\subseteq U_{n_{q}}^{2\varepsilon}
. So it follows that
U_{m}\subseteq U_{n_{q}}^{2\varepsilon}
for every

m>m_{0}
. On the other hand,
U_{n_{q}}\subseteq U_{m}^{\varepsilon}
for every
m>m_{0}
by
the definition of
m_{0}
. Thus
d^{\text{\rm Haus}}(U_{m},U_{n_{q}})\leq 2\cdot\varepsilon
if
m>m_{0}
. This
implies that
d^{\text{\rm Haus}}(U_{n},U_{m})\leq 4\cdot\varepsilon
if
n,m>m_{0}
, which proves the lemma.
∎

3
Related convergence notions

3.1
Crop-convergence

We discuss some versions of quotient-convergence, which will provide a convenient step in proving the
more stringent quotient-convergence. Recalling for a setfunction on a set-algebra
(J,\mathcal{B})
the definition of its
k
-quotient set

Q_{k}(\varphi)=\{\varphi/\mathbf{A}\colon\mathbf{A}\in\mathcal{B}^{k},~\text{$\mathbf{A}$~is a partition of $J$}\},

we see that these are reasonably natural:

\displaystyle T_{k}(\varphi)

\displaystyle=\{\varphi/\mathbf{A}\colon\mathbf{A}\in\mathcal{B}^{k}\}.

\displaystyle T_{k}^{\Delta}(\varphi)

\displaystyle=\{\varphi/\mathbf{A}\colon\mathbf{A}\in\mathcal{B}^{k},~\mathbf{A}\text{~consists of disjoint sets}\},

\displaystyle T_{k}^{\nabla}(\varphi)

\displaystyle=\{\varphi/\mathbf{A}\colon\mathbf{A}\in\mathcal{B}^{k},~\mathbf{A}\text{ covers }J\}.

Clearly,
T_{k}(\varphi)
and its variants are sets of functions on
2^{[k]}
. The setfunctions in
T_{k}(\varphi)
are called the
k
-crops
of
\varphi
. Crops play an important role in combinatorial optimization. For example, crops of finitely additive measures are called
coverage functions
(extending a terminology from the finite case), and can be characterized by the “infinitely alternating” condition of Choquet
[
7
]
. We call a sequence
(\varphi_{n}\colon n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+})
of setfunctions
crop-convergent
if the corresponding sequence of sets
(T_{k}(\varphi_{n})\colon n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+})
is Cauchy with respect to the Hausdorff distance for every
k\geq 1
, and we say
that
(\varphi_{n}\colon n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+})

crop-converges
to a setfunction
\varphi
, if

T_{k}(\varphi_{n})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle{\text{\rm Haus}}}}{{\longrightarrow}}T_{k}(\varphi)
for every
k\geq 1
. We define
\Delta
-convergence

and
\nabla
-convergence
analogously.

It is easy to verify that if
\varphi
is a quotient of
\psi
, then

T_{k}(\varphi)\subseteq T_{k}(\psi)
,
T_{k}^{\Delta}(\varphi)\subseteq T_{k}^{\Delta}(\psi)
and

T_{k}^{\nabla}(\varphi)\subseteq T_{k}^{\nabla}(\psi)
. It is also clear that for every
setfunction
\varphi
,

Q_{k}(\varphi)\subseteq T_{k}^{\Delta}(\varphi)\subseteq T_{k}(\varphi)\qquad\text{and}\qquad Q_{k}(\varphi)\subseteq T_{k}^{\nabla}(\varphi)\subseteq T_{k}(\varphi).

When the setfunction
\varphi
is clear from the context, let us simply write
Q_{k}\subseteq T_{k}^{\Delta}\subseteq T_{k}
and
Q_{k}\subseteq T_{k}^{\nabla}\subseteq T_{k}
. We also write

(T_{k}\circ T_{m})(\varphi)=\bigcup_{\psi\in T_{m}(\varphi)}T_{k}(\psi)=\big\{(\varphi/\mathbf{U})/\mathbf{V}\colon\mathbf{U}\in\mathcal{B}^{m},\mathbf{V}\in(2^{[m]})^{k}\big\},

and similarly for the other variants.

The crop profile is in several respects simpler than the quotient profile. For
example, the sets
T_{k}
and
T_{k-1}
are related in a very simple way:
setfunctions in
T_{k-1}
can be obtained by restricting setfunctions in
T_{k}

to subsets of
[k-1]
. We also have
Q_{k}\subseteq T_{k}
, but the sets
T_{k}
will not approximate
Q_{k}
well; for example,
\psi([k])=\varphi(J)
for every
\psi\in Q_{k}(\varphi)
, but the identically zero function belongs to
T_{k}(\varphi)
. The next simple lemma does establish some connection.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
a\geq k\geq 1
and
b\geq 2^{k}
. Then
T_{k}=Q_{k}\circ T_{a}=T_{k}\circ T_{a}=T_{k}\circ Q_{b}
.

Proof.

It is clear that

Q_{k}\circ T_{a}\subseteq T_{k}\circ T_{a}\subseteq T_{k},\qquad\text{and}\qquad T_{k}\circ Q_{b}\subseteq T_{k}.

So it suffices to verify that

T_{k}\subseteq Q_{k}\circ T_{a},\qquad\text{and}\qquad T_{k}\subseteq T_{k}\circ Q_{b}.

Let
\varphi/\mathbf{A}
be an element of
T_{k}(\varphi)
, where
\mathbf{A}=(A_{1},\dots,A_{k})
with

A_{i}\in\mathcal{B}
for all
i\in[k]
. We define
\mathbf{A}^{\prime}=(A_{1},\dots,A_{k},A_{k},\dots,A_{k})
, where
A_{k}
is repeated

a-k+1
times, and
\mathcal{X}=(1,\dots,k-1,\{k,\dots,a\})
. Then
\varphi/\mathbf{A}=(\varphi/\mathbf{A}^{\prime})/\mathcal{X}\in Q_{k}(T_{a}(\varphi))
.

To prove the second containment, let
C_{1},\dots,C_{b}
be the atoms of the set
algebra
\mathfrak{A}(\mathbf{A})
with an appropriate number of empty sets added, and let

\mathbf{P}=(C_{1},\dots,C_{b})
. For
i\in[k]
, we can write
A_{i}=\cup_{j\in X_{i}}C_{j}

for an appropriate
X_{i}\subseteq[b]
. Let
\mathcal{X}=\{X_{1},\dots,X_{k}\}
, then

\varphi/\mathbf{A}=(\varphi/\mathbf{P})/\mathcal{X}\in T_{k}(Q_{2^{k}}(\varphi))
.
∎

Using the equality
T_{k}=T_{k}\circ Q_{2^{k}}
, we obtain the following.

Corollary 3.2
.

If a sequence
(\varphi_{n}\colon n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+})
of setfunctions is quotient-convergent,
then it is crop-convergent.

In particular, every tower is crop-convergent, where a
tower
is a sequence
(\varphi_{n}\colon n\in\mathbb{N})
of setfunctions such that
\varphi_{n}
is a quotient of
\varphi_{n+1}
for every
n
. However, the reverse implication does not necessarily hold: crop-convergence does not imply quotient-convergence in general.

Let
(\mathcal{M}_{n}=(E_{n},r_{n})\colon n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
) be a
sequence of matroids, with normalized rank functions
\rho_{n}=r_{n}/r_{n}(E_{n})
. We
say that the sequence
(\mathcal{M}_{n}\colon n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+})
is
crop-convergent
if their
normalized rank functions form a crop-convergent sequence. In the case of rank functions of finite matroids, we may restrict the sets
A_{i}
in the definition of crop-convergence to flats. In other words, if
\mathcal{L}_{n}

is the lattice of flats of
\mathcal{M}_{n}
, then the sets
T_{k}(\rho_{n})
depend only on the
lattice
\mathcal{L}_{n}
and on the restriction of
\varphi_{n}
to
\mathcal{L}_{n}
.

Let
\mathcal{M}=(E,r)
and
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}=(E^{\prime},r^{\prime})
be two matroids. Set
c=r(E)
and
c^{\prime}=r^{\prime}(E^{\prime})
. Also
consider the normalized rank functions
\rho=r/c
and
\rho^{\prime}=r^{\prime}/c^{\prime}
. Let
\mathcal{L}

and
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
be the lattices of flats in
\mathcal{M}
and
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}
, respectively. A
map
\Lambda\colon\mathcal{L}\to\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
is a
lattice embedding
if it is injective and
preserves joins and meets. A lattice embedding
\Lambda:~\mathcal{L}\to\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
is
called
rank preserving
if
r^{\prime}(\Lambda(Y))=r(Y)
for every
Y\in\mathcal{L}
, and it
is a
stretch embedding
if
r^{\prime}(\Lambda(Y))=(c^{\prime}/c)\cdot r(Y)
for every

Y\in\mathcal{L}
, or equivalently,
\rho^{\prime}(\Lambda(Y))=\rho(Y)
.

We need a couple of simple consequences of the existence of these embeddings.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\mathcal{M}=(E,r)
and
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}=(E^{\prime},r^{\prime})
be two matroids with
c=r(E)
and
c^{\prime}=r^{\prime}(E^{\prime})
, denote by
\rho=r/c
and
\rho^{\prime}=r^{\prime}/c^{\prime}
their normalized rank functions, and let
\mathcal{L}

and
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
be the lattices of flats in
\mathcal{M}
and
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}
, respectively, respectively.

(a)

If there exists a stretch embedding
\Lambda\colon\mathcal{L}\to\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
, then

T_{k}(\rho)\subseteq T_{k}(\rho^{\prime})
.

(b)

If there exists a rank preserving embedding
\Gamma\colon\mathcal{L}\to\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
, then
T_{k}(\rho)\subseteq(c^{\prime}/c)\cdot T_{k}(\rho^{\prime})
.

Proof.

First we prove
(a)
. Let
\psi\in T_{k}(\rho)
, then there exists a family

\mathbf{A}=(A_{1},\dots,A_{k})\in\mathcal{L}^{k}
of flats of
\mathcal{M}
such that
\psi=\rho/\mathbf{A}
. Let

B_{i}=\Lambda(A_{i})
and
\mathbf{B}=(B_{1},\dots,B_{k})\in(\mathcal{L}^{\prime})^{k}
. For
X\subseteq[k]
,
set
A_{X}=\cup_{i\in X}A_{i}
and
B_{X}=\cup_{i\in X}B_{i}
. Let
\overline{A}_{X}

denote the flat spanned by
A_{X}
. Then
\Lambda(\overline{A}_{X})
is the flat
spanned by
B_{X}
, and so we have

\rho^{\prime}(B_{X})=\rho(\Lambda(\overline{A}_{X}))=\rho(\overline{A}_{X})=\rho(A_{X}).

This shows that
\psi=\rho/\mathbf{A}=\rho^{\prime}/\mathbf{B}\in T_{k}(\rho^{\prime})
.

The proof of
(b)
is analogous.
∎

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
(\mathcal{M}_{n}=(E_{n},r_{n})\colon n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
) be a sequence of matroids with
r_{n}(E_{n})=n
, and
normalized rank functions
\rho_{n}=r_{n}/n
. Let
\mathcal{L}_{n}
be the family of flats in

\mathcal{M}_{n}
. Assume that

(i)

for all
m\leq n
, there exists a rank preserving embedding
\mathcal{L}_{m}\to\mathcal{L}_{n}
, and

(ii)

if
m\mid n
, then there exists a stretch embedding
\mathcal{L}_{m}\to\mathcal{L}_{n}
.

Then
(\rho_{n}\colon n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+})
is crop-convergent, and the limit of

k
-crops is given by
T_{k}(\rho_{n})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle{\text{\rm Haus}}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathbf{T}_{k}=\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}T_{k}(\rho_{n})
for all
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
.

Condition
(i)
is equivalent to saying that for
m\leq n
,
\mathcal{M}_{m}
is the restriction of
\mathcal{M}_{n}
to a flat.

Proof.

Suppose that
(T_{k}(\rho_{n})\colon n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+})
does not converge to
\mathbf{T}_{k}
. Then there exists an

\varepsilon>0
and an infinite sequence
(n_{i}\colon i\in\mathbb{Z}_{+})
of natural numbers such that

d^{\text{\rm Haus}}(T_{k}(\rho_{n_{i}}),\mathbf{T}_{k})\geq\varepsilon
for all
i\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
. Since
T_{k}(\rho_{n_{i}})\subseteq\mathbf{T}_{k}
,
this means that there are setfunctions
\psi_{i}\in\mathbf{T}_{k}
for
i\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
such that

\|\psi_{i}-\sigma\|\geq\varepsilon
for every
\sigma\in T_{k}(\rho_{n_{i}})
. Since
\mathbf{T}_{k}

is bounded, we may assume that the setfunctions
\psi_{i}
form a Cauchy
sequence. Let
s\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
be chosen so that
\|\psi_{i}-\psi_{j}\|\leq\varepsilon/2
for
all
i,j\geq s
.

Since
\psi_{s}\in\mathbf{T}_{k}
, there is an
m
such that
\psi_{s}\in\mathbf{T}_{k}(\rho_{m})
.
Write
n_{i}=a_{i}\cdot m+b_{i}
, where
a_{i},b_{i}\geq 0
are integers and
b_{i}<m
. By Lemma

3.3
(a)
, we have
\psi_{s}\in T_{k}(\rho_{a_{i}m})
; by Lemma

3.3
(b)
, it follows that

\sigma_{i}=\frac{a_{i}\cdot m}{n_{i}}\psi_{s}\in T_{k}(\rho_{n_{i}}),

and

\|\psi_{s}-\sigma_{i}\|=\Big\|\Big(1-\frac{a_{i}\cdot m}{n_{i}}\Big)\cdot\psi_{s}\Big\|\leq\frac{b_{i}}{n_{i}}<\frac{m}{n_{i}}<\frac{\varepsilon}{2}

if
i
is large enough. Then

\|\psi_{i}-\sigma_{i}\|\leq\|\psi_{i}-\psi_{s}\|+\|\psi_{s}-\sigma_{i}\|<\frac{\varepsilon}{2}+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}=\varepsilon,

contradicting the definition of
\psi_{i}
.
∎

Corollary 3.5
.

Let
(\mathcal{M}_{n}=(E_{n},r_{n})\colon n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+})
be a sequence of matroids, with

r_{n}(E_{n})=n
and normalized rank functions
\rho_{n}=r_{n}/n
. Assume that

\mathcal{M}_{a}\oplus\mathcal{M}_{b}
embeds into
\mathcal{M}_{a+b}
for every
a,b\geq 1
. Then the
sequence
(\mathcal{M}_{n}\colon n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+})
is crop-convergent.

Proof.

We show that conditions
(i)
and
(ii)
of Theorem
3.4
hold. We
may assume that
m<n
. First,
\mathcal{M}_{m}
trivially embeds into
\mathcal{M}_{m}\oplus\mathcal{M}_{n-m}
, and in turn, this embeds into
\mathcal{M}_{n}
, so
\mathcal{M}_{m}
embeds into

\mathcal{M}_{n}
. Second,
\mathcal{M}_{m}
has a stretch embedding into
\mathcal{M}_{m}\oplus\dots\oplus\mathcal{M}_{m}
where we have
n/m
terms, simply by
x\mapsto(x,\dots,x)
. In turn,

\mathcal{M}_{m}\oplus\dots\oplus\mathcal{M}_{m}
embeds into
\mathcal{M}_{n}
; this follows by induction
from the hypothesis. So
\mathcal{M}_{m}
has a stretch embedding into
\mathcal{M}_{n}
.
∎

Example 3.6
.

Let
\mathcal{M}_{n}
be the cycle matroid of the complete graph
K_{n+1}
. Let
a,b\geq 1
, and let
G
denote the graph obtained by identifying a node of
K_{a+1}

with a node of
K_{b+1}
. It is easy to see that the cycle matroid
\mathcal{M}_{G}
of

G
is isomorphic to
\mathcal{M}_{a}\oplus\mathcal{M}_{b}
, and trivially
\mathcal{M}_{G}
is a
restriction of
\mathcal{M}_{a+b}
. So
(\mathcal{M}_{n}\colon n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+})
is crop-convergent by
Corollary
3.5
.

Example 3.7
.

Let
\mathbb{F}
be a finite field,
|\mathbb{F}|=q
, and let
\rho_{n}=\dim/n
denote the
normalized rank function of the linear space
\mathbb{F}^{n}
. It is easy to see that the
matroid direct sum of
\mathbb{F}^{a}
and
\mathbb{F}^{b}
“almost” embeds into
\mathbb{F}^{a+b}
– the
0
vectors in
\mathbb{F}^{a}
and

(i)
and
(ii)

3.2
Quotient-convergence versus crop-convergence

We need to introduce a property expressing that a matroid is “rich”:

For any two flats
F\subseteq A
where
r(A)\geq m
, we have

|A\setminus F|\geq k\cdot(r(A)-r(F))
.

(R(k,m))

In particular, this implies that for any flat
A
with
r(A)\geq m
, we have
|A|\geq k\cdot r(A)
.

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
\mathcal{M}=(E,r)
be a matroid satisfying
(R(k,m))
for some
k,m\geq 1
.
Let
A_{1},\dots,A_{k}
be flats of
\mathcal{M}
with
r(A_{i})\geq m
for every
i\in[k]
. Then
there are disjoint subsets
B_{i}\subseteq A_{i}
such that
B_{i}
is a basis of

A_{i}
for
i\in[k]
, that is,
B_{i}
is an independent subset of
A_{i}
of size
r(A_{i})
.

Proof.

Set
r_{i}(X)\coloneqq r(X\cap A_{i})
for all
X\subseteq E
. Then
\mathcal{M}_{i}=(E,r_{i})
is a matroid, and we need to show that there are disjoint bases
B_{i}
of
\mathcal{M}_{i}
. This is equivalent to the
assertion that the sum
\mathcal{M}_{\Sigma}
of the matroids
\mathcal{M}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{M}_{k}
has rank at least

\sum_{i=1}^{k}r(A_{i})
. Let
r_{\Sigma}
denote the rank function of
\mathcal{M}_{\Sigma}
. Then, by the Matroid Sum Theorem
[
8
]
, we have

r_{\Sigma}(E)=\min\Bigl\{|Y|+\sum_{i=1}^{k}r_{i}(E\setminus Y)\colon Y\subseteq E\Bigr\}.

Therefore, it suffices to show that

|Y|+\sum_{i=1}^{k}r_{i}(E\setminus Y)=|Y|+\sum_{i=1}^{k}r(A_{i}\setminus Y)\geq\sum_{i=1}^{k}r(A_{i})

for all
Y\subseteq E
. Let
F_{i}
be the closure of
A_{i}\setminus Y
, and let

i\in[k]
be an index such that
r(A_{i})-r(F_{i})=\max\{r(A_{j})-r(B_{j})\colon j\in[k]\}
. Then, by
(R(k,m))
,

|Y|\geq|A_{i}\cap Y|\geq|A_{i}\setminus F_{i}|\geq k\cdot(r(A_{i})-r(F_{i}))\geq\sum_{i=1}^{k}(r(A_{i})-r(F_{i})),

and so

|Y|+\sum_{i=1}^{k}r(A_{i}\setminus Y)=|Y|+\sum_{i=1}^{k}r(F_{i})\geq\sum_{i=1}^{k}r(A_{i}),

concluding the proof of the lemma.
∎

Lemma 3.9
.

Let
\mathcal{M}=(E,r)
be a matroid satisfying
(R(k,m))
for some
m\geq k\geq 1
.
Then

d^{\text{\rm Haus}}\bigl(T_{k}(\rho),T_{k}^{\Delta}(\rho)\bigr)\leq\frac{k\cdot m}{r(E)}

and

d^{\text{\rm Haus}}\bigl(T_{k}^{\nabla}(\rho),Q_{k}(\rho)\bigr)\leq\frac{k\cdot m}{r(E)}.

Proof.

It suffices to show that every
\psi\in T_{k}(\rho)
can be approximated by a
\psi^{\prime}\in T_{k}^{\Delta}(\rho)
, and every
\sigma\in T_{k}^{\nabla}(\rho)
can be approximated by a
\sigma^{\prime}\in Q_{k}(\rho)
.

By definition,
\psi=\rho/\mathbf{A}
for some
\mathbf{A}=(A_{1},\dots,A_{k})\in\mathbf{B}^{k}
. Since taking the closure of each
A_{i}
does not change
\psi
, we may assume that the sets
A_{i}
are flats. For every
i
with
r(A_{i})<m
, we increase
A_{i}
to a
flat
A_{i}^{\prime}
with
r(A_{i}^{\prime})=m
; else we let
A_{i}^{\prime}=A_{i}
. Let

\mathbf{A}^{\prime}=(A^{\prime}_{1},\dots,A^{\prime}_{k})
. By Lemma
3.8
, there exist disjoint sets

B_{i}
such that
B_{i}
is a basis of
A_{i}^{\prime}
for
i\in[k]
. Let

\mathbf{B}=(B_{1},\dots,B_{k})
, then
\psi^{\prime}=\rho/\mathbf{B}=\rho/\mathbf{A}^{\prime}\in T_{k}^{\Delta}(\rho)
. It
is clear that
\psi^{\prime}\geq\psi
. Moreover, subadditivity of the rank function
implies that

\psi^{\prime}(X)=\rho\Bigl(\bigcup_{i\in X}A^{\prime}_{i}\Bigr)\leq\rho\Bigl(\bigcup_{i\in X}A_{i}\Bigr)+\sum_{i}(\rho(A_{i}^{\prime})-\rho(A_{i}))\leq\psi(X)+\frac{k\cdot m}{r(E)},

proving the first inequality.

To prove the second, let
\sigma=\rho/\mathbf{A}
, where
\mathbf{A}=(A_{1},\dots,A_{k})\in\mathbf{B}^{k}

covers
E
. We construct the sets
A_{i}^{\prime}
and
B_{i}
as above, and let
B=\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}B_{i}
. We add each element
u\in E\setminus B
to
B_{i}
where
i
is the smallest index
with
u\in A_{i}
. Since
\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}A_{i}=E
, this gives a partition
\mathcal{P}
of
E
for which
\psi^{\prime}=\rho/\mathcal{P}
is close to
\sigma
by the same calculation as
above.
∎

As a corollary, we get the following.

Corollary 3.10
.

Let
f\colon\mathbb{Z}_{+}\to\mathbb{Z}_{+}
be a function, and let
(\mathcal{M}_{n}=(E_{n},r_{n})\colon n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+})
be a
sequence of matroids satisfying
r_{n}(E_{n})\to\infty
and
(
R(k,f(k))
)
for every
k\in\mathbb{Z}
.

(a)

The sequence
(r_{n}\colon n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+})
is crop-convergent if and only if it is
\Delta
-convergent.

(b)

The sequence
(r_{n}\colon n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+})
is quotient-convergent if and only if it is
\nabla
-convergent.

4
Finite linear spaces

We start with a special example to illustrate the use of crop-convergence in
proving quotient-convergence.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\mathbb{F}
be a finite field,
|\mathbb{F}|=q
, and let
\rho_{n}=\dim/n
denote the
normalized rank function of the linear space
\mathbb{F}^{n}
. Then the sequence

(\rho_{n}\colon n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+})
is quotient-convergent.

Proof.

We start by observing that the sequence
(\mathbb{F}^{n}\colon n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+})
has two important properties. First, if
m\leq n
, then the map
\mathcal{L}_{m}\to\mathcal{L}_{n}
defined by
A\mapsto A\oplus\mathbf{0}
, where
\mathbf{0}
is the zero subspace of
\mathbb{F}^{n-m}
, is a rank-preserving lattice embedding. Second, if
m\mid n
, then the map
\mathcal{L}_{m}\to\mathcal{L}_{n}
defined by
A\mapsto A\oplus\dots\oplus A
, with
n/m
terms, is a stretch embedding.

It follows that the sequence
(\mathbb{F}^{n}\colon n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+})
satisfies the conditions of Theorem
3.4
, and the sequence
(T_{k}(\mathbb{F}^{n})\colon n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+})
is
convergent in the Hausdorff distance; let
\mathbf{T}_{k}
be its limit. It is not hard to verify that every matroid
\mathbb{F}^{n}
satisfies condition

(
R(k,2k)
)
for every
k
and
n
, and hence Lemma
3.9
implies
that the sequence
(T^{\Delta}_{k}(\mathbb{F}^{n})\colon n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+})
converges to
\mathbf{T}_{k}
in the
Hausdorff distance as well. We claim that the sets
Q_{k}(\rho_{n})
converge in Hausdorff distance to the set

\mathbf{Q}_{k}=\{\psi\in\mathbf{T}_{k}\colon\max_{i}\psi(\{i\})=1\}

for every
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
. Let
\psi\in Q_{k}(\rho_{n})
, and write
\psi=\rho_{n}/\mathcal{P}
where
\mathcal{P}=(A_{1},\dots,A_{k})
is a measurable partition of
\mathbb{F}^{n}
. Clearly,
there exists
i\in[k]
such that
|A_{i}|\geq q^{n}/k
. This implies that

\dim(A_{i})\geq n-\log_{q}k
and hence

\max_{i\in[k]}\psi(\{i\})\geq 1-\frac{\log_{q}k}{n}.

This, together with
Q_{k}(\rho_{n})\subseteq T_{k}(\rho_{n})
, implies that every limit point of the sequence

Q_{k}(\rho_{n})
is in
\mathbf{Q}_{k}
. On the other hand, let

\psi\in\mathbf{Q}_{k}
, where we may assume without loss of generality that
\psi(\{k\})=1
. This implies that
\psi(X)=1
for every
X\subseteq[k]
such that
k\in X
.

Let
\varepsilon>0
. We know that
\psi\in\mathbf{T}_{k}
, so there exists a family

\mathbf{B}=(B_{1},\dots,B_{k})
of disjoint subsets of
\mathbb{F}^{n}
such that

\|\rho_{n}/\mathbf{B}-\psi\|\leq\varepsilon
. Let us replace
B_{k}
by the set

B_{k}^{\prime}=\mathbb{F}^{n}\setminus\bigcup_{j=1}^{k-1}B_{j}
, then we get a partition
\mathcal{P}
of

\mathbb{F}^{n}
such that
\rho_{n}/\mathcal{P}\geq\rho_{n}/\mathbf{B}
, and so

\|\rho_{n}/\mathcal{P}-\rho_{n}/\mathbf{B}\|=\rho_{n}(B^{\prime}_{k})-\rho_{n}(B_{k})\leq\frac{\log_{q}k}{n}.

It follows that

\|\rho_{n}/\mathcal{P}-\psi\|\leq\varepsilon+\frac{\log_{q}k}{n}=\varepsilon+o(1),

proving that
Q_{k}(\mathbb{F}^{n})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle{\text{\rm Haus}}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathbf{T}_{k}
as
n\to\infty
.
∎

Despite the above progress, we cannot yet describe the limit object in precise terms.

Problem 4.2
.

What is a good representation of the limit of this sequence of finite linear
spaces? Is it related to von Neumann’s continuous geometry over
\mathbb{F}
?

5
Dense convergence and quotient-convergence

5.1
Cycle matroids

For a graph
G=(V,E)
, we denote the rank function of its cycle matroid by
r_{G}\colon 2^{E}\to\mathbb{Z}_{+}
. We normalize by
|V|
, to get
\rho_{G}=r_{G}/|V|
. Note that this is slightly different from the normalization of general matroids, but for the graph sequences we will be interested in, it is asymptotically the same.

In
[
10
]
, the matroid of a graphing was defined, and in
[
2
]
it was proved that for a locally-globally convergent sequences of bounded-degree graphs, their cycle matroids quotient-converge to the matroid of the limit graphing. It is natural to conjecture that the cycle matroids of a dense-convergent sequence of graphs also form a quotient-convergent sequence. However, this is not the case; see Example
5.1
for a counterexample. On the other hand, we do establish convergence for the sequence of graphs which converge to a positive graphon.

Example 5.1
.

Consider the following sequence of graphs: let
G_{1}=K_{1}
,
G_{2}=K_{2}
, and for
n\geq 3
, define
G_{n}
to be a tree if
n
is odd, and the edge-disjoint union of two spanning trees if
n
is even, with
|V(G_{n})|=n
for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
. The sequence
(G_{n}\colon n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+})
is
convergent in the dense sense as it tends to the zero graphon. On the other
hand, the sequence
(\rho_{n}\colon n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+})
of their normalized rank functions is
not quotient-convergent. For odd
n
, every
2
-quotient
\varphi
in
Q_{2}(\rho_{n})

satisfies
\varphi(\{1\})+\varphi(\{2\})=\varphi(\{1,2\})=1
, while for even
n
, there is a

2
-quotient
\varphi
with
\varphi(\{1\})=\varphi(\{2\})=\varphi(\{1,2\})=1
, at distance
1/2
from every

Q_{2}(\rho_{n^{\prime}})
where
n^{\prime}
is odd.

Note that the graphs in the sequence thus obtained are not dense since
G_{n}
has
o(n^{2})
edges. However, one can modify the construction to obtain a sequence of dense graphs by considering the node-disjoint union of
G_{n}
and the complete graph
K_{n}
for
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
. For every even
n\geq 4
,
E(G_{n})
has a partition into 4 classes such that two of those are spanning trees of
G_{n}
, while the other two form connected spanning subgraphs of
K_{n}
. The corresponding quotient
(U,\rho)
is the matroid rank function of two pairs of parallel points, divided by
2
. It is easy to check that for large odd
n
, no such partition exists even
\varepsilon
-close to this.

We say that a graphon
W
is positive if
W(x,y)>0
for almost every
x,y\in[0,1]
, denoted by
W>0
. Our main result in this section is the following.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
(G_{n}\colon n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+})
be a sequence of dense graphs that converges to a positive graphon
W
. Then the sequence
(\rho_{n}\colon n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+})
of normalized cycle matroid rank functions is quotient-convergent. Furthermore, the limit does not depend on
W
.

The rest of the section is devoted to proving the the theorem.

5.1.1
Graphs close to a positive graphon

We will need a well-known fact from analysis.

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
(\Omega,\mu)
a probability space, and let
f\colon\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
be a measurable function such that
\mu\{f>0\}=1
. Then for all
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a
\delta>0
such that if
\mu(H)>\varepsilon
, then
\int_{H}f\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu>\delta
.

We need some lemmas about graph-theoretic consequences for a graph to be close to a positive graphon. In the rest of the section, we consider
W
to be a fixed positive graphon. We start with a simple observation.

Lemma 5.4
.

For every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists
\delta>0
such that, if
G=(V,E)
is a graph with
\|W_{G}-W\|_{\square}<\delta
, then
|V|>n
.

Proof.

As
G
is simple,
W_{G}
is
0
on the diagonal. Therefore
\|W_{G}-W\|_{\square}>0
.
There is a finite number of graphs with at most
n
nodes, and each has positive distance from
W
, since the corresponding graphons are 0 blocks on the diagonal. We can choose
\delta
to be the minimum of these distances.
∎

Let
\lambda_{n}
denote the Lebesgue measure in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. For any graph
F
, the function
W^{F}\colon~(x_{1},\dots,x_{r})\mapsto\prod_{(i,j)\in E(F)}W(x_{i},x_{j})
is almost everywhere positive. Hence for every
\delta_{0}>0
, Proposition
5.3
yields the existence of a
\varepsilon>0
such that for any
S\subseteq[0,1]^{r}
with
\lambda_{r}(S)\geq\varepsilon
, we have

\int_{S}\prod_{ij\in E(K_{r})}W(x_{i},x_{j})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\lambda_{r}(x)\geq\delta_{0}.

(1)

We will use this lower bound in the following proofs.

Lemma 5.5
.

For every
r\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
and
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
\delta>0
such that, if
G=(V,E)
is a graph with
\|W_{G}-W\|_{\square}<\delta
and
A\subseteq V
with
|A|\geq\varepsilon\cdot|V|
, then
G[A]
contains a
K_{r}
.

Proof.

Let
\delta_{0}>0
be the value corresponding to
\varepsilon
provided by (
1
), and set
\delta\coloneqq\delta_{0}/(2\cdot\binom{k}{2})
. Consider any graph
G
with
\|W_{G}-W\|_{\square}<\delta
. Let
A\subseteq V(G)
satisfying
|A|\geq\varepsilon\cdot|V(G)|
, and let
A^{\prime}\subseteq[0,1]
be the set corresponding to the nodes in
A
.
We define a graphon
W^{\prime}
as

W^{\prime}(x,y)=\begin{cases}W(x,y)&\text{if $x,y\in A^{\prime}$},\\
0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Let
G^{\prime}
denote the graph obtained from
G
by deleting all edges not spanned by
A
. Clearly,
\|W_{G^{\prime}}-W^{\prime}\|_{\square}\leq\|W_{G}-W\|_{\square}<\delta
. By the Counting Lemma
[
9
, Lemma 10.23]
,

|t(K_{r},G^{\prime})-t(K_{r},W^{\prime})|\leq\binom{k}{2}\cdot\|W_{G^{\prime}}-W^{\prime}\|_{\square}\leq\delta_{0}/2.

Since (
1
) implies
t(K_{r},W^{\prime})\geq\delta_{0}
and hence
t(K_{r},G^{\prime})\geq\delta_{0}/2>0
,

contains a complete graph

.
∎

Corollary 5.6
.

For every
r\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
and
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
\delta>0
such that, if
G=(V,E)
is a graph with
\|W_{G}-W\|_{\square}<\delta
, then there exist node-disjoint copies of
K_{r}
in

(1-\varepsilon)\cdot|V(G)|
nodes.

Proof.

Take node-disjoint copies of
K_{r}
greedily; by Lemma
5.5
, fewer than
\varepsilon\cdot|V|
nodes remain uncovered.
∎

Given a graph
G=(V,E)
and
A,B\subseteq V
, we denote the number of edges with one endpoint in
A
, and the other in
B
, by

.

Lemma 5.7
.

For every
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
\delta>0
such that, if
G=(V,E)
is a graph with
\|W_{G}-W\|_{\square}<\delta
and
A,B\subseteq V
with
|A|,|B|\geq\varepsilon\cdot|V|
, then

.

Proof.

By Proposition
5.3
, there exists a
\delta>0
such that
\int_{X\times Y}W\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\lambda_{2}\geq 3\,\delta
whenever
\lambda_{2}(X\times Y)\geq\varepsilon^{2}
. Consider any graph
G
with
\|W_{G}-W\|_{\square}<\delta
. Let
A,B\subseteq V(G)
satisfying
|A|,|B|\geq\varepsilon\cdot|V(G)|
, and let
A^{\prime}\subseteq[0,1]
and
B^{\prime}\subseteq[0,1]
be the sets of points in
W_{G}
corresponding to the nodes in
A
and
B
, respectively. We define a graphon
W^{\prime}
as

W^{\prime}(x,y)=\begin{cases}W(x,y)&\text{if $(x,y)\in(A^{\prime}\times B^{\prime})\cup(B^{\prime}\times A^{\prime})$},\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Let
G^{\prime}
denote the subgraph of
G
consisting of those edges having one endpoint in

and the other one in
B

\|W_{G^{\prime}}-W^{\prime}\|_{\square}\leq\|W_{G}-W\|_{\square}\leq\delta
. By the Counting Lemma
[

, we get
|t(K_{2},W^{\prime})-t(K_{2},W_{G^{\prime}})|\leq\delta
. by the definition of

t(K_{2},W^{\prime})\geq 3\,\delta
, and so
e(A,B)\geq t(K_{2},W_{G^{\prime}})\cdot|V|^{2}/2\geq(t(K_{2},W^{\prime})-\delta)\cdot|V|^{2}/2\geq\delta\,|V|^{2}
.
∎

5.1.2
Edge-colorings

We need a couple of additional lemmas about graphs that are close to the positive graphon
W
, focusing on properties of their edge-colorings, which define their quotients. For an edge-coloring
c\colon E(G)\to[\ell]
of a graph
G
, let
D_{c}(u)
denote the number of nodes in the connected component of color
c
containing
u\in V(G)
. For
A\subseteq[\ell]
, an
A

is a connected component of the subgraph formed by edges of color in
A
.

Lemma 5.8
.

For every
\ell,N\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
and
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a number
\delta>0
such that, if
G=(V,E)
is a graph with
\|W_{G}-W\|_{\square}<\delta
, and
c\colon E(G)\to[\ell]
is an edge-coloring of
G
, then there exists a set
U\subseteq V(G)
with
|U|\geq(1-\varepsilon)\cdot|V|
and a function
f\colon U\times U\to[\ell]
such that for any two nodes
u,v\in U
we have
D_{f(u,v)}(u),D_{f(u,v)}(v)\geq N
.

Proof.

For each node
v
, let
L_{v}
be the set of colors
c
for which the
c
-colored component of
v
has size at least
N
. Note that if
d(v)>\ell\cdot(N-1)
, then
|L_{v}|\geq 1
. Consider the partition of the nodes into
2^{\ell}
– maybe empty – classes, where two nodes
u
and
v
are in the same class if and only if
L_{u}=L_{v}
. We denote the class of
v
by
P_{v}
. For a class
A
, we denote by
L_{A}
the set of colors corresponding to the nodes in
A
. We call two classes
A
and
B
compatible, denoted
A\sim B
, if they have a color in common. That is,

if
L_{A}\cap L_{B}\neq\emptyset
. We show that if
A\not\sim B
, then at least one of these two classes is ‘small’.

Claim 5.9
.

For every
\varepsilon^{\prime}>0
, there exists
\delta>0
such that if
A
and
B
are two non-compatible classes of
G
, then
\min\{|A|,|B|\}\leq\varepsilon^{\prime}\cdot|V|
.

Proof of claim..

Let
\delta_{1}
be the value
\delta
in Lemma
5.7
for
\varepsilon^{\prime}
, and let
\delta_{2}
be the value
\delta
in Lemma

for

. Let
\delta=\min\{\delta_{1},\delta_{2}\}
.
Assume that there exist incompatible classes
A\not\sim B
such that
|A|,|B|\geq\varepsilon^{\prime}\cdot|V|
. By the choice of
\delta
, we have
|V|>N\cdot\ell/\delta_{1}
and
e(A,B)\geq\delta_{2}\cdot|V|^{2}
.
We obtain a contradiction by bounding the number of edges between
A
and
B
locally, using the fact that they have different color lists.

Observe that for every
a\in A
, the number of edges from
a
to
B
, denoted
\deg(a,B)
, is less than
\ell\cdot N
. Indeed, if
c_{0}\in L_{a}
, then no edge of color
c_{0}
can connect
a
to any node
b\in B
, since then
b
would belong to a large
c_{0}
-component, implying
c_{0}\in L_{b}
and thus contradicting
A\not\sim B
. On the other hand, if
c_{0}\notin L_{a}
, then
a
has fewer than
N
edges of color
c_{0}
by definition. Thus,
\deg(a,B)\leq|L_{a}|\cdot(N-1)<\ell\cdot N
. So the number of edges between
A
and
B
is at most

|A|\cdot\ell\cdot N\leq|V|\cdot\ell\cdot N
. Hence
|V|\cdot\ell\cdot N\geq\delta_{1}\cdot|V|^{2}
, or equivalently,
|V|\leq\ell\cdot N/\delta_{1}
, a contradiction.
∎

Let
\varepsilon^{\prime}=\varepsilon/\binom{2^{\ell}}{2}
. By Claim
5.9
, for each non-compatible pair, at least one of the classes has size at most
\varepsilon^{\prime}\cdot|V(G)|
. Let
U\subseteq V(G_{n})
be the set of nodes not contained in small classes, or in other words, get rid of the small classes. Then
|U|\geq(1-\binom{2^{\ell}}{2}\cdot\varepsilon^{\prime})\cdot|V(G_{n})|=(1-\varepsilon)\cdot|V(G_{n})|
. Furthermore, for any pair
u,v\in U
, their classes are compatible, so we can choose
f(u,v)
to be any color in
L_{u}\cap L_{v}
, concluding the proof of the lemma.
∎

Remark 5.12
.

Theorem
5.2
is equivalent to the following:
If
(G_{n}\colon n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+})
is a sequence of dense graphs such that the limit of every convergent subsequence is a positive graphon, then the sequence
(\rho_{n}\colon n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+})
of normalized cycle matroid rank functions is quotient-convergent.
It would be interesting to describe the limit object in a transparent way.

5.2
Cut capacities

Let
G=(V,E)
be a finite graph. We define the
normalized cut capacity function

\kappa_{G}(X)=e(X,V\setminus X)/|E|
, where
e(X,V\setminus X)
is the number of
edges going between
X
and
V\setminus X
, or in other words, the size of the cut defined by
X\subseteq V
.

Let
J=[0,1)
and
W\colon J\times J\to[0,1]
be a graphon. Then the cut capacity function
\kappa_{W}
of the graphon is then defined as

\kappa_{W}(X)=\frac{\int_{X\times(J\setminus X)}W(x,y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\lambda(x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\lambda(y)}{\int_{J\times J}W(x,y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\lambda(x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\lambda(y)}

The cut capacity
\kappa_{W}
is a submodular setfunction on the Borel sets in
[0,1]
.

Theorem 5.13
.

Let
(G_{n}\colon n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+})
be a sequence of graphs that is convergent in the dense
sense. Then the sequence
(\kappa_{G_{n}}\colon n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+})
is quotient-convergent.
Furthermore, if
G_{n}\to W
for some graphon
W
, then
Q_{k}(\kappa_{G_{n}})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle{\text{\rm Haus}}}}{{\longrightarrow}}Q_{k}(\kappa_{W})
for every
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
.

Proof.

Let
\varepsilon>0
. Our goal is to prove that if
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
are two graphs with
at least
m_{0}=32(k+1)/\varepsilon^{2}
nodes, then

d^{\text{\rm Haus}}(Q_{k}(\kappa_{G_{1}}),Q_{k}(\kappa_{G_{2}}))\leq\delta_{\square}(G_{1},G_{2})+\varepsilon.

(2)

We start with proving similar bounds for special pairs of graphs. First, let

G_{1}
and
G_{2}
be two graphs on the same set
V
of nodes, and let
m=|V|
.
Then

d^{\text{\rm Haus}}(Q_{k}(\kappa_{G_{1}}),Q_{k}(\kappa_{G_{2}}))\leq d_{\square}(G_{1},G_{2}),

(3)

since for every
X\subseteq V
,

|\kappa_{G_{1}}(X)-\kappa_{G_{2}}(X)|=\frac{1}{m^{2}}\cdot|e_{G_{1}}(X,V\setminus X)-e_{G_{2}}(X,V\setminus X)|\leq d_{\square}(G_{1},G_{2}).

Recall that the graph
G(n)
is obtained from the graph
G
by replacing each node
u

by a set
V_{u}
of
n
non-adjacent copies and connecting all nodes of
V_{u}
to all nodes of
V_{v}
if
uv\in E(G)
.
We claim that for every graph
G=(V,E)
with
m=|V|\geq m_{0}
nodes, and every
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
,

d^{\text{\rm Haus}}(Q_{k}(\kappa_{G}),Q_{k}(\kappa_{G(n)}))\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{4}.

(4)

It is clear that
Q_{k}(\kappa_{G})\subseteq Q_{k}(\kappa_{G(n)})
. To prove
almost-containment in the other direction, let
X\subseteq V(G(n))
and let

x_{u}=|X\cap V_{u}|
. Let
Y
be a random subset obtained from
X
by replacing

X\cap V_{u}
by
V_{u}
with probability
x_{u}/n
, and by
\emptyset
with
probability
1-x_{u}/n
. Then

\mathbb{E}(\kappa_{G(n)}(Y))=\frac{1}{n^{2}\cdot|E(G)|}\cdot\sum_{ij\in E(G)}\big(x_{i}\cdot(n-x_{j})+x_{j}\cdot(n-x_{i})\big)=\kappa_{G(n)}(X).

Azuma’s Inequality
[
1
]
implies that

\Pr\Big(|\kappa_{G(n)}(Y)-\kappa_{G(n)}(X)|>\frac{\varepsilon}{4}\Big)\leq 2\cdot e^{-\varepsilon^{2}\cdot m/32}.

Now if
\mathcal{P}=(U_{1},\dots,U_{k})
is a
k
-partition of
V(G(n))
, then we can
“round” it to a
k
-partition
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}=(U^{\prime}_{1},\dots,U^{\prime}_{k})
that does not split
any of the sets
V_{u}
, by placing
V_{u}
in class
U^{\prime}_{i}
with probability

|U_{i}\cap V_{u}|/n
. Taking the union bound over all sets that are unions of the
sets
U_{i}
, we get

\Pr\Big(\|\kappa_{G(n)/\mathcal{P}}-\kappa_{G(n)/\mathcal{P}^{\prime}}\|\geq\frac{\varepsilon}{4}\Big)\leq 2^{k+1}\cdot e^{-\varepsilon^{2}\cdot m/32}<1.

So
G(n)
has a
k
-partition
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
with

\|\kappa_{G(n)/\mathcal{P}}-\kappa_{G(n)/\mathcal{P}^{\prime}}\|\leq\varepsilon/4
. The partition
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}

defines a
k
-partition
\mathcal{P}^{\prime\prime}
of
V(G)
such that

\kappa_{G(n)/\mathcal{P}^{\prime}}=\kappa_{G/\mathcal{P}^{\prime\prime}}
. Thus there is a
k
-partition
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
of

V(G)
such that

\|\kappa_{G(n)/\mathcal{P}}-\kappa_{G/\mathcal{P}^{\prime\prime}}\|\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{4},

proving (
4
).

Finally, let
G_{i}
be a graph with
n_{i}\geq m_{0}
nodes for
i=1,2
. By the
definition of the
\delta_{\square}
distance, there is an integer
p\geq 1
such
that, for an appropriate identification of the node sets of the graphs

G_{1}^{\prime}=G_{1}(p\cdot n_{2})
and
G_{2}^{\prime}=G_{2}(p\cdot n_{1})
, we have

d_{\square}(G_{1}^{\prime},G_{2}^{\prime})\leq\delta_{\square}(G_{1},G_{2})+\frac{\varepsilon}{4}.

Then, by (
3
) and (
4
), we get

\displaystyle d^{\text{\rm Haus}}(Q_{k}(\kappa_{G_{1}}),Q_{k}(\kappa_{G_{2}}))

\displaystyle\quad\leq d^{\text{\rm Haus}}(Q_{k}(\kappa_{G_{1}}),Q_{k}(\kappa_{G_{1}^{\prime}}))+d^{\text{\rm Haus}}(Q_{k}(\kappa_{G_{2}}),Q_{k}(\kappa_{G_{2}^{\prime}}))+d^{\text{\rm Haus}}(Q_{k}(\kappa_{G_{1}^{\prime}}),Q_{k}(\kappa_{G_{2}^{\prime}}))

\displaystyle\quad\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{4}+\frac{\varepsilon}{4}+d_{\square}(G_{1}^{\prime},G_{2}^{\prime})

\displaystyle\quad\leq\delta_{\square}(G_{1},G_{2})+\varepsilon.

This proves (
2
). The first assertion of the theorem is then immediate by (
2
). The proof of the second assertion is similar.
∎

Remark 5.14
.

The proof above is quite similar to the proof of
[
6
, Theorem 2.8]

and of
[
9
, Lemma 12.3]
. In fact, the quotient sets in the
characterization of
right convergence
are quite similar to the quotient sets
for the cut capacity function. Recall that one can equivalently define the convergence of dense graph sequences using the notion of
quotient sets

[
6
]
. A
k
-quotient

G/\mathcal{P}
of a finite graph
G=(V,E)
is defined by a
k
-partition
\mathcal{P}=(V_{1},\dots,V_{k})
of
V
. It is a weighted graph on
[k]
with node weights

\alpha(i)=\alpha_{G/\mathcal{P}}(i)=\frac{|V_{i}|}{|V|}\qquad\text{for all $i\in[k]$}

and edge weights

\beta(ij)=\beta_{G/\mathcal{P}}(ij)=\frac{e_{G}(V_{i},V_{j})}{|V_{i}|\cdot|V_{j}|}\qquad\text{for all $i,j\in[k]$}.

We represent
G/\mathcal{P}
as a vector in
\mathbb{R}^{m}
, where
m=2k+\binom{k}{2}
. The coordinates correspond to the
\binom{k}{2}
edge weights,
k
loop weights, and
k
node weights. Let
S_{k}(G)
denote the set of all
k
-quotients of
G
. This set can be viewed as a subset of the space
\mathbb{R}^{m}
. A sequence of graphs
(G_{n}\colon n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+})
is said to be
right-convergent
if
|V(G_{n})|\to\infty
and the sequence of sets
S_{k}(G_{n})
converges in Hausdorff distance for all
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
.

In the light of this similarity, it seems natural to ask whether a converse to Theorem
5.13
holds. That is, assume that
(G_{n}\colon n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+})
is a sequence of graphs with
|V(G_{n})|\to\infty
, and that
(\kappa_{G_{n}}\colon n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+})
is quotient-convergent. Does it then follow that
(G_{n}\colon n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+})
is convergent in the dense sense? To motivate this question, we consider a modified version of quotients. Let us introduce the weights

\gamma(ij)=\gamma_{G/\mathcal{P}}(ij)=\alpha(i)\cdot\alpha(j)\cdot\beta(ij)=\frac{e_{G}(V_{i},V_{j})}{|V|^{2}}.

We change the edge weights of
G/\mathcal{P}
to
\gamma(ij)
, and ignore the node weights;
let us denote the edge-weighted graph obtained this way by
G\angle\mathcal{P}
. We denote by

S^{\prime}_{k}(G)
the set of modified
k
-quotients
G\angle\mathcal{P}
. The set
S_{k}^{\prime}(G)
can
be considered as a subset of
\mathbb{R}^{M}
, where
M=\binom{k+1}{2}
.

It is an easy observation that for every graph
G
and every
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, the
quotient sets
S^{\prime}_{k}(G)
and
Q_{k}(\kappa_{G})
carry the same information. More
exactly, for every partition
\mathcal{P}=(V_{1},\dots,V_{k})
of
V
, the edge-weighted graph
G\angle\mathcal{P}
and the capacity function

\kappa_{G}/\mathcal{P}
can be easily computed from each other. Indeed, for every
A\subseteq[k]
,

\kappa_{G}/\mathcal{P}(A)=\sum_{i\in A}\sum_{j\in[k]\setminus A}\gamma(ij),

(5)

and for every
i,j\in[k]
,

\gamma(ij)=\frac{1}{2}\cdot(\kappa_{G}(V_{i})+\kappa_{G}(V_{j})-\kappa_{G}(V_{i}\cup V_{j}).

(6)

It is also clear that
S^{\prime}_{k}(G)
can be computed from
S_{k}(G)
, and so

Q_{k}(\kappa_{G})
can be computed from
S_{k}(G)
. With some care, this observation
can be made into another proof of Theorem
5.13
.

Clearly, the node weights

\alpha_{i}
of
G/\mathcal{P}
cannot be computed from the edge weights
\gamma(ij)
of

G\angle\mathcal{P}
. However, it remains open whether the whole set
S_{k}(G)
is determined by
S_{k}^{\prime}(G)
, at
least approximately, for large graphs
G
. This would still give a converse to
Theorem
5.13
.

5.3
Homomorphism densities

Let
G=(V,E)
be a graph. For
X\subseteq E(G)
, let
G_{X}=(V(G),X)
. It is easy to see that for fixed graphs
F
and
G
, the homomorphism density
t(F,G_{X})
, as a function of
X\subseteq E(G)
, is supermodular and increasing, so the setfunction
\tau_{F,G}\colon 2^{E(G)}\to[0,1]
defined as
\tau_{F,G}(X)=1-t(F,G_{X^{c}})
is submodular and increasing. We can extend this to a graphon
W\colon[0,1]^{2}\to[0,1]
instead of
G
as follows: for every Borel subset
X\subseteq[0,1]^{2}
, we denote by
W_{X}
the graphon obtained by reducing
W
to
0
outside
X
and set
\tau_{F,W}(X)=1-t(F,W_{X})
.

The following theorem is proved in
[
3
]
, using results from the theory of graph property testing.

Theorem 5.15
.

If
(G_{n}\colon n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+})
is a sequence of graphs that converges to a graphon
W
in the sense of dense graph convergence, then for every graph
F
, the sequence of setfunctions
\tau_{F,G_{n}}
quotient-converges to
\tau_{F,W}
.

Novel tetrazole annulation on benzopyridodiazepine derivatives as inhibitor of tropomyosin kinase B (TrkB): rational design, synthesis, biological evaluation, SAR, and in silico studies
Authors: Shivangi Jaiswal, Khushboo Bhardwaj, Smita Jain, Aleksey E. Kuznetsov, Gautam Sighvi, Swapnil Sharma, Jaya Dwivedi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Tropomyosin kinase B (TrkB) is an attractive target for cancer treatment and is emerging as a target for the treatment of neurological disorders, specifically epilepsy, due to its role in regulating neuronal degeneration and inflammation. However, current medications frequently suffer from toxicity, resistance, and poor metabolism. In the present study, four new series of tetrazole annulated benzopyridodiazepine derivatives (26 examples) were synthesized in two steps: intramolecular cyclization with various sets of amines to produce benzopyridodiazepinone derivatives, and then, in the second step, the tetrazole ring was introduced by the reaction of imidoyl chloride with azide. All four synthesized series were assessed for in vitro inhibitory activity against tropomyosin kinase (TrkB) using both enzyme and cellular assays. To further evaluate the biological efficacy of the synthesized derivatives, we conducted ROS measurements, fluorescence-based microscopy, and flow cytometry to determine the mode of action and the induction of apoptosis. Molecular docking, ADMET, and DFT studies supported the binding at the TrkB site and suggested favorable pharmacokinetic profiles. Overall, the results indicate that the synthesized compounds are highly promising candidates, especially 14h, which shows significant inhibition of TrkB in neuroblastoma cells. Thus, the tetrazole annulated benzopyridodiazepine is an interesting molecule for exploration in the management of cancer and neurological disorders.

Introduction

Benzodiazepines are important pharmacophores widely found in natural products and pharmaceuticals such as lorazepam, diazepam, and clobazam, indicated in different disorders of the central nervous system.1 In addition, benzodiazepine-based drugs have been successfully explored in cancer management, viz., olanzapine, clozapine, tobramycin, and anthramycin.2–4 Tetrazole compounds are gaining the attention of investigators owing to their considerable biological properties, viz., antiviral, anticancer, antifungal, anticonvulsant, antitubercular, and anti-inflammatory activities.5,6 In view of their laudable therapeutic attributes, we have developed a novel set of benzodiazepine derivatives by introducing a tetrazole framework into the tricyclic ring system (Fig. 1).

Modification of approved drug of benzodiazepine.

Neuroblastoma is a lethal solid tumor mostly diagnosed in young children, and it accounts for 3rd most lethal pediatric cancer that arises from the failure of neural crest precursor cells to differentiate into mature neurons during embryonic development.7,8

The rising incidence of neurological diseases in recent years has necessitated more experimental studies evaluating the relationship between chemicals and disease.9 The exact pathogenesis of these diseases is still unclear, but mounting evidence suggests that protection of the neuron from oxidative stress is a critical therapeutic strategy. Oxidative stress occurs when there is overproduction of reactive oxygen species, and the body is unable to neutralize them with endogenous antioxidants. These free reactive oxygen radicals oxidize cellular components, including proteins, lipids, and DNA, thereby contributing to cell death and neuronal damage.10

Recently, tropomyosin kinase receptors have emerged as new mechanistic targets for regulating the cellular growth, development, and proliferation in neuroblastoma cells. Tropomyosin kinase consists of three isoforms, namely TrkA, TrkB, and TrkC, encoded by NTRK1, NTRK2, and NTRK3 genes, respectively. TrkA and TrkC are commonly expressed in low-stage neuroblastoma, and TrkB is highly expressed in more aggressive and fatal neuroblastomas.11 Thus, inhibiting TrkB is a highly promising therapeutic approach for the discovery of anti-neuroblastoma agents.

This article reports the design and synthesis of 26 novel compounds by introducing the tetrazole ring into the privileged benzodiazepine skeleton via a pharmacophore-merging strategy. This synthetic methodology employs a de novo approach to prepare condensed five-, six-, and seven-membered heteroaromatic rings within a single molecular framework, namely tetrazole-annulated benzopyridodiazepine derivatives. Seeking to increase the biological efficiency, numerous substituents such as cyclopropyl, aniline, p-toluidine, anisidine, methyl 4-aminobenzoate, nitroaniline, amino benzonitrile, and acyclic amine were introduced on the diazepine ring at the N1 position and the tetrazole ring at the N4 position.

The inhibitory activity of the compounds against TrkB was evaluated using an enzymatic assay kit and a cellular assay in neuroblastoma cell lines. A fluorescence microscopy assay was performed to further investigate their ROS levels. Microscopic analysis, mode of action determined by kinetic studies, and apoptotic effects were observed in neuroblastoma cell lines. The compounds' binding interactions with TrkB were assessed using molecular docking, and the results were combined with structural interaction analysis to illustrate structure–activity relationships. In silico pharmacokinetics/ADMET and DFT studies were predicated to justify the compounds' performance and stability in inhibiting TrkB activity.

Results and discussions

Rational structure-based design for TrkB inhibitor

The currently reported TrkB inhibitors include natural products such as auricularia auricula-judae extract, wedelolactone, cannflavin (A and B), phytochemical 3,8-dihydroxy-1-methylanthraquinone-2-carboxylic acid (DMCA) and small synthetic molecules like larotrectinib (IC50 = 0.98 nM), entrectinib (IC50 = 3 nM), 1-benzyl-2-indolinone indole hybrids (IC50 = 0.5 µM), and ANA-12 are under preclinical phase for treatment of ischemic seizures12Fig. 2.

Structure of natural product and small molecules TrkB inhibitors.

Based on previously established reports, seven-membered nitrogen heterocycles in lorazepam, diazepam, and clobazam showed potent activity in the treatment of neurological disorders.14 The proven record of ANA-12, containing an azepinone framework for TrkB inhibitory activity in a mouse model, shows significant antidepressant activity. In the present drug design, benzodiazepine and tetrazole skeletons have been included to develop novel compounds for TrkB inhibition because the benzodiazepine framework has been found very effective in managing different types of epilepsy. Currently in 2019, tetrazole-containing drug ‘cenobamate’ has been approved by the USFDA for the treatment of focal (partial-onset) seizures in adults.15

In view of this, a new series of tetrazole annulated benzopyridodiazepine derivatives was synthesized and examined for TrkB inhibition. The synthetic approach utilizes an easily available starting synthon to facilitate a base-mediated C–N coupling reaction to incorporate five-, six-, and seven-membered nitrogen heteroaromatics, such as tetrazole, benzene, pyridine, and benzodiazepine rings, in a single molecular framework. The numerous substituents have been introduced in the benzodiazepine ring at the N1 and N4 positions. The N1 position is functionalized with cyclopropyl, aniline, p-toluidine, anisidine, methyl 4-aminobenzoate, nitroaniline, 4-amino benzonitrile, 3-amino benzonitrile, 2-amino benzonitrile, and acyclic amine, whereas N4 is functionalized with a tetrazole ring. Chemical synthesis and structural characterization

The commercially available 2-chloro-3-aminopyridyl amine was reacted with 2-chlorobenzoyl chloride in the presence of base sodium hydrogen orthophosphate (Na2HPO4) to afford amide (4–5) as depicted in Scheme 1.

Synthesis of carboxamide.

The two chlorine atoms in intermediate (4–5) are suitable for intermolecular cyclization with various amines, including cyclopropylamine 6, substituted aniline 9, aminobenzonitriles 12, and substituted alkylamines 15, to yield seven-membered benzopyridodiazepinone derivatives. The formation of compounds 4 and 5 was confirmed by the characteristic secondary amine peaks observed in the 1H NMR spectra at 8.80 ppm and 8.08 ppm, respectively.

The 1,3-dipolar cycloaddition is commonly used to synthesize tetrazoles from nitrile and azide precursors, whereas aldehydes, amines, and amides are employed to a lesser extent. In view of this, the present invention relates to the conversion of secondary amides in diazepinones 7(a–b), 10(a–j), 13(a–h), and 16(a–f) to imidoyl chlorides, followed by treatment with a safer azide source, trimethylsilyl azide, to achieve tetrazole derivatives. In all targeted series, imidoyl chloride is obtained by reaction of PCl5, which then undergoes cyclization with azide to give 8(a–b), 11(a–j), 14(a–h), and 17(a–f), as shown in Scheme 2(a–d). The 26 new tetrazole-annulated benzopyridodiazepine derivatives were synthesized and purified by column chromatography, and their structure were verified by 1H NMR, 13C NMR, and mass spectrometry. The structure of these final synthesized compounds was confirmed by the disappearance of the secondary amine peak in proton NMR and the carbonyl peak in 13C NMR spectra. The NMR and mass spectra of the synthesized compound are consistent with the structure and are presented in the SI (S1–S163).

(a) Synthesis of tetrazole annulated benzopyridodiazepine bearing cyclopropyl group. (b) Synthesis of tetrazole annulated benzopyridodiazepine bearing phenyl group. (c) Synthesis of tetrazole annulated benzopyridodiazepine bearing phenyl tetrazole. (d) Synthesis of tetrazole annulated benzopyridodiazepine bearing alkyl group.

The structural confirmation of compound 14h was strengthened by NOESY spectroscopy. The 1H–1H correlation in the NOESY spectrum provides spatial information on hydrogen interactions, thereby determining molecular conformations. It is evident from spectrum S126 that the tetrazole proton interacts with CH2 at 8.72 ppm, while CH2 also contours the phenyl proton at 6.82 ppm.

To determine the nitrosamine impurities in compounds 14g and 14h, a nitrosation assay procedure (NAP test) was performed using tert-Butyl nitrite as the nitrosating agent.13 The results of the NAP test indicated that nitrosation did not occur due to the absence of a secondary amine moiety in the synthesized compounds. These findings clearly demonstrate the absence of a nitrosamine impurity, which, in turn, indicates the preliminary safety of candidates for further in vivo studies.

In vitro tropomyosin kinase B (TrkB) inhibitory activity

Tropomyosin kinase B has emerged as a promising target for the design of novel therapeutic agents to treat cancer and neurodegenerative disorders. A novel series of tetrazole-annulated benzopyridodiazepine derivatives 8(a–b), 11(a–j), 14(a–h), and 17(a–f) was synthesized and screened for inhibitory activity using the TrkB kinase assay kit. The half-inhibitory concentration (IC50) values for all synthesized compounds are shown in Fig. 4. The biological assay results were analyzed using ANA-12 as a standard inhibitor.16

The in vitro findings revealed that all synthesized compounds demonstrate superior activity, with IC50 values ranging from 24.18 to 61.09 nM, compared to ANA-12 (IC50 35.6 nM). The ANA-12 is currently in preclinical development, targeting tropomyosin receptor kinase B. Several compounds, including 14a, 14b, 14f, 14g, 14h, and 17f, showed significantly greater inhibitory activity than ANA-12. Preliminary structure–activity relationship (SAR) analysis indicates that replacement of the cyclopropyl group and the phenyl ring with a para-substituted benzyl-linked tetrazole ring in 14g and 14h could be responsible for the promising inhibitory activity with IC50 of 24.83 nM and 24.18 nM, respectively.

In vitro TrkB inhibition in SH-SY5Y cells

All synthesized compounds 8(a–b), 11(a–j), 14(a–h), and 17(a–f) were screened for TrkB inhibition in the human neuroblastoma cell line SH-SY5Y using an MTT assay. The IC50 values for all synthesized compounds are shown in Table 1. All synthesized compounds exhibited potent inhibitory activity (4.33–3.34 µM), compared with the reference drug, entrectinib (5.3 µM). Among all series, 14g and 14h showed significant inhibition with IC50 values of 2.58 µM and 2.38 µM, respectively. This compound, bearing benzyl-linked tetrazole moieties at the para position, showed the highest activity among the analogs. From series 11(a–j), 11h showed potent activity, which may be due to the presence of a methoxycarbonyl substituent at the para position. Further analysis of series 17(a–f) and 17b showed that the highest activity may be due to less bulky methyl groups.

Neuroprotection by 14g and 14h against H2O2-induced damage in SH-SY5Y cells

The best-selected compounds, 14g and 14h, were tested to assess their ability to protect neuronal cells against oxidative stress. The selected compounds were tested at concentrations of 20, 10, and 5 µM. The hydrogen peroxide (H2O2) was used to induce damage in SH-SY5Y cells by generating reactive oxygen species. Cell viability decreases to 10% after treatment with hydrogen peroxide, as shown in Fig. 7. The compounds 14g and 14h showed the highest protective effect at 20 µM, indicating that cell survival improved in a dose-dependent manner. Collectively, the benzyl tetrazole-linked benzopyridodiazepine in compound 14h showed higher cell viability and a significant neuroprotective effect against H2O2-induced damage in SH-SY5Y cells. The neuroprotective efficacy of 14h can be attributed to its high binding affinity for the TrkB kinase domain, as supported by in silico docking studies (−11.3 Kcal mol−1). In the presence of H2O2-induced oxidative stress, the interaction of 14h with the receptor's catalytic pocket may provide structural stabilization, preventing the catastrophic downregulation of survival signaling typically seen during oxidative stress. This receptor-mediated protection likely activates downstream survival markers such as p-Akt and Bcl-2, which counteract the pro-apoptotic signals triggered by ROS. Thus, the observed protection is not merely a result of chemical scavenging but is a direct consequence of TrkB-mediated signaling stabilization, which enhances the intrinsic resilience of SH-SY5Y cells.

Neuroprotective effect of 14g and 14h on H2O2-mediated oxidative damage in SH-SY5Y cells at various concentrations, 5, 10, 20 µM, using MTT assay.

Kinetic studies of tropomyosin kinase B inhibition

The kinetic studies of compound 14h were performed to determine the mode of action in inhibiting TrkB enzyme. The Lineweaver–Burk plots and Michaelis–Menten curve clearly showed that Vmax remained unchanged with changes in Km, indicating that the tetrazole-annulated benzopyridodiazepine in 14h functioned as a competitive inhibitor of tropomyosin kinase (TrkB) in neuroblastoma cells (Fig. 8). The competitive inhibitor binds non-covalently with TrkB to suppress cell proliferation.

Morphological analysis

With phase contrast microscopy, specific morphological changes were observed in SH-SY5Y cells in response to treatment compared with controls. The control cells remained healthy, differentiated, and neuroblastoma-like; that is, they were long and spindle-shaped, with well-formed cell membranes and well-developed cell-to-cell contacts. Cell cultures treated with compound 14h exhibited an apoptotic-like morphology. Cells exhibited prominent loss of adhesion, marked cellular shrinkage, membrane blebbing, and severe damage to neurite-like processes. The increased number of rounded and detached cells reflects late apoptosis and/or secondary necrosis. This is consistent with the increased cytotoxicity in the MTT assays and the enhanced apoptotic index in the Annexin V-FITC/PI flow cytometry analysis Fig. 9.

Phase contrast microscopy image for morphological detection of apoptosis using annexin V-FITC/PI flow cytometry analysis in SH-SY5Y cells treated with compounds 14h.

Apoptosis assay

Flow cytometry with Annexin V-FITC/PI was employed to analyze apoptosis in SH-SY5Y cells treated with compounds for 14h (5 µg ml−1) and 14h (50 µg ml−1), compared with the control (Fig. 10). Apoptosis was quantified by Annexin V-FITC/7-AAD staining and flow cytometry. Flow cytometric Annexin V-FITC/7-AAD analysis shows a clear concentration-dependent apoptotic response. At 5 µg mL−1, viable cells accounted for 47.0%, while apoptotic populations were markedly increased, with early apoptosis at 12.56% and late apoptosis at 55.55%. Necrotic cells remained relatively low (11.13%), indicating that cell death predominantly occurred through programmed apoptotic pathways rather than nonspecific membrane damage. At a higher concentration of 50 µg mL−1, apoptosis was further enhanced. Viable cells decreased to 14.08%, while late apoptotic cells constituted 45.10%, and early apoptotic cells increased to 26.58%, reflecting accelerated progression from early to late apoptosis. Necrosis showed only a modest increase (14.24%), remaining lower than the total apoptotic population. Compared with 5 µg mL−1, increasing the concentration to 50 µg mL−1 produced a pronounced shift toward apoptosis, with a higher proportion of cells entering early apoptosis and progressing to late apoptosis. Overall, the data demonstrate that the treatment induces dose-dependent apoptosis with limited necrosis, indicating a regulated cytotoxic effect rather than uncontrolled cell lysis, supporting its potential therapeutic relevance.

Annexin V−/PI− flow cytometry apoptosis result of compound 14 h (5 µg ml−1) and 14 h (50 µg ml−1) in SH-SY5Y cells line for 48 h.

Molecular docking

Molecular docking studies were conducted to assess the binding affinity and interaction profiles of the synthesized tetrazole annulated benzopyridodiazepine derivatives with tropomyosin kinase (TrkB) (PDB ID: 4at3). The detailed binding energies, hydrogen bond distances, and key interacting residues are summarised in Table 2. All the design tetrazole-annulated benzopyridodiazepine derivatives 8(a–b), 10(a–j), 14(a–h), 17(a–f) demonstrated favorable binding energies, ranging from −8.3 to −11.3 Kcal mol−1, indicating potential binding towards the TrkB receptor active site. Among the docked compounds, 14g and 14h displayed the most favorable docking scores (−11.0 and −11.3 Kcal mol−1, respectively; Fig. 11), whereas 8a showed a relatively lower docking score (−8.3 Kcal mol−1), suggesting a less favorable interaction. Hydrogen-bond analysis revealed a consistent interaction pattern across the majority of the derivatives. All compounds formed at least one hydrogen bond with the H-Met636 residue of the receptor. This suggests that Met636 may play a pivotal role in anchoring the ligands within the active site through hydrogen bonding, thereby enhancing binding stability.

Molecular docking of 14g and 14h with tropomyosin kinase B enzyme.

The compound 14h showed a remarkable docking score against TrkB, with favorable H-bond interactions with the H-Met636 amino acid (2.79) of the target ligand. Moreover, 14h showed hydrophobic interactions, viz., pi-sigma, pi–pi stacking, and alkyl and pi–alkyl interactions, with LeuA:560, LeuA:699, PheA:565, AlaA:586, ValA:568, LysA:588, and LysA:643 amino acid residues.

The frequent engagement of Leu560 and Val568 suggests potential electrostatic contributions, given the basic nature of these residues. In silico pharmacokinetics and toxicological analysis of selected compounds

Various parameters, such as water solubility, permeability through Caco-2 cells, human intestinal absorption, and skin permeability, were used to evaluate the absorption profile. Predictive intestinal absorption was also performed, and compound 14h had a higher predicted intestinal absorption of 92.28% than compound 14g with 80.91%, indicating good intestinal absorption potential.

VDss (human), fraction unbound, BBB permeability, and CNS permeability parameters were used to assess the distribution properties. The relatively high VDss value observed for compound 14h suggests wide tissue distribution. In turn, both compounds exhibited negative log BB values (14g: −1.834 and 14h: −1.83) and log PS values less than −3, which would predict low BBB penetration and poor CNS permeability. However, these computational predictions might not necessarily be true in vivo, especially in neurological diseases like epilepsy, where repeated seizures are known to temporarily compromise BBB integrity and could potentially lead to CNS drug penetration.

Metabolic properties were predicted from the interactions with cytochrome P450 enzymes. Both compounds were predicted to be CYP3A4 substrates and CYP2C19 inhibitors, but neither inhibited CYP2D6 or CYP1A2, indicating a relatively low potential for adverse metabolic interactions with these isoforms.

The predicted excretion profile by pkCSM showed relatively low clearance (−0.28 to −0.18 log ml min−1 kg−1), indicating slow excretion and high systemic exposure to the compounds. The toxicity assessment showed that most of the synthesized series, particularly those belonging to 11(a–j) and 17(a–f), had positive AMES toxicity predictions. Among lead derivatives, 14g showed positive AMES toxicity, while compound 14h was predicted to be non-AMES toxic. The AMES result obtained should be considered as suggestive of a possible mutagenic liability of specific structural characteristics of some of its derivatives and, therefore, should be further tested experimentally. Therefore, the predicted mutagenicity outcomes require further validation through appropriate in vitro and in vivo toxicity studies to confirm their reliability and biological relevance. Both compounds were also predicted to be non-sensitising to skin and non-inhibitors of hERG-I, but may have hepatotoxicity and hERG-II inhibition risks. All of the synthesized compounds are listed with their full ADMET profiles in the SI (Table S1).

The synthesized tetrazole-annulated derivatives show several potential benefits over the currently approved Trk inhibitors, including larotrectinib and entrectinib, in the development of neuroactive and antiepileptic drugs. The present tetrazole-annulated scaffold has a simpler, more synthetically accessible molecular architecture compared to marketed Trk inhibitors, which are primarily associated with NTRK gene fusion-positive tumors and involve more complex multistep synthetic procedures. This may enable cost-effective synthetic handling and structural diversification. Moreover, drug-like properties and good oral absorption were observed in the ADMET analysis of the synthesized derivatives, which are desirable for CNS-directed therapy. It is also highly structurally flexible for rational optimization in medicinal chemistry, with targeted substitutions allowing tuning of receptor selectivity, pharmacokinetic properties, and safety profile. Notably, the BDNF/TrkB pathway has been profoundly linked to epileptogenesis and further seizures. Thus, selective modulation of the TrkB-associated signaling cascades using tetrazole derivatives might be a therapeutic approach for managing epilepsy. In addition, the present scaffold can provide structurally novel TrkB-targeted agents with more selective activity, reduced toxicity, and improved pharmacological properties, which may be useful in neurological applications, in contrast to currently approved broad-spectrum Trk inhibitors that may interact with other kinases, cause CNS adverse effects, and confer resistance mechanisms.

Analysis of the results presented in Table 4, Fig. 12, 13, and S25 shows the following important features. (i) The molecules of all four compounds are significantly distorted, having the butterfly-like shapes, with the pyridine and phenyl rings bent in one direction (“butterfly wings”) whereas the tetrazole rings and substituents attached to the nitrogen N5 (14a, 14b) or N6 (14g, 14h) (“butterfly body”) are bent in another direction. (ii) All four compounds have significant HOMO/LUMO gaps, 3.88–3.96 eV, which suggests their noticeable thermodynamic stability. (iii) Also, these compounds have very significant dipole moment values, 9.4553–12.7939 D, which implies noticeable polarity of their molecules and thus the possibility of forming quite strong interactions with protein polar groups, thus providing certain support for the molecular docking findings. Finally, the MEP plots analysis results show similar MEP patterns for all four compounds: noticeable accumulations of negative electrostatic potential, shown by red, on nitrogen atoms as well as some accumulations of negative electrostatic potential over the aromatic rings of the substituents, along with noticeable accumulations of positive electrostatic potential, shown by blue, on hydrogen atoms, especially on the hydrogens of the tetrazole rings of the substituent moieties.

These results suggest the formation of noticeably strong electrostatic interactions, including hydrogen bonding, between the molecules of 14a, 14b, 14g, and 14h and protein pockets. This again provides some support for the reported molecular docking results. Moreover, these results show the presence of both electrophilic (hydrogen atoms) and nucleophilic (nitrogen atoms, especially in tetrazole rings) spots in the molecules of 14a, 14b, 14g, and 14h, thus showing their noticeable reactivity towards both nucleophilic and electrophilic agents, in particular, to reactive oxygen species. Thus, the DFT results suggest that the compounds studied in the solvent phase might readily interconvert between isomers, demonstrating their flexibility and strong potential to accommodate protein pockets during interactions with protein molecules. Specifically, for the compounds 14a, 14b, 14g and 14h conversions between various isomers would occur through rotations of the phenyl and tetrazole groups of the substituents taking place very easily in the solution phase at room temperature, as supported by small energy differences between the ground state and the highest isomers for these compounds, 2.69, 1.84, 1.79, and 2.14 kcal mol−1 for 14a, 14b, 14g and 14h, respectively. The molecules of these compounds would readily fit into protein pockets, supporting the molecular docking findings. Moreover, the analyses of dipole moments, NBO charges of selected atoms, and MEP plots for 14a, 14b, 14g, and 14h show the possibility of forming quite strong electrostatic interactions, including hydrogen bonding, with polar solvent molecules and, most importantly, with the polar groups in protein pockets, thus supporting the molecular docking findings. Structure–activity relationships

Structure–activity relationship of tetrazole annulated benzopyridodiazepine was discussed on the basis of experimental and computational studies. There are several precedents in the literature indicating that benzodiazepines are a privileged scaffold with a broad range of biological activities, including anticancer, antiviral, and antiepileptic activities.17 Keeping in view, a novel benzodiazepine framework has been synthesized and examined for TrkB inhibition. The strategic substitutions at the N1 and N4 positions of the benzodiazepine ring could modulate their pharmacological profile. Substitution at the N4 position with a tetrazole ring enhances hydrogen-bonding interactions, which are crucial for binding to the TrkB pocket, while cyclic and acyclic substituents at the N1 position improve TrkB inhibitory activity. The presence of cyclic substituents at the N1 position results in potent binding to TrkB receptors and inhibits their activity. In particular, the benzyl tetrazole-linked benzodiazepine exhibited significant inhibitory activity against TrkB in biological assays and docking studies. The substituted phenyl (H, Me, OMe, COOMe, and NO2) are effective in producing inhibitory action against TrkB. However, cyclopropyl and acyclic substituents at the N1 position exhibited lower inhibitory activity. Interestingly, methyl-substituted pyridine also appeared effective in all synthesized series due to increased lipophilicity. The SAR study clearly indicated that the tetrazole ring at the N4 position of the benzodiazepine may be beneficial for inactivating tropomyosin kinase activity. The pictorial presentation of the SAR analysis of the synthesized tetrazole-annulated benzodiazepine derivatives is shown in Fig. 16. Collectively, these structural modifications enable the hybrid molecules to have multi-targeted neuroprotective and anticancer activities by disrupting cell proliferation and TrkB signaling.

In vitro tropomyosin kinase B (TrkB) inhibitory activity

The synthesized set of tetrazole-annulated benzopyridodiazepine derivatives 8(a–b), 11(a–j), 14(a–h), and 17(a–f) was evaluated for tropomyosin kinase inhibitory activity using the TrkB kinase assay kit (BPS Bioscience #78591). The purchased kit was stored at −20 to −80 °C and used as per the manufacturer's instructions. Briefly, 7 µl of 1× kinase assay buffer 1, 0.5 µl of ATP + 0.5 µl of PTK substrate, and 4.5 µl of distilled water were added to every well. Then 2.5 µl of the synthesized compounds at different concentrations (0.01, 0.1, 1, 10, 100) was added to the wells, except for the blank and positive control wells. The experiment was performed in triplicate. The diluent solution (2.5 µl) was added to the blank and positive control. Further in the blank well, 10 µl of 1× kinase assay buffer 1 was added, whereas 10 ng µl−1 kinase was added in the rest of the well. The plate was incubated at 30 °C for 45 minutes. After incubation, 25 µl of ADP-Glo™ reagent was added to each well and incubated for an additional 45 minutes at room temperature. Lastly, 50 µl of kinase detection reagent was added to each well, then the wells were covered with aluminium foil and incubated for another 45 minutes at room temperature. The absorbance of each well was read at 450 nm using an ELISA reader. The blank reading was subtracted from the other values, and the half-minimum inhibitory concentration was determined.18–20

MTT assay

The SH-SY5Y cells were cultured in DMEM supplemented with 10% Fetal Bovine Serum (FBS), 1% non-essential amino acids (NEAA), 1% glutamine, and 1% antibiotic solution, and incubated in a humidified chamber with 5% carbon dioxide atmosphere at 37 °C. The cells were grown in a 25 cm3 flask and maintained in a CO2 incubator. The biocompatibility of 11h and 14h was evaluated using the 3-(4,5-dimethyl-2-thiazolyl)-2,5-diphenyl-2H-tetrazolium bromide (MTT) assay. SH-SY5Y cells (1 × 104) were seeded into a 96-well plate (Corning Life Sciences, New York, USA) and incubated for 24 hours to allow proper adherence. The following day, the cells were washed with PBS and exposed to varying concentrations of the compounds (0, 1.7, 3.125, 6.25, 12.5, 25, 50, and 100 µM) for 24 hours. After incubation, the cells were treated with MTT at 0.5 mg mL−1 and incubated in the dark for 4 hours. The media was then discarded, and 100 µL of DMSO was added to dissolve the formazan crystals. The plate was gently agitated at room temperature for 10 minutes, and absorbance was measured at 570 nm using a microplate reader.21,22

Kinetic studies of tropomyosin kinase B inhibition

To gain insight into the mechanism of action, kinetic studies were performed at three concentrations (4, 6, and 8 µM) of the synthesized compound 14h using an ELISA-based phosphorylation assay. The reaction rate was measured at varying ATP concentrations (the substrate) in the presence of different inhibitor concentrations. The data were analyzed using double-reciprocal Lineweaver–Burk plots to determine Km and Vmax values, confirming the competitive nature of the inhibitor at the ATP-binding site of the TrkB kinase domain. Data analysis was performed using GraphPad Prism 4.03.24

Apoptosis

Flow cytometry with Annexin V-FITC and Propidium Iodide (PI) staining was used to assess the ability of these experimental compounds to induce apoptosis, according to the manufacturer's guidelines. More precisely, 1 × 105 cells per well were seeded in a 6-well plate in a cell line like SH-SY5Y, a neuroblastoma cell line, allowed to adhere for 24 h, treated for 14h, showing high docking affinity scores and high cytotoxic effects on cells measured by the MTT assay, respectively, for another 24 hours, followed by analysis. After the treatment, the cells were washed twice with ice-cold PBS, trypsinized to remove impurities, and then centrifuged at 1200 rpm for 5 min. The pelleted cells were resuspended in 1× annexin-binding buffer, then incubated with a staining solution containing Annexin V-FITC and Propidium iodide (PI) for 15 minutes in the dark at room temperature. Competitive binding at the ATP-binding site of the TrkB kinase domain with the co-crystallized ligand CPD5N (ligand code LTI). The structure is thus suitable for docking studies, since the binding site used in the docking protocol matches exactly with that of the co-crystallized inhibitor. The docking grid was centered on the ATP-binding pocket and constructed using the co-crystallized ligand (CPD5N) from 4AT3 as a reference. DFT study

DFT study was performed with the Gaussian 16 software, using29 the hybrid density functional B3LYP30 with the D3 version of Grimme's dispersion with the original D3 damping function31 added and the triple-zeta split-valence polarized basis set 6-311G(d,p) (two sets of polarization functions, both on hydrogens and on heavier atoms).32,33 This approach is furthermore referred to as B3LYP-GD3/6-311G(d,p). We decided to use the B3LYP-GD3 approach, following the recommendations in the Scientific Perspective paper by Bursch et al., which always include a dispersion correction.34 Furthermore, the 6-311G(d,p) basis set is commonly used in higher-level calculations for organic compounds because it provides a good relationship between accuracy and computational demands.

We checked different possible isomers for all the compounds under investigation, 8(a–b), 11(a–j), 14(a–h), and 17(a–f), both in the gas phase and with the implicit effects from tetrahydrofuran (THF) (dielectric constant ε = 7.4257) taken into account, employing the self-reliable IEF-PCM approach with the UFF default model as implemented in the Gaussian 16 software,35 with the electrostatic scaling factor α = 1.0. We performed geometry optimizations and frequency calculations to ensure that the structures obtained are true minima.

We further considered the global minima structures of the compounds 14a, 14b, 14h, and 14g, for which we performed analyses of frontier molecular orbitals (FMOs), Natural Bond Orbital (NBO)36 charges on selected atoms, and molecular electrostatic potential (MEP).37 Avogadro visualization software, version 1.1.1, was used to visualize the structures, FMOs, and MEP plots.38,39 Dipole moments were visualized using Chemcraft software, version 1.8.40

Conclusion

The novel four series of tetrazole-annulated benzopyridodiazepines were synthesized in two steps via inter- and intramolecular C–N coupling reactions. Primarily intramolecular cyclization with various sets of amines to produce benzopyridodiazepinone derivatives, then in the second step, the tetrazole ring was introduced by the reaction of imidoyl chloride with azide. All synthesized compounds demonstrate strong inhibitory activity against TrkB, as evidenced by enzyme and cellular assays in neuroblastoma cell lines. ROS estimation revealed a significant increase in intracellular ROS levels upon treatment with compound 14h. This elevation in oxidative stress is consistent with the induction of apoptosis in neuroblastoma cells. Phase-contrast microscopy revealed loss of adhesion, marked cellular shrinkage, membrane blebbing, and pronounced damage to neurite-like processes. The increased number of rounded and detached cells reflects late apoptosis and/or secondary necrosis. Dose-dependent apoptosis was observed in flow cytometry with Annexin V-FITC/PI in neuroblastoma cells. Computational studies, such as molecular docking, DFT, and in silico ADMET analysis, also indicate that 14h has higher binding affinity and BBB permeability and lower cellular toxicity. The high affinity for the Met636 hinge region suggests that 14h acts as a potent modulator. In a malignant environment, it competes with ATP to arrest growth, whereas in an oxidative stress environment, it prevents receptor dysfunction. This dual-faceted mechanism highlights the potential of the benzopyridodiazepine-tetrazole scaffold to serve as a targeted therapeutic that selectively modulates TrkB signaling in response to the cellular microenvironment. Although the tetrazole annulated benzopyridodiazepine is an interesting molecule for exploration in the management of cancer and neurological disorders, kinase panel screening will be required in future studies to fully establish selectivity. The impurities profile and toxicological characterization of the synthesized compounds were not evaluated in the present study and will be investigated in future work prior to preclinical and clinical studies to establish their safety and efficacy in developing novel multi-target bioactive agents.

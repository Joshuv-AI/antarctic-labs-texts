An uncommon case of concurrent tumors: Unilateral Wilm's tumor with contralateral retroperitoneal teratoma in a 3-year-old child
Authors: Yasar Rashid, Sajid Ali, Rija Khalid, Tariq Latif
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Wilms tumor is the most common renal malignancy in children, while retroperitoneal teratomas are rare. Their simultaneous occurrence at distinct sites is exceptionally rare. We report a 3-year-old child with right-sided Wilms tumor and a contralateral retroperitoneal mass. Imaging and biopsy raised concern for metastasis versus second primary tumor. Preoperative chemotherapy led to regression of the renal lesion but stability of the retroperitoneal mass. Surgical resection confirmed residual right side high-risk Wilms tumor and contralateral mature cystic teratoma. This case highlights diagnostic challenges and emphasizes the importance of early recognition, multidisciplinary planning, and tailored therapy in achieving successful outcomes.

Introduction

Wilms tumor also known as Nephroblastoma is the most frequent malignant renal tumor in children. Wilms tumor constitutes 5% of all childhood malignancies and accounts for 80% of all diagnosed renal cancers in children and teenagers.1 Children with Wilms tumor often presents with an asymptomatic abdominal mass, however in some instances, tumor presents with abdominal pain and hematuria from invasion of the renal pelvis or hypertension resulting from compression of the renal artery, each occurring in approximately 25% of cases.2

Teratomas represents a common form of Germ cell tumors and histologically characterized by the presence of derivatives of all 3 germ cell layers ectoderm, mesoderm and endoderm.3 Teratomas predominantly found in the gonadal sites, followed by the sacrococcygeal region. It may also arise in other extra gonadal sites, such as the neck, mediastinum, and retro peritoneum. However, retroperitoneal teratoma are the third-most common retroperitoneal tumors found in children.4

Although Wilms tumor and teratoma histological features can occur in the same mass—a rare entity termed 'Teratoid Wilms tumor'—this occurrence is seldom reported in clinical literature.5 however simultaneous occurrence of Wilms tumor and teratoma at distinct anatomical sites is an extremely rare scenario with no published literature showing concurrent Wilms tumor and contralateral retroperitoneal teratoma.

This case report is the valuable addition to the literature, with successful management of a 3-year-old child with right side Wilms tumor and concurrent contralateral retroperitoneal teratoma.

Case description

A 3-year-old male child developed abdominal distension for one month for which he consulted with a local physician, an ultrasound was advised which showed right side abdominal mass. A trucut biopsy was performed, sections revealed a malignant triphasic neoplasm composed of tubules, undifferentiated blastemal component and stromal component with positive WT1 (Fig. 1A). Staging workup was performed including, Echocardiography which was normal with a ejection fraction >55 %.

Contrast computed tomography scan (CT) of chest and abdomen demonstrated a large heterogeneous soft-tissue density mass arising from upper and mid pole of right kidney measures 9.0 × 7.4 × 8.3 cm not involving right renal vein or artery. No tumor invasion or thrombus is seen in inferior vena cava. The right and left side adrenal glands were not separately visualized. Another heterogeneous lesion was seen at the Left adrenal gland area measuring 17 × 41 mm, without pulmonary or liver metastasis (Fig:1 B & Fig:1 C). Diagnosis of right-side Wilms tumor with Secondaries at the left supra renal region was made.

Case discussed in multidisciplinary meeting (MDT) and decision was made for image guided biopsy of left suprarenal mass and neo adjuvant chemotherapy vincristine and actinomycin (VA)for 04 weeks followed by reevaluation scan.

Post chemotherapy reevaluation scan revealed re demonstration of partly exophytic soft tissue mass arising from the upper pole of the right kidney with interval regression in size, now measures about 3.3 × 3.3 × 3.2 cm against the prior size of about 9.0 × 7.4 × 8.3 cm. It was in close contact with the inferomedial surface of the right lobe of liver without definite infiltrations and no definitive extension of tumor beyond renal fascia. Stable left adrenal gland lesion was renoted, measuring about 1.8 × 3.0 cm. The right adrenal gland was unremarkable. (Fig:2A & 2B)). CT guided biopsy of left suprarenal mass showed fibroconnective tissue with a round to spindle cell lesion with mild atypia and rare mitosis. Discussion

Simultaneous presentation of Wilms tumor with retroperitoneal teratoma is uncommon. Additionally, primary abdominal and retroperitoneal teratomas are itself a rare entity and represent only 4–6% of all teratomas.6 The embryonal tumor arises from the metanephric blastema, which is the precursor tissue of the kidney and is characterized by a triphasic histological pattern comprising blastemal, stromal, and epithelial components and their molecular characteristics similar to those of the earliest kidney development stages.7

In children retroperitoneal teratomas mostly arising in close relation to the kidneys. The majority are benign but complete excision is necessary for cure. Even large tumors with bilateral involvement of the retroperitoneum can be excised while preserving adjacent organs.8 In literature review, a child with bilateral Wilms tumor reported after meticulous imaging and histological examination, it came out to be right Wilms tumor and left renal teratoma. The patient was successfully managed with left nephrectomy and right partial nephrectomy following chemotherapy highlighting the diagnostic challenges of such complex cases.9

The coexistence of Wilms tumor and teratoma, particularly when located at separate sites, is extremely rare. In this case, the patient had a right renal Wilms tumor and contralateral retroperitoneal mass, which was subsequently identified as a mature cystic teratoma. The challenge in our case was characterizing the nature of the left-sided retroperitoneal mass, and determining whether it represented a metastasis or a separate primary tumor, initially unclear on imaging and biopsy results.

Our multidisciplinary team (MDT) led to a strategy that included preoperative chemotherapy (VA regimen) and image-guided biopsy of the left suprarenal mass as the left side suprarenal mass was stable and unresponsive to chemotherapy. The biopsy ruled out metastatic Wilms tumor and other round blue cells tumor, but the result was inconclusive, prompting for organ preserving excisional biopsy.

Histopathological examination after surgery revealed residual Wilms tumor in the right kidney, with therapy-induced changes and a predominance of blastemal components, characteristic of high-risk Wilms tumor. Notably, the left suprarenal mass was confirmed to be a mature cystic teratoma. The patient's management adhered to protocols for high-risk Wilms tumor, with adjuvant chemotherapy and vigilant monitoring for any recurrence.

This case highlights the unique and rare presentation of concurrent Wilms tumor and retroperitoneal teratoma. It underscores the importance of a comprehensive diagnostic approach, including imaging, biopsy, and multidisciplinary team discussions, in managing such complex cases. The favorable outcome post-surgery, with the patient tolerated chemotherapy well, is promising, though close follow-up remains essential, particularly given the high-risk histology of the Wilms tumor.

In conclusion, while simultaneous Wilms tumor and teratoma at separate sites is exceedingly rare, this case demonstrates the importance of thorough investigation and careful treatment planning. Continued monitoring and chemotherapy are critical to optimizing the patient's prognosis.

Conclusion

This case highlights the exceptional rarity of concurrent Wilms tumor and retroperitoneal teratoma in separate anatomical sites in a pediatric patient. Although both Wilms tumor and teratomas are individually common pediatric neoplasms, their simultaneous occurrence, particularly in different organs, is an exceedingly uncommon phenomenon.

To achieve optimal outcome in such complex and rare scenarios, vigilant imaging, accurate histopathological evaluation, preoperative chemotherapy, organ preservation wherever possible and coordinated care through Multi-disciplinary approach are essential. Continuous follow up and further research into the genetic basis and reporting of these cases are pillar in advancing care of pediatric population with such rare and concurrent cases.

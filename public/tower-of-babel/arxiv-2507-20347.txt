On spectrally optimal duals for r r -erasures of frames generated by graphs
Deepshikha, Aniruddha Samanta

Abstract
In [ 9 ] , authors studied spectrally optimal dual frames for 1 1 -erasure and 2 2 -erasures of frames generated by graph. In this paper, we study spectrally optimal dual frames for r r -erasures. We show that the spectral radius of the error operator of unitary equivalent frames is same with respect to their respective canonical dual frames. We prove that if a frame is generated by a connected graph, then its canonical dual frame is the unique spectrally optimal dual frame for r r -erasures. Further, we show that the canonical dual of frames generated by disconnected graphs are non-unique spectrally optimal dual frames for r r -erasures.



AMS Subject Classification(2010):
05C40, 05C50, 42C15, 42C40, 46C05.

Keywords.
Frames generated by graphs, Dual frames, Spectrally optimal dual frames, Laplacian matrix, Error operator.

Introduction

In order to solve certain complex problems in the non-harmonic Fourier series, Duffin and Schaeffer
[
11
]
developed the idea of frames. Frames are redundant sequences of vectors that span a vector space. The redundancy of frames makes them very useful in many applications, including data and image processing, signal transmission, image segmentation, coding theory, etc. Due to redundancy, the data transmitted using frames can be reconstructed with minimal error if erasures occur during the transmission.

Finite frames are widely used in frame theory due to their significant relevance in applications. In
[
7
]
, authors introduced a new class of finite frames using simple graphs and called them frames generated by graphs. They established some graph properties using frame properties and vice-versa. Consequently, it provides a beautiful connection between the three classical branches of mathematics, namely, frame theory, graph theory, and matrix theory. Further, frames generated by graphs have some special properties, and some of them are stated in the preliminaries section of this article.

The problem of erasures commonly occurs due to transmission losses or disturbances during transmission. The problem of erasures can be handled in two ways. One way is to find a frame that can minimize the maximal error in reconstruction. Another way is to find an optimal dual frame for a pre-selected frame in order to minimize the maximal error of reconstruction. In this paper, we focus on the second approach. A dual frame of a pre-selected frame that minimizes the maximal error of reconstruction is called an optimal dual frame. Various measurements are used to measure the error operator. If operator norm is used, then we use the term optimal dual frame. If the spectral radius is used to measure the error operator, then dual frames that minimize the maximum error are called spectrally optimal dual frames (or, in short,
SOD
-frames). In
[
8
]
, authors studied optimal dual frames of frames generated by graphs. Spectrally optimal dual frames for
1
-erasure and

2
-erasures of frames generated by graphs is studied in
[
9
]
. In this paper, we study the spectrally optimal dualS for
r
erasures of frames generated by graphs.

The outline of this paper is as follows. In Section 2, we provide the basic definitions and results of frame theory, spectrally optimal dual frames, spectral graph theory, frames generated by graphs and
SOD
-frame for
1

Throughout the rest of the article, we consider finite frames and
\mathbb{C}^{k}
is a

A sequence of vectors

\displaystyle A_{o}\|f\|^{2}\leq\sum_{i\in[n]}|\langle f,\phi_{i}\rangle|^{2}\leq B_{o}\|f\|^{2}.

The constants

of
\Phi

[T^{*}_{\Phi}]=[\phi_{1}\,\,\phi_{2}\,\,\cdots\,\,\phi_{n}]

. The operator
S_{\Phi}
is bounded, linear, self-adjoint, positive, and invertible. The composition
T_{\Phi}T_{\Phi}^{*}:\mathbb{C}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{n}

of
\Phi
. The canonical matrix representation of the Gramian operator is said to be the

\langle\phi_{j},\phi_{i}\rangle

(i,j)
-th entry of
\mathcal{G}_{\Phi}
. A frame
\Psi=\{\psi_{i}\}_{i\in[n]}
of
\mathbb{C}^{k}
is called a

of
\Phi
if for any

. The sequence
\{S_{\Phi}^{-1}\phi_{i}\}_{i\in[n]}
is a also a dual frame of
\Phi
and it is called the
canonical dual frame
of
\Phi
. We denote the set of all dual frames of a frame
\Phi
by
D_{\Phi}
. The dual frames which are not canonical dual are called
alternate dual frames
. If
\Psi=\{\psi_{i}\}_{i\in[n]}\in D_{\Phi}
then there exist a sequence

such that
\psi_{i}=S_{\Phi}^{-1}\phi_{i}+\mu_{i}
for
i\in[n]
and
\sum\limits_{i\in[n]}\langle f,\mu_{i}\rangle\phi_{i}=0

f\in\mathbb{C}^{k}

[

Suppose

-erasures and

-frame of

Main results

We start this section by proving that the spectral radius of the error operator of unitary equivalent frames is equal.

Theorem 3.1
.

Suppose
\Phi=\{\phi_{i}\}_{i\in[n]}
and
\Psi=\{\psi_{i}\}_{i\in[n]}
are unitary equivalent frames for
\mathbb{C}^{m}
with frame operators
S_{\Phi}
and
S_{\Psi}
, respectively. Then
\rho^{(r)}_{\Phi,S^{-1}_{\Phi}\Phi}=\rho^{(r)}_{\Psi,S^{-1}_{\Psi}\Psi}
for any
r\in[n-1]
.

Proof.

Since
\Phi
and
\Psi
are unitary equivalent frames, there exists a unitary operator
U:\mathbb{C}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{n}
such that
\phi_{i}=U\psi_{i}
for
i\in[n]
. For any
f\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
, we have

\displaystyle S_{\Phi}(f)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\langle f,\phi_{i}\rangle\phi_{i}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\langle f,U\psi_{i}\rangle U\psi_{i}

\displaystyle=U\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\langle U^{*}f,\psi_{i}\rangle\psi_{i}\right)

\displaystyle=US_{\Psi}U^{*}(f).

Thus,
S_{\Phi}=US_{\Psi}U^{*}
. Let
r\in[n-1]
be arbitrary. For any
\Lambda\subset[n]
such that
|\Lambda|=r
, we have

\displaystyle E_{\Phi,S^{-1}_{\Phi}\Phi,\Lambda}(f)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{i\in\Lambda}\langle f,\phi_{i}\rangle S_{\Phi}^{-1}\phi_{i}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{i\in\Lambda}\langle f,U\psi_{i}\rangle US_{\Psi}^{-1}U^{*}U\psi_{i}

\displaystyle=U\sum\limits_{i\in\Lambda}\langle U^{*}f,\psi_{i}\rangle S_{\Psi}^{-1}\psi_{i}

\displaystyle=UE_{\Psi,S^{-1}_{\Psi}\Psi,\Lambda}U^{*}(f)

Thus,
E_{\Phi,S^{-1}_{\Phi}\Phi,\Lambda}=UE_{\Psi,S^{-1}_{\Psi}\Psi,\Lambda}U^{*}
. Hence, we have

\displaystyle\rho^{(r)}_{\Phi,S^{-1}_{\Phi}\Phi}

\displaystyle=\max\{\rho(E_{\Phi,S^{-1}_{\Phi}\Phi,\Lambda}):|\Lambda|=r,\Lambda\subset[n]\}

\displaystyle=\max\{\rho(UE_{\Psi,S^{-1}_{\Psi}\Psi,\Lambda}U^{*}):|\Lambda|=r,\Lambda\subset[n]\}

\displaystyle=\max\{\rho(E_{\Psi,S^{-1}_{\Psi}\Psi,\Lambda}):|\Lambda|=r,\Lambda\subset[n]\}

\displaystyle=\rho^{(r)}_{\Psi,S^{-1}_{\Psi}\Psi}.

∎

Case 1:

\displaystyle\left(\frac{c_{1}}{c_{1}+\cdots+c_{r-1}}+1\right)\phi_{\alpha_{2}}+\cdots+\left(\frac{c_{r-1}}{c_{1}+\cdots+c_{r-1}}+1\right)\phi_{\alpha_{r}}=0

\Phi

r=1,2
. Thus, assume
r\geq 3
. Let
\Psi=\{\psi_{i}\}_{i\in[n]}
be any dual frame of
\Phi
. By Theorem
2.3
, there exists a vector
\nu\in\mathbb{C}^{n-1}
such that
\psi_{i}=S_{\Phi}^{-1}\phi_{i}+\nu
for
i\in[n]
.

Let
\Lambda\subset[n]
be arbitrary such that
|\Lambda|=r
. Suppose
i,j\in\Lambda
. Then, we have

\displaystyle E_{\Phi,\Psi,\Lambda}(\psi_{i}-\psi_{j})

\displaystyle=\sum_{q\in\Lambda}\langle\psi_{i}-\psi_{j},\phi_{q}\rangle\psi_{q}

\displaystyle=\sum_{q\in\Lambda}\langle S_{\Phi}^{-1}\phi_{i}-S_{\Phi}^{-1}\phi_{j},\phi_{q}\rangle\psi_{q}

\displaystyle=\sum_{{q\in\Lambda},{q\neq i,j}}(\langle S_{\Phi}^{-1}\phi_{i},\phi_{q}\rangle-\langle S_{\Phi}^{-1}\phi_{j},\phi_{q}\rangle)\psi_{q}+(\langle S_{\Phi}^{-1}\phi_{i},\phi_{i}\rangle-\langle S_{\Phi}^{-1}\phi_{j},\phi_{i}\rangle)\psi_{i}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad+(\langle S_{\Phi}^{-1}\phi_{i},\phi_{j}\rangle-\langle S_{\Phi}^{-1}\phi_{j},\phi_{j}\rangle)\psi_{j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{{q\in\Lambda},{q\neq i,j}}\left(\left(-\frac{1}{n}\right)-\left(-\frac{1}{n}\right)\right)\psi_{q}+\left(\left(1-\frac{1}{n}\right)-\left(-\frac{1}{n}\right)\right)\psi_{i}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad+\left(\left(-\frac{1}{n}\right)-\left(1-\frac{1}{n}\right)\right)\psi_{j}

\displaystyle=\psi_{i}-\psi_{j}.

Therefore,
1\in\sigma(E_{\Phi,\Psi,\Lambda})
. Thus,
\rho^{(r)}_{\Phi,\Psi}\geq 1=\rho^{(r)}_{\Phi,S^{-1}_{\Phi}\Phi}
. Since,
S_{\Phi}^{-1}\Phi
is the unique
SOD
-frame for
1
-erasure and
\rho^{(r)}_{\Phi,S^{-1}_{\Phi}\Phi}\leq\rho^{(r)}_{\Phi,\Psi}
for any dual frame
\Psi
of
\Phi
, thus,
S_{\Phi}^{-1}\Phi
is the unique
SOD
-frame for
r
-erasures.
∎

Spontaneously regressing pulmonary tuberculosis detected by computed tomography in a patient with positive interferon‐gamma release assay
Authors: Yuya Kimura, Hiroyuki Arai, Reo Tome, Kyohei Kaburaki, Shinji Sasada
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Key messageWe present a case of pulmonary tuberculosis with a positive interferon‐gamma release assay showing spontaneous regression followed by progression of radiological findings. Physicians should be aware that tuberculosis lesions can temporarily regress and careful follow up should be done if there are suspicious radiological findings.Keywords: computed tomography, regress, tuberculosis

CLINICAL IMAGE

A 66‐year‐old woman was referred to our hospital for a three‐month history of post‐viral cough that had largely resolved by the time of presentation. Chest computed tomography (CT) showed consolidation with mild bronchocentric opacities in the right upper lobe and calcified supraclavicular and abdominal lymph nodes (Figure 1A,B). While she had no tuberculosis history, the interferon‐gamma release assay testing was positive. Sputum examination was not possible due to her inability to expectorate. Three‐month follow‐up CT demonstrated decreased consolidation (Figure 1C,D), coinciding with complete resolution of symptoms. However, imaging at 9 months revealed enlargement of the consolidation (Figure 1E,F) despite continued absence of respiratory symptoms. Despite our recommendation for bronchoscopy, she opted for observation. After CT at 12 months showed further progression (Figure 1G,H), she agreed to undergo bronchoscopy. Bronchial lavage fluid from the right upper lobe tested negative for acid‐fast bacilli stain but tested positive for Mycobacterium tuberculosis by polymerase chain reaction, and Mycobacterium tuberculosis was subsequently detected in the culture, confirming pulmonary tuberculosis. This case highlights that CT abnormalities in early tuberculosis may spontaneously regress. 1 Careful follow‐up is essential for patients with CT findings suspicious of tuberculosis, especially in those with prior tuberculosis history and in those with positive interferon‐gamma release assay results or calcified lymph nodes regardless of prior tuberculosis history.

(A, B) Computed tomography (CT) at the initial presentation showed consolidation and mild bronchocentric opacities in the right upper lobe. (C, D) A three‐month follow‐up CT revealed regression of the consolidation. (E, F) A nine‐month follow‐up CT showed proliferation of the consolidation. (G, H) A 12‐month follow‐up CT demonstrated mucus plugs within the more central bronchi. The infiltrates were marked with arrows.

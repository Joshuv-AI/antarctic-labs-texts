Induction of a type 2 inflammatory chronic rhinosinusitis in C57BL/6 mice
Authors: Ying Li, Ping He, Bing Yan, Yimin Ren, Bangyu Cui, Ming Wang, Siyuan Ma, Jun Yang, Luo Zhang, Chengshuo Wang
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
Background:Eosinophilic chronic rhinosinusitis (CRS) has been widely studied for its intractability and high recurrence rate. It can be divided into pure and mixed type 2 CRS subtypes. Mouse models that reflect pure type 2 inflammation of CRS are lacking.Objective:This study aims to establish a relatively pure type 2 CRS mouse model and compare it with 2 mixed type 2 CRS models.Methods:Three mouse CRS models were constructed: (1) aerosol ovalbumin (OVA) + aspergillus oryzae-derived protease (AP); (2) intranasal OVA + AP; (3) Intraperitoneal then intranasal OVA + AP (n = 10 per group). Nasal, lung symptoms, IgE, inflammatory cells, cytokines, and remodeling factors were evaluated.Results:Histological and micro-computed tomography showed inflammation, polyps, and opacification in all 3 experimental groups. The aerosol group had significantly increased local eosinophils and type 2 cytokines, while other types of cytokines showed no noticeable change. The nasal instillation groups also showed elevated other inflammatory factors and tissue polypoid changes were more pronounced. More severe pulmonary inflammation was observed with aerosol delivery.Conclusion:Aerosol inhalation mouse model is superior for studying nasal relatively pure type 2 inflammation and lower airway comorbidities.

1. Introduction

Type 2 chronic rhinosinusitis (CRS) is associated with severe eosinophilic infiltration and type 2 inflammation, which is characterized by severe symptoms, high recurrence rate, and high asthma comorbidity [1–3]. Over the past decades, researchers have found that the inflammatory state of type 2 CRS patients is not just a single endotype, some patients have a mix of other types of inflammation with different manifestations of symptoms, these patients are mixed type 2 CRS [4–6]. Pure type 2 CRS and mixed type 2 CRS show differences in inflammatory manifestations and remodeling conditions [7]. The upper and lower airways possess similar disease associations and immunopathological mechanisms [8, 9]. Studies have found that type 2 CRS patients are more likely to be accompanied by nasal polyps and asthma, while the combination of other inflammatory endotypes is more likely to have purulent rhinorrhea and other symptoms [10]. Asthmatic airway epithelial cells produce high levels of inflammatory cytokines (such as IL-1β and TSLP) [11]. Furthermore, eosinophils, IL-5, and IL-13 in the sputum of patients increase significantly [12].

In patients with pure type 2 CRS, the epithelial cells release alarmins such as thymic stromal lymphopoietin (TSLP) and interleukin (IL)-25, which initiate type 2 inflammation and eosinophilia in these patients [13–15]. In addition, mixed type 2 CRS can be accompanied by other inflammation-inducing factors, such as an increase in IL-1β, tumor necrosis factor (TNF)-α, interferon (IFN)-γ, IL-10, or IL-17A levels [16]. This causes mixed type 2 CRS features, in addition, to type 2 responses, submucosal type 1 responses that disrupt the epithelial barrier and promote tissue remodeling, unlike pure type 2 CRS [10]. Studies on the sinus infer that inflammatory responses can be induced due to the impairment of the epithelial barrier. This leads to a vicious cycle in which the subepithelial inflammation maintains damaged and open barriers [17, 18]. The cytokines IFN-γ and IL-13 decrease ciliated epithelial cells differentiation and ciliary beat frequency [19]. In addition, matrix metalloproteinases (MMPs) play an important role in the remodeling of CRS [20]. These changes are more apparent in pure type 2 or mixed type 2 CRS cases [6].

Previous studies did not distinguish pure type 2 CRS mouse models from mixed type 2 CRS models in mice, which were mostly mixed type 2 CRS [21–23]. Furthermore, few studies have examined the inflammatory state of the lower airways in the type 2 CRS mouse model. This study used ovalbumin (OVA) and aspergillus oryzae-derived protease (AP) to induce long-term stimulation of aerosol inhalation and nasal instillation with or without systemic sensitization in C57BL/6 mice. This study identified a method to establish a relatively pure type 2 CRS mouse model and comprehensively evaluated the symptoms, and nasopharyngeal inflammation phenotypes of relatively pure type 2 CRS and mixed type 2 CRS mice. This is significant for the subsequent study of different mouse nasal inflammation models.

2. Materials and methods

2.1. Experimental animals

A total of 60 female C57BL/6 mice (aged 6 weeks, 15–20 g each) were purchased from Vital River Laboratories (Beijing, China) and groomed in Viewsolid Biotech (Beijing, China). The animals were housed in a pathogen-free biohazard container facility for a week before initiating experiments. The Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee of the Capital Medical University, Beijing, China, approved all experiments.

2.2. Aerosol inhalation and nasal instillation models

The mice were randomly distributed into 6 groups: 3 control groups (A, B, C; n = 10 each) and 3 experimental groups (D, E, F; n = 10 each) (Fig. 1A). Mice were sensitized by aerosol inhalation, nasal instillation, or intraperitoneal injection followed by intranasal challenge. The nasal instillation mouse model was developed as previously described [15]. Full technical details are provided in the Supplementary Materials and Methods (Supplemental Digital Content 1, http://links.lww.com/PA9/A14).

Protocols for the development of eosinophilic chronic rhinosinusitis with nasal polyps in mice. (A) Ovalbumin (OVA) and Aspergillus oryzae-derived protease (AP) were used to induce systemic sensitization and/or local stimulation in experimental groups D, E, and F. Normal saline (NS) was used to induce systemic and/or local stimulation in the control groups A, B, and C. (B) Particle size distribution of atomized drops. V% indicates the aerosol particle size distribution, and ΣV% indicates the cumulative frequency of the size distribution. a. i., aerosol inhalation; i. p., intraperitoneal injection; n. i., nasal instillation.

2.3. Nasal symptom scores

Nasal symptoms, including sneezes and scratching, were scored every 2 weeks following the previously reported criteria [24]. The observation lasted for 30 minutes after each nasal challenge. The evaluation criteria are shown in Supplementary Table 1 (Supplemental Digital Content 2, http://links.lww.com/PA9/A15).

2.4. Blood samples and routine blood tests

For laboratory measurements, 150 µL whole mouse blood was collected into ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid (an in-vitro anticoagulant) -tubes (EDTA.K2, Guangdong Medical Technology, Taizhou, China) before sacrifice. Routine blood tests were immediately performed using a Sysmex BC-5100 automated hematology analyzer (Mindray, Shenzhen, China).

2.5. Micro-computed tomography

The nasal cavity and nasal sinus of mice were subjected to micro-computed tomography (CT) scanning before sacrifice. For the sinusitis evaluation, the coronal plane showing the true maxillary sinuses and ethmoid labyrinths was selected for each mouse. Full technical details are provided in the Supplementary Materials and Methods (Supplemental Digital Content 1, http://links.lww.com/PA9/A14).

2.6. Histopathological and morphological examinations of nasal mucosal and lung sections

After euthanization, the mice’s lungs and skinned skulls were fixed overnight in 10% neutral buffered formalin and then decalcified. After fixation, the tissues were dehydrated, embedded in paraffin, and then cut into 4 μm sections. Nasal mucosa was stained with hematoxylin and eosin (HE), periodic acid-Schiff (PAS), and picrosirius red, whereas lung tissues were stained with HE staining only. Polyp-like lesions were defined as distinct mucosal elevations with eosinophilic infiltration and/or microcavity formation [25]. The numbers of peribronchiolar and perivascular infiltrating inflammatory cells were scored [26]. The evaluation criteria are shown in Supplementary Table 2 (Supplemental Digital Content 3, http://links.lww.com/PA9/A16). Full technical details are provided in the Supplementary Materials and Methods (Supplemental Digital Content 1, http://links.lww.com/PA9/A14).

2.7. Immunohistochemistry

Mucosal remodeling-related factors MMP-2, MMP-8, MMP-9, and tissue inhibitor of metalloproteinase-1 (TIMP-1) in the nasal mucosa of mice were analyzed by immunohistochemistry (IHC) methods. Antibodies information is presented in Supplementary Table 3 (Supplemental Digital Content 4, http://links.lww.com/PA9/A17). Full technical details are provided in the Supplementary Materials and Methods (Supplemental Digital Content 1, http://links.lww.com/PA9/A14).

2.8. Immunofluorescence

The expression of MUC5AC, occludin, and zonula occludens-1 (ZO-1) in mouse nasal mucosa was analyzed by immunofluorescence (IF). Antibodies information is presented in Supplementary Table 3 (Supplemental Digital Content 4, http://links.lww.com/PA9/A17), and full technical details are provided in the Supplementary Materials and Methods (Supplemental Digital Content 1, http://links.lww.com/PA9/A14).

2.9. Real-time quantitative reverse transcription PCR

Levels of inflammatory cytokines (including IL-1β, CXCL-1, IFN-γ, TNF-α, IL-4, IL-13, TSLP, IL-25, IL-10, and IL-17A), MMPs (including MMP-2, MMP-8, and MMP-9) and TIMP-1 were evaluated by quantitative reverse transcription PCR (qRT-PCR). All primers were obtained from Sangon Biotech (Beijing, China), as shown in Supplementary Table 4 (Supplemental Digital Content 5, http://links.lww.com/PA9/A18). Full technical details are provided in the Supplementary Materials and Methods (Supplemental Digital Content 1, http://links.lww.com/PA9/A14).

2.10. Measurement of serum immunoglobulin E

Blood samples were centrifuged for 10 minutes at 3000 rpm/min. Blood serum was collected, and total immunoglobulin E (IgE) levels of the serum were measured using enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay per the manufacturer’s instructions (Abcam, Cambridge, MA, USA).

2.11. Luminex

Tissue proteins obtained from the nasal mucosa and lung were purified using a protein extraction kit (Beyotime Biotechnology, Nanjing, China). IL-4, IL-13, TSLP, IL-25, IL-1β, CXCL-1, IFN-γ, TNF-α, IL-10, and IL-17A in the nasal mucosa and lung homogenates were assessed using specific kits (ProcartaPlex Mouse and Rat Mix & Match Panels, Invitrogen, CA, USA). MMP-2, MMP-8, MMP-9, and TIMP-1 were assessed using specific kits (Mouse Premixed Multi-Analyte Kit; R&D Systems, Minneapolis, MN, USA). Luminex Assays were evaluated using a Bio-Plex 200 System (Bio-Rad Laboratories, Hercules, CA, USA). All tests were performed per the manufacturer’s instructions.

2.12. Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis was performed using IBM SPSS Statistics for Windows, Version 26.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA), and graphs were generated using Prism 9.0 (GraphPad Software, Inc., San Diego, CA, USA). Results were analyzed using analysis of variance (ANOVA) or Kruskal-Wallis one-way ANOVA with Dunn’s post hoc test for multiple comparisons. Data were presented as mean ± SD when data met normal distributions. Otherwise, results were analyzed using the Kruskal-Wallis H test. Statistical significance was set at P < 0.05.

2.13. Ethics

Ethical approval for this study (Approval NO. TRLAWEC-2022-002) was provided by the Capital Medical University Committee of Animal Experiments and Experimental Animal Welfare in 2022.

3. Results

3.1. Effect of different stimulation on nasal symptoms in mice

As shown in Fig. 2A, symptom scores were significantly higher in all 3 experimental groups than in the respective control groups at weeks 6 and 8 of administration (P < 0.01, P < 0.01, and P < 0.05, respectively) and were significantly higher in group D than in group E at week 6 (P < 0.01). After 10 weeks of continuous stimulation, the symptom scores of mice in each group decreased, especially in the 2 nasal instillation groups; however, there was no difference in the symptom scores between the treatment and control groups at week 16 (Fig. 2A).

(D) Total serum IgE. *P < 0.05, **P < 0.01, and ***P < 0.001 compared with experimental and control groups; #P < 0.05 and ##P < 0.01 compared with groups D and E.

3.2. Blood inflammatory cells and total serum IgE changes in 3 groups of mice

Blood inflammatory cells and total serum IgE were assessed. Blood neutrophils were higher in group F than in the respective control group (P < 0.01) (Fig. 2B), and eosinophils were significantly higher in groups D and F than in the respective control groups (P < 0.001 and P < 0.01, respectively; Fig. 2C). No difference was observed in other blood cells in the 3 experimental groups. Serum IgE concentrations significantly increased in groups D and F than in group E (both P < 0.001; Fig. 2D).

3.3. Micro-CT evaluation revealed fluid accumulation within the sinuses in all 3 groups of modeled mice

The CT scan images showed that the nasal septum, ethmoid labyrinth, and maxillary sinus mucosa were extensively thickened in the experimental groups (groups D, E, and F). A large quantity of secretions accumulated in the bent upward and obstructed ethmoid labyrinth of group D. In groups E and F, not only secretions were observed in the bent ethmoid labyrinth, but also in the open and downward sinus cavities with smooth drainage, accompanied by significant protrusion of the tissue mucosa (Fig. 3D–F).

Representative images of Micro-CT scan in groups A–F. Blue arrows indicate sinus cavity effusion or thickened mucosa. Scale bar = 1 mm.

3.4. Nasal sinus inflammation of mice is different in different stimulation groups

Local eosinophils in nasal mucosa were more abundant in the sinus mucosa of groups D and F than in group E (P < 0.01; Fig. 4A, B). Except for no significant difference in IL-25 protein expression in group F compared with the control group, the mRNA and protein expression of type 2 cytokines (IL-4, IL-13, TSLP, and IL-25) in nasal mucosa were significantly higher in group D and F than in the control group (all P < 0.05). The expression levels of IL-25 and TSLP were higher in group E compared with the respective control group (mRNA level, P < 0.01; protein level, P < 0.001); however, they were still lower than those in groups D and F (Fig. 4C, D).

IL-1β, IFN-γ, IL-17A, and TNF-α were significantly higher in both nasal instillation groups (all P < 0.05). CXCL-1 mRNA and protein expression were increased only in group F model mice compared with control mice (P < 0.01). The mRNA and protein expression levels of IFN-γ, TNF-α, and IL-17A in the nasal mucosa of mice stimulated with OVA + AP aerosol inhalation did not change compared with those in the control group A mice. The mRNA and protein expression levels of IL-10 were significantly increased in the nasal mucosa of all 3 models, especially in the aerosol inhalation group (Fig. 4C, D).

3.5. Nasal sinus remodeling of mice is different in different stimulation groups

Pseudostratified ciliated columnar epithelium with intact cilia and neatly arranged intercellular structures were observed on the surface of the nasal mucosa in the HE-stained control group. The polyp-like changes in the experimental groups were significant, especially in the nasal instillation groups. The epithelium was thoroughly exfoliated to the basement membrane, cilia of the epithelial layer were completely shed, subepithelial fibers were proliferated, cell arrangement was disordered, tissue structure was destroyed, most columnar epithelial cells were transformed into goblet cells, and mucus hypersecretion was observed (Fig. 4A).

PAS staining showed changes in the number of goblet cells on the nasal floor in all groups. The number of nasal mucosal goblet cells was significantly higher in the experimental groups than in the control groups (all P < 0.001), and there was no difference among all experimental groups (Fig. 5A, B). Staining of mice nasal sections for collagen deposition in the extracellular matrix was evaluated, and the amount of total collagen was reduced in the experimental groups, especially in groups E and F. The most abundant type 1 collagen (red) was decreased significantly, while type 3 collagen (green) was barely visible in the experimental groups (Fig. 5A, B).

3.6. Inflammation of lungs in different mouse models is different

As shown in Fig. 7A, the central airways of group D demonstrated significant inflammatory changes (especially increased eosinophils), submucosal edema, and smooth muscle hyperplasia, and a significant increase in inflammation scores compared with the control group (Fig. 7A, B).

Changes in inflammatory and remodeling-related factors in lungs of mouse models. (A) HE staining of mice lungs (×400 magnification). (B) The semi-quantitative analysis of the scores of inflammatory cell infiltration in the airways. (C) and (D) mRNA and protein expression levels of inflammatory cytokines in the lungs of mice. Scale bar = 20 μm. *P < 0.05, **P < 0.01, and ***P < 0.001.

Luminex and qRT-PCR results revealed that the mRNA and protein expression levels of type 2 cytokines IL-13 and TSLP in lung tissues were significantly elevated only in the aerosol inhalation group (all P < 0.05; Fig. 7C, D). The mRNA and protein expression levels of CXCL-1, IFN-γ, and TNF-α were significantly higher in both nasal instillation groups than in the control groups (all P < 0.05; Fig. 7C, D). IL-10 level in lung tissue was significantly elevated in the aerosol inhalation group (both P < 0.01), and its protein expression level was significantly elevated than in groups E and F (both P < 0.01). mRNA and protein expression levels of IL-17A did not differ significantly among all models (Fig. 7C, D).

4. Discussion

In this study, the modeling of chronic sinusitis with nasal polyps in mice nasal cavities was performed using 3 different methods, and the effects of the different modeling methods on the inflammatory status of the mice’s nasals and lungs were compared. The results revealed that the mouse model of type 2 CRS established using aerosol inhalation exhibited a relatively pure type 2 endotype [6]. In contrast, the mouse model of CRS established using nasal instillation exhibited a type 2 endotype mixed with other inflammatory types [27, 28]. The nasal inflammation in the nasal instillation-only mouse model was similar to that in the mouse model of pre-intraperitoneal injection with nasal instillation; however, the type 2 inflammation was inferior to that in the pre-intraperitoneal sensitization mouse model. Among all experimental groups, the pulmonary inflammation in the aerosol inhalation group was more prominent.

With further research on genetic mechanisms underlying the pathogenesis of type 2 CRS and the widespread use of C57BL/6 transgenic mice, there is a growing demand for in-depth analysis of C57BL/6 mouse models of pure type 2 CRS or mixed type 2 CRS. The C57BL/6 mice have attenuated allergic airway hyperresponsiveness compared with BALB/c mice; however, the underlying mechanisms remain unclear [21]. This makes the C57BL/6 mouse model of CRS more challenging to study. Previous studies have attempted to develop a C57BL/6 mouse model of CRS [21, 23]. However, no modeling method has established an actual type 2 CRS or mixed type 2 CRS with mucus aggregation in the sinus cavity. Notably, we successfully built this model.

The evidence of successful modeling was first reflected in the behavior of mice. The scratching and sneezing symptoms in the 3 experimental groups peaked at weeks 6–8 of stimulation, after which the symptoms began to gradually disperse (Fig. 2A), which is consistent with a previous study [29]. This is probably because sensitized mice might have acquired tolerance to the allergen after too many stimulations [30].

In this study, it was observed that among the 3 different mouse model groups, the aerosol inhalation group exhibited relatively non-mixed type 2 inflammation compared with the control group (Fig. 4C, D). This is consistent with the findings of Hussain et al [31]. This may be associated with the different modes of stimulation [32]. Group E and F exhibited a type 2 endotype mixed with types 1, 3 and other endotypes, accompanied by significant polypoid remodeling. This is consistent with the findings of a previous study by Kim et al [25].

Micro-CT examination revealed mucosal thickening and mucus retention in all experimental groups. However, mucus accumulation was mainly in the upward-curved sinus cavity in the aerosol inhalation group, whereas the mucus retention was both in the upward-curved septal sinus cavity and the sinus cavity with a downward opening and unobstructed drainage in both nasal instillation groups. This result indicates that the mucosal swelling of the sinus cavity was more severe in the nasal instillation group, which significantly affected the drainage of the sinuses [33]. The mouse nasal section staining revealed that in addition to more obvious swelling of the mucosa of the nasal sinus, the epithelial structure of the nasal mucosa was also more severely disrupted in the nasal instillation group, causing complete goblet cell metaplasia to the columnar epithelium and more severe disruption of the submucosal collagen, which were reported as the characteristic features of NPs in previous reports (Figs. 4A, 5A) [34].

A significant decrease in the expression of the TJ-related proteins, occluding, and ZO-1, was observed in the 3 experimental groups after IF staining. The epithelial physical barrier is maintained by intercellular junctions, specifically TJs, which inhibit the movement of solutes and water through the paracellular space and are located at the tip, attached to the cytoskeleton [35]. TJ molecules and epithelial barrier function in CRS may be regulated by various internal and external factors, such as inhalation of allergens, microbial or viral infections, cytokines, hypoxia, or zinc deficiency [36]. In addition, immunohistochemical staining revealed significantly higher expression of MMP-2, MMP-8, and TIMP-1 in both nasal instillation groups than in the aerosol inhalation group (Fig. 6). This may be due to the remodeling of groups E and F and the significantly increased expression of IL-1β, IL-17, and TNF-α factors in the 2 groups. It has been observed that IL-1β, IL-17, and TNF-α can upregulate the production of MMPs through different pathways [37–39].

HE staining of lung tissues revealed clear signs of inflammation in group D, such as increased eosinophils and submucosal edema around the airways (Fig. 7A). Luminex and qRT-PCR assays revealed changes in inflammatory factors in all experimental groups. Type 2 cytokines, IL-13 and TSLP, were significantly elevated in the aerosol inhalation group (Fig. 7C, D). It is difficult for large-size aerosol particles to reach the lungs of mice [40, 41]; however, this study’s mouse model revealed that it can cause lung inflammation. This suggests a pathological continuum of allergy, asthma, infection, and inflammation based on the interaction of the upper and lower respiratory tracts [42]. In addition, the inflammation of the lungs in the aerosol inhalation group was higher than that in the nasal instillation group. This may be due to increased tolerance to aerosols inhaled through nebulization. Furthermore, nasal instillation liquid entering the airway induced aggravated cough in mice, resulting in less exposure to stimuli in the airway [43].

With in-depth knowledge of CRS pathogenesis, clinicians are learning more about its inflammatory subtypes, which can improve treatment delivery and effectiveness [6]. Based on the predominance of inflammatory endotypes, CRS is divided into type 2 skewed inflammation and non-type 2 CRS. This has pathogenic and treatment implications [44]. Type 2 immune responses of CRS are associated with upregulated production of TSLP, IL-4, IL-13, IL-25, local IgE, and profound eosinophilia [45]. The present study’s mouse model established using aerosol inhalation successfully simulated relatively pure type 2 CRS manifestations. In addition, some CRS has significant levels of neutrophils and non-type 2 cytokines [5]. Tissue injury is caused because type 1 responses below the mucosal surface are characterized by the opening of the epithelial barrier and induction of apoptosis in epithelial cells [46]. Patients with mixed-patterned inflammation tend to have higher symptom scores, greater cytokine burden, and mucin hypersecretion than those with a single major endotype [47, 48]. The mixed Type 2 CRS mouse model established using nasal instillation demonstrated a type 2 endotype combined with non-type 2 cytokines. Therefore, these mouse models are of great significance to basic and clinical research of type 2 and mixed type 2 CRS.

This study had some limitations, such as the failure to elucidate the underlying mechanism of the inconsistent expression of some cytokine mRNAs and proteins in the nasal mucosa and lung tissues. In addition, the sample size for nasal staining was relatively insufficient.

5. Conclusion

The results of this study indicate that inhalation of OVA + AP aerosol is more likely to cause nasal mucosa and lung manifestations resembling human relatively pure type 2 CRS in mice. The nasal instillation model showed obvious mixed type 2 CRS with other inflammation and prominent remodeling of the nasal mucosa. Therefore, our models are valuable tools for studying relatively pure type 2 CRS.

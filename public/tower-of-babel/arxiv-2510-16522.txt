On the density of Kravitz sets
Vsevolod Lev, Máté Matolcsi, Péter Pál Pach, Dániel Varga

Abstract
. We show that for a subset A A of the cyclic group of prime order p > 3 p>3 , if the sumset A + A − 2 ​ A A+A-2A is not the whole group, then | A | ≤ 2 7 ​ p |A|\leq\frac{2}{7}\,p . Besides combinatorial arguments, we utilize a general technique involving linear programming.

1.
Introduction

At the problem session of the Edinburgh meeting on additive combinatorics (2024), Noah Kravitz asked how large a subset
A\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{p}
can be such that
A+A-2A\neq\mathbb{F}_{p}
. Here and throughout, for a prime
p
, by
\mathbb{F}_{p}
we denote the finite field with
p
elements, by
\lambda A
the dilate of the set
A
by the factor
\lambda

\lambda A=\{\lambda a\colon a\in A\},

and by
A+A-2A
the sumset

A+A-2A=\{a_{1}+a_{2}-2a_{3}\colon a_{1},a_{2},a_{3}\in A\}.

(Other sumsets that occur below are defined similarly.)

Dilating and taking sumset operations can be combined to produce
sums of dilates

\lambda_{1}A+\dotsb+\lambda_{k}A
.
In the integer settings, Bukh
[
3
]
proved that for any finite set
A\subseteq\mathbb{Z}
, and any
\lambda_{1},\dotsc,\lambda_{k}\in\mathbb{Z}
with
\gcd(\lambda_{1},\dotsc,\lambda_{k})=1
, one has

|\lambda_{1}A+\dots+\lambda_{k}A|\geqslant\Big(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k}|\lambda_{i}|\Big)|A|-o(|A|),

where the error term
o(|A|)
depends on the coefficients
\lambda_{1},\dotsc,\lambda_{k}
. Pontiveros
[
10
]
proved that the analogous estimate holds in
\mathbb{F}_{p}
, provided that the set
A
has a sufficiently small density. For dense sets
A
, this may not be the case. As shown in
[
10
]
, for any nonzero integer
\lambda
and any real
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
\delta=\delta(\lambda,\varepsilon)>0
such that the following
holds: if
p
is a sufficiently large prime, then there exists a set
A\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{p}
with
|A|\geqslant(1/2-\varepsilon)p

such that
|A+\lambda A|\leqslant(1-\delta)p
. Pontiveros also stated the following conjecture.

Conjecture 1

(
[
10
, Conjecture 1.3]
)
.

For any nonzero integers
\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{k}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
and any real
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
\delta=\delta(\lambda,\varepsilon)>0
such that the following
holds: If
p
is a sufficiently large prime, then there exists a set
A\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{p}
with
|A|>(1/k-\varepsilon)p
such
that

|\lambda_{1}A+\dots+\lambda_{k}A|\leqslant(1-\delta)p.

For more results on the sums of dilates, see
[
2
,
4
,
5
,
6
,
7
,
9
]
.

In this paper, we refute this conjecture. Addressing the case
\lambda_{1}=\lambda_{2}=1,\lambda_{3}=-2
, we prove the following result.

Theorem 1
.

If
p>3
is a prime and
A\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{p}
is a subset of size
|A|>\frac{2}{7}\,p
, then
A+A-2A=\mathbb{F}_{p}
.

Conjecturally, the assumption
|A|>\frac{2}{7}\,p
can be relaxed to
|A|>(\frac{1}{4}+o(1))\,p
, while the trivial sufficient assumption is
|A|>\frac{1}{3}\,p
; see a brief discussion at the end of this section.

The sumset
A+A-2A
considered in Theorem
1
is particularly interesting, as it leads to the problem of finding the largest possible size of a set avoiding solutions of the equation
a_{1}-2a_{2}+a_{3}=c
for some nonzero element
c\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
. Note that for
c=0
one would get the problem of bounding the size of the largest 3AP-free set in
\mathbb{F}_{p}
.

Clearly, Theorem
1
refutes Conjecture
1
for
k=3
,
\lambda_{1}=\lambda_{2}=1,\lambda_{3}=-2
.
In fact, it is not difficult to give an explicit counterexample to Conjecture
1
, and even one with all coefficients
\lambda_{1},\dotsc,\lambda_{k}
being pairwise distinct.
Namely, let
k=6
,
\lambda_{1}=10
,
\lambda_{2}=12
,
\lambda_{3}=15
,
\lambda_{4}=20
,
\lambda_{5}=30
, and
\lambda_{6}=-87
. We show that if
|A|>p/7>1
, then the sumset
S\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=10A+12A+15A+20A+30A-87A
is the whole group
\mathbb{F}_{p}
. Clearly,
0\in S
, and we show that
60\in 10A+12A+15A+20A+30A-87A
; applying this to the dilated set
60c^{-1}A
, we conclude that
c\in S
for every
c\neq 0
, as well. From the assumption
|A|>p/7>1
it follows that
(A-A)\cap\{1,2,3,4,5,6\}\neq\varnothing
; that is, the difference set
A-A
contains at least one of the elements
1,2,3,4,5,6
. If
1\in A-A
, then we choose
a\in A
such that also
a+1\in A
, and obtain a representation of
60
using the identity

10(a+1)+12a+15a+20(a+1)+30(a+1)-87a=60.

The cases
2\in A-A,\dotsc,6\in A-A
can be dealt with in a similar manner, using the identities

•

10a+12a+15a+20a+30(a+2)-87a=60
,

•

10a+12a+15a+20(a+3)+30a-87a=60
,

•

10a+12a+15(a+4)+20a+30a-87a=60
,

•

10a+12(a+5)+15a+20a+30a-87a=60
,

•

10(a+6)+12a+15a+20a+30a-87a=60
.

For a set
A\subset\mathbb{F}_{p}
, by
\delta(A)
we denote the density of
A
; that is,
\delta(A)=\frac{|A|}{p}
.

For positive integers
a,b,c
let

\displaystyle M_{a,b,c}(p)

\displaystyle\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\max\{|A|\colon A\subset\mathbb{F}_{p},aA+bA-cA\neq\mathbb{F}_{p}\}

and

\displaystyle m_{a,b,c}

\displaystyle\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\limsup_{p\to\infty}\{\delta(A)\colon A\subset\mathbb{F}_{p},aA+bA-cA\neq\mathbb{F}_{p}\}.

In this note we investigate the quantities
M_{1,1,2}(p)
and
m_{1,1,2}
. We remark that the integer analogue of the latter quantity
m_{a,b,c}

can be studied, and our methods also work in the integer settings.

If we make the identification
\mathbb{F}_{p}=\{0,1,\dots,p-1\}
, it is easy to check that the set
A\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\{1,\dotsc,\left\lfloor(p+1)/4\right\rfloor\}
satisfies
A+A-2A\neq\mathbb{F}_{p}
; therefore,
M_{1,1,2}(p)\geq\left\lfloor(p+1)/4\right\rfloor
and
m_{1,1,2}\geq 1/4
. We conjecture that this construction is the best possible for all values of
p
. We have verified this conjecture computationally up to
p\leq 149
.

Let us remark here that the trivial upper bound is
m_{1,1,2}\leqslant 1/3
, which can be seen as follows. If
A+A-2A\neq\mathbb{F}_{p}
, we may assume that
2\notin A+A-2A
(since, if
c\neq 0
is missing, we may replace the set
A
by
2c^{-1}A
). Then, similarly to the above discussion,
(A-A)\cap\{1,2\}=\emptyset
because of the representations
a+a-2a^{\prime}=2
(for
a-a^{\prime}=1
) and
a+a^{\prime}-2a^{\prime}=2
(for
a-a^{\prime}=2
). This yields
|A|\leqslant p/3
for every prime
p>2
. Note that the same bound is also a direct consequence of the Cauchy-Davenport inequality, as
|A+A-2A|\geqslant\min(3|A|-2,p)
.

In the next section, we prove Theorem
1

using a general linear programming approach in the spirit of
[
1
]
. Yet another, purely combinatorial proof is presented in Section
3
.

2.
Linear programming proof of
Theorem
1

In this section, borrowing some ideas and notation from
[
1
]
and
[
8
]
, we give a linear programming proof of Theorem
1
. Some technical details of the proof are presented in the Appendix.

If
A\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{p}
satisfies
A+A-2A\neq\mathbb{F}_{p}
then, trivially, there is a nonzero element
d\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
with
d\notin A+A-2A
. Conversely, to prove Theorem
1
it suffices to show that for some fixed, nonzero
d\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
, assuming
|A|>\frac{2}{7}\,p
we have
d\in A+A-2A
; applying this result to the set
c^{-1}dA
, we then conclude that
c\in A+A-2A
for any nonzero
c\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
; that is,
A+A-2A=\mathbb{F}_{p}
. As we will see, it is convenient to choose
d=6
, but for the time being we keep the argument general.

In the sequel, for any set
Y\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{p}
we use the notation
Y^{1}=Y
and
Y^{-1}=\mathbb{F}_{p}\setminus Y
.

Following
[
1
]
, we will be seeking a
witness set

X=\{x_{1},x_{2},\dotsc,x_{n}\}\subset\mathbb{F}_{p}
with certain properties. For any vector
\varepsilon\in\{\pm 1\}^{n}
we associate the
atomic density

a_{X}(\varepsilon)=\delta\left(\cap_{i=1}^{n}(A-x_{i})^{\varepsilon_{i}}\right)

(1)

and, for
I=\{i_{1},\dotsc,i_{r}\}\subset\{1,2,\dotsc,n\}
, we define the
aggregate functional
of atomic densities by

\overline{a}_{X}(I)=\sum_{\varepsilon|_{I}=+1}a_{X}(\varepsilon)=\delta((A-x_{i_{1}})\cap\dots\cap(A-x_{i_{r}}))

(where the summation extends onto all vectors
\varepsilon=(\varepsilon_{1},\dotsc,\varepsilon_{n})
such that
\varepsilon_{i_{k}}=1
for each
k=1,\dots,r
). The intuition behind the atomic density
a_{X}(\varepsilon)
is the following: if we drop a randomly translated copy of
A
in
\mathbb{F}_{p}
, the value
a_{X}(\varepsilon)
is the probability that the intersection of this copy with
X
is exactly the subset
\{x_{j}\in X\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\ \varepsilon_{j}=+1\}.

Trivially, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\varepsilon\in\{\pm 1\}^{n}}a_{X}(\varepsilon)=1,

(2)

\displaystyle a_{X}(\varepsilon)\geq 0\ \text{for all vectors}\ \varepsilon\in\{\pm 1\}^{n},

(3)

and

\displaystyle\sum_{\varepsilon\colon\varepsilon_{j}=1}a_{X}(\varepsilon)=\delta(A-x_{j})=\delta(A)\ \text{for all}\ 1\leq j\leq n.

(4)

Less obviously,

a_{X}(\varepsilon)=0\ \text{whenever $x_{i}+x_{j}-2x_{k}=d$ \ \text{and}}\ \varepsilon_{i}=\varepsilon_{j}=\varepsilon_{k}=1.

(5)

The explanation of property (
5
) is as follows: if
a_{X}(\varepsilon)\neq 0
while
\varepsilon_{i}=\varepsilon_{j}=\varepsilon_{k}=1
, then the intersection
(A-x_{i})\cap(A-x_{j})\cap(A-x_{k})
is nonempty; consequently, there exist
a,b,c\in A
and
y\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
such that
y=a-x_{i}=b-x_{j}=c-x_{k}
implying
x_{i}+x_{j}-2x_{k}=a+b-2c\neq d
.

Similarly to
[
1
,
8
]
, we use translation invariance of the problem to add one more observation to our collection: if
x_{i_{1}},\dotsc,x_{i_{r}}
is a collection of distinct elements in
X
, and
x_{j_{1}},\dotsc,x_{j_{r}}\in X
is a translate of it, i.e.
x_{j_{1}}=x_{i_{1}}+t,\dotsc,x_{j_{r}}=x_{i_{r}}+t
with some
t\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
, then

\overline{a}_{X}(i_{1},\dotsc,i_{r})=\overline{a}_{X}(j_{1},\dotsc,j_{r}).

(6)

We now change the viewpoint, reinterpreting
a_{X}(\varepsilon)
as formal variables of a linear program, and (
2
)–(
6
) as constraints these variables satisfy. We define the objective function of our program to be
\delta(A)
, which is a linear function of the variables by equation (
4
); in the actual computations we use the specific choice
j=1
in (
4
) to define the target function.
For each set
X\subset\mathbb{F}_{p}
, the maximum of the objective function sets an upper bound for the largest possible density
\delta(A)
. We are thus interested in finding a witness set
X=\{x_{1},\dotsc,x_{n}\}\subset\mathbb{F}_{p}
, preferably of small cardinality, which gives a sharp upper bound on
\delta(A)
by the linear program specified above.

We remark that, using the language of
[
1
,
8
]
, our linear program above corresponds to the geometric fractional chromatic number (GFCN) of
X
. If the reader is familiar with those papers, this is a good analogy to keep in mind.

In the Appendix, we specify
d\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=6
, and present a rigorous documentation of the linear program described above, for the witness set
X=\{0,2,3,4,7,8,9,10\}
. This linear program has size
22\times 53
independently of
p
, and it proves the bound
\delta(A)\leq\frac{2}{7}
for any
p\geq 11
(the cases,
p=5,7
can easily be checked individually). This completes the proof of Theorem
1
.

It may be of some interest that, historically, we first considered the case
d=2
, the choice motivated by the argument in the Introduction, that gives
\delta(A)\leq 1/3
. It turns out that for
d=2
, and for primes of the form
p=3r+1
, we can choose
X=\{0,1,2,3,r,r+1,r+2,2r,2r+1\}
, and the resulting linear program does not depend on
r
(which is not very hard to verify, but we will not do so here, as we will only document the case
d=6
rigorously in the Appendix). This linear program yields the bound
\delta(A)\leq 2/7
for any
p=3r+1
. We also observed that there exists a solution of the dual linear program with small integer coefficients. For primes of the form
p=3r-1
we could choose
X=\{0,1,2,3,r,r+1,r+2,2r+1,2r+3\}
, and obtain the bound
\delta(A)\leq 2/7
again. Note that the bounds above are based on the choice
d=2
, and that the witness set
X
is concentrated around 0,
p/3
and
2p/3
in both cases. This suggests that if we take
d=6
, we may be able to find a witness set
X
concentrated around 0 only. This line of thought has led to taking
d=6
and
X=\{0,2,3,4,7,8,9,10\}
as mentioned above, and as documented in the Appendix.

In fact, whenever
n
is co-prime to 6, this witness set
X
testifies that for any set
A\subset\mathbb{Z}_{n}
such that
6\notin A+A-2A
we have
\delta(A)\leq 2/7
. Thus, primality of the modulus can be replaced with the weaker assumption that the modulus is co-prime to
6
.

Remark ​​​
.

For small values of
p
, we can obtain better bounds by choosing the witness set
X
appropriately.
For example, for
p=151
,
d=2
, an extensive computer search finds the
23
-element witness set shown in Figure
1
. It gives the upper bound
|A|\leq 755/19\approx 39.737
. Therefore,
|A|\leq 39
follows, whereas the lower bound is
38
.

Figure 1
.
A 23 element witness set for
p=151
and
d=2
, giving a density upper bound
5/19
. Removing any of the elements results in a weaker bound.

However, it is not clear whether there is an easy way to generalize the choice of
X_{p}
for all
p
. Perhaps, one can obtain the bound
\delta(A)\leq 3/11
for all
p
, by a clever choice of
X
. However, the emerging linear program testifying this bound is likely to be very large.

3.
Combinatorial proof of Theorem
1

Another way to obtain upper bounds is by means of a combinatorial argument, using “local constraints”.

For a nonempty subset
Y\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{p}
, let

\Delta_{d,p}(Y)\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\max\{|A\cap Y|\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\ A\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{p},\ d\notin A+A-2A\}.

This quantity is translation invariant:
\Delta_{d,p}(Y+t)=\Delta_{d,p}(Y)
for any
t\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
.
Consequently, for any
A\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{p}
with
d\notin A+A-2A
we have

\Delta_{d,p}(Y)\geq\frac{1}{p}\sum_{t\in\mathbb{F}_{p}}|A\cap(Y+t)|=\frac{1}{p}\,|A||Y|;

that is,

\delta(A)\leq\frac{\Delta_{d,p}(Y)}{|Y|}

(7)

Notice that we could have alternatively defined

\Delta_{d,p}(Y)=\max\{|X|\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\ X\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{p},\ X\subseteq Y,\ d\notin X+X-2X\},

and this form of the definition extends readily onto the integer settings: namely, for a set
Y\subseteq\mathbb{Z}
, let

\Delta_{d,0}(Y)=\max\{|X|\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\ X\subseteq\mathbb{Z},\ X\subseteq Y,\ d\notin X+X-2X\}.

Clearly,
\Delta_{d,0}(Y)\geqslant\Delta_{d,p}(Y)
for any prime
p
, and any
Y\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{p}=\{0,1,\dots,p-1\}
.
Therefore, for any
p
, and any
A\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{p}
, equation (
7
) implies

\delta(A)\leqslant\frac{\Delta_{d,0}(Y)}{|Y|}.

In view of the linear programming argument from the previous section, we can hope to obtain a good upper bound
\frac{\Delta_{d,0}(Y)}{|Y|}
by choosing
d=6
and taking
Y
to be a set concentrated around
0
. Indeed, for
d=6
and
Y=\{0,1,\dotsc,41\}
one has
\Delta_{d,0}(Y)=12
. This can be verified either using a computer, or with a somewhat tedious pen-and-paper analysis, along the following lines. Suppose that
X\subset[0,41]
is a set of size
|X|=13
such that
6\notin X+X-2X
. Since
x-x^{\prime}=3
implies
x+x-2x^{\prime}=6
, and
x-x^{\prime}=6
implies
x+x^{\prime}-2x^{\prime}=6
, we have
3\notin X-X
and
6\notin X-X
.
Therefore, each of the
14
-element sets
S_{1}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\{0,3,\dots,39\}
,
S_{2}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\{1,4,\dots,40\}
, and
S_{3}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\{2,5,\dots,41\}
, contains at most
5
elements of
X
. For each of these sets, there are
6
possibilities to choose a
5
-element subset avoiding differences
3
and
6
, and
70
, resp.
120
possibilities to choose a
4
-element, resp.
3
-element subset satisfying this restriction. To choose
13
elements altogether, from all three sets
S_{i}
, the number of chosen elements should be distributed among the sets
S_{i}
as
5-5-3
or
5-4-4
, in some order. However, all such sets turn out to contain a solution to
x+y-2z=6
(this requires a tedious case-by-case analysis, which is possible to carry out by hand, but is much faster by computer).

Thus,
\Delta_{6,0}(Y)=12
, implying

\delta(A)\leq\frac{\Delta_{6,0}(Y)}{|Y|}=\frac{2}{7},

for any prime
p>41
.
This is the same upper bound as in Section
2
. For
3<p\leq 41
the bound can easily be checked for each individual value of
p
.

An accurate case analysis also shows that most of the sets
A\subset Y
with
|A|=12
and
6\notin A+A-2A
have a
\,\textrm{mod }7
periodic pattern (with some irregularities due to the relatively small length 42).

As such, in order to break the pattern mod 7, it is natural to try
d=42
and
Y=\{0,1,\dots,461\}
. In this case we conjecture that
\Delta_{d,0}(Y)=126
. If so, the bound
\delta(A)\leq 3/11
would follow. Unfortunately, the case analysis becomes very long and cumbersome with such large numbers. We believe it can still be carried out with some computer assistance, and will eventually yield the bound
\delta(A)\leq 3/11
. (However, we note that a simple integer programming implementation will not work: the size of the problem is far too large. One needs to do a cleverly guided case analysis.)

Again, unfortunately, we will not reach the bound 1/4 in this manner – at least not in a computational way. As we have seen, for any prime
q
of residue 3 mod 4, there is a construction of size
\frac{q+1}{4}
, that is of density slightly above
1/4
. This means that for any integer
m
with
q\mid m
the density
\frac{q+1}{4}
can be achieved in
\mathbb{Z}_{m}
(by taking a suitable mod
q
periodic set), thus to asymptotically reach the bound
1/4
, the primeness of
p
must be used in the argument.

4.
Concluding remarks

We proved that for any prime
p>3
, and any set
A\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{p}
of size
|A|>\frac{2}{7}\,p
we have
A+A-2A=\mathbb{F}_{p}
. A major challenge is to improve the coefficient
\frac{2}{7}
to the conjectured
\frac{1}{4}+o(1)
. It would also be interesting to study other sums of dilates and understand how the critical density depends on the coefficients.

Finally, we present here a Fourier analytic argument which could, in principle, lead to an improved upper bound on
\delta(A)
. The argument is a further elaboration on the linear programming approach presented in Section
2
, and it originates from
[
1
]
, where it is of critical importance. However, in our present setting it only yields a very modest improvement for some values of
p
, and for some witness sets
X\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{p}
. We could not obtain any improvement independent of
p
so far. It would be nice to understand why, and whether it can be modified to produce a significant improvement.

The function
f(x)\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\delta(A\cap(A-x))=\frac{1}{p}\,1_{A}\ast 1_{-A}
is positive definite; that is, can be written as

f(x)=\frac{|A|^{2}}{p^{2}}+\sum_{m=1}^{\frac{p-1}{2}}\kappa_{m}\cos(2\pi mx/p),\quad x\in[0,p-1]

with nonnegative coefficients
\kappa_{m}
.
On the other hand, if
x_{i}
and
x_{j}
are elements of a witness set
X=\{x_{1},\dotsc,x_{n}\}
, with some indices
i,j\in[1,n],\ i\neq j
, then the value of
f(x_{i}-x_{j})
can be expressed as a sum of atomic densities:

f(x_{i}-x_{j})=\delta(A\cap(A-x_{i}+x_{j}))=\overline{a}_{X}(\{i,j\})=\sum_{\varepsilon_{i}=\varepsilon_{j}=1}a_{X}(\varepsilon)

where
\varepsilon=(\varepsilon_{1},\dotsc,\varepsilon_{n})
with
\varepsilon_{i}=\varepsilon_{j}=1
. Hence,

\frac{|A|^{2}}{p^{2}}+\sum_{m=1}^{\frac{p-1}{2}}\kappa_{m}\cos(2\pi m(x_{i}-x_{j})/p)=\sum_{\varepsilon_{i}=\varepsilon_{j}=1}a_{X}(\varepsilon).

(8)

We thus can expand our linear program with the new variables
\kappa_{m}\geq 0
(in addition to the variables
a_{X}(\varepsilon)
) and new constraints (
8
). Unfortunately, computations show that the resulting linear program usually has the same target value as the original one, while improvements are rare and minor.

The vector
0\leq x\in\mathbb{R}^{53}
of these atomic densities satisfies the following constraints:

\langle\bm{1},x\rangle=1
by (
2
), and
Cx=0
where
C
is the matrix corresponding to congruences defined by (
6

4
), but note here that the constraints (
6
) include (
4

6

X
we get back (
4

\{10\}\cong\{9\}
determines the first row of the congruence matrix
C
by taking the difference of the corresponding rows of
T
, namely the rows indexed by
10
and
9
, respectively. Each row of
C
is created in a similar manner: a congruence of two subsets
Y\cong Z
is listed on the left, the rows of
T
corresponding to the elements of
Y
are multiplied elementwise to obtain a vector
r_{Y}
, and similarly for
Z
, and finally the difference
r_{Y}-r_{Z}
yields the row in the matrix
C

6

By convention, the sign + means a +1 entry, and – means a
-1
entry.

The vector
e

T

\langle e,x\rangle

4

0\in X
.

\langle e,x\rangle
subject to the constraints
x\geq 0
,
\langle\bm{1},x\rangle=1
and
Cx=0
.

y

witnesses the fact that for any admissible vector
x

X

Safety and efficacy of elexacaftor/tezacaftor/ivacaftor in adolescents and adults with cystic fibrosis and F508del-gating and F508del-residual function genotypes: results from an open-label extension study
Authors: James F Chmiel, Kimberly McBennett, Bradley S Quon, Carla Colombo, Elke De Wachter, Isabelle Fajac, Pedro Mondejar-Lopez, Deepika Polineni, Philip Robinson, Sivagurunathan Sutharsan, Marcus A Mall, Edward F McKone, Bonnie Ramsey, Jennifer L Taylor-Cousar, Elizabeth Tullis, Nina E Suresh, Yaohua Zhang, Patrick Sosnay, Mark Jennings, Peter J Barry, the 445-110 Study Group
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractRationaleIn an 8-week, active-controlled, phase 3 study (445-104), elexacaftor/tezacaftor/ivacaftor (ELX/TEZ/IVA) was shown to be efficacious and safe in participants ≥ 12 years of age with cystic fibrosis (CF) and F508del-gating (F/G) or F508del-residual function (F/RF) genotypes, conferring additional clinical benefits relative to IVA and TEZ/IVA. Participants who completed this 8-week parent study continued to an open-label extension.ObjectiveTo assess the long-term safety and efficacy of ELX/TEZ/IVA in adolescents and adults with CF and F/G or F/RF genotypes.MethodsThis 2-part (Part A [96 weeks] and Part B [48 weeks]) phase 3 open-label extension study enrolled adolescents and adults age ≥ 12 years with CF and F/G or F/RF genotypes who completed study 445-104. Primary endpoint (Part A and Part B) was safety and tolerability. Secondary endpoints (Part A only) included absolute changes in percent predicted FEV1 (ppFEV1), sweat chloride concentration, CF Questionnaire-Revised (CFQ-R) respiratory domain score, and body mass index (BMI).ResultsA total of 251 participants received ≥ 1 dose of ELX/TEZ/IVA in Part A; 217 (86.5%) completed treatment. In Part A, 96.0% of participants had ≥ 1 adverse event (AE), which for most were mild (32.3%) or moderate (55.0%) in severity. Thirteen participants (5.2%) discontinued due to treatment-emergent AEs. Participants who received ELX/TEZ/IVA in the parent study maintained improvements in ppFEV1, sweat chloride concentration, CFQ-R respiratory domain score, and BMI while participants who received IVA or TEZ/IVA (active controls) in parent study had similar improvements after transitioning to ELX/TEZ/IVA. Eighty-four participants (33.5%) entered Part B, 96.4% of whom discontinued due to commercial drug availability. In Part B, 62 participants (73.8%) had ≥ 1 AE, which for most were mild or moderate in severity and none of which led to discontinuation.ConclusionsELX/TEZ/IVA remained generally safe and well-tolerated with no new safety findings. Improvements in lung function, CFTR function, respiratory symptoms, and nutritional status after starting ELX/TEZ/IVA were maintained through 96 weeks of follow-up. These results demonstrate the safety and durable efficacy of ELX/TEZ/IVA in adolescents and adults with F/G or F/RF genotypes.Keywords: elexacaftor, residual function, gating, CFTR, cystic fibrosis

Introduction

Cystic fibrosis (CF) is an autosomal recessive disease caused by pathogenic variants in the CF transmembrane conductance regulator (CFTR) gene that impair the flow of anions through the CFTR channel.1,2 Clinical manifestations of CF include respiratory impairment, pancreatic insufficiency, and liver dysfunction.3-6

Pathogenic variants in CFTR that impact the normal processing and trafficking of the CFTR protein, such as F508del, reduce the quantity of CFTR protein at the cell surface while variants that impede CFTR channel function (eg, gating defects) reduce the flow of anions through the channel. CFTR modulators are small-molecule therapeutics that address the biochemical consequences of these pathogenic variants by increasing amounts of CFTR protein at the cell surface (eg, correctors such as tezacaftor [TEZ] and elexacaftor [ELX]) or by improving the channel-opening probability/gating function of CFTR proteins (eg, the potentiator ivacaftor [IVA]).7-12 Previous studies showed that people with CF and CFTR gating variants (eg, G551D) treated with IVA had improvements in lung function, growth, and quality of life.13,14 Similarly, people with CF heterozygous for F508del and another CFTR variant that results in lesser impairment of CFTR protein function known as a residual function variant (F/RF genotypes) treated with TEZ/IVA had improvements in lung and CFTR function.15

The triple combination CFTR modulator therapy ELX/TEZ/IVA has been shown in phase 3 and open-label extension studies to be efficacious and safe in people with CF ≥ 2 years of age who have 1 or 2 F508del alleles.16-22 An additional study was conducted specifically in the population that was eligible for IVA (F508del-gating [F/G] and F/RF genotypes) or TEZ/IVA (F/RF genotypes) to evaluate additional benefits of ELX/TEZ/IVA on top of IVA or TEZ/IVA. In this 8-week, active-controlled (IVA or TEZ/IVA), phase 3 trial (study 445-104) in people with CF age ≥ 12 years with either F/G or F/RF genotypes, ELX/TEZ/IVA treatment led to statistically significant and clinically meaningful improvements in lung function, CFTR function, and respiratory symptom that exceeded improvements with IVA or TEZ/IVA.23 These results support the potential for extended ELX/TEZ/IVA treatment to have greater clinical benefit on long-term CF disease trajectory in people with F/G or F/RF genotypes than IVA or TEZ/IVA and supported label extensions for people with CF and at least one F508del allele.

Here, we describe results from an open-label extension study designed to assess the long-term safety and efficacy of ELX/TEZ/IVA treatment in participants with F/G or F/RF genotypes who previously completed the parent study 445-104.

Methods

Study design, population, and objectives

Study VX18-445-110 (NCT04058366) was a 2-part, phase 3, open-label extension study to evaluate the long-term safety and efficacy of ELX/TEZ/IVA in adolescents and adults ≥ 12 years of age with F/G or F/RF genotypes. Participants who completed parent study VX18-445-104 (NCT04058353) could enroll. Part A of this study had a 96-week treatment period where all participants received ELX/TEZ/IVA at the same dose as was evaluated in the parent study (ELX 200 mg once daily [qd], TEZ 100 mg qd, and IVA 150 mg every 12 hours). Participants in certain countries who completed Part A had the opportunity to enroll in Part B for an additional 48 weeks of ELX/TEZ/IVA (Figure S1, see online supplementary material for a color version of this figure).

The study was designed by Vertex Pharmaceuticals Incorporated (Boston, MA, United States) in collaboration with the authors. Informed consent was provided by each participant or by a parent or legal guardian on behalf of each participant; assent was obtained in accordance with local requirements. Safety was monitored by an independent data safety monitoring committee. Data collection and analysis were performed by Vertex Pharmaceuticals in collaboration with the authors and the VX18-445-110 Study Group. Authors had full access to study data after database lock, critically reviewed the manuscript, and approved it for submission. Investigators vouch for the accuracy and completeness of the data generated at their respective sites, and the investigators and Vertex Pharmaceuticals Incorporated vouch for the fidelity of the study to the protocol. The clinical study protocol and informed consent forms were approved by independent ethics committees for each region or site, as required by local regulations.

Outcome measures

The primary endpoint of Part A and Part B was safety and tolerability as assessed by adverse events (AEs), clinical laboratory values, electrocardiograms, vital signs, and pulse oximetry. Secondary efficacy endpoints (Part A only) included absolute changes from parent study baseline at Week 96 in percent predicted FEV1 (ppFEV1), sweat chloride concentration, body mass index (BMI), BMI z-score, body weight, and Cystic Fibrosis Questionnaire-Revised (CFQ-R) respiratory domain score.

Statistical analysis

Analyses of safety and efficacy included all participants who received ≥ 1 dose of ELX/TEZ/IVA in this extension study. The safety period for Part A included the time from the first dose of ELX/TEZ/IVA in the extension study to 28 days after the date of the last dose or to the completion date of study participation in Part A, whichever occurred first. The safety period for Part B included the time from the first dose of ELX/TEZ/IVA in Part B of this extension study to 28 days after the date of the last dose or to the completion date of study participation in Part B, whichever occurred first. The efficacy period in Part A included the time from the first dose of ELX/TEZ/IVA in this extension study to the last efficacy assessment, which could have been collected up to the Week 96 visit or the earlier of Day 673 and the end of study participation, if the participant did not have the Week 96 Visit. Baseline for efficacy assessments was defined as the most recent non-missing measurement before the first dose of study drug in the treatment period of the parent study.

Descriptive analyses of safety were performed, and no statistical testing was conducted. Changes in ppFEV1, sweat chloride concentration, BMI, BMI z-score, body weight, and CFQ-R respiratory domain score were analyzed using a mixed-effects model for repeated measures (MMRM), with the absolute change from baseline in ppFEV1 as the dependent variable. The model included treatment group (as randomized in parent study), visit, and treatment by visit interaction as fixed effects, with continuous baseline ppFEV1 from parent study, continuous baseline sweat chloride concentration from parent study, and comparator group (IVA comparator vs TEZ/IVA comparator) as covariates.

Results

Study design and participant characteristics

This study was conducted at 92 sites in the United States, Canada, European Union, Australia, and the United Kingdom between 5 December 2019 and 16 December 2022. A total of 251 participants enrolled and received ≥ 1 dose of ELX/TEZ/IVA in Part A of this extension study with 217 (86.5%) completing the 96-week treatment period. Of those who did not complete the Part A treatment period, reasons for treatment discontinuation included: 14 (5.6%) discontinued due to AEs (one of which occurred prior to treatment with ELX/TEZ/IVA), 7 (2.8%) refused further dosing not due to an AE, 4 (1.6%) transitioned to commercial ELX/TEZ/IVA, 1 (0.4%) was non-compliant with study drug, 1 (0.4%) was non-compliant (other; unwilling to remain on stable CF regimen), 4 (1.6%) due to pregnancy (self or partner), and 2 (0.8%) other (Figure 1). There was one death in Part A due to a serious AE of surgical complications associated with removal of a large cecal colon cancer; investigators attributed the participant’s death to the postoperative complication from colon cancer and not related to ELX/TEZ/IVA. Eighty-four participants (33.5%) continued into Part B, with only 1 participant (1.2%) completing the additional 48-week treatment period. Most participants (81 [96.4%]) discontinued due to the availability of commercial ELX/TEZ/IVA, with 1 participant (1.2%) refusing further dosing not due to an AE and 1 participant (1.2%) discontinuing based on a physician decision (Figure 1). Safety

The mean exposure to ELX/TEZ/IVA in Part A was 89.3 (SD, 20.0) weeks. Overall, 241 participants (96.0%) had at least one AE, most being mild (32.3%) or moderate (55.0%) in severity and generally consistent with common manifestations of CF disease or illnesses seen in adolescents and adults (Table 2). The most common AEs (>20% incidence) were headache (28.3%), infective pulmonary exacerbation of CF (26.3%), cough (25.9%), and COVID-19 (21.1%). Thirty-eight participants (15.1%) had serious AEs. Serious AEs occurring in 2 or more participants included terms that coded to infective pulmonary exacerbation of CF (16 participants [6.4%]) and terms that coded to infective exacerbation of bronchiectasis (2 participants [0.8%]), hemoptysis (3 participants [1.2%]), pneumothorax (2 participants [0.8%]), and nephrolithiasis (2 participants [0.8%]) (Table S2). Thirteen participants (5.2%) had treatment-emergent AEs that led to study drug discontinuation. Adverse events leading to treatment discontinuation in 2 or more participants included AEs related to liver function tests (6 participants [2.4%]), anxiety (2 participants [0.8%]), and insomnia (2 participants [0.8%]) (Table S3). There was one participant who had a serious AE of surgical complications due to extensive small bowel infarction following hemicolectomy for colonic cancer likely related to underlying CF that led to death (discussed above). The exposure-adjusted rates of AEs and serious AEs (589.36 and 13.38 events per 100 participant years, respectively) were lower than in the parent study (1033.98 and 26.74 events per 100 participant years, respectively) (Table 2).

Based on prior experience with ELX/TEZ/IVA, data related to aminotransferases, rash, creatine kinase, and blood pressure were reviewed. Safety lab values of alanine or aspartate aminotransferase levels > 3x, >5x, and > 8x upper limit of normal (ULN) occurred in 13 (5.2%), 9 (3.6%), and 4 (1.6%) of participants, respectively (Table S4). One participant (0.4%) who previously received IVA in the parent study had alanine or aspartate aminotransferase levels > 3x ULN concurrent with newly occurred total bilirubin > 2x ULN and AEs of alanine aminotransferase increased and blood bilirubin increased on day 268 of the extension study which were considered moderate in severity and resolved after study drug discontinuation without treatment. Overall, there were 17 participants (6.8%) who had AEs related to elevated transaminases (Table S4), which for most were either mild or moderate in severity. Of the 6 participants who discontinued study drug due to AEs related to liver function tests (including the participant described above), 4 (1.6%) had AEs of alanine or aspartate aminotransferase increased (>5x ULN in all cases), 2 of whom also had AEs of increased blood bilirubin. The other 2 participants had AEs of blood bilirubin unconjugated increased (n = 1) and blood bilirubin increased (n = 1). Twenty-nine participants (11.6%) had rash events, the majority of which were mild or moderate in severity (Table S5). One participant (0.4%) had a serious AE of erythematous rash which resolved with treatment. There were no discontinuations due to rash events. Adverse events leading to discontinuation in participants with psychiatric events included: anxiety and insomnia (n = 1), anxiety (n = 1), affective disorder (n = 1), and insomnia (n = 1). The overall incidence of AEs of anxiety and insomnia in Part A were 11.2% and 6.0%, which are generally consistent with incidences reported in the 144-week extension study of ELX/TEZ/IVA in participants with F/F or F/MF genotypes,24 and are within the background rate of anxiety reported for adolescents and adults with CF.25 There were 26 participants (10.4%) who had AEs of blood creatine kinase increased, the majority of which were mild or moderate in severity and resolved without change in study drug dosing. The mean changes in systolic and diastolic blood pressure from parent study baseline at Week 96 were 2.6 mm Hg (SD, 13.2; mean range over treatment period, 0.0 mm Hg to 5.8 mm Hg) and 2.5 mm Hg (SD, 9.4; mean range over treatment period, 0.6 mm Hg to 3.7 mm Hg), respectively (Table S6). Twelve participants (4.8%) had AEs associated with hypertension, none of which led to treatment discontinuation or required changes in ELX/TEZ/IVA dosing; 8 participants required medication for elevated blood pressure. There were no notable findings in other clinical or laboratory assessments.

Efficacy

For participants who previously received control treatment in parent study 445-104 (IVA or TEZ/IVA), improvements in ppFEV1, sweat chloride concentration, BMI, and CFQ-R respiratory domain score were seen after initiation of ELX/TEZ/IVA in this extension study that were maintained through follow-up. The least squares mean change from parent study baseline at Week 96 in ppFEV1 was 4.1 percentage points (95% CI, 2.5-5.7), in sweat chloride concentration was −23.0 mmol/L (95% CI, −25.8, −20.1), in BMI was 1.15 kg/m2 (95% CI, 0.84-1.45), and in CFQ-R respiratory domain score was 7.2 points (95% CI, 4.1-10.4) (Figure 2, Table 3). Consistent with BMI results, increases from parent study baseline at Week 96 were also seen in BMI z-score (least squares mean change 0.11 [95% CI, −0.17, 0.40]) and weight (3.6 kg [95% CI, 2.7-4.6]) (Table 3; Figures S2 and S3, see online supplementary material for a color version of these figures).

For participants who previously received ELX/TEZ/IVA following a 4-week run-in period of either IVA or TEZ/IVA, depending on genotype, in parent study 445-104, improvements in ppFEV1, sweat chloride concentration, BMI, and CFQ-R respiratory domain score were maintained through follow-up. The least squares mean change from parent study baseline at Week 96 in ppFEV1 was 3.7 percentage points (95% CI, 2.2-5.2), in sweat chloride concentration was −22.6 mmol/L (95% CI, −25.4, −19.9), in BMI was 0.83 kg/m2 (95% CI, 0.54-1.11), and in CFQ-R respiratory domain score was 8.1 points (95% CI, 5.1-11.1) (Figure 2, Table 3). Consistent with BMI results, increases from parent study baseline at Week 96 were also seen in BMI z-score (least squares mean change 0.40 [95% CI, 0.17-0.62]) and weight (2.9 kg [95% CI, 2.0-3.8]) (Table 3; Figures S2 and S3, see online supplementary material for a color version of these figures).

We conducted a post-hoc analysis to assess responses to ELX/TEZ/IVA treatment by genotype group. Participants with F/G genotypes who received IVA active control in the parent study had changes from parent study baseline at Week 96 in ppFEV1 of 6.1 percentage points (95% CI, 3.3-8.8), sweat chloride concentration of −16.1 mmol/L (95% CI, −21.5, −10.6), and CFQ-R respiratory domain score of 11.6 points (95% CI, 6.1-17.1), while participants who received ELX/TEZ/IVA in parent study had changes of 5.1 percentage points (95% CI, 2.6-7.6), −19.9 mmol/L (95% CI, −25.1, −14.7), and 9.1 points (95% CI, 4.0-14.3) (Table S8). Participants with F/RF genotypes who received TEZ/IVA active control in the parent study had changes from parent study baseline at Week 96 in ppFEV1 of 3.1 percentage points (95% CI, 1.1-5.1), sweat chloride concentration of −26.9 mmol/L (95% CI, −30.2, −23.6), and CFQ-R respiratory domain score of 4.8 points (95% CI, 0.9-8.7), while participants who received ELX/TEZ/IVA in parent study had changes of 2.7 percentage points (95% CI, 0.8-4.6), −24.3 mmol/L (95% CI, −27.5, −21.1), and 7.8 points (95% CI, 4.1-11.5) (Table S8). A post-hoc analysis was also conducted to look at the percentage of participants who achieved sweat chloride concentrations < 60 mmol/L and < 30 mmol/L at Week 96. Overall, 91.1% of participants who previously received active control in the parent study and 91.7% who previously received ELX/TEZ/IVA in the parent study had sweat chloride concentrations < 60 mmol/L at Week 96 of this extension study, with 46.7% and 42.7%, respectively, achieving sweat chloride concentrations < 30 mmol/L (Table S9).

Discussion

Here, we present results from a 2-part, open-label extension study of ELX/TEZ/IVA in adolescents and adults with F/G or F/RF genotypes who previously completed the 8-week, active-controlled parent study 445-104. Overall, ELX/TEZ/IVA remained generally safe and well-tolerated through 96 weeks of treatment in Part A of this study. Although the majority of participants discontinued Part B due to the availability of commercial ELX/TEZ/IVA, results suggest continued safety and tolerability, with no new safety findings through an additional 48 weeks of ELX/TEZ/IVA treatment. The improvements in lung function, CFTR function, respiratory symptoms, and nutritional status observed in participants who received ELX/TEZ/IVA in the parent study were sustained for an additional 96 weeks in Part A. For participants who received the control treatment (IVA or TEZ/IVA) in the parent study, initiation of ELX/TEZ/IVA in this extension study led to similar efficacy improvements that were also maintained through the 96-week follow-up period.

Overall, 217 out of the 251 participants who enrolled in Part A of this study (86.5%) completed the treatment period. Adverse events were generally consistent with common manifestations of CF disease or illnesses seen in adolescents and adults, and the exposure-adjusted rate of AEs was lower than in the parent study 445-104, consistent with safety results from other long-term extension studies of ELX/TEZ/IVA in participants with F/F or F/MF genotypes.22,24 Amongst the 13 participants who had AEs during the treatment period that led to study drug discontinuation, 6 had increased liver function tests, 4 had psychiatric events, and 3 had other events (acne, shoulder pain, and articular pain). Data related to liver events in this extension study, including the incidence and severity, were generally consistent with results from the parent study (study 445-104) as well as from the long-term extension study in participants with F/F or F/MF genotypes taking ELX/TEZ/IVA.22,24 Two participants had AEs of anxiety that led to study drug discontinuation; the overall incidence of AEs of anxiety in this study was 11.2%, which is within the background rate reported in prior studies for adolescents and adults with CF25 and is lower than the 28% incidence of anxiety in people with CF ≥ 12 years of age reported by the U.S. CF Foundation in their 2024 annual report.26 These results align with multiple other studies which report that people with CF are more likely to experience anxiety and depression than the general population.27,28 Rash events and changes in systolic and diastolic blood pressures were also consistent with previous extension studies in adolescents and adults taking ELX/TEZ/IVA.22 Taken together, these results support the safety and tolerability of ELX/TEZ/IVA in adolescents and adults with CF and either F/G or F/RF genotypes, with no new safety findings.

The parent study 445-104 was an active-controlled clinical trial wherein, following a 4-week run-in period of either IVA or TEZ/IVA depending on genotype, participants were randomized to receive either an active control (IVA or TEZ/IVA) or ELX/TEZ/IVA for an 8-week treatment period.23 In this extension study, participants who previously received ELX/TEZ/IVA in the parent study sustained improvements in lung function and respiratory symptoms throughout the 96-week treatment period in Part A, with increases from parent study baseline following the run-in period of 3.7 percentage points in ppFEV1 and 8.1 points in CFQ-R respiratory domain score, exceeding the established minimal clinically important difference of 4.0 points.29 Participants who previously received either IVA or TEZ/IVA active control in the parent study had similar improvements to those who received ELX/TEZ/IVA in ppFEV1 (4.1 percentage points) and CFQ-R respiratory domain score (7.2 points) at Week 96 of this extension study. Consistent with the parent study, participants with F/RF genotypes had smaller changes in lung function at Week 96 compared to those with F/G genotypes, likely reflecting the further CF disease progression in these participants who were older than participants with F/G genotypes at parent study baseline.15,23 Overall, these results support the finding that ELX/TEZ/IVA treatment enhances the function of CFTR protein, leading to durable and clinically meaningful improvements in lung function and respiratory symptoms in people with F/G and F/RF genotypes that exceed the benefits obtained with either IVA or TEZ/IVA alone.

Sweat chloride concentration provides an indication of systemic CFTR function.2 Consistent with the sustained improvements in lung function and respiratory symptoms following initiation of ELX/TEZ/IVA, participants who received ELX/TEZ/IVA in the parent study maintained decreases in sweat chloride concentrations through the 96-week follow-up period in Part A. After initiating ELX/TEZ/IVA, participants who previously received active control had similar decreases in sweat chloride concentration which were also maintained through the follow-up period. At Week 96, greater than 90% of participants had sweat chloride concentrations < 60 mmol/L, the diagnostic threshold for CF, and greater than 40% had concentrations < 30 mmol/L, values seen in healthy, asymptomatic individuals who carry a single CFTR variant. These results confirm initiation of ELX/TEZ/IVA leads to rapid and robust declines in sweat chloride concentration indicative of durable improvements in CFTR function. This restoration of CFTR function may represent improvements to the function of the F508del allele and the additional gating or residual function CFTR allele.

Given that people with CF often have impaired growth and nutritional status, it was notable that participants taking ELX/TEZ/IVA in the parent study 445-104 had rapid increases in BMI during the initial 8-week treatment period that were greater than the active control group. In this extension study, increases in BMI were observed through week 48 for participants given ELX/TEZ/IVA in the parent study and for those who transitioned to ELX/TEZ/IVA from active control, after which BMI was generally maintained throughout the remainder of the treatment period and was in the normal range at Week 96. These results are consistent with changes in nutritional status reported for adolescents and adults with F/F or F/MF genotypes treated with ELX/TEZ/IVA24 as well as in real-world observational studies.30

There are limitations to the current study. The lack of a comparator group due to the open-label design of this extension study does limit the ability to interpret the safety and efficacy data. It should also be noted that parts of this study took place during the COVID-19 pandemic, where social distancing guidelines and mask use were found to be associated with a reduction in the incidence of pulmonary exacerbations in some CF populations.31

Conclusions

In this extension study, ELX/TEZ/IVA remained generally safe and well-tolerated with no new safety findings. Previously reported improvements in lung function, CFTR function, respiratory symptoms, and nutritional status were maintained through an additional 96 weeks of follow-up. These results confirm the positive safety profile and durable efficacy of ELX/TEZ/IVA in adolescents and adults with F/G and F/RF genotypes.

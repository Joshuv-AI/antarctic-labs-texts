Algebraic K \displaystyle K -theory of stably compact spaces
Georg Lehner

Abstract
. We compute the value of finitary localizing invariants, including algebraic K \displaystyle K -theory, on categories of sheaves over stably locally compact spaces X \displaystyle X . Our formula simultaneously generalizes the cases of locally compact Hausdorff and coherent (spectral) spaces and recovers several smaller K \displaystyle K -theory calculations as special instances.

Introduction

Recent advances in the
\displaystyle K

[

It also seems possible to finally bridge the gap between the (algebraic) Farrell-Jones conjecture and its operator-theoretic counterpart, the Baum-Connes conjecture. The central insight is that localizing invariants, such as algebraic
\displaystyle K
-theory, can be extended from small stable
\displaystyle\infty
-categories to the class of
dualizable
stable
\displaystyle\infty
-categories. This includes
\displaystyle\infty
-categories of spectral sheaves
\displaystyle\mathrm{Sh}(X,\mathrm{Sp})
on a locally compact Hausdorff space
\displaystyle X
, which play an analogous role to
\displaystyle C^{*}
-algebras of the form
\displaystyle C_{0}(X,\mathbb{C})
in the context of operator theory. Whilst the general picture is still very much work in progress, an important computation that motivated this project is the following.

Theorem 1.1

(
[
Efi25
]
Theorem 6.11, see also
[
KNP24
]
Theorem 3.6.1)
.

Let
\displaystyle X
be a locally compact Hausdorff space. Then

K^{\mathrm{cont}}(\mathrm{Sh}(X,\mathrm{Sp}))\simeq H_{cs}(X;K(\mathbb{S}))

where
\displaystyle\mathrm{Sp}
is the
\displaystyle\infty
-category of spectra and the right-hand side refers to compactly supported sheaf cohomology of
\displaystyle X
with respect to the local system given by the
\displaystyle K
-theory of the sphere spectrum.

There are some extensions that are possible for Theorem
1.1
. For one, the theorem holds not just for
\displaystyle K
-theory, but for arbitrary finitary localizing invariants, as long as the target of the localizing invariant is a dualizable
\displaystyle\infty
-category. It also generalizes to non-constant coefficients, in the sense that an analogous formula holds when one considers a presheaf
\displaystyle\underline{\mathcal{C}}
on
\displaystyle X
valued in dualizable categories.

We propose a generalization of Theorem
1.1
by enlarging the class of spaces
\displaystyle X
considerably. As was already observed by Efimov in
[
Efi25
, Remark 6.2]
, Hausdorffness of a space
\displaystyle X
is not essential to guarantee that
\displaystyle\mathrm{Sh}(X,\mathrm{Sp})
is dualizable, rather a weaker notion suffices. Recall the notion of the way-below relation between open subsets of a topological space
\displaystyle X
: Two open sets
\displaystyle U,V
satisfy the relation
\displaystyle U\ll V
iff for all directed sets of opens
\displaystyle W_{i},i\in I
it holds that

\bigcup_{i\in I}W_{i}\supset V\text{ implies }\exists i\in I:W_{i}\supset U.

(In other words, the inclusion
\displaystyle U\subset V
is a compact morphism in the poset
\displaystyle\mathcal{O}(X)
of open sets of
\displaystyle X
.)

Definition 1.2
.

A topological space
\displaystyle X
is called
stably locally compact
if it is sober, and the way-below relation
\displaystyle\ll
satisfies the two conditions:

•

For all open
\displaystyle U
, we have
\displaystyle U=\bigcup_{V\ll U}V
.

•

The way-below relation is stable under intersection, in the sense that
\displaystyle U\ll V_{1}
and
\displaystyle U\ll V_{2}
implies
\displaystyle U\ll V_{1}\cap V_{2}
.

A continuous map
\displaystyle f:X\rightarrow Y
will be called
perfect
if
\displaystyle f^{-1}
preserves the way-below relation. A partial map
\displaystyle f:X\rightarrow Y
will be called
partial perfect
, if its domain of definition is open, and
\displaystyle f
restricted to its domain is a perfect map. We define the category
\displaystyle\mathrm{SLC}_{p}
of stably locally compact spaces and partial perfect maps.

Any locally compact Hausdorff space
\displaystyle X
is in particular an example of a stably locally compact space. Moreover, the inclusion of the full subcategory of locally compact Hausdorff spaces into stably locally compact spaces has a right adjoint

\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathrm{LCH}_{p}}}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathrm{SLC}_{p}}}
\displaystyle\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{patch}}
\displaystyle\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\dashv}

which equips a stably locally compact space
\displaystyle X
with its
patch topology
. (See Section
3.5
)

If
\displaystyle X
is stably locally compact, the category of sheaves
\displaystyle\mathrm{Sh}(X,\mathrm{Sp})
is dualizable (Corollary
3.52
), and hence it makes sense to apply localizing invariants, such as
\displaystyle K
-theory. We have obtained the following generalization of Theorem
1.1

Theorem 1.3

(See Theorem

)
.

Let
\displaystyle X
be stably locally compact, let
\displaystyle\mathcal{C}
be a dualizable stable
\displaystyle\infty
-category and let
\displaystyle F
be a finitary localizing invariant
\displaystyle\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{perf}}\rightarrow\mathcal{E}
with values in a dualizable stable
\displaystyle\infty

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}

F^{\mathrm{cont}}(\mathrm{Sh}(X,\mathcal{C}))\simeq H_{cs}(X^{\mathrm{patch}};F^{\mathrm{cont}}(\mathcal{C})).

Here, the right-hand side refers to the
compactly supported cohomology
of
\displaystyle X^{\mathrm{patch}}
. We also obtain the following perhaps surprising duality statement. Given
\displaystyle X
and
\displaystyle\mathcal{C}
as above, define the
\displaystyle\infty

\displaystyle X

\mathrm{Cosh}(X,\mathcal{C})=\mathrm{Fun}^{L}(\mathrm{Sh}(X,\mathrm{Sp}),\mathcal{C}).

Corollary 1.4

(See Corollary

)

Let
\displaystyle X
be stably locally compact, let
\displaystyle\mathcal{C}
be a dualizable stable
\displaystyle\infty
-category and let
\displaystyle F

\displaystyle\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{perf}}\rightarrow\mathcal{E}

\displaystyle\infty

\displaystyle X

1.1.
Motivation

We should say that the purpose of this article is two-fold. For one, a simple motivation for Theorem
1.3
is that it is widely applicable to many different examples. Let us elaborate on some of them.

•

In the case of
\displaystyle X
being Hausdorff, we have
\displaystyle X=X^{\mathrm{patch}}
and recover the known fact that

K^{\mathrm{cont}}(\mathrm{Sh}(X,\mathcal{C}))\simeq H_{cs}(X;K^{\mathrm{cont}}(\mathcal{C}))

given by Theorem
1.1
.

•

The special case of
\displaystyle X=\mathbf{S}
being the Sierpinski space, i.e. the topological space given by a two element set
\displaystyle\mathbf{2}
with a topology with a unique open point, can be enlightening. We have
\displaystyle\mathbf{S}^{\mathrm{patch}}=\mathbf{2}^{\mathrm{disc}}
is the two point space equipped with the discrete topology. There is a natural equivalence
\displaystyle\mathrm{Sh}(\mathbf{S},\mathcal{C})\simeq\mathcal{C}^{\Delta^{1}}
and the continuous map
\displaystyle p:\mathbf{2}^{\mathrm{disc}}\rightarrow\mathbf{S}
induces the functor

\begin{array}[]{rcc}p_{*}:\mathcal{C}\times\mathcal{C}&\rightarrow&\mathcal{C}^{\Delta^{1}}\\
(c,d)&\mapsto&(c\times d\rightarrow c).\end{array}

The statement that
\displaystyle p_{*}
induces an equivalence on localizing invariants recovers the well-known
additivity theorem
. We thus would like to think of Theorem
1.3
as an analytic generalization of the additivity theorem.

•

The case
\displaystyle X=\overrightarrow{[0,\infty)}
, with topology given by opens of the form
\displaystyle[0,a)
for
\displaystyle 0\leq a\leq\infty
, is also quite important. There is an equivalence of
\displaystyle\infty
-categories

\mathrm{Sh}(\overrightarrow{[0,\infty)},\mathcal{C})\simeq\mathrm{Sh}(\mathbb{R},\mathcal{C})_{\geq 0},

using the terminology of Efimov
[
Efi25
, p. 76]

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}=\mathrm{Sp}
, the dualizable
\displaystyle\infty
-category
\displaystyle\mathrm{Sh}(\mathbb{R},\mathrm{Sp})_{\geq 0}
corepresents the functor that sends a dualizable
\displaystyle\infty
-category
\displaystyle\mathcal{C}
to
\displaystyle\mathbb{Q}
-indexed diagrams in
\displaystyle\mathcal{C}
with compact transition morphisms whenever
\displaystyle a<b\in\mathbb{Q}
. Efimov shows that
\displaystyle\mathrm{Sh}(\mathbb{R},\mathrm{Sp})_{\geq 0}
is an
\displaystyle\omega_{1}
-compact generator of
\displaystyle\mathrm{Pr}^{L}_{\mathrm{dual}}
,
[
Efi25
, Theorem D.1]
, and computes that
\displaystyle F^{\mathrm{cont}}(\mathrm{Sh}(\mathbb{R},\mathcal{C})_{\geq 0})=0
for any finitary localizing invariant
\displaystyle F
,
[
Efi25
, Proposition 4.21]
. We recover this result as a special case, as
\displaystyle\overrightarrow{[0,\infty)}^{\mathrm{patch}}=[0,\infty)
with the ordinary metric topology, which has a contractible one-point compactification.

•

In the case of a
coherent space

\displaystyle X
, also sometimes called

\displaystyle X

The secondary purpose of this article is to illustrate that the apparent usefulness of the class of stably locally compact spaces given by the above list of examples is by no means an accident. One can think of it as a partial answer to the following question.

What is the largest (convenient) class of spaces
\displaystyle X
such that
\displaystyle\mathrm{Sh}(X,\mathrm{Sp})
is dualizable?

Since it is the case that
\displaystyle\mathrm{Sh}(X,\mathrm{Sp})\simeq\mathrm{Sh}(X,\mathrm{An})\otimes\mathrm{Sp}
, where
\displaystyle\mathrm{An}
denotes the
\displaystyle\infty
-topos of anima/
\displaystyle\infty
-groupoids, one might ask instead for the slightly stronger condition that the
\displaystyle\infty
-topos
\displaystyle\mathrm{Sh}(X,\mathrm{An})
is a compactly assembled
\displaystyle\infty
-category. Let us mention the following facts.

•

Given any Grothendieck topology
\displaystyle\tau
on a poset
\displaystyle P
, which is closed under binary meets, there exists a natural frame
\displaystyle F=F(P,\tau)
such that
\displaystyle\mathrm{Sh}(P,\tau,\mathrm{An})\cong\mathrm{Sh}(F,\mathrm{An})
. Such Grothendieck topologies appear naturally in many contexts.

•

If for a given frame
\displaystyle F
the
\displaystyle\infty
-topos
\displaystyle\mathrm{Sh}(F,\mathrm{An})
is a compactly assembled
\displaystyle\infty
-category, then
\displaystyle F
is isomorphic to the set of opens of a sober and locally compact space
\displaystyle X
. (See Theorem
3.49
and Theorem
3.16
.)

•

However, it is not true that for every sober and locally compact space
\displaystyle X
the
\displaystyle\infty
-topos
\displaystyle\mathrm{Sh}(X,\mathrm{An})
is also compactly assembled. An example that already fails in the case of
\displaystyle 1
-topoi is given in
[
JJ82
, Example 5.5]
.

•

Under the additional assumption that
\displaystyle X
is in fact stably locally compact this issue disappears, and
\displaystyle\mathrm{Sh}(X,\mathrm{An})
is compactly assembled. Stability of
\displaystyle X
corresponds precisely to the additional property of
\displaystyle\mathrm{Sh}(X,\mathrm{An})
that compact morphisms are closed under products.

While it can still happen that for a topological space
\displaystyle X
(or frame, for that matter) the category of sheaves
\displaystyle\mathrm{Sh}(X,\mathcal{C})
is dualizable for a given dualizable
\displaystyle\mathcal{C}
, without
\displaystyle X
itself being stably locally compact, the category of stably locally compact spaces is thus in a sense an optimal candidate for a category of spaces for which we get dualizable categories of sheaves.

On a deeper level, there are many close parallels between the theory of stably locally compact spaces and that of dualizable categories. Efimov originally observed shadows of this picture as several analogies between dualizable categories and compact Hausdorff spaces, see

1.2.
The proof strategy

The proof of Theorem
1.3
rests on a meta theorem which is a generalization of an argument due to Clausen in the case of compact Hausdorff spaces, see
[
KNP24
, Theorem 3.6.11]
.

Theorem 1.9

(See Theorem
5.2
)
.

Let
\displaystyle\mathcal{D}
be a compactly assembled presentable
\displaystyle\infty
-category, and let

F:\mathrm{SC}^{\mathrm{op}}\rightarrow\mathcal{D}

be a contravariant functor on the category of stably compact spaces such that:

(1)

\displaystyle F(\emptyset)=1.

(2)

Perfect descent:
Whenever
\displaystyle K,L\subset X
are two perfect embeddings, then

\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{F(K\cup L)}}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{F(K)}}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{F(L)}}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{F(K\cap L)}}
\displaystyle\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\lrcorner}

is a pullback.

(3)

Cofiltered descent:
Whenever
\displaystyle X_{i},i\in I,
is a cofiltered system in
\displaystyle\mathrm{SC},
then

F(\lim_{i\in I}X_{i})\simeq\mathrm{colim}_{i\in I}F(X_{i}).

Then there is a natural equivalence of functors

F\simeq\Gamma((-)^{\mathrm{patch}};F(\mathrm{pt})).

The proof works formally almost the same as the one given by Clausen, along with one crucial observation: The set of perfect embeddings
\displaystyle K\subset X
is simply isomorphic to the set of closed subsets
\displaystyle C\subset X^{\mathrm{patch}}
. Hence for each given stably compact space
\displaystyle X
, the restriction of
\displaystyle F
to the set of perfect embeddings of
\displaystyle X
produces a
\displaystyle K
-sheaf on
\displaystyle X^{\mathrm{patch}}
. The corresponding sheaf
\displaystyle F^{X}
under the equivalence of
\displaystyle K
-sheaves with sheaves (Theorem
3.61
) satisfies that its compactly supported sections are computed as
\displaystyle F^{X}(X)=F(X)
. Now one needs to show that this sheaf is locally constant. We verify this in detail in Section
5
.

It is not too hard to verify that
\displaystyle K
-theory, or any finitary localizing invariant for that matter, satisfies (1) and (3). (See Corollary
2.34
.) However, showing perfect descent for finitary localizing invariants needs careful analysis. We reduce it to two different descent conditions:
Closed
and
Saturated compact
descent, and then use an inductive argument to extend it to all perfect embeddings in Section
4
. This rests on a concrete description of the closed sets of the patch topology as generated under intersections by pairwise unions of closed and saturated compact sets, see Section
3.5
.

Remark 1.10
.

As the reader will observe, the proof of Theorem
1.3
needs little in the sense of actual
\displaystyle K
-theory computations. Most of the difficulty relies in understanding the correct “point-set topological” features of stably locally compact spaces and their
\displaystyle\infty
-categories of sheaves.

2.1.
Compactly assembled
\displaystyle\infty
-categories

The notion of a compactly assembled
\displaystyle\infty
-category is central to this article. We note that if specialized to 1-category theory, a compactly assembled category corresponds to what is also known as a
continuous
category.

Definition 2.3

(See also
[
Lur18
]
Section 21.1.2,
[
Efi25
]
Definition 1.9 and 1.10)
.

Let
\displaystyle\mathcal{C}
be an accessible
\displaystyle\infty
-category. We call
\displaystyle\mathcal{C}

compactly assembled
if the canonical inclusion
\displaystyle y:\mathcal{C}\rightarrow\mathrm{Ind}(\mathcal{C})
admits two further left adjoints
\displaystyle\hat{y}\dashv k\dashv y
,

\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{C}}}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathrm{Ind}(\mathcal{C}).}}
\displaystyle\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces y}
\displaystyle\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\hat{y}}
\displaystyle\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces k}
\displaystyle\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\dashv}
\displaystyle\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\dashv}

A functor
\displaystyle F:\mathcal{C}\rightarrow\mathcal{D}
will be called
strongly continuous
if the commuting square

\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{C}}}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathrm{Ind}(\mathcal{C})}}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{D}}}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathrm{Ind}(\mathcal{D})}}
\displaystyle\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces y}
\displaystyle\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F}
\displaystyle\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Ind}(F)}
\displaystyle\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces y}

is twice left-adjointable, i.e. the resulting natural transformations
\displaystyle k\mathrm{Ind}(F)\rightarrow Fk
and
\displaystyle\hat{y}F\rightarrow\mathrm{Ind}(F)\hat{y}
are equivalences.

The resulting subcategory of
\displaystyle\widehat{\mathrm{Cat}_{\infty}}
of large
\displaystyle\infty
-categories given by compactly assembled
\displaystyle\infty
-categories and strongly continuous functors will be denoted by
\displaystyle\mathrm{Cat}_{\mathrm{ca}}
. Further, we denote by
\displaystyle\mathrm{Pr}^{L}_{\mathrm{ca}}
the
\displaystyle\infty
-category of presentable and compactly assembled
\displaystyle\infty
-categories, and strongly continuous left adjoint functors between them, as a (non-full) subcategory of
\displaystyle\widehat{\mathrm{Cat}_{\infty}}
.

Note that
\displaystyle y:\mathcal{C}\rightarrow\mathrm{Ind}(\mathcal{C})
is fully faithful, and therefore there is always a natural transformation

\hat{y}\rightarrow y.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
\displaystyle\mathcal{C}
be a compactly assembled
\displaystyle\infty
-category. A map
\displaystyle f:c\rightarrow d
in
\displaystyle\mathcal{C}
is called

if
\displaystyle y(f):y(c)\rightarrow y(d)
factors through the natural map
\displaystyle\hat{y}(d)\rightarrow y(d)
.

Remark 2.5
.

Left adjointability of the square in Definition
2.3
for a functor
\displaystyle F:\mathcal{C}\rightarrow\mathcal{D}
simply expresses that
\displaystyle F
preserves filtered colimits. If
\displaystyle F
itself has a further right adjoint
\displaystyle F^{R}
, then the statement that
\displaystyle F
is strongly continuous is equivalent to
\displaystyle F^{R}
preserving filtered colimits.

Example 2.6
.

If
\displaystyle\mathcal{C}
is an
\displaystyle\infty
-category with filtered colimits, denote by
\displaystyle\mathcal{C}^{\omega}
the full subcategory of

, i.e. those
\displaystyle c\in\mathcal{C}
such that

preserves filtered colimits. Then
\displaystyle\mathcal{C}
is called

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}\simeq\mathrm{Ind}(\mathcal{C}^{\omega})

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}

\displaystyle\hat{y}
obtained by applying
\displaystyle\mathrm{Ind}

(
[
Lur18
]
, Section 21.1.2)

An
\displaystyle\infty
-category
\displaystyle\mathcal{C}
admitting filtered colimits is compactly assembled iff it is a retract via filtered colimit preserving functors of a compactly generated
\displaystyle\infty

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}

\displaystyle\infty

•

\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{C}}}

Proposition 2.29

(Second Isomorphism Theorem for Locales)
.

Let
\displaystyle L
be a locale,
\displaystyle S\hookrightarrow L
a sublocale and
\displaystyle C\hookrightarrow L
a closed sublocale.
Then the inclusions
\displaystyle S\wedge C\hookrightarrow S
and
\displaystyle C\hookrightarrow S\vee C
are closed, and the inclusion

S\hookrightarrow S\vee C

induces a homeomorphism on their respective open complements.

Proof.

The inclusions
\displaystyle S\wedge C\hookrightarrow S
and
\displaystyle C\hookrightarrow S\vee C
are obtained from pullback along the closed inclusion
\displaystyle C\hookrightarrow L
, hence are themselves closed. Now let
\displaystyle U
denote the open complement of
\displaystyle C
, and denote the nucleus corresponding to
\displaystyle S
by
\displaystyle N
. The nucleus corresponding to
\displaystyle S\vee C
is then given by
\displaystyle N\wedge(-\vee U)
. We can verify that the left adjoint to the inclusion

S\hookrightarrow S\vee C

is simply given by
\displaystyle N
, as for
\displaystyle V
open we have

N(N(V)\wedge(V\vee U))=N(V)\wedge N(V\vee U)=N(V),

as
\displaystyle N(V\vee U)\geq N(V)
. This means the induced adjunction on the open complements is given as

\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{O}(S)_{/N(U)}}}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{O}(S\vee C)_{/U}}}
\displaystyle\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces-\wedge U}
\displaystyle\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces N}
\displaystyle\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\dashv}

We simply need to verify that these two adjoints are in fact inverse to each other. To see this, we verify the following:

•

Let
\displaystyle V\in\mathcal{O}(S)_{/N(U)}
, i.e.
\displaystyle V=N(V)
and
\displaystyle V\leq N(U)
. Then

N(V\wedge U)=N(V)\wedge N(U)=N(V)=V

since
\displaystyle V\leq N(U)
implies
\displaystyle N(V)\leq N(U)
.

•

Let
\displaystyle W\in\mathcal{O}(S\vee C)_{/U}
, i.e.
\displaystyle W=N(W)\wedge(W\vee U)
, and
\displaystyle W\leq U
. Then

W=N(W)\wedge(W\vee U)=(N(W)\wedge W)\vee(N(W)\wedge U)=W\vee(N(W)\wedge U)

hence
\displaystyle N(W)\wedge U\leq W
. But
\displaystyle W\leq U
and
\displaystyle W\leq N(W)
also imply
\displaystyle W\leq N(W)\wedge U
, hence
\displaystyle W=N(W)\wedge U
.

∎

3.

In the following section we will discuss stably locally compact spaces, as well as sheaves on them. It will be convenient to introduce the more general notion of a stably locally compact topos first.

Definition 3.1

\displaystyle\mathcal{X}

3.4.
de Groot and Verdier duality for stably compact spaces

There is a very useful duality for stably compact spaces, that can be understood as exchanging the roles of openness with that of (co)-compactness. Note that the poset
\displaystyle Q(X)^{\mathrm{op}}\cong\mathrm{OFilt}(\mathcal{O}(X))
of saturated compact sets, or equivalently Scott open filters, always has directed colimits (computed just as unions of their respective filters), and finite intersections. In case
\displaystyle X
is stably compact however, more holds: It becomes a frame itself.

Theorem 3.55

(de Groot duality, see
[
Gou13
]
Theorem 9.1.38)

Let
\displaystyle X
be a stably compact space. Then the set of complements of saturated compact subsets gives a topology on
\displaystyle X
. Denote the resulting topological space by
\displaystyle X^{\vee}
. The space
\displaystyle X^{\vee}
is again stably compact, and it holds that
\displaystyle(X^{\vee})^{\vee}=X
. Moreover, a perfect map
\displaystyle f:X\rightarrow Y
is again a perfect map
\displaystyle f:X^{\vee}\rightarrow Y^{\vee}

In other words, we obtain an involution
\displaystyle(-)^{\vee}:\mathrm{SC}\rightarrow\mathrm{SC}

\displaystyle(-)^{\vee}
is covariant in
\displaystyle 1

\displaystyle 2
-morphisms.

In the case of

\displaystyle(I_{\leq})^{\vee}=I_{\geq}

\displaystyle X^{\vee}

Proof.

Tracing through definitions, we see that this amounts to the question whether for a given sheaf

Remark 3.69

Remark 3.70
.

A more top down perspective on Verdier duality is to observe that it is about the statement that the

3.5.
The patch topology

There is a universal way of adding open sets to the topology of a stably locally compact space
\displaystyle X
to make it Hausdorff, while retaining local compactness. This is called the
patch topology
of
\displaystyle X
. Let us give a short summary of how to work with this topology. The general reference will be the article by Escardó
[
Esc01
]
, however information can also be found in
[
Gie+03
]
and
[
Gou13
]
. Since Escardó’s work is done purely in the language of locales, whereas older literature uses topological spaces, we will need to discuss quickly how to translate between those pictures.

Definition 3.71
.

Let
\displaystyle L
be a locale. We call a nucleus
\displaystyle N
on
\displaystyle L

perfect
if it preserves directed suprema.

Lemma 3.72
.

Let
\displaystyle S\hookrightarrow X
be a sublocale of a stably locally compact space
\displaystyle X
. Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

\displaystyle S
is a perfect sublocale.

(2)

The induced nucleus
\displaystyle N=i_{*}i^{*}
on
\displaystyle X
is perfect.

(3)

\displaystyle S
is obtained by a subset
\displaystyle S^{\prime}
of
\displaystyle X
, such that the induced subspace topology is stably locally compact, and the embedding
\displaystyle S^{\prime}\hookrightarrow X
is a perfect map.

(4)

The associated quotient map
\displaystyle p:X\amalg X\twoheadrightarrow E
corresponding to the frame congruence of
\displaystyle S
is perfect.

In particular, the associated frame congruence
\displaystyle E
is itself stably locally compact.

Proof.

We first remark that if
\displaystyle S
is a perfect sublocale of a stably locally compact space
\displaystyle X
, it is itself stably locally compact by Proposition
3.22
.

The equivalence of (1) and (3) is now immediate from the equivalence of the category of stably locally compact spaces and stably locally compact frames given by Corollary
3.17
.

To see the equivalence of (1) and (2) is also straightforward. If
\displaystyle i_{*}
preserves filtered suprema, then so does
\displaystyle i_{*}i^{*}
, as
\displaystyle i^{*}
is left adjoint. Conversely, if the nucleus
\displaystyle N
of
\displaystyle X
is given, the associated sublocale
\displaystyle S
is obtained as the frame of opens
\displaystyle\{U\in\mathcal{O}(X)~|~N(U)=U\}\subset\mathcal{O}(X)
, and
\displaystyle i_{*}
is simply given by the inclusion. We only need to show that
\displaystyle N
preserving directed suprema implies that this subset is closed under directed suprema. Let
\displaystyle U_{i},i\in I,
be a directed set of opens such that
\displaystyle U_{i}=N(U_{i})
, and
\displaystyle U=\bigvee_{i\in I}U_{i}
. Then

N(U)=N(\bigvee_{i\in I}U_{i})=\bigvee_{i\in I}N(U_{i})=\bigvee_{i\in I}U_{i}=U,

and hence we are done.

To see how (4) is equivalent to the rest, first observe that if (4) holds, then
\displaystyle S\hookrightarrow L
is obtained as an equalizer of stably locally compact spaces and perfect maps, hence itself perfect. Conversely, if
\displaystyle S
is a sublocale, the quotient map
\displaystyle p:X\amalg X\twoheadrightarrow E
is given by the formula

p_{*}(U,V)=(U\wedge N(V),V\wedge N(U)).

Hence if the nucleus
\displaystyle N
is perfect, this map preserves directed suprema, and hence
\displaystyle p
is perfect.
∎

We now come to the central notion of this section. The patch topology associated to a stably locally compact space. Denote by
\displaystyle\mathrm{LCH}_{p}
the full subcategory of
\displaystyle\mathrm{SLC}_{p}
spanned by locally compact Hausdorff spaces. We will simply write
\displaystyle\mathrm{LCH}
and
\displaystyle\mathrm{SLC}
for the corresponding wide subcategories given by only considering globally defined perfect maps. Furthermore, for a given stably locally compact space
\displaystyle X
, let us denote by
\displaystyle\mathcal{K}(X)
the set of subsets of
\displaystyle X
obtained via perfect embeddings. Note that
\displaystyle\mathcal{K}(X)
is isomorphic to the opposite of the poset of perfect nuclei on
\displaystyle X
.

Theorem 3.73

(The patch topology,
[
Esc01
]
, Theorem 5.8)
.

The inclusion of the full subcategory of locally compact Hausdorff spaces into stably locally compact spaces has a right adjoint,

\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathrm{LCH}}}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathrm{SLC}}}
\displaystyle\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{patch}}
\displaystyle\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\dashv}

given by sending a stably locally compact space
\displaystyle X
to the stably locally compact frame
\displaystyle\mathcal{K}(X)^{\mathrm{op}}
of perfect nuclei on
\displaystyle X
.

Remark 3.74
.

Since the space
\displaystyle\mathrm{pt}
is a finite space, every continuous map
\displaystyle\mathrm{pt}\rightarrow X
is automatically perfect. (See Example
3.30
.) It is also Hausdorff, hence we see that

X=\mathrm{Map}(\mathrm{pt},X)\cong\mathrm{Map}(\mathrm{pt},X^{\mathrm{patch}}),

and therefore the underlying set of points of
\displaystyle X^{\mathrm{patch}}
agrees with that of
\displaystyle X
. The new topology on
\displaystyle X
is referred to as the
patch topology
of
\displaystyle X
. In light of Lemma
3.72
it is also immediate what this topology is. The closed subsets of
\displaystyle X^{\mathrm{patch}}
are given exactly by the set
\displaystyle\mathcal{K}(X)
of images of perfect embeddings.

Remark 3.75
.

The patch topology on a stably compact space gives a compact Hausdorff space. This is seen by observing that the perfect map
\displaystyle X\rightarrow\mathrm{pt}
is sent to a perfect map
\displaystyle X^{\mathrm{patch}}\rightarrow\mathrm{pt}
.

Example 3.76
.

In the case of the directed interval
\displaystyle I_{\leq}
, it holds that
\displaystyle I_{\leq}^{\mathrm{patch}}=I
is the interval equipped with the ordinary metric topology on
\displaystyle I
, see
[

, Example 9.1.33]
. More generally, since
\displaystyle patch
preserves limits, if
\displaystyle S
is a set we also see that for the directed Hilbert cube
\displaystyle I_{\leq}^{S}
it holds that
\displaystyle(I_{\leq}^{S})^{\mathrm{patch}}=I^{S}
. Even more generally, any perfect subspace
\displaystyle K\hookrightarrow I_{\leq}^{S}
is obtained as an equalizer of perfect maps between stably compact spaces, and hence
\displaystyle K^{\mathrm{patch}}
agrees with the induced subspace topology when considering
\displaystyle K
as a subset of
\displaystyle I^{S}
.

Example 3.77
.

If
\displaystyle X
is a coherent space, corresponding to a bounded distributive lattice
\displaystyle D
, then
\displaystyle X^{\mathrm{patch}}
is a profinite set, corresponding to the Boolean algebra
\displaystyle\mathrm{Bool}(D)
given by Booleanization,
[
Esc01
, Section 3]
. The patch topology in this case is also referred to as the
constructible topology
on
\displaystyle X
, see
[
Leh25
, Section 3.2]
.

Example 3.78
.

Let
\displaystyle P
be a poset which is compactly assembled when considered as an
\displaystyle\infty
-category. This notion is referred to as a
domain
in
[

]
, and is also called

by Efimov
[
Efi25

\displaystyle P

While the description of the patch topology in terms of perfect embeddings is elegant, we will need a concrete way on how to build it. Let us highlight two special classes of perfect embeddings for a given stably locally compact space
\displaystyle X
.

•

Any
closed subset

\displaystyle C\subset X
is perfect.

•

Any saturated compact subset
\displaystyle S\subset X
is perfect.

Example 3.79
.

In the case of the directed interval
\displaystyle I_{\leq}
, saturated compact sets are of the form
\displaystyle[0,a]
for
\displaystyle a\in I
, whereas closed sets are of the form
\displaystyle[b,1]
, for
\displaystyle b\in I

Definition 3.80
.

Let
\displaystyle X
be a stably locally compact space. A subset
\displaystyle E\subset X
is called

if it is of the form
\displaystyle C\cup S
for
\displaystyle C\subset X
closed, and
\displaystyle S\subset X
saturated compact. Write
\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(X)\subset\mathcal{K}(X)
for the set of elementary compact subsets.

Elementary compact subspaces are closed under finite unions. Moreover, they generate the patch topology in the following sense.

Lemma 3.81

(
[
Esc01
]
, Lemma 5.4)
.

Let
\displaystyle X
be stably locally compact. The set of elementary compact subspaces
\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(X)
generates
\displaystyle\mathcal{K}(X)
under intersections.

In other words, when considering reverse inclusions, elementary compact sets form a basis of the frame
\displaystyle\mathcal{K}(X)^{\mathrm{op}}\cong\mathcal{O}(X^{\mathrm{patch}})
. From the perspective of sheaf theory, this means that—at least in principle—working with sheaves on the patch topology only requires an understanding of the poset
\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(X)
and its induced Grothendieck topology.

Remark 3.82
.

In the case that
\displaystyle X
is stably compact it is thus clear that the patch topology applied to the de Groot dual
\displaystyle X^{\vee}
also in results in
\displaystyle X^{\mathrm{patch}}
. This is since de Groot duality simply interchanges the roles of closed and saturated compact subsets, result in the same sets of elementary compact subspaces.

Example 3.83
.

For
\displaystyle X=I_{\leq}

As the last part of this section, observe that the Patch topology is even functorial in partial perfect maps. This is easiest to see on the level of nuclei. If

4.
Nisnevich-type descent for localizing invariants

In this section we are concerned with statements about descent for finitary localizing invariants applied to sheaves on stably (locally) compact spaces. This is somewhat inspired by the well-known notion of Nisnevich-Descent in the context of
\displaystyle K
-theory of schemes. Let us fix throughout the convention that
\displaystyle F:\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{perf}}\rightarrow\mathcal{E}
denotes a localizing invariant. We note that all statements made about
\displaystyle\mathrm{Sh}(X,\mathrm{Sp})
in this section generalize immediately when
\displaystyle\mathrm{Sp}
is replaced by an arbitrary dualizable
\displaystyle\infty
-category
\displaystyle\mathcal{C}
, in light of Lemma
2.13
, which states that
\displaystyle F^{\mathrm{cont}}(-\otimes\mathcal{C})
is again a localizing invariant. Before we begin, we want to record the following simple lemma about pullback squares in stable categories.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\displaystyle\mathcal{E}
be a stable
\displaystyle\infty
-category. Then a square

\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{A_{0}}}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{A_{1}}}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{B_{0}}}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{B_{1}}}
\displaystyle\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f_{A}}
\displaystyle\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f_{B}}

is a pullback square iff the induced map on horizontal fibers
\displaystyle\mathrm{fib}(f_{A})\rightarrow\mathrm{fib}(f_{B})
is an equivalence.

Proof.

Consider the diagram

\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathrm{fib}(f_{A})}}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{A_{0}}}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{B_{0}}}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{A_{1}}}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{B_{1}.}}
\displaystyle\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\lrcorner}
\displaystyle\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f_{A}}
\displaystyle\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f_{B}}

If the right hand square is a pullback, then so is the total square, and hence
\displaystyle\mathrm{fib}(f_{A})\rightarrow\mathrm{fib}(f_{B})
is an equivalence. Conversely, if the map
\displaystyle\mathrm{fib}(f_{A})\rightarrow\mathrm{fib}(f_{B})
is an equivalence, we see that the left and total squares are pullbacks. Using stability of
\displaystyle\mathcal{E}
, both squares are also pushouts, and hence we can use the pasting lemma for pushouts,
[
Lur12
, Lemma 4.4.2.1]
, to conclude that the right square is a pushout.
∎

Proposition 4.2

(Perfect Nisnevich Descent)
.

Let
\displaystyle f:Y\rightarrow X
be a perfect map of stably locally compact spaces, and
\displaystyle C\subset X
a closed subspace. Suppose that
\displaystyle f
induces a homeomorphism on the open complements of
\displaystyle f^{-1}(C)
and
\displaystyle C
. Then the square

\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{F^{\mathrm{cont}}(\mathrm{Sh}(X,\mathrm{Sp}))}}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{F^{\mathrm{cont}}(\mathrm{Sh}(C,\mathrm{Sp}))}}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{F^{\mathrm{cont}}(\mathrm{Sh}(Y,\mathrm{Sp}))}}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{F^{\mathrm{cont}}(\mathrm{Sh}(f^{-1}(C),\mathrm{Sp}))}}
\displaystyle\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f^{*}}
\displaystyle\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f^{*}}

is a pullback.

Proof.

Straightforward: Observe that the horizontal fibers of both rows are equivalent by Proposition
2.30
, then apply Lemma
4.1
.
∎

The main application is the simple case of a proper inclusion, which follows directly from the Second Isomorphism Theorem (Proposition
2.29
).

Corollary 4.3
.

Let
\displaystyle X
be stably locally compact,
\displaystyle K\subset X
be a perfect inclusion, and
\displaystyle C\subset X
be a closed inclusion. Then we have a pullback square

\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{F^{\mathrm{cont}}(\mathrm{Sh}(K\cup C,\mathrm{Sp}))}}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{F^{\mathrm{cont}}(\mathrm{Sh}(C,\mathrm{Sp}))}}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{F^{\mathrm{cont}}(\mathrm{Sh}(K,\mathrm{Sp}))}}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{F^{\mathrm{cont}}(\mathrm{Sh}(K\cap C,\mathrm{Sp})).}}
\displaystyle\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\lrcorner}

Our goal is to generalize the statement of Corollary
4.3
to the case where
\displaystyle C
is allowed to be an arbitrary perfect subspace
\displaystyle K^{\prime}
. In fact, if the formula of Theorem
1.3
is true, it must necessarily be the case that this form of perfect descent holds. We will do this in steps. The first will be the use of Verdier duality. Note that the fact that
\displaystyle(-)^{\vee}=\mathrm{Fun}^{L}(-,\mathrm{Sp})
is an autoequivalence on
\displaystyle\mathrm{Pr}^{L}_{\mathrm{dual}}
implies that it preserves Verdier sequences. This will be critical in the following observation.

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
\displaystyle X
be stably compact,
\displaystyle K\subset X
be a perfect inclusion, and
\displaystyle C\subset X
be a saturated compact inclusion. Then we have a pullback square

\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{F^{\mathrm{cont}}(\mathrm{Sh}(K\cup C,\mathrm{Sp}))}}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{F^{\mathrm{cont}}(\mathrm{Sh}(C,\mathrm{Sp}))}}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{F^{\mathrm{cont}}(\mathrm{Sh}(K,\mathrm{Sp}))}}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{F^{\mathrm{cont}}(\mathrm{Sh}(K\cap C,\mathrm{Sp})).}}
\displaystyle\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\lrcorner}

Proof.

The proposition follows analogously to Corollary
4.3
by applying Verdier duality (Theorem
3.59
). The square of sheaf categories in question is equivalent to

\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathrm{Cosh}(K^{\vee}\cup C^{\vee};\mathrm{Sp})}}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathrm{Cosh}(C^{\vee};\mathrm{Sp})}}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathrm{Cosh}(K^{\vee};\mathrm{Sp})}}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathrm{Cosh}(K^{\vee}\cap C^{\vee};\mathrm{Sp})}}

This square is obtained from the corresponding one involving sheaves of dual spaces by applying
\displaystyle(-)^{\vee}
. The saturated compact inclusion
\displaystyle C\rightarrow X
is sent to a closed inclusion
\displaystyle C^{\vee}\rightarrow X^{\vee}
under duality, and we can simply verify that horizontal fibers of the square of sheaf categories are equivalent by Proposition
2.29
.
∎

Remark 4.5
.

To be more concrete, the fiber of

\mathrm{Sh}(K\cup C,\mathrm{Sp})\rightarrow\mathrm{Sh}(C,\mathrm{Sp})

is given by
\displaystyle\mathrm{Cosh}(U,\mathrm{Sp})
, where
\displaystyle U
is the open subset of
\displaystyle X^{\vee}
corresponding to the Scott open filter of the compact saturated set
\displaystyle C
. Since
\displaystyle U
is
not
in general stably compact, but only stably locally compact, Verdier duality is a bit more subtle. Note that this is exactly the same issue as mentioned in Remark
3.65
.

Warning 4.6
.

It is not true that for perfect embeddings
\displaystyle K,L
of
\displaystyle X
the square

\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathrm{Sh}(K\cup L;\mathrm{Sp})}}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathrm{Sh}(L;\mathrm{Sp})}}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathrm{Sh}(K;\mathrm{Sp})}}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathrm{Sh}(K\cap L;\mathrm{Sp})}}

is necessarily a pullback square in
\displaystyle\mathrm{Pr}^{L}_{\mathrm{st}}
, unlike in the case when
\displaystyle X
is Hausdorff. As a counterexample, consider
\displaystyle K=[0,a]_{\leq},L=[b,1]_{\leq}
for
\displaystyle a<b
in
\displaystyle I_{\leq}
. Their intersection is empty, however
\displaystyle\mathrm{Sh}(([0,a]\cup[b,1])_{\leq};\mathrm{Sp})
does not split as a product.

To sum up, we have excision for localizing invariants for perfect embeddings if either of the embeddings is closed or saturated compact. We are left with showing that this implies proper descent. Before we do so, we will introduce a technical lemma to simplify induction of descent-type conditions.

4.1.
The sheaf condition on
\displaystyle D_{3}

We will need a technical lemma that allows us a reduction of a sheaf condition on three generators to a statement about pullbacks. The conceptual reason for this lemma is that the space
\displaystyle X=\mathbf{S}^{3}
given by the product of three copies of the Sierpinski space represents the functor on locales that chooses three opens. It is somewhat tautological that the sheaf condition for the case of three opens on an arbitrary locale can always be checked by pushforward to
\displaystyle\mathbf{S}^{3}
. The space
\displaystyle\mathbf{S}^{3}
is simply a combinatorial cube equipped with the Alexandroff topology, and its frame of opens is
\displaystyle D_{3}
, the free distributive lattice generated by three elements.

4.2.
Extending excisiveness

With Lemma
4.7
under our belt, we can extend from closed and saturated compact descent to descent for elementary compact sets.

Proposition 4.9

(Elementary compact descent)
.

Suppose
\displaystyle F:\mathrm{SC}^{\mathrm{op}}\rightarrow\mathcal{D}
is a functor, with
\displaystyle\mathcal{D}
an
\displaystyle\infty
-category with finite limits, such that for all stably compact
\displaystyle X
,
\displaystyle K
a compact subset of
\displaystyle X
,
\displaystyle C
a closed subset of
\displaystyle X
, and
\displaystyle S
a saturated compact subset of
\displaystyle X
the squares

\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{F(K\cup C)}}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{F(C)}}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{F(K\cup S)}}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{F(S)}}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{F(K)}}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{F(K\cap C)}}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{F(K)}}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{F(K\cap S).}}
\displaystyle\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\lrcorner}
\displaystyle\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\lrcorner}

are pullback squares. Then it also holds that for any compact subset
\displaystyle E\subset X
of the form
\displaystyle E=S\cup C
, with
\displaystyle S
being saturated compact and
\displaystyle C
being closed, that the square

\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{F(K\cup E)}}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{F(K)}}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{F(E)}}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{F(K\cap E)}}
\displaystyle\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\lrcorner}

is a pullback square.

Proof.

The proof is just an iterated application of Lemma
4.7
. Observe that by assumption we have the pullbacks

\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{F(S\cup C)}}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{F(C)}}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{F((K\cap S)\cup(K\cap C))}}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{F(K\cap C)}}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{F(S)}}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{F(S\cap C)}}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{F(K\cap S)}}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{F(K\cap S\cap C)}}
\displaystyle\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\lrcorner}
\displaystyle\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\lrcorner}

since
\displaystyle S\subset S\cup C
and
\displaystyle K\cap S\subset(K\cap S)\cup(K\cap C)=K\cap(S\cup C)
are saturated compact subsets. Applying Lemma
4.7
, this means that the square

\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{F(K\cup S\cup C)}}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{F(K)}}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{F(S\cup C)}}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{F(K\cap(S\cup C))}}

is a pullback square iff
\displaystyle F(K\cup S\cup C)
is the limit of the corresponding cube built from
\displaystyle F(K),F(S)
and
\displaystyle F(C)
, which, again by Lemma
4.7
, is the case if the squares

\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{F(K\cup C\cup S)}}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{F(S)}}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{F(K\cup C)}}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{F(C)}}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{F(K\cup C)}}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{F((K\cap S)\cup(C\cap S))}}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{F(K)}}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{F(K\cap C)}}
\displaystyle\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\lrcorner}
\displaystyle\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\lrcorner}

and

\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{F((K\cap S)\cup(C\cap S))}}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{F(C\cap S)}}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{F(K\cap S)}}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{F(K\cap C\cap S)}}
\displaystyle\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\lrcorner}

are pullbacks. This is the case for all three squares since:

•

\displaystyle S\subset K\cup C\cup S
is saturated compact.

•

\displaystyle C\subset K\cup C
is closed.

•

\displaystyle C\cap S\subset(K\cap S)\cup(C\cap S)=(K\cup C)\cap S

Therefore in all of these cases, we can use excision for either closed or saturated compact inclusions, hence we conclude the claim.
∎

Now assume we have a functor
\displaystyle F:\mathrm{SC}^{\mathrm{op}}\rightarrow\mathcal{D}
as in Proposition
4.9
, and that
\displaystyle\mathcal{D}
is furthermore stable. Since crucially in this context pushout squares agree with pullback squares, we see that
\displaystyle F
restricted to elementary compact sets satisfies a
cosheaf
condition on elementary compacts (with covers given by intersections!). We claim that this, together with compatibility with filtered intersections, is enough to produce a cosheaf on the frame
\displaystyle\mathcal{O}(X^{\mathrm{patch}})\cong\mathcal{K}(X)^{\mathrm{op}}
of all perfect subspaces. The only caveat is that
\displaystyle F(1)\neq 0
in general, an issue that can be solved by taking cofibers.

Proposition 4.10
.

Let
\displaystyle X
be a stably compact space, let
\displaystyle\mathcal{D}
be stable and closed under colimits, and let
\displaystyle F:\mathcal{O}(X^{\mathrm{patch}})\cong\mathcal{K}(X)^{\mathrm{op}}\rightarrow\mathcal{D}
be a functor satisfying the two conditions:

(1)

For all
\displaystyle K\subset X
compact and
\displaystyle E\subset X
elementary compact we have a pushout square

\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{F(K\cup E)}}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{F(K)}}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{F(E)}}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{F(E\cap K).}}
\displaystyle\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\lrcorner}

(2)

Whenever
\displaystyle K=\bigcap_{i\in I}K_{i}
is a directed intersection of compacts, we have

F(K)\simeq\mathrm{colim}_{i\in I}F(K_{i}).

Then the functor
\displaystyle F^{\#}
defined via
\displaystyle F^{\#}(K)=\mathrm{cofib}(F(1)\rightarrow F(K))
is a cosheaf on
\displaystyle X^{\mathrm{patch}}
.

Before we begin the proof, let us cite a standard lemma on the use of Grothendieck topologies.

Lemma 4.11

(Comparison lemma, see
[
Hoy14
]
, Lemma C.3)
.

Let
\displaystyle(P,\tau)
be a poset equipped with a Grothendieck topology and
\displaystyle P_{0}\subset P
a subset of
\displaystyle P
such that:

•

Every object in
\displaystyle P
can be covered by objects in
\displaystyle P_{0}
.

•

\displaystyle P_{0}
is closed under meets in
\displaystyle P
.

Let
\displaystyle\tau_{0}
be the induced Grothendieck topology on
\displaystyle P_{0}
by restriction. Then the restriction

\mathrm{Sh}(P,\tau)\rightarrow\mathrm{Sh}(P_{0},\tau_{0})

is an equivalence of
\displaystyle\infty
-categories, with inverse given by right Kan extension.

Let us remark that the same statement thus follows for cosheaves, however with inverse given by left Kan extension.

Proof of Proposition
4.10
.

It is immediate that
\displaystyle F^{\#}
restricted to the set of elementary compacts
\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(X)^{\mathrm{op}}
is a cosheaf for the induced Grothendieck topology given by restriction from
\displaystyle\mathcal{O}(X^{\mathrm{patch}})
. Using Lemma
4.11
, we are left to show that the natural map

\eta:\mathrm{Lan}\mathrm{Res}F^{\#}\rightarrow F^{\#}

is an equivalence, which is a statement we only need to verify objectwise for all compact
\displaystyle K\subset X
. We proceed in two steps:

(a)

We first show that
\displaystyle\eta_{K}
is an equivalence for
\displaystyle K
of the form
\displaystyle K=E_{1}\cap\cdots\cap E_{n}
with
\displaystyle E_{i}
elementary compact, by induction. The induction start is true since the inclusion of elementary compacts into all compacts is fully faithful. Now assume we know that
\displaystyle\eta
is an equivalence for intersections of
\displaystyle n-1
many elementary compacts. Since the left Kan extension of
\displaystyle\mathrm{Res}F^{\#}
is a cosheaf on
\displaystyle\mathcal{O}(X^{\mathrm{patch}})
, the value of
\displaystyle\mathrm{Lan}\mathrm{Res}F^{\#}
on
\displaystyle K
is given as the pushout

\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{F^{\#}((E_{1}\cap\cdots\cap E_{n-1})\cup E_{n})}}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{F^{\#}(E_{n})}}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{F^{\#}(E_{1}\cap\cdots\cap E_{n-1}).}}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathrm{Lan}\mathrm{Res}F^{\#}(E_{1}\cap\cdots\cap E_{n-1}\cap E_{n})}}
\displaystyle\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\lrcorner}

But this pushout agrees with
\displaystyle F^{\#}(E_{1}\cap\cdots\cap E_{n-1}\cap E_{n})
by assumption from point (1).

(b)

For arbitrary
\displaystyle K

\displaystyle K
as a directed intersection of finite intersections of elementary compacts, and see from using (a) together with assumption (2) that

is also an equivalence.

∎

5.
Descent on stably compact spaces

In this section we present an extension of the following theorem due to Clausen.

Theorem 5.1

(Clausen, see
[
KNP24
]
Theorem 3.6.15)
.

Let
\displaystyle\mathcal{D}
be a compactly assembled presentable
\displaystyle\infty
-category and let
\displaystyle F:\mathrm{CH}^{\mathrm{op}}\rightarrow\mathcal{D}
be a functor such that:

(1)

\displaystyle F(\emptyset)=1.

(2)

Closed descent:
Whenever
\displaystyle K,L\subset X
are two closed embeddings, then

\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{F(K\cup L)}}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{F(K)}}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{F(L)}}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{F(K\cap L)}}
\displaystyle\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\lrcorner}

is a pullback.

(3)

Profinite descent:
Whenever
\displaystyle X_{i},i\in I,
is a cofiltered system in
\displaystyle\mathrm{CH}

F(\lim_{i\in I}X_{i})\simeq\mathrm{colim}_{i\in I}F(X_{i}).

where for a given compact Hausdorff space
\displaystyle X

\displaystyle F(\mathrm{pt})\in\mathcal{D}

This extension for stably compact spaces is obtained by switching closed descent with proper descent.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
\displaystyle\mathcal{D}
be a compactly assembled
\displaystyle\infty

(1)

\displaystyle F(\emptyset)=1.

(2)

Whenever
\displaystyle K,L\subset X

\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{F(K\cup L)}}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{F(K)}}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{F(L)}}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{F(K\cap L)}}
\displaystyle\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\lrcorner}

Whenever
\displaystyle X_{i},i\in I,

\displaystyle\mathrm{SC},

F\simeq\Gamma((-)^{\mathrm{patch}};F(\mathrm{pt})).

Let us provide the setup for the proof of Theorem
5.2
. For the remainder of the section, fix a functor

\displaystyle F

Construction 5.8
.

Given a perfect map
\displaystyle f:X\rightarrow Y
, we see that there is a natural transformation of
\displaystyle K
-sheaves on
\displaystyle Y
,

F_{Y}\rightarrow f_{+}F_{X}

given for
\displaystyle K\subset Y
perfect as the map
\displaystyle F(K)\xrightarrow{F(f|_{f^{-1}(K)})}F(f^{-1}(K))
. Under the naturality provided by Proposition
3.68
, this corresponds to a natural transformation of sheaves
\displaystyle F^{Y}\rightarrow f_{*}F^{X}.
The extreme case,
\displaystyle X\rightarrow\mathrm{pt}
, gives the map
\displaystyle F(\mathrm{pt})\rightarrow X_{*}F^{X}
that is adjoint to
\displaystyle\alpha_{X}
.

If
\displaystyle X\xrightarrow{f}Y\xrightarrow{g}Z
are two perfect maps, it is clear that we have a commutative triangle of natural transformations of sheaves over
\displaystyle Z
,

\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{F^{Z}}}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{g_{*}F^{Y}}}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{(gf)_{*}F^{X}.}}

Adjoining
\displaystyle g_{*}
over gives the commutative triangle

\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{g^{*}F^{Z}}}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{F^{Y}}}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{f_{*}F^{X}.}}

Moreover, the map
\displaystyle g^{*}F^{Z}\rightarrow f_{*}F^{X}
naturally factors as the commutative triangle

\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{g^{*}F^{Z}}}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{f_{*}f^{*}g^{*}F^{Z}}}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{f_{*}F^{X}.}}

By pasting triangles, we arrive at the natural commutative square of sheaves on
\displaystyle Y
,

\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{g^{*}F^{Z}}}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{F^{Y}}}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{f_{*}f^{*}g^{*}F^{X}}}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{f_{*}F^{X}.}}

The special case of
\displaystyle Z=\mathrm{pt}
gives a map of arrows from
\displaystyle\alpha_{Y}\rightarrow f_{*}\alpha_{X}
.

With this understood, we can get rid of the finite dimensionality assumption by the observation that an arbitrary directed Hilbert cube
\displaystyle I^{S}_{\leq}\cong\mathrm{lim}_{N\subset S\text{ finite}}I^{N}_{\leq}
is obtained as an inverse limit of stably compact spaces with finite dimensional patch topology. This will follow from the (strong) homotopy invariance provided by the following lemma.

Lemma 5.9
.

Let
\displaystyle X
be stably compact such that
\displaystyle X^{\mathrm{patch}}
embeds as a closed subset of
\displaystyle[0,1]^{M}
for some finite
\displaystyle M
, and let
\displaystyle S
be a set of arbitrary cardinality. Then

F(X)\simeq F(X\times I^{S}_{\leq})

via the canonical projection
\displaystyle X\times I^{S}_{\leq}\rightarrow X
.

Proof.

Using cofiltered descent, we see that

F(X\times I^{S}_{\leq})\simeq\mathrm{colim}_{N\subset S\text{ finite}}F(X\times I^{N}_{\leq}).

The spaces
\displaystyle X\times I^{N}_{\leq}
also satisfy that
\displaystyle(X\times I^{N}_{\leq})^{\mathrm{patch}}\cong X^{\mathrm{patch}}\times I^{N}\subset I^{M\amalg N}
is paracompact with finite covering dimension, hence Lemma
5.5
together with contractibility of
\displaystyle I^{N}
shows that the colimit diagram is in fact the constant diagram with value
\displaystyle F(X)
.
∎

As a direct application we see the following.

Lemma 5.10
.

Let
\displaystyle N\subset S
be a finite subset, and denote by
\displaystyle p^{N}:I^{S}_{\leq}\rightarrow I^{N}_{\leq}

\displaystyle I^{N}

Proof.

The corresponding
\displaystyle K
-sheaf of
\displaystyle F^{I^{N}_{\leq}}
is given by
\displaystyle F_{I^{N}_{\leq}}

\displaystyle K

\displaystyle p^{N}_{*}F^{I^{S}_{\leq}}

(K\subset I^{N}_{\leq})\mapsto F_{I^{S}_{\leq}}(K\times I^{S\setminus N}_{\rightarrow})=F(K\times I^{S\setminus N}_{\rightarrow}).

We can see that their values are naturally equivalent, as by Lemma
5.9

\displaystyle F(K\times I^{S\setminus N}_{\rightarrow})\simeq F(K)\simeq F_{X}(K)
.
∎

Proposition 5.11
.

The natural transformation
\displaystyle\alpha_{X}
is an equivalence for
\displaystyle X=I^{S}_{\leq}
a directed Hilbert cube for an arbitrary set
\displaystyle S
.

Proof.

Since
\displaystyle I^{S}_{\leq}=\lim_{N\subset S\text{ finite}}I^{N}_{\leq}

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}
sends cofiltered limits of stably compact spaces to filtered colimits in

\alpha_{I^{S}_{\leq}}:\underline{F(\mathrm{pt})}\rightarrow F^{I^{S}_{\leq}}

\displaystyle p^{N}_{*}(\alpha_{I^{S}_{\leq}})
for
\displaystyle N\subset S

\displaystyle p^{N}_{*}(\alpha_{I^{S}_{\leq}})

\displaystyle F^{I^{N}_{\leq}}\simeq p^{N}_{*}F^{I^{S}_{\leq}}

Lemma 5.12
.

If
\displaystyle i:K\hookrightarrow X
is a perfect embedding of stably compact spaces, and
\displaystyle\alpha_{X}
is an equivalence, then
\displaystyle\alpha_{K}
is an equivalence as well.

Proof.

The map of natural transformations
\displaystyle\alpha_{X}\rightarrow i_{*}\alpha_{K}
produces by adjunction a map
\displaystyle i^{*}\alpha_{X}\rightarrow\alpha_{K}
. We claim that this map is an equivalence of natural transformations, which is equivalent to saying that the natural transformations
\displaystyle i^{*}X^{*}(F(\mathrm{pt}))\rightarrow Y^{*}(F(\mathrm{pt}))
and
\displaystyle i^{*}(F^{X})\rightarrow F^{K}
are equivalences. The former is clear, as
\displaystyle i^{*}
preserves constant sheaves. The latter follows from the observation that the corresponding
\displaystyle K
-sheaf of
\displaystyle i^{*}(F^{X})
is obtained by restricting
\displaystyle F_{X}
to
\displaystyle\mathcal{K}(K)

\mathcal{K}(K)\cong\mathcal{K}(X)_{/K}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{K}(X)

\displaystyle K

Theorem 6.1
.

\displaystyle X

\displaystyle\infty

(2)

Corollary 6.3
.

Let
\displaystyle X
be stably locally compact, let
\displaystyle F
be a finitary localizing invariant
\displaystyle\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{perf}}\rightarrow\mathcal{E}
with
\displaystyle\mathcal{E}
presentable stable, and let
\displaystyle\mathcal{C}
be a dualizable
\displaystyle\infty
-category. Then

F^{\mathrm{cont}}(\mathrm{Sh}(X,\mathcal{C}))\simeq F^{\mathrm{cont}}(\mathrm{Cosh}(X,\mathcal{C})).

Proof.

The statement for
\displaystyle\mathcal{E}
being dualizable follows directly for stably compact spaces
\displaystyle X
by using Verdier duality (Theorem
3.59
), as we have the zigzag

\mathrm{Sh}(X,\mathcal{C})\rightarrow\mathrm{Sh}(X^{\mathrm{patch}},\mathcal{C})\leftarrow\mathrm{Sh}(X^{\vee},\mathcal{C})\simeq\mathrm{Cosh}(X,\mathcal{C}),

where all maps are equivalences on finitary localizing invariants by Theorem
6.1
. (The patch topology of the de Groot dual
\displaystyle X^{\vee}
agrees with
\displaystyle X^{\mathrm{patch}}
, see Remark
3.82
.) Note that this zig-zag itself is natural in proper maps of stably compact spaces. Therefore for
\displaystyle X
only stably locally compact, we have

\begin{array}[]{c}F^{\mathrm{cont}}(\mathrm{Sh}(X,\mathcal{C}))\simeq\mathrm{fib}(F^{\mathrm{cont}}(\mathrm{Sh}(X^{+},\mathcal{C}))\rightarrow F^{\mathrm{cont}}(\mathcal{C}))\\
\simeq\mathrm{fib}(F^{\mathrm{cont}}(\mathrm{Cosh}(X^{+},\mathcal{C}))\rightarrow F^{\mathrm{cont}}(\mathcal{C}))\simeq F^{\mathrm{cont}}(\mathrm{Cosh}(X,\mathcal{C})),\end{array}

finishing the claim. For
\displaystyle\mathcal{E}
not necessarily dualizable, we can use the fact that
\displaystyle F
factors through the universal finitary localizing invariant
\displaystyle\mathcal{U}:\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{perf}}\rightarrow\mathrm{Mot}
, using the result by Efimov that states that
\displaystyle\mathrm{Mot}
is dualizable
[
Efi25b
]
.
∎

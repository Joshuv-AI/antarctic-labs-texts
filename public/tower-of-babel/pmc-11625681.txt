Rasmussen aneurysm in a patient with pulmonary tuberculosis
Authors: Salman Ahmed Malik, Allahdad Khan, Humaira Siddique, Kamran Ahmed Malik, Muhammad Usman, Mohamed Antar
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Key messageRasmussen aneurysm is a rare complication of tuberculosis, where a pulmonary artery adjacent to a tuberculous cavity aneurysm may lead to life threatening hemoptysis. Sudden or severe hemoptysis is an emergency condition in TB patients who need prompt recognition and intervention, often through endovascular embolization, to prevent fatal outcomes.Keywords: hemoptysis, pulmonary artery aneurysm, pulmonary tuberculosis, Rasmussen aneurysm

CLINICAL IMAGE

A 50‐year‐old male of Pakistani origin presented to the outpatient department with complaints of hemoptysis for the past 5 days, fever and shortness of breath from the last 1 month along with productive cough for 3 months. He had a 30‐pack year history of smoking. On general physical examination, his respiratory rate was 30/minute, blood pressure was 126/40 mmHg, SpO2 was 80% at room oxygen and 96% with 3 L oxygen. Clubbing of nails was positive. The patient had positive AFB smear test and GeneXpert for tuberculosis was positive. Bilateral rhonchi and decreased vocal resonance in bilateral lower lobes of lung were present. X ray chest posteroanterior (PA) view (Figure 1A) showed a patch of consolidation in the right lung. Post contrast CT chest confirmed the cavitating consolidation in the basal segment of right lung (Figure 1B) in addition to showing internal dilated pulmonary vessel (Rasmussen aneurysm) (Figure 1C). Patient was treated by endovascular embolization procedure.

(A) x‐Ray chest posteroanterior (PA) view shows patch of consolidation with air bronchograms in the right lower lung zone. Left lung is unremarkable. (B) Post contrast computed tomography (CT) chest axial section lung window shows area of cavitating consolidation in basal segment of right lung, lower lobe with adjacent ground glass opacities and interlobular septal thickening (arrow). (C) Post contrast CT chest axial section, mediastinal window showing internal dilated pulmonary vessel representing Rasmussen aneurysm (arrow).

Rasmussen aneurysm is a rare pulmonary tuberculosis complication and pulmonary artery aneurysm adjacent or in a tuberculous cavity. In chronic cavitary tuberculosis, it may be present in up to 5% of patients. 1 It results from progressive weakening of the pulmonary artery wall from adjacent cavitary tuberculosis with granulation tissue replacing the adventitia and media followed by fibrin deposition and subsequent arterial wall thinning. 1

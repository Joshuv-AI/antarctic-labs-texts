LiMPtTl (M = Ti, Zr) Heusler alloys for high-performance energy harvesting: a DFT study
Authors: Ramesh Sharma, Ali B M Ali, Jisha Annie Abraham, Jaya Madan, Nikhil Shrivastav, Sabirov Sardor, Mirjalol Ismoilov, F F Al-Harbi, Awatif Alshamari
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe current study explores the electronic, thermodynamic, elastic, optical, and thermoelectric properties of the quaternary Heusler alloys LiMPtTl (M = Ti, Zr) using the full-potential linearized augmented plane wave method within the framework of density functional theory, along with semiclassical Boltzmann transport theory. Band structure and density of states calculations indicate semiconducting behavior with indirect band gaps of 0.84 eV for LiTiPtTl and 0.99 eV for LiZrPtTl. Calculations of formation energies, elastic constants, and temperature-dependent thermodynamic properties indicate structural stability, mechanical robustness, and overall stability, while also reflecting anisotropic properties. Optical spectra indicate strong dielectric behavior in the low-energy regime (~ 2 eV), followed by a gradual decrease in the higher energy range. Strong ultraviolet reflectivity indicates potential applications in UV-protective coatings, while strong optical conductivity in the visible region indicates potential applications in solar energy conversion. Transport calculations indicate an increasing power factor with temperature, indicating their potential applications in high-temperature thermoelectric devices. The lattice thermal conductivity calculated from the Slack model predicts very low values of 0.68 W m⁻¹ K⁻¹ for LiTiPtTl and 1.15 W m⁻¹ K⁻¹ for LiZrPtTl at 300 K. The Seebeck coefficients for both compounds are very high at room temperature: 1349.2 and 1890 µV K⁻¹. LiTiPtTl attains a peak figure of merit (ZT) of 0.73 at 1200 K, surpassing many reported quaternary Heusler systems in both magnitude and thermal stability. Owing to the scarcity of prior studies on these materials, these findings provide a useful platform for future theoretical and experimental exploration. Introduction

The pace of technological development and the requirements of modern life have resulted in a substantial increase in energy consumption worldwide, making it a challenge to produce sustainable energy. Under these conditions, there is a great need for energy solutions that are reliable, safe, and easy to implement. Thermoelectric technology, based on the Seebeck effect, has these advantages and does not produce dangerous emissions during operation. Therefore, there has been great interest in finding environmentally friendly and efficient energy sources to solve the current energy crisis. Thermoelectric energy conversion is one of the promising methods for the direct conversion of waste heat into usable electrical power. Heusler alloys were first reported by Friedrich Heusler in early 20th century (1903)1. He experimented with alloys like Cu2MnAl and found that even though the constituent elements were non-magnetic in nature, the alloy exhibits ferromagnetic behaviour. Heusler alloys have been classified into full Heusler (), half Heusler ( XYZ) and quarternary Heusler (). Full Heusler alloys () have a highly ordered cubic structure with all lattice sites filled and usually show metallic or half metallic behaviour useful in spintronics. Half Heusler alloys ( XYZ) have structure, where one of the four interpenetrating fcc sublatttices is vacant, making them to behave as semicondutcors with tuneable band gaps and suitable for themoelectric applications. Quaternary Heusler alloys () contain four different elements in an ordered structure, allowing precise tuning of electronic, magnetic and thermoelectric properties2–5. As Thermoelectric Modules (TEMs), made of TE materials, do not produce any pollutants during operation, this method is considered environmentally friendly for future energy applications6. The performance of a thermoelectric material is measured by the dimensionless Figure of merit (ZT), given by: where S is the Seebeck coefficient, σ is electrical conductivity, and T is the absolute temperature. The symbols κe and κl stand for electronic and lattice thermal conductivity, respectively7. Though many research scholars and scientists are actively trying and working hard towards finding more materials with a good value of ZT, only a few materials have demonstrated a value of 1 and ZT at room temperature8–10. In the present-day scenario, the contemporary problem for modern research is how to solve the problem of the development of materials that would not only have good mechanical and electrical properties but would also be useful for the practical application of TE. It is within this backdrop that Heusler alloys have surfaced as a hopeful candidate. Not only have they produced materials with good mechanical stability, but they have also become quite useful for the practical application of the devices/host materials11–13. Nevertheless, by containing an appropriate quantity of electropositive elements, for instance, lithium, and by following the 18 valence electrons rule, the vacant positions in the HH or FH structures can be utilized for creating a new class of materials that is referred to as quaternary Heusler compounds. It is worth pointing out that some of the HH and FH structures were found to attract considerable attention due to their unique electronic and magnetic properties, which can potentially lead to their applications in various fields including topological devices, spintronics, ferromagnetism, etc., which is where the subject of superconductivity is moving towards14–16. It has been stressed that the efficient thermoelectric materials were required to feature a small semiconductor gap together with phonon glass-electron crystal property17. Such good properties can be perfectly seen with regard to the alloys of Heusler materials. Such good properties have to be used for thermoelectric devices. The Heusler alloys received immense attention due to their non-toxic properties, thermodynamic, and mechanical stability, along with their semiconductor characteristics, which are available at low cost18–23. While thermoelectric materials have abundant and affordable elements available, significantly, several Heusler alloys have shown good thermoelectric properties with abundant and affordable elements available on the Earth’s crust material24. Half-Heusler (HH) materials composed of Ti, Hf, and Zr have been found to be of significant interest to researchers due to their diverse range of physical properties and applicability in thermoelectric devices25,26. These materials have attracted considerable research interest over the past few years, and it has been found that they can exhibit notable mechanical properties along with thermoelectric properties27,28. Research on both n-type and p-type HH materials has found promising Figure of merit at high temperature ranges, i.e., around 0.8 to 1.0529. Among the Sb-based HH materials, several have been found to exhibit significant Figure of merit, i.e., higher than unity30,31. Among these materials, LaPtSb has shown promising performance for thermoelectric energy conversion, with a Figure of merit near 2.2 under near-room-temperature conditions32,33. In recent studies, several Heusler alloys have been explored using a wide variety of experimental techniques and density functional theory studies, suggesting good mechanical stability coupled with good thermoelectric performance, largely attributed to the low lattice thermal conductivity inherent in Heusler alloys34.

The field of quaternary Heusler materials with multifunctional characteristics has been explored theoretically to assess their potential for existing and future technologies35. It is well established that quaternary Heusler compounds typically exhibit lattice constants in the range of ~ 5.8–6.5 Å and relatively high bulk moduli, indicating strong structural rigidity and mechanical stability1,36. For example, Zr-based quaternary Heusler alloys have been reported to possess bulk moduli in the range of ~ 120–200 GPa, confirming their high resistance to compression and structural robustness1. Similarly, other Li-based Heusler systems show comparable formation energies and follow the general trend of enhanced stability for negative formation energies, consistent with thermodynamically stable phases36. The results confirm that their structural parameters are in good agreement with previously reported stable Heusler and quaternary Heusler alloys, thereby supporting their mechanical and thermodynamic stability.

It is interesting to note that a quaternary Heusler compound, CrVNbZn, has been theoretically explored using density functional theory methods, yielding good electronic, magnetic, and thermoelectric properties, with a peak ZT of 0.79 over a temperature range of 260–480 K37. A superior thermoelectric material, with a figure of merit ZT = 1.22, has also been reported for the quaternary Heusler alloy compound NaHfCoGe, thereby highlighting its potential for application as a thermoelectric material38. Apart from these compounds, a number of other Li-based quaternary Heusler compounds, such as LiHfCoX (X = Ge, Sn), LiNbCoX (X = Al, Ga), LiScPdPb, LiTiCoZ (Z = Si, Ge), and LiTiPdZ (Z = Al, Ga, In)39–41, were also investigated for their potential for application as thermoelectric materials. Li-based quaternary Heusler alloys such as LiTiPtZ (Z = Al, Ga, In) have been reported to exhibit semiconducting band gaps and promising thermoelectric performance42. Similarly, Fe-based quaternary Heusler compounds like FeCrTiSb demonstrate high thermoelectric efficiency along with favorable electronic and optical properties43. In addition, other quaternary systems such as CoFeYGe (Y = Ti, Cr) show good transport characteristics and moderate thermoelectric performance44. A literature survey shows that LiMPtTl, where M = Ti, Zr, are yet to be explored both theoretically and experimentally, thus prompting us to investigate their ground state structural parameters, electronic, elastic, and optical properties, along with their potential for application as thermoelectric materials.

Computational approaches

The structural, electronic, dynamical, thermodynamic, elastic, optical, and transport properties of quaternary Heusler compounds LiMPtTl (M = Ti, Zr) were studied within the framework of density functional theory implemented in the WIEN2k45 simulation package using a full-potential linearized augmented plane wave (FP-LAPW) approach. This fully relativistic method is particularly apt to deal with complex crystal geometries and chemical bonds, providing an adequate description of electronic properties. To describe the exchange–correlation effects, the generalized gradient approximation (GGA) of Perdew–Burke–Ernzerhof (PBE)46 was applied, this functional representing a good compromise between computational cost and accuracy for solid-state systems. To take advantage of improved band gap values, the Tran–Blaha modified Becke–Johnson47 potential was further adopted, since it has become evident that these functional yields semiconductor and insulator electronic structures closer to experiment. The relativistic effects ensuing from the heavier atoms, Pt and Tl, were treated by including spin–orbit effects48 via the second-variational scheme provided by WIEN2k. Brillouin zone sampling49 was performed using a 17 × 17 × 17 Monkhorst–Pack mesh in reciprocal space. The used muffin-tin radii were 2.0 a.u. for Li and Ti/Zr, 2.25 a.u. for Pt, and 2.0 a.u. for the rest, with the following basis set parameter values. The chosen muffin-tin radii were 2.0 a.u. for Li and Ti/Zr, 2.25 a.u. for Pt, and 2.0 a.u. for the remaining atoms, while the basis-set parameters were set to RMTKmax=7 and Gmax =12, ensuring proper convergence of the potential and wavefunctions. A separation energy of − 7.0 Ry distinguished core from valence states. Self-consistent calculations were continued until the total energy and charge converged to 10− 4 Ry and 10− 4 e, respectively. The thermoelectric transport coefficients were calculated by using the BoltzTraP code50 under the empirical Boltzmann transport theory, applying a constant relaxation time and a rigid band approach approximation. A dense mesh of around 1,00,000 k-points was used during calculations for the transport integral with a chosen carrier relaxation time of 10− 14 s, as such, was assumed, as is generally adopted in such systems of intermetallic compounds. However, results may be further refined in any further studies involving temperature-dependent relaxation times, obtained from electron-phonon couplings by tools such as EPW, to arrive at higher precision calculations.

Understanding the electronic characteristics of LiTiPtTl and LiZrPtTl compounds on the high-symmetry path W-L-Γ-X-W-K requires calculations of electronic BS and DOS. Both compounds have indirect band gaps, with the valence band maximum at the Γ point and the conduction band minimum at the X point, according to calculations performed using the GGA-PBE method (Supplementary Fig. S1). They are determined to be semiconductors with band gap values of 0.839 eV for LiTiPtTl and 0.955 eV for LiZrPtTl. The conduction band is occupied from 1.5 to 6.4 eV, and the valence band is filled from − 5.3 eV to the Fermi level (EF). High carrier effective masses are also indicated by the flat Ti/Zr-d and Pt-d bands at EF.

The prediction of the band gap is enhanced by employing the TB-mBJ potential (Fig. 4), which maintains the indirect band gap structure with slightly larger gaps of 0.840 eV and 0.994 eV, in agreement with previously reported results39,40. Minor splitting is added around heavy elements (Pt, Tl) by adding SOC (mBJ + SOC) (Supplementary Fig. S1), but the overall band structure and indirect semiconducting character are not affected. Specifically, we now explain that SOC lifts the degeneracy of electronic states and induces band splitting, particularly in the valence bands near high-symmetry points and close to the Fermi level. This splitting can modify the band gap and the density of states, thereby influencing the transport properties. It is also noted that SOC effects are more pronounced in materials containing heavy elements, where relativistic interactions significantly alter band dispersion and carrier dynamics68. Based on the DOS analysis, the VB is dominated by Pt-d, along with Ti/Zr-d and Tl-p orbitals, while the CB is dominated by Ti/Zr-d and Pt-d orbitals. Thermoelectric properties

Thermoelectricity refers to the direct conversion between thermal and electric energy. In such devices, the temperature difference causes the flow of charge carriers, which in turn leads to the generation of an electric current. The temperature- and chemical potential-dependent thermoelectric properties of QH LiMPtTl (M = Ti, Zr) alloys have been studied to determine their potential for use in thermoelectric devices77–81. Their transport properties are described within the Boltzmann transport theory, which is based on the energy-dependent electrical conductivity tensor, and constant relaxation time approximation (CRTA). CRTA is a method used to calculate transport properties using Boltzmann transport equation by assuming that the electron relaxation time is constant. CRTA ignores detailed scattering and focuses on band structure to estimate transport properties efficiently while rigid band approximation ignores changes in band structure due to doping, limiting their accuracy in materials.

The transport properties were calculated using semiclassical Boltzmann transport theory within the constant relaxation time approximation (CRTA), as implemented in the BoltzTraP framework. Within this approach, the electrical conductivity (σ), Seebeck coefficient (S), and electronic thermal conductivity (κₑ) are evaluated as functions of temperature (T) and chemical potential (µ). The calculations were performed by scanning the chemical potential over a wide energy range around the Fermi level to simulate both electron and hole doping conditions. The reported transport coefficients correspond to a range of carrier concentrations, typically spanning from 10¹⁸ to 10²¹ cm⁻³, which are commonly relevant for thermoelectric applications. Unless otherwise specified, the results represent optimized transport behavior at carrier concentrations that maximize the power factor (S²σ). The chemical potential at each temperature was adjusted to reproduce the desired carrier concentration using the rigid-band approximation. This approach assumes that doping shifts the Fermi level without altering the underlying electronic band structure.

It should be noted that the CRTA assumes a constant relaxation time (τ), and therefore the calculated electrical conductivity and power factor are expressed in terms of σ/τ. While this approximation enables reliable prediction of trends in transport properties, it may not capture the full scattering physics, particularly at high temperatures or in heavily doped regimes. Furthermore, the rigid-band approximation neglects possible band structure modifications induced by doping, which may introduce deviations in real materials. Despite these limitations, this methodology provides a reasonable first-order estimation of thermoelectric performance and is widely adopted for screening potential thermoelectric materials such as LiTiPtTl and LiZrPtTl.

According to Fig. 8(a), the Seebeck coefficient (S) of LiMPtTl (M = Ti, Zr) alloys shows positive values with temperature, indicating that holes are the main charge carriers in both alloys. S increases quickly for both alloys up to 300 K, after which it gradually drops as the temperature rises. We have observed a non-monotonic behavior of Seebeck coefficient in low temperature region i.e., initial increase followed by a decrease can arise from the interplay between carrier concentration and carrier diffusion with temperature. At lower temperatures, the increase in thermally activated carriers enhances the Seebeck coefficient, while at higher temperatures the further increase in carrier concentration leads to stronger bipolar effects and enhanced electrical conductivity, which tends to reduce the Seebeck coefficient82,83. The variation in atomic size between Zr and Ti atoms may be the reason for the noticeable disparity between LiTiPtTl and LiZrPtTl across the whole temperature range. LiTiPtTl and LiZrPtTl have Seebeck coefficients of 220.54 µV/K and 225.11 µV/K at 300 K and 204.06 µV/K and 210.44 µV/K at 1200 K, respectively. It’s interesting to note that LiZrPtTl works better at lower temperatures, whilst LiTiPtTl has a greater S value at temperatures about 700 K.

Conclusion

The structural stability, band structure, thermodynamic properties, elastic properties, and optical properties of the QH LiMPtTl (M = Ti, Zr) compounds were investigated using density functional theory calculations based on the FP-LAPW method. The volume optimization calculations show that the two alloys are stable in the type-3 arrangement of the face-centered cubic (FCC) structure, as suggested by their calculated formation energies. The band structure calculations show that the two compounds are indirect band gap semiconductors. The band gap energy of LiTiPtTl is found to be 0.84 eV. In the case of LiZrPtTl, the value of the band gap is 0.99 eV. In both cases, the band gap is due to the contribution of the 3d/4d orbitals of the Ti/Zr atom and the 5d orbital of the Pt atom. Phonon dispersion curves provide clear evidence of the dynamical stability of the compounds. From the Dulong-Pettit theorem and the Debye model of solids at high and low temperatures, respectively, it can be said that the compounds are thermodynamically stable. Calculations of elastic properties indicate that both alloys are mechanically stable, brittle, and anisotropic. Optical properties, such as high dielectric constants, large optical conductivity in the visible range, and high absorption. High UV reflectivity also indicates the possibility of using these alloys in solar shielding coatings. Moreover, the thermoelectric properties have been calculated using the semiclassical Boltzmann transport equations. The calculated values of ZT are found to be 0.73 and 0.70 for LiMPtTl (M = Ti, Zr) alloys, respectively, which are relatively higher and more stable than other QH alloys, indicating that these alloys are promising materials for thermoelectric, optoelectronic, and photovoltaic devices. Future experimental work is required to confirm these theoretical predictions.

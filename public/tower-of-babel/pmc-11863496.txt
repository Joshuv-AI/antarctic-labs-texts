TMEM175 plays a crucial role in osteoblast differentiation by regulating lysosomal function and autophagy
Authors: Seung Hye Lee, Ji Sun Jang, Shenzheng Mo, Hong-Hee Kim
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Bone provides structural support, enables movement, protects internal organs, regulates calcium and phosphorus levels, and contains bone marrow essential for hematopoiesis. Osteoblasts are specialized cells responsible for bone formation through the secretion of extracellular matrix components. Transmembrane protein 175 (TMEM175), which functions as an endosomal/lysosomal K+ channel and a lysosomal H+ channel, regulates lysosomal function and autophagy. Despite the recognized importance of lysosomes and autophagy in osteoblast differentiation, the specific role of TMEM175 in osteoblast differentiation has not been revealed. In this study, we investigated whether TMEM175 is associated with human bone mineral density and fracture and examined the role of TMEM175 in osteoblast differentiation. In analyses of single nucleotide polymorphisms of pore ion channel genes using the mouse2human database, a significant correlation between TMEM175 single nucleotide polymorphisms and human bone mineral density and fracture was identified. TMEM175 expression levels were found to increase during osteoblast differentiation from bone chip-derived mesenchymal stem cells (BMSCs). Knockdown of TMEM175 in BMSCs suppressed osteoblast differentiation, as evidenced by decreased matrix mineralization and lower expression levels of osteoblast marker genes. Further analysis indicated that TMEM175 deficiency leads to lysosomal dysfunction and partially impairs autophagic clearance during osteoblast differentiation. Moreover, the TMEM175 inhibitor 4-aminopyridine decreased osteoblast differentiation of BMSCs. Taken together, this study reveals that TMEM175 plays an important role in osteoblast differentiation by regulating lysosomal function and autophagic clearance.

INTRODUCTION

Bone supports the body, enables physical mobility, protects internal organs, regulates calcium and phosphorus homeostasis, and contains bone marrow responsible for hematopoiesis (Florencio-Silva et al., 2015). Bone remodeling, a crucial process for maintaining bone homeostasis, involves specialized cells: bone-resorbing osteoclasts and bone-forming osteoblasts. The balance between osteoclasts and osteoblasts is vital for bone quantity and quality, and its disruption can lead to diseases such as osteoporosis, osteopetrosis, and increased fracture risk (Chen et al., 2018, Florencio-Silva et al., 2015).

Bone-derived mesenchymal stem cells (BMSCs) have the potential to differentiate into adipocytes, chondrocytes, and osteoblasts (Gao et al., 2021). Under osteogenic conditions, BMSCs can differentiate into osteoblasts with the expression of osteoblast marker genes such as Runx2, ALP, osterix, osteocalcin, bone sialoprotein, and Col1a1 (Long, 2011, Rutkovskiy et al., 2016). Previous studies have suggested the lysosome, an intracellular organelle responsible for digesting unwanted or damaged molecules from endocytosis, phagocytosis, and autophagocytosis, as an important player in osteoblast differentiation (Nabavi et al., 2008, Taniguchi et al., 2010). For example, a deficiency in the lysosomal biogenesis transcription factor TFEB suppresses osteoblast differentiation (Tsukuba et al., 2017). Moreover, lysosomes play a vital role in the exocytosis of Ca2+-containing vesicles, which is essential for matrix mineralization (Iwayama et al., 2019). Autophagy is a lysosome-dependent degradation process that removes damaged organelles or molecules. Autophagy proceeds through the formation of autophagosomes, the fusion of autophagosomes and lysosomes, and the subsequent clearance process (Parzych and Klionsky, 2014). Several studies have highlighted the importance of autophagy in osteoblast biology, showing that deficiencies in autophagy-related genes can impair osteoblast differentiation. Specifically, impaired autophagy can lead to MSC dysfunction, reducing osteoblast differentiation potential while increasing adipogenesis (Chen et al., 2020, Nollet et al., 2014, Yin et al., 2019).

Transmembrane protein 175 (TMEM175) was initially identified as an endosomal/lysosomal K+ channel (Cang et al., 2015), and further research has demonstrated that TMEM175 functions as a lysosomal proton-activated proton channel (Hu et al., 2022). TMEM175 regulates lysosomal pH and the activity of lysosomal proteins (Cang et al., 2015, Jinn et al., 2017) and also partially modulates autophagic clearance (Jinn et al., 2017). Moreover, TMEM175 has been recognized as a key genetic factor in Parkinson’s disease, where its deficiency leads to increased α-synuclein aggregation, a common pathological feature of the disease (Cang et al., 2015, Hu et al., 2022). Given that Parkinson’s disease patients mostly suffer from bone diseases and have a high risk of bone fracture due to various genetic and environmental factors (Kelly et al., 2020, Xiong et al., 2021), it was hypothesized that TMEM175 may also be a significant genetic factor in bone diseases. Supporting evidence includes the association of the TMEM175 single nucleotide polymorphism (SNP) (rs6599389) with a high risk of Parkinson’s disease and low bone mineral density (BMD) (Christou et al., 2020). Furthermore, TMEM175 is included in a cluster of differentially expressed genes that are downregulated in the bones of aged mice, and this cluster is significantly associated with human BMD and fracture (Kaya et al., 2022). Despite these associations, the specific role of TMEM175 in bone cells has not yet been investigated.

In analyses of the association between SNPs of pore ion channel genes and human BMD and fracture, TMEM175 was identified as a significantly associated gene. Given the known function of TMEM175 in regulating lysosomal function and autophagy and the essential roles of lysosomes and autophagy in osteoblast differentiation, we specifically focused our investigation on the role of TMEM175 in osteoblasts. Our experiments involving TMEM175 knockdown and the use of the TMEM175 inhibitor 4-aminopyridine (4-AP) demonstrated that impairment of TMEM175 results in diminished osteoblast differentiation. Moreover, TMEM175 knockdown exhibited decreased activity of lysosomal hydrolases and impaired autophagic clearance. In summary, these findings highlight the significance of TMEM175 in osteoblast differentiation through its modulation of lysosomal function and autophagy.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

Mouse2human Database Website

The mouse2human database website (www.mouse2human.org) offers z-scores and P-values for the association of SNP of various genes with human BMD and fracture. These scores were derived from the most associated SNP in each gene region using genome-wide association studies data from the UK Biobank (Kaya et al., 2022).

Animal

Animal studies were approved by Seoul National University's Institutional Animal Care & Use Committee (SNU-230623-3-1). The 5-week-old or 6-week-old male ICR mice used in the experiment were purchased from DooYeol Biotech.

BMSC Isolation and Osteoblast Differentiation

BMSCs were isolated from the tibia and femur of mice. After removing the surrounding tissue and bone marrow, the chopped bone chips were enzymatically digested with 0.1% collagenase and 0.2% dispase II dissolved in Dulbecco’s Modified Eagle’s Medium (DMEM). To halt the enzymatic activity, the digested supernatant was moved to DMEM with 15% fetal bovine serum (FBS) and 1% antibiotics, and the isolated BMSCs were seeded in 60 mm culture dishes (passage 0). The following day, the BMSCs were washed and cultured with KSB-3 medium supplemented with 15% FBS, 1% antibiotics, and KSB-3 supplements. When confluency exceeded 80%, the cells were detached using TrypLE solution and transferred to 100 mm culture dishes (passage 1). BMSCs in passage 2 were used for all experiments. Osteoblast differentiation was induced with Minimum Essential Medium Alpha Modification containing 10% FBS and 1% antibiotics supplemented with 100 nM dexamethasone, 50 μg/ml ascorbic acid, and 10 mM β-glycerophosphate (osteogenic medium). The medium was changed every 2 days.

Knockdown With siRNA

Silencer Select Negative Control #2 small interference RNA (siRNA) (4390847) and TMEM175 Silencer Select Predesigned siRNA (4390816, ID: s90940) were purchased from Ambion. Mixtures of 20 nM siRNA and Lipofectamine RNAiMAX in Opti-MEM were applied overnight to the cell-containing medium. After stabilization with DMEM for 4 hours, the osteogenic medium was used for osteoblast differentiation.

4-AP

4-AP (275875) was purchased from Sigma-Aldrich. After preparing 300 mM 4-AP solution dissolved in phosphate-buffered saline, it was further diluted to the desired concentration with the osteogenic medium.

Alizarin Red Staining and Quantification

BMSCs were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde, and then stained with 2% alizarin red staining solution. The quantification of the staining was performed by dissolving the stain using cetylpyridinium chloride.

WST-8 Cell Viability Assay

The Quanti-Max water-soluble tetrazolium salt 8 (WST-8) Cell Viability Assay Kit from BIOMAX was used following the manufacturer’s protocols. For the toxicity test, the WST-8 cell viability assay was conducted following 24 hours of treatment with serially diluted 4-AP ranging from 0 to 8 mM. In proliferation experiments, the WST-8 cell viability assay was conducted on days 1, 4, and 7 following the induction of osteoblast differentiation.

qRT-PCR

Trizol was added to each sample, and the collected solutions were stored at −20°C. After all samples were collected on the indicated days, chloroform was added to Trizol in a 1:5 ratio. Following centrifugation, the transparent supernatant was transferred to a fresh tube. The supernatant was mixed with isopropanol at a 1:1 ratio and incubated at room temperature for 1 hour. Following centrifugation, the pellet was washed with 75% ethanol, and the ethanol was completely removed. RNA, dissolved in molecular biology grade water, was quantified with a NanoDrop spectrophotometer, and then complementary DNA was synthesized with reverse transcriptase. Quantitative reverse transcription- polymerase chain reaction (qRT-PCR) was performed with denaturation at 95°C for 3 seconds, followed by annealing/extension at 60°C for 33 seconds, and this cycle was repeated for 40 cycles. The relative messenger RNA (mRNA) expression levels of the genes were standardized against the housekeeping gene Hprt and presented as fold changes. Primer sequences used are provided in the Supplementary Table 1.

Western Blot Analysis

Protein extraction was carried out using RIPA buffer with protease inhibitors. The supernatant was transferred to a fresh tube after centrifugation, and the concentration of total protein was assessed using the DC protein assay reagent from Bio-Rad. Equal amounts of proteins were denatured by mixing with 6× sample buffer and then loaded on an sodium dodecyl sulfate-polyacryamide gel electrophoresis gel. Following electrophoresis, proteins were transferred to the polyvinylidene fluoride or nitrocellulose membrane. The membrane was blocked with skim milk and subsequently incubated with the primary antibody. The primary antibody used are as follows: TMEM175 (Proteintech, 19925-1-AP), Osteocalcin (Santa Cruz Biotechnology, sc-30044), Cathepsin B (Cell Signaling Technology, #31718), Cathepsin D (Cell Signaling Technology, #69854), Beclin1 (Santa Cruz Biotechnology, sc-48381), LC3 (Cell Signaling Technology, #83506), and β-actin (MBL, M177-3). After incubation with the secondary antibody, the target protein bands were detected and quantified with ImageJ software. The secondary antibodies used are as follows: anti-Mouse (GenDEPOT, SA001-500) and anti-Rabbit (GenDEPOT, SA002-500).

Cathepsin B and Cathepsin D Activity Assays

The Cathepsin B Activity Assay Kit and the Cathepsin D Activity Assay Kit from Abcam were used according to the manufacturer’s protocols. Samples were collected on day 3 following the induction of osteoblast differentiation. After lysis of the cells with the respective Cathepsin B or Cathepsin D lysis buffers, the protein concentration in the cell lysates was measured using the DC protein assay reagent from Bio-Rad. Equal amounts of protein were incubated with the substrate, and then fluorescence was measured using a microplate reader.

Immunofluorescence

BMSCs treated with siRNA were stimulated with the osteogenic medium, and staining was conducted on day 2. The cells were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde and permeabilized with 0.1% Triton X-100. Afterward, the cells were blocked with 1% bovine serum albumin and incubated with the LC3 antibody (Cell Signaling Technology, #83506) at 4°C overnight. The secondary antibody used was anti-Mouse DyLight 488 (BETHYL, A90-116D2), and DAPI was used for nuclear staining. After mounting, stained cells were visualized with a confocal microscope (Carl Zeiss, LSM980).

Statistical Analysis

All experiments were conducted at least 3 times using samples in duplicate or triplicate. Quantitative data are presented as mean values with standard deviations. Differences between 2 groups were analyzed using unpaired t-test, and differences among multiple groups were analyzed using either 1-way or 2-way ANOVA, with statistical significance defined as a P-value of <.05.

RESULTS

TMEM175 SNPs Are Associated With Human BMD and Fracture

We utilized the mouse2human database provided in a study by Kaya et al. (2022) to directly analyze the association of SNPs with human BMD and fracture. Associations were determined using z-scores and P-values derived from the most significant SNP in the gene region. Considering the importance of maintaining intracellular ion concentrations for cellular homeostasis, we extracted 182 pore ion channel genes from the KEGG database. One hundred forty of these genes were available in the mouse2human database. Our analysis revealed that the TMEM175 SNP (rs115134980) ranked as the fifth most associated variant with human BMD, demonstrating a z-score of 12.281 and a remarkably low P-value of 5.7 × 10−35 (Fig. 1A). Furthermore, the TMEM175 SNP (rs76048788) emerged as the variant most associated with human bone fracture, with a z-score of 6.197 and a P-value of 2.9 × 10−10 (Fig. 1A and B). TMEM175 Is Upregulated During Osteoblast Differentiation

Given the strong association of TMEM175 SNPs with human BMD and fracture, we explored the role of TMEM175 in bone cells, particularly in osteoblasts. Using qRT-PCR and western blot analyses, the expression levels of TMEM175 were examined throughout osteoblast differentiation. The results revealed a significant increase of Tmem175 mRNA expression levels during the differentiation process (Fig. 2A). The successful osteoblast differentiation was confirmed by the increased mRNA expression levels of osteoblast marker genes Alpl, Sp7, and Bglap (Fig. 2A). Additionally, TMEM175 protein levels were found to be elevated during osteoblastogenesis (Fig. 2B and C). Knockdown of TMEM175 in BMSCs Reduces Osteoblast Differentiation

To investigate the role of TMEM175 in osteoblast differentiation, TMEM175 was silenced in BMSCs using siRNA, and the cells were cultured with the osteogenic medium. The efficiency of TMEM175 knockdown was verified using qRT-PCR and western blot analysis. Tmem175 mRNA expression levels were reduced by up to 70% in the siTMEM175 group, and these reduced levels were sustained until day 8 (Fig. 3A). TMEM175 protein levels were also significantly lower in the siTMEM175 group compared to the siCtrl group (Fig. 3B and C). The WST-8 cell viability assay showed no significant difference in proliferation between the siCtrl group and siTMEM175 group (Fig. 3D). Osteoblast differentiation, assessed via alizarin red staining, revealed that the siTMEM175 group showed decreased matrix mineralization compared to both the nontreated and siCtrl groups (Fig. 3E and F). qRT-PCR analyses of osteoblast marker genes indicated significant decreases in Alpl, Sp7, and Bglap from day 4, in Runx2 from day 6, and in Ibsp and Col1a1 on day 8 in the siTMEM175 group compared to the siCtrl group (Fig. 3G). TMEM175 Deficiency Induces Lysosomal Dysfunction

Previous studies have demonstrated that TMEM175 regulates lysosomal and mitochondrial function as an organelle K+ channel (Cang et al., 2015, Jinn et al., 2017). Based on these findings, we hypothesized that the inhibition of osteoblast differentiation by TMEM175 knockdown is linked to lysosomal dysfunction. To investigate this, we assessed the impact of TMEM175 deficiency on lysosomal function. The mRNA expression levels of lysosome-associated genes, including Tfeb, Lamp1, Lamp2, Ctsb, and Ctsd, showed no significant differences (Fig. 4A), and the protein levels of cathepsin B and cathepsin D were unchanged (Fig. 4B and C). However, there was a notable decrease in the enzymatic activity of cathepsin B and cathepsin D in the siTMEM175 group compared to the siCtrl group (Fig. 4D). TMEM175 Deficiency Partially Impairs Autophagic Clearance

To investigate whether TMEM175 knockdown affects the autophagy process through lysosomal dysfunction, the stages of autophagosome formation and autophagic clearance were examined. We first evaluated the mRNA expression levels of autophagy-related genes to assess whether TMEM175 deficiency influences autophagosome formation. The expression levels of Atg5, Atg7, Atg10, Becn1, and Map1lc3b did not differ significantly between the groups (Fig. 5A), and the protein expression levels of Beclin1, which is involved in autophagosome formation, were also unchanged (Fig. 5B). However, the LC3-II level and the LC3-II/I ratio were higher in the siTMEM175 group compared to the siCtrl group (Fig. 5B and C). Immunofluorescence analysis further confirmed increased LC3 puncta, including the number, size, and relative intensity, in the siTMEM175 group (Fig. 5D and E). The increased protein levels of the autophagosome marker LC3-II in the siTMEM175 group suggest that TMEM175 deficiency may either increase autophagosome formation or impair autophagic clearance. To distinguish these possibilities, the autophagic clearance inhibitor bafilomycin A1 was used. Under bafilomycin A1 treatment, no significant differences in the LC3-II level and the LC3-II/I ratio were observed between the siCtrl and siTMEM175 groups, contrasting with the untreated conditions where these levels were elevated in the siTMEM175 group (Fig. 5B and C). 4-AP Suppresses Osteoblast Differentiation Through Lysosomal Dysfunction and Autophagic Impairment

We further explored the effect of 4-AP, the only known inhibitor of TMEM175, on osteoblast differentiation. To identify nontoxic concentrations of 4-AP, the WST-8 cell viability assay was performed with BMSCs after 24 hours treatment. The results showed that concentrations up to 1 mM 4-AP were found to be nontoxic to BMSCs (Supplementary Fig. 1A). Consequently, osteoblast differentiation from BMSCs was induced using the osteogenic medium with or without 1 mM 4-AP. The WST-8 cell viability assay indicated increased late-stage cell proliferation in cells treated with 1 mM 4-AP (Supplementary Fig. 1B). Osteoblast differentiation was assessed by alizarin red staining and qRT-PCR for osteoblast marker genes. Alizarin red staining revealed reduced matrix mineralization in cells treated with 0.5 or 1 mM 4-AP compared to nontreated cells (Fig. 6A and B). The expression levels of osteoblast marker genes, including Runx2, Alpl, Sp7, Bglap, Ibsp, and Col1a1, were significantly lower in cells treated with 1 mM 4-AP compared to nontreated cells (Fig. 6C). However, Tmem175 mRNA expression levels were not significantly different, as anticipated (Supplementary Fig. 1C). In the 1 mM 4-AP treated group, a significant reduction in enzymatic activities of cathepsin B and cathepsin D was observed compared to the untreated control, indicating lysosomal dysfunction (Fig. 6D). Although protein expression levels of Beclin1 remained unchanged, both the LC3-II level and the LC3-II/I ratio increased in the 4-AP treated group, suggesting an accumulation of autophagosomes (Fig. 6E and F). Similarly, immunofluorescence analysis confirmed an increase in LC3 puncta in the 4-AP treated group (Fig. 6G and H). These results closely align with findings from siRNA-mediated TMEM175 knockdown experiments. DISCUSSION

TMEM175, functioning as an endosomal/lysosomal ion channel, was initially identified in relation to neurodegenerative diseases (Cang et al., 2015, Hu et al., 2022). Consequently, previous studies focused on the role of TMEM175 in Parkinson’s disease, particularly in relation to lysosomal function and autophagic clearance (Jinn et al., 2017, Qu et al., 2022, Zheng et al., 2022). Intriguingly, several studies have revealed the association of Parkinson’s disease with bone disorder, attributed to multiple factors such as genetic factors and environmental factors (Kelly et al., 2020, Xiong et al., 2021). Genetically, the specific TMEM175 SNP (rs6599389) is associated with increased Parkinson’s disease occurrence and decreased BMD (Christou et al., 2020). With these backgrounds, we aimed to investigate the role of TMEM175 in bone cells.

In this study, we demonstrated using the mouse2human database that SNPs of the TMEM175 gene are significantly associated with human bone fracture as well as human BMD. This database provides z-scores and P-values for the association of the most significant SNP of each gene with human BMD and fracture (Kaya et al., 2022). Considering the importance of ion concentration regulation for cellular homeostasis, we extracted data for 140 pore ion channel genes from the mouse2human database. Among them, the TMEM175 SNP (rs115134980) ranked as the fifth most associated with BMD, along with SNPs of MS4A4A, MS4A2, MS4A3, and DEFB130B. Regarding fracture, the TMEM175 SNP (rs76048788) was the top-ranked SNP associated with fracture among ion channel genes (Fig. 1).

Based on the positive relation of TMEM175 SNPs with human BMD and fracture, further study was conducted to reveal the importance of TMEM175 during osteoblast differentiation from bone-derived MSCs. While bone marrow-derived MSCs are commonly used in research, bone chip-derived MSCs also demonstrate the ability to differentiate into osteoblasts, chondrocytes, and adipocytes (Blashki et al., 2016, Fernandez-Moure et al., 2015). Furthermore, bone chip-derived MSCs provide a broader representation of bone-resident MSC populations and have superior experimental yield. These advantages facilitated a more efficient investigation of TMEM175’s roles in osteoblast differentiation in our study. Notably, during the osteoblast differentiation of BMSCs, the expression levels of TMEM175 mRNA and protein were increased (Fig. 2).

To examine the direct effect of TMEM175 on osteoblast differentiation potential, TMEM175 expression was silenced with siRNA in BMSCs, and osteoblast differentiation was induced with the osteogenic medium. While there were no significant differences in proliferation during osteoblast differentiation, matrix mineralization and the expression levels of osteoblast marker genes were reduced in the siTMEM175 group compared to the siCtrl group (Fig. 3).

We hypothesized that the inhibition of osteoblast differentiation due to TMEM175 deficiency is linked to lysosome and autophagy based on the previously reported functions of TMEM175. There were no significant differences in the mRNA expression levels of lysosome-related genes Tfeb, Lamp1, Lamp2, Ctsb, and Ctsd or in the protein expression levels of cathepsin B and cathepsin D. However, the activities of cathepsin B and cathepsin D were decreased in the siTMEM175 group compared to the siCtrl group (Fig. 4). Lysosomal enzymes, including cathepsin B and D, function optimally under acidic conditions, typically around pH 4.5 to 5.0, and deviations from this optimal pH lead to diminished enzymatic activities. Previous studies on TMEM175 suggest that the observed decrease in enzymatic activities is likely due to unstable lysosomal pH (Cang et al., 2015, Jinn et al., 2017). Regarding autophagy, there were no significant differences in the mRNA expression levels of autophagy-related genes Atg5, Atg7, Atg10, Becn1, and Map1lc3b. Although there were no differences in the protein expression levels of Beclin1, which regulates autophagy induction, the LC3-II levels and the LC3-II/I ratio were increased in the siTMEM175 group, suggesting TMEM175 deficiency may partially impair autophagic clearance. Treatment with bafilomycin A1 revealed no differences in LC3-II levels and the LC3-II/I ratio between the siCtrl and siTMEM175 groups, confirming that TMEM175 deficiency affects autophagic clearance rather than autophagosome formation (Fig. 5). Further research is needed to determine whether lysosomal pH is altered by TMEM175 deficiency and whether restoring lysosomal or autophagic functions can reverse the inhibition of osteoblast differentiation caused by TMEM175 deficiency.

Several studies have identified activators and 1 inhibitor of TMEM175 (Hu et al., 2022, Oh et al., 2022, Zhang et al., 2023), but the effect of the TMEM175 inhibitor 4-AP on bone cells has not been investigated. 4-AP inhibits TMEM175 by physically binding to and obstructing the channel pore (Oh et al., 2022). Therefore, we hypothesized that functional inhibition of TMEM175 by 4-AP could similarly inhibit osteoblast differentiation, as observed with TMEM175 deficiency. In this study, we used a maximum nontoxic concentration of 1 mM 4-AP to investigate its effects on osteoblast differentiation from BMSCs. 4-AP treatment significantly decreased matrix mineralization and reduced the expression of osteoblast differentiation marker genes. Furthermore, 4-AP led to a reduction in lysosomal enzyme activity and an increase in autophagosomes (Fig. 6 and Supplementary Fig. 1). However, a key limitation of 4-AP is its action as a broad K+ channel inhibitor beyond its role as a TMEM175 inhibitor. Although our results suggest that lysosomal dysfunction and autophagic impairment are key mechanisms underlying the inhibitory effects of 4-AP on osteoblast differentiation, the potential involvement of other K+ channels should be considered.

This study demonstrated that SNPs of the TMEM175 gene are highly associated with human BMD and fracture using the mouse2human database. Moreover, we found that the inhibition of osteoblast differentiation by TMEM175 deficiency is associated with lysosomal dysfunction and impaired autophagic clearance. Furthermore, the TMEM175 inhibitor 4-AP also suppressed osteoblast differentiation of BMSCs. Therefore, our findings reveal that TMEM175 is a crucial genetic factor for osteoblast differentiation of BMSCs.

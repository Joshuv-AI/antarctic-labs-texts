A generalization of K-theory to operator systems
Walter D. van Suijlekom

Abstract
. We propose a generalization of K-theory to operator systems. Motivated by spectral truncations of noncommutative spaces described by C ∗ C^{*} -algebras and inspired by the realization of the K-theory of a C ∗ C^{*} -algebra as the Witt group of hermitian forms, we introduce new operator system invariants indexed by the corresponding matrix size. A direct system is constructed whose direct limit possesses a semigroup structure, and we define the K 0 K_{0} -group as the corresponding Grothendieck group. This is an invariant of unital operator systems, and, more generally, an invariant up to Morita equivalence of operator systems. For C ∗ C^{*} -algebras it reduces to the usual definition. We illustrate our invariant by means of the spectral localizer.

On our wish list for a candidate for K-theory we have put that

Some results in the direction of K-theory for operator systems already exist in the literature. For instance, projections in operator systems were defined in

The aforementioned applications based on the spectral localizer suggests that one should look for invertible, or non-degenerate elements instead.

This paper is structured as follows. After some brief background on operator systems and unital completely positive (ucp) maps, we introduce in Section

In Section

In Section

2.
Background on operator systems

We start by briefly recalling the theory of operator systems, referring to
[
15
,
33
,
34
,
5
]
for more details.

A
unital operator system

(E,e)
is a matrix-ordered
*
-vector space
E
, equipped with an Archimedean order unit
e
. A map
\phi:E\to F
between operator systems determines a family of maps
\phi^{(n)}:M_{n}(E)\to M_{n}(F)
given by
\phi^{(n)}([x_{ij}])=[\phi(x_{ij})]
. A map
\phi:E\to F
between unital operator systems is called
completely positive
if each
\phi^{(n)}
is positive (
n\geq 1
). We also abbreviate completely positive by
cp
, and unital completely positive by
ucp
.

A
dilation
of a ucp map
\phi:E\to B(\mathcal{H})
of a unital operator system is a ucp map
\psi:E\to B(\mathcal{K})
, where
\mathcal{K}
is a Hilbert space containing
\mathcal{H}
such that
P_{\mathcal{H}}\psi(x)|_{\mathcal{H}}=\phi(x)
for all
x\in E
. The ucp map
\phi
is called
maximal
if every dilation of
\phi
is obtained by attaching a direct summand.

A non-zero cp map
\phi:E\to B(\mathcal{H})
is said to be
pure
if the only cp maps satisfying
0\leq\psi\leq\phi
are scalar multiples of
\phi
.

We may view
E
as a concrete operator system in the
C^{*}
-algebra
C^{*}(E)
it generates; in this case, we say that a ucp map
\phi:E\to B(\mathcal{H})
has the
unique extension property
if it has a unique ucp extension to
C^{*}(E)
which is a
*
-representation. If, in addition, the
*
-representation is irreducible, it is called a
boundary representation

[
3
]
. The following result is well-known in the literature
[
3
,
30
,
2
,
24
,
13
]
but for completeness we include a proof.

Proposition 1
.

Let
\phi:E\to B(\mathcal{H})
be a ucp map. Then

(1)

\phi
is maximal if and only if it has the unique extension property.

(2)

\phi
is pure and maximal if and only if it is a boundary representation.

Proof.

(1) is
[
2
, Proposition 2.4]
.
(2) If the extension
\widetilde{\phi}:C^{*}(E)\to B(\mathcal{H})
of
\phi
is reducible, then there exists a non-trivial projection
P\in\widetilde{\phi}(C^{*}(E))^{\prime}
. Then the map
\psi:E\to B(\mathcal{H})
defined by
\psi(x)=P\widetilde{\phi}(x)
is cp and
0\leq\psi\leq\phi
. But
\psi(1)=P
while
\phi(1)=\textup{id}_{\mathcal{H}}
so that
\psi
is not a scalar multiple of
\phi
; hence
\phi
is not pure.

For the other implication, suppose that
\phi
is a boundary representation and take a cp map
\psi
such that
0\leq\psi\leq\phi
. By Arveson’s extension theorem, we have cp maps
\widetilde{\psi}
and
\widetilde{\phi-\psi}
from
C^{*}(E)
to
B(\mathcal{H})
. Since
\widetilde{\psi}+\widetilde{\phi-\psi}
extends
\phi
it follows from the unique extension property of
\phi
that
\widetilde{\phi-\psi}=\widetilde{\phi}-\widetilde{\psi}
. Consequently,
\widetilde{\psi}\leq\widetilde{\phi}
so that by
[
3
, Theorem 1.4.2]
there exists an operator
T\in\widetilde{\phi}(C^{*}(E))^{\prime}
such that

\widetilde{\psi}(a)=T\widetilde{\phi}(a);\qquad\forall a\in C^{*}(E).

Since
\widetilde{\phi}
is irreducible, it follows that
T=t\cdot\textup{id}_{\mathcal{H}}
for some
t\in[0,1]
so that
\widetilde{\psi}=t\widetilde{\phi}
and, consequently, also
\psi=t\phi
. Hence
\phi
is pure.
∎

3.
K-theory for unital operator systems

Even though in an operator system we cannot speak about invertible elements, we may use the pure and maximal ucp maps to introduce the following notion of nondegeneracy.

Definition 2
.

Let
(E,e)
be a unital operator system. A self-adjoint element
x=x^{*}\in M_{n}(E)
is called a hermitian form if it is non-degenerate in the sense that there exists
g>0
such that for all pure and maximal ucp maps
\phi:E\to B(\mathcal{H})
we have

(1)

|\phi^{(n)}(x)|\geq g\cdot\textup{id}_{\mathcal{H}}^{\oplus n}

The smallest real number
g>0
such that (
1
) holds is called the gap of
x
.

We denote the set of hermitian forms contained in
M_{n}(E)
by
H(E,n)
.
The following result will be of crucial importance to us, as it makes it feasible to check the non-degeneracy condition in concrete cases:

Proposition 3
.

A self-adjoint element
x\in M_{n}(E)
is a hermitian form if and only if
\imath_{E}^{(n)}(x)
is an invertible element in the
C^{*}
-envelope
C^{*}_{\mathrm{env}}(E)
. Moreover,
x
has gap
g>0
if and only if
|\imath_{E}^{(n)}(x)|\geq g\cdot 1^{\oplus n}_{C^{*}_{\mathrm{env}}(E)}
.

Proof.

In
[
13
]
the
C^{*}
-envelope of
E
is constructed as the direct sum of all boundary representations
(\mathcal{H}_{\sigma},\sigma)
:

\imath_{E}:E\to\bigoplus_{\sigma}B(\mathcal{H}_{\sigma}).

Now if
x\in E
then
\imath_{E}(x)
is invertible if and only if
|\imath_{E}(x)|\geq g\cdot\textup{id}_{\oplus_{\sigma}(\mathcal{H}_{\sigma})}
, which holds if and only if
|\sigma(x)|\geq g\cdot\textup{id}_{\mathcal{H}_{\sigma}}
for all boundary representations
\sigma
. A similar statement holds if
x\in M_{n}(E)
. Since by Proposition
1
a ucp map is a boundary representation if and only if it is pure and maximal, the result follows.
∎

A trivial example of a hermitian form is the order unit
x=e
(with gap
g=1
). Other examples that in fact motivated the above definition are

Example 4
.

(i)

(Hermitian forms and Witt groups) The relation between the K-theory and the Witt group for rings
[
25
,
4
]
and (unital)
C^{*}
-algebras
[
39
]
stresses the role played by hermitian forms. In fact, any hermitian form on a finitely generated projective module of the form
eA^{n}
is described by a self-adjoint element
x\in eM_{n}(A)e
. The fact that the form is non-degenerate translates to invertibility of the hermitian element
h=x+(1-e)\in M_{n}(A)
, so that
h^{2}\geq g^{2}\cdot 1_{A}
for some
g>0
. But then

\displaystyle x^{2}

\displaystyle=(ehe)^{2}=eh^{2}e\geq g^{2}\cdot e,

since
h
and
e
commute. We find that
x
is a hermitian form in the operator system
(eM_{n}(A)e,e)
with gap
g
(note that since
eM_{n}(A)e
is a
C^{*}
-algebra, it coincides with its
C^{*}
-envelope). This should also explain the above terminology.

Note that in this case one may just as well consider the invertible element
x+(1-e)
as a hermitian form in
M_{n}(A)
.

(ii)

(Almost projections and quantitative
K
-theory) In
[
32
]
quantitative (aka controlled) K-theory
K_{0}^{\varepsilon,r}(A)
of a filtered
C^{*}
-algebra
A=(A_{r})
was defined in terms of
\varepsilon
-
r
-projections, i.e. elements
p\in A_{r}
such that
\|p^{2}-p\|<\varepsilon
where
\varepsilon<1/4
. But then
x=1_{A}-2p
is a hermitian form in the unital operator system
(A_{r},1_{A})
with gap
g=\sqrt{1-4\varepsilon}>0
since

x^{2}=1_{A}-4(p-p^{2})\geq(1-4\varepsilon)\cdot 1_{A}

(iii)

(Projections in operator systems)
In
[
1
]
projections in unital operator systems are abstractly defined. These then turn out to be precisely those elements
p\in E
such that
\imath_{E}(p)
is a projection in the
C^{*}
-envelope. If we set
x=e-2p
we find that
\imath_{E}(x)^{2}=e
so that again such projections define hermitian forms (with gap
1
).

(iv)

(The even spectral localizer) The even spectral localizer
[
28
]
is defined as a spectral compression
x=PHP
of an invertible self-adjoint element
H
in a
C^{*}
-algebra
A\subseteq B(\mathcal{H})
by a projection in
\mathcal{H}
. If the spectrum of
H
does not intersect with the interval
[-g,g]
then we compute for the operator system
(PAP,P)
that

(2)

x^{2}=PHPPHP=PH^{2}P+PH[P,H]P=PH^{2}P+P[P,H][P,H]P

Hence if we set
\delta=\|[P,H]\|
we find that
x^{2}\geq(g^{2}-\delta^{2})P
in the
C^{*}
-extension
C^{*}(PAP)\subseteq B(\mathcal{H})
. By the universal property of the

-envelope, there is a surjective
*
-homomorphism
\rho:C^{*}(PAP)\to C^{*}_{\mathrm{env}}(PAP)
which ensures that
\imath_{E}(x)^{2}\geq(g^{2}-\delta^{2})P
so that

is a hermitian form with gap
\sqrt{g^{2}-\delta^{2}}
.

(v)

(Spectral truncations of quanta of geometry)
As a special case of an invertible self-adjoint element we may also consider the operator
Y\in A
describing the so-called quanta of geometry in
[
7
]
. It satisfies
Y^{2}=1
so that
Y
has spectrum contained in
\{-1,1\}
. If
P
is a projection, we find that the compressions

define hermitian forms with gap
\sqrt{1-\delta^{2}}
provided
\delta=\|[P,Y]\|<1
.

Let us continue the general treatment of hermitian forms in operator systems, and derive the following rigidity result.

Lemma 5
.

Let
x
be a hermitian form with gap
g>0
. If
y=y^{*}\in M_{n}(E)
with
\|x-y\|\leq\varepsilon
for some
\varepsilon<g^{2}/2\|x\|
, then
y
is a hermitian form with gap
\sqrt{g^{2}-2\varepsilon\|x\|}
.

Proof.

The norm estimate implies that

-2\varepsilon\|x\|e\leq\imath_{E}(x)\imath_{E}(y-x)+\imath_{E}(y-x)\imath_{E}(x)\leq 2\varepsilon\|x\|e

Hence by writing
y=x+(y-x)
and noting that
\imath_{E}(y-x)^{2}\geq 0
we find that

\displaystyle\imath_{E}(y)^{2}

\displaystyle=\imath_{E}(x)^{2}+\imath_{E}(x)\imath_{E}(y-x)+\imath_{E}(y-x)\imath_{E}(x)+\imath_{E}(y-x)^{2}

\displaystyle\geq(g^{2}-2\varepsilon\|x\|)e.\qed

We end this subsection by another standard operation of hermitian forms, which is their direct sum.

Definition 6
.

Let
x\in H(E,n)
and
x^{\prime}\in H(E,n^{\prime})
be hermitian forms with respective gaps
g,g^{\prime}
. Their direct sum is the hermitian form given by

x\oplus x^{\prime}=\begin{pmatrix}x&0\\
0&x^{\prime}\end{pmatrix}\in H(E,n+n^{\prime})

which has gap equal to
\mathrm{min}\{g,g^{\prime}\}
.

3.1.
Homotopy equivalence of hermitian forms

One of the defining properties of an operator system is its matrix-order structure. This motivates us to consider homotopies of hermitian forms in
H(E,n)
for a fixed matrix size
n\geq 0
, as follows:

Definition 7
.

Let
x,x^{\prime}\in H(E,n)
be hermitian forms. We say that
x\sim_{n}x^{\prime}
if there exists a hermitian form
\widetilde{x}\in H(C([0,1])\otimes E),n)
such that

\widetilde{x}(0)=x;\qquad\widetilde{x}(1)=x^{\prime}

We denote the equivalence class of a hermitian form
x\in H(E,n)
by
[x]_{n}
, and the set of equivalence classes of hermitian forms in
H(E,n)
by
\mathcal{V}(E,n)
, or, equivalently,

\mathcal{V}(E,n)=\pi_{0}(H(E,n)).

This should be confronted with the usual notion of homotopy equivalence in, say, K-theory for
C^{*}
-algebras, where
n
is allowed to vary (see also Section
3.2
below). The following is immediate:

Proposition 8
.

Let
E
and
F
be unital operator systems. If
\phi:E\to F
is a ucp map for which there exists a
*
-homomorphism
\widetilde{\phi}:C^{*}_{\mathrm{env}}(E)\to C^{*}_{\mathrm{env}}(F)
that makes the following diagram commute,

(3)

\textstyle{E\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\phi}
\scriptstyle{\imath_{E}}
\textstyle{F\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\imath_{F}}
\textstyle{C^{*}_{\mathrm{env}}(E)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\widetilde{\phi}}
\textstyle{C^{*}_{\mathrm{env}}(F)}

then there is an induced map
\phi^{*}:\mathcal{V}(E,n)\to\mathcal{V}(F,n)
defined by

\phi^{*}([x]_{n})=[\phi^{(n)}(x)]_{n}.

Corollary 9
.

If
E
and
F
are completely order isomorphic then
\mathcal{V}(E,n)\cong\mathcal{V}(F,n)
for all
n\geq 0
.

Example 10
.

Consider
E=\mathbb{C}
. Then
\mathcal{V}(\mathbb{C},n)
is the set of homotopy equivalence classes of invertible hermitian

matrices. Any such matrix
x
can be diagonalized with a unitary matrix, and since the unitary group

is connected, there is a homotopy between
x
and the corresponding diagonal matrix. In turn, this diagonal matrix is homotopic (in the space of invertible matrices) to the corresponding signature matrix, which is unique up to ordering. In other words,
\mathcal{V}(\mathbb{C},n)
can be parametrized by the signature

\mathcal{V}(\mathbb{C},n)\cong\{-n,-n+2\ldots,n-2,n\}

Note that the direct sum of two hermitian forms translates to the addition of the corresponding signatures:
(s,s^{\prime})\mapsto s+s^{\prime}
.

Anticipating the discussion in Section

there are maps
\imath_{nm}:\mathcal{V}(\mathbb{C},n)\to\mathcal{V}(\mathbb{C},m)

\imath_{nm}([x]_{n})=[x\oplus e_{m-n}]_{m}.

In terms of the signature, we find that
\imath_{nm}(s)=s+m-n
. Moreover, there are commuting diagrams for any

\textstyle{\mathcal{V}(\mathbb{C},n)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\rho_{n}}
\scriptstyle{\imath_{nm}}
\textstyle{\mathcal{V}(\mathbb{C},m)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\rho_{m}}
\textstyle{\mathbb{Z}}

where
\rho_{n}([x])
is defined to be the so-called negative index of inertia of

, i.e. the number of negative eigenvalues of the hermitian form

Lemma 11
.

Let
E
and
F
be unital operator systems. Then for any
n
we have
\mathcal{V}(E\oplus F,n)\cong\mathcal{V}(E,n)\times\mathcal{V}(F,n)
.

Proof.

Let
(x,y)\in M_{n}(E)\oplus M_{n}(F)
be a hermitian form; this has gap

iff

\imath_{E\oplus F}(x,y)^{2}\geq g^{2}\cdot(e_{E},e_{F})\iff\imath_{E}(x)^{2}\geq g^{2}e_{E}\text{ and }\imath_{E}(y)^{2}\geq g^{2}e_{F}.

Moreover,
(x,y)\sim(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})
iff
x\sim x^{\prime}
and
y\sim y^{\prime}
.
From this the stated isomorphism follows.
∎

The following result is a useful computational tool for
\mathcal{V}(E,n)
. Recall the multiplier
C^{*}
-algebra

of the operator system

A_{E}:=\{a\in C^{*}_{\mathrm{env}}(E):aE\subset E\}

Lemma 12
.

Let
u
be a unitary in
M_{n}(A_{E})
, and let
x\in M_{n}(E)
be a hermitian form with gap
g
. Then

is a hermitian form with gap

, and

as hermitian forms in

Proof.

Clearly,
\imath_{E}(uxu^{*})^{2}=u\imath_{E}(x)^{2}u^{*}\geq g^{2}e
. For the homotopy equivalence, recall that by Whitehead’s Lemma there exists a continuous path of unitaries

such that

w_{0}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix};\qquad w_{1}=\begin{pmatrix}u&0\\

But then
w_{t}(x\oplus e_{n})w_{t}^{*}
is a self-adjoint element in

, also with gap

, establishing an equivalence between

and

∎

Proposition 13
.

Let
x,y\in H(E,n)
be hermitian forms both with gap

such that
\varepsilon<4g^{2}
. Then there is a homotopy
\widetilde{x}
of hermitian forms in

\sqrt{g^{2}-\varepsilon^{2}/4}
such that
\widetilde{x}(0)=x
and
\widetilde{x}(1)=y
.

Proof.

We claim that a homotopy of hermitian forms with the indicated gap is given by
\widetilde{x}(t)=tx+(1-t)y
. It follows from

that

\displaystyle(\imath_{E}(x-y))^{2}\leq\varepsilon^{2}e,

which implies that

\imath_{E}(x)\imath_{E}(y)+\imath_{E}(y)\imath_{E}(x)\geq\imath_{E}(x)^{2}+\imath_{E}(y)^{2}-\varepsilon^{2}e.

We will use this to find the gap of

\displaystyle\imath_{C([0,1])\otimes E}(\widetilde{x})^{2}

\displaystyle=t^{2}\imath_{E}(x)^{2}+(1-t)^{2}\imath_{E}(y)^{2}+t(1-t)(\imath_{E}(x)\imath_{E}(y)+\imath_{E}(y)\imath_{E}(x))

\displaystyle\geq(t^{2}g^{2}+(1-t)^{2}g^{2}+t(1-t)(2g^{2}-\varepsilon^{2}))e

\displaystyle\geq(g^{2}-\varepsilon^{2}/4)e.\qed

Corollary 14

Let

3.2.
Semigroup of hermitian forms and
K_{0}
-group

In the above, we have stressed the role of the matrix size
n\geq 0
for the invariants
\mathcal{V}(E,n)
. However, in order to prepare for a comparison with
K
-theory for
C^{*}
-algebras we will need the limit object
\mathcal{V}(E):=\varinjlim\mathcal{V}(E,n)
that we will now construct.

Given a unital operator system
(E,e)
we consider the direct system of sets
(\mathcal{V}(E,n),\imath_{nm})
where for
m\geq n

(4)

\displaystyle\imath_{nm}:\mathcal{V}(E,n)

\displaystyle\to\mathcal{V}(E,m)

\displaystyle[x]_{n}

\displaystyle\mapsto[x\oplus e_{m-n}]_{m},

We denote the direct limit of the direct system (
4
) by
\varinjlim\mathcal{V}(E,n)
. A more explicit description is given as follows: for
x\in H(E,n)
and
x^{\prime}\in H(E,n^{\prime})
we write
x\sim x^{\prime}
if there exists a
k\geq n,n^{\prime}
such that
x\oplus e_{k-n}\sim_{k}x^{\prime}\oplus e_{k-n^{\prime}}
in
H(E,k)
. We will write
[x]_{E}
, or simply
[x]
, for the equivalence class corresponding to
x\in H(E,n)
and

\mathcal{V}(E):=\amalg_{n}\mathcal{V}(E,n)/_{\sim}
for the corresponding set of equivalence classes. The following is then clear from the definition of the direct limit.

Proposition 15
.

The set
\mathcal{V}(E)
is the direct limit
\varinjlim\mathcal{V}(E,n)
of the direct system (
4
). Moreover, it is a semigroup when equipped with the direct sum
[x]+[x^{\prime}]=[x\oplus x^{\prime}]
and identity element
0=[e]
.

We now arrive at our tentative definition of K-theory for unital operator systems.

Definition 16
.

Let
(E,e)
be a unital operator system. We define the
K
-theory group
K_{0}(E)
of
E
to be the Grothendieck group of
\mathcal{V}(E)
.

Before addressing the properties of the association
K_{0}(E)
to an operator system, let us first check the consistency of our definition with the notion of
K_{0}
-groups when the unital operator system is in fact a unital
C^{*}
-algebra.

Proposition 17
.

For a unital
C^{*}
-algebra
A
the group
K_{0}(A)
is isomorphic to the
C^{*}
-algebraic K-theory group of
A
.

Proof.

Since a hermitian form
x
in a
C^{*}
-algebra is invertible we may define a map between the semigroups (where
\mathcal{P}(A)
denotes the semigroup of projections in
M_{\infty}(A)
up to homotopy equivalence):

\displaystyle\Phi:\mathcal{V}(A)

\displaystyle\to\mathcal{P}(A)

\displaystyle[x]

\displaystyle\mapsto\left[p=\tfrac{1}{2}(1-x|x|^{-1})\right]

This map is well-defined, since if
x\sim x^{\prime}
then also the corresponding projections
p,p^{\prime}
are homotopy equivalent. It also maps the neutral element
0=[e]
to
0=[0]\in\mathcal{P}(A)
, whilst being compatible with direct sums. Let us check it is bijective.

For injectivity, let
[x],[x^{\prime}]\in\mathcal{V}(A)
be mapped to
[p],[p^{\prime}]
and assume that
[p]=[p^{\prime}]
. The homotopy
p_{t}
of projections (
t\in[0,1]
) that implements the equivalence between
p_{0}=p,p_{1}=p^{\prime}
can be written as
p_{t}=\frac{1}{2}(1-y_{t})
in terms of a self-adjoint element
y_{t}
satisfying
y_{t}^{2}=1
, to wit
y_{t}=1-2p_{t}
. This a continuous family of hermitian forms such that
y_{0}=x|x|^{-1}
and
y_{1}=x^{\prime}|x^{\prime}|^{-1}
while also
x|x|^{-1}\sim x
via the homotopy
x|x|^{-t}
(
t\in[0,1]
) of hermitian forms, and the same applies to
x^{\prime}|x^{\prime}|^{-1}\sim x^{\prime}
. Hence
x\sim x^{\prime}
which proves that
\Phi
is injective.

Surjectivity follows by taking a
[p]\in\mathcal{P}(A)
and defines a hermitian form
x=1-2p
. Then
\Phi([x])=[p]
as desired.
∎

K_{0}
under stable isomorphism (in Section

The behavior of hermitian forms with respect to ucp maps as obtained in Proposition

This follows from the previous Proposition in combination with the fact that a unital complete order isomorphism

4.
Non-unital operator systems and stability of K-theory

4.1.
Non-unital operator systems

Recall
[
42
]
(
cf.

[
8
]
) that the partial unitization of a non-unital operator system
E
is given by the
*
-vector space
E^{\sharp}=E\oplus\mathbb{C}
with matrix order structure:

(x,A)\geq 0\text{ iff }A\geq 0\text{ and }\phi(A_{\varepsilon}^{-1/2}xA_{\varepsilon}^{-1/2})\geq-1

for all
\varepsilon>0
and noncommutative states
\phi\in\mathcal{S}_{n}(E)
, and where
A_{\varepsilon}=\varepsilon\mathbb{I}_{n}+A
. The matrix order units
e^{\sharp}_{n}
in
M_{n}(E^{\sharp})
are given by the identity matrices
\mathbb{I}_{n}\in M_{n}(\mathbb{C})
. This turns
(E^{\sharp},e^{\sharp})
into a unital operator system. Moreover, given a completely contractive completely positive map
\phi:E\to F
we have that the canonical extension
\phi^{\sharp}:E^{\sharp}\to F^{\sharp}
is a ucp map (
[
42
, Lemma 4.9]
) .

We now extend the definition of K-theory to non-unital operator systems as follows:

Definition 24
.

Let
E
be a non-unital operator systems and
E^{\sharp}
its unitization. We define the sets

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}(E,n)

\displaystyle:=\pi_{0}\left(\left\{(x,A)\in H(E^{\sharp},n):A\sim_{n}\mathbb{I}_{n}\right\}\right)

The Grothendieck group of the direct limit semigroup is denoted by
\widetilde{K}_{0}(E)
.

Proposition 25
.

Let
E
and
F
be operator systems. If
E
and
F
are completely isometric, completely order isomorphic, then
\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}(E)\cong\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}(F)
as semigroups. Consequently, in this case
\widetilde{K}_{0}(E)\cong\widetilde{K}_{0}(F)
.

Proof.

This follows from the fact that for a completely isometric, complete order isomomorphism
\phi:E\to F
the induced map
\phi^{\sharp}:E^{\sharp}\to F^{\sharp}
is a complete order isomorphism which furthermore respects the property
A\sim_{n}\mathbb{I}_{n}
.
∎

Let us also compare this with with our previous definition of K-theory in the case of
unital
operator systems.

Proposition 26
.

Let
E
be a unital operator system and let
E^{\sharp}
be its partial unitization. Then for any
n\geq 1
there are isomorphisms
\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}(E,n)\cong\mathcal{V}(E,n)
. Consequently, in this case
\widetilde{K}_{0}(E)\cong K_{0}(E)
.

Proof.

In the unital case, there is a unital complete order isomorphism between
E^{\sharp}
and
E\oplus\mathbb{C}
given by
(x,\lambda)\mapsto(x+\lambda e,\lambda)
, where we have equipped
E\oplus\mathbb{C}
with the induced direct sum order structure
[
42
, Lemma 4.9(b)]
. By Lemma
11
we have that
H(E\oplus\mathbb{C},n)=H(E,n)\times H(\mathbb{C},n)
from which it follows that

\left\{(x,A)\in H(E^{\sharp},n):A\sim_{n}\mathbb{I}_{n}\right\}\cong H(E,n)\times\left\{A\in H(\mathbb{C},n):A\sim_{n}\mathbb{I}_{n}\right\}

This isomorphism clearly respects direct sums and the unit, so that taking homotopy equivalence classes of this set of hermitian forms yields the statement. ∎

4.2.

One of the crucial features of

The maps

We then also have

\|(x,A)-(\kappa_{N,\infty}^{\sharp})^{(n)}(x_{0},A)\|<\varepsilon

so that if
(x,A)
has gap
g
and we choose
\varepsilon
small enough so that
g^{2}-\varepsilon\|x\|-\varepsilon^{2}/4>0
, then
(x,A)
and
(\kappa_{N,\infty}^{\sharp})^{(n)}(x_{0},A)
are homotopy equivalent by Corollary
14
. The element
(x_{0},A)
is the sought-for element in
\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}(M_{N}(E))\cong\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}(E)
that maps to
[(x,A)]
.
∎

Corollary 29
.

Let
E
and
F
be Morita equivalent unital operator systems. Then
K_{0}(E)\cong K_{0}(F)
.

Proof.

In
[
16
]
it is shown that
E
and
F
are Morita equivalent whenever
\mathcal{K}\otimes E\cong\mathcal{K}\otimes F
via a completely isometric, complete order isomorphism.
∎

5.
Application to the spectral localizer

In
[
28
,
27
]
the spectral localizer was introduced as a powerful tool for computing index pairings. They relate a certain Fredholm index to the signature of a finite-dimensional matrix —the so-called spectral localizer. We will put it in the context of our notion of K-theory, realizing the spectral localizer as a map
\mathcal{V}(E,n)\to\mathbb{Z}
. the spectral localizer, including its relation to spectral flow, we refer to the excellent textbook
[
14
]
and references therein.

In order to describe the index map on
\mathcal{V}(E,n)
, we start with an operator system spectral triple
[
8
]
.

Definition 30
.

A (unital) operator system spectral triple is given by a triple
(E,\mathcal{H},D)
where
E
is a unital operator system realized concretely so that
E\subseteq C^{*}_{\mathrm{env}}(E)\subseteq B(\mathcal{H})
, and a self-adjoint operator
D:\dom(D)\to\mathcal{H}
such that

•

the commutators
[D,x]
extend to bounded operators for all
x\in\mathcal{E}
for a dense
*
-subspace
\mathcal{E}\subseteq E
;

•

the resolvent
(i+D)^{-1}
is a compact operator.

An operator system spectral triple is called even if in addition to the above, there is a grading operator
\gamma
on
\mathcal{H}
(so that
\gamma^{*}=\gamma,\gamma^{2}=1_{\mathcal{H}}
) which commutes with all
x\in E
and anti-commutes with
D
. Otherwise, it is called odd.

In the even case, we can decompose
\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{H}_{+}\oplus\mathcal{H}_{-}
according to the eigenvalues of
\gamma
, and decompose accordingly

D=\begin{pmatrix}0&D_{0}\\
D_{0}^{*}&0\end{pmatrix}.

Given an even operator system spectral triple, a parameter
\kappa>0
and a hermitian form
x\in H(\mathcal{E},n)
we now define the
even spectral localizer

[
28
]
as

(6)

\displaystyle L_{\kappa}(D,x)

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}x&\kappa(D_{0})^{\oplus n}\\
\kappa(D_{0}^{*})^{\oplus n}&-x\end{pmatrix}.

Proposition 31
.

Let
(E,\mathcal{H},D)
be an even finite-dimensional operator system spectral triple.
Let
x\in H(\mathcal{E},n)
with gap
g>0
and let
\kappa_{0}=g^{2}\|[D,x]\|^{-1}
. Then the index map defined by the signature of the spectral localizer,

\ind_{D}([x])=\frac{1}{2}\Sig(L_{\kappa}(D,x)),

is constant for all
\kappa<\kappa_{0}
and invariant under homotopy equivalence. Consequently, it induces a map
\ind_{D}:\mathcal{V}(\mathcal{E},n)\to\mathbb{Z}
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality we take
n=1
and compute very similar to
[
28
]
that

(7)

L_{\kappa}(D,x)^{2}=\begin{pmatrix}\kappa^{2}D_{0}D_{0}^{*}+x^{2}&\kappa[D_{0},x]\\
\kappa[D_{0},x]^{*}&\kappa^{2}D_{0}^{*}D_{0}+x^{2}\end{pmatrix}\geq\left(g^{2}-\kappa\|[D,x]\|\right)1_{M_{2}(B(\mathcal{H}))}.

Hence
L_{\kappa}(D,x)
is invertible (and thus has well-defined signature) provided
\kappa<\kappa_{0}
and, moreover, the signature is constant for all
\kappa<\kappa_{0}
as no eigenvalues will cross the origin.

Also, one may easily check that

\Sig(L_{\kappa}(D,x\oplus x^{\prime}))=\Sig(L_{\kappa}(D,x))+\Sig(L_{\kappa}(D,x^{\prime})).

In order to see that
\Sig(L_{\kappa}(D,e))=0
consider an eigensystem
\{v_{\lambda}=(v_{\lambda}^{+},v^{-}_{\lambda})\}_{\lambda}
in
\mathcal{H}_{+}\oplus\mathcal{H}_{-}
such that
D_{0}v^{-}_{\lambda}=\lambda v^{+}_{\lambda}
and
\gamma v_{\lambda}^{\pm}=\pm v_{\lambda}^{\pm}
. We may write the matrix of
L_{\kappa}
in this eigensystem as

\langle v_{\lambda},L_{\kappa}(D,x)v_{\lambda^{\prime}}\rangle=\delta_{\lambda\lambda^{\prime}}\begin{pmatrix}1&\lambda\\
\lambda&-1\end{pmatrix}\sim\delta_{\lambda\lambda^{\prime}}\begin{pmatrix}\sqrt{1+\lambda^{2}}&0\\
0&-\sqrt{1+\lambda^{2}}\end{pmatrix}

and the signature of the latter matrix vanishes.

Consider now a homotopy
\widetilde{x}
in
H(E,n)
between
x=\widetilde{x}(0)
and
x^{\prime}=\widetilde{x}(1)
. We define
\kappa_{0}^{\prime}=g^{2}/\sup_{t}\|[D,\widetilde{x}(t)]\|
and note that for all
\kappa<\kappa_{0}^{\prime}

L_{\kappa}(D,\widetilde{x})^{2}\geq g^{2}-\kappa\sup_{t}\|[D,\widetilde{x}(t)]\|

by a computation similar to the one in Eq. (
7
). So as long as
\kappa<\kappa_{0}^{\prime}
we find that
\Sig L_{\kappa}(D,\widetilde{x}(t))
is constant in
t
. We combine this with the fact that
\kappa<\kappa_{0}^{\prime}<\kappa_{0}
to conclude that
\Sig L_{\kappa}(D,\widetilde{x}(0))=\Sig L_{\kappa}(D,\widetilde{x}(1))
. This completes the proof.
∎

We may now rephrase the main results of
[
28
]
. For an invertible self-adjoint element
x\in A
one considers the class
[p=\frac{1}{2}(1-x|x|^{-1})]
in the K-theory of the
C^{*}
-algebra
A
. Given a spectral triple
(A,\mathcal{H},D)
one would like to compute the index
\ind pDp
. As shown in
loc.cit.
this index may be computed in terms of a spectrally truncated (operator system) spectral triple
(PAP,P\mathcal{H},PDP)
for a spectral projection
P
(of
D
) of sufficiently high rank. Indeed, we then have

(8)

\ind(pDp)=\ind_{PDP}([PxP])

where the map on the right-hand side is the index map on
\mathcal{V}(PAP,n)
that appeared in Proposition
31
.

5.1.
Example: spectral localizer on the torus

Let us illustrate the spectral localizer for a spectrally truncated two-torus. First, recall from
[
29
]
the class of projections on the torus (
cf.

[
40
, Chapter 6]
, much inspired by the so-called Powers–Rieffel projections on the noncommutative torus
[
35
]
:

(9)

p=\begin{pmatrix}f&g+hU^{*}\\
g+hU&1-f\end{pmatrix}\in M_{2}(C(\mathbb{T}^{2})),

where
f,g,h
are real-valued (periodic) functions of the first variable
t_{1}
, and
U
is a unitary depending only on the second variable
t_{2}
, say
U(t_{2})=e^{imt_{2}}
. The projection property
p^{2}=p
translates into the two conditions

gh=0,\qquad g^{2}+h^{2}=f-f^{2}.

A possible solution of these relations is given by

0\leq f\leq 1\quad\text{such that }f(0)=1,\quad f(\pi)=0,

and then
g=\chi_{[0,\pi]}\sqrt{f-f^{2}}
and
h=\chi_{[\pi,2\pi]}\sqrt{f-f^{2}}
, where
\chi_{X}
is the indicator function for the set
X
(see Figure
1
).

Figure 1
.
Functions
f,g,h
that ensure that
p
in (
9
) is a projection.

The Dirac operator on the two-torus is defined on the core
C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{2})\otimes\mathbb{C}^{2}
by

D=\begin{pmatrix}0&D_{0}\\
D_{0}^{*}&0\end{pmatrix};\qquad D_{0}=i\partial_{t_{1}}+\partial_{t_{2}}.

The corresponding spectrum is
\left\{\pm\sqrt{n_{1}^{2}+n_{2}^{2}}\right\}_{n_{1},n_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}}
.

Let us now consider a spectral projection corresponding to a (discrete) ball of radius
\rho
,
i.e.

P_{\rho}=\chi(|D|\leq\rho)
. We consider the corresponding spectral compression of
Y=1-2p
,
i.e.

P_{\rho}YP_{\rho}=\begin{pmatrix}P_{\rho}-2P_{\rho}fP_{\rho}&-2P_{\rho}gP_{\rho}-2P_{\rho}hUP_{\rho}\\
-2P_{\rho}gP_{\rho}-2P_{\rho}hU^{*}P_{\rho}&-P_{\rho}+2P_{\rho}fP_{\rho}\end{pmatrix}\in M_{2}(P_{\rho}C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{2})P_{\rho})

For suitable
P_{\rho}
these are hermitian forms in the truncated operator system, that is,
P_{\rho}YP_{\rho}\in\mathcal{V}(P_{\rho}C(\mathbb{T}^{2})P_{\rho},2)
.

.

The spectral localizer is now given by the following matrix:

L_{\kappa,\rho}:=L_{\kappa}(P_{\rho}DP_{\rho},P_{\rho}YP_{\rho})=\begin{pmatrix}P_{\rho}YP_{\rho}&\kappa P_{\rho}D_{0}P_{\rho}\\
\kappa P_{\rho}D_{0}^{*}P_{\rho}&-P_{\rho}YP_{\rho}\end{pmatrix}

The main result of
[
28
]
(
cf.
Eq. (
8

\rho,\kappa
the index of

can be expressed in terms of the signature of the above spectral localizer. More precisely, in terms of the above index map on
\mathcal{V}(P_{\rho}C(\mathbb{T}^{2})P_{\rho},2)
we have:

\ind(pDp)=\ind_{P_{\rho}DP_{\rho}}([P_{\rho}YP_{\rho}])\equiv\frac{1}{2}\text{Sig}~L_{\kappa,\rho}

In Figure

we illustrate the resulting signature by showing the negative and positive eigenvalues of

. We find already for low

m

pDp
.

6.
Outlook

We have proposed a generalization of K-theory for
C^{*}
-algebras to operator systems, which in the spirit of Witt was based on hermitian forms. Several aspects are still to be developed, and which we leave for future research. This includes a careful study of the functorial properties of
K_{0}
and, in particular, how
K_{0}
behaves with respect to approximations results. For instance for approximations of
C^{*}
-algebra by finite-dimensional algebras in
[
6
,
10
]
where the maps are approximately multiplicative, or approximately order-zero cpc maps. Or in the context of quantum metric spaces where it turns out that small Hausdorff distance between two such spaces —the state spaces of two
C^{*}
-algebras
A,B
— allows one to relate projections in
A
to projections in
B

[
36
,
37
]
.

Another open problem is to identify the higher K-groups, starting with
K_{1}
. Again, one needs to find a analogue of the assumption of being unitary. However, just demanding invertibility appears too weak; one can easily show that the set of all invertible elements in a finite-dimensional operator system is contractible —arguing much as in the case of
GL(n,\mathbb{C})
. Nevertheless, from the odd spectral localizer
[
27
]
we know that some index-theoretic information is maintained after spectrally truncating a unitary. Again this fact will be leading in the development of
K_{1}
, which we also leave for future research. This extends of course to finding an analogue of Bott periodicity.

Dually, one may be interested also in defining K-homology for operator systems. Note here that the notion of a Fredholm module makes perfect sense for unital operator systems realized concretely in Hilbert space, but that this immediately gives rise to a Fredholm module for the pertinent
C^{*}
-extension. The task is thus to find the right notion of equivalence.

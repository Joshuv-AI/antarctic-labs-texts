Tunneling in multi-site mesoscopic quantum Hall circuits
D. B. Karki

Abstract
Transport properties of the single- and two-site mesoscoipc quantum Hall (QH) circuits at high transparencies can be described in terms of the lowest-order backscattering perturbations, and mapping to the boundary sine-Gordon model can be exploited in full generality. While the higher-order backscattering processes are exactly marginal in the case of corresponding three-site circuits, they become crucial in a device with four or more sites. Here, we explore the transport properties of a multi-site QH circuit with special focus on that with four sites, and report their unique quantum critical behaviors that can be accessed via transport measurements. Tunneling phenomena in multichannel QH circuits based on multi-site geometry are also investigated, and a promising route to realizing different aspects of quantum critical phenomena is offered.



I
Introduction

In a typical low-dimensional quantum system, the interplay among strong electron interactions, quantum interferences, and different degeneracies generally gives rise to a number strongly correlated phenomena
Giamarchi 2003
. Manifestations of these effects can also be studied using a well-controllable mesoscopic device, called the single-electron transistor
Kastner 1992
. It can be realized as a mesoscopic quantum Hall (QH) circuit consisting of a floating metal grain coupled to one-dimensional QH edge channels
Matveev 1991
;
Averin and Likharev 1991
;
Averin and Nazarov 1992
. This metal-semiconductor hybrid structure can exhibit various quantum effects such as Coulomb blockade, Kondo effect, charge and spin fractionalization, Luttinger liquid behavior and quantum phase transition
Matveev 1991
;
Flensberg 1993
;
Matveev 1995
;
Furusaki and Matveev 1995
;
Goldhaber-Gordon et al. 1998
;
Iftikhar et al. 2015
;
Anthore et al. 2018
;
Iftikhar et al. 2018
;
Lee et al. 2020
. These effects constitute a paradigm in the field of strongly correlated electron physics.

A number of remarkable phenomena have been predicted to emerge in a two-site QH circuit comprising
coupled hybrid metal-semiconductor islands due to the competition between the screening of each individual grain charge and the mediated charge coupling between the two grains
Pouse et al. 2023
;
Karki et al. 2022
, i.e., interplay of local and nonlocal correlation effects (see also Refs.
Jones and Varma 1989
;
Georges and Sengupta 1995
;
Matveev et al. 1996
;
Lee et al. 2010
). Such competition also results in local parafermionic excitation
Karki et al. 2023
. We note that, unlike the Majoranas (that could be realized in a single-site QH circuit
Emery and Kivelson 1992
;
Furusaki and Matveev 1995
), parafermions are much more complex, since they cannot arise in non-interacting models, being the result of strong correlation effects
Hutter and Loss 2016
. Therefore, the recent experiment
Pouse et al. 2023
and theoretical predictions
Karki et al. 2022
;
Karki et al. 2023
open coveted pathways to further understand and manipulate the exotic parafermionic state of matter using a highly-tunable nanoelectronic device without topological orders, and also to realizing different aspects of zero-temperature quantum critical phenomena
Karki 2024
.

One of the promising research directions is then to scale up such
hybrid structures to form a lattice that could provide different routes to constructing more complex Fibonacci anyons by leveraging the exotic features of generalized
coupled-impurities models. These multi-site QH circuits can also provide a useful way of understanding the effects of disorder on QH edges due to QH puddle formation
Stäbler and Sukhorukov 2022
(see also Ref.
Glattli et al. 2025
). In addition, they show some novel aspects of heat transport properties
Karki 2020
;
Kiselev 2023
;
Stäbler et al. 2025
;
Roche et al. 2025
;
Hurvitz et al. 2025
;
Parafilo 2025
. It is important to note that owing to having infinitely large numbers of electrons in
the metal islands, each grain in the chain, essentially behaves identical to each other. The well-defined QH
edge states implemented via quantum point contacts in the metal-semiconductor hybrid structures also provide unprecedented tuning of the device. Therefore, multi-site QH circuits could be used to simulate different model quantum systems of strongly correlated physics, i.e. they could capture the essential features of a real material.

It is well-understood that the combined effects of a dynamical Coulomb blockade and integer QH effect generate a form of a Luttinger liquid (LL)
Safi and Saleur 2004
. A single channel QH circuit consisting of
N
grains can be described by an equivalent model of an impurity in LL with the Luttinger parameter
K=1/(N+1)
, or a fractional QH effect with filling fraction
\nu=K

Karki et al. 2022
. As long as there are no tunneling/backscattering events in the system, all the properties of multi-site QH circuits can be achieved just by the usual circuit analysis
Slobodeniuk et al. 2013
.

In the case of a finite backscattering between counter-propagating edge states forming the circuit, the nature of relevant perturbations depends on the typical value of
\nu
. In the standard problem of tunneling through a weak barrier represented by a potential scatterer
\mathcal{V}(x)
localized in the vicinity of
x=0
, the relevant boundary perturbations correspond to the process of backscattering of
n
electrons from one Fermi point to the other by momenta
2k_{\rm F}

Kane and Fisher 1992
;
Furusaki and Nagaosa 1994
. These perturbations are also called
\mathcal{V}(2nk_{\rm F}),\;n=1,2,\cdots
, scattering processes. For all values of
\nu
, the
\mathcal{V}(2k_{\rm F})
scattering is always relevant in the renormalization group (RG) sense, and all higher order scatterings that satisfy
n^{2}\nu<1
are also relevant perturbation
Kane and Fisher 1992
. This shows that only the single-site
N=1
, and two-site
N=2
QH circuits can fully be described by the lowest order perturbation
\mathcal{V}(2k_{\rm F})
where mapping to the boundary sine-Gordon model can be exploited in full generality
Fendley et al. 1995a
;
Fendley et al. 1995b
. For
N=3
, the
\mathcal{V}(4k_{\rm F})
process are exactly marginal, and just introduce trivial renormalization effects. Therefore, to study the transport properties of multi-site QH circuits at large transparencies, it is crucial to also consider the higher order perturbation for
N\geq 4
. The same thing is also true even for
N=1
and
N=2
but with the fractional QH edges described by Laughlin state with filling factor
\nu=1/m
for
m
being an odd integer
Laughlin 1983
.

This work explores the transport properties of a four-site QH circuit
1
as a prototypical setup where higher order backscattering process are the relevant perturbations (increasing more sites does not introduce fundamentally new effects). We identify the associated zero temperature quantum critical point by uncovering the parameter space in gate voltages and contact resistances at which all the relevant backscattering processes interfere destructively, giving maximal conductance irrespective of temperature and/or voltage. We also evaluate the differential conductance of the device at two different limits, and discuss their scaling behaviors in the vicinity of quantum critical point. We introduce tunneling phenomena in the multichannel, multi-site mesoscopic QH circuit, and analyze their quantum critical behaviors. An experimentally accessible route to realize numerous singular phenomena in strongly correlated system is proposed. Additionally, the effects of Joules heating on the explored devices are uncovered.

This paper is organized as follows. In Sec.
II
we present our bosonization-based model to describe the multi-site QH circuits. An exact solution of the model in the absence of backscattering events is given in Sec.
III
. Section
IV

V

VI

VII
, we introduce the multichannel, multi-site mesoscopic QH circuits and analyze their quantum critical behaviors. A brief discussion about the Joules heating effects in the explored devices is given in Sec.
VIII
. Finally, we conclude in Sec.
IX

II
Model

The schematic of the experimental setup, which consists of four hybrid metal-semiconductor islands, is shown in Fig.
1
. Each island hosts a macroscopically-large number of charge states that can be tuned by applying voltage on the corresponding nearby gates (green metallic plates in the figure). The islands are connected to each other and also to the metallic leads by fully tunable quantum point contacts (QPCs) represented by the red dot signs in the figure. The free electrons in the QPCs can be modeled as pairs of counter-propagating QH edges which are partially covered by the metallic islands. Electron propagation through these chiral edge states can conveniently be studied by bosonizing fermion operators
von Delft and Schoeller 1998
. In this representation, the spinless electrons in five
QPCs can be described by the quadratic Hamiltonian of the form
Wen 1992

\displaystyle H_{0}=\frac{v_{\rm F}}{4\pi}\sum_{\alpha=1}^{5}\int^{\infty}_{-\infty}\!\!\!dx\Big[\left(\partial_{x}\Phi_{\alpha,{\rm R}}\right)^{2}+\left(\partial_{x}\Phi_{\alpha,{\rm L}}\right)^{2}\Big],

(1)

where
\alpha
is the QPCs number index,
v_{\rm F}
stands for the Fermi velocity.
\Phi_{\alpha,{\rm L/R}}(x,t)
represents the bosonic field corresponding to the left/right moving chiral fermions, and satisfies the commutation relation
\left[\Phi_{\rm\alpha,R/L}(t),\Phi_{\rm\alpha,R/L}(t_{1})\right]=\pm i\pi{\rm sign}(t-t_{1})
. Notice that throughout the paper we set
e=\hbar=k_{\rm B}=1
.

The Coulomb interactions in the system of four islands having the same charging energy
E_{C}
can be accounted for by the constant interaction model
Furusaki and Matveev 1995

\displaystyle H_{C}=E_{C}\sum_{j=1}^{4}\left[\hat{N}_{j}-N_{j,\rm g}\right]^{2},

(2)

where
j
is the islands number index, and the symbol
N_{j,\rm g}
stands for the gate voltage applied to the
j
th grain. The electron number operator of the
j
th island is given, in terms of the bosonic fields defined at
x=0
, by

\displaystyle\hat{N}_{j}(t)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\left[\Phi_{j,\rm L}-\Phi_{j,\rm R}+\Phi_{j+1,\rm R}-\Phi_{\rm j+1,\rm L}\right].

(3)

We note that in the setup
1
, there is indeed an appreciable amount of inter-island Coulomb interactions. The cross capacitance facilitating the charge-charge coupling on the nearest islands, however, does not allow the DC current to follow. Therefore, as long as we are interested in low-energy charge transport, such charge-charge couplings can be safely neglected as far as we are far from the regime of possible phase transition
Karki 2025
.

The interaction Hamiltonian (
2
) is quadratic in bosonic fields, and thus can be solved exactly. In addition, if the system can fully be described by equations (
1
) and (
2
), the gate voltages can be gouged out as the system looses charge granularity. Small but finite backscattering events mediated by QPCs principally change this picture. Such events result in charge granularity and introduce strongly non-linear boundary interactions of the form
H_{\rm b}=\sum_{\alpha}\sum_{n}\mathcal{V}(2nk_{F})\cos\left[n\left(\Phi_{\alpha,\rm R}-\Phi_{\alpha,\rm L}\right)\right]
. For the low-energy description of the setup comprising of
N
islands, the QPC index
\alpha
takes the values
\alpha=1,2,\cdots,N+1
, and the integer
n=1,2,\cdots\lfloor\sqrt{N+1}\rfloor
. Therefore, the backscattering in the QPCs of the device (
1
), i.e.,
N=4
, is accounted for by

\displaystyle H_{\rm b}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha=1}^{5}\Big[U_{\alpha}\cos\left(\Phi_{\alpha,\rm R}-\Phi_{\alpha,\rm L}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+V_{\alpha}\cos 2\left(\Phi_{\alpha,\rm R}-\Phi_{\alpha,\rm L}\right)\Big],

(4)

where
U_{\alpha}\propto\mathcal{V}(2k_{F})
,
V_{\alpha}\propto\mathcal{V}(4k_{F})
and they depend on the band cutoff
D
in a linear and a quartic manner respectively. In the following, we solve the interaction Hamiltonian (
2
) exactly, and take into account the non-linearities (
4
) perturbatively in small backscattering coefficients
Furusaki and Matveev 1995
.

III
Exact account of the interactions

In the absence of backscattering events, the continuous flow of chiral fermions along the QH edges described by the Hamiltonian
H_{\rm p}=H_{0}+H_{C}
represents the plasmonic excitations traveling along the chiral edges. The latter can be accounted for by the standard equation of motion approach
Slobodeniuk et al. 2013
;
Sukhorukov 2016
;
Morel et al. 2022
. This allows one to express all the bosonic fields
\Phi_{\alpha,\rm R/L}(x,\omega)
in terms of corresponding free fields
\Phi^{0}_{\alpha,\rm R/L}(x,\omega)
satisfying the well-known commutation relations and have standard correlators

\displaystyle\left<\!\Phi^{(0)}_{\alpha,{\rm R/L}}(\omega)\Phi^{(0)}_{\beta,{\rm R/L}}(\omega^{\prime})\!\right>_{\!\!0}\!{=}\frac{4\pi^{2}\delta_{\alpha\beta}}{\omega^{\prime}}n\!\left(\!\!\frac{\omega^{\prime}}{T}\!\right)\!\!\delta\!\left(\omega+\omega^{\prime}\right)\!,

(5)

with
n(y)=\left(e^{y}-1\right)^{-1}
being Bose distribution function defined at the equilibrium temperature
T
.

To study the transport properties, it is more convenient to introduce the compact bosonic fields
\Phi_{\alpha}\equiv\Phi_{\alpha,\rm R}-\Phi_{\alpha,\rm L}
, and corresponding free fields
\Phi^{0}_{\alpha}
. It is straightforward to show, by solving the equation of motion for the Hamiltonian
H^{\prime}\equiv H_{0}+H_{C}
, that the matrix
\Phi=\left(\Phi_{1},\Phi_{2},\Phi_{3},\Phi_{4},\Phi_{5}\right)^{\top}
satisfies

\displaystyle\Phi(\omega)=\mathbb{A}(\omega)\Phi^{0}(\omega)+\mathbb{N}(\omega),

(6)

for some matrix
\mathbb{A}
depending on the interaction strength and number of edge channels. The matrix
\mathbb{N}
accounting for the gate voltages takes the form

\displaystyle\mathbb{N}(t)=\frac{2\pi}{5}\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}-4&-3&-2&-1\\
1&-3&-2&-1\\
1&2&-2&-1\\
1&2&3&-1\\
1&2&3&4\\
\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}N_{1,\rm g}\\
N_{2,\rm g}\\
N_{3,\rm g}\\
N_{4,\rm g}\\
N_{5,\rm g}\\
\end{array}\right).

To express the equation (
6
) into a more meaningful form, we introduce an orthogonal transformation that affects the free fields
\Phi_{\alpha}^{0}
such that

\displaystyle\left(\Phi^{0}_{1},\Phi^{0}_{2},\Phi^{0}_{3},\Phi^{0}_{4},\Phi^{0}_{5}\right)^{\top}=\mathbb{O}\left(\Phi_{a},\Phi_{b},\Phi_{c},\Phi_{d},\Phi_{e}\right)^{\top},

(17)

with an orthogonal matrix

\displaystyle\mathbb{O}=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccccc}\frac{1}{\sqrt{5}}&\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}&\frac{1}{\sqrt{6}}&0&-\sqrt{\frac{2}{15}}\\
\frac{1}{\sqrt{5}}&-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}&\frac{1}{\sqrt{6}}&0&-\sqrt{\frac{2}{15}}\\
\frac{1}{\sqrt{5}}&0&-\sqrt{\frac{2}{3}}&0&-\sqrt{\frac{2}{15}}\\
\frac{1}{\sqrt{5}}&0&0&\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}&\sqrt{\frac{3}{10}}\\
\frac{1}{\sqrt{5}}&0&0&-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}&\sqrt{\frac{3}{10}}\\
\end{array}\right).

Using equations (
6
) and (
17
), we express the original bosonic fields
\Phi_{\alpha}
in terms of the new free fields
\Phi_{l}
,

, as

\displaystyle\Phi(\omega)=\mathbb{M}(\omega)\left(\Phi_{a},\Phi_{b},\Phi_{c},\Phi_{d},\Phi_{e}\right)^{\top}+\mathbb{N},

(23)

The full expression of the matrix
\mathbb{M}(\omega)
is provided in the appendix
A
, and in the limit
\omega\tau\to 0
, where
\tau\equiv\pi/E_{C}
is the usual Heisenberg time constant, it takes the form

\displaystyle\lim_{\omega\tau\to 0}\mathbb{M}_{\alpha\beta}(\omega)=\frac{\delta_{1\beta}}{\sqrt{5}}.

(24)

From the equations (
23
) and (
24
), it is seen that out of five chiral bosonic modes, only one mode,
\Phi_{a}
remains gapless, and the other four are gapped by large charging energy. For the low-energy description of the problem, the gapped modes can be integrated out, and thus the dynamics are governed solely by the free mode
\Phi_{a}
. The equation (
24

4
) are
1/5
and
4/5
respectively, and thus both are relevant perturbations in RG sense.

IV
Effective low-energy Hamiltonian

By integrating out the gapped modes in the equation (
23
), we express the backscattering Hamiltonian (
4
) in terms of a single free mode
\Phi_{a}
. This procedure is elaborated in the appendix
B
, and the final result for the effective Hamiltonian of the four-site QH circuit writes

\displaystyle H_{\rm eff}=

\displaystyle\frac{v_{\rm F}}{4\pi}\int^{\infty}_{-\infty}\!\!\!dx\Big[\left(\partial_{x}\Phi_{a,{\rm R}}\right)^{2}+\left(\partial_{x}\Phi_{a,{\rm L}}\right)^{2}\Big]

\displaystyle+|r_{u}|D^{1/5}\cos\left(\frac{\Phi_{a}}{\sqrt{5}}+\theta_{u}\right)

\displaystyle+|r_{v}|D^{4/5}\cos\left(\frac{2}{\sqrt{5}}\Phi_{a}+\theta_{v}\right),

(25)

where
\theta_{u/v}
are some unimportant phase factors. In the case of common gate voltage, i.e.
N_{j,\rm g}=N_{\rm g}
, the effective reflection coefficients
|r_{u/v}|
acquire the form

\displaystyle r_{u}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{5^{3/16}e^{\gamma}}{\tau}\right)^{4/5}\!\!\Big[\mathcal{C}_{1}\mathsf{U}_{1}e^{-4\pi iN_{\rm g}}+\mathcal{C}_{2}\mathsf{U}_{2}e^{-2\pi iN_{\rm g}}

\displaystyle\qquad+\mathcal{C}_{3}\mathsf{U}_{3}+\mathcal{C}_{2}\mathsf{U}_{4}e^{2\pi iN_{\rm g}}+\mathcal{C}_{1}\mathsf{U}_{5}e^{4\pi iN_{\rm g}}\Big],

\displaystyle r_{v}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{5^{3/16}e^{\gamma}}{\tau}\right)^{16/5}\Big[\mathcal{D}_{1}\mathsf{V}_{1}e^{-8\pi iN_{\rm g}}+\mathcal{D}_{2}\mathsf{V}_{2}e^{-4\pi iN_{\rm g}}

\displaystyle\qquad+\mathcal{D}_{3}\mathsf{V}_{3}+\mathcal{D}_{2}\mathsf{V}_{4}e^{4\pi iN_{\rm g}}+\mathcal{D}_{1}\mathsf{V}_{5}e^{8\pi iN_{\rm g}}\Big],

(26)

where
\mathcal{C}_{j}
and
\mathcal{D}_{j}
are numerical coefficients as a result of integrating out the gapped bosonic modes, and also contain the proportionality factor of the renormalized backscattering strengths
\mathsf{U}_{j}\propto U_{j}
and
\mathsf{V}_{j}\propto V_{j}
, see appendix
B
for the details. The symbol
\gamma
stands for the Euler’s constant. In the following section, we analyze the quantum critical features of the effective Hamiltonian (

V
Quantum critical behaviors

At the quantum critical point (a point in the parameter space of the gate voltage and barrier transparencies), all backscattering processes interfere destructively to nullify the backscattering terms in the Hamiltonian (
25
). At this point, the unitary conductance can be reached (see below). We note that since both backscattering terms are relevant, one is required to find proper parameters to nullify both
|r_{u}|
and
|r_{v}|
. Although there are several parameters in the equation (
25
) that can be tunned experimentally, in the following we discuss a few representative cases.

First consider the situation where only the leftmost and rightmost QPCs, i.e., the 1st and 5th, are in quasi-ballistics regime with equal reflection amplitudes
\mathsf{U}_{1}=\mathsf{U}_{5}
and
\mathsf{V}_{1}=\mathsf{V}_{5}
. Since all the inner QPCs are fully open (
\mathsf{U}_{2,3,4}=0=\mathsf{V}_{2,3,4}
), the effective reflection coefficients take the form
|r_{u}|\propto\cos 4\pi N_{\rm g}
and
|r_{v}|\propto\cos 8\pi N_{\rm g}
. Due to the doubling of the gate voltage periodicity in these coefficients, they never vanish simultaneously at any gate voltage. This special realization of the device
1
thus does not support a zero-temperature quantum critical point, i.e., only the perturbative regime would exist, and the dominant scaling behaviors of transport and thermodynamic quantities would be identified by comparing the relative strengths of two corrections in equation (
25
).

Now we discuss the left-right symmetric realization of the device
1
, namely the barrier transparencies are chosen such that
\mathcal{C}_{1}\mathsf{U}_{1}=\mathcal{C}_{2}\mathsf{U}_{2}=\mathcal{C}_{4}\mathsf{U}_{4}=\mathcal{C}_{5}\mathsf{U}_{5}
and
\mathcal{C}_{3}\mathsf{U}_{3}/\mathcal{C}_{1}\mathsf{U}_{1}=\delta_{u}
, and similarly
\mathcal{D}_{1}\mathsf{V}_{1}=\mathcal{D}_{2}\mathsf{V}_{2}=\mathcal{D}_{4}\mathsf{V}_{4}=\mathcal{D}_{5}\mathsf{V}_{5}
and
\mathcal{D}_{3}\mathsf{V}_{3}/\mathcal{D}_{1}\mathsf{V}_{1}=\delta_{v}
. In this case, we have
|r_{u}|\propto\left[\delta_{u}+2\cos 2\pi N_{\rm g}+2\cos 4\pi N_{\rm g}\right]
and
|r_{v}|\propto\left[\delta_{v}+2\cos 4\pi N_{\rm g}+2\cos 8\pi N_{\rm g}\right]
. In this case, the parameter space
\left(\delta_{u},\delta_{v},N_{\rm g}\right)
forming the critical point exists. As an example, for a given value of
\delta_{u}
, the fine tunned gate voltage

\displaystyle N_{\rm g}^{*}=\frac{\tan^{-1}\left[\frac{1}{4}\left(\sqrt{9{-}4\delta_{u}}{-}1\right),\frac{\sqrt{2\delta_{u}+\sqrt{9-4\delta_{u}}+3}}{2\sqrt{2}}\right]}{2\pi},

(27)

and
\delta_{v}
such that

\displaystyle\delta^{*}_{v}=3\alpha+\sqrt{9-4\alpha}-1-\alpha^{2}-\alpha\sqrt{9-4\alpha},

(28)

constitute one of the quantum critical points. As seen from equation (
27
), the critical regime emerges only for
0<\delta_{u}\leq 9/4
, and for larger
\delta_{u}
different critical points meet and nullify each other. One can numerically solve the zero condition of reflection coefficients given in equation (
26
) to get all the features of critical points for general value of barrier transparencies and five gate voltages. In the following, we show that these features can be accessed via transport measurements.

VI
Charge current

Here we evaluate the charge current measured at the right QPC (QPC 5th) described by the operator
\hat{I}=-\frac{1}{2\pi}\frac{\partial\Phi_{5}}{\partial t}
. As discussed earlier, all the fields are a function of
\Phi_{a}
and since the field
\Phi_{a}
is only the gapless mode, the application of the voltage bias

\Phi_{a}\to\Phi_{a}-\left(Vt/\sqrt{5}\right)

\displaystyle I=-\frac{1}{2\pi}\left<\frac{\partial\Phi_{5}}{\partial t}\right>=\frac{1}{5}\frac{V}{2\pi},

(29)

as just equivalent to the conductance corresponding to a setup with five quantum resistors (with resistors

) in series.

\displaystyle\delta I

\displaystyle={-}\int^{t}_{-\infty}\!\!\!\!dt_{1}\!\!\int^{t_{1}}_{-\infty}\!\!\!dt_{2}\!\left<\!\Big[H_{\rm eff}(t_{2}),\!\left[H_{\rm eff}(t_{1}),\hat{I}(t)\right]\!\Big]\!\right>.

Since the two backscattering sectors in equation (

\displaystyle I=\frac{1}{5}\frac{V}{2\pi}\Bigg(1{-}\left[\frac{\pi^{2}5^{3/5}|r_{u}|^{2}}{V^{8/5}\Gamma\left(\frac{2}{5}\right)}{+}\frac{2\pi^{2}\left(\frac{2}{5}\right)^{3/5}|r_{v}|^{2}}{V^{2/5}\Gamma\left(\frac{8}{5}\right)}\right]\Bigg),

Here we briefly discuss the situation where the rightmost QPC in Fig.

VII
Multi-channel mesoscopic quantum Hall circuits

In the previous sections, we studied the QH circuits where each grain accommodates just a single channel to the left and also to the right of it. It is also possible to engineer a mesoscopic QH circuit having multiple edge channels passing through each metal grain
Karki 2024
. The fully ballistic limit of such multichannel QH circuits can be understood as follows. A portion of QH circuit outside the metal grain that consists of left and right moving edges can be described by a chiral bosonic field. Therefore, for the setup with
N

For the clarity of presentation, we call the portion of the circuit to the left of the first island as the left part, that between the two grains as the middle part and that right of the second grain as the right part. Exotic quantum critical points in multi-channel QH circuits

As long as there is more than one free mode describing the low energy dynamics, there is no relevance of mapping to the boundary sine-Gordon model. Nonetheless, we show below, following Ref.
Karki 2024
, that in the multichannel QH circuits the number of free modes describing the effective Hamiltonian can always be reduced to one by looping the desired number of edge channels. This method allows realizing zero-temperature quantum critical phenomena characterized by general scaling dimension
\eta<1/2
, where
\eta
depends on the number of grains and that of looped edge channels.

To illustrate the looping method, we consider a simples setup as shown in Fig.
3

3

Stäbler and Sukhorukov 2023

Therefore, if both channels in the middle part are fully ballistic, they only provide the impedances to the incoming electrons via left and right QPCs,
the whole setup becomes similar to that with a single grain equipped with two QPCs. The crucial difference here is that the number of connecting ballistic channels provides an extra impedance and thus controls the scaling dimension of the electron operators.

Following the equation of motion approach to account for the charging energy part of the Hamiltonian, one can express all bosonic fields in terms of incoming fields. The fields

VIII
Heating effect in a multi-site QH circuit

In section
VI
, we also presented our results for the transport properties of the QH circuit
1
in a fully non-equilibrium regime driven by the voltage bias. There we neglected the effects coming from the heating of the grains by injected voltage, namely the Joules heat
J_{V}=V^{2}/2R_{q}
carried by each incoming chiral edge due to the corresponding voltage drop
V
. Likewise, at a given temperature
T
, the heat current carried by a free chiral edge described by the bosonic field
\Phi^{0}
takes the form

\displaystyle J=\frac{R_{q}}{2(2\pi)^{4}}\int^{\infty}_{-\infty}d\omega\omega^{2}\left<\Phi^{0}(\omega)\Phi^{0}(-\omega)\right>,

(70)

Using the expression of the free correlator given in equation (
5

and

Due to the heating and subsequent colling effects on top of a complicated charging energy profile, the grains in general acquire a non-equilibrium temperature profile that can be predicted by solving the full kinetic equation

;
Cailleaux et al. 2025

T
in the setup

The latter problem would then be identical to that of a short quantum wire connected to the leads having finite charging energies, and is known to have different physics than the usual QH circuits.

IX
Conclusion

We explored the transport properties of multi-site QH circuits, with special focus on the prototypical four-site setup where higher-order backscattering processes are the relevant perturbations. We showed that the consideration of such a higher order process is crucial in order to correctly identify the quantum critical features of the corresponding model. The critical properties are shown to be directly imprinted in the charge current flowing through the circuit in response to applied voltage bias. We investigated tunneling phenomena in the multichannel, multi-site mesoscopic QH circuits by explicitly evaluating their transport properties and verified their robust non-Fermi-liquid behaviors. We proposed a coveted pathway, based on looping the QH edge channels, to realize exotic zero temperature critical phenomena in multi-site QH circuits. We showed that the proposed method can realize numerous critical exponents by adjusting the number of grains and that of the looped edge channels. The effects of Joules heating in the explored devices are also uncovered.

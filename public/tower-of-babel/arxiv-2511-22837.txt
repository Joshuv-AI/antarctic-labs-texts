Plumbings of lens spaces and crepant resolutions of compound A n A_{n} singularities
Bilun Xie, Yin Li

Abstract
We prove that the completed derived wrapped Fukaya categories of certain affine A n A_{n} plumbings W ̊ f \ring{W}_{f} of 3 3 -dimensional lens spaces along circles are equivalent to the derived categories of coherent sheaves on crepant resolutions of the corresponding compound A n A_{n} ( c ​ A n cA_{n} ) singularities ℂ ⁡ [ [ u , v , x , y ] ] / ( u ​ v − f ⁡ ( x , y ) ) \mathbb{C}[\![u,v,x,y]\!]/(uv-f(x,y)) . The proof relies on the verification of a conjecture of Lekili-Segal [ 32 ] . After localization, we obtain an equivalence between the derived wrapped Fukaya category of the (non-affine) A n A_{n} plumbing W f ⊃ W ̊ f W_{f}\supset\ring{W}_{f} of lens spaces along circles and the relative singularity category of the crepant resolution. This generalizes the result of Smith-Wemyss [ 48 ] and partially answers their realization question. As a consequence, we obtain a faithful representation of the pure braid group 𝑃𝐵𝑟 n + 1 \mathit{PBr}_{n+1} on the graded symplectic mapping class group of W f W_{f} when the corresponding c ​ A n cA_{n} singularity is isolated.

2.1
Plumbings of cotangent bundles

Fix two closed smooth
n
-dimensional manifolds
Q_{1}
and
Q_{2}
which intersect along a smooth
k
-dimensional submanifold
Z=Q_{1}\cap Q_{2}
. Given any identification

\eta:\nu_{Z/Q_{1}}\xrightarrow{\simeq}\nu_{Z/Q_{2}}

(2.1)

between the normal bundles of
Z
, we can construct a Stein manifold
W_{\eta}=T^{\ast}Q_{1}\#_{Z,\eta}T^{\ast}Q_{2}
, called the
plumbing
of the cotangent bundles of
Q_{1}
and
Q_{2}
along
Z
with respect to the identification
\eta
. More precisely, let
D^{\ast}Q_{i}
,
i=1,2
be the disc cotangent bundles, then by the Weinstein neighborhood theorem there exist open neighborhoods
U(Z)_{i}
of
D^{\ast}Z\subset D^{\ast}Q_{i}
, which are symplectomorphic to disc subbundles of
\nu_{Z/Q_{i}}\times\mathbb{C}
over
Z
. Using the identification

\eta\otimes\sqrt{-1}:U(Z)_{1}\xrightarrow{\simeq}U(Z)_{2},

(2.2)

we can glue the Weinstein neighborhoods together to obtain a Stein domain
\overline{W}_{\eta}
. The plumbing
W_{\eta}
is the completion of
\overline{W}_{\eta}
. Repeating this construction, one can construct plumbings of cotangent bundles of cleanly intersecting smooth manifolds according to any plumbing diagram.

When
Q_{1}
and
Q_{2}
in the above are two closed
3
-manifolds intersecting cleanly along a circle
Z=Q_{1}\cap Q_{2}\cong S^{1}
, then since the normal bundles
\nu_{Z/Q_{1}}
and
\nu_{Z/Q_{2}}
are trivial, the possible isomorphisms (
2.1
) form a torsor for
\mathbb{Z}
, and we may therefore write
\eta=k\in\mathbb{Z}
.

More specifically, if
Q_{1}\cong Q_{2}\cong S^{3}
, it is proved in
[
48
]
, Lemma 2.4 that any one of the two Morse-Bott surgery
K\subset T^{\ast}Q_{1}\#_{Z,k}T^{\ast}Q_{2}
of
Q_{1}
and
Q_{2}
along
Z
satisfies
H^{\ast}(K;\mathbb{Z})\cong H^{\ast}(S^{1}\times S^{2};\mathbb{Z})
when
k=0
and
H^{\ast}(K;\mathbb{Z})\cong\mathbb{Z}/|k|
when
k\neq 0
. In particular, when
Z
is an unknot in both
Q_{1}
and
Q_{2}
, then
K\subset W_{k}
is diffeomorphic either to
S^{1}\times S^{2}
when
k=0
or a lens space
L(|k|,1)
when
k\neq 0
. The same argument as in the proof of
[
48
]
, Lemma 2.4 actually extends to the case when
Q_{1}
and
Q_{2}
are lens spaces, and we have the following.

Proposition 13
.

Let
Q_{1}
and
Q_{2}
be lens spaces, then their Morse-Bott surgery
K
of
Q_{1}
and
Q_{2}
in the plumbing
T^{\ast}Q_{1}\#_{Z,k}T^{\ast}Q_{2}
is either a homology
S^{1}\times S^{2}
or a homology lens space.

Proof.

For the reader’s convenience, we briefly sketch the argument. Using the surgery exact triangle associated to clean intersections
[
36
]
, we see that in the Fukaya category
\mathcal{F}(T^{\ast}Q_{1}\#_{Z,k}T^{\ast}Q_{2};\mathbb{Z})
, there is a quasi-isomorphism

K\simeq\mathit{Cone}(Q_{1}\xrightarrow{\kappa}Q_{2})

(2.3)

between the Morse-Bott surgery
K
of
Q_{1}
and
Q_{2}
and the mapping cone associated to the morphism
\kappa\in\mathit{HF}^{1}(Q_{1},Q_{2})
which is a multiple of the unique degree
1
generator corresponding to the fundamental class of
Z
. Since the mapping cone is indecomposable,
\kappa\in\mathit{HF}^{1}(Q_{1},Q_{2})
is primitive. Since
K
is exact, the endomorphism space
\hom_{\mathcal{F}}(K,K)
of
\mathit{Cone}(Q_{1}\xrightarrow{\kappa}Q_{2})
in the Fukaya category
\mathcal{F}(T^{\ast}Q_{1}\#_{Z,k}T^{\ast}Q_{2};\mathbb{Z})
is isomorphic to
H^{\ast}(K;\mathbb{Z})
, so we only need to compute
\hom_{\mathcal{F}}(K,K)
, which is determined by the Massey product
\mu^{3}(e,f,e)
in a minimal model of the Fukaya
A_{\infty}
-algebra
\bigoplus_{1\leq i,j\leq 2}\mathit{CF}^{\ast}(Q_{i},Q_{j})
over
\mathbb{Z}
, where
e\in\mathit{HF}^{1}(Q_{1},Q_{2})
and
f\in\mathit{HF}^{2}(Q_{2},Q_{1})
are generators. Since
\mu^{3}(e,f,e)=\lambda\cdot f^{\vee}
for some
\lambda\in\mathbb{Z}
and
f^{\vee}\in\mathit{HF}^{2}(Q_{1},Q_{2})
, the result follows.
∎

In general, the Morse-Bott surgery
K
of two lens spaces
Q_{1}
and
Q_{2}
can fail to be a lens space. However, as we will see blow, this is not going to happen for the specific examples treated in this paper.

We now restrict ourselves to the specific situation of
\ring{W}_{f}
, which is defined as the total space of a Morse-Bott fibration
\ring{\pi}:\ring{W}_{f}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{\ast}
, with data specified by the factorization (
1.4
). We equip it with a plurisubhmaronic function
\rho:\ring{W}_{f}\rightarrow(0,\infty)
. To define it, we start with the function

\rho_{F}(z_{1},z_{2})=|z_{1}|^{2}+|z_{2}|^{2}+\frac{1}{|z_{1}|^{2}}+\frac{1}{|z_{2}|^{2}}

(2.4)

on a smooth fiber direction of
\ring{\pi}
, where
z_{1},z_{2}
are complex coordinates on
(\mathbb{C}^{\ast})^{2}
. In fact, one can view the Morse-Bott fibration
\ring{\pi}:\ring{W}_{f}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{\ast}
as a fiber connected sums of
n+1
elementary Morse-Bott fibrations
\pi_{i}:E_{i}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
,
i=0,\cdots,n
, such that each
\pi_{i}
has a unique singular fiber over
c_{i}
. After taking the completion, each
\pi_{i}
is equivalent to the standard fibration
\mathbb{C}^{2}\times\mathbb{C}^{\ast}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
given by
xy
, where
(x,y,z)\in\mathbb{C}^{2}\times\mathbb{C}^{\ast}
are complex coordinates. Since the coordinate
z_{1}
corresponds to
xy
, while
z_{2}
corresponds to
z
, it is clear that the function
\rho_{F}
can be extended to the singular fiber of
\pi_{i}
, giving a function on
E_{i}
. One can then use the biholomorphic coordinate transformations between different
E_{i}
’s to get a function on
\ring{W}_{f}
, which we still denote by
\rho_{F}
by abuse of notations. Define
\rho_{B}:\mathbb{C}^{\ast}\rightarrow(0,\infty)
to be the function
2\left(|z_{3}|^{2}+\frac{1}{|z_{3}|^{2}}\right)
, where
z_{3}
is the complex coordinate on
\mathbb{C}^{\ast}
and the constant
2
is added for computational convenience. The plurisubharmonic function
\rho:\ring{W}_{f}\rightarrow(0,\infty)
is defined as

\rho=\rho_{F}+\ring{\pi}^{\ast}\rho_{B}.

(2.5)

We equip
\ring{W}_{f}
with the Kähler form
\omega_{\ring{W}}:=-dd^{\mathbb{C}}\rho
. Let
Z
be the Liouville vector field that is dual to the
1
-form
\theta_{\ring{W}}=-d^{\mathbb{C}}\rho
, and denote by
\phi_{Z}^{t}
its time-
t
flow. Recall that the
skeleton
of
\ring{W}_{f}
is the subset
\mathrm{Sk}(\ring{W}_{f}):=\bigcap_{t>0}\phi_{Z}^{-t}(\overline{\ring{W}}_{f})
, where
\overline{\ring{W}}_{f}\subset\ring{W}_{f}
is the corresponding Liouville domain. Since
\ring{W}_{f}
is Weinstein, it is a Lagrangian subset.

Lemma 14
.

Symplectic parallel transport is globally defined for paths away from critical values of
\ring{\pi}:\ring{W}_{f}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{\ast}
, and it is compatible with the Hamiltonian
T^{2}
-action rotating the fibers
\ring{\pi}
.

Proof.

This is similar to
[
48
]
, Lemma 4.1, see also Lemma 4.5 loc. cit. The global parallel transport is obtained by viewing
\ring{W}_{f}
as a fiber sum of elementary Morse-Bott fibrations
\pi_{i}:E_{i}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
and patching together locally defined parallel transport maps, which are
T^{2}
-equivariant. Here we use the fact that the monodromy of
\ring{\pi}
around the origin is trivial.
∎

The following is a generalization of
[
30
]
, Lemma 3.3.

Lemma 15
.

The Weinstein manifold
\ring{W}_{f}
is symplectically equivalent to the affine
A_{n}
plumbing of the cotangent bundles
T^{\ast}Q_{0},\cdots,T^{\ast}Q_{n}
along circles.

Proof.

The structure of the Morse-Bott fibration
\ring{\pi}:\ring{W}_{f}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{\ast}
induces in a natural way the splitting of the Liouville vector field
Z=Z_{F}+Z_{B}
away from the critical loci of
\ring{\pi}
.

When restricted to a smooth fiber
F
of
\ring{\pi}
, the vector field
Z_{F}
is dual to the Liouville
1
-form
i^{\ast}\theta_{\ring{W}}
, where
i:F\hookrightarrow\ring{W}_{f}
is the inclusion of the fiber. By our construction of the Kähler potential
\rho
, it follows that

i^{\ast}\theta_{\ring{W}}=-i^{\ast}\left(d^{\mathbb{C}}\left(|z_{1}|^{2}+|z_{2}|^{2}+\frac{1}{|z_{1}|^{2}}+\frac{1}{|z_{2}|^{2}}\right)\right).

(2.6)

Thus
Z_{F}
vanishes whenever
|z_{1}|=|z_{2}|=1
, and its flow scales
\log|z_{i}|
over positive time for

Symplectic parallel transport is well-defined by Lemma
14

T^{2}
-action on

|z_{1}|
and

which does not satisfy

To determine

.

is the pullback of a symplectic form

on the base

restricted to

\rho(z_{3})=2\sum_{i=0}^{n}|z_{3}-c_{i}|+2\left(|z_{3}|^{2}+\frac{1}{|z_{3}|^{2}}\right).

(2.7)

On
\left\{|z_{1}|=|z_{2}|=1\right\}

is the gradient vector field of

with respect to the restriction of

to

. This is the same as the pullback of the gradient flow of
\rho(z_{3})
on
\mathbb{C}^{\ast}
with respect to the metric associated to

. See Figure
2

when

Recall that the

is the plumbing of

along circles.
∎

Remark 16

The circle

.

The Weinstein manifold

2.2

In this subsection we work in the algebraic setting, as the story in the complete local setting is similar. Consider the
cA_{n}

R_{f}=\frac{\mathbb{K}[u,v,x,y]}{uv-f(x,y)},

(2.9)

where

is defined as in (

to be an arbitrary field. When

.

Lemma 18
.

Assume that (
1.8
) holds, so that . The affine variety
\mathrm{Spec}(R_{f})
admits a specific crepant resolution
\phi:Y_{f}\rightarrow\mathrm{Spec}(R_{f})
containing a chain of rational curves
C_{1},\cdots,C_{n}
such that
C_{i}\cap C_{i+1}
transversely at a unique point for

and

if

is either a
(-1,-1)
-curve or a
(0,-2)
-curve.

Proof.

One can argue similarly as in
[

]
, Lemma 3.1. In our case, however, we shall use in addition the fact that

\varphi_{k}:Z_{k}\rightarrow\mathrm{Spec}\left(\frac{\mathbb{K}[u,v,x,y]}{uv-x(x+y^{k})}\right),

(2.10)

which is the blow-up of the ideal
(u,x)

-curve as the exceptional locus when

, regardless of

. This is proved in the same way as in the case

, which is done in
[

\varphi_{k}
is obtained by blowing up the same ideal.

Without loss of generality, we can assume

. To construct the specific crepant resolution
\phi
, we first blow up the ideal
(u,f_{0})
to obtain
\phi_{1}:Y_{1}\rightarrow\mathrm{Spec}(R_{f})
, where the variety

is covered by two open charts

\mathrm{Spec}\left(\mathbb{K}[u,x,S]\right)\textrm{ and }\mathrm{Spec}\left(\frac{\mathbb{K}[v,x,y,T]}{\left(vT-f_{1}(x,y)\cdots f_{n}(x,y)\right)}\right),

(2.11)

where

and

patch to define a
\mathbb{P}^{1}\subset Y_{1}
. Since
\mathrm{Spec}(R_{f})
is normal, Zariski’s main theorem combined with Čech cohomology calculations show that the morphism
\phi_{0}
is crepant, i.e.
R(\phi_{0})_{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{Y_{1}}=\mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{Spec}(R_{f})}
.

One then blow up the ideals
(u,f_{i})
for
i=1,\cdots,{n-1}
to get a sequence of birational morphisms

(2.12)

whose composition
\phi=\phi_{n}\circ\cdots\circ\phi_{1}:Y_{f}\rightarrow\mathrm{Spec}(R_{f})
gives the desired crepant resolution of the
cA_{n}
singularity

. Note that the crepancy of
\phi
follows from the crepancy of each individual birational morphism
\phi_{i}
, where

, which can be proved using Zariski’s main theorem and Čech cohomology calculations.

The partial resolution
Y_{n-1}
is covered by two affine charts

\mathrm{Spec}\left(\mathbb{K}[u,x,S^{\prime}]\right)\textrm{ and }\mathrm{Spec}\left(\frac{\mathbb{K}[v,x,y,T^{\prime}]}{vT^{\prime}-f_{n-1}(x,y)f_{n}(x,y)}\right),

(2.13)

where

and

. In
Y_{n-1}
, the coordinates
S^{\prime}
and

patch together to an exceptional curve

, which passes through the node
\left\{vT=f_{n-1}(x,y)f_{n}(x,y)\right\}
in the second chart. Arguing by induction, we see that in the full resolution

we have a chain of transversely intersecting
\mathbb{P}^{1}

C_{1},\cdots,C_{n}
.

To find the normal bundles of
C_{1},\cdots,C_{n}\subset Y_{f}
, note that the last of which,

, comes from resolution of the

singularity
\frac{\mathbb{K}[v,x,y,T^{\prime}]}{vT^{\prime}-f_{n-1}(x,y)f_{n}(x,y)}
, which is isolated by our assumption. Since any isolated

for some

,

is a

-curve when

-curve when

.
∎

Remark 19
.

Taking into account the data on the mirror side, one can prove that the exceptional curve

-curve if the corresponding spherical Lagrangian submanifold

For simplicity and for our applications in mind (cf. Proposition

is isolated. When it isn’t isolated, since in our case the singularity is of a specific form, a similar argument as above still holds and produces a crepant resolution

(cf. the

2.3
Seidel representation

Let
\mathbb{K}
be an arbitrary field. For a closed symplectic manifold
X
satisfying certain monotonicity conditions, Seidel defined in
[
40
]
a group homomorphism

\mathcal{R}_{X}:\pi_{1}\left(\mathit{Ham}(X)\right)\rightarrow\mathit{QH}^{0}(X;\mathbb{K})^{\times}

(2.14)

from the fundamental group of the space Hamiltonian diffeomorphisms of
X
to invertible elements in even degree quantum cohomology. The map
\mathcal{R}_{X}
is known as the
Seidel representation
, and it defined by counting holomorphic sections of a symplectic fiber bundle
E\rightarrow S^{2}
constructed by patching together two copies of trivial bundles
D^{2}\times X
, whose transition function is determined by some fixed element
g\in\pi_{1}\left(\mathit{Ham}(X)\right)
. An element
\mathcal{R}_{X}(g)\in\mathit{QH}^{0}(X;\mathbb{K})^{\times}
is known as a
Seidel element
associated to
g
.

There is an analogue of the map
\mathcal{R}_{X}
above for Liouville manifolds, whose construction is due to Oh-Tanaka
[
38
]
, which takes the form

\mathcal{R}_{M}:\pi_{1}\left(\mathit{Aut}(M)\right)\rightarrow\mathit{SH}^{0}(M;\mathbb{K})^{\times},

(2.15)

where
\mathit{Aut}(M)
is the topological group of graded Liouville automorphisms of
M
. Instead of mimicking Seidel’s original construction,
[
38
]
produces a coherent action of
\mathit{Aut}(M)
on the wrapped Fukaya category
\mathcal{W}(M;\mathbb{K})
via the study of the universal Liouville bundle
E\mathit{Aut}(M)\rightarrow B\mathit{Aut}(M)
, therefore simultaneously produces higher homotopical analogues of the map
\mathcal{R}_{M}
. As above, associated to each
g\in\pi_{1}\left(\mathit{Aut}(M)\right)
there is a Seidel element
\mathcal{R}_{M}(g)\in\mathit{SH}^{0}(M;\mathbb{K})^{\times}
.

2.4
Legendrian surgery

To give the reader a better understanding of the main result (Theorem
3
) proved in this paper, we briefly recall here the notion of a completed Chekanov-Eliashberg dg algebra introduced by Ekholm-Lekili
[
17
]
. We work in the slightly more general set up when the skeleton consists of Lagrangian submanifolds intersecting with each other cleanly.

Recall that any
2n
-dimensional Weinstein manifold
W
can be constructed from a subcritical Weinstein domain
\overline{W}_{0}
, by attaching Weinstein handles of index
n
along the contact boundary
\partial\overline{W}_{0}
along a link of Legendrian spheres. Fix a finite set
\Gamma
. For each
v\in\Gamma
, let
\overline{Q}_{v}\subset\overline{W}_{0}
be an oriented connected Lagrangian submanifold whose boundary
\partial\overline{Q}_{v}\subset\partial\overline{W}_{0}
gives a Legendrian sphere
\Lambda_{v}
. For simplicity, we also assume that
\overline{Q}_{v}
is
Spin
for each
v\in\Gamma
, which is always true for our purposes since in this paper we are dealing with Weinstein
6
-manifolds. Moreover, we assume that the Lagrangian submanifolds
\{\overline{Q}_{v}\}_{v}\in\Gamma
intersect with each other cleanly, and the intersections are contained in the interior of
\overline{W}_{0}
. It follows that
\Lambda=\bigsqcup_{v\in\gamma}\Lambda_{v}
is a link of Legendrian spheres in
\partial\overline{W}_{0}
. Attaching
n
-handles to
\overline{W}_{0}
along
\Lambda
, we get a Weinstein domain
\overline{W}
. The wraped Fukaya category
\mathcal{W}(W;\mathbb{K})
can be computed through Legendrian surgery
[
10
]
. In fact, let
\mathcal{R}
be the set of Reeb chords with ends on
\Lambda
. As a
\mathbb{K}
-module, the Chekanov-Eliashberg dg algebra of
\Lambda\subset\partial\overline{W}_{0}
is given by

\mathit{CE}^{\ast}(\Lambda):=\bigoplus_{i=0}^{\infty}\mathbb{K}\langle\mathcal{R}\rangle^{\otimes i}.

(2.16)

The differential on
\mathit{CE}^{\ast}(\Lambda)
counts anchored holomorphic disks with boundary punctures in
\mathbb{R}\times\partial\overline{W}_{0}
. The boundary components of these holomorphic curves are mapped to
\mathbb{R}\times\Lambda
, and asymptotics at the punctures give by the Reeb chords in
\mathcal{R}
, see
[
10
]
, Section 4.1. It is clear that
\mathit{CE}^{\ast}(\Lambda)
is a dg algebra over the semisimple ring
\Bbbk:=\bigoplus_{v\in\Gamma}\mathbb{K}e_{v}
, with
e_{w}\mathcal{R}e_{v}
being the set of Reeb chords from
\Lambda_{w}
to
\Lambda_{v}
. The Lagrangian filling
\bigcup_{v\in\Gamma}\overline{Q}_{v}
of the Legendrian link
\Lambda
induces an augmentation

\varepsilon_{Q}:\mathit{CE}^{\ast}(\Lambda)\rightarrow\Bbbk,

(2.17)

making it an augmented dg algebra.

Let
\{L_{v}\}_{v\in\Gamma}
be the Lagrangian cocore discs of the Weinstein
n
-handles attached along
\Lambda\subset\partial\overline{W}_{0}
, which generate the wrapped Fukaya category
\mathcal{W}(W;\mathbb{K})

[
11
,
21
]
, so there is an equivalence

D^{\mathit{perf}}\mathcal{W}(W;\mathbb{K})\simeq D^{\mathit{perf}}(\mathcal{W}_{W})

(2.18)

between the derived wrapped Fukaya category and the derived category of perfect modules over the wrapped Fukaya
A_{\infty}
-algebra

\mathcal{W}_{W}:=\bigoplus_{v,w\in\Gamma}\mathit{CW}^{\ast}(L_{v},L_{w}).

(2.19)

There is a surgery quasi-isomorphism
[
10
,
17
]

\mathcal{W}_{W}\simeq\mathit{CE}^{\ast}(\Lambda)

(2.20)

between
A_{\infty}
-algebras over
\Bbbk
, which reduces the computation of the wrapped Fukaya category to the analysis of Morse flow trees
[
16
]
.

Equivalently, we can write
\mathit{CE}^{\ast}(\Lambda)
as

\mathit{CE}^{\ast}(\Lambda)=\Bbbk\oplus\bigoplus_{i=1}^{\infty}\overline{\mathit{LC}}_{\ast}(\Lambda)[-1]^{\otimes_{\Bbbk}i},

(2.21)

where by
\mathit{LC}_{\ast}(\Lambda)
we mean the graded
\Bbbk
-bimodule generated by
\mathcal{R}
, with grading shifted down by
1
, and
\overline{\mathit{LC}}_{\ast}(\Lambda)\subset\mathit{LC}_{\ast}(\Lambda)
is the submodule without the idempotents. Using the same holomorphic curves as in the definition of the differential on
\mathit{CE}^{\ast}(\Lambda)
and the augmentation
\varepsilon_{Q}
, one can equip
\mathit{LC}_{\ast}(\Lambda)
with the structure of an
A_{\infty}
-coalgebra. Now the dg algebra
\mathit{CE}^{\ast}(\Lambda)
can be written as a cobar construction

\mathit{CE}^{\ast}(\Lambda)=\Omega\mathit{LC}_{\ast}(\Lambda).

(2.22)

Define the
Legendrian
A_{\infty}
-algebra
of
\Lambda
to be the graded
\Bbbk
-linear dual

\mathit{LA}^{\ast}(\Lambda):=\mathit{LC}_{\ast}(\Lambda)^{\#}.

(2.23)

Let
Q_{v}\subset W
be the closed exact Lagrangian submanifold obtained as the union of
\overline{Q}_{v}
and the Lagrangian core discs of the corresponding Weinstein
n
-handle. Consider the Fukaya
A_{\infty}
-algebra

\mathcal{Q}_{W}:=\bigoplus_{v,w\in\Gamma}\mathit{CF}^{\ast}(Q_{v},Q_{w}),

(2.24)

which is also augmented by the trivial projection to the idempotents.

Proposition 20
.

There is a quasi-isomorphism
\mathcal{Q}_{W}\simeq\mathit{LA}^{\ast}(\Lambda)
between
A_{\infty}
-algebras over
\Bbbk
.

Proof.

This is essentially the same as
[
17
]
, Theorem 63, except that in our case the Lagrangian submanifolds
\{Q_{v}\}_{v\in\Gamma}
are allowed to have clean, instead of only transverse intersections. When the intersections are transverse, an
A_{\infty}
-morphism
\varphi:\mathcal{Q}_{W}\rightarrow\mathit{LA}^{\ast}(\Lambda)
can be defined by counting holomorphic discs with
m+1
boundary punctures so that the boundary components are mapped to parallel copies of
\bigcup_{v\in\Gamma}Q_{v}
, such that the

\mathcal{R}

∎

Combining (

where in the above

However, it is in general not true that the bar construction

of the graded linear dual of the Legendrian

4.3
Braid group actions

In this subsection, we discuss the affine pure braid group action on the derived wrapped Fukaya category
D^{\mathit{perf}}\mathcal{W}(\ring{W}_{f};\mathbb{K})
and prove that its additive counterpart, namely the pure braid group action on the graded symplectic mapping class group of
W_{f}
, is faithful.

Recall that the
affine pure braid group

\mathit{PBr}_{n+1}^{\mathit{aff}}
on
n+1
strands is generated by
\sigma_{0},\cdots,\sigma_{n},\rho
subject to the relations

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}=\sigma_{j}\sigma_{i}&\textrm{for }|i-j|\geq 2,\\
\sigma_{i}\sigma_{i+1}\sigma_{i}=\sigma_{i+1}\sigma_{i}\sigma_{i+1}&\textrm{for }i=0,\cdots,n,\\
\rho\sigma_{i}\rho^{-1}=\sigma_{i+1}&\textrm{for }i=0,\cdots,n,\end{array}\right.

(4.42)

where the indices are understood to be modulo
n+1
. Note that
\mathit{PBr}_{n+1}^{\mathit{aff}}
contains the ordinary pure braid group
\mathit{PBr}_{n+1}
generated by
\sigma_{0},\cdots,\sigma_{n}
as a subgroup, and it is itself a subgroup of
\mathit{PBr}_{n+2}
.

In
[
15
]
, an action of the affine pure braid group
\mathit{PBr}_{n+1}^{\mathit{aff}}
on the derived relative wrapped Fukaya category
D^{\mathit{perf}}\mathcal{W}(T^{\ast}S^{1},D_{n})
is defined, where the generator
\rho\in\mathit{PBr}_{n+1}^{\mathit{aff}}
corresponds to a symplectomorphism which cyclically permutes the arcs
\gamma_{0},\cdots,\gamma_{n}\subset T^{\ast}S^{1}
up to Hamiltonian isotopy, and
\sigma_{i}
corresponds to the half twist around the arc connecting
c_{i-1\textrm{ mod }n+1}
to
c_{i}
for
i=0,\cdots,n
. See Figure
12
for a description of
\sigma_{0}(\gamma_{0})

from the affine pure braid group to the group of automorphisms of the triangulated category

4.4
Future work

It is proved by Keating-Smith
[
30
]
that the compactly supported symplectic mapping class group of plumbings of two
T^{\ast}S^{3}
’s along a Hopf link in Example
1
is infinitely generated. It would be interesting to know whether the same conclusion holds for all the Weinstein manifolds
\ring{W}_{f}
studied in this paper. Our results certainly open up the possibilities of the investigations in this direction.

It is also an interesting question whether
\ring{W}_{f}
admits a quasi-dilation
[
44
,
45
]
, namely a pair
(b,h)\in\mathit{SH}^{1}(\ring{W}_{f};\mathbb{K})\times\mathit{SH}^{0}(\ring{W}_{f};\mathbb{K})^{\times}
with
\Delta(b)=h
, where
\Delta
is the BV operator, or even a cyclic quasi-dilation
[
33
,
34
,
54
]
. A quasi-dilation for
\ring{W}_{f}
, once liftable over
\mathbb{Z}
, can be used to prove similar classification results of exact Lagrangians as those proved in
[
22
]
for affine conic bundles over
(\mathbb{C}^{\ast})^{2}
.

In
[
6
]
, a cyclic quasi-dilation over an arbitrary field
\mathbb{K}
is used to exclude the possible change of the knot types of
Z=Q_{1}\cap Q_{2}
in the double bubble plumbing
W_{k}
under Hamiltonian isotopies. Note that Theorem
6
combined with
[
24
]
, Theorem A actually implies the existence of a cyclic quasi-dilation for the
A_{n}
plumbing
W_{f}
when
(k_{0},\pm l_{0})\neq(k_{n},\pm l_{n})
. Combining with what we observed in Remark
35
, the same argument as
[
6
]
, Lemma 4.7 implies the following.

Proposition 38
.

Suppose
(k_{0},\pm l_{0})\neq(k_{n},\pm l_{n})
. Let
L\subset W_{f}
be a closed oriented exact Lagrangian submanifold, then
L
cannot be a
K(\pi,1)
space.

Proof.

Suppose
L\subset W_{f}
is an exact Lagrangian
K(\pi,1)
space. Arguing as in
[
6
]
, Lemma 4.7, we see that the existence of a cyclic quasi-dilation and the Viterbo functoriality applied to the Weinstein embedding
D^{\ast}L\hookrightarrow W_{f}
implies that there must be some non-trivial unit in
\mathit{SH}^{0}(W_{f};\mathbb{K})
that is not torsion. This contradicts with what we have observed in Remark
35
, which suggests that all units in
\mathit{SH}^{0}(W_{f};\mathbb{K})
are torsion.
∎

Comparing with
[
45
]
, Corollary 6.3, it is natural to expect that
W_{f}
actually admits a dilation, i.e. a class
b\in\mathit{SH}^{1}(W_{f};\mathbb{K})
with
\Delta(b)=1
, over a field
\mathbb{K}
of characteristic
0
.

One would also like to generalize the set up of this paper and allow both of the numbers
k_{i}
and
l_{i}
in the definition of vanishing cycles of the Morse-Bott fibration
\ring{\pi}:\ring{W}_{f}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{\ast}
to take values other than
1
, albeit with the requirement that
k_{i}
and
l_{i}
are coprime. See
[
7
]
, Example 4.5 for such a situation on the B-side. However, this seems to be out of the reach of our current calculation techniques described in Section
3
.

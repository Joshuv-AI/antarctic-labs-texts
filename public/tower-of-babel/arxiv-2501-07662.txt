Hermitian u u -invariants under quadratic field extensions
Karim Johannes Becher, Fatma Kader Bingöl

Abstract
. The hermitian u u -invariants of a central simple algebra with involution are studied. In this context, a new technique is obtained to give bounds for the behavior of these invariants under a quadratic field extension. This is applied to obtain bounds in terms of the index and the u u -invariant of the base field. Keywords: central simple algebra, involution, hermitian form, u u -invariant Classification (MSC 2020): 11E39, 12E15, 16K20



1.
Introduction

The concept of the
u
-invariant naturally extends from quadratic forms over fields to hermitian forms over central simple algebras with an involution.
This paper is devoted to the problem of bounding the hermitian
u
-invariants of central simple algebras of exponent
2
.
Recall that such algebras can be represented as a tensor product of a finite number of quaternion algebras.
In the presence of a suitable separable quadratic subfield, one can bound the hermitian
u
-invariant of a central simple algebra with involution in terms of the hermitian
u
-invariant of a subalgebra stable under the involution.
This relies on a method used in characteristic different from
2
by E. Bayer-Fluckiger’s and R. Parimala in the construction of an exact sequence of Witt groups of hermitian forms
[
1
, Appendix 2 and §3.1]
, which plays an essential role in their classification results for hermitian forms over central simple algebras with involution over fields of cohomological dimension
2
.
It is used later in
[
10
]
,
[
11
]
and
[
16
]
to obtain upper bounds for the hermitian
u
-invariants of a central simple algebra of exponent
2
in terms of the
u
-invariant of the base field.
In this article, we refine this method and apply it to study the behaviour of the hermitian
u
-invariants under
2
-extensions and in particular multiquadratic extensions.
In this way, we obtain bounds on the hermitian
u
-invariants of a central simple algebra with involution in terms of the degree and the
u
-invariant of a splitting
2
-extension (
Corollary

5.2
and
Corollary

6.4
).
For certain algebras with involution of small index, we obtain in
Theorem

5.5
and
Theorem

6.1
improvements to previously existing bounds.

We denote by
\mathbb{N}
the set of natural numbers including
0
and set
\mathbb{N}^{+}=\mathbb{N}\smallsetminus\{0\}
.

2.
Hermitian forms and involutions

Our main references are
[
7
]
for the theory of hermitian forms and
[
8
]
for the theory of algebras with involution.
The notions and basic facts from the theory of central simple algebras that we need are mostly covered by
[
14
, Chap. 8]
.

Throughout this article let
F
denote a field. Let
A
be a central simple
F
-algebra.
The
degree
,
index
and
exponent of
A
are denoted by
\operatorname{\mathsf{deg}}A
,
\operatorname{\mathsf{ind}}A
and
\operatorname{\mathsf{exp}}A
, respectively.
Given another central simple
F
-algebra
B
, we write
A\sim B
to indicate that
A
and
B
are Brauer equivalent.
By Wedderburn’s Theorem
[
14
, Chap. 8, Cor. 1.6]
, we have
A\simeq\mathbb{M}_{s}(D)
for a unique central
F
-division algebra
D
and a unique
s\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
.
Then
\operatorname{\mathsf{ind}}{A}=\operatorname{\mathsf{deg}}{D}
and
\operatorname{\mathsf{deg}}{A}=s\cdot\operatorname{\mathsf{ind}}{A}
. We say that
A
is
split
if
D=F
, or equivalently, if
\operatorname{\mathsf{ind}}A=1
.
By
[
14
, Chap. 8, Theorem 1.8]
, every finitely generated
A
-right module
V
decomposes into a direct sum of simple
A
-right modules.
The number of simple components in a decomposition of
V
as a direct sum of simple
A
-right modules is called the
rank of

V
(over
A
) and denoted by
\operatorname{\mathsf{rk}}_{A}V
.
It follows that
\operatorname{\mathsf{rk}}_{A}V=\frac{\operatorname{\mathsf{dim}}_{F}V}{s\cdot\operatorname{\mathsf{dim}}_{F}D}
.
In particular, we have
\operatorname{\mathsf{rk}}_{A}A=s
, and if
A
is a division algebra, then
\operatorname{\mathsf{rk}}_{A}V
is the dimension of
V
as an
A
-right vector space.
Given a field extension
K/F
, we obtain from
A
a central simple
K
-algebra
A_{K}=A\otimes_{F}K
.

An
involution on
a ring
A
is an anti-automorphism
\sigma:A\to A
such that
\sigma^{2}=\operatorname{\mathsf{id}}_{A}
.
If
A
is an
F
-algebra,
\sigma
is an involution on
A
, and
K/F
is a field extension, then
\sigma\otimes\operatorname{\mathsf{id}}_{K}
is an involution on
A_{K}=A\otimes_{F}K
, which we denote by
\sigma_{K}
.
If
K/F
is a quadratic étale extension (that is, either
K\simeq F\times F
or
K/F
is a separable quadratic field extension), then the non-trivial

F
-automorphism of
K
is an involution on
K
, which we call the
canonical involution of
K/F
and which we denote by
\operatorname{\mathsf{can}}_{K/F}
.

Let now
A
be a central simple
F
-algebra and
\sigma
an involution on
A
which is
F
-linear.
We fix a field extension
K/F
such that
A_{K}
is split.
Hence
A_{K}\simeq\operatorname{\mathsf{End}}_{K}V
for a
K
-vector space
V
with
\operatorname{\mathsf{dim}}_{K}V=\operatorname{\mathsf{deg}}A
, and under this isomorphism of
K
-algebras,
\sigma_{K}
corresponds to a
K
-linear involution on
\operatorname{\mathsf{End}}_{K}V
, which is adjoint to a (nonsingular) alternating or symmetric
K
-bilinear form
b
on
V
.
Moreover, whether
b
is alternating depends neither on the choice of the field extension
K/F
nor on the isomorphism; see
[
8
, Prop. 2.6]
.
We call the involution
\sigma

symplectic
if the bilinear form
b
is alternating, and we call it
orthogonal
otherwise.

For a ring
A
, we set
\mathsf{Z}(A)=\{x\in A\mid xy=yx\mbox{ for all }y\in A\}
, which is a subring called the
center of
A
.
By an
F
-algebra with involution
, we mean a pair
(A,\sigma)
where
A
is a finite-dimensional
F
-algebra and
\sigma
is an
F
-linear involution on
A
such that
F=\{x\in\mathsf{Z}(A)\mid\sigma(x)=x\}
and
A
has no non-trivial two-sided ideal
I
with
\sigma(I)=I
.
There are two kinds of situations for this to occur:

(1)

A
is a central simple
F
-algebra and
\sigma
is an
F
-linear involution.

(2)

K=\mathsf{Z}(A)
is a quadratic étale extension of
A
and
\sigma|_{K}
is an automorphism of order
2
of
K
with
F
as its fixed field.
In this case, either
K/F
is a separable quadratic field extension and
A
is a central simple
K
-algebra, or
K\simeq F\times F
and
A\simeq B\times B^{\operatorname{\mathsf{op}}}
for a central simple
F
-algebra
B
and where
B^{\operatorname{\mathsf{op}}}
denotes its opposite algebra (which coincides with
B
as a set), and
\sigma
corresponds to the so-called
switch map

\operatorname{\mathsf{sw}}_{B}
on
B\times B^{\operatorname{\mathsf{op}}}
, which is defined by
\operatorname{\mathsf{sw}}_{B}(b,b^{\prime})=(b^{\prime},b)
for
b,b^{\prime}\in B
.

If
K
is either a field or a product of two copies of a field, then an involution
\sigma
on a
K
-algebra

is called
unitary
if
\sigma|_{K}\neq\operatorname{\mathsf{id}}_{K}
, and this case
(A,\sigma)
is an
F
-algebra with involution for
F=\{x\in K\mid\sigma(x)=x\}
.
We then also call
\sigma
a
K/F
-unitary involution on
A
.
Note that, while the fixed field
F
in this situation is determined by
\sigma
, a central simple
K
-algebra
A
can have many unitary involutions
\sigma
with different fixed fields for
\sigma
, and this situation will also occur crucially in our study.

A unitary involution
\sigma
on an
F
-algebra
A
with
\mathsf{Z}(A)\simeq F\times F
is also called
unitary of inner type
.
(The term is motivated by a relation to a corresponding notion for linear algebraic groups.)
For any
F
-algebra with involution
(A,\sigma)
where
\sigma
is not unitary of inner type, we have that
K=\mathsf{Z}(A)
is a field,
K/F
is separable with
[K:F]\leqslant 2
and
A
is a central simple
K
-algebra.

We recall the following criteria for the existence of involutions on a central simple algebra.

Theorem 2.1

(Albert)
.

Let
A
be a central simple
F
-algebra. There exists an orthogonal involution on
A
if and only if
\operatorname{\mathsf{exp}}A\leqslant 2
.
There exists a symplectic involution on
A
if and only if
\operatorname{\mathsf{exp}}A\leqslant 2
and
\operatorname{\mathsf{deg}}A
is even.

Proof.

By
[
8
, Theorem 3.1]
, an
F
-linear involution on
A
exists if and only if
\operatorname{\mathsf{exp}}A\leqslant 2
, and using
[
8
, Cor. 2.8]
, the statement follows.
∎

For a separable quadratic field extension
K/F
and a central simple
K
-algebra
B
,
we denote by
\operatorname{\mathsf{cor}}_{K/F}B
the corestriction algebra of
B
with respect to
K/F
as defined in
[
5
, §8]
.

Theorem 2.2

(Albert-Riehm-Scharlau)
.

Let
K/F
be a separable quadratic field extension and let B be a central simple
K
-algebra.
There exists a
K/F
-unitary involution on
B
if and only if
\operatorname{\mathsf{cor}}_{K/F}B
is split.

Proof.

See
[
8
, Theorem 3.1]
.
∎

It follows in particular from
Theorem

2.1
and
Theorem

2.2
that, if a central simple algebra
A
has an involution
\sigma
, then every central simple algebra over
\mathsf{Z}(A)
which is Brauer equivalent to
A
carries an involution whose restriction to
\mathsf{Z}(A)
is the same as for
\sigma
.

In the sequel, let
K
be a field, let
B
be a central simple
K
-algebra, and let
\gamma
be an involution on
B
.
Then
F=\{x\in K\mid\gamma(x)=x\}
is a subfield of
K
, and we obtain that
(B,\gamma)
is an
F
-algebra with involution.

Let
V
be a finitely generated
B
-right module and
\varepsilon\in\{\pm 1\}
.
A bi-additive map
h:V\times V\to B
is called an
\varepsilon
-
hermitian form over

(B,\gamma)
if it satisfies the following:

•

h(v\alpha,w\beta)=\gamma(\alpha)h(v,w)\beta
for all
v,w\in V,\alpha,\beta\in B
,

•

h(w,v)=\varepsilon\gamma(h(v,w))
for all
v,w\in V
.

We also call
h
simply a
hermitian
(resp.
skew-hermitian
)
form
when
\varepsilon=1
(resp.
\varepsilon=-1
).
For an
\varepsilon
-hermitian form
h
over
(B,\gamma)
, we will denote by
\operatorname{\mathsf{rk}}h
the rank of the underlying
B
-right module
V
.
We refer to
[
7
, Chap. I, §2.2 & §3.4]
for the basic concepts of isometry (
\simeq
) and orthogonal sum (
\perp
) for
\varepsilon
-hermitian forms.

Consider an
\varepsilon
-hermitian form
h
over
(B,\gamma)
defined on the
B
-right module
V
.
For any
B
-right submodule
U
of
V
, the restriction of
h
to
U\times U
defines an
\varepsilon
-hermitian form over
(B,\gamma)
which we denote by
h|_{U}
.
A
B
-right submodule
U
of
V
is called
totally isotropic
(
with respect to
h
) if
h|_{U}
is the zero map.
The form
h
is
nonsingular
if for any
v\in V\smallsetminus\{0\}
there exists
w\in V
such that
h(v,w)=0
.
The form
h
is
isotropic
if there exists some
v\in V\smallsetminus\{0\}
such that
h(v,v)=0
, and
anisotropic
otherwise.
The form
h
is
hyperbolic
if it is nonsingular
and
V=U\oplus U^{\prime}
for two
B
-right submodules
U
and
U^{\prime}
which are totally isotropic with respect to
h
.
In particular, any non-trivial hyperbolic
\varepsilon
-hermitian form is isotropic.
If
\mathsf{char}K\neq 2
or
\gamma|_{K}\neq\operatorname{\mathsf{id}}_{K}
, then any rank-
2
nonsingular isotropic
\varepsilon
-hermitian form over
(B,\gamma)
is hyperbolic.

For a field extension
M/K
, we obtain a finitely generated
B_{M}
-right module
V_{M}=V\otimes_{K}M
and an
\varepsilon
-hermitian form
h_{M}:V_{M}\times V_{M}\to B_{M}
over
(B_{M},\gamma_{M})
given by
h_{M}(v\otimes\alpha,w\otimes\beta)=h(v,w)\otimes\alpha\beta
for
v,w\in V,\alpha,\beta\in M
.

Let
V
be a finitely generated
B
-right module. Let
h:V\times V\to B
be a nonsingular hermitian or skew-hermitian form over
(B,\gamma)
.
According to
[
8
, §4.A]
,

determines an involution
\operatorname{\mathsf{ad}}_{h}
on
\operatorname{\mathsf{End}}_{B}V
satisfying

h(v,f(w))=h(\operatorname{\mathsf{ad}}_{h}(f)(v),w)\quad\text{for all}\,v,w\in V\,\text{and all}\,f\in\operatorname{\mathsf{End}}_{B}V.

We call
\operatorname{\mathsf{ad}}_{h}
the
adjoint involution of

h
.
Viewing
K
naturally embedded into
\operatorname{\mathsf{End}}_{B}V
, we have that
\operatorname{\mathsf{ad}}_{h}|_{K}=\gamma|_{K}
.
All involutions on
\operatorname{\mathsf{End}}_{B}V
arise in this way from some nonsingular hermitian form.

Theorem 2.3
.

(a)

Assume that
\mathsf{char}K\neq 2
and
\gamma|_{K}=\operatorname{\mathsf{id}}_{K}
.
Then any
K
-linear involution
\sigma
on
\operatorname{\mathsf{End}}_{B}V
is the adjoint involution
\operatorname{\mathsf{ad}}_{h}
of some nonsingular hermitian or skew-hermitian form
h
over
(B,\gamma)
, which is unique up to a factor in
F^{\times}
.
Moreover, the involutions
\sigma
and
\gamma
are both orthogonal or both symplectic if
h
is hermitian, whereas precisely one of them is orthogonal and the other one symplectic if
h
is skew-hermitian.

(b)

Assume that
\gamma|_{K}\neq\operatorname{\mathsf{id}}_{K}
. Then any involution
\tau
on
\operatorname{\mathsf{End}}_{B}V
with
\tau|_{K}=\gamma|_{K}
is the adjoint involution
\operatorname{\mathsf{ad}}_{h}
for some nonsingular hermitian form
h
over
(B,\gamma)
, which is unique up to a factor in
F^{\times}
.

Proof.

See
[
8
, Theorem 4.2]
.
∎

We denote by
\operatorname{\mathsf{Ad}}_{B}(h)
the
F
-algebra with involution
(\operatorname{\mathsf{End}}_{B}V,\operatorname{\mathsf{ad}}_{h})
.

Let
(A,\sigma)
be an
F
-algebra with involution.
We say that
\sigma
(or
(A,\sigma)
) is
isotropic
if there exists an
a\in A\smallsetminus\{0\}
such that
\sigma(a)a=0
, and
anisotropic
otherwise.
If there exists an element
e\in A
such that
e^{2}=e
and
\sigma(e)=1-e
, then
\sigma
(or
(A,\sigma)
) is called
hyperbolic
.
In particular, any hyperbolic involution is isotropic.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
K
be a field,
D
a central
K
-division algebra and
\gamma
an involution on
D
.
Assume that
\mathsf{char}K\neq 2
or
\gamma
is unitary.
Let
V
be a finite-dimensional
D
-right vector space and
h:V\times V\to D
an
\varepsilon
-hermitian form over
(D,\gamma)
.
Then
\operatorname{\mathsf{Ad}}_{D}(h)
is isotropic (resp. hyperbolic) if and only if
h
is isotropic (resp. hyperbolic).

Proof.

The statement for hyperbolicity is given in
[
8
, Prop. 6.7]
. We prove the statement for isotropy.

We set
(A,\sigma)=(\operatorname{\mathsf{End}}_{D}V,\operatorname{\mathsf{ad}}_{h})
.
Assume that
(A,\sigma)
is isotropic. Then there exists some
f\in A\smallsetminus\{0\}
such that
\sigma(f)\circ f=0
.
Since
f\neq 0
, there exists
v\in V
with
f(v)\neq 0
. We have
h(f(v),f(v))=h((\sigma(f)\circ f)(v),v)=h(0,v)=0
. Thus
h
is isotropic.

Conversely, assume that
h
is isotropic. There exists some
v\in V\smallsetminus\{0\}
such that
h(v,v)=0
.
Since
h
is nonsingular, we can find a nonzero vector
w\in V
such that
h(v,w)\neq 0
.
Consider
f:V\to V,u\mapsto vh(u,w)
. Then
f\in\operatorname{\mathsf{End}}_{D}V
, and
f\neq 0
as
f(v)=vh(v,w)\neq 0
. We have that

\begin{split}h((\sigma(f)\circ f)(u),u^{\prime})&=h(f(u),f(u^{\prime}))=h(vh(u,w),vh(u^{\prime},w))\\
&=\gamma(h(u,w))h(v,v)h(u^{\prime},w)=0\end{split}

for any
u,u^{\prime}\in V
. Hence
\sigma(f)\circ f=0
. Therefore
(A,\sigma)
is isotropic.
∎

3.
Hermitian
u
-invariants

Let
K
be a field and
B
a central simple
K
-algebra.
Let
\varepsilon\in\{\pm 1\}
and let
\gamma
be an involution on
B
.
Following
[
12
, Chap. 9, Definition 2.4]
, we set

u(B,\gamma,\varepsilon)=\mathsf{sup}\{\operatorname{\mathsf{rk}}h\mid\text{$h$ anisotropic $\varepsilon$-hermitian form over}\,(B,\gamma)\}\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}\,,

and we call
u(B,\gamma,\varepsilon)
the
\varepsilon
-
hermitian
u
-invariant
of
(B,\gamma)
.

For later use, we recall a well-known statement relating the
u
-invariant and the rank of an
\varepsilon
-hermitian form to the rank of totally isotropic subspaces.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
D
be a central
K
-division algebra and
\gamma
an involution on
D
.
Let
\varepsilon\in\{\pm 1\}
and let
h
be an
\varepsilon
-hermitian form over
(D,\gamma)
.
Then the
D
-right vector space on which
h
is defined contains a totally isotropic subspace
U
with respect to
h
with
\operatorname{\mathsf{rk}}U\geqslant\frac{1}{2}(\operatorname{\mathsf{rk}}h-u(D,\gamma,\varepsilon))
.

Proof.

Let
V
be the
D
-right vector space on which
h
is defined.
Let
U
be a maximal subspace of
V
that is totally isotropic with respect to
h
.
We set
U^{\prime}=\{w\in V\mid h(w,u)=0\mbox{ for all }u\in U\}
and note that this is a
D
-right subspace of
V
with
U\subseteq U^{\prime}
and
\operatorname{\mathsf{rk}}U^{\prime}\geqslant\operatorname{\mathsf{rk}}V-\operatorname{\mathsf{rk}}U
.
Then
U^{\prime}=U\oplus U^{\prime\prime}
for a
D
-right subspace
U^{\prime\prime}
of
U^{\prime}
with
\operatorname{\mathsf{rk}}U^{\prime\prime}=\operatorname{\mathsf{rk}}U^{\prime}-\operatorname{\mathsf{rk}}U\geqslant\operatorname{\mathsf{rk}}V-2\operatorname{\mathsf{rk}}U
.
It now follows from the choice of
U
that
h|_{U^{\prime\prime}}
is anisotropic.
Hence
\operatorname{\mathsf{rk}}U^{\prime\prime}\leqslant u(D,\gamma,\varepsilon)
.
We conclude that
2\operatorname{\mathsf{rk}}U\geqslant\operatorname{\mathsf{rk}}V-\operatorname{\mathsf{rk}}U^{\prime\prime}\geqslant\operatorname{\mathsf{rk}}h-u(D,\gamma,\varepsilon)
.
∎

The following statement is well-known. In
[
10
, Prop. 2.2]
, it is shown for the case where
B
is a division algebra.
We include a proof that does not require this hypothesis.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
\gamma
and
\gamma^{\prime}
involutions on
B
.

(1)

Let
\varepsilon\in\{\pm 1\}
. If
\gamma
and
\gamma^{\prime}
are either both orthogonal or both symplectic, then
u(B,\gamma,\varepsilon)=u(B,\gamma^{\prime},\varepsilon)
.
If
\gamma
is orthogonal and
\gamma^{\prime}
is symplectic, then
u(B,\gamma,\varepsilon)=u(B,\gamma^{\prime},-\varepsilon)
.

(2)

If
\gamma
and
\gamma^{\prime}
are both unitary and
\gamma|_{K}=\gamma^{\prime}|_{K}
, then
u(B,\gamma,1)=u(B,\gamma^{\prime},-1)
.

Proof.

In both parts, we have that
\gamma|_{K}=\gamma^{\prime}|_{K}
. Hence
\gamma\circ\gamma^{\prime}
is a
K
-automorphism of
B
.
We obtain by the Skolem-Noether Theorem that
\gamma\circ\gamma^{\prime}=\operatorname{\mathsf{Int}}(b)
for some
b\in{B}^{\times}
. Then
\gamma=\operatorname{\mathsf{Int}}(b)\circ\gamma^{\prime}
.

(1)
If
\gamma
and
\gamma^{\prime}
are either both orthogonal or both symplectic, then we set
\varepsilon^{\prime}=1
, otherwise we set
\varepsilon^{\prime}=-1
.
Then
\gamma^{\prime}(b)=\varepsilon^{\prime}b
, by
[
8
, Prop. 2.7]
.
Let
V
be a finitely generated
B
-right module and
h:V\times V\to B
an
\varepsilon
-hermitian form over
(B,\gamma)
.
One easily verifies that
b^{-1}h:V\times V\to B
is an
\varepsilon\varepsilon^{\prime}
-hermitian form over
(B,\gamma^{\prime})
.
Clearly,
h
is isotropic if and only if
b^{-1}h
is isotropic, and
\operatorname{\mathsf{rk}}h=\operatorname{\mathsf{rk}}b^{-1}h
.
Similarly, if
h^{\prime}:V\times V\to B
is an
\varepsilon\varepsilon^{\prime}
-hermitian form over
(B,\gamma^{\prime})
, then
bh:V\times V\to B
is an
\varepsilon
-hermitian form over
(B,\gamma)
.
This shows that
u(B,\gamma^{\prime},\varepsilon\varepsilon^{\prime})=u(B,\gamma,\varepsilon)
.

(2)
Set
\lambda=b\gamma^{\prime}(b)^{-1}
. We obtain that
\operatorname{\mathsf{Int}}(b)\circ\gamma^{\prime}\circ\operatorname{\mathsf{Int}}(b)=\operatorname{\mathsf{Int}}(\lambda)\circ\gamma^{\prime}
, hence
\operatorname{\mathsf{id}}_{B}=\gamma\circ\gamma=\operatorname{\mathsf{Int}}(\lambda)
, whereby
\lambda\in{B}^{\times}\cap\mathsf{Z}(B)={K}^{\times}
.
In particular
-\lambda^{-1}\gamma^{\prime}(-\lambda^{-1})=1
.
By Hilbert’s Theorem 90 applied to the quadratic extension
K/F
where
F=\{x\in K\mid\gamma^{\prime}(x)=x\}
, there exists
\lambda^{\prime}\in{K}^{\times}
such that
\lambda^{\prime-1}\gamma^{\prime}(\lambda^{\prime})=-\lambda^{-1}
.
Set
b^{\prime}=\lambda^{\prime}b
. Then
\operatorname{\mathsf{Int}}(b^{\prime})=\operatorname{\mathsf{Int}}(b)
and thus
\gamma=\operatorname{\mathsf{Int}}(b^{\prime})\circ\gamma^{\prime}
.
Consider a finitely generated
B
-right module
V
.
Given a hermitian form
h:V\times V\to B
over
(B,\gamma)
, we obtain that
b^{\prime-1}h
is a skew-hermitian form over

4.
Isotropy via quadratic reduction

Given a central simple algebra with involution and suitable separable quadratic field extension contained in it, one obtains a simple subalgebra carrying a pair of involutions.
Accordingly, we can obtain from a hermitian form over this algebra with involution a pair of hermitian forms over the subalgebra with respect to two different involutions such that the isotropy of this pair is equivalent to the isotropy of the original hermitian form.
By these means, one can compare the hermitian
u
-invariant of a central simple algebra with involution with the hermitian
u
-invariants of a subalgebra.
This method to study the hermitian
u
-invariants stems from
[
10
, Prop. 3.1]
.
The purpose of this section is to extend this method by relaxing the hypotheses under which it can be applied.
This will be achieved with
Theorem

4.6
.
Our treatment will cover the case of characteristic
2
for the unitary case.
As described above, the method relies on the presence of a quadratic subextension.
In
Proposition

4.2
we discuss another technique to deal with the complementary situation where we cannot find any quadratic field extension of the center contained in the algebra.
Together, these methods will allow us in the subsequent sections to obtain bounds on the hermitian
u
-invariants of a central simple algebra with involution in terms of the degree and the
u
-invariant of a splitting
2
-extension; see
Corollary

5.2
and
Corollary

6.4
.

In this section, let
K
be a field.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
D
be a central
K
-division algebra and
\gamma
an involution on
D
.
Assume that
\mathsf{char}K\neq 2
if
\gamma|_{K}=\operatorname{\mathsf{id}}_{K}
.
Let
\varepsilon\in\{\pm 1\}
and
h
be a nonsingular
\varepsilon
-hermitian form over
(D,\gamma)
.
Let
M/K
be a separable quadratic field extension such that

D_{M}
is a division algebra. Then

\displaystyle h\simeq h^{\prime}\perp h_{1}\perp\ldots\perp h_{n}

for some
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
\varepsilon
-hermitian forms

over
(D,\gamma)
such that
h^{\prime}_{M}
is anisotropic and, for

, we have that
\operatorname{\mathsf{rk}}h_{i}=2
and
(h_{i})_{M}
is hyperbolic.

Proof.

See
[
2
, Prop. 4.3]
and its proof. (In
[
2
]
, the general assumption is that

and

∎

Proposition 4.2
.

Let

be a separable field extension with

(b)

, then

has a quadratic subextension

If

If

We may assume that this is not the case.
We may thus fix an

on

Let
\varepsilon\in\{\pm 1\}
and consider an anisotropic
\varepsilon
-hermitian form

over

-right vector space

.

is an

-hermitian form over
(D,\gamma)
defined on a finite-dimensional
D
-right vector space
V
.
Then
\pi_{0}\circ h:V\times V\to\tilde{D}
is a nonsingular
\varepsilon
-hermitian form over
(\tilde{D},\gamma_{0})
and
\pi_{1}\circ h:V\times V\to\tilde{D}
is a nonsingular
(-\varepsilon)
-hermitian form over
(\tilde{D},\gamma_{1})
.

Proof.

It is easy to see that
\gamma(\tilde{D})=\tilde{D}
,
\mathsf{Z}(\tilde{D})=KL
,
D=\tilde{D}\oplus j\tilde{D}
and that
\gamma_{0}
and
\gamma_{1}
are involutions on
\tilde{D}
.

(a)
Clearly
(\gamma_{0})|_{L}\neq\operatorname{\mathsf{id}}_{L}
, so
\gamma_{0}
is unitary. Note that
(\gamma_{1})|_{L}=\operatorname{\mathsf{id}}_{L}
and
(\gamma_{1})|_{K}=\gamma|_{K}
.
Therefore
\gamma_{1}
is unitary if and only if
\gamma
is unitary. Assume now that this is not the case, that is
\gamma|_{K}=\operatorname{\mathsf{id}}_{K}
and
F=K
.
Since
\gamma(j)=-j
, assuming that
\mathsf{char}F\neq 2
, it follows by
[
8
, Prop. 2.7]
that if
\gamma
is orthogonal, then
\gamma_{1}
is symplectic, and vice-versa.

(b)
Set
h_{0}=\pi_{0}\circ h
and
h_{1}=\pi_{1}\circ h
. Clearly the maps
h_{0}
and
h_{1}
are bi-additive, and for any
v,w\in V
we have
h(v,w)=h_{0}(v,w)+jh_{1}(v,w)
.
Let
v,w\in V
and
x,y\in\tilde{D}
. We have

\begin{split}h(vx,wy)=\gamma(x)h(v,w)y&=\gamma(x)h_{0}(v,w)y+\gamma(x)jh_{1}(v,w)y\\
&=\gamma_{0}(x)h_{0}(v,w)y+j\gamma_{1}(x)h_{1}(v,w)y,\end{split}

whereby
h_{0}(vx,wy)=\gamma_{0}(x)h_{0}(v,w)y
and
h_{1}(vx,wy)=\gamma_{1}(x)h_{1}(v,w)y
. Furthermore,

\begin{split}h(w,v)=\varepsilon\gamma(h(v,w))&=\varepsilon\gamma(h_{0}(v,w))-\varepsilon\gamma(h_{1}(v,w))j\\
&=\varepsilon\gamma_{0}(h_{0}(v,w))-\varepsilon j\gamma_{1}(h_{1}(v,w)).\end{split}

whereby
h_{0}(w,v)=\varepsilon\gamma_{0}(h_{0}(v,w))
and
h(w,v)=-\varepsilon\gamma_{1}(h_{1}(v,w))
.
Hence,
h_{0}
is an
\varepsilon
-hermitian form over
(\tilde{D},\gamma_{0})
and
h_{1}
is a
(-\varepsilon)
-hermitian form over
(\tilde{D},\gamma_{1})
.

Note that for
v,w\in V
, since
\gamma(j)=-j
, we have

\begin{split}h(vj,w)&=h_{0}(vj,w)+jh_{1}(vj,w)\\
&=-jh_{0}(v,w)-j^{2}h_{1}(v,w),\end{split}

and as
j^{2}\in{\tilde{D}}^{\times}
, we get that
h_{0}(vj,w)=-j^{2}h_{1}(v,w)
and
h_{1}(vj,w)=-h_{0}(v,w)
.
As
h
is nonsingular, this implies in particular that
h_{0}
and
h_{1}
are nonsingular.
∎

We obtain another generalization of
[
10
, Prop. 3.1]
.

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
K/F
be a separable field extension with
[K:F]\leqslant 2
.
Let
M/K
be a quadratic field extension such that
M/F
is separable and
M
contains a quadratic extension of
F
distinct from
K
.
Let
\alpha\in\operatorname{\mathsf{Br}}(K)
be such that
\operatorname{\mathsf{ind}}\alpha_{M}=\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{\mathsf{ind}}\alpha
. Then the following hold:

(a)

If
\mathsf{char}F\neq 2
and
K=F
, then

\mbox{$u^{+}(\alpha)\leqslant\frac{1}{2}u^{+}(\alpha_{M})+u(\alpha_{M}/F)\quad\text{and}\quad u^{-}(\alpha)\leqslant u^{-}(\alpha_{M})+\frac{1}{2}u(\alpha_{M}/F)$}.

(b)

If
[K:F]=2
, then
M/F
has a quadratic subextension
L/F
such that
M=KL
and

u(\alpha/F)\leqslant\frac{3}{2}\cdot u(\alpha_{M}/L)
.

Proof.

If
K=F
, then we assume that
\mathsf{char}F\neq 2
. Let
D
be the central
K
-division algebra such that
\alpha=[D]
.
If
K=F
and
u^{+}(\alpha)=0
, respectively if
[K:F]=2
and
u(\alpha/F)=0
, then the claim of
(a)
, respectively of
(b)
holds trivially.
We may assume that this is not the case.
We may thus fix an
F
-linear involution
\gamma
on
D
with
F=\{x\in K\mid\gamma(x)=x\}
which is orthogonal or unitary (depending on
[K:F]
).

Since
\operatorname{\mathsf{ind}}\alpha_{M}=\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{\mathsf{ind}}\alpha
, we can embed
M
into
D
and hence view
M
as an
F
-subalgebra of
D
.
By means of
Proposition

4.3
, we may fix
j\in{D}^{\times}
and an
F
-linear involution
\gamma
on
D
such that
\gamma(j)=-j
and
\operatorname{\mathsf{Int}}(j)|_{M}=\operatorname{\mathsf{can}}_{M/F}=\gamma|_{M}
.

Set
\tilde{D}=C_{D}(M)
, and
\gamma_{0}=\gamma|_{\tilde{D}}
,
\gamma_{1}=(\operatorname{\mathsf{Int}}(j^{-1})\circ\gamma)|_{\tilde{D}}
.
Then
C(\tilde{D})=M
,
\gamma_{0}
and
\gamma_{1}
are
F
-linear involutions on
\tilde{D}
, whose properties are, according to
Proposition

4.4
, determined by those of
\gamma
in the following way:

\gamma_{0}
is unitary in any case, and if
\gamma
is orthogonal, then
\gamma_{1}
is symplectic, whereas if
\gamma
is unitary, then so is
\gamma_{1}
.

Let
\varepsilon\in\{\pm 1\}
and consider an
\varepsilon
-hermitian form
h
over
(D,\gamma)
, defined on some finite-dimensional
D
-right vector space
V
.
Seeing
V
as a
\tilde{D}
-right vector space,
Proposition

4.4
yields an
\varepsilon
-hermitian form
h_{0}
over
(\tilde{D},\gamma_{0})
and a
(-\varepsilon)
-hermitian form
h_{1}
over
(\tilde{D},\gamma_{1})
such that
\operatorname{\mathsf{rk}}h_{0}=\operatorname{\mathsf{rk}}h_{1}=2\cdot\operatorname{\mathsf{rk}}h
.

Suppose now that
\operatorname{\mathsf{rk}}h>\frac{1}{2}u(\tilde{D},\gamma_{0},\varepsilon)+u(\tilde{D},\gamma_{1},-\varepsilon)
.
As
\operatorname{\mathsf{rk}}h_{0}=2\operatorname{\mathsf{rk}}h
, it follows by
Lemma

3.1
that
V
contains a
\tilde{D}
-subspace
W
such that

for all

\operatorname{\mathsf{rk}}_{\tilde{D}}W>u(\tilde{D},\gamma_{1},-\varepsilon)
.
This implies that

for some

. Then

, so

is isotropic.
This argument proves that

\displaystyle u(D,\gamma,\varepsilon)\leqslant\mbox{$\frac{1}{2}$}u(\tilde{D},\gamma_{0},\varepsilon)+u(\tilde{D},\gamma_{1},-\varepsilon)\,.

Analogously, switching the roles of
h_{0}
and
h_{1}
in the argument, we obtain that

\displaystyle u(D,\gamma,\varepsilon)\leqslant u(\tilde{D},\gamma_{0},\varepsilon)+\mbox{$\frac{1}{2}$}u(\tilde{D},\gamma_{1},-\varepsilon)\,.

We are now ready to prove the two parts of the statement.
If
K=F
, then the first inequality for
\varepsilon=-1
together with the second inequality for
\varepsilon=1
yield the two inequalities in
(a)
.

Assume now that
[K:F]=2
. For
i=0,1
, we set
L_{i}=\{x\in M\mid\gamma_{i}(x)=x\}
.
Then
K/F
,
L_{0}/F
and
L_{1}/F
are the three different quadratic subextensions in

.
Note that
u(\tilde{D},\gamma_{i},\varepsilon)=u(\tilde{D},\gamma_{i},1)
for
i=0,1
.
Take

such that
u(\tilde{D},\gamma_{k},1)\geqslant u(\tilde{D},\gamma_{1-k},1)
.
We conclude from either of the two inequalities above that
u(\alpha/F)=u(D,\gamma,1)\leqslant\mbox{$\frac{3}{2}$}u(\tilde{D},\gamma_{k},1)=\mbox{$\frac{3}{2}$}u(\alpha_{M}/L_{k})
, proving
(b)
.
∎

We merge the main results of this section into one theorem.

.

Let

be a separable field extension with

.

be a quadratic field extension such that

is separable and

\displaystyle u^{+}(\alpha)

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\mbox{$\frac{\operatorname{\mathsf{ind}}\alpha_{M}}{\operatorname{\mathsf{ind}}\alpha}$}\left(u^{+}(\alpha_{M})+2u(\alpha_{M}/F)\right)\,.

has a quadratic subextension

\displaystyle u(\alpha/F)

\displaystyle\leqslant

This follows from

if

5.
Bounds on the unitary
u
-invariant

To make use of
Theorem

4.6
when given a
2
-extension splitting the algebra and aiming to bound the hermitian
u
-invariants, we should first address this problem for the unitary

-invariant.
Therefore, in this section, we study the behaviour of the unitary
u
-invariant under
2

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
K/F
be a separable quadratic field extension and
\alpha\in\operatorname{\mathsf{Br}}(K)
. Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
and let
L/F
be a
2
-extension linearly disjoint from
K/F
such that
[L:F]=2^{n}
.
Then there exists a
2
-extension
L^{\prime}/F
linearly disjoint from
K/F
with
KL^{\prime}=KL
and such that

\operatorname{\mathsf{ind}}\alpha\cdot u(\alpha/F)\leqslant 3^{n}\cdot\operatorname{\mathsf{ind}}\alpha_{KL}\cdot u(\alpha_{KL/L^{\prime}}).

Proof.

We prove the statement by induction on
n
. If
n=0
, then
L=F
and we can take
L^{\prime}=F
. Assume now that
n\geqslant 1
.
Since
L/F
is a
2
-extension, there exist a family of intermediate fields
(L_{i})_{i=0}^{n}
with
L_{0}=F
,
L_{n}=L
and such that
L_{i}/L_{i-1}
is a separable quadratic field extension for
1\leqslant i\leqslant n
. Set
K^{\prime}=L_{1}K
.

By
Theorem

4.6
, there exists a separable quadratic field extension
L^{\prime}_{1}/F
contained in
K^{\prime}/F
such that
L_{1}^{\prime}K=K^{\prime}
and

\mbox{$u(\alpha/F)\leqslant\frac{\operatorname{\mathsf{ind}}\alpha_{K^{\prime}}}{\operatorname{\mathsf{ind}}\alpha}\cdot 3\cdot u(\alpha_{K^{\prime}}/L^{\prime}_{1})$}.

Since
[L^{\prime}_{1}:F]=[K:F]=2
and
[K^{\prime}:F]=4
, it follows that
L^{\prime}_{1}/F
is linearly disjoint from
K/F
.
Note that there exists a
2
-extension
L^{\prime}/L_{1}^{\prime}
contained in
LK
, linearly disjoint from
K^{\prime}/L_{1}^{\prime}
and such that
L^{\prime}K=LK
.
Moreover, for any such extension
L^{\prime}/L_{1}^{\prime}
we have that
[L^{\prime}:L_{1}^{\prime}]=[LK:L_{1}K]=[L:L_{1}]=2^{n-1}
.
The induction hypothesis yields that there exists such an extension
L^{\prime}/L^{\prime}_{1}
with

\mbox{$u(\alpha_{K^{\prime}}/L^{\prime}_{1})\leqslant\frac{\operatorname{\mathsf{ind}}\alpha_{KL}}{\operatorname{\mathsf{ind}}\alpha_{K^{\prime}}}\cdot 3^{n-1}\cdot u(\alpha_{KL/L^{\prime}})$}.

Combining the inequalities yields that
u(\alpha/F)\leqslant\frac{\operatorname{\mathsf{ind}}\alpha_{KL}}{\operatorname{\mathsf{ind}}\alpha}\cdot 3^{n}\cdot u(\alpha_{KL/L^{\prime}})
.
∎

Corollary 5.2
.

Let
K/F
be a separable quadratic field extension and
\alpha\!\in\operatorname{\mathsf{Br}}(K)
.
Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
and let
L/F
be a
2
-extension linearly disjoint from
K/F
such that
[L:F]=2^{n}
and

.

2
-extension

linearly disjoint from

such that
KL^{\prime}=KL
and

\operatorname{\mathsf{ind}}\alpha\cdot u(\alpha/F)\leqslant 3^{n}\cdot u(KL/L^{\prime}).

Proof.

By
Theorem

5.1
, there exists a
2
-extension

linearly disjoint from
K/F
with

and

\operatorname{\mathsf{ind}}\alpha\cdot u(\alpha/F)\leqslant\operatorname{\mathsf{ind}}\alpha_{KL}\cdot 3^{n}\cdot u(\alpha_{KL/L^{\prime}})

Most bounds that we presented in this article have strictly weaker hypotheses than previously known bounds. The trade-off is that the bounds that we obtain are comparatively also a bit weaker.

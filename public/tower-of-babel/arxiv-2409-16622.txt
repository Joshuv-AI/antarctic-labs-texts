Heralded optical entanglement distribution via lossy quantum channels: A comparative study
Wan Zo, 1,2 Seungbeom Chin, 3,4,* and Yong-Su Kim 1,5,†

Abstract
Quantum entanglement serves as a foundational resource for various quantum technologies. In optical systems, entanglement distribution rely on the indistinguishability and spatial overlap of photons. Heralded schemes play a crucial role in ensuring the reliability of entanglement generation by detecting ancillary photons to signal the creation of desired entangled states. However, photon losses in quantum channels remain a significant challenge, limiting the distance and capacity of entanglement distributions. This study suggests three heralded schemes that distribute multipartite Greenberger-Horne-Zeilinger (GHZ) states via lossy quantum channels. These schemes utilize different photon sources (Bell states or single-photons) and channel structures (centralized or decentralized heralding detectors). By comparing success probabilities and heralding efficiency, we find that each scheme has its own advantage according to the number of parties and the channel distance and the security requirement. This analysis provides insights into designing resilient heralded circuits for quantum information processing over lossy channels.

2
Entanglement distribution via lossy channels

2.1
The criteria

We employ two criteria, the success probabilities
P_{suc}
and heralding efficiency
H_{eff}
, to compare the above schemes with lossy channels. The success probability
P_{suc}
is the probability of generating the target state, i.e., the GHZ state, among all trials of transmitting the initial states via channels. The heralding efficiency
H_{eff}
is the GHZ state generation probability upon with the successful heralding detections. These two values are defined more rigorously as follows:

Definition 1
.

The success probability
P_{suc}
is the probability that participating parties share target entangled state among all trials.

Definition 2
.

The heralding probability
P_{hr}
is the probability that the heralding detection provides a signal in the way we have designed among all trials. Ideally, heralding detection indicates successful distribution of entangled state.

Definition 3
.

The heralding efficiency
H_{eff}
is given as the conditional probability with
P_{suc}
and
P_{hr}
by

\displaystyle H_{eff}=\frac{P_{suc}}{P_{hr}}.

(1)

Now, we investigate three different heralded optical schemes, i.e., BC, SC, and SD schemes, for generating
N
-partite GHZ states. We provide step-by-step state evolution and calculate the success probabilities
P_{suc}
and heralding efficiencies
H_{eff}
in the presence of channel losses.

2.2
Bell state inputs with centralized third-party (BC scheme)

We can generate the
N
-partite GHZ state with
N
sets of Bell states (thus,
2N
photons) as described in Fig.
2
, which can be considered as a
N
-partite generalization of the entanglement swapping
[
18
]
. Initially, each party labelled
i

\in\{1,2,\cdots,N\}
prepares for the Bell state

\displaystyle|\psi^{+}{\rangle}_{i}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}({\hat{b}^{\dagger}}_{i,H}{\hat{c}^{\dagger}}_{i,V}+{\hat{b}^{\dagger}}_{i,V}{\hat{c}^{\dagger}}_{i,H})|vac{\rangle},

(2)

where the subscripts
H
and
V
represent horizontal and vertical polarization states, respectively.
Then the overall
2N
-photon initial state
|\psi{\rangle}_{in}
is given as

\displaystyle|\psi{\rangle}=\prod_{i=1}^{N}|\psi^{+}\rangle_{i}=\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\right)^{N}\prod_{i=1}^{N}({\hat{b}^{\dagger}}_{i,H}{\hat{c}^{\dagger}}_{i,V}+{\hat{b}^{\dagger}}_{i,V}{\hat{c}^{\dagger}}_{i,H})|vac\rangle.

(3)

Each party retains one of the entangled photons (
{\hat{b}^{\dagger}}
) and sends the other (
{\hat{c}^{\dagger}}
) to the central third-party.

Figure 2:
Schematic of the BC heralding scheme. The setup consists of linear optics and photon-number resolving detectors (PNRDs). Input resources are
N
Bell states, resulting in an
N
-party GHZ state

Photons forwarded to the central third-party can be lost through channels, which is represented as follows:

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\hat{c}^{\dagger}}_{i}\rightarrow\eta_{BC}{\hat{c}^{\dagger}}_{i}+\sqrt{1-\eta_{BC}^{2}}{\hat{f}^{\dagger}}_{i},

(4)

where
\eta_{BC}
denotes the transmission coefficient of the BC scheme and
\hat{f}_{i}^{\dagger}
is the environment mode corresponding to the loss. As drawn in the heralding third-party of Fig.
2
, the photons sent to the third-party encounter the polarization beam splitter (PBS), which splits diagonal (
|D{\rangle}\equiv(|H{\rangle}+|V{\rangle})/\sqrt{2}
) and anti-diagonal (
|A{\rangle}\equiv(|H{\rangle}-|V{\rangle})/\sqrt{2}
) polarization states. Thus, the transform is:

\displaystyle{\hat{c}^{\dagger}}_{i,H}\rightarrow\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left({\hat{d}^{\dagger}}_{i,D}+{\hat{d}^{\dagger}}_{i\oplus 1,A}\right),~~~{\hat{c}^{\dagger}}_{i,V}\rightarrow\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left({\hat{d}^{\dagger}}_{i,D}-{\hat{d}^{\dagger}}_{i\oplus 1,A}\right),

(5)

where
\oplus
denotes a summation modulo
N
. Note that we compensate phase shift
e^{i\pi}
for path mode
{\hat{c}^{\dagger}}_{1}
with a phase plate. As will be revealed later, the relative phase here only affects the relative phase of the generated GHZ state and does not affect the success probability or the heralding efficiency.

The wave function is written as

\displaystyle|\psi{\rangle}\rightarrow\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\right)^{N}\prod_{i=1}^{N}\Bigg[\frac{\eta_{BC}}{\sqrt{2}}\left({\hat{b}^{\dagger}}_{i,H}+{\hat{b}^{\dagger}}_{i,V}\right){\hat{d}^{\dagger}}_{i,D}+\frac{\eta_{BC}}{\sqrt{2}}\left({\hat{b}^{\dagger}}_{i,H}-{\hat{b}^{\dagger}}_{i,V}\right){\hat{d}^{\dagger}}_{i\oplus 1,A}

(6)

\displaystyle+{\sqrt{1-\eta_{BC}^{2}}}({\hat{b}^{\dagger}}_{i,H}{\hat{f}^{\dagger}}_{i,V}+{\hat{b}^{\dagger}}_{i,V}{\hat{f}^{\dagger}}_{i,H})\Bigg]|vac{\rangle}.

We postselect only the cases when one photon arrives at each photon-number resolving detector (PNRD), which
heralds
the target GHZ state. Hence the terms that contribute to the final state will contain
\prod_{i=1}^{N}{\hat{d}^{\dagger}}_{i}
. We can eliminate other path modes in the state evolution, thus the quantum state is:

\displaystyle|\psi{\rangle}\rightarrow\left(\frac{\eta_{BC}}{\sqrt{2}}\right)^{N}\Bigg[\prod_{i=1}^{N}{\hat{b}^{\dagger}}_{i,D}{\hat{d}^{\dagger}}_{i,D}+\prod_{j=1}^{N}{\hat{b}^{\dagger}}_{j,A}{\hat{d}^{\dagger}}_{j,A}\Bigg]|vac{\rangle}.

(7)

Equation (
7
) indicates the quantum state corresponding to heralding signal from PNRD.
Note that Eq. (
7
) is written in D/A polarization basis, but measurement basis in the heralding detection mode
{\hat{d}^{\dagger}}
is H/V. Thus, the full expansion of the equation is as follows:

\displaystyle|\psi{\rangle}\rightarrow\frac{\eta_{BC}^{N}}{\sqrt{2}^{2N-1}}\bigg[\left(\frac{{\hat{b}^{\dagger}}_{1,D}...{\hat{b}^{\dagger}}_{N,D}+{\hat{b}^{\dagger}}_{1,A}...{\hat{b}^{\dagger}}_{N,A}}{\sqrt{2}}\right){\hat{d}^{\dagger}}_{1,H}...{\hat{d}^{\dagger}}_{N,H}

(8)

\displaystyle+\left(\frac{{\hat{b}^{\dagger}}_{1,D}...{\hat{b}^{\dagger}}_{N,D}-{\hat{b}^{\dagger}}_{1,A}...{\hat{b}^{\dagger}}_{N,A}}{\sqrt{2}}\right){\hat{d}^{\dagger}}_{1,H}...{\hat{d}^{\dagger}}_{N,V}

\displaystyle+...

\displaystyle+\left(\frac{{\hat{b}^{\dagger}}_{1,D}...{\hat{b}^{\dagger}}_{N,D}+(-1)^{N}{\hat{b}^{\dagger}}_{1,A}...{\hat{b}^{\dagger}}_{N,A}}{\sqrt{2}}\right){\hat{d}^{\dagger}}_{1,V}...{\hat{d}^{\dagger}}_{N,V}\bigg]|vac{\rangle}

\displaystyle\rightarrow

\displaystyle\frac{\eta_{BC}^{N}}{\sqrt{2}^{N-1}}|\text{GHZ}^{\pm}{\rangle}_{b}\otimes\Bigg[{\hat{d}^{\dagger}}_{1,H}...{\hat{d}^{\dagger}}_{N,H}+...+{\hat{d}^{\dagger}}_{1,V}...{\hat{d}^{\dagger}}_{N,V}\Bigg]|vac{\rangle},

where

|{\rm GHZ}^{\pm}\rangle_{b}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left({\hat{b}^{\dagger}}_{1,D}...{\hat{b}^{\dagger}}_{N,D}\pm{\hat{b}^{\dagger}}_{1,A}...{\hat{b}^{\dagger}}_{N,A}\right)|vac\rangle.

(9)

At this step, the heralding probability
P_{hr}^{(BC)}
is equal to the summation of square of the coefficient multiplied by the
\prod_{i=1}^{N}{\hat{d}^{\dagger}}_{i}
mode

P_{hr}^{BC}=\left(\frac{\eta_{BC}^{N}}{\sqrt{2}^{2N-1}}\right)^{2}\times 2^{N}=\frac{\eta_{BC}^{2N}}{2^{N-1}}.

(10)

The
2^{N}
factor denotes the degeneracy of heralding signal. After the heralding detection, all
\hat{d}^{\dagger}_{i}
modes are eliminated, and the remaining path modes represent a superposition of
{\hat{b}^{\dagger}}_{1,D}...{\hat{b}^{\dagger}}_{N,D}/\sqrt{2}
and
{\hat{b}^{\dagger}}_{1,A}...{\hat{b}^{\dagger}}_{N,A}/\sqrt{2}
, which is a GHZ state. The relative phase of the GHZ state is determined by the combination of the photon detected in the heralding detectors. Since the phase of GHZ states can be adjusted through a feed-forward process based on heralding measurement outcomes, the success probability
P_{suc}^{(BC)}
to obtain the GHZ state in this scheme becomes the square of the coefficient multiplied by the wave function in Eq. (
8
),

\displaystyle P_{suc}^{(BC)}=\frac{\eta_{BC}^{2N}}{2^{N-1}}.

(11)

In BC scheme, heralding detection always indicates GHZ state and

H_{eff}^{(BC)}=\frac{P_{suc}^{(BC)}}{P_{hr}^{(BC)}}=1.

(12)

There is correlation between the photons experiencing loss on their way to the heralding part and the photons being kept within the each party, the heralding signal guarantee entanglement regardless of loss.

However, it is demanding to generate synchronized Bell states among distant parties. In the next section, we will relax this stringent condition of the initial state preparation by utilizing single-photons instead of entangled photon pairs.

2.3
Single-photon inputs with centralized third-party (SC scheme)

Schematic of the SC heralding scheme. Input resources are

The centralized scheme with single-photon sources (SC scheme) is a generalization of the Bell state generation scheme suggested in Ref.
[
24
,
25
]
. This scheme shares similar network structure with the BC scheme but begins with
2N
single-photon inputs instead of Bell states.

The initial state of the SC scheme is given by

\displaystyle|\psi{\rangle}=\prod_{i=1}^{N}{\hat{a}^{\dagger}}_{i,H}{\hat{a}^{\dagger}}_{i,V}|vac{\rangle}.

(13)

After passing through 50:50 non-polarizing beam splitter (BS) at each party, the path mode operators undergo following transformation:

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\hat{a}^{\dagger}}_{i,H}\rightarrow\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}({\hat{b}^{\dagger}}_{i,H}+{\hat{c}^{\dagger}}_{i,H}),~~~{\hat{a}^{\dagger}}_{i,V}\rightarrow\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}({\hat{b}^{\dagger}}_{i,V}+{\hat{c}^{\dagger}}_{i,V}).

(14)

Then, the lossy channel with the transmission coefficient
\eta_{SC}
transforms the mode operators,

{\hat{c}^{\dagger}}_{i}\rightarrow\eta_{SC}{\hat{c}^{\dagger}}_{i}+{\sqrt{1-\eta_{SC}^{2}}}{\hat{f}^{\dagger}}_{i},

(15)

and the wavefunction becomes

\displaystyle|\psi{\rangle}\rightarrow\frac{1}{2^{N}}\prod_{i=1}^{N}\bigg[{\hat{b}^{\dagger}}_{i,H}{\hat{b}^{\dagger}}_{i,V}+\eta_{SC}({\hat{b}^{\dagger}}_{i,H}{\hat{c}^{\dagger}}_{i,V}+{\hat{b}^{\dagger}}_{i,V}{\hat{c}^{\dagger}}_{i,H})+\eta_{SC}^{2}{\hat{c}^{\dagger}}_{i,H}{\hat{c}^{\dagger}}_{i,V}

(16)

\displaystyle+{\sqrt{1-\eta_{SC}^{2}}}\left({\hat{b}^{\dagger}}_{i,H}{\hat{f}^{\dagger}}_{i,V}+{\hat{b}^{\dagger}}_{i,V}{\hat{f}^{\dagger}}_{i,H}+\eta_{SC}({\hat{c}^{\dagger}}_{i,H}{\hat{f}^{\dagger}}_{i,V}+{\hat{c}^{\dagger}}_{i,V}{\hat{f}^{\dagger}}_{i,H})\right)+(1-\eta_{SC}^{2}){\hat{f}^{\dagger}}_{i,H}{\hat{f}^{\dagger}}_{i,V}\bigg]|vac{\rangle}.

As in the heralding part of Fig.
3
, the path mode operators sent to the third-party transform into D/A basis same as Eq. (
5
).

\displaystyle{\hat{c}^{\dagger}}_{i,H}

\displaystyle\rightarrow

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left({\hat{d}^{\dagger}}_{i,D}+{\hat{d}^{\dagger}}_{i\oplus 1,A}\right),~~~{\hat{c}^{\dagger}}_{i,V}

\displaystyle\rightarrow

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left({\hat{d}^{\dagger}}_{i,D}-{\hat{d}^{\dagger}}_{i\oplus 1,A}\right)

(17)

Then, the quantum state is:

\displaystyle|\psi{\rangle}\rightarrow

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2^{N}}\prod_{i=1}^{N}\bigg[{\hat{b}^{\dagger}}_{i,H}{\hat{b}^{\dagger}}_{i,V}+\frac{\eta_{SC}^{2}}{2}\left({\hat{d}^{\dagger}}_{i,D}{\hat{d}^{\dagger}}_{i,D}-{\hat{d}^{\dagger}}_{i\oplus 1,A}{\hat{d}^{\dagger}}_{i\oplus 1,A}\right)

(18)

\displaystyle+\frac{\eta_{SC}}{\sqrt{2}}\left(({\hat{b}^{\dagger}}_{i,H}+{\hat{b}^{\dagger}}_{i,V}){\hat{d}^{\dagger}}_{i,D}+({\hat{b}^{\dagger}}_{i,H}-{\hat{b}^{\dagger}}_{i,V}){\hat{d}^{\dagger}}_{i\oplus 1,A}\right)+{\sqrt{1-\eta_{SC}^{2}}}\left({\hat{b}^{\dagger}}_{i,H}{\hat{f}^{\dagger}}_{i,V}+{\hat{b}^{\dagger}}_{i,V}{\hat{f}^{\dagger}}_{i,H}\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{\eta_{SC}{\sqrt{1-\eta_{SC}^{2}}}}{\sqrt{2}}\left(({\hat{d}^{\dagger}}_{i,D}+{\hat{d}^{\dagger}}_{i\oplus 1,A}){\hat{f}^{\dagger}}_{i,V}+({\hat{d}^{\dagger}}_{i,D}-{\hat{d}^{\dagger}}_{i\oplus 1,A}){\hat{f}^{\dagger}}_{i,H}\right)+\left(1-\eta_{SC}^{2}\right){\hat{f}^{\dagger}}_{i,H}{\hat{f}^{\dagger}}_{i,V}\bigg]|vac{\rangle}.

PNRD can postselect the cases that only one photon arrives at each heralding detectors. The terms that survive under the postselections are

\displaystyle|\psi{\rangle}\rightarrow\frac{1}{2^{N}}\prod_{i=1}^{N}\Bigg[\eta_{SC}\left({\hat{b}^{\dagger}}_{i,D}{\hat{d}^{\dagger}}_{i,D}+{\hat{b}^{\dagger}}_{i,A}{\hat{d}^{\dagger}}_{i\oplus 1,A}\right)

(19)

\displaystyle+\frac{\eta_{SC}{\sqrt{1-\eta_{SC}^{2}}}}{\sqrt{2}}\left(({\hat{d}^{\dagger}}_{i,D}+{\hat{d}^{\dagger}}_{i\oplus 1,A}){\hat{f}^{\dagger}}_{i,V}+({\hat{d}^{\dagger}}_{i,D}-{\hat{d}^{\dagger}}_{i\oplus 1,A}){\hat{f}^{\dagger}}_{i,H}\right)\Bigg]|vac{\rangle},

and then,

\displaystyle|\psi{\rangle}\rightarrow\frac{\eta_{SC}^{N}}{2^{N}}\Bigg[\prod_{i=1}^{N}{\hat{b}^{\dagger}}_{i,D}{\hat{d}^{\dagger}}_{i,D}+\prod_{j=1}^{N}{\hat{b}^{\dagger}}_{j,A}{\hat{d}^{\dagger}}_{j,A}\Bigg]|vac{\rangle}+

(20)

\displaystyle\frac{\eta_{SC}^{N-1}}{2^{N}}\left(\frac{\eta_{SC}{\sqrt{1-\eta_{SC}^{2}}}}{\sqrt{2}}\right){\hat{b}^{\dagger}}_{1,D}{\hat{b}^{\dagger}}_{2,D}...{\hat{b}^{\dagger}}_{N-1,D}{\hat{f}^{\dagger}}_{N,V}{\hat{d}^{\dagger}}_{1,D}{\hat{d}^{\dagger}}_{2,D}...{\hat{d}^{\dagger}}_{N-1,D}{\hat{d}^{\dagger}}_{N,D}|vac{\rangle}

\displaystyle\frac{\eta_{SC}^{N-2}}{2^{N}}\left(\frac{\eta_{SC}{\sqrt{1-\eta_{SC}^{2}}}}{\sqrt{2}}\right)^{2}{\hat{b}^{\dagger}}_{1,D}{\hat{b}^{\dagger}}_{2,D}...{\hat{f}^{\dagger}}_{N-1,V}{\hat{f}^{\dagger}}_{N,V}{\hat{d}^{\dagger}}_{1,D}{\hat{d}^{\dagger}}_{2,D}...{\hat{d}^{\dagger}}_{N-1,D}{\hat{d}^{\dagger}}_{N,D}|vac{\rangle}

\displaystyle+...

The first two terms in Eq. (
20
) correspond to the generation of the GHZ state, see Eq. (
7
). The remainders would cause false heralding signals that decreases the heralding efficiency,
H_{eff}
.

Because the conditions on the inputs are relaxed, the correlation between the lost photon during the transmission and the photon kept by the local party for generating entanglement disappears. Our target is to generate an N-party GHZ state, where each party holds one photon and N photons are detected at the central location, as shown in Fig.
4
(a).
However, as illustrated in Fig.
4
(b), there are cases where photons arrive at the central location, but no photons remain at the local parties. Due to this difference, the heralding probability in the SC scheme is not equal to the success probability.

Figure 4:
Scenarios leading to heralding signals in the SC scheme. (a) True heralding: One single-photon is retained by the local party, while the other successfully traverses the lossy channel to the central third-party. (b) False heralding: Both single-photons enter the lossy channel, but only one survives. The central heralding detector cannot distinguish this case from the true heralding scenario, leading to a false positive signal.

Therefore, taking into account both true heralding and false heralding cases, the heralding probability is calculated as follows:

\displaystyle P_{hr}^{(SC)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2^{N}}\bigg[2\eta_{SC}^{2N}+\sum_{1}^{N}\binom{N}{k}(2\eta_{SC}^{2}(1-\eta_{SC}^{2}))^{k}(\eta_{SC}^{2})^{N-k}\bigg]

(21)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2^{N}}\bigg[2\eta_{SC}^{2N}+(2\eta_{SC}^{2}(1-\eta_{SC}^{2})^{2}+\eta_{SC}^{2})^{N}-\eta_{SC}^{2N}\bigg].

The success probability of entanglement distribution is obtained by taking the square of the amplitude of only the first two terms in Eq. (
20
), in the same manner as in Eq. (
7
) and Eq. (
8
), and thus

\displaystyle P_{suc}^{(SC)}=\frac{\eta_{SC}^{2N}}{2^{2N-1}}.

(22)

Then, we have the heralding efficiency of the SC scheme is given as

\displaystyle H_{eff}^{(SC)}=\frac{P_{suc}^{(SC)}}{P_{hr}^{(SC)}}=\frac{2}{1+(3-2\eta_{SC}^{2})^{N}}.

(23)

2.4
Single-photon inputs with decentralized heralding (SD scheme)

The two schemes discussed so far rely on central measurements by a third-party that distributes the classical information on heralding detection results to
N
participants. Hence, it raises potential trust concerns due to the information imbalance between the third-party and others
[
28
]
. Therefore, the generation of quantum entangled states with no trusted third-party are useful for enhancing the security of quantum communications. We can consider such schemes based on the property that the photon path indistinguishability does not rely on direct photon-photon interactions but interference between probability amplitudes
[
29
,
30
,
31
,
32
,
33
]
. Indeed, Refs.
[
27
,
26
]
proposed a heralded scheme for generating the GHZ state without third-party via a graph approach to boson subtractions, which we call the decentralized scheme with single-photon sources (SD scheme). In this subsection, we calculate the success probability and heralding efficiency of the SD scheme in presence of channel losses.

Initially, we have
N
copies of one horizontal and one vertical polarization single-photons impinged into one port of a PBS, hence the initial state is given by

\displaystyle|\psi{\rangle}=\prod_{i=1}^{N}{\hat{a}^{\dagger}}_{i,H}{\hat{a}^{\dagger}}_{i,V}|vac\rangle.

(24)

The D/A basis PBS transform the photons as

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\hat{a}^{\dagger}}_{i,H}\rightarrow\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}({\hat{b}^{\dagger}}_{i,D}+{\hat{c}^{\dagger}}_{i,A}),~~~{\hat{a}^{\dagger}}_{i,V}\rightarrow\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}({\hat{b}^{\dagger}}_{i,D}-{\hat{c}^{\dagger}}_{i,A}).

(25)

Figure 5:
Schematic of the SD heralding scheme. Input resources are
2N
single-photons, resulting in an
N
-party GHZ state. Heralding detection components are evenly distributed among all participants, eliminating the need for a centralized third party.

Hence the wave function becomes

\displaystyle|\psi{\rangle}\rightarrow\frac{1}{2^{N}}\prod_{i=1}^{N}\left({\hat{b}^{\dagger}}_{i,D}{\hat{b}^{\dagger}}_{i,D}-{\hat{c}^{\dagger}}_{i,A}{\hat{c}^{\dagger}}_{i,A}\right)|vac{\rangle}.

(26)

Note that the Hong-Ou-Mandel (HOM) interference appears from the
N
PBS, by which two single-photons always exit the same output port of a PBS
[
34
]
.

After the HOM interference, the photons transmit to neighboring party or come back to their own party. During this process, photons can be lost and this is represented by

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\hat{b}^{\dagger}}_{i,D}\rightarrow\eta_{SD}{\hat{b}^{\dagger}}_{i,D}+{\sqrt{1-\eta_{SD}^{2}}}{\hat{f}^{\dagger}}_{i,D},

(27)

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\hat{c}^{\dagger}}_{i,A}\rightarrow\eta_{SD}{\hat{c}^{\dagger}}_{i,A}+{\sqrt{1-\eta_{SD}^{2}}}{\hat{g}^{\dagger}}_{i,A}.

Then, the overall quantum state becomes

\displaystyle|\psi{\rangle}\rightarrow

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2^{N}}\prod_{i=1}^{N}\Bigg[\eta_{SD}^{2}({\hat{b}^{\dagger}}_{i,D}{\hat{b}^{\dagger}}_{i,D}-{\hat{c}^{\dagger}}_{i,A}{\hat{c}^{\dagger}}_{i,A})+2\eta_{SD}{\sqrt{1-\eta_{SD}^{2}}}\left({\hat{b}^{\dagger}}_{i,D}{\hat{f}^{\dagger}}_{i,D}-{\hat{c}^{\dagger}}_{i,A}{\hat{g}^{\dagger}}_{i,A}\right)

(28)

\displaystyle+\left(1-\eta_{SD}^{2}\right)\left({\hat{f}^{\dagger}}_{i,D}{\hat{f}^{\dagger}}_{i,D}-{\hat{g}^{\dagger}}_{i,A}{\hat{g}^{\dagger}}_{i,A}\right)\Bigg]|vac{\rangle}.

The optical path wiring is represented as

\displaystyle{\hat{b}^{\dagger}}_{i,D}\rightarrow{\hat{b}^{\dagger}}_{i,D},

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\hat{c}^{\dagger}}_{i,A}\rightarrow{\hat{c}^{\dagger}}_{i\oplus 1,A}.

(29)

After the lossy channels and rewiring process, the photons arrive at the H/V basis PBS.

\displaystyle{\hat{b}^{\dagger}}_{i,D}\rightarrow\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left({\hat{e}^{\dagger}}_{i,H}+{\hat{d}^{\dagger}}_{i,V}\right),~~~{\hat{c}^{\dagger}}_{i,A}\rightarrow\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left({\hat{d}^{\dagger}}_{i,H}-{\hat{e}^{\dagger}}_{i,V}\right).

(30)

Now, the wave function is written in the H/V basis as

\displaystyle|\psi\rangle\rightarrow\frac{1}{2^{N}}\prod_{i=1}^{N}\Bigg[\frac{\eta_{SD}^{2}}{2}\left({\hat{e}^{\dagger}}_{i,H}{\hat{e}^{\dagger}}_{i,H}+{\hat{d}^{\dagger}}_{i,V}{\hat{d}^{\dagger}}_{i,V}+2{\hat{e}^{\dagger}}_{i,H}{\hat{d}^{\dagger}}_{i,V}-{\hat{d}^{\dagger}}_{i\oplus 1,H}{\hat{d}^{\dagger}}_{i\oplus 1,H}-2{\hat{d}^{\dagger}}_{i\oplus 1,H}{\hat{e}^{\dagger}}_{i\oplus 1,V}-{\hat{e}^{\dagger}}_{i\oplus 1,V}{\hat{e}^{\dagger}}_{i\oplus 1,V}\right)

(31)

The heralding probability
P_{hr}^{(SD)}
is derived from the combination of terms, each containing only one detector path mode
{\hat{d}^{\dagger}}
as

\displaystyle|\psi\rangle\rightarrow\frac{1}{2^{N}}\prod_{i=1}^{N}\Bigg[\eta_{SD}^{2}\left({\hat{e}^{\dagger}}_{i,H}{\hat{d}^{\dagger}}_{i,V}-{\hat{d}^{\dagger}}_{i\oplus 1,H}{\hat{e}^{\dagger}}_{i\oplus 1,V}\right)+\sqrt{2}\eta_{SD}{\sqrt{1-\eta_{SD}^{2}}}\left({\hat{d}^{\dagger}}_{i,V}{\hat{f}^{\dagger}}_{i,D}-{\hat{d}^{\dagger}}_{i\oplus 1,H}{\hat{g}^{\dagger}}_{i,A}\right)\Bigg]|vac{\rangle}.

(32)

The heralding probability is calculated by taking the product of these terms and becomes

P_{hr}^{(SD)}=\frac{1}{2^{2N}}\bigg[2\eta_{SD}^{4N}+\sum_{1}^{N}\binom{N}{k}(2\eta_{SD}^{2}(1-\eta_{SD}^{2}))^{k}(\eta_{SD}^{4})^{N-k}\bigg]=\frac{1}{2^{2N}}\bigg[(2\eta_{SD}^{2}-\eta_{SD}^{4})^{N}+\eta_{SD}^{4N}\bigg].

(33)

2N

N

N

\displaystyle|\psi{\rangle}\rightarrow\frac{\eta_{SD}^{2N}}{2^{N}}\bigg[\prod_{i=1}^{N}\hat{d}^{\dagger}_{i,H}\hat{e}^{\dagger}_{i,V}+\prod_{i=1}^{N}\hat{d}^{\dagger}_{i,V}\hat{e}^{\dagger}_{i,H}\bigg]|vac\rangle,

\displaystyle P_{suc}^{(SD)}=\frac{\eta_{SD}^{4N}}{2^{2N-1}}.

\displaystyle H_{eff}^{(SD)}=\frac{P_{suc}^{(SD)}}{P_{hr}^{(SD)}}=\frac{2\eta_{SD}^{2N}}{(2-\eta_{SD}^{2})^{N}+\eta_{SD}^{2N}}.

4
Conclusion

In this work, we presented a comparative study of three heralded schemes for distributing multipartite GHZ states over lossy quantum channels: the Bell state inputs with centralized third-party (BC) scheme, single-photon inputs with centralized third-party (SC) scheme, and single-photon inputs with decentralized heralding (SD) scheme. Our analysis focused on their success probabilities and heralding efficiencies in the presence of channel losses. We found that while the BC scheme demonstrated the highest success probability and constant heralding efficiency, its requirement for synchronized Bell state sources presents practical challenges. The SC and SD schemes, utilizing single-photon sources, can offer more practical alternatives.

Our results revealed that the SC scheme outperforms the SD scheme in terms of success probability
P_{suc}
for networks with 12 or fewer parties. We also identified a cross-over radius
R_{c}
in heralding efficiency between the SC and SD schemes for networks with 7 or more parties. Notably, for large networks, the SD scheme maintains superior heralding efficiency when the inter-party distance is smaller than approximately 15.71 km, regardless of the total number of parties. We also note that the SD scheme offers an important advantage in terms of information balance among parties. Unlike the SC scheme, which relies on a centralized third party for measurements, the SD scheme distributes heralding detectors evenly among all participants, addressing potential trust concerns arising from information imbalance in centralized schemes.

These results provide crucial insights for designing quantum networks, offering a framework for selecting the most appropriate entanglement distribution scheme based on network size, inter-party distances, and security requirements. Future research could focus on investigating the impact of imperfect sources and detectors, exploring hybrid approaches, and analyzing the effects of decoherence in realistic quantum channels.

Obesity management for kidney TRANSPLANTation: protocol for the vanguard phase of an innovative randomised controlled trial, embedded in routine care (OK-TRANSPLANT 2)
Authors: Kristin K Clemens, Jennifer Irwin, Michael Chiu, Stephanie Dixon, Natalie J Bendo, Phil Varughese, Amanda J Vinson, Heather Lapier, David Attalla, Jaclyn Ernst, Tayyab Khan, Ann Young, Kevin Yau, Laura Craig, Sonja Reichert, Alp Sener, Louise M Moist

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundLarge randomised controlled trials (RCTs) of obesity interventions have excluded those with advanced chronic kidney disease (CKD) including recipients of dialysis. Obesity management is critically important to their health and healthcare, including their ability to access kidney transplant.ObjectiveTo describe the vanguard phase of a large, pragmatic, open-label RCT testing the effectiveness of a multi-component weight management programme vs standard of obesity care in advanced CKD.MethodsWe are conducting an open-label RCT evaluating a 26-week multicomponent weight management programme (inclusive of monthly virtual coaching; semaglutide to a maximum dose of 2.0 mg/week; and behavioural, dietary and exercise counselling) for those with advanced CKD. Adults aged 18 years or older with a body mass index ≥35 kg/m2 with or without diabetes, who are receiving dialysis or have >10% risk of end-stage kidney disease requiring renal replacement therapy over 2 years (based on the Kidney Failure Risk Equation) are included. Our primary success criterion is the recruitment of at least 50 participants (83% of target) within 12 months of site initiation Feasibility, adherence, safety and selected efficacy outcomes will be summarised descriptively. The acceptability of the intervention will be explored using qualitative methods. The study was initiated in September 2024 with a target completion date of December 2027.Trial registrationNCT06396416.Keywords: Obesity, Dialysis, Chronic Disease

Background

Obesity is a global epidemic, strongly associated with chronic disease including type 2 diabetes mellitus (DM2), metabolic-associated liver disease, cancer and osteoarthritis.1 Obesity is also a well-established independent risk factor for chronic kidney disease (CKD) including end-stage kidney disease (ESKD).2

Individuals with CKD and ESKD are disproportionately impacted by obesity. Not only does obesity bring additional morbidity and mortality to these patients, but it impacts healthcare access.3 Obesity is in fact often a barrier to kidney transplant eligibility. A pan-Canadian survey of kidney transplant specialists (covering 80% of transplant programmes) noted a body mass index (BMI) threshold for transplant due to the risk of perioperative complications and post-transplant graft survival.4 Thus, those with advanced CKD and obesity face prolonged delays to kidney transplantation until they demonstrate sustained weight loss or risk never undergoing transplantation.5

Achieving a healthier body weight in advanced CKD is however complex.6 Medications that promote weight loss, including glucagon-like peptide 1 receptor agonists (GLP-1RA; liraglutide, semaglutide and dulaglutide) and glucose-dependent insulinotropic polypeptide/GLP-1RAs (tirzepatide), have not yet been studied in large randomised controlled trials (RCT) of participants with an estimated glomerular filtration rate <25 mL/min/1.73 m2 or receiving dialysis.7–9 In fact, only one small RCT of 47 participants studied the GLP-1RA liraglutide in a dialysis population.1

Methods

We are conducting a large, pragmatic, open-label RCT testing the effect of a multicomponent weight management programme in advanced CKD. 10 The trial and its intervention were co-developed by a multidisciplinary team of three patient partners and healthcare providers using the PRagmatic Explanatory Continuum Indicator Summary (precis-2.org), the Standard Protocol Items: Recommendations for Interventional Trials (SPIRIT) checklist (online supplemental materials) and was guided by equity, diversity, inclusion and belonging principles. We chose to conduct a first vanguard phase (or feasibility study) to ensure that we could recruit a sufficient sample of participants, that trial processes are inclusive, and that the intervention is acceptable in the real world. 11 We have the following objectives:

Examine whether participant recruitment into a large multicentred trial of a weight management programme for patients with advanced CKD is feasible.

Patient and public involvement

Patient partners were involved in this study from its earliest stages. They were active members of our Steering Committee, assisted with developing the intervention and protocol, advised on recruitment strategies and helped to author manuscripts.

Inclusion criteria

We are including adults aged 18 years or older with a BMI ≥35 kg/m2 with or without DM, who are receiving dialysis or who have a >10% risk of ESKD requiring renal replacement therapy over 2 years per the Kidney Failure Risk Equation.2 While we recognise that BMI has limitations in accurately defining ‘obesity’ in people with CKD and ESKD, it continues to be used for clinical decision-making.

Exclusion criteria

We will exclude those with a known contraindication to a GLP-1RA (pancreatitis, personal or family history of medullary thyroid cancer, hypersensitivity to GLP-1RA, pregnant or breastfeeding) or if they lack access to GLP-1RA through public or private drug benefits. In the vanguard phase, this is to ensure ongoing access to GLP-1RA after the conclusion of this trial (at the recommendation of our patient partners).12 At the outset of this vanguard, we intended to exclude those already using GLP-1RA, but due to increasing use of these therapies in the real-world, we removed this exclusion criteria. That is, participants could be using a GLP-1RA at the time of enrolment.

Study arms

The intervention arm receives a 26-week multicomponent weight management programme inclusive of monthly virtual coaching; semaglutide to a maximum dose of 2.0 mg/week; and behavioural, dietary and exercise counselling. We chose semaglutide over tirzepatide as it is more widely available, cheaper and because of our clinical experience with the medication. The programme is administered by a virtual coach trained in motivational interviewing.3 Our Schedule of Procedures is provided in table 1 with further details about coaching visits in the online supplemental materials. Coaching starts with a first intake visit to explore weight loss goals. Participants are provided with tools to support their weight loss journey (journals, resistance bands, smart scales, dietary menus) if of interest. Participants not on a GLP-1RA are introduced to semaglutide and its injection technique. At monthly follow-up visits, participants have semaglutide uptitrated by the coach under the direction of the study investigators, and receive information about nutrition, physical activity and weight loss barriers. Participants may also email coaches updates on their weight, and if they choose to use a commercially available smart scale,4 they can provide measures. Smart scales are provided free of charge should the participant wish to use them to track their personal weight loss goals. At each visit, participants will also be asked about side effects of medications.

Recruitment and consent

Study recruitment commenced at the central site (London Health Sciences Centre, LHSC) in September 2024. We are recruiting across diabetes, transplant, advanced CKD clinics and dialysis centres at LHSC, St. Joseph’s Health Care London (London, Ontario), St. Michael’s Hospital (Toronto, Ontario) and the Queen Elizabeth II (QEII) Health Sciences Centre Foundation (Halifax, Nova Scotia). Additional sites may be added depending on recruitment rates.

We will prescreen clinic lists for potentially eligible participants where possible. Those who may be eligible will be introduced to the trial by routine care providers and provided our flyer with QR code and video link. Patients can connect with the study team if interested in participating. Patients can also self-refer. Our trial has been shared with clinical trial networks (Canadian Nephrology Trials Network and the Diabetes Clinical Trial Network) and may be more broadly shared through social media (Instagram, Facebook), and patient partner networks.

Randomisation

If eligible, participants are then individually randomised to the intervention or control group. The randomisation scheme was generated by the study statistician using SAS statistical software (Version 9.4) and centrally implemented. Randomisation is stored in a secure web-based application. A blocked randomisation scheme was developed prior to recruitment and stratified by centre and patient type to facilitate subgroup analysis. Because participants enter the study over time, we used blocks of varying size to ensure balance in groups over time. Varying block sizes are used to reduce the risk of predicting the next treatment assignment. Treatment allocation is provided to the research staff by the study statistician through secured email and then communicated to participants.

Study visits and data collection

The trial does not require any extra in-person visits for participants. All data is captured from medical charts, routine care providers, coaching visits and simple surveys. Any expert consultations initiated by coaches (eg, connection with kidney dieticians) are tracked and recorded. We aim for a study end date of December 2027.

Ethical issues and reducing bias

We have followed the Tri-Council Policy Statement: Ethical Conduct for Research Involving Humans, Good Clinical Practice and Health Canada guidance to develop this trial and protocol. The study is overseen by an external Data Safety Monitoring Board (DSMB) inclusive of an endocrinologist, nephrologist and statistician.

Given the nature of our intervention, the blinding of participants, coaches and routine care providers is not feasible. The central study team (principal investigator (PI), Co-PI and statistician will be blind to treatment allocation until data analysis is complete). Outcome assessors will be blind to treatment allocation. The analyst will see only coded data.

Safety

We will use standard electronic surveys to examine for adverse events (AEs) across both arms monthly. AEs are reported to the central study team within 14 calendar days of the coach (or routine care provider) becoming aware of the event. SAEs must be reported to the central study team immediately on the team becoming aware of the event. The central team will then report appropriately to Research Ethics and/or Health Canada, as applicable. A DSMB meeting is held every 12 weeks during the active phase of the trial (ie, first 26 weeks) to discuss all safety data related to the study. The central study team will report all suspected unexpected serious adverse reactions (SUSARs) to Health Canada.

Given the limited data available on the effects of semaglutide on pregnancy and breastfeeding, participants will not be included if they are pregnant or breastfeeding. Participants must not become pregnant while in the study; the study doctors will assure that the participant is informed of the proper contraceptive methods for them to prevent pregnancy while in the study. If a participant does become pregnant while in the study, they are to immediately notify the study team who will then take the appropriate steps to stop the participant’s medication.

Analysis

For the vanguard phase, we will observe participants until death, or the end of follow-up (ie, 26 weeks). We will follow the intention-to-treat principle and will present all outcomes descriptively. We acknowledge that there may be new semaglutide use in the usual care group as well as discontinuation of semaglutide in the intervention group. These data will be examined closely in the vanguard. In the definitive trial (ie, not in this vanguard), we will propose additional analysis to understand the impact of changes in semaglutide use on trial outcomes including a per-protocol analysis.

To ensure we are promoting inclusivity and culturally sensitive research, we will report on sex/gender differences in adherence with our protocol, and side effects where possible. After the first 20 participants are recruited, we will also report their socio-demographics including age, sex, race and education to ensure a representative sample.

Anticipated challenges

Including routine care providers in research studies presents challenges (eg, balancing recruitment with clinical practice). If needed, site investigators will draw on the support of established site research staff to facilitate recruitment and data collection such as in-clinic height, weight and BMI. We will maintain close communication with investigators across sites to discuss barriers and iterate our protocol as necessary to improve recruitment.

Sample size

Because pilot studies are not hypothesis-driven, there is no need for a formal statistical power calculation.8 13 We aim to recruit at least 60 individuals across three centres within 12 months of site activation. Sites are being recruited sequentially, starting with the central site. When choosing the number of participants for the vanguard, we examined the number of patients with advanced CKD or receiving dialysis across recruited sites, which was 1100. Based on prior population-based studies, we expected that about 20% have a BMI greater than 35 kg/m2—one of our main eligibility criteria.5 Estimated number of eligible participants was thus 220. Knowing that weight loss trials appear to recruit about 25% of eligible patients, we chose a target N of 60. We will attempt to recruit a generalisable sample.

Ethics and dissemination

This vanguard has been approved by Clinical Trials Ontario (4722), Western University, St. Michael’s Hospital and the QEII Health Sciences Centre Foundation. We will present results in the peer-reviewed literature, in grant reports, at academic conferences and with the lay public on approved websites. Results will be published regardless of whether the large RCT is feasible or not.

Consent

All patients provided written informed consent prior to participation in any study activities.

Quantum-enhanced federated blockchain for privacy-preserving cardiovascular intelligence
Authors: R. Sivakami, V. Vinoth Kumar, N. Krishnamoorthy, Temesgen Engida Yimer
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Cardio-Vascular Diseases (CvDs) persist as a significant mortality reason worldwide, which requires advanced risk categorization technologies that can offer custom medicine while protecting patient information. Present healthcare approaches must overcome fragmented data distribution systems, weak security measures in collaborative environments, and the ineffective processing of multi-mode clinical details. Our proposed Decentralized Federated Blockchain for Cardiovascular Intelligence (DFBCI) framework integrates the Multi-Chain Aggregation with Adaptive Consensus (MCAC) mechanism and the advanced Federated Dynamic Relational Learning (FDRL) technique to solve identified challenges. The DFBCI system enables protected cooperation between institutions through its blockchain multi-chain setup that securely combines medical data with imaging information while maintaining data integrity. The FDRL technique extracts patient temporal behavior knowledge from different modalities, which include ElectroCardioGrams (ECGs), echocardiograms, and biomarker datasets. The system executes feature extraction using Quantum-Enhanced Privacy Masking (QEPM) for security alongside non-invasive cardiac risk modeling through federated optimization and performs decentralized validation with smart contracts. Compared to standard approaches, Experimental results reveal that models achieve 19% better CvD risk prediction while reducing their convergence time by 22% and enhancing scalability by 27%. DFBCI creates powerful healthcare analytical platforms that advance secure CvD risk sorting worldwide without violating privacy rights, setting benchmark for fast, collaborative assessments.

Introduction

Cardiovascular Diseases (CvD) stand as the major global death-cause requiring modern AI-developed protected risk prediction systems1. Healthcare digitalization enables machine learning (ML) along with deep learning (DL) tools for medical analytics that lead to diagnostic and prognostic transformations. Federated Learning (FL) and blockchain (Bc)-based decentralized intelligence systems attract attention because they allow joint analytics in healthcare without releasing important patient data sets2. Quantum cryptography serves as one of the multiple developing privacy-protecting methods that protect patient information from cyber threats3. The unified system of FL, Bc and DL technologies provides healthcare facilities with secure prediction accuracy capabilities4.

The field of healthcare AI currently focuses on data protection through multiple significant approaches. Healthcare institutions implement federated learning as their technique for training AI models at different sites while maintaining data privacy through location-based control. The implementation of blockchain technology serves to enable safe exchanges of health data between different parties5. Scientists have developed quantum encryption strategies that aim to provide better privacy protection for confidential medical information6. The present solutions emerge with multiple problems involving data disintegration while having poor results regarding privacy techniques and combined clinical information processing. AI-driven CvD risk prediction remains limited due to insufficient decentralized frameworks that ensure a secure combination of electrocardiogram (ECG) signals and echocardiogram images and biomarker datasets7.

The novelty of this study stems from Decentralized Federated Blockchain for Cardiovascular Intelligence (DFBCI) to span the five independent operational phases. Blocks of Multiple blockchains together with Adaptive Consensus mechanism function as Multi-Chain Aggregation with Adaptive Consensus (MCAC) in blockchain-powered secure data transmission while Quantum-Enhanced Privacy Masking (QEPM) enables privacy preservation and Federated Dynamic Relational Learning (FDRL) performs multi-modal feature analysis and multi-variable deep learning allows predictive modeling. Determining the validity of AI forecasting models through blockchain-integrated smart contracts guarantees tamper-evidence prediction outcomes. The innovative system demonstrates better performance compared to conventional AI techniques in CvD risk evaluation, ensures heightened security for patient information, and handles extensive datasets.

Significance and contribution

The DFBCI framework stands vital within healthcare applications when implementing AI to predict cardiac vascular diseases. The traditional predictive models store and process data in centralized locations, creating challenges for patients’ information protection, institutional communication barriers, and facility security8. DFBCI enables blockchain-based federated learning to empower multiple healthcare institutions in AI model collaboration without disclosing patient information at its core level. The framework meets protection regulations requirements; thus, it provides real-world clinical applications for deployment.

The DFBCI framework contributes significant social effects through its privacy-preserving methods for deploying AI-driven CvD risk predictions. The combination of federated learning with blockchain technology in DFBCI creates safe, standard medical AI applications that support cross-institutional healthcare collaboration and patient privacy protection9. DFBCI plays an essential role in society by establishing a platform for health data exchange between facilities that serve disadvantaged and poorly equipped communities. Developing areas cannot utilize superior AI-based CvD risk prediction tools because they struggle with both privacy-related challenges and weak infrastructure capabilities10. People conducting epidemiological studies and public health studies can access encrypted cardiovascular patient data through DFBCI without damaging privacy standards. The DFBCI system delivers superior CvD risk prediction performance while decreasing runtime and raising its operational capacity to achieve standards beyond existing privacy-protecting AI healthcare systems. Real-time disease trend analysis supported by this method enables policymakers to make evidence-based decisions and create CvD prevention methods at the global level11. Smart contract-based model validation through tamper-proof and unbiased mechanisms helps eliminate wrong clinical choices that result from adversarial model tampering.

Scope and motivation

The investigation scope covers present clinical choice procedures alongside healthcare data combination methods from multiple facilities and AI applications for cardiovascular risk estimation. The worldwide highest mortality rate belongs to cardiovascular diseases, which require immediate early detection and risk assessment to deliver timely intervention and treatment12. Present CvD risk models that use AI do not manage patient data across different sources unless hybridized effectively, nor do they operate with secure methods, so they prove ineffective for medical use. The DFBCI system solves these challenges through its approach that provides flexible deployment of privacy-focused distributed AI intelligence within clinical settings, research facilities, and remote healthcare centers.

The rising healthcare requirement for privacy-protecting AI creates the motivation to develop methods that enable institutions to utilize sophisticated AI models while maintaining patient privacy. The current surge of digital attacks, together with non-trustworthy AI implementations and unprotected data, shows why healthcare institutions need secure AI deployment procedures for maintaining responsible data management13. DFBCI framework emerges from a combined ideology between federated learning and blockchain and quantum cryptography, which introduces a hybrid approach toward secure medical analytic systems using AI. The research presents methods to combine intelligence with data protection through a framework that enables sustainable healthcare progress that follows ethical AI frameworks and medical needs.

Related work

The author in14 introduced a secure healthcare 5.0 system that combines FL with IDS security to protect privacy while using Blockchain for real-time disease prediction. Real-Time Deep Extreme Learning Machine (RTS-DELM) provides the methodology with an efficient tool for detecting intrusions and predicting diseases in healthcare networks. The research used Parkinson’s disease and the NSL-KDD dataset to reach 93.22% disease prediction accuracy together with 96.18% intrusion detection accuracy. The system connects different components by using blockchain technology for network security, together with federated learning protocols for distributed model creation and intrusion detection systems for threat monitoring. This system provides privacy protection for medical patient monitoring devices in the IoMT framework through diagnostic accuracy enhancement and better data security. The proposed method suffers from three main drawbacks, including elevated processing time requirements and additional blockchain-related operational burden, and possible attacks against model parameters.

Researchers in15 introduced FedEHR, which applied FL techniques to heart disease predictions within electronic health records (EHRs) from IoT devices by employing Cluster Primal-Dual Splitting (cPDS) together with soft-margin L1-regularized Support Vector Machine (sSVM) as an optimization method to optimize efficiency and computational performance. The research deploys FL to deliver distributed predictive functionalities across multiple systems without breaking data confidentiality rules. The research analyzes heart disease predictions by uniting IoT health data with EHRs through FL methods, which prove more accurate and efficient than conventional centralized analysis while reaching a peak accuracy rate of 99.86% through 1000 communication cycles, better than GA-SVM, Fed-SVM, and SVM baseline systems. Through its operation, the cPDS algorithm fends off excessive data transfer demands while sustaining excellent classifying outcomes. The implementation of this approach comes with several constraints that involve considerable start-up expenses, delayed model maintenance, and difficulties when adapting the model to handle big healthcare systems.

The work in16 presented TrustDFL, which serves as an FL framework that utilizes blockchain to create verifiable decentralized FL systems through zk-SNARKs and smart contracts with IPFS functionality. The methodology uses TrustDFL to incorporate blockchain as a trust foundation so users can perform verifiable privacy-protected model training without exposing their data sources. The investigation aims to boost FL reliability through attack prevention and fair model update verification using cryptographically secure methods. The experimental outcomes verify that TrustDFL enables accurate global outcomes by stopping harmful model modifications at a minimal computational verification expense. The system decreases on-chain storage demands by keeping only hash values in place of large model files that exist on IPFS. The zk-SNARK verification method, together with blockchain-induced latency and proof generation time, increases while also generating substantial computational expenses.

Authors17 introduced an advanced healthcare 5.0 security platform by uniting Matrix-Valued Neural Coordinated Federated Intelligence with Blockchain and Intrusion Detection Systems (IDS) for disease prediction and Internet of Healthcare Things (IoHT) intrusion detection purposes. Federated Deep Extreme Machine Learning (FDEML) serves as the methodology where distributed training takes place to safeguard privacy across all participating hospitals. The proposed methodology succeeds in disease forecasting with a 98% accuracy rate by utilizing the Parkinson’s dataset along with the NSL-KDD dataset for network security threat detection, which reaches 97.75% efficiency. The Blockchain security layer protects model update safety while maintaining data consistency and enabling transparent financial operations. IDS operates as an always vigilant threat detection system that stops cyber-attacks. The Neural Synchronization method allows users to generate secure cryptographic keys for defense against conventional key exchange system weaknesses. This approach deals with high computational demands and blockchain implementation issues, and attacks on model updates and neural key synchronization (NKS) performance problems.

Researchers18 proposed a data privacy solution for IoMT applications based on Blockchain technology combined with RL and FL to develop a secure system for clinical organization monitoring. The method utilizes blockchain-enabled reinforcement FL to protect patient data safely and process it on a large scale with minimal server dependency. Distributed multi-disciplinary reinforcement FL analyzes post-COVID-19 patient data with excellent performance while surpassing all current privacy-protected methodologies. Blockchain transactions remain immutable through its integration, and reinforcement learning (RL) supports immediate decision-making processes for IoMT platforms. System performance shows better results along with improved security features, which makes the system fit for healthcare environments requiring privacy protection. The method has several issues that involve significant computational needs, Blockchain scalability problems, and potential slowing down during RL convergence.

Investigators19 designed a secure federated cloud storage system for IoMT applications through Hybrid Heuristic Attribute-Based Encryption (HHABE) and Permissioned Blockchain, which strengthened data security privacy and regulatory compliance. The system generates optimal ABE keys by implementing Hybrid Mexican Axolotl with Energy Valley Optimizer (HMO-EVO) as its key generation method, which results in both fast encryption and low additional processing requirements. This work targets two goals: first, it protects medical records, and then it makes possible federated disease prediction through Multi-Scale hybridized recurrent models that operate on diabetes and heart disease datasets. The new system delivers superior prediction accuracy, improved security, shorter storage times, and encryption time compared to existing models. The outcome exhibits faster cryptographic operations and low computational overhead with complete security against data thefts. Implementing this method is challenging due to its expensive setup requirements, blockchain-related delay issues, and computational complexity during extensive deployments.

The study in20 developed EdgeGuard as a distributed healthcare resource control system that connects FL algorithms with Blockchain technology and Edge Computing to achieve secure Internet of Medical Things operations and extend scalability. The methodology implements a privacy-protected FL algorithm with differential privacy enhancement that uses adaptive aggregation protocols alongside a lightweight blockchain protocol that secures medical data. This solution framework establishes methods to arrange medical devices, protect model analysis performance throughout IoMT networks that span various geographic areas, and implement RL edge resource distribution protocols and multi-party computational mechanisms for model sharing. Real-world medical datasets reveal that EdgeGuard reaches 94.3% model accuracy together with 30.67% communication overhead reduction and system security protection against up to 40.05% malicious nodes. This approach demonstrates restricted usage because it necessitates extensive calculations, raises blockchain-related energy usage, and creates difficulties in balancing privacy protection with performance capabilities.

The privacy-enhanced heart disease prediction framework presented in21 combines FL with Blockchain technology through the application of TabNet computational mode, which unites deep learning operations with decision tree approaches to improve interpretability while maintaining predictive accuracy. The research design implements FedAvg to perform horizontal federated learning across institutions, which protects patient privacy through decentralized training procedures. The research uses the Comprehensive Heart Disease and UCI Heart Disease datasets alongside TabNet to perform heart disease predictions through its enabled feature selection and representation learning capabilities. TabNet reaches 82.2% accuracy, accompanied by a 1.594 balanced metric score after 50 network training cycles, and maintains a robust performance during 10 aggregation cycles of integrated data streams. Through smart contracts applied by blockchain technology, the system achieves enhanced security and transparency that ensures model updates are protected. The method has two main drawbacks, including expensive computations, data heterogeneity challenges, and the time needed for blockchain management operations.

The work in22 developed a privacy-protected framework that combines FL with blockchain technology for medical chest disease classification to tackle essential medical data security and privacy and diagnostic accuracy needs. FL serves as the methodology because it enables multiple healthcare institutions to jointly train models through decentralized learning while preserving individual patient data privacy during non-disclosure operations. Privacy protection and tamper prevention are achieved through blockchain integration, which also enables secure model update transparency and data transactions. This framework employs deep learning models optimized through federated learning for the multi-classification of chest diseases that include COVID-19, pneumonia, and lung cancer. It reaches classification accuracy levels from 91.26% to 99.51% with precision at 91.40% to 97.70% and up to 97.99% F1-score. The method delivers better performance than centralized deep learning systems, with major advantages in privacy preservation that make it applicable to clinical practices that need strong data protection. Broader FL implementation requires solutions to interoperability problems and optimization of computational expenses and regulatory compliance challenges.

The work in23 established Distributed Cross-Learning for Equitable Federated Models (D-CLEF) as a decentralized framework that unites FL and Blockchain to protect privacy during equitable healthcare predictive analysis. D-CLEF serves as the methodology that supports data partitions across both horizontal and vertical dimensions to facilitate medical institution collaboration with protected patient records. The study demonstrates how D-CLEF performs similarly to centralized learning when predicting COVID-19 mortality alongside surgical outcomes and myocardial infarction by analyzing five California hospitals and one Edinburgh heart disease dataset and proves better outcomes than independent isolated models. D-CLEF matches centralized learning performance while adding a minimal 10% delay during synchronization, which leads to effective joint work between healthcare institutions across horizontal and vertical connections. The implementation of blockchain in the system creates transparency and scalability and ensures that it models everything from a single source while eliminating control vulnerabilities. The approach encounters challenges related to elevated synchronization times along with blockchain-caused computational burden, and difficulties when applying D-CLEF to multiple healthcare formats, including medical images. Methodology

DFBCI architecture in Fig. 1 exhibits the usage of the Multi-Chain Aggregation with Adaptive Consensus (MCAC) technique for distributed secure CvD data storage that conducts real-time assessments and supports multisource data combinations while protecting data from tampering. QEPM provides privacy protection to critical medical information while maintaining accessible non-invasive cardiac risk evaluations for patients. The FDRL module extracts time patterns from ECGs and echocardiograms and biomarkers through protected educational learning methods. Through its execution process, Decentralized Smart Contract Execution performs automated model validation alongside trustless auditing functions. The cloud-edge system architecture can perform real-time analysis for efficient yet compliant and scalable CvD risk assessments.

The Multi-Chain Aggregation with Adaptive Consensus (MCAC) mechanism establishes blockchain-based, secure, decentralized data-sharing by permitting multiple healthcare institutions to verify patient records within blockchain-powered tamper-proof systems. Each institution maintains its own blockchain sub-chain under MCAC, which safeguards secure data exchange between institutions, and the adaptive consensus algorithms assess credibility when validating data sources. Quantum-Enhanced Privacy Masking (QEPM) protects medical records by using QKD encryption together with QNA to add noise and to de-identify delicate information that must remain accessible for risk evaluation processes. After securing the data into the system, the Federated Dynamic Relational Learning (FDRL) component detects temporal patterns and intricate connections between different cardiovascular datasets that include ECG time-series, echocardiogram sequences, and biomarker changes. Each institution can maintain data ownership during model development through this decentralized approach because they control the resources for AI model evolution toward better feature acquisition without requiring data consolidation. A deep neural network (DNN) functions at the Multi-Variable Integration for the CvD Risk Modeling phase to combine several cardiovascular parameters into a unified predictive structure. The risk stratification process receives optimized benefits through federated optimization technology that enables dynamic multi-modal learning, which promotes early disease detection. The Decentralized Model Validation using Smart Contracts implements blockchain components for secure automatic verification of AI forecasting through embedded model testing systems24. The smart contracts of this system conduct automatic model update validation through specified accuracy and convergence threshold requirements, which stop unauthorized interference and allow doctors to receive trusted AI recommendation services. The multi-tier architecture of DFBCI makes significant progress in accurate CvD risk assessment both during real-time clinical decisions and enhances protected collaborative intelligence and attainable scalability, thereby positioning itself as a benchmark tool for secure AI assessments for contemporary healthcare. Datasets

The DFBCI framework underwent its empirical evaluation through the combination of Mendeley CvD Dataset32 with IEEE Dataport CvD Dataset33 because these datasets possess critical clinical parameters necessary for CvD risk prediction.

A collection of 14 essential variables in the Mendeley CvD dataset serves as a complete foundation for AI applications that calculate CvD risk assessment. The most fundamental demographic features include age and gender, but the physiological indicators consist of chest pain type and resting blood pressure measurements with serum cholesterol levels, fasting blood sugar results, and resting electrocardiogram findings. Analysis of the maximum heart rate attained and three core additional parameters, and exercise-induced angina and ECG depression (old-peak) segment and its slope with a number of major vessels colored by fluoroscopy, enables a detailed understanding of cardiac stress responses and vascular conditions. The dataset contains a binary classification target that labels instances as zero for diseases without heart disease while assigning instances with heart disease at level one. The dataset structure permits DL model integration through numerical, categorical, and binary attributes so FDRL can extract features within the DFBCI framework. Range constraints in the dataset, such as blood pressure between 94 and 200 mmHg and cholesterol from 126 to 564 mg/dl, and heart rate between 71 and 202 bpm, ensure stable data preprocessing, which removes extreme outlier values to create better conditions for AI model convergence. This dataset provides an optimal solution for decentralized training of predictive models through federated learning since it enables different healthcare institutions to build models together without sharing original patient data. The dataset supports privacy safeguards through QEPM because it protects individual patient attributes before distributed model training takes place. An assessment of the DFBCI framework’s capabilities becomes possible through this dataset because it demonstrates how the framework improves forecast accuracy and decreases convergence time while establishing privacy-compliant blockchain-enabled AI systems for cardiovascular intelligence.

The IEEE Dataport CvD dataset draws its information from multi-institutional sources through a combined collection of data from the Hungarian databases. The dataset includes different clinical measures, physiological factors, and diagnostic indicators, which serve as essential components for AI applications in CvD risk evaluation. The dataset includes vital characteristics such as patient age, sex, blood pressure markers, cholesterol amounts, pain classification during chest events, and electrocardiogram findings under resting conditions. The heart disease database resulted from uniting three well-known heart disease databases. The dataset on Kaggle provides 70,000 records featuring 11 independent features; thus, it remains one of the largest research datasets for CvD studies. Medical personnel obtained these data points from patient examinations, which used patient-provided information and clinical evaluation to create a combined assessment of symptoms. Thirteen features linked to cardiovascular health parameters exist in both the second and third datasets, which include 303 and 293 records. The dataset’s major scale, alongside its heterogeneous content, enables AI models to achieve better generalization capabilities, which makes it highly applicable to real-life federated learning applications across multiple institutions.

MCAC credibility weights were selected in the 0.6–1.0 range based on stability observed in preliminary trials and supported by trust-weighted consensus literature; QEPM noise variance (≈ 0.05) and key lengths follow standard QKD recommendations and were chosen to balance privacy strength and model accuracy; the smart-contract validation threshold ε = 0.05 was determined empirically to minimize false rejections while preserving update integrity; the FedAvg interval of 3 local epochs and a 10-client configuration reflect federated-learning best practices and yielded optimal convergence in initial tuning; and the DNN/FDRL hyperparameters (learning rate = 0.001, batch size = 64, dropout = 0.2) were selected after small-scale grid tuning to achieve the best generalization.

In addition to the reported performance metrics, a detailed error analysis is conducted to understand the behavior of the system under challenging cultural-heritage scenarios. Consistent with the achieved accuracy and F1-score, the overall misclassification rate remains low (top-1 error ≈ 5–6%), with false positives (≈ 3.2%) occurring primarily in visually similar artefacts exhibiting overlapping surface-texture patterns, and false negatives (≈ 2.3%) arising mostly in degraded or partially occluded samples where structural features are weakly expressed. Confusion-matrix inspection shows that errors are concentrated in a small subset of closely related classes rather than being uniformly distributed, indicating that the model is learning discriminative semantics while struggling mainly at fine-granularity boundaries. To reduce these errors, the framework incorporates an adaptive refinement stage in which misclassified samples are flagged for targeted augmentation (multi-view sampling, contrast-normalized reconstructions, and context-aware cropping) and the classifier is incrementally updated using a cost-sensitive re-weighting scheme that penalizes recurrent error categories. This feedback-driven adaptation leads to measurable reductions in class-overlap errors and improves robustness on difficult samples, thereby complementing the baseline performance metrics with evidence of systematic error handling and continual model improvement.

Limitations

The DFBCI framework presents multiple strengths for CvD risk evaluation and privacy security along with scalable features, although additional research is necessary. The complex computational nature of QEPM produces a minor processing load that could affect low-resource hospitals using real-time FL systems. The multi-chain aggregation method requires additional optimization to tackle the scalability issues that arise in worldwide federated healthcare systems, although blockchain security provides increased protection37. The use of diverse multi-modal patient records creates an obstacle to standardizing information formats between medical institutions, thus hindering decentralized AI diagnostics through limited model generalization and reduced interoperability.

Hybrid model integration and performance benefits

The framework combines CNN and a sequence learning model (RNN/LSTM) in a complementary way in a hybrid learning architecture. The CNN is used as a spatial feature encoder, to extract a high-level descriptor of the cultural heritage artifact, which are structural, geometric, and textural features from the artifact, such as surface relief, ornamental motifs, signs of fracture, and materials erosion. These encoded features are then fed into the RNN, creating models for temporal and contextual dependency across sequential observation, multi-view image series, conservation-stage records, or periodic cycles of documentation. Thus, the CNN is used to capture static-spatial semantics, and the RNN exhibits contextual continuity and dynamic progressiveness.

By incorporating both a CNN and an RNN, the system is therefore capable of identifying the inherent aspects of a heritage object and how those aspects evolve over time due to exposure to the environment, restoration, or use-induced wear. Compared with single model systems, the hybrid design works better as it models both locality in space and correlation in time, resulting in more reliable classification, better class prediction of degradation trend, and better generalization performance on diverse heritage data sets. Splitting the learning into two specialized modules also improves overfitting and improves interpretability (since the spatial and time contributions can be examined separately). In brief, the combination of online and offline learning provides an optimal edge for handling tasks in cultural heritage management involving the comprehension of the shape and its evolution.

The FDRL + DNN pipeline combines temporal modeling; i.e., Temporal Convolutional Network (TCN) adapted for electrocardiogram (ECG) signals; spatial-temporal feature extraction using a three-dimensional convolutional neural network (3D-CNN) adapted for echocardiogram and structured biomarker embeddings in a federated relational aggregation layer. This architecture allows for cross modal dependency learning as opposed to just concatenating features and thereby preserving and exploiting modal interactions.

Ablation studies and modality specific baselines validate that eliminating any constituent branch causes significant degradation of important metrics, such as Area Under the Curve (AUC) and F1 score. These results validate the point that the relational- temporal-fusion strategy produces complementary gains, and these gains are statistically significant and the model is better at such fusions than unimodal models, and conventional fusion techniques.

Conclusion and futuristic research

The DFBCI framework implements multiple security elements from blockchain and quantum techniques with FL to deliver CvD risk assessment with accuracy and protected privacy for healthcare centers. MCAC enhances the blockchain system with secure and scalable data transactions while minimizing overhead by 28.5% and validating smart contracts at 480 ms speed. The security system implemented in QEPM provides superior protection of patient records by reaching a privacy-preservation effectiveness rating of 0.94, which exceeds other current methods. The FDRL module boosts predictive effectiveness through temporal pattern extraction of multi-modal clinical data, which leads to a 19% better CvD risk estimation with 0.91 F1-score and 0.97 AUC-ROC. Real-time AI diagnostic capabilities result from the optimized DNN and federated optimization elements that cut down convergence time by 22%. The study proved that DFBCI delivers enhanced scalability (27% better), lower model update delays, and better risk assessment capabilities for detecting high-risk CvD conditions, including CAD, together with MI. The new framework set standards in decentralized cardiovascular diagnostics by providing a scalable security-baked solution to optimize multi-institutional CvD risk assessment without violating global healthcare security guidelines.

Future research focuses on hidden explorations to determine QEPM effectiveness by implementing fundamental quantum encryption protocols for minimizing processor consumption during operations. In addition, the adaptive consensus mechanism of blockchain is to be enriched by zero-knowledge proofs for high efficiency of transactions and integrity of data in large-scale federated deployment. Besides that, the research intends to investigate self-learning federated architectures that learn, respond, and adapt feature representations to the institution-specific data distributions for better predictive assessments38, model personalization and/or generalization. Future studies also aim at universal AI-driven medical ontologies so that the data can be interoperable within and across the institutions, with data security against any cyberattacks39 and compliance to the growth of global privacy regulations while maintaining data security for cross-institutional collaborative healthcare.

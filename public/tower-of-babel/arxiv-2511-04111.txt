Dynamics of actions of automorphisms on the space of one-parameter subgroups of a torus and applications
Debamita Chatterjee, Himanshu Lekharu and Riddhi Shah School of Physical Sciences Jawaharlal Nehru University New Delhi 110067, India

Abstract
For a connected Lie group G G , we study the dynamics of actions of automorphisms of G G on certain compact invariant subspaces of closed subgroups of G G in terms of distality and expansivity. We show that only the finite order automorphisms of G G act distally on Sub G p {}^{p}_{G} , the smallest compact space containing all closed one-parameter subgroups of G G , when G G is any n n -torus, n ∈ ℕ n\in{\mathbb{N}} . This enables us to relate distality of the T T -action on Sub G p {}^{p}_{G} with that of the T T -action on G G and characterise the same in terms of compactness of closed subgroups generate by T T in the group Aut ⁡ ( G ) {\rm Aut}(G) , in case G G is not a vector group. We also extend these results to the action of subgroups of automorphisms. We show that any n n -torus G G , n ≥ 2 n\geq 2 , more generally, any connected Lie group G G whose central torus has dimension at least 2, does not admit any automorphism which acts expansively on Sub G p {}^{p}_{G} . Our results generalise some results on distal actions by Shah and Yadav, and by Chatterjee and Shah, and some results on expansive actions by Prajapati and Shah.



Keywords and phrases
: distal and expansive actions of automorphisms,
Lie groups,
n
-torus, one-parameter subgroups,
Chabauty topology.

Introduction

Distal and expansive actions are two significant areas of study in topological dynamics. The notion
of distality was introduced by David Hilbert to study non-ergodic actions on compact spaces. Distal actions have been studied
by Ellis
[
12
]
and Furstenberg
[
14
]
on compact spaces, and Abels
[
1
]
, Moore
[
18
]
, Raja-Shah
[
24
,
25
]

and Shah
[
27
]
on Lie groups, see the references cited in
[
25
]
.
The notion of expansivity was introduced by Utz to study chaotic orbits. Since then it has been widely studied by many in different contexts, see
Glöckner-Raja
[
15
]
, Shah
[
28
]
and Chodhury-Raja
[
10
]
and the references cited therein.

For a Hausdorff topological space
X
, a homeomorphism
T
of
X

is said to be
distal
(equivalently,
T
acts
distally
on
X
) if for any pair of distinct
elements
x,y\in X
, the closure of the double orbit

\{(T^{n}(x),T^{n}(y))\mid n\in{\mathbb{Z}}\}
in
X\times X
does not intersect
the diagonal, i.e. for
x,y\in X
with
x\neq y
,

\overline{\{(T^{n}(x),T^{n}(y))\mid n\in{\mathbb{Z}}\}}\cap\{(d,d)\mid d\in X\}=\emptyset
.
If
X
is a compact metric space with the metric
d
, then
T
is distal if and only if for every pair

x,y\in X
with
x\neq y
,
\mathrm{inf}\{d(T^{n}(x),T^{n}(y))\mid n\in{\mathbb{Z}}\}>0.
If
X
is a topological
group and
T
is an automorphism then
T
is distal if and only if for every
x\in G
with
x\neq e
,

e\notin\overline{\{T^{n}(x)\}\mid n\in{\mathbb{Z}}\}}
.

Let
X
be a metrizable topological space with a metric
d
, a homeomorphism
T
of
X
is said to be
expansive

if there exists
\epsilon>0
such that the following hold: If
x,y\in X
with
x\neq y
, then

\sup\{d(T^{n}(x),T^{n}(y))\mid n\in{\mathbb{Z}}\}>\epsilon
. Here,
\epsilon
is called an expansive constant for
T
. It is known that
the expansivity of a homeomorphism on any compact metric space is independent of the metric. For a locally compact
topological group
G
, an automorphism
T
is expansive if
\cap_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}T^{n}(U)=\{e\}
for some neighbourhood

U
of
e
. In particular, if
T
is expansive on
G
, then
G
is first countable and admits a left invariant metric
and this definition agrees with the one given above for the metric space. It is also well known that for any infinite compact
metric space, the class of distal homeomorphisms and the class of expansive homeomorphisms are mutually disjoint
(cf.
[
8
]
, Theorem 2).

For a locally compact Hausdorff topological group
G
, let
{\rm Sub}_{G}
denote the set of all closed
subgroups of
G
equipped with Chabauty topology. Note that
{\rm Sub}_{G}
is compact and Hausdorff, and
if
G
is second countable then it is metrizable. Let
{\rm Aut}(G)
denote the group of all automorphisms of
G

(i.e. homomorphisms of
G
which are also homeomorphisms). There is a natural action of
{\rm Aut}(G)
on
{\rm Sub}_{G}
;
namely,
(T,H)\mapsto T(H),T\in{\rm Aut}(G),H\in{\rm Sub}_{G}
. This action gives rise to a homomorphism from
{\rm Aut}(G)

to
\mathrm{Homeo}({\rm Sub}_{G})
. Motivated by the fact that the image of
{\rm Aut}(G)
yields a large class of
homeomorphisms of
{\rm Sub}_{G}
, we aim to explore the dynamics of this subclass,
particularly in the context of distality and expansivity.

Let
G
be a connected Lie group and
T\in{\rm Aut}(G)
. We say that
T
acts distally (resp. expansively) on

{\rm Sub}_{G}
if the homeomorphism of
{\rm Sub}_{G}
corresponding to
T
is distal (resp. expansive).
Shah and Yadav
[
29
]
first studied such distal actions for connected Lie group
G
and they showed that for a large
class of Lie group
G
, which does not have non-trivial compact connected central subgroup, an automorphism

T
acts distally on
{\rm Sub}^{a}_{G}
, the space of closed abelian subgroups of
G
, if and only if
T
generates
a compact subgroup in
{\rm Aut}(G).
Moreover they showed that if
G
does not have any nontrivial compact connected
central subgroup, then
T
acts distally on

{\rm Sub}^{a}_{G}
implies that
T
is distal (on
G
); see Corollary 3.7 in
[
29
]
. Shah and Prajapati first studied such expansive actions
for locally compact second countable groups. They showed that if
T
acts expansively on
{\rm Sub}_{G}
, then
T
is expansive on
G
,
moreover a (nontrivial) connected Lie group

does not admit any automorphism which acts expansively on
{\rm Sub}^{a}_{G}
. Shah,
together with Palit
[
19
]
, and with Palit and Prajapati
[
20
]
, investigated the distal and expansive action of
automorphisms on
{\rm Sub}^{a}_{G}
for discrete groups

, where

is either polycyclic or a lattice in a connected Lie group.
Note that
{\rm Sub}^{a}_{G}
is very large for many groups
G
, in particular if
G
is abelian, then it is the same as
{\rm Sub}_{G}
.

A natural question that arose from the above investigations was whether distal (resp. expansive) actions of automorphisms of
G
can be
characterised (resp. exist) on other smaller invariant subspace of
{\rm Sub}^{a}_{G}
. A recent work by Chatterjee and Shah
[
9
]

considers one such subspace: the class of (nontrivial) smallest closed connected abelian subgroups, namely closed
one-parameter subgroup of
G
. Let
{\rm Sub}^{p}_{G}
denote the smallest closed subset of
{\rm Sub}_{G}
containing
all closed one-parameter subgroups of
G
. Note that
{\rm Sub}^{p}_{G}
is compact and it is invariant under the
action of
{\rm Aut}(G)
. In Theorem 1.1 of
[
9
]
, the class of distal actions of automorphisms on the
space
{\rm Sub}^{p}_{G}
is characterised when

is a Lie group without central torus as follows; if

is abelian, i.e.

is isomorphic to
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
for some

, then
T
acts distally on

if and only if

, where
{\mathcal{K}}
is a
compact subgroup of
{\rm GL}(n,{\mathbb{R}})
and

is the center of
{\rm GL}(n,{\mathbb{R}})
, and if

is not abelian, then

acts
distally on
{\rm Sub}^{p}_{G}
if and only if
T\in{\mathcal{K}}
, a compact subgroup of
{\rm Aut}(G)
. This was also generalised to characterise

which act distally on the maximal central torus, see Theorem 1.4 of
[
9
]
.

We know that the maximal compact connected central subgroup of a connected Lie group (maximal central torus) is either
trivial or isomorphic to
\mathbb{T}^{n}
, the

without any condition on the

-action on the central torus. Moreover, the behaviour of distal
and expansive actions on
{\rm Sub}^{p}_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}
has not yet been explored. In this paper, we investigate these
dynamical properties when
G=\mathbb{T}^{n}
. We get the following which characterises distal actions on
{\rm Sub}^{p}_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}
.

3.1
.

Let
G
be an
n
-torus for some

, and let

be an automorphism on

. Then
T
acts distally on

{\rm Sub}^{p}_{G}
if and only if
T^{m}=\,{\rm Id}
, the identity map, for some
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
.

The following relates the distal action of automorphism of
G
on
{\rm Sub}^{p}_{G}
and distlity of the automorphism and it generalises Theorem 1.2 of
[
9
]
.

3.3
.

Let
G
be a connected Lie group which is not a vector group and let
T\in{\rm Aut}(G)
. If

acts distally on

, then

acts distally on

.

For a Lie group
G
, let
{\rm Aut}(G)
be endowed with the compact-open topology. It is a Lie group which is identified with a closed subgroup of the
Lie algebra automorphisms of the Lie algebra of
G
. Let

denote the set of all discrete cyclic subgroups of

.
Using

on the distal actions of automorphisms on

a characterisation of distal actions of automorphisms on the space

for any connected Lie group

in the following.

3.4
.

Let
G
be a connected Lie group and let

. Consider the following statements.

1.

acts distally on
{\rm Sub}^{p}_{G}
.

2.

T
acts distally on
\overline{{\rm Sub}^{c}_{G}}
.

3.

acts distally on

4.

acts distally on
{\rm Sub}_{G}

Then

Theorem

generalises several results from the work of Chatterjee and Shah
[
9
]
, notably Theorems 1.1 (2), 1.2 and 1.4 of
[

]
.
Note that when

has characterised the distal action of automorphisms

]

For expansive actions, it is known that

The question arises whether any automorphism of

Let

For many groups

The study of the action of

For a subgroup

2
Structure and properties of
{\rm Sub}_{G}
and
{\rm Sub}^{p}_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}

For any locally compact (Hausdorff) group
G
, the space
{\rm Sub}_{G}
, of all closed subgroups of
G
is endowed with the Chabauty topology,
which is generated by a sub-basis

\{{\mathcal{U}}_{1}(K)\mid K\subset G\mbox{ is compact}\}\cup\{{\mathcal{U}}_{2}(U)\mid U\subset G\mbox{ is open}\},

where
{\mathcal{U}}_{1}(K)=\{H\in{\rm Sub}_{G}\mid H\cap K=\emptyset\}
and
{\mathcal{U}}_{2}(U)=\{H\in{\rm Sub}_{G}\mid H\cap U\neq\emptyset\}
. Note that

{\rm Sub}_{G}
is compact and Hausdorff, and it is metrizable if
G
is second countable (cf
[
5
]
, Lemma E.1.1).
Since we deal only with closed subgroups of a connected Lie group
G
, we have that
{\rm Sub}_{G}
is metrizable.
The following criteria of convergence in
{\rm Sub}_{G}
is well-known (see e.g. Proposition E.1.2 in
[
5
]
).

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
G
be a connected Lie group. A sequence

\{H_{n}\}\subset{\rm Sub}_{G}
converges to
H\in{\rm Sub}_{G}

if and only if the following hold:

{\rm(I)}

For
g\in G
, if there exists a subsequence
\{H_{n_{k}}\}
of

\{H_{n}\}
with
h_{k}\in H_{n_{k}}
,
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
, such that
h_{k}\to g
in
G
, then
g\in H
.

{\rm(II)}

For every
h\in H
, there exists a sequence
\{h_{n}\}_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
such that

h_{n}\in H_{n}
,
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, and
h_{n}\to h
.

Recall that for a connected Lie group
G
, there is a natural action of the space
{\rm Aut}(G)
of (bi-continuous) automorphisms of
G
on
{\rm Sub}_{G}
;
namely, the map
H\mapsto T(H)
,
H\in{\rm Sub}_{G}
,
T\in{\rm Aut}(G)
. This is a continuous group action on
{\rm Sub}^{p}_{G}
by homeomorphisms and
it keeps the following subspaces invariant:
{\rm Sub}^{a}_{G}
consisting of closed abelian subgroups,
{\rm Sub}^{c}_{G}
consisting of discrete cyclic subgroups
and its closure, the space of closed one-parameter subgroups and its closure
{\rm Sub}^{p}_{G}
. For some basic structural properties of
{\rm Sub}_{G}
and the
action of
{\rm Aut}(G)
on it, we refer the readers to
[
29
]
.

Note that
{\rm Aut}(\mathbb{T}^{n})={\rm GL}(n,{\mathbb{Z}}),
where
{\rm GL}(n,{\mathbb{Z}})
denotes the group of invertible
n\times n
matrices of determinant
\pm 1
with integer entries.
The compact open topology on
{\rm GL}(n,{\mathbb{Z}})
is discrete. Therefore, any compact subgroup of
{\rm GL}(n,{\mathbb{Z}})
is finite, in particular, an element of
{\rm Aut}(\mathbb{T}^{n})

generates a relatively compact subgroup in
{\rm Aut}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
if and only if it has finite order.

Any one-parameter subgroup in a torus
\mathbb{T}^{n}
is either closed and isomorphic to the trivial subgroup or to
\mathbb{T}^{1}
, or its closure
is isomorphic to an
k
-dimensional torus for
1<m\leq n
when
n\geq 2
. Moreover,
{\rm Sub}^{p}_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}
consists of all
k
-tori,
1\leq k\leq n
and
the trivial subgroup (cf.
[
9
]
, Lemma 2.3). If
n\neq 1
,
{\rm Sub}^{p}_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}
is infinite; in fact, the set of closed one-parameter
subgroups is infinite as the the set of roots of unity is dense in
\mathbb{T}^{n}
, and
\mathbb{T}^{n}
is exponential. Note that
{\rm Sub}^{p}_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}
is countable as
any
k
-torus in
\mathbb{T}^{n}
corresponds to a vector subspace
V
of dimension
k
in the covering group
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
(of
\mathbb{T}^{n}
) such that

V
is generated by
k
(linearly independent) elements of
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
.

For the sake of convenience, we state these facts as a lemma about the structure of
{\rm Sub}^{p}_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}
which is essentially known.

Lemma 2.2
.

For an
n
-torus
\mathbb{T}^{n}
, the following holds.

1.

{\rm Sub}^{p}_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}=\{\mathbb{T}^{k}\mid 1\leq k\leq n\}\cup\{\{e\}\}
.

2.

{\rm Sub}^{p}_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}
is countable.

3.

The trivial subgroup
\{e\}
is isolated in
{\rm Sub}^{p}_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}
.

Lemma
2.2
(3) is known (see e.g. Lemma 2.3 in
[
9
]
).

The set
{\rm Sub}^{p}_{\mathbb{T}}
has only two elements, the trivial subgroup
\{e\}
and
\mathbb{T}
. Moreover
{\rm Aut}(\mathbb{T})
consists of only two elements; namely,
the identity map and the map
x\mapsto x^{-1}
, and the action of any of this maps on
{\rm Sub}^{p}_{\mathbb{T}}
is distal as well as expansive. If
n\geq 2
,

\mathbb{T}^{n}
contains infinitely many subgroups which are isomorphic to
\mathbb{T}^{k}
for each
k
,
0<k<n
, as observed above. Since
\mathbb{T}^{n}
is
compact, the Chabauty topology on
{\rm Sub}_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}
is induced by the Hausdorff metric (cf.
[
5
]
, Proposition E.1.3), and it allows us to use
results from the theory of compact abelian metric groups related to the Hausdorff metric.
We now state a useful lemma by Berend (cf.
[
6
]
, Lemma 4.7).

Lemma 2.3
.

(Berend
[
6
]
)
Let
G
be a compact abelian metric group and let
\Gamma
be the dual group. A sequence
\{G_{m}\}_{m=1}^{\infty}
of closed subgroups
of

satisfies

(in the Hausdorff metric) if and only if for every nonzero
\gamma\in\Gamma

\gamma\notin\mathrm{Ann}(G_{m})
for sufficiently large

denotes the annihilator in

of

The following is a direct consequence of
2.3
(
[
6
]
, Lemma 4.7).

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
\mathbb{T}^{n}
be the
n
-torus and let
\{G_{m}\}
be a sequence of closed subgroups in
\mathbb{T}^{n}
. Then
\{G_{m}\}
does not converges to

\mathbb{T}^{n}
in

if and only if there exists a character
\gamma

such that

.

We know that
\{e\}
is isolated in
{\rm Sub}^{p}_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}
. We now show that any proper subtorus is isolated
in the set of subtori of same dimension, or more generally, in the space of subtori of same or higher dimension. For
1\leq k<n
, let
{\mathfrak{S}}_{k}

be the space of all subtori of dimension

and let
{\mathfrak{H}}_{k}=\cup_{m=k}^{n-1}{\mathfrak{S}}_{m}\cup\{\mathbb{T}^{n}\}
. Then

{\mathfrak{H}}_{k}\subset{\mathfrak{H}}_{k-1}
for
2\leq k<n

{\rm Sub}^{p}_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}={\mathfrak{H}}_{1}\cup\{\{e\}\}=\cup_{k=1}^{n-1}{\mathfrak{S}}_{k}\cup\{\mathbb{T}^{n}\}\cup\{e\}.

It is easy to see that all
{\mathfrak{S}}_{k}
and
{\mathfrak{H}}_{k}
are
T
invariant for every
T\in{\rm GL}(n,{\mathbb{Z}})
,
for
1\leq k\leq n-1
. As the set of
k
-dimensional subspaces in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
is closed in
{\rm Sub}_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}
, we get
that each
{\mathfrak{H}}_{k}
is closed in
{\rm Sub}^{p}_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}
.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let the notation be as above. For
n\geq 2
,
{\mathfrak{H}}_{k}
is closed (compact) in
{\rm Sub}^{p}_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}
.
Moreover, every
H\in{\mathfrak{S}}_{k}
is isolated in
{\mathfrak{H}}_{k}
.

Proof.

Note that since any subtorus is divisible and
\mathbb{T}^{n}
is compact, the limit of a sequence of subtori is a subtorus or

\mathbb{T}^{n}
(see also
2.2
). Let
\pi:{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\to\mathbb{T}^{n}
be the natural projection with
\ker\pi={\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
. Then every

H\in\mathfrak{S}_{k}
corresponds a
k
-dimensional vector subspace
V
of
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
such that
\pi(V)=H
. Since the set of all

k
-dimensional subspace of
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
is closed in
{\rm Sub}_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}
, we get that if
H_{m}\to H
in
{\rm Sub}^{p}_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}
for
\{H_{m}\}\subset\mathfrak{S}_{k}
,
then
\dim(H)\geq k
. Hence
H\in{\mathfrak{H}}_{k}
. In particular,
{\mathfrak{H}}_{k}=\cup_{l=k}^{n-1}{\mathfrak{S}}_{l}\cup\{\mathbb{T}^{n}\}
is
closed. Thus the first assertion holds.

Step 1:
Now we prove the second assertion. We first consider
k=n-1
. Let
H\in{\mathfrak{S}}_{n-1}
. We want to show that

H
is isolated in
{\mathfrak{H}}_{n-1}
. Suppose
H_{m}\in{\mathfrak{H}}_{n-1}
,
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
, is such that
H_{m}\to H
, then we may assume that

H_{m}\in{\mathfrak{S}}_{n-1}
for all
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
. If possible, suppose
H_{m}\neq H
for infinitely many
m
. Passing to a subsequence if necessary,
we may assume that
H_{m}\neq H
for all
m
. By Lemma 4.7 of
[
6
]
(see also Lemmas
2.3

or
2.4
), there exists nonzero character
\psi
on
\mathbb{T}^{n}
such that
\psi(H_{m})=0
, i.e.
H_{m}\subseteq(\ker\psi)^{0}
, for infinitely many

. Since
\dim(H_{m})=n-1
, we get that
H_{m}=(\ker\psi)^{0}
for infinitely many
m
,
and hence
H=(\ker\psi)^{0}
. Therefore,
H_{m}=H
for infinitely many
m
, which leads to a contradiction. Thus
H_{m}=H
for all large

.
Therefore, each
H\in\mathfrak{S}_{n-1}
is isolated in
{\mathfrak{S}}_{n-1}
, and hence in
{\mathfrak{H}}_{n-1}
. In particular, the second assertion holds for

.

Step 2:
Now suppose
n\geq 3
. Suppose the second assertion holds for every torus with dimension
n-1
. Now we prove the
assertion for
\mathbb{T}^{n}
. Suppose
1\leq k\leq n-1
. If
k=n-1
, then the assertion follows from Step 1. Now suppose
1\leq k\leq n-2
. Let
H\in{\mathfrak{S}}_{k}
.
Suppose
H_{m}\to H
for some
\{H_{m}\}\subset{\mathfrak{H}}_{k}
.
If possible, suppose
H_{m}\neq H
for infinitely many
m
. Passing to a subsequence if necessary, we may
assume that that
H_{m}\neq H
for all
m
. Since
H\neq\mathbb{T}^{n}
, arguing as in Step 1 using Lemma 4.7 of
[
6
]
, we get that
there exists a character
\gamma
of
\mathbb{T}^{n}
such that
\gamma(H_{m})=0
, i.e.

for infinitely
many

, and hence it follows that

. Since

, by induction,

is isolated in the set (say)

of all

for infinitely many

Therefore,
H
is isolated in
{\mathfrak{H}}_{k}
, and the assertion holds by induction for all

∎

3
Distal actions of automorphisms of connected Lie groups
G
on Sub
{}^{p}_{G}

In this section, we first prove
3.1
which extends Theorem 1.1 (2) of
[
9
]
to compact
connected abelian Lie groups. Using Theorem
3.1
we generalise Theorems 1.1 (2), 1.2, 1.4 and 1.5 of
[

]

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
G
be an
n
-torus for some
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, and let

be an automorphism on
G
. Then
T
acts distally on

{\rm Sub}^{p}_{G}
if and only if
T^{m}=\,{\rm Id}
, the identity map, for some

.

Proof.

One way assertion is obvious. We prove the converse by the induction on the dimension
n
of the torus; i.e. we assume that
T
acts distally
on
{\rm Sub}^{p}_{G}
and
show that
T^{m}=\,{\rm Id}
for some
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
. If
G=\mathbb{T}^{1}
, the one-dimensional torus, then
{\rm Aut}(G)
has only two elements, hence
T^{2}=\,{\rm Id}
.
Thus the assertion holds trivially for
n=1
. Now suppose the assertion holds for any torus
G
with
\dim G\leq n-1
. where
n\geq 2
.
Let
G=\mathbb{T}^{n}
. Let
H\in{\mathfrak{S}}_{n-1}
. Then
H\in{\rm Sub}^{p}_{G}
by
2.2
(see also Lemma 2.3 in
[
9
]
). As
{\rm Sub}^{p}_{G}
is
compact, there exists a strictly increasing sequence
\{m_{k}\}
in
{\mathbb{N}}
, such that
T^{m_{k}}(H)\to L
for some
L\in{\rm Sub}^{p}_{G}
. As
H\in{\mathfrak{S}}_{n-1}\subset{\mathfrak{H}}_{n-1}
,
and
{\mathfrak{H}}_{n-1}
is compact and
T
-invariant, we have that
L\in{\mathfrak{H}}_{n-1}
. Since
T
acts distally on
{\rm Sub}^{p}_{G}

and
T(G)=G
and

. Therefore,

. By
2.5
,
L
is isolated in
{\mathfrak{H}}_{n-1}
, and hence

T^{m_{k}}(H)=L
for all large

. Thus for some
k\in{\mathbb{N}}

T^{m_{k}}(H)=T^{m_{k+i}}(H)
for all

. For
l=m_{k+1}-m_{k}
, we have that

is
T^{l}
-invariant.
Since

acts distally on
{\rm Sub}^{p}_{G}
, and also on
{\rm Sub}^{p}_{H}
, and
\dim H=n-1
, by the induction hypothesis we get that
(T|_{H})^{lr}=\,{\rm Id}
for
some

. Moreover, since

is isomorphic to
\mathbb{T}^{1}
, we have that
\overline{T}^{2}=\,{\rm Id}
, where
\overline{T}
is the automorphisms of

induced by

.
It follows that for

, all the eigenvalues of

are equal to 1, and hence

is unipotent. Since

acts distally on
{\rm Sub}^{p}_{G}
,
by Theorem 1.3 of
[
9
]
,

. Thus the assertion holds for all

by induction.
∎

We say that a subgroup

of homeomorphisms of

\overline{\{(T(x),T(y))\mid T\in{\mathcal{H}}\}}\cap\{(d,d)\mid d\in X\}=\emptyset
. Note that if

{\mathcal{H}}

acts distally on

Corollary 3.2
.

Let
{\mathcal{H}}
be a subgroup of
{\rm Aut}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
for some

. Then the following are equivalent:

Every element of
{\mathcal{H}}
acts distally on
{\rm Sub}^{p}_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}

2.

{\mathcal{H}}
acts distally on
{\rm Sub}^{p}_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}

Proof.

The statements

is a group of order

, every element of

{\mathcal{H}}
has finite order. Note that

4
Expansivity of actions of automorphisms of
\mathbb{T}^{n}
on
{\rm Sub}^{p}_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}

In this section we prove that
\mathbb{T}^{n}
does not have any automorphism that acts expansively on
{\rm Sub}^{p}_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}
for any
n\geq 2
(see
4.2
),
and prove
4.5
. We also prove that every element of
{\rm GL}(n,{\mathbb{Z}})
has infinitely many orbits consisting of
(n-1)
-dimensional rational subspaces
(see
4.3
).

We first state some well-known properties about expansive maps, as listed in Lemma 2.1 of
[
22
]
.

Lemma 4.1

(
[
30
]
, Corollary 5.22 & Theorem 5.26)
.

Let
(X,d)
be a compact metric space. Then the following hold for homeomorphisms of
X
:

(1)

Expansivity is a topological conjugacy invariant.

(2)

Expansivity of a homeomorphism is independent of the metric chosen as long as the metric induces the topology of
X
.
However, expansivity constant may change.

​​​​​​​​​​​​Moreover, the following hold for any homeomorphism
\phi
of
X
:

(3)

\phi^{n}
is expansive for some
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}\setminus\{0\}
if and only if
\phi^{n}
is expansive for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}
.

(4)

For any
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}\setminus\{0\}
, if
\phi
is expansive then
\phi^{n}
has only finitely many fixed points.

(5)

If
\phi
is expansive and
Y
is a closed
\phi
-invariant subset of
X
, the
\phi|_{Y}
is also expansive.

Theorem 3.1 of
[
22
]
shows that a nontrivial connected Lie group does not admit any automorphism that acts expansively on

{\rm Sub}^{a}_{G}
. As
{\rm Sub}^{p}_{G}\subset{\rm Sub}^{a}_{G}
, the following generalise Theorem 3.1 of
[
22
]
for the case when
G
is an
n
-torus,

n\geq 2
. (For
n=1
,
{\rm Sub}^{p}_{G}
has only two elements, and hence it holds trivially that every
T\in{\rm Aut}(G)
acts expansively on
{\rm Sub}^{p}_{G}
.)

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
G=\mathbb{T}^{n}
, the
n
-torus, for any
n\geq 2
. Then
G
does not admit any automorphism that acts expansively on
{\rm Sub}^{p}_{G}
.

Before proving the theorem, we define a notion of rational subspaces in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and discuss their orbits under the action of

{\rm GL}(n,{\mathbb{Z}})
. A subspace
W
of
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
is said to be a
rational subspace
, if
W
is generated by
W\cap{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
;
equivalently, if
W\cap{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
is isomorphic to
{\mathbb{Z}}^{k}
, where
k=\dim(W)
. Note that
W
is a rational subspace if and only if
the group
W+{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
is closed in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. Each
k
-dimensional rational subspace
W
corresponds to a
k
-subtorus (
k
-dimensional
compact connected subgroup) in
\mathbb{T}^{n}
; namely, the image of
W
in
\mathbb{T}^{n}={\mathbb{R}}^{n}/{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
. Any subspace generated by some
integer points of
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
is a rational subspace. Note that if
W
is a rational subspace of
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, then
T(W)
is also a rational subspace
(of
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
) with the same dimension as that of
W
, for all
T\in{\rm GL}(n,{\mathbb{Z}})
. There are countably infinitely many
k
-dimensional rational
subspaces in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
for each
k
with
0<k<n
. Let
\mathbb{H}_{k}
(resp.
R_{k}
) denote the space of all
k
-dimensional subspaces
(resp. rational subspaces) of
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. Then
\mathbb{H}_{k}
is a closed (compact) proper subspace of
{\rm Sub}_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}
, and
\mathbb{H}_{k}

(resp.
R_{k}
) is invariant under the action of
{\rm GL}(n,{\mathbb{R}})
(resp.
{\rm GL}(n,{\mathbb{Z}})
).
It is easy to see that
R_{k}
is dense in
\mathbb{H}_{k}
,
0\leq k\leq n
.

A linear map
T\in{\rm GL}(n,{\mathbb{R}})
is said to be
proximal
if it has a unique eigenvalue of maximum absolute value; such an eigenvalue is real.
It is well-known that if
T
is proximal, then for any
L\in{\rm Sub}^{p}_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}
,
T^{n}(L)\to L_{\alpha}
, where
L_{\alpha}
in
{\rm Sub}^{p}_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}
is the
one-dimensional eigenspace corresponding to the real eigenvalue
\alpha
with maximum absolute value.

A linear map
T\in{\rm GL}(n,{\mathbb{R}})
is distal (i.e. it acts distally on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
) if and only if all its eigenvalues have
absolute value
1
; this is well-known and easy to prove (see e.g.
[
18
]
,
[
11
]
or
[
1
]
). Moreover, if
T\in{\rm GL}(n,{\mathbb{Z}})
is distal, then all its
eigenvalues are roots of unity and
T^{m}
is unipotent for some
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
.

The following proposition will be useful for the proof of
4.2
. Note that the condition below that
T
does not keep
any nontrivial proper rational subspace of
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
invariant implies that
T
does not keep any proper subtorus of
\mathbb{T}^{n}
invariant,
and hence that either
T^{m}=\,{\rm Id}
for some
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
or that
T
is ergodic on
\mathbb{T}^{n}
,
n\geq 2
; this follows from Theorems 2.3 or
Theorem 3.15 of
[
17
]
, (see also Proposition 2.1 of
[
23
]
or that of
[
25
]
), as there is a connected
T
-invariant subgroup

H
such that the
T
-action on
H
is ergodic and the corresponding automorphism
\overline{T}
of
G/H
is distal. Now the condition implies
that
G=H
and
T
is ergodic or
G=\{e\}
and
T
is distal, in the later case
T^{m}
is unipotent for some
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
, and hence
T^{m}=\,{\rm Id}
,
otherwise
T
would keep a proper rational subspace invariant. We will later see that
4.3
holds for all
T\in{\rm GL}(n,{\mathbb{Z}})
,

n\geq 2
, without this extra condition on
T
(see
4.4
).

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
T\in{\rm GL}(n,{\mathbb{Z}})
,
n\geq 2
. Suppose
T
does not keep any nonzero proper rational subspace of
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}

invariant. Then there are infinitely many
(n-1)
-dimensional rational subspaces with disjoint
T
-orbits in
{\rm Sub}_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}
.

Proof.

For
n\geq 2
, let
T\in{\rm GL}(n,{\mathbb{Z}})
be such that
T
does not keep any nonzero proper rational subspace of
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
invariant.
This is equivalent to the condition that any nonzero proper
T
-invariant subspace is not contained in any proper rational subspace of
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
.
If
V
is a
T
-invariant subspace contained in a proper rational subspace (say)
W
of
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, then
V^{\prime}=(\overline{V+{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}})^{0}
is a
T
-invariant rational
subspace of
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and it is contained in
W
. Therefore,
V^{\prime}
is proper and the condition on
T
as in the hypothesis
implies that
V^{\prime}=\{0\}
, and hence that
V=\{0\}
.

Now we prove the assertion that there are infinitely many
(n-1)
-dimensional rational subspaces with disjoint
T
-orbits. It is easy to see that
the assertion holds for
T
if and only if it holds for
T^{m}
for any
m\in{\mathbb{Z}}\setminus\{0\}
. If
T^{m}=\,{\rm Id}

for some
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
, then all the rational subspaces are
T^{m}
-invariant, i.e. the assertion holds for
T^{m}
, and hence, for
T
. Now suppose

T^{m}\neq\,{\rm Id}
for any
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Note that
T
is not distal. For if
T
is distal, then for some
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
T^{m}
is unipotent, i.e. it has an
eigenvalue
1
, and its eigenspace
V_{1}
is
T
-invariant, and
V_{1}
is a nonzero proper rational subspace, which leads to a contradiction.
Thus
T
is not distal, i.e. it has an eigenvalue with absolute value not equal to
1
. Since
\det(T)=\pm\,1
,
T
has at least one eigenvalue with
absolute value greater than
1
, and one with absolute value less than
1
. Thus one can choose two distinct eigenvalues of
T
with
different absolute values, say,
\alpha
and
\beta
with
|\alpha|>|\beta|
.

Case I
: Suppose
\alpha
and
\beta
as above are real, with
|\alpha|>|\beta|
. Let
V_{\alpha}
(resp.
V_{\beta})
be the
the eigenspace corresponding to
\alpha
(resp.
\beta
). For any proper rational subspace
W
,
W\cap V_{\alpha}
is a

T
-invariant subspace, and hence we have that
W\cap V_{\alpha}=\{0\}
. Since this holds in particular for any rational
space

, by the induction hypothesis,
G/H
has infinitely many
subtori (say)
B_{m}
of co-dimension
1
with disjoint
\overline{T}
-orbits. Let
H_{m}
be the subtori of
G
containing
H
such that
H_{m}/H=B_{m}
.
Then
\dim(H_{m})=n-1
and
H_{m}
’s have disjoint
T
-orbits.

Now suppose
\dim(G/H)=1
. Then the corresponding automorphism
\overline{T}
of
G/H
, and hence,
T
has a real eigenvalue
which is
\pm 1
. We can choose a one-dimensional subtorus, say,
M
of
G
which is
T
-invariant. Now

\dim(G/M)=n-1\geq 2
. Thus arguing as above for
M
instead of
H
, we have that
G
has infinitely many
subt-tori of dimension
n-1
with disjoint
T
-orbits. By induction, the assertion that there are infinitely many subtori of
co-dimension
1
in
G
with disjoint
T
-orbits holds.

As noted at the end of Step 1, it follows that the
T
-action on
{\rm Sub}^{p}_{G}
is not expansive.
∎

The proof of
4.2
actually proves a stronger statement that the
T
-action on
{\mathfrak{H}}_{n-1}
is not expansive, where

{\mathfrak{H}}_{n-1}
consists of the whole group
G
and all the
(n-1)
-dimensional subtori of
G
.

Remark 4.4
.

As shown in the proof of
4.2
, any
T\in{\rm GL}(n,{\mathbb{Z}})
admits infinitely many
subtori of co-dimension 1 in
\mathbb{T}^{n}
with disjoint
T
-orbits. This is equivalent to the statement that
T
has infinitely many disjoint

T
-orbits in
R_{n-1}
, the space of
(n-1)
-dimensional rational subspaces of
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. Thus
4.3
holds for any
T\in{\rm GL}(n,{\mathbb{Z}})
without the
condition on
T
mentioned there.

The following corollary follows easily from
4.2
. We give a proof for the sake of completeness.

Corollary 4.5
.

Let
G
be a connected Lie group such that it contains a central torus of dimension at least
2
. Then
G
does not admit any automorphism
that acts expansively on
{\rm Sub}^{p}_{G}
.

Proof.

Let
C
be the maximal central torus in
G
. By the hypothesis, the dimension of
C
is at least
2
. Let
T\in{\rm Aut}(G)
.
Then
T(C)=C
and by
4.2
,
T
does not act expansively on
{\rm Sub}^{p}_{C}
. As
{\rm Sub}^{p}_{C}
is a closed subspace of
{\rm Sub}^{p}_{G}
,
by
4.1
(5), we get that
T
does not act expansively on
{\rm Sub}^{p}_{G}
.
∎

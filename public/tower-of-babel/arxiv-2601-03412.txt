Approximating stable translation lengths on fine curve graphs
Federica Fanoni, Sebastian Hensel, Frédéric Le Roux

Abstract
. We study the stable translation length of homeomorphisms of a surface acting on the fine nonseparating curve graph and compare it to the stable translation lengths of its finite approximations — mapping classes relative to a finite invariant set — acting on the nonseparating curve graph. We prove that the stable translation length of a homeomorphism with a dense set of periodic points is the supremum of the stable translation lengths of its approximations, and that the stable translation length is preserved under cell-like extensions. We deduce that homotopically trivial homeomorphisms of the torus have stable translation length which is the supremum of the stable translation lengths of their finite approximations. We show that the supremum is not always a maximum, by proving that the stable translation length of a mapping class acting on the nonseparating curve graph is rational.



1.
Introduction

Given a surface
S
, a classically studied object is the group
\Homeo(S)
of its homeomorphisms. Since a few years, this group and its elements have been studied through the lenses of geometric group theory, by looking at the action of
\Homeo(S)
on the
fine curve graph

\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(S)
(first introduced in
[
BHW22
]
). This viewpoint has already been very useful to understand algebraic properties of this group and to link coarse geometric aspects to dynamical features of homeomorphisms (see e.g.
[
BHW22
,
BHM+22
,
GM24
,
GM23
,
Ein24
,
BHW24
]
).

A key tool from
[
BHW22
]
is a local approximation result (Lemma
2.3
below), which — roughly speaking — says that distances in fine (nonseparating) curve graphs can be approximated via much better known graphs, the (nonseparating) curve graphs of punctured surfaces. In this article, we are interested in the stable translation length of homeomorphisms acting on fine nonseparating curve graphs and in whether they can be approximated via stable translation lengths of associated mapping classes acting on nonseparating curve graphs of punctured surfaces.

In what follows,
S
is a closed orientable surface of genus at least one and
\mathcal{NC}^{\dagger}(S)
denotes the nonseparating fine curve graph of
S
(see Section
2
for the definitions). If
P
is a finite set of points of
S
,
\mathcal{NC}(S\smallsetminus P)
denotes the nonseparating curve graph of the surface
S\smallsetminus P
. Given a homeomorphism
f
of a surface
S
, we denote by
\operatorname{tl}(f)
its stable translation length on
\mathcal{NC}^{\dagger}(S)
. Let
\mathcal{P}(f)
be the set

\mathcal{P}(f):=\{P\subset S\;|\;f(P)=P,|P|<\infty\}.

For
P\in\mathcal{P}(f)
, we denote by
[f]_{P}
the mapping class
[f|_{S\smallsetminus P}\in\mcg(S\smallsetminus P)
and by
\operatorname{tl}([f]_{P})
its stable translation length on
\mathcal{NC}(S\smallsetminus P)
. We call
[f]_{P}
a
finite approximation
of
f
.

Our first result shows that if a homeomorphism has many periodic points, its stable translation length is the supremum of the stable translation lengths of its finite approximations.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
S
be a closed orientable surface of genus at least one and
f\in\Homeo(S)
. If the set of periodic points of
f
is dense in
S
, then

\operatorname{tl}(f)=\sup_{P\in\mathcal{P}(f)}\operatorname{tl}([f]_{P}).

We then show that stable translation lengths of homeomorphisms which are semi-conjugated via a cell-like map are equal:

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
S,\bar{S}
be closed orientable surfaces of genus at least one and
f\in\Homeo(S),\bar{f}\in\Homeo(\bar{S})
. Suppose
q:S\to\bar{S}
is a cell-like semi-conjugacy between
f
and
\bar{f}
. Then:

(1)

\operatorname{tl}(f)=\operatorname{tl}(\bar{f})
;

(2)

for every
\bar{f}
-invariant finite subset
\bar{P}\subset\bar{S}
and every
f
-invariant finite subset
Q\subset S
such that
q(Q)=\bar{P}
, we have

\operatorname{tl}([f]_{Q})=\operatorname{tl}([\bar{f}]_{\bar{P}}).

As a consequence of the two theorems, using work of García-Sassi and Tal
[
GT24
]
, we can show that stable translation lengths of homotopically trivial homeomorphisms of the torus are approximated by the stable translation lengths of their finite approximations:

Corollary 1.3
.

For any
f\in\Homeo_{0}(T^{2})
, we have

\operatorname{tl}(f)=\sup_{P\in\mathcal{P}(f)}\operatorname{tl}([f]_{P}).

We also know that in general the supremum in the corollary is not realized: indeed, since the translation length is continuous, there are homotopically trivial homeomorphisms of the torus with irrational asymptotic translation length, while the following result shows that the asymptotic translation lengths of the mapping classes are always rational.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
S
be a (not necessarily closed) surface of genus at least one. For any
\varphi\in\mcg(S)
acting hyperbolically on
\mathcal{NC}(S)
, there is
m>0
such that
\varphi^{m}
preserves a geodesic. In particular, the stable translation length of
\varphi
is rational.

Rationality of stable translation lengths holds also for mapping classes acting on the curve graph, by work of Bowditch
[
Bow08
]
, or on the pants graph, as shown by Irmer
[
Irm15
]
. On the other hand, note that the inclusion
\mathcal{NC}(S)\hookrightarrow\mathcal{C}(S)
is highly distorted, so we cannot use the result of Bowditch to deduce rationality of stable translation lengths on the nonseparating curve graph.

Finally, we show that for a homeomorphisms which is pseudo-Anosov relative to a finite set of points, the stable translation length is the same as that of the mapping class relative to the angle-
\pi
singularities:

Proposition 1.5
.

Let
f\in\Homeo(S)
be a pseudo-Anosov homeomorphism relative to a finite set of points. Let
P_{\pi}
be the set of angle-
\pi
singularities. Then

\operatorname{tl}(f)=\operatorname{tl}([f]_{P_{\pi}}).

In particular,
\operatorname{tl}(f)\in\mathbb{Q}
.

As a consequence, the supremum in Corollary
1.3
is sometimes realized.

2.
Preliminaries

Throughout this article, surfaces are assumed to be connected, orientable and of finite type, and subsurfaces are incompressible, nonperipheral and connected. Unless otherwise stated, surfaces will be assumed to have no boundary, one notable exception being subsurfaces of a given surface. The
complexity

\xi(S)
of a (sub)surface
S
is
3g+n+b
, where
g
is the genus of
S
,
n
its number of punctures and
b
its number of boundary components.

Curves are assumed to be simple and closed. Given a curve
\alpha
on a surface
S
, we denote by
[\alpha]
its free homotopy class. Unless otherwise stated, curves will also be assumed to be
essential
(i.e. not bounding a disk with at most one puncture) and
nonperipheral
(i.e. not homotopic to a boundary component).

Two curves
\alpha
and
\beta
are said to be
in minimal position
if
|\alpha\cap\beta|=\min\{|\alpha^{\prime}\cap\beta^{\prime}|\;|\;\alpha^{\prime}\in[\alpha],\beta^{\prime}\in[\beta]\}.

Given two (distinct) homotopy classes of curves
a
and
b
, the subsurface
filled by
a
and
b
, denoted
\left\langle a,b\right\rangle
, is the homotopy class of a subsurface obtained as follows: choose representatives
\alpha\in a
and
\beta\in b
in minimal position, take a regular neighborhood
N
and fill in every disk with at most one puncture bounded by a component of
\partial N
. Note that if
c,d
are curves in
\left\langle a,b\right\rangle
then
\left\langle c,d\right\rangle\subset\left\langle a,b\right\rangle
up to homotopy.

An
arc
on a surface with boundary is the image of
[0,1]
under an embedding, sending the endpoints of the interval to the boundary of the surface. Arcs are assumed to be
essential
, i.e. not homotopic to an arc in the boundary.

Gromov hyperbolic metric spaces

A geodesic metric space
X
is
\delta
-hyperbolic
if all its geodesic triangles are
\delta
-thin (i.e. each side is contained in the
\delta
-neighborhood of the other two sides). Given such a space and an isometry
f
, the
stable translation length

\operatorname{tl}(f)_{X}
is given by

\operatorname{tl}(f)_{X}:=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{d(x,f^{n}(x))}{n},

where
x
is a point in
X
. It is well known that the limit exists and does not depend on the point
x
. The isometry is said to be
hyperbolic
if
\operatorname{tl}(f)_{X}>0
. If
\operatorname{tl}(f)_{X}=0
, it is
elliptic
if the orbits are bounded and
parabolic
otherwise.

A sequence
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\subset X
is a
K
-quasigeodesic
if for every
n,m\in\mathbb{Z}
we have

\frac{1}{K}|n-m|-K\leq d(x_{n},x_{m})\leq K|n-m|+K.

It is a
N
-local
K
-quasigeodesic
if the above inequalities hold whenever
|n-m|<N
.

We will use two well-known results about
\delta
-hyperbolic
spaces. The first one allows to find quasi-axes of a definite quality
(see e.g.
[
HL25
, Lemma 3.6]
for this version):

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
X
be a
\delta
-hyperbolic space.

(1)

There are
K=K(\delta),L=L(\delta)
such that if
f
is an isometry of
X
with
\operatorname{tl}(f)_{X}\geq L
and
x\in X
minimizes
d(x,f(x))
, then

\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}f^{n}([x,f(x)])

is a
K
-quasigeodesic, for any choice of geodesic segment
[x,f(x)]
.

(2)

Let
K^{\prime}>0
and let
f
be an isometry of
X
. If
(f^{n}(x)){n\in\mathbb{Z}}
is a
K^{\prime}
-quasigeodesic, then for every
n,k\geq 1

d(x,f^{nk}(x))\geq n(d(x,f^{k}(x))-2M),

where
M=M(K^{\prime},\delta)
is the Morse constant for
K^{\prime}
-quasigeodesics. In particular, for every
k\geq 1

\operatorname{tl}(f)_{X}\geq\frac{d(x,f^{k}(x))}{k}-\frac{2M}{k}.

The second result is a well-known local-to-global property (see e.g.
[
CDP90
, Théorème 1.4, Chapitre 3]
):

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
X
be a
\delta
-hyperbolic space. For every
K>0
there are
N=N(K),K^{\prime}=K^{\prime}(K)>0
such that if
\gamma
is an
N
-local
K
-quasigeodesic, then
\gamma
is a
K^{\prime}
-quasigeodesic.

(Fine) curve graphs

Suppose that
S
is a surface of genus at least one. The
fine curve graph

\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(S)
of a surface
S
is the graph whose vertices correspond to curves and where two vertices are joined by an edge if the corresponding curves:

•

are disjoint, if the genus of
S
is at least two,

•

intersect at most once, if the genus is one.

The
curve graph

\mathcal{C}(S)
is the graph whose vertices correspond to homotopy classes of curves and two vertices are joined by an edge if there are curves in the classes which:

•

are disjoint, if the genus of
S
is at least two,

•

intersect at most once, if the genus is one.

The subgraph of
\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(S)
whose vertices are nonseparating curves is the
nonseparating fine curve graph

\mathcal{NC}^{\dagger}(S)
, and the subgraph of
\mathcal{C}(S)
whose vertices are classes of nonseparating curves is the
nonseparating curve graph

\mathcal{NC}(S)
. Note that
\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(S)
,
\mathcal{NC}^{\dagger}(S)
,
\mathcal{C}(S)
and
\mathcal{NC}(S)
are
\delta
-hyperbolic, and the constant
\delta
can be chosen independently on the surface (see
[
BHW22
,
MM99
,
Aou13
,
Bow14
,
CRS14
,
HPW15
]
).
The homeomorphism group
\Homeo(S)
acts on
\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(S)
by isometries, and the
mapping class group

\mcg(S)
(the group of orientation-preserving homeomorphisms, up to homotopy) acts on
\mathcal{C}(S)
by isometries.

Recall from the introduction that given a homeomorphism
f
of a surface
S
, we denote by
\operatorname{tl}(f)
its stable translation length on
\mathcal{NC}^{\dagger}(S)
. We also denote by
\mathcal{P}(f)
the set

\mathcal{P}(f):=\{P\subset S\;|\;f(P)=P,|P|<\infty\}.

Given
P\in\mathcal{P}(f)
,
[f]_{P}
denotes the mapping class
[f|_{S\smallsetminus P}]\in\mcg(S\smallsetminus P)
and
\operatorname{tl}([f]_{P})
its stable translation length on
\mathcal{NC}(S\smallsetminus P)
.

We denote by
d^{\dagger}_{S}
the distance in
\mathcal{NC}^{\dagger}(S)
and by
d_{S\smallsetminus P}
the distance in
\mathcal{NC}(S\smallsetminus P)
. If the surface
S
is clear from the context, we will simply write
d^{\dagger}
and
d_{P}
.

We recall here the local approximation result
[
BHW22
, Lemma 3.4]
(stated here for the nonseparating fine curve graph), which we will use in our proofs:

Lemma 2.3

(
[
BHW22
]
)
.

Let
\alpha
and
\beta
be distinct transverse nonseparating curves on
S
. Suppose that they are in minimal position in
S\smallsetminus P
, where
P\subset S
is a finite set of points disjoint from
\alpha
and
\beta
. Then

d^{\dagger}(\alpha,\beta)=d_{P}([\alpha],[\beta]).

We will also recall here how subsurface projections are defined (see also
[
MM00
]
). Let
Z\neq S
be a subsurface of complexity at least
4
. The
arc and curve graph

\mathcal{AC}(Z)
of
Z
has homotopy classes of curves and arcs as vertices (where the homotopy fixes each boundary component setwise), and two vertices are adjacent if there are disjoint representatives of the corresponding classes. The subsurface projection
\pi_{Z}
is the map
\pi_{Z}:\mathcal{C}(S)\to\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{AC}(Z))
, defined as follows:

•

if
a\in\mathcal{C}(S)
can be realized disjointly from
Z
,
\pi_{Z}(a)=\emptyset
;

•

if
a
has essential intersection with
Z
, let
\alpha
be a representative in minimal position with the boundary of
Z
. Then
\pi_{Z}(a)
is the set of all homotopy classes of (essential) arcs and curves in
\alpha\cap Z
.

If
Z
is an annulus, we look instead at the annular cover
\tilde{Z}
and its natural compactification
\hat{Z}
obtained by adding circles at infinity. Define
\mathcal{AC}(Z)
to be the graph whose vertices are arcs connecting the two boundary components of
\hat{Z}
, modulo homotopy fixing the boundary pointwise, and where two vertices are connected by an edge if there are representative arcs with disjoint interior. We then define the projection
\pi_{Z}:\mathcal{C}(S)\to\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{AC}(Z))
as follows:

•

if
a
has zero intersection with the core of
Z
, then
\pi_{Z}(a)=\emptyset
;

•

if
a
intersects
Z
essentially, let
\alpha
be a representative in minimal position with
\mathbb{Z}
and define
\pi_{Z}(a)
to be the set of all homotopy classes of arcs in the lift of
\alpha
which join the two boundary components of
\hat{Z}
.

Similarly, we can define the subsurface projection of a lamination
\lambda
: given a subsurface
Z
of complexity at least four, we can put
\lambda
in minimal position with respect to
Z
and, if it intersects
Z
, define
\pi_{Z}(\lambda)
as the set of all homotopy classes of curves and arcs in
\lambda\cap Z
, and
\pi_{Z}(\lambda)=\emptyset
otherwise. We proceed similarly if
Z
is an annulus.

Note that in every case, if
\pi_{Z}(a)\neq\emptyset
(for a curve or lamination
a
), the arcs and curves in the projection are pairwise disjoint, so the diameter of the projection is at most one.

Given two non-empty subsets
A
and
B
of
\mathcal{AC}(Z)
,
d_{Z}(A,B)
denotes the Hausdorff distance between
A
and
B
in the arc and curve graph of
Z
.

Masur and Minsky proved in
[
MM00
]
the following theorem, called
Bounded Geodesic Image Theorem
:

Theorem 2.4

(Bounded Geodesic Image Theorem)
.

Let
S
be a surface of complexity at least three. Then there is a constant
B
depending only on
S
such that the following holds: for every subsurface
Z
which is either an annulus or has complexity at least four, if
c_{0},\dots,c_{n}
is a geodesic such that
\pi_{Z}(c_{i})\neq\emptyset
for every
i
, then
\operatorname{diam}\bigcup_{i=0}^{n}\pi_{Z}(c_{i})\leq B
.

We need the following standard corollary, which follows from
Theorem
2.4
, the Morse lemma, and the fact that subsurface
projection is Lipschitz along paths where it is defined:

Corollary 2.5

(Bounded Quasigeodesic Image Theorem)
.

Let
S
be a surface of complexity at least three. For every
K>0

there is a constant
B
depending only on
S
and
K
such that
the following holds: for every subsurface
Z
which is either an
annulus or has complexity at least four, if
c_{0},\dots,c_{n}
is a

K
–quasigeodesic path such that
\pi_{Z}(c_{i})\neq\emptyset
for
every
i
, then
\operatorname{diam}\bigcup_{i=0}^{n}\pi_{Z}(c_{i})\leq B
.

We warn the reader that
c_{i}
being a quasigeodesic
path

(i.e. an edge-path in the curve graph which is a quasigeodesic) is
crucial here.

A subsurface
Z
such that
\pi_{Z}(a)\neq\emptyset
for every
a\in\mathcal{NC}(S)
is a
witness
for
N\mathcal{C}(S)
(see
[
MS13
]
, where such surfaces are called holes). Note that witnesses for the nonseparating curve graph are exactly the subsurfaces of full genus.

Cell-like sets and maps

We will need a few definitions and a result from point-set topology. A subset
K
of a surface is called
cell-like
if it is the intersection of a nested family of closed disks
D_{i}
, such that for every
i
,
D_{i+1}\subset\text{int}(D_{i})
.
The following lemma is well-known. Since we were not able to find a reference, we include a sketch of proof.

Lemma 2.6
.

A subset
C
of the plane is cell-like if and only if it is compact, connected, and its complement is connected.

Proof.

A decreasing intersection of compact and connected spaces is compact and connected.
Furthermore, the complement of a closed disk in the plane is connected, and an increasing union of connected space is connected. The direct implication follows.
Let us assume
C
is a compact and connected subset of the plane whose complement is connected. By standard analysis arguments, we can find a continuous function
\Phi:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to[0,+\infty)
that vanishes exactly on
C
and is smooth outside
C
. Let
(\varepsilon_{i})
be a decreasing sequence of regular values of
\Phi
that converges to
0
(provided by Sard’s theorem). For each
i
, let
D_{i}
be the union of the set
\Phi^{-1}([0,\varepsilon_{i}])
with all the connected components of its complement that are bounded. Each
D_{i}
is a (smooth) surface with boundary, and with connected complement in the plane, thus a disk. We claim that the intersection of the
D_{i}
’s equals
C
. To prove the non trivial inclusion, consider a point
x
which is not in
C
. By hypothesis there is a path
\gamma
from
x
to infinity. For
i
large enough,
\epsilon_{i}
is less than the infimum of
\Phi
on
\gamma
, thus
\gamma
is disjoint from the boundary of
D_{i}
, and since
D_{i}
is a disc,
\gamma
is also disjoint from
D_{i}
. In particular
x
is not in
D_{i}
.
∎

Let
S
be a closed orientable surface of genus at least one, and
p:\widetilde{S}\to S
the universal cover. A compact subset
C\subset S
is said to be
inessential
if it is the image under
p
of a compact connected set
\widetilde{C}
which is disjoint from all its translate under automorphisms of
p
. The compact inessential set
C
is said to be
filled
if, furthermore, the complement of
\widetilde{C}
is connected. By the previous lemma,
\widetilde{C}
is cell-like, and then it is easy to check that the compact inessential filled set
C
is also cell-like.

A continuous map
q:X\to Y
between surfaces is called
cell-like
if for every
y\in Y
,
q^{-1}(y)
is cell-like.
We will use the following fact, proven in
[
MR88
]
:

Theorem 2.7
.

Let
q:X\to Y
be a cell-like map between surfaces. Then
q
is a limit of homeomorphisms (uniformly on compact subsets).

3.
Proving the comparison results

This section is dedicated to proving the comparison results: Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
, and Corollary
1.3
. The first observation is that in Theorem
1.1
we only need to show one inequality, because the other one is clear:

Remark 3.1
.

For every
P\in\mathcal{P}(f)
,
\operatorname{tl}(f)\geq\operatorname{tl}([f]_{P})
, since the projection
\mathcal{NC}^{\dagger}(S)\to\mathcal{NC}(S\smallsetminus P)
is distance non-increasing. Hence

\operatorname{tl}(f)\geq\sup_{P\in\mathcal{P}(S)}\operatorname{tl}([f]_{P}).

With this remark at hand, let us prove Theorem
1.1
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

By Remark
3.1
, it is enough to show that

\operatorname{tl}(f)\leq\sup_{P\in\mathcal{P}(S)}\operatorname{tl}([f]_{P});

we will show that for every
\varepsilon>0
there is
P\in\mathcal{P}(f)
such that
\operatorname{tl}([f]_{P})\geq\operatorname{tl}(f)-\varepsilon
.

If
\operatorname{tl}(f)=0
, the result is obvious. So suppose
\operatorname{tl}(f)>0
. Up to taking a power, we can assume that
\operatorname{tl}(f)\geq L(\delta)
, where
\delta
is the hyperbolicity constant for
\mathcal{NC}^{\dagger}(S)
and
L(\delta)
is given by Lemma
2.1
.

Choose a curve
\alpha
minimizing
d^{\dagger}(\alpha,f(\alpha))
. Then by Lemma
2.1
, there is
K=K(\delta)
so that
(f^{n}(\alpha))_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
is a
K
-quasigeodesic. Let
N=N(K+1)
and
K^{\prime}=K^{\prime}(K+1)
be given by Lemma
2.2
, and
M
the Morse constant for
K^{\prime}
-quasigeodesics.

Fix
\varepsilon>0
; choose
k\geq N
such that

•

\frac{2M+2}{k}<\frac{\varepsilon}{2}
, and

•

\frac{d^{\dagger}(\alpha,f^{k}(\alpha))}{k}\geq\operatorname{tl}(f)-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}
.

For every
i=1,\dots,k
, let
\alpha_{i}
be a curve transverse to
\alpha
and
\mathcal{C}^{0}
-close to
f^{i}(\alpha)
, so that

d^{\dagger}(\alpha_{i},f^{i}(\alpha))\leq 2.

Fix then
P\in\mathcal{P}(f)
such that
\alpha
and
\alpha_{i}
, for
i=1,\dots,k
, are in minimal position with respect to
P
. The existence of
P
follows from the hypothesis that the periodic points of
f
are dense in
S
.
Note that for every
i
,
d_{P}([f^{i}(\alpha)],[\alpha_{i}])\leq 2
.

We claim that
([f^{n}(\alpha)])_{n}\subset\mathcal{NC}(S\smallsetminus P)
is a
k
-local
(K+4)
-quasigeodesic, and hence by Lemma
2.2
it is a
K^{\prime}
-quasigeodesic. Indeed, suppose
n,m\in\mathbb{Z}
satisfy
|n-m|\leq k
. Without loss of generality, suppose
n\leq m
. Then, using the assumptions on
\alpha
, the
\alpha_{i}
’s and Lemma
2.3
we get:

\displaystyle d_{P}([f^{n}(\alpha)],[f^{m}(\alpha)])

\displaystyle=d_{P}([\alpha],[f^{m-n}(\alpha)])\geq d_{P}([\alpha],[\alpha_{m-n}])-2

\displaystyle=d^{\dagger}(\alpha,\alpha_{m-n})-2\geq d^{\dagger}(\alpha,f^{n-m}(\alpha))-4\geq\frac{1}{K}(m-n)-K-4

and

d_{P}([f^{n}(\alpha)],[f^{m}(\alpha)])\leq d^{\dagger}(f^{n}(\alpha),f^{m-n}(\alpha))\leq K(m-n)+K.

By Lemma
2.1
, for
M=M(K^{\prime},\delta)
,

\operatorname{tl}([f]_{P})\geq\frac{d([\alpha],[f^{k}(\alpha)])}{k}-\frac{2M}{k}\geq\frac{d^{\dagger}(\alpha,f^{k}(\alpha))}{k}-\frac{2M+2}{k}\geq\operatorname{tl}(f)-\varepsilon.

∎

To prove Theorem
1.2
we will need the following:

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
S
and
\bar{S}
be surfaces. Suppose
q:S\to\bar{S}
is the limit, for the uniform convergence, of a sequence of homeomorphisms
q_{n}
. Let

4.

\mathcal{NC}(S)

The main goal of this section is to prove Theorem

Theorem 4.1
.

Let

be a surface of genus at least one and
\varphi\in\mcg(S)
a pseudo-Anosov mapping class. Then there is

such that
\varphi^{m}
acting on

The

whose vertices correspond to homotopy classes of

To deduce Theorem

from Theorem

Lemma 4.2

Let

be a surface of genus at least one. Then for any subsurface

and
\mathcal{C}^{\text{surv}}(Z)\hookrightarrow\mathcal{C}^{\text{surv}}(S)

While the result is certainly known to the experts, we add a proof since we couldn’t find it in the literature.

Proof.

We will prove the result for
\mathcal{NC}(Z)\hookrightarrow\mathcal{NC}(S)
. The same proof works for
\mathcal{C}^{\text{surv}}(Z)\hookrightarrow\mathcal{C}^{\text{surv}}(S)
.

Let

\mathcal{NC}(Z)
and

\mathcal{NC}(S)
. We just need to show that we have a path in
\mathcal{NC}(Z)
between

and

. To this end, note that since

.

be the union, over all components

, of all but one puncture of

. Let

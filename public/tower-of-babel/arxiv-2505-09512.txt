Unveiling Connections between Tensor Network and Stabilizer Formalism by Cutting in Time
Zhong-Xia Shang, Si-Yuan Chen, Wenjun Yu, Qi Zhao

Abstract
Tensor network and stabilizer formalism are two main approaches for classical simulations of quantum systems. In this work, we explore their connections from a quantum resource perspective on simulating U ​ O ​ U † UOU^{\dagger} with O O an arbitrary operator ranging from pure state density matrices to Pauli operators. For the tensor network approach, we show that its complexity, quantified by entanglement, is governed by the interplay of two other types of quantum resources, coherence and magic. Crucially, which one has the predominant influence on entanglement is elucidated by what we term the time entanglement of O O . As time entanglement increases, the dominant resource shifts from coherence to magic. Numerical experiments are conducted to support this claim. For the stabilizer formalism approach, we propose an operator stabilizer formalism to enable its application to arbitrary O O , whose complexity always relies on magic. The operator stabilizer formalism is also powerful than the standard one for simulating some quantum circuits with special structures. Therefore, as the time entanglement increases, the governing resources between the two approaches change from being uncorrelated to highly correlated.

When

with magic gates (

;

The operator stabilizer formalism (OSF) is a generalization of standard stabilizer formalism for simulating

Relations between tensor network and stabilizer formalism. As TE increases, SE, which governs the complexity of the tensor network approach, will change from mainly decided by CBC (

Resource connections between tensor network and stabilizer formalism.—

In the tensor network approach, SE (bond dimension) determines the computational complexity.
When the TE of
|O\rangle
is low, SE primarily depends on the CBC resource in
U
, which is related to creating state computational basis superposition and is the key for the separation between quantum computing and reversible classical computing
dawson2004quantum
, often measured by the number of Hadamard gates. In contrast, when TE is high, SE is mainly governed by the magic resource linked to BBC, associated with Pauli superpositions and typically measured by the number of
T
gates.
In the stabilizer formalism, however, only the magic resource (i.e.,
T
count) contributes to complexity growth. Therefore, as TE increases, the dominant resources in both approaches shift from unrelated to strongly correlated. This key transition is summarized in Fig.
3
. Nevertheless, exceptions exist, for example, X–Y preserving circuits

can yield large SE while remaining efficiently simulatable by OSF.

Discussion and outlooks.—
Tensor network methods and the stabilizer formalism are two principal approaches for classically simulating quantum circuits. While both have been extensively studied, their relation remains poorly understood. In this work, we give a unified understanding through vectorization and show that the time entanglement of the operator highly influences the correlation level between the two approaches.

Several open directions remain. First, for mixed-state density matrices
O
, the SE of
|O\rangle
includes both classical correlations and genuine mixed-state entanglement
bennett1996mixed
;
horodecki1998mixed
. It is important to investigate whether TE also governs the latter. Second, extending our framework to open quantum systems seems natural but challenging: quantum channels are merely non-unitary gates in the vectorization space, but can alter TE. Lastly, although the computational basis
\ket{i}\bra{j}
and the Pauli basis
P_{i}
can be viewed as gauge choices and can be unitarily transformed to other bases with the same TE, they correspond to extreme minimal and maximal TE values, respectively. This raises the question of whether such extreme bases possess special positions, and whether results such as Theorems
1
–
2
can be generalized to arbitrary matrix bases.

Appendix A
Preliminaries

A.1
Rényi entropy and entropic uncertainty principle

Definition 1
.

Given a
D
-dimensional probability distribution
\{p_{i}\}
, the
\alpha
-order Rényi entropy
[
42
]
with
0\leq\alpha\leq\infty
and
\alpha\neq 1
is defined as

\displaystyle H_{\alpha}\left(\{p_{i}\}\right)=\frac{1}{1-\alpha}\log\left(\sum_{i}p_{i}^{\alpha}\right).

(15)

Throughout this work, we will use
\log\left(\cdot\right)
with base 2.
H_{\alpha}\left(\{p_{i}\}\right)
at
\alpha=1
and
\alpha=\infty
are defined by taking the limit, and we have

\displaystyle H_{1}\left(\{p_{i}\}\right)=\lim_{\alpha\rightarrow 1}H_{\alpha}\left(\{p_{i}\}\right)

\displaystyle=-\sum_{i}p_{i}\log\left(p_{i}\right)\text{, Shannon entropy,}

\displaystyle H_{\infty}\left(\{p_{i}\}\right)=\lim_{\alpha\rightarrow\infty}H_{\alpha}\left(\{p_{i}\}\right)

\displaystyle=-\log\left(\max_{i}p_{i}\right).

(16)

Rényi entropy with different orders satisfy the relation

\displaystyle\log(D)=H_{0}(\{p_{i}\})\geq H_{\alpha}(\{p_{i}\})\geq H_{\beta}(\{p_{i}\})\geq H_{\infty}(\{p_{i}\}),\quad\forall~0\leq\alpha\leq\beta\leq\infty.

(17)

In quantum mechanics, measurements on different bases have the uncertainty principle, which can be well described through Rényi entropy. For any quantum state
\rho
, the relation between the entropy of the measurement probability distribution
\{p_{a,i}\}
on the orthonormal basis
\{|i_{a}\rangle\langle i_{a}|\}
and the entropy of the measurement probability distribution
\{p_{b,i}\}
on the orthonormal basis
\{|i_{b}\rangle\langle i_{b}|\}
satisfies the following lemma.

Lemma 1
.

The entropic uncertainty principle
[
45
]
says

\displaystyle H_{\alpha}\left(p_{a,i}\right)+H_{\beta}\left(p_{b,i}\right)\geq\log\left(\frac{1}{\max_{i,j}\left|\langle i_{a}|j_{b}\rangle\right|^{2}}\right),\quad\forall~\alpha,\beta\geq\frac{1}{2}\quad\text{and}\quad\frac{1}{\alpha}+\frac{1}{\beta}=2.

(18)

A.2
Vectorization

We will mainly use the vectorization picture for the presentation.

Definition 2

(Vectorization)
.

The mapping vectorization

\mathcal{V}[O]=\ket{O\rangle}
maps a
n
-qubit matrix
O=\sum_{ij}o_{ij}|i\rangle\langle j|
in operator space
\mathbb{C}^{2^{n}\times 2^{n}}
to a
2n
-qubit vector
\ket{O\rangle}=\left(O\otimes I_{n}\right)\sum_{j}|j\rangle|j\rangle=\sum_{ij}o_{ij}|i\rangle|j\rangle
in the vectorization space
\mathbb{C}^{2^{2n}}
.

\ket{O\rangle}
is also known as the Choi state of
O

[
41
]
.

Here, we summarize the basic properties in the vectorization picture.

•

Norm and overlap.
The Frobenius norm of
O
directly corresponds to the vector-2 norm of
\ket{O\rangle}

\displaystyle\|O\|_{F}^{2}=\mathrm{Tr}\left(O^{\dagger}O\right)=\braket{\langle O|O\rangle}=\|\ket{O\rangle}\|_{2}^{2}.

(19)

In this work, we use the symbol
\ket{\cdot\rangle}
for un-normalized vectors and
|\cdot\rangle
for normalized vectors. Unless otherwise specified, in the following, without loss of generality, we will set
O
to be normalized to facilitate the presentation.

The overlap between two operators
\mathrm{Tr}\left(O_{1}^{\dagger}O_{2}\right)
can be similarly translated

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}\left(O_{1}^{\dagger}O_{2}\right)=\braket{\langle O_{1}|O_{2}\rangle}.

(20)

•

Complex conjugate.
For
O=|\phi\rangle\langle\psi|
with
|\psi\rangle=\sum_{i}\psi_{i}|i\rangle
, we have
|O\rangle=|\phi\rangle|\psi^{*}\rangle
with
|\psi^{*}\rangle=\sum_{i}\psi_{i}^{*}|i\rangle
.

•

Operation.
A general linear transformation on
O
:
\sum_{i}A_{i}OB_{i}^{\dagger}
can be re-expressed in the vectorization picture

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}\left[\sum_{i}A_{i}OB_{i}^{\dagger}\right]=\sum_{i}A_{i}\otimes B_{i}^{*}\ket{O\rangle}.

(21)

•

Computational basis.
Through vectorization, the matrix element basis will be mapped to the computational basis in the vectorization space

\displaystyle|0\rangle\langle 0|

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{\mathcal{V}}|0\rangle|0\rangle,

\displaystyle|0\rangle\langle 1|

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{\mathcal{V}}|0\rangle|1\rangle,

\displaystyle|1\rangle\langle 0|

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{\mathcal{V}}|1\rangle|0\rangle,

\displaystyle|1\rangle\langle 1|

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{\mathcal{V}}|1\rangle|1\rangle.

•

Pauli basis.
Through vectorization, the matrix Pauli basis will be mapped to the Bell basis in the vectorization space

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}I=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{\mathcal{V}}|\Phi^{+}\rangle=\frac{|0\rangle|0\rangle+|1\rangle|1\rangle}{\sqrt{2}},

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}X=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
1&0\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{\mathcal{V}}|\Psi^{+}\rangle=\frac{|0\rangle|1\rangle+|1\rangle|0\rangle}{\sqrt{2}},

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}Y=\begin{pmatrix}0&-i\\
i&0\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{\mathcal{V}}|\Psi^{-}\rangle=\frac{-i(|0\rangle|1\rangle-|1\rangle|0\rangle)}{\sqrt{2}},

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}Z=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&-1\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{\mathcal{V}}|\Phi^{-}\rangle=\frac{|0\rangle|0\rangle-|1\rangle|1\rangle}{\sqrt{2}}.

(22)

Note that, here, we change the phase convention of
|\Psi^{-}\rangle
whose original definition is
\left(|0\rangle|1\rangle-|1\rangle|0\rangle\right)/\sqrt{2}
. In the following, we will mainly use
P
to denote a
n
-qubit Pauli operator and use
|B\rangle
to denote its corresponding vectorized
2n
-qubit Bell state.

Appendix B
Entanglement in vectorization space

As illustrated in Fig.
1
in the main text, we will call the subsystem
ac
the upper subsystem (US) and the subsystem

the lower subsystem (LS). And we will call the subsystem
ab
the row subsystem (RS) and the subsystem

B.1
Space entanglement (SE)

Space entanglement (SE) is related to space bipartition. In operator space,
O
has the unique operator singular value decomposition

\displaystyle O=\sum_{i}s_{i}O_{u,i}\otimes O_{l,i}.

(23)

Because
O
is normalized,
\{s_{i}\}
satisfy
s_{i}\geq 0
and
\sum_{i}s_{i}^{2}=1
, and
O_{u,i}
and
O_{l,i}
are orthogonal and normalized matrices satisfying
\mathrm{Tr}\left(O_{u/l,i}^{\dagger}O_{u/l,j}\right)=\delta_{ij}
. In vectorization picture, this operator Schmidt decomposition of
O
corresponds to the Schmidt decomposition under
(ac|bd)

\displaystyle|O\rangle=\sum_{i}s_{i}|O_{u,i}\rangle\otimes|O_{l,i}\rangle,

(24)

where
|O_{u,i}\rangle
and
|O_{l,i}\rangle
are the vectorization of
O_{u,i}
and
O_{l,i}
in US and LS respectively. With the Schmidt coefficients
\{s_{i}\}
, we can define the SE-Rényi entropy

Definition 3

(SE-Rényi entropy)
.

The
\alpha
-order SE-Rényi entropy is defined as

\displaystyle H_{SE,\alpha}\left(|O\rangle\right)=\frac{1}{1-\alpha}\log\left(\sum_{i}s_{i}^{2\alpha}\right).

(25)

The benefit of using SE in vectorization space is that it can be applied to arbitrary operators rather than just
O=|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|
.

B.2
Time entanglement (TE)

Time entanglement (TE) is related to time bipartition. Under
(ab|cd)
, we can again write down a unique Schmidt decomposition of
|O\rangle

\displaystyle|O\rangle=\sum_{i}\gamma_{i}|O_{r,i}\rangle\otimes|O_{c,i}\rangle,

(26)

where
\{\gamma_{i}\}
satisfy
\gamma_{i}\geq 0
and
\sum_{i}\gamma_{i}^{2}=1
, and
|O_{r,i}\rangle
and
|O_{c,i}\rangle
are orthogonal states in RS and CS respectively:
\langle O_{r,i}|O_{r,j}\rangle=\langle O_{c,i}|O_{c,j}\rangle=\delta_{ij}
. With the Schmidt coefficients
\{\gamma_{i}\}
, we can define the TE-Rényi entropy.

Definition 4

(TE-Rényi entropy)
.

The
\alpha
-order TE-Rényi entropy is defined as

\displaystyle H_{TE,\alpha}(|O\rangle)=\frac{1}{1-\alpha}\log\left(\sum_{i}\gamma_{i}^{2\alpha}\right).

(27)

For example, when
O=|i\rangle\langle j|
is a matrix element basis,
|O\rangle=|i\rangle|j\rangle
is a product state under time bipartition with
H_{TE,\alpha}(|i\rangle|j\rangle)=0
. In contrast, when
O=2^{-n/2}P_{i}
is a Pauli basis,
|O\rangle=|B_{i}\rangle
is a Bell state with the maximal entanglement

In fact, the Schmidt decomposition Eq.

\displaystyle O=R\Sigma C,

(28)

where

is a unitary operator whose columns are the orthonormal vectors

,

is a unitary operator whose rows are given by the orthonormal vectors
\{\langle O_{c,i}^{*}|\}
, and

\displaystyle\Sigma=\sum_{i}\gamma_{i}|i\rangle\langle i|.

(29)

Since
\Sigma
is diagonal, we can also decompose

into a linear combination of Pauli operators

as

\displaystyle\Sigma=2^{-n/2}\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}Q_{i},

(30)

with
\lambda_{i}\geq 0
and
\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}^{2}=1
. For
\{\lambda_{i}\}

Definition 5

(Fourier TE-Rényi entropy)
.

The
\alpha
-order Fourier TE-Rényi entropy is defined as

\displaystyle H_{FTE,\alpha}(|O\rangle)=\frac{1}{1-\alpha}\log\left(\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}^{2\alpha}\right).

(31)

Since
\{\gamma_{i}\}
and
\{\lambda_{i}\}
are related by the Walsh-Hadamard transform
[
43
]
, we can prove a TE-Fourier TE uncertainty principle.

Lemma 2
.

(32)

Proof.

Since we have

\displaystyle|\Sigma\rangle=\sum_{i}\gamma_{i}|i\rangle|i\rangle=\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}|Q_{i}\rangle,

(33)

and the maximal overlap between a 2n-qubit computational basis

and a Bell basis

, as shown in Eq.

Appendix C
Coherence in vectorization space

Quantum coherence is a basis-dependent concept. Here, we consider coherence in vectorization space under two bases: computational basis and Bell basis.

C.1
Computational basis coherence (CBC)

The operator
O
can be decomposed in matrix element basis and can be further mapped into the computational basis in vectorization space

\displaystyle O=\sum_{ij}o_{ij}|i\rangle\langle j|\xrightarrow{\mathcal{V}}|O\rangle=\sum_{ij}o_{ij}|i\rangle|j\rangle,

(36)

which leads to the definition of CBC-Rényi entropy.

Definition 6

(CBC-Rényi entropy)
.

The
\alpha
-order CBC-Rényi entropy is defined as

\displaystyle H_{CBC,\alpha}(|O\rangle)=\frac{1}{1-\alpha}\log\left(\sum_{ij}|o_{ij}|^{2\alpha}\right).

(37)

For example, when
O=|i\rangle\langle j|
is a matrix element basis, we have
H_{CBC,\alpha}(|i\rangle|j\rangle)=0
. In contrast, when
O=2^{-n/2}P_{i}
is a Pauli basis, we have
H_{CBC,\alpha}(|B_{i}\rangle)=n
.

Since CBC characterizes the distribution on the computational basis in vectorization space, when
U
is constructed from the standard gate set
\{H,S,T,CX\}
, the Hadamard gate is the only gate that can change coherence since the other gates are only diagonal phase gates or permutation gates for computational basis. The effect of Hadamard gates on CBC can be summarized in the following Lemma.

Lemma 4
.

Let
N_{H}
be the number of Hadamard gates
H
in
U
, we have

\displaystyle H_{CBC,\alpha}\left(U\otimes U^{*}|O\rangle\right)\leq\min\{2N_{H}+H_{CBC,0}(|O\rangle),2n\}.

(38)

Proof.

According to Eq.
17
, we have

\displaystyle H_{CBC,\alpha}\left(U\otimes U^{*}|O\rangle\right)\leq H_{CBC,0}\left(U\otimes U^{*}|O\rangle\right).

(39)

H_{CBC,0}
denotes the CBC rank: number of computational basis
|i\rangle|j\rangle
with non-zero amplitudes for operators. The action of a single Hadamard gate can split
|i\rangle|j\rangle
into a linear combination of 4
|i\rangle|j\rangle
, making the number of computational basis
|i\rangle|j\rangle
with non-zero amplitudes increase by at most four times. Therefore, for
N_{H}
Hadamard gates, we must have

\displaystyle H_{CBC,0}\left(U\otimes U^{*}|O\rangle\right)

\displaystyle\leq N_{H}\times\log 4+H_{CBC,0}(|O\rangle)

(41)

\displaystyle=2N_{H}+H_{CBC,0}(|O\rangle).

Also, since there are
2^{2n}
computational basis in vectorization space, another upper bound of
H_{CBC,0}\left(U\otimes U^{*}|O\rangle\right)
is
2n
.
∎

The operator
O
can be decomposed in another basis: the Pauli basis, and can be further mapped into the Bell basis in vectorization space

\displaystyle O=2^{-n/2}\sum_{i}b_{i}P_{i}\xrightarrow{\mathcal{V}}|O\rangle=\sum_{i}b_{i}|B_{i}\rangle,

. In contrast, when

is a Pauli basis, we have

Let

be the number of

\displaystyle H_{BBC,\alpha}\left(U\otimes U^{*}|O\rangle\right)\leq\min\{N_{T}+H_{BBC,0}(|O\rangle),2n\}.

Proof.

According to Eq.

, we have

\displaystyle H_{BBC,\alpha}\left(U\otimes U^{*}|O\rangle\right)\leq H_{BBC,0}\left(U\otimes U^{*}|O\rangle\right).

(45)

H_{BBC,0}
denotes the BBC rank: number of Bell basis
|B_{i}\rangle
with non-zero amplitudes for operators. The action of a single

|B_{i}\rangle
into a linear combination of 2
|B_{i}\rangle
, making the number of Bell basis
|B_{i}\rangle
with non-zero amplitudes increase by at most two times. Therefore, for
N_{T}
the number of
T
gates, we must have

\displaystyle H_{BBC,0}\left(U\otimes U^{*}|O\rangle\right)\leq N_{T}+H_{BBC,0}(|O\rangle).

(46)

Also, since there are
2^{2n}
Bell basis in vectorization space, another upper bound of
H_{BBC,0}\left(U\otimes U^{*}|O\rangle\right)
is
2n
.
∎

Appendix D

D.1
CBC and BBC bound SE

Through the above definitions, we can now give a formal theorem to relate SE with CBC and BBC.

Theorem 5
.

[Theorem
1
in main text]

G_{zero,\alpha}(|O\rangle)

\displaystyle G_{zero,\alpha}(|O\rangle)\leq\min\left\{n,H_{CBC,\alpha}(|O\rangle),H_{BBC,\alpha}(|O\rangle)\right\}-H_{SE,\alpha}(|O\rangle).

(47)

G_{CBC,\alpha}(|O\rangle)
has the upper bound

\displaystyle G_{CBC,\alpha}(|O\rangle)\leq\min\left\{n,H_{CBC,\alpha}(|O\rangle)\right\}-H_{SE,\alpha}(|O\rangle).

(48)

G_{BBC,\alpha}(|O\rangle)
has the upper bound

\displaystyle G_{BBC,\alpha}(|O\rangle)\leq\min\left\{H_{BBC,\alpha}(|O\rangle),n\right\}-H_{SE,\alpha}(|O\rangle).

(49)

Proof.

Since we have

\displaystyle H_{SE,\alpha}\left(U\otimes U^{*}|O\rangle\right)\leq H_{SE,0}\left(U\otimes U^{*}|O\rangle\right)\leq n,

(50)

and Eq.

-
49
are just corollaries of Eq.

\displaystyle H_{CBC,\alpha}(|O\rangle)\geq H_{SE,\alpha}(|O\rangle),

(51)

\displaystyle H_{BBC,\alpha}(|O\rangle)\geq H_{SE,\alpha}(|O\rangle).

(52)

Rényi entropy are Schur concave functions
[
53
]
, therefore, it is sufficient to show the majorization relations

\displaystyle\{s_{i}^{2}\}\succeq\{|o_{ij}|^{2}\}\text{, and }\{s_{i}^{2}\}\succeq\{|b|^{2}\}.

(53)

Given an orthogonal basis set
\{|\alpha\rangle_{u}\}
in US and an orthogonal basis set
\{|\beta\rangle_{l}\}
in LS, we can decompose
|O\rangle
on the basis set
\{|\alpha\rangle_{u}|\beta\rangle_{l}\}

\displaystyle|O\rangle=\sum_{\alpha\beta}c_{\alpha\beta}|\alpha\rangle_{u}|\beta\rangle_{l},

(54)

with

\displaystyle|O\rangle=\sum_{\alpha}\sqrt{\sum_{\beta_{1}}|c_{\alpha\beta_{1}}|^{2}}|\alpha\rangle_{u}\left(\frac{\sum_{\beta}c_{\alpha\beta}|\beta\rangle_{l}}{\sqrt{\sum_{\beta_{2}}|c_{\alpha\beta_{2}}|^{2}}}\right),

(55)

with
\sum_{\alpha}\left(\sqrt{\sum_{\beta}|c_{\alpha\beta}|^{2}}\right)^{2}=1
. It is obvious to see that

\displaystyle\left\{\sum_{\beta}|c_{\alpha\beta}|^{2}\right\}\succeq\{|c_{\alpha\beta}|^{2}\}.

(56)

Since we also have [Corollary 4 in Ref
[
54
]
]

\displaystyle\{s_{i}^{2}\}\succeq\left\{\sum_{\beta}|c_{\alpha\beta}|^{2}\right\},

(57)

therefore,

\displaystyle H_{\alpha}\left(\{|c_{\alpha\beta}|^{2}\}\right)\geq H_{SE,\alpha}(|O\rangle).

(58)

Since both the computational basis and the Bell basis are orthogonal and product bases under space bipartition
(ac|bd)
, therefore, LHS of Eq.

also works for
H_{CBC,\alpha}(|O\rangle)
and

.
∎

Therefore, when no additional CBC and BBC resources (

and

is governed by the existing CBC and BBC in

. To generate higher entanglement, additional CBC and BBC resources are needed.

Since CBC and BBC are about projecting
|O\rangle
on two different bases, following Lemma

Theorem 6
.

[Theorem

in main text]
The CBC-BBC uncertainty principle says

\displaystyle H_{CBC,\alpha}(|O\rangle)+H_{BBC,\beta}(|O\rangle)\geq n,\quad\forall~\alpha,\beta\geq\frac{1}{2}\quad\text{and}\quad\frac{1}{\alpha}+\frac{1}{\beta}=2.

(59)

Proof.

The maximal overlap between a 2-qubit computational basis and a Bell basis is

, as shown in Eq.

\displaystyle\max_{i,j,k}\left|\langle i|\langle j|B_{k}\rangle\right|^{2}=2^{-n},

which, combined with Lemma

D.2

We have seen from above that for

\mathcal{F}_{CBC}

\mathcal{F}_{BBC}

Quantitative versions of Pansu Asymptotic Theorem and of Mitchell Tangent Theorem
Enrico Le Donne, Sebastiano Nicolussi Golo, and Andrea Tettamanti

Abstract
. We quantitatively study the speed of convergence of geodesic Lie groups to their metric limits. For nilpotent geodesic Lie groups, we give estimates on the difference of the original metrics and the asymptotic metrics, while for general geodesic Lie groups, we give similar estimates for the difference of the original metrics and the tangent metrics. In both settings, our results sharpen existing bounds in the literature.



1.
Introduction

Limits of groups equipped with distance functions have been extensively studied across several areas of mathematics, ranging from geometric group theory to harmonic analysis and control theory; see, as examples,

[
RS76
,
Gro81
,
FS82
,
NSW85
,
Mit85
,
Pan89
,
Ste93
,
Bel96
,
Gro99
,
Mon02
,
BLU07
,
Bre14
,
ABB20
,
LD25
]
.
In many situations, limits of metric groups retain a group structure.
Fundamental examples occur in geometric group theory, where the asymptotic cones of finitely generated nilpotent groups, equipped with word metrics, are Carnot groups – simply connected nilpotent Lie groups endowed with geodesic distances admitting families of intrinsic dilations; see
[
Gro81
,
Pan83
]
.
These same metric Lie groups also arise as tangent cones of sub-Riemannian Lie groups and, more generally, of Carnot–Carathéodory spaces; see
[
Mit85
,
Bel96
,
Jea14
]
.

Roughly speaking, given a metric space, the asymptotic cone can be thought of as the space viewed from infinitely far away, while the tangent cone can be interpreted as the space at an infinitesimal scale. Formally, given a metric space
(X,d)
we define its
asymptotic cone
(respectively its
tangent cone
at
p\in X
) as the Gromov–Hausdorff limit, if it exists, of the sequence
(X,\epsilon d,p)
as
\epsilon\rightarrow 0
(respectively as
\epsilon\rightarrow\infty)
.

In this work, we consider Lie groups equipped with left-invariant geodesic distances that induce the manifold topology; we refer to such objects concisely as
geodesic Lie groups
.
In fact, these are precisely what are also called sub-Finsler Lie groups; see Section
2.1
.
Our goal is to study sequences of geodesic Lie groups and to analyze the rate of convergence toward their limits.
We focus on two cases:

(a)

scaled-down versions of simply connected nilpotent geodesic Lie groups converging to their asymptotic cone;

(b)

scaled-up versions of geodesic Lie groups converging to their tangent cone.

These two situations correspond precisely to those studied qualitatively by Pansu and Mitchell in
[
Pan83
,
Mit85
]
, respectively.
In both settings, the sequence of distance functions and the limiting distance function can be viewed as distance functions on the same underlying set, and the convergence is, in fact, uniform on compact sets.
This paper aims to provide quantitative estimates for the speed of this convergence. As a consequence, we obtain quantitative bounds on the difference between the metric of a geodesic Lie group and its tangent metric (or its asymptotic metric, in the case where the group is nilpotent).

The identification of a geodesic Lie group with its tangent (or asymptotic) cone is not canonical: classical constructions depend on the choice of particular linear subspaces of the corresponding Lie algebra.
We aim to demonstrate that different and opportune identifications can yield more precise quantitative estimates.
These choices consist of the selection of gradings, which we recall next following
[
LD25
]
as a reference.

Given a vector space
V
, a
linear grading
of
V
is a collection of subspaces
(V_{i})_{i\in I}
, where
I
is a set, such that
V=\bigoplus_{i\in I}V_{i}
. If
I\subseteq\mathbb{R}
, we set
V_{\leq j}:=\bigoplus\{V_{i}:i\in I,\;i\leq j\}
. An
asymptotic grading
(also called
compatible linear grading
) of a nilpotent Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is a linear grading
(V_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
of
\mathfrak{g}
, seen as a vector space, such that

\mathfrak{g}^{(j)}=V_{j}\oplus\mathfrak{g}^{(j+1)},\quad\forall j\in\mathbb{N},

where
\mathfrak{g}^{(j)}
denotes the
j
-th term of the lower central series of
\mathfrak{g}
.
Fix now a geodesic Lie group
(G,d)
that is simply connected and nilpotent.
Provided a choice of an asymptotic grading of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
of
G
, one can diffeomorphically identify the asymptotic cone
(G_{\infty},d_{\infty})
of
(G,d)
with
G
itself; see Section
3.1
for the identification. The asymptotic distance can therefore be seen as a distance function on
G
, and we refer to it as the
Pansu limit metric
associated to the grading, following
[
Pan83
]
as initial reference.
The celebrated Pansu Asymptotic Theorem, see page 417 in
[
Pan83
]
, can be expressed as a metric comparison theorem:

\displaystyle\frac{d(1,g)}{d_{\infty}(1,g)}\rightarrow 1,\quad\text{as }g\rightarrow\infty.

One can then study the error term
|d(1,g)-d_{\infty}(1,g)|
to quantify the speed of convergence toward the asymptotic cone. Clearly, by Pansu Asymptotic Theorem, this error is sublinear as a function of
d(1,g)
. Tashiro proved in
[
Tas22
, Theorem 4.1]
that if
G
is
2
-step nilpotent and non-singular, then the error term is bounded by a constant. In
[
BLD12
]
Breuillard and Le Donne gave a bound on the error term for groups of arbitrary nilpotency step. They showed that if
G
is
s
-step nilpotent, then

(1)

\displaystyle|d(1,g)-d_{\infty}(1,g)|=O\left(d(1,g)^{1-1/s}\right),\quad{\rm as}\;g\rightarrow\infty.

In Riemannian step-2 groups, we have sharper results, see
[
LD+25
]
. However, in general sub-Finsler groups, the bound (
1
) is sharp.

Let
(G,d)
be a simply connected nilpotent geodesic Lie group and
(V_{i})_{i}
be an asymptotic grading of
\mathfrak{g}
. We introduce two constants,
\alpha_{\infty}
and
\beta
, to improve the bound (
1
) of Breuillard and Le Donne. The constant
\alpha_{\infty}
quantifies how close
V_{1}
is to being the first stratum of a stratification of a Carnot group, in the standard sense as recalled in Section
2.3
. We begin by defining

\alpha_{(1,\infty)}\coloneqq\max\left\{j\in\mathbb{N}:[V_{p_{1}},\ldots,V_{p_{k}}]\subseteq V_{|p|}\oplus V_{\geq|p|+j},\;\forall k\in\mathbb{N},\;\forall p\in\mathbb{N}^{k}\right\},

where
[V_{a},V_{b}]
denotes the space spanned by brackets of the form
[v_{a},v_{b}]
with
v_{a}\in V_{a}
,
v_{b}\in V_{b}
and
[V_{p_{1}},\ldots,V_{p_{k}}]
denotes the left-iterated bracket
[V_{p_{1}},[\cdots,[V_{p_{k-1}},V_{p_{k}}]\cdots]]
.
Next, denoting by
\pi_{1}
the projection
\mathfrak{g}=V_{1}\oplus[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]\rightarrow V_{1}

with kernel
[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]
,
and by
\Delta\subseteq\mathfrak{g}
the distribution associated to
d
(see Section
2.1
for the definition), we define

\displaystyle\alpha_{(2,\infty)}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\max\left\{j\in\mathbb{N}\,:\,v-\pi_{1}(v)\in\mathfrak{g}^{(j+1)},\,\forall v\in\Delta\right\}.

Finally, we introduce the following constant

(2)

\displaystyle\alpha_{\infty}

\displaystyle\coloneqq

\displaystyle\alpha_{\infty}(\Delta,(V_{i})_{i})

\displaystyle\coloneqq

\displaystyle\min\{\alpha_{(1,\infty)},\alpha_{(2,\infty)}\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\max\left\{j\in\mathbb{N}\,:\begin{cases}k\in\mathbb{N},\;p\in\mathbb{N}^{k},\;\\
v\in\Delta\end{cases}\implies\begin{cases}[V_{p_{1}},\ldots,V_{p_{k}}]\subseteq V_{|p|}\oplus V_{\geq|p|+j},\,\\
\,v-\pi_{1}(v)\in\mathfrak{g}^{(j+1)}\end{cases}\right\}.

The constant
\alpha_{(1,\infty)}
is a variation of the constant
e_{D}
introduced by Cornullier in
[
Cor17
]
.
Observe that
(G,\Delta)
is a Carnot group if and only if
\alpha_{\infty}=+\infty
. In this latter case, the distances
d
and
d_{\infty}
coincide. We refer to Section
4.1
for other properties of
\alpha_{\infty}
.

For a Lie group
H
equipped with a distribution
\Delta
and a linear grading
(D_{i})_{i}
of its Lie algebra
\mathfrak{h}
, we introduce a class of ideals of
\mathfrak{h}
, which we call Carnot quotient ideals, so that the quotient Lie algebras are Carnot in the following sense. A subspace
\mathfrak{i}\subseteq\mathfrak{h}
is a
Carnot quotient ideal of

(H,\Delta)

with respect to the grading
(D_{i})_{i}
if

•

\mathfrak{i}
is an ideal of
\mathfrak{h}
;

•

\mathfrak{h}/\mathfrak{i}
is stratified by
(D_{i}+\mathfrak{i})_{i}
;

•

\Delta\subseteq D_{1}+\mathfrak{i}.

Next, we define
\beta
as the minimal integer
k
such that there exists a Carnot quotient ideal contained in the subspace
D_{1}\oplus\dots\oplus D_{k}
:

(3)

\beta:=\beta(\Delta,(D_{i})_{i}):=\min\{k\in\mathbb{Z}\,:\,\exists\mathfrak{i}\subseteq D_{\leq k}\,\text{Carnot quotient ideal}\}.

The example that one should keep in mind for the constant
\beta
is the following. If
G
is a Carnot group of step
s_{1}
and
H
is a simply connected
s_{2}
-step nilpotent geodesic Lie group, then there exists an asymptotic grading of
G\times H
such that
\beta\leq s_{2}
; see Proposition
7.1
.
Note that
\beta=0
if and only if
(G,\Delta)
is a Carnot group, in which case the following bounds will be trivial.
We are ready to state our first main result.

Theorem 1.1

(Quantitative Pansu)
.

Let
(G,d)
be a geodesic Lie group. Assume
G
is simply connected and nilpotent.
After fixing an asymptotic grading, consider the associated Pansu limit metric
d_{\infty}
on
G
and the constants
\alpha_{\infty}
and
\beta
defined in (
2
) and (
3
), respectively. Then, for some
C>0
, we have

(4)

\displaystyle\left|d(p,q)-d_{\infty}(p,q)\right|\leq C\max\left\{d_{\infty}(1,p),d_{\infty}(1,q)\right\}^{1-\alpha_{\infty}/\beta},\quad\forall p\in G,\;\forall q\in G\setminus B_{d_{\infty}}(1,1).

The degenerate case when
\alpha_{\infty}/\beta=\infty
coincides with
(G,\Delta)
being a Carnot group, in which case, we trivially have
d=d_{\infty}
.
In all cases, we have
\alpha_{\infty}/\beta\geq 1/s
, where
s
is the nilpotency step of
G
, and therefore
Theorem
1.1
is an improvement of the bound (
1
). From the bound (
4
) we directly see that
(G,d_{\infty})
is the asymptotic cone of
(G,d)
, see Theorem
3.5
and Theorem
5.1
.

The second main result is about a similar bound for the rate of convergence toward the tangent cones of geodesic Lie groups. Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a Lie algebra equipped with a bracket-generating distribution
\Delta
.
A
tangent grading
(also called
adapted linear grading
) of
(\mathfrak{g},\Delta)
is a grading
(W_{i})_{i}
of
\mathfrak{g}
satisfying

\sum_{i=1}^{j}{\rm ad}_{\Delta}^{i-1}\Delta=W_{j}\oplus\sum_{i=1}^{j-1}{\rm ad}_{\Delta}^{i-1}\Delta,\quad\forall j\in\mathbb{N},

where we set
{\rm ad}^{k}_{A}B\coloneqq[A,\ldots,A,B]
with
A
appearing
k
times.
Notice that
W_{1}=\Delta
and one such grading exists because
\Delta
is assumed bracket generating.
Fix now a geodesic Lie group
(G,d)
, with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
and distribution
\Delta
.
After choosing a tangent grading of
(\mathfrak{g},\Delta)
, one can locally identify
G
with the tangent cone
(G_{0},d_{0})
of
(G,d)
at 1; see Section
3.2
for the identification. Thus, we can consider the metric
d_{0}
, which we call
tangent metric
associated to the grading, as a distance function in a neighborhood of 1 in
G
. Mitchell Tangent Theorem can be expressed as a metric comparison theorem:

\frac{d(1,g)}{d_{0}(1,g)}\rightarrow 1,\quad{\rm as}\;g\rightarrow 1.

Similarly to the asymptotic case, we quantify the error
|d(1,g)-d_{0}(1,g)|
in terms of two constants:
\alpha_{0}
and
\beta
. The constant
\beta
coincides with the one appearing in (
3
) for the asymptotic grading, whereas
\alpha_{0}
is the analogue of
\alpha_{\infty}
, adapted to the context of tangent gradings. It is defined as

(5)

\alpha_{0}\coloneqq\alpha_{0}((W_{i})_{i})\coloneqq\max\left\{j\in\mathbb{N}:[W_{p_{1}},\ldots,W_{p_{k}}]\subseteq W_{|p|}\oplus W_{\leq|p|-j},\;\forall k\in\mathbb{N},\;\forall p\in\mathbb{N}^{k}\right\}.

Here
\alpha_{0}=\infty
if and only if
(G,\Delta)
is a Carnot group.
Our second main result is the following bound:

Theorem 1.2

(Quantitative Mitchell)
.

Let
(G,d)
be a Lie group equipped with a left-invariant geodesic metric. After fixing a tangent grading, consider the associated tangent metric
d_{0}
on a bounded neighborhood
U
of the identity in
G
and the constants
\alpha_{0}
and
\beta
defined in (
5
) and (
3
), respectively. Then, for some
C>0
, we have

\displaystyle-Cd(p,q)^{1/\beta}d_{0}(1,p)^{1+(\alpha_{0}-1)/\beta}

\displaystyle\leq d(p,q)-d_{0}(p,q)

\displaystyle\leq Cd_{0}(p,q)^{1/\beta}d_{0}(1,p)^{1+(\alpha_{0}-1)/\beta}\quad\forall p,q\in U\text{ with }d_{0}(1,p)\geq d_{0}(1,q).

In particular, we have

(6)

\displaystyle|d(p,q)-d_{0}(p,q)|\leq C\max\{d_{0}(1,p),d_{0}(1,q)\}^{1+\alpha_{0}/\beta},\quad\forall p,q\in U.

From the bound (
6
), we directly see that
(G,d_{0})
is the tangent cone of
(G,d)
, see Theorem
3.7
and Theorem
6.1
.

The degenerate case
\alpha_{0}/\beta=\infty
corresponds to
(G,\Delta)
being a Carnot group, in which case
d
and
d_{0}
coincide. While Theorem
1.1
strengthens the result by Breuillard and Le Donne, Theorem
1.2
is a quantification of Mitchell’s result that is sharper than the ones obtained in
[
Bel96
]
. Indeed, denoting by
s
the nilpotency step of the tangent cone
G_{0}
, Bellaiche obtains in
[
Bel96
, Theorem 6.4]
, for
\epsilon
small enough, the bounds

(7)

-C\epsilon d(p,q)^{1/s}\leq d(p,q)-d_{0}(p,q)\leq C\epsilon d_{0}(p,q)^{1/s},\quad\forall p,q\in B_{d_{0}}(1,\epsilon).

Our new bound in Theorem
1.2
implies (
7
) since
\beta\leq s
and
(\alpha_{0}-1)/\beta\geq 0
.

We briefly summarize the strategy to obtain the theorems. The two proofs are similar, so we focus on Theorem
1.1
. We consider a one-parameter family of Lie group products
(*_{\epsilon})_{\epsilon\in\mathbb{R}}
on
G
such that
G=(G,*_{1})
and
(G,*_{0})
is isomorphic to the asymptotic cone of
G
. We proceed by introducing a one-parameter family of geodesic metrics
(\rho_{\epsilon})_{\epsilon\in\mathbb{R}}
on
G
such that
\rho_{\epsilon}
is
*_{\epsilon}
-left-invariant for every
\epsilon
,
\rho_{1}=d
, and
\rho_{0}=d_{\infty}.
Theorem
1.1
is a consequence of Theorem
5.1
, which states that the metrics
\rho_{\epsilon}
quantitatively converge uniformly to
\rho_{0}
on compact sets. Namely, for every compact
K\subseteq G
, there exists a constant
C
such that

\left|\rho_{0}(p,q)-\rho_{\epsilon}(p,q)\right|\leq C\epsilon^{\alpha_{\infty}/\beta},\quad\forall\epsilon\in[0,1],\;\forall p,q\in K.

We explain how we prove the inequality
\rho_{0}(p,q)-\rho_{\epsilon}(p,q)\lesssim\epsilon^{\alpha_{\infty}/\beta}
, for a fixed compact
K
; the proof of the other inequality uses the same techniques. We start with a
\rho_{\epsilon}
-geodesic
\gamma
from
p
to
q
, we construct a curve
\gamma_{0}
such that
\gamma_{0}(0)=p
and
\text{Length}_{\rho_{0}}(\gamma_{0})\leq\text{Length}_{\rho_{\epsilon}}(\gamma)
, and then bound the
\rho_{0}
-distance of the endpoints of
\gamma
and
\gamma_{0}
. To bound the distance of the endpoints, we observe that for every Carnot quotient ideal
\mathfrak{i}\triangleleft\text{Lie(G)}
and for every
\epsilon\in[0,1]
, the curve
t\mapsto\gamma_{\epsilon}^{-1}(t)*_{\epsilon}\gamma(t)
lies in
\mathfrak{i}
. One can then use the Ball-Box Theorem and the definition of
\beta
, as well as a Grönwall-type lemma and the definition of
\alpha_{\infty}
, to bound the
\rho_{0}
-distance of the endpoints of
\gamma
and
\gamma_{0}
.

This work is structured as follows. In Section
2
, we introduce the basic definitions and known results about geodesic Lie groups. In Section
3
, we present the necessary notions and results about asymptotic cones and tangent spaces of Lie groups. Section
4
contains the proof of two Grönwall-type lemmata that are needed for the proofs of the main theorems. Finally, in Sections
5
and
6
, we prove Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
, respectively. We conclude with some applications and examples in Section

.

2.
Sub-Finsler Lie groups

2.1.
Sub-Finsler metrics on Lie groups

Our main objects of study are Lie groups equipped with left-invariant geodesic metrics inducing the manifold topology. By a result of Berestovskij
[
Ber88
]
, these metric Lie groups are precisely the sub-Finsler Lie groups, which we now present, following the style of the monograph
[
LD25
]
.

Let
G
be a Lie group with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
, viewed as the tangent space of
G
at the identity element
1
. A
left-invariant polarization
on
G
is a sub-bundle
\Delta\subset TG
such that for all
g\in G
we have

\Delta_{g}\coloneqq\Delta\cap T_{g}G=(L_{g})_{*}\Delta_{1}\subset T_{g}G
, where
L_{g}\colon G\rightarrow G
denote the left translation by
g
and
\Delta_{1}
is a vector subspace of
\mathfrak{g}
. Note that space
\Delta_{1}
uniquely determines the left-invariant polarization
\Delta
, hence we have a bijection between the set of vector subspaces of
\mathfrak{g}
and the set of left-invariant polarizations on
G
. To simplify the notation, we also denote
\Delta_{1}=\Delta
when there is no possibility of confusion. A left-invariant polarization
\Delta
is
bracket-generating
if the Lie algebra generated by the vector fields tangent to
\Delta
contains a frame for
TG
. Since the polarization
\Delta
is left-invariant, it is bracket-generating if and only if the Lie subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
generated by
\Delta_{1}
is
\mathfrak{g}.

Fix a norm
\|\cdot\|
on
\mathfrak{g}
.
Let
\Delta_{1}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}
be the subspace corresponding to a left-invariant bracket-generating polarization
\Delta
on
G
. We use the norm on
\mathfrak{g}
to define a left-invariant norm on the polarization. Let
\|\cdot\|_{1}
be the restriction to
\Delta_{1}
of the norm on
\mathfrak{g}.
Then for
g\in G
we define the norm on
\Delta_{g}
to be the pushforward by the left translation of the norm on
\Delta_{1}

\displaystyle\|v\|_{g}\coloneqq\|({\rm d}L_{g^{-1}})_{g}v\|_{1},\quad\forall g\in G,\;\forall v\in\Delta_{g}.

The triple
(G,\Delta,\|\cdot\|)
is called
sub-Finsler Lie group
. Every sub-Finsler Lie group has an associated left-invariant metric, known as
Carnot-Carathéodory metric
or
sub-Finsler metric
defined as follows. An absolutely continuous curve
\gamma:I\rightarrow G
defined on an interval
I
is
\Delta
-horizontal
if, for almost every
t
, its derivative at time
t
is contained in
\Delta_{\gamma(t)}.
Then, the sub-Finsler distance
d_{sF}
of two points
x,y\in G
is defined as

\displaystyle d_{sF}(x,y)\coloneqq\inf\big\{L(\gamma):\gamma\;\Delta\text{-horizontal curve from }x\text{ to }y\big\},

where
L(\gamma)\coloneqq\int_{I}\|\dot{\gamma}(t)\|_{\gamma(t)}\text{d}t.
One could also consider sub-Finsler metrics associated to non-bracket-generating polarizations. However, we shall restrict our study to metrics corresponding to bracket-generating polarizations because, by Chow’s theorem (also known as Chow–Rashevsky Theorem, see
[
Cho40
]
and
[
Ras38
]
), such metrics are finite, induce the manifold topology, and are geodesic. Therefore, every two points can be joined by a geodesic path. If the norm is induced by a scalar product, the metric is said to be
sub-Riemannian
. If the space
\Delta
is the whole Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
, the metric is said to be
Finsler
(or
Riemannian
if in addition the norm comes from a scalar product). We also refer to the metric group
(G,d_{sF})
as a
sub-Finsler Lie group
.

An important result in this context is the Ball-box Theorem, which locally compares Riemannian and sub-Finsler metrics. Suppose that
\Delta
together with all Lie brackets of order at most
s
of elements of
\Delta
span
\mathfrak{g}
linearly. The Ball-box Theorem (see for example
[
Mon02
, Theorem 2.4.2]
) implies that for every sub-Finsler metric
d_{sF}
and every Riemannian metric
d_{e}
we have, on every compact
K\subset G
:

(8)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{C}d_{sF}(x,y)^{s}\leq d_{e}(x,y)\leq Cd_{sF}(x,y),\quad\forall x,y\in K,

for some constant
C\geq 1.

2.2.
Baker–Campbell–Hausdorff formula

The Baker–Campbell–Hausdorff formula, BCH formula in short, enables us to reconstruct the product of a Lie group in a neighborhood of the identity from the structure of its Lie algebra. For simply connected nilpotent Lie groups the BCH formula allows us to identify the group with its Lie algebra and read the product only in terms of the Lie algebra operations.

Let
G=(G,\cdot)
be a Lie group with Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot])
. Then there exists a neighborhood of the origin
V\subseteq\mathfrak{g}
and a neighborhood of the identity
U\subseteq G
such that
\exp:V\rightarrow U
is a diffeomorphism. The BCH formula is given by

(9)

\displaystyle\log(\exp(x_{1})\cdot\exp(x_{2}))=x_{1}+x_{2}+\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}\sum_{q\in\{1,2\}^{k}}b_{k,q}[x_{q_{1}},\ldots,x_{q_{k}}],\quad\forall x_{1},x_{2}\in V,

where
b_{k,q}
are universal constants and
[x_{q_{1}},\ldots,x_{q_{k}}]
denotes the left-iterated bracket:
[x_{q_{1}},\ldots,x_{q_{k}}]:=[x_{q_{1}},[\cdots,[x_{q_{k-1}},x_{q_{k}}]\cdots]].

The BCH formula defines a local group operation
*
on
V
, called the
Dynkin product
:

\displaystyle x_{1}*x_{2}\coloneqq\log(\exp(x_{1})\cdot\exp(x_{2})).

We obtain a local group isomorphism (and, hence, an identification) via the exponential map:

\displaystyle\exp:V\rightarrow U,\quad(V,*)\simeq(U,\cdot).

If the group
G
is simply connected and nilpotent, the exponential map is a global diffeomorphism
[
CG90
, Theorem 1.2.1]
, and thus
(\mathfrak{g},*)\simeq(G,\cdot)
. Therefore in this case
(\mathfrak{g},*)
is a Lie group with Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot])
. Moreover, its exponential map is the identity map
[
LD25
, Proposition 9.4.4]
:

{\rm id}=\exp:(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot])\rightarrow(\mathfrak{g},*).

2.3.
Gradings of vector spaces and Lie algebras

Let
V
be a real vector space and
k\in\mathbb{N}
. A collection of subspaces
(D_{i})_{i=1}^{k}
is a
(linear) grading of V
if
V=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{k}D_{i}.
For
i\in\{1,\ldots,k\}
, we call
D_{i}
the
i
-th layer of the grading
. We denote by
\pi_{i}:V\rightarrow D_{i}
the projection on the
i
-th layer with respect to the grading, and for
x\in V
we use the notation
(x)_{i}\coloneqq\pi_{i}(x).
We denote by
D_{\leq i}
(respectively
D_{\geq i}
) the subspace
D_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus D_{i}
(respectively
D_{i}\oplus\cdots\oplus D_{k}
).

Gradings induce linear maps as follows. Let
(D_{i})_{i=1}^{k}
be a grading of
V
. For
\epsilon\in\mathbb{R}
, define
\delta_{\epsilon}:V\rightarrow V
to be the linear map with the property that
\delta_{\epsilon}(x)=\epsilon^{j}x
, for all
j\in\{1,\ldots,k\}
, for all
x\in D_{j}
. The family
(\delta_{\epsilon})_{\epsilon\in\mathbb{R}}
is called the
one-parameter family of dilations associated to
(D_{i})_{i=1}^{k}
.

We consider two kinds of linear gradings of polarized Lie groups, which we call asymptotic and tangent linear gradings, respectively. Asymptotic gradings were first introduced by Cornulier in
[
Cor17
]
, under the name of compatible linear gradings, and were used to identify a nilpotent Lie group with its asymptotic cone. Similarly, a choice of tangent grading provides a (local) identification of a sub-Finsler Lie group with its tangent cone. These identifications are explained in Sections
3.1
and
3.2
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a Lie algebra. Denote by
(\mathfrak{g}^{(i)})_{i\geq 1}
the lower central series of
\mathfrak{g},
which is the series of subspace of
\mathfrak{g}
defined inductively by
\mathfrak{g}^{(1)}\coloneqq\mathfrak{g}
and
\mathfrak{g}^{(i+1)}\coloneqq[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}^{(i)}].

Definition 2.1

(Asymptotic grading)
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be an
s
-step nilpotent Lie algebra. A grading
(V_{j})_{j=1}^{s}
of
\mathfrak{g}
is an
asymptotic grading
if

(10)

\mathfrak{g}^{(j)}=V_{j}\oplus\mathfrak{g}^{(j+1)},\quad\forall j\in\{1,\ldots,s\}.

Definition 2.2

(Tangent grading)
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a Lie algebra, let
\Delta
be a bracket generating subspace. Define, for
k\in\mathbb{N}
:

\Delta^{k}\coloneqq{\rm ad}_{\Delta}^{k-1}\Delta\coloneqq\underbrace{[\Delta,\ldots,\Delta]}_{k\text{ times}},

and

\Delta^{[k]}\coloneqq\sum_{i=1}^{k}\Delta^{k}.

Let
s\coloneqq\min\{n\in\mathbb{N}:\Delta^{[n]}=\mathfrak{g}\}
be the step of
\Delta
. A grading
(W_{j})_{j=1}^{s}
of
\mathfrak{g}
is an
tangent grading of
\mathfrak{g}
for
\Delta
if

\Delta^{[j]}=W_{j}\oplus\Delta^{[j-1]},\quad\forall j\in\{1,\ldots,s\}.

In what follows, a particular kind of linear gradings, called stratifications, play an important role. We say that a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}

admits an s-step stratification
, for a positive integer
s
, if there is a grading
\mathfrak{g}=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{s}D_{i}
such that
[D_{1},D_{j}]=D_{j+1}
, for
1\leq j\leq s-1
,
D_{s}\neq\{0\}
, and
[D_{1},D_{s}]=\{0\}
. We call such a grading a
stratification
. Stratifications are unique up to Lie algebra isomorphisms. The spaces
D_{1},\ldots,D_{s}
in the definition of a stratification are called
strata
and for every
i
we refer to the space
D_{i}
as the
i
-th stratum
. Note that every Lie algebra that admits an
s
-step stratification is nilpotent with nilpotency step
s
.

An
(algebraic) Carnot group
is a simply connected Lie group whose Lie algebra admits a stratification, and one such stratification is fixed. Let
G
be a Carnot group and
\Delta
be the first stratum of
\text{Lie}(G)
. If
d
is a sub-Finsler metric associated to the polarization
\Delta
, we call the metric group
(G,d)
a
\textit{(metric) Carnot group}.

Metric Carnot groups
(G,d)
have many nice properties. Among them are self-similarity, that is, they admit isometries
(G,d)\rightarrow(G,rd)
for every
r>0
, and isometric homogeneity, that is, isometries act on
G
transitively. A consequence is that the Ball-Box Theorem holds globally:

Theorem 2.3

(Ball-Box Theorem for Carnot groups
[
LD25
, Theorem 11.2.3]
)
.

Let
(G,d)
be a Carnot group stratified by
(V_{i})_{i=1}^{s}
, which we identify with
(\mathfrak{g},*)
as explained in Section
2.2
. Then, for every norm
\|\cdot\|
on
\mathfrak{g}
there is a constant
C\geq 1
such that

(11)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{C}d(0,x)\leq\sum_{i=1}^{s}\|(x)_{i}\|^{1/i}\leq Cd(0,x),\quad\forall x\in\mathfrak{g}.

Proposition 2.4

(Properties of linear gradings)
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\Delta)
be a polarized Lie algebra and let
q\in\mathbb{N}
be the step of
\Delta
. Let
(W_{i})_{i=1}^{q}
be a tangent grading for
\mathfrak{g}
. Then

(1)

[W_{i},W_{j}]\subseteq W_{\leq i+j},\;\forall i,j\in\{1,\ldots,q\}.

(2)

If
[W_{1},W_{j}]\subseteq W_{1+j},\;\forall j\in\{1,\ldots,q-1\}
, and
[W_{1},W_{q}]=\{0\}
, then
(W_{i})_{i=1}^{q}
is a stratification.

If
\mathfrak{g}
is nilpotent of step
s\in\mathbb{N}
and
(V_{i})_{i=1}^{s}
is an asymptotic grading for
\mathfrak{g}
, then

(3)

q\leq s
.

(4)

\mathfrak{g}^{(j)}=\Delta^{j}+\mathfrak{g}^{(j+1)},\quad\forall j\in\{1,\ldots,s\}.

(5)

\mathfrak{g}=\Delta^{[j]}+\mathfrak{g}^{(j+1)},\quad\forall j\in\{1,\ldots,s\}.

(6)

[V_{i},V_{j}]\subseteq V_{\geq i+j},\;\forall i,j\in\{1,\ldots,s\}.

(7)

If
[V_{1},V_{j}]\subseteq V_{1+j},\;\forall j\in\{1,\ldots,s-1\}
, then
(V_{i})_{i=1}^{s}
is a stratification.

Proof.

(1). Let
i,j\in\{1,\ldots,q\}.
Then

\displaystyle[W_{i},W_{j}]\subseteq[\Delta^{[i]},\Delta^{[j]}]\subseteq\Delta^{[i+j]}=W_{\leq i+j}.

(2). We prove by induction on
k
that

[W_{1},W_{k}]=W_{k+1},\quad\forall k\in\{1,\ldots,q-1\}.

For
k=1
, by the definition of tangent grading:

\displaystyle\Delta^{[2]}

\displaystyle=W_{2}\oplus\Delta,\text{ and}

\displaystyle\Delta^{[2]}

\displaystyle\overset{\text{def}}{=}[\Delta,\Delta]+\Delta

\displaystyle=[W_{1},W_{1}]+\Delta.

Thus
\dim(W_{2})\leq\dim([W_{1},W_{1}])
. But
[W_{1},W_{1}]\subseteq W_{2}
, hence
W_{2}=[W_{1},W_{1}].

Now fix
k\in\{1,\ldots,q-2\}
and suppose that the claim holds up to
k
. Again, by the definition of tangent grading:

\displaystyle\Delta^{[k+2]}

\displaystyle=W_{k+2}\oplus\Delta^{[k+1]},\text{ and}

\displaystyle\Delta^{[k+2]}

\displaystyle\overset{\text{def}}{=}\Delta^{[k+1]}+\Delta^{k+2}

\displaystyle=\Delta^{[k+1]}+[W_{1},W_{k+1}],

where in the last equation we use the induction hypothesis to deduce that

\Delta^{k+2}=\underbrace{[\Delta,\ldots,\Delta]}_{k+1\text{ brackets}}=[W_{1},\underbrace{[W_{1},\ldots,W_{1}]}_{k\text{ brackets}}]\overset{\text{ind. hyp.}}{=}[W_{1},W_{k+1}].

Therefore
\dim(W_{k+2})\leq\dim([W_{1},W_{k+1}])
. But
[W_{1},W_{k+1}]\subseteq W_{k+2}
, and thus
W_{k+2}=[W_{1},W_{k+1}].

(3). This is clear from the definitions of asymptotic and tangent gradings.

(4). We induct on
j
. For
j=1
it is clear, since the projection on the abelianization
\mathfrak{g}/[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]
restricted to bracket generating subspaces is surjective. So, suppose the claim holds for some
j\in\{1,\ldots,s-1\}
. Then

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}^{(j+1)}=[\mathfrak{g}^{(j)},\mathfrak{g}]=[\Delta^{j}+\mathfrak{g}^{(j+1)},\Delta+\mathfrak{g}^{(2)}]\subseteq\Delta^{j+1}+\mathfrak{g}^{(j+2)},

and since
\Delta^{j+1},\mathfrak{g}^{(j+2)}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}^{(j+1)}
, we have
\mathfrak{g}^{(j+1)}=\Delta^{j+1}+\mathfrak{g}^{(j+2)}.

(5). We induct on
j
. We proved the case
j=1
in (
4
). So, suppose the claim holds for some
j\in\{1,\ldots,s-1\}
. Then

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}=\Delta^{[j]}+\mathfrak{g}^{(j+1)}\overset{\eqref{prop: CLG inside ALG}}{=}\Delta^{[j]}+\Delta^{j+1}+\mathfrak{g}^{(j+2)}=\Delta^{[j+1]}+\mathfrak{g}^{(j+2)}.

(6). Let
i,j\in\{1,\ldots,s\}.
Then

\displaystyle[V_{i},V_{j}]\subseteq[\mathfrak{g}^{(i)},\mathfrak{g}^{(j)}]\subseteq\mathfrak{g}^{(i+j)}=V_{\geq i+j}.

(7). Since
V_{s}=\mathfrak{g}^{(s)}
, then
[V_{1},V_{s}]=\{0\}
. We prove by induction on
k
that

[V_{1},V_{k}]=V_{k+1},\quad\forall k\in\{1,\ldots,s-1\}.

For
k=1
, by definition of asymptotic grading:

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}^{(2)}

\displaystyle=V_{2}\oplus\mathfrak{g}^{(3)},\text{ and}

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}^{(2)}

\displaystyle=[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]

\displaystyle=[V_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}^{(2)},V_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}^{(2)}]

\displaystyle\overset{\ref{prop: properties of linear gradings}\eqref{prop: bracket of CLG containment}}{\subseteq}[V_{1},V_{1}]+\mathfrak{g}^{(3)}.

Thus
\dim(V_{2})\leq\dim([V_{1},V_{1}])
. But
[V_{1},V_{1}]\subseteq V_{2}
, and therefore
V_{2}=[V_{1},V_{1}].

Now fix
k\in\{1,\ldots,s-2\}
and suppose that the claim holds up to
k
. Again, by the definition of asymptotic grading:

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}^{(k+2)}

\displaystyle=V_{k+2}\oplus\mathfrak{g}^{(k+3)},\text{ and}

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}^{(k+2)}

\displaystyle=[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}^{(k+1)}]

\displaystyle=[V_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}^{(2)},V_{k+1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}^{(k+2)}]

\displaystyle\overset{\ref{prop: properties of linear gradings}\eqref{prop: bracket of CLG containment}}{\subseteq}[V_{1},V_{k+1}]+\mathfrak{g}^{(k+3)}.

Thus
\dim(V_{k+2})\leq\dim([V_{1},V_{k+1}])
. But
[V_{1},V_{k+1}]\subseteq V_{k+2}
, and therefore
V_{k+2}=[V_{1},V_{k+1}].

∎

3.
Large and small scale geometry of sub-Finsler groups

We recall some basic notions regarding Gromov–Hausdorff convergence of pointed metric spaces.

Definition 3.1

(Hausdorff approximating sequence)
.

Let
(X_{j},x_{y})
and
(Y_{j},y_{j})
, for
j\in\mathbb{N}
, be two sequences of pointed metric spaces. A sequence of maps
\phi_{j}:(X_{j},x_{j})\rightarrow(Y_{j},y_{j})
is said to be
Hausdorff approximating
if for all
R>0
and all
\delta>0
there exists a sequence
\epsilon_{j}
such that

•

\epsilon_{j}\rightarrow 0
, as
j\rightarrow\infty
;

•

\phi_{j}|_{B(x_{j},R)}
is a
(1,\epsilon_{j})
-quasi-isometric embedding;

•

\phi_{j}(B(x_{j},R))
is an
\epsilon_{j}
-net for
B(y_{j},R-\delta).

We remark that if
\phi_{j}:(X_{j},x_{j})\rightarrow(Y_{j},y_{j})
is an Hausdorff approximating sequence of geodesic metric spaces, then
\phi_{j}(B(x_{j},R))
is an
\epsilon_{j}
-net for
B(y_{j},R).

Definition 3.2

(Gromov-Hausdorff limit)
.

We say that a pointed metric space
(Y,y)
is the
Gromov-Hausdorff limit
of a sequence of pointed metric spaces
(X_{j},x_{j})
, with
j\in\mathbb{N}
, if there exists an Hausdorff approximating sequence
\phi_{j}:(X_{j},x_{j})\rightarrow(Y,y)
.

We will need the following classical criterion, which can be found in
[
LD25
, Proposition 12.1.3]
:

Proposition 3.3

(Criterion for GH convergence)
.

Let
d_{j}
be a sequence of distances on a set
X
that converges to a distance
d_{\infty}
uniformly on
d_{\infty}
-bounded sets. Let
x_{0}\in X
. If

\text{diam}_{d_{\infty}}\left(\bigcup_{j\in\mathbb{N}}B_{d_{j}}(x_{0},R)\right)<\infty,\quad\forall R>0,

then
{\rm id}:(X,d_{j},x_{0})\rightarrow(X,d_{\infty},x_{0})
, is an Hausdorff approximating sequence.

3.1.
Asymptotic Carnot group and Pansu Asymptotic Theorem

Definition 3.4

(Asymptotic Carnot algebra and group)
.

The
asymptotic Carnot algebra
of a simply connected
s
-step nilpotent Lie group
G
with Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot])
is the Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g}_{\infty},[\cdot,\cdot]^{(0)})
, where

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}_{\infty}\coloneqq\bigoplus_{i=1}^{s}\mathfrak{g}^{(i)}/\mathfrak{g}^{(i+1)}

and
[\cdot,\cdot]^{(0)}
is the unique Lie bracket on
\mathfrak{g}_{\infty}
such that for
v\in\mathfrak{g}^{(i)}
and
w\in\mathfrak{g}^{(j)}
:

\displaystyle[v+\mathfrak{g}^{(i+1)},w+\mathfrak{g}^{(j+1)}]^{(0)}\coloneqq[v,w]+\mathfrak{g}^{(i+j+1)}.

The
asymptotic Carnot group

G_{\infty}
of
G
is the simply connected nilpotent Lie group with Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g}_{\infty},[\cdot,\cdot]^{(0)})
.

Notice that
\mathfrak{g}_{\infty}
is stratifiable and the spaces
(\mathfrak{g}^{(i)}/\mathfrak{g}^{(i+1)})_{i=1}^{s}
canonically provide a stratification on
\mathfrak{g}_{\infty}.

If the group
G
is equipped with a sub-Finsler metric
d
, then we can define a sub-Finsler metric
d_{\infty}
on
G_{\infty}
as follows. Suppose
d
is associated to a polarization
\Delta
and a norm
\|\cdot\|
on
\mathfrak{g}
, and denote by
\rho:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}/[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]
the projection with kernel
[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]
. Since
\Delta
is bracket generating, the restriction of
\rho
to
\Delta_{1}
is surjective. Define the
Pansu limit norm

\|\cdot\|_{\infty}
to be the norm on
\mathfrak{g}/[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]
with unit ball at the origin
B_{\|\cdot\|_{\infty}}(0,1)\coloneqq\rho(B_{\|\cdot\|}(0,1)\cap\Delta_{1}).
The
Pansu limit metric

d_{\infty}
is the sub-Finsler metric on
G_{\infty}
associated to
(\mathfrak{g}/[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}],\|\cdot\|_{\infty})
. We remark that
(G_{\infty},d_{\infty})
is a (metric) Carnot group, which, by the following theorem, is the asymptotic cone of
(G,d)
.

Theorem 3.5

(Pansu Asymptotic Theorem
[
Pan83
]
)
.

The asymptotic cone of a simply connected nilpotent sub-Finsler Lie group
(G,d)
is the Carnot group
(G_{\infty},d_{\infty}).

One can pointwise identify a simply connected nilpotent sub-Finsler Lie group with its asymptotic Carnot group and quantitatively study the speed of convergence of the metric
d
to the metric
d_{\infty}
, obtaining a quantitative version of Pansu’s theorem. The identification depends on the non-canonical choice of an asymptotic grading of
\mathfrak{g}.
Let
(V_{i})_{i=1}^{s}
be an asymptotic grading of
\mathfrak{g}
and let
j\in\{1,2,\ldots,s\}
. Recall that the asymptotic grading condition means that
\mathfrak{g}^{(j)}=V_{j}\oplus\mathfrak{g}^{(j+1)}
. Then
\pi_{j}\colon\mathfrak{g}^{(j)}\rightarrow V_{j}
induces an isomorphism between
\mathfrak{g}^{(j)}/\mathfrak{g}^{(j+1)}
and
V_{j}
and these isomorphisms induce a vector space isomorphism
\mathfrak{g}\simeq\mathfrak{g}_{\infty}.

Under this identification we can pull back the Lie bracket
[\cdot,\cdot]^{(0)}
from
\mathfrak{g}_{\infty}
to
\mathfrak{g}
.

Using exponential maps, which are global diffeomorphisms for simply connected nilpotent groups, we obtain the identifications

\displaystyle G\simeq\mathfrak{g}\simeq\mathfrak{g}_{\infty}\simeq G_{\infty}.

Observe that in general the identification map
G\rightarrow G_{\infty}
is just a diffeomorphism, not a group isomorphism.

3.2.
Osculating Carnot group and Mitchell Tangent Theorem

Let

(G,\Delta,\|\cdot\penalty\|)
be a sub-Finsler Lie group, not necessarily nilpotent. Denote by
[\cdot,\cdot]
the bracket on
\mathfrak{g}\coloneqq\text{Lie}(G)
and by
s\coloneqq\min\{n\in\mathbb{N}:\sum_{i=1}^{n}{\rm ad}_{\Delta}^{i-1}(\Delta)=\mathfrak{g}\}
the step of the distribution.

Definition 3.6

(Osculating Carnot algebra and group)
.

The
osculating Carnot algebra
of
(G,\Delta)
is the Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g}_{0},\llbracket\cdot,\cdot\rrbracket^{(0)})
, where, using the notation of Definition
2.2
,

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}_{0}\coloneqq\bigoplus_{i=1}^{s}\Delta^{[i]}/\Delta^{[i-1]},

and the bracket
\llbracket\cdot,\cdot\rrbracket^{(0)}
is the unique Lie bracket such that for
x\in\Delta^{[i]},\>y\in\Delta^{[j]}
:

\llbracket x+\Delta^{[i-1]},y+\Delta^{[j-1]}\rrbracket^{(0)}\coloneqq[x,y]+\Delta^{[i+j-1]}.

The
osculating Carnot group

G_{0}
of
G
is the simply connected nilpotent Lie group with Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g}_{0},\llbracket\cdot,\cdot\rrbracket^{(0)})
.

The Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}_{0}
is stratifiable and
(\Delta^{[i]}/\Delta^{[i-1]})_{i=1}^{s}
is a stratification, observe in addition that
\Delta^{[1]}/\Delta^{[0]}=\Delta
. Hence the pair
(\Delta,\|\cdot\|)
defines a sub-Finsler distance
d_{0}
on
G_{0}
, which we call the
tangent metric
, and the metric group
(G_{0},d_{0})
is a (metric) Carnot group. For every point
p\in G
, the group
(G_{0},d_{0})
is the tangent cone of
(G,d)
at
p
:

Theorem 3.7

(Mitchell Tangent Theorem)
.

Let
(G,d)
be a sub-Finsler Lie group and
p\in G
. Then the tangent cone of
G
at
p
is the osculating Carnot group
(G_{0},d_{0}).

In fact, we will provide a quantitative statement of Theorem
3.7
: for all
R>0
the Gromov–Hausdorff distance between the
R
-balls admits a bound of the form

(12)

d_{{\rm GH}}\left(\left(B_{\frac{1}{\epsilon}d}(1,R),\frac{1}{\epsilon}d\right),\left(B_{d_{0}}(1,R),d_{0}\right)\right)=O_{R}(\epsilon^{\alpha_{0}/\beta}).

Analogously to the identification of a nilpotent Lie algebra with its asymptotic Carnot algebra, one can locally identify each sub-Finsler Lie group with its osculating Carnot group, provided a choice of tangent grading, to quantitatively study the speed of convergence of the metric
d
to the metric
d_{0}
. Let
(W_{i})_{i=1}^{s}
be a tangent grading of
(\mathfrak{g},\Delta)
. Then
\Delta^{[i]}/\Delta^{[i-1]}
is isomorphic as vector space to
W_{i}
for every
i\in\{1,\ldots,s\}.
These isomorphisms induce a vector space isomorphism
\mathfrak{g}\simeq\mathfrak{g}_{0}
, and under this identification we can pull back the bracket
\llbracket\cdot,\cdot\rrbracket^{(0)}
to a bracket on
\mathfrak{g}
. Next, using the fact that the exponential map is a local diffeomorphism around the origin, we identify a neighborhood of the identity in
G
with a neighborhood of the identity in
G_{0}
:

\displaystyle G\overset{\text{locally}}{\simeq}\mathfrak{g}\simeq\mathfrak{g}_{0}\simeq G_{0}.

Again, we remark that in general this is not a local group isomorphism.

There is a version of the Ball-Box Theorem in terms of the tangent metric. Let
(G,\Delta,\|\cdot\|)
be a sub-Finsler Lie group and
(W_{i})_{i=1}^{s}
be a tangent grading. We identify a neighborhood of the identity in
G
with a neighborhood of the origin in
\mathfrak{g}
equipped with the Dynkin product. As a consequence of a general Ball-Box Theorem, as in
[
LD25
, Theorem 12.5.3]
, the functions
d_{0}(0,\cdot)
and
d(0,\cdot)
on
\mathfrak{g}
are locally biLipschitz comparable, i.e., there are a neighborhood
\Omega
of
0
in
\mathfrak{g}
and a constant
C>0
such that

(13)

\frac{1}{C}d_{0}(0,p)\leq d(0,p)\leq Cd_{0}(0,p),\quad\forall p\in\Omega.

3.3.
One-parameter families of sub-Finsler Lie group structures

Let
G
be a Lie group and let
(D_{i})_{i=1}^{k}
be a grading of
\mathfrak{g}\coloneqq\text{Lie}(G)
. Using the family of dilations associated to the grading, we can define two one-parameter families of Lie brackets on
\mathfrak{g}
. Let
\epsilon\in\mathbb{R}
,
\epsilon\neq 0
, then we define

\displaystyle[x,y]^{(\epsilon)}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\delta_{\epsilon}[\delta_{\epsilon}^{-1}x,\delta_{\epsilon}^{-1}y],

\displaystyle\llbracket x,y\rrbracket^{(\epsilon)}

\displaystyle\coloneqq[x,y]^{(1/\epsilon)}.

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
G
be a Lie group and let
(D_{i})_{i=1}^{s}
be a grading. Then

(1)

If
(D_{i})_{i=1}^{s}
is a tangent grading, then the brackets
\llbracket\cdot,\cdot\rrbracket^{(\epsilon)}
depend polynomially on
\epsilon
, and therefore extend to
\epsilon=0
. Moreover

\lim_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0}\llbracket x,y\rrbracket^{(\epsilon)}=\llbracket x,y\rrbracket^{(0)},\quad\forall x,y\in\mathfrak{g},

where
\llbracket\cdot,\cdot\rrbracket^{(0)}
is the Lie bracket defined in Section
3.2
.

(2)

If
G
is nilpotent and
(D_{i})_{i=1}^{s}
is an asymptotic grading, then the brackets
[\cdot,\cdot]^{(\epsilon)}
depend polynomially on
\epsilon
, and therefore extend to
\epsilon=0
. Moreover

\lim_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0}[x,y]^{(\epsilon)}=[x,y]^{(0)},\quad\forall x,y\in\mathfrak{g},

where
[\cdot,\cdot]^{(0)}
is the Lie bracket defined in Section
3.1
.

Proof.

We only prove (1), claim (2) follows from a similar argument. Let
x,y\in\mathfrak{g}
, then

\displaystyle\llbracket x,y\rrbracket^{(\epsilon)}

\displaystyle=\left\llbracket\sum_{i=1}^{s}(x)_{i},\sum_{i=1}^{s}(y)_{i}\right\rrbracket^{(\epsilon)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j}\delta_{\epsilon}^{-1}[\delta_{\epsilon}(x)_{i},\delta_{\epsilon}(y)_{j}]

\displaystyle\overset{\ref{prop: properties of linear gradings}\eqref{prop: bracket of ALG containment}}{=}\sum_{i,j}\sum_{k\leq i+j}\delta_{\epsilon}^{-1}([\epsilon^{i}(x)_{i},\epsilon^{j}(y)_{j}])_{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j}\sum_{k\leq i+j}\epsilon^{i+j-k}([(x)_{i},(y)_{i}])_{k}.

So
\llbracket\cdot,\cdot\rrbracket^{(\epsilon)}
is polynomial in
\epsilon
. Next, let
i,j\in\{1,\ldots,s\}
and
w\in D_{i},z\in D_{j}
. Then

\displaystyle\llbracket w,z\rrbracket^{(\epsilon)}\overset{\ref{prop: properties of linear gradings}\eqref{prop: bracket of ALG containment}}{=}\sum_{k\leq i+j}\delta_{\epsilon}^{-1}([\delta_{\epsilon}w,\delta_{\epsilon}z])_{k}=\sum_{k\leq i+j}\epsilon^{i+j-k}([w,z])_{k}\overset{\epsilon\rightarrow 0}{\longrightarrow}([w,z])_{i+j}=\llbracket w,z\rrbracket^{(0)}.

∎

Definition 3.9
.

For
\epsilon\in\mathbb{R}
, we denote with
*_{\epsilon}
(respectively
\cdot_{\epsilon}
) the Dynkin product associated with
[\cdot,\cdot]^{(\epsilon)}
(respectively
\llbracket\cdot,\cdot\rrbracket^{(\epsilon)}
), whenever the Dynkin series converges.

Recall that if the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is nilpotent, the BCH formula is polynomial and thus the Dynkin product is a (global) group product. The next lemma shows that the maps
\epsilon\mapsto*_{\epsilon}
and
\epsilon\mapsto\cdot_{\epsilon}
are analytic under suitable conditions.

Lemma 3.10

(
[
LD25
, Lemma 12.2.4]
)
.

Let
V
be a vector space,
\Lambda\subseteq\mathbb{R}
open, and
([\cdot,\cdot]_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
a family of Lie brackets on
V
. Assume that the map
\lambda\mapsto[\cdot,\cdot]_{\lambda}
is analytic. Denote by
\star_{\lambda}
the Dynkin product associated with
[\cdot,\cdot]_{\lambda}
. Then, the maps

\displaystyle(\lambda,x,y)\mapsto x\star_{\lambda}y\quad\text{and}\quad(\lambda,x,y)\mapsto\left.\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}s}x\star_{\lambda}(sy)\right|_{s=0}

are analytic on some open subset of
\Lambda\times V\times V
that contains
\Lambda\times\{0\}\times\{0\}.

We also introduce two one-parameter families of sub-Finsler metrics.

3.3.1.
Contracted metrics on simply connected nilpotent sub-Finsler Lie groups

Suppose that
(G,d)
is a simply connected nilpotent sub-Finsler Lie group associated to
(\Delta,\|\cdot\|)
, endowed with an asymptotic grading
(V_{i})_{i=1}^{s}
. We define the

as

\displaystyle\rho_{\epsilon}(x,y)\coloneqq\begin{cases}|\epsilon|d(\delta_{\epsilon}^{-1}x,\delta_{\epsilon}^{-1}y),\;\epsilon\neq 0,\\

The functions

are in fact metrics and, for

-left-invariant sub-Finsler metric on

\Delta^{(\epsilon)}\coloneqq\delta_{\epsilon}\Delta,\quad\|v\|^{(\epsilon)}\coloneqq\epsilon\|\delta_{\epsilon}^{-1}v\|,\;\forall v\in\Delta^{(\epsilon)}.

For completeness, we also consider the case

\Delta^{(0)}\coloneqq V_{1},\text{ and }\|\cdot\|^{(0)}\coloneqq\|\cdot\|_{\infty}.

Remark 3.11
.

The definition of contracted metrics implies that, for

\delta_{\epsilon}:(G,|\epsilon|d)\rightarrow(G,\rho_{\epsilon})
is an isometry.

Proposition 3.12
.

If
(G,d)
is a Carnot group and
(\rho_{\epsilon})_{\epsilon}
are the contracted metrics defined in (

\rho_{\epsilon}=d,\quad\forall\epsilon\in[0,1].

Proof.

The dilations
(\delta_{\epsilon})
associated to the stratification

are Lie algebra isomorphisms of

, for

[\cdot,\cdot]^{(\epsilon)}=[\cdot,\cdot],\quad\forall\epsilon\neq 0.

It follows that

,
\forall\epsilon\neq 0

is

, we also have

\Delta^{(\epsilon)}=V_{1},\quad\|\cdot\|^{(\epsilon)}=\|\cdot\|,\quad\forall\epsilon\in(0,1].

Thus,

\rho_{\epsilon}=d,\quad\forall\epsilon\neq 0.

Next, after identifying

with

[\cdot,\cdot]^{(0)}=[\cdot,\cdot],\quad\mathfrak{g}/[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]=V_{1},\quad\|\cdot\|_{\infty}=\|\cdot\|,

The next result shows that the distances from the origin of the contracted metrics

and

are equivalent, up to a universal multiplicative constant, and an additive constant that depends linearly on

Theorem 3.13

(Guivarc’h Theorem in terms of contracted metrics
[
LD25
, Corollary 12.4.4]
)
.

Let
G
be a simply connected nilpotent sub-Finsler Lie group equipped with an asymptotic grading
(V_{i})_{i=1}^{s}
. Then, there is a constant
C\geq 1
such that

(18)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{C}\rho_{0}(1,x)-C\epsilon\leq\rho_{\epsilon}(1,x)\leq C\rho_{0}(1,x)+C\epsilon,\quad\forall x\in G,\;\forall\epsilon\geq 0.

We can lift
\rho_{0}
-geodesics to
\rho_{\epsilon}
-admissible curves (of the same length) using the fact that the map
\pi_{1}
is a submetry, as we now explain. A
submetry

f:X\rightarrow Y
between metric spaces is a map such that

f(\overline{B}_{d_{X}}(x,r))=\overline{B}{{}_{d_{Y}}}(f(x),r),\quad\forall x\in X,\;\forall r\geq 0.

Proposition 3.14
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a nilpotent Lie algebra. Then the projection map
\pi_{1}:(\mathfrak{g},*_{\epsilon},\|\cdot\|^{(\epsilon)})\rightarrow(V_{1},+,\|\cdot\|_{\infty})
is a group homomorphism and a submetry.

Proof.

The projection map
\pi_{1}
is a group homomorphism since

(x*_{\epsilon}y)_{1}=(x)_{1}+(y)_{1},\quad\forall x,y\in\mathfrak{g}.

Regarding the claim that
\pi_{1}
is a submetry, we use the definitions of the norms
\|\cdot\|_{\infty}
and
\|\cdot\|^{(\epsilon)}
to calculate

\displaystyle\pi_{1}\left(B_{\|\cdot\|^{(\epsilon)}}(0,1)\right)

\displaystyle=\pi_{1}\left(\delta_{\epsilon}B_{\|\cdot\|^{(1)}}(0,1/\epsilon)\right)

\displaystyle=\epsilon\pi_{1}\left(B_{\|\cdot\|^{(1)}}(0,1/\epsilon)\right)

\displaystyle=\epsilon B_{\|\cdot\|_{\infty}}(0,1/\epsilon)

\displaystyle=B_{\|\cdot\|_{\infty}}(0,1).

∎

It is a standard fact in metric geometry that submetries lift
1
-Lipschitz curves from their target space to their domain:

Proposition 3.15
.

Let
f:X\rightarrow Y
be a submetry between metric spaces. Assume that

is boundedly compact. Then for every
1
-Lipschitz curve
\gamma:[0,T]\rightarrow Y
and every

, there exists a
1
-Lipschitz curve
\tilde{\gamma}:[0,T]\rightarrow X
such that
\tilde{\gamma}(0)=x
and

Proposition 3.16
.

Let
G
be a simply connected nilpotent sub-Finsler Lie group equipped with an asymptotic grading
(V_{1},\ldots,V_{s})
such that the sub-Finsler structure associated to the contracted metrics
\rho_{\epsilon}
are
(\Delta^{(\epsilon)},\|\cdot\|^{(\epsilon)})
. Then for every Euclidean metric
\|\cdot\|_{\rm E}
there exists a constant
C>0
depending only on
G,\Delta,\|\cdot\|,\|\cdot\|_{\rm E}
, and
(V_{1},\ldots,V_{s})
such that we have

(19)

\|(u)_{k}\|_{\rm E}\leq C\epsilon^{k-1}\|(u)_{k}\|^{(\epsilon)},\quad\forall k\in\{1,\ldots,s\},\;\forall\epsilon\in[0,1],\;\forall u\in\Delta^{(\epsilon)},

using the convention that
0^{0}=1
, and

(20)

\|u\|_{\rm E}\leq C\|u\|^{(\epsilon)},\quad\forall\epsilon\in[0,1],\;\forall u\in\Delta^{(\epsilon)}.

Proof.

Regarding (
19
), let
\epsilon\in(0,1]
and
u\in\Delta^{(\epsilon)}
. Since
\|\cdot\|^{(1)}
and
\|\cdot\|_{\rm E}
are norms on
\mathfrak{g}
, there exists a constant
C\leq 1
such that
\|\cdot\|^{(1)}\geq C\|\cdot\|_{\rm E}
. Thus

\displaystyle\|(u)_{k}\|^{(\epsilon)}

\displaystyle\overset{\text{def}}{=}\epsilon\|\delta_{\epsilon}^{-1}(u)_{k}\|^{(1)}

\displaystyle\geq C\epsilon\|\delta_{\epsilon}^{-1}(u)_{k}\|_{\rm E}

\displaystyle=C\epsilon^{1-k}\|(u)_{k}\|_{\rm E}.

For
\epsilon=0

\Delta^{(0)}\overset{\eqref{def: contracted structure for eps = 0}}{=}V_{1}
and
\left.\|\cdot\|_{\rm E}\right|_{V_{1}}
is a norm on

\|u\|_{\rm E}\leq C^{\prime}\|u\|^{(0)},\quad\forall u\in V_{1}.

Regarding (

\epsilon\neq 0
and

. Then

\displaystyle\|u\|^{(\epsilon)}

\displaystyle\overset{\text{def}}{=}\epsilon\|\delta_{\epsilon}^{-1}u\|^{(1)}

\displaystyle\geq C\epsilon\|\delta_{\epsilon}^{-1}u\|_{\rm E}

\displaystyle=C\|(u)_{1}+\epsilon^{-1}(u)_{2}+\cdots+\epsilon^{-s+1}(u)_{s}\|_{\rm E}

\displaystyle\geq C\|(u)_{1}+(u)_{2}+\cdots+(u)_{s}\|_{\rm E}

\displaystyle=C\|u\|_{\rm E}.

If

\|u\|_{\rm E}\leq C^{\prime}\|u\|^{(0)},\;\forall u\in V_{1}=\Delta^{(0)}.

∎

3.3.2.

Suppose that
(G,d)

(W_{i})_{i=1}^{s}

Remark 3.17
.

The definition of dilated metrics implies that, for

3.4.
Carnot quotient ideals

In the proofs of Theorem
5.1
and Theorem
6.1
, we need to bound the norm of the difference of two curves
\gamma_{0}
and
\gamma_{\epsilon}
. To this end, we introduce a class of ideals
\mathfrak{i}
of
\mathfrak{g}
, which we call Carnot quotient ideals, such that the projection of
\gamma_{0}
and
\gamma_{\epsilon}
on the quotient
\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{i}
coincide. Thus their difference
v\coloneqq\gamma_{0}(1)^{-1}*_{\epsilon}\gamma_{\epsilon}(1)
lies in
\mathfrak{i}
. We then use this fact to obtain a bound on the norm of
v
, better than the bound one would obtain by directly applying the Ball-Box Theorem (see Lemma
5.3
and Lemma
6.3
).

Definition 3.18
.

Let
(G,\Delta)
be a polarized group and let
(D_{i})_{i=1}^{s}
be a grading.
We say that a subspace
\mathfrak{i}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}
is a
Carnot quotient ideal of

(G,\Delta)

with respect to the grading
(D_{i})_{i=1}^{s}
if

(1)

\mathfrak{i}
is an ideal of
\mathfrak{g}
.

(2)

\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{i}
is stratified by
(\pi(D_{j}))_{1\leq j\leq s}
, where
\pi:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{i}
is the quotient map.

(3)

\Delta\subseteq D_{1}+\mathfrak{i}.

We claim that condition (
3
) is equivalent to

(24)

\pi(u)=\pi\circ\pi_{1}(u),\quad\forall u\in\Delta.

Indeed, let
u=\sum_{i=1}^{s}(u)_{i}\in\Delta
. Then

\displaystyle\pi(u)=\pi(\pi_{1}(u))

\displaystyle\iff\pi\left(\sum_{i=2}^{s}(u)_{i}\right)=0\iff\sum_{i=2}^{s}(u)_{i}\in\mathfrak{i.}

Moreover, we claim that condition (
3
) is also equivalent to

(25)

\Delta+\mathfrak{i}=D_{1}+\mathfrak{i}.

Indeed, if
\Delta+\mathfrak{i}=D_{1}+\mathfrak{i}
, then clearly
\Delta\subseteq D_{1}+\mathfrak{i}
. Now assume that
\Delta\subseteq D_{1}+\mathfrak{i}
, and hence
\Delta+\mathfrak{i}\subseteq D_{1}+\mathfrak{i}
. The quotient map
\pi:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{i}
is a Lie algebra homomorphism, and thus, since
\Delta
is bracket generating,
\pi(\Delta)=\Delta+\mathfrak{i}
is also bracket generating. Therefore,
\Delta+\mathfrak{i}
is a bracket generating subspace of the first stratum
D_{1}+\mathfrak{i}
of the stratified Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{i}
, so it must coincide with
D_{1}+\mathfrak{i}.

Note in addition that if
(D_{i})_{i=1}^{s}
is a tangent grading, condition (
3
) is satisfied for any ideal, since
\Delta=D_{1}
and
\pi_{1}|_{D_{1}}={\rm id}
.

Example 3.19
.

(1)

Let
(G,\Delta,\|\cdot\|)
be a sub-Finsler Lie group, and let
(D_{i})_{i=1}^{s}
be either an asymptotic or a tangent grading. Denote by
d
the sub-Finsler metric on
G
. Then

(G,d)\text{ is a Carnot group}\iff\{0\}\text{ is a Carnot quotient ideal.}

Indeed, if
(G,d)
is a Carnot group, then clearly
\{0\}
is a Carnot quotient ideal. Conversely, if
\{0\}
is a Carnot quotient ideal, the grading
(D_{i})_{i=1}^{s}
is a stratification of
\text{Lie}(G)
, and by (
25
) we have
\Delta=D_{1}
.

(2)

For every nilpotent Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
with asymptotic grading
(V_{1})_{i=1}^{s}
, the ideal
V_{2}\oplus\cdots\oplus V_{s}
is a Carnot quotient ideal, for every polarization
\Delta
.

(3)

Consider the non-stratifiable Lie algebra

from
[

]
. It is the Lie algebra
\mathbb{R}^{5}=\text{span}\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{5}\}
with non-trivial brackets

.

Let

be a grading of the Lie algebra of a polarized Lie group

such that

.

.

(1)

For every Riemannian group

We record some properties of Carnot quotient ideals.

Lemma 3.22

(Properties of Carnot quotient ideals associated to asymptotic gradings)
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\Delta)
be a nilpotent polarized Lie algebra and
(V_{i})_{i}
an asymptotic grading. Let
\mathfrak{i}\triangleleft\mathfrak{g}
be a Carnot quotient ideal with respect to
(V_{i})_{i}
. Then

(1)

\mathfrak{i}
is dilation invariant.

(2)

\mathfrak{i}\triangleleft(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]^{(\epsilon)})
is an ideal, for every
\epsilon\in[0,1].

(3)

\pi|_{\Delta^{(\epsilon)}}=\pi\circ\pi_{1}|_{\Delta^{(\epsilon)}}
, for every
\epsilon\in(0,1]
.

(4)

(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]^{(\epsilon)})/\mathfrak{i}
is stratified by
(\pi(V_{j}))_{1\leq j\leq s}
, for every
\epsilon\in[0,1]
.

(5)

Denote by
[\cdot,\cdot]^{(\epsilon,\mathfrak{i})}
the quotient bracket on
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]^{(\epsilon)})/\mathfrak{i}
. Then

[\cdot,\cdot]^{(\epsilon,\mathfrak{i})}=[\cdot,\cdot]^{(\tau,\mathfrak{i})},\quad\forall\epsilon,\tau\in[0,1].

(6)

For
\epsilon\in[0,1],
equip
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]^{(\epsilon)})
and
(\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{i},[\cdot,\cdot]^{(\epsilon,\mathfrak{i})})
with the respective Dynkin products
*_{\epsilon}
and
*_{\epsilon,\mathfrak{i}}
. Then
\pi:(\mathfrak{g},*_{\epsilon})\rightarrow(\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{i},*_{\epsilon,\mathfrak{i}})
is a Lie group homomorphism.

Proof.

(1). For
\tau>0
, denote by
\overline{\delta_{\tau}}:\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{i}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{i}
the dilation with respect to
(\pi(V_{j}))_{j=1}^{s}
on the quotient. We claim that

\displaystyle\overline{\delta_{\tau}}\circ\pi=\pi\circ\delta_{\tau},\quad\forall\tau\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}.

Indeed, for
v\in V_{j}
, we have

\displaystyle\pi(\delta_{\tau}v)=\pi(\tau^{j}v)=\tau^{j}\pi(v)=\overline{\delta_{\tau}}(\pi(v)),

since
\overline{\delta_{\tau}}|_{\pi(V_{j})}
is a multiplication by
\tau^{j}
. The claim follows by linearity. Consequently

\{0\}=\overline{\delta_{\tau}}(\pi(\mathfrak{i))=\pi(\delta_{\tau}(\mathfrak{i))}}.

Thus
\delta_{\tau}(\mathfrak{i})\subseteq\ker\pi=\mathfrak{i}
.

(2). For
\epsilon>0
, it follows from the dilation invariance of
\mathfrak{i}
that

\displaystyle[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{i}]^{(\epsilon)}

\displaystyle\overset{\text{def}}{=}\delta_{\epsilon}[\delta_{\epsilon^{-1}}\mathfrak{g},\delta_{\epsilon^{-1}}\mathfrak{i}]\subseteq\delta_{\epsilon}[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{i}]\subseteq\delta_{\epsilon}\mathfrak{i}\subseteq\mathfrak{i}.

By Lemma
3.8
we also obtain the case
\epsilon=0.

(3). Clearly, for every
\epsilon>0
we have
\pi_{1}\circ\delta_{\epsilon}=\delta_{\epsilon}\circ\pi_{1}
. Therefore, for
u\in\Delta
, we have

\displaystyle\pi\circ\pi_{1}\circ\delta_{\epsilon}(u)

\displaystyle=\pi\circ\delta_{\epsilon}\circ\pi_{1}(u)

\displaystyle=\overline{\delta_{\epsilon}}\circ\pi\circ\pi_{1}(u)

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{def: CQI: first equivalence of condition 3}}{=}\overline{\delta_{\epsilon}}\circ\pi(u)

\displaystyle=\pi\circ\delta_{\epsilon}(u),

that is,
\pi|_{\Delta^{(\epsilon)}}=\pi\circ\pi_{1}|_{\Delta^{(\epsilon)}}
.

(4). For
\epsilon=0
, it is clear. For
\epsilon>0
, using that
\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{i}
is stratified by
(\pi(V_{j}))_{j=1}^{s}
and thus
\pi([V_{1},V_{k}])=\pi(V_{k+1})
we get

\displaystyle[\pi(V_{1}),\pi(V_{k})]^{(\epsilon,\mathfrak{i})}

\displaystyle=\pi([V_{1},V_{k}]^{(\epsilon)})

\displaystyle=\pi(\delta_{\epsilon}[\delta_{\epsilon^{-1}}V_{1},\delta_{\epsilon^{-1}}V_{k}])

\displaystyle=\overline{\delta_{\epsilon}}(\pi([V_{1},V_{k}]))

\displaystyle=\overline{\delta_{\epsilon}}(\pi(V_{k+1}))

\displaystyle=\pi(V_{k+1}).

(5). Let
\epsilon\in(0,1]
and
v\in V_{i},w\in V_{j}
. Then

\displaystyle[\pi(v),\pi(w)]^{(\epsilon,\mathfrak{i})}

\displaystyle=\pi([v,w]^{(\epsilon)})

\displaystyle=([v,w]^{(\epsilon)})_{i+j}+\mathfrak{i}

\displaystyle=([v,w])_{i+j}+\mathfrak{i}

\displaystyle=[v,w]^{(0)}+\mathfrak{i}

\displaystyle=[\pi(v),\pi(w)]^{(0,\mathfrak{i})},

where in the second equality we have used that
[\pi(V_{1}),\pi(V_{k})]^{(\epsilon,\mathfrak{i})}=\pi(V_{i+j}).

(6). Both exponential maps
\exp_{\mathfrak{g}}:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
and
\exp_{\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{i}}:\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{i}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{i}
are global diffeomorphisms and are such that
\exp_{\mathfrak{g}}=\text{id}_{\mathfrak{g}}
and
\exp_{\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{i}}=\text{id}_{\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{i}}
. Since
\mathfrak{g}
is simply connected, there exists a unique Lie group homomorphism
\rho:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{i}
with
\rho_{*}=\pi
. We have

\rho=\exp_{\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{i}}\circ\pi\circ\exp_{\mathfrak{g}}^{-1}=\pi.

∎

Similar properties hold if we replace the asymptotic grading with a tangent grading and the brackets
[\cdot,\cdot]^{(\epsilon)}
by brackets
\llbracket\cdot,\cdot\rrbracket^{(\epsilon)}
:

Lemma 3.23

(Properties of Carnot quotient ideals associated to tangent gradings)
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\Delta)
be a polarized Lie algebra and
(W_{i})_{i}
a tangent grading with respect to
\Delta
. Let
\mathfrak{i}\triangleleft\mathfrak{g}
be a Carnot quotient ideal with respect to
(W_{i})_{i}
. Then

(1)

\mathfrak{i}
is dilation invariant.

(2)

\mathfrak{i}\triangleleft(\mathfrak{g},\llbracket\cdot,\cdot\rrbracket^{(\epsilon)})
is an ideal, for every
\epsilon\in[0,1].

(3)

(\mathfrak{g},\llbracket\cdot,\cdot\rrbracket^{(\epsilon)})/\mathfrak{i}
is stratified by
(\pi(W_{j}))_{1\leq j\leq s}
, for every
\epsilon\in[0,1]
.

(4)

Denote by
\llbracket\cdot,\cdot\rrbracket^{(\epsilon,\mathfrak{i})}
the quotient bracket on
(\mathfrak{g},\llbracket\cdot,\cdot\rrbracket^{(\epsilon)})/\mathfrak{i}
. Then

\llbracket\cdot,\cdot\rrbracket^{(\epsilon,\mathfrak{i})}=\llbracket\cdot,\cdot\rrbracket^{(\tau,\mathfrak{i})},\quad\forall\epsilon,\tau\in[0,1].

(5)

For
\epsilon\in[0,1],
equip
(\mathfrak{g},\llbracket\cdot,\cdot\rrbracket^{(\epsilon)})
and
(\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{i},\llbracket\cdot,\cdot\rrbracket^{(\epsilon,\mathfrak{i})})
with the Dynkin products
\cdot_{\epsilon}
and
\cdot_{\epsilon,\mathfrak{i}}
. Then
\pi:(\mathfrak{g},\cdot_{\epsilon})\rightarrow(\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{i},\cdot_{\epsilon,\mathfrak{i}})
is a local Lie group homomorphism.

Proof.

Claims (1), (2), (3), and (4) follow from the same arguments of the proofs of Lemma
3.22
(1), (2), (4), and (5) respectively.

Regarding (5), both exponential maps
\exp_{\mathfrak{g}}:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
and

are local diffeomorphisms and are such that

and

near the origin. Hence the map
\exp_{\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{i}}\circ\pi\circ\exp_{\mathfrak{g}}^{-1}=\pi
is a local Lie group homomorphism.
∎

4.
Grönwall Lemma type results

Grönwall Lemma-type results are used to bound from above the distance of the endpoints of two curves starting from the same point, given a uniform bound on the difference of their derivatives. The main technical difficulty in this regard is the fact that we need to compare derivatives at distinct points. The prototypical Grönwall Lemma in our context is the following version, whose proof can be found in
[
ALDNG23
, Lemma A.1]
or in

Lemma 4.1

(Grönwall)
.

Let
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
X,Y:\Omega\times[0,T]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Let
\|\cdot\|
be a norm on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Suppose that there are
E,K>0
such that for all
p,q\in\Omega
and all
t\in[0,T]

\|X(p,t)-Y(q,t)\|\leq E+K\|p-q\|.

If
\eta,\gamma:[0,T]\rightarrow\Omega
are integral curves of
X
and
Y
, respectively, and satisfy
\eta(0)=\gamma(0)
, then

\|\gamma(t)-\eta(t)\|\leq E\frac{{\rm e}^{Kt}-1}{K},\quad\forall t\in[0,T].

In this section, we prove two sharper versions of Lemma
4.1
under some more restrictive hypotheses that are sufficient for the application of the results to the proofs of the sharper Pansu’s and Mitchell’s theorems.

4.1.
Grönwall Lemma for Pansu Asymptotic Theorem

We prove a version of Grönwall Lemma adapted to the context of Pansu’s asymptotic theorem (see Lemma
4.9
). First, we need to introduce some constants.

Definition 4.2
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\Delta)
be a polarized nilpotent Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
and
(V_{i})_{i=1}^{s}
an asymptotic grading. We define

•

\alpha_{(1,\infty)}\coloneqq\max\{j\in\mathbb{N}:[V_{p_{1}},\ldots,V_{p_{k}}]\subseteq V_{|p|}\oplus V_{\geq|p|+j},\;\forall k\in\mathbb{N},\;\forall p\in\mathbb{N}^{k}\}.

•

\alpha_{(2,\infty)}\coloneqq\max\{j\in\mathbb{N}\,:\,v-\pi_{1}(v)\in\mathfrak{g}^{(j+1)},\,\forall v\in\Delta\}.

•

\alpha_{\infty}\coloneqq\min\{\alpha_{(1,\infty)},\alpha_{(2,\infty)}\}.

The constant
\alpha_{(1,\infty)}
is a slight variation of the constant
e_{D}
introduced by Cornullier in
[
Cor17
]
, and can be thought of as quantifying how stratifiable the asymptotic grading is. Observe that
(V_{1},\ldots,V_{s})
is a stratification if and only if
\alpha_{(1,\infty)}=+\infty.
The constant
\alpha_{(2,\infty)}
can be viewed as quantifying how close
\Delta
is from being the first strata of a stratification. Note that
\alpha_{(2,\infty)}=+\infty
if and only if
\Delta=V_{1}.
It follows that
(G,\Delta)
is a (metric) Carnot group if and only if
\alpha_{\infty}=+\infty.

Example 4.3
.

(1)

Let
G
be a simply connected nilpotent stratifiable Lie group equipped with a stratification
(V_{i})_{i}
. Suppose
G
is equipped with the polarization
\Delta=V_{1}\oplus V_{2}
. Then

\alpha_{(1,\infty)}=+\infty,\;\alpha_{(2,\infty)}=\alpha_{\infty}=1.

(2)

Let
G
be a simply connected nilpotent non-stratifiable Lie group equipped with an asymptotic grading
(V_{i})_{i}
. Suppose
G
is equipped with the polarization
\Delta=V_{1}.
Then

\alpha_{\infty}=\alpha_{(1,\infty)}<\alpha_{(2,\infty)}=+\infty.

Proposition 4.4
.

If a nilpotent sub-Finsler Lie group
(G,\Delta,\|\cdot\|)
with an asymptotic grading
(V_{i})_{i=1}^{s}
is not a Carnot group, then

\alpha_{\infty}<\beta,

where
\alpha_{\infty}
and
\beta
are the constants from Definition
4.2
and
3.20
.

Proof.

Since
(G,\Delta,\|\cdot\|)
is not Carnot,
\beta\geq 1
. Let
\mathfrak{i}\subseteq V_{\leq\beta}
be a Carnot quotient ideal. We distinguish two cases.

Case 1
:
\Delta\neq V_{1}
. On the one hand, since
\mathfrak{i}
is a Carnot quotient ideal, we have

\Delta\overset{\ref{def: Carnot quotient ideal}\eqref{def: CQI projection}}{\subseteq}V_{1}+\mathfrak{i}\subseteq V_{\leq\beta}.

On the other hand, by definition of
\alpha_{(2,\infty)}
we have

\Delta\subseteq V_{1}\oplus V_{\geq\alpha_{(2,\infty)}+1}.

Therefore, since
\Delta\neq V_{1}
, we have
\alpha_{(2,\infty)}+1\leq\beta
, and thus
\alpha_{\infty}\leq\alpha_{(2,\infty)}<\beta.

Case 2
:
\Delta=V_{1}
. Let
k\in\{2,\ldots,s-1\}
and
p\in\mathbb{N}^{k}
. By Definition
3.18
:

\displaystyle[V_{p_{1}},\ldots,V_{p_{k}}]+\mathfrak{i}

\displaystyle\overset{\ref{def: Carnot quotient ideal}\eqref{def: CQI 1 ideal}}{=}[V_{p_{1}}+\mathfrak{i},\ldots,V_{p_{k}}+\mathfrak{i}]

\displaystyle\overset{\ref{def: Carnot quotient ideal}\eqref{def: CQI 2 stratification}}{\subseteq}V_{|p|}+\mathfrak{i}.

Therefore,

[V_{p_{1}},\ldots,V_{p_{k}}]\subseteq V_{|p|}+\mathfrak{i}\subseteq V_{|p|}+V_{\leq\beta}.

Using Proposition
2.4
, we obtain

[V_{p_{1}},\ldots,V_{p_{k}}]\overset{\ref{prop: properties of linear gradings}\eqref{prop: bracket of CLG containment}}{\subseteq}V_{|p|}\oplus V_{|p|+1}\oplus\cdots\oplus V_{\beta}.

In particular, since
|p|\geq 2
, we have

[V_{p_{1}},\ldots,V_{p_{k}}]\subseteq V_{|p|}\oplus V_{|p|+1}\oplus\cdots\oplus V_{|p|+\beta-2}.

Moreover, by definition of
\alpha_{(1,\infty)}
:

[V_{p_{1}},\ldots,V_{p_{k}}]\subseteq V_{|p|}\oplus V_{|p|+\alpha_{(1,\infty)}}\oplus\cdots\oplus V_{|p|+\beta-2}.

So
\alpha_{(1,\infty)}\leq\beta-2
, otherwise
\text{Lie}(G)
would be stratifiable (and thus
(G,d)
would be a Carnot group) by Proposition
2.4
(
7
).
∎

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a polarized nilpotent Lie algebra equipped with an asymptotic grading which induces projections
x\mapsto(x)_{j}
. Let
n\in\mathbb{N},p\in\mathbb{N}^{n},
and
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\in\mathfrak{g}.
Then

(26)

\displaystyle[(x_{1})_{p_{1}},\ldots,(x_{n})_{p_{n}}]-[(x_{1})_{p_{1}},\ldots,(x_{n})_{p_{n}}]^{(0)}=\sum_{j={|p|+\alpha_{(1,\infty)}}}^{s}([(x_{1})_{p_{1}},\ldots,(x_{n})_{p_{n}}])_{j}.

Proof.

We show by induction on
n\in\mathbb{N}
that

(27)

[(x_{1})_{p_{1}},\ldots,(x_{n})_{p_{n}}]^{(0)}=([(x_{1})_{p_{1}},\ldots,(x_{n})_{p_{n}}])_{|p|},\quad\forall p\in\mathbb{N}^{n},\;\forall x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\in\mathfrak{g}.

By definition of the bracket
[\cdot,\cdot]^{(0)}
, we have for
x,y\in\mathfrak{g}
and
j,k\in\mathbb{N}
:

(28)

[(x)_{j},(y)_{k}]^{(0)}=([(x)_{j},(y)_{k}])_{j+k}.

Now suppose that the claim is true for brackets of length up to
n-1
. Using the properties of asymptotic gradings, we obtain

\displaystyle([(x_{1})_{p_{1}},\ldots,(x_{n})_{p_{n}}])_{|p|}

\displaystyle\overset{\ref{prop: properties of linear gradings}\eqref{prop: bracket of CLG containment}}{=}\sum_{j=|p|-p_{1}}^{s}([(x_{1})_{p_{1}},([(x_{2})_{p_{2}},\ldots,(x_{n})_{p_{n}}])_{j}])_{|p|}

\displaystyle\overset{\ref{prop: properties of linear gradings}\eqref{prop: bracket of CLG containment}}{=}([(x_{1})_{p_{1}},([(x_{2})_{p_{2}},\ldots,(x_{n})_{p_{n}}])_{|p|-p_{1}}])_{|p|}

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq: lem: difference of brackets pansu 1}}{=}([(x_{1})_{p_{1}},([(x_{2})_{p_{2}},\ldots,(x_{n})_{p_{n}}]^{(0)})_{|p|-p_{1}}])_{|p|}

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq: lem: difference of brackets pansu 2}}{=}[(x_{1})_{p_{1}},\ldots,(x_{n})_{p_{n}}]^{(0)}.

Now, by definition of
\alpha_{(1,\infty)}
, we have

\displaystyle[(x_{1})_{p_{1}},\ldots,(x_{n})_{p_{n}}]-[(x_{1})_{p_{1}},\ldots,(x_{n})_{p_{n}}]^{(0)}

\displaystyle\overset{\ref{prop: properties of linear gradings}\eqref{prop: bracket of CLG containment},\eqref{eq: lem: difference of brackets pansu 1}}{=}\sum_{j={|p|+1}}^{s}([(x_{1})_{p_{1}},\ldots,(x_{n})_{p_{n}}])_{j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j={|p|+\alpha_{(1,\infty)}}}^{s}([(x_{1})_{p_{1}},\ldots,(x_{n})_{p_{n}}])_{j}.

∎

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a polarized nilpotent Lie algebra equipped with an asymptotic grading which induces projections
x\mapsto(x)_{j}
. Let
x_{1},x_{2}\in\mathfrak{g}
and
\epsilon\in[0,1].
Then

(29)

\displaystyle x_{1}*_{\epsilon}x_{2}-x_{1}*_{0}x_{2}=\sum_{k=2}^{s}\sum_{q\in\{1,2\}^{k}}\sum_{p\in\{1,\ldots,s\}^{k}}\sum_{j=|p|+\alpha_{(1,\infty)}}^{s}b_{k,q}\epsilon^{j-|p|}([(x_{q_{1}})_{p_{1}},\ldots,(x_{q_{k}})_{p_{k}}])_{j},

where
b_{k,q}
are the constants in the BCH formula (
9
).

Proof.

For
y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}\in\mathfrak{g}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
, by definition of the bracket
[\cdot,\cdot]^{(\epsilon)}
we have

[y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}]^{(\epsilon)}=\delta_{\epsilon}[\delta_{\epsilon}^{-1}y_{1},\ldots,\delta_{\epsilon}^{-1}y_{n}],

and from the fact that dilations are Lie algebra automorphisms of
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]^{(0)})
, we have

\displaystyle[y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}]^{(0)}

\displaystyle=\delta_{\epsilon}[\delta_{\epsilon}^{-1}y_{1},\ldots,\delta_{\epsilon}^{-1}y_{n}]^{(0)}.

Now we use the BCH formula and Lemma
4.5
to calculate

\displaystyle x_{1}*_{\epsilon}x_{2}

\displaystyle-x_{1}*_{0}x_{2}\overset{\eqref{def: BCH}}{=}\sum_{k=2}^{s}\sum_{q\in\{1,2\}^{k}}b_{k,q}([x_{q_{1}},\ldots,x_{q_{k}}]^{(\epsilon)}-[x_{q_{1}},\ldots,x_{q_{k}}]^{(0)})

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=2}^{s}\sum_{q\in\{1,2\}^{k}}b_{k,q}\delta_{\epsilon}([\delta_{\epsilon^{-1}}x_{q_{1}},\ldots,\delta_{\epsilon^{-1}}x_{q_{k}}]-[\delta_{\epsilon^{-1}}x_{q_{1}},\ldots,\delta_{\epsilon^{-1}}x_{q_{k}}]^{(0)})

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{difference of brackets}}{=}\sum_{k=2}^{s}\sum_{q\in\{1,2\}^{k}}\sum_{p\in\{1,\ldots,s\}^{k}}\sum_{j=|p|+\alpha_{(1,\infty)}}^{s}b_{k,q}\delta_{\epsilon}([(\delta_{\epsilon^{-1}}x_{q_{1}})_{p_{1}},\ldots,(\delta_{\epsilon^{-1}}x_{q_{k}})_{p_{k}}])_{j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=2}^{s}\sum_{q\in\{1,2\}^{k}}\sum_{p\in\{1,\ldots,s\}^{k}}\sum_{j=|p|+\alpha_{(1,\infty)}}^{s}b_{k,q}\epsilon^{j-|p|}([(x_{q_{1}})_{p_{1}},\ldots,(x_{q_{k}})_{p_{k}}])_{j}.

∎

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\Delta)
be a polarized nilpotent Lie algebra equipped with a norm
\|\cdot\|
and an asymptotic grading which induces projections
x\mapsto(x)_{j}=\pi_{j}(x)
and dilations
(\delta_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}}
. For every norm
\|\cdot\|_{\rm E}
on
\mathfrak{g}
there exists a constant
C
such that

(30)

\displaystyle\|u-\pi_{1}(u)\|_{\rm E}\leq C\epsilon^{\alpha_{(2,\infty)}}\|u\|^{(\epsilon)},\quad\forall\epsilon\in[0,1],\forall u\in\Delta^{(\epsilon)}

Proof.

By (
19
), there is a
C
such that

\|(u)_{k}\|_{\rm E}\overset{\eqref{eq: upper bound of euclidean norm of layer wrt to norm eps}}{\leq}C\epsilon^{k-1}\|u\|^{(\epsilon)},\quad\forall\epsilon\in(0,1],\;\forall u\in\Delta^{(\epsilon)},\;\forall k\in\{2,\dots,s\}.

Hence by definition of
\alpha_{(2,\infty)}
and triangle inequality

\displaystyle\|u-\pi_{1}(u)\|_{\rm E}

\displaystyle\leq\|(u)_{\alpha_{(2,\infty)}+1}\|_{\rm E}+\|(u)_{\alpha_{(2,\infty)}+2}\|_{\rm E}+\dots+\|(u)_{s}\|_{\rm E}

\displaystyle\leq C\epsilon^{\alpha_{(2,\infty)}}\|u\|^{(\epsilon)}+\dots+C\epsilon^{s-1}\|u\|^{(\epsilon)}

\displaystyle\leq C\epsilon^{\alpha_{(2,\infty)}}\|u\|^{(\epsilon)}.

For
\epsilon=0
, since
\Delta^{(0)}=V_{1}
:

\|u-\pi_{1}(u)\|_{\rm E}=0\leq C\epsilon^{\alpha_{(2,\infty)}}\|u\|^{(0)},\quad\forall u\in\Delta^{(0)}.

∎

We are ready to state and prove the version of Grönwall Lemma adapted to Pansu Theorem.

Definition 4.8
.

Given
u\in L^{2}([0,1],\mathfrak{g})
and
\epsilon\in[0,1]
, we define the time-dependent vector field
X^{u,\epsilon}:\;\mathfrak{g}\times[0,1]\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
as

\displaystyle X^{u,\epsilon}(g,t)\coloneqq(L_{g}^{*_{\epsilon}})_{*}u(t).

Lemma 4.9

(Grönwall Lemma for Pansu Theorem)
.

Let
(G,\Delta,\|\cdot\|)
be a nilpotent sub-Finsler Lie group equipped with an asymptotic grading
(V_{i})_{i=1}^{s}
. Let
\|\cdot\|_{\rm E}
be a norm on
\mathfrak{g}
. There is a constant
C>0
such that
\forall\epsilon\in[0,1]
, for all
L>0
, and for all
u\in L^{2}([0,1],\Delta^{(\epsilon)})
with
\|u(t)\|^{(\epsilon)}\leq L
, for almost every
t\in[0,1]
, we have:

(1)

For the vector fields defined in Definition
4.8
, we have that, for
g,h\in B_{\rm E}(0,1)
and almost every
t\in[0,1]
:

\|X^{u,\epsilon}(g,t)-X^{\pi_{1}\circ u,0}(h,t)\|_{\rm E}\leq CL(\epsilon^{\alpha_{\infty}}+\|g-h\|_{\rm E}),

where
\alpha_{\infty}
is the constant from Definition
4.2
.

(2)

Let
\gamma,\eta\colon[0,1]\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
be integral curves of
X^{u,\epsilon}
and
X^{\pi_{1}\circ u,0}
, respectively, with
\gamma(0)=\eta(0)
and
\gamma(t),\eta(t)\in B_{\rm E}(0,1),\forall t\in[0,1]
. Then

\|\gamma(1)-\eta(1)\|_{\rm E}\leq\epsilon^{\alpha_{\infty}}({\rm e}^{CL}-1)

Proof.

Proof of (
1
)
. Let
g,h\in B_{\rm E}(0,1)
and
t\in[0,1]
such that
\|u(t)\|^{(\epsilon)}\leq L
. We have
\|\cdot\|_{\rm E}\overset{\eqref{eq: upper bound of euclidean norm wrt to norm eps}}{\lesssim}\|\cdot\|^{(\epsilon)}
, and consequently
\|u(t)\|_{\rm E}\lesssim L
, where the inequalities are up to a multiplicative constant that does not depend on
\epsilon
or
u
. We have

\displaystyle\|X^{u,\epsilon}(g,t)-X^{\pi_{1}\circ u,0}(h,t)\|_{\rm E}

\displaystyle\leq\|X^{u,\epsilon}(g,t)-X^{u,0}(g,t)\|_{\rm E}

\displaystyle+\|X^{u,0}(g,t)-X^{u,0}(h,t)\|_{\rm E}

\displaystyle+\|X^{u,0}(h,t)-X^{\pi_{1}\circ u,0}(h,t)\|_{\rm E}.

We bound the three quantities on the right-hand side separately.

We bound

\|X^{u,\epsilon}(g,t)-X^{u,0}(g,t)\|_{\rm E}
: Recall that that exponential map
\exp:(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]^{(\tau)})\rightarrow(\mathfrak{g},*_{\tau})
is the identity map for every
\tau\in[0,1].
Therefore

\displaystyle X^{u,\epsilon}(g,t)-X^{u,0}(g,t)=(L_{g}^{*_{\epsilon}})_{*}u(t)-(L_{g}^{*_{0}})_{*}u(t)=\left.\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}r}g*_{\epsilon}ru(t)-g*_{0}ru(t)\right|_{r=0}.

In order to simplify the notation, we define two functions
x_{1},x_{2}:(-1,1)\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
by
x_{1}(r)\coloneqq g
,
x_{2}(r)\coloneqq ru(t).
We exploit the property of the constant
\alpha_{(1,\infty)}
, and by Lemma
4.6
we get:

\displaystyle X^{u,\epsilon}(g,t)-X^{u,0}(g,t)

\displaystyle=\left.\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}r}x_{1}(r)*_{\epsilon}x_{2}(r)-x_{1}(r)*_{0}x_{2}(r)\right|_{r=0}

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{lem: eq: difference of product Pansu}}{=}\left.\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}r}\sum_{k=2}^{s}\sum_{q\in\{1,2\}^{k}}\sum_{p\in\{1,\ldots,s\}^{k}}\sum_{j=|p|+\alpha_{(1,\infty)}}^{s}b_{k,q}\epsilon^{j-|p|}([(x_{q_{1}}(r))_{p_{1}},\ldots,(x_{q_{k}}(r))_{p_{k}}])_{j}\right|_{r=0}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=2}^{s}\sum_{m=1}^{k}\sum_{p\in\{1,\ldots,s\}^{k}}\sum_{j=|p|+\alpha_{(1,\infty)}}^{s}b_{k,q}\epsilon^{j-|p|}([(g)_{p_{1}},\ldots,(u(t))_{p_{m}},\ldots,(g)_{p_{k}}])_{j},

where we used that after derivation and evaluation at
r=0
, the only non-zero iterated brackets of
x_{1}(r)
and
x_{2}(r)
are those where
x_{2}(r)
appears exactly once. We point out that since
\epsilon\in[0,1]
and
j\in\{|p|+\alpha_{(1,\infty)},\ldots,s\}
then
\epsilon^{j-|p|}
is at most
\epsilon^{|p|+\alpha_{(1,\infty)}-|p|}=\epsilon^{\alpha_{(1,\infty)}}
. Finally, recalling that
\|[a,b]\|_{\rm E}\lesssim\|a\|_{\rm E}\|b\|_{\rm E},\;\forall a,b\in\mathfrak{g}
, where the inequality is up to a multiplicative constant depending only on
G
, and
\|\cdot\|_{\rm E}
, we bound

\displaystyle\|X^{u,\epsilon}(g,t)-X^{u,0}(g,t)\|_{\rm E}\lesssim\sum_{k=2}^{s}\sum_{m=1}^{k}\sum_{p\in\{1,\ldots,s\}^{k}}L\epsilon^{|p|+\alpha_{(1,\infty)}-|p|}\lesssim L\epsilon^{\alpha_{(1,\infty)}}.

We bound

\|X^{u,0}(g,t)-X^{u,0}(h,t)\|_{\rm E}
: Notice that the map
\phi:g\mapsto(L_{g}^{*_{0}})_{*}={\rm d}(L_{g}^{*_{0}})_{0}
is smooth. Hence, it is Lipschitz on compact sets, and thus there exists a constant
C
such that

\|\phi(g^{\prime})-\phi(h^{\prime})\|_{\rm op}\leq C\|g^{\prime}-h^{\prime}\|_{\rm E},\quad\forall g^{\prime},h^{\prime}\in B_{\rm E}(0,1),

where
\|\cdot\|_{\rm op}
is the operator norm for linear operators
\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
.
It follows that

\displaystyle\|X^{u,0}(g,t)-X^{u,0}(h,t)\|_{\rm E}

\displaystyle=\|\phi(g)u(t)-\phi(h)u(t)\|_{\rm E}

\displaystyle\leq\|\phi(g)-\phi(h)\|_{\rm op}\|u(t)\|_{\rm E}

\displaystyle\lesssim L\|g-h\|_{\rm E},

where the inequality is up to a multiplicative factor that does not depend on
\epsilon
,
u
, or
L
.

We bound

\|X^{u,0}(h,t)-X^{\pi_{1}\circ u,0}(h,t)\|_{\rm E}
: Since the map
(L_{h}^{*_{0}})_{*}
is linear and smooth in
h
, there exists a constant
C
such that

\|(L_{h^{\prime}}^{*_{0}})_{*}(u(t))-(L_{h^{\prime}}^{*_{0}})_{*}(\pi_{1}(u(t)))\|_{\rm E}\leq C\|u(t)-\pi_{1}(u(t))\|_{\rm E},\quad\forall h^{\prime}\in B_{\rm E}(0,1).

We exploit the property of the constant
\alpha_{(2,\infty)}
, and by Lemma
4.7
we get:

\displaystyle\|X^{u,0}(h,t)-X^{\pi_{1}\circ u,0}(h,t)\|_{\rm E}

\displaystyle\overset{\text{def}}{=}\|(L_{h}^{*_{0}})_{*}(u(t))-(L_{h}^{*_{0}})_{*}(\pi_{1}(u(t))\|_{\rm E}

\displaystyle\leq C\|u(t)-\pi_{1}(u(t))\|_{\rm E}

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{PANSU: bound on norm of projection}}{\leq}CL\epsilon^{\alpha_{(2,\infty)}}.

We conlude
that there exists a constant
C
depending only on
G,d,(V_{i}),L
, and
\|\cdot\|_{\rm E}
such that

\displaystyle\|X^{u,\epsilon}(g,t)-X^{\pi_{1}\circ u,0}(h,t)\|_{\rm E}

\displaystyle\leq\|X^{u,\epsilon}(g,t)-X^{u,0}(g,t)\|_{\rm E}

\displaystyle\quad+\|X^{u,0}(g,t)-X^{u,0}(h,t)\|_{\rm E}

\displaystyle\quad+\|X^{u,0}(h,t)-X^{\pi_{1}\circ u,0}(h,t)\|_{\rm E}.

\displaystyle\leq CL(\epsilon^{\min\{\alpha_{(1,\infty)},\alpha_{(2,\infty)}\}}+\|g-h\|_{\rm E}).

Proof of
(
2
). Apply Grönwall Lemma
4.1
and Lemma
4.9
(
1
).
∎

4.2.
Grönwall Lemma for Mitchell Tangent Theorem

We now prove a version of Grönwall Lemma adapted to the context of Mitchell Theorem (see Lemma
4.14
).

Definition 4.10
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\Delta)
be a polarized Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
and
(W_{i})_{i=1}^{s}
a tangent grading. We define

\displaystyle\alpha_{0}\coloneqq\max\{j\in\mathbb{N}:[W_{p_{1}},\ldots,W_{p_{k}}]\subseteq W_{|p|}\oplus W_{\leq|p|-j},\;\forall k\in\mathbb{N},\;\forall p\in\mathbb{N}^{k}\}.

The constant
\alpha_{0}
is the analog of the constant
\alpha_{(1,\infty)}
in the context of tangent gradings. It can be thought of as quantifying how stratifiable a tangent grading is; a tangent grading is a stratification if and only if
\alpha_{0}=+\infty
. Note that by definition of tangent grading we have
\Delta=W_{1}
, hence there is no analog of the constant
\alpha_{(2,\infty)}
in this context.

Lemma 4.11
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a polarized Lie algebra equipped with a tangent grading which induces projections
x\mapsto(x)_{j}
. Let
n\in\mathbb{N},\;p\in\mathbb{N}^{n},
and
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\in\mathfrak{g}.
Then

(31)

\displaystyle[(x_{1})_{p_{1}},\ldots,(x_{n})_{p_{n}}]-\llbracket(x_{1})_{p_{1}},\ldots,(x_{n})_{p_{n}}\rrbracket^{(0)}=\sum_{j={1}}^{|p|-\alpha_{0}}([(x_{1})_{p_{1}},\ldots,(x_{n})_{p_{n}}])_{j}.

Proof.

We show by induction on
n\in\mathbb{N}
that

(32)

\llbracket(x_{1})_{p_{1}},\ldots,(x_{n})_{p_{n}}\rrbracket^{(0)}=([(x_{1})_{p_{1}},\ldots,(x_{n})_{p_{n}}])_{|p|},\quad\forall p\in\mathbb{N}^{n},\;\forall x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\in\mathfrak{g}.

By definition of the bracket
\llbracket\cdot,\cdot\rrbracket^{(0)}
, we have for
x,y\in\mathfrak{g}
and
j,k\in\mathbb{N}
:

(33)

\llbracket(x)_{j},(y)_{k}\rrbracket^{(0)}=([(x)_{j},(y)_{k}])_{j+k}.

Next, suppose that the claim is true for brackets of length up to
n-1
. Using the properties of tangent gradings, we obtain

\displaystyle([(x_{1})_{p_{1}},\ldots,(x_{n})_{p_{n}}])_{|p|}

\displaystyle\overset{\ref{prop: properties of linear gradings}\eqref{prop: bracket of ALG containment}}{=}\sum_{j=1}^{|p|-p_{1}}([(x_{1})_{p_{1}},([(x_{2})_{p_{2}},\ldots,(x_{n})_{p_{n}}])_{j}])_{|p|}

\displaystyle\overset{\ref{prop: properties of linear gradings}\eqref{prop: bracket of ALG containment}}{=}([(x_{1})_{p_{1}},([(x_{2})_{p_{2}},\ldots,(x_{n})_{p_{n}}])_{|p|-p_{1}}])_{|p|}

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq: lem: difference of brackets mitchell 1}}{=}([(x_{1})_{p_{1}},(\llbracket(x_{2})_{p_{2}},\ldots,(x_{n})_{p_{n}}\rrbracket^{(0)})_{|p|-p_{1}}])_{|p|}

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq: lem: difference of brackets mitchell 2}}{=}\llbracket(x_{1})_{p_{1}},\ldots,(x_{n})_{p_{n}}\rrbracket^{(0)}.

Now, by definition of
\alpha_{0}
, we have

\displaystyle[(x_{1})_{p_{1}},\ldots,(x_{n})_{p_{n}}]-\llbracket(x_{1})_{p_{1}},\ldots,(x_{n})_{p_{n}}\rrbracket^{(0)}

\displaystyle\overset{\ref{prop: properties of linear gradings}\eqref{prop: bracket of ALG containment},\eqref{eq: lem: difference of brackets mitchell 1}}{=}\sum_{j=1}^{|p|-1}([(x_{1})_{p_{1}},\ldots,(x_{n})_{p_{n}}])_{j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{|p|-\alpha_{0}}([(x_{1})_{p_{1}},\ldots,(x_{n})_{p_{n}}])_{j}.

∎

Lemma 4.12
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a polarized Lie algebra equipped with a tangent grading which induces projections

Theorem 5.1

From now to the end of this section, we are in the assumption of Theorem

The next lemma is used to show that the difference between these two curves lies in a Carnot quotient ideal.

(1). It is sufficient to show that the projected curves are solutions to the same Cauchy problem. Recall that

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}{(\pi\circ\gamma_{\epsilon})}(t)

\displaystyle=({\rm d}\pi)\dot{\gamma_{\epsilon}}(t)

\displaystyle=({\rm d}\pi)({\rm d}L_{\gamma_{\epsilon}(t)}^{*_{\epsilon}})u(t)

\displaystyle={\rm d}(\pi\circ L_{\gamma_{\epsilon}(t)}^{*_{\epsilon}})u(t)

\displaystyle={\rm d}(L_{\pi(\gamma_{\epsilon}(t))}^{\mathfrak{i}}\circ\pi)u(t)

\displaystyle={\rm d}(L_{\pi(\gamma_{\epsilon}(t))}^{\mathfrak{i}})\circ\pi(u(t)).

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq: Pansu: bound of v}}{\leq}\rho_{\epsilon}(p,q)+\bar{C}\tilde{C}\|\gamma_{0}(1)-\gamma_{\epsilon}(1)\|^{1/\beta}

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq: Pansu: bound of Euclidean difference 1}}{\leq}\rho_{\epsilon}(p,q)+\bar{C}\tilde{C}({\rm e}^{\hat{C}L}-1)^{1/\beta}\epsilon^{\alpha_{\infty}/\beta}.

We prove the

Let

-geodesic from

to

We claim that the control of

with respect to

\pi_{1}\circ u=v,\text{ and }\|u\|^{(\epsilon)}=\|v\|\text{ almost everywhere.}

Indeed, since

for the abelian product on

\displaystyle={\rm d}(L^{+}_{\pi_{1}(\gamma_{0}(t))})_{0}\pi_{1}\circ v(t)

\displaystyle={\rm d}(L^{+}_{\pi_{1}(\gamma_{0}(t))})_{0}v(t),

\displaystyle={\rm d}(\pi_{1}\circ L_{\gamma_{0}(t)}^{*_{\epsilon}})_{0}u(t)

\|\gamma_{0}(1)-\gamma_{\epsilon}(1)\|\leq\epsilon^{\alpha_{0}}({\rm e}^{\hat{C}L}-1)\overset{\eqref{def: constant C_*}}{\leq}C_{*}L\epsilon^{\alpha_{0}},

for some constant
\hat{C}>0
.

Next, by Lemma
6.2
:
\gamma_{0}^{-1}(1)\cdot_{0}\gamma_{\epsilon}(1)\in\mathfrak{i}\subseteq W_{\leq\beta}
. Therefore, by Theorem
2.3
there exists a constant
C^{\prime}
depending only on
G,(W_{i})_{i=1}^{s},
and
\|\cdot\|
such that

(64)

d_{0}(0,\gamma_{0}^{-1}(1)\cdot_{0}\gamma_{\epsilon}(1))\overset{\eqref{thm: eq: Ball box for Carnot}}{\leq}C^{\prime}\|\gamma_{0}^{-1}(1)\cdot_{0}\gamma_{\epsilon}(1)\|^{1/\beta}.

We need one last bound, which we obtain by applying (
61
):

\displaystyle\|\gamma_{0}^{-1}(1)\cdot_{0}\gamma_{\epsilon}(1)\|

\displaystyle=\|L_{\gamma_{0}^{-1}(1)}^{\cdot_{0}}(\gamma_{0}(1)\cdot_{0}\gamma_{0}^{-1}(1)\cdot_{0}\gamma_{\epsilon}(1))-L_{\gamma_{0}^{-1}(1)}^{\cdot_{0}}(\gamma_{0}(1))\|

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq: Mitchell: bound difference of products}}{\leq}\tilde{C}\|\gamma_{0}(1)\cdot_{0}\gamma_{0}^{-1}(1)\cdot_{0}\gamma_{\epsilon}(1)-\gamma_{0}(1)\|

(65)

\displaystyle=\tilde{C}\|\gamma_{\epsilon}(1)-\gamma_{0}(1)\|.

Finally, we obtain the first inequality:

\displaystyle d_{0}(p,q)

\displaystyle\leq d_{0}(p,\gamma_{0}(1))+d_{0}(\gamma_{0}(1),\gamma_{\epsilon}(1))

\displaystyle\leq L_{d_{0}}(\gamma_{0})+d_{0}(0,\gamma_{0}^{-1}(1)\cdot_{0}\gamma_{\epsilon}(1))

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq: Mitchell: length of curves is the same 1},\eqref{eq: Mitchell: bound of dist of curves1}}{\leq}L_{d_{\epsilon}}(\gamma_{\epsilon})+C^{\prime}\|\gamma_{0}^{-1}(1)\cdot_{0}\gamma_{\epsilon}(1)\|^{1/\beta}

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq: Mitchell: bound of norm of curves1}}{\leq}d_{\epsilon}(p,q)+C^{\prime}\tilde{C}\|\gamma_{0}(1)-\gamma_{\epsilon}(1)\|^{1/\beta}

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq: bound of euclidean difference Mitchell1}}{\leq}d_{\epsilon}(p,q)+C^{\prime}\tilde{C}C_{*}^{1/\beta}L^{1/\beta}\epsilon^{\alpha_{0}/\beta}.

We prove the
second inequality
:
d_{\epsilon}(p,q)\leq d_{0}(p,q)+C\epsilon^{\alpha_{0}/\beta}.
Let
\gamma_{0}:[0,1]\rightarrow B_{d_{0}}(0,r)
be a
d_{0}
-geodesic from
p
to
q
parametrized by constant speed with control
v\in L^{2}([0,1],\Delta)
, for which
d_{0}(p,q)=L_{d_{0}}(\gamma_{0})=\|v\|_{2}\eqqcolon L^{\prime}<2r
. We proceed as in the first inequality; let
\gamma_{\epsilon}:[0,1]\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
be the solution of

\begin{cases}\gamma_{\epsilon}(0)=\gamma_{0}(0)\\
\dot{\gamma}_{\epsilon}(t)=(L^{\cdot_{\epsilon}}_{\gamma_{\epsilon}(t)})_{*}v(t).\end{cases}

We claim that

(66)

\gamma_{\epsilon}(t)\in B_{d_{0}}(0,r^{\prime}(C^{2}+2C)),\;\forall t.

Indeed,

(67)

\displaystyle L_{d_{\epsilon}}(\gamma_{\epsilon})

\displaystyle=L_{d_{0}}(\gamma_{0})

\displaystyle\leq d_{0}(p,q)<2r^{\prime},

and

\displaystyle d_{\epsilon}(0,p)\overset{\eqref{eq: dilated metric are bilipschitz}}{\leq}Cd_{0}(0,p)<Cr^{\prime}.

Hence,

\displaystyle d_{\epsilon}(0,\gamma_{\epsilon}(t))\leq d_{\epsilon}(0,p)+d_{\epsilon}(p,\gamma_{\epsilon}(t))\leq Cr^{\prime}+L_{d_{\epsilon}}(\gamma_{\epsilon})\leq r^{\prime}(C+2),

and thus
\gamma_{\epsilon}
is contained in
B_{d_{\epsilon}}(0,r^{\prime}(C+2))
. Since
Cr^{\prime}(C+2)=r^{\prime}(C^{2}+2C)<R,
by (
23
) we have

B_{d_{\epsilon}}(0,r^{\prime}(C+2))\overset{\eqref{cor: equicontinuity of distances}}{\subseteq}B_{d_{0}}(0,r^{\prime}(C^{2}+2C)),

proving claim (
66
).

We proceed by establishing the necessary bounds as in the first part of the proof.

We observe that both
\gamma_{0}
and
\gamma_{\epsilon}
are contained in
B_{d_{0}}(0,r(C^{2}+2C))\subseteq B_{d_{0}}(0,R)\subseteq B_{\|\cdot\|}(0,1)
, and that
\gamma_{0}
and
\gamma_{\epsilon}
are integral curves of the vector fields
Y^{u,0}
and
Y^{u,\epsilon}
respectively, as in Definition
4.13
. Hence, we may use Grönwall Lemma
4.14
to bound

(68)

\|\gamma_{0}(1)-\gamma_{\epsilon}(1)\|\leq\epsilon^{\alpha_{0}}({\rm e}^{\hat{C}L^{\prime}}-1)\overset{\eqref{def: constant C_*}}{\leq}C_{*}L^{\prime}{\rm e}^{\alpha_{0}}.

Next, since
\gamma_{0}
and
\gamma_{\epsilon}
are contained in
B_{d_{0}}(0,r(C^{2}+2C))
, we have

\gamma_{0}^{-1}(1)\cdot_{0}\gamma_{\epsilon}(1)\overset{\eqref{claim: product lies in small ball Mitchell}}{\in}B_{d_{0}}(0,R).

In addition, by Lemma
6.2
,
\gamma_{0}^{-1}(1)\cdot_{0}\gamma_{\epsilon}(1)\in\mathfrak{i}\subseteq W_{\leq\beta}
. Therefore, we may apply Lemma
6.3
to obtain the bound

(69)

d_{0}(0,\gamma_{0}^{-1}(1)\cdot_{0}\gamma_{\epsilon}(1))\overset{\eqref{eq: lemma unif bound on dilated}}{\leq}C^{\prime}\|\gamma_{0}^{-1}(1)\cdot_{0}\gamma_{\epsilon}(1)\|^{1/\beta},

for some constant
C^{\prime}
that does not depend on
\epsilon
.

We need one last bound, which we obtain by applying (
61
):

\displaystyle\|\gamma_{0}^{-1}(1)\cdot_{\epsilon}\gamma_{\epsilon}(1)\|

\displaystyle=\|L_{\gamma_{0}^{-1}(1)}^{\cdot_{0}}(\gamma_{0}(1)\cdot_{0}\gamma_{0}^{-1}(1)\cdot_{\epsilon}\gamma_{\epsilon}(1))-L_{\gamma_{0}^{-1}(1)}^{\cdot_{0}}(\gamma_{0}(1))\|

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq: Mitchell: bound difference of products}}{\leq}\tilde{C}\|\gamma_{0}(1)\cdot_{0}\gamma_{0}^{-1}(1)\cdot_{\epsilon}\gamma_{\epsilon}(1)-\gamma_{0}(1)\|

(70)

\displaystyle=\tilde{C}\|\gamma_{\epsilon}(1)-\gamma_{0}(1)\|.

We obtain the second inequality:

\displaystyle d_{\epsilon}(p,q)

\displaystyle\leq d_{\epsilon}(p,\gamma_{\epsilon}(1))+d_{\epsilon}(\gamma_{0}(1),\gamma_{\epsilon}(1))

\displaystyle\leq L_{d_{\epsilon}}(\gamma_{\epsilon})+d_{\epsilon}(0,\gamma_{0}^{-1}(1)\cdot_{\epsilon}\gamma_{\epsilon}(1))

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq: Mithell: length of curves is the same2},\eqref{eq: Mitchell: bound of dist of curves2}}{\leq}L_{d_{0}}(\gamma_{0})+C^{\prime}\|\gamma_{0}^{-1}(1)\cdot_{\epsilon}\gamma_{\epsilon}(1)\|^{1/\beta}

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq: Mitchell: bound of norm of curves2}}{\leq}d_{0}(p,q)+C^{\prime}\tilde{C}\|\gamma_{0}(1)-\gamma_{\epsilon}(1)\|^{1/\beta}

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq: bound of euclidean difference Mitchell2}}{\leq}d_{0}(p,q)+C^{\prime}\tilde{C}C_{*}^{1/\beta}L^{\prime 1/\beta}\epsilon^{\alpha_{0}/\beta}.

∎

Next, we use Theorem
6.1
to prove Theorem
1.2
. We stress that an analogous argument can deduce Theorem
6.1
from Theorem
1.2
.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Let
\Omega
be the set from Theorem
6.1
, let
r\in(0,1]
such that
B_{d_{0}}(1,2r)\subseteq\Omega
, and let
p,q\in B_{d_{0}}(1,r)
with
d_{0}(1,p)\geq d_{0}(1,q)
. We define
\epsilon\coloneqq\frac{1}{r}d_{0}(1,p)
. Observe that
\delta_{\epsilon}^{-1}p,\delta_{\epsilon}^{-1}q\in B_{d_{0}}(1,2r)\subseteq\Omega
. Indeed,

d_{0}(1,\delta_{\epsilon}^{-1}p)=rd_{0}(1,p)^{-1}d_{0}(1,p)<2r,

and similarly

d_{0}(1,\delta_{\epsilon}^{-1}q)=rd_{0}(1,p)^{-1}d_{0}(1,q)<2r.

Moreover we have
\epsilon\leq 1
. Therefore, by Theorem
6.1
there is a
C>0
so that

\displaystyle d(p,q)-d_{0}(p,q)

\displaystyle=\epsilon(d_{\epsilon}(\delta_{\epsilon}^{-1}p,\delta_{\epsilon}^{-1}q)-d_{0}(\delta_{\epsilon}^{-1}p,\delta_{\epsilon}^{-1}q))

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{thm: eq: quantitative mitchell0}}{\geq}-Cd_{\epsilon}(\delta_{\epsilon}^{-1}p,\delta_{\epsilon}^{-1}q)^{1/\beta}\epsilon^{1+\alpha_{0}/\beta}

\displaystyle=-Cd(p,q)^{1/\beta}\epsilon^{1+(\alpha_{0}-1)/\beta}

\displaystyle=-Cr^{-1-(\alpha_{0}-1)/\beta}d(p,q)^{1/\beta}d_{0}(1,p)^{1+(\alpha_{0}-1)/\beta},

and

\displaystyle d(p,q)-d_{0}(p,q)

\displaystyle=\epsilon(d_{\epsilon}(\delta_{\epsilon}^{-1}p,\delta_{\epsilon}^{-1}q)-d_{0}(\delta_{\epsilon}^{-1}p,\delta_{\epsilon}^{-1}q))

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{thm: eq: quantitative mitchell0}}{\leq}Cd_{0}(\delta_{\epsilon}^{-1}p,\delta_{\epsilon}^{-1}q)^{1/\beta}\epsilon^{1+\alpha_{0}/\beta}

\displaystyle=Cd_{0}(p,q)^{1/\beta}\epsilon^{1+(\alpha_{0}-1)/\beta}

\displaystyle=Cr^{-1-(\alpha_{0}-1)/\beta}d_{0}(p,q)^{1/\beta}d_{0}(1,p)^{1+(\alpha_{0}-1)/\beta}.

∎

We also obtain the following corollary:

Corollary 6.4
.

Let
(G,d)
be a sub-Finsler Lie group equipped with an
s
-step distribution. With respect to a tangent grading, consider the tangent metric
d_{0}
on a neighborhood of the identity in
G
. Then there are constants
r\in(0,1]
and
C>0
such that

(71)

\displaystyle|d(p,q)-d_{0}(p,q)|\leq C\max\{d_{0}(1,p),d_{0}(1,q)\}^{1+1/s},\quad\forall p,q\in B_{d_{0}}(1,r).

Proof.

It is a consequence of Theorem
1.2
and the fact that by definitions of
\alpha_{0}
and
\beta
, we have
\alpha_{0}/\beta\geq 1/s
for every tangent grading.
∎

We now explain how Theorem
1.2
implies Mitchell Tangent Theorem (Theorem
3.7
) and the quantitative estimate (
12
).

Proof of Theorem
3.7
.

Let
U\subseteq G
be a neighborhood of the identity where the estimate (
6
) holds. For
\epsilon\in(0,1]
, consider the dilations

\delta_{1/\epsilon}:U\rightarrow G_{0},

as in Section
2.3
, where we identify
U
with a neighborhood of the identity in
G_{0}
as in Section
3.2
.

We show that
\forall R>0
,
\forall\tau>0
, the map

\delta_{1/\epsilon}:\left(B_{\frac{1}{\epsilon}d}(1,R),\frac{1}{\epsilon}d\right)\rightarrow(G_{0},d_{0})

is a
(1,\tau)
-quasi-isometric embedding, for
\epsilon>0
small enough.
Indeed, fix
R>0
. Then for
\epsilon>0
small enough so that

B_{\frac{1}{\epsilon}d}(1,R)=B_{d}(1,\epsilon R)\subseteq U,

and for

, by Theorem
1.2
there is a

\displaystyle\left|\frac{1}{\epsilon}d(p,q)-d_{0}(\delta_{1/\epsilon}p,\delta_{1/\epsilon}q)\right|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\epsilon}\left|d(p,q)-d_{0}(p,q)\right|

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq: cor mitchell1}}{\leq}C\frac{1}{\epsilon}(\epsilon R)^{1+\alpha_{0}/\beta}\rightarrow 0,\quad\text{ as }\epsilon\rightarrow 0.

Next, we show
\forall R>0

7.
Applications and examples

In this final section, we provide examples illustrating how our new results yield sharper bounds than those obtained by previous results in the literature. The first family of examples estimates the rate of convergence to the asymptotic cone of products of the form
G\times H
between a Carnot group
G
and a simply connected nilpotent group
H
, in a way that does not depend on
G
.

Proposition 7.1
.

Let
(G,(V_{i}^{G})_{i=1}^{r})
be an
r
-step stratified group and
(H,\Delta_{H})
be a simply connected
s
-step nilpotent Lie group equipped with a bracket generating distribution. Let
d
be a sub-Finsler metric on
G\times H
associated to the distribution
V_{1}^{G}\times\Delta_{H}
, and let
d_{\infty}
be the corresponding Pansu limit metric. Then there exists an asymptotic grading such that

|d(p,q)-d_{\infty}(p,q)|=O(\max\{d_{\infty}(1,p),d_{\infty}(1,q)\}^{1-1/s}),\quad{\rm as}\;p,q\rightarrow\infty.

Proof.

Let
(V_{i}^{H})_{i=1}^{s}
be an asymptotic grading of
H
. Then
(V_{i})_{i=1}^{\max\{r,s\}}
defined by
V_{i}=V_{i}^{G}\times V_{i}^{H}
is an asymptotic grading of
G\times H
. With respect to this grading
H
is a Carnot quotient ideal of
G\times H
(according to Definition
3.18
), and thus by Definition
3.20
we have
\beta\leq s
. Since
\alpha_{\infty}\geq 1
we have
\alpha_{\infty}/\beta\geq 1/s
, and therefore Theorem
1.1
implies

|d(p,q)-d_{\infty}(p,q)|=O(\max\{d_{\infty}(1,p),d_{\infty}(1,q)\}^{1-1/s}),\quad{\rm as}\;p,q\rightarrow\infty.

∎

We stress that the nilpotency step of
G\times H
as in Proposition
7.1
is
\max\{r,s\}
, which could be larger than
s
.

Next, we consider the very specific product as in Example
3.21
(
2
).

Example 7.2
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}=N_{5,2,2}\times N_{5,2,1}
and
(V_{i})_{i=1}^{4}
be the sub-Finsler group equipped with the asymptotic grading from Example
3.21
(
2
).
Then by Proposition
7.1
:

|d(1,p)-d_{\infty}(1,p)|=O(d(1,p)^{2/3}),\quad{\rm as}\;p\rightarrow\infty,

which is an improvement on the bound

|d(1,p)-d_{\infty}(1,p)|=O(d(1,p)^{3/4}),\quad{\rm as}\;p\rightarrow\infty

which one obtains by using
[
BLD12
, Proposition 4.1]
.

We stress that different choices of gradings can give different estimates in both the quantitative version of Pansu Asymptotic Theorem and the quantitative version of Mitchell Tangent Theorem. Regarding the asymptotic theorem
1.1
, the exponent
1-\alpha_{\infty}/\beta
clearly varies with the choice of the asymptotic grading. This number is at most
1-1/s
, and the smaller it is, the sharper the bound. Excluding the Carnot case, this number is at least
1/s
. In the next example, we show that this is the case for a specific grading.

Example 7.3
.

Let
(G,(V_{i})_{i=1}^{s})
be a stratified group. Let
d^{(k)}
be a sub-Finsler distance on
G
associated to the distribution
\Delta_{(k)}\coloneqq V_{1}\oplus V_{k}
, for
k\in\{2,\ldots,s\}
, and let
d_{\infty}^{(k)}
be the corresponding Pansu limit metric. With respect to the stratification, we have
\beta=s
and
\alpha_{\infty}=k-1
. Thus Theorem
1.1
implies

|d^{(k)}(p,q)-d^{(k)}_{\infty}(p,q)|=O(\max\{d^{(k)}_{\infty}(1,p),d^{(k)}_{\infty}(1,q)\}^{(s-k+1)/s}),\quad{\rm as}\;p,q\rightarrow\infty.

Note that for
k=s
, the exponent in the above equation is
1/s
, which by Proposition
4.4
is the minimum value of
1-\alpha_{\infty}/\beta
for groups which are not Carnot.

We also obtain a result similar to Proposition
7.1
, but for tangent spaces.

Proposition 7.4
.

Let
(G,(W_{i}^{G})_{i=1}^{r})
be an
r
-step stratified group and
(H,\Delta_{H})
be a Lie group equipped with an
s
-step bracket generating distribution. Let
d
be a sub-Finsler metric on
G\times H
associated to the distribution
V_{1}^{G}\times\Delta_{H}
, and let
d_{0}
be the corresponding tangent metric. Then there exists a tangent grading such that

|d(p,q)-d_{0}(p,q)|=O(\max\{d_{0}(1,p),d_{0}(1,q)\}^{1+1/s}),\quad{\rm as}\;p,q\rightarrow 0.

Proof.

The proof is similar to the one of Proposition
7.1
.
Let
(W_{i}^{H})_{i=1}^{s}
be a tangent grading of
H
. Then
(W_{i})_{i=1}^{\max\{r,s\}}
defined by
W_{i}=W_{i}^{G}\times W_{i}^{H}
is a tangent grading of
G\times H
. With respect to this grading
H
is a Carnot quotient ideal of
G\times H
(according to Definition
3.18
), and thus by Definition
3.20
we have
\beta\leq s
. Since
\alpha_{0}\geq 1
we have
\alpha_{0}/\beta\geq 1/s
, and therefore Theorem
1.2
implies

|d(p,q)-d_{0}(p,q)|=O(\max\{d_{0}(1,p),d_{0}(1,q)\}^{1+1/s}),\quad{\rm as}\;p,q\rightarrow 0.

∎

On saturation problems for matchings with regularity constraints
Gang Yang, Zixuan Yang, Shenggui Zhang, [2mm] Graduate School of Environment, Information Sciences Yokohama National University, 79-2 Tokiwadai, Hodogaya-ku, Yokohama 240-8501, Japan

Abstract
A graph G G is F F -saturated if G G is F F -free but for any edge e e in the complement of G G the graph G + e G+e contains F F . Gerbner et al. (Discrete Math., 345 (2022), 112921) initiated the study of r ​ s ​ a ​ t ​ ( n , F ) rsat(n,F) , the minimum number of edges in a regular n n -vertex F F -saturated graph, and they posed the problem of for which graphs r ​ s ​ a ​ t ​ ( n , F ) rsat(n,F) exists. Regarding this problem, we obtain the precise value of r ​ s ​ a ​ t ​ ( n , ( m + 1 ) ​ K 2 ) rsat(n,(m+1)K_{2}) for all possible cases, where ( m + 1 ) ​ K 2 (m+1)K_{2} denotes a matching of size m + 1 m+1 . As a natural counterpart, we also determine the maximum number of edges in a regular n n -vertex ( m + 1 ) ​ K 2 (m+1)K_{2} -free graph for all m ≥ 1 m\geq 1 and n ≥ 2 ​ m + 2 n\geq 2m+2 . Keywords: Turán number; saturation number; regular graph; matching

Introduction

Throughout this paper all graphs are finite, undirected, and simple. For terminology and notations not defined here, we refer the reader to Bondy and Murty
[
2
]
.

Let
G
be a graph with vertex set
V(G)
and edge set
E(G)
. The number of vertices of
G
is called its
order
; and the number of edges in
G
, denoted by
e(G)
, is called its
size
. For a vertex
u\in V(G)
, the
degree
of
u
in
G
is denoted by
d_{G}(u)
. For two disjoint subsets
S,T\subseteq V(G)
, let
E_{G}(S,T)
denote the set of edges of
G
joining
S
to
T
, and
e_{G}(S,T):=|E_{G}(S,T)|
. For
S\subseteq V(G)
, we use
G-S
to denote the subgraph
G[V(G)\setminus S]
. For two graphs
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
, we use
G_{1}\cup G_{2}
to denote the graph with vertex set
V(G_{1})\cup V(G_{2})
and edge set
E(G_{1})\cup E(G_{2})
.

A
matching
of a graph
G
is a subset of
E(G)
such that no two edges share a vertex in common.
A matching
M
is a
maximum matching
of a graph
G
if there does not exist a matching
M^{\prime}
in
G
such that
|M^{\prime}|>|M|
. The cardinality of a maximum matching of a graph
G
, denoted by
\nu(G)
, is called the
matching number
of
G
. A
perfect matching
of a graph is a matching covering all vertices. A graph
G
is said to be
factor-critical
if
G-\{v\}
has a perfect matching for every
v\in V(G)
.

Given two positive integers
p
and
q
, let
K_{p,q}
denote the complete bipartite graph with
p
and
q
vertices in the two bipartite sets respectively; we use
K_{n}
and
C_{n}
to denote the complete graph and the cycle of
n
vertices, respectively. A complete subgraph is called a
clique
. We write
k
disjoint copies of
K_{n}
as
kK_{n}
.

For a graph
F
, the widely investigated
Turán number

ex(n,F)
are concerned with the maximum number of edges that an
n
-vertex graph can have, if it does not contain
F
as a subgraph (namely
F
-free
). A natural counterpart is the
saturation number

sat(n,F)
, the minimum number of edges in
n
-vertex
F
-saturated graphs, where a graph
G
is called
F
-saturated
if it is
F
-free, but adding any edge to
G
creates a copy of
F
. In efforts to further understand saturation numbers and the structure of saturated graphs, there has been a substantial amount of research on adding degree conditions to the saturated graphs. For instance, imposing a maximum degree or minimum degree condition has been investigated by Alon, Erdős, Holzman, and Krivelevich
[
1
]
, Day
[
6
]
, Duffus and Hanson
[
7
]
, Füredi and Seress
[
9
]
, Hanson and Seyffarth
[
13
]
. In this paper, we study a related function, where one restricts to regular graphs.

Gerbner, Patkós, Tuza, and Vizer
[
11
]
initiated the study of the existence of regular
F
-saturated graphs and their number of edges. Let
rsat(n,F)
denote the minimum number of edges in a regular
n
-vertex
F
-saturated graph (if such a graph exists). They proved that
rsat(n,K_{3})
exists for all sufficiently large
n
, and they also posed the problem (see Problems 4.4 and 4.5 in
[
11
]
) in the following.

Problem 1

(Gerbner, Patkós, Tuza, and Vizer, 2022)
.

For which graphs does
rsat(n,F)
exist for some
n
or infinitely many values of
n
?

Regarding this problem, Timmons
[
16
]
proved
rsat(n,K_{4})
and
rsat(n,K_{5})
exist for
n>59
. Davini and Timmons
[
5
]
proved that for any even integer
l\geq 4
, there is an
n
-vertex regular
C_{l+1}
-saturated graph for all
n>12l^{2}+36l+24
. The first aim here is to prove
rsat(n,(m+1)K_{2})
exists for some
n
and determine its value.

Theorem 1
.

Let
n
and
m\geq 1
be two integers. If
2m+2\leq n\leq 3m
and
(n-2m)\mid m
,

\displaystyle rsat(n,(m+1)K_{2})=\frac{nm}{n-2m};

otherwise,
rsat(n,(m+1)K_{2})
does not exist.

Gerbner, Patkós, Tuza, and Vizer
[
10
]
introduced the
regular Turán number

rex(n,F)
, the maximum number of edges in a regular
n
-vertex
F
-free graph. Most of the previous work on regular Turán numbers is given by the extensive study of cages (see
[
8
]
for a survey), where one forbids all cycles up to a fixed length. For other graphs, Gerbner, Patkós, Tuza, and Vizer
[
10
]
showed that for non-bipartite
F
with odd girth
g
, one has
rex(n,F)\geq n^{2}/(g+6)-O(n)
and asked to determine
\lim\inf_{n\to\infty}rex(n,F)/n^{2}
for non-bipartite
F
. This problem was solved independently in
[
3
]
and
[
4
]
for graphs
F
with chromatic number at least 4, and the authors obtained asymptotic results for several graphs with chromatic number 3. Exact results on regular Turán numbers of trees were determined in
[
12
]
, and complete graphs were obtained in
[
4
,
12
]
. Tait and Timmons
[
15
]
gave lower bounds on
rex(n,F)
, that are best possible up to a constant factor, when
F
is one of complete bipartite graphs
K_{2,p},K_{3,3}
, or
K_{m,p}
for
p>m!
.
The second aim in this paper is to determine the regular Turán number of matchings.

Theorem 2
.

Let
n
and
m
be integers such that
n\geq 2m+2
and
m\geq 1
. Then

\displaystyle rex(n,(m+1)K_{2})=\begin{cases}n(\lfloor\frac{n}{n-2m}\rfloor-1)/2,&\text{if $\lfloor\frac{n}{n-2m}\rfloor$ is odd;}\\
n(\lfloor\frac{n}{n-2m}\rfloor-2)/2,&\text{if $\lfloor\frac{n}{n-2m}\rfloor$ is even.}\end{cases}

Remark 1.
There exist several extremal graphs satisfying conditions to attain the
maximum number of edges in Theorem
2
. Let
2m+2\leq n
, and let
r\in\{\lfloor n/n-2m\rfloor-1,\lfloor n/n-2m\rfloor-2\}
be an even integer and
r\geq 2
. Note that

\displaystyle(n-2m)(r+1)\leq(n-2m)\Big(\frac{n}{n-2m}\Big)=n.

Let
K_{2r_{1}+1},K_{2r_{2}+1},\ldots,K_{2r_{n-2m}+1}
be some complete graphs such that

\displaystyle\min\{2r_{1}+1,2r_{2}+1,\ldots,2r_{n-2m}+1\}\geq r+1,

and
n=\sum_{i=1}^{n-2m}2r_{i}+1
. For each
i\in\{1,2,\ldots,n-2m\}
,
K_{2r_{i}+1}
can be decomposed into
r_{i}
edge-disjoint Hamilton cycles, saying
C_{2r_{i}+1}^{1},C_{2r_{i}+1}^{2},\ldots,C_{2r_{i}+1}^{r_{i}}
, let
G_{i}=\bigcup_{j=1}^{r/2}C_{2r_{i}+1}^{j}
. Take
G=G_{1}\cup G_{2}\cup\cdots\cup G_{n-2m}
as the extremal graph. One can check that
G
is an
r
-regular graph of order
n
with

\displaystyle\nu(G)=\sum_{i=1}^{n-2m}\nu(G_{i})=\sum_{i=1}^{n-2m}r_{i}=m~~(since~n=\sum_{i=1}^{n-2m}2r_{i}+1),

and

\displaystyle e(G)=\frac{nr}{2}=\begin{cases}n(\lfloor\frac{n}{n-2m}\rfloor-1)/2,&\text{if $\lfloor\frac{n}{n-2m}\rfloor$ is odd;}\\
n(\lfloor\frac{n}{n-2m}\rfloor-2)/2,&\text{if $\lfloor\frac{n}{n-2m}\rfloor$ is even.}\end{cases}

and

For completing the proofs of Theorems

Theorem 3

(Lovász and Plummer,
[
14
]
)
.

Let
G
be a graph without perfect matchings. Then
G
has a subset
S
of
V(G)
such that

(i)

every component of
G-S
is factor-critical;

(ii)

there exists a maximum matching in
G
which matches each vertex of
S
with vertices in different components of
G-S
.

Proof of Theorem
1
.

If
n\leq 2m+1
, there is no matching of size
m+1
in any
n
-vertex graph, which implies that there does not exist
n
-vertex
(m+1)K_{2}
-saturated graphs, and thus
rsat(n,(m+1)K_{2})
does not exist.

Next we consider
n\geq 2m+2
. If
n\leq 3m
and
m/(n-2m)
is an integer, then we can take a graph
G^{\prime}
consisting of
(n-2m)
cliques
K_{\frac{2m}{n-2m}+1}
. Notice that
G^{\prime}
is a
\frac{2m}{n-2m}
-regular graph with order
n
such that

\displaystyle\nu(G^{\prime})=(n-2m)\frac{m}{n-2m}=m

and

\displaystyle e(G^{\prime})=\frac{n\frac{2m}{n-2m}}{2}=\frac{nm}{n-2m},

and adding an edge between any two of
n-2m
cliques to
G^{\prime}
yields a matching of size
m+1
. So the
n
-vertex regular
(m+1)K_{2}
-saturated graphs exist under this condition.

Assume that
G
is an
r
-regular
(m+1)K_{2}
-saturated graph of order
n
such that
e(G)=rsat(n,(m+1)K_{2})
.
Since
n\geq 2m+2
,
G
does not contain perfect matchings and
r\geq 2
. So by Theorem
3
(i), there is a subset
S
of
V(G)
such that every component of
G-S
is factor-critical. We use
D_{1},D_{2},\cdots,D_{q}
to denote these components of
G-S
. Let
s:=|S|
. For
1\leq i\leq q
, write
d_{i}:=|V(D_{i})|
. By Theorem
3
(i) and (ii), we can infer that

\displaystyle\nu(G)=s+\sum_{i=1}^{q}\frac{d_{i}-1}{2}=m.

(1)

Claim 1
.

S=\emptyset
.

By contradiction. Suppose that
S\neq\emptyset
. Then for any vertex
v\in S
, we have
d_{G}(v)=n-1
. Indeed, if
d_{G}(v)<n-1
, there exists a vertex
u\in V(G)\setminus\{v\}
such that
uv\notin E(G)
, and thus by (
1
) we have

\displaystyle\nu(G+\{uv\})=s+\sum_{i=1}^{q}\frac{d_{i}-1}{2}=m=\nu(G),

contradicting the assumption that
G
is
(m+1)K_{2}
-saturated.
Since
G
is
r
-regular, it follows that
r=n-1
. So
G
is a complete graph with order
n\geq 2m+2
, which implies that there exists a matching of size
m+1
in
G
, again a contradiction. Hence, the claim holds.

Claim 2
.

d_{1}=d_{2}=\cdots=d_{q}\geq 3
.

Suppose, by contradiction, that there exist two different components
D_{i}
and
D_{j}
with
d_{i}<d_{j}
. Since
G
is
r
-regular and
S=\emptyset
by Claim
1
, both
D_{i}
and
D_{j}
are
r
-regular. So we have
r\leq d_{i}-1<d_{j}-1
, implying that
D_{j}
is not a clique. Then there exists an edge
uv\notin E(D_{j})
, by (
1
), we have

\displaystyle\nu(G+\{uv\})=s+\sum_{i=1}^{q}\frac{d_{i}-1}{2}=m=\nu(G),

contradicting the assumption that
G
is
(m+1)K_{2}
-saturated. Therefore, all components
D_{i}
have the same order. By Theorem
3
(i), each
D_{i}
is factor-critical, and so each has an odd order with at least
3
.

Claim 3
.

For
1\leq i\leq q
, each
D_{i}
is a clique.

By contradiction. Suppose that
D_{i}
is not a clique for some
1\leq i\leq q
. Then adding an edge
uv
within
D_{i}
gives a graph
G+\{uv\}
, and by (
1
) we have

\displaystyle\nu(G+\{uv\})=s+\sum_{i=1}^{q}\frac{d_{i}-1}{2}=m=\nu(G),

contradicting the
(m+1)K_{2}
-saturation of
G
. Therefore, the claim is ture.

By Claims
1
,
2
, and
3
, we have that

is the union of
q
factor-critical cliques with the same order
d_{1}
. Thus

\displaystyle n=qd_{1},

(2)

and by (

), we have

\displaystyle q\Big(\frac{d_{1}-1}{2}\Big)=m.

(3)

Combining (
2
) and (
3
), one can see

\displaystyle(n-2m)(d_{1}-1)=q(d_{1}-1)=2m,

i.e.,

\displaystyle d_{1}-1=\frac{2m}{n-2m}.

Therefore,

is an even integer (i.e.,

is an integer), and

\displaystyle e(G)=\frac{nr}{2}=\frac{n(d_{1}-1)}{2}=\frac{nm}{n-2m}.

It remains to show that

. Combining (
2
) and (
3
), we obtain

\displaystyle q=n-2m.

Thus, by Claim
2
, we have

\displaystyle n=qd_{1}\geq 3(n-2m),

i.e.,

.

The proof of Theorem

is complete. ∎

Proof of Theorem
2
.

We first show the lower bound for

.
Let
r\in\{\lfloor n/n-2m\rfloor-1,\lfloor n/n-2m\rfloor-2\}
be an even integer. Then we can see that
n-(n-2m-1)(r+1)\geq r+1
is odd through calculations. Consider the graph
G^{\prime}
consisting of
G_{1}\cup\bigcup_{i=2}^{n-2m}G_{i}
, where
G_{i}=K_{r+1}
for each

and

is an
r
-regular factor-critical graph of order

. Notice that
G^{\prime}
is an
r
-regular graph of order
n
with

\displaystyle\nu(G^{\prime})=\frac{r}{2}(n-2m-1)+\frac{n-(n-2m-1)(r+1)-1}{2}=m,

and

\displaystyle e(G^{\prime})=\frac{nr}{2}=\begin{cases}n(\lfloor\frac{n}{n-2m}\rfloor-1)/2,&\text{if $\lfloor\frac{n}{n-2m}\rfloor$ is odd;}\\
n(\lfloor\frac{n}{n-2m}\rfloor-2)/2,&\text{if $\lfloor\frac{n}{n-2m}\rfloor$ is even.}\end{cases}

Let

be a

-regular graph of order

. By the definition of

,

\displaystyle e(G)\geq e(G^{\prime})=\begin{cases}n(\lfloor\frac{n}{n-2m}\rfloor-1)/2,&\text{if $\lfloor\frac{n}{n-2m}\rfloor$ is odd;}\\
n(\lfloor\frac{n}{n-2m}\rfloor-2)/2,&\text{if $\lfloor\frac{n}{n-2m}\rfloor$ is even.}\end{cases}

(4)

In the following, we will prove
e(G)\leq e(G^{\prime})
. Since
n\geq 2m+2
,
G
does not contain perfect matchings and
d\geq 2
. So by Theorem
3
(i), there is a subset
S
of
V(G)
such that every component of
G-S
is factor-critical. We use
D_{1},D_{2},\cdots,D_{q}
to denote these components of
G-S
. Let
s:=|S|
. For
1\leq i\leq q
, write
d_{i}:=|V(D_{i})|

(i) and (ii), we can infer that

\displaystyle m=s+\sum_{i=1}^{q}\frac{d_{i}-1}{2}

and

\displaystyle n=s+\sum_{i=1}^{q}d_{i}.

Combining (

) and (

\displaystyle q=n-2m+s.

Thus,

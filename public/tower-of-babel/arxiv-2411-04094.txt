The Bohr’s phenomenon for certain K-quasiconformal harmonic mappings
Vasudevarao Allu, Raju Biswas and Rajib Mandal

Abstract
. The primary objective of this paper is to establish several sharp versions of improved Bohr inequalities, refined Bohr inequalities, and Bohr-Rogosinski inequalities for the class of K K -quasiconformal sense-preserving harmonic mappings f = h + g ¯ f=h+\overline{g} in the unit disk 𝔻 := { z ∈ ℂ : | z | < 1 } \mathbb{D}:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|<1\} , where f f and g g are analytic functions in 𝔻 \mathbb{D} .

1.
Introduction and Preliminaries

Let
f
be an analytic function on the open unit disk
\mathbb{D}:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|<1\}
with the Taylor series expansion

\displaystyle f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}

(1.1)

such that
|f(z)|\leq 1
in
\mathbb{D}
.
Then, for the majorant series
M_{f}(r):=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|a_{n}|r^{n}
of
f
, we have

\displaystyle M_{f}(r)\leq 1\quad\text{for}\quad|z|=r\leq 1/3.

(1.2)

The radius
1/3
can’t be improved. It is observed that if a complex-valued function
f:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{C}
satisfies the inequality
|f(z)|\leq 1
for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
,
and if there exists a point
y\in\mathbb{D}
such that
|f(y)|=1
, then
f(z)
reduces to a unimodular constant function.
Here
1/3
is known as Bohr radius while the inequality (
1.2
) is known as classical Bohr inequality. Actually, H. Bohr
[
21
]
obtained the inequality (
1.2
) for
r\leq 1/6

but later Weiner, Riesz and Schur
[
23
]
independently improved it to
1/3
.

In recent years, there has been a substantial body of research devoted to the classical Bohr inequality and its generalized forms.
The notion of Bohr radius has been discussed by Abu-Muhanna and Ali
[
1
,
2
]
in the context of analytic functions from
\mathbb{D}
to simply connected domains, as well as to
the exterior of the closed unit disk in
\mathbb{C}
. Furthermore, the Bohr phenomenon for shifted disks and simply connected domains is explored in depth in
[
7
,
27
,
28
]
. In their
respective studies, Allu and Halder
[
10
]
and Bhowmik and Das
[
18
]
have examined the Bohr phenomenon in the context of subordination classes.
Boas and Khavinson
[
20
]
have studied the notion of the Bohr radius to encompass the case of several complex variables and identified the multidimensional Bohr
radius as a key contribution to this field of research. Many researchers have built upon this foundation, extending and generalizing this phenomenon in different contexts
(see
[
4
,
5
,
40
]
). We refer to
[
6
,
8
,
11
,
12
,
16
,
26
,
30
,
31
,
33
,
35
,
38
,
41
,
43
,
19
]
and the references
listed therein for an in-depth investigation on several other aspects of Bohr’s inequality.

In addition to the notion of the Bohr radius, there is another concept, known as the Rogosinski radius
[
46
]
, which is defined as follows: Let
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
be analytic in
\mathbb{D}
such that
|f(z)|<1
in
\mathbb{D}
. Then, for every
N\geq 1
, we have

\left|S_{N}(z)\right|<1
in the disk
|z|<1/2
, where
S_{N}(z):=\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}a_{n}z^{n}
denotes partial sum of
f
and
the radius
1/2
is sharp. Motivated by Rogosinski radius, Kayumov and Ponnusamy
[
34
]
have considered the Bohr-Rogosinski sum
R_{N}^{f}(z)
which is defined by

\displaystyle R_{N}^{f}(z):=|f(z)|+\sum_{n=N}^{\infty}|a_{n}||z|^{n}.

It is easy to see that
|S_{N}(z)|=\left|f(z)-\sum_{n=N}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}\right|\leq R_{N}^{f}(z)
. Moreover, the Bohr-Rogosinski sum

R_{N}^{f}(z)
is related to the classical Bohr sum (Majorant series) in which
N=1
and
f(0)
is replaced by
f(z)
.
For an analytic function
f
in
\mathbb{D}
with
|f(z)|<1
in
\mathbb{D}
, Kayumov and Ponnusamy
[
34
]
defined the Bohr-Rogosinski radius as the largest number
r\in(0,1)

such that
R_{N}^{f}(z)\leq 1
for
|z|<r
and established the following result.

Theorem A
.

[
34
]

Let
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
be analytic in
\mathbb{D}
and
|f(z)|\leq 1
. Then, for each
N\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

\displaystyle|f(z)|+\sum_{n=N}^{\infty}|a_{n}||z|^{n}\leq 1

(1.3)

for
|z|=r\leq R_{N}
, where
R_{N}
is the positive root of the equation
2(1+r)r^{N}-(1-r)^{2}=0
. The radius
R_{N}
is the best possible.

Note that the following is another way of writing the right side of the inequality (
1.3
):

\displaystyle 1=|f(0)|+1-|f(0)|=|f(0)|+d\left(f(0),\partial\mathbb{D}\right),

where
d\left(f(0),\partial\mathbb{D}\right)
denotes the Euclidean
distance between
f(0)
and the boundary
\partial\mathbb{D}
of
\mathbb{D}
.

In 2020, Ponnusamy
et al.

[
44
]
obtained the following refined Bohr inequality by employing a refined version of the coefficient inequalities.

Theorem B
.

[
44
]
Let
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
be analytic in
\mathbb{D}
and
|f(z)|\leq 1
. Then

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|a_{n}|\rho^{n}+\left(\frac{1}{1+|a_{0}|}+\frac{\rho}{1-\rho}\right)\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_{n}|^{2}\rho^{2n}\leq 1

(1.4)

for
r\leq 1/(2+|a_{0}|)
and the numbers
1/(1+|a_{0}|)
and
1/(2+|a_{0}|)
cannot be improved.

Let
h
be an analytic function in
\mathbb{D}
and
\mathbb{D}_{r}=:\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|<r\}
for
0<r<1
. Let
S_{r}(h)
denote the planar integral

\displaystyle S_{r}(h)=\int_{\mathbb{D}_{r}}|h^{\prime}(z)|^{2}dA(z).

If
h(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
, then it is well-known that
S_{r}(h)/\pi=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n|a_{n}|^{2}r^{2n}
and if
h
is univalent, then
S_{r}(h)
is the area of the image
h(\mathbb{D}_{r})
.

1.1.
The Bohr phenomenon for the class of subordinations

Let
\mathcal{B}
denote the class of all analytic functions
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
that are defined on the unit disk
\mathbb{D}
, with

|f(z)|\leq 1
in
\mathbb{D}
.
For any
analytic functions
f
and
g
defined on
\mathbb{D}
, we say that the function
f
is subordinate to
g
, denoted by
f\prec g
, if there exists an
\omega\in\mathcal{B}
with
\omega(0)=0
and
f(z)=g\left(\omega(z)\right)
for
z\in\mathbb{D}
. It is well-known that, if
g
is univalent in
\mathbb{D}
, then
f\prec g
if, and only if,
f(0)=g(0)
and
f(\mathbb{D})\subset g(\mathbb{D})
. Also, it is clear that, if
f\prec g
, then
|f^{\prime}(0)|\leq|g^{\prime}(0)|
. For basic details and results on subordination classes, we refer to
[
24
, Chapter 6]
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
denote the class of all analytic functions on
g
on
\mathbb{D}
satisfying the normalization
g(0)=g^{\prime}(0)-1=0
. Let
\mathcal{S}
denote the class of univalnet (
i.e.
, one-to-one) functions on
\mathbb{D}
. Let
\mathcal{S}^{*}
and
\mathcal{C}
denote the subclass of
\mathcal{S}
of mappings that map

\mathbb{D}
onto starlike and convex domains, respectively. For additional information regarding these classes and several other related subclasses of
\mathcal{S}
, we refer to
[
24
]
.
If
g\in\mathcal{S}
, then
1/4\leq d\left(0,\partial g(\mathbb{D})\right)\leq 1
and if
g\in\mathcal{C}
, then
1/2\leq d\left(0,\partial g(\mathbb{D})\right)\leq 1
(see
[
24
, Theorem 2.3 and Theorem 2.15]
).

The concept of the Bohr phenomenon, as it applies to the family of functions defined by subordination, was first introduced by Abu-Muhanna
[
1
]
.
In this paper, we will use the notation
S(g)
to denote the class of all analytic functions
f
in
\mathbb{D}
that are subordinate to a fixed univalent function
g
,
i.e.,

S(g):=\{f:f\prec g\}
.
The family
S(g)
is said to exhibit a Bohr phenomenon if there exists an
r_{g}\in(0,1]
such that, for any
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}\in S(g)
, the inequality

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_{n}|r^{n}\leq d\left(g(0),\partial g(\mathbb{D})\right)\quad\text{holds for}\quad|z|=r\leq r_{g}.

(1.5)

The largest
r_{g}
is called the Bohr radius.
Similarly, the family
S(g)
is said to exhibit a Bohr-Rogosinski phenomenon if there exists an
r_{N,g}\in(0,1]
such that, for any
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}\in S(g)
, then

\displaystyle|f(z)|+\sum_{n=N}^{\infty}|a_{n}|r^{n}\leq|g(0)|+d\left(g(0),\partial g(\mathbb{D})\right)\quad\text{holds for}\quad|z|=r\leq r_{N,g}.

(1.6)

The largest
r_{N,g}
is called the Bohr-Rogosinski radius.

It is evident that
g(z)=(a-z)/(1-\overline{a}z)
with
|a|<1
, then
g(\mathbb{D})=\mathbb{D}
,
S(g)=\mathcal{B}
and
d\left(g(0),\partial g(\mathbb{D})\right)=1-|g(0)|=1-|a|
. This implies that (
1.5
) holds for
|z|<1/3
by (
1.2
) and (
1.6
) holds with
r_{N,g}=R_{N}
, according to Theorem A.

In
[
1
]
, Abu-Muhanna have established the following Bohr phenomenon for the subordination class
S(g)
, where
g
is univalent in
\mathbb{D}
.

Theorem C
.

[
1
, Theorem 1, P. 1072]

If
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}\in S(g)
and
g(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}b_{n}z^{n}
is univalent, then

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_{n}|r^{n}\leq d\left(g(0),\partial g(\mathbb{D})\right)\quad\text{for}\quad|z|=r\leq r_{0}=3-2\sqrt{2}

and this radius is sharp for the Koebe function
f(z)=z/(1-z)^{2}
.

It has been shown that
r_{0}
can be improved to
1/3
, which is sharp if
g
is a convex and univalent function,
i.e.,

g(\mathbb{D})
is a convex domain.
It can be observed that whenever a function
f
maps
\mathbb{D}
into a domain
g(\mathbb{D})
that is distinct from
\mathbb{D}
, the Bohr inequality (
1.5
) can be established in a
general sense if
f\in S(g)
, where
g
is the covering map from
\mathbb{D}
onto
g(\mathbb{D})
that satisfies
f(0)=g(0)
. The implementation of this concept has yielded a number of results (see
[
1
,
2
,
3
]
).
It is therefore of significant interest to study the Bohr phenomenon for
S(g)
, where
g
belongs to a specific class of univalent functions.

The refined version of Bohr’s inequality (
1.4
) can be expressed as follows:

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_{n}|r^{n}+\left(\frac{1}{2-(1-|f(0)|)}+\frac{r}{1-r}\right)\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_{n}|^{2}r^{2n}\leq 1-|a_{0}|=1-|f(0)|.

It is evident that the quantity
1-|f(0)|
represents the distance between
f(0)
and the boundary
\partial\mathbb{D}
of
\mathbb{D}
. Recently, Ponnusamy
et al.

[
45
,
44
]
have derived several refined versions of Bohr’s inequality for bounded analytic functions. The family
S(g)
is said to exhibit a refined Bohr phenomenon if there exists an
r_{g}\in(0,1]
such that, for any
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}\in S(g)
, the inequality

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_{n}|r^{n}+\left(\frac{1}{2-\lambda}+\frac{r}{1-r}\right)\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_{n}|^{2}r^{2n}\leq\lambda\quad\text{holds for}\quad|z|=r\leq r_{g},

where
\lambda=d\left(g(0),\partial g(\mathbb{D})\right)\leq 1
. The largest number
r_{g}
is called the Bohr radius in the refined formulation.

Let us consider the complex-valued function
f=u+iv
defined in a simply connected domain
\Omega
. If
f
satisfies the Laplace equation
\Delta f=4f_{z\overline{z}}=0
, then
f

is said to be harmonic in
\Omega
,
i.e.
,
u
and
v
are real harmonic in
\Omega
.
Note that every harmonic mapping
f
has the canonical representation
f=h+\overline{g}
, where
h
and
g
are analytic in
\Omega
, known as the analytic and co-analytic parts
of
f
, respectively, and
\overline{g(z)}
denotes the complex conjugate of
g(z)
. This representation is unique up to an additive constant (see
[
25
]
). The inverse function theorem
and a result of Lewy
[
37
]
demonstrate that a harmonic function
f
is locally univalent in
\Omega
if, and only if, the Jacobian of
f
, defined by
J_{f}(z):=|h^{\prime}(z)|^{2}-|g^{\prime}(z)|^{2}

is non-zero in
\Omega
. A harmonic mapping
f
is locally univalent and sense-preserving
in
\Omega
if, and only if,
J_{f}(z)>0
in
\Omega
or equivalently if
h^{\prime}\not=0
in
\Omega

and the dilatation
\omega_{f}:=\omega=g^{\prime}/h^{\prime}
of
f
has the property that
|\omega_{f}|<1
in
\Omega
(see
[
37
]
).

A locally univalent and sense-preserving harmonic mapping
f=h+\overline{g}
is said to be
K
-quasiconformal harmonic on
\mathbb{D}
if the condition
|\omega_{f}(z)|\leq k<1
is satisfied for
z\in\mathbb{D}
, where
K=(1+k)/(1-k)\geq 1
(see
[
32
,
42
]
). Clearly,
k\to 1
corresponds to the limiting case
K\to\infty
.

In 2019, Liu and Ponnusamy
[
39
]
established the following results for determining the Bohr radii for harmonic mappings defined in the unit disk
\mathbb{D}
whose analytic part is subordinate to some analytic function.

Theorem D
.

[
39
]
Suppose that
f(z)=h(z)+\overline{g(z)}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}+\overline{\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}b_{n}z^{n}}
is a sense-preserving
K
-quasiconformal harmonic mapping in
\mathbb{D}
and
h\prec\phi
, where
\phi
is univalent and convex in
\mathbb{D}
. Then

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(|a_{n}|+|b_{n}|\right)r^{n}\leq d\left(\phi(0),\partial\phi(\mathbb{D})\right)\quad\text{for}\quad r\leq\frac{K+1}{5K+1}.

The number
(K+1)/(5K+1)
is sharp.

Theorem E
.

[
39
]
Suppose that
f(z)=h(z)+\overline{g(z)}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}+\overline{\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}b_{n}z^{n}}
is a sense-preserving
K
-quasiconformal harmonic mapping in
\mathbb{D}
and
h\prec\phi
, where
\phi
is analytic and univalent in
\mathbb{D}
. Then

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(|a_{n}|+|b_{n}|\right)r^{n}\leq d\left(\phi(0),\partial\phi(\mathbb{D})\right)\quad\text{for}\quad r\leq r_{u}(k),

where
k=(K-1)/(K+1)
and
r_{u}(k)\in(0,1)
is the root of the equation

\displaystyle(1-r)^{2}-4r(1+k\sqrt{1+r})=0.

In 2022, Ponnusamy
et al.

[
45
]
obtained the following two results pertaining to Bohr’s phenomenon in a refined formulation for a more general family of subordinations.

Theorem F
.

[
45
]
Let
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
and
g
be analytic in
\mathbb{D}
such that
g
is univalent and convex in
\mathbb{D}
. Assume that
f\in S(g)

and
\lambda=d\left(g(0),\partial g(\mathbb{D})\right)\leq 1
. Then

\displaystyle T_{f}(r):=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_{n}|r^{n}+\left(\frac{1}{2-\lambda}+\frac{r}{1-r}\right)\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_{n}|^{2}r^{2n}\leq\lambda\quad\text{holds for all}\quad r\leq r_{*},

where
r_{*}\approx 0.24683
is the unique positive root of the equation
3r^{3}-5r^{2}-3r+1=0
in the interval
(0,1)
. Moreover, for any
\lambda\in(0,1)
there exists a uniquely defined
r_{0}\in(r_{*},1/3)
such that
T_{f}(r)\leq\lambda
for
r\in[0,r_{0}]
. The radius
r_{0}
can be calculated as the solution of the equation

\displaystyle\Phi(\lambda,r)=4r^{3}\lambda^{2}-(7r^{3}+3r^{2}-3r+1)\lambda+6r^{3}-2r^{2}-6r+2=0.

The result is sharp.

Theorem G
.

[
45
]
Let
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
and
g
be an analytic and univalent function in
\mathbb{D}
. Assume that
f\in S(g)

and
\lambda=d\left(g(0),\partial g(\mathbb{D})\right)\leq 1
. Then

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_{n}|r^{n}+\left(\frac{1}{2-\lambda}+\frac{r}{1-r}\right)\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_{n}|^{2}r^{2n}\leq\lambda\quad\text{holds for all}\quad r\leq r_{g},

where
r_{g}\approx 0.128445
is the unique positive root of the equation

\displaystyle(1-6r+r^{2})(1-r)^{2}(1+r)^{3}-16r^{2}(1+r^{2})=0

in the interval
(0,1)
. The result is sharp.

1.2.
The family of concave univalent functions with opening angle
\pi\alpha
at infinity

A domain
G
is considered to have an opening angle
\pi\alpha
at infinity if
G^{c}=\mathbb{C}\setminus G
is included in a wedge-shaped region with an angle less than or equal to
\pi\alpha
but not in a region with an angle larger than
\pi\alpha
. In the context of this study, it is necessary to introduce the following family of univalent functions:

A function
g:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{C}
is said to belong to the family of concave univalent functions with an opening angle
\pi\alpha
at infinity, where
\alpha\in[1,2]
, if
g
satisfies the following conditions:

(i)

g\in\mathcal{A}
and
g(1)=\infty
.

(ii)

g
maps
\mathbb{D}
conformally onto a set whose complement with respect to
\mathbb{C}
is convex.

(iii)

The opening angle angle of
g(\mathbb{D})
at infinity is less than or equal to
\pi\alpha
,
\alpha\in[1,2]
.

This family of functions is represented by
\widehat{C_{0}}(\alpha)
(see
[
9
,
18
]
). For
g\in\widehat{C_{0}}(\alpha)

(\alpha\in[1,2])
, the boundary of
g(\mathbb{D})
is
contained in a wedge shaped region with opening angle
\pi\alpha
but not in any bigger opening angle. Further, the closed set
\mathbb{C}\setminus g(\mathbb{D})
is convex and
unbounded. The family of concave univalent normalized functions with opening angle
\pi\alpha
,
\alpha\in[1,2]
, at infinity is denoted by
C_{0}(\alpha):=\widehat{C_{0}}(\alpha)\cap\mathcal{S}
. In the case where
\alpha=1
, the image domain is reduced to a convex half plane. It can be observed that concave univalent functions are related to convex functions and that every
g\in\widehat{C_{0}}(1)
is the convex function. For further insight into the topic of concave functions, we refer to
[
13
,
14
,
15
,
22
,
17
]
.

In 2018, Bhowmik and Das
[
18
]
established the following result on the Bohr inequality for the family
\widehat{C_{0}}(\alpha)
.

Theorem H
.

[
18
]

Let
f\in\widehat{C_{0}}(\alpha)
,
\alpha\in[1,2]
and
g\in S(f)
, with
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
and
g(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}b_{n}z^{n}
in
\mathbb{D}
.
Then, the inequality

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|b_{n}|r^{n}\leq d\left(f(0),\partial f(\mathbb{D})\right)\quad\text{holds for}\quad|z|=r\leq r_{0}=(2^{1/\alpha}-1)(2^{1/\alpha}+1).

This result is sharp.

In 2023, Allu and Arora
[
9
]
established the following Bohr-Rogosinski inequality for the family
\widehat{C_{0}}(\alpha)
.

Theorem I
.

[
9
]

Let
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
and
g(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}b_{n}z^{n}
be two analytic functions in
\mathbb{D}
such that
f\in\widehat{C_{0}}(\alpha)
,
\alpha\in[1,2]
and
g\in S(f)
.
Then, for each
N\in\mathbb{N}
, the inequality

\displaystyle|g(z)|+\sum_{n=N}^{\infty}|b_{n}|r^{n}\leq|f(0)|+d\left(f(0),\partial f(\mathbb{D})\right)\quad\text{holds for}\quad|z|=r<\min\{r_{\alpha,1}^{N},1/3\},

where
r_{\alpha,1}^{N}
is the positive of the equation

\displaystyle F_{\alpha,1}^{N}(x)=\sum_{n=N}^{\infty}A_{n}x^{n}+f_{\alpha}(x)-\frac{1}{2\alpha}=0

in
(0,1)
and
f_{\alpha}(x)=(1/(2\alpha))\left(((1+x)/(1-x))^{\alpha}-1\right)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}A_{n}x^{n}
. If
r_{\alpha,1}^{N}\leq 1/3
, then the radius
r_{\alpha,1}^{N}
is the best possible.

In light of the aforementioned results, the following questions naturally arise with regard to this study.

Question 1.1
.

Can we establish the sharp improved Bohr inequality for harmonic mappings of Theorems D and E by using the non-negative quantity
S_{r}(h)
?

Question 1.2
.

Can we establish the sharp improved Bohr inequality of Theorems D and E by using the concept of replacing the initial coefficients with the absolute values of the analytic function and its derivative in the majorant series?

Question 1.3
.

Can we establish the refined Bohr inequality for harmonic mappings of Theorems F and G?

Question 1.4
.

Is it possible to find the sharp Bohr radius for harmonic mappings of Theorem H, where the analytic component is subordinate to a function
\phi\in\widehat{C_{0}}(\alpha)
?

Question 1.5
.

Can we establish the sharp Bohr-Rogosinski inequality for harmonic mappings of Theorem I?

The affirmative answers to Questions
1.1
to
1.5
are the primary purpose of this paper.

The organization of the remaining part of the paper is as follows: In Section 2, we present some necessary lemmas and in Section 3, we establish two results on the refined versions of Bohr
inequalities. In Section 4, we establish some sharp results on the improved Bohr inequalities. In Section 5, we obtain the sharp Bohr radius and Bohr-Rogosinski inequality associated with subordination to a concave univalent function.

2.

Lemma 2.1
.

[

Suppose

\displaystyle|b_{n}|\leq n|g^{\prime}(0)|\leq 4n\;d\left(g(0),\partial g(\mathbb{D})\right)\quad\text{for}\quad n\geq 1.

\mathbb{D}
and
h\prec\phi
, where
\phi\in\widehat{C_{0}}(\alpha)
and
\alpha\in[1,2]
. Then

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(|a_{n}|+|b_{n}|\right)r^{n}\leq d\left(\phi(0),\partial\phi(\mathbb{D})\right)\quad\text{for}\quad r\leq r_{0},

where
r_{0}\in(0,1/3)
is the unique root of the equation

\displaystyle\left(\frac{2K}{K+1}\right)\left(\left(\frac{1+r}{1-r}\right)^{\alpha}-1\right)-1=0.

The number
r_{0}
is sharp.

Proof.

As
h\prec\phi
and
\phi\in\widehat{C_{0}}(\alpha)
,
\alpha\in[1,2]
with
\phi(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}c_{n}z^{n}
, thus, in view of Lemmas
2.4
and
2.5
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_{n}|r^{n}\leq\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|c_{n}|r^{n}\leq 2\alpha\;d\left(\phi(0),\partial\phi(\mathbb{D})\right)\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}A_{n}r^{n}\quad\text{for}\quad|z|=r\leq 1/3.

(5.1)

Using similar arguments as in the proof of Theorem
3.1
, and in view of Lemma
2.6
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|b_{n}|r^{n}\leq k\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_{n}|r^{n}\leq 2k\alpha\;d\left(\phi(0),\partial\phi(\mathbb{D})\right)\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}A_{n}r^{n}\quad\text{for}\quad|z|=r\leq 1/3.

(5.2)

Therefore,

\displaystyle S_{3}(r):=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(|a_{n}|+|b_{n}|\right)r^{n}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\alpha(k+1)\;d\left(\phi(0),\partial\phi(\mathbb{D})\right)\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}A_{n}r^{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(k+1)\left(\left(\frac{1+r}{1-r}\right)^{\alpha}-1\right)d\left(\phi(0),\partial\phi(\mathbb{D})\right).

Let

\displaystyle G_{7}(r)=(k+1)\left(\left(\frac{1+r}{1-r}\right)^{\alpha}-1\right)-1,\quad\text{where}\quad k=(K-1)/(K+1).

Then,

\displaystyle G_{7}^{\prime}(r)=\frac{2\alpha(k+1)(1+r)^{\alpha}}{(1-r^{2})(1-r)^{\alpha}}>0,

which shows that
G_{7}(r)
is a strictly increasing function of
r\in[0,1)
. Moreover,
G_{7}(0)=-1<0
and
\lim_{r\to 1/3}G_{7}(r)=(2^{r}-2)+(2^{r}-1)k>0
. Thus, the equation

G_{7}(r)=0
has a unique positive root
r_{0}\in(0,1/3)
.
Hence,
S_{3}(r)\leq d\left(\phi(0),\partial\phi(\mathbb{D})\right)
for
r\leq r_{0}
, where
r_{0}
is the positive root of the equation
G_{7}(r)=0
.

To prove the sharpness of the result, we consider the function
f_{5}(z)=h_{5}(z)+\overline{g_{5}(z)}
in
\mathbb{D}
such that

\displaystyle\phi(z)=h_{5}(z)=\frac{1}{2\alpha}\left(\left(\frac{1+z}{1-z}\right)^{\alpha}-1\right)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}A_{n}z^{n}\quad\text{and}\quad g_{5}(z)=k\lambda\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}A_{n}z^{n},

where
|\lambda|=1
and
k=(K-1)/(K+1)
. It is evident that
d\left(\phi(0),\partial\phi(\mathbb{D})\right)=1/(2\alpha)
.
Thus,

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(|A_{n}|+|k\lambda A_{n}|\right)r^{n}=(1+k)\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}A_{n}r^{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(1+k)}{2\alpha}\left(\left(\frac{1+r}{1-r}\right)^{\alpha}-1\right)

\displaystyle>

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\alpha}=d\left(\phi(0),\partial\phi(\mathbb{D})\right)\quad\text{for}\quad r>r_{0},

where
r_{0}
is the positive root of the equation
G_{7}(r)=0
.
This shows that
r_{0}
is the best possible. This completes the proof.
∎

In the following, we obtain the Bohr-Rogosinski inequality for harmonic mappings in which the analytic part is subordinated to a function belonging to the family
\widehat{C_{0}}(\alpha)
,
\alpha\in[1,2]
.

Theorem 5.2
.

Suppose that
f(z)=h(z)+\overline{g(z)}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}+\overline{\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}b_{n}z^{n}}
is a sense-preserving
K
-quasiconformal harmonic mapping in
\mathbb{D}
and
h\prec\phi
, where
\phi\in\widehat{C_{0}}(\alpha)
and
\alpha\in[1,2]
. Then

\displaystyle|h(z)|+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(|a_{n}|+|b_{n}|\right)r^{n}\leq|\phi(0)|+d\left(\phi(0),\partial\phi(\mathbb{D})\right)\quad\text{for}\quad r\leq r_{0},

where
r_{0}\in(0,1/3)
is the unique root of the equation

\displaystyle\left(\frac{3K+1}{K+1}\right)\left(\left(\frac{1+r}{1-r}\right)^{\alpha}-1\right)-1=0.

The number
r_{0}
is sharp.

Proof.

Using similar arguments as in the proof of Theorem
5.1
and in view of Lemmas
2.4
,
2.5
and
2.6
, we have the inequalities (
5.1
) and (
5.2
). Since
h\prec\phi
, where
\phi\in\widehat{C_{0}}(\alpha)
and
\alpha\in[1,2]
, thus

\displaystyle|h(z)|\leq|h(0)|+|h(z)-h(0)|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\phi(0)|+\left|\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|\phi(0)|+2\alpha\;d\left(\phi(0),\partial\phi(\mathbb{D})\right)\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}A_{n}r^{n}

for
|z|=r\leq 1/3
. Therefore,

\displaystyle S_{4}(r):

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|h(z)|+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(|a_{n}|+|b_{n}|\right)r^{n}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|\phi(0)|+2\alpha(k+2)\;d\left(\phi(0),\partial\phi(\mathbb{D})\right)\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}A_{n}r^{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\phi(0)|+(k+2)\left(\left(\frac{1+r}{1-r}\right)^{\alpha}-1\right)d\left(\phi(0),\partial\phi(\mathbb{D})\right).

Let

\displaystyle G_{8}(r)=(k+2)\left(\left(\frac{1+r}{1-r}\right)^{\alpha}-1\right)-1,\quad\text{where}\quad k=(K-1)/(K+1).

Then,

\displaystyle G_{8}^{\prime}(r)=\frac{2\alpha(k+2)(1+r)^{\alpha}}{(1-r^{2})(1-r)^{\alpha}}>0,

which shows that
G_{8}(r)
is a strictly increasing function of
r\in[0,1)

G_{8}(0)=-1<0
and
\lim_{r\to 1/3}G_{8}(r)=(2^{1+r}-3)+(2^{r}-1)k>0
. Thus, the equation
G_{8}(r)=0
has a unique positive root
r_{0}\in(0,1/3)
.
Hence,
S_{4}(r)\leq d\left(\phi(0),\partial\phi(\mathbb{D})\right)
for
r\leq r_{0}
, where
r_{0}

Constructing Thompson representatives via pointed links
Susanna Terron

Abstract
. We extend Jones’ construction to obtain a surjective map from the Brown–Thompson group F 3 F_{3} to the set of pointed links up to pointed isotopy. We then introduce an operation on F 3 F_{3} , and use it to define a new monoid ( F 3 , ⋄ ) (F_{3},\diamond) , called the central monoid. Using the extended version of Jones’ construction, we obtain a surjective monoid homomorphism from the central monoid to the monoid of pointed links with connected sum. This allows us to introduce a standard form for connected sum representatives in F 3 F_{3} , and we extend this construction to a certain family of links by defining disjoint union and linking moves on F 3 F_{3} .

1.
Introduction

Inspired by questions arising from conformal field theory, Vaughan Jones introduced a construction associating links to elements of Thompson’s group
F

[
8
]
and its generalisation
F_{3}

[
9
]
. In analogy to the theory relating braid groups and knot theory, this construction gives a map
F\to\mathsf{Links}
similar to the closure map
\bigsqcup_{n}B_{n}\to\mathsf{Links}
. In the case of braids, surjectivity of the map is guaranteed by the Alexander Theorem, and a set of moves sufficient to establish when two braids give the same link is described by the Markov Theorem.

In his paper Jones proved an Alexander type theorem showing that all links can be obtained from an element of
F

[
8
, Theorem 5.3.1]
, proving that the map above is surjective. The question of when two group elements result in the same link is still unanswered. Maintaining the parallel with braid groups, answering this question would result in a Markov type theorem for either
F
or
F_{3}
. We call these elements Thompson representatives of the link.

Various authors have explored the connection between Thompson’s group and knot theory, see for example
[

[

We first extend Jones’ construction to a map

with image in the set of pointed links up to pointed isotopy,

Theorem

The map

3.
Extension to pointed link

Denote by
\mathsf{Links}_{*}

\mathsf{Links}_{*}

Each element of
\mathsf{Links}_{*}

(L,C)

Definition 4.0
.

We call the monoid

the central monoid.

Observe that

For all

Theorem 5.0

The map
\mathcal{K}
gives a surjective homomorphism of monoids from the central monoid to knots with connected sum

(F_{3},\diamond)\twoheadrightarrow(\mathsf{Knots},\#).

For

their image under
\mathcal{L}
could result in links with more than one component, for which the connected sum is not well-defined. Consider the monoid of pointed links

Corollary 5.0
.

For all

we have

\mathcal{L}_{*}(f\diamond g)=\mathcal{L}_{*}(f)\#_{*}\mathcal{L}_{*}(g).

In particular

\mathcal{L}_{*}(f\diamond g)=\mathcal{L}_{*}(g\diamond f).

Remark 5.0

In order to consider connected sum we had to orient the central component. \mathcal{L}_{*}

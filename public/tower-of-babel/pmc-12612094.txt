Quantitative assessment of benign and malignant bone tumours using synthetic magnetic resonance imaging and diffusion measures
Authors: Haitian Liu, Yinghua Zhang, Xiaohui Ma, Lei Gao, Bokang Wang, Tiantian Xu, Enpeng Gao, Jian Zhao, Zuzhuo Zhang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractOur study aimed to evaluate the feasibility of quantitative magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) for characterising benign and malignant bone tumours. We analysed forty-seven patients with bone tumours who underwent Synthetic MRI with magnetic resonance image complication (MAGIC) and intravoxel incoherent motion (IVIM) sequence imaging performed at 3 T MRI. Based on different pathological results, the patients were divided into benign and malignant bone tumour groups. Two radiologists performed statistical analysis to analyse whether the parameters T1 relaxation time (T1), T2 relaxation time (T2), proton density relaxation time from MAGIC and diffusion-coefficient (D), perfusion-coefficient (D*), perfusion fraction (f) from IVIM to differentiate malignant and benign bone tumours effectively. T1 and D for malignant tumours were significantly lower than benign bone tumours. The combined T1 and D parameters had the best performance for differentiating malignant from benign bone tumours with receiver operating characteristic curves of 0.860. The MAGIC and IVIM techniques can provide quantitative parameters for identifying benign and malignant bone tumours, with the parameters T1 and D demonstrating high diagnostic efficiency. This approach may serve as an effective strategy for improving bone tumour differentiation.Keywords: Benign bone tumour, Malignant bone tumour, Quantitative magnetic resonance imaging, Synthetic magnetic resonance imaging, Diffusion weighted imagingSubject terms: Bone cancer, Medical research

Introduction

Primary bone tumours are common in the clinic and can be classified into benign and malignant bone tumours. In recent years, the incidence of bone tumours among young and middle-aged people has gradually increased1. Benign bone tumours are easy to treat and generally have a favorable prognosis, whereas malignant bone tumours usually result in poor outcomes. Different types of malignant bone tumours require radiotherapy and chemotherapy, and the extent of surgical resection varies significantly. Therefore, the differential diagnosis of benign and malignant bone tumours and early qualitative diagnosis are the key steps in the process of treatment. Pathological examination is the gold standard for the diagnosis of bone tumours. However, decalcification requires a significant amount of time in the process of preparing pathological sections for bone tumours. Additionally, pathological biopsies can be affected by various factors, such as sampling site and the quantity of tissue taken. Meanwhile, the sampling process is an invasive examination, which brings difficulties to pathological diagnosis and limits its clinical application2. Bone tumours exhibit various complex imaging features, which we cannot distinguish benign from malignant bone tumours simply by observing the lesion shape, signal intensity, density and relationship with surrounding tissues, and there is a lack of sufficient ability to distinguish the changes in subtle structure. Therefore, an objective and quantitative method is desirable for diagnosing the benign and malignant bone tumours, as it would further elucidate the pathophysiological changes of the disease and provide a more scientific basis for its clinical management.

As documented in the literature, IVIM diffusion-weighted(DW) imaging3 has drawn attention. Using the IVIM model with sufficiently low multiple b-values(less than 200 s/mm2), microcirculation or perfusion-related effects can be separated from pure tissue diffusion (D). Subsequently, perfusion characteristics, including the pseudo diffusion coefficient (D*) and the perfusion fraction (f) can be derived4. The parameter D value reflects diffusion more, which is greatly influenced by the heterogeneity of tumour cells and the nuclear cytoplasm ratio. The D * and f values reflect information related to blood perfusion, which are greatly affected by the distribution of neovascularisation, vascular geometry, blood flow velocity, and blood volume in the tumour. However, previous studies have reported variable sensitivity and specificity when distinguishing malignant from benign bone lesions5–7.

Compared to benign tumours, malignant tumours exhibit higher cell density. Additionally, malignant tumours are larger in size, with the cytoplasm and interstitial tissue being infiltrated by lymphocytes and plasma cells, which reduces extracellular space and free water in the tissue. The microstructural difference at the cellular level leads to changes in tissue relaxation times T1, T2, as well as PD value8,9. Recently proposed synthetic MRI using the MAGIC with a multi-echo and multi-delay acquisition method to quantify the intrinsic magnetic properties simultaneously and investigate all measures within a practically acceptable scan time10. It can not only provide the basic structural information of tissues and organs, but also deliver rich biological and pathophysiological information during the occurrence and development of lesions. Recently, it has been demonstrated that MAGIC help distinguish benign and malignant bone tumours11. At present, the MAGIC sequence has shown significant potential in the preliminary diagnosis of craniocerebral multiple sclerosis, benign and malignant breast, prostate cancer and knee osteoarthritis12–14. However, only a few studies have focused on bone lesions. In this experiment, MAGIC sequence was used to analyse the bone tumours with quantitative T1, T2 and PD maps. And we hypothesised that synthetic MRI could be used to identify benign and malignant bone tumours.

Therefore, this study aimed to investigate the value of parameters derived from MAGIC quantitative maps and IVIM sequence in differentiating benign and malignant bone tumours, aiming to find an objective and quantitative alternative to pathological biopsies.

Materials and methods

Patients

The protocol of this study was discussed, approved and recorded by the ethics committee of the Third Hospital of Hebei Medical University. The ethics approval number is W2024-066-1. The institutional and licensing committee approved the experiments, including any relevant details and all experiments were performed following the relevant guidelines and regulations. A total of 135 patients with suspicious bone tumours (detected by X-ray or ultrasound) who underwent synthetic MAGIC and IVIM examinations were enrolled from January 2024 to November 2024 in the Department of Bone Oncology of our hospital. All patients enrolled in the study signed the Informed Consent Form before undergoing MRI. All the lesions were confirmed by biopsy or pathology.

Inclusion and exclusion criteria for bone tumours

The inclusion criteria were as follows: (1) Bone tumours were diagnosed by imaging examination and surgical pathological examination; (2) Imaging examination data were complete; (3) Patients without antitumour treatment, radiotherapy and chemotherapy and puncture biopsy before operation.

Exclusion criteria were as follows: (1) Contraindications of MRI examination; (2) MRI examination data are incomplete and difficult to analyse because of poor image quality; (3) Examination was not confirmed by pathology within two weeks; (4) Nonsolid tumours (such as aneurysmal bone cysts and haemangiomas) and metastatic tumour; (5) Patients with bone metabolic diseases.

MRI protocol

All scans were performed on a 3.0 T magnetic resonance scanner (Architect, General Electric Medical System, Milwaukee, WI, USA) with 16-channel flex coils or phased array coils for the body. All participants underwent MAGIG and IVIM examination with routine T1-weighted imaging (T1WI), T2-weighted imaging (T2WI) and T2WI fat suppression scans. The scanning range covered the bone tumour lesions. Detailed scan parameters of MAGIC, IVIM and routine sequences are summarised in Table 1.

Image analysis and data measurement

MAGIC images are processed using the MAGIC viewer post-processing software, which can obtain various contrast images such as T1WI, T2WI, Proton Density Weighted Imaging (PDWI), Fluid Attenuated Inversion Recovery (FLAIR), Short TI inversion Recovery (STIR), and Dual Inversion Recovery (DIR). At the same time, quantitative maps such as T1map, T2map, and PDmap were also obtained (Fig. 1). The maximum diameter layer of the lesion was selected to delineate the region of interest, and the average values of T1, T2, and PD were automatically calculated by the MAGIC viewer. The IVIM sequence was reconstructed by double exponential model fitting by Advantage Windows Workstation (GE Healthcare, AW 4.6) MADC software to obtain the functional maps of D, D*, and f (Fig. 2), and the same ROI covering all three functional maps. The area of the ROI is approximately 50–100 mm2. Analyse their signal characteristics by taking the pathological tissues of bone tumours as research objects. The ROI was drawn at the maximum level of the axial lesion, to avoid necrosis, liquefaction, calcification, ossification, and large blood vessel. Three ROIs in the solid tumour components were placed manually, and the average was calculated. Two radiologists with ten and seven years of experience in radiology (Reader A and Reader B) measured the data of the ROI set as required for the subjects respectively, without knowing the pathological conditions.

Statistical analysis

Data were analysed using SPSS 21.0 statistical software (SPSS, Inc., Chicago, IL, USA). The quantitative MRI parameters were represented by using the mean ± standard deviation. Differences in the parameters between benign and malignant bone tumours were compared with an independent student’s t-test (normally distributed data) or a Wilcoxon test (non-normally distributed data). Quantitative parameters with significant differences between benign and malignant groups were further combined for multiparametric analysis with logistic regression. Multiparameter analysis was analysed by MedCalc software (version 13.0), the receiver operating characteristic (ROC) area under the curve (AUC), 95% confidence interval (CI), sensitivity (Sen), specificity (Spe), positive predictive value (PPV) and negative predictive value (NPV) were calculated at maximal Youden’s index. The interobserver consistencies for all quantitative parameters between the two radiologists were evaluated with the intraclass correlation coefficient (ICC) analysis. The ICC values of ≥ 0.75, 0.40–0.75, and < 0.4 were regarded to indicate strong, moderate and weak reliability, respectively; The significance level of α = 0.05 was tested, and p < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Data consistency analysis

The data consistency analysis showed that the intraclass correlation coefficient of T1 was 98.9%, T2 was 99.1% and PD was 97% (ICC > 0.75); the data consistency analysis showed that the intraclass correlation coefficient of D was 99.2%, D* was 90.1% and f was 96.8% (ICC > 0.75).

Clinical and pathological results

Of the total 135 patients, 88 patients were excluded because of preoperative treatment or no pathological finding (n = 42), and poor image quality caused by motion artefacts (n = 46). Thus, finally, 47 patients (22 with malignant lesions, 25 with benign lesions) satisfied the inclusion criteria (Fig. 3) and were enrolled in the study.

The flowchart of patient enrolment.

The age range of the patients was 12 ~ 76 years, with an average age of 37 years. Based on different pathological results, the patients were divided into benign and malignant bone tumour groups. There were 25 patients in the benign bone tumours group: 2 cases of fibrous dysplasia of the bone; 4 cases of giant cell tumour of the bone; 4 cases of non-ossifying fibroma; 1 case of fat sclerosing fibromyxoid tumour; 1case of osteoblastoma; 2 cases of endogenous chondroma; 3 case of atypical cartilage tumour; and 8 cases of osteochondroma.

There were 22 patients in the malignant bone tumour group, including 4 cases of Ewing’s sarcoma, 2 cases of mesenchymal malignant tumours, 11 cases of osteosarcoma, 4 cases of chondrosarcoma grade II, and one case of dedifferentiated chondrosarcoma.

The ROC curve analysis results show that the combination of T1 and D has high diagnostic value in differentiating between benign and malignant bone tumours. Discussion

This study evaluated differences in the quantitative parameters of MAGIC sequences (T1, T2, PD) and IVIM sequences (D, D*, f) between benign and malignant bone tumours. The results showed significant statistical differences in the parameters of T1 and D between benign and malignant bone tumours. Combining T1 and D has a higher diagnostic accuracy to distinguish benign from malignant bone tumours.

The MAGIC sequences are two-dimensional fast spin-echo (2D FSE) multidynamic, multiecho sequences that use an interleaved slice-selective 120° saturation pulse combined with multiecho acquisition, and images were obtained with different combinations of TE and saturation delay time. Longitudinal relaxation time is an inherent characteristic of tissue15, and the quantitativeT1 mapping can be used to noninvasively visualise histopathological changes. The research results showed that the T1 of the benign bone tumour group (1644.9 ± 521.3 ms) was significantly higher than that of the malignant bone tumour group (1134.9 ± 299.8 ms).This finding is consistent with the research results of Xie C et al.13, who demonstrated that MAGIC quantitative values can effectively differentiate and diagnose nasopharyngeal carcinoma from benign nasopharyngeal hyperplasia. Among them, the T1, T2, and PD values of nasopharyngeal carcinoma are significantly lower than those of benign nasopharyngeal hyperplasia. Kayal B et al.16 has concluded that osteosarcoma has a lower T1 value (830.9 ± 193.9 ms) compared to our study. This difference may be explained by the fact that all included tumours in Kayal B’s study were osteosarcomas. Kayal B’s study demonstrated that T1 values and their histogram analysis could characterize the chemotherapeutic changes in osteosarcoma. However, different from Meng T et al.17‘s study, who investigated the MAGIC quantitative maps in diagnosing breast cancer and found that the T1 value of benign breast lesions was 1242.9 ± 139.3 ms and that of malignant breast lesions was 1611.6 ± 215.9 ms. Specifically, the T1 value of malignant breast lesions was significantly higher than that of benign breast lesions. The reason for the difference between our study and Meng et al. may be because the site studied by Meng et al. is the mammary gland. In postprocessing, both quantitative T1 and T2 are influenced by fat molecules, resulting in differences in T1. Moreover, it may be related to the magnetic field environment and differences in sequence selection. Arita Y et al.11 discovered that in the measurement of prostate tumours using synthetic MRI, the relaxation time of various tissues is directly proportional to their free water content. Regarding the significant decrease in T1 of malignant bone tumours, we speculated the possible reason as follows: Malignant bone tumours grow rapidly, and the cell density of malignant tissue is denser than that of benign bone tumour tissue. At the same time, tumour tissue is often accompanied by cell necrosis, and macromolecules are released into the space around the cells, further reducing the extracellular space and free water content. The dense arrangement of cancer cells in malignant bone tumours, increased nuclear plasma ratio, and infiltration of inflammatory cells result in a decrease in the free water content of the extracellular space18. As a result, the T1 of malignant tumour tissue is lower than that of benign bone tumour tissue, the measurement of relaxation time helps to determine the invasiveness of the tumour. The T1 reflects the slow frequency interaction between water and extracellular matrix molecules in the tissue. It is sensitive to variations in proteoglycan levels within the tissue and independent of the signal intensity of the reference tissue. The density of bone tumour cells is quantified through T1, reflecting the intrinsic characteristics and changes in the water content of the bone tumour parenchyma. Ni H et al.19 found more hypoxic haemoglobin in malignant bone tumour. At the same time, the paramagnetism of deoxyhemoglobin leads to a decrease in T1 values, which may be another explanation for the reduction of the T1.

The results of our study showed that the T2 of benign bone tumours (108.6 ± 47.3 ms) is slightly higher than that of malignant bone tumours (91.6 ± 38.0 ms), but there was no significant statistical difference. Many factors affect the T2 of bone tumours, including water content, fibrous tissue content, tumour cell density, and so on. The possible reasons for the decrease in T2 in malignant bone tumours may be as follows: in malignant bone tumours, the cell density is higher than that of benign lesions, tumour tissue is often accompanied by cell necrosis20, macromolecular substances are released into the surrounding space of cells, causing a reduction in extracellular space, and the decrease in free water leads to a shortened lateral relaxation time. In benign lesions, the fibre tissue content causes interstitial oedema and prolongs the lateral relaxation time21. The results of our study showed that the PD value of the benign bone tumour group was slightly higher than that of the malignant bone tumour group, and there was no significant statistical difference. Theoretically, there may also be differences in PD values between benign and malignant lesions, but the results do not support this. It is speculated that this is related to differences in the research sites, disease types, sample sizes, and ROI sets. Further exploration is still needed.

Bone tumours have a wide range and originate from various cell types. In our study’s analysis of benign bone tumours, endogenous chondroma has the highest T1 value 2579.6 ms. Non-ossifying fibroma has the lowest T1 value 1459.1 ms. The T2 value of endogenous chondroma (100.3 ms) is higher than that of other benign tumours. This may be caused by more chondrocytes in endogenous chondroma. The hydrophilic properties of their proteoglycans and the avascular matrix environment in the tumours prolong the relaxation time22. The low T1 value in non-ossifying fibroma may have been due to the high density of fibres and low water content in both the extracellular and intracellular space23. For malignant tumours, our study revealed that osteosarcomas had a lower T1 value (1030.9 ± 175.4 ms) compared to chondrosarcomas (1338.7 ± 27.4 ms). This may be related to the fact that chondrosarcoma is composed of tumour-derived chondrocytes with varying degrees of differentiation and cartilaginous matrix. The abundant myxoid matrix for low-grade tumours leads to high water content, while high-grade tumours may show decreased water content due to dense cell proliferation and collagen deposition24. Thus, future studies should focus on comprehensive analyses of MRI quantitative parameters across a large cohort of tumours derived from distinct cellular origins.

Conventional DWI is affected by microcirculation perfusion and cannot reflect the true information of water molecule diffusion restriction in tumours. IVIM-DWI is an imaging method that describes the microscopic motion of voxels based on a bi-exponential model, using multiple b-values for image acquisition of DWI sequences. The D, D*, and f parameters were obtained, where D is the actual diffusion coefficient, reflecting the diffusion state of relatively real water molecules in the tissue. IVIM can conduct a more accurate quantitative analysis of the tissue ground diffusion coefficient values and microcirculation perfusion information, which can identify the benign and malignant myoskeletal tumours to some extent. This study found that the D value of benign bone tumours was significantly higher than that of malignant bone tumours, which was similar to the research results of Lim et al., indicating that with the increase in the cellular structure and cell density of malignant tumours, the diffusion between tissues was reduced. The D value in our study is slightly lower than that in Study Baidya K25, which may be related to the fact that all cases included were osteosarcoma patients who received neoadjuvant chemotherapy. Simultaneously, the diversity of malignant bone tumours in our study may also contribute to differences in the data. Similar to a prior study26 that evaluated D* and f values in differentiating benign and malignant bone tumours. Our study also found slightly elevated D* and f parameters in malignant bone tumours compared to benign lesions, despite ultilising a different ROI measurement technique. However, these differences lack statistical significance. A large number of immature neovascularization is an essential feature of malignant tumours. Scholars have used micro vessel density (MVD) as an indicator to conduct research and analysis on the angiogenesis of various solid tumours, and MVD is the most sensitive and objective indicator for evaluating tumour angiogenesis and the gold standard of tumour angiogenesis. Some studies27,28 found that the D* and f value, which reflect perfusion ability, have a moderate correlation with MVD and are positively correlated with the pathological grading of malignant tumours. With the increase of malignant degree, the D* and f increase. However, our study did not find any difference in these two parameters between benign and malignant tumours. The reason for this may be the heterogeneous blood vessels of malignant bone tumours and the similar blood volume of benign and malignant bone tumours within unit volume, resulting in no statistical difference between the D *and f in the microcirculation. In our study, chondrosarcoma had the highest D value 1.55 × 10−3 mm2/s, and Ewing’s sarcoma had the lowest D value 0.61 × 10−3 mm2/s. The D value of chondrosarcoma was higher than that of malignant tumours, which may be related to varying degrees of cellularity within a cartilaginous stroma. Ewing’s sarcoma is a small round cell malignant tumour, belonging to a group of undifferentiated and aggressive embryonic tumours. The lower D value may be caused by high cell density and a high nuclear-to-cytoplasmic ratio29. Further subgroup analysis will be conducted.

Benign bone tumours often exhibit expansive growth with clear boundaries, However, such as haemangiomas exhibit infiltrative growth without capsule formation and have unclear boundaries. Therefore, whether the boundaries are clear or not, there is significant overlap in the imaging of benign and malignant bone tumours. Therefore, we cannot be determined solely based on imaging findings. Some malignant bone tumours exhibit infiltrative growth with blurry boundaries on MR imaging, but some may also have pseudocapsule formation with clear boundaries30. Obtaining certain pathological tissues through puncture biopsy before treatment for qualitative and aetiological diagnosis is of great significance for selecting treatment plans and evaluating efficacy. However, the invasive nature of puncture biopsy brings great pain to patients, and may pose a risk of cancer cell implantation and metastasis. This study found that the T1 measured by quantitative synthesis of MAGIC sequences and D measured by IVIM-DWI effectively distinguish benign and malignant bone tumours, providing a more comprehensive functional characterisation of bone tumour lesions. We believe that their combination may be beneficial in improving the diagnostic performance. Compared with the use of any parameter alone, multivariate analysis shows improved sensitivity and specificity with AUC reaching as high as 0.860. The physiological and pathological changes indicated by the quantitative T1 of synthetic MRI and D of the IVIM may provide effective information for distinguishing tumour boundaries and displaying the extent of tumour infiltration, which may be the direction of further research.

This study has some limitations: (1) The longitudinal relaxation time T1 value is one of the inherent properties of tissues31. In magnetic field environments with different field strengths, the same organisation has different T1 values, which may be affected by the magnetic field environment. Our study used the same scanning equipment and sequence parameters throughout the process with adopted automatic shimming. Among the bone tumours included in this study, there was no obvious interference from fat components. This study obtained MRI quantitative data under a strict standard process. (2) The contrast of synthetic MRI is relatively high, however, the noise level of T1WI and T2WI images is also high, which may affect the postprocessing measurement results. Nevertheless, Blystad et al.32 found that synthetic MRI achieved diagnostic accuracy similar to traditional sequences in brain imaging; (3) The measurement plane was aligned with the level of the lesion’s largest diameter. The whole lesion is not outlined, which poses a risk of missing details of the lesion. Moreover, the measurement of the ROI is manually delineated, which may introduces measurement errors. There is no standard method for placing a ROI, measurement of the quantitative values is subject to underestimation of tumour cellularity33. These errors may have a slight impact on the calculation of quantitative parameters34. Arslan Set al.26 draw volumetric measurements may be useful and less susceptible to interobserver variability compared to ROI measurement techniques. In our study, three ROIs were measured manually by two independent radiologists. The ROI was performed using the tumour maximum layer with a multi-point sampling method and good interobserver consistency, which rapidly evaluated the spatial heterogeneity of tumours to avoid sampling bias. (4) The sample size is limited, and the differences in the origin of bone tumours in different parts are ignored. In our study, the essence of benign and malignant grouping is based on the clinical decision-making needs of biological behaviour, and the grouping model meets the actual clinical needs, including the selection of surgical plans. In the future, the sample size will be expanded for subgroup analysis (such as distinguishing chondrosarcoma from osteosarcoma), and the universality of the current technology for different tumour subtypes will be verified.

Conclusion

The combination of quantitative T1 and D reveals new information that can effectively distinguish between benign and malignant bone tumours, demonstrating high diagnostic efficiency. Combining the two quantitative parameters further improved their diagnostic performance. The application of synthetic MRI and IVIM-DWI enables us to obtain quantitative values that cannot be obtained from conventional comparative images, improving the diagnostic value of benign and malignant bone tumours and providing a powerful quantitative tool for disease diagnosis and efficacy evaluation.

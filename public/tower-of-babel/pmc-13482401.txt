Plasma exosomal microRNAs as early predictors of subclinical acute kidney injury in septic patients undergoing major non-cardiac surgery: a prospective observational cohort study
Authors: Ali Mohammadi, Zhale Bakhtiyari, Negar Mokabber, Vahid Fattahi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Early identification of acute kidney injury (AKI), including subclinical AKI, remains a critical challenge in septic patients undergoing major non-cardiac surgery. Exosomal microRNAs (miRNAs) represent emerging biomarkers capable of reflecting early renal cellular stress before detectable changes in serum creatinine occur. This prospective observational cohort study enrolled 120 adult septic patients undergoing major non-cardiac surgery (> 2 h) at Imam Sajjad Hospital, Tabriz, between early 2025 and August 2025 (Ethics Approval ID: IR.IAU.TABRIZ.REC.1404.179). Peripheral blood was obtained at three perioperative time points (preoperative, intraoperative, and 24 h postoperative) for exosome isolation and miRNA quantification. Clinical AKI was defined according to KDIGO criteria. Subclinical AKI was defined as urinary TIMP-2 × IGFBP7 > 0.3 in the absence of any rise in serum creatinine. Diagnostic performance of candidate miRNAs was assessed using receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analysis based on observed patient data. Among the 120 enrolled patients, a proportion developed AKI during the 24-hour postoperative observation period (final numbers provided in the Results section). Patients who developed AKI demonstrated distinct perioperative alterations in specific exosomal miRNAs compared with those who did not develop AKI. Several miRNAs showed measurable discriminatory ability for early AKI detection across different sampling time points, with areas under the ROC curve (AUCs) ranging within clinically relevant thresholds (AUC values reported in the Results and Tables following recalculation using observed data with 95% confidence intervals). These findings suggest that perioperative plasma exosomal miRNAs may serve as promising early indicators of acute kidney injury in septic patients undergoing major surgery. Their potential role in identifying subclinical AKI warrants further investigation in studies specifically powered for this endpoint.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-026-55837-7.

Introduction

Acute kidney injury (AKI) represents one of the most frequent and clinically significant complications among septic patients undergoing major surgery. Despite advances in perioperative monitoring and hemodynamic optimization, early detection of renal injury remains challenging, as serum creatinine— the standard clinical marker—typically rises only after substantial loss of renal function1,2. This diagnostic delay limits opportunities for timely intervention and contributes to elevated morbidity and mortality in the perioperative setting3.

In recent years, attention has shifted toward identifying molecular biomarkers capable of detecting renal cellular stress prior to overt functional decline. Exosomal microRNAs (miRNAs)—small, non-coding RNA molecules encapsulated within extracellular vesicles—have emerged as promising candidates because they reflect injury-specific alterations in tubular epithelial cells and are stably transported in circulation4–6. Exosomes released during renal ischemia, oxidative stress, inflammation, and endothelial dysfunction carry distinctive miRNA signatures that may serve as early indicators of kidney injury7–9. Several candidate miRNAs, including miR-21, miR-150, miR-16, and miR-223, have been implicated in renal ischemia–reperfusion injury, inflammatory signaling, septic organ dysfunction, and apoptosis, suggesting potential roles in perioperative AKI risk stratification10–14.

A major challenge in perioperative nephrology is the identification of subclinical AKI, defined by biochemical evidence of tubular stress in the absence of creatinine elevation. The combination of urinary TIMP-2 × IGFBP7 has emerged as a validated marker of early tubular stress and short-term AKI risk, supported by multiple multicenter trials and incorporated into contemporary perioperative kidney injury research15–17. Distinguishing biochemical renal stress (subclinical AKI) from clinically manifest AKI, as defined by the kidney disease: Improving Global Outcomes (KDIGO) criteria1, is essential for accurate classification of injury phenotypes and for evaluating the predictive performance of novel biomarkers.

Given these gaps, exosomal miRNAs may provide an integrative molecular signature of early renal injury in high-risk septic patients undergoing major non-cardiac surgery—individuals who are particularly susceptible to hemodynamic instability, oxidative stress, mitochondrial dysfunction, and inflammatory activation18–20. However, evidence remains limited regarding dynamic perioperative changes in exosomal miRNAs across sequential surgical time points and their potential role in predicting both subclinical and clinical AKI.

The present prospective observational cohort study was designed to investigate perioperative alterations in plasma exosomal miRNAs at three defined time points—preoperative, intraoperative, and 24 h postoperative—and to evaluate their ability to discriminate subsequent AKI. By integrating a longitudinal sampling strategy with validated clinical and biochemical endpoints, this study aims to clarify the diagnostic utility of circulating exosomal miRNAs as early biomarkers of renal injury in septic surgical patients.

Methods

Study design and setting

This study was conducted as a prospective observational cohort at Imam Sajjad Hospital, Tabriz, from early 2025 to August 2025. The cohort design reflects the temporal structure of data acquisition, which included serial perioperative sampling and subsequent assessment of postoperative clinical outcomes. The study protocol was approved by the Research Ethics Committee of Islamic Azad University–Tabriz Branch (Approval ID: IR.IAU.TABRIZ.REC.1404.179), and written informed consent was obtained from all participants prior to enrollment.

Participants and eligibility criteria

A total of 120 adult septic patients scheduled for major non-cardiac surgery exceeding 2 h were prospectively enrolled. Inclusion criteria comprised age ≥ 18 years, diagnosis of sepsis based on Sepsis-3 criteria, and candidacy for major surgical intervention requiring intraoperative hemodynamic monitoring. Exclusion criteria included pre-existing end-stage renal disease, chronic dialysis, renal transplantation, pregnancy, age below 18 years, and inability to provide informed consent.

Samples exhibiting visible hemolysis, excessive red discoloration, or abnormal absorbance on quality-control inspection were excluded to avoid confounding effects on circulating RNA measurements, consistent with established extracellular vesicle collection standards.

Clinical variables—including demographics, comorbidities, intraoperative hemodynamics, laboratory indices, urine output, and postoperative events—were prospectively recorded. These variables were selected based on their known or hypothesized association with perioperative AKI.

Outcome definitions

Clinical AKI was defined using the KDIGO criteria, based on changes in serum creatinine and/or urine output within 24 h following surgery.

Subclinical AKI was defined as urinary TIMP-2 × IGFBP7 > 0.3 in the absence of serum creatinine elevation, representing biochemical evidence of tubular stress without functional impairment. Subclinical AKI classification was recorded as an exploratory variable.

The primary diagnostic endpoint for all ROC analyses was clinical AKI.

Exosome isolation and characterization

Exosomes were isolated from platelet-free plasma using commercially standardized precipitation and ultracentrifugation protocols in accordance with current extracellular vesicle methodological guidelines. Characterization of vesicles included particle-size distribution analysis (NTA) and representative transmission electron microscopy (TEM) imaging to verify vesicle morphology and membrane integrity. A typical microvesicle population with diameters consistent with exosomal size ranges (30–150 nm) was confirmed.

Representative TEM and NTA images are provided in Supplementary Fig. S1.

Although representative TEM and NTA images are shown for illustrative purposes, exosome identity was primarily confirmed based on size distribution and RNA profiling in accordance with MISEV2018 recommendations. Representative TEM and NTA images provided in Supplementary Fig. S1 are for illustrative purposes only and were not derived from the actual study samples. Western blot validation of common exosomal protein markers (CD63, CD81, or TSG101) was not performed on the study samples.

microRNA extraction and quantification

Total exosomal RNA was extracted using silica-membrane spin-column methodology optimized for small RNA recovery. Candidate microRNAs (miR-21, miR-150, miR-16, miR-223, and others specified in experimental design) were quantified using SYBR-based quantitative real-time PCR. Cycle threshold (Ct) values were normalized using an internal reference miRNA according to the ΔCt method. All reactions were performed in triplicate with appropriate no-template and no-reverse-transcription controls to ensure analytical specificity.

All profiled miRNAs correspond to human miRNA sequences (hsa-miR- annotations). Any non-human or incorrectly annotated sequences identified during validation were excluded from final analyses, in accordance with reviewer guidance.

Statistical analysis

Analyses were conducted in accordance with the cohort design. Continuous variables were examined using appropriate distribution-based methods (parametric or non-parametric as applicable). Categorical variables were compared using χ² or Fisher’s exact test. Group differences in miRNA expression between AKI and non-AKI patients across each perioperative time point were evaluated using independent-samples tests appropriate to distributional characteristics. Diagnostic performance of each miRNA was assessed using receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analysis with calculation of the area under the curve (AUC) and 95% confidence intervals. Optimal thresholds were derived using the Youden index. A two-sided p-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. All statistical analyses were performed using SPSS version 26.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA; https://www.ibm.com/products/spss-statistics). Figures (box plots and ROC curves) were generated using Python (version 3.9) with the Matplotlib and Seaborn libraries.

Results

Baseline characteristics

A total of 120 septic patients undergoing major non-cardiac surgery were included in the analysis, of whom 80 (66.7%) developed AKI within the first 24 h postoperatively and 40 (33.3%) did not. Clinical AKI was diagnosed according to KDIGO criteria. The observed incidence of 66.7% (80/120) is consistent with the high-risk profile of septic patients undergoing major non-cardiac surgery. KDIGO stage distribution (stage 1, 2, and 3) was not stratified in the primary binary (AKI vs. non-AKI) analysis presented here; formal stage-specific miRNA performance was not the primary endpoint of this study. Baseline demographic characteristics were similar between the groups, with no significant difference in age distribution. In contrast, illness severity was substantially higher among patients who developed AKI, as reflected by greater SOFA and APACHE II scores at enrollment.

Inflammatory and hematologic indices also differed between groups. Patients with AKI had higher total leukocyte counts, higher neutrophil counts and proportions, and lower lymphocyte counts and percentages compared with non-AKI patients. Consistent with a greater systemic inflammatory burden, C-reactive protein and procalcitonin levels were markedly elevated in the AKI group. Sex distribution did not differ significantly between groups. Detailed baseline clinical and laboratory characteristics are summarized in Table 1.

Preoperative exosomal miRNA expression

At the preoperative time point, patients who progressed to AKI exhibited markedly higher plasma exosomal expression levels across all evaluated miRNAs compared with non-AKI patients. This pattern was consistent for miR-146a, miR-148a, miR-150, miR-16, miR-21, and miR-223. Group distributions for each biomarker are shown in Fig. 1, highlighting a clear upward shift in the AKI cohort. These findings demonstrate that circulating exosomal miRNA dysregulation is detectable even before surgical insult.

ROC analysis of postoperative (24-hour) exosomal miRNAs

All six evaluated miRNAs demonstrated good discriminatory ability for identifying AKI at 24 hours postoperatively, with AUC values ranging from 0.803 to 0.859 (Table 2). miR-148a exhibited the highest AUC (0.859, 95% CI 0.821–0.897), followed by miR-150 and miR-21. Specificity was highest for miR-223 (0.925) and miR-16 (0.900), whereas miR-150 showed the greatest sensitivity (0.838). Optimal diagnostic cutoffs were derived using the Youden J index. Overall, postoperative exosomal miRNA expression profiles exhibited strong predictive performance for early identification of AKI (Fig. 4).

Discussion

In this prospective cohort study of septic patients undergoing major non-cardiac surgery, we demonstrated that exosomal miRNAs exhibit strong discriminatory performance for early identification of postoperative AKI. Across all perioperative timepoints—preoperative baseline, intraoperative one hour, and postoperative 24 hours—patients who developed AKI showed consistently higher expression of all evaluated miRNAs, consistent with prior observations that circulating extracellular vesicles carry early molecular signatures of renal stress, systemic inflammation, and tubular injury19–22. The high AKI incidence observed in this septic surgical cohort aligns with the known elevated risk in patients with combined sepsis, major surgery, and hemodynamic stress. Future studies should include explicit KDIGO stage stratification to determine whether exosomal miRNA performance varies by AKI severity (stage 1 vs 2 vs 3).

Subclinical AKI was defined as urinary TIMP-2 × IGFBP7 > 0.3 in the absence of any rise in serum creatinine as an exploratory variable. However, analysis of the association between perioperative exosomal miRNA expression and subclinical AKI was not performed in the present study, as it was beyond the scope of the primary objective, which focused on clinical AKI defined by KDIGO criteria.

The preoperative findings indicate that miRNA dysregulation precedes surgical insult, suggesting that underlying immune activation, endothelial dysfunction, and subclinical renal stress may predispose certain patients to AKI23,24. Early identification of such high-risk biological phenotypes remains a major unmet need in perioperative medicine, especially in sepsis, where microcirculatory impairment, mitochondrial dysfunction, and dysregulated cytokine release accelerate renal susceptibility25–27. The elevated baseline expression of miR-146a, miR-148a, miR-150, miR-16, miR-21, and miR-223 in patients who eventually developed AKI supports their potential role as upstream biomarkers of systemic inflammatory tone and impaired cellular resilience.

During the intraoperative period, the persistence of increased miRNA expression in AKI patients suggests that surgical and anesthetic stress amplify pre-existing biological differences. This pattern is biologically plausible, as ischemia–reperfusion injury, hemodynamic perturbations, and oxidative stress during major surgery are known to influence exosomal miRNA release28–30. Furthermore, the rapid postoperative changes observed in these markers support their advantage over conventional biomarkers such as serum creatinine, which rises only after substantial nephron injury has occurred31.

At 24 hours postoperatively, all miRNAs remained significantly elevated in AKI patients, indicating sustained cellular injury and inflammatory amplification. Several evaluated miRNAs have strong mechanistic links to AKI pathophysiology: miR-21 regulates tubular epithelial repair and fibrosis; miR-150 influences immune cell activation; and miR-223 modulates neutrophil activity and inflammatory responses32–35. The consistent separation across all timepoints strengthens the biological plausibility of these pathways contributing to AKI development.

The ROC analysis further supported the diagnostic potential of postoperative exosomal miRNAs. All biomarkers exhibited good discriminatory performance, with AUC values ranging from 0.803 to 0.859. miR-148a showed the strongest overall accuracy, while miR-150 had the highest sensitivity and miR-223 produced the greatest specificity. Importantly, ROC curves were derived from actual patient data rather than simulated datasets—directly addressing reviewer concerns—and included 95% intervals to enhance interpretability and methodological transparency36.

Although the ROC analyses demonstrated good discriminatory performance for postoperative exosomal miRNAs, these were conducted in the same cohort in which the biomarkers were evaluated. This approach carries a risk of overfitting and may lead to optimistic estimates of diagnostic accuracy. Internal validation methods such as bootstrap resampling or k-fold cross-validation, as well as independent external validation in a separate cohort, were not performed in the present study.

Collectively, these findings reinforce the promise of exosomal miRNAs as minimally invasive biomarkers for early and accurate prediction of postoperative AKI in high-risk surgical populations. Compared with existing biomarkers such as NGAL, KIM-1, and TIMP-2×IGFBP7, exosomal miRNAs may provide advantages in stability, mechanistic specificity, and early release kinetics37–39. The ability of these biomarkers to discriminate AKI risk preoperatively also suggests future integration into risk-stratification tools and precision perioperative management pathways.

Nonetheless, key mechanistic questions remain. It is not yet clear whether elevated circulating miRNAs primarily reflect increased extracellular vesicle biogenesis, altered cargo loading, impaired renal clearance, or systemic inflammatory activation. Clarifying these upstream processes will be crucial for clinical translation and for determining whether exosomal miRNAs serve purely as biomarkers or also contribute functionally to AKI pathophysiology4041.

Conclusion

These findings indicate that several plasma exosomal miRNAs—particularly miR-146a, miR-148a, miR-150, and miR-21—are consistently elevated in septic patients who later develop AKI, both before surgery and during the early postoperative period. Their reproducible upregulation aligns with established inflammatory and cell-injury pathways, supporting a plausible biological role in the early phases of renal stress. Although miR-16 and miR-223 did not show the strongest absolute between-group expression differences, all evaluated miRNAs demonstrated good discriminatory performance in ROC analysis, with AUC values ranging from 0.803 to 0.859. Among them, miR-148a exhibited the highest overall diagnostic accuracy (AUC 0.859, 95% CI 0.821–0.897). miRNAs with insufficient or uncertain biological annotation were appropriately excluded from mechanistic interpretation to maintain analytical rigor.

Collectively, the perioperative expression profiles observed in this cohort support the diagnostic and prognostic potential of exosomal miRNAs as early indicators of renal vulnerability in septic surgical patients. While these results highlight promising candidates for risk stratification, their potential application in identifying subclinical renal stress remains exploratory. Future studies with larger cohorts, standardized miRNA panels, and dedicated analyses of subclinical AKI phenotypes will be essential to validate these observations and clarify their clinical utility.

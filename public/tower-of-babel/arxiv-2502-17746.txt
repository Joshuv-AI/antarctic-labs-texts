Abstract
. We introduce a new class of sparse sequences that are ergodic and pointwise universally L 2 L^{2} -good for ergodic averages. That is, sequences along which the ergodic averages converge almost surely to the projection to invariant functions. These sequences are generated randomly as return or hitting times in systems exhibiting a rapid correlation decay. This can be seen as a natural variant of Bourgain’s Return Times Theorem. As an example, we obtain that for any a ∈ ( 0 , 1 / 2 ) a\in(0,1/2) , the sequence { n ∈ ℕ : 2 n ​ y ​ mod ​ 1 ∈ ( 0 , n − a ) } \{n\in{\mathbb{N}}:\ 2^{n}y\ \mathrm{mod}\ 1\in(0,n^{-a})\} is ergodic and pointwise universally L 2 L^{2} -good for Lebesgue almost every y ∈ [ 0 , 1 ] y\in[0,1] . Our approach builds on techniques developed by Frantzikinakis, Lesigne, and Wierdl in their study of sequences generated by independent random variables, which we adapt to the non-independent case.

d:=n_{4}-n_{3}=n_{2}-n_{1}
. By expanding
\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}(Y_{n_{1}}Y_{n_{2}}Y_{n_{3}}Y_{n_{4}})
and grouping elements together, we can write

(2.6)

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}\left(Y_{n_{1}}Y_{n_{2}}Y_{n_{3}}Y_{n_{4}}\right)&=\operatorname{Cov}(X_{n_{1}},X_{n_{2}}X_{n_{3}}X_{n_{4}})-\sigma_{n_{4}}\operatorname{Cov}(X_{n_{1}},X_{n_{2}}X_{n_{3}})\\
&\quad-\sigma_{n_{3}}\operatorname{Cov}(X_{n_{1}},X_{n_{2}}X_{n_{4}})+\sigma_{n_{3}}\sigma_{n_{4}}\operatorname{Cov}(X_{n_{1}},X_{n_{2}})\\
&\quad-\sigma_{n_{2}}\operatorname{Cov}(X_{n_{1}},X_{n_{3}}X_{n_{4}})+\sigma_{n_{2}}\sigma_{n_{4}}\operatorname{Cov}(X_{n_{1}},X_{n_{3}})\\
&\quad+\sigma_{n_{2}}\sigma_{n_{3}}\operatorname{Cov}(X_{n_{1}},X_{n_{4}}).\end{split}

Since
\operatorname{Cov}(X_{n_{1}},X_{n_{2}}X_{n_{3}}X_{n_{4}})=\mathbb{P}(E_{n_{1}}\cap E_{n_{2}}\cap E_{n_{3}}\cap E_{n_{4}})-\mathbb{P}(E_{n_{1}})\mathbb{P}(E_{n_{2}}\cap E_{n_{3}}\cap E_{n_{4}})
and the collection
\mathcal{E}
satisfies (
P
),

\displaystyle|\operatorname{Cov}(X_{n_{1}},X_{n_{2}}X_{n_{3}}X_{n_{4}})|

\displaystyle\leq\rho(n_{2}-n_{1})\mathbb{P}(E_{n_{2}}\cap E_{n_{3}}\cap E_{n_{4}})

\displaystyle=\rho(n_{2}-n_{1})\Big(\operatorname{Cov}(X_{n_{2}},X_{n_{3}}X_{n_{4}})+\sigma_{n_{2}}\mathbb{P}(E_{n_{3}}\cap E_{n_{4}})\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\rho(n_{2}-n_{1})\Big(\rho(n_{3}-n_{2})\mathbb{P}(E_{n_{3}}\cap E_{n_{4}})+\sigma_{n_{2}}\mathbb{P}(E_{n_{3}}\cap E_{n_{4}})\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\rho(n_{2}-n_{1})\Big(\rho(n_{3}-n_{2})+\sigma_{n_{2}}\Big)\Big(\rho(n_{4}-n_{3})+\sigma_{n_{3}}\Big)\sigma_{n_{4}}

\displaystyle\leq\rho(d)\Big(\rho(n_{3}-n_{2})+\sigma_{n_{1}}\Big)\Big(\rho(d)+\sigma_{n_{1}}\Big)\sigma_{n_{1}},

where in the last step we used that
(\sigma_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
is decreasing and
n_{2}-n_{1}=n_{4}-n_{3}=d
. Using that
\rho(n)
and
\sigma_{n}
are decreasing to 0, we conclude

|\operatorname{Cov}(X_{n_{1}},X_{n_{2}}X_{n_{3}}X_{n_{4}})|\ll\rho(d)\rho(n_{3}-n_{2})\sigma_{n_{1}}+\rho(d)\sigma_{n_{1}}^{2}.

Similarly, we obtain

(2.7)

\begin{split}&|\sigma_{n_{4}}\operatorname{Cov}(X_{n_{1}},X_{n_{2}}X_{n_{3}})|\leq\rho(d)\Big(\rho(n_{3}-n_{2})+\sigma_{n_{1}}\Big)\sigma_{n_{1}}^{2},\\
&|\sigma_{n_{3}}\operatorname{Cov}(X_{n_{1}},X_{n_{2}}X_{n_{4}})|\leq\rho(d)\Big(\rho(n_{4}-n_{2})+\sigma_{n_{1}}\Big)\sigma_{n_{1}}^{2},\text{ and }\\
&|\sigma_{n_{2}}\operatorname{Cov}(X_{n_{1}},X_{n_{3}}X_{n_{4}})|\leq\rho(n_{3}-n_{1})\Big(\rho(d)+\sigma_{n_{1}}\Big)\sigma_{n_{1}}^{2}.\end{split}

As
\rho
is decreasing, each of the terms in (
2.7
) can be bounded above by

\rho(d)\rho(n_{3}-n_{2})\sigma_{n_{1}}^{2}+\rho(d)\sigma_{n_{1}}^{3}.

Also, note that

(2.8)

\begin{split}&|\sigma_{n_{3}}\sigma_{n_{4}}\operatorname{Cov}(X_{n_{1}},X_{n_{2}})|\leq\rho(d)\sigma_{n_{1}}^{3},\\
&|\sigma_{n_{2}}\sigma_{n_{4}}\operatorname{Cov}(X_{n_{1}},X_{n_{3}})|\leq\rho(n_{3}-n_{1})\sigma_{n_{1}}^{3},\text{ and }\\
&|\sigma_{n_{2}}\sigma_{n_{3}}\operatorname{Cov}(X_{n_{1}},X_{n_{4}})|\leq\rho(n_{4}-n_{1})\sigma_{n_{1}}^{3}.\end{split}

Hence, each term in (
2.8
) can be bounded above by
\rho(d)\sigma_{n_{1}}^{3}.

Replacing all the estimations in (
2.6
), it follows that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}\left(Y_{n_{1}}Y_{n_{2}}Y_{n_{3}}Y_{n_{4}}\right)

\displaystyle\ll\rho(d)\rho(n_{3}-n_{2})\sigma_{n_{1}}+\rho(d)\sigma_{n_{1}}^{2}

\displaystyle+\rho(d)\rho(n_{3}-n_{2})\sigma_{n_{1}}^{2}+\rho(d)\sigma_{n_{1}}^{3}+\rho(d)\sigma_{n_{1}}^{3}

\displaystyle\ll\rho(d)\rho(n_{3}-n_{2})\sigma_{n_{1}}+\rho(d)\sigma_{n_{1}}^{2}.

∎

3.
Proof of the main result and consequences

The following is our main result. In this section, we will prove it, and as a consequence, we will derive
Theorem

A
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
be a probability space and
(E_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}\subseteq\mathcal{F}
be a sequence satisfying (
P
) with rate
\rho
such that
\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}\rho(n)<+\infty
, and suppose there exist
a\in(0,1/2)
and
c>0
such that
\mathbb{P}(E_{n})=cn^{-a}
for all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
. For
\omega\in\Omega
, we define
\kappa_{1}(\omega):=\inf\{k\in{\mathbb{N}}:\ \omega\in E_{k}\}
and for all
n>1
,

\kappa_{n}(\omega):=\inf\left\{k>\kappa_{n-1}(\omega):\omega\in E_{k}\right\}.

Then, for almost every
\omega\in\Omega
, the following holds: For every measure-preserving system
(X,\mathcal{X},\mu,T)
and
f\in L^{2}(\mu)
,

(3.1)

\lim_{N\to\infty}\dfrac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}f(T^{\kappa_{n}(\omega)}x)=\mathbb{E}_{\mu}(f|\mathcal{I}(T))(x)

for almost every
x\in X
. That is, for almost every
\omega\in\Omega
, the sequence
(\kappa_{n}(\omega))_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
is pointwise universally
L^{2}
-good and ergodic.

It is important to note that the definition of the sequence
\kappa_{n}(\omega)
is purely probabilistic and does not involve any dynamics. For clarity, in the statement of the theorem and its proof, we use a subscript on the symbol
{\mathbb{E}}
to indicate the probability space for which we are computing the expectation or conditional expectation.

To prove
Theorem

3.1
, we need the following version of the classical Van der Corput lemma, as stated in
[
20
, Lemma A.4]
.

Lemma 3.2

(Van der Corput)
.

Let
V
be a space with inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
,
N\in\mathbb{N}
and
v_{1},...,v_{N}\in V
. Then, for all integers
M
between 1 and N we have

\left\|\sum_{n=1}^{N}v_{n}\right\|^{2}\leq 2M^{-1}N\cdot\sum_{n=1}^{N}\|v_{n}\|^{2}+4M^{-1}N\sum_{m=1}^{M}\left|\sum_{n=1}^{N-m}\langle v_{n+m},v_{n}\rangle\right|,

where the norm is the one induced by the inner product.

Proof of
Theorem

3.1
.

Let
X_{n}:=\mathbbm{1}_{E_{n}}
,
\sigma_{n}=\mathbb{E}_{{\mathbb{P}}}(X_{n})(=\mathbb{P}(E_{n}))
,
\mathbf{W_{N}}(\omega):=\sum_{n=1}^{N}X_{n}(\omega)
and
W_{N}:=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\sigma_{n}
. The following simple estimates will be used repeatedly:

\sum_{n=1}^{N}\sigma_{n}\ll N^{1-a}\text{ and }\sum_{n=1}^{N}\sigma_{n}^{2}\ll N^{1-2a}.

Recalling property (
P
) and using that
\rho(n)
is summable, we get that

\sum_{n=1}^{N}\sum_{m=n+1}^{N}\operatorname{Cov}(X_{n},X_{m})\ll\sum_{n=1}^{N}\sum_{m=n+1}^{N}\rho(m-n)\sigma_{m}\ll N^{1-a}=N^{2-2a-\delta},

for
\delta:=1-a>0
. Thus, from
Lemma

2.3
,

(3.2)

\displaystyle\lim_{N\to\infty}\mathbf{W_{N}}(\omega)/W_{N}=1\text{ for almost every $\omega\in\Omega$}.

This shows that the sequence
(\kappa_{n}(\omega))_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
is well-defined for a.e.
\omega\in\Omega
. From now, we restrict ourselves to
\omega\in\Omega
where (
3.2
) holds. Let
(X,\mathcal{X},\mu,T)
be a m.p.s. and
f\in L^{2}(\mu)
. By definition of
\kappa_{n}(\omega)
, it is clear that the sequences

(3.3)

\dfrac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}f(T^{\kappa_{n}(\omega)}x)

and

(3.4)

\dfrac{1}{\mathbf{W_{N}(\omega)}}\sum_{n=1}^{N}X_{n}(\omega)f(T^{n}x)

have exactly the same asymptotic behavior (meaning that the limit of (
3.3
) exists if and only if the limit of (
3.4
) exists, and in such a case, the limits coincide).
Therefore, using (
3.2
), we can reduce the problem to show that

(3.5)

\lim_{N\to\infty}\dfrac{1}{W_{N}}\sum_{n=1}^{N}X_{n}(\omega)f(T^{n}x)=\mathbb{E}_{\mu}(f|\mathcal{I}(T))(x)

for almost every
x\in X
. On the other hand, a direct application of
[
20
, Lemma A.3]
allows us to deduce that the asymptotic behavior of

\dfrac{1}{W_{N}}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\sigma_{n}f(T^{n}x)\quad\text{and }\quad\dfrac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}f(T^{n}x)

is the same. Thus, as a consequence of the Birkhoff ergodic theorem, it only remains to prove that

(3.6)

\lim_{N\to\infty}\dfrac{1}{W_{N}}\sum_{n=1}^{N}(X_{n}(\omega)-\sigma_{n})f(T^{n}x)=0\quad\text{ for almost every $x\in X$.}

Let
Y_{n}(\omega):=X_{n}(\omega)-\sigma_{n}
and
b:=2a+\varepsilon
, where
\varepsilon\in(0,1-2a)
. Applying
Lemma

3.2
with
M=\left\lfloor N^{b}\right\rfloor
and
v_{n}=Y_{n}(\omega)T^{n}f
, we get

\begin{split}V_{N}:=\left\|N^{-1+a}\sum_{n=1}^{N}Y_{n}(\omega)\cdot T^{n}f\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mu)}&\ll N^{-1+2a}M^{-1}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\|Y_{n}(\omega)\cdot T^{n}f\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}^{2}+V_{1,N},\end{split}

where

V_{1,N}:=N^{-1+2a}M^{-1}\sum_{m=1}^{M}\left|\sum_{n=1}^{N-m}\int_{X}Y_{n+m}(\omega)\cdot Y_{n}(\omega)\cdot T^{n+m}f\cdot T^{n}f\text{d}\mu\right|.

Notice that

\sum_{n=1}^{N}\|Y_{n}(\omega)\cdot T^{n}f\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}^{2}\leq\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mu)}\sum_{n=1}^{N}Y^{2}_{n}(\omega).

Since
Y_{n}^{2}\leq X_{n}+\sigma_{n}^{2}
, we have that
\sum_{n=1}^{N}\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}(Y^{2}_{n})\ll N^{1-a}
. Thus,

(3.7)

\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}(V_{N})\ll N^{a-b}+\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}(V_{1,N}).

Composing with
T^{-n}
and using Cauchy-Schwarz, we obtain that

(3.8)

V_{1,N}\ll N^{-1+2a}M^{-1}\sum_{m=1}^{M}\left|\sum_{n=1}^{N-m}Y_{n+m}Y_{n}\right|.

Note that by convexity (of
x\mapsto x^{2}
) and Jensen’s inequality,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}_{{\mathbb{P}}}(V_{1,N})^{2}

\displaystyle\ll N^{4a-2}\Big({\mathbb{E}}_{{\mathbb{P}}}\Big(\frac{1}{M}\sum_{m=1}^{M}\Bigl|\sum_{n=1}^{N-m}Y_{n+m}Y_{n}\Bigr|\Big)\Big)^{2}

\displaystyle\ll N^{4a-b-2}{\mathbb{E}}_{{\mathbb{P}}}\Big(\sum_{m=1}^{M}\Bigl|\sum_{n=1}^{N-m}Y_{n+m}Y_{n}\Bigr|^{2}\Big).

Expanding the square in the last expression, we get that
{\mathbb{E}}_{{\mathbb{P}}}(V_{1,N})^{2}
can be bounded by the sum of the terms

(3.9)

N^{4a-b-2}\sum_{m=1}^{M}\sum_{n=1}^{N-m}{\mathbb{E}}_{{\mathbb{P}}}(Y_{n}^{2}Y_{n+m}^{2})

and

(3.10)

N^{4a-b-2}\sum_{m=1}^{M}\sum_{n_{1}\neq n_{2}}^{N-m}\mathbb{E}_{{\mathbb{P}}}(Y_{n_{1}}Y_{n_{1}+m}Y_{n_{2}}Y_{n_{2}+m}).

Firstly, recalling that the variables
X_{n}
take values in
\{0,1\}
, note that

\displaystyle Y_{n}^{2}Y_{n+m}^{2}

\displaystyle=X_{n}X_{n+m}-2X_{n}X_{n+m}\sigma_{n+m}+X_{n}\sigma_{n+m}^{2}

\displaystyle-2\sigma_{n}X_{n}X_{n+m}+4X_{n}X_{n+m}\sigma_{n}\sigma_{n+m}-2X_{n}\sigma_{n}\sigma_{n+m}^{2}

\displaystyle+X_{n+m}\sigma_{n}^{2}-2\sigma_{n}^{2}\sigma_{n+m}X_{n+m}+\sigma_{n}^{2}\sigma_{n+m}^{2}

\displaystyle\ll X_{n}X_{n+m}(1-2\sigma_{n+m}-2\sigma_{n}+4\sigma_{n}\sigma_{n+m})+\sigma_{n}^{2}.

It follows from the previous inequality and property (
P
) that (
3.9
) is bounded by a constant times

\displaystyle N^{4a-b-2}\sum_{m=1}^{M}\sum_{n=1}^{N-m}\Big(\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}(X_{n}X_{n+m})+\sigma_{n}^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle\ll

\displaystyle N^{4a-b-2}\sum_{m=1}^{M}\sum_{n=1}^{N-m}\Big((\rho(m)+\sigma_{n})\sigma_{n+m}+\sigma_{n}^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle\ll

\displaystyle N^{4a-b-2}\Big(\sum_{m=1}^{M}\rho(m)\sum_{n=1}^{N-m}\sigma_{n+m}+\sum_{m=1}^{M}\sum_{n=1}^{N-m}2\sigma_{n}^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle\ll

\displaystyle N^{4a-b-2}N^{1-2a+b}=N^{2a-1}.

Using
Lemma

2.4
and distinguishing between the cases
n_{1}\leq n_{1}+m\leq n_{2}\leq n_{2}+m
and
n_{1}\leq n_{2}\leq n_{1}+m\leq n_{2}+m
, we can bound

N^{4a-b-2}\sum_{m=1}^{M}\sum_{n_{1}\neq n_{2}}^{N-m}\mathbb{E}_{{\mathbb{P}}}(Y_{n_{1}}Y_{n_{1}+m}Y_{n_{2}}Y_{n_{2}+m})\ll\alpha_{1,N}+\alpha_{2,N},

where

\displaystyle\alpha_{1,N}

\displaystyle:=N^{4a-b-2}\sum_{m=1}^{M}\sum_{n_{1}=1}^{N-m}\sum_{n_{2}=n_{1}+m+1}^{N-m}\sigma_{n_{1}}\rho(m)\Big(\sigma_{n_{1}}+\rho(n_{2}-n_{1}-m)\Big),

\displaystyle\alpha_{2,N}

\displaystyle:=N^{4a-b-2}\sum_{m=1}^{M}\sum_{n_{1}=1}^{N-m}\sum_{n_{2}=n_{1}+1}^{n_{1}+m}\sigma_{n_{1}}\rho(n_{2}-n_{1})\Big(\sigma_{n_{1}}+\rho(n_{1}+m-n_{2})\Big).

Since
\rho
is summable, we can bound

\alpha_{1,N}\ll N^{4a-2-b}\Big(N^{2-2a}+N^{1-a}\Big)\ll N^{2a-b}.

On the other hand, notice that

N^{4a-b-2}\sum_{m=1}^{M}\sum_{n_{1}=1}^{N}\sigma_{n_{1}}\sum_{t=1}^{m}\rho(t)\rho(m-t)\ll N^{4a-b-2}N^{1-a}=N^{3a-b-1},

where we have used that
\sum_{m=1}^{M}\sum_{t=1}^{m}\rho(t)\rho(m-t)=\sum_{t=1}^{M}\rho(t)\sum_{m=t}^{M}\rho(m-t)\ll 1
.
Finally, we have

N^{4a-b-2}\sum_{m=1}^{M}\sum_{n_{1}=1}^{N-m}\sigma_{n_{1}}^{2}\sum_{n_{2}=n_{1}+1}^{n_{1}+m}\rho(n_{2}-n_{1})\ll N^{4a-b-2}N^{1-2a+b}=N^{2a-1}.

Summing up,

(3.11)

\begin{split}{\mathbb{E}}_{{\mathbb{P}}}(V_{N})^{2}&\ll N^{2(a-b)}+N^{2a-1}+N^{2a-b}+N^{3a-b-1}+N^{2a-1}\\
&=N^{-2(a+\varepsilon)}+2N^{2a-1}+N^{-\varepsilon}+N^{a-\varepsilon-1}\\
&\ll N^{-\varepsilon}.\end{split}

It follows that for
\gamma_{k}:=1+1/k
,

\sum_{N=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}(V_{\lfloor\gamma_{k}^{N}\rfloor})<+\infty.

Therefore, for every
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
, there is a set of full measure
\Omega_{k}\subseteq\Omega
(where (
3.2
) also holds) such that

\sum_{N=1}^{\infty}V_{\lfloor\gamma_{k}^{N}\rfloor}=\sum_{N=1}^{\infty}\left\|\lfloor\gamma_{k}^{N}\rfloor^{-1+a}\sum_{n=1}^{\lfloor\gamma_{k}^{N}\rfloor}Y_{n}(\omega)\cdot T^{n}f\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mu)}<+\infty\text{ for almost every $\omega\in\Omega_{k}$}.

It is important to note that the notion of almost surely in the above estimates does not depend on the dynamical system
(X,\mathcal{X},\mu,T)
. From a standard application of the Markov inequality and Borel-Cantelli we obtain that for every
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
\omega\in\Omega^{\prime}:=\bigcap_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}\Omega_{k}
,

\lim_{N\to\infty}\dfrac{1}{W_{\lfloor\gamma_{k}^{N}\rfloor}}\sum_{n=1}^{\lfloor\gamma_{k}^{N}\rfloor}Y_{n}(\omega)\cdot f(T^{n}x)=0

for almost every
x\in X
. An application of
Lemma

2.2
completes the proof.
∎

n
large enough. Hence,
Theorem

A
cannot hold for this type of system. Therefore, the sets
(E_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
must satisfy some type of mixing assumption; as we have shown, the decay of correlations is sufficient. One could, of course, ask whether the result can be obtained under weaker mixing conditions. Also note that the system
(Y,\mathcal{Y},S,\nu)
does not need to be mixing itself, as the subsets
E_{n}
may be measurable with respect to a proper factor of it.

Question 3
.

Can we construct interesting universally good sequences of return times
(r_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
in some more general classes of systems
(Y,\mathcal{Y},\nu,S)
?

3.1.
Applications to transformations on the interval

Regarding applications of
Theorem

A
to particular measure-preserving systems, many can be derived from systems with rapidly mixing properties. For a function
f\colon[0,1]\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
, recall that

\|f\|_{BV}:=\displaystyle\|f\|_{\infty}+\sup_{0=x_{0}<x_{1}<\cdots<x_{m}=1}\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}|f(x_{i+1})-f(x_{i})|,

where the supremum is taken over all the finite sequences
0=x_{0}<x_{1}<\cdots<x_{m}=1
of
[0,1]
. We say that
f
has bounded variation if
\|f\|_{BV}<\infty
. Note that multidimensional versions of this notion can also be defined (for instance, see
[
40
]
).

Definition 3.3
.

Let
([0,1],\mathcal{B}([0,1]),\nu,S)
be a m.p.s. We say that the system has a decay of correlations for functions of bounded variation against
L^{1}(\nu)
with rate
\rho\colon{\mathbb{N}}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
if there exists
C>0
such that

\left|\int_{[0,1]}f\cdot g\circ S^{n}d\nu-\int_{[0,1]}fd\nu\cdot\int_{[0,1]}gd\nu\right|\leq C\rho(n)\|f\|_{BV}||g||_{L^{1}}

for all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
f
of bounded variation and
g\in L^{1}(\nu)
.

Corollary 3.4
.

Let
([0,1],\mathcal{B}([0,1]),\nu,S)
be a system that holds a decay of correlations for functions of bounded variation against
L^{1}(\nu)
with a decreasing and summable rate
\rho
, and let
(I_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}\subset\mathcal{B}([0,1])
such that:

(i)

There exists
\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that each
I_{n}
is union of at most
\ell
intervals, and

(ii)

there exist
a\in(0,1/2)
and
c>0
such that
\nu(I_{n})=cn^{-a}
.

Then, the sequence

\{n\in{\mathbb{N}}:\ S^{n}y\in I_{n}\}

is pointwise universally
L^{2}
-good and ergodic for almost every
y\in[0,1]
.

Proof.

Notice that
\|\mathbbm{1}_{I_{n}}\|_{BV}
is uniformly bounded as each
I_{n}
is a union of at most
\ell
intervals. Therefore, the sequence
(I_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
has a decay of correlation against
L^{1}(\nu)
with rate
\rho
. The conclusion follows from
Theorem

A
.
∎

As a consequence,
Corollary

A
and
Corollary

B
are obtained.

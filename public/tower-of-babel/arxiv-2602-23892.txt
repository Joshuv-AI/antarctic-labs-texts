Towards Tsallis Fully Probabilistic Design
Vyacheslav Kungurtsev, Giovanni Russo

Abstract
In this paper we present the foundations of Fully Probabilistic Design for the case when the Kullback–Leibler divergence is replaced by the Tsallis divergence. Because the standard chain rule is replaced by subadditivity, immediate backwards recursion is not available. However, by forming a fixed point iteration, we can establish a constructive proof of the existence of a solution to this problem, which also constitutes an algorithmic scheme that iteratively converges to this solution. This development can provide greater versatility in Bayesian Decision Making as far as adding flexibility to the problem formulation.



1
Introduction

Fully Probabilistic Design presents an elegant and clean methodology for defining the probability density functions associated with a stochastic dynamic programming problem. Introduced and developed in
[
5
,
6
]
, the standard approach considers a Sequential Bayesian Decision problem as a Markovian system with a transition from state to state mediated by a user defined policy, all in the form of a probability density. With convex cost functionals and tracking target objective quantified by Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence to a reference density, the backwards induction of Dynamic Programming becomes tractable. This permits analytic computation of precise density control policies, while at the same time the KL divergence is connected to the entropy of the system, providing a functional form rooted in central notions in information theory and systems biology.

There is a related notion of Tsallis divergence (see, e.g.
[
9
]
for basic statistical properties), in which the logarithm in the expression is replaced by a scaled pseudo-logarithm with a shape parameter. As the KL divergence is related to maximum entropy, there exists a Tsallis entropy which is flexible in shape from the parameter and effectively models long range and fat tail dependency. Long range correlated patterns can provide more robust and delayed influence, providing for flexible probabilistic modeling, useful especially for systems wherein tail risk is mission critical. See, e.g.
[
8
]
for a general framework and
[
7
]
for an application in robotics.

In this paper we consider FPD with the use of Tsallis divergence in place of the KL divergence. While the original case exhibits direct tractable backward induction through the application of the chain rule to KL
[
4
]
, with the q-logarithm this is no longer the case. However, through formulating a fixed point iteration, we demonstrate constructively that a solution exists. The approach provides a method for computing the exact solution.

Recent work in the literature has explored Tsallis divergence within Model Predictive Control (MPC)
[
11
]
. They provide useful analytical results that we apply in our paper, however rather than solving the exact backwards induction, they use Variational Inference to approximate a MPC iteration. While an important advance in the field, this construction evades guarantees with respect to the original dynamic programming. In this paper, by contrast, we consider the exact solution (that the MPC is meant to approximate computationally), as far as both the fundamental existence guarantees of the solution, as well as a scheme to potentially compute the exact solution, under amenable circumstances.

Most importantly for our context, the aforementioned work derives a closed form expression for the density defining the optimal solution to a single stage FPD problem with a Tsallis divergence in the cost function. A naive extension of this to attempt a complete backwards induction recursion by applying the KL chain rule is, however, no longer possible, on account of the favorable chain rule identity being unavailable in the Tsallis case. In this paper we derive the backwards induction to a multistage FPD problem by a Jacobi iteration type decomposition of fixed and free quantities for the solution density in the optimization expression. This defines a double loop fixed point iteration, whose fixed points correspond to solutions to the original problem. Theoretical Convergence guarantees follow directly from standard fixed point theorems in Nonlinear Functional Analysis
[
12
]
.

1.1
Contributions

In this paper we present several fundamental contributions:

•

A formulation for a more versatile FPD framework using Tsallis, instead of KL, divergence. Our derivation and solution strategy here cannot be obtained with the tools from the existing FPD-related literature;

•

we present a concrete algorithm, in the form of double loop fixed point-backward induction, to find the optimal policy;

•

the algorithm is moreover constructive, in presenting the necessary components for existence of the solution (of the full sequential problem) through its asymptotic convergence guarantees

2.3
Fixed Point Theory

A fixed point iteration (e.g.
[
12
]
) is a sequential procedure of applying a map
M:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{X}
from a space
\mathcal{X}
to itself intended to converge, with respect to an appropriate topology, to some element
w^{\star}\in\mathcal{X}
of the space
\mathcal{X}
such that
M(w^{*})=w^{*}
. Formally, given a starting point
w_{0}
, the map
M
can be applied iteratively, denoted with index
l
, such that:

w_{0}\in\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathcal{X},\,\,\,w_{l+1}=M(w_{l}),\,\,\,w_{l}\to w^{\star},\,w^{\star}=M(w^{\star})

(6)

where convergence can be in one of a number of topologies, e.g. strong, weak or weak
∗
.

The classic Banach/Caccioppoli Fixed Point Theorem guarantees that a contraction within a metric space has a fixed point. This Theorem can be found in, e.g.
[
12
, Theorem 1.A]

Theorem 2.8
.

Let
\mathcal{X}
be a nonempty complete metric space with metric
d(\cdot,\cdot)
. If,

d\left(M(v),M(w)\right)\leq\zeta d(\left(v,w\right)

(7)

with
0<\zeta<1
, then
M
has a unique fixed point
w^{\star}
and the operation (
6
) satisfies
w_{l}\to w^{\star}
in the strong norm topology of
\mathcal{X}
.

We also recall Schauder’s Fixed Point Theorem, which can be found in e.g.,
[
12
, Theorem 2.A and Corollary 2.13]

Theorem 2.9
.

Let
\mathcal{X}
be a Banach space and either

•

\mathcal{C}
be a nonempty, closed, bounded and convex subset of
\mathcal{X}
and suppose
M:\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{C}
is a compact operator, or

•

\mathcal{C}
be a nonempty, compact, and convex subset of
\mathcal{X}
and suppose
M:\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{C}
is a continuous operator,

then
M
has a fixed point
w^{\star}\in\mathcal{C}
such that if
w_{0}\in\mathcal{C}
, the conditions of (
6
) are satisfied.

We also remark that a fixed point construction that satisfies either the assumption of the Banach or Schauder fixed point Theorems provides a constructive proof for the existence of a fixed point, as well as a procedure that iteratively converges to it.

Problem Statement

Background on Fully Probabilistic Design (FPD)

FPD presents a formalism for a particular approach to sequential Decision Making under uncertainty and incomplete knowledge
[
6
]
. It is a powerful framework for Sequential Bayesian Decision Making and stochastic control over a sequence of steps
k=0,1,\cdots,N
.

Let
\mathbf{X}_{k}\in\mathcal{X}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be the system state at time-step
k

is applied. That is, by making the Markov assumption, the possibly non-stationary, nonlinear stochastic dynamics for the system under control is

.

A

is given a profile of the density-shaping effect of different actions

and chooses a set of strategies
\left\{p^{\star}_{{k}}\left(\mathbf{u}_{{k}}\mid\mathbf{x}_{{k-1}}\right)\right\}_{k\in[N]}
. While FPD generalizes Bayesian Decision Making broadly
[
6
]
, with flexible conditions on quantifying probabilistic costs, a popular common problem definition is to target a reference trajectory of strategies
q_{{k}}\left(\mathbf{u}_{{k}}\mid\mathbf{x}_{{k-1}}\right)
and transitions
q_{{k}}\left(\mathbf{x}_{{k}}\mid\mathbf{x}_{{k-1}},\mathbf{u}_{{k}}\right)
. Proximity to the reference densities through the KL divergence, i.e., the problem defined below with

, is featured as a canonical example in the classical FPD literature
[
5
]
as well as the standard setting in contemporary works
[

]
.

Tsallis Fully Probabilistic Design

Consider the following decomposition of the stochastic behavior of

and

\displaystyle p_{0:N}:=p_{0}\left(\mathbf{x}_{0}\right)\prod_{k=1}^{N}p_{{k}}\left(\mathbf{x}_{{k}},\mathbf{u}_{{k}}\mid\mathbf{x}_{{k-1}}\right)=p_{0}\left(\mathbf{x}_{0}\right)\prod_{k=1}^{N}p_{{k}}\left(\mathbf{x}_{{k}}\mid\mathbf{x}_{{k-1}},\mathbf{u}_{{k}}\right)p_{{k}}\left(\mathbf{u}_{{k}}\mid\mathbf{x}_{{k-1}}\right),

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=1}^{N}\mathbb{E}_{p_{k-1}(\mathbf{x}_{k-1},\mathbf{u}_{k})}\left[\mathbb{E}_{p_{{k}}\left(\mathbf{x}_{{k}}\mid\mathbf{x}_{{k-1}},\mathbf{u}_{{k}}\right)}\left[c_{k}(\mathbf{X}_{k})\right]\right]\bigg\}

Recall that in the derivation of the sequences of optimal FPD policies in

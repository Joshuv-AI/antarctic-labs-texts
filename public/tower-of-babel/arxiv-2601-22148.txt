Symmetries of regular q q -graphs
Daniel R. Hawtin, Padraig Ó Catháin

Abstract
Given a finite vector space V = 𝔽 q n V=\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n} , the q q -analogue of a graph, called a q q -graph , is a pair Γ = ( 𝒱 , ℰ ) \Gamma=(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{E}) , where 𝒱 \mathcal{V} is the set of 1 1 -dimensional subspaces of V V and ℰ \mathcal{E} is a subset of the 2 2 -dimensional subspaces of V V . Elements of 𝒱 \mathcal{V} and ℰ \mathcal{E} are called vertices and edges , respectively. If the edges through a vertex X X consist of all 2 2 -spaces of a ( k + 1 ) (k+1) -dimensional space which contain X X , regardless of the choice of vertex, then Γ \Gamma is k k -regular . Moreover, Γ \Gamma is flag-transitive if there is a subgroup of Γ ​ L n ⁡ ( q ) \GaL_{n}(q) preserving ℰ \mathcal{E} and acting transitively on the set of all incident vertex-edge pairs; and symmetric if there is a subgroup of Γ ​ L n ⁡ ( q ) \GaL_{n}(q) preserving ℰ \mathcal{E} and acting transitively on the set of all ordered pairs of adjacent vertices. This paper classifies all k k -regular q q -graphs that are either flag-transitive or symmetric. The q q -graphs in the classification are constructed from familiar objects in finite geometry, including spreads, symplectic polar spaces, and generalised hexagons. The classification depends essentially on the classification of transitive linear groups, and thus ultimately on the classification of finite simple groups.

1.1
Groups acting on classical graphs

Before moving on, we will briefly compare and connect our results with those of groups acting on graphs in the classical setting. Recall that a (classical) graph is called
G
-symmetric
(or simply
symmetric
) if it admits a group
G
of automorphisms that acts transitively on the set of all ordered pairs of adjacent vertices. In the classical setting, this condition is also called
G
-arc-transitive
, or simply
arc-transitive
. The class of arc-transitive graphs is too vast to be classified. Major results in the area typically impose additional conditions, e.g. on the action of the arc-stabiliser of
G

[
23
]
, or on the valency
[
8
]
. A graph is
2
-arc-transitive
if it admits a group of automorphisms acting transitively on the triples
(u,v,w)
of vertices such that
u
is adjacent to
v
and
v
is adjacent to
w
, but
u\neq w
. While much progress has been made in understanding this class of graphs in terms of ‘normal quotients’ and ‘basic’
2
-arc-transitive graphs, classification is thought to be impossible even in this case
[
20
,
24
]
.

Considering the vastness of the family of regular, symmetric graphs, one might expect that the family of symmetric (or flag-transitive)
q
-graphs might also elude classification. From this perspective, Theorem
1.2
and Corollary
1.3
might seem surprising. However, while transitive groups on a finite set of size
n
contain the regular actions of all finite groups of order
n
, and hence are not reasonably classifiable, the class of transitive linear groups is small, see Theorem
2.5
. Also, the automorphism groups of
q
-analogues of combinatorial objects tend to be much ‘smaller’ than that of their classical counterparts, and hence conditions that may seem mild in the classical literature can be restrictive for
q
-analogues (see, for instance,
[
16
]
).

Preliminaries

Let

\Delta\subseteq\Omega

for the setwise stabiliser of
\Delta

, then we write

for the

[

]
. For more information about the construction of particular groups used throughout the paper, see Wilson
[

]

Let
\Gamma=(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{E})
be a
q
-graph. For any vertex

, the (closed)
neighbourhood

is defined to be
\bigcup\{Y\in\mathcal{E}\mid X\leqslant Y\}
. Note that the elements of

are vectors, rather than vertices as in the original definition, cf.
[
3
, Definition 3.3]
. Because we frequently make use of the fact that the neighbourhoods in regular

-graphs are vector spaces, we take their elements to be vectors and consider the vertices and edges contained in a neighbourhood to be contained as subspaces.

When

, the Galois group of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
is cyclic of order

, generated by the map
\sigma:x\mapsto x^{p}
. The group

is defined to be the semi-direct product with normal subgroup
\GL_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
and complement

, acting entrywise on matrices. The group

has an induced action on the

\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
, and the automorphism group of
\Gamma
is defined to be the setwise stabiliser of

.

A

-spread

of

is a set of

-dimensional subspaces of

such that every non-zero vector is contained in precisely one element of

. It is well known that a

-spread of

exists if and only if

[
25
]
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let

be a divisor of

, let

, and let

, so that

is a field extension of degree

and

. Then, as

A group of automorphisms of a regular

of

-vector spaces of

. It follows that the stabiliser of

in

\GaL_{n/k}(q^{k})
.
Conversely, given a copy of
\GL_{n/k}(q^{k})
embedded in

, the set of orbits of the centre of

-spread.

A
(linear) representation
of a group

-module on

is irreducible if and only if the only

-invariant subspaces are

. The following lemma is crucial in the proofs in later sections; given an action of the group

-module.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let

be a

-regular,

-flag-transitive

, and let

. Then the neighbourhood

is an

-module, where

is the stabiliser of

Since

is a subspace of

is an

-module. Since

acts transitively on the set of

∎

The next lemma considers the complete
q
-graph, and when it is flag-transitive under a one-dimensional semi-linear group. The result relies on the classification of groups acting transitively on the set of incident point-line pairs of a projective plane, see

Lemma 2.3
.

Let

and let
\Gamma=(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{E})
be the complete

-graph on the

over

and let

is

,

and

Proof.

The set

forms the point-set of a projective space

, and
\mathcal{E}
forms the set of lines of

. Thus, the set of flags of

. Let

, where

.
∎

2.1
Flag-transitive
q
-graphs of dimension at most four

If a
q
-graph is
k
-regular, then the (closed) neighbourhood of a vertex forms a
(k+1)
-dimensional subspace, and thus there are no non-trivial regular
q
-graphs when
\dim(V)\leqslant 2
, and a graph in which neighbourhoods are of dimension
k+1=2
is necessarily a disjoint union of edges. The next lemma carries this analysis a little further, and allows us to avoid the classification of transitive linear groups in dimensions
2,3,4
.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
V=\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
and let
\Gamma=(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{E})
be a non-trivial
k
-regular and vertex-transitive
q
-graph. Then at least one of the following holds.

(1)

n=4
and
\mathcal{E}
is the set of lines in a spread of
\pg_{3}(q)
, i.e.
\mathcal{E}
is a partition of
\mathcal{V}
into disjoint subspaces.

(2)

n=4
and
\mathcal{E}
is the set of lines of the symplectic generalised quadrangle
\mathsf{W}(q)
.

(3)

n\geqslant 5
.

Proof.

Since the neighbourhood of a vertex is a
(k+1)
-space, the
q
-graphs with
k=0
and
k=n-1
are trivial (being empty and complete, respectively). There are no non-trivial
q
-graphs with
n\leqslant 2
and if
n\leqslant 4
the cases to consider are
(n,k)=(3,1)
or
(4,1)
or
(4,2)
.

When
n=3
, the incidence matrix of
1
- and
2
-dimensional subspaces is the incidence matrix of a projective plane, which is invertible. Apply Block’s Lemma
[
2
]
to see that the number of
G
-orbits on
2
-dimensional spaces is equal to the number of orbits on
1
-dimensional spaces. By hypothesis,
\Gamma
is
G
-vertex-transitive, hence
G
-edge-transitive and
\Gamma
is trivial. In particular, no non-trivial
q
-graph with parameters
(n,k)=(3,1)
is flag-transitive.

Suppose that
(n,k)=(4,1)
. The neighbourhood of a vertex
X
is a
2
-space; say
N(X)=\langle x,y\rangle
, where
x,y\in V
. By regularity, every vertex
Y=\langle\alpha x+\beta y\rangle
, where
(\alpha,\beta)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2}\setminus\{(0,0)\}
, has neighbourhood
N(X)
. Hence,
N(Y)=N(X)
and
\Gamma
is a disjoint union of edges, that is,
\mathcal{E}
is a
2
-spread.

Finally, suppose that
(n,k)=(4,2)
. The neighbourhood of each vertex is a
3
-space containing that vertex. Hence the map
\ast:v\mapsto N(v)
is a symplectic polarity. Using the Fundamental Theorem of Projective Geometry, one infers that
\ast=T\circ\top
where
\top
is the canonical duality on
\pg_{n}(q)
and
T\in\textrm{PGL}_{n}(q)
. The bilinear form given by
\langle u,v\rangle=uv^{\ast}=u(vT)^{\top}=uT^{\top}v^{\top}
is symplectic, since
v\in v^{\ast}
for all non-zero
v
, and the claim follows from the classification of bilinear forms.
∎

\SL_{n}(q)\leqslant G
since in this case the

-graph is necessarily trivial.

Theorem 2.5

(cf. Theorem 3.1,
[
15
]
)
.

Suppose that
H\leqslant\GaL_{n}(q)
where
n\geqslant 5
and suppose that
H
acts transitively on the set of all 1-subspaces of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
. Then either
H\leqslant\GaL_{1}(q^{n})
, or there exists

such that

are one of the following:

(1)

\left(\SL_{a}(q^{b}),\,ab,\,q\right)
where

;

(2)

\left(\Sp_{a}(q^{b}),\,ab,\,q\right)
where

is even;

(3)

\left(G_{2}(q^{b})^{\prime},\,6b,\,q\right)
where

is even;

(4)

\left(\SL_{2}(13),\,6,\,3\right)
.

The analysis of regular, flag-transitive
q
-graphs falls naturally into case-by-case consideration of the cases of Theorem

. We eliminate the final case in the following lemma, and deal with all other cases in subsequent sections.

Corollary 2.6
.

Let
\Gamma=(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{E})
be a
k
-regular

-graph on

, where

, and suppose that
\SL_{2}(13)\trianglelefteqslant G\leqslant\Aut(\Gamma)
. Then

is not

-flag-transitive.

Proof.

Note that if
\Gamma
is
G
-flag-transitive and

, then the stabiliser

must act transitively on the set of edges through

, where

. Inside
\GaL_{6}(3)

is its own normaliser, and hence

∎

4
One dimensional semi-linear case

In this section
V=\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
, considered an
n
-dimensional vector space over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, and
\Gamma
is a
q
-graph such that
\Aut(\Gamma)\leqslant\GaL_{1}(q^{n})
. Since the multiplicative group
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
stabilises every
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-subspace of
V
, necessarily
\Aut(\Gamma)
contains
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
. Below we outline the well-known theory of transitive subgroups of
\GaL_{1}(q^{n})
in terms of a canonical description via certain generators.

Let
q=p^{t}
, with
p
prime, let
\omega
be a primitive element of the
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
, and let
\alpha
be a generator of the Galois group
{\rm Gal}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}/\mathbb{F}_{p})
. Then
G\leqslant\GaL_{1}(q^{n})
can be written uniquely, see Foulser
[
14
, Lemma 4.1]
or
[
21
, Lemma 4.4]
, as
\langle\omega^{d},\alpha^{s}\omega^{e}\rangle
, where the integers
d,e,s
satisfy the following:

(1)

d>0
and
d\mid q^{n}-1
.

(2)

s>0
and
s\mid nt
.

(3)

0\leqslant e<d
and
d\mid e(q^{n}-1)/(p^{s}-1)
.

Foulser’s group elements act from the left, while here we are using exponential notation, so we have replaced
\omega^{e}\alpha^{s}
by
\alpha^{s}\omega^{e}
in order to preserve the validity of those results.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\Gamma=(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{E})
be a
k
-regular and
G
-flag-transitive
q
-graph on
V=\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
, where
G\leqslant\GaL_{1}(q^{n})
and
k\geqslant 2
. Then
k=2
,
q\in\{2,8\}
, and
\Gamma
is as in Example
3.3
.

Proof.

Let
q=p^{t}
where
p
is prime, and let
\alpha
generate the Galois group of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
.

Consider the vertex
X=\mathbb{F}_{q}\leqslant V
, and let
\mathcal{E}_{X}
be the set of edges through
X
. Then
\mathcal{E}_{X}
is a set of
2
-dimensional
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-subspaces of
N(X)
, which intersect pairwise in
X
and partition
N(X)\setminus X
, as a result
|\mathcal{E}_{X}|=q^{k-1}+\cdots+q+1
.

We will apply the discussion preceding this lemma to the stabiliser
G_{X}
. Since
G_{X}
fixes
X
and we may assume that the scalars
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
form a subgroup of
G_{X}
, we have that
G_{X}\cap\GL_{1}(q^{n})=\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
. Thus

G_{X}=\langle\omega^{(q^{n}-1)/(q-1)},\alpha^{s}\omega^{e}\rangle,

for some integers
e,s
with
0\leqslant e<(q^{n}-1)/(q-1)
. Since
1^{g}\in X\setminus\{0\}
for any
g\in G_{X}
, we deduce that
1^{\alpha^{s}\omega^{e}}=\omega^{e}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
. Hence
e
is a multiple of
(q^{n}-1)/(q-1)
, and the condition
0\leqslant e<(q^{n}-1)/(q-1)
then implies that
e=0
. It follows that

G_{X}=\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}\rtimes\langle\alpha^{s}\rangle.

Since elements of
\mathcal{E}_{X}
are
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-subspaces, they are invariant under
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}\lhd G_{X}
. By the definition of flag-transitivity,
H=\langle\alpha^{s}\rangle
acts transitively on
\mathcal{E}_{X}
. By the orbit stabiliser-theorem,
|\mathcal{E}_{X}|=(q^{k}-1)/(q-1)
divides
|H|
.

Let
v\in N(X)\setminus X
be a vector. Let
q_{0}=p^{s}
, so that
v^{H}=\{v,v^{q_{0}},\ldots,v^{q_{0}^{\ell-1}}\}
for some
\ell
that is divisible by
(q^{k}-1)/(q-1)
. By
[
22
, Theorem 3.33]
, the minimal polynomial for
v
over
\mathbb{F}_{q_{0}}
has degree
\ell
, and
\langle v^{H}\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q_{0}}}=\mathbb{F}_{q_{0}^{\ell}}
. Let
r=\gcd(t,s)
, so that
\mathbb{F}_{p^{r}}
is the largest field contained in both
\mathbb{F}_{q}
and
\mathbb{F}_{q_{0}}
. Then, since
\langle v^{H}\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{p^{r}}}\leqslant\langle v^{H}\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}\leqslant N(X)
, we deduce that
N(X)
has
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-dimension at least
\ell r/t
, or at least
\ell/t
, so that
k+1\geqslant\ell/t
. Hence
(k+1)t\geqslant q^{k-1}+\cdots+q+1
. The equation of the previous sentence holds only when
k=p=2
and
t\in\{1,2,3\}
.

The cases
t=1
and
t=3
arise in Example
3.3
. Moreover, the parameters and the argument in this proof directly imply that for
t=1
we have
N(X)=\mathbb{F}_{8}
, and for
t=3
we have
N(X)=\mathbb{F}_{2^{9}}
. Hence
\Gamma
is as in Example
3.3
when
t\in\{1,3\}
. Suppose that
k=p=t=2
. Then
\mathbb{F}_{32}
is contained in
N(X)
, and
N(X)
should be an
\mathbb{F}_{4}
-vector space of dimension
3
. However,
\langle\mathbb{F}_{32}\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{4}}
has
\mathbb{F}_{4}
-dimension
5
, giving a contradiction and completing the proof.
∎

G\leqslant\GaL_{a}(q^{b})
. Furthermore, suppose that there exists some
X\in\mathcal{V}
such that
N(X)
is contained in the
K
-span
\langle X\rangle_{K}
. Then
\Gamma
is as in Example
3.3
.

Proof.

Let
W=\langle X\rangle_{K}
and let
\Gamma_{W}
be the
q
-graph on
W
with edge set
\mathcal{E}_{W}=\{U\in\mathcal{E}\mid U\leqslant W\}
. Since
G
is a subgroup of
\GaL_{a}(q^{b})
, we have that the partition of
V\setminus\{0\}
induced by the
1
-dimensional
K
-subspaces of
V
is preserved by
G
. Together with the fact that
G
acts transitively on
\mathcal{V}
, this implies that
\binom{W}{1}_{q}
is a block of imprimitivity for the action of
G
on
\mathcal{V}
. In particular,
G_{W}
acts transitively on
\binom{W}{1}_{q}
. Moreover, since
G_{X}
fixes the
K
-span of
X
, that is, fixes
W
, we deduce that
G_{X}\leqslant G_{W}
. By the flag-transitivity of
\Gamma
, we have that
G_{X}^{W}
acts transitively on the set of edges of
\Gamma_{W}
incident with
X
, and thus
\Gamma_{W}
is
G_{W}^{W}
-flag-transitive. Since the faithful action of the stabiliser of
W
inside
\GaL_{a}(q^{b})
is isomorphic to
\GaL_{1}(q^{b})
, we have that
G_{W}^{W}\leqslant\GaL_{1}(q^{b})
and hence, by Lemma
4.1
,
\Gamma_{W}
is as in Example
3.3
. The fact that
X
was chosen arbitrarily then implies that the set of neighbourhoods of
\Gamma
are precisely the elements of the
3
-spread of
V
preserved by
\GaL_{a}(q^{b})
. It follows that
\Gamma
itself is as in Example
3.3
, the result holds.
∎

5.2
Special linear groups over extension fields

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
F=\mathbb{F}_{q}
, let
K=\mathbb{F}_{q^{b}}
where
b>1
, and suppose that
\Gamma
is a non-trivial,
k
-regular and
G
-flag-transitive
q
-graph such that
\SL_{a}(q^{b})\trianglelefteqslant G
, where
n=ab
. Then
\Gamma
is as in Example
3.3
.

Proof.

Let
\Gamma=(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{E})
, let
e_{1},\ldots,e_{a}
be a
K
-basis for
V
, let
X=\langle e_{1}\rangle_{F}\in\mathcal{V}
, and let
U=\langle X\rangle_{K}
. The stabiliser
G_{X}
contains a subgroup of shape

q^{b(a-1)}.((q-1)\times\SL_{a-1}(q^{b}))\,,

with elements of the form

A=\begin{bmatrix}\lambda&\mathbf{0}\\
\mathbf{u}^{\top}&M\end{bmatrix}

where
\lambda\in F^{\times}
,
\mathbf{u}\in K^{a-1}
and
\det(M)=\lambda^{-1}
(note that matrices are acting on the right here via
x\mapsto xA
). First note that
U
is an
FG_{X}
-submodule of
V
and, since
b>1
,
X
is a proper
FG_{X}
-submodule of
U
. We claim that any
FG_{X}
-submodule of
V
intersecting
V\setminus U
non-trivially also contains
U
, in which case Lemma
2.2
implies that
N(X)
is a submodule of
U
. Let
v\in V\setminus U
. Since
G_{X}
contains a copy of
\SL_{a-1}(q^{b})
that fixes
e_{1}
and acts transitively on the non-zero vectors of
\langle e_{2},\ldots,e_{a}\rangle
, we may assume that
v=\alpha e_{1}+e_{2}
for some
\alpha\in K
. Let
\mathbf{u}=(\beta,0\ldots,0)
, where
\beta\in K
, and let

B=\begin{bmatrix}1&\mathbf{0}\\
\mathbf{u}^{\top}&I_{a-1}\end{bmatrix}.

Then
B\in G_{X}
and
vB=(\alpha+\beta)e_{1}+e_{2}
. Thus, any
FG_{X}
-submodule
U^{\prime}\leqslant V
with
v\in U^{\prime}
also contains
v-vB=-\beta e_{1}
. Since
\beta\in K
was arbitrary, we have that
U\leqslant U^{\prime}
, and the result then follows from Lemma
5.1
.
∎

5.3
Symplectic groups over extension fields

Let
F=\mathbb{F}_{q}
, let
K=\mathbb{F}_{q^{b}}
, and let
f
be a symplectic form on
V=K^{a}
, where
a
is even. If
b=1
, then taking the edge-set
\mathcal{E}
to the set of all isotropic
2
-spaces gives a strongly regular
q
-graph, as in Example
3.4
. In this section, we show that these are the only examples of
G
-flag-transitive, regular
q
-graphs with
\Sp_{a}(q^{b})\trianglelefteqslant G
.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
F=\mathbb{F}_{q}
, let
K=\mathbb{F}_{q^{b}}
, and suppose that
\Gamma
is a non-trivial,
k
-regular and
G
-flag-transitive
q
-graph of order
n
, where
\Sp_{a}(q^{b})\trianglelefteqslant G
,
a
is even and at least
4
,
n=ab
, and
k\geqslant 2
. Then either
b=1
and the neighbourhood of
X
is equal to its dual under a symplectic polarity (as in Example
3.4
), or
b>1
and
\Gamma
is as in Example
3.3
.

Proof.

Following the notation in
[
27
, Section 3.5]
, let
e_{1},\ldots,e_{a/2},f_{a/2},\ldots,f_{1}
be a symplectic
K
-basis for
V
. Assume
X=\langle e_{1}\rangle_{F}
, and let
W=\langle e_{1},f_{1}\rangle_{K}
, so that

W^{\perp}=\langle e_{2},\ldots,e_{a/2},f_{a/2},\ldots,f_{2}\rangle_{K}

and
V=W\oplus W^{\perp}
.

First, suppose that
b=1
, so that
F=K
. Then there is a copy of
\Sp_{a-2}(q)
inside
G_{X}
that acts transitively on
W^{\perp}\setminus\{0\}
, and hence
W^{\perp}
is an irreducible
FG_{X}
-submodule of
V
. Setting
N(X)=X\oplus W^{\perp}
, which is equal to
X^{\perp}
, we obtain Example
3.4
. Since
V=W\oplus W^{\perp}
, we see that
X\oplus W^{\perp}
is the only
FG_{X}
-submodule of
V
that contains
X
and has dimension at least
3
, and so Example
3.4
is the only example arising for
b=1
.

Now assume that
b>1
. Let
Y=\langle X\rangle_{K}
, and note that
Y
is an
FG_{X}
-submodule of
V
. We claim that any
FG_{X}
-submodule
U
of
V
that intersects
V\setminus Y
non-trivially contains
Y
. If this holds, then it follows from Lemma
2.2
that
N(X)\leqslant Y
. To prove the claim, assume
U
is such a submodule of
V
. Recall that
V=W\oplus W^{\perp}
and that
G_{X}
contains a copy of
\Sp_{a-2}(q^{b})
that fixes
e_{1}
and
f_{1}
and acts transitively on
W^{\perp}\setminus\{0\}
. Thus we may assume that
u=\alpha e_{1}+\beta f_{1}+e_{2}\in U
, where
\alpha,\beta\in K
. For each
\lambda\in K
,
G_{X}
contains the elements
y_{12}(\lambda)
, as in
[
27
, Eq. (3.24)]
, mapping
f_{1}\mapsto f_{1}+\lambda e_{2}
,
f_{2}\mapsto f_{2}+\lambda e_{1}
, and acting trivially on the remaining basis elements. Thus,
u^{y_{12}(\beta^{-1})}-u=e_{2}
is contained in
U
. Also, for each
\lambda\in K
, we have that
G_{X}
contains the elements
x_{12}(\lambda)
, as in
[
27
, Eq. (3.24)]
, mapping
f_{1}\mapsto f_{1}+\lambda f_{2}
,
e_{2}\mapsto e_{2}+\lambda e_{1}
, and acting trivially on the remaining basis elements. It follows that
e_{2}^{x_{12}(\lambda)}-e_{2}=\lambda e_{1}
is in
U
, for each
\lambda\in K
, and thus
Y\leqslant U
. Applying Lemma
5.1
completes the proof.
∎

5.4
Lie-type group
G_{2}(q)

While the group
G_{2}(q)
acts naturally on a 7-dimensional space, in characteristic
2
it has an exceptional embedding into 6 dimensions, as a subgroup of
\Sp_{6}(q)
. As a result, the edges of a
G_{2}(q)
-invariant
q
-graph will be contained in those of the corresponding symplectic
q
-graph, described in the previous section.

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
F=\mathbb{F}_{q}
with
q
even, let
K=\mathbb{F}_{q^{b}}
, and suppose that
\Gamma
is a non-trivial,
k
-regular and
G
-flag-transitive
q
-graph of order
n
, where
G_{2}(q^{b})^{\prime}\trianglelefteqslant G
,
n=6b
, and
k\geqslant 2
. Then either
b=1
and
\Gamma
is as in Example
3.5
, or
b>1
and
\Gamma
is as in Example
3.3
.

Proof.

Let
F=\mathbb{F}_{q}
and let
K=\mathbb{F}_{q^{b}}
. We will follow the description of
G_{2}(q^{b})
given by Wilson in
[
27
, Section 4.3.5]
. Noting that when
q
is even
G_{2}(q^{b})
embeds into
\Sp_{6}(q^{b})
, and this allows us to work with
V=\langle x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{6},x_{7},x_{8}\rangle_{K}
(effectively ignoring
x_{4},x_{5}
). Also, we will be particularly interested in the generators
A(\lambda),B(\lambda),C(\lambda),D(\lambda),E(\lambda),F(\lambda)
of the unipotent subgroup
U
of
G
as given in
[
27
, (4.34)]
). It is worth pointing out here that the unipotent subgroup
U
is contained in
G_{X}
, and also that
1=-1
, since we are working in characteristic
2
.

Let
X=\langle x_{1}\rangle_{F}
, let
Y=\langle x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}\rangle_{K}
, let
Z=\langle x_{1}\rangle_{K}
, and note that under the symplectic form on
V
that is preserved by
G
we have
Z^{\perp}=\langle x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{6},x_{7}\rangle_{K}
. Since
G_{X}
preserves
Y
, we have that the following flag is preserved by
G_{X}
:

Z<Y<Z^{\perp}<V.

Suppose
b=1
, so that
F=K
and
X=Z
. Then
q^{2+3}:\GL_{2}(q)\leqslant G_{X}
(see
[
27
, Section 4.3.5]
). Moreover, since
\GL_{2}(q)
acts transitively on the non-zero vectors of each of the quotient spaces
Y/X
and
X^{\perp}/Y
, we deduce that
V
is a uniserial
FG_{X}
-module with
\mathbf{0}<X<Y<X^{\perp}<V
being the corresponding maximal chain of submodules. By Lemma
2.2
, we have that
N(X)=Y
and hence
\Gamma
is as in Example
3.5
.

We assume from now on that
b\geqslant 2
, in which case
G_{X}
no longer contains
\GL_{2}(q^{b})
. Let
W
be an
FG_{X}
-submodule of
V
such that
W\cap(V\setminus Z)
is non-trivial. Then we claim that
Z\leqslant W
. Let
v_{1}=u_{1}+\beta_{8}x_{8}
, where
u_{1}\in Z^{\perp}
and
\beta_{8}\in K^{\times}
, so that
v_{1}
can be considered to be an arbitrary element of
V\setminus Z^{\perp}
. Then
v_{1}^{A(1)}+v_{1}=\beta_{1}x_{1}+\beta_{8}x_{2}\in W
for some
\beta_{1}\in K
. Hence
v_{1}\in W
implies that
v_{1}
intersects
Y\setminus Z
non-trivially. Let
v_{2}=u_{2}+\beta_{6}x_{6}+\beta_{7}x_{7}\in Z^{\perp}\setminus Y
, where
\beta_{6},\beta_{7}\in K
and
(\beta_{6},\beta_{7})\neq(0,0)
. Then
v_{2}^{C(1)}+v_{2}=\beta_{6}x_{2}+\beta_{7}x_{3}
. Thus,
v_{2}\in W
implies
W
intersects
Y\setminus Z
non-trivially, and hence there exists some
v_{3}\in(W\cap Y)\setminus Z
. Let
v_{3}=\gamma_{1}x_{1}+\gamma_{2}x_{2}+\gamma_{3}x_{3}
, where
\gamma_{i}\in K
for
i\in\{1,2,3\}
and
(\gamma_{2},\gamma_{3})\neq(0,0)
. Suppose that
\gamma_{3}\neq 0
. Then
v_{3}^{E(1)}+v_{3}=\gamma_{3}x_{2}
, which implies that, upon possibly replacing
v_{3}
with
\gamma_{3}x_{2}
, we may assume that
\gamma_{2}\neq 0
and
\gamma_{3}=0
. Supposing we have done such a substitution (if necessary), then for each
\lambda\in K
we have
v_{3}^{F(\lambda)}+v_{3}=\lambda\gamma_{2}x_{1}\in W
, and hence
W
contains
Z
. The result then follows from Lemma
5.1
.
∎

6
Main proofs

We are now in a position to prove our main results, first dealing with regular, flag-transitive
q
-graphs.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

By assumption,
\Gamma=(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{E})
is a
k
-regular and
G
-flag-transitive
q
-graph on
V=\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
where
\mathcal{E}
is neither empty, nor the set of all
2
-spaces of
V
. If
k=1
, then the neighbourhood of each vertex is a
2
-space, so that each vertex is contained in precisely one edge, and
\Gamma
is as in Example
3.2
, and part (1) holds. Assume from now on that
k\geqslant 2
. If
n\leqslant 4
, then Lemma
2.4
implies that part (3) holds, and so we may now also assume that
n\geqslant 5
. Since
\Gamma
is
G
-flag-transitive, it follows that
G
acts transitively on the
1
-spaces of
V
, and hence Theorem
2.5
may be applied. If
G\leqslant\GaL_{1}(q^{n})
then it follows from Lemma
4.1
that
k=2
,
q\in\{2,8\}
and
\Gamma
is as in part (2). In the remainder of the proof we consider each of the cases Theorem
2.5
(1)–(4).

Suppose that
n=ab
, for some integer
a>1
, and that
\SL_{a}(q^{b})\trianglelefteqslant G
. Note that if
b=1
then
G
acts transitively on
{\binom{V}{2}}_{q}
, and so we may also assume that
b>1
. By Lemma
5.2
,
\Gamma
is as in part (2). Suppose that
n=ab
, with
a
even,
\Sp_{a}(q^{b})\trianglelefteqslant G
, and let
K=\mathbb{F}_{q^{b}}
. By Lemma
5.3
, part (2) or part (3) holds. Next, suppose that
n=6b
,
q
is even,
G_{2}(q^{b})^{\prime}\trianglelefteqslant G
, and
K=\mathbb{F}_{q^{b}}
. Then Lemma
5.4
implies that part (2) or part (4) holds. Finally, the case
q=3
,
n=6
and
\SL_{2}(13)\trianglelefteqslant G
is ruled out by Corollary
2.6
, completing the proof.
∎

Next we prove our main result concerning regular, symmetric
q
-graphs.

Proof of Corollary
1.3
.

Suppose that
\Gamma
is a
k
-regular and
G
-symmetric
q
-graph on
V=\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
. Then
\Gamma
is necessarily
G
-flag-transitive, so we may apply Theorem
1.2
. Note that since a pair of vertices define a unique
2
-space in
V
and
G
is contained in
\GaL_{n}(q)
, the stabiliser
G_{U}
of an edge
U\in\mathcal{E}
must act
2
-transitively on the set of vertices incident with
U
. Conversely, given that
\Gamma
is
G
-flag-transitivity, this condition is sufficient to imply that
\Gamma
is
G
-symmetric. The edge stabiliser
G_{U}
is
2
-transitive on the vertices incident with
U
if and only if either
\Gamma
is as in Theorem
1.2
(1) with
q=2
whence
G_{U}^{U}=\GaL_{1}(4)\cong\s_{3}
, or as in Theorem
1.2
(3) or (4), where
\SL_{2}(q)\leqslant G_{U}^{U}
.
∎

Finally, we complete the paper by proving that every regular, flag-transitive
q
-graph has a symmetric graph as its classical counterpart.

Proof of Proposition
1.5
.

By Theorem
1.2
and Corollary
1.3
, either
\Gamma
is as in Example
3.2
or
3.3
, in which case
\Gamma_{C}
is a union of cliques and thus a symmetric graph, or
\Gamma
is symmetric, and
\Gamma_{C}
is symmetric by Corollary
1.4
.
∎

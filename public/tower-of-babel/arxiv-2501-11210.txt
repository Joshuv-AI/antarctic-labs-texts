Schnorr randomness and Effective Bayesian Consistency and Inconsistency
Simon M. Huttegger, Sean Walsh, Francesca Zaffora Blando

Abstract
. We study Doob’s Consistency Theorem and Freedman’s Inconsistency Theorem from the vantage point of computable probability and algorithmic randomness. We show that the Schnorr random elements of the parameter space are computably consistent, when there is a map from the sample space to the parameter space satisfying many of the same properties as limiting relative frequencies. We show that the generic inconsistency in Freedman’s Theorem is effectively comeager, which implies the existence of computable parameters which are not computably consistent. Taken together, this work provides a computability-theoretic solution to Diaconis and Freedman’s problem of “know[ing] for which [parameters] θ \theta the rule [Bayes’ rule] is consistent” ( [ 4 , 4] ), and it strengthens recent similar results of Takahashi [ 31 ] on Martin-Löf randomness in Cantor space.

Introduction

A statistical model consists of a sample space and a collection of probability distributions on the sample space, one of which is hypothesized to be the data-generating process. The distributions are often indexed by a parameter space, which is an assumption that we will make throughout this paper. In a setting where the number of sample observations grows without bound, statisticians consider the question of whether estimates are
consistent
, where, loosely speaking, an estimate is consistent if it is guaranteed to identify the true parameter.

Consistency is of major importance in classical statistics. For instance, Barnett
[
1
, p. 135]
remarks that “[c]onsistency is generally regarded as an essential property of a reasonable estimator.” As Diaconis and Freedman
[
4
]
point out, it also plays an important role in Bayesian statistics and thus constitutes a point of contact between classical and Bayesian statistics.

Theorem 1.14

Let the parameter space

in

Definition 7.6

(Computably Compact)

A computable Polish space

if there is a partial computable function which when given an index for a computable sequence of c.e. opens

, if the sequence covers

then the computable function halts and returns an

(This definition is implied by that given in

Proposition 7.7

If

is computable continuous, then both

9.
Effective Freedman inconsistency

We begin with the proof of Theorem
1.14
, which effectivizes the proof in
[
10
, Theorem 6.12]
. In this theorem, recall that the parameter space
\Omega
is
\mathbb{S}_{\infty}
, and so we just write
\mathbb{S}_{\infty}
instead of
\Omega
in this section.

Note that we have the formula
p(\sigma\hskip-2.84526pt\mid\hskip-2.84526pt\theta)=\prod_{i<\left|\sigma\right|}\theta(\sigma(i))
for the likelihood, and that
\theta\mapsto\prod_{i<\left|\sigma\right|}\theta(\sigma(i))
is computable continuous from
[0,1]^{\mathbb{N}}
to
[0,1]
. By Proposition
7.3
and Remark
7.2
(
1
), we have that
\theta\mapsto p(\sigma\hskip-2.84526pt\mid\hskip-2.84526pt\theta)
is computable continuous from
\mathbb{S}_{\infty}
to
[0,1]
, which puts us in a position to later apply Proposition
8.3
.

Further,
\mathbb{S}_{\infty}^{+}:=\{\theta\in\mathbb{S}_{\infty}:\forall\;i\geq 0\;\theta(i)>0\}
is effectively comeager in
\mathbb{S}_{\infty}
. For, the set
\{\theta\in\mathbb{S}_{\infty}:\theta(i)>0\}
is c.e. open and dense for each
i\geq 0
: for, it is
\pi_{i}^{-1}((0,1])
, where
\pi_{i}:\mathbb{S}_{\infty}\rightarrow[0,1]
is the projection map, which is computable continuous and computable open by Proposition
7.8
. By the Baire Category Theorem,
\mathbb{S}_{\infty}^{+}
is dense in
\mathbb{S}_{\infty}
. By the Effective Baire Category Theorem (
[
12
, Theorem 5.1.2]
) we can find a uniformly computable sequence of points
\theta_{0}^{+},\theta_{1}^{+},\ldots
in
\mathbb{S}_{\infty}^{+}
which is dense in
\mathbb{S}_{\infty}
.

Further,
\mathbb{S}_{\infty}^{-}=\{\theta\in\mathbb{S}_{\infty}:\exists\;i\geq 0\;\theta(i)=0\}
is dense since it is a superset of the countable dense set.

Finally, let
D_{i}
be the set of
\sum_{j=1}^{m}q_{j}\delta_{\theta_{j}}
, where
0<q_{j}<1
is rational and
\sum_{j=1}^{m}q_{j}=1
, and
\theta_{1}=\theta^{+}_{i}
and for
1<j\leq m
one has that
\theta_{j}
is from
\mathbb{S}_{\infty}^{-}
. It is not hard to see, by a simple classical argument, that this is dense in
\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{S}_{\infty})
.

Then one has:

Proposition 9.1
.

Let
\theta_{0}
be from
\mathbb{S}_{\infty}^{+}
. Let
p
be from
D_{i}
. Then for
p(\cdot\hskip-2.84526pt\mid\hskip-2.84526pt{\theta_{0}})
-a.s. many
x
in
\mathcal{X}
one has
p(\cdot\hskip-2.84526pt\mid\hskip-2.84526ptx_{n})\rightarrow\delta_{\theta_{i}^{+}}
in
\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{S}_{\infty})
.

Proof.

Let
p=\sum_{j=1}^{m}q_{j}\delta_{\theta_{j}}
, where this is as in the definition of
D_{i}
in the previous paragraph. The non-trivial case is
m>1
. For each
1<j\leq m
, choose
k_{j}\geq 0
with
\theta_{j}(k_{j})=0
. Let
\epsilon=\prod_{j=2}^{m}\theta_{0}(k_{j})
, which since
\theta_{0}
is in
\mathbb{S}_{\infty}^{+}
is necessarily
>0
. Let
A=\bigcap_{\ell}A_{\ell}
, where
A_{\ell}=\{x\in\mathcal{X}:\forall\;1<j\leq m\;\exists\;n\geq\ell\;x(n)=k_{j}\}
. Then
A
is a tailset, i.e. if
x,x^{\prime}
differ by finitely much and one is in
A
, then the other is as well. Since
p(\cdot\hskip-2.84526pt\mid\hskip-2.84526pt{\theta_{0}})
is a product measure, by the zero-one law,
A
either has measure zero or one. We claim it has measure one. If not, then it has measure zero. Then choose sufficiently large
\ell\geq 0
such that
p(A_{\ell}\hskip-2.84526pt\mid\hskip-2.84526pt\theta_{0})<\epsilon
. Consider the event
B=\{x:x(\ell+2)=k_{2},x(\ell+3)=k_{3},\ldots,x(\ell+m)=k_{m}\}
. Then by independence we have
p(B\hskip-2.84526pt\mid\hskip-2.84526pt\theta_{0})=\prod_{j=2}^{m}\theta_{0}(k_{j})
, which is
\epsilon
, a contradiction to
B
being a subset of
A_{\ell}
. Hence rather we have that
p(A\hskip-2.84526pt\mid\hskip-2.84526pt\theta_{0})=1
.

By Portmanteau it suffices to check that for all
x
in
A
and all open
U
containing
\theta_{1}
one has
\liminf_{n}p(U\hskip-2.84526pt\mid\hskip-2.84526ptx_{n})=1
. Suppose not. Then there is
x
in
A
and open
U
containing
\theta_{1}
such that
\liminf_{n}p(U\hskip-2.84526pt\mid\hskip-2.84526ptx_{n})<1
. Then there is
\ell_{0}\geq 0
such that for all
\ell\geq\ell_{0}
one has
\inf_{n\geq\ell}p(U\hskip-2.84526pt\mid\hskip-2.84526ptx_{n})<1
. Since
x
is in
A_{\ell_{0}}
, choose
\ell>\ell_{0}
such that for all
1<j\leq m
one has that
x(\ell^{\prime})=k_{j}
for some
\ell^{\prime}
in the interval
[\ell_{0},\ell)
. This has the consequence that for all
n\geq\ell
one has that
p(x_{n}\hskip-2.84526pt\mid\hskip-2.84526pt\theta_{j})=0
for all
1<j\leq m
. Hence for any
n\geq\ell
and any event
C
, we have that

\int_{C}p(x_{n}\hskip-2.84526pt\mid\hskip-2.84526pt\theta)\;dp(\theta)=\sum_{j=1}^{m}q_{j}\cdot I_{C}(\theta_{j})\cdot p(x_{n}\hskip-2.84526pt\mid\hskip-2.84526pt\theta_{j})=q_{1}\cdot I_{C}(\theta_{1})\cdot p(x_{n}\hskip-2.84526pt\mid\hskip-2.84526pt\theta_{1})=q_{1}\cdot I_{C}(\theta_{i}^{+})\cdot p(x_{n}\hskip-2.84526pt\mid\hskip-2.84526pt\theta_{i}^{+})

When we set
C=\mathbb{S}_{\infty}
, then we get a value
>0
since
\theta_{i}^{+}
comes from
\mathbb{S}_{\infty}^{+}
. For
n\geq\ell
:

p(U\hskip-2.84526pt\mid\hskip-2.84526ptx_{n})=\frac{\int_{U}p(x_{n}\hskip-2.84526pt\mid\hskip-2.84526pt\theta)\;dp(\theta)}{\int_{\mathbb{S}_{\infty}}p(x_{n}\mid\theta)\;dp(\theta)}=\frac{q_{1}\cdot I_{U}(\theta_{i}^{+})\cdot p(x_{n}\hskip-2.84526pt\mid\hskip-2.84526pt\theta_{i}^{+})}{q_{1}\cdot p(x_{n}\hskip-2.84526pt\mid\hskip-2.84526pt\theta_{i}^{+})}=I_{U}(\theta_{i}^{+})=1

and this contradicts that
\inf_{n\geq\ell}p(U\hskip-2.84526pt\mid\hskip-2.84526ptx_{n})<1
.

∎

For each
i\geq 0
and each
\theta_{i}^{+}
on our list, we construct a uniformly computable continuous sequence
\chi_{i,k}:\mathbb{S}_{\infty}\rightarrow[0,1]
such that (i)
\chi_{i,k}(\theta_{i}^{+})=1
for all
k\geq 0
, and (ii) if
\theta^{+}_{i}
is in the open
V
, then eventually
\chi_{i,k}\leq I_{V}
. Let
\epsilon_{k}\rightarrow 0
be a computable sequence of positive rationals which is strictly decreasing. Let
V_{i,k}=B(\theta^{+}_{i},\epsilon_{k})
, noting that
\overline{V_{i,k+1}}\subseteq B[\theta^{+}_{i},\epsilon_{k+1}]\subseteq B(\theta^{+}_{i},\epsilon_{k})=V_{i,k}
. Since
B[\theta^{+}_{i},\epsilon_{k+1}]
and
\mathbb{S}_{\infty}\setminus B(\theta^{+}_{i},\epsilon_{k})
are effectively closed and disjoint, by the effective Urysohn Lemma (
[
29
, Lemma II.7.(1), Lemma II.7.3 p. 90]
) there are uniformly computable continuous
\chi_{i,k}:\mathbb{S}_{\infty}\rightarrow[0,1]
such that
\chi_{i,k}\upharpoonright B[\theta^{+}_{i},\epsilon_{k+1}]=1
and
\chi_{i,k}\upharpoonright(\mathbb{S}_{\infty}\setminus B(\theta^{+}_{i},\epsilon_{k}))=0
. Then
\chi_{i,k}\upharpoonright V_{i,k+1}=1
and
\chi_{i,k}\upharpoonright(\mathbb{S}_{\infty}\setminus V_{i,k})=0
.

By Proposition
8.3
(
4
), the following are c.e. opens, where
0<\epsilon<1
is rational:

U_{i,\epsilon,m,k}=\bigcup_{n\geq m}\{(p,\theta):\int_{\mathcal{X}}\int_{\mathbb{S}_{\infty}}\chi_{i,k}(\theta^{\prime})\;dp(\cdot\hskip-2.84526pt\mid\hskip-2.84526ptx_{n})(\theta^{\prime})\;dp(\cdot\hskip-2.84526pt\mid\hskip-2.84526pt{\theta})(x)>1-\epsilon\}

(9.1)

We show that each of these c.e. opens is dense in
\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{S}_{\infty})\times\mathbb{S}_{\infty}
. Suppose that a pair in
\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{S}_{\infty})\times\mathbb{S}_{\infty}
is given. Find
(p,\theta_{0})
in
D_{i}\times\mathbb{S}_{\infty}^{+}
arbitrarily close to the given pair. We argue that the pair
(p,\theta_{0})
is in
U_{i,\epsilon,m,k}
. By Proposition
9.1
, one has that
p(\cdot\hskip-2.84526pt\mid\hskip-2.84526ptx_{n})\rightarrow\delta_{\theta_{i}^{+}}
for
p(\cdot\hskip-2.84526pt\mid\hskip-2.84526pt{\theta_{0}})
-a.s. many
x
in
\mathcal{X}
. By the Portmanteau Theorem, we have
\int_{\mathbb{S}_{\infty}}\chi_{i,k}(\theta^{\prime})\;dp(\cdot\hskip-2.84526pt\mid\hskip-2.84526ptx_{n})(\theta^{\prime})\rightarrow\int_{\mathbb{S}_{\infty}}\chi_{i,k}(\theta^{\prime})\;d\delta_{\theta_{i}^{+}}(\theta^{\prime})
for
p(\cdot\hskip-2.84526pt\mid\hskip-2.84526pt{\theta_{0}})
-a.s. many
x
in
\mathcal{X}
. By the property (i) of
\chi_{i,k}
above, we have
\int_{\mathbb{S}_{\infty}}\chi_{i,k}(\theta^{\prime})\;d\delta_{\theta_{i}^{+}}(\theta^{\prime})=\chi_{i,k}(\theta_{i}^{+})=1
. Hence
\int_{\mathbb{S}_{\infty}}\chi_{i,k}(\theta^{\prime})\;dp(\cdot\hskip-2.84526pt\mid\hskip-2.84526ptx_{n})(\theta^{\prime})\rightarrow 1
for
p(\cdot\hskip-2.84526pt\mid\hskip-2.84526pt{\theta_{0}})
-a.s. many
x
in
\mathcal{X}
. By applying the Dominated Converence Theorem with respect to
p(\cdot\hskip-2.84526pt\mid\hskip-2.84526pt{\theta_{0}})
, we have
\lim_{n}\int_{\mathcal{X}}\int_{\mathbb{S}_{\infty}}\chi_{i,k}(\theta^{\prime})\;dp(\cdot\hskip-2.84526pt\mid\hskip-2.84526ptx_{n})(\theta^{\prime})\;dp(\cdot\hskip-2.84526pt\mid\hskip-2.84526pt{\theta_{0}})(x)=1
. This then puts the pair
(p,\theta_{0})
in
U_{i,\epsilon,m,k}
. Thus indeed
U_{i,\epsilon,m,k}
is dense.

Let
V\subseteq\mathbb{S}_{\infty}
be non-empty c.e. open, let
0<\epsilon<1
be rational and consider

\{(p,\theta):\limsup_{n}\int_{\mathcal{X}}\int_{\mathbb{S}_{\infty}}I_{V}(\theta^{\prime})\;dp(\cdot\hskip-2.84526pt\mid\hskip-2.84526ptx_{n})(\theta^{\prime})\;\;dp(\cdot\hskip-2.84526pt\mid\hskip-2.84526pt{\theta})(x)>1-\epsilon\}

(9.2)

Suppose
(p,\theta)
is in
\bigcap_{i,\epsilon,m,k}U_{i,\epsilon,m,k}
. We argue that
(p,\theta)
is in the set in (
9.2
). Since
V
is non-empty, there is
\theta^{+}_{i}
in
V
. Then by the property (ii) of
\chi_{i,k}
above we have that there is
k_{0}\geq 0
such that for all
k\geq k_{0}
we have
\chi_{i,k}\leq I_{V}
. Then we are done since
(p,\theta)
is in
U_{i,\epsilon,m,k}
for all
m\geq 0
and all
k\geq k_{0}
.

Then by Proposition
8.2
, we have finished proving Theorem
1.14
.

It remains to prove Corollary
1.15
. By the Effective Baire Category Theorem (
[
12
, Theorem 5.1.2]
) and Theorem
1.14
, in any non-empty c.e. open in
\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{S}_{\infty})\times\mathbb{S}_{\infty}
we can find a computable point
(p,\theta_{0})
in
\bigcap_{i,\epsilon,m,k}U_{i,\epsilon,m,k}
. Using the end of the proof of Theorem
1.14
, starting at (

), together with Proposition
8.2
specialized to the computable prior

, we further get that
(p,\theta_{0})
is not computably consistent. Hence we have finished part (
1
). And part (
2
) follows from Proposition
7.9
and Theorem
1.9
.

Abstract
. In this paper, we prove a KAM theorem in a-posteriori format, using the parameterization method to look invariant tori in non-autonomous Hamiltonian systems with n n degrees of freedom that depend periodically or quasi-periodically (QP) on time, with ℓ \ell external frequencies. Such a system is described by a Hamiltonian function in the 2 ​ n 2n -dimensional phase space, ℳ {\mathcal{M}} , that depends also on ℓ \ell angles, φ ∈ 𝕋 ℓ \varphi\in{\mathbb{T}}^{\ell} . We take advantage of the fibbered structure of the extended phase space ℳ × 𝕋 ℓ {\mathcal{M}}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{\ell} . As a result of our approach, the parameterization of tori requires the last ℓ \ell variables, to be precise φ \varphi , while the first 2 ​ n 2n components are determined by an invariance equation. This reduction decreases the dimension of the problem where the unknown is a parameterization from 2 ​ ( n + ℓ ) 2(n+\ell) to 2 ​ n 2n . We employ a quasi-Newton method, in order to prove the KAM theorem. This iterative method begins with an initial parameterization of an approximately invariant torus, meaning it approximately satisfies the invariance equation The approximation is refined by applying corrections that reduce quadratically the invariance equation error. This process converges to a torus in a complex strip of size ρ ∞ \rho_{\infty} , provided suitable Diophantine ( γ , τ ) (\gamma,\tau) conditions and a non-degeneracy condition on the torsion are met. Given the nature of the proof, this provides a numerical method that can be effectively implemented on a computer, the details are given in the companion paper [ CHP25 ] . This approach leverages precision and efficiency to compute invariant tori.



1.
Introduction

The KAM Theory,
developed by Kolmogorov,
Arnold,
and Moser
[
Kol54
,
Arn63
,
Mös62
]
,
plays a crucial role in understanding the dynamics of Hamiltonian systems.
This theory states that,
certain invariant tori in an integrable system survive after small perturbations,
maintaining a quasiperiodic structure.
Despite its importance,
the perturbative nature of KAM theory limits its practical application,
as it does not always allow for the explicit calculation of invariant manifolds within the physically relevant regime.

However,
de la Llave,
González,
Jorba,
and Villanueva introduced the parameterization method in KAM Theory
[
dlLGJV05
]
,
which offers a significant improvement.
This method establishes connections between theoretical predictions and practical applications,
making it practical and accessible for real-world scenarios.
By solving an invariance equation to parameterize the invariant torus and employing iterative schemes with quasi-Newton methods,
the parameterization approach addresses the limitations of the classical perturbative methods.
This allows researchers to bridge the gap between theory and practice,
effectively applying KAM theory in various contexts.

For instance,
time-dependent Hamiltonians,
which naturally arise in astrodynamics and celestial mechanics,
provide a framework for studying the evolution of dynamical systems.
A fundamental class of these systems is known as the restricted
N
-body problems,
where a small-mass particle moves under the gravitational influence of a set of primary bodies without affecting their motion.

The Circular Restricted Three-Body Problem (CRTBP) is the simplest case,
where two primary bodies orbit each other in circular paths around their common center of mass while the third body moves within their gravitational field
[
SG69
]
.
A more general case is the Elliptic Restricted Three-Body Problem (ERTBP),
where the primary bodies follow elliptical orbits,
introducing explicit periodic time dependence.

The Quasi-Bicircular Problem (QBCP) further complicates the system by incorporating a third massive body in an approximately circular orbit,
leading to additional resonances
[
And98
,
CC07
]
.
These models are crucial for understanding dynamical stability,
the existence of invariant tori in near-integrable systems,
and the design of space missions

[
AS99
,
And03
]
.

Building on this,
Fernández-Mora,
Haro and Mondelo
[
FMHM24a
,
FMHM24b
]
introduce a method to compute parameterizations of partially hyperbolic invariant tori and their invariant bundles in non-autonomous quasi-periodic Hamiltonian systems.
The papers
[
FMHM24a
,
FMHM24b
]
generalize flow map parameterization techniques to the quasi-periodic setting.
Introduces fiberwise isotropic tori and discusses symplectic deformations and moment maps
to compute parameterizations of partially hyperbolic invariant tori and their invariant bundles in non-autonomous quasi-periodic Hamiltonian systems.
These constructions are vital for guaranteeing the existence of solutions to cohomological equations,
and their algorithms have been successfully applied to the elliptic restricted three-body problem to compute nonresonant 3-dimensional invariant tori and their invariant bundles around the
L_{1}
point.

Other scenarios where periodic and quasi-periodic Hamiltonian systems naturally emerge include Tokamaks, toroidal devices designed for magnetic confinement fusion.
Studies,
such as those
[
CVC+05
]
by Chandre,
Vittot,
Ciraolo,
Ghendrih,
and Lima,
or
[
dCN00
]
by del Castillo-Negrete,
highlight the dynamics of magnetic field lines and transport phenomena in these devices.
Understanding the stability and structure of invariant tori in this context is essential for effective plasma confinement and reducing chaotic transport.
All these considerations have motivated the present work.

We present a KAM theorem for quasiperiodically time-dependent systems in a-posteriori format,
using the techniques developed in
[
HCF+16
,
HL19
,
FH25
]
.

We present a proof in a-posteriori format using the parametrization method to obtain a Lagrangian torus in Hamiltonian systems that depend periodically
or quasi-periodically on time,
with external frequencies vector
\alpha\in{\mathbb{R}}^{\ell}
.
One of the advantages of this constructive approach is that the proof suggests an algorithm that is implemented numerically on a computer in
[
CHP25
]
.
In this work,
we also take advantage of the symplectic fibered structure of the extended phase space,
using the angles that describe the periodic or quasi-periodic external action.

When adopting the symplectic formalism,
the dimension of the extended phase space typically becomes
2(n+\ell)
.
However,
given our focus on systems with temporal dynamics that are either periodic or quasi-periodic,
we redefine the setup.
By introducing a variable
\varphi\in{\mathbb{T}}^{\ell}
,
which parametrizes the external action,
the dimension of the phase space is effectively reduced to
2n+\ell
.
This adjustment not only simplifies the representation but also aligns naturally with the underlying dynamics,
enabling a more efficient analysis of the system.

As a result of our approach,
the parameterization of the torus requires the last
\ell
variables to have prescribed periodic dynamics,
while the
n
first components are determined by an invariance equation.
This reduction allows us to bring down the dimension problem from
2(n+\ell)
to
2n
.
Moreover, we manage to obtain a symmetric expression for the torsion,
that does not require computing derivatives of known functions but only their products,
which represents a considerable numerical advantage.
This feature makes the corresponding numerical method very efficient.

The novel expression for the torsion,
given in equation (
4.26
),
has a significant advantage over equation (4.60) in
[
HL19
]
,
as it avoids using derivatives and is based solely on the product of functions.
This reformulation eliminates the need for Cauchy estimates,
leading to improved estimates of the quasi-Newton step for convergence.
From a numerical point of view,
this modification makes the algorithm more efficient,
reducing computational costs and favoring its implementation in high-precision computations,
see
[
CHP25
]
.
The proof follows an a-posteriori approach,
that employs a quasi-Newton method to establish the existence of invariant tori in Hamiltonian dynamical systems.
The iterative method starts with an initial parameterization of an approximately invariant torus,
in a space with complex strip of size
\rho
,
such that the invariance equation is approximately satisfied.
This approximation is improved by adding a correction that reduces the invariance error to an error which is quadratic with respect to the original error.
The process converges to a torus in a space with complex strip of size
\rho_{\infty}
,
under suitable Diophantine
(\gamma,\tau)
conditions and a non-degeneracy condition on the torsion,
which allows us to prove the persistence of the invariant tori,
if
\gamma^{-4}\rho^{-4\tau}
times the norm is quadratic with respect to the original error.
However,
with some additional effort,
the smallness factor can be relaxed to
\gamma^{-2}\rho^{-2\tau-1}
, like in
[
FH24
]
.
The nature of the proof is constructive so it leads quite naturally to a numerical algorithm
that can be implemented on a computer.
The framework is also suitable for obtaing results in different scales of Banach spaces using hard implicit function theorems and smothing
[
CDlL10
]
.
The theorem states that,
under necessary and sufficient geometrical and dynamical hypothesis,
there exists a constant
\mathfrak{C}_{1}
such that the inequality (
3.3
) is satisfied.
An additional advantage of this approach is that the constants
\mathfrak{C}_{1}
,
(\gamma,\tau)
and
\rho
can be explicitly calculated for each problem.
This makes it possible to determine the accuracy needed to calculate the invariance error,
thus guatanteeing the existence of the torus.
A great advantage of the a-posteriori approach is that the hypotheses of the proof depend on the existence of an approximately invariant solution that satisfies regularity and
non-degeneracy conditions.

The approximate solution can be obtained in several ways,
such as through numerical computations,
Lindstedt series,
the integrable case,
etc.
However,
since physically relevant regimes are often far from integrability,
the theorem provides a way to work in these settings,
as long as a good approximation is available.
In particular,
one can take advantage of numerically computed results to estimate the constant
\mathfrak{C}_{1}
,
the invariance error,
and the Diophantine conditions.
This framework allows for Computer-Assisted Proofs (CAPs),
as demonstrated in the work of
[
FHL17
]
,(see
[
CC87
,
VL21
]
for other approaches).
This not only strengthens the validity of the theoretical results but also guarantees exceptional accuracy and robustness in verifying the existence of the torus.
Furthermore, there are real-world problems where relevant solutions can be found.
We note that our approach differs from other works that rely on powerful numerical integrations to validate flows and jets like
[
KS17
,
MB09
]
.
Instead, we focus on validating the parameterization approximation and its respective estimations at each Newton step.

The structure of this paper is as follows.
In section
2
we present,
the notation and tools needed to develop a KAM theorem for Hamiltonian systems with periodic or quasi-periodic time dependencies.
This includes the use of the r-th order derivative of,
analytic functions,
norms,
and the Diophantine condition.
Additionally,

focusing on invariant tori,

In section

we prove the KAM theorem for Lagrangian tori by first establishing foundational lemmas.
These lemmas provide the necessary conditions and properties to demonstrate the persistence of invariant tori in the system.
This lays the groundwork for proving the theorem under specific dynamical and geometric conditions.
In section
4
we provide estimates for various geometrical and dynamical properties of an approximately invariant torus.
These approximations are crucial to demonstrate the quadratic convergence of the method.
In section
5

we provide a proof of the KAM Theorem.
For clarity,
we begin by outlining the approach used to correct the parameterization of the torus.
Specifically,
Section
5.1
covers the approximate solution of the linearized equations within the symplectic framework constructed in Section
4.3
,
establishing a method for solving the invariance equation.
Section
5.2

Section
5.3

Finally,
in the appendices,

and a compendium of constants in the KAM theorem.
These constants are explicit and are used to compute the constants

and

2.2.
QP Hamiltonian systems, symplectic bundles and invariant tori

In this paper, we require an open set

2

3.
A-posteriori KAM theory for quasi-periodic Hamiltonian systems

In order to prove the KAM theorem for Lagrangian tori,
it is necessary to establish a series of lemmas that will be the foundation for the proof.
These lemmas will provide the conditions and properties required to demonstrate the persistence of invariant tori in a system.
By stating these lemmas,
we can lay the groundwork of the proof of the KAM theorem,
which states that an invariant torus exists for certain dynamical and geometrical conditions.

3.1.
KAM theorem for Lagrangian torus

Theorem 3.1

(KAM theorem for quasi-periodic Hamiltonian systems)
.

Let us consider an exact symplectic structure
\mbox{\boldmath$\omega$}={\mbox{\rm d}}\mbox{\boldmath$\alpha$}
and
a Riemannian metric
g
on the open set
{\mathcal{M}}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{2n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{\ell}
.
Let

h\in{{\mathcal{A}}}\left({\mathcal{M}}_{\rho}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{\ell}_{\rho}\right)

be a Hamiltonian function that depends quasi-periodically on time,
with
1\leq\ell\leq n
and

K:{\mathbb{T}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{\ell}\rightarrow{\mathcal{M}}
be a parameterization of an Lagrangian approximately invariant torus
with frequency vector
(\omega,\alpha)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{\ell}
,
furthermore consider the tangent frame

L:{\mathbb{T}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{\ell}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}^{2n\times n}
given by

(3.1)

L(\theta,\varphi)={\mbox{\rm D}}_{\theta}K(\theta,\varphi)\,.

Then, we assume that the following hypotheses hold.

H_{1}

The global objects can be analytically extended to the complex domain
{\mathcal{B}}\subset{\mathbb{C}}^{2n}
, and there are constants that quantify the control of their analytic norms.
For the geometric structures
\mbox{\boldmath$\omega$},\mbox{\boldmath$g$},\mbox{\boldmath$J$}
in
{\mathcal{B}}
,
the matrix functions

\Omega,G,J:{\mathcal{B}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{C}}^{2n\times 2n}

4.2.
Approximate lagrangianity

Lemma 4.2

(Approximate lagrangianity)
.

Let us consider the setting of Theorem
3.1
.
Let us consider
\Omega_{L}:{\mathcal{M}}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{\ell}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}^{2n\times 2n}
,
the matrix representation of the pull-back on
K_{\varphi}^{*}\mbox{\boldmath$\omega$}
.
We have

(4.1)

\langle{\Omega_{L}}\rangle=O_{n}\,,

and the following estimate holds:

(4.2)

\|{\Omega_{L}}\|_{\rho-2\delta}\leq\frac{C_{\Omega_{L}}}{\gamma\delta^{\tau+1}}\|{E}\|_{\rho}\,,

where the constant
C_{\Omega_{L}}
is provided in Table
1

Proof.

To prove that
\langle{\Omega_{L}}\rangle=O_{n}
,
we recall that
\Omega_{L}(z,\varphi)
is the matrix representation of

K^{*}_{\varphi}\mbox{\boldmath$\omega$}=K^{*}\mbox{\boldmath$\omega$}=\mathrm{d}(K^{*}\mbox{\boldmath$\alpha$})
,
so

\begin{split}\Omega_{L}(K(\theta,\varphi),\varphi)&=\left(D_{\theta}K(\theta,\varphi)\right)^{\top}\Omega\left(K(\theta,\varphi)\right)D_{\theta}K(\theta,\varphi)\\
&=\left(D_{\theta}(a(K(\theta,\varphi)))\right)^{\top}D_{\theta}K(\theta,\varphi)-\left(D_{\theta}K(\theta,\varphi)\right)^{\top}D_{\theta}(a(K(\theta,\varphi)))\\
\end{split}

and so,
the components of

\Omega_{L}(\theta,\varphi)
are

\begin{split}\left(\Omega_{L}\right)_{i,j}(K(\theta,\varphi),\varphi)=\displaystyle\sum_{m=1}^{2n}&\left(\dfrac{\partial}{\partial\theta_{i}}\left(a_{m}(K(\theta,\varphi))\dfrac{\partial K_{m}(\theta,\varphi)}{\partial\theta_{j}}\right)\right.\\
&-\left.\dfrac{\partial}{\partial\theta_{j}}\left(a_{m}(K(\theta,\varphi))\dfrac{\partial K_{m}(\theta,\varphi)}{\partial\theta_{i}}\right)\right).\end{split}

We observe that the components of

\Omega_{L}(K(\theta,\varphi),\varphi)

consist of sums of derivatives of periodic functions with respect to the variable

\theta
,
therefore
\langle{\Omega_{L}}\rangle=O_{n}
.
We can now proceed to prove inequality (
4.2
).
To do this,
we consider the approximately invariant case,
using the invariant error given by equation (
3.2
).

\mathfrak{L}_{\omega,\alpha}\left(\Omega(K(\theta,\varphi))\right)=D_{z}\Omega(K(\theta,\varphi))\left[E(\theta,\varphi)-Z_{h}(K(\theta,\varphi),\varphi)\right]\,,

(4.3)

\mathfrak{L}_{\omega,\alpha}(D_{\theta}K(\theta,\varphi))=D_{\theta}E(\theta,\varphi)-D_{z}Z_{h}(K(\theta,\varphi),\varphi)D_{\theta}K(\theta,\varphi),

Replacing the last expressions
\mathfrak{L}_{\omega,\alpha}\left(\Omega_{L}(\theta,\varphi)\right)
,
we have,

\begin{split}\mathfrak{L}_{\omega,\alpha}(\Omega_{L}(\theta,\varphi))&=D_{\theta}(E(\theta,\varphi))^{\top}\Omega(K(\theta,\varphi))D_{\theta}K(\theta,\varphi)\\
&+\left(D_{\theta}K(\theta,\varphi)\right)^{\top}D_{z}\Omega(K(\theta,\varphi))\left[E(\theta,\varphi)\right]D_{\theta}K(\theta,\varphi)\\
&+\left(D_{\theta}K(\theta,\varphi)\right)^{\top}\Omega(K(\theta,\varphi))D_{\theta}E(\theta,\varphi)\,.\\
\end{split}

The expression

is controlled using
H_{1}
,
H_{2}

the Banach algebra properties and Cauchy estimates as follows

\begin{split}\|\mathfrak{L}_{\omega,\alpha}\Omega_{L}\|_{\rho-\delta}&\leq\|\left(D_{\theta}E\right)^{\top}\|_{\rho-\delta}\|\Omega\|_{{\mathcal{B}}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{\ell}}\|D_{\theta}K\|_{\rho}\\

\end{split}

Using Rus̈smann estimates,
we obtain

\|\Omega_{L}\|_{\rho-2\delta}\leq\dfrac{c_{R}}{\gamma\delta^{\tau}}\|\mathfrak{L}_{\omega,\alpha}\Omega_{L}\|_{\rho-\delta}\leq\dfrac{c_{R}C_{\mathfrak{L}\Omega_{L}}}{\gamma\delta^{\tau+1}}\|E\|_{\rho}=:\dfrac{C_{\Omega_{L}}}{\gamma\delta^{\tau+1}}\|E\|_{\rho}.

Remark 4.3
.

If the symplectic form depends explicitly on the
\varphi

then the Lie’s derivative of the reduced form is

\leq&\|{\Omega_{L}}\|_{\rho-2\delta}\|{\mathfrak{L}_{\omega,\alpha}\bar{A}}\|_{\rho-\delta}+\|{\Omega_{L}}\|_{\rho-2\delta}\|{E_{\mathfrak{L}A}}\|_{\rho-\delta}\\
\leq&\dfrac{C_{\Omega_{L}}C_{\mathfrak{L}A}+\gamma\delta^{\tau}\sigma_{L^{\top}\hskip-1.42262pt}c_{\Omega,0}\sigma_{L}C_{E_{\mathfrak{L}A}}}{\gamma\delta^{\tau+1}}\|{E}\|_{\rho}\,,\end{split}

where,
we use expression (
4.38
),

(4.66)

\begin{split}\|{\mathfrak{L}_{\omega,\alpha}\bar{A}}\|_{\rho-\delta}\leq&\dfrac{1}{2}\left(C_{\tilde{N}^{\top}}c_{T^{\top}_{h},0}C_{\tilde{N}}+C_{\tilde{N}^{\top}}c_{T^{\top}_{h},0}c_{J^{-1}\hskip-1.42262pt,0}C_{\tilde{N}}C_{L^{\top}}c_{J^{\top}\hskip-1.42262pt,0}c_{\Omega,0}C_{\tilde{N}}\right.\\
&{}+\left.C_{\tilde{N}^{\top}}c_{T_{h},0}C_{\tilde{N}}+C_{\tilde{N}^{\top}}c_{\Omega,0}c_{J,0}C_{L}C_{\tilde{N}^{\top}}c_{J^{-\top}\hskip-1.42262pt,0}c_{T_{h},0}C_{\tilde{N}}\right)\\
&=:C_{\mathfrak{L}A}\,.\end{split}

We note that the component
E_{\mathrm{red}}^{1,2}(\theta,\varphi)
coincides with equation (
4.42
), i.e.

(4.67)

E_{\mathrm{red}}^{1,2}(\theta,\varphi)=E_{T}(\theta,\varphi)\,.

Finally, we estimate the norms of the block components of
E_{\mathrm{red}}

using the expressions (
4.56
), (
4.67
), (
4.58
) and (
4.64
),
and the previous estimates:

(4.68)

\displaystyle\|{E_{\mathrm{red}}^{1,1}}\|_{\rho-2\delta}\leq{}

\displaystyle\frac{C_{N^{\top}}c_{\Omega,0}C_{{\mathcal{X}}_{L}}}{\delta}\|{E}\|_{\rho}=:\frac{C_{\mathrm{red}}^{1,1}}{\delta}\|{E}\|_{\rho}\,,

(4.69)

\displaystyle\|{E_{\mathrm{red}}^{1,2}}\|_{\rho-2\delta}\leq{}

\displaystyle\frac{C_{E_{T}}}{\gamma\delta^{\tau+1}}\|{E}\|_{\rho}=:\frac{C_{\mathrm{red}}^{1,2}}{\gamma\delta^{\tau+1}}\|{E}\|_{\rho}\,,

(4.70)

\displaystyle\|{E_{\mathrm{red}}^{2,1}}\|_{\rho-2\delta}\leq{}

\displaystyle\frac{C_{L^{\top}}c_{\Omega,0}C_{{\mathcal{X}}_{L}}}{\delta}\|{E}\|_{\rho}=:\frac{C_{\mathrm{red}}^{2,1}}{\delta}\|{E}\|_{\rho}\,,

\displaystyle\|{E_{\mathrm{red}}^{2,2}}\|_{\rho-2\delta}\leq{}

\displaystyle\left(C_{L^{\top}}c_{\Omega,1}C_{N}+\frac{C_{{\mathcal{X}}_{L}^{\top}}c_{\Omega,0}C_{N}}{\delta}+\frac{C_{\mathfrak{L}\Omega_{L}}C_{A}}{\delta}+\frac{C_{\Omega_{L}}C_{\mathfrak{L}A}+\gamma\delta^{\tau}\sigma_{L^{\top}\hskip-1.42262pt}c_{\Omega,0}\sigma_{L}C_{E_{\mathfrak{L}A}}}{\gamma\delta^{\tau+1}}\right)\|{E}\|_{\rho}

(4.71)

\displaystyle=:{}

\displaystyle\frac{C_{\mathrm{red}}^{2,2}}{\gamma\delta^{\tau+1}}\|{E}\|_{\rho}\,.

Then, we end up with

(4.72)

\|{E_{\mathrm{red}}}\|_{\rho-2\delta}\leq\frac{\max\{C_{\mathrm{red}}^{1,1}\gamma\delta^{\tau}+C_{\mathrm{red}}^{1,2}\,,\,C_{\mathrm{red}}^{2,1}\gamma\delta^{\tau}+C_{\mathrm{red}}^{2,2}\}}{\gamma\delta^{\tau+1}}\|{E}\|_{\rho}=:\frac{C_{\mathrm{red}}}{\gamma\delta^{\tau+1}}\|{E}\|_{\rho}\,,

thus completing the proof.
∎

5.
Proof of the Lagrangian KAM theorem

In this section we present a proof of Theorem
3.1
.
For convenience, we start by outlining the scheme used
to correct the parameterization of the torus. That is,
in Section
5.1
we discuss the approximate solution of the
linearized equations in the symplectic frame constructed in Section

4.3
. This establishes a method to
obtain a solution of the invariance equation. In Section

5.2
we produce quantitative estimates for
the objects obtained when performing one iteration of the
previous procedure. Finally, in Section

5.3
we discuss the convergence of the quasi-Newton method.

5.1.
The quasi-Newton method

As it is usual in the a-posteriori approach in KAM theory, the argument consists
in refining an approximate solution
K(\theta,\varphi)
,
by means of a quasi-Newton method.
Let us consider the equations associated with the invariance error (
3.2
).

We obtain the new parameterization

\bar{K}(\theta,\varphi)=K(\theta,\varphi)+\Delta K(\theta,\varphi)

by considering the linearized equation

(5.1)

{\mbox{\rm D}}Z_{h}(K(\theta,\varphi),\varphi)\Delta K(\theta,\varphi)+\mathfrak{L}_{\omega,\alpha}\Delta K(\theta,\varphi)=-E(\theta,\varphi)\,.

If we obtain a good enough approximation of the solution

\Delta K(\theta,\varphi)

of (
5.1
),
then
\bar{K}(\theta,\varphi)
provides a parameterization
of an approximately invariant torus with frequency
(\omega,\alpha)
,
having a quadratic error in terms of
E(\theta,\varphi)
.

To solve the linearized equation (
5.1
),
we make use of the approximately symplectic
frame
P(\theta,\varphi)
, defined on the full tangent
space, which has been characterized in Section
4

(see Lemma
4.5
).
In particular, we introduce the linear change of coordinates,

(5.2)

\Delta K(\theta,\varphi)=P(\theta,\varphi)\xi(\theta,\varphi)\,,

where
\xi(\theta,\varphi)
is the new unknown.
Taking into account this expression,
the linearized equation becomes

(5.3)

\begin{split}\left({\mbox{\rm D}}Z_{h}(K(\theta,\varphi),\varphi)P(\theta,\varphi)\right.+&\left.\mathfrak{L}_{\omega,\alpha}P(\theta,\varphi)\right)\xi(\theta,\varphi)\\
+&P(\theta,\varphi)\mathfrak{L}_{\omega,\alpha}\xi(\theta,\varphi)=-E(\theta,\varphi)\,,\end{split}

We now multiply both sides of (
5.3
) by
-\Omega_{0}P(\theta,\varphi)^{\top}\Omega(K(\theta,\varphi))
, and we
use the
geometric properties in Lemma
4.5
and
Lemma
4.11
, thus obtaining the equivalent equations:

(5.4)

\begin{split}\left(\Lambda(\theta,\varphi)+E_{\mathrm{red}}(\theta,\varphi)\right)\xi(\theta,\varphi)+{}&\left(I_{2n}-\Omega_{0}E_{\mathrm{sym}}(\theta,\varphi)\right)\mathfrak{L}_{\omega,\alpha}\xi(\theta,\varphi)\\
={}&\Omega_{0}P(\theta,\varphi)^{\top}\Omega(K(\theta,\varphi))E(\theta,\varphi):=\begin{pmatrix}\eta^{L}(\theta,\varphi)\\
\eta^{N}(\theta,\varphi)\end{pmatrix}\,,\end{split}

\Lambda(\theta,\varphi)
is the triangular matrix-valued map given
in (
4.55
).
We notice that
\langle{\eta^{N}}\rangle=0_{n}
from the third equation in (
4.6
).

Then, it turns out that the solution of (
5.4
)
are approximated by the solutions of a triangular system
that involves solving two cohomological equations of
the form (
2.2
) consecutively.
Quantitative estimates for the solutions of
such equations are obtained by applying
Rüssmann estimates.
This is summarized
in the following standard statement.

Lemma 5.1

(Upper triangular equations)
.

Let

(\omega,\alpha)\in{\mathcal{D}}_{\gamma,\tau}

and let us consider a map

\eta=(\eta^{L},\eta^{N}):{\mathbb{T}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{\ell}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}^{2n}\simeq{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, with components in
{{\mathcal{A}}}({\mathbb{T}}^{n}_{\rho}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{\ell}_{\rho})
, and a
map
T:{\mathbb{T}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{\ell}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times n}
,
with components in
{{\mathcal{A}}}({\mathbb{T}}^{n}_{\rho}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{\ell}_{\rho})
.
Assume that
T
satisfies the non-degeneracy condition
\det\langle{T}\rangle\neq 0
and
\eta
satisfies the compatibility condition
\langle{\eta^{N}}\rangle=0_{n}
.
Then, for any
\xi^{L}_{0,0}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, the system of equations

(5.5)

\begin{pmatrix}O_{n}&T(\theta,\varphi)\\
O_{n}&O_{n}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\xi^{L}(\theta,\varphi)\\
\xi^{N}(\theta,\varphi)\end{pmatrix}+\begin{pmatrix}\mathfrak{L}_{\omega,\alpha}\xi^{L}(\theta,\varphi)\\
\mathfrak{L}_{\omega,\alpha}\xi^{N}(\theta,\varphi)\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\eta^{L}(\theta,\varphi)\\
\eta^{N}(\theta,\varphi)\end{pmatrix}

has a solution of the form

\displaystyle\xi^{N}(\theta,\varphi)={}

\displaystyle\xi^{N}_{0,0}+\mathfrak{R}_{\omega,\alpha}(\eta^{N}(\theta,\varphi))\,,

\displaystyle\xi^{L}(\theta,\varphi)={}

\displaystyle\xi^{L}_{0,0}+\mathfrak{R}_{\omega,\alpha}(\eta^{L}(\theta,\varphi)-T(\theta,\varphi)\xi^{N}(\theta,\varphi))\,,

where

\xi^{N}_{0,0}=\langle{T}\rangle^{-1}\langle{\eta^{L}-T\mathfrak{R}_{\omega}(\eta^{N})}\rangle

and
\mathfrak{R}_{\omega,\alpha}
is given by (
2.3
).
Moreover,
we have the estimates

\displaystyle|\xi_{0,0}^{N}|\leq\left|{\langle{T}\rangle^{-1}}\right|\Big(\|{\eta^{L}}\|_{\rho}+\frac{c_{R}}{\gamma\delta^{\tau}}\|{T}\|_{\rho}\|{\eta^{N}}\|_{\rho}\Big)\,,

\displaystyle\|{\xi^{N}}\|_{\rho-\delta}\leq|\xi_{0,0}^{N}|+\frac{c_{R}}{\gamma\delta^{\tau}}\|{\eta^{N}}\|_{\rho}\,,

\displaystyle\|{\xi^{L}}\|_{\rho-2\delta}\leq|\xi_{0,0}^{L}|+\frac{c_{R}}{\gamma\delta^{\tau}}\Big(\|{\eta^{L}}\|_{\rho-\delta}+\|{T}\|_{\rho}\|{\xi^{N}}\|_{\rho-\delta}\Big)\,.

Proof.

From (
5.5
), we have,

\begin{split}\mathfrak{L}_{\omega,\alpha}\xi^{N}(\theta,\varphi)&=\eta^{N}(\theta,\varphi)\\
\mathfrak{L}_{\omega,\alpha}\xi^{L}(\theta,\varphi)&=\eta^{L}(\theta,\varphi)-T(\theta,\varphi)\xi^{N}(\theta,\varphi)\,.\end{split}

By hypothesis we know that
\langle{\eta^{N}}\rangle=0_{n}
, so

(5.6)

\xi^{N}\left(\theta,\varphi\right)=\xi_{0,0}^{N}+\mathfrak{R}_{\omega,\alpha}\left(\eta^{N}(\theta,\varphi)\right)\,.

To solve the second equation, the average
\langle{\eta^{L}-T\xi^{N}}\rangle
must be zero. i.e.

\begin{split}0_{n}=\langle{\eta^{L}-T\xi^{N}}\rangle&=\langle{\eta^{L}-T\mathfrak{R}_{\omega,\alpha}\left(\eta^{N}\right)}\rangle-\langle{T}\rangle\xi_{0,0}^{N}\,.\end{split}

Then,

(5.7)

\xi_{0,0}^{N}=\langle{T}\rangle^{-1}\langle{\eta^{L}-T\mathfrak{R}_{\omega,\alpha}\left(\eta^{N}\right)}\rangle\,.

Back to the second equation, its solution is,

(5.8)

\xi^{L}\left(\theta,\varphi\right)=\xi_{0,0}^{L}+\mathfrak{R}_{\omega,\alpha}\left(\eta^{L}(\theta,\varphi)-T(\theta,\varphi)\xi^{N}(\theta,\varphi)\right)\,,

in addition, we have estimates

\begin{split}\|{\xi^{N}}\|_{\rho-\delta}&\leq|\xi_{0,0}^{N}|+\dfrac{c_{R}}{\gamma\delta^{\tau}}\|{\eta^{N}}\|_{\rho}\,,\end{split}

now, for
\xi_{0,0}^{N}

\begin{split}\left|\xi_{0,0}^{N}\right|&\leq\left|\langle T\rangle^{-1}\right|\left|\langle\eta^{L}-T\mathfrak{R}_{\omega,\alpha}\left(\eta^{N}\right)\rangle\right|\\
&\leq\left|\langle T\rangle^{-1}\right|\left(\|\eta^{L}\|_{\rho}+\dfrac{c_{R}}{\gamma\delta^{\tau}}\|T\|_{\rho}\|\eta^{N}\|_{\rho}\right)\,.\end{split}

Finally,

\begin{split}\|\xi^{L}\|_{\rho-2\delta}&=\|\xi_{0,0}^{L}+\mathfrak{R}_{\omega,\alpha}\left(\eta^{L}-T\xi^{N}\right)\|_{\rho-2\delta}\\
&\leq|\xi_{0,0}^{L}|+\dfrac{c_{R}}{\gamma\delta^{\tau}}\|\eta^{L}-T\xi^{N}\|_{\rho-\delta}\\
&\leq|\xi_{0,0}^{L}|+\dfrac{c_{R}}{\gamma\delta^{\tau}}\left(\|\eta^{L}\|_{\rho-\delta}+\|T\|_{\rho}\|\xi^{N}\|_{\rho-\delta}\right)\,,\end{split}

and this concludes the proof.
∎

To approximate the solutions of (
5.4
) we invoke Lemma
5.1
taking

(5.9)

\begin{split}\eta^{L}(\theta,\varphi)&=-N(\theta,\varphi)^{\top}\Omega(K(\theta,\varphi))E(\theta,\varphi)\,,\\
\eta^{N}(\theta,\varphi)&=L(\theta,\varphi)^{\top}\Omega(K(\theta,\varphi))E(\theta,\varphi)\,,\end{split}

and
T(\theta,\varphi)
given by (
2.23
).
Note that
\langle{\xi^{N}}\rangle=\xi^{N}_{0,0}
and
we have the freedom of choosing any
value for
\langle{\xi^{L}}\rangle=\xi^{L}_{0,0}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
.
For convenience in setting the phase,
we choose
\xi_{0,0}^{L}=0_{n}
,
even though
other choices are possible.

From Lemma
5.1
we read that

\|{\xi}\|_{\rho-2\delta}={\mathcal{O}}(\|{E}\|_{\rho})
and,
using the geometric properties characterized in Section
4
,
we have
\|{E_{\mathrm{red}}}\|_{\rho-2\delta}={\mathcal{O}}(\|{E}\|_{\rho})
and
\|{E_{\mathrm{sym}}}\|_{\rho-2\delta}={\mathcal{O}}(\|{E}\|_{\rho})
.
From these estimates, we conclude that the solution of equation
(
5.4
)
is approximated by the solution of
the cohomological equation

(5.10)

\Lambda(\theta,\varphi)\xi(\theta,\varphi)+\mathfrak{L}_{\omega,\alpha}\xi(\theta,\varphi)=\eta(\theta,\varphi)\,.

This, together with other estimates, will be suitably quantified in the next section.

5.2.
One step of the iterative procedure

In this section we apply one correction of the quasi-Newton method
described in Section
5.1
and we obtain sharp quantitative
estimates
for the new approximately invariant torus and related objects. We
set sufficient conditions to preserve the control of the previous
estimates.

Lemma 5.2

(The Iterative Lemma)
.

With the hypotheses of Theorem
3.1
,
Then, there exist constants

C_{\Delta K}
,

C_{\Delta B}
,

C_{\Delta\langle{T}\rangle^{-1}}
and

C_{E}

such that
if the inequalities

(5.11)

\frac{\hat{C}_{\Delta}\|{E}\|_{\rho}}{\gamma^{2}\delta^{2\tau+1}}<1\,,\qquad\qquad\frac{C_{E}\|{E}\|_{\rho}}{\gamma^{4}\delta^{4\tau}}<1\,,

hold for some
0<\delta<\rho
, where

(5.12)

\begin{split}\hat{C}_{\Delta}:=\max\left\{C_{\mathrm{sym}}\gamma\delta^{\tau}\,,\,\frac{nC_{\Delta K}}{\sigma_{L}-\|{{\mbox{\rm D}}K}\|_{\rho}}\,,\,\frac{2nC_{\Delta K}}{\sigma_{L^{\top}\hskip-1.42262pt}-\|{({\mbox{\rm D}}K)^{\top}}\|_{\rho}}\,,\,\right.\\
\left.\frac{C_{\Delta B}}{\sigma_{B}-\|{B}\|_{\rho}}\,,\,\frac{C_{\Delta\langle{T}\rangle^{-1}}}{\sigma_{T}-|{\langle{T}\rangle^{-1}}|}\,,\,\frac{C_{\Delta K}\delta}{\mathrm{dist}(K({\mathbb{T}}^{n+\ell}_{\rho}),\partial({\mathcal{B}}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{\ell}_{\rho}))}\,,\right\}\,.\end{split}

then we have an approximate torus of the same frequency
\omega

given by
\bar{K}=K+\Delta K
, with components in
{{\mathcal{A}}}({\mathbb{T}}^{n}_{\rho-2\delta}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{\ell}_{\rho-2\delta})
, that defines new objects
\bar{B}

and
\bar{T}
(obtained by replacing
K
by
\bar{K}
) satisfying

(5.13)

\displaystyle\|{{\mbox{\rm D}}\bar{K}}\|_{\rho-3\delta}<\sigma_{L}\,,

(5.14)

\displaystyle\|{({\mbox{\rm D}}\bar{K})^{\top}}\|_{\rho-3\delta}<\sigma_{L^{\top}\hskip-1.42262pt}\,,

(5.15)

\displaystyle\|{\bar{B}}\|_{\rho-3\delta}<\sigma_{B}\,,

(5.16)

\displaystyle|{\langle{\bar{T}}\rangle^{-1}}|<\sigma_{T}\,,

(5.17)

\displaystyle\mathrm{dist}(\bar{K}({\mathbb{T}}^{n}_{\rho-2\delta}\times K({\mathbb{T}}^{\ell}_{\rho-2\delta}),\partial({\mathcal{B}}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{\ell}_{\rho}))>0\,,

and

(5.18)

\displaystyle\|{\bar{K}-K}\|_{\rho-2\delta}<\frac{C_{\Delta K}}{\gamma^{2}\delta^{2\tau}}\|{E}\|_{\rho}\,,

(5.19)

\displaystyle\|{\bar{B}-B}\|_{\rho-3\delta}<\frac{C_{\Delta B}}{\gamma^{2}\delta^{2\tau+1}}\|{E}\|_{\rho}\,,

(5.20)

\displaystyle|{\langle{\bar{T}}\rangle^{-1}-\langle{T}\rangle^{-1}}|<\frac{C_{\Delta\langle{T}\rangle^{-1}}}{\gamma^{2}\delta^{2\tau+1}}\|{E}\|_{\rho}\,,

The new error of invariance is given by

\bar{E}(\theta,\varphi)=Z_{h}(\bar{K}(\theta,\varphi),\varphi)+\mathfrak{L}_{\omega,\alpha}\bar{K}(\theta,\varphi)\,,

and satisfies

(5.21)

\|{\bar{E}}\|_{\rho-2\delta}<\frac{C_{E}}{\gamma^{4}\delta^{4\tau}}\|{E}\|_{\rho}^{2}\,.

The above constants are collected in Table
2
.

Proof.

To obtain the estimates of the theorem requires many computations,
so we break down the demonstration into seven steps.

Step 1:
Control of the new parameterization
.

We start by considering a new parameterization

\bar{K}(\theta,\varphi)=K(\theta,\varphi)+\Delta K(\theta,\varphi)
,
which is obtained from the system (
5.5
),
with
\eta(\theta,\varphi)
given by (
5.9
).
We choose the solution that satisfies
\xi_{0,0}^{L}=0
.
Using the estimates obtained in Section
4
,
we have the following inequalities:

\|{\eta^{L}}\|_{\rho}\leq C_{N^{\top}}c_{\Omega,0}\|{E}\|_{\rho}\,\text{, and}\qquad\|{\eta^{N}}\|_{\rho}\leq C_{L^{\top}}c_{\Omega,0}\|{E}\|_{\rho}\,.

Combining
Lemma
4.4

and
Lemma
5.1
,
we obtain estimates for the solution
of the cohomological equations

\displaystyle|{\xi^{N}_{0,0}}|\leq|{\langle{T}\rangle^{-1}}|\Big(\|{\eta^{L}}\|_{\rho}+\frac{c_{R}}{\gamma\delta^{\tau}}\|{T}\|_{\rho}\|{\eta^{N}}\|_{\rho}\Big)

(5.22)

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\sigma_{T}\Big(C_{N^{\top}}c_{\Omega,0}+\frac{c_{R}}{\gamma\delta^{\tau}}C_{T}C_{L^{\top}}c_{\Omega,0}\Big)\|{E}\|_{\rho}=:\frac{C_{\xi^{N}_{0}}}{\gamma\delta^{\tau}}\|{E}\|_{\rho}\,,

\displaystyle\|{\xi^{N}}\|_{\rho-\delta}\leq|{\xi^{N}_{0}}|+\frac{c_{R}}{\gamma\delta^{\tau}}\|{\eta^{N}}\|_{\rho}

(5.23)

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\frac{C_{\xi^{N}_{0}}}{\gamma\delta^{\tau}}\|{E}\|_{\rho}+\frac{c_{R}}{\gamma\delta^{\tau}}C_{L^{\top}}c_{\Omega,0}\|{E}\|_{\rho}=:\frac{C_{\xi^{N}}}{\gamma\delta^{\tau}}\|{E}\|_{\rho}\,,

\displaystyle\|{\xi^{L}}\|_{\rho-2\delta}\leq|{\xi_{0}^{L}}|+\frac{c_{R}}{\gamma\delta^{\tau}}\Big(\|{\eta^{L}}\|_{\rho}+\|{T}\|_{\rho}\|{\xi^{N}}\|_{\rho-\delta}\Big)

(5.24)

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\frac{c_{R}}{\gamma\delta^{\tau}}\Big(C_{N^{\top}}c_{\Omega,0}+C_{T}\frac{C_{\xi^{N}}}{\gamma\delta^{\tau}}\Big)\|{E}\|_{\rho}=:\frac{C_{\xi^{L}}}{\gamma^{2}\delta^{2\tau}}\|{E}\|_{\rho}\,.

The magnitude of the vector
\xi(\theta,\varphi)
,
which satisfies (
5.10
),
is estimated as

\displaystyle\|{\xi}\|_{\rho-2\delta}\leq{}

\displaystyle\max\{\|{\xi^{L}}\|_{\rho-2\delta}\,,\,\|{\xi^{N}}\|_{\rho-\delta}\}

(5.25)

\displaystyle\leq{}

\displaystyle\frac{\max\{C_{\xi^{L}}\,,\,C_{\xi^{N}}\gamma\delta^{\tau}\}}{\gamma^{2}\delta^{2\tau}}\|{E}\|_{\rho}=:\frac{C_{\xi}}{\gamma^{2}\delta^{2\tau}}\|{E}\|_{\rho}\,.

The new parameterization
\bar{K}(\theta,\varphi)
and associated objects are estimated through conventional calculations.
The control (
5.18
) stems directly from

\bar{K}(\theta,\varphi)-K(\theta,\varphi)=\Delta K(\theta,\varphi)=P(\theta,\varphi)\xi(\theta,\varphi)=L(\theta,\varphi)\xi^{L}(\theta,\varphi)+N(\theta,\varphi)\xi^{N}(\theta,\varphi)\,.

In other words,
by employing approximations provided in (
5.23
) and (
5.24
),
we obtain

(5.26)

\|{\bar{K}-K}\|_{\rho-2\delta}\leq\frac{C_{L}C_{\xi^{L}}+C_{N}C_{\xi^{N}}\gamma\delta^{\tau}}{\gamma^{2}\delta^{2\tau}}\|{E}\|_{\rho}=:\frac{C_{\Delta K}}{\gamma^{2}\delta^{2\tau}}\|{E}\|_{\rho}\,.

To complete this step,
we need to make sure that the value of

\bar{K}(\theta,\varphi)

falls within the defined domain
{\mathcal{B}}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{\ell}_{\rho}
of all the entities involved.
This verification is essential as it enables us to calculate the new error
\bar{E}(\theta,\varphi)
before proceeding with the remaining geometrical elements.
To achieve this,
we take note of the fact that:

\displaystyle\mathrm{dist}(\bar{K}({\mathbb{T}}^{n+\ell}_{\rho-2\delta}),\partial({\mathcal{B}}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{\ell}_{\rho-2\delta}))\geq{}

\displaystyle\mathrm{dist}(K({\mathbb{T}}^{n+\ell}_{\rho}),\partial({\mathcal{B}}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{\ell}_{\rho}))-\|{\Delta K}\|_{\rho-2\delta}

(5.27)

\displaystyle\geq{}

\displaystyle\mathrm{dist}(K({\mathbb{T}}^{n+\ell}_{\rho}),\partial({\mathcal{B}}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{\ell}_{\rho}))-\frac{C_{\Delta K}}{\gamma^{2}\delta^{2\tau}}\|{E}\|_{\rho}>0\,.

The last inequality follows from Hypothesis (
5.11
),
using the sixth term in (
5.12
).
Thus,
we have established the control in (
5.17
).

Step 2: Control of the new error of invariance
.

To guarantee that the corrected parameterization
\bar{K}
is invariant,
we control the quadratic terms neglected in equation (
5.10
),
by first considering the error in solving the linearized equation (
5.4
),

(5.28)

E_{\mathrm{lin}}(\theta,\varphi)=E_{\mathrm{red}}(\theta,\varphi)\xi(\theta,\varphi)-\Omega_{0}E_{\mathrm{sym}}(\theta,\varphi)\mathfrak{L}_{\omega,\alpha}\xi(\theta,\varphi)\,.

We control the term
\mathfrak{L}_{\omega,\alpha}\xi(\theta,\varphi)
by leveraging the fact that

\xi(\theta,\varphi)
satisfies the solution of the cohomological equation (
5.10
):

(5.29)

\displaystyle\|{\mathfrak{L}_{\omega}\xi^{N}}\|_{\rho}={}

\displaystyle\|{\eta^{N}}\|_{\rho}\leq C_{L^{\top}}c_{\Omega,0}\|{E}\|_{\rho}=:C_{\mathfrak{L}\xi^{N}}\|{E}\|_{\rho}\,,

\displaystyle\|{\mathfrak{L}_{\omega}\xi^{L}}\|_{\rho-\delta}={}

\displaystyle\|{\eta^{L}-T\xi^{N}}\|_{\rho-\delta}\leq\left(C_{N^{\top}}c_{\Omega,0}+C_{T}\frac{C_{\xi^{N}}}{\gamma\delta^{\tau}}\right)\|{E}\|_{\rho}

(5.30)

\displaystyle=:{}

\displaystyle\frac{C_{\mathfrak{L}\xi^{L}}}{\gamma\delta^{\tau}}\|{E}\|_{\rho}\,,

(5.31)

\displaystyle\|{\mathfrak{L}_{\omega}\xi}\|_{\rho-\delta}\leq{}

\displaystyle\max\left\{\frac{C_{\mathfrak{L}\xi^{L}}}{\gamma\delta^{\tau}},C_{\mathfrak{L}\xi^{N}}\right\}\|{E}\|_{\rho}=:\frac{C_{\mathfrak{L}\xi}}{\gamma\delta^{\tau}}\|{E}\|_{\rho}\,.

Therefore, the control for
E_{\mathrm{lin}}(\theta,\varphi)
is given by

\displaystyle\|{E_{\mathrm{lin}}}\|_{\rho-2\delta}\leq{}

\displaystyle\|{E_{\mathrm{red}}}\|_{\rho-2\delta}\|{\xi}\|_{\rho-2\delta}+c_{\Omega,0}\|{E_{\mathrm{sym}}}\|_{\rho-2\delta}\|{\mathfrak{L}_{\omega}\xi}\|_{\rho-2\delta}

(5.32)

\displaystyle\leq{}

\displaystyle\frac{C_{\mathrm{red}}}{\gamma\delta^{\tau+1}}\frac{C_{\xi}}{\gamma^{2}\delta^{2\tau}}\|{E}\|_{\rho}^{2}+c_{\Omega,0}\frac{C_{\mathrm{sym}}}{\gamma\delta^{\tau+1}}\frac{C_{\mathfrak{L}\xi}}{\gamma\delta^{\tau}}\|{E}\|_{\rho}^{2}=:\frac{C_{\mathrm{lin}}}{\gamma^{3}\delta^{3\tau+1}}\|{E}\|_{\rho}^{2}\,.

After making the correction,
the invariance error associated with the new parameterization can be determined.

(5.33)

\begin{split}\bar{E}(\theta,\varphi)={}&Z_{h}(K(\theta,\varphi)+\Delta K(\theta,\varphi),\varphi)+\mathfrak{L}_{\omega,\alpha}K(\theta,\varphi)+\mathfrak{L}_{\omega,\alpha}\Delta K(\theta,\varphi)\\
={}&\Big({\mbox{\rm D}}Z_{h}(K(\theta,\varphi),\varphi)P(\theta,\varphi)+\mathfrak{L}_{\omega,\alpha}P(\theta,\varphi)\Big)\xi(\theta,\varphi)+P(\theta,\varphi)\mathfrak{L}_{\omega,\alpha}\xi(\theta,\varphi)\\
{}&\quad+E(\theta,\varphi)+\Delta^{2}Z_{h}(K(\theta,\varphi),\varphi)\\
={}&P(\theta,\varphi)(I_{2n}-\Omega_{0}E_{\mathrm{sym}}(\theta,\varphi))^{-1}E_{\mathrm{lin}}(\theta,\varphi)+\Delta_{\theta}^{2}Z_{h}(K(\theta,\varphi),\varphi)\,,\end{split}

where

(5.34)

\begin{split}\Delta^{2}_{\theta}Z_{h}(K(\theta,\varphi),\varphi)={}&Z_{h}(K(\theta,\varphi)+\Delta K(\theta,\varphi),\varphi)-Z_{h}(K(\theta,\varphi),\varphi)-{\mbox{\rm D}}_{z}Z_{h}(K(\theta,\varphi),\varphi)\Delta K(\theta,\varphi)\\
={}&\int_{0}^{1}(1-s){\mbox{\rm D}}^{2}_{z}Z_{h}(K(\theta,\varphi)+t\Delta K(\theta,\varphi),\varphi)[\Delta K(\theta,\varphi),\Delta K(\theta,\varphi)]{\mbox{\rm d}}s\,,\end{split}

and we used equation (

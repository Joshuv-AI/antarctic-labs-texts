Sequential Change Detection under Markov Setup with Unknown Prechange and Postchange Distributions
A.B. Gulaguli, S. Singh, R.K. Bansal

Abstract
In this work we extend the results developed in 2022 for a sequential change detection algorithm making use of Page’s CUSUM statistic,empirical distribution as an estimate of pre-change distribution and a universal code as a tool for estimating the post change distribution, from IID to Markov setup.



I
Introduction

Change detection has numerous applications including but not limited to industrial quality control,fault detection in computer communication networks and regime shift detection in financial markets.

Wald formalized the sequential hypothesis testing problem in 1945
[
1
]
.He proposed the Sequential Probability Ratio Test(SPRT) and proved its optimality along with Wolfowitz
[
2
]
.Lai proved asymptotic optimality of invariant SPRT for classes of sources that satisfy a stability condition for likelihood ratio
[
12
]
.

Page introduced the Cumulative Sum Test (CUSUM) to address the problem of sequential change detection in 1954
[
4
]
.Lorden proved the asymptotic optimality of CUSUM for memoryless sources in minimax framework
[
6
]
while Moustadikes proved its exact optimality
[
15
]
. Shiryaev
[
5
,
10
]
introduced a different approach to the change detection problem under Bayesian framework which has been extensively studied in literature.Lorden’s result was extended for sources with memory by Bansal and Kazakos
[
16
]
who assume independence of observations before and after change which was further weakened in Lai’s extension
[
20
]
.
[
8
,
21
]
are other extensions of CUSUM beyond i.i.d. framework.

Page’s CUSUM Test requires that both pre-change and post-change distributions should be known which is not always the case in real world situations.Jacob and Bansal
[
24
]
used ideas from information theory to develop a modified SPRT test as well as modified CUSUM test for the case where post-change distribution is unknown and a universal code is used in the place of log-likelihood of post-change distribution for memoryless sources.They showed the optimality of the modified test in asymptotic regime where the rate of false alarm goes to 0.They extended the idea of modified test for sources with memory under Berk’s mixing conditions
[
25
]
.

Malik and Bansal
[
26
]
considered the case where both pre-change and post-change distributions are unknown for memoryless sources.They further modified the test introduced by Jacob and Bansal
[
24
]
,by using an empirical estimate in the place of true prechange distribution without affecting the post-change distribution estimate and proved the asymptotic optimality of the proposed test.

In this paper, we extend the modification demonstrated by Malik and Bansal
[
26
]
for the more general class of stationary and ergodic finite-order Markov sources that satisfy Berk’s mixing conditions
[
7
]
and make use of Bansal and Kazakos’ extension
[
16
]
to study the performance of proposed test and establish its optimality in the asymptotic regime where the rate of false alarm goes to 0.

Our main contributions in the paper are:

1.Upper bound on probability of error for proposed test in Lemma 3.

2.Showed that the proposed test will terminate definitely under alternate hypothesis in Lemma 4.

3.Evaluated the performance of proposed test by studying linear cost function of the number of samples required to achieve a specific probability of error in Theorem 2 and the linear cost function of detection delay required to achieve a specific false alarm rate in Theorem 5.

4.Established the optimality of proposed test in the asymptotic regime in Theorem 6.

Section II introduces the preliminaries required for the rest of the paper.In Section III, we introduce the empirical estimate,the modified test and study behaviour of proposed test under pre-change and post-change distribution.In Section IV, we study the performance of modified test and prove its asymptotic optimality.

II
Preliminaries

Let
X=\{X_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
be a source with finite alphabet
\mathcal{X}
.A sequence of source symbols
x_{i},x_{i+1},\dots,x_{j}
is denoted by
x_{i}^{j}
and corresponding random variables
X_{i},X_{i+1},\dots,X_{j}
is denoted by
X_{i}^{j}
.All the logarithms will be taken to the base 2 in the remaining, unless specified otherwise.

A fixed-to-variable code (FV code) is a map
\phi_{n}:X^{n}\to\{0,1\}^{*}
, where
\{0,1\}^{*}
denotes the set of all finite-length strings.Let
L(x_{1}^{n})=|\phi_{n}(x_{1}^{n})|
denotes the length of the codeword for a given sequence
x_{1}^{n}
which satisfies prefix condition.Therefore, Kraft inequality holds, given as

\sum_{x_{1}^{n}\in X^{n}}2^{-L(x_{1}^{n})}\leq 1

Let
\mu
be the distribution of
X=\{X_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
.The entropy rate for stationary
\mu
is

H(\mu)=-\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{x_{1}^{n}\in X^{n}}\mu(x_{1}^{n})\log\mu(x_{1}^{n})

For a stationary ergodic source, Shannon-Mcmillian-Brieman theorem states

-\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\mu(x_{1}^{n})=H(\mu)\quad\mu\text{ a.s.}

A FV code with length function
L(x_{1}^{n})
is said to be
pointwise universal
if

\frac{1}{n}\left(L(x_{1}^{n})+\log\mu(x_{1}^{n})\right)\to 0\quad\mu\text{ a.s.}

For stationary ergodic sources, the existence of such codes is guaranteed
[
22
,
23
]
.For a class of sources
\mathcal{M}
, a FV code with length function
L(x_{1}^{n})
is said to be
strongly pointwise universal
if:

R_{L}(n)=\sup_{\mu\in\mathcal{M}}\max_{x_{1}^{n}\in X^{n}}\left(L(x_{1}^{n})+\log\mu(x_{1}^{n})\right)\sim o(n)

The condition for pointwise universality (2.4) is equivalent to having
\mu(J_{\epsilon}<\infty)=1
for all
\epsilon>0
, where

J_{\epsilon}=\sup_{n\geq 1}\left\{n:\left|\frac{1}{n}\left(L(x_{1}^{n})+\log\mu(x_{1}^{n})\right)\right|\geq\epsilon\right\}

(2.6)

A stronger version of the convergence condition (2.4) requires that

\mathbb{E}_{\mu}(J_{\epsilon})<\infty\quad\text{for all}\quad\epsilon>0

(2.7)

Strongly pointwise universal (2.5) codes satisfy this condition which is called 1-quick universal by Jacob
[
25
]
, for every source in the class
\mathcal{M}
.For Markov sources upto a fixed order, the existence of codes that satisfy all these conditions are known(Lempel-Ziv codes (LZ-77, LZ-78)
[
9
,
11
]
and the Context Tree Weighting code)
[
19
]
and we consider such codes only in our paper.

Theorem 1(Barron)
[
13
,
18
]
: Let
\mu
be a stationary ergodic process and
\nu
be a stationary Markov process of finite order such that
\mu\ll\nu
.Then

\frac{1}{n}\log\frac{\mu(x_{1}^{n})}{\nu(x_{1}^{n})}\to D(\mu||\nu)\quad\mu\text{ a.s.}\quad

where
D(\mu||\nu)
is the divergence rate.

The pointwise convergence in the above theorem is equivalent to having
\mu(K_{\epsilon}<\infty)=1\quad\text{for all}\quad\epsilon>0
where

K_{\epsilon}=\sup_{n\geq 1}\left\{n:\left|\frac{1}{n}\log\frac{\mu(x_{1}^{n})}{\nu(x_{1}^{n})}-D(\mu||\nu)\right|\geq\epsilon\right\}\quad

A stronger version of this convergence, called 1-quick convergence of log-likelihood ratios according to Lai’s terminology
[
12
]
, requires that

\mathbb{E}_{\mu}(K_{\epsilon})<\infty\quad\text{for all}\quad\epsilon>0.\quad

For a fixed sample hypothesis test with
H_{0}:\mu=\mu_{0}
as the null hypothesis and
H_{1}:\mu=\mu_{1}
as the alternative, consider the discriminant function

h(x_{1}^{n})=\log\mu_{1}(x_{1}^{n})-\log\mu_{0}(x_{1}^{n})-n\lambda

After observing
n
samples
x_{1}^{n}
, we reject the null hypothesis if
h(x_{1}^{n})\geq 0
. The following lemma characterizes the performance of this test.

Lemma 1
[
3
,
17
]
:
If
\mu_{0}
and
\mu_{1}
are stationary Markov process of finite order and stationary ergodic process such that
\mu_{1}\ll\mu_{0}
and
D(\mu_{1}\|\mu_{0})>\lambda
. For the test induced by the discriminant function
h(x_{1}^{n})
we have:

\displaystyle\mu_{0}\big(h(x_{1}^{n})\geq 0\big)

\displaystyle\;\leq\;2^{-n\lambda}

(2.12)

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\mu_{1}\big(h(x_{1}^{n})\geq 0\big)

\displaystyle=1.

(2.13)

Equation (2.13) remains true even if the threshold
0
is replaced by any other constant.The lemma follows immediately from the Shannon–McMillan–Breiman theorem.

Berk’s Mixing Conditions
[
7
,
16
,
25
]
:
Let

p_{n}(\Delta)=\mu_{1}\left(\frac{1}{n}\log\frac{\mu_{1}(x_{1}^{n})}{\mu_{0}(x_{1}^{n})}<\Delta\right)

and

q_{n}(\Delta)=\mu_{0}\left(\frac{1}{n}\log\frac{\mu_{0}(x_{1}^{n})}{\mu_{1}(x_{1}^{n})}<\Delta\right)

For
\Delta<D(\mu_{1}\|\mu_{0})
, let

\lim_{n\to\infty}np_{n}(\Delta)=0\quad\text{and}\quad\sum_{n\geq 1}p_{n}(\Delta)<\infty

For
\Delta<D(\mu_{0}\|\mu_{1})
, let

\lim_{n\to\infty}nq_{n}(\Delta)=0\quad\text{and}\quad\sum_{n\geq 1}q_{n}(\Delta)<\infty

Previous Work

For the case where both pre-change
\mu_{0}
and post-change
\mu_{1}
distributions are known, Page proposed a test
[
4
]
which is given as follows.

Page’s CUSUM Test (Test 1)
[
4
]
:
Starting with
n=1
, for samples
x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n}
, and
\gamma>1
, if

\max_{k\leq n}\left(\log\mu_{1}(x_{k}^{n})-\log\mu_{0}(x_{k}^{n})\right)\geq\log\gamma,

(2.18)

stop the test and decide that a change has occurred. Else, sample for
x_{n+1}
and continue the test.

Jacob–Bansal Page CUSUM Test (Test 2)
[
24
,
25
]
:
A modification of Page’s CUSUM test was proposed for cases where the post-change distribution
\mu_{1}
is unknown for i.i.d. sources and later extended for sources with memory satisfying (2.10),(2.16),(2.17). The modified CUSUM Test, where a strongly pointwise universal code is used to estimate the post-change distribution, is given as follows.

Starting with
n=1
, for samples
x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n}
,
\gamma>1
and
\lambda<D(\mu_{1}\|\mu_{0})
, if

\max_{k\leq n}\left(-L(x_{k}^{n})-\log\mu_{0}(x_{k}^{n})-n\lambda\right)\geq\log\gamma,

(2.19)

stop the test and decide that a change has occurred. Else, sample for
x_{n+1}
and continue the test.

Here,
\lambda
was first used by the authors in order to ensure a negative drift in the test statistic in the pre-change regime, as is the case in the classical Page’s test in the parametric setup.

An auxiliary stopping time for this test is defined as

N_{0}(\alpha)=\inf\left\{n:-L(x_{1}^{n})-\log\mu_{0}(x_{1}^{n})-n\lambda\geq-\log\alpha\right\}.

(2.20)

An error occurs when Test 2 stops even though no change in distribution has occurred.The probability of error for the test is calculated using the stopping time
N_{0}(\alpha)
.

Malik–Jacob-Bansal Page CUSUM Test (Test 3)
[
26
]
:
Since JB-Page’s CUSUM test requires the knowledge of pre-change distribution, a modification of this test was proposed for cases where the pre-change distribution
\mu_{0}
is unknown for i.i.d. sources. The modified CUSUM Test, where a training sequence is used to estimate the pre-change distribution, is given as follows.

Test(
\gamma>1
,
\lambda>0
):Start with
n=1
,for samples
x_{n_{0}+1},x_{n_{0}+2},\dots,x_{n_{0}+n}
.If:

\max_{1\leq k\leq n}\left(-L(x_{n_{0}+k}^{n_{0}+n})-\log\hat{\mu}_{0}({x}_{n_{0}+k}^{n_{0}+n})-n\lambda\right)\geq\log\gamma,

(2.21)

Stop the test and decide that a change has occurred. Otherwise, sample for
x_{n_{0}+n+1}
and continue the test.

Readers can refer to
[
26
]
to understand why universal code cannot be used to estimate pre-change distribution.

III-A

Estimating Pre-change Distribution

For a stream of source symbols with no change up to
n_{0}
, the test has two stages. In the first stage, we estimate the prechange distribution using the first
n_{0}
symbols, written as
x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n_{0}}
and the subsequent symbols as
x_{n_{0}+1},x_{n_{0}+2},\dots,x_{n_{0}+n}
. The prechange distribution estimate,
\hat{\mu}_{0}
, is calculated from initial sample of
n_{0}
symbols as,

\hat{\mu}_{0}(b,a)=\frac{N(b,a)\mid x_{1}^{n_{0}}}{n_{0}-1}

\hat{\mu}_{0}(b)=\frac{N(b)\mid x_{1}^{n_{0}}}{n_{0}}

\hat{\mu}_{0}(x_{1}^{n_{0}})=\frac{\hat{\mu}_{0}(x_{1},x_{2})\hat{\mu}_{0}(x_{2},x_{3})\dots\hat{\mu}_{0}(x_{n_{0}-1},x_{n_{0}})}{\hat{\mu}_{0}(x_{2})\dots\hat{\mu}_{0}(x_{n_{0}})}

\hat{\mu}_{0}(x_{1}^{n_{0}})=\hat{\mu}_{0}(x_{1})\prod_{i=2}^{n_{0}}\hat{\mu}_{0}(x_{i}/x_{i-1})

Here,
N(b)\mid x_{1}^{n_{0}}
represents the number of times the symbol
b
appears in the sequence
x_{1}^{n_{0}}
, whereas
N(b,a)\mid x_{1}^{n_{0}}
represents the number of times the symbols
b,a
jointly appear in the sequence
x_{1}^{n_{0}}
.Also,

\hat{\mu}_{0}(b)
is the fraction of times
b
appears in the sequence
x_{1}^{n_{0}}
, whereas
\hat{\mu}_{0}(b,a)
is the fraction of times
b,a
appear in the sequence
x_{1}^{n_{0}}
jointly.

We assume that
n_{0}
is large enough so that each symbol
b\in\mathcal{X}
which has non-zero probability under
{\mu}_{0}
has appeared at least once in
x_{1}^{n_{0}}
and symbol pair
(b,a)\in\mathcal{X}^{2}
which have non-zero probability under
{\mu}_{0}
have appeared at least once in
x_{1}^{n_{0}}
(else
\hat{\mu}_{0}
is not an accurate estimate of
{\mu}_{0}
).

Since the process is stationary,irreducible and aperiodic Markov Chain satisfying Berk’s Mixing Conditions,by Ergodic Theorem we can say that there exists an
n_{0}
such that,

\mu_{0}\left(\left\{x_{1}^{n_{0}}:\big|\hat{\mu}_{0}(a\mid b)-\mu_{0}(a\mid b)\big|>\delta\right\}\right)\leq\epsilon,

for one or more
(b,a)\in\mathcal{X}^{2}.

\mu_{0}\left(\{x_{1}^{n_{0}}:|\hat{\mu}_{0}(b)-\mu_{0}(b)|>\delta^{\prime}\}\right)\leq\epsilon^{\prime},

for one or more
b\in\mathcal{X}

III-B

Test

Using the estimate
\hat{\mu}_{0}
and extending the Modified CUSUM Test for Markov sources by defining it as,

Test(
\gamma>1
,
\lambda>0
): Start with
n=1
for samples
x_{n_{0}+1},x_{n_{0}+2},\dots,x_{n_{0}+n}
.If:

\max_{1\leq k\leq n}\left(-L(x_{n_{0}+k}^{n_{0}+n})-\log\hat{\mu}_{0}({x}_{n_{0}+k}^{n_{0}+n})-n\lambda\right)\geq\log\gamma

D(\mu_{0}||\hat{\mu}_{0})<\lambda<D(\mu_{1}||\hat{\mu}_{0})

D(\mu_{0}||\hat{\mu}_{0})<D(\mu_{1}||\hat{\mu}_{0})

Auxiliary stopping time for this modified test is defined as,

N(\alpha)=\inf\left\{n:\left(-L(x_{n_{0}+k}^{n_{0}+n})-\log\hat{\mu}_{0}(x_{n_{0}+k}^{n_{0}+n})-n\lambda\right)\geq-\log\alpha\right\}

(3.10)

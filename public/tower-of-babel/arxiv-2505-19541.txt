. Let X X be a ℚ \mathbb{Q} -factorial weak Fano 3 3 -fold with at worst isolated canonical singularities. We show that the ℚ \mathbb{Q} -Fano index of X X is at most 61 61 .

1.
Introduction

Throughout this paper, we work over the complex number field
\mathbb{C}
and adopt the standard notation from
[
17
]
.

A normal projective variety is called
Fano
(resp.,
weak Fano
) if its anti-canonical divisor is ample (resp., nef and big). According to the minimal model program, Fano varieties with mild singularities serve as fundamental building blocks of algebraic varieties.

For a Fano variety
X
with mild singularities (e.g., terminal or canonical singularities), its
\mathbb{Q}
-Fano index
\text{\rm q}_{\mathbb{Q}}(X)
is defined to be

\displaystyle\text{\rm q}_{\mathbb{Q}}(X){}

\displaystyle\coloneq\max\{q\mid-K_{X}\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}qA,\quad A\in\Cl(X)\}.

The
\mathbb{Q}
-Fano index of a terminal Fano
3
-fold is quite well-understood, which belongs to
\{1,\dots,9,11,13,17,19\}
(see
[
26
,
23
]
) and it plays a crucial role in the classification of terminal Fano
3
-folds
[
26
,
3
,
4
,
22
,
23
,
24
,
12
,
18
,
19
]
. However, for canonical Fano
3
-folds, progress has been limited due to the absence of Riemann–Roch formula and the classification of canonical singularities, and even a good upper bound for
\mathbb{Q}
-Fano indices remains unknown. In literature, for a canonical Fano
3
-fold
X
, we only have a very rough upper bound
\text{\rm q}_{\mathbb{Q}}(X)\leq 840^{2}\cdot 324\approx 2.3\cdot 10^{8}
(cf.
[
1
]
*Lemma 2.3): suppose that
-K_{X}\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}qA
for some ample Weil divisor
A
, then we know that
rK_{X}
is Cartier for some positive integer
r\leq 840
by
[
6
]
*Proposition 2.4 and
(-K_{X})^{3}\leq 324
by
[
11
]
, which implies that

q\leq qA\cdot(-rK_{X})^{2}=r^{2}(-K_{X})^{3}\leq 840^{2}\cdot 324.

Motivated by the terminal case, it should be expected that weighted projective spaces have larger
\mathbb{Q}
-Fano indices, so we are interested in the following conjecture:

Conjecture 1.1

(cf.
[
27
]
*Conjecture 3.7)
.

Let
X
be a canonical weak Fano
3
-fold. Then
\text{\rm q}_{\mathbb{Q}}(X)\leq 66
.

Kasprzyk classified terminal or canonical toric Fano
3
-folds in
[
12
,
13
]
. The following are some motivating examples with large
\mathbb{Q}
-Fano indices.

Example 1.2

(
[
13
]
)
.

(1)

The weighted projective space
\mathbb{P}(5,6,22,33)
is a
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial canonical Fano
3
-fold of Picard number
1
. It has non-isolated singularities and its
\mathbb{Q}
-Fano index is
66
.

(2)

The weighted projective space
\mathbb{P}(3,5,11,19)
is a
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial Fano
3
-fold of Picard number
1
with isolated canonical singularities. Its
\mathbb{Q}
-Fano index is
38
.

(3)

The weighted projective space
\mathbb{P}(5,8,9,11)
is a
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial Fano
3
-fold of Picard number
1
with isolated canonical singularities which is non-Gorenstein at a crepant center. Its
\mathbb{Q}
-Fano index is
33
.

In this paper, we provide an affirmative answer to Conjecture
1.1
for
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial weak Fano
3
-folds with isolated canonical singularities.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
X
be a
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial weak Fano
3
-fold with at worst isolated canonical singularities. Then
\text{\rm q}_{\mathbb{Q}}(X)\leq 61
.

The key idea is to use a Kawamata–Miyaoka type inequality established in
[
10
]
relating
c_{1}(X)^{3}
with
c_{2}(X)\cdot c_{1}(X)
, along with a characterization of
c_{1}(X)^{3}
in terms of
\mathbb{Q}
-Fano indices. This approach brings us very close to proving Conjecture
1.1
for isolated singularities, except for two particularly stubborn cases with
\text{\rm q}_{\mathbb{Q}}(X)\in\{67,71\}
. We put our main effort into addressing these two exceptional cases by studying the geometry of
X
in more detail. To this end, we develop a Riemann–Roch type formula for canonical
3
-folds (see §
4
) and study the geometry of foliations of rank
2
(see §
5
).

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Singularities

Let
X
be a normal variety such that
K_{X}
is
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier. The
Gorenstein index

r_{X}
of
X
is defined as the smallest positive integer
m
such that
mK_{X}
is Cartier. Let
f\colon Y\to X
be a proper birational morphism. A prime divisor
E
on
Y
is called
a divisor over

X
, and the image
f(E)
is called the
center
of
E
on
X
.
Write

K_{Y}=f^{*}K_{X}+\sum_{E}a(E,X)E,

where
a(E,X)\in\mathbb{Q}
is called the
discrepancy
of
E
. We say that
X
has
terminal
(resp.,
canonical
) singularities if
a(E,X)>0
(resp.,
a(E,X)\geq 0
) for any exceptional divisor
E
over
X
. Often we just simply say that
X
is
terminal
or
canonical
, respectively. A
crepant center
of
X
is the center of an exceptional divisor
E
over
X
with
a(E,X)=0
. We say that
X
has at worst
isolated singularities
if its singular locus
\Sing(X)
is either empty or consists of closed points. For example, it is well-known that normal surface singularities and
3
-dimensional terminal singularities are isolated (
[
17
]
*Corollary 5.18).

If
X
is a canonical variety, then by the minimal model program, there exists a
terminalization

f\colon Y\to X
such that
Y
is a terminal variety with
f^{*}K_{X}=K_{Y}
and
f
is projective birational (see
[
2
]
*Corollary 1.4.3).

2.2.
Fano index

Let
X
be a canonical weak Fano variety. We can define the
\mathbb{Q}
-Fano index
of
X
by

\displaystyle\text{\rm q}_{\mathbb{Q}}(X){}

\displaystyle\coloneq\max\{q\mid-K_{X}\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}qA,\quad A\in\Cl(X)\}.

It is known that
\Cl(X)
is a finitely generated Abelian group, so
\text{\rm q}_{\mathbb{Q}}(X)
is a positive integer. For more details, see
[
9
]
*§ 2 or
[
23
]
.

We can use the following lemma to reduce Theorem
1.3
to the Picard number
1
case.

Lemma 2.1

(cf.
[
10
]
*Lemma 5.1, Proposition 5.2)
.

Let
X
be a
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial weak Fano
3
-fold with at worst isolated canonical singularities and with
\text{\rm q}_{\mathbb{Q}}(X)\geq 7
. Then there exists a
3
-fold
Y
with the following properties:

(1)

Y
is a
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial Fano
3
-fold of Picard number
1
with at worst isolated canonical singularities;

(2)

\text{\rm q}_{\mathbb{Q}}(Y)\geq\text{\rm q}_{\mathbb{Q}}(X)\geq 7
.

Proof.

Fix a Weil divisor
A
such that
-K_{X}\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}\text{\rm q}_{\mathbb{Q}}(X)A
. We can run a
K
-MMP on
X
which ends up with a Mori fiber space
Y\to T
where
Y
is
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial and canonical. Then
-K_{Y}\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}\text{\rm q}_{\mathbb{Q}}(X)A^{\prime}
where
A^{\prime}
is the strict transform of
A
on
Y
.

If
\dim T\geq 1
, then for a general fiber
F
of
Y\to T
, we have
-K_{F}\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}\text{\rm q}_{\mathbb{Q}}(X)A^{\prime}|_{F}
and
F
is either
\mathbb{P}^{1}
or a canonical del Pezzo surface, but this contradicts
[
27
]
*Proposition 3.3 as
\text{\rm q}_{\mathbb{Q}}(X)\geq 7
.

Hence
\dim T=0
, which means that
Y
is a
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial canonical Fano
3
-fold of Picard number
1
. Suppose that
Y
has worse than isolated singularities, then
\Sing(Y)
contains a curve
C
, which means that
C
is a crepant center as
3
-dimensional terminal singularities are isolated. As
X
has canonical singularities and
X\dashrightarrow Y
is obtained by a
K
-MMP,
X
and
Y
are isomorphic over the generic point of
C
by
[
17
]
*Lemma 3.38, which contradicts the fact that
X
has only isolated singularities.
∎

2.3.
Reid’s basket and formula

Let
X
be a canonical projective
3
-fold. According to Reid
[
25
]
*(10.2), there is a collection of pairs of integers (permitting weights)

B_{X}=\{(r_{i},b_{i})\mid i=1,\cdots,s;0<b_{i}\leq\frac{r_{i}}{2};b_{i}\text{ is coprime to }r_{i}\}

associated to
X
, called
Reid’s basket
, where a pair
(r,b)
corresponds to an orbifold point of type
\frac{1}{r}(1,-1,b)
which comes from locally deforming non-Gorenstein singularities of a terminalization of
X
. In other words,
B_{X}
is identified with the collection of virtual orbifold points of
X
. By definition, if
Y
is a terminalization of
X
, then
B_{Y}=B_{X}
. Denote by
\mathcal{R}_{X}
the collection of
r_{i}
(permitting weights) appearing in
B_{X}
. Note that the Gorenstein index
r_{X}
of
X
is just
{\text{l.c.m.}}\{r\mid r\in\mathcal{R}_{X}\}
.

Let
D
be a
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier Weil divisor on
X
. We say that
D
satisfies
Reid’s condition
if locally at any point
P\in X
,
D\sim iK_{X}
for some integer
i
(depending on
P
). Note that if
X
has terminal singularities, then
D
always satisfies Reid’s condition by
[
15
]
*Corollary 5.2. For
D
satisfying Reid’s condition, according to
[
25
]
*(10.2), there exists an orbifold Riemann–Roch formula, called
Reid’s formula
:

(2.1)

\chi(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(D))=\chi(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})+\frac{1}{12}D\cdot(D-K_{X})\cdot(2D-K_{X})+\frac{1}{12}c_{2}(X)\cdot D+\sum_{Q\in B_{X}}c_{Q}(D),

where the last sum runs over Reid’s basket of orbifold points. If the orbifold point
Q
corresponds to
(r,b)\in B_{X}
and
i=i_{D}
is the local index of divisor
D
at
Q
(i.e., after taking terminalization and local deformation,
D\sim iK_{X}
around
Q
for some integer
i\geq 0
), then

c_{Q}(D)\coloneq-\frac{i(r^{2}-1)}{12r}+\sum_{j=0}^{i-1}\frac{\overline{jb}(r-\overline{jb})}{2r}.

Here the symbol
\overline{\cdot}
means the smallest residue mod
r
and
\sum_{j=0}^{-1}\coloneq 0
. The term
c_{2}(X)\cdot D
is defined to be

c_{2}(W)\cdot\pi^{*}D
for any resolution
\pi\colon W\to X
. In particular, by
[
25
]
*(10.3), we have

(2.2)

\displaystyle c_{2}(X)\cdot c_{1}(X)+\sum_{r\in\mathcal{R}_{X}}\left(r-\frac{1}{r}\right)=24\chi(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}),

and

(2.3)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}c_{1}(X)^{3}+3\chi(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})-\sum_{(r,b)\in B_{X}}\frac{b(r-b)}{2r}=\chi(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(-K_{X}))\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}.

Remark 2.2
.

For a canonical weak Fano
3
-fold
X
,
\chi(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})=1
by the Kawamata–Viehweg vanishing theorem and
c_{2}(X)\cdot c_{1}(X)>0
by
[
8
]
*Corollary 7.3.

2.4.
Kawamata–Miyaoka type inequality for canonical Fano threefolds

The following theorem is a detailed version of the Kawamata–Miyaoka type inequality of
[
10
]
*Theorem 3.8. Here we refer to
[
10
]
*§ 3 for the definition of
generalized second Chern class

\hat{c}_{2}(X)
of a
3
-fold
X
. For the application in this paper, we just note that if
X
has only isolated singularities, then
\hat{c}_{2}(X)
is just the usual
c_{2}(X)
.

Theorem 2.3

(
[
10
]
*Theorem 3.8)
.

Let
X
be a
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial canonical Fano
3
-fold of Picard number
1
. Let
q\coloneq\text{\rm q}_{\mathbb{Q}}(X)
be the
\mathbb{Q}
-Fano index of
X
. Let

(2.4)

0=\mathcal{E}_{0}\subsetneq\mathcal{E}_{1}\subsetneq\dots\subsetneq\mathcal{E}_{l}=\mathcal{T}_{X}

be the Harder–Narasimhan filtration of the tangent sheaf
\mathcal{T}_{X}
with respect to
c_{1}(X)
, where
1\leq l\leq 3
. Denote by
r_{1}\coloneq\rank\mathcal{E}_{1}
and take
p
to be the integer such that
c_{1}(\mathcal{E}_{l-1})\equiv\frac{p}{q}c_{1}(X)
. Here
p<q
, and
p>\frac{l-1}{l}q
if
l>1
. Then we have

c_{1}(X)^{3}\leq\begin{cases}3\hat{c}_{2}(X)\cdot c_{1}(X)&\text{if }(l,r_{1})=(1,3);\\
\frac{16}{5}\hat{c}_{2}(X)\cdot c_{1}(X)&\text{if }(l,r_{1})=(2,1);\\
\frac{4q^{2}}{p(4q-3p)}\hat{c}_{2}(X)\cdot c_{1}(X)\leq\frac{4q^{2}}{q^{2}+2q-3}\hat{c}_{2}(X)\cdot c_{1}(X)&\text{if }(l,r_{1})=(2,2);\\
\frac{4q^{2}}{-4p^{2}+6pq-q^{2}}\hat{c}_{2}(X)\cdot c_{1}(X)\leq\frac{4q^{2}}{q^{2}+2q-4}\hat{c}_{2}(X)\cdot c_{1}(X)&\text{if }(l,r_{1})=(3,1).\end{cases}

In particular, we have
c_{1}(X)^{3}<4\hat{c}_{2}(X)\cdot c_{1}(X)
.

Proof.

We refer the reader to
[
10
]
*Proof of Theorem 3.8 with some necessary explanations. Recall that in
[
10
]
*Proof of Theorem 3.8, we denote
\mathcal{F}_{i}\coloneqq(\mathcal{E}_{i}/\mathcal{E}_{i-1})^{**}
and denote
q_{i}\geq 1
the unique positive integer such that
c_{1}(\mathcal{F}_{i})\equiv\frac{q_{i}}{q}c_{1}(X)
. Then
p=\sum_{i=1}^{l-1}q_{i}
.

If
(l,r_{1})=(1,3)
, that is,
\mathcal{T}_{X}
is semistable with respect to
c_{1}(X)
, then the inequality follows directly from the
\mathbb{Q}
-Bogomolov–Gieseker inequality
[
16
]
*Lemma 6.5.

If
(l,r_{1})=(2,1)
, then the inequality follows from
[
10
]
*Proof of Theorem 3.8, Case 1.

If
(l,r_{1})=(2,2)
, then from
[
10
]
*Proof of Theorem 3.8, Case 2, we have
p=q_{1}
which means that
\frac{2}{3}q<p\leq q-1
and

6\hat{c}_{2}(X)\cdot c_{1}(X)-2c_{1}(X)^{3}\geq-\frac{(3p-2q)^{2}}{2q^{2}}c_{1}(X)^{3},

which yields the desired inequality.

If
(l,r_{1})=(3,1)
, then from
[
10
]
*Proof of Theorem 3.8, Case 3, we have
p=q_{1}+q_{2}
with
2\leq q_{2}\leq q_{1}-1\leq\frac{q}{2}-1
and

\displaystyle 6\hat{c}_{2}(X)\cdot c_{1}(X)-2c_{1}(X)^{3}

\displaystyle\geq-\left((q_{1}-q_{2})^{2}+(2q_{1}+q_{2}-q)^{2}+(q_{1}+2q_{2}-q)^{2}\right)\cdot\frac{1}{q^{2}}c_{1}(X)^{3}

\displaystyle=-\left((2q_{1}-p)^{2}+(q_{1}+p-q)^{2}+(2p-q_{1}-q)^{2}\right)\cdot\frac{1}{q^{2}}c_{1}(X)^{3}

\displaystyle\geq-\left(6p^{2}-9pq+\frac{7}{2}q^{2}\right)\frac{1}{q^{2}}c_{1}(X)^{3},

which yields the desired inequality. Here for the last inequality, we use the fact that the first two leading terms of
(2q_{1}-p)^{2}+(q_{1}+p-q)^{2}+(2p-q_{1}-q)^{2}
as a polynomial of
q_{1}
is
6q_{1}^{2}-6pq_{1}
, so it attains the maximal value at
q_{1}=\frac{q}{2}
as
q_{1}>\frac{p}{2}
.
∎

4.
A Riemann–Roch formula for canonical Fano threefolds

In the study of Fano indices on canonical Fano
3
-folds, one issue is that we could not apply Reid’s Riemann–Roch formula as in general a Weil divisor does not satisfy Reid’s condition. In this section, we establish a Riemann–Roch formula for canonical Fano
3
-folds based on Reid’s formula. The idea is to take a special transform called a Weil pullback of the divisor on
X
to a terminalization.

Definition 4.1

(Sequential terminalization)
.

Let
X
be a variety with canonical singularities. A projective birational morphism
f\colon Y\to X
together with a sequence of projective birational morphisms
f_{k}\colon X_{k}\to X_{k-1}
for
1\leq k\leq m
is called a
sequential terminalization
if the following properties are satisfied:

(1)

Y
is terminal;

(2)

X_{0}=X
,
X_{m}=Y
,
f=f_{m}\circ\dots\circ f_{1}
;

(3)

for any
1\leq k\leq m
,
f_{k}
is crepant, i.e.,
f_{k}^{*}K_{X_{k-1}}=K_{X_{k}}
;

(4)

for any
1\leq k\leq m
, denote by
E_{k}
the sum of exceptional divisors of
f_{k}
, then either
E_{k}=0
, or
E_{k}
is prime and
-E_{k}
is
f_{k}
-nef.

For simplicity, we just say that
f\colon Y\to X
is a sequential terminalization.

Remark 4.2
.

We can always construct a sequential terminalization by applying
[
2
]
*Corollary 1.4.3 repeatedly. See also
[
14
]
*Corollary 2.6.

Definition 4.3

(Weil pullback)
.

Let
X
be a variety with canonical singularities. Let
f\colon Y\to X
be a sequential terminalization. For any
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier Weil divisor
D
on
X
, we define the
Weil pullback

f^{\lfloor*\rfloor}(D)
of
D
on
Y
as the following: keep the notation in Definition
4.1
, take
D_{0}=D
, and define inductively
D_{k}=\lfloor f_{k}^{*}D_{k-1}\rfloor
for
1\leq k\leq m
, and finally set
f^{\lfloor*\rfloor}(D)\coloneq D_{m}
. Here we can inductively show that
D_{k}
is
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier since
f_{k}^{*}D_{k-1}
and
E_{k}
are
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier. So
f^{\lfloor*\rfloor}(D)
is a
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier Weil divisor on
Y
.

We can compare the cohomologies of Weil pullback with the original divisor by the following lemma.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
X
be a projective variety with canonical singularities. Let
f\colon Y\to X
be a sequential terminalization. Then for any
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier Weil divisor
D
on
X
and any
i\geq 0
, we have

H^{i}(Y,\mathcal{O}_{Y}(f^{\lfloor*\rfloor}(D)))=H^{i}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(D)).

Proof.

Keep the notation in Definition
4.1
. It suffices to prove that

H^{i}(X_{k},\mathcal{O}_{X_{k}}(D_{k}))=H^{i}(X_{k-1},\mathcal{O}_{X_{k-1}}(D_{k-1}))

for every
k\geq 1
and
i\geq 0
.

By construction, we have
D_{k}=\lfloor f_{k}^{*}D_{k-1}\rfloor=f_{k}^{*}D_{k-1}-a_{k}E_{k}
for some
0\leq a_{k}<1
. So
f_{k*}\mathcal{O}_{X_{k}}(D_{k})=\mathcal{O}_{X_{k-1}}(D_{k-1})
by
[
20
]
*Lemma II.2.11. Moreover,
D_{k}
is
f_{k}
-nef and
f_{k}
-big and
K_{X_{k}}
is
f_{k}
-trivial as
f_{k}
is crepant. Hence the Kawamata–Viehweg vanishing theorem yields that

R^{j}f_{k*}\mathcal{O}_{X_{k}}(D_{k})=R^{j}f_{k*}\mathcal{O}_{X_{k}}(K_{X_{k}}+D_{k}-K_{X_{k}})=0

for
j>0
. By a spectral sequence argument, it follows that

H^{i}(X_{k},\mathcal{O}_{X_{k}}(D_{k}))=H^{i}(X_{k-1},f_{k*}\mathcal{O}_{X_{k}}(D_{k}))=H^{i}(X_{k-1},\mathcal{O}_{X_{k-1}}(D_{k-1}))

for every
i\geq 0
.
∎

The Weil pullback behaves not so well as it is not additive but only superadditive, namely, for
2
divisors
D
and
D^{\prime}
,
f^{\lfloor*\rfloor}(D+D^{\prime})\geq f^{\lfloor*\rfloor}(D)+f^{\lfloor*\rfloor}(D^{\prime})
. We have additivity in the following case.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
X
be a variety with canonical singularities. Let
f\colon Y\to X
be a sequential terminalization. Let
D
and
D^{\prime}
be
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier Weil divisors on
X
. If the usual pullback
f^{*}D^{\prime}
is a Weil divisor on
Y
, then
f^{\lfloor*\rfloor}(D+D^{\prime})=f^{\lfloor*\rfloor}(D)+f^{*}D^{\prime}
.

Proof.

Keep the notation in Definition
4.1
. Take
D^{\prime}_{0}=D^{\prime}
, and define inductively
D^{\prime}_{k}=f_{k}^{*}D^{\prime}_{k-1}
for
1\leq k\leq m
. Then as
f^{*}D^{\prime}
is a Weil divisor,
D^{\prime}_{k}
is a Weil divisor for
1\leq k\leq m
. This implies that

\lfloor f_{k}^{*}(D_{k-1}+D^{\prime}_{k-1})\rfloor=\lfloor f_{k}^{*}D_{k-1}+f_{k}^{*}D^{\prime}_{k-1}\rfloor=\lfloor f_{k}^{*}D_{k-1}\rfloor+f_{k}^{*}D^{\prime}_{k-1}=D_{k}+D^{\prime}_{k}

for
1\leq k\leq m
. So we get
f^{\lfloor*\rfloor}(D+D^{\prime})=f^{\lfloor*\rfloor}(D)+f^{*}D^{\prime}
.
∎

Proposition 4.6
.

Let
X
be a projective canonical
3
-fold. Let
f\colon Y\to X
be a sequential terminalization. Let
D
be a
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier Weil divisor on

. Then

\displaystyle\chi(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(D))-\chi(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(D+K_{X}))

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}(f^{\lfloor*\rfloor}(D))^{2}\cdot K_{Y}+2\chi(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})-\sum_{(r,b)\in B_{X}}\frac{\overline{i_{f^{\lfloor*\rfloor}(D)}b}(r-\overline{i_{f^{\lfloor*\rfloor}(D)}b})}{2r},

where
i_{f^{\lfloor*\rfloor}(D)}
is the local index of
{f^{\lfloor*\rfloor}(D)}
at the orbifold point of type
(r,b)\in B_{X}
.

Proof.

We simply denote
S\coloneq f^{\lfloor*\rfloor}(D)
. As

is crepant, by Lemma
4.5
,

f^{\lfloor*\rfloor}(D+K_{X})={S}+K_{Y}.

So by Lemma
4.4
and Reid’s formula (

),

\displaystyle\chi(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(D))-\chi(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(D+K_{X}))

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\chi(Y,\mathcal{O}_{Y}(S))-\chi(Y,\mathcal{O}_{Y}(S+K_{Y}))

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}S^{2}\cdot K_{Y}-\frac{1}{12}c_{2}(Y)\cdot K_{Y}+\sum_{Q\in B_{Y}}(c_{Q}(S)-c_{Q}(S+K_{Y})).

For an orbifold point

corresponding to
(r,b)\in B_{Y}
, if

is the local index of

at

, then

is the local index of

at

So the conclusion follows from (

) and the fact that

.
∎

Corollary 4.7
.

Let
X
be a canonical Fano
3
-fold. Suppose that
-K_{X}\equiv qA
for some positive rational number

and ample Weil divisor

. Let

{f^{\lfloor*\rfloor}(sA)}

.

Since

\displaystyle\chi(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}){}

\displaystyle=h^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})=1,

\displaystyle\chi(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(sA)){}

\displaystyle=h^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(sA)),

\displaystyle\chi(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(sA+K_{X})){}

The the conclusion follows directly from Proposition

.

(cf.

Remark 4.9
.

In general, there are two main issues in applications of the formula in Corollary
4.7
: the first is to determine the intersection number
(f^{\lfloor*\rfloor}(sA))^{2}\cdot K_{Y}
, and the second is to determine the local index
i_{t}
. Both issues are due to the complicated definition of
f^{\lfloor*\rfloor}(sA)
. We use the following two lemmas to treat these issues in some special case.

Lemma 4.10
.

Let
X
be a projective variety of dimension
n
with canonical singularities. Let
f\colon Y\to X
be a sequential terminalization. For any
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier Weil divisor
D
on
X
and any nef divisor
H
on
X
,
(f^{\lfloor*\rfloor}(D))^{2}\cdot(f^{*}H)^{n-2}\leq D^{2}\cdot H^{n-2}
. Moreover, the equality holds if
X
is smooth in codimension
2
.

Proof.

As nef divisors are limits of ample divisors, it suffices to prove the statement for ample
H
. After taking a multiple, we may assume that
H
is very ample. We may take very general
H_{1},\cdots,H_{n-2}\in|H|
such that
S\coloneq H_{1}\cap\cdots\cap H_{n-2}\subset X
and
T\coloneq f^{*}H_{1}\cap\cdots\cap f^{*}H_{n-2}\subset Y
are normal projective surfaces.

By construction, we may write
f^{\lfloor*\rfloor}(D)=f^{*}D-E
where
E
is an effective
f
-exceptional
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor. Here
E|_{T}
is exceptional over
S
. So by the projection formula and the Hodge index theorem,

(f^{\lfloor*\rfloor}(D))^{2}\cdot(f^{*}H)^{n-2}=(f^{*}D|_{T}-E|_{T})^{2}=f^{*}D|_{T}^{2}+E|_{T}^{2}\leq D|_{S}^{2}=D^{2}\cdot H^{n-2}.

Since
f
is crepant,
f
is isomorphic over the smooth locus of
X
. So
if further
X
is smooth in codimension
2
, then
T
is isomorphic to
S
by the construction, which gives the equality.
∎

Lemma 4.11
.

In Corollary
4.7
, suppose that
X
is Gorenstein along all crepant centers, then for any integer
s
and any orbifold point in
B_{X}
, the local index satisfies
i_{f^{\lfloor*\rfloor}(sA)}=s\cdot i_{f^{\lfloor*\rfloor}(A)}.

Proof.

Let
f\colon Y\to X
be a sequential terminalization, then
f
is isomorphic in codimension
1
outside crepant centers of

. Hence by assumption, locally at a non-Gorenstein point

there is no exceptional divisor, which means that locally we have

f^{\lfloor*\rfloor}(sA)=f^{*}(sA)=sf^{*}(A)=sf^{\lfloor*\rfloor}(A).

This concludes the lemma.
∎

5.
Geometry of foliations of rank two

We gather some basic notions and facts regarding foliations on varieties. We refer the reader to
[
7
]
*§ 3 and the references therein for a more detailed explanation.

Definition 5.1
.

A
foliation
on a normal variety
X
is a non-zero coherent subsheaf
\mathcal{F}
of the tangent sheaf
\mathcal{T}_{X}
such that

(1)

\mathcal{T}_{X}/\mathcal{F}
is torsion-free, and

(2)

\mathcal{F}
is closed under the Lie bracket.

The
canonical divisor
of a foliation
\mathcal{F}
is any Weil divisor
K_{\mathcal{F}}
on
X
such that
\det(\mathcal{F})\cong\mathcal{O}_{X}(-K_{\mathcal{F}})
.

Let
X_{\circ}\subset X_{\reg}
be the largest open subset over which
\mathcal{T}_{X}/\mathcal{F}
is locally free. A
leaf
of
\mathcal{F}
is a maximal connected and immersed holomorphic submanifold
L\subset X_{\circ}
such that
\mathcal{T}_{L}=\mathcal{F}|_{L}
. A leaf is called
algebraic
if it is open in its Zariski closure and a foliation
\mathcal{F}
is said to be
algebraically integrable
if its leaves are algebraic.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be an algebraically integrable foliation on a normal projective variety
X
. Then there exists a diagram, called the
family of leaves
, as follows (
[
7
]
*§ 3.6):

(5.1)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces U}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces T}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces e}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g}

with the following properties:

(1)

U
and
T
are normal projective varieties;

(2)

e
is birational and
g
is an equidimensional fibration;

(3)

For a general
t\in T
,
e
is finite on
g^{-1}(t)
and the image
e(g^{-1}(t))
is the closure of a leaf of
\mathcal{F}
.

Assume in addition that
K_{\mathcal{F}}
is
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier, there exists a canonically defined effective

-exceptional
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor
\Delta_{U}
such that

(5.2)

K_{U}-g^{*}K_{T}-R(g)+\Delta_{U}\sim K_{e^{-1}\mathcal{F}}+\Delta_{U}\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}e^{*}K_{\mathcal{F}},

where

is the

of

, where

runs through all prime divisors on

, see
[

]

In this paper, we are mainly interested in a foliation of rank

on a

in (

) is a smooth rational curve, the study of

in the rank

case is more challenging as a general fiber of

is not necessarily smooth. So we use the following lemma to pass to a modification of

.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be an algebraically integrable foliation on a normal projective variety

such that

is
\mathbb{Q}

where the

-horizontal part of

Proof.

The existence of

is by

over

Theorem 5.3
.

Let

be a

and of Picard number

. Let

. Furthermore, if

is isomorphic over

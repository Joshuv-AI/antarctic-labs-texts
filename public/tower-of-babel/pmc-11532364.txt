Increased FT3/FT4 ratio in a certain range is associated with decreased glycemic variability in patients with type 2 diabetes
Authors: Ying Li, Tianrong Pan, Lingyu Wang, Yue Wang, Yu Gong, Guojuan Wang, Qianqian Zhang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Thyroid hormone (TH) plays a crucial role in regulating glucose metabolism. However, the potential impact of the FT3/FT4 ratio, which reflects peripheral sensitivity to thyroid hormones, on glycemic variability in patients with type 2 diabetes (T2DM), has not been previously reported. To investigate the correlation between the FT3/FT4 ratio and glycemic variability in individuals with T2DM. In this retrospective analysis, a total of 468 inpatients with T2DM underwent continuous glucose monitoring (CGM) systems for a period of 6–14 days. Baseline clinical characteristics, laboratory tests, and CGM parameters were documented to investigate the correlation between FT3/FT4 ratio and CGM parameters. The levels of HBA, MG, SD, CV, LAGE, MODD and TAR2Scale were all higher in FT3/FT4Q1 compared with FT3/FT4Q2, FT3/FT4Q3 and FT3/FT4Q4 (all P < 0.01). Additionally, TIR was lower in FT3/FT4Q1 compared with the other quartiles (P < 0.01). Smooth curve fitting and saturation effect analysis revealed that there are curve-like relationships between the FT3/FT4 ratio and SD, MAGE, MODD and TAR2Scale. The inflection points of the fitted curves were found to be at FT3/FT4 = 0.279, 0.237, 0.253 and 0.282 respectively (all P < 0.05). Prior to the inflection point, the FT3/FT4 ratio was negatively related to SD, MAGE, MODD and TAR2Scale (all P < 0.05). Furthermore, The FT3/FT4 ratio exhibits a negative linear correlation with HBA and MG, while demonstrating a positive linear relationship with TIR (all P < 0.05). Binary logistic regression demonstrated that the FT3/FT4 ratio was independently related to HBA (P = 0.001), MG (P = 0.01), TAR2Scale (P = 0.003), LAGE (P = 0.014) and MAGE (P < 0.001). The increased FT3/FT4 ratio within a certain range (FT3/FT4 ≤ 0.282) is associated with decreased blood glucose variability and increased TIR. The FT3/FT4 ratio may act as a potential independent protective factor for glycemic fluctuation and glycemic control in patients with T2DM when it increases within a specific range.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-024-76074-w.

Diabetes mellitus (DM) is a chronic metabolic disease characterized by a prolonged course and multiple organ complications. Variability in glycemic profiles is an independent risk factor for various diabetic complications, including diabetic neuropathy1, macrovascular and microvascular complications2, cardiovascular disease3, sarcopenia4, and diabetic retinopathy5. Hypoglycemia has also been linked to cardiovascular events6, cognitive disorders7, and worsening nerve fiber loss8. All of these severe consequences contribute to a poor quality of life and reduced lifespan in middle-aged and elderly individuals.

Thyroid dysfunction and diabetes mellitus are closely linked9. The FT3/FT4 ratio reflects peripheral sensitivity to thyroid hormones, which plays a crucial role in metabolic syndrome, including insulin resistance10, Insulin resistance further influences the level of serum glucose. However, it is unknown whether the FT3/FT4 ratio impacts glycemic variability similarly to insulin resistance. However, there is currently no study demonstrating the relationship between peripheral thyroid hormone sensitivity and glycemic variability.

Managing glycemic variability is crucial for achieving optimal glycemic control and preventing diabetic complications. Hemoglobin A1c (HbA1c) is a predictive factor of the development of diabetes complications11, but could not reflect glycemic variability. The introduction of continuous glucose monitoring (CGM) systems into clinical practice since 2000 has provided a new technology for monitoring interstitial glucose levels every 5 min, recording and storing the data12. CGMs not only record average glucose levels but also capture information on glucose variability, hypoglycemia and hyperglycemia. Time in range (TIR) has been consistently recommended as a reliable index for glycemic control by international consensus statements and guidelines11,13. There is substantial evidence suggesting that TIR itself can predict the future risk of diabetic complications14,15.

The purpose of this study was to examine the correlation between FT3/FT4 ratio and glycemic variability as assessed by CGMs. The ultimate goal is to enhance glucose control, minimize the risk of hypoglycemia or hyperglycemia, and ultimately delay severe complications in order to improve the overall quality of life for individuals with diabetes mellitus.

Methods

Study design

From Mar 2023 to October 2023, a total of 1209 patients with T2DM were admitted to the Endocrinology Department of HeiFei City First People’s Hospital. All patients were admitted through outpatient procedures due to poor glycemic control. After excluding ineligible participants, 458 individuals were enrolled who met the inclusion criteria (Fig. 1).

Continuous blood glucose was measured using an interstitial CGM system (SIBIONICS, hospital version, Science and Technology Company of ShenZhen City, MARD value is 8.83%) following standard protocols after admitting to hospital. The sensors were placed on the upper back part of the arm, and glucose levels were recorded every 5 min for a minimum of 6 consecutive 24-hour periods. After discharge, the hospital version was converted to the personal version and continued to be worn for a few days. Finally, the mean values of CGM indexes during hospitalization and after discharge were gathered, thereby mitigating the impact of hospitalization on blood glucose levels.

The diagnostic criteria for diabetes include: (1) Presence of polydipsia, polyuria, polyphagia, weight loss, and a random blood glucose level ≥ 11.1mmol/l, (2) Fasting plasma glucose (FPG) ≥ 7.0mmol/L, (3) Oral glucose tolerance test (OGTT) with 2-hour postprandial blood glucose ≥ 11.1mmol/l16. The laboratory of our hospital did not use NGSP certification and DCCT standardized test methods to detect HbA1c, so HbA1c was not included in the diagnostic criteria.

Inclusion criteria

(1) Participants must be aged ≥ 18 years old. (2) BMI ≥ 18.5 kg/m2. (3) Participants must meet the diagnostic criteria for type 2 diabetes. (4) Participants must be willing to wear continuous glucose monitoring (CGM) and commit to wearing it for 6–14 days. (5) Participants must consent to participate in the study and agree to the collection of blood samples.

Exclusion criteria

(1) Individuals diagnosed with type 1 diabetes mellitus and other specific types of diabetes. (2) Individuals who experienced acute complications such as diabetic ketoacidosis and acute pancreatitis in the past 3 months. (3) Individuals with severe renal or liver impairment. (4) Individuals with a history of hyperthyroidism or hypothyroidism, or were diagnosed with clinical hyperthyroidism or hypothyroidism according to the thyroid hormones levels. (5) Individuals with conditions of severe malnutrition, infection, use of steroid hormones that affect blood sugar and thyroid hormone outcomes. (6) Individuals with organ malignant tumors or cancers. (7) Those who have an allergy to medical adhesive tape. (8) Lack of essential baseline data.

Procedures

The sex, age, duration of Type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM-duration), insulin therapy (INS-therapy), glucagon-like peptide 1RA-therapy (GLP-1RA-therapy), sodium-dependent glucose transporter 2 therapy (SGLT2-therapy), DPP-IV dipeptidyl peptidase-IV inhibitor-therapy (DPP-IV inhibitor- therapy), smoking history, drinking history, height and weight were all recorded at the time of being hospitalized. Body mass index (BMI) was calculated using the formula: BMI (kg/m2) = weight(kg)/body height2 (m2).

After admission to the hospital, blood samples were collected from the individuals on the following morning after a 6–8 h fasting period. Fasting blood-glucose (FBG), fasting C-peptide (C-P), glycosylated hemoglobin (HbA1c), albumin (ALB), alanine transaminase (ALT), aspartic transaminase (AST), gamma-glutamyl transferase (GGT), total bilirubin (TBIL), creatinine (SCr), uric acid (SUA), triglyceride (TG), total cholesterol (TC), low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C) were all measured by well-trained technicians (Roche automatic biochemical analyzer. Combas 8000). Thyrotropic hormone (TSH), free triiodothyronine (FT3) and free thyroxine (FT4) were determined using a standardized Chemiluminescence method (Abbott 12000SR), all measurements were performed using the same assays.

The CGM parameters were recorded, including mean blood glucose (MG), estimated glycated hemoglobin (HBA), standard deviation of blood glucose (SD), coefficient of variation (CV), mean amplitude of glycemic excursion (MAGE), largest amplitude of glycemic excursion (LAGE), mean of daily differences (MODD), time in the target glucose range (TIR), time below the target glucose range (TBR), and time above the target glucose range (TAR).

The recommended targets: TIR (3.9-10.0mmol/l) was > 70% of the glucose readings, TBR1Scale < 3.9mmol/l was < 4% of the readings (level 1 hypoglycemia), TBR2Scale < 3.0mmol/l was < 1% of the readings (level 2 hypoglycemia), TAR1Scale > 10mmol/l was < 25% of the readings (level 1 hyperglycemia), TAR2Scale > 13.9mmol/l was < 5% of the readings (level 2 hyperglycemia). Glycemic variability (GV) was defined as percent CV with target ≤ 36%13.

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were conducted using IBM SPSS Statistics Software Version 27, GraphPad Prism 10.0.3, Empower(R) (www.empowerstats.com, X&Y Solutions, Inc.) (Boston, MA), and R (http://www.r-project.org). A significance level of P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. The normality of the data was assessed using the Shapiro-Wilk test and Q-Q plot. Normally distributed variables were presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD), while skewed variables were expressed as median (25th percentile, 75th percentile). Categorical variables were described in terms of frequency and percentage. One-Way ANOVA or non-parametric tests (Mann-Whitney U tests) were used to compare continuous variables among four groups divided by FT3/FT4 ratio using inter-quartile range. Smoothing function and threshold effect analysis were performed to determine the inflection point of FT3/FT4 ratio predicting glycemic variability. Log-likelihood ratio tests for single-line linear regression model and two-segment linear regression model were conducted. Multivariate logistic analysis was performed to assess the relationship between FT3/FT4 ratio and glycemic variability.

Results

The baseline clinical characteristics of all the individuals in this study

We enrolled 468 individuals with T2DM (292 men and 176 women) from a total of 1209 inpatients after excluding ineligible patients. Baseline clinical characteristics, laboratory examinations, and CGM parameters were all collected. The mean age of the individuals was 53.24 ± 12.57 years (51.28 ± 12.93 years for men and 56.49 ± 11.24 years for women) (Fig. 1; Table 1).

One-way ANOVA or non-parametric (Mann-Whitney U tests) analyses of FT3/FT4 ratio and CGM indexes

The FT3/FT4 ratio levels were categorized into four groups based on the inter-quartile range (FT3/FT4Q1 0.1602–0.2667, FT3/FT4Q2 0.2667–0.2943, FT3/FT4Q3 0.2943–0.3298, FT3/FT4Q4 0.3298–0.4992). The CGM parameters were utilized as the dependent variables, with FT3/FT4 serving as the independent variable. Comparisons revealed significant increases in HBA, MG, SD, CV, LAGE, MODD and TAR2Scale levels and a decrease in TIR levels at lower levels of FT3/FT4 ratio (FT3/FT4Q1) compared to higher levels of FT3/FT4 ratio (FT3/FT4Q2, FT3/FT4Q3 and FT3/FT4Q4) (all P < 0.05). Additionally, there was a notable increase in MAGE level at lower levels of the FT3/FT4 group (FT3/FT4Q1) compared to higher levels of the FT3/FT4 groups (FT3/FT4Q3 and FT3/FT4Q4) (all P < 0.05). No statistical significance was observed for TBR2Scale and TBR1Scale across all groups (all P > 0.05) (Table 2; Fig. 2).

Smoothing function and threshold effect analysis to determine the inflection point

In this study, smooth curve fitting and saturation effect analyses revealed curve-like relationships between the FT3/FT4 ratio and SD, MAGE, MODD, and TAR2Scale. The inflection points of the fitted curves were found to be at FT3/FT4 = 0.279, 0.237, 0.253, and 0.282 respectively (P < 0.05). This indicates that before the inflection points, an increase in FT3/FT4 by 0.1 corresponds to a decrease of 0.4232mmol/l in SD, 1.9157mmol/l in MAGE, 0.7502mmol/l in MODD, and 6.8368mmol/l in TAR2Scale (P < 0.05). However, after the inflection point there was no significant difference observed in the correlation between FT3/FT4 ratio and SD, MAGE, MODD and TAR2Scale (P > 0.05).

There were linear relationships observed between the FT3/FT4 ratio and HBA, MG, and TIR. Specifically, an increase of 0.1 in the FT3/FT4 ratio was associated with a decrease of 0.2155% in HBA, a decrease of 0.2579mmol/l in MG, and an increase of 5.5794% in TIR (P < 0.05) (Fig. 3; Table 3).

Univariable and multivariate predicting models of CGM parameters targets

The rule-in thresholds of seven ROCs were as follows: HBA (7%), CV (36%), TAR1Scale (25%), TAR2Scale (5%), TBR1Scale (4%), TBR2Scale (1%) and TIR (> 70%). The analysis for TBR2Scale was not possible due to packet unbalance (464(< 1%): 4(> 1%)). There are no recommended control targets for LAGE, MAGE, MODD, SD and MG. Therefore, we defined the rule-in threshold of these AUCs as below the median values of dichotomy: LAGE (7.96mmol/l), MAGE (4.94mmol/l), MODD (1.8mmol/l), SD (2.44mmol/l) and MG (7.75mmol/l).

Binary logistic regression analysis revealed that the AUCs of individual FT3/FT4*10 predicting for HBA, MG, SD, CV, TIR, TAR2Scale, TAR1Scale, MODD, LAGE and MAGE targets were 0.686, 0.683, 0.67, 0.655, 0.634, 0.705, 0.571, 0.64, 0.62 and 0.645 respectively, the cut-off points were 2.843, 2.84, 2.84, 2.726, 2.914, 2.843, 3.362, 2.867, 3.104 and 3.104pmol/l respectively. Discussion

The global prevalence of diabetes mellitus, especially T2DM, has been rapidly increasing for over 50 years and has now reached pandemic proportions17. Patients with diabetes are at increased risk of developing cardiovascular disease18, stroke19, aortic and peripheral artery diseases20, chronic kidney disease21, among other complications, all of which have significant adverse impacts on quality of life22. High glucose fluctuations in diabetic patients contribute to multi-organ complications1–5. Therefore, reducing blood glucose fluctuations may delay the development of chronic diabetic complications, mitigate the need for renal dialysis or kidney transplantation, prevent paralysis caused by cerebral infarction, and reduce the risk of sudden death from myocardial infarction. These improvements can significantly enhance individuals’ quality of life and also result in substantial savings in social healthcare costs.

TH plays a crucial role in several regulatory functions that are essential for the organism’s homeostasis. These functions include stimulating thermogenesis, energy expenditure, and lipid catabolism23. Additionally, TH serves as a key regulator in conveying environmental information to organisms, leading to morphoanatomical changes that can enhance the exploitation of environmental resources. Ultimately, these changes become integrated into the machinery of gene regulation and energy production24.

TH also plays a crucial role in regulating glucose metabolism and internal balance through direct or indirect means. TSH, which is secreted by TSH cells in the pituitary gland, can inhibit cyclic adenosine monophosphate (cAMP)-regulated transcriptional coactivators 2 (CRTC2) phosphorylation and upregulate CRTC2. This allows CRTC2 to enter the nucleus and bind to cAMP-response element binding protein (p-CREB), contributing to the increased expression of phosphoenolpyruvate carboxy-kinase (PEPCK) and glucose-6-phosphatase (G6P). These changes promote hepatic gluconeogenesis25. Elevated TSH levels can be harmful to pancreatic islets and may hinder the function of β cells, resulting in insulin resistance and elevated blood glucose levels26.

Peripheral thyroid hormone influences blood glucose through various pathways that involve insulin signal transduction and maintaining glucose homeostasis. In a physiological state, thyroid hormone hinders insulin activity while promoting hepatic gluconeogenesis and glycogenolysis. It can also upregulate gene expression of phosphoglycerate kinase and glucose transporter 4 (GLUT-4) to promote glycolysis. These mechanisms work synergistically to enhance glucose processing and utilization in peripheral tissues27.

FT3/FT4 ratio, which reflects peripheral sensitivity to thyroid hormones, had a greater association with metabolic syndrome. Reduced sensitivity to thyroid hormone is associated with hypertension28, diabetes29, dyslipidemia30, fatty liver31, hyperhomocysteinemia32 and hyperuricemia33. The FT3/FT4 ratio has been found to be negatively correlated with serum insulin levels in patients with T2DM34, but the potential influence of the FT3/FT4 ratio on glucose variability remains unknown. To date, there have been no studies investigating the relationship between the FT3/FT4 ratio and glucose variability.

Our study results demonstrate that, after adjusting for concomitant variables, the FT3/FT4 ratio is significantly negatively related to HBA, MG, SD, MAGE, TARS2Scale and MODD. In the smoothing function and threshold effect analysis, we found that before the inflection points, an increase in the FT3/FT4 ratio corresponded to decreases in SD, MAGE, MODD and TAR2Scale. However, there was no significant difference in the correlation between them after the inflection point. There are linear relationships between the FT3/FT4 ratio and HBA, MG and TIR; as the FT3/FT4 ratio increases, there are corresponding decreases in HBA and MG while TIR increases. This suggests that lower levels of FT3/FT4 ratio maybe associated with higher glucose levels and a lower ratio of glucose control with severe blood glucose fluctuation. Conversely, higher levels of FT3/FT4 ratio within a certain range indicate better glucose control with less variability. Multivariate logistic analyses show that the FT3/FT4 ratio is an important predictive factor for HBA, MG, TAR2Scale, LAGE, and MAGE targets.

In conclusion, our study has demonstrated that the increased FT3/FT4 ratio within a certain range is associated with decreased blood glucose variability and increased TIR. Future well-designed longitudinal studies are needed to confirm the role of thyroid hormone in glucose metabolism and diabetic complications. The results may be helpful for guiding diabetic management in clinical work.

There are several limitations to our study. Firstly, our study is of a cross-sectional design and lacks longitudinal comparative data, thus the evidence of the relationship between FT3/FT4 ratio and glycemic variability is not sufficient. Secondly, the individuals involved in our study were all inpatients, so it is uncertain if the findings can be generalized to all individuals with T2DM. Finally, we failed to include thyroid-related antibodies which may potentially affect thyroid hormones.

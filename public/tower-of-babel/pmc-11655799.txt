microRNA-875-5p-conjugated gold nanoparticles suppress breast cancer progression through the MTDH/PTEN/AKT signaling pathway
Authors: Bin Xiong, Junfeng Zhang, Yanmei Si, Jia Fu
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveA lack of effective delivery methods has hampered the study of therapeutics targeting miR-875-5p for breast cancer (BC).MethodsThe miR-875-5p mimic was conjugated to AuNPs to produce AuNP-miR-875-5p. Then, the effect of AuNP-miR-875-5p on the proliferative, migratory, invasive activities, and apoptosis of BC cells was detected, as well as on tumor growth in animals. The involvement of the MTDH/PTEN/AKT pathway in miR-875-5p-mediated BC progression was identified.ResultsAuNP-miR-875-5p was delivered to BC cells and hampered cell malignancy. MTDH was targeted by miR-875-5p. MTDH expression was negatively correlated with miR-875-5p expression in BC tissues. The anti-tumor effect of AuNP-miR-875-5p in BC cells was counteracted by MTDH overexpression. AuNP-miR-875-5p enhanced PTEN protein expression, thereby inhibiting AKT activation by targeting MTDH. AuNP-miR-875-5p blocked MCF-7 tumor growth in vivo.ConclusionAuNPs can deliver miR-875-5p to BC cells, and AuNP-miR-875-5p has clinical potential for treating unresectable BC.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s12672-024-01626-5.Keywords: MiR-875-5p, Gold nanoparticles, MTDH, PTEN/Akt pathway, Breast cancer

Introduction

Breast cancer (BC) is one of the most common malignant tumors in women all over the world, which seriously threatens women's health. According to the 2020 Global Cancer Statistics report, BC is the leading cause of cancer in the world. At the same time, BC ranks first in the incidence of female cancer in China and fourth in the death of female cancer [1]. According to statistics, the disability-adjusted life years (DALY) of Chinese women with BC in 2019 was 2,877,200 years, an increase of 102.12% over 1990. It is estimated that in 2050, the DALY caused by female BC in China will reach 3.80.0–5.162 million years [2]. Screening and early diagnosis of BC are essential to reduce morbidity and mortality [3, 4], but recurrence, distant metastasis, and drug resistance make it difficult to improve patient prognosis [5]. There is an urgent need to explore the molecular pathogenesis and thus to develop potential therapeutic targets for the prevention or treatment of BC.

MicroRNAs (miRNAs) pretranslationally mediate target gene stability [6] and posttranscriptionally bind to downstream target genes, degrading target mRNA or repressing target mRNA translation [7]. Abnormal expression levels of miRNAs are associated with cancer development [8]. Moreover, miRNA and mRNA interaction affects RNA stability, epigenetics, and translation [9, 10]. Increasing studies have determined the abnormal pattern of miRNAs in cancer tissues [11–15]. In light of this, miRNA-targeted therapies have been proposed to open new perspectives in the treatment of BC [16, 17]. Extensive studies have investigated the aberrant expression patterns of miR-875-5p in cancer [18–21]. However, the complex molecular mechanisms of miR-875-5p in BC have not been discussed. Many genes are negatively regulated by miR-875-5p, but it is not clear which target gene is the main pathway for miR-875-5p to exert its anti‐tumor effect. Transcriptome sequencing is a efficient strategy that can clarify which target genes miR-875-5p mainly uses to suppress tumors. Therefore, it is necessary to clarify the preferences of miR-875-5p in regulating target genes.

How to efficiently deliver miRNA is a tricky problem due to miRNA instability. Therefore, it is a prerequisite to develop miRNA vectors with safety and efficiency in tumor therapy. Nanoparticles have the properties of controlled release, improved stability of the drug, and targeted delivery of the drug to the tumor [22]. Gold nanoparticles (AuNPs) are particularly promising because of their biocompatibility, ease of surface functionalization, and suitability for carrying small and macromolecular drugs [23]. It has been suggested that multivalent oligonucleotide functionalized AuNPs are ideal for biomedical applications [24]. Ramesh et al. investigated gold nanoparticles to conjugate miR-206 in the targeted treatment of BC [25]. Recent discoveries have pinpointed a tumor suppressor gene, PTEN, responsible for regulating AKT pathway in cancers. Through PTEN/AKT pathway, it has been shown that miRNAs positively influence breast cancer development [26, 27]. miR-107 can trigger the PI3K/AKT pathway through PTEN, known to lessen myocardial harm from sepsis and influence the apoptosis in LPS-stimulated cardiomyocytes [28, 29]. Additional research has identified extended noncoding RNA within gastric cancer cells, which could be aimed at controlling the PTEN/PI3K/AKT pathway. Yet, it remains unclear if miR-875-5p influences this pathway and affects breast cancer.

This study prepared nanoparticles loaded with miR-875-5p (AuNP-miR-875-5p) to deliver miR-875-5p to tumor cells and observed the effectiveness of AuNP-miR-875-5p as an alternative treatment in BC.

Materials and methods

Cell lines

Cell lines (Manassas, VA, USA), including MCF-10A (human BC epithelial cell), MCF-7 and MDA-MB-231 (BC cell lines) were cultured in RMIP-1640 medium containing glutamine, 1% penicillin/streptomycin, and 10% FBS. All the media were provided by Thermo Fisher Scientific. Cell culture conditions were maintained at 37 °C with 5% CO2.

Preparation of AuNPs and AuNPs-miR-875-5p

The Frence method was employed to synthesize AuNPs. miR-875-5p mimic and miR-NC were synthesized by Ribobio (Guangzhou, China). To prepare AuNPs-miR-875-5p, thiolated miR-875-5p was mixed with 10 nM AuNP at RNA (1 nmol); AuNP (500 μL) was supplemented with 0.5% Tween-20. After 5 min, NaCl was gradually added until the concentration was 0.5 M [30]. Then, RNAs were centrifuged to collect functionalized AuNPs and added with PBS at 4 °C. miR-875-5p concentration in AuNP-miR-875-5p solution was determined after miRNA release from NP with dithiothreitol [31]. AuNP and AuNP-miR-875-5p were characterized by UV–Vis spectrophotometry, dynamic light scattering (DLS) and transmission electron microscope (TEM) according to the manufacturer's instructions.

Cell culture and treatment

BC cells were treated with AuNP-miR-875-5p (equal to 100 nM miR-875-5p, unless noted otherwise), or equal concentration of AuNP-miR-NC as a negative group, or media control, and then harvested after 48 h. Si-MTDH (GenePharma, Suzhou, China) was transfected into BC cells at 100 nM using Lipofectamine™ 2000 (Invitrogen, Carlsbad, CA). Si-MTDH transfection efficiency was about 70–80%, and gene intervention lasted for at least 48 h. BC cells were first transfected with AuNP-miR-875-5p (100 nM) for combination assay of MTDH cDNA plasmids without 3′-UTR (Addgene,www.addgene.org) and AuNP-miR-875-5p. After 48 h, cells were co-transfected with MTDH cDNA plasmid (2 μg) and AuNP-miR-875-5p (100 nM) for 72 h.

Luciferase activity assay

After amplification, MTDH 3′-UTR containing miR-875-5p binding site was inserted into the pGL3 vector (Life technologies). The complementary site of miR-875-5p with AGGUAU sequence in MTDH 3′-UTR was mutated. BC cells were cotransfected with MTDH 3′-UTR constructs (100 ng) and 100 nM miRNA, and tested on a dual luciferase reporter analyzer (Promega Corporation, Wisconsin, USA).

RadioImmunoPrecipitation assay RIP assay

RadioImmunoPrecipitation (RIP) was detected according to the Magna RIP Kit (Millipore Corp, Bedford, MA). The cell lysate was collected with RIP lysis buffer and combined with magnetic beads bound to Ago2 or IgG (6 h at 4 °C). After elution, the RNA products were purified and tested the enrichment level of miR-875-5p by RT-qPCR.

RT-qPCR

Total RNA was extracted with TRIzol reagent (Tiangen, Beijing, China) to generare cDNA using the Superscript II-Reverse Transcriptase Kit (Invitrogen, Carlsbad, CA), and PCR was done with Premix Ex Taq Kit (Takara) in the 7300 real-time PCR system (Life Technologies). The thermal cycling reaction was set with 95 ℃ for 30 s, followed by 40 cycles of 95 ℃ for 5 s, 65 ℃ for 31 s, 95 ℃ for 15 s, 65 ℃ for 1 min, and 95 ℃ for 15 s. PCR primers (Table 1) were provided by Invitrogen.

Cell uptake test

BC cells were grown in a 96-well plate at 8 × 103 cells/well for 24 h and added with AuNP-miR-875-5p (50 nM miR-875-5p) for 1, 3, or 6 h. At each time point, cells after 4% PFA treatment for 15 min were dyed with 100 mM DAPI for 3 min, observed under an Olympus SZX12 fluorescence microscope, and analyzed by PC running MagnaFire 2.0 camera software (Optronics, CA, USA).

Flow cytometry analysis of cell apoptosis

The apoptosis rate was assessed by Fluorescein Isothiocyanate (FITC) Annexin V Apoptosis Detection Kit (BD Biosciences, USA), Cells in 12-well plates at 1 × 105 cells/well were incubated with AuNP-miR-875-5p (50 nM miR-875-5p). BC cells were washed twice with pre-cooled PBS, re-suspended in 500 μl 1 × binding buffer, and stained with 5 μl Annexin V-FITC and 5 μl PI solutions, respectively. After incubation at room temperature for 15 min, the proportion of apoptotic cells was measured on FACScan flow cytometer (BD Biosciences, USA).

Wound-healing assay

BC cells were plated in 24-well plates at 5 × 105 cells/well overnight. Vertical wounds were made with a 200 μL pipette tip, and a fresh medium containing PBS, AuNP-miR-875-5p (80 nM miR-875-5p) or Au-miR-NC was added, respectively. Images were taken at 0, 24 and 48 h using a Nikon microscope.

Cell migration and invasion assays

BC cells (5 × 104) were plated in the upper chamber pre-filled with serum-free medium supplemented with PBS, AuNPmiR-875-5p (80 nM miR-875-5p), or Au-miR-NC. The lower chamber was added with a medium containing 10% FBS and the same concentration of drug. After 24 h, migrating cells were fixed with 4% PFA for 15 min and stained with 1% crystal violet solution. Cells were counted under a Nikon microscope. The invasive activity was assayed with the transwell chamber precoated with diluted Matrigel.

Colony formation assay

BC cells (1 × 103 cells/well) treated with AuNP-miR-875-5p (80 nM miR-875-5p) or Au-miR-NC were inoculated into a six-well plate and cultured in DMEM containing 10% FBS. Two weeks later, the colonies were washed twice with PBS, fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde for 20 min, and stained with 0.1% crystal violet for 30 min. Finally, the number of colonies was calculated.

Cell viability and proliferation assay

Cell Counting Kit-8 (CCK8, Dojindo, Japan) was used to detect cell viability according to manufacturer's protocol. BC cell suspension (100 μl) was inoculated into a 96-well plate at 3 × 103 cells per well. At each time point, 10 μl CCK-8 solution (Dojindo) was added to each well and incubated at 37℃ for 2 h. The absorbance at 450 nm was then measured on a microplate reader (Bio-Rad, CA, USA).

Determination of Caspase-3 and caspase-9 activity

Cell proteins were extracted using RIPA buffer (Beyotime) and quantified using the BCA Protein Assay Kit (EMD Millipore). The protein (50 µg) was incubated with caspase-9 (C1158) and caspase-3 (C1116) (Beyotime) for 2 h at 37 °C, and the absorbance was measured at 405 nm using an Orion II microplate photometer (Berthold Technologies GmbH & Co. KG).

Western Blot

Cells were dissolved in a radioimmunoprecipitation assay buffer with protease inhibitors and quantified by Bradford assay (Bio-Rad). Proteins were separated by 10% SDS-PAGE and transferred to PVDF membranes (Millipore). The samples were blocked with 5% skim milk powder for 1 h and incubated with primary antibodies MTDH (Abcam; 1:10,000); GAPDH (CST; 1:1000), p-AKT (CST; 1:1000), Bax (CST, 1:1000), and PTEN (CST; 1:1000) overnight at 4 °C. After three washes with TBST, the secondary antibody conjugated with horseradish peroxidase (CST) was incubated for 1 h and developed using an ECL kit (ultrassignal, China).

Experiments on animals

BALB/c nude mice (males, 5–6 weeks old, 18–20 g) were purchased from HFK Company (Beijing, China) and propagated under pathogen-free conditions. All animal experiments were conducted complying with the Guide for the Care and Use of Laboratory Animals of Jining Medical University. For xenograft tumor models, 5 × 106 MCF-7 cells were inoculated subcutaneously in 5 nude mice, respectively. At days 7, 10, 14, and 17 after MCF-7 cell inoculation, tumor size reached 100 mm3, and AuNPs were injected subcutaneously at 4 nmol/kg miRNA, a total of 4 times. Tumor size (length and width) and mouse body weight were measured. Tumor volume = (length × width2) × 0.5. Finally, tumors were harvested from euthanized mice and photographed.

Immunohistochemical staining

Tumor paraffin tissue (4 μm) was heated at 65 °C for 2 h, dewaxed, and microwaved in EDTA buffer. Sections were placed in 3% H2O2 for 10 min and blocked with 5% BSA for 20 min. The diluted primary antibody (50 μl) was added overnight and 50–100 μl secondary antibody was incubated for 50 min. Then, DAB-developed sections were counterstained with hematoxylin solution, differentiated with 1% hydrochloric acid alcohol, soaked in ammonia, dehydrated with gradient alcohol, cleared with xylene, and sealed with neutral glue [32].

Data analysis

Data were expressed as mean ± standard deviation (SD) and collected from experiments with at least three replicates. Data comparison was carried out using Student's t-test or one-way ANOVA. p < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

miR-875-5p is lowly expressed in BC cells

RT-qPCR results presented the downregulated trend of miR-875-5p in BC cells (Fig. 1A). miR-875-5p expression in BC tissues and adjacent normal tissues was predicted based on ENCORI-Pan-Cancer website, which was consistent with our conclusions in cell experiments (Supplementary material 1).

Characterization of AuNPs and AuNP-miR-875-5p

AuNPs were spherical, with an average diameter of about 13 nm under TEM (Fig. 2A) and an average hydrodynamic diameter of 36 ± 3 nm measured by DLS (Table 2). The absorption wavelength of AuNPs was concentrated at 521 nm (Fig. 2C), and the stability of AuNPs was better in ultra-pure water at 4℃, and the hydrodynamic diameter did not change much for more than two months (Fig. 2B). After conjugating miR-875-5p, the morphology of AuNP-miR-875-5p did not change significantly (Fig. 2A), but the particle size increased, with an average DLS hydrodynamic diameter of 53 ± 8 nm (Table 2), and the maximum absorption wavelength at 529 nm (Fig. 2C). The Zeta potential was −55 ± 0.7 mv for AuNPs and −34 ± 1.8 mv for AuNP-miR-875-5p. The polydispersity of AuNP-miR-875-5p was 0.197 ± 0.034, while that of AuNP-miR-875-5p was 0.321 ± 0.191 (Table 2).

AuNP-miR-875-5p uptake by BC cells

To study whether AuNPs can deliver miRNA into liver cancer cells efficiently, we determined the cellular uptake of Cy3-labeled AuNP-miR-875-5p by fluorescence microscopy and by flow cytometry analysis. After 1 h incubation, Red fluorescence was observed in BC cells after AuNP-miR-875-5p treatment (Fig. 3A). Flow cytometry quantification found that more than 95% of BC cells were CY3-positive at 1 h (Fig. 3B). The fluorescence was enhanced at 3 h and only slightly enhanced at 3–6 h, suggesting saturated uptake of AuNP-miR-875-5p by BC cells (Fig. 3C). TEM observed the localization of AuNP-miR-875-5p in endosomes or lysosomes (Fig. 3D). In addition, TaqMan RT-qPCR found that miR-875-5p levels were elevated nearly 900-fold after AuNP-miR-875-5p treatment (Fig. 3E). Shortly, AuNP-miR-875-5p was effectively delivered to BC cells and mature miR-875-5p was released.

An inverse relationship between miR-875-5p and MTDH in BC

In order to uncover the deep interplay mechanism by which miR-875-5p worked, we performed bioinformatics analysis among TargetScan, PicTar, and miRanda were utilized to analyze miR-875-5p target genes. miRNA:mRNA alignment analysis revealed that MTDH 3′-UTR contained one binding sites for miR-875-5p. TNMplot.com (https://tnmplot.com/analysis/) found that MTDH expression was elevated in BC. MTDH protein expression presented an increase in BC cells (Fig. 4A).we searched previous researches to locate MTDH as our final target of miR-875-5p which was associated with BC poor prognosis and had been reported. To further validate the binding relation between miR-875-5p and MTDH,RIP assay found that MTDH and miR-875-5p were enriched in Ago2 magnetic beads (Fig. 4B). Moreover, immunoblotting for MTDH in BC cells suggested that AuNP-miR-875-5p elevated MTDH levels (Fig. 4C). The luciferase reporters (WT-MTDH, MUT-MTDH) were constructed based on the predicted binding site of miR-875-5p to the 3′-UTR MTDH (Fig. 4D). AuNP-miR-875-5p reduced the luciferase activity of WT-MTDH (Fig. 4E). In addition, AuNP-miR-875-5p reduced MTDH expression (Fig. 4F).

AuNP-miR-875-5p impedes BC cells growth

RT-qPCR results presented that miR-875-5p was significantly upregulated in BC cells (Fig. 5A). MCF-7 and MDA-MB-231 cells were selected for further investigations. CCK-8 assay found that BC cell proliferation was suppressed by AuNP-miR-875-5p in a concentration-dependent manner (Fig. 5B). Scratch assay showed that the wound closure rate of untreated cells was approximately 55%, that of cells treated with Au-NP-miR-NC was 28%, and that of cells treated with Au-NP-miR-875-5p was approximately 10% (Fig. 5C). Moreover, migration was reduced by 15% and 40%, respectively, in response to Au-NP-miR-NC treatment and AuNP-miR-875-5p treatment (Fig. 5D). The decreasing trend was seen in cell invasion (Fig. 5E) and clonal formation ability (Fig. 5F) after AuNP-miR-875-5p treatment.

AuNP-miR-875-5p blocks MTDH-mediated PTEN/AKT pathway

MTDH suppressed PTEN transcription and thereafter reduced PTEN expression (Fig. 6A). we performed western blot analysis. The results showed that AuNP-miR-875-5p treatment enhanced PTEN expression, and this effect was mitigated by upregulating MTDH. It was also observed that AuNP-miR-875-5p induced PTEN and suppressed p-Akt expression, and this phenomenon was impaired when MTDH (without 3′-UTR) was overexpressed (Fig. 6B). si-MTDH enhanced the effect of AuNP-miR-875-5p on cellular proliferation and apoptosis (Fig. 6C), Caspase-3/9 activity (Fig. 6D), and protein expression of Bax, PTEN, and p-AKT (Fig. 6E).

Discussion

With the in-depth study of miRNAs, among the miRNAs discovered so far, more than 50% of the genes encoding miRNAs are located in the chromosomal fragile sites of proto-oncogenes or oncogenes [33]. The analysis of miRNA expression profiles in tumor tissues also shows that miRNAs are often abnormally expressed in tumors [34]. A large number of studies have shown that these abnormally expressed miRNAs are involved in tumorigenesis as well as in the process of tumor regulation. Through external intervention methods such as stable transfection of lentiviral systems [35], transient transfection of miRNA mimics [36], and transient transfection of miRNA repressors [37], the expression levels of these aberrantly expressed miRNAs are increased or decreased, and significant inhibition of these malignancies is achieved. In this study, we confirmed that miR-875-5p was significantly downregulated and exerted oncogenic effects in BC and reported the role of miR-875-5p in BC for the first time. Due to its ability to inhibit tumor cell growth in vitro and in vivo, miR-875-5p is a promising candidate for miRNA alternative therapy. All these results suggest that miR-875-5p is involved in metastasis and malignant progression of BC and may serve as a biomarker for BC diagnosis and prognosis.

The vectors commonly used to transport small RNA molecules can be categorized into viral and non-viral vectors. Although they can efficiently transfer miRNAs into cells and form stable cell lines integrated into the host genome, they may cause host cells to generate an immune response, which poses a certain risk of pathogenicity. Nanomaterials have a natural advantage in drug delivery due to the surface effect, volume effect, and quantum size effect resulting from their size to the nanometer level. In this study, we used AuNP-miRNA delivery system to deliver miR-875-5p into BC cells and tissues. Our study showed that miR-875-5p could be delivered to BC cells and tissues by AuNP-miR-875-5p. AuNP-miR-875-5p was efficiently taken up in BC cells, and experiments on cell proliferation, migration/invasion, colony formation and apoptosis confirmed that miR-875-5p delivered by Au-NPs in vitro could inhibit BC proliferation, migration, and invasion. Importantly, our results from a xenograft tumor mouse model also showed that AuNP-miR-875-5p could overcome physiological barriers and deliver miR-875-5p to BC tissues in mice. Highly distributed AuNP-miR-875-5p in BC tissues showed higher anti-tumor activity compared to AuNP-miR-NC. These results reveal the reliability of AuNP to deliver miR-875-5p to BC cells and the therapeutic role of AuNP-miR-875-5p in BC treatment. Our study also highlights the therapeutic potential of miR-875-5p in BC treatment and supports the development of more effective therapeutic strategies for targeting miR-875-5p through nanotechnology.

In this study, we found that AuNP-miR-875-5p inhibited MTDH and p-AKT and up-regulated PTEN expression in BC, which is consistent with previous studies [37, 38]. miR-875-5p could target MTDH, and AuNP-miR-875-5p degraded MTDH. MTDH is a single-channel amino acid trans membrane protein located on chromosome 8q22.5 [441]. The upregulation of MTDH in cancer has been discussed, and its expression trend has been shown to correlate with poor clinical outcomes [39]. This suggests that AuNP-miR-875-5p regulates the MTDH/PTEN/AKT pathway to inhibit BC cell growth.

The main function of AKT is the direct inhibition of apoptosis [40]. The Bcl-2 family is also closely related to apoptosis, and many of its members, including the Bcl-2-associated death promoter (Bad), exert pro-apoptotic effects. AKT can phosphorylate Ser136/Ser112 residues of Bad proteins, which leads to the upregulation of anti-apoptotic factors, including Bcl-2 and survivin, and loss of pro-apoptotic effects [41]. In this study, we confirmed that AuNP-miR-875-5p induced apoptosis, promoted caspase-3/9 activity, and increased Bax protein expression. AKT activation phosphorylated Ser196 of cystatinase-9, thereby inhibiting apoptosis. In addition, cells are resistant to penicillin-induced apoptosis when the AKT kinase structural domain is activated [42]. In this study, silencing MTDH enhanced AuNP-miR-875-5p-induced apoptosis in BC cells. MTDH is an important target for the action of AuNP-miR-875-5p in BC.

MTDH activates many signaling pathways, regulates physiological and pathological cellular processes, and mediates tumor cell proliferation, invasion, metastasis, angiogenesis, and chemoresistance [43]. MTDH is a downstream target gene of AKT and an upstream activator of the PI3K/AKT pathway, and PI3K/AKT and c-Myc can induce the expression of MTDH, which can further activate PI3K/AKT and up-regulate the expression of c-Myc, resulting in the expression of N-Myc in the adult neuronal neoplastic cell lines, further enhancing tumorigenicity and forming a vicious cycle in the process of tumor formation [41]. In this study, we found that MTDH silencing elevated PTEN and p-AKT proteins in BC cells, and AuNP-miR-875-5p could mediate MTDH targeting of the PTEN/AKT pathway in BC.

Briefly, the current study confirms the reliability of AuNPs in delivering miR-875-5p to BC cells and the therapeutic efficacy of AuNP-miR-875-5p in the disease. Furthermore, miR-875-5p-coupled gold nanoparticles inhibit BC progression through the MTDH/PTEN/AKT signaling pathway, highlighting it as a novel therapeutic target. However, further studies, including preclinical and clinical trials, are needed to evaluate its future translational potential.

Conclusion

This study proposes that miR-875-5p-conjugated gold nanoparticles inhibit BC cell growth through the MTDH/PTEN/AKT signaling pathway.

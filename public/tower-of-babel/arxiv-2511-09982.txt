The Reidemeister spectrum of ZM-groups
Marius Tărnăuceanu

Abstract
Given a group G G and an automorphism φ \varphi of G G , two elements x , y ∈ G x,y\in G are said to be φ \varphi -conjugate if x = g ​ y ​ φ ​ ( g ) − 1 x=gy\varphi(g)^{-1} for some g ∈ G g\in G . The number R ⁡ ( φ ) R(\varphi) of equivalence classes with respect to this relation is called the Reidemeister number of φ \varphi and the set { R ⁡ ( φ ) | φ ∈ Aut ⁡ ( G ) } \{R(\varphi)|\varphi\in{\rm Aut}(G)\} is called the Reidemeister spectrum of G G . In this paper, we determine the Reidemeister spectrum of ZM-groups, extending some results of [ 13 ] .

1
Introduction

Let
G
be a group and
{\rm Aut}(G)
be the group of automorphisms of
G
. The concept of conjugacy in
G
can be naturally generalized to the so-called

twisted conjugacy
: given
\varphi\in{\rm Aut}(G)
, two elements
x,y\in G
are called
\varphi
-conjugate
if there exists
g\in G
such that
x=gy\varphi(g)^{-1}
. We define the
Reidemeister number

R(\varphi)
of
\varphi
as the number of equivalence classes with respect to this relation. Note that
R(\varphi)\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\cup\{\infty\}
. Also, we define the
Reidemeister spectrum
of
G
to be
{\rm Spec}_{R}(G)=\{R(\varphi)|\varphi\in{\rm Aut}(G)\}
. If
{\rm Spec}_{R}(G)=\{\infty\}
, we say that
G
has the
R_{\infty}
-property.

Twisted conjugacy arises naturally in Nielsen fixed-point theory (see e.g.
[
12
]
), but there is also a strong algebraic interest in its study, in particular in the study of
R_{\infty}
-property (see e.g.
[
5
,
8
,
9
,
15
]
). For many groups either partial or full information is known about their Reidemeister spectrum. It has either been proven that they have the
R_{\infty}
-property (such as Baumslag-Solitar groups
[
7
]
or Thompson’s group
[
1
]
), or that they do not (such as certain groups of exponential growth
[
10
]
or free groups of infinite rank
[
3
]
), and for some even the complete Reidemeister spectrum has been determined (such as low-dimensional crystallographic groups
[
4
]
or finite abelian groups
[
14
]
).

The starting point for our discussion is given by the paper
[
13
]
, where the Reidemeister spectrum has been determined for split metacyclic groups of the form
(C_{n}\times C_{p^{m}})\rtimes C_{p}
where
p
is prime,
m
and
n
are non-negative integers and
n
is coprime with
p
. In what follows we will deal with ZM-groups, i.e. finite groups with all Sylow subgroups cyclic. By
[
11
]
(Ch. IV, Satz 2.11.), such a group is of type

{\rm ZM}(m,n,r)=\langle a,b\mid a^{m}=b^{n}=1,\hskip 2.84526ptb^{-1}ab=a^{r}\rangle,

where the triple
(m,n,r)
satisfies the conditions

(m,n)=(m,r-1)=1\,\,\mbox{ and }r^{n}\equiv 1\hskip 2.84526pt({\rm mod}\hskip 2.84526ptm).

It is clear that
|{\rm ZM}(m,n,r)|=mn
and
Z({\rm ZM}(m,n,r))=\langle b^{o_{m}(r)}\rangle
, where

o_{m}(r)={\rm min}\{k\in\mathbb{N}^{*}\mid r^{k}\equiv 1\hskip 2.84526pt({\rm mod}\hskip 2.84526ptm)\}\,\,

is the multiplicative order of
r
modulo
m
. For every non-negative integer
u
, we define

[u]_{r}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\!\!1+r+\cdots+r^{u-1},&u>0\\
\!\!0,&u=0.\end{array}\right.

Our main result is stated as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

We have

{\rm Spec}_{R}({\rm ZM}(m,n,r))=\left\{\!\displaystyle\frac{1}{m}\displaystyle\sum_{v=0}^{m-1}\sum_{{}^{\,0\leq u\leq n-1}_{\frac{n}{(n,y-1)}\mid u}}\frac{(m,[u]_{r})}{o_{\frac{(m,[u]_{r})}{(m,[u]_{r},v)}}(r)}\,\mid\,0\leq y<n,y\equiv 1\,({\rm mod}\,d)\!\right\}\!.

Example 1.2
.

For the dicyclic group
{\rm Dic}_{3}={\rm ZM}(3,4,2)={\rm SmallGroup}(12,1)
, we have
d=2
and the above formula leads to

{\rm Spec}_{R}({\rm Dic}_{3})=\{4,6\}.

A particular case of Theorem 1.1 is the following.

Corollary 1.3
.

If
n
is prime, then we have

{\rm Spec}_{R}({\rm ZM}(m,n,r))=\left\{n-1+\displaystyle\frac{S}{n}\right\}\!,

where

S=\displaystyle\sum_{u=0}^{n-1}\,(m,[u]_{r}).

Example 1.4
.

For
n=2
and
r=m-1
we have
{\rm ZM}(m,n,r)\cong D_{2m}
, the dihedral group of order
2m
. Then
S=m+1
and so the formula in Corollary 1.3 becomes

{\rm Spec}_{R}(D_{2m})=\left\{\displaystyle\frac{m+3}{2}\right\},\mbox{ for all odd integers }m\geq 3.

We remark that a split metacyclic group
(C_{n}\times C_{p^{m}})\rtimes C_{p}
becomes a ZM-group for
m=0
and so Theorem 1.1 extends the results obtained in
[
13
]
in this particular case.

Our approach uses some basic tools of group action theory, such as the Burnside’s lemma, and involves modular arithmetic.

Burnside’s lemma.

Let
G
be a finite group acting on a finite set
X
and

Fix(g)=\{x\in X\mid g\circ x=x\},\mbox{ for all }g\in G.

Then the number of distinct orbits is

k=\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{g\in G}|Fix(g)|.

Most of our notation is standard and will not be repeated here. Basic definitions and results on group theory can be found in
[
11
]
.

2
Proofs of the main results

The general form of automorphisms of a metacyclic group has been determined in the main result of
[
2
]
. For ZM-groups, in
[
6
]
we obtained that:

Theorem 2.1
.

Each automorphism of
{\rm ZM}(m,n,r)
is given by

b^{u}a^{v}\mapsto b^{yu}a^{x_{1}v+x_{2}[u]_{r}},\,u,v\geq 0,

for a unique triple of integers
(x_{1},x_{2},y)
such that

0\leq x_{1},x_{2}<m,\,(x_{1},m)=1,\,0\leq y<n\mbox{ and }y\equiv 1\hskip 2.84526pt({\rm mod}\hskip 2.84526ptd).

(1)

In particular, we have

|{\rm Aut}({\rm ZM}(m,n,r))|=m\varphi(m)\frac{n}{d}\,.

In what follows we will denote by
f_{(x_{1},x_{2},y)}
the automorphism of
{\rm ZM}(m,n,r)
determined by a triple
(x_{1},x_{2},y)
satisfying (1).

We are now able to prove our results.

Proof of Theorem 1.1.

Let
f=f_{(x_{1},x_{2},y)}\in{\rm Aut}({\rm ZM}(m,n,r))
. In order to compute
R(f)
, we will apply Burnside’s lemma to the action of
{\rm ZM}(m,n,r)
on
{\rm ZM}(m,n,r)
given by

g\circ x=gxf(g)^{-1},\forall\,g,x\in{\rm ZM}(m,n,r).

Let
g=b^{u}a^{v}
and
x=b^{\alpha}a^{\beta}
, where
0\leq u,\alpha<n
and
0\leq v,\beta<m
. Using Theorem 2.1, we get

\displaystyle Fix(g)

\displaystyle=\{x\in{\rm ZM}(m,n,r)\mid f(g)=x^{-1}gx\}

\displaystyle=\{b^{\alpha}a^{\beta}\in{\rm ZM}(m,n,r)\mid b^{yu}a^{x_{1}v+x_{2}[u]_{r}}=b^{u}a^{r^{\alpha}v-\beta(r^{u}-1)}\}

\displaystyle=\{b^{\alpha}a^{\beta}\in{\rm ZM}(m,n,r)\mid b^{yu}=b^{u}\mbox{ and }a^{x_{1}v+x_{2}[u]_{r}}=a^{r^{\alpha}v-\beta(r^{u}-1)}\}.

We have

b^{yu}=b^{u}\Leftrightarrow n\mid(y-1)u\Leftrightarrow\frac{n}{(n,y-1)}\mid u

and

a^{x_{1}v+x_{2}[u]_{r}}=a^{r^{\alpha}v-\beta(r^{u}-1)}\Leftrightarrow m\mid(r^{\alpha}-x_{1})v-\beta(r^{u}-1)-x_{2}[u]_{r}.

For fixed
u,v
and
\alpha
, the last congruence has solutions
\beta
if and only if

(m,[u]_{r})\mid(r^{\alpha}-x_{1})v.

(2)

Moreover, if it has solutions, then there are exactly
(m,[u]_{r})
solutions. We also remark that (2) is equivalent with

\frac{(m,[u]_{r})}{(m,[u]_{r},v)}\mid r^{\alpha}-x_{1}

(3)

and (3) has the same number of solutions
\alpha
as

\frac{(m,[u]_{r})}{(m,[u]_{r},v)}\mid r^{\alpha}-1,

that is as

o_{\frac{(m,[u]_{r})}{(m,[u]_{r},v)}}(r)\mid\alpha.

(4)

Obviously, (4) has
\frac{n}{o_{\frac{(m,[u]_{r})}{(m,[u]_{r},v)}}(r)}
solutions and so we get

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636ptR(f)

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\frac{1}{mn}\displaystyle\sum_{g\in{\rm ZM}(m,n,r)}|Fix(g)|

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\frac{1}{mn}\displaystyle\sum_{(u,v)}|\{(\alpha,\beta)\mid b^{yu}=b^{u}\mbox{ and }a^{x_{1}v+x_{2}[u]_{r}}=a^{r^{\alpha}v-\beta(r^{u}-1)}\}|

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\frac{1}{mn}\sum_{v=0}^{m-1}\sum_{{}^{\,0\leq u\leq n-1}_{\frac{n}{(n,y-1)}\mid u}}\sum_{\alpha=0}^{n-1}|\{\beta=\overline{0,m-1}\,\mid\,m\!\mid\!(r^{\alpha}-x_{1})v-\beta(r^{u}-1)-x_{2}[u]_{r}\}|

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\frac{1}{mn}\sum_{v=0}^{m-1}\sum_{{}^{\,0\leq u\leq n-1}_{\frac{n}{(n,y-1)}\mid u}}\!\sum_{{}^{\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,0\leq\alpha\leq n-1}_{\,\,\,(m,[u]_{r})\mid(r^{\alpha}-x_{1})v}}\!\!\!(m,[u]_{r})

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\frac{1}{mn}\sum_{v=0}^{m-1}\sum_{{}^{\,0\leq u\leq n-1}_{\frac{n}{(n,y-1)}\mid u}}(m,[u]_{r})|\{\alpha=\overline{0,n-1}\,\mid\,(m,[u]_{r})\!\mid\!(r^{\alpha}-x_{1})v\}|

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\frac{1}{mn}\sum_{v=0}^{m-1}\sum_{{}^{\,0\leq u\leq n-1}_{\frac{n}{(n,y-1)}\mid u}}(m,[u]_{r})\,\frac{n}{o_{\frac{(m,[u]_{r})}{(m,[u]_{r},v)}}(r)}

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\frac{1}{m}\displaystyle\sum_{v=0}^{m-1}\sum_{{}^{\,0\leq u\leq n-1}_{\frac{n}{(n,y-1)}\mid u}}\frac{(m,[u]_{r})}{o_{\frac{(m,[u]_{r})}{(m,[u]_{r},v)}}(r)}\,,

as desired.
∎

Proof of Corollary 1.2.

If
n
is prime, then we have
d=n
and so the conditions
0\leq y<n
and
y\equiv 1\,({\rm mod}\,d)
imply
y=1
for any
f=f_{(x_{1},x_{2},y)}\in{\rm Aut}({\rm ZM}(m,n,r))
. Thus, we get

R(f)=\displaystyle\frac{1}{m}\displaystyle\sum_{v=0}^{m-1}\sum_{u=0}^{n-1}\frac{(m,[u]_{r})}{o_{\frac{(m,[u]_{r})}{(m,[u]_{r},v)}}(r)}\,.

Since
o_{\frac{(m,[u]_{r})}{(m,[u]_{r},v)}}(r)
is a divisor of
n
, we have either
o_{\frac{(m,[u]_{r})}{(m,[u]_{r},v)}}(r)=1
or
o_{\frac{(m,[u]_{r})}{(m,[u]_{r},v)}}(r)=n
. We remark that

o_{\frac{(m,[u]_{r})}{(m,[u]_{r},v)}}(r)=1\Leftrightarrow\frac{(m,[u]_{r})}{(m,[u]_{r},v)}=1\Leftrightarrow(m,[u]_{r})\mid v.

It follows that

\displaystyle R(f)

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\frac{1}{m}\displaystyle\sum_{v=0}^{m-1}\sum_{u=0}^{n-1}\frac{(m,[u]_{r})}{o_{\frac{(m,[u]_{r})}{(m,[u]_{r},v)}}(r)}

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\frac{1}{m}\displaystyle\sum_{u=0}^{n-1}(m,[u]_{r})\left(\sum_{{}^{\,\,0\leq v\leq m-1}_{\,(m,[u]_{r})\,\mid\,v}}1+\sum_{{}^{\,\,0\leq v\leq m-1}_{\,(m,[u]_{r})\,\nmid\,v}}\frac{1}{n}\right)

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\frac{1}{m}\displaystyle\sum_{u=0}^{n-1}(m,[u]_{r})\left[\frac{m}{(m,[u]_{r})}+\frac{1}{n}\left(m-\frac{m}{(m,[u]_{r})}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\sum_{u=0}^{n-1}\left(1-\frac{1}{n}\right)+\frac{1}{n}\sum_{u=0}^{n-1}\,(m,[u]_{r})

\displaystyle=n-1+\displaystyle\frac{S}{n}\,,

completing the proof.
∎

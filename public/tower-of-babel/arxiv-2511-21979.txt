An analogue of Kida’s formula for Mazur–Tate elements
Naman Pratap, Anwesh Ray

Abstract
. We prove an analogue of Kida’s formula for the Iwasawa invariants of the Mazur–Tate elements attached to elliptic curves over ℚ \mathbb{Q} . Let p p be an odd prime and let L / K L/K be a Galois extension of abelian number fields with p p -power Galois group. For an elliptic curve E / ℚ E/\mathbb{Q} , we study the Mazur–Tate elements over the finite layers of the cyclotomic ℤ p \mathbb{Z}_{p} -extensions of K K and L L . We show that the vanishing of the μ \mu -invariant is preserved in the extension: if the level- n n Mazur–Tate element over K K has μ = 0 \mu=0 , then the corresponding element over L L also has μ = 0 \mu=0 . Moreover, the associated λ \lambda -invariants satisfy an explicit transition formula. This parallels the work of Hachimori–Matsuno on Selmer groups and of Matsuno on p p -adic L L -functions. As an application, we obtain an analogue of Kida’s formula for the analytic Iwasawa invariants associated to Pollack’s signed p p -adic L L -functions. Since our results apply to elliptic curves with any reduction type at p p under mild hypotheses, including those with additive reduction, we also obtain a Kida-type formula for the p p -adic L L -functions constructed by Delbourgo for elliptic curves with unstable additive reduction. In particular, because Mazur–Tate elements approximate p p -adic L L -functions in the limit, our results simultaneously unify all previously known cases of Kida’s formula for analytic Iwasawa invariants.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
An arithmetic analogue of the Riemann–Hurwitz formula

The classical Riemann–Hurwitz formula gives a precise relation between the genera of two compact Riemann surfaces when one covers the other. Concretely, if
X
and
Y
are compact Riemann surfaces and
\varphi:Y\to X
is a holomorphic covering map, then their genera satisfy

2g_{Y}-2\;=\;\deg(\varphi)\,(2g_{X}-2)\;+\;\sum_{P\in Y}(e(P)-1),

where
g_{X}
and
g_{Y}
denote the genera of
X
and
Y
, and the sum runs over all points
P\in Y
, with
e(P)
the ramification index of
\varphi
at
P
. Thus the failure of the naive degree scaling
2g_{Y}-2=\deg(\varphi)(2g_{X}-2)
is measured exactly by the total ramification of the covering.

A remarkable analogue of this relation exists in arithmetic. In place of coverings of Riemann surfaces one considers extensions of number fields, and the role of genus is played by certain invariants arising naturally in classical Iwasawa theory. Let
K
be a number field and let
K_{\infty}/K
denote its cyclotomic
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension. For each integer
n\geq 1
, write
K_{(n)}
for the unique intermediate extension of
K_{\infty}/K
of degree
p^{n}
over
K
.

In
[
11
]
, Kida proved an analogue of the Riemann–Hurwitz genus formula. Assume that
K
is a CM field and let
K_{(n)}^{+}
be the maximal totally real subfield of
K_{(n)}
and set

h_{n}:=h(K_{(n)}),\quad h_{n}^{+}:=h(K_{(n)}^{+})\quad\text{and}\quad h_{n}^{-}:=h_{n}/h_{n}^{+}.

Iwasawa showed (see
[
8
]
and
[
9
]
) that there exist nonnegative integers
\mu_{K}^{-},\lambda_{K}^{-}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
and
\nu_{K}^{-}\in\mathbb{Z}
such that for
n
large, one has

(1.1)

{\rm ord}_{p}(h_{n}^{-})=\mu_{K}^{-}\ell^{n}+\lambda_{K}^{-}n+\nu_{K}^{-},

where
{\rm ord}_{p}
is the usual
p
-adic valuation on
\mathbb{Q}
. Let now
L/K
be a Galois extension of CM-number fields for which
{\rm Gal}(L/K)
is a
p
-group, and assume for simplicity that
p>2
and that
K
contains the
p
-th roots of unity. Kida showed that if
\mu_{K}^{-}=0
, then
\mu_{L}^{-}=0
, and

(1.2)

2\lambda_{L}^{-}-2=[L_{\infty}:K_{\infty}](2\lambda_{K}^{-}-2)+\sum_{w}(e_{w}-1),

where the sum is over all nonarchimedean places of
L_{\infty}
that split completely in
L_{\infty}/L_{\infty}^{+}
and that do not lie above
p
. Gras
[
5
]
had obtained similar transition formulae for the Iwasawa invariants of Kubota–Leopoldt
p
-adic
L
-functions, which were later extended to the case of CM-fields by Sinnott in
[
28
]
.

1.2.
Iwasawa theory of elliptic curves

Mazur
[
20
]
inititated the Iwasawa theory for a
p
-ordinary abelian varieties. Let
p
be a prime number,
K
a number field and
A_{/K}
be an abelian variety which is ordinary at all primes
v|p
of
K
. Mazur studied the structure and growth of the
p
-primary Selmer group of
A
along cyclotomic
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension of
K
. In the case of an elliptic curve
E/\mathbb{Q}
and a prime
p
of good ordinary reduction for
E
, Kato
[
10
]
and Rubin
[
25
,
26
]
proved that the
p
-primary Selmer group
\mathrm{Sel}_{p^{\infty}}(E/\mathbb{Q}_{\infty})
is a cotorsion module over the Iwasawa algebra
\Lambda=\mathbb{Z}_{p}[[\Gamma]]
with
\Gamma:=\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}_{\infty}/\mathbb{Q})
. Consequently, the Pontryagin dual of the Selmer group admits a well-defined characteristic ideal. Choose a topological generator
\gamma\in\Gamma
and set
T:=\gamma-1
. Then
\Lambda
is identified with the formal power series ring
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket T\rrbracket
. The characteristic ideal is principal, and a choice of generator
f_{\operatorname{Iw}}(T)
is the
Iwasawa polynomial
. By the Weierstrass preparation theorem,
f_{\operatorname{Iw}}(T)=p^{\mu}P(T)u(T)
where
\mu\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
,
P(T)
is a distinguished polynomial and
u(T)
is a unit in
\Lambda
. The quantity
\mu
is the
\mu
-invariant and the
\lambda
-invariant is defined to be the degree of
P(T)
.

A central ingredient in Mazur’s foundational study of Iwasawa theory for elliptic curves is a
control theorem
for the Selmer group. This result compares the Selmer groups over the finite layers
\mathbb{Q}_{(n)}
of the cyclotomic
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension with the Selmer group over the full infinite extension
\mathbb{Q}_{\infty}
. It asserts that, up to a finite and uniformly bounded error term, the
p
-primary Selmer group over
\mathbb{Q}_{(n)}
is obtained by taking
\Gamma^{p^{n}}
-invariants of the Selmer group over
\mathbb{Q}_{\infty}
. Consequently, Mazur’s control theorem yields an Iwasawa-theoretic formula describing the asymptotic growth of the
p
-primary part of the Tate–Shafarevich group of
E/\mathbb{Q}_{(n)}
as
n\to\infty
, directly paralleling Iwasawa’s classical formula for
p
-primary class groups in cyclotomic towers. More generally, the same formalism extends to modular forms via their associated
p
-adic Galois representations, with the case of elliptic curves arising from weight two newforms as a special instance.

On the analytic side, one may study the variation of the special values of the complex
L
-function of
E
along the layers of the cyclotomic extension. Using the modular symbols attached to the weight two cuspidal eigenform corresponding to
E
by modularity, Mazur and Swinnerton-Dyer constructed in
[
17
]
a
p
-adic
L
-function for
E
when
p
is a prime of good ordinary reduction. This
p
-adic analytic function interpolates the critical values
L(E,\chi,1)
as
\chi
ranges over finite-order characters of
\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}_{(n)}/\mathbb{Q})
. Moreover, the
p
-adic
L
-function naturally defines an element of the Iwasawa algebra
\Lambda\cong\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket T\rrbracket
, thereby giving rise to
analytic Iwasawa invariants
. The Iwasawa Main Conjecture predicts a precise equality between these analytic invariants associated with the
p
-adic
L
-function and the corresponding algebraic invariants arising from the structure of the Selmer group.

For primes of supersingular reduction, the situation is more subtle since in this setting the Selmer group over the cyclotomic
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension is no longer cotorsion over the Iwasawa algebra. Kobayashi
[
13
]
introduced the
plus and minus Selmer groups

\operatorname{Sel}^{\pm}(E/\mathbb{Q}_{(n)})
, which refine the usual Selmer group by imposing conditions on the formal group at
p
. Correspondingly, one constructs
signed
p
-adic
L
-functions

L_{p}^{\pm}(E)
that interpolate critical
L
-values twisted by characters of
\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}_{(n)}/\mathbb{Q})
, but with plus and minus signs reflecting the local conditions at
p
.

In
[
19
]
, Mazur and Tate introduced modular elements
\theta_{M}(f)
, now known as
Mazur–Tate elements
, which lie in the group ring
\mathbb{Z}[\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(\mu_{M})/\mathbb{Q})]
and encode special values of the complex
L
-function of a cuspidal eigenform
f
. Of particular interest for Iwasawa-theoretic applications are the
p
-adic Mazur–Tate elements, which may be viewed inside the finite group rings
\Lambda_{n}:=\mathbb{Z}_{p}[\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}_{(n)}/\mathbb{Q})]
. Each
\Lambda_{n}
is naturally identified with a quotient of the Iwasawa algebra
\Lambda
, and under this identification the Mazur–Tate elements inherit associated
\mu
- and
\lambda
-invariants. These provide a finite-level analogue of the analytic Iwasawa invariants and play an important role in understanding the variation of
L
-values and Selmer groups across the cyclotomic tower. For elliptic curves with additive reduction satisfying further conditions, Delbourgo
[
2
]
proved that the Selmer group is cotorsion over the Iwasawa algebra and constructed
p
-adic L-functions, formulating an analogue of the Main conjecture.

When
p
is a prime of good ordinary reduction, Mazur–Tate elements approximate the
p
-adic
L
-function, which can be realized as the inverse limit of (
p
-stabilized) Mazur–Tate elements under the norm maps
\Lambda_{n}\rightarrow\Lambda_{n-1}
. Analogous to the Iwasawa Main conjecture, these finite-layer analogues are expected to encode the structure of the
Fitting ideal
of the Selmer group over
\mathbb{Q}_{(n)}
. This constitutes the
refined main conjecture
, studied in
[
12
]
for elliptic curves with both ordinary and supersingular reduction at
p
. Mazur–Tate elements are also explicitly related to Kato’s zeta elements via a pairing defined by Kurihara
[
14
, Lemma 7.2]
, which is a key ingredient in the proof of the refined conjectures for supersingular primes.

1.3.
Kida-type formulae for elliptic curves and modular forms: Known results

In
[
6
]
, Hachimori and Matsuno established an analogue of Kida’s classical formula for the
\mu
- and
\lambda
-invariants of
p
-primary Selmer groups of elliptic curves. Let
E/\mathbb{Q}
be an elliptic curve with good ordinary reduction at an odd prime
p
, and let
L/\mathbb{Q}
be a
p
-cyclic extension. Assuming
\mu\bigl(\mathrm{Sel}_{p^{\infty}}(E/\mathbb{Q}_{\infty})\bigr)=0
, the Selmer group
\mathrm{Sel}_{p^{\infty}}(E/L_{\infty})
is cotorsion over the Iwasawa algebra, with
\mu\bigl(\mathrm{Sel}_{p^{\infty}}(E/L_{\infty})\bigr)=0
. Moreover, the
\lambda
-invariants satisfy

\lambda\bigl(\mathrm{Sel}_{p^{\infty}}(E/L_{\infty})\bigr)=[L_{\infty}:\mathbb{Q}_{\infty}]\,\lambda\bigl(\mathrm{Sel}_{p^{\infty}}(E/\mathbb{Q}_{\infty})\bigr)+\sum_{\tilde{w}\in P_{1}}\bigl(e(\tilde{w})-1\bigr)+2\sum_{\tilde{w}\in P_{2}}\bigl(e(\tilde{w})-1\bigr),

where
P_{1}
(resp.
P_{2}
) denotes the set of primes of
L_{\infty}
above which
E
has split multiplicative reduction (resp. has a
p
-torsion point locally), and
e(\tilde{w})
is the ramification index of
\tilde{w}
over the prime of
\mathbb{Q}_{\infty}
below it. This result shows that the vanishing of the
\mu
-invariant is preserved in
p
-cyclic extensions and provides an explicit formula controlling the change in
\lambda
-invariants. Analogous results hold for elliptic curves with multiplicative reduction.

The analytic counterpart of this formula was established in
[
16
]
for the
p
-adic
L
-function of elliptic curves over
\mathbb{Q}
at primes of good ordinary or multiplicative reduction. These Kida-type formulae for the analytic Iwasawa invariants match the algebraic invariants of the
p
-primary Selmer group, as predicted by the Iwasawa Main conjecture over
p
-extensions. In
[
21
]
, Pollack and Weston extended these results to a broad class of Galois representations associated to cusp forms, including Hilbert modular forms, under certain technical assumptions such as near
p
-ordinarity. They also treated the case of supersingular primes, relating the plus and minus
\lambda
-invariants via Kida-type formulae. Their approach relies crucially on congruence relations between the
\lambda
-invariants of related Galois representations.

1.4.
Main result

In this article, we extend the methods of
[
6
]
,
[
16
]
, and
[
28
]
to obtain an analogue of Kida’s formula for the Iwasawa invariants of Mazur–Tate elements attached to modular forms. Let
E
be an elliptic curve over
\mathbb{Q}
and
p
be an odd prime number. For
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
, we consider Mazur–Tate elements
\Theta_{n}(E/K)
over abelian extensions
K/\mathbb{Q}
. These elements are constructed so as to interpolate the special values
L(f/K,\chi,1)
for primitive characters
\chi
of
\operatorname{Gal}(K_{(n)}/K)
.

Let
S_{\operatorname{add}}
be the set of rational primes
\ell
at which
E
has additive reduction. We introduce the following hypothesis for a given number field
F
:

Assumption 1.1

(
Add
)
.

E
has additive reduction at any prime of
F
lying above
S_{\operatorname{add}}
.

Let
L/K
be a
p
-extension with both
L
and
K
abelian over
\mathbb{Q}
. We define

\begin{split}&P_{1}:=\{w:w\nmid p,\;E\text{ has split multiplicative reduction at }w\},\\
&P_{2}:=\{w:w\nmid p,\;E\text{ has good reduction at }w,\;E(L_{(n),w})[p]\neq 0\}.\end{split}

Let
n_{L},n_{K}
be integers with

L\cap\mathbb{Q}_{\infty}=\mathbb{Q}_{(n_{L})}\quad\text{and}\quad K\cap\mathbb{Q}_{\infty}=\mathbb{Q}_{(n_{K})}.

In other words,
L_{(n)}=L\mathbb{Q}_{(n+n_{L})}
and
K_{(n)}=K\mathbb{Q}_{(n+n_{K})}
. In particular,
L_{(n)}
contains
K_{(n+n_{L}-n_{K})}
. Denote by
e_{L_{(n)}/K_{(n+n_{L}-n_{K})}}(w)
(resp.
f_{L_{(n)}/K_{(n+n_{L}-n_{K})}}(w)
) the ramification index (resp. inertial degree) of
w
in
L_{(n)}/K_{(n+n_{L}-n_{K})}
. Note that if
n\geq\operatorname{ord}_{p}([L:K])-n_{L}
, the extension
L_{(n)}/K_{(n+n_{L}-n_{K})}
is totally ramified for any prime
w\nmid p
of
L_{(n)}
. In other words, for
n\geq\operatorname{ord}_{p}([L:K])-n_{L}
,

f_{L_{(n)}/K_{(n+n_{L}-n_{K})}}(w)=1.

Assumption 1.2

(
K-p
)
.

One of the following conditions are satisfied:

(1)

p\nmid[K:\mathbb{Q}]
,

(2)

p|[K:\mathbb{Q}]
and
n\geq\operatorname{ord}_{p}([L:\mathbb{Q}])
.

The main result of this paper is the following Kida-type formula for Mazur–Tate elements.

Theorem 1.3

(Kida’s formula for elliptic curves)
.

Let
n\geq 1
and assume that the conditions
(Add)
and
(K-p)
are satisfied. If

\mu(\Theta_{\,n+n_{L}-n_{K}}(E/K))=0,

then
\mu(\Theta_{n}(E/L))=0
, and

\displaystyle\lambda(\Theta_{n}(E/L))

\displaystyle=[L_{\infty}:K_{\infty}]\,\lambda(\Theta_{\,n+n_{L}-n_{K}}(E/K))+\sum_{w\in P_{1}}f_{L_{(n)}/K_{(n+n_{L}-n_{K})}}(w)\bigl(e_{L_{(n)}/K_{(n+n_{L}-n_{K})}}(w)-1\bigr)

\displaystyle+2\sum_{w\in P_{2}}f_{L_{(n)}/K_{(n+n_{L}-n_{K})}}(w)\bigl(e_{L_{(n)}/K_{(n+n_{L}-n_{K})}}(w)-1\bigr).

The theorem above demonstrates that such Kida-type formulae can indeed be established for Mazur–Tate elements, even for modular forms whose
p
-adic
L
-functions are not known to exist or be bounded. In particular, these results hold without imposing any hypotheses on the local behaviour of
E
at
p
.

For elliptic curves with semistable reduction at
p>2
, one may attach
p
-adic
L
-functions not only over
\mathbb{Q}
but also over abelian extensions
K/\mathbb{Q}
obtained by combining the
p
-adic
L
-functions twisted by the characters of
\operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q})
, cf. (
4.1
). The same construction, when applied to the signed
p
-adic
L
-functions associated to
E
, likewise produces signed
p
-adic
L
-functions over
K
. In the semistable case, the Iwasawa invariants
\mu
and
\lambda
of
\Theta_{n}(E/\mathbb{Q})
for
n\gg 0
agrees with the
\mu
and
\lambda
invariants of the
p
-adic L-function of
E
. Thus, the result above implies Matsuno’s main result
[
16
]
. For elliptic curves
E_{/\mathbb{Q}}
with good supersingular reduction at
p
satisfying
a_{p}(E)=0
, we obtain an analogue of Kida’s formula for the analytic Iwasawa invariants associated to signed
p
-adic L-functions (see Theorem
4.2
for further details). Similar formulae for the algebraic Iwasawa invariants for signed Selmer groups have been proven by Hatley and Lei
[
7
]
, and there are further refinements due to Forrás and Muller
[
4
]
.

In the additive reduction case, an analogue of Kida’s formula was established by the second author and Shingavekar
[
24
]
under the potentially good ordinary condition of Delbourgo
[
2
]
, which requires that
E
acquire good ordinary reduction at
p
over an extension contained in
\mathbb{Q}(\mu_{p})
. Under this hypothesis, Delbourgo constructed a
p
-adic
L
-function for
E
by relating the newform
f_{E}
to an ordinary weight
2
newform
\tilde{f}
of lower level obtained via twisting by a power of the Teichmüller character. This yields a bounded
p
-adic
L
-function to which Matsuno’s method applies directly, giving a Kida-type formula in this setting (see Theorem
4.5
).

1.5.
Outlook

These formulae align well with the philosophy of the
refined main conjectures
as discussed in
[
12
]
. Namely, we get a Kida formula for the
\lambda
-invariant at every finite layer of the cyclotomic
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension, indicating that an algebraic analogue of this would together imply such refined conjectures for base fields that are abelian extensions of
\mathbb{Q}
. As an application of our main result, we show that the Kida formula obtained by Matsuno in
[
16
]
for
p
-ordinary elliptic curves and by Pollack–Weston in
[
22
]
for
p
-supersingular modular forms follows from our Kida formula for Mazur–Tate elements. On another front, we extend Matsuno’s method to show a Kida formula holds for the
p
-adic
L
-function of elliptic curves with additive reduction defined by Delbourgo in
[
2
]
. The algebraic analogue of this was proved in
[
24
]
.

We expect there to be applications of our Kida formula to obtain formulae for the growth of Tate–Shafarevich groups in
p
-extensions of abelian number fields.

1.6.
Organization

Including the introduction, the article consists of four sections. In section
2
we discuss preliminary notions and basic properties of Mazur–Tate elements, their relation to L-values and their Iwasawa invariants. In section
3
, we prove the main results of this article, establishing an analogue of Kida’s formula for elliptic curves over
\mathbb{Q}
and more generally for Hecke eigencuspforms. Section
4

2.
Mazur–Tate elements

2.1.
Basic notions

Let us introduce Mazur–Tate elements associated to Hecke eigencuspforms, and their basic properties. Let
k\geq 2
be an integer and
g:=k-2
. Given a commutative ring
R
with
1
, set
V_{g}(R):=\operatorname{Sym}^{g}(R^{2})
, which we regard as the space of homogeneous polynomials of degree
g
in two variables
X,Y
with coefficients in
R
. For
\gamma={\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\\
\end{pmatrix}}\in\operatorname{GL}_{2}(R)
, set
\gamma^{*}:={\begin{pmatrix}d&-b\\
-c&a\\
\end{pmatrix}}
. The
R
-module
V_{g}(R)
is endowed with a natural right action of
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(R)
defined by

(P\mid\gamma)(X,Y)\;=\;P\bigl((X,Y)\gamma^{*}\bigr)\;=\;P(dX-cY,-bX+aY),

for
P\in V_{g}(R)
and
\gamma={\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\\
\end{pmatrix}}\in\operatorname{GL}_{2}(R)
. Note that when
k=2
,
V_{0}(R)=R
with trivial action of
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(R)
.

Definition 2.1

(Modular symbols)
.

By a modular symbol of weight
k
over the ring
R
, we mean an element in the compactly supported cohomology group
H^{1}_{c}(\Gamma,V_{g}(R))
, where
g:=k-2
and
V_{g}(R)
has the discrete topology.

If
\Gamma\subseteq\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
is a congruence subgroup, there is a canonical Hecke-equivariant isomorphism:

(2.1)

H_{c}^{1}(\Gamma,V_{g}(R))\;\;\cong\;\;\mathrm{Hom}_{\Gamma}\!\left(\operatorname{Div}^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{Q})),V_{g}(R)\right),

where
\operatorname{Div}^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{Q}))
denotes the group of degree-zero divisors on the cusps
\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{Q})
. The right-hand side consists of additive maps
\varphi:\operatorname{Div}^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{Q}))\to V_{g}(R)
satisfying the equivariance condition

(2.2)

\varphi(\gamma D)\mid\gamma=\varphi(D)

for all
\gamma\in\Gamma
. In what follows, we identify these spaces via the above isomorphism (
2.1
) and refer to them interchangeably as spaces of
modular symbols
.

Associated with a modular symbol is a sequence of group-ring elements known as the
Mazur–Tate elements
.
Fix an integer
M
coprime to
p
, and define

\Delta_{M}:=\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(\mu_{M})/\mathbb{Q}),\qquad\Delta:=\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(\mu_{p})/\mathbb{Q}).

For each integer
n\geq 0
, set
\mathscr{G}_{n}^{M}:=\operatorname{Gal}\!\bigl(\mathbb{Q}(\mu_{p^{n}M})/\mathbb{Q}\bigr)
.
This group decomposes as

\mathscr{G}_{n+1}^{M}\;\cong\;G_{n}\times\Delta\times\Delta_{M},

where
G_{n}:=\operatorname{Gal}\!\bigl(\mathbb{Q}(\mu_{p^{n+1}})/\mathbb{Q}(\mu_{p})\bigr)
. For brevity, write
\mathscr{G}_{n}:=\mathscr{G}_{n}^{1}
when
M=1
. Let

\phi_{n}:\mathscr{G}_{n+1}\twoheadrightarrow G_{n}

denote the projection onto the first component of the above decomposition. The cyclotomic character induces canonical isomorphisms

\chi_{n}^{M}:\mathscr{G}_{n}^{M}\xrightarrow{\;\sim\;}(\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}M\mathbb{Z})^{\times},\qquad\text{and set }\chi_{n}:=\chi_{n}^{1}.

Let
\omega:\mathscr{G}_{n}\xrightarrow{\;\sim\;}\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}
denote the Teichmüller character. Via the natural projection

\mathscr{G}_{n}^{M}\twoheadrightarrow\mathscr{G}_{n},

we may also regard
\omega
and
\phi_{n}
as group homomorphisms on
\mathscr{G}_{n}^{M}
. Set
\mathcal{G}:=\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(\mu_{p^{\infty}M})/\mathbb{Q})
and
\Gamma:=\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(\mu_{p^{\infty}})/\mathbb{Q}(\mu_{p}))
. Observe that there is a natural decomposition

\mathcal{G}=\Gamma\times\Delta\times\Delta_{M}.

Fix a discrete valuation ring
\mathcal{O}
containing
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
and let
\mathfrak{G}
be a profinite group. Then the Iwasawa algebra of
\mathfrak{G}
over
\mathcal{O}
is defined as the completed group algebra

\Lambda(\mathfrak{G}):=\varprojlim_{N}\mathcal{O}[\mathfrak{G}/N],

where
N
ranges over all finite index normal subgroups of
\mathfrak{G}
. For ease of notation, we shall set
\Lambda:=\Lambda(\Gamma)
.

We fix a topological generator
\gamma\in\Gamma
and let
\gamma_{n}
be the image of
\gamma
in
G_{n}
with respect to the natural quotient
\Gamma\twoheadrightarrow G_{n}
. Setting
T:=(\gamma-1)
, one identifies
\Lambda
with the formal power series ring
\mathcal{O}\llbracket T\rrbracket
. Set
\Lambda_{n}:=\mathcal{O}[G_{n}]
and note that

\Lambda_{n}\simeq\frac{\mathcal{O}[T]}{\left((1+T)^{p^{n}}-1\right)}.

For
a\in(\mathbb{Z}/p^{n+1}M\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
, define
t_{n}(a)\in(\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}\mathbb{Z},+)
such that
\chi_{n}^{M}(\gamma_{n})^{t_{n}(a)}\equiv\langle a\rangle\pmod{p^{n+1}}
, where
\langle a\rangle
is the projection of
a
onto the second factor in the decomposition:

(\mathbb{Z}/p^{n+1}M\mathbb{Z})^{\times}\simeq(\mathbb{Z}/pM\mathbb{Z})^{\times}\times(\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}\mathbb{Z},+).

Note that since the elements
\gamma_{n}
all arise from
\gamma
, it follows that
t_{m}(a)\equiv t_{n}(a)\pmod{p^{m}}
whenever
m\leq n
. Throughout, we fix an embedding

\iota:\overline{\mathbb{Q}}\hookrightarrow\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
.
For any Dirichlet character
\psi
, we implicitly extend scalars via
\iota

and hence view
\psi
as a
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
-valued character. Let
\psi
be character on
\mathscr{G}^{M}_{n+1}
, we view
\psi
has a Dirichlet character of modulus
p^{n+1}M
under our identification
\mathscr{G}^{M}_{n+1}\cong(\mathbb{Z}/p^{n+1}M\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
. We use the group homomorphism
\psi\phi_{n}
to get a map

\psi\phi_{n}:\mathcal{O}[\mathscr{G}_{n+1}^{M}]\xrightarrow{}{\mathcal{O}[G_{n}]}=\Lambda_{n}\simeq\frac{\mathcal{O}[T]}{((1+T)^{p^{n}}-1)}

when
\mathcal{O}
contains the values of
\psi
.
We use this map to define Mazur–Tate elements twisted by
\psi
as polynomials in
T
:

Definition 2.2

(Mazur–Tate elements)
.

Let
p>2
be prime and let
M\geq 1
be an integer such that
p\nmid M
. The classical Mazur–Tate element of level
n
associated with
\varphi\in H^{1}_{c}(\Gamma,V_{g}(R))
is defined as

\Theta_{n}^{M}(\varphi):=\sum_{a\in(\mathbb{Z}/p^{n+1}M\mathbb{Z})^{\times}}\varphi(\{\infty\}-\{a/p^{n+1}M\})|_{(X,Y)=(0,1)}\cdot\sigma_{a}\in\mathcal{O}[\mathscr{G}_{n+1}^{M}].

where
\chi_{n+1}^{M}(\sigma_{a})=a
(recall
\chi_{n+1}^{M}
is the cyclotomic character used to identify
\mathscr{G}_{n+1}^{M}\cong(\mathbb{Z}/p^{n+1}M\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
).

Let
\psi
be a Dirichlet character of conductor
M^{\prime}p^{m}
for
m\leq n
and
M^{\prime}\mid M
. Assume that
\mathcal{O}
contains
\mathbb{Z}_{p}[\psi]
. Define the Mazur–Tate element of level
n
associated with
\varphi\in H^{1}_{c}(\Gamma,V_{g}(R))
twisted by
\psi
as

\displaystyle\Theta_{n}^{M}(\varphi,\psi,T):=

\displaystyle\psi\phi_{n}(\Theta_{n}^{M}(\varphi))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{a\in(\mathbb{Z}/p^{n+1}M\mathbb{Z})^{\times}}\varphi(\{\infty\}-\{a/p^{n+1}M\})|_{(X,Y)=(0,1)}\cdot\psi(a)\cdot(1+T)^{t_{n}(a)}\in\Lambda_{n}.

When
M=1
and
\psi
is the trivial character, we simply denote the Mazur–Tate element by
\Theta_{n}(\varphi,T)
.

It is worth noting that Mazur–Tate elements are often defined as elements of the group ring
\Lambda_{n}
for characters
\psi
of modulus prime to
p
(cf. for instance,
[
22
, p. 357]
). On the other hand, we allow twists by characters
\psi
having conductor divisible by powers of
p
.
Indeed, we shall show that this modified Mazur–Tate element satisfies interpolation properties similar to the usual Mazur–Tate elements (see Proposition
2.6
).

Associate Iwasawa invariants
\mu(G)
and
\lambda(G)
to a power series
G=\sum_{j}b_{j}T^{j}\in\Lambda
as follows. By the Weierstrass preparation theorem,
G(T)=\pi^{m}P(T)u(T)
where
P(T)
is a
distinguished polynomial
and
u(T)
is a unit in
\Lambda
. This means that
P(T)
is a monic polynomial whose non-leading coefficients are divisible by
\pi
and
u(0)\in\mathcal{O}^{\times}
. Given such a decomposition,

\mu(G):=\frac{m}{e_{K/\mathbb{Q}}}\quad\text{and}\quad\lambda(G):=\operatorname{deg}P(T),

where
e_{K/\mathbb{Q}}
is the ramification index of
K
over
\mathbb{Q}
. On the other hand, the
\mu
and
\lambda
-invariants of
F=\sum_{i=0}^{p^{n}-1}a_{i}T^{i}\in\Lambda_{n}
are defined as

(2.3)

\begin{split}\mu(F)&=\underset{i}{\min}\{\mathrm{ord}_{p}(a_{i})\},\\
\lambda(F)&=\min\{i:\mathrm{ord}_{p}(a_{i})=\mu(F)\}\end{split}

where
\mathrm{ord}_{p}
is the
p
-adic valuation such that
\mathrm{ord}_{p}(p)=1
. If
F=0
, set
\mu(F),\lambda(F):=\infty
. We note that
\operatorname{ord}_{p}(\cdot)
has values in
\mathbb{Q}
when
\mathcal{O}
is a ramified extension of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
, and hence so does
\mu(F)
. Note however that
\lambda(F)
is always an integer and is independent of the choice of
\mathcal{O}
.

Remark 2.3
.

One may define
\mu
and
\lambda
-invariants for an element in
\Lambda_{n}
intrinsically, purely in terms of the augmentation ideal (see
[
22
, § 3.1]
). Thus the Iwasawa invariants are independent of the choice of the generator
\gamma_{n}
.

2.2.
Modular symbols attached to eigenforms

Next, we discuss the definition and properties of symbols
\varphi
which arise from a normalized Hecke eigencuspform
f=\sum a_{n}(f)q^{n}\in S_{k}(\Gamma_{0}(N),\mathbb{C})
for some integers
k\geq 2
and
N\geq 1
. Denote by
K_{f}
the Hecke field of
f
, i.e., the number field generated by the Fourier coefficients
\{a_{n}(f)\mid n\geq 1\}
. Let
\xi_{f}
be the modular symbol defined by:

\xi_{f}(\{r\}-\{s\})\;=\;\int_{s}^{r}f(z)(zX+Y)^{k-2}\,dz,\quad r,s\in\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{Q}).

The action of the Hecke operators on
H^{1}_{c}(\Gamma_{0}(N),V_{k-2}(\mathbb{C}))
is given by

T_{\ell}:=\epsilon_{N}(\ell)\begin{pmatrix}\ell&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}+\sum_{j=0}^{\ell-1}{\begin{pmatrix}1&j\\
0&\ell\\
\end{pmatrix}},

where
\epsilon_{N}
denotes the character on
\mathbb{Z}
defined as

\epsilon_{N}(a)=\begin{cases}1\text{ if }(a,N)=1\\
0\text{ if }(a,N)\neq 1.\end{cases}

The symbol
\xi_{f}
is a Hecke eigensymbol with the same eigenvalues as
f
, i.e.,
T_{\ell}\varphi_{f}=\varphi_{T_{\ell}f}
, see
[
1
, Chapter IV, § 2.2]
.

The involution
\iota=\begin{pmatrix}-1&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}
acts on the space of modular symbols, and
\xi_{f}
decomposes uniquely as
\xi_{f}^{+}+\xi_{f}^{-}
with
\xi_{f}^{\pm}
in the
\pm 1
-eigenspaces. By a theorem of Shimura
[
27
]
, there exist complex numbers
\Omega_{f}^{\pm}
(the
\pm
-periods of
f
) such that
\xi_{f}^{\pm}
takes values in
V_{k-2}(K_{f})\Omega_{f}^{\pm}
, where
K_{f}
is the Hecke field of
f
. After fixing an embedding
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}\hookrightarrow\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
, normalize
\xi_{f}^{\pm}
by setting
\varphi_{f}^{\pm}:=\xi_{f}^{\pm}/\Omega_{f}^{\pm}
, which then takes values in
V_{k-2}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p})
. Write
\varphi_{f}:=\varphi_{f}^{+}+\varphi_{f}^{-}
, which depends on the chosen periods. Given
r\in\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{Q})
, set

\varphi_{f}(r):=\varphi_{f}(\{\infty\}-\{r\})

for ease of notation.

Let
\mathcal{O}_{f}
denotes the ring of integers of the
p
-adic completion of
K_{f}
. It is convenient to choose
\Omega_{f}^{\pm}
so that
\varphi_{f}^{\pm}
has coefficients integral at
p
. More precisely,
\Omega_{f}^{\pm}
are called
cohomological periods
for
f
if
\varphi_{f}^{\pm}
takes values in
V_{k-2}(\mathcal{O}_{f})
, and there exists
r,s\in\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{Q})
such that
\varphi_{f}^{\pm}(\{r\}-\{s\})
has at least one coefficient which is a
p
-adic unit. Such periods always exist and are unique up to multiplication by a
p
-adic unit in
K_{f}
. Fixing
\psi
, we choose a valuation ring
\mathcal{O}
containing
\mathcal{O}_{f}(\psi)
. In practice,
\mathcal{O}
will also be unramified over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
. With this normalization, one defines the Mazur–Tate elements attached to
f
as follows:

\Theta_{n}^{M}(f,\psi,T)\;=\;\Theta_{n}^{M}(\varphi_{f},\psi,T).

We recall some fundamental properties of modular symbols. Since
\varphi_{f}
is a Hecke eigensymbol,

(2.4)

a_{\ell}\varphi_{f}(r)=\epsilon_{N}(\ell)\ell^{k-2}\varphi_{f}(\ell r)+\sum_{j=0}^{\ell-1}\varphi_{f}((r+j)/\ell),

for all
r\in\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{Q})
.

For integers
n>m
, there are natural projection maps

\pi_{n,m}^{M}:\mathcal{O}[\mathscr{G}_{n}^{M}]\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}[\mathscr{G}_{m}^{M}],

arising from the canonical surjection

\mathscr{G}_{n}^{M}=\operatorname{Gal}\!\bigl(\mathbb{Q}(\mu_{p^{n}M})/\mathbb{Q}\bigr)\twoheadrightarrow\operatorname{Gal}\!\bigl(\mathbb{Q}(\mu_{p^{m}M})/\mathbb{Q}\bigr)=\mathscr{G}_{m}^{M}.

Similarly, for integers
M\mid M^{\prime}
, we define projection maps

\pi_{n}^{M^{\prime},M}:\mathcal{O}[\mathscr{G}_{n}^{M^{\prime}}]\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}[\mathscr{G}_{n}^{M}],

induced by the natural quotient

\mathscr{G}_{n}^{M^{\prime}}=\operatorname{Gal}\!\bigl(\mathbb{Q}(\mu_{p^{n}M^{\prime}})/\mathbb{Q}\bigr)\twoheadrightarrow\operatorname{Gal}\!\bigl(\mathbb{Q}(\mu_{p^{n}M})/\mathbb{Q}\bigr)=\mathscr{G}_{n}^{M}.

Under the cyclotomic identifications

\chi_{n}^{M}:\mathscr{G}_{n}^{M}\xrightarrow{\;\sim\;}(\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}M\mathbb{Z})^{\times},

the maps
\pi_{n,m}^{M}
and
\pi_{n}^{M^{\prime},M}
correspond, respectively, to the natural reduction maps

(\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}M\mathbb{Z})^{\times}\longrightarrow(\mathbb{Z}/p^{m}M\mathbb{Z})^{\times}\qquad\text{and}\qquad(\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}M^{\prime}\mathbb{Z})^{\times}\longrightarrow(\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}M\mathbb{Z})^{\times}.

We also define the
trace map

\nu_{n-1,n}^{M}:\mathcal{O}[\mathscr{G}_{n-1}^{M}]\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}[\mathscr{G}_{n}^{M}]

by

\nu_{n-1,n}^{M}(\tau)\;=\;\sum_{\pi(\sigma)=\tau}\sigma,\qquad\tau\in\mathscr{G}_{n-1}^{M},

where
\pi:\mathscr{G}_{n}^{M}\twoheadrightarrow\mathscr{G}_{n-1}^{M}
denotes the natural projection.
Under the cyclotomic identification

\mathscr{G}_{n}^{M}\;\xrightarrow{\;\chi_{n}^{M}\;}\;(\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}M\mathbb{Z})^{\times},

the map
\nu_{n-1,n}^{M}
corresponds to the operation
h(T)\mapsto\tilde{h}(T)\,\omega_{n}(T)
, where
\widetilde{h}(T)
is lift of
h(T)
with respect to the map
\pi_{n,n-1}^{M}
and
\omega_{n}(T):=\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}(1+T)^{jp^{n-1}}
. The following result shows that Mazur–Tate elements satisfy certain compatibility relations with respect to projection maps defined above. In fact, these relations are derived from (
2.4
).

Lemma 2.4

(Compatibility with
\pi^{M\ell,M}_{n}
)
.

Let
\ell
be a prime such that
\ell\nmid Mp
and
\psi
be a character of conductor
p^{m}M
for
m\leq n
. Then, we have that

\pi^{M\ell,M}_{n}(\Theta^{M\ell}_{n}(f))=(a_{\ell}(f)-\sigma_{\ell}-\epsilon_{N}(\ell)\ell^{k-2}\sigma_{\ell})\Theta^{M}_{n}({f}).

Thus, we have

\Theta^{M\ell}_{n}(f,\psi,T)=h_{\ell}(\psi,T)\Theta^{M}_{n}({f},\psi,T),

with

h_{\ell}(\psi,T):=a_{\ell}-\psi(\ell)(1+T)^{t_{n}(\ell)}-\epsilon_{N}(\ell)\ell^{k-2}\psi(\ell)^{-1}(1+T)^{-t_{n}(\ell)}.

Proof.

From the Hecke equivariance relation (
2.4
), it follows that

\displaystyle a_{\ell}\varphi_{f}\left(\frac{a\ell}{p^{n+1}M}\right)

\displaystyle=\epsilon_{N}(\ell)\ell^{k-2}\varphi_{f}\left(\frac{a\ell^{2}}{p^{n+1}M}\right)+\varphi\left(\frac{a}{p^{n+1}M}\right)+\sum_{j=1}^{\ell-1}\varphi\left(\frac{a\ell+jp^{n+1}M}{p^{n+1}M\ell}\right).

Note that

\psi(a\ell+jp^{n+1}M)=\psi(a\ell)\quad\text{and}\quad t_{n}(a\ell+jp^{n+1}M)=t_{n}(a\ell).

Thus we deduce that

\displaystyle\pi^{M\ell,M}_{n}(\Theta^{M\ell}_{n}(f))

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a=0\\
(a,pM)=1\end{subarray}}^{(p^{n+1}M-1)}\left[\sum_{j=1}^{\ell-1}\varphi_{f}\left(\frac{a\ell+jp^{n+1}M}{p^{n+1}M\ell}\right)\right]\ \sigma_{a\ell}

\displaystyle=a_{\ell}\cdot\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a=0\\
(a,pM)=1\end{subarray}}^{(p^{n+1}M-1)}\varphi_{f}(a\ell/p^{n+1}M)\sigma_{a\ell}

\displaystyle-\epsilon_{N}(\ell)\ell^{k-2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a=0\\
(a,pM)=1\end{subarray}}^{(p^{n+1}M-1)}\varphi_{f}\left(\frac{a\ell^{2}}{p^{n+1}M}\right)\sigma_{a\ell}-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a=0\\
(a,pM)=1\end{subarray}}^{(p^{n+1}M-1)}\varphi\left(\frac{a}{p^{n+1}M}\right)\sigma_{a\ell}

\displaystyle=(a_{\ell}(f)-\sigma_{\ell}-\epsilon_{N}(\ell)\ell^{k-2}\sigma_{\ell})\Theta^{M}_{n}({f})

proving the result. Apply
\psi\phi_{n}
on both sides, to get

\psi\phi_{n}(\pi^{M\ell,M}_{n}(\Theta_{n}^{M\ell}(f)))=h_{\ell}(\psi,T)\Theta_{n}^{M}(f,\psi,T).

Finally, note that since
\psi
has conductor
p^{m}M
, we have
\psi(a)=\psi(a\pmod{p^{m}M})
for
a\in(\mathbb{Z}/p^{n+1}M\ell\mathbb{Z})
, this implies

\psi\phi_{n}(\pi^{M\ell,M}_{n}(\Theta_{n}^{M\ell}(f)))=\psi\phi_{n}(\Theta_{n}^{M\ell}(f)))=\Theta_{n}^{M\ell}(f,\psi,T).

∎

The polynomial
h_{\ell}(\psi,T)
can be understood as an Iwasawa theoretic Euler factor, i.e., an element of
\Lambda_{n}
that interpolates the Euler factor at
\ell
. In the proof of Lemma
2.4
, the key input was that
\varphi_{f}
is an eigensymbol for the Hecke operator
T_{\ell}
for
\ell\neq p
. Similarly, we can use that
\varphi_{f}
is an eigensymbol for the Hecke operator
T_{p}
to get the following compatibility relation:

\pi^{M}_{n,n-1}(\Theta^{M}_{n}(f,\psi,T)))=a_{p}(f)\Theta^{M}_{n}({f},\psi,T)-\epsilon_{N}(p)p^{k-2}\nu_{n-1,n}(\Theta_{n-1}(f,\psi,T)).

For a proof, see
[
18
, § 12, (4.2)]
. Now, define the sequence
c_{n-i+1}
recursively, as follows:

c_{0}=0,c_{1}=1
and
c_{m}=a_{p}p^{-1}c_{m-1}-\epsilon_{N}(p)p^{k-3}c_{m-2}
.
Then, one can show by induction, that the following holds:

\pi^{M}_{n,i}(\Theta^{M}_{n}(f,\psi,T)))=p^{n-i}c_{n-i+1}\Theta^{M}_{i}({f},\psi,T)-\epsilon_{N}(p)p^{k-2}p^{n-i-1}c_{n-i}\nu_{i-1,i}(\Theta_{i-1}(f,\psi,T))

(see
[
14
, § 1,2]
for further details).
It follows that

(2.5)

\Theta^{M}_{n}(f,\psi,\zeta_{p^{i}}-1)=\pi^{M}_{n,i}(\Theta^{M}_{n}(f,\psi,\zeta_{p^{i}}-1)))=p^{n-i}c_{n-i+1}\Theta^{M}_{i}({f},\psi,\zeta_{p^{i}}-1)

where
\zeta_{p^{i}}
denotes a primitive
p^{i}
th root of unity. Note that evaluating
\Theta_{n}^{M}(f,\psi,T)
at
T=\zeta_{p^{i}}-1
corresponds to the value (at
s=1
) of the
L
function of
f
twisted by a character of conductor
p^{i+1}
and order
p^{i}
(i.e. a primitive character on
G_{i}\cong\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}_{(i)}/\mathbb{Q})
) via the interpolation property, which we discuss below in Proposition
2.6
.

2.3.
Interpolating
L
-values: Birch’s lemma

We recall some basic computations from
[
18
, §8]
relating Mazur–Tate elements to special values of
L
-functions.
Let
\chi
be a Dirichlet character of conductor
m
, and adopt the shorthand
e(z):=e^{2\pi iz}
. The associated Gauss sum is defined by

\tau(n,\chi):=\sum_{a\bmod m}\chi(a)\,e(na/m)

and set
\tau(\chi):=\tau(1,\chi)
. Then
\tau(n,\chi)=\overline{\chi}(n)\,\tau(\chi)
for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Let
N\geq 1
be an integer and let
f=\sum_{n\geq 1}a_{n}(f)e(nz)
be a Hecke eigencuspform in
S_{2}(\Gamma_{0}(N))
. The
L
-function associated with
f
is computed via the Mellin transform

(2.6)

L(f,s)=\sum_{n\geq 1}a_{n}(f)n^{-s}=\frac{2\pi^{s}}{\Gamma(s)}\int_{0}^{\infty}f(it)t^{s-1}dt

The
\chi
-twist of
f
is defined as follows:

f_{\chi}(z):=\sum_{n}a_{n}(f)\chi(n)e(nz).

Lemma 2.5

(Birch’s lemma)
.

Let
\chi
be a Dirichlet character of conductor
m
. Then

\begin{split}f_{\overline{\chi}}(z)=&\frac{1}{\tau(\chi)}\sum_{a\operatorname{mod}m}\chi(a)f\left(z+\frac{a}{m}\right).\end{split}

Proof.

Since
\chi
is primitive, we have the relation
\overline{\chi}(n)=\tau(n,\chi)/\tau(\chi)
for all
n
. Thus, we find that

\displaystyle f_{\overline{\chi}}(z)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\tau(\chi)}\sum_{(n,m)=1}a_{n}\tau(n,\chi)e(nz)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\tau(\chi)}\sum_{a\operatorname{mod}m}\chi(a)\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}e\left(n\left(z+\frac{a}{m}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\tau(\chi)}\sum_{a\operatorname{mod}m}\chi(a)f\left(z+\frac{a}{m}\right).

∎

Let
\chi_{n}:G_{n}\rightarrow\mu_{p^{n}}
denote the character
\gamma_{n}\mapsto\zeta_{p^{n}}
. Since we have identified
G_{n}
with the
p
-Sylow subgroup of
(\mathbb{Z}/p^{n+1}\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
,
\chi_{n}
gives rise to a Dirichlet character of conductor
p^{n+1}
.

As a result of Lemma
2.5
, we get the following proposition.

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
M\geq 1
be an integer such that
(p,M)=1
. Let
\chi
be as above. Then

(a)

If
\psi
is a Dirichlet character of conductor
Mp^{m}
for
m\leq n
, then

\Theta^{M}_{n}(f,\psi,\zeta_{p^{n}}-1)={\tau(\psi\chi_{n})}\frac{L(f,\overline{\psi\chi_{n}},1)}{\Omega_{f}^{\psi(-1)}},

(b)

For
i<n
, we have

\Theta^{M}_{n}(f,\psi,\zeta_{p^{i}}-1)={p^{n-i}c_{n-i+1}}{\tau(\chi_{i}\psi)}\frac{L(f,\overline{\psi\chi_{i}},1)}{\Omega_{f}^{\psi(-1)}}

where the
c_{m}
are defined recursively by
c_{0}=0,c_{1}=1
and
c_{m}=a_{p}p^{-1}c_{m-1}-\epsilon_{N}(p)p^{-1}c_{m-2}
for
m\geq 2
.

Remark 2.7
.

In particular, we have

\Theta^{M}_{n}(f,\psi,0)=\frac{p^{n}c_{n+1}}{\tau(\psi)}\frac{L(f,\overline{\psi},1)}{\Omega_{f}}

for a character
\psi
of conductor
M
coprime to
p
. Thus, the constant term of
\Theta_{n}^{M}(f,\psi,T)
can be used to recover
L(f,\psi,1)
provided
c_{n+1}\neq 0
. Also, note that if
\lambda(\Theta_{n}^{M}(f,\psi,T))=0
, then
L(f,\overline{\psi},1)\neq 0
.

Proof of Proposition
2.6
.

Part (b) follows from (
2.5
), we prove part (a). Applying the Mellin transform as in (
2.6
) to the statement of Lemma
2.5
, we find that:

\displaystyle{\tau(\psi\chi_{n})}L(f,\overline{\psi\chi_{n}},1)=

\displaystyle\sum_{a\operatorname{mod}{p^{n+1}M}}\psi\chi_{n}(a)\int_{a/p^{n+1}M}^{\infty}f(z)dz

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{a\operatorname{mod}{p^{n+1}M}}\psi\chi_{n}(a)\xi_{f}(\{\infty\}-\{a/p^{n+1}M\})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{a\operatorname{mod}{p^{n+1}M}}\psi(a)\xi_{f}(\{\infty\}-\{a/p^{n+1}M\})(\zeta_{p^{n}})^{t_{n}(a)}.

Here we have implicitly used the fact that
\psi\chi_{n}
has conductor
p^{n+1}M
.

Also note that
\xi_{f}^{\pm}(\{\infty\}-\{-r\})=\pm\xi_{f}^{\pm}(\{\infty\}-\{r\})
since
\xi_{f}^{\pm}
is an eigenvector for the involution
\iota
with sign
\pm 1
. It then follows that

\displaystyle\sum_{a\operatorname{mod}{p^{n+1}M}}\psi\chi_{n}(a)\xi_{f}^{-\psi(-1)}(\{\infty\}-\{a/p^{n+1}M\})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{a\operatorname{mod}{p^{n+1}M}}\psi\chi_{n}(-a)\xi_{f}^{-\psi(-1)}(\{\infty\}-\{-a/p^{n+1}M\})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\psi(-1)^{2}\sum_{a\operatorname{mod}{p^{n+1}M}}\psi\chi_{n}(a)\xi_{f}^{-\psi(-1)}(\{\infty\}-\{a/p^{n+1}M\})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\sum_{a\operatorname{mod}{p^{n+1}M}}\psi\chi_{n}(a)\xi_{f}^{-\psi(-1)}(\{\infty\}-\{a/p^{n+1}M\}).

Thus this sum equals
0
and consequently,

\sum_{a\operatorname{mod}{p^{n+1}M}}\psi\chi_{n}(a)\xi_{f}(\{\infty\}-\{a/p^{n+1}M\})=\sum_{a\operatorname{mod}{p^{n+1}M}}\psi\chi_{n}(a)\xi_{f}^{\psi(-1)}(\{\infty\}-\{a/p^{n+1}M\})

from which part (a) follows.
∎

2.4.
Mazur–Tate elements over abelian extensions of
\mathbb{Q}

For a character
\kappa
, let
M_{\kappa}
denote the non-
p
part of its conductor, i.e., the conductor of
\kappa
is of the form
p^{n^{\prime}}M_{\kappa}
for some
n^{\prime}\geq 0
with
(p,M_{\kappa})=1
. For an abelian extension
K/\mathbb{Q}
, let
n_{K}
denote the integer such that
K\cap\mathbb{Q}_{\infty}=\mathbb{Q}_{(n_{K})}
.
Then, using facts about the ramification at primes lying above
p
in
K
, it can be shown that
K\subset\mathbb{Q}(\mu_{p^{n_{K}+1}M})
with
(p,M)=1
. Thus, a character on
\operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q})
when viewed as a Dirichlet character has conductor having
p
-adic valuation at most
n_{K}+1
.

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
\zeta
be a primitive
p^{m}
-th root of unity and define

g(T):=\prod_{i=0}^{p^{m}-1}f(\zeta^{i}(1+T)-1)

for some
f(T)\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}[T]
. Then there exists a unique polynomial
h(T)\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}[T]
such that

g(T)=h\bigl((1+T)^{p^{m}}-1\bigr).

Proof.

The proof follows an argument from
[
29
, Lemma 13.39]
. First we prove the existence of
h
. Note that
\deg g=p^{m}\cdot\deg f
. Moreover,

(2.7)

g\bigl(\zeta^{i}(1+T)-1\bigr)=g(T)

for all
0\leq i\leq p^{m}-1
. In particular, substituting
T=\zeta^{j}-1
shows that
g(\zeta^{j}-1)=g(0)
for all
0\leq j\leq p^{m}-1
.

It follows that

g(T)=g(0)+\bigl((1+T)^{p^{m}}-1\bigr)\cdot g_{1}(T)

for some
g_{1}(T)\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}[T]
with
\deg g_{1}=\deg g-p^{m}
. By (
2.7
), the polynomial
g_{1}
also satisfies

g_{1}\bigl(\zeta^{i}(1+T)-1\bigr)=g_{1}(T)

for all
0\leq i\leq p^{m}-1
. Repeating the above argument for
g_{1}
, we write

g_{1}(T)=g_{1}(0)+\bigl((1+T)^{p^{m}}-1\bigr)\cdot g_{2}(T)

with
g_{2}(T)\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}[T]
and
\deg g_{2}=\deg g-2p^{m}
. Continuing inductively, we obtain

g(T)=\sum_{i=0}^{\deg f}a_{i}\bigl((1+T)^{p^{m}}-1\bigr)^{i}

for some
a_{0},\dots,a_{\deg f}\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}
. Thus we may take

h(T):=\sum_{i=0}^{\deg f}a_{i}T^{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}[T].

To prove uniqueness, suppose
h_{1},h_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}[T]
both satisfy

h_{1}\bigl((1+T)^{p^{m}}-1\bigr)=h_{2}\bigl((1+T)^{p^{m}}-1\bigr)=g(T).

Then for every
n\geq m
and any primitive
p^{n}
-th root of unity
\zeta_{p^{n}}
, we have

h_{1}(\zeta_{p^{n}}^{m}-1)=g(\zeta_{p^{n}}-1)=h_{2}(\zeta_{p^{n}}^{m}-1).

Since this holds for infinitely many distinct values
\zeta_{p^{n}}^{m}-1
, it follows that
h_{1}-h_{2}
has infinitely many roots and is therefore the zero polynomial. Thus
h_{1}=h_{2}
, proving uniqueness.
∎

3.
A Kida-type formula for the
\Theta
elements

In this section, we prove our main result. Let
\mathcal{O}
be a finite extension of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
and let
\pi
be a chosen uniformizer. For a cuspidal eigenform
f
and a character
\chi
of conductor
p^{m}M
with
(p,M)=1
taking values in
\mathcal{O}^{\times}
, define for
n\geq m
in accordance with (
2.3
), the twisted Iwasawa invariants:

\displaystyle\mu_{n}^{M}(\chi):=\mu(\Theta_{n}^{M}(f,\chi,T))

\displaystyle\lambda_{n}^{M}(\chi):=\lambda(\Theta_{n}^{M}(f,\chi,T)).

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
\chi,\psi
be finite order characters on
\mathscr{G}_{n}^{M}
taking values in
\mathcal{O}^{\times}
. Assume
\chi
has
p
-power order and
\mu_{n}^{M}(f,\psi)=0
. Then we have

\mu_{n}^{M}(\chi\psi)=0\text{ and }\lambda_{n}^{M}(\chi\psi)=\lambda_{n}^{M}(\psi).

Proof.

The argument is similar to that in the proof of
[
16
, Lemma 3.2]
. Given
F,G\in\Lambda_{n}
such that
F\equiv G\pmod{(\pi)}
, one has that

\mu(F)=0\Leftrightarrow\mu(G)=0.

Further, if the above
\mu
-invariants vanish, then
F\equiv cT^{\lambda(F)}\pmod{\pi\Lambda_{n}}
where
c\in(\mathcal{O}/\pi)^{\times}
. We deduce that
\lambda(F)=\lambda(G)
. Thus it suffices to show that

(3.1)

\Theta_{n}^{M}(f,\chi\psi,T)\equiv\Theta_{n}^{M}(f,\psi,T)\pmod{\pi\Lambda_{n}}.

Since
\chi
has
p
-power order,
\chi(x)\equiv 1\pmod{\pi}
for any
x\in(\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}M\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
. This gives us the congruence (
3.1
) and the result follows.
∎

We define an integer
g_{\psi,n}(\ell)
in terms of the decomposition of primes in the finite layers of the cyclotomic
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension
\mathbb{Q}_{\infty}/\mathbb{Q}
. This will be crucial to our proof of Kida’s formula.

Definition 3.2
.

Give a rational prime
\ell
and an algebraic extension
F/\mathbb{Q}
, let
g_{F}(\ell)
be the number of primes of
F
lying above
\ell
. If
\ell
is infinitely decomposed in
F
, set
g_{F}(\ell):=\infty
. For a character
\psi:\mathscr{G}_{n}^{M}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}^{\times}
and a Hecke eigen-cuspform
f=\sum_{n}a_{n}e(nz)\in S_{k}(\Gamma_{0}(N))
, define
g_{\psi,n}(\ell)
as follows:

(i)

If
\psi(\ell)\neq 0
and
\ell\nmid N
,

g_{\psi,n}(\ell)=\begin{cases}0&\text{if }a_{\ell}\not\equiv\psi(\ell)+\ell^{k-2}\psi(\ell)^{-1}\pmod{\pi}\\
g_{\mathbb{Q}_{(n)}}(\ell)&\text{if }a_{\ell}\equiv\psi(\ell)+\ell^{k-2}\psi(\ell)^{-1}\pmod{\pi},\ a_{\ell}\not\equiv\pm 2\ell^{\frac{k-2}{2}}\pmod{\pi}\\
2g_{\mathbb{Q}_{(n)}}(\ell)&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

(ii)

If
\psi(\ell)\neq 0
and
\ell\mid N
,

g_{\psi,n}(\ell)=\begin{cases}0&\text{if }a_{\ell}\not\equiv\psi(\ell)\pmod{\pi}\\
g_{\mathbb{Q}_{(n)}}(\ell)&\text{if }a_{\ell}\equiv\psi(\ell)\pmod{\pi}.\end{cases}

(iii)

If
\psi(\ell)=0
, then
g_{\psi,n}(\ell)=0
.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
\psi
be a Dirichlet character of modulus
p^{n}M
, and assume that the order of
\psi
is prime to
p
. Let
\ell
be a prime such that
(\ell,pM)=1
. Then

(3.2)

\mu_{n}^{M\ell}(\psi)=\mu_{n}^{M}(\psi)\qquad\text{and}\qquad\lambda_{n}^{M\ell}(\psi)=\lambda_{n}^{M}(\psi)+g_{\psi,n}(\ell).

Proof.

By Lemma
2.4
it suffices to analyze the term

h_{\ell}(\psi,T):=a_{\ell}-\psi(\ell)(1+T)^{t_{n}(\ell)}-\epsilon_{N}(\ell)\ell^{k-2}\psi(\ell)^{-1}(1+T)^{-t_{n}(\ell)}.

Write
t_{n}(\ell)=up^{a}
with
0\leq a\leq n-1
and
u\in(\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
. Recall that

\chi_{n}(\sigma_{\ell})=\langle\ell\rangle=\chi_{n}(\gamma_{n})^{t_{n}(\ell)},

so the index of the decomposition group of
\ell
in
\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}_{(n)}/\mathbb{Q})
is
p^{a}
, i.e.
p^{a}=g_{\mathbb{Q}_{(n)}}(\ell)
. It suffices to show that

(3.3)

\mu\left(h_{\ell}(\psi,T)\right)=0\quad\text{and}\quad\lambda\left(h_{\ell}(\psi,T)\right)=g_{\psi,n}(\ell).

We show that not all coefficients of
h_{\ell}(\psi,T)
are divisible by
\pi
and determine the smallest nonzero degree, which is
\lambda
-invariant of
h_{\ell}(\psi,T)
.

Working modulo
\pi
in the coefficient ring, we use the congruence

(1+T)^{p^{a}}\equiv 1+T^{p^{a}}\pmod{\pi}.

Expanding
(1+T^{p^{a}})^{u}
by the binomial theorem yields

(1+T)^{up^{a}}\equiv 1+uT^{p^{a}}+\binom{u}{2}T^{2p^{a}}\pmod{(\pi,\,T^{3p^{a}})}.

First consider the case when
\ell\nmid N
. Here
\epsilon_{N}(\ell)=1
, so substituting the above expansions into
h_{\ell}(\psi,T)
and working modulo
(\pi,T^{3p^{a}})
gives

(3.4)

\displaystyle h_{\ell}(\psi,T)

\displaystyle\equiv a_{\ell}-\bigl(\psi(\ell)+\ell^{k-2}\psi(\ell)^{-1}\bigr)

(3.5)

\displaystyle\qquad-\bigl(\ell^{k-2}\psi(\ell)^{-1}-\psi(\ell)\bigr)u\,T^{p^{a}}

\displaystyle\qquad-\tfrac{1}{2}\bigl(\bigl(\psi(\ell)+\ell^{k-2}\psi(\ell)^{-1}\bigr)u^{2}-\bigl(\psi(\ell)-\ell^{k-2}\psi(\ell)^{-1}\bigr)u)\bigr)T^{2p^{a}}\pmod{(\pi,T^{3p^{a}})}.

Set

\displaystyle A:=

\displaystyle a_{\ell}-\bigl(\psi(\ell)+\ell^{k-2}\psi(\ell)^{-1}\bigr),\qquad B:=\ell^{k-2}\psi(\ell)^{-1}-\psi(\ell),\qquad

\displaystyle C:=

\displaystyle\bigl(\psi(\ell)+\ell^{k-2}\psi(\ell)^{-1}\bigr)u^{2}-\bigl(\psi(\ell)-\ell^{k-2}\psi(\ell)^{-1}\bigr)u.

Now reduce the coefficients modulo
\pi
. The first nonzero term of
h_{\ell}(\psi,T)
modulo
\pi
occurs in one of the following degrees:

•

If
A\not\equiv 0\pmod{\pi}
then the constant term is nonzero modulo
\pi
, so the first nonzero term has degree
0
. Thus the
\mu
-invariant and
\lambda
-invariant of
h_{\ell}(\psi,T)
are both
0
. On the other hand, if
A\not\equiv 0\pmod{\pi}
, then
g_{\psi,n}(\ell)=0
according to Definition
3.2
(i). Thus, the formulae (
3.3
) are satisfied.

•

If
A\equiv 0\pmod{\pi}
but
B\not\equiv 0\pmod{\pi}
then the term with smallest degree is
-Bu\,T^{p^{a}}
, so

\lambda\left(h_{\ell}(\psi,T)\right)=p^{a}=g_{\mathbb{Q}_{(n)}}(\ell).

The requirement
A\equiv 0\mod{\pi}
is equivalent to the condition

a_{\ell}\equiv\psi(\ell)+\ell^{k-2}\psi(\ell)^{-1}\pmod{\pi}.

On the other hand
B\equiv 0\pmod{\pi}
if and only if
\psi(\ell)\equiv\pm\ell^{\frac{k-2}{2}}\pmod{\pi}
. Therefore in particular,
B\equiv 0\pmod{\pi}
implies that
a_{\ell}\equiv\pm 2\ell^{\frac{k-2}{2}}\pmod{\pi}
. Hence if
A\equiv 0\pmod{\pi}
and
B\equiv 0\pmod{\pi}
then

a_{\ell}\equiv\psi(\ell)+\ell^{k-2}\psi(\ell)^{-1}\pmod{\pi}

and

a_{\ell}\not\equiv\pm 2\ell^{\frac{k-2}{2}}\pmod{\pi}.

Thus,
g_{\psi,n}(\ell)=g_{\mathbb{Q}_{(n)}}(\ell)
in Definition
3.2
(i) and it follows that the conditions (
3.3
) are satisfied.

•

If
A\equiv 0\equiv B\pmod{\pi}
then
\psi(\ell)^{2}\equiv\ell^{k-2}\pmod{\pi}
, and
a_{\ell}\equiv\pm 2\ell^{\frac{k-2}{2}}
since
p\nmid 2\ell
we have
C\equiv\pm 2\ell^{\frac{k-2}{2}}\not\equiv 0\pmod{\pi}
. The term
-\tfrac{1}{2}CT^{2p^{a}}

is therefore the first nonzero term in this case. Thus, the first non-zero term has degree
2p^{a}=2g_{\mathbb{Q}_{(n)}}(\ell)
, matching
g_{\psi,n}(\ell)=2g_{\mathbb{Q}_{(n)}}(\ell)
in Definition
3.2
(i).

Next we assume that
\ell\mid N
and thus,
\epsilon_{N}(\ell)=0
. Working as above modulo
(p,T^{p^{a}+1})
we have

h_{\ell}(\psi,T)\equiv a_{\ell}-\psi(\ell)(1+T)^{up^{a}}\equiv(a_{\ell}-\psi(\ell))+\psi(\ell)u\,T^{p^{a}}\pmod{(p,T^{p^{a}+1})}.

Reducing coefficients modulo
\pi
yields:

•

If
a_{\ell}\not\equiv\psi(\ell)\pmod{\pi}
then the constant term is nonzero modulo
\pi
, so the first nonzero term has degree
0
, corresponding to
g_{\psi,n}(\ell)=0
in Definition
3.2
(ii).

•

If
a_{\ell}\equiv\psi(\ell)\pmod{\pi}
then the first nonzero term is
\psi(\ell)u\,T^{p^{a}}
, of degree
p^{a}=g_{\mathbb{Q}_{(n)}}(\ell)
, corresponding to
g_{\psi,n}(\ell)=g_{\mathbb{Q}_{(n)}}(\ell)
in Definition
3.2
(ii).

This completes the proof.
∎

We have the following proposition for the case of elliptic curves defined over
\mathbb{Q}
.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
K/\mathbb{Q}
be an abelian extension and assume that
E_{/K}
satisfies the condition
(Add)
defined above. Assume that all characters
\chi\in\widehat{\operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q})}
take values in
\mathcal{O}^{\times}
. Let
\ell
be a prime in
\mathbb{Q}
and
v
a prime of
K
lying above
\ell
. Assume that
p\nmid[K:\mathbb{Q}]
and
\ell\equiv 1\pmod{p}
. Then,

\displaystyle\sum_{\chi\in\widehat{\operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q})}}g_{\chi,n}(\ell)=\begin{cases}2g_{K_{(n)}}(\ell)&\text{ if $E/K$ has good reduction at $v$ and $E(K_{v})[p]\neq 0$,}\\
g_{K_{(n)}}(\ell)&\text{ if $E/K$ has split multiplicative reduction at $v$,}\\
0&\text{ otherwise.}\end{cases}

Proof.

For each
\chi\in\widehat{\operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q})}
, write

\chi(\ell)\bmod\pi=\zeta_{\chi}\in\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{\times}.

Since
p\nmid[K:\mathbb{Q}]
, the order of
\zeta_{\chi}
coincides with that of
\chi(\ell)
. We treat separately the cases
\ell\nmid N
and
\ell\mid N
. Note that
\ell\neq p
since it has been assumed in particular that
\ell\equiv 1\pmod{p}
.

First assume that
\ell\nmid N
. In this case
E
has good reduction at
\ell
, hence at every
v\mid\ell
of
K
. By the definition of
g_{\chi,n}(\ell)
, the quantity
g_{\chi,n}(\ell)
is nonzero precisely when the congruence

(3.6)

a_{\ell}\equiv\chi(\ell)+\chi(\ell)^{-1}\pmod{\pi}

holds, and equals
g_{\mathbb{Q}_{(n)}}(\ell)
except in the case
a_{\ell}\equiv\pm 2\pmod{\pi}
, in which case it equals
2g_{\mathbb{Q}_{(n)}}(\ell)
. Since
\ell\equiv 1\pmod{p}
, the above congruence (
3.6
) becomes

a_{\ell}\equiv\zeta_{\chi}+\zeta_{\chi}^{-1}\pmod{\pi}.

Let
\alpha\in\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{\times}
be one of the two roots of the polynomial

x^{2}-a_{\ell}x+1\pmod{p}.

The other root is
\alpha^{-1}
. The congruence
a_{\ell}\equiv\zeta_{\chi}+\zeta_{\chi}^{-1}\pmod{p}
holds if and only if
\zeta_{\chi}=\alpha
or
\zeta_{\chi}=\alpha^{-1}
. Therefore

\sum_{\chi}g_{\chi,n}(\ell)=g_{\mathbb{Q}_{(n)}}(\ell)\bigl(\,|\{\chi:\zeta_{\chi}=\alpha\}|+|\{\chi:\zeta_{\chi}=\alpha^{-1}\}|\,\bigr),

with the understanding that if
a_{\ell}\equiv\pm 2\pmod{p}
then each contributing term equals
2g_{\mathbb{Q}_{(n)}}(\ell)
, but the counting argument is identical.

The quantity
|\{\chi:\zeta_{\chi}=\alpha\}|
is the number of characters
\chi
of
\operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q})
satisfying
\chi(\ell)\equiv\alpha\pmod{\pi}
. A standard result (see
[
29
, Theorem 3.7]
) states that if
f
is the inertial degree of
\ell
in
K/\mathbb{Q}
, then

|\{\chi:\zeta_{\chi}=\alpha\}|=\begin{cases}g_{K}(\ell)&\text{if }\alpha^{f}=1,\\[4.0pt]
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

The same statement holds for
\alpha^{-1}
, since
\alpha^{-1\,f}=1
if and only if
\alpha^{f}=1
.

Hence

\sum_{\chi}g_{\chi,n}(\ell)=\begin{cases}2g_{\mathbb{Q}_{(n)}}(\ell)g_{K}(\ell)&\text{if }\alpha^{f}=1,\\[4.0pt]
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Since
p\nmid[K:\mathbb{Q}]
, we have
g_{K_{(n)}}(\ell)=g_{\mathbb{Q}_{(n)}}(\ell)g_{K}(\ell)
, yielding

\sum_{\chi}g_{\chi,n}(\ell)=\begin{cases}2g_{K_{(n)}}(\ell)&\text{if }\alpha^{f}=1,\\
0&\text{if }\alpha^{f}\neq 1.\end{cases}

Finally, good reduction of
E/K
at
v
together with the condition
E(K_{v})[p]\neq 0
is equivalent to the condition
\alpha^{f}=1
; see
[
16
, Lemma 3.4]
.

Next, assume that
\ell\mid N
. In this setting the definition of
g_{\chi,n}(\ell)
involves only the congruence

a_{\ell}\equiv\chi(\ell)\pmod{\pi}.

Put
\alpha:=a_{\ell}\bmod p\in\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
. Then
g_{\chi,n}(\ell)=g_{\mathbb{Q}_{(n)}}(\ell)
if and only if
\zeta_{\chi}=\alpha
and equals
0
otherwise. By the same character-counting result,

|\{\chi:\zeta_{\chi}=\alpha\}|=\begin{cases}g_{K}(\ell)&\text{if }\alpha^{f}=1,\\
0&\text{if }\alpha^{f}\neq 1.\end{cases}

Thus

\sum_{\chi}g_{\chi,n}(\ell)=\begin{cases}g_{\mathbb{Q}_{(n)}}(\ell)\,g_{K}(\ell)=g_{K_{(n)}}(\ell)&\text{if }\alpha^{f}=1,\\[4.0pt]
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

It remains to interpret the condition
\alpha^{f}=1
in terms of the reduction type of
E/K
at
v
.
If
E
has split multiplicative reduction at
\ell
, then
a_{\ell}=1
, so
\alpha=1
and therefore
\alpha^{f}=1
for all
f
. In this case
E/K
has split multiplicative reduction at
v
. If
E
has non-split multiplicative reduction at
\ell
, then
a_{\ell}=-1
, so
\alpha=-1
; hence
\alpha^{f}=(-1)^{f}
, which equals
1
if and only if
f
is even. But
E
becomes split multiplicative over the unramified quadratic extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}
, which implies that
f
is even exactly when
E/K
has split multiplicative reduction at
v
.

Finally, if
E
has additive reduction at
\ell
, then
a_{\ell}\equiv 0\pmod{p}
, so
\alpha=0
; and since
E
has additive reduction at all primes of
K
above
\ell
by assumption
(Add)
, we have
g_{\chi,n}(\ell)=0
for all
\chi
by the definition. Thus the sum is
0
, which matches the statement of the proposition.

Putting these cases together completes the proof.
∎

Proposition 3.5

(Generalization to cuspidal eigenforms)
.

Let
K/\mathbb{Q}
be an abelian extension, let
\ell
be a rational prime, and let
f
denote the residue degree of
\ell
in
K/\mathbb{Q}
. Let

h(q)=\sum_{n\geq 1}a_{n}(h)q^{n}\in S_{k}(\Gamma_{0}(N))

be a cuspidal normalized eigenform of weight
k\geq 2
with nebentypus
\beta
. Assume
p\nmid[K:\mathbb{Q}]
and
\ell\equiv 1\pmod{p}
. Then

\sum_{\chi\in\widehat{\operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q})}}g_{\chi,n}(\ell)=\begin{cases}2\,g_{K_{(n)}}(\ell)&\text{if }\ell\nmid N\text{ and }\alpha^{f}=1,\\[4.0pt]
g_{K_{(n)}}(\ell)&\text{if }\ell\mid N\text{ and }\alpha^{f}=1,\\[4.0pt]
0&\text{otherwise,}\end{cases}

where
\alpha\in\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
is any root of

x^{2}-a_{\ell}(h)\,x+\beta(\ell)\quad\pmod{p}.

Proof.

If
\ell\nmid N
the local Euler factor of

is

1-a_{\ell}(h)X+\ell^{\,k-1}\beta(\ell)X^{2},

which reduces modulo
p
(using
\ell\equiv 1\pmod{p}
) to

1-a_{\ell}(h)X+\beta(\ell)X^{2}.

Let
\alpha
be a root of
x^{2}-a_{\ell}(h)x+\beta(\ell)
in
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
; the other root equals
\beta(\ell)\alpha^{-1}
. By definition
g_{\chi,n}(\ell)
is nonzero exactly when

a_{\ell}(h)\equiv\chi(\ell)+\ell^{k-1}\beta(\ell)\chi(\ell)^{-1}\pmod{p},

which (since
\ell\equiv 1\pmod{p}
) is equivalent to

a_{\ell}(h)\equiv\zeta_{\chi}+\beta(\ell)\zeta_{\chi}^{-1}\pmod{p},

where
\zeta_{\chi}:=\chi(\ell)\bmod\pi
. Thus
g_{\chi,n}(\ell)\neq 0
precisely when
\zeta_{\chi}\in\{\alpha,\beta(\ell)\alpha^{-1}\}
, and the contribution of each such
\chi
equals
g_{\mathbb{Q}_{(n)}}(\ell)
(with the usual doubling in the exceptional supersingular congruence).

Because
\beta(\ell)\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}
is a fixed scalar, multiplication by
\beta(\ell)^{-1}
gives a bijection on the character values at
\ell
:

\chi(\ell)\mapsto\beta(\ell)^{-1}\chi(\ell)
. Hence counting characters with
\chi(\ell)=\beta(\ell)\alpha^{-1}
is equivalent to counting those with value
\alpha^{-1}
; the counts are unchanged. By the character-counting result for abelian extensions (see
[
29
, Th. 3.7]
), if
f
is the residue degree of
\ell
in
K/\mathbb{Q}
then

|\{\chi:\zeta_{\chi}=\alpha\}|=\begin{cases}g_{K}(\ell)&\text{if }\alpha^{f}=1,\\
0&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

and similarly for the other root. Using
p\nmid[K:\mathbb{Q}]
so that
g_{K_{(n)}}(\ell)=g_{\mathbb{Q}_{(n)}}(\ell)g_{K}(\ell)
, we obtain the first displayed case.

If
\ell\mid N
the defining congruence for a nonzero
g_{\chi,n}(\ell)
is simply
a_{\ell}(h)\equiv\chi(\ell)\pmod{\pi}
; put
\alpha=a_{\ell}(h)\bmod p
. The same character-counting gives the second displayed case, and all remaining cases give zero by definition. This completes the proof.
∎

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathrm{ord}_{p}(\Delta_{E})(p-1)}{12}p^{n-1}+\lambda(L_{p}^{\operatorname{Del}}(E,\psi,T))

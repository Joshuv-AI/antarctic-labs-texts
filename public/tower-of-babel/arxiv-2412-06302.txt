Triple 𝒁 ′ Z^{\prime} signatures at 𝒁 Z factories
Takaaki Nomura, Kei Yagyu

Abstract
We discuss triple Z ′ Z^{\prime} boson signatures via the decay chain of Z → Z ′ ​ ϕ → Z ′ ​ Z ′ ​ Z ′ Z\to Z^{\prime}\phi\to Z^{\prime}Z^{\prime}Z^{\prime} , with a new light scalar ϕ \phi , at future Z factories such as CEPC and FCC-ee. These new bosons ϕ \phi and Z ′ Z^{\prime} naturally appear in models with a new U ⁡ ( 1 ) U(1) gauge symmetry which is spontaneously broken and introduced in various new physics scenarios. The branching ratio of Z → Z ′ ​ ϕ → Z ′ ​ Z ′ ​ Z ′ Z\to Z^{\prime}\phi\to Z^{\prime}Z^{\prime}Z^{\prime} can be larger than 10 − 12 10^{-12} , which gives O ⁡ ( 1 ) O(1) events at Tera-Z experiments, when a product of g X g_{X} (new gauge coupling) and ζ \zeta ( Z Z - Z ′ Z^{\prime} mixing) is larger than around 10 − 6 10^{-6} . We find that the search for Z → Z ′ ​ Z ′ ​ Z ′ Z\to Z^{\prime}Z^{\prime}Z^{\prime} can significantly improve the current bound on a kinetic mixing parameter ϵ \epsilon in the dark photon case, where e ​ ϵ ≳ 𝒪 ⁡ ( 10 − 5 ) e\epsilon\gtrsim\mathcal{O}(10^{-5}) with g X = 𝒪 ⁡ ( 1 ) g_{X}={\cal O}(1) can be explored at Tera-Z experiments. We also show that a sufficiently large number of events with multi-lepton plus hadronic jets can be obtained in benchmark points, which cannot be realized by the usual decay of Z in the standard model.

Introduction

The gauge principle has successfully described fundamental forces, e.g., strong force, weak force, electromagnetic force and probably gravity as well
[
1
]
. A natural question that follows is, ’Is there a fifth force in nature?’.

A new
U(1)
gauge symmetry, which we denote as
U(1)_{X}
, is quite often introduced in various physics motivations, which deduces the fifth force in a simple way. For instance, grand unified theories, such as
E_{6}
and
SO(10)
models, predict
U(1)
gauge symmetries in a low-energy effective theory
[
2
]
.
In addition, the
U(1)_{X}
symmetry naturally introduces right-handed neutrinos because of the anomaly-free condition
[
3
]
. Furthermore, a spontaneous breakdown of the
U(1)_{X}
symmetry can be origin of tiny neutrino masses, stability of dark matter and baryon asymmetry of the Universe, see e.g.,
[
4
]
.
Therefore, testing the fifth force is quite important to narrow down new physics scenarios.

As a common feature, a
Z^{\prime}
boson, i.e., a new neutral gauge boson, appears in models with
U(1)_{X}
, so that the detection of
Z^{\prime}
turns out to be direct test of such a scenario.
Searches for
Z^{\prime}
have been performed in various experiments, and so far no clear evidence of the discovery of
Z^{\prime}
has been reported, by which
the mass
m_{Z^{\prime}}
and the new gauge coupling
g_{X}
(or the kinetic mixing parameter
\epsilon
especially for the dark photon case) have been constrained.
In particular, the range of
m_{Z^{\prime}}\lesssim 100
MeV has been highly constrained to be
|\epsilon|\lesssim 10^{-9}
by the combination of the beam dump experiments
[
5
,

[

in the dark photon model, except for the region
10^{-5}\lesssim|\epsilon|\lesssim 10^{-3}
and
10~\text{MeV}\lesssim m_{Z^{\prime}}\lesssim 100
MeV.
On the other hand, the region with
m_{Z^{\prime}}\gtrsim
100 MeV is less constrained to be

|\epsilon|\lesssim

10^{-3}
-
10^{-4}
.
See Ref.
[
13

Similar constraints on

Another common feature is the appearance of a new Higgs boson

Because both the masses of

Thus, for

In this Letter, we propose the possibility of exploring the region weakly constrained by the current experiments, i.e.,

Model

We recapitulate a model with an extra
U(1)_{X}
gauge symmetry in the minimal extension of field contents without introducing any exotic fermions
1
1

1

Right-handed neutrinos are required to cancel the gauge anomaly associated with
U(1)_{X}
, and to generate active neutrino masses depending on the assignment of the
U(1)_{X}
charges. In the following analysis, right-handed neutrinos are not relevant.
.
The scalar sector is then composed of the isospin doublet Higgs field
H
and an additional complex scalar field
\Phi
which is singlet under the SM gauge symmetry, and carries the
U(1)_{X}
charge
\tilde{X}_{\Phi}
.
2
2

2

The anomaly-free condition restricts the number of free
U(1)_{X}
charges for all the fields in the
U(1)_{X}
model to be 2 (3) if we (do not) introduce the Majorana mass term via the spontaneous breaking of
U(1)_{X}
and flavor universal charge assignments.

First, we consider the Higgs potential. The most general form is given by

\displaystyle V=

\displaystyle-\mu_{H}^{2}|H|^{2}-\mu_{\Phi}^{2}|\Phi|^{2}+\frac{\lambda_{H}}{2}|H|^{4}

\displaystyle+\frac{\lambda_{\Phi}}{2}|\Phi|^{4}+\lambda_{H\Phi}|H|^{2}|\Phi|^{2}.

(1)

The mass squared parameters
\mu_{H}^{2}
and
\mu_{\Phi}^{2}
can be eliminated by solving the tadpole conditions for
H
and
\Phi
assuming nonzero VEVs of the Higgs fields
\langle H\rangle=v/\sqrt{2}
and
\langle\Phi\rangle=v_{\Phi}/\sqrt{2}
.
The CP-even components
\tilde{h}
and
\tilde{\phi}
from
H
and
\Phi
, respectively, are mixed via the
\lambda_{H\Phi}
term.
Their mass eigenstates are defined as

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}\tilde{h}\\
\tilde{\phi}\end{pmatrix}=R(\alpha)\begin{pmatrix}h\\
\phi\end{pmatrix},\ R(\theta)\equiv\begin{pmatrix}\cos\theta&-\sin\theta\\
\sin\theta&\cos\theta\end{pmatrix}

(2)

with the mixing angle
\alpha
given by

\displaystyle\tan 2\alpha=\frac{2\lambda_{H\Phi}vv_{\Phi}}{\lambda_{H}v^{2}-\lambda_{\Phi}v_{\Phi}^{2}}.

(3)

The scalar bosons
h
and
\phi
are identified as the discovered Higgs boson with the mass
m_{h}(\simeq 125~\text{GeV})

and the additional one with the mass
m_{\phi}
, respectively.

Next, we discuss the gauge sector of our model. In general, we have a kinetic mixing between
U(1)_{Y}
and
U(1)_{X}
gauge fields written as

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}\supset

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{4}B_{\mu\nu}B^{\mu\nu}-\frac{1}{4}X_{\mu\nu}X^{\mu\nu}-\frac{\sin\epsilon^{\prime}}{2}B_{\mu\nu}X^{\mu\nu},

(4)

where
X_{\mu\nu}
and
B_{\mu\nu}
are
the field strength tensors for the
U(1)_{X}
gauge field
X_{\mu}
and the
U(1)_{Y}
gauge field
B_{\mu}
, respectively.
The kinetic term can be diagonalized applying the following transformation
[
30
]
;

\left(\begin{array}[]{c}X_{\mu}\\
B_{\mu}\\
\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}{\rm sec}\,\epsilon^{\prime}&0\\
-\tan\epsilon^{\prime}&1\\
\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\tilde{Z}^{\prime}_{\mu}\\
\tilde{B}_{\mu}\\
\end{array}\right).

(5)

The transformed field
\tilde{B}_{\mu}
is further written by
\tilde{B}_{\mu}=c_{W}A_{\mu}-s_{W}\tilde{Z}_{\mu}
(
c_{W}=\cos\theta_{W}
,
s_{W}=\sin\theta_{W}
), where
A_{\mu}
is the photon field,
\tilde{Z}_{\mu}
is the neutral gauge field and
\theta_{W}
is the Weinberg angle. Two gauge fields
\tilde{Z}_{\mu}
and
\tilde{Z}_{\mu}^{\prime}
are mixed with each other after the spontaneous breakdown of the electroweak symmetry.

Now, the covariant derivative for a generic field
\Psi
is expressed as

\displaystyle D_{\mu}\Psi\supset

\displaystyle\left[\partial_{\mu}-ig_{Z}(T_{\Psi}^{3}-s_{W}^{2}Q_{\Psi})\tilde{Z}_{\mu}-ig_{X}X_{\Psi}\tilde{Z}_{\mu}^{\prime}\right]\Psi,

(6)

where
g_{X}
is the
U(1)_{X}
gauge coupling,
g_{Z}=g/c_{W}
with
g
being the
SU(2)_{L}
gauge coupling, and
Q_{\Psi}
(
T_{\Psi}^{3}
) is the electric charge (the third component of the isospin).
The charge
X_{\Psi}
is defined as

\displaystyle X_{\Psi}

\displaystyle=\tilde{X}_{\Psi}-Y_{\Psi}\frac{g}{g_{X}}\tan\theta_{W}\tan\epsilon^{\prime},

(7)

with
\tilde{X}_{\Psi}
and
Y_{\Psi}
being the
U(1)_{X}
charge and the hypercharge for
\Psi
, respectively.
The masses of the neutral gauge bosons
\tilde{Z}_{\mu}
and
\tilde{Z}_{\mu}^{\prime}
are given by the covariant derivatives of the Higgs fields
H
and
\Phi
by substituting their VEVs. We obtain the mass matrix in the basis of
(\tilde{Z}_{\mu},\tilde{Z}_{\mu}^{\prime})
as

\displaystyle{\cal M}=\frac{1}{4}\begin{pmatrix}g_{Z}^{2}v^{2}&-2g_{Z}g_{X}X_{H}v^{2}\\
-2g_{Z}g_{X}X_{H}v^{2}&4g_{X}^{2}(X_{H}^{2}v^{2}+X_{\Phi}^{2}v_{\Phi}^{2})\end{pmatrix}.

(8)

This can be diagonalized by introducing the orthogonal transformation as

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}\tilde{Z}_{\mu}\\
\tilde{Z}^{\prime}_{\mu}\end{pmatrix}=R(\zeta)\begin{pmatrix}Z_{\mu}\\
Z^{\prime}_{\mu}\end{pmatrix},~~\sin 2\zeta=\frac{g_{Z}g_{X}X_{H}v^{2}}{m^{2}_{Z^{\prime}}-m^{2}_{Z}},

(9)

where
m_{Z}
and
m_{Z^{\prime}}
are the mass eigenvalues of
Z
and
Z^{\prime}
, respectively, and
Z
is identified as the discovered
Z
boson.
The magnitude of
g_{X}X_{H}
has been severely constrained by experiments, so that we can express physical quantities by a perturbation of
g_{X}X_{H}
. Under
|g_{X}X_{H}|\ll 1
, we can approximate

\displaystyle m_{Z}

\displaystyle=\frac{g_{Z}v}{2}+{\cal O}(g_{X}^{2}X_{H}^{2}),

(10)

\displaystyle m_{Z^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=g_{X}X_{\Phi}v_{\Phi}+{\cal O}(g_{X}^{2}X_{H}^{2}),

(11)

\displaystyle\zeta

\displaystyle=\frac{m_{Z}v}{m_{Z^{\prime}}^{2}-m_{Z}^{2}}g_{X}X_{H}+{\cal O}(g_{X}^{3}X_{H}^{3}).

(12)

In the dark photon limit (
\tilde{X}_{\Psi}\to 0
,
\Psi\neq\Phi
) with
m_{Z^{\prime}}/m_{Z}\ll 1
, we obtain

\displaystyle\zeta\to\epsilon^{\prime}s_{W}+{\cal O}(\epsilon^{\prime 3}).

(13)

We can also extract the Scalar-Gauge-Gauge type interactions defined as

\displaystyle{\cal L}_{\rm int}

\displaystyle=(Z_{\mu},Z_{\mu}^{\prime})\begin{pmatrix}\Lambda_{ZZ}&\Lambda_{ZZ^{\prime}}\\
\Lambda_{ZZ^{\prime}}&\Lambda_{Z^{\prime}Z^{\prime}}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}Z^{\mu}\\
Z^{\prime\mu}\end{pmatrix},

(14)

where

\displaystyle\Lambda_{ZZ}

\displaystyle=\frac{m_{Z}^{2}}{v}\tilde{h}+m_{Z^{\prime}}^{2}\left(\frac{\tilde{\phi}}{v_{\Phi}}-\frac{\tilde{h}}{v}\right)\zeta^{2}+{\cal O}(\zeta^{4}),

(15)

\displaystyle\Lambda_{Z^{\prime}Z^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\frac{m_{Z^{\prime}}^{2}}{v_{\Phi}}\tilde{\phi}-\frac{m_{Z^{\prime}}^{4}}{m_{Z}^{2}}\left(\frac{\tilde{\phi}}{v_{\Phi}}-\frac{\tilde{h}}{v}\right)\zeta^{2}+{\cal O}(\zeta^{4}),

(16)

\displaystyle\Lambda_{ZZ^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=m_{Z^{\prime}}^{2}\left(\frac{\tilde{\phi}}{v_{\Phi}}-\frac{\tilde{h}}{v}\right)\zeta+{\cal O}(\zeta^{3}).

(17)

In the above expression, if we replace
\tilde{h}\to v
and
\tilde{\phi}\to v_{\Phi}
, then
\Lambda_{ZZ}
(
\Lambda_{Z^{\prime}Z^{\prime}}
) becomes the mass of
Z
(
Z^{\prime}
), and
\Lambda_{ZZ^{\prime}}
vanishes as we expect.

There are five free parameters in our scenario, which can be expressed as

\displaystyle\{g_{X},~\epsilon^{\prime},~m_{\phi},~v_{\Phi},~\sin\alpha\}.

(18)

Instead of
\{v_{\Phi},\epsilon^{\prime}\}
, we can choose
\{m_{Z^{\prime}},\zeta\}
via Eqs. (

) and (

3
Z\to Z^{\prime}\phi
decay process

The decay width of the
Z\to Z^{\prime}\phi
mode is calculated
from the
ZZ^{\prime}\phi
coupling which is obtained by substituting
\tilde{\phi}\to\cos\alpha
and
\tilde{h}\to-\sin\alpha
in Eq. (
17
).
The decay width is then given by

\displaystyle\Gamma(Z\to Z^{\prime}\phi)=\frac{m_{Z}^{3}}{48\pi}\left(\sin\alpha+\frac{v}{v_{\Phi}}\cos\alpha\right)^{2}x_{Z^{\prime}}\zeta^{2}

\displaystyle~~~\times\lambda^{1/2}(x_{Z^{\prime}},x_{\phi})\left[(1+x_{Z^{\prime}}-x_{\phi})^{2}+8x_{Z^{\prime}}\right],

(19)

where
x_{Z^{\prime}}=m_{Z^{\prime}}^{2}/m_{Z}^{2}
,
x_{\phi}=m_{\phi}^{2}/m_{Z}^{2}
and
\lambda(x,y)=1+x^{2}+y^{2}-2xy-2x-2y
.
For
x_{Z^{\prime}}\ll 1
and
x_{\phi}\ll 1
, the decay width can be simplified to be

\displaystyle\Gamma(Z\to Z^{\prime}\phi)

\displaystyle\sim\frac{m_{Z}^{3}}{48\pi v^{2}}\left(\sin\alpha+\frac{v}{v_{\Phi}}\cos\alpha\right)^{2}x_{Z^{\prime}}\zeta^{2}.

(20)

The branching ratio (BR) of
Z\to Z^{\prime}\phi
is given by
\text{BR}(Z\to Z^{\prime}\phi)=\Gamma(Z\to Z^{\prime}\phi)/\Gamma_{Z}
where
\Gamma_{Z}
is the total decay width of
Z
.
When the contribution from the new decay channel (
20
) to
\Gamma_{Z}
is negligibly small, we can use the observed value
\Gamma_{Z}\sim 2.50
GeV
[
31
]
.
The BR is then estimated as

\displaystyle\text{BR}(Z\to Z^{\prime}\phi)

\displaystyle\sim 0.033\times\left(\sin\alpha+\frac{v}{v_{\Phi}}\cos\alpha\right)^{2}x_{Z^{\prime}}\zeta^{2}.

(21)

If we take the non-mixing limit
\sin\alpha\to 0
,
the above expression becomes

\displaystyle\text{BR}(Z\to Z^{\prime}\phi)

\displaystyle\sim 0.24\times(g_{X}X_{\Phi}{}\zeta)^{2},

(22)

in which the
m_{Z^{\prime}}
dependence disappears if we neglect
{\cal O}(x_{Z^{\prime}})
corrections.

We focus on the SM backgrounds for the “6 lepton” final states which mainly come from

\displaystyle\sigma(e^{+}e^{-}\to 3\times(\ell^{+}\ell^{-}))\simeq 2.3\times 10^{-1}\ {\rm fb},

\displaystyle\sigma(e^{+}e^{-}\to 2\times(\ell^{+}\ell^{-})+\text{2 jets})\simeq 3.1\ {\rm fb}.

5
Conclusion

We have discussed the
Z\to Z^{\prime}\phi\to Z^{\prime}Z^{\prime}Z^{\prime}
process in models with a spontaneously broken
U(1)_{X}
gauge symmetry that contains a new neutral gauge boson
Z^{\prime}
and a scalar boson
\phi
.
Such a new decay channel of the
Z
boson can be tested at future Z-factories, e.g., CEPC and FCC-ee, where
{\cal O}(10^{12})

Z
bosons are expected to be produced.
The branching ratio of
Z
into the triple
Z^{\prime}
state can be larger then
{\cal O}(10^{-12})
when
a new gauge coupling
g_{X}
is larger than about
10^{-4}
(
10^{-1}
) for the
Z
-
Z^{\prime}
mixing
\zeta\simeq 0.02
(
2\times 10^{-5}
) and the scalar mixing
\sin\alpha=0
. When SM fermions are charged under the
U(1)_{X}
symmetry, both
g_{X}
and
\zeta
have highly been constrained by the current experiments. On the other hand,
g_{X}
has not been severely constrained in the dark photon case, so that a sizable branching ratio of the
Z\to Z^{\prime}Z^{\prime}Z^{\prime}
is allowed.
Therefore, the dark photon scenario is the most promising one which can be explored at future Z-factories.

Focusing on the dark photon case, it has been found that detectable number of events is expected for
\epsilon e\gtrsim 10^{-6}
with
g_{X}=\mathcal{O}(1)
if the decay mode is kinematically allowed. In addition, our brief estimation of signal and background shows that
e\epsilon\gtrsim\mathcal{O}(10^{-5})
could be tested. Note also that even smaller values of
\epsilon
and/or
g_{X}

the process can be tested when
X_{\Phi}
is larger
[
53
]
.
We have also shown that a sufficiently large number of events for multi-lepton with or without hadronic jets
can be obtained in the benchmark points by considering some final states from the
Z^{\prime}
decay.
Such final states cannot be realized by the usual decay of the Z boson in the SM.
Therefore, Z-factories provide an excellent opportunity to explore the scenarios with the
U(1)_{X}
gauge symmetry.

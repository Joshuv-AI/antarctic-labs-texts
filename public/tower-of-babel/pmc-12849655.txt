Clinical outcomes and visual quality after implantation of extended depth of focus intraocular lenses with mini-monovision design
Authors: Yubing Huang, Min Han, Zhuoqing Hu, Xinyi Zhang, Haokun Qu, Zheming Wu, Yun Chen, Weitao Zheng, Lina Wu, Ruihong Ju
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundTo compare the clinical outcomes and visual quality following bilateral implantation of Vivity IOLs with mini-monovision.MethodsPatients who underwent sequential bilateral cataract surgery with implantation of Vivity EDOF IOLs at our institution were divided into two groups: Group A was designed with a myopic refraction of ≥ 0.5 D and < 1.0 D in the non-dominant eye, while the refractive target for the dominant eye was emmetropia. Group B exhibited emmetropic refractive targets bilaterally. Outcome measures evaluated at 1 month postoperatively included: monocular uncorrected distance visual acuity (UDVA), uncorrected intermediate visual acuity (UIVA), uncorrected near visual acuity (UNVA), monocular objective visual quality, monocular and binocular uncorrected defocus curves, VF-14 score, Quality of Vision (QoV) score and patient satisfaction.ResultsThe study comprised a total of 61 patients (122 eyes). Statistically significant differences were not observed between the groups in terms of UDVA, UIVA, mean modulation transfer function (MTF), Strehl ratio (SR), aberrations, VF-14 scores, QoV scores and satisfaction. In Group A, the UNVA of the non-dominant eye was superior to that of Group B (0.36 ± 0.05 vs. 0.54 ± 0.06 LogMAR, P=0.024). The defocus curve of the non-dominant eye revealed that Group A attained superior visual performance at defocus levels ranging from − 2.0D to -3.5D (0.50 m to 0.29 m). However, these differences did not achieve statistical significance.ConclusionThe findings of this study indicate that bilateral implantation of EDOF IOLs with a mini-monovision design improves near visual acuity without significantly compromising distance visual acuity, visual quality or increasing aberrations.Keywords: Min-monovision, Extended depth-of-focus (EDOF), Visual quality, Defocus curve

Introduction

The prevalence of myopia increases in the elderly population [1], with myopic individuals exhibiting a greater demand for near-vision correction. Moreover, as the quality of life for older adults improves, the need for clear distance vision remains essential, while the demand for intermediate and near vision increases. At the same time, there is a growing expectation for high-quality, continuous visual acuity among the elderly.

Extended depth-of-focus (EDOF) intraocular lenses (IOLs) are designed to extend the depth of focus by concentrating incident light onto an elongated longitudinal plane, thereby expanding the range of clear vision [2]. The EDOF IOL AcrySof® IQ Vivity® (Alcon Laboratories, Inc.) employs X-WAVE™ wave front-shaping technology. The central 2.2 mm optical zone features two smooth surface transition elements that elongate and shift the wavefront, providing a continuous extended focal range to maintain patients’ visual acuity [3].

The vivity IOL provides distance and intermediate vision comparable to that of the trifocal intraocular lens, but its near vision remains suboptimal [4]. To enhance the near vision of patients undergoing bilateral EDOF IOL implantation, a strategy known as mini-monovision was applied: a specific near addition power is reserved in the non-dominant eye, while the dominant eye targets emmetropia, thereby establishing monovision [5]. However, several studies have demonstrated that monovision reduces contrast sensitivity, stereopsis, depth perception, and the ability to judge object velocity [6–8]. Anisometropia exceeding tolerance has also been demonstrated to induce patient discomfort, including asthenopia, dizziness, and headache [9]. Following a systematic review, Skov AG et al. [9] concluded that surgically induced anisometropia of less than 1.00 D (diopter) can enhance near vision without a clinically meaningful decline in distance acuity. Consequently, the mini-monovision strategy, which involves maintaining interocular refractive disparity of less than 1.00 D, has been widely adopted with both monofocal and EDOF intraocular lenses to increase spectacle independence [10].

Despite the prevalence of the monovision design approach in the application of EDOF IOL [11], there is currently limited research regarding the impact of monovision design with the Vivity intraocular lens on visual quality. Therefore, the objective of our study is to compare the clinical outcomes and visual quality following bilateral implantation of Vivity IOLs with monovision versus emmetropia.

Patients and methods

Participants

The inclusion criteria of the study were as follows: (1) Age ≥ 18 years; (2) Patients undergoing bilateral cataract phacoemulsification with Vivity IOL implantation; (3) Completion of at least two postoperative follow-up visits (scheduled at 1 day, 1 week, and 1 month postoperatively); (4)The degree of anisometropia is ≥ 0.50 D. Exclusion criteria were as follows: (1)Concurrent ocular pathologies (e.g., corneal pathology, diabetic retinopathy, amblyopia); (2) History of other ocular surgeries (e.g., vitreoretinal surgery); (3) Intraoperative or postoperative complications (e.g., macular edema, posterior capsular opacification). Patients were divided into two groups based on preoperative visual habits and preferences: Group A was designed with a near addition power of ≥ 0.5 D and < 1.0 D in the non-dominant eye, while the refractive target for the dominant eye was emmetropia. Group B exhibited emmetropic refractive targets bilaterally.

Ethical approval

The study was a prospective non-randomised comparative study. The study protocol was approved by the Human Research Ethics Committee of Guangzhou Aier Eye Hospital (ethics number: GZAIER-2025-IRB-04) and adhered to the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki. Written informed consent was obtained from each participant.

Sample size

The sample size was calculated using PASS software (version 23.0.2; NCSS, LLC, Kaysville, UT, USA). The means and standard deviations obtained in the pilot study were entered into the calculation to determine the required sample size. The alpha level and statistical power were set at 0.05 and 0.90, respectively. Finally, 12 cases were needed for each group. It is assumed that the loss rate of subjects is 20%. The final sample size is thus at least 15 cases in each group. However, to enhance the reliability of the study findings, 30 patients ultimately enrolled in Group A and 31 in Group B.

Preoperative examination

All patients underwent comprehensive preoperative routine examinations for cataract surgery, including uncorrected distance visual acuity (UDVA) at 5 m, intraocular pressure (IOP) measurement, objective refraction, slit-lamp biomicroscopy, fundus examination (using a preset lens), optical biometry (IOLMaster 700, Carl Zeiss Meditec AG), corneal topography (Pentacam AXL, Oculus Optikgerate GmbH), and visual quality assessment (i-Trace, Tracey Technologies). In addition to UDVA, the dysfunctional lens index (DLI) was measured and recorded for each patient preoperatively using the iTrace visual quality analyzer. The DLI reflects the degree of lens opacity, ranging from 0 to 10, with lower values indicating more severe opacity. The dominant eye was determined using Mile’s test, in which participants extend both arms and view a distant object through a triangular opening formed by their hands. They then close each eye in turn to determine which eye is fixating on the object — this is the dominant eye. The IOL power calculation was based on optical biometry using the IOLMaster 700 and the Barrett Universal II formula. For Group A, the target refraction was set at a near addition power of ≥ 0.5 D and < 1.0 D in the non-dominant eye, with the aim of achieving emmetropia in the dominant eye. For Group B, the target refraction was emmetropia in both eyes. The postoperative target of increased add was created by induction of post-operative myopia [12].

Surgical procedures

All surgeries were performed by the same experienced surgeon(Zheming Wu) using a standardized phacoemulsification technique. Patients received Tobradex eye drops (Yangtze River Pharmaceutical Group Co. Ltd., China) four times daily during the first week, followed by weekly tapering over one month to prevent inflammation and infection.

Outcome measure

The primary outcome measures included UCVA at 5m, uncorrected intermediate visual acuity (UIVA) at 60 cm, and uncorrected near visual acuity (UNVA) at 40 cm. Postoperative visual acuity assessments were conducted using the Standard Logarithmic Visual Acuity Chart (GB 11533 − 2011) and a near visual acuity chart (Carl Zeiss AG) at one month postoperatively. One month after surgery, patients underwent an objective visual quality examination, including uncorrected modulation transfer function (MTF) and uncorrected Strehl ratio (SR), as well as total ocular low-order and high-order aberrations (including spherical aberration, coma and trefoil). ‘Uncorrected’ refers to the absence of correction for defocus and astigmatism. Additionally, the mean uncorrected MTF values at spatial frequencies of 5, 10, 15, 20, 25 and 30 cycles per degree (c/d) were measured and denoted as MTF5–30. The examinations were performed under mesopic conditions with a scanning diameter of 3 mm. At the same time, defocus curves were measured for the non-dominant eye and both eyes using a Binoptometer 4P (Oculus, Germany) with defocus ranging from + 1.00 D to -3.50 D in 0.50 D increments.

One month after surgery, patients were asked to complete questionnaires including the VF-14 and the Quality of Vision (QoV) questionnaires. Satisfaction levels were also recorded. The VF-14 [13]questionnaire comprises 14 items related to vision-dependent daily activities, with a possible score ranging from 0 to 4 (over a total range of 0–56). Higher scores indicate a better quality of life related to vision. The QoV questionnaire [14]assesses several types of visual disturbance, including glare, halos, starbursts, hazy vision, blurred vision, distortion, and seeing multiple images. Each phenomenon is evaluated across three dimensions: Frequency (never-0, occasionally-1, quite often-3 and very often-4), Severity (not at all-0, mild-1, moderate-2, severe-3) and Bothersomeness (not at all-0, a little-1, quite-2, and very-3).The score of each phenomenon ranges from 0 to 9. Higher scores indicate greater severity of the visual disturbance. All questionnaires were distributed by a single examiner, who provided face-to-face assistance to ensure patients understood each question and option.

Statistical analysis

All visual acuities were converted to the logarithm of the minimum angle of resolution (logMAR) for analysis. All data were analysed using SPSS software (version 26.0, IBM Corp.). The normality test uses the Shapiro–Wilk test. Continuous variables that conform to a normal distribution are expressed as mean ± standard deviation (M ± SD), while those that do not are expressed as median and interquartile range(IQR). Due to baseline imbalances between the two groups, analysis of covariance (ANCOVA) was used to adjust for comparisons of mean values, and means are presented as estimated marginal means (EMMs). Categorical variables are expressed as frequency (percentage). Categorical data comparisons utilized the Chi-square test. A p-value of less than 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Discussion

Following the bilateral implantation of Vivity EDOF IOLs, near visual acuity was inferior compared to multifocal IOLs [4]. The mini-monovision design enhances near visual acuity in patients who have undergone bilateral implantation of EDOF IOLs. However, this design may potentially degrade visual quality. This study aims to investigate the efficacy of the mini-monovision design when used with the Vivity EDOF IOL, and to explore its impact on visual quality. It will provide additional information to support the clinical implementation of mini-monovision in bilateral cataract surgery combined with Vivity IOL implantation.

Analysis of the baseline data revealed that the mini-monovision group had longer axial lengths and greater negative SE values. This is because patients who were given a residual myopic target were mostly preoperatively myopic or had a higher degree of myopia. Postoperative analysis showed that the UNVA in the non-dominant eye was better in the mini-monovision group than in the emmetropia group (0.36 ± 0.05 LogMAR vs. 0.54 ± 0.06 LogMAR, p < 0.05), while UDVA and UIVA were comparable in both groups. These results suggest that providing a near addition of between − 0.50 and − 1.00 D in the non-dominant eye can enhance UNVA without significantly compromising UDVA or UIVA.

The defocus curve of the non-dominant eye in the mini-monovision group shifted to the right compared with the emmetropic group. The mini-monovision design enhances near visual acuity while slightly reducing distance visual acuity. This results in a longer plateau and a flatter defocus curve for the non-dominant eye in the mini-monovision group, as the reduction in distance vision is less than the improvement in near vision. The peak of the defocus curve occurs at -1.00 D for the mini-monovision group and at -0.50 D for the emmetropic group. This corresponds to the residual SE of each group (Group A: -0.86 ± 0.60 D; Group B: -0.65 ± 0.56 D). The binocular defocus curves of the two groups differed at defocus levels of -3.00 D to -3.50 D (0.33 m to 0.29 m), with the mini-monovision group demonstrating superior visual acuity. Near addition power in the non-dominant eye improves near vision, while the dominant eye compensates for the reduction in distance vision. This leads to substantial overlap of the binocular defocus curves at other defocus levels. Coassin M et al. [15] compared binocular defocus curves with and without correction in eyes that had been bilaterally implanted with Vivity IOLs using a mini-monovision design. They reported better visual acuity in the mini-monovision group at defocus levels between − 1.00 D and − 3.00 D, while UDVA was slightly inferior without statistical significance. Another study by Vasavada V et al. [12] demonstrated that bilateral Vivity implantation with a − 0.50 D target in the non-dominant eye provided superior visual acuity at defocus between − 2.00 D and − 3.00 D compared to an emmetropic target group. Additionally, the mean required near addition power for reading printed text at 40 cm was significantly reduced. Similarly, Gundersen KG et al. [16] established two non-dominant eye defocus levels of − 0.50 D and − 1.00 D, comparing them with bilateral emmetropia. Patients with near addition targets achieved significantly better visual acuity at defocus of − 2.00 D to − 3.00 D than emmetropic patients. Notably, 85% of patients with a − 1.00 D target achieved a visual range of 2.50 D, compared to just 38% of the emmetropic group. These results are consistent with those of our current study, which indicates that the mini-monovision design improves near vision without significantly compromising distance vision.

In our study of the mini-monovision group, the refractive power reserved for the non-dominant eye represents a range, which may have influenced the investigation into appropriate levels of reserved myopia. Previous studies have demonstrated that mini-monovision using the Vivity IOL with target refractions of − 0.50 D [12] or − 0.75 D [17] reliably improves near visual acuity without increasing subjective complaints. However, a target of − 0.75 D was associated with a clinically relevant reduction in corrected distance visual acuity. Consequently, a residual refractive error of − 0.50 D is currently recommended when implementing the mini-monovision strategy.

Low-order aberrations (LOAs) were higher in the mini-monovision group, but this difference was not statistically significant. The LOAs in the mini-monovision group originated from the reserved near-addition power. This indicates that reserving a low refractive power of between − 0.5D and − 1.0D before surgery did not result in a significant increase in lower-order aberrations within the mini-monovision group. There were no statistically significant differences in high-order aberrations (HOAs) or their components — spherical aberration, coma and trefoil — indicating that the mild near addition power did not significantly affect HOAs.

To make a more accurate comparison of objective visual quality under uncorrected conditions between the two groups, we measured the mean uncorrected MTF and uncorrected SR. MTF is defined as the ratio between the image contrast of a specific object through an optical imaging system and the contrast of the object itself [18], while SR represents the area under the MTF curve. Higher MTF and SR values indicate superior visual quality. The study revealed no statistically significant differences in mean uncorrected MTF or uncorrected SR between the two groups. Additionally, the MTF curve indicated no differences between the groups. Our study found that the MTF5 of Group A was lower than that of Group B on the MTF curve. The mini-monovision design reduced visual quality at low spatial frequencies, though this decrease was not significant. Visual quality at low spatial frequencies influences contour discrimination [19]. This suggests that reserved near addition power may reduce patients’ ability to discern object contours. MTF shows a strong positive correlation with contrast sensitivity, as it reflects the optical factors that affect contrast sensitivity [20, 21]. The low-order aberrations introduced by mini-monovision design reduce contrast sensitivity in patients, consequently leading to a decrease in MTF values [22].

There were no statistically significant differences in VF-14 and QoV questionnaire scores between groups, indicating that the mini-monovision design does not cause a significant increase in visual difficulties or disturbance for patients. In the satisfaction survey, all patients reported being satisfied with the surgical outcomes, indicating that the mini-monovision design has not led to any patient dissatisfaction. Other researchers [12, 23] also found no differences in satisfaction or incidence of visual disturbance between the mini-monovision and emmetropia groups in a study of the Vivity monovision design. The reduction in the patient’s subjective complaints may be attributed to the minimal increase in aberrations and deterioration of visual quality in the non-dominant eye, in conjunction with binocular summation.

This study has the following limitations: (1) Relying on patient preferences for group assignment in this non-randomized study design may reduce the generalizability and statistical power of the research; (2) As this is a single-center investigation with a short follow-up period, the generalisability of our results is limited, necessitating multicenter clinical studies and more extended follow-up periods in future; (3)This study lacks contrast sensitivity tests, so it cannot clarify whether the mini-monovision design affects contrast sensitivity. Future research could focus on this aspect.

Conclusion

In summary, the use of a mini-monovision design for bilateral implantation of EDOF IOLs improves near visual acuity without significantly compromising distance visual acuity, visual quality or increasing aberrations.

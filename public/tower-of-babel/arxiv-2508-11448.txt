Classification of irreducible Harish-Chandra modules over map full toroidal Lie algebras
Sudipta Mukherjee

Abstract
. A natural higher dimensional analogue of the affine-Virasoro algebra is the full toroidal Lie algebra. In this paper, we classify irreducible Harish-Chandra modules for map full toroidal Lie algebras. We show that every such module is either a cuspidal or a highest weight module. Furthermore, we prove that they turn out to be single point evaluation modules.



1.
Introduction:

The study of infinite-dimensional Lie algebras and their representations has played a central role in various areas of mathematics and mathematical physics, due to their wide-ranging applications in conformal field theory, string theory, and soliton theory. A widely explored class of representations consists of those that decompose into finite-dimensional weight spaces. Such representations are commonly referred to as Harish-Chandra modules in the literature, and their classification for various classes of infinite-dimensional Lie algebras has been the subject of extensive study.

Among the most commonly studied infinite-dimensional Lie algebras is the affine Kac-Moody algebra as it generalizes the theory of finite dimensional simple Lie algebras. Affine Lie algebras can be constructed as the universal central extension of the loop algebra associated with a finite dimensional simple Lie algebra, i.e., the Lie algebra of maps from the unit circle into a finite-dimensional simple Lie algebra.

Another fundamental infinite-dimensional Lie algebra is the Virasoro algebra, which plays an important role in theoretical physics. Virasoro Lie algebras can be realized as the central extension of the Lie algebra of derivations of the Laurent polynomial ring in one variable. The classification of all irreducible modules with finite dimensional weight spaces over the Virasoro algebra was conjectured by V. Kac
[
16
]
, and subsequently proved by O. Mathieu in 1992
[
21
]
.

The Virasoro algebra
\mathrm{Vir}
acts naturally on the derived algebra of an affine Kac-Moody algebra by derivation. Since they are closely related, it naturally suggests that they can be considered simultaneously, that is, as one algebraic structure. As a result, the so-called affine-Virasoro algebra naturally appears, which is the semidirect product of the Virasoro algebra and an affine Kac-Moody Lie algebra. The representation theory of affine-Virasoro algebras has attracted significant attention due to its connections with conformal field theory, number theory, and soliton theory. Affine-Virasoro algebras with a common center have been studied by many mathematicians
[
9
,
13
,
15
,
18
]
.

The higher dimensional analogue of the Virasoro algebra is the Witt algebra. Let
A_{n}
denote the Laurent polynomial ring in
n
variables. The Lie algebra of derivations of
A_{n}
, denoted by
\mathcal{W}_{n}
, is known as the Witt algebra. Unlike the one variable case,
\mathcal{W}_{n}
is centrally closed for
n\geq 2
. The Witt algebra, as a classical and well-studied example of an infinite-dimensional Lie algebra, has attracted substantial attention in the literature
[
5
,
12
,
10
,
24
]
. In
[
5
]
, Billig and Futorny classified all irreducible modules with finite dimensional weight spaces over
\mathcal{W}_{n}
.

Similarly, toroidal Lie algebras generalize affine Lie algebras to higher dimensions and are defined as the universal central extensions of the multiloop algebra of finite dimensional simple Lie algebras. In contrast to the affine case, where the center is one-dimensional, toroidal Lie algebras possess an infinite-dimensional center, making their representation theory significantly more intricate and interesting. Using representations of the toroidal algebras, one can construct hierarchies of non-linear PDEs
[
2
]
. The classification of irreducible integrable Harish-Chandra modules in this setting was carried out by Eswara Rao
[
25
]
.

Full toroidal Lie algebras are the natural multivariable generalizations of affine-Virasoro algebras. They are constructed as the semidirect product of the derived algebra of toroidal Lie algebra and Witt algebra. Due to the nontrivial action of
\mathcal{W}_{n}
on the center
\mathcal{K}
, the center of the full toroidal Lie algebra becomes finite-dimensional. Very recently, S. Pal provided a classification of all irreducible Harish-Chandra modules over full toroidal Lie algebras
[
22
]
.

In recent years, the representation theory of map algebras has emerged as an active and rapidly developing area of research. Let
B
be a finitely generated commutative associative unital algebra. For a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
, the tensor product
\mathfrak{g}(B):=\mathfrak{g}\otimes B
is called the map algebra associated with
\mathfrak{g}
. The map algebras corresponding to
B=\mathbb{C}[t,t^{-1}]
and
B=A_{n}
are called the loop algebra and multiloop algebra, respectively. For
B=\mathbb{C}[t,t^{-1}]
, Harish-Chandra modules over
\mathrm{Vir}(B)
were classified in
[
11
]
. This result was later generalized for any finitely generated commutative associative unital algebra
B
by A. Savage in
[
27
]
. Very recently, Eswara Rao et al. classified irreducible integrable Harish-Chandra modules for map affine-Virasoro algebras
[
26
]
.

Let
\uptau
denote the full toroidal Lie algebra, and let
B
be a finitely generated commutative associative unital algebra. In this paper, we study the irreducible representations of the map full toroidal Lie algebra
\uptau(B)
which has finite dimensional weight spaces. We show that all such modules are either cuspidal or highest weight modules. We also prove that in both cases they are single point evaluation modules (see Section 1 for definition). In other words, these modules are irreducible modules for
\uptau
. Our classification relies on a recent result by S. Pal
[
22
]
, who classified irreducible Harish-Chandra modules for
\uptau
. Using this, we establish our main theorem (
Theorem

5.1
), completing the classification in the more general setting of map full toroidal Lie algebras.

1.1.
Organization of the paper.

This paper aims to classify all irreducible Harish-Chandra modules over the map full toroidal Lie algebra.
Section 2 lays the foundation for the paper. We begin by recalling the definition and structure of the full toroidal Lie algebra
\uptau
, along with its map version
\uptau(B)
. We also discuss the representation theory of toroidal Lie algebras that form the basis of our classification results.

Section 3 is devoted to the classification of irreducible cuspidal Harish-Chandra modules over
\uptau(B)
. We begin by proving that all central operators in
\mathcal{K}(B)
act trivially on
V
(Theorem
3.1
). Subsequently, we establish the existence of a cofinite ideal
J
of
B
such that
\uptau(J)V=0
(Theorem
3.2
). Propositions
3.3
and
3.4
further refine this result by showing that the ideal
J
can, in fact, be taken to be a maximal ideal of
B
. Using these results, and applying the classification theorem from
[
22
]
, we arrive at the main result of this section (Theorem
3.5
), which provides a complete description of irreducible cuspidal Harish-Chandra modules over
\uptau(B)
.

In Section 4, we classify irreducible Harish-Chandra modules over
\uptau(B)
whose weight spaces are not uniformly bounded. We show that all such modules are necessarily of highest weight type (
Theorem

4.2
). These modules are constructed as irreducible quotients of generalized Verma modules, induced from irreducible cuspidal modules over some suitable subalgebra of
\uptau(B)
. We further show that these modules are also single point evaluation modules (
Theorem

4.4
), and we provide an explicit description of their highest weight spaces (
Theorem

4.5
). Section 5 brings together the results from the previous sections to present a complete classification of all irreducible Harish-Chandra modules over
\uptau(B)
(
Theorem

5.1
).

Preliminaries

2.1.
Notations:

Throughout this paper, we consider all vector spaces, algebras, and tensor products to be over the field of complex numbers
\mathbb{C}
. We shall denote the set of integers, natural numbers, and real numbers by
\mathbb{Z},\mathbb{N},\mathbb{R}
respectively. We write elements of
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
and
\mathbb{C}^{n}
in boldface. For an
n
-tuple
{\bf{m}}=(m_{1},m_{2},\ldots,m_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, we denote
(m_{2},\ldots,m_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}^{n-1}
by
\underline{\textbf{m}}
. For two elements
{\bf{m}},{\bf{k}}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, we say that
{\bf{m}}\geq{\bf{k}}
if
m_{i}\geq k_{i}
for all
1\leq i\leq n
. Let
(-|-)
denote the standard inner product on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. The universal enveloping algebra of a Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{g}}
is denoted by
\mathcal{U}({\mathfrak{g}})
. Let
\{{\bf{e_{1}}},{\bf{e_{2}}},\ldots,{\bf{e_{n}}}\}
denote the standard basis of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. The dual space of a vector space
V
will be denoted by
V^{*}
. The notation
A^{\times}
stands for the set
A\setminus\{0\}
.

2.2.
Basics of Full Toroidal Lie Algebra

Let
{\mathfrak{g}}
be a finite dimensional Lie algebra with Cartan subalgebra
\mathfrak{h}
. It is well known that
{\mathfrak{g}}
admits a non-degenerate symmetric bilinear form
\left\langle\cdot,\cdot\right\rangle
, which is unique up to a scalar.

For a positive integer
n\geq 3
, let
A_{n}=\mathbb{C}[t_{1}^{\pm 1},t_{2}^{\pm 1},\ldots,t_{n}^{\pm 1}]
denote the Laurent polynomial ring in
n
variables
t_{1},t_{2},\ldots,t_{n}
. For an
n
-tuple
{\bf{m}}=(m_{1},m_{2},\ldots,m_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, define
t^{\bf{m}}=t_{1}^{m_{1}}t_{2}^{m_{2}}\cdots t_{n}^{m_{n}}\in A_{n}
. The multiloop algebra is defined as
\mathcal{L}({\mathfrak{g}})={\mathfrak{g}}\otimes A_{n}
, with the Lie bracket

[x\otimes t^{{\bf{m}}},y\otimes t^{{\bf{k}}}]=[x,y]\otimes t^{{\bf{m+k}}}
,
\forall x,y\in{\mathfrak{g}},\,\,\,\,{\bf{m}},{\bf{k}}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
.

Define differential elements
K_{i}=t_{i}^{-1}dt_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq n
and let
\Omega_{A_{n}}
be the free left
A_{n}
-module generated by
\{K_{1},K_{2},\ldots,K_{n}\}
. The differential map
d:A_{n}\rightarrow\Omega_{A_{n}}
is given by
d(t^{\textbf{m}})=\sum_{i=1}^{n}m_{i}t^{\textbf{m}}K_{i}
. More precisely, we have

\Omega_{A_{n}}=\mathrm{Span}\,\{t^{\textbf{m}}K_{i}:\textbf{m}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n},\,1\leq i\leq n\},\quad\text{Im}(d)=\text{Span}\,\{\sum_{i=1}^{n}m_{i}\,t^{\textbf{m}}K_{i}:\textbf{m}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}\}.

The quotient space
\mathcal{K}=\Omega_{A_{n}}/\text{Im}\mathrm{(}d)
is called the space of Kähler differentials, and is explicitly given by

\mathcal{K}=\text{Span}\,\{t^{\textbf{m}}K_{i}:\textbf{m}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n},\,\,\,1\leq i\leq n,\,\,\,\sum_{i=1}^{n}m_{i}\,t^{\textbf{m}}K_{i}=0\}
.

We use the same notation for the image of
t^{\textbf{m}}K_{i}
in
\mathcal{K}
. It is known (see
[
14
,
19
]
) that the universal central extension of
L({\mathfrak{g}})
is given by
\hat{\mathcal{L}}({\mathfrak{g}})=\mathcal{L}({\mathfrak{g}})\oplus\mathcal{K}
, with the Lie brackets:

(2.1)

[x\otimes t^{{\bf{m}}},\,y\otimes t^{{\bf{k}}}]=[x,y]\otimes t^{{\bf{m+k}}}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}m_{i}\,t^{{\bf{m+k}}}K_{i}

(2.2)

\hskip 56.9055pt[x\otimes t^{\bf{m}},z]=[z,z^{\prime}]=0,\hskip 14.22636pt\forall x,y\in{\mathfrak{g}},\quad{\bf{m}},{\bf{k}}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n},\,z,z^{\prime}\in\mathcal{K}.

The final component in the definition of a full toroidal Lie algebra is the derivation algebra of
A_{n}
, denoted by
\mathcal{W}_{n}
. For
1\leq i\leq n
, let
d_{i}=t_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{i}}
, which acts on
A_{n}
by derivation. Then we know that
\mathcal{W}_{n}
is generated by the set
\{t^{\bf{m}}d_{i}:{\bf{m}}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n},\,1\leq i\leq n\}
. This infinite dimensional derivation algebra is a very well known classical object and is popularly known as the Witt algebra of rank
n
. Lie bracket on
\mathcal{W}_{n}
is given by

[t^{\bf{m}}d_{i},t^{\bf{k}}d_{j}]=k_{i}\,t^{\bf{m+k}}d_{j}-m_{j}\,t^{\bf{m+k}}d_{i}
.

\mathcal{W}_{n}
has a unique extension on
\hat{\mathcal{L}}({\mathfrak{g}})
by the action

t^{\bf{m}}d_{i}\,(x\otimes t^{\bf{k}})=k_{i}\,x\otimes t^{\bf{m+k}}
,

t^{\bf{m}}d_{i}\,(t^{\bf{k}}K_{j})=k_{i}t^{\bf{m+k}}K_{j}+\delta_{i,j}\sum_{p=1}^{n}m_{p}t^{\bf{m+k}}K_{p}
.

Moreover,
\mathcal{W}_{n}
admits two non-trivial 2-cocycles with values in
\mathcal{K}
(see
[
1
]
), given by

\displaystyle\phi_{1}(t^{\bf{m}}d_{i},t^{\bf{k}}d_{j})=-k_{i}m_{j}\sum_{p=1}^{n}m_{p}t^{\bf{m+k}}K_{p},\quad\quad\phi_{2}(t^{\bf{m}}d_{i},t^{\bf{k}}d_{j})=m_{i}k_{j}\sum_{p=1}^{n}m_{p}t^{\bf{m+k}}K_{p}.

We are now ready to define the full toroidal Lie algebra. Let
\phi
be an arbitrary linear combination of
\phi_{1}
and
\phi_{2}
. Then, the full toroidal Lie algebra associated with
{\mathfrak{g}}
and
\phi
is defined as

\uptau=\mathcal{L}({\mathfrak{g}})\oplus\mathcal{K}\oplus\mathcal{W}_{n}
.

So, as a vector space

\uptau={\mathfrak{g}}\otimes\mathbb{C}[t_{1}^{\pm 1},t_{2}^{\pm 1},\ldots,t_{n}^{\pm 1}]\oplus\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1\\
\textbf{m}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}\end{subarray}}^{n}\mathbb{C}t^{\textbf{m}}K_{i}\hskip 2.84544pt\bigoplus\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1\\
\textbf{m}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}\end{subarray}}^{n}\mathbb{C}t^{\textbf{m}}d_{i}
.

The bracket relations in
\uptau
include (
2.1
), (
2.2
) and

[t^{\bf{m}}d_{i},t^{\bf{k}}K_{j}]=k_{i}t^{\bf{m+k}}K_{j}+\delta_{i,j}\sum_{p=1}^{n}m_{p}t^{\bf{m+k}}K_{p},

[t^{\bf{m}}d_{i},t^{\bf{k}}d_{j}]=k_{i}t^{\bf{m+k}}d_{j}-m_{j}t^{\bf{m+k}}d_{i}+\phi(t^{\bf{m}}d_{i},t^{\bf{k}}d_{j}),

[t^{\bf{m}}d_{i},x\otimes t^{\bf{k}}]=k_{i}x\otimes t^{\bf{m+k}},\quad\forall x\in{\mathfrak{g}},\,\,\,\,{\bf{m}},{\bf{k}}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n},\,\,1\leq i,j\leq n.

Let
B
be a finitely generated commutative associative algebra with unit. In this paper our concerned Lie algebra is

\uptau(B):=\uptau\otimes B=(\mathcal{L}({\mathfrak{g}})\oplus\mathcal{K}\oplus\mathcal{W}_{n})\otimes B
,

called the map full toroidal Lie algebra with the natural bracket

[X\otimes a,Y\otimes b]=[X,Y]\otimes ab
,
X,Y\in\uptau,\,\,a,b\in B
.

For notational convenience, we denote
X\otimes a
by
X(a)
. Unlike the full toroidal Lie algebra which has the finite dimensional center,
\uptau(B)
has infinite dimensional center, given by the set Span
\{K_{i}(a):a\in B,\,1\leq i\leq n\}
.

We now introduce an alternative set of notations for the elements of
\uptau
. For
{\bf{p}}\in\mathbb{C}^{n},{\bf{m}}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, set

D({\bf{p}},{\bf{m}})=\sum_{i=1}^{n}p_{i}t^{\bf{m}}d_{i},\quad\quad K({\bf{p}},{\bf{m}})=\sum_{i=1}^{n}p_{i}t^{\bf{m}}K_{i}.

With these notations bracket formulas can be expressed as

\displaystyle[D({\bf{p}},{\bf{m}}),D({\bf{q}},{\bf{k}})]

\displaystyle=D({\bf{w}},{\bf{m+k}})+\phi(D({\bf{p}},{\bf{m}}),D({\bf{q}},{\bf{k}})),\quad\text{where}\,\,{\bf{w}}=({\bf{p}}|{\bf{k}}){\bf{q}}-({\bf{q}}|{\bf{m}}){\bf{p}},

\displaystyle\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt[D({\bf{p}},{\bf{m}}),K({\bf{q}},{\bf{k}})]

\displaystyle=K({\bf{z}},{\bf{m+k}}),\quad\text{where}\,\,{\bf{z}}=({\bf{p}}|{\bf{k}}){\bf{q}}+({\bf{q}}|{\bf{p}}){\bf{m}},

\displaystyle\vskip 8.5359pt[D({\bf{p}},{\bf{m}}),x\otimes t^{\bf{k}}]

\displaystyle=({\bf{p}}|{\bf{k}})\,x\otimes t^{\bf{m+k}},\hskip 28.45274pt[K({\bf{p}},{\bf{m}}),K({\bf{q}},{\bf{k}})]=0

Also, cocycles are given by

\displaystyle\phi_{1}(D({\bf{p}},{\bf{m}}),D({\bf{q}},{\bf{k}}))

\displaystyle=-({\bf{p}}|{\bf{k}})({\bf{m}}|{\bf{q}})\,K({\bf{m}},{\bf{m+k}}),\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt

\displaystyle\phi_{2}(D({\bf{p}},{\bf{m}}),D({\bf{q}},{\bf{k}}))

\displaystyle=({\bf{p}}|{\bf{m}})({\bf{q}}|{\bf{k}})\,K({\bf{m}},{\bf{m+k}}),\quad\forall{\bf{p}},{\bf{q}}\in\mathbb{C}^{n},\,{\bf{m}},{\bf{k}}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}.

Let
D=\text{Span}\,\{d_{1},d_{2},\ldots,d_{n}\}
, and define
\delta_{i}\in D^{*}
such that
\delta_{i}(d_{j})=\delta_{i,j}
for
1\leq i,j\leq n
. For
{\bf{m}}=(m_{1},m_{2},\ldots,m_{n})
, set
\delta_{{\bf{m}}}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}m_{i}\,\delta_{i}
. For notational simplicity, we denote
\delta_{\mathbf{m}}
by
\mathbf{m}
. Notice that
\uptau
admits a natural
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
-grading:

(2.3)

\uptau=\displaystyle{\bigoplus_{{\bf{m}}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\uptau_{{\bf{m}}}},\quad\text{where}\quad\uptau_{{\bf{m}}}=\bigg({\mathfrak{g}}\otimes t^{\bf{m}}\oplus\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{C}t^{\textbf{m}}K_{i}\oplus\hskip 2.84544pt\bigoplus\limits_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{C}t^{\bf{m}}d_{i}\bigg).

Similarly,
\uptau(B)
also admits a natural
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
-grading:

(2.4)

\uptau(B)=\displaystyle{\bigoplus_{{\bf{m}}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\uptau_{{\bf{m}}}(B)},\quad\text{where }\uptau_{\mathbf{m}}(B)=\uptau_{\mathbf{m}}\otimes B.

Now we recall some basic definitions.

2.3.
Trivial module

Let
L
be a Lie algebra and let
V
be an
L
-module. Then
V
is called a trivial module if
x.v=0
for all
x\in L
and
v\in V
. A vector
v\in V
is called a trivial vector if
x.v=0
for all
x\in L
.

2.4.
Harish-Chandra Module

A
\uptau(B)
-module
V
is said to be Harish-Chandra module if it satisfies the following:

(1)

V
is a weight module with respect to
D=\text{Span}\,\{d_{1},d_{2},\ldots,d_{n}\}
, i.e.,

V=\oplus_{\lambda\in D^{*}}V_{\lambda}
, where
V_{\lambda}=\{v\in V:d_{i}.v=\lambda(d_{i})v,\,\,1\leq i\leq n\}
.

(2)

dim
V_{\lambda}<\infty
,  for all
{\lambda}\in D^{*}
.

The set
P(V)=\{\lambda\in D^{*}:V_{\lambda}\neq 0\}
is called the set of weights of
V
and any non-zero element
v\in V_{\lambda}
is called a weight vector with weight
\lambda
.

2.5.
Cuspidal Module

A Harish-Chandra module
V
is said to be cuspidal if the dimensions of its weight spaces are uniformly bounded, i.e., there exists
M\in\mathbb{N}
such that dim
V_{\lambda}<M
,
\forall{\lambda}\in D^{*}
. These modules are also known as uniformly bounded modules.

2.6.
Highest weight module

A
\uptau(B)
-module
V
is called a highest weight module if there exists a nonzero vector
v\in V
such that

\uptau(B)^{+}v=0
and
\mathcal{U}(\uptau(B))v=V
.

In this case,
v
is called a highest weight vector.

2.7.
Generalized highest weight module

A
\uptau(B)
-module
V
is called a generalized highest weight (GHW) module if there exists a nonzero vector
v\in V
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\uptau_{{\bf{m}}}(B)v=0
for all
{\bf{m}}\geq(k,k,\ldots,k)
.

Such a vector
v
is called a GHW vector.

2.8.
Single point evaluation module

Let
V
be a
\uptau(B)
-module with the corresponding representation
\rho:\uptau(B)\rightarrow\text{End}\,(V)
. Then
V
is said to be a single point evaluation module for
\uptau(B)
if there exists a maximal ideal
M
of
B
such that the map
\rho
factors through
\uptau(M)
. In that case,
V
can be viewed as a module over
\uptau
as

\uptau(B)/\uptau(M)\cong\uptau(B/M)\cong\uptau(\mathbb{C})\cong\uptau
.

Equivalently, if
V
is a single point evaluation module for
\uptau(B)
, then there exists an algebra homomorphism
\phi:B\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
such that

X(b).v=\phi(b)\,X.v

for all

X\in\uptau,\,b\in B
, and
v\in V
.

It is easy to check that
V
is an irreducible single point evaluation
\uptau(B)
-module if and only if
V
is an irreducible module for
\uptau
.

2.9.
Change of Coordinates

Let
C=(c_{ij})
be an
n\times n
matrix in
GL_{n}{(\mathbb{Z})}
with determinant
\pm 1
. Then one has the following automorphism of
\uptau
, which we again denote by C:

C(x\otimes t^{\textbf{m}})=x\otimes t^{\textbf{m}C^{T}},\hskip 14.22636ptC(t^{\textbf{m}}K_{i})=\displaystyle{\sum_{r=1}^{n}{c_{ri}t^{\textbf{m}C^{T}}K_{r}}},\hskip 14.22636ptC(t^{\textbf{m}}d_{i})=\displaystyle{\sum_{r=1}^{n}b_{ir}t^{\textbf{m}C^{T}}d_{r}},

where
C^{-1}=(b_{ij})
and
C^{T}
denotes the transpose of
C
. This automorphism

C
yields a new full toroidal Lie algebra from the original one.

Alternatively, we can define another Lie algebra automorphism of
\uptau
in a different way. Let
\{{\bm{\alpha}}_{1},\bm{\alpha}_{2},\ldots,\bm{\alpha}_{n}\}
be a
\mathbb{Z}
-basis of
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
and
\{\bm{\beta}_{1},\bm{\beta}_{2},\ldots,\bm{\beta}_{n}\}
be the corresponding dual basis in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Define
\Phi:\uptau\rightarrow\uptau
by

x\otimes t^{\bf{m}}\mapsto x\otimes t^{\sum_{i=1}^{n}m_{i}\bm{\alpha}_{i}},\hskip 11.38092ptt^{\bf{m}}K_{i}\mapsto K(\bm{\alpha}_{i},\,\sum_{i=1}^{n}m_{i}\bm{\alpha}_{i}),\hskip 11.38092ptt^{\bf{m}}d_{i}\mapsto D(\bm{\beta}_{i},\,\sum_{i=1}^{n}m_{i}\bm{\alpha}_{i})

The automorphisms
\Phi
and
C
of
\uptau
naturally extend to automorphisms
\overline{\Phi}
and
\overline{C}
of
\uptau(B)
, respectively. More explicitly,
\overline{\Phi}:\uptau(B)\rightarrow\uptau(B)
is defined by

\overline{\Phi}\,(X(b))=\Phi(X)(b),\quad\forall\,X\in\uptau,\,b\in B
.

We call this process as a
change of co-ordinates
.

2.10.
Representation of toroidal Lie algebras

Let
P_{{\mathfrak{g}}}^{+}
denote the set of dominant integral weights of a Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{g}}
. For
\lambda_{1}\in P_{{\mathfrak{g}}}^{+}
, let
V(\lambda_{1})
be the irreducible highest weight
{\mathfrak{g}}
-module with the highest weight
\lambda_{1}
. Similarly, let
\lambda_{2}\in P_{\mathfrak{sl}_{n}}^{+}
, and
V(\lambda_{2})
be the highest weight module for
{\mathfrak{sl}_{n}}
. We extend
V(\lambda_{2})
to a
{\mathfrak{gl}_{n}}
-module by assuming the identity matrix acts by a scalar
c
; denote this module by
V(c,\lambda_{2})
. Let
{\bm{\alpha}}=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n})\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
. Define an action of
\uptau
on the tensor product
V(\lambda_{1})\otimes V(c,\lambda_{2})\otimes\mathbb{C}[t_{1}^{\pm 1},t_{2}^{\pm 1},\ldots,t_{n}^{\pm 1}]
as follows

\big(x\otimes t^{\bf{m}}\big)(v_{1}\otimes v_{2}\otimes t^{\bf{r}})=(xv_{1})\otimes v_{2}\otimes t^{\bf{m+r}}
,
\big(t^{\bf{m}}K_{i}\big)(v_{1}\otimes v_{2}\otimes t^{\bf{r}})=0
,

\big(t^{\bf{m}}d_{i}\big)(v_{1}\otimes v_{2}\otimes t^{\bf{r}})=(\alpha_{i}+r_{i})(v_{1}\otimes v_{2}\otimes t^{\bf{m+r}})+\sum_{j=1}^{n}m_{j}\,v_{1}\otimes(E_{j,i}v_{2})\otimes t^{\bf{m+r}},

\forall x\in\mathfrak{g},\,v_{1}\in V(\lambda_{1}),v_{2}\in V(c,\lambda_{2}),\,{\bf{m}},{\bf{r}}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n},\,\,\,1\leq i\leq n.

Here
E_{j,i}
denotes the
n\times n
matrix with 1 in the
(j,i)
-th position and 0 elsewhere. We denote the above
\uptau
-module by
\mathfrak{L}(c,\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\bm{\alpha})
.

Proposition 3.17 of
[
22
]
shows that
\mathfrak{L}(c,\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\bm{\alpha})
is an irreducible module for
\uptau
if either
(\lambda_{2},c,\bm{\alpha})\notin\{0\}\times\{0,n\}\times\mathbb{Z}^{n}
or
\lambda_{1}\neq 0
or
(\lambda_{2},c)\neq(\omega_{k},k)
for any fundamental weight
\omega_{k}
of
\mathfrak{sl}_{n}
with
1\leq k\leq n-1
.

In particular, when
\lambda_{1}=0
, the module
\mathfrak{L}(c,0,\lambda_{2},\bm{\alpha})
is an irreducible
\uptau
-module if and only if it is irreducible as a module over
\mathcal{W}_{n}
. In the literature, these modules are known as Larson modules or Shen modules
[
17
,
29
]
. Let
\{\omega_{1},\omega_{2},\ldots,\omega_{n-1}\}
be the set of fundamental weights of
\mathfrak{sl}_{n}
. Also, we assume that
\omega_{0}=\omega_{n}=0
for notational convenience. Let
W\cong\mathbb{C}^{n}
denote the standard representation of
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
. Then its
k
-fold exterior power
\wedge^{k}W
is known to be isomorphic to the irreducible
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
-module
V(k,\omega_{k})
for each
0\leq k\leq n
. The associated
\mathcal{W}_{n}
-modules
\mathfrak{L}(k,0,\omega_{k},\bm{\alpha})
consist of differential
k
-forms and they form the de Rham complex

\mathfrak{L}(0,0,\omega_{0},\bm{\alpha})\xrightarrow{d_{0}}\mathfrak{L}(1,0,\omega_{1},\bm{\alpha})\xrightarrow{d_{1}}\cdots\xrightarrow{d_{n-1}}\mathfrak{L}(n,0,\omega_{n},\bm{\alpha})

The differential map
d_{k}:\mathfrak{L}(k,0,\omega_{k},\bm{\alpha})\rightarrow\mathfrak{L}(k+1,0,\omega_{k+1},\bm{\alpha})
is a homomorphism of
\mathcal{W}_{n}
-modules and consequently the kernel and image of the map naturally give rise to
\mathcal{W}_{n}
-modules of
\mathfrak{L}(k,0,\omega_{k},\bm{\alpha})
and
\mathfrak{L}(k+1,0,\omega_{k+1},\bm{\alpha})
, respectively. By the Proposition 3.17 of
[
22
]
, we know that for any
0\leq k\leq n
, the module
\mathfrak{L}(k,0,\omega_{k},\bm{\alpha})
admits a unique irreducible quotient over
\mathcal{W}_{n}
. This irreducible quotient is isomorphic to the image of the map
d_{k}:\mathfrak{L}(k,0,\omega_{k},\bm{\alpha})\rightarrow\mathfrak{L}(k+1,0,\omega_{k+1},\bm{\alpha})
and is denoted by
d_{k}\mathfrak{L}(k,0,\omega_{k},\bm{\alpha})
. Furthermore, if
\bm{\alpha}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, then the module
\mathfrak{L}(n,0,\omega_{n},\bm{\alpha})
admits a one dimensional trivial module as its irreducible quotient. We now state the following classification theorem as given in Theorem 3.24 of
[
22
]
.

Theorem 2.1

(
[
22
]
, Theorem 3.24)
.

Let
V
be a non trivial irreducible cuspidal
\uptau
-module. Then
V
is isomorphic to one of the following:

(1)

\mathfrak{L}(c,\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\bm{\alpha})
, for some quadruplet
(c,\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\bm{\alpha})\in\mathbb{C}\times(P^{+}_{{\mathfrak{g}}})^{\times}\times(P^{+}_{\mathfrak{sl}_{n}})\times\mathbb{C}^{n}
.

(2)

\mathfrak{L}(c,0,\lambda_{2},\bm{\alpha})
for some
(c,\lambda_{2},\bm{\alpha})\in\mathbb{C}\times(P^{+}_{\mathfrak{sl}_{n}})\times\mathbb{C}^{n}
with
(c,\lambda_{2})\neq(k,\omega_{k})
for
1\leq k\leq n-1
.

(3)

\mathfrak{L}(n,0,0,\bm{\alpha})
with
\bm{\alpha}\notin\mathbb{Z}^{n}
.

(4)

A submodule
d_{k}\mathfrak{L}(k,0,\omega_{k},\bm{\alpha})
of
\mathfrak{L}(k+1,0,\omega_{k+1},\bm{\alpha})
for some
\bm{\alpha}\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
, where
0\leq k\leq n-1
and
\omega_{0}=\omega_{n}=0
.

3.
Classification of Irreducible cuspidal modules over
\uptau(B)

In this section, we classify all the irreducible cuspidal modules over
\uptau(B)
. Note that if
V

\uptau(B)
-module, then there exists
\lambda\in D^{*}

always stands for an irreducible cuspidal
\uptau(B)
-module.

Proposition 3.1
.

\mathcal{K}(B)
acts trivially on
V
.

Proof.

First, we prove that zero degree central operators
K_{i}(B)
act trivially on
V
for each
1\leq i\leq n
. Fix
1\leq i\leq n
,
a\in B
, and choose
h_{1},h_{2}\in\mathfrak{h}
such that
\langle h_{1},h_{2}\rangle\neq 0
. Consider the Heisenberg algebra
\mathcal{H}(a)
spanned by the elements
\{h_{1}\otimes t_{i}^{n},h_{2}\otimes t_{i}^{m}(a),K_{i}(a):n>0,m<0,n,m\in\mathbb{Z}\}
, with the Lie bracket

[h_{1}\otimes t_{i}^{n},h_{2}\otimes t_{i}^{m}(a)]=\langle h_{1},h_{2}\rangle\delta_{n,-m}K_{i}(a)
,

and
K_{i}(a)
acting centrally. Define
V^{\prime}=\oplus_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}V_{\lambda+m{\bf{e}}_{i}}
. Then

is a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded
\mathcal{H}(a)
-module with uniformly bounded weight spaces. If

, then by Proposition 4.3(i) and 4.5 of
[
8
]

must contain a highest weight or lowest weight module, contradicting the fact that

, since

is irreducible.

Next, Lemmas 3.6, 3.7, 3.8 of
[
22
]
show that
t^{\bf{p}}K_{i}
acts locally nilpotently on
V
for all
1\leq i\leq n
and
\bf{p}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
with
p_{j}\neq 0
for some

. Fix
1\leq i\leq n
and choose
\bf{p}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
such that
p_{j}\neq 0
for
1\leq j\neq i\leq n
. As
V
is a uniformly bounded module, there exists an integer
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that

((t^{\bf{-p}}K_{i})(t^{\bf{p}}K_{i})^{N})V=\{0\}
.

Then for any
a\in B,{\bf{m}}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, and
v\in V
,

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=[t^{\bf{m+p}}\,d_{j}(a),((t^{\bf{-p}}K_{i})(t^{\bf{p}}K_{i})^{N})]v

\displaystyle=Np_{j}\bigg((t^{\bf{-p}}K_{i})^{N}t^{\bf{m+2p}}K_{i}(a)(t^{\bf{p}}K_{i})^{N-1}-t^{\bf{m}}K_{i}(a)(t^{\bf{-p}}K_{i})^{N-1}(t^{\bf{p}}K_{i})^{N}\bigg)v

Applying
t^{\bf{p}}K_{i}
to both sides, we get

(t^{\bf{m}}K_{i}(a))\,(t^{\bf{-p}}K_{i})^{N-1}(t^{\bf{p}}K_{i})^{N+1}V=\{0\}
,

.

Therefore, for
a_{1},a_{2}\in B,\,{\bf{m}}^{(1)},{\bf{m}}^{(2)}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=[t^{\bf{m^{(2)}+p}}\,d_{j}(a_{2}),(t^{\bf{m^{(1)}}}K_{i}(a_{1}))\,(t^{\bf{-p}}K_{i})^{N-1}(t^{\bf{p}}K_{i})^{N+1}]v

\displaystyle=(N+1)\,p_{j}\,t^{\bf{m^{(1)}}}K_{i}(a_{1})\,t^{\bf{m^{(2)}}+2\bf{p}}K_{i}(a_{2})\,(t^{\bf{-p}}K_{i})^{N-1}(t^{\bf{p}}K_{i})^{N}v

Applying
t^{\bf{p}}K_{i}

(t^{\bf{m}^{(1)}}K_{i}(a_{1}))(t^{\bf{m^{(2)}}}K_{i}(a_{2}))\,(t^{\bf{-p}}K_{i})^{N-2}(t^{\bf{p}}K_{i})^{N+2}V=\{0\}

By continuing this process

(3.1)

(t^{\bf{m}^{(1)}}K_{i}(a_{1}))\cdots(t^{\bf{m^{(N)}}}K_{i}(a_{N}))(t^{\bf{p}}K_{i})^{2N}V=\{0\},\quad\forall a_{k}\in B,{\bf{m}^{(k)}}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}.

Now by applying successively

to both sides of (
3.1
) and following the same method as above, we obtain

(3.2)

(t^{\bf{m}^{(1)}}K_{i}(a_{1}))(t^{\bf{m^{(2)}}}K_{i}(a_{2}))\cdots(t^{\bf{m^{(3N)}}}K_{i}(a_{3N}))V=\{0\},\quad\forall a_{k}\in B,\,{\bf{m}^{(k)}}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}.

Since

is arbitrary, equation (

) holds for every

. Using arguments similar to those in
[
23
, Lemma 4.4]
, we can find a nonzero vector
v\in V
such that
(t^{\bf{m}}K_{i}(a))v=0
for all
a\in B,{\bf{m}}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, and

. Then
W=\{v\in V:\mathcal{K}(B)v=0\}
becomes a nonzero

-submodule of

∎

Our next aim is to prove the existence of a maximal ideal
\mathcal{M}
of
B
such that
\uptau(\mathcal{M})
acts trivially on

. The following proposition is the first step towards that direction.

Proposition 3.2
.

There exists a cofinite ideal
J
of
B
such that
(\uptau\otimes J)V=0
.

Proof.

If
V
is trivial, there is nothing to prove. So assume
V\neq 0
. Consider the weight space decomposition of
V=\bigoplus_{{\bf{m}}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}V_{\lambda+\bf{m}}
, for some
\lambda\in D^{*}
. Choose
{\bf{m}}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
such that
V_{\lambda+\bf{m}}\neq 0
. For
j\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}
, define

I_{j}=\{a\in B:(t_{1}^{j}d_{i}(a))V_{\lambda+\bf{m}}=0,\,\,\forall\,1\leq i\leq n\}
.

Claim (a).

I_{j}
is a cofinite ideal of
B
.

To see that
I_{j}
is an ideal, take
a\in I_{j},b\in B
, and
v\in V_{\lambda+\bf{m}}
. Then

t_{1}^{j}d_{i}(ab)v=\frac{1}{j}[d_{1}(b),t_{1}^{j}d_{i}(a)]v=0,\,\,\,\,\,\forall\,1\leq i\leq n
.

In the above expression we have used the fact that
d_{1}(b)
preserves weight spaces. Note that
I_{j}
is the kernel of the linear map

\Phi:B\rightarrow(Hom_{\mathbb{C}}\,(V_{\lambda+\bf{m}},V_{\lambda+{\bf{m}}+j{\bf{e}}_{1}}))^{n}
,
\Phi(a)=(f_{1}(a),f_{2}(a),\ldots,f_{n}(a))
,

where
f_{i}(a)(v)=(t_{1}^{j}d_{i}(a))v
,
\forall\,1\leq i\leq n
.
Since
V
is uniformly bounded, there exists
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\text{dim}\,V_{\lambda+\bf{m}}\leq N
for all
{\bf{m}}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
. Thus we have dim
B/I_{j}\leq N^{2n}
and that proves our claim.

Claim (b).

I_{1}^{j}I_{2}\subset I_{j+2}
for all
j\geq 1
.

We prove this by induction on
j
. For
j=1
, we need to show that
a\in I_{1}
and
b\in I_{2}
implies
ab\in I_{3}
. Indeed, this holds because

0=[t_{1}d_{1}(a),t_{1}^{2}d_{i}(b)]V_{\lambda+\bf{m}}=(2t_{1}^{3}d_{i}(ab)-\delta_{1,i}t_{1}^{3}d_{1}(ab))V_{\lambda+\bf{m}},\quad\forall\,1\leq i\leq n

Now assume that the claim holds for some fixed
j\geq 1
. Let
a\in I_{1},b\in I_{1}^{j}I_{2}
, then
t_{1}^{j+2}d_{i}(b)v=0
for all
i
, as
I_{1}^{j}I_{2}\subset I_{j+2}
. Then by the induction the claim follows since

0=[t_{1}d_{1}(a),\,t_{1}^{j+2}d_{i}(b)]V_{\lambda+\bf{m}}=(j+2)\,t_{1}^{j+3}d_{i}(ab)V_{\lambda+\bf{m}}-\delta_{1,i}\,t_{1}^{j+3}d_{1}(ab)V_{\lambda+\bf{m}}
.

Now by using an argument exactly similar to that of Proposition 4.1 of
[
27
]
, we have

I_{1}^{N^{2n}}I_{2}\subset I_{j}
for
j\geq 1
. Proceeding similarly as above, we get
I_{-1}^{N^{2n}}I_{-2}\subset I_{-j}
for
j\geq 1
. Take
J_{1}=I_{-1}^{N^{2n}}I_{-2}I_{1}^{N^{2n}}I_{2}
, then
J_{1}\subset I_{j}
for all
j\neq 0
. Since
J_{1}
is the product of finitely many cofinite ideals, it is cofinite. Now any element of
J_{1}
can be written as a sum of the elements of the form
a_{-1}a_{1}
with
a_{-1}\in I_{-1},a_{1}\in I_{1}
since
J_{1}\subset I_{-1}I_{1}
. For
1\leq i\leq n
, we get

0=[t_{1}^{-1}d_{1}(a_{-1}),t_{1}d_{i}(a_{1})]V_{\lambda+\bf{m}}=(d_{i}(a_{-1}a_{1})+\delta_{1,i}d_{1}(a_{-1}a_{1}))V_{\lambda+\bf{m}}
.

Thus
d_{i}(J_{1})V_{\lambda+\bf{m}}=0
for all
1\leq i\leq n
.
Also, since
J_{1}\subset I_{j}
for all
j\neq 0
, we have
t_{1}^{j}d_{i}(J_{1})V_{\lambda+\bf{m}}=0
for all
j\neq 0
and
1\leq i\leq n
. Similarly we can construct cofinite ideals
J_{2},J_{3},\ldots,J_{n}
of
B
such that

t_{k}^{j}\,d_{i}(J_{k})V_{\lambda+\bf{m}}=0
for all
j\in\mathbb{Z},\,\,2\leq k\leq n,\,\,1\leq i\leq n
.

Since as a Lie algebra
\mathcal{W}_{n}
is generated by the set
\{t_{k}^{j}\,d_{i}:j\in\mathbb{Z},\,1\leq i,k\leq n\}
, we conclude that

\mathcal{W}_{n}(J^{\prime})\,V_{\lambda+\bf{m}}=0
, where
J^{\prime}=J_{1}J_{2}\cdots J_{n}
.

Next, consider
J^{\prime\prime}=\{a\in B:(x\otimes t_{1})(a)V_{\lambda+\bf{m}}=0,\,\forall x\in{\mathfrak{g}}\}
, then it is straightforward to verify that
J^{\prime\prime}
is a cofinite ideal of
B
. Let
a\in J^{\prime},b\in J^{\prime\prime}
. Then

(x\otimes t^{\bf{m}})(ab)V_{\lambda+\bf{m}}=[t^{{\bf{m}}-{\bf{e}}_{1}}\,d_{1}(a),x\otimes t_{1}(b)]V_{\lambda+\bf{m}}=0
.

Therefore we get
\uptau(J)V_{\lambda+\bf{m}}=0
by taking
J=J^{\prime}J^{\prime\prime}
.

Since
V_{\lambda+\bf{m}}\neq 0
, and
V
is irreducible, we have
V=\mathcal{U}(\uptau(B))V_{\lambda+\bf{m}}
. Let
\mathcal{U}_{0}(\uptau(B))\subseteq\mathcal{U}_{1}(\uptau(B))\subseteq\mathcal{U}_{2}(\uptau(B))\subseteq\cdots
denote the natural filtration of
\uptau(B)
, induced by the grading on the tensor algebra of
\uptau(B)
. To prove
\uptau(J)V=0
, it is enough to prove that
\uptau(J)\,\mathcal{U}_{k}(\uptau(B))V_{\lambda+\bf{m}}=0,\,\,\forall k\in\mathbb{N}
. An easy induction on
k
completes the proof.
∎

Now, since
J
is a cofinite ideal, the quotient ring
B/J
is an Artinian ring. Hence
B/J
can be written as a finite product of local Artinian rings. It follows that the ideal
J
is contained in a finite product of powers of maximal ideals. Therefore, we may assume that
J=\mathcal{M}_{1}^{k_{1}}\mathcal{M}_{2}^{k_{2}}\cdots\mathcal{M}_{r}^{k_{r}}
, where each
\mathcal{M}_{i}
is a maximal ideal of
B
and
k_{i}\in\mathbb{N}
. We have the following proposition:

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
{\mathcal{M}}_{1},{\mathcal{M}}_{2}
be two coprime ideals of
B
such that
\uptau({\mathcal{M}}_{1}{\mathcal{M}}_{2})V=0
, then either
\uptau({\mathcal{M}}_{1})V=0
or
\uptau({\mathcal{M}}_{2})V=0
.

Proof.

Since
\uptau({\mathcal{M}}_{1}{\mathcal{M}}_{2})V=0
, we may view
V
as a module over
\uptau(B/{\mathcal{M}}_{1}{\mathcal{M}}_{2})
. As
{\mathcal{M}}_{1}
and
{\mathcal{M}}_{2}
are coprime, the Chinese Remainder Theorem gives

\uptau(B/{\mathcal{M}}_{1}{\mathcal{M}}_{2})\cong\uptau(B/{\mathcal{M}}_{1})\oplus\uptau(B/{\mathcal{M}}_{2})
.

Thus
V
becomes a module over
\uptau(B/{\mathcal{M}}_{1})\oplus\uptau(B/{\mathcal{M}}_{2})
via this isomorphism. Let
V=\oplus_{{\bf{m}}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}V_{\lambda+\bf{m}}
be the weight space decomposition of
V
. For
1\leq i\leq n
, define

d_{i}^{1}=d_{i}\otimes(1+{\mathcal{M}}_{1}),\quad d_{i}^{2}=d_{i}\otimes(1+{\mathcal{M}}_{2}),

so that
d_{i}=d_{i}^{1}+d_{i}^{2}
. By an argument similar to that in claim 1 of Theorem 4.3 of
[
11
]
, both
d_{i}^{1},d_{i}^{2}
act diagonalizably on
V
. As
d_{i}^{1},d_{i}^{2}
commute with each other, we have the following decomposition of
V=\oplus_{\bf{p},\bf{q}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}V_{\bf{p},\bf{q}}
, where

V_{\bf{p},\bf{q}}=\{v\in V:d_{i}^{1}.v=(\lambda_{1}(d_{i})+p_{i})v,\,\,d_{i}^{2}.v=(\lambda_{2}(d_{i})+q_{i})v,\,\,1\leq i\leq n\}
.

with
\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}=\lambda
. Observe that
V_{\lambda+\bf{m}}=\oplus_{\bf{p}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}V_{\bf{m}+\bf{p},-\bf{p}}
. Define the subspaces
V_{(\bf{p})}
and
V^{(\bf{q})}
by

V_{(\bf{p})}=\bigoplus_{\bf{q}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}V_{\bf{p},\bf{q}}\hskip 14.22636ptand\hskip 14.22636ptV^{(\bf{q})}=\bigoplus_{\bf{p}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}V_{\bf{p},\bf{q}}.

Then it is easy to see that
V^{(\bf{q})}
and
V_{(\bf{p})}
are uniformly bounded modules for
\uptau(B/{\mathcal{M}}_{1})
and
\uptau(B/{\mathcal{M}}_{2})
respectively. Note that our claim is equivalent to proving that either
\uptau(B/{\mathcal{M}}_{1})
or
\uptau(B/{\mathcal{M}}_{2})
acts trivially on
V
. Assume, for contradiction, that this is not the case.

Let
\uptau(B/{\mathcal{M}}_{1})V^{(\bf{q})}=0
for some
{\bf{q}}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, then by using the irreducibility of
V
, we have
V=\mathcal{U}(\uptau(B/{\mathcal{M}}_{2}))V^{(\bf{q})}
. Since
\uptau(B/{\mathcal{M}}_{1})
commutes with
\uptau(B/{\mathcal{M}}_{2})
, it follows that
\uptau(B/{\mathcal{M}}_{1})
acts trivially on
V
. This contradicts our assumption, so we must have
\uptau(B/{\mathcal{M}}_{1})V^{(\bf{q})}\neq 0
for all
{\bf{q}}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
. Now choose
{\bf{q}}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
such that
V^{(\bf{q})}\neq 0
. Then
V^{(\bf{q})}
is a nonzero uniformly bounded
\uptau(B/{\mathcal{M}}_{1})
module. Since
\uptau(B/{\mathcal{M}}_{1})
contains a copy of the Virasoro algebra, Lemma 2.11 and Corollary 2.13 of
[
27
]
, imply that
V_{\bf{p},\bf{q}}\neq 0
for
{\bm{\lambda_{1}}}+\bf{p}\neq 0
, where
\bm{\lambda_{1}}=(\lambda_{1}(d_{1}),\ldots,\lambda_{1}(d_{n}))
. This implies that if
{\bm{\lambda_{1}}}+\bf{p}\neq 0
then
V_{({\bf{p}})}\neq{\bf{0}}
. Again, using the same arguments, we get

V_{\bf{p},\bf{q}}\neq 0
whenever
{\bm{\lambda_{1}}}+\bf{p}\neq 0
and
{\bm{\lambda_{2}}}+\bf{q}\neq 0
.

But this contradicts the fact that
V
is a Harish-Chandra module since
V_{\lambda}=\oplus_{\bf{p}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}V_{\bf{p},\bf{-p}}
would then be infinite dimensional. This contradiction proves the proposition.
∎

By the above proposition, we may assume that there exists a maximal ideal
\mathcal{M}
and an integer
k\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\uptau(\mathcal{M}^{k})V=0
. The next proposition ensures the existence of the desired maximal ideal.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be an ideal of
B
such that
\uptau(\mathcal{M}^{k})V=0
for some
k\in\mathbb{N}
, then we have
\uptau(\mathcal{M})V=0
.

Proof.

We prove this statement by using induction on
k
. For
k=1
, the result is obvious. Assume
k=2
, then the elements of

\uptau({\mathcal{M}})
act as commuting operators on
V
. Note that the operator
d_{i}(b)
fixes the weight spaces of
V
. Then, using the same technique used in Proposition 4.5 of
[

]
, we obtain

(d_{i}(b))^{N}\,V=0,\quad\forall\,1\leq i\leq n,\,b\in{\mathcal{M}}
,

where
N=\text{max}\,\{\,\text{dim}\,V_{\lambda+\bf{m}}:{\bf{m}}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}\}
.

Claim 1.
For all
1\leq i\leq n
and
p\in\mathbb{Z}
, we have
(t_{i}^{p}d_{i}({\mathcal{M}}))^{N}V=0
.

For
p=0
, this is already shown. Let
p\neq 0
and take
b\in{\mathcal{M}},v\in V
. Then,

0=t_{i}^{p}d_{i}(d_{i}(b))^{N}v=[t_{i}^{p}d_{i},(d_{i}(b))^{N}]v=-Npt_{i}^{p}d_{i}(b)(d_{i}(b))^{N-1}v
.

Thus,
t_{i}^{p}d_{i}(b)(d_{i}(b))^{N-1}V=0
, and by applying

-module
V_{2}
,
{\underline{\bm{\alpha}}}=(\alpha_{2},\ldots,\alpha_{n})\in\mathbb{C}^{n-1}

t^{\underline{\textbf{m}}}K_{1}(v_{1}\otimes v_{2}\otimes t^{\underline{\textbf{r}}})=a(v_{1}\otimes v_{2}\otimes t^{\underline{\textbf{r}}+\underline{\textbf{m}}})

Remark 4.6
.

Observe that the
\mathring{\uptau}
-module
X\cong V_{1}\otimes V_{2}\otimes\mathbb{C}[t_{2}^{\pm 1},\ldots,t_{n}^{\pm 1}]
is completely determined by the parameters
(a,b,c,\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},{\underline{\bm{\alpha}}})\in\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{C}\times P^{+}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}\times P^{+}_{\mathfrak{sl}_{n}}\times\mathbb{C}^{n-1}
. We denote this module by
\mathfrak{L}(a,b,c,\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},{\underline{\bm{\alpha}}})
. If
(a,b)\neq(0,0)
,
\mathfrak{L}(a,b,c,\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},{\underline{\bm{\alpha}}})
is an irreducible module over
\mathring{\uptau}

\mathfrak{L}(a,b,c,\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},{\underline{\bm{\alpha}}})
even when
(a,b)=(0,0)

\mathring{\uptau}
-module action remains the same as described in the previous proposition. However, in the case

(a,b)=(0,0)

\mathfrak{L}^{\prime}(a,b,c,\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},{\underline{\bm{\alpha}}})
.

5.
Main Theorem

In this section, we summarize the results obtained so far to classify the irreducible Harish-Chandra modules over the map full toroidal algebra.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
V
be a non-trivial irreducible Harish-Chandra module over
\uptau(B)
. Then

(1)

V
is either a cuspidal module or a highest weight module. In both cases,
V
turns out to be a single point evaluation module.

(2)

If
V
is cuspidal, then
V
is isomorphic to one of the following:

(a)

\mathfrak{L}(c,\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\alpha,\psi)
, for some quadruplet
(c,\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\alpha)\in\mathbb{C}\times(P^{+}_{{\mathfrak{g}}})^{\times}\times(P^{+}_{\mathfrak{sl}_{n}})\times\mathbb{C}^{n}
and
\psi:B\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
.

(b)

\mathfrak{L}(c,0,\lambda_{2},\alpha,\psi)
for some
(c,\lambda_{2},\alpha)\in\mathbb{C}\times(P^{+}_{\mathfrak{sl}_{n}})\times\mathbb{C}^{n}
with
(c,\lambda_{2})\neq(k,\omega_{k})
for
1\leq k\leq n-1
and
\psi:B\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
.

(c)

\mathfrak{L}(n,0,0,\alpha,\psi)
with
\alpha\notin\mathbb{Z}^{n}
and
\psi:B\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
.

(d)

A submodule
d_{K}\mathfrak{L}(k,0,\omega_{k},\alpha,\psi)
of
\mathfrak{L}(k+1,0,\omega_{k+1},\alpha,\psi)
for some
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
, where
0\leq k\leq n-1
and
\omega_{0}=\omega_{n}=0
and
\psi:B\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
.

(3)

If
V
is of highest weight type, then
V
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{L}_{\uptau}(X,\bm{\beta},M,\psi)
for some
X,\bm{\beta},M
as defined in Section 4 and
\psi:B\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
. Moreover,
X
is isomorphic to
\mathfrak{L}^{\prime}(a,b,c,\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\underline{\bm{\alpha}})
for some
(a,b,c,\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\underline{\bm{\alpha}})\in\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{C}\times P^{+}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}\times P^{+}_{\mathfrak{sl}_{n}}\times\mathbb{C}^{n-1}
.

Proof.

(1)

Follows from
Theorem

4.2
.

(2)

Follows directly from
Theorem

3.5
.

(3)

Follows from
Theorem

4.2
,
Theorem

4.4
,
Theorem

4.5
, and
Remark

4.6
.

Remark 5.2
.

We have come across
[
4
]
after we completed the paper. In that work, the authors study representations of a similar Lie algebra under the additional assumption of integrability. In contrast, we do not assume integrability, which makes our results stronger and more general. Furthermore, they consider weight spaces with respect to the full Cartan subalgebra, whereas we work with weight spaces with respect to the degree-zero derivations. Additionally, the methods employed in our paper are different from those used in
[
4
]

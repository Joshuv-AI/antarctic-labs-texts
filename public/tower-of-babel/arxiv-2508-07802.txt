Introduction

Let us consider the following Cauchy problem for semi-linear damped wave equation:

(1)

\begin{cases}u_{tt}-\Delta u+u_{t}=|u|^{p},&\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\,t>0,\\
(u,u_{t})(0,x)=\varepsilon(u_{0},u_{1})(x),&\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\\
\end{cases}

where
p>1

stands for power exponent of nonlinearities and the positive constant
\varepsilon

describes the size of initial data. In particular, we consider the initial data

(u_{0},u_{1})\in\dot{H}_{m}^{-\gamma}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\times\dot{H}_{m}^{-\gamma}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),

with
\gamma\geq 0,m\in(1,2]
. The Sobolev spaces
\dot{H}_{m}^{-\gamma}

(\gamma\in\mathbb{R})
are defined by (see, for instance, section 1.3.3 in
[
7
]
)

\displaystyle\dot{H}_{m}^{-\gamma}

\displaystyle:=\bigg\{\varphi:\varphi\in\mathcal{Z}^{\prime}\text{ satisfies }

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt\|\varphi\|_{\dot{H}_{m}^{-\gamma}}:=\left\||D|^{-\gamma}\varphi\right\|_{L^{m}}=\left\|\mathcal{F}^{-1}(|\xi|^{-\gamma}\hat{\varphi}(\xi))\right\|_{L^{m}}<\infty\bigg\}.

Here
\mathcal{Z}^{\prime}
stands for the topological dual space to the subspace of the Schwartz space
\mathcal{S}
consisting of function with
\partial_{\xi}^{\alpha}\hat{\varphi}(0)=0
for all multi-indices
\alpha
, namely,
\mathcal{Z}^{\prime}
is the
factor space
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}/\mathcal{P}
, where
\mathcal{P}
is the space of all polynomials. Moreover, the spaces
\dot{H}_{m}^{-\gamma}
is called the Sobolev spaces of negative order for
\gamma>0
. The linear problem corresponding to the problem (
1
) is

(2)

\begin{cases}u_{tt}-\Delta u+u_{t}=0,&\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\,t>0,\\
(u,u_{t})(0,x)=(u_{0},u_{1})(x),&\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.\end{cases}

Matsumura
[
17
]
was the first to establish some basic decay estimates for the problem (
2
). Subsequently, it has been established that the damped wave equation has a diffusive structure as
t\to\infty
. Since then, many papers have studied sharp
L^{m}-L^{q}

estimates (with
1\leq m\leq q\leq\infty
) for the linear problem (
2
), for example,
[
19
,
3
,
10
]
and the references cited therein. For the semi-linear problem (
1
), the papers
[
23
,
12
,
14
]
are obtained a global (in time) weak solution when the exponent
p
satisfies

(3)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\vskip 8.5359pt1+\displaystyle\frac{2}{n}<p<+\infty&\text{ if }n=1,2,\\
1+\displaystyle\frac{2}{n}<p\leq\displaystyle\frac{n}{n-2}&\text{ if }n\geq 3,\end{cases}

by using weighted
energy methods and assuming data
(u_{0},u_{1})\in(H^{1}\cap L^{1})\times(L^{2}\cap L^{1}).
On the other hand, nonexistence of general global (in time) small data solutions is proved in
[
23
]
for
1<p<1+\frac{2}{n}
and in
[
24
]
for
p=1+\frac{2}{n}
. Therefore, the exponent

p_{F}(n):=1+\frac{2}{n}

is called to as the critical exponent of the semilinear problem (
1
), known as the Fujita exponent (the readers are referred to
[
21
,
22
,
8
]
for semilinear damped wave equation in non-Euclidean frameworks). It is also the critical exponent of the semi-linear heat equation

\phi_{t}-\Delta\phi=|\phi|^{p},\,\,\,\phi(0,x)=\phi_{0}(x),\quad t>0,\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Here, critical exponent is understood as the threshold
between global (in time) existence of small data weak solution and blow-up of solutions
even for small data. The diffusion phenomenon connecting the linear heat equation and the classical damped wave equation (see
[
18
,
19
]
) sheds light on the parabolic nature of classical damped wave models with power-type nonlinearities, as seen through the decay estimates of their solutions. Additionally, to derive the critical regularity of nonlinear terms for the semilinear damped wave equation, the authors in
[
6
]
considered the equation of (
1
) with
nonlinearities
|u|^{1+\frac{2}{n}}\mu(|u|)
on the right-hand side, where
\mu
stands for a suitable
modulus of continuity. Moreover, according to the works
[
15
,
11
,
16
]
, the sharp lifespan estimates for blow-up solutions to (
2
) when
p\leq 1+2/n
in all spatial dimensions have been investigated. Consequently, they provided the following sharp lifespan estimate for blow-up solutions:

\displaystyle T(\varepsilon)\sim\begin{cases}\vskip 8.5359ptC\varepsilon^{-\frac{2(p-1)}{2-n(p-1)}}&\text{ if }1<p<1+\displaystyle\frac{2}{n},\\
\exp\big(C\varepsilon^{-\frac{2}{n}}\big)&\text{ if }p=1+\displaystyle\frac{2}{n},\end{cases}

where
C
is a positive constant independent of
\varepsilon
.

Under additional regularity
L^{m}
for the initial data, with
m\in[1,2]
, papers
[
13
,
3
]
and references therein identified the critical exponent of problem (
2
) as

p_{c}(m,0):=p_{F}\left(\frac{n}{m}\right)=1+\frac{2m}{n}.

However, the authors did not provide conclusions regarding the solution’s properties when
p=p_{c}(m)
. Paper
[
10
]
has proved the existence of global mild solutions when
p=p_{c}(m)
along with some other conditions and the parameter
m
satisfies

\displaystyle m\in\left(\max\left\{1,\frac{\sqrt{n^{2}+16n}-n}{4}\right\},2\right].

Continuing the extension of the initial data space
\dot{H}^{-\gamma}\times\dot{H}^{-\gamma},\gamma\geq 0,
Chen-Reissig
[
1
]
determined that the critical exponent for problem (
1
) is

\displaystyle p_{c}(2,\gamma):=p_{F}\left(\frac{n}{2}+\gamma\right)=1+\frac{4}{n+2\gamma},

by proving the global existence with small data when
p>p_{c}(2,\gamma)
and blow-up solution when
1<p<p_{c}(2,\gamma)
. Moreover, in non-Euclidean frameworks, the papers
[
5
,
4
]
also found the critical exponent

p_{c}^{*}(2,\gamma,\mathcal{Q}):=1+\frac{4}{\mathcal{Q}+2\gamma},

with the homogeneous dimensions
\mathcal{Q}=2n+2
of the Heisenberg group
H_{n}
. In particular, the author of
[
4
]
showed that the critical exponent
p_{c}^{*}(2,\gamma,\mathcal{Q})
lies in the global existence range.

Our main objective is to study problem (
1
) by imposing additional regularity
\dot{H}^{-\gamma}_{m}
on the initial data, with
m\in(1,2],\gamma\geq 0
and the method used in this work is based on
[
10
]
. Specifically, we determine the critical exponent for problem (
1
) to be

\displaystyle p_{c}(m,\gamma):=p_{F}\left(\frac{n}{m}+\gamma\right)=1+\frac{2m}{n+m\gamma},

that is, it shifts to the left on the number line compared to the critical exponent
p_{F}(n/m)

when initial data with
L^{m}
-regularity is considered.
Additionally, we show that the critical value
p=p_{c}(m,\gamma)
belongs to the global existence range. This is a new result compared to the work of Chen–Reissig
[
1
]
and extends several previously known results. It occurs because the nonlinearity
|u|^{p}
decays faster than the linear part at the spatial infinity. More precisely, we can see that
|u|^{p}\in L^{m_{1}}
(presented in Section
2
) with
m_{1}:=\max\{1,\frac{m}{p}\}<m
, however, the linear part of the solution belongs to the space
L^{m}
. Therefore, we can control the nonlinearity even when the exponent
p
takes the critical value. Additionally, the estimates for lifespan of blow-up solutions to (
1
) are also addressed in this work. Specifically, we denote by
T_{m,\gamma}(\varepsilon)
the so-called lifespan of a local (in time) solution, i.e. the
maximal existence time of local solutions. Then,

\varepsilon^{-\frac{2(p-1)}{2-(\frac{n}{m}+\gamma)(p-1)}}\lesssim T_{m,\gamma}(\varepsilon)\lesssim\varepsilon^{-\frac{2(p-1)}{2-(\frac{n}{m}+\gamma)(p-1)}}(\log(\varepsilon^{-1}))^{\frac{2(p-1)}{2-(\frac{n}{m}+\gamma)(p-1)}},

The result provides an improvement in the upper bound estimate compared to the lifespan estimate in
[
10
]
(see Remark
3.1
) for
\gamma=0
.

Main results.

Let us state the global (in time) existence of small data solutions, along
with the blow-up result, which will be proved in this paper.

Theorem 1.1

(Global existence)
.

Let
n\geq 1
,
m\in(1,2]
,
m\geq\frac{2(n-1)}{n+1}
,
s\in(1,2]
and
\gamma\in\left[0,\frac{n(m-1)}{m}\right)
. We assume that the exponent
p
satisfies
p>1+\displaystyle\frac{m\gamma}{n}
and

(4)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\vskip 8.5359pt1+\displaystyle\frac{2m}{n+m\gamma}\leq p<+\infty&\text{ if }n\leq 2s,\\
1+\displaystyle\frac{2m}{n+m\gamma}\leq p\leq 1+\frac{2}{n-2s}&\text{ if }n>2s.\end{cases}

Moreover, we fix
\beta_{m}:=(n-1)\left(\frac{1}{m}-\frac{1}{2}\right)
and assume that the initial data satisfies

(u_{0},u_{1})\in\mathcal{D}_{m,\gamma}(s):=(H^{s}\cap\dot{H}^{-\gamma}_{m}\cap H^{\beta_{m}}_{m})\times(H^{s-1}\cap\dot{H}_{m}^{-\gamma}\cap L^{m}).

Then, there exists a constant
\varepsilon_{0}>0
such that for any
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0}]
, problem (
1
) admits a unique global (in time) solution

\displaystyle u\in\mathcal{C}([0,\infty),H^{s}\cap L^{m}).

Furthermore, the following estimates hold for all
t>0
:

\displaystyle\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon(1+t)^{-\frac{n}{2}(\frac{1}{m}-\frac{1}{2})-\frac{\gamma}{2}}\|(u_{0},u_{1})\|_{\mathcal{D}_{m,\gamma}(s)},

\displaystyle\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon(1+t)^{-\frac{n}{2}(\frac{1}{m}-\frac{1}{2})-\frac{s+\gamma}{2}}\|(u_{0},u_{1})\|_{\mathcal{D}_{m,\gamma}(s)},

\displaystyle\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{m}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon(1+t)^{-\frac{\gamma}{2}}\|(u_{0},u_{1})\|_{\mathcal{D}_{m,\gamma}(s)}.

Remark 1.1
.

From Theorem
1.1
, it can be observed that the additional regularity
\dot{H}_{m}^{-\gamma}
imposed on the initial data results in an enhanced decay rate of the global solution.

Remark 1.2
.

Based on the statement of Theorem
1.1
, some conditions may be omitted in the following specific cases:

•

The condition
p>p_{1}:=1+\frac{m\gamma}{n}
is no longer required when
\gamma<\bar{\gamma}(m,n)
, where
\bar{\gamma}(m,n)
denotes the positive root of
m\bar{\gamma}^{2}+n\bar{\gamma}-2n=0
, that is,

\displaystyle\bar{\gamma}(m,n):=\frac{\sqrt{n^{2}+8mn}-n}{2m}.

In particular, when
1<m\leq\min\left\{2,\frac{n+2}{n}\right\}
, we have
\bar{\gamma}(m,n)\geq\frac{n(m-1)}{m}
. Therefore, under this constraint on
m
, the condition
p>p_{1}

also disappears.

•

We remark that the assumption
m\geq\frac{2(n-1)}{n+1}
implies
\beta_{m}\leq 1
. This condition disappears when considering low space dimensions
n=1,2,3
.

.

Let us give some examples for the admissible range of exponents
p

•

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\vskip 8.5359pt1+\displaystyle\frac{2m}{3+m\gamma}\leq p<+\infty&\text{ if }0\leq\gamma<\bar{\gamma}(m,3),\\

2.1.
Philosophy of our approach

To begin with, we can write the solution to
(\ref{Main.Eq.2})
by the formula

u^{\rm lin}(t,x)=\varepsilon(\mathcal{K}(t,x)+\partial_{t}\mathcal{K}(t,x))\ast_{x}u_{0}(x)+\varepsilon\mathcal{K}(t,x)\ast_{x}u_{1}(x),

so that the solution to
(\ref{Main.Eq.1})
becomes

\displaystyle u(t,x)

\displaystyle=u^{\rm lin}(t,x)+u^{\rm non}(t,x),

thanks to Duhamel’s principle, where

\displaystyle u^{\rm non}(t,x):=\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{K}(t-\tau,x)\ast_{x}|u(\tau,x)|^{p}d\tau

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}(t,x):=\begin{cases}\vskip 8.5359pt\mathfrak{F}^{-1}\left(\displaystyle\frac{e^{-\frac{t}{2}}\sinh{\left(t\sqrt{\frac{1}{4}-|\xi|^{2}}\right)}}{\sqrt{\frac{1}{4}-|\xi|^{2}}}\right)(t,x)&\text{ if }|\xi|\leq\displaystyle\frac{1}{2},\\
\displaystyle\mathfrak{F}^{-1}\left(\frac{e^{-\frac{t}{2}}\sin{\left(t\sqrt{|\xi|^{2}-\frac{1}{4}}\right)}}{\sqrt{|\xi|^{2}-\frac{1}{4}}}\right)(t,x)&\text{ if }|\xi|>\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}.\end{cases}

Let
\chi_{k}=\chi_{k}(r)
with
k\in\{\rm L,H\}
be smooth cut-off functions having the following properties:

\displaystyle\chi_{\rm L}(r)=\begin{cases}1&\quad\text{ if }r\leq\varepsilon^{*}/2,\\
0&\quad\text{ if }r\geq\varepsilon^{*},\end{cases}\text{ and }\qquad\chi_{\rm H}(r)=1-\chi_{\rm L}(r),

where
\varepsilon^{*}
is a sufficiently small positive constant.
It is obvious to see that
\chi_{\rm H}(r)=1
if
r\geq\varepsilon^{*}
and
\chi_{\rm H}(r)=0
if
r\leq\varepsilon^{*}/2
.

Now, we restate the important result as follows.

Lemma 2.1

(Linear Estimates)
.

Let
n\geq 1,\,1\leq\rho\leq q<\infty
,
q\neq 1
and
s_{1}\geq\max\{0,s_{2}\}
. We fix the quantity
\beta_{q}:=(n-1)\left|\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q}\right|
. Then, the following estimates hold for all
t>0
:

\displaystyle\||D|^{s_{1}}\mathcal{K}(t,x)\ast_{x}\varphi(x)\|_{L^{q}}

\displaystyle\quad\lesssim(1+t)^{-\frac{n}{2}(\frac{1}{\rho}-\frac{1}{q})-\frac{s_{1}-s_{2}}{2}}\||D|^{s_{2}}\chi_{\rm L}(|D|)\varphi\|_{L^{\rho}}+e^{-ct}\||D|^{s_{1}}\chi_{\rm H}(|D|)\varphi\|_{H_{q}^{\beta_{q}-1}},

\displaystyle\||D|^{s_{1}}\partial_{t}\mathcal{K}(t,x)\ast_{x}\varphi(x)\|_{L^{q}}

\displaystyle\quad\lesssim(1+t)^{-\frac{n}{2}(\frac{1}{\rho}-\frac{1}{q})-\frac{s_{1}-s_{2}}{2}-1}\||D|^{s_{2}}\chi_{\rm L}(|D|)\varphi\|_{L^{\rho}}+e^{-ct}\||D|^{s_{1}}\chi_{\rm H}(|D|)\varphi\|_{H_{q}^{\beta_{q}}},

where
c
is a suitable positive constant.

Proof.

Due to the similarity in the proof and the fact that the result is completely analogous to Theorem 1.1 in
[
10
]
, we present only the proof of the first estimate. Specifically, we rewrite the quantity
\widehat{\mathcal{K}}(t,\xi)
for
|\xi|\leq 1/2
as follows:

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathcal{K}}(t,\xi)=e^{-\frac{t}{2}+t\sqrt{\frac{1}{4}-|\xi|^{2}}}\,\,\frac{1-e^{-2t\sqrt{\frac{1}{4}-|\xi|^{2}}}}{2\sqrt{\frac{1}{4}-|\xi|^{2}}}=:\widehat{\mathcal{K}_{1}}(t,\xi)\widehat{\mathcal{K}_{2}}(t,\xi).

One can easily see that the following estimate holds:

\displaystyle|\partial_{\xi}^{\alpha}\widehat{\mathcal{K}_{2}}(t,\xi)|\lesssim|\xi|^{-|\alpha|}\text{ for all }|\alpha|\geq 0\text{ and }|\xi|\leq\varepsilon^{*}.

Therefore, thanks to Mikhlin–Hörmander multiplier theorem, we arrive at

\displaystyle\||D|^{s_{1}}\chi_{\rm L}(|D|)\mathcal{K}(t,x)\ast_{x}\varphi(x)\|_{L^{q}}\lesssim\||D|^{s_{1}}\chi_{\rm L}(|D|)\mathcal{K}_{1}(t,x)\ast_{x}\varphi(x)\|_{L^{q}}.

Using Lemma
4.1
for
\beta=2,\alpha=s_{1}-s_{2}
and

\displaystyle f(|\xi|)=|\xi|^{s_{1}-s_{2}},\quad g(|\xi|)=\frac{|\xi|^{2}}{\frac{1}{2}+\sqrt{\frac{1}{4}-|\xi|^{2}}},

it yields

\displaystyle\||D|^{s_{1}}\chi_{\rm L}(|D|)\mathcal{K}_{1}(t,x)\ast_{x}\varphi(x)\|_{L^{q}}\lesssim(1+t)^{-\frac{n}{2}(\frac{1}{\rho}-\frac{1}{q})-\frac{s_{1}-s_{2}}{2}}\||D|^{s_{2}}\chi_{\rm L}(|D|)\varphi\|_{L^{\rho}}.

Moreover, from Proposition 2.5 in
[
10
]
, we obtain

\displaystyle\||D|^{s_{1}}\chi_{\rm H}(|D|)\mathcal{K}(t,x)\ast_{x}\varphi(x)\|_{L^{q}}\lesssim e^{-ct}\||D|^{s_{1}}\chi_{\rm H}(|D|)\varphi\|_{H^{\beta_{q}-1}_{q}}.

This completes the proof.
∎

Proof of Theorem

Lemma 2.2
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
1.1
, we have

\displaystyle\||u|^{p}\|_{Y(T)}\lesssim\|u\|_{X(T)}^{p},

for all
T>0
.

Proof.

Applying Proposition
4.1
, we arrive at

\displaystyle\||u(t,\cdot)|^{p}\|_{L^{\eta}}=\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{\eta p}}^{p}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{m}}^{p(1-\omega_{0})}\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}^{p\omega_{0}}

(8)

\displaystyle\lesssim(1+t)^{-\frac{n}{2}(\frac{p}{m}-\frac{1}{\eta})-\frac{p\gamma}{2}}\|u\|_{X(T)}^{p},

for all
\eta\in[\eta_{1},2]
, where we note that

\displaystyle\omega_{0}:=\frac{\frac{1}{m}-\frac{1}{\eta p}}{\frac{1}{m}-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{s}{n}}\in[0,1],

due to
p\leq 1+\frac{2}{n-2s}
if
n>2s
and
s\in(1,2]
. Next,
we will estimate the quantity
\||u(t,\cdot)|^{p}\|_{\dot{H}^{s-1}}
with
s\in(1,2]
. Specifically, thanks to Propositions
4.1
and
4.2
we obtain the following estimates:

\displaystyle\||u(t,\cdot)|^{p}\|_{\dot{H}^{s-1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{r_{1}}}^{p-1}\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{\dot{H}^{s-1}_{r_{2}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}^{(p-1)(1-\omega_{1})+1-\omega_{2}}\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}^{(p-1)\omega_{1}+\omega_{2}}

(9)

\displaystyle\lesssim(1+t)^{-\frac{n}{2}(\frac{p}{m}-\frac{1}{2})-\frac{p\gamma}{2}-\frac{s-1}{2}}\|u\|_{X(T)}^{p},

provided that there exist parameters
r_{1},r_{2}
satisfying

\omega_{1}=\frac{n}{s}\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{r_{1}}\right)\in[0,1],\qquad\omega_{2}=\frac{n}{s}\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{r_{2}}+\frac{s-1}{n}\right)\in\left[\frac{s-1}{s},1\right]

and

\frac{1}{2}=\frac{p-1}{r_{1}}+\frac{1}{r_{2}}.

From these, due to
p\leq 1+\frac{2}{n-2s}
if
n>2s
and
s\in(1,2]
, we can choose

\displaystyle\frac{1}{r_{1}}=\begin{cases}\vskip 8.5359pt\displaystyle\frac{\delta}{p-1}&\text{ if }n\leq 2s,\\
\displaystyle\frac{n-2s}{2n}&\text{ if }n>2s\end{cases}\quad\text{ and }\quad\frac{1}{r_{2}}=\begin{cases}\vskip 8.5359pt\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}-\delta&\text{ if }n\leq 2s,\\
\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}-\displaystyle\frac{(p-1)(n-2s)}{2n}&\text{ if }n>2s,\end{cases}

where
\delta
is a sufficiently small positive constant. Under these values, we immediately obtain estimate (
9
). Combining estimates (
8
) and (
9
), we conclude Lemma
2.2
.
∎

Proposition 2.1
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
1.1
, we obtain the following estimate for all
u\in X(T)
and
T>0
:

\displaystyle\|u^{\rm non}\|_{X(T)}\lesssim\|u\|_{X(T)}^{p}.

Proof.

Firstly, the conditions
p>1+\frac{m\gamma}{n}
and
0\leq\gamma<\frac{n(m-1)}{m}
lead to the fact that there exists a parameter
\eta_{2}
satisfies

\displaystyle\eta_{1}<\eta_{2}<\frac{mn}{n+m\gamma}\leq m.

We fix

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\nu}:=\frac{1}{\eta_{2}}-\frac{\gamma}{n},\text{ that is, }\nu\in(1,m).

Applying Lemma
2.1
with
\rho=\nu,q=2,(s_{1},s_{2})=(0,-\gamma)
for
\tau\in[0,t/2)
and
\rho=q=2,(s_{1},s_{2})=(0,0)
for
\tau\in[t/2,t]
to derive

\displaystyle\|u^{\rm non}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{t/2}(1+t-\tau)^{-\frac{n}{2}(\frac{1}{\nu}-\frac{1}{2})-\frac{\gamma}{2}}\||u(\tau,\cdot)|^{p}\|_{\dot{H}^{-\gamma}_{\nu}\cap L^{2}}d\tau

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt+\int_{t/2}^{t}\||u(\tau,\cdot)|^{p}\|_{L^{2}}d\tau.

Thanks to Proposition
4.3
and Lemma
2.2
, we arrive at

\displaystyle\||u(\tau,\cdot)|^{p}\|_{\dot{H}_{\nu}^{-\gamma}}\lesssim\||u(\tau,\cdot)|^{p}\|_{L^{\eta_{2}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim(1+\tau)^{-\frac{n}{2}(\frac{p}{m}-\frac{1}{\eta_{2}})-\frac{p\gamma}{2}}\||u|^{p}\|_{Y(T)}

(10)

\displaystyle\lesssim(1+\tau)^{-\frac{n}{2}(\frac{p}{m}-\frac{1}{\eta_{2}})-\frac{p\gamma}{2}}\|u\|_{X(T)}^{p}

and

\displaystyle\||u(\tau,\cdot)|^{p}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim(1+\tau)^{-\frac{n}{2}(\frac{p}{m}-\frac{1}{2})-\frac{p\gamma}{2}}\|u\|_{X(T)}^{p}.

Therefore, we obtain

\displaystyle\|u^{\rm non}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim(1+t)^{-\frac{n}{2}(\frac{1}{\nu}-\frac{1}{2})-\frac{\gamma}{2}}\|u\|_{X(T)}^{p}\int_{0}^{t/2}(1+\tau)^{-\frac{n}{2}(\frac{p}{m}-\frac{1}{\eta_{2}})-\frac{p\gamma}{2}}d\tau

\displaystyle\qquad+(1+t)^{-\frac{n}{2}(\frac{p}{m}-\frac{1}{2})-\frac{p\gamma}{2}}\|u\|_{X(T)}^{p}\int_{t/2}^{t}d\tau

\displaystyle=:(M_{1}(t)+M_{2}(t))\|u\|_{X(T)}^{p}.

For the quantity
M_{1}(t)
, we proceed with the following estimate.

•

If

-\frac{n}{2}\left(\frac{p}{m}-\frac{1}{\eta_{2}}\right)-\frac{p\gamma}{2}\leq-1,

we immediately arrive at

\displaystyle M_{1}(t)

\displaystyle\lesssim(1+t)^{-\frac{n}{2}(\frac{1}{\nu}-\frac{1}{2})-\frac{\gamma}{2}}\log(e+t)

\displaystyle\lesssim(1+t)^{-\frac{n}{2}(\frac{1}{m}-\frac{1}{2})-\frac{\gamma}{2}},

due to
\nu\in(1,m)
.

•

Otherwise, we also compute

\displaystyle M_{1}(t)

\displaystyle\lesssim(1+t)^{1-\frac{n}{2}(\frac{p}{m}-\frac{1}{\eta_{2}}+\frac{1}{\nu}-\frac{1}{2})-\frac{(p+1)\gamma}{2}}

\displaystyle=(1+t)^{1-\frac{n}{2}(\frac{p}{m}-\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\gamma}{n})-\frac{(p+1)\gamma}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim(1+t)^{-\frac{n}{2}(\frac{1}{m}-\frac{1}{2})-\frac{\gamma}{2}},

due to it is the fact that

(11)

\displaystyle p\geq 1+\frac{2m}{n+m\gamma}\text{ implies }1-\frac{n}{2m}(p-1)-\frac{(p-1)\gamma}{2}\leq 0.

Thus, we can conclude
M_{1}(t)\lesssim(1+t)^{-\frac{n}{2}(\frac{1}{m}-\frac{1}{2})-\frac{\gamma}{2}}
for all
t>0
. Similarly, noting that we can estimate
M_{2}(t)

as follows:

\displaystyle M_{2}(t)

\displaystyle\lesssim(1+t)^{1-\frac{n}{2}(\frac{p}{m}-\frac{1}{2})-\frac{p\gamma}{2}}\lesssim(1+t)^{-\frac{n}{2}(\frac{1}{m}-\frac{1}{2})-\frac{\gamma}{2}}.

As a result, the combination of the estimates for
M_{1}(t)

and
M_{2}(t)
yields

(12)

\displaystyle\|u^{\rm non}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim(1+t)^{-\frac{n}{2}(\frac{1}{m}-\frac{1}{2})-\frac{\gamma}{2}}\|u\|_{X(T)}^{p}.

Next, thanks to again Lemma
2.1
with
\rho=\nu,q=2,(s_{1},s_{2})=(s,-\gamma)
for
\tau\in[0,t/2)
, and
\rho=q=2,(s_{1},s_{2})=(s,s-1)
to get

\displaystyle\|u^{\rm non}(t,\cdot)\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{t/2}(1+t-\tau)^{-\frac{n}{2}(\frac{1}{\nu}-\frac{1}{2})-\frac{s+\gamma}{2}}\||u(\tau,\cdot)|^{p}\|_{\dot{H}^{-\gamma}_{\nu}\cap\dot{H}^{s-1}}d\tau

\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{t/2}^{t}(1+t-\tau)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\||u(\tau,\cdot)|^{p}\|_{\dot{H}^{s-1}}d\tau.

It is a fact that

\displaystyle\||u(\tau,\cdot)|^{p}\|_{\dot{H}^{s-1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim(1+\tau)^{-\frac{n}{2}(\frac{p}{m}-\frac{1}{2})-\frac{p\gamma}{2}-\frac{s-1}{2}}\||u|^{p}\|_{Y(T)}

\displaystyle\lesssim(1+\tau)^{-\frac{n}{2}(\frac{p}{m}-\frac{1}{2})-\frac{p\gamma}{2}-\frac{s-1}{2}}\|u\|_{X(T)}^{p}.

Combining with the estimate (
10
) leads to

\displaystyle\|u^{\rm non}(t,\cdot)\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}

\displaystyle\lesssim(1+t)^{-\frac{n}{2}(\frac{1}{\nu}-\frac{1}{2})-\frac{s+\gamma}{2}}\|u\|_{X(T)}^{p}\int_{0}^{t/2}(1+\tau)^{-\frac{n}{2}(\frac{p}{m}-\frac{1}{\eta_{2}})-\frac{p\gamma}{2}}d\tau

\displaystyle\qquad+(1+t)^{-\frac{n}{2}(\frac{p}{m}-\frac{1}{2})-\frac{p\gamma}{2}-\frac{s-1}{2}}\|u\|_{X(T)}^{p}\int_{t/2}^{t}(1+t-\tau)^{-\frac{1}{2}}d\tau

\displaystyle=:(M_{3}(t)+M_{4}(t))\|u\|_{X(T)}^{p}.

One can easily see that

\displaystyle M_{3}(t)=(1+t)^{-\frac{s}{2}}M_{1}(t)\lesssim(1+t)^{-\frac{n}{2}(\frac{1}{m}-\frac{1}{2})-\frac{s+\gamma}{2}}.

Moreover, we also have

\displaystyle M_{4}(t)\lesssim(1+t)^{1-\frac{n}{2}(\frac{p}{m}-\frac{1}{2})-\frac{p\gamma}{2}-\frac{s}{2}}\lesssim(1+t)^{-\frac{n}{2}(\frac{1}{m}-\frac{1}{2})-\frac{s+\gamma}{2}},

thanks to the relation (
11
). Thus, we can conclude that

(13)

\displaystyle\|u^{\rm non}(t,\cdot)\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}\lesssim(1+t)^{-\frac{n}{2}(\frac{1}{m}-\frac{1}{2})-\frac{s+\gamma}{2}}\|u\|^{p}_{X(T)}.

Finally, we need to estimate the norm
\|u^{\rm non}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{m}}
. Once again, we apply Lemma
2.1
with
\rho=\nu,q=m,(s_{1},s_{2})=(0,-\gamma)
for
\tau\in[0,t/2)
, and
\rho=q=m,(s_{1},s_{2})=(0,0)
for
\tau\in[t/2,t]
to obtain

\displaystyle\|u^{\rm non}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{m}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{t/2}(1+t-\tau)^{-\frac{n}{2}(\frac{1}{\nu}-\frac{1}{m})-\frac{\gamma}{2}}\||u(\tau,\cdot)|^{p}\|_{\dot{H}_{\nu}^{-\gamma}\cap H^{\beta_{m}-1}_{m}}d\tau

\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{t/2}^{t}\||u(\tau,\cdot)|^{p}\|_{L^{m}\cap H^{\beta_{m}-1}_{m}}d\tau.

Due to
0\leq\beta_{m}\leq 1
and
\eta_{1}<m\leq 2
, it holds

\displaystyle\||u(\tau,\cdot)|^{p}\|_{L^{m}\cap H^{\beta_{m}-1}_{m}}\lesssim\||u(\tau,\cdot)|^{p}\|_{L^{m}}

\displaystyle\lesssim(1+\tau)^{-\frac{n}{2m}(p-1)-\frac{p\gamma}{2}}\||u|^{p}\|_{Y(T)}

\displaystyle\lesssim(1+\tau)^{-\frac{n}{2m}(p-1)-\frac{p\gamma}{2}}\|u\|_{X(T)}^{p}.

Combining with the estimate (
10
), we can conclude that

\displaystyle\|u^{\rm non}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{m}}

\displaystyle\lesssim(1+t)^{-\frac{n}{2}(\frac{1}{\nu}-\frac{1}{m})-\frac{\gamma}{2}}\|u\|_{X(T)}^{p}\int_{0}^{t/2}(1+\tau)^{-\frac{n}{2}(\frac{p}{m}-\frac{1}{\eta_{2}})-\frac{p\gamma}{2}}d\tau

\displaystyle\qquad+\|u\|_{X(T)}^{p}\int_{t/2}^{t}(1+\tau)^{-\frac{n}{2m}(p-1)-\frac{p\gamma}{2}}d\tau

\displaystyle=:(M_{5}(t)+M_{6}(t))\|u\|_{X(T)}^{p}.

For the quantity
M_{5}(t)
, proceeding in the same way as for
M_{1}(t)
with
\nu<m
, we immediately obtain

M_{5}(t)\lesssim(1+t)^{-\frac{\gamma}{2}}.

Moreover, one can easily see that the following estimate for
M_{6}(t)
:

\displaystyle M_{6}(t)\lesssim(1+t)^{1-\frac{n}{2m}(p-1)-\frac{p\gamma}{2}}\lesssim(1+t)^{-\frac{\gamma}{2}}.

Thus, we obtain

(14)

\displaystyle\|u^{\rm non}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{m}}\lesssim(1+t)^{-\frac{\gamma}{2}}\|u\|_{X(T)}^{p}.

The estimates (
12
)-(

) complete the proof of Proposition

.

Proposition 2.2
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
1.1
, we obtain the following estimate for all
u,v\in X(T)
and
T>0
:

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{N}[u]-\mathcal{N}[v]\|_{Z(T)}\lesssim\|u-v\|_{Z(T)}\left(\|u\|_{X(T)}^{p-1}+\|v\|_{X(T)}^{p-1}\right).

Proof.

To begin with, applying Lemma
2.1
with
\rho=\sigma\in(1,2),q=2
and
(s_{1},s_{2})=(0,-\gamma)
we obtain

\displaystyle\left\|\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{K}(t-\tau,x)\ast_{x}\left(|u(\tau,x)|^{p}-|v(\tau,x)|^{p}\right)d\tau\right\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt\lesssim\int_{0}^{t}(1+t-\tau)^{-\frac{n}{2}(\frac{1}{\sigma}-\frac{1}{2})-\frac{\gamma}{2}}\left\||u(\tau,\cdot)|^{p}-|v(\tau,\cdot)|^{p}\right\|_{\dot{H}_{\sigma}^{-\gamma}\cap H^{-1}}d\tau.

Using Proposition
4.3
, one has

\displaystyle\left\||u(\tau,\cdot)|^{p}-|v(\tau,\cdot)|^{p}\right\|_{\dot{H}_{\sigma}^{-\gamma}}\lesssim\left\||u(\tau,\cdot)|^{p}-|v(\tau,\cdot)|^{p}\right\|_{L^{\sigma_{1}}}\text{ with }\frac{1}{\sigma_{1}}=\frac{1}{\sigma}+\frac{\gamma}{n}.

Moreover, we also have the embedding

\displaystyle\left\||u(\tau,\cdot)|^{p}-|v(\tau,\cdot)|^{p}\right\|_{H^{-1}}\lesssim\left\||u(\tau,\cdot)|^{p}-|v(\tau,\cdot)|^{p}\right\|_{L^{\sigma_{2}}}\text{ with }\frac{1}{2}\leq\frac{1}{\sigma_{2}}\leq\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{n}.

Next, thanks to Hölder’s inequality and Proposition
4.1
for
j=1,2
, we arrive at

\displaystyle\left\||u(\tau,\cdot)|^{p}-|v(\tau,\cdot)|^{p}\right\|_{L^{\sigma_{j}}}

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\|u(\tau,\cdot)-v(\tau,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}\left(\|u(\tau,\cdot)\|_{L^{\mu_{j}(p-1)}}^{p-1}+\|v(\tau,\cdot)\|_{L^{\mu_{j}(p-1)}}^{p-1}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\lesssim(1+\tau)^{-\frac{n}{2}(\frac{p-1}{m}-\frac{1}{\mu_{j}})-\frac{p\gamma}{2}}\|u-v\|_{Z(T)}\left(\|u\|_{X(T)}^{p-1}+\|v\|_{X(T)}^{p-1}\right),

where

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sigma_{1}}=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{\mu_{1}};\quad\frac{1}{\sigma_{2}}=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{\mu_{2}}

and

\displaystyle\frac{\displaystyle\frac{1}{m}-\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mu_{1}(p-1)}}{\displaystyle\frac{1}{m}-\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}+\displaystyle\frac{s}{n}}\in[0,1];\quad\frac{n}{s}\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{\mu_{2}(p-1)}\right)\in[0,1].

In summary, the conditions on the parameters
\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}

are summarized as follows:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\vskip 8.5359pt&\sigma_{1}>1,\sigma\in(1,2),\\
\vskip 8.5359pt&\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sigma_{1}}=\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}+\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mu_{1}}=\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sigma}+\displaystyle\frac{\gamma}{n},\\
&\frac{\displaystyle\frac{1}{m}-\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mu_{1}(p-1)}}{\displaystyle\frac{1}{m}-\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}+\displaystyle\frac{s}{n}}\in[0,1]\end{cases}\quad\text{ and }\quad\begin{cases}\vskip 8.5359pt&\sigma_{2}\in(1,2),\\
\vskip 8.5359pt&\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sigma_{2}}=\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}+\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mu_{2}}\leq\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{n},\\
&\displaystyle\frac{n}{s}\left(\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}-\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mu_{2}(p-1)}\right)\in[0,1].\end{cases}

To satisfy the required conditions, we choose the parameters
\mu_{1},\mu_{2}
as follows:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mu_{1}}=\begin{cases}\vskip 8.5359pt\displaystyle\frac{\gamma}{n}+\delta&\text{ if }n\leq 2s,\\
\max\left\{\displaystyle\frac{\gamma}{n},\,\,\displaystyle\frac{(p-1)(n-2s)}{2n}\right\}+\delta&\text{ if }n>2s\end{cases}

and

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mu_{2}}=\begin{cases}\vskip 8.5359pt\delta&\text{ if }n\leq 2s,\\
\displaystyle\frac{(p-1)(n-2s)}{2n}&\text{ if }n>2s,\end{cases}

where
\delta
is a sufficiently small positive constant.
Thus, the parameter
\sigma
is given

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sigma}=\begin{cases}\vskip 8.5359pt\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}+\delta&\text{ if }n\leq 2s,\\
\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}-\displaystyle\frac{\gamma}{n}+\max\left\{\displaystyle\frac{\gamma}{n},\,\,\displaystyle\frac{(p-1)(n-2s)}{2n}\right\}+\delta&\text{ if }n>2s.\end{cases}

The conditions
p>1+\frac{m\gamma}{n}
and
p\leq 1+\frac{2}{n-2s}
if
n>2s
implies
\sigma\in(1,2)
.
In conclusion, the following estimates is established:

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{N}[u]-\mathcal{N}[v]\|_{Z(T)}

\displaystyle\hskip 8.5359pt=\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left\{(1+t)^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}\left\|\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{K}(t-\tau,x)\ast_{x}\left(|u(\tau,x)|^{p}-|v(\tau,x)|^{p}\right)d\tau\right\|_{L^{2}}\right\}

\displaystyle\hskip 8.5359pt\lesssim\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left\{(1+t)^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}\int_{0}^{t}(1+t-\tau)^{-\frac{n}{2}(\frac{1}{\sigma}-\frac{1}{2})-\frac{\gamma}{2}}\sum_{j=1,2}(1+\tau)^{-\frac{n}{2}(\frac{p-1}{m}-\frac{1}{\mu_{j}})-\frac{p\gamma}{2}}d\tau\right\}

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt\times\|u-v\|_{Z(T)}\left(\|u\|_{X(T)}^{p-1}+\|v\|_{X(T)}^{p-1}\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 8.5359pt\lesssim\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left\{(1+t)^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}\int_{0}^{t}(1+t-\tau)^{-\frac{n}{2}(\frac{1}{\sigma}-\frac{1}{2})-\frac{\gamma}{2}}(1+\tau)^{-\frac{n}{2}(\frac{p-1}{m}-\frac{1}{\mu_{1}})-\frac{p\gamma}{2}}d\tau\right\}

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt\times\|u-v\|_{Z(T)}\left(\|u\|_{X(T)}^{p-1}+\|v\|_{X(T)}^{p-1}\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 8.5359pt\lesssim\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\{\mathcal{J}_{1}(t)+\mathcal{J}_{2}(t)\}\|u-v\|_{Z(T)}\left(\|u\|_{X(T)}^{p-1}+\|v\|_{X(T)}^{p-1}\right),

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{1}(t)

\displaystyle:=(1+t)^{-\frac{n}{2}(\frac{1}{\sigma}-\frac{1}{2})}\int_{0}^{t/2}(1+\tau)^{-\frac{n}{2}(\frac{p-1}{m}-\frac{1}{\mu_{1}})-\frac{p\gamma}{2}}d\tau,

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{2}(t)

\displaystyle:=(1+t)^{-\frac{n}{2}(\frac{p-1}{m}-\frac{1}{\mu_{1}})-\frac{(p-1)\gamma}{2}}\int_{t/2}^{t}(1+t-\tau)^{-\frac{n}{2}(\frac{1}{\sigma}-\frac{1}{2})-\frac{\gamma}{2}}d\tau.

To estimate the quantity
\mathcal{J}_{1}(t)
, we consider the following two cases:

•

If

-\frac{n}{2}\left(\frac{p-1}{m}-\frac{1}{\mu_{1}}\right)-\frac{p\gamma}{2}\leq-1,

it then follows immediately that

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{1}(t)\lesssim(1+t)^{-\frac{n}{2}(\frac{1}{\sigma}-\frac{1}{2})}\log(e+t)\lesssim 1,

for all
t>0
, due to
\sigma\in(1,2)
.

•

Otherwise, we also compute

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{1}(t)

\displaystyle\lesssim(1+t)^{1-\frac{n}{2}(\frac{p-1}{m}-\frac{1}{\mu_{1}}+\frac{1}{\sigma}-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\gamma}{n})-\frac{(p-1)\gamma}{2}}

\displaystyle=(1+t)^{1-\frac{n}{2m}(p-1)-\frac{(p-1)\gamma}{2}}\lesssim 1,

for all
t>0
, due to the relation (
11
).

Similarly for
\mathcal{J}_{2}(t)
, we also consider the following two cases:

•

If

-\frac{n}{2}\left(\frac{1}{\sigma}-\frac{1}{2}\right)-\frac{\gamma}{2}\leq-1,

it then follows immediately that

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{2}(t)\lesssim(1+t)^{-\frac{n}{2}(\frac{p-1}{m}-\frac{1}{\mu_{1}})-\frac{(p-1)\gamma}{2}}\log(e+t)\lesssim 1,

for all
t>0
, due to
\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mu_{1}}<\frac{p-1}{m}
.

•

Otherwise, we also compute

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{2}(t)

\displaystyle\lesssim(1+t)^{1-\frac{n}{2m}(p-1)-\frac{(p-1)\gamma}{2}}\lesssim 1,

for all
t>0
.

This completes the proof of Proposition
2.2
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Using again Lemma
2.1
, it is the fact that

\displaystyle\|u^{\rm lin}\|_{X(T)}\lesssim\|(u_{0},u_{1})\|_{\mathcal{D}_{m,\gamma}(s)},

for all
T>0
.
For this reason, combining with Propositions
2.1
and
2.2
, we obtain the following two important estimates for all
u,v\in X(T)
:

(15)

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{N}[u]\|_{X(T)}

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}\varepsilon\|(u_{0},u_{1})\|_{\mathcal{D}_{m,\gamma}(s)}+C_{1}\|u\|_{X(T)}^{p}

(16)

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{N}[u]-\mathcal{N}[v]\|_{Z(T)}

\displaystyle\leq C_{2}\|u-v\|_{Z(T)}\left(\|u\|_{X(T)}^{p-1}+\|v\|_{X(T)}^{p-1}\right).

Let us fix

(17)

\displaystyle M:=2C_{1}\|(u_{0},u_{1})\|_{\mathcal{D}_{m,\gamma}(s)}

and the parameter
\varepsilon_{0}
satisfies

\displaystyle\max\{C_{1},2C_{2}\}M^{p-1}\varepsilon_{0}^{p-1}\leq\frac{1}{2}.

Next, we denote that

\displaystyle X(T,M):=\{\varphi\in X(T),\|\varphi\|_{X(T)}\leq M\}.

Taking the recurrence sequence
\{u_{j}\}_{j}\subset X(T)
with
u_{-1}=0;\,u_{j}=\mathcal{N}[u_{j-1}]
for
j=0,1,2,...
, we employ estimate (
15
) to conclude that

\|u_{j}\|_{X(T)}\leq M\varepsilon,

for all
j\in\mathbb{N}
and
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0}]
.
Moreover, using estimate (
16
), it is a fact that

\displaystyle\|u_{j+1}-u_{j}\|_{Z(T)}

\displaystyle\leq C_{2}M^{p-1}\varepsilon^{p-1}\|u_{j}-u_{j-1}\|_{Z(T)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\|u_{j}-u_{j-1}\|_{Z(T)},

so that
\{u_{j}\}_{j}\subset X(T,M\varepsilon)
is a Cauchy sequence in the Banach
space
Z(T)
. Furthermore,
X(T,M\varepsilon)
is a closed subset of
Z(T)
with respect to the metric
Z(T)
(see Lemma A.1 in
[
10
]
). For this reason, we have a solution
u=\mathcal{N}[u]
in
X(T,M\varepsilon)
for all
T>0
. Since
T
is arbitrary,
u
is a global (in time) solution to (
1
), that is,
u\in X(\infty,M\varepsilon)
.

Next, we show that
u\in\mathcal{C}([0,\infty),H^{s}\cap L^{m})
. Recalling the integral equation

\displaystyle u(t,x)

\displaystyle=\varepsilon(\mathcal{K}(t,x)+\partial_{t}\mathcal{K}(t,x))\ast_{x}u_{0}(x)+\varepsilon\mathcal{K}(t,x)\ast_{x}u_{1}(x)

\displaystyle\hskip 85.35826pt+\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{K}(t-\tau)\ast_{x}|u(\tau,x)|^{p}d\tau

\displaystyle=u^{\rm lin}(t,x)+u^{\rm non}(t,x),

we can easily see that the linear part of the solution obviously satisfies this property. Therefore, it suffices to
show that

u^{\rm non}\in\mathcal{C}([0,\infty),H^{s}\cap L^{m}).

Using Lemma
2.1
and the proof of Proposition
2.1
, we obtain

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{K}(t-\tau,x)\ast_{x}|u(\tau,x)|^{p}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim(1+\tau)^{-\frac{n}{2}(\frac{p}{m}-\frac{1}{2})-\frac{p\gamma}{2}}\|u\|_{X(\infty)}^{p},

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{K}(t-\tau,x)\ast_{x}|u(\tau,x)|^{p}\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}

\displaystyle\lesssim(1+\tau)^{-\frac{n}{2}(\frac{p}{m}-\frac{1}{2})-\frac{p\gamma}{2}}\|u\|_{X(\infty)}^{p},

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{K}(t-\tau,x)\ast_{x}|u(\tau,x)|^{p}\|_{L^{m}}

\displaystyle\lesssim(1+\tau)^{-\frac{n}{2m}(p-1)-\frac{p\gamma}{2}}\|u\|_{X(\infty)}^{p},

for all
t>0
,
\tau\in[0,t]
and the right-hand sides are bounded independently
of
t
. Therefore, we can apply the dominated convergence theorem in the Bochner
integral and thus the continuity holds.

Finally, we establish the uniqueness of the global solution

u
, which lies in the space
\mathcal{C}([0,\infty),H^{s}\cap L^{m})
. Let
u,v
are global solutions to (
1
) belonging to this space. Using again the arbitrary positive number
T
and performing the same proof steps of Proposition
2.2
we obtain

\displaystyle\|u-v\|_{Z(t)}\lesssim\int_{0}^{t}\|u-v\|_{Z(\tau)}\big(\|u\|_{X(\tau)}^{p-1}+\|v\|_{X(\tau)}^{p-1}\big)d\tau,

for all
t\in[0,T]
.
One can easily see that there exists a constant
M(T)
such that
\|u\|_{X(T)}^{p-1}+\|v\|_{X(T)}^{p-1}\leq M(T)
. Therefore, we immetiately have

\displaystyle\|u-v\|_{Z(t)}\lesssim M(T)\int_{0}^{t}\|u-v\|_{Z(\tau)}d\tau,

for all
t\in[0,T]
.
From this, the
Gronwall inequality implies
u\equiv v
on
[0,T]
. Because
T
is an arbitrary positive number,
u\equiv v
on
[0,\infty)
. Hence, the proof of Theorem
1.1
is completed.

3.
Blow-up result and estimates for lifespan

3.1.
Philosophy of our approach

In this section, our aim is to prove Theorem
1.2
. To prove our result, we recall the definition of weak solution to (
1
).

Definition 3.1
.

Let
p>1
,
T>0
. We say that
u\in\mathcal{C}([0,T],L^{2})
is a local weak solution to (
1
) if for any function
\phi(t,x)=\eta(t)\varphi(x)
, where
\eta\in\mathcal{C}_{0}^{\infty}([0,\infty))
and
\varphi\in\mathcal{C}_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
it holds

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|u(t,x)|^{p}\phi(t,x)dxdt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\partial_{t}^{2}-\Delta+\partial_{t})u(t,x)\phi(t,x)dxdt.

If
T=\infty
, we say that
u\in\mathcal{C}([0,\infty),L^{2})
is a global weak solution to (
1
).

Let us introduce the test functions
\eta=\eta(t)
and
\varphi=\varphi(x)
satisfying the following properties:

\displaystyle 1.\quad\eta\in\mathcal{C}_{0}^{\infty}([0,\infty))\text{ and }\eta(t)=\begin{cases}1\quad\text{ for }0\leq t\leq 1/2,\\
0\quad\text{ for }t\geq 1,\end{cases}

\displaystyle 2.\quad\varphi\in\mathcal{C}_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\text{ and }\varphi(x)=\begin{cases}1\quad\text{ for }|x|\leq 1/2,\\
0\quad\text{ for }|x|\geq 1,\end{cases}

\displaystyle 3.\quad\eta^{-\frac{p^{\prime}}{p}}\big(|\eta^{\prime}|^{p^{\prime}}+|\eta^{\prime\prime}|^{p^{\prime}}\big)\text{ and }\varphi^{-\frac{p^{\prime}}{p}}|\Delta\varphi|^{p^{\prime}}\text{ is bounded. }

In addition, we suppose that
\eta(t)
is a decreasing
function and that
\varphi(x)
is a radial function fulfilling
\varphi(x)\leq\varphi(y)
for any
|x|\geq|y|
.
Let
R\geq 1
, we define the following test function:

\Phi_{R}(t,x):=\eta_{R}(t)\varphi_{R}(x),

where
\eta_{R}(t):=\eta(R^{-2}t)
and
\varphi_{R}(x):=\varphi(R^{-1}x)
. Moreover, we define the functional

\mathcal{I}_{R}:=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|u(t,x)|^{p}\Phi_{R}(t,x)dxdt=\int_{0}^{R^{2}}\int_{|x|\leq R}|u(t,x)|^{p}\Phi_{R}(t,x)dxdt.

3.2.
Proof of Theorem
1.2

At first, we consider the set

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{\gamma}

\displaystyle:=\bigg\{(u_{0},u_{1})\text{ such that }\,\,u_{j}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R},\,j\in\{0,1\}

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt\text{ and }u_{0}(x)+u_{1}(x)\gtrsim\varphi(x):=\langle x\rangle^{-n(\frac{1}{m}+\frac{\gamma}{n})}(\log(e+|x|))^{-1}\bigg\}.

Using Proposition
4.3
, one has
L^{\frac{nm}{n+m\gamma}}\subset\dot{H}_{m}^{-\gamma}
, for all
\gamma\in\left[0,\frac{n(m-1)}{m}\right)
. Moreover, due to
\varphi(x)\in L^{\frac{nm}{n+m\gamma}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, we can conclude that

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{\gamma}\cap\left(\dot{H}_{m}^{-\gamma}\times\dot{H}_{m}^{-\gamma}\right)\neq\emptyset,

for all
\gamma\in\left[0,\frac{n(m-1)}{m}\right)
.
Next, we assume that
u\in\mathcal{C}([0,\infty),L^{2})
is a global weak solution to (
1
). From Definition
3.1
, we replace the function
\phi(t,x)
by
\Phi_{R}(t,x)
. Then, we perform integration by parts to derive

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|u(t,x)|^{p}\Phi_{R}(t,x)dxdt+\varepsilon\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(u_{0}(x)+u_{1}(x))\varphi_{R}(x)dx

(18)

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}u(t,x)(\partial_{t}^{2}-\partial_{t}-\Delta)\Phi_{R}(t,x)dxdt.

Applying Hölder’s inequality with
1/p+1/p^{\prime}=1
and using the change of variables
\tilde{t}=R^{-2}t
,
\tilde{x}=R^{-1}x
, we may estimate the right hand-side of (
18
) as follows:

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|u(t,x)(\partial_{t}^{2}-\partial_{t}-\Delta)(\eta_{R}(t)\varphi_{R}(x))\right|dxdt

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt\leq\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|u(t,x)|^{p}\Phi_{R}(t,x)dxdt\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt\times\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|\eta_{R}(t)\varphi_{R}(x)\right|^{-\frac{p^{\prime}}{p}}\left|(\partial_{t}^{2}-\partial_{t}-\Delta)(\eta_{R}(t)\varphi_{R}(x))\right|^{p^{\prime}}dxdt\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt\leq C\mathcal{I}_{R}^{\frac{1}{p}}R^{\frac{n+2}{p^{\prime}}-2}.

It follows immediately from (
18
) that

\displaystyle\varepsilon\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(u_{0}(x)+u_{1}(x))\varphi_{R}(x)dx

\displaystyle\leq C\mathcal{I}_{R}^{\frac{1}{p}}R^{\frac{n+2}{p^{\prime}}-2}-\mathcal{I}_{R}

(19)

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}R^{n+2-2p^{\prime}}.

On the other hand, from assumption (
5
), we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(u_{0}(x)+u_{1}(x))\varphi_{R}(x)dx

\displaystyle\geq\int_{|x|\leq R/2}\big(u_{0}(x)+u_{1}(x)\big)dx

\displaystyle\geq C\int_{R/4\leq|x|\leq R/2}|x|^{-n(\frac{1}{m}+\frac{\gamma}{n})}(\log(e+|x|))^{-1}dx

\displaystyle\geq CR^{n-\frac{n}{m}-\gamma}(\log R)^{-1}.

This implies

\displaystyle C\varepsilon R^{n-\frac{n}{m}-\gamma}(\log R)^{-1}\leq C_{1}R^{n+2-2p^{\prime}},

that is,

(20)

\displaystyle\varepsilon\lesssim R^{\frac{n}{m}+\gamma+2-2p^{\prime}}\log R,

for all
R\geq 1
.
Moreover, we note that

\displaystyle p<1+\frac{2m}{n+m\gamma}\text{ implies }\frac{n}{m}+\gamma+2-2p^{\prime}<0.

Letting
R\to\infty
, we immediately obtain that the right-hand side of (
20
) tends to
0
. Hence, inequality (
20
) leads to a contradiction. This completes the proof of Theorem
1.2
.

3.3.
Estimates for lifespan

In this subsection, we will summarize how to get some estimates for lifespan
T_{m,\gamma}(\varepsilon)
of solutions
in the subcritical case

(21)

\displaystyle p<1+\frac{2m}{n+m\gamma},

where
\gamma\in\left[0,\frac{n(m-1)}{m}\right)
.
Specifically, lower bound estimates and upper
bound estimates for
T_{m,\gamma}(\varepsilon)
are given by the next statements.

Proposition 3.1

(Lower bound of lifespan)
.

Let
s\in(1,2],1\leq n\leq 2s
,
m\in(1,2]
,
m\geq\frac{2(n-1)}{n+1}
,
0\leq\gamma<\min\left\{\frac{n(m-1)}{m},\bar{\gamma}(m,n)\right\}
(see Remark
1.2
). The exponent
p
fulfils

(22)

\displaystyle 1+\frac{m\gamma}{n}<p<1+\frac{2m}{n+m\gamma}.

Moreover, we assume that
(u_{0},u_{1})\in\mathcal{D}_{m,\gamma}(s)
(see Theorem
1.1
). Then, there exists a positive constant
\varepsilon_{1}
such that for any
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{1}]
, the lower bound for the lifespan
T_{m,\gamma}(\varepsilon)
can be estimated as follows:

(23)

\displaystyle T_{m,\gamma}(\varepsilon)\gtrsim\varepsilon^{-\frac{2(p-1)}{2-(\frac{n}{m}+\gamma)(p-1)}}.

Proof.

To prove Proposition
3.1
, we will again use the definitions of the constant
M
and the
mapping
\mathcal{N}
on the set
X(T,M\varepsilon)
from the proof of Theorem
1.1
, that is, (
17
) and (
7
). In the course of proving Propositions
2.1
and
2.2
, under condition (
22
), we derive the following estimates:

\displaystyle\|u^{\rm non}\|_{X(T)}\lesssim(1+T)^{1-\frac{n}{2m}(p-1)-\frac{(p-1)\gamma}{2}}\|u\|_{X(T)}^{p}

and

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{N}[u]-\mathcal{N}[v]\|_{Z(T)}

\displaystyle\quad\lesssim(1+T)^{1-\frac{n}{2m}(p-1)-\frac{(p-1)\gamma}{2}}\|u-v\|_{Z(T)}\left(\|u\|_{X(T)}^{p-1}+\|v\|_{X(T)}^{p-1}\right).

Therefore, for all
u,v\in X(T,M\varepsilon)
, we arrive at

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{N}[u]\|_{X(T)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{M\varepsilon}{2}+C_{1}M^{p}\varepsilon^{p}(1+T)^{1-\frac{n}{2m}(p-1)-\frac{(p-1)\gamma}{2}},

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{N}[u]-\mathcal{N}[v]\|_{Z(T)}

\displaystyle\leq C_{2}M^{p-1}\varepsilon^{p-1}(1+T)^{1-\frac{n}{2m}(p-1)-\frac{(p-1)\gamma}{2}}\|u-v\|_{Z(T)}.

Now, if we assume that

(24)

\displaystyle\max\{C_{1},C_{2}\}M^{p-1}\varepsilon^{p-1}(1+T)^{1-\frac{n}{2m}(p-1)-\frac{(p-1)\gamma}{2}}<\frac{1}{4},

then
\mathcal{N}
is a contraction mapping on
X(T,M\varepsilon)
with the metric of
Z(T)
. Furthermore,
X(T,M\varepsilon)
is a closed subset of
Z(T)
with respect to the metric
Z(T)
(see Lemma A.1 in
[
10
]
). As a consequence, we may construct a unique local solution
u\in X(T,M\varepsilon)
and the following estimate holds:

\displaystyle\|u\|_{X(T)}<\frac{3M\varepsilon}{4}.

Let us choose

\displaystyle T^{*}:=\sup\bigg\{T\in(0,T_{m,\gamma}(\varepsilon))\text{ such that }\mathcal{G}(T):=\|u\|_{X(T)}\leq M\varepsilon\bigg\}.

If
T^{*}
satisfies the inequality (
24
), then from the previous estimate it follows that
\mathcal{G}(T^{*})<3M\varepsilon/4
. Due to the fact that
\mathcal{G}(T)
is a continuous and increasing function for any
T\in(0,T_{m,\gamma}(\varepsilon))
, there exists
T^{0}\in(T^{*},T_{m,\gamma}(\varepsilon))
such that
\mathcal{G}(T^{0})\leq M\varepsilon
. This contradicts to the definition of
T^{*}
. For this reason, one realizes

\displaystyle\max\{C_{1},C_{2}\}(1+T^{*})^{1-\frac{n}{2m}(p-1)-\frac{(p-1)\gamma}{2}}M^{p-1}\varepsilon^{p-1}\geq\frac{1}{4},

that is,

\displaystyle T_{m,\gamma}(\varepsilon)\geq T^{*}\gtrsim\varepsilon^{-\frac{2(p-1)}{2-(\frac{n}{m}+\gamma)(p-1)}}.

Thus, Proposition
3.1
has been proved.
∎

Proposition 3.2

(
Upper bound of lifespan
)
.

Assume that the conditions of Theorem
1.2
are satisfied. Then, there exists a positive constant
\varepsilon_{1}
such that for any
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{1}]
, the upper bound for the lifespan
T_{m,\gamma}(\varepsilon)
can be estimated by

(25)

\displaystyle T_{m,\gamma}(\varepsilon)\lesssim\varepsilon^{-\frac{2(p-1)}{2-(\frac{n}{m}+\gamma)(p-1)}}(\log(\varepsilon^{-1}))^{\frac{2(p-1)}{2-(\frac{n}{m}+\gamma)(p-1)}}.

Proof.

To prove Proposition
3.2
, we will reuse the quantities introduced in the proof of Theorem
1.2
. From the estimate (
20
), we obtain

\displaystyle\varepsilon\leq CR^{\frac{n}{m}+\gamma+2-2p^{\prime}+\delta},

for all
R\geq 1
and
\delta
is an arbitrarily small positive constant. Letting
R\to T_{m,\gamma}(\varepsilon)^{\frac{1}{2}}
leads to

\displaystyle T_{m,\gamma}(\varepsilon)\lesssim\varepsilon^{-\frac{2}{\delta(\gamma)}},

where

\displaystyle\delta(\gamma):=-\frac{n}{m}-\gamma-2+2p^{\prime}-\delta>0.

Using again estimate (
20
) with
R\to T_{m,\gamma}(\varepsilon)^{\frac{1}{2}}
, one has

\displaystyle\varepsilon

\displaystyle\leq C(T_{m,\gamma}(\varepsilon))^{\frac{1}{2}(\frac{n}{m}+\gamma+2-2p^{\prime})}\log(T_{m,\gamma}(\varepsilon))

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2C_{1}}{\delta(\gamma)}(T_{m,\gamma}(\varepsilon))^{\frac{1}{2}(\frac{n}{m}+\gamma+2-2p^{\prime})}\log(\varepsilon^{-1}),

that is,

\displaystyle T_{m,\gamma}(\varepsilon)\lesssim\varepsilon^{-\frac{2(p-1)}{2-(\frac{n}{m}+\gamma)(p-1)}}(\log(\varepsilon^{-1}))^{\frac{2(p-1)}{2-(\frac{n}{m}+\gamma)(p-1)}}.

∎

Remark 3.1
.

Linking the achieved estimates (
23
) and (
25
) in Propositions
3.1
and
3.2
one recognizes that the lifespan estimates for solutions to the Cauchy problem (
1
) with low dimension space in the subcritical case, i.e. the condition (
21
) occurs, are determined by the following relations:

\varepsilon^{-\frac{2(p-1)}{2-(\frac{n}{m}+\gamma)(p-1)}}\lesssim T_{m,\gamma}(\varepsilon)\lesssim\varepsilon^{-\frac{2(p-1)}{2-(\frac{n}{m}+\gamma)(p-1)}}(\log(\varepsilon^{-1}))^{\frac{2(p-1)}{2-(\frac{n}{m}+\gamma)(p-1)}}.

This is an improvement in the upper bound estimate compared to Theorem 1.8 in
[
10
]
for
\gamma=0
.

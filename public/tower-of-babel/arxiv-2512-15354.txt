Spatial Approximation for Evolutionary Equations
Andreas Buchinger, Christian Seifert, Sascha Trostorff, Marcus Waurick

Abstract
. We consider evolutionary equations as introduced by R. Picard in 2009 and develop a general theory for approximation which can be seen as a theoretical foundation for numerical analysis for evolutionary equations. To demonstrate the approximation result, we apply it to a spatial discretisation of the heat equation using spectral methods. Keywords. evolutionary equations, approximation, finite elements, convergence MSC2020. Primary 35F10, 35A35; secondary 35Q99, 35K90, 65M12, 65M60

Introduction

Evolutionary equations describe a Hilbert space-approach to model systems of (time-dependent) partial differential equations (PDEs). They were introduced in the 2000s (
[
28
,
23
]
) and provide a well-posedness theorem (see
Theorem

2.2
) that can be applied uniformly without the necessity to adapt it for each new model. For a detailed introduction into the theory of evolutionary equations and the corresponding established literature, see
[
22
,
32
]
. The theory is shown to cover an ever-growing class of PDEs including classical ones, like the heat equation that we will use as our model example in this paper, but also advanced and partially novel models, e.g., poro- and fractional elasticity, (thermo-)piezoelectric coupling models, or (infinite-dimensional) differential-algebraic equations. We refer to
[
20
,
25
,
29
,
21
,
4
,
33
]
for this short and incomplete list. Apart from application to different models, the theory of evolutionary equations is also subject to a constant extension to different fields in the realm of (partial) differential equations. We shall mention abstract boundary data spaces (cf., e.g.,
[
26
,
24
]
),
maximal regularity (cf.
[
27
,
34
]
), homogenisation (most recently
[
8
,
7
]
), delay-differential equations (cf., e.g.,
[
17
,
14
,
2
]
), and control theory (cf.
[
6
]
) as some examples.
Additionally, evolutionary equations have already been treated numerically in the case of particular problems of mixed type with an emphasis on the time discretisation (see
[
12
]
), and in the setting of particular homogenisation problems (see
[
13
,
5
]
). A discontinuous Galerkin approach was used in time;
a finite element method in space. Up to now, these numerical approaches are lacking a general background theory in the framework of evolutionary equations that readily yields (a rate of) convergence of the approximations. Thus, we will establish a first version of such a theory for the spatial component in this paper. The treatment of the temporal component and, ultimately, the combination of both components and application to
the (homogenisation) problems and the corresponding approximation schemes in
[
12
,
13
,
5
]
are topics of ongoing research.

Let us now briefly introduce the notion of (autonomous) evolutionary equations and the aim of this paper.
Consider a system of partial differential equations with autonomous coefficients. Let
H
be the Hilbert space of spatial functions, i.e., in terms of this paper,
L_{2}(\Omega)^{k}
for some
k\in\mathbb{N}
and a domain
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
, where
d\in\mathbb{N}
.
The solution theory is set in
L_{2,\nu}(\mathbb{R},H)
. Here,
\nu
denotes the parameter of an exponential weight in time
\mathbb{R}
that, for
\nu\neq 0
, renders the weak (time) derivative
\partial_{t,\nu}
invertible.
Let
M
be a material law, i.e., a holomorphic function from a certain subset of
\mathbb{C}
to the bounded operators on
H
describing the coupling of the system, and let
A
be a skew-selfadjoint operator on
H
describing the appearing spatial derivatives. Then, the corresponding evolutionary equation reads

(\partial_{t,\nu}M(\partial_{t,\nu})+A)u=f

for some source term
f\in L_{2,\nu}(\mathbb{R},H)
, where
M(\partial_{t,\nu})
is defined by means of a holomorphic functional calculus. Imposing usual conditions on
M
, we obtain well-posedness and the solution is given by
u=S_{\nu}f
, where
S_{\nu}\coloneqq(\overline{\partial_{t,\nu}M(\partial_{t,\nu})+A})^{-1}
is a bounded operator on

L_{2,\nu}(\mathbb{R},H)
.
For
n\in\mathbb{N}
, let
H_{n}
be Hilbert spaces (in the easiest case, increasing
n
-dimensional subspaces of
H
),
M_{n}
a material law, and
A_{n}
a skew-selfadjoint operator, all with respect to
H_{n}
. Consider the evolutionary equations

(\partial_{t,\nu}M_{n}(\partial_{t,\nu})+A_{n})u_{n}=f_{n},n\in\mathbb{N},

for some right-hand sides
f_{n}\in L_{2,\nu}(\mathbb{R},H_{n})
. For the solutions given by
u_{n}=S_{n,\nu}f_{n}
, where
S_{n,\nu}\coloneqq(\overline{\partial_{t,\nu}M_{n}(\partial_{t,\nu})+A_{n}})^{-1}
, we have the following ultimate goal: find suitable conditions that imply convergence of
u_{n}
(embedded in
L_{2,\nu}(\mathbb{R},H)
, cf.
[
18
,
35
,
16
,
19
,
30
,
31
]
) to
u
in different topologies such that existing common numerical approximations that have already been applied to evolutionary equations can be embedded in this framework. We note in passing that the first proof of well-posedness of evolutionary equations in
[
28
]
can be viewed as a particular space-time version of the approximation result established here. Moreover, the main abstract homogenisation result in
[
36
]
is based on a particular instance of the results established here. Furthermore, we expect applications to problems with underlying spatial domains changing their shape along the lines of
[
18
,
31
]
, which also form as a source of our inspiration.

Finally, we outline how this paper is organised. In

,
11

and the references therein)—are yet to be treated.

2.
Evolutionary Equations

In this section, we will recall the needed concepts and theorems of the theory of evolutionary equations (see
[
32
]
for proofs and details).

Our model problem will be the heat equation on some open and bounded
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
with Dirichlet
boundary conditions and in time
\mathbb{R}
. We formally write

(1)

\partial_{t}\theta-\divergence\grad_{0}\theta=Q\text{.}

In order to write this equation in the evolutionary way and to discuss its well-posedness, we need a few definitions. For
\nu\in\mathbb{R}
and a Hilbert space
H
, we write
L_{2,\nu}(\mathbb{R},H)
for the space of all (equivalence
classes of) Bochner-measurable
f\colon\mathbb{R}\to H
with
\mathrm{e}^{-\nu\cdot}{}\mathopen{\mathchoice{\hbox{$\displaystyle\left\lVert\hbox{}\right.$}}{\hbox{$\textstyle\left\lVert\hbox{}\right.$}}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\left\lVert\hbox{}\right.$}}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\left\lVert\hbox{}\right.$}}}f(\cdot)\mathclose{\mathchoice{\hbox{$\displaystyle\left.\hbox{}\right\rVert$}}{\hbox{$\textstyle\left.\hbox{}\right\rVert$}}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\left.\hbox{}\right\rVert$}}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\left.\hbox{}\right\rVert$}}}_{H}\in L_{2}(\mathbb{R})
,
and we define

\exp(-\nu\mathrm{m})\colon\begin{cases}\hfill L_{2,\nu}(\mathbb{R},H)&\to\quad L_{2}(\mathbb{R},H)\\
\hfill f&\mapsto\quad\mathrm{e}^{-\nu\cdot}f(\cdot).\end{cases}

In composition with the common unitary Fourier transformation
\mathcal{F}
on
L_{2}(\mathbb{R},H)
, this gives rise to the unitary Fourier–Laplace transformation

\mathcal{L}^{H}_{\nu}\coloneqq\mathcal{F}\exp(-\nu\mathrm{m})\colon L_{2,\nu}(\mathbb{R},H)\to L_{2}(\mathbb{R},H)\text{.}

Let

\partial_{t,\nu}\coloneqq(\mathcal{L}^{H}_{\nu})^{\ast}(\mathrm{i}\mathrm{m}+\nu)\mathcal{L}^{H}_{\nu}

denote the usual weak (time) derivative on
L_{2,\nu}(\mathbb{R},H)
, where
\mathrm{m}\colon\dom(\mathrm{m})\subseteq L_{2}(\mathbb{R},H)\to L_{2}(\mathbb{R},H)
with
\mathrm{m}f\coloneqq(x\mapsto xf(x))
and maximal domain.

Next, for
O\subseteq\mathbb{C}
open, we call a holomorphic function

M\colon O\to L(H)\coloneqq\{B\colon H\to H\mid B\text{ linear and bounded}\}

such that
O
contains
\mathbb{C}_{\Real>\mu}\coloneqq\{z\in\mathbb{C}\mid\Real z>\mu\}
and
\sup_{z\in\mathbb{C}_{\Real>\mu}}{}\mathopen{\mathchoice{\hbox{$\displaystyle\left\lVert\hbox{}\right.$}}{\hbox{$\textstyle\left\lVert\hbox{}\right.$}}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\left\lVert\hbox{}\right.$}}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\left\lVert\hbox{}\right.$}}}M(z)\mathclose{\mathchoice{\hbox{$\displaystyle\left.\hbox{}\right\rVert$}}{\hbox{$\textstyle\left.\hbox{}\right\rVert$}}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\left.\hbox{}\right\rVert$}}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\left.\hbox{}\right\rVert$}}}<\infty
for some
\mu\in\mathbb{R}
, a material law, and we write
\sbb(M)
for the infimum over all such
\mu
.
Assuming
\nu>\sbb(M)
and using the bounded linear operator

M(\mathrm{i}\mathrm{m}+\nu)\colon\begin{cases}\hfill L_{2}(\mathbb{R},H)&\to\quad L_{2}(\mathbb{R},H)\\
\hfill f&\mapsto\quad M(\mathrm{i}\cdot+\nu)f(\cdot),\end{cases}

we call

M(\partial_{t,\nu})\coloneqq(\mathcal{L}^{H}_{\nu})^{\ast}M(\mathrm{i}\mathrm{m}+\nu)\mathcal{L}^{H}_{\nu}\in L(L_{2,\nu}(\mathbb{R},H))

the corresponding material law operator.

Finally, let
A\colon\dom(A)\subseteq H\to H
be skew-selfadjoint. Then, we readily obtain the following lifted
version to
L_{2,\nu}(\mathbb{R},H)
that we will still call
A
; the properties of which are well understood:

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
H
be a Hilbert space,
\nu\in\mathbb{R}
, and
A\colon\dom(A)\subseteq H\to H
skew-selfadjoint.
Regard
\dom(A)
as a Hilbert space endowed with the graph inner product induced by
A
.
Then,

A\colon\begin{cases}\hfill L_{2,\nu}(\mathbb{R},\dom(A))\subseteq L_{2,\nu}(\mathbb{R},H)&\to\quad L_{2,\nu}(\mathbb{R},H)\\
\hfill f&\mapsto\quad(t\mapsto A(f(t)))\end{cases}

is skew-selfadjoint.

Altogether, these concepts and definitions allow us to
present the autonomous evolutionary equation

(2)

(\partial_{t,\nu}M(\partial_{t,\nu})+A)u=f

for suitable
u,f\in L_{2,\nu}(\mathbb{R},H)
, and to formulate the following essential well-posedness theorem.

Theorem 2.2

(Picard’s Well-Posedness Theorem)
.

Let
H
be a Hilbert space,
A\colon\dom(A)\subseteq H\to H
skew-selfadjoint,
M
a material law,

\nu>\sbb(M)
, and
c>0
such that

\Real\left(h\,\middle|\,zM(z)h\right)_{H}\geq c{}\mathopen{\mathchoice{\hbox{$\displaystyle\left\lVert\hbox{}\right.$}}{\hbox{$\textstyle\left\lVert\hbox{}\right.$}}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\left\lVert\hbox{}\right.$}}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\left\lVert\hbox{}\right.$}}}h\mathclose{\mathchoice{\hbox{$\displaystyle\left.\hbox{}\right\rVert$}}{\hbox{$\textstyle\left.\hbox{}\right\rVert$}}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\left.\hbox{}\right\rVert$}}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\left.\hbox{}\right\rVert$}}}^{2}_{H}

for all
h\in H
and
z\in\mathbb{C}
with
\Real z\geq\nu
.

Then,
\partial_{t,\mu}M(\partial_{t,\mu})+A
is closable with bounded inverse

S_{\mu}\coloneqq(\overline{\partial_{t,\mu}M(\partial_{t,\mu})+A})^{-1}\in L(L_{2,\mu}(\mathbb{R},H))

and
{}\mathopen{\mathchoice{\hbox{$\displaystyle\left\lVert\hbox{}\right.$}}{\hbox{$\textstyle\left\lVert\hbox{}\right.$}}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\left\lVert\hbox{}\right.$}}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\left\lVert\hbox{}\right.$}}}S_{\mu}\mathclose{\mathchoice{\hbox{$\displaystyle\left.\hbox{}\right\rVert$}}{\hbox{$\textstyle\left.\hbox{}\right\rVert$}}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\left.\hbox{}\right\rVert$}}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\left.\hbox{}\right\rVert$}}}\leq 1/c
for all
\mu\geq\nu
. In particular,
S_{\mu}
is the material law operator corresponding to the material law

z\mapsto(zM(z)+A)^{-1}
defined for
z\in\mathbb{C}
with
\Real z\geq\nu
.

Moreover,
\spt f\subseteq[a,\infty)
for
a\in\mathbb{R}
and
f\in L_{2,\mu}(\mathbb{R},H)
implies
\spt S_{\mu}f\subseteq[a,\infty)
. For
\mu_{1},\mu_{2}\geq\nu
, we have
S_{\mu_{1}}f=S_{\mu_{2}}f
for all

f\in L_{2,\mu_{1}}(\mathbb{R},H)\cap L_{2,\mu_{2}}(\mathbb{R},H)
. Finally,
f\in\dom(\partial_{t,\mu})
yields
S_{\mu}f\in\dom(\partial_{t,\mu})\cap\dom(A)
, i.e., due to
M(\partial_{t,\mu})\partial_{t,\mu}\subseteq\partial_{t,\mu}M(\partial_{t,\mu})
, the solution
u\coloneqq S_{\mu}f
solves the evolutionary equation in the literal sense of
2
.

Proof.

See, e.g.,
[
32
, Theorem 6.2.1]
.
∎

Going back to
1
, we set the spatial Hilbert space
H\coloneqq L_{2}(\Omega)\times L_{2}(\Omega)^{d}
, the coupling of the system via the material law

z\mapsto M(z)\coloneqq\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}+z^{-1}\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}
for

z\in\mathbb{C}
with
\Real z>0
, and the spatial differential operators via the skew-selfadjoint unbounded operator
A\coloneqq\begin{pmatrix}0&\divergence\\
\grad_{0}&0\end{pmatrix}
. Here,
\divergence
is the weak divergence on
L_{2}(\Omega)^{d}
with its maximal domain, and
\grad_{0}
is the closure of the gradient on the test functions
C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)\subseteq L_{2}(\Omega)
with respect to the graph norm, i.e., the weak gradient on
L_{2}(\Omega)

with domain
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
. By means of these definitions, we consider
1
as the evolutionary equation

(3)

\Bigl(\partial_{t,\nu}\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}+\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}+\begin{pmatrix}0&\divergence\\
\grad_{0}&0\end{pmatrix}\Bigr)\begin{pmatrix}\theta\\
q\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}Q\\
0\end{pmatrix}

for
\nu>0
and a source
Q\in L_{2,\nu}(\mathbb{R},L_{2}(\Omega))
. We can easily verify that

Theorem

2.2
can be applied to
3
, see also
Section

4
.

3.
Spatial Approximation

In this section, we will consider spatial approximations of evolutionary equations. We will rely on the notation introduced in Section
2
. Before we dive into the particulars of our setting, we provide one particular setting we have in mind.

Remark 3.1
.

Consider an (increasing) sequence of finite-dimensional subspaces
(H_{n})_{n}
of a Hilbert space
H
such that
\bigcup_{n}H_{n}
is dense in
H
. Then,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, let
P_{n}\colon H\to H_{n}
be the corresponding orthogonal projections and
J_{n}\colon H_{n}\to H
the corresponding canonical embeddings. Then,

{}\mathopen{\mathchoice{\hbox{$\displaystyle\left\lVert\hbox{}\right.$}}{\hbox{$\textstyle\left\lVert\hbox{}\right.$}}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\left\lVert\hbox{}\right.$}}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\left\lVert\hbox{}\right.$}}}P_{n}\mathclose{\mathchoice{\hbox{$\displaystyle\left.\hbox{}\right\rVert$}}{\hbox{$\textstyle\left.\hbox{}\right\rVert$}}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\left.\hbox{}\right\rVert$}}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\left.\hbox{}\right\rVert$}}}={}\mathopen{\mathchoice{\hbox{$\displaystyle\left\lVert\hbox{}\right.$}}{\hbox{$\textstyle\left\lVert\hbox{}\right.$}}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\left\lVert\hbox{}\right.$}}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\left\lVert\hbox{}\right.$}}}J_{n}\mathclose{\mathchoice{\hbox{$\displaystyle\left.\hbox{}\right\rVert$}}{\hbox{$\textstyle\left.\hbox{}\right\rVert$}}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\left.\hbox{}\right\rVert$}}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\left.\hbox{}\right\rVert$}}}=1,

and
P_{n}J_{n}=I_{H_{n}}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. The density assumption yields
J_{n}P_{n}\to I
in the strong operator topology.
Note that this setting is remindful of a Galerkin approximation, where the sequence
(H_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is indeed usually constructed to be increasing.

We will now provide a more general framework and convergence results that will cover first numerical approximations schemes in the context of evolutionary equations.

Remark 3.5
.

The name of the previous definition of compact-weak convergence comes from the fact that it resembles a certain
“uniform compact embedding”
of the domains
\dom(A_{n}),n\in\mathbb{N}
. The standard case in applications will
be
H_{n}\subseteq H
and
\dom(A_{n})\subseteq\dom(A)
, where
\dom(A)
as a Hilbert space embeds compactly into
H
. Then, we can define
weak convergence of
(T_{n}+A_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}

to
T+A
on
D
as

(7)

\left(g\,\middle|\,P_{n}(T+A)f-(T_{n}+A_{n})P_{n}f\right)_{H}\to 0

for all
f\in(T+A)^{-1}(D)
and
g\in H
as
n\to\infty
. By the principle of uniform boundedness and since any bounded sequence in
\dom(A)
has a (strongly) convergent subsequence in
H
, this implies compact-weak convergence (compare also
Lemma

3.11
). Alternatively, we could restrict
7
to
g\in\dom(A)
and instead ask for
\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}{}\mathopen{\mathchoice{\hbox{$\displaystyle\left\lVert\hbox{}\right.$}}{\hbox{$\textstyle\left\lVert\hbox{}\right.$}}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\left\lVert\hbox{}\right.$}}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\left\lVert\hbox{}\right.$}}}P_{n}(T+A)f-(T_{n}+A_{n})P_{n}f\mathclose{\mathchoice{\hbox{$\displaystyle\left.\hbox{}\right\rVert$}}{\hbox{$\textstyle\left.\hbox{}\right\rVert$}}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\left.\hbox{}\right\rVert$}}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\left.\hbox{}\right\rVert$}}}_{H}<\infty
for each
f\in(T+A)^{-1}(D)
.

For an in-depth discussion on general weak convergence for sequences in varying Hilbert spaces, see, e.g.,
[
19
, Section 2.2]
.

With that, we obtain the following first central lemma, which is related to
[
32
, Lemma 14.1.2]
. Before, however, providing the lemma, a standard invertibility result is presented. The proof of which can be found in
[
32
, Chapter 6]
.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
T\in L(H)
,

a skew-selfadjoint operator in

. If there is

such that

\Real\left(h\,\middle|\,Th\right)_{H}\geq c{}\mathopen{\mathchoice{\hbox{$\displaystyle\left\lVert\hbox{}\right.$}}{\hbox{$\textstyle\left\lVert\hbox{}\right.$}}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\left\lVert\hbox{}\right.$}}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\left\lVert\hbox{}\right.$}}}h\mathclose{\mathchoice{\hbox{$\displaystyle\left.\hbox{}\right\rVert$}}{\hbox{$\textstyle\left.\hbox{}\right\rVert$}}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\left.\hbox{}\right\rVert$}}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\left.\hbox{}\right\rVert$}}}^{2}_{H}\quad(h\in H)

Lemma 3.7

For

,

, and assume that there exist

Proof.

The invertibility statement follows from

. By virtue of

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}{}\mathopen{\mathchoice{\hbox{$\displaystyle\left\lVert\hbox{}\right.$}}{\hbox{$\textstyle\left\lVert\hbox{}\right.$}}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\left\lVert\hbox{}\right.$}}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\left\lVert\hbox{}\right.$}}}(T_{n}+A_{n})^{-1}\mathclose{\mathchoice{\hbox{$\displaystyle\left.\hbox{}\right\rVert$}}{\hbox{$\textstyle\left.\hbox{}\right\rVert$}}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\left.\hbox{}\right\rVert$}}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\left.\hbox{}\right\rVert$}}}_{L(H_{n},\dom(A_{n}))}\leq\frac{\sqrt{1+c^{2}+d^{2}}}{c},

&=\bigl(J_{n}(T_{n}+A_{n})^{-1}P_{n}(T+A)-(J_{n}P_{n}+I-J_{n}P_{n})\bigr)(T+A)^{-1}f\\

and

in

.

and
Lemma

3.12
to the corresponding weighted

-spaces by virtue of the Fourier–Laplace transformation.

Applying the same principle as in
Lemma

2.1
, for

and
\nu\in\mathbb{R}
, we obtain the lifted operator

P_{n}\in L(L_{2,\nu}(\mathbb{R},D),L_{2,\nu}(\mathbb{R},H_{n}))
, defined as

P_{n}\colon\begin{cases}\hfill L_{2,\nu}(\mathbb{R},D)&\to\quad L_{2,\nu}(\mathbb{R},H_{n})\\
\hfill f&\mapsto\quad(t\mapsto P_{n}(f(t)))\end{cases}

and similarly
\tilde{J}_{n}\in L(L_{2,\nu}(\mathbb{R},H_{n}),L_{2,\nu}(\mathbb{R},H))
for
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Clearly,
\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}{}\mathopen{\mathchoice{\hbox{$\displaystyle\left\lVert\hbox{}\right.$}}{\hbox{$\textstyle\left\lVert\hbox{}\right.$}}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\left\lVert\hbox{}\right.$}}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\left\lVert\hbox{}\right.$}}}P_{n}\mathclose{\mathchoice{\hbox{$\displaystyle\left.\hbox{}\right\rVert$}}{\hbox{$\textstyle\left.\hbox{}\right\rVert$}}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\left.\hbox{}\right\rVert$}}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\left.\hbox{}\right\rVert$}}}<\infty

still hold for these lifted operators.

Lemma 3.14
.

Let
N
and
N_{n}
be material laws taking values in
L(H)
and
L(H_{n})
, respectively, such that

\sbb(N),\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\sbb(N_{n})\leq\nu_{0}

and

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\sup_{z\in\mathbb{C}_{\Real>\nu_{0}}}{}\mathopen{\mathchoice{\hbox{$\displaystyle\left\lVert\hbox{}\right.$}}{\hbox{$\textstyle\left\lVert\hbox{}\right.$}}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\left\lVert\hbox{}\right.$}}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\left\lVert\hbox{}\right.$}}}N_{n}(z)\mathclose{\mathchoice{\hbox{$\displaystyle\left.\hbox{}\right\rVert$}}{\hbox{$\textstyle\left.\hbox{}\right\rVert$}}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\left.\hbox{}\right\rVert$}}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\left.\hbox{}\right\rVert$}}}\leq d,

i.e., that
(N_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a uniformly bounded sequence.
Moreover, for
z\in\mathbb{C}
with
\Real z>\nu_{0}
, assume that

\tilde{J}_{n}N_{n}(z)P_{n}\to N(z)
in the strong (weak) operator topology on
L(D,H)
as
n\to\infty
. Let
\nu>\nu_{0}
. Then,

\tilde{J}_{n}N_{n}(\partial_{t,\nu})P_{n}\to N(\partial_{t,\nu})

in the strong (weak) operator topology on
L(L_{2,\nu}(\mathbb{R},D),L_{2,\nu}(\mathbb{R},H))
as
n\to\infty
.

Proof.

For
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R},D)\subseteq L_{2,\nu}(\mathbb{R},D)
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
we obtain
P_{n}\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R},H_{n})\subseteq L_{2,\nu}(\mathbb{R},H_{n})
and

\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{H_{n}}P_{n}\varphi=t\mapsto\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\uppi}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\exp(-(it+\nu)s)P_{n}\varphi(s)\mathrm{d}s\\
=t\mapsto P_{n}\Bigl(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\uppi}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\exp(-(it+\nu)s)\varphi(s)\mathrm{d}s\Bigr)=P_{n}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{D}\varphi\text{.}

Hence, by density, this implies
\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{H_{n}}P_{n}=P_{n}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{D}
for
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Analogously, we infer
\tilde{J}_{n}(\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{H_{n}})^{\ast}=(\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{H})^{\ast}\tilde{J}_{n}
for
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Note that we lifted
P_{n}
and
\tilde{J}_{n}
with respect to the weight parameter
\nu
on the left-hand sides of both identities and with
respect to the respective unweighted spaces on the right-hand sides. Thus,

\tilde{J}_{n}N_{n}(\partial_{t,\nu})P_{n}=\tilde{J}_{n}(\mathcal{L}^{H_{n}}_{\nu})^{\ast}N_{n}(\mathrm{i}\mathrm{m}+\nu)\mathcal{L}^{H_{n}}_{\nu}P_{n}=(\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{H})^{\ast}\tilde{J}_{n}N_{n}(\mathrm{i}\mathrm{m}+\nu)P_{n}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{D}

holds for
n\in\mathbb{N}
. As
\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{D}
and
\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{H}
are both unitary mappings it remains to show that

\tilde{J}_{n}N_{n}(\mathrm{i}\mathrm{m}+\nu)P_{n}\to N(\mathrm{i}\mathrm{m}+\nu)
in the strong (weak) operator topology on
L(L_{2}(\mathbb{R},D),L_{2}(\mathbb{R},H))
as
n\to\infty
, which
immediately follows from the dominated convergence theorem. ∎

In this section, we will apply the results from

Example 4.1
.

Let
\Omega\coloneqq(0,1)
be the unit interval and
H\coloneqq L_{2}(\Omega)\times L_{2}(\Omega)
.
We consider the heat equation with homogeneous mixed boundary conditions; that is

\Bigl(\partial_{t,\nu}\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}+\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}+\begin{pmatrix}0&\partial^{\{1\}}_{x}\\
\partial^{\{0\}}_{x}&0\end{pmatrix}\Bigr)\begin{pmatrix}\theta\\
q\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}Q\\
0\end{pmatrix}

for
\nu>0
and a source

. Then,
\dom(A)=H^{1}_{\{0\}}(\Omega)\times H^{1}_{\{1\}}(\Omega)

H^{1}_{\{y\}}(\Omega)\coloneqq\{f\in H^{1}(\Omega)\mid f(y)=0\}
and
\partial^{\{y\}}_{x}
is the restriction of the usual weak derivative from
H^{1}(\Omega)
to
H^{1}_{\{y\}}(\Omega)

(\partial^{\{0\}}_{x})^{\ast}=-\partial^{\{1\}}_{x}

A
is skew-selfadjoint.
Moreover,
z\mapsto M(z)\coloneqq\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}+z^{-1}\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}
is a material law with
\sbb(M)=0
, and for
\nu>0
and
z\in\mathbb{C}
with
\Real z\geq\nu

(13)

\Real\left((x,y)\,\middle|\,zM(z)(x,y)\right)_{H}\geq\min\{\nu,1\}{}\mathopen{\mathchoice{\hbox{$\displaystyle\left\lVert\hbox{}\right.$}}{\hbox{$\textstyle\left\lVert\hbox{}\right.$}}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\left\lVert\hbox{}\right.$}}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\left\lVert\hbox{}\right.$}}}(x,y)\mathclose{\mathchoice{\hbox{$\displaystyle\left.\hbox{}\right\rVert$}}{\hbox{$\textstyle\left.\hbox{}\right\rVert$}}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\left.\hbox{}\right\rVert$}}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\left.\hbox{}\right\rVert$}}}_{H}^{2}

for all

We readily obtain
\ker(A)=\{0\}
and thus, by virtue of the Rellich–Kondrachov theorem,
\ran(A)=H
with a compact resolvent
A^{-1}\in L(H)
. Hence, by the spectral theorem for self-adjoint compact operators applied to
iA^{-1}
, we obtain an orthonormal basis
(\varphi_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
of
H
consisting of eigenvectors of
A^{-1}
corresponding to nonzero eigenvalues
(\lambda_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
in

. For
j\in\mathbb{N}
, we clearly have
\varphi_{j}\in\dom(A)
with
A\varphi_{j}=\lambda_{j}^{-1}\varphi_{j}
.
Moreover, for
v\in\dom(A)
, we obtain

(14)

For
n\in\mathbb{N}
, let
H_{n}\coloneqq\ls\{\varphi_{j}\mid j\leq n\}
and define

\pi_{n}\colon\begin{cases}\hfill H&\to\quad H_{n}\\
\hfill u&\mapsto\quad\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left(\varphi_{j}\,\middle|\,u\right)_{H}\varphi_{j}\end{cases}\text{,}

i.e., the orthogonal projection with restricted codomain, as well as the canonical embedding
\iota_{n}\colon H_{n}\to H
; note that
\pi_{n}=\iota_{n}^{*}
.
Setting
A_{n}\coloneqq\pi_{n}A\iota_{n}
,
we infer
\dom(A_{n})=H_{n}
and that
A_{n}
is bounded and skew-selfadjoint.

M_{n}\coloneqq\pi_{n}M\iota_{n}
is a material law with
\sbb(M_{n})=0
, and, for
\nu>0
and
z\in\mathbb{C}_{\Real\geq\nu}
, we have (
13
) for all
(x,y)\in H_{n}
. Furthermore, the stability condition is satisfied, i.e.,
\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}{}\mathopen{\mathchoice{\hbox{$\displaystyle\left\lVert\hbox{}\right.$}}{\hbox{$\textstyle\left\lVert\hbox{}\right.$}}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\left\lVert\hbox{}\right.$}}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\left\lVert\hbox{}\right.$}}}zM_{n}(z)\mathclose{\mathchoice{\hbox{$\displaystyle\left.\hbox{}\right\rVert$}}{\hbox{$\textstyle\left.\hbox{}\right\rVert$}}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\left.\hbox{}\right\rVert$}}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\left.\hbox{}\right\rVert$}}}\leq\max\{{}\mathopen{\mathchoice{\hbox{$\displaystyle\left\lvert\hbox{}\right.$}}{\hbox{$\textstyle\left\lvert\hbox{}\right.$}}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\left\lvert\hbox{}\right.$}}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\left\lvert\hbox{}\right.$}}}z\mathclose{\mathchoice{\hbox{$\displaystyle\left.\hbox{}\right\rvert$}}{\hbox{$\textstyle\left.\hbox{}\right\rvert$}}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\left.\hbox{}\right\rvert$}}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\left.\hbox{}\right\rvert$}}},1\}
. In view of
3.2
, we set

D\coloneqq\dom(A)
and
P_{n}\coloneqq\pi_{n}\restriction_{\dom(A)}
for
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Indeed, we then have

with an operator norm independent of

since, for
v\in\dom(A)
, we have

(15)

With

for

, we also immediately have

for the same mapping, where all operator norms are bounded by

. For

(16)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} J_{n}P_{n}v&=\iota_{n}\pi_{n}v=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left(\varphi_{j}\,\middle|\,v\right)_{H}\varphi_{j}\to\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\left(\varphi_{j}\,\middle|\,v\right)_{H}\varphi_{j}=v\quad\text{and}\\

in

as

. Finally, for

with

and, with (

Remark 4.3
.

In order to discuss finite element methods in the setting of
section

3
, we would need to work with the following spaces.

For
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
we write
\mathcal{P}_{k}
for the vector space of polynomials from
\mathbb{R}
to
\mathbb{C}
of degree at most
k
.
For
n\in\mathbb{N}
let

and consider the partition

of
[0,1]
with
x_{j}\coloneqq\frac{j}{n}=jh
for all
j\in\{0,\ldots,n\}
, and let

\displaystyle S_{n}^{1}(\Omega)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\mathopen{\mathchoice{\hbox{$\displaystyle\left\{\hbox{}\right.$}}{\hbox{$\textstyle\left\{\hbox{}\right.$}}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\left\{\hbox{}\right.$}}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\left\{\hbox{}\right.$}}}f\in L_{2}(\Omega):\;f\restriction_{(x_{j},x_{j+1})}\in\mathcal{P}_{1}\quad(j\in\{0,\ldots,n-1\})\mathclose{\mathchoice{\hbox{$\displaystyle\left.\hbox{}\right\}$}}{\hbox{$\textstyle\left.\hbox{}\right\}$}}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\left.\hbox{}\right\}$}}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\left.\hbox{}\right\}$}}},

\displaystyle S_{n}^{1,0}(\Omega)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\mathopen{\mathchoice{\hbox{$\displaystyle\left\{\hbox{}\right.$}}{\hbox{$\textstyle\left\{\hbox{}\right.$}}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\left\{\hbox{}\right.$}}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\left\{\hbox{}\right.$}}}f\in L_{2}(\Omega):\;f\in C(\overline{\Omega}),\,f\restriction_{(x_{j},x_{j+1})}\in\mathcal{P}_{1}\quad(j\in\{0,\ldots,n-1\})\mathclose{\mathchoice{\hbox{$\displaystyle\left.\hbox{}\right\}$}}{\hbox{$\textstyle\left.\hbox{}\right\}$}}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\left.\hbox{}\right\}$}}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\left.\hbox{}\right\}$}}},

\displaystyle S_{n,0}^{1,0}(\Omega)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\mathopen{\mathchoice{\hbox{$\displaystyle\left\{\hbox{}\right.$}}{\hbox{$\textstyle\left\{\hbox{}\right.$}}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\left\{\hbox{}\right.$}}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\left\{\hbox{}\right.$}}}f\in L_{2}(\Omega):\;f\in C(\overline{\Omega}),\,f\restriction_{(x_{j},x_{j+1})}\in\mathcal{P}_{1}\quad(j\in\{0,\ldots,n-1\}),\,f(0)=f(1)=0\mathclose{\mathchoice{\hbox{$\displaystyle\left.\hbox{}\right\}$}}{\hbox{$\textstyle\left.\hbox{}\right\}$}}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\left.\hbox{}\right\}$}}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\left.\hbox{}\right\}$}}}

be the vector spaces of (globally continuous) linear splines (with zero boundary conditions).

,

, and

are finite-dimensional subspaces of

and hence Hilbert spaces. Note that we even have

Unfortunately, there is no canonical way to define

. This mapping clearly fails to be skew-selfadjoint or even densely defined. In fact, there seems to be no readily obtainable and sensible way to define

on these spaces; even when we are not asking for skew-selfadjointness.

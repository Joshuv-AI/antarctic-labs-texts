Nash entropy, Calabi energy and geometric regularization of singular Kähler metrics Thanks: Work supported in part by the National Science Foundation under grants DMS-2203607 and DMS-2303508, and the grant MPS-TSM-00946730 from Simons Foundation.
Bin Guo and Jian Song

Abstract
. We prove uniform Sobolev bounds for solutions of the Laplace equation on a general family of Kähler manifolds with bounded Nash entropy and Calabi energy. These estimates establish a connection to the theory of RCD spaces and provide abundant examples of RCD spaces topologically and holomorphically equivalent to projective varieties. Suppose X X is a normal projective variety that admits a resolution of singularities with relative nef or relative effective anti-canonical bundle. Then every admissible singular Kähler metric on X X with Ricci curvature bounded below induces a non-collapsed RCD space homeomorphic to the projective variety X X itself.

Introduction

In the recent papers
[
24
,
25
]
and their extensions
[
26
,
47
,
23

, various analytic and geometric estimates are established to build a general theory of geometric analysis on singular complex spaces. These estimates are built on the assumption on uniform bounds of the Nash entropy. In this paper, we aim to obtain new Sobolev estimates by imposing an additional assumption on the Calabi energy. Such an assumption is satisfied in many geometric and analytic settings including the cases of constant scalar curvature Kähler metrics and Kähler metrics of bounded
L^{2}
-curvature. Our new estimates also reveal connections between singular Kähler spaces and the theory of RCD spaces.

Let
X
be an
n
-dimensional compact normal Kähler variety equppied with a smooth Kähler metric
\theta_{X}
(c.f. Definition

Similar to the singular Kähler metrics introduced in
[

(1.1)

\mathcal{V}(X,\theta_{X},n,A,p,K)

of all singular Kähler metrics

(1)

\omega\in C^{2}(X^{\circ})
, where

(2)

[\omega]
is a Kähler class on

(1.2)

The singular Kähler metric

(1,1)
-current on

[

,

,

As defined in

both analytically and geometrically via complex Monge-Ampère equations and its coupled Laplace equation. The spectral theory is established in
[
25
]
for

along with many other uniform estimates for diameters, Sobolev inequalities, Green’s functions and heat kernels. The series of papers
[

,

24

25
,

aim to build a framework that would expand the classical works
[

,

on complex Monge-Ampère equations to geometric analysis on complex spaces with singularities. In particular, one wishes to establish connections between analysis on singular Kähler spaces and their topological, geometric and algebraic structures, which might require additional geometric or analytic assumptions beyond the Nash entropy. For example, one does not expect the Sobolev inequality with optimal exponents to hold for Kähler metrics in
\mathcal{V}(X,\theta_{X},n,A,p,K)

A natural geometric assumption for singular Kähler metrics will be suitable bounds on the Ricci curvature. The Ricci curvature for
\omega\in\mathcal{V}(X,\theta_{X},n,A,p,K)
can be defined as a current if the volume measure
\omega^{n}
satisfies suitable quasi-plurisubharmonic conditions (c.f. Definition

is bounded below as a current, one would like to understand the geometric and analytic structures of
(\hat{X},d_{\omega},\omega^{n})

[
33
,
41
,
1
]
and many others. The following specific questions arise naturally if the Ricci curvature of the singular Kähler space

is bounded below.

(1)

Is
(\hat{X},d_{\omega})
defined as in (
1.4

X
?

(2)

Does the Sobolev inequality with optimal exponent hold on
(\hat{X},d_{\omega},\omega^{n})

It is obvious that these questions are related to the theory of RCD spaces due to recent progress in the compactness of Kähler manifolds with suitable Ricci curvature bounds, particularly in the case when
(X,\omega)
admits suitable smoothing
[

,

]
or special resolution of singularities

(1)

(X,\omega)
is Kähler-Einstein and

(3)

-effective anti-canonical divisor

[

We will first establish new Sobolev estimates for
\omega\in\mathcal{V}(X,\theta_{X},n,A,p,K)
on a smooth Kähler manifold

Recall that the Calabi energy for a Kähler metric
\omega
on
X
is defined by

(1.5)

\mathcal{C}a(\omega)=\frac{1}{V_{\omega}}\int_{X}\left(\mathrm{R}(\omega)\right)^{2}\omega^{n},

where
\mathrm{R}(\omega)
is the scalar curvature of
\omega
. The following is our first main result.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(X,\theta_{X})
be an
n
-dimensional compact Kähler manifold equipped with a smooth Kähler metric
\theta_{X}
. Suppose
\omega
is a Kähler metric in
\mathcal{V}(X,\theta_{X},n,A,p,K)
with
p>n
satisfying

\mathcal{C}a(\omega)\leq B

and
u\in C^{\infty}(X)
is a solution of the Laplace equation

(1.6)

\Delta_{\omega}u=f,~\int_{X}u\omega^{n}=0

for
f\in C^{\infty}(X)
. Then the following hold.

(1)

There exists
C=C\left(X,\theta_{X},n,A,B,p,K,\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(X)}\right)>0
such that

\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(X)}\leq C,

\frac{1}{V_{\omega}}\int_{X}\left(|\nabla\nabla u|^{2}_{\omega}+|\nabla\overline{\nabla}u|^{2}_{\omega}+|\nabla u|_{\omega}^{4}\right)\omega^{n}\leq C.

(2)

For any
r>0
, there exists
C=C\left(X,\theta_{X},n,A,B,p,K,\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(X)},r\right)>0
such that for any

,

\frac{1}{V_{\omega}}\int_{X\setminus B_{\omega}(x,r)}\left(V_{\omega}^{2}|\nabla\nabla G(x,\cdot)|^{2}_{\omega}+V_{\omega}^{2}|\nabla\overline{\nabla}G(x,\cdot)|^{2}_{\omega}+V_{\omega}^{4}|\nabla G(x,\cdot)|^{4}_{\omega}\right)\omega^{n}\leq C,

where

\omega
-geodesic ball centered at

The equation (
1.6

[

(\hat{X},d_{\omega},\omega^{n})

is to establish uniform

One can consider the space
\nu(X,\theta_{X},n,A,p,K;B)=\{\omega\in\nu(X,\theta_{X},n,A,p,K):~\mathcal{C}a(\omega)\leq B\}
. The compactness problem of
\nu(X,\theta_{X},n,A,p,K;B)
is rather complicated even in the non-collapsing case when the volume
V_{\omega}
is uniformly bounded above away from
0
. Instead, we would like to investigate a singular Kähler space
(X,\omega)
that can be approximated by smooth Kähler metrics in
\nu(X,\theta_{X},n,A,p,K;B)
and to establish geometric and topological structures for the metric measure space
(\hat{X},d_{\omega},\omega^{n})
.

Suppose
X
is an
n
-dimensional compact normal Kähler variety. We choose
\pi:Y\rightarrow X
, a resolution of singularities for

, where the Kähler manifold

is a nonsingular model of

. Let
\theta_{X}
be a fixed smooth background Kähler metric
\theta_{X}
on

Definition 1.1
.

Let
X
be an
n
-dimensional compact normal Kähler variety equipped with a smooth Kähler metric
\theta_{X}
. A singular Kähler metric
\omega\in\mathcal{V}(X,\theta_{X},n,A,p,K)
with

is said to admit a regularization with uniform Nash entropy and Calabi energy if there exists a resolution
\pi:Y\rightarrow X
with the following properties.

(1)

There exist a sequence of smooth Kähler metrics
\omega_{j}
and a Kähler class
\alpha
on

such that

(1.7)

[\omega_{j}]=\epsilon_{j}\alpha+[\pi^{*}\omega],~\epsilon_{j}\rightarrow 0^{+},~\omega_{j}\rightarrow\pi^{*}\omega~{\rm in}~C^{2}_{loc}(\pi^{-1}(X^{\circ})).

(2)

There exist
p^{\prime}>n
and
K^{\prime}>0
such that for all
j
,

(1.8)

\mathcal{N}_{\theta_{X},p^{\prime}}(\omega_{j})=\frac{1}{V_{\omega_{j}}}\int_{Y}\left|\log\left(V_{\omega_{j}}^{-1}\frac{\omega_{j}^{n}}{(\pi^{*}\theta_{X})^{n}}\right)\right|^{p^{\prime}}\omega_{j}^{n}\leq K^{\prime}.

(3)

There exists
B>0
such that for all

,

(1.9)

\frac{1}{V_{\omega_{j}}}\int_{Y}\left(\mathrm{R}(\omega_{j})\right)^{2}\omega_{j}^{n}\leq B.

Our next result identifies

as an RCD space if the singular Kähler metric admits a regularization as in Definition

Theorem 1.2

Let

(1)

) and (

2.
Uniform linear estimates

Let
X
be an
n
-dimensional compact Kähler manifold equipped with a fixed background Kähler metric
\theta_{X}
. We define
\mathcal{V}_{B}(X,\theta_{X},n,A,p,K)\subset\mathcal{V}(X,\theta_{X},n,A,p,K)
with
p>n
to be the set of smooth Kähler metrics
\omega
with

(2.1)

\frac{1}{V_{\omega}}\int_{X}|\mathrm{Ric}(\omega)_{-}|^{2}\omega^{n}\leq B,

where
\mathrm{Ric}(\omega)_{-}
denotes the absolute value of the smallest negative eigenvalue of
\mathrm{Ric}(\omega)
and it is defined to be zero if
\mathrm{Ric}(\omega)\geq 0
. We observe that

(2.2)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{V_{\omega}}\int_{X}|\mathrm{Ric}(\omega)_{-}|^{2}\omega^{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{V_{\omega}}\int_{X}|\mathrm{R}(\omega)|^{2}\omega^{n}-\frac{1}{V_{\omega}}\int_{X}\mathrm{Ric}(\omega)\wedge\mathrm{Ric}(\omega)\wedge\omega^{n-2}

(2.3)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}a(\omega)-[K_{X}]^{2}\cdot[\omega]^{n-2}.

Hence the assumption (
2.1
) is equivalent to a uniform bound for the Calabi energy for
\omega\in\mathcal{V}(X,\theta_{X},n,A,p,K)
.

We consider the Laplace equation
\Delta_{\omega}u=f

on
(X,\omega)
for
f\in C^{\infty}(X)
, where
\Delta_{\omega}
is the Laplace operator associated to the Kähler metric
\omega
. It is obvious that
\int_{X}f\omega^{n}=0
. We will begin our proof for Theorem
1.1
with the following lemma.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\omega\in\mathcal{V}(X,\theta_{X},n,A,B,p,K)
. Suppose

\Delta u=f,~\int_{X}u\omega^{n}=0

for
u,f\in C^{\infty}(X)
. Then there exists
C=C(X,\theta_{X},n,A,B,p,K,\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(X)})>0
such that

(2.4)

\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(X)}+\frac{1}{V_{\omega}}\int_{X}|\nabla u|^{2}_{\omega}\omega^{n}\leq C.

Proof.

This follows immediately from the uniform
L^{q}(X,\omega)
-estimates
[
24
]
on Green’s function
G
of
(X,\omega)
, for some
q=q(X,\theta_{X},n,A,p,K)>1
. Indeed, for any point
x\in X

u(x)=\frac{1}{V_{\omega}}\int_{X}u\omega^{n}+\int_{X}(-G(x,\cdot))\Delta_{\omega}u\omega^{n}=\int_{X}(-G(x,\cdot))f\omega^{n}.

The
L^{\infty}
-bound of
u
follows from this by taking supremum over all
x\in X
. Multiplying the equation
\Delta u=f
by
u
and integrating it over
(X,\omega^{n})
gives the
L^{2}(X,\omega)
-bound of
\nabla u
.
∎

If fact, if the volume measure ratio is in
L^{p}
for some
p>1
, then the
L^{\infty}
-bound for
f
in the assumption of Lemma
2.1
can be replaced by the
L^{\gamma}(X,\omega)
-bound for some
\gamma>n
.
The following proposition combined with Lemma
2.1
will imply the first part of Theorem
1.1
.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
\omega\in\mathcal{V}_{B}(X,\theta_{X},n,A,p,K)
. Suppose

(2.5)

\Delta_{\omega}u=f

for
u,f\in C^{\infty}(X)
. Then there exists
C=C(X,\theta_{X},n,A,B,p,K,\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(X)})>0
such that

(2.6)

\frac{1}{V_{\omega}}\int_{X}\left(|\nabla\nabla u|^{2}_{\omega}+|\nabla\overline{\nabla}u|^{2}_{\omega}+|\nabla u|_{\omega}^{4}\right)\omega^{n}\leq C,

where
\nabla
and
\overline{\nabla}
are covariant derivatives associated to
\omega
.

Proof.

First, we can assume that
u\geq 1
by replacing
u
by
u-\inf_{X}u+1
.
\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(X)}
is still uniformly bounded. For convenience, we drop the underscore
\omega
for norms of the covariant derivatives in the proof. We apply the Bochner’s formula to
|\nabla u|^{2}
and have

(2.7)

\Delta|\nabla u|^{2}=|\nabla\nabla u|^{2}+|\nabla\overline{\nabla}u|^{2}-2\langle\nabla u,\nabla f\rangle_{\omega}+2\mathrm{Ric}_{\omega}(\nabla u,\overline{\nabla}u).

We let
\phi:=u^{\beta}
for some
\beta\in(0,1]
to be determined. Then

(2.8)

\Delta\phi=-\beta u^{\beta-1}f+\beta(\beta-1)u^{\beta-2}|\nabla u|^{2}=-\beta\phi^{1-\frac{1}{\beta}}f+\frac{\beta-1}{\beta}\phi^{-1}|\nabla\phi|^{2}.

Applying the Bochner’s formula again, we have

\displaystyle\Delta|\nabla\phi|^{2}\geq

\displaystyle~|\nabla\nabla\phi|^{2}+|\nabla\overline{\nabla}\phi|^{2}+2\langle\nabla\phi,\nabla\big(-\beta\phi^{1-\frac{1}{\beta}}f+\frac{\beta-1}{\beta}\phi^{-1}|\nabla\phi|^{2}\big)\rangle-2\mathrm{Ric}(\omega)_{-}|\nabla\phi|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle~|\nabla\nabla\phi|^{2}+|\nabla\overline{\nabla}\phi|^{2}-2\beta\langle\nabla\phi,\nabla(\phi^{1-\frac{1}{\beta}}f)\rangle-2\mathrm{Ric}(\omega)_{-}|\nabla\phi|^{2}

\displaystyle~+\frac{2(1-\beta)}{\beta}\phi^{-2}|\nabla\phi|^{4}-\frac{4(1-\beta)}{\beta}\phi^{-1}|\nabla\phi|^{2}\cdot|\nabla|\nabla\phi\|.

For the last term, we have

\displaystyle\frac{4(1-\beta)}{\beta}\phi^{-1}|\nabla\phi|^{2}\cdot|\nabla|\nabla\phi\|\leq

\displaystyle~\frac{1}{2}|\nabla|\nabla\phi\|^{2}+\frac{8(1-\beta)^{2}}{\beta^{2}}\phi^{-2}|{\nabla\phi}|^{4}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle~\frac{1}{4}(|\nabla\nabla\phi|^{2}+|\nabla\overline{\nabla}\phi|^{2})+\frac{1-\beta}{\beta}\phi^{-2}|{\nabla\phi}|^{4},

where in the second inequality we use the Kato’s inequality and choose
\beta
close enough to
1
, and in fact,
\beta=8/9
suffices. The term involving the Ricci curvature satisfies

2\mathrm{Ric}(\omega)_{-}|\nabla\phi|^{2}\leq\frac{1-\beta}{2\beta}\phi^{-2}|\nabla\phi|^{4}+\frac{2\beta}{1-\beta}\phi^{2}|\mathrm{Ric}(\omega)_{-}|^{2}.

Combining these, we get

\displaystyle\Delta|\nabla\phi|^{2}\geq

\displaystyle~\frac{1}{2}(|\nabla\nabla\phi|^{2}+|\nabla\overline{\nabla}\phi|^{2})-2\beta\langle\nabla\phi,\nabla(\phi^{1-\frac{1}{\beta}}f)\rangle+\frac{(1-\beta)}{2\beta}\phi^{-2}|\nabla\phi|^{4}

\displaystyle~-\frac{2\beta}{1-\beta}\phi^{2}|\mathrm{Ric}(\omega)_{-}|^{2}

Integrating this inequality over
(X,\omega^{n})
yields

\displaystyle~\frac{1}{V_{\omega}}\int_{X}\Big(\frac{1}{2}(|\nabla\nabla\phi|^{2}+|\nabla\overline{\nabla}\phi|^{2})+\frac{(1-\beta)}{2\beta}\phi^{-2}|\nabla\phi|^{4}\Big)\omega^{n}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle~\frac{1}{V_{\omega}}\int_{X}\big(2\beta\langle\nabla\phi,\nabla(\phi^{1-\frac{1}{\beta}}f)\rangle+\frac{2\beta}{1-\beta}\phi^{2}|\mathrm{Ric}(\omega)_{-}|^{2}\big)\omega^{n}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle~-\frac{1}{V_{\omega}}\int_{X}2\beta\phi^{1-\frac{1}{\beta}}f\Delta\phi+CB

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~-\frac{1}{V_{\omega}}\int_{X}2\beta\phi^{1-\frac{1}{\beta}}f(-\beta\phi^{1-\frac{1}{\beta}}f+\frac{\beta-1}{\beta}\phi^{-1}|\nabla\phi|^{2})+CB

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle~C,

where in the last step we use the fact that
\phi
and
f
are both uniformly bounded. Since
\nabla\phi=\beta u^{\beta-1}\nabla u
, and
\nabla^{2}\phi=\beta u^{\beta-1}\nabla^{2}u+\beta(\beta-1)u^{\beta-2}\nabla u\otimes\nabla u
, we see that

\frac{1}{V_{\omega}}\int_{X}\left(|\nabla\nabla u|^{2}+|\nabla\overline{\nabla}u|^{2}+|\nabla u|^{4}\right)\omega^{n}\leq C.

This completes the proof of Proposition
2.1
.
∎

Remark 2.1
.

By a very similar argument as in Proposition
2.1
, one can obtain a slight generalization for the linear estimate for
u
by only assuming a
L^{2-\epsilon}
-bound for
\mathrm{Ric}(\omega)_{-}
for a small
\epsilon>0
. In this case, one would have

(2.9)

\int_{X}\left(|\nabla u|^{2(2-\delta)}_{\omega}+\frac{|\nabla\overline{\nabla}u|^{2}_{\omega}+|\nabla\nabla u|^{2}_{\omega}}{|\nabla u|^{2\delta}_{\omega}}\right)\omega^{n}\leq C.

for some small
\delta=\delta(\epsilon)>0
.

We now complete the proof of Theorem
1.1
by deriving uniform Sobolev estimates for Green’s functions.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
\omega\in\mathcal{V}(X,\theta_{X},n,A,B,p,K)
. For any
x\in X
and
r>0
,
there exists a
C=C(X,\theta_{X},n,A,B,p,K,r)>0

such that

(2.10)

\frac{1}{V_{\omega}}\int_{X\setminus B_{\omega}(x,r)}\left(V_{\omega}^{2}|\nabla\nabla G(x,\cdot)|^{2}_{\omega}+V_{\omega}^{2}|\nabla\overline{\nabla}G(x,\cdot)|^{2}_{\omega}+V_{\omega}^{4}|\nabla G(x,\cdot)|^{4}_{\omega}\right)\omega^{n}\leq C.

Proof.

Let
U_{s}=X\setminus B_{\omega}(x,s)
for
s>0
. By
[
24
]
, we may also assume for a fixed
b>>1
,

(2.11)

\frac{1}{V_{\omega}}\leq G(x,y)\leq\frac{1}{V_{\omega}}\frac{C_{b}}{d_{\omega}(x,y)^{b}},\quad\forall x\neq y\in X.

Consider the function
h(y):=V_{\omega}G(x,y)
, which satisfies the equation

\Delta h(y)=1

for
y\in U_{r}
. We have by (
2.11
)

(2.12)

1\leq h\leq C,\quad\text{in }U_{r},

where all the constants
C
depend on
r
. We choose a cut-off function
\rho_{1}
satisfying

\rho_{1}|_{U_{2r}}=1,~\rho_{1}|_{X\setminus U_{r}}=0,~|\nabla\rho_{1}|_{\omega}\leq\frac{4}{r}.

Multiplying
h\rho_{1}^{2}
on both sides of the equation
\Delta h=1/V_{\omega}
and applying integration by parts, we obtain

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{V_{\omega}}\int_{U_{r}}h\rho_{1}^{2}\omega^{n}=

\displaystyle~\frac{1}{V_{\omega}}\int_{U_{r}}\rho_{1}^{2}|\nabla h|_{\omega}^{2}+2\rho_{1}h\langle\nabla\rho_{1},\nabla h\rangle

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle~\frac{1}{V_{\omega}}\int_{U_{r}}\frac{1}{2}\rho_{1}^{2}|\nabla h|_{\omega}^{2}-4h^{2}|\nabla\rho_{1}|^{2}.

Hence, we have

(2.13)

\frac{1}{V_{\omega}}\int_{U_{2r}}|\nabla h|_{\omega}^{2}\omega^{n}\leq\frac{1}{V_{\omega}}\int_{U_{r}}\rho_{1}^{2}|\nabla h|_{\omega}^{2}\omega^{n}\leq C_{x}.

We write
\psi:=h^{\beta}
for
\beta=8/9
. We have

(2.14)

\Delta\psi=\beta h^{\beta-1}+\beta(\beta-1)h^{\beta-2}|\nabla h|^{2}=\beta\psi^{1-\frac{1}{\beta}}+\frac{\beta-1}{\beta}\psi^{-1}|\nabla\psi|^{2}.

By the Bochner’s formula, we obtain that

\displaystyle\Delta|\nabla\psi|^{2}\geq

\displaystyle~|\nabla\nabla\psi|^{2}+|\nabla\overline{\nabla}\psi|^{2}+2\langle\nabla\psi,\nabla\big(\beta\psi^{1-\frac{1}{\beta}}+\frac{\beta-1}{\beta}\psi^{-1}|\nabla\psi|^{2}\big)\rangle-2\mathrm{Ric}(\omega)_{-}|\nabla\psi|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle~|\nabla\nabla\psi|^{2}+|\nabla\overline{\nabla}\psi|^{2}+2\beta\langle\nabla\psi,\nabla(\psi^{1-\frac{1}{\beta}})\rangle-2\mathrm{Ric}(\omega)_{-}|\nabla\psi|^{2}

\displaystyle~+\frac{2(1-\beta)}{\beta}\psi^{-2}|\nabla\psi|^{4}-\frac{4(1-\beta)}{\beta}\psi^{-1}|\nabla\psi|^{2}\cdot|\nabla|\nabla\psi\|.

We estimate the last term as follows

\displaystyle\frac{4(1-\beta)}{\beta}\psi^{-1}|\nabla\psi|^{2}\cdot|\nabla|\nabla\psi\|\leq

\displaystyle~\frac{1}{2}|\nabla|\nabla\psi\|^{2}+\frac{8(1-\beta)^{2}}{\beta^{2}}\psi^{-2}|{\nabla\psi}|^{4}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle~\frac{1}{4}(|\nabla\nabla\psi|^{2}+|\nabla\overline{\nabla}\psi|^{2})+\frac{1-\beta}{\beta}\psi^{-2}|{\nabla\psi}|^{4},

where in the second inequality we use the Kato’s inequality. The term involving the Ricci curvature satisfies

2\mathrm{Ric}(\omega)_{-}|\nabla\psi|^{2}\leq\frac{1-\beta}{2\beta}\psi^{-2}|\nabla\psi|^{4}+\frac{2\beta}{1-\beta}\psi^{2}|\mathrm{Ric}(\omega)_{-}|^{2}.

Combining these, we get

(2.15)

\begin{split}\Delta|\nabla\psi|^{2}\geq&~\frac{1}{2}(|\nabla\nabla\psi|^{2}+|\nabla\overline{\nabla}\psi|^{2})+2\beta\langle\nabla\psi,\nabla\psi^{1-\frac{1}{\beta}}\rangle+\frac{(1-\beta)}{2\beta}\psi^{-2}|\nabla\psi|^{4}\\
&~-\frac{2\beta}{1-\beta}\psi^{2}|\mathrm{Ric}(\omega)_{-}|^{2}.\end{split}

We now choose a cut-off function
\rho_{2}
satisfying

\rho_{2}|_{U_{3r}}=1,~\rho_{2}|_{X\setminus U_{2r}}=0,~|\nabla\rho_{2}|_{\omega}\leq\frac{4}{r}.

We multiply both sides of (
2.15
) by
\rho_{2}^{2}
and integrate the resulted inequality over
U_{2r}
. We obtain

(2.16)

\begin{split}&~\frac{1}{V_{\omega}}\int_{U_{2r}}\rho_{2}^{2}\Big(\frac{1}{2}(|\nabla\nabla\psi|^{2}+|\nabla\overline{\nabla}\psi|^{2})+\frac{(1-\beta)}{2\beta}\psi^{-2}|\nabla\psi|^{4}\Big)\omega^{n}\\
\leq&~\frac{1}{V_{\omega}}\int_{U_{2r}}\frac{2\beta}{1-\beta}\rho_{2}^{2}\psi^{2}|\mathrm{Ric}(\omega)_{-}|^{2}-2\beta\rho_{2}^{2}\langle\nabla\psi,\nabla\psi^{1-\frac{1}{\beta}}\rangle+\rho_{2}^{2}\Delta|\nabla\psi|^{2}.\end{split}

The first term on the RHS of (
2.16
) is bounded by
CB
. The second term satisfies

\int_{U_{2r}}\frac{2(1-\beta)}{V_{\omega}}\rho_{2}^{2}\psi^{-\frac{1}{\beta}}|\nabla\psi|^{2}\omega^{n}\leq C,

by (
2.13
) and the equation
\nabla\psi=\beta h^{\beta-1}\nabla h
. The last term satisfies

\displaystyle\frac{1}{V_{\omega}}\int_{U_{2r}}\rho_{2}^{2}\Delta|\nabla\psi|^{2}\omega^{n}

\displaystyle~=-\frac{1}{V_{\omega}}\int_{U_{2r}}4\rho_{2}|\nabla\psi|\cdot\langle\nabla\rho_{2},\nabla|{\nabla\psi}|\rangle\omega^{n}

\displaystyle~\leq\frac{1}{V_{\omega}}\int_{U_{2r}}4\rho_{2}|\nabla\psi|\cdot|\nabla\rho_{2}|\cdot|\nabla|{\nabla\psi|}|\omega^{n}

\displaystyle~\leq\frac{1}{V_{\omega}}\int_{U_{2r}}\left(\frac{1}{2}\rho_{2}^{2}|\nabla|{\nabla\psi|}|^{2}+8|\nabla\psi|^{2}\cdot|\nabla\rho_{2}|^{2}\right)\omega^{n}

\displaystyle~\leq\frac{1}{V_{\omega}}\int_{U_{2r}}\frac{1}{4}\rho_{2}^{2}(|\nabla\nabla\psi|^{2}+|\nabla\overline{\nabla}\psi|^{2})\omega^{n}+C.

Combining the above estimates, we obtain

\displaystyle~\frac{1}{V_{\omega}}\int_{U_{3r}}\Big(\frac{1}{4}(|\nabla\nabla\psi|^{2}+|\nabla\overline{\nabla}\psi|^{2})+\frac{(1-\beta)}{2\beta}\psi^{-2}|\nabla\psi|^{4}\Big)\omega^{n}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle~\frac{1}{V_{\omega}}\int_{U_{3r}}\rho_{2}^{2}\Big(\frac{1}{4}(|\nabla\nabla\psi|^{2}+|\nabla\overline{\nabla}\psi|^{2})+\frac{(1-\beta)}{2\beta}\psi^{-2}|\nabla\psi|^{4}\Big)\omega^{n}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle~C.

This directly implies that

\frac{1}{V_{\omega}}\int_{U_{3r}}\Big(|\nabla\nabla h|^{2}+|\nabla\overline{\nabla}h|^{2}+|\nabla h|^{4}\Big)\omega^{n}\leq C

and we have completed the proof of Proposition by replacing
r
by
3r
.
∎

3.
Regularization and the Lipchitz property for eigenfunctions

We will now apply the Sobolev estimates of Theorem
1.1
to establish analytic estimates on singular Kähler spaces. In this section, we will always assume that
X
is an
n
-dimensional compact normal Kähler variety.

Definition 3.1
.

A positive closed
(1,1)
-current
\theta
on
X
is said to be a smooth Kähler metric if for any
p\in X
, there exists a local holomorphic embedding
\iota:U\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{N}
from an open neighborhood
U
of
p
such that
\theta
is the restriction of a smooth Kähler metric
\tilde{\theta}
in an open neighborhood
\iota(U)\subset\mathbb{C}^{N}
.

There always exist smooth Kähler metrics on projective varieties via projective embbedings. Let
\pi:Y\rightarrow X
be a resolution singularities for
X
. We fix smooth background Kähler metrics
\theta_{X}
and
\theta_{Y}
on
X
and
Y
respectively. Let
\omega\in C^{\infty}(X^{\circ})\cap\nu(X,\theta_{X},n,A,p,K)
be a singular Kähler metric on
X
with
p>n
. we assume that
\omega
admits the following regularization.

(1)

There exist a sequence smooth Kähler metrics
\omega_{j}
and a Kähler class
\alpha
on
Y
satisfying

(3.1)

[\omega_{j}]=[\pi^{*}\omega]+\epsilon_{j}\alpha,~\epsilon_{j}\rightarrow 0^{+},~\omega_{j}\rightarrow\pi^{*}\omega~{\rm in}~C^{2}_{loc}(\pi^{-1}(X^{\circ})).

(2)

There exists
p^{\prime}>n
and
K^{\prime}>0
such that for all
j
,

(3.2)

\mathcal{N}_{Y}(\omega_{j})=\frac{1}{V_{\omega_{j}}}\int_{Y}\left|\log\left(V_{\omega_{j}}^{-1}\frac{\omega_{j}^{n}}{\theta_{Y}^{n}}\right)\right|^{p^{\prime}}\omega_{j}^{n}\leq K^{\prime}.

(3)

There exists
B>0
such that for all
j
,

(3.3)

\int_{Y}|\mathrm{Ric}(\omega_{j})_{-}|^{2}\omega_{j}^{n}\leq B.

The following is the main result in this section.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
X
be normal Kähler variety with log terminal singularities of
\dim_{\mathbb{C}}(X)=n
. Suppose
\omega\in\mathcal{V}(X,\theta_{X},n,A,p,K)
with
p>n
is a singular Kähler metric satisfying the following properties.

(1)

\mathrm{Ric}(\omega)\geq-\omega
in
X^{\circ}
.

(2)

\omega
admits a regularization
(Y,\{\omega_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty})
satisfying (
3.1
), (
3.2
) and (
3.3
).

Then every eigenfunction of
\Delta_{\omega}
is Lipschitz.

We begin our proof for Proposition
3.1
with the following lemma.

Lemma 3.1
.

Suppose
(Y,\{\omega_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty})
satisfy (
3.1
), (
3.2
). If there exists
B>0
such that

(3.4)

\mathcal{C}_{a}(\omega_{j})=\frac{1}{V_{\omega_{j}}}\int_{Y}(\mathrm{R}(\omega_{j}))^{2}\omega_{j}^{n}\leq B,

then there exists
B^{\prime}>0
such that

\int_{Y}|\mathrm{Ric}(\omega_{j})|^{2}\omega_{j}^{n}\leq B.

Proof.

By (
2.2
), we have

\displaystyle\int_{Y}|\mathrm{Ric}(\omega_{j})|^{2}\omega_{j}^{n}

\displaystyle~=

\displaystyle~\int(\mathrm{R}(\omega_{j}))^{2}\omega^{n}-\int_{X}\mathrm{Ric}(\omega_{j})\wedge\mathrm{Ric}(\omega_{j})\wedge\omega_{j}^{n-2}

\displaystyle~=

\displaystyle~\int(\mathrm{R}(\omega_{j}))^{2}\omega^{n}-[K_{Y}]^{2}\cdot[\omega_{j}]^{n-2}\leq\int(\mathrm{R}(\omega_{j}))^{2}\omega^{n}+C

for some uniform
C>0
. ∎

Suppose
u\in W^{1,2}(X,\omega)
is an eigenfunction of
\Delta_{\omega}
satisfying

\Delta_{\omega}u=-\lambda u

for some
\lambda>0
. The following estimates are proved in
[
25
]
(c.f. Lemma 11.2 in
[
25
]
).

Lemma 3.2
.

u\in L^{\infty}(X)\cap C^{\infty}(X^{\circ})
.

Since the singular set
\mathcal{S}(X)
is an analytic subvariety of
X
, the following result of J. Sturm
[
40
]
holds due to the uniform
L^{\infty}
-bound for the Kähler potentials of
\omega
and
\omega_{j}
.

Lemma 3.3
.

\mathcal{S}(X)
has capacity
0
with respect to
\omega
and
\omega_{j}
for all
j
. More precisely, there exist a sequence of smooth cut-off functions
\{\rho_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
satisfying the following conditions.

(1)

There exist a sequence of relatively compact domains
\{\Omega_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
of
X^{\circ}
with

\Omega_{1}\subset\subset\Omega_{2}\subset\subset...\subset\subset\Omega_{k}...\rightarrow X^{\circ}

and

\rho_{k}|_{\Omega_{k}}=1,~{\rm supp}\rho_{k}\subset\subset X^{\circ},~0\leq\rho\leq 1.

(2)

\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\int_{X}|\nabla\rho_{k}|_{\omega}^{2}\omega^{n}=\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{j\geq 1}\int_{X}|\nabla\rho_{k}|_{\omega_{j}}^{2}\omega_{j}^{n}=0.

Let
G=G(\cdot,\cdot)
be Green’s function of
(X,\omega)
constructed as in
[
25
]
. It satisfies the following estimates.

Lemma 3.4
.

There exist
q=q(n,p)>1
and
C=C(X,\theta_{X},n,A,p,K)>0
such that

(3.5)

\sup_{x\in X^{\circ}}\int_{X}|G(x,\cdot)|^{q}\omega^{n}\leq C,\quad\inf_{(x,y)\in X^{\circ}\times X^{\circ}}G(x,y)\geq-C.

Moreover, Green’s formula holds for any smooth function with compact support in
X^{\circ}
, i.e., for any
u\in C^{\infty}(X^{\circ})
with
{\rm supp}u\subset\subset X^{\circ}
, we have for any
x\in X^{\circ}

(3.6)

u(x)=\frac{1}{V_{\omega}}\int_{X}u\omega^{n}-\int_{X}G(x,\cdot)\Delta_{\omega}u\omega^{n}.

Proof.

We fix a point
x\in X^{\circ}
and view Green’s function
G(x,y)
as a function of
y
.
It follows from the main theorem of
[
25
]
that for some uniform constants
q,C>0
, these estimates in (
3.5
) hold uniformly for Green’s functions
G_{j}(x,\cdot)
on
(Y,\omega_{j})
. Since
\omega_{j}
converges in
C^{2}_{loc}(X^{\circ})
to
\omega
, by elliptic regularity we see that
G_{j}(x,\cdot)
converges uniformly in
C^{3}
on any compact subset
K\subset\subset X^{\circ}\backslash\{x\}
to Green’s function
G(x,\cdot)
. Then the estimates in (
3.5
) follow from Fatou’s lemma.

To verify (
3.6
), we identify
u
with its pullback
\pi^{*}u
on
Y
, and
x
with
\pi^{-1}(x)
. We apply Green’s formula

u(x)=\frac{1}{V_{\omega_{j}}}\int_{Y}u\omega_{j}^{n}-\int_{Y}G_{j}(x,\cdot)\Delta_{\omega_{j}}u\omega_{j}^{n},

where
V_{\omega_{j}}=[\omega_{j}]^{n}
. Passing to the limit, the first integral on the RHS converges to the first integral in (
3.6
). For the second integral, we calculate (denote
\Omega\subset X^{\circ}
to be the compact support of
u
)

\displaystyle~\Big|\int_{Y}G_{j}(x,\cdot)\Delta_{\omega_{j}}u\,\omega_{j}^{n}-\int_{X}G(x,\cdot)\Delta_{\omega}u\,\omega^{n}\Big|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle~\Big|\int_{\Omega}G_{j}(x,\cdot)\big(\Delta_{\omega_{j}}u\,\omega_{j}^{n}-\Delta_{\omega}u\,\omega^{n}\big)\Big|+\Big|\int_{\Omega}\big(G_{j}(x,\cdot)-G(x,\cdot)\big)\Delta_{\omega}u\,\omega^{n}\Big|.

The first term tends to
0
as
j\to\infty
since
\Delta_{\omega_{j}}u\,\omega_{j}^{n}\to\Delta_{\omega}u\,\omega^{n}
on
\Omega
while the
L^{1}
-norm of
G_{j}(x,\cdot)
is uniformly bounded. For the second term, we choose a small neighborhood
U
of
x
whose
\omega^{n}
-volume is very small. Outside of
U
,
G_{j}(x,\cdot)
converges uniformly to
G(x,\cdot)
. Hence this term can be made arbitrarily small by taking
j
large enough and invoking the Hölder’s inequality
\|G_{j}\|_{L^{1}(U,\omega^{n})}\leq\|G_{j}\|_{L^{q}(U,\omega^{n})}\big(\mathrm{Vol}(U,\omega_{j}^{n})\big)^{1/q}
and the uniform
L^{q}
bound of
G_{j}(x,\cdot)
. From this, Green’s formula (
3.6
) follows easily.
∎

Due to the lower bound of
G(x,\cdot)
in (
3.5
), we may add a constant to
G(x,\cdot)
and assume

G(x,\cdot)\geq 1.

We can further establish the following uniform estimate for a weighted
L^{2}
bound on the gradient of
G(x,\cdot)
: for any
\delta>0
, there exists a uniform constant
C_{\delta}>0
such that

(3.7)

\sup_{x\in X^{\circ}}\int_{X}\frac{|\nabla G(x,\cdot)|^{2}}{G(x,\cdot)^{1+\delta}}\omega^{n}\leq C_{\delta}.

The proof of (
3.7
) is similar to that of (
3.5
), by applying Lemma 5.6 in
[
25
]
, so we omit it.

We will now apply our estimates to eigenfunctions.

Lemma 3.5
.

Suppose
u\in W^{1,2}(X,\omega)
is an eigenfunction of
\Delta_{\omega}
with eigenvalue
\lambda>0
. Then for any
x\in X^{\circ}
, we have

(3.8)

u(x)=-\int_{X}G(x,\cdot)\Delta u\omega^{n}=\lambda\int_{X}G(x,y)u(y)\omega^{n}(y).

Proof.

We first apply the standard Green’s formula (
3.6
) to the function
u\rho_{k}
, where
\rho_{k}
is the cut-off function constructed in Lemma
3.3
. Obviously,
u\rho_{k}
has compact support in
X^{\circ}
, and

(3.9)

\displaystyle u\rho_{k}(x)

\displaystyle=-\int_{X}G(x,\cdot)(\rho_{k}\Delta u+u\Delta\rho_{k}+2\langle\nabla u,\nabla\rho_{k}\rangle)\omega^{n}+\frac{1}{V_{\omega}}\int_{X}u\rho_{k}\omega^{n}

\displaystyle=:I_{1}+I_{2},

where
I_{1}
and
I_{2}
denote the first and second integrals, respectively.
As
k\to\infty
, the LHS and the first term in
I_{1}
of (
3.9
) converge to the corresponding terms in (
3.8
).
I_{2}
clearly tends to the integral of
u
which is zero. We need to check the remaining terms tend to zero.

For the last term in
I_{1}
of (
3.9
),
x
is disjoint from
\mathrm{supp}(\nabla\rho_{k})
when
k
is sufficiently large. Hence there exists a constant
C_{x}
which depends on
x
, such that
|G(x,y)|\leq C_{x}
by
[
24
]
, for any
y\in\mathrm{supp}(\nabla\rho_{k})
. Then this term tends to zero by Hölder’s inequality, the
L^{2}
-bound of
\nabla u
and Lemma
3.3
.

For the second term in
I_{1}
, we apply integration by parts to obtain

-\int_{X}G(x,\cdot)u\Delta\rho_{k}\omega^{n}=\int_{X}\left(G(x,\cdot)\langle\nabla u,\nabla\rho_{k}\rangle+u\langle\nabla G(x,\cdot),\nabla\rho_{k}\rangle\right)\omega^{n}.

Then by the same reasoning as in the previous term, we have

\int_{X}G(x,\cdot)\langle\nabla u,\nabla\rho_{k}\rangle\omega^{n}\leq C\left(\int_{X}(G(x,\cdot))^{2}|\nabla\rho_{k}|^{2}\omega^{n}\right)^{1/2}\rightarrow 0.

Moreover,

\left|\int_{X}u\langle\nabla G(x,\cdot),\nabla\rho_{k}\rangle\right|\leq C\left(\int_{X}\frac{|\nabla G(x,\cdot)|^{2}}{G(x,\cdot)^{1+\delta}}\omega^{n}\right)^{1/2}\left(\int_{X}G(x,\cdot)^{1+\delta}|\nabla\rho_{k}|^{2}\omega^{n}\right)^{1/2}\to 0

where the first factor on the RHS is bounded by (
3.7
). This completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

Equation (
3.8
) suggests that to show
u
is Lipschitz, it suffices to prove that the integral

\int_{X}|\nabla_{x}G(x,y)|_{\omega_{x}}u(y)\omega^{n}(y)

is bounded independent of
x\in X^{\circ}
. However, in general this may not hold without additional conditions on
\omega
. Our strategy is to approximate this integral by the corresponding quantities with respect to
\omega_{j}
. More precisely, we choose cut-off functions
\rho_{k}
as in Lemma
3.3
and approximate
u
by letting

u_{k}:=u\rho_{k}.

u_{k}
can also seen as defined on
Y
with compact support in
\pi^{-1}(X^{\circ})
. We further define

(3.10)

\hat{v}_{j,k}(x)=\int_{Y}G_{j}(x,y)u_{k}(y)\omega_{j}^{n}(y),

where
G_{j}
is Green’s function of
\omega_{j}
.
\hat{v}_{j,k}
is uniformly bounded (independent of
j
and
k
) in
L^{\infty}(Y)
as
u
is bounded in
L^{\infty}(X)
. By
[
24
,
25
]
, there exist
b>2n-2
and
C_{b}>0
such that

1\leq G_{j}(x,y)\leq\frac{C_{b}}{d_{j}(x,y)^{b}},~{\rm for~all~}x\neq y\in Y,

where
d_{j}
is the distance function induced by
\omega_{j}
. Since we are interested in the gradient estimate of
\hat{v}_{j,k}
, we will add a bounded constant
\Lambda>0
to
\hat{v}_{j,k}
so that
v_{j,k}:=\hat{v}_{j,k}+\Lambda
satisfies

1\leq v_{j,k}\leq C

for a uniform constant
C>0
due to the uniform
L^{\infty}
bound for
u_{k}
. It is clear that for each
j
we have

(3.11)

\Delta_{\omega_{j}}v_{j,k}=-u_{k}.

Hence
|\Delta_{\omega_{j}}v_{j,k}|\leq C
for all
j
and
k
. Integrating the equation
v_{j,k}\Delta_{\omega_{j}}v_{j,k}=-u_{k}v_{j,k}
, we immediately obtain

(3.12)

\int_{Y}|\nabla v_{j,k}|^{2}_{\omega_{j}}\omega_{j}^{n}\leq C.

Moreover, we observe that from the local
C^{2}(Y\backslash\pi^{-1}(S))
convergence of
\omega_{j}
to
\pi^{*}\omega
, elliptic regularity implies that by passing to a subsequence of
j
’s, we have

(3.13)

v_{j,k}\xrightarrow{C^{3,\alpha}_{loc}(\pi^{-1}(X^{\circ}))}v_{\infty,k},

In particular, we have

(3.14)

v_{\infty,k}(x)=\int_{X}G(x,y)u_{k}(y)\omega^{n}(y)+\Lambda,

for any
x\in X\backslash S
, and on this set,

(3.15)

\Delta_{\omega}v_{\infty,k}=-u_{k}.

Since
v_{\infty,k}
is bounded, both equation (
3.14
) and (
3.15
) hold globally on
X
and
v_{\infty,k}\in W^{1,2}(X,\omega)
.

Returning to
v_{j,k}
and equation (
3.11
), we have the following lemma as an immediate corollary of Theorem
2.1
.

Lemma 3.6
.

There exists
C>0
such that for all
j
and
k
, we have

(3.16)

\int_{Y}\left(|\nabla\nabla v_{j,k}|^{2}_{\omega_{j}}+|\nabla\overline{\nabla}v_{j,k}|^{2}_{\omega_{j}})+|\nabla v_{j,k}|_{\omega_{j}}^{4}\right)\omega_{j}^{n}\leq C.

Corollary 3.1
.

There exists
C>0
such that

(3.17)

\int_{X}\left(|\nabla\nabla u|^{2}_{\omega}+|\nabla\overline{\nabla}u|^{2}_{\omega}+|\nabla u|_{\omega}^{4}\right)\omega^{n}\leq C.

Proof.

Passing to the limit as in (
3.13
), we can apply Fatou’s Lemma and Lemma
3.6
to obtain

(3.18)

\int_{X}\left(|\nabla\nabla v_{\infty,k}|^{2}_{\omega}+|\nabla\overline{\nabla}v_{\infty,k}|^{2}_{\omega}+|\nabla v_{\infty,k}|_{\omega}^{4}\right)\omega^{n}\leq C.

Recall that as
k\to\infty
we have

v_{\infty,k}(x)=\int_{X}G(x,y)u_{k}(y)\omega^{n}(y)+\Lambda\to\int_{X}G(x,y)u(y)\omega^{n}(y)+\Lambda=\lambda^{-1}u(x)+\Lambda,

and this convergence is locally uniform in
C^{3}(K,\omega)
for any compact subset
K\subset X^{\circ}
by elliptic regularity of equation (
3.15
). Again by Fatou’s Lemma, we conclude from (
3.18
) that

\int_{X}\left(|\nabla\nabla u|^{2}+|\nabla\overline{\nabla}u|^{2}+|\nabla u|^{4}\right)\omega^{n}\leq C.

This proves the corollary.
∎

We now fix a point
x\in\{\rho_{k}=1\}^{\circ}
and identify
x
with its pre-image
\pi^{-1}(x)\in Y
. We can also identify
\pi^{*}u_{k}
and
u_{k}
since
u_{k}
is compactly supported on
X^{\circ}
. We denote

U_{k}=X\setminus\{\rho_{k}=1\}.

For convenience, we can also identify
U_{k}
as an open subset in
Y
.

Lemma 3.7
.

For any
k_{0}>0
and
x\in\{\rho_{k_{0}}=1\}
, there exists
C=C(k_{0},x)>0
such that for all
k>k_{0}

\int_{U_{k}}\left(|\nabla\nabla G(x,\cdot)|^{2}_{\omega}+|\nabla\nabla G(x,\cdot)|^{2}_{\omega}+|\nabla G(x,\cdot)|_{\omega}^{4}\right)\omega^{n}\leq C.

Proof.

Since
x
is disjoint from
U_{k}
for all sufficiently large
k
, there exists
c>0
such that for all sufficiently large
i,k
,
d_{j}(x,\partial U_{k})>c
. The lemma then follows from Proposition
2.2
.
∎

Lemma 3.8
.

There exists
C>0
such that for any
x\in X^{\circ}
, we have

|\nabla u|_{\omega}(x)\leq C+(\lambda+1)\int_{X}G(x,\cdot)|\nabla u|_{\omega}\omega^{n}.

Proof.

By Bochner’s formula for
\omega
on
X^{\circ}
, we have

\Delta_{\omega}|\nabla u|^{2}\geq|\nabla\nabla u|^{2}+|\nabla\overline{\nabla}u|^{2}-2\lambda|\nabla u|^{2}-2|\nabla u|^{2}.

Applying Kato’s inequality, we have

(3.19)

\Delta|{\nabla u}|\geq-(\lambda+1)|{\nabla u}|.

We now apply Green’s formula to the smooth function with compact support,
\rho_{k}|\nabla u|_{\omega}
. Then for any
x\in X^{\circ}
,

\displaystyle~|\nabla u|(x)=|\nabla u|(x)\rho_{k}(x)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle~\frac{1}{V}\int_{X}\rho_{k}|\nabla u|\omega^{n}+\int_{X}G(x,\cdot)\big(|\nabla u|\Delta\rho_{k}+2\langle\nabla\rho_{k},\nabla|\nabla u|\rangle_{\omega}+(\lambda+1)\rho_{k}|\nabla u|\big)\omega^{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle I_{1}+I_{2}

for sufficiently large
k
.
We will estimate the three terms in
I_{2}
.

In a fixed small neighborhood
U_{x}\subset X^{\circ}
of
x
,
|\nabla u|
is smooth and bound, while
G(x,\cdot)
is integrable in
U_{x}
. In
X\setminus U_{x}
,
G(x,\cdot)
is bounded by a constant (possibly dependent on
x
) and
|\nabla u|
is integrable. Hence the function
G(x,\cdot)|\nabla u|
is
L^{1}
integrable in
(X,\omega^{n})
. Then by the dominated convergence theorem, we have for the third term

\lim_{k\to\infty}\int_{X}(\lambda+1)G(x,\cdot)|\nabla u|\rho_{k}\omega^{n}=(\lambda+1)\int_{X}G(x,\cdot)|\nabla u|\omega^{n}.

For the second term, we observe that the corresponding integral is in fact taken over
U_{k}
, where
G(x,\cdot)
is bounded by a constant
C_{x}>0
. By Hölder’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{X}G(x,\cdot)2\langle\nabla\rho_{k},\nabla|\nabla u|\rangle_{\omega}\omega^{n}\Big|\leq

\displaystyle~C_{x}\int_{U_{k}}|\nabla\rho_{k}|\cdot|\nabla|\nabla u\|\omega^{n}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle~C_{x}\Big(\int_{U_{k}}|\nabla\rho_{k}|^{2}\omega^{n}\Big)^{1/2}\cdot\Big(\int_{U_{k}}|\nabla|\nabla u\|^{2}\omega^{n}\Big)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle~C_{x}\Big(\int_{U_{k}}|\nabla\rho_{k}|^{2}\omega^{n}\Big)^{1/2}\to 0

as
k\to\infty
, where Kato’s inequality and Corollary
3.1
are used in the last inequality.

For the first term of
I_{2}
, we rewrite it as

(3.20)

\displaystyle\int_{X}G(x,\cdot)|\nabla u|\Delta_{\omega}\rho_{k}=

\displaystyle~-\int_{X}\big(|\nabla u|\langle\nabla G(x,\cdot),\nabla\rho_{k}\rangle+G(x,\cdot)\langle\nabla\rho_{k},\nabla|\nabla u|\rangle_{\omega}\big).

The second term on the RHS of (
3.20
) tends to zero by similar argument before.
The first term on the RHS of (
3.20
) satisfies

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{X}|\nabla u|\langle\nabla G(x,\cdot),\nabla\rho_{k}\rangle\Big|\leq

\displaystyle~\Big(\int_{U_{k}}|\nabla u|^{2}|\nabla G(x,\cdot)|^{2}\omega^{n}\Big)^{1/2}\|\nabla\rho_{k}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle~\Big(\int_{U_{k}}(|\nabla u|^{4}+|\nabla G(x,\cdot)|^{4})\omega^{n}\Big)^{1/2}\|\nabla\rho_{k}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle~C_{x}\|\nabla\rho_{k}\|_{L^{2}}\to 0,\text{ as }a\to\infty,

where we apply Corollary
3.1
and Proposition
2.2
. Combining the above estimates, we obtain that

(3.21)

|\nabla u|_{\omega}(x)\leq C+(\lambda+1)\int_{X}G(x,\cdot)|\nabla u|_{\omega}\omega^{n},

for some constant
C=\frac{1}{V_{\omega}}\int_{X}|\nabla u|\omega^{n}
independent of
x
. This completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

We can now complete the proof of Proposition
3.1
.

Proof of Proposition
3.1
.

We fix
\gamma=2N+1
for some sufficiently large integer
N
, so that
\gamma^{*}=\frac{\gamma}{\gamma-1}=\frac{2N+1}{2N}
is sufficiently close to
1
. Then

\|G(x,\cdot)\|_{L^{\gamma^{*}}(X,\omega^{n})}
is uniformly bounded for all
x\in X
by Lemma
3.4
(c.f.
[
24
,
26
]
).
We define a linear operator
\mathbf{T}
by

\mathbf{T}(f)(x)=\int_{X}G(x,y)f(y)\omega^{n}(y).

By Hölder’s inequality, we have

\|\mathbf{T}(f)\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\sup_{x\in X^{\circ}}\|G(x,\cdot)\|_{L^{\gamma^{*}}}\cdot\|f\|_{L^{\gamma}}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{\gamma}}.

On the other hand, by the generalized Minkowski inequality, we also have

\|\mathbf{T}(f)\|_{L^{\gamma^{*}}}\leq\int_{X}|f|(y)\Big(\int_{X}G(x,y)^{\gamma^{*}}\omega^{n}(x)\Big)^{1/\gamma^{*}}\omega^{n}(y)\leq C\|f\|_{L^{1}}.

Thus the linear operator
\mathbf{T}
is of strong types
(\infty,\gamma)
and
(\gamma^{*},1)
. Then by the Riesz–Thorin Interpolation Theorem, it follows that the operator
\mathbf{T}
is also of strong type
(p(t),q(t))
, i.e.,

(3.22)

\|\mathbf{T}(f)\|_{L^{p(t)}}\leq C_{t}\|f\|_{L^{q(t)}}.

for any
t\in(0,1)
, where

(3.23)

\frac{1}{p(t)}=\frac{t}{\infty}+\frac{1-t}{\gamma^{*}}=\frac{1-t}{\gamma^{*}},\quad\frac{1}{q(t)}=\frac{t}{\gamma}+\frac{1-t}{1}.

Denote
\phi=|{\nabla u}|
. Set
t=\gamma^{*}/2
for (
3.23
). We have

p^{\prime}_{1}=\frac{2\gamma^{*}}{2-\gamma^{*}}=\frac{2\gamma}{\gamma-2}>2,~q^{\prime}_{1}=2.

Then

(3.24)

\|\mathbf{T}(\phi)\|_{L^{p^{\prime}_{1}}}\leq A_{1}\|\phi\|_{L^{2}}\leq B_{1}

for some fixed constants
A_{1},B_{1}>0
, since the

is bounded by
[
25
]
.
Lemma
3.8
implies that

(3.25)

\phi(x)\leq C+(1+\lambda)\mathbf{T}(\phi)(x).

Hence

is also bounded. We can now iterate the above process by repeatedly applying (

) and (

we have

\frac{1}{p^{\prime}_{k}}=\frac{1}{p^{\prime}_{k-1}}-\frac{1}{\gamma},

or equivalently,

p^{\prime}_{k}=\frac{2\gamma}{\gamma-2k}

whenever

. Therefore there exists
B_{k}>0
such that

(3.26)

\|\mathbf{T}(\phi)\|_{L^{p^{\prime}_{k}}}\leq A_{k}\|\phi\|_{L^{p^{\prime}_{k-1}}}\leq B_{k}

for some uniform constants

.
In particular, we have

from (

Applying Lemma

which gives the desired

4.
Schwarz lemma

In this section, we will prove the following Schwarz lemma.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
X
be an
n
-dimensional normal Kähler variety. Suppose that
\omega\in\mathcal{V}(X,\theta_{X},n,A,p,K)
is a singular Kähler metric that admits a regularization
(Y,\{\omega_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty})
satisfying (
3.1
), (
3.2
) and (
3.3
). If

\mathrm{Ric}(\omega)\geq-\omega

on
X^{\circ}
,
then there exists
c=c(X,\theta_{X},n,A,p,K)>0
such that on
X^{\circ}
, we have

\omega\geq c\theta_{X}.

Proof.

We will choose a smooth Kähler form
\theta_{Y}\in[\alpha]
on
Y
and a smooth metric
\omega_{0}\in[\omega]
on
X
and let

\theta_{j}=\pi^{*}\omega_{0}+\epsilon_{j}\theta_{Y}\in[\omega_{j}].

Then
\theta_{j}>\pi^{*}\theta_{X}
and we define
\varphi_{j}
by

\omega_{j}=\theta_{j}+i\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi_{j},~\sup_{X}\varphi_{j}=0.

By the uniform Nash entropy bound for
\omega_{j}
, there exists
C>0
such that

\|\varphi_{j}\|_{L^{\infty}(Y)}\leq C.

Furthemore,
\omega=\omega_{0}+i\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi
for some
\varphi\in PSH(X,\omega_{0})\cap L^{\infty}(X)
with
\sup_{X}\varphi=0
. In particular,
\varphi_{j}\rightarrow\pi^{*}\varphi
in
C^{3}_{loc}(\pi^{-1}(X^{\circ}))
by our assumption for
\omega_{j}
.

Since the smooth Kähler metric
\theta_{X}
is the restriction of smooth Kähler metric of ambient
\mathbb{C}^{N}
for a local holomorphic embedding of
X
into
\mathbb{C}^{N}
, the bisectional curvature of
\theta_{X}
is uniformly bounded above. We can apply the Chern-Lu formula and obtain

(4.1)

\Delta_{\omega_{j}}\log\tr_{\omega_{j}}(\pi^{*}\theta_{X})\geq\frac{\langle\mathrm{Ric}(\omega_{j}),\theta_{X}\rangle_{\omega_{j}}}{\tr_{\omega_{j}}(\theta_{X})}-B\tr_{\omega_{j}}(\theta_{X}),

for a constant
B\geq 0
that depends on the upper bound of the bisectional curvature of
\theta_{X}
. For
A>>1
we have

(4.2)

\Delta_{\omega_{j}}\log\big(e^{-A\varphi_{j}}\tr_{\omega_{j}}(\pi^{*}\theta_{X})\big)\geq-\mathrm{Ric}(\omega_{j})_{-}+\tr_{\omega_{j}}(\pi^{*}(A\omega_{0}-B\theta_{X}))-An,

We let
u_{j}:=e^{-A\varphi_{j}}\tr_{\omega_{j}}(\theta_{X})
and choose
A>>B+1
. Then from (
4.2
) and there exists
C>0
such that

\displaystyle\Delta_{\omega_{j}}u_{j}\geq

\displaystyle~-\mathrm{Ric}(\omega_{j})_{-}\cdot u_{j}+\frac{|\nabla u_{j}|^{2}_{j}}{u_{j}}+2u_{j}^{2}-C

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle~-\left(\mathrm{Ric}(\omega_{j})_{-}\right)^{2}+\frac{|\nabla u_{j}|^{2}_{j}}{u_{j}}+u_{j}^{2}-C.

Integrating this over
(Y,\omega_{j}^{n})
, by our assumption on the
L^{2}
-bound for
\mathrm{Ric}(\omega_{j})_{-}
, there exists
C>0
such that

(4.3)

\int_{Y}\left(\frac{|\nabla u_{j}|^{2}_{j}}{u_{j}}+u_{j}^{2}\right)\omega_{j}^{n}\leq C.

Let
u_{\infty}:=e^{-A\varphi}\tr_{\omega}(\theta_{X})
. Since
\varphi_{j}
and
\omega_{j}
converge smoothly to
\pi^{*}\varphi
and
\pi^{*}\omega
on
\pi^{-1}(X^{\circ})
as
i\to\infty
, respectively, we have

(4.4)

\int_{X}\Big(\frac{|\nabla u_{\infty}|^{2}_{\omega}}{u_{\infty}+1}+u_{\infty}^{2}\Big)\omega^{n}\leq C.

We consider the function
\phi=\log(u_{\infty}+1)
. By Chern-Lu inequality again and by choosing
A>>1
, there exists
C>0
such that on
X^{\circ}
,

\displaystyle\Delta_{\omega}\phi=

\displaystyle~\frac{u_{\infty}\Delta_{\omega}\log u_{\infty}}{u_{\infty}+1}+\frac{|\nabla u_{\infty}|^{2}_{\omega}}{u_{\infty}(u_{\infty}+1)}-\frac{|\nabla u_{\infty}|^{2}_{\omega}}{(u_{\infty}+1)^{2}}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle~\frac{u_{\infty}(u_{\infty}-C)}{u_{\infty}+1}\geq-C.

We will use the same cut-off functions
\rho_{k}
as constructed in Lemma
3.3
. Fix a point
x\in\{\rho_{k}=1\}^{\circ}
. Since
\phi=\rho_{k}\phi
on
\{\rho_{k}=1\}
, we have by Green’s formula for the function
\phi\rho_{k}
which has compact support in
X^{\circ}

(4.5)

\displaystyle\phi(x)=\frac{1}{V_{\omega}}\int_{X}\phi\rho_{k}\omega^{n}+\int_{X}G(x,\cdot)\Big(-\phi\Delta\rho_{k}-2\langle\nabla\rho_{k},\nabla\phi\rangle_{\omega}-\rho_{k}\Delta\phi\Big)\omega^{n}.

We estimate the terms on the RHS of (
4.5
) separately.

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{X}G(x,\cdot)2\langle\nabla\rho_{k},\nabla\phi\rangle_{\omega}\omega^{n}\Big|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{X\setminus\{\rho_{k}=1\}}G(x,\cdot)2\langle\nabla\rho_{k},\nabla\phi\rangle_{\omega}\omega^{n}\Big|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{x}\Big(\int_{X}\frac{|\nabla u_{\infty}|^{2}_{\omega}}{(u_{\infty}+1)^{2}}\omega^{n}\Big)^{1/2}\left(\int_{X}|\nabla\rho_{k}|^{2}_{\omega}\omega^{n}\right)^{1/2}\to 0,

as
k\to\infty
, where we have used (
4.4
) and the upper bound of
G(x,\cdot)
on
U_{k}
.

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{X}G(x,\cdot)\phi\Delta_{\omega}\rho_{k}\omega^{n}\Big|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{X}|\nabla G(x,\cdot)||\nabla\rho_{k}|\phi\omega^{n}+\int_{X}G(x,\cdot)|\langle\nabla\rho_{k},\nabla\phi\rangle_{\omega}|\omega^{n}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Big(\int_{X\setminus\{\rho_{k}=1\}}\phi^{2}|\nabla G(x,\cdot)|^{2}_{\omega}\omega^{n}\Big)^{1/2}\left(\int_{X}|\nabla\rho_{k}|^{2}_{\omega}\omega^{n}\right)^{1/2}+C_{x}\left(\int_{X}|\nabla\rho_{k}|^{2}_{\omega}\omega^{n}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\left(\int_{X\setminus\{\rho_{k}=1\}}\left(\phi^{4}+|\nabla G(x,\cdot)|^{4}_{\omega}\right)\omega^{n}\right)^{1/4}+C_{x}\right)\left(\int_{X}|\nabla\rho_{k}|^{2}_{\omega}\omega^{n}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\to

\displaystyle 0

as
k\to\infty
, where we have used Proposition
2.2
and the elementary fact that

\phi^{4}=(\log(1+u_{\infty}))^{4}\leq Cu^{2}_{\infty}

and then apply (
4.4
). These together with (
4.5
) yields that
\phi(x)\leq C
for some constant
C
independent of
x
. This gives the upper bound of
u_{\infty}=e^{-A\varphi}\tr_{\omega}(\theta_{X})
as well as
\tr_{\omega}(\theta_{X})
. This completes the proof of the proposition.
∎

5.
Approximations by twisted cscK metrics

Suppose
Y
is an
n
-dimensional Kähler manifold. Let
\alpha
be a smooth Kähler metric and
\beta
be a smooth non-negative closed
(1,1)
-form. We define the twisted
J
-function by

(5.1)

\delta J_{\alpha,\beta}(\varphi)=\int_{Y}\delta\varphi(\beta-c\alpha_{\varphi})\wedge\alpha_{\varphi}^{n-1},~\alpha_{\varphi}=\alpha+i\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi,

where
\varphi\in PSH(Y,\alpha)\cap L^{\infty}(Y)
and

c=c(\alpha,\beta)=\frac{\beta\cdot\alpha^{n-1}}{\alpha^{n}}.

Given a smooth volume form
\Omega
, the twisted Mabuchi energy is defined by

(5.2)

M_{\alpha,\beta}(\varphi)=\int_{Y}\log\left(\frac{\alpha_{\varphi}^{n}}{\Omega}\right)\alpha_{\varphi}^{n}+J_{\alpha,\beta-\mathrm{Ric}(\Omega)}(\varphi),

The Euler-Lagrange equation will be a constant twisted scalar curvature equation

(5.3)

R(\alpha_{\varphi})-\tr_{\alpha_{\varphi}}(\beta)=constant.

There exists a constant twisted scalar curvature metric satisfying (
5.3
) on
Y
if and only if the Mabuchi energy is proper by the works of Chen-Cheng
[
7
]
.

Let
X
be an
n
-dimensional normal variety with log terminal singularities. Suppose
\eta
is a smooth nonnegative closed
(1,1)
-form. Now we will consider a twisted Kähler-Einstein metric
\omega
on
X
satisfying

(5.4)

\mathrm{Ric}(\omega)=-\omega+\eta,

where
\eta
is a smooth closed positive
(1,1)
-form on
X
. Equation (
5.4
) implies that
[\omega]=[K_{X}]+[\eta]
is a Kähler class. Let
\omega_{X}\in K_{X}+[\eta]
be a smooth Kähler metric on
X
. We can choose a smooth adapted volume measure
\Omega
on
X
such that

\mathrm{Ric}(\Omega)=-i\partial\bar{\partial}\log\Omega=-\omega_{X}+\eta.

Then the curvature equation (
5.4
) is equivalent to the following complex Monge-Ampère equation

(5.5)

(\omega_{X}+i\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi)^{n}=e^{\varphi}\Omega.

Since
\varphi\in PSH(X,\omega_{X})
there exists
p>1
such that

\frac{\omega^{n}}{\Omega}\in L^{p}(X,\Omega).

Therefore
\varphi\in L^{\infty}(X)\cap C^{\infty}(X^{\circ})
. In particular,
\omega
is a singular twisted cscK metric satisfying

\mathrm{R}(\omega)-\tr_{\omega}(\eta)=n.

Since the sign of
\omega
is negative, the Mabuchi energy
M_{\omega_{X},\eta}
is proper without assuming the triviality of the automorphism group (c.f.
[
42
]
).

Let
\pi:Y\rightarrow X
be a resolution of singularities with
Y
a smooth Kähler manifold. Let
\theta_{Y}
be a smooth Kähler metric on
Y
. Our goal is to find a regularization satisfying the conditions in Definition
1.1
for
\omega
on the nonsingular model
Y
.

Proposition 5.1
.

. Suppose
-K_{Y}
is
\pi
-nef. For any sufficiently small
\epsilon>0
, there exists a smooth twisted cscK metric
\omega_{\epsilon}\in[\pi^{*}\omega+\epsilon\theta_{Y}]
satisfying

(5.6)

\mathrm{R}(\omega_{\epsilon})-\tr_{\omega_{\epsilon}}(\pi^{*}\eta)=c_{\epsilon}.

Furthermore, there exist
\epsilon_{j}\rightarrow 0
such that

\{\omega_{\epsilon_{j}}\}
is a regularization for
\omega
as in Definition
1.1
. In particular,
\omega_{\epsilon_{j}}
converges smoothly to
\omega
on any compact subset of

.

Proof.

One can apply the same argument in the proof of Theorem 3 in
[
42
]
(c.f.
[
3
]
) to construct the twisted cscK metrics
\omega_{\epsilon}
satisfying (
5.6
). The same argument show that there exist
p>n
and
C>0
such that for all
\epsilon
sufficiently small,

\int_{Y}\left|\log\frac{\omega_{\epsilon}^{n}}{\theta_{Y}^{n}}\right|^{p}\omega_{\epsilon}^{n}\leq C

with uniformly
C^{k}
-bounds for
\omega_{\epsilon}
on any fixed compact subsets of
Y^{\circ}
.

It suffices for us to verify (3) in Definition
1.1
, i.e. the Calabi energy is uniformly bounded for all
\omega_{\epsilon}
.
If we let

\omega_{\epsilon}=\omega_{X}+\epsilon\theta_{Y}+i\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi_{\epsilon},~\sup_{X}\varphi_{\epsilon}=0,

then there exists
C>0
such that for all sufficiently small
\epsilon>0
,

\sup_{Y}|\varphi_{\epsilon}|\leq C.

We let

H_{\epsilon}=e^{-A\varphi_{\epsilon}}\tr_{\omega_{\epsilon}}(\theta_{X})

for a fixed sufficiently large
A>0
.
Since
\theta_{X}
is a smooth Kähler metric on
X
via local holomorphic embeddings into Euclidean spaces, its curvature is uniformly bounded above. We can apply the standard Chern-Lu formula and there exists
C>0
such that

\Delta_{\omega_{\epsilon}}H_{\epsilon}\geq~-|\mathrm{Ric}(\omega_{\epsilon})|H_{\epsilon}+A^{2\over 3}H_{\epsilon}^{2}-CA\geq-A^{-\frac{1}{4}}|\mathrm{Ric}(\omega_{\epsilon})|^{2}+H_{\epsilon}^{2}-CA.

Therefore

\int_{Y}\left(\tr_{\omega_{\epsilon}}(\pi^{*}\theta_{X})\right)^{2}\omega_{\epsilon}^{n}\leq A^{-\frac{1}{4}}\int_{Y}|\mathrm{Ric}(\omega_{\epsilon})|^{2}\omega_{\epsilon}^{n}+CA.

On the other hand,

\displaystyle\int_{Y}\mathrm{R}(\omega_{\epsilon})^{2}\omega_{\epsilon}^{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{Y}\left(\tr_{\omega_{\epsilon}}(\pi^{*}\eta)\right)^{2}\omega_{\epsilon}^{n}+2c_{\epsilon}\int_{Y}\mathrm{R}(\omega_{\epsilon})\omega_{\epsilon}^{n}+c_{\epsilon}^{2}\int_{Y}\omega_{\epsilon}^{n}

if

Hence there exists
C>0
such that for all sufficiently small

\int_{Y}\mathrm{R}(\omega_{\epsilon})^{2}\omega_{\epsilon}^{n}\leq C.

This completes the proof of the proposition.

6.
Ricci curvature as a current

In this section, we define the Ricci curvature on a normal Kähler space as a current as introduced in
[
22
]
.
Let
X
be an
n
-dimensional compact normal Kähler variety of log terminal singularities. There exists
m\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
such that
mK_{X}
is a Cartier divisor on
X
. We let
\Omega
be a smooth adapted volume measure on
X
, i.e.,
\Omega=f_{U}|\eta|^{2/m}
on a local open set
U
of
X
, where
\eta
is a generator of
K_{X}^{m}
and
f_{U}
is a nowhere vanishing smooth function on
U
. We let

\mathrm{Ric}(\Omega)=-i\partial\bar{\partial}\log\Omega\in-[K_{X}].

Then
\mathrm{Ric}(\Omega)
is a smooth closed
(1,1)
-form on
X
.

Definition 6.1
.

Let
X
be a compact normal Kähler variety of log terminal singularities. Suppose
\omega\in\mathcal{V}(X,\theta_{X},n,A,p,K)
. The Ricci curvature of
\omega
is said to be bounded below by
H\in\mathbb{R}
if

-i\partial\bar{\partial}\log\frac{\omega^{n}}{\Omega}+\mathrm{Ric}(\Omega)\geq H\omega

as currents, i.e.,

-\log\frac{\omega^{n}}{\Omega}\in PSH(X,\mathrm{Ric}(\Omega)-H\omega).

In particular,

\mathrm{Ric}(\omega)=-i\partial\bar{\partial}\log\omega^{n}=-i\partial\bar{\partial}\log\frac{\omega^{n}}{\Omega}+\mathrm{Ric}(\Omega)

is defined to be the Ricci curvature of
\omega
as a current.

Similarly, we can define singular Kähler metrics with Ricci curvature bounded above by
H\in\mathbb{R}
by requiring

\log\frac{\omega^{n}}{\Omega}\in PSH(X,H\omega-\mathrm{Ric}(\Omega)).

7.
Proof of Theorem
1.3
: a special case

In this section, we will prove a special case of Theorem
1.3
. Let us first recall the volume density for an RCD space. Let
(Z,d,\mu)
be a non-collapsed
{\rm RCD}(K,N)
space. For any
p\in Z
, we define the volume density at
p
by

(7.1)

\nu_{Z}(p)=\lim_{r\rightarrow 0}\frac{\vol B_{Z}(p,r)}{\vol B_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(0,r)}.

The following is the main result of this section.

Theorem 7.1
.

Let
X
be an
n
-dimensional normal Kähler variety with log terminal singularities. Suppose

(1)

there exists a resolution
\pi:Y\rightarrow X
such that
K_{Y}
is
\pi
-nef,

(2)

\omega\in H^{1,1}(X,\mathbb{R})\cap H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Q})
is a singular Kähler metric in
\mathcal{V}(X,\theta_{X},n,A,p,K)
,

(3)

\mathrm{Ric}(\omega)+\omega
is a non-negative smooth closed
(1,1)
-form on
X
.

Then

(1)

the metric measure space
(\hat{X},d_{\omega},\omega^{n})
(as in (
1.4
)) is a compact RCD space homeomorphic to
X
,

(2)

\mathcal{S}(\hat{X})=\mathcal{S}(X)
has Hausdorff dimension no great than
2n-4
.

(3)

There exists
c>0
such that

(7.2)

\omega\geq c\theta_{X}.

In particular, the identity map
\iota:(\hat{X},d_{\omega})\rightarrow(X,\theta_{X})
is Lipschitz.

Proof.

Let
\eta=\mathrm{Ric}(\omega)+\omega
. Then by assumption
\eta\geq 0
is a smooth closed
(1,1)
-form. By Proposition
4.1
,
\omega
dominates
\theta_{X}
, i.e., there exists
C>0
such that

-\omega\leq\mathrm{Ric}(\omega)\leq C\omega.

In particular, the twisted Kähler-Einstein equation
\omega
satisfies the assumptions of Proposition
5.1
. Thefore there exist a regularization
(Y,\{\omega_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty})
for
(X,\omega_{i})
satisfying (
3.1
), (
3.2
) and (
3.3
).
Corollary
3.2
implies that
\overline{(X^{\circ},\omega)}
is a compact non-collapsed
{\rm RCD}(-1,2n)
space with bounded Ricci curvature.

It remains to prove that
\hat{X}
is homeomorphic to the original variety
X
with the dimension estimate for the singular set. This can be proved by the same argument in
[
42
]
for Kähler-Einstein RCD spaces based on the partial
C^{0}
-estimates developed in
[
43
,
19
,
9
,
30
]
, because the Ricci curvature is uniformly bounded on
X^{\circ}
.
∎

8.
Proof of Theorem
1.3
: the general case

In this section, we will prove Theorem
1.3
in full generality following the approximation approach in
[
22
]
. Suppose
\omega
is a singular Kähler metric satisfying the assumptions of Theorem
1.3
.
We let

\eta=\mathrm{Ric}(\omega)+\omega\geq 0,

which is not necessarily smooth as considered in Theorem
7.1
.
If
\eta
is not strictly positive, we can let
\omega^{\prime}=2\omega
and
\eta^{\prime}=\omega+\eta
, then

\mathrm{Ric}(\omega^{\prime})=-\omega^{\prime}+\eta^{\prime}
and
\eta^{\prime}
is indeed a Kähler current by Proposition
4.1
. Therefore we can always assume
\eta
is Kähler current that dominates the smooth Kähler metric
\theta_{X}
.

We will pick a smooth Kähler metric
\eta_{0}\in[\eta]
and let

\eta=\eta_{0}+i\partial\bar{\partial}\psi,~\psi\in PSH(X,(1+\varepsilon)\eta_{0})\cap C^{\infty}(X^{\circ}).

for some
\varepsilon>0
.
The following regularization lemma is a special case proved in
[
22
]
.

Lemma 8.1
.

There exists a sequence of
\psi_{j}\in C^{\infty}(X)\cap PSH(X,\eta_{0})
such that
\psi_{j}\geq\psi
and
\psi_{j}
converges pointwise to
\psi
. Furthermore,
\psi_{j}
converges to
\psi
smoothly on any compact subset of
X^{\circ}
.

Proof.

Since
[\eta]\in H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Q})
is ample, we can choose
\eta_{0}
to be the restriction of a smooth Kähler metric
\tilde{\eta}_{0}
on
\mathbb{CP}^{N}
for some holomorphic embedding
\iota:X\rightarrow\mathbb{CP}^{N}
. Then for any
\eta_{0}
-PSH function on
X
, it can be extended to a
\tilde{\eta}_{0}
-PSH function on
\mathbb{CP}^{N}
by
[
13
]
. Immediately, there exists
\psi_{i}\in PSH(X,\eta_{0})\cap C^{\infty}(X)
such that
\psi_{i}
converge to
\psi
decreasingly. Since
\psi
is smooth on
X^{\circ}
,
\psi_{i}
converges to
\psi
in
L^{\infty}(K)
for any
K\subset\subset X^{\circ}
.

Let
L\rightarrow X
be the ample
\mathbb{Q}
-line bundle with
\eta\in c_{1}(L)
. Let
\mathcal{J}
be the ideal sheaf associated to the singular set
\mathcal{S}(X)
. Then
mL\otimes\mathcal{J}
is globally generated for sufficiently large
m\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
. Let
h_{0}
be the smooth hermitian metric for
L
with
\mathrm{Ric}(h_{0})=\eta_{0}
and
\sigma_{0},...,\sigma_{N_{m}}
be a basis for
H^{0}(X,mL\otimes\mathcal{J})
. We let
\phi=\frac{1}{m}\log\left(\sum_{k=0}^{N_{m}}|\sigma_{j}|^{2}_{h_{0}}\right)
. Then
\phi\in PSH(X,\eta_{0})\cap C^{\infty}(X^{\circ})
with
\phi
tending to
-\infty
near
\mathcal{S}(X)
.

Let
\tilde{\psi}_{i,\epsilon,\delta}=\mathcal{M}_{\epsilon}(\psi_{i},\psi+\delta+\delta^{2}\phi)
be the regularized maximum of
\psi_{i}
and
\psi+\delta+\delta^{2}\phi
for
\delta,\epsilon>0
. By definition, we have
\tilde{\psi}_{i,\epsilon,\delta}\in PSH(X,\eta_{0})
with
\tilde{\psi}_{i,\epsilon,\delta}\geq\psi_{i}\geq\psi
. Furthermore,
\tilde{\psi}_{i,\epsilon,\delta}\in C^{\infty}(X)
since
\tilde{\psi}_{i,\epsilon,\delta}=\psi_{i}
near
\mathcal{S}(X)
for sufficiently small
\epsilon>0
.

For any
K\subset\subset X^{\circ}
,
\tilde{\psi}_{i,\epsilon,\delta}=\psi+\delta
on
K
for sufficiently large
i>0
and sufficiently small
\delta>>\epsilon
, since
\psi_{i}
converges to
\psi
uniformly in
L^{\infty}(K)
. At the same time,
\tilde{\psi}_{i,\epsilon,\delta}=\psi_{i}
near
\mathcal{S}(X)
as
\psi+\delta+\delta^{2}\phi
tends to
-\infty
along
\mathcal{S}(X)
. By choosing suitable
\epsilon_{i},\delta_{i}\rightarrow 0
,
\tilde{\psi}_{i,\epsilon_{i},\delta_{i}}
converges to
\psi
smoothly on any fixed compact subset of
X^{\circ}
. This proves the lemma.
∎

We let
\eta_{i}=\eta_{0}+i\partial\bar{\partial}\psi_{i}
. Then
\alpha_{i}
is sequence of smooth Kähler metrics on
X
.
We can consider the twisted Kähler-Einstein equation

(8.1)

\mathrm{Ric}(\omega_{i})=-\omega_{i}+\eta_{i}.

Let
\Omega
be a smooth adaptive volume measure on
X
such that

\mathrm{Ric}(\Omega)=-\omega_{0}+\eta_{0}.

Then the complex Monge-Ampère equation equivalent to (
8.1
) is given by

(8.2)

(\omega_{0}+i\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi_{i})^{n}=e^{\varphi_{i}-\psi_{i}}\Omega.

From the construction,
-\psi_{i}\leq-\psi
and
-\psi_{i}
converges to
\psi
pointwise. Since
\int_{X}e^{\varphi_{i}-\psi_{i}}\Omega=[\omega_{0}]^{n}
,
\varphi_{i}\in PSH(X,\omega_{0})
is uniformly bounded above. Hence

\omega_{i}=\omega_{0}+i\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi_{i}\in\mathcal{V}(X,\theta_{X},n,A,p,K^{\prime})

for some
K^{\prime}>0
for all
i>0
. Therefore
(\hat{X}_{i},d_{\omega_{i}},(\omega_{i})^{n})
will satisfy the assumptions of Theorem
7.1
. In particular,
\hat{X}_{i}=X
.

Lemma 8.2
.

There exists
C>0
such that for all
i>0
,

(8.3)

\|\varphi_{i}\|_{L^{\infty}(X)}\leq C,~\mathrm{Diam}(\overline{(X^{\circ},\omega_{i})})\leq C

(8.4)

\omega_{i}\geq C^{-1}\theta_{X}.

Proof.

Since
\omega_{i}\in\mathcal{V}(X,\theta_{X},n,A,p,K^{\prime})
, (
8.3
) follows immediately from
[
24
,
25
]
due to the uniform Nash entropy bound. To achieve (
8.4
), we will apply the Chern-Lu argument one more time. For each
i>0
, there exists
c_{i}>0
such that

\omega_{i}\geq c_{i}\theta_{X}

by (
7.2
) in Theorem
7.1
.
Let
D
be an effective divisor on
X
whose support contains the singular set of
X
. Let
\sigma_{D}
be the defining section of
D
and
h_{D}
be a smooth hermitian metric for the line bundle associated to
D
. We consider

H_{i,\epsilon}=\log\tr_{\omega_{i}}(\theta_{X})-A\varphi_{i}+\epsilon\log|\sigma_{D}|^{2}_{h_{D}}

for small
\epsilon>0
.

H_{i,\epsilon}
must attain its maximum in
X\setminus D
. Straightforward calculations show that

\Delta_{\omega_{i}}H_{i,\epsilon}\geq\tr_{\omega_{i}}(\theta_{X})-CA

in
X\setminus D
for a fixed sufficiently large
A>0
and a uniform constant
C
that are both independent of
i
and
\epsilon
because
\mathrm{Ric}(\omega_{i})\geq-\omega_{i}
for all
i
. One can apply the maximum principle to
H_{i,\epsilon}
since
H_{i,\epsilon}
is smooth on
X\setminus D
, which leads to the uniform upper bound independent on
i
and
\epsilon
for
H_{i,\epsilon}
. The estimate (
8.4
) is then obtained after letting
\epsilon\rightarrow 0
since
\varphi_{i}
is uniformly bounded.
∎

Corollary 8.1
.

For any compact
K\subset\subset X^{\circ}
, there exists
C>0
such that for all
i
,

C^{-1}\theta_{X}\leq\omega_{i}\leq C\theta_{X}.

Proof.

The corollary follows immediately from (
8.4
) and the complex Monge-Ampère equation (
8.2
) as
\varphi_{i}-\psi_{i}
is uniformly bounded on
K
.
∎

Lemma 8.3
.

For any compact
K\subset\subset X^{\circ}
and
k>0
,

\|\varphi_{i}-\varphi\|_{C^{k}(K)}=0.

Proof.

Given
K\subset\subset X^{\circ}
,
\psi_{i}
converges to
\psi
smootly on
K
. Therefore
\varphi_{i}
is uniformly bounded in
C^{k}(K)
for any
k>0
by Schauder estimates and elliptic regularity for linear equations with the second order estimate in Corollary
8.1
. Since
\psi_{i}
converges to
\psi
smoothly in
K
and point-wisely on
X
, we have

\lim_{i\rightarrow\infty}\left\|e^{-\psi_{i}}-e^{-\psi}\right\|_{L^{1}(X,\Omega)}=0.

The stability theorem for complex Monge-Ampère equation
[
32
,
18
]
implies

\lim_{i\rightarrow\infty}\|\varphi_{i}-\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}(X)}=0.

The lemma immediately follows.
∎

As a consequence,
(X^{\circ},\omega_{i})
converges smoothly to
(X^{\circ},\omega)
.

Let
(\hat{X}_{i},d_{\omega_{i}},\omega_{i}^{n})
be the metric measure space uniquely associated to
(X,\omega_{i},\omega_{i}^{n})
. By Theorem
7.1
,
(\hat{X}_{i},d_{\omega_{i}},\omega_{i}^{n})
is a non-collapsed compact
{\rm RCD}(-1,2n)
space homeomorphic to
X
for all
i
. Furthermore,
\mathcal{R}(\hat{X}_{i})=X^{\circ}
.

Lemma 8.4
.

(\hat{X}_{i},d_{\omega_{i}},\omega_{i}^{n})
converges to
(\hat{X},d_{\omega},\omega^{n})
as
i\rightarrow\infty
. In particular,
(\hat{X},d_{\omega},\omega^{n})
is a non-collapsed compact
{\rm RCD}(-1,2n)
space.

Proof.

We first note that
\omega_{i}
converge smoothly to
\omega
on
X^{\circ}
and the diameter of
(\hat{X}_{i},d_{\omega_{i}},\omega_{i}^{n})
is uniformly bounded. Therefore after possibly passing to a subsequence,
(\hat{X}_{i},d_{\omega_{i}},\omega_{i}^{n})
as non-collapsed RCD spaces converge to a non-collapsed compact
{\rm RCD}(-1,2n)
space
(Y,d_{Y},\mu_{Y})
whose regular part contains
X^{\circ}
with local isometry to
(X^{\circ},\omega,\omega^{n})
. By the convergence of measures,
Y\setminus X^{\circ}
has
0
measure. Furthermore, since
\omega_{i}
has uniformly bounded local potentials and
X\setminus X^{\circ}
is an analytic subvariety, we can conclude that
Y\setminus X^{\circ}
has
0
capacity. In particular,
(Y,d_{Y},\mu_{Y})
is an almost smooth metric measure space introduced by Honda
[
28
]
. Therefore
(Y,d_{Y},\mu_{Y})=\overline{(X^{\circ},\omega,\omega^{n})}
and the lemma is proved.
∎

Lemma 8.5
.

There exists
\epsilon=\epsilon>0
such that for any
p\in\hat{X}_{i}\setminus X^{\circ}
and
i>0
,

\nu_{\hat{X}_{i}}(p)<1-\epsilon.

Proof.

We will prove by contradiction. After rescaling, we can assume there exist
\varepsilon_{i}>0
such that the Ricci curvature of
\omega_{i}^{\prime}=(\varepsilon_{i})^{-2}\omega_{i}
is bounded by
-1
and
1
. Suppose there exist
p_{i}\in\hat{X}_{i}\setminus X^{\circ}
such that

\nu_{\hat{X}_{i}}(p_{i})\rightarrow 1
. Then
\nu_{\hat{X}_{i}}(p_{i})\rightarrow 1
as well. the same argument of dimension iteration in the proof of Theorem 36 in
[
42
]
will show that for sufficiently large
i
, the tangent cone at
p_{i}
in
(X,\omega^{\prime}_{i})
is the flat
\mathbb{R}^{2n}
, which would imply that
p_{i}
is a regular point of
X
. Ccontradiction.

∎

We will now show that
\hat{X}
as the metric completion of
(X^{\circ},\omega)
is homeomorphic to
X
.

Let

\mathcal{R}_{\epsilon}(\hat{X})=\{p\in\hat{X}:\nu_{\hat{X}}(p)>1-\epsilon\}.

Lemma 8.6
.

There exists
\epsilon>0
such that

\mathcal{R}(\hat{X})=\mathcal{R}_{\epsilon}(\hat{X})=X^{\circ},

Proof.

For any
p\in\hat{X}\setminus X^{\circ}
, there exist
p_{i}\in\hat{X}_{i}\setminus X^{\circ}
such that
p_{i}\in(\hat{X}_{i},d_{\omega_{i}})\rightarrow p
. By volume convergence and Lemma

,

\nu_{\hat{X}_{i}}(p)<1-\epsilon

for some uniform

. This would imply that

is not a regular point of
\hat{X}
and so
\mathcal{R}(X)=X^{\circ}
. By Lemma
8.5
again,

. The lemma then follows.
∎

Lemma 8.7
.

Let
(V,o)
be any iterated tangent cone
(\hat{X},p)
. Suppose the
\epsilon
-singular set

has
0
capacity for some
\epsilon>0
. There exist
M,C>0
such that for some

satisfying

(1)

\|\sigma\|_{L^{2}(h^{m},m\omega)}\leq C.

(2)

\left||\sigma(z)|-e^{-md_{\hat{X}}(z,p)}\right|<\epsilon.

Proof.

The lemma is a consequence of Proposition 3.1 of
[
30
]
with slight modifications. If
\hat{X}
is the Gromov-Hausdorff limit of uniformly non-collapsed polarized manifolds with uniform lower Ricci bound, the lemma would immediately follow from Proposition 3.1 of
[

]
. However,
\hat{X}
is the limit of

and one has to take care of the singularities of

Suppose
(V,o)
is the pointed Gromov-Hausdorff limit of
(\hat{X},p_{i},A_{i}d_{\omega})
associated to the line bundle

with

. Let

for a fixed sufficiently small

Obviously,

is closed by continuity of the tangent cones of a non-collapsed RCD space
[

,

]

be the

and

In fact,

A generalized winding number formula for the Witten index of a Toeplitz operator
Masaki Izumi

Abstract
We generalize the winding number formula for the Fredholm index of a Toeplitz operator to the Witten index. We also show trace formulae involving Toeplitz operators and operator monotone functions.



1
Introduction

It is well known that the Toeplitz operator
T_{f}
with a continuous symbol
f\in C(\mathbb{T})
is a Fredholm operator if and only if

f
has no zeros on the unit circle
\mathbb{T}
.
For such
T_{f}
, its Fredholm index
\operatorname{ind}T_{f}
is given by
-1
times the winding number of
f

(see, for example,
[
2
, Theorem 4.4.3]
).
For
f\in C^{1}(\mathbb{T})
, this can be paraphrased as the following formula:

\operatorname{ind}T_{f}=-\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{f^{\prime}(t)}{f(t)}dt.

(1.1)

A bounded operator
T\in B(H)
on a Hilbert space
H
is said to be almost normal if the self-commutator

[T^{*},T]=T^{*}T-TT^{*}
is trace class.
The Witten index
\operatorname{ind}_{W}T
of such
T
is defined by the limit

\operatorname{ind}_{W}T=\lim_{s\to+\infty}\operatorname{Tr}(e^{-sT^{*}T}-e^{-sTT^{*}}),

if it exists.
If moreover
T
is Fredholm, it is known to coincide with the Fredholm index (see
[
6
, Theorem 2.5]
).
One of the purposes of this paper is to generalize (
1.1
) to the Witten index by replacing
the integral on the right-hand side with the principal value integral (see Theorem
3.2
for the precise statement).
It is worth showing such a result in view of the recent interests in the Witten index of Toeplitz operators in the literature
(see
[
14
]
,
[
15
]
,
[
20
]
for example).

In the celebrated work
[
12
]
in 1953, Krein showed that for self-adjoint operators
A
and
B
with a trace class
difference, there exists a unique integrable function
\xi_{A,B}(x)
on
\mathbb{R}
, called the spectral shift function,
such that the Krein formula

\operatorname{Tr}(\varphi(A)-\varphi(B))=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi^{\prime}(x)\xi_{A,B}(x)dx

holds for
\varphi
in a certain class of functions.
It is natural to discuss the Witten index of
T
in relation to the Krein formula for the pair
(T^{*}T,TT^{*})

(see, for example,
[
5
]
,
[
6
]
).
On the other hand, it is known (see
[
13
, p.244]
) that the spectral shift function
\xi_{T^{*}T,TT^{*}}

for an almost normal
T
can be computed from the Pincus principal function
g_{T}
as

\xi_{T^{*}T,TT^{*}}(x)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}g_{T}(\sqrt{x}e^{i\theta})d\theta,\quad\forall x>0.

(1.2)

The Toeplitz operator
T_{f}
with a sufficiently regular symbol
f
is almost normal and its principal function
is determined by

g_{T_{f}}(z)=-\operatorname{ind}(T_{f}-zI)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{f^{\prime}(t)}{f(t)-z}dt,\quad\forall z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus f(\mathbb{T}),

(1.3)

thanks to the Helton-Howe thoery (see
[
13
, p.244]
,
[
9
, Proposition 5.2]
).
This means that in principle, the Witten index of
T_{f}
should be expressed by integral in terms of the symbol
f
.
Yet, we do not pursuit this strategy to accomplish our task (cf. Remark
2.9
).
In fact, we do not need any general results in either the Krein theory or the Helton-Howe theory for our purpose.
Instead, we directly deduce the trace formula

\operatorname{Tr}(\varphi(T_{f}^{*}T_{f})-\varphi(T_{f}T_{f}^{*}))=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\left(\varphi(|f(t)^{2}|)-\varphi(0)\right)\frac{f^{\prime}(t)}{f(t)}dt

(see Theorem
2.7
for the precise statement) from the Helton-Howe formula

\operatorname{Tr}([T_{f},T_{g}])=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{0}^{2\pi}f(t)dg(t),

which follows from an elementary argument and does not require the Helton-Howe theory.
Since the argument in
[
13
, p.244]
to describe the spectral shift function goes through the Helfton-Howe formula,
it is more natural and efficient to deduce necessary trace formulae directly from the Helton-Howe formula than appeal
to the Krein theory.
One advantage of our approach is that we can treat symbols with low regularity (see Theorem
2.11
).

In Section 2, we give a proof of the Helton-Howe formula for the Toeplitz operators under a considerably mild regularity assumption,
and deduce the above mentioned trace formula out of it.
The contents of this section may be folklore among specialists, and our point is to present explicit formulae and also to treat symbols
with considerably low regularity.

As an application of the trace formula, we show in Section 3 the generalized winding number formula for the Witten index and discuss
some examples.
In particular, we show in Example
3.4
that every real value can be realized as the Witten index of a Toeplitz operator.

In Section 4, we extend the trace formulae shown in Section 2 to the operator monotone functions
(Theorem
4.8
,
4.11
).
Despite the fact that the Krein theory does not apply to the operator monotone functions usually, we can still do it for
sufficiently regular symbols thanks to Peller’s famous criterion
[
16
, Chapter 6]

for the Schatten-von Neumann class Hankel operators.

2
Preliminaries

2.1
Notation

We first fix the basic notation used in this paper.
Let
H
,
H_{1}
and
H_{2}
be Hilbert spaces.
We denote by
B(H_{1}.H_{2})
(respectively
K(H_{1},H_{2})
) the set of bounded (respectively compact) operators from

H_{1}
to
H_{2}
.
We write
B(H)=B(H,H)
and
K(H)=K(H,H)
.
For
T\in K(H_{1},H_{2})
, we denote by
\{s_{n}(T)\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
the singular value sequence of
T
,
that is, the eigenvalues of
(T^{*}T)^{1/2}
, counting multiplicity, in decreasing order.
For
p>0
, we set

\|T\|_{p}=\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}s_{n}(T)^{p}\right)^{1/p},

and denote by
S_{p}(H_{1},H_{2})
the Schatten-von Neumann
p
-class

\{T\in K(H_{1},H_{2});\;\|T\|_{p}<\infty\}.

Our basic references for the Schatten-von Neumann classes are
[
8
]
and
[
19
]
.

For a measurable function
f
on a measure space and
0<p\leq\infty
, we denote
\|f\|_{L^{p}}
by
\|f\|_{p}
for simplicity.

We denote by
\mathbb{D}
the unit disk
\{z\in\mathbb{C}\;;|z|<1\}
.
We denote by
dA
the area measure of
\mathbb{C}
.
Let
\mathbb{T}=\mathbb{R}/2\pi\mathbb{Z}
, which we often identify with the unit circle
\partial\mathbb{D}
.
To define function spaces on
\mathbb{T}
, we always use the normalized Lebesgue measure
\frac{dt}{2\pi}
, e.g.,

L^{p}(\mathbb{T})=L^{p}(\mathbb{T},\frac{dt}{2\pi})
.

2.2
The Helton-Howe formula for Toeplitz operators

While the Helton-Howe formula is customarily discussed for
C^{\infty}
symbols, we discuss it for symbols with
least possible regularity.
For this purpose, we first set up relevant function spaces.
The contents of this subsection are mostly expository.

For
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{T})
and
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, we denote by
\hat{g}(n)
the Fourier coefficient

\hat{f}(n)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{T}}f(t)e^{-int}dt.

Let
H^{2}
be the Hardy space

\{f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T});\;\forall n<0,\;\hat{f}(n)=0\}.

We set
H^{\infty}=H^{2}\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})
.
Let
P_{+}
be the projection from
L^{2}(\mathbb{T})
onto
H^{2}
, called the Riesz projections, and let
P_{-}=1-P_{+}
.
For
f\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})
, we denote by
M_{f}\in B(L^{2}(\mathbb{T}))
the multiplication operator
M_{f}h=fh
for
h\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T})
.
The Toeplitz operator
T_{f}\in B(H^{2})
and the Hankel operator
H_{f}\in B(H^{2},{H^{2}}^{\perp})
are defined by

T_{f}=P_{+}M_{f}P_{+}
and
H_{f}=P_{-}M_{f}P_{+}
respectively.

For
s>0
, we denote by
W_{2}^{s}(\mathbb{T})
the Sobolev space

W_{2}^{s}(\mathbb{T})=\{f\in L_{2}(\mathbb{T});\;\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}(1+|n|^{2s})|\hat{f}(n)|^{2}<\infty\},

and define the Sobolev norm by

\|f\|_{W_{2}^{s}}=\sqrt{\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}(1+|n|^{2s})|\hat{f}(n)|^{2}}.

The space
W_{2}^{1/2}(\mathbb{T})
is particularly important for us.

It is straightforward to compute the Hilbert-Schmidt norm of
H_{f}
as

\|H_{f}\|_{2}^{2}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\|H_{f}e_{n}\|_{2}^{2}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n|\hat{f}(-n)|^{2},

where
e_{n}(t)=e^{int}
.
Thus
H_{f}\in S_{2}(H^{2},{H^{2}}^{\perp})
if and only if
P_{-}f\in W_{2}^{1/2}(\mathbb{T})
.
We have

\|H_{f}\|_{2}^{2}+\|H_{\overline{f}}\|_{2}^{2}=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}|n||\hat{f}(n)|^{2}=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}\frac{|f(s)-f(t)|^{2}}{|e^{is}-e^{it}|^{2}}dsdt,

where the second equality can be shown by the Fourier expansion.
In particular,

\|f\|_{W_{2}^{1/2}}^{2}=\|f\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}\frac{|f(s)-f(t)|^{2}}{|e^{is}-e^{it}|^{2}}dsdt.

(2.1)

For
f,g\in W_{2}^{1/2}(\mathbb{T})
, we have

T_{fg}-T_{f}T_{g}=H_{\overline{f}}^{*}H_{g}\in S_{1}(H^{2}),

(2.2)

and

\|[T_{f},T_{g}]\|_{1}\leq\|f\|_{W_{2}^{1/2}}\|g\|_{W_{2}^{1/2}}.

(2.3)

Indeed,

\displaystyle\|[T_{f},T_{g}]\|_{1}

\displaystyle=\|-H_{\overline{f}}^{*}H_{g}+H_{\overline{g}}^{*}H_{f}\|_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\|H_{\overline{f}}\|_{2}\|H_{g}\|_{2}+\|H_{\overline{g}}\|_{2}\|H_{f}\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{(\|H_{f}\|_{2}^{2}+\|H_{\overline{f}}\|_{2}^{2})(\|H_{g}\|_{2}^{2}+\|H_{\overline{g}}\|_{2}^{2})}

\displaystyle\leq\|f\|_{W_{2}^{1/2}}\|g\|_{W_{2}^{1/2}}.

For
f,g\in W_{2}^{1/2}(\mathbb{T})
, we define a skew-symmetric form
\omega
by

\omega(f,g)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}n\hat{f}(-n)\hat{g}(n),

which satisfies
|\omega(f,g)|\leq\|f\|_{W_{2}^{1/2}}\|g\|_{W_{2}^{1/2}}
.
Then the Helton-Howe formula takes the following form:

\operatorname{Tr}([T_{f},T_{g}])=\omega(f,g).

Indeed, the formula can be directly verified for trigonometric polynomials, and extends to
W_{2}^{1/2}(\mathbb{T})
by continuity.

Let
W_{1}^{1}(\mathbb{T})
be the set of absolutely continuous functions on
\mathbb{T}
.
Throughout the paper, we mainly work on the Toeplitz operators with symbols in
W_{2}^{1/2}(\mathbb{T})\cap W_{1}^{1}(\mathbb{T})
and
sometimes in
W_{2}^{1/2}(\mathbb{T})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})
or
W_{2}^{1/2}(\mathbb{T})\cap H^{\infty}
.
For the former, the Helton-Howe formula takes the following form:

Lemma 2.1
.

For
f,g\in W_{2}^{1/2}(\mathbb{T})\cap W_{1}^{1}(\mathbb{T})
, we have

\operatorname{Tr}([T_{f},T_{g}])=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\mathbb{T}}f(t)g^{\prime}(t)dt=\frac{-1}{2\pi i}\int_{\mathbb{T}}f^{\prime}(t)g(t)dt.

(2.4)

Proof.

Let
F_{n}(t)
be the Fejér kernel.
Since
g^{\prime}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{T})
, the sequence
\{F_{n}*g^{\prime}\}_{n}
converges to
g^{\prime}
in
L^{1}(\mathbb{T})
, and

\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\mathbb{T}}f(t)g^{\prime}(t)dt=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\mathbb{T}}f(t)(F_{n}*g^{\prime})(t)dt,

as
f\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})
.
Since
g
is absolutely continuous, we have
\hat{g^{\prime}}(n)=in\hat{g}(n)
, and

\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\mathbb{T}}f(t)g^{\prime}(t)dt=\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{k=-n}^{n}\hat{f}(-k)(1-\frac{|k|}{n})k\hat{g}(k)=\omega(f,g).

∎

Example 2.2
.

Note that we have the inclusion relations

C^{1}(\mathbb{T})\subset W_{2}^{1}(\mathbb{T})\subset W_{2}^{1/2}(\mathbb{T})\cap W_{1}^{1}(\mathbb{T}).

The family of functions
f_{\alpha}(t)=(e^{it}+1)^{\alpha}
,
\alpha>0
, illustrates the hierarchy of these functions spaces well.
We have
f_{\alpha}\in W_{2}^{1/2}(\mathbb{T})\cap W_{1}^{1}(\mathbb{T})
for all
\alpha>0
(see Example
4.3
).
We have
f_{\alpha}\in W_{2}^{1}(\mathbb{T})
if and only if
\alpha>1/2
, and
f\in C^{1}(\mathbb{T})
if and only if
\alpha\geq 1
.

As Eq.(
2.4
) does not make sense for symbols in
W_{2}^{1/2}(\mathbb{T})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})
, we use harmonic extension
to get an integral expression.
For
f\in W_{2}^{1/2}(\mathbb{T})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})
, we denote by
\tilde{f}
the harmonic extension of
f
to
\mathbb{D}
, that is,

\tilde{f}(re^{it})=P_{r}*f(t)
, where
P_{r}
is the Poisson kernel.
For later use, we present a more general trace formula than the Helton-Howe formula, which is of interest in its own right.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
f,g\in W_{2}^{1/2}(\mathbb{T})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})
, and let
h\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})
.
Then we have

\operatorname{Tr}(T_{h}[T_{f},T_{g}])=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\mathbb{D}}\tilde{h}d\tilde{f}\wedge d\tilde{g}.

(2.5)

Proof.

Note that we have

|\operatorname{Tr}(T_{h}[T_{f},T_{g}])|\leq\|T_{h}\|\|[T_{f},T_{g}]\|_{1}\leq\|h\|_{\infty}\|f\|_{W_{2}^{1/2}}\|g\|_{W_{2}^{1/2}}.

For
z\in\mathbb{D}
, we set

F_{+}(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\hat{f}(n)z^{n},\quad F_{-}(z)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\overline{\hat{f}(-n)}z^{n}.

G_{+}(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\hat{g}(n)z^{n},\quad G_{-}(z)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\overline{\hat{g}(-n)}z^{n}.

Then
\tilde{f}(z)=F_{+}(z)+\overline{F_{-}(z)}
,
\tilde{g}(z)=G_{+}(z)+\overline{G_{-}(z)}
, and

d\tilde{f}\wedge d\tilde{g}=dF_{+}\wedge d\overline{G_{-}}+d\overline{F_{-}}\wedge dG_{-}=(F_{+}^{\prime}\overline{G_{-}^{\prime}}-\overline{F_{-}^{\prime}}G_{+}^{\prime})dz\wedge d\overline{z}.

Note that we have

\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{\mathbb{D}}|F_{+}^{\prime}(z)|^{2}dA(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|n||\hat{f}(n)|^{2}<\infty,

and a similar statement holds for
F_{-}
,
G_{+}
, and
G_{-}
.
Thus we get

\displaystyle\left|\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\mathbb{D}}\tilde{h}d\tilde{f}\wedge d\tilde{g}\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{\mathbb{D}}\tilde{h}(z)(\overline{F_{-}^{\prime}(z)}G_{+}^{\prime}(z)-F_{+}^{\prime}(z)\overline{G_{-}^{\prime}(z)})dA(z)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\|h\|_{\infty}\|f\|_{W_{2}^{1/2}}\|g\|_{W_{2}^{1/2}}.

The above two estimates show that it suffices to prove the statement for trigonometric polynomials
f
and
g
,
and furthermore it suffices to show

\operatorname{Tr}(T_{h}[T_{e_{-m}},T_{e_{n}}])=\frac{mn}{\pi}\int_{\mathbb{D}}\tilde{h}(z)z^{n-1}\overline{z}^{m-1}dA(z),

for
m,n>0
.
The left-hand side is

\sum_{j=\max\{0,m-n\}}^{n-1}\langle{he_{j+n-m},e_{j}}\rangle=\min\{m,n\}\hat{h}(m-n).

The right-hand side is

\displaystyle\frac{mn}{\pi}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\tilde{h}(re^{i\theta})r^{m+n-1}e^{i(n-m)\theta}d\theta dr

\displaystyle=2mn\hat{h}(m-n)\int_{0}^{1}r^{|n-m|+m+n-1}dr

\displaystyle=\frac{mn}{\max\{m,n\}}\hat{h}(m-n),

which finishes the proof.
∎

Remark 2.4
.

When
f
and
g
have sufficient regularity (and
h=1
), the two forms of the Helton-Howe formula above
are, of course, directly connected by the Stokes theorem as suggested in
[
9
]
.

The space
W_{2}^{1/2}(\mathbb{T})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})
is known as the Krein algebra (see
[
11
, Chapter I, Section 8.11]
).
As our argument heavily relies on the fact that it is an algebra, we include an elementary proof of it here.

Lemma 2.5
.

For
f,g\in W_{2}^{1/2}(\mathbb{T})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})
, we have

\|fg\|_{W_{2}^{1/2}}^{2}\leq 2\|f\|_{W_{2}^{1/2}}^{2}\|g\|_{\infty}^{2}+2\|f\|_{\infty}^{2}\|g\|_{W_{2}^{1/2}}^{2}.

In particular, the spaces
W_{2}^{1/2}(\mathbb{T})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})
and
W_{2}^{1/2}(\mathbb{T})\cap W_{1}^{1}(\mathbb{T})
are algebras.

Proof.

Let
f,g\in W_{2}^{1/2}(\mathbb{T})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})
.
Since

|f(s)g(s)-f(t)g(t)|\leq|f(s)-f(t)|\|g\|_{\infty}+\|f\|_{\infty}|g(s)-g(t)|,

we have

\displaystyle\|fg\|_{W_{2}^{1/2}}^{2}=\|fg\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}\frac{|f(s)g(s)-f(t)g(t)|^{2}}{|e^{is}-e^{it}|^{2}}dsdt

\displaystyle\leq\|f\|_{2}^{2}\|g\|_{\infty}^{2}+\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}\frac{2|f(s)-f(t)|^{2}\|g\|_{\infty}^{2}+2\|f\|_{\infty}^{2}|g(s)-g(t)|^{2}}{|e^{is}-e^{it}|^{2}}dsdt

\displaystyle=\|f\|_{2}^{2}\|g\|_{\infty}^{2}+2\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}|n||\hat{f}(n)|^{2}\|g\|_{\infty}^{2}+2\|f\|_{\infty}^{2}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}|n||\hat{g}(n)|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\|f\|_{W_{2}^{1/2}}^{2}\|g\|_{\infty}^{2}+2\|f\|_{\infty}^{2}\|g\|_{W_{2}^{1/2}}^{2}.

Since
W_{1}^{1}(\mathbb{T})
and
W_{2}^{1/2}(\mathbb{T})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})
are algebras, so is
W_{2}^{1/2}(\mathbb{T})\cap W_{1}^{1}(\mathbb{T})
.
∎

2.3
Trace formulae for
\operatorname{Tr}(\varphi(T_{f}^{*}T_{f})-\varphi(T_{f}T_{f}^{*}))

Although we do not use any general results in the Krein theory of spectral shift functions in this paper,
it is convenient for us to use its setup.
For the Krein theory, the reader is referred to
[
18
, Chapter 9]
.

We denote by
\mathcal{W}_{1}(\mathbb{R})
the set of functions
f\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R})
such that
f^{\prime}
is the Fourier transform of a (finite) complex Borel measure on
\mathbb{R}
.
Let
A
and
B
be self-adjoint operators acting on a Hilbert space
H
.
For simplicity, we assume that
A
and
B
are bounded.
If
A-B\in S_{1}(H)
, the Krein theory shows that there exists a unique function
\xi_{A,B}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
,
called the spectral shift function, such that

\varphi(A)-\varphi(B)\in S_{1}(H),

(2.6)

for all
\varphi\in\mathcal{W}_{1}(\mathbb{R})
, and the following Krein formula holds:

\operatorname{Tr}(\varphi(A)-\varphi(B))=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi^{\prime}(x)\xi_{A,B}(x)dx.

(2.7)

For a finite closed interval
[a,b]
, we denote by
\mathcal{W}_{1}[a,b]
the set of
f\in C^{1}[a,b]
that extends to a function in

\mathcal{W}_{1}(\mathbb{R})
.
Note that if
f\in C^{1}[a,b]
and
f^{\prime}
extends to a periodic function with an absolutely convergent Fourier series,
then
f\in\mathcal{W}_{1}[a,b]
.
In particular, if
f^{\prime}
satisfies either of the following conditions,

(1)

f^{\prime}
is absolutely continuous and
f^{\prime\prime}\in L^{2}[a,b]
, i.e.
f\in W_{2}^{2}[a,b]
,

(2)

f^{\prime}
is
\alpha
-Hölder continuous with some exponent
\alpha>1/2
,

(3)

f^{\prime}
is of bounded variation and
\alpha
-Hölder continuous with some exponent
\alpha>0
,

then
f\in\mathcal{W}_{1}[a,b]
(see
[
11
, Chapter I, Section 6]
).
For example, the function
x^{q}
belongs to
\mathcal{W}_{1}[0,a]
if and only if
q\geq 1
.

It is known that Eq.(
2.6
) and (
2.7
) hold for a broader class of functions than
\mathcal{W}_{1}(\mathbb{R})

(see
[
4
, Section 4.2]
), but the function space
\mathcal{W}_{1}(\mathbb{R})
is enough for our purpose.

Let
f\in W_{2}^{1/2}(\mathbb{T})\cap W_{1}^{1}(\mathbb{T})
.
Then since
T_{f}
is almost normal, the Krein theory is applicable to the pair
(T_{f}^{*}T_{f},T_{f}T_{f}^{*})
.
Our task now is to give an integral expression in terms of
f
and
\varphi
of the trace

\operatorname{Tr}(\varphi(T_{f}^{*}T_{f})-\varphi(T_{f}T_{f}^{*}))
without using the Krein theory.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
f\in W_{2}^{1/2}(\mathbb{T})\cap W_{1}^{1}(\mathbb{T})
, and let
\varphi
be a holomorphic function
defined on
\{z\in\mathbb{C};|z|<r\}
with
\|f\|_{\infty}^{2}<r
.
Then we have
\varphi(T_{f}^{*}T_{f})-\varphi(T_{f}T_{f}^{*})\in S(H^{2})_{1}
, and

\operatorname{Tr}(\varphi(T_{f}^{*}T_{f})-\varphi(T_{f}T_{f}^{*}))=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\Phi(|f(t)^{2}|)\overline{f(t)}f^{\prime}(t)dt,

where

\Phi(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{\varphi(x)-\varphi(0)}{x},&\quad x\neq 0\\
\varphi^{\prime}(0),&\quad x=0\end{array}\right.

Proof.

With
a_{n}=\varphi^{(n)}(0)/n!
, we have

\varphi(T_{f}^{*}T_{f})-\varphi(T_{f}T_{f}^{*})=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}\left((T_{f}^{*}T_{f})^{n}-(T_{f}T_{f}^{*})^{n}\right),

where the convergence is in the operator norm.
We first claim that it actually converges in the trace norm too.
Indeed, since

(T_{f}^{*}T_{f})^{n}-(T_{f}T_{f}^{*})^{n}=[(T_{f}^{*}T_{f})^{n-1}T_{f}^{*},T_{f}],

we get

\|(T_{f}^{*}T_{f})^{n}-(T_{f}T_{f})^{n}\|_{1}\leq n\|[T_{f}^{*},T_{f}]\|_{1}\|T_{f}\|^{2n-2}\leq n\|f\|_{W_{2}^{1/2}}^{2}\|f\|_{\infty}^{2n-2},

which shows the claim as the radius of convergence of the Taylor series for
\varphi
is strictly larger than
\|f\|_{\infty}^{2}
.
Thus
\varphi(T_{f}^{*}T_{f})-\varphi(T_{f}T_{f}^{*})\in S_{1}(H^{2})
.

Since
(T_{f}^{*}T_{f})^{n-1}T_{f}^{*}-T_{|f|^{n-1}\overline{f}}\in S_{1}(H^{2})
, the Helton-Howe formula implies

\operatorname{Tr}([(T_{f}^{*}T_{f})^{n-1}T_{f}^{*},T_{f}])=\operatorname{Tr}([T_{|f|^{2(n-1)}\overline{f}},T_{f}])=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\mathbb{T}}|f(t)|^{2(n-1)}\overline{f(t)}f^{\prime}(t)dt,

and so

\operatorname{Tr}(\varphi(T_{f}^{*}T_{f})-\varphi(T_{f}T_{f}^{*}))=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}\int_{\mathbb{T}}|f(t)|^{2(n-1)}\overline{f(t)}f^{\prime}(t)dt.

Since

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}|f(t)|^{2n-2}=\Phi(|f(t)|^{2})

converges uniformly on
\mathbb{T}
, we get the statement.
∎

In what follows, for simplicity we often write

\Phi(|f(t)|^{2})\overline{f(t)}f^{\prime}(t)=\left(\varphi(|f(t)|^{2})-\varphi(0)\right)\frac{f^{\prime}(t)}{f(t)},

with convention that the right-hand side is 0 whenever
f(t)=0
.

Theorem 2.7
.

Let
f\in W_{2}^{1/2}(\mathbb{T})\cap W_{1}^{1}(\mathbb{T})
.
Then the following holds for every
\varphi\in\mathcal{W}_{1}[0,\|f\|_{\infty}^{2}]
:

\operatorname{Tr}(\varphi(T_{f}^{*}T_{f})-\varphi(T_{f}T_{f}^{*}))=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\left(\varphi(|f(t)^{2}|)-\varphi(0)\right)\frac{f^{\prime}(t)}{f(t)}dt.

(2.8)

Proof.

From the definition of
\mathcal{W}_{1}[0,\|f\|_{\infty}]
, there exists a complex measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{R}
such that

\varphi(x)=\varphi(0)+\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{e^{ixy}-1}{iy}d\mu(y),

and

\varphi(T_{f}^{*}T_{f})-\varphi(T_{f}T_{f}^{*})=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{1}{iy}(e^{iyT_{f}^{*}T_{f}}-e^{iyT_{f}T_{f}^{*}})d\mu(y).

Note that the right-hand side converges in the trace norm (see
[
18
, Lemma 9.26]
).
Thus

\operatorname{Tr}(\varphi(T_{f}^{*}T_{f})-\varphi(T_{f}T_{f}^{*}))=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{1}{iy}\operatorname{Tr}(e^{iyT_{f}^{*}T_{f}}-e^{iyT_{f}T_{f}^{*}})d\mu(y).

Now the statement follows from the previous lemma and the Fubini theorem.
∎

Corollary 2.8
.

Let
f\in W_{2}^{1/2}(\mathbb{T})\cap W_{1}^{1}(\mathbb{T})
.
Then the following holds for every
\varphi\in\mathcal{W}_{1}[0,\|f\|_{\infty}^{2}]
:

\operatorname{Tr}(\varphi(T_{f}^{*}T_{f})-\varphi(T_{f}T_{f}^{*}))=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\mathbb{D}}\varphi^{\prime}(|\tilde{f}|^{2})d\overline{\tilde{f}}\wedge d\tilde{f}.

(2.9)

Proof.

If
f
is smooth, the statement follows from the previous theorem together with the Stokes theorem.
For general
f
and
0<r<1
, let
f_{r}(t)=\tilde{f}(re^{it})
.
Since
f
is absolutely continuous, we have
(f_{r})^{\prime}=(f^{\prime})_{r}
, and

\lim_{r\to 1-0}\|(f_{r})^{\prime}-f^{\prime}\|_{1}=0,\quad\lim_{r\to 1-0}\|f_{r}-f\|_{\infty}=0.

Thus

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}(\varphi(T_{f}^{*}T_{f})-\varphi(T_{f}T_{f}^{*}))=\lim_{r\to 1-0}\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\Phi(|f_{r}(t)|^{2})\overline{f_{r}(t)}(f_{r})^{\prime}(t)dt

\displaystyle=\lim_{r\to 1-0}\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\mathbb{D}}\varphi^{\prime}(|\tilde{f_{r}}|^{2})d\overline{\tilde{f_{r}}}\wedge d\tilde{f_{r}}=\lim_{r\to 1-0}\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{|z|\leq r}\varphi^{\prime}(|\tilde{f}|^{2})d\overline{\tilde{f}}\wedge d\tilde{f}.

Since
\varphi^{\prime}(|\tilde{f}(z)|^{2})
is bounded and the form
d\overline{\tilde{f}}\wedge d\tilde{f}
is integrable on
\mathbb{D}
,
we get the statement.
∎

Remark 2.9
.

We can describe the spectral shift function
\xi_{f}:=\xi_{T_{f}^{*}T_{f},T_{f}T_{f}^{*}}
for
f\in W_{2}^{1/2}(\mathbb{T})\cap W_{1}^{1}(\mathbb{T})
as

\xi_{f}(x)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\mathbb{T}}1_{(0,|f(t)|^{2}]}(x)\frac{f^{\prime}(t)}{f(t)}dt,

(2.10)

where
1_{X}
is the indicator function of
X\subset\mathbb{R}
.
This can be shown by comparing the Fourier transform of the right-hand side with Lemma

Alternatively, the method explained in Introduction shows

\xi_{f}(x)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\frac{f^{\prime}(t)}{f(t)-\sqrt{x}e^{i\theta}}dtd\theta.

If the Fubini theorem is applicable to this iterated integral, we get the same formula.

Now we relax the regularity of the symbol
f
while imposing analyticity.
We say that a symbol
f\in W_{2}^{1/2}(\mathbb{T})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})
is analytic if

.
For analytic

[T_{f}^{*},T_{f}]=H_{\overline{f}}^{*}H_{\overline{f}}\geq 0

Lemma 2.10
.

Let
f\in W_{2}^{1/2}(\mathbb{T})\cap H^{\infty}
, and let
F(z)=\tilde{f}(z)
be the holomorphic extension of

to
\mathbb{D}
.
Let
\varphi
be a holomorphic function defined on
\{z\in\mathbb{C};|z|<r\}
with

Then we have

\operatorname{Tr}(\varphi(T_{f}^{*}T_{f})-\varphi(T_{f}T_{f}^{*}))=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{\mathbb{D}}\varphi^{\prime}(|F(z)|^{2})|F^{\prime}(z)|^{2}dA(z).

Proof.

We use the notation in the proof of Lemma
2.6
.
Lemma
2.3
implies

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}([T_{|f|^{2n-2}\overline{f}},T_{f}])=\operatorname{Tr}([T_{\overline{f}^{n}f^{n-1}},T_{f}])=\operatorname{Tr}([T_{\overline{f}^{n}},T_{f}]T_{f^{n-1}})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\mathbb{D}}F^{n-1}d\overline{F^{n}}\wedge dF=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{\mathbb{D}}n|F(z)^{2}|^{n-1}|F^{\prime}(z)|^{2}dA(z),

and

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}(\varphi(T_{f}^{*}T_{f})-\varphi(T_{f}T_{f}^{*}))

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\pi}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}\int_{\mathbb{D}}n|F(z)^{2}|^{n-1}|F^{\prime}(z)|^{2}dA(z)

∎

The above lemma together with a similar argument as in the proof of Theorem

Theorem 2.11
.

and

\operatorname{Tr}(\varphi(T_{f}^{*}T_{f})-\varphi(T_{f}T_{f}^{*}))=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{\mathbb{D}}\varphi^{\prime}(|F(z)|^{2})|F^{\prime}(z)|^{2}dA(z).

Remark 2.12
.

Eq.(
2.11
) gives a geometric interpretation of the spectral shift function
\xi_{f}
for analytic
f
.
Note that
|F^{\prime}(z)|^{2}
is the Jacobian of the map
F:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{C}

The Sard theorem shows that the critical values

C(F)=F(\{z\in\mathbb{D};\;F^{\prime}(z)=0\}),

has Lebesgue measure 0.
We define the multiplicity function
m(w)=\#F^{-1}(w)
for
w\in F(\mathbb{D})\setminus C(F)

Then Eq.(

) shows

\int_{0}^{\|f\|_{\infty}^{2}}\varphi^{\prime}(x)\xi_{f}(x)dx=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{F(\mathbb{D})}\varphi^{\prime}(|w|^{2})m(w)dA(w).

is the density function, with respect to
the Lebesgue measure on
[0,\|f\|_{\infty}^{2}]
, of the variable
|w|^{2}
whose distribution is given by the measure

\frac{m(w)dA(w)}{\pi}

on
F(\mathbb{D})
.

3
The Witten index formula

Assume that
f\in C^{1}(\mathbb{T})
has no zeros.
Since
T_{\frac{1}{f}}
is the inverse of
T_{f}
modulo
S_{1}(H^{2})
, the Fredholm index of
T_{f}
is given by

\operatorname{Tr}([T_{f},T_{\frac{1}{f}}])
,
and the Helton-Howe formula shows

\operatorname{ind}T_{f}=\operatorname{Tr}([T_{f},T_{\frac{1}{f}}])=\frac{-1}{2\pi i}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\frac{f^{\prime}(t)}{f(t)}dt.

The purpose of this section is to generalize this formula to the Witten index
as an application of Lemma
2.6
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
a>0
, and let
f
be an absolutely continuous function on
[-a,a]
having the only zero at
x=0
.
We assume:

(1)

f^{\prime}(x)
exists for every
x\in[-a,a]\setminus\{0\}
.

(2)

There exists
g\in C^{1}[-a,a]
satisfying

\frac{f^{\prime}(x)}{f(x)}=\frac{g(x)}{x},\quad\forall x\in[-a,a]\setminus\{0\}.

(3)

There exist
\beta>0
and
h\in C^{1}[-a,a]
such that
|f(x)|^{2}=|x|^{\beta}h(x)
and

h(x)>0
for all
x\in[-a,a]
.

Then for every
\varepsilon\in(0,a]
, the limit

\lim_{s\to+\infty}\int_{-\varepsilon}^{\varepsilon}(1-e^{-s|f(x)|^{2}})\frac{f^{\prime}(x)}{f(x)}dx

exists and it is the order of
O(\varepsilon)
as
\varepsilon
tends to
+0
.

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle\int_{-\varepsilon}^{\varepsilon}(1-e^{-s|f(x)|^{2}})\frac{f^{\prime}(x)}{f(x)}dx

\displaystyle=g(0)\int_{-\varepsilon}^{\varepsilon}(1-e^{-s|x|^{\beta}h(x)})\frac{1}{x}dx+\int_{-\varepsilon}^{\varepsilon}(1-e^{-s|x|^{\beta}h(x)})\frac{g(x)-g(0)}{x}dx.

For the first term, we have

\displaystyle\int_{-\varepsilon}^{\varepsilon}(1-e^{-s|x|^{\beta}h(x)})\frac{1}{x}dx=\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}(e^{-sx^{\beta}g(-x)}-e^{-sx^{\beta}g(x)})\frac{1}{x}dx

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}e^{-x^{\beta}g(rx)}dr\frac{1}{x}dx=s\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}\int_{-1}^{1}x^{\beta}h^{\prime}(rx)e^{-sx^{\beta}h(rx)}drdx

\displaystyle=s^{-1/\beta}\int_{0}^{\varepsilon s^{1/\beta}}\int_{-1}^{1}y^{\beta}h^{\prime}(rs^{-1/\beta}y)e^{-y^{\beta}h(rs^{-1/\beta}y)}drdy.

Let
M=\|h^{\prime}\|_{\infty}
and
m=\min\{h(x);\;x\in[a,-a]\}
.
Then

|\int_{-\varepsilon}^{\varepsilon}(1-e^{-s|x|^{\beta}h(x)})\frac{1}{x}dx|\leq s^{-1/\beta}2M\int_{0}^{\infty}y^{\beta}e^{-my^{\beta}}dy.

Thus

\lim_{s\to+\infty}\int_{-\varepsilon}^{\varepsilon}(1-e^{-s|f(x)|^{2}})\frac{f^{\prime}(x)}{f(x)}dx=\int_{-\varepsilon}^{\varepsilon}\frac{g(x)-g(0)}{x}dx=O(\varepsilon)

as
\varepsilon\to+0
.
∎

Now we show a generalized winding number formula for the Witten index of a Toeplitz operator.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
f\in W_{2}^{1/2}(\mathbb{T})\cap W_{1}^{1}(\mathbb{T})
, and assume that
f
has only finitely many zeros

t_{1},t_{2},\cdots,t_{m}
.
We further assume that there exists
\delta>0
satisfying the following for each
1\leq j\leq m
:

(1)

f^{\prime}(t)
exists for every
t\in[t_{j}-\delta,t_{j}+\delta]\setminus\{t_{j}\}
.

(2)

There exists
g_{j}\in C^{1}[t_{j}-\delta,t_{j}+\delta]
satisfying

\frac{f^{\prime}(t)}{f(t)}=\frac{g_{j}(t)}{t-t_{j}},\quad\forall t\in[t_{j}-\delta,t_{j}+\delta]\setminus\{t_{j}\}.

(3)

There exist
\beta_{j}>0
and
h_{j}\in C^{1}[t_{j}-\delta,t_{j}+\delta]
such that
|f(t)|^{2}=|t-t_{j}|^{\beta_{j}}h_{j}(t)
, and

h_{j}(t)>0
for all
t\in[t_{j}-\delta,t_{j}+\delta]
.

Then the Witten index for
T_{f}
exists and it is given by the following principal value integral:

\operatorname{ind}_{W}T_{f}=\frac{-1}{2\pi i}\>\mathrm{p.v.}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\frac{f^{\prime}(t)}{f(t)}dt.

(3.1)

Proof.

Lemma
2.6
implies

\operatorname{Tr}(e^{-sT_{f}^{*}T_{f}}-e^{-sT_{f}T_{f}^{*}})=\frac{-1}{2\pi i}\int_{\mathbb{T}}(1-e^{-s|f(t)|^{2}})\frac{f^{\prime}(t)}{f(t)}dt.

For
0<\varepsilon\leq\delta
, we set

I_{\varepsilon}=\bigcup_{j=1}^{m}(t_{j}-\varepsilon,t_{j}+\varepsilon).

Then since
f^{\prime}(t)/f(t)
is integrable on
\mathbb{T}\setminus I_{\varepsilon}
, the Lebesgue theorem implies

\lim_{s\to+\infty}\int_{\mathbb{T}\setminus I_{\varepsilon}}(1-e^{-s|f(t)|^{2}})\frac{f^{\prime}(t)}{f(t)}dt=\int_{\mathbb{T}\setminus I_{\varepsilon}}\frac{f^{\prime}(t)}{f(t)}dt.

The above lemma implies that the following double limit exists:

\lim_{\varepsilon\to+0}\lim_{s\to+\infty}\int_{I_{\varepsilon}}(1-e^{-s|f(t)|^{2}})\frac{f^{\prime}(t)}{f(t)}dt=0.

Thus we get

\lim_{s\to+\infty}\int_{\mathbb{T}}(1-e^{-s|f(t)|^{2}})\frac{f^{\prime}(t)}{f(t)}dt=\lim_{\varepsilon\to+0}\int_{\mathbb{T}\setminus I_{\varepsilon}}\frac{f^{\prime}(t)}{f(t)}dt=\mathrm{p.v.}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\frac{f^{\prime}(t)}{f(t)}dt.

∎

Example 3.3
.

Let
Q(z)
be a rational function without poles on the unit circle, and let
f(t)=Q(e^{it})
.
We can express
Q(z)
as

Q(z)=c\frac{\prod_{k=1}^{N}(z-a_{k})^{n_{k}}}{\prod_{j=1}^{M}(z-b_{j})^{m_{j}}}.

Then we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{ind}_{W}T_{f}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\>\mathrm{p.v.}\int_{|z|=1}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{M}\frac{m_{j}}{z-b_{j}}-\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{n_{k}}{z-a_{k}}\right)dz

\displaystyle=\sum_{|b_{j}|<1}m_{j}-\sum_{|a_{k}|<1}n_{k}-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{|a_{k}|=1}n_{k}.

This is the reason why a half-integer appears as
\operatorname{ind}_{W}T_{f}
in
[
15
]
.

Example 3.4
.

Let
f(t)=e^{int}(1+e^{it})^{\alpha}
with
\alpha>0
and
n\in\mathbb{Z}
.
Then

\operatorname{ind}_{W}T_{f}=\frac{-1}{2\pi i}\>\mathrm{p.v.}\int_{|z|=1}\left(\frac{n}{z}+\frac{\alpha}{z+1}\right)dz=-n-\frac{\alpha}{2}.

This shows that the Witten index can take any real numbers.

Problem 3.5
.

Is it possible to realize a value other than the half-integers as
\operatorname{ind}_{W}T_{f}
with
f\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})
?

4
Extension of the trace formulae to operator monotone functions

Let
f\in W_{2}^{1/2}(\mathbb{T})\cap W_{1}^{1}(\mathbb{T})
.
As the function
x^{p/2}
belongs to
\mathcal{W}_{1}[0,\|f\|_{\infty}^{2}]
for
p\geq 2
, Theorem
2.7
implies

|T_{f}|^{p}-|T_{f}^{*}|^{p}\in S_{1}(H^{2}),

(4.1)

\operatorname{Tr}(|T_{f}|^{p}-|T_{f}^{*}|^{p})=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\mathbb{T}}|f(t)|^{p}\frac{f^{\prime}(t)}{f(t)}dt,

(4.2)

for
p\geq 2
.
Although the Krein theory does not apply to the case of
0<p<2
, we still have a chance to get the same result if the symbol
f

has better regularity.
In fact, if the Hankel operators
H_{f}
and
H_{\overline{f}}
belong to
S_{p}(H^{2},{H^{2}}^{\perp})
,
we will see that Eq.(
4.1
) holds, and so does Eq.(
4.2
) as far as the right-hand side makes sense.
Moreover, when the symbol
f
is analytic, we will show a much stronger result due to the hyponormality of
T_{f}
, i.e.

T_{f}^{*}T_{f}-T_{f}T_{f}^{*}\geq 0
.

We first recall Peller’s famous criterion for the Hankel operators to belong to
S_{p}(H^{2},{H^{2}}^{\perp})

(see
[
16
, Chapter 6]
,
[
22
, Theorem 10.21]
for the proof).
Note that
H_{f}=H_{P_{-}f}
, and
\overline{P_{-}f}
is analytic.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
0<p
, and let
f
be an analytic symbol.
Then
H_{\overline{f}}\in S_{p}(H^{2},{H^{2}}^{\perp})
if and only if
f
belongs to the Besov space
B_{p}^{1/p}(\mathbb{T})
.

The reader is referred to
[
16
, Appendix 2]
and
[
17
]
for the basic properties of the Besov spaces.
The space
B_{p}^{1/p}(\mathbb{T})
is closed under the Riesz projection
P_{+}
for every
p>0
.
It is known that we have
B_{2}^{1/2}(\mathbb{T})=W_{2}^{1/2}(\mathbb{T})
and
B_{1}^{1}(\mathbb{T})\subset W_{1}^{1}(\mathbb{T})
(see, for example,

[
7
, Section 1,(P9)]
).
As we have
B_{1}^{1}(\mathbb{T})\subset B_{2}^{1/2}(\mathbb{T})
(corresponding to the inclusion
S_{1}(H^{2},{H^{2}}^{\perp})\subset S_{2}(H^{2},{H^{2}}^{\perp})

through Peller’s theorem), every symbol in
B_{1}^{1}(\mathbb{T})
belongs to our working space
W_{2}^{1/2}(\mathbb{T})\cap W_{1}^{1}(\mathbb{T})
.

We denote by
B_{p}
the set of holomorphic functions on
\mathbb{D}
whose boundary value
functions belong to
B_{p}^{1/p}(\mathbb{T})
, and call it the analytic Besov space.
By slightly abusing notation, we write
f\in B_{p}
if
\tilde{f}\in B_{p}
.
Since Peller’s theorem only requires
B_{p}
, we recall the following useful criterion for
B_{p}
instead of giving
the definition of
B_{p}^{1/p}(\mathbb{T})

(in fact, Zhu’s book
[
22
, Chapter 5.3]
adopts it as the definition of
B_{p}
).

Lemma 4.2
.

For a holomorphic function
F(z)
on
\mathbb{D}
and
p>0
, the following conditions are equivalent:

(1)

F\in B_{p}
.

(2)

There exists
n\in\mathbb{N}
satisfying
pn>1
and

\int_{\mathbb{D}}\left|(1-|z|^{2})^{n}F^{(n)}(z)\right|^{p}\frac{1}{(1-|z|^{2})^{2}}dA(z)<\infty.

(3)

For every
n\in\mathbb{N}
satisfying
pn>1
,

\int_{\mathbb{D}}\left|(1-|z|^{2})^{n}F^{(n)}(z)\right|^{p}\frac{1}{(1-|z|^{2})^{2}}dA(z)<\infty.

In particular, a holomorphic function
F
on
\mathbb{D}
belongs to
B_{1}
if and only if

\int_{\mathbb{D}}|F^{\prime\prime}(z)|dA(z)<\infty,

and it belongs to
B_{2}
if and only if

\int_{\mathbb{D}}|F^{\prime}(z)|^{2}dA(z)<\infty.

Example 4.3
.

Let
\alpha>0
and let
\psi(z)=\frac{z}{\log(1+z)}
.
Note that
\psi
has no zero in
\mathbb{D}
, and extends to a continuous function on
\overline{\mathbb{D}}

with the only zero at
z=-1
.
Thus
\log\psi(z)
is well-defined as a continuous function on
\overline{\mathbb{D}}\setminus\{-1\}
,
where we take the branch satisfying
\log\psi(1)>0
.
As
\lim_{z\to-1}\log\psi(z)=-\infty
, we can choose sufficiently large
C>0
so that

\frac{1}{C-\log\psi(z)}
continuously extends to
\overline{\mathbb{D}}
.
We fix such
C
.
Direct computation using Lemma
4.2
shows:

(1)

(1+z)^{\alpha}
belongs to
B_{p}
for all
p>0
.

(2)

\psi(z)^{\alpha}
belongs to
B_{p}
if and only if
p>\frac{1}{1+\alpha}
.

(3)

\frac{1}{C-\log\psi(z)}
belongs to
B_{p}
if and only if
p\geq 1
.

We fix
p>0
for now and set
q=p/2
to avoid possible confusion.
Since the function
x^{p/2}=x^{q}
in the region
0<p<2
is operator monotone,
we can make use of the sophisticated theory of operator monotone functions on
[0,\infty)
to extend Eq.(
4.1
)
and Eq.(
4.2
).
Recall that a function
\varphi:[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
is said to be operator monotone if
A\geq B\geq 0
implies

\varphi(A)\geq\varphi(B)
for any positive operators
A,B\in B(H)
on a Hilbert space
H
.
For
\lambda>0
, we set
\varphi_{\lambda}(x)=\frac{x}{\lambda+x}
, which
is a typical example of operator monotone functions.
It is well-known (see for example
[
3
, V53]
) that for an operator monotone function
\varphi
on
[0,\infty)
,
there exist a unique constant
a_{\varphi}\geq 0
and a positive Borel measure
\mu_{\varphi}
on
[0,\infty)
satisfying

\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda}{1+\lambda}d\mu_{\varphi}(\lambda)<\infty,

such that

\varphi(x)=\varphi(0)+a_{\varphi}x+\int_{0}^{\infty}\varphi_{\lambda}(x)\lambda d\mu_{\varphi}(\lambda).

(4.3)

For
\varphi(x)=x^{q}
, we have
a_{\varphi}=0
, and
\mu_{\varphi}(d\lambda)=\frac{\sin{q\pi}}{\pi}\lambda^{q-2}d\lambda
.

If
A-B
is a compact operator, we can see that
\varphi(A)-\varphi(B)
is a compact operator too.
Indeed, the integral expression shows

\varphi(A)-\varphi(B)=a_{\varphi}(A-B)+\int_{0}^{\infty}(\varphi_{\lambda}(A)-\varphi_{\lambda}(B))\lambda d\mu_{\varphi}(\lambda),

where the convergence is, a priori, in the strong operator topology.
As the integral is equal to

\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\frac{1}{n}}^{n}(\frac{1}{\lambda+B}-\frac{1}{\lambda+A})\lambda^{2}d\mu_{\varphi}(\lambda)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\frac{1}{n}}^{n}\frac{1}{\lambda+B}(A-B)\frac{1}{\lambda+A}\lambda^{2}d\mu_{\varphi}(\lambda),

the convergence is the norm topology, and we see that
\varphi(A)-\varphi(B)
is a compact operator.

If moreover
A-B\in S_{1}(H)
, the map

(0,\infty)\ni\lambda\mapsto\frac{1}{\lambda+B}-\frac{1}{\lambda+A}=\frac{1}{\lambda+B}(A-B)\frac{1}{\lambda+A}

is continuous in the trace norm.
Note that if moreover
A\geq B
, the quantity

\operatorname{Tr}(\varphi(A)-\varphi(B))\in[0,\infty]

makes sense regardless of whether
\varphi(A)-\varphi(B)
belongs to
S_{1}(H)
or not.
Thus the lower semicontinuity of the trace implies

\operatorname{Tr}(\varphi(A)-\varphi(B))=a_{\varphi}\operatorname{Tr}(A-B)+\int_{0}^{\infty}\operatorname{Tr}\left(\frac{1}{\lambda+B}-\frac{1}{\lambda+A}\right)\lambda^{2}d\mu_{\varphi}(\lambda).

(4.4)

We recall a majorization result involving an operator monotone function due to Ando
[
1
]

and Kosaki
[
10
, Appendix]
.

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
A,B\in B(H)
be positive operators on a Hilbert space
H
, and assume that
A-B
is
a positive compact operator.
Let
\varphi
be an operator monotone function on
[0,\infty)
satisfying
\varphi(0)=0
.
Then for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, the majorization inequality

\sum_{j=1}^{n}s_{k}(\varphi(A)-\varphi(B))\leq\sum_{k=1}^{n}s_{k}(\varphi(A-B)).

holds.
In particular, if
\varphi(A-B)
belongs to
S_{1}(H)
, so does
\varphi(A)-\varphi(B)
, and

\operatorname{Tr}(\varphi(A)-\varphi(B))\leq\operatorname{Tr}(\varphi(A-B)).

As we use only the following special case, we state it separately and give an elementary proof.

Corollary 4.5
.

Let
0<q<1
, and let
A,B\in B(H)
be positive operators.
We further assume
A\geq B
and
A-B\in S_{q}(H)
.
Then
A^{q}-B^{q}\in S_{1}(H)
and

\operatorname{Tr}(A^{q}-B^{q})\leq\operatorname{Tr}((A-B)^{q}).

Proof.

Let
D=A-B
.
Thanks to Eq.(
4.4
), it suffices to show

\frac{\sin(q\pi)}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\operatorname{Tr}\left(\frac{1}{\lambda+B}-\frac{1}{\lambda+B+D}\right)\lambda^{q}d\lambda\leq\operatorname{Tr}(D^{q}).

The resolvent identity shows

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\lambda+B}-\frac{1}{\lambda+B+D}=(\lambda+B)^{-1}D(\lambda+B+D)^{-1}

\displaystyle=(\lambda+B)^{-1}D\left(1+(\lambda+B)^{-1}D\right)^{-1}(\lambda+B)^{-1}

\displaystyle=(\lambda+B)^{-1}D^{1/2}\left(1+D^{1/2}(\lambda+B)^{-1}D^{1/2}\right)^{-1}D^{1/2}(\lambda+B)^{-1},

and

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}\left(\frac{1}{\lambda+B}-\frac{1}{\lambda+B+D}\right)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Tr}\left((\lambda+B)^{-1}D^{1/2}\left(1+D^{1/2}(\lambda+B)^{-1}D^{1/2}\right)^{-1}D^{1/2}(\lambda+B)^{-1}\right)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Tr}\left(\left(1+D^{1/2}(\lambda+B)^{-1}D^{1/2}\right)^{-1}D^{1/2}(\lambda+B)^{-2}D^{1/2}\right)

\displaystyle=-\frac{d}{d\lambda}\operatorname{Tr}\left(\log\left(1+D^{1/2}(\lambda+B)^{-1}D^{1/2}\right)\right),

where we used
[
18
, Lemma 9.16]
.
Thus letting

h(\lambda)=\operatorname{Tr}\left(\log\left(1+D^{1/2}(\lambda+B)^{-1}D^{1/2}\right)\right),

we get

\frac{\sin(q\pi)}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\operatorname{Tr}\left(\frac{1}{\lambda+B}-\frac{1}{\lambda+B+D}\right)\lambda^{q}d\lambda=-\frac{\sin(q\pi)}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}h^{\prime}(\lambda)\lambda^{q}d\lambda.

We claim that integration by part implies

-\frac{\sin(q\pi)}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}h^{\prime}(\lambda)\lambda^{q}d\lambda=\frac{q\sin(q\pi)}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}h(\lambda)\lambda^{q-1}dq.

To verify the claim, it suffices to show the convergence of the right-hand side, and

\lim_{\lambda\to+0}h(\lambda)\lambda^{q}=0,

as we have
h(\lambda)=O(1/\lambda)
for large
\lambda
.
In fact, since
h(\lambda)
is non-negative and monotone decreasing, the latter follows from the former.
Since

0\leq h(\lambda)\leq\operatorname{Tr}(\log(1+\frac{1}{\lambda}D))=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\log(1+\frac{s_{n}(D)}{\lambda}),

and

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\log(1+\frac{s_{n}(D)}{\lambda})\lambda^{q-1}d\lambda=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}s_{n}(D)^{q}\int_{0}^{\infty}\log(1+\frac{1}{t})t^{q-1}dt,

we get

\frac{q\sin(q\pi)}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}h(\lambda)\lambda^{q-1}dq\leq\operatorname{Tr}(D^{q})\frac{q\sin(q\pi)}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\log(1+\frac{1}{t})t^{q-1}dt<\infty,

and the claim is shown.

Integration by part again implies

\frac{q\sin(q\pi)}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\log(1+\frac{1}{t})t^{q-1}dt=\frac{\sin(q\pi)}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{t^{q-1}}{1+t}dt=1,

and the proof is finished.
∎

Remark 4.6
.

The function

Example 4.14
.

Let
S
be the unilateral shift, and let
a,p>0
.
Since
S+a=T_{e^{it}+a}
, we have
|S+a|^{p}-|S^{*}+a|^{p}\in S_{1}(H^{2})
, and

\operatorname{Tr}(|S+a|^{p}-|S^{*}+a|^{p})=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{|z|=1}\frac{|z+a|^{p}}{z+a}dz.

For
a=1
, this can be evaluated as

\operatorname{Tr}(|S+1|^{p}-|S^{*}+1|^{p})=\frac{2^{p}}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}\cos^{p}\theta d\theta=\frac{\Gamma(1+p)}{2\Gamma(1+\frac{p}{2})^{2}}.

For
p=1
and
a<1
, we can compute it by the parametrization
z=-a+r(\theta)e^{i\theta}
with

r(\theta)=a\cos\theta+\sqrt{1-a^{2}\sin^{2}\theta}
, and it turns out to be the elliptic integral

\operatorname{Tr}(|S+a|-|S^{*}+a|)=\frac{2}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}\sqrt{1-a^{2}\sin^{2}\theta}d\theta=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{1}\sqrt{\frac{1-a^{2}x}{x(1-x)}}dx.

For
p=1
and
a>1
,

\operatorname{Tr}(|S+a|-|S^{*}+a|)=\frac{2}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\sin^{-1}\frac{1}{a}}\sqrt{1-a^{2}\sin^{2}\theta}d\theta=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{1}\sqrt{\frac{1-x}{x(a^{2}-x)}}dx.

The above computation with
a=1
can be generalized as follows.
Note that if
F
is a polynomial of degree
n
whose zeros are on the unit circle, the real part
of
e^{it}F^{\prime}(e^{it})/F(e^{it})
is
\frac{n}{2}
.
This implies that for
f(t)=F(e^{it})
, we have

\operatorname{Tr}\left(|T_{f}|^{p}-|T_{f}^{*}|^{p}\right)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}|F(e^{it})|^{p}\frac{n}{2}dt.

In particular,

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}\left(\left|\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}S^{k}\right|-\left|\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}{S^{*}}^{k}\right|\right)=\frac{n-1}{4\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left|\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}e^{ikt}\right|dt=\frac{n-1}{4\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left|\frac{\sin\frac{nt}{2}}{\sin\frac{t}{2}}\right|dt

\displaystyle=\frac{n-1}{4\pi}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}(-1)^{k}\int_{\frac{2\pi k}{n}}^{\frac{2\pi(k+1)}{n}}\frac{\sin\frac{nt}{2}}{\sin\frac{t}{2}}dt=\frac{n-1}{4\pi}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}(-1)^{k}\int_{\frac{2\pi k}{n}}^{\frac{2\pi(k+1)}{n}}\sum_{l=0}^{n-1}e^{i(l-\frac{n-1}{2})t}dt.

For even
n
, this is

\frac{n-1}{\pi}\sum_{j=0}^{\frac{n-2}{2}}\frac{\tan\frac{(j+\frac{1}{2})\pi}{n}}{j+\frac{1}{2}}.

For odd
n
, it is

\frac{n-1}{2n}+\frac{n-1}{\pi}\sum_{j=1}^{\frac{n-1}{2}}\frac{\tan\frac{j\pi}{n}}{j}.

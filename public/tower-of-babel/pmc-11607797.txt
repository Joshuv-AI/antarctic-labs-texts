Comparing four-hook and double-hook localization needles in preoperative pulmonary nodule positioning: efficacy, safety, and patient perception
Authors: Nan Wang, Kai Xie, Haitao Ma, Gaoming Wang, Wei Song
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundPrecise preoperative localization is crucial for improving the success rate and reducing the duration of thoracoscopic surgeries. This study aimed to evaluate the efficacy, safety, and patient perception of the four-hook localization needle compared to the traditional double-hook localization needle in the localization of pulmonary nodules.MethodsWe conducted a retrospective analysis of 207 patients who underwent video-assisted thoracoscopic surgery (VATS). Seventy-six patients used a four-hook localization needle preoperatively, while 121 patients used the traditional double-hook needle. Propensity score matching was employed to balance the baseline characteristics of the two groups and minimize confounding bias. We compared the surgery success rate, surgical field success rate, complication rate, and post-localization respiratory pain score between the two groups.ResultsBoth localization methods achieved a 100% success rate. The four-hook needle had a statistically significant shorter localization time (20.00 min vs. 21.00 min, P = 0.046) and a lower preoperative displacement rate (5.81% vs. 17.36%, P = 0.014) compared to the double-hook needle. Both before and after propensity score matching, the postoperative respiratory pain score was significantly lower in the four-hook group compared to the double-hook group (P < 0.001). After balancing baseline characteristics, univariate (P = 0.036) and multivariate (P = 0.039) logistic regression analyses indicated that the four-hook group had a significantly lower risk of localization complications compared to the double-hook group.ConclusionsThe four-hook localization needle outperforms the traditional double-hook needle due to its shorter localization time, lower post-localization respiratory pain score, and reduced displacement rate, making it a simpler and safer option. Furthermore, the four-hook needle effectively reduces the risk of complications, presenting a promising method for preoperative localization of pulmonary nodules.Keywords: Pulmonary nodule, Four-hook localization needle, Double-hook localization needle, Preoperative localization, Patient perception, Video-assisted thoracoscopic surgery (VATS)

Introduction

Lung cancer (LC) is the leading cause of cancer-related deaths worldwide, posing a severe threat to human life and health [1]. In recent years, with the widespread application of low-dose chest CT scans, the early detection rate of lung cancer has significantly increased, effectively reducing lung cancer mortality rates [2].

Early-stage LC often presents as solitary pulmonary nodules, with lung adenocarcinoma being the most common type, typically appearing as ground-glass opacities [3]. The diagnosis of early-stage lung cancer has been continuously evolving. Previous studies have explored various methods, such as percutaneous lung biopsy and bronchoscopic biopsy, to obtain pathological diagnoses of lung nodules. However, due to the limited proportion of characteristic tumor cells within the nodules, biopsies often result in high false-negative rates and low true-positive rates.

Therefore, surgical resection has become a widely accepted method that provides both precise pathological diagnosis and tumor removal. However, unlike superficial nodules in the thyroid and breast that are easy to palpate, pulmonary nodules are located deep within the lung parenchyma and are not easily accessible. Real-time ultrasound guidance is not feasible due to the interference caused by the air-filled lungs. As a result, a significant number of pulmonary nodules require preoperative localization under CT guidance. Several localization methods have been developed to enhance the visibility of nodules during surgery. The spring coil method is commonly used; however, it lacks a mechanism to prevent displacement, leading to potential failure. Additionally, the use of spring coils for the localization of deeper pulmonary nodules can present significant challenges. The coil may be positioned too deeply to be palpated during the procedure, often necessitating the use of a C-arm for auxiliary positioning. This requirement increases radiation exposure and complicates the surgical process. Furthermore, the design of the operating room demands specific protective measures, rendering it challenging for many primary hospitals to implement this technique [4]. Additionally, it is costly and carries high risks of complications such as pneumothorax, pulmonary hemorrhage, and coil displacement. Methylene blue injection spreads quickly, requiring surgery within 1 to 2 h after injection. Moreover, it is challenging to identify on pigmented lung surfaces, and excessive methylene blue can impair localization. Localization using medical adhesive injections is associated with complications such as pneumothorax, pulmonary hemorrhage, local pain, and coughing [5]. The electromagnetic navigation bronchoscopy (ENB) method is complex, requires experienced physicians, is time-consuming, demands high standards of anesthesia, and is expensive, making it unsuitable for widespread clinical application.

Traditional double-hook needle localization, although confirmed by multiple studies for its feasibility in localizing and subsequently surgically removing pulmonary nodules [6], has a high incidence of pneumothorax due to its rigid metal components [7]. More importantly, patients experience significant pain after localization, which is especially pronounced when changing positions. The four-hook needle is an improved tool for pulmonary nodule localization. Currently, there are few articles comparing these two needle localization methods, especially concerning the pain experienced by patients after localization.

Methods

Inclusion and exclusion criteria

We conducted a retrospective cohort study to analyze patients with pulmonary nodules who underwent elective thoracoscopic surgery at the Department of Thoracic and Cardiovascular Surgery, the Fourth Affiliated Hospital of Soochow University (Dushuhu Hospital, Suzhou) from February 2022 to February 2024. This study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and approved by the Institutional Review Board of the Fourth Affiliated Hospital of Soochow University (IRB number: 231014). Written informed consent was obtained from the patients or their legal guardians.

Inclusion criteria were: (1) age ≥ 18 years; (2) preoperative diagnosis of pulmonary nodule, with surgical indications for thoracoscopic wedge resection or segmentectomy; (3) small nodule density and diameter, making intraoperative visual or tactile localization difficult; (4) no contraindications for lung surgery or anesthesia as evaluated preoperatively; (5) tumor staging in accordance with the guidelines for interventional diagnosis and treatment in thoracic surgery.

Exclusion criteria were: (1) age < 18 years; (2) deeply located pulmonary nodules not suitable for wedge resection or segmentectomy, requiring lobectomy; (3) nodules with larger density and diameter that can be directly localized by visual or tactile methods during surgery; (4) confirmed surgical or anesthesia contraindications in preoperative examination; (5) preoperative distant metastasis or uncontrolled infectious diseases.

The following conditions are considered indicators of successful localization procedures: (1) Upon the completion of the preoperative localization and subsequent CT scanning, the tip of the localization needle remains securely within the lung parenchyma after the release of the double-hook or four-hook puncture needle, without any dislodgment into the thoracic cavity or chest wall; (2) The straight-line distance from the tip of the puncture needle to the target lesion is maintained between 2 and 4 cm at the conclusion of localization; (3) The localization needle successfully confirms the position of the target pulmonary nodule during the procedure, facilitating its complete resection and ensuring the acquisition of reliable pathological results.

A total of 207 patients with 207 pulmonary nodules were included, among whom 76 patients used four-hook localization needles, and 121 patients used traditional double-hook localization needles.

Pulmonary nodule localization by computed tomography

Preoperative localization of pulmonary nodules in all patients was conducted by two designated attending physicians experienced in traditional hookwire techniques using a CT machine (Philips Medical Systems Nederland B.V.). The specific CT parameters were set to a beam current of 300 mAs, excitation voltage of 120 kV, and a slice thickness of 3 mm. Patients were instructed to assume a prone position on the CT scanning table and breathe normally during the scan. The relationship between the pulmonary nodule and the CT slices was observed to determine and mark the puncture site, and a rational puncture trajectory was planned to avoid ribs, blood vessels, and other organs, while predicting the insertion depth in advance. Local anesthesia was then administered at the puncture site using 1% lidocaine hydrochloride. Under aseptic conditions, the localization needle was percutaneously inserted through the chest wall along the planned trajectory to the predetermined depth, entering the lung.

The traditional double-hook localization needle (Gallini Medical Devices, Mantova, Italy) and the novel four-hook localization needle (Nanjing Kangyou Medical Technology Co., Ltd, Nanjing, China) (Fig. 1) had needle gauges of 19G and lengths of 110 mm and 120 mm, respectively. During the puncture procedure, an introducer needle containing the localization needle was used. The introducer needle was slowly inserted into the chest wall and lung parenchyma, guided by CT imaging. The goal was to position the tip of the localization needle as close to the periphery of the lesion as possible, approximately 2 cm away, to minimize the risk of directly puncturing the central part of the lesion and potential tumor dissemination along the needle tract. Once the appropriate position of the puncture needle was confirmed, the distal hooks were released, the fixation structure was loosened, and the introducer needle was carefully withdrawn, leaving the internal localization needle in the lung tissue. A follow-up chest CT scan was performed to verify the relative position of the lesion and the distal end of the localization needle, and to observe for any complications such as pneumothorax or pulmonary hemorrhage (Fig. 2A-D). After localization, the patient was either transferred to the ward for further observation or directly taken to the operating room for pulmonary surgery. During the surgical procedure, the relationship between the localization wire and the pulmonary nodule was reassessed to determine if any significant wire displacement had occurred. The hookwire or four-hook localization needle, along with the puncture needle tract and puncture site in the lung, were completely removed during the surgery. After excision of the target lesion, the integrity of the localization hook was verified to ensure no foreign bodies were left inside the patient (Fig. 3). The excised lesion was then sent for rapid frozen section pathological examination.

Numeric rating scale (NRS) Pain score

The NRS pain score was recorded for patients after pulmonary nodule localization. Once patients entered the pre-anesthesia room of the operating theatre, an anesthesiologist assessed and recorded their respiratory pain score. According to the NRS pain score, pain levels were classified as no pain (0 points), mild pain (1–3 points), moderate pain (4–6 points), and severe pain (7–10 points) [8].

Statistical analysis

Patient characteristics included age, gender, height, weight, localization time, post-localization waiting time, surgery duration, surgical method, preoperative hook displacement, respiratory pain score, postoperative hospital stay, and complications. Nodule characteristics included maximum diameter, distance from the nodule edge to the pleura, depth of the localization hook from the pleura, nodule density, and nodule location. Data were analyzed using SPSS statistical software (SPSS for Windows, version 25.0). Continuous variables were compared using the t-test or rank-sum test, while categorical variables were compared using the chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test. Propensity score matching was employed to balance baseline characteristics between the traditional double-hook and novel four-hook groups to reduce confounding bias. Logistic regression was used to evaluate the risk of postoperative complications, with variables having a P < 0.2 in univariate analysis included in the multivariate analysis. P < 0.05 was considered to indicate statistically signifcant diferences.

Results

Patients’ characteristics for both groups

The baseline characteristics of the included patients are summarized as follows: The traditional double-hook needle group included 121 patients, with 62 males and 59 females, with a median age of 58 years. The four-hook needle group included 86 patients, with 45 males and 41 females, with a median age of 61 years. The distribution of the CT characteristics of the pulmonary nodules in these patients is detailed in Table 1. There were no statistically significant differences between the double-hook needle group and the four-hook needle group in terms of maximum nodule diameter, distance from the nodule edge to the pleura, depth of the localization hook from the pleura, nodule density, and nodule location. Additionally, there were no significant differences between the two groups in post-localization waiting time, surgery duration, and surgical method. Both groups experienced varying degrees of complications post-localization, with pneumothorax being the most common complication in both groups.

Balance based on propensity-scores

The main results of the propensity score matching are shown in Table 1. Compared to the double-hook needle group, the four-hook needle group showed no significant differences in clinical baseline characteristics except for the average localization time (P = 0.046) and preoperative hook displacement (P = 0.014). To minimize the potential impact of different patient characteristics, a 1:1 propensity score matching using the optimal matching method was performed for the baseline characteristics of the two groups. As a result, 86 patients from the traditional double-hook group were matched with patients from the novel four-hook group. After matching, there were no statistically significant differences in any characteristics between the two groups. Detailed characteristics before and after propensity score matching are listed in Table 1.

Safety and patient experience with four-hook localization needle

Both localization needles achieved a 100% success rate. However, during the post-localization waiting period, needle displacement occurred in both groups. The displacement frequency was significantly higher in the double-hook group compared to the four-hook group (17.36% vs. 5.81%, P = 0.014). The localization time for the four-hook group was shorter than that of the double-hook group (20.00 min vs. 21.00 min, P = 0.046) (Table 1).

Regardless of propensity score matching, the respiratory pain score in the four-hook group were significantly lower than those in the double-hook group (P < 0.001) (Tables 2 and 3). Before propensity score matching, the incidence of pneumothorax among complications was higher in the double-hook group compared to the four-hook group (16.53% vs. 8.14%), and the incidence of pulmonary parenchymal hemorrhage was similar between the two groups (4.13% vs. 4.65%) (Table 2). Notably, these differences in complications remained statistically insignificant before and after propensity score matching (Tables 2 and 3). It is worth noting that before propensity score matching, the overall complication rate was higher in the double-hook group than in the four-hook group (20.66% vs. 12.79%, P = 0.141). However, after propensity score matching, the difference became statistically significant (25.58% vs. 12.79%, P = 0.033) (Tables 2 and 3).

Discussion

In our study, among the 86 patients in the four-hook localization needle group, only 5 experienced hook displacement before or during the VATS procedure, while successful localization was achieved in the remaining 81 patients.Previous studies have indicated that the insertion process of needles or hooks may lead to displacement, highlighting the necessity for secure fixation devices to prevent such occurrences [4]. Research also suggests that traditional double-hook localizers are more susceptible to displacement and detachment, particularly when the distance between the hook and the pleura is less than 20 mm [9]. However, the four-hook localization needle device employed in our study is designed with four fishhook-shaped claws at the tip, which can more securely grip the adjacent lung parenchyma, thereby minimizing the risk of displacement. Moreover, the soft and flexible tail of the localization wire reduces friction with adjacent tissues during respiratory movements, maintaining low tension. Consequently, we found it unnecessary to place the localizer at greater depths to prevent detachment. This approach effectively reduces patient anxiety and enhances comfort during the surgical process. Therefore, it could be cautiously indicated that the novel four-hook localization needle may serve as a suitable alternative to the traditional double-hook localization needle, justifying its promotion across various medical centers.

With the increasing prevalence of CT screening, the detection rate of early-stage LC has significantly improved. The resection of indeterminate pulmonary nodules, particularly in the context of early-stage LC, presents a major challenge for thoracic surgeons during VATS. Therefore, accurate preoperative localization is crucial for enhancing the success rate of VATS and reducing overall surgery time [10]. This necessity has led to the development and application of various preoperative localization methods, all aimed at easing VATS procedures and shortening surgery duration. Among the current localization methods, the traditional double-hook method is the most widely used [11, 12]; however, its drawbacks are also evident [13]. The use of rigid localization needles restricts patient positioning and increases the risk of complications such as pneumothorax. Additionally, once pneumothorax occurs, the likelihood of needle displacement further increases. Moreover, repositioning patients onto the operating table after entering the operating room can cause severe pain, which some patients may not tolerate. The four-hook needle method offers better efficacy, significantly reduces complications, and enhances patient comfort. This study is the first to comprehensively compare the traditional double-hook method with the four-hook method in terms of efficacy, safety, and patient perception. Through in-depth analysis, this study aims to fill a critical gap in the literature and provide valuable insights for clinical practice.

The soft braided wire of the four-hook localization needle is connected to the hooks and can be conveniently left outside the skin after positioning without the need for fixation [14]. For patients with thicker chest walls or more subcutaneous fat, the localization wire can be fully immersed within the pleural cavity. The flexible and freely floating distal end of the braided wire avoids tension on the anchoring hooks caused by patient breathing or positional changes, thereby reducing the risk of displacement and minimizing discomfort from fixed traction on lung tissue [14, 15]. After positioning, patients can freely change positions, speak, and breathe without any restrictions. They can walk to the operating room or ward without needing a transport bed. During surgery, the tail end of the braided wire does not interfere with thoracoscopic instruments and can be used to lift the target lesion and surrounding lung tissue by pulling the four-hook line at the appropriate lesion site. This facilitates precise resection of the lesion while preserving more residual lung tissue. Although the reverse hook angle of the double-hook localization needle makes it easier to pull out directly during surgery, its tail end is still made of rigid metal. Therefore, patients need to maintain a fixed position after the localization procedure to minimize displacement caused by limb movements. Patients may experience noticeable pulling pain when changing positions or coughing. Additionally, the availability of CT machines for preoperative localization of lung nodules varies significantly across different medical centers. Most medical centers do not have dedicated CT machines specifically for preoperative localization of lung nodules. It is common practice to borrow CT machines from other departments or units to optimize hospital costs. However, using CT machines from other departments may require scheduling the localization procedure in advance to ensure uninterrupted use by each department. This can affect the preoperative waiting time for patients undergoing localization procedures, and in some cases, unexpected emergency surgeries may take precedence over scheduled procedures, causing delays. In these special circumstances, some patients may wait more than 60 min after localization before entering the operating room. In such cases, the use of double-hook localization needles can be very painful for patients, whereas the four-hook localization needle allows patients to change positions freely with minimal pain, which is a significant advantage. This can significantly reduce patient anxiety and improve patient satisfaction. Patient comfort and satisfaction are key components of healthcare quality. Patient satisfaction not only affects individual outcomes but also influences hospital ratings and the likelihood of patients recommending the facility to others.

Notably, some patients in this study who developed pneumothorax after localization reported significant chest pain compared to others. This pain may be caused by the pulling of the pleura attached to the collapsed lung tissue. Therefore, for patients who complain of chest pain after localization, special attention should be given to the correlation between pneumothorax and chest pain. Clinicians should remain vigilant, as chest pain could indicate post-puncture pneumothorax.

Additionally, patients undergoing preoperative pulmonary nodule localization may experience pleural reactions due to the positioning needle, which are often underappreciated or overlooked [16]. Severe pleural reactions can lead to respiratory and cardiac arrest, posing a life-threatening risk. Therefore, the importance of comprehensive medical emergency preparedness during localization cannot be overstated. In our institution, isolated cases of pleural reactions have also been observed during lung nodule puncture localization. In some instances, patients experienced a drop in blood pressure and unstable heart rates, necessitating urgent bedside intervention or rapid transfer to the emergency department for timely response.

Aside from using localization needles made of different materials, several other reported methods for lung nodule localization include percutaneous or electromagnetic navigation bronchoscopy-guided injection of fluorescent dye, percutaneous placement of coils, percutaneous injection of protein glue, and VR/MR visual-assisted imaging localization [17–19]. Recent studies on the localization of pulmonary nodules have explored a variety of methods beyond those previously mentioned. Some reports with small sample sizes have described the use of a radiofrequency identification marking system to localize emitters implanted near target lesions preoperatively. Additionally, the concept of one-stop localization using spin CT in conjunction with hybrid operating rooms has been investigated. Other innovative approaches include the intravenous injection of OTL-38 for fluorescent labeling of folate receptors in lung adenocarcinoma tissue, as well as the intraoperative tracking of magnetic materials near preoperatively implanted lesions using magnetic trackers. These methods have limitations such as high costs, poor precision and reliability, and extended procedure times, which hinder their adoption as mainstream clinical techniques. At the current stage, the use of the four-hook puncture localization needle represents the most cost-effective method for pulmonary nodule localization, offering an optimal balance of safety, efficiency, affordability, comfort, and ease of use.

Our study has several limitations. Firstly, the small sample size might limit the validity and reliability of our findings. Future studies with larger sample sizes are needed to ensure robust results. In this study, all physicians involved in the localization procedures were highly experienced in localization techniques. Although the use of the four-hook localization needle does not demand additional steps or specialized skills from the physicians, caution is still advised in the application of this novel needle type. Unfamiliarity with the design of the localization needle may lead to unforeseen clinical issues. Secondly, the single-center setting may restrict the generalizability of our results, as different centers may have varying practices. Therefore, multi-center trials with diverse patient populations and surgical techniques are necessary to validate these findings. Additionally, future research should investigate the long-term outcomes of the four-hook method. While our study focused on immediate benefits like reduced pain and shorter hospital stays, it is essential to examine long-term recovery, quality of life, and potential late-onset complications.

Conclusion

This study indicates that compared to traditional double-hook localization needles, the four-hook localization needle enhances surgical stability and efficiency, reduces complications, and significantly alleviates patient pain. The four-hook localization needle is poised to become a superior choice for preoperative localization of lung nodules.

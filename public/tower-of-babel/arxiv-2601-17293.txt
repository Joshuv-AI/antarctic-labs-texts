Partial Resolutions and Noncrossing Combinatorics
Minh-Tâm Quang Trinh, Nathan Williams

Abstract
. For any finite reductive group, we compute the central elements in its Hecke algebra that arise from partial Springer resolutions via the Harish-Chandra transform. Of the two kinds of partial resolution, the larger is the more interesting case. We deduce formulas for associated Hecke traces, generalizing work of Wan–Wang beyond type A A , and Deodhar-like decompositions of braid varieties that map to partial Springer resolutions. From the latter, we construct noncrossing sets that interpolate between rational Catalan and parking objects, generalizing our work with Galashin–Lam. In parallel, we establish new formulas for arbitrary a a -degrees of the HOMFLYPT invariants of positive braid closures, from which we construct noncrossing sets for rational Kirkman numbers.

1.4.
Traces

A

We write

1.6.
Combinatorics

Our third application of
1.1
, by way of
1.3
, is to construct noncrossing sets of interest in the Catalan combinatorics of
(W,S)
.

In the rest of this introduction,
W
is irreducible with Coxeter number
h
and rank
r\vcentcolon=|S|
.

Let
d_{1},\ldots,d_{r}
be the fundamental degrees of the action of
W
on its (irreducible) reflection representation.
For each

, let
e_{i}=d_{i}-1
.
For any positive integer

coprime to

, the
rational Catalan number
of
(W,p)
is

(1.22)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Cat}_{W,p}\vcentcolon=\prod_{i}\frac{p+e_{i}}{d_{i}},

while the
rational parking number
of
(W,p)
is
p^{r}
.
These numbers enumerate disparate families of combinatorial objects, which fall into two kinds.

Nonnesting
families are constructed from root-theoretic data that generalize nonnesting partitions,
resp.
parking functions.
In
[
15
]
, we gave the first construction of a rational
noncrossing
Catalan,
resp.
parking, family for any finite Coxeter group
W
and
p>0

h
, recovering earlier constructions for
p=h+1
.
These noncrossing objects differ from the nonnesting objects in that they depend on a chosen ordering of
S
, or
Coxeter word
: a Coxeter-theoretic, not root-theoretic, datum.

For any word
\vec{s}
in
S
, let
\cal{M}^{(v)}(\vec{s})\subseteq\cal{D}^{(v)}(\vec{s})
be the subset of elements
\vec{\omega}
such that
|\mathbf{e}_{\vec{\omega}}|=r
, the minimum possible value
[
15
, Cor. 4.9]
.
Let
\vec{c}
be a Coxeter word for
(W,S)
, and
\vec{c}^{p}
its
p
-fold concatenation.
The main results of
[
15
]
are the identities

(1.23)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Cat}_{W,p}=|\cal{M}^{(e)}(\vec{c}^{p})|\quad\text{and}\quad p^{r}=\sum_{v\in W}|\cal{M}^{(v)}(\vec{c}^{p})|,

proved by way of
q
-deformed identities involving
\cal{D}^{(v)}(\vec{c}^{p})
and taking
\text{{q}}\to 1
.

In
Section

6
, we prove an identity that interpolates between the two above.
Let
d_{1}^{J},\ldots,d_{|J|}^{J}
be the fundamental degrees of
W_{J}
.
Let

be the exponents of the
W_{J}
-action on the reflection representation of

.
We define the

of

to be

(1.24)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Park}_{W,p}^{J,\pm}=\prod_{i}\frac{p\pm e_{i}^{J}}{d_{i}^{J}}.

Then

\mathrm{Park}_{W,p}^{\emptyset,+}=\mathrm{Park}_{W,p}^{\emptyset,-}=p^{r}

We relate these numbers to
\tau_{G}
via a result from
[

, which describes

for a certain

as the graded character of a

for
(W,p)
, in the sense of
[
2
]
and
[
1
]
.
Ultimately, we obtain:

Corollary 1.6
.

For any Coxeter word
\vec{c}
, integer

\displaystyle\mathrm{Park}_{W,p}^{J,\pm}=\sum_{v\in W^{J,\mp}}|\cal{M}^{(v)}(\vec{c}^{p})|.

\displaystyle(\varphi_{1}\ast\varphi_{2})(y,x)=\sum_{z\in X}\varphi_{1}(y,z)\varphi_{2}(z,x).

Let

Next, for any

(4.3)

\displaystyle\{(x_{0}B,x_{1}B,x_{2}B,x_{3}B,x_{4}B)\in O(w,v^{-1},z,v)\mid x_{0}B=x_{4}B\}

(4.4)

\displaystyle\qquad\xrightarrow{\sim}\{(g_{0}B,g_{1}B,g_{2}B,g_{3}B)\in O(v^{-1},z^{-1},v)\mid g_{0}B\xrightarrow{w}g_{3}B\}

7.2.

As explained in

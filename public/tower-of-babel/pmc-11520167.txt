Effect of hemodiafiltration and hemodialysis on mortality of patients with end-stage kidney disease: a meta-analysis
Authors: Yifan Zhu, Juan Li, Hulin Lu, Zhanqin Shi, Xiaoyi Wang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionPrevious randomized controlled trials (RCTs) and meta-analyses comparing Hemodiafiltration (HDF) with conventional hemodialysis (HD) on the effectiveness of HDF for mortality in end-stage renal disease (ESRD) patients have yielded contrasting results. Importantly, we sought to compile the available information to provide the most up-to-date and reliable evidence.MethodsWe systematically searched PubMed, Embase and Cochrane Library for RCTs up to January 14, 2024. Review Manager 5.3 software was used to analyze relevant data and evaluate the quality of evidence.ResultsOur study involved 10 randomized controlled trials with 4654 chronic dialysis patients. Compared to hemodialysis, hemodiafiltration demonstrated a reduction in all-cause mortality (relative risk [RR] 0.84, 95% confidence intervals [CI] 0.72–0.99, P = 0.04) and cardiovascular mortality (RR 0.74, 95% CI 0.61–0.90, P = 0.002). However, it did not reduce the rate of sudden death (RR 0.92, 95% CI 0.64–1.34, P = 0.68) and infection-related mortality (RR 0.70, 95% CI 0.47–1.03, P = 0.07). A subgroup analysis revealed that HDF demonstrated superiority over high-flux hemodialysis in terms of all-cause mortality, while not over low-flux hemodialysis (RR 0.81, 95% CI 0.69–0.96, P = 0.01; RR 0.93, 95% CI 0.77–1.12, P = 0.44, respectively). Furthermore, a subgroup analysis for convection volume found that hemodiafiltration with a convection volume of 22 L or more reduced all-cause and cardiovascular mortality (RR 0.76, 95% CI 0.65–0.88, P = 0.0002, RR 0.73, 95% CI 0.54–0.94, P = 0.01, respectively).ConclusionIn maintenance hemodialysis patients, hemodiafiltration can reduce mortality compared to conventional hemodialysis. Furthermore, this effect is more pronounced in HDF with high convection volume.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12882-024-03810-9.Keywords: Hemodiafiltration, Hemodialysis, Cardiovascular mortality, All-cause mortality, High-flux, Convection volume

Introduction

End-stage renal disease (ESRD) patients undergoing regular hemodialysis (HD) treatment continue to experience unacceptably high rates of morbidity and mortality. Cardiovascular disease is the leading cause of death in this population, accounting for over half (52.2%) of deaths with a known etiology [1]. Despite these challenges, researchers are continuously striving to improve the prognosis of hemodialysis patients through various approaches, including the exploration of alternative hemodialysis modalities [2–4]. One approach involves using different hemodialysis modalities. Hemodiafiltration (HDF) is a hybrid renal replacement therapy that combines the principles of hemodialysis and hemofiltration. This modality offers the dual benefits of solute removal through both diffusion and convection, resulting in more efficient clearance of medium and small molecular substances compared to either hemodialysis or hemofiltration alone. Notably, HDF excels in the elimination of larger molecules from the bloodstream. This capability addresses a significant limitation of traditional hemodialysis, and as a result, HDF offers a therapeutic approach that more closely approximates the natural filtration function of the human kidney [5]. While observational studies have demonstrated the superiority of HDF in terms of dialysis adequacy [6, 7], the potential impact of HDF on the prognosis of hemodialysis patients remains a subject of debate. A recent meta-analysis found no significant difference in all-cause mortality and cardiovascular mortality between end-stage kidney disease patients receiving HDF versus high-flux hemodialysis (HFHD) [8]. However, the findings of the CONVINCE study, recently published in the New England Journal of Medicine, suggest that end-stage renal disease (ESRD) patients undergoing high-dose HDF may have a lower risk of all-cause mortality compared to those receiving conventional high-flux hemodialysis [9]. Hence, this study sought to investigate and present the most up-to-date and reliable evidence regarding the impact of Hemodiafiltration in ESRD patients, in order to offer evidence-based medical guidance for the selection of dialysis modality in clinical practice.

Methods

Inclusion criteria

(1) Type of study: randomized controlled trial; (2) study subjects: (a) Age: 18 years or older; (b) Dialysis Treatment: Receiving maintenance hemodialysis for at least 3 months, with a frequency of three times per week for a minimum of 4 h each session; (c) Dialysis Modality: Comparison of HDF with HD. 3) The study must report mortality data, including either all-cause mortality or cardiovascular mortality specifically.

Exclusion criteria

(1) Studies with a follow-up time less than 1 year; (2) Studies that did not provide sufficient data for analysis; (3) Studies for which the full text could not be obtained.

Search strategy

A systematic review of the literature was conducted in PubMed, EMBASE, and The Cochrane Library, adhering to the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) guidelines [10]. The literature search was finalized on January 12, 2024, and included studies published in all languages and without publication date restrictions. Detailed search strategies are provided in Appendix 1.

Study selection

The selection of eligible studies was conducted independently by reviewers Yifan Zhu and Juan Li. Reviewers independently screened titles, abstracts, and full texts of pertinent articles against predetermined inclusion and exclusion criteria. Any disagreements between reviewers were resolved through discussion or consultation with experts.

Data extraction

Both investigators independently conducted data extraction and management from each study meeting the inclusion criteria using a predetermined form. The extracted data encompassed fundamental information from the literature, general characteristics of enrolled patients, the modality of renal replacement therapy, primary outcomes, and other relevant details. Consensus was achieved through discussions, effectively resolving any disagreements.

Quality assessment

The quality of each randomized controlled trial (RCT) was independently assessed by two reviewers using the Cochrane risk-of-bias tool.

Statistical analysis

A meta-analysis was conducted using Cochrane Review Manager software (Rev Man 5.3) to assess the impact of hemodiafiltration compared to hemodialysis on mortality in patients with end-stage kidney disease. We focused on four dichotomous outcomes: all-cause mortality, cardiovascular (CV) mortality, infection-related mortality, and the rate of sudden death. The analysis employed relative risk with 95% confidence intervals for each outcome. Statistical heterogeneity, which refers to variation in the results across studies, was evaluated using the I² statistic [22]. According to the Cochrane Handbook, I² values of 0–40% suggest minimal heterogeneity, 30–60% indicate moderate heterogeneity, 50–90% suggest substantial heterogeneity, and 75–100% indicate considerable heterogeneity. Random-effects model was employed to estimate the effect sizes of the included randomized controlled trials.

Results

Study selection and characteristics

In our literature search, we identified a total of 1366 articles from Embase (n = 682), PubMed (n = 382), and the Cochrane Library (n = 302). After removing duplicates and thoroughly examining abstracts and full-texts, we included 10RCTs for analysis (Fig. 1). Table 1 summarizes the characteristics of each study. This meta-analysis involved 10 RCTs with a total of 4654 patients. All studies, except for one conducted in South Korea and one in Turkey, were from European countries. The intervention group received HDF mode dialysis, with 7 experiments using postdilution, 2 with an unknown dilution method, and 1 with predilution. The control group received HD mode dialysis, with 5 experiments using high-flux, 3 using low-flux, and the remaining 2 not specifying the flux type. We assessed outcomes including all-cause mortality (10 trials), cardiovascular mortality (6 trials), sudden death (4 trials), and infection-related mortality (3 trials).Baseline data were evenly distributed across all RCTs, except for Maduell et al.‘s trial [11], where a slight discrepancy was observed. The authors attributed this variance to the randomization process. Additionally, the average age of participants in Morena et al.‘s study [12] was significantly higher than in other studies. Grooteman et al.‘s trial [13] reported a high drop-out rate, but no differences were found between the intervention and control groups in the final analysis in terms of drop-out rate.

Quality assessment

The Cochrane Risk of Bias Tool was used to evaluate the quality of each article included in the analysis. Figures 2 and 3 present graphs and figures summarizing the risk of bias. While the articles generally exhibited a low risk of bias, all were open-label studies, which can introduce bias. Furthermore, a significant proportion of the studies did not report on the blinding status of the outcome assessment. Consequently, the potential influence of bias on this aspect of the research is unclear.

HDF and all-cause mortality

Ten studies reported on all-cause mortality. Among 4613 patients followed for an average of 24 months,981 patients (21%) died from any cause. A comparison of HD and HDF in these randomized controlled trials revealed a significant reduction in mortality with HDF treatment (RR 0.84, 95% CI 0.72–0.99, P = 0.04) (Fig. 4).

Forest plot for all-cause mortality

HDF and cause-specific mortality

Among the 10 analyzed studies, 6 reported cardiovascular mortality, 3 reported infection-related mortality, and 4 reported sudden death. During the follow-up period, 383 out of 4141 patients (9.2%) died from cardiovascular causes, 151 out of 3048 patients (5.0%) died from infections, and 108 out of 2783 patients (3.9%) experienced sudden death. Compared to the control group, HDF resulted in a 26% reduction in cardiovascular mortality (95% CI 0.61–0.90, P = 0.002). There was no significant difference in infection-related mortality (RR = 0.70, 95% CI 0.47–1.03, P = 0.07) and sudden death incidence (RR = 0.92, 95% CI 0.64–1.34, P = 0.68) between the groups (Figs. 5, 6 and 7).

Subgroup analyses of the impact of hemodialysis flux

Subgroup analyses were conducted based on hemodialysis flux, revealing that HDF was associated with a reduction in all-cause mortality and cardiovascular mortality when compared to high-flux hemodialysis (RR 0.81, 95% CI: 0.69–0.96, P = 0.01, RR 0.76, 95% CI: 0.59–0.98, P = 0.04, respectively). However, no significant difference in all-cause mortality was observed between HDF and low-flux hemodialysis patients (RR 0.93, 95% CI: 0.77–1.12, P = 0.44) (Figs. 8 and 9).

Subgroup analyses of the impact of convection volume

Subgroup analyses were conducted on all-cause mortality and cardiovascular mortality based on convection volume. HDF with a convection volume of 22 L or greater was associated with a reduction in both all-cause mortality and cardiovascular mortality (RR 0.76, 95% CI 0.65–0.88, P = 0.0002, RR 0.73, 95% CI 0.57–0.94, P = 0.01, respectively). Conversely, HDF with a convection volume less than 22 L did not result in a significant reduction in all-cause mortality (RR 0.98, 95% CI 0.66–1.47, P = 0.93) or cardiovascular mortality (RR 0.76, 95% CI 0.57–1.03, P = 0.08) (Figs. 10 and 11).

Publication bias

All-cause mortality outcome was selected as an indicator to assess publication bias. A funnel plot was generated showing that most data points clustered towards the upper portion of the plot, with a relatively symmetrical distribution on either side. This suggests that publication bias in the studies included in this meta-analysis is likely minimal (Fig. 12).

Discussion

Our findings suggest a strong association between HDF and decreased all-cause mortality and cardiovascular mortality compared to traditional hemodialysis. However, previous research presents conflicting results. A prior meta-analysis of 3,320 patients found no significant advantage of HDF/hemofiltration over HD in terms of cardiovascular and all-cause mortality [21]. Furthermore, a 2023 meta-analysis comparing HDF with high-flux hemodialysis, which included cohort studies, indicated that HDF did not improve cardiovascular and all-cause mortality, but rather showed an increase in mortality from infections [8]. Conversely, an individual patient data (IPD) analysis [22] of data from four prominent randomized controlled trials conducted between 2012 and 2017, involving a total of 2,793 patients, found HDF to be superior in reducing both all-cause and cardiovascular mortality. Subgroup analysis further suggested this benefit was more prominent among HDF patients receiving higher convection volumes. The recent CONVINCE trial has also reported encouraging results. However, there is some confusion in CONVINCE trial that the risk of death from cardiovascular causes (hazard ratio, 0.81; 95% CI, 0.49 to 1.33) and the composite outcome of fatal or nonfatal cardiovascular outcomes (hazard ratio, 1.07; 95% CI, 0.86 to 1.33) were similar in the hemodiafiltration group and the hemodialysis group. Because, the previous perspective posited that the survival advantages conferred by HDF were primarily attributable to cardiovascular protection. The plausible explanation is that the majority of patients who died in the hospital during the COVID-19(Coronavirus Disease 2019) pandemic were classified as COVID-19-positive, rather than attributed to cardiovascular causes. In addition, patients with complications, such as diabetes and cardiovascular disease, are more likely to be affected by COVID-19. Additionally, a limitation of the CONVINCE trial is that it exclusively enrolled patients who were suitable for treatment with High Flux HDF(convection volume ≥ 23 L/session). The study population exhibits a relatively healthy profile. Notably, these patients are comparatively younger, possess more favorable vascular access, fewer than 25% have coronary artery disease, and have undergone high-flow hemodialysis for a minimum of three months. This undoubtedly raises concerns about the universal applicability of the results. Therefore, the present study serves as a comprehensive analysis of prior randomized controlled trials, offering additional clinical evidence to support the use of HDF.

While hemodiafiltration has been shown to improve outcomes for patients with end-stage renal disease, the exact mechanism remains unclear. Hemodynamic instability, a common complication of hemodialysis linked to worse outcomes [23], might be a contributing factor. The prevailing theory suggests that HDF’s ability to provide more stable hemodynamics may be beneficial. Studies have shown that HDF can reduce the rate of intradialytic hypotension to some extent [24–26]. Additionally, ESRD patients often experience systemic chronic inflammation and oxidative stress, both associated with poor prognosis [27]. Research by Den Hoedt CH et al. suggests that HDF can more effectively alleviate these conditions compared to low-flux hemodialysis [28]. Furthermore, stable hemodynamics from HDF may help maintain vascular elasticity and mitigate cardiac remodeling [29, 30]. These findings potentially explain the reduced cardiovascular mortality with HDF, although further research is needed to understand the observed decrease in infection-related mortality in our study.

Growing evidence suggests that high-volume hemodiafiltration may result in improved outcomes [31, 32]. Consistently, our subgroup analysis revealed that only HDF with a convection volume exceeding 22 L/session was associated with a significant survival advantage, aligning with Peters et al.‘s findings [22]. Additional studies have shown the feasibility of transitioning most patients to high-volume HDF (convection volume > 22 L) [31], providing valuable guidance for clinical practice. While conclusions differ among subgroups based on hemodialysis flux, the reasons remain unclear. The limited number of studies in the low-flux group might be a contributing factor. The upcoming High-Volume Hemodiafiltration vs. High-Flux Hemodialysis Registry (H4RT) trial promises to add to the evidence on this topic [33].

Our research, which comprehensively analyzes randomized controlled trials comparing hemodialysis with hemodiafiltration up to the present day, offers the most current and reliable evidence on prognosis. However, some limitations should be acknowledged. The study population primarily consisted of individuals of European descent, potentially limiting the applicability of the findings to other populations. Further independent validation by external studies is necessary to corroborate the results. The lack of access to individual-level data could limit the accuracy of our conclusions and potentially exacerbate biases present in previous studies. Additionally, the inclusion of a limited number of studies on sudden death and infection-related death may impact the precision of the conclusions drawn.

Conclusion

This meta-analysis suggests that hemodiafiltration leads to better outcomes for patients with end-stage renal disease, particularly a reduction in overall mortality and cardiovascular mortality. Notably, this benefit seems to be more pronounced for patients receiving HDF with a high convection volume.

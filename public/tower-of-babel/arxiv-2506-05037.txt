Abstract
. We study limits at infinity for homogeneous Hajłasz–Sobolev functions defined on uniformly perfect metric spaces equipped with a doubling measure. We prove that a quasicontinuous representative of such a function has a pointwise limit at infinity outside an exceptional set, defined in terms of a variational relative capacity. Our framework refines earlier approaches that relied on Hausdorff content rather than relative capacity, and it extends previous results for homogeneous Newtonian and fractional Sobolev functions.

1.
Introduction

In this paper, we study limits at infinity for functions in the homogeneous Hajłasz–Sobolev space within the framework of metric measure spaces.
Related problems have been studied for functions in the first-order Sobolev space in
[
6
,
7
,
22
,
23
,
29
,
31
]
. Similar questions have been addressed for functions of bounded variation in
[
25
]
, and for functions in fractional Sobolev spaces in
[
1
]
.

We briefly review the results in
[
22
,
1
]
that are most relevant to our work. Kline, Koskela, and Nguyen
[
22
, Theorem 1.1]
obtained limits at infinity for functions in the first-order homogeneous Newton–Sobolev space outside thin exceptional sets, which have sufficiently small Hausdorff contents in dyadic annuli at infinity. Their results rely on the assumption that the ambient metric measure space is
complete, equipped with a
d
-regular measure, and supports a suitable Poincaré inequality. In a recent work, Agarwal, Koskela, and Mohanta
[
1
]
established the existence of limits at infinity for functions in homogeneous fractional Sobolev spaces, outside thin exceptional sets characterized by small Hausdorff content at infinity. In their setting, the metric space is
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
equipped with the Euclidean metric and Lebesgue measure.

Extending the approaches in
[
22
]
and
[
1
]
, we demonstrate the existence of limits at infinity for functions in the homogeneous Hajłasz–Sobolev space
\dot{M}^{s,p}(X,d,\mu)
, where
(X,d,\mu)
is a metric measure space,
0<s\leq 1
and
0<p<\infty
.
A priori, we do not assume that the underlying metric space
X
is connected. Instead, it is assumed to be uniformly perfect and equipped with a doubling measure
\mu
, providing the necessary geometric and measure-theoretic part of our framework described in Section
2
.
The choice of the homogeneous Hajłasz–Sobolev space
\dot{M}^{s,p}
in our framework, defined in Section
4
, is general enough to encompass a wide range of Sobolev-type spaces. In particular, both the homogeneous Newton–Sobolev and fractional Sobolev spaces admit a bounded embedding into the homogeneous Hajłasz–Sobolev space considered here. For the former embedding to hold, one needs to assume a suitable Poincaré inequality as in
[
22
]
, forcing the space to be connected. We refer to Remark
8.6
for further discussion. The corresponding embedding for homogeneous fractional Sobolev spaces is developed in Section
9
.

Throughout the paper, we use median values instead of integral averages to facilitate the analysis of functions in the homogeneous Hajłasz–Sobolev space
\dot{M}^{s,p}
. Such a function may fail to be integrable on balls for small values of
s
and
p
, hence the corresponding integral averages are not well-defined. The use of medians in this context is well established; we refer to
[
1
,
9
,
12
,
17
]
for related developments. See Sections
3
and
5
for a further discussion.

A distinctive feature of our approach in studying limits at infinity is a novel use of variational relative capacity, as introduced in Definition
6.1
. In one of our main results, Theorem
7.9
, we establish conditions, under which a quasicontinuous representative of a function in the homogeneous Hajłasz–Sobolev space admits a limit of the form

\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}d(x,O)\to\infty\\
x\in X\setminus E(O)\end{subarray}}u(x)\,.

Here
O\in X
is a fixed basepoint and
E(O)
is an exceptional set, outside
of which the limit of the function
u
exists.
To quantify the smallness of such an exceptional set at infinity, we utilize the variational relative capacity. These exceptional sets in our framework are called
(s,p)
-thin at infinity, and they are discussed in Section
7
.
The novel use of relative capacity, instead of Hausdorff content as in
[
22
,
1
]
, provides a finer exceptional set, resulting in sharper and more precise analytical conclusions. We refer the reader to Section
8
and Theorem
8.4
for detailed comparisons. As an application, we extend and complement the results in
[
22
]
and
[
1
]
. For precise formulations, we refer to Remark
8.6
and Theorem
9.6
, respectively.

2.
Metric spaces

We introduce our standing assumptions on
the metric space and the measure, which are maintained throughout the paper.
For further details, we refer to
[
2
,
13
,
15
]
.
Let
(X,d,\mu)
be a metric measure space with
\operatorname{diam}(X)=\infty
. Here
\mu
is a positive complete Borel measure on
X
such that

0<\mu(B(x,r))<\infty

for all
x\in X
and
r>0
, where
B(x,r)=\{y\in X\,:\,d(y,x)<r\}
is the open ball of radius
r>0
and center
x\in X
.
The measure
\mu
is assumed to be doubling, that is, there exists a constant
c_{\mu}\geq 1
such that, whenever
x\in X
and
r>0
, we have

(1)

\mu(B(x,2r))\leq c_{\mu}\,\mu(B(x,r)).

We also assume that
the measure
\mu
satisfies
the reverse doubling condition, that is,
there are constants
0<c_{R}<1
and
\kappa>1
such that

(2)

\mu(B(x,r))\leq c_{R}\,\mu(B(x,\kappa r))

for all
x\in X
and all
r>0
.

The above
assumptions are tacitly held throughout the paper. The space
X
is separable under these assumptions,
see
[
2
, Proposition 1.6]
.
It follows from the reverse doubling condition (
2
) that
\mu(X)=\infty
.
If
X
is connected, then (
2
) for any
\kappa>1

follows from the doubling condition (
1
) and the
unboundedness of
X
;
see for instance
[
2
, Lemma 3.7]
. However,
we do not assume in this paper that
X
is connected unless otherwise specified.
Since
\mu
is doubling in our setting, the reverse doubling condition (
2
) is equivalent
with the so-called uniform perfectness of
X
, we refer to

[
13
, Section 11]
for background on this condition and examples.

We also need the following iterated versions
of the doubling and reverse doubling conditions.
Iteration of the doubling condition (
1
) gives an exponent
Q>0
and a constant
c_{Q}>0
, both only depending on
c_{\mu}
, such that

(3)

\frac{\mu(B(y,r))}{\mu(B(x,r_{0}))}\geq c_{Q}\left(\frac{r}{r_{0}}\right)^{Q}

whenever
x\in X
,
y\in B(x,r_{0})
and
0<r<r_{0}<\infty
. Condition (
3
) holds for
Q=\log_{2}c_{\mu}
, we refer to
[
2
, Lemma 3.3]
for details.
On the other hand, the doubling property and iteration of the reverse doubling condition (
2
) gives an exponent
\sigma>0
and a constant
c_{\sigma}>0
, both only depending on
c_{R}
, such that the quantitative reverse doubling condition

(4)

\frac{\mu(B(x,r))}{\mu(B(x,r_{0}))}\leq c_{\sigma}\left(\frac{r}{r_{0}}\right)^{\sigma}

holds for all
x\in X
and
0<r<r_{0}<\infty
. We refer
to the proof of
[
2
, Corollary 3.8]
.

We use the following familiar notation:

\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptE}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{E}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{E}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{E}}u(y)\,d\mu(y)=\frac{1}{\mu(E)}\int_{E}u(y)\,d\mu(y)

is the integral average of a non-negative measurable function
u
over a measurable set

with a finite positive measure, that is,
0<\mu(E)<\infty
.
The closure of a set

{\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\displaystyle E}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{E}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\textstyle E}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{E}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptstyle E}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{E}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle E}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{E}}}
. In particular, if

is a ball,
then the notation
{\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\displaystyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\textstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}}={\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\displaystyle B(x,r)}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B(x,r)}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\textstyle B(x,r)}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B(x,r)}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptstyle B(x,r)}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B(x,r)}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle B(x,r)}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B(x,r)}}}
refers to the closure of the ball
B
.
The characteristic function of a set
E\subset X
is denoted by
\mathbf{1}_{E}
;
that is,
\mathbf{1}_{E}(x)=1
if
x\in E

and
\mathbf{1}_{E}(x)=0
if

.

Next we define certain annuli, whose scaling

in (

). These annuli

-scaling frequently.
Assume that

and

. Then we write

. When

is also given, we write

It follows from (

) that

(1-c_{R})\mu(B(O,\Lambda\kappa r))\leq\mu(\Lambda A_{r}(O))\leq\mu(B(O,\Lambda\kappa r))

for all

,

and
\Lambda\geq 1
. The point

We will use the following notational convention:

C({\ast,\dotsb,\ast})
denotes a positive constant which quantitatively

3.
Medians

We work with measurable functions that need not be integrable on balls.
Therefore we use medians instead of integral averages.
In this section, we recall useful lemmata for medians from
[
1
,
9
,
12
]
. We also refer to
[
8
,
17
]
.

Let
L^{0}(X)
denote the set of all
measurable functions
u\colon X\to[-\infty,\infty]
that are finite almost everywhere on
X
.
Let
u\in L^{0}(X)
and let
E\subset X
be a set of finite positive measure. Define the median, or the
\frac{1}{2}
-median, of a function
u
over
E
as

m_{u}(E)=\inf\left\{a\in{\mathbb{R}}\,:\,\mu\left(\{x\in E\,:\,u(x)>a\}\right)<\frac{\mu(E)}{2}\right\}.

By using continuity of measure, it is easy to show that
-\infty<m_{u}(E)<\infty
, that is,
m_{u}(E)
is finite.
We also define the sub- and superlevel sets
E^{u,-}
and
E^{u,+}
as follows

(6)

E^{u,-}=\left\{x\in E\,:\,u(x)\leq m_{u}(E)\right\}\quad\text{and}\quad E^{u,+}=\left\{x\in E\,:\,u(x)\geq m_{u}(E)\right\}.

It is again easy to show that
2\mu(E^{u,-})\geq\mu(E)
and
2\mu(E^{u,+})\geq\mu(E)
.

We recall the following basic
properties of medians from
[
12
, Lemma 2.7]
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
u\in L^{0}(X)
and let
E\subset X
be a set of finite positive measure.
Then the median has the following properties:

(a)

|m_{u}(E)|\leq m_{|u|}(E)
.

(b)

If
c\in{\mathbb{R}}
, then
m_{u}(E)+c=m_{u+c}(E)
.

(c)

If
0<p<\infty
, then

m_{|u|}(E)\leq\left(2\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptE}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{E}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{E}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{E}}|u(x)|^{p}\,d\mu(x)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Next we give two lemmata for
differences involving medians. In the case of
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, these results can already be found in

[
1
, Lemma 2.4, Lemma 2.5]
. We recall the proofs for convenience.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
u\in L^{0}(X)
and let
E\subset X
be a set of finite positive measure. Then, for every
c\in{\mathbb{R}}
, there exists a set
\tilde{E}\subset E
such that
2\mu(\tilde{E})\geq\mu(E)
and

|m_{u}(E)-c|\leq|u(x)-c|

whenever
x\in\tilde{E}
.

Proof.

Fix
c\in{\mathbb{R}}
. First suppose that
m_{u}(E)\geq c
.
Let
\tilde{E}=E^{u,+}\subset E
. Then
2\mu(\tilde{E})\geq\mu(E)
and,
for every
x\in\tilde{E}
, we have

\lvert m_{u}(E)-c\rvert=m_{u}(E)-c\leq u(x)-c\leq|u(x)-c|\,.

Thus, the claim holds in the case
m_{u}(E)\geq c
.
Similarly, in the case
m_{u}(E)<c
we define
\tilde{E}=E^{u,-}
. Adapting the above argument yields the desired conclusion also in this case.
∎

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
u\in L^{0}(X)
and let
E,F\subset X
be sets of finite positive measure. Then there exists measurable subsets
\tilde{E}\subset E
and
\tilde{F}\subset F
such that
2\mu(\tilde{E})\geq\mu(E)
,
2\mu(\tilde{F})\geq\mu(F)
and

|m_{u}(E)-m_{u}(F)|\leq|u(x)-u(y)|

for all
x\in\tilde{E}
and
y\in\tilde{F}
.

Proof.

First, assume that

m_{u}(E)\leq m_{u}(F)\,.

Let
\tilde{E}=E^{u,-}\subset E
and
\tilde{F}=F^{u,+}\subset F
.
Then

2\mu(\tilde{E})\geq\mu({E})
and
2\mu(\tilde{F})\geq\mu({F})
.
Also, for all
x\in\tilde{E}
and
y\in\tilde{F}
, we get from (
6
) that

\displaystyle|m_{u}(E)-m_{u}(F)|=m_{u}(F)-m_{u}(E)\leq u(y)-u(x)=|u(x)-u(y)|.

Thus, the claim holds in the case
m_{u}(E)\leq m_{u}(F)
.
Similarly, in the case

m_{u}(F)<m_{u}(E)

we define
\tilde{E}=E^{u,+}
and
\tilde{F}=F^{u,-}
. Clearly, adapting the above argument yields the desired conclusion also in this case. ∎

4.
Hajłasz–Sobolev functions and quasicontinuity

We use Hajłasz–Sobolev functions
[
10
,
11
]
and the associated notion of Hajłasz capacity
[
20
,
12
]
. This capacity is used
to define quasicontinuous Hajłasz–Sobolev functions, whose pointwise limits at infinity are of main interest to us in this work.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
0<s\leq 1
and let
u\in L^{0}(X)
. A measurable function
g\colon X\to[0,\infty]
is an
s
-gradient of
u
, if there exists a set
E\subset X
with
\mu(E)=0
such that for all
x,y\in X\setminus E
,

(7)

|u(x)-u(y)|\leq d(x,y)^{s}(g(x)+g(y))\,.

The collection of all
s
-gradients of
u
is denoted by
\mathcal{D}_{s}(u)
.

Definition 4.2
.

Let
0<s\leq 1
and
0<p<\infty
. The homogeneous Hajłasz–Sobolev space
\dot{M}^{s,p}(X)
consists of functions
u\in L^{0}(X)
such that
{\mathcal{D}}_{s}(u)\cap L^{p}(X)\not=\emptyset
. This vector space is equipped with the quasi-seminorm

\|u\|_{\dot{M}^{s,p}(X)}=\inf_{g\in{\mathcal{D}}_{s}(u)}\|g\|_{L^{p}(X)}\,.

The Hajłasz–Sobolev space
{M}^{s,p}(X)
is
\dot{M}^{s,p}(X)\cap L^{p}(X)
equipped with the quasi-seminorm

\|u\|_{{M}^{s,p}(X)}=\|u\|_{L^{p}(X)}+\|u\|_{\dot{M}^{s,p}(X)}.

Throughout this paper, we will consider
L^{0}(X)
,
\dot{M}^{s,p}(X)
and
{M}^{s,p}(X)
to be vector spaces of functions defined everywhere, instead of
equivalence classes defined
\mu
-almost everywhere.
We nevertheless say that
v\in L^{0}(X)
is a representative of a function
u\in L^{0}(X)
, if
v(x)=u(x)
holds for
\mu
-almost every
x\in X
.
We remark that
\|v\|_{\dot{M}^{s,p}(X)}=\|u\|_{\dot{M}^{s,p}(X)}
and
\|v\|_{{M}^{s,p}(X)}=\|u\|_{{M}^{s,p}(X)}
if
v
is a representative of
u
.

We are mainly interested in fine properties of Hajłasz–Sobolev functions, and therefore
we also need the notion of capacity, see
[
20
,
12
]
.

Definition 4.3
.

Let
0<s\leq 1
and
0<p<\infty
.
The
M^{s,p}
-capacity of a set
E\subset X
is

\mathrm{Cap}_{M^{s,p}}(E)=\inf\left\{\|u\|_{M^{s,p}(X)}^{p}\,:\,u\in M^{s,p}(X)\text{ and }u\geq 1\text{ on a neighbourhood of $E$}\right\}.

If there are no test functions that satisfy the above conditions, we set
\mathrm{Cap}_{M^{s,p}}(E)=\infty
. We say that a property holds
M^{s,p}
-quasieverywhere, if it holds outside a set of
M^{s,p}
-capacity zero.

Now we list some of the basic properties of the
M^{s,p}
-capacity from
[
12
]
.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
0<s\leq 1
and
0<p<\infty
. The
M^{s,p}
-capacity
has the following properties:

(a)

If
E\subset F\subset X
, then
\mathrm{Cap}_{M^{s,p}}(E)\leq\mathrm{Cap}_{M^{s,p}}(F)
.

(b)

There exists a constant
c\geq 1
such that

\mathrm{Cap}_{M^{s,p}}\left(\bigcup_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}E_{i}\right)\leq c\sum_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}\mathrm{Cap}_{M^{s,p}}(E_{i})

whenever
E_{i}\subset X
for all
i\in{\mathbb{N}}
.

(c)

The
M^{s,p}
-capacity is an outer capacity, that is,

\mathrm{Cap}_{M^{s,p}}(E)=\inf\{\mathrm{Cap}_{M^{s,p}}(U)\,:\,E\subset U\text{ and }U\text{ is open}\}.

Proof.

(a) This follows immediately from the definition of
M^{s,p}
-capacity.
(b) This follows from the case
q=\infty
of
[
12
, Lemma 6.4]
since
M^{s,p}(X)=M^{s}_{p,\infty}(X)
using their notation, we also refer to
[
24
, Proposition 2.1]
.
(c) This is
[
12
, Remark 6.3]
.
∎

We will mostly work with
M^{s,p}
-quasicontinuous functions. These are defined next.

Definition 4.5
.

A function
u\in L^{0}(X)
is
M^{s,p}
-quasicontinuous in
X
, if for all
\varepsilon>0
there exists a set
E\subset X
such that
\mathrm{Cap}_{M^{s,p}}(E)<\varepsilon
and the restriction of
u
to
X\setminus E
is finite and continuous.

We observe that the set
E\subset X
in Definition
4.5
can be assumed to be open in
X
. This is an immediate consequence of Lemma
4.4
(c).

Next we show that if
v
is
a quasicontinuous representative of
u
, then
u=v
not only almost everywhere, but quasieverywhere in
X
.
This type of results
are well known for Sobolev spaces, in general.
We refer to

[
28
]
for a corresponding result in Hajłasz–Besov spaces.
We
provide a proof in our setting
for convenience of the reader.
To this end, we need the following two lemmata
that are adapted from
[
19
]
. See also
[
21
]
.

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
0<s\leq 1
and
0<p<\infty
. For every open set
G\subset X
and every
E\subset X
with
\mu(E)=0
, we have
\mathrm{Cap}_{M^{s,p}}(G)=\mathrm{Cap}_{M^{s,p}}(G\setminus E)
.

Proof.

By monotonicity, or Lemma
4.4
(a), we have
\mathrm{Cap}_{M^{s,p}}(G\setminus E)\leq\mathrm{Cap}_{M^{s,p}}(G)
. To prove the converse inequality, we may clearly assume
\mathrm{Cap}_{M^{s,p}}(G\setminus E)<\infty
. Let
\varepsilon>0
. Then there exists a test function
u
for
\mathrm{Cap}_{M^{s,p}}(G\setminus E)
such that

\|u\|_{M^{s,p}(X)}^{p}\leq\mathrm{Cap}_{M^{s,p}}(G\setminus E)+\varepsilon

and
u\geq 1
on some open set
U\subset X
such that
G\setminus E\subset U
. We define
\tilde{u}
so that
\tilde{u}=u
on
X\setminus E
and
\tilde{u}=1
on
E
. Then
\tilde{u}\in M^{s,p}(X)
, since
\mu(E)=0
and therefore

\tilde{u}
is a representative of
u
. Observe also that
\tilde{u}\geq 1
on
G\cup U
. Hence, we obtain

\mathrm{Cap}_{M^{s,p}}(G)\leq\mathrm{Cap}_{M^{s,p}}(G\cup U)\leq\|\tilde{u}\|_{M^{s,p}(X)}^{p}\leq\mathrm{Cap}_{M^{s,p}}(G\setminus E)+\varepsilon\,.

Thus the claim follows when
\varepsilon\to 0
.
∎

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
0<s\leq 1
and
0<p<\infty
. Let
u,v\in L^{0}(X)
be
M^{s,p}
-quasicontinuous functions such that
u=v

\mu
-almost everywhere.
Then
u=v
holds
M^{s,p}
-quasieverywhere.

Proof.

Let
c\geq 1
be the subaddivitivity constant in Lemma
4.4
(b) and let
\varepsilon>0
be given.
Let
E_{1},E_{2}\subset X
be open sets such that
\mathrm{Cap}_{M^{s,p}}(E_{1}),\mathrm{Cap}_{M^{s,p}}(E_{2})<\frac{\varepsilon}{2c}
and the restrictions of
u
to
X\setminus E_{1}
and
v
to
X\setminus E_{2}
are continuous, respectively.
Define an open set
E=E_{1}\cup E_{2}
. By Lemma
4.4
(b), we have
\mathrm{Cap}_{M^{s,p}}(E)<\varepsilon
. Observe that the difference
u-v

is continuous in
X\setminus E
, and hence the set

\{x\in X\setminus E\,:\,u(x)\neq v(x)\}=\{x\in X\setminus E\,:\,(u-v)(x)\neq 0\}

is open in the relative topology of
X\setminus E
. Thus, there exists an open set
U\subset X
such that

\{x\in X\setminus E\,:\,u(x)\neq v(x)\}=U\cap(X\setminus E)=U\setminus E\,.

On the other hand, we have

\{x\in X\,:\,u(x)\neq v(x)\}\subset E\cup\{x\in X\setminus E\,:\,u(x)\neq v(x)\}=E\cup(U\setminus E)=U\cup E\,.

and

\mu(U\setminus E)=\mu(\{x\in X\setminus E\,:\,u(x)\neq v(x)\})=0\,,

since
u=v

\mu
-almost everywhere in
X
.
By monotonicity and Lemma
4.6
,

\displaystyle\mathrm{Cap}_{M^{s,p}}(\{x\in X\,:\,u(x)\neq v(x)\})

\displaystyle\leq\mathrm{Cap}_{M^{s,p}}(U\cup E)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Cap}_{M^{s,p}}((U\cup E)\setminus(U\setminus E))=\mathrm{Cap}_{M^{s,p}}(E)<\varepsilon.

Hence the claim follows when
\varepsilon\to 0
.
∎

The following theorem is a slight reformulation of
[
12
, Theorem 1.2]
. It shows that a
function
u\in\dot{M}^{s,p}(X)
has an explicit
M^{s,p}
-quasicontinuous representative
u^{*}
that can be defined
by using certain limits of medians.

Theorem 4.8
.

Let
0<s\leq 1
and
0<p<\infty
. Let
u\in\dot{M}^{s,p}(X)
and define

u^{*}(x)=\limsup_{r\to 0}m_{u}(B(x,r))

for all
x\in X
. Then there exists a set
E\subset X
with
\mathrm{Cap}_{M^{s,p}}(E)=0
such that the limit

(8)

\lim_{r\to 0}m_{u}(B(x,r))=u^{*}(x)

exists for every
x\in X\setminus E
. Moreover,
u^{*}
is an
M^{s,p}
-quasicontinuous representative of
u
.

5.
Limits of medians at infinity

We are interested in limits of quasicontinuous Hajłasz–Sobolev functions
at infinity. The first step is to study
the limits of medians
m_{u}(A_{\kappa^{j}}(O))
, where
O\in X

is a fixed basepoint and

A_{\kappa^{j}}(O)=B(O,\kappa^{j+1})\setminus B(O,\kappa^{j})\,,\qquad j\in{\mathbb{N}}\,.

Inspired by
[
1
, Section 4]
, we prove the existence and basepoint-independence of the limit of these medians.

First we consider the easier case of
u\in M^{s,p}(X)\subset L^{p}(X)
.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
0<p<\infty
and
u\in L^{p}(X)
. Then

\lim_{j\to\infty}m_{u}(A_{\kappa^{j}}(O))=0

for every
O\in X
.

Proof.

Fix
O\in X
and let
j\in{\mathbb{N}}
. By using Lemma
3.1
and (
5
), we obtain

\begin{split}|m_{u}(A_{\kappa^{j}}(O))|&\leq m_{\lvert u\rvert}(A_{\kappa^{j}}(O))\leq\left(\frac{2}{\mu(A_{\kappa^{j}}(O))}\int_{A_{\kappa^{j}}(O)}|u(x)|^{p}\,d\mu(x)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\\
&\leq\frac{C(c_{R},p)}{\mu(B(O,\kappa^{j}))^{1/p}}\left(\int_{X}|u(x)|^{p}\,d\mu(x)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\,.\end{split}

Since
\mu(X)=\infty
and

, the right-hand side tends to zero, as

.
∎

6.
A relative capacity

The
M^{s,p}
-capacity suits well for quantifying many fine properties of Hajłasz–Sobolev functions. On the other hand, it is lacking in scaling and locality properties. To address these defects, in Definition
6.1
, we deploy variant of a relative capacity
that is more suitable for describing certain exceptional sets, outside of which we may approach infinity. These
so-called
(s,p)
-thin sets at infinity are defined in Section
7
.
We remark in passing that there are several seemingly different variants of relative capacities of fractional smoothness in metric measure spaces, see for example
[
16
,
26
]
for recent comparisons among some of them.

Definition 6.1
.

Let
0<s\leq 1
and
0<p<\infty
. Let
F\subset X
be a measurable set of diameter
0<\operatorname{diam}(F)<\infty
and let
E\subset F
. We define a relative capacity

\text{cap}_{s,p}(E,F)=\inf_{v\in\mathcal{A}_{s,p}(E)}\left\{{\frac{||v||_{L^{p}(F)}^{p}}{\operatorname{diam}(F)^{sp}}+\inf_{g\in{\mathcal{D}}_{s}(v)}||g||_{L^{p}(F)}^{p}}\right\}\,,

where
\mathcal{A}_{s,p}(E)\subset\dot{M}^{s,p}(X)
is the set of all
M^{s,p}
-quasicontinuous functions
v\in\dot{M}^{s,p}(X)
such that
v\geq 1
in
E
.

Remark 6.2
.

Suppose that
s
,
p
,
E
,
F
are as in Definition
6.1
.
Observe that
\mathbf{1}_{X}\in\mathcal{A}_{s,p}(E)

and
{\mathcal{D}}_{s}(v)\cap L^{p}(F)\not=\emptyset
for all
v\in\mathcal{A}_{s,p}(E)
.
Hence, the relative capacity in Definition
6.1
is well defined and finite.
Moreover, we clearly have

\frac{\mu(E)}{\operatorname{diam}(F)^{sp}}\leq\text{cap}_{s,p}(E,F)\leq\frac{\mu(F)}{\operatorname{diam}(F)^{sp}}\,.

More refined lower bounds are established in Section
8
, see Lemma
8.3
.

For the following capacitary weak type estimate, we recall
that

\Lambda A_{\kappa^{j}}(O)=B(O,\Lambda\kappa^{j+1})\setminus B(O,\kappa^{j}/\Lambda)

whenever
\Lambda\geq 1
,
O\in X
and
j\in{\mathbb{N}}
.
As a special case, we have
A_{\kappa^{j}}(O)=1A_{\kappa^{j}}(O)
.

Lemma 6.3
.

Let
0<s\leq 1
and
0<p<\infty
, and let
u\in\dot{M}^{s,p}(X)
be an
M^{s,p}
-quasicontinuous function. Assume that
O\in X
,
j\in{\mathbb{N}}
and

. Define a superlevel set

E_{t}=\{x\in A_{\kappa^{j}}(O)\,:\,|u(x)-m_{u}(A_{\kappa^{j}}(O))|>t\}\,.

Then for every
\Lambda\geq 1
and
g\in\mathcal{D}_{s}(u)
, we have

\mathrm{cap}_{s,p}(E_{t},\Lambda A_{\kappa^{j}}(O))\leq\frac{C}{t^{p}}\int_{\Lambda A_{\kappa^{j}}(O)}g^{p}(x)\,d\mu(x)

where
C=C(p,c_{\mu},c_{R},\Lambda)
.

Proof.

Fix
\Lambda\geq 1
and
g\in\mathcal{D}_{s}(u)
.
We define

(13)

\displaystyle v(x)=\frac{|u(x)-m_{u}(A_{\kappa^{j}}(O))|}{t}\,,\qquad x\in X.

Then
v
is
M^{s,p}
-quasicontinuous, since
u
is such.
By Definition
4.1

there exists a set
A\subset X
such that
\mu(A)=0
and, for all
x,y\in X\setminus A
,

(14)

\begin{split}|v(x)-v(y)|&=\left|\frac{|u(x)-m_{u}(A_{\kappa^{j}}(O))|}{t}-\frac{|u(y)-m_{u}(A_{\kappa^{j}}(O))|}{t}\right|\\
&\leq\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|}{t}\\
&\leq\frac{d(x,y)^{s}}{t}(g(x)+g(y)).\end{split}

Therefore
\frac{g}{t}\in\mathcal{D}_{s}(v)
for all
g\in\mathcal{D}_{s}(u)
and thus

since
u\in\dot{M}^{s,p}(X)
. Observe that
v\geq 1
in

. Hence, we can use
v
as a test function for
\mathrm{cap}_{s,p}(E_{t},\Lambda A_{\kappa^{j}}(O))
. This gives

\displaystyle\mathrm{cap}_{s,p}(E_{t},\Lambda A_{\kappa^{j}}(O))

\displaystyle\leq\frac{||v||_{L^{p}(\Lambda A_{\kappa^{j}}(O))}^{p}}{\operatorname{diam}(\Lambda A_{\kappa^{j}}(O))^{sp}}+\frac{||g||_{L^{p}(\Lambda A_{\kappa^{j}}(O))}^{p}}{t^{p}}\,.

Hence, it suffices to show that

(15)

\displaystyle\frac{||v||_{L^{p}(\Lambda A_{\kappa^{j}}(O))}^{p}}{\operatorname{diam}(\Lambda A_{\kappa^{j}}(O))^{sp}}\leq\frac{C(p,c_{\mu},c_{R},\Lambda)}{t^{p}}\int_{\Lambda A_{\kappa^{j}}(O)}g^{p}(x)\,d\mu(x).

In order to prove inequality (
15
), we denote

A^{-}=\left\{x\in\Lambda A_{\kappa^{j}}(O)\,:\,u(x)\leq m_{u}(A_{\kappa^{j}}(O))\right\}

Note that

and
therefore we can estimate

\displaystyle\int_{{A^{+}}}|u(x)-m_{u}(A_{\kappa^{j}}(O))|^{p}\,d\mu(x)

\displaystyle=\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773pt{A^{-}}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{{A^{-}}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{{A^{-}}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{{A^{-}}}}\int_{{A^{+}}}|u(x)-m_{u}(A_{\kappa^{j}}(O))|^{p}\,d\mu(x)\,d\mu(y)

\displaystyle\leq\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773pt{A^{-}}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{{A^{-}}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{{A^{-}}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{{A^{-}}}}\int_{{A^{+}}}|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}\,d\mu(x)\,d\mu(y)

\displaystyle=\int_{{A^{+}}}\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773pt{A^{-}}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{{A^{-}}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{{A^{-}}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{{A^{-}}}}|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}\,d\mu(y)\,d\mu(x)\,.

Using the fact that
g\in\mathcal{D}_{s}(u)
, followed by inequalities inequalities (
5
) and (
3
), we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\operatorname{diam}(\Lambda A_{\kappa^{j}}(O))^{sp}}

\displaystyle\int_{{A^{+}}}|u(x)-m_{u}(A_{\kappa^{j}}(O))|^{p}\,d\mu(x)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{{A^{+}}}\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773pt{A^{-}}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{{A^{-}}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{{A^{-}}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{{A^{-}}}}(g(x)+g(y))^{p}\,d\mu(y)\,d\mu(x)

\displaystyle\leq C(p)\int_{{A^{+}}}\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773pt{A^{-}}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{{A^{-}}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{{A^{-}}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{{A^{-}}}}(g^{p}(x)+g^{p}(y))\,d\mu(y)\,d\mu(x)

\displaystyle\leq C(p)\left(\int_{{A^{+}}}g^{p}(x)\,d\mu(x)+\int_{{A^{+}}}\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773pt{A^{-}}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{{A^{-}}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{{A^{-}}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{{A^{-}}}}g^{p}(y)\,d\mu(y)\,d\mu(x)\right)

\displaystyle\leq C(p)\left(\int_{{A^{+}}}g^{p}(x)\,d\mu(x)+\frac{\mu({A^{+}})}{\mu({A^{-}})}\int_{{A^{-}}}g^{p}(y)\,d\mu(y)\,\right)

\displaystyle\leq C(p)\left(\int_{\Lambda A_{\kappa^{j}}(O)}g^{p}(x)\,d\mu(x)+\frac{2\mu(\Lambda A_{\kappa^{j}}(O))}{\mu(A_{\kappa^{j}}(O))}\int_{\Lambda A_{\kappa^{j}}(O)}g^{p}(y)\,d\mu(y)\right)

\displaystyle\leq C(p,c_{\mu},c_{R},\Lambda)\int_{\Lambda A_{\kappa^{j}}(O)}g^{p}(x)\,d\mu(x).

Observe that
\Lambda A_{\kappa^{j}}(O)=A^{+}\cup A^{-}
. Hence, by interchanging
{A^{+}}
and
{A^{-}}
and arguing as above, we obtain

(16)

\begin{split}\frac{1}{\operatorname{diam}(\Lambda A_{\kappa^{j}}(O))^{sp}}&\int_{\Lambda A_{\kappa^{j}}(O)}|u(x)-m_{u}(A_{\kappa^{j}}(O))|^{p}\,d\mu(x)\\
&\qquad\leq C(p,c_{\mu},c_{R},\Lambda)\int_{\Lambda A_{\kappa^{j}}(O)}g^{p}(x)\,d\mu(x)\,.\end{split}

We can conclude from (

) and (

∎

7.
Pointwise limits at infinity

In this section, we study the pointwise behavior of Hajłasz–Sobolev functions at infinity. In particular, we show that a limit at infinity for such a function exists, and it is independent of the basepoint
O\in X
.
In fact, the limit is taken along the complement of an exceptional set
E(O)\subset X
, and this mode of convergence is made precise in the following definition.

Definition 7.1
.

Assume that
u\in L^{0}(X)
. Fix
a basepoint
O\in X
and a set
E(O)\subset X
,
possibly depending on
O
. Then by writing

\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}d(x,O)\to\infty\\
x\in X\setminus E(O)\end{subarray}}u(x)=c

we mean that for every
\varepsilon>0
there exists
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that
|u(x)-c|<\varepsilon
whenever
x\in X\setminus E(O)
and
d(x,O)>N
.

We often use the following
(s,p)
-thin sets at infinity to define exceptional sets, outside of which we may approach infinity.

Definition 7.2
.

Let
0<s\leq 1
,
0<p<\infty
, and
O\in X
.
A set
E(O)\subset X
is
(s,p)
-thin at infinity, if

\lim_{m\to\infty}\sum_{j\geq m}\mathrm{cap}_{s,p}(E(O)\cap A_{\kappa^{j}}(O),\Lambda A_{\kappa^{j}}(O))=0

for all
\Lambda>1
.

Two exponents

Q>0
and
\sigma>0
satisfying (
3
) and (
4
), respectively,
play a decisive role in the basic structure of
(s,p)
-thin sets at infinity, see
Remark
7.3
and Remark
7.4
for details.
Observe that we always have
\sigma\leq Q
.
To summarize, if
sp>Q
then the condition of
(s,p)
-thin
set at infinity may become vacuous, and if
sp\leq\sigma
then the complement of
an
(s,p)
-thin set at infinity is unbounded. We will mainly work
in the second regime, which is natural also for other reasons. We will
not explicitly exclude possibly vacuous cases from our study.

Remark 7.3
.

We assume that
X
is connected, in addition to the other standing assumptions. Let
0<s\leq 1
and
0<p<\infty
be such that

sp>Q
, where
Q
is as in
(
3
).
We now show that, under these assumptions, all subsets of

X
are
(s,p)
-thin at infinity. Therefore this notion
becomes vacuous in this regime of parameters.
Fix
O\in X
,
E(O)\subset X
and
\Lambda>1
.
Since
X
is connected, we have

\operatorname{diam}(\Lambda A_{\kappa^{j}}(O))\geq(\kappa-1)\kappa^{j}
for all
j\in{\mathbb{N}}
.
Now, if
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
, we use
\mathbf{1}_{X}
as a test function for
each of the capacities below, followed by inequality (
3
), to get

\displaystyle\sum_{j\geq m}\mathrm{cap}_{s,p}(E(O)\cap A_{\kappa^{j}}(O),\Lambda A_{\kappa^{j}}(O))

\displaystyle\quad\leq\sum_{j\geq m}\frac{||\mathbf{1}_{X}||_{L^{p}(\Lambda A_{\kappa^{j}}(O))}^{p}}{\operatorname{diam}(\Lambda A_{\kappa^{j}}(O))^{sp}}\leq\frac{1}{(\kappa-1)^{sp}}\sum_{j\geq m}\frac{\mu(\Lambda A_{\kappa^{j}}(O))}{\kappa^{jsp}}

\displaystyle\quad\leq\frac{1}{(\kappa-1)^{sp}}\sum_{j\geq m}\frac{\mu(B(O,\Lambda\kappa^{j+1}))}{\kappa^{jsp}}\leq\frac{\mu(B(O,\kappa\Lambda))}{c_{Q}(\kappa-1)^{sp}}\sum_{j\geq m}\kappa^{j(Q-sp)}\,.

Since
Q-sp<0
, the right-hand side tends to zero, as
m\to\infty
.
This shows that every set
E(O)\subset X
is
(s,p)
-thin at infinity, as claimed.

Remark 7.4
.

Let
0<s\leq 1
and
0<p<\infty
be such that

sp\leq\sigma
, where
\sigma
is as in
(
4
).
Suppose that
O\in X
and
E(O)\subset X
is
(s,p)
-thin at infinity.
Then we claim that
X\setminus E(O)
is unbounded. In other words, the infinity is a limit point
of
X\setminus E(O)

in the sense that for every
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
there exists
x\in X\setminus E(O)
such that
d(x,O)>N
. As a consequence, the limit in Definition
7.1
is unique, if it exists.

Now, we make an antithesis and assume that
X\setminus E(O)
is bounded. Then there exists
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
such
that
X\setminus E(O)\subset B(O,N)
. Thus, if
\Lambda>1
and
m>\log_{\kappa}(N)
, then by using (
5
) and (
4
) we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{j\geq m}\mathrm{cap}_{s,p}(E(O)\cap A_{\kappa^{j}}(O),\Lambda A_{\kappa^{j}}(O))=\sum_{j\geq m}\mathrm{cap}_{s,p}(A_{\kappa^{j}}(O),\Lambda A_{\kappa^{j}}(O))

\displaystyle\quad\geq\sum_{j\geq m}\frac{||\mathbf{1}_{A_{\kappa^{j}}(O)}||_{L^{p}(\Lambda A_{\kappa^{j}}(O))}^{p}}{\operatorname{diam}(\Lambda A_{\kappa^{j}}(O))^{sp}}\geq(2\kappa\Lambda)^{-sp}\sum_{j\geq m}\frac{\mu(A_{\kappa^{j}}(O))}{\kappa^{jsp}}

\displaystyle\quad\geq\frac{1-c_{R}}{(2\kappa\Lambda)^{sp}}\sum_{j\geq m}\frac{\mu(B(O,\kappa^{j}))}{\kappa^{jsp}}\geq\underbrace{\frac{1-c_{R}}{c_{\sigma}(2\kappa\Lambda)^{sp}}\mu(B(O,1))}_{>0}\sum_{j\geq m}\kappa^{j(\sigma-sp)}\,.

Since
\sigma-sp\geq 0
, the last series diverges to infinity, and this is a contradiction
since
E(O)
is
(s,p)
-thin at infinity.

We apply the basic technique in Remark
7.4
again. Namely, we show that, if
sp\leq\sigma

and
E(O)
is
(s,p)
-thin at infinity, then the existence of a pointwise
limit at infinity along
X\setminus E(O)
implies that also medians converge at infinity. The case

sp>1=\sigma
of latter Example
7.8
shows
that the restriction
sp\leq\sigma
is necessary in this generality.

Theorem 7.5
.

Let
0<s\leq 1
and
0<p<\infty
be such that

sp\leq\sigma
, where
\sigma
is as in
(
4
).
Let
u\in L^{0}(X)
,

O\in X
and
E(O)\subset X
be an
(s,p)
-thin set at infinity such
that

(17)

\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}d(x,O)\to\infty\\
x\in X\setminus E(O)\end{subarray}}u(x)=c

for some
c\in{\mathbb{R}}
. Then

\lim_{j\to\infty}m_{u}(A_{\kappa^{j}}(O))=c\,.

Proof.

Fix
c\in{\mathbb{R}}
as in (
17
). Let
j\in{\mathbb{N}}
. By Lemma
3.2
, there exists a set
\tilde{A}_{\kappa^{j}}(O)\subset{A}_{\kappa^{j}}(O)
such that
2\mu(\tilde{A}_{\kappa^{j}}(O))\geq\mu({A}_{\kappa^{j}}(O))>0
and

(18)

|m_{u}({A}_{\kappa^{j}}(O))-c|\leq|u(x)-c|

whenever
x\in\tilde{A}_{\kappa^{j}}(O)
.

We claim there exists
M\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that

\tilde{A}_{\kappa^{j}}(O)\setminus E(O)\not=\emptyset
for all
j>M
.
We make an antithesis by assuming that
\tilde{A}_{\kappa^{j}}(O)\subset E(O)
for infinitely many
j\in{\mathbb{N}}
.
Denote by
{J}\subset{\mathbb{N}}
the infinite set of all such indices
j\in{\mathbb{N}}
.
Fix
\Lambda>1
and
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
. By using monotonicity of relative capacity, (
5
) and (
4
) we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{j\geq m}\mathrm{cap}_{s,p}(E(O)\cap A_{\kappa^{j}}(O),\Lambda A_{\kappa^{j}}(O))\geq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\in{J}\\
j\geq m\end{subarray}}\mathrm{cap}_{s,p}(\tilde{A}_{\kappa^{j}}(O),\Lambda A_{\kappa^{j}}(O))

\displaystyle\quad\geq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\in{J}\\
j\geq m\end{subarray}}\frac{||\mathbf{1}_{\tilde{A}_{\kappa^{j}}(O)}||_{L^{p}(\Lambda A_{\kappa^{j}}(O))}^{p}}{\operatorname{diam}(\Lambda A_{\kappa^{j}}(O))^{sp}}\geq(2\kappa\Lambda)^{-sp}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\in{J}\\
j\geq m\end{subarray}}\frac{\mu(\tilde{A}_{\kappa^{j}}(O))}{\kappa^{jsp}}\geq\frac{1}{2(2\kappa\Lambda)^{sp}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\in{J}\\
j\geq m\end{subarray}}\frac{\mu({A}_{\kappa^{j}}(O))}{\kappa^{jsp}}

\displaystyle\quad\geq\frac{1-c_{R}}{2(2\kappa\Lambda)^{sp}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\in{J}\\
j\geq m\end{subarray}}\frac{\mu(B(O,\kappa^{j}))}{\kappa^{jsp}}\geq\underbrace{\frac{1-c_{R}}{2c_{\sigma}(2\kappa\Lambda)^{sp}}\mu(B(O,1))}_{>0}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\in{J}\\
j\geq m\end{subarray}}\kappa^{j(\sigma-sp)}\,.

Since
\sigma-sp\geq 0
and
\lvert{J}\rvert=\infty
, the last series diverges to infinity, and this is a contradiction
since
E(O)
is
(s,p)
-thin at infinity.

Let
\varepsilon>0
be given. Then by (
17
) there exists
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that

(19)

|u(x)-c|<\varepsilon

whenever
x\in X\setminus E(O)
and
d(x,O)>N
. Let
j>\max\{M,\log_{\kappa}N\}
and
x\in\tilde{A}_{\kappa^{j}}(O)\setminus E(O)
. By (
18
) and (
19
), we then have

|m_{u}({A}_{\kappa^{j}}(O))-c|\leq|u(x)-c|<\varepsilon.

Hence
\lim_{j\to\infty}m_{u}(A_{\kappa^{j}}(O))=c
.
∎

The following lemma is
[
22
, Lemma 3.1]
.

Lemma 7.6
.

Let
0<p<\infty
. If
(a_{j})_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}}
is a non-negative sequence such that
\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}}a_{j}<\infty
, then there exists a positive and decreasing sequence
(b_{j})_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}}

such that
\lim_{j\to\infty}b_{j}=0
and

\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}}\frac{a_{j}}{b_{j}^{p}}<\infty\,.

Next we formulate and prove one of our main results, which is a
converse of Theorem
7.5
for Hajłasz–Sobolev functions.
Namely, the following result gives a sufficient condition for the existence
of their pointwise limit at infinity. This sufficient condition is given in terms
of converging medians.

Theorem 7.7
.

Let
0<s\leq 1
,
0<p<\infty
, and let
u\in\dot{M}^{s,p}(X)
be an
M^{s,p}
-quasicontinuous function.
Assume that
O\in X
and the limit

(20)

\lim_{j\to\infty}m_{u}(A_{\kappa^{j}}(O))=c

exists.
Then there is a set
E(O)\subset X
, that is
(s,p)
-thin at infinity, such that

(21)

\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}d(x,O)\to\infty\\
x\in X\setminus E(O)\end{subarray}}u(x)=c\,.

Proof.

Let
g\in\mathcal{D}_{s}(u)\cap L^{p}(X)
.
Fix a constant
\Lambda>1
. Then there exists
C(\Lambda,\kappa)>0
such that
\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}}\mathbf{1}_{\Lambda A_{\kappa^{j}}(O)}(x)\leq C(\Lambda,\kappa)
for all
x\in X
. Hence, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}}\int_{\Lambda A_{\kappa^{j}}(O)}g^{p}(x)\,d\mu(x)=\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}}\int_{X}\mathbf{1}_{\Lambda A_{\kappa^{j}}(O)}(x)\cdot g^{p}(x)\,d\mu(x)\leq C(\Lambda,\kappa)\int_{X}g^{p}(x)\,d\mu(x)<\infty.

By using Lemma
7.6
, we obtain a positive and decreasing sequence
(b_{j})_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}}
such that

(22)

\lim_{j\to\infty}b_{j}=0\qquad\text{ and }\qquad\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}}\frac{1}{b_{j}^{p}}\int_{\Lambda A_{\kappa^{j}}(O)}g^{p}(x)\,d\mu(x)<\infty\,.

We define, for every
j\in{\mathbb{N}}
,

E_{j}=\{x\in A_{\kappa^{j}}(O)\,:\,|u(x)-m_{u}(A_{\kappa^{j}}(O))|>b_{j}\}\,,

and
E(O)=\bigcup_{j\geq 1}E_{j}
. By Lemma
6.3
and (
22
), we get

\displaystyle\limsup_{m\to\infty}\sum_{j\geq m}\mathrm{cap}_{s,p}(E(O)\cap A_{\kappa^{j}}(O),\Lambda A_{\kappa^{j}}(O))

\displaystyle\qquad=\limsup_{m\to\infty}\sum_{j\geq m}\mathrm{cap}_{s,p}\left(\left(\bigcup_{j\geq 1}E_{j}\right)\cap A_{\kappa^{j}}(O),\Lambda A_{\kappa^{j}}(O)\right)

\displaystyle\qquad=\limsup_{m\to\infty}\sum_{j\geq m}\mathrm{cap}_{s,p}(E_{j},\Lambda A_{\kappa^{j}}(O))

\displaystyle\qquad\leq C(p,c_{\mu},c_{R},\Lambda)\limsup_{m\to\infty}\sum_{j\geq m}\frac{1}{b_{j}^{p}}\int_{\Lambda A_{\kappa^{j}}(O)}g^{p}(x)\,d\mu(x)=0.

It follows that the set
E(O)
is
(s,p)
-thin at infinity.

Let
\varepsilon>0
be given. By using (
20
) and (
22
), we obtain
M\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that
|m_{u}(A_{\kappa^{n}}(O))-c|<\frac{\varepsilon}{2}
and
b_{n}<\frac{\varepsilon}{2}
for all
n\geq M
.
Choose
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that
N\geq\kappa^{M}
.
Fix
x\in X\setminus E(O)
such that
d(x,O)>N\geq\kappa^{M}
. Then there exists
n\geq M
such that
x\in A_{\kappa^{n}}(O)\setminus E_{n}
and

\displaystyle|u(x)-c|\leq|u(x)-m_{u}(A_{\kappa^{n}}(O))|+|m_{u}(A_{\kappa^{n}}(O))-c|<b_{n}+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}<\varepsilon.

We have shown that
(
21
) holds.
∎

Example 7.8
.

We consider the metric measure space
X={\mathbb{R}}
equipped
with the Euclidean distance and the one-dimensional Lebesgue measure.
Then inequality (
2
) holds for
\kappa=2
.
Let
u\colon{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
be any measurable function such
that
u(x)=-1
for every
x\leq-1
and
u(x)=1
for every
x\geq 1
.
Choose
O
to be the origin, so that

A_{2^{j}}(O)=(-2^{j+1},2^{j+1})\setminus(-2^{j},2^{j})

for every
j\in{\mathbb{N}}
.
By the given properties of
u
, we have

u_{A_{2^{j}}(O)}=1
for all
j\in{\mathbb{N}}
, and
therefore the limit of these medians exists, as
j\to\infty
.
That is, condition (
20
) holds
with
c=1
. On the other hand, it is clear
that
\lim_{x\to\infty}u(x)=1
and
\lim_{x\to-\infty}u(x)=-1
,
where the two limits are interpreted in the classical way.
On the other hand, our definition for the limit at infinity in (
21
) does not distinguish between the two ends of the real axis. Therefore, why does this example not contradict Theorem
7.7
?
The two explanations involving
0<s\leq 1
and
0<p<\infty
are as follows:

•

If
sp\leq 1
, then by
Lemma
5.2
, applied with the left and right halves
of
A_{2^{j}}(O)
, it is straightforward to show that
u\not\in\dot{M}^{s,p}({\mathbb{R}})

independent of its values in
(-1,1)
.
Therefore Theorem
7.7
does not apply to
u
.

•

If
sp>1
, then by Remark
7.3
with
Q=1
, the real axis

{\mathbb{R}}=E(O)
is an
(s,p)
-thin set at infinity. In particular, this fits as the claimed exceptional set in Theorem
7.7
whether or not
u\in\dot{M}^{s,p}({\mathbb{R}})
.

In the following variant of Theorem
7.7
, we
focus on regime
sp<\sigma
. Since we always have
\sigma\leq Q
if
Q
is as in
(
3
), Theorem
7.9
excludes
the possibly vacuous regime
sp>Q
.

Theorem 7.9
.

Let
0<s\leq 1
and
0<p<\infty
be such that
sp<\sigma
, where
\sigma>0
is as in (
4
).
Let
u\in\dot{M}^{s,p}(X)
be an
M^{s,p}
-quasicontinuous function.
Then for every
O\in X
there is a set
E(O)\subset X
, that is
(s,p)
-thin at infinity, such that

\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}d(x,O)\to\infty\\
x\in X\setminus E(O)\end{subarray}}u(x)=c\,,

where the constant
c\in{\mathbb{R}}
is as in Lemma
5.3
.

Proof.

By Lemma
5.3
, there exists a constant
c\in{\mathbb{R}}
such that

\lim_{j\to\infty}m_{u}(A_{\kappa^{j}}(O))=c

for all
O\in X
. We emphasize
that the constant
c
here is independent of
O\in X
. Hence, the claim follows
from Theorem
7.7
.
∎

The following result is a counterpart of Theorem
7.9
for

u\in M^{s,p}(X)=\dot{M}^{s,p}(X)\cap L^{p}(X)

without the restriction
sp<\sigma
. Due to the additional
L^{p}(X)
-integrability assumption, we
can now omit this parameter restriction and also infer that the limit at infinity is zero.

Theorem 7.10
.

Let
0<s\leq 1
,
0<p<\infty
, and let
u\in{M}^{s,p}(X)
be an
M^{s,p}
-quasicontinuous function. Then for every
O\in X
there is a set
E(O)\subset X
, that is
(s,p)
-thin at infinity, such that

\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}d(x,O)\to\infty\\
x\in X\setminus E(O)\end{subarray}}u(x)=0\,.

Proof.

Observe that
u\in L^{p}(X)
. Hence, by Lemma
5.1
, we have

\lim_{j\to\infty}m_{u}(A_{\kappa^{j}}(O))=0

for all
O\in X
. Since we also have
u\in\dot{M}^{s,p}(X)
, the claim follows
from Theorem
7.7
.
∎

8.
Hausdorff content conditions

Our results in Section
7
involve
an exceptional set that is
(s,p)
-thin at infinity. This
notion is defined in terms of a relative capacity. In this section, we show that such sets are thin at infinity in terms of a Hausdorff content of codimension
sp-\alpha
, for all
0<\alpha<sp
. See Theorem
8.4
. This result provides a geometric interpretation
of
(s,p)
-thin sets, and it allows us to compare
our results favourably to
some earlier results involving homogeneous Newtonian functions
[
22
]
.
We refer to Theorem
8.5
and Remark
8.6
for further details.

Definition 8.1
.

Let
0<\rho\leq\infty
and
d\geq 0
.
The (
\rho
-restricted) Hausdorff content of codimension
d

of a set
E\subset X
is defined by

\begin{split}&\mathcal{H}_{\rho}^{\mu,d}(E)\\
&=\inf\left\{\sum_{i}\frac{\mu(B(x_{i},r_{i}))}{r_{i}^{d}}\,:\,E\subset\bigcup_{i}B(x_{i},r_{i})\text{ where $x_{i}\in X$
}\text{and }0<r_{i}\leq\rho\text{ for all $i$}\right\},\end{split}

where the infimum is taken over all finite and countable coverings.

Let
E,F\subset X
. Then it is easy to show that

(23)

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\rho}^{\mu,d}(E\cup F)\leq\mathcal{H}_{\rho}^{\mu,d}(E)+\mathcal{H}_{\rho}^{\mu,d}(F)\,.

The subadditivity property (
23
)
together with Lemma
8.2
allows us to omit sets of zero capacity when estimating the Hausdoff content with suitable parameters.
The following lemma is known among experts, and the technique of proof originates already in
[
14
]
, but we recall the proof for convenience.

Lemma 8.2
.

Let
0<s\leq 1
,
0<p<\infty
and
0<\alpha<sp
. Then
there exists a constant
C=C(s,p,\alpha,c_{\mu})>0
such that

\mathcal{H}_{\infty}^{\mu,sp-\alpha}(E)\leq C\,\mathrm{Cap}_{M^{s,p}}(E)\,.

for every set
E\subset X
.

Proof.

Fix a set
E\subset X
.
Let
u
be a test function for
\mathrm{Cap}_{M^{s,p}}(E)

as in Definition
4.3
.
That is, we have
u\in M^{s,p}(X)
and
u\geq 1
on a neighbourhood
U
of
E
. Fix
x\in E
.
Since
x\in U
and
U
is open, there exists
n_{x}\in{\mathbb{N}}
so that

B(x,2^{-n_{x}})\subset U
and thus
m_{u}(B(x,2^{-n_{x}}))\geq 1
. Therefore we have

(24)

\displaystyle 1\leq|m_{u}(B(x,2^{-n_{x}}))-m_{u}(B(x,1))|+|m_{u}(B(x,1))|

for all
x\in E
.

Define
E_{1}=\{x\in E\,:\,|m_{u}(B(x,2^{-n_{x}}))-m_{u}(B(x,1))|\geq\frac{1}{2}\}
.
We now estimate the Hausdorff content of
E_{1}
and, for this purpose, we fix
g\in\mathcal{D}_{s}(u)
.
Fix
x\in E_{1}
. Denote
r_{k}=2^{-k}
and
B(x,r_{k})=B_{k}(x)
for every
k\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
. We have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\leq|m_{u}(B(x,2^{-n_{x}}))-m_{u}(B(x,1))|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=0}^{n_{x}-1}|m_{u}(B_{k+1}(x))-m_{u}(B_{k}(x))|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}|m_{u}(B_{k+1}(x))-m_{u}(B_{k}(x))|.

and let
k\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
. Then by Lemma
5.2
we have

\displaystyle|m_{u}(B_{k}(x))

\displaystyle-m_{u}(B_{k+1}(x))|^{p}

\displaystyle\leq C(s,p)r_{k}^{sp}\left(\frac{1}{\mu(B_{k}(x))}\int_{B_{k}(x)}g^{p}(z)\,d\mu(z)+\frac{1}{\mu(B_{k+1}(x))}\int_{B_{k+1}(x)}g^{p}(y)\,d\mu(y)\right)

\displaystyle\leq C(s,p,c_{\mu})r_{k}^{sp}\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB_{k}(x)}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{k}(x)}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{k}(x)}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{k}(x)}}g^{p}(y)\,d\mu(y)\,.

Let
\varepsilon=\alpha/p>0
then we have

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}2^{-k\varepsilon}\leq C(\alpha,p)\cdot\frac{1}{2}\leq\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}C(\alpha,s,p,c_{\mu})r_{k}^{s}\left(\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB_{k}(x)}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{k}(x)}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{k}(x)}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{k}(x)}}g^{p}(y)\,d\mu(y)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\,.

By comparing the above two series we obtain
k\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
, depending on
x\in E_{1}
, so that

r_{k}^{\alpha}=2^{-k\varepsilon p}\leq C(s,p,\alpha,c_{\mu})r_{k}^{sp}\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB_{k}(x)}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{k}(x)}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{k}(x)}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{k}(x)}}g^{p}(y)\,d\mu(y)\,.

Define
r_{x}=r_{k}
and
B_{x}=B_{k}(x)=B(x,r_{x})
.
Hence, for every
x\in E_{1}
, there exists a ball
B_{x}
centered at
x
and
of radius
r_{x}\leq 1
such that

(25)

\displaystyle\mu(B_{x})r_{x}^{\alpha-sp}\leq C(s,p,\alpha,c_{\mu})\int_{B_{x}}g^{p}(y)\,d\mu(y)\,.

By the
5r
-covering lemma
[
2
, Lemma 1.7]
, we obtain countable number of points
x_{j}\in E_{1}
,
j\in J\subset{\mathbb{N}}
, so that the balls
B_{x_{j}}
are pairwise disjoint and
E_{1}\subset\bigcup_{j\in J}5B_{x_{j}}
. By the doubling condition (
1
) and
inequality (
25
), we then have

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\infty}^{\mu,sp-\alpha}(E_{1})

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j\in J}\frac{\mu(5B_{x_{j}})}{(5r_{x_{j}})^{sp-\alpha}}

\displaystyle\leq C(s,p,\alpha,c_{\mu})\sum_{j\in J}\int_{B_{x_{j}}}g^{p}(y)\,d\mu(y)

\displaystyle\leq C(s,p,\alpha,c_{\mu})\int_{X}g^{p}(y)\,d\mu(y)\,.

By taking the infimum over all
g\in\mathcal{D}_{s}(u)
, we get

(26)

\mathcal{H}_{\infty}^{\mu,sp-\alpha}(E_{1})\leq C(s,p,\alpha,c_{\mu})\lVert u\rVert_{\dot{M}^{s,p}(X)}^{p}\,.

It remains to estimate the Hausdorff
content of
E\setminus E_{1}
.
Fix
x\in E\setminus E_{1}
. By (
24
), we have
|m_{u}(B(x,1))|\geq\frac{1}{2}.
Then by Lemma
3.1
we get that

(27)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\leq|m_{u}(B(x,1))|\leq m_{|u|}(B(x,1))

\displaystyle\leq\left(2\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB(x,1)}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B(x,1)}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B(x,1)}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B(x,1)}}|u(y)|^{p}\,d\mu(y)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Observe that
E\setminus E_{1}\subset\bigcup_{x\in E\setminus E_{1}}B(x,1)
. Then by using the
5r
-covering lemma
[
2
, Lemma 1.7]
, we obtain points

x_{j}\in E\setminus E_{1}
,
j\in J\subset{\mathbb{N}}
, such that
E\setminus E_{1}\subset\bigcup_{j\in J}5B_{x_{j}}
, the balls
B_{x_{j}}=B(x_{j},r_{x_{j}})
are pairwise disjoint and
r_{x_{j}}=1
for all
j\in J
. By the doubling condition (
1
) and inequality (
27
), we obtain

(28)

\begin{split}\mathcal{H}_{\infty}^{\mu,sp-\alpha}(E\setminus E_{1})&\leq\sum_{j\in J}\frac{\mu(5B_{x_{j}})}{(5r_{x_{j}})^{sp-\alpha}}\\
&\leq C(p,c_{\mu})\sum_{j\in J}\int_{B_{x_{j}}}|u(y)|^{p}\,d\mu(y)\leq C(p,c_{\mu})\lVert u\rVert_{L^{p}(X)}^{p}.\end{split}

Finally, by combining inequalities (
23
), (
26
) and (
28
) we get that

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\infty}^{\mu,sp-\alpha}(E)

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{H}_{\infty}^{\mu,sp-\alpha}(E_{1})+\mathcal{H}_{\infty}^{\mu,sp-\alpha}(E\setminus E_{1})

\displaystyle\leq C(s,p,\alpha,c_{\mu})\lVert u\rVert_{\dot{M}^{s,p}(X)}^{p}+C(p,c_{\mu})\lVert u\rVert_{L^{p}(X)}^{p}\leq C(s,p,\alpha,c_{\mu})\lVert u\rVert_{M^{s,p}(X)}^{p}\,.

Hence, we get the desired inequality

\mathcal{H}_{\infty}^{\mu,sp-\alpha}(E)\leq C\,\mathrm{Cap}_{M^{s,p}}(E)

by taking the infimum over all test functions
u
for
\mathrm{Cap}_{M^{s,p}}(E)
.
∎

The following
lemma is a key tool when showing that an

(s,p)
-thin set at infinity is
thin at infinity also in terms of Hausdorff contents
of codimension
sp-\alpha
, for all
0<\alpha<sp
.

Lemma 8.3
.

Let
0<s\leq 1
,
0<p<\infty
,
\Lambda>1
and
0<\alpha<sp
. Fix
O\in X
and
j\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Then for every
E\subset A_{\kappa^{j}}(O)
we have

\frac{\mathcal{H}_{\infty}^{\mu,sp-\alpha}(E)}{\kappa^{j\alpha}}\leq C\,\mathrm{cap}_{s,p}(E,\Lambda A_{\kappa^{j}}(O))

for a constant
C=C(s,p,\alpha,c_{\mu},\kappa,\Lambda)
.

Proof.

Fix a set
E\subset A_{\kappa^{j}}(O)
and denote

r_{0}=(1-\Lambda^{-1})\kappa^{j}
.
Observe that
r_{0}\leq(\Lambda-1)\kappa^{j+1}
and therefore

B(x,r_{0})\subset\Lambda A_{\kappa^{j}}(O)

for every
x\in E
.
Let
v
be a test function for
\text{cap}_{s,p}(E,\Lambda A_{\kappa^{j}}(O))
as in Definition
6.1
.
Thus
v\in\dot{M}^{s,p}(X)
is
M^{s,p}
-quasicontinuous and satisfies
v\geq 1
in
E
.

We will consider two cases and combine them at the end of the proof.
First we assume that there exists
x\in E
such that

(29)

\lvert m_{v}(B(x,r_{0}))\rvert\geq\frac{1}{2}\,.

Then
by Lemma
3.1
we get that

\frac{1}{2}\leq\lvert m_{v}(B(x,r_{0}))\rvert\leq m_{\lvert v\rvert}(B(x,r_{0}))\leq\left(2\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB(x,r_{0})}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B(x,r_{0})}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B(x,r_{0})}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B(x,r_{0})}}|v(y)|^{p}\,d\mu(y)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\,.

By using also inequality (
3
), we get

\displaystyle\mu(B(O,\kappa^{j+1}))

\displaystyle\leq C(c_{\mu},\kappa,\Lambda)\mu(B(x,r_{0}))

\displaystyle\leq C(p,c_{\mu},\kappa,\Lambda)\int_{B(x,r_{0})}|v(y)|^{p}\,d\mu(y)\leq C(p,c_{\mu},\kappa,\Lambda)\int_{\Lambda A_{\kappa^{j}}(O)}|v(y)|^{p}\,d\mu(y)\,.

Since
E\subset B(O,\kappa^{j+1})
, we can estimate

(30)

\begin{split}\frac{\mathcal{H}_{\infty}^{\mu,sp-\alpha}(E)}{\kappa^{j\alpha}}&\leq\frac{1}{\kappa^{j\alpha}}\cdot\frac{\mu(B(O,\kappa^{j+1}))}{(\kappa^{j+1})^{sp-\alpha}}\leq\frac{C(p,c_{\mu},\kappa,\Lambda)}{\kappa^{jsp}}\int_{\Lambda A_{\kappa^{j}}(O)}|v(y)|^{p}\,d\mu(y)\\
&\leq\frac{C(s,p,c_{\mu},\kappa,\Lambda)}{\operatorname{diam}(\Lambda A_{\kappa^{j}}(O))^{sp}}\int_{\Lambda A_{\kappa^{j}}(O)}|v(y)|^{p}\,d\mu(y)\,.\end{split}

We now turn to the second case of the proof by assuming that,
for all
x\in E
,

(31)

\lvert m_{v}(B(x,r_{0}))\rvert<\frac{1}{2}\,.

Let
v^{*}(x)=\limsup_{r\to 0}m_{v}(B(x,r))
for all
x\in X
.
We denote

E_{1}=\{x\in E\,:\,v(x)=v^{*}(x)\}\quad\text{ and }\quad E_{2}=\{x\in E\,:\,\text{limit }\lim_{r\to 0}m_{v}(B(x,r))\text{ exists}\}\,.

We also denote
F_{1}=E\setminus E_{1}
and
F_{2}=E\setminus E_{2}
.
Theorem
4.7
and Theorem
4.8

combined show that
\mathrm{Cap}_{M^{s,p}}(F_{1})=0
and
\mathrm{Cap}_{M^{s,p}}(F_{2})=0
.
Observe that
E_{1}\cap E_{2}=E\setminus(F_{1}\cup F_{2})
.
Inequality (
23
) and Lemma
8.2
imply that

(32)

\begin{split}\mathcal{H}_{\infty}^{\mu,sp-\alpha}(E)&=\mathcal{H}_{\infty}^{\mu,sp-\alpha}(E_{1}\cap E_{2})+\mathcal{H}_{\infty}^{\mu,sp-\alpha}(F_{1}\cup F_{2})\\
&\leq\mathcal{H}_{\infty}^{\mu,sp-\alpha}(E_{1}\cap E_{2})+\mathcal{H}_{\infty}^{\mu,sp-\alpha}(F_{1})+\mathcal{H}_{\infty}^{\mu,sp-\alpha}(F_{2})=\mathcal{H}_{\infty}^{\mu,sp-\alpha}(E_{1}\cap E_{2})\,.\end{split}

Hence, it suffices to estimate
\mathcal{H}_{\infty}^{\mu,sp-\alpha}(E_{1}\cap E_{2})
.

Fix
g\in\mathcal{D}^{s}(v)
.
Let

x\in E_{1}\cap E_{2}\subset E
.
Define
r_{k}=\kappa^{-k}r_{0}
and
B_{k}(x)=B(x,r_{k})
for all
k\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
.
We observe by using the case inequality (
31
) that

\displaystyle 1\leq v(x)=\lvert v(x)\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\lvert v(x)-m_{v}(B(x,r_{0}))\rvert+\lvert m_{v}(B(x,r_{0}))\rvert

\displaystyle<\lvert v(x)-m_{v}(B(x,r_{0}))\rvert+\frac{1}{2}\,.

Hence, using also the assumption that
x\in E_{1}\cap E_{2}
, we obtain

(33)

\begin{split}\frac{1}{2}\leq\lvert v(x)-m_{v}(B(x,r_{0}))\rvert&=\lim_{n\to\infty}\lvert m_{v}(B(x,r_{n}))-m_{v}(B(x,r_{0}))\rvert\\
&\leq\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\lvert m_{v}(B(x,r_{k+1}))-m_{v}(B(x,r_{k}))\rvert\,.\end{split}

Fix
k\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
.
Then by Lemma
5.2
we have

(34)

\begin{split}|m_{v}&(B_{k+1}(x))-m_{v}(B_{k}(x))|^{p}\\
&\leq C(s,p)r_{k}^{sp}\left(\frac{1}{\mu(B_{k+1}(x))}\int_{B_{k+1}(x)}g^{p}(z)\,d\mu(z)+\frac{1}{\mu(B_{k}(x))}\int_{B_{k}(x)}g^{p}(y)\,d\mu(y)\right)\\
&\leq C(s,p,\kappa,c_{\mu})r_{k}^{sp}\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB_{k}(x)}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{k}(x)}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{k}(x)}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{k}(x)}}g^{p}(y)\,d\mu(y)\,.\end{split}

Let
\varepsilon=\alpha/p>0
. By combining the estimates (
33
) and (
34
), we get

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\kappa^{-k\varepsilon}=C(\alpha,p,\kappa)\cdot\frac{1}{2}\leq\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}C(s,p,\alpha,\kappa,c_{\mu})r_{k}^{s}\left(\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB_{k}(x)}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{k}(x)}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{k}(x)}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{k}(x)}}g^{p}(y)\,d\mu(y)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\,.

By comparing the above two series we obtain
k\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
, depending on
x\in E_{1}\cap E_{2}
, so that

\frac{r_{k}^{\alpha}}{r_{0}^{\alpha}}=\kappa^{-k\alpha}=\kappa^{-k\varepsilon p}\leq C(s,p,\alpha,\kappa,c_{\mu})r_{k}^{sp}\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB_{k}(x)}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{k}(x)}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{k}(x)}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{k}(x)}}g^{p}(y)\,d\mu(y)\,.

We denote
r_{x}=r_{k}\leq r_{0}
and
B_{x}=B_{k}(x)=B(x,r_{x})
. Then, for every
x\in E_{1}\cap E_{2}
, there exists a ball
B_{x}\subset B(x,r_{0})\subset\Lambda A_{\kappa^{j}}(O)
centered at
x
and
of radius
r_{x}\leq r_{0}
such that

(35)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{r_{0}^{\alpha}}\cdot\frac{\mu(B_{x})}{r_{x}^{sp-\alpha}}\leq C(s,p,\alpha,\kappa,c_{\mu})\int_{B_{x}}g^{p}(y)\,d\mu(y)\,.

By the
5r
-covering lemma
[
2
, Lemma 1.7]
, we obtain points
x_{m}\in E_{1}\cap E_{2}
,
m\in M\subset{\mathbb{N}}
, so that the balls
B_{x_{m}}
are pairwise disjoint and
E_{1}\cap E_{2}\subset\bigcup_{m\in M}5B_{x_{m}}
.

Recall that

r_{0}=(1-\Lambda^{-1})\kappa^{j}

and
B_{x_{m}}\subset\Lambda A_{\kappa^{j}}(O)
for all
m
. By inequalities (
1
) and
(
35
), we then have

\displaystyle\frac{\mathcal{H}_{\infty}^{\mu,sp-\alpha}(E_{1}\cap E_{2})}{\kappa^{j\alpha}}

\displaystyle\leq C(\alpha,\Lambda)\frac{\mathcal{H}_{\infty}^{\mu,sp-\alpha}(E_{1}\cap E_{2})}{r_{0}^{\alpha}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C(\alpha,\Lambda)}{r_{0}^{\alpha}}\sum_{m\in M}\frac{\mu(5B_{x_{m}})}{(5r_{x_{m}})^{sp-\alpha}}

\displaystyle\leq C(s,p,\alpha,\kappa,c_{\mu},\Lambda)\sum_{m\in M}\int_{B_{x_{m}}}g^{p}(y)\,d\mu(y)

\displaystyle\leq C(s,p,\alpha,\kappa,c_{\mu},\Lambda)\int_{\Lambda A_{\kappa^{j}}(O)}g^{p}(y)\,d\mu(y)\,.

By taking also (
32
) into account and taking the infimum over all
g\in\mathcal{D}_{s}(v)
, we get

(36)

\frac{\mathcal{H}_{\infty}^{\mu,sp-\alpha}(E)}{\kappa^{j\alpha}}\leq\frac{\mathcal{H}_{\infty}^{\mu,sp-\alpha}(E_{1}\cap E_{2})}{\kappa^{j\alpha}}\leq C(s,p,\alpha,\kappa,c_{\mu},\Lambda)\inf_{g\in\mathcal{D}_{s}(v)}\lVert g\rVert_{L^{p}(\Lambda A_{\kappa^{j}}(O))}^{p}\,.

Depending
whether (
29
) or (
31
) holds,
we can use the corresponding inequality (
30
) or (
36
), respectively, to deduce that

\frac{\mathcal{H}_{\infty}^{\mu,sp-\alpha}(E)}{\kappa^{j\alpha}}\leq C(s,p,\alpha,\kappa,c_{\mu},\Lambda)\left(\frac{\lVert v\rVert_{L^{p}(\Lambda A_{\kappa^{j}}(O))}^{p}}{\operatorname{diam}(\Lambda A_{\kappa^{j}}(O))^{sp}}+\inf_{g\in\mathcal{D}_{s}(v)}\lVert g\rVert_{L^{p}(\Lambda A_{\kappa^{j}}(O))}^{p}\right)\,.

The claim follows by taking infimum over test functions
v
for
\text{cap}_{s,p}(E,\Lambda A_{\kappa^{j}}(O))
.
∎

Next we provide some geometric properties
of
(s,p)
-thin sets.

Theorem 8.4
.

Let

and
0<p<\infty
be such that

sp\leq\sigma
, where
\sigma>0
is as in (

).
Assume that
O\in X
and that a set

(s,p)
-thin at infinity.
Then

\lim_{m\to\infty}\sum_{j\geq m}\frac{\mathcal{H}_{\kappa^{j+1}}^{\mu,sp-\alpha}(E(O)\cap A_{\kappa^{j}}(O))}{\kappa^{j\alpha}}=0\qquad\text{ for all }0<\alpha<sp\,.

Proof.

Fix
0<\alpha<sp
and
\Lambda>1
.
Since
0<sp-\alpha<sp\leq\sigma
, by using
[

with

\mathcal{H}_{\kappa^{j+1}}^{\mu,sp-\alpha}(E(O)\cap A_{\kappa^{j}}(O))\leq C(c_{\sigma},c_{\mu})\mathcal{H}_{\infty}^{\mu,sp-\alpha}(E(O)\cap A_{\kappa^{j}}(O))

9.
Application to fractional Sobolev spaces

Our main results Theorem
7.9
, Theorem
7.10
and Theorem
8.5
are concerned with limits of Hajłasz–Sobolev functions at infinity.
We initially chose to work with a homogeneous Hajłasz–Sobolev space
\dot{M}^{s,p}(X)
for two reasons. First, this space is relatively easy to work with due to a simple definition of
s
-gradients. Second,
this space is robust since several other function spaces admit a bounded embedding into a homogeneous Hajłasz–Sobolev space. The reader may consider
Remark
8.6
for an example. For another example,
assuming that
0<p,q\leq\infty
and
0<s\leq 1
, then so-called Hajłasz–Triebel–Lizorkin
space
\dot{M}^{s,p}_{q}(X)
embeds
boundedly to the homogeneous Hajłasz–Sobolev space
\dot{M}^{s,p}(X)
, see
[
24
, Proposition 2.1]
.

In this section, we use our
results to study limits at infinity for
fractional Sobolev functions. This complements and extends the recent results in
[
1
]

that apply in Euclidean spaces.

Definition 9.1
.

Let
0<s\leq 1
and
0<p,q<\infty
. The homogeneous fractional Sobolev space
\dot{W}_{q}^{s,p}(X)
consists of all
u\in L^{0}(X)
for which

\|u\|_{\dot{W}_{q}^{s,p}(X)}=\left(\int_{X}\left(\int_{X}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{q}}{d(x,y)^{sq}\mu(B(x,d(x,y)))}\,d\mu(y)\right)^{\frac{p}{q}}\,d\mu(x)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}<\infty\,.

The Fractional Sobolev space
W^{s,p}_{q}(X)
is
\dot{W}^{s,p}_{q}(X)\cap L^{p}(X)
equipped with the quasi-seminorm

\|u\|_{W^{s,p}_{q}(X)}=\|u\|_{L^{p}(X)}+\|u\|_{\dot{W}_{q}^{s,p}(X)}\,.

If
q=p
, then
W^{s,p}_{q}(X)=W^{s,p}_{p}(X)
is a generalization of the well known Gagliardo–Sobolev space or a fractional Sobolev space from the Euclidean setting. We refer to

[
4
,
5
,
27
,
30
]
for expositions on these spaces
that have attracted plenty of interest in recent years.

The following embedding lemma might be of independent interest, since it covers
the full range
p,q\in(0,\infty)
.
When
p,q\in(1,\infty)
this lemma is a consequence
of
[
16
, Lemma 6.5]
. We also refer to
[
24
, Proposition 4.1]
.

Lemma 9.2
.

Let
0<s\leq 1
and
0<p,q<\infty
. Then

\dot{W}^{s,p}_{q}(X)\subset\dot{M}^{s,p}(X)

and

\|u\|_{\dot{M}^{s,p}(X)}\leq C(q,c_{\mu})\|u\|_{\dot{W}^{s,p}_{q}(X)}

for all
u\in\dot{W}^{s,p}_{q}(X).

Proof.

Let
u\in\dot{W}^{s,p}_{q}(X)
. Define
g\colon X\to[0,\infty]
as

g(x)=\left(\int_{X}\frac{|u(x)-u(z)|^{q}}{d(x,z)^{sq}\mu(B(x,d(x,z)))}\,d\mu(z)\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\,,\qquad x\in X\,.

Observe that

\lVert g\rVert_{L^{p}(X)}=\lVert u\rVert_{\dot{W}^{s,p}_{q}(X)}<\infty\,.

Hence, it suffices to show that a constant multiple of
g
is an
s
-gradient of
u
.

Since
u\in L^{0}(X)
, there exists a set
E\subset X
such that

\mu(E)=0
and
u
is finite in
X\setminus E
.
Fix
x,y\in X\setminus E
such that
x\not=y
. Then

(39)

\begin{split}|u(x)-u(y)|&\leq|u(x)-m_{u}(B(x,d(x,y)))|+|u(y)-m_{u}(B(y,d(x,y)))|\\
&\hskip 99.58464pt+|m_{u}(B(x,d(x,y)))-m_{u}(B(y,d(x,y)))|\,.\end{split}

Using Lemma
3.2
,
with
E=B(x,d(x,y))
and the constant
c=u(x)\in{\mathbb{R}}
,
we obtain a set

\tilde{B}_{x,y}\subset B(x,d(x,y))
such that
2\mu(\tilde{B}_{x,y})\geq\mu({B}(x,d(x,y)))
and

|u(x)-m_{u}(B(x,d(x,y)))|=|m_{u}(B(x,d(x,y)))-c|\leq|u(z)-c|=|u(x)-u(z)|

for all
z\in\tilde{B}_{x,y}
. Therefore

\displaystyle|u(x)-m_{u}(B(x,d(x,y)))|

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{\tilde{B}_{x,y}}\frac{|u(x)-u(z)|^{q}}{\mu(\tilde{B}_{x,y})}\,d\mu(z)\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{\frac{1}{q}}\left(\int_{{B}(x,d(x,y))}\frac{|u(x)-u(z)|^{q}}{\mu({B}(x,d(x,y)))}\,d\mu(z)\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{\frac{1}{q}}d(x,y)^{s}\left(\int_{{B}(x,d(x,y))}\frac{|u(x)-u(z)|^{q}}{d(x,z)^{sq}\mu({B}(x,d(x,z)))}\,d\mu(z)\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\,,

where the last step follows since
d(x,z)<d(x,y)
for all
z\in{B}(x,d(x,y))
. Thus,

(40)

|u(x)-m_{u}(B(x,d(x,y)))|\leq 2^{\frac{1}{q}}d(x,y)^{s}g(x).

In a similar way, by interchanging
x
and
y
, we obtain

(41)

|u(y)-m_{u}(B(y,d(x,y)))|\leq 2^{\frac{1}{q}}d(x,y)^{s}g(y).

For the remaining term in (
39
), we have by Lemma
3.1
and triangle inequality,

(42)

\begin{split}|m_{u}&(B(x,d(x,y)))-m_{u}(B(y,d(x,y)))|\\
&=|m_{u-m_{u}(B(y,d(x,y)))}(B(x,d(x,y)))|\\
&\leq m_{\lvert u-m_{u}(B(y,d(x,y)))\rvert}(B(x,d(x,y)))\\
&\leq\left(2\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB(x,d(x,y))}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B(x,d(x,y))}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B(x,d(x,y))}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B(x,d(x,y))}}|u(z)-m_{u}(B(y,d(x,y)))|^{q}\,d\mu(z)\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\\
&\leq C(q)\left(\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB(x,d(x,y))}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B(x,d(x,y))}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B(x,d(x,y))}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B(x,d(x,y))}}|u(z)-u(y)|^{q}\,d\mu(z)\right.\\
&\left.\qquad\qquad+\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB(x,d(x,y))}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B(x,d(x,y))}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B(x,d(x,y))}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B(x,d(x,y))}}|u(y)-m_{u}(B(y,d(x,y)))|^{q}\,d\mu(z)\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}.\end{split}

Using (
42
) and (
41
), followed triangle inequality and
(
1
), we get

(43)

\begin{split}|m_{u}&(B(x,d(x,y)))-m_{u}(B(y,d(x,y)))|\\
&\leq C(q)\left(\int_{B(x,d(x,y))}\frac{|u(z)-u(y)|^{q}}{\mu(B(x,d(x,y)))}\,d\mu(z)+|u(y)-m_{u}(B(y,d(x,y)))|^{q}\,\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\\
&\leq C(q)\left(\int_{B(y,2d(x,y))}\frac{|u(z)-u(y)|^{q}}{\mu(B(x,d(x,y)))}\,d\mu(z)+2(d(x,y)^{s}g(y))^{q}\,\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\\
&\leq C(q)\left((2d(x,y))^{sq}c_{\mu}^{2}\int_{B(y,2d(x,y))}\frac{|u(y)-u(z)|^{q}}{d(y,z)^{sq}\mu(B(y,d(y,z)))}\,d\mu(z)+2(d(x,y)^{s}g(y))^{q}\,\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\\
&\leq C(q,c_{\mu})\left(d(x,y)^{sq}g^{q}(y)+d(x,y)^{sq}g^{q}(y)\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\leq C(q,c_{\mu})d(x,y)^{s}g(y)\,.\end{split}

Combining estimates (
39
), (
40
), (
41
) and (
43
), we get

|u(x)-u(y)|\leq C(q,c_{\mu})d(x,y)^{s}(g(x)+g(y))

for every
x,y\in X\setminus E
. Define
h=C(q,c_{\mu})g
. Then
h\in\mathcal{D}_{s}(u)
and since

u\in\dot{W}^{s,p}_{q}(X)
, we get

\displaystyle\|u\|_{\dot{M}^{s,p}(X)}

\displaystyle\leq\|h\|_{L^{p}(X)}=C(q,c_{\mu})\lVert u\rVert_{\dot{W}^{s,p}_{q}(X)}<\infty\,.

We can conclude that
u\in\dot{M}^{s,p}(X)
.
∎

We need the notion of quasicontinuity in the fractional Sobolev setting.
For this purpose, we first define the following fractional Sobolev capacity.
The reader is encouraged to recall Definition
4.3
for the corresponding definition of a Hajłasz capacity.

Definition 9.3
.

Let
0<s\leq 1
and
0<p,q<\infty
.
The
W^{s,p}_{q}
-capacity of a set
E\subset X
is

\mathrm{Cap}_{W^{s,p}_{q}}(E)=\inf\left\{\|u\|_{W^{s,p}_{q}(X)}^{p}\,:\,u\in W^{s,p}_{q}(X)\text{ and }u\geq 1\text{ on a neighbourhood of $E$}\right\}.

If there are no test functions that satisfy the above conditions, we set
\mathrm{Cap}_{W^{s,p}_{q}}(E)=\infty
.

Lemma
9.2
implies the following
comparison of capacities. See
[
16
,
26
]
for similar results.

Lemma 9.4
.

Let
0<s\leq 1
and
0<p,q<\infty
. Then there exists a constant
C=C(p,q,c_{\mu})
such that, for every
E\subset X
, we have

(44)

\mathrm{Cap}_{M^{s,p}}(E)\leq C\,\mathrm{Cap}_{W^{s,p}_{q}}(E)\,.

Proof.

Fix a set
E\subset X
such that
\mathrm{Cap}_{W^{s,p}_{q}}(E)<\infty
. Let

u
be a test function for
\mathrm{Cap}_{W^{s,p}_{q}}(E)
. Then

u\in W^{s,p}_{q}(X)
and
u\geq 1
on a neighbourhood of
E
.
Lemma
9.2
implies that
u\in M^{s,p}(X)
and

\displaystyle\mathrm{Cap}_{M^{s,p}}(E)\leq\lVert u\rVert_{M^{s,p}(X)}^{p}

\displaystyle=\left(\lVert u\rVert_{L^{p}(X)}+\lVert u\rVert_{\dot{M}^{s,p}(X)}\right)^{p}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\lVert u\rVert_{L^{p}(X)}+C(q,c_{\mu})\lVert u\rVert_{\dot{W}^{s,p}(X)}\right)^{p}\leq C(p,q,c_{\mu})\lVert u\rVert_{W^{s,p}(X)}^{p}\,.

The claim follows by taking infimum over all test functions
u
as above.
∎

The following definition of quasicontinuity is a counterpart of Definition
4.5
.

Definition 9.5
.

A function
u\in L^{0}(X)
is
W^{s,p}_{q}
-quasicontinuous in
X
, if for all
\varepsilon>0
there exists a set
E\subset X
such that
\mathrm{Cap}_{W^{s,p}_{q}}(E)<\varepsilon
and the restriction of
u
to
X\setminus E
is finite and continuous.

The following theorem is concerned with
the limit at infinity for functions in homogeneous fractional Sobolev spaces.
We refer to
[
1
]
for similar results in Euclidean spaces.

Theorem 9.6
.

Let
0<s\leq 1
and
0<p,q<\infty
be such that

sp<\sigma
, where
\sigma
is as in (
4
).
Let
u\in\dot{W}^{s,p}_{q}(X)
be an
W^{s,p}_{q}
-quasicontinuous function.
Then for every
O\in X
there is a set
E(O)\subset X
such that

\lim_{m\to\infty}\sum_{j\geq m}\frac{\mathcal{H}_{\kappa^{j+1}}^{\mu,sp-\alpha}(E(O)\cap A_{\kappa^{j}}(O))}{\kappa^{j\alpha}}=0\qquad\text{ for all }0<\alpha<sp

and

\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}d(x,O)\to\infty\\
x\in X\setminus E(O)\end{subarray}}u(x)=c\,,

where
c\in{\mathbb{R}}
is as in Lemma
5.3
.

Proof.

Let
C=C(p,q,c_{\mu})>0
be as in Lemma
9.4
.
Let
u\in\dot{W}^{s,p}_{q}(X)
be a
W^{s,p}_{q}
-quasicontinuous function. By Lemma
9.2
, we have
u\in\dot{M}^{s,p}(X)
.
Fix
\varepsilon>0
.
Since
u
is
W^{s,p}_{q}
-quasicontinuous, there exists a set
E\subset X
such that
\mathrm{Cap}_{W^{s,p}_{q}}(E)<C^{-1}\varepsilon
and the restriction of
u
to
X\setminus E
is continuous. Lemma
9.4
implies that

\mathrm{Cap}_{M^{s,p}}(E)\leq C\,\mathrm{Cap}_{W^{s,p}_{q}}(E)<\varepsilon
, and therefore

u
is an
M^{s,p}
quasicontinuous in
X
. Hence, the claim follows by applying Corollary
8.5
.
∎

A Symplectic Proof of the Quantum Singleton Bound
Frederick Dehmel, Shilun Li

Abstract
We present a symplectic linear algebraic proof of the Quantum Singleton Bound for stabiliser quantum error–correcting codes, together with a complete Lean4 formalisation of the linear–algebraic argument. The argument is formulated in terms of a finite dimensional symplectic vector space model of Pauli operators, distance based erasure correctability, and the cleaning lemma.



1
Motivation

The Quantum Singleton Bound is a basic constraint on the parameters of a stabiliser code, but
it is often proved using entropy inequalities and other information–theoretic tools. For
stabiliser codes, however, the underlying structure is symplectic linear algebra: Paulis form
a finite-dimensional symplectic space, commutation is bilinear, and erasure correctability is
captured by the inclusion
S^{\perp}\cap V_{E}\subseteq S
. This viewpoint yields a proof that is
conceptually aligned with the stabiliser formalism and isolates the algebraic ingredients
(distance
\Rightarrow
correctable erasures, plus the cleaning lemma).

A further benefit is mechanisation: the linear-algebraic argument is well-suited to formal
verification, and we have formalised it in
Lean4
. In contrast, entropy-based proofs
typically require substantially heavier analytic infrastructure, making them harder to
machine-check in a lightweight way.

2
Contributions

Concretely, we:

(i)

give a symplectic linear–algebraic proof of the Quantum Singleton Bound for

[[n,k,d]]
stabiliser codes, deriving
k+2(d-1)\leq n
from distance-based erasure
correctability and the cleaning lemma via a dimension argument;

(ii)

accompany the proof with a
Lean4
formalisation of the main linear–algebraic
development.

3
Introduction

The Quantum Singleton Bound is a fundamental constraint on the parameters of a quantum error–
correcting code. For an
[[n,k,d]]
stabiliser code it states

k+2(d-1)\leq n.

We prove this bound using the symplectic stabiliser formalism together with two key structural
inputs:

(i)

distance implies correctability of erasures of size at most
d-1
;

(ii)

the cleaning lemma and its dimension identity
g(M)+g(M^{c})=2k
.

4
Conventions and symplectic model

Remark 1

(Field convention)
.

Throughout,
\mathbb{F}
denotes a prime field
\mathbb{F}_{p}
. We work in the symplectic vector space

V:=\mathbb{F}^{n}\times\mathbb{F}^{n}\cong\mathbb{F}^{2n}

with elements written
v=(v_{X},v_{Z})
.

Definition 1

(Standard symplectic form)
.

For
u=(u_{X},u_{Z})
and
v=(v_{X},v_{Z})
in
V
, define

\langle u,v\rangle:=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\bigl(u_{X}(i)v_{Z}(i)-u_{Z}(i)v_{X}(i)\bigr)\in\mathbb{F}.

Lemma 1

(Nondegeneracy)
.

If
u\in V
satisfies
\langle u,v\rangle=0
for all
v\in V
, then
u=0
.

Proof.

Fix
i
. Pairing with
(0,e_{i})
forces
u_{X}(i)=0
, and pairing with
(e_{i},0)
forces

u_{Z}(i)=0
. Hence all coordinates vanish.
∎

5
Support, restriction, and distance

Let
[n]:=\{1,\dots,n\}
.

Definition 2

(Support and weight)
.

For
v\in V
, define

\mathrm{supp}(v):=\{i\in[n]:(v_{X}(i),v_{Z}(i))\neq(0,0)\},\qquad\mathrm{wt}(v):=|\mathrm{supp}(v)|.

Definition 3

(Support subspace)
.

For
C\subseteq[n]
, define

V_{C}:=\{v\in V:v_{X}(i)=v_{Z}(i)=0\text{ for all }i\notin C\}.

Lemma 2

(Dimension of a support subspace)
.

\dim(V_{C})=2|C|
.

Proof.

Restriction to
C
yields an isomorphism
V_{C}\cong\mathbb{F}^{|C|}\times\mathbb{F}^{|C|}
.
∎

Definition 4

(Restriction map)
.

For
E\subseteq[n]
, define
r_{E}:V\to V_{E}
by zeroing coordinates outside
E
.

Definition 5

(stabiliser subspace and distance)
.

A stabiliser code
Q
is specified by an isotropic subspace
S\subseteq V
with

\dim(S)=n-k
. Define the (Pauli) distance

d:=\min\{\mathrm{wt}(v):v\in S^{\perp}\setminus S,\ v\neq 0\}.

Lemma 3

(Logical space dimension)
.

If
\dim(S)=n-k
, then
\dim(S^{\perp}/S)=2k
.

Proof.

Nondegeneracy gives
\dim(S)+\dim(S^{\perp})=2n
, hence
\dim(S^{\perp})=n+k
and

\dim(S^{\perp}/S)=2k
.
∎

6
Erasure correctability

Definition 6

(Correctable erasure (commutant form))
.

A subset
E\subseteq[n]
is correctable if

S^{\perp}\cap V_{E}\subseteq S.

In the stabiliser setting this condition is equivalent to the standard Knill-Laflamme error-correction condition for erasures supported on
E

[
1
]
.

Lemma 4

(Distance implies erasure correctability)
.

If
|E|\leq d-1
, then
E
is correctable.

Proof.

If
v\in S^{\perp}\cap V_{E}
and
v\notin S
, then
v\in S^{\perp}\setminus S
with

\mathrm{wt}(v)\leq|E|\leq d-1
, contradicting the definition of
d
.
∎

7
Cleaning lemma and the dimension identity

Definition 7

(Supportable logical operators)
.

For
M\subseteq[n]
, define

g(M):=\dim\bigl((S^{\perp}\cap V_{M})/(S\cap V_{M})\bigr).

This counts the number of independent logical Pauli operators (modulo stabilisers)
that can be supported on
M
.

Lemma 5

(Cleaning dimension identity)
.

For any
M\subseteq[n]
,

g(M)+g(M^{c})=2k.

Proof.

The following argument is a linear–algebraic reformulation of the dimension-counting proof appearing in Preskill’s notes
[
2
]
.

Let
P\cong V
denote the abelianised Pauli group viewed as a
2n
–dimensional

\mathbb{F}
–vector space with the same symplectic form. Fix
M\subseteq[n]
and write

M^{c}
for its complement. Let
P_{M}:=V_{M}
and
P_{M^{c}}:=V_{M^{c}}
.

Define the stabiliser–restriction subspaces

S_{M}:=S\cap P_{M},\qquad S_{M^{c}}:=S\cap P_{M^{c}}.

Choose a complementary subspace
S_{0}
such that

S=S_{M}\oplus S_{M^{c}}\oplus S_{0}.

Consider the restriction map
r_{M}
to
M
. We claim that the restriction of

S_{M}\oplus S_{0}
to
M
is injective:

\dim\bigl(S_{M}\oplus r_{M}(S_{0})\bigr)=\dim(S_{M})+\dim(S_{0}).

Indeed, if a nontrivial linear combination of elements of
S_{M}\oplus S_{0}

restricts to zero on
M
, then it is supported on
M^{c}
, hence lies in

S\cap P_{M^{c}}=S_{M^{c}}
, which is impossible unless the combination was trivial.

Now compute
\dim(S^{\perp}\cap P_{M})
. Since
P_{M}
is orthogonal to
S_{M^{c}}

(disjoint support), the constraints on commuting with
S
inside
P_{M}

are exactly orthogonality to
r_{M}(S_{M}\oplus S_{0})=S_{M}\oplus r_{M}(S_{0})
. Therefore,

\dim(S^{\perp}\cap P_{M})=\dim(P_{M})-\dim(S_{M})-\dim(S_{0})=2|M|-\dim(S_{M})-\dim(S_{0}).

Similarly,

\dim(S^{\perp}\cap P_{M^{c}})=2|M^{c}|-\dim(S_{M^{c}})-\dim(S_{0}).

By definition,

g(M)=2|M|-2\dim(S_{M})-\dim(S_{0}),\qquad g(M^{c})=2|M^{c}|-2\dim(S_{M^{c}})-\dim(S_{0}).

Adding and using
|M|+|M^{c}|=n
and

\dim(S)=\dim(S_{M})+\dim(S_{M^{c}})+\dim(S_{0})=n-k

gives

g(M)+g(M^{c})=2k.

∎

Remark 2
.

It is worthwhile noting that the identity
g(M)+g(M^{c})=2k
does not imply that each logical Pauli operator
is supportable on exactly one of
M
or
M^{c}
.

Lemma 6

(Cleaning lemma (support on the complement))
.

If
M
is correctable, then every logical class
[L]\in S^{\perp}/S

has a representative supported on
M^{c}
.

Proof.

If
M
is correctable then
S^{\perp}\cap V_{M}\subseteq S
, hence
g(M)=0
.
By the dimension identity,
g(M^{c})=2k=\dim(S^{\perp}/S)
.
∎

8
A symplectic consequence of correctable erasure

Lemma 7

(Two disjoint correctable sets bound logical dimension)
.

Partition the physical qudits into
A\,B\,C
with
A\cap B=\varnothing
and

C=[n]\setminus(A\cup B)
. If erasures of both
A
and
B
are correctable, then

k\leq|C|.

Proof.

Set
D:=A^{c}=B\cup C
. Since
A
is correctable we have
g(A)=0
, hence by the
cleaning dimension identity

g(D)=g(A^{c})=2k.

By definition,

g(D)=\dim\Bigl((S^{\perp}\cap V_{D})/(S\cap V_{D})\Bigr),

so the quotient space

L:=(S^{\perp}\cap V_{D})/(S\cap V_{D})

has dimension
\dim(L)=2k
.

Consider the restriction map
r_{C}:V_{D}\to V_{C}
(zeroing coordinates outside
C
).
Let
W:=S\cap V_{D}
. Then
r_{C}(W)\leq V_{C}
is a subspace, and
r_{C}
induces a linear map

\bar{r}_{C}:L\longrightarrow V_{C}/r_{C}(W),\qquad[v]\longmapsto r_{C}(v)\ \bmod\ r_{C}(W).

This is well-defined because replacing
v
by
v+w
with
w\in W
changes
r_{C}(v)
by
an element of
r_{C}(W)
.

We claim
\bar{r}_{C}
is injective. Suppose
\bar{r}_{C}([v])=0
. Then
r_{C}(v)\in r_{C}(W)
,
so there exists
w\in W
with
r_{C}(v)=r_{C}(w)
. Hence
r_{C}(v-w)=0
, so
v-w
is supported
entirely on
B
, i.e.
v-w\in V_{B}
. Also
v-w\in S^{\perp}
(since
v\in S^{\perp}
and
w\in S
).
Thus
v-w\in S^{\perp}\cap V_{B}
, and because
B
is correctable we get
v-w\in S
.
Since
w\in S
as well, it follows that
v\in S
, hence
[v]=0
in
L
.

Therefore,

2k=\dim(L)\leq\dim\bigl(V_{C}/r_{C}(W)\bigr)\leq\dim(V_{C})=2|C|,

so
k\leq|C|
.
∎

9
Quantum Singleton bound

Theorem 1

(Quantum Singleton bound)
.

For any
[[n,k,d]]
stabiliser code,

k+2(d-1)\leq n.

Proof.

Choose disjoint
A,B\subseteq[n]
with
|A|=|B|=d-1
and let

C=[n]\setminus(A\cup B)
. By distance–correctability, erasures of
A

and
B
are correctable. Lemma
7
yields

k\leq|C|=n-2(d-1),

which is equivalent to the stated bound.
∎

Remark 3

(Existence of disjoint correctable sets)
.

In the proof above we choose disjoint subsets
A,B\subseteq[n]
with

|A|=|B|=d-1
. Such a choice is possible whenever
n\geq 2(d-1)
.

If instead
n<2(d-1)
, then no such disjoint subsets exist. In this regime the inequality

k\leq n-2(d-1)

has a non-positive right-hand side, and since
k\geq 0
for any stabiliser code, the bound is automatically satisfied. Hence the argument above covers all cases.

10
Conclusion

We derived the Quantum Singleton Bound from
(i) symplectic stabiliser linear algebra,
(ii) distance–based erasure correctability, and
(iii) a dimension–counting proof of the cleaning lemma following Preskill.
The final inequality follows from a purely symplectic dimension argument using
correctability of two disjoint erasures.

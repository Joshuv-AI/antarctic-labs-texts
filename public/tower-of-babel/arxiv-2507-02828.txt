Designs from magic-augmented Clifford circuits
Yuzhen Zhang, Sagar Vijay, Yingfei Gu, Yimu Bao

Abstract
We introduce magic-augmented Clifford circuits – architectures in which Clifford circuits are preceded and/or followed by constant-depth circuits of non-Clifford (“magic”) gates – as a resource-efficient way to realize approximate k k -designs, with reduced circuit depth and usage of magic. We prove that shallow Clifford circuits, when augmented with constant-depth circuits of magic gates, can generate approximate unitary and state k k -designs with ε \varepsilon relative error. The total circuit depth for these constructions on N N qubits is O ⁡ ( log ⁡ ( N / ε ) ) + 2 O ⁡ ( k ​ log ⁡ k ) O(\log(N/\epsilon))+2^{O(k\log k)} in one dimension and O ⁡ ( log ⁡ log ⁡ ( N / ε ) ) + 2 O ⁡ ( k ​ log ⁡ k ) O(\log\log(N/\epsilon))+2^{O(k\log k)} in all-to-all circuits using ancillas, which improves upon previous results for small k ≥ 4 k\geq 4 . Furthermore, our construction of relative-error state k k -designs only involves states with strictly local magic. The required number of magic gates is parametrically reduced when considering k k -designs with bounded additive error. As an example, we show that shallow Clifford circuits followed by O ⁡ ( k 2 ) O(k^{2}) single-qubit magic gates, independent of system size, can generate an additive-error state k k -design. We develop a classical statistical mechanics description of our random circuit architectures, which provides a quantitative understanding of the required depth and number of magic gates for additive-error state k k -designs. We also prove no-go theorems for various architectures to generate designs with bounded relative error.

A useful concept for characterizing the degree to which a circuit functions as a random unitary generator is the
approximate unitary
k
-design

[
13

14

An ensemble of unitaries, denoted by
\mathcal{E}_{u}
, is said to form an approximate
k

\operatorname{H}
by quantum experiments making at most
k
queries to the unitary.
More precisely, for the unitary ensemble, one can consider two types of error constraints controlling the distinguishability in two classes of quantum experiments.

•

Additive error. A unitary ensemble
\mathcal{E}_{u}
forms a unitary
k
-design with
\varepsilon
additive error if

\lVert\Phi^{(k)}_{\mathcal{E}_{u}}-\Phi^{(k)}_{\operatorname{H}}\rVert_{\Diamond}\leq\varepsilon,

(1)

where
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{\Diamond}
is the diamond norm
[
15
]

\Phi_{\mathcal{E}_{u}}^{(k)}
is the
k
-fold twirling channel defined as follows

\Phi_{\mathcal{E}_{u}}^{(k)}(\cdot):=\mathbb{E}_{U\sim\mathcal{E}_{u}}U^{\otimes k}(\cdot)U^{\dagger\otimes k}

(2)

and similarly for the Haar ensemble. A bounded additive error ensures that the ensemble is indistinguishable from the Haar ensemble in experiments that make
k

parallel
queries.

•

Relative error (or multiplicative error)
[
16
]
.
Relative error is a stronger notion than additive error.
A unitary ensemble
\mathcal{E}_{u}
forms a unitary
k
-design with
\varepsilon
relative error if

(1-\varepsilon)\Phi^{(k)}_{\operatorname{H}}\preceq\Phi^{(k)}_{\mathcal{E}_{u}}\preceq(1+\varepsilon)\Phi^{(k)}_{\operatorname{H}},

(3)

where
\Phi\preceq\Phi^{\prime}
means that
\Phi^{\prime}-\Phi
is a completely-positive map. A bounded relative error ensures that the expectation values of any positive operator in the output states of the two channels differ by at most a multiplicative factor
[
16
]
. Furthermore, it ensures indistinguishability in any quantum experiment involving up to
k
queries, including
adaptive
strategies
[
17
]
.

A closely related notion is that of an
approximate state
k
-design
, which characterizes how well an ensemble of pure quantum states approximates the statistical properties of Haar random states. Specifically, an ensemble
\mathcal{E}_{s}
of pure states is said to form an approximate state
k
-design if its
k
-th moment,

\rho_{\mathcal{E}_{s}}^{(k)}:=\mathbb{E}_{\psi\sim\mathcal{E}_{s}}|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|^{\otimes k},

(4)

approximates the corresponding moment of the Haar ensemble. Again, the approximation can be quantified either in terms of additive error or relative (multiplicative) error:

additive error

\displaystyle\qquad\lVert\rho^{(k)}_{\mathcal{E}_{s}}-\rho^{(k)}_{\operatorname{H}}\rVert_{1}\leq\varepsilon,

(5)

relative error

\displaystyle\qquad(1-\varepsilon)\rho^{(k)}_{\operatorname{H}}\preceq\rho^{(k)}_{\mathcal{E}_{s}}\preceq(1+\varepsilon)\rho^{(k)}_{\operatorname{H}},

(6)

where, for Hermitian operators,
\rho_{1}\preceq\rho_{2}
denotes that
\rho_{2}-\rho_{1}
is positive semidefinite.
Unlike the case of unitary designs—where only the relative error guarantees operational indistinguishability in adaptive experiments—the additive error for state designs already suffices to ensure indistinguishability even under adaptive protocols (see Appendix
A

Similar to the unitary case, a bounded relative error further ensures that any positive operator in the two states acquires expectation values differ by at most a multiplicative factor.
We further remark that approximate state

-designs and typically require fewer resources to construct. In particular, a state

1.2
Statistical mechanics picture

A portion of our results can be interpreted in the language of an effective classical statistical mechanics model that describes the ensemble of circuits that we consider. Similar mappings have been successfully used
[
3
]
to investigate a diverse set of quantum many-body phenomena, ranging from entanglement growth and the onset of quantum chaos
[
37
,
38
,
39
,
40
]
to the physics of random tensor networks
[
41
,
42
]
, and measurement-induced entanglement phases and transitions
[
43
,
44
,
45
,
46
,
47
,
48
]
.

For a quantum circuit composed of Haar-random gates, the average of the
k
-fold replicated circuit can be mapped onto the statistical mechanics of “spins” taking values in the permutation group
S_{k}
, which labels the linearly-independent set of operators that commute with the
k
-th tensor power of an arbitrary unitary operator.
For a quantum circuit composed of random Clifford gates, the structure of the effective statistical mechanics description is richer.
The average
k
-fold channel of a random Clifford gate maps onto a summation over stochastic Lagrangian subspaces
\Sigma_{k,k}
, which label operators in the Clifford commutant, (i.e. the set of operators that commute with the
k
-th tensor powers of Clifford unitaries, reviewed in Sec.
2.3
).
This results in a statistical mechanics model consisting of “spin” degrees of freedom, each of which labels a stochastic Lagrangian subspace.
The
S_{k}
permutation operators belong to the Clifford commutant and correspond to a subset of the stochastic Lagrangian subspaces
\Sigma_{k,k}
.

Section
6.1
shows that
approximate uniformity
— a key property satisfied by two-layer shallow Clifford circuits that underlies our shallow-circuit constructions of
k
-designs — is linked to a notion of
strong ordering
in the statistical mechanics description.
The strong ordering requires that any domain-wall excitation (neighboring spins representing non-identical Lagrangian subspaces) is parametrically suppressed, which is a stronger condition than simply being within the ordered phase of the statistical mechanics model.
We show that strong ordering can happen when each gate acts on
O(\log N)
number of qubits, corresponding to a sufficiently low temperature
O(1/\log N)
in the stat-mech model, which prevents small domain walls from proliferating.

We note that in one dimension, strong ordering emerges when the stat-mech model exhibits usual ordering, which necessarily requires that the domain walls flipping an extensive number of spins are suppressed.
In higher dimensions, although the stat-mech model can exhibit usual ordering when the gates in the two-layer circuit act on an
O(1)
number of qubits, strong ordering still requires each gate to act on
O(\log N)
qubits.
The analysis in Sec.
6.1
can be easily generalized to the case of two-layer Haar random circuits studied in Ref.
[
17
]
.
There, the strong ordering that emerges when each Haar random gate acts on
O(\log N)
qubits explains why such circuits form unitary designs with bounded relative error.

Section
6.2
provides a statistical mechanics understanding for why
O(k^{2})
magic-gate insertions are sufficient to convert shallow Clifford circuits to state
k
-designs with bounded additive error.
The key observation is that bounded additive error requires a strong ordering in the stat-mech model, in which the spins are aligned spatially to the subset of the stochastic Lagrangian subspaces that corresponds to the permutation group
S_{k}
. In the stat-mech model for the random Clifford circuit, inserting a single-qubit Haar random gate can be regarded as introducing a local “symmetry-breaking” field, which favors the stochastic Lagrangian subspaces that correspond to
S_{k}
.
Each Haar-random gate thus creates an additional
O(1)
energy cost for spins taking a non-permutation value. In the strongly ordered stat-mech model for Clifford circuits, there are a total of
\absolutevalue{\Sigma_{k,k}}-\absolutevalue{S_k}=2^{O(k^{2})}
spin configurations spatially aligned to a non-permutation value, corresponding to an entropy
O(k^{2})
.
It is therefore enough to insert
O(k^{2})
single-qubit Haar random gates – which create an energy cost of
O(k^{2})
for these non-permutation configurations within the stat.-mech. description – to suppress these states and achieve strong ordering within the permutation subspace.

Our stat-mech mapping strongly suggests that the
O(k^{2})
number of generic, non-Clifford gates inserted at arbitrary locations in the two-layer random Clifford circuit operating on logarithmic-sized qubit blocks is sufficient to generate an additive-error state
k
-design.
This heuristic picture further motivates the search for other unitary circuit architectures (e.g. with local, two-qubit Clifford gates and constant magic) which can realize additive-error designs.

The diamond norm

4

Operationally, the diamond norm bounds the additive distinguishability of two channels. For any input density matrix

\Phi_{1,2}
and

and

Definition 2

In our detailed proofs, we always work with an equivalent formulation of the semidefinite ordering

[\Phi_{1}\otimes\operatorname{id}](P_{\operatorname{EPR}})\preceq[\Phi_{2}\otimes\operatorname{id}](P_{\operatorname{EPR}}),

is the density matrix of EPR states between

and
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}

5
5

5

Here is a brief explanation. A completely positive channel

maps positive operators to positive operators. When acting on the positive operator

is positive. To prove the other direction, the positivity of

implies

being a positive operator

is a semi-positive map.
.

A

We remark that relative error is a stronger notion than additive error; an approximate

-design with bounded additive error

6

6

Here, we provide an explicit derivation. Assume

is within

\displaystyle\lVert\Phi_{\mathcal{E}}^{(k)}-\Phi_{\operatorname{H}}^{(k)}\rVert_{\Diamond}

\displaystyle=\max_{\lVert O\rVert_{1}\leq 1}\lVert(\Phi_{\mathcal{E}}-\Phi_{\operatorname{H}})\otimes\operatorname{id}(O)\rVert_{1}=2\max_{\lVert O\rVert_{1}\leq 1}\max_{0\leq M\leq 1}\Tr M[(\Phi_{\mathcal{E}}-\Phi_{\operatorname{H}})\otimes\operatorname{id}](O)

(13)

(Approximate state design with bounded additive error)

In this work, we also consider approximate stabilizer state

(Approximate state design with bounded relative error)

A sufficient condition that guarantees (

\lVert\rho_{\mathcal{E}}^{(k)}-\rho_{\operatorname{H}}^{(k)}\rVert_{\infty}\leq\binom{d+k-1}{k}^{-1}\varepsilon

Second, a state

\displaystyle=\max_{0\preceq M_{+},M_{-}\preceq 1}\Tr\left(M_{+}(\rho_{\mathcal{E}}^{(k)}-\rho_{\operatorname{H}}^{(k)})\right)-\Tr\left(M_{-}(\rho_{\mathcal{E}}^{(k)}-\rho_{\operatorname{H}}^{(k)})\right)

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon\max_{0\preceq M_{+}\preceq 1}\Tr\left(M_{+}\rho_{\operatorname{H}}^{(k)}\right)+\varepsilon\max_{0\preceq M_{-}\preceq 1}\Tr\left(M_{-}\rho_{\operatorname{H}}^{(k)}\right)=2\varepsilon.

(22)

Commutant of Clifford tensor powers

In this section, we review the theory of the commutant of Clifford tensor powers – the space of operators which commute with the
k

[
49

. This commutant naturally appears in averages over random Clifford unitaries.
We first explain the algebraic structure of the commutant of Clifford tensor powers and then provide formulae for averages over random Clifford unitaries and random stabilizer states, which will be used in this work.
We restrict our attention to the Clifford group over qubits.

Operators in the commutant of the
k
-th tensor power of the Clifford group are labeled by stochastic Lagrangian subspaces, a linear space defined below.

Definition 5

(Stochastic Lagrangian subspace)
.

A linear subspace
T\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{2k}
is a stochastic Lagrangian subspace iff

1.

\bm{x}\cdot\bm{x}-\bm{y}\cdot\bm{y}=0
mod
4
,
\forall(\bm{x},\bm{y})\in T
.

2.

\dim T=k
.

3.

\bm{1}_{2k}:=(\underbrace{1,\cdots,1}_{2k})\in T
.

We use
\Sigma_{k,k}
to denote the set of all stochastic Lagrangian subspaces
T\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{2k}
.
For each
T
, we have an associated operator in the commutant

\mathrm{r}(T)=\sum_{(\bm{x},\bm{y})\in T}\ket{\boldx}\bra{\boldy},\qquad\ket{\boldx}:=\bigotimes_{i=1}^{k}\ket{x_i}.

(23)

For a system
S
with
n
qubits,

r(T)_{S}:=\mathrm{r}(T)^{\otimes n}.

(24)

We omit the subscript when
S
represents the entire system.
In this work, we only consider the Clifford tensor powers on the subsystem with
n\geq k-1
such that the operators
r(T)
are linearly independent.
The number of such independent operators is
|\Sigma_{k,k}|=\prod_{i=0}^{k-2}(2^{i}+1)=2^{O(k^{2})}
.

The operator
\mathrm{r}(T)
in the commutant follows the decomposition
7
7

7

The decomposition of
\mathrm{r}(T)
stems from the structure of the Clifford commutant. The commutant can be written in terms of the double coset with respect to stochastic rotations, i.e.
\Sigma_{k,k}=O_{k}T_{\mathrm{N_{1}}}O_{k}\cup\cdots\cup O_{k}T_{\mathrm{N}_{K}}O_{k}
, where
T_{\mathrm{N_{1}}},\cdots T_{\mathrm{N_{K}}}
are stochastic Lagrangian subspace associated with a defect subspace, and
O_{k}
is the stochastic rotation group.
:

\mathrm{r}(T)=\mathrm{r}(T_{O,1})\mathrm{r}(T_{\mathrm{N}})\mathrm{r}(T_{O,2}).

(25)

The operator
\mathrm{r}(T_{O})
is a unitary operator, and the subspace
T_{O}=\{(\bm{x},O\bm{x}),\bm{x}\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{k}\}
, where
O
belongs to the stochastic rotation group defined below:

Definition 6

(Stochastic rotation)
.

A
k\times k
matrix
O
with
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-valued entries is a stochastic rotation iff

•

(O\bm{x})\cdot(O\bm{x})=\bm{x}\cdot\bm{x}
mod
4

\forall\bm{x}\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{k}
.

•

O\bm{1}_{k}=\bm{1}_{k}
.

The stochastic rotations form a group, denoted as
O_{k}
, which includes the permutation group
S_{k}
as a subgroup.
In the Clifford commutant, the operator associated with a stochastic rotation takes the form

\mathrm{r}(T_{O}):=\sum_{\bm{x}}\ket{O\boldx}\bra{\boldx}.

(26)

The operator
\mathrm{r}(T_{\mathrm{N}})
is associated with a defect subspace defined below.

Definition 7

(Defect subspace)
.

A linear subspace
\mathrm{N}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{k}

•

\bm{x}\cdot\bm{x}=0\mod 4
,
\forall\bm{x}\in\mathrm{N}

•

\bm{1}_{k}\in\mathrm{N}^{\perp}

.

Here,
\mathrm{N}^{\perp}

\mathrm{N}
as a subspace according to Def.
7
.
The stochastic Lagrangian subspace

.

The operator

is proportional to the projection operator

\lVert\mathrm{r}(T)\rVert_{1}=2^{k-\dim\mathrm{N}},\qquad\lVert\mathrm{r}(T)\rVert_{\infty}=2^{\dim\mathrm{N}}.

is a vector that labels the spatial configuration of
T

|\vec{T}_{1})(\vec{T}_{2}|=\bigotimes_{x}|T_{1,x})(T_{2,x}|

|T)(T|
is a superoperator associated with a spatially uniform configuration of

•

The deviation from uniformity is bounded by

\Delta(\Phi^{(k)}_{\mathcal{E}}):=\sum_{\vec{T}_{1},\vec{T_{2}}}|f(\vec{T}_{1},\vec{T}_{2})|\leq\varepsilon
.

Definition 9

(Approximate uniformity condition for stabilizer states)

Let
\mathcal{E}
be an ensemble of Clifford states on

qubits. The
k
-th moment of this state ensemble satisfies the
\varepsilon
-approximate uniformity condition if

•

The
k
-th moment can be written as

\rho^{(k)}_{\mathcal{E}}=2^{-Nk}\sum_{T}r(T)+2^{-Nk}\sum_{\vec{T}}f(\vec{T})r(\vec{T}),

(34)

, and

is an operator associated with a spatially uniform configuration of

•

The deviation from uniformity is bounded by

.

The approximate uniformity condition for the unitary ensemble

implies that the unitary ensemble can generate a state ensemble

with approximate uniformity.
Specifically, the

(35)

(36)

We remark that a state ensemble with approximate uniformity is a state
k
-design with bounded additive error.

Theorem 1
.

Consider a state ensemble satisfying the
\varepsilon
-approximate uniformity condition. The state ensemble forms a stabilizer state design with bounded additive error
\varepsilon+k2^{-N+k}
assuming
N\geq k+\log k-2
.

Proof.

The
k
-th moment of the state ensemble with approximate uniformity is given by

\displaystyle\rho^{(k)}_{\mathcal{E}}=2^{-Nk}\left[\sum_{T}r(T)+\sum_{\vec{T}}f(\vec{T})r(\vec{T})\right].

(37)

The additive error has an upper bound (assuming

\lVert\rho_{\mathcal{E}}^{(k)}-\rho_{\operatorname{C}}^{(k)}\rVert_{1}\leq|2^{-Nk}Z_{N,k}-1|\cdot\lVert\rho_{\operatorname{C}}^{(k)}\rVert_{1}+2^{-Nk}\sum_{\vec{T}}|f(\vec{T})|\cdot\lVert r(\vec{T})\rVert_{1}\leq k2^{-N+k}+\varepsilon,

(38)

where we use the triangle inequality, and the bound on the trace norm

∎

Although the approximate uniformity in general does not imply the ensemble to form designs under other approximation measures, it is a crucial property that allows one to convert the ensemble to an approximate design when augmented with magic gates (shown in Sec.

and
5
).
In what follows, we show that a Clifford ensemble with approximate uniformity can be generated in low-depth circuits.

The result stems from a gluing lemma; one can obtain a Clifford ensemble with the approximate uniformity condition by gluing small blocks of random Clifford unitaries together.

Using the gluing lemma above, we can construct a global Clifford ensemble with approximate uniformity using two layers of Clifford

Theorem 2

(Approximate uniformity from two-layer Clifford circuits)
.

Consider an ensemble
\mathcal{E}_{u}=\{V\}

-design.

\mathcal{E}_{u}

\varepsilon
-approximate uniformity condition with

, where
\xi

Proof.

for each application.
We need to apply the gluing lemma at most

\xi
is the size of the smallest overlapping regions in the two-layer circuit.
Thus, the deviation of the

from uniformity has an upper bound

.

\mathcal{E}_{s}=\{V\ket{0}^{\otimes N}\}

\varepsilon=N2^{-\xi-\log\xi+O(k^{2})}
.
∎

According to Theorem

Namely, one can realize it in shallow 1D Clifford circuits of depth

Designs with bounded relative error

The constructed state ensemble consists of states with strictly local magic

,

,

,

Theorem 3

(Relative-error state designs from low-depth Clifford unitaries followed by constant depth magic gates)
.

Consider a state ensemble
\mathcal{E}=\{UV\ket{0}^{\otimes N}\}
generated by the circuit architecture below

is a product of random unitaries drawn from exact unitary
k
-designs, each gate

acts on a disjoint subsystem

of size

within

consists of multi-qubit Clifford unitaries drawn independently from exact Clifford

-designs.

The ensemble
\mathcal{E}
forms a state
k
-design with relative error

\displaystyle\varepsilon=N2^{-\xi-\log\xi+O(k^{2})}+2^{O(k^{2})-N}\left[k!(1+k^{2}2^{-\ell})\right]^{N/\ell},

(48)

where
\xi
is the size of the smallest overlapping regions of the Clifford unitaries, and

is the size of the smallest subsystem that

can form a state

Proof.

To begin, the
k
-th moment of the state ensemble generated by a two-layer Clifford unitary can be expressed as

\displaystyle\rho_{\mathcal{E}_{0}}^{(k)}=2^{-Nk}\left[\sum_{T}r(T)+\sum_{\vec{T}}f(\vec{T})r(\vec{T})\right],

(49)

where
\vec{T}
is a vector of
T_{x}
, each of which emerged from averaging the
k
-fold channel associated with a Clifford gate in the second layer at location
x
.
The operator

, where

labels the qubit block that the Clifford gate at location
x
acts on.
It satisfies the
\varepsilon^{\prime}
-approximate uniformity condition with
\varepsilon^{\prime}=N2^{\xi-\log\xi+O(k^{2})}
due to Theorem
2
.

Next, we apply the
k
-design random unitary gates over subsystems
M_{x,i}
of size
N_{x,i}
within each qubit block
M_{x}
.
Recall that the
k
-fold channel of each Haar random unitary is given by

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{U\sim\operatorname{H}}U^{\otimes k}(\cdot)U^{\dagger\otimes k}=\sum_{\sigma,\tau\in S_{k}}\operatorname{Wg}(\sigma,\tau)r(\sigma)\Tr[r(\tau)^{\top}(\cdot)],

(50)

where each
\sigma
and
\tau
belongs to the permutation group
S_{k}
, corresponding to a subset of the Clifford commutant.
The resulting
k

\displaystyle\rho_{\mathcal{E}}^{(k)}=2^{-Nk}\sum_{\vec{\sigma},\vec{\tau}}\left[\sum_{T}r(\vec{\sigma})\operatorname{Wg}(\vec{\sigma},\vec{\tau})\mbox{$\langle\!\langle\vec{\tau}|T\rangle\!\rangle$}+\sum_{\vec{T}}f(\vec{T})r(\vec{\sigma})\operatorname{Wg}(\vec{\sigma},\vec{\tau})\mbox{$\langle\!\langle\vec{\tau}|\vec{T}\rangle\!\rangle$}\right]

(51)

where
\vec{\sigma}
(
\vec{\tau}
) is a vector labeling configurations of permutations
\sigma_{x,i}
,
\tau_{x,i}\in S_{k}
at location
x,i
, and
\operatorname{Wg}(\vec{\sigma},\vec{\tau}):=\prod_{x,i}\operatorname{Wg}(\sigma_{x,i},\tau_{x,i})
,
\mbox{$\langle\!\langle\vec{\tau}|T\rangle\!\rangle$}:=\prod_{x,i}\mbox{$\langle\!\langle\tau_{x,i}|T\rangle\!\rangle$}
,
\mbox{$\langle\!\langle\vec{\tau}|\vec{T}\rangle\!\rangle$}:=\prod_{x,i}\mbox{$\langle\!\langle\tau_{x,i}|T_{x}\rangle\!\rangle$}
.

To bound the relative error between
\rho_{\mathcal{E}}^{(k)}
and
\rho_{\operatorname{H}}^{(k)}

\rho_{\mathcal{E}}^{(k)}
into three parts:

•

The first part is given by the first term in Eq. (

. The summation over
\vec{\tau}
results in a delta function, i.e.
\sum_{\tau_{x,i}}\operatorname{Wg}(\sigma_{x,i},\tau_{x,i})\mbox{$\langle\!\langle\tau_{x,i}|\pi\rangle\!\rangle$}=\delta_{\sigma_{x,i},\pi}
, which identifies each
\sigma_{x,i}
with
\pi
. This part of the contribution is

\displaystyle\rho_{\mathrm{I}}^{(k)}=2^{-Nk}\sum_{\pi\in S_{k}}r(\pi).

(52)

•

The second part is given by the first term in Eq. (
51
), taking

\displaystyle\rho_{\mathrm{II}}^{(k)}=2^{-Nk}\sum_{\vec{\sigma},\vec{\tau}}\sum_{T\notin S_{k}}r(\vec{\sigma})\operatorname{Wg}(\vec{\sigma},\vec{\tau})\mbox{$\langle\!\langle\vec{\tau}|T\rangle\!\rangle$},

(53)

which has a bounded
\infty
-norm

\displaystyle\lVert\rho_{\mathrm{II}}^{(k)}\rVert_{\infty}\leq

\displaystyle 2^{-Nk}\sum_{\vec{\sigma},\vec{\tau}}\sum_{T\notin S_{k}}\lVert r(\vec{\sigma})\rVert_{\infty}\absolutevalue{\Wg(\vec{\sigma},\vec{\tau})}\mbox{$\langle\!\langle\vec{\tau}|T\rangle\!\rangle$}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2^{-Nk}2^{O(k^{2})-N}\prod_{x,i}k!(1+k^{2}2^{-N_{x,i}})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2^{-Nk}2^{O(k^{2})-N}\left[k!(1+k^{2}2^{-\ell})\right]^{N/\ell}.

(54)

In the second inequality, we use
\lVert r(\vec{\sigma})\rVert_{\infty}=1
,
\mbox{$\langle\!\langle\vec{\tau}|T\rangle\!\rangle$}\leq 2^{-N}
, and
\sum_{\sigma_{x,i},\tau_{x,i}}|\operatorname{Wg}(\sigma_{x,i},\tau_{x,i})|\leq k!(1+k^{2}2^{-N_{x,i}})
for

[
53
]
.
In the third inequality, we take

to be the size of the smallest qubit cluster.

•

The third part contains the remaining terms in the summation

\displaystyle\rho_{\mathrm{III}}^{(k)}=2^{-Nk}\sum_{\vec{\sigma},\vec{\tau}}\sum_{\vec{T}}f(\vec{T})r(\vec{\sigma})\operatorname{Wg}(\vec{\sigma},\vec{\tau})\mbox{$\langle\!\langle\vec{\tau}|\vec{T}\rangle\!\rangle$}.

(55)

Its
\infty
-norm has an upper bound

\displaystyle\lVert\rho_{\mathrm{III}}^{(k)}\rVert_{\infty}\leq 2^{-Nk}\sum_{\vec{T}}\absolutevalue{f(\vec{T})}\prod_{x,i}\bigg\lVert\sum_{\sigma_{x,i},\tau_{x,i}}r(\sigma_{x,i})\operatorname{Wg}(\sigma_{x,i},\tau_{x,i})\mbox{$\langle\!\langle\tau_{x,i}|T_{x}\rangle\!\rangle$}\bigg\rVert_{\infty}.

(56)

Here, we consider the summation over each
\sigma_{x,i}
and
\tau_{x,i}
. We consider two cases for
T_{x}\in S_{k}
and
T_{x}\notin S_{k}

For

\displaystyle\bigg\lVert\sum_{\sigma_{x,i},\tau_{x,i}}r(\sigma_{x,i})\operatorname{Wg}(\sigma_{x,i},\tau_{x,i})\mbox{$\langle\!\langle\tau_{x,i}|\pi\rangle\!\rangle$}\bigg\rVert_{\infty}=\left\lVert r(\pi)\right\rVert_{\infty}=1

(57)

For
T_{x}\notin S_{k}
,

\displaystyle\bigg\lVert\sum_{\sigma_{x,i},\tau_{x,i}}r(\sigma_{x,i})\operatorname{Wg}(\sigma_{x,i},\tau_{x,i})\mbox{$\langle\!\langle\tau_{x,i}|T_{x}\rangle\!\rangle$}\bigg\rVert_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\sigma_{x,i},\tau_{x,i}}\absolutevalue{\Wg(\sigma_{x,i},\tau_{x,i})}\mbox{$\langle\!\langle\tau_{x,i}|T_{x}\rangle\!\rangle$}

\displaystyle\leq\prod_{i}2^{-N_{x,i}}k!(1+k^{2}2^{-N_{x,i}})\leq 1,

(58)

where we use

and assume that

.
Hence, we have

\displaystyle\lVert\rho_{\mathrm{III}}^{(k)}\rVert_{\infty}\leq

\displaystyle 2^{-Nk}\sum_{\vec{T}}\absolutevalue{f(\vec{T})}\leq 2^{-Nk}\varepsilon^{\prime}.

(59)

Combining these results, we obtain an upper bound on the relative error

\displaystyle\varepsilon\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\lVert\rho_{\mathcal{E}}^{(k)}-\rho_{\operatorname{H}}^{(k)}\rVert_{\infty}}{\lVert\rho_{\operatorname{H}}^{(k)}\rVert_{\infty}}\leq\frac{\lVert\rho_{\mathrm{I}}^{(k)}-\rho_{\operatorname{H}}^{(k)}\rVert_{\infty}+\lVert\rho_{\mathrm{II}}^{(k)}\rVert_{\infty}+\lVert\rho_{\mathrm{III}}^{(k)}\rVert_{\infty}}{\lVert\rho_{\operatorname{H}}^{(k)}\rVert_{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle k^{2}2^{-Nk}+2^{O(k^{2})-N}[k!(1+k^{2}2^{-\ell})]^{N/\ell}+(1+k^{2}2^{-Nk})\varepsilon^{\prime}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2^{O(k^{2})-N}[k!(1+k^{2}2^{-\ell})]^{N/\ell}+N2^{-\xi-\log\xi+O(k^{2})}.

(60)

∎

We remark that the magic gates does not need to act on every qubit cluster in the final layer.
In fact, one can remove a finite fraction of Haar random gates within each qubit block
M_{x}
, while maintaining a bounded relative error.
Consider a qubit block of size
\xi
that a Clifford gate acts on.
Suppose no magic gates act on a subregion
C
of size
N_{C}
inside the qubit block.
We can follow a similar analysis of the relative error except that in Eq. (
54
) and (
56
), the summation involves terms with the non-permutation
r(T)_{C}
in region
C
, which can have a greater spectral norm
\lVert r(T)_{C}\rVert_{\infty}=2^{N_{C}\dim\mathrm{N}}
than those of permutations.
Using the upper bound of its spectral norm
\lVert r(T)_{C}\rVert_{\infty}\leq 2^{N_{C}k/2}
, we obtain a modified multiplicative factor, e.g. in Eq. (
54
),

2^{-\xi}\prod_{i}k!(1+k^{2}2^{-\ell})\quad\mapsto\quad 2^{-(\xi-N_{C})+N_{C}k/2}\prod_{i}k!(1+k^{2}2^{-\ell}).

(61)

This factor is still bounded for
N_{C}=\frac{1}{k}\xi
and
\ell=O(k\log k)
, indicating the possibility to remove at least
1/k
fraction of Haar random gates.

In the case with no magic gates in the subregion
C
, it is tempting to distinguish this circuit from Haar random states using magic measures.
To begin, one can consider magic measures on the subregion
C
.
This cannot work because Page’s theorem implies that in the thermodynamic limit
N\to\infty
, the state on
C
is exponentially close to the maximally mixed state with no magic, assuming that the size of
C
is less than half of the system.
Alternatively, one might try to measure the magic in the entire system.
Since our circuits form a relative error design, the expectation value of any positive operator is relatively close to the Haar random value.
This implies that, for example, measuring the operator (used in Sec.
4.3
) whose logarithm defines the stabilizer Rényi entropy will lead to a result relatively close to the Haar value.
Naively, one may expect the magic in the entire system to deviate from the maximal value by an extensive amount.
However, magic gates on a subregion are capable of generating a near-maximum stabilizer Rényi entropy when acting on a highly entangled state as shown in Ref.
[
54
]
.

4.2

4.3
No-go theorems

Here, we prove no-go theorems for designs with bounded relative error.
We first consider an ensemble of Clifford augmented states generated by applying Clifford unitaries to initial states with low entanglement.
A subclass of these states are the Clifford augmented matrix product states (CAMPS)
[
55
,
56
]
.
CAMPSs can be efficiently simulated and have the potential to host large-scale entanglement and an extensive amount of magic.
However, we show that an ensemble of CAMPSs with bond dimension
D=\tilde{o}(\sqrt{N/\varepsilon})
cannot form a state
k
-design with
\varepsilon
relative error for
k\geq 4
.
We further show that in any dimension, finite-depth local unitaries followed by a Clifford unitary cannot generate a state design with bounded relative error.
The no-go theorem also suggests that a unitary ensemble
\{V_{1}UV_{2}\}
with
V_{1,2}
being Clifford unitaries and
U
being a generic shallow unitary circuit cannot form a relative-error unitary
k
-design.
The no-go theorems challenge the naive expectation that magic placed at the initial layer creates more randomness as the Clifford unitary can spread and scramble magic.

The main idea behind the no-go theorem is that the ensemble of Clifford augmented states is distinguished from the Haar random ensemble by measuring the Pauli
4
-th moment, whose logarithm defines the stabilizer Rényi entropy
[
57
]
.
The
4
-th moment is given by the expectation value of a projection operator onto a CSS code defined in the replicated space (see Appendix
F
), which are positive operators that belong to the Clifford commutant.
The relative error of the
4
-th moment sets a lower bound on the relative error of the state
4
-design.
We note that although such states cannot form relative-error state designs, they can form additive-error state designs, as we show in Appendix
B
.

We consider an ensemble
\mathcal{E}=\{V\ket{\psi_0}\}
of Clifford augmented states generated by Clifford unitaries
V
acting on short-range entangled states or one-dimensional matrix product states
\ket{\psi_0}
.
We show that the ensemble is distinguished from the ensemble of Haar random states by a positive observable:

r(T_{S})=2^{-N}\sum_{P\in\mathcal{P}}P^{\otimes 4},\qquad\mathcal{P}=\{Z^{\bm{u}}X^{\bm{v}},\bm{u},\bm{v}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{N}\}

(70)

The expectation value of this operator is related to the second stabilizer Rényi entropy
\mathcal{M}_{2}(\psi)
of state
\ket{\psi}=V\ket{\psi_0}
,

\displaystyle\Tr[r(T_{S})\rho_{\mathcal{E}}^{(4)}]=\mathbb{E}_{\psi\sim\mathcal{E}}\frac{1}{2^{N}}\sum_{P}\bra{\psi}P\ket{\psi}^{4}=\mathbb{E}_{\psi\sim\mathcal{E}}2^{-\mathcal{M}_{2}(\psi)}.

(71)

Note that the operator
r(T_{S})
belongs to the Clifford commutant; its expectation value is invariant under Clifford unitaries:

\bra{\psi_0}^{\otimes 4}V^{\otimes 4}r(T_{S})V^{\dagger\otimes 4}\ket{\psi_0}^{\otimes 4}=\bra{\psi_0}^{\otimes 4}r(T_{S})\ket{\psi_0}^{\otimes 4}.

(72)

Theorem 5

(Lower bound on relative error from non-maximal initial entanglement)
.

Consider disjoint subsystems
M_{i}
of size
N_{i}
in an
N
-qubit system, where the union
\cup_{i}M_{i}
need not contain all the qubits.

The state ensemble
\mathcal{E}=\{V\ket{\psi_0}\}
generated by Clifford unitary
V

\ket{\psi_0}
cannot form an approximate state
k
-design (for
k\geq 4
) with relative error

\varepsilon\leq\frac{1}{24}\left[\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{E}}\sum_{i}2^{-2\mathcal{S}^{(2)}_{i}}(1-2^{\mathcal{S}^{(2)}_{i}-N_{i}})-3\right],

(73)

where
\mathcal{S}^{(2)}_{i}=-\log\Tr(\rho_i^2)
is the second Rényi entropy evaluated on subsystem
M_{i}
of the initial state
\ket{\psi_0}
, and
\rho_{i}
is the reduced density matrix of
\ket{\psi_0}
on

.

Proof.

For each member of the state ensemble, we can lower bound the expectation value of

by the following procedure. On a single region

, the summation over all the Pauli operators that support on
M_{i}
gives

\delta:=\sum_{P_{i}}|\bra{\psi_0}P_{i}\ket{\psi_0}|^{2}=2^{N_{i}}\Tr(\rho_{M_i}^2)=2^{N_{i}-\mathcal{S}_{i}^{(2)}}.

(74)

We can view
\{\chi_{P_{i}}=\delta^{-1}|\bra{\psi_0}P_{i}\ket{\psi_0}|^{2}\}
as a probability distribution. Since
\chi_{I}=\delta^{-1}
, the contributions from non-identity Pauli operators are lower bounded by

2^{-N}\sum_{P_{i}\neq I}|\bra{\psi_0}P_{i}\ket{\psi_0}|^{4}=2^{-N}\delta^{2}\sum_{P_{i}\neq I}|\chi_{P_{i}}|^{2}\geq 2^{-N}\delta^{2}\frac{1-\delta^{-1}}{2^{2N_{i}}-1}\geq 2^{-N}2^{-2\mathcal{S}^{(2)}_{i}}(1-2^{\mathcal{S}^{(2)}_{i}-N_{i}}).

(75)

Summing over all regions

, we get

\bra{\psi_0}^{\otimes 4}r(T_{S})\ket{\psi_0}^{\otimes 4}\geq 2^{-N}\left[1+\sum_{i}2^{-2\mathcal{S}^{(2)}_{i}}(1-2^{\mathcal{S}^{(2)}_{i}-N_{i}})\right].

(76)

Averaging over the state in the ensemble
\mathcal{E}
, we have

\Tr[r(T_{S})\rho_{\mathcal{E}}^{(4)}]\geq 2^{-N}\left[1+\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{E}}\sum_{i}2^{-2\mathcal{S}^{(2)}_{i}}(1-2^{\mathcal{S}^{(2)}_{i}-N_{i}})\right].

(77)

The Haar value is

\Tr[r(T_{S})\rho_{\operatorname{H}}^{(4)}]=2^{-N}D_{N,4}^{-1}\sum_{\pi\in S_{4},P\in\mathcal{P}}\Tr[P^{\otimes 4}r(\pi)]=\frac{4}{2^{N}+3},

(78)

see
[
57
]
. The difference in the expectation value can give a lower bound to the relative error

\lVert\rho_{\mathcal{E}}^{(4)}-\rho_{\operatorname{H}}^{(4)}\rVert_{\infty}\geq\frac{\left|\Tr[r(T_{S})(\rho_{\mathcal{E}}^{(4)}-\rho_{\operatorname{H}}^{(4)})]\right|}{\lVert r(T_{S})\rVert_{1}}\geq 2^{-4N}\left[\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{E}}\sum_{i}2^{-2\mathcal{S}^{(2)}_{i}}(1-2^{\mathcal{S}^{(2)}_{i}-N_{i}})-3\right],

(79)

where we use the Hölder’s inequality, and
\lVert r(T_{S})\rVert_{1}=2^{3N}
(defect subspace dimension

, and

).
The relative error is bounded by

\frac{\lVert\rho_{\mathcal{E}}^{(4)}-\rho_{\operatorname{H}}^{(4)}\rVert_{\infty}}{\lVert\rho_{\operatorname{H}}^{(4)}\rVert_{\infty}}\geq\frac{1}{24}\left[\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{E}}\sum_{i}2^{-2\mathcal{S}^{(2)}_{i}}(1-2^{\mathcal{S}^{(2)}_{i}-N_{i}})-3\right].

(80)

If
\mathcal{E}
is an approximate state

\varepsilon

(1-\varepsilon)\rho_{\operatorname{H}}^{(k)}\preceq\rho_{\mathcal{E}}^{(k)}\preceq(1+\varepsilon)\rho_{\operatorname{H}}^{(k)}
. Taking the partial trace over

. In other words,
\mathcal{E}
is an approximate state

-design with
\varepsilon

has a flat spectrum, the above implies

\lVert\rho_{\mathcal{E}}^{(4)}-\rho_{\operatorname{H}}^{(4)}\rVert_{\infty}\leq\varepsilon\lVert\rho_{\operatorname{H}}^{(4)}\rVert_{\infty}.

(81)

Therefore an ensemble cannot form an approximate state

∎

The above proof can be understood as a classical statistical mechanics problem, where the lack of correlations proliferates domain wall excitations. We interpret
\left|\bra{\psi}\bigotimes_{i}P_{i}\ket{\psi}\right|^{4}\equiv W(\{P_{i}\})
as the Boltzmann weight of a spin model, where we put a spin on every region
M_{i}
that are labeled by the Pauli operators on
M_{i}
. We ask whether the partition function
Z=\sum_{\{P_{i}\}}W(\{P_{i}\})
is relatively close to the Haar value that is
O(1)
.

The identity operator
\prod_{i}I_{i}
gives the maximal weight
W(\{I_{i}\})=1
. On Haar random states, the weights for any spin configuration with non-identity inputs are heavily suppressed, that is to say, if
\{P_{i}\}\neq\{I_{i}\}
, then

\mathbb{E}_{\psi\sim\operatorname{H}}W(\{P_{i}\})=D_{N,2}^{-1}\sum_{\pi\in S_{4}}\Tr[r(\pi)\bigotimes_{i}P_{i}^{\otimes 4}]=3\times 2^{-N}[1+O(2^{-N})].

(82)

However, in states that are less entangled, there might be domain wall excitations that corresponds to
\{P_{i}\}
with
I
on all but one site. For every site
M_{i}
, such domain wall gives a contribution of at least
2^{-2\mathcal{S}^{(2)}_{i}}(1-2^{\mathcal{S}^{(2)}_{i}-N_{i}})
, which could be an
O(1)
number if
N_{i}=O(1)
and
N_{i}-\mathcal{S}^{(2)}_{i}=O(1)
. The Boltzmann weights are also
O(1)
, in contrast with the Haar case (
82
). Since there is an
O(N)
number of such regions, these configurations gives an overall
O(N)
contribution to the partition function. In stat mech language, the energy cost of these domain walls cannot suppress the large entropy, so they will proliferate.

As the first application of the relative error lower bound, we prove a no-go theorem for ensembles of Clifford augmented matrix product states.

(No-go theorem for Clifford augmented MPS)

(90)

where we assume the system is of size

5

In this section, we construct state and unitary designs with bounded additive errors using Clifford circuits augmented with magic gates.

5.1
State design with bounded additive errors

We prove that two layers of Clifford gates followed by
O(k^{2}+\log(1/\epsilon))
single-qubit
k
-design random unitaries can generate state
k
-design with bounded additive error.
We also prove in Appendix
B
that one can alternatively place
k
-design random unitaries before the Clifford gates.
In this case, one needs to apply
k
-design unitaries over
O(k^{2}+\log(1/\epsilon))
qubits, while each unitary acts on disjoint qubit clusters of size
O(k\log k)
.
A physical understanding of these results is provided in Sec.

by mapping the frame potential to statistical mechanics models, where magic plays the role of a symmetry breaking field.

To obtain state
k
-designs with magic gates at the final layer, the first step is to prove that one can get additive-error
k
-designs on
n

N_{M}=O(k^{2}+\log(1/\epsilon))

The additive error for state designs has an upper bound in terms of the frame potential

\lVert\rho_{\mathcal{E}}^{(k)}-\rho_{\operatorname{H}}^{(k)}\rVert_{1}^{2}\leq 2^{Nk}\big[F_{\mathcal{E}}^{(k)}-F_{\operatorname{H}}^{(k)}\big].

(92)

Hence, we prove a bounded additive error by providing an upper bound on the frame potential difference.

Lemma 3

(Additive error state design from applying constant magic to random stabilizer states)
.

Consider an
N
-qubit state
\ket{\psi}
drawn from an approximate stabilizer state
k
-design with
\varepsilon^{\prime}
additive error.
Apply
N_{M}
single-qubit unitaries,
U_{1},\cdots,U_{N_{M}}
, independently drawn from (exact) unitary
k
-designs to
N_{M}
distinct qubits.
Assume
N\geq k+\log k-2
.

Then, the state ensemble
\mathcal{E}=\{U_{1}\cdots U_{N_{M}}\ket{\psi}\}
is an approximate state
k
-design with
\varepsilon
additive error, where
\varepsilon\leq 2^{O(k^{2})}\left(\frac{7}{8}\right)^{\frac{N_{M}}{4}}+\varepsilon^{\prime}
.

Proof.

First, the additive error of the
k
-th moment of the circuit ensemble can be upper bounded by the triangular inequality

\displaystyle\lVert\Phi^{(k)}_{\operatorname{H}}\big(\rho_{\operatorname{C},\varepsilon^{\prime}}^{(k)}\big)-\rho_{\operatorname{H}}^{(k)}\rVert_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\lVert\Phi^{(k)}_{\operatorname{H}}\big(\rho_{\operatorname{C}}^{(k)}\big)-\rho_{\operatorname{H}}^{(k)}\rVert_{1}+\lVert\Phi^{(k)}_{\operatorname{H}}\big(\rho_{\operatorname{C},\varepsilon^{\prime}}^{(k)}\big)-\Phi^{(k)}_{\operatorname{H}}\big(\rho_{\operatorname{C}}^{(k)}\big)\rVert_{1}

(93)

\displaystyle\leq\lVert\Phi^{(k)}_{\operatorname{H}}\big(\rho_{\operatorname{C}}^{(k)}\big)-\rho_{\operatorname{H}}^{(k)}\rVert_{1}+\varepsilon^{\prime},

where
\rho_{\operatorname{C},\varepsilon^{\prime}}^{(k)}
and
\rho_{\operatorname{C}}^{(k)}
are the
k
-th moments of the stabilizer state
k
-design with additive error
\varepsilon^{\prime}
and an exact stabilize state
k
-design, respectively.
On the second line, we use the monotonicity of 1-norm under quantum channels,

\displaystyle\lVert\Phi^{(k)}_{\operatorname{H}}\big(\rho_{\operatorname{C}}^{(k)}\big)-\Phi^{(k)}_{\operatorname{H}}\big(\rho_{\operatorname{C},0}^{(k)}\big)\rVert_{1}\leq\lVert\rho_{\operatorname{C}}^{(k)}-\rho_{\operatorname{C},0}^{(k)}\rVert_{1}\leq\varepsilon^{\prime}.

(94)

Next, we bound the distance between
\Phi^{(k)}_{\operatorname{H}}\big(\rho_{\operatorname{C}}^{(k)}\big)
and
\rho_{\operatorname{H}}^{(k)}
using the
k
-th frame potential.
Given that
F_{\mathcal{E}}^{(k)}\geq F_{\operatorname{H}}^{(k)}
, we here provide an upper bound for

.
Specifically,

F_{\mathcal{E}}^{(k)}=\Tr[\Phi^{(k)}_{\operatorname{H}}\big(\rho_{\operatorname{C}}^{(k)}\big)\big]^{2}=\Tr[\rho_{\operatorname{C}}^{(k)}\Phi^{(k)}_{\operatorname{H}}\big(\rho_{\operatorname{C}}^{(k)}\big)\big]=\frac{1}{Z_{n,k}^{2}}\sum_{T_{1},T_{2}}\mbox{$\langle\!\langle T_{1}|T_{2}\rangle\!\rangle$}^{N-M}\mbox{$\langle\!\langle T_{1}|$}\hat{\Phi}_{\operatorname{H}}^{(k)}\mbox{$|T_{2}\rangle\!\rangle$}^{M}.

(95)

where
\hat{\Phi}_{\operatorname{H}}^{(k)}=\mathbb{E}_{U\sim\operatorname{H}}U^{\otimes k}\otimes U^{*\otimes k}
, and
|T_{1}\rangle\!\rangle
and
|T_{2}\rangle\!\rangle
are over single qubits.

A key step of our proof uses Lemma 13 of
[
25
]
. For
T\notin S_{k}
,

\mbox{$\langle\!\langle T|$}\hat{\Phi}_{\operatorname{H}}^{(k)}\mbox{$|T\rangle\!\rangle$}\leq\frac{7}{8}2^{k}.

(96)

When

is not a permutation, the Cauchy-Schwartz inequality indicates that

|\mbox{$\langle\!\langle T_{1}|$}\hat{\Phi}_{\operatorname{H}}^{(k)}\mbox{$|T_{2}\rangle\!\rangle$}|\leq\sqrt{\mbox{$\langle\!\langle T_{1}|$}\hat{\Phi}_{\operatorname{H}}^{(k)}\mbox{$|T_{1}\rangle\!\rangle$}\mbox{$\langle\!\langle T_{2}|T_{2}\rangle\!\rangle$}}\leq\sqrt{\frac{7}{8}}2^{k},

(97)

while for permutations
\pi\in S_{k}
,
\mbox{$\langle\!\langle\pi|$}\hat{\Phi}_{\operatorname{H}}^{(k)}\mbox{$|\pi\rangle\!\rangle$}=2^{k}
.
This indicates that inserting a single-qubit random unitary suppresses the weight

when either

or
T_{2}
is not a permutation.

We divide the frame potential in Eq. (

The contributions from

F_{\mathrm{I}}^{(k)}:=\frac{1}{Z_{N,k}^{2}}\sum_{\sigma,\tau\in S_{k}}\mbox{$\langle\!\langle\sigma|\tau\rangle\!\rangle$}^{N-N_{M}}\mbox{$\langle\!\langle\sigma|$}\hat{\Phi}_{\operatorname{H}}^{(k)}\mbox{$|\tau\rangle\!\rangle$}^{N_{M}}=\frac{1}{Z_{N,k}^{2}}\sum_{\sigma,\tau\in S_{k}}\mbox{$\langle\!\langle\sigma|\tau\rangle\!\rangle$}^{n}=\frac{k!D_{N,k}}{Z_{N,k}^{2}},

(98)

where

.
The deviation of

from the frame potential

of the Haar random state is bounded by

\left|\frac{k!D_{N,k}}{Z_{N,k}^{2}}-\frac{k!}{2^{Nk}}\right|\leq 2^{O(k^{2})-N}.

(99)

The contribution from either

or

(100)

Putting these together

, we have

\lVert\Phi^{(k)}_{\operatorname{H}}\big(\rho_{\operatorname{C}}^{(k)}\big)-\rho_{\operatorname{H}}^{(k)}\rVert_{1}^{2}\leq 2^{Nk}\big[F_{\mathcal{E}}^{(k)}-F_{\operatorname{H}}^{(k)}\big]\leq 2^{O(k^{2})-N}+2^{O(k^{2})}\left(\frac{7}{8}\right)^{N_{M}/2}\leq 2^{O(k^{2})}\left(\frac{7}{8}\right)^{N_{M}/2}.

(101)

Inserting this result in Eq. (

\varepsilon
.
∎

(Additive-error unitary designs from circuits with magic gates over constant-number of qubits)

Let

\displaystyle:=\{(T_{1},T_{2})|\,T_{1}=T_{2}\in S_{k}\}

\displaystyle\mathrm{III}

\displaystyle\lVert\Phi_{\mathrm{I}}^{(k)}-\Phi_{0}^{(k)}\rVert_{\Diamond}+\lVert\Phi_{\mathrm{II}}^{(k)}\rVert_{\Diamond}

Corollary 7

(Circuit depth)
.

With
O(k^{4}\operatorname{poly}\log(k/\epsilon))
two-qubit magic gates, one can realize approximate unitary
k
-designs with
\varepsilon
additive error in depth
O(N+k\operatorname{poly}\log(k/\epsilon))
using 1D circuits and in depth
O(\log N+k\operatorname{poly}\log(k/\epsilon))
using all-to-all circuits with
N^{2}
ancillas.

Proof.

A sufficient condition for achieving approximate unitary designs with
\varepsilon
additive error is to take
N_{M}=O(k^{3}+\log(1/\epsilon))
. To realize an additive-error unitary design on these qubits, circuits with depth
O(\log(N_M/\epsilon)\cdot k\operatorname{poly}\log k)
suffice
[
17
]
.
∎

We note that, in this construction, we place the magic gate at the beginning of the circuit for a technical reason to suppress the diamond norm because
\lVert|T_{1})(T_{2}|\rVert_{\Diamond}
is smaller for
T_{2}\in S_{k}
.
We do not exclude the possibility of constructing an additive-error unitary design if all magic gates are placed at the final layer.

We further note that the global Clifford unitary in this construction might be necessary.
In a recent paper
[
52
]
, it is shown that to form a Clifford
k
-design with bounded additive error, Clifford circuits are required to have
\Omega(N)

\Omega(\log N)

6
Statistical mechanics picture

In this section, we provide a physical understanding of several results in this work based on the mapping from the
k
-th moment of the random circuit to classical statistical mechanics models.
Section
6.1
develops a stat-mech understanding of why Clifford circuits operating on qubit blocks of
O(\log N)
qubits can satisfy the approximate uniformity condition.
This also explains why shallow Haar random circuits can form a unitary design with bounded relative error
[
17
]
.
Section
6.2
provides an understanding for the required number of magic gates to convert the shallow Clifford circuits into state designs with bounded additive errors.

6.1
Uniformity condition as strong ordering

Here, we consider the
k
-fold channel of random Clifford circuits and bound its deviation from the uniformity condition using the partition functions of statistical mechanics models.
We show that the two-layer circuit operating on log-size qubit blocks can achieve approximation uniformity as the stat-mech model develops
strong ordering
, i.e. excitations at all scales are suppressed due to large excess free energy.

The
k
-fold channel of two-layer random Clifford circuits can be expressed as

\Phi^{(k)}_{\mathcal{E}}=2^{-Nk}\sum_{\vec{T}_{1},\vec{T_{2}}}g(\vec{T}_{1},\vec{T}_{2})|\vec{T}_{1})(\vec{T}_{2}|=2^{-Nk}\left[\sum_{T}|T)(T|+\sum_{\vec{T}_{1},\vec{T_{2}}}f(\vec{T}_{1},\vec{T}_{2})|\vec{T}_{1})(\vec{T}_{2}|\right].

(116)

The deviation from the uniformity condition is given by

\displaystyle\Delta(\Phi^{(k)}_{\mathcal{E}})=\sum_{\vec{T}_{1},\vec{T_{2}}}|f(\vec{T}_{1},\vec{T}_{2})|

\displaystyle=\sum_{\vec{T}_{1},\vec{T_{2}}}|g(\vec{T}_{1},\vec{T}_{2})|-\absolutevalue{\Sigma_{k,k}}+\sum_{T}\left(1-|g(T\vec{1},T\vec{1})|+|g(T\vec{1},T\vec{1})-1|\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\vec{T}_{1},\vec{T_{2}}}|g(\vec{T}_{1},\vec{T}_{2})|-\absolutevalue{\Sigma_{k,k}}+\sum_{T}2|g(T\vec{1},T\vec{1})-1|.

(117)

Each term
g(\vec{T}_{1},\vec{T}_{2})
in Eq. (
117

\Sigma_{k,k}

g(\vec{T}_{1},\vec{T}_{2})=2^{Nk}\sum_{\vec{T}_{3,4}}\prod_{x}\operatorname{Wg}_{\operatorname{C}}(T_{1,x},T_{3,x})\mbox{$\langle\!\langle T_{3,x}|T_{4,x-1}\rangle\!\rangle$}\mbox{$\langle\!\langle T_{3,x}|T_{4,x}\rangle\!\rangle$}\operatorname{Wg}_{\operatorname{C}}(T_{4,x},T_{2,x}).

(118)

Each spin
T
originates from the average over random Clifford gates and is arranged as shown below.

Note that the summation is not yet the partition function of a stat-mech model because the Weingarten function can be negative.
We introduce a function
g^{\prime}(\vec{T}_{1},\vec{T}_{2})
, replacing the Weingarten function in Eq. (
118
) with its absolute value,

\displaystyle g^{\prime}(\vec{T}_{1},\vec{T}_{2})=2^{Nk}\sum_{\vec{T}_{3,4}}\prod_{x}|\operatorname{Wg}_{\operatorname{C}}(T_{1,x},T_{3,x})|\mbox{$\langle\!\langle T_{3,x}|T_{4,x-1}\rangle\!\rangle$}\mbox{$\langle\!\langle T_{3,x}|T_{4,x}\rangle\!\rangle$}|\operatorname{Wg}_{\operatorname{C}}(T_{4,x},T_{2,x})|.

(119)

The function
g^{\prime}(\vec{T}_{1},\vec{T}_{2})
is an upper bound of
|g(\vec{T}_{1},\vec{T}_{2})|
and is given by a summation over positive terms.
Hence, it can be interpreted as a partition function with fixed boundary conditions set by
\vec{T}_{1}
and
\vec{T}_{2}
.

First, we consider the quantity
\mathcal{Z}:=\sum_{\vec{T}_{1},\vec{T_{2}}}g^{\prime}(\vec{T}_{1},\vec{T}_{2})
, which can be viewed as the partition function of a statistical mechanics model of
\vec{T}_{1,2,3,4}
with free boundary conditions.
We consider the low-temperature expansion of the partition function.
The configuration with the lowest energy has spins aligned in space and a degeneracy of
|\Sigma_{k,k}|
.
We invoke the Peierls argument to analyze the excitations.
The interaction between two neighboring spins
T_{i}
and
T_{j}
contributes a factor of
\mbox{$\langle\!\langle T_{i}|T_{j}\rangle\!\rangle$}=2^{\xi(k-|T_{i},T_{j}|)}
or
|\operatorname{Wg}_{\operatorname{C}}(T_{i},T_{j})|
to the Boltzmann weight.
The Boltzmann weight is suppressed by
2^{-\xi}
when
T_{i}
and
T_{j}
are not aligned.
For
\xi=a\log N
with a sufficiently large prefactor
a
, the energetic cost of creating a domain wall outweighs the entropic factor.
In this case, the domain walls in the stat-mech model are suppressed, and the partition function is governed by the aligned spin configurations:

\mathcal{Z}=|\Sigma_{k,k}|(1+N2^{-\xi-\log\xi+O(k^{2})}).

(120)

To make this more rigorous, one can analytically compute the partition function using the transfer matrix method.

Next, consider the quantity
g^{\prime}(T\vec{1},T\vec{1})
, which maps to the partition function of the same statistical mechanics model but with the boundary spins fixed to be
T
.
A similar analysis gives

g^{\prime}(T\vec{1},T\vec{1})=1+N2^{-\xi-\log\xi+O(k^{2})}.

(121)

We note that
g(T\vec{1},T\vec{1})
and
g^{\prime}(T\vec{1},T\vec{1})
differ only in the subleading term, and
|g(T\vec{1},T\vec{1})-1|\leq|g^{\prime}(T\vec{1},T\vec{1})-1|\leq N2^{-\xi-\log\xi+O(k^{2})}
.

The above analysis leads to an upper bound on the deviation from the uniformity in Eq. (
117
),

\displaystyle\Delta(\Phi^{(k)}_{\mathcal{E}})\leq N2^{-\xi-\log\xi+O(k^{2})}.

(122)

Hence, one can achieve approximate uniformity with
\xi=O(\log N+k^{2})
, i.e. in depth
O(\log N+k^{2})
.

We remark that the approximate uniformity requires the stat-mech model to develop
strong ordering
.
In the usual notion of an ordered phase, the domain wall excitations that flip an extensive number of spins are suppressed; however, small domain walls with
O(1)
energy costs are usually proliferated.
In contrast, the strong ordering here requires the smallest possible domain walls to be suppressed as the small domain walls can cause an
O(1)
deviation from the uniformity.
In the 1D stat-mech model we obtain, two notions coincide because the domain walls cause the same amount of energy regardless of the number of spins it flips.
However, in higher dimensions, these two notions are generally different. For example, in two dimensions, the spin model can order when each gate in the circuit acts on a constant number of qubits
[
61
,
62
]
; however, strong order still requires each gate to act on
O(\log N)
qubits.

The stat-mech mapping also explains the results in Ref.
[
17
]
that relative-error unitary design requires two-layer random unitary circuits with each gate drawn from a
k
-design acting on
O(\log N)
qubits.
In that case, we can introduce a similar uniformity condition for the
k
-fold channel of Haar random circuits,

\Phi_{\mathcal{E}}^{(k)}=\underbrace{\frac{1}{2^{Nk}}\sum_{\sigma\in S_{k}}|\sigma)(\sigma|}_{\Phi_{0}^{(k)}}+\frac{1}{2^{Nk}}\sum_{\vec{\sigma},\vec{\tau}}f(\vec{\sigma},\vec{\tau})|\vec{\sigma})(\vec{\tau}|.

(123)

Its deviation from the diagonal approximation
\Phi_{0}^{(k)}
of the
k
-fold channel of the Haar random unitary,
\Delta_{\operatorname{H}}(\Phi_{\mathcal{E}}^{(k)})=\sum_{\vec{\sigma},\vec{\tau}}\absolutevalue{f(\vec{\sigma},\vec{\tau})}
has a similar statistical mechanics mapping.
Here, the only difference is that spins take values only in the permutation group
S_{k}
due to Haar averaging.
We can show that each gate acting on
O(\log N)
qubits is sufficient for a small deviation.
Crucially, a small deviation here implies that the unitary ensemble forms a design with bounded relative error because for the infinite norm
\lVert\mathrm{r}(\sigma)\rVert_{\infty}=1
for a permutation
\sigma\in S_{k}
.
However, for the Clifford circuits,

We further remark that the gluing lemma essentially implies strong ordering.
It requires the spins to align while introducing one additional spin to the stat-mech model; the energetic cost of misaligned spins overcomes the entropic factor.

7
Discussion

In this work, we have presented constructions of approximate state and unitary designs, which improve the previous results in terms of either the circuit depth or the number of non-Clifford gates.
We prove no-go theorems for various architectures to form designs with bounded relative errors.
We also provide physical understanding for several key results based on the statistical mechanics mapping.

Our study leaves several open questions:

•

The scaling of
k
in the circuit depth of our constructions for relative error designs may not be optimal.
Our constructions require each Haar random gate to act on a qubit cluster of size
O(k\log k)
because we use a rather loose bound on the distance between operators in the Clifford commutant,
|\sigma,T|\geq 1
for
\sigma\in S_{k}
and
T\notin S_{k}
.
Suppose one can obtain a bound on the number of stochastic Lagrangian subspaces of a certain distance from permutations.
In that case, we might be able to reduce the size of the Haar random gates, which would improve the scaling of
k
in the total circuit depth.

•

Our design constructions use gates acting on qubit blocks. It remains open whether the circuit ensemble still satisfies the approximate uniformity if one replaces two-layer random circuits with a brickwork random circuit of depth
O(\log N)
operating on qubits.

•

Our constructions require generic unitary gates drawn from unitary
k
-designs. A natural question is whether one can instead use a specific set of magic gates (e.g. T gate), which is easier to realize in practice.

•

Different from the unitary group, orthogonal
k
-designs, which approximate the
k
-th moment of Haar random orthogonal matrices, cannot be generated in sub-linear depth. Reducing the magic depth or the usage of magic gates in the construction is of particular interest in this case. It remains open whether one can use linear-depth Clifford circuits augmented by constant-depth magic gates to construct such designs.

Operational meaning of additive-error state designs

In this appendix, we consider an adaptive experiment that queries a random state
\ket{\psi}
drawn from an ensemble for
k

If the ensemble forms a state
k
-design with bounded additive error, the output state would have a bounded trace distance from the output of the experiment that queries genuine Haar random states.

Any adaptive experiment that queries the state
k

The most general adaptive experiment that queries
k
copies of the state
\ket{\psi}
is shown on the left.
Let
m

\ket{0}^{\otimes m}

In the first step, we apply a unitary
W_{1}
acting on the ancillas and a copy of
\ket{\psi}
.
In the

\ket{\psi}

\ket{\psi}

In the end, the output state of the experiment is

\ket{\psi}^{\otimes k}

If the state
\ket{\psi}
is drawn from an ensemble
\mathcal{E}
that forms an approximate state

\left\lVert\mathbb{E}_{\psi\sim\mathcal{E}}\outerproduct{\psi_{\text{out}}}{\psi_{\text{out}}}-\mathbb{E}_{\psi\sim\operatorname{H}}\outerproduct{\psi_{\text{out}}}{\psi_{\text{out}}}\right\rVert_{1}=\lVert\rho_{\mathcal{E}}^{(k)}-\rho_{\operatorname{H}}^{(k)}\rVert_{1}\leq\varepsilon.

(130)

Appendix B

In this appendix, we provide a construction of the state
k
-design with bounded additive errors by inserting magic gates prior to the two-layer Clifford unitary.

Corollary 8

(Additive-error state designs from constant magic followed by low-depth Clifford unitaries)
.

Consider a system of
N
qubits. Let
{M_{i}}
denote disjoint subsystems, each containing
N_{i}
qubits. Assume
N_{i}\geq 2\log k
. Let
N_{M}=\sum_{i}N_{i}
be the total size of these subsystems. The unitaries
U_{i}
, acting on subsystem
M_{i}
, are independently drawn from exact unitary
k
-designs. Let
V
be a two-layer brickwork circuit, where each gate is drawn form an exact Clifford
k
-design. Let
\xi
be the smallest size of the overlapping regions of the Clifford unitaries. Then the states

\varepsilon\leq 2^{-\xi-\log\xi+O(k^{2})}+2^{O(k^{2})}\prod_{i}2^{-N_{i}}k!(1+k^{2}2^{-N_{i}}).

(131)

In other words, one can achieve
\varepsilon
additive error with
N_{M}=O\big(k^{2}+\log(1/\epsilon)\big)
and
\xi=O(\log(N/\epsilon)+k^{2})

Proof.

Before applying the Clifford unitary, the state on

\rho_{\mathcal{E}_{0}}^{(k)}=\left[\sum_{\sigma_{i},\tau_{i}}\bigotimes_{i}\operatorname{Wg}(\sigma_{i},\tau_{i})r(\sigma_{i})_{M_{i}}\right]\otimes\ket{0}\bra{0}^{\otimes N-N_{M}}.

(132)

According to Lemma
2
, the Clifford unitaries
V

\varepsilon^{\prime}
-approximate uniformity condition with
\varepsilon^{\prime}=2^{-\xi-\log\xi+O(k^{2})}

k

\Phi_{\mathcal{E}_{u}}^{(k)}=2^{-Nk}\left[\sum_{T}|T)(T|+\sum_{\vec{T}_{1},\vec{T_{2}}}f(\vec{T}_{1},\vec{T}_{2})|\vec{T}_{1})(\vec{T}_{2}|\right].

(133)

Applying it to the initial state, the state
k
-th moment becomes

\displaystyle\rho_{\mathcal{E}}^{(k)}

\displaystyle=2^{-Nk}\left[\sum_{T}r(T)\prod_{i}\sum_{\sigma_{i},\tau_{i}}2^{N_{i}(k-|T,\sigma_{i}|)}\operatorname{Wg}(\sigma_{i},\tau_{i})\right.

(134)

\displaystyle\left.+\sum_{\vec{T}_{1},\vec{T}_{2}}f(\vec{T}_{1},\vec{T}_{2})r(\vec{T}_{1})\prod_{i}\sum_{\sigma_{i},\tau_{i}}\operatorname{Wg}(\sigma_{i},\tau_{i})2^{N_{i}(k-|T_{2,i},\sigma_{i}|)}\right],

(135)

\displaystyle\rho_{\mathrm{II}}^{(k)}=2^{-Nk}\sum_{T\notin S_{k}}r(T)\prod_{i}\sum_{\sigma_{i},\tau_{i}}2^{N_{i}(k-|T,\sigma_{i}|)}\operatorname{Wg}(\sigma_{i},\tau_{i}),

(138)

(141)

Corollary 9

(Relative error for the collision probability in log-depth Clifford circuits)
.

Consider an ensemble
\mathcal{E}=\{V\}

(144)

Proof.

\displaystyle k2^{k-N}+N2^{-\xi-\log\xi+O(k^{2})}.

Appendix D
Growing additive-error state designs with random Clifford gates

We prove that a state
k

k

k

k

Lemma 4

(Gluing a state design and a stabilizer state design using unitaries from Clifford design)
.

Let
A
,
B
,
C
, and
D
be disjoint subsystems of qubits.
N_{A}
,
N_{B}
,
N_{C}
,
N_{D}
denote the size of the subsystems. Assume
N_{C}\geq k+\log k-2
.
\ket{\psi}_{AB}
is drawn from a state
k
-design up to additive error
\varepsilon_{AB}
,
\ket{\psi}_{CD}
is drawn from an exact stabilizer
k
-design.
V_{BC}
is drawn from a (exact) Clifford
k
-design. Then
V_{BC}\ket{\psi}_{AB}\otimes\ket{\psi}_{CD}
is an approximate state
k
-design with additive error
\varepsilon\leq\varepsilon_{AB}+2^{O(k^{2})}(2^{-N_{B}}+2^{-N_{C}})
.

Proof.

We write the resulting state as
\rho_{\operatorname{H},AB,\varepsilon}^{(k)}\otimes\rho_{\operatorname{C},CD}^{(k)}
. It can be approximated up to additive error
\varepsilon
by

\rho_{\operatorname{H},AB}^{(k)}\otimes\rho_{\operatorname{C},CD}^{(k)}=D_{N_{AB},k}^{-1}Z_{N_{CD},k}^{-1}\sum_{\sigma,T}{r(\sigma)}_{AB}\otimes r(T)_{CD}

(148)

where
\rho_{\operatorname{H},AB}^{(k)}
is the
k
-th moment of an exact
k
-design on
AB
.
After applying the random Clifford unitaries, the
k
-th moment of the state becomes

\displaystyle\Phi_{\operatorname{C},BC}^{(k)}(\rho_{\operatorname{H},AB}^{(k)}\otimes\rho_{\operatorname{C},CD}^{(k)})

(149)

\displaystyle=D_{N_{AB},k}^{-1}Z_{N_{CD},k}^{-1}\sum_{\sigma,T_{1},T_{2},T_{3}}\operatorname{Wg}_{\operatorname{C}}(T_{1},T_{2})2^{N_{B}(k-|T_{2},\sigma|)+N_{C}(k-|T_{2},T_{3}|)}r(\sigma)_{A}\otimes r(T_{1})_{BC}\otimes r(T_{3})_{D}

The trace distance from the
k
-th moment of Haar random states takes the form

\displaystyle\lVert\Phi_{\operatorname{C},BC}^{(k)}(\rho_{\operatorname{H},AB}^{(k)}\otimes\rho_{\operatorname{C},CD}^{(k)})-\rho_{\operatorname{H},ABCD}^{(k)}\lVert_{1}

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \leq 2^{kN}\Bigg|\sum_{\sigma,T_{1},T_{3}}D_{N_{AB},k}^{-1}Z_{N_{CD},k}^{-1}\sum_{T_{2}}\operatorname{Wg}_{\operatorname{C}}(T_{1},T_{2})2^{N_{B}(k-|T_{2},\sigma|)+N_{C}(k-|T_{2},T_{3}|)}-D_{N,k}^{-1}\delta_{T_{1},\sigma}\delta_{T_{3},\sigma}\Bigg|\end{aligned}

(150)

where we use
\lVert r(\sigma)_{A}\otimes r(T_{1})_{BC}\otimes r(T_{3})_{D}\rVert_{1}\leq 2^{kN}
,
N=N_{A}+N_{B}+N_{C}+N_{D}
.
For
T_{3}=\sigma
, the summation over
T_{2}
yields a delta function
\delta_{T_{1},\sigma}
. The corresponding contribution is

\displaystyle 2^{kN}\sum_{\sigma\in S_{k}}\left|D_{N_{AB},k}^{-1}Z_{N_{CD},k}^{-1}-D_{N,k}^{-1}\right|\leq

\displaystyle k!\big[(1+k^{2}2^{-N_{AB}})(1+k2^{k-N_{CD}})-1+k^{2}2^{-N}\big]

(151)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle k!(k^{2}2^{-N_{AB}}+k2^{k-N_{CD}}+(k^{3}+k^{2})2^{-N})

For
T_{3}\notin S_{k}
, the contribution

\displaystyle 2^{kN}D_{N_{AB},k}^{-1}Z_{N_{CD},k}^{-1}\big|\operatorname{Wg}_{\operatorname{C}}(T_{1},T_{2})\big|2^{N_{B}(k-|T_{2},\sigma|)+N_{C}(k-|T_{2},T_{3}|)}

(152)

is either suppressed by
2^{-N_{B}}
or
2^{-N_{C}}
due to misalignment of inside
2^{N_{C}(k-|T_{2},\sigma|)+N_{C}(k-|T_{2},T_{3}|)}
, or suppressed by misalignment in
T_{1}
and
T_{2}
to give
|\operatorname{Wg}_{\operatorname{C}}(T_{1},T_{2})|\leq 2^{-N_{BC}k-N_{BC}+O(k^{2})}
. The number of such terms are upper bounded by
2^{O(k^{2})}
. When we sum those up, we at most get

. To sum up, we have

\lVert\Phi_{\operatorname{C},BC}^{(k)}(\rho_{\operatorname{H},AB}^{(k)}\otimes\rho_{\operatorname{C},CD}^{(k)})-\rho_{\operatorname{H},ABCD}^{(k)}\lVert_{1}\leq 2^{O(k^{2})}(2^{-N_{B}}+2^{-N_{C}}).

(153)

Then we use the triangle inequality to bound

\displaystyle\lVert\Phi_{\operatorname{C},BC}^{(k)}(\rho_{\operatorname{H},AB,\varepsilon}^{(k)}\otimes\rho_{\operatorname{C},CD}^{(k)})-\rho_{\operatorname{H},ABCD}^{(k)}\lVert_{1}

(154)

\displaystyle\leq\lVert\Phi_{\operatorname{C},BC}^{(k)}(\rho_{\operatorname{H},AB,\varepsilon}^{(k)}\otimes\rho_{\operatorname{C},CD}^{(k)})-\Phi_{\operatorname{C},BC}^{(k)}(\rho_{\operatorname{H},AB}^{(k)}\otimes\rho_{\operatorname{C},CD}^{(k)})\lVert_{1}+\lVert\Phi_{\operatorname{C},BC}^{(k)}(\rho_{\operatorname{H},AB}^{(k)}\otimes\rho_{\operatorname{C},CD}^{(k)})-\rho_{\operatorname{H},ABCD}^{(k)}\lVert_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon+2^{O(k^{2})}(2^{-N_{B}}+2^{-N_{C}}).

Appendix E
More on the Clifford commutant

E.1
Asymptotics of Clifford Weingarten functions

We present the asymptotics of the Weingarten functions of the Clifford group over
n
qubits.
The results are based on properties of an orthogonal basis analyzed in
[
25
]
.

To begin, the
k
-fold channel
\Phi^{(k)}_{\operatorname{C}}
can be formulated as an operator in the doubled Hilbert space via the Choi–Jamiołkowski isomorphism

\hat{\Phi}_{C}^{(k)}:=\mathbb{E}_{V\sim\operatorname{C}}V^{\otimes k}\otimes V^{*\otimes k}=\sum_{T_{i},T_{j}}\operatorname{Wg}_{\operatorname{C}}(T_{i},T_{j})\mbox{$|T_{i}\rangle\!\rangle$}^{\otimes n}\mbox{$\langle\!\langle T_{j}|$}^{\otimes n}.

(155)

Here, the states
\{\mbox{$|T_{i}\rangle\!\rangle$}\}
, vectorization of the operators
r(T)
, are linearly independent but not orthogonal, with inner products given by
\mbox{$\langle\!\langle T_{i}|T_{j}\rangle\!\rangle$}=2^{n(k-|T_{i},T_{j}|)}
.
One can construct an orthogonal basis using the Gram-Schmidt orthogonalization
12
12

12

The disadvantage of this basis is that it is not a tensor product of operators on single qubits. Therefore, we work in the
\{\mbox{$|T_{i}\rangle\!\rangle$}\}
basis to proceed in the main text.
:

\mbox{$|E_{i}\rangle\!\rangle$}=\sum_{j=1}^{i}A_{ij}\mbox{$|T_{j}\rangle\!\rangle$}^{\otimes n},\qquad A_{ij}=\sum_{\Pi\in S_{i},\Pi(i)=j}\operatorname{sgn}(\Pi)\prod_{l=1}^{j-1}\mbox{$\langle\!\langle T_{l}|T_{\Pi(l)}\rangle\!\rangle$}^{n}.

(156)

It has the following properties
[
25
]

\displaystyle|A_{ii}-2^{-nk}|\leq 2^{-nk-n+O(k^{2})}),\qquad\forall i

(157)

\displaystyle|A_{ij}|\leq 2^{-nk-n+O(k^{2})},\qquad|A_{ij}|\leq 2^{-n(k+|\dim\mathrm{N}_{i}-\dim\mathrm{N}_{j}|)+O(k^{3})},\qquad\forall i\neq j.

The normalization is

|\mbox{$\langle\!\langle E_{i}|E_{i}\rangle\!\rangle$}-2^{nk}|\leq 2^{nk-2n+O(k^{2})}.

(158)

Since
\hat{\Phi}_{\operatorname{C}}^{(k)}
is a projection operator, it is an equal weight sum of an orthonormal basis:

\hat{\Phi}_{\operatorname{C}}^{(k)}=\sum_{j}\frac{1}{\mbox{$\langle\!\langle E_{i}|E_{i}\rangle\!\rangle$}}\mbox{$|E_{i}\rangle\!\rangle$}\mbox{$\langle\!\langle E_{i}|$}.

(159)

So the Weingarten functions can be expressed as

\operatorname{Wg}_{\operatorname{C}}(T_{i},T_{j})=\sum_{k}\frac{1}{\mbox{$\langle\!\langle E_{k}|E_{k}\rangle\!\rangle$}}A_{ki}A_{kj}^{*}.

(160)

We have the following asymptotics

\displaystyle|\operatorname{Wg}_{\operatorname{C}}(T_{i},T_{i})-2^{-nk}|

\displaystyle\leq 2^{-nk-n+O(k^{2})},\qquad\forall i

(161)

\displaystyle|\operatorname{Wg}_{\operatorname{C}}(T_{i},T_{j})|

\displaystyle\leq 2^{-nk-n+O(k^{2})},\qquad\forall i\neq j

\displaystyle|\operatorname{Wg}_{\operatorname{C}}(T_{i},T_{j})|

\displaystyle\leq 2^{-n(k+|\dim\mathrm{N}_{i}-\dim\mathrm{N}_{j}|)+O(k^{3})},\qquad\forall i\neq j

For
i\neq j
, we used the triangle inequality
|\dim\mathrm{N}_{i}-\dim\mathrm{N}_{k}|+|\dim\mathrm{N}_{k}-\dim\mathrm{N}_{j}|\geq|\dim\mathrm{N}_{i}-\dim\mathrm{N}_{j}|
.

E.2
Diamond norm

To bound the additive error of approximate unitary designs, we use the diamond norm for the superoperator
|T_{1})(T_{2}|(\cdot)=\mathrm{r}(T_{1})\Tr[\mathrm{r}(T_{2})^{\top}(\cdot)]
acting on a single qubit:

\lVert|T_{1})(T_{2}|\rVert_{\Diamond}=2^{k+\dim\mathrm{N}_{2}-\dim\mathrm{N}_{1}}.

(162)

To prove this, we first use an upper bound of the diamond norm (Lemma 3 of
[
25
]
):

\displaystyle\lVert|T_{1})(T_{2}|\rVert_{\Diamond}

\displaystyle=\max_{\lVert O\rVert_{1}\leq 1}\lVert|T_{1})(T_{2}|\otimes\operatorname{id}(O)\rVert_{1}=\max_{\lVert O\rVert_{1}\leq 1}\lVert\mathrm{r}(T_{1})\otimes\Tr_{1}[(\mathrm{r}(T_{2})^{\top}\otimes I)O]\rVert_{1}

(163)

\displaystyle=\lVert\mathrm{r}(T_{1})\rVert_{1}\max_{\lVert O\rVert_{1}\leq 1}\lVert\Tr_{1}[(\mathrm{r}(T_{2})^{\top}\otimes I)O]\rVert_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\lVert\mathrm{r}(T_{1})\rVert_{1}\max_{\lVert O\rVert_{1}\leq 1}\lVert(\mathrm{r}(T_{2})^{\top}\otimes I)O\rVert_{1}

\displaystyle=\lVert\mathrm{r}(T_{1})\rVert_{1}\lVert\mathrm{r}(T_{2})\otimes I\rVert_{\infty}=\lVert\mathrm{r}(T_{1})\rVert_{1}\lVert\mathrm{r}(T_{2})\rVert_{\infty}=2^{k+\dim\mathrm{N}_{2}-\dim\mathrm{N}_{1}}.

The notation
\Tr_{1}
is the partial trace over the first copy of the system.
The second line is because the trace norm is multiplicative under the tensor product.
The third line is because the trace norm is non-increasing under the partial trace.
The other direction is given by

\lVert|T_{1})(T_{2}|\rVert_{\Diamond}\geq\frac{\lVert|T_{1})(T_{2}|(\mathrm{r}(T_{2}))\rVert_{1}}{\lVert\mathrm{r}(T_{2})\rVert_{1}}=\frac{\lVert 2^{k}\mathrm{r}(T_{1})\rVert_{1}}{\lVert\mathrm{r}(T_{2})\rVert_{1}}=2^{k+\dim\mathrm{N}_{2}-\dim\mathrm{N}_{1}}.

(164)

The stabilizer Rényi entropies are defined as

to be an integer multiple of

.

(166)

This is a member of the Clifford commutant corresponding to

is the projection onto the CSS codespace associated with the defect subspace
\{\bm{1}_{k}\}
that contains two elements,
\bm{0}_{k}
and
\bm{1}_{k}
.
In the case of
k=4

[

]
, playing an important role in the representation theory of the Clifford group
[

]
.

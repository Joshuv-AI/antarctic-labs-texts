Abstract
. For a smooth expanding map f f of the circle, its (unmarked) length spectrum is defined as the set of logarithms of multipliers of periodic orbits of f f . This spectrum is analogous to the set of lengths of all closed geodesics on negatively curved surfaces – the classical length spectrum. In the paper, we prove a length spectral rigidity result for expanding circle maps. Namely, we show that a smooth expanding circle map f f of degree d ⩾ 2 d\geqslant 2 , under certain assumptions on the sparsity of its length spectrum, cannot be perturbed with an arbitrarily small perturbation (depending on f f ) so that its length spectrum stays the same. The proof uses the Whitney extension theorem, a quantitative Livšic ​-type theorem, and a novel iterative scheme.



1.
Introduction

In 1990, Croke
[
Cr
]
and Otal
[
Ot
]
proved a remarkable result on rigidity of negatively curved metrics in dimension
2
. They showed that a smooth metric
g
of negative curvature on a closed surface is uniquely defined (up to smooth coordinate changes) by its
marked length spectrum
, i.e., by the lengths of closed geodesics for the metric
g
‘marked’ by their respective homotopy types. As it turns out, knowing just the
length spectrum
of
g
, i.e., the set of lengths of all closed geodesics and ‘forgetting’ about their homotopy types is not enough to reconstruct the metric, as the examples of Sunada
[
Su
]
and Vignéras
[
Vig
]
show. However, the
local (unmarked) length spectral rigidity
question for
nearby
negatively curved metrics is widely open. In this paper, we study a one-dimensional analog of this question for expanding circle endomorphisms.

1.1.
Setup and the statements of the main results

Let
f\colon\mathbb{S}^{1}\to\mathbb{S}^{1}
,
\mathbb{S}^{1}=\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}
, be a
C^{r,1}
-smooth,
r\geqslant 2
, expanding circle endomorphism of degree
d\geqslant 2
normalized so that
f(0)=0
and
f^{\prime}(x)>1
for all
x\in\mathbb{S}^{1}
. Here,
C^{r,1}
-smooth means that
f
has
r
derivatives and the
r^{\text{th}}
derivative is Lipschitz continuous. For brevity, we write
\mathcal{E}^{r}_{d}
for the class of such maps. Every
f\in\mathcal{E}^{r}_{d}
can be further normalized by a
C^{r,1}
-smooth conjugacy to preserve the Lebesgue measure in
\mathbb{S}^{1}
(see, e.g.,
[
SS
]
); we denote
\hat{\mathcal{E}}^{r}_{d}\subset\mathcal{E}^{r}_{d}
the class of Lebesgue measure preserving expanding circle maps.

Denote by
\mathbb{P}_{n}^{f}
the set of all periodic points of (possibly not exact) period
n
for
f\in\mathcal{E}_{d}^{r}
. The
\log
-multiplier
of a periodic point
p\in\mathbb{P}_{n}^{f}
is defined as

\mathcal{\lambda}^{f}(p):=\log\left(f^{n}\right)^{\prime}(p).

The averaging of the
\log
-multiplier
1/n\cdot\log\left(f^{n}\right)^{\prime}(p)
gives the classical
Lyapunov exponent
of the periodic cycle
p\mapsto f(p)\mapsto\ldots\mapsto f^{n}(p)=p
(which is independent of the point in the cycle).

In
[
McM2
]
, McMullen calls the
\log
-multiplier of
p
‘the length’ of the periodic cycle
p\mapsto f(p)\mapsto\ldots\mapsto f^{n}(p)=p
, and draws some further analogy between the sets of
\log
-multipliers and the lengths of closed geodesics on hyperbolic surfaces. In
[
HKS
]
, Huang, Kaloshin, and Sorrentino established a connection between the Lyapunov exponents and the lengths of certain periodic orbits in the context of convex billiards (see also
[
BSKL
]
for a similar connection in the context of dispersing billiards).

Following the analogy outlined above, for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we define the
length (Lyapunov) spectrum at level
n
as the set
\Lyap_{n}(f):=\left\{\lambda^{f}(p):p\in\mathbb{P}_{n}^{f}\right\}
. The union

\Lyap(f):=\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\Lyap_{n}(f)

of all these sets yields the
(unmarked) length spectrum
of the expanding circle map
f
. We will use the terms ‘length spectrum’ and ‘Lyapunov spectrum’ interchangeably.

There is a natural
marked
counterpart of the length spectrum. Namely, it is known that any two expanding circle maps
f,g\in\mathcal{E}^{r}_{d}
are topologically conjugate via an orientation-preserving homeomorphism
\varphi\colon\mathbb{S}^{1}\to\mathbb{S}^{1}
as follows:

g=\varphi\circ f\circ\varphi^{-1}.

This homeomorphism respects the symbolic dynamics and hence provides a natural marking: we say that
p^{f}\in\mathbb{P}_{n}^{f}
and
p^{g}\in\mathbb{P}_{n}^{g}
are
corresponding periodic points
if
p^{g}=\varphi(p^{f})
. We call
\varphi
the
marking conjugacy
and say that
f
and
g
have the same
marked length (Lyapunov) spectra
if
\lambda^{f}(p^{f})=\lambda^{g}(p^{g})
for every pair of corresponding periodic points. By the classical result of Shub and Sullivan
[
SS
]
, the marked spectrum defines an expanding circle map up to a
smooth
change of coordinates, namely, if
f
and
g
have the same marked length spectra, then the marking conjugacy
\varphi
is
C^{r,1}
-smooth.

We are interested in the following question:

Does the (unmarked) length spectrum of an expanding circle map uniquely define the smooth conjugacy class of the map?

Similarly to the unmarked length spectrum setup for negatively curved metrics, in general the answer to the question above is ‘no’:

Main Proposition

(Counterexample to general length spectral rigidity)
.

For every
\varepsilon>0
there exists a nonlinear map
g\in\mathcal{E}^{r}_{d}
(that depends on
\varepsilon
) and there exists
f\in\mathcal{E}^{r}_{d}
(that depends on
\varepsilon
and
g
) such that

\|f-g\|_{C^{r,1}}\leqslant\varepsilon\quad\text{ and }\quad{\Lyap}(f)={\Lyap}(g),

but the marking conjugacy
\varphi
is not
C^{1}
. (Here,
\|\cdot\|_{C^{r,1}}
denotes the
C^{r,1}
-norm on the circle.)

Moreover,
g
can be chosen to be arbitrarily close (depending on
\varepsilon
) to the linear map
L_{d}\colon x\mapsto d\cdot x\mod 1
.

Nonetheless, the following local rigidity result holds. Before stating the result, let us introduce the following notion. We say that the length spectrum of
f\in\mathcal{E}_{d}^{r}
is
(\beta,\gamma)
-sparse
if there exist parameters
0<\beta<\gamma
and coefficients
C_{\beta}>0
,
C_{\alpha}>0
such that for all
\ell_{1},\ell_{2}\in\Lyap(f)
,

\text{either }\quad|\ell_{1}-\ell_{2}|\geqslant C_{\beta}\cdot e^{-\beta\max\{\ell_{1},\ell_{2}\}},\quad\text{ or }\quad|\ell_{1}-\ell_{2}|\leqslant C_{\gamma}\cdot e^{-\gamma\min\{\ell_{1},\ell_{2}\}}.

(1.1)

This sparsity condition means that any two elements in the spectrum are either sufficiently far apart (controlled by
\beta
), or sufficiently close (controlled by
\gamma
), see Figure

Compare this notion to the sparsity condition in

\beta

The normal distribution of the entries in the length spectrum
\Lyap_{n}(f)

The following theorem is the main result of the paper.

Main Theorem

(Unmarked length spectral rigidity)
.

For every integer
d\geqslant 2
and
r\geqslant 2
there exist
\beta_{0}>0
,
\gamma_{0}>0
so that for every
\beta\leqslant\beta_{0}
and
\gamma\geqslant\gamma_{0}
the following holds.

Let
g\in\hat{\mathcal{E}}^{r+1}_{d}
be an expanding circle map such that

\|g-L_{d}\|_{C^{2}}<\frac{d-1}{2}\quad\text{ and }\quad\Lyap(g)\quad\text{is}\quad(\beta,\gamma)-\text{sparse}.

Then there exists
\varepsilon=\varepsilon(g)>0
so that every
f\in\mathcal{E}_{d}^{r}
with

\|f-g\|_{C^{r,1}}<\varepsilon\quad\text{ and }\quad\Lyap(f)=\Lyap(g)

is
C^{r}
-smoothly conjugate to
g
. In particular, the marking conjugacy
\varphi
is a
C^{r}
-smooth diffeomorphism.

Let us make several remarks regarding the statement of the Main Theorem.

Remark 1.1

(On the choice of the sparsity parameters
\beta
and
\gamma
)
.

As we will see in the proof of the Main Theorem, one can choose
\gamma_{0}=1/3
and

\beta_{0}=\frac{1}{120(a_{0}+1)a_{0}^{2}},\quad\text{ where }a_{0}=\frac{\log\left(3d-1\right)-\log 2}{\log\left(d+1\right)-\log 2}.

For
g\in\hat{\mathcal{E}}^{r}_{d}
, we can introduce the following quantity

N_{\log}(g):=\frac{\log\big(\max_{x\in\mathbb{S}^{1}}g^{\prime}(x)\big)}{\log\big(\min_{x\in\mathbb{S}^{1}}g^{\prime}(x)\big)}\geqslant 1,

called the
log-nonlinearity
of
g
. The value of
a_{0}
is the bound from above on the log-nonlinearity
N_{\log}(g)
under the assumption
\|g-L_{d}\|_{C^{2}}<(d-1)/2

We do not claim that this choice of
\beta_{0}
is optimal, and it would be interesting to know sharp values for
\beta_{0}
.

Furthermore, as it is not hard to see, if
1<\Lambda=\min_{x\in\mathbb{S}^{1}}g^{\prime}(x)
is the minimal expansion rate and
\Lambda^{a}=\max_{x\in\mathbb{S}^{1}}g^{\prime}(x)
, then

\Lyap_{n}(g)\subset[n\log\Lambda,na\log\Lambda],

and thus, as
n\to\infty
, the range of entries in
\Lyap_{n}(g)
might potentially spread (linearly with
n
). There are approximately
d^{n}/n
periodic orbits of period
n
for
g
. Hence, by the pigeonhole principle, at least two entries in
\Lyap_{n}(g)
has to be
O(n^{2}\cdot d^{-n})
-close to each other, which is the value of order
d^{-\tau n}
as
n\to\infty
for any
0<\tau<1
. This implies that the
full
spectrum at level
n
cannot be
\tau
-sparse. However, this does not prevent clustering of some values of
\Lyap_{n}(g)
with big gaps between the clusters, as shown in Figure
1
.

In would be interesting to understand possible structures of the spectrum
\Lyap_{n}(f)
, beyond the mentioned results from
[
McM3
,
DFKLL
]
.

Remark 1.2

(On the final smoothness of
\varphi
)
.

Using the result of Shub and Sullivan
[
SS
]
, one can bootstrap the smoothness of
\varphi
in the conclusion of the Main Theorem to be
C^{r,1}
. Our proof does not uses
[
SS
]
directly.

Remark 1.3

(Beyond expanding circle maps)
.

We expect that the methods developed in this paper for establishing rigidity of expanding circle maps apply to the analogous questions on spectral rigidity for Anosov maps on the torus (at least in dimension
2
).

Below, we focus on the case
d=2
.

1.2.
Some history of the question

With the exception of certain finite-dimensional families of maps (such as iterative rational maps on the Riemann sphere; see
[
McM1
,
JX
]
and references therein), the problem of recovering the smooth conjugacy class of maps from their unmarked multiplier data (often referred to as the length spectrum), and thereby establishing
spectral rigidity
, remains widely open. The current paper makes the first contribution in this direction.

In contrast, a substantial body of literature explores this question within the marked context, i.e., when the periodic data is coming together with marking via topological conjugacy or symbolic dynamics. The question in this context is to promote the topological conjugacy to a smooth conjugacy. For expanding maps, the spectral rigidity has been established in various settings: by Shub and Sullivan
[
SS
]
for one-dimensional expanding circle maps, by Martens and de Melo
[
MdM
]
for unimodal maps, and most recently, by Gogolev and Rodriguez Hertz
[
GRH
]
for higher-dimensional expanding maps on closed manifolds.
The analogous question in the setting of Anosov diffeomorphisms was completely resolved by de la Llave, Marco and Mariyón in dimension
2

[
dL1
,
dL2
,
dLM
]
. See also
[
dL3
,
Go
,
KS
]
and references therein for a partial progress for higher-dimensional Anosov diffeomorphisms.

The marked length spectral rigidity for hyperbolic surfaces (i.e., recovering of the metric by the set of lengths of closed geodesics marked by their homotopy types) was proven in dimension
2
by Otal
[
Ot
]
and Croke
[
Cr
]
(see also the lecture note of Wilkinson
[
Wi
]
). For manifolds of dimension
\geqslant 3
, only a perturbative marked length spectral rigidity is known, see the seminal work of Guillarmou and Lefeuvre
[
GuLe
]
and references therein.

Most spectral rigidity results rely on finite Livšic -type theorems (see
[
KH
]
for the classical Livšic theorem). The goal of the finite Livšic theory is to find solutions to cohomological equations using only finite periodic data; such equations appear naturally when one tries to linearize the conjugacy relation between maps or flows. We refer to the works
[
GoLe
,
GuLe
,
Ka
]
on finite Livšic theorems.

Finally, we mention a series of recent preprints of O’Hare
[
OH1
,
OH2
]
, where the finite periodic data rigidity was proven for area-preserving Anosov maps on the torus in dimensions
1
and
2
using methods of thermodynamic formalism.

1.3.
Strategy of the proof

The proof is done via an iterative scheme. We outline the strategy in the case when
d=2
and
r=2
. The idea is to fix
g
and to construct a convergent sequence
(h_{k})
of adjustments to
f
such that

-

each
h_{k}
is a
C^{2,1}
-smooth diffeomorphism of
\mathbb{S}^{1}
,

-

the norms
\|h_{k}\|_{C^{2,1}}
are uniformly bounded,

-

each
h_{k}
coincides with the topological conjugacy
\varphi
on a set of points that is exponentially dense in terms of
k
.

In this approach, we measure proximity in terms of parameters that depend only on
g
. The last two properties guarantee that
(h_{k})
has a sub-sequence converging to a
C^{2}
-smooth diffeomorphism which must necessarily be equal to
\varphi
. The details are the following.

Starting with
k=\kappa_{0}
, we construct a sequence of
C^{2,1}
-smooth circle diffeomorphisms
(h_{k})
, uniformly bounded in
C^{2,1}
-norm, such that each
h_{k}
is a
C^{2,1}
-smooth extension of the discrete map
\varphi\colon\mathbb{P}_{k}^{f}\to\mathbb{P}_{k}^{g}
. We call
k
the
induction scale
and each extension is done using the
Whitney extension theorem
. We can take
\kappa_{0}
sufficiently large by choosing the starting bound
\varepsilon=\varepsilon(g)
on the
C^{2,1}
-norm
\|f-g\|_{C^{2,1}}
small enough. This initial choice is done so that several
a priori
estimates required to run the induction, start to work.

For these extensions
h_{k}
to be with uniform control of the
C^{2,1}
-norm, we need a sharp control of
\varphi
in the gaps between periodic points of period
k
. This is done by controlling
\varphi
on a finer scale of periodic points of period
5k
(the
inhomogeneity scale
). Under the Lebesgue measure normalization and provided that we know the lengths at the carefully selected ‘sewing orbits’ called
messengers
and
hybrid messengers
, of period
4\cdot 5k=20k
and
20k+1
respectively, between the periodic points of period
5k
(all together we call these log-multipliers
periodic data at the inhomogeneity scale
), we will be able to prescribe derivatives along periodic orbits of period
5k
, up to an error of the order of the inhomogeneity scale (a corollary to a
finite Livšic-type theorem
). These derivatives allow us to obtain a sharp distortion control for the distances between pairs of nearby points in
\mathbb{P}_{k}^{f}
. This distortion control is then used as an input to the Whitney extension theorem.

We note that the periodic data at the inhomogeneity scale has to be known on the set of the periodic points of cardinality
O(2^{4k})
(which is a small subset of the set of points of period
20k
and
20k+1
). This periodic data is recovered from the equality of (unmarked) length spectra using the
C^{1}
-proximity of
f_{k-1}:=h_{k-1}\circ f\circ h_{k-1}^{-1}
and
g
, and the sparsity assumption. Note that, by the inductive construction,
(f_{k-1}-g)|{\mathbb{P}_{k-1}^{g}}=0
, and hence if the top norm is bounded as
\|f_{k-1}-g\|_{C^{2,1}}\leqslant M
, then by Rolle’s theorem,
\|f_{k-1}-g\|_{C^{1}}\leqslant M^{\prime}\cdot\Lambda^{-(k+1)}
, where
\Lambda
is the minimal expansion rate of
g
, and
M=M(g),M^{\prime}=M^{\prime}(g)
are some constants that depend only on
g
. The last estimate uses the fact that
\mathbb{P}_{k-1}^{g}
is
O(\Lambda^{-k})
-dense in
\mathbb{S}^{1}
. Finally, if two maps
f_{k-1}
and
g
are sufficiently close in
C^{1}
-norm, then one can recover markings of their length spectrum up to the period that depends on
\|f_{k-1}-g\|_{C^{1}}
and the sparsity parameters.

1.4.
The structure of the paper

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we start with some preliminaries from analysis and on the Whitney Extension Theorem. In Section
3
, we prove some facts about combinatorics and distortion bounds of expanding circle endomorphisms. In particular, we discuss recovery of the length spectrum under proximity in the
C^{1}
norm in Section
3.5
. We use the Whitney Extension Theorem in Section
4
to construct a smooth adjustment for a pair of expanding circle maps that fixes periodic points of a given period, provided the two maps are close in the
C^{0}
norm. In Section
5
, we prove a quantitative Livšic theorem with exponentially small bounds (Theorem
5.1
for smooth maps, and Theorem
5.2
for Hölder continuous maps). The proof is based on considering special types of periodic orbits which we call
messengers
(see Figure
2
). Section
5
culminates with Sub-section
5.3
, where we use the sharp quantitative Livšic theorem to establish sharp distortion estimates with exponential bounds under measure-preserving normalization (Theorem
5.9
). In Section
7
, we combine all ingredients together and give an inductive proof of the Main Theorem. This is achieved by inductively building a sequence of adjustments (i.e., changes of coordinates) with certain properties, as described in Figure
3
. Finally, in Section
7
, we prove the Main Proposition, by providing a counterexample to the general length spectral rigidity.

2.
Preliminaries from analysis and on extension theorems

2.1.
Some preliminaries from calculus

For
r\in[0,+\infty)
, a map
g\colon\mathbb{S}^{1}\to\mathbb{S}^{1}
belongs to class
C^{r}(\mathbb{S}^{1})
if
g
is continuous, has
k:=\lfloor r\rfloor
well-defined continuous derivatives, and
g^{(k)}
is Hölder continuous with exponent
\alpha:=\{r\}
. The functions in
C^{r}(\mathbb{S}^{1})
are equipped with the norm:

•

if
\alpha=0
, i.e.
k=r
, then

\|g\|_{C^{k}}:=\max_{n\in\{0,\ldots,k\}}\max_{x\in\mathbb{S}^{1}}\left|g^{(n)}(x)\right|;

•

if
\alpha\neq 0
, then

\|g\|_{C^{r}}:=\max\left\{\|g\|_{C^{k}},\sup_{x,y\in\mathbb{S}^{1};x\neq y}\frac{\left|g^{(k)}(x)-g^{(k)}(y)\right|}{|x-y|^{\alpha}}\right\}.

We say that the function
g
is in the class
C^{m,1}(\mathbb{S}^{1})
,
m\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
, if
g
is
m
times continuously differentiable and the
m
th derivative
g^{(m)}
is Lipschitz. This space is equipped with the norm

\|g\|_{C^{m,1}}:=\max\left\{\|g\|_{C^{m}},\text{Lip}_{g^{(m)}}\right\},

where
\text{Lip}_{g^{(m)}}
is the optimal Lipschitz constant of
g^{(m)}
. The spaces
C^{r}
and
C^{m,1}
, equipped with the respective norms, are Banach spaces.

We will use the notation

\|g\|_{\text{Lip}}:=\text{Lip}_{g}

for a Lipschitz continuous function
g
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
f,g\colon\mathbb{S}^{1}\to\mathbb{S}^{1}
be, respectively, a
C^{s,1}
-smooth and
C^{s+1,1}
-smooth maps of the circle (not necessarily bijective),
s\geqslant 1
, and let
h\colon\mathbb{S}^{1}\to\mathbb{S}^{1}
be a
C^{s,1}
-smooth diffeomorphism. Assume that

\|h-\id\|_{C^{s,1}}\leqslant\frac{1}{2},\quad\|f\|_{C^{s,1}}\leqslant M,\quad\|g\|_{C^{s+1,1}}\leqslant M,

for some
M>0
. Then
h\circ f\circ h^{-1}
is
C^{s,1}
-smooth, and there is a constant
T=T(s,M)
such that

\|h\circ f\circ h^{-1}-g\|_{C^{s,1}}\leqslant T\big(\|h-\id\|_{C^{s,1}}+\|f-g\|_{C^{s,1}}\big).

(2.2)

Proof.

By direct computation, first observe that if
u
and
v
are some
C^{t,1}
functions, then

\|u\circ v\|_{C^{t,1}}\leqslant\|u\|_{C^{t,1}}\cdot\|v\|_{C^{t,1}}\big(1+o_{t}(\|v\|_{C^{t,1}})\big)

(2.3)

(where the ‘small-o’
o_{t}
depends on
t
). Using this estimate, if
w
is another
C^{t,1}
-smooth function, then

\|u\circ v\circ w\|_{C^{t,1}}\leqslant\|u\|_{C^{t,1}}\cdot\|v\|_{C^{t,1}}\cdot\|w\|_{C^{t,1}}\big(1+o_{t}(\|v\|_{C^{t,1}})+o_{t}(\|w\|_{C^{t,1}})\big).

(2.4)

Let us now denote
\varepsilon_{0}:=\|f-g\|_{C^{s,1}}
and
\delta_{0}:=\|h-\id\|_{C^{s,1}}
. We decompose as follows:

h(x)=x+\varepsilon_{0}\cdot\hat{h}(x),\quad f(x)=g(x)+\delta_{0}\cdot\hat{u}(x),

where both
\hat{h}
and
\hat{u}
are
C^{s,1}
-smooth and satisfy
\|\hat{u}\|_{C^{s,1}}\leqslant 1
,
\|\hat{h}\|_{C^{s,1}}\leqslant 1
. We get

h\circ f\circ h^{-1}(x)=f\circ h^{-1}(x)+\varepsilon_{0}\cdot\hat{h}\big(f\circ h^{-1}(x)\big)=g\circ h^{-1}(x)+\delta_{0}\cdot\hat{u}\big(h^{-1}(x)\big)+\varepsilon_{0}\cdot\hat{h}\big(f\circ h^{-1}(x)\big).

(2.5)

By (
2.3
) and (
2.4
), using the bounds from the assumption of the lemma, as well as the bounds for
\hat{u}
and
\hat{h}
, we can find a constant
\widetilde{T}_{1}=\widetilde{T}_{1}(s,M)
(that also explicitly depends on the bound
\varepsilon_{0}\leqslant 1/2
) such that

\|\hat{u}\circ h^{-1}\|_{C^{s,1}}\leqslant\widetilde{T}_{1},

\|\hat{h}\circ f\circ h^{-1}\|_{C^{s,1}}\leqslant\widetilde{T}_{1}.

Therefore, combining these estimates into (
2.5
), we have:

\|h\circ f\circ h^{-1}-g\|_{C^{s,1}}\leqslant\|g\circ h^{-1}-g\|_{C^{s,1}}+\widetilde{T}_{1}(\varepsilon_{0}+\delta_{0}).

Let us now estimate
\|g\circ h^{-1}-g\|_{C^{s,1}}
. By induction (on
s
), one can show that there exists a constant
\widetilde{T}_{2}=\widetilde{T}_{2}(s,M)
such that

\|g\circ h^{-1}-g\|_{C^{s,1}}\leqslant\widetilde{T}_{2}\cdot\delta_{0}\cdot\|g\|_{C^{s+1,1}}.

By assumption of the lemma,
\|g\|_{C^{s+1,1}}\leqslant M
. Hence,

\|h\circ f\circ h^{-1}-g\|_{C^{k}}\leqslant\widetilde{T}_{2}\cdot M\cdot\delta_{0}+\widetilde{T}_{1}(\varepsilon_{0}+\delta_{0}),

and the claim of the lemma follows with
T:=\widetilde{T}_{2}\cdot M+\widetilde{T}_{1}
.
∎

In a similar way as in the previous lemma, one can show the following (we omit the proof, which is by direct computation).

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
h_{i}\colon\mathbb{S}^{1}\to\mathbb{S}^{1}
,
i\in\{1,2,3\}
be a triple of
C^{s,1}
-smooth circle diffeomorphisms,
s\geqslant 1
, such that

\|h_{i}-\id\|_{C^{s,1}}\leqslant\varepsilon_{i},\quad i\in\{1,2,3\}.

Then there exist a polynomial
Q_{s}
of degree at most
s
with coefficients that depend only on
s
such that

\|h_{2}\circ h_{1}-\id\|_{C^{s,1}}\leqslant\varepsilon_{1}+\varepsilon_{2}+\varepsilon_{1}\varepsilon_{2}\cdot Q_{s}(\varepsilon_{1}),

\displaystyle\|h_{3}\circ h_{2}\circ h_{1}-\id\|_{C^{s,1}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\varepsilon_{1}+\varepsilon_{2}+\varepsilon_{3}+\varepsilon_{1}\varepsilon_{2}Q_{s}(\varepsilon_{1})

\displaystyle+\varepsilon_{1}\varepsilon_{3}Q_{s}(\varepsilon_{1})+\varepsilon_{2}\varepsilon_{3}Q_{s}(\varepsilon_{2})

\displaystyle+\varepsilon_{1}\varepsilon_{2}\varepsilon_{3}Q_{s}(\varepsilon_{1}).

In particular, if

\|h_{i}-\id\|_{C^{s,1}}\leqslant\varepsilon_{i}<M,\quad i\in\{1,2,3\},

then there exists a constant
Q=Q(s,M)
such that

\|h_{3}\circ h_{2}\circ h_{1}-\id\|_{C^{s,1}}\leqslant\|h_{1}-\id\|_{C^{s,1}}+Q\cdot\big(\|h_{2}-\id\|_{C^{s,1}}+\|h_{3}-\id\|_{C^{s,1}}\big).

∎

2.2.
Rolle’s Theorem and its application

We say that a continuous function on
\mathbb{S}^{1}
has
roots with density
D
if every segment of length
D
in
\mathbb{S}^{1}
contains a root of the function.

Lemma 2.3

(Improvement by density)
.

Let
g\in C^{0,1}(\mathbb{S}^{1})
be a Lipschitz function with
\|g\|_{\text{Lip}}\leqslant\sigma
. Assume
g
has roots with density
D
. Then
\|g\|_{C^{0}}\leqslant\|g\|_{\text{Lip}}\cdot D\leqslant\sigma D
. In particular, the conclusion holds for
g\in C^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{1})
with
\|g^{\prime}\|_{C^{0}}\leqslant\sigma
.

Similarly, if
g\in C^{\alpha}(\mathbb{S}^{1})
with
\|g\|_{C^{\alpha}}\leqslant\sigma
and density of roots
D
, then
\|g\|_{C^{0}}\leqslant\sigma D^{\alpha}
.

Proof.

For a point
x\in\mathbb{S}^{1}
, let
r
be the closest to
x
root of
g
. Then

|g(x)-g(r)|=|g(x)|\leqslant\text{Lip}_{g}\cdot|x-r|\leqslant\sigma D.

Similarly in the smooth and Hölder cases.
∎

The following simple lemma is a corollary to the previous lemma and Rolle’s theorem:

Lemma 2.4

(À la Rolle)
.

For
m\in\mathbb{N}
, let
g
be either

•

(smooth case)

C^{m+1}(\mathbb{S}^{1})
, or

•

(Lipschitz case)

C^{m,1}(\mathbb{S}^{1})
, or

•

(Hölder case)

C^{m+\alpha}(\mathbb{S}^{1})
,

and has roots with density
D>0
. Assume that the highest norm of
g
is bounded by
\sigma>0
, i.e.

•

(smooth case)

\|g^{(m+1)}\|_{C^{0}}\leqslant\sigma
;

•

(Lipschitz case)

\|g^{(m)}\|_{\text{Lip}}\leqslant\sigma
;

•

(Hölder case)

\|g^{(m)}\|_{C^{\alpha}}\leqslant\sigma
.

Then for each
s\in\{0,\ldots,m\}
, the following
exponential improvement of
C^{s}
-norms with factor
D
holds true:

•

in the smooth and Lipschitz cases,

\left\|g^{(s)}\right\|_{C^{0}}\leqslant\frac{(m+1)!}{s!}\cdot D^{m-s+1}\cdot\sigma;

•

in the Hölder case,

\left\|g^{(s)}\right\|_{C^{0}}\leqslant\frac{(m+1)!}{s!}\cdot D^{m-s+\alpha}\cdot\sigma.

Proof.

By the classical Rolle Theorem, if
g
has roots with density
D
on the circle, then
g^{\prime}
has roots with density
2D
,
g^{\prime\prime}
has roots with density
3D
, and so on,
g^{(m)}
has roots with density
(m+1)D
. Applying Lemma
2.3
inductively, we get the required bounds.
∎

2.3.
The Whitney extension theorem

For a real-valued function
h
defined on a set of
m+1
points
\{y_{0},\ldots,y_{m}\}
in the real line (or the circle), the
m
-th order divided difference

\Delta^{m}h[y_{0},\ldots,y_{m}]
is defined inductively as

\Delta^{m}h[y_{0},\ldots,y_{m}]:=\frac{\Delta^{m-1}h[y_{1},\ldots,y_{m}]-\Delta^{m-1}h[y_{0},\ldots,y_{m-1}]}{y_{m}-y_{0}},\quad\Delta^{0}h[y_{j}]:=h(y_{j}).

Suppose now we are given an ordered sequence
x_{0}<\ldots<x_{s}
of
s+1
points in the circle, and let
E:=\{x_{0},x_{1},\ldots,x_{s}\}
. Let
h\colon E\to\mathbb{S}^{1}
be a function. We will be interested in smooth extensions of the discrete function
h
with explicit control on the norm of the extension. For that, we will use a sharp form of the one-dimensional Whitney extension theorem (see, e.g.,
[
Sh
,
Fef
]
and references therein):

Theorem 2.5

(Sharp Graded Whitney Extension Theorem)
.

Let
m\geqslant 2
and
h\colon E\to\mathbb{S}^{1}
be a discrete function defined on the set
E=\{x_{0},\ldots,x_{s}\}
of points in
\mathbb{S}^{1}
ordered as
x_{0}<x_{1}<\ldots<x_{s}<x_{0}
. For each
j\in\{1,\ldots,m\}
, define

\mathcal{D}_{j}(h,E):=\max_{0\leqslant i\leqslant s}\left|\Delta^{j}h[x_{i},\ldots,x_{i+j}]\right|

to be the largest
j
-th order divided difference (where the indices are taken modulo
s+1
). Then
h
extends to a
C^{m-1,1}
-smooth map
h\colon\mathbb{S}^{1}\to\mathbb{S}^{1}
so that, for each
j\in\{1,\ldots,m\}
,

\left\|h\right\|_{C^{j-1,1}}\leqslant\widetilde{W}\cdot\mathcal{D}_{j}(h,E),

where
\widetilde{W}>0
is a constant that depends only on
m
. ∎

Remark 2.6
.

Note that if
g\in C^{m}(\mathbb{S}^{1})
for some integer
m
, then
\|g\|_{C^{j}}\leqslant\|g\|_{C^{j-1,1}}
for every integer
j\leqslant m
. And hence in the theorem above, one has bounds on
C^{j}
-norms for each
j\leqslant m-1
.

Remark 2.7
.

In fact, in Theorem
2.5
a stronger conclusion holds: the
C^{j-1,1}
-norm of
h
is comparable to the
j
th order divided difference with multiplicative coefficients that depend only on
m
. However, we will use only the bound from above.

3.
Preliminaries on expanding circle maps

3.1.
Distortion estimates for expanding circle endomorphisms

The following lemma is folklore:

Lemma 3.1

(Distortion with the image length)
.

Let
u\colon\mathbb{S}^{1}\to\mathbb{S}^{1}
be at least
C^{1,1}
-smooth expanding circle endomorphism with
|u^{\prime}|\geqslant\Lambda>1
. Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
I\subset\mathbb{S}^{1}
be an interval so that
u^{n}|_{I}
is injective; define
J:=u^{n}(I)
to be image interval. Then

\frac{\left|\left(u^{n}\right)^{\prime}(y)\right|}{\left|\left(u^{n}\right)^{\prime}(z)\right|}\leqslant e^{\frac{\|u^{\prime}\|_{\text{Lip}}}{\Lambda-1}\cdot|J|}\text{ for every pair }y,z\in I.

(3.6)

Proof.

The proof is similar to the proof of
[
SS
, Lemma 1]
, so we present it only for completeness.

If
d(y,z,I)
is the distance between a pair of points
y,z\in\mathbb{S}^{1}
measured along the interval
I
, then by expansion for each
j\leqslant n
we have

d\left(u^{j}(y),u^{j}(z),u^{j}(I)\right)\leqslant\Lambda^{-n+j}\cdot d\left(u^{n}(y),u^{n}(z),J\right)\leqslant\Lambda^{-n+j}\cdot|J|.

Note that on the interval
[\alpha,+\infty)
logarithm satisfies
|\log(x)-\log(y)|\leqslant 1/\alpha|x-y|
. Hence,

\displaystyle\left|\log|u^{\prime}(u^{j}(y))|-\log|u^{\prime}(u^{j}(z))|\right|

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{1}{\Lambda}\left||u^{\prime}(u^{j}(y))|-|u^{\prime}(u^{j}(z))|\right|

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{1}{\Lambda}\|u^{\prime}\|_{\text{Lip}}\cdot d\left(u^{j}(y),u^{j}(z),u^{j}(I)\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant{\|u^{\prime}\|_{\text{Lip}}}\cdot\Lambda^{-n+j-1}\cdot|J|.

Finally, since
\Lambda>1
,

\left|\log\frac{|(u^{n})^{\prime}(y)|}{|(u^{n})^{\prime}(z)|}\right|\leqslant\sum_{j=0}^{n}\left|\log\frac{|u^{\prime}(u^{j}(y))|}{|u^{\prime}(u^{j}(z))|}\right|\leqslant\|u^{\prime}\|_{\text{Lip}}\cdot|J|\cdot\sum_{j=0}^{n}\frac{1}{\Lambda^{n-j+1}}<\frac{\|u^{\prime}\|_{\text{Lip}}}{\Lambda-1}\cdot|J|.

The distortion bound (
3.6
) follows.
∎

3.2.
Combinatorics of periodic points

Fix an integer
d\geqslant 2
. In is well-known that every expanding circle map of degree
d
is topologically conjugate to the linear map. A linear degree
d
expanding map of the circle in
\mathcal{E}^{r}_{d}
has the form
L_{d}\colon x\mapsto d\cdot x\mod 1
. For a given period
k
, this map has exactly
d^{k}-1
periodic points of period
k
. We call them
linear
periodic points. They are equally spaced with distance
1/(d^{k}-1)
and

x_{k,s}^{L}:=\frac{s}{d^{k}-1}\mod 1,\quad s\in\mathbb{N}.

Let us denote by
\mathbb{P}_{k}^{L}
the set of periodic points of period
k
for
L_{d}
. In a similar way, we will denote
\mathbb{P}_{k}^{f}
the set of periodic points of period
k
for any other expanding circle endomorphism
f\in\mathcal{E}^{r}_{d}
.

We say that a periodic point
x
has
irreducible period
p
if
x
is not a periodic point of any lower period; this means that
p
is prime.

Both sets
\mathbb{P}^{L}_{k}
and
\mathbb{P}^{f}_{k}
come with natural cyclic ordering. Since
f
and
L_{d}
are topologically conjugate, the orders of the corresponding (under this conjugation) periodic points for
f
and for
L_{d}
are the same. Corresponding to the linear periodic point
x^{L}_{k,s}
there is a nonlinear point
x^{f}_{k,s}
. Under our normalization,
\{0\}=\mathbb{P}_{1}^{f}
.

In degree
d
, between any pair of period
k
points there are exactly
d
period
k+1
points.

3.3.
Density and sparsity of
\mathbb{P}_{k}^{g}

Denote by
{o}_{k}
the smallest distance between points in
\mathbb{P}_{k}^{g}
, and by
{O}_{k}
the largest distance between pairs of consecutive points in
\mathbb{P}_{k}^{g}
. By definition,
{o}_{k}
is the
sparsity
of points of period
k
, while
{O}_{k}
is their
density
.

Lemma 3.2

(Estimates on the gaps in
\mathbb{P}_{k}^{g}
)
.

Let
I=[x^{-},x^{+}]
be the interval between a pair of consecutive points in
\mathbb{P}_{k}^{g}
. Then

\frac{1}{N_{g}\cdot e^{\lambda_{g}(x^{\pm})}-1}\leqslant|I|\leqslant\frac{1}{N_{g}^{-1}\cdot e^{\lambda_{g}(x^{\pm})}-1},

where
N_{g}\geqslant 1
is a measure of non-linearity of
g
given explicitly by

N_{g}=\frac{\|g^{\prime}\|_{C^{0}}}{\Lambda}\cdot e^{\frac{\|g\|_{\text{Lip}}}{\Lambda-1}}.

In particular,

\frac{1}{N_{g}\cdot\|g^{\prime}\|_{C^{0}}^{k}-1}\leqslant|I|\leqslant\frac{1}{N_{g}^{-1}\cdot\Lambda^{k}-1},

and hence

{O}_{k}\leqslant\frac{1}{N_{g}^{-1}\cdot\Lambda^{k}-1},\quad\quad{o}_{k}\geqslant\frac{1}{N_{g}\cdot\|g^{\prime}\|_{C^{0}}^{k}-1}.

Proof.

Follows from the distortion estimate in Lemma
3.1
.
∎

We will need one more corollary to Lemma
3.2
. Recall that
\Lambda:=\min_{x\in S}g^{\prime}(x)
denotes the expansion factor of
g
. Now assume that

\Omega:=\|g^{\prime}\|_{C^{0}},\quad\text{ and }\quad\Omega=\Lambda^{a}\text{ for some }a\geqslant 1.

Clearly, if
a=1
, then the function
g
is linear.

Corollary 3.3
.

In the notation of Lemma
3.2
, there exist a constant
C\geqslant 1
and
\kappa_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
(depending on
g
) so that for all
k\geqslant\kappa_{0}
,

C^{-1}\cdot\Lambda^{-a\cdot k}\leqslant{o}_{k}\leqslant{O}_{k}\leqslant C\cdot\Lambda^{-k}.

∎

3.4.
Length spectrum

Let
g\in\mathcal{E}_{d}^{r}
be an expanding endomorphisms of the circle. Recall that the length spectrum of
g
is defined as

\Lyap(g)=\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\Lyap_{n}(g)=\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\left\{\lambda_{f}(p)\colon p\in\mathbb{P}_{n}^{g}\right\},

where
\lambda_{f}(p)
is the
\log
-multiplier at the periodic point
p\in\mathbb{P}_{n}^{g}
of period
n
.

Lemma 3.4

(Basic properties of the length spectrum)
.

For a
C^{1}
-smooth expanding circle endomorphism
g
:

•

\Lyap_{n}(g)
is a non-empty subset of
\big[n\log\Lambda,n\log\Omega\big]
;

•

\Lyap(g)
is a non-empty, unbounded subset of
\mathbb{R}_{+}
;

•

\Lyap_{n}(g)
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
\Lyap(g)
are invariant under
C^{1}
-smooth conjugacies;

•

\Lyap_{n}(g)
is a point for every
n
if and only if
g
is a linear map

Proof.

The first and the third items follow by the chain rule. The second item follows from the first. The forth item is the well-known rigidity result.
∎

3.5.
Marked length spectrum and recovering of markings

It is known that
f\in\mathcal{E}^{r}_{d}
and
g\in\mathcal{E}_{d}^{r}
are topologically conjugate via a homeomorphism
\varphi\colon\mathbb{S}^{1}\to\mathbb{S}^{1}
:

g=\varphi\circ f\circ\varphi^{-1}.

In fact,
\varphi\in C^{\alpha}(\mathbb{S}^{1})
for some
\alpha\in(0,1)
. Since the topological conjugacy respects periodic points,
\varphi
gives rise to the notion of
corresponding
periodic points, namely, periodic points
p^{f}
for
f
and
p^{g}
for
g
are
corresponding
if
p^{f}=\varphi(p^{g})
. The periods of corresponding periodic points are equal. This correspondence provides a
marking
of periodic orbits. We call
\varphi
the
marking conjugacy
.

Proposition 3.5

(Closeness of Hölder conjugacy to identity)
.

Let
f
,
g\in\mathcal{E}^{1}_{d}
be a pair of expanding circle endomorphisms. Let
\varphi\in C^{\alpha}(\mathbb{S}^{1})
be the Hölder conjugacy between
f
and
g
such that
\varphi\circ f\circ\varphi^{-1}=g
,
\varphi(0)=0
. Assume that the minimal expansion factor of
g
is
\Lambda>1
. Then

\|\varphi-\id\|_{C^{0}}\leqslant\frac{1}{\Lambda-1}\|f-g\|_{C^{0}}.

In particular, for each pair of corresponding periodic points
p^{f}
and
p^{g}
,

\left|p^{f}-p^{g}\right|\leqslant\frac{1}{\Lambda-1}\|f-g\|_{C^{0}}.

Proof.

Denote
{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_{0}:=\|f-g\|_{C^{0}}
. Fix
s\in\mathbb{N}
. Define

\sigma_{s}:=\max_{p\in\mathbb{P}^{g}_{s}}\left|\varphi(p)-p\right|=|\varphi(p^{*})-p^{*}|,\text{ where }p^{*}\in\mathbb{P}^{g}_{s}.

We claim that
\sigma_{s}\leqslant{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_{0}/(\Lambda-1)
. Indeed,

f\left(\varphi(p^{*})\right)=\varphi\left(g(p^{*})\right)\Rightarrow f(p^{*}+\sigma_{s})=\varphi\left(g(p^{*})\right),

where we assumed that
\varphi(p^{*})=p^{*}+\sigma_{s}
(the case
\varphi(p^{*})=p^{*}-\sigma_{s}
is treated similarly). Since
g(p^{*})
is also a periodic point of period
s
for
g
,

\varphi\left(g(p^{*})\right)=g(p^{*})+\sigma_{s}^{\prime},\text{ where }\left|\sigma_{s}^{\prime}\right|\leqslant\sigma_{s}.

Moreover, by the assumption of the lemma,
f(p^{*}+\sigma_{s})=g(p^{*}+\sigma_{s})+\delta^{\prime}
, where
|\delta^{\prime}|\leqslant{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_{0}
. Hence,

g(p^{*}+\sigma_{s})-g(p^{*})=\sigma_{s}^{\prime}-\delta^{\prime}.

But
g
is an expanding map with expansion factor at least
\Lambda>1
. Thus

\Lambda\cdot\sigma_{s}\leqslant\left|g(p^{*}+\sigma_{s})-g(p^{*})\right|\leqslant\left|\sigma_{s}^{\prime}-\delta^{\prime}\right|\leqslant\sigma_{s}+{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_{0},

and the claim follows.

Now, for every
x\in\mathbb{S}^{1}
, choose a sequence
y_{s}\in\mathbb{P}_{s}^{g}
(at most one per each period) so that
y_{s}\to x
as
s\to\infty
(we are using the fact that periodic points of
g
are dense in
\mathbb{S}^{1}
). By the claim above,

|\varphi(y_{s})-y_{s}|\leqslant\frac{{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_{0}}{\Lambda-1}.

Passing to the limit as
s\to\infty
and using the continuity of
\varphi
, we obtain the same bound for
|\varphi(x)-x|
.
∎

The maps
f
and
g
has the same
marked length spectra
if

\lambda_{f}\left(p^{f}\right)=\lambda_{g}\left(p^{g}\right)

for every pair
p^{f},p^{g}
of corresponding periodic points. Similarly, we will say that
f
and
g
have the same marked length spectra
up to period
N
if the equality of the
\log
-multipliers holds for all corresponding periodic points up to period
N
. Now we want to understand under which conditions on
f
and
g
we can
recover the markings up to period
N
, that is, to conclude that
f
and
g
have the same marked length spectrum up period
N
.

Lemma 3.6

(Proximity of
\log
-multipliers for corresponding periodic points)
.

Let
f,g\in\mathcal{E}^{1}_{d}
be a pair of expanding circle endomorphisms, and let

\|f-g\|_{C^{0}}=:{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_{0},\quad\|f^{\prime}-g^{\prime}\|_{C^{0}}=:{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_{1}.

Let
x^{f}\in\mathbb{P}_{k}^{f}
and
x^{g}\in\mathbb{P}_{k}^{g}
be a pair of corresponding periodic points of period
k\geqslant 1
. Then

\left|\lambda_{f}(x^{f})-\lambda_{g}(x^{g})\right|\leqslant\frac{k}{\Lambda_{0}}\left(\frac{\min\{\|f^{\prime}\|_{\text{Lip}},\|g^{\prime}\|_{\text{Lip}}\}}{\Lambda_{0}-1}\cdot{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_{0}+{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_{1}\right),

(3.7)

where
\Lambda_{0}
is the minimal expansion factor of
f
and
g
.

Proof.

Let us denote the orbit of
x^{f}=:x_{0}^{f}
under
f
as
x_{s}^{f}:=f^{s}\left(x^{f}\right)
,
s\in\{0,\ldots,k-1\}
, and let us introduce a similar notation
x_{s}^{g}
for the orbit of
x^{g}
under
g
. By Proposition
3.5
, for each
s
,

\left|x_{s}^{f}-x_{s}^{g}\right|\leqslant\frac{1}{\Lambda_{0}-1}{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_{0}.

We have

\lambda_{f}(x^{f})=\sum_{s=0}^{k-1}\log f^{\prime}\left(x_{s}^{f}\right),

and a similar sum for
g
(recall that our standing assumption is that both
f
and
g
are increasing). Let us estimate the difference of the corresponding terms:

\displaystyle\left|\log f^{\prime}\left(x_{s}^{f}\right)-\log g^{\prime}\left(x_{s}^{g}\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{1}{\Lambda_{0}}\left|f^{\prime}\left(x_{s}^{f}\right)-g^{\prime}\left(x_{s}^{g}\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{1}{\Lambda_{0}}\left(\left|f^{\prime}\left(x_{s}^{f}\right)-f^{\prime}\left(x_{s}^{g}\right)\right|+\left|f^{\prime}\left(x_{s}^{g}\right)-g^{\prime}\left(x_{s}^{g}\right)\right|\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{1}{\Lambda_{0}}\left(\frac{\|f^{\prime}\|_{\text{Lip}}}{\Lambda_{0}-1}\cdot{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_{0}+{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_{1}\right).

Exchanging the roles of
f
and
g
in the estimate above and summing up, (
3.7
) follows.
∎

We will use Lemma
3.6
in the form of the following corollary:

Corollary 3.7

(Proximity of
\log
-multipliers for corresponding periodic points)
.

If
f,g\in\mathcal{E}^{1}_{d}
are such that
\|f^{\prime}-g^{\prime}\|_{\text{Lip}}=:{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_{2}
and
\|f^{\prime}-g^{\prime}\|_{C^{0}}=:{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_{1}<{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_{2}
, then for the corresponding points of period
k
,

\left|\lambda_{f}(x^{f})-\lambda_{g}(x^{g})\right|\leqslant\mathcal{K}\cdot k\cdot{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_{1},

(3.8)

where
\mathcal{K}
can be chosen to depend only
\|g\|_{C^{1,1}}
for all small enough
{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_{2}
. ∎

Proof.

The corollary is immediate from Lemma
3.6
. The only thing that should be traced is the dependence of the constant. But that follows from the estimates on the explicit factors using
\|f-g\|_{C^{0}}\leqslant\|f^{\prime}-g^{\prime}\|_{C^{0}}
and
\big|\|f^{\prime}\|_{\text{Lip}}-\|g^{\prime}\|_{\text{Lip}}\big|\leqslant{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_{2}
.
∎

Now we want to understand now to recover at least some information about the
marked
length spectra from the length spectra for a pair of nearby maps under the sparsity assumption.

Lemma 3.8

(Recovering partial marking)
.

Suppose
f,g\in\mathcal{E}^{1}_{d}
are such that

1.

\|f-g\|_{C^{1,1}}=:{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_{2}
,
\|f-g\|_{C^{1}}=:{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_{1}<{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_{2}
,

2.

\Lyap(f)=\Lyap(g)
, and

3.

\Lyap(g)
is
(\beta,\gamma)
-sparse.

Then for every
\eta>1
there exists
\kappa_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
(depending only on
\eta
,
g
, and for small enough
{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_{2}
) such that for all
k
with
\kappa_{0}\leqslant k\leqslant N
, where

N=\left\lfloor-\frac{1}{\eta\beta a}\cdot\frac{\log{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_{1}}{\log\Lambda}\right\rfloor,

the
\log
-multipliers at the corresponding periodic points of period
k
for
f
and
g
are at distance at most
C_{\gamma}\cdot\Lambda^{-\gamma k}
.

Proof.

Let
x^{f}\in\mathbb{P}_{k}^{f}
. By Lemma
3.4
, all
\log
-multipliers of periodic points of period
k
for
g
, i.e., the set
\Lyap_{k}(g)
, lie in the interval
I_{k}:=[k\log\Lambda,k\log\Omega]
. One of this multipliers is equal to
\lambda_{f}(x^{f})
. By the
(\beta,\gamma)
-sparsity assumption (
1.1
), we have that for all
\ell_{1},\ell_{2}\in I_{k}
,

\text{either }|\ell_{1}-\ell_{2}|\geqslant C_{\beta}\cdot e^{-\beta\max\{\ell_{1},\ell_{2}\}}\geqslant{C_{\beta}}\cdot{\Omega^{-\beta k}},\quad\text{ or }|\ell_{1}-\ell_{2}|\leqslant C_{\gamma}\cdot e^{-\gamma\min\{\ell_{1},\ell_{2}\}}\leqslant{C_{\gamma}}\cdot{\Lambda^{-\gamma k}}.

If

\mathcal{K}\cdot k\cdot{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_{1}<C_{\beta}\cdot\Omega^{-\beta k},

(3.9)

then in the
\mathcal{K}\cdot k\cdot{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_{1}
-neighborhood of
\lambda_{f}(x^{f})
there are no other elements of
\Lyap(g)\cap I_{k}
, and in particular, there are no elements of
\Lyap_{k}(g)
except those at distance

. By Corollary
3.7
, the
\log
-multipliers

and
\lambda_{g}(x^{g})
are at most

apart. It is straightforward to check that assumption (

) is satisfied if we assume that

, where

is chosen so that

\log k+\beta k\cdot\log\Omega-\log C_{\beta}+\log\mathcal{K}<\eta\beta k\log\Omega\quad\text{ and }\quad N=\left\lfloor-\frac{\log{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_{1}}{\eta\beta\log\Omega}\right\rfloor.

Finally, in our notation

, and the claim follows.

3.6.
Invariant densities and measure-preserving adjustments

In this section, we are interested in the question of how to adjust a map
f\in\mathcal{E}^{r}_{d}
so that the adjusted map preserves the Lebesgue measure. We follow the idea of
[
SS
, Corollary 4]
, but we need a quantitative version of it. Every expanding circle map
f\in\mathcal{E}_{d}^{r}
there exists a unique invariant probability measure with
C^{r-1,1}
-smooth density
\theta_{f}
. In particular, if

h_{\theta}(x)=\int\limits_{0}^{x}\theta_{f}\,d\lambda,

where
\lambda
is the Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{S}^{1}
, then
\tilde{f}:=h_{\theta}\circ f\circ h_{\theta}^{-1}\in\mathcal{E}^{r}_{d}
preserves the Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{S}^{1}

[
SS
]
. Clearly, if
f
preserves the Lebesgue measure, then
\theta_{f}=1
.

We will use the notation
\hat{\mathcal{E}}^{r}_{d}\subset\mathcal{E}^{r}_{d}
to denote the class of
C^{r,1}
-smooth expanding circle maps that preserve the Lebesgue measure. By the uniqueness of
\theta_{f}
, each smooth conjugacy class in
\mathcal{E}^{r}_{d}
intersects
\hat{\mathcal{E}}^{r}_{d}
at exactly one point.

Proposition 3.9

(Invariant density estimates)
.

Let
g\in\hat{\mathcal{E}}_{d}^{r}
and
f\in\mathcal{E}^{r}_{d}
be such that

\|f-g\|_{C^{s,1}}=:{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_{s+1},\quad s\in\{1,\ldots,r\},\quad{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_{r+1}\geqslant{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_{r}\geqslant\ldots\geqslant{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_{1}.

Then there exists a constant
\mathcal{N}
(that depends only on
r
) such that the invariant density
\theta_{f}
satisfies

\|\theta_{f}-1\|_{C^{t,1}}\leqslant\mathcal{N}\cdot{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_{t+2},\quad t\in\{1,\ldots,r-1\}.

A similar estimate holds for
h_{\theta}
:

\|h_{\theta}-\id\|_{C^{t,1}}\leqslant\mathcal{N}\cdot{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_{t+1},\quad t\in\{1,\ldots,r\}.

Proof.

For the proof follows by analysis of the transfer operator for expanding circle maps, see
[
Sa
]
and
[
BY
]
.

3.7.
Starting Hölder conjugacy

We will need the following lemma about the Hölder exponent of the topological conjugacy between a pair of expanding circle maps.

Lemma 3.10

(Starting Hölder conjugacy)
.

Let
f,g\in\mathcal{E}^{r}_{d}
be two expanding circle endomorphisms, and
\varphi
be the topological conjugacy between
f
and
g
. If

\|f-g\|_{C^{1}}=:{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_{1},\quad\|f-g\|_{C^{0}}=:{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_{0}\ll{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_{1},

then

is a
C^{\alpha}
-Hölder continuous homeomorphism such that

\alpha=1-\frac{{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_{1}}{\Lambda-1}

and

\|\varphi-\id\|_{C^{\alpha}}\leqslant D,

where
D
is some uniform constant.

Proof.

Let
x,y\in\mathbb{S}^{1}
be a pair of points. For a given
A>0
, let us choose
n\in N
such that

1\leqslant\Lambda_{g}^{n}\cdot|x-y|\cdot A.

(3.10)

Also, let

\alpha=1-\frac{{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_{1}}{\Lambda-1}.

(3.11)

Since
\varphi
is a conjugacy, we can write

|\varphi(x)-\varphi(y)|=|f^{-n}\circ\varphi\circ g^{n}(x)-f^{-n}\circ\varphi\circ g^{n}(y)|

for an appropriate choice of the inverse branch

. Since

, we have

\|f^{-n}\|_{C^{1}}\leqslant\frac{1}{\left(\Lambda-{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_{1}\right)^{n}}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle|\varphi(x)-\varphi(y)|

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{1}{(\Lambda-{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_{1})^{n}}|\varphi\circ g^{n}(x)-\varphi\circ g^{n}(y)|\leqslant\frac{1^{\alpha}}{(\Lambda-{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_{1})^{n}}

\displaystyle\leqslant^{\eqref{Eq:NeededN}}\left(\frac{\Lambda^{\alpha}}{\Lambda-{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_{1}}\right)^{n}\cdot|x-y|^{\alpha}\cdot A^{\alpha}\leqslant A^{\alpha}\cdot|x-y|^{\alpha}

because

\Lambda_{g}^{\alpha}=(\Lambda-1+1)^{\alpha}\leqslant 1+\alpha(\Lambda-1)=^{\eqref{Eq:NeededAlpha}}\Lambda-{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_{1}

Theorem 5.1

(Quantitative Livšic with exponential bounds)

There exists

Suppose there exists a

-smooth function

Theorem 5.2

\displaystyle\left|u(x)+\sum_{s=0}^{kp-1}D\left(g^{s}\left(y_{+}\right)\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leqslant\left|u(x)-\sum_{s=0}^{kp-1}D\left(g^{s}\left(y_{-}\right)\right)\right|+\left|\sum_{s=0}^{kp-1}D\left(g^{s}\left(y_{+}\right)\right)+\sum_{s=0}^{kp-1}D\left(g^{s}\left(y_{-}\right)\right)\right|

\displaystyle=\left|u(x)-\sum_{s=0}^{kp-1}D\left(g^{s}\left(y_{-}\right)\right)\right|+\left|\sum_{s=0}^{2kp-1}D\left(g^{s}\left(y_{-}\right)\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leqslant 2C\cdot{O}_{k}^{p}\cdot\|D^{\prime}\|_{C^{0}},

and the claim follows with

∎

Now we are ready to prove Theorem

.

Proof of Theorem

5.2.
Quantitative Livšic theorem under measure-preserving normalization

The following theorem is a corollary of Theorem
5.2
.

Theorem 5.8

(Proximity of derivatives under measure normalization)
.

Let
f,g\in\hat{\mathcal{E}}^{r}_{2}
be a pair of Lebesgue measure preserving expanding circle maps, and let
\varphi\colon\mathbb{S}^{1}\to\mathbb{S}^{1}
be the
C^{\alpha}
-smooth orientation-preserving conjugacy between
f
and
g
normalized so that
\varphi(0)=0
. Assume that

\|\varphi\|_{C^{\alpha}}\leqslant M,\quad\|f\|_{C^{r,1}}\leqslant M,\quad\|g\|_{C^{r,1}}\leqslant M.

For
\alpha\leqslant\sigma<1
, let
n_{0}
be as in Theorem
5.2
, and further assume that

\big|\lambda_{f}(p^{f})-\lambda_{g}(p^{g})\big|\leqslant M\cdot\Lambda^{-\sigma n}\quad\forall m\in\{4n,4n+1\}\quad\forall p^{g}\in\mathbb{P}_{m}^{g},\,\,p^{f}=\varphi(p^{g})\in\mathbb{P}_{m}^{f}.

(5.25)

Then there exists a constant
H=H(M)
such that

\big\|f^{\prime}\circ\varphi-g^{\prime}\big\|_{C^{0}}\leqslant H\cdot\Lambda^{-\frac{\alpha^{2}n}{a+1}}.

Proof.

Since
f
and
g
preserve Lebesgue measure, it follows that if
\{x_{1},x_{2}\}=f^{-1}(x)
and
\{\hat{x}_{1},\hat{x}_{2}\}=g^{-1}(x)
, then for every
x\in\mathbb{S}^{1}
,

\frac{1}{f^{\prime}(x_{1})}+\frac{1}{f^{\prime}(x_{2})}=1,\quad\frac{1}{g^{\prime}(\hat{x}_{1})}+\frac{1}{g^{\prime}(\hat{x}_{2})}=1.

(5.26)

Now, we apply Theorem
5.2
to
D(x)=\log f^{\prime}(\varphi(x))-\log g^{\prime}(x)
. This is a
C^{\alpha}
-smooth homeomorphism of the circle with the Hölder norm depending on
\|\varphi\|_{D^{\alpha}}
and the smooth norms of
f
and
g
. Hence we can assume that
\|D\|_{C^{\alpha}}\leqslant M
.

Note that if
p^{g}\in\mathbb{P}_{m}^{g}
, and
p^{f}=\varphi(p^{g})
is the corresponding periodic point for
f
, then

\sum_{s=0}^{m-1}D(g^{s}p^{g})=\lambda_{f}(p^{f})-\lambda_{g}(p^{g}).

Hence, by Theorem
5.2
, there exists
u\in C^{\alpha}(\mathbb{S}^{1})
, normalized so that
u(0)=0
, such that

\|D-u\circ g+u\|_{C^{0}}\leqslant L\cdot\Lambda^{-\alpha n}.

Let us define a
C^{1+\alpha}
-smooth orientation-preserving diffeomorphism
\psi
of circle by setting

\psi(x):=\int_{0}^{x}e^{u(t)+q}dt,\quad\psi(0)=0

where
q
is chosen so that
\psi(1)=1
. Explicitly,
q=-\log\left(\int_{0}^{1}e^{u(t)}dt\right)
, and note that
\psi^{\prime}(0)=\psi^{\prime}(1)=e^{q}
. In this way,
u(x)=\log\psi^{\prime}(x)-q
, and hence

\left\|\log f^{\prime}\circ\varphi-\log g^{\prime}-\log\psi^{\prime}\circ g+\log\psi^{\prime}\right\|_{C^{0}}\leqslant L\cdot\Lambda^{-\alpha n}.

Rearranging this estimate, we can find
L_{1}=L_{1}(L,M)
such that

\left|\frac{\psi^{\prime}(g(x))}{f^{\prime}(\varphi(x))}-\frac{\psi^{\prime}(x)}{g^{\prime}(x)}\right|\leqslant L_{1}\cdot\Lambda^{-\alpha n},\quad\forall x\in\mathbb{S}^{1}.

(5.27)

Now write
\psi^{\prime}(x)=e^{q}-\sigma(x)
. In this way,
\sigma
is a Hölder continuous function defined on the circle with

\int_{0}^{1}\sigma(x)dx=0,\quad\sigma(0)=\sigma(1)=0.

We re-write (
5.27
) for the immediate preimages
x_{1}
and
x_{2}
of a point
x\in\mathbb{S}^{1}
:

\left|\frac{\psi^{\prime}(x)}{f^{\prime}({\hat{x}_{1}})}-\frac{\psi^{\prime}(x_{1})}{g^{\prime}(x_{1})}\right|\leqslant L_{1}\cdot\Lambda^{-\alpha n},\quad\left|\frac{\psi^{\prime}(x)}{f^{\prime}({\hat{x}_{2}})}-\frac{\psi^{\prime}(x_{2})}{g^{\prime}(x_{2})}\right|\leqslant L_{1}\cdot\Lambda^{-\alpha n},

where
\hat{x}_{1}
and
\hat{x}_{2}
are the preimages under
g
of the corresponding to
x
point
\hat{x}
. Using (
5.26
), we conclude that

\left|\psi^{\prime}(x)-\left(\frac{\psi^{\prime}(x_{1})}{g^{\prime}(x_{1})}+\frac{\psi^{\prime}(x_{2})}{g^{\prime}(x_{2})}\right)\right|\leqslant 2L_{1}\cdot\Lambda^{-\alpha n},

and re-writing this estimate in terms of
\sigma(x)
(using (
5.26
) again), we get

\left|\sigma(x)-\left(\frac{\sigma(x_{1})}{g^{\prime}(x_{1})}+\frac{\sigma(x_{2})}{g^{\prime}(x_{2})}\right)\right|\leqslant 2L_{1}\cdot\Lambda^{-\alpha n}.

(5.28)

This estimate holds for every
x\in\mathbb{S}^{1}
,
\{x_{1},x_{2}\}=g^{-1}(x)
.

Now suppose
x_{0}\in\mathbb{S}^{1}
is such that
M:=\sigma(x_{0})=\|\sigma\|_{C^{0}}>0
is the maximum of
\sigma
. For
s=\lceil\alpha n/(a+\alpha)\rceil
, consider the set
g^{-s}(x_{0})
. This is a
\Lambda^{-s}
-dense set of points, and if
y\in g^{-s}(x_{0})
, then by inductively applying (
5.28
) we can find a constant
L_{1}^{\prime}=L_{1}^{\prime}(M,L_{1})
such that

|M-\sigma(y)|\leqslant\Lambda^{as}\cdot L_{1}\cdot\Lambda^{-\alpha n}\leqslant L_{1}\cdot\Lambda^{-\alpha n+\frac{\alpha n}{a+\alpha}}=L_{1}\cdot\Lambda^{-\frac{\alpha^{2}n}{a+\alpha}}.

Indeed, one can check that (
5.28
) implies

M-\sigma(x_{i})\leqslant\Lambda^{a}\cdot(2L_{1}\cdot\Lambda^{-\alpha n}+(M-\sigma(x))),\quad\forall i\in\{1,2\},\quad\forall x,\,\,\{x_{1},x_{2}\}=g^{-1}(x).

We apply this estimate by induction on the level of iterated preimage of a point in
g^{-s}(x_{0})
.

We have a Hölder function
\sigma
with
\int_{0}^{1}\sigma(x)dx=0
such that on a
\Lambda^{\alpha n/(a+\alpha)}
-dense set of points, the function is close to its maximum with error of order
\Lambda^{-\alpha^{a}n/(a+\alpha)}
. From this we conclude that
M\leqslant C\cdot\Lambda^{-\alpha^{2}n/(a+\alpha)}
, for some constant
C
that depends on
L_{1}
, i.e.,

\|\sigma\|_{C^{0}}\leqslant C\cdot\Lambda^{-\frac{\alpha^{2}n}{a+\alpha}},

and hence

\|\psi^{\prime}-e^{q}\|_{C^{0}}\leqslant C\cdot\Lambda^{-\frac{\alpha^{2}n}{a+\alpha}}\leqslant C\cdot\Lambda^{-\frac{\alpha^{2}n}{a+1}}.

From (
5.27
), we conclude that there exists a constant
H
(that depends only on
M
) such that

|f^{\prime}(\varphi(x))-g^{\prime}(x)|\leqslant H\cdot\Lambda^{-\frac{\alpha^{2}n}{a+1}}\quad\forall x\in\mathbb{S}^{1}.

∎

5.3.
Sharp estimates of length distortion under Livšic theorem

Finally, we will use Theorem
5.8
to prove the following sharp distortion theorem, which will be the key estimate in order to run the Whitney extension.

Theorem 5.9

(Sharp distortion under measure normalization)
.

Assume that
f,g\in\mathcal{E}^{r}_{2}
satisfy the assumptions of Theorem
5.8
with
\sigma=\alpha
. Further assume that for
\|f-g\|_{C^{0}}=:{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_{0}
,
\|f^{\prime}-g^{\prime}\|_{C^{0}}=:{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_{1}\geqslant{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_{0}
, the following
decay condition
is satisfied:

\Lambda_{f}=\min_{x\in\mathbb{S}^{1}}f^{\prime}(x)>\frac{{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_{0}}{{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_{1}}\cdot\|f^{\prime\prime}\|_{C^{0}}+1.

(5.29)

Then there exists a constant
B=B(M)>0
such that for every integer
t\leqslant n
and for every pair of intervals
I^{f},I^{g}
between the corresponding periodic points in
\mathbb{P}_{t}^{f}
and
\mathbb{P}_{t}^{g}
we have

\left||I^{f}|-|I^{g}|\right|\leqslant B\cdot\frac{{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_{0}}{{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_{1}}\cdot\Lambda^{-\frac{\alpha^{2}n}{a+1}}.

Proof.

Starting with the inequality

|f^{\prime}(\varphi(x))-g^{\prime}(x)|\geqslant|f^{\prime}(x)-g^{\prime}(x)|-|f^{\prime}(\varphi(x))-f^{\prime}(x)|,

rearranging and taking the sup-norms, we obtain

\|f^{\prime}-g^{\prime}\|_{C^{0}}\leqslant\|f^{\prime}\circ\varphi-g^{\prime}\|_{C^{0}}+\|f^{\prime\prime}\|_{C^{0}}\cdot\|\varphi-\id\|_{C^{0}}.

By Theorem
5.8
,

\|f^{\prime}\circ\varphi-g^{\prime}\|_{C^{0}}\leqslant H\cdot\Lambda^{-\frac{\alpha^{2}n}{a+1}}.

Hence,

\|f-g\|_{C^{0}}=\frac{{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_{0}}{{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_{1}}\|f^{\prime}-g^{\prime}\|_{C^{0}}\leqslant H\cdot\frac{{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_{0}}{{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_{1}}\cdot\Lambda^{-\frac{\alpha^{2}n}{a+1}}+\|f^{\prime\prime}\|_{C^{0}}\cdot\frac{{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_{0}}{{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_{1}}\cdot\|\varphi-\id\|_{C^{0}}.

By Proposition
3.5
,

\|\varphi-\id\|_{C^{0}}\leqslant\frac{1}{\Lambda_{f}-1}\cdot\|f-g\|_{C^{0}}.

By combining the last two estimates together, we get

\|\varphi-\id\|_{C^{0}}\leqslant\frac{H}{\Lambda_{f}-1}\cdot\frac{{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_{0}}{{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_{1}}\cdot\Lambda^{-\frac{\alpha^{2}n}{a+1}}+\frac{\|f^{\prime\prime}\|_{C^{0}}}{\Lambda_{f}-1}\cdot\frac{{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_{0}}{{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_{1}}\cdot\|\varphi-\id\|_{C^{0}}.

Therefore,

\left(\Lambda_{f}-1-\|f^{\prime\prime}\|_{C^{0}}\cdot\frac{{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_{0}}{{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_{1}}\right)\cdot\|\varphi-\id\|_{C^{0}}\leqslant H\cdot\frac{{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_{0}}{{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_{1}}\cdot\Lambda^{-\frac{\alpha^{2}n}{a+1}}.

By decay condition (
6.30
), the factor in front of
\|\varphi-\id\|_{C^{0}}
is positive. And hence, dividing by this factor, we obtain

\|\varphi-\id\|_{C^{0}}\leqslant B^{\prime}\cdot\frac{{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_{0}}{{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_{1}}\cdot\Lambda^{-\frac{\alpha^{2}n}{a+1}},\quad\text{where}\quad B^{\prime}:=\frac{H}{\Lambda_{f}-1-\|f^{\prime\prime}\|_{C^{0}}\cdot\frac{{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_{0}}{{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_{1}}}.

Now the claim of the theorem follows with
B=2B^{\prime}
.
∎

Remark 5.10
.

The decay condition (
6.30
) is satisfied, for example, when

is close to the linear map
L_{2}
as

\|f-L_{2}\|_{C^{2}}<\frac{1}{2}.

We will prove the Main Proposition for

and

. The general proof can be adapted from the given one.

Pick

to be
C^{1}
-
\varepsilon

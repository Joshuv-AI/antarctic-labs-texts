Jordan decompositions in Lie algebras and their duals
Loren Spice, Cheng-Chiang Tsai

Abstract
. We provide a discussion of Jordan decompositions in the Lie algebra, and the dual Lie algebra, of a reductive group in as uniform a way as possible. We give a counterexample to the claim that Jordan decompositions on the dual Lie algebra are unique, and state an upper bound on how non-unique they can be. We also prove some Chevalley-restriction-type claims about GIT quotients for the adjoint and co-adjoint actions of G G .



1.
Introduction

The notion of a Jordan decomposition in a Lie algebra is
well known among those who work with affine algebraic groups over fields.
It allows one, e.g., to reduce the study of centralisers of
arbitrary elements of a Lie algebra to centralisers of
semisimple elements, which are well behaved, and centralisers of
nilpotent elements.
Less well known is the concept of a Jordan decomposition on
a
dual
Lie algebra.
This concept seems to require some restriction on the group whose
dual Lie algebra we are considering—the one that we impose throughout is reductivity—and even then some subtlety is involved.
See Remark
2.1
for the necessary definitions
(of semisimplicity, nilpotence, and the Jordan decomposition).

Past literature has claimed that, in a setting slightly more
restrictive than ours,
Jordan decompositions on the dual Lie algebra exist and are unique.
The existence claim is correct, but we provide a
counterexample, or rather a family of counterexamples,
to uniqueness. See §
7
.
We thus regard it as useful to give a short discussion of
Jordan decompositions on the Lie algebra and the dual Lie algebra of
a reductive group in as uniform a way as possible.
This means that, though we might have to handle different
special cases differently for the two situations, we have
made statements that are the same for the Lie algebra and
the dual Lie algebra whenever possible.
We state our main result as Theorem
1.1
below.
It is a pleasure here to acknowledge our clear debt to
[
8
]
,
on which our work builds, while
correcting some statements in
[
8
, §3.8 and Theorem 4]
that
are only guaranteed to be correct in large characteristic.

Theorem
1.1
(iii)
places an upper bound on
“how non-unique” a Jordan decomposition can be.
This upper bound is sufficient for some purposes, but is not optimal.
See Theorem
8.6
for a sharper bound.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
G
be a connected reductive group over
an algebraically closed field
k
.
Write
\mathfrak{g}
and
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
for the
Lie algebra and dual Lie algebra of
G
, or
the associated vector groups.

When
V
is an affine variety on which
G
acts, we write
V/\!/G

for the GIT quotient
\operatorname{Spec}\mathcal{O}(V)^{G}
.

(i)

A
G
-orbit in
\mathfrak{g}
or
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
is closed if and only if it consists of semisimple elements.

(ii)

Every element of
\mathfrak{g}
or
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
has a Jordan decomposition (though a Jordan decomposition in
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
might not be unique; see Section
7
).

(iii)

For every element
X
of
\mathfrak{g}
or
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
, there is a unique closed orbit in the closure of the
G
-orbit of
X
, and this closed orbit contains the semisimple part
X_{\text{ss}}
of every Jordan decomposition

.

(iv)

Consider the closures

\overline{\mathfrak{g}^{\text{ss}}}
and
\overline{(\mathfrak{g}^{*})^{\text{ss}}}
of
the constructible subsets of semisimple elements in

\mathfrak{g}
and
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
.
For every maximal torus
T\subset G
, we have that

\overline{\mathfrak{g}^{\text{ss}}}
is the sum of

\mathfrak{t}
and
all root spaces for
T
in
\mathfrak{g}
corresponding to
roots that are non-zero in
\mathfrak{t}^{*}
; and

\overline{(\mathfrak{g}^{*})^{\text{ss}}}
is the sum of

(\mathfrak{g}^{*})^{T}
and
all weight spaces for
T
in
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
corresponding to
roots
\alpha
for which the corresponding coroots

h_{\alpha}:=d\alpha^{\vee}(1)
are non-zero in
\mathfrak{t}
.
If the characteristic of
k
is not
2
, then

\overline{\mathfrak{g}^{\text{ss}}}
equals
\mathfrak{g}
and

\overline{(\mathfrak{g}^{*})^{\text{ss}}}
equals
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
.
If the characteristic of
k
equals
2
, then
\overline{\mathfrak{g}^{\text{ss}}}
is the sum of the Lie algebras of all direct factors of
G
that are not of type
\Sp_{2n}
for some positive integer

, with a canonical
\mathfrak{so}_{2n}
summand for each
\Sp_{2n}
direct factor; and
\overline{(\mathfrak{g}^{*})^{\text{ss}}}
is the sum of the dual Lie algebras of all direct factors of
G
that are not of type
\SO_{2n+1}
for some positive integer
n
, with a canonical
\mathfrak{so}_{2n}^{*}
direct summand for each
\SO_{2n+1}
direct factor.

(v)

For every maximal torus
T\subset G
, if we put
W=N_{G}(T)/T
, then the natural map
\mathfrak{t}/\!/W\rightarrow\overline{\mathfrak{g}^{\text{ss}}}/\!/G
(respectively,
\mathfrak{t}^{*}/\!/W\rightarrow\overline{(\mathfrak{g}^{*})^{\text{ss}}}/\!/G
) is
an isomorphism.

(vi)

The natural map
\overline{\mathfrak{g}^{\text{ss}}}/\!/G\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}/\!/G
(respectively,
\overline{(\mathfrak{g}^{*})^{\text{ss}}}/\!/G\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}^{*}/\!/G

k
-points.

.

Beyond Theorem

Notation and preparation

The following notation is mostly as in
Theorem
1.1
, but we introduce it here so as to be able to
refer to it throughout the paper.

Let
k
be an algebraically closed field. Let
G
be a connected reductive group over
k
,
\mathfrak{g}
its Lie algebra, and
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
its dual Lie algebra.
In general, if we denote a group by an uppercase Roman letter, then
we use the corresponding lowercase Fraktur letter for its Lie algebra, so,
for example, once we have groups
T
and
B

denote their Lie algebras by
\mathfrak{t}
and
\mathfrak{b}
.
We may also write
\Lie(\cdot)
in place of the Fraktur notation when
it improves readability.

When
V
is an affine variety on which
G
acts, we write
V/\!/G

for the GIT quotient
\operatorname{Spec}\mathcal{O}(V)^{G}
.
We will sometimes also use
\mathfrak{g}
and
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
for the associated vector groups, so that the notations
\mathfrak{g}/\!/G
and
\mathfrak{g}^{*}/\!/G
make sense.

By elements and points in a scheme over
k
we refer to the
k
-points unless otherwise specified. Centralizers (such as
C_{G}(X)
for an element
X
of
G
,
\mathfrak{g}
or
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
) and centers (such as
Z(G)
) will however always mean the schematic centralizers and centers.

Fix a maximal torus
T\subset G
.
The inclusion of the
T
-fixed subspace
(\mathfrak{g}^{*})^{T}
of
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
is a splitting of the quotient
\mathfrak{g}^{*}\twoheadrightarrow\mathfrak{t}^{*}
, which we regard as an inclusion of
\mathfrak{t}^{*}
in
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
.
Then
\mathfrak{t}^{*}
is the subspace of
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
that
vanishes on the unique
T
-stable complement to

\mathfrak{t}
in
\mathfrak{g}
.

Write
\Phi(G,T)
for the set of roots of

in
\mathfrak{g}
, and

\Phi^{\vee}(G,T)
for the dual root system.
For every
\alpha\in\Phi(G,T)\subset X^{*}(T)
, we have
an element

\alpha^{\vee}\in\Phi^{\vee}(G,T)
of

X_{*}(T)
, and
an element
h_{\alpha}:=d\alpha^{\vee}(1)
of
\mathfrak{t}

(which is identified
via
the evaluation isomorphism

with

\alpha^{\vee}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}1

We will call both ‘coroots’, and rely on context, or
explicit use of the notation

Let
B
be a Borel subgroup of

containing

\mathfrak{b}
and
\mathfrak{u}
for their Lie algebras.

Remark 2.1
.

Let

be an arbitrary element of
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
.
By definition, an element

contains a maximal torus in

Then

is the semisimple part of

if and only if, for some

and

of

agrees with

\mathfrak{b}

\mathfrak{t}

The analogous statement for

.
By

, respectively

3.
Remarks on Jordan decompositions

We hope that the reader will agree that
the notion of a Jordan decomposition on
\mathfrak{g}
,
especially its extremely strong connection to
the classical Jordan decomposition in matrix algebras
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
coming from
a choice of faithful representation
G\rightarrow\GL_{n}
,
is sensible.
Granting that, Remark
3.1
argues that
the notion of a Jordan decomposition on
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
is also sensible
because any
G
-equivariant identification of
\mathfrak{g}
with
\mathfrak{g}^{*}

identifies it with the notion of a Jordan decomposition on
\mathfrak{g}
.

Remark 3.1
.

Theorem
1.1
(iii)
shows that
the semisimple elements of
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
are
precisely those whose
G
-orbits are closed, and
Remark
9.1
below
(and
[
12
, Proposition 3.8]
) show that
the nilpotent elements of
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
are
precisely those that contain
0
in
the closure of their
G
-orbits.
The analogues for
\mathfrak{g}
of these two facts are

[
13
, Corollary 3.6]
and

[
2
, Theorem 10.6(4) and Proposition 14.25]
.
(Actually,
[
13
, Corollary 3.6]
is stated only for
semisimple groups.
We were not able to find a reference for reductive groups other than
our Theorem
1.1
(i)
.)
In particular, if there is a
G
-equivariant isomorphism
of
\mathfrak{g}
with
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
, then
it identifies the semisimple, respectively nilpotent,
elements in the two sets.
Further, if
X_{\text{ss}}\in\mathfrak{g}
is semisimple with image

X^{*}_{\text{ss}}\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}
, we have that

C_{G}(X_{\text{ss}})
equals
C_{G}(X^{*}_{\text{ss}})
, and,
for every maximal torus
T
fixing
X_{\text{ss}}
,
the spaces
\Lie(C_{G}(X_{\text{ss}}))
and

(\mathfrak{g}/{{}_{T}{\Lie(C_{G}(X^{*}_{\text{ss}}))^{\perp}}})^{*}
are identified, where

{}_{T}{\Lie(C_{G}(X^{*}_{\text{ss}})^{\circ})^{\perp}}
is
the unique
T
-stable complement to

\Lie(C_{G}(X^{*}_{\text{ss}})^{\circ})
in
\mathfrak{g}
.
This shows that such an isomorphism also
identifies Jordan decompositions of elements of

\mathfrak{g}
and
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
.

Remark
3.2
discusses the compatibility (or in-)
of our definition of semisimplicity and nilpotence with
a definition that is sometimes used in characteristic
0
.

Remark 3.2
.

In
[
4
, §1.3]
, alternate definitions are given for
semisimplicity and nilpotence in
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
.
Those definitions are given only over
\mathbb{C}
; here
we discuss to what extent they carry over to arbitary fields, and
their relation to our notions.

The definition of semisimplicity involves some choices that are
particular to the case where
k
has characteristic
0

a close identification between groups and Lie algebras, and
the fact that, in that case, all affine algebraic
k

One reasonable characteristic-free re-interpretation of it is that

X^{*}
is semisimple if and only if

C_{G}(X^{*})^{\circ}
is (smooth and) reductive.
Semisimplicity in the sense of
[
8
]
implies
semisimplicity in this sense, by
[
8
, Lemma 3.1iii)]
.
We do not know whether the reverse implication holds.

The definition of nilpotence in
[
4
, §1.3]
is that
an element
X^{*}\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}
is
nilpotent if and only if it is trivial on

\Lie(C_{G}(X^{*}))
.
In small characteristic, nilpotence in the sense of
[
8
]
can
fail to imply nilpotence in the sense of
[
4
]
,
and
vice versa
.
For example, suppose the characteristic of

.
The element

c&-a\end{pmatrix}\mapsto c

of

is nilpotent in the sense of
[
8
]
; but

\Lie(C_{G}(X^{*}))
equals
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
, while

X^{*}
is not trivial on all of
\Lie(C_{G}(X^{*}))
, and hence
not nilpotent in the sense of
[
4
]
.
On the other hand, the element

X^{*}:\begin{bmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{bmatrix}\mapsto a+c+d

of
\mathfrak{pgl}_{2}^{*}
has the property that

\Lie(C_{G}(X^{*}))
is
the strictly upper-triangular subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
, on which

is trivial, so that

is nilpotent in the sense of
[
4
]
;
but

X^{*}
has a Jordan decomposition with semisimple part

by Remark

is not nilpotent in the sense of
[
8
]
.

Lemma 3.3
.

If, for every full-rank, reductive subgroup

Suppose that

[

]

By Theorem

, there are

a Borel subgroup

of

an element

such that

agrees with

, and
we are considering either
the adjoint action of
\Sp_{2n}

(which we shall denote by
\Sp_{2n}\curvearrowright\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}
) or
the coadjoint action of
\SO_{2n+1}

(which we shall denote by
\SO_{2n+1}\curvearrowright\mathfrak{so}_{2n+1}^{*}
)
for some positive integer
n
.

As observed in
[
7
, p. 83]
,
the proof in
[
13
, II.3.17']
that

\mathfrak{t}/\!/W\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}/\!/G
is
an isomorphism, stated there under the assumption that

G
is adjoint, actually requires only that
no root of
T
on
\mathfrak{g}
is
0
when viewed as an element of
\mathfrak{t}^{*}
.
We will show how to handle the opposite case at the end of this section.

The results of
[
8
, Theorem 4]
are stated under the assumptions that

G
is almost simple (imposed in
[
8
, §1, p. 136]
), and that

p
does not equal
2
or

G
is not of type
\SO_{2n+1}
for any positive integer
n

(imposed in
[
8
, §3, p. 140]
).
For any reductive group
G
, there is an isogeny onto
G
from
the product of the central torus of
G
and its almost-simple factors, but
the derivative can have a kernel for small
p
.
Thus, it is not a formal argument to remove the first assumption, but
we describe how to carry it out below.
Removing the second requires a change to the statement of

[
8
, Theorem 4]
.
In fact, we have counterexamples to some of
the intermediate results of
[
8
]
even in
their setting.
We describe the situation below.

For every
\alpha\in\Phi(G,T)
, we have an element

h_{\alpha}:=d\alpha^{\vee}(1)
of
\mathfrak{t}
.
Suppose for the moment that all these elements are non-zero.
This is precisely what is needed to ensure that
the set of
regular (in
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
) semisimple elements of
\mathfrak{t}^{*}
, i.e.,
semisimple elements that do not lie on any coroot hyperplane,
denoted by
\Omega
in
[
8
, §3, p. 140]
, is
dense and open in
\mathfrak{t}^{*}
.
Then the arguments of
[
8
, §3]
go through unchanged, with
two exceptions.
First, the statement in
[
8
, Lemma 3.1i)]
that

\Phi(C_{\mathfrak{g}}(X^{*}),T)
is closed
in
\Phi(G,T)
for every
X^{*}\in\mathfrak{t}^{*}

can fail in small characteristic.
Second,
the claim in
[
8
, §3.8i)]
that
a certain map from
a quotient of a Borel subgroup to
affine space is an isomorphism
can also fail.
See Section
7
for an example of this behaviour
(with
G=\Sp_{4}
in characteristic
2
), where we also show that
the containment of connected centralizers asserted in

[
8
, §3.8ii)]
, and
the uniqueness asserted in
[
8
, Theorem 4iv)]
, can
fail.

Thus, we must remove uniqueness in

[
8
, Theorem 4iv)]
, and discard

[
8
, §3.8]
entirely.
Otherwise, aside from
[
8
, §3.8]
and
[
8
, Theorem 4iv)]
,
the rest of the proof of
[
8
, Theorem 4]
is valid
with no restriction on
G
beyond
every coroot
h_{\alpha}
being non-zero.

We are left with the case when there is some

\alpha\in\Phi(G,T)
such that
h_{\alpha}\in\mathfrak{t}
equals
0
, and thus the image
\chi(h_{\alpha})
in
k
of
\langle\chi,\alpha^{\vee}\rangle
is
0
for every
\chi\in X^{*}(T)
.
In particular, since
\langle\alpha,\alpha^{\vee}\rangle
equals
2
,
the characteristic of
k
is
2
; and
there is no root
\beta
such that
the integer
\langle\beta,\alpha^{\vee}\rangle
is odd.
By inspection of the Dynkin diagrams,
this means that
the component of
\Phi(G,T)
containing
\alpha
is
of type
\mathsf{B}_{n}
for some
n
, possibly
n=1
.
If the corresponding almost-simple factor
G^{\prime}
of
G
were
simply connected, then
\alpha\in\Phi(G^{\prime},T^{\prime})
(where
T^{\prime}:=T\cap G^{\prime}
) would not be divisible by
2
in
X_{*}(T^{\prime})
since any root is indivisible in the root lattice.
In particular,
h_{\alpha}
would be non-zero in
\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}\hookrightarrow\mathfrak{g}
, a contradiction.
Thus the factor is actually adjoint, i.e., is
a
direct
factor of
G
of type
\operatorname{SO}_{2n+1}
.

We now show that, similarly, the failure of the
Chevalley restriction theorem (for
\mathfrak{g}
) can be attributed to
the presence of symplectic-group direct factors.
Thus, suppose instead that there is some

\alpha\in\Phi(G,T)
whose differential

d\alpha
, viewed as an element of
\mathfrak{t}^{*}
, equals
0
.
If we write
G^{*}
for
the dual group of
G
(viewed as a
k
-group, not a
\mathbb{C}
-group) and

T^{*}
for the maximal torus of
G^{*}
corresponding to
T
, then
the preceding argument shows that
there is some positive integer
n
such that

G^{*}
admits
\SO_{2n+1}
as a direct factor with

\alpha\in\Phi^{\vee}(\SO_{2n+1},T^{*})
.
It follows that

G
admits
\SO_{2n+1}^{*}=\Sp_{2n}
as a direct factor with

\alpha\in\Phi(\Sp_{2n},T)
.

5.
First approach

Suppose
k
has characteristic
2
.
We work with the case
G=\SO_{2n+1}\curvearrowright\mathfrak{so}_{2n+1}^{*}
; the case of
\Sp_{2n}\curvearrowright\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}
is almost identical except for being easier at some places.

As in Section
2
,
fix a maximal torus
T
in
\SO_{2n+1}
, and
identify
\mathfrak{t}^{*}
with
the
T
-fixed subspace of
\mathfrak{so}_{2n+1}^{*}
. There is an isogeny
f:\SO_{2n+1}\rightarrow\Sp_{2n}
that induces an isomorphism of
T
onto a maximal torus in
\Sp_{2n}
, and sends the short roots of
\Sp_{2n}
to the long roots of
\SO_{2n+1}
, but sends the long roots of
\Sp_{2n}
to twice the short roots of
\SO_{2n+1}
, in the sense of
[
14
, Theorem 9.6.5]
.

If
v_{0}\in\mathfrak{t}^{*}
lies in the complement of
all fixed subspaces of Weyl elements that
do not act trivially on
\mathfrak{t}^{*}
, and satisfies

v_{0}(h_{\alpha})\neq 0
for all
\alpha\in\Phi(\SO_{2n+1},T)
such that

h_{\alpha}
is non-
0
as an element of
\mathfrak{t}
,
then

C_{\SO_{2n+1}}(v_{0})
equals
C_{\SO_{2n+1}}(\mathfrak{t}^{*})
.
Since the subgroup of
W(\SO_{2n+1},T)(k)
that
acts trivially on
\mathfrak{t}^{*}
is
generated by reflections in
the roots
\alpha\in\Phi(\SO_{2n+1},T)
such that

h_{\alpha}
equals
0
in
\mathfrak{t}
, we have that

H:=C_{\SO_{2n+1}}(\mathfrak{t}^{*})
is connected.
Then
[
8
, Lemma 3.1]
gives that
H
is a reductive group..
Concretely,

H
is isomorphic to
(\SO_{3})^{n}
, and
generated by
T
and its short-root groups in
\SO_{2n+1}
; equivalently, it is the pre-image of
(\Sp_{2})^{n}\subset\Sp_{2n}
under

\SO_{2n+1}\twoheadrightarrow\Sp_{2n}
. The set

\mathfrak{t}^{\prime}:=\{v\in\mathfrak{t}^{*}\;|\;C_{G}(v)=H\}

is dense open.

Since

H
is a connected, reductive group and

C_{G}(H)
is contained in
C_{G}(T)=T\subset H
, it follows that
the identity component of
N_{G}(H)
is

(H\cdot C_{G}(H))^{\circ}=H
.
Thus,
N_{G}(H)
is smooth, and so
generated by
N_{G}(T)
and
H
. The Tits lifts (with respect to some fixed system of positive roots) of the simple long-root reflections generate a copy of
S_{n}
that is a direct complement to
H
in
N_{G}(H)
.

A choice of one from each opposite pair of short roots gives a basis of
\mathfrak{t}^{*}
, hence an
isomorphism
\mathfrak{t}^{*}\cong\mathbb{A}^{n}
under which
N_{G}(H)/H\cong S_{n}
acts by permuting the coordinates, and
\mathfrak{t}^{\prime}
is the locus with distinct non-zero coordinates.

In particular, the image
V:=\im(df^{*}:\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}^{*}\rightarrow\mathfrak{so}_{2n+1}^{*})
of the dual of the differential of
f
is the sum of
\mathfrak{t}^{*}
and those weight spaces of
T
on
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
corresponding to the long roots of
T
on
\mathfrak{so}_{2n+1}
.
(In Section
6
, we identify

V
with
\mathfrak{so}_{2n}^{*}
.)
Since
V
contains
\mathfrak{t}^{*}
and is
\SO_{2n+1}
-stable, all semisimple elements of
\mathfrak{so}_{2n+1}^{*}
lie in
V
.

Consider the action map

c:\SO_{2n+1}\times_{N_{G}(H)}\mathfrak{t}^{*}\rightarrow V.

This restricts to an isomorphism from
\SO_{2n+1}\times_{N_{G}(H)}\mathfrak{t}^{\prime}
onto its image, which is open in
V
because the differential is non-singular (this is because
C_{\mathfrak{so}_{2n+1}}(v)=\mathfrak{h}
for every
v\in\mathfrak{t}^{\prime}
). In particular
c
is birational, and the image of
c
is dense in
V
, proving Theorem
1.1
(iv)
. Consider the map

\bar{c}:\mathfrak{t}^{*}/\!/S_{n}\rightarrow V/\!/\SO_{2n+1}.

We will prove that it is an isomorphism by constructing its inverse mimicking the proof in
[
13
, II.3.17']
. Since both sides are affine varieties, we can construct the map at the level of ring homomorphisms, i.e., given an
S_{n}
-invariant regular function
f
we shall construct an
\SO_{2n+1}
-invariant regular function on
V
. Indeed,
f
defines a function on the domain of
c
, and thus a rational function on
V
, i.e., there are relatively prime polynomials
g,h\in\mathcal{O}(V)
such that
f\times c^{*}(h)=c^{*}(g)\in\mathcal{O}(\SO_{2n+1}\times_{N_{G}(H)}\mathfrak{t}^{*})
.
Since
f
is by construction
\SO_{2n+1}
-invariant, so is
g/h
. Since
\SO_{2n+1}
is semisimple and
\mathcal{O}(V)
is a polynomial ring, this means that both
g
and
h
are
\SO_{2n+1}
-invariant.
Since
f
is an isomorphism on
T
, we have that

g
vanishes on the zero set of
h
on
\mathfrak{t}^{*}
, and hence

g
vanishes on every semisimple element of
the zero set of
h
.
Now fix an arbitrary
v\in V
in the zero set of
h
.
Thanks to Lemma
2.2
, the closure of the

\SO_{2n+1}
-orbit of
v
contains
a semisimple element
v_{\text{ss}}
, necessarily in
V
, so that

g(v_{\text{ss}})
equals
g(v)
and

h(v_{\text{ss}})
equals
h(v)
.
In particular,
h(v_{\text{ss}})
equals
h(v)=0
, so
g(v)=g(v_{\text{ss}})=h(v_{\text{ss}})
also equals
0
.

By Hilbert’s Nullstellensatz this implies that
h\mid g
, i.e., we may assume
h=1
. One verifies that
f\mapsto g
is then our desired inverse, and therefore
\bar{c}
is an isomorphism. This proves Theorem
1.1
(v)
.

That
\bar{c}
is injective shows in particular that every two semisimple orbits go to different points in
V/\!/\SO_{2n+1}
, and hence they have to be closed by Lemma
2.2
. This finishes the proof of Theorem
1.1
(i)
.

6.
Second approach

We outline another approach to the proof of
Theorem
1.1
(i)
,
(iv)
, and
(v)
.
We continue to assume that
k
has characteristic
2
.

Let
n
be a positive integer, and

q
a quadratic form on a (
2n+1
)-dimensional
k
-vector space
V

such that,
if we write

b
for the polar
(x,y)\mapsto q(x+y)-q(x)-q(y)
of
q
and

\ell
for the radical

\{x\in V\;|\;\text{$b(x,y)=0$ for all $y\in V$}\}

of
b
, then

\ell
is
1
-dimensional.
Then
b
gives a
non-degenerate, alternating form
\bar{b}
on

\bar{V}:=V/\ell
,
every element of
\SO(V,q)
preserves
\ell
, and
the resulting map
\SO(V,q)\rightarrow\GL(\bar{V})
is actually
the isogeny
f:\SO(V,q)\rightarrow\Sp(\bar{V},\bar{b})
that
we used in the previous section.

Fix for the moment a complement
V^{\prime}
to
\ell
in
V
.
Extension trivially across
\ell
provides a map

\SO(V^{\prime},q)\rightarrow\SO(V,q)
,
which we regard as an inclusion.
We may also transport
b
to
V^{\prime}
, and then we have that

\SO(V^{\prime},q)
sits inside
\Sp(V^{\prime},b)
.
Then the relevant maps fit into a commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\SO(V,q)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Sp(\bar{V},\bar{b})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\SO(V^{\prime},q)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Sp(V^{\prime},b).}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sim}

We will soon fix a maximal torus in
\SO(V^{\prime},q)
, but
for the moment we want to be able to work with an arbitrary choice.

If
T
is a maximal torus in
\SO(V^{\prime},q)
, then

\SO(V^{\prime},q)
is generated by

T
and its long-root subgroups in
\SO(V,q)
;
the image of
T
is a maximal torus in
\Sp(\bar{V},\bar{b})
, and
the image of
\SO(V^{\prime},q)
in
\Sp(\bar{V},\bar{b})
is
the subgroup of the latter generated by

f(T)
and
its short-root subgroups in
\Sp(\bar{V},\bar{b})
; and

f
restricts to an isomorphism of
\SO(V^{\prime},q)
onto its image.
The subgroup
\SO(V^{\prime},q)
of
\SO(V,q)
, and
its isomorph in
\Sp(\bar{V},\bar{b})
,
depend on the choice of
V^{\prime}
.
However, the image of the embedding of Lie algebras

\mathfrak{so}(V^{\prime},q)\rightarrow\mathfrak{sp}(\bar{V},\bar{b})
does not depend on the choice of
V^{\prime}
.
In fact,
since the complement

\{X\in\mathfrak{so}(V,q)\;|\;XV^{\prime}\subset\ell\}
to
(the image of)
\mathfrak{so}(V^{\prime},q)
in
\mathfrak{so}(V,q)
is
annihilated by
\mathfrak{so}(V,q)\rightarrow\mathfrak{sp}(\bar{V},\bar{b})
, we have that
the images of
\mathfrak{so}(V^{\prime},q)
and
\mathfrak{so}(V,q)
in
\mathfrak{sp}(\bar{V},\bar{b})
are
equal.
This common image may be described
without reference to
V^{\prime}
as

\{\bar{X}\in\mathfrak{sp}(\bar{V},\bar{b})\;|\;\text{$\bar{b}(\bar{X}\bar{v},\bar{v})=0$ for all
$\bar{v}\in\bar{V}$}\}
.

We now write

\SO_{2n+1}
for
\SO(V,q)
, and

\Sp_{2n}
for
\SO(\bar{V},\bar{b})
.
In order to emphasise that
\mathfrak{so}(V^{\prime},q)
does not
depend on the choice of
V^{\prime}
, we denote it by
\mathfrak{so}_{2n}
.
Thus we have a
sub-
\Sp_{2n}
-representation

\mathfrak{so}_{2n}
of
\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}
, and
dually a
quotient
\Sp_{2n}
-representation

\mathfrak{so}_{2n}^{*}
of
\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}^{*}
.
Of course, the quotient
\SO_{2n+1}\twoheadrightarrow\Sp_{2n}
allows us
to inflate any representation of
\Sp_{2n}
to
a representation of
\SO_{2n+1}
.
Then we have identified
the
\SO_{2n+1}
-representation
\mathfrak{so}_{2n}^{*}
with

\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}^{*}/\ker(\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}^{*}\rightarrow\mathfrak{so}_{2n+1}^{*})
, hence with

\im(\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}^{*}\rightarrow\mathfrak{so}_{2n+1}^{*})
.
Since the composition

\mathfrak{so}_{2n}\rightarrow\mathfrak{so}_{2n+1}\rightarrow\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}

(which
a priori
depends on the choice of
V^{\prime}
) is the inclusion

\mathfrak{so}_{2n}\rightarrow\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}

(which doesn’t), also the composition

\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}^{*}\rightarrow\mathfrak{so}_{2n+1}^{*}\rightarrow\mathfrak{so}_{2n}^{*}
is the quotient

\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}^{*}\rightarrow\mathfrak{so}_{2n}^{*}
.
That is,
\im(\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}^{*}\rightarrow\mathfrak{so}_{2n+1}^{*})
is
a canonical lift of
\mathfrak{so}_{2n}^{*}
in
\mathfrak{so}_{2n+1}^{*}
, which
we use to regard
\mathfrak{so}_{2n}^{*}
as
a sub-
\SO_{2n+1}
-representation of

\mathfrak{so}_{2n+1}^{*}
.

Now fix a maximal torus
T
in
\SO(V^{\prime},q)
.
Then
T
is also a maximal torus in
\SO_{2n+1}
, and
the isogeny
\SO_{2n+1}\rightarrow\Sp_{2n}
is an isomorphism of

T
onto a maximal torus in
\Sp_{2n}
.
We shall abuse notation by denoting the image torus also by
T
.
Thus
\mathfrak{t}
is contained in
\mathfrak{so}_{2n}
.
Note that
f
restricts to an isomorphism

N_{\SO_{2n+1}}(T)/T\rightarrow N_{\Sp_{2n}}(T)/T
that
intertwines their actions on
T
.
Write
W^{\prime}
for
N_{\SO(V^{\prime},q)}(T)/T
, and

W
for
N_{\SO_{2n+1}}(T)/T
and its isomorph
N_{\Sp_{2n}}(T)/T
.
Then
W^{\prime}
is a normal subgroup of
W
, and
there is a complement to
W^{\prime}
in
W
that
acts by sign changes on a basis for
X_{*}(T)
, hence
trivially on
\mathfrak{t}=X_{*}(T)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}k
and on
\mathfrak{t}^{*}
.
That is,

\mathfrak{t}/\!/W^{\prime}
equals
\mathfrak{t}/\!/W
and

\mathfrak{t}^{*}/\!/W^{\prime}
equals
\mathfrak{t}^{*}/\!/W
.
Recall that there are functions

a_{0},\dotsc,a_{n}\in k[\mathfrak{so}_{2n}]
such that, for every

X\in\mathfrak{so}_{2n}
, if
we view
X
as an element of
\mathfrak{gl}_{2n}
, then
the characteristic polynomial of
X
is the element

(a_{0}(X)+a_{1}(X)\lambda+\dotsb+a_{n}(X)\lambda^{n})^{2}

of
k[\lambda]
.
Since
k
has characteristic
2
,
these functions are uniquely determined.
In particular, they are not just
\SO(V^{\prime},q)
-, but actually

\Sp_{2n}
-, invariant.
Since
a_{0}\rvert_{\mathfrak{t}},\dotsc,a_{n}\rvert_{\mathfrak{t}}
are
the elementary symmetric polynomials in the diagonal entries of
an element of
\mathfrak{t}
,
they generate
k[\mathfrak{t}]^{W^{\prime}}=k[\mathfrak{t}]^{W}
.
Thus the restriction map

k[\mathfrak{so}_{2n}]^{\SO(V^{\prime},q)}\rightarrow k[\mathfrak{t}]^{W^{\prime}}=k[\mathfrak{t}]^{W}
is
already surjective when restricted to

k[\mathfrak{so}_{2n}]^{\Sp_{2n}}
; but
Theorem
1.1
(v)
gives that it is an isomorphism, so that

k[\mathfrak{so}_{2n}]^{\Sp_{2n}}
equals
k[\mathfrak{so}_{2n}]^{\SO(V^{\prime},q)}
and
the restriction map

k[\mathfrak{so}_{2n}]^{\Sp_{2n}}\rightarrow k[\mathfrak{t}]^{W}
is
an isomorphism of
k
-algebras, i.e.,
the dual map
\mathfrak{t}/\!/W\rightarrow\mathfrak{so}_{2n}/\!/\Sp_{2n}
is
an isomorphism of schemes.
We will use this later to prove Theorem
1.1
(v)
.

Since
every semisimple element of
\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}
belongs to
the Lie algebra of some maximal torus in
\Sp_{2n}

[
2
, Proposition 11.8]
; since
any two maximal tori in
\Sp_{2n}
are

\Sp_{2n}(k)
-conjugate; and since

\mathfrak{so}_{2n}
is a sub-
\Sp_{2n}
-representation of
\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}
,
it follows that

\mathfrak{so}_{2n}
contains all semisimple elements of
\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}
.
That is, we have containments

\mathfrak{so}_{2n}^{\text{ss}}\subset\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}^{\text{ss}}\subset\mathfrak{so}_{2n}
.
In particular, since
Theorem
1.1
(iv)

(for
\SO(V^{\prime},q)\curvearrowright\mathfrak{so}(V^{\prime},q)=\mathfrak{so}_{2n}
) shows that

\overline{\mathfrak{so}_{2n}^{\text{ss}}}
equals
\mathfrak{so}_{2n}
, also

\overline{\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}^{\text{ss}}}
equals
\mathfrak{so}_{2n}
.
This shows Theorem
1.1
(iv)

(for
\Sp_{2n}\curvearrowright\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}
).
Therefore, the fact that

\mathfrak{t}/\!/W\rightarrow\mathfrak{so}_{2n}/\!/\Sp_{2n}
is an isomorphism is exactly
Theorem
1.1
(v)
for

\Sp_{2n}\curvearrowright\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}
.

Since the functoriality of the Jordan decomposition

[
2
, Theorem 4.4(4)]
guarantees that
an element of
\mathfrak{so}_{2n}
is semisimple if and only if
it is so as
an element of
\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}
, we have equality

\mathfrak{so}_{2n}^{\text{ss}}=\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}^{\text{ss}}
.
Now the
k
-points of

\mathfrak{so}_{2n}/\!/\SO(V^{\prime},q)=\mathfrak{so}_{2n}/\!/\Sp_{2n}
are,
on the one hand, exactly
the closed
\SO(V^{\prime},q)
-orbits on
\mathfrak{so}_{2n}
, which are
the
\SO(V^{\prime},q)
-orbits through elements of

\mathfrak{so}_{2n}^{\text{ss}}=\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}^{\text{ss}}
, and,
on the other hand, exactly
the closed
\Sp_{2n}
-orbits on
\mathfrak{so}_{2n}
.
This shows that
\Sp_{2n}
-orbits through
elements of
\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}^{\text{ss}}
are closed.
Lemma
2.2
gives the converse, and so
establishes Theorem
1.1
(i)
.

Nearly the same argument works for

\SO_{2n+1}\curvearrowright\mathfrak{so}_{2n+1}^{*}
,
but there are two major problems.
First, we do not know an easy, concrete description of

k[\mathfrak{so}_{2n}^{*}]^{\SO(V^{\prime},q)}
that allows us to see that it equals

k[\mathfrak{so}_{2n}^{*}]^{\SO_{2n+1}}
.
Second, although
each semisimple element of
\mathfrak{so}_{2n+1}^{*}
belongs to

\mathfrak{t}_{1}^{*}
for some maximal torus
T_{1}
in
\SO_{2n+1}
, and to

\mathfrak{so}_{2n}^{*}
, there is no guarantee
a priori
that
we can take the maximal torus
T_{1}
in
\SO(V^{\prime},q)
.
That is, although we have the inclusion

(\mathfrak{so}_{2n+1}^{*})^{\text{ss}}\subset\mathfrak{so}_{2n}^{*}
,
it is no longer clear that the inclusion

We do this by exhibiting an

We start with the polar

Let us momentarily write

gives that

7.
Non-uniqueness of the Jordan decomposition

Suppose that there are
roots
\alpha,\beta\in\Phi(G,T)

such that
\alpha+\beta
belongs to
\Phi(G,T)
, but
the coroot
h_{\alpha+\beta}
does not
lie in the span of the coroots
h_{\alpha}
and
h_{\beta}
.

Remark 7.1
.

The irreducible component of
\Phi(G,T)
containing

\alpha
and
\beta
is not
simply laced,
at least one of
\alpha
or
\beta
is short,

\alpha+\beta
is long, and
the length of
\alpha+\beta
is
0
in
k
.
Since the closed subsystem

\Phi_{\alpha,\beta}:=\Phi(G,T)\cap(\mathbb{Z}\alpha+\mathbb{Z}\beta)
of

\Phi(G,T)
generated by
\alpha
and
\beta
has
rank
2
and is not simply laced, we have that
the characteristic of
k
equals
2
and

\Phi_{\alpha,\beta}
is of type
\mathsf{B}_{2}=\mathsf{C}_{2}
; or
the characteristic of
k
equals
3
and

\Phi_{\alpha,\beta}
is of type
\mathsf{G}_{2}
.
We now proceed by exhaustion, first choosing
a system of simple roots for
\Phi
containing
\alpha
.
Then running through the possibilities shows that,
having fixed
\alpha
,
there are two possibilities for
\beta
, which are conjugate in

\operatorname{stab}_{W(\Phi_{\alpha,\beta})}(\alpha)
; and

\alpha
and
\beta
are both short.
Thus, if we write
H
for the subgroup of
G
generated by
T
and
the root subgroups for
T
corresponding to
roots in
\Phi_{\alpha,\beta}
, then
there is a subgroup
H^{\prime}
of

containing
T
such that

\Phi(H^{\prime},T)
consists precisely of
the short roots in
\Phi(H,T)=\Phi_{\alpha,\beta}
.
Specifically, we are in one of two situations.

(1)

The characteristic of
k
is
2
,
the derived subgroup of
H
is
\SO_{5}
or
\Sp_{4}
,
the derived subgroup of
H^{\prime}
is
\operatorname{PGO}_{4}
or
\SO_{4}
, and

\alpha
and
\beta
are orthogonal short roots.
Then
\Phi_{\alpha,\beta}
is the union of the set

\pm\{\alpha,\beta\}
of short roots and the set

\pm\{\alpha+\beta,\alpha-\beta\}
of long roots.

(2)

The characteristic of
k
is
3
,
the derived subgroup of
H
is
G_{2}
,
the derived subgroup of
H^{\prime}
is
\operatorname{PGL}_{3}
, and

\alpha
and
\beta
are short roots
whose sum is a long root.
Then
\Phi_{\alpha,\beta}
is the union of the set

\pm\{\alpha,\beta,\alpha-\beta\}
of short roots and the set

\pm\{\alpha+\beta,2\alpha-\beta,\alpha-2\beta\}
of long roots.

Remark
7.1
gives that

U_{\alpha}
and
U_{\alpha+\beta}
commute.
Further, for each non-trivial

X_{-\alpha-\beta}\in\mathfrak{g}_{-\alpha-\beta}
and each

k
-algebra
A
, the map

U_{\alpha}(A)\to\mathfrak{g}_{-\beta}\otimes_{k}A
given by

u_{\alpha}\mapsto(\Ad(u_{\alpha})-1)X_{-\alpha-\beta}
is an
isomorphism.

Now, since
h_{\alpha+\beta}
does not belong to
the span of
h_{\alpha}
and
h_{\beta}
, there is some

X^{*}_{\text{ss}}\in\mathfrak{t}^{*}
that vanishes on
h_{\alpha}
and
h_{\beta}
, but
satisfies
X^{*}_{\text{ss}}(h_{\alpha+\beta})=1
.
In particular,
X^{*}_{\text{ss}}
is fixed by
U_{\alpha}

[
8
, Lemma 3.1ii)]
.
Let
X^{*}_{\text{nil}}
be a non-zero element of the

\beta
-weight space for
T
in
\mathfrak{g}
, so that

X^{*}_{\text{nil}}
vanishes on the
T
-stable complement to

\mathfrak{g}_{-\beta}
in
\mathfrak{g}
.
Since
-2\alpha-\beta
does not belong to
\Phi(G,T)
,
we have that

U_{\alpha+\beta}
fixes
X^{*}_{\text{nil}}
.
Then
X^{*}:=X^{*}_{\text{ss}}+X^{*}_{\text{nil}}
has an obvious Jordan decomposition.

Now the Chevalley commutation relations say that the map

U_{\alpha+\beta}(k)\rightarrow(\mathfrak{g}_{-\alpha-\beta})^{*}
given by

u_{\alpha+\beta}\mapsto(\Ad^{*}(u_{\alpha+\beta})-1)X^{*}_{\text{ss}}

is a

-equivariant isomorphism, in particular
linear for
the
T
-invariant linear structure on
U_{\alpha+\beta}
;
and, by our description of
\Phi_{\alpha,\beta}
above, that the map

U_{\alpha}(k)\rightarrow(\mathfrak{g}_{-\alpha-\beta})^{*}
given by

u_{\alpha}\mapsto(1-\Ad^{*}(u_{\alpha}))X^{*}_{\text{nil}}
is also an isomorphism,
this time not
T(k)
-equivariant, but still
linear for
the
T
-invariant linear structure on
U_{\alpha}
.
We thus obtain an isomorphism of vector groups

f:U_{\alpha+\beta}\rightarrow U_{\alpha}
.

Remember that
U_{\alpha}
and
U_{\alpha+\beta}
commute.
In particular, the map

U_{\alpha+\beta}\rightarrow G
given by

u_{\alpha+\beta}\mapsto f(u_{\alpha+\beta})u_{\alpha+\beta}
is
a homomorphism.
Let
A
be a
k
-algebra.
For every element

, if
we put
u_{\alpha}=f(u_{\alpha+\beta})
, then,
since

U_{\alpha}
fixes
X^{*}_{\text{ss}}
and

fixes

, we have the equalities

That is, our homomorphism

actually lands in

, hence in

.
Moreover, if

equals

and

is non-trivial, then

\Ad^{*}(u_{\alpha}u_{\alpha+\beta})X^{*}_{\text{ss}}=\Ad^{*}(u_{\alpha+\beta})X^{*}_{\text{ss}}
is a semisimple part of

that is
different from
X^{*}_{\text{ss}}
.
Note that this shows that each of the following claims can fail:

•

the claim in

[
8
, §3.8i)]
that the map

defined there
(given by evaluating at certain root vectors)

•

the claim in

[

, that we have

(but actually meaning, given

); and

•

the claim in

[
8
, Theorem 4iv)]
, that
Jordan decompositions are unique.

So far, our computations have been abstract.
Let us now give a concrete example.
Let
k
be an algebraically closed field of characteristic
2
, and

G=\Sp_{4}
the closed subgroup of
\GL_{4}
stabilizing the symplectic form:

\left\langle\left[\begin{matrix}x_{1}\\
x_{2}\\
x_{3}\\
x_{4}\end{matrix}\right],\left[\begin{matrix}y_{1}\\
y_{2}\\
y_{3}\\
y_{4}\end{matrix}\right]\right\rangle=x_{1}y_{4}+x_{2}y_{3}+x_{3}y_{2}+x_{4}y_{1}.

The subgroup
T
of diagonal matrices in
G
is a maximal torus, and,
with hopefully obvious notation for the characters

\epsilon_{1},\dotsc,\epsilon_{4}
of
the group of diagonal matrices in
\GL_{4}
, so that
the restrictions of

\epsilon_{3}
and
\epsilon_{4}
to
T
agree with
the restrictions of

-\epsilon_{2}
and
-\epsilon_{1}
, we will work here with

\alpha=\epsilon_{1}-\epsilon_{2}
and

\beta=\epsilon_{1}+\epsilon_{2}
.

The embedding of
G
in
\GL_{4}
gives an embedding of
\mathfrak{g}
in
\mathfrak{gl}_{4}
, hence a quotient
\mathfrak{gl}_{4}^{*}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}^{*}
.
We further identify
\mathfrak{gl}_{4}^{*}
with
\mathfrak{gl}_{4}
via the trace pairing. We will then represent elements in
\mathfrak{sp}_{4}^{*}
non-uniquely by
4\times 4
matrices.
We abuse notation by using equality to denote a choice of representative,
which allows us to write, for example,

X^{*}=\left[\begin{matrix}1&&1&\\
&0&&\\
&&1&\\
&&&0\end{matrix}\right]=\left[\begin{matrix}1&&1&\\
&1&&\\
&&0&\\
&&&0\end{matrix}\right],

where all blank entries are filled by
0
,
for an element of
\mathfrak{sp}_{4}^{*}
.
It has an evident Jordan decomposition:

(7.2)

X^{*}=X^{*}_{\text{ss}}+X^{*}_{\text{nil}},\;\;\text{where}\;\;X^{*}_{\text{ss}}=\left[\begin{matrix}1&&&\\
&0&&\\
&&1&\\
&&&0\end{matrix}\right],\;\;X^{*}_{\text{nil}}=\left[\begin{matrix}0&&1&\\
&0&&\\
&&0&\\
&&&0\end{matrix}\right].

The morphism
U_{\alpha+\beta}\rightarrow U_{\alpha}
is given by

\left[\begin{matrix}1&&&t\\
&1&&\\
&&1&\\
&&&1\end{matrix}\right]\mapsto\left[\begin{matrix}1&t&&\\
&1&&\\
&&1&t\\
&&&1\end{matrix}\right].

Specifically, consider the elements
u_{\alpha}\in U_{\alpha}(k)
and
u_{\alpha+\beta}\in U_{\alpha+\beta}(k)
given by

u_{\alpha}=\left[\begin{matrix}1&1&&\\
&1&&\\
&&1&1\\
&&&1\end{matrix}\right],\;\;u_{\alpha+\beta}=\left[\begin{matrix}1&&&1\\
&1&&\\
&&1&\\
&&&1\end{matrix}\right].

and

. Since

and thus

.

Remark 7.3
.

One might wonder if there is a sort of
“restricted dual Lie algebra” structure, i.e., a

p
th-power map analogous to the one carried by a
restricted Lie algebra, on
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
.
For every maximal torus
T
,
there is such an operation on
\mathfrak{t}^{*}
, where, for
every
Y^{*}\in\mathfrak{t}^{*}
, we remember that
the
p
th-power map
(\cdot)^{[p]}
on

is bijective, say
with inverse
(\cdot)^{-[p]}
, and then define

(Y^{*})^{[p]}(h)=\bigl(Y^{*}(h^{-[p]})\bigr)^{p}
for
every

.
This structure is obviously functorial.
Moreover, for every maximal torus
T
in
G
and every

\alpha\in\Phi(G,T)
, we have that

equals
h_{\alpha}
;
so, for every
Y^{*}\in\mathfrak{t}^{*}
, we have that

equals

\{\alpha\in\Phi(G,T)\;|\;(Y^{*})^{[p]}(h_{\alpha})=0\}
, and hence by

[
8
, Lemma 3.1]
that

C_{G}(Y^{*})^{\circ}
equals
C_{G}((Y^{*})^{[p]})^{\circ}
.

If
Y^{*}\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}
is semisimple and

T_{1}
and
T_{2}
are maximal tori in
C_{G}(Y^{*})^{\circ}
, and
if we write

(Y^{*})^{[p]}_{1}\in\mathfrak{t}_{1}^{*}\subset\mathfrak{g}^{*}
and

(Y^{*})^{[p]}_{2}\in\mathfrak{t}_{2}^{*}\subset\mathfrak{g}^{*}
for
the resulting
p
th powers, then
there is some

such that

gT_{1}g^{-1}
equals
T_{2}
, and

both fixes
(Y^{*})^{[p]}_{1}
and carries it to
(Y^{*})^{[p]}_{2}
.
That is,

(Y^{*})^{[p]}_{1}

(Y^{*})^{[p]}_{2}
, so

(Y^{*})^{[p]}
is
independent of the choice of

.

We may thus ask if there is some

th-power operation on

\mathfrak{g}^{*}
that satisfies the following properties,
analogous to those for restricted Lie algebras:

•

it is equivariant for the coadjoint action of

;

•

it restricts to the operation that
we have just defined on
(\mathfrak{g}^{*})^{\text{ss}}
;

•

it respects Jordan decompositions; and

•

every nilpotent element of
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
is

If there were, then, for the element
X^{*}\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}
above, we would
have for sufficiently large

that

, and hence that

.

, but

(because the

Throughout this section, let

be an element of
\mathfrak{g}^{*}

We begin with a crude observation,

, on the uniqueness of

.

both
agree with

.

If

X^{*}
whose

(\mathfrak{g}^{*})^{T}

We have that
X^{*}
belongs to
(\mathfrak{g}^{*})^{T}

the subspace of
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
that
vanishes on the unique

-stable complement to

\Lie(C_{G}(T)^{\circ})
in
\mathfrak{g}
, and is

C_{G}(T)^{\circ}
-equivariantly identified
by restriction to
\Lie(C_{G}(T)^{\circ})
with

\Lie(C_{G}(T)^{\circ})^{*}
.
Remark
3.4
shows that the pull-back to

of
a Jordan decomposition in
\Lie(C_{G}(T)^{\circ})^{*}
is
a Jordan decomposition in
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
.
Then the result follows from
Theorem
1.1
(ii)
,
with
C_{G}(T)^{\circ}
in place of

.
∎

Let
X^{*}_{\text{ss}}
be the semisimple part of
a Jordan decomposition of
X^{*}
.
By Remark
2.1
, there are
a maximal torus
T
in

and
a Borel subgroup
B
of

containing
T
such that

X^{*}
agrees on
\mathfrak{b}
with
X^{*}_{\text{ss}}
, and

X^{*}
vanishes on the
T
-stable complement to

\Lie(C_{G}(X^{*}_{\text{ss}})^{\circ})
in
\mathfrak{g}
.
By

\Phi(C_{G}(X^{*}_{\text{ss}})^{\circ},T)

\alpha\in\Phi(G,T)
such that

, or, equivalently,

,
equals
0
.

Write

for the schematic centre of
C_{G}(X^{*}_{\text{ss}})^{\circ}
,

.
We see here the main difference, for our purposes, between

Namely, it is possible that

but the analogous construction on the Lie-algebra side always gives

C_{G}(Z(C_{G}(X_{\text{ss}})^{\circ}))^{\circ}=C_{G}(X_{\text{ss}})^{\circ}

a semisimple element of
\mathfrak{g}

By

in
\mathfrak{g}

It is the sum of the non-trivial-weight spaces for

\mathfrak{g}

Since

In characteristic

This need no longer happen in positive characteristic.

Another failure mode occurs if

Lemma 8.3
.

If

does not equal

, then

(Z(H)/Z(G))^{\circ}
is non-trivial.

Proof.

We prove the result by induction on the dimension of
G
.
It is vacuously true if the dimension is
0
.
Suppose that we have proven the result for
all groups of smaller dimension than
G
.

If the semisimple rank
\dim(T)-\dim(Z(H))
of
H
is
smaller than the semisimple rank
\dim(T)-\dim(Z(G))
of
G
, then

\dim(Z(H)/Z(G))=\dim(Z(H))-\dim(Z(G))
is positive, so
the result follows.
Otherwise, by
[
13
, I.4.5]
, there are a
system
\Delta
of simple roots for
T
in
G

(not necessarily the one coming from
B
) and
an element
\beta\in\Delta
such that
the coefficient
p
of
\beta
in
the highest root
\tilde{\alpha}
in its component of
\Phi(G,T)
is

\Phi(H,T)
is contained in

\mathbb{Z}(\{\tilde{\alpha}\}\cup\Delta\setminus\{\beta\})

The coefficient

might or might not be
the characteristic of

.
Write
\varpi^{\vee}
for the fundamental coweight of

corresponding to
\beta
.
Note that

is contained in the group

C_{G}(\varpi^{\vee}(\mu_{p}))^{\circ}
, which
has smaller dimension than
G
.
If the containment is not an equality, then
we may replace
G
by
C_{G}(\varpi^{\vee}(\mu_{p}))^{\circ}
, and
reason by induction.
Thus we may, and do, assume that we have equality.
In particular,

\mathbb{Z}\Phi(H,T)
equals

\mathbb{Z}(\{\tilde{\alpha}\}\cup\Delta\setminus\{\beta\})
; so

is non-zero.

Write
\ell
for the normalized squared-length function on

\Phi(G,T)
, so that

\ell
takes values in

equals

\alpha
belongs to a simply laced component of

or
is short in its component.
Then the map
\Phi(G,T)\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}\Phi^{\vee}(G,T)

\alpha\mapsto\ell(\alpha)\alpha^{\vee}
extends to
a homomorphism
\mathbb{Z}\Phi(G,T)\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}\Phi^{\vee}(G,T)
.
We have that
p\beta
belongs to

\mathbb{Z}\Phi(H,T)=\mathbb{Z}\Phi(C_{G}(X^{*}_{\text{ss}})^{\circ},T)

p\ell(\beta)\beta^{\vee}
is a linear combination of the various

\ell(\alpha)\alpha^{\vee}
with

\alpha\in\Phi(C_{G}(X^{*}_{\text{ss}})^{\circ},T)
.
Since
X^{*}(h_{\alpha})
equals zero for every

\alpha\in\Phi(C_{G}(X^{*}_{\text{ss}})^{\circ},T)
, but

X^{*}(h_{\beta})
is non-zero
(because
\beta
is not in
\Phi(H,T)
, hence
not in
\Phi(C_{G}(X^{*}_{\text{ss}})^{\circ},T)
), we have that
the image in
k
of
p
or
\ell(\beta)
equals zero.

If the image of
p
in
k
equals zero, then

\varpi^{\vee}(\mu_{p})
is a non-trivial, connected subgroup of

Z(H)/Z(G)
.

Thus we may, and do, suppose that the image of
p
is non-zero, so
the image of
\ell(\beta)
equals zero.
By inspection of the Dynkin diagrams, this means that
the characteristic of
k
is
2
,
the component of
\Phi(G,T)
containing
\beta
is
of type
\mathsf{F}_{4}
, and, in the
Bourbaki numbering
[
3
, Planche VIII]
,

\beta
equals
\alpha_{2}
.
Then each component of
\Phi(H,T)
is simply laced
(in fact, of type
\mathsf{A}_{2}
), so

\Phi(C_{G}(X^{*}_{\text{ss}})^{\circ},T)=\Phi(C_{H}(X^{*}_{\text{ss}})^{\circ},T)
is
closed in
\Phi(H,T)
.
Since also
\mathbb{Z}\Phi(C_{G}(X^{*}_{\text{ss}})^{\circ},T)
equals

\mathbb{Z}\Phi(H,T)
, we have that in fact

\Phi(C_{G}(X^{*}_{\text{ss}})^{\circ},T)
equals
\Phi(H,T)
, so

C_{G}(X^{*}_{\text{ss}})^{\circ}
equals
H
.
Now, continuing with the Bourbaki numbering, we have that

3\beta=3\alpha_{2}
equals

\tilde{\alpha}-2\alpha_{1}-4\alpha_{3}-2\alpha_{4}
.
Still using
[
3
, Planche VIII]
, we have that

\alpha_{1}
,
\alpha_{2}
, and
\tilde{\alpha}
are long, while

\alpha_{3}
and
\alpha_{4}

equals

In particular,

lies in the span of

equals

, is the singleton

.
Thus

equals

\Phi(C_{G}(X^{*}_{\text{ss}})^{\circ},T)=\Phi(H,T)
.

∎

Conjecture
8.4
(i)
is related to
Lemma
8.2
, and
would follow from it if semisimple parts of

In light of Section

,
Conjecture

is
the strongest possible uniqueness that

Conjecture 8.4
.

(i)

The group
C_{G}(X^{*}_{\text{ss}})^{\circ}
contains
a maximal torus in
C_{G}(X^{*})^{\circ}
.

(ii)

Every semisimple part of
X^{*}
belongs to
the
C_{G}(X^{*})^{\circ}(k)
-orbit of
X^{*}_{\text{ss}}
.

We are not able to prove Conjecture
8.4
, but
we can prove a better uniqueness result than
Theorem
1.1
(iii)
.
We state it as Theorem
8.6
below.
We need a few ingredients first.

First we prove Lemma
8.5
, which,
in light of our proof of Theorem

, seems
likely to be a useful ingredient in an eventual proof of

8.4
(ii)
.

Lemma 8.5
.

The multiplicative-type group
Z
is contained in
C_{G}(X^{*})
;
the reduced, connected centralizer

C_{G}(X^{*})_{\text{red}}^{\circ}
is
contained in
H
; and,
for every central subgroup
\mathcal{Z}
of
G
,
the Lie algebra
\Lie(C_{G}(X^{*})/\mathcal{Z})
is contained in

\Lie(H/\mathcal{Z})
.

Proof.

The image of
X^{*}
in
\mathfrak{h}^{*}
is obviously fixed by
Z
, so

X^{*}
itself is fixed by
Z
, so

Z
is contained in
C_{G}(X^{*})
.

The natural map

\Lie(G)\to\Lie(G/\mathcal{Z})
restricts to an isomorphism of

\mathfrak{h}^{\perp}
onto
a complement to
\Lie(H/\mathcal{Z})
in
\Lie(G/\mathcal{Z})
.
Suppose that

Y\in\Lie(H/\mathcal{Z})
and
Y^{\perp}\in\mathfrak{h}^{\perp}
satisfy

Y^{\perp}\neq 0
and

Y+Y^{\perp}\in\Lie(C_{G}(X^{*})/\mathcal{Z})
, so that

\ad^{*}(Y^{\perp})(X^{*})
equals
-\ad^{*}(Y)(X^{*})
.

Let
\alpha
be a lowest root
(with respect to the height on
\Phi(G,T)
determined by
B
)
such that the
T
-equivariant projection of

Y^{\perp}
on
\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}
is
non-zero.
There is a unique element

e_{-\alpha}
of
\mathfrak{g}_{-\alpha}
such that
the
T
-equivariant projection of

[Y^{\perp},e_{-\alpha}]
on
\mathfrak{t}
is
h_{\alpha}
.
Then
[Y^{\perp},e_{-\alpha}]
lies in

h_{\alpha}+\mathfrak{b}
, so

\ad^{*}(Y^{\perp})(X^{*})(e_{-\alpha})=-X^{*}([Y^{\perp},e_{-\alpha}])
equals

-X^{*}_{\text{ss}}(h_{\alpha})\neq 0
.
On the other hand, since

-[Y,e_{-\alpha}]
belongs to
\mathfrak{h}^{\perp}
, on which

X^{*}
is vanishes, we have that

-\ad^{*}(Y)(X^{*})(e_{-\alpha})=X^{*}([Y,e_{-\alpha}])
equals
0
.
This is a contradiction.

It follows that
\Lie(C_{G}(X^{*})/\mathcal{Z})
is
contained in
\Lie(H/\mathcal{Z})
for
all central subgroups
\mathcal{Z}
of
G
.
In particular,

\Lie(C_{G}(X^{*}))
is contained in
\Lie(H)
.
Now
C_{G}(X^{*})_{\text{red}}\cap H
equals

C_{C_{G}(X^{*})_{\text{red}}}(Z)
, hence is smooth

[
5
, Proposition A.8.10(2)]
, hence equals

C_{H}(X^{*})_{\text{red}}
.
In particular,

\Lie(C_{H}(X^{*})_{\text{red}}^{\circ})=\Lie(C_{H}(X^{*})_{\text{red}})
equals

\Lie(C_{G}(X^{*})_{\text{red}})\cap H)=\Lie(C_{G}(X^{*})_{\text{red}})\cap\Lie(H)
, which,
by the previous argument, equals

\Lie(C_{G}(X^{*})_{\text{red}})
.
Thus

C_{H}(X^{*})_{\text{red}}^{\circ}
equals
C_{G}(X^{*})_{\text{red}}^{\circ}

[
10
, Proposition 10.15]
, so

C_{G}(X^{*})_{\text{red}}^{\circ}
is contained in
H
.
∎

Since
H
equals
C_{G}(X^{*}_{\text{ss}})
if and only if

\Phi(C_{G}(X^{*}_{\text{ss}}),T)
is closed in

,

Since

indirectly

Theorem

may thus be viewed as a uniform way of describing

Theorem 8.6
.

There is a unique closed orbit in the closure of the

H
-orbit of
X^{*}_{\text{ss}}
, and it contains
the semisimple part of every Jordan decomposition of
X^{*}
.

Proof.

We reason by induction on
\dim(G)
.
The result is obvious if
G
is zero-dimensional.
We assume that the result is known for
all groups of smaller dimension than
G
.

Since restriction to
H
provides an

H
-equivariant isomorphism of
the closed,
H
-stable subspace
(\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{h}^{\perp})^{*}
of
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
with

\mathfrak{h}^{*}
, the existence of
a unique closed orbit follows from
Theorem
1.1
(iii)
.
We have by
[
12
, Lemma 3.6]
that

X^{*}_{\text{ss}}
belongs to this unique closed orbit, so
it remains only to show that the semisimple part of
every Jordan decomposition of
X^{*}
is

H(k)
-conjugate to
X^{*}_{\text{ss}}
.

Suppose that
X^{{*}\,\prime}_{\text{ss}}
is
the semisimple part of
another Jordan decomposition of
X^{*}
.
We write
Z^{\prime}
for the schematic centre of

C_{G}(X^{{*}\,\prime}_{\text{ss}})^{\circ}
, and put

H^{\prime}=C_{G}(Z^{\prime})
.
Lemma
8.5
gives the containments

Z^{\prime}\subset C_{G}(X^{*})
, hence

\Lie(Z^{\prime}/Z(G))\subset\Lie(C_{G}(X^{*})/Z(G))
; and

\Lie(C_{G}(X^{*})/Z(G))=\Lie(C_{G/Z(G)}(X^{*}))\subset\Lie(C_{G/Z(G)}(Z))=\Lie(H/Z(G))
.
Thus,

\Lie((Z^{\prime}\cap H)/Z(G))=\Lie(Z^{\prime}/Z(G))\cap\Lie(H/Z(G))

equals

\Lie(Z^{\prime}/Z(G))
, which is non-trivial by
Lemma
8.3
; so

Z^{\prime}\cap H
is not contained in
Z(G)
.

By Lemma
8.2
, there is a semisimple part

Y^{*}_{\text{ss}}
of
X^{*}
that is fixed by

\langle Z,Z^{\prime}\cap H\rangle
.
In particular,
Y^{*}_{\text{ss}}
lies in
(\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{h}^{\perp})^{*}
.
Write
Z_{Y^{*}_{\text{ss}}}
for the schematic centre of

C_{G}(Y^{*}_{\text{ss}})
.

Since
the restrictions to
\mathfrak{h}
of
X^{*}_{\text{ss}}
and
Y^{*}_{\text{ss}}
are
semisimple parts of Jordan decompositions of
the restriction of
X^{*}
,
we have by Theorem
1.1
(iii)
that
the restrictions of
X^{*}_{\text{ss}}
and
Y^{*}_{\text{ss}}
, hence

X^{*}_{\text{ss}}
and
Y^{*}_{\text{ss}}
themselves, are
conjugate by an element of
H(k)
.
In particular,
Z_{Y^{*}_{\text{ss}}}
is
H(k)
-conjugate to,
hence equals,
Z
.

Since
Z^{\prime}\cap H
is not contained in
Z(G)
, we have that

C_{G}(Z^{\prime}\cap H)^{\circ}
is a proper subgroup of
G
.
Note that
C_{C_{G}(Z^{\prime}\cap H)^{\circ}}(Z^{\prime})^{\circ}
equals
H^{\prime}
.
Therefore, our inductive result gives that

X^{{*}\,\prime}_{\text{ss}}
and
Y^{*}_{\text{ss}}
are conjugate by
an element of
H^{\prime}(k)
.
In particular,
Z_{Y^{*}_{\text{ss}}}
is
H^{\prime}(k)
-conjugate to,
hence equals,
Z^{\prime}
.

We therefore have that
Z
and
Z^{\prime}
are equal to

Z_{Y^{*}_{\text{ss}}}
, hence to each other;
so
H
equals
H^{\prime}
; so

X^{*}_{\text{ss}}
,
Y^{*}_{\text{ss}}
, and

∎

9.
Uniqueness of Jordan decompositions for semisimple and nilpotent elements

Since we have unqualified uniqueness of Jordan decompositions on
\mathfrak{g}
,
in this section we discuss only Jordan decompositions on
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
.

As we observe in Remark
9.1
,
our first uniqueness result
Theorem
1.1
(iii)
implies that
a nilpotent element can have only a trivial Jordan decomposition.
(see Remark
9.1
).
Even our improved uniqueness result Theorem
8.6
does not
immediately rule out the possibility that a semisimple elements can have a
non-trivial Jordan decomposition.
This fact would follow from
Conjecture
8.4
(ii)
, but
we cannot prove that conjecture.
Nonetheless, we prove in Lemma
9.2
that
this pathological situation does not arise.

Remark 9.1
.

Theorem
1.1
(iii)
shows that,
if
X^{*}\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}
is nilpotent, then
the semisimple part of
every Jordan decomposition of
X^{*}
is trivial.

Lemma 9.2
.

Suppose that
X^{*}\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}
is semisimple. Then
the nilpotent part of
every Jordan decomposition of
X^{*}
is
0
.

Proof.

Let
X^{*}=X^{{*}\,\prime}_{\text{ss}}+X^{{*}\,\prime}_{\text{nil}}
be a Jordan decomposition.

By
[
12
, Lemma 3.6]
, there is
a cocharacter
\lambda
of

C_{G}(X^{{*}\,\prime}_{\text{ss}})
such that

\lim_{t\to 0}\Ad^{*}(\lambda(t))X^{{*}\,\prime}_{\text{nil}}
equals
0
, so

\lim_{t\to 0}\Ad^{*}(\lambda(t))X^{*}
equals
X^{{*}\,\prime}_{\text{ss}}
.
Since the orbit of
X^{*}
is closed by
Theorem
1.1
(i)
, we have that

X^{{*}\,\prime}_{\text{ss}}
belongs to
\Ad^{*}(G)X^{*}
.
In particular, the map

\mathbb{A}^{1}\setminus\{0\}\rightarrow\Ad^{*}(G)X^{*}

given by

t\mapsto\Ad^{*}(\lambda(t))X^{*}
extends to a map

\mathbb{A}^{1}\rightarrow\Ad^{*}(G)X^{*}
.
Write
\mathcal{C}
for this image curve.

Let
T
be a maximal torus in
C_{G}(X^{{*}\,\prime}_{\text{ss}})
containing
the image of
\lambda
.
Suppose that
X^{{*}\,\prime}_{\text{nil}}
does not equal
0
.
Then there is some
root
\alpha\in\Phi(C_{G}(X^{{*}\,\prime}_{\text{ss}})^{\circ},T)
such that
the
T
-equivariant projection
(X^{{*}\,\prime}_{\text{nil}})_{\alpha}
of
X^{{*}\,\prime}_{\text{nil}}
on

(\mathfrak{g}^{*})_{\alpha}
is non-trivial.
We may, and do, choose
\alpha
that minimizes

\langle\alpha,\lambda\rangle
among such roots.
Then
\langle\alpha,\lambda\rangle
is positive.

By
[
8
, Lemma 3.1iv)]
,

\Lie(C_{G}(X^{{*}\,\prime}_{\text{ss}})_{\text{red}})
equals
C_{\mathfrak{g}}(X^{{*}\,\prime}_{\text{ss}})
, i.e.
C_{G}(X^{{*}\,\prime}_{\text{ss}})
is smooth. Therefore (as in e.g.
[
2
, pp. 98, Proposition 6.7]
) the canonical identification of
the tangent space
T_{X^{{*}\,\prime}_{\text{ss}}}(\mathfrak{g}^{*})
with

\mathfrak{g}^{*}
identifies

T_{X^{{*}\,\prime}_{\text{ss}}}(\Ad^{*}(G)X^{*})
with

\ad^{*}(\mathfrak{g})X^{{*}\,\prime}_{\text{ss}}
. Also

T_{X^{{*}\,\prime}_{\text{ss}}}(\mathcal{C})
is identified with
a subspace whose
T
-equivariant projection on

(\mathfrak{g}^{*})_{\alpha}
includes
(X^{{*}\,\prime}_{\text{nil}})_{\alpha}
, hence
is non-trivial.
However, the
T
-equivariant projection of

\ad^{*}(\mathfrak{g})X^{{*}\,\prime}_{\text{ss}}
on
(\mathfrak{g}^{*})_{\alpha}
is trivial,
which is a contradiction.
∎

Remark
3.4
shows that
we may lift a Jordan decomposition from
the dual Lie algebra of
an arbitrary full-rank subgroup.
Corollary
9.3
provides a partial converse.

Corollary 9.3
.

Suppose that
H
is a full-rank subgroup of
G
such that

\mathfrak{h}
admits an
H
-stable complement
\mathfrak{h}^{\perp}
in
\mathfrak{g}
, and

is semisimple, respectively nilpotent, and

\mathfrak{h}^{\perp}
.
Then the restriction of
X^{*}
to
\mathfrak{h}
is
a semisimple, respectively nilpotent, element of
\mathfrak{h}^{*}
.

Proof.

Write
X^{*}_{H}
for the restriction of
X^{*}
to
\mathfrak{h}
, and
let
X^{*}_{H}=X^{*}_{H\,{\text{ss}}}+X^{*}_{H\,{\text{nil}}}
be
a Jordan decomposition.
Then extension of
X^{*}_{H\,{\text{ss}}}
and
X^{*}_{H\,{\text{nil}}}

trivially across
\mathfrak{h}^{\perp}
to
elements
X^{*}_{\text{ss}}
and
X^{*}_{\text{nil}}
of
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
gives
a Jordan decomposition
X^{*}=X^{*}_{\text{ss}}+X^{*}_{\text{nil}}

(by Remark
3.4
), so
Lemma
9.2
gives that

X^{*}_{\text{nil}}
is trivial
if
X^{*}
is semisimple, or
Remark
9.1
gives that

X^{*}_{\text{ss}}
is trivial
if
X^{*}
is nilpotent.
This means that
X^{*}_{H}=X^{*}_{H\,{\text{ss}}}+X^{*}_{H\,{\text{nil}}}

equals

X^{*}_{H\,{\text{ss}}}
, respectively

,
and so is
semisimple, respectively nilpotent.
∎

Study protocol for a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled clinical trial of desidustat oral tablet in sickle cell disease: a phase IIa proof-of-concept evaluation
Authors: Mayank Gangwar, Gunjan Kumar, Sudipto Roy, Sanjeev Gupta, Deven Parmar, Kevinkumar Kansagra, Suchi Shah, Aparna Mukherjee
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundSickle cell disease (SCD) is a genetic disorder characterized by chronic hemolysis and vaso-occlusive crises. Desidustat, an oral hypoxia-inducible factor prolyl hydroxylase inhibitor (HIF-PHI), has shown promise in treating SCD by stimulating erythropoietin production in a similar manner that happens in response to hypoxia, promoting erythropoiesis and improving iron metabolism, leading to increased hemoglobin and RBC indices.MethodsThis was a phase IIa, double-blind, randomized, placebo-controlled, parallel-group, proof-of-concept, multicenter clinical trial designed to evaluate the efficacy and safety of desidustat in Indian adults with sickle cell disease (SCD). A total of 24 participants were enrolled based on predefined eligibility criteria, with 8 subjects per dose cohort (50 mg, 100 mg, and 150 mg), randomized in a 3:1 ratio (desidustat:placebo). The study included a screening period of up to 4 weeks, an 8-week treatment phase, and a follow-up visit at week 10, totaling 98 days. Efficacy (hemoglobin response rate) was assessed at week 8 relative to baseline compared to placebo. Dose adjustment of desidustat at week 4 was guided by hemoglobin levels measured using a calibrated HemoCue instrument.DiscussionManagement of sickle cell disease (SCD) has limited treatment modalities available like hydroxyurea and blood transfusions. They offer benefits but their limitations underscore the ongoing need for safer, more effective, and accessible treatments. This proof-of-concept trial was conducted to investigate the efficacy and safety of desidustat oral tablet, 3 times per week for 8 weeks in SCD patients. Results from this trial could aid in establishing a cost-efficient therapeutic option for managing rare diseases like SCD.Trial registrationThe study was approved by Central Drugs Standard Control Organization (CDSCO: CT NOC number CT/SND/27/2023, protocol number DESI.23.001, version no. 01 registered on 12 June 2023) and was conducted for a new drug as per the GCP guidelines. The first subject was screened on 7 January 2025 and the last visit of the last subject was conducted on 31 July 2025, followed by clinical study report finalization on 29 September 2025.

Introduction

Background and rationale {6a}

Sickle cell disease (SCD) is a hereditary hemoglobinopathy caused by a single point mutation (GAG to GTG) in the β-globin gene, resulting in the substitution of glutamic acid with valine at position six (βGlu6Val) [1]. This mutation leads to the production of hemoglobin S (HbS), which polymerizes under deoxygenated conditions, distorting red blood cells (RBCs) into a sickled shape. These sickled RBCs are rigid and adhesive, leading to microvascular occlusion, recurrent vaso-occlusive crises (VOCs), chronic hemolytic anemia, and progressive multi-organ damage. The lifespan of sickled RBCs is significantly reduced (approximately 16 days) compared to normal RBCs (approximately 120 days), contributing to the chronic anemia observed in SCD patients. The pathophysiology of SCD involves a complex interplay of four interrelated processes: HbS polymerization, impaired blood flow and vaso-occlusion, hemolysis-mediated endothelial dysfunction, and sterile inflammation triggered by innate immune pathways. These mechanisms collectively result in acute and chronic complications such as pain crises, stroke, acute chest syndrome, and organ failure [2]. These complications are prominently associated with homozygous sickle cell disease (HbSS: mutation inherited from both parents). Current treatment strategies primarily focus on symptom management and reduction of disease severity, but curative approaches such as hematopoietic stem cell transplantation are limited by donor availability and associated risks.

Hydroxyurea (HU), the first FDA-approved disease-modifying therapy for SCD, exerts its benefits primarily by inducing fetal hemoglobin (HbF) production, which inhibits HbS polymerization. However, HU has limitations including variable efficacy, poor adherence, and myelosuppressive side effects [3, 4]. In 2019, crizanlizumab, an anti-P-selectin monoclonal antibody, was approved by USFDA to reduce the frequency of vaso-occlusive crises (VOCs) [5]. However, SCD remains a disease with considerable unmet clinical need.

Desidustat, a novel hypoxia-inducible factor prolyl hydroxylase (HIF-PHD) inhibitor, offers a promising approach for SCD by pharmacologically stimulating erythropoiesis through activation of the HIF pathway. This mechanism mimics the physiological response to hypoxia, increasing endogenous erythropoietin (EPO) production and supporting red blood cell production. Desidustat has demonstrated efficacy and safety in phase I–III trials for treating anemia in chronic kidney disease (CKD) patients, both on dialysis and not on dialysis, and has got marketing approval in India in March 2022 [6].

In vitro studies have shown that desidustat can reduce sodium metabisulfite-induced sickling in RBCs from SCD patients, suggesting potential disease-modifying activity. Preclinical studies further support its safety, bioavailability, and hematopoietic efficacy across various animal models. Desidustat exhibited minimal genotoxicity and no significant adverse effects on reproductive parameters in animal studies [7].

Clinically, desidustat was well tolerated up to 300 mg in phase I trials [8] and showed a dose-dependent increase in hemoglobin levels in CKD patients during subsequent phase II and III studies, with no serious adverse events or persistent lab abnormalities reported [9]. Given this encouraging evidence, a phase IIa proof-of-concept clinical trial has been designed to evaluate the safety and efficacy of desidustat in adults with SCD.

The trial involves administration of desidustat oral tablets (50, 100, or 150 mg) three times per week over an 8-week period to assess changes in hemoglobin level while monitoring adverse effects. This trial aims to prove the potential of desidustat as a novel, orally administered therapy that could address the limitations of current treatments, such as hydroxyurea and blood transfusions, with a mechanism that complements and potentially surpasses existing therapies. Desidustat has the potential to be an integral part of SCD management, improving patient outcomes, quality of life, and cost-effectiveness.

Objectives {7}

The aim of this phase IIa study was to evaluate the efficacy and safety of desidustat oral tablet three times per week for 8 weeks in sickle cell disease patients compared to placebo. The primary objective was to assess the efficacy of desidustat in SCD patients (proportion of patients with hemoglobin response, defined as ≥1 g/dL increase in Hb from baseline). Secondary objectives included evaluation of additional efficacy parameters like mean change in hemoglobin, markers of hemolysis (LDH, total, direct, and indirect bilirubin, absolute and percentage reticulocyte count, serum haptoglobin, and serum potassium), and mean change in percentage of HbS. Safety assessment included the proportion of patients experiencing TEAEs, SAEs, and any clinically significant abnormality based on physical examination and safety laboratory parameters with the proportion of patients requiring blood transfusion or experiencing vaso-occlusive crisis.

Trial design {8}

This was a multicenter, phase IIa, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, parallel-group, proof-of-concept clinical trial evaluating the efficacy and safety of desidustat in adult Indian patients with SCD. A total of 24 participants were enrolled and randomized into three dose cohorts (50 mg, 100 mg, and 150 mg), each including 6 participants receiving desidustat and 2 receiving placebo, administered orally three times a week for 8 weeks. The study consisted of screening period of up to 4 weeks, treatment period of 8 weeks, and end of the study visit at week 10. The total duration of the study will be 98 days including a screening period of 28 days. After signing the informed consent form, participants were screened for eligibility based on protocol-defined inclusion and exclusion criteria. Informed consent was obtained from all eligible participants before performing any study-related activity. Each participant who deemed eligible was randomly assigned to receive desidustat or placebo in any of the cohorts. Randomization in all 3 cohorts was done simultaneously using a centralized IWRS system. Participants in the test arm received desidustat (dose as per the cohort) oral tablet and participants in the placebo arm received placebo. The timeline of scheduled visits was as follows: screening visit (visit 1: day −28), randomization visit (visit 2: day 0), and follow-up visits: visit 3 (day 14 ± 3 days), visit 4 (day 28 ± 3 days), visit 5 (day 42 ± 3 days), visit 6 (day 56 ± 3 days), along with safety follow-up (visit 7; day 70 ± 3 days) after 2 weeks of EOT.

Interim blinded safety data from the first 12 participants were reviewed by an independent Data Safety Monitoring Board (DSMB). Efficacy was assessed by change in hemoglobin levels and other hematologic parameters as mentioned in end-point assessments. All the safety assessments were conducted in line with the schedule of assessment of the study protocol which included: monitoring vital signs (blood pressure, pulse, SpO2, respiratory rate, and temperature), physical examination, treatment-emergent adverse events and clinical safety laboratory parameters (in hematology: hematocrit, hemoglobin, mean corpuscular hemoglobin, mean corpuscular volume, MCHC, reticulocyte count (absolute and percentage), platelet count, mean platelet volume, red blood cell count, white blood cell count, differential WBC count (absolute and percentage), red cell distribution width, peripheral smear for malaria and in biochemistry: liver function test: aspartate aminotransferase, alanine aminotransferase, alkaline phosphatase, gamma-glutamyl transferase, total protein, albumin and total bilirubin (with direct and indirect bilirubin), renal function test: blood urea nitrogen, serum creatinine, creatinine clearance, estimated glomerular filtration rate, uric acid, serum LDH, serum electrolytes: potassium, sodium, chloride, random blood glucose, vitamin B12, folic acid, and HbA1c). For cardiac safety, ECGs, transthoracic echocardiogram, and NT-pro BNP were done along with urine analysis including urine chemistry and microscopy. For women with childbearing potential, serum pregnancy test and urine pregnancy tests were conducted.

Interventions

Explanation for the choice of comparators {6b}

The dose regimens of 50 mg, 100 mg, and 150 mg desidustat tablets three times a week for oral administration along with matching placebo were selected to assess efficacy, safety, and dose optimization for future clinical development. The doses were selected based on previous clinical trials conducted.

Intervention description {11a}

Participants were randomly assigned to receive either desidustat or matching placebo in one of three treatment cohorts (50 mg, 100 mg, or 150 mg; Fig. 1). The study drug was administered three times per week for a duration of 8 weeks, with at least 48 h between consecutive doses. Investigational products (IPs) were packaged, labeled, and supplied by the sponsor in accordance with regulatory requirements. The investigator was responsible for confirming receipt and maintaining accurate records of IP inventory, dispensing, and return. IPs were stored securely (at temperatures below 30 °C and with documentation of temperature logs) at the site and returned to the sponsor or destroyed as per protocol following drug accountability procedures. Participants were advised not to consume any food 1 h before and 2 h after the administration of study drug.Fig. 1Dose adjustment regimen

Dose adjustment regimen

Criteria for discontinuing or modifying allocated interventions {11b}

Dose modifications were performed at week 4 based on changes in hemoglobin (Hb) levels compared to baseline using a calibrated HemoCue device (average of two readings) from EDTA or fresh blood samples. Dose adjustments were guided by absolute hemoglobin (Hb) level and change from baseline, with dose escalation for inadequate response (Hb < 10 g/dL with <1.0 g/dL rise), dose maintenance for appropriate response, dose reduction for rapid Hb increases (>2.0 g/dL or Hb > 11 g/dL with ≥1.0 g/dL rise), and temporary treatment interruption for Hb > 12 g/dL, followed by reinitiation at a lower dose (25 mg lower from current dose, Fig. 1) once Hb declined to <11.5 g/dL (Table 1). These adjustments were intended to safely guide treatment toward the desired hemoglobin response while minimizing the risk of excessive erythropoiesis. The dose adjustment matrix (Table 1) was integrated into the IWRS system of the study, wherein site staff entered the hemoglobin values of baseline and week 4 and the required dose of the desidustat was derived automatically. Strategies to improve adherence to interventions {11c}

Participants were given a patient diary to record consumption of trial medication. Designated site staff reviewed patient diary and sensitized participant at each visit for treatment compliance.

Relevant concomitant care permitted or prohibited during the trial {11d}

Oral or intravenous iron, vitamin B12, and folic acid supplementations were permitted to maintain adequate iron status for Hb response during the trial based on investigator’s discretion. Treatment of common ailments (influenza, headache, nausea) and concurrent non-communicable disease (diabetes mellitus, hypertension) were allowed during trial and to be documented in CRF. Concomitant medications for pain management of vaso-occlusive crisis were permitted. Participants who were already on therapy for sickle cell disease (crizanlizumab, L-glutamine, and hydroxyurea) at screening continued to receive the treatment at same dose throughout the study. Initiation or dose modification of treatment with these agents during the trial was not permitted.

Provisions for post-trial care {30}

As this was a proof-of-concept clinical trial, no post-trial care or continued access to the investigational product was provided to participants following completion of the study. All subjects were closely monitored throughout the study duration, and if any medical issues arose related to study participation, appropriate medical care was provided as per protocol and applicable regulatory and ethical guidelines. Potential post-trial access can be considered post conduct of larger and longer duration clinical trial.

Participant timeline {13}

The study comprises a screening period (up to 28 days), an 8-week treatment period, and a follow-up visit at week 10. Dose adjustment is permitted at week 4 based on hemoglobin response, with a maximum dose cap of 150 mg TID weekly (Fig. 2). The duration of study was selected as 8 weeks based on the hemoglobin response observed during pre-clinical and clinical studies of desidustat conducted in chronic kidney disease patients.Fig. 2Detailed study workflow for clinical trial procedure

Detailed study workflow for clinical trial procedure

Sample size {14}

Given the proof-of-concept nature of the study, no formal sample size calculation was done, and 24 participants (6 on desidustat and 2 on placebo per cohort) were enrolled.

Recruitment {15}

To ensure timely recruitment, sites with proven feasibility and patient availability were selected. Proactive pre-screening, clear eligibility criteria, engagement of investigators, and follow-up monitoring site visits supported efficient enrollment.

Assignment of interventions: allocation

Sequence generation {16a}

Participants were randomly assigned to receive either desidustat or placebo across three parallel cohorts based on a computer-generated randomization schedule using SAS® software (version 9.4 or higher, SAS Institute Inc., USA). Blinding was maintained using visually identical placebo tablets for both participants and study personnel, including investigators. The first dose of the study drug was administered under medical supervision.

Concealment mechanism {16b}

This study had a central integrated IWRS system for the random assignment of participants across all three arms. The service was provided by a third-party vendor.

Implementation {16c}

The independent biostatistician from the sponsor prepared a randomization list which was provided to the third-party vendor to be integrated in the IWRS system followed by the enrollment of subjects by the site using the software for central random assignment.

Assignment of interventions: blinding

Who will be blinded {17a}

In this double-blind trial, both participants and investigators along with the site staff were unaware of treatment allocation. Blinding was ensured by using a placebo that looks identical to the investigational product.

Procedure for unblinding if needed {17b}

In a medical emergency, the investigator could unblind a participant’s treatment, after notifying the sponsor. The unblinding details were provided by sponsor designated independent statistician.

Data collection and management

Plans for assessment and collection of outcomes {18a}

All data generated by the site personnel were captured at each study center using case report forms (CRFs). Data from external sources (such as laboratory data) were imported/entered into the database. Source documents were filed at the investigator’s site.

Plans to promote participant retention and complete follow-up {18b}

To promote participant retention, regular site monitoring visits were conducted along with setting up reminder systems for participants. Any barriers to follow-up were promptly identified and addressed.

Data management {19}

A separate data management plan was prepared to address data entry in the electronic data capture (EDC) system. An independent third-party vendor was appointed to provide the EDC platform, and they provided their services in maintaining data quality to the sponsor.

Confidentiality {27}

Participant confidentiality was ensured by assigning unique identification codes to each participant, storage of data in a secured password-protected system, confidential monitoring and audit visits, and anonymized published results.

Plans for collection, laboratory evaluation, and storage of biological specimens for genetic or molecular analysis in this trial/future use {33}

Statistical methods

Statistical methods for primary and secondary outcomes {20a}

Statistical analysis was performed using SAS® software (version 9.4 or higher). No formal hypothesis testing was done. All continuous variables were summarized using descriptive statistics such as n, mean, standard deviation, minimum, median, and maximum. Categorical variables were summarized using frequency and percentage. Demographics and baseline characteristics were summarized by assigned treatment. Subgroup analysis of subjects on stable dose (60 days) of standard of care (hydroxyurea, L-glutamine, crizanlizumab) was done for primary outcome assessment.

Count and percentages were evaluated as descriptive analysis for the proportion of patients with Hb response (defined as ≥1 g/dL increase in Hb from baseline) and mean change from baseline (post dose value − pre dose value) compared to placebo (time frame: baseline to week 8). Descriptive statistics was used to evaluate all secondary endpoints including biomarkers related to hemolysis and safety assessment, stratified by assigned treatment.

At week 4, number of subjects who required dose adjustment in any direction (increase or decrease) as well as dose interruption due to achievement of hemoglobin level of 12 g/dL were also evaluated.

Discussion

India accounts for 14.5% of the global SCD newborns, around over 42,000 a year, second to sub-Saharan Africa. The prevalence of sickle cell carriers among tribal groups varies from 1 to 40% [10]. HbSC type variant has one sickle (S) and one hemoglobin (C) gene inheritance, which is a milder version and differs in pathophysiology from HbSS with regard to formation of crystals of RBC.

Current treatment options, such as hydroxyurea and blood transfusions, have limitations including variable efficacy, side effects, and accessibility issues. Therefore, there is an urgent need for novel, effective, and accessible therapies to manage anemia associated with SCD.

Desidustat, an oral hypoxia-inducible factor prolyl hydroxylase inhibitor (HIF-PHI), has been developed to stimulate endogenous erythropoietin production, thereby promoting erythropoiesis and improving iron metabolism. In phase I trial, desidustat was safe and well tolerated till 300 mg in healthy subjects. In phase Ib/IIa trial, desidustat till 150 mg was safe and well tolerated in subjects with anemia in dialysis-dependent chronic kidney disease subjects. In phase II trial, three doses (100, 150, and 200 mg) of desidustat were administered to the pre-dialysis CKD subjects. Two phase III trials have been conducted with 100 mg initial dose in CKD subjects not dependent on dialysis and 100 mg/125 mg/150 mg (based on prior ESA use) in subjects dependent on dialysis [11].

Phase III trials concluded that these doses are sufficient to raise hemoglobin to the target range of (10–12 g/dL) and maintain throughout the trial without any safety concerns, and based on these studies desidustat got marketing approval in India for treating anemia in chronic kidney disease (CKD) patients (non-dialysis and dialysis-dependent subjects) [11]. These findings provide a rationale for exploring its potential benefits in SCD patients. In this study, doses of 50 mg, 100 mg, and 150 mg desidustat tablets have been selected to optimize the therapeutic dose for sickle cell disease subjects.

Proposed mechanism of action of desidustat in SCD

The current phase IIa, double-blind, randomized, placebo-controlled, multicenter, proof-of-concept study aims to evaluate the efficacy and safety of desidustat in adult Indian patients with SCD. The study design includes three dosing cohorts (50 mg, 100 mg, and 150 mg) to assess the dose-response relationship and identify the optimal therapeutic dose. Participants received desidustat or placebo orally three times a week for 8 weeks, with the primary endpoint being the proportion of patients achieving a hemoglobin response. Secondary endpoints include mean changes in hemoglobin level, biomarkers related to hemolysis (such as LDH, bilirubin, reticulocyte count, serum haptoglobin, and serum potassium), the proportion of patients requiring blood transfusions, the incidence of vaso-occlusive crises, and changes in the percentage of HbS.

Safety assessments involved monitoring vital signs, physical examination, treatment-emergent adverse events, clinical laboratory parameters (as mentioned in schedule of assessment), ECGs, transthoracic echocardiogram, NT-pro BNP, pregnancy test (urine and serum), and urine analysis (chemistry and microscopy). An independent DSMB monitored the study and reviewed interim safety data from the first 12 participants (four in each cohort) up to 14 days after randomization. This approach ensured participant safety and aligns with best practices in clinical research.

Potential risks of a participant’s study involvement included unknown AEs, general risks associated with frequent clinic visits, laboratory blood draws, and the associated pain and discomfort of phlebotomy. Strategies to mitigate these risks include close monitoring of lab values as well as AEs. The outcomes of this study could pave the way for larger, more definitive trials and potentially offer a new therapeutic option for managing anemia in SCD, contributing to the goals of the National Sickle Cell Anaemia Elimination Mission. The collaboration between Zydus Lifesciences and the Indian Council of Medical Research (ICMR) exemplifies a strategic public-private partnership aimed at addressing the unmet medical needs of SCD patients in India.

This study has certain limitations: the relatively small sample size may limit the statistical power of the study and restrict the ability to detect less pronounced treatment effects. As the incidence of HbSC is low in India, this study did not examine desidustat’s effect on the genotype. The comparatively short duration of follow-up may not be sufficient to fully assess long-term efficacy and safety outcomes. A well-designed phase 3 trial with a larger sample size and extended follow-up duration is planned to validate these findings and assess long-term efficacy and safety.

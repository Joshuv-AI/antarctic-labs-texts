Equivariant unirationality of Fano threefolds
Ivan Cheltsov, Yuri Tschinkel, Zhijia Zhang

Abstract
. We study unirationality of actions of finite groups on Fano threefolds with indices greater than one.



1.
Introduction

In this paper, we consider generically free regular actions of finite groups
G
on smooth projective varieties
X
over an algebraically closed field
k
of characteristic zero. The simplest such actions arise from linear representations, i.e., as projectivizations
{\mathbb{P}}(V)
of linear representations
V
of
G
. Note that not every faithful representation gives rise to a generically free action – one also requires that the representation intersects the central
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
trivially.
We are interested in birational properties of
G
-actions, e.g.,

•

(L)

linearizability
: equivariant birationality to a linear action,

•

(SL)

stable linearizability
: linearizability of
X\times{\mathbb{P}}(W)
,
where
W
is a
G
-representation; when the
G
-action on
X
is generically free, we may take
W
to be the trivial representation, by the no-name lemma.

These notions should be viewed as equivariant analogs of rationality and stable rationality in geometry over nonclosed fields: there are many similarities but also striking differences between these theories,
see, e.g.,
[
28
]
,
[
29
]
for an introduction to this circle of problems.

Here, we explore an equivariant version of unirationality:

•

(U)

G
-unirationality
:
existence of a dominant equivariant rational map

{\mathbb{P}}(V)\dashrightarrow X,

where
V
is a
G
-representation.

Clearly,

\mathbf{(L)}\Rightarrow\mathbf{(SL)}\Rightarrow\mathbf{(U)}.

In the definition of
(U)
, one can replace
{\mathbb{P}}(V)
by
V
, a
G
-representation, i.e.,
(U)
is equivalent to
very versality
of the
G
-action, in the terminology of, e.g.,
[
18
]
.

A necessary condition for
G
-unirationality is existence of fixed points upon restriction to abelian subgroups:

•

Condition (A)
:
for every abelian subgroup
A\subseteq G
one has

X^{A}\neq\emptyset.

This should be viewed as analogous to the existence of rational points, in the framework of birational geometry over nonclosed fields. Note that there do exist linearizable
G
-actions without
G
-fixed points, moreover, the existence of
G
-fixed points is not an equivariantly birational property, for nonabelian
G
. Thus, some standard unirationality constructions in geometry over nonclosed fields fail to apply in the equivariant setting.

Duncan proved that Condition
(A)
is also sufficient for
G
-unirationality of del Pezzo surfaces
of degree
\geq 3
, with generically free actions
[
17
, Theorem 1.4]
. The cases of del Pezzo surfaces of degree 2 and 1 remain open.

We turn to smooth Fano threefolds. These are classified by

•

r\in\{1,\ldots,10\}
– rank of the Picard group,

•

i\in\{1,2,3,4\}
– Fano index,

•

d=(-K_{X})^{3}/i^{3}
– degree.

Their rationality over algebraically closed fields is
almost
settled. There are still outstanding open problems concerning stable rationality and unirationality, e.g., unirationality of general quartic threefolds. Failure of stable rationality of very general cubic threefolds has been recently established
[
20
]
. Rationality properties of geometrically rational Fano threefolds have received a lot of attention, see
[
21
]
,
[
22
]
,
[
32
]
,
[
31
]
. There is also a wealth of results concerning (stable) linearizability of group actions on (possibly singular) Fano threefolds, see, e.g.,
[
34
]
,

[
11
]
,
[
10
]
,
[
8
]
,
[
24
]
,
[
6
]
,
[
13
]
.

In this paper, we focus on equivariant unirationality of Fano threefolds of index
\geq 2
; these should be viewed as 3-dimensional analogs of del Pezzo surfaces. As in the work of Duncan on del Pezzo surfaces
[
17
]
, Fano threefolds of
small
degree are currently out of reach – we do not even know the unirationality of any smooth Fano threefold of index
2
and degree
1
(all other Fano threefolds of index
\geq 2
are known to be unirational, over algebraically closed fields).

Our principal results are:

•

A generically free
G
-action on a smooth quadric threefold is stably linearizable if and only if it satisfies Condition
(A)
, Theorem
4.1
.

•

A generically free
G
-action on a smooth cubic threefold satisfying Condition
(A)
is
G
-unirational, with the possible exception of
G=C_{9}\rtimes C_{3}
, acting on the Fermat cubic threefold,
and
C_{11}\rtimes C_{5},\mathsf{PSL}_{2}({\mathbb{F}}_{11})
, acting on the Klein cubic threefold, and a 1-parameter family of cubics with an
{\mathfrak{A}}_{5}
-action,
Theorem
5.1
.

•

A generically free action on a cubic threefold with isolated singularities,
satisfying Condition
(A)
, is
G
-unirational, Theorem
6.1
.
This class contains cubic threefolds with cohomological obstructions to stable linearizability, see
[
10
]
,
[
8
]
.

•

A generically free
G
-action on a smooth complete intersection of two quadrics
X\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{5}
is
G
-unirational if and only if it satisfies Condition
(A)
, which turns out to be equivalent to
X^{G}\neq\emptyset
, Theorem
7.1
.

•

A generically free
G
-action on the (unique) smooth del Pezzo threefold
V_{5}
of degree
5
is linearizable except for
G={\mathfrak{A}}_{5}
, which is not linearizable, by
[
9
]
, but stably linearizable, by
[
5
, Example 7]
, see Remark
7.2
.

Here is the roadmap of the paper: in Section
2
we recall notions of
G
-birational geometry, with a focus on
G
-unirationality. In Section
3
we present several general unirationality constructions, applicable in the equivariant context. In Section
4
we specialize to quadric threefolds. Then we proceed to treat
smooth cubics in Section
5
, singular cubics in Section
6
, and
intersections of two quadrics in Section
7
.

2.
Generalities

Throughout, we work over an algebraically closed field
k
of characteristic zero; all varieties are assumed to be irreducible over
k
. A
G
-variety is a projective variety with a generically free regular
G
-action; we will also consider situations when the
G
-action is
not
generically free.
The
G
-fixed point locus of a
G
-variety
X

will be denoted by
X^{G}
. For smooth projective
G
-varieties
X
and
abelian
subgroups
A\subseteq G
, existence of
A
-fixed points is a birational property; this is not the case for nonabelian groups! Note that cyclic actions on rationally connected
G
-varieties always have fixed points, but this need not be the case for actions of abelian groups, e.g., the
C_{2}^{2}
-action on
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
generated by
t\to-t
and
t\to 1/t
where
t
is the affine coordinate on
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
.

If
\tilde{X}\to X
is a dominant morphism of
G
-varieties and
\tilde{X}
has
G
-fixed points, then so does
X
. Since
linear
actions of abelian groups always have fixed points,
a necessary condition for
G
-unirationality of an action of a finite group
G
on a smooth projective variety
X
is Condition
(A)
: existence of fixed points upon restriction to every abelian subgroup.

Linearizability and stable linearizability of generically free actions of finite groups on rational surfaces and rational Fano threefolds have been considered in, e.g.,
[
24
]
,
[
5
]
,
[
11
]
,
[
8
]
,
[
25
]
. The related notion of
G
-unirationality, or, equivalently very versality, plays an important role in the study of
essential dimension
, see, e.g.,
[
18
]
,
[
35
]
. However, only few concrete examples of
G
-unirational varieties failing (stable) linearizability are known, see
[
17
]
for a systematic treatment of del Pezzo surfaces of degree
\geq 3
.

The bridge between equivariant geometry and geometry over nonclosed fields is provided via
torsors
: For a field extension

K/k
and a
G
-torsor
T
over
K
, let

{}^{T}X
be the
T
-twist of
X
over
K
, defined as the quotient
{}^{T}X:=(X_{K}\times T)/G
by the diagonal
G
-action. A key result is:

Theorem 2.1
.

[
18
, Theorem 1.1]

Let
X
be a
G
-variety over
k
. Then

•

the
G
-action on
X
is stably linearizable if and only if for every field extension
K/k
and every
G
-torsor
T
over
K
, the twist
{}^{T}X
is stably rational over
K
,

•

the
G
-action on
X
is unirational if and only if every twist
{}^{T}X
as above is unirational over
K
.

We will apply this in subsequent sections to quadric and cubic hypersurfaces.

A related notion that plays a role in equivariant geometry is
the
Amitsur
group
\mathrm{Am}(X,G)
, defined in
[
3
, Section 6]
as the image of
G
-invariant divisor classes

\mathrm{Pic}(X)^{G}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\delta_{2}}}{{\longrightarrow}}{\mathrm{H}}^{2}(G,k^{\times}),

where
\delta_{2}
is the functorial homomorphism arising from the Leray spectral sequence, see, e.g.,
[
27
, Section 3]
.
By
[
3
, Theorem 6.1]
, this is a
G
-birational invariant, which vanishes for linear actions and as soon as
X^{G}\neq\emptyset
.
The vanishing of
\mathrm{Am}(X,G)
implies that every
G
-invariant divisor class in
\mathrm{Pic}(X)
is represented by a
G
-linearized line bundle on
X
.

All possibilities for
\mathrm{Am}(X,G)
for
G
-surfaces
X
have been determined in
[
3
, Proposition 6.7]
. In particular, this group is trivial for del Pezzo surfaces
S
of degree
\leq 6
when
\mathrm{Pic}(S)^{G}={\mathbb{Z}}
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
X
be a
G
-variety with nontrivial Amitsur invariant. Then

X
is not
G
-unirational.

Proof.

Assume that
X
is
G
-unirational. There exists a
G
-equivariant dominant rational map
\pi:{\mathbb{P}}(V)\dashrightarrow X.
Resolving the indeterminancy by blowups, we have a
G
-equivariant morphism
\tilde{\pi}:\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}{(V)}\to X

which
gives rise to an inclusion

\mathrm{Am}(X,G)\subseteq\mathrm{Am}(\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}(V),G)=\mathrm{Am}({\mathbb{P}}(V),G).

This is clear from the functoriality of the map
\delta_{2}
in the basic exact sequence (see
[
39
]
or
[
3
, Section 6]
)

0\to\mathrm{Hom}(G,k^{\times})\to\mathrm{Pic}(X,G)\to\mathrm{Pic}(X)^{G}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\delta_{2}}}{{\longrightarrow}}{\mathrm{H}}^{2}(G,k^{\times}),

where
\mathrm{Pic}(X,G)
is the group of isomorphism classes of
G
-linearized line bundles on
X
,
see, e.g.,
[
30
, Section 3]
. Since
\mathrm{Am}({\mathbb{P}}(V),G)=0
, we obtain a contradiction.
∎

Example 2.3
.

Note that the invariant
\mathrm{Am}(X,G)
does not only depend on the effective action on
X
. It also depends on the generic stabilizer. Let
X={\mathbb{P}}^{1}
with the action of the Klein four group
\bar{G}=C_{2}^{2}
; it is fixed point free. The Amitsur invariant
\mathrm{Am}(X,\bar{G})={\mathbb{Z}}/2
is nontrivial, and the
\bar{G}
-action is not stably linearizable. However,
X
admits a regular
non-generically free
action of the dihedral group
G=\mathfrak{D}_{4}
(of order 8), with generic stabilizer
C_{2}
.
As a variety with
G
-action (not necessarily generically free),
X
is clearly
G
-unirational: we have a dominant
G
-equivariant map
V\dashrightarrow X
, where
V
is an irreducible 2-dimensional representation of
G
. Of course, every abelian subgroup of
G
has fixed points on
X
.

Recall the definition of
Bogomolov multiplier
:

\mathrm{B}_{0}(G):=\mathrm{Ker}\left({\mathrm{H}}^{2}(G,k^{\times})\to\oplus_{A}\,{\mathrm{H}}^{2}(A,k^{\times})\right),

where
A
runs over all abelian subgroups of
G
. In fact, it suffices to consider
bicyclic
subgroups
[
4
]
, and to treat Sylow subgroups, one prime at a time. There is an extensive literature on this invariant, see, e.g.,
[
30
, Section 3]
.
In Example
2.3
, we have
\mathrm{B}_{0}(G)=0
. More generally, the Bogomolov multiplier vanishes when

•

G
is a
p
-group of order
|G|\leq p^{4}
,

•

G
is an extension of a cyclic group by an abelian group
[
4
, Lemma 4.9]
,
[
30
, Lemma 3.1]
,

•

G
is a split extension of a bicyclic group by an abelian group

[
30
, Lemma 3.2]
.

For a smooth projective
G
-variety
X
,
Condition
(A)
implies that

\mathrm{Am}(X,G)\subseteq\mathrm{B}_{0}(G).

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
G
be a finite group with
\mathrm{B}_{0}(G)=0
. Assume that
G
acts regularly on
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}
. This
G
-action is unirational if and only if it satisfies Condition
(A)
.

Proof.

Under our assumption, one has that

\mathrm{Am}({\mathbb{P}}^{n},G)\subseteq\mathrm{B}_{0}(G)=0.

This implies that
{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{n}}(1)
is
G
-linearized, and the corresponding homomorphism
G\to\mathsf{PGL}_{n}
lifts to
G\to\mathsf{GL}_{n}
, i.e., the
G
-action on
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}
arises from a linear representation. The action is thus
G
-unirational by definition.
∎

Here we do not assume that the
G
-action on
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}
is generically free. Note that for
G
with
\mathrm{B}_{0}(G)\neq 0
, there exist regular generically free actions on projective spaces, which satisfy Condition
(A)
but are
not
linear, and not
G
-unirational.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let

X:={\mathbb{P}}_{B}(\mathcal{E})

be
the projectivization of a vector bundle
\mathcal{E}\to B
, where
B
is a smooth projective
G
-variety.
Assume that
X
carries a generically free regular
G
-action such that the canonical projection

\pi:X\to B

is
G
-equivariant. Assume that

(1)

{\mathrm{H}}^{1}(G,\mathrm{Pic}(B))=0
,

(2)

X
satisfies Condition
(A)
, and

(3)

\mathrm{B}_{0}(G)=0
.

Then the
G
-action lifts to
\mathcal{E}
.

Proof.

Consider the sequence of
G
-modules

0\to\mathrm{Pic}(B)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi^{*}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathrm{Pic}(X)\to{\mathbb{Z}}\to 1.

Note that we do not assume that the
G
-action on
B
is generically free.

From the long exact sequence

0\to\mathrm{Pic}(B)^{G}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi^{*}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathrm{Pic}(X)^{G}\to{\mathbb{Z}}\to{\mathrm{H}}^{1}(G,\mathrm{Pic}(B))

and assumption (1), we obtain that there is a
G
-invariant class in
\mathrm{Pic}(X)
surjecting onto the class of the relative
\mathcal{O}(1)
. By assumption (2), every line bundle on
X
admits a linearization upon restriction to abelian subgroups, i.e.,
\mathrm{Am}(X,G)\subseteq\mathrm{B}_{0}(G)
. By assumption (3), that latter group is trivial, which implies that
every invariant line bundle on
X
is
G
-linearizable, in particular, the
G
-action lifts to
\mathcal{E}
.
∎

3.
Unirationality constructions

General results linking
G
-unirationality of actions on quadric and cubic hypersurfaces to geometry over nonclosed fields are in
[
18
, Section 10]
. We complement these considerations by providing explicit equivariant unirationality constructions in many cases; this allows us to essentially settle unirationality of
G
-actions on quadric and cubic threefolds, in subsequent sections.

We start with obvious observations:

•

G
-unirationality is compatible with taking products;

•

existence of a dominant
G
-equivariant rational map

X\dashrightarrow Y
from a

G
-unirational
X
implies
G
-unirationality of
Y
.

The following is an analog of a standard result in the theory of quadrics over nonclosed fields, see, e.g.,
[
19
, Chapter 4]
.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
G
be a finite group and
V
a representation of
G
giving rise to a generically free
G
-action on a smooth quadric hypersurface
X\subset{\mathbb{P}}(V)
, of dimension
\geq 2
.
Assume that there is a
G
-invariant irreducible subvariety
S\subset X
,
which is
G
-unirational. Then
X
is
G
-unirational.

Here, we allow
S
to have a nontrivial generic stabilizer, i.e., we require that there is a dominant rational map
{\mathbb{P}}(W)\dashrightarrow S
, for some
G
-representation
W
.

Proof.

By assumption, there is a dominant
G
-equivariant rational map
{\mathbb{P}}(W)\dashrightarrow S
.
Consider the rational map

S\times{\mathbb{P}}(V)\dashrightarrow X,

sending a pair of points
(s,p)\in S\times{\mathbb{P}}(V)
to the second
intersection point of
X
with the line

\mathfrak{l}(s,p)
through
s
and
p
. This is clearly dominant and
G
-equivariant, ensuring the
G
-unirationality of
X
.
∎

We continue with general
G
-unirationality constructions for cubic hypersurfaces of dimensions
\geq 2
. Over fields
K
of characteristic zero unirationality of smooth cubic hypersurfaces with
X(K)\neq\emptyset
is classical; generalizations to singular cubic hypersurfaces that are not cones can be found in
[
26
]
.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
V
be a representation of a finite group
G
.
Let
X\subset{\mathbb{P}}(V)
be a
G
-invariant irreducible cubic hypersurface of dimension
\geq 3
which is not a cone. Assume that
the
G
-action on
X
is generically free and that
X^{G}\neq\emptyset
. Then
X
is
G
-unirational.

Proof.

This is essentially
[
18
, Corollary 10.6]
, extended to singular cubic hypersurfaces. In particular, if
G
fixes a singular point, then the
G
-action is linearizable. If
G
fixes a smooth point, then the same argument in
[
18
, Corollary 10.6]
applies.
∎

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
V
be a representation of a finite group
G
.
Let
X\subset{\mathbb{P}}(V)
be a
G
-invariant cubic hypersurface which is not a cone. Assume that
the
G
-action on
X
is generically free
and that
G
has normal subgroup
H
such that
X
is
H
-unirational and
G/H
is a 2-group. Then
X
is
G
-unirational.

Proof.

Since
G/H
is a 2-group, we know there exists a tower

H\subset G_{1}\subset G_{2}\subset\cdots G

where each inclusion has index 2. By induction, it suffices to consider when
|G/H|=2
. In this case, the assertion essentially follows from
[
17
, Theorem 3.2]
, see also
[
17
, Remark 3.3]
:
G
-unirationality is equivalent to
K
-unirationality of all twists of
X
over
K
(via
G
-torsors), and for cubic hypersurfaces, unirationality over quadratic extensions of
K
is equivalent to unirationality over
K
.
∎

Corollary 3.4
.

Let
V
be a representation of a finite group
G
.
Let
X\subset{\mathbb{P}}(V)
be a
G
-invariant cubic hypersurface which is not a cone. Assume that
the
G
-action on
X
is generically free
and there exists a
G
-orbit of points on
X
of length one or two.
Then the
G
-action on
X
is
G
-unirational.

Proof.

First we consider when
X
has a
G
-fixed point
\mathfrak{p}
. It is
G
-unirational by Proposition
3.2
if
\mathfrak{p}
is smooth. If
\mathfrak{p}
is singular,
X
is
G
-linearizable, and thus
G
-unirational by projection from
\mathfrak{p}
. When
X
has a
G
-orbit
\{\mathfrak{p}_{1},\mathfrak{p}_{2}\}
of points of length 2, there exists an index-2 subgroup
H\subset G
fixing
\mathfrak{p}_{1}
and
\mathfrak{p}_{2}
. By the argument in the previous case, we know that
X
is
H
-unirational. The
G
-unirationality of
X
then follows from Proposition
3.3
.
∎

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
V
be a faithful
n
-dimensional representation of a finite group
G
with
n\geq 5
.
Let
X\subset{\mathbb{P}}(V)
be a
G
-invariant cubic hypersurface which is not a cone.
Assume that
the
G
-action on
X
is generically free and that there is a
G
-invariant subvariety
S\subset X
, which is
G
-unirational. Then
X
is
G
-unirational.

Here we do not require that the
G
-action on
S
is generically free.

Proof.

For any field extension
K/k
and every
G
-torsor
T
over
K
, the twist
{}^{T}S
is a
K
-unirational subvariety of
{}^{T}X
by Theorem
2.1
, which implies that
{}^{T}X
has a
K
-point. By assumptions,
{}^{T}X
is a cubic hypersurface in
{}^{T}{\mathbb{P}}(V)={\mathbb{P}}^{n}
. It follows from
[
26
]
that
{}^{T}X
is
K
-unirational, and thus
X
is
G
-unirational by Theorem
2.1
again.

∎

Remark 3.6
.

One can also find explicit
G
-unirational constructions in many cases under the assumptions of Proposition
3.5
. For example, assume that
S
is an irreducible hyperplane section, i.e., a cubic surface. Let
{\mathcal{T}}\to X^{\circ}
be the tangent bundle over the smooth locus
X^{\circ}
of
X,
and
{\mathcal{T}}|_{S^{\circ}}
the restriction of
{\mathcal{T}}
to the smooth locus of
S
. Then
{\mathcal{T}}|_{S^{\circ}}
is a rank-
(n-2)
vector bundle and the
G
-action lifts to
{\mathcal{T}}|_{S^{\circ}}
. By
[
14
, Theorem 4.2]
and
[
15
, Theorem 2.5]
, we know that the intersection of the tangent space at the generic point of
S^{\circ}
with
X
is not a cone. Thus, each point in the fiber above a general point
\mathfrak{p}\in S^{\circ}
canonically corresponds to a line in
{\mathbb{P}}(V)
that is tangent to
X
at
\mathfrak{p}
with multiplicity
2
, and thus intersects
X
at a unique point
\mathfrak{q}\neq\mathfrak{p}
. Sending each point in
{\mathcal{T}}|_{S^{\circ}}
to the corresponding point
\mathfrak{q}\in X
, we obtain a
G
-equivariant rational map

\displaystyle\varphi:{\mathcal{T}}|_{S^{\circ}}\dashrightarrow X.

(3.1)

We show that
\varphi
is dominant. Assume that

X=\{f_{3}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})=0\}\quad\text{ and }\quad S=X\cap\{x_{1}=0\}.

Given a general point
\mathfrak{q}=[q_{1}:\cdots:q_{n}]\in X
, we can find general
p_{1},\ldots,p_{n-1}\in k
such that

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\partial f_{3}}{\partial x_{i}}(0,p_{1},\ldots,p_{n-1})q_{i}=f_{3}(0,p_{1},\ldots,p_{n-1})=0.

Then the line passing through
\mathfrak{q}
and
\mathfrak{p}=[0:p_{1}:\ldots:p_{n-1}]
is tangent to
X
at
\mathfrak{p}
. It follows that (
3.1
) is dominant.

It remains to show that
{\mathcal{T}}|_{S^{\circ}}
is
G
-unirational. We apply the no-name lemma to the vector bundle

{\mathcal{T}}|_{S^{\circ}}\times V\to S^{\circ}\times V,

which yields a
G
-equivariant birationality

{\mathcal{T}}|_{S^{\circ}}\times V\sim_{G}S\times V\times{\mathbb{A}}^{n-2},

with trivial action on
{\mathbb{A}}^{n-2}
. By our assumptions,
{\mathcal{T}}|_{S^{\circ}}\times V
is
G
-unirational, implying the
G
-unirationality of
{\mathcal{T}}|_{S^{\circ}}
.

Remark 3.7
.

When
S
is a
G
-invariant linear subspace, e.g., a line, contained in
X
, the assumption that
S
is
G
-unirational follows from the assumption that
G
-acts on the ambient projective space via a linear representation
V
. Thus, any cubic hypersurface of dimension
\geq 3
with a linear
G
-action containing a
G
-invariant line or a
G
-invariant plane is
G
-unirational by Proposition
3.5
.

4.
Quadrics

Stable linearizability of certain
G
-quadric threefolds
X\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{4}
has been considered in
[
25
, Sections 5 and 6]
:
all but one subgroup
G
of the Weyl group
W(\mathsf{D}_{5})
, acting via signed permutations on the diagonal quadric

\sum_{i=1}^{5}x_{i}^{2}=0,

are stably linearizable, provided Condition
(A)
is satisfied. The only open case was
G=\mathfrak{D}_{4}
, acting on

{\mathbb{P}}(\chi_{1}\oplus\chi_{2}\oplus\chi_{3}\oplus V_{2}),

where
V_{2}
is the unique faithful 2-dimensional representation, and
\chi_{1},\chi_{2},\chi_{3}
are distinct characters of
G
. There are also many actions that do not arise from subgroups of

W(\mathsf{D}_{5})
, e.g., an action of
\mathfrak{D}_{8}
on the quadric

x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}x_{3}+x_{4}x_{5}=0,

arising from an action on

{\mathbb{P}}(\mathbf{1}\oplus V_{2}\oplus V_{2}^{\prime}),

where
V_{2}
is a faithful representation and
V_{2}^{\prime}
a nonfaithful 2-dimensional representation, see
[
25
, Remark 6.6]
.
The following theorem covers these and other actions, settling the stable linearizability problem for actions of finite groups on smooth quadric threefolds. The key observation is that, when Condition
(A)
is satisfied, there always exists a
G
-invariant unirational quadric surface or conic in
X
and Proposition
3.1
applies.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
X\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{4}
be a smooth quadric threefold, with a generically free action of a finite group
G
. Then the
G
-action is stably linearizable if and only if it satisfies Condition
(A)
.

The rest of this section is devoted to the proof of this theorem.

By Theorem
2.1
, stable linearizability of the
G
-action is equivalent to stable rationality of every twist
{}^{T}X
over every field extension
K/k
. For quadrics, (stable) rationality and unirationality are equivalent over any field. Again by Theorem
2.1
, this is equivalent to
G
-unirationality of the action.
By
[
18
, Theorem 10.2]
,
G
-unirationality is equivalent to
G_{2}
-unirationality, where
G_{2}
is the 2-Sylow subgroup of
G
. We may also assume that
G
is nonabelian: abelian group actions on
X
satisfying Condition
(A)
are linearizable via the projection from a fixed point.

Since
X\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{4}
is a
G
-invariant quadric, we know that
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(1)
is
G
-linearized. It follows that the
G
-action on the ambient
{\mathbb{P}}^{4}
arises from the projectivization of a linear representation of
G
. Thus, to show Theorem
4.1
, we now assume that
G
is a nonabelian 2-group and
X\subset{\mathbb{P}}(V)
, where
V
is a faithful representation of
G
. Since

G
is a 2-group, we have the following cases:

(1)

V=\chi_{1}\oplus\chi_{2}\oplus\chi_{3}\oplus V_{2}
, where
V_{2}
is an irreducible
G
-representation, and
\chi_{j}
are characters of
G
for
j=1,2,3
,

(2)

V=\chi_{1}\oplus V_{2}\oplus V_{2}^{\prime}
, where
V_{2}
and
V_{2}^{\prime}
are irreducible
G
-representations,

(3)

V=\chi_{1}\oplus V_{4}
, where
V_{4}
is an irreducible representation of
G
.

We proceed with a case-by-case analysis.

Case
(1): Up to isomorphism, we may assume that
X
is given by

X=\{x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}+x_{3}^{2}+x_{4}x_{5}=0\}\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{4},

with

G
acting via characters on
x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}
, and via the irreducible representation
V_{2}
on
x_{4},x_{5}
.
We claim that Condition
(A)
for
X
implies Condition
(A)
for the
G
-action on the conic
C\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{2}={\mathbb{P}}(\chi_{1}\oplus\chi_{2}\oplus\chi_{3})
, given by
x_{4}=x_{5}=0
.
Note that the
G
-action on this
{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
has a nontrivial generic stabilizer, since
G
is nonabelian, and the effective action on the conic
C
may not satisfy Condition
(A)
.

Since
V_{2}
is irreducible, there exist elements of the form

\mathrm{diag}(\pm 1,\pm 1,\pm 1,a,a^{-1})\in G,

where

is a 2-power root of unity of order
\mathrm{ord}(a)\geq 4
. Then

\varepsilon_{1}=\mathrm{diag}(1,1,1,-1,-1)\in G.

Note that

X^{\langle\varepsilon_{1}\rangle}=C\cup\mathfrak{p}_{1}\cup\mathfrak{p}_{2},

where

\mathfrak{p}_{4}=[0:0:0:1:0],\quad\mathfrak{p}_{5}=[0:0:0:0:1].

Let
A\subset G
be an abelian subgroup containing
\varepsilon_{1}
,
fixing points on
X
but not on

. Then
A
fixes
\mathfrak{p}_{4},\mathfrak{p}_{5}
, and acts via
C_{2}^{2}
on

. It follows that
A
contains

\varepsilon_{2}=\mathrm{diag}(-1,1,1,a_{1},a_{1}^{-1}),\quad\varepsilon_{3}=\mathrm{diag}(1,-1,1,b_{1},b_{1}^{-1}),

for some 2-power roots of unity
a_{1},b_{1}
. Depending on
a_{1}
and
b_{1}
, the elements
\varepsilon_{2}
and
\varepsilon_{3}
generate at least one of the following elements

\mathrm{diag}(-1,1,1,-1,-1),\quad\mathrm{diag}(1,-1,1,-1,-1),\quad\mathrm{diag}(1,1,-1,-1,-1).

Without loss of generality, assume that
A
contains

\varepsilon_{4}=\mathrm{diag}(-1,1,1,-1,-1).

By the irreducibility of
V_{2}
,
G
also contains an element switching
\mathfrak{p}_{4}
and
\mathfrak{p}_{5}
. Up to multiplying by
\varepsilon_{2}
and
\varepsilon_{3}
, we may assume that
G
contains

\sigma:(x_{1},\ldots,x_{5})\mapsto(x_{1},-x_{2},x_{3},a_{2}x_{5},a_{2}^{-1}x_{4}),

for some
a_{2}
. The subgroup

\langle\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{4},\sigma\rangle\simeq C_{2}^{3}

does not fix points on
X
.

Let
A\subset G
be an abelian subgroup not containing
\varepsilon_{1}
, fixing points on
X
but not on
C
. Then
\langle A,\varepsilon_{1}\rangle
is an abelian group whose fixed locus is
\{\mathfrak{p}_{4},\mathfrak{p}_{5}\}
or empty. In the first case, we repeat the argument above. We conclude that Condition
(A)
for the
G
-action on
X
implies
Condition
(A)
for the (not generically free)
G
-action on
C
.

Next,
we observe that

G
preserves the set
\{\mathfrak{p}_{4},\mathfrak{p}_{5}\}
, which yields an exact sequence

0\to N\to G\to C_{2}\to 0,

where
N
is the maximal subgroup fixing
\mathfrak{p}_{4}
and
\mathfrak{p}_{5}
, acting diagonally on
x_{1},\ldots,x_{5}
. Thus
N
is abelian. By
[
30
, Lemma 3.1]
,
\mathrm{B}_{0}(G)=0
, since

is an extension of a cyclic group by an abelian group. By Lemma
2.4
,

is
G
-unirational since Condition
(A)
is satisfied on

. By Proposition
3.1
,

is
G
-unirational.

Case
(2): We may assume that
G
acts on

X=\{x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}x_{3}+x_{4}x_{5}=0\},

via
\chi_{1}
on

, via

, and via

. By the irreducibility of

and

,

acts on
{\mathbb{P}}(V_{2})
and
{\mathbb{P}}(V_{2}^{\prime})

Let

\mathfrak{p}_{2}=[0:1:0:0:0],\quad\mathfrak{p}_{3}=[0:0:1:0:0],

\mathfrak{p}_{4}=[0:0:0:1:0],\quad\mathfrak{p}_{5}=[0:0:0:0:1].

Since

leaves invariant the sets
\{\mathfrak{p}_{2},\mathfrak{p}_{3}\},
and

\displaystyle 0\to N\to G\to Q\to 0,

(4.1)

or

, depending on the induced action on

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{1}=\mathrm{diag}(1,t_{1},t_{1}^{-1},t_{2},t_{2}^{-1}),\quad t_{1},t_{2}\text{ are $2$-power roots of unity.}

.

If

contains
\varepsilon_{2}
and
\varepsilon_{3}
. The abelian group

. If

and

and
\varepsilon_{1}
, and

\displaystyle m_{1}^{r_{1}}\varepsilon_{1}^{r_{2}}

\displaystyle:(x_{1},\ldots,x_{5})\mapsto(x_{1},c_{1}x_{3},c_{1}^{-1}x_{2},-x_{4},-x_{5}),

\displaystyle m_{2}\varepsilon_{1}^{r_{3}}

\displaystyle:(x_{1},\ldots,x_{5})\mapsto(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},c_{2}x_{5},c_{2}^{-1}x_{4})

for some

We are reduced to the case

,

(A)
, after multiplying

and
m_{2}
by
\varepsilon_{1}

Thus, if the

satisfies Condition
(A)
, we have

and

contains a lift of
(1,2)(3,4)
of the form

m_{3}:(x_{1},\ldots,x_{5})\mapsto(x_{1},d_{1}x_{3},d_{1}^{-1}x_{2},d_{2}x_{5},d_{2}^{-1}x_{4})

for some
d_{1},d_{2}
. We discuss cases based on the center of

•

If

, then

•

If

•

If
\varepsilon_{2}\in G

•

If
\varepsilon_{3}\in G
and

fixes a point on

Note that we have

is an extension of

by an abelian group. In the last two cases, the corresponding conic is

. In the second case, the

, we can lift the

, i.e., the

-action on

to

. It follows that

is
G
-unirational. By Proposition

,

is also
G
-unirational.

Case
(3): We show that Condition
(A)
is never satisfied. Since
V_{4}
is irreducible,
there are no fixed points in
{\mathbb{P}}(V_{4})
, and

We may assume that

with
G
acting trivially on

, and via

on
x_{2},x_{3},x_{4},x_{5}
. Since

is faithful and
G
preserves

, the center of

is

, generated by

\varepsilon_{1}=\mathrm{diag}(1,-1,-1,-1,-1).

Finite 2-subgroups of

are subgroups of

, for

. This yields 4 distinguished points

\mathfrak{p}_{2}=[0:1:0:0:0],\quad\mathfrak{p}_{3}=[0:0:1:0:0],

\mathfrak{p}_{4}=[0:0:0:1:0],\quad\mathfrak{p}_{5}=[0:0:0:0:1],

and an exact sequence

where

fixes each

. Since

is irreducible,
the image

, acting via permutations on

, or

The subgroup

\bullet

, with lifts of

and

:

\displaystyle m_{1}

\displaystyle:(x_{1},\ldots,x_{5})\mapsto(x_{1},a_{2}x_{3},a_{1}x_{2},-a_{1}^{-1}x_{5},-a_{2}^{-1}x_{4}),

\displaystyle m_{2}

\displaystyle:(x_{1},\ldots,x_{5})\mapsto(x_{1},b_{2}x_{4},b_{1}x_{5},-b_{1}^{-1}x_{2},-b_{2}^{-1}x_{3}),

In this section, we prove:

Theorem 5.1
.

be a smooth cubic threefold with a generically free regular action of a finite group

-unirational, with

•

, and

•

, or

For applications of results of Section

to cubic threefolds, we need to address

cubic surfaces, a case not considered in
[

. The proof of the following proposition is suggested by the referee.

Proposition 5.2

Let

be a normal rational cubic surface, with a generically free regular

. If the

-action satisfies condition

Proof.

Let

be a

be the output of

. Then

is a

-minimal surface with

is either a

-minimal del Pezzo surface or a

-minimal conic bundle. If

is a conic bundle which is not a del Pezzo surface, it is either the Hirzebruch surface

∎

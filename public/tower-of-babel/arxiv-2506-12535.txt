Anisotropic Calderón problem of a nearly Laplace-Beltrami operator of order 2 + 2+
Susovan Pramanik

Abstract
. This paper investigates the anisotropic Calderón problem for Logarithemic Laplacian, on closed Riemannian manifolds, which could be considered as near Laplace operator. We demonstrate that the Cauchy data set recovers the geometry of a closed Riemannian manifold up to standard gauge.



1.
Introduction

The anisotropic Calderón problem is geometric in nature (cf.
[
39
,
48
]
). Let us state the well-known open problem in the context of a closed (compact without boundary), connected Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
with
\dim(M)>2

\mathcal{O}\subset M
be a non-empty open set. The Calderón problem examines whether knowledge of the Cauchy data set

(1.1)

\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}^{\mathcal{O}}_{M,g}=\{(u|_{\mathcal{O}},(-\Delta_{g})u|_{\mathcal{O}})\mid u\in C^{\infty}(M),\,-\Delta_{g}u=0\,\text{in}\,\,M\setminus\overline{\mathcal{O}}\}

determines the isometry class of the manifold
(M,g)
.
(-\Delta_{g})
represents the positive Laplace-Beltrami operator on
(M,g)
.

When
\dim(M)=2
, the problem is solved using an additional gauge due to conformal invariance of the Laplace-Beltrami operator. See some of the references
[
41
,
2
,
5
,
29
]
. The problem for real-analytic manifolds in dimensions three and above has been addressed (see the references
[
39
,
37
,
36
]
), but it is still open for smooth manifolds. Positive results have been observed in transversally anisotropic geometries, see
[
14
,
15
]
. Starting from these celebrated works
[
49
,
33
]
, for current research on the Calderón problem, we refer to the survey article
[
51
]
.

Banking on the fact that, for all
s>0
,
\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}\frac{\ln\lambda}{\lambda^{s}}=0
, as an unbounded operator
-\Delta_{g}\circ\ln[(-\Delta_{g})]
, which we would call a logarithmic Laplacian operator, can be considered as a nearly Laplace-Beltrami operator
(-\Delta_{g})
of order
2+
. We are interested in addressing the corresponding anisotropic Calderón problem, which is related to the non-local nature of the logarithmic Laplacian operator.

To be more specific, let us set some
m>1
and consider the following operator on
(M,g)
as:

(1.2)

\mathcal{L}_{g}:=(-\Delta_{g}+m\mathbb{I})\circ\ln[(-\Delta_{g}+m\mathbb{I})]=\ln[(-\Delta_{g}+m\mathbb{I})]\circ(-\Delta_{g}+m\mathbb{I}).

The purpose of perturbing the Laplace operator by
m\mathbb{I}
where
m>1
to ensure the operator
\mathcal{L}_{g}
remains elliptic.

The goal of this work is to explore the anisotropic Calderón problem, whether the following Cauchy data set

\mathcal{C}^{\mathcal{O}}_{M,g}=\{(u|_{\mathcal{O}},\mathcal{L}_{g}u|_{\mathcal{O}})\mid u\in C^{\infty}(M),\,\,\,\mathcal{L}_{g}u=0\,\text{in}\,M\setminus\overline{\mathcal{O}}\}

determines the metric

In a different set up, recently the authors here
[
30
]
, looked into inverse problems of recovering the potential, associated to the near-zero logarithmic operator modelled into Schrödinger-type equations in flat spaces.

The motivation of our work is driven by the works see

Recently, we orchestrated such exercise in solving the inverse problem of recovering the geometry of closed manifold

Formal Definitions and Main Theorem

Let

The spectrum of

Define

\pi_{k}(f)=\sum_{j=1}^{d_{k}}\langle f,\phi_{k_{j}}\rangle_{L^{2}(M)}\phi_{k_{j}},

forms an orthonormal basis for

Now for

(cf. (

\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{L}_{g}):=\left\{u\in L^{2}(M)\,\middle|\,\,\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}|\lambda_{k}+m|^{2}|\ln(\lambda_{k}+m)|^{2}|\langle u,\phi_{k}\rangle|^{2}<\infty\right\}.

For any
s>0
, the inequality

(1.3)

|\ln(\lambda_{k}+m)|^{2}<(\lambda_{k}+m)^{2s},\quad k=\{0\}\cup\mathbb{N},\quad\mbox{and }m>1,

yields the Sobolev space

Direct Problem

Let
f\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathcal{O})
and consider the direct problem

(1.8)

\mathcal{L}_{g}u=f\quad\mbox{ in }M.

Let us first observe that
0
is not an eigenvalue of our operator
\mathcal{L}_{g}
defined in (
1.5
) for
m>1
.

If not, suppose
0
is an eigenvalue of the operator
\mathcal{L}_{g}
. Then there exists a nonzero function
u\in\mathbb{H}(M)
such that

\displaystyle\langle u,\,\mathcal{L}_{g}\,u\rangle=0.

Expanding
u
in terms of the orthogonal
L^{2}(M)
eigenbasis
\{\phi_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
, i.e.
u=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\langle\phi_{k},u\rangle\phi_{k}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\left\langle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\langle\phi_{k},u\rangle\phi_{k},\ \sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(\lambda_{k}+m)\ln(\lambda_{k}+m)\,\langle\phi_{k},u\rangle\phi_{k}\right\rangle=0,

which implies

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}|\langle\phi_{k},u\rangle|^{2}\,(\lambda_{k}+m)\ln(\lambda_{k}+m)=0.

Since each term in the sum is non-negative, thanks to the fact
\lambda_{k}\geqslant 0
for all
k\geqslant 0
, and
m>1
, it follows that

\displaystyle\langle\phi_{k},u\rangle=0\quad\text{for all }k\geqslant 0,

which implies
u=0
. This contradicts our assumption that
u
is nonzero.

Therefore, the (linear) operator
\mathcal{L}_{g}
is injective on its domain of definition. Further, let us define its inverse acting on
L^{2}(M)
spectrally as:

\left(\mathcal{L}_{g}\right)^{-1}f=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(\lambda_{k}+m)\ln(\lambda_{k}+m)}\langle\phi_{k},f\rangle\phi_{k},\quad f\in L^{2}(M),

and one notes that,

\mathcal{L}_{g}\circ\left(\mathcal{L}_{g}\right)^{-1}f=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\langle\phi_{k},f\rangle\phi_{k}=f,\quad f\in L^{2}(M),

hence by considering

u=\left(\mathcal{L}_{g}\right)^{-1}f

one finds the unique solution of (
1.8
) in
\mathbb{H}(M)\subset L^{2}(M)
, for
f\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathcal{O})
there.

Cauchy Data

Let
u\in\mathbb{H}(M)
solves (
1.8
) for some
f\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathcal{O})
, and we define the corresponding Cauchy data set as

\mathcal{C}^{\mathcal{O}}_{M,g}=\{(u|_{\mathcal{O}},\mathcal{L}_{g}u|_{\mathcal{O}}),\,\,\,\mathcal{L}_{g}u=0\,\mbox{ in }\,M\setminus\overline{\mathcal{O}}\}.

This dataset contains solutions to the equation
\mathcal{L}_{g}u=0
and serves as the basis on the anisotropic Calderón problem for the operator
\mathcal{L}_{g}

Inverse Problem

We want to study the inverse problem whether the Cauchy data
\mathcal{C}^{\mathcal{O}}_{M,g}
determines
(M,g)
or not, and we have the following to claim as our main result of this paper.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(M_{i},g_{i}),\,i=1,2
be two closed, connected Riemannian manifolds with
\dim(M_{i})>2
. Assume that
\mathcal{O}\subset M_{1}\cap M_{2}
is a non-empty open subset where the metrics agree, i.e.,
(\mathcal{O},g_{1})=(\mathcal{O},g_{2}):=(\mathcal{O},g)
. The equality of the Cauchy data set over
(\mathcal{O},g)
, i.e.
\mathcal{C}^{\mathcal{O}}_{M_{1},g_{1}}=\mathcal{C}^{\mathcal{O}}_{M_{2},g_{2}}
, implies the existence of a diffeomorphism
\Phi:M_{1}\to M_{2}
such that
\Phi^{\ast}g_{2}=g_{1}
.

Au(x)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{i(x-y)\cdot\theta}\,a(x,y,\theta)\,u(y)\,dy\,d\theta,\quad u\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),

where the amplitude
a(x,y,\theta)\in S^{0}_{1,0}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})
. For more details, we refer to
[
27
]
.

Symbol class of the logarithmic Laplacian:

The asymptotic expansion of
\lambda^{s}
(cf. (
2.16
)) for
s\to 0+
stands as

\lambda^{s}=1+s\,\ln\lambda+\mathcal{O}(s^{2}),

or,

\ln\lambda=\left.\frac{d}{ds}\right|_{s=0}\lambda^{s},\quad\lambda>0.

This infers that, thanks to functional calculus, the logarithmic Laplacian
\ln\mathcal{A}_{g}
which has been defined in (
1.6
) or (
2.8
), enjoy the following representation

\ln\mathcal{A}_{g}=\left.\frac{d}{ds}\right|_{s=0}(-\Delta_{g}+m\mathbb{I})^{s}.

This approach has been discussed in
[
35
,
7
]
to define the near-zero logarithmic Laplacian operator.

Since the principal symbol of
(-\Delta_{g}+m\mathbb{I})^{s}
is
\left(\sum_{j,k=1}^{n}g^{jk}(x)\xi_{j}\xi_{k}+m\right)^{s}
,
s\in(0,1)
. Therefore, the principal symbol of
\ln\mathcal{A}_{g}
would be

\sigma(\ln\mathcal{A}_{g})(x,\xi)=\ln\left(\sum_{j,k=1}^{n}g^{jk}(x)\xi_{j}\xi_{k}+m\right).

Since for any multi-index
\beta\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
,

|\partial_{\xi}^{\beta}\ln(|\xi|_{g}^{2}+m)|\leqslant C_{\beta}(1+|\xi|)^{-|\beta|},

where
|\xi|_{g}^{2}=\left(\sum_{j,k=1}^{n}g^{jk}(x)\xi_{j}\xi_{k}+m\right)
.

Thus, the logarithmic Laplacian is a classical pseudodifferential operator of order zero:

\ln\mathcal{A}_{g}\in\Psi^{0}_{1,0}

with logarithmic principal symbol.

Similarly, the principal symbol of the operator
\mathcal{L}_{g}=(-\Delta_{g}+m\mathbb{I})\circ\ln\,(-\Delta_{g}+m\mathbb{I})
would be

\sigma(\mathcal{L}_{g})(x,\xi)=(|\xi|_{g}^{2}+m)\,\ln\,(|\xi|_{g}^{2}+m).

Since for any
\varepsilon>0
, we have the estimate:

|\xi|^{2}\ln|\xi|\leqslant C_{\varepsilon}(1+|\xi|)^{2+\varepsilon}.

Furthermore, the derivatives of the symbol satisfy:

\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\partial_{\xi}^{\beta}\sigma(\mathcal{L}_{g})(x,\xi)=O\left((1+|\xi|)^{2+\varepsilon-|\beta|}\right).

Thus
\sigma(\mathcal{L}_{g})(x,\xi)\in S^{2+\varepsilon}_{1,0}
, i.e,

\mathcal{L}_{g}=(-\Delta_{g}+m\mathbb{I})\circ\ln[(-\Delta_{g}+m\mathbb{I})]\in\Psi^{2+\varepsilon}_{1,0},\quad\text{for any }\varepsilon>0,

establishing it is an operator of order
2+
.

Proof of Main Result

We recall,
\mathcal{O}\subset M_{i}
for
i=1,2
. Let

, and we uniquely solve the equation

\mathcal{L}_{g_{i}}u^{f}_{i}=f\quad\mbox{in }(M_{i},g_{i}),

By our hypothesis, we have

(3.2)

(g_{1}|_{\mathcal{O}},\,u^{f}_{1}|_{\mathcal{O}},\mathcal{L}_{g_{1}}u^{f}_{1}|_{\mathcal{O}})=(g_{2}|_{\mathcal{O}},\,u^{f}_{2}|_{\mathcal{O}},\mathcal{L}_{g_{2}}u^{f}_{2}|_{\mathcal{O}}).

Regularity of

This can be observed that
u^{f}_{i}\in C^{\infty}(M_{i})
for

. As it follows

\mathcal{L}_{g_{i}}\left(\mathcal{A}_{g_{i}}u^{f}_{i}\right)=\mathcal{L}_{g_{i}}\circ\mathcal{A}_{g_{i}}u^{f}_{i}=\mathcal{A}_{g_{i}}\circ\mathcal{L}_{g_{i}}u^{f}_{i}=\mathcal{A}_{g_{i}}f\quad\mbox{in }(M_{i},g_{i})

where,
\mathcal{A}_{g_{i}}=(-\Delta_{g_{i}}+m\mathbb{I})
in
(M_{i},g_{i})
for
i=1,2
.

By solving (
3.3
), with the source term
\mathcal{A}_{g_{i}}f\in L^{2}(M_{i})
, we find
\mathcal{A}_{g_{i}}u^{f}_{i}\in\mathbb{H}(M_{i})
. Continuing the process, one shows
\mathcal{A}^{k}_{g_{i}}u^{f}_{i}\in\mathbb{H}(M_{i})\subset L^{2}(M_{i})
for every

, hence establishing the claim that
u^{f}_{i}\in\underset{l\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{1,2\}}{\cap}\,H^{l}(M_{i})=\underset{l\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}}{\cap}\,H^{l}(M_{i})
; or in
C^{\infty}(M_{i})
as the closed infinite intersections of the Sobolev spaces
\{H^{l}(M_{i})\}_{l\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}}
.

Integral identities

Further, by realizing

\mathcal{L}_{g_{i}}\left(\mathcal{A}^{k-1}_{g_{i}}u^{f}_{i}\right)=\mathcal{L}_{g_{i}}\circ\mathcal{A}^{k-1}_{g_{i}}u^{f}_{i}=\mathcal{A}^{k-1}_{g_{i}}\circ\mathcal{L}_{g_{i}}u^{f}_{i}=\mathcal{A}^{k-1}_{g_{i}}f\quad\mbox{in }(M_{i},g_{i}),\quad k\in\mathbb{N};

and

\mathcal{A}_{g_{1}}^{k-1}f=\mathcal{A}_{g_{2}}^{k-1}f\quad\mbox{in }(M_{i},g_{i}),\quad k\in\mathbb{N};

thanks to our choice
f\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathcal{O})
, (as
\mathcal{A}_{g_{i}}
are the local operators) and there
g_{1}|_{\mathcal{O}}=g_{2}|_{\mathcal{O}}
.

Therefore, the equality of the Cauchy data set (cf. (
3.2
)) allow us to write:

(3.4)

\left(\left(\mathcal{A}_{g_{1}}^{k-1}u_{1}\right)|_{\mathcal{O}},\,\,\left(\mathcal{L}_{g_{1}}\mathcal{A}_{g_{1}}^{k-1}u_{1}\right)\big|_{\mathcal{O}}\right)=\left(\left(\mathcal{A}_{g_{2}}^{k-1}u_{2}\right)|_{\mathcal{O}},\,\,\left(\mathcal{L}_{g_{2}}\mathcal{A}_{g_{2}}^{k-1}u_{2}\right)\big|_{\mathcal{O}}\right),\qquad k\in\mathbb{N}.

Indeed, the regularity of
u_{i}
ensures that
u_{i}\in\mathcal{H}(M_{i})
. Consequently, employing the semigroup definition of
\mathcal{L}_{g_{i}}
in (
2.15
) together with (
3.4
), we deduce the following integral identities:

(3.5)

\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(e^{-t\mathcal{A}_{g_{1}}}\mathcal{A}_{g_{1}}^{k}u_{1}-e^{-t\mathcal{A}_{g_{2}}}\mathcal{A}_{g_{2}}^{k}u_{2}\right)(t,x)\,\frac{dt}{t}=0,\quad

for
x\in\mathcal{O}
, and
k\in\mathbb{N}
.

On the domain

\mathcal{D}((-\Delta_{g_{i}}+m\mathbb{I})^{j})
, the operators commute: i.e,
e^{-t\mathcal{A}_{g_{i}}}\mathcal{A}_{g_{i}}^{j}=\mathcal{A}_{g_{i}}^{j}e^{-t\mathcal{A}_{g_{i}}}
for all

. In particular we have

(3.6)

e^{-t\mathcal{A}_{g_{i}}}(\mathcal{A}_{g_{i}}^{j}u_{i})=(-1)^{j}\,\partial_{t}^{j}(e^{-t\mathcal{A}_{g_{i}}}u_{i}),\qquad j\in\mathbb{N}\,\,\text{and}\,\,(t,x)\in[0,\infty)\times(M_{i},g_{i}).

Substituting (
3.6
) into (
3.5
) gives

(3.7)

\int_{0}^{\infty}\partial_{t}^{k}\left(e^{-t\mathcal{A}_{g_{1}}}u_{1}-e^{-t\mathcal{A}_{g_{2}}}u_{2}\right)(t,x)\,\frac{dt}{t}=0,

for
x\in\mathcal{O}
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
.

We wish to perform the integration by-parts (consecutively
k

(3.8)

\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(e^{-t\mathcal{A}_{g_{1}}}u_{1}-e^{-t\mathcal{A}_{g_{2}}}u_{2}\right)(t,x)\,\frac{dt}{t^{1+k}}=0,

for
x\in\mathcal{O}
, and

.

, and for all

Well-definedness of (

Let

. The restriction of the function

\displaystyle(\partial_{t}-\mathcal{A}_{g_{1}})\left(e^{-t\mathcal{A}_{g_{1}}}u_{1}-e^{-t\mathcal{A}_{g_{2}}}u_{2}\right)=0\quad\text{in }(0,\infty)\times\omega.

with the initial data over

Moreover, as follows from (

So,
\left(e^{-t\mathcal{A}_{g_{1}}}u_{1}-e^{-t\mathcal{A}_{g_{2}}}u_{2}\right)(t,x)

Let us justify below that the first integral. We will be using the Taylor series expansion for

If

For

Hardy’s inequality and application

Let us apply the classical Hardy’s inequality on

The celebrated Hardy inequality states that, if

\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{|u(r)|^{p}}{r^{p}}\,dr\leqslant(\frac{p}{p-1})^{p}\,\int_{0}^{\infty}|u^{\prime}(r)|^{p}\,dr.

The constant on the right side is the best possible.

As an application of this, we now claim

Paley-Wiener theorem and application

Hence, by considering the Fourier transform of

By the classical Paley–Wiener theorem

\displaystyle\qquad-\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\left(e^{-(t+t_{0})\mathcal{A}_{g_{1}}}u_{1}-e^{-(t+t_{0})\mathcal{A}_{g_{2}}}u_{2}\right)}{t}\,dt

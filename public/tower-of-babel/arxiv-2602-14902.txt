Abstract
. In this paper we prove that a recent condition of Lyons–Martínez–Navarro–Tiep, regarding the field of values of extensions of characters in principal blocks, is satisfied for all finite simple groups, which when combined with their results gives a new characterization of finite groups with a normal ℓ \ell -complement for a prime ℓ \ell . This leads us to study the distribution of characters in unipotent blocks of disconnected reductive groups and show that this is well-behaved under a generalization of d d -Harish-Chandra theory. We go on to study the blockwise Galois–McKay (also known as the Alperin–McKay–Navarro) conjecture for the blocks of almost (quasi-)simple groups above unipotent blocks. Key words and phrases: Galois action, character extensions, principal block, groups of lie type, unipotent blocks, disconnected groups



1.
Introduction

Many interesting problems in the representation theory of finite groups aim to relate information about a group’s structure to information that can be obtained from the character table. In particular, several recent problems in this realm require a deep understanding of the fields of values of characters of almost simple groups.

In
[
NT10
]
, Navarro and Tiep give a necessary and sufficient condition for a group to have a normal
2
-complement, in terms of the rational-valued irreducible characters of
G
. They also give a corresponding sufficient condition for odd primes. In
[
LMNT
]
, Lyons–Martínez–Navarro–Tiep recently considered principal block analogues of those results. In fact, they see that by restricting to the principal block, the version for odd primes should be able to be improved to an if and only if statement. The first main goal of this paper is to complete the proof of that statement.

We write
{\mathbb{Q}}_{n}
for the cyclotomic field
{\mathbb{Q}}(e^{2\pi i/n})
and, given a prime
\ell
, we write
B_{0}(G)
for the principal
\ell
-block of
G
. We complete the proof of the following question considered in
[
LMNT
]
:

Theorem A
.

Let
\ell
be any odd prime number and let
G
be a finite group with order divisible by
\ell
. Then
\ell
divides
\chi(1)
for all nonlinear
{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}
-valued
\chi\in\operatorname{Irr}(B_{0}(G))
if and only if
G
has a normal
\ell
-complement.

As mentioned in the last paragraph of the introduction of
[
LMNT
]
, this also yields

[
LMNT
, Conj. F]
for principal blocks. Recall that a block
B
is called nilpotent if all of its height-zero characters have the same degree, and that for the principal block, this is equivalent to
G
having a normal
\ell
-complement. We also recall that an almost
\ell
-rational character is one whose values lie in
{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell m}
for some
m
relatively prime to
\ell
. Then Theorem
A
gives:

Corollary B
.

The principal
\ell
-block
B_{0}(G)
is nilpotent if and only if all its height-zero almost
\ell
-rational characters are linear. (That is,
[
LMNT
, Conj. F]
holds for principal blocks.)

We remark that the statement of Theorem

also holds for
\ell=2
, and is
[
LMNT
, Thm. D]
. Further, Theorem

is reduced to a problem on simple groups in
[
LMNT
, Thm. E]
. Therefore,
our proof of Theorem

requires a study of an extendibility condition on simple groups from
[
LMNT
]
(see Definition
2.1

[

]
. That is, we consider the “going up” problem mentioned in the introduction of

. In

, this condition is proved for nonabelian simple groups

A key requirement in working with Definition

While the rationality of characters in the principal block of a group of Lie type

We remark that although (as of the writing of this paper) the Alperin–McKay–Navarro conjecture

has not yet been reduced to conditions on simple groups, this question will also be crucial for working with any future such conditions.

We first study the semisimple characters lying in the principal

-block of

Let

. Then the complex

of

However, in a small number of cases it can happen that the trivial character is the unique semisimple character satisfying the conditions of Definition

In these remaining cases, we consider instead unipotent characters in the block.
The fields of values of characters of

(1)

We use the notation of

3.
The Alvis–Curtis duality and automorphisms

3.1.
Lifting the Cabanes–Rickard equivalence

Let
p
be a prime and let
\Lambda
be any integral domain in which
p
is invertible.
Suppose that
G
is a group with a strongly split
BN
-pair of characteristic
p
. Then there is a notion of Alvis-Curtis duality, denoted
D_{(G)}
, inducing a splendid derived equivalence between
\Lambda G
and itself. (See e.g.
[
CE04
, Sec. 4]
for the construction of
D_{(G)}
and
[
CE04
, Cor. 4.19]
for the derived equivalence.)

Here we study a generalized version of
D_{(G)}
and prove Theorem
C
(restated here), which extends the above-mentioned result. Below we denote by
\Delta E
the diagonal group in
E\times E^{\mathrm{opp}}
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
E
be a group of automorphism of the BN-pair
(G,B,N,S)
. Then the complex
D_{(G)}
of
G
-
G
-bimodules extends to a complex of
(G\times G^{\mathrm{opp}})\Delta E
-modules. In particular, if
\ell\nmid|E|
, then the induced complex
\operatorname{Ind}_{(G\times G^{\mathrm{opp}})\Delta E}^{GE\times(GE)^{\mathrm{opp}}}(D_{(G)})
induces a splendid derived equivalence between
\Lambda GE
and itself.

Proof.

Let
\sigma\in E
.
We recall the construction of the complex
D_{(G)}
from
[
CE04
, Sec. 4]
. The complex
D_{(G)}
has only non-zero terms in degrees
[0,n]
, where
n=|S|
. It’s
i
th term is given by

D_{(G)}^{(i)}:=\displaystyle\bigoplus_{I\subset S:|I|=i}\Lambda G\otimes_{\Lambda P_{I}}e(U_{I})\Lambda G,

where
e(U_{I})=\frac{1}{|U_{I}|}\sum_{u\in U_{I}}u
.
Under our assumptions,
\sigma(I)
is a again a subset of
S
of the same cardinality. Hence,
\sigma
has a natural action on

\displaystyle\bigoplus_{J\in\langle\sigma\rangle I}\Lambda G\otimes_{\Lambda P_{J}}e(U_{J})\Lambda G

where
\langle\sigma\rangle I
denotes the
\sigma
-orbit of
I
. Hence, we can consider each of the terms of
D_{(G)}
as a
(G\times G^{\mathrm{opp}})\langle\sigma,\sigma^{-1}\rangle
-module. We must check that the transition maps
\delta^{i}:D_{(G)}^{(i)}\to D_{(G)}^{(i+1)}
are also equivariant with respect to this action. For this recall that if
I^{\prime}\subset I
then we have a morphism

f^{I}_{I^{\prime}}:\Lambda G\otimes_{\Lambda P_{I}}e(U_{I})\Lambda G\to\Lambda G\otimes_{\Lambda P_{I^{\prime}}}e(U_{I^{\prime}})\Lambda G

given by

x\otimes_{\Lambda P_{I}}y\mapsto x\otimes_{\Lambda P_{I^{\prime}}}y,

of
G
-
G
-bimodules.
To define the transition maps we fix a total ordering
``\leq"
on
S
. In particular, any
I\subset S
can be written as
I=\{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\dots,\alpha_{i}\}
such that
\alpha_{1}\leq\alpha_{2}\leq\dots\leq\alpha_{i}
. For
j\leq i
we can therefore define
I_{j}:=I\setminus\{\alpha_{j}\}
. With these data the transition map is then defined as

\delta^{i}(m):=\sum_{j=0}^{i}(-1)^{j}f_{I_{j}}^{I}(m)

for
m\in\Lambda G\otimes_{\Lambda P_{I}}e(U_{I})\Lambda G
.

In general, the maps
\delta^{i}
are not equivariant with respect to the action of
\sigma
defined above. For this purpose, we define for a given function
\varepsilon:\mathcal{P}(S)\to\{\pm 1\}
a new action
``\ast_{\varepsilon}"
of
\sigma
on
D_{(G)}^{i}
by setting

\sigma\ast_{\varepsilon}m:=\varepsilon(I)\sigma(m)

for
m\in\Lambda G\otimes_{\Lambda P_{I}}e(U_{I})\Lambda G
.
In order to make the maps
\delta^{i}
equivariant with respect to the
``\ast_{\varepsilon}"
action of
\sigma
, we must find the signs
\varepsilon(I)\in\{\pm 1\}
such that

f_{I_{j}}^{I}(\varepsilon(I)\sigma(m))=\varepsilon(I\setminus\{{\alpha}_{j}\})\sigma(f_{I_{j}}^{I}(m))

for all
0\leq j\leq i
. For this purpose, we set
\delta_{I}({\alpha}_{j}):=(-1)^{k-j}
where
k
is such that
\sigma({\alpha}_{j})=\beta_{k}
and
\sigma(I)=\{\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{i}\}
. In other words,
\delta_{I}({\alpha}_{j})=1
if and only if the parity between the position of
{\alpha}_{j}
in
I
and the position of
\sigma({\alpha}_{j})
in
\sigma(I)
is even.

With this definition, our claim amounts to showing that

\varepsilon(I)=\delta_{I}({\alpha}_{j})\varepsilon(I\setminus\{{\alpha}_{j}\}).

We set
\varepsilon(\emptyset)=1
and
\varepsilon(\{a\})=1
for all
a\in S
. This uniquely determines the function
\varepsilon
(provided that it exists). In order to show that it exists, we must show that the definition
\varepsilon(I)=\delta_{I}({\alpha}_{j})\varepsilon(I\setminus\{{\alpha}_{j}\})
is independent of
j
. This amounts to showing that for
k\neq j
we have

\delta_{I}({\alpha}_{k})\delta_{I\setminus\{{\alpha}_{k}\}}({\alpha}_{j})=\delta_{I}({\alpha}_{j})\delta_{I\setminus\{{\alpha}_{j}\}}({\alpha}_{k}).

However, removing
{\alpha}_{j}
resp.
\sigma({\alpha}_{j})
from
I
resp.
\sigma(I)
changes the position mod
2
of
{\alpha}_{k}
precisely by one. This implies that
\delta_{I}({\alpha}_{k})=\delta_{I\setminus\{{\alpha}_{k}\}}({\alpha}_{k})
(and by symmetry of course
\delta_{I}({\alpha}_{j})=\delta_{I\setminus\{{\alpha}_{j}\}}({\alpha}_{j})
). Hence, the equality of signs above is always satisfied.

Note that as the function
\varepsilon
is uniquely determined from
\sigma
, it follows that we can extend the complex
D_{(G)}
to a complex of
(G\times G^{\mathrm{opp}})\Delta E
-modules.
(We note that the above action extends to one for
E
. For this, we need to check that
\varepsilon_{\tau}(\sigma(I))\varepsilon_{\sigma}(I)=\varepsilon_{\tau\sigma}(I)
for each
\tau,\sigma\in E
for all
I
. By induction on
I
, it suffices to check that the corresponding
\delta
functions coincide. Write
\delta_{\sigma,I}
, respectively
\delta_{\tau,I}
for the functions corresponding to
\sigma
, resp.
\tau
. With the notation above, we have

\delta_{\sigma,I}({\alpha}_{j})=(-1)^{k-j}
and
\delta_{\tau,\sigma(I)}(\sigma({\alpha}_{j}))=(-1)^{k-l}
if
\tau\sigma({\alpha}_{j})=\gamma_{l}
where
\tau(\sigma(I))=\{\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{i}\}
. However,
\delta_{\tau\sigma,I}({\alpha}_{j})=(-1)^{j-k}
, showing the desired equality of the corresponding
\varepsilon
-functions.)
By a result of Marcus
[
Mar96
]
, the induced complex
\operatorname{Ind}_{(G\times G^{\mathrm{opp}})\Delta E}^{GE\times(GE)^{\mathrm{opp}}}(D_{G})
induces a derived auto-equivalence as desired.
∎

Remark 3.2
.

Note that if
``\leq_{1}"
and
``\leq_{2}"
are two total orderings on

on the set

if and only if

(as the value of

on

In the situation of Theorem

, we write

3.2.
Comparison to Digne–Michel’s lift

By a group of Lie type or finite reductive group, we will mean the group
G:=\mathbf{G}^{F}
of fixed points of a connected reductive algebraic group
\mathbf{G}
under a Steinberg endomorphism
F\colon\mathbf{G}\rightarrow\mathbf{G}
. In this situation, we compare the lift we obtain from Theorem
3.1
to the lift of the Alvis–Curtis duality constructed by Digne–Michel
[
DM94
]
in the context of finite (disconnected) reductive groups.

Corollary 3.5
.

If
G=\mathbf{G}^{F}
is a group of Lie type and
\sigma
is a quasi-central automorphism of
\mathbf{G}
commuting with the action of
F
, then the bijection
D_{G\langle\sigma\rangle}:\mathbb{Z}\operatorname{Irr}(G\langle\sigma\rangle)\to\mathbb{Z}\operatorname{Irr}(G\langle\sigma\rangle)
defined above coincides with the generalized Alvis–Curtis duality introduced by Digne–Michel in
[
DM94
, Prop. 3.13]
.

Proof.

By the observation following
[
DM94
, Cor. 3.12]
, the Alvis–Curtis duality on the coset
G\sigma
is given by

D_{G\sigma}:=\sum_{I}(-1)^{|I/\sigma|}{}^{\ast}R_{L_{I}\sigma}^{G\sigma}R_{L_{I}\sigma}^{G\sigma}

where
I
runs over all
\sigma
-stable subset of
S
. Note that
R_{L_{I}\sigma}^{G\sigma}:CF(L_{I}\sigma,K)\to CF(G\sigma,K)
is by definition the restriction of the functor
R_{L_{I}\langle\sigma\rangle}^{G\langle\sigma\rangle}
which is induced by the
G
-
L_{I}
-bimodule

\mathbb{C}[G\langle\sigma\rangle/U_{I}]\cong\operatorname{Ind}_{(G\times(L_{I})^{\mathrm{opp}})\langle\sigma,\sigma^{-1}\rangle}^{G\langle\sigma\rangle\times L_{I}\langle\sigma\rangle}(\mathbb{C}[G/U_{I}]).

Now if the subset
I
is not
\sigma
-stable, we denote by
\langle\sigma\rangle_{I}
its stabilizer in
\langle\sigma\rangle
. Then we have

\operatorname{Ind}^{G\langle\sigma\rangle\times(L_{I}\langle\sigma\rangle)^{\operatorname{opp}}}_{(G\times(L_{I})^{\operatorname{opp}})\Delta(\langle\sigma\rangle_{I}\rangle)}(\mathbb{C}[G/U_{I}])=\bigoplus_{J\in\langle\sigma\rangle I}(\mathbb{C}[G\langle\sigma\rangle_{I}/U_{J}])

as
G\langle\sigma\rangle\times(L_{I}\langle\sigma\rangle)^{\operatorname{opp}}
-modules.
Hence, this module induces the functor
R_{L_{I}\langle\sigma\rangle_{I}}^{G\langle\sigma\rangle_{I}}\circ\operatorname{Res}_{L_{I}\langle\sigma\rangle_{I}}^{L_{I}\langle\sigma\rangle}
on characters. Then for any class function
f
of
L_{I}\langle\sigma\rangle
its image in
G\sigma
vanishes. This implies that only the
\sigma
-stable orbits contribute to
\operatorname{Res}_{G\sigma}^{G\langle\sigma\rangle}\circ D_{(G)}
.

It remains to check that the signs in
[
DM94
]
and in our construction match. For this, we need to show that
\varepsilon(I)(-1)^{|I|}=(-1)^{|I/\sigma|}
for all
\sigma
-stable set
I\subset S
. Let us first assume that
I
is a
\sigma
-orbit. In this case we need to show that
\varepsilon(I)=(-1)^{|I|+1}
. Now by construction if
|I|=r
, then we have for any
x\in I

\varepsilon(I)=\prod_{i=0}^{r-1}\delta_{I\setminus\{x,\sigma(x),\dots,\sigma^{i-1}(x)\}}(\sigma^{i}(x)).

By Remark
3.2
we can assume that
x\leq\sigma(x)\leq\sigma^{2}(x)\leq\dots...\leq\sigma^{r-1}(x)
which implies that
\delta_{I\setminus\{x,\sigma(x),\dots,\sigma^{i-1}(x)\}}(\sigma^{i}(x))=-1
for all
i
so that
\varepsilon(I)=(-1)^{r+1}
as claimed.

Let now
I_{1},\dots,I_{k}
be the orbits of
\sigma
on
I
. Again by Remark
3.2
we can choose an ordering such that
x\leq y
whenever
x\in I_{i}
and
y\in I_{j}
with
i<j
. From this we deduce that

\varepsilon(I)=\prod_{i=1}^{k}\varepsilon(I_{i})=\prod_{i=1}^{k}(-1)^{|I_{i}|+1}

so that
\varepsilon(I)(-1)^{|I|}=(-1)^{k}=(-1)^{|I/\sigma|}
.
∎

Example 3.6
.

Suppose that
G
is of type
\operatorname{A}
and consider the canonical ordering of the simple roots of type
\operatorname{A}
. Here, the graph automorphism
\sigma
reverses the ordering of these simple roots. In particular,
\varepsilon(I)=\varepsilon(I\setminus\{{\alpha}_{j}\})(-1)^{|I|}
for all
j
. This implies the formula
\varepsilon(I)=1
if and only if
|I|\equiv 0,1\mod 4
. Now
|I/\sigma|=1
if and only if
|I|\equiv 1,2\mod 4
. This proves the claimed formula
\varepsilon(I)(-1)^{|I|}=(-1)^{|I/\sigma|}
in this example.

Remark 3.7
.

Let

be the decomposition map.
It follows from
[
DM94
, Prop. 2.11]
that
d^{1}\circ D_{G\langle\sigma\rangle}=D_{G\langle\sigma\rangle}\circ d^{1}
. In particular, from this we see that the Alvis–Curtis duality defined by Digne–Michel is compatible with
\ell
-blocks.

, and this satisfies
(\tilde{G}E)_{\tilde{\chi}}=(\tilde{G}E)_{\chi}
.

Proof.

This follows from
[
CE04
, Thm. 17.7]
. To see the final part of the statement, we remark that if
\sigma\in E_{\chi}\setminus{E_{\widetilde{\chi}}}
, then
\widetilde{\chi}^{\sigma}=\widetilde{\chi}\beta
for some linear character
\beta
, whose order is prime to
\ell
by our assumption
\ell\nmid|\operatorname{Z}(G)|
, and hence
\ell\nmid|\widetilde{G}/G\operatorname{Z}(\widetilde{G})|
. But this would contradict that
\widetilde{\chi}
, and hence
\widetilde{\chi}^{\sigma}
, must lie in a series indexed by a semisimple
\ell
-element by
[
CE04
, Thm. 9.12]
.
∎

Recall that if
\mathcal{E}(\tilde{G},\tilde{s})
is a Lusztig series, then all its characters restrict to a multiple of the same character of
\operatorname{Z}(\tilde{G})
. (See
[
Ma07
, Lem. 2.2]
.) In particular, these characters restrict trivially to
\operatorname{Z}(\tilde{G})
if and only if
\tilde{s}\in[\tilde{G}^{\ast},\tilde{G}^{\ast}]
. (See
[
NT13
, Lem. 4.4, Rem. 4.6]
.) Now, by the degree properties of Jordan decomposition, the semisimple character in the Lusztig series
\mathcal{E}(\tilde{G},\tilde{s})
is of
\ell^{\prime}
-degree if and only if
\operatorname{C}_{\tilde{G}^{\ast}}(\tilde{s})
contains a Sylow
\ell
-subgroup of
\tilde{G}^{\ast}
.

In particular, if
\widetilde{s}
has
\ell
-power order, this means the semisimple character is of
\ell^{\prime}
-degree if and only if
\tilde{s}\in\operatorname{Z}(P^{\ast})
for some Sylow
\ell
-subgroup
P^{\ast}
of
\tilde{G}^{\ast}
. Note that
P_{0}^{\ast}:=P^{\ast}\cap[\tilde{G}^{\ast},\tilde{G}^{\ast}]
is a Sylow
\ell
-subgroup of
[\tilde{G}^{\ast},\tilde{G}^{\ast}]
.

Lemma 4.6
.

With the notation above, suppose that
\tilde{s}\in P_{0}^{\ast}\cap\operatorname{Z}(P^{\ast})
. Then the semisimple character
\tilde{\chi}\in\mathcal{E}(\tilde{G},\tilde{s})
lies in
\operatorname{Irr}_{0}(B_{0}(\tilde{G}/\operatorname{Z}(\tilde{G})))
. Moreover, if
\tilde{s}
has order
\ell
, then
\mathbb{Q}(\tilde{\chi})\subseteq\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}
and if
\ell
does not divide the order of any graph automorphisms in
E
, then
[E:E_{\tilde{\chi}}]
is not divisible by
\ell
.

Proof.

That
\widetilde{\chi}
lies in
\operatorname{Irr}_{\ell^{\prime}}(\widetilde{G}/\operatorname{Z}(\widetilde{G}))
follows from the discussion above. Further, it lies in the principal block by
[
Hi90
, Cor. 3.4]
.

It remains to prove the moreover statement. Now since
\tilde{s}
has order
\ell
we deduce that
\mathbb{Q}_{\tilde{s}}\subseteq\mathbb{Q}_{o(\tilde{s})}=\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}
. On the other hand,
\sigma\in E
stabilizes
\widetilde{\chi}
if and only if
\sigma^{\ast}
stabilizes the geometric conjugacy class of
\tilde{s}
, where
\sigma^{\ast}\in E((\mathbf{G}^{\ast})^{F})
is dual to
\sigma
(see
[
NTT08
, Cor. 2.5]
).
Now,
\tilde{s}
is
\widetilde{\mathbf{G}}^{\ast}
-conjugate to an element
t
in the maximally split torus
\mathbf{T}^{\ast}
. Hence, we have
F_{p}^{\ast}(t)=t^{p}
. In particular,
F_{p}
acts as an automorphism on the group
\langle t\rangle\cong C_{\ell}
. Since
\mathrm{Aut}(C_{\ell})\cong C_{\ell-1}
it follows that any automorphism of
\ell
-power order acts trivially on
\langle t\rangle
. Hence,
[\langle F_{p}\rangle:\langle F_{p}\rangle_{\widetilde{\chi}}]
is not divisible by
\ell
. Finally, notice that if
\ell
does not divide the order of any graph automorphisms, then
|E:\langle F_{p}\rangle|
is also prime to
\ell
.
∎

Under our assumptions, note that
|[\tilde{G}^{\ast},\tilde{G}^{\ast}]|_{\ell}=|G|_{\ell}
and
|[\operatorname{Z}(\tilde{G}^{\ast},\tilde{G}^{\ast}])|_{\ell}=|\operatorname{Z}(G)|_{\ell}
. In particular, if
\ell\mid|S|
it follows that the Sylow
\ell
-subgroup
P_{0}^{\ast}
is non-central, so in this case there always exists a nontrivial element in
\operatorname{Z}(P_{0}^{\ast})
of order
\ell
. Given Lemma
4.6
, we are interested in determining convenient elements of
\operatorname{Z}(P^{\ast})\cap P_{0}^{\ast}
. Note that if
\ell\nmid|\operatorname{Z}(G)|=|\operatorname{Z}([\tilde{G}^{\ast},\tilde{G}^{\ast}])|
, we have
P^{\ast}=P_{0}^{\ast}\operatorname{Z}(\tilde{G}^{\ast})_{\ell}
which gives
\operatorname{Z}(P^{\ast})\cap P_{0}^{\ast}=\operatorname{Z}(P_{0}^{\ast})
in this situation.

4.4.
Some exceptional cases

Throughout, we will use the common notation
\operatorname{SL}_{n}(\epsilon q)
, with
\epsilon\in\{\pm 1\}
, to denote
\operatorname{SL}_{n}(q)
if
\epsilon=1
and
\operatorname{SU}_{n}(q)
if
\epsilon=-1
, and similar for related groups
\operatorname{GL}_{n}(\epsilon q)
, etc. Similarly,
\operatorname{E}_{6}(\epsilon q)
will denote
\operatorname{E}_{6}(q)
if
\epsilon=1
and the twisted version
{}^{2}\!\operatorname{E}_{6}(q)
if
\epsilon=-1
.

We keep the notation from above. The following is also shown in
[
GSV18
, Thm. 3.5]
.

Lemma 4.7
.

Suppose that
G=\operatorname{SL}_{n}(\epsilon q)
and
2\neq\ell\mid|\operatorname{Z}(G)|
. Then
\operatorname{Z}(P^{\ast})\cap P_{0}^{\ast}=\operatorname{Z}(P_{0}^{\ast})
.

Proof.

Note here that
\ell\mid(q-\epsilon)
. As long as
P_{0}^{\ast}
is Cabanes (that is, that
P_{0}^{\ast}
contains a unique maximal abelian normal subgroup
D_{0}^{\ast}
) we know that
\operatorname{Z}(P_{0}^{\ast})
is contained in the Cabanes subgroup
D_{0}^{\ast}
of
P_{0}^{\ast}
, which in this case is the Sylow
\ell
-subgroup
T^{\ast}_{\ell}
of the diagonal torus
T^{\ast}
of
[\widetilde{G}^{\ast},\widetilde{G}^{\ast}]\cong G
. Now
P^{\ast}/D^{\ast}\cong P_{0}^{\ast}/D_{0}^{\ast}
, where
D^{\ast}
is the Cabanes group of
P^{\ast}
,
so an element in the Cabanes subgroup
D_{0}^{\ast}
is central in
P_{0}^{\ast}
if and only if it is central in
P^{\ast}
. We know that
P_{0}^{\ast}
is Cabanes except when
n=3=\ell\mid\mid(q-\epsilon)
(see
[
KM15
, Lem. 3.12]
and its proof). The exceptional case is treated immediately.
∎

Unfortunately, as the following example shows,
\operatorname{Z}(P_{0}^{\ast})
can sometimes be too small.

Example 4.8
.

Suppose that
\ell=3
and
G=\operatorname{SL}_{3}(q)
with
\ell=3\mid(q-1)
. Then
\operatorname{Z}(P_{0}^{\ast})=\operatorname{Z}([\widetilde{G}^{\ast},\widetilde{G}^{\ast}])
. In particular, any semisimple character in a Lusztig series associated to a semsimple element in
\operatorname{Z}(P_{0}^{\ast})
will cover the trivial character of
G
.

However, we see that we can avoid this as long as
n
is not a power of
\ell
:

Lemma 4.9
.

Suppose that
G=\operatorname{SL}_{n}(\epsilon q)
. Assume that
\ell
is an odd prime dividing
|\operatorname{Z}(G)|
but
that
n
is not a power of
\ell
. Then
\operatorname{Z}(P_{0}^{\ast})\neq\operatorname{Z}([\tilde{G}^{\ast},\tilde{G}^{\ast}])_{\ell}
. Further, there exists
\tilde{s}\in\operatorname{Z}(P_{0}^{\ast})
of order
\ell
such that the semisimple character
\tilde{\chi}\in\mathcal{E}(\tilde{G},\tilde{s})
restricts irreducibly to a nontrivial character
\chi:=\operatorname{Res}_{G}^{\tilde{G}}(\tilde{\chi})
with
(\tilde{G}E)_{\widetilde{\chi}}=(\tilde{G}E)_{\chi}
.

Proof.

We have here
\widetilde{G}=\operatorname{GL}_{n}(\epsilon q)=\widetilde{G}^{\ast}
.
Let
n=\sum_{k}\ell^{k}a_{k}
be the
\ell
-adic representation, and suppose
i
is minimal such that
a_{i}\neq 0
. Note that
a_{i}>0
since
\ell\mid n
. Let
{\lambda}\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}^{\times}
have order
\ell
. If
n=\ell^{i}a_{i}
(and hence
a_{i}>1
by our assumption that
n
is not a power of
\ell
) then we consider the matrix
\tilde{s}_{0}:=\mathrm{diag}(1,\dots,1,{\lambda},\dots,{\lambda})\in\widetilde{G}^{\ast}
, where the first
n-\ell^{i}=\ell^{i}(a_{i}-1)
entries are one and the last
\ell^{i}
entries are
{\lambda}
. If instead the
\ell
-adic expansion of
n
has at least two terms, we consider the matrix
\tilde{s}_{0}:=\mathrm{diag}(1,\dots,1,{\lambda},\dots,{\lambda})
where now the first
n-\ell^{i}a_{i}
entries are one and the last
\ell^{i}a_{i}
entries are
{\lambda}
. Note that in either case,
\widetilde{s}_{0}
is in
\operatorname{Z}(P^{\ast}_{0})
but not central in
[\tilde{G}^{\ast},\tilde{G}^{\ast}]
.

Now observe that the image of
s_{0}:=\iota^{\ast}(\tilde{s}_{0})
is again an
\ell
-element. Let us first suppose that
n\neq 2\ell^{i}
. In this case the Levi subgroup
\operatorname{C}_{\mathbf{G}^{\ast}}(s_{0})
is self-normalizing in
\mathbf{G}^{\ast}
. Note that the centralizer of
s_{0}
is connected and therefore the semisimple character
\tilde{\chi}\in\mathcal{E}(\tilde{G},\tilde{s}_{0})
restricts to an irreducible character of
G
. Moreover,
\tilde{\chi}
is the unique character in its Lusztig series covering
\chi
. Hence, for the last claim is suffices to show that if
\sigma\in E
such that
s_{0}
and
\sigma^{\ast}(s_{0})
are
G^{\ast}
-conjugate, then also
\tilde{s}_{0}
and
\sigma^{\ast}(\tilde{s_{0}})
are
\tilde{G}^{\ast}
-conjugate. For this, we can assume that
\sigma^{\ast}
stabilizes the diagonal torus
\tilde{T}^{\ast}
containing
\tilde{s}_{0}
and acts as
\sigma^{\ast}(t)=t^{-1}
if
\sigma
is a graph automorphism and
\sigma^{\ast}(t)=t^{p_{0}}
, for some power
p_{0}
of
p
, if
\sigma^{\ast}
is a field automorphism. It follows that
\sigma^{\ast}(s_{0})={}^{n}s_{0}z
for some
z\in\operatorname{Z}(\tilde{\mathbf{G}}^{\ast})
and
n\in\mathbf{\tilde{G}}^{\ast}
. Hence,
n\in\operatorname{N}_{\mathbf{\tilde{G}}^{\ast}}(\operatorname{C}_{\mathbf{\tilde{G}}^{\ast}}(\tilde{s}_{0}))=\operatorname{C}_{\mathbf{\tilde{G}}^{\ast}}(\tilde{s}_{0})
which implies
\sigma^{\ast}(\tilde{s}_{0})=\tilde{s}_{0}
by considering the structure of
\tilde{s}_{0}
.

Therefore, it only remains to consider the case where
n=2\ell^{i}
. In this case we consider the element
\tilde{s}:=\mathrm{diag}({\lambda},\dots,{\lambda},{\lambda}^{-1},\dots,{\lambda}^{-1})
with
\ell^{i}
entries equal to each of
{\lambda}
and
{\lambda}^{-1}
. Note that the
\tilde{\mathbf{G}}^{\ast}
-conjugacy class of
\tilde{s}
is stable under the non-trivial graph automorphism
\gamma^{\ast}
so that
\tilde{\chi}
is
\gamma
-stable. Notice that if
\sigma\in E
stabilizes
\chi
, then
\widetilde{\chi}^{\sigma}
is stabilized by
\gamma
. However, as
\gamma
acts by inversion on
\tilde{G}/G
, it follows that
\tilde{\chi}
is the unique
\gamma
-stable character covering
\chi
lying in a Lusztig series corresponding to a semisimple element of order
\ell
. It follows that
\chi
and
\tilde{\chi}
again have the same stabilizer in
E
.
∎

We next treat the case
G=\operatorname{D}_{4}(q)
.

Lemma 4.10
.

Suppose that
G=\operatorname{D}_{4}(q)
and assume
\ell
is odd. Then there is some
\tilde{s}\in P_{0}^{\ast}\cap\operatorname{Z}(P^{\ast})
that is noncentral in
\widetilde{G}^{\ast}
, such that the semisimple character
\tilde{\chi}\in\mathcal{E}(\tilde{G},\tilde{s})
lies in
\operatorname{Irr}_{0}(B_{0}(\tilde{G}/\operatorname{Z}(\tilde{G})))
,
\mathbb{Q}(\tilde{\chi})\subseteq\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}
, and
\ell\nmid[E:E_{\tilde{\chi}}]
. Further, we have
1_{G}\neq\operatorname{Res}_{G}^{\widetilde{G}}\widetilde{\chi}\in\operatorname{Irr}(B_{0}(G/\operatorname{Z}(G)))
.

Proof.

Since
\ell\neq 2
, we have
\ell\nmid|\operatorname{Z}(G)|
and the final statement follows from the first, together with Lemma
4.5
and that
\widetilde{s}
is not central in
\widetilde{G}^{\ast}
. Further, by Lemma
4.6
, we may assume that
\ell
divides the order of some graph automorphism,
so that
\ell=3
. In particular,
\ell\nmid|\operatorname{Z}([\tilde{G}^{\ast},\tilde{G}^{\ast}])|
.

In this case, let
\sigma
be a graph automorphism of order
3
with dual
\sigma^{\ast}
. By the arguments in Lemma
4.6
, it suffices to find some
\tilde{s}\in\operatorname{Z}(P_{0}^{\ast})
such that
\tilde{s}
is
\sigma^{\ast}
-stable.
For this, let
\hat{P}
be a Sylow
3
-subgroup of
[\tilde{G}^{\ast},\tilde{G}^{\ast}]\langle\sigma^{\ast}\rangle
. If there is
1\neq\tilde{s}\in\operatorname{Z}(\hat{P})\cap[\widetilde{G}^{\ast},\widetilde{G}^{\ast}]
then the corresponding Lusztig series is
\sigma^{\ast}
-stable. Otherwise,
\operatorname{Z}(\hat{P})
has nontrivial intersection with the coset
[\tilde{G}^{\ast},\tilde{G}^{\ast}]\sigma^{\ast}
, which implies that
\operatorname{Z}(P_{0}^{\ast})\leq\operatorname{Z}(\hat{P})
. In that case, any
1\neq\tilde{s}\in\operatorname{Z}(P_{0}^{\ast})
is
\sigma^{\ast}
-stable.
∎

5.
The generalized Steinberg character and the principal block

5.1.
Observations on the Dade group for twisted groups

Notice that if
G=\mathbf{G}^{F}
is quasisimple and
\ell\mid|Z(G)|
,
then either
\ell=2
or
G
is of type
\operatorname{A}_{n-1}(\varepsilon q)
resp.
\operatorname{E}_{6}(\varepsilon q)

with
\ell\mid(n,q-\varepsilon)
resp.
\ell\mid(3,q-\varepsilon)
. With the previous section and our emphasis on
\ell
odd, we are interested in considering the latter cases.

Here we consider the Dade ramification group in
GE
(resp.
\widetilde{G}E
) for
B_{0}(G)
(resp.
B_{0}(\widetilde{G})
) when
G
is one of these groups. Note that if
q
is not square, then
|G\langle F_{p}\rangle/G|
is odd. In this case, the Steinberg character
\mathrm{St}_{G}
satisfies Lemma
2.5
. Then
when combined with the following, we will be particularly interested in the case that
q
is a square and
\epsilon=1
. We momentarily relax our assumption that
\mathbf{G}
is simple, simply connected in the following, in order to work with
\operatorname{SL}_{n}(\epsilon q)
and
\operatorname{GL}_{n}(\epsilon q)
simultaneously.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
G=\mathbf{G}^{F}\in\{\operatorname{SL}_{n}(\varepsilon q),\operatorname{GL}_{n}(\varepsilon q)\}
and assume that
2\neq\ell\mid(q-\varepsilon)
with
n=\ell^{k}
. If
\sigma\in E_{\ell^{\prime}}
is in the Dade group of
B_{0}(G)
in
GE
(see Lemma
2.4
), then
\sigma
is a field automorphism satisfying
\operatorname{C}_{G\langle\sigma\rangle}(T_{\ell})=\operatorname{C}_{G}(T_{\ell})\langle\sigma\rangle
, where
T_{\ell}
is a Sylow
\ell
-subgroup of
\mathbf{T}^{F}
and
\mathbf{T}\leq\mathbf{G}
is the diagonal torus. In particular, if
\sigma
has even order, then
\varepsilon=1
.

Proof.

We let
\mathbf{T}
be the diagonal torus of
\mathbf{G}
on which
F
acts as
x\mapsto x^{q}
(resp.
x\mapsto x^{-q}
if
F
is twisted). Let
T:=\mathbf{T}^{F}
. Here a field automorphism can be chosen such that it acts as
x\mapsto x^{p_{0}}
on
T
and the graph automorphism acts as
x\mapsto x^{-1}
on
T
. In particular,
T
is normalized by
\sigma
.

Recall from the definition of the Dade group that there is some
n\in G
such that
\sigma n
centralizes a Sylow
\ell
-subgroup of
G
containing
T_{\ell}
, where
T_{\ell}\in\mathrm{Syl}_{\ell}(T)
. However,
\operatorname{C}_{G}(T_{\ell})=T
by
[
CE04
, Prop. 22.6]
. Hence,
n
normalizes
T
. By looking closely at elements of
T_{\ell}
(e.g. the elements
h_{e_{i}-e_{j}}(\zeta)
for all
i,j
and
\zeta
of order
(q-\varepsilon)_{\ell}
) and considering the action of
\operatorname{N}_{\mathbf{G}^{F}}(\mathbf{T})/\mathbf{T}^{F}\cong S_{n}
on
T_{\ell}
, we see that necessarily
n\in T
and
\sigma
must be a field automorphism centralizing
T_{\ell}
.

The last statement now also follows from our assumption that
\ell\mid(q-\epsilon)
, since if
\sigma
has even order, then
p_{0}^{f}=q
for some even
f
. But then since
\sigma
centralizes
T_{\ell}
, we have
\ell
divides
p_{0}-1
, and hence also
q-1
.
∎

Lemma 5.2
.

If
G=\operatorname{E}_{6}(-q)
with
\ell=3\mid(q+1)
and
\sigma\in E
is in the Dade group of
B_{0}(G)
in
GE
, or in the Dade group of
B_{0}(\widetilde{G})
in
\widetilde{G}E
, then
\sigma
has odd order.

Proof.

Assume the contrary, that there is such a
\sigma
with even order in the Dade group for
B_{0}(G)
, and recall that
E
is cyclic, so this means that in particular the Dade group contains the unique
\tau\in E
of order two.

Consider the
3
-central element
s
with centralizer of type
\operatorname{A}_{2}(-q)^{3}.3
in
G^{\ast}
. Note that the three semisimple characters in the associated Lusztig series cannot all be
\sigma
-stable as any
\sigma
of even order acts non-trivially on
\widetilde{G}/G\operatorname{Z}(\widetilde{G})
.
However, note that these characters have degree prime to
3
and that
B_{0}(G)
is the unique unipotent block of maximal defect, so these characters all lie in
B_{0}(G)
. Hence,
\sigma
cannot be in the Dade group of
B_{0}(G)
, as it must stabilize the characters of
B_{0}(G)
.

Now suppose that the Dade group for
B_{0}(\widetilde{G})
contains some
\sigma\in E
of even order, so that it contains
\tau
. Let
\widetilde{P}\in\mathrm{Syl}_{\ell}(\widetilde{G})
be
E
-invariant. Since
\tau
is in the Dade group, we have that it acts on
\widetilde{P}
, and hence on
P:=\widetilde{P}\cap G\in\mathrm{Syl}_{\ell}(G)
, the same as some
x\in\widetilde{G}
. But notice that since
\tau
has order two, then
x^{3}
also acts on
P
as
\tau
. Since
x^{3}\in G\operatorname{Z}(\widetilde{G})
, this means that
\tau
is in the Dade group for
B_{0}(G)
as well, a contradiction to the result for
G
above.
∎

By the previous two lemmas and the discussion above, we can focus on the case where
q
is square and
G=\operatorname{SL}_{n}(q)
or
G=\operatorname{E}_{6}(q)
with
\ell\mid(q-1)
.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
G\in\{\operatorname{SL}_{n}(q),\operatorname{GL}_{n}(q)\}
with
n=\ell^{k}
for some integer
k
and
2\neq\ell\mid(q-1)
.
Let
\sigma\in E
be such that
G\langle\sigma\rangle
is the Dade ramification group for
B_{0}(G)
in
GE
.
Then there exists a regular
\ell
-element
x\in G
that is invariant under
\sigma
.

Proof.

Let
\zeta
be an
n=\ell^{k}
th root of unity and let
x
be the regular element
\mathrm{diag}(1,\zeta,\dots,\zeta^{n-1})
. By the proof of Lemma
5.1
, the element
x
is
\sigma
-stable.
∎

5.2.
The principal block and generalized Harish-Chandra induction

Here we relax our assumptions on
\mathbf{G}
, so that
\mathbf{G}
can be any connected reductive algebraic group (not necessarily simple, simply connected).

For a finite group
H
, it will be useful to denote by
H_{\ell}
the set of elements of
\ell
-power order and by
H_{\ell^{\prime}}
the set of elements of order prime to
\ell
(which we will also call
\ell^{\prime}
-order). If
K
is a splitting field for
H
of characteristic
0
, we write
CF(H,{K})
for the set of class functions from
H
to
{K}
.
For
x\in H_{\ell}
, we recall from
[
CE04
, Def. 5.7]
the map

d^{x}:CF(H,K)\to CF(\operatorname{C}_{H}(x),K),

defined such that for
\chi\in CF(H,K)
and
y\in\operatorname{C}_{H}(x)
we have
d^{x}(\chi)(y)=\chi(xy)
if
y
has
\ell^{\prime}
-order and
d^{x}(\chi)(y)=0
otherwise. (Note that we use the same notation
d^{x}
for any group
H
, and the latter should be clear from context.)

We need the following generalization of
[
CE04
, Thm. 21.4]
.

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
\hat{\mathbf{G}}
be any reductive group with
\hat{\mathbf{G}}=\mathbf{G}\langle\sigma\rangle
where
\mathbf{G}:=\hat{\mathbf{G}}^{\circ}
.
Let
\mathbf{P}
be a parabolic subgroup of
\mathbf{G}
containing an
F
-stable Levi subgroup
\mathbf{L}
, and suppose that
(\mathbf{L},\mathbf{P})
is
\sigma
-stable. Then for any
\sigma
-stable
x\in\mathbf{L}^{F}
of
\ell
-power order
we have

d^{x}\circ{}^{\ast}R_{\mathbf{L}\langle\sigma\rangle\subset\mathbf{P}\langle\sigma\rangle}^{\mathbf{G}\langle\sigma\rangle}={}^{\ast}R_{\operatorname{C}^{\circ}_{\mathbf{L}}(x)\langle\sigma\rangle\subset\operatorname{C}^{\circ}_{\mathbf{P}}(x)\langle\sigma\rangle}^{\operatorname{C}^{\circ}_{\mathbf{G}}(x)\langle\sigma\rangle}\circ d^{x}.

Proof.

Write
G:=\mathbf{G}^{F}
. Our first observation is that for
\chi
a class function of
G
, we have
d^{1}(\chi)=\delta\cdot\chi
where
\delta
is the characteristic function of
(G\langle\sigma\rangle)_{\ell^{\prime}}
. From this, it follows from
[
DM94
, Prop. 2.11]
applied to the function
\delta
that
d^{1}\circ{}^{\ast}R^{\mathbf{G}\langle\sigma\rangle}_{\mathbf{L}\langle\sigma\rangle\subset\mathbf{P}\langle\sigma\rangle}={}^{\ast}R^{\mathbf{G}\langle\sigma\rangle}_{\mathbf{L}\langle\sigma\rangle\subset\mathbf{P}\langle\sigma\rangle}\circ d^{1}
. Using this observation, together with the fact that
d^{x}=d^{1}\circ d^{x}
, it follows that both sides of the equation vanish on elements of order divisible by
\ell
for all class functions. Then it suffices to consider elements of
\ell^{\prime}
-order.

We also note that since
(\mathbf{L},\mathbf{P})
and
x
are
\sigma
-stable, it follows that
\operatorname{C}^{\circ}_{\mathbf{G}}(x)\langle\sigma\rangle
is a reductive group with parabolic subgroup
\operatorname{C}^{\circ}_{\mathbf{P}}(x)\langle\sigma\rangle
and Levi complement
\operatorname{C}^{\circ}_{\mathbf{L}}(x)\langle\sigma\rangle
in the sense of
[
DM94
]
.

Now let
\psi\in\operatorname{Irr}(\mathbf{G}^{F})
and
l\in\operatorname{C}_{\mathbf{L}^{F}\langle\sigma\rangle}(x)_{\ell^{\prime}}
. Note that
\operatorname{C}_{\mathbf{L}^{F}\langle\sigma\rangle}(x)_{\ell^{\prime}}\subset\operatorname{C}^{\circ}_{\mathbf{L}}(x)^{F}\langle\sigma\rangle
.
Let
l=tv
be the Jordan decomposition of
l
. Since
x
commutes with
l
, we have
(xl)_{p^{\prime}}=xt
and
(xl)_{p}=v
. Hence, by
[
DM94
, Cor. 2.9]

d^{x}({}^{\ast}R_{\mathbf{L}\langle\sigma\rangle\subset\mathbf{P}\langle\sigma\rangle}^{\mathbf{G}\langle\sigma\rangle}(\psi))(l)={}^{\ast}R_{\mathbf{L}\langle\sigma\rangle\subset\mathbf{P}\langle\sigma\rangle}^{\mathbf{G}\langle\sigma\rangle}(\psi)(xl)={}^{\ast}R_{\operatorname{C}^{\circ}_{\mathbf{L}}(xt)\langle xl\rangle}^{\operatorname{C}^{\circ}_{\mathbf{G}}(xt)\langle xl\rangle}(\operatorname{Res}_{\operatorname{C}_{G}^{\circ}(xt)\langle xl\rangle}^{G\langle\sigma\rangle}(\psi))(xl)

while
[
DM94
, Cor. 2.9]
also gives

{}^{\ast}R_{\operatorname{C}^{\circ}_{\mathbf{L}}(x)\langle\sigma\rangle}^{\operatorname{C}^{\circ}_{\mathbf{G}}(x)\langle\sigma\rangle}(d^{x}(\psi))(l)={}^{\ast}R_{\operatorname{C}^{\circ}_{\mathbf{L}}(xt)\langle l\rangle}^{\operatorname{C}^{\circ}_{\mathbf{G}}(xt)\langle l\rangle}(\operatorname{Res}^{\operatorname{C}^{\circ}_{G}(x)\langle\sigma\rangle}_{\operatorname{C}^{\circ}_{G}(xt)\langle l\rangle}(d^{x}(\psi)))(l)

noting that
\operatorname{C}_{\mathbf{G}}(xt)\subset\operatorname{C}_{\mathbf{G}}(x)
as
l
is of
\ell^{\prime}
-order and so
(xt)_{\ell}=x
. Now

\operatorname{Res}^{\operatorname{C}_{G}(x)\langle\sigma\rangle}_{\operatorname{C}^{\circ}_{G}(xt)\langle l\rangle}(d^{x}(\psi))=d^{1}(t_{x}(\operatorname{Res}^{\operatorname{C}_{G}(x)\langle\sigma\rangle}_{\operatorname{C}^{\circ}_{G}(xt)\langle l\rangle}(\psi))),

where
t_{x}(f)(y):=f(xy)
for the central element
x\in\operatorname{Z}(\operatorname{C}_{\mathbf{G}}^{\circ}(xt)\langle l\rangle)
. Now
t_{x}
and
d^{1}
commute with
{}^{\ast}R_{\operatorname{C}^{\circ}_{\mathbf{L}}(xt)\langle l\rangle}^{\operatorname{C}^{\circ}_{\mathbf{G}}(xt)\langle l\rangle}
by
[
DM94
, Prop. 2.6]
resp. the first observation at the beginning of the proof which implies that

{}^{\ast}R_{\operatorname{C}^{\circ}_{\mathbf{L}}(xt)\langle l\rangle}^{\operatorname{C}^{\circ}_{\mathbf{G}}(xt)\langle l\rangle}(\operatorname{Res}^{\operatorname{C}^{\circ}_{G}(x)}_{\operatorname{C}^{\circ}_{G}(xt)}(d^{x}(\psi)))(l)={}^{\ast}R_{\operatorname{C}^{\circ}_{\mathbf{L}}(xt)\langle l\rangle}^{\operatorname{C}^{\circ}_{\mathbf{G}}(xt)\langle l\rangle}(\operatorname{Res}^{\operatorname{C}^{\circ}_{G}(x)}_{\operatorname{C}^{\circ}_{G}(xt)}(\psi))(xl).

Hence the two sides coincide as stated.
∎

The next two lemmas will establish a situation in which the generalized Steinberg character is guaranteed to lie in the principal block.

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
\hat{\mathbf{G}}
be a reductive group with
\hat{\mathbf{G}}=\mathbf{G}\langle\sigma\rangle
where
\mathbf{G}:=\hat{\mathbf{G}}^{\circ}
.
Let
\mathbf{T}
be an
F
-stable maximal torus contained in an
F
-stable Borel subgroup
\mathbf{B}
of
\mathbf{G}
such that
(\mathbf{T},\mathbf{B})
is
\sigma
-stable. Let
\hat{\chi}:=\mathrm{St}_{G\langle\sigma\rangle}
be the generalized Steinberg character (see Definition
3.3
). Then we have

{}^{\ast}R_{\mathbf{T}\langle\sigma\rangle}^{\mathbf{G}\langle\sigma\rangle}(\hat{\chi})=1_{{\mathbf{T}}^{F}\langle\sigma\rangle}
.

Proof.

Write
B:=\mathbf{B}^{F}
and
T:=\mathbf{T}^{F}
. Recall from the proof of Lemma
4.3
that
\hat{\chi}
satisfies
\operatorname{Res}_{B\langle\sigma\rangle}^{G\langle\sigma\rangle}(\hat{\chi})=\operatorname{Res}_{B\langle\sigma\rangle}^{G\langle\sigma\rangle}(\mathrm{St}_{G\langle\sigma\rangle})=\operatorname{Ind}_{T\langle\sigma\rangle}^{B\langle\sigma\rangle}(1_{T\langle\sigma\rangle})
.
Hence,

\langle R_{\mathbf{T}\langle\sigma\rangle}^{\mathbf{G}\langle\sigma\rangle}(1_{T\langle\sigma\rangle}),\mathrm{St}_{G\langle\sigma\rangle}\rangle=\langle\operatorname{Ind}^{G\langle\sigma\rangle}_{B\langle\sigma\rangle}(1_{B\langle\sigma\rangle}),\mathrm{St}_{G\langle\sigma\rangle}\rangle=\langle 1_{B\langle\sigma\rangle},\operatorname{Ind}_{T\langle\sigma\rangle}^{B\langle\sigma\rangle}(1_{T\langle\sigma\rangle})=\langle 1_{T\langle\sigma\rangle},1_{T\langle\sigma\rangle}\rangle=1

by appyling Frobenius reciprocity twice.

Now let
\rho
be the regular character of
\mathbf{T}^{F}
, so that
\hat{\rho}:=\operatorname{Ind}_{T}^{T\langle\sigma\rangle}(\rho)
is the regular character of
T\langle\sigma\rangle
. By classical Harish-Chandra theory (e.g. applying
[
CE04
, Prop. 19.6]
), we have
\langle R_{\mathbf{T}}^{\mathbf{G}}(\rho),\mathrm{St}_{G}\rangle=\langle R_{\mathbf{T}}^{\mathbf{G}}(1_{T}),\mathrm{St}_{G}\rangle=1
. We also have

(1)

R_{\mathbf{T}\langle\sigma\rangle}^{\mathbf{G}\langle\sigma\rangle}\circ\operatorname{Ind}_{T}^{T\langle\sigma\rangle}=\operatorname{Ind}_{G}^{G\langle\sigma\rangle}\circ R_{\mathbf{T}}^{\mathbf{G}}

from
[
DM94
, Cor. 2.4(ii)]

by adjunction.
Hence,

\langle R_{\mathbf{T}\langle\sigma\rangle}^{\mathbf{G}\langle\sigma\rangle}\operatorname{Ind}_{T}^{T\langle\sigma\rangle}(\rho),\hat{\chi}\rangle=\langle\operatorname{Ind}_{G}^{G\langle\sigma\rangle}R_{\mathbf{T}}^{\mathbf{G}}(\rho),\hat{\chi}\rangle=\langle R_{\mathbf{T}}^{\mathbf{G}}(\rho),\chi\rangle=1,

which by positivity of Harish-Chandra-induction shows that
\langle R_{\mathbf{T}\langle\sigma\rangle}^{\mathbf{G}\langle\sigma\rangle}\hat{\theta},\hat{\chi}\rangle=0
for any nontrivial
\hat{\theta}\in\operatorname{Irr}(T\langle\sigma\rangle)
.
∎

Lemma 5.6
.

Keep the notation of Lemma
5.5
.
Assume that there exists some
\sigma
-stable element
x\in\mathbf{T}_{\ell}^{F}
that is regular (i.e. satisfies
\operatorname{C}_{\mathbf{G}}^{\circ}(x)=\mathbf{T}
) where
\mathbf{T}
is the maximally split torus of
\mathbf{G}
. Then every irreducible constituent of
R_{\mathbf{T}\langle\sigma\rangle}^{\mathbf{G}\langle\sigma\rangle}(1_{T\langle\sigma\rangle})
lies in the principal block.

Proof.

We follow the proof of
[
CE04
, Thm. 21.14]
, applying Lemma
5.4
in place of
[
CE04
, Thm. 21.4]
.
Let
\hat{\chi}\in\operatorname{Irr}(G\langle\sigma\rangle)
be an irreducible constituent of
R_{\mathbf{T}\langle\sigma\rangle}^{\mathbf{G}\langle\sigma\rangle}(1_{T\langle\sigma\rangle})
.
We note that

{}^{\ast}R_{\mathbf{T}\langle\sigma\rangle}^{\mathbf{G}\langle\sigma\rangle}(\hat{\chi})=\displaystyle\sum_{\hat{\theta}\in\operatorname{Irr}(T\langle\sigma\rangle\mid 1_{T})}n_{\hat{\theta}}\hat{\theta}

by positivity of Harish-Chandra induction and again applying
[
DM94
, Cor. 2.4]
. Note that
d^{x}(\hat{\theta})=d^{1}(\hat{\theta})
for each
\hat{\theta}
in the sum since
x\in T
and each (linear)
\hat{\theta}
in the sum is trivial on
T
. Hence,

f:=B_{0}(T\langle\sigma\rangle)d^{x}({}^{\ast}R_{\mathbf{T}\langle\sigma\rangle}^{\mathbf{G}\langle\sigma\rangle}(\hat{\chi}))=\displaystyle\sum_{\hat{\theta}\in\operatorname{Irr}(T\langle\sigma\rangle\mid 1_{T})\cap\operatorname{Irr}(B_{0}(T\langle\sigma\rangle))}n_{\hat{\theta}}d^{1}(\hat{\theta}),

where (by an abuse of notation), the left-hand side denotes multiplication by the idempotent corresponding to
B_{0}(T\langle\sigma\rangle)
.

Note that each
n_{\hat{\theta}}
is a nonnegative integer, each
d^{1}(\hat{\theta})
is an irreducible Brauer character, and
n_{1_{T\langle\sigma\rangle}}\neq 0
. This implies that
f\neq 0
and thus
\langle f,f\rangle\neq 0
. Hence, there is some
\hat{\theta}\in\operatorname{Irr}(T\langle\sigma\rangle\mid 1_{T})\cap\operatorname{Irr}(B_{0}(T\langle\sigma\rangle))
with
\langle f,d^{1}(\hat{\theta})\rangle\neq 0
.

According to Brauer’s first main theorem, it suffices to show that
d^{x}(\hat{\chi})\in CF(\operatorname{C}_{G\langle\sigma\rangle}(x),K)
has a non-zero projection onto the principal block of
\operatorname{C}_{G\langle\sigma\rangle}(x)
.
Note that since
\operatorname{C}_{\mathbf{G}}^{\circ}(x)=\mathbf{T}
, we have
d^{x}(\hat{\chi})={}^{\ast}R_{\operatorname{C}^{\circ}_{\mathbf{T}}(x)\langle\sigma\rangle}^{\operatorname{C}^{\circ}_{\mathbf{G}}(x)\langle\sigma\rangle}(d^{x}(\hat{\chi}))
and
\hat{\theta}=R_{\operatorname{C}^{\circ}_{\mathbf{T}}(x)\langle\sigma\rangle}^{\operatorname{C}^{\circ}_{\mathbf{G}}(x)\langle\sigma\rangle}(\hat{\theta})
. Then we have

\langle B_{0}(T\langle\sigma\rangle)d^{x}(\hat{\chi}),\hat{\theta}\rangle_{T\langle\sigma\rangle}=\langle B_{0}(T\langle\sigma\rangle)d^{x}(\hat{\chi}),R_{\operatorname{C}^{\circ}_{\mathbf{T}}(x)\langle\sigma\rangle}^{\operatorname{C}^{\circ}_{\mathbf{G}}(x)\langle\sigma\rangle}(\hat{\theta})\rangle_{\operatorname{C}_{\mathbf{G}}^{\circ}(x)^{F}\langle\sigma\rangle}

and by Lemma
5.4
this is also the same as

\langle B_{0}(T\langle\sigma\rangle)d^{x}({}^{\ast}R_{\mathbf{T}\langle\sigma\rangle}^{\mathbf{G}\langle\sigma\rangle}(\hat{\chi})),\hat{\theta}\rangle_{T\langle\sigma\rangle}
, which is nonzero from above.
This shows that
d^{x}\hat{\chi}
has a non-zero projection onto the principal block of
\operatorname{C}_{\mathbf{G}}^{\circ}(x)^{F}\langle\sigma\rangle
. Following the last seven lines of the proof of
[
CE04
, Thm. 21.14]
then yields the statement.
∎

5.3.
Consequences for the principal block

We now establish that in some cases needed for our purposes in which
\mathrm{St}_{G}
lies in the principal block, the extended Steinberg character also lies in the principal block. (However, we refer the reader to Example
8.11
below to see that this, perhaps unexpectedly, is not always the case!)

Corollary 5.7
.

Assume that
G=\operatorname{SL}_{n}(q)
with
n=\ell^{k}
for some integer
k
and
2\neq\ell\mid(q-1)
. Then the Steinberg character
\mathrm{St}_{G}
has a rational-valued extension to
\tilde{G}E
that lies in
B_{0}(\widetilde{G}E)
.

Proof.

We begin by remarking that
\mathrm{St}_{\widetilde{G}}
(which extends
\mathrm{St}_{G}
) extends to a rational character
\mathrm{St}_{\widetilde{G}E}
of
\widetilde{G}E
, by Remark
3.4
. (See also
[
S92
]
.)
Further, recall that
E
is abelian, so every character above
\mathrm{St}_{\widetilde{G}}
is an extension.

Let
\sigma\in E
be such that
\widetilde{G}\langle\sigma\rangle
is the Dade ramification group for
B_{0}(\widetilde{G})
in
\widetilde{G}E
and let
\hat{\chi}:=\mathrm{St}_{\widetilde{G}\langle\sigma\rangle}
. Recall from Remark
3.4
that
\hat{\chi}
is a rational extension of
\mathrm{St}_{G}
.
By Lemma
5.3
, there is an
x
as in Lemma
5.6
, and hence combining with Lemma
5.5
, we have
\hat{\chi}
lies in
B_{0}(\widetilde{G}\langle\sigma\rangle)
.

Note that
B_{0}(\widetilde{G}E)
is the unique block lying above
B_{0}(\widetilde{G}\langle\sigma\rangle)
(see Lemma
2.4
).
In particular, note that the rational character
\mathrm{St}_{\widetilde{G}E}
, which extends
\hat{\chi}
, must lie in the principal block.
∎

Note that a regular element
x
as in Lemma
5.6
does not necessarily exist in every case. In some such cases, we may proceed inductively, as in the following.

Lemma 5.8
.

Assume that
G=\operatorname{E}_{6}(q)
with
\ell=3
and
\ell\mid(q-1)
. Then the Steinberg character
\mathrm{St}_{G}
has a rational-valued extension to
\tilde{G}E
that lies in
B_{0}(\widetilde{G}E)
.

Proof.

As before, note that
\mathrm{St}_{G}
has a rational extension to
\widetilde{G}E
and that
E
is abelian.
Let
\sigma\in E
be such that
G\langle\sigma\rangle
is the Dade ramification group for
B_{0}(G)
in
G\langle F_{p}\rangle
. We first claim that
\hat{\chi}:=\mathrm{St}_{G\langle\sigma\rangle}
lies in
B_{0}(G\langle\sigma\rangle)
.

We note that there exists an element
x
in the maximally split torus
T=\mathbf{T}^{F}
satisfying that
x
is
\sigma
-stable and has centralizer
\mathbf{H}:=\operatorname{C}_{\mathbf{G}}(x)
of type
\operatorname{A}_{2}(q)^{3}
. Moreover, there exists a
\sigma
-stable element
y\in H:=\mathbf{H}^{F}
of order
3
with
\operatorname{C}^{\circ}_{\mathbf{H}}(y)^{F}=T
. Applying Lemma
5.6
to
\sigma
viewed as an automorphism of
H
, we observe that all irreducible constituents of
R_{\mathbf{T}\langle\sigma\rangle}^{\mathbf{H}\langle\sigma\rangle}(1_{T\langle\sigma\rangle})
lie in the principal block.

Consider

\langle d^{x}\hat{\chi},R_{\operatorname{C}^{\circ}_{\mathbf{T}}(x)\langle\sigma\rangle}^{\operatorname{C}^{\circ}_{\mathbf{G}}(x)\langle\sigma\rangle}(1)\rangle_{\operatorname{C}^{\circ}_{\mathbf{G}}(x)^{F}\langle\sigma\rangle}.

By Lemmas
5.4
and
5.5
, this is the same as

\langle d^{x}{}^{\ast}R_{\mathbf{T}\langle\sigma\rangle}^{\mathbf{G}\langle\sigma\rangle}(\hat{\chi}),d^{1}(1_{T\langle\sigma\rangle})\rangle_{T\langle\sigma\rangle}=\langle d^{x}(1_{T\langle\sigma\rangle}),d^{1}(1_{T\langle\sigma\rangle})\rangle_{T\langle\sigma\rangle}=1/|T_{\ell}|\neq 0,

which shows the claim as in the end of the proof of Lemma
5.6
, as all constituents of
R_{\operatorname{C}^{\circ}_{\mathbf{T}}(x)\langle\sigma\rangle}^{\operatorname{C}^{\circ}_{\mathbf{G}}(x)\langle\sigma\rangle}(1)=R_{\mathbf{T}\langle\sigma\rangle}^{\mathbf{H}\langle\sigma\rangle}(1)
lie in the principal block of
H\langle\sigma\rangle
.
The same proof shows that
\mathrm{St}_{\widetilde{G}\langle\sigma\rangle}
lies in the principal block, taking
\widetilde{G}\langle\sigma\rangle
to be the Dade group for
B_{0}(\widetilde{G})
in
\widetilde{G}\langle F_{p}\rangle
.

If
\sigma
has odd order, then by Lemma
2.6
,
\mathrm{St}_{\widetilde{G}\langle\sigma\rangle}
is the unique rational extension of
\mathrm{St}_{\widetilde{G}}
to
\widetilde{G}\langle\sigma\rangle
, and therefore must lie below one of the rational extensions to
\widetilde{G}E
. If instead
\sigma
has even order, then the two rational extensions of
\mathrm{St}_{\widetilde{G}}
to
\widetilde{G}\langle F_{p}\rangle
must lie above the two rational extensions of
\mathrm{St}_{\widetilde{G}}
to
\widetilde{G}\langle\sigma\rangle
. In particular, in either case there is a rational extension, say
\chi_{1}
, of
\mathrm{St}_{\widetilde{G}\langle\sigma\rangle}
to
\widetilde{G}\langle F_{p}\rangle
lying in the principal block, as this is the unique block above
\widetilde{G}\langle\sigma\rangle
.

Now, note that the two extensions of
\mathrm{St}_{\widetilde{G}}
to
\widetilde{G}\langle\tau\rangle
, where
\tau
is a nontrivial graph automorphism, must be rational-valued since there is a rational extension to
\widetilde{G}E
.
Then applying Lemma
2.3
to
\chi_{1}
and these two extensions to
\widetilde{G}\langle\tau\rangle
yields that the two characters of
\widetilde{G}E
lying above
\chi_{1}
must be rational-valued. In particular, at least one of these lies in the principal block, completing the proof.
∎

We end this section by considering the Suzuki and Ree groups.

Lemma 5.9
.

Let
S
be a simple Suzuki or Ree group
{}^{2}\!\operatorname{F}_{4}(q^{2})^{\prime}
,
{}^{2}\!\operatorname{G}_{2}(q^{2})^{\prime}
, or
{}^{2}\!\operatorname{B}_{2}(q^{2})
and let
\ell
be any prime dividing
|S|
but not dividing
q^{2}
. Then
S
satisfies condition (
\star
) of Definition
2.1
for
({\mathbb{Q}},\ell)
.

Proof.

Note that the case of the Tits group
{}^{2}\!\operatorname{F}_{4}(2)^{\prime}
or
S={}^{2}\!\operatorname{G}_{2}(3)^{\prime}\cong\operatorname{PSL}_{2}(8)
can be checked directly in GAP, so we may assume that
S\in\{{}^{2}\!\operatorname{F}_{4}(q^{2}),{}^{2}\!\operatorname{G}_{2}(q^{2}),{}^{2}\!\operatorname{B}_{2}(q^{2})\}
with
q^{2}=2^{2n+1},3^{2n+1},2^{2n+1}
, respectively, with
n\geq 1
.

Here the Steinberg character
\mathrm{St}_{S}
is rational-valued, lies in
\operatorname{Irr}_{0}(B_{0}(S))
, and extends to
\operatorname{Aut}(S)
. Further,
\operatorname{Aut}(S)/S
is cyclic of odd order. Let
SE_{\ell^{\prime}}\lhd\operatorname{Aut}(S)
with
SE_{\ell^{\prime}}/S
the
\ell
-complement in
\operatorname{Aut}(S)/S
. Then there is a unique rational extension
\widetilde{\chi}
of
\mathrm{St}_{S}
to
SE_{\ell^{\prime}}
by Lemma
2.6
.
Further, by Lemma
2.5
, we have
\mathrm{St}_{S}
extends to a rational-valued character
\hat{\chi}
of
B_{0}(M)
, where
M\leq SE_{\ell^{\prime}}
is the Dade ramification group. Note also that
\hat{\chi}
is the unique rational extension of
\chi
to
M
. It follows that
\operatorname{Res}_{M}^{SE_{\ell^{\prime}}}(\widetilde{\chi})=\hat{\chi}
. Then by Lemma
2.4
, it must be that
\widetilde{\chi}\in\operatorname{Irr}_{0}(B_{0}(SE_{\ell^{\prime}}))
. Note also that
\ell\nmid o(\widetilde{\chi})
since

, so the linear characters of
SE_{\ell^{\prime}}
are of
\ell^{\prime}
-order.

Now
\operatorname{Aut}(S)/SE_{\ell^{\prime}}
is an
\ell
-group, so
B_{0}(\operatorname{Aut}(S))
is the unique block above
B_{0}(SE_{\ell^{\prime}})
. Then since
\ell\nmid o(\widetilde{\chi})\widetilde{\chi}(1)
, by
[
Na18
, Cor. 6.4]
, there is a canonical extension of
\widetilde{\chi}
to
\operatorname{Aut}(S)
, which must be rational and necessarily lies in

. This completes the proof.
∎

6.
Proof of Theorem
A

Finally, here we use the results of the preceding sections to prove the following, which will give Theorem
A
.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
S
be a simple group of Lie type defined in characteristic
p
, and let
\ell\neq p
be a prime dividing
|S|
. Then there exists a nontrivial character
\chi\in\operatorname{Irr}_{0}(B_{0}(S))
, where
B_{0}(S)
is the principal
\ell
-block of
S
, such that
\chi
extends to a character
\hat{\chi}
in
\operatorname{Irr}_{0}(B_{0}(\operatorname{Aut}(S)_{\chi}))
with
\mathbb{Q}(\hat{\chi})\subseteq\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}
and
\ell\nmid|\mathrm{Aut}(S):\mathrm{Aut}(S)_{\chi}|
. In particular,
S
satisfies condition (
\star
) of Definition
2.1
for
({\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell},\ell)
.

Proof.

We may write
S:=G/\operatorname{Z}(G)
with
G:=\mathbf{G}^{F}
, where
F
is a Steinberg endomorphism and
\mathbf{G}
is a simple, simply connected algebraic group. By Lemma
5.9
, we may further assume that
F
is a Frobenius endomorphism. We have
\operatorname{Aut}(S)=\widetilde{S}\rtimes E
, where
E=E(\mathbf{G}^{F})
is as before and
\widetilde{S}=\widetilde{G}/\operatorname{Z}(\widetilde{G})
with
\widetilde{G}=\widetilde{\mathbf{G}}^{F}
as before.

We may assume
\ell
is odd, since if
\ell=2
, then every rational-valued character of odd degree is in the principal block by
[
G
, Cor. 1.3]
. In particular, the Steinberg character
\chi=\mathrm{St}_{G}
works in that case, as we know the extended character
\mathrm{St}_{\widetilde{G}E}
from Definition
3.3
is rational-valued (see Remark
3.4
). (This case is also claimed in
[
LMNT
]
, using results in
[
NT10
]
.)

Next, suppose that
G=\operatorname{SL}_{n}(\epsilon q)
with
\ell\mid(q-\epsilon)
and
n=\ell^{k}
or that
G=\operatorname{E}_{6}(\epsilon q)
with
\ell=3\mid(q-\epsilon)
. Then
by Corollary
5.7
and Lemma
5.8
, we may assume
\epsilon=-1
. But in this case, by Lemmas
5.1
and
5.2
,
we know the Dade ramification group
M
of
B_{0}(\widetilde{G})
in
\widetilde{G}E
is
\widetilde{G}\langle\sigma\rangle
, where
\sigma
has odd order. By Lemma
2.5
, the principal block
B_{0}(M)
contains the unique rational-valued extension of
\mathrm{St}_{\widetilde{G}}
to
M
. Then applying Lemma
2.4
(and recalling Remark
3.4
), the extended Steinberg character
\mathrm{St}_{\widetilde{G}E}
from Definition
3.3
lies in
B_{0}(\widetilde{G}E)
and is rational-valued.

Hence we may assume that either
\ell\nmid|\operatorname{Z}(G)|
or
G=\operatorname{SL}_{n}(\epsilon q)
satisfying that
\ell\mid(n,q-\epsilon)
but that
n
is not a power of
\ell
.
By Lemmas
4.6
,
4.7
,
4.9
, and
4.10
,
there is then some nontrivial semisimple
\widetilde{\chi}\in\operatorname{Irr}_{0}(B_{0}(\widetilde{S}))
, where
\widetilde{S}:=\widetilde{G}/\operatorname{Z}(\widetilde{G})
, with
{\mathbb{Q}}(\widetilde{\chi})\subseteq{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}
and
\ell\nmid[E:E_{\widetilde{\chi}}]
.

Note that
\operatorname{Aut}(S)_{\widetilde{\chi}}=\widetilde{S}E_{\widetilde{\chi}}
contains the Sylow
\ell
-subgroup of
\operatorname{Aut}(S)
.
If
\ell\nmid|Z(G)|
, by Lemma
4.5
,
\widetilde{\chi}
restricts to some nontrivial
\chi\in\operatorname{Irr}_{0}(B_{0}(S))
. If instead
G=\operatorname{SL}_{n}(\epsilon q)
with
\ell\mid(n,q-\epsilon)
, the same holds by Lemma
4.9
. Further, in these situations the same Lemmas give
E_{\chi}=E_{\widetilde{\chi}}
, so that
\ell\nmid|\operatorname{Aut}(S):\operatorname{Aut}(S)_{\chi}|
since
\operatorname{Aut}(S)_{\chi}=\widetilde{S}E_{\chi}=\widetilde{S}E_{\widetilde{\chi}}
; and certainly
{\mathbb{Q}}(\chi)\subseteq{\mathbb{Q}}(\widetilde{\chi})\subseteq{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}
.

Note that
\langle\widetilde{\chi},D_{\widetilde{G}}(\Gamma)\rangle=\pm 1
by
[
GM20
, 2.6.10(d), Rem. 3.4.18]
, where
\Gamma
is the Gelfand Graev character of
\widetilde{G}
.
Write
E^{\prime}=E_{\widetilde{\chi}}\cap E_{\ell^{\prime}}=E_{\chi}\cap E_{\ell^{\prime}}
. By Lemma
4.4
, there is a unique
\bar{\chi}\in\operatorname{Irr}(\widetilde{G}E^{\prime})
with
\langle\bar{\chi},D_{\widetilde{G}E^{\prime}}(\hat{\Gamma})\rangle=\pm 1
extending
\widetilde{\chi}
, which satisfies
{\mathbb{Q}}(\bar{\chi})={\mathbb{Q}}(\widetilde{\chi})\subseteq{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}
.
From the results of Hiss
[
Hi90
]
, we have
\langle\widetilde{\chi},D_{\widetilde{G}}(\Gamma_{1})\rangle\neq 0
, so in fact
\langle\bar{\chi},D_{\widetilde{G}E^{\prime}}(\hat{\Gamma}_{1})\rangle=\pm 1
, and by Lemma

7.
Block theory for disconnected reductive groups

The results of this section generalize many ideas of the previous sections and may be of independent interest. More precisely, our aim is to develop a block theory for the blocks of a disconnected reductive group.

For this purpose, let
\hat{\mathbf{G}}
be a reductive group equipped with a Frobenius endomorphism
F
and let
\mathbf{G}
be the connected component of its unit. Recall that a closed subgroup
\mathbf{P}^{\prime}
of
\hat{\mathbf{G}}
is called parabolic if and only if
\mathbf{P}:={\mathbf{P}^{\prime}}^{\circ}
is a parabolic subgroup of
\mathbf{G}
. Then
\mathbf{L}^{\prime}:=\operatorname{N}_{\hat{\mathbf{G}}}(\mathbf{P}^{\prime},\mathbf{L})
with
\mathbf{L}
a Levi subgroup of
\mathbf{P}
in
\mathbf{G}
is called a Levi subgroup of
\hat{\mathbf{P}}
.

In particular, we have that
\hat{\mathbf{P}}:=\operatorname{N}_{\hat{\mathbf{G}}}(\mathbf{P})
is a parabolic subgroup of
\hat{\mathbf{G}}
with Levi subgroup
\hat{\mathbf{L}}=\operatorname{N}_{\hat{\mathbf{G}}}(\mathbf{L},\mathbf{P})
. (We note that this is what is called a ‘parabolic’, resp. ‘Levi’, in
[
DM94
]
). Clearly,
\mathbf{P}\leq\mathbf{P}^{\prime}\leq\hat{\mathbf{P}}
and
\mathbf{L}\leq\mathbf{L}^{\prime}\leq\hat{\mathbf{L}}
. Notice however that if
\mathbf{L},\mathbf{L}^{\prime},\hat{\mathbf{L}}
are
F
-stable then we have

(2)

\operatorname{Res}_{\mathbf{L}^{\prime F}}^{\hat{\mathbf{L}}^{F}}\circ{}^{\ast}R_{\hat{\mathbf{L}}\subset\hat{\mathbf{P}}}^{\hat{\mathbf{G}}}={}^{\ast}R_{\mathbf{L}^{\prime}\subset\mathbf{P}^{\prime}}^{\hat{\mathbf{G}}}={}^{\ast}R_{\mathbf{L}^{\prime}\subset\mathbf{P}^{\prime}}^{\mathbf{G}\mathbf{L}^{\prime}}\circ\operatorname{Res}^{\hat{\mathbf{G}}^{F}}_{(\mathbf{G}\mathbf{L}^{\prime})^{F}},

as can be seen from the proof of
[
DM94
, Prop. 2.3]
.

Remark 7.1
.

Note that for any
x\in\mathbf{G}
, the centralizer
\operatorname{C}_{\hat{\mathbf{G}}}(x)
is again a reductive group with parabolic subgroup
\operatorname{C}_{\hat{\mathbf{P}}}(x)
, which has Levi subgroup
\operatorname{C}_{\hat{\mathbf{L}}}(x)
. Note that in contrast to the situation above, we may not necessarily have that
\operatorname{C}_{\hat{\mathbf{P}}}(x)
is the full normalizer in
\operatorname{C}_{\hat{\mathbf{G}}}(x)
of
\operatorname{C}_{\hat{\mathbf{P}}}(x)
, even if this was the case for
(\hat{\mathbf{L}},\hat{\mathbf{P}})
inside
\hat{\mathbf{G}}
.

Throughout, we will continue to let
\ell
be a prime distinct from the defining characteristic for
\hat{\mathbf{G}}
.
We first generalize Lemma
5.4
.

Lemma 7.2
.

Let
\hat{\mathbf{G}}
be a reductive group with connected component
\mathbf{G}
. Let
\hat{\mathbf{L}}
be a Levi subgroup of
\hat{\mathbf{G}}
contained in the parabolic subgroup
\hat{\mathbf{P}}
and
x\in\mathbf{L}^{F}=(\hat{\mathbf{L}}^{\circ})^{F}
of
\ell
-power order. Then

d^{x}\circ{}^{\ast}R_{\hat{\mathbf{L}}\subset\hat{\mathbf{P}}}^{\hat{\mathbf{G}}}={}^{\ast}R^{\operatorname{C}_{\hat{\mathbf{G}}}(x)}_{\operatorname{C}_{\hat{\mathbf{L}}}(x)\subset\operatorname{C}_{\hat{\mathbf{P}}}(x)}\circ d^{x}.

Proof.

In order to show that the two sides of the equation coincide, note that it suffices to show that their restrictions to
\operatorname{C}_{\mathbf{L}}^{\circ}(x)^{F}\langle\sigma\rangle
coincide for every
\sigma\in\operatorname{C}_{\hat{\mathbf{L}}}(x)^{F}
.

Now if
\sigma\in\operatorname{C}_{\hat{\mathbf{L}}}(x)^{F}
then we have

\operatorname{Res}_{\operatorname{C}_{\mathbf{L}}^{\circ}(x)^{F}\langle\sigma\rangle}^{\operatorname{C}_{\hat{\mathbf{L}}}(x)^{F}}(d^{x}\circ{}^{\ast}R_{\hat{\mathbf{L}}\subset\hat{\mathbf{P}}}^{\hat{\mathbf{G}}})=d^{x}\circ\operatorname{Res}_{\mathbf{L}^{F}\langle\sigma\rangle}^{\hat{\mathbf{L}}^{F}}\circ{}^{\ast}R_{\hat{\mathbf{L}}\subset\hat{\mathbf{P}}}^{\hat{\mathbf{G}}}=d^{x}\circ{}^{\ast}R_{\mathbf{L}\langle\sigma\rangle\subset\mathbf{P}\langle\sigma\rangle}^{\mathbf{G}\langle\sigma\rangle}\circ\operatorname{Res}_{\mathbf{G}^{F}\langle\sigma\rangle}^{\hat{\mathbf{G}}^{F}},

using the definition of
d^{x}
and the formula (
2
) for generalized Lusztig restriction above. By Lemma
5.4
, we have

d^{x}\circ{}^{\ast}R_{\mathbf{L}\langle\sigma\rangle\subset\mathbf{P}}^{\mathbf{G}\langle\sigma\rangle}\circ\operatorname{Res}_{\mathbf{G}^{F}\langle\sigma\rangle}^{\hat{\mathbf{G}}^{F}}={}^{\ast}R_{\operatorname{C}^{\circ}_{\mathbf{L}}(x)\langle\sigma\rangle}^{\operatorname{C}^{\circ}_{\mathbf{G}}(x)\langle\sigma\rangle}\circ d^{x}\circ\operatorname{Res}_{\mathbf{G}^{F}\langle\sigma\rangle}^{\hat{\mathbf{G}}^{F}}=\operatorname{Res}_{\operatorname{C}_{\mathbf{L}}^{\circ}(x)^{F}\langle\sigma\rangle}^{\operatorname{C}_{\hat{\mathbf{L}}}(x)^{F}}({}^{\ast}R^{\operatorname{C}_{\hat{\mathbf{G}}}(x)}_{\operatorname{C}_{\hat{\mathbf{L}}}(x)}\circ d^{x}),

where the second equation again follows from (
2
) and the definition of
d^{x}
, completing the proof.
∎

Denote by
\mathcal{E}(\hat{G},1)
the set of characters covering a unipotent character of
G
, where
\hat{G}:=\hat{\mathbf{G}}^{F}
and
G:=\mathbf{G}^{F}
. We need the following lemma (where we also write
\hat{L}:=\hat{\mathbf{L}}^{F}
):

Lemma 7.3
.

Generalized Lusztig induction
R_{\hat{\mathbf{L}}\subset\hat{\mathbf{P}}}^{\hat{\mathbf{G}}}
restricts to a functor
R_{\hat{\mathbf{L}}\subset\hat{\mathbf{P}}}^{\hat{\mathbf{G}}}:\mathbb{Z}\mathcal{E}(\hat{L},1)\to\mathbb{Z}\mathcal{E}(\hat{G},1)
and its adjoint restricts to a functor
{}^{\ast}R_{\hat{\mathbf{L}}\subset\hat{\mathbf{P}}}^{\hat{\mathbf{G}}}:\mathbb{Z}\mathcal{E}(\hat{G},1)\to\mathbb{Z}\mathcal{E}(\hat{L},1)
.

Proof.

Let
\hat{\chi}\in\operatorname{Irr}(\hat{G})
such that
\langle R_{\hat{\mathbf{L}}\subset\hat{\mathbf{P}}}^{\hat{\mathbf{G}}}(\hat{{\lambda}}),\hat{\chi}\rangle\neq 0
for some
\hat{\lambda}\in\mathcal{E}(\hat{L},1)
. Then there is some
i
such that
\hat{\chi}
appears as a direct summand of
H^{i}_{c}(Y_{\mathbf{U}}^{\hat{\mathbf{G}}})\otimes_{\hat{\mathbf{L}}^{F}}\hat{\lambda}
where
\mathbf{U}
is the unipotent radical of
\hat{\mathbf{P}}
. Since
\operatorname{Res}_{L}^{\hat{L}}(\hat{{\lambda}})
is a sum of unipotent characters, it follows from
[
GM20
, Example 2.2.26b]
that there is some
F
-stable torus
\mathbf{T}
of
\mathbf{L}
such that
\langle R_{\mathbf{T}}^{\mathbf{L}}(1_{T}),\operatorname{Res}_{L}^{\hat{L}}(\hat{{\lambda}})\rangle=\langle R_{\mathbf{T}}^{\hat{\mathbf{L}}}(1_{T}),\hat{{\lambda}}\rangle\neq 0
. Hence, there is some
j
such that
\hat{\lambda}
is a direct summand of
H^{j}_{c}(\mathbf{Y}_{\mathbf{V}}^{\hat{\mathbf{L}}})
where
\mathbf{V}
is the unipotent radical of a Borel subgroup of
\mathbf{L}
containing the maximal torus
\mathbf{T}
. By the Künneth formula, there is some
k
such that
H^{i}_{c}(Y_{\mathbf{U}}^{\hat{\mathbf{G}}})\otimes_{\hat{\mathbf{L}}^{F}}H^{j}_{c}(\mathbf{Y}_{\mathbf{V}}^{\hat{\mathbf{L}}})
is a direct summand of

. Hence,
\hat{\chi}
is a direct summand of
H^{k}_{c}(Y_{\mathbf{U}\mathbf{V}}^{\hat{\mathbf{G}}})
and so
\operatorname{Res}_{G}^{\hat{G}}(\hat{\chi})
is a direct summand of
H^{k}_{c}(Y_{\mathbf{U}\mathbf{V}}^{\mathbf{G}})
. This gives the claim by
[
Bon06
, Thm. 11.8]
.

The second statement about generalized Luzstig restriction follows in the same way.
∎

7.1.
More on the Dade group

In Section
2.2
, we introduced the Dade group for the principal block. We now consider the generalization to arbitrary blocks, see
[
Da73
,
Mur13
]
. Given
X\lhd Y
, We denote by
Y[b]\leq Y
the Dade group in
Y
for a block
b
of
X
. We often use the following fact, which is a consequence of
[
Mur13
]
:

Theorem 7.4
.

Let
X\lhd Y
such that
Y/X
is an
\ell^{\prime}
-group. Let
B
be a block of
Y
covering a block
b
of
X
. Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

Y[b]=Y
and some character in
\operatorname{Irr}(B)
has a multiplicity-free restriction to
X
.

(2)

The blocks
B\otimes{\lambda}
for
{\lambda}\in\operatorname{Lin}(Y/X)
are all distinct.

Moreover, when the above hold, we have
Y=X\operatorname{C}_{Y}(D)
, where
D
is a defect group for
b
, and every character of
\operatorname{Irr}(b)
extends to a character of
\operatorname{Irr}(B)
.

Proof.

In the situation of (1), suppose that
\chi\in\operatorname{Irr}(B)
restricts multiplicity-freely to
X
and let
\vartheta\in\operatorname{Irr}(X\mid\chi)
. Then for
y\in Y
we have
\langle X,y\rangle[b]=\langle X,y\rangle
so
[
Mur13
, Thm. 3.9]
implies that
\vartheta
is
y
-stable. Hence,
\chi
restricts irreducibly to
X
and so (1) is equivalent to
[
Mur13
, Thm. 4.1(i)]
. In particular, the equivalence of
[
Mur13
, Thm. 4.1(i)]
and
[
Mur13
, Thm. 4.1(v)]
now implies the equivalence of (1) and (2). The moreover statement is a consequence of
[
Mur13
, Thms. 3.13 and 4.1]
, respectively.
∎

Corollary 7.5
.

Let
X\lhd Y
such that
Y/X
is an
\ell^{\prime}
-group. Let
B
be a block of
Y
covering a block
b
of
X
. Suppose that some character in
\operatorname{Irr}(B)
restricts irreducibly
to
X
. Then for
{\lambda}\in\operatorname{Lin}(Y/X)
we have
B\otimes{\lambda}=B
if and only if
Y[b]\leq\ker({\lambda})
.

Proof.

Let
\chi\in\operatorname{Irr}(B)
be a character that restricts irreducibly to
X
and let
B^{\prime}
be the block of
Y[b]
containing the restriction of
\chi
. Observe that block induction gives a surjective map
\mathrm{Bl}(Y[b]\mid b)\to\mathrm{Bl}(Y\mid b)
(by applying the Fong–Reynolds theorem
[
Na98
, Thm. 9.14]
and
[
Mur13
, Lem. 2.1(iii) and Thm. 3.5(i)]
), which induces a bijection
\mathrm{Bl}(Y[b]\mid b)/Y\to\mathrm{Bl}(Y\mid b)
, where
\mathrm{Bl}(Y[b]\mid b)/Y
denotes the orbits under the action of
Y
.

Under this map,
B^{\prime}
maps to
B
and for each
{\lambda}\in\operatorname{Lin}(Y/X)
, the
Y
-stable block
B^{\prime}\otimes\operatorname{Res}_{Y[b]}^{Y}({\lambda})
maps to
B\otimes{\lambda}
. Hence,
B=B\otimes{\lambda}
if and only if
\operatorname{Res}_{Y[b]}^{Y}({\lambda})=1_{Y[b]}
. This gives the claim.
∎

We also wish to control blocks in the situation of Lemma
2.3
. In the following, given a character
\chi
of a group
X
, we write
b_{X}(\chi)
to denote the block of
X
containing
\chi
.

Lemma 7.6
.

In the situation of Lemma
2.3
assume additionally that
X_{2}\lhd X
and that the quotient

is abelian of
\ell^{\prime}
-order, where

Y[b_{X}(\vartheta_{0})]=X_{1}[b_{X}(\vartheta_{0})]X_{2}[b_{X}(\vartheta_{0})]

and

Write

and

We now return to the situation of disconnected reductive groups

Definition 7.7
.

Let
\ell
be an odd prime and
\mathbf{G}
a connected reductive group. We will say
\ell
satisfies Definition

for
\mathbf{G}
if
\ell
is a good prime for
(\mathbf{G},F)
distinct from the defining characteristic of
\mathbf{G}
,
\ell\nmid|(\operatorname{Z}(\mathbf{G})/\operatorname{Z}(\mathbf{G})^{\circ})^{F}|

\ell\neq 3

\mathbf{G}^{F}
has a component of type
{}^{3}\!\operatorname{D}_{4}
.

We observe that if
t\in(\mathbf{G}^{\ast})^{F}_{\ell}
then
\operatorname{C}^{\circ}_{\mathbf{G}^{\ast}}(t)
is an
F
-stable Levi subgroup of
\mathbf{G}^{\ast}
and we denote by
\mathbf{G}(t)
an
F
-stable Levi subgroup of
\mathbf{G}^{F}
in duality with it. From
[
CE94
]
(see also
[
CE04
, Secs. 21, 23]
), the unipotent blocks of
G
are of the form
b_{G}(\mathbf{L},{\lambda})
for a unipotent
d
-cuspidal pair
(\mathbf{L},{\lambda})
. (Recall here that a unipotent
d
-cuspidal pair
(\mathbf{L},{\lambda})
consists of a
d
-split Levi subgroup
\mathbf{L}
for
(\mathbf{G},F)
and a cuspidal unipotent character
{\lambda}\in\operatorname{Irr}(\mathbf{L}^{F})
.) Further, for an
\ell

we have

\operatorname{Irr}(b_{G}(\mathbf{L},{\lambda}))\cap\mathcal{E}(G,t)=\{R_{\mathbf{G}(t)}^{\mathbf{G}}(\hat{t}\chi_{t})\mid\chi_{t}\in\mathcal{E}(\mathbf{G}(t),(\mathbf{L}_{t},\lambda_{t}))\text{ with }(\mathbf{L},{\lambda})\sim(\mathbf{L}_{t},\lambda_{t})\}.

The following will be useful in computing Dade groups in this situation.

Lemma 7.8
.

Assume the prime
\ell
satisfies Definition
7.7
for
\mathbf{G}
.
Let

such that
\hat{G}/G
is abelian of
\ell^{\prime}
-order. Suppose that

is a unipotent

-block of
G
with
\hat{L}G=\hat{G}
where
\hat{L}:=\operatorname{C}_{\hat{G}}(\operatorname{Z}(\mathbf{L})^{F}_{\ell})
. If
\lambda
is
\hat{L}
-stable and extends to a character of
\operatorname{N}_{\hat{G}}(\mathbf{L})_{\lambda}
, then
\hat{G}[b]=\hat{G}
.

Proof.

We first note that there exists a defect group
D
of
b
such that
\operatorname{N}_{G}(D)\leq N:=\operatorname{N}_{G}(\mathbf{L})
. Indeed, by
[
CE99
, Lem. 4.16]
(see also
[

, Sec. 5.2]
for the case that

),
we have

is the unique maximal abelian subgroup, so
\operatorname{N}_{G}(D)\leq\operatorname{N}_{G}(Z)\leq\operatorname{N}_{G}(\operatorname{C}_{\mathbf{G}}^{\circ}(Z))=N
.

By Theorem
7.4
, it suffices to show that
|\mathrm{Bl}(\hat{G}\mid b)|=|\hat{G}:G|
. Set
\hat{N}:=N\hat{L}
. Recall from
[

that
\mathbf{L}=\operatorname{C}_{\mathbf{G}}(\operatorname{Z}(\mathbf{L})_{\ell}^{F})
. In particular, there exists by
[

a unique block

of

covering

. By the Harris–Knörr correspondence
[

, Thm. 9.28]
applied to both

and

, it then suffices to show that

. (Indeed, note here that the Brauer correspondents of

and

must coincide since

and

.)

For
\hat{\lambda}\in\operatorname{Irr}(\hat{L}\mid\lambda)
, we observe that since
\hat{L}=\operatorname{C}_{\hat{G}}(\operatorname{Z}(L)_{\ell})
by construction, there exists a unique block
\hat{B}(\hat{\lambda})
of
\hat{N}
covering

again by
[
Na98
, Cor. 9.21]
. Therefore we have a map
\operatorname{Irr}(\hat{L}\mid\lambda)\to\mathrm{Bl}(\hat{N}\mid B)
defined by

7.3.
Unipotent blocks and Alvis–Curtis duality

Recall that if
\chi\in\operatorname{Irr}(G)
, there exists a unique sign
\varepsilon_{\chi}\in\{\pm 1\}
and
\chi^{\ast}\in\operatorname{Irr}(G)
such that
\operatorname{D}_{\mathbf{G}}(\chi)=\varepsilon_{\chi}\chi^{\ast}
. In particular, if
\hat{\chi}\in\operatorname{Irr}(\hat{G})
is an extension of
\chi
, then
\operatorname{D}_{\hat{\mathbf{G}}}(\hat{\chi})=\varepsilon_{\chi}\hat{\chi}^{\ast}
for some
\hat{\chi}^{\ast}\in\operatorname{Irr}(\hat{G})
extending
\chi^{\ast}
. (Recall the construction of
\operatorname{D}_{\hat{\mathbf{G}}}
in Section
3
.)
As the Mackey formula holds for unipotent characters, we obtain by
[
GM20
, Thm. 3.44]

\varepsilon_{\mathbf{G}}\varepsilon_{\mathbf{L}}\operatorname{D}_{\mathbf{G}}R_{\mathbf{L}}^{\mathbf{G}}({\lambda})=R_{\mathbf{L}}^{\mathbf{G}}\operatorname{D}_{\mathbf{L}}({\lambda}).

In particular,
(\mathbf{L},{\lambda}^{\ast})
is again a
d
-cuspidal pair and labels the block
\operatorname{D}_{\mathbf{G}}(b_{\mathbf{G}}(\mathbf{L},{\lambda}))
. Let
\hat{\mathbf{G}}=\mathbf{G}\langle\sigma\rangle
for some
\sigma
such that every power of
\sigma
is a rational quasi-central automorphism. If
\mathbf{L}
is
\sigma
-stable and
\hat{\mathbf{L}}=\mathbf{L}\langle\sigma\rangle
is a Levi subgroup of
\hat{\mathbf{G}}
, then under suitable assumptions
[
DM94
, Thm. 3.11]
shows that this equality generalizes to

\varepsilon\operatorname{D}_{\hat{\mathbf{G}}}R_{\hat{\mathbf{L}}}^{\hat{\mathbf{G}}}(\hat{\lambda})=R_{\hat{\mathbf{L}}}^{\hat{\mathbf{G}}}\operatorname{D}_{\hat{\mathbf{L}}}(\hat{\lambda}),

where
\varepsilon
is the class function defined by
\varepsilon(G\sigma^{i}):=\varepsilon_{\mathbf{G}^{\sigma^{i}}}\varepsilon_{\mathbf{L}^{\sigma^{i}}}.

We want to compute the image of
b_{\hat{\mathbf{G}}}(\hat{\mathbf{L}},\hat{{\lambda}})
in the situation of Theorem
7.16
under
\operatorname{D}_{\hat{\mathbf{G}}}
. Under some reasonable assumptions we compute this image and we will describe two interesting cases below where these assumptions are satisfied and illustrate what can happen in these cases.

The formula above shows that
\langle\hat{\chi},R_{\hat{\mathbf{L}}}^{\hat{\mathbf{G}}}(\hat{{\lambda}})\rangle\neq 0
if and only if
\langle\varepsilon\operatorname{D}_{\hat{\mathbf{G}}}(\hat{\chi}),R_{\hat{\mathbf{L}}}^{\hat{\mathbf{G}}}(\operatorname{D}_{\hat{\mathbf{L}}}(\hat{{\lambda}}))\rangle\neq 0.

On the other hand, since
\operatorname{D}_{\mathbf{G}}
fixes
b_{\mathbf{G}}(\mathbf{L},{\lambda})
, there exists a linear character
\varepsilon_{0}
such that
\operatorname{D}_{\hat{\mathbf{G}}}
maps the block
b_{\hat{G}}(\hat{\mathbf{L}},\hat{{\lambda}})
to its tensor product with
\varepsilon_{0}
. Using the fact that
\langle\chi,R_{\mathbf{L}}^{\mathbf{G}}({\lambda})\rangle=\langle\hat{\chi},R_{\hat{\mathbf{L}}}^{\hat{\mathbf{G}}}(\hat{{\lambda}})\rangle
for the unique character
\hat{\chi}\in\operatorname{Irr}(\hat{G}\mid\chi)
lying in the block
b_{\hat{\mathbf{G}}}(\hat{\mathbf{L}},\hat{{\lambda}})
, we deduce that

\varepsilon_{\mathbf{G}}\varepsilon_{\mathbf{L}}\varepsilon_{0}\operatorname{D}_{\hat{\mathbf{G}}}R_{\hat{\mathbf{L}}}^{\hat{\mathbf{G}}}(\hat{{\lambda}})=R_{\hat{\mathbf{L}}}^{\hat{\mathbf{G}}}\operatorname{D}_{\hat{\mathbf{L}}}(\hat{{\lambda}}).

If we assume additionally that
R_{\hat{\mathbf{L}}}^{\hat{\mathbf{G}}}\operatorname{D}_{\hat{\mathbf{L}}}(\hat{{\lambda}})
does not vanish on any coset of
G
in
\hat{G}
then we have

\varepsilon=\varepsilon_{\mathbf{G}}\varepsilon_{\mathbf{L}}\varepsilon_{0}

which in particular shows that
\varepsilon\in\varepsilon_{\mathbf{G}}\varepsilon_{\mathbf{L}}\operatorname{Lin}(\hat{G})
. Since
\varepsilon\in\pm\operatorname{Lin}(\hat{G})
we thus have

\operatorname{D}_{\hat{\mathbf{G}}}(b_{\hat{G}}(\hat{\mathbf{L}},\hat{{\lambda}}))=\varepsilon b_{\hat{G}}(\hat{\mathbf{L}},\hat{{\lambda}}^{\ast})

in the situation of Theorem
7.16
.

Assume that
\mathbf{L}
is a torus so that the formula in
[
DM94
, Thm. 3.11]
holds. Then we have
{\lambda}^{\ast}={\lambda}=1_{\mathbf{L}^{F}}
. Moreover, by
[
DM94
, Thm. 4.13]
we have
R_{\hat{\mathbf{L}}}^{\hat{\mathbf{G}}}(\operatorname{D}_{\hat{\mathbf{L}}}(\hat{{\lambda}}))(\sigma^{i})=R_{\mathbf{L}^{\sigma^{i}}}^{\mathbf{G}^{\sigma^{i}}}(1)\operatorname{D}_{\hat{\mathbf{L}}}(\hat{{\lambda}})(\sigma^{i})\neq 0
.
In particular, if
\varepsilon\neq\pm 1_{\hat{G}}
then
\operatorname{D}_{\mathbf{G}}
fixes the block
b_{G}(\mathbf{L},{\lambda})
while
\operatorname{D}_{\hat{\mathbf{G}}}
maps
b_{\hat{G}}(\hat{\mathbf{L}},\hat{{\lambda}})
to
\varepsilon b_{\hat{G}}(\hat{\mathbf{L}},\hat{{\lambda}})
. See Example
8.11
for a specific situation in which this occurs.

Assume now that
(\mathbf{L},{\lambda})
is a cuspidal pair and that
\hat{\mathbf{L}}
is contained in an
F
-stable parabolic of
\hat{\mathbf{G}}
. In this situation the conclusion of
[
DM94
, Thm. 3.11]
holds again. Then
{\lambda}
is a cuspidal character and the formula for (generalized) Alvis–Curtis duality shows that

and
\operatorname{D}_{\hat{\mathbf{L}}}(\hat{{\lambda}})=(-1)^{\mathrm{rk}(\mathbf{L})}\hat{{\lambda}}
. Moreover,

which shows that
\operatorname{D}_{\mathbf{G}}(b_{G}(\mathbf{L},{\lambda}))=b_{G}(\mathbf{L},{\lambda})
and also
\operatorname{D}_{\hat{\mathbf{G}}}(b_{\hat{G}}(\hat{\mathbf{L}},\hat{{\lambda}}))=b_{\hat{G}}(\hat{\mathbf{L}},\hat{{\lambda}})
.

Lemma 8.1

Keep the assumptions on

Before considering the analogue of Lemma

Lemma 8.2

Proof.

We now prove Theorem

, which we restate here and which solves part of the block distribution problem for the Alperin–McKay–Navarro conjecture.

Theorem 8.10
.

Keep the assumptions of the first paragraph of this section. In particular,
\mathbf{G}
is simple of classical type, but not of type
\operatorname{D}_{4}
, and
\ell
is an odd prime not dividing
|\operatorname{Z}(G)|
. Further,
(\mathbf{L},{\lambda})
is a unipotent
d
-cuspidal pair, where
d:=d_{\ell}(q)
.
Then there exists a block-preserving

\mathcal{H}
-equivariant bijection

\operatorname{Irr}_{0}(GE\mid\mathcal{E}(G,(\mathbf{L},{\lambda})))\to\operatorname{Irr}_{0}(\operatorname{N}_{GE}(\mathbf{L})\mid{\lambda}).

Proof.

Note that
E
is abelian by our assumptions on
G
. Further,
by Lemma
7.9
we know that for any
G\leq H\leq GE
we can fix an extension
\hat{\Lambda}({\lambda})\in\operatorname{Irr}(\operatorname{N}_{H}(\mathbf{L})_{\lambda})
of
{\lambda}\in\operatorname{Irr}(L)
.

We therefore obtain a bijection

\operatorname{Irr}_{0}(H)\to\operatorname{Irr}_{0}(\operatorname{N}_{H}(\mathbf{L}))

by sending the set of characters
\operatorname{Irr}(H\mid R_{\mathbf{L}}^{\mathbf{G}}({\lambda})_{\eta})
(where
R_{\mathbf{L}}^{\mathbf{G}}({\lambda})_{\eta}
is the character in
\mathcal{E}(G,(\mathbf{L},{\lambda}))
corresponding to
\eta
under the bijection in
[
BMM93
, Thm. 3.2]
) bijectively to the set of characters
\{\operatorname{Ind}_{\operatorname{N}_{H}(\mathbf{L})_{\lambda}}^{\operatorname{N}_{H}(\mathbf{L})}(\hat{{\Lambda}}({\lambda})\hat{\eta})\mid\hat{\eta}\in\operatorname{Irr}(W_{H}(\mathbf{L},{\lambda})\mid\eta)\}
.

Set
\hat{G}:=G\langle F_{p}\rangle
and let
b:=b_{G}(\mathbf{L},{\lambda})
. Then either
\hat{G}[b]=(GE)[b]
or there exists some involution
e\in E
such that
GE[b]=\hat{G}[b]G\langle e\rangle[b]
. Now for
\chi\in\operatorname{Irr}(b)
, the two extensions to
G\langle e\rangle
(and similarly in the local case) have the same field of values. Hence, by applying Lemma
7.6
, it suffices to show the statement for
\hat{G}
instead of
GE
. Note that the unipotent characters are
\hat{G}
-invariant by
[
GM20
, Thm. 4.5.11]
.

Set
\hat{N}:=\operatorname{N}_{\hat{G}}(\mathbf{L})
and
\hat{L}^{\prime}:=\operatorname{C}_{\hat{G}}(\operatorname{Z}(L)_{\ell})
, and denote by
\hat{{\lambda}}
the restriction of
\hat{{\Lambda}}({\lambda})
to
\hat{L}^{\prime}\cap\hat{N}=\hat{L}^{\prime}
. Note that the character
\psi:=\operatorname{Ind}_{\hat{N}_{\lambda}}^{\hat{N}}(\hat{{\Lambda}}({\lambda})\hat{\eta})
lies in the unique block of
\operatorname{N}_{\hat{G}}(\mathbf{L})
that covers the block
b_{\hat{L}^{\prime}}(\operatorname{Res}_{\hat{L}^{\prime}}^{\hat{N}_{\lambda}}(\hat{{\Lambda}}({\lambda})\hat{\eta}))
.
(Recall that the uniqueness follows by
[
Na98
, Cor. 9.21]
, and that this unique block is given by block induction.)
Now, recall that the unique block
b^{\prime}
of
N
covering
b_{L}({\lambda})
induces to the block
b=b_{G}(\mathbf{L},{\lambda})
of
G
by
[
CE04
, Thm. 21.7]
. Then using a generalized Harris–Knörr theorem as in
[
KS15
, Thm. B]
, the block of
\psi
induces to
\hat{G}
and this construction gives a surjection from the blocks of
\hat{N}
above
b^{\prime}
and those of
\hat{G}
above
b
. Since
\hat{G}/G
is cyclic, we may then use this and the fact that the other blocks above
b
are tensors with
b
to obtain that there is some block-preserving bijection between
\operatorname{Irr}_{0}(\hat{G}\mid\mathcal{E}(G,(\mathbf{L},{\lambda}))\to\operatorname{Irr}_{0}(\hat{N}\mid{\lambda})
. It remains to show that such a bijection can be chosen to be
\mathcal{H}
-equivariant.

Decompose
\hat{G}=\hat{G}_{2}\hat{G}_{2^{\prime}}
such that
\hat{G}_{\pi}/G
is a Hall
\pi
-subgroup of
\hat{G}/G
for
\pi=2
and
2^{\prime}
, respectively. (Here by abuse of notation, in this context we let
2
denote the set
\{2\}
and
2^{\prime}
denote the set of odd primes dividing
|\hat{G}|
.) Since the decomposition here is coprime, note that Lemma
7.6
applies, and hence it suffices to show the statement for
\hat{G}_{2}
and
\hat{G}_{2^{\prime}}
individually. That is, it suffices to show for
\pi\in\{2,2^{\prime}\}
that there exists block-preserving
\mathcal{H}
-equivariant bijections

\operatorname{Irr}_{0}(\hat{G}_{\pi}\mid\mathcal{E}(G,(\mathbf{L},{\lambda}))\to\operatorname{Irr}_{0}(\hat{N}_{\pi}\mid{\lambda}),

where
\hat{N}_{\pi}:=\hat{G}_{\pi}\cap NE
.

We first consider the
2^{\prime}
-part. For
\hat{G}_{2^{\prime}}
, we observe that every unipotent character is real (even rational) so that there exists a unique real character of
\hat{G}_{2^{\prime}}
extending it and this character lies in the unique real block covering

, see Lemmas
2.6
and
2.7
. On the local side, note that block induction is compatible with complex conjugation (see Lemma

\hat{N}_{2^{\prime}}
that covers the block

(see
[

(see Lemma

). Hence, this bijection can always be chosen such that it preserves the distribution of
\ell
-blocks, since the quotient is abelian, and hence the remaining blocks are tensors of these real blocks with linear characters.

We now consider the extensions to
\hat{G}_{2}
. We have shown in
[

, Thm. 7.6, Sec. 8.A]
that
\mathbb{Q}(\hat{\chi})\subset\mathbb{Q}_{f_{2}}
for every

and

Assume first that

-adic numbers contains all

th roots of unity since then

are

-invariant. Then here any block-preserving bijection (which exists as discussed for
\hat{G}
above) works.

We can therefore assume that

is odd.
In this case,
Proposition
8.9
shows that for every
\chi\in\mathcal{E}(G,(\mathbf{L},{\lambda}))
labeled by the character

, there exists a

-equivariant bijection

.

[

\hat{{\Lambda}}(\hat{{\lambda}})\in\operatorname{Irr}((\hat{N}_{2})_{\lambda}\mid\hat{{\lambda}})
with

.
Moreover, as
\hat{N}_{2}=\operatorname{N}_{\hat{G}_{2}}(\operatorname{Z}(L)_{\ell})
, the characters
\operatorname{Ind}_{(\hat{N}_{2})_{\lambda}}^{\hat{N}_{2}}(\hat{{\Lambda}}(\hat{\lambda})\hat{\eta})

∎

8.1.
An Example

We end the paper by explicitly treating the case where
G=\operatorname{SL}_{2}(q)
and
\ell\mid(q-1)
, which in particular illustrates a situation where the Steinberg character lies in the principal block of
G
but the extended Steinberg character from Definition
3.3
does
not
lie in the principal block.

Example 8.11
.

Suppose that
G=\operatorname{SL}_{2}(q)
and
\ell
is an odd prime with
\ell\mid(q-1)
. Note that the Steinberg character
\mathrm{St}_{G}
lies in the principal block
b:=B_{0}(G)
. Let
\mathbf{T}_{0}\leq\mathbf{B}
be the diagonal torus and corresponding
F
-stable Borel, and write
B:=\mathbf{B}^{F}
and
T_{0}:=\mathbf{T}_{0}^{F}
. Let
p_{0}
be minimal such that there is some positive integer
f_{0}
with
p_{0}^{f_{0}}=q
an
d_{\ell}(p_{0})=1
. In this case,
\operatorname{Ind}_{B\langle F_{p_{0}}\rangle}^{G\langle F_{p_{0}}\rangle}(1_{B\langle F_{p_{0}}\rangle})
coincides with generalized Harish-Chandra induction and contains both the trivial character and the generalized Steinberg character
\mathrm{St}_{G\langle F_{p_{0}}\rangle}
. These are precisely the unipotent characters that lie in the principal block of
G\langle F_{p_{0}}\rangle
.

Assume now that there is
p_{1}
such that
p_{1}^{2}=p_{0}
, so that
d_{\ell}(p_{1})=2
. In particular, if
n\in\operatorname{N}_{G}(\mathbf{T}_{0})\setminus T_{0}
then
nF_{p_{1}}
centralizes
(T_{0})_{\ell}
, as
(p_{0}-1)_{\ell}=(p_{1}+1)_{\ell}(p_{1}-1)_{\ell}
. In this situation, the generalized Steinberg character of
G\langle F_{p_{1}}\rangle
will not be in the principal block
B_{1}:=B_{0}(G\langle F_{p_{1}}\rangle)

but in the block
B_{1}\otimes\varepsilon
, where
\varepsilon\in\operatorname{Irr}(G\langle F_{p_{1}}\rangle/G\langle F_{p_{0}}\rangle)
is the unique character of order two. This can be computed as follows: Using Theorem
7.16
together with
[
DM94
, Theorem 3.11]
(whose assumptions are satisfied in our case as observed in the remarks following the theorem) shows that generalized Alvis–Curtis duality swaps the blocks
B_{1}
and
B_{1}\otimes\varepsilon
. (Indeed, note that
|\mathbf{T}^{nF}|=p+1
so Alvis–Curtis duality and Lusztig induction do not commute on the coset
GF_{p_{0}}
but rather a
-1
sign is introduced. See also Section
7.3
). Since generalized Alvis–Curtis duality maps the trivial character to the generalized Steinberg character, it follows that the Steinberg character is not in the principal block of
G\langle F_{p_{1}}\rangle
. In particular, if
p_{2}^{2}=p_{1}
then
G\langle F_{p_{2}}\rangle[b]=G\langle F_{p_{1}}\rangle
and therefore the principal block
B_{2}
of
G\langle F_{p_{2}}\rangle
gets swapped via Alvis–Curtis duality with
B_{2}\otimes\varepsilon_{2}
where
\varepsilon_{2}
is some linear character (necessarily of order
4
) extending
\varepsilon
. Consequently, the characters covering the trivial character are the unique unipotent characters in
B_{2}
with real character field. Note that on the other hand
d_{\ell}(p_{2})=4
so that all characters in
B_{2}
are
\mathcal{H}
-invariant.

Compare this to the situation where
\tilde{G}=\operatorname{GL}_{2}(q)
in which case
\tilde{G}[B_{0}(\tilde{G})]=\tilde{G}\langle F_{p_{0}}\rangle
. In particular, the image of the principal block under generalized Alvis–Curtis duality is always again the principal block.

Abstract
. We prove that the discrete Dirac operators in three dimensions converge to the continuum Dirac operators in the strong resolvent sense, but not in the norm resolvent sense. Keywords: Discrete Dirac operators, Discrete Fourier transform, continuum limit, spectrum MSC: 47A10, (47B37, 47B93)



1.
Introduction

Consider the free Dirac operator in three dimensions,

(1.1)

\displaystyle\hskip-35.0pt{\mathbb{D}}_{m}=-i\alpha\cdot\nabla+m\beta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\begin{matrix}m&0&-i\partial_{3}&\!-i\partial_{1}-\partial_{2}\!\\
0&m&\!-i\partial_{1}+\partial_{2}\!&i\partial_{3}\\
-i\partial_{3}&\!-i\partial_{1}-\partial_{2}\!&-m&0\\
\!-i\partial_{1}+\partial_{2}\!&i\partial_{3}&0&-m\end{matrix}\right),

where
\alpha_{1}
,
\alpha_{2}
,
\alpha_{3}
and
\beta
are Dirac matrices
and
m\geq 0
is the particle mass,
and the corresponding operator discretised on a cubic lattice of mesh size

h>0
,

(1.2)

\displaystyle{\mathbb{D}}_{m,h}=\left(\begin{matrix}m&0&i\partial_{3,h}^{*}&\!\!i\partial_{1,h}^{*}\!+\partial_{2,h}^{*}\!\\
0&m&\!i\partial_{1,h}^{*}\!-\partial_{2,h}^{*}\!\!&-i\partial_{3,h}^{*}\\
-i\partial_{3,h}&\!-i\partial_{1,h}\!-\partial_{2,h}\!\!&-m&0\\
\!-i\partial_{1,h}\!+\partial_{2,h}\!\!&i\partial_{3,h}&0&-m\\
\end{matrix}\right).

In the latter operator, the partial derivatives are replaced by difference
operators

\displaystyle\begin{split}&[\partial_{j,h}f](hn):=\frac{1}{h}\Big\{f(h(n+e_{j}))-f(hn)\Big\},\\
&[\partial_{j,h}^{*}f](hn):=\frac{1}{h}\Big\{f(h(n-e_{j}))-f(hn)\Big\}\end{split}

where
e_{j}
is the unit vector in the direction of the
j
th coordinate axis.
We prove that the resolvent of the embedded discrete Dirac operator
({\mathbb{D}}_{m,h}\oplus\mathbf{0}_{h}-z)^{-1}
strongly converges to
({\mathbb{D}}_{m}-z)^{-1}
in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})^{4}
as
h\rightarrow 0
;
see the beginning of Section 2 for details of the embedding.
The proof presented here in the three dimensional case
is simpler, shorter and more natural than in

[
3
]
, where the two dimensional case was discussed.
In addition,
we prove that
({\mathbb{D}}_{m,h}\oplus\mathbf{0}_{h}-z)^{-1}
does not converge in the operator norm
sense to
({\mathbb{D}}_{m}-z)^{-1}
as
h\rightarrow 0
.
The proof of the non-convergence is an adaptation of the proof of the corresponding result in the
two-dimensional case in
[
3
]
;
the adaptation enables us to provide an explicit lower estimate of the norm of the resolvent difference as shown in Theorem
2.1
(ii) below (compare
[
1
, Theorems 4.11 and 5.7]
).
In order to get norm resolvent convergence,
one has to modify the discrete Dirac operators in the cubic (resp., square)
lattices.
Such modifications are discussed in
[
1
]
and
[
2
]
.
We remark that our imbedding operator
J_{h}
defined in the next section
is different from those introduced in
[
1
]
and
[
2
]
and exactly preserves the discrete Dirac operator
in
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{h}^{3})
.

We mention that using central difference operators, instead of
forward and backward difference operators, to define
{\mathbb{D}}_{m,h}

does not change the fundamental issue that leads to the positive lower bound for the norm of the resolvent difference in the limit
(Theorem
2.1
(ii)).
The authors would like to thank John Pryce, Cardiff University, for suggesting the use of central difference operators.

2.
Main theorem

As in
[
3
]
, we make the resolvents of
\mathbb{D}_{m}
and of
\mathbb{D}_{m,h}
comparable by embedding the discrete Hilbert space
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{h}^{3})
,
where
\mathbb{Z}_{h}^{3}=h\mathbb{Z}^{3}
,
into the continuum Hilbert space
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
by extending any function

f\in\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{h}^{3})
to the step function

(J_{h}f)(x)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}}f(hn)\,\chi_{I_{n,h}}(x)\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}),

where
\chi_{I_{n,h}}
is the characteristic function of the half-open cube

I_{n,h}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\mid hn_{j}\leq x_{j}<h\,(n_{j}+1)\ (j\in\{1,2,3\})\}.

Clearly

\|J_{h}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}=h^{3}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}}|f(hn)|^{2}
.
The image of the embedding

L^{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{h}^{3}):=J_{h}(\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{h}^{3}))

is a closed subspace of
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
, and
\mathbb{D}_{m,h}
naturally
acts on this subspace. Then

\mathbb{D}_{m,h}\oplus\mathbf{0}_{h}
is the extension of this operator to
all of
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
by setting it equal to the null operator on the
orthogonal complement of
L^{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{h}^{3})
.
The orthogonal projector
P_{h}
of
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
onto
L^{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{h}^{3})

is given by
(cf.
[
3
, eq. (2.10)]
)

(2.1)

(P_{h}\varphi)(x):=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}}\frac{1}{h^{3}}\int_{I_{n,h}}\varphi(y)\,dy\ \chi_{I_{n,h}}(x).

We now state the main theorem, in which

\mathbf{B}
denotes the Banach space of all bounded linear operators in
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})^{4}
,
equipped with the operator norm.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{R}
. Then

(i)

({\mathbb{D}}_{m,h}\oplus\mathbf{0}_{h}-z)^{-1}
strongly converges to

({\mathbb{D}}_{m}-z)^{-1}
in

L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})^{4}
as
h\to 0
.

(ii)

\displaystyle\liminf_{h\to 0}\|({\mathbb{D}}_{m,h}\oplus\mathbf{0}_{h}-z)^{-1}-({\mathbb{D}}_{m}-z)^{-1}\,\|_{\mathbf{B}}\geq\max\Big(\frac{1}{|m-z|},\frac{1}{|m+z|}\Big).

The proof will use the following asymptotics.

Lemma 2.1
.

For
\beta>0
,

(2.2)

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}e^{-\beta k^{2}}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{\beta}}+O(1)\qquad(\beta\rightarrow 0),

(2.3)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}k^{2}\,e^{-\beta k^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{4\sqrt{\beta}^{3}}+O(\beta^{-1})\qquad(\beta\rightarrow 0).

Proof
of Lemma
2.1
.
For the proof of (
2.2
), we split the sum into the parts
with positive and with negative
k
(these sums are evidently equal) and the
term for
k=0
, which can be subsumed in the
O(1)
term.
For a differentiable function
f:[0,\infty)\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
, integration by parts gives the first-order Euler-Maclaurin formula

\sum_{k=1}^{n}f(k)=\int_{0}^{n}f(t)\,dt+\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\int_{k}^{k+1}f^{\prime}(t)\,(t-k)\,dt\qquad(n\in\mathbb{N}).

Hence

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}e^{-\beta k^{2}}=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\beta t^{2}}\,dt-\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\int_{k-1}^{k}2\beta t\,e^{-\beta t^{2}}\,(t-k)\,dt=\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{\beta}}+O(1),

as
|t-k|\leq 1
in each integral in the sum, and also

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}k^{2}e^{-\beta k^{2}}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{2}\,e^{-\beta t^{2}}\,dt+\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\int_{k}^{k+1}2t\,(1-\beta t^{2})\,e^{-\beta t^{2}}\,(t-k)\,dt

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\beta}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\beta t^{2}}\,dt+O(\beta^{-1})=\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{4\sqrt{\beta}^{3}}+O(\beta^{-1})

by an integration by parts in the first integral and as

\left|\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\int_{k}^{k+1}2t\,(1-\beta t^{2})\,e^{-\beta t^{2}}\,(t-k)\,dt\right|\leq\frac{2}{\beta}\int_{0}^{\infty}|s|\,|1-s^{2}|\,e^{-s^{2}}\,ds.\hskip 10.0pt\blacksquare

In the proof of Theorem
2.1
, we need both the discrete Fourier transform

{\mathcal{F}}_{h}:L^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}_{h}^{3})\to L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}_{\!1/h}^{3})
and
the Fourier transform
\mathcal{F}:L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})\to L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})
,
where

{\mathbb{T}}_{\!1/h}^{3}=[-\pi/h,\,\pi/h]^{3}=\{\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}\mid\max\{|\xi_{1}|,|\xi_{2}|,|\xi_{3}|\}\leq\pi/h\}.

The precise definitions of
{\mathcal{F}}_{h}
,
\mathcal{F}
and their basic properties
can be found in sections 2 and 3 of
[
3
]
.
Following

[
3
]
, we
define the (multiplication) operators
\widehat{\mathbb{D}}_{m}:=\mathcal{F}\,\mathbb{D}_{m}\overline{\mathcal{F}}
and

\widehat{\mathbb{D}}_{m,h}:={\overline{\mathcal{F}}}_{h}D_{m,h}{\mathcal{F}}_{h}
.
Since

(2.4)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&[{\mathcal{F}}_{h}(\partial_{j,h}f)](\xi)=\frac{1}{h}(e^{ih\xi_{j}}-1)[{\mathcal{F}}_{h}f](\xi)\\
&[{\mathcal{F}}_{h}(\partial_{j,h}^{*}f)](\xi)=\frac{1}{h}(e^{-ih\xi_{j}}-1)[{\mathcal{F}}_{h}f](\xi)\end{split}

we can obtain the matrix kernel
\widehat{\mathbb{D}}_{m,h}(\xi)
in an explicit form which shows that
for each
\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
,
\big(\widehat{\mathbb{D}}_{m,h}(\xi)-\widehat{\mathbb{D}}_{m}(\xi)\big)\chi_{{\mathbb{T}}_{\!1/h}^{3}}(\xi)\to 0
as
h\to 0
.

The orthogonal projection
Q_{1\!/h}
of
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})

onto
L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}_{\!1/h}^{3})
is
defined as the operator of multiplication with the characteristic function of
{\mathbb{T}}_{\!1/h}^{3}
.
Using the projections
P_{h}
,

Q_{\!1\!/h}\in\mathbf{B}(L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3}))
, we
extend
{\mathcal{F}}_{h}
and its inverse
{\overline{\mathcal{F}}}_{h}

to become elements of
\mathbf{B}(L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3}))

by setting

(2.5)

{\mathcal{F}}_{h}:=Q_{\!1\!/h}\,{\mathcal{F}}_{h}P_{h},\quad{\overline{\mathcal{F}}}_{h}:=P_{h}{\overline{\mathcal{F}}}_{h}Q_{\!1\!/h}.

It is clear that

(2.6)

{\overline{\mathcal{F}}}_{h}{\mathcal{F}}_{h}=P_{h},\qquad{\mathcal{F}}_{h}{\overline{\mathcal{F}}}_{h}=Q_{\!1\!/h},

and

(2.7)

P_{h}^{\perp}{\overline{\mathcal{F}}}_{h}=\mathbf{0},\quad Q_{\!1\!/h}^{\perp}{\mathcal{F}}_{h}=\mathbf{0}.

Proof
of Theorem
2.1
.
(i)
Since
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{3})^{4}
, the Schwartz space of rapidly decreasing functions,
is dense in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})^{4}

and

(2.8)

\|({\mathbb{D}}_{m,h}-z)^{-1}\oplus\mathbf{0}_{h}\|_{\mathbf{B}}\leq 1/|\mathfrak{Im}\,z|,

it is sufficient to prove that

\lim_{h\to 0}\|\big\{({\mathbb{D}}_{m,h}-z)^{-1}\oplus\mathbf{0}_{h}-({\mathbb{D}}_{m}-z)^{-1}\big\}\varphi\|_{[L^{2}]^{4}}=0

for

\varphi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{3})^{4}
.
We begin with

(2.9)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\big\{({\mathbb{D}}_{m,h}-z)^{-1}\oplus\mathbf{0}_{h}&-({\mathbb{D}}_{m}-z)^{-1}\big\}\varphi-\overline{\mathcal{F}}(\widehat{\mathbb{D}}_{m}-z)^{-1}\mathcal{F}\varphi\\
&={\overline{\mathcal{F}}}_{h}\big\{(\widehat{\mathbb{D}}_{m,h}-z)^{-1}\oplus\mathbf{0}_{h}\big\}({\mathcal{F}}_{h}-\mathcal{F})\varphi\\
&\qquad+({\overline{\mathcal{F}}}_{h}-\overline{\mathcal{F}})\big\{(\widehat{\mathbb{D}}_{m,h}-z)^{-1}\oplus\mathbf{0}_{h}\big\}\mathcal{F}\varphi\\
&\hskip 33.0pt+\overline{\mathcal{F}}\big\{(\widehat{\mathbb{D}}_{m,h}-z)^{-1}\oplus\mathbf{0}_{h}-(\widehat{\mathbb{D}}_{m}-z)^{-1}\big\}\mathcal{F}\varphi.\end{split}

The
L^{2}
norm of the first term on the right hand side of (
2.9
) can be estimated by

\|(\widehat{\mathbb{D}}_{m,h}-z)^{-1}\oplus\mathbf{0}_{h}\|_{\mathbf{B}}\|({\mathcal{F}}_{h}-\mathcal{F})\varphi\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})^{4}}\leq\Big(\frac{1}{|\mathfrak{Im}\,z|}\Big)\|({\mathcal{F}}_{h}-\mathcal{F})\varphi\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})^{4}}

which, by
[
3
, Lemma 3.6]
,
tends to
0
as
h\to 0
.
The second term on the right hand side of (
2.9
) can be written as

(2.10)

\displaystyle\begin{split}({\overline{\mathcal{F}}}_{h}-\overline{\mathcal{F}})&\big\{(\widehat{\mathbb{D}}_{m,h}-z)^{-1}\oplus\mathbf{0}_{h}\big\}\mathcal{F}\varphi\\
&=({\overline{\mathcal{F}}}_{h}-\overline{\mathcal{F}})\big\{(\widehat{\mathbb{D}}_{m}-z)^{-1}\oplus\mathbf{0}_{h}\big\}\mathcal{F}\varphi\\
&\qquad+({\overline{\mathcal{F}}}_{h}-\overline{\mathcal{F}})\big\{(\widehat{\mathbb{D}}_{m,h}-z)^{-1}\oplus\mathbf{0}_{h}-(\widehat{\mathbb{D}}_{m}-z)^{-1}\big\}\mathcal{F}\varphi,\end{split}

where, by
[
3
, Lemma 3.5]
, the
L^{2}
norm of the term (
2.10
) tends
to
0
as
h\to 0
.
Combining all the arguments above, we infer that

\displaystyle\limsup_{h\to 0}\|\big\{({\mathbb{D}}_{m,h}-z)^{-1}\oplus\mathbf{0}_{h}-({\mathbb{D}}_{m}-z)^{-1}\big\}\varphi\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})^{4}}

\displaystyle\quad\leq 2\limsup_{h\to 0}\|\big\{(\widehat{\mathbb{D}}_{m,h}-z)^{-1}\oplus\mathbf{0}_{h}-(\widehat{\mathbb{D}}_{m}-z)^{-1}\big\}\mathcal{F}\varphi\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})^{4}},

We finish the proof of statement (i) by showing that

(2.11)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\lim_{h\to 0}\|\big\{(\widehat{\mathbb{D}}_{m,h}-z)^{-1}\oplus\mathbf{0}_{h}-(\widehat{\mathbb{D}}_{m}-z)^{-1}\big\}\mathcal{F}\varphi\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})^{4}}=0.\end{split}

The expression in (
2.11
)
is estimated by

(2.12)

\displaystyle\lim_{h\to 0}\|\big\{(\widehat{\mathbb{D}}_{m,h}-z)^{-1}\oplus\mathbf{0}_{h}-(\widehat{\mathbb{D}}_{m}-z)^{-1}\big\}Q_{1\!/h}\mathcal{F}\varphi\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})^{4}}

\displaystyle\qquad+\lim_{h\to 0}\|\big\{(\widehat{\mathbb{D}}_{m}-z)^{-1}\big\}Q_{1\!/h}^{\perp}\mathcal{F}\varphi\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})^{4}}.

The second term of (
2.12
)
equals
0
since

(\widehat{\mathbb{D}}_{m}(\xi)-z)^{-1}=(|\xi|^{2}+m^{2}-z^{2})^{-1}(\widehat{\mathbb{D}}_{m}(\xi)+z).

The first term of (
2.12
)
is bounded by

\displaystyle\lim_{h\to 0}

\displaystyle\|(\widehat{\mathbb{D}}_{m,h}-z)^{-1}\big(\widehat{\mathbb{D}}_{m}-\widehat{\mathbb{D}}_{m,h})(\widehat{\mathbb{D}}_{m}-z)^{-1}Q_{1\!/h}\mathcal{F}\varphi\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})^{4}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{|\mathfrak{Im}\,z|}\lim_{h\to 0}\!\Big\{\!\!\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\!\Big|\big(\widehat{\mathbb{D}}_{m}(\xi)-\widehat{\mathbb{D}}_{m,h}(\xi)\big)\chi_{{\mathbb{T}}_{\!1/h}^{3}}(\xi)(\widehat{\mathbb{D}}_{m}(\xi)-z)^{-1}[\mathcal{F}\varphi](\xi)\Big|_{{\mathbb{C}}^{4}}^{2}\!d\xi{\Big\}}^{1/2}.

Note that

\mathcal{F}\varphi

on the right hand side of the above inequality
belongs to
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{3})^{4}
.
In a similar manner to
[
3
, Lemma 4.2]
,
one can show that

\displaystyle\big\|\big(\widehat{\mathbb{D}}_{m,h}(\xi)-\widehat{\mathbb{D}}_{m}(\xi)\big)\chi_{{\mathbb{T}}_{\!1/h}^{3}}(\xi)\big\|_{\mathbf{B}({\mathbb{C}}^{4})}\leq\frac{h}{2}|\xi|^{2}.

Equation (
2.11
) then follows by the Lebesgue dominated convergence theorem.

(ii)
Let

u_{h}=(y_{h},0,0,0)^{T}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})^{4}
,

y_{h}(x)=h^{\frac{3}{4}}\,e^{i\frac{\pi}{2h}(x_{1}-x_{2})}\,e^{-h(x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}+x_{3}^{2})}\quad(x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}),

for all
h>0
;
then

\|u_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})^{4}}=\left(\frac{\pi}{2}\right)^{\frac{3}{4}}
.

As the function
e^{-x^{2}/2}

(x\in\mathbb{R})
is its own
Fourier transform, we find
for all
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{3}

(\mathop{\mathcal{F}}y_{h})(\xi)=\frac{1}{(4h)^{\frac{3}{4}}}\,e^{-\frac{1}{4h}[(\xi_{1}-\frac{\pi}{2h})^{2}+(\xi_{2}+\frac{\pi}{2h})^{2}+\xi_{3}^{2}]}.

Let
z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{R}
.
In the following, we show that
(\mathbb{D}_{m}-z)\,u_{h}
tends to 0 as
h\rightarrow 0
(step 1),
whereas the norm of
(\mathbb{D}_{m,h}\oplus\mathbf{0}_{h}-z)^{-1}\,u_{h}
remains
bounded from below by
|m-z|^{-1}
, up to an error that vanishes in the limit
(step 3). In step 2, we prepare application of the embedded discrete Dirac operator by calculating the projection
P_{h}y_{h}
, up to small error.

Step 1.

Applying the Fourier transformation to the Dirac operator (
1.1
)
and taking the matrix inverse, we find

\displaystyle(\mathbb{D}_{m}-z)^{-1}u_{h}

\displaystyle=\overline{\mathcal{F}}\frac{1}{m^{2}-z^{2}+\xi_{1}^{2}+\xi_{2}^{2}+\xi_{3}^{2}}

\displaystyle\qquad\times\left(\begin{matrix}m+z&0&\xi_{3}&\xi_{1}-i\xi_{2}\\
0&m+z&\xi_{1}+i\xi_{2}&-\xi_{3}\\
\xi_{3}&\xi_{1}-i\xi_{2}&-m+z&0\\
\xi_{1}+i\xi_{2}&-\xi_{3}&0&-m+z\end{matrix}\right)\left(\begin{matrix}\mathcal{F}y_{h}\\
0\\
0\\
0\end{matrix}\right).

As the Fourier transform is an isometry on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
,

\displaystyle\|(\mathbb{D}_{m}-z)^{-1}u_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})^{4}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{|m+z|^{2}+\xi_{1}^{2}+\xi_{2}^{2}+\xi_{3}^{2}}{|m^{2}-z^{2}+\xi_{1}^{2}+\xi_{2}^{2}+\xi_{3}^{2}\,|^{2}}

\displaystyle\qquad\times\frac{1}{(4h)^{\frac{3}{2}}}\,e^{-[(\xi_{1}-\frac{\pi}{2h})^{2}+(\xi_{2}+\frac{\pi}{2h})^{2}+\xi_{3}^{2}]/2h}\,d\xi.

Now we observe that there is a constant
C_{z}>0
(which may depend on
z
)
such that

\frac{|m+z|^{2}+\xi_{1}^{2}+\xi_{2}^{2}+\xi_{3}^{2}}{|m^{2}-z^{2}+\xi_{1}^{2}+\xi_{2}^{2}+\xi_{3}^{2}|}\leq C_{z}\qquad(\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{3}),

since the denominator is bounded below by a positive constant and the fraction tends to 1 as
|\xi|\rightarrow\infty
.
Hence

\displaystyle\|(\mathbb{D}_{m}-z)^{-1}u_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})^{4}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C_{z}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{e^{-[(\xi_{1}-\frac{\pi}{2h})^{2}+(\xi_{2}+\frac{\pi}{2h})^{2}+\xi_{3}^{2}]/2h}}{(4h)^{\frac{3}{2}}\,|m^{2}-z^{2}+\xi_{1}^{2}+\xi_{2}^{2}+\xi_{3}^{2}|}\,d\xi

\displaystyle=\frac{C_{z}}{8}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{e^{-(\zeta_{1}^{2}+\zeta_{2}^{2}+\zeta_{3}^{2})/2}}{|m^{2}\!-\!z^{2}\!+\!(\!\sqrt{h}\zeta_{1}\!+\!\frac{\pi}{2h})^{2}\!+\!(\!\sqrt{h}\zeta_{2}\!-\!\frac{\pi}{2h})^{2}\!+\!h\zeta_{3}^{2}|}\,d\zeta.

The last integral converges to 0 as
h\rightarrow 0
by
dominated convergence, as the integrand tends to 0 pointwise and
can be bounded above by an
h
-independent multiple of
e^{-(\zeta_{1}^{2}+\zeta_{2}^{2}+\zeta_{3}^{2})/2}
.
We have thus established that

\lim_{h\rightarrow 0}\|(\mathbb{D}_{m}-z)^{-1}u_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})^{4}}=0.

Step 2.

Before we can apply the discrete Dirac operator (
1.2
), we need to project

u_{h}
into the subspace

L^{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{h}^{3})^{4}
.
By
equation (
2.1
), the projection is given by

P_{h}y_{h}=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}}\widetilde{y}_{h}(hn){\chi}_{{}_{I_{n,h}}},

where

\widetilde{y}_{h}(hn)=h^{\frac{3}{4}}\left(\int_{n_{1}}^{n_{1}+1}e^{i\frac{\pi}{2}t}\,e^{-h^{3}t^{2}}\,dt\right)\left(\int_{n_{2}}^{n_{2}+1}e^{-i\frac{\pi}{2}t}\,e^{-h^{3}t^{2}}\,dt\right)\!\!\left(\int_{n_{3}}^{n_{3}+1}e^{-h^{3}t^{2}}\,dt\right)

for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}
.
By an integration by parts, we find for all
k\in\mathbb{Z}

(2.13)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int_{k}^{{k}+1}e^{\pm i\frac{\pi}{2}t}e^{-h^{3}t^{2}}\,dt=\frac{2}{\pi}(1\pm i)\,e^{\pm i\frac{\pi}{2}{k}}e^{-h^{3}{k}^{2}}+\frac{4h^{3}}{\pi}K^{\pm}_{{{k}},h}\end{split}

with

\displaystyle K^{\pm}_{k,h}

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{\pm i\frac{\pi}{2}k}}{2h^{3}}\,(e^{-h^{3}(k+1)^{2}}-e^{-h^{3}k^{2}})\mp i\int_{k}^{k+1}e^{\pm i\frac{\pi}{2}t}\,t\,e^{-h^{3}t^{2}}\,dt

\displaystyle=\int_{k}^{k+1}\!\!e^{\pm i\frac{\pi}{2}k}\,t\,e^{-h^{3}t^{2}}dt\mp i\!\!\int_{k}^{k+1}\!\!e^{\pm i\frac{\pi}{2}t}\,t\,e^{-h^{3}t^{2}}\,dt.

Noting that

xe^{-x^{2}/2}\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{e}}

(x\geq 0)
, we obtain the estimate

(2.14)

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}|h^{\frac{3}{2}}K^{\pm}_{k,h}|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\displaystyle{\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}}\left(2\int_{k}^{k+1}h^{\frac{3}{2}}\,|t|\,e^{-(h^{\frac{3}{2}}|t|)^{2}/2}\,e^{-h^{3}t^{2}/2}\,dt\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\hskip-68.0pt\leq\dfrac{4}{e}\displaystyle{\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}}\left(\int_{k}^{k+1}e^{-h^{3}t^{2}/2}\,dt\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\hskip-8.0pt\leq\dfrac{8}{e}\displaystyle{\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}}e^{-h^{3}{k^{2}}}=\dfrac{4\sqrt{\pi}}{eh^{\frac{3}{2}}}+O(1)\qquad(h\rightarrow 0)

by (
2.2
).
Similarly,

(2.15)

\int_{k}^{k+1}e^{-h^{3}t^{2}}\,dt=e^{-h^{3}k^{2}}+2h^{3}K^{0}_{k,h}

with

\displaystyle K^{0}_{k,h}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2h^{3}}\int_{k}^{k+1}(e^{-h^{3}t^{2}}-e^{-h^{3}k^{2}})\,dt=-\int_{k}^{k+1}\int_{k}^{t}s\,e^{-h^{3}s^{2}}\,ds\,dt

\displaystyle=-\int_{k}^{k+1}(k+1-s)\,s\,e^{-h^{3}s^{2}}\,ds.

and we have the estimate

(2.16)

\displaystyle\displaystyle{\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}}|h^{\frac{3}{2}}K^{0}_{k,h}|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\displaystyle{\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}}\left(\int_{k}^{k+1}\hskip-5.0pth^{\frac{3}{2}}\,|s|\,e^{-(h^{\frac{3}{2}}|s|)^{2}/2}\,e^{-h^{3}{s^{2}}/2}\,ds\right)^{2}

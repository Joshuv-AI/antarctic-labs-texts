Extreme mass-ratio inspirals and extra dimensions: Insights from modified Teukolsky framework
Shailesh Kumar, Tieguang Zi, Arpan Bhattacharyya

Abstract
Extreme mass-ratio inspirals (EMRIs) offer a promising avenue to test extra-dimensional physics through gravitational wave (GW) observations. In this work, we study equatorial eccentric EMRIs around a spherically symmetric braneworld black hole, focusing on the influence of a tidal charge parameter arising as extra dimensions. Using the fact of t ​ r tr -symmetry of the spacetime under consideration, we implement the Modified Teukolsky Equation (MTE) framework and compute observables, as well as compare the results with those from the standard Teukolsky formalism for general relativity (GR). Our analysis reveals significant deviations in dephasing and mismatch values, especially for the higher eccentricities. These findings highlight not only the observational potential of future low-frequency detectors like the Laser Interferometer Space Antenna (LISA) but also the necessity of using the MTE framework for accurately modeling binaries in theories beyond GR.

We know that the original Teukolsky formalism was developed specifically within the framework of GR, applicable for the background spacetime satisfying the vacuum Einstein equations and belonging to the Petrov type D class–such as the Kerr geometry–where all Weyl scalars vanish except

;
Wagle et al. 2024

This sets up a framework for understanding how bulk gravitational phenomena can influence the physics we observe in four dimensions, particularly in the context of localized gravitational objects like black holes. V
Discussion

EMRIs are distinctive binary systems that have drawn considerable attention in GW astronomy due to their exceptional potential to probe the strong-field regime of gravity and test alternative theories beyond GR, particularly through future space-based detectors. Within this context, the possibility of detecting signatures of extra dimensions via GW observations has been an active area of research. Previous studies-such as
Rahman et al. 2023
;
Zi 2024
;
Kumar et al. 2025
-have explored EMRI dynamics assuming circular and eccentric orbits, typically employing the standard Teukolsky formalism originally developed within the GR framework. However, these approaches inherently omit potential deviations introduced by theories beyond GR. In contrast, the present study adopts the MTE framework for a
tr
-symmetric spactime, which incorporates corrections to the Teukolsky potential arising from non-Ricci flatness of the braneworld geometry. Such a formalism not only has the ability to capture the full theoretical contributions beyond GR but also allows for a more accurate modeling of EMRI waveforms.

Our analysis emphasizes the critical importance of employing the MTE framework when investigating EMRIs in the context of theories beyond GR.
The central objective of this study is to perform a detailed comparison between results obtained using the MTE and those derived from the conventional GR Teukolsky formalism, thereby highlighting the necessity and advantage of the MTE approach in capturing non-GR effects such as the extra-dimensional parameter tidal charge. To this end, we compute key waveform diagnostics-specifically, the GW dephasing and mismatch-for various combinations of eccentricity
e
and tidal charge
Q
, using both the GR Teukolsky and MTE formalisms. Our findings reveal that both the GW dephasing and mismatch are consistently larger when computed using the MTE, indicating a greater sensitivity to non-GR corrections
1
1

1

The values of
Q
considered throughout this analysis remain consistent with those inferred from LIGO-Virgo-KAGRA GWTC-3 data for mass ratios relevant to stellar-mass black holes, as explicitly demonstrated in
Kumar et al. 2025
;
Kumar et al. 2024
;
Chowdhuri et al. 2024
.
. In particular, with the mismatch analysis, we find that for an eccentric orbit with
e=0.1
, the tidal charge can be constrained to an order of magnitude of
\sim 10^{-5}
, while for a more eccentric orbit with
e=0.4
, the constraint becomes tighter, improving to
\sim 5\times 10^{-6}
. This trend suggests that higher eccentricity enhances the detectability of extra-dimensional effects, making EMRIs with larger
e
especially valuable for probing beyond GR physics. A similar behavior is observed in the dephasing analysis, where the difference between the two formalisms grows with increasing eccentricity, especially more pronounced for MTE case, further reinforcing the need for MTE-based modeling in future EMRI waveform predictions and parameter estimation.

While the present work incorporates part of the theory correction emerging from the non-Ricci flatness of the spacetime, encoded in the MTE potential, it highlights the need for a more comprehensive treatment that includes the full contributions from the underlying gravity theory. In particular, extending the formalism to accommodate spacetimes that lack
tr
-symmetry would be essential for capturing a broader range of dynamical effects inherent in more general non-GR geometries. Such an advancement would allow for a more complete and realistic modeling of EMRI waveforms in modified gravity scenarios. Although our analysis remains an approximation, it represents an important initial step in this direction. The results clearly illustrate that even the non-Ricci flatness contribution, when incorporated through the MTE framework, can lead to significant deviations in GW observables. This highlights the critical importance of moving beyond the standard GR-based approaches and developing refined perturbative tools capable of accurately reflecting the structure of alternative gravitational theories in the context of precision GW astrophysics.

Impact of umeclidinium/vilanterol dual bronchodilator therapy on pulmonary function and inflammatory responses in stable COPD patients
Authors: Rongbin Jiang, Weijie Zhan, Xiaoyun Jian, Jiachun Li
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractChronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) represents a significant global health burden necessitating optimized therapeutic interventions. Contemporary guidelines advocate for individualized bronchodilator therapy, yet comparative real-world effectiveness data remain limited, particularly in Chinese populations. To evaluate the comparative effectiveness and safety of umeclidinium/vilanterol (UMEC/VI) dual bronchodilator therapy versus tiotropium monotherapy and budesonide/formoterol combination in patients with stable COPD over a 12-month observation period. This prospective observational cohort study enrolled 171 patients with stable COPD from February 2020 to February 2022 at a tertiary respiratory center. Participants were allocated to treatment groups based on clinical discretion following GOLD guidelines: UMEC/VI (n = 57), tiotropium (n = 57), or budesonide/formoterol (n = 57). The primary outcome was change in forced expiratory volume in one second (FEV1) from baseline to 12 months. Secondary outcomes included functional capacity, symptom burden, inflammatory biomarkers, exacerbation rates, and adverse events. Statistical analysis employed multivariable mixed-effects models with propensity score adjustment to control for confounding. All treatment groups demonstrated significant improvements from baseline, with UMEC/VI showing superior outcomes. The adjusted mean change in FEV1 was 12.6% (95% CI: 9.2–15.9%) for UMEC/VI versus 8.1% (95% CI: 5.4–10.8%) for tiotropium and 5.3% (95% CI: 2.8–7.8%) for budesonide/formoterol (p < 0.001). UMEC/VI was associated with greater improvements in exercise tolerance (6-minute walking distance: +4 5.7 m, 95% CI: 32.5–58.9 m), symptom control (CAT score reduction: − 4.2 points, 95% CI: -5.1 to -3.3), and inflammatory markers. Exacerbation rates were lowest with UMEC/VI (0.42 vs. 0.61 vs. 0.89 events per patient-year, respectively). Adverse event incidence was significantly lower in the UMEC/VI group (8.8%, 95% CI: 3.7–19.6%) compared to budesonide/formoterol (29.8%, 95% CI: 19.2–42.9%). This observational study suggests that UMEC/VI dual bronchodilator therapy is associated with superior clinical outcomes and a favorable safety profile in patients with stable moderate COPD. However, these findings require validation through adequately powered randomized controlled trials before definitive therapeutic recommendations can be established.Keywords: COPD, Dual bronchodilator, Umeclidinium, Vilanterol, Effectiveness, SafetySubject terms: Diseases, Respiratory tract diseases, Chronic obstructive pulmonary disease

Introduction

Chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) represents a debilitating respiratory condition that features chronic inflammation and irreversible airflow limitation, leading to significant morbidity and mortality on a global scale1. Over the past two decades, the global burden of COPD has escalated, positioning the disease as the fourth leading cause of death worldwide1]– [2. COPD caused an estimated 3.23 million deaths worldwide in 2019 (5.8% of all deaths), ranking as the third leading cause of mortality2. In Western nations, advancements in healthcare have not prevented a 163% surge in COPD mortality rates over the past decade3. COPD represents a significant public health issue in China, resulting in approximately 1.28 million fatalities each year, constituting 9.7% of all deaths in 20154,5.

Airflow limitation associated with COPD remains irreversible; therefore, medication cannot fully reverse the condition. However, it can enhance pulmonary ventilation, alleviate wheezing, decrease the frequency of acute exacerbations, and improve exercise capacity in patients6,7.

The treatment of COPD in internal medicine encompasses bronchodilators, hormones, expectorants, anti-inflammatory drugs, and additional therapies. Bronchodilator agents consist of theophylline, β2 adrenoceptor agonists, and muscarinic receptor antagonists. Inhalation dosage forms primarily consist of β2-adrenoceptor agonists and muscarinic receptor antagonists8. Long-acting β2 adrenoceptor agonists (LABAs), short-acting β2 adrenoceptor agonists (SABAs), long-acting muscarinic receptor antagonists (LAMAs), and short-acting muscarinic receptor antagonists (SAMAs) are included in this category9,10. β2 receptor agonists induce bronchodilation by activating β2 receptors in the respiratory system, facilitating the conversion of intracellular adenosine triphosphate into cyclic adenosine monophosphate, thereby eliciting relaxation of tracheal and bronchial smooth muscles. Muscarinic receptor antagonists inhibit cholinergic receptors in the trachea and bronchus, preventing acetylcholine from binding to these receptors, thereby reducing muscle contraction around the airway and decreasing mucus secretion2,10.

The efficacy of umeclidinium/vilanterol (UMEC/VI) dual-bronchodilator therapy has been robustly demonstrated in multiple randomized controlled trials. The EMAX study (n = 1,504) established significant improvements in trough FEV1 compared to placebo over 24 weeks, with mean differences of 144 mL (95% CI: 115–174 mL, p < 0.001)11. The UNITE trial demonstrated superior bronchodilation with UMEC/VI versus individual component monotherapies, showing the additive benefits of the dual-mechanism approach. Comparative effectiveness studies against tiotropium monotherapy, including the CRYSTAL trial, have consistently shown non-inferiority to superiority in lung function outcomes, with UMEC/VI demonstrating mean FEV improvements of 54–90 mL over tiotropium in multiple studies12,13.

Long-term safety data from the IMPACT trial (n = 10,355) and real-world evidence studies have established the cardiovascular safety profile of the UMEC/VI, addressing historical concerns regarding LABA-associated cardiovascular risk14. Meta-analyses encompassing over 15,000 patients have confirmed the superior efficacy-to-safety ratio of dual bronchodilator therapy compared to ICS-containing regimens in patients without frequent exacerbations15.

Specific randomized controlled trials have illustrated the variability in design parameters and outcome measures. The TRILOGY study focused on patients with moderate-to-severe COPD over 52 weeks, while the FLAME trial specifically examined patients with an exacerbation history over 52 weeks, and the ETHOS study evaluated triple therapy in high-risk patients over 52 weeks16,17. These design distinctions in duration, exacerbation risk stratification, and primary endpoints have contributed to the heterogeneity in clinical evidence synthesis.

In the context of acute exacerbation of COPD, the use of glucocorticoids has several beneficial effects, including enhancing the responsiveness of β2 adrenoceptor agonists, reducing mucosal edema and respiratory secretions, decreasing airway hyper-responsiveness, and alleviating irreversible airflow limitation18. Umeclidinium functions as a long-acting muscarinic antagonist (LAMA) with reversible action, whereas vilanterol acts as a long-acting beta-agonist (LABA). The Global Initiative for Chronic Obstructive Lung Disease (GOLD) endorses the combined administration of these medications as a recommended approach for diagnosing, treating, and preventing COPD19,20.

The evolution from single bronchodilator to dual bronchodilator therapy represents a paradigmatic shift in COPD management, predicated on the recognition that combined LAMA/LABA therapy provides superior bronchodilation through complementary mechanisms21. This therapeutic advancement has been particularly relevant in Chinese COPD populations, where high disease prevalence and historically limited access to single-inhaler dual bronchodilators have created substantial unmet clinical needs22. During the study period, several provincial formularies lacked dual bronchodilators, creating a therapeutic gap that necessitates continued reliance on alternative treatment strategies. Post-hoc analyses of landmark trials, such as FORWARD, suggested potential benefits of ICS therapy even at eosinophil counts of 60–100 cells/µL, albeit with attenuated efficacy compared to higher eosinophil populations23. Consequently, physicians maintain legacy prescription patterns while awaiting comprehensive guideline updates and enhanced medication accessibility.

In the context of chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD), researchers define “stable” patients as those who do not experience an acute exacerbation and whose symptoms remain consistent within their typical day-to-day variations according to the National Institute for Health and Care Excellence (NICE) Clinical Knowledge Summaries24. Extensive research on the UMEC/VI has revealed that variability in trial design and outcome measures has hindered the derivation of consistent conclusions regarding optimal therapeutic positioning. This study comprehensively evaluated the efficacy and safety of UMEC/VI in patients with stable COPD, focusing on respiratory symptoms, lung function, and exacerbation rates, within the context of real-world clinical practice patterns in a Chinese healthcare setting.

We hypothesized that dual long-acting bronchodilator therapy with umeclidinium/vilanterol will demonstrate superior clinical outcomes compared to tiotropium monotherapy or budesonide/formoterol combination therapy in patients with stable COPD. Specifically, we test the null hypothesis (H₀): UMEC/VI does not differ from tiotropium or high-dose budesonide/formoterol in 12-month change in FEV1, against the alternative hypothesis (H1): UMEC/VI produces superior improvement in lung function parameters.

Objectives

This study evaluated the effectiveness of combining umeclidinium and vilanterol as dual long-acting bronchodilator therapy in patients with stable chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD), with the aim of enhancing treatment options and improving patient outcomes through a comprehensive assessment of pulmonary function, symptom control, exercise capacity, and exacerbation prevention.

Materials and methods

Study design and ethical approval

This retrospective observational cohort study enrolled 171 patients with stable COPD between February 2020 and February 2022. The study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and approved by the Ethics Committee of the Foshan Hospital of Traditional Chinese Medicine (Approval No. FHTCMIRB2025#1025). Informed consent was obtained from all patients.

Study participants were consecutively recruited from the outpatient respiratory clinic at the Foshan Hospital of Traditional Chinese Medicine between February 2020 and February 2022. Eligible patients were identified through electronic health record screening using ICD-10 diagnostic codes for COPD (J44.0-J44.9) and subsequently assessed for inclusion criteria during routine clinical visits.

Treatment allocation

Treatment allocation was performed through physician-directed clinical decision-making based on individual patient characteristics, disease phenotype, symptom burden, exacerbation history, and alignment with the GOLD 2020 guideline recommendations24. No formal randomization protocol was implemented, reflecting the observational nature of this study. Specifically, UMEC/VI allocation was considered for patients with moderate-to-severe symptoms (CAT ≥ 10), limited exacerbation history (< 2 moderate exacerbations annually), and physician preference for dual bronchodilation based on individual patient assessments. Tiotropium allocation was chosen for patients with mild-to-moderate symptoms suitable for LAMA monotherapy or step-down from combination therapy following achievement of clinical stability25. ICS/LABA allocation was selected for patients with documented exacerbation history (≥ 2 moderate or ≥ 1 severe exacerbations annually) or elevated blood eosinophils (> 150 cells/µL when available); during the study period, several provincial formularies lacked dual bronchodilators, creating a therapeutic gap that influenced prescribing decisions.

Pandemic mitigation protocol

A comprehensive pandemic mitigation protocol was implemented to address COVID-19 considerations during the study period, including routine RT-PCR testing at each visit, exclusion of patients with confirmed or suspected SARS-CoV-2 infection, utilization of teleconsultations for 24% of follow-up visits when clinically appropriate, and sensitivity analysis censoring data from any participant subsequently diagnosed with COVID-19 (n = 4), which demonstrated unchanged primary outcomes and maintained the study validity.

Participants

The cohort included 171 patients, categorized into three treatment groups: dual long-acting bronchodilator therapy with Umeclidinium/Vilanterol (n = 57), single bronchodilator therapy with tiotropium bromide (n = 57), and ICS + LABA therapy with budesonide/formoterol (n = 57). The study cohort included 171 patients, comprising 116 males (67.8%) and 55 females (32.2%), with a mean age of 54.97 ± 6.93 years and a median body mass index (BMI) of 23.97 ± 5.20 kg/m2. Smoking prevalence was 75.4%, 73.7%, and 68.4% in the UMEC/VI, tiotropium, and ICS/LABA groups, respectively, with no significant between-group differences (p = 0.674). The mean blood eosinophil count measured 58.72 ± 19.79 cells/µL, indicating a relatively stable inflammatory profile with minimal eosinophilic burden.

The inclusion criteria were as follows: (1) all participants met the diagnostic criteria for COPD during the stable phase; (2) patients aged 40–80 years with post-bronchodilator FEV1/FVC ratio < 0.70, confirmed by standardized spirometry; (3) stable clinical condition without exacerbations for ≥ 4 weeks prior to enrollment; and (4) informed consent obtained from patients and families.

The exclusion criteria were as follows: (1) patients with other respiratory diseases, specifically asthma, cystic fibrosis, and pulmonary fibrosis; (2) severe acute or chronic infections, defined as conditions requiring hospitalization or intravenous antimicrobial therapy according to the American Thoracic Society/Infectious Diseases Society of America (ATS/IDSA) pneumonia criteria and FDA “serious adverse event” terminology; (3) patients with severe immune deficiency; (4) patients with mental illness; (5) patients who had used other therapeutic drugs within 2 weeks before admission, including systemic corticosteroids or immunomodulators, or who were allergic to drugs used in this study; and (6) patients who were lost during the study.

Outcomes assessed

Primary outcome Change in forced expiratory volume in one second (FEV1) from baseline to 12 months, measured using standardized spirometry according to American Thoracic Society/European Respiratory Society (ATS/ERS) guidelines.

Secondary outcomes (in order of clinical importance): Change in FEV1/FVC ratio from baseline to 12 months; Six-minute walking distance (6 MWD) improvement; COPD Assessment Test (CAT) score reduction; time to first COPD exacerbation; annualized exacerbation rate; changes in inflammatory biomarkers (CRP, TNF-α); hematological parameters (hemoglobin, hematocrit); incidence and severity of adverse events.

Exploratory outcomes Quality of life measures; healthcare utilization patterns; medication adherence rates.

Data measurements

Routine laboratory examination: Peripheral blood samples (4 mL fasting venous blood) were collected in EDTA tubes at standardized morning times (8:00–10:00 AM) to minimize diurnal variation before and after treatment at 3, 6, and 12 months. Samples were processed within 1 h of collection using a hematology analyzer (Shanghai Jumu Medical Equipment Co., Ltd.) to detect the levels of hemoglobin (HGB), hematocrit (HCT), and eosinophils. Quality controls included daily calibration with manufacturer standards and duplicate measurements for 10% of samples (coefficient of variation < 5%). No concurrent arterial blood gas analysis or oxygen saturation monitoring was performed, limiting the interpretation of hematological changes (e.g., potential compensatory erythropoiesis due to chronic hypoxemia cannot be ruled out).

Lung function examination: The Jaeger MS-Diffusion Lung Function Instrument (Jaeger Company, Germany) was used to record lung function test indices of patients before and after treatment at 3, 6, and 12 months. The measurements included Fractional Exhaled Nitric Oxide (FeNO), forced expiratory volume in 1s (FEV1), Forced Vital Capacity (FVC), and FEV1/FVC%. The doctor conducted the measurements three times, both before and after treatment, selecting the best result from these assessments. The dyspnea score was assessed using a standardized scale, specifically, the Modified Medical Research Council (mMRC) Dyspnea Scale. This scale evaluates breathlessness in relation to the level of activity that triggers the symptoms, thereby offering a quantifiable measure of respiratory discomfort. We performed spirometry tests in accordance with ATS/ERS standards to ensure measurement accuracy. Our spirometry testing procedure included calibration methods and operator training to minimize variability. Standardized guidelines outlined by the American Thoracic Society (ATS) and European Respiratory Society (ERS) for pulmonary function testing were followed.

Disease severity assessment: The researchers evaluated the disease severity of patients using CAT before and after treatment at 3, 6, and 12 months. The total score was 40 points, with the disease status of the patients aligned with the final score: (1) very serious (score > 30), (2) severe (20 < score ≤ 30), (3) moderate (10 < score ≤ 20), and (4) mild (score ≤ 10).

Six-minute walking test: Testing was conducted strictly according to the reference methodology, before and after treatment at 3, 6, and 12 months.

Serum inflammatory factors: A fasting venous blood sample (4 ml was collected and centrifuged for 5 min at 3500 rpm in standardized morning times (8:00–10:00 AM) to minimize variability. Researchers determined the levels of CRP and TNF-α in the upper serum using enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) kits (manufacturer: Abcam; model: ab99995 for CRP, ab181421 for TNF-α; detection range: 0.1–100 mg/L for CRP, 0.1–10 ng/mL for TNF-α). Testing was conducted before and after treatment at 3-, 6-, and 12 months, with quality controls, including duplicate assays (CV < 8%) and standard curves for each run.

Treatment protocols

The dual long-acting bronchodilator therapy group received inhaled umeclidinium/vilanterol (Glaxo Operations UK Ltd., registration no. H20180005; specification 62.5 µg/25µg), administered at 62.5 µg once daily. The single bronchodilator group received tiotropium bromide (Zhengda Tianqing Pharmaceutical Group Co., Ltd.; H20060454; 18 µg), dosed at 18 µg once daily. The ICS + LABA group received budesonide/formoterol (AstraZeneca AB, H20140457) at 320/9µg three times daily, resulting in a total daily budesonide dose of 960 µg. This high-dose regimen, which exceeds the 320–640 µg/day ceiling endorsed by GOLD and national guidelines, reflected specific contextual factors, including import regulations that limited available strengths to 320/9 µg formulations, hospital procurement policies that permitted thrice-daily labeling protocols adapted from asthma management, and historical extrapolation from acute-exacerbation trials demonstrating non-inferiority of high-dose inhaled budesonide to systemic corticosteroids in selected patient populations. This supratherapeutic budesonide dose may have inflated the observed adverse-event rate in the ICS + LABA arm and is acknowledged as a study limitation26,27.

Study size

Sample size calculation was performed using one-way ANOVA parameters for three-group comparisons: effect size f = 0.25 (medium effect); α = 0.05 (two-tailed), power = 0.80, yielding the required n = 159 total participants. Assuming a 20% attrition rate, the target enrollment was 199. The achieved sample size of n = 171 provided post-hoc power approximately 0.75 for detecting medium effect sizes, which was considered adequate for this observational study design. The analysis determined the estimated total sample size to be 171 patients, with 57 patients assigned to each group.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using SPSS version 28.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA) and R version 4.3.0, following intention-to-treat principles based on the initial treatment assignment. Descriptive statistics are presented as mean ± standard deviation for normally distributed continuous data, and as frequencies (n) and percentages (%) for categorical data. Continuous variables were assessed for normality using Shapiro-Wilk tests and Q-Q plots. Normally distributed variables are presented as mean ± standard deviation, whereas non-normally distributed variables are presented as medians (interquartile ranges). Categorical variables were presented as frequencies and percentages.

The primary analysis utilized mixed-effects models with restricted maximum likelihood estimation, incorporating fixed effects for time (baseline, 3, 6, and 12 months), treatment group, and time-by-treatment interaction with random intercepts for individual participants. This approach accounts for within-subject correlations and missing data under the missing-at-random assumption. Multivariable models included pre-specified covariates: age, sex, BMI, smoking history (pack-years), baseline FEV1, COPD duration, Charlson Comorbidity Index, and prior medication history.

To address potential confounding inherent in the observational design, we employed multiple analytical approaches: propensity score matching (1:1:1 nearest-neighbor matching with caliper width of 0.1 standard deviations) on age, sex, BMI, baseline FEV1 % predicted, prior-year exacerbations, Charlson Comorbidity Index, and blood eosinophils, as well as multivariable mixed-effects models that retained the entire cohort while adjusting for the same covariates. Standardized mean differences were calculated to assess balance, with differences < 0.10 considered adequate.

For post-hoc pairwise comparisons among the three treatment groups across the four time points (12 total comparisons), we applied Bonferroni correction with adjusted α = 0.004 (0.05/12) to minimize the risk of false positives. Effect sizes were calculated using Cohen’s d with interpretations of small (0.2), medium (0.5), and large (0.8) effects. Multiple imputation (m = 20 datasets) was performed for missing outcome data using chained equations that incorporated all baseline variables and available follow-up measurements. The researchers determined statistically significant differences at a corrected p-value of less than 0.05.

Results

Patient characteristics

The study cohort included 171 patients, comprising 116 males (67.8%) and 55 females (32.2%), with a mean age of 54.97 ± 6.93 years and a median body mass index (BMI) of 23.97 ± 5.20 kg/m2. Smoking prevalence was 75.4%, 73.7%, and 68.4% for UMEC/VI, tiotropium, and ICS/LABA groups respectively, with no significant between-group differences (p = 0.674). The mean blood eosinophil count measured 58.72 ± 19.79 cells/µL, indicating a relatively stable inflammatory profile with minimal eosinophilic burden. The baseline pulmonary function tests indicated mean FEV1 values of 59.23 ± 12.34% of predicted values, which aligns with a diagnosis of moderate-to-severe COPD. Following propensity score matching, standardized mean differences were < 0.10 for all baseline variables, indicating successful balance across treatment groups. The overall study attrition rate was 8.2% (14/171 patients) with balanced distribution across groups: UMEC/VI (n = 4), tiotropium (n = 5), and ICS/LABA (n = 5), with reasons including relocation (n = 6), loss to follow-up (n = 5), and treatment discontinuation due to adverse events (n = 3). Comparative analysis of treatment outcomes

Primary outcome: pulmonary function

The dual bronchodilator therapy group demonstrated superior improvements in FEV1 (mean change: 12.56%, 95% CI: 9.23–15.89%, Cohen’s d = 0.72) compared to tiotropium monotherapy (mean change: 8.41%, 95% CI: 5.67–11.15%, Cohen’s d = 0.54) and ICS + LABA therapy (mean change: 6.89%, 95% CI: 4.12–9.66%, Cohen’s d = 0.43) at 12 months (p < 0.001 for between-group comparisons, Fig. 2). The dual bronchodilator group exhibited clinically meaningful improvements in FEV1/FVC% (mean change: 8.92%, 95% CI: 6.23–11.61%) compared to other treatment groups (p < 0.05). A total of 65% of patients in the dual bronchodilator group achieved clinically meaningful enhancement in FEV1 (≥ 100 mL) compared with 47% in the tiotropium group and 35% in the ICS + LABA group (Table 2).

Secondary outcomes

COPD exacerbation analysis

COPD exacerbations were systematically collected using the modified Anthonisen criteria and represented a crucial clinical endpoint. The UMEC/VI group demonstrated a significantly lower exacerbation incidence with 18/57 patients experiencing ≥ 1 exacerbation (31.6%, 95% CI: 20.8–44.7%) compared to tiotropium with 25/57 patients (43.9%, 95% CI: 31.7–56.8%) and ICS/LABA with 33/57 patients (57.9%, 95% CI: 44.7–70.2%). Annualized exacerbation rates were 0.42 events per patient-year (95% CI: 0.28–0.63) for UMEC/VI, 0.61 (95% CI: 0.44–0.85) for tiotropium, and 0.89 (95% CI: 0.66–1.20) for ICS/LABA. Time-to-first exacerbation analysis using Kaplan-Meier methodology demonstrated significantly longer exacerbation-free intervals in the UMEC/VI group (median: not reached) than in the tiotropium (median: 8.2 months) and ICS/LABA (median: 5.7 months; log-rank p = 0.031) groups (Table 3; Fig. 3).

Symptom assessment and exercise capacity

Dual bronchodilator therapy led to significant reductions in CAT scores (mean change: − 4.23 points, 95% CI: − 5.12, − 3.34) compared with tiotropium and ICS + LABA therapies (p < 0.05), indicating enhanced symptom control and quality of life. The dual bronchodilator group exhibited the most significant improvement in 6 MWD (mean change: 45.67 m, 95% CI: 32.45–58.89) compared to tiotropium and ICS + LABA groups (p < 0.05), exceeding the established minimal clinically important difference of 30–35 m for COPD patients (Table 4; Fig. 4).

Hematological parameters

Hematological parameters also improved, with UMEC/VI showing the largest increases in hemoglobin (from 12.8 ± 1.6 g/dL to 14.0 ± 1.8 g/dL; mean change: 1.23 g/dL, 95% CI: 0.85–1.61; Cohen’s d = 0.55) and hematocrit (from 38.4 ± 4.8% to 41.9 ± 5.5%; mean change: 3.45%, 95% CI: 2.34–4.56; Cohen’s d = 0.48). Progressive changes were evident at intermediate time points (e.g., hemoglobin + 0.4 g/dL at 3 months in UMEC/VI), but these findings require cautious interpretation due to the absence of arterial blood gas monitoring, which limits the differentiation between therapeutic oxygenation improvements and compensatory erythropoiesis in response to chronic hypoxemia (F = 8.42 for hemoglobin, p = 0.001; F = 6.78; hematocrit, p = 0.003). No significant changes in eosinophil counts were observed across groups (p > 0.05) (Fig. 5).

These biomarker trends, analyzed via multiple imputation for missing data (m = 20 datasets) and Bonferroni correction (adjusted α = 0.004), suggest the potential anti-inflammatory benefits of dual therapy, although broader panels (e.g., IL-6, IL-8) are needed for mechanistic confirmation.

Inflammatory biomarkers

All groups demonstrated significant post-treatment reductions in serum inflammatory markers compared to baseline values (p < 0.05). Dual bronchodilator therapy with UMEC/VI showed the most pronounced effects, with CRP levels decreasing from 8.2 ± 3.1 mg/L at baseline to 5.6 ± 2.8 mg/L at 12 months (mean change: -2.56 mg/L, 95% CI: -3.45 to -1.67; Cohen’s d = 0.68), compared to tiotropium (-1.58 mg/L, 95% CI: -2.47 to -0.69; Cohen’s d = 0.45) and ICS + LABA (-0.89 mg/L, 95% CI: -1.78 to -0.00; Cohen’s d = 0.28). Similar patterns were observed for TNF-α, with UMEC/VI achieving a mean change of -1.23 ng/mL (95% CI: -1.83 to -0.63; Cohen’s d = 0.42). Intermediate assessments at 3 and 6 months showed gradual progression of these reductions, with the greatest early benefits in the UMEC/VI group (e.g., CRP − 0.8 mg/L at 3 months vs. -0.5 mg/L in tiotropium). Mixed-effects modeling confirmed significant group differences (F = 19.345, p < 0.001), time effects (F = 23.124, p = 0.001), and interactions (F = 4.356, p = 0.014) adjusted for covariates including age, BMI, and baseline FEV1 (Table 5).

Adverse events and safety profile

The dual bronchodilator group exhibited a significantly lower incidence of adverse events at 8.57% (95% CI: 3.45–13.69%) than the tiotropium group at 11.43% (95% CI: 5.23–17.63%) and the ICS + LABA group at 30.56% (95% CI: 20.34–40.78%) (p < 0.05). However, these safety outcomes should be interpreted with substantial caution given the study’s limited statistical power for rare events. Post-hoc power calculations revealed only 35% power to detect a 20% absolute difference in adverse event rates between groups (α = 0.05), indicating considerable uncertainty around the point estimates (Table 6). The wide confidence intervals, particularly for the UMEC/VI group (ranging from 3.45 to 13.69%), indicate substantial statistical uncertainty that precludes definitive safety conclusions (Fig. 6).

Discussion

This observational study provides compelling preliminary evidence that umeclidinium/vilanterol dual bronchodilator therapy may confer superior clinical benefits compared with tiotropium monotherapy or high-dose budesonide/formoterol combination therapy in patients with stable, moderate chronic obstructive pulmonary disease. The magnitude of functional improvement observed with UMEC/VI therapy, characterized by a 12.56% enhancement in FEV1 and corresponding improvements in symptom control and exercise tolerance, represents clinically meaningful benefits that align with established minimal clinically important differences and corroborate findings from pivotal randomized controlled trials28–30.

The therapeutic superiority of dual bronchodilation observed in our cohort reflects the complementary mechanisms of action inherent in LAMA/LABA combination therapy, wherein umeclidinium provides sustained muscarinic receptor antagonism, while vilanterol delivers prolonged β2-adrenergic receptor activation. This mechanistic synergy has been consistently demonstrated across multiple randomized controlled trials, including the landmark EMAX study, which established significant improvements in trough FEV1 (144 mL improvement versus placebo), and the CRYSTAL trial, which demonstrated mean FEV improvements of 54–90 mL over tiotropium monotherapy29,31. The observed effect sizes in our study (Cohen’s d = 0.72, FEV improvement) exceeded those typically reported for single bronchodilator therapy and aligned with the regulatory approval standards for dual bronchodilator combinations32,33.

The substantially lower exacerbation rates observed in the UMEC/VI group (0.42 events per patient-year) compared to the tiotropium (0.61) and ICS/LABA (0.89) groups represent clinically significant reductions that extend beyond mere statistical significance to encompass meaningful improvements in disease stability and quality of life. These findings corroborate evidence from large-scale randomized controlled trials and real-world effectiveness studies, which have consistently demonstrated the capacity of dual bronchodilation to reduce clinically important deteriorations and prevent treatment failure in symptomatic patients with stable COPD30,32,34. The significantly longer time-to-first exacerbation observed with UMEC/VI therapy suggests sustained therapeutic benefits that may translate to reduced healthcare utilization and improved long-term outcomes.

The apparent safety advantage of UMEC/VI over ICS/LABA therapy, characterized by a lower incidence of adverse events (8.57% versus 30.56%), aligns with established knowledge regarding ICS-associated complications, particularly pneumonia risk, in patients with low blood eosinophil counts. Interpretation is tempered by the high budesonide exposure (960 µg·day⁻¹) and the cohort’s low median eosinophil count (58 cells·µL⁻¹), both of which bias against ICS efficacy. The poorer outcomes observed with high-dose ICS/LABA therapy in our cohort likely reflect inappropriate patient selection, given the mean eosinophil count of 58.72 cells/µL, which falls substantially below the threshold at which ICS therapy demonstrates optimal benefit-risk ratios28,35. Contemporary evidence from the ELLITHE trial and other real-world studies support the preferential use of dual bronchodilator therapy over ICS-containing regimens in patients without frequent exacerbations or elevated eosinophil counts28,34.

The observed hematological changes, although statistically significant, warrant cautious interpretation in the broader pathophysiological context of COPD management. The increases in hemoglobin and hematocrit may represent either a therapeutic benefit through enhanced bronchodilation and improved ventilation-perfusion matching, or ongoing compensatory responses to chronic hypoxemia independent of therapeutic intervention. Without concurrent arterial blood gas measurements or continuous oxygen saturation monitoring, definitive mechanistic conclusions regarding the clinical significance of these changes cannot be drawn. The absence of standardized blood collection protocols and potential variations in hydration status further complicates the interpretation of these findings.

The integration of our findings with contemporary clinical evidence requires careful consideration of population differences and variations in study design that may influence treatment responses. While the IMPACT trial demonstrated the benefits of triple therapy in patients with a documented exacerbation history, our study focused on stable COPD patients with limited exacerbation burden, representing a distinct clinical phenotype that may derive greater benefit from dual bronchodilator therapy without the added complexity and potential adverse effects of inhaled corticosteroids36,37. The observed treatment responses likely reflect the complementary bronchodilatory mechanisms of LAMA/LABA combination therapy, providing comprehensive airway smooth muscle relaxation through both the cholinergic and adrenergic pathways.

The potential influence of differential medication adherence on treatment outcomes is an important consideration, as the once-daily dosing regimen of UMEC/VI demonstrated superior adherence compared with the thrice-daily ICS/LABA regimen in our cohort. This dosing-frequency effect has been consistently demonstrated across multiple therapeutic areas and may partially explain the observed treatment differences while representing an important practical consideration for clinical implementation. Real-world effectiveness studies have confirmed that simplified dosing regimens contribute to improved long-term adherence and clinical outcomes in COPD management34,38.

Several methodological limitations must be acknowledged when interpreting these findings in the broader context of COPD therapeutics. The observational, non-randomized design introduces a considerable risk of indication bias and residual confounding despite propensity score matching and multivariable adjustment procedures. The relatively young age cohort (mean 54.97 years) compared to typical COPD populations may limit extrapolation to older patients with more severe disease and greater comorbidity burden. The single-center Chinese population may not reflect treatment responses in other ethnic groups or healthcare systems because of genetic polymorphisms affecting drug metabolism, environmental exposure patterns, and cultural factors influencing adherence patterns and symptom reporting.

The statistical power limitations for safety outcomes represent a significant constraint in clinical interpretation, as the relatively small sample size provided insufficient power to detect clinically meaningful differences in adverse event rates, particularly for rare but serious events, such as cardiovascular complications or severe respiratory infections. The wide confidence intervals for adverse event estimates indicate substantial uncertainty around the point estimates, precluding definitive safety conclusions. Additionally, the 12-month observation period may be insufficient to capture late-emerging safety signals or long-term cardiovascular effects associated with chronic bronchodilator use in vulnerable populations.

Our findings provide preliminary evidence supporting the potential benefits of dual bronchodilation in appropriately selected Chinese patients with COPD with stable disease characteristics. The observed associations between UMEC/VI therapy and favorable clinical outcomes warrant further investigation but should not be interpreted as definitive proof of therapeutic superiority. The apparent anti-inflammatory effects, as evidenced by the reduced C-reactive protein and tumor necrosis factor-alpha levels, require validation through broader biomarker panels and mechanistic studies to elucidate the underlying pathophysiological mechanisms of action.

Contemporary evidence from multiple therapeutic areas suggests that the early implementation of optimal bronchodilator therapy may prevent disease progression and reduce the risk of future exacerbations, supporting the concept of “early maximal bronchodilation” in symptomatic patients with stable COPD29,30. The sustained improvements in lung function, symptom control, and exercise tolerance observed with UMEC/VI therapy throughout the 12-month follow-up period suggest a durable therapeutic benefit that may translate to improved long-term outcomes and reduced healthcare utilization.

Future research should encompass adequately powered, multicenter, randomized controlled trials with comprehensive respiratory physiology assessments, longer follow-up periods to evaluate sustained benefits and long-term safety profiles, investigation in older patients with severe or very severe COPD, and economic evaluation of real-world effectiveness. The integration of arterial blood gas monitoring, exercise oximetry, and comprehensive inflammatory biomarker panels would enhance mechanistic understanding and clinical interpretation of therapeutic effects. Additionally, comparative effectiveness research examining different dual bronchodilator combinations and their positioning within the broader COPD therapeutic landscape would provide valuable insights for clinical decision making.

In conclusion, this observational study suggests that umeclidinium/vilanterol dual-bronchodilator therapy may be associated with superior improvements in lung function, symptom control, exercise tolerance, and exacerbation prevention in patients with stable COPD. However, these findings should be interpreted as hypothesis-generating evidence requiring confirmation through adequately powered randomized controlled trials before definitive therapeutic recommendations can be made. The observed treatment associations warrant further investigation in diverse patient populations with enhanced methodological rigor and longer follow-up periods to establish sustained benefits and a comprehensive safety profile. Although these results align with mechanistic expectations and previous randomized controlled trial evidence, definitive therapeutic positioning requires validation through multicenter studies with enhanced statistical power and broader patient representation.

Limitations

This study has several methodological limitations that must be considered when interpreting the findings and their clinical implications. The observational, non-randomized design represents the most significant limitation, introducing multiple sources of potential bias, including indication, performance, and detection biases. The absence of prospective registration and blinding further compromises methodological rigor and transparency. The relatively small sample size (n = 57 per group) provided insufficient statistical power for definitive safety comparisons, particularly for rare adverse events, with post-hoc power calculations revealing only 35% power to detect clinically meaningful differences in adverse event rates. The single-center design and Chinese population may limit generalizability to other ethnic groups and healthcare systems owing to genetic polymorphisms affecting drug metabolism, environmental exposure patterns, and cultural factors influencing treatment responses. Additionally, the lack of arterial blood gas monitoring precludes definitive interpretation of hematological changes, and the absence of comprehensive biomarker panels limits mechanistic insights into anti-inflammatory effects. The relatively young age cohort (mean 54.97 years) compared to typical COPD populations may limit extrapolation to older patients with more severe disease and greater comorbidity burden.

Conclusion

This observational study suggests that umeclidinium/vilanterol dual bronchodilator therapy may be associated with superior improvements in lung function, symptom control, exercise tolerance, and exacerbation prevention in patients with stable, moderate COPD. However, these findings should be interpreted as hypothesis-generating evidence requiring confirmation through adequately powered, randomized controlled trials before definitive therapeutic recommendations can be made. The observed treatment associations warrant further investigation in diverse patient populations with comprehensive respiratory physiology assessments and longer follow-up periods to establish the sustained benefits and long-term safety profiles. While these results align with mechanistic expectations and previous randomized controlled trials, definitive therapeutic recommendations require validation through multicenter studies with enhanced methodological rigor and broader patient representation.

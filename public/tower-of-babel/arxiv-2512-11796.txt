Loop-string-hadron approach to SU(3) lattice Yang-Mills theory, II: Operator representation for the trivalent vertex Preprint: IQuS@UW-21-116
Saurabh V. Kadam, Aahiri Naskar, Indrakshi Raychowdhury, Jesse R. Stryker

Abstract
This work is the second installment of a series on the Loop-String-Hadron (LSH) approach to SU(3) lattice Yang-Mills theory. Here, we present the infinite-dimensional matrix representation for arbitrary gauge-invariant operators at a trivalent vertex, which results in a standalone framework for computations that supersedes the underlying Schwinger-boson framework. To that end, we evaluate in closed form the result of applying any gauge-invariant operators on the LSH basis states introduced in Part I Kadam et al. 2025 . Classical calculations in the LSH basis run significantly faster than equivalent calculations performed using Schwinger bosons. A companion code script is provided, which implements the derived formulas and aims to facilitate rapid progress towards Hamiltonian-based calculations of quantum chromodynamics.

The starting point for any such calculation is a Hamiltonian framework
Kogut and Susskind 1975
, which comes with a description of basis vectors spanning the Hilbert space. An essential part of the Hamiltonian framework is the Gauss law constraints, which separate the physical Hilbert space from the entire Hilbert space containing gauge redundancy. The Hamiltonian operator commutes with the gauge theory Hilbert space, and hence the dynamics remain confined only within the physical subspace. This sets the initial tasks for quantum simulating lattice gauge theory to (I), constructing the basis vectors spanning the physical subspace and (II), finding the representations of observables (physical aka gauge invariant) in the chosen or constructed physical basis. The current work presents the task (II) for pure SU(3) lattice gauge theory in more than
1+1
dimensions.

It is worth mentioning the non-trivialities that arise beyond
1+1
dimensions, while even a pure gauge theory without any dynamical matter itself is of great interest. For the case of pure gauge theories, the gauge invariant Hilbert space is created by manifestly gauge-invariant Wilson loop operators. Computing the dynamics in this subspace is non-trivial as the Wilson loops are an overcomplete and non-local basis. Prepotential formulation provides a partial solution for this by solving the problem of non-locality in terms of site-local gauge invariant operators and states which are refered as ‘local loops’ for the theory. For the case of SU(2) gauge theory, the problem of overcompleteness is solved using various approaches
Watson 1994
;
Loll 1993
;
Mathur 2006
;
Anishetty and Raychowdhury 2014

The loop string hadron (LSH) framework for SU(2) LGTs, derived from the prepotential framework, provides an alternative for obtaining a local and orthonormal gauge invariant loop basis in any dimensions. This allows computing dynamics for SU(2) only within the physical subspace feasible and is being explored in the context of quantum simulating SU(2) LGTs
Raychowdhury and Stryker 2020b
;
Dasgupta and Raychowdhury 2022
;
Davoudi et al. 2021

Davoudi et al. 2023

Mathew et al. 2025

However, the generic solution of finding a complete and orthonormal basis for SU(3) gauge theory is still an open problem. The learning from the SU(2) LSH framework instructs us to tackle the problem at the level of its individual building blocks. While the point splitting scheme for SU(2) works perfectly
Raychowdhury and Stryker 2020a
, the same strategy does resolve all issues at a vertex for SU(3)
Kadam et al. 2025

In Part I
Kadam et al. 2025

The current article, Part II in the series, provides the matrix elements of the gauge-invariant observables as the building blocks for constructing the Hamiltonian in the SU(3) LSH framework.

Importantly, in Part I, all but a few results ultimately had to be calculated in terms of their Schwinger-boson (or prepotential) definitions. While Schwinger bosons remain the underlying foundation for the LSH formulation, the focus of this Part II paper is to report on how one can work exclusively with the trivalent vertex in terms of LSH variables. This goes beyond just defining and characterizing the gauge invariant basis, as one must also be able to express operators and demonstrate that all expected properties are carried over.
The basis used is nonorthogonal; however, we believe the results presented here are a prerequisite for eventually formulating the problem in terms of orthogonal degrees of freedom.
A Part III paper, the final in this series, will complete the story by focusing on applying the results of this paper beyond a single vertex – to actual lattices, where the additional generalizations needed in going from SU(2) to SU(3) are less substantial but are not yet explained in the literature.

III
Results

At the trivalent vertex there is a suite of gauge-invariant operators that are to be understood in order to normalize states, to express observables, and to construct a lattice Hamiltonian matrix.
The operators are ultimately constructed in terms of irreducible Schwinger bosons. However, as remarked in Part I, it is costly to work in terms of the underlying Schwinger bosons.
To lift the calculations from their Schwinger-boson definitions to a more compact, LSH-basis representation, it is sufficient to know the matrix representation of any local operator in this basis.
In an infinite-dimensional Hilbert space, what we mean by having a matrix representation is that for any linear operator
\mathcal{O}
and basis ket
\left.\left|\mathbf{q}\right\rangle\right\rangle
, the state

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}\left.\left|\mathbf{q}\right\rangle\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mathbf{q^{\prime}}}[\mathcal{O}]_{\mathbf{q^{\prime}},\mathbf{q}}\left.\left|\mathbf{q^{\prime}}\right\rangle\right\rangle

(3)

has coefficients
[\mathcal{O}]_{\mathbf{q^{\prime}},\mathbf{q}}
that are reported in, or derivable from, the Results below.

The operators one may form at the trivalent vertex may be defined along one, two, or all three legs.
The operators of principle interest are the so-called “corner” operators
Burbano and Bauer 2024
, involving two legs of the vertex
i,j
.
This is because the corner operators are used to construct Wilson loop operators on actual extended lattices
Anishetty et al. 2010
;
Raychowdhury and Stryker 2020a
.

III.2
Programming implementation

For illustrative purposes, we have prepared a companion Mathematica notebook that implements all of the above operators, provided as Supplemental Material
SM
.
The program follows a similar structure to the companion code provided for Part I, but instead of working in the underlying Hilbert space of Schwinger bosons—the tensor product of 18 harmonic oscillators—it works in the space of seven LSH quantum numbers.
The gauge-singlet operators are implemented as functions whose inputs and outputs are Hilbert-space kets.
In alignment with our Part II Results, some operators are hard-coded with formulas while others are implemented by taking advantage of commutator identities.

With the ability to do faster calculations in terms of strictly LSH degrees of freedom, example commands are included for tasks such as state normalization, Gram-Schmidt orthogonalization, and constructing the eigenstates of the seventh Casimir operator (
C_{T}=T_{A}^{\dagger}T_{B}^{\dagger}T_{A}T_{B}
) that was discussed in Part I.

IV
Methods and derivations

To derive our Results, calculations are done using the irreducible Schwinger bosons (ISBs).
These have already been described in detail in Part 1, so we only review here essential details for completeness.
The gauge-singlet operators are then expressed in terms of the ISBs.
The proofs of our formulas follow from using the properties of ISBs, especially their commutation relations.

IV.1
A brief reminder of SU(3) Prepotentials

With the SU(3) irreducible Schwinger bosons being the underlying foundation for the LSH formulation, we will below give a concise review of the most essential properties needed to derive all subsequent results.
The reader is referred to Part I (Ref.
Kadam et al. 2025
) and Refs.
Mathur 2005
;
Anishetty et al. 2009
;
Anishetty et al. 2010
for more details.

For each leg

and

for
\alpha=1,2,3
(a total of six modes per leg).
The color index

is said to be a fundamental (antifundamental) index if it is used as a lower (upper) index.
When only one vertex leg is being discussed, we may suppress the
i

In this work, any operator raised to the zeroth power is understood to be shorthand for the identity operator:
(L_{IJ}^{\dagger})^{0}=(T_{A}^{\dagger})^{0}=(T_{B}^{\dagger})^{0}\equiv 1

Above, the notation
\left.\left|\cdots\right\rangle\right\rangle
is used to indicate a basis ket that is not necessarily normalized to unity.
In this notation, the total vacuum ket
\ket{0}
may also be written as

\displaystyle\left.\left|0\right\rangle\right\rangle

\displaystyle\equiv\ket{0}=\left.\left|0\,0\,0;0\,0\,0;0\right\rangle\right\rangle.

(44)

The vacuum is normalized to unity by definition:
\braket{0|0}=1
.

The above construction is a direct generalization of the SU(2) trivalent vertex Hilbert space.
In Part I, we have discussed the fact that for SU(3), in contrast to SU(2), the basis states simply constructed from products of pure-creation singlets are not all orthogonal to each other.
The LSH “quantum numbers” therefore fail to be “good” quantum numbers for SU(3), in the sense that states with different quantum numbers are not necessarily orthogonal. It is for this reason we refer to the above basis as “naive.”
Nonetheless, we argued in Part I that the naive basis is still a valid one, i.e., the states are linearly independent and complete, but not overcomplete.
Our argument was not a mathematical proof, but the results and proofs reported in the present work demonstrate completeness for Hamiltonian dynamics;
the basis evidently spans the dynamical Hilbert space because it is closed under the application of any SU(3) singlet operator that could appear in the Hamiltonian.

At the time of writing, we do not know of a particular use case for these operators, but since we have claimed that our basis is closed under the action of arbitrary SU(3)-singlet operators at the vertex, it is worthwhile to address them.

V
Discussion

In this article, we have presented and justified a matrix representation of on-site gauge singlet operators in the SU(3) LSH framework. The results are obtained for a trivalent vertex equipped with the naive SU(3) gauge-invariant Hilbert space that was introduced in Ref.
Kadam et al. 2025
.

In mathematical terms, our construction means the following:
for any SU(3)-singlet operator
\mathcal{O}
at the trivalent vertex and any basis ket
\left.\left|\textbf{q}\right\rangle\right\rangle
, the state
\mathcal{O}\left.\left|\textbf{q}\right\rangle\right\rangle
can be expressed in the same basis:

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}\left.\left|\textbf{q}\right\rangle\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{\textbf{q}^{\prime}}[\mathcal{O}]_{\textbf{q}^{\prime},\textbf{q}}\left.\left|\textbf{q}^{\prime}\right\rangle\right\rangle

(61)

for known coefficients
[\mathcal{O}]_{\textbf{q}^{\prime},\textbf{q}}
given in Sec.
IV
.
This important task was also done in Refs.
Raychowdhury and Stryker 2020a
;
Kadam et al. 2023
, although it was much more straightforward for those theories, largely because the naive bases defined for them had orthogonality built in.

In practice, to actually do the matrix arithmetic on computers one must in principle truncate the basis to finite dimensionality, and order it.
Note that the usage of a basis that is not orthonormal can have unexpected consequences, for example, the matrix representation for a Hermitian operator
\mathcal{O}
may not be equal to its conjugate-transpose. However, basis-independent quantities, such as the eigenvalues, will remain identical. Analytic calculations are tedious, as can be inferred from the derivations presented in the section
IV
while unavoidable to make progress in computing dynamics for the theory. The novelty of the current manuscript is to present the results in a ready-to-use format so that the community can use it and proceed further. The numerical companion of this manuscript facilitates the same further for practical implementations.

It is important to note that, the Clebsch-Gordon coefficients for SU(3) remain difficult to work with analytically, with their evaluation often being outsourced to software that will calculate them on a case-by-case basis
Balaji et al. 2025
. The prepotential framework for SU(3), computations using the irreducible prepotentials, are far from being intuitive for all practical computations. The authors have further experienced that the cost of numerically implementing the Schwinger bosons or prepotential variables on a classical computer is prohibitively time-consuming for anything but the lowest truncations. The loop operators and their algebra are also not very intuitive (considering the coefficients associated with them), but having the analytic formulas allows them to be used rapidly on a classical computer. As the full-fledged quantum simulator for SU(3) LGTS beyond 1+1 dimensions remains to be achieved, reliance on classical computing and producing benchmarks for the theory is essential and is indeed the need of the hour. This work fulfills this crucial gap in progress of the field by providing carefully calculated operator representations and a ready-to-use numerical companion code for practical usefulness.

Work is in progress to develop the complete Hamiltonian matrix using the results of the current work, and the same will be reported as Part III of this series in the near future. The authors believe this series would stand as the fundamental building blocks towards quantum simulating the dynamics of QCD and benchmarking the results of quantum simulation.

On the CQC Conjecture: A sufficient condition and an extension
Hasan Iqbal

Abstract
The CQC conjecture by Schneeloch et al. (Physical Review A 90.6, 2014) asserts that the sum of classical mutual information between two parties obtained by measuring individual systems in two mutually unbiased bases can not exceed their shared quantum mutual information. This remarkable conjecture, which deserves more work, still remains open. In this article, using a result by Coles and Piani (Physical Review A 89.2, 2014), we derive a sufficient condition for which the CQC conjecture holds. We show that this condition validates the conjecture for a wider class of states compared to the states that was mentioned in the original work. Furthermore, we extend this conjecture to higher number of mutually unbiased bases and prime dimensions. We prove this extended CQC conjecture on isotropic states of arbitrary prime dimensions and simulate it extensively for dimension 3 and 5 for random bipartite states, observing no contradiction.



1
Introduction

Entropic uncertainty (EU) relations
[
1
,
2
]
are widely used in different areas of quantum information theory. These equations express fundamental limits of obtainable information from quantum systems if one performs different measurements
[
3
]
. Although the entropy-based formulations of these equations are more widely studied, mutual information based formulations are also quite useful as they capture both individual and combined entropies in a multipartite system. Hall
[
4
,
5
]
worked on the mutual information based formulations and posed interesting open questions. For example, Hall discussed the ideas of information exclusion relation which states that information gain from a measurement could only come at the expense of information gain in a measurement which is mutually unbiased to the first one
[
4
]
. In that work, after proving an upper bound on mutual information sum when the measured system could be correlated with a classical system, he also posed the open question about a similar upper bound when arbitrary number of mutually unbiased bases (MUB)
[
6
]
are involved. These correlated systems however, could be quantum too. As we have seen in the case of EU relation involving quantum memories formulated by Berta et al.
[
7
]
, it is interesting to ask about upper bounds for information exclusion relation when the correlated system is quantum and different MUBS are involved. This is precisely what Schneeloch et al. asked
[
8
]
. This very interesting conjecture, which the authors called the ‘CQC conjecture’, although appeared over a decade ago in 2014, still remains open. The authors showed that if proven, this conjecture will have nice applications in strengthening Berta et al.’s EU relation
[
7
]
, witnessing quantum entanglement
[
9
]
and also help in proving the security of quantum key distribution protocols
[
10
]
. They have also proved this conjecture for all pure states, states with one maximally mixed subsystems, and if one of the measurements is minimally disturbing, that is, if the measurement operator commutes with the one of the reduced subsystems. In this work, we show that the CQC conjecture holds for more states other than the ones mentioned earlier, by identifying a sufficient condition based on Coles and Piani’s work
[
11
]
. We also identify a further application of the CQC conjecture in generalizing the Maassen-Uffink EU relation
[
12
]
, whose potential applications may be of independent interest. Furthermore, we extend this conjecture to cover more mutually unbiased basis measurements and prime dimensions, which we call the ECQC conjecture. Then, we identify a sufficient condition for this ECQC conjecture. Moreover, with explicit calculations, we demonstrate the validity of this extended conjecture on isotropic states with arbitrary prime dimensions. We also perform experimental simulations of the validity of the ECQC conjecture on random pure and bipartite states in dimension 3 and 5, where we do not observe any contradiction to this conjecture.

H(B)

or

\Tr_{A}(\rho_{AB})=\rho_{B}
(
\Tr_{B}(\rho_{AB})=\rho_{A}

\ket{\Psi^{+}}_{AB}

A
and

’s dimensions, but it should be clear from the context. To denote the set of natural numbers from

[n]

2.1
Mutually Unbiased Bases

For an excellent review on MUBs, readers are encouraged to consult Durt et al.’s work
[
6
]
. Informally, two measurement bases are said to be mutually unbiased, if measuring a quantum state in one of these bases leaves us as maximally uncertain about the measurement outcomes in the other basis. More formally, we say that two measurement bases
\mathcal{Z}=\{\ket{z_{0}},\ket{z_{1}},...\ket{z_{d-1}}\}
and
\mathcal{X}=\{\ket{x_{0}},\ket{x_{1}},...\ket{x_{d-1}}\}
are mutually unbiased if it holds that:

\displaystyle|\braket{z_{i}|x_{j}}|^{2}=\frac{1}{d},\text{ for any }i,j\in\{0,1,...d-1\}.

A set of such measurement bases are said to be mutually unbiased if they are pairwise mutually unbiased. This class of measurements is important in a wide range of information processing tasks including quantum state tomography, where MUBs give the minimal and optimal set of measurements
[
13
]
to reconstruct the state’s density operator. Although it is an interesting open question to determine the maximum number of MUBs in a dimension
d
, it is known that there are
d+1
MUBs if
d
is a prime power
[
6
,
13
,
14
]
.

2.2
Entropic Uncertainty Relations

Entropic uncertainty relations are quite integral in the study of quantum information theory in general and its various applications such as analysis of quantum cryptographic protocols. See
[
1
,
2
]
for comprehensive surveys on this topic. As an example, we can see the well known EU relation by Maassen and Uffink
[
12
]
, whose strengthened version by Coles et al.
[
1
]
we present below. The Maassen-Uffink relation states that for a quantum state
\rho_{A}
, if one performs two MUB measurements
\mathcal{Z}
and
\mathcal{X}
on it, the resulting uncertainty will have the following lower bound:

\displaystyle H(Z^{A})+H(X^{A})\geq\log(d)+H(A).

(1)

However, quantum systems could be correlated with other quantum systems too. In that case, conditioning on those correlated systems gives rise to interesting uncertainty relations as shown by Berta et al.
[
15
]
. They have shown that if the subsystem
A
of a bipartite system
\rho_{AB}
is arbitrarily correlated with some quantum system
B
, then performing
\mathcal{Z}
and
\mathcal{X}
basis measurement on
A
will have the following lower bound for conditional entropies:

\displaystyle H(Z^{A}|B)+H(X^{A}|B)\geq\log(d)+H(A|B),

(2)

where
d
is the number of possible outcomes in
\mathcal{Z}
and
\mathcal{X}
basis measurement, and also the dimension of the subsystems
A
and
B
. Note that, in this case, we are considering
\mathcal{Z}
and
\mathcal{X}
to be MUB, but the original formulation by Berta et al.
[
7
]
holds for any pair of observables, and a slight modification comes on the right hand side of equation (
2
). Among the generalizations of the Maassen-Uffink relation to involve more than two MUB measurements, the one that we use in this work is found in one of the works by Sanchez
[
16
]
. In this generalization, if we consider
d
MUBs given that the dimension
d
is a prime number, then the sum of the entropies of the resulting classical random variables
M_{i}^{A}
for all
i\in\{1,...,d+1\}
, we have:

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{d+1}H(M_{i}^{A})\geq(d+1)(\log(d+1)-1),

(3)

where we see the usage of the fact that there are
d+1
MUBs if
d
is a prime power as discussed in the previous section.

3
The CQC conjecture

The CQC conjecture
[
8
]
asserts that the sum of two classical mutual information, obtained by bipartite measurements in the
\mathcal{Z}
and
\mathcal{X}
basis, is always upper bounded by the original mutual information. That is, on an arbitrary bipartite state
\rho_{AB}
, if we perform two measurements
\mathcal{Z}
and
\mathcal{X}
on both of the parties, we have that:

\displaystyle I(Z^{A}:Z^{B})+I(X^{A}:X^{B})\leq I(A:B),

where
I(.)
is the mutual information between the random variables. It is noted in
[
17
]
that, mutual information holds all types of classical and quantum correlations shared among the parties. In that sense, it is interesting to study the reasons for the validity of this conjecture. Because as noted in the work by Coles et al.
[
1
]
, the following does not always hold:

\displaystyle I(Z^{A}:B)+I(X^{A}:B)\leq I(A:B).

(4)

So, the CQC conjecture, one could argue that, captures the reduction of mutual information when the conditioning subsystem makes the transition from quantum to classical realm. Along with this particular transition being an interesting study on its own, the original paper on CQC conjecture by Schneeloch et al.
[
8
]
also mentions important applications. For example, this conjecture can strengthen Berta’s uncertainty relation
[
7
]
, whose formulation we shall see later in this work. It also helps witnessing entanglement as the authors of the CQC conjecture showed. If CQC conjecture holds, then we have
I(A:B)\leq\min\{H(A),H(B)\}
, and if it turns out that
I(Z^{A}:Z^{B})+I(X^{A}:X^{B})>\min\{H(A),H(B)\}
, then this implies that
H(A|B)<0
, signifying entanglement between
A
and
B
. The CQC conjecture also helps in proving security of quantum key distribution protocols as it places the following upper bound on the adversary Eve’s (
E
) mutual information with one of the honest party Alice (
A
)
[
18
]
:

\displaystyle I(A:E)\leq 2\log(d)-I(Z^{A}:Z^{B})-I(X^{A}:X^{B}).

Consequently, the quantum key distribution protocol designers can use this upper bound in establishing a rate for secure keys in Alice and Bob’s communication
[
10
]
. Here, we identify one other possible application of the CQC conjecture. In the literature, we have seen generalizations of Maassen-Uffink uncertainty relation, in the forms of involving multiple MUBs or expression in terms of conditional entropies involving quantum memories
[
1
,
15
]
. Interestingly if the CQC conjecture holds, we get another generalization of the Maassen-Uffink relation to two parties parties in the following form, which has not been discussed previously as far as we know.

Proposition 1
.

For a bipartite state
\rho_{AB}
, if we consider two mutually unbiased measurements
\mathcal{Z}
and
\mathcal{X}
in dimension
d=2
, then, given the CQC conjecture, we have that

\displaystyle H(Z^{A}Z^{B})+H(X^{A}X^{B})\geq 2+H(AB).

Proof.

We start from the CQC conjecture and see that proposition (
1
) follows directly from it.

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274ptI(Z^{A}:Z^{B})+I(X^{A}:X^{B})\leq I(A:B)

\displaystyle\implies H(Z^{A})+H(Z^{B})-H(Z^{A}Z^{B})+H(X^{A})+H(X^{B})-H(X^{A}X^{B})\leq I(A:B)

\displaystyle\implies H(Z^{A})+H(X^{A})+H(Z^{B})+H(X^{B})-H(Z^{A}Z^{B})-H(X^{A}X^{B})\leq I(A:B)

\displaystyle\implies 1+H(A)+1+H(B)-H(Z^{A}Z^{B})-H(X^{A}X^{B})\leq H(A)+H(B)-H(AB)

\displaystyle\implies H(Z^{A}Z^{B})+H(X^{A}X^{B})\geq 2+H(AB),

(5)

where in the penultimate line, we have used the Maassen-Uffink relation mentioned in equation (
1
), strengthened by Coles et al.
[
1
]
. We note that the potential applications of this generalized Maassen-Uffink could be investigated in the future. ∎

3.1
Extended CQC conjecture

The motivation to extend the CQC conjecture to cover more MUB measurements and prime dimension comes from the discussion presented in the excellent review paper by Coles et al
[
1
]
. In that work, the authors list instances of works by various authors who showed that using mutually unbiased bases is not a guarantee to obtain strong EU relations. This weakness in the lower bound of EU relations ultimately results in providing further credence to the ECQC and especially to the CQC conjecture as in both cases we are using less than the maximum number of MUBs available in the considered dimensions. Note that the CQC conjecture two MUBs and even in dimension
2
there are
3
MUBs available
[
6
]
. So considering more MUBs but that is always at least one less than the total number of MUBs available in a particular dimension
d
, we propose the following.

Conjecture 3.1
.

Let
\rho_{AB}
be a bipartite state where each subsystem has prime dimension
d
. If both parties make measurement
d+1
measurements on their individual subsystems, then the mutual information of the resulting random variables will have the following upper bound:

\displaystyle I(A:B)\geq\underset{\mathcal{S}\in[d+1]^{d}}{\min}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{S}}I(M^{A}_{i}:M^{B}_{i}).

(6)

The elements of the set
\mathcal{S}
are collections of
d
mutual information resulting from
d+1
bipartite MUB measurements. So, the right hand side of this equation (
6
) captures the quantity where both parties make
d+1
number of MUB measurements and select the
d
among those resulting classical mutual information that gives them the smallest sum. Inspired by the simulation on random states performed by the authors of the CQC conjecture, we perform similar simulations on random states and multiple dimensions and didn’t find any contradiction.

As an immediate application of this extension, as we have seen in the case of CQC, we will get a generalization of Maassen-Uffink uncertainty relation in bipartite case, for multiple MUB measurements and prime dimensions. We state that in the form of the following proposition.

Proposition 2
.

For a bipartite state
\rho_{AB}
, where each subsystem
A
and
B
has prime dimension
d
, for a set of mutually unbiased bases
\{M_{i}\}_{i=1}^{d+1}
, given the ECQC conjecture, we have that:

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{d+1}H(M_{i}^{A}M_{i}^{B})\geq 2((d+1)(\log(d)-1))-I(A:B)-I_{max}(M^{A}:M^{B}),

(7)

where
I_{max}(M^{A}:M^{B})=\underset{i}{\max}\text{ }I(M^{A}_{i}:M^{B}_{i}),\forall\{i=1,2,...,d+1\}
, for any measurement base
M_{i}
, from the set of MUBs in this dimension
d
.

Proof.

We follow the same strategy as proposition (
5
) to arrive at proposition (
7
). We start with a generalization of the Maassen-Uffink relation developed by Sanchez
[
16
]
. This establishes the following lower bound on the sum of entropies resulting from MUB measurements on a system with prime dimension
d
, where
d+1
MUB measurements exist
[
6
]
:

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{d+1}H(M_{i})\geq(d+1)(\log(d+1)-1).

Using this lower bound we see the following, given our conjecture:

\displaystyle\underset{\mathcal{S}\in[d+1]^{d}}{\min}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{S}}I(M^{A}_{i}:M^{B}_{i})\leq I(A:B)

\displaystyle\implies\underset{\mathcal{S}\in[d+1]^{d}}{\min}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{S}}I(M^{A}_{i}:M^{B}_{i})+I_{max}(M^{A}:M^{B})\leq I(A:B)+I_{max}(M^{A}:M^{B})

\displaystyle\implies\sum_{i=1}^{d+1}I(M^{A}_{i}:M^{B}_{i})\leq I(A:B)+I_{max}(M^{A}:M^{B})

\displaystyle\implies\sum_{i=1}^{d+1}H(M_{i}^{A})+\sum_{i=1}^{d+1}H(M_{i}^{B})-\sum_{i=1}^{d+1}H(M_{i}^{A}M_{i}^{B})\leq I(A:B)+I_{max}(M^{A}:M^{B})

\displaystyle\implies 2((d+1)(\log(d+1)-1))-\sum_{i=1}^{d+1}H(M_{i}^{A}M_{i}^{B})\leq I(A:B)+I_{max}(M^{A}:M^{B})

\displaystyle\implies\sum_{i=1}^{d+1}H(M_{i}^{A}M_{i}^{B})\geq 2((d+1)(\log(d+1)-1))-I(A:B)-I_{max}(M^{A}:M^{B}).

∎

4
Results

In this section, we present the results related to the CQC and ECQC conjectures. First we show that there is a sufficient condition for the validity of CQC conjecture that covers states which are currently not known to hold CQC. This new class of states could be of arbitrary dimension
d
, not just the prime dimensions which is considered for the ECQC conjecture.

4.1
A sufficient condition for CQC

Proposition 3
.

For any bipartite state
\rho_{AB}
and two MUB measurements
\mathcal{Z}
and
\mathcal{X}
, if

\displaystyle I(Z^{A}:B)-I(Z^{A}:Z^{B})+I(X^{A}:B)-I(X^{A}:X^{B})\geq\log(d)-H(A),

(8)

then the CQC conjecture holds.

Proof.

In the article by Coles and Piani
[
11
]
, we see that for a bipartite state
\rho_{AB}
, if we apply two MUB measurement
\mathcal{Z}
and
\mathcal{X}
on subsystem
A
, then the sum of the two resulting mutual information has the following upper bound:

\displaystyle I(Z^{A}:B)+I(X^{A}:B)\leq\log(d)-H(A|B).

(9)

Note that equation (
9
) that we have presented here is a special case involving MUB measurements only, of the more general form of the equation that deals with arbitrary orthonormal bases from the article
[
11
]
. The general equation had the following form, written in our notations:

\displaystyle I(Z^{A}:B)+I(X^{A}:B)\leq r-H(A|B),

where

\displaystyle r=min\{r(X,Z),r(Z,X)\},

\displaystyle r(X,Z)=\log_{2}\left(d\sum_{j}\underset{k}{max}|\braket{x_{j}|z_{k}}|^{2}\right),

\displaystyle r(Z,X)=\log_{2}\left(d\sum_{k}\underset{j}{max}|\braket{x_{j}|z_{k}}|^{2}\right),

and the
x_{j},z_{k}
are the basis states of the
\mathcal{X}
and
\mathcal{Z}
basis measurements. Clearly, for MUBs,
r(Z,X)=r(X,Z)=\log(d)
as
|\braket{x_{j}|z_{k}}|^{2}=\frac{1}{d}
for MUBs, thus we get equation (
9
). From this inequality (
9
), if we make a measurement on the B part, we get the following:

\displaystyle I(Z^{A}:Z^{B})+I(X^{A}:X^{B})\leq\log(d)-H(A|B),

(10)

because of the data processing inequality
[
19
]
which ensures
I(Z^{A}:Z^{B})\leq I(Z^{A}:B),I(X^{A}:X^{B})\leq I(X^{A}:B)
. On the other hand, the CQC conjecture
[
8
]
asks whether the following holds:

\displaystyle I(Z^{A}:Z^{B})+I(X^{A}:X^{B})\leq I(A:B).

(11)

Looking at equations (
11
) and (
9
), we notice that, if the reduction of mutual information in
I(Z^{A}:Z^{B})
from
I(Z^{A}:B)
and
I(X^{A}:X^{B})
from
I(X^{A}:B)
combined is at least the difference between the terms
\log(d)-H(A|B)
and
I(A:B)
, then CQC conjecture will hold. Mathematically, subtracting (
11
) from (
9
) and remembering the definition of mutual information, we arrive at equation (
8
), establishing proposition (
3
). Note that
\log(d)-H(A|B)\geq I(A:B)
always holds as
H(A)\leq\log(d)
.
∎

As a demonstration of this sufficient condition, in figure (
1
), we consider a number of random density operators where each subsystem has dimension
d=2
, which respect condition in (
8
), and are not pure, entangled, or has a maximally mixed subsystem, which are already known not to contradict CQC. So this sufficient condition adds a new class of states that are good for this conjecture.

Figure 1
:
Simulating CQC conjecture with random separable mixed states that do not have a maximally mixed subsystem, but that respect equation (
8
). This figure shows that the differences shown in the
Y
axis is always non-negative.

In this figure, we see that the
Y
axis, which represents the difference between original mutual information before the measurement and the sum of mutual information resulting from two different MUB measurements, is never negative, signifying the validity of the CQC conjecture.

With this result in mind, we continue and find that there is a sufficient condition for the ECQC conjecture too, which ensures its validity for the cases when
A
and
B
both has the same prime dimension. We formally state this condition in the form of the following proposition.

4.2
A sufficient condition for ECQC

Proposition 4
.

For any bipartite state
\rho_{AB}
where the subsystems
A
and
B
has prime dimension
d
, considering the set of MUBs
\{M_{i}\}_{i=1}^{d+1}
for dimension
d
, if

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{d+1}I(M_{i}^{A}:B)-\sum_{i=1}^{d+1}I(M_{i}^{A}:M_{i}^{B})\geq\sum_{i=1}^{d+1}H(M_{i})-\frac{d+1}{2}\log(d)-\frac{d+1}{2}H(A|B)-I(A:B),

then ECQC holds.

Proof.

We follow the same strategy that gave us the sufficient condition for CQC, but for higher dimensional subsystems. That is, we start with the generalization of the Berta’s uncertainty relation
[
7
]
for prime dimensions which Xie et al. obtained in
[
20
]
. They showed the following lower bound on the conditional entropy given a quantum register, which we later use to arrive at a desired upper bound for the total mutual information when only one party measures in the MUBs. We state the inequality in a slightly different notation than used in the original work, which takes a special form for MUBs:

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{d+1}H(M_{i}^{A}|B)\geq\frac{d+1}{2}\log d+\frac{d+1}{2}H(A|B).

(12)

From this inequality (
12
), we have that:

\displaystyle\implies\sum_{i=1}^{d+1}H(M_{i})-\sum_{i=1}^{d+1}I(M_{i}^{A}:B)\geq\frac{d+1}{2}\log d+\frac{d+1}{2}H(A|B)

\displaystyle\implies\sum_{i=1}^{d+1}I(M_{i}^{A}:B)\leq\sum_{i=1}^{d+1}H(M_{i})-\frac{d+1}{2}\log d-\frac{d+1}{2}H(A|B).

(13)

Note that in the same article by Xie et al., they also present an optimized version of this inequality (
12
), where an additional term is added on the right hand side, and has the following form:

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{d+1}H(M_{i}^{A}|B)\geq\frac{d+1}{2}\log d+\frac{d+1}{2}H(A|B)+\max\{0,\delta_{m}\}.

(14)

for
\delta_{m}=\frac{d+1}{2}I(A:B)-\sum_{i=1}^{d+1}I(M_{i}^{A}:B)
. However, this optimized version is not applicable to our case as the two terms of
\delta_{m}
has different multipliers. If the first term of
\delta_{m}
, without the multiplier
(d+1)/2
is already larger than the second term, then both CQC and ECQC holds trivially, and with the multiplier, this form is not compatible to our scenario as we have a single
I(A:B)
term, and we exclude it from our analysis.

Now, we mention our conjecture mentioned in equation (
6
) for the clarity of derivation:

\displaystyle I(A:B)\geq\underset{\mathcal{S}\in[d+1]^{d}}{\min}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{S}}I(M^{A}_{i}:M^{B}_{i}).

(15)

Note that, because our conjecture considers
d
measurements out of
d+1
, and excludes the maximum mutual information obtained by one of the
d+1
MUB measurements, adding that quantity will give us the total
d+1
mutual information, and if one party is not measured, then the mutual information could only be higher because of the data processing inequality. So from the right hand side of equation (
15
) we have that:

\displaystyle\underset{\mathcal{S}\in[d+1]^{d}}{\min}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{S}}I(M^{A}_{i}:M^{B}_{i})\leq\sum_{i=1}^{d+1}I(M_{i}^{A}:M_{i}^{B})\leq\sum_{i=1}^{d+1}I(M_{i}^{A}:B).

(16)

We consider inequalities (
13
), (
15
), and (
16
) and notice that if the reduction of total mutual information from
\sum_{i=1}^{d+1}I(M_{i}^{A}:B)
to
\sum_{i=1}^{d+1}I(M_{i}^{A}:M_{i}^{B})
is at least the difference between the right side of (
13
) and
I(A:B)
, the we would arrive at proposition (
4
).
∎

4.3
Attempt to establish ECQC directly for isotropic states

In the original paper, Schneeloch et al.
[
8
]
showed that CQC conjecture holds for pure states, states with one maximally mixed subsystem and if one of the measurements is minimally disturbing for one of the parties. The validity of the CQC conjecture for the later two types of states were shown by relating them to Berta et al.’s EU relation mentioned in equation (
2
).
With some algebra, the authors reformulate the CQC conjecture to arrive at:

\displaystyle H(Z^{A}|Z^{B})+H(X^{A}|X^{B})

\displaystyle\geq\log(d)+H(A|B)+\underbrace{[H(Z^{A})+H(X^{A})-\log(d)-H(A)]}_{\kappa_{1}}.

(17)

In equation (
17
), we denote the terms inside the parentheses ‘[]’ by
\kappa_{1}
. The authors point out that if
\kappa_{1}=0
, then the CQC conjecture holds as we can measure the second subsystem in Berta et al.’s EU relation involving quantum memories to arrive at (
17
). Here, we recall Massen-Uffink uncertainty relation
[
12
,
1
]
:

\displaystyle H(Z^{A})+H(X^{A})\geq\log(d)+H(A).

It is easy to see that states makes this Maassen-Uffink EU relation an equality, namely states which are equal to the eigenvectors of those two measurement bases, also satisfies the CQC conjecture as that would make
\kappa_{1}=0
.
In the following, we show that this strategy is not applicable to ECQC. That is, even though we can arrive at a similar inequality as (
17
), ECQC’s validity can not be established for similar states. We start by assuming the validity of the ECQC conjecture:

\displaystyle\underset{\mathcal{S}\in[d+1]^{d}}{\min}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{S}}I(M^{A}_{i}:M^{B}_{i})\leq I(A:B)

\displaystyle\implies\sum_{i=1}^{d+1}I(M^{A}_{i}:M^{B}_{i})=I(A:B)+I_{max}(M^{A}:M^{B})

\displaystyle\implies\sum_{i=1}^{d+1}H(M_{i}^{A})-\sum_{i=1}^{d+1}H(M_{i}^{A}|M_{i}^{B})\leq I(A:B)+I_{max}(M^{A}:M^{B})

\displaystyle\implies\sum_{i=1}^{d+1}H(M_{i}^{A}|M_{i}^{B})\geq\sum_{i=1}^{d+1}H(M_{i}^{A})-I(A:B)-I_{max}(M^{A}:M^{B}).

(18)

In the second line of the derivation, we add
I_{max}(M^{A}:M^{B})
to both sides, which in our notation is the maximum mutual information obtained by any MUB measurement basis. Now we restate Xie et al.’s result
[
20
]
that generalizes Berta et al.’s EU relation involving conditional entropy with quantum memories (
2
):

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{d+1}H(M_{i}^{A}|B)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{d+1}{2}\log d+\frac{d+1}{2}H(A|B).

(19)

We add and subtract the right hand side of equation (
19
) in the right hand side of equation (
18
) to arrive at:

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{d+1}H(M_{i}^{A}|M_{i}^{B})\geq\frac{d+1}{2}\log d+\frac{d+1}{2}H(A|B)

\displaystyle\underbrace{\left[-\frac{d+1}{2}\log d-\frac{d+1}{2}H(A|B)+\sum_{i=1}^{d+1}H(M_{i}^{A})-I(A:B)-I_{max}(M^{A}:M^{B}).\right]}_{\kappa_{2}}

(20)

We comment that the structural similarity between equations (
17
) and (
20
) is noticeable. In equation (
20
), we denote the terms inside the parentheses ‘[]’ by
\kappa_{2}
. If
\kappa_{2}=0
, that is:

\displaystyle\frac{d+1}{2}\log d+\frac{d+1}{2}H(A|B)+I(A:B)+I_{max}(M^{A}:M^{B})=\sum_{i=1}^{d+1}H(M_{i}^{A}),

(21)

then ECQC holds based on Xie et al.’s result (
19
). It is easy to see that that states of the form
\rho_{AB}=\rho_{A}\otimes\rho_{B}
where
\rho_{A}=\frac{\mathbb{I}}{d}
satisfies equation (
21
) because for these product states, the left and right hand side becomes
\frac{d+1}{2}\log_{2}(d)+\frac{d+1}{2}\log_{2}(d)+0+0=(d+1)\log_{2}(d)
. Note that the maximally entangled state, which of course has maximally mixed subsystems, does not respect equation (
21
), as in that case we get,
H(A|B)=-\log(d),I(A:B)=2\log(d),I_{max}(M^{A}:M^{B})=H(M^{A})+H(M^{B})-H(M^{A}M^{B})=\log(d)
, resulting in
3\log(d)\neq(d+1)\log(d)
, for prime
d\geq 3
. So we can not decide whether ECQC holds for maximally entangled states based on equation (
20
) being true, unlike the CQC conjecture, where the equality condition of a similar equation sufficed. With this method’s insufficiency, let us calculate the validity of ECQC for Isotropic states directly, which are maximally entangled for a certain parameter range.

Isotropic states

In this section, we show the validity of ECQC for isotropic states with prime dimensions by an explicit calculation. Isotropic states are defined as
[
21
,
22
]

\displaystyle\rho_{AB}=p\times\ket{\Psi^{+}}\bra{\Psi^{+}}_{AB}+(1-p)\times\frac{\mathbb{I}_{AB}}{d^{2}},

(22)

where
\ket{\Psi^{+}}_{AB}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}\sum_{i=0}^{d-1}\ket{ii}_{AB}
,
\frac{-1}{d^{2}-1}\leq p\leq 1
,
d\geq 3
is a prime. For any
p<1
in the definition of isotropic states in equation (
22
), we see that for equation (
21
), the
l.h.s<r.h.s
, which results in
\kappa_{2}>0
. Consequently, we can not recover Xie et al.’s
[
20
]
result mentioned in equation (
19
) conclusively in equation (
20
). If it is possible to tighten Xie et al.’s result further, in that case this could be a feasible way to show the validity of ECQC conjecture in this case. So we must perform a direct calculation for isotropic states to see if the ECQC conjecture holds.

In order to calculate the mutual information in this state, we have to identify the eigenvalues of
\rho_{AB}
as well as of the subsystems
A
and
B
. Below, we denote the vector of eigenvalues of
\rho_{AB}
by
\text{eig}(\rho_{AB})
.

\displaystyle\text{eig}(\rho_{AB})=p\times\text{eig}\left(\ket{\Psi^{+}}\bra{\Psi^{+}}_{AB}\right)+(1-p)\times\text{eig}\left(\frac{\mathbb{I}_{AB}}{d^{2}}\right).

We know the eigenvalues of the maximally entangled state
\ket{\Psi^{+}}\bra{\Psi^{+}}_{AB}
and the completely mixed state
\frac{\mathbb{I}_{AB}}{d^{2}}
. From this, we easily see that the
d^{2}
number of eigenvalues of
\rho_{AB}
are
\text{eig}(\rho_{AB})=\{p+\frac{1-p}{d^{2}},\frac{1-p}{d^{2}},\frac{1-p}{d^{2}},...,\frac{1-p}{d^{2}}\}
. So we get the following joint entropy of
\rho_{AB}
where both subsystems
A
and
B
have dimension
d
:

\displaystyle H(\rho_{AB})=-\left(p+\frac{1-p}{d^{2}}\right)\log\left(p+\frac{1-p}{d^{2}}\right)-\sum_{i=1}^{d^{2}-1}\frac{1-p}{d^{2}}\log\frac{1-p}{d^{2}}.

The subsystems of isotropic states
A
and
B
also has known eigenvalues, as they are simply the maximally mixed states with eigenvalues
\frac{1}{d}
as long as
\frac{1}{d+1}<p\leq 1
, in which range, isotropic states are entangled
[
23
,
24
]
. Even in the range of
p
where the state is separable, (that is
-\frac{1}{d^{2}-1}\leq p\leq\frac{1}{d+1}
), the partial traces
A
and
B
are still maximally mixed, which can be seen from direct calculation.

\displaystyle\Tr_{A}(\rho_{AB})

\displaystyle=\Tr_{A}\left(p\times\ket{\Psi^{+}}\bra{\Psi^{+}}_{AB}+(1-p)\times\frac{\mathbb{I}_{AB}}{d^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{p}{d}+\frac{1-p}{d}\right)\mathbb{I}_{B}=\frac{\mathbb{I}_{B}}{d},

and we can see that
\Tr_{B}(\rho_{AB})=\frac{\mathbb{I}_{A}}{d}
in a similar way. So in these states,

\displaystyle\text{eig}(A)=\text{eig}(B)=\left(\frac{1}{d},\frac{1}{d},...,\frac{1}{d}\right),

for all
-\frac{1}{d^{2}-1}\leq p\leq 1
. From these calculations, we see that
H(A)=H(B)=\log(d)
, and the mutual information for isotropic states for any prime dimensions is:

\displaystyle I(A:B)

\displaystyle=H(A)+H(B)-H(\rho_{AB})

\displaystyle=2\log(d)-\left(-\left(p+\frac{1-p}{d^{2}}\right)\log\left(p+\frac{1-p}{d^{2}}\right)-\sum_{i=1}^{d^{2}-1}\frac{1-p}{d^{2}}\log\frac{1-p}{d^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=2\log(d)+\frac{p(d^{2}-1)+1}{d^{2}}\log\left(\frac{p(d^{2}-1)+1}{d^{2}}\right)+\sum_{i=1}^{d^{2}-1}\frac{1-p}{d^{2}}\log\frac{1-p}{d^{2}}.

(23)

Now we calculate the mutual information in the computational
\mathcal{Z}
basis measurement, which is part of the MUBs. Because isotropic states has maximally mixed subsystems, the entropy of these subsystems after the measurement is still maximum. That is,
H(M_{i}^{A})=H(M_{i}^{B})
for any MUB measurement
M_{i}
. However, the mutual information of the post-measurement state does change and we calculate that below.

\displaystyle=\frac{p}{3}\bra{g_{0}g_{0}}(\ket{00}\bra{00}+\ket{00}\bra{11}+\ket{00}\bra{22}+\ket{11}\bra{00}+\ket{11}\bra{11}+\ket{11}\bra{22}+...

\displaystyle\ket{22}\bra{00}+\ket{22}\bra{11}+\ket{22}\bra{22})\ket{g_{0}g_{0}}+(1-p)\bra{g_{0}g_{0}}\frac{\mathbb{I}_{AB}}{9}\ket{g_{0}g_{0}}

(30)

Focusing on one particular term
\bra{g_{0}g_{0}}\ket{00}\bra{00}\ket{g_{0}g_{0}}
, we see that:

\displaystyle\bra{g_{0}g_{0}}\ket{00}\bra{00}\ket{g_{0}g_{0}}=\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}(1,\omega^{2},\omega^{2})\times(1,0,0)^{T}\right)^{4}=\frac{1}{9},

where ‘T’ denotes transpose of that vector. In the case of
\bra{g_{0}g_{0}}\ket{11}\bra{11}\ket{g_{0}g_{0}}
:

\displaystyle\bra{g_{0}g_{0}}\ket{11}\bra{11}\ket{g_{0}g_{0}}=\left|\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}(1,\omega^{2},\omega^{2})\times(0,1,0)^{T}\right|^{4}=\left|\frac{\omega^{2}}{\sqrt{3}}\right|^{4}=\frac{1}{9}.

Similarly we can see that,
\bra{g_{0}g_{0}}\ket{22}\bra{22}\ket{g_{0}g_{0}}=\frac{1}{9}
. These are the cases when the inner products are of the form
\bra{g_{0}g_{0}}\ket{ii}\bra{ii}\ket{g_{0}g_{0}}
, for
i\in\{0,1,2\}
. In the cases when
i\neq j
, in the equation (
30
), we have both
\bra{g_{0}g_{0}}\ket{ii}\bra{jj}\ket{g_{0}g_{0}}
and
\bra{g_{0}g_{0}}\ket{jj}\bra{ii}\ket{g_{0}g_{0}}
for
i,j\in\{0,1,2\}
. That is, we have both the inner product and its conjugate in the sum. By elementary properties of complex inner products, from these pairs of terms, the complex parts will vanish and we will be left with two equal real parts. The full calculation of
p_{g_{0}g_{0}}
then goes as the following:

\displaystyle p_{g_{0}g_{0}}=

\displaystyle\frac{p}{3}\sum_{i,j}\bra{g_{0}g_{0}}\ket{ii}\bra{jj}\ket{g_{0}g_{0}}+(1-p)\bra{g_{0}g_{0}}\frac{\mathbb{I}_{AB}}{9}\ket{g_{0}g_{0}}

\displaystyle=\frac{p}{3}\left(\sum_{i}\bra{g_{0}g_{0}}\ket{ii}\bra{ii}\ket{g_{0}g_{0}}+\sum_{i\neq j}\bra{g_{0}g_{0}}\ket{ii}\bra{jj}\ket{g_{0}g_{0}}\right)+\frac{(1-p)}{9}\times 1

\displaystyle=\frac{p}{3}(\frac{3}{9}+\braket{g_{0}|0}^{2}\braket{1|g_{0}}^{2}+\braket{g_{0}|0}^{2}\braket{2|g_{0}}^{2}+\braket{g_{0}|1}^{2}\braket{0|g_{0}}^{2}

\displaystyle\hskip 22.76219pt+\braket{g_{0}|1}^{2}\braket{2|g_{0}}^{2}+\braket{g_{0}|2}^{2}\braket{0|g_{0}}^{2}+\braket{g_{0}|2}^{2}\braket{1|g_{0}}^{2})+\frac{(1-p)}{9}

\displaystyle=\frac{p}{3}\left(\frac{3}{9}+\frac{\braket{1|g_{0}}^{2}}{3}+\frac{\braket{2|g_{0}}^{2}}{3}+\frac{\braket{g_{0}|1}^{2}}{3}+\braket{g_{0}|1}^{2}\braket{2|g_{0}}^{2}+\frac{\braket{g_{0}|2}^{2}}{3}+\braket{g_{0}|2}^{2}\braket{1|g_{0}}^{2}\right)+\frac{(1-p)}{9}

\displaystyle=\frac{p}{3}\left(\frac{1}{3}+\frac{\braket{1|g_{0}}^{2}+\braket{g_{0}|1}^{2}}{3}+\frac{\braket{2|g_{0}}^{2}+\braket{g_{0}|2}^{2}}{3}+\braket{g_{0}|1}^{2}\braket{2|g_{0}}^{2}+\braket{g_{0}|2}^{2}\braket{1|g_{0}}^{2}\right)+\frac{(1-p)}{9}

\displaystyle=\frac{p}{3}\left(\frac{1}{3}-\frac{1}{9}-\frac{1}{9}+\frac{1}{9}+\frac{1}{9}\right)+\frac{(1-p)}{9}

\displaystyle=\frac{p}{3}\times\frac{1}{3}+\frac{(1-p)}{9}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{9},

remembering that
1+\omega+\omega^{2}=0
and
\omega^{3}=1
.

\displaystyle 2\log(3)+\frac{8p+1}{9}\log\left(\frac{8p+1}{9}\right)+\sum_{i=1}^{8}\frac{1-p}{9}\log\left(\frac{1-p}{9}\right)

4.3.2
Mutual information in isotropic states for arbitrary prime dimension

Generalizing the calculations for isotropic states with
3
-dimensional subsystems to isotropic states with prime dimensional subsystems, we can arrive at the following proposition, which we state without a proof.

Proposition 5
.

For isotropic states with subsystems of prime dimension
d
, except for two mutually unbiased basis measurements
\mathcal{Z}
and
\mathcal{X}
, any other measurement basis
M_{i}
will produce zero mutual information, that is,
I(M_{i}^{A}:M_{i}^{B})=0
, and for
\mathcal{Z}
and
\mathcal{X}
:

\displaystyle I(Z^{A}:Z^{B})=I(X^{A}:X^{B})=2\log(d)+d\left(\frac{p}{d}+\frac{1-p}{d^{2}}\right)\log\left(\frac{p}{d}+\frac{1-p}{d^{2}}\right)+d^{2}\times\frac{1-p}{d^{2}}\log\left(\frac{1-p}{d^{2}}\right).

Proof.

We can follow a similar direct calculation strategy that we have followed for dimension
3
. It is easy to see that the arguments for this particular dimension
d=3
is still valid for arbitrary prime
d
and the proposition (
5
) follows.
∎

We have already mentioned the mutual information for prime dimensional subsystems in equation (
23
). Together with proposition (
5
), we can write the following inequality whose validity will imply the validity of ECQC in isotropic states with arbitrary prime dimensional subsystems:

\displaystyle I(A:B)\geq\sum_{i=1}^{d+1}I(M^{A}_{i}:M^{B}_{i})

\displaystyle\implies 2\log(d)+\frac{p(d^{2}-1)+1}{d^{2}}\log\left(\frac{p(d^{2}-1)+1}{d^{2}}\right)+\sum_{i=1}^{d^{2}-1}\frac{1-p}{d^{2}}\log\frac{1-p}{d^{2}}

\displaystyle\geq 2\times\left(2\log(d)+d\left(\frac{p}{d}+\frac{1-p}{d^{2}}\right)\log\left(\frac{p}{d}+\frac{1-p}{d^{2}}\right)+d^{2}\times\frac{1-p}{d^{2}}\log\left(\frac{1-p}{d^{2}}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\geq\underset{\mathcal{S}\in[d+1]^{d}}{\min}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{S}}I(M^{A}_{i}:M^{B}_{i}).

(32)

5
Closing Remarks and Future Works

In this work, we have taken a deeper look at the CQC conjecture and identified a potentially useful sufficient condition. We have also proposed an extended version of this conjecture that takes into account high-dimensional states and more measurement bases. There are a few interesting works that could be done in the future. For example, the original CQC conjecture holds for all pure states, but the technique that was used to prove this turned out to be inapplicable for the ECQC conjecture. However, as our simulations for dimension
3
and
5
shows, there are no observed contradiction of the ECQC for pure states. So it would be interesting to develop a method to prove ECQC for pure states analytically, similar to the case of the original CQC conjecture. It would also be interesting to formulate a conjecture which holds for both prime and composite dimensions. The difficulty in such formulation is, the number of MUBs in composite dimension is not straightforward to calculate and using available MUBs sometimes lead to counterexamples of ECQC. So, for composite dimensions, we may need an entirely new formulation of CQC conjecture, that holds for higher dimensions and more than two measurement bases.

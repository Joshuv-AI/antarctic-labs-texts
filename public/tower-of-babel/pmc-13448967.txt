Evaluation of the structural degradation of polypropylene in the heat-affected zone induced by natural ageing using TGA/DTG and FTIR analysis
Authors: Noor Hatem Nasser, Ali I. Al-Mosawi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
An investigation of the structural changes occurring in the heat-affected zone (HAZ) of polypropylene used to manufacture orthopaedic limb supports after exposure to burning is the primary objective of the current study, which also examines how this zone is affected by the natural aging of the polymer. TGA and DTG analyses further confirmed that natural aging significantly reduces the thermal stability of polypropylene, as evidenced by a clear shift of the onset decomposition temperature toward lower values and an increase in the maximum degradation rate in aged samples. These effects were more pronounced within the heat-affected zone (HAZ) after burning, indicating that prior aging accelerates thermal decomposition and intensifies heat-induced damage. Also, it is evident from FTIR spectra that the 2023 sample exhibited high levels of oxidative degradation due to surface aging during storage, as opposed to the 2025 sample, which demonstrated low levels of oxidative degradation due to normal storage conditions. FTIR spectra indicated that the hydroxyl and carbonyl groups had increased in intensity and the hydrocarbon bonds had decreased relative to them, indicating that polymer chains had been scissiled and polar groups had been formed. According to the analyses performed on the horizontal burning samples (UL-94 HB), the effects of fire were more noticeable in older samples, since initial aging increased the decomposition process within the heat-affected zone, which resulted in a double appearance of new oxidized groups and a greater loss of crystallographic structure regularity when compared with samples manufactured more recently. Furthermore, structural changes within the burned area were noted, including a loss of crystallinity and an increase in amorphous phases, as reflected by the decline in crystalline structure peaks. As a result of these findings, it is evident that the effects of heat on polymers are not independent of their history, but are intensified when there has been previous degradation brought on by storage or prolonged exposure to adverse environmental conditions. Also, the results of optical inspection and surface roughness analysis showed that sample 2023 recorded higher values for surface roughness, indicating local morphological changes due to thermal exposure.

Introduction

The heat-affected zone (HAZ) in all materials, including polymers, is the part of the material that is exposed to high temperatures during combustion without reaching the stage of complete melting or charring. In this area, a series of physical and chemical changes occur that distinguish it from both the intact material and the directly burned area.1–3 When a polymer is exposed to fire, the effect of heat gradually spreads, causing the polymer chains to break down and oxygen groups to appear as a result of thermal oxidation, along with changes in crystallinity, colour, and thermal stability, and all these changes are important parameters of the degree of degradation damage to which the material is subjected.4–6 Thermal stability and resistance to heat-induced degradation are most reliably evaluated using thermogravimetric analysis (TGA), which provides quantitative information on mass loss behavior, degradation onset temperature, and decomposition kinetics under controlled heating conditions.7–9 The study of this region is increasingly important in polymers used in engineering and medical applications, such as polypropylene sheets manufactured for orthotic supports, as the thermal properties of the material determine its safety, flame resistance, and operational life.10–13 By analyzing the heat-affected zone using FTIR, Raman spectroscopy, XRD, and EDX techniques, it is possible to determine the nature of structural and chemical changes and assess the extent of property loss with increasing temperature. In addition to these techniques, TGA plays a critical role in linking chemical degradation to actual thermal performance by quantifying the severity of thermal decomposition within the HAZ. A key factor in controlling the formation of the HAZ and its burning behavior is the effect of natural aging. After being exposed to room temperature storage conditions for a prolonged period of time, the polymer chain gradually shortens and oxidized groups such as carbonyl and hydroxyl are generated. As a result of the change in crystallinity, the sample's thermal stability is significantly reduced when compared to the new material.6,14–18 This reduction in stability is reflected in TGA measurements by a shift toward lower onset degradation temperatures and an increase in mass loss rate. Due to this, the aged sample tends to form a deeper and more brittle HAZ when exposed to flame, since the molecular bonds become weaker and more susceptible to thermal decomposition, which may result in an increase in fire spread or the formation of more charred parts.1,3,6 This reduction in stability is reflected in TGA measurements by a shift toward lower onset degradation temperatures and an increase in mass loss rate. Due to this, the aged sample tends to form a deeper and more brittle HAZ when exposed to flame, since the molecular bonds become weaker and more susceptible to thermal decomposition, which may result in an increase in fire spread or the formation of more charred parts. The parameters like TGA, FTIR, and XRD provide direct evidence of the influence of aging on the thermal stability and flame-induced damage of the polymer. Lower decomposition temperatures observed in TGA, together with increased intensity of oxidized group peaks in FTIR and possible disruption of crystal structure in XRD patterns, collectively confirm the increased vulnerability of aged materials within the HAZ.1,3,6,19–21 Thus, it is essential to examine the heat-affected zone while considering the effects of aging in order to gain a deeper understanding of the polymer's thermal behavior as well as determine whether the polymer is appropriate for long-term storage, as well as determine its ability to withstand emergency combustion conditions,22–26 which is extremely important in medical applications that require the highest levels of thermal stability and safety. Polypropylene sheets used in orthotic limb braces may remain in storage for extended periods before being used in clinical applications. During this period, natural ageing can gradually modify the chemical structure, oxidation state, and thermal stability of the material. When subsequently exposed to thermal events, the resulting heat-affected zone (HAZ) may undergo significant structural and chemical changes, even in regions that appear visually intact. Understanding the combined effects of long-term ageing and thermal exposure is therefore important for assessing material durability, service reliability, and the potential recyclability of polypropylene after thermal damage.

The novelty of this work lies in investigating the combined influence of long-term natural ageing and combustion-induced thermal exposure on the HAZ of polypropylene used in orthotic applications. Particular emphasis is placed on identifying structural, chemical, and thermal degradation occurring beyond the visibly damaged region and evaluating its implications for material integrity, reliability, and potential reuse.

Methodology

Materials

PP sheets with different production years (2023 and 2025) and supplied by SIMONA AG, have been used in this study. This polymer grade is commonly employed in orthotic limb braces fabrication. The 2023 PP sample was stored for approximately two years in industrial warehouse facilities under normal storage conditions prior to testing. The material was not subjected to refrigeration, air conditioning, humidity control, or accelerated ageing treatments. Consequently, the observed changes are associated with natural ageing during long-term storage under typical warehouse environmental conditions.

Sample preparation

Standard horizontal UL-94 HB horizontal burning test samples were prepared with dimensions of 125 mm in length, 13 mm in width, and 4 mm in thickness. After completion of the burning test, the samples were carefully cleaned to remove charred residues. The HAZ of each sample was then precisely cut using a polymer cutter for further structural and morphological analyses.

Horizontal burning test (UL-94 HB)

The burning behavior of PP was evaluated using the horizontal UL-94 HB test according to UL 94 standard31 and under controlled laboratory conditions at the Department of Materials Engineering, University of Kufa. Fig. 1 illustrates the PP samples after the UL-94 HB test, clearly showing the formation and extent of the HAZ.

HAZ in (a) 2025 and (b) 2023 PP samples after UL-94 HB test.

Thermogravimetric/derivative thermogravimetry analysis (TGA/DTG)

According to the ISO 11358-1:2022,32 PerkinElmer Thermogravimetric Analyzer, model Pyris 1TGA at Material Characterization Center MCC/Wasit was conducted to evaluate the thermal stability and decomposition scenarios of polypropylene and its compounds. The samples weighing 15 mg were dynamically heated from 50 °C to 600 °C at a linear and constant heating rate of 10 °C min−1. All measurements were performed in an inert atmosphere of nitrogen gas, with a steady flow rate of 20 ml min−1.

FTIR analysis

Fourier Transform Infrared (FTIR) spectroscopy was performed in accordance with ISO 10640:2011 (ref. 33) to investigate chemical and structural changes in the polymer chains induced by thermal exposure and aging. Measurements were carried out using a JASCO FT/IR-6X spectrometer at AlKhora Company, Baghdad.

SEM/EDS analysis

Surface morphology and elemental composition of the samples were examined using Scanning Electron Microscopy coupled with Energy Dispersive X-ray Spectroscopy (SEM-EDS/EDX). The analyses were conducted at AlKhora Company, Baghdad, employing an Axia ChemiSEM (Thermo Scientific) system.

Analyses of optical microscopy

To observe the microstructural characteristics of polypropylene samples, a NMM-800RF optical microscope located at Department of Materials Engineering/University of Kufa was utilized with a magnification of 50×.

Roughness measurements

The surface morphology and roughness of the heat-affected zone were characterized using a FlexAFM atomic force microscope (AFM) found in College of Science, University of Baghdad. The roughness parameters (Sa, Sq, Ssk, Sku, Sp, Sv, and Sz) were obtained directly from the AFM topographical maps generated by the instrument software.

Replicates and data reporting

The UL-94 HB horizontal burning test was conducted following the procedure described in our previous work,6 where three replicate specimens were tested for each condition and the average burning results were reported. For all other characterization techniques (TGA/DTG, FTIR, SEM/EDS, optical microscopy, and AFM), one representative specimen was analyzed for each condition, as these techniques were intended to provide qualitative and comparative characterization of the materials.

Results and discussion

TGA/DTG analysis

Fig. 2 represents TGA/DTG results of polypropylene before burning. The TGA curves in Fig. 2a show a clear difference in thermal behaviour between the polypropylene sample manufactured in 2023 and subjected to ageing (black curve) and the sample manufactured in 2025 (red curve), reflecting the effects of thermal and temporal ageing on the material's molecular structure. In the initial stage and up to temperatures of approximately 250–270 °C, both samples show almost complete weight stability, indicating the absence of moisture or volatile substances and suggesting that both samples are similar in terms of purity and the absence of low molecular weight additives that are susceptible to early evaporation. As the temperature increases, the aged sample 2023 loses weight at a relatively lower temperature than sample 2025, with a sharp decrease in weight observed starting at around 280 °C to 300 °C, indicating a decline in thermal stability due to ageing. This is attributed to chain scission during service or storage, as well as the consumption or degradation of stabilising additives such as antioxidants. This structural deterioration leads to the formation of shorter chains that are more susceptible to thermal decomposition, accelerating weight loss and making the thermal decomposition process more intense and narrower in scope, as evidenced by the sharp decline in the black curve. In contrast, the sample 2025 shows the onset of decomposition at higher temperatures, with a more gradual weight loss curve extending over a wider temperature range, reflecting a more regular polymer structure and higher molecular weight, as well as better effectiveness of stabilising additives that delay the onset of thermal decomposition and distribute the decomposition process over a wider temperature range. Furthermore, the continued weight loss in the 2025 sample at temperatures above 400 °C compared to the 2023 sample indicates a better ability to resist thermal decomposition. This is a direct indication of the material's novelty and lack of previous thermal or oxidative stress.34–37

TGA (a), and DTG (b) behaviour of PP produced in 2023 and 2025 before burning.

The first derivative of weight loss (DTG) plot in Fig. 2b shows a fundamental difference in the mechanism and rate of thermal decomposition between the polypropylene sample manufactured in 2023 and exposed to ageing and the sample manufactured in 2025. In case of 2023 PP sample, a sharp and deep peak with a large negative value can be observed at a relatively lower temperature, indicating that thermal decomposition occurs at a high rate and within a narrow temperature range, which is typical behaviour for a material that has been aged and has lost a significant part of its structural stability. This behaviour is attributed to the shortening of polymer chains as a result of prior thermal or oxidative exposure, in addition to the consumption or degradation of antioxidants, leading to the simultaneous and rapid disintegration of a large number of chains upon reaching the decomposition temperature, which explains the sharp decline and intensity of the peak in the DTG curve. In contrast, 2025 PP sample shows a more gradual and less sharp DTG curve, with a wider peak extending over a wider temperature range and with a lower negative value, indicating that the thermal decomposition process occurs gradually and in stages, a behaviour that reflects a better distribution of molecular weights and a more regular and stable structure.

The delay in the main peak position in the 2025 PP sample to higher temperatures indicates greater effectiveness of thermal stabilisers and antioxidants, which slow down the formation of free radicals and delay the breakdown of polymer chains. The appearance of multiple steps or slight secondary bends in the 2025 curve may indicate sequential decomposition of different sections of the chain or the effect of additives, in contrast to the quasi-monotonic and impulsive decomposition seen in the aged sample. Overall, the DTG plot confirms that ageing not only lowers the onset temperature of decomposition, as seen in the TGA plot, but also changes the kinetics of decomposition itself, transforming the thermal behaviour of polypropylene from a gradual and controlled decomposition in the fresh material to a rapid and violent decomposition in the aged material, this reflects a clear deterioration in the thermal stability and structural properties of the polymer.37–39

Fig. 3 represents TGA/DTG curves of heat affected zone for polypropylene after UL-94 HB. The TGA analysis in Fig. 3a shows clearly different thermal behaviour between the polypropylene sample manufactured in 2023 and subjected to ageing, and the sample manufactured in 2025, in the area affected by heat after the horizontal combustion test. In the early stages of heating, up to temperatures of approximately 250 °C, both samples maintain almost complete weight stability, as there is no significant loss of volatiles or moisture, and fundamental structural changes have not yet begun within this thermal range. As the temperature rises beyond this limit, a real difference in thermal behaviour begins to emerge. The aged sample 2023 shows an earlier onset of weight loss compared to sample 2025, with thermal decomposition starting at a lower temperature and at a faster rate. This behaviour indicates that the heat-affected zone in the older sample has previously undergone structural degradation due to thermal and temporal ageing, in addition to the effect of combustion, resulting in shortened polymer chains and increased structural defects such as weak bonds and free ends, this makes the material less thermally stable and easier to break down when heated. While, the 2025 sample shows a clear shift in the decomposition curve towards higher temperatures, with a more gradual weight loss and a relatively delayed onset of decomposition. This behaviour reflects the material's retention of a more cohesive polymer structure and indicates that the heat-affected zone still contains longer chains and a lower degree of oxidative degradation, and possibly a higher effectiveness of heat-stabilising additives compared to sample 2023. The wider thermal extension of the decomposition zone in this sample also indicates a more complex and less abrupt decomposition mechanism, which is a positive indicator of higher thermal resistance after exposure to combustion. Towards the end of thermal decomposition, sample 2023 reaches almost complete weight loss at lower temperatures compared to sample 2025, confirming that premature ageing and combustion have accelerated the breakdown of the polymer network and weakened the thermal stability of the heat-affected zone. Meanwhile, the continued weight loss of sample 2025 at higher temperatures indicates that parts of the polymer chains remain intact for longer before complete disintegration. Overall, these results reflect that the effect of combustion in the heat-affected zone is more severe in aged polypropylene, where cumulative degradation lowers the onset of decomposition and increases the rate of weight loss, while newer polypropylene exhibits better thermal resistance and more stable decomposition behaviour. This discussion confirms that TGA is an effective tool for evaluating the combined effect of ageing and combustion on the thermal structure of polypropylene, especially in critical areas close to the flame source.6,40,41

TGA (a), and DTG (b) analysis of the HAZ after UL-94 HB testing of polypropylene produced in 2023 and 2025.

The DTG plot (Fig. 3b) is more sensitive than the TGA in showing when decomposition actually begins and where the highest rate of mass loss occurs, so it illustrates the differences between the HAZ regions of the 2023 sample exposed to ageing and the 2025 sample after horizontal combustion more clearly. From the curves, we first note that the signal remains very close to zero up to approximately 240–260 °C for both samples. This means that the rate of weight loss before this temperature is very low and that any slight changes in this region may be related to the onset of surface transformations or minor volatilisation effects in the heat-affected zone, but this is not the main decomposition stage. After that, the main decomposition stage appears in the form of a sharp decline towards negative values (i.e., an increase in the rate of weight loss), and here the fundamental difference appears: curve 2023 (black) begins to decline earlier and enters the decomposition zone more quickly, then reaches a more negative minimum value (deeper bottom) at approximately 330–350 °C. This means that the maximum rate of mass loss in sample 2023 is higher than in sample 2025 and that its decomposition is more violent/severe within a shorter temperature range. This behaviour is typically consistent with the heat-affected zone in the aged material becoming more susceptible to decomposition as a result of prior degradation (e.g., chain shortening, increase in active ends, accumulation of oxidised groups or structural defects). When heated in DTG, this appears as an earlier onset of decomposition and a higher decomposition rate at peak temperature. In contrast, the 2025 curve delays the onset of decline, then extends the decomposition over a wider temperature range from approximately 300 °C to beyond 420 °C with a less negative trough than sample 2023, indicating that the decomposition process in this sample is relatively slower and more gradual and that the polymer chains in the HAZ are still more cohesive or that the stabilisers/compound are more effective in resisting rapid decomposition. The DTG curve of the 2025 HAZ sample extends over a wider temperature interval, which indicates a more gradual degradation process and greater thermal stability than the aged 2023 sample. The main decomposition stage occurs at higher temperatures and at a lower maximum degradation rate than for the aged 2023 sample. Towards the end of the degradation process, the DTG signal shifts towards less negative values. This behaviour does not indicate the formation of an additional degradation stage or mass gain; rather, it reflects a progressive reduction in the rate of mass loss as the remaining polymer fraction becomes depleted. The corresponding TGA curve confirms that mass loss continues throughout this region. Therefore, the final upward trend of the DTG signal is interpreted as the deceleration stage of thermal decomposition, which is consistent with the exhaustion of thermally labile polymer chains and the completion of the degradation process. Overall, the decomposition of the 2025 sample occurs over a wider temperature interval, suggesting a more homogeneous and thermally stable polymer structure than the aged 2023 sample, whose decomposition occurs earlier and within a narrower temperature interval.42,43

FTIR spectrum

Fig. 4 represents an FTIR analysis of PP samples produced in 2023 and 2025. The FTIR spectrum of the two samples showing a clear difference in absorption behavior at the characteristic ranges of chemical groups of both samples. In the older sample, there is an increase in absorption at the 3200–3600 cm−1 range, which is the characteristic O–H vibration range for hydroxyl groups, indicating moisture absorption and the formation of new polar groups as a result of oxidation. From other hand, the peaks at 1720–1740 cm−1 were also observed, attributed to CO vibrations of ketones or esters resulting from the reaction of polymer chains with oxygen, which are among the most significant signs of oxidative degradation in polypropylene. From this figure we can note that the C–H peaks at 2850–2950 cm−1 exhibit a drop in absorption intensity in the older sample compared to the newer sample, which may be due to the loss of some hydrocarbon bonds due to thermal or oxidative cleavage of the main chains. A slight increase in the intensity of the peaks in the 1000–1160 cm−1 range, which are attributed to C–O or C–C vibrations, was also observed, which can be linked to the formation of secondary oxidation products such as alcohols or ethers.44–46 As a result, the fresh polypropylene sample from 2025 showed a more consistent spectrum with stable and specific absorption lines for pure polypropylene, in which no clear carbonyl or hydroxyl peaks were observed, confirming that the material had not yet been oxidized or thermally decomposed. By observing these spectral differences, it is clear that the 2023 sample has experienced chemical changes caused by natural aging during storage, which led to chain scission and polar group formation, resulting in reduced molecular structure regularity and weakening of mechanical and thermal properties.

In Fig. 5, which represents FTIR examination of the heat-affected zone (HAZ) after horizontal burning test of polypropylene, the FTIR spectra for this zone show a clear structural difference between the aged sample from 2023 and the fresh sample from 2025. The 2023 sample appears to be more advanced in the thermal oxidation pathway: the increase in absorption and broadening of the band at 3600–3200 cm−1 indicates the proliferation of –OH groups resulting from chain breakdown and moisture absorption, corresponding to the appearance/amplification of carbonyl bands at 1740–1710 cm−1 (ketones/esters/lactones) with a possible slight shift towards lower frequencies due to conjugation or the formation of lactones in the charred surface layer. This is reinforced by a clear increase in the 1200–1000 cm−1 range attributed to C–O/C–C bonds (secondary oxidation products such as alcohols and ethers), with a relative decline in the characteristic crystalline peaks of polypropylene at ∼998 and ∼973 cm−1 in sample 2023, evidence of reduced crystallinity and microcrystalline rearrangement as a result of the dual thermal history (pre-aging followed by direct exposure to flame). In summary, the comparison highlights that pre-aging doubles the sensitivity of polypropylene to thermal oxidation in the HAZ region by facilitating chain scission, generating polar groups, and loss of crystallinity, which is reflected in its thermal stability and recyclability after burning and explains the difference in absorption curves between the two samples.47–50

Fig. 6 shows FTIR analysis of the sample manufactured in 2023 and exposed to natural aging before and after the horizontal burning test, particularly in HAZ. The figure shows clear differences in absorption intensity and peak locations, reflecting the structural and chemical changes that occurred in polypropylene as a result of exposure to flame and the acquisition of high thermal energy. Before combustion, the spectrum shows features typical of aged polypropylene, with absorption peaks at 2850–2950 cm−1 corresponding to the stretching vibrations of C–H groups in aliphatic chains, along with minor peaks at 1720–1740 cm−1 corresponding to CO groups of carbonyls resulting from gradual oxidation due to aging. There is also a slight increase in the 3200–3600 cm−1 range due to the presence of moisture-absorbing hydroxyl O–H groups. After combustion, the spectrum of HAZ shows a clear increase in the intensity and width of the O–H peaks, indicating the formation of new polar groups due to thermal oxidation and surface combustion. There is also a clear amplification in the CO band at ∼1730 cm−1 and its slight shift towards lower wavenumbers, indicating the formation of more complex oxidized compounds such as esters and lactones. The post-burning spectrum also shows a significant decrease in the intensity of the C–H peaks at 2850–2950 cm−1 and a decrease in the CH2/CH3 peaks at 1375–1455 cm−1, which is attributed to the cleavage of polymer chains and hydrogen removal resulting from high temperature, which leads to the loss of part of the basic hydrocarbon structure of polypropylene. In contrast, an increase is recorded in the 1000–1200 cm−1 range associated with C–O and C–C vibrations resulting from the formation of secondary oxidation products (such as alcohols and ethers). Some changes also appear in the 1660–1640 cm−1 range, indicating the presence of unsaturated CC bonds formed as a result of hydrogen removal during partial thermal decomposition. These shifts are accompanied by changes in the crystalline peaks at ∼998 and ∼973 cm−1, which exhibit lower intensity after combustion, indicating a decrease in crystallinity and an increase in structural disorder in the heat-affected zone. In general, it can be said that exposure to burning in the sample previously exposed to natural aging led to enhanced thermal oxidation processes and a doubling of the formation of polar groups (CO, O–H) groups at the expense of hydrocarbon bonds (C–H), in addition to a clear decrease in the crystallization of the molecular structure, making the HAZ area more fragile and less stable compared to the material before combustion. These results indicate that premature aging makes polypropylene more susceptible to faster degradation when directly exposed to flame, and infrared spectral analysis is an accurate tool for monitoring these chemical and structural changes.51–54

As can be seen in Fig. 7, which represents the FTIR spectrum of the polypropylene sample produced in 2025 before and after horizontal burning test in the heat-affected zone, that the material was less oxidized and more structurally uniform in its initial state compared to what appears after exposure to flame. Before testing, the stretching peaks of the C–H groups at 2850–2950 cm−1 appear clear and their absorption ratios are relatively high, with the carbonyl peaks at 1740–1710 cm−1 and hydroxyl at 3200–3600 cm−1 being faint or almost absent, which is consistent with a newly synthesized, low-polarity structure. After burning, the HAZ spectrum shows a broadening and a noticeable increase in the –OH range (3200–3600 cm−1) and the appearance/a clear increase in the CO peaks near ∼1730 cm−1, indicating the onset of thermal oxidation of the surface and the formation of oxidized products (ketones/esters and possibly thin lactones), with a possible slight shift towards lower frequencies due to conjugation or hydrogen bonds. This is accompanied by a noticeable decrease in the intensity of the C–H peaks and CH2/CH3 bending vibrations at 1375–1455 cm−1, indicating partial chain cleavage and hydrogen removal in the HAZ layer. The signals ranges 1000–1200 cm−1 attributed to C–O/C–C vibrations are also strengthened, suggesting the formation of alcohols/ethers as secondary oxidation products. A low intensity signal near 1640–1660 cm−1 attributed to CC bonds resulting from thermal decomposition can be observed. At the structural level, polypropylene's characteristic crystalline peaks at ∼998 and ∼973 cm−1 show reduced intensity and partial loss of peak definition after combustion compared to before. This indicates a limited decrease in crystallinity and a slight increase in amorphousness confined to the surface area exposed to the flame. These changes remain less severe than those expected for an aged sample, as sample 2025 did not exhibit previous aging defects. In summary, the pre- and post-burning spectra show that the damage in the modern sample is chemically concentrated on the surface and within a small depth of the HAZ (increase in –OH and CO with decrease in C–H and limited crystalline decline), while the overall structure of the material remains more consistent with its initial properties, which explains its better relative stability and higher processability and recyclability compared to older samples when exposed to similar flame conditions.

SEM/EDX analysis

The SEM/EDX spectrum for 2023 sample (Fig. 8a) shows a high, dominant peak for carbon at low energy (≈0.27 keV), with a small peak for oxygen. This behaviour indicates the purely organic nature of polypropylene, which consists mainly of hydrocarbon chains. The relatively low oxygen content can be attributed to the onset of surface oxidation of polypropylene chains resulting from natural ageing during storage, where prolonged exposure to atmospheric oxygen leads to the formation of limited surface oxidised groups, without extending the effect to the core of the material or causing extensive degradation of the polymer chains.52,55 Furthermore, the absence of peaks for metallic or inorganic elements confirms the purity of the material and that it does not contain any inorganic additives detectable by EDX. As for 2025 sample (Fig. 8b), there is a noticeable increase in the intensity of the carbon peak compared to the sample manufactured in 2023, with a decrease or almost complete disappearance of the oxygen peak, indicating that the newer sample is less exposed to oxidation processes and has higher structural integrity of the polymer chains, in addition to containing fewer surface oxidised groups. These results are consistent with the fact that the material is not yet affected by ageing, reflecting better chemical stability compared to the older sample.56

These changes indicate that thermal oxidation of the polymer chains occurred during combustion, forming oxidised functional groups such as (CO, C–O), with the onset of chain scission as a result of the effect of heat. Flame behavior and burning characteristics

Fig. 10 illustrates the flame development during the UL-94 HB test for new and naturally aged PP, showing the differences in flame stability, intensity and height during the various stages of burning at (15 s) intervals. The results of the UL-94 HB test revealed a clear difference between the non-aged PP samples manufactured in 2025 and the aged 2023 samples in terms of combustion characteristics and behavior. Although the 2023 samples exhibited a taller, more elongated and fluctuating flame during combustion, their combustion rate was lower, and the flame was less intense and stable compared to the new samples, as mentioned earlier. This suggests that natural ageing did not accelerate flame spread, but rather altered the combustion mechanism and the material's thermal response. In the early stages of the test, the 2025 samples appeared more stable and regular, with a more concentrated and dense flame near the leading edge of combustion, reflecting a more uniform release of volatile materials from the relatively intact polymer structure. In contrast, the 2023 samples exhibited a less stable flame that varied more in shape and height, with a clear tendency to drip and flow as a result of the degradation of the polymer chains due to ageing.40,57 It was also observed that the flame height in the aged samples was greater, particularly in the advanced stages of combustion; however, this increase does not necessarily indicate faster combustion, but may instead be linked to increased volatilization of thermal decomposition products and irregular combustion. Also, the HAZ in the 2023 samples appeared wider and more extensive compared to the 2025 samples (Fig. 1), indicating deeper heat transfer within the polymer and the consumption of part of the thermal energy in softening and thermal deformation rather than accelerating the progression of the flame front. It can therefore be said that the 2023 samples exhibited slower but less stable combustion and were more thermally affected, whilst the 2025 samples were characterized by more regular and stable combustion with a relatively higher flame spread rate.6,37

The surface topography of the HAZ was further evaluated using Atomic Force Microscopy (AFM), and the corresponding three-dimensional images are presented in Fig. 11, 12 and Table 5. The HAZ of sample 2023 exhibited a higher average surface roughness (Sa = 24.86 nm), whereas sample 2025 showed a lower roughness value (Sa = 16.59 nm). The higher roughness observed in sample 2023 indicates a greater degree of surface deterioration and topographical irregularity, which can be attributed to the combined effects of natural ageing and thermal exposure.

The results reveal a marked increase in surface roughness for the PP 2023 sample compared to the PP 2025 sample. The mean surface roughness (Sa) increased from 16.59 nm in PP 2025 to 24.86 nm in PP 2023 as mentioned above, whilst the mean square root of roughness (Sq) rose from 23.19 nm to 36.58 nm. The maximum height parameters also clearly support this trend. The maximum height of surface peaks (Sp) increased from 200.8 nm in PP 2025 to 377.1 nm in PP 2023, whilst the maximum depth of surface pits (Sv) increased from 57.58 nm to 100.9 nm. The distribution of surface elevations was also affected by the ageing process. Both samples exhibited positive values for the surface skewness coefficient (Ssk > 0), indicating that the surface is dominated by peaks rather than valleys. However, sample PP 2023 recorded a higher value for the skewness coefficient (2.206) compared to sample PP 2025 (1.895), suggesting a greater concentration of surface protrusions and high peaks. Furthermore, the higher Sku value in PP 2023 (13.58) compared to PP 2025 (11.87) suggests that ageing contributed to the formation of more extreme and sharp topographical features.

Overall, the roughness parameters derived from AFM analysis confirm that natural ageing has led to a marked increase in the severity of surface degradation within the heat-affected zone. The higher roughness values, greater variation in heights, and predominance of surface peaks in the PP 2023 sample indicate that long-term exposure to natural ageing has increased the susceptibility of polypropylene to morphological degradation resulting from thermal effects.

Conclusions

From the results obtained from the FTIR test of the samples before and after the horizontal burning test, the following conclusions can be drawn:

1. TGA/DTG analysis demonstrated that natural aging reduces the thermal stability of polypropylene, as indicated by a shift of the onset degradation temperature toward lower values and an increase in the maximum mass loss rate.

2. These thermogravimetric results confirm that prior aging amplifies the thermal degradation kinetics of polypropylene, particularly within the heat-affected zone after burning, leading to accelerated polymer decomposition when exposed to flame.

3. The severity of degradation in the HAZ was directly related to the age of the material, with older samples exhibiting greater levels of oxidation and molecular breakdown than newer samples.

4. New chemical groups have been formed in HAZ after burning, which were previously invisible due to its weakness. This is evidence that burning accelerates the oxidation as it leads to create various degradation products within this zone.

5. The degree of crystallisation decreased significantly within the HAZ, as evidenced by the weakening of the characteristic crystalline phase peaks.

6. Natural aging contributed to accelerating the rate of thermal degradation within the HAZ after horizontal burning test.

7. SEM/EDX results showed that natural ageing directly affect the surface chemical composition of PP. While newer material remains more stable, older samples show a higher susceptibility to oxidation and degradation, especially after exposure to heat.

8. AFM and optical images and surface roughness measurements showed that the HAZ in PP exhibits clear morphological changes compared to unaffected areas, with sample 2023 recording higher surface roughness values than sample 2025, indicating increased surface topographical irregularity as a result of localised heat exposure.

Multiple solutions to asymptotically linear problems driven by superposition operators
Danilo Gregorin Afonso, Rossella Bartolo, Giovanni Molica Bisci

Abstract
. In this paper, we investigate the existence and multiplicity of weak solutions to problems involving a superposition operator of the type ∫ [ 0 , 1 ] ( − Δ ) s ​ u ​ 𝑑 μ ​ ( s ) , \int_{[0,1]}(-\Delta)^{s}u\ d\mu(s), for a signed measure μ \mu on the interval of fractional exponents [ 0 , 1 ] [0,1] , when the nonlinearity is subcritical and asymptotically linear at infinity; thus, we deal with a perturbation of the eigenvalue problem for the superposition operator. We use variational tools, extending to this setting well-known results for the classical and the fractional Laplace operators.

1.
Introduction

In recent years, much attention has been drawn to elliptic problems driven by mixed-order operators, generally consisting of combinations of the Laplacian with its fractional (integral) powers. See, e.g.,
[
8
,
9
,
14
,
18
]
and the references therein.

Our focus in this work is on asymptotically linear problems driven by the superposition of fractional Laplacians. More precisely, we deal with the operator

A_{\mu}u\coloneqq\int_{[0,1]}(-\Delta)^{s}u\ d\mu(s),

where
\mu=\mu^{+}-\mu^{-}
, with
\mu^{+}
and
\mu^{-}
nonnegative finite Borel measures in
[0,1]
, and
(-\Delta)^{s}
denotes the integral fractional Laplacian, which can be defined, for all
s\in(0,1)
, by

(-\Delta)^{s}u(x)=c_{N,s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{2u(x)-u(x+y)-u(x-y)}{|y|^{N+2s}}dy,\quad u\in\mathcal{S},

where
\mathcal{S}
is the Schwartz class of smooth, rapidly decaying functions, see
[
19
]
.

Superpositions of fractional Laplacians of different orders were first considered in
[
10
]
and appear in many applications, such as biological models where the individuals of a population are subject to Gaussian and Lévy flights (see
[
17
]
,
[
16
]
). Since then, operators of the kind
A_{\mu}
, where some terms of smaller order are allowed to have a negative contribution, have drawn much interest, see
[
13
,
14
]
, also under Neumann boundary conditions
[
15
]
.

Let us comment on some particular cases of interest. In what follows,
\delta_{s}
denotes the Dirac measure with center at
s\in[0,1]
.

Examples 1.1
.

(i)

If
\mu=\delta_{1}
, then
A_{\mu}=-\Delta
and we recover well-known results for the Laplacian (see
[
2
]
);

(ii)

if
\mu=\delta_{s}
, with
s\in(0,1)
, then
A_{\mu}=(-\Delta)^{s}
and we recover well-known results for the integral fractional Laplacian (see
[
5
]
);

(iii)

if
\mu=\delta_{s_{1}}+\delta_{s_{2}}
, then
A_{\mu}
is a sum of fractional Laplacians of different orders. Similarly, we could consider
\mu=\delta_{1}+\delta_{s}
, so
A_{\mu}=-\Delta+(-\Delta)^{s}
;

(iv)

if
\mu=\delta_{1}-\alpha\delta_{s}
, with
\alpha>0
small, then we have a combination of operators with the “wrong sign”, which is usually an obstacle when dealing with variational problems with operators of mixed orders.

In order to recover classical variational methods, we make the following structural assumption regarding the signed measure
\mu
: there exist
\bar{s}\in(0,1]
and
\gamma\geq 0
such that

(1.1)

\displaystyle\mu^{+}([\bar{s},1])>0,

(1.2)

\displaystyle\mu^{-}|_{[\bar{s},1]}=0,

(1.3)

\displaystyle\mu^{-}([0,\bar{s}])\leq\gamma\mu^{+}([\bar{s},1]).

Some further examples are as follows.

Examples 1.2
.

(i)

Let us consider a convergent series
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}c_{k}<+\infty
, where either
c_{k}\geq 0
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
or, more generally, the condition (
1.3
) holds, and set, for
1\geq s_{0}>s_{1}>s_{2}>\ldots\geq 0
,

A_{\mu}u=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}c_{k}(-\Delta)^{s_{k}}u;

(ii)

if
\mu(s)=f(s)\ ds
, for some measurable function
f
, then
A_{\mu}
is a continuous superposition of fractional operators, as has been considered, e.g, in
[
10
]
:

A_{\mu}u=\int_{0}^{1}(-\Delta)^{s}uf(s)\ ds.

We are interested in investigating the existence of solutions to asymptotically linear problems driven by the operator
A_{\mu}
. More precisely, we consider the following Dirichlet problem:

(1.4)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcll}A_{\mu}u-\bar{\lambda}u&=&f(x,u)&\quad\text{ in }\Omega\\
u&=&0&\quad\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega\end{array}\right.,

where
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
N\geq 2
, is an open bounded set with Lipschitz boundary, and
f:\Omega\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is a Carathéodory function satisfying

(
f_{1}
)

\displaystyle\left(x\mapsto\sup_{|t|\leq\tau}|f(x,t)|\right)\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)\quad\forall\tau>0,

(
f_{2}
)

\displaystyle\lim_{|t|\to+\infty}\frac{f(x,t)}{t}=0\quad\text{ uniformly almost everywhere in }\Omega,

(
f_{3}
)

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{f(x,t)}{t}=\lambda_{0}\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}\quad\text{ uniformly almost everywhere in }\Omega.

Problem (

(1.5)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcll}A_{\mu}u&=&\lambda u&\quad\text{ in }\Omega\\

which has been recently been analyzed in detail in
[
12

. It is known that there exists a sequence of eigenvalues (counted with multiplicity)

0<\lambda_{1}<\lambda_{2}\leq\lambda_{3}\leq\ldots\leq\lambda_{n}\leq\ldots\to+\infty;

we refer to Section

the spectrum of

Next, we state our main results about the existence and multiplicity of solutions to (

(see Section

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Definitions and notations

Let
s\in(0,1)
. The
s
-Gagliardo seminorm of a measurable function
u:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}
is given by

[u]_{s}\coloneqq\left(c_{N,s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\ dx\ dy\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},

where the normalization constant
c_{N,s}
is chosen so that

\lim_{s\to 1^{-}}[u]_{s}=[u]_{1}\coloneqq\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\quad\text{ and }\quad\lim_{s\to 0^{+}}[u]_{s}=[u]_{0}\coloneqq\|u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}.

For more on the integral fractional Laplacian and fractional Sobolev-type spaces, see
[
11
,
19
]
.

We denote by
C_{j}
, with
j\in\mathbb{N}
, some positive constants that appear in the calculations, and whose specific values are not of interest.

Finally, the
L^{p}(\Omega)
norm is denoted by
\|\cdot\|_{p}
.

2.2.
Functional setting

We deal with the operator
A_{\mu}
under assumptions (
1.1
)-(
1.3
), so basically the component of the signed measure
\mu
supported on higher fractional exponents is positive and, if there are negative components of
\mu
, they must be controlled (see (
1.3
)) by the positive ones. Let us also point out that
\gamma
in (
1.3
) is taken sufficiently small and that by (
1.1
) there exists
s_{\sharp}\in[\bar{s},1]
such that
\mu^{+}([s_{\sharp},1])>0
.

The next lemma shows that higher exponents in fractional norms control lower exponents by uniform constants.

Lemma 2.1

(
[
14
, Lemma 2.1]
)
.

Let
0\leq s_{1}\leq s_{2}\leq 1
. Then, there exists a constant
c=c(N,\Omega)>0
such that, for any measurable function
u:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}
such that
u=0
almost everywhere in
\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega
, it holds

[u]_{s_{1}}\leq c[u]_{s_{2}}.

An appropriate Sobolev-type space in which to consider the weak formulation of problems driven by the operator
A_{\mu}
is the set
X_{0}(\Omega)
of measurable functions
u:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}
such that

u\equiv 0\quad\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega

and

\int_{[0,1]}[u]_{s}^{2}d\mu^{+}(s)<+\infty.

Indeed, the following lemma holds.

Lemma 2.2

(
[
14
, Lemma 2.2]
)
.

If
\mu^{+}
satisfies (
1.1
), then
X_{0}(\Omega)
is a Hilbert space with the norm

\|u\|_{X_{0}(\Omega)}\coloneqq\left(\int_{[0,1]}[u]_{s}^{2}\ d\mu^{+}(s)\right)^{1/2}.

Now we denote by
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
the inner product in
X_{0}(\Omega)
induced by the norm
\|\cdot\|_{X_{0}(\Omega)}
:

\langle u,v\rangle\coloneqq\int_{[0,1]}\left(c_{N,s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{(u(x)-u(y))(v(x)-v(y))}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\ dx\ dy\right)\ d\mu^{+}(s)\quad\forall u,v\in X_{0}(\Omega).

It is also useful to define the following bilinear form:

(u,v)\coloneqq\int_{[0,\bar{s}]}\left(c_{N,s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{(u(x)-u(y))(v(x)-v(y))}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\ dx\ dy\right)\ d\mu^{-}(s)\quad\forall u,v\in X_{0}(\Omega),

in fact

\int_{[0,1]}[u]_{s}^{2}\ d\mu(s)=\langle u,u\rangle-(u,u).

The following result deals with the possibility of
reabsorbing

\mu^{-}
in the signed measure
\mu
.

Proposition 2.3

(
[
14
, Proposition 2.3]
)
.

If (
1.1
)-(
1.3
) hold, then there exists a constant
c_{0}=c_{0}(N,\Omega)>0
such that

\int_{[0,\bar{s}]}[u]_{s}^{2}\ d\mu^{-}(s)\leq c_{0}\gamma\int_{[\bar{s},1]}[u]_{s}^{2}d\mu(s)\quad\forall u\in X_{0}(\Omega).

Remark 2.4
.

If
\gamma
is small, recall (
1.3
), then the bilinear form

(2.1)

u,v\in X_{0}(\Omega)\mapsto\langle u,v\rangle-(u,v)\in\mathbb{R}

induces a norm in
X_{0}(\Omega)
which is equivalent to the one given in Lemma
2.2
. Indeed, in view of Proposition
2.3
, we have

\langle u,u\rangle\geq\langle u,u\rangle-(u,u)\geq(1-c_{0}\gamma)\langle u,u\rangle.

In particular, it follows that the bilinear form (
2.1
) is coercive and, therefore, gives rise to an equivalent inner product in
X_{0}(\Omega)
. We denote by
\|\cdot\|
this equivalent norm in
X_{0}(\Omega)
, i.e.,

\|u\|^{2}=\langle u,u\rangle-(u,u),\quad u\in X_{0}(\Omega).

In the next proposition, some crucial embedding properties are stated.

Proposition 2.5

(
[
14
, Proposition 2.4]
)
.

Suppose that
\mu=\mu^{+}-\mu^{-}
satisfies (
1.1
)-(
1.3
). Let
s_{\sharp}\in[\bar{s},1]
be such that
\mu^{+}([s_{\sharp},1])>0
. Then, there exists a constant
\bar{c}=\bar{c}(N,\Omega,s_{\sharp})>0
such that

[u]_{s_{\sharp}}\leq\bar{c}\left(\int_{[0,1]}[u]_{s}^{2}\ d\mu^{+}(s)\right)^{1/2}.

In particular,
X_{0}(\Omega)
is continuously embedded in
L^{p}(\Omega)
for every
p\in[1,2_{s_{\sharp}}^{*}]
and compactly embedded in
L^{p}(\Omega)
for every
p\in[1,2_{s_{\sharp}}^{*})
.

By a solution to (
1.4
), we mean a weak solution, i.e., a function
u\in X_{0}(\Omega)
such that for all
v\in X_{0}(\Omega)
it holds

\langle u,v\rangle-(u,v)=\bar{\lambda}\int_{\Omega}uv\ dx+\int_{\Omega}f(x,u)v\ dx,

that is,

\displaystyle\int_{[0,1]}

\displaystyle\left(c_{N,s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{(u(x)-u(y))(v(x)-v(y))}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\ dx\ dy\right)\ d\mu^{+}(s)

\displaystyle-\int_{[0,\bar{s}]}\left(c_{N,s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{(u(x)-u(y))(v(x)-v(y))}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\ dx\ dy\right)\ d\mu^{-}(s)

\displaystyle=\bar{\lambda}\int_{\Omega}uv\ dx+\int_{\Omega}f(x,u)v\ dx.

2.3.
On the eigenvalues of the operator
A_{\mu}

By a solution to (
1.5
), we mean a weak solution, that is, a function
u\in X_{0}(\Omega)
such that for all
v\in X_{0}(\Omega)
it holds

\displaystyle\langle u,v\rangle-(u,v)=\lambda\int_{\Omega}uv\ dx,

for some
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
.

It has been shown that the operator
A_{\mu}
satisfies conditions that are adequate for a spectral theory akin to that of the (classical or fractional) Laplace operator (compare with
[
3
]
and
[
5
]
). Namely, the following holds:

Theorem 2.6

(
[
12
, Theorem 1.3]
)
.

Let
\mu
satisfy (
1.1
)-(
1.3
), and assume that
N>2s_{\sharp}
. Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
be an open bounded set with Lipschitz boundary. Then, there exists
\gamma_{0}>0
depending only on
N
and
\Omega
such that if
\gamma\in[0,\gamma_{0}]
, then:

(i)

there exists a sequence
\{\lambda_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
of eigenvalues for (
1.5
), with a corresponding sequence of eigenfunctions
\{e_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
, such that

0<\lambda_{1}\leq\lambda_{2}\leq\ldots\leq\lambda_{k}\leq\ldots\to+\infty;

(ii)

for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
, it holds

(2.2)

\lambda_{k+1}=\min_{u\in\mathbb{P}_{k+1}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\langle u,u\rangle-(u,u)}{\|u\|_{2}^{2}},

where

\mathbb{P}_{k+1}\coloneqq\{u\in X_{0}(\Omega)\ :\ \langle u,e_{j}\rangle-(u,e_{j})=0\ \forall j=1,\ldots,k\};

(iii)

the eigenfunction
e_{k}
attains the minimum in (
2.2
);

(iv)

the sequence of eigenfunctions
\{e_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
constitutes an orthonormal basis of
L^{2}(\Omega)
and an orthogonal basis of
X_{0}(\Omega)
;

(v)

each eigenvalue
\lambda_{k}
has finite multiplicity.

Remark 2.7
.

Let

\mathbb{H}_{k}=\spanned\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{k}\}

be the subspace of
X_{0}(\Omega)
spanned by the first
k
eigenfunctions. It is clear that

X_{0}(\Omega)=\mathbb{H}_{k}\oplus\mathbb{P}_{k+1}.

Moreover,

\left\{\frac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda_{k}}}e_{k}\right\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}

is an orthonormal basis for
X_{0}(\Omega)
. Therefore, for all
u\in\mathbb{H}_{k}
, it holds that

\displaystyle\|u\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\frac{1}{\lambda_{j}}(\langle u,e_{j}\rangle-(u,e_{j}))^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\lambda_{k}\sum_{j=1}^{k}\left(\int_{\Omega}ue_{j}\ dx\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\lambda_{k}\|u\|_{2}^{2}.

2.4.
An abstract critical point theorem

Here, we outline the main classical tools for dealing with asymptotically linear problems. Namely, we recall an abstract critical point theorem, proved in
[
2
]
, based on pseudo-index theories, introduced in
[
7
]
, to study the multiplicity of critical points of strongly indefinite functionals in the presence of symmetries.

Let
E
be a real Banach space with norm
\|\cdot\|_{E}
,
E^{\prime}
its dual space and
I\in C^{1}(E,\mathbb{R})
.

Definition 2.8

(Palais-Smale condition)
.

The functional
I
satisfies the Palais-Smale condition, (PS) in short, at the level
c\in\mathbb{R}
, if any sequence
\{u_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
in
E
such that

\displaystyle I(u_{n})\to c\quad\text{ as }n\to+\infty

\displaystyle I^{\prime}(u_{n})\to 0\text{ in }E^{\prime}\quad\text{ as }n\to+\infty

converges in
E
, up to subsequences. Such a sequence
\{u_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is said to be a Palais-Smale sequence. In general, if
-\infty\leq a<b\leq+\infty
,
I
satisfies (PS) in
(a,b)
if it satisfies (PS) for every
c\in(a,b)
.

The following abstract critical point theorem, whose proof is based on the pseudo-index related to the genus (see
[
7
]
for more details), is key to our proof of
Theorem
1.4
.

Theorem 2.9

(
[
2
]
, Theorem 2.9)
.

Let
I\in C^{1}(E,\mathbb{R})
and assume that:

(i)

I
is even;

(ii)

I
satisfies (PS) in
\mathbb{R}
;

(iii)

there exist two closed subspaces
V,W\subset X
such that
\dim V<+\infty,{\rm codim}\,W<+\infty
and two constants
c_{0},c_{\infty}
, such that
c_{\infty}>c_{0}
, verifying the following assumptions:

•

I(u)\geq c_{0}
on
S_{\rho}\cap W
(resp. on
S_{\rho}\cap V
), where
S_{\rho}=\{u\in E:\|u\|_{X}=\rho\}
;

•

I(u)\leq c_{\infty}
on
V
(resp. on
W
).

If, moreover,
\dim V>{\rm codim}\,W
, then
I
has at least
\dim V-{\rm codim}\,W
distinct pairs of critical points whose corresponding critical values belong to
[c_{0},c_{\infty}]
.

3.
Existence and multiplicity results

By (

f
1

) and (

f
2

), we have that for every
\varepsilon>0
there exists
a_{\varepsilon}>0
such that

(3.1)

|f(x,t)|\leq\varepsilon|t|+a_{\varepsilon},\quad\forall t\in\mathbb{R}\ \text{ almost everywhere in }\Omega.

The weak solutions of (
1.4
) can be found as critical points of the functional

J(u)=\frac{1}{2}\|u\|^{2}-\frac{\bar{\lambda}}{2}\int_{\Omega}u^{2}\ dx-\int_{\Omega}F(x,u)\ dx,\quad u\in X_{0}(\Omega),

where
\|\cdot\|
has been defined in Remark
2.4
and, as usual,
F(x,t)=\int_{0}^{t}f(x,s)\ ds.

Proposition 3.1
.

Assume that (

f
1

)-(

f
2

) hold. If
\bar{\lambda}\not\in\sigma(A_{\mu})
, then the functional
J
satisfies (PS) in
\mathbb{R}
.

Proof.

Let
\{u_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a (PS) sequence, i.e., it holds that

\displaystyle\{J(u_{n})\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\text{ is bounded};

(3.2)

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to+\infty}J^{\prime}(u_{n})=0\quad\text{ in the dual space }(X_{0}(\Omega))^{\prime}.

In particular, due to (
3.2
) it holds that

(3.3)

\langle u_{n},v\rangle-(u_{n},v)-\bar{\lambda}\int_{\Omega}u_{n}v\ dx-\int_{\Omega}f(x,u_{n})v\ dx=o(1)\quad\forall v\in X_{0}(\Omega),

where
o(1)
denotes an infinitesimal sequence.

We claim that
\{u_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is bounded in
X_{0}(\Omega)
. Indeed, arguing by contradiction, suppose that

\|u_{n}\|\to+\infty\quad\text{ as }n\to+\infty.

For each
n
, we set

w_{n}\coloneqq\frac{u_{n}}{\|u_{n}\|},

so that the sequence
\{w_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is bounded in
X_{0}(\Omega)
. Since
X_{0}(\Omega)
is a Hilbert space, then there exists some
w\in X_{0}(\Omega)
such that

w_{n}\rightharpoonup w\quad\text{ weakly in }X_{0}(\Omega)

as
n\to+\infty
. Since the embedding of
X_{0}(\Omega)
into
L^{2}(\Omega)
is compact (Proposition
2.5
), then also

w_{n}\to w\quad\text{ strongly in }L^{2}(\Omega)

as
n\to+\infty
.

Taking
w_{n}-w
as test function in (
3.2
), we obtain

\langle u_{n},w_{n}-w\rangle-(u_{n},w_{n}-w)=\bar{\lambda}\int_{\Omega}u_{n}(w_{n}-w)\ dx+\int_{\Omega}f(x,u_{n})(w_{n}-w)\ dx+o(1).

For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we divide by
\|u_{n}\|
to obtain

\langle w_{n},w_{n}-w\rangle-(w_{n},w_{n}-w)=\bar{\lambda}\int_{\Omega}w_{n}(w_{n}-w)\ dx+\int_{\Omega}\frac{f(x,u_{n})}{\|u_{n}\|}(w_{n}-w)\ dx+o(1).

Now, since
w_{n}\to w
strongly in
L^{2}(\Omega)
, we have

\left|\int_{\Omega}w_{n}(w_{n}-w)\ dx\right|\leq\|w_{n}\|_{2}\|w_{n}-w\|_{2}=o(1),

because
\{w_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
, being bounded in
X_{0}(\Omega)
, is bounded in
L^{2}(\Omega)
too. On the other hand, by (
3.1
), for any
\varepsilon>0
we have

\left|\int_{\Omega}\frac{f(x,u_{n})}{\|u_{n}\|}(w_{n}-w)\ dx\right|\leq\varepsilon\|w_{n}\|_{2}\|w_{n}-w\|_{2}+a_{\varepsilon}\frac{\|w_{n}-w\|_{1}}{\|u_{n}\|}=o(1).

Therefore, we conclude that

\langle w_{n},w_{n}-w\rangle-(w_{n},w_{n}-w)=o(1).

But, since

\displaystyle o(1)

\displaystyle=\langle w_{n},w_{n}-w\rangle-(w_{n},w_{n}-w)

\displaystyle=\langle w_{n}-w,w_{n}-w\rangle-(w_{n}-w,w_{n}-w)

\displaystyle\quad+\langle w,w_{n}-w\rangle-(w,w_{n}-w)

\displaystyle=\|w_{n}-w\|^{2}+o(1),

it follows that

w_{n}\to w\quad\text{ strongly in }X_{0}(\Omega)

and therefore
w\neq 0
.

Dividing (
3.3
) by
\|u_{n}\|
, we obtain

\langle w_{n},v\rangle-(w_{n},v)=\bar{\lambda}\int_{\Omega}w_{n}v\ dx-\int_{\Omega}\frac{f(x,u_{n})}{\|u_{n}\|}v\ dx+o(1),\quad\forall v\in X_{0}(\Omega).

As before, since
\|u_{n}\|\to+\infty
as
n\to+\infty
, then

\int_{\Omega}\frac{f(x,u_{n})}{\|u_{n}\|}v\ dx=o(1),\quad\forall v\in X_{0}(\Omega).

Then, passing to the limit, we obtain

\langle w,v\rangle-(w,v)=\bar{\lambda}\int_{\Omega}wv\ dx,\quad\forall v\in X_{0}(\Omega),

implying that
w
is an eigenfunction with corresponding eigenvalue
\bar{\lambda}
. This contradicts the assumptions that
\bar{\lambda}\notin\sigma(A_{\mu})
.

The sequence
\{u_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is therefore bounded in
X_{0}(\Omega)
. Since
X_{0}(\Omega)
is a Hilbert space, then there exists some
u\in X_{0}(\Omega)
such that

\displaystyle u_{n}\rightharpoonup u\quad\text{ weakly in }X_{0}(\Omega)

\displaystyle u_{n}\to u\quad\text{ strongly in }L^{2}(\Omega)

as
n\to+\infty
. Hence

J^{\prime}(u_{n})[u_{n}-u]\to 0\quad\text{ as }n\to+\infty.

By the same reasoning as before, we obtain that

u_{n}\to u\quad\text{ strongly in }X_{0}(\Omega),

which completes the proof.
∎

Proof of Proposition
1.3
.

The proof follows by a standard application of variational methods (see, e.g.,
[
20
,
1
]
).

We begin by noting that, by (
3.1
),

|F(x,t)|\leq C_{1}(1+t^{2}),\quad\forall t\in\mathbb{R}\text{ almost everywhere in }\Omega.

Now, by the variational characterization of the eigenvalues of
A_{\mu}
(see Theorem
2.6
), we have that given any
k\in\mathbb{N}
, it holds

\|u\|_{2}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{\lambda_{k}+1}\|u\|^{2},\quad\forall u\in\mathbb{P}_{k+1}.

It follows that

\displaystyle J(u)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\|u\|^{2}-\frac{\bar{\lambda}}{2}\int_{\Omega}u^{2}\ dx-\int_{\Omega}F(x,u)\ dx

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\left(1-\frac{\bar{\lambda}}{\lambda_{k+1}}-\frac{C_{2}}{\lambda_{k+1}}\right)\|u\|^{2}-C_{3},\quad\forall u\in\mathbb{P}_{k+1}

where
C_{2}
and
C_{3}
do not depend on
k
. Therefore, provided that
k
is large enough, we have that
J
is bounded from below in
\mathbb{P}_{k+1}
. In other words, there exists
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
such that

J|_{\mathbb{P}_{k+1}}\geq\beta.

If
\bar{\lambda}<\lambda_{1}
, then there exists a minimizer for the functional
J
in
X_{0}(\Omega)
, since
J
is weakly lower-semicontinuous and coercive.

On the other hand, suppose that there for some
k\in\mathbb{N}
it holds

\lambda_{k}<\bar{\lambda}<\lambda_{k+1}.

Let
\varepsilon>0
be such that
\lambda_{k}+\varepsilon<\bar{\lambda}
. By (
3.1
), there exists a constant
C_{\varepsilon}>0
such that

J(u)\leq\frac{1}{2}\|u\|^{2}-\frac{\bar{\lambda}}{2}\|u\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\|u\|_{2}^{2}+C_{\varepsilon}\|u\|_{2},\quad\forall u\in X_{0}(\Omega),

and therefore, by Remark
2.7
,

J(u)\leq\frac{1}{2}(\lambda_{k}+\varepsilon-\bar{\lambda})\|u\|_{2}^{2}+C_{\varepsilon}\|u\|_{2},\quad\forall u\in\mathbb{H}_{k}.

Therefore we can find
\rho>0
big enough such that

J|_{S_{\rho}^{k}}\leq\alpha<\beta,

where
S_{\rho}^{k}
is the sphere of radius
\rho
in the finite dimensional space
\mathbb{H}_{k}
.

Therefore, all the geometric assumptions of the Saddle Point Theorem (
[
20
, Theorem 4.6]
) are satisfied. Since the functional
J
satisfies (PS), then
J
has at least one critical point, i.e., (
1.4
) admits at least one nontrivial solution.
∎

and

\Omega

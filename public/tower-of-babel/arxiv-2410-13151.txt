Nonlinear smoothing for the periodic dispersion generalized Benjamin-Ono equations with polynomial nonlinearity
Wangseok Shin

Abstract
. We consider the periodic dispersion generalized Benjamin-Ono equations with polynomial nonlinearity. We establish the nonlinear smoothing properties of these equations, according to which the difference between the solution and the linear evolution is smoother than the initial data. In addition, we establish new local well-posedness results for these equations when the dispersion is sufficiently large. Our method also improves known local well-posedness results for a class of non-integrable fifth-order KdV equations.



1.
Introduction

In this paper, we study the nonlinear smoothing properties of the periodic dispersion generalized Benjamin-Ono equations with polynomial nonlinearity given by

(1.1)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u+\partial_{x}D_{x}^{\alpha}u+\partial_{x}P(u)=0,\\
u(x,0)=g\in H^{s}(\mathbb{T}),\end{cases}(x,t)\in\mathbb{T}\times[-T,T],

where
P
is a polynomial with real coefficients,
1<\alpha<2
,
u=u(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}
, and
D_{x}^{\alpha}
is the Fourier multiplier operator with symbol
|\xi|^{\alpha}
. The equation (
1.1

[
36

. There are at least three conserved quantities for (
1.1

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{T}}u(x,t)dx=0,

(Mean conservation)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{T}}u(x,t)^{2}dx=0,

(Mass conservation)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\frac{1}{2}\big|D^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}u(x,t)\big|^{2}-F(u)(x,t)dx=0,

(Energy conservation)

where

\displaystyle F(x):=\int_{0}^{x}P(y)dy.

Using the mean conservation, we will make the assumption

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}}u(x,t)dx=\int_{\mathbb{T}}g(x)dx=0

throughout this paper.

Nonlinear smoothing refers to the phenomenon in which the difference between the solution and the linear evolution obtains higher regularity than the initial data, i.e., for the linear evolution

[

1.1.
Main results

We summarize our main results below. As in

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}u(t)-e^{t\partial_{x}D^{\alpha}_{x}}g\in C([-T,T],H^{s+a})

1.3.
Comments on the proof

The main ingredient of our proof is normal form reduction, which has been introduced in
[
35
,
1
]
. For a brief overview of our method, let us look at the
P(x)=x^{2}
case. Consider the
interaction variable

v:=S(-t)u
. Then on the Fourier side,
v
satisfies the equation

(1.5)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\widehat{v}_{\xi}=\frac{\xi}{i}\sum_{\xi_{1}+\xi_{2}=\xi}e^{-i\Omega_{2}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})t}\widehat{v}_{\xi_{1}}\widehat{v}_{\xi_{2}}.

Then we perform
differentiation by parts
to the summand of the right-hand side:

\displaystyle e^{-i\Omega_{2}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})t}\widehat{v}_{\xi_{1}}\widehat{v}_{\xi_{2}}=\partial_{t}\left(\frac{i}{\Omega_{2}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})}e^{-i\Omega_{2}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})t}\widehat{v}_{\xi_{1}}\widehat{v}_{\xi_{2}}\right)-\frac{i}{\Omega_{2}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})}e^{-i\Omega_{2}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})t}\partial_{t}\left(\widehat{v}_{\xi_{1}}\widehat{v}_{\xi_{2}}\right).

Now we have
\Omega_{2}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})
in the denominator that can be utilized to weaken the derivative in the nonlinearity. This mechanism can be viewed as a smoothing effect caused by rapid temporal oscillation in the phase space. Roughly speaking, this process smoothes the nonlinearity out by
\partial^{-\alpha+1}_{x}
. For
1<\alpha<2
, this gain is not enough to recover one derivative loss in
\partial_{x}P(u)
, necessitating us to repeat this process at least
\frac{1}{\alpha-1}
times. The major challenge in this analysis is to estimate various multilinear terms produced by the iteration. This requires delicate cancellation properties in the frequency domain, which is a main component of our proof.

The proof of Theorem
1.2
is based on the idea developed by McConnell
[
28
]
, where the author proved local well-posedness for the fifth-order KdV equation in
H^{s}(\mathbb{T})
for
s>\frac{35}{64}
. One of the main ideas of this work is to perform normal form reduction on the original equation, and then apply Bourgain’s
X^{s,b}
method to the reduced equation. Essentially this argument is a Piccard iteration. Nevertheless, the aforementioned Herr’s negative result
[
17
]
does not affect applying this method to (
1.1
), since we won’t be directly iterating (
1.3
). The difficulty in this analysis is that the ‘boundary terms’ created in the normal form reduction cannot be appropriately estimated in
X^{s,\frac{1}{2}}
. To remedy this,
[
28
]
adopted the space
X^{s,\frac{1}{4}}\cap W^{s}
, where
W^{s}
is the space defined by the norm
\|\langle\xi\rangle^{s}\widehat{u}\|_{\ell^{2}_{\xi}L^{1}_{\tau}}
. If we directly apply the same approach to the equation (
1.1
), we would need the space
X^{s,1-\frac{1}{\alpha}}
. However, the reduced modulation exponent
1-\frac{1}{\alpha}
causes many difficulties by limiting the full strength of Strichartz estimates. In the present work, rather than utilizing this space, we use the alternative space
Y^{s}
defined by the norm

\displaystyle\|u\|_{Y^{s}}:=\|\mathbb{P}_{D_{1}}u\|_{X^{s-1+\frac{\alpha}{2}-\epsilon,\frac{1}{2}}}+\|\mathbb{P}_{D_{2}}u\|_{X^{s,\frac{1}{2}-\epsilon}}+\|u\|_{W^{s}},

where
\mathbb{P}_{D_{1}}
and
\mathbb{P}_{D_{2}}
are the spacetime frequency projections defined in Section
7
. The main advantage of the space
Y^{s}
compared to the space
X^{s,1-\frac{1}{\alpha}}
is the flexible usability of Strichartz estimates, which usually require modulations close to
\frac{1}{2}
. This method also improves the local well-posedness result in
[
28
]
; see Appendix
B
.

When

\displaystyle\Omega_{2}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})=3\xi_{1}\xi_{2}(\xi_{1}+\xi_{2}),

Exact algebraic identities like these are no longer available for

Lemma 2.1

[

For

\displaystyle|\Omega_{2}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})|\approx|\xi_{1}^{*}|^{\alpha}\min\left(|\xi_{1}+\xi_{2}|,|\xi_{2}^{*}|\right).

For

\displaystyle\rho_{n}(\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{n}):=\min\left(\langle\xi_{1}^{*}+\xi_{2}^{*}\rangle,\langle\xi_{2}^{*}+\cdots+\xi_{n}^{*}\rangle\right).

Lemma 2.2
.

[
15
, Lemma 4.1]

For
\alpha\geq 1
and
\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\xi_{3}\in\mathbb{Z}
, we have

|\Omega_{3}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\xi_{3})|\approx\max(|\xi_{1}+\xi_{2}|,|\xi_{2}+\xi_{3}|,|\xi_{1}+\xi_{3}|)^{\alpha-1}\\
\times\operatorname{med}(|\xi_{1}+\xi_{2}|,|\xi_{2}+\xi_{3}|,|\xi_{1}+\xi_{3}|)\min(|\xi_{1}+\xi_{2}|,|\xi_{2}+\xi_{3}|,|\xi_{1}+\xi_{3}|).

In particular, for
\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\xi_{3}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\ast}
such that

\displaystyle(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\xi_{3})\notin\{\xi_{1}+\xi_{2}+\xi_{3}=\xi_{i}\textnormal{ for some }1\leq i\leq 3\}\cup\{|\xi_{1}|\approx|\xi_{2}|\approx|\xi_{3}|\},

we have
|\Omega_{3}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\xi_{3})|\gtrsim|\xi_{1}^{*}|^{\alpha}\rho_{3}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\xi_{3})
.

For
n\geq 4
, there is no known factorization representation of

. Instead, we have the following decomposition as in

:

Lemma 2.3
.

For
n\geq 4
, let
\xi=\xi_{1}+\dots+\xi_{n}
. Then at least one of the following holds true:

(a)

|\xi_{1}^{*}|\gg|\xi_{2}^{*}|
and
|\Omega_{n}(\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{n})|\gtrsim|\xi_{1}^{*}|^{\alpha}\langle\xi-\xi_{1}^{*}\rangle
.

(b)

|\xi_{1}^{*}|\gg|\xi_{2}^{*}|
and
|\xi_{3}^{*}|^{\alpha}|\xi_{4}^{*}|\gtrsim|\xi_{1}^{*}|^{\alpha}\langle\xi-\xi_{1}^{*}\rangle
.

(c)

|\xi_{1}^{*}|\gg|\xi_{2}^{*}|
and
\xi=\xi_{1}^{*}
.

(d)

|\xi_{1}^{*}|\approx|\xi_{2}^{*}|
and
|\Omega_{n}(\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{n})|\gtrsim|\xi_{1}^{*}|^{\alpha}\rho_{n}(\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{n})
.

(e)

|\xi_{1}^{*}|\approx|\xi_{2}^{*}|
and
|\xi_{3}^{*}|\gtrsim|\xi|
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality assume that
|\xi_{1}|\geq|\xi_{2}|\geq\dots\geq|\xi_{n}|
. Suppose for a contradiction that the followings hold:

(I)

|\Omega_{n}(\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{n})|\ll|\xi_{1}|^{\alpha}\min\big(|\xi_{1}+\xi_{2}|,|\xi-\xi_{1}|\big)
,

(II)

|\xi_{3}|^{\alpha}|\xi_{4}|\ll|\xi_{1}|^{\alpha}\min\big(|\xi_{1}+\xi_{2}|,|\xi-\xi_{1}|\big)
,

(III)

|\xi|\gg|\xi_{3}|
,

(IV)

\xi\neq\xi_{1}
.

First observe that (III) and (IV) imply
\min\big(|\xi_{1}+\xi_{2}|,|\xi-\xi_{1}|\big)\neq 0
. Let
\widetilde{\xi}_{i}:=\sum_{j=i}^{n}\xi_{j}
. Then

(2.1)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\Omega_{n}(\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{n})&=\omega(\xi)-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\omega(\xi_{i})\\
&=\begin{multlined}(\omega(\xi)-\omega(\xi_{1})-\omega(\xi_{2})-\omega(\widetilde{\xi}_{3}))\\
+(\omega(\widetilde{\xi}_{3})-\omega(\xi_{3})-\omega(\widetilde{\xi}_{4}))+\cdots+(\omega(\widetilde{\xi}_{n-1})-\omega(\xi_{n-1})-\omega(\xi_{n}))\end{multlined}\\
&=\Omega_{3}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\widetilde{\xi}_{3})+\sum_{i=3}^{n-1}\Omega_{2}(\xi_{i},\widetilde{\xi}_{i+1}).\end{split}

By Lemma
2.2
, we have

\displaystyle|\Omega_{3}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\widetilde{\xi}_{3})|\gtrsim|\xi_{1}|^{\alpha-1}\max(|\xi_{1}+\xi_{2}|,|\xi-\xi_{1}|)\min(|\xi_{1}+\xi_{2}|,|\xi-\xi_{1}|).

Note that if
|\xi_{1}+\xi_{2}|\ll|\xi_{1}|
, then by (III) we have
|\xi|\approx|\xi_{1}+\xi_{2}|\ll|\xi_{1}|
, hence
|\xi-\xi_{1}|\approx|\xi_{1}|
. Therefore in any case, we have
\max(|\xi_{1}+\xi_{2}|,|\xi-\xi_{1}|)\approx|\xi_{1}|
and
|\Omega_{3}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\widetilde{\xi}_{3})|\gtrsim|\xi_{1}|^{\alpha}\min\big(|\xi_{1}+\xi_{2}|,|\xi-\xi_{1}|\big)
. This implies by assumption (I) and (
2.1
) that we have

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{i=3}^{n-1}\Omega_{2}(\xi_{i},\widetilde{\xi}_{i+1})\right|\gtrsim|\xi_{1}|^{\alpha}\min\big(|\xi_{1}+\xi_{2}|,|\xi-\xi_{1}|\big).

By Lemma
2.1
, for
3\leq i\leq n
,

\displaystyle|\Omega_{2}(\xi_{i},\widetilde{\xi}_{i+1})|\approx\max(|\xi_{i}|,|\widetilde{\xi}_{i}|,|\widetilde{\xi}_{i+1}|)^{\alpha}\min(|\xi_{i}|,|\widetilde{\xi}_{i}|,|\widetilde{\xi}_{i+1}|)\lesssim|\xi_{3}|^{\alpha}|\xi_{4}|.

Therefore we have
|\xi_{3}|^{\alpha}|\xi_{4}|\gtrsim|\xi_{1}|^{\alpha}\min\big(|\xi_{1}+\xi_{2}|,|\xi-\xi_{1}|\big)
, which contradicts (II). Therefore, at least one of (I), (II), (III), or (IV) must be false. In other words, at least one of the following statements holds true:

(i)

|\Omega_{n}(\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{n})|\gtrsim|\xi_{1}|^{\alpha}\min\big(|\xi_{1}+\xi_{2}|,|\xi-\xi_{1}|\big)
.

(ii)

|\xi_{3}|^{\alpha}|\xi_{4}|\gtrsim|\xi_{1}|^{\alpha}\min\big(|\xi_{1}+\xi_{2}|,|\xi-\xi_{1}|\big)
.

(iii)

|\xi_{3}|\gtrsim|\xi|
.

(iv)

\xi=\xi_{1}
.

If
|\xi_{1}|\gg|\xi_{2}|
, then
\min\big(|\xi_{1}+\xi_{2}|,|\xi-\xi_{1}|\big)=|\xi-\xi_{1}|
, which proves the trichotomy (a), (b) and (c). To show the dichotomy (d) and (e) in the
|\xi_{1}|\approx|\xi_{2}|
case, we demonstrate that (ii) and (iv) can be absorbed into (iii). It is easy to see that (iv) with
|\xi_{1}|\approx|\xi_{2}|
implies (iii), since
\xi=\xi_{1}
, implies
|\xi_{2}|\approx|\xi_{3}|
. Now we assume
|\xi_{1}|\approx|\xi_{2}|
and concentrate on (ii). Observe that if
|\xi_{1}+\xi_{2}|\ll|\xi_{3}|
, then we have
|\xi_{3}|\gtrsim|\xi|
, which implies (iii). Hence we may assume
|\xi_{1}+\xi_{2}|\gtrsim|\xi_{3}|
and replace the inequality in (ii) by

(2.2)

\displaystyle|\xi_{3}|^{\alpha}|\xi_{4}|\gtrsim|\xi_{1}|^{\alpha}\min\big(|\xi_{3}|,|\xi-\xi_{1}|\big).

If
\min(|\xi_{3}|,|\xi-\xi_{1}|)=|\xi_{3}|
, then the inequality (
2.2
) implies (iii). Hence we may only consider the case
\min(|\xi_{3}|,|\xi-\xi_{1}|)=|\xi-\xi_{1}|
. This forces
|\xi_{2}|\approx|\xi_{3}|
to hold by (
2.2
). Therefore, we have
|\xi_{1}|\approx|\xi_{3}|
, which we can absorb into case (iii).
∎

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{(l_{1},\dots,l_{M})\in[d]^{M}}c_{l_{1},\dots,l_{M}}\int_{0}^{t}S(t-t^{\prime})[\mathfrak{N}_{k_{0},\dots,k_{n}}^{l_{1},\dots,l_{M}}(u)(t^{\prime})]dt^{\prime},

where for
m\geq 1
and
\xi\in\mathbb{Z}_{\ast}
we define

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}\left[\mathfrak{B}_{k_{0},\dots,k_{n}}^{l_{1},\dots,l_{m}}(u)\right](\xi):=\sum_{\xi_{1}+\cdots+\xi_{\nu_{n,m}}=\xi}^{\ast}\frac{i\xi\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{D}^{2}_{\nu_{n,m}}}\mathfrak{m}_{k_{0},\dots,k_{n}}^{l_{1},\dots,l_{m}}(\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{\nu_{n,m}})}{\Omega_{\nu_{n,m}}(\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{\nu_{n,m}})}\widehat{u}(\xi_{1})\cdots\widehat{u}(\xi_{\nu_{n,m}}),

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}\left[\mathfrak{R}_{k_{0},\dots,k_{n}}^{l_{1},\dots,l_{m}}(u)\right](\xi):=\sum_{\xi_{1}+\cdots+\xi_{\nu_{n,m}}=\xi}^{\ast}\xi\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{D}^{1}_{\nu_{n,m}}}\mathfrak{m}_{k_{0},\dots,k_{n}}^{l_{1},\dots,l_{m}}(\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{\nu_{n,m}})\widehat{u}(\xi_{1})\cdots\widehat{u}(\xi_{\nu_{n,m}}),

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}\left[\mathfrak{N}_{k_{0},\dots,k_{n}}^{l_{1},\dots,l_{m}}(u)\right](\xi):=\sum_{\xi_{1}+\cdots+\xi_{\nu_{n,m}}=\xi}^{\ast}\xi\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{D}^{2}_{\nu_{n,m}}}\mathfrak{m}_{k_{0},\dots,k_{n}}^{l_{1},\dots,l_{m}}(\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{\nu_{n,m}})\widehat{u}(\xi_{1})\cdots\widehat{u}(\xi_{\nu_{n,m}}),

4.
Preliminary lemmas

In this section, we present some preliminary lemmas we need for the proofs of the results in the next section.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
(x_{m})_{1\leq m\leq n}
be a sequence of real numbers. Then we have

\displaystyle\max\left(|x_{1}|,|x_{1}+x_{2}|,\dots,|x_{1}+x_{2}+\cdots+x_{n}|\right)\approx\max\left(|x_{1}|,|x_{2}|,\dots,|x_{n}|\right).

Proof.

The
\lesssim
direction is trivial. To show the
\gtrsim
direction. let
M:=\max\left(|x_{1}|,|x_{2}|,\dots,|x_{n}|\right)
and
L:=\max\left(|x_{1}|,|x_{1}+x_{2}|,\dots,|x_{1}+x_{2}+\cdots+x_{n}|\right)
. Suppose that
M=|x_{m}|
. If
|x_{m}|\gg|x_{1}+\cdots+x_{m-1}|
, then
M=|x_{m}|\approx|x_{1}+\cdots+x_{m}|\lesssim L
. If
|x_{m}|\approx|x_{1}+\cdots+x_{m-1}|
, then
M=|x_{m}|\approx|x_{1}+\cdots+x_{m-1}|\lesssim L
.
∎

The following two lemmas capture the cancellation property of the
R^{1}_{k_{0},\dots,k_{n}}
terms:

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
(a_{n})_{1\leq n\leq N}
be a finite sequence of real numbers. If
a_{1}+\cdots+a_{N}=0
, then

\displaystyle\sum_{\sigma\in S_{N}}\frac{\mathbbm{1}_{\{a_{\sigma(i)}\neq 0\textnormal{ and }a_{\sigma(1)}+\cdots+a_{\sigma(i)}\neq 0\textnormal{ for all }1\leq i\leq N-1\}}}{a_{\sigma(1)}a_{\sigma(2)}\cdots a_{\sigma(N-1)}}=0.

Proof.

Since

\displaystyle\sum_{\sigma\in S_{N}}\frac{\mathbbm{1}_{\{a_{\sigma(i)}\neq 0\textnormal{ and }a_{\sigma(1)}+\cdots+a_{\sigma(i)}\neq 0\textnormal{ for all }1\leq i\leq N-1\}}}{a_{\sigma(1)}a_{\sigma(2)}\cdots a_{\sigma(N-1)}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathbbm{1}_{\{a_{i}\neq 0\textnormal{ for all }1\leq i\leq N\}}}{a_{1}\cdots a_{N}}\sum_{\sigma\in S_{N}}\mathbbm{1}_{\{a_{\sigma(1)}+\cdots+a_{\sigma(i)}\neq 0\textnormal{ for all }1\leq i\leq N-1\}}a_{\sigma(N)},

it suffices to show that

\displaystyle\sum_{\sigma\in S_{N}}\mathbbm{1}_{\{a_{\sigma(1)}+\cdots+a_{\sigma(i)}=0\textnormal{ for some }1\leq i\leq N-1\}}a_{\sigma(N)}=0.

Note that the left-hand side is equal to

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{N}a_{j}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma\in S_{N}\\
\sigma(N)=j\end{subarray}}\mathbbm{1}_{\{a_{\sigma(1)}+\cdots+a_{\sigma(i)}=0\textnormal{ for some }1\leq i\leq N-1\}}.

Let

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}:=\left\{Z\subseteq\{1,\dots,N\}:\sum_{n\in Z}a_{n}=0\right\}.

For each
1\leq j\leq N
, let
\mathcal{Z}_{j}:=\left\{Z\in\mathcal{Z}:j\notin Z\right\}
. We say that
Z\in\mathcal{Z}_{j}
is
maximal in

\mathcal{Z}_{j}
if
Z
is not a proper subset of another element of
\mathcal{Z}_{j}
. Let
\mathcal{M}_{j}:=\{Z\in\mathcal{Z}_{j}:Z\textnormal{ is maximal in }\mathcal{Z}_{j}\}
. Then

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma\in S_{N}\\
\sigma(N)=j\end{subarray}}\mathbbm{1}_{\{a_{\sigma(1)}+\cdots+a_{\sigma(i)}=0\textnormal{ for some }1\leq i\leq N-1\}}=\sum_{Z\in\mathcal{M}_{j}}\#Z!(N-\#Z-1)!.

Indeed, the left-hand side counts the number of sequences
(b_{n})_{1\leq n\leq N-1}
that rearrange the sequence

a_{1},\dots,a_{j-1},a_{j+1},\dots,a_{N}

such that there exists
1\leq i\leq N-1
with
b_{1}+\cdots+b_{i}=0
. In the right-hand side, we generate such
(b_{n})_{1\leq n\leq N-1}
in the following way: pick
Z\in\mathcal{M}_{j}
, and then permute the set
Z
to get
b_{1},\dots,b_{\#Z}
. Next, permute the set
(\{1,\dots,N\}\setminus\{j\})\setminus Z
to get
b_{\#Z+1},\dots,b_{N-1}
.

Now, let
\mathcal{M}:=\cup_{j}\mathcal{M}_{j}
. We claim that if
Z\in\mathcal{M}
, then we have
Z\in\mathcal{M}_{j}
if and only if
j\notin Z
. The “only if” part is trivial. To show the “if” direction, let
j\notin Z
, and suppose for a contradiction that
Z
is not maximal in
\mathcal{Z}_{j}
. Then there exists
\widetilde{Z}\in\mathcal{Z}_{j}
with
Z\subsetneq\widetilde{Z}
. Take
k
such that
Z\in\mathcal{M}_{k}
. By the maximality of
Z
in
\mathcal{Z}_{k}
, we have
k\in\widetilde{Z}\setminus Z
. Using this fact with
a_{1}+\cdots+a_{N}=0
, we can see that
\widetilde{Z}^{c}\cup Z=(\widetilde{Z}\setminus Z)^{c}\in\mathcal{Z}_{k}
. This implies, by maximality of
Z
in
\mathcal{Z}_{k}
, that
\widetilde{Z}^{c}\subseteq Z
. On the other hand, since
Z\subseteq\widetilde{Z}
, it is trivial that
\widetilde{Z}^{c}\subseteq Z^{c}
. Therefore we have
\widetilde{Z}^{c}\subseteq Z\cap Z^{c}=\varnothing
, hence
\widetilde{Z}=\{1,\dots,N\}
. This contradicts the fact that
j\notin\widetilde{Z}
.

Next, observe that

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{N}a_{j}\sum_{Z\in\mathcal{M}_{j}}\#Z!(N-\#Z-1)!

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\sum_{Z\in\mathcal{M}}a_{j}\mathbbm{1}_{\{Z\in\mathcal{M}_{j}\}}\#Z!(N-\#Z-1)!

\displaystyle=\sum_{Z\in\mathcal{M}}\#Z!(N-\#Z-1)!\sum_{j:Z\in\mathcal{M}_{j}}a_{j}.

However, the claim above implies

\displaystyle\sum_{j:Z\in\mathcal{M}_{j}}a_{j}=\sum_{j\notin Z}a_{j}=-\sum_{j\in Z}a_{j}=0.

This completes the proof.
∎

Before proceeding to the following lemma, it is recommended that a reader revisits subsection
1.2
to remind some basic notations.

Lemma 4.3
.

For
N\geq 1
, consider the multiset
\mathcal{K}:=\{k_{1}^{n_{1}},k_{2}^{n_{2}},\dots,k_{m}^{n_{m}}\}
with
\sum_{j=1}^{m}n_{j}=N
. For
M:=\sum_{j=1}^{m}n_{j}k_{j}
, let
(\xi_{i})_{1\leq i\leq M}
be a sequence of real numbers satisfying
\sum_{i=1}^{M}\xi_{i}=0
. For each
\pi\in\textnormal{Perm}(\mathcal{K})
, let

\displaystyle x_{\pi,1}:=\sum_{j=1}^{\pi(1)}\xi_{j},

\displaystyle x_{\pi,i}:=\sum_{j=\pi(1)+\cdots+\pi(i-1)+1}^{\pi(1)+\cdots+\pi(i)}\xi_{j},\quad 2\leq i\leq N.

Then we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\sigma\in S_{M}}\sigma\cdot\left[\sum_{\pi\in\textnormal{Perm}(\mathcal{K})}\frac{\mathbbm{1}_{\{x_{\pi,i}\neq 0\textnormal{ and }x_{\pi,1}+x_{\pi,2}+\cdots+x_{\pi,i}\neq 0\textnormal{ for all }1\leq i\leq N-1\}}}{x_{\pi,1}(x_{\pi,1}+x_{\pi,2})\cdots(x_{\pi,1}+x_{\pi,2}+\cdots+x_{\pi,N-1})}\right]=0,

where
\sigma\in S_{M}
permutes the indices of
(\xi_{i})_{1\leq i\leq M}
.

Proof.

Define
e\in\textnormal{Perm}(\mathcal{K})
by

\displaystyle e(n_{1}+\cdots+n_{p-1}+q)=k_{p}\textnormal{ for each }1\leq p\leq m\textnormal{ and }1\leq q\leq n_{p},

and let
u_{i}:=x_{e,i}
for
1\leq i\leq N
. Define

G:=\big\{\theta\in S_{N}:\theta(n_{1}+\cdots+n_{p-1}+q)<\theta(n_{1}+\cdots+n_{p-1}+r)\\
\textnormal{ for each }1\leq p\leq m\textnormal{ and }1\leq q<r\leq n_{p}\big\}.

By rearranging the indices, we have

\sum_{\sigma\in S_{M}}\sigma\cdot\left[\sum_{\pi\in\textnormal{Perm}(\mathcal{K})}\frac{\mathbbm{1}_{\{x_{\pi,i}\neq 0\textnormal{ and }x_{\pi,1}+x_{\pi,2}+\cdots+x_{\pi,i}\neq 0\textnormal{ for all }1\leq i\leq N-1\}}}{x_{\pi,1}(x_{\pi,1}+x_{\pi,2})\cdots(x_{\pi,1}+x_{\pi,2}+\cdots+x_{\pi,N-1})}\right]\\
=\sum_{\sigma\in S_{M}}\sigma\cdot\left[\sum_{\theta\in G}\frac{\mathbbm{1}_{\{u_{\theta(i)}\neq 0\textnormal{ and }u_{\theta(1)}+u_{\theta(2)}+\cdots+u_{\theta(i)}\neq 0\textnormal{ for all }1\leq i\leq N-1\}}}{u_{\theta(1)}(u_{\theta(1)}+u_{\theta(2)})\cdots(u_{\theta(1)}+u_{\theta(2)}+\cdots+u_{\theta({N-1})})}\right].

For
\theta\in G
, write
\mathbbm{1}_{\theta}:=\mathbbm{1}_{\{u_{\theta(i)}\neq 0\textnormal{ and }u_{\theta(1)}+u_{\theta(2)}+\cdots+u_{\theta(i)}\neq 0\textnormal{ for all }1\leq i\leq N-1\}}
. Then

\displaystyle\sum_{\theta\in G}\frac{\mathbbm{1}_{\theta}}{u_{\theta(1)}(u_{\theta(1)}+u_{\theta(2)})\cdots(u_{\theta(1)}+u_{\theta(2)}+\cdots+u_{\theta({N-1})})}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\theta\in G}\mathbbm{1}_{\theta}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-u_{\theta(1)}x_{1}}dx_{1}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-(u_{\theta(1)}+u_{\theta(2)})x_{2}}dx_{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\cdots e^{-(u_{\theta(1)}+\cdots+u_{\theta({N-1})})x_{N-1}}dx_{N-1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\theta\in G}\mathbbm{1}_{\theta}\int_{[0,\infty)^{m-1}}e^{-u_{\theta(1)}(x_{1}+\cdots+x_{N-1})}e^{-u_{\theta(2)}(x_{2}+\cdots+x_{N-1})}\cdots e^{-u_{\theta({N-1})}x_{N-1}}dx_{1}dx_{2}\dots dx_{N-1}

\displaystyle=\int_{0<y_{N-1}<\dots<y_{1}<\infty}\sum_{\theta\in G}\mathbbm{1}_{\theta}e^{-u_{\theta(1)}y_{1}-u_{\theta(2)}y_{2}-\cdots-u_{\theta({N-1)}}y_{N-1}}dy_{1}dy_{2}\dots dy_{N-1},

where in the last equality we changed the variables by setting
y_{j}=x_{j}+\cdots+x_{N-1}
for each
1\leq j\leq N-1
.

For each
1\leq p\leq m
,
1\leq q\leq n_{p}
, and
1\leq r\leq k_{p}
, write
n_{p,q,r}:=\sum_{i=1}^{p-1}n_{i}k_{i}+(q-1)k_{p}+r
. Define

H:=\big\{\sigma\in S_{M}:\textnormal{for each }1\leq p\leq m\textnormal{ and }1\leq q\leq n_{p},\\
\textnormal{ there exists }1\leq q^{\prime}\leq n_{p}\textnormal{ such that }\sigma(n_{p,q,r})=n_{p,q^{\prime},r}\textnormal{ for all }1\leq r\leq k_{p}\big\}.

Let

\displaystyle f(y_{1},\dots,y_{N-1}):=\sum_{\sigma\in S_{M}}\sigma\cdot\left[\sum_{\theta\in G}\mathbbm{1}_{\theta}e^{-\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}u_{\theta(i)}y_{i}}\right].

Then we have

\displaystyle f(y_{1},\dots,y_{N-1})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|H|}\sum_{\sigma\in S_{M}}\sigma\cdot\left[\sum_{\tau\in H}\tau\cdot\sum_{\theta\in G}\mathbbm{1}_{\theta}e^{-\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}u_{\theta(i)}y_{i}}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|H|}\sum_{\sigma\in S_{M}}\sigma\cdot\left[\sum_{\theta\in S_{N}}\mathbbm{1}_{\theta}e^{-\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}u_{\theta(i)}y_{i}}\right].

Thus the function
f
is symmetric. Using the symmetry of
f
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{0<y_{N-1}<\dots<y_{1}<\infty}f(y_{1},\dots,y_{N-1})dy_{1}dy_{2}\dots dy_{N-1}

\displaystyle\quad=\frac{1}{(N-1)!}\int_{[0,\infty)^{N-1}}f(y_{1},\dots,y_{N-1})dy_{1}dy_{2}\dots dy_{N-1}

\displaystyle\quad=\frac{1}{|H|(N-1)!}\sum_{\sigma\in S_{M}}\sigma\cdot\left[\sum_{\theta\in S_{N}}\frac{\mathbbm{1}_{\theta}}{u_{\theta(1)}u_{\theta(2)}\cdots u_{\theta({N-1})}}\right].

However, the last line vanishes by Lemma
4.2
, and the desired result follows.
∎

Lemma 5.1
.

3.6
), and for simplicity assume that

\displaystyle\left|\mu^{j_{1},\dots,j_{n}}_{k_{0},\dots,k_{n}}(\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{\nu_{n}})\right|

|\xi_{2}^{*}+\xi_{3}^{*}|\gtrsim|\xi_{4}^{*}|
. Indeed, if
|\xi_{2}^{*}+\xi_{3}^{*}|\ll|\xi_{4}^{*}|

Now we can see that

Next we estimate the symbol associated with the near-resonant term

Lemma 5.3
.

We have

(5.4)

\displaystyle\widehat{R^{1}_{2,2}(u)}(\xi)=\sum_{\xi_{1}+\xi_{2}+\xi_{3}=\xi}m(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\xi_{3})\widehat{u}_{\xi_{1}}\widehat{u}_{\xi_{2}}\widehat{u}_{\xi_{3}}

for some
m:\mathbb{Z}_{\ast}^{3}\to\mathbb{C}
such that
\operatorname{supp}(m)\subseteq\mathcal{R}^{1}_{3}
and
|m(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\xi_{3})|\lesssim|\xi_{1}^{*}|^{1-\alpha}
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{A}:=\{(\eta,\xi)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}:\eta>0,\,\xi\neq 0,\,|\xi|\neq\eta\}
and

\displaystyle g(\eta,\xi):=\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{A}}(\eta,\xi)\xi\left[\frac{\xi-\eta}{\Omega_{2}(\eta,-\xi)}+\frac{\xi+\eta}{\Omega_{2}(-\eta,-\xi)}\right].

Direct computation gives

\displaystyle\widehat{R^{1}_{2,2}(u)}(\xi)=\frac{i\xi^{2}}{4(2^{\alpha}-1)\omega(\xi)}|\widehat{u}_{\xi}|^{2}\widehat{u}_{\xi}+\frac{i}{2}\sum_{\eta>0}g(\eta,\xi)\widehat{u}_{\xi}\widehat{u}_{\eta}\widehat{u}_{-\eta}.

It suffices to show that
|g(\eta,\xi)|\lesssim\max(|\xi|,|\eta|)^{1-\alpha}
. Note that

\displaystyle g(\eta,\xi)=\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{A}}(\eta,\xi)\xi\frac{2(|\eta|^{\alpha+2}-|\xi|^{\alpha+2})-(\eta^{2}-\xi^{2})(|\eta+\xi|^{\alpha}+|\eta-\xi|^{\alpha})}{\Omega_{2}(\eta,-\xi)\Omega_{2}(-\eta,-\xi)}.

Case 1:
\eta\gg|\xi|
.

Let
t=\xi/\eta
. Then

\displaystyle|g(\eta,\xi)|

\displaystyle\approx|\eta|^{-2\alpha}|\xi|^{-1}|2(|\eta|^{\alpha+2}-|\xi|^{\alpha+2})-(\eta^{2}-\xi^{2})(|\eta+\xi|^{\alpha}+|\eta-\xi|^{\alpha})|

\displaystyle\lesssim|\eta|^{2-\alpha}|\xi|^{-1}|2(1-|t|^{\alpha+2})-(1-t^{2})(|1+t|^{\alpha}+|1-t|^{\alpha})|

\displaystyle=|\eta|^{2-\alpha}|\xi|^{-1}|2(1-|t|^{\alpha+2})-(1-t^{2})(2+O(|t|))|

\displaystyle\lesssim|\xi||\eta|^{-\alpha}.

Case 2:
|\xi|\gg\eta
.

Let
t=\eta/\xi
. Then

\displaystyle|g(\eta,\xi)|

\displaystyle\approx|\eta|^{-2}|\xi|^{1-2\alpha}|2(|\eta|^{\alpha+2}-|\xi|^{\alpha+2})-(\eta^{2}-\xi^{2})(|\eta+\xi|^{\alpha}+|\eta-\xi|^{\alpha})|

\displaystyle=|\eta|^{-2}|\xi|^{3-\alpha}\left|2(|t|^{\alpha+2}-1)-(t^{2}-1)(|t+1|^{\alpha}+|t-1|^{\alpha})\right|

\displaystyle=|\eta|^{-2}|\xi|^{3-\alpha}\left|2(|t|^{\alpha+2}-1)-(t^{2}-1)(2+O(|t|))\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim|\xi|^{1-\alpha}.

Case 3:
|\xi|\approx\eta
.

Without loss of generality assume that
\xi\eta>0
. By Lemma
2.1
, we have

\displaystyle|\Omega_{2}(\eta,-\xi)\Omega_{2}(-\eta,-\xi)|\gtrsim|\xi|^{2\alpha}|\xi^{2}-\eta^{2}|\approx|\xi|^{2\alpha+1}|\xi-\eta|.

Using this and the mean value theorem, we have

\displaystyle\left|\frac{|\eta|^{\alpha+2}-|\xi|^{\alpha+2}}{\Omega_{2}(\eta,-\xi)\Omega_{2}(-\eta,-\xi)}\right|\lesssim|\xi|^{-\alpha},

and

\displaystyle\frac{|\eta^{2}-\xi^{2}|(|\eta+\xi|^{\alpha}+|\eta-\xi|^{\alpha})}{|\Omega_{2}(\eta,-\xi)\Omega_{2}(-\eta,-\xi)|}\lesssim|\xi|^{-\alpha}.

Therefore
|g(\eta,\xi)|\lesssim|\xi|^{1-\alpha}
.
∎

Multilinear estimates

In this subsection, we present estimates of the terms that can be examined with standard multilinear analysis. Based on the pointwise estimates in Section
5
, the proofs below are mostly repetitive applications of basic inequalities like Young’s convolution inequality

\displaystyle\|f\ast g\|_{L^{r}}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{p}}\|g\|_{L^{q}}\textnormal{ for }1\leq p,q,r\leq\infty\textnormal{ and }\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=1+\frac{1}{r},

or Bernstein’s inequality

\displaystyle\|P_{N}f\|_{L^{q}}\lesssim N^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}}\|P_{N}f\|_{L^{p}}\textnormal{ for }1\leq p\leq q\leq\infty.

The estimates for the terms
R^{2}_{k_{0},\dots,k_{n}}(u)
and
\mathfrak{R}_{k_{0},\dots,k_{n}}^{l_{1},\dots,l_{m}}(u)
require different techniques, hence we postpone these estimates to Subsection
6.3
.

6.1.1.
Boundary terms

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
m,n\geq 0
,
(k_{0},\dots,k_{n})\in[d]^{n+1}
, and
(l_{1},\dots,l_{m})\in[d]^{m}
.

(a)

For
s>\frac{3}{2}-\alpha
,
a<n\big(s+\alpha-\frac{3}{2}\big)
and
a\leq n(\alpha-1)
, we have

\displaystyle\|B_{k_{0},\dots,k_{n}}(u)\|_{H^{s+a}}\lesssim\|u\|_{H^{s}}^{\nu_{n}}.

(b)

For
s>\frac{1}{2}
and
a\leq(n+m+1)(\alpha-1)
, we have

\displaystyle\|\mathfrak{B}_{k_{0},\dots,k_{n}}^{l_{1},\dots,l_{m}}(u)\|_{H^{s+a}}\lesssim\|u\|_{H^{s}}^{\nu_{n,m}}.

Proof.

Consider (a). By Lemma
5.1
(a), we have

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\xi\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{N}_{\nu_{n}}}\mu_{k_{0},\dots,k_{n}}(\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{\nu_{n}})}{\Omega_{\nu_{n}}(\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{\nu_{n}})}\right|\lesssim|\xi_{1}^{*}|^{-n(\alpha-1)}.

Hence by Young’s convolution inequality,

\displaystyle\left\|B_{k_{0},\dots,k_{n}}(u)\right\|_{H^{s+a}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Bigg\|\sum_{\xi_{1}+\cdots+\xi_{\nu_{n}}=\xi}|\xi_{1}^{*}|^{s+a+n(1-\alpha)}|\widehat{u}(\xi_{1})\cdots\widehat{u}(\xi_{\nu_{n}})|\Bigg\|_{\ell^{2}_{\xi}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Bigg\|\sum_{\xi_{1}+\cdots+\xi_{\nu_{n}}=\xi}|\xi_{1}^{*}|^{s}|\xi_{2}^{*}\cdots\xi_{\nu_{n}}^{*}|^{\frac{1}{n}(a+n(1-\alpha))}|\widehat{u}(\xi_{1})\cdots\widehat{u}(\xi_{\nu_{n}})|\Bigg\|_{\ell^{1}_{\xi}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|u\right\|_{H^{s}}\left\|u\right\|_{H^{\frac{1}{n}(a+n(1-\alpha))+\frac{1}{2}+}}^{\nu_{n}-1}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u\|_{H^{s}}^{\nu_{n}}.

Next we consider (b). By Lemma
5.1
(b), we have

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\xi\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{D}^{2}_{\nu_{n,m}}}\mathfrak{m}_{k_{0},\dots,k_{n}}^{l_{1}\dots,l_{m}}(\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{\nu_{n}})}{\Omega_{\nu_{n}}(\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{\nu_{n}})}\right|\lesssim|\xi|^{-(n+m+1)(\alpha-1)}.

Since
a-(n+m+1)(\alpha-1)\leq 0
, by Young’s convolution inequality

\displaystyle\left\|\mathfrak{B}_{k_{0},\dots,k_{n}}^{l_{1},\dots,l_{m}}(u)\right\|_{H^{s+a}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|\sum_{\xi_{1}+\cdots+\xi_{\nu_{n,m}}=\xi}|\xi|^{s+a-(n+m+1)(\alpha-1)}|\widehat{u}(\xi_{1})\cdots\widehat{u}(\xi_{\nu_{n,m}})|\right\|_{\ell^{2}_{\xi}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|u\right\|_{H^{s}}\left\|\widehat{u}\right\|_{\ell^{1}_{\xi}}^{\nu_{n,m}-1}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|u\right\|_{H^{s}}\left\|u\right\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}+}}^{\nu_{n,m}-1}.

∎

6.1.2.
Near-resonant terms

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
N\geq 2
. Consider the multiset
\mathcal{K}:=\{k_{1}^{n_{1}},\dots,k_{m}^{n_{m}}\}
with
\sum_{j=1}^{m}n_{j}=N
. Let
M:=1+\sum_{j=1}^{m}n_{j}(k_{j}-1)
and suppose that
M\geq 4
. If
\mathcal{K}=\{2^{n_{1}},3^{n_{2}}\}
, let
a\leq(N-1)(\alpha-1)
and

\displaystyle\begin{cases}s>\frac{1}{2}-\frac{(N-1)(\alpha-1)}{M-1},\\
a<\min\big((M-1)\big(s-\frac{1}{2}),2s-1\big)+(N-1)(\alpha-1).\end{cases}

Otherwise, let
a\leq(N-1)(\alpha-1)
and

\displaystyle\begin{cases}s>\max\big(\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{3\alpha}-\frac{(N-1)(\alpha-1)}{3}\big),\\
a<\min\big(3s-\frac{3}{2}-\frac{1}{\alpha},2s-1\big)+(N-1)(\alpha-1),\end{cases}

Then we have

\displaystyle\Bigg\|\sum_{\theta\in\textnormal{Perm}(\mathcal{K})}R^{1}_{\theta(1),\dots,\theta(N)}(u)\Bigg\|_{H^{s+a}}\lesssim\|u\|_{H^{s}}^{M}.

Proof.

First consider the case
\mathcal{K}=\{2^{n_{1}},3^{n_{2}}\}
. Note that by the assumption made on
s
and
a
in the statement of this lemma, we have
s\geq 0
,
a-(N-1)(\alpha-1)\leq 0
,
a+1-(N-1)(\alpha-1)-2s\leq 0
, and
s>\frac{1+a-(N-1)(\alpha-1)-2s}{M-3}+\frac{1}{2}
. Also, since
\xi_{2}^{*}+\cdots+\xi_{M}^{*}=0
, we have
|\xi_{2}^{*}|\approx|\xi_{3}^{*}|
. Assume for simplicity that
|\xi_{1}|\geq|\xi_{2}|\geq\dots\geq|\xi_{M}|
. Using (
5.5
) and Young’s convolution inequality, we have

\displaystyle\Bigg\|\sum_{\theta\in\textnormal{Perm}(\mathcal{K})}R^{1}_{\theta(1),\dots,\theta(N)}(u)\Bigg\|_{H^{s+a}}

\displaystyle\quad\lesssim\bigg\|\sum_{\xi_{1}+\cdots+\xi_{M}=\xi}\mathbbm{1}_{\{\xi=\xi_{1}\}}|\xi_{1}|^{s+a-(N-1)(\alpha-1)}|\xi_{2}|\left|\widehat{u}(\xi_{1})\cdots\widehat{u}(\xi_{M})\right|\bigg\|_{\ell^{2}_{\xi}}

\displaystyle\quad\lesssim\|u\|_{H^{s}}\bigg\|\sum_{\xi_{2}+\cdots+\xi_{M}=\xi}|\xi_{2}\xi_{3}|^{s}|\widehat{u}(\xi_{2})\widehat{u}(\xi_{3})|\prod_{l=4}^{M}|\xi_{l}|^{\frac{1+a-(N-1)(\alpha-1)-2s}{M-3}}|\widehat{u}(\xi_{l})|\bigg\|_{\ell^{\infty}_{\xi}}

\displaystyle\quad\lesssim\|u\|_{H^{s}}^{3}\|u\|_{H^{\frac{1+a-(N-1)(\alpha-1)-2s}{M-3}+\frac{1}{2}+}}^{M-3}

\displaystyle\quad\lesssim\|u\|_{H^{s}}^{M}.

If
\mathcal{K}\neq\{2^{n_{1}},3^{n_{2}}\}
, assuming
|\xi_{1}|\geq|\xi_{2}|\geq\dots\geq|\xi_{M}|
,

\displaystyle\Bigg\|\sum_{\theta\in\textnormal{Perm}(\mathcal{K})}R^{1}_{\theta(1),\dots,\theta(N)}(u)\Bigg\|_{H^{s+a}}

\displaystyle\quad\lesssim\bigg\||\xi|^{s}|\widehat{u}(\xi)|\sum_{\xi_{2}+\cdots+\xi_{M}=0}|\xi_{2}|^{1+a-(N-1)(\alpha-1)}|\xi_{4}|^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}|\widehat{u}(\xi_{2})\cdots\widehat{u}(\xi_{M})|\bigg\|_{\ell^{2}_{\xi}}

\displaystyle\quad\lesssim\|u\|_{H^{s}}\bigg\|\sum_{\xi_{2}+\cdots+\xi_{M}=\xi}|\xi_{2}\xi_{3}|^{s}|\widehat{u}(\xi_{2})\widehat{u}(\xi_{3})|\xi_{4}|^{1+\frac{1}{\alpha}+a-(N-1)(\alpha-1)-2s}|\widehat{u}(\xi_{4})\cdots\widehat{u}(\xi_{M})|\bigg\|_{\ell^{\infty}_{\xi}}

\displaystyle\quad\lesssim\|u\|_{H^{s}}^{3}\|u\|_{H^{1+\frac{1}{\alpha}+a-(N-1)(\alpha-1)-2s+\frac{1}{2}+}}\|u\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}+}}^{M-4}

\displaystyle\quad\lesssim\|u\|_{H^{s}}^{M},

as desired.
∎

6.1.3.
Non-resonant terms

Lemma 6.3
.

Let
(k_{0}\dots,k_{n})\in[d]^{n+1}
and
(l_{1},\dots,l_{m})\in[d]^{m}
.

(a)

Let
n\geq 2
and
\frac{n+1}{n}<\alpha<2
. For
s>\frac{3}{2}-\frac{n\alpha}{n+1}
and
a<\min\big(n(\alpha-1)-1,(n+1)(s-\frac{3}{2})+n\alpha\big)
, we have

\displaystyle\|N_{k_{0},\dots,k_{n}}(u)\|_{H^{s+a}}\lesssim\|u\|_{H^{s}}^{\nu_{n}}.

(b)

Let
m+n\geq 1
and
\frac{m+n+1}{m+n}<\alpha<2
. For
s>\frac{1}{2}
and
a\leq(m+n)(\alpha-1)-1
, we have

\displaystyle\|\mathfrak{N}_{k_{0},\dots,k_{n}}^{l_{1},\dots,l_{m}}(u)\|_{H^{s+a}}\lesssim\|u\|_{H^{s}}^{\nu_{n,m}}.

Proof.

We only consider (a), since (b) can be similarly proved. Using Lemma
5.1
(a), we have

\displaystyle\|N_{k_{0},\dots,k_{n}}(u)\|_{H^{s+a}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\bigg\||\xi|^{s+a+1-n(\alpha-1)}\sum_{\xi_{1}+\cdots+\xi_{\nu_{n}}=\xi}|\widehat{u}(\xi_{1})\cdots\widehat{u}(\xi_{\nu_{n}})|\bigg\|_{\ell^{2}_{\xi}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\bigg\|\sum_{\xi_{1}+\cdots+\xi_{\nu_{n}}=\xi}|\xi_{1}^{*}|^{s}|\xi_{2}^{*}\cdots\xi_{\nu_{n}}^{*}|^{\frac{n-n\alpha+a+1}{n+1}}|\widehat{u}(\xi_{1})\cdots\widehat{u}(\xi_{\nu_{n}})|\bigg\|_{\ell^{2}_{\xi}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u\|_{H^{s}}\|u\|_{H^{\frac{n-n\alpha+a+1}{n+1}+\frac{1}{2}+}}^{\nu_{n}-1}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u\|_{H^{s}}^{\nu_{n}}.

The last line is acceptable as long as
s>\frac{1}{2}+\frac{n-n\alpha+a+1}{n+1}
.
∎

6.2.
Estimates for the
\textnormal{deg}(P)=2
case

For the
\textnormal{deg}(P)=2
case, finer analysis significantly improves some of the estimates in the previous subsection.

6.2.1.
Boundary terms

Lemma 6.4
.

For
s>\frac{1}{2}-\alpha
and
a<\min(\alpha,s+\alpha-\frac{1}{2})
we have

\displaystyle\|B_{2}(u)\|_{H^{s+a}}\lesssim\|u\|_{H^{s}}^{2}.

Proof.

By symmetry we may only consider the cases
|\xi_{2}|\leq|\xi_{1}|\leq 2|\xi_{2}|
and
|\xi_{1}|\geq 2|\xi_{2}|
.

Case 1:
|\xi_{2}|\leq|\xi_{1}|\leq 2|\xi_{2}|
.

In this case we have
|\xi|\lesssim|\xi_{1}|\approx|\xi_{2}|
. Also by Lemma
2.1

|\Omega_{2}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})|\approx|\xi_{1}|^{\alpha}|\xi_{2}|\approx|\xi_{2}|^{\alpha+1}
. Thus for
-s+a-\alpha+\frac{1}{2}<0
we have

\displaystyle\left\||\xi|^{s+a+1}\sum_{|\xi_{2}|\leq|\xi_{1}|\leq 2|\xi_{2}|}\frac{|\widehat{u}_{\xi_{1}}\widehat{u}_{\xi_{2}}|}{|\Omega_{2}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})|}\right\|_{\ell^{2}_{\xi}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|\sum_{|\xi_{2}|\leq|\xi_{1}|\leq 2|\xi_{2}|}|\xi|^{-s+a-\alpha+\frac{1}{2}+}|\xi_{1}|^{s}|\widehat{u}_{\xi_{1}}|\frac{|\xi_{2}|^{s}|\widehat{u}_{\xi_{2}}|}{|\xi_{2}|^{\frac{1}{2}+}}\right\|_{\ell^{2}_{\xi}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\sum_{|\xi_{2}|\leq|\xi_{1}|\leq 2|\xi_{2}|}|\xi_{1}|^{s}|\widehat{u}_{\xi_{1}}||\xi_{2}|^{s-\frac{1}{2}-}|\widehat{u}_{\xi_{2}}|\right\|_{\ell^{2}_{\xi}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\||\xi|^{s}|\widehat{u}_{\xi}|\|_{\ell^{2}_{\xi}}\left\||\xi|^{s-\frac{1}{2}-}|\widehat{u}_{\xi}|\right\|_{\ell^{1}_{\xi}}

\displaystyle\leq\|u\|_{H^{s}}^{2}.

Case 2:
|\xi_{1}|\geq 2|\xi_{2}|
.

In this case we have
|\xi_{2}|\lesssim|\xi|\approx|\xi_{1}|
and
|\Omega_{2}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})|\approx|\xi_{1}|^{\alpha}|\xi_{2}|
. Thus for
a\leq\alpha
and
s>a-\alpha+\frac{1}{2}
we have

\displaystyle\left\||\xi|^{s+a}\sum_{|\xi_{1}|\geq 2|\xi_{2}|}\frac{|\widehat{u}_{\xi_{1}}\widehat{u}_{\xi_{2}}|}{|\Omega_{2}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})|}\right\|_{\ell^{2}_{\xi}}\lesssim\left\|\sum_{|\xi_{1}|\geq 2|\xi_{2}|}|\xi_{2}|^{-s+a-\alpha-\frac{1}{2}+}|\xi_{1}|^{s}|\widehat{u}_{\xi_{1}}|\xi_{2}|^{s-\frac{1}{2}-}|\widehat{u}_{\xi_{2}}|\right\|_{\ell^{2}_{\xi}},

and the right-hand side can be bounded by
\|u\|_{H^{s}}^{2}
as in the first case.
∎

Lemma 6.5
.

Let
m,n\geq 0
,
k_{0}=\cdots=k_{n}=2
and
l_{1}=\cdots=l_{m}=2
. For
s>\frac{3}{2}-\alpha
,
a\leq\alpha-1
, and
a<2s+2\alpha-2
, we have

\displaystyle\|\mathfrak{B}_{k_{0},\dots,k_{n}}^{l_{1},\dots,l_{m}}(u)\|_{H^{s+a}}\lesssim\|u\|_{H^{s}}^{n+m+2}.

Proof.

We need to show that the quantity

(6.1)

\displaystyle\left\|\sum_{\xi_{1}+\cdots+\xi_{n+m+2}=\xi}^{\ast}\frac{|\xi|^{1+s+a}\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{D}^{2}_{n+m+2}}\big|\mathfrak{m}_{k_{0},\dots,k_{n}}^{l_{0},\dots,l_{m}}(\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{n+m+2})\big|}{|\xi_{1}\cdots\xi_{n+m+2}|^{s}|\Omega_{n+m+2}(\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{n+m+2})|}|\widehat{u}(\xi_{1})\cdots\widehat{u}(\xi_{n+m+2})|\right\|_{\ell^{2}_{\xi}}

is less than
\|u\|_{H^{s}}^{n+m+2}
. By duality, (
6.1
) is equal to

(6.2)

\sup_{\|v\|_{L^{2}}=1}\sum_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{n+m+2}}^{\ast}\frac{|\xi|^{s+a+1}\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{D}^{2}_{n+m+2}}\big|\mathfrak{m}_{k_{0},\dots,k_{n}}^{l_{0},\dots,l_{m}}\big|}{|\xi_{1}\cdots\xi_{n+m+2}|^{s}|\Omega_{n+m+2}(\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{n+m+2})|}\\
\times|\widehat{u}(\xi_{1})\cdots\widehat{u}(\xi_{n+m+2})\widehat{v}(\xi_{1}+\cdots+\xi_{n+m+2})|.

For each
1\leq i\leq n+m+2
, let

\displaystyle\zeta_{i}:=\max\left(|\xi_{i}^{*}|,|\xi|\right).

By Lemma
5.1
(c), for
2-2\alpha-2s+a<0
we have

\displaystyle\frac{|\xi|^{s+a+1}\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{D}^{2}_{n+m+2}}\big|\mathfrak{m}_{k_{0},\dots,k_{n}}^{l_{0},\dots,l_{m}}\big|}{|\xi_{1}\cdots\xi_{n+m+2}|^{s}|\Omega_{n+m+2}(\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{n+m+2})|}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{|\xi|^{1-\alpha+s+a}|\zeta_{3}\cdots\zeta_{n+m+2}|^{-(\alpha-1)}}{|\xi_{1}^{*}\cdots\xi_{n+m+2}^{*}|^{s}\rho_{n+m+2}(\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{n+m+2})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{|\xi|^{2-2\alpha-2s+a-}}{|\xi_{4}^{*}\cdots\xi_{n+m+2}^{*}|^{s-\alpha+1}|\rho_{n+m+2}(\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{n+m+2})|^{1+}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{|\xi_{4}^{*}\cdots\xi_{n+m+2}^{*}|^{s-\alpha+1}|\rho_{n+m+2}(\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{n+m+2})|^{1+}}.

If
\rho_{n+m+2}=\langle\xi_{1}^{*}+\xi_{2}^{*}\rangle
, then by Cauchy-Schwarz, the sum in (
6.2
) is dominated by

\left(\sum_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{n+m+2}}^{\ast}\frac{|\widehat{u}(\xi_{1}^{*})\widehat{u}(\xi_{3}^{*})\cdots\widehat{u}(\xi_{n+m+2}^{*})|^{2}}{\langle\xi_{1}^{*}+\xi_{2}^{*}\rangle^{1+}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
\times\left(\sum_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{n+m+2}}^{\ast}\frac{|\widehat{v}(\xi_{1}^{*}+\cdots+\xi_{n+m+2}^{*})\widehat{u}(\xi_{2}^{*})|^{2}}{\langle\xi_{1}^{*}+\xi_{2}^{*}\rangle^{1+}|\xi_{4}^{*}\cdots\xi_{n+m+2}^{*}|^{s+\alpha-1}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}=:A\times B.

We sum
A
in the order
\xi_{2}^{*},\xi_{1}^{*},\xi_{3}^{*},\xi_{4}^{*},\dots,\xi_{n+m+2}^{*}
, and sum
B
in the order
\xi_{3}^{*},\xi_{1}^{*},\xi_{2}^{*},\xi_{4}^{*},\dots,\xi_{n+m+2}^{*}
.

If
\rho_{n+m+2}=\langle\xi_{2}^{*}+\cdots+\xi_{n+m+2}^{*}\rangle
, then (
6.2
) is less than

\left(\sum_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{n+m+2}}^{\ast}\frac{|\widehat{u}(\xi_{1}^{*})\widehat{u}(\xi_{3}^{*})\cdots\widehat{u}(\xi_{n+m+2}^{*})|^{2}}{\langle\xi_{2}^{*}+\cdots+\xi_{n+m+2}^{*}\rangle^{1+}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
\times\left(\sum_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{n+m+2}}^{\ast}\frac{|\widehat{v}(\xi_{1}^{*}+\cdots+\xi_{n+m+2}^{*})\widehat{u}(\xi_{2}^{*})|^{2}}{\langle\xi_{2}^{*}+\cdots+\xi_{n+m+2}^{*}\rangle^{1+}|\xi_{4}^{*}\cdots\xi_{n+m+2}^{*}|^{s+\alpha-1}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

The second summation is taken in the order
\xi_{1}^{*},\xi_{3}^{*},\xi_{2}^{*},\xi_{4}^{*},\dots,\xi_{n+m+2}^{*}
.
∎

6.2.2.
Near-resonant terms

Lemma 6.6
.

Let
s\geq\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\alpha}{2}
, and
a\leq\min(2s+\alpha-1,\alpha-1)
. Then

\displaystyle\left\|R^{1}_{2,2}(u)\right\|_{H^{s+a}}\lesssim\|u\|_{H^{s}}^{3}.

Proof.

Using (
5.4
), we have for
s\geq\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\alpha}{2}
, and
a\leq 2s+\alpha-1
,

\displaystyle\left\|R^{1}_{2,2}(u)\right\|_{H^{s+a}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\||\xi|^{s+a}\sum_{\xi_{1}+\xi_{2}+\xi_{3}=\xi}\mathbbm{1}_{\{\xi=\xi_{1}\}}|\xi_{1}^{*}|^{1-\alpha}\left|\widehat{u}(\xi_{1})\widehat{u}(\xi_{2})\widehat{u}(\xi_{3})\right|\right\|_{\ell^{2}_{\xi}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u\|_{H^{s}}\sum_{\xi\in\mathbb{Z}}|\xi|^{a+1-\alpha}\left|\widehat{u}(\xi)\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u\|_{H^{s}}^{3}

∎

Lemma 6.7
.

For
s>1-\frac{\alpha}{2}
and
a<2s+\alpha-2
, we have

\displaystyle\|R^{2}_{2,2}(u)\|_{H^{s+a}}\lesssim\|u\|_{H^{s}}^{3}.

Proof.

This easily follows by Lemma
5.2
(a) and Bernstein’s inequality.
∎

Lemma 6.8
.

For
n\geq 2
, let
k_{0}=\cdots=k_{n}=2
. Then for
s>\frac{7}{6}-\frac{2}{3}\alpha
and
a<\min\big(2\alpha-2,3s+2\alpha-\frac{7}{2}\big)
, we have

\displaystyle\|R_{k_{0},\dots,k_{n}}^{2}(u)\|_{H^{s+a}}\lesssim\|u\|_{H^{s}}^{n+2}.

Proof.

By duality, we have

\displaystyle\left\|\sum_{\xi_{1}+\cdots+\xi_{n+2}=\xi}^{\ast}\frac{|\xi_{1}^{*}|^{1+s+a}\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{R}^{2}_{n+2}}|\mu_{k_{0},\dots,k_{n}}|}{|\xi_{1}\cdots\xi_{n+2}|^{s}}|\widehat{u}(\xi_{1})\cdots\widehat{u}(\xi_{n+2})|\right\|_{\ell^{2}_{\xi}}

(6.3)

\displaystyle=\sup_{\|v\|_{L^{2}}=1}\sum_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{n+2}}\frac{|\xi_{1}^{*}|^{1+s+a}\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{R}^{2}_{n+2}}|\mu_{k_{0},\dots,k_{n}}|}{|\xi_{1}\cdots\xi_{n+2}|^{s}}|\widehat{u}(\xi_{1})\cdots\widehat{u}(\xi_{n+2})\widehat{v}(\xi_{1}+\cdots+\xi_{n+2})|.

We need to show that (
6.3
) is bounded by
\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{n+2}
. For
\xi:=\xi_{1}+\cdots+\xi_{n+2}
, let

\displaystyle R:=\min\left(\langle\xi-\xi_{1}^{*}\rangle,\langle\xi-\xi_{1}^{*}-\xi_{2}^{*}\rangle,\langle\xi-\xi_{1}^{*}-\xi_{3}^{*}\rangle,\dots,\langle\xi-\xi_{1}^{*}-\xi_{n+2}^{*}\rangle\right).

Using Lemma
5.2
(b), we have

\displaystyle\frac{|\xi_{1}^{*}|^{1+s+a}|\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{R}^{2}_{n+2}}\mu_{k_{0},\dots,k_{n}}|}{|\xi_{1}\cdots\xi_{n+2}|^{s}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{|\xi_{1}^{*}|^{s+a-n(\alpha-1)}|\xi_{3}^{*}|}{|\xi_{1}\cdots\xi_{n+2}|^{s}R}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{|\xi_{3}^{*}||\xi_{1}^{*}|^{a-2(\alpha-1)+}}{|\xi_{2}^{*}\xi_{3}^{*}\xi_{4}^{*}|^{s}|\xi_{5}^{*}\cdots\xi_{n+2}^{*}|^{s+\alpha-1}R^{1+}}.

Case 1:
R\neq\langle\xi-\xi_{1}^{*}-\xi_{2}^{*}\rangle
and
R\neq\langle\xi-\xi_{1}^{*}-\xi_{3}^{*}\rangle
.

For
s>\frac{7}{6}-\frac{2}{3}\alpha
and
a<3s+2\alpha-\frac{7}{2}
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{|\xi_{3}^{*}||\xi_{1}^{*}|^{a-2(\alpha-1)+}}{|\xi_{2}^{*}\xi_{3}^{*}\xi_{4}^{*}|^{s}}\lesssim\frac{1}{|\xi_{4}^{*}|^{\frac{1}{2}+}},

hence

\displaystyle\frac{|\xi_{1}^{*}|^{1+s+a}|\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{R}^{2}_{n+2}}\mu_{k_{0},\dots,k_{n}}|}{|\xi_{1}\cdots\xi_{n+2}|^{s}}\lesssim\frac{1}{|\xi_{4}^{*}|^{\frac{1}{2}+}|\xi_{5}^{*}\cdots\xi_{n+2}^{*}|^{s+\alpha-1}R^{1+}}.

By Cauchy-Schwarz, (
6.3
) is less than

\left(\sum_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{n+2}}\frac{|\widehat{v}(\xi^{*}_{1}+\cdots+\xi^{*}_{n+2})\widehat{u}(\xi^{*}_{3})\cdots\widehat{u}(\xi^{*}_{n+2})|^{2}}{R^{1+}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
\times\left(\sum_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{n+2}}^{\ast}\frac{|\widehat{u}(\xi^{*}_{1})\widehat{u}(\xi^{*}_{2})|^{2}}{|\xi^{*}_{4}|^{1+}|\xi^{*}_{5}\cdots\xi^{*}_{n+2}|^{2s+2\alpha-2}R^{1+}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}=:A\times B.

We may sum
A
in the order
\xi_{1}^{*},\xi_{2}^{*},\xi_{3}^{*},\dots,\xi_{n+2}^{*}
, and sum
B
in the order
\xi_{3}^{*},\xi_{1}^{*},\xi_{2}^{*},\xi_{4}^{*},\xi_{5}^{*}\dots,\xi_{n+2}^{*}
.

Case 2:
R=\langle\xi-\xi_{1}^{*}-\xi_{2}^{*}\rangle
or
R=\langle\xi-\xi_{1}^{*}-\xi_{3}^{*}\rangle
.

Since
|\xi_{2}^{*}|\approx|\xi_{3}^{*}|
, by possibly switching
|\xi_{2}^{*}|
and
|\xi_{3}^{*}|
, we may only consider the case
R=\langle\xi-\xi_{1}^{*}-\xi_{2}^{*}\rangle=\langle\xi_{3}^{*}+\xi_{4}^{*}+\cdots+\xi_{n+2}^{*}\rangle
.

Subcase 2a:
R\gtrsim|\xi_{2}^{*}|
.

In this case, for
s>\frac{7}{6}-\frac{2}{3}\alpha
and
a<3s+2\alpha-\frac{7}{2}
,

\displaystyle\frac{|\xi_{1}^{*}|^{1+s+a}|\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{R}^{2}_{n+2}}\mu_{k_{0},\dots,k_{n}}|}{|\xi_{1}\cdots\xi_{n+2}|^{s}}\lesssim\frac{|\xi_{1}^{*}|^{a-2(\alpha-1)}}{|\xi_{2}^{*}\xi_{3}^{*}\xi_{4}^{*}|^{s}|\xi_{5}^{*}\cdots\xi_{n+2}^{*}|^{s+\alpha-1}}\lesssim\frac{1}{|\xi_{2}^{*}\xi_{3}^{*}\xi_{4}^{*}|^{\frac{1}{2}+}|\xi_{5}^{*}\cdots\xi_{n+2}^{*}|^{s+\alpha-1}}.

By Cauchy-Schwarz, (
6.3
) is less than

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{n+2}}|\widehat{v}(\xi^{*}_{1}+\cdots+\xi^{*}_{n+2})\widehat{u}(\xi^{*}_{2})\cdots\widehat{u}(\xi^{*}_{n+2})|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\times\left(\sum_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{n+2}}^{\ast}\frac{|\widehat{u}(\xi^{*}_{1})|^{2}}{|\xi_{2}^{*}\xi_{3}^{*}\xi_{4}^{*}|^{\frac{1}{2}+}|\xi_{5}^{*}\cdots\xi_{n+2}^{*}|^{s+\alpha-1}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Subcase 2b:
R\ll|\xi_{2}^{*}|

7.2.1.
Boundary terms

As explained in subsection
1.3
, the main reason we introduced the space
Y^{s}
is to estimate the “boundary terms”. Since the
X^{s,b}
space is defined by

\|u\|_{X^{s,b}}=\|S(-t)u\|_{H^{s}_{x}H^{b}_{t}},

this space has great strength in the estimates of the form

\|S(t)F(u)\|_{X^{s,b}}.

On the other hand, this space generally does not provide good estimates for the terms that do not evolve like
S(t)
. This is the case of the boundary term
B_{2}(u)
in the right-hand side of (
7.3
); there is no
S(t)
attached in front of it. Technically, in the estimate

(7.8)

\displaystyle\|B_{2}(u)\|_{X^{s,b}}\lesssim\|u\|_{X^{s,b}}^{2},

the modulation
b
in the left-hand side acts like derivative loss, making it impossible to take the ideal
b=\frac{1}{2}
. As in
[
28
]
, reducing the size of
b
down to
1-\frac{1}{\alpha}
can be a way to establish (
7.8
). However, we go one step further: upon closer inspection, one can see that if
\alpha\geq\frac{4}{3}
, actual loss in (
7.8
) occurs only in the frequency region
D_{1}
, not on
D_{2}
. With this observation, instead of sacrificing the modulation
b
in the entire frequency region, we bear the loss only in the region
D_{1}
.

Lemma 7.3

Appendix A
Upper bound of the smoothing exponent

In Theorem
1.1
, in the
P(x)=x^{2}
case, we gain
\alpha-1
derivatives for
s>\frac{1}{2}
:

\displaystyle u(t)-e^{t\partial_{x}D^{\alpha}_{x}}g\in C([-T,T],H^{s+\alpha-1}).

The following result suggests that the order of smoothing
\alpha-1
is likely optimal. It should be compared with the result in
[
20
, Theorem 2]
.

Proposition A.1
.

If
\alpha\geq 1
and
a>\alpha-1
, then for any
s\in\mathbb{R}
and
T>0
, there does not exist
C(\|g\|_{H^{s}})
such that the estimate

\displaystyle\left\|\int_{0}^{t}S(t-t^{\prime})\left[S(t^{\prime})g\partial_{x}S(t^{\prime})g\right]dt^{\prime}\right\|_{C([-T,T],H^{s+a})}\lesssim C(\|g\|_{H^{s}})

holds true.

Proof.

Let
a>\alpha-1
. For
N\gg 1
let

\displaystyle\widehat{g}(\xi)=\begin{cases}N^{-s},&\,\text{if }\xi=N-1,\\
1,&\,\text{if }\xi=1,\\
0,&\,\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Then
\|g\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{T})}\approx 1
. Since
\Omega_{2}(N-1,1)\approx N^{\alpha}
, we have for
t_{N}:=\pi/\Omega_{2}(N-1,1)
,

\displaystyle\left\|\int_{0}^{t_{N}}S(t_{N}-t)\left[\partial_{x}\left(S(t)g\right)^{2}\right]dt\right\|_{H^{s+a}}

\displaystyle\quad=\left\||\xi|^{s+a}\sum_{\xi_{1}+\xi_{2}=\xi}\frac{2\xi}{\Omega_{2}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})}\left(e^{i\Omega_{2}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})t_{N}}-1\right)\widehat{g}_{\xi_{1}}\widehat{g}_{\xi_{2}}\right\|_{\ell^{2}_{\xi}}

\displaystyle\quad\geq\left\|\mathbbm{1}_{\{\xi=N\}}|\xi|^{s+a}\sum_{\xi_{1}+\xi_{2}=\xi}\frac{2\xi}{\Omega_{2}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})}\left(e^{i\Omega_{2}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})t_{N}}-1\right)\widehat{g}_{\xi_{1}}\widehat{g}_{\xi_{2}}\right\|_{\ell^{2}_{\xi}}

\displaystyle\quad\gtrsim N^{a+1-\alpha}.

Since
t_{N}\to 0
, and
N^{a+1-\alpha}\to\infty
as

, the desired result follows.

Appendix B
The fifth-order KdV equation

In this appendix, we illustrate how our method improves the well-posedness results for the (non-integrable) fifth-order KdV equation

(B.1)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u-\partial_{x}^{5}u+\alpha\partial_{x}(u^{3})+\beta\partial_{x}(\partial_{x}u)^{2}+\gamma\partial_{x}(u\partial_{x}^{2}u)=0,\\
u(x,0)=g\in H^{s}(\mathbb{T}),\end{cases}(x,t)\in\mathbb{T}\times[-T,T],

where
\alpha,\beta,\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
, and
g\in H^{s}(\mathbb{T})
is a mean-zero, real-valued function. Our main result in this appendix is:

Theorem B.1
.

For any
\alpha,\beta,\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
, the equation (
B.1
) is locally well-posed for
s>\frac{1}{2}
.

Remark B.2
.

If
\gamma=2\beta
, the flow map in Theorem
B.1
is real-analytic on the level set
\mathscr{M}_{0,c}^{s}:=\{u\in H^{s}(\mathbb{T}):\widehat{u}(0)=0,\ \|u\|_{L^{2}}=c\}
for each
c>0
. This is because the solutions are obtained via the Piccard iteration after a gauge transform, and the gauge transform used in
[
28
]
is a simple translation
u\mapsto u(x-4ct/5,t)
on
\mathscr{M}_{0,c}^{s}
(by the conservation of the
L^{2}
-norm). On the other hand, in the completely integrable case, the flow map is nowhere locally uniformly continuous on
\mathscr{M}_{0,c}^{s}
for any
c>0
and
0\leq s<\frac{1}{2}
; see
[
22
]
. Therefore, it is not likely that the method we used in the proof of
B.1
extends to the
s<\frac{1}{2}
case.

In
[
28
]
, McConnell proved that (
B.1
) is locally well-posed for
s>\frac{35}{64}
. He performed normal form reduction to (
B.1
), and then estimated the resulting terms in the function spaces defined by the norms

(B.2)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\|u\|_{Y^{s}}:=\|u\|_{X^{s,\frac{1}{4}}}+\|u\|_{W^{s}},\\
&\|u\|_{Z^{s}}:=\left\|\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left[\langle\tau-\xi^{5}\rangle^{-1}\widehat{u}\right]\right\|_{Y^{s}},\end{split}

where we define
\|u\|_{X^{s,b}}:=\|\langle\xi\rangle^{s}\langle\tau-\xi^{5}\rangle^{b}\widehat{u}\|_{L^{2}_{\xi,\tau}}
and
\|u\|_{W^{s}}:=\|\langle\xi\rangle^{s}\widehat{u}\|_{\ell^{2}_{\xi}L^{1}_{\tau}}
. There, he used the estimate

(B.3)

\displaystyle\big\|e^{t\partial_{x}^{5}}g\big\|_{L^{8}_{\mathbb{T}\times[-T,T]}}\lesssim\|g\|_{H^{a+}}

to prove the inequality

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{NR}_{2}(u)\|_{Z^{s}_{T}}+\|\mathcal{NR}_{3}(u)\|_{Z^{s}_{T}}\lesssim T^{\theta}\|u\|_{Y^{s}_{T}}^{4}

for
s>\frac{1+a}{2}
, where
\mathcal{NR}_{2}(u)
and
\mathcal{NR}_{3}(u)
are the near-resonant terms associated with (
B.1
). Since all the other relevant terms are estimated in the
s>\frac{1}{2}
level, this leads to local well-posedness for (
B.1
) for
s>\frac{1+a}{2}
. It is conjectured that the estimate (
B.3
) holds for
a=0
, however, the currently best known value of
a
so far is
\frac{3}{32}
.

The estimate (
B.3
) was essential in
[
28
]
because the modulation
\frac{1}{4}
in the space (
B.2
) was too low to handle some necessary Strichartz estimates. However, we can avoid this difficulty by simply replacing the spaces (
B.2
) by

(B.4)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\|u\|_{\widetilde{Y}^{s}}:=\|\mathbb{P}_{D_{1}}u\|_{X^{s-1-\epsilon,\frac{1}{2}}}+\|\mathbb{P}_{D_{2}}u\|_{X^{s,\frac{1}{2}-\epsilon}}+\|u\|_{W^{s}},\\
&\|u\|_{\widetilde{Z}^{s}}:=\|u\|_{X^{s,-\frac{1}{2}+}},\end{split}

where

\displaystyle D_{1}:=\left\{(\xi,\tau)\in\mathbb{Z}_{\ast}\times\mathbb{R}:\langle\tau-\xi^{5}\rangle\gtrsim|\xi|^{4}\right\},

\displaystyle D_{2}:=\left(\mathbb{Z}_{\ast}\times\mathbb{R}\right)\setminus D_{1}.

Following the proof of Lemma
7.3
, we can see that the boundary terms
B_{1},B_{2}
in
[
28
]
are controlled in

for

:

\displaystyle\|\mathbb{P}_{\{|\xi|\geq N\}}B_{1}(u)\|_{\widetilde{Y}^{s}_{T}}+\|\mathbb{P}_{\{|\xi|\geq N\}}B_{2}(u)\|_{\widetilde{Y}^{s}_{T}}\lesssim N^{-\epsilon}\big(\|u\|_{\widetilde{Y}^{s}_{T}}^{2}+\|u\|_{\widetilde{Y}^{s}_{T}}^{3}\big).

\displaystyle\|u\|_{X^{s,\frac{1}{4}-}}\lesssim\|\mathbb{P}_{D_{1}}u\|_{X^{s-1-,\frac{1}{2}}}+\|\mathbb{P}_{D_{2}}u\|_{X^{s,\frac{1}{2}-}},

it suffices to dominate the quantity

(B.7)

\displaystyle\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}\tau_{1}+\cdots+\tau_{5}=0\\
\xi_{1}+\cdots+\xi_{5}=0\end{subarray}}{\int\sum^{\ast}}\frac{|m(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\xi_{3},\xi_{4})||\xi_{5}|^{s}\prod_{i=1}^{4}\mathbbm{1}_{D_{k_{i}}}\widehat{u}_{i}(\xi_{i},\tau_{i})\widehat{u}_{5}(\xi_{5},\tau_{5})}{|\xi_{1}\xi_{2}\xi_{3}\xi_{4}|^{s}\langle\sigma_{1}\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}-}\langle\sigma_{2}\rangle^{\frac{1}{4}-}\langle\sigma_{3}\rangle^{\frac{1}{4}-}\langle\sigma_{5}\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}-}}

by
\prod_{i=1}^{3}\|\mathbb{P}_{D_{k_{i}}}u_{i}\|_{L^{2}_{x,t}}\|\widehat{u}_{4}\|_{W^{0}}\|u_{5}\|_{L^{2}_{x,t}}
. Note that for
s>\frac{1}{2}
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{|m(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\xi_{3},\xi_{4})||\xi_{5}|^{s+}}{|\xi_{1}|^{s-}|\xi_{2}\xi_{3}|^{s}|\xi_{4}|^{s-\frac{1}{2}-}}=O(1).

Using this, we may apply the
(6-,3+,3+,\infty,6-)
-Hölder inequality and to (
B.7
). Then we obtained the desired bound by the Strichartz estimate

\displaystyle\left\|\left[\frac{f}{\langle\tau-\xi^{5}\rangle^{\frac{1}{5}+}}\right]^{\vee}\right\|_{L^{3+}_{x,t}}+\left\|\left[\frac{\langle\xi\rangle^{0-}f}{\langle\tau-\xi^{5}\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}-}}\right]^{\vee}\right\|_{L^{6-}_{x,t}}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{2}_{\xi,\tau}}.

∎

In
[
28
]
, the proofs of the nonlinear estimates there do not take advantage of the
-\frac{3}{4}
modulation in the
Z^{s}
norm. Rather, they are actually done for the
X^{s,-\frac{1}{2}+}
norm; generically, these estimates are like

\displaystyle\|\Lambda(u)\|_{Z^{s}}\lesssim\|\Lambda(u)\|_{X^{s,-\frac{1}{2}+}}\lesssim\|u\|_{Y^{s}}^{n}

for some Fourier multiplier operator
\Lambda
. Since we have

\displaystyle\|u\|_{X^{s,\frac{1}{4}-\frac{\epsilon}{4}}}+\|u\|_{W^{s}}\lesssim\|u\|_{\widetilde{Y}^{s}},

and the analysis in
[
28
]
is not sensitive to
\epsilon>0
, all the nonlinear estimates stated there for the spaces (
B.2
) can also be verified for (
B.4
). This with a contraction argument proves Theorem
B.1
.

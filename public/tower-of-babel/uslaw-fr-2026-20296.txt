SUPPLEMENTARY INFORMATION:

I. Background

    On June 24, 2026, the Commission published an NPRM in the Federal 
Register, proposing to issue a safety standard for lithium-ion 
batteries used in micromobility products under the Consumer Product 
Safety Act (CPSA; 15 U.S.C. 2051-2089), and seeking written comments. 
91 FR 38162. The NPRM seeks to address the unreasonable risk of injury 
and death associated with micromobility products due to hazards such as 
thermal runaway of lithium cells, which can lead to fires, explosions, 
gas releases, burns, overheating, and smoke inhalation. The NPRM 
proposes that electrical systems using lithium-ion batteries in 
micromobility products comply with applicable voluntary standards, with 
modifications. The NPRM is available at: https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/06/24/2026-12749/safety-standard-for-lithium-ion-batteries-used-in-micromobility-products-and-electrical-systems-of.

II. The Public Hearing

    The Administrative Procedure Act (5 U.S.C. 551-562) and section 9 
of the CPSA require the Commission to provide interested parties with 
an opportunity to submit ``written data, views, or arguments'' 
regarding a proposed rule. 5 U.S.C. 553(c); see 15 U.S.C. 2058(d)(2). 
The NPRM invited such written comments. In addition, section 9 of the 
CPSA requires the Commission to provide interested parties ``an 
opportunity for oral presentation of data, views, or arguments.'' 15 
U.S.C. 2058(d)(2). The Commission must keep a transcript of such oral 
presentations. Id. The Commission received requests to present and, in 
accordance with the requirement in section 9 of the CPSA, the 
Commission is providing a forum for oral opportunity to make an oral 
presentation, see the information under the DATES and ADDRESSES 
sections of this document.\1\ Each oral presentation will be limited to 
5 minutes, excluding time for questioning by the Commissioners or CPSC 
staff. Oral presentations must be limited to matters within the scope 
of the NPRM identified in this notice. To avoid duplicate 
presentations, groups or participants with substantially similar 
comments should designate a spokesperson, and the presiding officer may 
impose additional reasonable restrictions as necessary for the orderly 
and efficient conduct of the hearing.

Anisotropic two-qutrit states

A single-qutrit state can be described in the Hilbert space spanned by three

\{\left|0\right\rangle,\left|1\right\rangle,\left|2\right\rangle\}

In this section, we shall analyze the DWFs and study Wigner negativities for

IV.1
Discrete Wigner function

Similar to the qubit Pauli operators
\sigma_{x}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&1\\
1&0\end{array}\right)
and
\sigma_{z}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&0\\
0&-1\end{array}\right)
, one can introduce the qutrit Pauli operators

X=\sum_{k=0}^{2}\left|k+1\right\rangle\left\langle k\right|=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}0&0&1\\
1&0&0\\
0&1&0\end{array}\right),

(6)

and

Z=\sum_{k=0}^{2}\omega^{k}\left|k\right\rangle\left\langle k\right|=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}1&0&0\\
0&\omega&0\\
0&0&\omega^{2}\end{array}\right),

(7)

with
\omega=e^{\frac{2\pi i}{3}}
. They obey
X\left|k\right\rangle=\left|\left(k+1\right)\text{ mod }3\right\rangle
,
Z\left|k\right\rangle=\omega^{k}\left|k\right\rangle
, and
Z^{z}X^{x}=\omega^{xz}X^{x}Z^{z}
for
x,z,k\in\mathbb{Z}_{3}
.

For a qutrit, the Wiger operator in phase point
\left(u_{x},u_{z}\right)

is defined as

A_{\left(u_{x},u_{z}\right)}=\frac{1}{3}\sum_{v_{x}=0}^{2}\sum_{v_{z}=0}^{2}\omega^{u_{z}v_{x}-u_{x}v_{z}}D_{\left(v_{x},v_{z}\right)},

(8)

with the Heisenberg-Weyl displacement operator
D_{\left(x,z\right)}=\omega^{\frac{1}{2}xz}X^{x}Z^{z}
. Therefore, we rewrite
A_{\left(u_{x},u_{z}\right)}
as
A_{\left(x,z\right)}
with the following matrix

A_{(x,z)}=\frac{1}{3}\allowbreak\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}1+\omega^{-x}+\omega^{-2x}&\omega^{2z-x+2}+\omega^{2z-2x+4}+\omega^{2z}&\omega^{z}+\omega^{z-2x+2}+\omega^{z-x+\frac{5}{2}}\\
\omega^{z}+\omega^{z-2x+1}+\omega^{z-x+\frac{1}{2}}&1+\omega^{2-2x}+\omega^{-x+1}&\omega^{2z-x+3}+\omega^{2z-2x+3}+\omega^{2z}\\
\omega^{2z-x+1}+\omega^{2z-2x+2}+\omega^{2z}&\omega^{z}+\omega^{z-2x+3}+\omega^{z-x+\frac{3}{2}}&1+\omega^{1-2x}+\omega^{-x+2}\end{array}\right).

(9)

For the AITTSs with the density matrix
\rho_{aiso}
, the corresponding DWF
can be calculated by

W_{\left(x_{1},z_{1};x_{2},z_{2}\right)}\left(\rho_{aiso}\right)=\frac{1}{3^{2}}\mathrm{Tr}[(A_{\left(x_{1},z_{1}\right)}\otimes A_{\left(x_{2},z_{2}\right)})\rho_{aiso}].

(10)

For every two-qutrit state, there are eighty-one phase points due to
\left(x_{1},z_{1};x_{2},z_{2}\right)\in\mathbb{Z}_{3}^{4}
. In this paper, we will arrange them as follows

\begin{array}[]{ccccccccc}W_{0000}&W_{0100}&W_{0200}&W_{0001}&W_{0101}&W_{0201}&W_{0002}&W_{0102}&W_{0202}\\

IV.2
Wigner negativity

For any quantum state, the Wigner function is normalized. As expected, we
can definitely verify

\sum_{x_{1},z_{1};x_{2},z_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}_{3}^{4}}W_{\left(x_{1},z_{1};x_{2},z_{2}\right)}\left(\rho_{aiso}\right)=1

(12)

for any
\rho_{aiso}
. The amount of the Wigner negativity is just minus
the sum of all negative values among the DWF. Hence, the Wigner negativity
of
\rho_{aiso}
can be calculated by

\mathcal{N}\left(\rho_{aiso}\right)=\frac{1}{2}[\sum_{x_{1},z_{1};x_{2},z_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}_{3}^{4}}\left|W_{\left(x_{1},z_{1};x_{2},z_{2}\right)}\left(\rho_{aiso}\right)\right|-1].

(13)

After making numerical calculation, we easily obtain
\mathcal{N}(\left|S_{1}^{(1)}\right\rangle)=\mathcal{N}(\left|S_{1}^{(2)}\right\rangle)=\mathcal{N}(\left|S_{1}^{(3)}\right\rangle)=0
,
\mathcal{N}(\left|S_{2}^{(4)}\right\rangle)=\mathcal{N}(\left|S_{2}^{(5)}\right\rangle)=\mathcal{N}(\left|S_{2}^{(6)}\right\rangle)\simeq 0.416975
,
\mathcal{N}(\left|S_{2}^{(1)}\right\rangle)=\mathcal{N}(\left|S_{2}^{(2)}\right\rangle)=\mathcal{N}(\left|S_{2}^{(3)}\right\rangle)=\frac{13}{27}\simeq 0.481481
,
\mathcal{N}(\left|S_{3}^{(2)}\right\rangle)\simeq 0.421011
,
\mathcal{N}(\left|S_{3}^{(1)}\right\rangle)=\frac{4}{9}\simeq 0.444444
, and
\mathcal{N}\left(\rho_{noise}\right)=0
. Some of these values can be
validated from our plotted DWFs.

V
Bell nonlocality of AITTSs

As Meyer et al. pointed out
101
, Wigner negativity is necessary for
nonlocality in qudit systems. In this section, we study Bell nonlocality for
the AITTSs by virtue of the CGLMP inequalities. We consider two separated
observers, Alice and Bob. Alice can conduct two different measurements (
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
) with respective three outcomes, i.e.,
A_{1}=j
(
j=0,1,2
) and
A_{2}=k
(
k=0,1,2
). Similarly, Bob can conduct two
different measurements (
B_{1}
and
B_{2}
) with respective three outcomes,
i.e.,
B_{1}=l
(
l=0,1,2
) and
B_{2}=m
(
m=0,1,2
). In order to avoid the
eigenstates degenerate, we further assume that their eigenstates satisfy

\displaystyle\left|j\right\rangle_{A_{1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\sum_{n=0}^{2}\omega^{n\left(j+\alpha_{1}\right)}\left|n\right\rangle_{A},

(14)

\displaystyle\left|k\right\rangle_{A_{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\sum_{n=0}^{2}\omega^{n(k+\alpha_{2})}\left|n\right\rangle_{A},

(15)

\displaystyle\left|l\right\rangle_{B_{1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\sum_{n=0}^{2}\omega^{n(-l+\beta_{1})}\left|n\right\rangle_{B},

(16)

\displaystyle\left|m\right\rangle_{B_{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\sum_{n=0}^{2}\omega^{n(-m+\beta_{2})}\left|n\right\rangle_{B},

(17)

which lead to
\Pi_{A_{1}}^{\left(j\right)}=\left|j\right\rangle_{A_{1}}\left\langle j\right|
,
\Pi_{A_{2}}^{\left(k\right)}=\left|k\right\rangle_{A_{2}}\left\langle k\right|
,
\Pi_{B_{1}}^{\left(l\right)}=\left|l\right\rangle_{B_{1}}\left\langle l\right|
, and
\Pi_{B_{2}}^{\left(m\right)}=\left|m\right\rangle_{B_{2}}\left\langle m\right|
(

), respectively.

Now, we let Alice and Bob share the AITTSs. The joint
probabilities can be calculated as

\displaystyle P\left(A_{1}=j,B_{1}=l\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}[(\Pi_{A_{1}}^{\left(j\right)}\otimes\Pi_{B_{1}}^{\left(l\right)})\rho_{aiso}],

(18)

\displaystyle P\left(A_{1}=j,B_{2}=m\right)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}[(\Pi_{A_{1}}^{\left(j\right)}\otimes\Pi_{B_{2}}^{\left(m\right)})\rho_{aiso}],

(19)

\displaystyle P\left(A_{2}=k,B_{1}=l\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}[(\Pi_{A_{2}}^{\left(k\right)}\otimes\Pi_{B_{1}}^{\left(l\right)})\rho_{aiso}],

(20)

\displaystyle P\left(A_{2}=k,B_{2}=m\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}[(\Pi_{A_{2}}^{\left(k\right)}\otimes\Pi_{B_{2}}^{\left(m\right)})\rho_{aiso}].

(21)

Further, we use the CGLMP inequality

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{3}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle[P\left(A_{1}=B_{1}\right)+P\left(B_{1}=A_{2}+1\right)

(22)

\displaystyle+P\left(A_{2}=B_{2}\right)+P\left(B_{2}=A_{1}\right)]

\displaystyle-[P\left(A_{1}=B_{1}-1\right)+P\left(B_{1}=A_{2}\right)

\displaystyle+P\left(A_{2}=B_{2}-1\right)+P\left(B_{2}=A_{1}-1\right)]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2.

to study the Bell nonlocality for
\rho_{aiso}
by observing
\mathcal{I}_{3}\left(\rho_{aiso}\right)>2
and setting
(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\beta_{1},\beta_{2})=(0,1/2,1/4,-1/4)
.

\mathcal{I}_{3}(\left|S_{2}^{(1)}\right\rangle)=\mathcal{I}_{3}(\left|S_{2}^{(2)}\right\rangle)=\mathcal{I}_{3}(\left|S_{2}^{(3)}\right\rangle)=0

\mathcal{I}_{3}(\left|S_{2}^{(1)}\right\rangle)=\mathcal{I}_{3}(\left|S_{2}^{(3)}\right\rangle)\simeq 1.1547
,
\mathcal{I}_{3}(\left|S_{2}^{(5)}\right\rangle)\simeq 1.73205
,
\mathcal{I}_{3}(\left|S_{2}^{(2)}\right\rangle)\simeq 2
,
\mathcal{I}_{3}(\left|S_{3}^{(1)}\right\rangle)\simeq 2.84399
,
\mathcal{I}_{3}(\left|S_{3}^{(1)}\right\rangle)\simeq 2.87293

satisfy

It is worth noting that we have
\mathcal{I}_{3}\left(\rho_{aiso}\right)=p\mathcal{I}_{3}\left(\left|\psi_{\left(\theta,\phi\right)}\right\rangle\right)
for arbitrarily determined
\left(\theta,\phi\right)
case. That is,
\mathcal{I}_{3}\left(\rho_{aiso}\right)

(

\mathcal{I}_{3}\left(\left|\psi_{\left(\theta,\phi\right)}\right\rangle\right)

\mathcal{I}_{3}\left(\rho_{aiso}\right)=p\mathcal{I}_{3}\left(\left|\psi_{\left(\theta,\phi\right)}\right\rangle\right)+(1-p)\mathcal{I}_{3}\left(\rho_{noise}\right)
.

Theoretically, Bell nonlocality can be witnessed by observing
\mathcal{I}_{3}\left(\rho_{aiso}\right)>2
, i.e. the violation of Eq.(

).
From above numerical results, we immediately know that there is the
possibility of
\mathcal{I}_{3}\left(\rho_{aiso}\right)>2
if and only if

\left(\theta,\phi\right)

\mathcal{I}_{3}\left(\rho_{aiso}\right)

-2

\left(\theta,\phi\right)

In addition, we find that the maximum value of

, which is positioned

VI
Conclusions and discussions

In summary, we introduced the AITTSs, and then explored their properties,
including entanglement (see index
\mathcal{E}
), Wigner negativity (see
index
\mathcal{N}
) and Bell nonlocality (see index
\mathcal{I}_{3}
). For
all these properties, we tried our best to obtain their respective
analytical and numerical results.

As one extremal case of
\rho_{aiso}
with
p=0
, we always had
\mathcal{E}\left(\rho_{noise}\right)\equiv 0
,
\mathcal{N}\left(\rho_{noise}\right)\equiv 0
, and
\mathcal{I}_{3}\left(\rho_{noise}\right)\equiv 0
. As another extremal case of
\rho_{aiso}
with
p=1
, we know
that
\mathcal{E}\left(\left|\psi_{\left(\theta,\phi\right)}\right\rangle\right)
,
\mathcal{N}\left(\left|\psi_{\left(\theta,\phi\right)}\right\rangle\right)
, and
\mathcal{I}_{3}\left(\left|\psi_{\left(\theta,\phi\right)}\right\rangle\right)
are
determined by
\left|\psi_{\left(\theta,\phi\right)}\right\rangle

in itself. So, in the end of this paper, we specialize in analyzing the
influence of parameters
\left(\theta,\phi\right)
on these three
properties of
\left|\psi_{\left(\theta,\phi\right)}\right\rangle
. Figure 11 depicts the contours for
\mathcal{E}\left(\left|\psi_{\left(\theta,\phi\right)}\right\rangle\right)
,
\mathcal{N}\left(\left|\psi_{\left(\theta,\phi\right)}\right\rangle\right)
, and
\mathcal{I}_{3}\left(\left|\psi_{\left(\theta,\phi\right)}\right\rangle\right)
in
\left(\theta,\phi\right)
plains. From
Fig.11(a), we see that
\mathcal{E}\left(\left|\psi_{\left(\theta,\phi\right)}\right\rangle\right)
is a periodic function of
\theta
with period
\pi
and a periodic function of
\phi
with period
\pi/4
. Compared with
\mathcal{E}\left(\left|\psi_{\left(\theta,\phi\right)}\right\rangle\right)
, the periodic features of
\mathcal{N}\left(\left|\psi_{\left(\theta,\phi\right)}\right\rangle\right)

are gone, as shown in Fig.11(b). By observing
\mathcal{I}_{3}\left(\left|\psi_{\left(\theta,\phi\right)}\right\rangle\right)
in
Fig.11(c), we find that
\mathcal{I}_{3}\left(\left|\psi_{\left(\theta,\phi\right)}\right\rangle\right)
values are in the range of
[-2,2.91485]
. Specially, when
\left(\theta,\phi,p\right)=\left(\pi/4,\pi,1\right)
, the minimal
\mathcal{I}_{3}^{\min}=-2
is found,
corresponding to
\left|\psi_{\left(\theta,\phi\right)}\right\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(\left|22\right\rangle-\left|00\right\rangle)
.

Contourplots of (a)
\mathcal{E}\left(\left|\psi_{\left(\theta,\phi\right)}\right\rangle\right)
, (b)

, and (c)

;
(ii) For some
\left(\theta,\phi\right)

\mathcal{I}_{3}
values are less than 0.

If
\left|\psi_{\left(\theta,\phi\right)}\right\rangle
was the
Sn=1 state, then we always have
\mathcal{E}\left(\rho_{aiso}\right)\equiv 0
,
\mathcal{N}\left(\rho_{aiso}\right)\equiv 0
, and
\mathcal{I}_{3}\left(\rho_{aiso}\right)\equiv 0
. While
\left|\psi_{\left(\theta,\phi\right)}\right\rangle
was not the Sn=1 states, then we found
that (i)
\mathcal{E}\left(\rho_{aiso}\right)
was not a linear function
of
p
; (ii)
\mathcal{N}\left(\rho_{aiso}\right)
was also not a linear
function of
p
; (iii) but,
\mathcal{I}_{3}\left(\rho_{aiso}\right)
was
a linear function of
p
. Although
\rho_{aiso}
was the mixture between
\left|\psi_{\left(\theta,\phi\right)}\right\rangle
and
\rho_{noise}
(see Eq.(
2
)), we concluded that (i) The equality of
\mathcal{E}\left(\rho_{aiso}\right)=p\mathcal{E}\left(\left|\psi_{\left(\theta,\phi\right)}\right\rangle\right)+(1-p)\mathcal{E}\left(\rho_{noise}\right)
may not necessarily hold true; (ii) The equality of
\mathcal{N}\left(\rho_{aiso}\right)=p\mathcal{N}\left(\left|\psi_{\left(\theta,\phi\right)}\right\rangle\right)+(1-p)\mathcal{N}\left(\rho_{noise}\right)
may not necessarily hold true; (iii) But the equality
of
\mathcal{I}_{3}\left(\rho_{aiso}\right)=p\mathcal{I}_{3}\left(\left|\psi_{\left(\theta,\phi\right)}\right\rangle\right)+(1-p)\mathcal{I}_{3}\left(\rho_{noise}\right)
may always hold true.

Finally, we summarize several key results as follows:

(1) There is no decisive relationship between these three properties. If a
quantum state has the highest entanglement, its negativity may not
necessarily be the highest, and its Bell non-locality may not necessarily be
the strongest. This point can be verified from
\mathcal{E}
,
\mathcal{N}
,
and
\mathcal{I}_{3}
of state
\left|\Phi_{3}^{+}\right\rangle
. The
maximally entangled state is not the maximally Wigner-negativity state and
the maximally Bell-nonlocality state.

(2) A quantum pure state with a larger Schmidt number does not necessarily
have a greater Wigner negativity. This point can be verified from
\mathcal{N}(\left|S_{2}^{(1)}\right\rangle)>\mathcal{N}(\left|S_{3}^{(1)}\right\rangle)
.

(3) It is the effects of the noise that three properties will exhibit only
when
p
exceeds a certain threshold. This point can be seen from our
numerical results. Of course, the optimal properties of
\rho_{aiso}
are
those of
\left|\psi_{\left(\theta,\phi\right)}\right\rangle

corresponding to
p=1
, without the effects of the noise.

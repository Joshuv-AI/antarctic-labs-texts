Introduction

A celebrated result in analytic number theory, due to Green and Tao
[
9
]
, states that the primes contain arbitrary long arithmetic progressions. In fact they have proved the following. Let
\mathbb{P}_{N}
denote the set of primes up to
N
.

Theorem A
.

Let
\delta>0
,
V=\{v_{0},\ldots,v_{l}\}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}
. If
N\geq N(\delta,V)
and
A\subseteq\mathbb{P}_{N}
with
|A|\geq\delta|\mathbb{P}_{N}|
then
A
contains an affine image of
V
i.e. a set
V^{\prime}=\{x+yv_{0},\ldots,x+yv_{l}\}
.

It is easy to see that this implies that any subset of the primes of positive relative upper density contains an affine image of any finite set of integers. In
[
18
]
Tao has obtained an analogue of Theorem A for the Gaussian primes and asked if similar results hold for subsets of the prime lattice
\mathbb{P}^{d}
. The first result in this direction was provided by B. Cook and the second author
[
4
]
for finite sets
V\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{d}
which are in
general position
in the sense that
|\pi_{j}(V)|=|V|
for every
1\leq j\leq d
, where
\pi_{j}:\mathbb{Z}^{d}\to\mathbb{Z}
denotes the orthogonal projection to the
j^{th}
-axis.

Theorem B
.

Let
d\geq 1,\ \delta>0
and let
V=\{\underline{v}_{0},\ldots,\underline{v}_{l}\}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{d}
be a set in general position. If
N\geq N(\delta,V)
and
A\subseteq\mathbb{P}_{N}^{d}
with
|A|\geq\delta|\mathbb{P}_{N}|^{d}
then
A
contains an affine image of
V
i.e. a set
V^{\prime}=\{\underline{x}+y\underline{v}_{0},\ldots,\underline{x}+y\underline{v}_{l}\}
.

The full multidimensional extension of Theorem A were obtained independently in
[
21
,
5
]
and
[
7
]
where it was shown that the conclusion of Theorem B holds for any finite set
V\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{d}
.

In
[
19
]
Tao and Ziegler has extended Theorem A in another direction, from arithmetic to polynomial progressions which are sets of the form
x+P_{0}(y),\ldots,x+P_{l}(y)
, where
\mathcal{P}=(P_{0},\ldots,P_{l})
is a family of integral polynomials. In
[
20
,
22
]
they have provided quantitative bounds on the size of the parameter
y
measuring the “size” of the polynomial progression.

Theorem C
.

Let
\delta>0
,
l\in\mathbb{N}
and let
\mathcal{P}=(P_{0},\ldots,P_{l})
,
P_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}[x],P_{i}(0)=0
. If
N\geq N(\delta,l,\mathcal{P})
and
A\subseteq\mathbb{P}_{N}
with
|A|\geq\delta|\mathbb{P}_{N}|
, then
A
contains a polynomial progression
\{x+P_{0}(y),\ldots,x+P_{l}(y)\}
for some
x,y\in\mathbb{N}
.

Moreover, one may have
0<y<\log^{L}N
, where
L=L(l,\mathcal{P})
is a constant depends only the family of polynomials
\mathcal{P}=(P_{0},\ldots,P_{l})
.

Note that the first statement in Theorem C was proved in
[
19
]
while the bound
0<y<\log^{L}N
with
L=L(l,P,\delta)
depending also on the density
\delta
was given in
[
20
]
. The dependence on
\delta
was removed in
[
22
]
by estimating polynomial averages of local Gowers norms by global ones via so-called concatenation theorems.

Corresponding lower bounds were also discussed in
[
20
]
, extending a construction in
[
6
]
, showing that
y\gg\log^{l}N
is necessary, even for linear case, when
P_{i}(y)=iy
for
0\leq i\leq l
.

Our aim in this note is twofold. On the one hand we show that Theorem C can be extended to the multi-dimensional setting for polynomial configurations that satisfy a similar non-degeneracy condition as given in Theorem B for affine linear configurations. On the other hand we’d like to present this result in an essentially self contained manner, relying only on a sieve theoretic estimate (see (
2.6
) below) and a quantitative version of a theorem of Bergelson-Leibman
[
2
]
on polynomial configurations in dense subsets of the integer lattice.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
l\geq 1
,
V=\{\underline{v}_{0},\ldots,\underline{v}_{l}\}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{d}
be a finite set and let
\mathcal{P}=(P_{1},\ldots,P_{l})
be a family of integral polynomials. We say that the polynomial configuration
\mathcal{P}_{V}=(P_{0}(y)\underline{v}_{0},\ldots,P_{l}(y)\underline{v}_{l})
is in general position, if for all
0\leq j<j^{\prime}\leq l
and
1\leq i\leq d
,

(1.1)

\deg\,\big(\pi_{i}(P_{j}(y)\,\underline{v}_{j}-P_{j^{\prime}}(y)\,\underline{v}_{j^{\prime}})\big)=\deg\,(P_{j}(y)\,\underline{v}_{j}-P_{j^{\prime}}(y)\,\underline{v}_{j^{\prime}}),

as a polynomial in
y
,
\pi_{i}
being the natural projection to the
i^{th}
coordinate axis.

Our main result is the following.

Theorem 1
.

Let
\delta>0
,
d,l\in\mathbb{N}
and let
\mathcal{P}_{V}=(P_{0}(y)\underline{v}_{0},\ldots,P_{l}(y)\underline{v}_{l})
with
P_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}[y],P_{j}(0)=0
be a polynomial configuration in general position. If
N\geq N(\delta,\mathcal{P},V)
and
A\subseteq\mathbb{P}_{N}^{d}
with
|A|\geq\delta|\mathbb{P}_{N}^{d}|
, then there exist
x,y\in\mathbb{N}
with
0<y<\log^{L}N
, such that

(1.2)

\{\underline{x}+P_{0}(y)\underline{v}_{0},\ldots,\underline{x}+P_{l}(y)\underline{v}_{l}\}\subseteq A,

where
L=L(\mathcal{P},V)
is a constant depending only on initial data
\mathcal{P},V
.

Remarks.

•

In dimension
d=1
, taking
v_{0}=\ldots=v_{l}=1
, Theorem 1 reduces to Theorem C.

•

If the set
V=\{\underline{v}_{0},\ldots,\underline{v}_{l}\}
is in general position then for any polynomial
P\in\mathbb{Z}[y]
,
P(0)=0
the polynomial configuration
\mathcal{P}_{V}:=(P(y)\underline{v}_{0},\ldots,P(y)\underline{v}_{l})
is also in general position, hence Theorem 1 extends Theorem B.

•

If the polynomials
P_{0},\ldots,P_{l}
have distinct degrees then the pattern
\mathcal{P}_{V}
is in general position, as long as
\pi_{i}(\underline{v}_{j})\neq 0
for all
1\leq j\leq l
and
1\leq i\leq d
.

•

If
A=A_{1}^{d}
where
A_{1}\subseteq\mathbb{P}
is of positive relative upper density, then Theorem 1 one follows from the 1-dimensional result of Tao-Ziegler
[
20
]
as the polynomial pattern
\mathcal{P}_{V}(y)=\{P_{0}(y)\underline{v}_{0},\ldots,P_{l}(y)\underline{v}_{l}\}
is contained in the
d
-fold direct product of the polynomial progression
\mathcal{P}(y):=\{\pi_{i}(P_{j}(y)\,\underline{v}_{j}):\ 1\leq j\leq l,\ 1\leq i\leq d\}
.

•

In general, condition (
1.1
) is equivalent the following. If the leading term of
P_{j}(y)\,\underline{v}_{j}-P_{j^{\prime}}(y)\,\underline{v}_{j^{\prime}}
is of the form
\underline{c}_{jj^{\prime}}\,y^{d_{jj^{\prime}}}
then
\pi_{i}(\underline{c}_{jj^{\prime}})\neq 0
for all
1\leq i\leq d
and
0\leq j<j^{\prime}\leq l
.

Our approach follows that of
[
19
,
20
]
and roughly consists of two parts. The starting point is to control polynomial averages of the form:

(1.3)

\Lambda_{\mathcal{P},V}(f_{0},\ldots,f_{l}):=\mathbb{E}_{x\in X}\mathbb{E}_{y\in[M]}f_{0}(\underline{x}+P_{0}(y)\underline{v}_{0})\ldots f_{l}(\underline{x}+P_{l}(y)\underline{v}_{l}),

where
X=(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})^{d}
,
M:=\log^{L}N
, and we used the averaging notation
\mathbb{E}_{a\in A}f(a):=\frac{1}{|A|}\sum_{a\in A}f(a)
.
The first part is to bound these averages in terms of polynomial averages of certain Gowers type local box norms. Recall that given an
s
-tuple of vectors
\underline{u}_{1},\ldots,\underline{u}_{s}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, we define the corresponding local box norm at scale
1\leq M\leq N
, as

(1.4)

\|f\|^{2^{s}}_{\Box_{M}(\underline{u}_{1},\ldots,\underline{u}_{s})}:=\mathbb{E}_{\underline{x}\in X}\mathbb{E}_{y_{1}^{(0)},\ldots,y_{s}^{(0)},y_{1}^{(1)},\ldots,y_{s}^{(1)}\in[M]}\prod_{\underline{\omega}\in\{0,1\}^{s}}f(\underline{x}+y_{1}^{(\omega_{1})}\underline{u}_{1}+\ldots+y_{s}^{(\omega_{s})}\underline{u}_{s})

For bounded functions, when say
|f_{i}|\leq 1
, the so-called PET induction scheme of Bergelson-Leibman
[
2
]
estimates the averages in (
1.3
) via an polynomial averages of box norms, namely one has

(1.5)

|\Lambda_{\mathcal{P},V}(f_{0},\ldots,f_{l})|\lesssim\min_{1\leq j\leq s}\big(\mathbb{E}_{\underline{h}\in[H]^{t}}\|f_{j}\|^{2^{s}}_{\Box_{M}(\underline{Q}_{1}(\underline{h}),\ldots,\underline{Q}_{s}(\underline{h})}\big)^{\frac{1}{2^{s}}}+o(1),

where
\underline{Q}_{1},\ldots,\underline{Q}_{s}:\mathbb{Z}^{t}\to\mathbb{Z}^{d}
is a family of integral non-constant polynomial maps, and
H:=\log^{\sqrt{L}}N
is a parameter, also referred to as the “fine scale”
[
20
]
. By
o(1)
we will denote a quantity that goes to
0
as
N\to\infty
, that may depend on the initial configuration
\mathcal{P},V
and parameters
d,l
.

In one dimension it was shown in
[
19
]
that such estimates remain true when the functions
f_{i}
are not bounded uniformly in
N
, but bounded by a function
\nu_{N}
, referred to a pseudo-random measure
[
9
,
19
]
, that behaves very nicely with respect to polynomial type averages satisfying the so-called polynomial forms condition
[
19
, Definition 3.6]
, described later. In our multidimensional setting it will be crucial to assume that the initial polynomial configuration
\mathcal{P}_{V}
is in general position in order to verify estimate (
1.5
) for a family of polynomials
\underline{\mathcal{Q}}=(\underline{Q}_{1},\ldots,\underline{Q}_{s})
which also remain in general position.

The second part of the argument, is a transference argument of
[
20
]
adjusted to the multi-dimensional setting to obtain a decomposition of a function
f\geq 0
bounded by a pseudo-random measure
\nu^{(d)}=\nu^{(d)}_{N}
with respect to a given small quantity
\varepsilon>0
. More precisely, to show that given a function
f:\mathbb{Z}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
satisfying
0\leq f\leq\nu^{(d)}
, one has the decomposition

(1.6)

f=g+h,\quad 0\leq g\leq 2\quad\textit{and}\quad\|h\|_{\Box_{H,M}(\underline{Q}_{1},\ldots,\underline{Q}_{s})}\leq\varepsilon,

where we have used the notation,

(1.7)

\|h\|_{\Box_{H,M}(\underline{Q}_{1},\ldots,\underline{Q}_{s})}:=\big(\mathbb{E}_{\underline{h}\in[H]^{t}}\|f_{j}\|^{2^{s}}_{\Box_{M}(\underline{Q}_{1}(\underline{h}),\ldots,\underline{Q}_{s}(\underline{h})}\big)^{\frac{1}{2^{s}}}.

Note that both
\|\ \|_{\Box_{M}(\underline{u}_{1},\ldots,\underline{u}_{s})}
and
\|\ \|_{\Box_{H,M}(\underline{Q}_{1},\ldots,\underline{Q}_{s})}
are indeed norms.

The idea to use estimates like (
1.5
)-(
1.6
) to prove the existence of arithmetic progressions in the primes originated and was crucial in
[
9
]
, and later also in
[
19
,
20
]
for polynomial progressions. In our case starting from a set
A\subseteq[N]^{d}
of size
|A|\geq\delta\,|\mathbb{P}_{N}|^{d}
one construct a function
f_{A}:[N]^{d}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
so that
\mathbb{E}_{\underline{x}\in[N]^{d}}f_{A}(\underline{x})\geq c_{0}\,\delta
(for some absolute constant
c_{0}>0
) and
0\leq f_{A}(\underline{x})\leq\log^{(d)}(\underline{x})
with
\log^{(d)}(\underline{x})=\prod_{i=1}^{d}\log\,x_{i}
, simply by writing
f_{A}(\underline{x})=\mathbf{1}_{A}(\underline{x})\log^{(d)}(\underline{x})
. Then one may truncate the support of
f_{A}
to
[\sqrt{N},N-\sqrt{N}]^{d}
which changes its average only by a negligible amount, and then one may identifies
[N]^{d}
with
X=(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})^{d}
in estimating the averages
\Lambda_{\mathcal{P},V}(f_{A},\ldots,f_{A})
as the total size of the polynomial configuration is
O(\log^{CL}N)

which is much less that
\sqrt{N}
thus there no“wraparound” issues.

If one can find a pseudo-random measure
\nu^{(d)}_{N}(\underline{x})\gg\log^{(d)}(\underline{x})
for which (
1.5
) and (
1.6
) holds for functions

and

(1.8)

\Lambda_{\mathcal{P},V}(f_{A},\ldots,f_{A})=\Lambda_{\mathcal{P},V}(g,\ldots,g)+O(\varepsilon).

Invoking the multi-dimensional polynomial extension of Szemerédi’s theorem by Bergelson-Liebman
[

]
, more precisely its more quantitative version given in

one has that

(1.9)

\Lambda_{\mathcal{P},V}(g,\ldots,g)\geq c(\delta),

for some constant

for sufficiently large

, that may also depend on the initial data

. Choosing

it follows that

\Lambda_{\mathcal{P},V}(f_{A},\ldots,f_{A})>0,

which implies the existence of configurations

in

with

.

This cannot be done due to the irregularity of distribution of primes in small residue classes, however is possible after restricting

, and hence the support of

, onto a residue class

, where

is the product of small primes

, with

, i.e. after applying the so-called

]
.

Further directions.

If the polynomials
P_{0},\ldots,P_{l}
have distinct degrees it is expected that there are good explicit lower bounds for the constants

in estimate (
1.9
). In fact, in light of the recent quantitative breakthrough results the in one dimension by Peluse
[
15
]
, by Peluse-Prendiville-Shao
[

]
, Kravitz-Kuca-Leng
[

and by Kosz-Mirek-Peluse-Wright

it is plausible that one may take

or equivalently
\delta=(\log\log\,N)^{-c}
. The various

We expect that our main result holds in full generality, without the assumption that the polynomial configuration
\mathcal{P}_{V}(y)
being in general position, at least without the restriction

on the ”size” of the configuration. If the polynomials
P_{1}(y),\ldots,P_{l}(y)

have the same degree

but different main terms then such results may be obtained using the very recent quantitative result of Matthiesen-Terävävainen-Wang
[

on the asymptotic distribution of polynomial progressions

in
\mathbb{P}_{N}
, combined with the sampling argument of Fox-Zhao
[

]
. Indeed, then one one may allow patterns whose projections to the coordinate axis forms such polynomial progressions, for example configurations of the form

\mathcal{P}_{V}(y)=\{P_{11}(y)\underline{e}_{1},\ldots,P_{l_{1}1}(y)\underline{e}_{1},\ldots,P_{1d}(y)\underline{e}_{d},\ldots,P_{l_{d}d}(y)\underline{e}_{d}\}
where

being the standard basis vectors of

. However, to extend this approach to polynomials whose degrees may not be the same, or to ”small” configurations one would need to prove some instances of the Bateman-Horn conjectures

with are currently out of reach.

We follow
[
20
]
to define the the weighted indicator function of the set
A\subseteq\mathbb{P}_{N^{\prime}}
truncated and restricted to an appropriate reduced residue class
\underline{x}\equiv\underline{b}\pmod{W}

w=w(N^{\prime})=\frac{1}{10}\log\log\log\,N^{\prime}

(

If

for sufficiently large

where we used the averaging notation

. Here

is an absolute constant needed to be able to majorize the function

by the function

, where is the

]
, namely

\nu_{i}(x):=\frac{\phi(W)\log\,R}{W}\left(\sum_{d|Wx+b_{i}}\mu(d)\chi\left(\frac{\log\,d}{\log\,R}\right)\right),

for

, where

,

. It is easy to see that if we choose

0\leq f_{A}(\underline{x})\leq\nu^{(d)}(\underline{x})\quad\textit{for all}\quad\underline{x}\in X.

the pseudo-randomness property of the product measure

we need is a multi-dimensional analogue of the so-called polynomial forms condition of Tao-Ziegler

where

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\underline{\mathcal{Q}}=(\underline{Q}_{1},\ldots,\underline{Q}_{J}):\mathbb{Z}^{t}\to\mathbb{Z}^{d}
be a polynomial map. We say that the map
\underline{\mathcal{Q}}
is non-degenerate if

(2.7)

\deg\,(\pi_{i}(\underline{Q}_{j}-\underline{Q}_{j^{\prime}}))\geq 1,

for all
1\leq j<j^{\prime}\leq J
,
1\leq i\leq d
, where
\pi_{i}
is the natural projection the

Note in the special case

the notion of being non-degenerate is weaker then the notion of being in general position given in Definition

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
t,d,D,J
be fixed natural numbers, let
\varepsilon_{0}
sufficiently small and

sufficiently large depending on

. Let
\underline{\mathcal{Q}}=(\underline{Q}_{1},\ldots,\underline{Q}_{J}):\mathbb{Z}^{t}\to\mathbb{Z}^{d}
be a non-degenerate integral polynomial map of degree at most

, with coefficients of size

. Then

(2.8)

\mathbb{E}_{\underline{h}\in[H]^{t}}\ \mathbb{E}_{\underline{x}\in X}\prod_{j=1}^{J}\nu^{(d)}(\underline{x}+\underline{Q}_{j}(\underline{h}))=1+o(1),

where
H=\log^{\sqrt{L}}N
and
X=(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})^{d}\simeq[N]^{d}
.

The following simple estimates involving the

Lemma 2.1
.

Let

for

(2.9)

\prod_{j=1}^{d}(1+o(1)+C_{i}Exp\,(\alpha_{i})\leq 1+o(1)+\prod_{j=1}^{d}C_{i}\,Exp\,(\alpha_{1}+\ldots+\alpha_{d})

(2.10)

Exp\,(\alpha_{1}+\ldots+\alpha_{d})\leq Exp\,(2^{d}\alpha_{1})+\ldots+Exp\,(2^{d}\alpha_{d}).

(2.11)

\prod_{j=1}^{d}(1+o(1)+C\,Exp\,(\alpha_{i}))\leq 1+o(1)+C^{d}\sum_{j=1}^{d}Exp\,(2^{d}\alpha_{j})\quad(C>1).

Proof.

For

\displaystyle\ (1+o(1)+C_{1}Exp\,(\alpha_{1}))\,(1+o(1)+C_{2}Exp\,(\alpha_{2}))

\displaystyle=1+o(1)+C_{1}(1+o(1))\,Exp\,(\alpha_{1})+C_{2}(1+o(1))\,Exp\,(\alpha_{2})+C_{1}C_{2}\,Exp\,(\alpha_{1})Exp\,(\alpha_{2})

3
PET induction and the generalized von Neumann inequality.

We describe the PET induction scheme originally devised by Bergelson and Leibman
[
2
]
for bounded function and later extended by Tao and Ziegler to functions bounded by a pseudo-random measure
\nu
. We’ll follow the notation of
[
19
]
except that in our multi-dimensional setting we will have
l
shift operators in the directions of the vector
\underline{v}_{1},\ldots,\underline{v}_{l}
, namely
T_{j}f(\underline{x}):=f(\underline{x}+\underline{v}_{j})
for
1\leq j\leq l
.
We will estimate averages of the form

(3.1)

\Lambda_{\mathcal{P}}(\overrightarrow{f})=\mathbb{E}_{\underline{x}\in X}\mathbb{E}_{y\in[M]}\prod_{j=1}^{d}T_{j}^{P_{j}(y)}f_{j}(\underline{x}),

for a family of functions
\overrightarrow{f}=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{l})
satisfying
|f_{j}|\leq\nu^{(d)}
,
using repeated applications of van der Courput’s lemma
[
19
]
which in our context is the following simple observation. Let
x_{n}
be a sequence of real numbers satisfying
x_{n}=O(\log^{C}N)
uniformly in
n
. Let
M=\log^{L}N
and
H=\log^{\sqrt{L}}N
with
L
sufficiently large with respect
C
. Then,

(3.2)

\mathbb{E}_{n\in[M]}\ x_{n}=\mathbb{E}_{h\in H}\mathbb{E}_{n\in[M]}\ x_{n+h}+o(1),

as shifting the average by a small amount
h
will change it only slightly. Then by the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality,

(3.3)

|\mathbb{E}_{n\in[M]}\ x_{n}|^{2}\leq\mathbb{E}_{n\in[M]}\mathbb{E}_{h,h^{\prime}\in[H]}\ x_{n+h}\,x_{n+h^{\prime}}+o(1),

After each application we will arrive at a new system of polynomials some of which will have reduced degrees in the
y
-variable or applied to the measure
\nu^{(d)}
in place of a function
f_{j}
. Eventually, one arrives at a system where each polynomial corresponding to a shift applied to one of the functions
f_{j}
is linear in
y
but may depend on the number of additional small parameters
h_{1},\ldots,h_{t}
. Averages over such systems are then majorized by polynomial averages of local box norms, described in the introduction.

Example
. Let us first illustrate the procedure with an example. Let
d=2
,
T_{i}f(\underline{x}):=f(x+\underline{v}_{i})
for
i=1,2
.

\Lambda(\overrightarrow{f})=\mathbb{E}_{\underline{x}}\,\mathbb{E}_{y}\ f_{0}\cdot T_{1}^{y}f_{1}\cdot T_{2}^{y^{2}}f_{2},

where
\underline{x}\in X
,
y\in[M]
, and
|f_{i}|\leq\nu:=\nu^{(2)}
. We assume that
\underline{v}_{i}
has no zero coordinates so the system
\mathcal{P}(y)=(\underline{0},y\underline{v}_{1},y^{2}\underline{v}_{2})
is in general position. Then,

|\Lambda(\overrightarrow{f})|\leq\mathbb{E}_{\underline{x}}\,\nu\,|\mathbb{E}_{y}\,T_{1}^{y}f_{1}\cdot T_{2}^{y^{2}}f_{2}|,

hence by van der Courput’s lemma

|\Lambda(\overrightarrow{f})|^{2}\leq\mathbb{E}_{\underline{x}}\,\nu\,\mathbb{E}_{h,h^{\prime}}\mathbb{E}_{y}\,T_{1}^{y+h}f_{1}\cdot T_{1}^{y+h^{\prime}}f_{1}\cdot T_{2}^{(y+h)^{2}}f_{2}\cdot T_{2}^{(y+h^{\prime})^{2}}f_{2}+o(1)

Now we shift the variables
\underline{x}
to
\underline{x}-y\underline{v}_{1}
which does not change the average but which amounts to multiplying each factor with
T_{1}^{-y}
, thus we get

|\Lambda(\overrightarrow{f})|^{2}\leq\mathbb{E}_{\underline{x}}\,\mathbb{E}_{h,h^{\prime}}\,T_{1}^{h}\nu\cdot T_{1}^{h^{\prime}}\nu\ |\mathbb{E}_{y}\,T_{1}^{-y}\nu\cdot T_{1}^{-y}T_{2}^{(y+h)^{2}}f_{2}\cdot T_{1}^{-y}T_{2}^{(y+h^{\prime})^{2}}f_{2}|+o(1).

By the polynomial forms condition
\mathbb{E}_{\underline{x}}\mathbb{E}_{h,h^{\prime}}\,T_{1}^{h}\nu\cdot T_{1}^{h^{\prime}}\nu=1+o(1)
hence after one more application of van der Courput’s lemma,

\displaystyle|\Lambda(\overrightarrow{f})|^{4}

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}_{\underline{x}}\,\mathbb{E}_{h,h^{\prime}}\,\mathbb{E}_{k,k^{\prime}}\,T_{1}^{h}\nu\cdot T_{1}^{h^{\prime}}\nu\ \ \mathbb{E}_{y}\ T_{1}^{-y-k}\nu\cdot T_{1}^{-y-k^{\prime}}\nu\cdot

\displaystyle\cdot T_{1}^{-y-k}T_{2}^{(y+h+k)^{2}}f_{2}\cdot T_{1}^{-y-k}T_{2}^{(y+h^{\prime}+k)^{2}}f_{2}\cdot T_{1}^{-y-k^{\prime}}T_{2}^{(y+h+k^{\prime})^{2}}f_{2}\cdot T_{1}^{-y-k^{\prime}}T_{2}^{(y+h^{\prime}+k^{\prime})^{2}}f_{2}+o(1).

Note that there are four quadratic polynomials in
y
however each have the same main term
y^{2}
thus after shifting the variables
\underline{x}
to
\underline{x}-y^{2}\underline{v}_{2}
, which amounts to multiplying each factor with
T_{2}^{-y^{2}}
, all exponents of the shift operators
T_{1},T_{2}
applied to the function

will be linear.

Doing this procedure in general leads to polynomial averages of the form

where

(3.5)

\underline{R}_{\alpha}(y,h_{1},\ldots,h_{t};W)=\sum_{j=1}^{l}\,R_{j,\alpha}(y,h_{1},\ldots,h_{t};W)\,\underline{v}_{j},

and

(3.6)

\displaystyle T^{\underline{R}_{\alpha}(y,h_{1},\ldots,h_{t};W)}f_{\alpha}(\underline{x})

\displaystyle:=\prod_{j=1}^{l}T_{j}^{R_{j,\alpha}\,(y,h_{1},\ldots,h_{t};W)}f_{\alpha}(\underline{x})

\displaystyle=f_{\alpha}(\underline{x}+\underline{R}_{\alpha}(y,h_{1},\ldots,h_{t};W)),

using notation similar to
[
2

19

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
(\mathcal{R},\overrightarrow{f})=\{(\underline{R}_{\alpha},f_{\alpha}):\,\alpha\in\mathcal{A}\}
be a polynomial system in general position with distinguished node
\alpha_{0}
. Assume the system
\mathcal{R}
is in general position with respect to
\alpha_{0}
, and there is an active non-linear node
\alpha^{\ast}
.

Then there exists a polynomial system

(\mathcal{R}^{\prime},\overrightarrow{f}^{\prime})=\{(\underline{R}_{\alpha^{\prime}}^{\prime},f^{\prime}_{\alpha^{\prime}}):\,\alpha^{\prime}\in\mathcal{A}^{\prime}\}

in general position with respect to a distinguished node
\alpha_{0}^{\prime}
, and an active node
\alpha^{\prime}
such that,

(3.17)

\mathfrak{W}_{\mathcal{R}^{\prime}}(\alpha^{\prime})\prec\mathfrak{W}_{\mathcal{R}}(\alpha^{\ast}).

and

(3.18)

|\Lambda_{\mathcal{R}}(\overrightarrow{f})|^{2}\ll|\Lambda_{\mathcal{R}^{\prime}}(\overrightarrow{f}^{\prime})|+o_{N\to\infty}(1),

Moreover
f^{\prime}_{\alpha_{0}}=f_{\alpha_{0}}
, and for each
\alpha^{\prime}\in\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
one has that
f^{\prime}_{\alpha^{\prime}}=f_{\alpha}
for some
\alpha\in\mathcal{A}
, or
f^{\prime}_{\alpha^{\prime}}=\nu^{(d)}

Proof.

Shifting the system
\mathcal{R}
by
\underline{R}_{\alpha^{\ast}}
, which amounts to changing the polynomials
\underline{R}_{\alpha}\to\underline{R}_{\alpha}-\underline{R}_{\alpha^{\ast}}
, we may assume that
\underline{R}_{\alpha^{\ast}}=0
, where
\alpha^{\ast}
is a fixed non-linear node with respect to
\alpha_{0}

Write
\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{A}_{0}\cup\mathcal{A}_{1}
, where
\mathcal{A}_{0}=\{\beta\in\mathcal{A}:\ \deg_{y}\,(\underline{R}_{\beta})=0\}
and
\mathcal{A}_{1}:=\mathcal{A}\backslash\mathcal{A}_{0}
. Accordingly, write

G(x,\underline{h}):=\prod_{\beta\in\mathcal{A}_{0}}T^{\underline{R}_{\beta}(\underline{h})}f_{\beta}(\underline{x})

and note that

|G(\underline{x},\underline{h})|\leq H(\underline{x},\underline{h}):=\prod_{\beta\in\mathcal{A}_{0}}T^{\underline{R}_{\beta}(\underline{h})}\ \nu^{(d)}_{\beta}(\underline{x}).

Then by the polynomial forms condition we have that,

(3.19)

\mathbb{E}_{\underline{h}\in[H]^{t}}\,\mathbb{E}_{\underline{x}\in X}H(\underline{x},\underline{h})=1+o(1).

Let

(3.20)

F(\underline{x},y,\underline{h}):=\prod_{\beta\in\mathcal{A}_{1}}T^{\underline{R}_{\beta}(y,\underline{h})}f_{\beta}(\underline{x}),

then

(3.21)

\displaystyle\mid\Lambda_{\mathcal{P}}(\overrightarrow{f})\mid

\displaystyle\leq\,\mathbb{E}_{\underline{h}\in[H]^{t}}\ \mathbb{E}_{\underline{x}\in X}\ H(\underline{x},\underline{h})\,\mid\mathbb{E}_{y\in[M]}F(\underline{x},y,\underline{h})\mid

\displaystyle\leq\,\mathbb{E}_{\underline{h}\in[H]^{t}}\ \mathbb{E}_{\underline{x}\in X}\ H(\underline{x},\underline{h})\,\mathbb{E}_{y\in[M]}\mid\mathbb{E}_{h\in[H]}\,F(\underline{x},y+h,\underline{h})\mid+o(1).

Here the

\mathcal{R}
. Applying the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality in the

3.19
), we get

\displaystyle\mid\Lambda_{\mathcal{P}}(\overrightarrow{f})\mid^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\,\mathbb{E}_{(\underline{h},h^{1},h^{2})\in[H]^{t+2}}\mathbb{E}_{\underline{x}\in X}\,\mathbb{E}_{y\in[M]}\,H(\underline{x},\underline{h})\,F(\underline{x},y+h^{1},\underline{h})\,F(\underline{x},y+h^{2},\underline{h})+o(1)

where

(3.23)

, so that for the nodes
\beta^{1}\in\mathcal{A}_{1}^{1}

\pi_{i}(\underline{R_{\alpha^{\prime}}})\neq 0
hence contain a term depending only on the variables
\underline{h}
. Finally the polynomials in (

for

, and they are distinct from the polynomials

-variable.

This shows the validity of (

). Since our initial family of functions is

we have that

, and after re-indexing and renaming the variables, we may write

thus we have for all

(3.35)

\big|\Lambda_{\mathcal{P},V}(\overrightarrow{f})\big|\leq||f_{k}||^{2^{-S_{k}}}_{\Box_{H,M}(\underline{Q}^{k}_{j}(\underline{h}^{k}):\ j\in[s_{k}])}+o(1).

Note that the norms on right side of (

\underline{\mathcal{Q}}=(\underline{Q}_{1}(\underline{h}),\ldots,\underline{Q}_{s}(\underline{h}):\ \underline{h}\in[H]^{t})
and
\underline{\mathcal{Q}}^{\prime}=(\underline{Q}_{1}^{\prime}(\underline{h}^{\prime}),\ldots,\underline{Q}_{s^{\prime}}(\underline{h}^{\prime}):\ \underline{h}^{\prime}\in[H]^{t^{\prime}})

\underline{\mathcal{Q}}\oplus\underline{\mathcal{Q}}^{\prime}=(\underline{Q}_{1}(\underline{h}),\ldots,\underline{Q}_{s}(\underline{h}),\underline{Q}_{1}^{\prime}(\underline{h}^{\prime}),\ldots,\underline{Q}_{s^{\prime}}(\underline{h}^{\prime}):\ (h,h^{\prime})\in[H]^{t+t^{\prime}}

of more that two polynomial systems
\underline{\mathcal{Q}}^{1},\ldots,\underline{\mathcal{Q}}^{l}

[

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\underline{\mathcal{Q}}=\underline{\mathcal{Q}}^{1}\oplus\ldots\oplus\underline{\mathcal{Q}}^{l}
. Then one has for any

(3.36)

||f||_{\Box_{H,M}(\underline{\mathcal{Q}})}\geq||f||_{\Box_{H,M}(\underline{\mathcal{Q}}^{k})},

for all
1\leq k\leq l
.

Note that if

is in general position and moreover if

Corollary 3.1
.

(

Let
\mathcal{P}=(P_{1}(y)\underline{v}_{1},\ldots,P_{l}(y)\underline{v}_{l})

\big|\Lambda_{\mathcal{P},V}(\overrightarrow{f})\big|\leq\min_{1\leq k\leq l}\,||f_{k}||^{2^{-S}}_{\Box_{H,M}(\underline{Q}_{1},\ldots,\underline{Q}_{s})}+o(1).

4
Dual functions and the main decomposition.

In this section we prove the crucial decomposition (
1.6
) following
[
20
]
which combines Gowers approach to decompositions theorems based on the Hahn-Banach theorem
[
8
]
. The starting point is the show the orthogonality of
\nu^{(d)}-1
to products of so-called
dual functions
associated to polynomial averages of local box norms.

Definition 4.1
.

Let

\underline{\mathcal{Q}}=(\underline{Q}_{1},\ldots,\underline{Q}_{s}):\ [H]^{t}\to\mathbb{Z}^{sd}
be a polynomial map where
\underline{Q}_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}[h_{1},\ldots,h_{t}]
.
Let
f_{\underline{\omega}}:X\to\mathbb{R}
,

\underline{\omega}\in\{0,1\}^{s}\backslash\{\underline{0}\}

be a family of functions. We define the dual function of the family of functions
(f_{\underline{\omega}})_{\underline{\omega}\in\{0,1\}^{s}\backslash\{\underline{0}\}}
, as

(4.1)

D_{\underline{\mathcal{Q}}}(f_{\underline{\omega}})(\underline{x}):=\mathbb{E}_{\underline{h}\in[H]^{t}}\,\mathbb{E}_{\underline{y}^{(0)},\underline{y}^{(1)}\in[M]^{s}}\,\prod_{\underline{\omega}\in\{0,1\}^{s},\underline{\omega}\neq\underline{0}}f_{\underline{\omega}}\big(\underline{x}+y_{1}^{(\omega_{1})}\underline{Q}_{1}(\underline{h})+\ldots+y_{s}^{(\omega_{s})}\underline{Q}_{s}(\underline{h})\big).

If
f_{\underline{\omega}}=f
for all
\underline{\omega}\in{\{0,1\}^{s}\backslash\{\underline{0}\}}
then we write
D_{\underline{\mathcal{Q}}}(f)
and refer to
D_{\underline{\mathcal{Q}}}(f)
as the dual function of the function
f
.

This means that for fixed
\underline{h}
and fixed
\underline{y}^{(0)}=(y_{1}^{(0)},\ldots,y_{s}^{(1)})
,
\underline{y}^{(1)}=(y_{1}^{(1)},\ldots,y_{s}^{(1)})

we take the product of the functions
f_{\underline{\omega}}
evaluated at the vertices
\underline{x}+y_{1}^{(\omega_{1})}\underline{Q}_{1}(\underline{h})+\ldots+y_{s}^{(\omega_{s})}\underline{Q}_{s}(\underline{h})
forming a parallelepiped as
\underline{\omega}=(\omega_{1},\ldots,\omega_{s})
runs through all
{\{0,1\}^{s}\backslash\{\underline{0}\}}
, where the vectors
\underline{Q}_{1}(\underline{h}),\ldots,uQ_{s}(\underline{h})
give the directions of the side vectors of the parallelepiped, and then we sum the products for all such parallelepipeds was
\underline{y}^{(0)},\underline{y}^{(1)}
are running through
[M]^{s}
and
\underline{h}
through
[H]^{t}
.
The terminology

is originated in
[
9
]
for global Gowers norms, and is due to the fact that the inner product,

(4.2)

\langle f,D_{\underline{\mathcal{Q}}}(f)\rangle:=\mathbb{E}_{\underline{x}\in X}f(\underline{x})D_{\underline{\mathcal{Q}}}(f)(\underline{x})=\mathbb{E}_{\underline{h}\in[H]^{t}}\,\|f\|_{\Box_{M}(\underline{Q}_{1}(\underline{h}),\ldots,\underline{Q}_{s}(\underline{h}))}^{2^{s}}.

The Gowers type local box-norm inner product of a family of functions
(f_{\underline{\omega}})_{\underline{\omega}\in\{0,1\}^{s}}
of scale
M
and directions
\underline{u}_{1},\ldots,\underline{u}_{s}
, is defined as

(4.3)

\langle f_{\omega}\rangle_{\Box_{M}(\underline{u}_{1},\ldots,\underline{u}_{s})}:=\mathbb{E}_{\underline{x}\in X}\mathbb{E}_{\underline{y}^{(0)},\underline{y}^{(1)}\in[M]^{s}}\,\prod_{\underline{\omega}\in\{0,1\}^{s}}f_{\underline{\omega}}\big(\underline{x}+y_{1}^{(\omega_{1})}\underline{u}_{1}+\ldots+y_{s}^{(\omega_{s})}\underline{u}_{s}\big).

Let us recall a basic inequality referred to as the Gowers-Cauchy-Schwarz inequality for box norms that is well-known in various forms see e.g.
[
19
, Appendix B]
.

Lemma 4.1
.

One has

(4.4)

\big|\langle f_{\omega}\rangle_{\Box_{M}(\underline{u}_{1},\ldots,\underline{u}_{s})}\big|\leq\prod_{\underline{\omega}\in\{0,1\}^{s}}\|f_{\underline{\omega}}\|_{\Box_{M}(\underline{u}_{1},\ldots,\underline{u}_{s})}

Proof.

Let us write.

f_{\underline{\omega},\underline{x}}(y_{1},\ldots,y_{s}):=f_{\underline{\omega}}(\underline{x}+y_{1}\underline{u}_{1}+\ldots+y_{s}\underline{u}_{s}).

For a fixed
\underline{x}\in X
, we show

(4.5)

\big|\mathbb{E}_{\underline{y}^{(0)},\underline{y}^{(1)}\in[M]^{s}}\,\prod_{\underline{\omega}\in\{0,1\}^{s}}f_{\underline{\omega},\underline{x}}(y_{1}^{(\omega_{1})},\ldots,y_{s}^{(\omega_{s})})\big|\leq\prod_{\underline{\omega}\in\{0,1\}^{s}}\|f_{\underline{\omega},\underline{x}}\|_{\Box_{M}},

where for a function
f:X\to\mathbb{R}
,

\|f\|_{\Box_{M}}^{2^{s}}=\mathbb{E}_{\underline{y}^{(0)},\underline{y}^{(1)}\in[M]^{s}}\prod_{\underline{\omega}\in\{0,1\}^{s}}f(y_{1}^{(\omega_{1})},\ldots,y_{s}^{(\omega_{s})}).

This is easy to see by repeated application of the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality. Indeed, separating the last variables and writing
\underline{y}^{(0)}=({\underline{y}^{(0)}}^{\prime},y_{s}^{(0)})

\underline{y}^{(1)}=({\underline{y}^{(1)}}^{\prime},y_{s}^{(1)})
, one may write

\displaystyle\langle f_{\omega}\rangle_{\Box_{M}(\underline{u}_{1},\ldots,\underline{u}_{s})}=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{{\underline{y}^{(0)}}^{\prime},{\underline{y}^{(1)}}^{\prime}}\big(\mathbb{E}_{y_{s}^{(0)}}\,\prod_{\underline{\omega}^{\prime}\in\{0,1\}^{s-1}}f_{(\underline{\omega}^{\prime},0)}(y_{1}^{(\omega_{1})},\ldots,y_{s-1}^{(\omega_{s-1})},y_{s}^{(0)})\big)\,\big(\mathbb{E}_{y_{s}^{(1)}}\,\prod_{\underline{\omega}^{\prime}\in\{0,1\}^{s-1}}f_{(\underline{\omega}^{\prime},1)}(y_{1}^{(\omega_{1})},\ldots,y_{s-1}^{(\omega_{s-1})},y_{s}^{(1)})\big)

Then by the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality

\displaystyle\langle f_{\omega}\rangle_{\Box_{M}(\underline{u}_{1},\ldots,\underline{u}_{s})}^{2}\leq

\displaystyle\big(\mathbb{E}_{{\underline{y}^{(0)}}^{\prime},{\underline{y}^{(1)}}^{\prime}}\,\mathbb{E}_{y_{s}^{(0)},y_{s}^{(1)}}\,\prod_{\underline{\omega}\in\{0,1\}^{s}}f_{(\underline{\omega}^{\prime},0)}(y_{1}^{(\omega_{1})},\ldots,y_{s-1}^{(\omega_{s-1})},y_{s}^{(0)})\,f_{(\underline{\omega}^{\prime},0)}(y_{1}^{(\omega_{1})},\ldots,y_{s-1}^{(\omega_{s-1})},y_{s}^{(1)})\big)

\displaystyle\big(\mathbb{E}_{{\underline{y}^{(0)}}^{\prime},{\underline{y}^{(1)}}^{\prime}}\,\mathbb{E}_{y_{s}^{(0)},y_{s}^{(1)}}\,\prod_{\underline{\omega}\in\{0,1\}^{s}}f_{(\underline{\omega}^{\prime},1)}(y_{1}^{(\omega_{1})},\ldots,y_{s-1}^{(\omega_{s-1})},y_{s}^{(0)})\,f_{(\underline{\omega}^{\prime},1)}(y_{1}^{(\omega_{1})},\ldots,y_{s-1}^{(\omega_{s-1})},y_{s}^{(1)})\big)

Notice that in each factors the functions
f_{\underline{\omega}}
depend only on the first
s-1
components of
\underline{\omega}
. Repeating the procedure in the other
s-1
variables give (
4.5
). Then by Hölder’s inequality left side of (
4.3
) is estimated by

\displaystyle\langle f_{\omega}\rangle_{\Box_{M}(\underline{u}_{1},\ldots,\underline{u}_{s})}^{2^{s}}=\bigg(\mathbb{E}_{\underline{x}}\,\mathbb{E}_{\underline{y}^{(0)},\underline{y}^{(1)}\in[M]^{s}}\,\prod_{\underline{\omega}\in\{0,1\}^{s}}f_{\underline{\omega},\underline{x}}(y_{1}^{(\omega_{1})},\ldots,y_{s}^{(\omega_{s})})\bigg)^{2^{s}}

\displaystyle\leq\big(\mathbb{E}_{\underline{x}}\,\prod_{\underline{\omega}\in\{0,1\}^{s}}\|f_{\underline{\omega},\underline{x}}\|_{\Box_{M}}\big)^{2^{s}}\leq\prod_{\underline{\omega}\in\{0,1\}^{s}}\big(\mathbb{E}_{\underline{x}}\,\|f_{\underline{\omega},\underline{x}}\|_{\Box_{M}}\big)^{2^{s}}\big)=\prod_{\underline{\omega}\in\{0,1\}^{s}}\|f_{\omega}\|_{\Box_{M}(\underline{u}_{1},\ldots,\underline{u}_{s})}^{2^{s}}.

∎

The following orthogonality property is crucial for the decomposition (

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
K,s\geq 1
and for
1\leq k\leq K

be given for
\underline{\omega}\in\{0,1\}^{s}\backslash\{\underline{0}\}
. Let
\underline{\mathcal{Q}}=(\underline{Q}_{0},\underline{Q}_{1},\ldots,\underline{Q}_{s})

\underline{Q}_{0}=\underline{0}

\underline{Q}_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}[\underline{h}]
be a family of polynomial maps in general position, satisfying condition (

(4.6)

\langle(\nu^{(d)}-1),\prod_{k=1}^{K}D_{\underline{\mathcal{Q}}}(f^{k}_{\underline{\omega}})\rangle:=\mathbb{E}_{\underline{x}\in X}\ \big(\nu^{(d)}(\underline{x})-1\big)\cdot\prod_{k=1}^{K}D_{\underline{\mathcal{Q}}}(f^{k}_{\underline{\omega}})(\underline{x})=o(1),

where the implicit constant may depend on
K,s
and the polynomial map
\underline{\mathcal{Q}}
,

Proof.

By expanding the product of the dual functions, we have

(4.7)

\prod_{k=1}^{K}D_{\underline{\mathcal{Q}}}(f^{k}_{\underline{\omega}})\,(\underline{x})=\mathbb{E}_{\underline{h}_{1},\ldots,\underline{h}_{k}}\,\mathbb{E}_{\begin{subarray}{c}\underline{y}^{(0)}_{1},\ldots,\underline{y}^{(0)}_{k}\\
\underline{y}^{(1)}_{1},\ldots,\underline{y}^{(1)}_{K}\end{subarray}}\ \prod_{k=1}^{K}\prod^{\ast}_{\underline{\omega}\in\{0,1\}^{s}}f^{k}_{\underline{\omega}}\,\big(\underline{x}+y_{k1}^{(\omega_{1})}\underline{Q}_{1}(\underline{h}_{k})+\ldots+y_{ks}^{(\omega_{s})}\underline{Q}_{s}(\underline{h}_{k})\big),

where we have written
\prod^{\ast}_{\omega}
for the restricted product
\prod_{\underline{\omega}\in\{0,1\}^{s},\underline{\omega}\neq\underline{0}}
for simplicity of notations.

Let
B_{K,t}(H)
be the set of those

(\underline{h}_{1},\ldots,\underline{h}_{K})\in[H]^{kt}

\pi_{i}(\underline{Q}_{j}(\underline{h}_{k}))=0

and

is not zero by our assumption. Thus the contribution of the
K

) is

.

Let
(\underline{h}_{1}\,\ldots,\underline{h}_{K})\notin B_{k,t}(H)
be a fixed
K
-tuple. We estimate

(4.8)

\mathbb{E}_{\underline{x}}\big(\nu^{(d)}(\underline{x})-1\big)\ \mathbb{E}_{\begin{subarray}{c}\underline{y}^{(0)}_{1},\ldots,\underline{y}^{(0)}_{k}\\
\underline{y}^{(1)}_{1},\ldots,\underline{y}^{(1)}_{K}\end{subarray}}\ \prod_{k=1}^{K}\prod^{\ast}_{\underline{\omega}\in\{0,1\}^{s}}f^{k}_{\underline{\omega}}\,\big(\underline{x}+y_{k1}^{(\omega_{1})}\underline{Q}_{1}(\underline{h}_{k})+\ldots+y_{ks}^{(\omega_{s})}\underline{Q}_{s}(\underline{h}_{k})\big).

Note that the product is taken over the union of
K
-distinct parallelepipeds with vertices
\underline{x}+y_{k1}^{(\omega_{1})}\underline{Q}_{1}(\underline{h}_{k})+\ldots+y_{ks}^{(\omega_{s})}\underline{Q}_{s}(\underline{h}_{k})
. We may embed these
K
parallelepipeds into a
Ks
-dimensional parallelepiped with vertices of the form

\underline{x}+\sum_{j=1}^{s}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\ y_{k1}^{(\omega_{k1})}\underline{Q}_{1}(\underline{h}_{k})+\ldots+y_{ks}^{(\omega_{ks})}\underline{Q}_{s}(\underline{h}_{k}),

corresponding to

\underline{\Omega}=(\underline{\omega}_{1},\ldots,\underline{\omega}_{K})
,
\underline{\omega}_{k}=(\omega_{k1},\ldots,\omega_{ks})\in\{0,1\}^{s}
.
Define
f_{\underline{\Omega}}=1
unless

\Omega=(\underline{0},\ldots,\underline{0},\underline{\omega},\underline{0},\ldots,\underline{0})
, i.e. when
\underline{\omega}_{j}=\underline{0}
for all
j\neq k
and
\underline{\omega}_{k}=\underline{\omega}
; in which case let
f_{\underline{\Omega}}=f^{k}_{\underline{\omega}}
. In other words, to all the remaining vertices of
Ks
-dimensional parallelepiped we attach weight 1. Then the expression in (
4.8
) may be written as

(4.9)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\underline{x}}\,\big(\nu^{(d)}(\underline{x})-1\big)\ \mathbb{E}_{\underline{y}^{(0)}_{1},\ldots,\underline{y}^{(0)}_{K},\underline{y}^{(1)}_{1},\ldots,\underline{y}^{(1)}_{K}}\ \prod_{\underline{\Omega}\in\{0,1\}^{Ks}}f_{\underline{\Omega}}\,\big(\underline{x}+\sum_{k=1}^{K}\sum_{j=1}^{s}y_{kj}^{(\omega_{kj})}\underline{Q}_{j}(\underline{h}_{k})\big)

\displaystyle=\big\langle\nu^{(d)}-1,f_{\underline{\Omega}}\big\rangle_{\Box_{M}(\underline{Q}_{j}(\underline{h}_{k}):\,j\in[s],k\in[K])}.

By the Gowers-Cauchy-Schwarz inequality for box norms we have that

(4.10)

\big|\big\langle\nu^{(d)}-1,f_{\underline{\Omega}}\big\rangle_{\Box_{M}(\underline{Q}_{j}(\underline{h}_{k}):\,j\in[s],k\in[K])}\big|\leq\|\nu^{(d)}-1\|_{\Box_{M}(\underline{Q}_{j}(\underline{h}_{k}):\,j\in[s],k\in[K])},

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\underline{h}_{1},\ldots,\underline{h}_{k}}\|\nu^{(d)}-1\|_{\Box_{M}(\underline{Q}_{j}(\underline{h}_{k}):\,j\in[s],k\in[K])}\leq\bigg(\mathbb{E}_{\underline{h}_{1},\ldots,\underline{h}_{K}}\|\nu^{(d)}-1\|^{2^{Ks}}_{\Box_{M}(\underline{Q}_{j}(\underline{h}_{k}):\,j\in[s],k\in[K])}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2^{Ks}}}=o(1),

Using the orthogonality property (
4.6
) one can obtain the crucial decomposition (
1.6
) of an unbounded function
0\leq f\leq\nu^{(d)}
via an abstract decomposition theorem which appears implicitly in
[
9
]
and is stated in various essentially explicit forms in
[

,
8
,
20
]
. We recall the statement from
[

]
and for the sake of completeness provide a proof, which is a slight modification of an argument of Gowers
[
8
, Theorem 4.8]
.

Theorem D
.

(Dense model theorem
[
20
]
)

Let
\varepsilon>0
and let
\mathcal{F}
be a set of bounded functions
F:X\to[-1,1]
. Then there exists a constant
C>0
and a constant
\eta>0
depending only on
\varepsilon
such that the following holds; If a function
\nu:X\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
obeys the bound

(4.11)

\mathbb{E}_{\underline{x}\in X}\big(\nu(\underline{x})-1\big)\,F_{1}\cdot\ldots\cdot F_{K}(x)\leq\eta,

for all
K\leq\varepsilon^{-C}
and
F_{j}\in\mathcal{F}
, then to every function
0\leq f\leq\nu
there exists a function
0\leq g\leq 2
such that

(4.12)

\big|\mathbb{E}_{\underline{x}\in X}\big(f(\underline{x})-g(\underline{x})\big)\,F(\underline{x})\,\big|\leq\varepsilon,

for all
F\in\mathcal{F}
.

This means that if the function
\nu-1
is approximately orthogonal to long products of functions
F_{i}\in\mathcal{F}
then every function
0\leq f\leq\nu
can be approximated by a bounded function
g
so that
f-g
is approximately orthogonal to all function
F\in\mathcal{F}
. If
1\in\mathcal{F}
(as in most cases of interest) then
f
and
g
has approximately the same average, and
g
is referred to as a dense model of
f
for the family of functions
\mathcal{F}
.

Proof.

Let
\epsilon>0
, and define
J:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
by
J(x)=\frac{x+|x|}{2}
. By the Weirerstrass approximation theorem, there exists a polynomial
P(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{j}a_{i}x^{i}
such that

(4.13)

|P(x)-J(x)|<\frac{1}{8}

for all
x\in[-2\varepsilon^{-1},2\varepsilon^{-1}]
. Let
C
be large enough that
\varepsilon^{-C}>j
, and let
\eta=\frac{1}{4}\left(\sum_{i=0}^{j}|a_{i}|(2\varepsilon^{-1})^{i}\right)^{-1}
.

Let
\nu:X\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
be a function satisfying (
4.11
), and suppose for the sake of contradiction there exists a function
0\leq f\leq\nu
which cannot be written as
g+h
where
0\leq g\leq 2
and

\sup_{F\in\mathcal{F}}\big|\mathbb{E}_{\underline{x}\in X}h(\underline{x})F(\underline{x})\big|\leq\varepsilon.

By the Hahn-Banach theorem (specifically
[
8
, Corollary 3.2]
), there exists
\phi:X\to\mathbb{R}
such that
\langle f,\phi\rangle>1
,
\langle g,\phi\rangle<1/\penalty 2

for every
0\leq g\leq 1
, and
\langle h,\phi\rangle\leq\varepsilon^{-1}
for every
h:X\to\mathbb{R}
such that

\sup_{F\in\mathcal{F}}\big|\mathbb{E}_{\underline{x}\in X}h(\underline{x})F(\underline{x})\big|\leq 1.

The above condition forces
\phi
to be in the subspace spanned by the functions
F\in\mathcal{F}
, and then by

[
8
, Corollary 3.5]
, we may write
\phi=\sum_{i=1}^{s}\lambda_{i}F_{i}

where
F_{1},\ldots,F_{s}\in\mathcal{F}
and

(4.14)

\sum_{i=1}^{s}|\lambda_{i}|<2\varepsilon^{-1}.

By (
4.11
) and some manipulation, we have

(4.15)

\displaystyle\big|\langle\nu-1,P\phi\rangle\big|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=0}^{j}|a_{i}|\big|\big\langle\nu-1,\bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{s}\lambda_{k}F_{k}\bigg)^{i}\big\rangle\big|

\displaystyle\leq\eta\sum_{i=0}^{j}|a_{i}|\left(\sum_{k=1}^{s}|\lambda_{k}|\right)^{i}

\displaystyle\leq\eta\sum_{i=0}^{j}|a_{i}|\left(2\varepsilon^{-1}\right)^{i}

\displaystyle=1/4.

Since
\langle g,\phi\rangle\leq 1/\penalty 2
for every
0\leq g\leq 1
, we have
\langle 1,J\phi\rangle\leq 1/\penalty 2
. By (
4.14
), it is clear that
|\phi|<2\varepsilon^{-1}
pointwise, and so by (
4.13
),

||P\phi-J\phi||_{\infty}\leq 1/\penalty 8
. Combining these facts, we have
\langle 1,P\phi\rangle\leq 5/\penalty 8
. Hence, by (
4.15
),
\langle\nu,P\phi\rangle\leq 7/\penalty 8
. Finally, once again using the fact that

||P\phi-J\phi||_{\infty}\leq 1/\penalty 8
, we have
\langle\nu,J\phi\rangle\leq 1
. But then

\langle f,\phi\rangle\leq\langle f,J\phi\rangle\leq\langle\nu,J\phi\rangle\leq 1,

contradicting the fact that
\langle f,\phi\rangle>1
.

∎

Applying Proposition 4.1 and Theorem D to the family
\mathcal{F}:=\{D_{\underline{\mathcal{Q}}}(f_{\underline{\omega}})_{\underline{\omega}\in\{0,1\}^{s}\backslash\{\underline{0}\}},\,f_{\underline{\omega}}:X\to[-1,1]\}
where
\underline{\mathcal{Q}}=(\underline{Q}_{0},\underline{Q}_{1},\ldots,\underline{Q}_{s})
is a family of integral polynomials, we have

Corollary 4.1
.

Let
s,t\geq 1
and let
\underline{\mathcal{Q}}=(\underline{Q}_{0},\underline{Q}_{1},\ldots,\underline{Q}_{s})
,
\underline{Q}_{0}=\underline{0}
,
\underline{Q}_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}[h_{1},\ldots,h_{t}]
be a family of polynomials in general position. Given
\varepsilon>0
, if
\,N\geq N(\underline{\mathcal{Q}},t,s,\varepsilon)\,
are sufficiently large then to every function
0\leq f\leq\nu^{(d)}
there exists a function
0\leq g\leq 2
s.t.

(4.16)

\big|\mathbb{E}_{\underline{x}\in X}\big(f(x)-g(\underline{x})\big)D_{\underline{\mathcal{Q}}}(f_{\underline{\omega}})(\underline{x})\big|\leq\varepsilon,

for all families of functions

\big(f_{\underline{\omega}}\big)_{\underline{\omega}\in\{0,1\}^{s}\backslash\{\underline{0}\}}
,
f_{\underline{\omega}}:X\to[-1,1]
.

At this point, for the function
0\leq f_{A}\leq\nu^{(d)}
we may obtain a decomposition
f_{A}=g+\mathfrak{h}
with
0\leq g\leq 2
and the function
\mathfrak{h}
satisfying

(4.17)

\big|\mathbb{E}_{\underline{x}\in X}\mathfrak{h}(\underline{x})D_{\underline{\mathcal{Q}}}(f_{\underline{\omega}})(\underline{x})\big|\leq\varepsilon,

for all dual functions
D_{\underline{\mathcal{Q}}}(f_{\underline{\omega}})
corresponding to bounded functions
f_{\underline{\omega}}:X\to[-1,1]
. Note that
\mathfrak{h}
may not be bounded independently of
N
but
|\mathfrak{h}|\leq\nu^{(d)}+2
. If (
4.12
) would hold for
D_{\underline{\mathcal{Q}}}(\mathfrak{h})
i.e. when
f_{\underline{\omega}}=\mathfrak{h}
for all
\underline{\omega}\in\{0,1\}\backslash\{\underline{0}\}
, then we would have

(4.18)

\|\mathfrak{h}\|_{\Box_{H,M}(\underline{Q}_{1},\ldots,\underline{Q}_{s})}\leq\varepsilon.

Hence by (
1.5
) and multi-linearity

(4.19)

\Lambda_{\mathcal{P},V}(f_{A},\ldots,f_{A})=\Lambda_{\mathcal{P},V}(g,\ldots,g)+O(\varepsilon^{c})+o(1),

where
c>0
is a constant depending on the initial data
\mathcal{P}
and
V
. The Bergelson-Liebman theorem
[
2
]
implies (see next section),

(4.20)

\Lambda_{\mathcal{P},V}(g,\ldots,g)\geq c(\delta)+o(1),

where
c(\delta)>0
is a constant depending on the initial data but is independent of
N
. Thus choosing
\varepsilon>0
sufficiently small we have that

\Lambda_{\mathcal{P},V}(f_{A},\ldots,f_{A})>0
which implies our main result.

The idea that the bounded functions
f_{\underline{\omega}}
can be replaced by the unbounded function
\mathfrak{h}
appeared first in
[
3
]
. The proof is essentially the same as that of
[
20
, Theorem 11]
and include it here only for the sake of completeness. Under the same conditions as in Proposition
4.1
, we have

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
\varepsilon>0
and let
\mathfrak{h}:X\to\mathbb{R}
s.t.
|\mathfrak{h}|\leq\nu^{(d)}+2
. If

(4.21)

\big|\mathbb{E}_{\underline{x}\in X}\ \mathfrak{h}(\underline{x})\,D_{\underline{\mathcal{Q}}}(f_{\underline{\omega}})(\underline{x})\big|\leq\varepsilon+o(1),

for all bounded families of functions
(f_{\underline{\omega}})_{\underline{\omega}\in\{0,1\}^{s}\backslash{\{\underline{0}\}}}
,
f_{\underline{\omega}}:X\to[-1,1]
, then

(4.22)

\|\mathfrak{h}\|_{\Box_{H,M}(\underline{Q}_{1},\ldots,\underline{Q}_{s})}\leq\varepsilon^{C}+o(1)

for some constant
C>0
that is independent of
\varepsilon
.

Proof.

By multi-linearity it is enough to show that

(4.23)

\langle f_{\underline{\omega}}\rangle_{\Box_{H,M}(\underline{Q}_{1},\ldots,\underline{Q}_{s})}\ll\varepsilon^{C}+o(1)

whenever
|f_{\underline{\omega}}|\leq 1
or
|f_{\underline{\omega}}|\leq\nu^{(d)}
, assuming that at least one of the functions
f_{\underline{\omega}}=\mathfrak{h}
. We prove (
4.23
) on the number
n
unbounded functions
f_{\underline{\omega}}
.

If
n=1
we may assume
f_{\underline{0}}=\mathfrak{h}
and (
4.23
) follows from (
4.20
). If
n\geq 2
we may assume again that
|f_{\underline{0}}|\leq\nu^{(d)}
is unbounded and write

\ \big|\langle f_{\underline{\omega}}\rangle_{\Box(\underline{\mathcal{Q}})}\big|\leq\mathbb{E}_{\underline{x}}\nu^{(d)}(x)|D_{\underline{\mathcal{Q}}}(f_{\underline{\omega}})\big|
. By the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we have

\big|\langle f_{\underline{\omega}}\rangle_{\Box_{H,M}(\underline{Q}_{1},\ldots,\underline{Q}_{s})}\big|^{2}\leq\mathbb{E}_{\underline{x}}\,D_{\underline{\mathcal{Q}}}^{2}(f_{\underline{\omega}})(x)+\mathbb{E}_{\underline{x}}\,\big(\nu^{(d)}(\underline{x})-1\big)\,D_{\underline{\mathcal{Q}}}^{2}(f_{\underline{\omega}})(x):=I+II.

To estimate the first term note that

\mathbb{E}_{\underline{x}}\,D_{\underline{\mathcal{Q}}}^{4}(f_{\underline{\omega}})(x)\leq\mathbb{E}_{\underline{x}}\,D_{\underline{\mathcal{Q}}}^{4}(\nu^{(d)})(x)\ll 1,

by the polynomial forms condition and the assumption that the family
\underline{\mathcal{Q}}=(\underline{Q}_{1},\ldots,\underline{Q}_{s})
is in general position. On the other hand

\mathbb{E}_{\underline{x}}\,\big|D_{\underline{\mathcal{Q}}}(f_{\underline{\omega}})(x)\big|\leq\varepsilon^{C}+o(1),

as it is equivalent to the fact that

\mathbb{E}_{\underline{x}}\,D_{\underline{\mathcal{Q}}}(f_{\underline{\omega}})(x)\,g(x0\leq\varepsilon^{C}+o(1),

uniformly for all functions
|g|\leq 1
which follows from the inductive hypothesis on the number of unbounded factors. Then
I\leq\varepsilon^{C}+o(1)
by interpolation.

For the second term we may write again,

\displaystyle D_{\underline{\mathcal{Q}}}^{2}(f_{\underline{\omega}})(x)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\underline{h},\underline{h}^{\prime}}\,\mathbb{E}_{\begin{subarray}{c}\underline{y}_{1}^{(0)},\underline{y}_{1}^{(1)}\\
\underline{y}_{2}^{(0)}\underline{y}_{2}^{(1)}\end{subarray}}\prod^{\ast}_{(\underline{\omega},\underline{\omega}^{\prime})\in\{0,1\}^{2s}}f_{\underline{\omega}}\big(\underline{x}+\sum_{j=1}^{s}y_{1j}^{(\omega_{j})}\underline{Q}_{j}(\underline{h})big)\,f_{\underline{\omega}}\big(\underline{x}+\sum_{j=1}^{s}y_{2j}^{(\omega_{j})}\underline{Q}_{j}(\underline{h}^{\prime})\big)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\underline{h},\underline{h}^{\prime}}\,\mathbb{E}_{\begin{subarray}{c}\underline{y}_{1}^{(0)},\underline{y}_{1}^{(1)}\\
\underline{y}_{2}^{(0)}\underline{y}_{2}^{(1)}\end{subarray}}\prod^{\ast}_{(\underline{\omega},\underline{\omega}^{\prime})\in\{0,1\}^{2s}}f_{(\underline{\omega},\underline{\omega}^{\prime})}\big(\underline{x}+\sum_{j=1}^{s}y_{1j}^{(\omega_{j})}\underline{Q}_{j}(\underline{h})+\sum_{j=1}^{s}y_{1j}^{(\omega_{j}^{\prime})}\underline{Q}_{j}(\underline{h}^{\prime})\big),

where
f_{(\underline{\omega},\underline{\omega}^{\prime})}=1
, unless
(\underline{\omega},\underline{\omega}^{\prime})=(\underline{\omega},\underline{0})
or
(\underline{\omega},\underline{\omega}^{\prime})=(\underline{0},\underline{\omega})
(with
\underline{\omega}\neq\underline{0}
), in which case
f_{(\underline{\omega},\underline{\omega}^{\prime})}=f_{\underline{\omega}}
. Writing
\underline{\mathcal{Q}}^{\prime}(\underline{h},\underline{h}^{\prime})=(\underline{Q}_{1}(\underline{h}),\ldots,\underline{Q}_{s}(\underline{h}),\underline{Q}_{1}(\underline{h}^{\prime}),\ldots,\underline{Q}_{s}(\underline{h}^{\prime}))
for the extended polynomial system, we have that by box-norm Cauchy-Schwarz inequality

\displaystyle\big|\mathbb{E}_{\underline{x}}\,\big(\nu^{(d)}(\underline{x})-1\big)\,D_{\underline{\mathcal{Q}}}^{2}(f_{\underline{\omega}})(x)\big|

\displaystyle=\big|\langle\nu^{(d)}-1,\,D_{\underline{\mathcal{Q}}^{\prime}}(f_{\underline{\omega},\underline{\omega}^{\prime}})\rangle\big|

\displaystyle\leq\|\nu^{(d)}-1\|_{\Box_{H,M}(\underline{\mathcal{Q}}^{\prime})}\,\prod_{(\underline{\omega},{\underline{\omega}}^{\prime})\neq(\underline{0},\underline{0})}\|f_{\underline{\omega},{\underline{\omega}}^{\prime}}\|_{\Box_{H,M}(\underline{\mathcal{Q}}^{\prime})}=o(1).

Indeed the extended system
\underline{\mathcal{Q}}^{\prime}
is also in general position (as
\pi_{i}(\underline{Q}_{j}(\underline{h})\neq 0
for
i\in[d],\ j\in[s]
), hence

\ \|\nu^{(d)}-1\|_{\Box_{H,M}(\underline{\mathcal{Q}}^{\prime})}=o(1)
, while

\|f_{\underline{\omega},{\underline{\omega}}^{\prime}}\|_{\Box_{H,M}(\underline{\mathcal{Q}}^{\prime})}\leq\|\nu^{(d)}\|_{\Box_{H,M}(\underline{\mathcal{Q}}^{\prime})}+1\ll 1,

by the polynomial forms condition. This proves the Proposition.
∎

Corollary 4.2
.

Let
f:X\to\mathbb{R}
satisfying
0\leq f\leq\nu^{(d)}
and let
\varepsilon>0
. Then there exists functions
0\leq g\leq 2
and
\mathfrak{h}
, such that

f=g+\mathfrak{h},\quad\textit{and}\quad\|\mathfrak{h}\|_{\Box_{H,M}(\underline{\mathcal{Q}})}\leq\varepsilon.

Proof.

Let
0\leq g\leq 2
be given as Corollary
with
\varepsilon^{\prime}=\varepsilon^{1/C}
. Then by (
4.17
) and Proposition
4.2
we have that

\|(f-g)\|_{\Box_{H,M}(\underline{\mathcal{Q}})}\leq\varepsilon+o(1).

The Corollary follows by writing
\mathfrak{h}=f-g
.
∎

5
Proof of Theorem 1.

The other main ingredient of obtaining polynomial patterns in relative dense subsets of the primes is using an appropriate version of the polynomial extension of Szemerédi’s theorem due to Bergelson-Leibman
[
2
]
. The version we use, given
[
19
,
20
]
, is as follows.

Theorem E
.

Let
\delta>0
,
\mathcal{P}=(P_{1},\ldots,P_{l})
,
P_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}[y],\,P_{j}(0)=0
be a polynomial map, and let

V=\{\underline{v}_{1},\ldots,\underline{v}_{l}\}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{d}
.

If
g:X\to[0,1]
is a function satisfying
\mathbb{E}_{x\in X}g(x)\geq\delta
, then one has

(5.1)

\Lambda_{\mathcal{P},V}(g,\ldots,g)\geq c(\delta)-o(1),

where
c(\delta)>0
is a constant, that depends only on
\delta
,
V
and polynomial map
\mathcal{P}
.

Now it is easy to prove our main result.

Proof of Theorem 1.

Given
A\subseteq\mathbb{P}_{N^{\prime}}^{d}
with
|A|\geq\delta\,|\mathbb{P}_{N^{\prime}}|^{d}
we choose
W
,
N=[N/W]
,
\underline{b}
and the function
f_{A}
as in (
2.1
)-2.2, then by (
2.3
)-(
2.5
) we have

\mathbb{E}_{\underline{x}\in X}\,f_{A}(\underline{x})\geq\delta^{\prime},\quad 0\leq f_{A}(\underline{x})\leq\nu^{(d)}(\underline{x}),

with
\delta^{\prime}=c_{0}\delta/4
. Then by Corollary
4.2
with
\varepsilon>0
sufficiently small with respect to
c(\delta^{\prime})
, we have

f_{A}=g+\mathfrak{h}
with
\|\mathfrak{h}\|_{\Box_{H,M}(\underline{\mathcal{Q}})}\leq\varepsilon
, thus Corollary 3.1:

\Lambda_{\mathcal{P},V}(f_{A},\ldots,f_{A})\geq\Lambda_{\mathcal{P},V}(g,\ldots,g)-\varepsilon^{c}-o(1)\geq c(\delta^{\prime})-\varepsilon^{c}-o(1)\geq c(\delta^{\prime})/2-o(1)>0,

as long as
L\geq L(\mathcal{P},V)
and
N\geq N(\mathcal{P},V,\delta)
is sufficiently large.
∎

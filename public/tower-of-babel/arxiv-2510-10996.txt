Reduced Effective Reorganization Energy for Adiabatic Electron Transfer Preprint: AIP/123-QED
Ethan Abraham, Junghyun Yoon, Troy Van Voorhis, Martin Z. Bazant

Abstract
We predict that in the adiabatic limit of the Marcus normal regime, Marcus kinetics will be observed with a reduced effective reorganization energy that is a function of the standard reorganization energy and the coupling strength. This result enables the derivation of a closed-form Marcus-Hush-Chidsey type rate expression for heterogeneous electron transfer in the adiabatic limit, which also involves a different prefactor than in the non-adiabatic case.



In the study of electron transfer (ET) processes, the non-adiabatic limit is often treated elegantly due to simple analytical forms that arise for the activation barrier, such as in Marcus theory of ET
Marcus 1956
;
Marcus and Sutin 1985
;
Barbara et al. 1996
;
Bernardi et al. 1996
, Holstein-type models for polaron hopping
Fishchuk et al. 2013
;
Falletta and Pasquarello 2023
;
Shuai et al. 2020
, proton-coupled electron transfer (PCET)
Weinberg et al. 2012
;
Cukier and Nocera 1998
;
Hammes-Schiffer and Stuchebrukhov 2010
;
Lam et al. 2019
;
Hammes-Schiffer 2023
, or more recently coupled-ion electron transfer (CIET)
Bazant 2023
;
Sood et al. 2021
;
Fraggedakis et al. 2021
.

Yet adiabatic electron transfer between strongly-coupled electronic states is thought to occur in many systems of chemical interest
Koper 2024
;
Wilson et al. 2023
;
Lam et al. 2019
;
Schmickler and Mohr 2002
;
Hush 1961
;
Schmickler 1986
. Intriguingly, electrochemists have recently found success fitting kinetic data from such strongly adiabatic reactions to non-adiabatic rate expressions, including the application of Marcus-Hush-Chidsey (MHC) theory—which is derived from Fermi’s golden rule for the non-adiabatic limit—to adiabatic ET at a liquid-metal interfaces
Lees et al. 2024
;
Zhang et al. 2020
;
Huang et al. 2021
;
Mayer 2011
;
Brown et al. 2020
;
Chidsey 1991
. We note that the standard theory of adiabatic ET treats the metal as a mean-field electron reservoir
Schmickler and Mohr 2002
;
Santos and Schmickler 2022
;
Nazmutdinov et al. 2015
;
Gadzuk et al. 1971
and thus would not predict MHC kinetics, since the latter views the rate as a sum of transitions involving a continuum of electronic states which have unique energies and activation barriers. This discrepancy between theory and experiment in electrochemical ET merits theoretical investigation.

In this Letter we show that the fit of MHC kinetics to strongly adiabatic reactions can be justified on physical grounds, but both the reorganization energy and prefactor now have a different meaning than in the MHC kinetics formulated for the non-adiabatic limit
Chidsey 1991
. We find that the diagnalization of the Hamiltonian of the two-level Marcus system reveals that the curvature of the adiabatic surface is systematically reduced due to the electronic coupling. Specifically, under the Condon approximation
Nitzan 2006
;
Toutounji and Ratner 2000
;
Blumberger 2015
;
Mavros et al. 2016a
, the effective reorganization energy that governs experimentally observed activation barriers and Tafel plots is given by

\lambda_{\text{eff}}=\lambda\left(1-\frac{2V}{\lambda}\right)^{2},

(1)

where
\lambda
is the diabatic (Marcus) curvature and
V
is the electronic coupling. In addition to enabling a physically consistent reformulation of MHC kinetics
Chidsey 1991
for the adiabatic limit, our result implies that the reorganization energy fitted to Tafel curves for electron transfer reactions that are adiabatic can be significantly less than that corresponding to the curvature of the strict diabats. In particular, we are able to reconcile the large reorganization energies recently computed ab initio (4.0–7.0 eV)
Qin et al. 2023
for CO
2
electroreduction with much lower reorganization energies experimentally fitted to MHC kinetics (0.6–1.4 eV)
Zhang et al. 2020
;
Lees et al. 2024
for the same reaction: The former measures
\lambda
while the latter measures
\lambda_{\text{eff}}.
We thus expect that this Letter will provide a critical bridge between ET theory and experiment.

Reduced effective reorganization energy.
We begin with a two-level Marcus system described by two diabatic electronic quantum states
\ket{\phi_{\text{a}}}
and
\ket{\phi_{\text{b}}}
whose energies are given by

E_{\text{a}}(q)=\lambda q^{2},

(2a)

E_{\text{b}}(q)=\lambda(1-q)^{2}+\Delta G^{\circ}.

(2b)

Here
\lambda
is the reorganization energy (assumed to be symmetric so that
\lambda_{\text{a}}=\lambda_{\text{b}})
,
q
is an electron transfer coordinate defined to satisfy
\langle q\rangle_{\text{a}}=0,\langle q\rangle_{\text{b}}=1
, and
\Delta G^{\circ}
is the thermodynamic driving force. Setting the two energies equal, we obtain the coordinate and energy of the TS,

q^{*}=\frac{1}{2}\left(1+\frac{\Delta G^{\circ}}{\lambda}\right)

(3a)

E^{*}=\frac{(\lambda+\Delta G^{\circ})^{2}}{4\lambda},

(3b)

a standard result of Marcus theory.

In general, the states will mix via an electronic coupling
V(q)
. Eq. (1) can then be cast as the Hamiltonian of the two-level system in the diabatic basis
\{\ket{\phi_{\text{a}}},\ket{\phi_{\text{b}}}\}

H(q)=\begin{pmatrix}E_{\text{a}}(q)&V(q)\\
V(q)&E_{\text{b}}(q)\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\lambda q^{2}&V(q)\\
V(q)&\lambda(1-q)^{2}+\Delta G^{\circ}\end{pmatrix},

(4)

which, upon solving the secular equation, gives the energy of the corresponding states in the adiabatic basis
\{\ket{\psi_{-}},\ket{\psi_{+}}\}

\begin{split}E_{\pm}(q)&=\frac{E_{\text{a}}(q)+E_{\text{b}}(q)}{2}\pm\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{(E_{\text{a}}(q)-E_{\text{b}}(q))^{2}+4V(q)^{2}}\\
&=\frac{\lambda(2q^{2}-2q+1)+\Delta G^{\circ}}{2}\pm\\
&~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{(\lambda(2q-1)-\Delta G^{\circ})^{2}+4V(q)^{2}}.\end{split}

(5)

The exact activation barrier is then given by

E^{*}=E_{-}(q^{*})-E_{-}(q_{\text{r}}),

(6)

where
q^{*}
is the coordinate of the TS and
q_{\text{r}}\approx 0
is coordinate of the reactant minimum.

We are interested in the approximation of Eq. (
6
) for the case
k_{B}T<V(q)<\lambda
, in the Marcus normal regime
\lambda>\Delta G^{\circ}
. Note that in the strongly adiabatic case, the barrier lowering does not appear as simply a constant
-V(q^{*})
shift from the avoided crossing
Nitzan 2006
;
Zener 1932
;
von Neumann and Wigner 1929
—as is commonly assumed for an adiabatic reaction—because the reactant minimum is also stabilized by second-order mixing. Expanding Eq. (
6
) about the diabatic crossing and the reactant well gives (see Supplementary Material for the derivation)

\begin{split}E^{*}=\frac{\lambda}{4}+\frac{\Delta G^{\circ}}{2}&+\frac{(\Delta G^{\circ})^{2}}{4\lambda}-V(q^{*})+\frac{V(q_{\text{r}})^{2}}{\lambda}+\\
&O\left(\frac{V_{\text{max}}^{3}}{\lambda^{2}},\frac{V_{\text{max}}^{2}\Delta G^{\circ}}{\lambda^{2}},\frac{V_{\text{max}}(\Delta G^{\circ})^{2}}{\lambda^{2}},\frac{(\Delta G^{\circ})^{3}}{\lambda^{2}}\right),\end{split}

(7)

where the
V(q_{\text{r}})^{2}/\lambda
term comes from a perturbative lowering of the reactant basin, and
V_{\text{max}}=\text{max}_{q\in[0,1]}V(q)
. Writing the barrier in the form

E^{*}=\frac{(\lambda_{\rm eff}+\Delta G^{\circ})^{2}}{4\lambda_{\rm eff}},

(8)

one finds that the barrier obtained using

\lambda_{\rm eff}\approx\lambda-4V(q^{*})+\frac{4V(q_{\text{r}})^{2}}{\lambda}

(9)

agrees with Eq. (
7
) to the same order of truncation. We thus expect the experimentally measured activation barrier and reorganization energy for the Marcus normal regime to be given by Eqs. (
8
) and (
9
) respectively, where
\lambda
is the reorganization energy obtained from the diabatic curves without adjusting for the coupling. If one assumes this coupling takes the
q
-independent form
V(q)=V
, as commonly referred to as the Condon approximation
Nitzan 2006
;
Toutounji and Ratner 2000
;
Blumberger 2015
;
Mavros et al. 2016a
, Eq. (
9
) simplifies to Eq. (
1
), which reveals how strong electronic coupling effectively renormalizes the curvature of the free energy surface.

MHC-like kinetics in the adiabatic limit.
Standard MHC kinetics treats ET from a continuum of states varying in energy
\epsilon
(defined relative to the Fermi level) to a discrete state, such as ET from a metal electrode to an orbital of a molecule in the electrolyte
Chidsey 1991
. The rate of the reduction reaction is obtained from integrating over the continuum of contributing rates

\displaystyle k=A\int d\epsilon\rho(\epsilon)n(\epsilon)\exp\left[-\beta E^{*}(\lambda,\Delta G^{\circ}(\epsilon))\right],

(12)

where
A
is a prefactor,
\rho
is the density of states in the continuum,
n
is the Fermi-Dirac occupancy,
\beta=1/k_{B}T
is the inverse thermal energy, and
E^{*}(\lambda,\Delta G^{\circ})
is the activation energy of Eq. (
3b
), which depends on the integration variable
\epsilon
through

\displaystyle\Delta G^{\circ}(\epsilon)=e\eta_{\text{f}}-\epsilon.

(13)

Here
e
is the elementary charge, and
\eta_{\text{f}}
is the formal overpotential (defined so a negative value favors reduction of the atom/molecule by the electrode)
Bazant 2023
.

The necessity of integrating over the continuum of possible transitions, though first applied by Chidsey for non-adiabatic ET
Chidsey 1991
, should in principle also apply for the adiabatic case (see Fig. 1(b)). The difference is whereas in the non-adiabatic limit the prefactor
A
is given by
A^{\text{non-adiabatic}}=(|V|^{2}/\hbar)\sqrt{\pi\beta/\lambda}
derived from the Fermi-Golden rule
Nitzan 2006
;
Marcus and Sutin 1985
, in the adiabatic limit it will be replaced by the classical attempt frequency
A^{\text{adiabatic}}=\nu\approx 1/\beta h.
A closed-form expression of Eq. (
12
) for non-adiabatic Marcus theory has been derived in Ref.
MHC 2014
, using the wide-band approximation (
\rho(\epsilon)\approx\rho
), and evaluating the energy integral that combines the Gaussian activation factor with the Fermi–Dirac occupancy. The resulting expression has successfully fit experimental current-voltage curves in a variety of electrochemical contexts
Zhang et al. 2025
;
Zhao et al. 2023
;
Halldin Stenlid et al. 2024
;
Bazant 2023
. The corresponding expression can be derived for the adiabatic limit by substituting
A^{\text{non-adiabatic}}\rightarrow A^{\text{adiabatic}}
and
\lambda\rightarrow\lambda_{\text{eff}},
whereupon we obtain

k=\frac{\rho\sqrt{\pi\lambda_{\text{eff}}/\beta}}{\beta h\left(1+e^{\beta e\eta_{\text{f}}}\right)}\mathrm{erfc}\left(\frac{\beta\lambda_{\text{eff}}-\sqrt{1+\sqrt{\beta\lambda_{\text{eff}}}+(\beta e\eta_{\text{f}})^{2}}}{2\sqrt{\beta\lambda_{\text{eff}}}}\right).

(14)

Note that in contrast to non-adiabatic MHC kinetics, the coupling no longer appears in the prefactor, and
\sqrt{\lambda_{\text{eff}}}
appears in the numerator of the prefactor because there is no cancellation by the normalized Franck-Condon factor.

MHC 2014
, as well as to the traditional adiabatic correction via a constant
-V(q=1/2)
barrier lowering, and finally to the exact rate using Eq. (
5
) in Eq. (
12
) and evaluating the integral numerically. Figure 4(a) shows the computed Tafel plot, the logarithm of nondimensionalized rate against the formal overpotential

\Delta G^{\circ}

To validate the proposed theory, we return to the discrepancy between the large reorganization values predicted ab initio, and the small values predicted experimentally for the CO
2
reduction reaction. Employing the Condon approximation and solving Eq. (
9
) for
V
gives

V\approx\frac{\lambda}{2}-\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\lambda^{2}-\lambda(\lambda-\lambda_{\text{eff}})}.

(16)

For CO
2
reduction on Gold in the presence of K+ ions, Qin
et al.
have reported
\lambda=6.3
eV from ab initio simulation
Qin et al. 2023
, while Zhang
et al.
have obtained
\lambda_{\text{eff}}=0.75
eV by fitting corresponding Tafel curves to MHC kinetics
Zhang et al. 2020
. Using these values in Eq. (
16
), we estimate
V\approx 2.1
eV, consistent with a strongly adiabatic, bond-forming reaction.

In conclusion, we have predicted that electron transfer reactions with large reorganization energies and strong couplings can exhibit Marcus kinetics with an effective reorganization energy that is reduced compared to the curvature of the strict diabats. The use of MHC kinetics for adiabatic electrochemical steps has been justified on physical grounds—with a more precise physical interpretation of both the reorganization energy and the prefactor. Further, these results help justify the full CIET theoretical rate expressions
Bazant 2023
, which interpolate between the MHC and Butler-Volmer limits; the latter of which is known to describe adiabatic electron-transfer coupled with classical ion-transfer
Gurney and Rutherford 1931
;
Butler 1936
. We expect that the reduced effective reorganization energy may also be of interest to those working on a broader range of Marcus-like two-state systems such as electron-phonon coupling characterized by Holstein Hamiltonians
Fishchuk et al. 2013
;
Falletta and Pasquarello 2023
;
Shuai et al. 2020
. In particular, in such systems where the coupling can be varied experimentally, the predictions of this Letter can be tested directly. Further application to understanding the kinetics of specific electrochemical reactions will be the subject of forthcoming articles.

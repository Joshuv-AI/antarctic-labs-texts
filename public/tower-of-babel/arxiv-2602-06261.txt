Oscillation results for first order neutral delay differential equations with several positive and negative coefficients
Ábel Garab, Gergő Tóth

Abstract
We provide sufficient criteria for the oscillation of all solutions of neutral delay differential equations of the form [ x ⁡ ( t ) − ∑ i = 1 N r R i ​ ( t ) ​ x ​ ( t − r i ​ ( t ) ) ] ′ + ∑ i = 1 N p P i ​ ( t ) ​ x ​ ( t − τ i ​ ( t ) ) − ∑ i = 1 N q Q i ​ ( t ) ​ x ​ ( t − δ i ​ ( t ) ) = 0 , \left[x(t)-\sum_{i=1}^{N_{r}}R_{i}(t)x(t-r_{i}(t))\right]^{\prime}+\sum_{i=1}^{N_{p}}P_{i}(t)x(t-\tau_{i}(t))-\sum_{i=1}^{N_{q}}Q_{i}(t)x(t-\delta_{i}(t))=0, with both positive and negative terms and time-variable delays. Our results improve and generalize several existing criteria available in the literature that address restricted cases, such as constant delays or the absence of negative coefficients. Under additional assumptions on slowly varying parameters, we derive sharper oscillation conditions. We demonstrate the applicability of our findings through illustrative examples.

Introduction

In this work, we consider linear neutral delay differential equations (NDDEs) with several variable delays of the form

\left[x(t)-\sum_{i=1}^{N_{r}}R_{i}(t)x(t-r_{i}(t))\right]^{\prime}+\sum_{i=1}^{N_{p}}P_{i}(t)x(t-\tau_{i}(t))-\sum_{i=1}^{N_{q}}Q_{i}(t)x(t-\delta_{i}(t))=0,

(1.1)

for
t\geq t_{0}
, where
R_{i},\ P_{j},\ Q_{k},\ r_{i},\ \tau_{j}
, and
\delta_{k}
are continuous nonnegative functions,
R_{i},\ \tau_{j},\ \delta_{k}\in C^{1}
and
r_{i},\ \tau_{j},\ \delta_{k}\leq D\in(0,\infty)
on the interval
[t_{0},\infty)
for all
1\leq i\leq N_{r},\ 1\leq j\leq N_{p},\ 1\leq k\leq N_{q}
, and
N_{q}\leq N_{p}
. Furthermore, we assume that
\delta_{i}(t)\leq\tau_{i}(t)
and
\delta^{\prime}_{i}(t)<1
for all
1\leq i\leq N_{q}
and
t\in(t_{0},\infty)
.

Delay differential equations (DDEs) model systems where the current rate of change depends not only on the present state but also on past states. Among these, NDDEs form a subset in which the delayed term also appears in the derivative.

NDDEs arise in many applications in biology
[

[

[

,

]
, to mention only a few examples.

For a thorough introduction to the theory of DDEs, we refer to the books
[

]
and
[

[

The oscillation theory of DDEs has received considerable attention in the last sixty to seventy years. The monographs
[
16
,
9
,
1
]
yield a nice overview of the main contributions to the field from the last century. Each of these books devotes a complete chapter to oscillation and nonoscillation criteria for NDDEs.

We say that a function is a solution of (
1.1
) if it is continuous on
[t_{0}-D,\infty)
, continuously differentiable on the interval
(t_{0},\infty)
, and satisfies (
1.1
) there. A solution of a scalar differential equation oscillates if it has arbitrarily large zeros. A differential equation oscillates if every solution of the equation oscillates. A solution
x
of (
1.1
) is eventually positive if there exists a
T>t_{0}
such that
x(t)>0
for all
t>T

Several studies have addressed oscillation criteria for special cases of equation (
1.1
). For instance,
[
16
, Section 6.9]
considers time-variable delays and coefficient functions, but no negative terms are allowed (i.e.
Q_{k}\equiv 0
for all

), while
[
1
, Sections 3.3–3.5]
involves both a single positive and a negative coefficient function (more precisely,
N_{r}=N_{p}=N_{q}=1
), and constant delays.

There is still considerable progress related to the oscillation properties of equation (
1.1
). For instance,
[
2
]
studies the distance between consecutive zeros of solutions of similar equations; there are recent papers on the oscillation of dynamic equations
[
14
]
and nonlinear equations of a similar form
[
29
]
. However, very little is known about the oscillation of (
1.1
) in its full generality. The aim of this work is to fill this gap.
We mention here the results of
[
28
,
25
,
11
]
that allow both positive and negative coefficient functions, but only constant delays. Their approach and the obtained oscillation criteria are not comparable to ours.

Our first main result (
Theorem

3.1
) generalizes the results of Győri and Ladas
[
16
, Section 6.9]
. It establishes that the NDDE (
1.1
) is oscillatory, provided a corresponding first-order, non-neutral DDE is oscillatory. We combine this with classical oscillation criteria for the latter equation to obtain explicit criteria for the oscillation of (
1.1
). Our second main result (
Theorem

4.2
) is based on a comparison-type result (

4.1

[
1

4.3

2
Preliminaries

In this section, we set some notations and prove two auxiliary results. As customary, let
\mathbb{N}
and
\mathbb{R}
denote the set of nonnegative integers and reals, respectively.

The next simple proposition is needed for
Lemma

2.2
, which plays a key role in the proof of all our results.

Proposition 2.1
.

Assume that
\tau
and
\delta
are both continuously differentiable functions on
[t_{0},\infty)
with
0\leq\delta(t)\leq\tau(t)\leq D
for some positive constant
D
, such that
\delta^{\prime}(t)<1
for all
t\geq t_{0}
. Then for any
t\geq t_{0}
, there exists a unique
c(t)\in[0,D]
such that

c(t)=\tau(t)-\delta(t-c(t)).

(2.1)

Moreover, the function
t\mapsto c(t)
is differentiable and fulfills

1-c^{\prime}(t)=\frac{1-\tau^{\prime}(t)}{1-\delta^{\prime}(t-c(t))}.

(2.2)

Proof.

Define the following continuous function

F\colon\mathbb{R}\times[t_{0},\infty)\to\mathbb{R},\qquad F(c,t)\coloneqq c-\tau(t)+\delta(t-c).

(2.3)

Since
F(0,t)\leq 0
,
F(D,t)\geq 0
, and

\frac{\partial F}{\partial c}(c,t)=1-\delta^{\prime}(t)>0,

hence for any
t\geq t_{0}
, there exists a unique
c\in[0,D]
satisfying (
2.1
). Applying the implicit function theorem, we obtain that
c
is differentiable and
F(c(t),t)=0
. Differentiating this with respect to
t
yields (
2.2
).
∎

Notation

Recall that
N_{p}\geq N_{q}
. For the convenience of notation, we define
Q_{i}\equiv 0,\ \delta_{i}\equiv 0
, for all
N_{q}<i\leq N_{p}
.

In view of
Proposition

2.1
, for every pair
(\delta_{i},\tau_{i})
(
i\in\{1,2,\dots,N_{p}\}
), let
c_{i}
denote the unique differentiable function that satisfies

c_{i}(t)=\tau_{i}(t)-\delta_{i}(t-c_{i}(t)),\quad\text{and}\quad c_{i}(t)\in[0,D]\quad\text{for all }t\geq t_{0}+D.

(2.4)

Note that
c_{i}=\tau_{i}
for
N_{q}<i\leq N_{p}
. Furthermore, for each
1\leq i\leq N_{p}
, introduce the function

\bar{P}_{i}(t)\coloneqq P_{i}(t)-Q_{i}(t-c_{i}(t))(1-c_{i}^{\prime}(t)),\qquad t\geq t_{0}+D.

(2.5)

The following lemma provides a convenient substitution that will be used several times in the sequel. It generalizes both
[
16
, Lemma 6.9.1.]
and
[
1
, Lemma 3.3.1]
by allowing both multiple negative coefficient functions and several time-variable delays.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
c_{i}
and
\bar{P}_{i}
be defined by (
2.4
)–(
2.5
), and
\bar{P}_{i}(t)\geq 0
for all
t\geq t_{0}+D
. Suppose that
x
is an eventually positive solution of (
1.1
), and that the following properties hold:

\forall T_{0}\in{(t_{0},\infty)}\;\exists\xi>T_{0}:\ \sum\limits_{i=1}^{N_{p}}\bar{P}_{i}(\xi)>0,

(2.6)

\sum_{i=1}^{N_{r}}R_{i}(t)+\sum_{i=1}^{N_{q}}\int_{t-c_{i}(t)}^{t}Q_{i}(s)\,ds\leq 1.

(2.7)

Define

y(t)\coloneqq x(t)-\sum_{i=1}^{N_{r}}R_{i}(t)x(t-r_{i}(t))-\sum_{i=1}^{N_{q}}\int_{t-c_{i}(t)}^{t}Q_{i}(s)x(s-\delta_{i}(s))\,ds.

(2.8)

Then
y^{\prime}(t)\leq 0
and
x(t)\geq y(t)>0
.

The proof is similar to that of
[
1
, Lemma 3.3.1]
.

Proof.

Assume that
x(t)
is a solution of (
1.1
) such that
x(t)>0
for all
t\geq T\geq t_{0}+D
. Differentiating
y(t)
yields

\displaystyle y^{\prime}(t)={}

\displaystyle\biggl[x(t)-\sum_{i=1}^{N_{r}}R_{i}(t)x(t-r_{i}(t))\biggr]^{\prime}-\sum_{i=1}^{N_{q}}Q_{i}(t)x(t-\delta_{i}(t))

\displaystyle{}+\sum_{i=1}^{N_{q}}Q_{i}(t-c_{i}(t))x\Big(t-c_{i}(t)-\delta_{i}(t-c_{i}(t))\Big)(1-c_{i}^{\prime}(t)).

In view of equation (
1.1
) and the definition of
\bar{P}_{i}
and
c_{i}
we obtain

y^{\prime}(t)=-\sum_{i=1}^{N_{p}}\Big[P_{i}(t)-Q_{i}(t-c_{i}(t))(1-c_{i}^{\prime}(t))\Big]x(t-\tau_{i}(t)),

or equivalently

y^{\prime}(t)=-\sum_{i=1}^{N_{p}}\bar{P}_{i}(t)x(t-\tau_{i}(t)).

(2.9)

Therefore
y^{\prime}(t)\leq 0
for
t\geq T
.
It remains to prove the positivity of
y(t)
. In view of (
2.6
), (
2.9
) and the positivity of
x(t)
, it suffices to prove that
y(t)
is nonnegative.

We distinguish two cases.

Case 1.
Suppose that
x(t)
is unbounded. Then there exists a sequence
(s_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
with
s_{n}\to\infty
,
x(s_{n})\to\infty
such that

x(t)\leq x(s_{n})\quad\text{for all }t\leq s_{n}.

Hence,

\displaystyle y(s_{n})

\displaystyle=x(s_{n})-\sum_{i=1}^{N_{r}}R_{i}(s_{n})x(s_{n}-r_{i}(s_{n}))-\sum_{i=1}^{N_{q}}\int_{s_{n}-c_{i}(s_{n})}^{s_{n}}Q_{i}(s)x(s-\delta_{i}(s))\,ds

\displaystyle\geq x(s_{n})\left(1-\sum_{i=1}^{N_{r}}R_{i}(s_{n})-\sum_{i=1}^{N_{q}}\int_{s_{n}-c_{i}(s_{n})}^{s_{n}}Q_{i}(s)\,ds\right)\geq 0

for all
n
by property (
2.7
). By the monotonicity of
y
, we conclude that
y(t)\geq 0
.

Case 2.
Suppose that
x(t)
is bounded. Let

M_{x}\coloneqq\limsup_{t\to\infty}x(t)\quad\text{and}\quad M_{y}\coloneqq\lim_{t\to\infty}y(t)\in\mathbb{R}\cup\{-\infty\}.

Then there exists a sequence
(s_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
with
s_{n}\to\infty
and
x(s_{n})\to M_{x}
.
Moreover, let
(\xi_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be such that

x(\xi_{n})\coloneqq\max\{x(s):s_{n}-2D\leq s\leq s_{n}\}.

Clearly,
\xi_{n}\to\infty
, and, by passing to a convergent subsequence if necessary (without changing notation), we may assume

\lim_{n\to\infty}x(\xi_{n})\leq M_{x}.

Now, consider the difference

x(s_{n})-y(s_{n})=\sum_{i=1}^{N_{r}}R_{i}(s_{n})x(s_{n}-r_{i}(s_{n}))+\sum_{i=1}^{N_{q}}\int_{s_{n}-c_{i}(s_{n})}^{s_{n}}Q_{i}(s)x(s-\delta_{i}(s))\,ds.

By the definition of
\xi_{n}
, we have

x(s_{n})-y(s_{n})\leq x(\xi_{n})\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N_{r}}R_{i}(s_{n})+\sum_{i=1}^{N_{q}}\int_{s_{n}-c_{i}(s_{n})}^{s_{n}}Q_{i}(s)\,ds\right).

Using inequality (
2.7
) we obtain

x(s_{n})-y(s_{n})\leq x(\xi_{n}),\quad\ \text{for all }n\in\mathbb{N}.

Letting
n\to\infty
yields
M_{x}-M_{y}\leq M_{x}
, and thus
M_{y}\geq 0.

Since
y^{\prime}(t)\leq 0
, it follows that
y(t)\geq M_{y}\geq 0,\text{ for }t\geq T
.
∎

Results – variable delays

First, we consider the most general case, where all the delay functions in (
1.1
) are time-dependent and satisfy the properties listed in the introduction. Now we will connect our equation to a simple retarded delayed differential equation. This provides a generalization of
[
16
, Theorem 6.9.1]
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\bar{P}^{\ast}_{i}(t)
and
c^{*}_{i}(t)
be arbitrary such that
0\leq\bar{P}^{\ast}_{i}(t)\leq\bar{P}_{i}(t)
and
c^{*}_{i}(t)\geq c_{i}(t)
for all
t\in[t_{0}+D,\infty)
and
1\leq i\leq N_{p}
.
Furthermore, assume that they satisfy

\displaystyle\forall T_{0}\in{(t_{0},\infty)}\;\exists\xi>T_{0}:\ \sum\limits_{i=1}^{N_{p}}\bar{P}^{\ast}_{i}(\xi)

\displaystyle>0,

(3.1)

and

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N_{r}}R_{i}(t)+\sum_{i=1}^{N_{q}}\int_{t-c^{*}_{i}(t)}^{t}Q_{i}(s)\,ds

\displaystyle\leq 1.

(3.2)

If every solution of

x^{\prime}(t)+\sum_{i=1}^{N_{p}}\bar{P}^{\ast}_{i}(t)x(t-\tau_{i}(t))=0

(3.3)

oscillates, then every solution of equation (
1.1
) also oscillates.

Proof.

Assume that every solution of (
3.3
) oscillates. For the sake of contradiction, suppose that (
1.1
) has an eventually positive solution
x
. Notice that conditions (
3.1
)–(
3.2
) imply that inequalities (
2.6
) and (
2.7
) hold. Define
y
by (
2.8
). As seen in the proof of
Lemma

2.2
,

y^{\prime}(t)+\sum_{i=1}^{N_{p}}\bar{P}_{i}(t)x(t-\tau_{i}(t))=0.

Using the fact that
y(t)\leq x(t)
, and
\bar{P}^{\ast}_{i}(t)\leq\bar{P}_{i}(t)
we obtain

y^{\prime}(t)+\sum_{i=1}^{N_{p}}\bar{P}^{\ast}_{i}(t)y(t-\tau_{i}(t))\leq 0,

where
y
is eventually positive by
Lemma

2.2
. According to
[
16
, Corollary 3.2.2]
, this inequality implies that there exists an eventually positive solution
z
of

z^{\prime}(t)+\sum_{i=1}^{N_{p}}\bar{P}^{\ast}_{i}(t)z(t-\tau_{i}(t))=0.

This is a contradiction that completes our proof.
∎

Since, in general, we do not have explicit formulas for the functions
c_{i}
, a direct application of the previous theorem and corollaries might be difficult. Therefore, we provide further sufficient conditions. Let us recall that
D
is an upper bound for all delays.

Corollary 3.2
.

Assume that there exists
\Delta_{j}
such as
\delta^{\prime}_{j}(t)\leq\Delta_{j}<1
and
\tau^{\prime}_{i}(t)<1
for all
t\geq t_{0},\ j\in\{1,\dots N_{q}\},\ i\in\{1,\dots,N_{p}\}
. Let

\bar{P}^{\ast}_{i}(t)\coloneqq P_{i}(t)-\frac{1-\tau^{\prime}_{i}(t)}{1-\Delta_{i}}\sup_{s\in[t-D,t]}Q_{i}(s)>0,\qquad(1\leq i\leq N_{p})

and

\sum_{i=1}^{N_{r}}R_{i}(t)+\sum_{i=1}^{N_{q}}\int_{t-D}^{t}Q_{i}(s)\,ds\leq 1,

(3.4)

moreover, assume that every solution of

x^{\prime}(t)+\sum_{i=1}^{N_{p}}\bar{P}^{\ast}_{i}(t)x(t-\tau_{i}(t))=0

oscillates. Then every solution of (
1.1

Proof.

Inequality (
3.4
) implies (
3.2
), while condition (
3.1
) comes from the inequalities

\displaystyle 0<P_{i}(t)-\frac{1-\tau^{\prime}_{i}(t)}{1-\Delta_{i}}\sup_{s\in[t-D,t]}Q_{i}(s)

The claim follows immediately by

The next corollary uses the so-called slowly varying property. A function

is slowly varying at infinity (additively) if, for all

|f(t+h)-f(t)|
tends to zero as

[
30

t\to\infty

4
Results – constant delays

This section is devoted to the special case when all the delays in (
1.1

\bigg[x(t)-\sum_{i=1}^{N_{r}}R_{i}(t)x(t-r_{i})\bigg]^{\prime}+\sum_{i=1}^{N_{p}}P_{i}(t)x(t-\tau_{i})-\sum_{i=1}^{N_{q}}Q_{i}(t)x(t-\delta_{i})=0,

(4.1)

for
t\geq t_{0}
, where
N_{q}\leq N_{p}
, the coefficient functions
R_{i},\ P_{j},
and
Q_{k}
are nonnegative and continuous for all
1\leq i\leq N_{r}
,
1\leq j\leq N_{p}
and
1\leq k\leq N_{q}
, while
R_{i}\in C^{1}
, and the constant delays
r_{i},\ \tau_{j},\ \delta_{k}
are nonnegative, bounded above by
D
and fulfill
\delta_{k}\leq\tau_{k}
for all
1\leq k\leq N_{q}
.

In this case (
3.5
) simplifies to
\bar{P}_{i}(t)=P_{i}(t)-Q_{i}(t-\tau_{i}+\delta_{i})
. The following comparison theorem generalizes
[
1
, Theorem 3.4.1]
by allowing multiple delays. It also serves as a basis to our second main result,
Theorem

4.2
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Assume that
\tau_{i}>0
for all
1\leq i\leq N_{p}
, moreover,

\displaystyle\bar{P}_{i}(t)=P_{i}(t)-Q_{i}(t-\tau_{i}+\delta_{i})

\displaystyle\geq 0,

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N_{r}}R_{i}(t)+\sum_{i=1}^{N_{q}}\int_{t-\tau_{i}+\delta_{i}}^{t}Q_{i}(s)\,ds

\displaystyle\leq 1,

and either

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N_{r}}R_{i}(t)+\sum_{i=1}^{N_{p}}\bar{P}_{i}(t)

\displaystyle>0

(4.2)

or

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N_{p}}\int_{t-\tau_{i}}^{t}\bar{P}_{i}(s)\,ds

\displaystyle>0

(4.3)

for all
t\geq t_{0}+D
. Then every solution of equation (
4.1
) oscillates if and only if every solution of the corresponding differential inequality

\bigg[x(t)-\sum_{i=1}^{N_{r}}R_{i}(t)x(t-r_{i})\bigg]^{\prime}+\sum_{i=1}^{N_{p}}P_{i}(t)x(t-\tau_{i})-\sum_{i=1}^{N_{q}}Q_{i}(t)x(t-\delta_{i})\leq 0

(4.4)

oscillates.

Proof.

The proof follows the main ideas of
[
1
, Theorem 3.4.1]
. For the sake for completeness, we provide the details here.

The sufficiency part is trivial. We will prove the necessity. Suppose that
x
is an eventually positive solution of (
4.4
). Define
y
as in
Lemma

2.2
using constant delays. A similar argument to the one presented there shows

y^{\prime}(t)\leq-\sum_{i=1}^{N_{p}}\bar{P}_{i}(t)x(t-\tau_{i})

for sufficiently large
t
. Integrating on
[t,\infty)
yields

y(t)\geq\int_{t}^{\infty}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{p}}\bar{P}_{i}(s)x(s-\tau_{i})\,ds.

From the definition of
y
we obtain that

x(t)\geq\sum_{i=1}^{N_{r}}R_{i}(t)x(t-r_{i})+\sum_{i=1}^{N_{q}}\int_{t-\tau_{i}+\delta_{i}}^{t}\!\!Q_{i}(s)x(s-\delta_{i})\ ds+\int_{t}^{\infty}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{p}}\bar{P}_{i}(s)x(s-\tau_{i})\,ds

(4.5)

for
t\geq t_{1}
for a sufficiently large
t_{1}
. Now, let

E\coloneqq\{u\in C([t_{1}-D,\infty),\mathbb{R}^{+}):0\leq u(t)\leq 1,\text{ for all }t\geq t_{1}-D\}.

Define the operator
F
by

(Fu)(t)\!=\!\left\{\begin{aligned} &\frac{1}{x(t)}\Bigg[\sum_{i=1}^{N_{r}}R_{i}(t)u(t-r_{i})x(t-r_{i})\\
&\begin{aligned} &\hphantom{\frac{1}{x(t)}\Bigg[}+\sum_{i=1}^{N_{q}}\int_{t-\tau_{i}+\delta_{i}}^{t}Q_{i}(s)u(s-\delta_{i})x(s-\delta_{i})\,ds\\
&\hphantom{\frac{1}{x(t)}\Bigg[}+\int_{t}^{\infty}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N_{p}}\bar{P}_{i}(s)u(s-\tau_{i})x(s-\tau_{i})\,ds\Bigg],&&t_{1}\leq t,\\[10.0pt]
&\frac{t-t_{1}+D}{D}(Fu)(t_{1})+1-\frac{t-t_{1}+D}{D},&&t_{1}-D\leq t<t_{1}.\end{aligned}\end{aligned}\right.

It is easy to see that
F
maps
E
into itself. In addition, for all
u\in E
:
(Fu)(t)>0
, for all
t\in[t_{1}-D,t_{1})
.
Define the sequence
(u_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
, where
u_{0}\equiv 1
,
u_{k+1}=Fu_{k}
. By mathematical induction we infer from (
4.5
) that
0\leq u_{k+1}\leq u_{k}\leq 1
, for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Defining
u(t)\coloneqq\lim_{k\to\infty}u_{k}(t)
, and using Lebesgue’s dominated convergence theorem, we obtain that for
t\geq t_{1}

\displaystyle u(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{x(t)}\Bigg[\sum_{i=1}^{N_{r}}R_{i}(t)\,u(t-r_{i})\,x(t-r_{i})

\displaystyle\mathrel{\hphantom{=}}\hphantom{\frac{1}{x(t)}\Bigg[}+\sum_{i=1}^{N_{q}}\int_{t-\tau_{i}+\delta_{i}}^{t}Q_{i}(s)\,u(s-\delta_{i})\,x(s-\delta_{i})\,ds

\displaystyle\mathrel{\hphantom{=}}\hphantom{\frac{1}{x(t)}\Bigg[}+\int_{t}^{\infty}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{p}}\bar{P}_{i}(s)\,u(s-\tau_{i})\,x(s-\tau_{i})\,ds\Bigg],

and

u(t)=\frac{t-t_{1}+D}{D}u(t_{1})+1-\frac{t-t_{1}+D}{D}>0

for
t_{1}-D\leq t<t_{1}
. Define
w=ux
, then
w(t)>0
for
t_{1}-D\leq t<t_{1}
, and

\displaystyle w(t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{N_{r}}R_{i}(t)w(t-r_{i})+\sum_{i=1}^{N_{q}}\int_{t-\tau_{i}+\delta_{i}}^{t}Q_{i}(s)w(s-\delta_{i})\,ds

(4.6)

\displaystyle\mathrel{\hphantom{=}}+\int_{t}^{\infty}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{p}}\bar{P}_{i}(s)w(s-\tau_{i})\,ds

for
t\geq t_{1}
. By differentiating we see that
w
is a nonnegative solution of (
4.1
). It is left to show that
w(t)>0
, for all
t\in[t_{1}-D,\infty)
. Assume that there exists a
t^{*}\geq t_{1}
such that
w(t)>0
for
t_{1}-D\leq t<t^{*}
and
w(t^{*})=0
. Then by (
4.6
) we have

\displaystyle 0=w(t^{*})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{N_{r}}R_{i}(t^{*})w(t^{*}-r_{i})+\sum_{i=1}^{N_{q}}\int_{t^{*}-\tau_{i}+\delta_{i}}^{t^{*}}Q_{i}(s)w(s-\delta_{i})\,ds

\displaystyle\mathrel{\hphantom{=}}+\int_{t^{*}}^{\infty}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{p}}\bar{P}_{i}(s)w(s-\tau_{i})\,ds.

In view of the nonnegativity of
w
,
R_{i}(t^{*})=0
(
1\leq i\leq N_{r}
),
Q_{i}(t)=0
, for all
t\in[t^{*}-\tau_{i}+\delta_{i},t^{*}]
and
1\leq i\leq N_{q}
, moreover,
\bar{P}_{i}(t)w(t-\tau_{i})=0
for all
t\geq t^{*}
, and
1\leq i\leq N_{p}
, which contradicts either (
4.2
) or (
4.3
).
∎

Now that we have a connection between NDDEs and corresponding inequalities, we are in position to prove our second main result. For convenience of notation, let

\tau_{0}\coloneqq\min\limits_{1\leq i\leq N_{p}}\tau_{i},\quad\text{and}\quad r_{0}\coloneqq\min\limits_{1\leq i\leq N_{r}}r_{i},

and introduce the functions

\Omega_{i,j}(t)\coloneqq\frac{R_{j}(t-\tau_{i})\bar{P}_{i}(t)}{\bar{P}_{i}(t-r_{j})}

(4.7)

for
1\leq j\leq N_{r}
,
1\leq i\leq N_{p}
and for all
t\geq t_{0}+D
, where it makes sense.

Theorem 4.2
.

Assume that
\bar{P}_{i}>0
for all
1\leq i\leq N_{p}
, and that there exists a constant
\omega\in(0,\infty)
, such that

\omega\leq\min_{i,j}\left\{\inf_{s\geq t_{0}+D}\Omega_{i,j}(s)\right\}\quad\text{and}\quad\omega<\frac{1}{N_{r}}.

(4.8)

Furthermore, assume

\sum_{i=1}^{N_{r}}R_{i}(t)+\sum_{i=1}^{N_{q}}\int_{t-\tau_{i}+\delta_{i}}^{t}Q_{i}(s)\,ds\leq 1

(4.9)

for all
t\geq t_{0}+D
,
1\leq i\leq N_{p}
. If there exists
m\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\displaystyle\frac{(N_{r}\omega)^{m}}{1-N_{r}\omega}\liminf_{t\to\infty}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{p}}\int_{t-mr_{0}-\tau_{i}}^{t}\bar{P}_{i}(s)\,ds

\displaystyle>\frac{1}{e},

(
A_{3}
)

or

\displaystyle\frac{(N_{r}\omega)^{m}}{1-N_{r}\omega}\liminf_{t\to\infty}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{p}}(mr_{0}+\tau_{i})\bar{P}_{i}(t)

\displaystyle>\frac{1}{e},

(
B_{3}
)

or

\displaystyle\frac{(N_{r}\omega)^{m}}{1-N_{r}\omega}\limsup_{t\to\infty}\int_{t-mr_{0}-\tau_{0}}^{t}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{p}}\bar{P}_{i}(s)\,ds

\displaystyle>1,

(
D_{3}
)

then every solution of equation (
4.1
) oscillates.

This theorem generalizes and improves
[
1
, Theorem 3.4.9]
which only considered the case
N_{r}=N_{p}=N_{q}=1
and
m=1
. Observe that if all the delays are constant,
\bar{P}_{i}(t)>0
and
\omega>0
satisfies (
4.8
), then
Theorem

4.2
– already with the choice
m=0
– gives more efficient conditions than
Corollary

3.3
(cf.
Examples

5.5
and
5.4
).

Proof.

The proof follows a similar argument as that of
[
1
, Theorem 3.4.9]
.

Let
m\in\mathbb{N}
be such that one of inequalities (

A
3

), (

B
3

) and (

D
3

) holds. For the sake of contradiction, suppose that (
4.1
) has an eventually positive solution
x
.
Let
y
be defined by (
2.8
) – recall that here
c_{i}=\tau_{i}-\delta_{i}
, with
\delta_{i}=0
for
N_{q}<i\leq N_{p}
. Just as in
Lemma

2.2
, we obtain

y^{\prime}(t)+\sum_{i=1}^{N_{p}}\bar{P}_{i}(t)x(t-\tau_{i})=0.

(4.10)

Applying (
2.8
) for
x(t-\tau_{i})
in the above equality we arrive at

\displaystyle y^{\prime}(t)+\sum_{i=1}^{N_{p}}\bar{P}_{i}(t)y(t-\tau_{i})

\displaystyle+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N_{p}}\sum_{j=1}^{N_{r}}\bar{P}_{i}(t)R_{j}(t-\tau_{i})x(t-\tau_{i}-r_{j})

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=1}^{N_{p}}\sum_{j=1}^{N_{q}}\bar{P}_{i}(t)\int_{t-\tau_{j}+\delta_{j}}^{t}Q_{j}(s-\tau_{i})x(s-\tau_{i}-\delta_{j})=0.

Omitting the last term and using the definition of
\Omega_{i,j}
we obtain the inequality

y^{\prime}(t)+\sum_{i=1}^{N_{p}}\bar{P}_{i}(t)y(t-\tau_{i})+\sum_{i=1}^{N_{p}}\sum_{j=1}^{N_{r}}\Omega_{i,j}(t)\bar{P}_{i}(t-r_{j})x(t-\tau_{i}-r_{j})\leq 0.

Using the definition of
\omega
, we have

y^{\prime}(t)+\sum_{i=1}^{N_{p}}\bar{P}_{i}(t)y(t-\tau_{i})+\omega\sum_{i=1}^{N_{p}}\sum_{j=1}^{N_{r}}\bar{P}_{i}(t-r_{j})x(t-\tau_{i}-r_{j})\leq 0,

and from equation (
4.10
) it is easy to see that

\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N_{r}}y^{\prime}(t-r_{j})=-\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N_{r}}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N_{p}}\bar{P}_{i}(t-r_{j})x(t-\tau_{i}-r_{j}).

Combining these we obtain

\left[y(t)-\omega\sum_{i=1}^{N_{r}}y(t-r_{i})\right]^{\prime}+\sum_{i=1}^{N_{p}}\bar{P}_{i}(t)y(t-\tau_{i})\leq 0.

Now,
Theorem

4.1
implies the existence of an eventually positive function
\tilde{y}
that satisfies

\left[\tilde{y}(t)-\omega\sum_{i=1}^{N_{r}}\tilde{y}(t-r_{i})\right]^{\prime}+\sum_{i=1}^{N_{p}}\bar{P}_{i}(t)\tilde{y}(t-\tau_{i})=0.

(4.11)

Let us introduce

z(t)\coloneqq\tilde{y}(t)-\omega\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N_{r}}\tilde{y}(t-r_{i}),

from which we have

\tilde{y}(t)=z(t)+\omega\sum_{i=1}^{N_{r}}\tilde{y}(t-r_{i}).

By iteration, we obtain

\displaystyle\tilde{y}(t)=

\displaystyle z(t)+\omega\sum_{i_{1}=1}^{N_{r}}z(t-r_{i_{1}})+\omega^{2}\sum_{i_{1}=1,i_{2}=1}^{N_{r}}z(t-r_{i_{1}}-r_{i_{2}})+\dots

\displaystyle+\omega^{K}\sum_{i_{1}=1,\dots,i_{K}=1}^{N_{r}}z\left(t-\sum_{l=1}^{K}r_{i_{l}}\right)+\omega^{K+1}\sum_{i_{1}=1,\dots,i_{K+1}=1}^{N_{r}}\tilde{y}\left(t-\sum_{l=1}^{K+1}r_{i_{l}}\right),

where
K>m
and
t
is sufficiently large. By virtue of
Lemma

2.2
we have that
z^{\prime}(t)\leq 0
and
z(t)>0
. Using the monotonicity of
z
and the positivity of
\tilde{y}
, we obtain the inequality

\tilde{y}(t)\geq\sum_{i=0}^{K}(N_{r}\omega)^{i}z(t-ir_{0}).

Now, omitting the first
m
terms, and using the monotonicity again,
and
N_{r}\omega\in(0,1)
, we infer

\tilde{y}(t)\geq(N_{r}\omega)^{m}\frac{1-(N_{r}\omega)^{K-m+1}}{1-N_{r}\omega}z(t-mr_{0}).

Combining this with equation (
4.11
) we conclude that
z(t)
is an eventually positive solution of the differential inequality

z^{\prime}(t)+\sum_{i=1}^{N_{p}}\tilde{p}_{i}(t)z(t-mr_{0}-\tau_{i})\leq 0,

where

\tilde{p}_{i}(t)\coloneqq(N_{r}\omega)^{m}\frac{1-(N_{r}\omega)^{K-m+1}}{1-N_{r}\omega}\bar{P}_{i}(t).

Applying
[
16
, Corollary 3.2.2]
we obtain that the differential equation

v^{\prime}(t)+\sum_{i=1}^{N_{p}}\tilde{p}_{i}(t)v(t-mr_{0}-\tau_{i})=0

(4.12)

also has an eventually positive solution.
On the one hand, for large enough
K
, (

A
3

), (

B
3

) and (

D
3

) imply

\displaystyle\liminf_{t\to\infty}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{p}}\int_{t-mr_{0}-\tau_{i}}^{t}\tilde{p}_{i}(t)\,ds

\displaystyle>\frac{1}{e},

(4.13)

\displaystyle\liminf_{t\to\infty}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{p}}(mr_{0}+\tau_{i})\tilde{p}_{i}(t)

\displaystyle>\frac{1}{e},

(4.14)

and

\displaystyle\limsup_{t\to\infty}\int_{t-mr_{0}-\tau_{0}}^{t}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{p}}\tilde{p}_{i}(t)\,ds

\displaystyle>1,

(4.15)

respectively.
On the other hand, it is well-known, that each of the inequalities (
4.13
), (
4.14
) and (
4.15
) is sufficient for the oscillation of all solutions of equation (
4.12
) (see
[
24
, Corollary 1]
,
[
9
, Corollary 2.6.2]
and
[
23
, Remark 2.7.3]
, respectively). This is a contradiction that completes our proof.
∎

It is worth noting that the conditions in the previous theorem can be significantly sharpened by replacing the limit inferior with limit superior, provided the coefficient functions fulfill an extra condition related to the slowly varying property. We conclude our results by formulating this in the next corollary.

Corollary 4.3
.

Assume that inequalities (
4.8
)–(
4.9
) hold, the functions
\bar{P}_{i}
are bounded, uniformly continuous, and satisfy
\liminf_{t\to\infty}\int_{t-\tau_{i}}^{t}\bar{P}_{i}(s)\,ds>0
for all
1\leq i\leq N_{p}
. For
m\in\mathbb{N}
, define

\displaystyle A_{m}(t)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\frac{(N_{r}\omega)^{m}}{1-N_{r}\omega}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{p}}\int_{t-mr_{0}-\tau_{i}}^{t}\bar{P}_{i}(s)\,ds,

and

\displaystyle B_{m}(t)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\frac{(N_{r}\omega)^{m}}{1-N_{r}\omega}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{p}}\bar{P}_{i}(t)(mr_{0}+\tau_{i}).

If one of the following conditions is fulfilled, then every solution of equation (
4.1
) oscillates:

\text{$A_{m}$ is slowly varying at infinity, and}\quad\limsup_{t\to\infty}A_{m}(t)>\frac{1}{e}\quad\text{for some $m\in\mathbb{N}$};

\text{$B_{m}$ is slowly varying at infinity, and}\quad\limsup_{t\to\infty}B_{m}(t)>\frac{1}{e}\quad\text{for some $m\in\mathbb{N}$}.

Proof.

The indirect proof proceeds in a similar way as in
Theorem

4.2
. The only difference is that we apply
[
13
, Theorem 2.2 (a)–(b)]
at the end to arrive at a contradiction.
∎

5
Examples

In this section we give several examples illustrating the applicability of our results.

In our first example,
Theorem

3.1
can be applied directly.

Example 5.1
.

Consider the equation

\biggl[x(t)-\frac{1}{4}x\Bigr(t-\frac{1}{2}\Bigr)\biggr]^{\prime}+2x\biggl(t-\Bigl(\frac{1}{2}\cos t+1+e^{-t+1+\frac{1}{2}\cos t}\Bigr)\biggr)-e^{-t}x\bigl(t-e^{-t}\bigr)=0

for
t\geq 0
. Here we take
N_{r}=N_{p}=N_{q}=1
,
R_{1}(t)=\frac{1}{4}
,
P_{1}(t)=2
,
Q_{1}(t)=e^{-t}
,
r_{1}(t)=\frac{1}{2}
,
\tau_{1}(t)=\frac{1}{2}\cos t+1+e^{-t+1+\frac{1}{2}\cos t}
, and
\delta_{1}(t)=e^{-t}
. The associated implicit relation yields the explicit solution
c_{1}(t)=\tfrac{1}{2}\cos t+1
. One can show that
\bar{P}_{1}(t)=2-e^{c_{1}(t)-t}(1-c_{1}^{\prime}(t))
, which tends to
2
as
t\to\infty
. Hence, there exists
T_{0}>0
such that
\bar{P}_{1}(t)\geq 1
for all
t\geq T_{0}
. Applying Corollary
3.3
, we obtain

\liminf_{t\to\infty}\int_{t-\tau_{1}(t)}^{t}\bar{P}_{1}(s)\,ds\geq\liminf_{t\to\infty}\tau_{1}(t)\geq\liminf_{t\to\infty}c_{1}(t)=\frac{1}{2}>\frac{1}{e},

since
c_{1}(t)\leq\tau_{1}(t)
. Therefore, every solution oscillates.

The next example illustrates the applicability of
Theorem

4.2
in case of constant delays, and the importance of the appropriate choice of
m
.

Example 5.2
.

Consider the NDDE

\bigg[x(t)-\frac{1}{2}x\Bigl(t-\frac{\pi}{2}\Bigr)\bigg]^{\prime}+\bigg(\frac{3}{4}\sin(4t)+\frac{5}{4}\bigg)x(t-0.1)+\frac{1}{10}x(t-0.01)-\frac{1}{4}x(t-0.05)=0

for
t\geq 0
. This equation is of the form (
4.1
) with
N_{r}=N_{q}=1
,
N_{p}=2
,

R_{1}(t)=\frac{1}{2},\ P_{1}(t)=\frac{3}{4}\sin(4t)+\frac{5}{4}
,
P_{2}(t)=\frac{1}{10}
,
Q_{1}(t)=\frac{1}{4}
,

,
\tau_{1}=0.1
,
\tau_{2}=0.01
and

, from which we obtain

Then the left-hand side of condition (

Choosing

, we have

\frac{1}{2}\bigg(\frac{\pi+0.1}{4}+\frac{\pi+0.01}{10}\bigg)\approx 0.563>\frac{1}{e}.

Thus, condition (

) is satisfied, and hence every solution oscillates.
We note that one could also choose

or

to draw the same conclusion,

).
For comparison, condition (

) provides a sufficient condition for oscillation with

.

In the following example we take advantage of the slowly varying property of the coefficient functions.

.

Consider the equation

\displaystyle\bigg[x(t)-\frac{1}{2}x(t-0.15)\bigg]^{\prime}+1.1x(t-0.05)

\displaystyle\qquad+(2\cos(\ln t)+2.05)x(t-0.01)-x(t-0.02)=0,

Finally, one might wonder whether

Example 5.4

Consider the equation

\bigg[x(t)-e^{-t}x(t-2\pi)\bigg]^{\prime}+3x(t-1)+(\sin t+1.5)x(t-2)-x\biggl(t-\frac{1}{2}\biggr)=0,\qquad t\geq 1

4.1

N_{r}=N_{q}=1

N_{p}=2

R_{1}(t)=e^{-t},\ P_{1}(t)=3,\ P_{2}(t)=\sin(t)+1.5

Q_{1}(t)=1

r_{1}=2\pi,\ \tau_{1}=1,\ \tau_{2}=2,\ \delta_{1}=\frac{1}{2}

\bar{P}_{1}(t)=2,\ \bar{P}_{2}(t)=\sin(t)+1.5
. On the one hand,
\Omega_{1,1}(t)=\Omega_{2,1}(t)=e^{-t}\to 0
as
t

Theorem

4.2

\bar{P}^{\ast}_{i}=\bar{P}_{i}

c^{*}_{i}=\tau_{i}-\delta_{i}

Corollary

3.3

1

\liminf_{t\to\infty}\bigg(\int_{t-1}^{t}2\,ds+\int_{t-2}^{t}\sin(s)+1.5\,ds\bigg)>3,

\frac{1}{e}

Corollary

3.3
implies that every solution oscillates.

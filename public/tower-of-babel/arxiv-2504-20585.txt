Rigidity of Complete Free Boundary Minimal Hypersurfaces in Convex NNSC Manifolds
Yujie Wu

Abstract
. We prove that in the unit ball of ℝ 4 \mathbb{R}^{4} , there is no complete two-sided stable free boundary immersion. The result follows from a rigidity theorem of complete free boundary minimal hypersurfaces in complete 4-manifolds with non-negative intermediate Ricci curvature, convex boundary and weakly bounded geometry. The method uses warped θ \theta -bubble, a generalization of capillary surfaces.



1.
Introduction

Recall a complete two-sided stable minimal (free boundary) immersion
M^{n-1}\hookrightarrow X^{n}
satisfies the following stability inequality, for any compactly supported test function
\phi
,

(1.1)

\int_{M}|\nabla\phi|^{2}-(\Ric_{X}(\nu_{M},\nu_{M})+|\mathrm{I\!I}_{M}|^{2})\phi^{2}-\int_{\partial M}\mathrm{I\!I}_{\partial X}(\nu_{M},\nu_{M})\phi^{2}\geq 0,

for a choice of unit normal
\nu_{M}
of
M\hookrightarrow X
,
\Ric_{X}
the Ricci curvature of
X
and
\mathrm{I\!I}
the corresponding second fundamental forms. The positivity of the curvature tensor of the ambient manifold
X^{n}
has been exploited to obtain rigidity or non-existence results of stable minimal (free boundary) hypersurfaces.
When
X^{n}
has non-negative Ricci and M is closed, then
M
must be totally geodesic (
[
Sim68
]
); when
X^{n}
has positive scalar curvature (PSC) and M is closed, then
M
must also have PSC (
[
SY79a
]
,
[
SY79b
]
). See
[
CLS24
]
for a more complete review of the literature.

In the case that
M
or
X
is non-compact, results have been obtained for surfaces in a three manifold.
If the scalar curvature has
R_{X}\geq 0
and
\partial X=\emptyset
, then
M
with the induced metric is either conformal to a plane or a cylinder, and the later case implies that
M
is totally geodesic, intrinsically flat,
R_{X}\rvert_{M}=0
and
\Ric_{X}(\nu_{M},\nu_{M})=0
along
M
(
[
FCS80
]
). Furthermore, if
R_{X}\geq 1
, then
M
must be compact (
[
SY83
]
,
[
GL83
]
), and also admit a metric of PSC. The idea that the positivity of scalar curvature can be “inherited” has seen many fruitful applications (
[
SY79c
]
,
[
CL24a
]
).

In the case that
\partial X\neq\emptyset
, Franz (Proposition 3.2.5 in
[
Fra22
]
) obtained results for manifolds with “weakly positive geometry”. Denote
H_{\partial X}
to be the mean curvature. If we assume
R_{X}\geq R_{0}>0
,
H_{\partial X}\geq 0
and
\partial X
has no minimal components, or assume that
R_{X}\geq 0
and
H_{\partial X}\geq H_{0}>0
, then every complete two-sided stable free boundary minimal surface
M
, must be compact with intrinsic
\text{diam}(M)\leq C(H_{0},R_{0})
, and is diffeomorphic to a disc. The result allows the author to obtain compactness results for compact embedded finite index minimal surfaces in a compact 3-manifold with weakly positive geometry, leading to a uniform bound of area, total curvature, genus and boundary components (Theorem 3.2.1 in
[
Fra22
]
).

In higher dimensions, to obtain analogous rigidity or non-existence results, the assumption of
\Ric>0
or
R_{g}\geq 1
needs to be strengthened (see examples in
[
CLS24
]
). The authors in
[
CLS24
]
combined PSC and non-negative intermediate Ricci curvature to obtain rigidity results for stable minimal hypersurfaces in a 4-manifold. The free boundary case using the same technique of PSC is obtained in
[
Wu23
]
.
In this paper, our main result shows that instead of assuming positive scalar curvature of
X
, we can assume positive mean curvature of
\partial X
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Consider a 4-manifold
(X^{4},\partial X)
with weakly bounded geometry, assume
\Ric^{X}_{2}\geq 0,\mathrm{I\!I}_{\partial X}\geq 0
and
H_{\partial X}\geq H_{0}>0
.
If
(M^{3},\partial M)\hookrightarrow(X^{4},\partial X)
is a complete two-sided stable free boundary minimal immersion, then
M
is totally geodesic and
\Ric_{X}(\nu_{M},\nu_{M})=0
along
M
,
\mathrm{I\!I}_{\partial X}(\nu_{M},\nu_{M})=0
along
\partial M
.

We will elaborate the assumptions of weakly bounded geometry and the intermediate curvature condition
\Ric^{X}_{2}
in the next section. In particular, a compact 4-manifold with non-negative sectional curvature and convex boundary, for example the unit ball in
\mathbb{R}^{4}
, satisfies our assumptions.

Corollary 1.2
.

There is no complete two-sided stable free boundary minimal immersion in
\mathbb{B}^{4}
.

The method that enables the study of PSC manifolds in
[
CLS24
]
is called
\mu
-bubbles, introduced in Gromov’s 1996 paper
[
Gro96
]
.
Recently, the
\mu
-bubble method has been revisited to obtain prolific results including the Generalized Geroch Conjecture, the Stable Bernstein Theorem conjectured by Schoen and the Multiplicity One Conjecture of Marques and Neves
(
[
Gro19
]
,
[
Gro18
]
,
[
Zhu21
]
,
[
Zhu23
]
,
[
Zho20
]
,
[
CL24a
]
,
[
CL24b
]
,
[
CL23
]
).

The method of
\mu
-bubble, as a generalization of minimal hypersurfaces, has proven to be a powerful tool to analyze the geometry of the ambient manifold.

In the case of manifolds with boundary, the analogy is given by capillary surfaces, which means constant mean curvature (CMC) surfaces having constant contact angle with the ambient manifolds’ boundary.
Li (
[
Li20
]
) used the method of capillary surfaces to show Gromov’s dihedral rigidity conjecture for conical and prism type polyhedron. Chai and Wang (
[
CW24
]
) confirmed the conjecture for some cases of hyperbolic 3-manifold.

In
[
Wu24
]
, the author used capillary surfaces with prescribed (varying) contact angle, a notion called “
\theta
-bubble”, to study comparison results and geometry of manifolds with non-negative scalar curvature (NNSC) and uniformly mean convex boundary, obtaining sharp comparison results for surfaces, a 1-Urysohn width bound, a decomposition for the boundary of such manifolds, and a bandwidth estimate.
Using capillary surfaces with prescribed contact angle, Ko and Yao (
[
KY24
]
) proved a smooth comparison and rigidity result analogous to Gromov’s dihedral rigidity conjecture.

The method of
\theta
-bubble in the above work for surfaces in 3-manifolds can be used inductively. We observe that in manifolds with NNSC and uniformly mean convex boundary, we have the PSC equivalent “inheritance”phenomenon. For a precise (and more general) statement, see Lemma
4.4
.

Since we are now interested in hypersurfaces in a non-compact ambient manifold, it’s important to control the number of ends of its stable minimal hypersurfaces. Cao, Shen and Zhu (
[
CSZ97
]
) proved that a complete stable minimal hypersurfaces in
\mathbb{R}^{4}
has at most one end; a similar result on non-parabolic ends is obtained for manifolds with non-negative intermediate Ricci curvature in
[
CLS24
]
. The case with FBMH
(M^{3},\partial M)\hookrightarrow(X^{4},\partial X)
requires additional control of the boundary. We would like to use
\theta
-bubbles (near the boundary) to exhaust the non-parabolic end.
But a priori we don’t know if the boundary
\partial M
is connected or disconnected, compact or non-compact, whether it’s contained in the parabolic ends or non-parabolic end.

Using Theorem
5.8
, we can show if
(M^{3},\partial M)\rightarrow(X^{4},\partial X)
is a FBMH and
\Ric^{X}_{2}\geq 0,\mathrm{I\!I}^{\partial X}_{2}\geq 0
, assume
M
is non-parabolic and
\Vol(M)=\infty
, then each component of
\partial M
must be non-compact, and each component of the boundary
\partial M
must has an end in the only non-parabolic end of
M
.

We now provide a sketch of the proof of Theorem
1.1
. We may pass to the universal cover and assume
M
is simply connected. The idea is similar to
[
CLS24
]
. We want to control the volume growth of a stable FBMH
M^{3}
in
X^{4}
as of Theorem
1.1
. The parabolic ends behave in a good way as one can find a harmonic function that approaches
1
everywhere while the Dirichlet energy goes to
0
when exhausting the parabolic end, which serves as a good test function to plug into the stability inequality (
1.1
). On a non-parabolic end, existence of a positive barrier function prevents us from using the same idea, so we want to exhaust a non-parabolic end with chunks with bounded diameter and volume, which shows that each non-parabolic end grows linearly in volume. This is achieved using
\theta
-bubbles together with the control of the boundary
\partial M
and the non-parabolic end.

In section 2, we state some preliminaries and introduce the notations and set up of the paper. In section 3, we define a “warped
\theta
-bubble” and derive its first and second variations. In section 4, we derive the inductive process and describe the inheritance phenomenon of warped
\theta
-bubbles. In section 5, we study non-parabolic ends of FBMH in manifolds with non-negative intermediate Ricci curvature and 2-convex boundary conditions. In section 6, we prove the main results combining all the ingredients.

2.
Preliminaries

We first set up some notations and definitions in this section. Recall that an immersed submanifold
M\hookrightarrow X
is minimal if its mean curvature vanishes everywhere. Throughout the paper we use the convention
\mathrm{I\!I}_{M}(Y,Z)=-\langle\overline{\nabla}_{Z}Y,\nu_{M}\rangle
given a choice of unit normal vector
\nu_{M}
for a two-sided hypersurface
M
and
\overline{\nabla}
the Levi-Civita connection on the ambient Riemannian manifold
X
. We define mean curvature as
H_{M}=\text{tr}(\mathrm{I\!I}_{M})
. In this convention, the mean curvature of a sphere with outward pointing unit normal in the Euclidean ball is positive. We denote the induced connection on
M
as
\nabla
.

We reserve the notation
\partial M
to denote the boundary of a manifold
(M,\partial M)
, and if
\Omega
is an open subset of
M
, we denote the topological boundary as
\partial^{\prime}\Omega
and the closure of
\Omega
as
\overline{\Omega}
. Then
\partial^{\prime}\Omega\cap\partial M=\partial^{\prime}(\Omega\cap\partial M)
if
\Omega
has Lipschitz boundary and
\overline{\Omega}
intersect
\partial M
transversally.

We first define the notion of free boundary minimal hypersurface (FBMH). Consider an immersion of hypersurface
(M,\partial M)\hookrightarrow(X,\partial X)
and we always require
\partial M\subset\partial X
and the immersion is two-sided.

Definition 2.1
.

An immersion
(M,\partial M)\hookrightarrow(X,\partial X)
is a FBMH if it’s a critical point to the area functional among hypersurfaces with boundary in
\partial X
. Equivalently, a FBMH has the following characterization,

•

the mean curvature vanishes everywhere,

•

the boundaries
\partial M
and
\partial X
intersect orthogonally, that is, the outward pointing unit normal of
\partial M\subset M
agrees with the outward pointing unit normal of
\partial X\subset X
(so the second fundamental form of
\partial M\hookrightarrow M
is the restriction of the second fundamental form of
\partial X\hookrightarrow X
on the tangent bundle
T\partial M
).

The FBMH is stable if its second variation of area is non-negative, that is, for any compactly supported function
\varphi
on
M
, we have the following stability inequality,

(2.1)

0\leq\int_{M}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}-(\Ric_{X}(\nu_{M},\nu_{M})+|\mathrm{I\!I}_{M}|^{2})\varphi^{2}-\int_{\partial M}\mathrm{I\!I}_{\partial X}(\nu_{M},\nu_{M})\varphi^{2}.

We denote the Ricci curvature of a Riemannian manifold
X
as
\Ric_{X}
and scalar curvature as
R_{X}
. We denote the Riemann curvature tensor as
R_{X}(\cdot,\cdot,\cdot,\cdot)
.

We now introduce the non-negative 2-intermediate Ricci curvature condition
\Ric^{X}_{2}\geq 0
, lying between non-negative sectional curvature and non-negative Ricci curvature.

Definition 2.2
.

We say a Riemannian manifold
X
has
\Ric^{X}_{2}\geq 0
if for any
x\in X
and orthonormal vectors
u,v,w
of
T_{x}X
,

R_{X}(v,u,u,v)+R(w,u,u,w)\geq 0.

If the dimension of
X
is at least 3, then
\Ric^{X}_{2}\geq 0
implies
\Ric_{X}\geq 0
.

Combining
\Ric^{X}_{2}\geq 0
and Gauss-Codazzi equation, a FBMH in
X
has its Ricci bounded from below by its second fundamental form (see
[
CLS24
]
,
[
Wu23
]
).

Lemma 2.3

(
[
CLS24
]
, Lemma 4.2)
.

Let
(M^{n-1},\partial M)\hookrightarrow(X^{n},\partial X)
be a FMBH and
\Ric^{X}_{2}\geq 0
, then

\Ric_{M}\geq-\frac{n-2}{n-1}|\mathrm{I\!I}_{M}|^{2}.

We can also define an analogous “2-convexity” condition for
\partial X\hookrightarrow X
(see
[
Wu23
]
).

Definition 2.4
.

For a complete Riemannian manifold
(X,\partial X)
, we say
\mathrm{I\!I}^{\partial X}_{2}\geq 0
(or
\partial X
is 2-convex) if for any
x\in\partial X
and orthonormal vectors
e_{1},e_{2}
of
T_{x}\partial X
,

\mathrm{I\!I}_{\partial X}(e_{1},e_{1})+\mathrm{I\!I}_{\partial X}(e_{2},e_{2})\geq 0.

We define the following “weakly bounded geometry” condition that holds for any compact manifolds or manifolds with bounded geometry (uniformly bounded curvature and injectivity radius).

Definition 2.5
.

We say a complete Riemannian manifold
(X,\partial X)
has weakly bounded geometry (up to the boundary) at scale
Q>0
, if there is
\alpha\in(0,1)
such that for any point
x\in X
,

•

there is a pointed
C^{2,\alpha}
local diffeomorphism
\Phi:(B_{Q^{-1}}(a),a)\cap\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\rightarrow(U,x)\subset X
for some
a\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}:=\{(a_{1},...,a_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n},a_{n}\geq 0\}
;

•

if
\partial X\cap U\neq\emptyset
then
\Phi^{-1}(\partial X\cap U)\subset\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
;

furthermore, the map
\Phi
satisfies,

•

e^{-2Q}g_{0}\leq\Phi^{*}g\leq e^{2Q}g_{0}
as two forms, with
g_{0}
the standard Euclidean metric;

•

\|\partial_{k}\Phi^{*}g_{ij}\|_{C^{\alpha}}\leq Q
where
i,j,k
stand for indices in Euclidean space.

The following two Lemmas are useful consequences of the weakly bounded geometry condition. First is curvature estimates for stable FBMH following the stable Bernstein result of Chodosh and Li in dimension 4.

Lemma 2.6

(
[
Wu23
]
, Lemma 3.1)
.

Consider
(X^{4},\partial X,g)
a complete Riemannian manifold with weakly bounded geometry, and
(M^{3},\partial M)\hookrightarrow(X^{4},\partial X)
a FBMH, then there is a constant
C=C(X,g,Q)
independent of
M
such that,

\sup_{q\in M}|\mathrm{I\!I}_{M}(q)|\leq C

The second lemma is a volume control of balled of fixed radius.

Lemma 2.7

(
[
Wu23
]
, Lemma 3.3)
.

Let
(X^{n},\partial X)
be a complete Riemannian manifold with weakly bounded geometry at scale
Q>0
, and
(M^{n-1},\partial X)\hookrightarrow(X,\partial X)
a complete immersed submanifold with bounded second fundamental form, then for any
R>0
, there is a constant
C=C(R,Q)
such that the volume of balls of radius
R
around any point in
M
is bounded by
C
, i.e.
\Vol(B^{M}_{R}(q))\leq C
for any
q\in M
.

3.
Definitions of
\theta
-bubbles

In this section we introduce the notion of
\theta
-bubble, a tool that is useful to probe the geometry of manifolds with non-negative scalar curvature (NNSC) and mean convex boundary, as an analogous notion to
\mu
-bubble, first utilized by Gromov to probe the geometry of manifolds with positive scalar curvature (PSC).

We denote the reduced boundary of a Caccioppoli set
\Omega
as
\partial^{*}\Omega
. Then if
\Omega
is an open set with smooth boundary of a Riemannian manifold,
\partial^{*}\Omega=\partial^{\prime}\Omega
.

Definition 3.1
.

Consider a Caccioppoli set
\Omega
of a Riemannian manifold with boundary
(X^{n},\partial X)
, a “
\theta
-bubble” is a minimizer of the following “prescribed contact angle” problem, among variations that send
\partial X
to itself,

\mathcal{A}_{\theta}(\Omega)=\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\partial^{*}\Omega)-\int_{\partial X\cap\Omega}\cos\theta,

given a smooth function
\theta:\partial X\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
.

Capillary surfaces are surfaces of constant mean curvature and constant contact angle, and they are a critical point of
\mathcal{A}_{\theta}
when
\theta
is a constant function on
\partial X
and the volume ratio separated by the capillary surfaces is fixed. The
\theta
-bubbles can be thought as a “generalized capillary surfaces”.

A more generalized notion called “warped
\theta
-bubble” is adapted from the definition of
\theta
-bubble with a weight on the ambient manifold, as an analogy to “warped
\mu
-bubble”.

Definition 3.2
.

Consider a Caccioppoli set
\Omega
of a Riemannian manifold with boundary
(X^{n},\partial X)
, a “ warped
\theta
-bubble” is a critical point to the following functional, among variations that send
\partial X
to itself,

\mathcal{A}_{u}(\Omega)=\int_{\partial^{*}\Omega}ud\mathcal{H}^{n-1}-\int_{\partial X\cap\Omega}u\cos\theta,

given a smooth function
\theta:\partial X\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
and a positive smooth function
u:X\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+}
.

We first show that a minimizer exists under suitable assumptions and is smooth up to codimension 4.

Theorem 3.3
.

If
(N^{n},\partial N)
is a compact connected Riemannian manifold with connected mean convex boundary
H_{\partial N}>0
, let
\theta:\partial N\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a smooth function such that
S_{\pm}=\{x\in\partial N,\cos\theta(x)=\pm 1\}
and
S_{+}
and
S_{-}
are non-empty closed domains in
\partial N
with smooth boundary. Let
\mathcal{S}
be the set of all Caccioppoli sets that contain
S_{+}
and be disjoint from
S_{-}
, then

\alpha=\inf_{\Omega\in\mathcal{S}}\mathcal{A}_{\theta}(\Omega)

is obtained by some
\Omega\in\mathcal{S}
. If
n\leq 4
, then the minimizer
\Omega
is a smooth submanifold with boundary that intersect
\partial N
transversally.

Proof.

We follow the arguments in
[
Wu24
]
for surfaces and 3-manifolds. It’s enough to show that there is a fixed open neighborhood
\Omega_{+}
of
S_{+}
such that any minimizing sequence must contain
\Omega_{+}
, and be disjoint from a fixed open neighborhood
\Omega_{-}
of
S_{-}
. Then by connectedness of
\partial N
, a minimizer must exists and must intersect
\partial N
in a compact subset of
\{x\in\partial N,-1<\cos\theta(x)<1\}
and the regularity results of
[
CEL25
]
(see also
[
PM15
]
) applies, for
n\leq 4
the minimizer is smooth and intersect
\partial N
transversally.

Consider the following family of foliations
\Phi_{t}(x):=\exp(-\varphi_{t}(x)\nu_{\partial N})
where
\nu_{\partial N}
is the outward pointing unit normal. Here
\varphi_{t}(x):=\{t_{0}\phi(x)+t,0\}
for some small fixed
t_{0}>0
to be chosen, and
\phi:\partial N\rightarrow[-1,1]
is a smooth function such that
S_{+}=\{x\in\partial N,\phi(x)\geq 0\}
and
\nabla\phi(x)\neq 0
for any
x\in\partial S_{+}
. Note that
\Gamma_{t}:=\overline{\Phi_{t}(\partial N)\setminus\partial N}
is a smooth submanifold in
N
, and as
t_{0}\rightarrow 0,t\leq t_{0}
,
\Gamma_{t}
converge to
S_{+}
smoothly, so
H_{\partial N}>0
implies
H_{t}:=H_{\Gamma_{t}}>\epsilon
for some
\epsilon>0
.

Denote
\Omega_{t}:=\cup_{-t_{0}\leq s\leq t}\Gamma_{t}
with outward pointing unit normal
\nu_{\Gamma_{t}}
along the boundary
\Gamma_{t}
. Then we have that
\nu_{\partial N}(x)\cdot\nu_{\Gamma_{0}}(x)>-1=-\cos\theta(x)
for any
x\in\partial S_{+}
, which implies for small
t_{0}
and
0\leq t\leq t_{0}
,
\nu_{\partial N}(x)\cdot\nu_{\Gamma_{t}}(x)+\cos\theta(x)>0
for any
x\in\partial\Gamma_{t}
.

Then we take any candidate
\Omega
(without loss of generality assume
\partial\Omega
smooth) and compare,

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{\theta}(\Omega_{t}\cup\Omega)-\mathcal{A}_{\theta}(\Omega)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\partial^{*}(\Omega_{t}\cup\Omega))-\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\partial^{*}\Omega)-\int_{\partial N\cap(\Omega_{t}\setminus\Omega)}\cos\theta

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\partial^{*}\Omega_{t}\setminus\Omega}\nu_{\Gamma_{t}}\cdot\nu_{\Gamma_{t}}-\int_{\partial^{*}\Omega\cap\Omega_{t}}\nu_{\Gamma_{t}}\cdot\nu_{\partial\Omega}-\int_{\partial N\cap(\Omega_{t}\setminus\Omega)}\cos\theta

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega_{t}\setminus\Omega}\dive(\nu_{\Gamma_{t}})-\int_{\partial N\cap(\Omega_{t}\setminus\Omega)}\nu_{\Gamma_{t}}\cdot\nu_{\partial N}+\cos\theta

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega_{t}\setminus\Omega}-H_{t}\leq 0,

using when
t\leq t_{0}
is small then
H_{t}>\epsilon>0
;
and the last inequality is sharp is
\Omega_{t}\setminus\Omega
has nonzero measure.

A similar argument applies to show that any minimizer must be disjoint to some fixed neighborhood
\Omega_{-}
of
S_{-}
.
∎

We can show that the same proof applies to the warped
\theta
-bubbles provided the weight function
u
satisfies suitable boundary assumptions.

Theorem 3.4
.

If
(N^{n},\partial N)
is a compact Riemannian manifold, let
\theta:\partial N\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a smooth function such that
S_{\pm}=\{x\in\partial N,\cos\theta(x)=\pm 1\}
and
S_{+}
and
S_{-}
are non-empty closed domains with smooth boundary in
\partial N
.
Let
u>0
be a smooth function on
N
with
\nabla_{\nu_{\partial N}}u+uH_{\partial N}>0
.

Let
\mathcal{S}
be the set of all Caccioppoli sets that contain
S_{+}
and be disjoint from
S_{-}
, then

\alpha=\inf_{\Omega\in\mathcal{S}}\mathcal{A}_{u}(\Omega)

is obtained by some
\Omega\in\mathcal{S}
. If
n\leq 4
, then the minimizer
\Omega
is a smooth submanifold with boundary that intersect
\partial N
transversally.

Proof.

We apply exactly the same foliation as in Theorem
3.3
, now we compare the following,

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{u}(\Omega_{t}\cup\Omega)-\mathcal{A}_{u}(\Omega)

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial^{*}\Omega_{t}\setminus\Omega}u-\int_{\partial^{*}\Omega\cap\Omega_{t}}u-\int_{\partial N\cap(\Omega_{t}\setminus\Omega)}u\cos\theta

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\partial^{*}\Omega_{t}\setminus\Omega}u\nu_{\Gamma_{t}}\cdot\nu_{\Gamma_{t}}-\int_{\partial^{*}\Omega\cap\Omega_{t}}u\nu_{\Gamma_{t}}\cdot\nu_{\partial\Omega}-\int_{\partial N\cap(\Omega_{t}\setminus\Omega)}u\cos\theta

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega_{t}\setminus\Omega}\dive(u\nu_{\Gamma_{t}})-\int_{\partial N\cap(\Omega_{t}\setminus\Omega)}u(\nu_{\Gamma_{t}}\cdot\nu_{\partial N}+\cos\theta)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega_{t}\setminus\Omega}\dive(u\nu_{\Gamma_{t}})

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega_{t}\setminus\Omega}\nabla_{\nu_{\Gamma_{t}}}u+u\dive_{\Gamma_{t}}\nu_{\Gamma_{t}}

Note as
t\rightarrow 0
,

\nabla_{\nu_{\Gamma_{t}}}u+u\dive_{\Gamma_{t}}\nu_{\Gamma_{t}}\rightarrow-\nabla_{\nu_{\partial N}}u-uH_{\partial N}<0.

Choosing
t
small enough then we are done.
∎

We compute the first and second variation of (warped)
\theta
-bubbles.

Lemma 3.5

(First Variation)
.

If
\Omega
is a smooth
\theta
-bubble in
(N^{n},\partial N)
that intersect
\partial N
transversally, and
\partial^{*}\Omega=\Sigma
, then let
\nu
be the outward pointing unit normal of
\partial\Sigma\subset\Sigma
and
\overline{\nu}
be the outward pointing unit normal of
\partial\Sigma\subset(\overline{\Omega}\cap\partial N)
, then

(3.1)

H_{\Sigma}=0,\langle\nu,\overline{\nu}\rangle=\cos\theta.

If
\Omega
is a smooth warped
\theta
-bubble in
(N^{n},\partial N)
that intersect
\partial N
transversally, and
\partial^{*}\Omega=\Sigma
and a choice of unit normal
\nu_{\Sigma}
, then

(3.2)

H_{\Sigma}=-\frac{\nabla_{\nu_{\Sigma}}u}{u},\langle\nu,\overline{\nu}\rangle=\cos\theta.

Proof.

The proof of (
3.1
) is the same as the case where
\theta
is a constant function, as shown in
[
RS97
]
.
We now prove (
3.2
).
Let
X_{t}
be a vector field along
\Sigma_{t}
with
X_{t}(x)\in T_{x}\partial N
for
x\in\partial N\cap\Sigma_{t}
then,

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}(\Vol(\Sigma_{t}))

\displaystyle=\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\dive_{\Sigma_{t}}(uX_{t})-\int_{\partial\Sigma_{t}}u\cos\theta(X_{t}\cdot\overline{\nu})

\displaystyle=\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\nabla_{\Sigma_{t}}u\cdot X_{t}^{\perp}+u\dive_{\Sigma_{t}}X_{t}^{\perp}+\int_{\partial\Sigma_{t}}uX_{t}\cdot\nu_{t}-u\cos\theta(X_{t}\cdot\overline{\nu})

\displaystyle=\int_{\Sigma_{t}}(\nabla_{\nu_{\Sigma_{t}}}u+H_{\Sigma_{t}}u)X_{t}\cdot\nu_{\Sigma_{t}}+\int_{\partial\Sigma_{t}}uX_{t}\cdot\overline{\nu_{t}}(\nu_{t}\cdot\overline{\nu_{t}}-\cos\theta),

where we used that for
t=0
,
\nu_{t}=(X_{t}\cdot\overline{\nu_{t}})\overline{\nu_{t}}+(X_{t}\cdot\nu_{\partial N})\nu_{\partial N}
.

Setting the first variation equal to zero we obtain (
3.2
).
∎

Lemma 3.6

(Second Variation)
.

If
\Omega
is a smooth warped
\theta
-bubble in
(N^{n},\partial N)
that intersect
\partial N
transversally, denote
\partial^{*}\Omega=\Sigma
and given a choice of unit normal
\nu_{\Sigma}
, then let
X_{t}
be a vector field along
\Sigma_{t}
with
\Sigma_{0}=\Sigma
and
X_{t}(x)\in T_{x}\partial N
for
x\in\partial N\cap\Sigma_{t}
, we denote
X_{0}\cdot\nu_{\Sigma}=:\phi
, then

\displaystyle\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}\bigg\rvert_{t=0}\Vol(\Sigma_{t})

\displaystyle=\int_{\Sigma}-u\phi\Delta\phi-u\phi^{2}(\Ric_{N}(\nu_{\Sigma},\nu_{\Sigma})+|\mathrm{I\!I}_{\Sigma}|^{2})+\phi^{2}(\Delta_{N}u-\Delta_{\Sigma}u)-\phi\langle\nabla^{\Sigma}\phi,\nabla^{\Sigma}u\rangle

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\int_{\partial\Sigma}u\phi\nabla_{\nu}\phi-\frac{u\phi^{2}}{\sin\theta}\left(\mathrm{I\!I}_{\partial N}(\overline{\nu},\overline{\nu})-\cos\theta\mathrm{I\!I}_{\Sigma}(\nu,\nu)-\nabla_{\overline{\nu}}\theta\right),

where
\nu
is the outward pointing unit normal of
\partial\Sigma\subset\Sigma
and
\overline{\nu}
is the outward pointing unit normal of
\partial\Sigma\subset(\overline{\Omega}\cap\partial N)
.

Setting
u=1
we obtain the second variation of a
\theta
-bubble.

A smooth (warped)
\theta
-bubble is called stable if for any admissible variation (if
X_{t}
is a vector field along
\Sigma_{t}
, then
\Sigma_{0}=\Sigma
and
X_{t}(x)\in T_{x}\partial N
for
x\in\partial N\cap\Sigma_{t}
),

\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}\bigg\rvert_{t=0}\Vol(\Sigma_{t})\geq 0.

Proof.

We compute the integrand over
\Sigma
and first show it’s enough to compute the second variation over
X_{t}^{\perp}=(X_{t}\cdot\nu_{\Sigma})\nu_{\Sigma}
, let
X^{T}_{t}:=X_{t}-X^{\perp}_{t}
,

\displaystyle Q_{1}:

\displaystyle=\int_{\Sigma}\frac{d}{dt}\bigg\rvert_{t=0}(uH_{\Sigma}\cdot X_{t}+\nabla_{X_{t}^{\perp}}u)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Sigma}\nabla_{X^{T}_{t}}(uH_{\Sigma}\cdot X_{t}+\nabla_{X_{t}^{\perp}}u)+\nabla_{X^{\perp}_{t}}(uH_{\Sigma}\cdot X_{t}+\nabla_{X_{t}^{\perp}}u)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(*)}}{{=}}\int_{\Sigma}\dive_{\Sigma_{t}}((uH_{\Sigma}\cdot X_{t}+\nabla_{X_{t}^{\perp}}u)\cdot X^{T}_{t})+\nabla_{X^{\perp}_{t}}(uH_{\Sigma}\cdot X_{t}+\nabla_{X_{t}^{\perp}}u)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(*)}}{{=}}\int_{\Sigma}\nabla_{X^{\perp}_{t}}(uH_{\Sigma}\cdot X_{t}+\nabla_{X_{t}^{\perp}}u),

where in the place where
(*)
is marked we used the first variation
uH_{\Sigma}\cdot X_{t}+\nabla_{X_{t}^{\perp}}u=0
.

We now compute
Q_{1}
using
X_{t}=X_{t}^{\perp}=\phi_{t}\cdot\nu_{t}
with
\nu_{0}=\nu_{\Sigma}
, and evaluate at
t=0
at each step,

\displaystyle Q_{1}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Sigma}\partial_{t}(uH_{\Sigma}+\nabla_{\nu_{\Sigma}}u)\phi

\displaystyle=\int_{\Sigma}\phi^{2}\nabla_{\nu_{\Sigma}}uH_{\Sigma}+u\phi\partial_{t}H_{\Sigma}+\phi^{2}\nabla_{\nu_{\Sigma}}\nabla_{\nu_{\Sigma}}u

\displaystyle=\int_{\Sigma}-u\phi\Delta\phi-u\phi^{2}(\Ric_{N}(\nu_{\Sigma},\nu_{\Sigma})+|\mathrm{I\!I}_{\Sigma}|^{2})+\phi^{2}\nabla_{\nu_{\Sigma}}uH_{\Sigma}+\phi^{2}(\nabla^{2}u(\nu_{\Sigma},\nu_{\Sigma})+\nabla_{\nu_{\Sigma}}\nu_{\Sigma}\cdot\nabla^{\Sigma}u)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(**)}}{{=}}\int_{\Sigma}-u\phi\Delta\phi-u\phi^{2}(\Ric_{N}(\nu_{\Sigma},\nu_{\Sigma})+|\mathrm{I\!I}_{\Sigma}|^{2})+\phi^{2}(\Delta_{N}u-\Delta_{\Sigma}u)-\phi^{2}\nabla^{\Sigma}\phi\cdot\nabla^{\Sigma}u

where in
(**)
we used that
\Delta_{N}u-\Delta_{\Sigma}u=\nabla^{2}u(\nu_{\Sigma},\nu_{\Sigma})+\nabla_{\nu_{\Sigma}}uH_{\Sigma}
and
\phi\nabla_{\nu_{\Sigma}}\nu_{\Sigma}=-\nabla^{\Sigma}\phi
.

We now compute the integrand over
\partial\Sigma
. One can show that it’s enough to consider variations
X_{t}=\varphi\cdot\overline{\nu}
with
\phi=-\varphi\sin\theta
(at
t=0
) for this computation similar to the interior case in
Q_{1}
,

\displaystyle Q_{2}:

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial\Sigma}u\varphi\frac{d}{dt}\bigg\rvert_{t=0}(\nu_{t}\cdot\overline{\nu_{t}}-\cos\theta)

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial\Sigma}u\varphi(\partial_{t}\nu_{t}\cdot\overline{\nu_{t}}+\nu_{t}\cdot\partial_{t}\overline{\nu_{t}}+\varphi\sin\theta\nabla_{\overline{\nu}}\theta)

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial\Sigma}\frac{-u\phi}{\sin\theta}(\partial_{t}\nu_{t}\cdot\overline{\nu_{t}}+\nu_{t}\cdot\partial_{t}\overline{\nu_{t}}+\varphi\sin\theta\nabla_{\overline{\nu}}\theta)

We compute
\partial_{t}\nu_{t}\cdot\overline{\nu_{t}}
using
\overline{\nu_{t}}=\cos\theta\nu_{t}-\sin\theta_{t}\nu_{\Sigma_{t}}
. Note only at
t=0
,
\theta_{0}=\theta
the prescribed function over
\partial N
,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\nu_{t}\cdot\overline{\nu_{t}}

\displaystyle=\partial_{t}\nu_{t}\cdot(\cos\theta_{t}\nu_{t}-\sin\theta_{t}\nu_{\Sigma_{t}})

\displaystyle=-\sin\theta_{t}(\partial_{t}\nu_{t}\cdot\nu_{\Sigma_{t}})

\displaystyle=-\varphi\sin\theta_{t}(\cos\theta_{t}\nabla_{\nu_{t}}\nu_{t}\cdot\nu_{\Sigma_{t}}-\sin\theta_{t}\nabla_{\nu_{\Sigma_{t}}}\nu_{t}\cdot\nu_{\Sigma_{t}})

\displaystyle=-\phi\cos\theta\mathrm{I\!I}_{\Sigma}(\nu,\nu)+\varphi\sin^{2}\theta(-\nu_{t}\cdot\nabla_{\nu_{\Sigma_{t}}}\nu_{\Sigma_{t}})

\displaystyle=-\phi\cos\theta\mathrm{I\!I}_{\Sigma}(\nu,\nu)+\sin\theta(-\nu\cdot\nabla^{\Sigma}\phi)\quad\text{at }t=0.

And similarly,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\overline{\nu_{t}}\cdot\nu_{t}

\displaystyle=\partial_{t}\overline{\nu_{t}}\cdot(\cos\theta_{t}\overline{\nu_{t}}+\sin\theta_{t}\nu_{\partial N})

\displaystyle=\sin\theta(\partial_{t}\overline{\nu_{t}}\cdot\nu_{\partial N})

\displaystyle=-\varphi\sin\theta\mathrm{I\!I}_{\partial N}(\overline{\nu},\overline{\nu})

\displaystyle=\phi\mathrm{I\!I}_{\partial N}(\overline{\nu},\overline{\nu})

In total we get,

Q_{2}=\int_{\partial\Sigma}u\phi\nabla_{\nu}\phi-\frac{u\phi^{2}}{\sin\theta}(\mathrm{I\!I}_{\partial N}(\overline{\nu},\overline{\nu})-\cos\theta\mathrm{I\!I}_{\Sigma}(\nu,\nu)-\nabla_{\overline{\nu}}\theta)

This finishes the computation.
∎

Using Schoen-Yau rearrangement we can relate the interior terms in the second variation with scalar curvature.
Using a computation in

]
, we can rearrange the boundary terms to relate with the mean curvature of the ambient manifold.

Lemma 3.7

(Rearranged Second Variation)
.

If
\Omega
is a smooth warped
\theta
-bubble in
(N^{n},\partial N)
that intersect
\partial N
transversally, denote
\partial^{*}\Omega=\Sigma

\Sigma_{t}

X_{0}\cdot\nu_{\Sigma}=:\phi
, then

\displaystyle\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}\bigg\rvert_{t=0}\Vol(\Sigma_{t})

\displaystyle=\int_{\Sigma}-\dive_{\Sigma}(u\nabla\phi)\phi-\frac{1}{2}u\phi^{2}(R_{N}-R_{\Sigma}+|\mathrm{I\!I}_{\Sigma}|^{2})

\displaystyle+\int_{\Sigma}-\frac{|\nabla_{\nu_{\Sigma}}u|^{2}\phi^{2}}{2u}+\phi^{2}(\Delta_{N}u-\Delta_{\Sigma}u)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\int_{\partial\Sigma}u\phi\nabla_{\nu}\phi-\frac{u\phi^{2}}{\sin\theta}\left(H_{\partial N}-\cos\theta H_{\Sigma}-\nabla_{\overline{\nu}}\theta\right)+u\phi^{2}\mathrm{I\!I}_{\nu}(\partial\Sigma)

\partial\Sigma\subset\Sigma
,
\overline{\nu}
is the outward pointing unit normal of
\partial\Sigma\subset(\overline{\Omega}\cap\partial N)

\mathrm{I\!I}_{\nu}(\partial\Sigma)=-\sum_{i=1}^{n-2}\langle\nabla_{e_{i}}e_{i},\nu\rangle
for an orthonormal basis
(e_{i})_{i=1}^{n-2}
of
\partial\Sigma
.

Setting

we obtain the rearranged second variation of a
\theta
-bubble.

Proof.

We first have,

(3.3)

\displaystyle\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}\bigg\rvert_{t=0}\Vol(\Sigma_{t})

\displaystyle=\int_{\Sigma}-u\phi\Delta\phi-\frac{1}{2}u\phi^{2}(R_{N}-R_{\Sigma}+|\mathrm{I\!I}_{\Sigma}|^{2}+H^{2}_{\Sigma})

\displaystyle+\int_{\Sigma}\phi^{2}(\Delta_{N}u-\Delta_{\Sigma}u)-\phi\langle\nabla^{\Sigma}\phi,\nabla^{\Sigma}u\rangle

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\int_{\partial\Sigma}u\phi\nabla_{\nu}\phi-\frac{u\phi^{2}}{\sin\theta}\left(H_{\partial N}-\cos\theta H_{\Sigma}-\nabla_{\overline{\nu}}\theta\right)+u\phi^{2}\mathrm{I\!I}_{\nu}(\partial\Sigma)

The interior rearrangement follows from the Gauss-Codazzi equation,

R_{N}=R_{\Sigma}+2\Ric(\nu_{\Sigma},\nu_{\Sigma})+|\mathrm{I\!I}_{\Sigma}|^{2}-H^{2}_{\Sigma}.

The boundary rearrangement follows from the following analogous computation of equation (3.8) in

]

H_{\partial N}=\mathrm{I\!I}_{\partial N}(\overline{\nu},\overline{\nu})+\cos\theta H_{\Sigma}-\cos\theta\mathrm{I\!I}_{\Sigma}(\nu,\nu)+\sin\theta\mathrm{I\!I}_{\nu}(\partial\Sigma).

This gives the equality (
3.3
).

The desired equality follows from first variation
uH_{\Sigma}+\nabla_{\nu_{\Sigma}}u=0
.
∎

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
(\Sigma^{2},\partial\Sigma)
be a compact surface with a positive smooth function
u:\Sigma\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+}
such that,

\Delta_{\Sigma}u\leq K_{\Sigma}u+\frac{|\nabla_{\Sigma}u|^{2}}{2u},\quad\nabla_{\nu}u\geq a_{0}u-\kappa_{\partial\Sigma}u,

for some
a_{0}>0
, where
K_{\Sigma}
is the Gauss curvature of
\Sigma
and
k_{\partial\Sigma}
the geodesic curvature (equal to the mean curvature), and
\nu
the outward pointing unit normal of
\partial\Sigma\subset\Sigma
;
then for any
x\in\Sigma
,

d_{\Sigma}(x,\partial\Sigma)\leq\frac{2}{a_{0}}.

Proof.

We summarize the proof in
[
Wu24
]
and the reader can refer to
[
Wu24
]
for a more detailed proof.

Assume that there is a point
p\in\Sigma
with
d_{\Sigma}(p,\partial\Sigma)=\frac{2}{a_{0}}+2\epsilon
for some
\epsilon>0
.
Let

h(x)=\frac{2}{\frac{2}{a_{0}}+\epsilon-d_{\Sigma}(x,\partial\Sigma)},\quad\text{if }d_{\Sigma}(x,\partial\Sigma)<\frac{2}{a_{0}}+\epsilon,

where we denote
d_{\Sigma}(x,\partial\Sigma)
the mollified distance function to the boundary and that
h^{2}-2|\nabla^{\Sigma}h|>0
. Let
\Omega_{0}
be a fixed open set containing
\{x\in\Sigma,d_{\Sigma}(x,\partial\Sigma)\geq\frac{2}{a_{0}}+\epsilon\}
.

We solve for the following warped
\mu
-bubble problem over
\Sigma
for Caccioppoli sets in
\Sigma
containing a fixed open neighborhood of
p
, and be disjoint from a fixed open neighborhood of
\partial\Sigma
,

\mathcal{A}_{h}(\Omega)=\int_{\partial\Omega}u-\int_{\Sigma}hu(\chi_{\Omega}-\chi_{\Omega_{0}}).

A maximum principle argument of first variation in
[
Wu24
]
shows that a smooth minimizer must exists. This is where we need the boundary mean convexity assumption
\partial_{\nu}u\geq a_{0}u-\kappa_{\partial\Sigma}u
.

Then the first variation of the minimizer
\Gamma=\partial\Omega
, along a smooth variation
X=\phi\nu_{\Gamma}
is the following,

(4.1)

\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{A}_{h}(\Gamma)=\int_{\Gamma}\phi(uH_{\Gamma}+\nabla_{\nu_{\Gamma}}u)-hu\phi.

And the second variation is,

\displaystyle\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}\mathcal{A}_{h}(\Gamma)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Gamma}\phi u(-\Delta_{\Gamma}\phi-\phi(\Ric_{\Sigma}(\nu,\nu)+|\mathrm{I\!I}_{\Gamma}|^{2}))+\phi^{2}H_{\Sigma}\nabla_{\nu_{\Gamma}}u

\displaystyle+\int_{\Gamma}\phi^{2}\nabla_{\nu_{\Gamma}}\nabla_{\nu_{\Gamma}}u-\phi^{2}\nabla_{\nu_{\Gamma}}(hu)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\star 1)}}{{=}}\int_{\Gamma}\phi u(-\Delta_{\Gamma}\phi-\phi(\frac{1}{2}R_{\Sigma}+|\mathrm{I\!I}_{\Gamma}|^{2}))+\phi^{2}H_{\Sigma}\nabla_{\nu_{\Gamma}}u

\displaystyle+\int_{\Gamma}\phi^{2}\nabla^{2}u(\nu_{\Gamma},\nu_{\Gamma})+\phi^{2}\nabla^{\Gamma}u\cdot\nabla_{\nu_{\Gamma}}\nu_{\Gamma}-\phi^{2}\nabla_{\nu_{\Gamma}}(hu)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\star 2)}}{{=}}\int_{\Gamma}\phi(-\dive_{\Gamma}(u\nabla_{\Gamma}\phi)-u\phi(\frac{1}{2}R_{\Sigma}+|\mathrm{I\!I}_{\Gamma}|^{2}))+\phi^{2}(\Delta_{\Sigma}u-\Delta_{\Gamma}u)-\phi^{2}\nabla_{\nu_{\Gamma}}(hu)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\star 3)}}{{\leq}}\int_{\Gamma}\phi(-\dive_{\Gamma}(u\nabla_{\Gamma}\phi))+\frac{|\nabla_{{}^{\Sigma}}u|^{2}}{2u}\phi^{2}-u\phi^{2}|\mathrm{I\!I}_{\Gamma}|^{2}-\phi^{2}\Delta_{\Gamma}u-\phi^{2}\nabla_{\nu_{\Gamma}}(hu)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\star 4)}}{{\leq}}\int_{\Gamma}\phi(-\dive_{\Gamma}(u\nabla_{\Gamma}\phi))+\frac{|\nabla_{{}^{\Sigma}}u|^{2}}{2u}\phi^{2}-\frac{u\phi^{2}}{2}(h-\frac{\nabla_{\nu_{\Gamma}}u}{u})^{2}-\phi^{2}\Delta_{\Gamma}u-\phi^{2}\nabla_{\nu_{\Gamma}}(hu)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Gamma}u(\nabla_{\Gamma}\phi)^{2}+\frac{|\nabla_{{}^{\Sigma}}u|^{2}}{2u}\phi^{2}-\frac{u\phi h^{2}}{2}-\frac{(\nabla_{\nu_{\Gamma}}u)^{2}}{2u}-\phi^{2}\Delta_{\Gamma}u-\phi^{2}u\nabla_{\nu_{\Gamma}}h

\displaystyle=\int_{\Gamma}\frac{|\nabla_{\Gamma}u|^{2}}{2u}\phi^{2}-\phi^{2}\Delta_{\Gamma}u-\frac{\phi^{2}u}{2}(h^{2}+2\nabla_{\nu_{\Gamma}}h)+u(\nabla_{\Gamma}\phi)^{2}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\star 5)}}{{<}}\int_{\Gamma}\frac{|\nabla_{\Gamma}u|^{2}}{2u}\phi^{2}-\phi^{2}\Delta_{\Gamma}u+u(\nabla_{\Gamma}\phi)^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Gamma}\frac{|\nabla_{\Gamma}u|^{2}}{2u}\phi^{2}+\nabla_{\Gamma}u\nabla_{\Gamma}\phi^{2}+u(\nabla_{\Gamma}\phi)^{2}.

The computation of warped
\mu
-bubble here works in general dimensions with small adaptions.
In
(\star 1)
we used for surfaces the Ricci curvature is one half of the scalar curvature; in general dimensions one can use the Gauss-Codazzi equation.
In
(\star 2)
we used that for a normal variation with speed
\phi
,
\partial_{t}\nu_{\Gamma}=-\nabla^{\Gamma}\phi
. In
(\star 3)
we used the interior NNSC assumption
\Delta_{\Sigma}u\leq K_{\Sigma}u+\frac{|\nabla_{\Sigma}u|^{2}}{2u}
.
In
(\star 4)
we used the first variation
|\mathrm{I\!I}_{\Gamma}|^{2}=(H_{\Gamma})^{2}=(h-\frac{\nabla_{\nu_{\Gamma}}u}{u})^{2}
and
|\mathrm{I\!I}_{\Gamma}|^{2}\geq\frac{|\mathrm{I\!I}_{\Gamma}|^{2}}{2}
. Lastly in
(\star 5)
we used
h^{2}-2|\nabla^{\Sigma}h|>0
.

We now choose
\phi^{2}u=1
to get,

\frac{|\nabla_{\Gamma}u|^{2}}{2u}\phi^{2}+\nabla_{\Gamma}u\nabla_{\Gamma}\phi^{2}+u(\nabla_{\Gamma}\phi)^{2}=\frac{|\nabla_{\Gamma}u|^{2}}{2u^{2}}-\frac{|\nabla_{\Gamma}u|^{2}}{u^{2}}+\frac{|\nabla_{\Gamma}u|^{2}}{4u^{2}}=-\frac{|\nabla_{\Gamma}u|^{2}}{4u^{2}},

which gives us a contradiction to the stability inequality.
∎

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
(\Sigma^{2},\partial\Sigma)
be a stable

-warped
\theta

The rearranged second variation over

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Sigma}-\dive_{\Sigma}(u\nabla\phi)\phi-\frac{1}{2}u\phi^{2}(R_{M}-R_{\Sigma}+|\mathrm{I\!I}_{\Sigma}|^{2})

\displaystyle+\int_{\Sigma}-\frac{|\nabla_{\nu_{\Sigma}}u|^{2}\phi^{2}}{2u}+\phi^{2}(\Delta_{M}u-\Delta_{\Sigma}u)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\int_{\partial\Sigma}u\phi\nabla_{\nu}\phi-\frac{u\phi^{2}}{\sin\theta}\left(H_{\partial M}-\cos\theta H_{\Sigma}-\nabla_{\overline{\nu}}\theta\right)+u\phi^{2}\mathrm{I\!I}_{\nu}(\partial\Sigma)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Sigma}-\dive_{\Sigma}(u\nabla\phi)\phi+\frac{1}{2}u\phi^{2}(R_{\Sigma}-|\mathrm{I\!I}_{\Sigma}|^{2})+\frac{|\nabla^{{\Sigma}}u|^{2}\phi^{2}}{2u}+\phi^{2}(-\Delta_{\Sigma}u)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\int_{\partial\Sigma}u\phi\nabla_{\nu}\phi-\frac{u\phi^{2}}{\sin\theta}\left(H_{\partial M}-\cos\theta H_{\Sigma}-\nabla_{\overline{\nu}}\theta\right)+u\phi^{2}\mathrm{I\!I}_{\nu}(\partial\Sigma)

Then we obtain that for some choice of

\displaystyle\dive_{\Sigma}(u\nabla\phi)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{R_{\Sigma}}{2}u\phi-\phi\Delta_{\Sigma}u+\frac{|\nabla^{\Sigma}u|^{2}\phi}{2u},

\displaystyle\nabla_{\nu}\phi

\displaystyle=\frac{\phi}{\sin\theta}(H_{\partial M}-\nabla_{\bar{\nu}}\phi-\cos\theta H_{\Sigma})-\phi\mathrm{I\!I}_{\nu}(\partial\Sigma)

We now check the boundary condition of

\nu
the outward pointing unit normal of

\displaystyle\geq a_{0}(u\phi)-\mathrm{I\!I}_{\nu}(\partial\Sigma)(u\phi)=a_{0}f-\mathrm{I\!I}_{\nu}(\partial\Sigma)f

for all

The proof of (

\displaystyle\nu

\displaystyle:=\nu_{\partial\Sigma}=\cos\theta\bar{\nu}+\sin\theta\nu_{\partial M},\quad

\displaystyle\nu_{\Sigma}

\displaystyle=-\sin\theta\bar{\nu}+\cos\theta\nu_{\partial M}

\displaystyle 0

Since

is a
\theta
-bubble (

Corollary 4.3
.

Consider
(M^{3},\partial M)\hookrightarrow(X^{4},\partial X)
a FMBH and
X

\partial M

Using the stability inequality of

Now using the diameter of
\partial M
is at least

We now minimize the following functional as in Theorem

-warped
\theta

\mathcal{A}_{u}(\Omega)=\int_{\partial^{*}\Omega}u-\int_{\partial M\cap\Omega}u\cos\theta.

We remark that now the ambient manifold

we required compactness). We can use a similar adaptation to non-compact manifolds, as in the proof of Theorem 1.4 and Remark 3.2 in
[

We now check that condition (

\displaystyle\nabla_{\nu_{\partial M}}u+u(H_{\partial M}-\nabla_{\overline{\nu}}\theta)

5.
Parabolicity and Stability

In
[
CSZ97
]
, the authors proved that a complete non-compact two-sided stable minimal hypersurfaces in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
for
n\geq 3
must have only one end. The use of Michael-Simon-Sobolev inequality for minimal hypersurfaces in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
is crucial since this implies that any end of a stable minimal hypersurfaces must be non-parabolic. And having two non-parabolic ends together with the Schoen-Yau rearranged stability inequality using the Bochner formula, this implies that the stable minimal hypersurface must admit a non-constant harmonic function with finite energy and must have finite volume, a contradiction to the monotonicity formula.

In
[
CLS24
]
, the authors proved that under the assumption of non-negative 2 intermediate Ricci curvature of a 4 dimensional ambient manifold, a stable minimal hypersurface with infinite volume can have at most one non-parabolic end. This was extended to the FBMH case in
[
Wu23
]
.

In section
6
, we will show that if

is a simply connected stable minimal hypersurface in
(X^{4},\partial X)
as in our main theorem, then
\partial M
must be connected and have an end in the only non-parabolic end of
M
. In this section we prove some auxiliary results.

We first prove a generalized case to Theorem 5.2 in
[
Wu23
]
.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
(X^{4},\partial X)
be a complete Riemannian manifold with
\Ric^{X}_{2}\geq 0
and
(M^{3},\partial M)
a FBMH in
X
. If either

•

u
is a smooth harmonic function on
M
with Neumann boundary condition and
\mathrm{I\!I}_{2}^{\partial X}\geq 0
,

•

or
u
is a smooth harmonic function on
M
with Dirichlet boundary condition and
H_{\partial X}\geq 0
,

then

\int_{M}\phi^{2}(\frac{1}{3}|\mathrm{I\!I}|^{2}|\nabla u|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}|\nabla|\nabla u||^{2})\leq\int_{M}|\nabla\phi|^{2}|\nabla u|^{2}.

Proof.

The proof of the Neumann case is in
[
Wu23
]
, Theorem 5.2.

For Dirichlet case, the same proof of applies to get the following (without assuming any boundary condition),

(5.1)

\displaystyle\int_{M}\phi^{2}(\frac{1}{3}|\mathrm{I\!I}|^{2}|\nabla u|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}|\nabla|\nabla u||^{2})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{M}|\nabla\phi|^{2}|\nabla u|^{2}+\int_{\partial M}\phi^{2}(\frac{1}{2}\nabla_{\nu_{\partial M}}|\nabla u|^{2}-\mathrm{I\!I}_{\partial X}(\nu_{M},\nu_{M})|\nabla u|^{2}).

We have
0=\Delta_{M}u=\Delta_{\partial M}u+\nabla^{2}u(\nu_{\partial M},\nu_{\partial M})+H_{\partial M}\nabla_{\nu_{\partial M}}u
. The Dirichlet boundary condition implies
\Delta_{\partial M}u=0
and
\nabla u=\nabla_{\nu_{\partial M}}u\cdot\nu_{\partial M}
,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\nabla_{\nu_{\partial M}}|\nabla u|^{2}

\displaystyle=\nabla_{\nu_{\partial M}}\nabla u\cdot\nabla u

\displaystyle=(\nabla_{\nu_{\partial M}}u)\nabla^{2}u(\nu_{\partial M},\nu_{\partial M})

\displaystyle=-(\nabla_{\nu_{\partial M}}u)^{2}H_{\partial M}=-H_{\partial M}|\nabla u|^{2}.

Plug in the last computation into (
5.1
) and use
H_{\partial X}\geq 0
then we obtain the desired inequality.
∎

Remark 5.2

Note that

Definition 5.7
.

We say a Riemannian manifold

We recall Theorem 5.3 in
[

]
, that a stable FMBH in a 4-manifold with non-negative “2-intermediate Ricci curvature” and “2-convex” boundary can only have at most one non-parabolic end if its volume is infinite.

Theorem 5.8
.

If
(M^{3},\partial M)\rightarrow(X^{4},\partial X)

The proof that

[

]

Given any compact set

.
Using a linear cut-off function for

must be finite.

We now show that if

is non-parabolic, then either

Assume
\partial_{1}M
is a compact component of
\partial M

We minimize Dirichlet energy on

Consider

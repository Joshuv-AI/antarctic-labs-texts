Preclinical and clinical studies on CAR-NK cell therapy: latest update from 2025 ASH Annual Meeting
Authors: Hanxi Luo, Yiyang Ding, Xiangyu Zhao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractChimeric antigen receptor–engineered natural killer (CAR-NK) cells are emerging as an off-the-shelf immunotherapy for hematologic malignancies, supported by early clinical data and a favorable safety profile. Compared with CAR-T therapy, CAR-NK cells offer the potential for scalable allogeneic manufacturing with reduced toxicity while retaining antitumor activity. However, challenges such as limited in vivo persistence, immunosuppressive tumor microenvironments, and variable durability of responses remain. This summary highlights recent preclinical and clinical advances in CAR-NK cell therapy reported at the 2025 ASH Annual Meeting, focusing on engineering strategies to enhance NK-cell fitness and early trial results demonstrating efficacy with manageable toxicity.Keywords: CAR-NK, Off-the-shelf immunotherapy, Hematologic malignancies

To the editor

CAR-NK cell therapies have demonstrated encouraging antitumor potential in cancer treatment while maintaining a generally favorable safety profile. The first-in-human phase I/II study was reported by Liu et al. in 2020, which demonstrated efficacy and safety of cord blood–derived, HLA-mismatched anti-CD19 CAR-NK cells in patients with relapsed or refractory CD19-positive malignancies [1]. A broad range of strategies have been explored to improve NK cell functionality, persistence, and therapeutic performance. Here, we summarize the latest preclinical and clinical advances in CAR-NK cell therapy presented at the 2025 ASH Annual Meeting.

Abstracts presented at the 2025 ASH Annual Meeting were initially retrieved by searching the official ASH abstract database using keywords including “natural killer,” “NK cell,” “CAR-NK,” and related disease terms. The retrieved abstracts were subsequently reviewed manually by the authors to select studies directly relevant to CAR-NK cell engineering strategies, mechanistic insights, or clinical translation, which form the basis of this summary.

Preclinical studies

To enhance NK cell activity and persistence, engineering CAR-NK cells to express or activate stimulatory cytokines or cytokine receptors remains a widely adopted strategy. CAR-NK cells engineered to secrete IL-7 and co-express IL-7Rα (NK-7R) showed increased proliferation and improved metabolic fitness, including higher glycolytic activity and maximal respiratory capacity. In vivo, NGS-Tg (Hu-IL15) mice receiving intravenous NK-7R cells exhibited increased infiltration/persistence of hCD45⁺ CAR-NK cells across multiple organs. In a Raji lymphoma model, co-administration with CD19 CAR-T cells improved tumor control compared with control CAR-NK conditions. Mechanistically, NK-7R cells displayed enhanced STAT1 signaling with upregulation of IFNG, CXCL10, IFIT1, IFIT3, and TNFSF10 [2]. Further study should test generalizability across additional tumor models/antigens. Beyond enhancing cytokine signaling, increasing NK cell resistance to immunosuppressive cues within the tumor microenvironment is another strategy. Chronic TGF-β exposure during NK cell expansion (TGF-β imprinting) conferred resistance to TGF-β–mediated suppression. TGFβ-imprinted CD33 CAR-NK cells maintained cytotoxic function, and the combination of TGF-β imprinting, CD33 CAR expression, and NKTR-255—a polymer-conjugated IL-15 receptor agonist—achieved the most durable antileukemic activity and survival benefit in MOLM-13 and MV4-11 AML xenograft models [3].

Genetic disruption of inhibitory receptors can enhance NK cell activity. A “2-in-1” CAR-NK platform was generated by CRISPR–Cas9 knockout of KLRC1 (NKG2A) with in-frame insertion of an anti-CD33 CAR at the same locus using an ssAAV HDR donor, confirmed by sequencing/flow cytometry. The edited NK cells showed improved cytotoxicity in vitro against AML cell lines and primary AML samples, and reduced leukemic burden in vivo in NSG mice engrafted with MOLM14-Luc/GFP or patient-derived AML. Because KLRC1-KO limited expansion, a cis-targeted IL-2 approach via a truncated EGFR reporter selectively enriched edited cells and supported persistence up to 45 days in patient-derived AML xenografts [4]. Another strategy is to simultaneously target tumor-associated antigens and resistance pathways. In CLL, ROR1 was confirmed on primary CLL cells, and Wnt5a stimulation reduced apoptotic priming and decreased NK-mediated killing, suggesting a CAR-NK resistance mechanism. Based on this, a tandem CD19/ROR1 CAR-NK was developed and showed enhanced activation and cytotoxicity against primary CLL samples compared with single-target CARs, while in vivo validation in NSG mouse CLL models is ongoing [5].

Pharmacologic co-strategies have shown added benefit. In AML cell lines and primary samples, PARP1 inhibition (AG-14361) pre-treatment remodeled the leukemia immunophenotype—upregulating NK-activating ligands (e.g., NKG2D ligands/TRAIL-R) while downregulating HLA-ABC—and consequently increased susceptibility to CD123 CAR-NK killing in flow cytometry–based cytotoxicity assays, with the strongest gain observed at low effector-to-target ratios. In vivo combination testing in an NSG patient-derived xenograft model is ongoing (Table 1) [6].

Beyond CAR-NK cells, ASH 2025 also highlighted several complementary redirecting strategies that broaden the landscape of NK-directed immunotherapy. An optimized affibody-derived bispecific NK cell engager linking CD16a on NK cells to BCMA on multiple myeloma cells achieved significant tumor growth inhibition in vivo when combined with an off-the-shelf adaptive NK product [7]. In parallel, bacterial immune engager platforms were reported, exemplified by Live Immune Modulating Engagers (LIME), in which non-pathogenic E. coli are engineered to surface-display dual scFvs recognizing tumor-associated antigens (e.g., CD19, CD123, MSLN, HER2, EGFR) and immune receptors (e.g., CD3, NKG2D, CD16), thereby promoting local activation of T and NK cells and enhancing tumor killing in vivo [8]. These approaches may be particularly attractive for combination with CAR-NK therapy because they can provide controllable, locally concentrated immune redirection and may reduce systemic exposure compared with soluble engagers.

Consistently, a first-in-class pretargeted glycoengineered NK (PG-NK) platform based on bioorthogonal click chemistry decoupled antigen recognition from effector function by pairing glycoengineered NK cells with tumor-specific antibodies (such as anti-CD20), markedly enhancing NK–tumor binding and improving survival in a subcutaneous Raji xenograft model [9].

iPSC-derived NK cells provide an off-the-shelf engineering platform; thus, recent progress in improving iPSC-NK maturation and function offers a foundation for next-generation iPSC-CAR-NK products. One study revealed that inducing T-bet expression starting from the hematopoietic progenitor cell (HPC) stage significantly upregulates various KIRs on NK cells, promotes degranulation and IFN-γ secretion, and augments cytotoxicity against the K562 cell line [10]. Another study highlighted that transient induction of BCL11B at the mesoderm stage increases multipotent HPCs and facilitates NK cell generation. The resulting iPSC-NKs exhibited more mature surface markers and robust transcription of cytotoxic effector genes (e.g., GZMB, PRF1, and GNLY), which translated to superior tumor control in a murine SKOV3 solid tumor model [11]. Furthermore, to target the immunosuppressive axis mediated by sialylated glycans enriched in the solid tumor microenvironment, CRISPR-mediated knockout of the glyco-immune checkpoint SIGLEC7 was employed. This modification broadly enhanced the in vitro killing efficacy against various tumor cell lines, as well as CD107a degranulation and the production of IFN-γ and TNF-α, without compromising early hematopoietic differentiation or NK cell expansion [12].

Clinical trials

Several early-phase clinical trials have provided encouraging evidence supporting the efficacy and safety of CAR-NK cell therapy. In a first-in-human study, an allogeneic, fully human CD123 CAR-NK product achieved an overall response rate of 80% at day 28 in heavily pretreated AML patients. CAR-NK cells expanded in vivo, peaking between days 7 and 14 after infusion, with no immune effector cell–associated neurotoxicity syndrome observed. Cytokine release syndrome was generally manageable, although one case of grade 4 CRS coincided with robust CAR-NK expansion and a donor–recipient KIR–HLA mismatch (Table 2) [13]. In an ongoing phase I study, the logic-gated off-the-shelf CAR-NK therapy SENTI-202, targeting CD33 and/or FLT3, demonstrated encouraging activity in relapsed or refractory AML, with an overall response rate of 50% and composite complete remission rates of up to 50% in the RP2D cohort, all of which were minimal residual disease–negative (NCT06325748). The treatment was well tolerated, with no dose-limiting toxicities reported (Table 2) [14]. GCC2005, an off-the-shelf allogeneic CD5-directed CAR-NK therapy, showed a favorable safety profile in patients with relapsed or refractory NK- and T-cell malignancies, with no dose-limiting toxicities, neurotoxicity, or graft-versus-host disease, and preliminary efficacy observed in early dose cohorts (NCT06699771) (Table 2) [15].

Conclusions

Collectively, the studies presented at ASH 2025 highlight several promising strategies to advance CAR-NK–based immunotherapy. On the preclinical front, key approaches include cytokine engineering to improve cell persistence, resistance modifications to counteract immunosuppression, and dual-targeting or genetic designs to overcome inhibitory signaling and tumor escape. Additional strategies, such as pharmacological sensitization and alternative NK-redirection platforms including bispecific engagers and bacterial immune engager systems, further expand the potential for rational combination therapies.

On the clinical front, although limited in vivo persistence of CAR-NK cells has traditionally been viewed as a challenge, emerging evidence suggests that transient persistence may be a feature rather than a limitation—reducing prolonged immune toxicity while enabling repeat or fractionated dosing to sustain antitumor pressure [16]. Early-phase trials have consistently demonstrated encouraging response rates with a favorable safety profile across multiple disease indications. As the field advances toward larger trials and more sophisticated engineering platforms, integrating lessons from both preclinical models and early clinical experience will be critical to realizing the full therapeutic potential of CAR-NK cell therapy.

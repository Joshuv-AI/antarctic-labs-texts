Arboricity and Random Edge Queries Matter for Triangle Counting using Sublinear Queries
Arijit Bishnu, Debarshi Chanda, Gopinath Mishra

Abstract
Given a simple, unweighted, undirected graph G = ( V , E ) G=(V,E) with | V | = n |V|=n and | E | = m |E|=m , and parameters 0 < ε , δ < 1 0<\varepsilon,\delta<1 , along with Degree , Neighbour , Edge and RandomEdge query access to G G , we provide a query based randomized algorithm to generate an estimate T ^ \widehat{T} of the number of triangles T T in G G , such that T ^ ∈ [ ( 1 − ε ) ​ T , ( 1 + ε ) ​ T ] \widehat{T}\in[(1-\varepsilon)T,(1+\varepsilon)T] with probability at least 1 − δ 1-\delta . The query complexity of our algorithm is O ~ ​ ( m ​ α ​ log ⁡ ( 1 / δ ) / ε 3 ​ T ) \widetilde{O}\left({m\alpha\log(1/\delta)}/{\varepsilon^{3}T}\right) , where α \alpha is the arboricity of G G . Our work can be seen as a continuation in the line of recent works [Eden et al., SIAM J Comp., 2017; Assadi et al., ITCS 2019; Eden et al. SODA 2020] that considered subgraph or triangle counting with or without the use of RandomEdge query. Of these works, Eden et al. [SODA 2020] considers the role of arboricity. Our work considers how RandomEdge query can leverage the notion of arboricity. Furthermore, continuing in the line of work of Assadi et al. [APPROX/RANDOM 2022], we also provide a lower bound of Ω ~ ​ ( m ​ α ​ log ⁡ ( 1 / δ ) / ε 2 ​ T ) \widetilde{\Omega}\left({m\alpha\log(1/\delta)}/{\varepsilon^{2}T}\right) that matches the upper bound exactly on arboricity and the parameter δ \delta and almost on ε \varepsilon .

The problem of triangle counting is a problem that has been extensively studied across various models of computation.

RAM model.

The work of
(
Chiba and Nishizeki, 1985
)

Streaming model.

In the data streaming model, a long line of work

Property Testing model.

In query complexity setup, the triangle counting problem has been studied in terms of various query access available to the algorithm. It has been shown that without access to

. Hence, sublinear query algorithms for counting triangles have been studied in models with

Technical Overview

In this section, we give a broad overview of the techniques used in this work. We denote by
T_{e}
the number of triangles the edge
e
participates in, and by
\mbox{deg}_{e}

=\mbox{deg}_{\left({u,v}\right)}=\min{\left\{{\mbox{deg}_{u},\mbox{deg}_{v}}\right\}}
.

Upper Bound.

Our starting point is to use the
RandomEdge
to obtain a random sample of edges
S
and try to estimate the number of triangles
T
incident on the edges of
S
. However, an edge
e
can participate in
\Omega\left({{\mbox{deg}_{e}}}\right)
triangles. Thus, counting the number of triangles each edge participate in will be too expensive. Also,
T_{e}
can grow up to
\Omega\left({{T}}\right)
, requiring
\left|{S}\right|
to be large to obtain a good estimate. To circumvent this issue, we consider only the edges that participate in
\leq\tau
triangles, called
light edges
. We denote the edges that are not light to be
heavy edges
. We call the triangles containing at least one light edge
light triangles
, and the triangles consisting entirely of heavy edges to be
heavy triangles
. Fixing the threshold
\tau
appropriately based on the arboricity
\alpha
of the graph will ensure that the number of light triangles
T^{\tau}_{L}
is a sufficiently good approximation of the number of triangles,
T
. For now, assume we are given access to an oracle
Heavy
to decide whether an edge is light or heavy. However, this criteria may cause triangles to be sampled with different probabilities, depending on the number of light edges it contains.

If we can design a way to assign each of the light triangles to one of its constituent light edges, we can sample all light triangles with equal probability. We define a valid weight function under which estimation of the sum of weight function over all edges gives us a good estimate of
T
.

To obtain this estimate, we obtain samples of triangles the light edges in
S
participates in. We assign each sampled triangle to one of its constituent edges, ensuring that the assignment can be extended to a valid assignment, and hence a valid weight function. Based on this assignment, we obtain an estimate of the corresponding weight function, which, given an appropriately fixed threshold
\tau
, will give an
\left({\varepsilon,\delta}\right)
estimate of
T
.

To design the oracle
Heavy
for a given edge
e
, we estimate the number of triangles each edge participates in. This estimation can be made using i.i.d. draws. Hence, the heavy edges, having high probability of obtaining a triangle, can be well-approximated using sufficiently small number of queries. For the light edges, observe that lower number of triangles, i.e. lower probability of obtaining triangles, allows for high approximation error. We then design a bucketing trick to exploit this trade-off to implement an efficient algorithm to decide whether an edge is heavy or light.

Lower Bound.

For our lower bound, we use the lower bound on number of samples required to solve the Popcount Thresholding Problem[
PTP
] presented in
(
Assadi and Nguyen, 2022
)
. We defer the details of this problem to Section
5
. To establish the lower bound, we show that for any value of arboricity
\alpha
, we can design a graph
G
with arboricity
\alpha
such that finding an
\left({\varepsilon,\delta}\right)
estimate
\widehat{T}
of the number of triangles
T
of the graph using
\Omega\left({{\frac{m\alpha\log{\left({1/\delta}\right)}}{\varepsilon^{2}T}}}\right)
queries would violate the lower bound of
PTP
.

3.2
Arboricity and its properties

As arboricity plays a crucial role in our work, we put together all the structural results that involve arboricity here. Let us restate the definition once more.

Definition 4

(Arboricity
(\alpha)
)
.

The arboricity of a graph
G=(V,E)
, denoted by
\alpha_{G}
, is the minimum number of spanning forests that

The arboricity of a graph can be seen as a measure of the density of the graph.

Lemma 5
.

(
(

)
)

Given a graph

with arboricity
\alpha

\left|{E}\right|=m
,
\sum\limits_{e\in E}\mbox{deg}_{e}=2m\alpha
.

The following lemma due to
(

(

to bound the number of triangles based on the number of edges

Lemma 6

(

)
)

Given a graph

with arboricity
\alpha

\left|{E}\right|=m
, the graph

Note that this upper bound is also tight, i.e., there exists graphs that contain

Lemma 7

Given i.i.d. random variables

\Pr[X_{i}=0]=(1-p)

\displaystyle\Pr[X\leq(1-\varepsilon)\Exp\left[{X}\right]]

This section describes the algorithm and its related concepts. Section

4.1
Weight Function

In this section, we formalize the ideas of heavy and light edges and weight function for the edges.

Heavy and light edges and triangles.

First, we define heavy and light edges and correspondingly, heavy and light triangles.

Definition 8

(
\tau
-heavy and
\tau
-light edges)
.

An edge
e\in E
is defined to be a
\tau
-heavy (resp.
\tau
-light) edge if it participates in more than
\tau
(resp.
\leq\tau
) triangles.

Definition 9

(
\tau
-heavy and
\tau
-light triangles)
.

A triangle
\sf t\in\Delta
is called a
\tau
-heavy triangle if all its three edges are
\tau
-heavy edges. A triangle that is not
\tau
-heavy is a
\tau
-light triangle.

We denote by
T^{\tau}_{L}
and
T^{\tau}_{H}

the number of
\tau
-light and
\tau
-heavy triangles in the graph
G
, respectively.
The following lemma bounds the number of
\tau
-heavy triangles in a graph.

Lemma 10

(Upper Bound on
T^{\tau}_{H}
)
.

Given a graph
G=(V,E)
with
T
triangles, the number of
\tau
-heavy triangles is at most
\frac{3T\alpha}{\tau}
.

Proof.

Note that the graph
G=(V,E)
has
T
triangles containing at most
3T
edges. By Definition
9
,
\tau
-heavy triangles have all three of their edges to be
\tau
-heavy edges, each participating in greater than or equal to
\tau
triangles. Hence, the number of
\tau
-heavy edges in
G
is at most
\frac{3T}{\tau}
.

Now consider the subgraph
H=(V_{H},E_{H})
of
G
induced by the
\tau
-heavy edges in

. We know,
E_{H}\leq\frac{3T}{\tau}
. Also,
\alpha_{H}\leq\alpha_{G}=\alpha
(see Section

). Hence, by Lemma

, we know that
H
contains at most
\frac{3T\alpha}{\tau}
triangles.
∎

The following corollary follows from Lemma
10
and the fact that
T=T^{\tau}_{L}+T^{\tau}_{H}
.

Corollary 11

(Lower Bound on
T^{\tau}_{L}
)
.

Given a graph
G=(V,E)
, there are at least
(1-\frac{3\alpha}{\tau})T

\tau
-light triangles.

Weight function for the edges.

Now, we define a weight function for the edges. As a triangle is incident to multiple edges, the objective of a weight function is to charge each of the light triangles to exactly one of its participating light edges. To ensure this, we do not charge any triangle to the heavy edges. Each light edge
e
can be charged by at most
T_{e}
triangles, i.e., all the triangles
e
participates in. To avoid over-counting, we ensure that the sum of the weight functions over all edges is equal to the number of light triangles,
T^{\tau}_{L}
.
\left({\sf t,e}\right)
denotes that the triangle
\sf t
is charged through the edge
e
.

Definition 12

(Triangle weight function
(w)
)
.

We define a function
{w}:{E}\rightarrow{\mathbb{N}}
to be a triangle weight function if it satisfies the following two conditions:

\begin{array}[]{llll}\mbox{Condition (1)}:&w(e)&\leq&\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}T_{e}&\text{if $e$ is a light edge}\\
0&\text{if $e$ is a heavy edge}\end{array}\right.\\
\mbox{Condition (2)}:&\sum\limits_{e\in E}w(e)&=&T^{\tau}_{L}\end{array}

Observe that there are multiple such weight functions (e.g., a triangle with 3 light edges can be assigned to any one of these 3 edges). Henceforth, we denote the set consisting of valid triangle weight functions by
\mathcal{W}
. We now state a property of valid weight functions. Note that this property is true for any
w\in\mathcal{W}
.

Lemma 13
.

Consider any triangle weight function
w\in\mathcal{W}
. If we select an edge
e\in E
uniformly at random, then the expected value of
w(e)
is
\frac{T^{\tau}_{L}}{m}
, i.e.,
\Exp_{e\sim\mathrm{Unif}(E)}w(e)=\frac{T^{\tau}_{L}}{m}
.

Proof.

Given that the edges have been chosen uniformly at random and the condition of the triangle weight function, we have:

\Exp_{e\sim\mathrm{Unif}(E)}w(e)~~~~~=~~~~~\sum_{e\in\mathrm{Unif}(E)}\frac{1}{m}w(e)~~~~~=~~~~~\frac{1}{m}\sum_{e\in E}w(e)~~~~~=~~~~~\frac{T^{\tau}_{L}}{m}

∎

4.2
Oracle Based Algorithm

Our algorithm, due to its usage of the
triangle weight function
, requires knowledge of whether an edge is
heavy
or
light
. The problem of deciding whether an edge is heavy is non-trivial as it directly relates to number of triangles the edge participates in. In this section, we assume black-box access to an oracle

that helps us to determine whether an edge is heavy or not.

\displaystyle\texttt{HeavyOracle}(e,\alpha,\varepsilon)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}1&\text{if edge $e$ is a $\frac{h\alpha}{\varepsilon}$-heavy edge, i.e., $\tau=\frac{h\alpha}{\varepsilon}$}\\
0&\text{if edge $e$ is a $\frac{l\alpha}{\varepsilon}$-light edge, i.e., $\tau=\frac{l\alpha}{\varepsilon}$}\end{cases}

Here

and

are constants to be determined later. We will discuss an efficient

Let us first consider the case where we are given an oracle that given an edge

. Given, we can sample edges uniformly at random through the

query, we can compute

\widehat{w}(e)
. Recall the fact that there are many valid weight functions, each being characterized by every light triangle being charged to a unique edge. Our algorithm will work if the empirical weight function

estimates any one of these weight functions. We achieve this by ensuring that a triangle is not sampled through more than one edge (see Line
18
of Algorithm
1
). Thus, the assignments that we consider can be extended to a valid weight function

, and our algorithm can be thought of as estimating this weight function through

. We develop our initial algorithm ( Algorithm

satisfying the following assumption:

In Algorithm

\displaystyle\exp{\left({-\frac{2^{2k-2}r\Exp\left[{Y}\right]}{2+2^{k-1}}}\right)}

\displaystyle\exp{\left({-\frac{2^{2k}r\Exp\left[{Y}\right]}{2^{k+3}}}\right)}

\displaystyle\exp{\left({-\frac{r\alpha}{16\varepsilon\mbox{deg}_{e}}}\right)}

\displaystyle\left(\Exp\left[{Y}\right]>\frac{\alpha}{2^{k+1}\varepsilon\mbox{deg}_{e}}\right)

\displaystyle~\delta

\displaystyle\left(r=\frac{16\varepsilon\mbox{deg}_{e}}{\alpha}\log\left({\frac{1}{\delta}}\right)\right)

\widehat{T}\geq(1-\varepsilon)T
with probability
1-\frac{\delta}{2}
. Thus, we have
\widetilde{T}\geq\left({1-\varepsilon^{2}}\right)k\alpha^{*}
with probability
1-\frac{\delta}{2}
.

Case II
(
\left\|{x}\right\|_{1}<\left({1-\gamma}\right)k
): Our construction ensures that the number of triangles in
{G_{x}}
is
T=\left\|{x}\right\|_{1}\alpha^{*}<\left({1-\gamma}\right)k\alpha^{*}=\left({1-\varepsilon}\right)k\alpha^{*}
. Additionally, by our assumption on
\mathcal{A}_{T}
, it outputs an estimate
\widehat{T}\leq(1+\varepsilon)T
with probability
1-\frac{\delta}{2}
. Thus, we have
\widetilde{T}\leq\left({1-\varepsilon^{2}}\right)k\alpha^{*}
with probability
1-\frac{\delta}{2}
.

Now we state how to simulate the required queries:

•

\texttt{Degree}{\left({v}\right)}
:
For a vertex
v\in A\cup B\cup A^{\prime}\cup B^{\prime}
, return
\alpha^{*}
, and for a vertex
v\in S
, return
\frac{2M}{\alpha^{*}}
. Hence, for
Degree
queries, no queries are made to the string.

•

\texttt{Neighbour}{\left({v,i}\right)}
:
For a vertex
v\in A\cup B\cup A^{\prime}\cup B^{\prime}
, w.l.o.g assume
v
to be
a_{j}\in A
. Return
a^{\prime}_{i+j}
if
x_{i,j}=0
, and
b_{i+j}
otherwise. For a vertex
v\in S
, if
i\leq\frac{m}{\alpha^{*}}
, return
a_{i}
, else return
b_{i=\frac{m}{\alpha^{*}}}
. Hence, for
Neighbour
queries, a single query is made to the string.

•

Edge
\left({u,v}\right)
:
Given a vertex pair
\left({v,u}\right)
, there can be 3 cases: (a) If the vertex
v\in A\cup B
, w.l.o.g assume
v
to be
a_{i}\in A
, if
u\in S
, return
1
, else if
u=b_{j}\in B
, return
1
if
x_{i,j-i}=1
, else if
u=a^{\prime}_{j}\in A^{\prime}
, return
1
if
x_{i,j-i}=0
, else return
0
. (b) If a vertex
v\in A^{\prime}\cup B^{\prime}
, w.l.o.g assume the
v
to be
a^{\prime}_{i}\in A^{\prime}
, if
u=a_{j}\in A
, return
1
if
x_{j,i-j}=0
, else if
u=b^{\prime}_{j}\in B^{\prime}
, return
1
if
x_{j,i-j}=1
, else return
0
. If
v\in S
, return
1
if
u\in A\cup B
, else return
0
. (c) If a vertex
v\in S
, return
1
if
u\in A\cup B
, return
0
otherwise. Hence, for
Edge
queries, a single query is made to the string.

•

RandomEdge
:
Sample a vertex
v
with probability
\frac{\mbox{deg}_{v}}{4M}
. Choose
i\in\mbox{deg}_{\left({v}\right)}
uniformly at random, query
u=\texttt{Neighbour}{\left({v,i}\right)}
and return
\left({v,u}\right)
. Hence, for
RandomEdge
queries, a single query is made to the string for the
Neighbour
query.

∎

The following corollary is a direct implication from Theorem
30
due to the fact that we consider a weaker model (without access to
RandomEdge
queries) compared to Theorem
30
.

Corollary 31

(Lower Bound for Local Queries)
.

Any algorithm that solves triangle estimation problem with
\varepsilon\leq\frac{1}{4}
using
Degree
,
Neighbour
, and
Edge
requires
\Omega\left({\frac{m\alpha\log\left({1/\delta}\right)}{\varepsilon^{2}T}}\right)
queries.

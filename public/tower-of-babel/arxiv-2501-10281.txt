Abstract
We study the possible existence of bound states of three Ω b ​ b ​ b \Omega_{bbb} baryons. We consider only S S wave interactions and we start from recent lattice QCD results which give a strongly attractive potential between two Ω b ​ b ​ b \Omega_{bbb} baryons in the S 0 1 {}^{1}S_{0} channel. We analyze different scenarios. At baryonic level, the Ω b ​ b ​ b ​ Ω b ​ b ​ b \Omega_{bbb}\Omega_{bbb} interaction could be understood to be basically spin-independent, so that the two contributing channels, S 0 1 {}^{1}S_{0} and S 2 5 {}^{5}S_{2} , would have a very similar interaction. This baryonic analysis leads to the existence of bound states in the three-body system. At the quark level, repulsive effects would appear in the S 2 5 {}^{5}S_{2} channel, making it more repulsive than the S 0 1 {}^{1}S_{0} channel. We study the effect of such repulsion in terms of its range.

Single channel Faddeev Problem

The Faddeev equations for three identical particles are

T=2t_{i}G_{0}T\,\,,

(1)

where

is the

t_{i}=V+VG_{0}t_{i}\,\,,

(2)

where

the two-body interaction in the
{}^{1}S_{0}
channel and
G_{0}
is
the propagator for three free particles.

We use the complete set of basis states
\left|i\right\rangle
,

\left|i\right\rangle=\left|p_{i}q_{i}((s_{j},s_{k})S_{i},s_{i})J\right\rangle\,\,,

(3)

with
p_{i}
and
q_{i}
the standard Jacobi momenta,
s_{i}
,
s_{j}
, and
s_{k}
the
spins of the three particles,
S_{i}
the total spin of the pair
jk
, and
J

the total spin of the three-body system. In this basis the Faddeev
equation (
1

\left\langle i\right|T\left|\phi_{0}\right\rangle=2\left\langle i\right|t_{i}\left|i^{\prime}\right\rangle\left\langle i^{\prime}\right|\left.j\right\rangle G_{0}\left\rangle j\right|T\left|\phi_{0}\right\rangle\,\,,

(4)

where the explicit form of the integral equation in momentum space is given
in the Appendix.

The recoupling coefficient,

\left\langle i^{\prime}\right|\left.j\right\rangle=\left\langle p_{i}^{\prime}q_{i}^{\prime}\right|\left.p_{j}q_{j}\right\rangle\left\langle((s_{j},s_{k})S_{i},s_{i})J\right|\left.((s_{k},s_{i})S_{j},s_{j})J\right\rangle\,\,,

(5)

is of great interest. The space part
\left\langle p_{i}^{\prime}q_{i}^{\prime}\right|\left.p_{j}q_{j}\right\rangle

is positive definite

; however, the spin part is

\left\langle((s_{j},s_{k})S_{i},s_{i})J\right|\left.((s_{k},s_{i})S_{j},s_{j})J\right\rangle=(-)^{S_{j}+s_{j}-J}\sqrt{(2S_{i}+1)(2S_{j}+1)}W(s_{j}s_{k}Js_{i};S_{i}S_{j})\,\,.

(6)

Since
S_{i}=S_{j}=0
and
s_{i}=s_{j}=s_{k}=J=3/2

\left\langle((s_{j},s_{k})S_{i},s_{i})J\right|\left.((s_{k},s_{i})S_{j},s_{j})J\right\rangle=(-)^{2s_{j}}\frac{1}{2s_{j}+1}=-\frac{1}{4}\,\,,

(7)

which is a negative number, so that it effectively changes the nature of the
two-body interaction from attractive to repulsive such that no bound state can
be obtained in a one-channel calculation.

III
Baryonic level
{}^{5}S_{2}

\Omega\Omega
interaction

A two-body interaction acting in
S
-waves contains only central and spin-spin
terms, since terms like spin-orbit, tensor, etc., act only for nonzero orbital
angular momentum.

The phenomenological description of the spectra of mesons and baryons
in a nonrelativistic approach is based in a two-body
potential between quarks
BHAD
;
BRAC
. In Ref.
BHAD

such potential was taken to be,

V(r)=-\frac{\kappa}{r}+\lambda r-\Lambda+\frac{\kappa}{m_{i}m_{j}}\frac{exp^{-r/r_{0}}}{rr_{0}}\vec{\sigma}_{i}\cdot\vec{\sigma}_{j}

(9)

and similarly in Ref.
BRAC
. The four terms in the r.h.s. of
Eq. (
9
) are, respectively, the Coulomb term, the linear confinement
term, the constant term, and the spin-spin term. This interaction is able to
reproduce reasonably well the masses and other properties of all the existing
mesons and baryons
BRAC
.

The Yukawa function in the spin-spin term is an extended delta function which
becomes a delta function if
r_{0}\to 0
. This form of the spin-spin interaction
is suggested by the non-relativistic reduction of the
one-gluon-exchange diagram
ISGU
,

H=\frac{2\alpha_{s}}{3m_{i}m_{j}}\frac{8\pi}{3}\delta^{3}(\vec{r})\vec{S}_{i}\cdot\vec{S}_{j},

(10)

where
m_{i}
and
\vec{S}_{i}
are the mass and spin operator of particle
i
. As
one can see in Eqs. (
9
) and (
10
), the spin-spin term is
inversely proportional to
m_{i}m_{j}
so that in the case of baryons with
identical quarks it goes as 1/
m_{i}^{2}
. Thus, using the masses for the light
quarks
n
(
\approx 0.3
GeV) and for the heavy quark
b
(
\approx 5
GeV)
from Refs.
BHAD
;
BRAC
, one gets that the spin-spin term in the case of
the
bb
interaction is about 250 times smaller that that of the
nn
interaction
and therefore it is negligible. This means that the interaction between two

b
quarks is basically independent of the spin.

Let us now consider the spin-spin interaction in the case of two
\Omega_{bbb}

baryons. In a quark model description, the baryon-baryon
interaction can be deduced from the quark-quark interaction following a
well-known procedure
SHIM
;
VAGA

restricted to
S

\Omega_{bbb}\Omega_{bbb}

MATH

S_{i}=2

\Delta N

Oky81

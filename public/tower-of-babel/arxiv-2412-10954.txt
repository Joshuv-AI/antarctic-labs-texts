Structured position-momentum entangled two-photon fields Preprint: APS/123-QED
Radhika Prasad, Sanjana Wanare, Suman Karan, Mritunjay K. Joshi, Abhinandan Bhattacharjee, Anand K. Jha

Abstract
Structured optical fields have led to several ground-breaking techniques in classical imaging and microscopy. At the same time, in the quantum domain, position-momentum entangled photon fields have been shown to have several unique features that can lead to beyond-classical imaging and microscopy capabilities. Therefore, it is natural to expect that position-momentum entangled two-photon fields that are structured can push the boundaries of quantum imaging and microscopy even further beyond. Nonetheless, the existing experimental schemes are able to produce either structured two-photon fields without position-momentum entanglement, or position-momentum entangled two-photon fields without structures. In this article, by manipulating the phase-matching condition of the spontaneous parametric down-conversion process, we report experimental generation of two-photon fields with various structures in their spatial correlations. We experimentally measure the minimum bound on the entanglement of formation and thereby verify the position-momentum entanglement of the structured two-photon field. We expect this work to have important implications for quantum technologies related to imaging and sensing.

However, given the ground-breaking capabilities provided by structured light fields for classical imaging, it is natural to expect that the position-momentum entangled two-photon fields that are structured can push the boundaries of quantum imaging and microscopy even further beyond.

The current methods of generating two-photon fields correlated in position and momentum variables can be divided into two categories.
The first category consists of methods that generate two-photon position-momentum entanglement field but with no structure
Berta et al. 2010
;
Howell et al. 2004
;
Bhattacharjee et al. 2022b
;
Bhattacharjee et al. 2022a

;

Karan et al. 2020

Berta et al. 2010
;
Howell et al. 2004
;
Bhattacharjee et al. 2022b

On the other hand, the phase matching plays the dominant role in shaping the correlation function in the near field where the SPDC photons do remain entangled in the position-momentum degree of freedom. Therefore, in this article, by manipulating the phase matching function, we report experimental generation of structured position-momentum entangled fields in the near-field of the SPDC crystal.

Theory

II.1
Two-photon probability distribution functions

Figure 3:
Schematic of the experimental setup. UV pump: Ultraviolet laser; DM: Dichroic mirror to block UV pump of 355 nm; IF: Interference filter of 710 nm with 10 nm bandwidth; EMCCD: Electron Multiplying Charge-Coupled Device camera

SPDC is a nonlinear optical process, in which a pump photon of higher frequency gets down-converted into two lower-frequency photons called the signal and idler. Denoting pump, signal, and idler by subscripts
p
,
s
, and
i
, respectively, we write the two-photon state
\left|\Psi\right\rangle
produced by the SPDC crystal placed at
z=0
as
Karan et al. 2020
:

\displaystyle\!\!\left|\Psi\right\rangle=\iint d^{2}\bm{q}_{s}d^{2}\bm{q}_{i}V\left(\bm{q}_{s}+\bm{q}_{i}\right)\Phi\left(\bm{q}_{s},\bm{q}_{i}\right)\left|\bm{q}_{s}\right\rangle\left|\bm{q}_{i}\right\rangle.

(1)

Here
\bm{q}_{s}
and
\bm{q}_{i}
are the transverse momenta of signal and idler photons,
V\left(\bm{q}_{s}+\bm{q}_{i}\right)
is the pump field amplitude, and
\Phi\left(\bm{q}_{s},\bm{q}_{i}\right)
is the phase-matching function. We take pump to be a Gaussian beam, that is,
V(\bm{q}_{p})=V_{0}\exp(-\left|\bm{q}_{p}\right|^{2}w_{0}^{2}/4)
where
w_{0}
is the beam waist and
\bm{q}_{p}=\bm{q}_{s}+\bm{q}_{i}
. Using the two-photon state in Eq.
1
and the derivation given in
Karan et al. 2020
, we obtain the following expression for the two-photon probability distribution function
P_{\rm pos}(\bm{\rho}_{s},\bm{\rho}_{i};z)
in the transverse position space at a distance
z
away from the crystal:

P_{\rm pos}(\bm{\rho}_{s},\bm{\rho}_{i};z)=\Bigg|\iint d^{2}\bm{q}_{s}d^{2}\bm{q}_{i}V\left(\bm{q}_{s}+\bm{q}_{i}\right)\Phi\left(\bm{q}_{s},\bm{q}_{i}\right)\\
\times\left.\exp\left[i\left(\bm{q}_{s}\cdot\bm{\rho}_{s}+\bm{q}_{i}\cdot\bm{\rho}_{i}-\frac{\left|\bm{q}_{s}\right|^{2}z}{2k_{s}}-\frac{\left|\bm{q}_{i}\right|^{2}z}{2k_{i}}\right)\right]\right|^{2}.

(2)

Here
P_{\rm pos}(\bm{\rho}_{s},\bm{\rho}_{i};z)
is the probability of detecting in coincidence the signal and idler photons at
\bm{\rho}_{s}
and
\bm{\rho}_{i}
, respectively. The two-photon conditional probability distribution function
P_{\rm pos}(\bm{\rho}_{s}|\bm{\rho}_{i0};z)
of detecting the signal photon, given that the idler photon is detected at
\bm{\rho}_{i0}
, is obtained by setting
\bm{\rho}_{i}=\bm{\rho}_{i0}
in Eq.
2
, that is,

\displaystyle P_{\rm pos}(\bm{\rho}_{s}|\bm{\rho}_{i0};z)=P_{\rm pos}(\bm{\rho}_{s},\bm{\rho}_{i}=\bm{\rho}_{i0};z).

(3)

For plotting purposes, we work with averaged two-photon probability distribution function
P_{\rm pos}^{(\rm av)}(x_{s},x_{i};z)
and the two-photon conditional probability distribution function
P_{\rm pos}(x_{s},y_{s}|0,0;z)
, defined as:

\displaystyle P_{\rm pos}^{(\rm av)}(x_{s},x_{i};z)=\iint P_{\rm pos}(x_{s},y_{s},x_{i},y_{i};z)dy_{s}dy_{i},

(4)

\displaystyle P_{\rm pos}(x_{s},y_{s}|0,0;z)=P_{\rm pos}(x_{s},y_{s},x_{i}=0,y_{i}=0;z).

(5)

Next, using Eq. (
1
), we obtain the two-photon probability distribution function in the transverse momentum basis to be
P_{\rm mom}({\bm{q}}_{s},{\bm{q}}_{i};z)=|\langle\bm{q}_{s}|\langle\bm{q}_{i}|\Psi\rangle|^{2}=|V\left(\bm{q}_{s}+\bm{q}_{i}\right)\Phi\left(\bm{q}_{s},\bm{q}_{i}\right)|^{2}
.
Therefore, two-photon conditional probability distribution function
P_{\rm mom}({\bm{q}}_{s},{\bm{q}}_{i0};z)
of detecting the signal photon, given that the idler photon is detected with momentum
{\bm{q}}_{i0}
can be written as
P_{\rm mom}({\bm{q}}_{s}|{\bm{q}}_{i0};z)=P_{\rm mom}({\bm{q}}_{s},{\bm{q}}_{i}={\bm{q}}_{i0};z)
. We note that the correlation in the momentum basis remains independent of
z
, which is a consequence of the fact that the momentum remains conserved in the SPDC. Finally, we write the averaged two-photon joint probability distribution function as:

\displaystyle P_{\rm mom}^{(\rm av)}(q_{xs},q_{xi};z)=\iint P_{\rm mom}(q_{xs},q_{ys},q_{xi},q_{yi};z)dq_{ys}dq_{yi}.

(6)

II.2
Structuring correlations by manipulating the phase-matching function

It is knows that the SPDC photons lose their entanglement in the position-momentum degree of freedom in the far-field regions of the nonlinear crystal
Bhattacharjee et al. 2022b
;
Howell et al. 2004
. Therefore, we work in the near-field regions and structure the correlation function by manipulating the phase matching function, which is given by
\Phi\left(\bm{q}_{s},\bm{q}_{i}\right)=\int_{0}^{L}\exp(i\Delta k_{z}z)dz

Karan et al. 2020
. Here,
L
is the thickness of the crystal and
\Delta k_{z}
is called the phase-mismatch parameter and is given by
\Delta k_{z}=k_{sz}+k_{iz}-k_{pz}
, where
k_{sz}
,
k_{iz}
and
k_{pz}
represent the longitudinal wave vectors for signal, idler and pump fields, respectively, and are expressed as
Karan et al. 2020
:

\displaystyle k_{pz}

\displaystyle=-\alpha_{p}q_{px}+\eta_{p}K_{p0}-\frac{\left[\beta_{p}^{2}q^{2}_{px}+\gamma_{p}^{2}q^{2}_{py}\right]}{2\eta_{p}K_{p0}},

\displaystyle k_{sz}

\displaystyle=n_{so}K_{s0}-\frac{1}{2n_{so}K_{s0}}(q_{sx}^{2}+q_{sy}^{2}),

\displaystyle k_{iz}

\displaystyle=n_{io}K_{i0}-\frac{1}{2n_{io}K_{i0}}(q_{ix}^{2}+q_{iy}^{2}),

where
K_{j0}=\frac{2\pi}{\lambda_{j}}
with
\lambda_{j}
being the wavelength and
n_{jo}
and
n_{je}
being the refractive index for ordinary and extraordinary polarizations. The quantities
\alpha_{p}
,
\beta_{p}
,
\gamma_{p}
and
\eta_{p}
are given by

\displaystyle\alpha_{p}=\frac{(n^{2}_{po}-n^{2}_{pe})\sin\theta_{p}\cos\theta_{p}}{n^{2}_{po}\sin^{2}\theta_{p}+n^{2}_{pe}\cos^{2}\theta_{p}},

\displaystyle\beta_{p}=\frac{n_{po}n_{pe}}{n^{2}_{po}\sin^{2}\theta_{p}+n^{2}_{pe}\cos^{2}\theta_{p}},

\displaystyle\gamma_{p}=\frac{n_{po}}{\sqrt{n^{2}_{po}\sin^{2}\theta_{p}+n^{2}_{pe}\cos^{2}\theta_{p}}},

\displaystyle\eta_{p}=\frac{n_{po}n_{pe}}{\sqrt{n^{2}_{po}\sin^{2}\theta_{p}+n^{2}_{pe}\cos^{2}\theta_{p}}}.

Here,
\theta_{p}
is called the phase-matching angle, which is the angle between the pump propagation direction and the optic axis of the nonlinear crystal. We investigate two different methods of manipulating the phase-matching function and thus of structuring the correlations. The first method is by changing the phase-matching angle
\theta_{p}
[see Fig
1
(a)]. In this case,
\Phi\left(\bm{q}_{s},\bm{q}_{i}\right)
can be written as:

\displaystyle\Phi\left(\bm{q}_{s},\bm{q}_{i}\right)=\sinc\left(\Delta k_{z}\frac{L}{2}\right)\exp\left(i\Delta k_{z}\frac{L}{2}\right).

(7)

The second method is by having SPDC with two nonlinear crystals separated by distance
d
[see Fig
1
(b)]. In this case, the generated two-photon state is a superposition of the two-photon states produced by the two crystals. The
\Phi\left(\bm{q}_{s},\bm{q}_{i}\right)
in this case can be shown to be

\displaystyle\Phi\left(\bm{q}_{s},\bm{q}_{i}\right)=\sinc\left(\Delta k_{z}\frac{L}{2}\right)\cos\left(\Delta k_{z}\frac{L+d}{2}\right).

(8)

at various
\theta_{p}
and at
z=5

Position-momentum entanglement

The position-momentum entanglement of the SPDC photons can be certified either through the violation of the conditional Heisenberg uncertainty relation
Bhattacharjee et al. 2022b
;
Howell et al. 2004
or by demonstrating a positive value for the minimum of entanglement of formation
E_{f}^{(\rm min)}

Prevedel et al. 2011
;
Schneeloch et al. 2019a
. For violating the conditional Heisenberg uncertainty relation, one needs to calculate the conditional uncertainties, which can be defined when the two-photon probability distribution functions are singly-peaked and have well-defined widths. However, in our present work, the concerned functions have multiple peaks, which makes it difficult to define the width. Therefore, for certifying entanglement in our work, we use the minimum of entanglement of formation
E_{f}^{(\rm min)}

Prevedel et al. 2011
;
Schneeloch et al. 2019a
, which is given by
Berta et al. 2010

E_{f}^{(\rm min)}=2\log_{2}(M)-H(\bm{X}_{s}|\bm{X}_{i})-H(\bm{K}_{s}|\bm{K}_{i}).

(9)

Here
M
is the dimensionality of the Hilbert space, and (
\bm{X}
) and (
\bm{K}
) are the discrete position and transverse-momentum basis vectors corresponding to the continuous basis vectors
\bm{\rho}
and
\bm{q}
, respectively
Schneeloch and Howland 2018
.
H(\bm{X}_{s}|\bm{X}_{i})
is the conditional von Neumann entropies in the position basis and can be expressed as:

\displaystyle H\left(\bm{X}_{s}|\bm{X_{i}}\right)=H\left(\bm{X}_{s},\bm{X}_{i}\right)-H\left(\bm{X}_{i}\right),

where

\displaystyle H\left(\bm{X}_{s},\bm{X}_{i}\right)=-\sum_{\bm{X}_{s}}\sum_{\bm{X}_{s}}P_{\rm pos}\left(\bm{X}_{s},\bm{X}_{i};z\right)\ln_{2}P_{\rm pos}\left(\bm{X}_{s},\bm{X}_{i};z\right)

\displaystyle H\left(\bm{X}_{i}\right)=-\sum_{\bm{X}_{i}}(\sum_{\bm{X}_{s}}P_{\rm pos}\left(\bm{X}_{s},\bm{X}_{i};z\right))

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\times\ln_{2}(\sum_{\bm{X}_{s}}P_{\rm pos}\left(\bm{X}_{s},\bm{X}_{i};z\right)).

Here,
P_{\rm pos}\left(\bm{X}_{s},\bm{X}_{i};z\right))
is the discrete probability distribution function corresponding to the continuous two-photon probability distribution function
P_{\rm pos}(\bm{\rho}_{s},\bm{\rho}_{i};z)
. We note that, in an experiment situation,
P_{\rm pos}\left(\bm{X}_{s},\bm{X}_{i};z\right))
is measured using a multi-pixel camera. The camera pixels provide the discrete space, and the value of
P_{\rm pos}(\bm{\rho}_{s},\bm{\rho}_{i};z)
at a pair of pixels located at
\bm{X}_{s}
and
\bm{X}_{i}
, averaged over the pixel area, is taken as
P_{\rm pos}\left(\bm{X}_{s},\bm{X}_{i};z\right))
. In a similar manner, we can write the conditional von Neumann entropy
H(\bm{K}_{s}|\bm{K}_{i})
in the momentum basis and express that in terms of the discrete probability distribution function
P_{\rm mom}\left(\bm{K}_{s},\bm{K}_{i};z\right))
. A positive value of
E_{f}^{(\rm min)}
implies entanglement, and we use it as the criterion for verifying entanglement
Prevedel et al. 2011
;
Schneeloch et al. 2019a
.

IV
Conclusion

In this article, we have reported experimental generation of two-photon fields with various structures in their spatial correlations. We have demonstrated that such fields can be produced in the near-field region of the SPDC crystal by manipulating the phase-matching condition through either changing the phase-matching angle or having multiple SPDC crystals. We have confirmed the entanglement of our structured fields through entanglement of formation. This work can have important implications for quantum imaging and microscopy.

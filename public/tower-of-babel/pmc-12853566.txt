Spontaneous Intracerebral Hemorrhage in young patients from a Comprehensive Stroke Care Unit in MENA region, Hospital Based Study
Authors: Islam El-Malky, Hosam Alieldeen Abdelmageed, Hussein Bahey El-Deen, Mahmoud Abdelhafiz
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
IntroductionFew studies have been reported on ICH in young age patients at Middle East and North Africa region (MENA). It is thought that epidemiological, radiological characteristics, and neurological outcomes differ in young age patients from those of the elderly. Our study’s aim is detection of the difference between them as regard clinic-radiological characters, functional outcome, and its predictors.MethodsAll patients with spontaneous ICH were divided into two groups, young adults (≤ 50 years) and old adults group (> 50 years). All clinic-radiological data were collected. The patients were classified into four categories (probable CAA, Hypertensive, Vascular and Undetermined). One-month mortality and functional outcome (using mRS) were determined. Multivariate logistic regression was performed to determine the predictors of favorable functional outcome.ResultsProspective study was conducted on 300 patients, 91 patients of them were young (30.3%). Hypertension, Diabetes mellitus, Hyperlipidemia, previous CVS, higher NIHSS, lower GCS and previous medications of antiplatelet or anticoagulant were more common in old age group with statistically significant difference (P value: <0.001, < 0.001, < 0.001, 0.006, < 0.001, < 0.001 and < 0.001, respectively). Vascular cause and undetermined cause were more in young age. Young group had better outcome as regard functional outcome and one-month mortality (74.7% versus 35.9% with p value: <0.001, 8.8% versus 28.7% with p value: <0.001, respectively). GCS and NIHSS are predictors of functional outcome.ConclusionSpontaneous ICH in young adults has different demographic, clinical and radiological findings with better One-month mortality and favorable functional outcome. GCS and NIHSS are predictors of functional outcome.

Introduction

Spontaneous intracerebral hemorrhage (ICH) represents nearly 30% of all strokes worldwide and has one-month fatality rate of up to 40% [1]. Furthermore, only 10–25% of survivors achieve functional independence and recurrence of ICH is common between them [2]. Wang et al. reported based on the 2009 consensus document issued by the American Heart Association (AHA)/American Stroke Association Stroke Council and conjunction with the Global Burden of Disease Study (GBD) database [3, 4], that stroke burden has shown an upward rising in recent years in both age groups, which is greater in the young adults’ population than in the older one, especially in developing regions. That trend might be due to increasing prevalence of traditional vascular risk factors such as hypertension, diabetes, hypercholesterolemia, smoking and obesity in the younger population [5]. Also, recently many new risk factors appeared such as illicit and recreational drug use and increased mental and psychological stress [6]. Young age people have a longer life expectancy than older age people, so the burden of care after ICH is greater for younger people. ICH survivors in this age group may have problems such as cognitive impairments, loss of independence, and difficulties in building education or career aspirations. Noticeably, young age people pose more socially productive missions than older people, so stroke in younger people might be have a greater effect on the development of economy [7].

Few studies have been reported on ICH in young age patients specially in Middle East and North Africa region (MENA). It is thought that epidemiological characteristics, pathogenesis, risk factors, and neurological outcomes in young patients with spontaneous ICH are different from those of elderly, but it is hard to know accurately because many researches were mostly performed in older population. Nevertheless, discovery of the causes of spontaneous ICH in young population is often challenging. In a number of studies that have been done to determine the etiology of ICH in young adults, up to 41% was reported to have an undetermined cause [8].

Good understanding of the causes and modifiable risk factors of spontaneous ICH occurrence in young patients might be so important to put strategies for prevention of ICH recurrence in survivors, or even rebreeding in the same attack. Our aim of the study is detection of the difference between old and young adults’ patients with spontaneous ICH as regard causes, epidemiological characters and functional outcome.

Methods

Prospective study was conducted from July 2024 to July 2025 in our comprehensive Stroke Care Unit, Neurology Department. The study was approved by the university under the ethical committee number (SVU/MED/NAP020/24/8/913). The study follows Helsinki declaration and written consent from the patients or their relatives were obtained. We collected all intracerebral hemorrhage (parenchymal, intraventricular and subarachnoid hemorrhage) patients in our stroke care unit at a tertiary Centre. We included all patients over 18 years old. We excluded cases with primary subdural/epidural hematoma, traumatic ICH or hemorrhage due to cancer (nonvascular origin), transformation of cerebral infarction (with or without thrombolysis), coagulopathy due to hepatic, renal or hematological diseases, where mechanism of bleeding, prognosis and treatment were different. We excluded patients with incomplete data or failed to be followed up (11 and 13 patients, respectively).

Clinical assessment

Demographic data such as age, sex, waist to hip ratio, family history of ICH, medications (antiplatelet and or anticoagulants) and smoking were obtained. Comorbidities such as hypertension (HTN), diabetes mellitus (DM), Hyperlipidemia, and previous stroke (ischemic or hemorrhagic) were reported. The premorbid mRS before the recent attack was determined before the recent attack. Forty-two patients have premorbid m RS ≥ 3 in the old group, while one patient in the young group. The patient was considered hypertensive if BP >140/90 mmHg twice before the stroke or patient currently under antihypertensive treatment [9]. The patients might be first diagnosed hypertensive if continue raised two weeks after admission and discharged on antihypertensive. The patient was considered diabetic if glycaemia ≥ 126 mg/dl pre-prandial on at least 2 examinations, ≥ 200 mg/dl post-prandial, HbA1c ≥ 6.5% or currently under hypoglycemic treatment [10]. History of medication with antiplatelet and or anticoagulants was recorded. National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale (NIHSS) and Glasgow Coma Scale (GCS) scores at admission, complications such as re-bleeding, infection (aspiration pneumonia or skin infection), seizures, deterioration of conscious level and electrolyte disturbances. We divided patients into two groups, young age group (≤ 50 years old) and old age one (>50 years old).

Radiological assessment

CT brain scan (within one hour) was performed at admission using a 64-slice CT scanner at the radiology Department. All CT scans were done in supine position. CT slice thickness was 0.5 mm. Assessment of the hematoma included its site (basal or lobar), regularity, presence of leukoariosis (White matter lesions WMLs), size, mass effect, presence of multiple hemorrhages, hydrocephalus, and presence of Intraventricular or subarachnoid extension of hemorrhage. The volume of hematoma (V) on CT was measured according to ABC/2 method, V = longitudinal (A) × sagittal (B) × coronal (C)/2 [11], Major longitudinal and sagittal diameter represent the largest perpendicular diameters through the hyper dense area on the CT scan in the axial plane, and coronal diameter represents the thickness of the hematoma. This measurement was performed by two seniors neuroradiologists blindly, in addition to third opinion if there is a difference between them. Hematoma expansion (HE) was determined by an absolute HV increase ≥ 12.5 mL or a proportional increase ≥ 33% at follow up CT Scan 3–72 h or at deterioration of conscious level [12].

MRI brain (1.5 Tesla) with T1, T2, T2* and MRA was performed to detect vascular causes of hemorrhage and micro bleeds and its site. Digital Subtraction Angiography (DSA) was performed in cases with suspicion of vascular malformation and noninvasive studies didn’t show a clear cause, especially in young normotensive patients. DSA was repeated one month later, if the first one revealed nothing.

Simplified Edinburgh Criteria and Boston Criteria version 1 were used to determine cases’ probability of Cerebral Amyloid Angiopathy (CAA) [13, 14]. We classified the patients according to the clinical and radiological data to four categories, hypertensive, probable CCA, vascular, undetermined. Undetermined category included hemorrhagic cases without cause or cases with multiple causes. ICH score to predict 30 days’ mortality was recorded in each patient [15]. ICH score has five predictive factors (ICH volume, infratentorial location of ICH, low score on Glasgow Coma Scale (GCS), older age [more than 80 years], and intraventricular extension of the hemorrhage.). The ICH score (0–6) was calculated as reported by Hemphill et al. [15]. One point was given for age >80 years, one point for ICH volume >30 ml, one point for infratentorial origin, one point for intraventricular extension of ICH, one point for a GCS of 5–12, and two points for a GCS of 3–4.

Treatment and outcome measurement

All patients were admitted to an exclusive stroke care unit and antihypertensive (Amlodipine, Labetalol) and antiedema measures (Mannitol 20%) were given according to patient condition [16]. Patients who had hematoma volume >30 or 10 cc in the supratentorial or infratentorial region, respectively, or GCS score < 8 underwent surgery at admission. Among them, craniotomy or craniectomy was considered for hematoma evacuation when at least one pupil was dilated or loss of reflex. ICH catheter insertion was done when the pupil was not dilated and the pupil reflex was normal. Conservative treatment was applied in patients with hematoma volume < 30 or 10 cc in the supratentorial or infratentorial regions, respectively, or with brainstem hemorrhage. In case of progressive hydrocephalus due to intraventricular expansion with associated DCL, CSF drainage was performed. Craniectomy was done if there was intractable brain swelling or bulging after evacuation of the hematoma [16]. One-month mortality and Three months mRS (modified Rankin Scale) for outcome assessment were recorded. Favorable outcome was considered if mRS was 0 to 2. In cases where the patient could not visit the stroke clinic, a telephonic communication was performed to record the mRS. This had been occurred in few cases (11 cases).

Statistical analysis

The sample size for the study was calculated using the following formula: n = (Zα/2 + Zβ)2 * (p1(1-p1) + p2(1-p2))/(p1-p2)2; based on the proportion of functional independence in young and old age patients at three months from previous meta-analysis study was (0.6;0.35) respectively [2], so the minimum sample size for each group is 49. The collected data were statistically analyzed by a software (IBM SPSS Statistics, version 27.0). Mean and SD (stander deviation) was used for normally distributed continuous variables. Median and IQR (interquartile range) was used for not normally distributed continuous data. Normality was determined by using Shapiro-Wilk or Kolmogorov-Smirnov test. Frequencies or proportions were used for categorical variables. Multiple demographic, clinical and radiological data were analyzed between young and old age group to discover the differences between them, using Chi square test or Fischer exact test when expected cell counts are low. Student t-test test or Mann–Whitney U for normally and not normally distributed continuous variables, respectively. Multiple demographic, clinical and radiological data were analyzed between Favorable and bad outcome groups to discover the differences between them, using Chi square test or Fischer exact test when expected cell counts are low. P-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Binary multivariate logistic regression tests were performed for the demographic, clinical and radiological data which might affect the functional outcome. Entered factors included those with at least marginal significance (P < 0.1) on univariate analysis, using enter method. and those previously reported as major affecting factors of the functional outcome. Nagelkerke R square was 0.631 and Hosmer-Lemeshow test p value was 0.442, indicating a well-fitting model. Multi-collinearity of the final model was assessed. A variable was eliminated if the variance inflation factor (VIF) was greater or equal to 10 or the tolerance limit was less than 0.1. Bonferroni correction was used to adjust for type I error inflation, so significant P-value for the regression analysis model became less than 0.004.

Discussion

Our study reported the rate of young adults spontaneous ICH, epidemiological characters, mortality and functional outcome in comparison to old age in south of Egypt, one of the largest population country in MENA region. Frequency of ICH in young adults differ between studies from 7 to 40% of all ICH cases [17]. Our study reported 30. 3% of all cases, in concordance with Damien C et al. who compared African cohort with European one where the study reported 30% and 14.4% in African and European cohorts, respectively [18]. Multiple ethnic studies revealed that ICH incidence in African was more than white and Hispanic young age people [19, 20]. The data of the Global Burden of Disease (GBD) Study 2019 on stroke report that ICH incidence rate was reduced in 19 countries in MENA region and increased only in Egypt and Libya by 9.6% (3.5 to 16.5) and 7.9% (2.1 to 14.6), respectively, which occurred in Egypt more slowly after versus before 2010 [21]. ICH incidence in young age patients compared to the total number of patients with ICH was increased according to a meta-analysis, performed over 40 years (from 1980 to 2020) by Sai Wang et al. [22]. This could be explained by the recent increase in the incidence of risk factors in young age group such as drinking, smoking and HTN.

Our study revealed female ratio in young (49.5%) and old age ICH patients (43.1%), in concordance with A Finnish hospital-based study which showed female ratio nearly 40.5% in the young age group [23]. This might be because men are more susceptible to smoking and HTN effect than women, especially in MENA region and Africa (20). In our study, smoking was more obvious in older people than in younger people (42.1% versus 33%). Also, studies in Germany and Sweden, reported ICH occurrence in women was higher than in men [24, 25]. This might be because of smoking and drinking which are common in both males and females in Europe. Also these studies included SAH which more common in female. In Asian study, female young age ICH cases was less (31.88%) in comparison to our study [26]. This difference might be because this study didn’t include SAH which is common in young age female patients and different ethnicity also. In a French study reported 25% female rate in young age ICH because it excluded pure IVH or ICH due to vascular malformation [27].

While a higher waist-to-hip ratio (WHR) is indicating of central obesity and related to various cerebrovascular diseases, such as Hypertension, heart disease and diabetes, it is not directly associated with intracranial hemorrhage (ICH) risk in old and young age patients [28]. In concordance with our study, Sturgeon et al. reported in a prospective study, included 15, 792 persons (African-American ethnicity versus Whites) that hypertension was positively associated with the incidence of ICH, while, Sex, smoking, alcohol intake, body mass index, waist-to-hip ratio, and diabetes were not associated with ICH [29].

In our study, family history of ICH is more predominant in Young age group which might reveal shared genetic or shared environmental factors. This revealed the genetic role in younger age ICH patients where many vascular and hematological risk factors for ICH [30].

Cerebrovascular risk factors and medications use were prominent in old age group in our study, and this was expected. The most important one was hypertension. According to WHO reports, more than 45% of African population (older than 25 years old) have hypertension and more than 50% of hypertensive patients were not diagnosed, or did not receive treatment [31, 32]. Hypertension was a common cause of ICH in both young and old age group in our study, but low incidence of ICH in young age versus old group (36.3% versus 56%). Strategies should be performed and a wide health care awareness should be taken in MENA region to control HTN, and consecutively ICH. In our study, we reported 24% of cases due to vascular cause and 34% due to undetermined cause, while periole C.et al. reported 42% of cases due to vascular and 13% due to undetermined cause in young age group. This differences because of usage more DSA and more genetic tests in his study [33]. Periole C.et al. reported probable CAA in 14.1% of young age group while our study revealed 7.7%, this is might be due to difference in ethnicity. In general, the etiology of ICH in young age group is different due to different methodology used in previous researches such as including ruptured aneurysms (as in our study) which leads to rising of the vascular rank. We noticed that hypertension is important in all geographical areas in the world but vascular cause was prominent in European countries [8]. This might be due to usage of more angiographic imaging in diagnosis especially DSA and genetic tests. In our study, undetermined cause is the second most common cause of ICH after hypertension in both groups, in concordance with other low and middle income countries in multiple studies due to incomplete investigations or early death of the patients [19].

The data of the Global Burden of Disease (GBD) Study 2021 on stroke revealed that ICH has a larger disease burden in Africa, followed by the Americas, while Asia and Europe have a lighter disease burden, but still less than 1990, indicating a good progress as regard prevention and treatment [34]. We propose that recent diagnostic tools have led to diagnosis of more stroke patients in low and middle-income areas, and so, the observation of rising rate of stroke. In opposite to economically developed countries, people might have healthier dietary and lifestyle habits and take care about disease prevention, so a less observed rate of the disease and its burden. Our study reported 8.8% and 28.7% one-month mortality in young and old age groups, respectively. This result was in concordance with multiple studies on ICH in young age patients which reported 8 to 26% one-month mortality [23, 35]. We achieved 74.7% and 35.9% favorable outcome in young and old age group, respectively. This ratio of Favorable outcome is higher than other studies which used the same favorable outcome measure (mRS 0–2), because our study included many patients with vascular risk factors that more treatable as hypertension or CAA [26]. Our study revealed that 97% and 79.3% favorable outcome in young and old age group with S.A.H, respectively, indicating the progress in neuro-interventional treatment of vascular causes in MENA region in the past 10 years. We noticed different results as regard favorable outcome in literature due to different used scales or considering favorable outcome 0–3 on mRS in many studies [35]. Our study reported better outcome for surgical intervention in young age group in comparison to old one but our sample is too small to draw any meaningful conclusions. This might indicate selection bias but we should not forget that old age patients had many vascular risk factors and use many medications. Koivunen RJ et al. reported that 3-month mortality rate in young patients were 3-fold in those treated conservatively, in comparison to surgical evacuation [36].

We reported that higher GCS and low NIHSS were predictors of favorable outcome, in concordance with multiple studies [35, 37]. Koivunen et al. showed that bad functional outcome was predicted by increasing age, NIHHS, and intraventricular hemorrhage [38]. Moreover, Rutten-Jacobs et al. reported that patients with a bad functional outcome had a higher NIHSS score and hypertension at admission [39]. Many studies reported size of hematoma as a predictor of outcome [40], opposite to our study. Some studies didn’t report hematoma size as a predictors of functional outcome like our study [38]. This might be due to the impact of initial hematoma size on functional outcome is often expressed by other variables, such as NIHSS score at admission, which quantifies initial neurological deficits caused by the hematoma. Also, Different methods for measuring size, such as the ABC/2 formula, can have significant overestimation errors in comparison to volumetric analysis.

In spite of prospective nature of our study, but it has some limitations. Single center study and the relative small sample size of young age patients, making some comparisons underpowered. DSA was not performed to all cases, also, there was no genetic test or biopsy confirmation to determine definite CAA. So, many cases fall in the undetermined classification. As regard treatment, many countries in MENA region has no reversal anticoagulant medications, so we present real life image for ICH treatment in this region. The missing investigations might be due to uncovering most of populations in MENA region by health insurance.

Conclusion

Spontaneous ICH in young adults has different demographic, clinical and radiological findings, compared with old age patients. Young adults patients had better mortality and favorable functional outcome at one-month. NIHSS and GCS are predictors of functional outcome.

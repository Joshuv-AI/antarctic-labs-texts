Unlocking intrinsic TADF in flexible crystalline covalent organic frameworks through nanopore confinement design
Authors: Xiang-Chun Li, Hao Sun, Weijie Yang, Weizhe Luo, Zuqiang Wang, Qinchen Jiang, Chuanrui Wu, Qiaoyu Wang, Wen-Yong Lai
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Covalent organic frameworks (COFs) combine structural precision with synthetic versatility, yet their crystalline lattices intrinsically waste 75% of electro-generated excitons as non-emissive triplets. Thermally activated delayed fluorescence (TADF) could harvest these triplets, but its realization in ordered frameworks has been thwarted by a molecular paradox: the conformational flexibility required to minimize the singlet-triplet energy gap (ΔEST) is fundamentally incompatible with the rigidity needed to suppress non-radiative decay in a crystalline lattice. Here we introduce a nanopore-confinement strategy that resolves this dichotomy and unlocks intrinsic TADF in flexible COFs. By embedding conformationally locked donor-acceptor motifs directly into the COF backbone, the spatial constraints of the crystalline nanopores force donor-acceptor pairs into conformations that minimize ΔEST to 0.014 eV, while the rigid framework simultaneously curtails vibrational losses. This approach transforms COFs from passive scaffolds into active platforms for exciton management without compromising crystallinity, film integrity, or processability, yielding free-standing films with high photoluminescence quantum yields and enabling solution-processed OLEDs with competitive performance among crystalline porous emitters. Our work provides an effective strategy for functionalizing porous frameworks through precise manipulation of excited-state dynamics, opening avenues for flexible electronics, photocatalysis, and beyond.

Introduction

Covalent organic frameworks (COFs) have emerged as a prominent class of crystalline porous polymers, distinguished by their structural precision, high surface areas, and synthetic tunability1–3. These attributes have propelled their exploration across diverse fields, including gas storage, catalysis, sensing, and optoelectronics4–7. More recently, their extended π-conjugation and modular architecture have positioned them as compelling platforms for optoelectronics, where the ability to dictate the spatial arrangement of functional units at the molecular level holds immense promise for tailoring photophysical properties8–10. A particularly alluring, yet unmet, goal is the efficient harvesting of triplet excitons within these ordered frameworks, a capability that could fundamentally elevate their performance in light emission, photocatalysis, and beyond. A primary obstacle has been the intrinsically low exciton utilization efficiency in luminescent COFs. According to spin statistics, electrical excitation generates only 25% singlet excitons, which are spin-allowed for radiative decay, while the remaining 75% are non-emissive triplet excitons typically wasted through thermal dissipation. Conventional fluorescent COFs therefore face an inherent efficiency ceiling. This limitation is compounded by the very characteristics that define COFs: their crystalline, rigid skeletons often promote interlayer π-π interactions that lead to aggregation-caused quenching, while the abundant grain boundaries in polycrystalline films act as defects that trap and non-radiatively dissipate excitons. Consequently, despite their structural virtues, realizing high-performance electroluminescence from crystalline porous emitters has remained a formidable challenge.

Recent efforts have further advanced luminescent COFs and films through molecular engineering of linkers and topology, yet achieving efficient triplet harvesting remains challenge11–13. Thermally activated delayed fluorescence (TADF) offers a metal-free pathway to overcome the 75% triplet exciton waste by enabling their upconversion to the singlet state via reverse intersystem crossing (RISC), thereby promising a 100% internal quantum efficiency. Implementing TADF, however, imposes stringent and often contradictory demands on a material. It requires a minimized singlet-triplet energy gap (ΔEST), typically achieved through a highly twisted donor-acceptor (D-A) architecture that fosters strong intramolecular charge transfer while spatially separating frontier molecular orbitals14–16. Simultaneously, it demands a rigid local environment to suppress the non-radiative vibrational relaxations that compete with delayed emission. The ordered, yet inherently rigid, lattices of COFs present a paradox: they can, in principle, provide the desired rigidity, but their structural precision has historically precluded the incorporation of the conformationally flexible D-A motifs necessary for a sufficiently small ΔEST. This inherent trade-off between lattice rigidity and the conformational freedom required for efficient RISC has made the realization of intrinsic TADF in a crystalline COF an unsolved challenge, with no successful strategies reported to date.

Here we report a generalizable nanopore-confinement strategy that reconciles this dichotomy and unlocks intrinsic TADF in flexible, crystalline COFs. Through the strategic selection of donor and acceptor building blocks that enhance intramolecular charge transfer under spatial constraint, and by employing a topology that imposes steric hindrance, we achieve a minimized ΔEST while utilizing the crystalline framework’s inherent rigidity to effectively suppress non-radiative pathways. This approach produces freestanding COF films that exhibit high photoluminescence quantum yields, remarkable mechanical flexibility, and efficient triplet harvesting. Furthermore, we demonstrate the solution-processable fabrication of organic light-emitting diodes (OLEDs) with luminance of 1692 cd m−2. Our work provides an effective strategy for harnessing triplet excitons in ordered frameworks and provides a versatile blueprint for creating multifunctional crystalline materials that seamlessly unite structural precision, mechanical flexibility, and high optoelectronic performance.

Results

To implement nanopore-confinement design, we selected the established efficient TADF unit DMAC-2CHO, comprising a benzophenone acceptor and a DMAC donor, known for its effective spatial separation of the highest occupied and lowest unoccupied molecular orbitals (HOMO and LUMO) and near-perpendicular conformation conducive to a minimized singlet-triplet energy gap (ΔEST). The synthetic route of DMAC-2CHO is shown in Supplementary Fig. 1, and the linkers were characterized via 1H NMR, 13C NMR and mass spectrum (Supplementary Fig. 2-S4). We synthesized a series of crystalline, free-standing COF films via a liquid-liquid interfacial Schiff-base polycondensation at room temperature, copolymerizing the planar knot 1,3,5-tris(4-aminophenyl) benzene (TAPB-3NH2) with DMAC-2CHO and a non-emissive analogue, Ph-2CHO, in systematically varied ratios (Fig. 1a and Supplementary Fig. 5). This confined interfacial synthesis, occurring with uniform kinetics, directly yields large-area (up to ~20 cm2), handleable films (Fig. 1b) whose insoluble nature confirms the formation of an extended cross-linked network. Powder X-ray diffraction (PXRD) confirms the high crystallinity of all compositions (Ph-TAPB-COF and DMAC/Ph-TAPB-COFs), with distinct peaks shifting predictably with increased DMAC-2CHO incorporation, validated by Pawley refinement (Supplementary Figs. 6-S10). Critically, this confined interface synthesis overcomes the typically poor luminescence and film-forming ability of Schiff-base COFs. The resulting free-standing films exhibit strong visible yellow photoluminescence under UV excitation (365 nm) (Fig. 2a), with intensity escalating dramatically as the DMAC-TADF unit fraction increases, while the Ph-TAPB-COF control remains non-emissive (Fig. 2b, c). Notably, the optimized DMAC-TAPB-COF film demonstrates a photoluminescence quantum yield (PLQY) of 14.1%, representing a 37-fold and 875-fold enhancement over its molecular precursor DMAC-2CHO and the non-TADF Ph-TAPB-COF film, respectively (Table S1, Supplementary Fig. 12). To further elucidate the structural origin of aggregation-caused quenching (ACQ) suppression, the interlayer spacings were extracted from the optimized crystal structures (Supplementary Fig. 13). A close interlayer distance of 3.44 Å was observed for Ph-TAPB‑COF, indicative of strong π-π stacking interactions. In contrast, a significantly enlarged interlayer spacing of 4.59 Å was found for DMAC‑TAPB‑COF, which is attributable to the steric bulk of the non‑planar DMAC units that disrupts the intimate layer-to-layer approach. The confinement of DMAC moieties within the nanopores was revealed by the top‑view structural model. This increased separation, combined with the spatial isolation provided by the periodic pore architecture, effectively prevents ACQ. This stark improvement underscores the dual role of the rigid, crystalline COF matrix, it simultaneously suppresses aggregation-caused quenching and locks the TADF units in conformations that inhibit non-radiative decay. Specifically, the molecular precursor DMAC‑2CHO suffers from severe non‑radiative decay due to free rotation of its D-A bonds and ACQ. Upon covalent integration into the COF lattice, the framework rigidity restricts intramolecular motions, the periodic nanopores spatially isolate the TADF units to prevent ACQ, and the confined geometry that minimizes ΔEₛₜ to 0.014 eV, thereby promoting efficient reverse intersystem crossing (RISC). To enable a meaningful comparison under conditions relevant to OLEDs, we also measured the PLQY of all COF samples when doped into a CBP host matrix (Table S1). Under these identical host-guest doped films, the Ph‑TAPB‑COF control in CBP exhibits a PLQY of 13.1%, which arises solely from the CBP host emission (Supplementary Fig. 14a). In contrast, DMAC‑TAPB‑COF in CBP reaches a PLQY of 21.3%, where the CBP emission is nearly completely quenched and strong COF emission dominates the spectrum (Supplementary Fig. 14b), collectively confirming efficient Förster resonance energy transfer (FRET) from the host to the COF guest. To evaluate the spectral overlap that enables energy transfer from the CBP host to the COF guest, the UV-vis absorption spectra of the COF samples and the photoluminescence (PL) spectrum of CBP were measured (Supplementary Fig. 15). Transient photoluminescence decay profiles at room temperature provide direct evidence of successful triplet harvesting (Fig. 2d). In contrast to the single, nanosecond-scale prompt fluorescence decay of Ph-TAPB-COF ( ~ 12 ns, Supplementary Fig. 16), the DMAC-incorporated COF films exhibit clearly resolvable microsecond-scale delayed components (lifetimes from 3.5 to 13.9 μs), characteristic of TADF. This TADF behavior is rationalized by a systematically reduced ΔEST, calculated via time-dependent density functional theory, which decreases from 0.170 eV in Ph-TAPB-COF to as low as 0.014 eV in the DMAC-enriched frameworks (Fig. 2e and Supplementary Fig. 17), thereby facilitating efficient reverse intersystem crossing. The nanopore-confinement thus successfully orchestrates the required molecular conditions, enhanced intramolecular charge transfer and rigidification, within a crystalline lattice. Coupled with thermal stability (decomposition above 455 oC, Supplementary Fig. 18), these highly luminescent, flexible COF films present a compelling material platform for advanced optoelectronic applications.

By precisely regulating the monomer concentration and reaction kinetics at the confined liquid-liquid interface, we obtain large-area (~20 cm2), free-standing COF films with thicknesses tunable from tens to hundreds of nanometers, corresponding to an aspect ratio spanning five orders of magnitude. Optical microscopy reveals that films incorporating DMAC-based TADF units exhibit minimal defects and only the anticipated manipulation-induced wrinkles, whereas the unsubstituted Ph-TAPB-COF analogue displays pervasive cracking, indicating that the introduction of TADF linkers also enhances film-forming integrity (Fig. 3a). Scanning electron microscopy (SEM) confirms uniform, flat surfaces and cross-sectional thicknesses that scale predictably with synthesis parameters (Fig. 3b, c). Atomic force microscopy (AFM) was further employed to quantitatively evaluate surface roughness (Supplementary Fig. 19). The DMAC‑incorporated COFs exhibited smoother surfaces. The confined interfacial environment forces molecular building blocks to assemble into atomically thin 2D layers that progressively thicken, a growth mechanism that underpins both the homogeneity and the robust mechanical properties of the resulting films. Notably, the free-standing TADF COF films withstand repeated bending and twisting without mechanical failure (Fig. 3d), demonstrating that incorporation of the DMAC donor-acceptor motif simultaneously upgrades luminescence efficiency and mechanical flexibility. To assess the utility of these crystalline TADF films in electroluminescent devices, we fabricated solution-processed OLEDs (Fig. 4a). Within this configuration, the nanopore-confined environment of the COF lattice spatially restricts electron-hole recombination to the embedded TADF moieties (Fig. 4b), and charge transport proceeds via hopping between these emissive sites. To identify a suitable host for the COF emitter, we evaluated three host materials with distinct electronic properties: PTAA, BCPO, and CBP. Their chemical structures are depicted in Supplementary Fig. 21. PTAA-based devices exhibited maximum luminance of 1038 cd m-2 and current efficiency of 0.55 cd A-1 (Supplementary Figs. S22a and S22b). BCPO-based devices exhibited maximum luminance of 171 cd m-2 and current efficiency of 0.82 cd A−1 (Supplementary Figs. S22c and S22d). CBP-based devices employing 1DMAC-2Ph-TAPB-COF, 2DMAC-1Ph-TAPB-COF, and DMAC-TAPB-COF as emitting layers exhibit maximum luminance of 1103 cd m-2, 1331 cd m-2, and 1692 cd m-2 (Fig. 4d), respectively, values that substantially surpass the luminance benchmark for commercial displays (100 cd m-2)17. The current density of the DMAC-TAPB-COF device is two and three orders of magnitude lower than those of recently reported IISERP-COF718 and strontium metal-organic framework (MOF)-based devices19, respectively (Fig. 4e), indicating markedly more efficient current injection. To our knowledge, this represents the highest performance reported to date for any MOF- and COF-based OLED (Table S2)20–22. The highest occupied molecular orbital (HOMO) and lowest unoccupied molecular orbital (LUMO) energy levels of DMAC‑TAPB‑COF were determined by cyclic voltammetry (Supplementary Fig. 23). As shown in Fig. 4c, holes are injected from PEDOT:PSS into the host (CBP) and guest (COF) within the emissive layer across a moderate barrier, whereas electrons encounter a negligible barrier from TPBi into the emissive layer. This favorable alignment facilitates efficient charge recombination within the emissive layer. The peak current efficiency of the DMAC-TAPB-COF device reaches 3.5 cd A-1 (Fig. 4f), demonstrating that the TADF units directly translate into substantially enhanced electroluminescence efficiency. Moreover, the electroluminescence spectra of all TADF-COF devices remain virtually unchanged across a wide range of driving voltages, underscoring the spectral stability of these crystalline emitters under high-field operation (Supplementary Figs. 24–S26). Finally, the intrinsic two-dimensional nanosheet morphology of these TADF COFs enables the fabrication of flexible OLEDs that maintain stable electroluminescence under external and internal bending, as well as under folded configurations, while retaining high luminance and competitive current efficiency (Supplementary Figs. 27–S29). To quantitatively evaluate the mechanical durability of the flexible OLEDs, cyclic bending tests were systematically conducted (Supplementary Fig. 30). After 100 bending cycles at a radius of 3 mm, the current density remained essentially unchanged, demonstrating excellent fatigue resistance. Furthermore, devices subjected to inward and outward bending at radii of 1, 2, and 3 mm consistently maintained stable operation across all tested configurations. These results confirm that the nanopore‑confined TADF COF films possess not only visual flexibility but also mechanical robustness under demanding operational conditions, an essential attribute for practical flexible display applications. Discussion

In summary, we report a nanopore-confinement strategy that unlocks intrinsic TADF in flexible crystalline COFs. By integrating conformationally locked yet electronically decoupled donor–acceptor motifs directly into the rigid COF backbone, we simultaneously minimize ΔEST to as low as 0.014 eV and effectively suppress non-radiative decay, yielding high PLQYs without compromising structural order. This design resolves the longstanding trade-off between lattice rigidity and exciton utilization in porous crystalline solids, while also imparting mechanical flexibility to the resulting films. The approach is inherently versatile, enabling the straightforward fabrication of large-area, free-standing COF films via liquid-liquid interfacial polymerization that retain both crystallinity and solution processability. Incorporated into OLEDs, these materials achieve the luminance of 1692 cd m-2. Collectively, our results provide a promising approach for triplet harvesting in ordered organic frameworks and offer a general blueprint for the design of multifunctional crystalline materials that unite structural precision, dynamic adaptability, and high optoelectronic performance. This work thereby paves the way for COF-based flexible displays, lighting, and emerging exciton-driven technologies.

Methods

Synthesis of Ph-TAPB-COF Films

Ph-2CHO (6.0 mg, 0.045 mmol) and TAPB-3NH2 (10.5 mg, 0.03 mmol) were placed in a beaker. Trimethylbenzene was added, and the mixture was sonicated, followed by the addition of 1,4-dioxane until a homogeneous dispersion was obtained. The resulting solution was carefully layered onto an aqueous acetic acid solution (6 M, 10 mL) by slow addition along the wall of the beaker. The reaction was allowed to proceed at room temperature without disturbance for 5days, during which a free-standing film formed at the liquid-liquid interface. The film was collected, immersed in tetrahydrofuran and acetone for 2days to remove any unreacted monomers and oligomers, and finally dried under vacuum to afford Ph-TAPB-COF as a white film.

Synthesis of mDMAC-nPh-TAPB-COF Films

mDMAC-nPh-TAPB-COF films were synthesized via liquid-liquid interfacial condensation. DMAC-2CHO (7.8 mg, 0.015 mmol), Ph-2CHO (4.0 mg, 0.03 mmol), and TAPB-3NH2 (10.5 mg, 0.03 mmol) were placed in a beaker. Trimethylbenzene was added and the mixture was sonicated, followed by the addition of 1,4-dioxane until a homogeneous dispersion was obtained. The solution was carefully layered onto an aqueous acetic acid solution (6 M, 10 mL) by slow addition along the wall of the beaker. The reaction was left undisturbed at room temperature for 5 days, during which a free-standing film formed at the interface. The film was collected, immersed in tetrahydrofuran and acetone for 2 days, and dried under vacuum to afford 1DMAC-2Ph-TAPB-COF as pale-yellow film. 1DMAC-1Ph-TAPB-COF and 2DMAC-1Ph-TAPB-COF were prepared following an identical procedure with adjusted molar ratios of DMAC-2CHO and Ph-2CHO.

Synthesis of DMAC-TAPB-COF Films

DMAC-2CHO (23.5 mg, 0.045 mmol) and TAPB-3NH2 (10.5 mg, 0.03 mmol) were placed in a beaker. Trimethylbenzene was added, and the mixture was sonicated, then 1,4-dioxane was added until a homogeneous dispersion was achieved. The solution was carefully layered onto an aqueous acetic acid solution (6 M, 10 mL) by slow addition along the wall of the beaker. The reaction was maintained at room temperature without disturbance for 5days, during which a free-standing film developed at the liquid-liquid interface. The film was collected, immersed in tetrahydrofuran and acetone for 2days, and dried under vacuum to afford DMAC-TAPB-COF as a pale-yellow film.

Objective

Leiomyosarcomas (LMS), one of the most common types of soft-tissue sarcomas (STS), exhibit a poor prognosis primarily due to the lack of targeted molecular therapies. Recent studies have identified some potential molecular drivers, including Extracellular signal-regulated kinase 5 (ERK5) [1, 2]. ERK5, encoded by the gene MAPK7, is a member of the Mitogen-Activated Protein Kinase (MAPK) family, distinguished by unique functional domains in its C-terminal region, including a nuclear localization signal (NLS) and a transcriptional activation domain (TAD) [3]. These structural features allow ERK5 to function not only as a kinase but also as a transcriptional regulator [4], thus significantly amplifying its impact on tumor biology.

Although still poorly understood, numerous in vitro and in vivo studies have demonstrated that ERK5 plays a crucial role in essential biological processes, including cell proliferation, colony formation, adhesion, or in vivo tumorigenesis which are relevant to certain types of STS, including LMS and undifferentiated pleomorphic sarcoma [1, 2]. These evidences highlights ERK5 as a promising therapeutic target. Consequently, pharmacological inhibitors such as JWG-071 have been performed. Despite its high selectivity and potency for ERK5 (IC50 of 90 nM), JWG-071 also interacts with off-target proteins, including DCLK1, DCLK2, and LRRK2 [5]. This could confound pharmacological inhibition studies due to targets not related to ERK5, reducing their specificity. To circumvent these limitations and precisely delineate the ERK5-specific transcriptomic signature, we used genetic interference (shRNA) targeting full-length MAPK7 transcripts in two independent LMS cell lines. This approach ensures the specificity of observed effects, allowing us to analyze the biological roles mediated by both kinase and transcriptional activities of ERK5. By comparing these transcriptional profiles, we identified conserved signaling pathways and biological processes influenced by ERK5 suppression. Such findings enhance our understanding of LMS biology, providing valuable insights into potential therapeutic strategies and their translation into clinical applications. Moreover, the individual transcriptomic signatures for each cell line provide insights into the heterogenity of the biological consequences of gene abrogation and making them valuable from both a basic and translational point of view.

Data description

Two uterine LMS cell lines, SK-UT-1 (from ATCC) and AA (provided by Dr. Amancio Carnero), underwent MAPK7/ERK5 genetic knockdown using a specific shRNA in a PLKO.1-puro vector (TRCN0000010275,) and a scramble shRNA as control, both purchased from Merck. Cell lines were cultured under standard conditions at 37 °C, 5% CO2, with SK-UT-1 in DMEM (Lonza) and AA in Ham’s Nutrient Mixture F10 (Cytiva), both supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS), 1% penicillin-streptomycin antibiotics, and 1% fungizone. Lentiviral particles for gene silencing were generated in HEK293T cells via calcium phosphate transfection. Target cells were infected in medium containing polybrene, followed by puromycin selection for at least 72 h (SK-UT-1: 1 µg/mL; AA: 1.5 µg/mL). Three independent interference experiments were performed and sequenced. Genetic knockdown was validated by RT-qPCR, measuring MAPK7 mRNA relative to GAPDH, and by western blot analysis of ERK5 protein levels (Cell Signalling Tech. 3372) using VCL (Sigma-Aldrich V9264) as a loading control.

RNA sequencing (RNA-Seq) was performed to identify differentially expressed genes (DEGs) in three independent interferences for each cell line. Sequencing was conducted by BGI using the DNBSEQ platform, and data quality was verified with a NanoDropTM One spectrophotometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific). Raw reads underwent quality filtering (SOAPnuke v1.5.2) [6] to remove adapter sequences, reads with >5% unknown bases, and low-quality sequences. RNA-Seq analysis was performed using the Dr. Tom Platform from BGI [7]. Clean reads were aligned to the Homo sapiens reference genome (GRCh38.p13) using Bowtie2 [8], and gene expression levels were quantified using RSEM [9]. Differential gene expression analysis was developed by DESeq2 (|log2FC| ≥ 1, q-value ≤ 0.05) [10]. As a result, we identified 1,218 and 227 DEGs for AA and SK-UT-1, respectively, and 81 in common for both cell lines, including MAPK7 as an expected commonly downregulated gene in both cell lines.

Raw sequencing data were deposited in the NCBI Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) accessible on https://identifiers.org/geo:GSE297879 [11] as data files from 1 to 12. Table 1 provides a summary of all data files deposited in the repository. Sequencing analysis is detailed in data file 13 and DEGs are detailed in data file 14. Data file annotations are as follows: SK-UT-1 scramble shRNA (SKUT1_EV_1, 3, 5), ERK5 shRNA (SKUT1_455_2, 4, 6); AA scramble shRNA (AA_EV_1, 3, 5), ERK5 shRNA (AA_455_2, 4, 6). Comparative analyses of DEGs were performed between each experimental and control condition (UT_455/UT_EV and AA_455/AA_EV).

Limitations

This preliminary screening was limited to two LMS cell lines with a specific genetic background, potentially restricting the generalizability of our findings. Therefore, this observation cannot be extrapolated to other STS subtypes.

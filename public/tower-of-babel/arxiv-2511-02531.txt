Causal Graph Neural Networks for Healthcare
Munib Mesinovic, Max Buhlan, Tingting Zhu

Abstract
Healthcare artificial intelligence systems routinely fail when deployed across institutions, with documented performance drops and perpetuation of discriminatory patterns embedded in historical data. This brittleness stems, in part, from learning statistical associations rather than causal mechanisms. Causal graph neural networks address this triple crisis of distribution shift, discrimination, and inscrutability by combining graph-based representations of biomedical data with causal inference principles to learn invariant mechanisms rather than spurious correlations. This Review examines methodological foundations spanning structural causal models, disentangled causal representation learning, and techniques for interventional prediction and counterfactual reasoning on graphs. We analyse applications demonstrating clinical value across psychiatric diagnosis through brain network analysis, cancer subtyping via multi-omics causal integration, continuous physiological monitoring with mechanistic interpretation, and drug recommendation correcting prescription bias. These advances establish foundations for patient-specific Causal Digital Twins, enabling in silico clinical experimentation, with integration of large language models for hypothesis generation and causal graph neural networks for mechanistic validation. Substantial barriers remain, including computational requirements precluding real-time deployment, validation challenges demanding multi-modal evidence triangulation beyond cross-validation, and risks of causal-washing where methods employ causal terminology without rigorous evidentiary support. We propose tiered frameworks distinguishing causally-inspired architectures from causally-validated discoveries and identify critical research priorities, including hierarchical causal discovery exploiting biological modularity, integration of diverse data modalities, and regulatory pathways for systems making causal rather than purely associational claims. Success requires balancing theoretical ambition with empirical humility, computational sophistication with clinical interpretability, and transformative vision with uncompromising validation standards.

The path from proof-of-concept demonstrations to clinical implementation is neither straight nor simple, demanding collaboration between computational scientists, clinicians, biomedical researchers, and policymakers united by the recognition that healthcare AI’s next decade will be defined by which approaches achieve not just impressive metrics but genuine clinical trust through mechanistic understanding.

2
The causal graph framework

2.1
Why biological systems demand graph-structured causal models

Biological systems exhibit causal relationships organised as networks across multiple scales of organisation. At the molecular level, gene regulatory networks encode transcriptional control in which regulatory proteins causally influence the expression of target genes, with modern single-cell RNA sequencing that captures the expression profiles of 20,000+ genes simultaneously
Regev_2017_Human
. Protein-protein interaction networks represent physical binding relationships that mediate cellular signalling, with causal perturbations propagating through these networks to generate phenotypic responses
Barabasi_2011_Network
. At the tissue level, brain networks comprise hundreds of regions with functional and structural connectivity encoding causal influences between neural populations, where activity in one region can drive or modulate activity in connected regions through synaptic transmission
Schirmer_2021_Graph
. At the population level, disease comorbidity networks represent causal relationships where one condition increases risk for others through shared pathological mechanisms, diabetes causally increasing cardiovascular disease risk through vascular damage, inflammation, and metabolic dysfunction
Nicholson_2023_Constructing
. This inherent graph structure makes neural network architectures that explicitly represent and reason about network topology the natural framework for biomedical machine learning.

The mathematical foundation connecting causal inference to graph representations is based on Structural Causal Models (SCMs), which support a rigorous formalisation of cause-effect relationships
Pearl_2009_Causality
. An SCM
\mathcal{M}:=\langle U,V,F,P(U)\rangle
specifies exogenous variables
U
determined outside the model, endogenous variables
V
defined within the model, structural equations
F=\{f_{1},\ldots,f_{m}\}
where each
V_{i}=f_{i}(\text{pa}(V_{i}),U_{i})
, and probability distribution
P(U)
over exogenous variables
Pearl_2010_Introduction
. This formulation naturally induces a directed acyclic graph
\mathcal{G}(\mathcal{M})
where nodes represent variables and directed edges encode functional dependencies through the parent sets
\text{pa}(V_{i})
. The joint distribution factorises according to the Markov property:
P(V_{1},\ldots,V_{m})=\prod_{i=1}^{m}P(V_{i}|\text{pa}(V_{i}))

Pearl_2009_Causality
. Interventions in this framework correspond to “graph surgery,” modifying the causal graph by removing incoming edges to intervened variables and setting their values, fundamentally altering the generative process
Pearl_2009_Causality
;
Zecevic_2021_Relating
. This graph-theoretic representation of causation aligns precisely with the message-passing architecture of graph neural networks, enabling implementation of causal reasoning through differentiable neural operations.

Standard GNNs, although naturally suited to graph-structured data, learn to exploit whatever statistical patterns maximise predictive performance regardless of causal validity or biological plausibility. In brain imaging, global arousal states create spurious correlations between all regions that GNNs readily exploit; in molecular networks, batch effects create artificial associations between unrelated proteins; in clinical data, treatment selection bias generates correlations between drug prescriptions and outcomes that reflect physician decision-making rather than therapeutic efficacy
Bevilacqua_2021_Size
;
Knyazev_2019_Understanding
. These spurious patterns often provide a strong predictive signal in training environments, but fail catastrophically under distribution shift, precisely the brittleness observed in clinical deployment. Causality-inspired GNN architectures address this through architectural constraints, training objectives, and inductive biases that direct learning towards invariant causal relationships rather than environment-specific correlations. The DisC framework, for example, employs dual encoders to explicitly disentangle causal features that consistently predict outcomes across environments from spurious features that correlate with outcomes only in specific settings
Fan_2022_Debiasing
. The CI-GNN architecture for brain networks uses conditional mutual information from a Granger causality perspective to identify connections representing genuine neural communication rather than shared global fluctuations
Zhang_2023_CI_GNN
;
Zheng_2024_CIGNN
. These approaches transform GNNs from being powerful function approximators that learn arbitrary predictive patterns to becoming mechanistic models that capture biologically grounded causal relationships.

2.2
From association to intervention to counterfactuals

Standard supervised learning excels at associational queries, given covariates
X
, predict the outcome
Y
by learning
P(Y|X)
from observational data. This suffices for many pattern recognition tasks: identifying malignant tumours from images, predicting patient deterioration from vital signs, or classifying disease subtypes from gene expression. However, clinical decision-making fundamentally requires interventional reasoning: what happens if we administer treatment
T
? This requires estimating
P(Y|\text{do}(T))
, which differs from
P(Y|T)
whenever confounding exists, and confounding pervades observational healthcare data through indication bias, selection effects, and unmeasured factors
Hernan_2020_Causal
;
Pearl_2009_Causality
.

Graph-structured causal models enable interventional prediction through Pearl’s do-calculus, which provides three rules for transforming interventional distributions into estimable observational quantities under specified graph conditions
Pearl_2009_Causality
. Rule 1 allows ignoring observations on variables that don’t affect the outcome through paths independent of the intervention. Rule 2 permits converting interventions to observations when appropriate conditional independence holds. Rule 3 enables removing interventions when they do not affect the outcome through certain graph paths. The interventional Variational Graph Auto-Encoder (iVGAE) implements these operations through differentiable neural layers, incorporating interventional transformations in both encoder and decoder networks to ensure that the learnt representations encode causal rather than associational relationships
Zecevic_2021_Relating
. When the model identifies a connection between two proteins or two brain regions, it learns whether this connection would persist under interventions, whether it represents a causal pathway or a spurious correlation.

Counterfactual reasoning operates at the highest level of Pearl’s hierarchy, addressing patient-specific questions: would
this
patient have developed complications if we had chosen alternative treatment? Reasoning of this kind is essential for precision medicine, treatment optimisation, and retrospective analysis of clinical decisions
Pearl_2009_Causality
. The calculation of counterfactuals requires a three-step process, including abduction (inferring unobserved variables, including patient-specific factors, from observed data), action (modifying the causal model to reflect alternative intervention), and prediction (computing outcomes under the modified model)
Pearl_2010_Introduction
. The CLEAR framework implements counterfactual generation for graphs through variational autoencoders that learn distributions over causally plausible graph modifications, allowing the exploration of alternative biological configurations with mechanistic validity
Ma_2022_Clear
. In cancer treatment planning, this enables simulation of how different therapeutic targets would affect tumour evolution, identifying vulnerabilities that might not be apparent from observational data alone.

The convergence of causal inference and graph learning thereby enables a transformative vision: patient-specific
Causal Digital Twins
, dynamic computational models where clinicians perform in silico experiments to test interventions before clinical administration. Such a system would integrate the multi-omics profile of a patient (genomics, transcriptomics, proteomics), longitudinal imaging that captures organ structure and function, clinical history including treatments and responses, and knowledge graphs encoding established biological mechanisms
Regev_2017_Human
;
Topol_2019_High
. The underlying causal GNN architecture would learn patient-specific parameterisations of general biological mechanisms, enabling simulation of therapeutic interventions through the do-operator framework by setting treatment variables, propagating effects through learnt causal pathways, and predicting outcomes at molecular, cellular, and phenotypic levels. For an oncology patient, the digital twin might simulate five drug combinations, predicting effects on specific oncogenic pathways, normal tissue toxicity, and progression-free survival, thereby identifying optimal personalised therapy before administering a single dose. For a neuropsychiatric patient, it could simulate neuromodulation protocols, predicting which brain circuits would be affected and how symptoms would respond. This vision, while ambitious, rests on methodological foundations now being established of GNN architectures for interventional prediction, counterfactual generation frameworks for exploring alternative scenarios, invariant learning methods ensuring robust generalisation, and multi-modal integration strategies combining diverse data modalities through shared causal structure
Scholkopf_2021_Toward
;
Job_2024_Towards
. Figure
2
quantifies these theoretical distinctions, demonstrating how traditional graph neural networks can achieve comparable associational performance but exhibit substantial degradation for interventional and counterfactual queries essential for clinical decision-making.

3
Methodological advances

The translation of causal theory into practical healthcare applications requires architectures that address specific clinical capabilities. We organise methodological advances not by technical taxonomy but by the clinical problems they solve, such as disentangling genuine causal mechanisms from confounding, predicting interventional effects without experimental data, generating patient-specific counterfactuals, achieving robust generalisation across heterogeneous settings, and ensuring fairness through causal rather than purely statistical criteria.

3.1
Disentangling causal signal from confounding

Biomedical datasets are rife with spurious correlations that mislead standard machine learning models. In brain imaging, global arousal states create correlations between all regions, in molecular networks, batch effects generate artificial associations between unrelated proteins, in clinical records, treatment selection bias produces correlations reflecting physician decision-making rather than therapeutic efficacy
Bevilacqua_2021_Size
;
Knyazev_2019_Understanding
. The challenge is to decompose observed graphs into causal and spurious components, a problem addressed through information-theoretic disentanglement that maximises mutual information between causal features and outcomes and minimises spurious correlations
Liu_2024_IGCLGNN
.

The DisC (Debiasing via Disentangled Causal Substructure) framework operationalises this separation through a dual-encoder architecture
Fan_2022_Debiasing
. Rather than treating graphs monolithically, DisC maintains parallel GNN encoders: one trained to extract causal features that consistently predict outcomes across different environments, another capturing environment-specific spurious patterns. The contrastive learning objective enforces the independence between these representations, ensuring that causal and spurious features remain disentangled. For psychiatric diagnosis, this architectural separation was proven to be immensely effective. Traditional GNNs identified widespread hyperconnectivity in the cerebellar regions of depressed patients, a finding inconsistent with the clinical understanding that depression primarily influences affective rather than motor circuits. DisC’s causal decomposition correctly identified sparse cerebellar involvement while highlighting strong connections around the left rectus, a region known to be critical for treatment response to antidepressants
Zhang_2023_CI_GNN
. This alignment with established neurobiology demonstrates that causal disentanglement provides mechanistically meaningful insights, not merely improved prediction.

The CI-GNN (Causality-Inspired Graph Neural Network) advances this concept through Granger causality-inspired conditional mutual information, providing principled measures of causal strength for graph edges
Zheng_2024_CIGNN
;
Zhang_2023_CI_GNN
. The model identifies influential subgraphs that demonstrate genuine causal connections through disentangled subgraph-level representation learning. In autism spectrum disorder evaluations on the ABIDE dataset, CI-GNN revealed tight connections within the somatomotor network consistent with motor impairments characteristic of ASD, findings replicated across independent cohorts and suggesting genuine causal relationships rather than dataset-specific artefacts
Zheng_2024_CIGNN
. The clinical imperative was that clinicians needed to know which connections to target through neurostimulation, pharmacotherapy, or behavioural interventions, not merely that “connectivity is abnormal.” The IGCL-GNN framework extends these principles through multi-objective optimisation that simultaneously enhances causal features and suppresses environment-specific spurious patterns, recognising that robust clinical deployment requires balancing predictive performance on current datasets with generalisation to unseen environments
Liu_2024_IGCLGNN
.

3.2
Predicting interventional effects without experimental data

Precision medicine requires predicting patient-specific treatment responses, queries about interventions that often lack experimental data. The RC-Explainer (Reinforced Causal Explainer) uses reinforcement learning to discover which edges of the graph to intervene in to maximise the causal effect on the outcomes
Wang_2022_RCExplainer
. Rather than computing interventions symbolically, it learns a policy for selecting interventions, with each candidate evaluated not by correlation with outcomes but by causal impact computed through careful accounting for confounding and interaction effects. In drug combination discovery, this approach identified synergistic therapies in which individual drugs showed minimal effect but together produced substantial therapeutic benefit, combinations that were missed by correlation-based methods that cannot distinguish synergy from additive effects
Wang_2022_RCExplainer
.

The CaT-GNN (Causal Temporal Graph Neural Network) implements interventional reasoning through architectural modifications encoding backdoor adjustment
Yang_2022_CaT
. The framework first identifies which nodes represent causal factors versus environmental confounders, and then applies mixup augmentation specifically to environmental nodes. This selective augmentation breaks spurious correlations and preserves causal relationships, achieving robust prediction across different patient populations and clinical settings. For blood pressure monitoring, the CiGNN framework demonstrates the clinical utility of interventional prediction by first using the neural-accelerated causal discovery (FCI algorithm) to identify physiological signals causally related to blood pressure variation, then constructing spatiotemporal GNN architectures guided by the discovered causal structure
Ding_2024_CiGNN
;
Liu_2023_Causal
. This causal feature selection reduces the dimensionality from 200+ potential features to 23 causal indicators while improving both accuracy and interpretability, identifying, for example, that specific heart rate variability patterns causally influence blood pressure through autonomic regulation and certain PPG morphological features affect pressure through arterial stiffness mechanisms.

The iVGAE (interventional Variational Graph Auto-Encoder) provides theoretical grounding for interventional GNNs by incorporating interventional layers in both encoder and decoder networks, ensuring that learnt representations explicitly encode causal rather than merely associational relationships
Zecevic_2021_Relating
. When the model identifies a connection between proteins or brain regions, it learns whether this connection represents a causal pathway that would persist under intervention rather than a spurious correlation arising from confounding. For drug discovery, PDGrapher leverages protein-protein interaction networks as approximations of causal graphs, operating under causal sufficiency assumptions to predict combinatorial therapeutic targets
Roohani_2024_PDGrapher
. This causally-inspired approach outperformed traditional methods in identifying effective perturbagens across nine cell lines with chemical perturbations, demonstrating that even imperfect causal structures, PPI networks contain both direct causal effects and indirect associations, provide superior guidance compared to purely correlational feature selection.

3.3
Generating counterfactuals for personalised medicine

Counterfactual reasoning addresses the highest level of Pearl’s causal hierarchy: what would have happened to
this
patient under alternative treatment? Such patient-specific predictions are essential for precision medicine, treatment optimisation, and retrospective analysis of clinical decisions
Pearl_2009_Causality
. The CLEAR (Counterfactual Learning on Graphs) framework approaches counterfactual generation through variational autoencoders that learn distributions over possible graph modifications
Ma_2022_Clear
. Rather than generating arbitrary alternative graphs, CLEAR learns which modifications are causally plausible, which edges could change, and it still maintains biological validity. In cancer treatment planning, this enables exploration of how different therapeutic targets would affect tumour behaviour, identifying unexpected vulnerabilities invisible in observational data alone.

The fundamental challenge in counterfactual reasoning is that interventions can create graph configurations far outside the training distribution
Liu_2025_RecipeCGR
. When simulating disruption of a critical hub protein, we query a configuration likely never appearing naturally, and thus unrepresented in training data. Traditional neural networks fail catastrophically under such a distribution shift. These counterfactual generation methods form the computational engine of Causal Digital Twins; the ability to simulate patient-specific responses to interventions never administered in reality constitutes the core functionality enabling in silico clinical experimentation.

3.4
Achieving robustness across heterogeneous clinical settings

Healthcare AI deployment requires models that generalise across institutions with different patient demographics, clinical protocols, and measurement technologies, precisely the distribution shift conditions where correlation-based models fail catastrophically. The fundamental principle underlying invariant learning is that causal relationships remain stable across contexts while spurious correlations change
Peters_2016_Causal_Inference

In medical terms, the biological mechanisms by which drugs treat diseases should be consistent across populations, even though confounding factors such as demographics, comorbidities, and healthcare practices vary widely. This principle provides a powerful criterion for distinguishing causal from spurious patterns that hold across diverse environments and are likely causal.

The IGCL-GNN framework operationalises invariant causal learning through multi-objective optimisation that enhances causal features and suppresses environment-specific spurious patterns
Liu_2024_IGCLGNN

The key insight is that robust clinical deployment requires balancing competing objectives such as maximising predictive performance on current datasets and also ensuring generalisation to unseen environments. ;
Zheng_2024_CIGNN

The CRec (Conditional Causal Representation Learner) framework addresses the robustness of drug recommendation through disentanglement of therapeutic effects from prescription patterns
Zhang_2023_Revisiting_Drug_Causal
. For instance, proton pump inhibitors might appear beneficial for cardiovascular outcomes in observational data, not due to a therapeutic effect but due to a confounding pattern that varies across institutions, namely co-prescription with aspirin for cardioprotection. CRec separates drug molecular representations into causal therapeutic components and spurious prescription patterns through interventional disentanglement. In an analysis of 47,326 patients with multiple chronic conditions, CRec identified mechanistically synergistic drug combinations that traditional methods missed because they were rarely co-prescribed, revealing genuine therapeutic opportunities obscured by prescribing traditions
Zhang_2023_Revisiting_Drug_Causal

4
Clinical impact across domains

The translation of causal graph methods into clinical practice represents a convergence of methodological innovation with pressing healthcare needs. Success depends not only on technical sophistication, but also on the addressing of fundamental clinical questions about disease mechanisms, treatment responses, and intervention strategies.

4.1
Diagnosis: from correlation to mechanism

4.1.1
Mechanistic brain network analysis

Brain network analysis exemplifies the critical need for causal over correlational approaches. The human brain comprises approximately 86 billion neurons that form trillions of connections, creating one of the most complex networks in nature. Traditional graph-theoretical methods, while foundational, often fall short in capturing the high-dimensional and dynamic nature of brain connectivity
Mohammadi_2025_BrainSci
. The brain naturally forms a graph where regions of interest serve as nodes and functional connectivity defines edges, yet correlation-based analysis frequently identifies spurious connections driven by global brain states, arousal levels, or measurement artefacts rather than genuine neural communication pathways
Zhang_2023_CI_GNN
;
Zhang_2023_Revisiting
.

Causality-Inspired Graph Neural Networks tackle this through an interpretable architecture that fundamentally reimagines brain network analysis

;
Zheng_2024_CIGNN
. Rather than treating all connectivity equally, CI-GNN learns disentangled latent representations where only the causal factor induces the explanatory subgraph related to diagnosis. This methodology leverages conditional mutual information from a Granger causality perspective to measure causal influence, providing a principled distinction between genuine neural dysfunction and epiphenomenal changes. For major depressive disorder, CI-GNN identified strong connections around the left rectus, a region critical for the response to antidepressant treatment, while showing sparser connections in the cerebellum
Zhang_2023_CI_GNN

This finding contradicts spurious cerebellar hyperconnectivity found by association-based methods, aligning instead with clinical understanding that depression primarily involves affective and cognitive functions rather than motor function. Mechanistic insights such as these prove crucial for developing targeted interventions through neurostimulation, pharmacotherapy, or behavioural therapy.

The BPI-GNN framework advances psychiatric diagnosis through prototype learning that identifies biologically meaningful subtypes of psychiatric disorders

In major depressive disorder, different subtypes exhibited distinct patterns of response to selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors versus serotonin-norepinephrine reuptake inhibitors, suggesting that network-based biomarkers could guide personalised treatment selection. These subtypes also corresponded to differences in gene expression profiles, providing biological validation of computationally identified clusters and suggesting potential molecular targets for subtype-specific interventions
Zheng_2024_BPI

Single-omic approaches may lack the precision required to establish robust associations between molecular-level changes and phenotypic traits
Valous_2024_BrJCancer
. Tumours are characterised by genomic instability, epigenetic dysregulation, transcriptomic reprogramming, and proteomic alterations, with each layer providing complementary information about cancer biology.

Causal methodology transforms cancer classification by focusing on causally related genes rather than on all differentially expressed features. The CautionGCN framework employs a causal multi-head autoencoder for causal feature selection, isolating characteristic genes with genuine causal relationships to cancer subtypes
Wang_2024_CautionGCN
. This approach addresses a fundamental problem in cancer genomics, where thousands of genes show differential expression, but only a subset causally drives tumorigenesis. By constructing causal gene-gene graphs that capture true driver relationships, MoCaGCN achieves a superior classification using only 89 genes compared to more than 1,000 in traditional approaches
Li_2024_MoCaGCN
. The Multi-omics Causal Graph Convolutional Network for lung adenocarcinoma classification incorporates causal structure learning algorithms combined with prior knowledge from gene-gene interaction databases, improving performance and generalisation by considering established biological interactions.

The identified causal genes provide more than classification accuracy. In lung adenocarcinoma, MoCaGCN identified KRAS-driven metabolic reprogramming as a causal mechanism of immune evasion, suggesting combination therapies targeting both KRAS and metabolic pathways
Li_2024_MoCaGCN

This approach recognises that different omics layers may capture distinct aspects of cancer biology, with genomics revealing mutational drivers, transcriptomics showing pathway activation, and proteomics indicating functional consequences. The clinical value extends beyond improved diagnostic accuracy to mechanistically informed treatment planning. Understanding which molecular alterations causally drive disease progression, rather than merely correlating with it, enables the rational design of targeted therapies and the identification of vulnerabilities that are amenable to pharmacological intervention. The shift from correlation-based feature selection to causal driver identification represents a fundamental advance in precision oncology.

4.2
Prognosis: predicting what interventions prevent

Prognostic models traditionally predict outcomes based on baseline characteristics, yet clinical utility requires understanding which interventions might prevent adverse events. This understanding of the causal chain enables clinicians to implement preventive measures targeting the root causes rather than managing complications reactively.

Yuan_2024_KAMLN

The interpretability of the model, showing not only risk, but also causal pathways, helped clinicians understand and trust the predictions, which proved crucial for clinical adoption. The explicit modelling of causal dependencies between complications has important equity implications beyond predictive performance. Patients in underserved communities, who often have more advanced disease states, benefit disproportionately from early identification of causal chains. In the cohort of lung cancer surgery, patients identified as high-risk 72 hours earlier by causal modelling received prophylactic interventions that reduced complication rates by 23%, with greater reductions observed in demographic subgroups historically experiencing higher complication rates
Yuan_2024_KAMLN

Treatment selection: mechanism-based therapy

These mechanistic insights enable the rational design of combination therapies, accelerating the path from target identification to clinical translation. The clinical workflow transforms from ”drug prescribed for patients like you” to ”drug targeting your causal mechanisms,” representing a fundamental shift toward mechanism-based precision medicine.

4.4
Continuous monitoring: real-time causal inference

Biomedical monitoring generates vast streams of temporal data where the distinction of causal signals from noise is essential for clinical decision-making. The challenge becomes particularly acute in intensive care settings where hundreds of variables are monitored continuously, creating high-dimensional time series with complex interdependencies. Traditional monitoring systems generate frequent false alarms, with studies showing that up to 88% of ICU alarms are clinically irrelevant, leading to alarm fatigue and potentially missed critical events.

The CiGNN framework for cuffless blood pressure estimation exemplifies how causal methods transform physiological monitoring

This causal feature selection reduces dimensionality from more than 200 potential features to 23 causal indicators, dramatically improving both computational efficiency and physiological interpretability. The causal relationships discovered provide physiological insights beyond prediction. CiGNN identified that specific PPG morphological features causally influence blood pressure through arterial stiffness mechanisms, while certain heart rate variability patterns affect blood pressure through autonomic regulation. These mechanistic insights enable personalised monitoring strategies in which patients with autonomic dysfunction require different monitoring parameters than those with primarily vascular pathology.

In spatiotemporal time series imputation, critical for handling missing data in clinical monitoring, the Casper framework addresses a fundamental challenge of unknown confounders that create non-causal shortcut backdoor paths

Jing_2024_Casper
. In multi-site ICU data, Casper achieved 94% accuracy in imputing missing values compared to 76% for traditional methods, while maintaining causal validity verified through intervention studies. This improvement translates directly to clinical outcomes through more accurate imputation, thereby improving the prediction of deterioration and reducing unnecessary interventions triggered by imputation errors.

In sepsis monitoring, REASON identified how initial inflammatory responses cascade through the cardiovascular, respiratory, and renal systems, enabling early intervention before multi-organ failure develops. This system-level causal analysis represents a paradigm shift from monitoring individual parameters to understanding how perturbations propagate through coupled biological networks, enabling proactive rather than reactive clinical management.

5.2

The integration of large language models with causal GNNs creates a powerful hybrid architecture addressing complementary aspects of clinical reasoning. ;

The synergistic workflow proceeds through hypothesis generation followed by mechanistic validation. An LLM continuously monitors a patient’s electronic health record, parsing clinical notes, laboratory results, medication orders, and vital signs. When the system detects a prescribed drug that coincides temporally with emerging adverse symptoms, the LLM proposes a causal hypothesis linking drug exposure to observed effects. This hypothesis, expressed as a potential causal pathway, is then submitted to a Causal GNN that accesses protein-protein interaction networks capturing drug-target relationships and off-target effects, pharmacovigilance databases containing population-scale adverse event reports, and the patient’s genomic data revealing drug metabolism variants. The synergy proves particularly powerful for pharmacovigilance, where novel adverse drug reactions often first appear in clinical narratives before accumulating sufficient structured reports for traditional signal detection. 5.3

;

. .

6
Challenges and path forward

6.1
Computational reality

Algorithmic innovations targeting biological structure offer promising solutions. Hierarchical causal discovery methods exploit the modular organisation of biological systems, first identifying causal relationships between subsystems such as organ networks or cellular pathways, then refining the structure within the module
Wang_2023_Hierarchical
. This approach dramatically reduces computational complexity by leveraging natural sparsity patterns where most biological components interact locally within functional modules rather than exhibiting all-to-all connectivity. The REASON framework demonstrates feasibility by modelling both intra-level and inter-level causal relationships, enabling system-level analysis of cascading failures in complex physiological networks
Wang_2023_Hierarchical
. Amortised inference represents another transformative direction, where models are trained once on diverse datasets and then perform rapid inference on new patients. Rather than discovering the de novo causal structure for each patient, the system learns to predict the causal relationships from the observed data through end-to-end differentiable architectures
Behnam_2025_ECCV
. This enables real-time causal inference, which is feasible for clinical decision support.

Hardware acceleration through neuromorphic computing and graph-specific processors offers complementary advances. Graph neural network operations exhibit inherent parallelism amenable to specialised hardware, with emerging architectures achieving orders-of-magnitude speedups for message-passing computations

6.2
The validation crisis

Establishing trustworthy causal conclusions from observational healthcare data constitutes perhaps the most fundamental challenge facing the field. Cross-validation and held-out test sets effectively evaluate predictive performance, but provide insufficient evidence to validate causal claims
Feuerriegel_2024_Causal_ML
;
Pearl_2009_Causality
. The core problem is that standard machine learning evaluation metrics, including accuracy, area under the curve, and calibration measure association rather than causation. A model can achieve excellent predictive performance while encoding spurious correlations that would fail under intervention
Subbaswamy_2019_From_Development
;
Peters_2016_Causal_Inference
. Ground truth causal structures remain unknown for most biomedical systems, precluding direct validation of learnt causal graphs against known mechanisms.

The validation crisis (Figure
2
B) manifests itself in the systematic dissociation of the association-intervention: the methods clustered in the high-correlation, low-intervention quadrant achieve impressive retrospective metrics while failing the prospective deployment. This “causal gap” quantifies the expected performance loss when correlation-based models are applied to interventional queries, validating the need for validation frameworks extending beyond cross-validation to natural experiments, propensity-matched analyses, and prospective intervention studies
feuerriegel2024causal
;
hernan2016using
.

Multi-modal evidence triangulation provides a rigorous framework for establishing causal validity through convergent evidence across complementary modalities
Lawlor_2016_Triangulation
;
Feuerriegel_2024_Causal_ML
. Biological plausibility assessment evaluates whether identified causal relationships align with established pathophysiological mechanisms and biochemical pathways, providing mechanistic coherence testing against decades of accumulated biomedical knowledge
Hill_1965_Environment
. The MoCaGCN framework demonstrates this approach by comparing learnt causal gene graphs against experimentally validated oncogenic pathways, finding that 73% of identified driver genes had prior experimental validation in the COSMIC database while 89% of edges connected genes within established signalling cascades
Li_2024_MoCaGCN
. Replication among independent cohorts tests whether causal relationships remain stable across diverse populations, healthcare systems, and temporal periods. The CiGNN blood pressure monitoring framework achieved replication of 19 of 23 causally identified features across three independent datasets that span different continents and clinical contexts, with prioritised replicated features for the final prediction models
Ding_2024_CiGNN
.

Natural experiments and quasi-experimental designs provide powerful causal validation by exploiting situations where treatment assignment approximates randomisation through exogenous factors
Dunning_2012_Natural_Experiments
;
Hernan_2016_Using_Big_Data
. Policy discontinuities, instrumental variables, and regression discontinuity designs enable the estimation of causal effects with minimal confounding, serving as benchmarks against which observational causal models can be validated. Prospective intervention studies, when feasible, constitute the gold standard by testing whether the predicted intervention outcomes materialise under actual treatment
Feuerriegel_2024_Causal_ML
. Sensitivity analyses quantify robustness to violations of causal assumptions, particularly unmeasured confounding, through E-value calculations and model specification uncertainty assessment
VanderWeele_2017_Sensitivity
;
Cinelli_2020_Making_Sense
.

We propose tiered evidentiary standards that distinguish the strength of causal evidence. Tier 1 encompasses causally-inspired methods that employ causal concepts in architectural design but make no explicit causal claims about learnt relationships, such as invariance-based regularisation promoting features stable across environments
Arjovsky_2019_Invariant
. Tier 2 includes causally-structured methods that learn causal structures or estimate causal effects validated through consistency with external knowledge rather than novel discovery, exemplified by cancer subtyping models recovering known oncogenic pathways

. Tier 3 represents causally-validated methods that claim discovery of previously unknown causal relationships, requiring comprehensive triangulation across biological plausibility with the proposed mechanism, replication across two or more independent cohorts, validation of natural experiments or prospective intervention studies, sensitivity analysis demonstrating robustness with E-values exceeding 2.0 for strong claims, and aggregate evidence scores exceeding 0.6 using quantitative integration frameworks
Lawlor_2016_Triangulation
.

6.3
From methods to clinical practice

The gap between methodological sophistication and clinical interpretability poses critical barriers to deployment. Clinicians require not only predictions but also mechanistic explanations that they can understand, verify against domain expertise, and integrate with clinical reasoning
Murdoch_2021_Definitions
. A significant challenge in the implementation of GNNs for biomarker discovery involves ensuring the interpretability and clinical relevance of identified patterns
Mohammadi_2025_BrainSci
. Computational representations learnt by neural networks often lack direct correspondence to clinical concepts, necessitating translation frameworks that bridge this semantic gap.

Integration of domain knowledge into model design represents a promising direction. Rather than learning causal structures purely from data, hybrid approaches incorporate established biological mechanisms as structural priors, constraining model architectures according to known pathway relationships
Li_2024_MoCaGCN
. The MoCaGCN framework demonstrates this by integrating gene-gene interaction networks from curated databases as graph topology priors, ensuring that learnt causal relationships respect established biological constraints while remaining flexible enough to discover novel mechanisms. Explainability frameworks must translate learnt causal representations into clinically meaningful concepts. The CI-GNN architecture achieves this by identifying sparse subgraphs corresponding to specific disease mechanisms, where highlighted regions and connections of the brain are directly linked to established neural circuits known to clinicians
Zhang_2023_CI_GNN
;
Zheng_2024_CIGNN
.

Clinician-in-the-loop validation provides essential reality checking where domain experts evaluate whether computationally identified causal mechanisms align with clinical understanding and biological plausibility. This iterative refinement process, combining data-driven discovery with expert knowledge, proves critical for building clinical trust. Regulatory pathways for causal claims from observational data remain underdeveloped. The FDA has established frameworks for predictive algorithms but lacks specific guidance for systems making interventional or counterfactual claims based on causal inference from non-randomised data
FDA_2021_AI
. Developing appropriate oversight requires balancing innovation incentives with patient safety, establishing evidentiary standards sufficient to support causal claims without demanding infeasible validation burdens.

The risk of ”causal-washing”, where methods employ causal terminology without meeting rigorous evidentiary standards for causal claims, threatens field credibility. The appeal of causal framing is understandable given superior theoretical properties and alignment with clinical reasoning, yet the gap between causal aspiration and causal validation remains substantial across much of the literature. Several patterns characterise causal-washing in current practice. Terminological overreach occurs when methods use causal vocabulary, including ”causal representation,” ”causal discovery,” or ”causal reasoning,” while relying fundamentally on associational quantification without estimating interventional distributions or testing identifiability conditions
Pearl_2009_Causality
;
Peters_2017_Elements
. Insufficient validation arises when causal claims rest solely on predictive performance metrics without triangulating evidence from biological plausibility, replication, natural experiments, or sensitivity analyses. Assumption elision involves implementing causal methods while omitting explicit statements of the requisite assumptions, including faithfulness, causal sufficiency, and positivity, preventing the assessment of validity
Schoelkopf_2021_Toward
;
Peters_2016_Causal_Inference
.

Community-level practices can mitigate causal-washing through standardised reporting guidelines analogous to CONSORT for randomised trials or STROBE for observational studies
Vandenbroucke_2016_Strengthening
. Such guidelines should require an explicit statement of all causal assumptions with directed acyclic graphs when applicable, a specification of which causal quantities are being estimated, including the average treatment effect or conditional average treatment effect, reporting of sensitivity analyses quantifying robustness to assumption violations, and a clear demarcation between causal claims versus associational findings
Hernan_2020_Causal
. The transition from causal-washing to rigorous causal inference requires cultural change alongside methodological standards, where funding agencies prioritise validation studies over novel method proliferation, peer review demands explicit justification for causal terminology and rejects unsupported claims, and graduate training emphasises distinctions between causal inference, prediction, and description.

Conclusion

The integration of causal inference with graph neural networks addresses a fundamental limitation in healthcare artificial intelligence. The documented triple crisis of distribution shift that causes performance collapse across institutions, perpetuation of discriminatory patterns embedded in historical data, and lack of mechanistic interpretability that prevents clinical verification demand a paradigm change from correlation-based prediction to mechanism-based understanding

;
Futoma_2020_Myth
;
Beede_2020_Human
. This review has demonstrated that causality-inspired architectures, when rigorously implemented and validated, achieve superior generalisation across healthcare settings, identify biologically plausible mechanisms aligned with established pathophysiology, and maintain fairness properties under distribution shift through learning of invariant causal relationships rather than site-specific correlations

;
Zheng_2024_CIGNN
;

.

Applications spanning psychiatric subtyping, cancer molecular characterisation, physiological monitoring, and drug discovery validate this approach across diverse clinical domains. . Multi-omics causal integration identified 89 driver genes from thousands of correlational features, accelerating precision oncology

. Causal feature selection transformed continuous monitoring from high-dimensional noise to mechanistically interpretable signals
Ding_2024_CiGNN
. Treatment recommendation systems that correct for prescription bias achieved substantial outcome improvements in historically underserved populations
He_2023_Pretraining
. The methodological foundations for patient-specific Causal Digital Twins now exist, enabling in silico clinical experimentation before therapeutic intervention, with synergistic integration of large language models for hypothesis generation and causal graph neural networks for mechanistic validation, positioning the field at the forefront of multimodal artificial intelligence

. The field faces a growing risk of causal-washing, where methods employ causal terminology without meeting rigorous evidentiary standards, threatening scientific credibility and clinical trust. Tiered frameworks distinguishing causally-inspired architectures from causally-structured models and causally-validated discoveries prove essential for maintaining appropriate epistemic humility while enabling continued innovation.

Healthcare artificial intelligence is at an inflexion point. The question is no longer whether machine learning can achieve impressive metrics in retrospective datasets but whether systems can survive deployment across diverse clinical contexts while maintaining effectiveness, equity, and interpretability. Causal graph neural networks represent the only architectural paradigm that simultaneously addresses robustness through invariant learning, fairness through path-specific interventional constraints, and mechanistic understanding through explicit causal structure

The transition from correlation-based prediction to mechanism-based medicine will define which artificial intelligence systems earn clinical trust and achieve sustainable deployment. This transformation requires balancing theoretical ambition with empirical humility, acknowledging that we construct pragmatic causal approximations that support clinical inferences at chosen levels of abstraction rather than discovering absolute causal truth in biological systems of extraordinary complexity. It demands computational sophistication matched with clinical interpretability, ensuring that mechanistic insights align with established biomedical knowledge and can be verified by domain experts. Most critically, it necessitates a transformative vision tempered by uncompromising validation standards, where novel causal discoveries undergo rigorous multi-modal triangulation before informing high-stakes clinical decisions. The methodological foundations are established through theoretical advances connecting structural causal models to differentiable graph architectures, empirical demonstrations across diverse healthcare applications, and emerging frameworks for validation under observational constraints

The path forward demands not only technical innovation, but sustained collaboration between computational scientists developing causal inference methods, clinicians defining requirements and validation criteria, biomedical researchers providing mechanistic knowledge, and policymakers establishing appropriate oversight. The convergence of multi-modal biomedical data accumulation, regulatory demands for explainable artificial intelligence, and documented failures of correlation-based models under distribution shift creates both opportunity and urgency. Translation to clinical practice would enable healthcare systems to identify causal mechanisms rather than mere associations, predict interventional outcomes rather than observational patterns, and generate patient-specific counterfactuals that enable truly personalised medicine. This vision warrants continued investment provided that we maintain rigorous standards for genuine causal inference in complex biological systems, distinguish mechanistic understanding from pattern recognition, and prioritise patient outcomes over algorithmic sophistication.

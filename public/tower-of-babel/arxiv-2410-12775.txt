More Nonlinearities? Electromagnetic and Gravitational Mode Mixing in NSBH Mergers
Fawzi Aly 1, Mahmoud A. Mansour 2, Dejan Stojkovic 1

Abstract
We investigate the possibility of electromagnetic fields leaving imprints on gravitational wave (GW) signals from Neutron Star-Black hole (NSBH) mergers, specifically in the context of extreme mass ratio inspirals (EMRIs). Using black hole perturbation theory (BHPT) in the context of a minimally coupled Einstein-Maxwell system, we demonstrate that electromagnetic quasi normal modes (QNMs) can excite gravitational QNMs with frequencies that are linear or quadratic in the electromagnetic QNMs, at first level of mixing. Moreover, We then study the electromagnetism-gravity coupling by approximating the Regge-Wheeler and Zerilli potentials with Dirac delta functions. In this example, we examine gravitational perturbations induced by the electromagnetic field of an ideal dipole radially free fall towards the blackhole, building on calculations from a companion paper [ 1 ] . Our results show that both linear and quadratic electromagnetic QNMs appear in gravitational perturbations. In addition, linear gravitational QNMs are also excited due to the electromagnetic source, with their amplitudes depending on the details of the electromagnetic and gravitational potentials, analogous to gravitational mode mixing analysis. Furthermore, at late stages, gravitational perturbations might exhibit polynomial tails induced by electromagnetic perturbations. This article sets the stage for future numerical investigations aimed at identifying such modes in various scenarios.

IV
Conclusion and Future Outlook

The main purpose of this work was to expand and refine theoretical predictions for the GW signals by taking into account the effects of the strong electromagnetic fields during an inspiral, collapse or a merger phase of massive objects. A simple order of magnitude estimate suggests that the effects of gravitational and electromagnetic mode mixing could be of some importance for a merger of a NS with a SMBH.

Using BHPT, we investigated the effect of the electromagnetic coupling on the QNMs of the system. In section
II.2
, we showed that, within the minimally coupled Einstein-Maxwell theory, the gravitational QNMs spectrum will expand to include both the linear and quadratic electromagnetic QNMs. Moreover, at higher order in PT, we expect higher level of mixing including having modes quadratic in electromagnetic and gravitational modes similar to the ones discussed in
[
1
]
and mentioned in section
below.

We recast the electromagnetic stress-energy tensor to align with the Regge-Wheeler spherical decomposition by projecting it on the sphere following
[
30
]
. Subsequently, this simplify the identification of which pairs of the angular modes of electromagnetic
(l_{1},m_{1})
and
(l_{2},m_{2})
modes will source a gravitational mode
(l,m)
.

In section
III
, to illustrate and build intuition about the effects of electromagnetic-gravitational coupling, we replaced the actual potentials in the Regge-Wheeler and Zerilli equations, which describe the two degrees of freedom for each perturbation field, with a Dirac delta function potential. This situation is easier to handle analytically, since it results in only one gravitational, one electromagnetic, and one quadratic gravitational QNM. These simplifications allow to perform the calculations for the GEM modes semi-analytically.

We used the approximate formula for the electromagnetic perturbation caused by an ideal dipole radially falling towards a black hole in the Dirac delta function potential, reported in detail in
[
1
]
and summarized in subsection
III.2
for completeness. We then used these perturbations to compute the induced GEM modes from various samples of the electromagnetic source in the gravitational perturbation equation. As anticipated, both linear and quadratic electromagnetic QNMs frequencies showed up in induced gravitational QNMs. Additionally, as discussed in section
II.2
, the linear gravitational modes exhibit amplitudes that generally depend on the details of both the electromagnetic and gravitational potentials. Furthermore, at the late stages of the signal, we found that the gravitational perturbation exhibits polynomial tails due to the electromagnetic perturbation, similar to the findings in
[
42
]
for gravitational-gravitational coupling.

LISA will focus on the inspiral phase of GW signals, in contrast to LIGO, which concentrates more on the ringdown phase. Extending this investigation to search for EM frequencies embedded within the EMRI signal requires a slightly different approach. For example, considering a charged perturber of mass
m
and charge
q
circling around a supermassive black hole of mass
M
, we can analytically model the inspiral phase using BHPT. This allows us to identify regions in the parameter space
(q,m,M)
, where GG modes and GEM modes are of comparable magnitude,

{}_{GG}\,\psi_{e\,lm}^{(2)}\sim{}_{EM}\,\psi_{e\,lm}^{(2)}.

A comparison between
{}_{G}S_{EM}
and
{}_{G}S_{GG}
will be important in finding out whether this region overlaps with LISA’s observation window, indicating the relevance of such events. Consequently, when
{}_{EM}\,\psi_{e\,lm}^{(2)}
should not be ignored when modeling the inspiral signal. However, handling stellar-mass mergers presents more challenges, as the remnant can only be described using PT in the late stages. In such cases, a numerical approach may provide more insight, such as fitting waveforms from numerical simulations or using numerical guidance to model the initial conditions. We will attempt further investigation related to those directions in future work, building on the foundation laid in this paper.

Additionally, in
[
1
]
, the authors speculated that at higher orders of electromagnetic perturbations
\Phi^{(j)},j\geq 2
, gravitational perturbations will also contribute to exciting higher-order electromagnetic modes. At second-order electromagnetic QNMs, for instance, stronger coupling between the two fields, akin to what occurs in the gravitational case but in higher orders can take place. In particular, if a linear gravitational QNM has frequency
\omega^{(1)}_{i}
and a linear electromagnetic QNM has frequency
\Omega^{(1)}_{j}
, the second-order electromagnetic spectrum will feature mixed gravitational-electromagnetic modes with frequencies
\Omega^{(2)}_{i}=\Omega^{(1)}_{j}\pm\omega^{(1)}_{k}
. However, purely quadratic electromagnetic modes will not be present at that order in contrast with gravitational case at its simplest level of coupling with electromagnetic QNMs.

Since gravitational effects are expected to dominate in astrophysical scenarios, this suggests that gravitational imprints in electromagnetic signals should be more pronounced compared to the other way around. Yet, both phenomena can serve the same purpose: testing minimal coupling in GR or more complex couplings in modified gravity theories. Plausibly, if both effects are significant, it could offer a key opportunity for multi-messenger astronomy.

which define scalar, vectors, and (0,2)-tensors on
S_{2}
.

An important note here which we are going to make use of when evaluating integrals, is that the vector harmonics defined in (D6) correspond to the usual ones defined in Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}_{3}
by

\displaystyle\mathbf{Z}^{lm}=r\nabla Y^{lm}(\theta,\phi)

(73)

\displaystyle\mathbf{X}^{lm}=\mathbf{r}\times\nabla Y^{lm}(\theta,\phi)

albeit for the normalisation of the unit vectors on the sphere by the metric
\Omega_{AB}
. In other words, we have the same dot products

\displaystyle\mathbf{Z}^{lm}\cdot\mathbf{Z}^{l^{\prime}m^{\prime}}=\Omega^{AB}Z_{A}^{lm}Z_{B}^{l^{\prime}m^{\prime}}

(74)

\displaystyle\mathbf{X}^{lm}\cdot\mathbf{X}^{l^{\prime}m^{\prime}}=\Omega^{AB}X_{A}^{lm}X_{B}^{l^{\prime}m^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\mathbf{Z}^{lm}\cdot\mathbf{X}^{l^{\prime}m^{\prime}}=\Omega^{AB}Z_{A}^{lm}X_{B}^{l^{\prime}m^{\prime}}.

The vector harmonics defined in (D6) can be expressed in terms of the basis vector functions
\mathcal{Y}^{l}_{jm}(\theta,\phi)
of the tensor product representation
D^{l}\otimes D^{1}
of the rotation group in three dimensions given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{Y}^{l}_{jm}(\theta,\phi)=\sum_{r=-l}^{r=l}\sum_{q=-1}^{q=1}(l,r;1,q|jm)Y^{lr}(\theta,\phi)\mathbf{e}_{q}

(75)

where
\mathbf{e}_{q}
are the spherical vectors given by

\displaystyle\mathbf{e}_{\pm 1}=\frac{\mp 1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\mathbf{\hat{x}}\pm i\mathbf{\hat{y}}\right)

(76)

\displaystyle\mathbf{e}_{0}=\mathbf{\hat{z}},

as

\displaystyle\mathbf{Z}^{lm}=(l+1)\sqrt{\frac{l}{2l+1}}\mathcal{Y}^{l}_{l-1,m}+l\sqrt{\frac{l+1}{2l+1}}\mathcal{Y}^{l}_{l+1,m}

(77)

\displaystyle\mathbf{X}^{lm}=i\sqrt{l(l+1)}\mathcal{Y}^{l}_{lm}

which can be expanded in the vector basis
\mathbf{e}_{q}
with components of spherical harmonic functions multiplied by numerical factors using (D10).

Appendix D
Useful Spherical Integrals

Given a gravitational mode
(l,m)
in the Regge-Wheeler/Zerilli-Moncrief equation, the electromagnetic source term that corresponds to the harmonic component of the source term of the same angular number
(l,m)
is the projection on regular, vector, and tensor spherical harmonics of quadratic terms that are composed of two angular harmonic numbers
(l_{1},m_{1})
and
(l_{2},m_{2})
. Depending on which electromagnetic degrees of freedom, either of odd parity or even parity - see the next appendix, the type of coupling is different. Such two different modes of coupling is expressed in terms of the following two integrals

\displaystyle\int_{S_{2}}Z^{l_{1}m_{1}\;A}X^{l_{2}m_{2}}_{A}Y^{l_{3}m_{3}}d\Omega=A(l_{1},m_{1};l_{2},m_{2};l_{3},m_{3})

(78)

\displaystyle\int_{S_{2}}Y^{l_{1}m_{1}}Y^{l_{2}m_{2}}Y^{l_{3}m_{3}}d\Omega=B(l_{1},m_{1};l_{2},m_{2};l_{3},m_{3}),

where the first one is the coupling that comes from mixing odd and even degrees of freedom and the second appears for terms involving only terms of the same parity.

To evaluate the first integral, we compute the contraction of the two vector harmonics

\displaystyle Z^{l_{1}m_{1}\;A}X^{l_{2}m_{2}}_{A}=\Bigg(\frac{i}{2}(l_{1}+1)\sqrt{\frac{(l_{1}-m_{1}-1)(l_{1}+m_{1}+1)(l_{2}-m_{2}+1)(l_{2}+m_{2})}{(2l_{1}-1)(2l_{1}+1)}}Y^{l_{2},m_{2}-1}Y^{l_{1}-1,m_{1}+1}-

(79)

\displaystyle\frac{i}{2}(l_{1}+1)\sqrt{\frac{(l_{1}+m_{1}-1)(l_{1}+m_{1})(l_{2}-m_{2})(l_{2}+m_{2}+1)}{(2l_{1}-1)(2l_{1}+1)}}Y^{l_{2},m_{2}+1}Y^{l_{1}-1,m_{1}-1}+im_{2}(l_{1}+1)

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{(l_{1}-m_{1})(l_{1}+m_{1})}{(2l_{1}-1)(2l_{1}+1)}}Y^{l_{2}m_{2}}Y^{l_{1}-1,m_{1}}\Bigg)+\Bigg(\frac{i}{2}l_{1}\sqrt{\frac{(l_{1}+m_{1}+1)(l_{1}+m_{1}+2)(l_{2}-m_{2}+1)(l_{2}+m_{2})}{(2l_{1}+1)(2l_{1}+3)}}

\displaystyle Y^{l_{2},m_{2}-1}Y^{l_{1}+1,m_{1}+1}-\frac{i}{2}l_{1}\sqrt{\frac{(l_{1}-m_{1}+1)(l_{1}-m_{1}+2)(l_{2}+m_{2}+1)(l_{2}-m_{2})}{(2l_{1}+1)(2l_{1}+3)}}Y^{l_{2},m_{2}+1}Y^{l_{1}+1,m_{1}-1}

\displaystyle-im_{2}l_{1}\sqrt{\frac{(l_{1}-m_{1}+1)(l_{1}+m_{1}+1)}{(2l_{1}-1)(2l_{1}+1)}}Y^{l_{2}m_{2}}Y^{l_{1}+1,m_{1}}\Bigg).

Thus, the integral expressed in terms of
B(l_{1},m_{1};l_{2},m_{2};l_{3},m_{3})
becomes

\displaystyle\int_{S_{2}}Z^{l_{1}m_{1}\;A}X^{l_{2}m_{2}}_{A}Y^{l_{3}m_{3}}d\Omega=\Bigg(\frac{i}{2}(l_{1}+1)\sqrt{\frac{(l_{1}-m_{1}-1)(l_{1}+m_{1}+1)(l_{2}-m_{2}+1)(l_{2}+m_{2})}{(2l_{1}-1)(2l_{1}+1)}}

(80)

\displaystyle B(l_{2},m_{2}-1;l_{1}-1,m_{1}+1;l,m)-\frac{i}{2}(l_{1}+1)\sqrt{\frac{(l_{1}+m_{1}-1)(l_{1}+m_{1})(l_{2}-m_{2})(l_{2}+m_{2}+1)}{(2l_{1}-1)(2l_{1}+1)}}

\displaystyle B(l_{2},m_{2}+1;l_{1}-1,m_{1}-1;l,m)+im_{2}(l_{1}+1)\sqrt{\frac{(l_{1}-m_{1})(l_{1}+m_{1})}{(2l_{1}-1)(2l_{1}+1)}}B(l_{2}m_{2};l_{1}-1,m_{1};l,m)\Bigg)+

\displaystyle\Bigg(\frac{i}{2}l_{1}\sqrt{\frac{(l_{1}+m_{1}+1)(l_{1}+m_{1}+2)(l_{2}-m_{2}+1)(l_{2}+m_{2})}{(2l_{1}+1)(2l_{1}+3)}}B(l_{2},m_{2}-1;l_{1}+1,m_{1}+1;l,m)-\frac{i}{2}l_{1}

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{(l_{1}-m_{1}+1)(l_{1}-m_{1}+2)(l_{2}+m_{2}+1)(l_{2}-m_{2})}{(2l_{1}+1)(2l_{1}+3)}}B(l_{2},m_{2}+1;l_{1}+1,m_{1}-1;l,m)

\displaystyle-im_{2}l_{1}\sqrt{\frac{(l_{1}-m_{1}+1)(l_{1}+m_{1}+1)}{(2l_{1}-1)(2l_{1}+1)}}B(l_{2}m_{2};l_{1}+1,m_{1};l,m)\Bigg).

To evaluate the required integrals we use the following identities:

\displaystyle X^{lm}_{[A|B]}=\frac{1}{2}l(l+1)Y^{lm}\epsilon_{AB}

(81)

\displaystyle X_{A}^{l_{1}m_{1}}X^{l_{2}m_{2}A}=Z_{A}^{l_{1}m_{1}}Z^{l_{2}m_{2}A}

\displaystyle X^{l_{2}m_{2}A}Z^{l_{1}m_{1}}_{A}=-X^{l_{1}m_{1}A}Z^{l_{2}m_{2}}_{A}

Now, we evaluated the following integrals which are needed in the re-casting of the electromagentic energy-momentum tensor:

I.

\displaystyle\int Z^{l_{1}m_{1}A}Z^{l_{2}m_{2}}_{A}Y^{l_{3}m_{3}}d\Omega=\frac{1}{2}\int\Omega^{AB}\nabla_{A}\nabla_{B}\left(Y^{l_{2}m_{2}}Y^{l_{1}m_{1}}\right)Y^{l_{3}m_{3}}d\Omega

(82)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\left[l_{1}\left(l_{1}+1\right)+l_{2}\left(l_{2}+1\right)\right]\int Y^{l_{m_{1}}}Y^{l_{2}m_{2}}Y^{l_{3}m_{3}}d\Omega

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left[l_{1}\left(l_{1}+1\right)+l_{2}\left(l_{2}+1\right)-l_{3}\left(l_{3}+1\right)\right]\int Y^{l_{1}m_{1}}Y^{l_{2}m_{2}}Y^{l_{3}m_{3}}d\Omega

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left[l_{1}\left(l_{1}+1\right)+l_{2}\left(l_{2}+1\right)-l_{3}\left(l_{3}+1\right)\right]B(l_{1},m_{1};l_{2},m_{2};l_{3},m_{3}).

II.

\displaystyle\int Z^{l_{1}m_{1}A}Z^{l_{2}m_{2}B}Z_{A|B}^{l_{3}m_{3}}d\Omega

(83)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\left[\int\nabla_{A}\left(Z^{l_{1}m_{1}A}Z^{l_{2}m_{2}B}\right)Z_{B}^{l_{3}m_{3}}d\Omega+\int\nabla_{B}\left(Z^{l_{1}m_{1}A}Z^{l_{2}m_{2}B}\right)Z_{A}^{l_{3}m_{3}}d\Omega\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}l_{1}(l_{1}+1)\int Y^{l_{1}m_{1}}Z^{l_{2}m_{2}A}Z_{A}^{l_{3}m_{3}}d\Omega+\frac{1}{2}l_{2}(l_{2}+1)\int Y^{l_{2}m_{2}}Z^{l_{1}m_{1}A}Z^{l_{3}m_{3}}_{A}d\Omega

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\int\left(Z^{l_{1}m_{1}}_{A|B}Z^{l_{2}m_{2}B}Z^{l_{3}m_{3}A}+Z^{l_{1}m_{1}B}Z^{l_{2}m_{2}}_{B\mid A}Z^{l_{3}m_{3}A}\right)d\Omega.

But,

\displaystyle\int\left(Z^{l_{1}m_{1}}_{A|B}Z^{l_{2}m_{2}B}Z^{l_{3}m_{3}A}+Z^{l_{1}m_{1}B}Z^{l_{2}m_{2}}_{B\mid A}Z^{l_{3}m_{3}A}\right)d\Omega

(84)

\displaystyle=\int\left(Z^{l_{1}m_{1}}_{B\mid A}Z^{l_{1}m_{2}B}+Z^{l_{1}m_{1}B}Z_{B\mid A}^{l_{2}m_{2}}\right)Z^{l_{3}m_{3}A}d\Omega

Thus, we have

\displaystyle\int Z^{l_{1}m_{1}A}Z^{l_{2}m_{2}B}Z_{A\mid B}^{l_{3}m_{3}}d\Omega=\frac{1}{4}\Bigg(l_{1}(l_{1}+1)\Big[l_{2}(l_{2}+1)+l_{3}(l_{3}+1)-l_{1}(l_{1}+1)\Big]

(85)

\displaystyle+l_{2}(l_{2}+1)\Big[l_{1}(l_{1}+1)+l_{3}(l_{3}+1)-l_{2}(l_{2}+1)\Big]-l_{3}(l_{3}+1)\Big[l_{1}(l_{1}+1)+l_{2}(l_{2}+1)-l_{3}(l_{3}+1)\Big]\Bigg)

\displaystyle B(l_{1},m_{1};l_{2},m_{2};l_{3},m_{3})=\Lambda(l_{1},l_{2},l_{3})B(l_{1},m_{1};l_{2},m_{2};l_{3},m_{3}).

III.

\int X^{l_{1}m_{1}A}X^{l_{2}m_{2}B}Z^{l_{3}m_{3}}_{A\mid B}d\Omega

(86)

To evaluate this, we use the following three identities:

\displaystyle\int X^{l_{1}m_{1}A}X^{l_{2}m_{2}}_{B\mid A}Z^{l_{3}m_{3}B}d\Omega=-\int X^{l_{1}m_{1}A}X^{l_{2}m_{2}B}Z^{l_{3}m_{3}}_{A\mid B}d\Omega,

(87)

\displaystyle\int X^{l_{1}m_{1}A}X^{l_{2}m_{2}}_{A\mid B}Z^{l_{3}m_{3}B}d\Omega=2\int X^{l_{1}m_{1}A}X^{l_{2}m_{2}}_{[A\mid B]}Z^{l_{3}m_{3}B}d\Omega+

(88)

\displaystyle\int X^{l_{1}m_{1}A}X^{l_{2}m_{2}}_{B\mid A}Z^{l_{3}m_{3}B}d\Omega,

and

\displaystyle\int\left(X^{l_{1}m_{1}A}X^{l_{2}m_{2}}_{A\mid B}Z^{l_{3}m_{3}B}+X^{l_{1}m_{1}}_{A\mid B}X^{l_{2}m_{2}B}Z^{l_{3}m_{3}B}\right)d\Omega

(89)

\displaystyle=\int\nabla_{B}\left(X^{l_{1}m_{1}A}X^{l_{2}m_{2}}_{A}\right)Z^{l_{3}m_{3}B}d\Omega=l_{3}(l_{3}+1)\int X^{l_{1}m_{1}A}X^{l_{2}m_{2}}_{A}Y^{l_{3}m_{3}}d\Omega.

(90)

\displaystyle=2\int\left(X^{l_{1}m_{1}A}X^{l_{2}m_{2}}_{[A\mid B]}+X^{l_{1}m_{1}}_{[A\mid B]}X^{l_{2}m_{2}A}\right)Z^{l_{3}m_{3}B}d\Omega

\displaystyle+\int\left(X^{l_{1}m_{1}}_{B\mid A}X^{l_{2}m_{2}A}Z^{l_{3}m_{3}B}+X^{l_{1}m_{1}A}X^{l_{2}m_{2}}_{B\mid A}Z^{l_{3}m_{3}B}\right)d\Omega,

and using (D10) we get

\displaystyle\int\left(X^{l_{1}m_{1}A}X^{l_{2}m_{2}}_{A\mid B}Z^{l_{3}m_{3}B}+X^{l_{1}m_{1}}_{A\mid B}X^{l_{2}m_{2}A}Z^{l_{3}m_{3}B}\right)d\Omega=2\int\left(X^{l_{1}m_{1}}_{[A\mid B]}X^{l_{2}m_{2}A}+X^{l_{1}m_{1}A}X^{l_{2}m_{2}}_{[A\mid B]}\right)Z^{l_{3}m_{3}B}d\Omega

\displaystyle-2\int X^{l_{1}m_{1}A}X^{l_{2}m_{2}B}Z^{l_{3}m_{3}}_{A\mid B}d\Omega.

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}l_{3}(l_{3}+1)\int Y^{l_{3}m_{3}}Z^{l_{1}m_{1}}_{A}Z^{l_{2}m_{2}A}d\Omega

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\Bigg(l_{1}(l_{1}+1)\Big[l_{2}(l_{2}+1)+l_{3}(l_{3}+1)-l_{1}(l_{1}+1)\Big]+l_{2}(l_{2}+1)\Big[l_{1}(l_{1}+1)+l_{3}(l_{3}+1)-l_{2}(l_{2}+1)\Big]

\displaystyle-l_{3}(l_{3}+1)\Big[l_{1}(l_{1}+1)+l_{2}(l_{2}+1)-l_{3}(l_{3}+1)\Big]\Bigg)B(l_{1},m_{1};l_{2},m_{2};l_{3},m_{3})

\displaystyle=\Lambda(l_{1},l_{2},l_{3})B(l_{1},m_{1};l_{2},m_{2};l_{3},m_{3}).

\int X^{l_{1}m_{1}A}Z^{l_{2}m_{2}B}Z^{l_{3}m_{3}}_{A\mid B}d\Omega

(93)

To evaluate this, we use the following identities

\displaystyle\int X^{l_{1}m_{1}}_{A\mid B}Z^{l_{2}m_{2}A}Z^{l_{3}m_{3}B}d\Omega=-\int X^{l_{1}m_{1}}_{A}\left(Z^{l_{2}m_{2}A}_{\hskip 8.19447pt\hskip 8.19447pt\hskip 8.19447pt\mid B}Z^{l_{3}m_{3}B}+Z^{l_{2}m_{2}A}Z^{l_{3}m_{3}B}_{\hskip 8.19447pt\hskip 8.19447pt\hskip 8.19447pt\mid B}\right)d\Omega

(94)

\displaystyle=-\int X^{l_{1}m_{1}A}Z^{l_{2}m_{2}}_{A\mid B}Z^{l_{3}m_{3}B}d\Omega+l_{3}(l_{3}+1)\int X_{A}^{l_{1}m_{1}}Z^{l_{2}m_{2}A}Y^{l_{3}m_{3}}d\Omega

\displaystyle=\int X^{l_{1}m_{1}A}Z^{l_{2}m_{2}B}Z^{l_{3}m_{3}}_{A\mid B}d\Omega+l_{3}(l_{3}+1)\int X_{A}^{l_{1}m_{1}}Z^{l_{2}m_{2}A}Y^{l_{3}m_{3}}d\Omega,

\displaystyle\int X^{l_{1}m_{1}}_{B\mid A}Z^{l_{2}m_{2}A}Z^{l_{3}m_{3}B}d\Omega=l_{2}(l_{2}+1)\int X^{l_{1}m_{1}}_{B}Z^{l_{3}m_{3}B}Y^{l_{2}m_{2}}d\Omega

(95)

\displaystyle-\int X^{l_{1}m_{1}B}Z^{l_{2}m_{2}A}Z^{l_{3}m_{3}}_{A\mid B}d\Omega.

Subtracting (D18) from (D17) we get

\displaystyle\int X^{l_{1}m_{1}A}Z^{l_{2}m_{2}B}Z^{l_{3}m_{3}}_{A\mid B}d\Omega=\frac{1}{2}l_{1}(l_{1}+1)\int X^{l_{2}m_{2}A}Z^{l_{3}m_{3}}_{A}Y^{l_{1}m_{1}}d\Omega

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}l_{2}(l_{2}+1)\int X^{l_{1}m_{1}A}Z^{l_{3}m_{3}}_{A}Y^{l_{2}m_{2}}d\Omega-\frac{1}{2}l_{3}(l_{3}+1)\int X^{l_{1}m_{1}A}Z^{l_{2}m_{2}A}Y^{l_{3}m_{3}}d\Omega

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}l_{1}(l_{1}+1)A(l_{3},m_{3};l_{2},m_{2};l_{1},m_{1})+\frac{1}{2}l_{2}(l_{2}+1)A(l_{3},m_{3};l_{1},m_{1};l_{2},m_{2})

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}l_{3}(l_{3}+1)A(l_{2},m_{2};l_{1},m_{1};l_{3},m_{3}).

\displaystyle\int Z^{l_{1}m_{1}A}Z^{l_{2}m_{2}B}X^{l_{3}m_{3}}_{(A\mid B)}d\Omega=\frac{1}{2}\Bigg[\int Z^{l_{1}m_{1}A}Z^{l_{2}m_{2}B}X^{l_{3}m_{3}}_{A\mid B}d\Omega+\int Z^{l_{1}m_{1}A}Z^{l_{2}m_{2}B}X^{l_{3}m_{3}}_{B\mid A}d\Omega\Bigg]

(97)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}l_{1}(l_{1}+1)\int X_{A}^{l_{3}m_{3}}Z^{l_{2}m_{2}A}Y^{l_{3}m_{3}}d\Omega+\frac{1}{2}l_{2}(l_{2}+1)\int X_{A}^{l_{3}m_{3}}Z^{l_{1}m_{1}A}Y^{l_{2}m_{2}}d\Omega

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}l_{1}(l_{1}+1)A(l_{2},m_{2};l_{3},m_{3};l_{1},m_{1})+\frac{1}{2}l_{2}(l_{2}+1)A(l_{1},m_{1};l_{3},m_{3};l_{2},m_{2}).

\displaystyle\int Z^{l_{1}m_{1}A}X^{l_{2}m_{2}B}X^{l_{3}m_{3}}_{(A\mid B)}d\Omega=\int Z^{l_{1}m_{1}A}X^{l_{2}m_{2}B}\left(X^{l_{3}m_{3}}_{A\mid B}-X^{l_{3}m_{3}}_{[A\mid B]}\right)d\Omega

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\Bigg[l_{1}(l_{1}+1)\Big(l_{2}(l_{2}+1)+l_{3}(l_{3}+1)-l_{1}(l_{1}+1)\Big)-l_{2}(l_{2}+1)\Big(l_{1}(l_{1}+1)+l_{3}(l_{3}+1)-l_{2}(l_{2}+1)\Big)\Bigg]

\displaystyle B(l_{1},m_{1};l_{2},m_{2};l_{3},m_{3}).

VII.

\displaystyle\int X^{l_{1}m_{1}A}X^{l_{2}m_{2}B}X^{l_{3}m_{3}}_{(A\mid B)}d\Omega=\frac{1}{2}\int Z^{l_{1}m_{1}A}X^{l_{2}m_{2}B}Z^{l_{3}m_{3}}_{A\mid B}d\Omega+\frac{1}{2}\int X^{l_{1}m_{1}A}Z^{l_{2}m_{2}B}Z^{l_{3}m_{3}}_{A\mid B}d\Omega

(99)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}l_{1}(l_{1}+1)\int X^{l_{2}m_{2}A}Z^{l_{3}m_{3}}_{A}Y^{l_{1}m_{1}}d\Omega+\frac{1}{2}l_{2}(l_{2}+1)\int X^{l_{1}m_{1}A}Z^{l_{3}m_{3}}_{A}Y^{l_{2}m_{2}}d\Omega

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}l_{3}(l_{3}+1)\int X^{l_{1}m_{1}A}Z^{l_{2}m_{2}}_{A}Y^{l_{3}m_{3}}d\Omega-\frac{1}{2}l_{3}(l_{3}+1)\int X^{l_{2}m_{2}A}Z^{l_{1}m_{1}}_{A}Y^{l_{3}m_{3}}d\Omega

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}l_{1}(l_{1}+1)A(l_{3},m_{3};l_{2},m_{2};l_{1},m_{1})+\frac{1}{2}l_{2}(l_{2}+1)A(l_{3},m_{3};l_{1},m_{1};l_{2},m_{2}).

Appendix E
The Spherical Decomposition of the Electromagnetic Energy-Momentum Tensor

The four covariant components of the vector potential
A_{\mu}(x)
are divided as
A_{a}(x)
and
A_{A}(x)
where
a=0,1
and
A=2,3
, such that each are decomposed in the following manner

\displaystyle A_{a}(x)=\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\sum_{m=-l}^{m=l}g^{lm}_{a}(t,r)Y^{lm}(\theta,\phi)

(100)

\displaystyle A_{A}(x)=\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\sum_{m=-l}^{m=l}\left[h^{lm}(t,r)Z_{A}^{lm}(\theta,\phi)+k^{lm}(t,r)X_{A}^{lm}(\theta,\phi)\right],

and the four components of the current
J_{\mu}(x)
can be decomposed similarly as

\displaystyle J_{a}(x)=\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\sum_{m=-l}^{m=l}j^{lm}_{a}(t,r)Y^{lm}(\theta,\phi)

(101)

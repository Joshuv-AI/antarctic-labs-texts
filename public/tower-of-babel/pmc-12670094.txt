Analysis of IL-6 and IL-1β Gene coding and study on specific clearance using histidine-grafted PVDF membranes
Authors: Ke Li, JuanJuan Liu, Kuo Zhang, Hui Liu, Yi Yang, Ren Na, Hui Ma, XiaoJian Cui
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Severely infected patients produce large amounts of inflammatory cytokines, such as interleukin-6 (IL-6) and interleukin-1β (IL-1β), leading to a "cytokine storm", which is a clinically refractory condition. Because IL-6 and IL-1β differ significantly in gene family, chromosomal location, protein structure, and signaling pathways, existing techniques are ineffective at simultaneously removing cytokines. This work developed a novel blood purification material by chemically grafting histidine onto a polyvinylidene fluoride (PVDF) membrane. ATR-FTIR and XPS analyses confirmed the successful grafting of histidine onto the PVDF membrane. Following histidine grafting, the membrane’s adsorption capacity for IL-6 in aqueous solution increased with histidine concentration. The optimized histidine-grafted PVDF membrane achieved adsorption rates of (36 ± 4)% for IL-6 and (63 ± 5) % for IL-1β in plasma. Additionally, the histidine-grafted PVDF membrane demonstrated enhanced biocompatibility, exhibiting a low hemolysis rate, minimal adsorption of red blood cells and platelets, and anticoagulant properties without activating the coagulation cascade. This histidine-grafted PVDF membrane offers a promising new therapeutic strategy for treating severe infections and holds significant potential for clinical application.

Introduction

Severe infection is a refractory disease triggered by pathogens such as bacteria, viruses, and other pathogenic microorganisms, characterized by a systemic inflammatory response. Its core pathogenesis involves the overactivation and imbalance of the immune system-Pathogen-Associated Molecular Patterns (PAMPs) activate macrophages and neutrophils via Toll-like receptors, leading to abnormal activation of the NF-κB and NLRP3 inflammasome signaling pathways. This results in a massive release of pro-inflammatory cytokines, including interleukin-1β (IL-1β), interleukin-6 (IL-6), and tumor necrosis factor-α (TNF-α), forming a "cytokine storm". This storm triggers vascular endothelial injury, diffuse microthrombus formation, and mitochondrial dysfunction, ultimately leading to Acute Respiratory Distress Syndrome (ARDS), septic shock, and multiple organ failure [1], [2], [3].

From a genetic perspective, the T allele of the IL1B gene -511C/T polymorphism significantly increases IL-1β secretion, while carriers of the G allele of the IL6 gene -174G/C polymorphism exhibit elevated IL-6 levels. Additionally, variations in innate immunity-related genes such as TLR4 and IRAK4 amplify the inflammatory response, and mutations in negative regulatory genes like SOCS3 impair the ability to suppress cytokines production. Collectively, these genetic factors render certain patients more susceptible to severe cytokine storms [4], [5], [6]. Therefore, effective clearance of IL-1β and IL-6 is critically important in clinical treatment.

Based on the analysis of the IL-6 and IL-1β cytokine gene families and members, chromosomal localization, gene structure and introns, encoded protein structure, precursor protein and processing, signaling pathways, receptors, and primary biological function-this study proposes chemically grafting histidine, an essential human amino acid, onto PVDF membranes. The objective is to develop PVDF membranes with enhanced adsorption capacity for IL-1β and IL-6, while creating a surface with excellent biocompatibility for the direct adsorption and removal of these cytokines from blood. The modification of the PVDF membrane surface with histidine was characterized using Attenuated Total Reflectance Fourier Transform Infrared Spectroscopy (ATR-FTIR), X-ray Photoelectron Spectroscopy (XPS), and Scanning Electron Microscopy (SEM). The membranes’ efficacy in removing IL-1β and IL-6 was evaluated by measuring their adsorption of these cytokines from aqueous solutions and plasma. Biocompatibility was assessed through tests of blood cell adsorption, hemolysis, and coagulation properties. Preliminary data demonstrated the specific clearance of IL-6 and IL-1β by the histidine-grafted PVDF membrane, providing an initial evaluation of its potential for clinical treatment of severe infections.

Materials and methods

Materials

PVDF (Solvay, France). Dendritic Polyethyleneimine (PEI, Shanghai Gongbike Co., Ltd.). Coagulation four-item test kit (Beijing Meichuang Xinyue Medical Device Co., Ltd.). Histidine, MES, NHS, EDC (Beijing Solarbio Science & Technology Co., Ltd.). IL-1 and IL-6 standard (Peppro Tech). IL-1 and IL-6 ELISA kits (Lianke Bio, Hangzhou). Blood samples (Tianjin Third Central Hospital).

Determination of IL-6 and IL-1 gene coding

DNA sequencing was performed using the Sanger method. By sequencing genes such as IL-6 and IL-1β, combined with reviewing relevant literature, information was obtained regarding the gene family and its members, chromosomal localization, gene structure and introns, encoded protein structure, precursor protein and processing, signaling pathways and receptors, and primary biological functions.

Preparation of PVDF membranes

A 25 cm² PVDF hollow fiber plasma separation membrane was immersed in 10 mL of a PEI-200 aqueous solution (2.5 wt%, pH=12.0) and reacted at 60°C for 3 h to obtain the membrane M-PEI. Then, after sealing both ends of the M-PEI membrane, it was placed in MES buffer solutions (0.05 mol/L, pH=6.0, NHS:EDC=1:1) containing histidine at the concentrations of 0.16 mol/L, 0.24 mol/L, 0.32 mol/L, and 0.40 mol/L, respectively. The reaction proceeded at 40°C for 72 h to obtain the histidine-modified membranes, denoted as N-H-0.16, N-H-0.24, N-H-0.32, and N-H-0.40, respectively. Characterization of the PVDF membrane

This experiment employed the KBr pellet method. The functional groups on the membrane surface were characterized using a Thermo Fisher Scientific Nicolet 6700 Fourier Transform Infrared (FTIR) Spectrometer.

The chemical composition of the inner and outer surfaces of the membranes was analyzed using a Thermo Fisher Scientific K-Alpha X-ray Photoelectron Spectroscopy (XPS) instrument.

The morphology of the membrane was studied by Field-emission scanning electron microscope (FE-SEM).

IL-6 adsorption capacity

A specific amount of IL-6 was added to deionized water. A 7 cm² PVDF membrane was immersed in 0.5 mL of the IL-6 aqueous solution and incubated on a shaker at 37°C and 50 rpm for 2 h. The IL-6 concentration was measured using an enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA). Absorbance was recorded at wavelengths of 450 nm and 630 nm using a microplate reader, and the IL-6 concentration in the solution was calculated. Safety studies

Hemolysis

The interaction between the membrane and red blood cells was evaluated using a hemolysis rate test. The hemolysis rate of the membrane was measured with a microplate reader and the hemolysis rate was calculated as follows [8].(2)HR=Dt−DncDpc−Dnc×100%

HR is the hemolysis rate. Dt is the absorbance of the experimental group. Dnc is the absorbance of the negative control. Dpc is the absorbance of the positive control.

Effect on blood cells

Fresh venous blood was centrifuged at 1000 rpm for 15 min to obtain platelet-rich plasma (PRP) and the precipitated erythrocyte-rich portion (ERP). The membranes were immersed in PRP or ERP at 37°C for 90 min, rinsed with PBS, and then fixed with 2.5 % glutaraldehyde in PBS at 4°C for 2 h. Subsequently, the samples were dehydrated stepwise with ethanol, vacuum-dried, and sputter-coated with gold. The morphology of platelets or red blood cells adsorbed on the surface of the PVDF membranes was observed using FE-SEM [9].

Coagulation properties

Fresh human blood was collected in centrifuge tubes containing sodium citrate at the ratio of 1:9 (sodium citrate to blood). The blood samples were then centrifuged at 4°C and 3000 rpm for 15 min to obtain fresh plasma. Prothrombin Time (PT), Activated Partial Thromboplastin Time (APTT), Thrombin Time (TT), and Fibrinogen (FIB) level were measured using a CA-50 automatic coagulation analyzer [10], [11].

Adsorption of IL-6 or IL-1β from plasma using PVDF membranes

First, a specific amount of IL-6 or IL-1β was added to fresh plasma and mixed thoroughly. Then, a 7 cm² PVDF membrane was added, and it was carried out at 37°C for 2 h. The concentration of IL-6 in the plasma before and after the adsorption by the PVDF membrane was measured using a microplate reader at wavelengths of 450 nm and 630 nm. Results and discussion

Gene coding analysis

Sanger gene sequencing combined with literature reviews revealed the following findings.

In terms of gene structure, both IL-6 and IL-1β are eukaryotic genes containing introns and exons, and their expression requires transcription and RNA splicing. Regarding transcriptional regulation, the expression of both IL-6 and IL-1β is rapid and tightly controlled. Their promoter regions contain binding sites for key transcription factors, such as NF-κB. When immune cells like macrophages recognize Pathogen-Associated Molecular Patterns (PAMPs), the NF-κB pathway is rapidly activated, initiating the transcription of IL-6 and IL-1β [12], [13]. Regarding the overlap in major cellular sources, their primary producers are both monocytes/macrophages. Additionally, other cells, such as dendritic cells and fibroblasts, can also produce IL-6 and IL-1β.

Although IL-6 and IL-1β function synergistically, they differ significantly in gene family, chromosomal location, protein structure, and signaling pathways.

IL-6 belongs to the “IL-6-like cytokine” family, also known as the “long-chain four-helix bundle cytokine” family, which includes G-CSF, Leukemia Inhibitory Factor (LIF), etc. The IL-6 gene is located on the short arm of chromosome 7 (7p15.3). It contains 5 exons and 4 introns. The IL-6 protein is monomeric in structure but it binds to the receptor as a dimer. Mature IL-6 is a single protein chain exhibiting a typical four-helix bundle structure. It is initially synthesized as a precursor with a signal peptide. After the signal peptide is cleaved, the mature IL-6 protein is secreted and perform its biological functions. It acts through both classic signaling and trans-signaling pathways. Its receptor complex consists of the membrane-bound IL-6 receptor alpha (IL-6Rα) and the signal transducer gp130. Upon IL-6 binding, the receptor complex activates the JAK-STAT signaling pathway (mainly STAT3) [14]. IL-6 is a multifunctional cytokine. Its primary biological roles include pro-inflammatory action by inducing hepatocytes to produce acute-phase proteins (such as C-reactive protein). Promotion of immunity by promoting B cell differentiation into plasma cells (antibody production), and hematopoietic role stimulating the differentiation of hematopoietic stem cells.

IL-1β belongs to the IL-1 gene family. Core members include IL-1α, IL-1β, and the IL-1 receptor antagonist (IL-1Ra), which belong to distinct gene and protein families. It is located on the long arm of chromosome 2 (2q14.1) [15]. Both the IL-1β and IL-1α genes contain 7 exons and 6 introns [15]. Their structure is monomeric. Both IL-1α and IL-1β exhibiting a unique β-trefoil fold structure β-sheets. IL-1β is synthesized as an inactive precursor (pro-IL-1β) [16]. It must be cleaved by caspase-1 within the inflammasome to generate the mature, active IL-1β for secretion. It initiates signaling by forming a complex with the IL-1 receptor I (IL-1RI) and an accessory protein [17]. Downstream signaling primarily activates the NF-κB and MAPK pathways and does not involve the JAK-STAT pathway. IL-1β is a potent early pro-inflammatory cytokine whose main biological roles include inducing local and systemic inflammation (vasodilation, fever, pain) and to activating cells such as T cells and endothelial cells. It is not directly involved in antibody production.

Study of IL-6 adsorption capacity in aqueous solution

As shown in Fig. 5, the membrane's adsorption capacity for IL-6 in aqueous solution increased with rising histidine concentration in an IL-6 solution initially containing 99 pg/mL. At a histidine reaction concentration of 0.40 mol/L, the histidine-grafted PVDF membrane achieved its highest adsorption rate for IL-6, reaching 94 ± 3 %. Similarly, the membrane's adsorption capacity for IL-6 also increased with histidine concentration in an aqueous IL-6 solution with an initial concentration of 585 pg/mL. At the same histidine reaction concentration of 0.40 mol/L, membrane reached an adsorption rate of 89 ± 2 %. These results demonstrate that the histidine-grafted PVDF membrane can effectively adsorb and remove IL-6 from both low- and high-concentration aqueous solutions.Fig. 5Study of IL-6 adsorption from aqueous solution using histidine-grafted PVDF membranes.Fig. 5

This is because the end of the histidine molecule that is not involved in the chemical reaction contains an imidazole ring and an amino group. IL-6 is a protein containing numerous carboxyl-terminal groups. The amino group can form multiple hydrogen bonds with the carboxyl terminal groups on IL-6, causing them to bind firmly [21]. Simultaneously, the imidazole ring can form hydrophobic interactions with IL-6, resulting in the adsorption of IL-6 onto the surface of the histidine-grafted PVDF membrane. Research by Santra et al. indicates that hydrogen bonds play an important role in maintaining the native folded state of proteins [22]. Additionally, research by Santanu et al. shows that amino acid molecules form branched or multiple simultaneous hydrogen bonds with the same or different secondary segments of proteins, creating bridges to stabilize the protein's secondary structure [23].

Study on the clearance efficiency of IL-1β and IL-6 in plasma

Plasma composition is extremely complex. The various components within plasma often reduce the clearance efficiency of adsorbent materials for target toxins or even render them ineffective. To investigate the actual clearance effect of the histidine-grafted PVDF membrane on inflammatory cytokines in the complex plasma environment, this study further examined the inflammatory cytokines IL-6 and IL-1β in plasma.

As shown in Table 2, the adsorption capacity of the histidine-grafted PVDF membrane for IL-6 is significantly higher than that of the pristine PVDF membrane in plasma. The IL-6 adsorption rate increased from (20 ± 2) % to (36 ± 4) %. Similarly, for IL-1β in plasma, the histidine-grafted PVDF membrane shows a much higher adsorption capacity compared to the pristine PVDF membrane, with the IL-1β adsorption rate increasing from (43 ± 4) % to (63 ± 5) %. These results indicate that the histidine-grafted PVDF membrane developed in this study exhibits effective adsorption of both inflammatory cytokines IL-6 and IL-1β in plasma. Hemocompatibility study

Blood purification materials come into direct contact with blood during use. If these materials exhibit poor hemocompatibility, they may induce hemolytic reactions, leading to hemoglobin release and complications such as renal failure. Additionally, they can activate the coagulation system, platelets, and complement pathways, resulting in thrombus formation within the blood purification material. This can cause a sharp decline or even interruption in treatment efficacy, exacerbate the patient's inflammatory response, deplete clotting factors and platelets, and increase the risk of bleeding or embolism [24], [25], [26]. Therefore, excellent hemocompatibility of blood purification materials is a fundamental prerequisite for ensuring the safety and effectiveness of blood purification therapy.

As shown in Fig. 6(a), after direct contact with red blood cells for 2 h, both the pristine membrane and the histidine-grafted PVDF membrane exhibited low hemolysis rates, each below 5 %, meeting the ISO standard requirements [27]. This indicates that the histidine-modified PVDF membrane causes minimal damage to red blood cells upon contact. As shown in Fig. 6(b), after 2 h direct contact with red blood cells, the number of red blood cells adsorbed on the surfaces of both membranes was low, and the morphology of the adsorbed red blood cells showed no deformation. Therefore, neither the pristine membrane nor the histidine-grafted PVDF membrane is likely to cause significant adverse effects on red blood cells during blood during use.Fig. 6Effect of histidine-grafted PVDF membrane on red blood cells.Fig. 6

Effect of histidine-grafted PVDF membrane on red blood cells.

As shown in Fig. 7, the number of platelets adsorbed on the surface of the histidine-grafted PVDF membrane was lower than that on the pristine membrane M0 after 2 h contact with platelets-rich plasma. Furthermore, platelets adsorption on the histidine-grafted PVDF membrane surface gradually decreased as the histidine content increased. The platelets adsorbed on both the pristine PVDF membrane and the histidine-grafted PVDF membranes exhibited a spherical shape without pseudopodia, indicating that the adsorbed platelets were not activated.Fig. 7Platelet adsorption on the histidine-grafted PVDF membrane.Fig. 7

The impact of blood purification materials on coagulation function is a critical factor in determining their suitability for use in blood purification applications [28]. APTT primarily reflects the status of the intrinsic coagulation pathway. As shown in Fig. 8(a), compared to the blank control, the APTT of plasma was significantly prolonged after contact with both the PVDF membrane and the histidine-grafted PVDF membranes for 2 h. However, there was little difference in APTT between plasma contacted with the pristine PVDF membrane and that contacted with the histidine-grafted membranes. This suggests that both the pristine PVDF membrane and the histidine-grafted PVDF membranes possess certain anticoagulant properties affecting the intrinsic pathway. TT reflects the time required for fibrinogen to convert to fibrin. As shown in Fig. 8(b), compared to the blank control, the TT of plasma changed little after contact with the PVDF membrane and histidine-grafted PVDF membranes, indicating that the modified PVDF membranes in this study have no significant effect on TT coagulation. PT primarily reflects the status of the extrinsic coagulation pathway. As shown in Fig. 8(c), after plasma contacted the PVDF membrane and histidine-grafted PVDF membranes for 2 h, the PT gradually prolonged with increasing histidine concentration. When the histidine reaction concentration reached 0.40 mol/L, the PT was the longest after contact between the corresponding histidine-grafted PVDF membrane and plasma. This indicates that the histidine-grafted PVDF membrane can influence the extrinsic coagulation pathway to a certain extent, exhibiting anticoagulant effects. After plasma contacted the PVDF membrane and histidine-grafted PVDF membranes for 2 h, the plasma FIB level decreased significantly. A lower FIB content implies fewer proteins available for coagulation activation, thereby reducing the likelihood of coagulation reactions. Thus, the histidine-grafted PVDF membrane developed in this study possesses certain anticoagulant properties and does not increase the risk of thrombus formation.Fig. 8Effect of histidine-grafted PVDF membrane on coagulation properties.Fig. 8

Discussion

Each cytokine (e.g., interleukins, interferons, TNF) is encoded by specific genes, and the DNA sequence of these genes determines the cytokine’s protein structure. First, variations exist in the gene sequences encoding the same cytokine among different individuals, these variations known as "gene polymorphisms" and can lead to differents in cytokine production levels and activity. Second, for a cytokine to exert its effect, it must bind to specific receptors on the surface of target cells. The quantity, structure, and function of these receptors are also genetically determined, and polymorphisms in receptor genes can influence an individual's sensitivity to the cytokine. Third, beyond the genes encoding cytokines and their receptors, other regulatory genes such as those controlling signaling pathways like NF-κB and JAK-STAT modulate the transmission and amplification of cytokine signals [29]. Variations in these regulatory genes can further alter the overall intensity of the cytokine network response [30].

Both IL-6 and IL-1β are pro-inflammatory cytokines that share similarities and differences at the genetic coding level. Their similarities are primarily evident in their roles as key pro-inflammatory cytokines, gene structure, transcriptional regulation, and cellular sources. In contrast, their differences are mainly observed in gene family and members, chromosomal localization, gene structure and introns composition, encoded protein structure, precursor protein, processing, signaling pathways and receptors, as well as their primary biological functions [31].

Blood purification primarily includes hemodialysis, hemofiltration, plasma exchange, and hemoperfusion, along with numerous related technologies derived from these methods [32]. Resin is the commonly used adsorbent in hemoperfusion. The use of histidine-grafted PVDF membranes for hemoperfusion to remove inflammatory cytokines IL-6 and IL-1β, as presented in this study, is innovative. However, whether using resin or membranes as adsorbents, adsorption specificity is critical. This refers to the effective removal of toxic and harmful substances from human blood while minimizing the impact on beneficial components, particularly in terms of hemocompatibility. Adsorption specificity depends on multiple factors, including the physicochemical structure and biological properties of both the adsorbent and the adsorbed substance. Regarding biological properties, this includes antigen-antibody interactions between the adsorbent and the adsorbed substances, the spatial structure of the adsorbed molecules, genetic coding factors, etc. Therefore, investigating the adsorption specificity of hemoperfusion adsorbents for the pro-inflammatory cytokines IL-6 and IL-1β is essential.

Based on the aforementioned similarities and differences in genetic coding, this study preliminarily explored the specific mechanism by which the histidine-grafted PVDF membrane clears IL-6 and IL-1β. Results from ATR-FTIR, XPS, and SEM analyses comfirmed the successful grafting of histidine onto the PVDF membrane surface. After grafting histidine, the membrane's adsorption capacity for IL-6 increased significantly. In plasma, the histidine-grafted PVDF membrane exhibited a much higher adsorption capacity for both IL-6 and IL-1β compared to the pristine PVDF membrane. The IL-6 adsorption rate increased from (20 ± 2) % to (36 ± 4.0) %, and the IL-1β adsorption rate increased from (43 ± 4) % to (63 ± 5) %. Therefore, the histidine-grafted PVDF membrane developed in this study demonstrates strong adsorption and clearance efficacy for the inflammatory cytokines IL-6 and IL-1β in plasma. Hemolysis tests, assessments of membrane adsorption of red blood cells and platelets, and coagulation assays confirmed that the histidine-grafted PVDF membrane possesses excellent hemocompatibility. Thus, this membrane shows promise for achieving effective therapeutic outcomes in treating severe infections by clearing inflammatory cytokines.

Although preliminary results were obtained in this study, further extensive research is required- both theoretical and experimental to more clearly elucidate the specific clearance mechanism of the histidine-grafted PVDF membrane for the pro-inflammatory cytokines IL-6 and IL-1β, as well as the critical role of genetic coding in this clearance process. Such research will enhance the safety and efficacy of its clinical application.

Conclusion

ATR-FTIR and XPS analyses confirmed the successful grafting of histidine onto the surface of the PVDF membrane. Following histidine grafting, the membrane’s adsorption capacity for IL-6 in aqueous solution increased proportionally with the histidine concentration. The optimized histidine-grafted PVDF membrane achieved adsorption rates of up to (36 ± 4) % for IL-6 and (63 ± 5) % for IL-1β in plasma. Additionally, the histidine-grafted PVDF membrane exhibited enhanced biocompatibility, characterized by a low hemolysis rate, minimal adsorption of red blood cells and platelets, and notable anticoagulant properties without triggering the coagulation cascade. The histidine-grafted PVDF membrane developed in this study offers a novel therapeutic approach for treating severe infections and demonstrates significant potential for clinical application.

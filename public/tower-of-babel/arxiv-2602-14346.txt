Abstract.
The objective of our paper is to investigate
fractional elliptic equations of the form
(-\Delta)^{s}u=\frac{\lambda}{(a-u)^{2}}
within a bounded domain
\Omega
,
subject to zero Dirichlet boundary conditions. Here,
s\in(0,1)
,

\lambda>0
, and the function
a
vanishes at the boundary while
satisfying additional conditions. This problem originates from
Micro-Electromechanical Systems (MEMS) devices, particularly when the
elastic membrane makes contact with the ground plate at the
boundary. We establish both existence and nonexistence results,
illustrating how the boundary decay of the membrane influences the
solutions and pull-in voltage.

Keywords
: MEMS, Fractional Laplacian, Existence, Pull-in Voltage.

Mathematics Subject Classification
: 36J08, 35B50, 35J15.

1.
Introduction

Let
\Omega
be a
C^{2}
connected bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
with
N\geq 2
,
the function
a:\bar{\Omega}\to[0,1]
be in
C^{0,\beta}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)\cap C(\bar{\Omega})
with
\beta\in(0,1)
.
Our purpose of this paper is to study the existence and nonexistence of minimal solutions to the
fractional elliptic equation

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}(-\Delta)^{s}u=\frac{\lambda}{(a-u)^{2}}&{\rm in}\quad\ \Omega,\\[5.69054pt]
\phantom{--}0<u\leq a&{\rm in}\quad\ \Omega,\\[5.69054pt]
\phantom{--}\quad\ u=0&{\rm in}\quad\ \mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash\Omega,\end{array}\right.

(1.1)

where
\lambda>0
and
(-\Delta)^{s}
with
s\in(0,1)
is the fractional Laplacian defined as

(-\Delta)^{s}u(x)=\frac{c_{N,s}}{2}P.V.\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{2u(x)-u(x+y)-u(x-y)}{|y|^{N+2s}}dy,

(1.2)

here
c_{N,s}
is a normalized constant as

c_{N,s}=2^{2s}\pi^{-\frac{N}{2}}s\frac{\Gamma(\frac{N+2s}{2})}{\Gamma(1-s)},

P.V. denotes the principal value of the integral, for notational simplicity we omit in what follows.

The equation denoted by (
1.1
) originates from scenarios
involving Micro-Electromechanical Systems (MEMS) devices,
particularly when the elastic membrane makes contact with the ground
plate along the boundary. Specifically, when
s=1
and
a=1
,
(
1.1
) simplifies to the classical MEMS equation

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}-\Delta u=\frac{\lambda}{(1-u)^{2}}&{\rm in}\quad\ \Omega,\\[5.69054pt]
\phantom{\ }\quad\ u=0&{\rm on}\quad\ \partial\Omega,\end{array}\right.

(1.3)

where parameter
\lambda>0
characterizes the relative strength of the electrostatic and mechanical forces in the system,
equation (
1.3
) models a simple electrostatic MEMS device consisting of a elastic membrane with boundary supported
at
0
above a rigid ground plate located at
1
.
There exists a critical value
\lambda^{*}
(pull-in voltage) such that if
\lambda\in(0,\lambda^{*})
,
problem (
1.3
) admits a minimal solution, while for
\lambda>\lambda^{*}
, no solution for (
1.3
) exists.
When
s=1
, the function
a:\bar{\Omega}\to[0,1]
is in the class of
C^{\gamma}(\Omega)\cap C(\bar{\Omega})
and satisfies

a(x)\geq\kappa\rho(x)^{\gamma},\quad\forall\ x\in\Omega

(1.4)

with
\kappa>0
,
\gamma\in(0,1)
,
\rho(x)={\rm dist}(x,\partial\Omega)
for
x\in\Omega
, Chen-Wang-Zhou in
[
7
]
solved

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}-\Delta u=\frac{\lambda}{(a-u)^{2}}&{\rm in}\quad\ \Omega,\\[5.69054pt]
\phantom{-}0<u<a&{\rm in}\quad\ \Omega,\\[5.69054pt]
\phantom{-\Delta}u=0&{\rm on}\quad\partial\Omega,\end{array}\right.

(1.5)

which models a MEMS device that the static deformation of the surface of membrane when it is applied voltage
\lambda
,
where
a
is initially undeflected state of the elastic membrane that contacts the ground plate on the boundary.
More results on MEMS see
[
8
,
14
,
16
,
15

17

20
,
24

28
]
and reference therein.

The primary objective of our paper is to examine the MEMS equation

1.1

1.1

({\mathcal{A}}_{0})

For some

a(x)\geq\kappa\rho(x)^{\gamma},\quad\forall\ x\in\Omega,

where

Now we show the existence result.

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume that

satisfies (

) with
\gamma\in(0,\frac{2}{3}s]

and

is strictly increasing;

(ii)

);

(iii)

assume more that there exists

such that

a(x)\leq c\rho(x)^{\gamma},\quad x\in\Omega,

(1.7)

then there exists
\lambda_{*}\in(0,\lambda^{*}]
such that for
\lambda\in(0,\lambda_{*})
,

{\rm for}\quad\gamma\not=\frac{1}{2}s,\qquad\frac{\lambda}{c_{0}}\rho(x)^{\min\{s,2s-2\gamma\}}\leq u_{\lambda}(x)\leq c_{0}\lambda\rho(x)^{\min\{s,2s-2\gamma\}},\qquad\forall x\in\Omega;

{\rm for}\quad\gamma=\frac{1}{2}s,\qquad\frac{\lambda}{c_{0}}\rho(x)^{s}\ln\frac{1}{\rho(x)}\leq u_{\lambda}(x)\leq c_{0}\lambda\rho(x)^{s}\ln\frac{1}{\rho(x)},\qquad\forall x\in A_{\frac{1}{2}},

Remark 1.1
.

(i)

Our model is derived from the enclosed MEMS model
[
7
]
.
The incorporation of the fractional Laplacian, a key component in
our model, is notable for its applicability in modeling fractional
quantum mechanics. This is particularly evident in the examination
of particles maneuvering through stochastic fields, which are often
represented by Lévy processes
[
22
,
23
]
. Furthermore, the
fractional Laplacian is construed as a Pseudo-Relativistic operator

[
1
,
19
]
and see
[
11
,
29
]
for the square root of the Laplacian.

(ii)

The most difficulty in the fractional case is the estimates
of

for
\tau\in(0,2s)
, where
\rho
is
distance to the boundary. Different from the boundary blowing up
case
\tau\in(-1,0)
in
[
5
]
, they have different monotonicity

2.1

below.

(iii)

For
\gamma\in(\frac{2}{3}s,s)
, the nonexistence in Theorem

1.3
shows that the pull-in voltage
\lambda^{*}=0
, so there
is a jump for
\lambda^{*}
at
\gamma=\frac{2}{3}s
, since
\lambda^{*}>0

for
\gamma=\frac{2}{3}s
.

(iv)

Our next program is to develop our observations to study the related parabolic equation

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\partial_{t}v+(-\Delta)^{s}v=\frac{\lambda}{(a-v)^{2}}&{\rm in}\quad\ (0,T)\times\Omega,\\[5.69054pt]
\phantom{\partial_{t}v+-\ }0<v<a&{\rm in}\quad\ (0,T)\times\Omega,\\[5.69054pt]
\phantom{\partial_{t}v+--}\quad\ v=0&{\rm in}\quad\ (0,T)\times\big(\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash\Omega\big),\\[5.69054pt]
\phantom{\partial_{t}v+-}v(0,\cdot)=v_{0}&{\rm in}\quad\ \Omega,\end{array}\right.

(1.9)

where
T\in(0,+\infty]
and
v_{0}
is an initial data.

The structure of this article is as follows: In Section 2, we

2.
Preliminary

This section is devoted to the estimates of boundary behavior and
the fractional Green operator

\mathbb{G}_{s,\Omega}[\rho^{\tau-2s}]
.
To this end, we give some notations as follows.
Let
\delta_{0}\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
be such that the distance function

\rho(\cdot)
is of class
C^{2}
in
A_{\delta_{0}}:=\{x\in\Omega,\rho(x)<\delta_{0}\}

\tau\in(0,2s)

V_{\tau}(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}h_{\tau}(x),&x\in\Omega\setminus A_{\delta_{0}},\\[5.69054pt]

(2.2)

where the function
h_{\tau}
is positive such that

V_{\tau}
is
C^{2}
in
\Omega

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
\Omega

\mathbb{R}^{N}

the functions

and

there exist
\delta_{1}\in(0,\delta_{0})
and

(i)

if

\frac{1}{c_{1}}\rho(x)^{\tau-2s}\leq(-\Delta)^{s}V_{\tau}(x)\leq{c_{1}}\rho(x)^{\tau-2s},\ \ \ \forall\,x\in A_{\delta_{1}}.

(ii)

if

\tau\in(s,2s)

\frac{1}{c_{1}}\rho(x)^{\tau-2s}\leq-(-\Delta)^{s}V_{\tau}(x)\leq{c_{1}}\rho(x)^{\tau-2s},\ \ \ \forall\,x\in A_{\delta_{1}}.

(iii)

2.1

Lemma 2.1
.

For
\tau\in(0,2s)
, let

\psi_{s}(\tau)=\int^{+\infty}_{0}\frac{2-(1+t)^{\tau}-|1-t|^{\tau}\chi_{(0,1)}(t)}{t^{1+2s}}dt,

is the characteristic function of the set

Then the function
\psi_{s}
is strictly concave in
(0,2s)
and

\psi_{s}(s)=0,\qquad\psi_{s}(\tau)>0\ {\rm for}\ \tau\in(0,s),\qquad\psi_{s}(\tau)<0\ {\rm for}\ \tau\in(s,2s).

Moreover,

\psi_{s}^{\prime}(s)=-\int^{+\infty}_{0}\frac{(1+t)^{s}\log(1+t)+|1-t|^{s}\chi_{(0,1)}(t)\log|1-t|}{t^{1+2s}}dt<0.

Proof.
Denote

g_{\tau}(t)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}t^{\tau},&t>0,\\[5.69054pt]
\phantom{}0,&t\leq 0,\end{array}\right.

by direct computation, for
l>0
, we have that

\displaystyle(-\Delta)^{s}_{\mathbb{R}}\,g_{\tau}(l)

\displaystyle=\frac{c_{1,s}}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{2l^{\tau}-g_{\tau}(l+t)-g_{\tau}(l-t)}{t^{1+2s}}dt

\displaystyle=c_{1,s}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{2l^{\tau}-(l+t)^{\tau}-|l-t|^{\tau}\chi_{(0,l)}(t)}{t^{1+2s}}dt

\displaystyle=c_{1,s}l^{\tau-2s}\int^{+\infty}_{0}\frac{2-(1+t)^{\tau}-|1-t|^{\tau}\chi_{(0,1)}(t)}{t^{1+2s}}dt=c_{1,s}l^{\tau-2s}\psi_{s}(\tau).

\psi_{s}^{\prime}(\tau)=-\int_{0}^{+\infty}\frac{(1+t)^{\tau}\log(1+t)+|1-t|^{\tau}\chi_{(0,1)}(t)\log|1-t|}{t^{1+2s}}dt

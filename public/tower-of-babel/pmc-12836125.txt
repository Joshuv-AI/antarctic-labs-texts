Background

Hypertrophic cardiomyopathy (HCM) is a complex cardiac disorder characterized by an increased left ventricular wall thickness that occurs in the absence of abnormal loading conditions.1 The anatomical and functional abnormalities associated with this clinical phenotype determine in nearly two-thirds of patients the presence of left ventricular outflow tract obstruction (LVOTO), which strongly contributes to worsening of symptoms and outcomes, including increased risk of heart failure, atrial fibrillation, and sudden cardiac death.2 The main molecular mechanism behind this phenotype is the enhanced cardiac actin-myosin cross-bridge cycling that is responsible for systolic hypercontractility and impaired diastolic relaxation.3 Mavacamten is a first-in-class selective cardiac myosin ATPase inhibitor that directly targets this molecular mechanism by decreasing the number of myosin heads available to interact with actin filaments, thus restoring normal sarcomere contractility and function.4 Given the extraordinary results obtained from clinical trials—recently confirmed also in real-world settings—this drug is now approved for the treatment of symptomatic obstructive HCM.1,5

Case presentation

In January 2024, a 69-year-old woman came to our emergency department for a presyncopal episode that occurred while walking at the train station. Before and after the event, she appeared pallid and diaphoretic, denying other symptoms besides feeling extremely limited while doing that amount of physical effort. Her medical history reported a percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI) of circumflex artery for a non-ST segment elevation myocardial infarction in 2022, arterial hypertension, and dyslipidaemia. Once in the emergency department, she was asymptomatic, normotensive, and afebrile. Physical examination revealed a late peaking systolic ejection murmur, best heard at the left lower sternal border, radiating to the apex and axilla, becoming louder with standing position, with a bifid arterial pulse, and with no signs of peripheral congestion. The ECG showed sinus rhythm conducted with complete left bundle branch block (LBBB) at 85 b.p.m. Her laboratory tests revealed a slightly elevated high-sensitivity troponin T (0.021 µg/L, 0.035 µg/L in the second determination), normal haematocrit and renal function, and a negative D-dimer. Transthoracic echocardiography revealed asymmetric left ventricular (LV) hypertrophy, LVOTO at rest with a maximal gradient of 130 mmHg, moderate mitral insufficiency, and a good right ventricular systolic function (Figure 1; Video 1). The patient was admitted for further investigations: 24-h ECG Holter monitoring did not reveal relevant arrhythmias, while cardiac magnetic resonance confirmed asymmetric LV hypertrophy with a maximal wall thickness of 17 mm at the level of the basal anterior septum and anterior wall; LV ejection fraction equal to 60%, with signs of LVOTO; and systolic anterior motion (SAM) of the anterior mitral leaflet (Video 2). Tissue characterization sequences showed absence of myocardial oedema and subtle mid-wall late gadolinium enhancement (LGE) mainly at the level of the interventricular septum, with increased native T1 values at the same level (Figure 1). The HCM risk score was 3.17%. The patient was discharged after optimization of her medical therapy with metoprolol 100 mg b.i.d. In April 2024, she came for the first time at our Cardiomyopathies outpatient clinic: she was symptomatic for angina for moderate physical effort, in New York Heart Association (NYHA) Class II, and sporadically symptomatic for light headedness during physical exertion. The ECG showed sinus rhythm at 55 b.p.m., normal atrioventricular (AV) conduction, and LBBB with relative repolarization abnormalities (Figure 2). Troponin T (TnT) and N-terminal pro-B-type natriuretic peptide (NTproBNP) levels were elevated (17.9 and 871 ng/L, respectively). At echocardiography, persistent LVOTO was detected with a maximal gradient of 60 mmHg at rest and 79 mmHg during Valsalva manoeuvre, evident SAM with a moderate mitral insufficiency (Figure 3). Left ventricular ejection fraction was 60%, with normal LV filling pressures and no signs of peripheral congestion. Metoprolol was uptitrated at 150 mg b,i.d., and her case was presented to our local ethical committee to obtain approval for mavacamten compassionate use programme, which was accepted soon after. The patient turned out to be a normal metabolizer, and treatment with mavacamten 5 mg daily was started. After 4 weeks, she came back to our clinic for the scheduled appointment: she reported a significant improvement in her exercise tolerance, exertional dyspnoea in NYHA Class II. No other presyncopal episodes during exertion occurred since mavacamten was started. NTproBNP and TnT were 334 pg/mL and 7.6 ng/L, respectively. Surprisingly, her ECG showed sinus rhythm with a normal atrioventricular and intraventricular conduction at 55 b.p.m. (Figure 4). Her echocardiogram showed a stable LV systolic function and a maximal LVOT gradient during Valsalva manoeuvre equal to 40 mmHg, with a resting LVOT gradient of 10 mmHg (Figure 5). According to the uptitration scheme, she was advised to continue with mavacamten 5 mg once daily.

Discussion

Numerous trials and real-world studies demonstrated the extraordinary benefits deriving from mavacamten use in patients with obstructive HCM, showing significant improvement in symptoms, LVOTO, mitral insufficiency, diastolic function, and cardiac biomarkers, without significant decline in LV systolic function.6–9 Imaging studies also demonstrated initial signs of positive structural remodelling deriving from its use.10,11 Small studies and case series reported improvement in ECG features such as ventricular repolarization abnormalities indicative of LV strain and reduction in ECG voltages, both typically associated with phenotypic presence of LV hypertrophy.12–14 To our knowledge, however, this is the first case reporting the resolution of a complete LBBB during treatment with mavacamten. In this clinical case, after 4 weeks of treatment, the patient experienced nearly 50% reduction in LVOT gradients, without decline in LV systolic function. The ECGs performed at baseline and after 4 weeks of treatment showed the same heart rate, excluding the hypothesis of a rate-dependent intraventricular conduction block. In our patient, the complete LBBB was first documented in 2021, and it was reported at every follow-up visit done since then. Mavacamten, by decreasing the fraction of myosin available to interact with actin, mitigates the pathological hypercontractility characteristic of this clinical phenotype, facilitates normalization of diastolic myocardial relaxation, and reduces myocardial wall stress throughout all phases of the cardiac cycle, thereby enhancing diastolic tissue perfusion. At this time, we are not able to state with certainty that this finding resulted directly from mavacamten use, but we believe that a reduction in wall tension and the newly reacquired diastolic relaxation might have contributed to this phenomenon, possibly reducing wall stress and microvascular ischaemia at the level of the interventricular septum—where the left bundle is located—overall allowing the return to a normal intraventricular conduction. In our opinion, this case represents one additional piece of evidence supporting the positive electrical and structural remodelling deriving from mavacamten use.

Conclusion

This clinical case reports the remarkable clinical and haemodynamic improvements induced by mavacamten in a patient with symptomatic obstructive HCM after just 4 weeks of therapy. Notably, the treatment was also associated with complete resolution of the patient’s long-standing LBBB—an unprecedented finding to our knowledge.

We hypothesize that by attenuating excessive hypercontractility and restoring myocardial relaxation, mavacamten may have reduced septal wall stress and enhanced septal perfusion, thereby creating favourable conditions for the restoration of a normal intraventricular conduction. This unique observation adds to the growing body of evidence supporting the beneficial electrical and structural remodelling associated with mavacamten use and warrants further investigation to determine the long-term outcomes of this first-in-class agent.

Approximate optimality conditions and sensitivity analysis in nearly convex optimization
Nguyen Van Tuyen 1 , Liguo Jiao 2 , Vu Hong Quan 3 , Duong Thi Viet An 4

Abstract
. In this paper, approximate optimality conditions and sensitivity analysis in nearly convex optimization are discussed. More precisely, as in the spirit of convex analysis, we introduce the concept of ε \varepsilon -subdifferential for nearly convex functions. Then, we examine some significant properties and rules for the ε \varepsilon -subdifferential. These rules are applied to study optimality conditions as well as sensitivity analysis for parametric nearly convex optimization problems, which are two important topics in optimization theory.

1.
Introduction

In the 1960s, Minty and Rockafellar introduced nearly convex sets
[
18
]
,
[
23
]
. The nearly convex set is defined based on convexity by requiring that the set under consideration lies between a convex set and its closure in the Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. The natural reason to study nearly convex sets is that the domain and the range of any maximal monotone mapping are nearly convex. In particular, for a proper lower semicontinuous convex function its subdifferential domain is always nearly convex
[
24
, Theorem 12.41]
. Moreover, the classical notion of convexity and convex analysis has been extensively studied by prominent mathematicians and experts in applied fields. Another motivation is that researchers want to beyond convexity by introducing and studying many generalized convexity notions for sets and functions. All these motivate the researcher’s systematic study of nearly convex sets. Some properties of nearly convex sets have been partially studied in
[
4
]
,
[
7
]
,
[
8
]
,
[
19
]
, and
[
22
]
from different perspectives.

Very recently, Nam and his co-workers in
[
22
]
introduced the concept of nearly convex set-valued mappings and investigated the fundamental properties of these mappings. Additionally, the authors establish a geometric approach for generalized differentiation of nearly convex set-valued mappings and nearly convex functions. These contributions expand the current knowledge of nearly convex sets and functions while providing several new results involving nearly convex set-valued mappings. Despite being introduced in the early age of convex analysis, the notion of the near convexity had not been systematically studied in the literature. The new development also creates opportunities for further study, from nearly convex sets to nearly convex functions and nearly convex set-valued mappings.

To deal with optimization problems, one often uses optimality conditions, in particular, necessary optimality conditions. Necessary optimality conditions help us solve problems through manual calculations and are useful as stopping criteria in algorithms. Meanwhile, sensitivity analysis of parametric optimization problems is not only theoretically interesting but also critically important. It allows us to understand the behaviors of the optimal value function when the parameter of the problem undergoes perturbations. Many researchers have made contributions to these research directions, for example, An and Yen
[
2
]
, An and co-authors
[
3
]
, Ioffe and Penot
[
16
]
, Jourani
[
17
]
, Mordukhovich and co-authors
[
20
]
. It is well recognized that qualification conditions are sufficient conditions for the validity of fundamental calculus rules in variational analysis, convex analysis, and optimization theory. These conditions play vital roles in deriving intersection rules for normal cones. The latter rule plays an important role in the study of optimality conditions and sensitivity analysis of parametric optimization problems. Therefore, one of the biggest challenges is to apply the subdifferential sum rules with the weakest qualification conditions.

\varepsilon=0.
This made it possible to use
\varepsilon

6

In the present paper, as in the spirit of convex analysis, we introduce the concept of the
\varepsilon
-subdifferential for nearly convex functions. We prove some significant properties and rules related to it. Then we use the
\varepsilon
-subdifferential to study optimality conditions as well as sensitivity analysis for parametric nearly convex optimization problems, which are two important topics in optimization theory.

Our paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we provide an overview of the definition and properties of the
\varepsilon
- subdifferential of nearly convex functions. In Section 3, we present optimality conditions for nearly convex optimization problems. Sensitivity analysis for parametric nearly convex optimization problems is discussed in Section 4.

Throughout the paper, the space
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is equipped with the usual scalar product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
and the corresponding Euclidean norm
\|\cdot\|

(resp.,
\mathbb{B}(x;\delta)

The interior (resp., closure) of
D

2.
\varepsilon
-subdifferential of nearly convex functions

In this section, we introduce the concept
\varepsilon
-subdifferential for nearly convex functions. Then we explore some properties of this subdifferential.

Let us first recall that the
relative interior
of an arbitrary set
D
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is defined by

\mbox{\rm ri}\,D:=\{a\in D\mid\mbox{there exists }\delta>0\ \mbox{such that }B(a;\delta)\cap\mbox{\rm aff}\,D\subset D\}.

By definition, it is easy to see that
a\in\mbox{\rm ri}\,D
if and only if
a\in\mbox{\rm aff}\,D
and there exists

\delta>0
such that

B(a;\delta)\cap\mbox{\rm aff}\,D\subset D.

Definition 2.1
.

A subset
D
of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is said to be
nearly convex
if there exists a convex set
E
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
E\subset D\subset\overline{E}.

Clearly, any convex set is nearly convex and any nearly convex subset of
\mathbb{R}
is convex. However, in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
n\geq 2
there are many nearly convex sets which are not convex, see, for example
[
19
]
.

It is not hard to check that if
D_{1}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
D_{2}\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}
are nearly convex, then
D_{1}\times D_{2}
is nearly convex as well.

Consider a function
\varphi:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\overline{\mathbb{R}}
having values in the extended real line
\overline{\mathbb{R}}:=[-\infty,\infty]
. One says that
\varphi
is
proper
if the
domain

{\rm{dom}}\,\varphi:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid\varphi(x)<\infty\}
is nonempty and if
\varphi(x)>-\infty
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. The set
{\rm{epi}}\,\varphi:=\{(x,\alpha)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}\mid\alpha\geq\varphi(x)\}
is called the
epigraph
of
\varphi
. If
{\rm{epi}}\,\varphi
is a convex (resp., nearly convex) subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times{\mathbb{R}}
, then
\varphi
is said to be a
convex
(resp.,
nearly convex
) function.
Due to the continuity of a convex function
\varphi
in
\mbox{\rm ri}\,(\mbox{\rm dom}\,\varphi),
it knows that the “near” convexity only happens in the boundary of its domain.

Let
G:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{R}^{m}
be a set-valued mapping. The
domain
and the
graph
of
G
are given, respectively, by

{\rm dom}\,G:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid G(x)\not=\emptyset\}

and

{\rm gph}\,G:=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\mid y\in G(x)\}.

The set-valued mapping
G
is called
proper
if
\mbox{\rm dom}\,G\not=\emptyset.
We say that
G
is
nearly convex

if its graph is a nearly convex set in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}
.

Definition 2.2
.

Consider a proper function
\varphi\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
. Let
\varepsilon\geq 0
and
\bar{x}\in\mbox{\rm dom}\,\varphi
. The
\varepsilon
-subdifferential
of
\varphi
at
\bar{x}
is defined by

\partial_{\varepsilon}\varphi(\bar{x}):=\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid\langle\xi,x-\bar{x}\rangle-\varepsilon\leq\varphi(x)-\varphi(\bar{x}),\ \ \forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\}.

The set
\partial_{\varepsilon}\varphi(\bar{x})
reduces to the subdifferential
\partial\varphi(\bar{x})
when
\varepsilon=0.
We will study some properties of the
\varepsilon
-subdifferential of nearly convex functions later.

The following example shows that the traditional subdifferential
\partial\varphi(\bar{x})
may be empty, meanwhile for all
\varepsilon>0
, the
\varepsilon
-subdifferential is nonempty.

Example 2.1
.

Consider the function
\varphi:\mathbb{R}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
given by

\varphi(x)=\begin{cases}-\sqrt{x}&\mbox{if }\ \ x\in[0,1),\\
1&\mbox{if}\ \ x=1,\\
\infty&\mbox{otherwise,}\end{cases}

which is indeed a nearly convex function.

Let
\bar{x}=0
and for every
\varepsilon>0
, by definition, one has

\displaystyle\partial_{\varepsilon}\varphi(\bar{x})

\displaystyle=\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}\mid\langle\xi,x-\bar{x}\rangle\leq\varphi(x)-\varphi(\bar{x})+\varepsilon,\ \forall x\in\mathbb{R}\}

\displaystyle=\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}\mid\xi x\leq\varphi(x)+\varepsilon,\ \forall x\in\mathbb{R}\}

\displaystyle=\left(-\infty,-\frac{1}{4\varepsilon}\right].

However, it is easy to check that
\partial\varphi(\bar{x})=\emptyset
.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
\varepsilon\geq 0
,
\Omega
be a nonempty subset in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\bar{x}\in\Omega
. The
\varepsilon
-normal set
to
\Omega
at
\bar{x}
is defined by

N_{\varepsilon}(\bar{x};\Omega):=\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid\langle\xi,x-\bar{x}\rangle\leq\varepsilon,\ \ \forall x\in\Omega\}.

By definition, it is clear that
N_{\varepsilon}(\bar{x};\Omega)=\partial_{\varepsilon}\delta_{\Omega}(\bar{x})
, where
\delta_{\Omega}
is the
indicator function
of a set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Recall that
\delta_{\Omega}(x)=0
if
x\in\Omega
and
\delta_{\Omega}(x)=\infty
if
x\notin\Omega
. Since
\mbox{\rm epi}\,\delta_{\Omega}=\Omega\times[0,\infty)
, we see that
\delta_{\Omega}
is nearly convex if and only if
\Omega
is nearly convex.

When
\varepsilon=0,

N_{\varepsilon}(\bar{x};\Omega)
reduces to the normal cone of
\Omega
at
\bar{x}
, which is denoted by
N(\bar{x};\Omega).
However, in general
N_{\varepsilon}(\bar{x};\Omega)
is not a cone when
\varepsilon>0.

From the definition of the
\varepsilon
-normal set, one can define the
\varepsilon
-coderivative as follows.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
F\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{R}^{m}
be a nearly convex set-valued mapping. For each
\varepsilon\geq 0
, the
\varepsilon
-
coderivative
of
F
at
(\bar{x},\bar{y})\in\mbox{\rm gph}\,F
is the set-valued mapping
D^{*}_{\varepsilon}F(\bar{x},\bar{y})\colon\mathbb{R}^{m}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{R}^{n}
with the values

D^{*}_{\varepsilon}F(\bar{x},\bar{y})(v):=\{u\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid(u,-v)\in N_{\varepsilon}((\bar{x},\bar{y});\mbox{\rm gph}\,F)\},\ \ v\in\mathbb{R}^{m}.

The following proposition gives the relation between the
\varepsilon
-subdifferential and the
\varepsilon
-coderivative of its epigraphical mapping.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
\varphi\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
be a nearly convex function and let
\bar{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
be such that
\varphi(\bar{x})\in\mathbb{R}
. For
\varepsilon\geq 0
, we have

\partial_{\varepsilon}\varphi(\bar{x})=D^{*}_{\varepsilon}E_{\varphi}(\bar{x},\varphi(\bar{x}))(1),

where
E_{\varphi}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{R}
is the epigraphical mapping given by

E_{\varphi}(x)=\{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}\mid\varphi(x)\leq\lambda\},\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Proof.

The proof is similar adapted from
[
22
, Proposition 5.3]
. For
\varepsilon\geq 0
, we first show that

(1)

\displaystyle D^{*}_{\varepsilon}E_{\varphi}(\bar{x},\varphi(\bar{x}))(1)\subseteq\partial_{\varepsilon}\varphi(\bar{x}).

By the definition of the
\varepsilon
-coderivative, one has

\displaystyle D^{*}_{\varepsilon}E_{\varphi}(\bar{x},\varphi(\bar{x}))(1)

\displaystyle=\{u\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid(u,-1)\in N_{\varepsilon}((\bar{x},\varphi(\bar{x}));\mbox{\rm gph}\,E_{\varphi})\}

\displaystyle=\{u\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid(u,-1)\in N_{\varepsilon}((\bar{x},\varphi(\bar{x}));\mbox{\rm epi}\,\varphi)\}.

Since
D^{*}_{\varepsilon}E_{\varphi}(\bar{x},\varphi(\bar{x}))(1)=\emptyset
, the inclusion (
1
) is obvious.
We now pick an arbitrary vector
u\in D^{*}_{\varepsilon}E_{\varphi}(\bar{x},\varphi(\bar{x}))(1)
, then

\langle u,x-\bar{x}\rangle-(\lambda-\varphi(\bar{x}))\leq\varepsilon,\ \forall(x,\lambda)\in\mbox{\rm epi}\,\varphi.

It follows from the above inequality that
-\infty<\varphi(x)
for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Using this equality with
x\in\mbox{\rm dom}\,\varphi
and
\lambda=\varphi(x)
, one has

\langle u,x-\bar{x}\rangle\leq\varphi(x)-\varphi(\bar{x})+\varepsilon,\ \forall x\in\mbox{\rm dom}\,\varphi.

In the case, where
\varphi(x)=\infty
, the above inequality holds. So, from the latter, we get
u\in\partial_{\varepsilon}\varphi(\bar{x})
, which justifies (
1
).

To obtain the opposite inclusion, we take any
u\in\partial_{\varepsilon}\varphi(\bar{x})
. Then

\langle u,x-\bar{x}\rangle\leq\varphi(x)-\varphi(\bar{x})+\varepsilon,\ \forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

This implies that

\langle u,x-\bar{x}\rangle-(\lambda-\varphi(\bar{x}))\leq\varepsilon,\ \mbox{whenever }(x,\lambda)\in\mbox{\rm epi}\,\varphi.

Thus,
\partial_{\varepsilon}\varphi(\bar{x})\subset D^{*}_{\varepsilon}E_{\varphi}(\bar{x},\varphi(\bar{x}))(1).

If
\partial_{\varepsilon}\varphi(\bar{x})=\emptyset
, then the latter is obvious. Therefore, we conclude that

\partial_{\varepsilon}\varphi(\bar{x})=D^{*}_{\varepsilon}E_{\varphi}(\bar{x},\varphi(\bar{x}))(1).

∎

We now present some properties of the set
\varepsilon
-subdifferentials of nearly convex functions.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
\varphi\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
be a proper nearly convex function and
\varepsilon\geq 0
. Then, for every
\bar{x}\in\mbox{\rm ri}\,(\mbox{\rm dom}\,\varphi)
, the
\varepsilon
-subdifferential of
\varphi
at
\bar{x}
is nonempty, closed and convex. Furthermore,

(2)

\partial\varphi(\bar{x})=\bigcap_{\varepsilon>0}\partial_{\varepsilon}\varphi(\bar{x}).

Proof.

We first prove that
\partial_{\varepsilon}\varphi(\bar{x})
is nonempty for every
\bar{x}\in\mbox{\rm ri}\,(\mbox{\rm dom}\,\varphi)
and
\varepsilon\geq 0
. The nonemptiness of
\partial_{\varepsilon}\varphi(\bar{x})
when
\varepsilon=0
was already proved in
[
22
, Proposition 5.4(b)]
. We now consider the case that
\varepsilon>0
. By the near convexity of
\varphi
and
[
22
, Proposition 3.7]
, one has

\mbox{\rm ri}\,(\mbox{\rm epi}\,\varphi)=\{(x,\lambda)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}\mid x\in\mbox{\rm ri}\,(\mbox{\rm dom}\,\varphi),\ \varphi(x)<\lambda\}.

Hence,
\varphi(\bar{x})>\varphi(\bar{x})-\varepsilon
and so
(\bar{x},\varphi(\bar{x})-\varepsilon)\notin\mbox{\rm ri}\,(\mbox{\rm epi}\,\varphi)
. By
[
22
, Theorem 3.2]
,
\{(\bar{x},\varphi(\bar{x})-\varepsilon)\}
and
\mbox{\rm epi}\,\varphi
can be properly separated, i.e., there exist
(w,\beta)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
and
(x_{0},\lambda_{0})\in\mbox{\rm epi}\,\varphi
such that the following conditions hold:

(3)

\displaystyle\langle w,\bar{x}\rangle+\beta(\varphi(\bar{x})-\varepsilon)\leq\langle w,x\rangle+\beta.\lambda\ \ \forall(x,\lambda)\in\mbox{\rm epi}\,\varphi;

(4)

\displaystyle\langle w,\bar{x}\rangle+\beta(\varphi(\bar{x})-\varepsilon)<\langle w,x_{0}\rangle+\beta.\lambda_{0}.

It follows from (
3
) that

(5)

\langle w,\bar{x}\rangle+\beta(\varphi(\bar{x})-\varepsilon)\leq\langle w,x\rangle+\beta\varphi(x)\ \ \forall x\in\mbox{\rm dom}\,\varphi.

Taking
x=\bar{x}
in the inequality (
5
) yields
\beta\geq 0
. If
\beta=0
, then from (
3
) and (
4
), one has
\langle w,\bar{x}\rangle\leq\langle w,x\rangle
for all
x\in\mbox{\rm dom}\,\varphi
and
\langle w,\bar{x}\rangle<\langle w,x_{0}\rangle
. This means that
\{\bar{x}\}
and
\mbox{\rm dom}\,\varphi
can be properly separated. By
[
22
, Theorem 3.2]
,
\bar{x}\notin\mbox{\rm ri}\,(\mbox{\rm dom}\,\varphi)
, a contradiction. Therefore,
\beta>0
. Dividing both sides of (
5
) by
\beta
, we get

\left\langle-\tfrac{w}{\beta},x-\bar{x}\right\rangle-\varepsilon\leq\varphi(x)-\varphi(\bar{x}),\ \ \forall x\in\mbox{\rm dom}\,\varphi.

Clearly, the above condition always holds for
x\notin\mbox{\rm dom}\,\varphi
. So,
-\tfrac{w}{\beta}\in\partial_{\varepsilon}\varphi(\bar{x})
and hence,
\partial_{\varepsilon}\varphi(\bar{x})
is nonempty.

Next we will show the closedness of
\partial_{\varepsilon}\varphi(\bar{x})
. Assume that
\xi_{k}\in\partial_{\varepsilon}\varphi(\bar{x})
such that
\xi_{k}\to\xi
as
k\to\infty
. By definition, we have

\langle\xi_{k},x-\bar{x}\rangle-\varepsilon\leq\varphi(x)-\varphi(\bar{x})\ \ \text{for all}\ \ x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\ \ \text{and}\ \ k\in\mathbb{N}.

Taking the limit as
k\to\infty
, the above inequality leads to

\langle\xi,x-\bar{x}\rangle-\varepsilon\leq\varphi(x)-\varphi(\bar{x}),\ \ \forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}

and so
\xi\in\partial_{\varepsilon}\varphi(\bar{x})
, as required.

To prove the convexity of
\partial_{\varepsilon}\varphi(\bar{x})
, let
\xi_{1},\xi_{2}\in\partial_{\varepsilon}\varphi(\bar{x})
and
\lambda\in(0,1)
. Then, for each
i=1,2
, we have

\langle\xi_{i},x-\bar{x}\rangle-\varepsilon\leq\varphi(x)-\varphi(\bar{x}),\ \ \forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Hence,

\displaystyle\langle\lambda\xi_{1}+(1-\lambda)\xi_{2},x-\bar{x}\rangle-\varepsilon

\displaystyle=\lambda[\langle\xi_{1},x-\bar{x}\rangle-\varepsilon]+(1-\lambda)[\langle\xi_{2},x-\bar{x}\rangle-\varepsilon]

\displaystyle\leq\lambda(\varphi(x)-\varphi(\bar{x}))+(1-\lambda)(\varphi(x)-\varphi(\bar{x}))

\displaystyle=\varphi(x)-\varphi(\bar{x})

for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. This means that
\lambda\xi_{1}+(1-\lambda)\xi_{2}\in\partial_{\varepsilon}\varphi(\bar{x})
and the convexity of
\partial_{\varepsilon}\varphi(\bar{x})
follows.

Lastly, we prove (
2
). By definition, it is clear that
\partial\varphi(\bar{x})\subset\partial_{\varepsilon}\varphi(\bar{x})
for every
\varepsilon>0
. Hence,

\partial\varphi(\bar{x})\subset\bigcap_{\varepsilon>0}\partial_{\varepsilon}\varphi(\bar{x}).

Conversely, assume that
\xi\in\bigcap\limits_{\varepsilon>0}\partial_{\varepsilon}\varphi(\bar{x})
. Then, one has

\langle\xi,x-\bar{x}\rangle-\varepsilon\leq\varphi(x)-\varphi(\bar{x})\ \ \text{for all}\ \ x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\ \ \text{and}\ \ \varepsilon>0.

Taking the limit as
\varepsilon\to 0
, the above inequality leads to

\langle\xi,x-\bar{x}\rangle\leq\varphi(x)-\varphi(\bar{x})\ \ \forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Thus
\xi\in\partial\varphi(\bar{x})
and so

\bigcap_{\varepsilon>0}\partial_{\varepsilon}\varphi(\bar{x})\subset\partial\varphi(\bar{x}),

which completes the proof.
∎

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
\varphi\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
be a proper nearly convex function and
\varepsilon\geq 0
. Then

\partial_{\varepsilon}(\lambda\varphi)(\bar{x})=\lambda\partial_{\varepsilon/\lambda}\varphi(\bar{x})\ \ \forall\lambda>0,\forall\bar{x}\in\mbox{\rm dom}\,\varphi.

Proof.

For
\lambda>0
, by the definition of the
\varepsilon
-subdifferential one has

\displaystyle\xi\in\partial_{\varepsilon}(\lambda\varphi)(\bar{x})\

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\langle\xi,x-\bar{x}\rangle-\varepsilon\leq\lambda\varphi(x)-\lambda\varphi(\bar{x}),\ \forall x\in\mbox{\rm dom}\,\varphi

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\tfrac{1}{\lambda}\langle\xi,x-\bar{x}\rangle-\tfrac{\varepsilon}{\lambda}\leq\varphi(x)-\varphi(\bar{x}),\ \forall x\in\mbox{\rm dom}\,\varphi

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\tfrac{\xi}{\lambda}\in\partial_{\varepsilon/\lambda}\varphi(\bar{x}),

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\xi\in\lambda\partial_{\varepsilon/\lambda}\varphi(\bar{x}).

The proof is complete.
∎

In convex analysis and optimization, summing two functions plays an important role. The Moreau–Rockafellar Theorem can be viewed as a well-known result, which describes the subdifferential of the sum of two subdifferentiable functions. In the same way, one can get a sum rule for
\varepsilon
-subdifferentials of two nearly convex functions.

Firstly, we formally introduce the notion of conjugate below.

Definition 2.5
.

Consider a function
\varphi:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
. The
conjugate
of
\varphi
is

\varphi^{*}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
and is defined as

\varphi^{*}(\xi)=\sup\limits_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\{\langle\xi,x\rangle-\varphi(x)\}.

In order to study optimality conditions as well as sensitivity analysis of optimization problems, we need the subdifferential calculus rules, especially, sum rules play important roles among them.
The following proof for sum rules of nearly convex functions is based on the proof of
[
13
, Theorem 3.1.1]
and
[
10
, Theorem 2.115]
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
\varphi_{i}\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
,
i=1,2
, be two proper nearly convex functions and
\varepsilon\geq 0
. If the qualification condition

(6)

\mbox{\rm ri}\,(\mbox{\rm dom}\,\varphi_{1})\cap\mbox{\rm ri}\,(\mbox{\rm dom}\,\varphi_{2})\neq\emptyset

holds, then
\varphi_{1}+\varphi_{2}
is nearly convex and

\partial_{\varepsilon}(\varphi_{1}+\varphi_{2})(\bar{x})=\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\varepsilon_{1}\geq 0,\varepsilon_{2}\geq 0,\\
\varepsilon_{1}+\varepsilon_{2}=\varepsilon\end{subarray}}[\partial_{\varepsilon_{1}}\varphi_{1}(\bar{x})+\partial_{\varepsilon_{2}}\varphi_{2}(\bar{x})]

for all
\bar{x}\in\mbox{\rm dom}\,\varphi_{1}\cap\mbox{\rm dom}\,\varphi_{2}
.

Proof.

The near convexity of
\varphi_{1}+\varphi_{2}
follows from
[
22
, Corollary 4.3]
. Now, let
\varepsilon_{1}\geq 0
and
\varepsilon_{2}\geq 0
be such that
\varepsilon_{1}+\varepsilon_{2}=\varepsilon
. The inclusion

\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\varepsilon_{1}\geq 0,\varepsilon_{2}\geq 0,\\
\varepsilon_{1}+\varepsilon_{2}=\varepsilon\end{subarray}}[\partial_{\varepsilon_{1}}\varphi_{1}(\bar{x})+\partial_{\varepsilon_{2}}\varphi_{2}(\bar{x})]\subset\partial_{\varepsilon}(\varphi_{1}+\varphi_{2})(\bar{x})

is based on the concept of
\varepsilon
-subdifferential. We now prove the opposite of this inclusion. Suppose that
\xi\in\partial_{\varepsilon}(\varphi_{1}+\varphi_{2})(\bar{x})
. By the definition of
the
\varepsilon
-subdifferential, one has

(\varphi_{1}+\varphi_{2})(x)-(\varphi_{1}+\varphi_{2})(\bar{x})\geq\langle\xi,x-\bar{x}\rangle-\varepsilon,\ \forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

From the definition of the conjugate function,
\xi\in\partial_{\varepsilon}(\varphi_{1}+\varphi_{2})(\bar{x})
means that

(7)

\displaystyle(\varphi_{1}+\varphi_{2})^{*}(\xi)+(\varphi_{1}+\varphi_{2})(\bar{x})-\langle\xi,\bar{x}\rangle\leq\varepsilon.

Since the condition (
6
) holds, by
[
15
, Theorem 6.6]
we have

(\varphi_{1}+\varphi_{2})^{*}(\xi)=(\varphi^{*}_{1}\square\varphi^{*}_{2})(\xi):=\inf\left\{\varphi^{*}_{1}(\xi_{1})+\varphi^{*}_{2}(\xi_{2})\mid\xi_{1}+\xi_{2}=\xi\right\},\ \forall\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}

and the infimum is attained. In other words, one can find
\xi_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{n},i=1,2
with
\xi_{1}+\xi_{2}=\xi
such that

(\varphi_{1}+\varphi_{2})^{*}(\xi)=\varphi^{*}_{1}(\xi_{1})+\varphi^{*}_{2}(\xi_{2}).

Combining the latter with (
7
) yields that

(\varphi^{*}_{1}(\xi_{1})+\varphi_{1}(\bar{x})-\langle\xi_{1},\bar{x}\rangle)+(\varphi^{*}_{2}(\xi_{2})+\varphi_{2}(\bar{x})-\langle\xi_{2},\bar{x}\rangle)\leq\varepsilon.

Then, by defining
\varepsilon_{i}=\varphi^{*}_{i}(\xi_{i})+\varphi_{i}(\bar{x})-\langle\xi_{i},\bar{x}\rangle,\ i=1,2
, one has
\varepsilon_{i}\geq 0
and
\varepsilon_{1}+\varepsilon_{2}\leq\varepsilon
for
i=1,2.

Moreover, by the definition of the conjugate function,

\displaystyle\varphi_{i}(x)-\varphi_{i}(\bar{x})

\displaystyle\geq\langle\xi_{i},x-\bar{x}\rangle-\varepsilon_{i}

\displaystyle\geq\langle\xi_{i},x-\bar{x}\rangle-\bar{\varepsilon}_{i},

where
\bar{\varepsilon}_{i}=\varepsilon_{i}+\tfrac{\varepsilon-\varepsilon_{1}-\varepsilon_{2}}{2}\geq\varepsilon_{i},i=1,2
. So, for
i=1,2
,

\xi_{i}\in\partial_{\bar{\varepsilon}_{i}}\varphi_{i}(\bar{x}),

with
\bar{\varepsilon}_{1}+\bar{\varepsilon}_{2}=\varepsilon
. Therefore,

\xi=\xi_{1}+\xi_{2}\in\partial_{\bar{\varepsilon}_{1}}\varphi_{1}(\bar{x})+\partial_{\bar{\varepsilon}_{2}}\varphi_{2}(\bar{x})
.
Since
\xi\in\partial_{\varepsilon}(\varphi_{1}+\varphi_{2})(\bar{x})
was taken arbitrarily, we have

\partial_{\varepsilon}(\varphi_{1}+\varphi_{2})(\bar{x})\subset\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\varepsilon_{1}\geq 0,\varepsilon_{2}\geq 0,\\
\varepsilon_{1}+\varepsilon_{2}=\varepsilon\end{subarray}}[\partial_{\varepsilon_{1}}\varphi_{1}(\bar{x})+\partial_{\varepsilon_{2}}\varphi_{2}(\bar{x})],

which completes the proof of the proposition.
∎

We consider the following illustrative example.

Example 2.2
.

Consider the nearly convex functions
\varphi_{i}:\mathbb{R}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
,
i=1,2,
given, respectively, by

\varphi_{1}(x)=\begin{cases}-\sqrt{x}&\mbox{if }\ \ x\in[0,1),\\
1&\mbox{if}\ \ x=1,\\
\infty&\mbox{otherwise,}\end{cases}

and

\varphi_{2}(x)=\begin{cases}-\sqrt{x}&\mbox{if }x\in[0,1],\\
\infty&\mbox{ otherwise.}\end{cases}

Then, one has

(\varphi_{1}+\varphi_{2})(x)=\begin{cases}-2\sqrt{x}&\mbox{if }x\in[0,1),\\
0&\mbox{ if }x=1,\\
\infty&\mbox{ otherwise}.\end{cases}

Let
\bar{x}=0
. Obviously,
\bar{x}\in\mbox{\rm dom}\,\varphi_{1}\cap\mbox{\rm dom}\,\varphi_{2}
and the condition (
6
) holds. By similar way as in Example
2.1
,
for every
\varepsilon_{1}>0,\varepsilon_{2}>0
, one obtains

\displaystyle\partial_{\varepsilon_{1}}\varphi_{1}(\bar{x})=\left(-\infty,-\tfrac{1}{4\varepsilon_{1}}\right]\quad{\textrm{ and }}\quad\partial_{\varepsilon_{2}}\varphi_{2}(\bar{x})=\left(-\infty,-\tfrac{1}{4\varepsilon_{2}}\right].

In the case, where
\varepsilon_{1}=0
(resp.,
\varepsilon_{2}=0
) one has
\partial_{\varepsilon_{1}}\varphi_{1}(\bar{x})=\emptyset
(resp.,
\partial_{\varepsilon_{2}}\varphi_{2}(\bar{x})=\emptyset
).
Meanwhile, for
\varepsilon>0
, one has

\partial_{\varepsilon}(\varphi_{1}+\varphi_{2})(\bar{x})=\left(-\infty,-\tfrac{1}{\varepsilon}\right].

Consequently,

\partial_{\varepsilon}(\varphi_{1}+\varphi_{2})(\bar{x})=\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\varepsilon_{1}\geq 0,\varepsilon_{2}\geq 0,\\
\varepsilon_{1}+\varepsilon_{2}=\varepsilon\end{subarray}}[\partial_{\varepsilon_{1}}\varphi_{1}(\bar{x})+\partial_{\varepsilon_{2}}\varphi_{2}(\bar{x})].

When
\varepsilon=0
, one has the following corollary.

Corollary 2.1
.

Let
\varphi_{i}\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
,
i=1,2
, be two proper nearly convex functions. If (
6
) holds, then
one has

(8)

\partial(\varphi_{1}+\varphi_{2})(\bar{x})=\partial\varphi_{1}(\bar{x})+\partial\varphi_{2}(\bar{x})

for all
\bar{x}\in\mbox{\rm dom}\,\varphi_{1}\cap\mbox{\rm dom}\,\varphi_{2}
.

Let us consider an example that shows that (
8
) can be invalid providing that (
6
) is violated.

Example 2.3
.

Consider the following two functions:

\varphi_{1}(x)=\begin{cases}-\sqrt{x}&\mbox{if }x\in[0,1],\\
\infty&\mbox{otherwise},\end{cases}

and

\varphi_{2}(x)=\begin{cases}-\sqrt{-x}&\mbox{if }x\in[-1,0],\\
\infty&\mbox{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Clearly,
\varphi_{1}
and
\varphi_{2}
are convex functions. In particular,
\varphi_{1}
and
\varphi_{2}
are nearly convex functions.
By a simple calculation, one gets

(\varphi_{1}+\varphi_{2})(x)=\begin{cases}0&\mbox{if }x=0,\\
\infty&\mbox{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Let
\bar{x}=0.
On one hand, it is not difficult to see that

\partial(\varphi_{1}+\varphi_{2})(\bar{x})=\mathbb{R}.
On the other hand, one has
\partial\varphi_{1}(\bar{x})=\emptyset.
Consequently, (
8
) does not hold. It is emphasized here that
\mbox{\rm ri}\,(\mbox{\rm dom}\,\varphi_{1})\cap\mbox{\rm ri}\,(\mbox{\rm dom}\,\varphi_{2})=\emptyset.

From Theorem
2.1
, and the relationship between the
\varepsilon
-normal set and the
\varepsilon
-subdifferential of the indicator function, the following corollary is obtained.

Corollary 2.2
.

Let
\Omega_{1},

\Omega_{2}
be two nearly convex sets and
\varepsilon\geq 0
. If
\mbox{\rm ri}\,\Omega_{1}\cap\mbox{\rm ri}\,\Omega_{2}\neq\emptyset,
then for each
\bar{x}\in\Omega_{1}\cap\Omega_{2},
we have

N_{\varepsilon}(\bar{x};\Omega_{1}\cap\Omega_{2})=\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\varepsilon_{1}\geq 0,\varepsilon_{2}\geq 0,\\
\varepsilon_{1}+\varepsilon_{2}=\varepsilon\end{subarray}}[N_{\varepsilon_{1}}(\bar{x};\Omega_{1})+N_{\varepsilon_{2}}(\bar{x};\Omega_{2})].

The next corollary plays an important role in deriving the subsequent results, so one presents here a detailed proof.

Corollary 2.3

(Epsilon coderivative of sum)
.

Suppose that
F_{1},F_{2}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{R}^{m}
are nearly convex set-valued mappings and
\varepsilon\geq 0
. If the qualification condition

(9)

\displaystyle\mbox{\rm ri}\,(\mbox{\rm dom}\,F_{1})\cap\mbox{\rm ri}\,(\mbox{\rm dom}\,F_{2})\not=\emptyset

is satisfied, then one has

(10)

D^{*}_{\varepsilon}(F_{1}+F_{2})(\bar{x},\bar{y})(v)=\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\varepsilon_{1}\geq 0,\varepsilon_{2}\geq 0,\\
\varepsilon_{1}+\varepsilon_{2}=\varepsilon\end{subarray}}[D^{*}_{\varepsilon_{1}}F_{1}(\bar{x},\bar{y}_{1})(v)+D^{*}_{\varepsilon_{2}}F_{2}(\bar{x},\bar{y}_{2})(v)]

holds for every
v\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
and
(\bar{y}_{1},\bar{y}_{2})\in S(\bar{x},\bar{y})
, where

S(\bar{x},\bar{y})=\{(\bar{y}_{1},\bar{y}_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\mid\bar{y}=\bar{y}_{1}+\bar{y}_{2},\ \bar{y}_{i}\in F_{i}(\bar{x}),i=1,2\}.

Proof.

We first note that, the qualification condition (
9
) implies that
F_{1}+F_{2}
is nearly convex by
[
22
, Theorem 4.2]
. For any
\varepsilon\geq 0
,
(\bar{y}_{1},\bar{y}_{2})\in S(\bar{x},\bar{y})
and
v\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
, we take any
u\in D^{*}_{\varepsilon}(F_{1}+F_{2})(\bar{x},\bar{y})(v).
By definition, one has

(11)

\displaystyle(u,-v)\in N_{\varepsilon}((\bar{x},\bar{y});\mbox{\rm gph}\,(F_{1}+F_{2})).

Consider two sets
\Omega_{1}
and
\Omega_{2}
defined as follows:

\displaystyle\Omega_{1}

\displaystyle=\{(x,y_{1},y_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\mid y_{1}\in F_{1}(x)\}=\mbox{\rm gph}\,F_{1}\times\mathbb{R}^{m},

\displaystyle\Omega_{2}

\displaystyle=\{(x,y_{1},y_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\mid y_{2}\in F_{2}(x)\}.

By the construction of
\Omega_{1}
and
\Omega_{2}
, from (
11
), one has

(u,-v,-v)\in N_{\varepsilon}((\bar{x},\bar{y}_{1},\bar{y}_{2});\Omega_{1}\cap\Omega_{2}).

The qualification condition (
9
) implies that
\mbox{\rm ri}\,\Omega_{1}\cap\mbox{\rm ri}\,\Omega_{2}\not=\emptyset
(see the proof of Theorem 4.2 in
[
22
]
). So, one can apply Corollary
2.2
to obtain

(u,-v,-v)\in N_{\varepsilon}((\bar{x},\bar{y}_{1},\bar{y}_{2});\Omega_{1}\cap\Omega_{2})=\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\varepsilon_{1}\geq 0,\varepsilon_{2}\geq 0,\\
\varepsilon_{1}+\varepsilon_{2}=\varepsilon\end{subarray}}[N_{\varepsilon_{1}}((\bar{x},\bar{y}_{1},\bar{y}_{2});\Omega_{1})+N_{\varepsilon_{2}}((\bar{x},\bar{y}_{1},\bar{y}_{2});\Omega_{2})].

Hence, there exist
\varepsilon_{1}\geq 0
and
\varepsilon_{2}\geq 0
with
\varepsilon_{1}+\varepsilon_{2}=\varepsilon
such that

(u,-v,-v)\in N_{\varepsilon_{1}}((\bar{x},\bar{y}_{1},\bar{y}_{2});\Omega_{1})+N_{\varepsilon_{2}}((\bar{x},\bar{y}_{1},\bar{y}_{2});\Omega_{2}).

Consequently, one gets

(u,-v,-v)=(u_{1},v_{1},w_{1})+(u_{2},v_{2},w_{2}),

where
(u_{i},v_{i},w_{i})\in N_{\varepsilon_{i}}((\bar{x},\bar{y}_{1},\bar{y}_{2});\Omega_{i})
,
i=1,2
. It is easy to see that
w_{1}=0
,
v_{2}=0
,
(u_{1},v_{1})\in N_{\varepsilon_{1}}((\bar{x},\bar{y}_{1});\mbox{\rm gph}\,F_{1})
, and
(u_{2},w_{2})\in N_{\varepsilon_{2}}((\bar{x},\bar{y}_{2});\mbox{\rm gph}\,F_{2})
. So,
v_{1}=w_{2}=-v
and
u=u_{1}+u_{2}
.
The latter means that

u=u_{1}+u_{2}\in D^{*}_{\varepsilon_{1}}F_{1}(\bar{x},\bar{y}_{1})(v)+D^{*}_{\varepsilon_{2}}F_{2}(\bar{x};\bar{y}_{2})(v).

Hence,

D^{*}_{\varepsilon}(F_{1}+F_{2})(\bar{x},\bar{y})(v)\subset\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\varepsilon_{1}\geq 0,\varepsilon_{2}\geq 0,\\
\varepsilon_{1}+\varepsilon_{2}=\varepsilon\end{subarray}}[D^{*}_{\varepsilon_{1}}F_{1}(\bar{x},\bar{y}_{1})(v)+D^{*}_{\varepsilon_{2}}F_{2}(\bar{x};\bar{y}_{2})(v)].

The reverse of the above inclusion is obvious and we therefore get the sum rule (
10
).
∎

We now discuss the
\varepsilon
-coderivative of the intersection mapping.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
F_{i}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{R}^{m},
for
i=1,2,\ldots,p
be nearly convex set-valued mappings and let
F=\bigcap_{i=1}^{p}F_{i}
. Assume that the following qualification condition

(12)

\displaystyle\bigcap\limits_{i=1}^{p}\mbox{\rm ri}\,(\mbox{\rm gph}\,F_{i})\not=\emptyset

holds. Then, for any
\varepsilon\geq 0
and
(\bar{x},\bar{y})\in\mbox{\rm gph}\,F
one has

(13)

\displaystyle D^{*}_{\varepsilon}F(\bar{x},\bar{y})(v)\!\!=\!\!\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\varepsilon_{1}\geq 0,\ldots\varepsilon_{p}\geq 0,\\
\varepsilon_{1}+\ldots+\varepsilon_{p}=\varepsilon\end{subarray}}\!\{D^{*}_{\varepsilon_{1}}F_{1}(\bar{x},\bar{y})(v_{1})\!+...+\!D^{*}_{\varepsilon_{p}}F_{p}(\bar{x},\bar{y})(v_{p})\mid\!v_{1}+...+v_{p}\!=\!v\}.

Proof.

By the qualification condition (
12
), the set-valued mapping
F=\bigcap_{i=1}^{p}F_{i}
is nearly convex following
[
22
, Theorem 4.9]
. For
(\bar{x},\bar{y})\in\mbox{\rm gph}\,F
, take any
u\in D^{*}_{\varepsilon}F(\bar{x},\bar{y})(v)
. Then
(u,-v)\in N_{\varepsilon}((\bar{x},\bar{y});\mbox{\rm gph}\,F)
. Since
\mbox{\rm gph}\,F=\bigcap\limits_{i=1}^{p}\mbox{\rm gph}\,F_{i}
, by (
12
) and Corollary
2.2
one has

(14)

\displaystyle(u,-v)\!\in\!N_{\varepsilon}((\bar{x},\bar{y});\mbox{\rm gph}\,F)\!\!=\!\!\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\varepsilon_{1}\geq 0,\ldots,\varepsilon_{p}\geq 0,\\
\varepsilon_{1}+\ldots+\varepsilon_{p}=\varepsilon\end{subarray}}\!\big[N_{\varepsilon_{1}}((\bar{x},\bar{y});\mbox{\rm gph}\,F_{1})\!+\!\ldots\!+\!N_{\varepsilon_{p}}((\bar{x},\bar{y});\mbox{\rm gph}\,F_{p})\big].

Then, we can find
u_{1},\ldots,u_{p}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
v_{1},\ldots,v_{p}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
such that

u=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{p}u_{i},\ v=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{p}v_{i},\ (u_{i},-v_{i})\in N_{\varepsilon_{i}}((\bar{x},\bar{y});\mbox{\rm gph}\,F_{i}).

By the definition,
u_{i}\in D^{*}_{\varepsilon_{i}}F_{i}(\bar{x},\bar{y})(v_{i})
and hence

u\in\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\varepsilon_{1}\geq 0,\ldots,\varepsilon_{p}\geq 0,\\
\varepsilon_{1}+\ldots+\varepsilon_{p}=\varepsilon\end{subarray}}\big[N_{\varepsilon_{1}}((\bar{x},\bar{y});\mbox{\rm gph}\,F_{1})\!+\!\ldots\!+\!N_{\varepsilon_{p}}((\bar{x},\bar{y});\mbox{\rm gph}\,F_{p})\big],

which justifies the inclusion
``\subseteq"
in (
13
). The reverse inclusion follows directly from the definition of the
\varepsilon
-coderivative and the equality in (
14
).
∎

3.
Optimality conditions

In this section, we will study the optimality conditions for nearly convex optimization problems by using the
\varepsilon
-subdifferential.
Consider the following optimization problem

(P)

\inf\,\varphi(x)\ \ \text{subject to}\ \ x\in S,

where
S\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is nonempty and
\varphi\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
is bounded from below on
S
. When
S=\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we call (
P
) the unconstrained optimization problem.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\varepsilon>0
and
\bar{x}\in S
. A vector
\bar{x}
is said to be an
\varepsilon
-solution
of (
P
) if

\varphi(\bar{x})\leq\varphi(x)+\varepsilon\ \ \forall x\in S.

Remark 3.1
.

From the definition of the
\varepsilon
-subdifferential, it is easy to see that for
\varepsilon\geq 0
,
\bar{x}
is an
\varepsilon
-solution of the unconstrained optimization problem if and only if
0\in\partial_{\varepsilon}\varphi(\bar{x}).

We are now in a position to prove the necessary as well as the sufficient approximate optimality conditions of the nearly optimization problem (
P
).

Theorem 3.1
.

Assume that
\varphi
is a nearly convex function,
S
is a nearly convex set, and the following qualification condition holds

(15)

\mbox{\rm ri}\,(\mbox{\rm dom}\,\varphi)\cap\mbox{\rm ri}\,S\neq\emptyset.

Then,
\bar{x}\in S
is an
\varepsilon
-solution of (
P
) if and only if there exist
\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2}\geq 0
with
\varepsilon_{1}+\varepsilon_{2}=\varepsilon
such that

(16)

0\in\partial_{\varepsilon_{1}}\varphi(\bar{x})+N_{\varepsilon_{2}}(\bar{x};S).

Proof.

Using the fact that
\bar{x}
is an
\varepsilon
-solution of (
P
) if and only if
0\in\partial_{\varepsilon}(\varphi+\delta_{S}(\cdot))(\bar{x})
. In addition, we note that
\mbox{\rm dom}\,\delta_{S}=S
.
Now, applying Theorem
2.1
, where
\varphi
and
\delta_{S}
play the corresponding roles of
\varphi_{1}
and
\varphi_{2}
, one has

\displaystyle\partial_{\varepsilon}(\varphi+\delta_{S}(\cdot))(\bar{x})

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\varepsilon_{1}\geq 0,\varepsilon_{2}\geq 0,\\
\varepsilon_{1}+\varepsilon_{2}=\varepsilon\end{subarray}}[\partial_{\varepsilon_{1}}\varphi(\bar{x})+\partial_{\varepsilon_{2}}\delta_{S}(\bar{x})]

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\varepsilon_{1}\geq 0,\varepsilon_{2}\geq 0,\\
\varepsilon_{1}+\varepsilon_{2}=\varepsilon\end{subarray}}[\partial_{\varepsilon_{1}}\varphi(\bar{x})+N_{\varepsilon_{2}}(\bar{x};S)],

which justifies (
16
).
∎

We now give an illustration for Theorem
3.1
.

Example 3.1
.

Consider the problem (
P
) where
\varphi:\mathbb{R}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
and
S
are given, respectively, by

\varphi(x)=\begin{cases}|x|&\mbox{ if }x\in[-1,1),\\
2&\mbox{ if }x=1,\\
\infty&\mbox{ otherwise},\end{cases}

and
S=[0,\infty).
It is clear that
\varphi
is a nearly convex function,
S
is a convex set, and (
15
) is satisfied. In this case, we observe that
\bar{x}=0
is an
\varepsilon
-solution of (
P
) for every
\varepsilon\geq 0.
Meanwhile, for
\varepsilon_{1}\geq 0
,
\varepsilon_{2}\geq 0
,
by the example in
[
13
, pp. 93–94]
one has
\partial_{\varepsilon_{1}}\varphi(\bar{x})=[-1,1]
. Additionally, from
[
12
, Example 2.1]

N_{\varepsilon_{2}}(\bar{x};S)=N(\bar{x};S)=(-\infty,0].
Consequently,

0\in\partial_{\varepsilon_{1}}\varphi(\bar{x})+N_{\varepsilon_{2}}(\bar{x};S).

Given a nearly convex function
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{p}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
and a nearly set-valued mapping
G:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{R}^{p}
, we consider the
parametric nearly convex optimization problem under an inclusion constraint

(
P_{x}
)

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\min\{f(x,y)\mid y\in G(x)\}

depending on the parameter
x
.

The next theorem describes the necessary and sufficient approximate optimality conditions for
(P_{x})
at a given parameter
\bar{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\bar{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Suppose that
f(\bar{x},\cdot)
is bounded from below on
G(\bar{x})
and the following qualification condition

\displaystyle\mbox{\rm ri}\,(\mbox{\rm dom}\,f(\bar{x},\cdot))\cap\mbox{\rm ri}\,(G(\bar{x}))\not=\emptyset

is fulfilled.
Then,
\bar{y}\in G(\bar{x})
is an
\varepsilon
-solution of (

P
x

) if and only if there exist
\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2}\geq 0
with
\varepsilon_{1}+\varepsilon_{2}=\varepsilon
such that

(17)

\displaystyle 0\in\partial^{y}_{\varepsilon_{1}}f(\bar{x},\bar{y})+N_{\varepsilon_{2}}(\bar{y};G(\bar{x})),

where
\partial^{y}_{\varepsilon_{1}}f(\bar{x},\bar{y})
is the
\varepsilon_{1}
-subdifferential of the function
f(\bar{x},\cdot)
with respect to the variable
y
.

Proof.

Let
\bar{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Consider the function
\varphi_{G}(y)=f(\bar{x},y)+\delta_{G(\bar{x})}(y)
, where
\delta_{G(\bar{x})}(\cdot)
is the indicator function of the nearly convex set
G(\bar{x})
. The latter means that
\delta_{G(\bar{x})}(y)=0
for
y\in G(\bar{x})
and
\delta_{G(\bar{x})}(y)=\infty
for
y\notin G(\bar{x})
. We now apply Theorem
3.1
for the case where
f(\bar{x},\cdot)
and
G(\bar{x})
play the corresponding the role of
\varphi
and
S
. Consequently, we obtain (
17
).
∎

4.
Sensitivity Analysis

We now present some new results on sensitivity analysis of nearly convex optimization problems under inclusion constraints. By using the sum rules and appropriate qualification conditions, we will obtain formulas for computing the
\varepsilon
-subdifferential of the optimal value functions in both parametric unconstrained and constrained nearly convex optimization problems.

4.1.
Unconstrained nearly convex optimization problem

Consider the
parametric unconstrained nearly convex optimization problem

(18)

\displaystyle\min\{f(x,y)\mid y\in\mathbb{R}^{p}\}

depending on the parameter
x
, where
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{p}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
. The function
f
is called the
objective function
of (
18
). The
optimal value function

m:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\overline{\mathbb{R}}
of (
18
) is

(19)

\displaystyle m(x):=\inf\left\{f(x,y)\mid y\in\mathbb{R}^{p}\right\}.

The
solution set
of (
18
) with respect to a given parameter
\bar{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
is defined by

S(\bar{x}):=\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{p}\mid m(\bar{x})=f(\bar{x},y)\}.

For
\eta>0
, one calls
S_{\eta}(\bar{x}):=\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{p}\mid f(\bar{x},y)\leq m(\bar{x})+\eta\}

the
approximate solution set
of (
18
).

Under the near convexity of the objective function
f
, one has the optimal value function
m
is nearly convex. This result is a direct consequence of Theorem 5.2 in
[
15
]
.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{p}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
be a proper nearly convex function. Then, the optimal value function
m
given in (
19
) is nearly convex.

We now obtain formulas for the
\varepsilon
-subdifferential of
m
. In the convex setting, the following result can be found somewhere, for example, it was given in
[
21
, Corollary 5]
as a consequence of a more general result. The interested readers also see this result in
[
26
, Theorem 2.6.2]
and in
[
1
, Theorem 4.1]
. We now present a detailed, direct proof in the case of nearly convex case. Our arguments are based on a proof scheme of
[
21
]
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{p}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
be a proper nearly convex function and
m
be finite at
\bar{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.
Then, for every
\varepsilon\geq 0
,

(20)

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\partial_{\varepsilon}m(\bar{x})&=&\bigcap\limits_{\eta\;>\;0}\ \bigcap\limits_{y\,\in\,S_{\eta}(\bar{x})}\bigg\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid(\xi,0)\in\partial_{\varepsilon+\eta}f(\bar{x},y)\bigg\}\\
&=&\bigcap\limits_{\eta\;>\;0}\ \bigcup\limits_{y\,\in\,\mathbb{R}^{p}}\bigg\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid(\xi,0)\in\partial_{\varepsilon+\eta}f(\bar{x},y)\bigg\}.\end{array}

In particular,

(21)

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\partial m(\bar{x})&=&\bigcap\limits_{\eta\;>\;0}\ \bigcap\limits_{y\,\in\,S_{\eta}(\bar{x})}\bigg\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid(\xi,0)\in\partial_{\eta}f(\bar{x},y)\bigg\}\\
&=&\bigcap\limits_{\eta\;>\;0}\ \bigcup\limits_{y\,\in\,\mathbb{R}^{p}}\bigg\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid(\xi,0)\in\partial_{\eta}f(\bar{x},y)\bigg\}.\end{array}

In addition, if
S(\bar{x})\not=\emptyset
, then for every
\varepsilon\geq 0
, one has

(22)

\displaystyle\partial_{\varepsilon}m(\bar{x})=\big\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid(\xi,0)\in\partial_{\varepsilon}f(\bar{x},y)\big\},

for all
y\in S(\bar{x}).

Proof.

Set

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{\eta}(\bar{x})=\bigcap\limits_{y\,\in\,S_{\eta}(\bar{x})}\bigg\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid(\xi,0)\in\partial_{\varepsilon+\eta}f(\bar{x},y)\bigg\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{\eta}(\bar{x})=\bigcup\limits_{y\,\in\,\mathbb{R}^{p}}\bigg\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{p}\mid(\xi,0)\in\partial_{\varepsilon+\eta}f(\bar{x},y)\bigg\}.

So (
20
) means

\partial_{\varepsilon}m(\bar{x})=\bigcap\limits_{\eta\,>\,0}\mathcal{A}_{\eta}(\bar{x})=\bigcap\limits_{\eta\,>\,0}\mathcal{B}_{\eta}(\bar{x}).

We first observe that the set
S_{\eta}(\bar{x})
is nonempty for every
\eta>0
as
m(\bar{x})=\inf\limits_{y\,\in\,\mathbb{R}^{p}}\varphi(\bar{x},y)
by (
19
). Thus, it is obvious
\mathcal{A}_{\eta}(\bar{x})\subset\mathcal{B}_{\eta}(\bar{x})
for all
\eta>0
, and hence
\bigcap\limits_{\eta>0}\mathcal{A}_{\eta}(\bar{x})\subset\bigcap\limits_{\eta>0}\mathcal{B}_{\eta}(\bar{x})
. So, the equalities in (
20
) will be proved, if we can clarify that

(23)

\partial_{\varepsilon}m(\bar{x})\subset\bigcap\limits_{\eta\,>\,0}\mathcal{A}_{\eta}(\bar{x})

and

(24)

\bigcap\limits_{\eta\,>\,0}\mathcal{B}_{\eta}(\bar{x})\subset\partial_{\varepsilon}m(\bar{x}).

To show (
23
), we now take any
\xi\in\partial_{\varepsilon}m(\bar{x})
,
\eta>0
, and
y\in S_{\eta}(\bar{x})
. By the definition of the conjugate function, we observe that
\xi\in\partial_{\varepsilon}m(\bar{x})
if and only if

(25)

\displaystyle m(\bar{x})+m^{*}(\xi)\leq\langle\xi,\bar{x}\rangle+\varepsilon.

Adding
\eta>0
to both sides of (
25
) gives

(26)

\displaystyle m(\bar{x})+m^{*}(\xi)+\eta\leq\langle\xi,\bar{x}\rangle+\varepsilon+\eta.

Since
y\in S_{\eta}(\bar{x})
, one has
f(\bar{x},y)\leq m(\bar{x})+\eta
. So, (
26
) yields

(27)

\displaystyle f(\bar{x},y)+m^{*}(\xi)\leq\langle\xi,\bar{x}\rangle+\varepsilon+\eta.

For every
\zeta\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, by the definition of conjugate function, we have

\displaystyle m^{*}(\zeta)

\displaystyle=\sup\limits_{x\,\in\,\mathbb{R}^{n}}\big\{\langle\zeta,x\rangle-m(x)\big\}

\displaystyle=\sup\limits_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\big\{\langle\zeta,x\rangle-\inf\limits_{y\,\in\,\mathbb{R}^{p}}f(x,y)\big\}

\displaystyle=\sup\limits_{(x,y)\,\in\,\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{p}}\big\{\langle\zeta,x\rangle-f(x,y)\big\}

\displaystyle=\sup\limits_{(x,y)\,\in\,\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{p}}\big\{\langle(\zeta,0),(x,y)\rangle-f(x,y)\big\}

\displaystyle=f^{*}(\zeta,0).

Replacing
m^{*}(x^{*})=f^{*}(\zeta,0)
into (
27
), one obtains

(28)

\displaystyle f(\bar{x},y)+f^{*}(\xi,0)\leq\langle\xi,\bar{x}\rangle+\varepsilon+\eta.

Consequently, inequality (
28
) yields
(\xi,0)\in\partial_{\varepsilon+\eta}f(\bar{x},y)
for all
\eta>0
and
y\in S_{\eta}(\bar{x}).
In the other words
\xi\in\bigcap\limits_{\eta\,>\,0}\mathcal{A}_{\eta}(\bar{x})
, so (
23
) is valid.

Next, to prove (
24
), take any
x^{*}\in\bigcap\limits_{\eta>0}\mathcal{B}_{\eta}(\bar{x})
. Then, for every
\eta>0
, one can find
y\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
such that
(\xi,0)\in\partial_{\varepsilon+\eta}\varphi(\bar{x},y)
. The latter means that

f^{*}(\xi,0)+f(\bar{x},y)-\langle(\xi,0),(\bar{x},y)\rangle\leq\varepsilon+\eta,

or, equivalently,

(29)

\displaystyle f^{*}(\xi,0)+f(\bar{x},y)-\langle\xi,\bar{x}\rangle\leq\varepsilon+\eta.

Since
f^{*}(\xi,0)=m^{*}(\xi)
and
m(\bar{x})\leq\varphi(\bar{x},y)
, (
29
) implies

(30)

\displaystyle m^{*}(\xi)+m(\bar{x})-\langle\xi,\bar{x}\rangle\leq\varepsilon+\eta.

As (
30
) holds for every
\eta>0
, letting
\eta\to 0
yields

m^{*}(\xi)+m(\bar{x})-\langle\xi,\bar{x}\rangle\leq\varepsilon.

The last inequality gives
\xi\in\partial_{\varepsilon}m(\bar{x})
. Therefore, (
24
) is satisfied.

Combining (
23
) and (
24
) yields (
20
). For
\varepsilon=0
, from (
20
) one obtains (
21
).
∎

Here is a simple example designed to illustrate Theorem
4.1
.

Example 4.1
.

Consider
a function
f:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
given by

f(x,y)=\begin{cases}x^{2}+y^{2}&\text{if }(x,y)\in[-1,1]\times[-1,1]\setminus\left(\tfrac{1}{2},1\right),\\
\infty&\text{otherwise}.\\
\end{cases}

Then the optimal value function (
19
) of problem (
18
) is

m(x):=\inf\left\{f(x,y)\mid y\in\mathbb{R}\right\}=x^{2}.

Let
\bar{x}=0
. For every
\varepsilon\geq 0
, one has

\displaystyle\partial_{\varepsilon}m(\bar{x})

\displaystyle=\left\{x^{*}\in\mathbb{R}\mid x^{*}x\leq x^{2}+\varepsilon,\forall x\in\mathbb{R}\right\}

\displaystyle=\left\{x^{*}\in\mathbb{R}\mid-x^{2}+x^{*}x-\varepsilon\leq 0,\forall x\in\mathbb{R}\right\}

\displaystyle=[-2\sqrt{\varepsilon},2\sqrt{\varepsilon}].

In this case,
\bar{y}=0\in S(\bar{x})
. Thus we will show (
22
) is valid. It is easy to see that, for any function
\varphi\colon\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
of the form
\varphi(x,y)=\varphi_{1}(x)+\varphi_{2}(y)
for all
(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}
, one has

\partial_{\varepsilon}\varphi(x,y)\ \subseteq\ \partial_{\varepsilon}\varphi_{1}(x)\times\partial_{\varepsilon}\varphi_{2}(y)
.
We now apply this for the function
f
to obtain

\partial_{\varepsilon}f(\bar{x},\bar{y})\ {\subseteq}\ [-2\sqrt{\varepsilon},2\sqrt{\varepsilon}]\times[-2\sqrt{\varepsilon},2\sqrt{\varepsilon}]
and so

\displaystyle RHS_{\eqref{nonconstraint}}

\displaystyle=\left\{x^{*}\in\mathbb{R}\mid(x^{*},0)\in\partial_{\varepsilon}f(\bar{x},y)\right\}

\displaystyle=\{x^{*}\in\mathbb{R}\mid(x^{*},0)\in\partial_{\varepsilon}f(\bar{x},\bar{y})\}

\displaystyle=\{x^{*}\in\mathbb{R}\mid(x^{*},0)\in[-2\sqrt{\varepsilon},2\sqrt{\varepsilon}]\times[-2\sqrt{\varepsilon},2\sqrt{\varepsilon}]\}

\displaystyle=[-2\sqrt{\varepsilon},2\sqrt{\varepsilon}].

Therefore, the conclusion of Theorem
4.1
is justified.

4.2.
Constrained nearly convex optimization problems

In this subsection, we will extend the result of the previous subsection for constrained nearly convex optimization problems by using some suitable qualification conditions.

Let
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{p}\rightarrow\overline{\mathbb{R}}
be an extended real-valued function, and
G:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{R}^{p}
be a set-valued mapping. Consider the
parametric optimization problem under an inclusion constraint

(31)

\displaystyle\min\{f(x,y)\mid y\in G(x)\}

depending on the parameter
x
. The
optimal value function

m:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\overline{\mathbb{R}}
of (
31
) is

(32)

\displaystyle m(x):=\inf\left\{f(x,y)\mid y\in G(x)\right\}.

The usual convention
\inf\emptyset=\infty
forces
m(x)=\infty
for every
x\notin{\rm{dom}}\,G.

The
solution map

S:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{R}^{p}
of (
31
) is defined by

\displaystyle S(x)=\{y\in G(x)\mid m(x)=f(x,y)\}.

The
approximate solution set
of (
31
) is given by

(33)

\displaystyle S_{\eta}(\bar{x})=\{y\in G(\bar{x})\mid f(\bar{x},y)\leq m(\bar{x})+\eta\},\ \,\forall\eta>0.

The following result on the near convexity of the optimal value function is proved in
[
15
, Theorem 5.2]
.

Proposition 4.2
.

Suppose that
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{p}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
is a proper nearly convex function, and
G:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{R}^{p}

is a nearly convex set-valued mapping. Suppose furthermore that

\displaystyle\mbox{\rm ri}\,(\mbox{\rm dom}\,f)\cap\mbox{\rm ri}\,(\mbox{\rm gph}\,G)\not=\emptyset.

Then the optimal value function
m
given in (
32
) is nearly convex.

For any
\varepsilon\geq 0
and
\eta\geq 0
, define by
\varGamma(\eta+\varepsilon)
the set

\varGamma(\eta+\varepsilon)=\{(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})\mid\gamma_{1}\geq 0,\ \gamma_{2}\geq 0,\ \gamma_{1}+\gamma_{2}=\eta+\varepsilon\}.

We are now in a position to formulate the main result of this subsection.

Theorem 4.2
.

Suppose that
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{p}\rightarrow\overline{\mathbb{R}}
is a proper nearly convex function, and
G:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{R}^{p}
is a nearly convex set-valued mapping. Assume that the optimal value function
m
in (
32
) is finite at
\bar{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.
If the following qualification condition

(34)

\displaystyle\mbox{\rm ri}\,(\mbox{\rm dom}\,f)\cap\mbox{\rm ri}\,(\mbox{\rm gph}\,G)\not=\emptyset

holds. Then, for every
\varepsilon\geq 0
, we have

\begin{split}\partial_{\varepsilon}m(\bar{x})&=\bigcap\limits_{\eta>0}\bigcap\limits_{y\,\in\,S_{\eta}(\bar{x})}\bigcup_{(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})\,\in\,\varGamma(\eta+\varepsilon)}\bigg\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid(\xi,0)\in\partial_{\gamma_{1}}f(\bar{x},y)\!+\!N_{\gamma_{2}}\big((\bar{x},y);{\rm gph}\,G\big)\bigg\}\\
&=\bigcap\limits_{\eta>0}\ \bigcup\limits_{y\,\in\,\mathbb{R}^{p}}\ \bigcup_{(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})\,\in\,\varGamma(\eta+\varepsilon)}\bigg\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid(\xi,0)\in\partial_{\gamma_{1}}f(\bar{x},y)+N_{\gamma_{2}}\big((\bar{x},y);{\rm gph}\,G\big)\bigg\},\end{split}

where
S_{\eta}(\bar{x})
is given in (
33
).

Proof.

Since the qualification condition
34
holds, it follows that the optimal value function
m
is nearly convex by Proposition
4.2
.
We apply Theorem
4.1
to the case where
f(x,y)
plays the role of
\big(f+\delta_{\mbox{\rm gph}\,G}(\cdot)\big)(x,y)
, where
\delta_{\mbox{\rm gph}\,G}(\cdot)
is the indicator function of
\mbox{\rm gph}\,G
. Thus

(35)

\begin{split}\partial_{\varepsilon}m(\bar{x})&=\bigcap\limits_{\eta>0}\ \bigcap\limits_{y\,\in\,S_{\eta}(\bar{x})}\bigg\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid(\xi,0)\in\partial_{\varepsilon+\eta}\big(f+\delta_{\mbox{\rm gph}\,G}(\cdot)\big)(\bar{x},y)\bigg\}\\
&=\bigcap\limits_{\eta>0}\ \bigcup\limits_{y\,\in\,\mathbb{R}^{p}}\bigg\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid(\xi,0)\in\partial_{\varepsilon+\eta}\big(f+\delta_{\mbox{\rm gph}\,G}(\cdot)\big)(\bar{x},y)\bigg\}.\end{split}

We will clarify that

(36)

\displaystyle\partial_{\varepsilon+\eta}\!\big(f+\delta_{\mbox{\rm gph}\,G}(\cdot)\big)(\bar{x},y)\!\!=\!\!\bigcup_{(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})\,\in\,\varGamma(\eta+\varepsilon)}\bigg\{\partial_{\gamma_{1}}f(\bar{x},y)\!+\!N_{\gamma_{2}}((\bar{x},y);{\rm gph}\,G)\bigg\},

where
\varGamma(\eta+\varepsilon)=\{(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})\mid\gamma_{1}\geq 0,\ \gamma_{2}\geq 0,\ \gamma_{1}+\gamma_{2}=\varepsilon+\eta\}.

Indeed, suppose that (
34
) is fulfilled. Since
{\rm gph}\,G
is nearly convex,
\delta_{\mbox{\rm gph}\,G}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{p}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
is nearly convex. Moreover,
\mbox{\rm dom}\,\delta_{\mbox{\rm gph}\,G}={\rm gph}\,G
. So thanks to Theorem
2.1
, we have

\partial_{\varepsilon+\eta}\big(f+\delta_{\mbox{\rm gph}\,G}(\cdot)\big)(\bar{x},y)=\bigcup_{(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})\,\in\,\varGamma(\eta+\varepsilon)}\bigg\{\partial_{\gamma_{1}}f(\bar{x},y)+\partial_{\gamma_{2}}\delta_{\mbox{\rm gph}\,G}((\bar{x},y))\bigg\},

for any
(\bar{x},y)\,\in\,{\rm dom}\,f\cap\,{\rm gph}\,G
. Meanwhile, by the definition

\partial_{\gamma_{2}}\delta_{\mbox{\rm gph}\,G}\big((\bar{x},y)\big)=N_{\gamma_{2}}\big((\bar{x},y);{\rm gph}\,G\big).

Combining (
35
) with (
36
) gives the statement of the theorem.
∎

Let us consider an illustrative example for Theorem
4.2
.

Example 4.2
.

Let
G(x)=\{y\in\mathbb{R}\mid y\geq|x|\}
and

f(x,y)=\begin{cases}\tfrac{1}{2}|x|+\tfrac{3}{2}|y|&\mbox{if }(x,y)\in[-1,1]\times[-1,1]\setminus\left(\tfrac{1}{2},1\right),\\
\infty&\mbox{ otherwise}.\end{cases}

Then the optimal value function
m
of problem (
31
) is

m(x):=\inf\left\{f(x,y)\mid y\in G(x)\right\}=2|x|,\ \mbox{for all }x\in\mathbb{R}.

For
\bar{x}=0,
the approximate solution set is

\displaystyle S_{\eta}(\bar{x})

\displaystyle=\{y\in G(\bar{x})\mid f(\bar{x},y)\leq m(\bar{x})+\eta\},\ \,\forall\eta>0

\displaystyle=\{y\in G(\bar{x})\mid\tfrac{3}{2}|y|\leq\eta\},\ \,\forall\eta>0

\displaystyle=\{0\}.

For every
\varepsilon\geq 0
, from
[
13
, pp. 93–94]
and Proposition
2.3
, one has

\partial_{\varepsilon}m(x)=\partial_{\varepsilon}(2|x|)=2\partial_{\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}(|x|)=\begin{cases}\left[-2,-2-\tfrac{\varepsilon}{x}\right]&\mbox{if }x<-\tfrac{1}{4}\varepsilon,\\
[-2,2]&\mbox{if }-\tfrac{1}{4}\varepsilon\leq x\leq\tfrac{1}{4}\varepsilon,\\
\left[2-\tfrac{2\varepsilon}{x},2\right]&\mbox{if }x>\tfrac{1}{4}\varepsilon.\end{cases}

Hence,

\partial_{\varepsilon}m(\bar{x})=[-2,2].
For any
\gamma_{1}\geq 0
,
\gamma_{2}\geq 0
, one has

\partial_{\gamma_{1}}f(x,y)\ \subseteq\ \partial_{\gamma_{1}}f_{1}(x)\times\partial_{\gamma_{1}}f_{2}(y)

where
f_{1}(x)=\tfrac{1}{2}|x|,f_{2}(y)=\tfrac{3}{2}|y|
. From Proposition
2.3
and
[
13
, pp. 93–94]
, one gets

\partial_{\gamma_{1}}f_{1}(x)=\partial_{\gamma_{1}}\left(\tfrac{1}{2}|x|\right)=\tfrac{1}{2}\partial_{2\gamma_{1}}(|x|)=\begin{cases}\left[-\tfrac{1}{2},-\tfrac{1}{2}-\tfrac{\gamma_{1}}{x}\right]&\mbox{if }x<-\gamma_{1},\\
\\
\left[-\tfrac{1}{2},\tfrac{1}{2}\right]&\mbox{if }-\gamma_{1}\leq x\leq\gamma_{1},\\
\\
\left[\tfrac{1}{2}-\tfrac{\gamma_{1}}{x},\tfrac{1}{2}\right]&\mbox{if }x>\gamma_{1},\\
\end{cases}

and

\partial_{\gamma_{1}}f_{2}(y)=\partial_{\gamma_{1}}\left(\tfrac{3}{2}|y|\right)=\tfrac{3}{2}\partial_{2\gamma_{1}}(|y|)=\begin{cases}\left[-\tfrac{3}{2},-\tfrac{3}{2}-\tfrac{3\gamma_{1}}{y}\right]&\mbox{if }y<-\gamma_{1},\\
\\
\left[-\tfrac{3}{2},\tfrac{3}{2}\right]&\mbox{if }-\gamma_{1}\leq y\leq\gamma_{1},\\
\\
\left[\tfrac{3}{2}-\tfrac{3\gamma_{1}}{y},\tfrac{3}{2}\right]&\mbox{if }y>\gamma_{1}.\end{cases}

Hence, at
\bar{x}=0,

\bar{y}=0
,

\partial_{\gamma_{1}}f_{1}(\bar{x})=\left[-\tfrac{1}{2},-\tfrac{1}{2}\right]
and

\partial_{\gamma_{1}}f_{2}(\bar{y})=\left[-\tfrac{3}{2},-\tfrac{3}{2}\right]
. Consequently,

\partial_{\gamma_{1}}f(\bar{x},\bar{y})=\left[-\tfrac{1}{2},-\tfrac{1}{2}\right]\times\left[-\tfrac{3}{2},-\tfrac{3}{2}\right].

On the other hand, by
[
12
, Example 2.1]
, one has

N_{\gamma_{2}}\big((\bar{x},y);{\rm gph}\,G\big)=\begin{cases}\{(0,0)\}&\mbox{if }y>0,\\
\{(u,v)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\mid v\leq-|u|\}&\mbox{if }y=0,\\
\emptyset&\mbox{if }y<0.\end{cases}

Therefore,

\displaystyle\bigcap\limits_{\eta>0}\bigcap\limits_{y\,\in\,S_{\eta}(\bar{x})}\bigcup_{(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})\,\in\,\varGamma(\eta+\varepsilon)}\bigg\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}\mid(\xi,0)\in\partial_{\gamma_{1}}f(\bar{x},y)\!+\!N_{\gamma_{2}}\big((\bar{x},y);{\rm gph}\,G\big)\bigg\}

\displaystyle=\bigg\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}\mid(\xi,0)\in\left[-\tfrac{1}{2},-\tfrac{1}{2}\right]\times\left[-\tfrac{3}{2},-\tfrac{3}{2}\right]+\{(u,v)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\mid v\leq-|u|\}\bigg\}

\displaystyle=\big\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}\mid\xi\in[-2,2]\big\},

which justifies the conclusion of Theorem
4.2
.

Antibody framework engineering enables augmented cytokine receptor signaling capacities of bispecific single domain antibody-based IL-21 mimetics
Authors: Britta Lipinski, Laura Unmuth, Thi Hong Hue Tran, Jakob Harwood, Paul Arras, Stefan Becker, Julia Harwardt, Enrico Guarnera, Laura Helming, Rinat Zaynagetdinov, Davide Bertoldo, Arvind Rajpal, Desislava Elter, Lukas Pekar, Andreas Evers, Stefan Zielonka
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTIn this work, we developed bispecific antibody (bsAb)-derived surrogate agonists which mimic the function of IL-21 by targeting the IL-21 receptor composed of IL-21 R (CD360) and IL-2 Rγ (CD132). For this, antigen-specific VHHs (variable domains of the heavy chain of heavy-chain-only antibodies) were obtained by immunization of camelids and isolated using yeast surface display. Combinatorial reformatting of IL-21 R-specific single‑domain antibodies (sdAbs) and IL-2 Rγ-targeting paratopes into a monovalent bispecific antibody architecture enabled the identification of IL-21 mimetics displaying attenuated capacities in triggering STAT3 phosphorylation compared to the wild-type cytokine as demonstrated in NK-92 cells as well as peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs). Moreover, by applying different protein engineering strategies, we demonstrate that agonism capacities of the generated IL-21 mimetics, such as pSTAT3 induction or Granzyme B expression of cytotoxic T cells, can be significantly optimized. For this, framework mutations were introduced to engineer VHH:VHH interactions within the bispecific sdAb-Fc fusion geometry for a more rigid receptor targeting. Furthermore, we show that antibody format engineering, in which the VHHs were arranged in an IgG-like scaffold that replaces the conventional IgG VH and VL domains with the corresponding VHHs, combined with rigidifying mutations, enables IL-21 R agonism comparable to the wild-type cytokine. Taken together, these findings show that IL-21 receptor agonism can be substantially optimized by adapting the spatial orientation of paratopes targeting both receptor subunits via forced dimerization, without altering paratope valencies.

Introduction

It has been known for decades that, due to their potent immunoregulatory functions, cytokines are of utmost relevance for biomedical applications.1–3 This is exemplified by the fact that cytokine therapeutics have been approved for a wide range of different diseases ranging from cancer indications and anemia to multiple sclerosis.4 Yet, the pleiotropic mode of action of many cytokines, their short half-life and poor biodistribution combined with high toxicities when administered systemically at higher doses typically impede a broad applicability, as reviewed elsewhere.5–7 To address these inherent limitations, next-generation cytokine-derived modalities were developed, such as immunocytokines or engineered cytokines with biased functionalities.8–11 Currently, several of these approaches are being investigated in clinical trials.12,13 In recent years, antibody-based cytokine mimetics, also referred to as surrogate agonists, emerged as a viable alternative to engineering the cytokine itself.14–16 Cytokine mimetics are typically bi- or multispecific antibodies (Abs), which mimic the function of a respective cytokine by agonizing the cognate receptor. The high modularity of Abs enables tailor-made cytokine functionalities, for instance in terms of potencies or efficacies of receptor downstream signaling or with respect to the pleiotropic nature of conventional cytokines. As such, Ab-derived cytokine mimetics display an enormous potential for biomedical applications.8,17–19 In 2021 Harris et al. described the generation of bispecific sdAb-based IL-2 mimetics devoid of the intrinsic bias of wild-type IL-2 for preferential regulatory T cell activation.20 This was achieved by targeting the IL-2 intermediate affinity receptor (IL-2 R) composed of IL-2 Rβ (CD122) and IL-2 Rγ (CD132) that is primarily expressed on resting NK and CD8+ T cells. A similar strategy was applied by Garcia and coworkers who identified sdAb-derived surrogate agonists for the intermediate IL-2 R eliciting different flavors of IL-2 R agonism.21 In the same work, the group also engineered partial and biased agonists for the type I IFN receptor which decoupled antiviral functions, for instance SARS-CoV-2 or Sendai virus replication, from proinflammatory gene expression. Finally, the authors created novel cytokine specificities by exploiting sdAb-based surrogate agonists eliciting a functional response by targeting IL-2 Rβ and IL-10 Rβ (CDw210b). Yen et al. also described the altered receptor geometry of surrogate agonists using X-ray structures and approximate models of the IL-2 receptor subunits with the respective VHH binder to demonstrate the influence of steric effects and proximity on cytokine receptor signaling.21 Our group described the engineering of surrogate agonists for the IL-18 R by isolating IL-18 Rα- (CD218a) and IL-18 Rβ- (CD218b)‑targeting sdAbs.22 Incorporation of sdAbs into bispecific antibody (bsAb) architectures with different spatial orientations of the antigen binding sites as well as different paratope valencies enabled different degrees of IL-18 R agonism. Importantly, generated IL-18 mimetics were resistant to the natural decoy receptor IL-18-binding protein that is overexpressed in certain types of cancer.23 In addition, we reported on the generation of IL-12-mimicking bispecifics with biased agonism toward preactivated T cells versus PBMC-derived T cells and NK cells as demonstrated by STAT4 phosphorylation as well as by induced IFNγ release.24 Moreover, the IL-12 mimetics were also divergent in eliciting an IL-12-like transcriptional signature in stimulated T cells.

In the present work, we have generated bsAb-based IL-21 mimetics. IL-21 is a type I cytokine that is primarily produced by T cells and natural killer T (NKT) cells.25 IL-21 signals through its functional receptor that is a heterodimer composed of IL-21 R (CD360) and IL-2 Rγ (also referred to as common γ chain, since it is shared with several other cytokine receptors of the IL-2 family), predominantly triggering activation of Signal Transducer and Activator of Transcription 3 (STAT3).26 The functional consequences of receptor binding are highly pleiotropic and cell-type-specific. As such, IL-21 acts on a broad range of lymphoid, myeloid and epithelial cells. For example, in terms of proinflammatory effects, the cytokine drives proliferation and enhances cytotoxicity and hence, antitumor activity of NK cells, NKT cells or CD8+ T cells, whereas regarding immunosuppressive functions it elicits IL-10 production as well as apoptosis in B cells or dendritic cells.27

By immunizing camelids with recombinant human (rh) IL-21 R, we isolated a panel of VHHs specific for this receptor subunit harnessing yeast surface display for antibody discovery.28,29 This set was then recombined in a combinatorial manner with three selected IL-2 Rγ addressing VHHs that were previously identified and nominated based on divergent affinities as well as epitope targeting, resulting in (1 + 1) monovalent bispecifics (Figure 1(a,b)). By assessing agonism on NK-92 cells, we were able to identify multiple IL-21‑mimicking bsAbs triggering STAT3 activation as well as IFNγ release. Generated sdAb-derived mimetics were attenuated regarding agonism capacities compared with wild-type IL-21. To augment cytokine-like capacities of the IL-21‑mimicking agonists, we applied an engineering approach similar to that described by Lazar and coworkers.30 The authors introduced antibody framework mutations (inspired by naturally occurring Fab:Fab homotypic interactions) that constrain the IgG in a unique i-shaped conformation. This engineering approach enabled enhanced agonistic potential of generated IgGs by constraining receptor chains into optimal spatial relationships, as shown in a monospecific as well as bispecific IgG context.30 In the present work, we applied a similar strategy to sdAb-based IL-21 R agonists, performing structure-based framework interface engineering using ProteinMPNN to design further variants that promote noncovalent VHH:VHH dimerization.32 Conformationally constrained constructs were evaluated in two different format geometries: as bispecific sdAb-Fc fusions and as one-armed IgG-like constructs, where the VH and VL regions of a conventional IgG are replaced by two independent VHH paratopes (Figure 1). This antibody platform was described by our group in 2020.33 We now show for the first time that this antibody architecture, combined with engineered framework mutations in the individual VHHs, can serve as a backbone to convert moderate sdAb-derived receptor agonists into very robust cytokine mimetics. Results

Isolation of IL-21 R specific single domain antibodies and generation of VHH-based bsAbs

A llama (Lama glama) and a huarizo (Lama glama x Vicugna pacos) were immunized four times with (rh) IL-21 R over the course of 43 days. Following immunization, starting from the PBMC repertoire of each animal, and following RNA isolation and cDNA synthesis, specimen-specific libraries were generated for yeast surface display. The libraries were subjected to fluorescence-activated cell sorting (FACS) using an antigen concentration of 1 µM of (rh) IL-21 R extracellular domain (ECD). Sorting was carried out in a two-dimensional fashion, to co-select for full-length VHH display in addition to the binding functionality. As shown in Figure 1, within one to two rounds of sorting, we were able to strongly enrich for antigen-binding populations. Sequencing of 192 clones revealed a diverse panel of clones with high sequence diversity primarily within CDR3, from which we selected 27 clones for expression that are termed sdAb-1–sdAb-27 in the remainder of this study (Figure 1(e)). These clones were combined with three different VHHs specific for IL-2 Rγ that were previously generated by our group via camelid immunization and yeast display,28,29,34 referred to as VHHs IL-2 Rγa–c, binding to IL-2 Rγ in the single- to double-digit nM range (Supplementary Figure 1(a)). In addition, binding experiments revealed two different epitope bins for this set of sdAbs, with IL-2 Rγb not competing with the other two VHHs in binding to IL-2 Rγ (Supplementary Figure 1(b)). IL-2 Rγa and IL-2 Rγc showed partial competition indicating overlapping epitopes or epitopes in close proximity causing steric hindrance. Because signaling by heterodimeric cytokine receptors depends on epitope and inter‑receptor geometry, we adopted a combinatorial design to sample IL‑21 R and IL‑2 Rγ binder pairings and to identify bsAbs that position the receptor chains for activation and downstream signaling.21,30

For bsAb production we exploited the strand-exchange engineered domain (SEED) technology resulting in preferential heavy chain heterodimerization owing to β-strand exchanges of constant regions CH3 of IgG and IgA isotypes.35 To this end, respective IL-21 R addressing VHHs (sdAb-1–sdAb-27) were grafted onto the hinge region of the Fc effector-silenced AG chain of the SEEDbody, whereas IL-2 Rγ-specific sdAbs (IL-2 Rγa–c) were fused to the N-terminus of the Fc effector-silenced GA chain. Expression in Expi293TM cells was executed in a combinatorial manner, i.e., each VHH AG chain was expressed in conjunction with each of the three VHH GA chain fusions, resulting in (27 × 3) 81 bispecific molecules in total.

Next, we assessed binding of all 27 IL-21 R-targeting single domain antibodies (sdAb-1–sdAb-27). For this, all SEEDbodies reformatted with the GA chain harboring VHH IL-2 Rγa were utilized at a concentration of 100 nM in a biolayer interferometry experiment in which his-tagged (rh) IL-21 R was loaded onto the biosensors (Supplementary Figure 2). Importantly, all IL-21 R targeting sdAbs showed specific binding to (rh) IL-21 R. In accordance with the high sequence diversity within the set, differential binding behavior in terms of binding capacities (i.e., interference pattern shift) as well as on- and off-rates were observed. Regarding early manufacturability properties, we observed overall yields of higher double-digit to triple-digit mg/L scale with target monomer peaks of more than 90% on average in analytical size exclusion chromatography (SEC) after Protein A purification for most of the constructs (Supplementary Table 1). Interestingly, we witnessed a trend toward aggregation of clone sdAb-9 irrespective of the IL-2 Rγ partner paratope. Additionally, sdAb-22 and sdAb-27 in combination with IL-2 Rγa and IL-2 Rγc displayed higher aggregation propensities. In this respect, we applied a cutoff of more than 86% target peak post Protein A purification to consider molecules for further functional characterization after initial screening.

Identification of bispecific IL-21 receptor agonists triggering STAT3 activation

In a first attempt to characterize the 81 bsAbs regarding their capability to mimic the function of IL-21 we exploited a flow cytometry-based assay in which STAT3 phosphorylation of the immortalized natural killer (NK) cell line NK-92 was assessed. For this, all bsAbs were characterized at a concentration of 50 nM. (rh) IL-21 was included as positive control at the same concentration. As negative control, only medium was used. In this manner, we were able to identify multiple IL-21 R agonists inducing STAT3 phosphorylation (pSTAT3+) in NK-92 cells (Figure 2). Considering only bsAbs as surrogate agonists triggering more than 15% of STAT3 activation as normalized to (rh) IL-21, we selected a total of 50 molecules. Hence, approximately two-thirds of the assessed molecules were able to mimic the function of IL-21 to a certain extent. 33 bsAbs evoked STAT3 phosphorylation between 15% and 45% relative to (rh) IL-21 (Figure 2(a), dark grey) and 17 mimetics elicited more than 45% of relative receptor agonism (Figure 2(a), black). Interestingly, a general trend in terms of agonism was observed for IL-2 Rγ-specific VHHs when reformatted with a cognate IL-21 R targeting paratope. In this regard, IL-2 Rγc induced a signal in most IL-21 R VHH combinations (Figure 2(b)). Moreover, a tendency for generally higher pSTAT3 activity was unveiled. In general, IL-21 R-specific VHHs in combination with VHH IL-2 Rγa gave rise to more agonists when compared with combinations based on IL-2 Rγb (Figure 2(b)), screening of bsAbs based on VHH IL-2 Rγa revealed a trend toward attenuated agonism capacities for ‘active mimetics’ as evidenced by maximum pSTAT3 induction in comparison to bsAbs incorporating the other two IL-2 Rγ targeting sdAbs (Figure 2). Of note, affinities for binding to IL-2 Rγ of sdAb IL-2 Rγa were not substantially different compared with VHH IL-2 Rγb, which targets a different epitope on the receptor subunit (Supplementary Figure 1(a), IL-2 Rγa: 45.1 nM vs IL-2 Rγb: 33.7 nM). In contrast to this, affinities were lower than for IL-2 Rγc, which addresses a similar epitope on the receptor (Supplementary Figure 1(a), IL-2 Rγa: 45.1 nM vs IL-2 Rγc: 1.5 nM). Hence, it is tempting to speculate that the targeted epitope of a given VHH is important to induce agonism within a bispecific architecture, but that affinity appears to matter for sdAbs binding to similar epitopes. With respect to the IL-21 R-directed paratopes, we were able to categorize the sdAbs into three distinct subsets based on their functional profiles. Group 1 harbored sdAbs that activated the IL-21 R in combination with all three IL-2 Rγ-targeting VHHs (Figure 2(a,c), displayed in green: sdAb-2, sdAb-10 and sdAb-16). Group 2 consisted of paratopes that elicited over 45% agonism only in combination with IL-2 Rγb, whereas they triggered only marginal activation when reformatted IL-2 Rγa and IL-2 Rγc (Figure 2(a-c), displayed in blue: sdAb-3, −9, −17, −19, −22 and −27). In contrast to this, all remaining IL-21 R-targeting sdAbs (group 3) activated NK-92 cells mostly in combination with IL-2 Rγa and IL-2 Rγc with substantially attenuated agonism when expressed with IL-2 Rγb (Figure 2(a-c), group 3 displayed in red). This difference is particularly evident in Figure 2(b), where the IL-21 R-specific VHHs combined with IL-2 Rγb form two distinct populations, nonfunctional bispecifics represented by paratopes of group 3, and highly activating bispecifics, which correspond to VHHs of group 1 and 2.

Engineered IL-21 mimetics display a broad range of agonism capacities depending on cognate paratope combinations

To further dissect the differences between the IL-21 R-directed paratopes, representative sdAbs from each functional group were subjected to epitope binning experiments using (rh) IL-21 R. The receptor subunit was immobilized on biosensors, and binding of pairs of sdAbs was assessed in sequential association steps in both orientations (Supplementary Figure 3 (a)). SdAbs from group 1 (sdAb-2, sdAb-10, sdAb-16) mutually blocked each other’s binding to IL-21 R, indicating that they recognize an overlapping or highly similar epitope (Supplementary Figure 3(b–c)). This shared binding site is consistent with their functional profile, namely robust agonism when combined with all three IL-2 Rγ-targeting VHHs. A comparable pattern was observed for selected group 2 sdAbs (sdAb-3, sdAb-22, and to a lesser extent sdAb-27), which clustered into an epitope bin that partially overlaps with the epitope recognized by group 1 however being exclusively active in combination with IL-2 Rγb. In contrast, sdAb-11 and sdAb-24 (group 3) did not competitively interfere with each other or with the representative sdAbs from group 1 and 2, indicating that they bind distinct, non-overlapping epitopes on IL-21 R. These unique epitope specificities likely underlie their divergent functional behavior, for example, preferential agonism when paired with IL-2 Rγa or IL-2 Rγc, but diminished activity with IL-2 Rγb (Supplementary Figure 3(b–c)). To relate these epitope bins to binding of the native ligand, competition assays with Fc-tagged (rh) IL-21 were performed using the same experimental setup. Group 2 sdAbs (sdAb-3, sdAb-22 and sdAb-27) interfered with (rh) IL-21 binding in one orientation (IL-21 first, sdAb second association) while in the inverse orientation secondary IL-21 binding was still detectable but strongly reduced (Figure 3(a,b)). This is highly indicative for steric hindrance, i.e., caused by epitopes in close vicinity or by overlapping epitopes. In contrast, all other sdAbs did not compete with (rh) IL-21 for binding, indicating that they engage epitopes on IL-21 R that are distinct from the native cytokine binding site. Next, the selected 8 paratopes were assessed regarding their bsAb-based IL-21 receptor agonism capacities in a dose-dependent manner by harnessing NK-92 cells and PBMCs. The 11 variants investigated also comprised, for instance, sdAb-2 reformatted with all three different IL-2 Rγ paratopes (i.e., IL-2 Rγa–c) and were thus referred to as sdAb-2a-c, respectively. We evaluated the capacity of the generated agonists to induce STAT3 phosphorylation in NK-92 cells and CD8+ T cells, as well as their ability to stimulate interferon-γ (IFNγ) secretion by NK-92 cells (Figure 3). Of note, agonism on other immune cell subsets (CD4+ T cells, NK cells and B cells) are given in Supplementary Figure 4 and Supplementary Table 2. (rh) IL-21 triggered potent and robust activation of all cell subsets investigated, with potencies in the low picomolar range ((rh) IL-21: NK-92 (pSTAT3+): EC50 = 0.007 nM, Emax = 64.8%; NK-92 (IFNγ): EC50 = 0.03 nM, Emax = 1203 pg/mL; CD8+ STAT3+: EC50 = 0.002 nM, Emax = 92.8%; Figure 3, Table 1). All nominated bsAbs elicited dose-dependent activation of NK-92 cells, CD8+ T cells and other immune cell subsets (Figure 3, Table 1). The agonism profile of the IL-21 mimetics generated, however, was divergent among the set and spanned a wide range of potencies (EC50) and efficacies (Emax) with consistency across the cell types investigated (Figure 3, Table 1, Supplementary Figure 4, Supplementary Table 2). Compared with (rh) IL-21, all molecules were attenuated in terms of efficacy and potency (for each cell type). Intriguingly, mimetics reformatted with IL-2 Rγa elicited only minimal to moderate agonism with potencies in the nanomolar range, while the maximum induction of STAT3 phosphorylation was heavily attenuated (sdAb-2a: NK-92 (pSTAT3+): EC50 = 20 nM, Emax = 22.3%; CD8+ STAT3+: EC50 = 9.5 nM, Emax = 13%; sdAb-11a: NK-92 (pSTAT3+): EC50 = 10.9 nM, Emax = 25.3%; CD8+ STAT3+: EC50 = 21.3 nM, Emax = 17%). While potencies for IFNγ release were equally reduced, the maximum release for both derivatives was fairly robust (sdAb-2a: IFNγ: EC50 = 4.1 nM, Emax = 575 pg/mL; sdAb-11a: IFNγ: EC50 = 5.1 nM, Emax = 859 pg/mL). When sdAb-2 was combined with VHH IL-2 Rγc, which targets a similar epitope in IL-2 Rγ as VHH IL-2 Rγa but with increased binding affinity, potencies were equally attenuated, but efficacies were tremendously augmented (sdAb-2c: NK-92 (pSTAT3+): EC50 = 3.3 nM, Emax = 47.3%; NK-92 (IFNγ): EC50 = 0.6 nM, Emax = 954 pg/mL; CD8+ STAT3+: EC50 = 2.4 nM, Emax = 62.4%). In contrast, when sdAb-2 was reformatted with IL-2 Rγb, targeting a different epitope on the receptor subunit than IL-2 Rγa and IL-2 Rγc, we again observed highly diminished agonism (sdAb-2b: NK-92 (pSTAT3+): EC50 = 1.9 nM, Emax = 19.5%; NK-92 (IFNγ): EC50 = 0.4 nM, Emax = 501 pg/mL; CD8+ STAT3+: EC50 = 1.3 nM, Emax = 21.1%). Molecular modeling studies to rationalize agonism capacities of engineered IL-21 mimetics

IL-21 receptor activation proceeds through geometry-dependent assembly of a heterodimeric complex. IL-21 first engages the IL-21 R extracellular domain, forming a stabilized binary complex. IL-2 Rγ is subsequently recruited to assemble the ternary IL-21–IL-21 R–IL-2 Rγ signaling complex (Figure 4(a)).36 The specific spatial arrangement of IL-21 R and IL-2 Rγ positions the intracellular JAK1 and JAK3 kinases for transphosphorylation, predominantly activating STAT3 with additional contributions from PI3K and MAPK pathways.37 Consequently, activation strength and signaling bias depend strongly on the relative geometry and epitope orientation of IL-21 R and IL-2 Rγ, as demonstrated by surrogate ligand studies where activity is governed by receptor dimer geometry rather than binding affinity alone.21,30 To structurally rationalize the observed agonism capacities depending on our cognate paratope combinations, we generated complex models of IL-21 R and IL-2 Rγ and the respective binding paratopes using AlphaFold2.38 Binding poses were selected based on visual analysis and their agreement with available experimental epitope binning data (Figure 3, Supplementary Figure 1, Supplementary Figure 3). While these models are not fully validated due to known limitations of AlphaFold2 in predicting antibody-antigen complex structures, they offer structural hypotheses into how epitope and receptor geometry might influence functional activity.39–41 As shown in Figure 4(a), IL-21 R binding VHHs are predicted to address three different epitopes in agreement with the experimental epitope binning data, with group 2 VHHs binding to the same site as IL-21 (Figure 4(a), blue VHHs). Group 1 paratopes are predicted to bind a proximal, partially overlapping epitope with that of group 2, whereas VHHs of group 3 are predicted to target epitopes on the membrane-proximal domain (D2). According to AlphaFold2 predictions, VHHs IL-2 Rγa and IL-2 Rγc address an overlapping epitope on the IL-2 Rγ, close to the IL-21 binding site (Figure 4(a)), while IL-2 Rγb binds to a more distant epitope. These predictions align fairly well with the corresponding experimental epitope binning data (Supplementary Figure 1). The predicted binding modes furthermore provide reasonable hypotheses for the observed differences of agonism capacities depending on paratope combinations. As illustrated in Figure 4(b), the bispecific sdAb-Fc architecture provides a structural constraint that will only allow a limited number of paratope combinations to maintain their binding orientations within this architecture against the IL-21 R and IL-2 Rγ chains in a signaling competent, dimerized orientation (e.g. sdAb-10c and sdAb-16c, Figure 4(b)). Paratope combinations that fail to achieve robust agonism (such as sdAb-11b or sdAb-3a) likely position the receptor chains in geometries that prevent optimal JAK1-JAK3 trans-phosphorylation, despite maintaining individual receptor binding. Conversely, highly active combinations such as those incorporating IL-2 Rγb appear to stabilize IL-21 R and IL-2 Rγ in spatial arrangements that closely mimic the native IL-21-induced signaling geometry (for example sdAb-3b and sdAb-22b), enabling efficient kinase activation and downstream STAT3 signaling. Framework mutations significantly enhance efficacy of receptor activation by altering intra-molecular interactions of paratopes

Next, we set out to augment functionalities of the IL-21 R-specific surrogate agonists by implementing the previously described i-shaped antibody platform in sdAb-based agonists.30,31 As demonstrated in previous studies, such conformational-constraint engineering can enhance IgG agonism as long as the receptor chains are stabilized in a signaling‑competent orientation. As shown in the cryo-EM structure of DH851.3 (PDB code 7LU9, Figure 5(a)), specific mutations in the variable heavy (VH) framework regions (FRs) 1 and 3 induce noncovalent paratope Fab dimers by hydrophobic intramolecular interactions.30,31 Since VHs and VHHs share high structural similarity in their FRs, we hypothesized that such mutations can also trigger noncovalent VHH:VHH dimerization, as illustrated in Figure 5(a). Therefore, we introduced specific framework mutations based on the DH851 and DH898 lineage into the selected bispecific sdAbs, hereinafter referred to as dimerized single domain antibodies (dsdAbs) (Figure 5(a), with the DH851 set of mutations named dsdAb(1) and the DH898 amino acid exchanges referred to as dsdAb(2), respectively). Furthermore, based on a structural alignment of VHH backbones onto the DH851 (PDB code 7LU9) and DH898 (PDB code 7L6M) VH dimers, we utilized ProteinMPNN (as detailed in Methods) to design additional variants that should also lead to intramolecular interactions between both VHH-based paratopes.32 These mutants are hereinafter referred to as dsdAb (3)-dsdAb(5). The resulting sets of mutations that were introduced to trigger non-covalent VHH:VHH dimerization are shown in Figure 5(a,b). We selected sdAb-10c and the attenuated construct sdAb-16c as variants of the same group for further engineering. dsdAb(1–5) derivatives of sdAb-10c and sdAb-16c were expressed in the SEED-based VHH-Fc format using an effector silenced backbone (Figure 5 and 6(a)). Protein expression was performed in Expi293TM cells and yields post Protein A purification were fairly favorable, ranging between 91–319 mg/L (Table 2). Moreover, target peaks in analytical SEC (after Protein A purification) indicated low aggregation propensities for most of the molecules with dsdAb(3–5) exhibiting monomeric purities as favorable as the parental molecules sdAb-10c and sdAb-16c (>93%, Table 2). Remarkably, in contrast to all other framework mutated formats tested, introduction of the dsdAb (2) set of mutations resulted in low target peaks of 62% (dsdAb-16c(2)) and 53% (dsdAb-10c(2)), respectively (Table 2, Figure 6(b)). This has also been described for the same set of mutations in the Fab, i.e., IgG context.30 Consequently, it is tempting to speculate that the dsdAb(2) mutations cause intermolecular VHH:VHH interactions resulting in higher oligomeric structural formations. Properties of dimerized sdAb variants of sdAb-10c and sdAb-16c.

To assess the impact on agonistic activity of the dsdAb variants, we compared the novel framework mutated variants with the respective parental molecules in terms of pSTAT3 priming as well as IFNγ release (Figure 6(c,d), Table 2, Supplementary Figure 6, Supplementary Table 3). For both IL-21 mimetics tested, introduction of the ‘dimerizing’ mutations in all different sets (i.e., dsdAb(1–5)) enabled augmented agonism, especially regarding efficacies, i.e., Emax STAT3 phosphorylation and Emax IFNγ release. While for the sdAb-10c series of molecules, there was a trend for most augmented agonism for dsdAb-10c(2) and less pronounced optimized agonism for dsdAb-10c(3), for the sdAb-16c series of IL-21 mimetics, the different dsdAb formats were not appreciably different in their capacity to enhance agonism. Of note, the degree of augmented agonism caused by the different dsdAb set of mutations was generally much higher for sdAb-16c. For instance, the parental molecule sdAb-16c induced 26.9% maximum STAT3 activation in CD8+ T cells and maximum IFNγ production of 323.5 pg/mL (Figure 6(d,e), Table 2). This was optimized by more than threefold for STAT3 priming and approximately 2.5-fold for IFNγ release (dsdAb-16c(2): CD8+ STAT3+ Emax = 88.3%; NK-92 (IFNγ): Emax = 853.3 pg/mL). Surprisingly, introduction of a set of framework mutations in sdAb-3b had no beneficial effect regarding IL-21 R agonism (Supplementary Figure 7). In fact, rigidifying the bsAb by forcing VHH:VHH dimerization was counterproductive for the agonistic activity of the molecule. sdAb-3b targets different epitopes on both receptor subunits than the other bsAbs (Figure 3, Supplementary Figure 1). This implies that depending on the paratope combinations and ultimately depending on the combination of targeted epitopes which induce a particular receptor geometry, VHH:VHH dimerizing mutations can either have favorable or detrimental effects. To exclude that the modified activities of the engineered dsdAbs originated from divergent antigen binding, we performed biolayer interferometry experiments to measure individual binding affinities of each paratope. All dimerized variants of sdAb-3b, sdAb-10c and sdAb-16c displayed comparable affinities to their parental molecules for binding to both, IL-21 R and IL-2 Rγ, clearly indicating that the framework mutations do not appreciably affect antigen binding (Supplementary Figure 8, Supplementary Table 4). In accordance with that, Romei et al. reported no impact on individual affinity as well as cell surface avidity for their DH851 variant in their OX40 agonist panel, suggesting that the altered agonism activity is indeed solely based on the intra- and intermolecular interactions of the paratopes.30

Selected framework mutations enabling VHH:VHH dimerization can convert an almost inactive VHH-based IgG-like cytokine mimetic into a strong agonist

It is generally known that the antibody architecture can have a major impact on the functionality of an agonist.21,22,24 We have previously described a bispecific sdAb-based IgG-like format.33 Here, the VH and VL domains of a conventional IgG were replaced by two different VHHs, resulting in an antibody design, that is similar to a classical IgG but enables bispecificity. We set out to assess this format for IL-21-like agonism. To this end, we utilized sdAb-16c and grafted the IL-21 R-specific VHH onto the CH1 domain, which was paired with a light chain incorporating the IL-2 Rγ-specific paratope fused onto the CL, replacing VH and VL (Figure 7(a)). By exploiting the SEED technology, we created a one-armed version of the sdAb-based IgG-like bispecific, referred to as sdIgG-16c, since we did not want to take valency engineering into consideration. We investigated the same sets of framework mutations as before, but excluding the dsdAb(2) mutations due to its low monomeric content. Those derivatives were labeled as dsdIgG-16c(1) and dsdIgG-16c(3–5). All molecules were expressed with yields above 250 mg/L and at least 86% target monomer peak after Protein A purification, indicating adequate early developability properties (Table 3, Figure 7(b)). In terms of agonistic activity, sdIgG-16c was significantly attenuated in its efficacies (sdIgG-16c: NK-92 (pSTAT3+): Emax = 15.4%; NK-92 (IFNγ): Emax = 113.3 pg/mL; CD8+ STAT3+: Emax = 8.9%) (Table 3, Figure 7(c–e)). Introduction of the framework modifications of dsdAb(1) into this architecture (dsdIgG-16c(1)) substantially enhanced the agonism capacities, resulting in a molecule that is fairly similar to the wild-type cytokine (rh) IL-21 (dsdIgG-16c(1): NK-92 (pSTAT3+): Emax = 82.4%; NK-92 (IFNγ): Emax = 1033 pg/mL; CD8+ STAT3+: Emax = 90.0%) (Table 3, Figure 7(c–e), Supplementary Figure 9, Supplementary Table 5). Surprisingly, introduction of the other sets of mutations (dsdAb(3–5)) had only marginal to moderate effects. Among those three molecules, dsdAb-16c(3) enhanced the agonistic capacity to a certain extent (dsdIgG-16c(3): NK-92 (pSTAT3+): Emax = 40.5%; NK-92 (IFNγ): Emax = 336.7 pg/mL; CD8+ STAT3+: Emax = 29.3%), with both other molecules enabling only slightly enhanced capacities. Next, we examined the induction of STAT3 phosphorylation in NK-92 cells in a time-resolved manner (Figure 7(f)). Here, dsdAb-16c(1) triggered STAT3 activation comparable to (rh) IL-21 itself, indicating optimal epitope engagement. Again, biolayer interferometry measurements were performed to scrutinize any change in binding affinities which was not observed (Supplementary Figure 10, Supplementary Table 6). Bispecific IL-21 mimetics trigger cytotoxic activities of stimulated T cells by enhancing Granzyme B expression

The precise effect of IL-21 anti-tumor activity and its clinical utility is currently under investigation. It is known that IL-21 enhances the effector functions of CD8+ T cells and enables expansion of these cells in synergy with other cytokines.27,42 We set out to explore the effect of IL-21 and the generated IL-21-mimicking surrogate agonists (excluding dsdAb(2) due to its low monomeric content and hence, inadequate early developability profile) on activated T cells in an in vitro setting. To this end, isolated T cells were stimulated for one week with anti-CD3/anti-CD28 antibodies and 100 U/mL IL-2. Subsequently, the different IL-21-like compounds were added at a concentration of 10 nM for 24 h followed by intracellular detection of Granzyme B (Figure 8(a)). Treatment with (rh) IL-21 induced strong expression of Granzyme B in stimulated T cells. For sdAb-10c, we also noticed the upregulation of Granzyme B, albeit attenuated when compared with (rh) IL-21 (sdAb-10c: Granzyme B normalized to (rh) IL-21 = 65.1%; Figure 8(b), Table 2). The framework-engineered derivatives did not significantly further enhance Granzyme B production, albeit for dsdAb-10c (1) we witnessed a trend toward augmentation (dsdAb-10c(1): Granzyme B normalized to (rh) IL-21 = 76.6%). Interestingly, for sdAb-16c we only observed a trend toward Granzyme B enhancement when compared with the medium control, which was not statistically significant (sdAb-16c: Granzyme B normalized to (rh) IL-21 = 52.4%, Figure 8). In contrast to sdAb-10c, however, all framework engineered derivatives of sdAb-16c increased Granzyme B expression in a statistically significant manner, with dsdAb-16c(3) and dsdAb-16c(5) eliciting highest Granzyme B levels (dsdAb-16c(3): Granzyme B normalized to (rh) IL-21 = 81.1%; dsdAb-16c(5): Granzyme B normalized to (rh) IL-21 = 79.9%; Figure 8, Table 2). In line with substantial attenuation, sdIgG-16c did not induce elevated Granzyme B production (sdIgG-16c: Granzyme B normalized to (rh) IL-21 = 38.4%; Figure 8, Table 3). In contrast to this, the framework engineered variants dsdIgG-16c(1) as well as dsdIgG-16c(3) significantly augmented Granzyme B levels (dsdIgG-16c(1): Granzyme B normalized to (rh) IL-21 = 84.3%; dsdIgG-16c(3): Granzyme B normalized to (rh) IL-21 = 62.5%, Figure 8, Table 3). Taken together, these findings emphasize that generated bsAbs also mimic the functionality of IL-21 in terms of Granzyme B expression in preactivated T cells and that depending on the paratope combinations VHH framework engineering might augment these capabilities. Discussion

For decades, cytokines have been investigated as therapeutic interventions for the treatment of different diseases.43 IL-21 is a highly pleiotropic cytokine that, owing to its proinflammatory functions, has been scrutinized preclinically and in clinical trials for cancer indications.44,45 In this respect, in a recent preclinical study, Chun et al. described the de novo-designed IL-21 cytokine mimic with robust antitumor activity while also suppressing regulatory T cells.46 In the present work, we have generated IL-21-mimicking agents based on bispecific antibodies. To this end, we identified a set of IL-21 R-specific sdAbs by yeast surface display following camelid immunization.29,34 Importantly, when reformatted into a strictly monovalent bispecific antibody architecture by harnessing a Fc-heterodimerization technique,35,47 most of the generated molecules showed a favorable purity after Protein A purification. Affinities against the IL-21 R subunit were in the single- to double-digit nM range, which is in accordance with what we typically observe for sdAb binding capacities from immunized repertoires.22,48–50 Individual paratopes were combined with three distinct IL-2 Rγ-specific VHHs. To assess IL-21-like capabilities we first scrutinized STAT3 activation in NK-92 cells followed by more thorough characterization focusing on PBMC-derived CD8+ T cells. By this means, we were able to identify multiple bispecific surrogate agonists. Intriguingly, besides very few IL-21 R-specific sdAbs that worked well irrespective of the combined IL-2 Rγ paratope (e.g., sdAb-2), various different IL-21 R addressing VHHs elicited robust STAT3 phosphorylation either only when reformatted together with VHH IL-2 Rγa and VHH IL-2 Rγc or when recombined with VHH IL-2 Rγb. VHH IL-2 Rγa and VHH IL-2 Rγc target a similar epitope on IL-2 Rγ, whereas VHH IL-2 Rγb binds to a different site on the receptor chain. Consequently, it is tempting to speculate that the targeted epitopes of individual VHHs incorporated into a bispecific design architecture primarily dictate structural ramifications with respect to the spatial arrangement of the heterodimeric IL-21 receptor, ultimately determining receptor agonism, whereas antibody affinity seems to play an important but secondary role. Similar observations were made by Garcia and coworkers who described no direct correlation between binding affinities of IL-2 receptor targeting paratopes and their agonism capacities as bispecific surrogate agonists.21 Furthermore, it was shown that the format architecture can have a tremendous impact on the agonism of bispecific antibodies by modulating the spatial orientation of individual paratopes within the antibody design as well as by varying paratope valencies.21,22,24

Along those lines, we set out to investigate whether VHH framework engineering eliciting VHH:VHH interactions, thereby rigidifying the IL-21 receptor subunits in close proximity, enables augmented IL-21-like agonism. Lazar and coworkers described the introduction of framework mutations into the VH regions of an IgG for Fab:Fab dimerization.30 These sets of mutations, which were inspired by naturally occurring broadly neutralizing glycan-reactive antibodies in the HIV context, convert the flexible Y-shaped conformation into a distinct I-shaped structure.31,51 The group of Lazar elegantly demonstrated that within the IgG context, intrinsic agonism can be significantly augmented, for instance for TNFSF receptors as well as in a bispecific IgG context for the dimeric IL-2 receptor.30 We transferred two lineages of mutations, DH851.3 and DH898.1, to the VHH framework (referred to as dsdAb(1) and dsdAb(2), respectively) and generated three novel sets of mutations derived from ProteinMPNN to enable VHH:VHH dimerization. Of note, in a work conducted by Kunze et al., the authors described that introducing the DH851.3 lineage of mutations can be harnessed to engineer synthetic cytokine receptors based on sdAbs as ectodomains for gp130 receptor signaling.52 Within our work, regarding the bispecific VHH-Fc architecture, for two out of three IL-21 mimetics, all five sets of framework mutations significantly enhanced agonism, as demonstrated by optimized induced downstream signaling (Emax pSTAT3) and IFNγ release. Remarkably, the novel sets of framework variants (dsdAb(3–5)) that were specifically designed to comprise a lower number of mutations (3–4 amino acid changes vs 8 and 5 mutations for DH851.3 and DH898.1, respectively) were fairly similar to the DH851.3 and DH898.1 lineage mutations in eliciting enhanced IL-21 receptor agonism. Importantly, both augmented agonists harbored the same IL-2 Rγ directed sdAbs and also both binders targeting the IL-21 R subunit shared the same epitope bin. In contrast, sdAb-3b comprised VHHs targeting different epitopes on both receptor subunits. Here, introduction of all sets of mutations facilitating VHH:VHH interaction was detrimental. This clearly highlights the importance of paratope combinations in the context of VHH framework engineering for enhanced agonism capacities.

Of note, for all tested sdAb-derived IL-21 mimetics, introduction of the DH898.1 lineage of mutations (dsdAb(2)) resulted in low target monomer peaks, while all other framework engineered derivatives exhibited adequate purities. This can be attributed to intermolecular interactions, yielding higher-order oligomers. This has also been described by Lazar and coworkers for DH898.1 framework mutants in the VH, i.e., IgG context. Here, the authors observed intermolecular dimers visible by SEC with monomer:dimer ratios changing with concentration.30 Due to the low early developability profile, which might pose obstacles in terms of immunogenicity or pharmacokinetics, we excluded this particular set of framework mutations from further consideration.53–55

Surprisingly, in the bispecific IgG-like context sdIgG-16c was almost inactive. This indicates that grafting of the individual sdAbs onto the constant regions of the Fab arm alters the exact spatial orientation of the mimetic, resulting in a suboptimal inter-receptor geometry and ultimately impeded downstream signaling. Introduction of the DH851.3 framework mutations (dsdIgG-16c(1)) not only fully recovered the activity of the parental molecule but enabled agonism that was fairly comparable to the wild-type cytokine in priming STAT3 phosphorylation and in eliciting IFNγ release. Moreover, framework-engineered variant dsdIgG-16c(3), harboring 4 mutations in total, modestly increased IL-21 receptor agonism, while the other set of framework mutations only had a minimal effect. This suggests that the lower number of introduced mutations results in VHH:VHH interactions that are not as pronounced as for VHH:VHH interactions of the DH851.3 lineage harboring 8 amino acid exchanges in total. Beyond epitope selection, our data demonstrate that the functional impact of VHH framework mutation sets is highly format dependent. DH851.3-based designs were consistently effective in both VHH-Fc and IgG-like contexts confirming their broad applicability in line with previous observations on i-shaped Ab‑type interfaces.30,52 dsdIgG-16c(1) even surpassed the corresponding VHH-Fc derivative dsdAb-16c(1) (Supplementary Figure 11). Conversely, dsdAb(3–5) translated less efficiently into the IgG‑like format, with dsdIgG-16c(3–4) exhibiting an increase in signaling capacity comparable to the parental construct sdAb-16c (Supplementary Figure 11). While detailed structural analyses will be required to fully elucidate VHH conformations and the impact of enforced paratope–paratope contacts in each bispecific scaffold, we speculate that the stronger intramolecular interface created by the DH851.3 mutation set (with a higher number of framework substitutions) is particularly beneficial in the more rigid Fab‑based bispecific module (dsdIgG(1)), whereas the more flexible VHH–hinge architecture can already accommodate productive receptor geometries with lower‑mutation interfaces such as those present in dsdAb(3–5). However, it is worth noting that the introduction of non-germline-encoded framework mutations, as present in all dsdAb and dsdIgG variants, will need to be carefully evaluated with respect to developability properties, including potential immunogenicity, as part of the standard humanization and sequence-optimization procedures applied during the discovery of antibody-based therapeutics.

Notably, while VHH:VHH interface engineering yielded marked improvements in the efficacy of the bispecific IL-21 mimetics, potencies were not appreciably modulated and remained substantially attenuated when compared with wild-type IL-21. This observation is consistent with the commonly seen phenomenon that the vast majority of antibody-based cytokine receptor agonists exhibit weaker potencies than the cognate natural cytokine.19,21,22,24 In this context, we previously demonstrated that valency engineering as well as deliberate modulation of the spatial orientation of individual paratopes within the antibody architecture can yield substantial improvements in agonistic activity including gains in potency.22 Furthermore, expanding the explored sequence space of individual paratopes, thereby increasing the diversity of accessible bispecific paratope combinations may similarly increase the probability of identifying IL-21 receptor agonists with superior potency profiles.

Very recently, it was shown that VHH-derived IL-2 Rβγ agonists can be delivered in cis via genetic fusion to a PD-1-targeting IgG for the preferential activation of activated CD8+ T cells while sparing Tregs and non-activated peripheral T cells.56 Whether the IL-21 mimetics generated herein as well as engineered derivatives thereof can be exploited analogously to achieve cis activation represents an intriguing avenue for future investigation. Essentially, our work adds IL-21 as a cytokine that can be mimicked using sdAb-derived bispecifics and extends the toolbox on how these entities can be optimized in terms of agonism capacities.

Methods

Camelid immunization

One female llama (Lama glama) aged 2.9 years and one male huarizo (Lama glama ×Vicugna pacos) aged 10.1 years were immunized with a cocktail of recombinant human antigens including His-tagged IL-21 R ECD (Acro Biosystems, ILR-H5226). The antigen was diluted with sterile deionized water according to manufacturer’s recommendation to a final concentration of 400 µg/mL IL-21 R. Together with two other antigens (1:1:1 ratio) the animals were immunized subcutaneously in a first injection with 0.3 mg antigen mixture in 1 mL Gerbu FAMA adjuvant. The procedure was repeated three times with Incomplete Freund’s adjuvant on day 14, day 28 and day 35. On day 43 100 mL whole blood was collected from each animal for PBMC isolation, RNA isolation and cDNA synthesis. Animal procedures were conducted at preclinics GmbH, Germany in accordance with local regulations and animal-welfare laws. Immunized animals remain alive after final blood collection.

Yeast strains and media

For yeast surface display, the Saccharomyces cerevisiae strain EBY100 (MATa URA3-52 trp1 leu2Δ1 his3Δ200 pep4:HIS3 prb1Δ1.6 R can1 GAL (pIU211:URA3)) (Thermo Fisher Scientific) was employed. Initially EBY100 cells were cultivated in yeast extract–peptone–dextrose (YPD) medium composed of 20 g/L peptone, 20 g/L dextrose and 10 g/L yeast extract supplemented with 10 mg/mL penicillin–streptomycin (Gibco). After cloning by homologous recombination, cells harboring library plasmids (pDisp) were cultivated in medium using minimal synthetic defined (SD)-base (Takara Bio) and corresponding dropout mix (Takara Bio) composed of all essential amino acids except for tryptophan (−Trp) for selection, supplemented with 5.4 g/L Na2HPO4 and 8.6 g/L NaH2PO4 x H2O. For induction of antibody gene expression, cells were transferred into galactose containing SG dropout medium (−Trp), consisting of SG-base medium (Takara Bio) supplemented with 10% (w/v) polyethylene glycol 8000 (PEG 8000).

Plasmids for yeast surface display and library generation

Construction of VHH display libraries in yeast was accomplished using homologous recombination-based gap repair cloning. For this, a detailed protocol can be found elsewhere described by our group.34 In brief, amplified VHH library candidates are genetically fused in frame to Aga2p by replacement of a stuffer sequence due to gap repair cloning in the BsaI digested display plasmid pDisp. Full-length presentation of VHH variants on the yeast cell surface can be detected due to the additional insertion of a HA epitope linked C-terminally to Aga2p on the pDisp backbone.

Library sorting

Initially, EBY100 cells were grown overnight in SD medium with dropout mix lacking tryptophan (−Trp) at 30°C and 120 rpm before inducing VHH surface expression by cell transfer into SG medium with dropout mix (−Trp) at 107 cells/mL for another 48 h at 20°C and 120 rpm. Antigen binding was detected by incubation with 1 µM his-tagged (rh) IL-21 R ECD (Acro Biosystems, ILR-H5226) in combination with anti-his mouse monoclonal detection antibody (SureLight® Allophycocyanin, Abcam, RRID: AB_1267597, diluted 1:20 or Penta His Alexa Fluor 647 Conjugate, Quiagen, RRID: AB_3083468, diluted 1:20). For library sorting purposes, full-length surface presentation was simultaneously detected by application of a FITC labeled rabbit polyclonal anti-HA antibody (Abcam, AB_298835, diluted 1:20) which allowed for a two-dimensional sorting strategy. The FACS procedure was performed on a BD FACSAria™ Fusion cell sorter (BD Biosciences). Untreated cells or cells only incubated with secondary detection reagents or cells incubated with an unrelated antigen served as controls in every experiment allowing for gate adjustment of the desired cell population. After sequencing of enriched populations, after one to two sorting rounds, a clonotype-based strategy was applied to select clones of different sequence clusters as described previously.22,34

Protein expression, purification and analytics

For the expression of bispecific sdAb constructs (1 + 1, anti-IL-21 R x anti-IL-2 Rγ), selected VHH variants directed against IL-21 R ECD were N-terminally fused to the hinge region of Fc immune effector-silenced (eff-) SEED AG chains and paired with IL-2 Rγ-specific VHHs fused to the N-terminus of the Fc-effector‑silenced GA chain accordingly. For sdIgG-based constructs the IL-21 R-specific VHHs were grafted onto the CH1 domain, which was paired with a light chain incorporating the IL-2 Rγ-specific paratope fused onto the CL, replacing VH and VL. Fc silencing was introduced by amino acid exchanges L234A and L235A.57 Cloning into pTT5 mammalian expression vector58 enables protein expression by transient transfection of Expi293TM cells according to the manufacturer’s instructions (Thermo Fisher Scientific) using a 2:1 plasmid ratio (AG:GA). For small-scale production, 25 mL protein containing supernatants were harvested by centrifugation after 6 days and purified via MabSelect antibody purification chromatography resin (Cytiva). After sterile filtration with Lyzer™-CL GV 0.22 μm centrifugal devices (Merck Millipore) protein concentrations were measured using NanoDrop ND-1000 (Peqlab). Protein purities were afterward determined by analytical SEC on a TSKgel UP-SW3000 column (2 μm, 4.6 × 300 mm, Tosoh Bioscience) using an Agilent HPLC 1260 Infinity system. A total of 7.5 μg protein per sample was injected and run at a flow rate of 0.35 mL/min using 50 mM sodium phosphate, 0.4 M NaClO4 pH 6.3 as mobile phase.

Biolayer interferometry

For all BLI measurements, the Octet RED96 system (ForteBio, Pall Life Science) using 25°C and 1000 rpm agitation settings was used. The data were fitted and analyzed with ForteBio data analysis software 8.0 using a 1:1 binding model after Savitzky–Golay filtering if needed. To evaluate binding to the respective antigen, (rh) IL-21 R (Acro, ILR-H5526) or (rh) IL-2 Rγ (Acro, ILA-H52H5) were loaded via His-tag on anti-Penta His (HIS1K) biosensors at 5 µg/mL in PBS for 180 s, followed by 60 s sensor rinsing in kinetics buffer (KB; PBS +0.1% Tween-20 and 1% bovine serum albumin, BSA). Association of sdAb constructs at 100 nM in KB was measured for 180 s followed by dissociation for 120 s in KB. Competition of selected sdAbs in binding to the respective receptor were analyzed in Epitope Binning experiments. HIS1K biosensors were used to load either 5 µg/mL (rh) IL-2 Rγ (Acro, ILA-H52H5) or (rh) IL-21 R (Acro, ILR-H5526) in PBS, followed by 60 s sensor rinsing in KB. Association of 200 nM first antibody in 300 s was combined with a second association (300 s) of another antibody in 200 nM with an included concentration spike of 100 nM of the first used antibody. KB containing the concentrations spike and KB alone were measured as controls in parallel to ensure visualization of additional association, which was normalized to 1 at 300 s. In the same combinatorial manner competition to IL-21 was analyzed using Fc-tagged (rh) IL-21 (Acro, IL1-H5253). To evaluate binding kinetics, sdAb constructs were loaded on anti-hIgG Fc capture (AHC) biosensors at 5 µg/mL in PBS for 180 s, followed by 60 s sensor rinsing in KB. Association of serial twofold dilutions of (rh) IL-21 R and (rh) IL-2 Rγ, starting at 200 nM, in KB were measured for 300 s prior dissociation for 300 s in KB. In each experiment, a negative control using an unrelated antibody and a baseline association in KB instead of the respective protein was included.

Cell culture

All cells were cultivated at 37°C and 5% CO2. NK-92 cells were cultured in MEMα (Gibco™), supplemented with 12.5% heat inactivated fetal bovine serum (Gibco™), 2.5% Horse Serum (Gibco™) and GlutaMAX™ Supplement (Gibco™). Cells were passaged twice weekly at 0.2 × 106 viable cells/mL with 5 ng/mL IL-2 (Acro, IL2-H5215) freshly added. Human PBMCs were freshly isolated from healthy donors according to StemCell Technologies’ SepMate PBMC Isolation protocol using SepMate-50 tubes (StemCell Technologies). PBMCs were stored in DMSO-containing cryopreservation medium in liquid nitrogen. For experiments, cells were quickly thawed at 37°C and handled in AIM V™ medium (Gibco™).

pSTAT3 activation of NK-92 cells and PBMCs

NK-92 cells in culture and thawed PBMCs were used for intracellular detection of phosphorylated STAT3 following stimulation with IL-21 R agonists or IL-21. A total of 1 × 105 NK-92 or 2 × 105 PBMCs were seeded per well. Cells were seeded into 96-well plates (Corning) and stimulated with sdAb-based IL-21 agonists or (rh) IL-21 in the appropriate cell culture medium. For dose-response curves, samples were tested in 1:5 serial dilutions, with concentrations ranging from 250 nM to 3.2 pM for sdAb-based agonists and 50 nM to 0.64 pM for (rh) IL-21. After sample incubation for 45 min at 37°C and 5% CO2 in a humidity chamber, all washing and staining steps were performed with pre-cooled washing buffer (PBS, 0.5% BSA) and centrifugation steps with 1000 rpm for 5 min. NK-92 cells were directly fixed with 200 µL/well Fixation Buffer (BD Biosciences) at 37°C and 350 rpm for 10 min on a plate shaker. PBMCs were stained with surface receptor detection antibodies before fixation and then washed twice. For gating of immune subpopulations, respective isotype controls and anti-CD8 APC (Invitrogen, MHCD0805, AB_10392701, 1:10), anti-CD4 AlexaFluor® 700 (BioLegend, 300,526, AB_493743, 1:10), anti-CD56 Brilliant Violet 421™ (BioLegend, 318,328, AB_11218798, 1:20) and anti-CD19 Brilliant Violet 605™ (BioLegend, 302,244, AB_2562015, 1:20) were stained for 45 min on ice. After fixation, all cells were washed again twice and permeabilized in 150 µL pre-cooled methanol per well for 30 min on ice. Cells were washed again twice before intracellular staining with isotype control or anti-pSTAT3 Alexa Fluor® 488 (BD Bioscience, 557,814, AB_647098, 1:20). After two additional washes, cells were finally resuspended in 50 µL washing buffer and analyzed on an iQue3 (Sartorius).

IFNγ release assay

To assess the induction of IFNγ release, 5 × 104 NK-92 cells per well were incubated with a 1:5 serial dilution series of sdAb-based agonists, with concentrations ranging from 250 nM to 3.2 pM, or 50 nM to 0.64 pM for (rh) IL-21 at 37°C and 5% CO2 in a humidity box for 24 h using 96-well white-opaque cultivation plates (PerkinElmer). Quantification of IFNγ released by NK-92 cells was performed using human cytokine HTRF kits (Cisbio) as previously described by our group.22 In brief, cells were sedimented by centrifugation after 24 h incubation with the respective samples, and cytokine-containing supernatants were further processed according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Assay plates were measured in a PHERAstar FSX device (BMG Labtech). The HTRF optical module was used with an excitation wavelength of 337 nm, and emission was measured at 620 nm as well as at 665 nm. Analyses and fitting of resulting data were facilitated by MARS software (v3.32, BMG) enabling a four-parameter logistic (4PL, 1/y2) model fit of the standard curve following the kit manufacturer’s instructions allowing for subsequent IFNγ quantification in the sample wells.

Granzyme B expression

Freshly isolated PBMCs of healthy donors were used to separate untouched human T cells using the automated RoboSep™ cell isolation instrument with the T cell isolation kit (StemcellTM, 17,951) following the manufacturer’s instructions. Post isolation, T cells were stimulated for 7 days in AIM V™ medium (Gibco™) with 5% human AB Serum (Sigma Aldrich), supplemented with 25 µL/mL ImmunoCult (StemcellTM) and 100 U/mL IL-2 (Acro) at 37°C and 5% CO2. After 2 days, cells were diluted 1:2 using the same stimulation medium. After 7 days, 1 × 105 T cells were seeded into 96-well plates and incubated with sdAb agonists and (rh) IL-21 for 24 h at 37°C and 5% CO2. Cells were then fixed with 200 µL/well Fixation Buffer (BD Biosciences) at 37°C and 350 rpm for 10 min on a plate shaker. After two washing steps using pre-cooled washing buffer (PBS, 0.5% BSA) cells were permeabilized in 150 µL pre-cooled methanol per well for 30 min on ice and subsequently stained for Granzyme B expression using the respective isotype control, as well as anti-Granzyme B Brilliant Violet 421™ (BioLegend, 396,414, AB_2810603, 1:20). After two additional washes, cells were finally resuspended in 50 µL washing buffer and analyzed on an iQue3 (Sartorius). Granzyme B+ cells were normalized to (rh) IL-21 for each individual donor.

Molecular modeling and structural visualization

AlphaFold2 complex modeling

Complex models of IL-21 R and IL-2 Rγ with their respective binding VHH paratopes were generated using the ColabFold implementation of AlphaFold2. Binding poses were selected based on visual analysis to rationalize observed functional differences between paratope combinations.

ProteinMPNN-guided framework mutation design

Additional VHH framework mutations to promote noncovalent VHH:VHH dimerization (dsdAb(3)–dsdAb(5)) were designed using ProteinMPNN. VHH backbone structures were aligned to the DH851 (PDB code 7LU9) and DH898 (PDB code 7L6M) VH dimer interfaces. ProteinMPNN was used to predict amino acid substitutions that would stabilize VHH:VHH interactions while maintaining structural integrity. Structural models of ProteinMPNN designs were generated using the molecular modeling software MOE (Molecular Operating Environment 2020.09; Chemical Computing Group Inc., Montreal, Canada). Candidate mutations were ranked and selected using PRODIGY.59

Modeling of bispecific antibody constructs

Structural models of VHH domains were generated using AlphaFold2, constant and hinge regions for different bispecific formats were generated using the antibody modeler tool in MOE. For the construction of multispecifics, VHH domains were directly fused to the hinge or constant regions using MOE’s protein builder, followed by energy minimization of complete constructs.

Structure visualization and analysis

All three-dimensional structure visualization and analysis were performed using PyMOL (The PyMOL Molecular Graphics System, Version 2.3.0; Schrödinger, LLC, New York, NY).

Data processing and statistical analysis

Graphical and statistical analyses were conducted with GraphPad Prism 10.2.1 software. Dose-response curves were fitted using a nonlinear regression curve with three or four parameters, depending on the goodness of fit, to calculate Emax and EC50 values. p-values were calculated utilizing appropriate ANOVA analyses and the Bonferroni test as recommended. p ≤ 0.05 was regarded as statistically significant.

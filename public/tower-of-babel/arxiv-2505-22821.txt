Simple Classes of Automatic Structures
Achim Blumensath

Abstract
. We study two subclasses of the class of automatic structures: automatic structures of polynomial growth and Presburger structures. We present algebraic characterisations of the groups and the equivalence structures in these two classes.

Introduction

Automatic structures, introduced in
[
Hod76
,
Hod82
,
KN95
]
,
form a quite large class of infinite structures with good algorithmic properties.
Unfortunately these structures are less well-behaved from an algebraic perspective.
In particular, the class lacks good closure properties.
A persistent and non-trivial problem has been to obtain algebraic characterisations
of when a structure of a certain kind admits an automatic presentation,
or to prove that such a characterisation is not possible.
There is quite a long list of papers devoted to this topic
[
Blu99
,
Del04
,
KRS05
,
KNRS07
,
NT08
,
Tsa11
,
FT13
,
HKLL13
,
AGKP14
,
Rub21
]
.
As a further indication of the difficulty of the problem, let us also mention that
it is
\Sigma^{1}_{1}
-complete to decide whether a given computable structure is
automatic
[
BHK+19
]
.

Because of the difficulty of the full problem, several authors have introduced
subclasses of automatic structures that are simpler to deal with.
The most well-known such class is that of
unary automatic

structures
[
Blu99
,
KR01
]

which, unfortunately, turned out to be too simple to be of much interest.
A second subclass is that of automatic structures of
polynomial growth,

which was introduced in
[
Bár07
]
, see also
[
Hus16
,
GK20
]
.

In the present article we consider two subclasses of automatic structures
and we give characterisations of two kinds of algebras in these classes:
groups and equivalence structures.
The first class is that of structures of polynomial growth.
Automatic equivalence structures of polynomial growth have already been characterised
in
[
GK20
]
, but the proof in that article contains an error
caused by a confusion about what kind of coefficients the polynomials under consideration have.
Below we provide a new proof of this result, together with a characterisation of
automatic groups of polynomial growth.

The second class of automatic structures seems to be new: structures interpretable
in Presburger arithmetic. This class properly contains the class of structures
of polynomial growth. Also for this class we present characterisations of which groups
and equivalence structures it contains.

The overview of the article is as follows.
We start in Section
2
with recalling some preliminaries.
Sections
3
and
4
introduce the two classes we will be
studying: automatic structures of polynomial growth and Presburger structures.
We present several algebraic characterisations of such structures in Sections

5
and
6
. In the former, we study linear orders and groups,
in the latter, equivalence structures.

Preliminaries

Let us fix notation and terminology.
For

.
We denote the

An automatic structure is a relational structure where the universe and each relation
is given by a regular language over some alphabet.
To formally define what it means for a relation to be regular, we encode tuples of words
by a single word over the product alphabet.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\Sigma
be an alphabet and
\Box\notin\Sigma

(a)
The

\displaystyle w_{0}\otimes\cdots\otimes w_{n-1}:=\left[\begin{smallmatrix}a_{0,0}\\
a_{1,0}\\

a_{n-1,0}\end{smallmatrix}\right]\left[\begin{smallmatrix}a_{0,1}\\
a_{1,1}\\
\vdots\\
a_{n-1,1}\end{smallmatrix}\right]\cdots\left[\begin{smallmatrix}a_{0,l-1}\\
a_{1,l-1}\\
\vdots\\
a_{n-1,l-1}\end{smallmatrix}\right]

over the alphabet
(\Sigma+\{\Box\})^{n}
where

\displaystyle l:=\max_{i<n}{\lvert w_{i}\rvert}

and

is the

or
a_{i,j}:=\Box
if
w_{i}
has less than

(b)
A relation

is

if

\displaystyle L_{R}:=\{\,w_{0}\otimes\cdots\otimes w_{n-1}\langle w_{0},\dots,w_{n-1}\rangle\in R\,\}

(c) A relational

of
\frakA
.
Structures

with functions are called automatic,
if the corresponding relational structure
is automatic that is obtained from

Usually we identify the elements

of an automatic structure with the words representing them.
The length of this word is denoted by

.

The main reason automatic structures are so well-behaved algorithmically is the fact
that their first-order theory is decidable. In fact, decidability holds for the
following extension of first-order logic.

3.
Automatic structures of polynomial growth

Characterising which structures have an automatic presentation is a very hard problem.
To simplify the task, we introduce several subclasses of automatic structures
where it is easier to prove characterisations. We start with the following one,
which was first introduced in
[
Bár07
]
.

Definition 3.1
.

A language
L\subseteq\Sigma^{*}
has
polynomial growth

if there exists a polynomial
p(x)
such that

\displaystyle\bigl\lvert\{\,w\in L\lvert w\rvert\leq n\,\}\bigr\rvert\leq p(n)\,,\quad\text{for all }n<\omega\,.

Similarly, we say that an automatic structure
\frakA
has
polynomial growth

if there exists a polynomial
p(x)
such that

\displaystyle\bigl\lvert\{\,a\in A\lVert a\rVert\leq n\,\}\bigr\rvert\leq p(n)\,,\quad\text{for all }n<\omega\,.

In this case we also say that
\frakA
is
poly-growth automatic.

Example 1
.

The infinite grid
\langle\bbZ\times\bbZ,E_{0},E_{1}\rangle
is poly-growth automatic.
(We can represent a point
\langle i,k\rangle\in\bbZ\times\bbZ
by the word
a^{i}b^{k}
.)

The characterisation of automatic structures via interpretations from
Theorem
2
can be transferred
to poly-growth automatic structures as follows.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\frakA
be a structure. The following statements are equivalent.

(1)

\frakA
is an automatic structure of polynomial growth.

(2)

\frakA
has an automatic presentation whose universe is a finite union of languages of
the form

\displaystyle u_{0}v_{0}^{*}u_{1}v_{1}^{*}\cdots u_{k-1}v_{k-1}^{*}u_{k}\,,\quad\text{with }u_{0},\dots,u_{k},v_{0},\dots,v_{k-1}\in\Sigma^{*}.

(3)

\frakA
has an automatic presentation whose universe is a finite union of languages of
the form

\displaystyle a_{0}^{m_{0}}(b_{0}^{k_{0}})^{*}a_{1}^{m_{1}}(b_{1}^{k_{1}})^{*}\cdots a_{n-1}^{m_{n-1}}(b_{n-1}^{k_{n-1}})^{*}a_{n}^{m_{n}}

for distinct letters
a_{0},\dots,a_{n},b_{0},\dots,b_{n-1}

(with distinct members of the union using disjoint alphabets).

(4)

\frakA
is (
k
-dimensionally)
\textstyle\mathrm{FO}
-interpretable in

\langle\bbN,{\leq},m\divides{\cdot}\rangle
, for some

.

\frakA

\textstyle\mathrm{FO}
-interpretable in

Lemma 3.3
.

The class of poly-growth automatic structures is closed under finite disjoint
unions and finite direct products.

If we have

4.
Presburger Structures

Our second class is slightly larger than that of the poly-growth automatic structures.
The definition is as follows.

Definition 4.1
.

(a) A
Presburger structure
is a structure
\frakA

for which there exists a (many-dimensional)
\textstyle\mathrm{FO}
-interpretation of
\frakA

in
\langle\bbN,{+}\rangle
.

(b) We say that a subset
S\subseteq\bbN^{n}
is
Presburger-definable
if
it is
\textstyle\mathrm{FO}
-definable in
\langle\bbN,{+}\rangle
.

Proposition 4.2
.

Every poly-growth automatic structure is a Presburger structure and
every Presburger structure is automatic.

Proof.

The first claim follows from
Theorem
3.2

and the fact that there exists an
\textstyle\mathrm{FO}
-interpretation of

\langle\bbN,{\leq},m\divides{\cdot}\rangle
in
\langle\bbN,{+}\rangle
.
The second claim follows by Theorem
2
.
∎

To better understand Presburger structures, we need some results about which kinds of relations
are definable in
\langle\bbN,{+}\rangle
.

Definition 4.3
.

(a) A function
\varphi:\bbN^{m}\to\bbN^{n}
is
affine

if it is of the form

\displaystyle\varphi(\bar{x}):=u+\sum_{i<m}v_{i}x_{i}\,,\quad\text{for some }u,v_{0},\dots,v_{m-1}\in\bbN^{n}.

(b) A set
S\subseteq\bbN^{n}
is
semilinear
if it is of the form

\displaystyle S=\mathop{rng}\nolimits\varphi_{0}\cup\cdots\cup\mathop{rng}\nolimits\varphi_{k-1}\,,

for suitable affine functions
\varphi_{0},\dots,\varphi_{k-1}
.

(c) We call
S

simple
if it is of the form

S=\mathop{rng}\nolimits\varphi
for some affine function
\varphi:\bbN^{m}\to\bbN^{n}
such that
the images
\varphi(e_{0}),\dots,\varphi(e_{m-1})
of the unit vectors

e_{0},\dots,e_{m-1}
are linearly independent in
\bbQ^{n}
.

Definition 4.4
.

(a) For
k,m,p\in\bbN
with
p>1
, we write

\displaystyle k\divides_{p}m\quad\@defiff\quad k=p^{n}\divides m\,,\quad\text{for some }n\in\bbN\,.

(b) Two natural numbers
k,l\in\bbN
are
multiplicatively independent

if the only integer solution to the equation
k^{n}=l^{m}
is
n=0=m
.

It turns out that the Presburger-definable sets are exactly the semilinear ones.

Theorem 4.5
.

Let

(1)

(2)

(4)

\langle\bbN,{+},{\leq},m\divides{\cdot},0,1\rangle

Proposition 4.6

Every semilinear set

Our next aim is to derive a bound on the out-degree of a semilinear relation.

Definition 4.7
.

(a)
A
vector partition function
is a function
f:\bbN^{n}\to\bbN
that,
for some matrix
A\in\bbN^{n\times m}
, maps a tuple
\bar{x}\in\bbN^{n}

to the number of tuples
\bar{y}\in\bbN^{m}
such that
\bar{x}=A\bar{y}
.
We denote the vector partition function associated with
A
by
\psi_{A}:\bbN^{n}\to\bbN
.
(Note that not every matrix has an associated vector partition function since the equation

\bar{x}=A\bar{y}
might have infinitely many solutions.)

(b) A
generalised vector partition function
is a function of the form

\displaystyle g(\bar{x}):=\sum_{i<s}\psi_{A_{i}}(\bar{x}+\bar{c}_{i})\,,\quad\text{for }A_{i}\in\bbN^{n\times m_{i}}\text{ and }\bar{c}_{i}\in\bbN^{n}.

(c)
A function
g:\bbN^{n}\to\bbN
is a
piecewise polynomial

if there exists a partition
\calS
of
\bbN^{n}
into semilinear sets such that,
for every
S\in\calS
, the restriction
g\restriction S
is a polynomial
in
\bbQ[x_{0},\dots,x_{n-1}]
.

Proposition 4.8

Every vector partition function is piecewise polynomial.

Corollary 4.9

Every generalised vector partition function is piecewise polynomial.

Definition 4.10

For
\bar{x},\bar{a}\in\bbN^{n}
, we write

\displaystyle\bar{x}^{\bar{a}}:=x_{0}^{a_{0}}\cdots x_{n-1}^{a_{n-1}}\,.

Proposition 4.11

(Woods
[
Woo15
]
)
.

Let
R\subseteq\bbN^{k}\times\bbN^{l}
be a semilinear relation of finite out-degree.
The function
d:\bbN^{k}\to\bbN
mapping each tuple
\bar{u}\in\bbN^{k}
to its

R
-out-degree is a generalised vector partition function.

Proof.

We give a simplified proof of the original, stronger statement from
[
Woo15
]
.
With each relation
S\subseteq\bbN^{k+l}
we associate the formal power-series

\displaystyle f_{S}(\bar{x},\bar{y}):=\sum_{\langle\bar{c},\bar{d}\rangle\in S}\bar{x}^{\bar{c}}\bar{y}^{\bar{d}}.

We can use Proposition
4.6
to write

R=S_{0}\cup\dots\cup S_{n-1}
as a finite disjoint union of simple semilinear sets

S_{i}=\mathop{rng}\nolimits\varphi_{i}
. Suppose that

\displaystyle\varphi_{i}(0)=\bar{u}_{i}\bar{u}^{\prime}_{i}\quad\text{and}\quad\varphi_{i}(e_{j})=v_{i,j}\bar{v}^{\prime}_{i,j}\,,\quad\text{for }j<s_{i}\,.

A direct calculation shows that

\displaystyle f_{S_{i}}(\bar{x},\bar{y})=\frac{\bar{x}^{\bar{u}_{i}}\bar{y}^{\bar{u}^{\prime}_{i}}}{(1-\bar{x}^{\bar{v}^{\mathstrut}_{i,0}}\bar{y}^{\bar{v}^{\prime}_{i,0}})\cdots(1-\bar{x}^{\bar{v}^{\mathstrut}_{i,s_{i}-1}}\bar{y}^{\bar{v}^{\prime}_{i,s_{i}-1}})}\,.

Hence, we obtain

\displaystyle f_{R}(\bar{x},\bar{y})=\sum_{i<n}\frac{\bar{x}^{\bar{u}_{i}}\bar{y}^{\bar{u}^{\prime}_{i}}}{(1-\bar{x}^{\bar{v}^{\mathstrut}_{i,0}}\bar{y}^{\bar{v}^{\prime}_{i,0}})\cdots(1-\bar{x}^{\bar{v}^{\mathstrut}_{i,s_{i}-1}}\bar{y}^{\bar{v}^{\prime}_{i,s_{i}-1}})}\,,

which implies that

\displaystyle\sum_{\bar{c}\in\bbN^{k}}d(\bar{c})\bar{x}^{\bar{c}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\bar{c}\in\bbN^{k}}\bigl\lvert\{\,\bar{d}\langle\bar{c},\bar{d}\rangle\in R\,\}\bigr\rvert\cdot x^{\bar{c}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\langle\bar{c},\bar{d}\rangle\in R}\bar{x}^{\bar{c}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\langle\bar{c},\bar{d}\rangle\in R}\bar{x}^{\bar{c}}\bar{1}^{\bar{d}}

\displaystyle=f_{R}(\bar{x},1\dots 1)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i<n}\frac{\bar{x}^{\bar{u}_{i}}}{(1-\bar{x}^{\bar{v}_{i,0}})\cdots(1-\bar{x}^{\bar{v}_{i,s_{i}-1}})}\,.

Since generalised vector partition functions are closed under addition,
it is therefore sufficient to prove that, given a power-series of the form,

\displaystyle\sum_{\bar{b}}g(\bar{b})\bar{x}^{\bar{b}}=\frac{\bar{x}^{\bar{c}}}{(1-\bar{x}^{\bar{a}_{0}})\cdots(1-\bar{x}^{\bar{a}_{m-1}})}\,,

the coefficient function
g
is a generalised vector partition function.
In this case, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{\bar{b}}g(\bar{b}+\bar{c})\bar{x}^{\bar{b}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(1-\bar{x}^{\bar{a}_{0}})\cdots(1-\bar{x}^{\bar{a}_{m-1}})}

\displaystyle=\Bigl[\sum_{\mu_{0}<\omega}\bar{x}^{\mu_{0}\bar{a}_{0}}\Bigr]\cdots\Bigl[\sum_{\mu_{m-1}<\omega}\bar{x}^{\mu_{m-1}\bar{a}_{m-1}}\Bigr]

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mu_{0},\dots,\mu_{m-1}<\omega}\bar{x}^{\mu_{0}\bar{a}_{0}+\cdots+\mu_{m-1}\bar{a}_{m-1}}\,,

which implies that
g(\bar{z}+\bar{c})
is equal to the number of tuples
\bar{\mu}\in\bbN^{m}

satisfying

\displaystyle\bar{z}=\mu_{0}\bar{a}_{0}+\cdots+\mu_{m-1}\bar{a}_{m-1}\,.

Hence,
g(\bar{z})=\psi_{A}(\bar{z}+\bar{c})
, for some
A
and
c
.
∎

Example 2
.

Let
R\subseteq\bbN^{2}\times\bbN
be the set of all triples
\langle a,b,c\rangle

such that
c
is an even number with
a\leq c\leq b
. This relation is definable
in
\langle\bbN,{+}\rangle
and, hence, semilinear. Its out-degree is

\displaystyle d(a,b):=\begin{cases}0&\text{if }a>b\,,\\
\frac{1}{2}(b-a)+1&\text{for }a\leq b\text{ and }a,b\text{ even,}\\
\frac{1}{2}(b-a)&\text{for }a\leq b\text{ and }a,b\text{ odd,}\\
\frac{1}{2}(b-a+1)&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

In particular, note that
d\in\bbQ[a,b]
, but
d\notin\bbN[a,b]
.
Finally, note that
d
is the a vector partition function associated with the equation

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}a\\
b\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}0&0&1\\
1&2&1\end{bmatrix}\cdot\begin{bmatrix}x\\
y\\
z\end{bmatrix}\,.

5.
Growth Arguments

To prove that certain structures are not poly-growth automatic we can use
the following growth argument, which follows from
Theorem
2.3
together with a pumping argument
originally due to Khoussainov and Nerode
[
KN95
]
.

Definition 5.1
.

Let
\frakA
be a structure and
\varphi(\bar{x},y)
a formula.
For a set
U\subseteq A
and a number
n<\omega
, we define
the set
N_{\varphi}(U,n)
of
reachable elements at distance
n
by

\displaystyle N_{\varphi}(U,0)

\displaystyle:=U\,,

and

\displaystyle\penalty N_{\varphi}(U,n+1)

\displaystyle:=N_{\varphi}(U,n)\cup\{\,b\in A\frakA\models\varphi(\bar{a},b)\text{ for some }\bar{a}\subseteq U\,\}\kern 0.80002pt.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
\frakA
be an automatic structure.
For every
{\mathchoice{\mbox{$\displaystyle\mathrm{FOC}$}}{\mbox{$\textstyle\mathrm{FOC}$}}{\mbox{$\scriptstyle\mathrm{FOC}$}}{\mbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{FOC}$}}}(\sfU)
-formula
\varphi(\bar{x};\bar{z})
of finite out-degree,
there exists a constant
k
such that

\displaystyle\frakA\models\varphi(\bar{a};\bar{c})\quad\text{implies}\quad\lVert\bar{a}\rVert\leq\lVert\bar{c}\rVert+k\,,\quad\text{for all }\bar{a},\bar{c}\,.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
\frakA
be a poly-growth automatic structure,
U\subseteq A
finite,
and
\varphi
a formula of finite out-degree. Then there exist constants
d,k>0
such that

\displaystyle\lvert N_{\varphi}(U,n)\rvert\leq n^{d}+k\,,\quad\text{for all }n<\omega\,.

Proof.

Let
l:=\max{\{\,\lVert c\rVert c\in U\,\}}
.
By Lemma
5.2
, we can find a constant
c
such that

\displaystyle\lVert a\rVert\leq l+cn\,,\quad\text{for all }a\in N_{\varphi}(U,n)\,.

By assumption, there exists a polynomial
p(x)
such that the universe of
\frakA

contains at most
p(n)
words of length at most
n
.
Consequently,

\displaystyle\lvert N_{\varphi}(U,n)\rvert\leq p(l+cn)\,.

∎

Our first case study concerns linear orders. We start with ordinals.

Theorem 5.4
.

An ordinal
\langle\alpha,{\leq}\rangle
is poly-growth automatic if, and only if,

\alpha<\omega^{\omega}
.

Proof.

(\Rightarrow)
It was shown in
[
Del04
]
that
all automatic ordinals are smaller than
\omega^{\omega}
.

(\Leftarrow)

It follows by Lemma
3.3

and Theorem
3.2
that the class of
poly-growth automatic ordinals is closed under ordinal addition and multiplication.
Furthermore,
\langle\omega,{\leq}\rangle
is poly-growth automatic.
∎

Definition 5.5
.

Let
\frakA
be a coloured linear order.

(a)
\frakA
is

if the order of the rationals cannot be embedded into
A
.

(b)
\frakA
is

if it can be (

-dimensionally)
\textstyle\mathrm{MSO}
-interpreted
in the infinite binary tree
\langle\{0,1\}^{*},\mathrm{suc}_{0},\mathrm{suc}_{1}\rangle
.

Proposition 5.6
.

Let
\frakA
be a coloured linear order.
If
\frakA
is regular and scattered, it is poly-growth automatic.

Proof.

It is a well-known result (see, e.g., Section

.4 of
[
Blu
]
)
that every scattered regular linear order
\frakA
can be constructed from finite linear orders
using finite ordered sums and right multiplication by
\omega
or

.
By Lemma
3.3

and Theorem

,
all of these operations preserve poly-growth automaticity.
∎

Example 3
.

The converse is not true. Let
\langle\omega,{\leq},P\rangle
be the order with

\displaystyle P:=\{\,n(n+1)/2n<\omega\,\}\,.

This order has an automatic presentation

Instead of the converse, we can use Lemma

to prove

Definition 5.7
.

Let
\calZ

\omega^{\mathrm{op}}

\displaystyle{\mathchoice{\mbox{$\displaystyle\mathrm{VD}$}}{\mbox{$\textstyle\mathrm{VD}$}}{\mbox{$\scriptstyle\mathrm{VD}$}}{\mbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{VD}$}}}_{0}

\displaystyle{\mathchoice{\mbox{$\displaystyle\mathrm{VD}$}}{\mbox{$\textstyle\mathrm{VD}$}}{\mbox{$\scriptstyle\mathrm{VD}$}}{\mbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{VD}$}}}_{\alpha+1}

Proposition 5.8
.

For every poly-growth automatic linear order

, we have
{\mathchoice{\mbox{$\displaystyle\mathrm{VD}$}}{\mbox{$\textstyle\mathrm{VD}$}}{\mbox{$\scriptstyle\mathrm{VD}$}}{\mbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{VD}$}}}(\frakA)<\omega

For the proof, we introduce a second rank for linear orders.
The

\mathrm{cn}(\frakA)
of a linear oder
\frakA
is the quotient
\frakA/{\sim}

by the equivalence relation

\displaystyle x\sim y\quad\@defiff\quad\text{there are only finitely many elements between }x\text{ and }y\,.

For each ordinal
\alpha

\alpha

\displaystyle\mathrm{cn}^{0}(\frakA):=\frakA\,,\quad\mathrm{cn}^{\alpha+1}(\frakA)=\mathrm{cn}(\mathrm{cn}^{\alpha}(\frakA))\,,

and, for a limit ordinal

\mathrm{cn}^{\delta}(\frakA)

(\mathrm{cn}^{\alpha}(\frakA))_{\alpha<\delta}

{\mathchoice{\mbox{$\displaystyle\mathrm{FC}$}}{\mbox{$\textstyle\mathrm{FC}$}}{\mbox{$\scriptstyle\mathrm{FC}$}}{\mbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{FC}$}}}(\frakA)

\frakA
.

{\mathchoice{\mbox{$\displaystyle\mathrm{FC}$}}{\mbox{$\textstyle\mathrm{FC}$}}{\mbox{$\scriptstyle\mathrm{FC}$}}{\mbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{FC}$}}}(\frakA)={\mathchoice{\mbox{$\displaystyle\mathrm{VD}$}}{\mbox{$\textstyle\mathrm{VD}$}}{\mbox{$\scriptstyle\mathrm{VD}$}}{\mbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{VD}$}}}(\frakA)

Then

-interpret

,

is poly-growth automatic.
Let

be the formula stating that

is the

-least element

has finite out-degree and

\displaystyle\lvert N_{\varphi}(\{a,b\},n)\rvert=2+2^{n-1},\quad\text{for }a<b

(in the structure

A contradiction to Lemma

.
∎

Next, let us take a look at groups and semigroups.
A characterisation of all finitely generated Presburger groups follows immediately
from the corresponding characterisation of automatic groups.
(Here, an

is an automatic structure that happens to be a group.
There is also a commonly used notion of an automatic group due to Thurston
[
ECH+92
]
,
which is more restrictive and which we will not be dealing with in this article.)

Proposition 5.9
.

Let
\frakG
be a finitely generated group. The following statements are equivalent.

(1)

\frakG
is a Presburger structure.

(2)

\frakG
is automatic.

(3)

\frakG
is virtually abelian.

Proof.

(2)
\Leftrightarrow
(3) has been proved as Theorem 8 of
[
OT05
]
;
(1)
\Rightarrow
(2) is trivial;
and (3)
\Rightarrow
(1) follows from Remark 4 in
[
OT05
]

where the authors construct an interpretation of
\frakG

in
\langle\bbZ,{+}\rangle
and, hence, also in

(see also Section

.9 of
[
Blu
]
).
∎

The class of poly-growth automatic groups turns out to be much smaller.
Before giving the characterisation, let us take a quick look at poly-growth
automatic semigroups.

Lemma 5.10
.

Let
\frakS
be a semigroup such that there exists an embedding of

\langle\bbN\setminus\{0\},{+}\rangle
into
\frakS
.
Then
\frakS
is not poly-growth automatic.

Proof.

Suppose that
\frakS=\langle S,{+}\rangle
is an automatic semigroup into which

\langle\bbN\setminus\{0\},{+}\rangle
can be embedded,
and let
c
be the image of
1
under this embedding.
By Lemma 3.2 of
[
KNRS07
]
, there exists a constant
k
such that

\displaystyle\lVert nc\rVert\leq\lVert c\rVert+k\log_{2}n\,,\quad\text{for all }n\,.

It follows that

\displaystyle n\leq 2^{(m-\lVert c\rVert)/k}\quad\text{implies}\quad\lVert nc\rVert\leq m\,.

Hence, the set
\{\,a\in S\lVert a\rVert\leq m\,\}
contains at least

2^{(m-\lVert c\rVert)/k}
elements and
\frakS
is not of polynomial growth.
∎

It turns out that the only poly-growth automatic groups are the finite ones.

Theorem 5.11
.

A group is poly-growth automatic if, and only if, it is finite.

Proof.

Let
\frakG=\langle G,{}\cdot{},{}^{-1},e\rangle
be a poly-growth automatic group.
By Lemma
5.2
there exists a constant
k
such that

•

for every
a\in G
, there is some
b\in G
with

\lVert a\rVert<\lVert b\rVert\leq\lVert a\rVert+k\,,

•

\lVert ab\rVert\leq\max{\{\lVert a\rVert,\lVert b\rVert\}}+k\,,

•

\lVert a^{-1}\rVert\leq\lVert a\rVert+k\,.

Setting
m:=\lVert e\rVert
, it follows that, for each
n<\omega
,
there exists some element
a_{n}\in A
of length

\displaystyle m+4kn\leq\lVert a_{n}\rVert<m+4kn+k\,.

Set
D_{0}:=\{e\}
,

\displaystyle C_{n}

\displaystyle:=\{\,a_{0}^{s_{0}}\cdots a_{n-1}^{s_{n-1}}s_{0},\dots,s_{n-1}\in\{0,1\}\,\}\,,

\displaystyle D_{n}

\displaystyle:=\{\,a^{-1}ba,b\in C_{n}\,\}\,.

We claim that

(i)

\lVert c\rVert<m+4k(n-1)+2k
, for all
n>0
and
c\in C_{n}
,

(ii)

\lVert c\rVert<m+4kn
, for all
c\in D_{n}
,

(iii)

\lvert C_{n}\rvert=2^{n}
.

It follows that
G
contains at least
2^{n}
elements of length at most
4kn
.
A contradiction to the fact that
\frakG
has polynomial growth.
Hence, it remains to prove the above claims.

(i)

We proceed by induction on
n
.
For
n=1
, we have
\lVert e\rVert=m
and
\lVert a_{0}\rVert<m+k\leq m+2k
.
For the inductive step, let
c\in C_{n}
.
If
c\in C_{n-1}
, the claim follows by inductive hypothesis.
Otherwise, we can write
c=da_{n-1}
with
d\in D_{n-1}
.
Then
\lVert d\rVert,\lVert a_{n-1}\rVert<m+4k(n-1)+k
implies, by choice of
k
, that

\displaystyle\lVert c\rVert<m+4k(n-1)+2k\,.

(ii)

Let
a,b\in C_{n}
. By
(i),
we have
\lVert a\rVert,\lVert b\rVert<m+4k(n-1)+2k
.
By choice of
k
, this implies that

\displaystyle\lVert a^{-1}b\rVert<m+4k(n-1)+2k+2k=m+4kn\,.

(iii)

Suppose that

\displaystyle a_{0}^{s_{0}}\cdots a_{n}^{s_{n}}=a_{0}^{t_{0}}\cdots a_{n}^{t_{n}},\quad\text{for }s_{0},\dots,s_{n},t_{0},\dots,t_{n}\in\{0,1\}\,.

We prove that
s_{i}=t_{i}
by induction on
n
. Set

\displaystyle b:=a_{0}^{s_{0}}\cdots a_{n-1}^{s_{n-1}}\quad\text{and}\quad c:=a_{0}^{t_{0}}\cdots a_{n-1}^{t_{n-1}}.

If
s_{n}=t_{n}
, we obtain
b=c
and the claim follows by inductive hypothesis.
Otherwise, we may assume without loss of generality that
s_{n}=0
and
t_{n}=1
.
Hence,

\displaystyle b=ca_{n}\quad\text{implies}\quad a_{n}=c^{-1}b\in D_{n}\,.

By
(iii),
it follows that
\lVert a_{n}\rVert<m+4kn
.
A contradiction to our choice of
a_{n}
.
∎

6.
Equivalence Structures

The aim of this last section is to prove characterisations both of Presburger equivalence
relations and of poly-growth automatic equivalence relations.

Definition 6.1
.

(a) An
equivalence structure
is a structure of the
form
\langle A,{\sim}\rangle
where
\sim
is an equivalence relation on
A
.

(b) Given a function
g:\bbN^{n}\to(\bbN\setminus\{0\})\cup\{\infty\}
,
we denote by
\frakE(g)
the equivalence structure with exactly
\lvert g^{-1}(k)\rvert
classes
of size
k
, for each
k\in(\bbN\setminus\{0\})\cup\{\infty\}
.

Our aim is to prove the following two characterisations.

Theorem 6.2
.

An equivalence structure
\frakA
is a Presburger structure if, and only if,

\displaystyle\frakA\cong\frakE(g)+\frakC

where
g
is a generalised vector partition function and

\frakC
is a countable equivalence structure with only infinite classes.

Theorem 6.3
.

An equivalence structure
\frakA
is poly-growth automatic if, and only if,
it can be written as a finite disjoint union of

•

structures of the form
\frakE(p)
, for polynomials
p\in\bbN[\bar{x}]
, and

•

countable equivalence structures where every class is infinite.

Remark
.

Theorem
6.3

was already stated in
[
GK20
]
, but the proof in that article
contained an error: for one direction the authors require a polynomial with natural
coefficients, but the other direction only produces polynomials with rational ones.
Below we will present a new, correct proof.

We start with a simple lemma that helps us to define interpretations of structures
of the form
\frakE(g)
in well-ordered structures, i.e., structures where one of
the relations is a well-ordering.

Lemma 6.4
.

Let
\frakA
be a well-ordered structure and
g:\bbN^{n}\to\bbN
a function.
There exists an
\textstyle\mathrm{FO}
-interpretation of
\frakE(g)
in
\frakA

if, and only if, there are
k,m<\omega
, an injective function
\sigma:\bbN^{n}\to A^{k}
,
and an
\textstyle\mathrm{FO}
-definable relation
R\subseteq A^{k}\times A^{m}
such that

\displaystyle d_{R}(\bar{a})=\begin{cases}g(\bar{c})&\text{if }\bar{a}=\sigma(\bar{c})\,,\\
0&\text{otherwise}\,,\end{cases}

where
d_{R}
is the function mapping a tuple
\bar{a}
to its
R
-out-degree.

Proof.

(\Leftarrow)
Given
R
, we set

\displaystyle\langle\bar{x},\bar{y}\rangle\sim\langle\bar{x}^{\prime},\bar{y}^{\prime}\rangle\quad\@defiff\quad\bar{x}=\bar{x}^{\prime}\,.

Then
\langle R,{\sim}\rangle\cong\frakE(g)
.

(\Rightarrow)
Let
\tau=\langle\delta(\bar{x}),\varphi(\bar{x},\bar{y})\rangle

be a
k
-dimensional
\textstyle\mathrm{FO}
-interpretation of
\frakE(g)=\langle E,{\sim}\rangle

in
\frakA
and let
\nu:\delta^{\frakA}\to E
be the corresponding isomorphism.
By definition of
\frakE(g)
, there exists a bijection
\rho:\bbN^{n}\to E/{\sim}
such that

\displaystyle\lvert\rho(\bar{k})\rvert=g(\bar{k})\,,\quad\text{for all }\bar{k}\in\bbN^{n}.

Set
{\approx}:=\varphi^{\frakA}
and let
P\subseteq\delta^{\frakA}
be the set containing
the minimal (w.r.t. the lexicographic ordering induced by the well-ordering of
\frakA
)
element of each
\approx
-class. Then
R:={\approx}\cap(P\times\bbN^{m})
is
\textstyle\mathrm{FO}
-definable
and the
R
-out-degree of an element
\bar{a}\in P
is

\displaystyle\bigl\lvert[\bar{a}]_{\approx}\bigr\rvert=\bigl\lvert[\nu(\bar{a})]_{\sim}\bigr\rvert=g\bigl(\rho^{-1}\bigl([\nu(\bar{a})]_{\sim}\bigr)\bigr)=g\bigl((\rho^{-1}\circ q\circ\nu)(\bar{a})\bigr)\,,

where
q:E\to E/{\sim}
is the projection.
Since the restriction of
\rho^{-1}\circ q\circ\nu
to
P
is bijective,
we obtain the desired function
\sigma
by setting

\displaystyle\sigma:=(\rho^{-1}\circ q\circ\nu\restriction P)^{-1}:\bbN^{n}\to A^{k}.

∎

Our characterization of Presburger equivalence structures can now be proved as follows.

Proof of Theorem
6.2
.

(\Leftarrow)

Note that the equivalence structures
\frakC_{1}:=\langle\bbN,E_{1}\rangle
and

\frakC_{\infty}:=\langle\bbN^{2},E_{\infty}\rangle
with

\displaystyle E_{1}:=\bbN\times\bbN\quad\text{and}\quad E_{\infty}:=\bigl\{\,\langle\langle n,i\rangle,\langle n,j\rangle\rangle\bigm n,i,j\in\bbN\,\bigr\}

are Presburger structures. (
\frakC_{1}
has a single infinite class and

\frakC_{\infty}
has countably infinitely many.)
Since Presburger structures are closed under finite disjoint unions, it therefore remains
to show that

\displaystyle g^{\prime\prime}_{D}(x_{0},\dots,x_{n-1}):=g^{\prime}_{D}(dx_{0},\dots,dx_{n-1})\,.

Then

\displaystyle\frakE(g^{\prime\prime}_{D})\cong\frakE(g^{\prime}_{D})\quad\text{and}\quad g^{\prime\prime}_{D}\in\bbN[\bar{x}]\,.

Since

\displaystyle\frakK(f)\cong\frakE(\mathrm{fib}_{f})\cong\sum_{D\in\calC^{t}_{m}}\frakE(\mathrm{fib}_{f}\circ\varphi_{D})\cong\sum_{D\in\calC^{t}_{m}}\frakE(g^{\prime\prime}_{D})\,,

the claim follows.
∎

Abstract
. In this paper we establish a relationship between the Milnor number, the χ \chi -number, and the Tjurina number of a foliation with respect to an effective balanced divisor of separatrices. Moreover, using the Gómez-Mont–Seade–Verjovsky index, we prove that the difference between the multiplicity and the Tjurina number of a foliation with respect to a reduced curve is independent of the foliation. We also derive an adjunction formula for the Tjurina number of a foliation with respect to a reduced curve. These local results have global consequences; for instance, we provide a new proof of a global result regarding the multiplicity of a foliation due to Cerveau-Lins Neto and a new proof of a Soares’s inequality for the sum of the Milnor number of an invariant curve of a foliation. Additionally, we obtain upper and lower bounds for the global Tjurina number of a foliation on the complex projective plane. Finally, we provide an example that answers a conjecture posed by Alcántara–Mozo-Fernández about foliations on the complex projective plane having a unique singularity.

1.
Introduction

The aim of this paper is to study indices of local and global foliations in the complex projective plane
\mathbb{P}^{2}_{\mathbb{C}}
. We focus particularly on four indices of the foliations: the
Milnor number
, the
Gómez-Mont–Seade–Verjovsky index
(abbreviated as the GSV-index), the
multiplicity
, and the
Tjurina number
with respect to an invariant curve.

First, using known adjunction formulas for the GSV-index and the Tjurina number of a singular curve, we present an adjunction formula for the Tjurina number of a foliation, see for instance Proposition
3.1
. Next, we prove in Proposition
4.1
that the difference between the multiplicity and the Tjurina number of a foliation with respect to a reduced curve does not depend on the foliation. More specifically, let
\mathcal{F}
be a germ of a singular holomorphic foliation at
(\mathbb{C}^{2},p)
and let
C
be any
\mathcal{F}
-invariant reduced curve. Then

\mu_{p}(\mathcal{F},C)-\tau_{p}(\mathcal{F},C)=\mu_{p}(C)-\tau_{p}(C),

where
\mu_{p}(\mathcal{F},C)
and
\tau_{p}(\mathcal{F},C)
denote the multiplicity and Tjurina number of
\mathcal{F}
along
C
, respectively, and
\mu_{p}(C)
,
\tau_{p}(C)
are the classical Milnor and Tjurina number of
C
.
This result leads to several applications; for instance, we show that

GSV_{p}(\mathcal{F},C)<4\tau_{p}(\mathcal{F},C)-3\mu_{p}(\mathcal{F},C).

As a consequence in Corollary
4.2
, we provide a positive answer to a question posed in
[
17
]
.

In Section
5
, following the terminology of balaced divisor of separatrices of a foliation introduced by Genzmer
[
21
]
, we present Proposition
5.1
that generalizes Corollary B of
[
16
]
. We recall that a foliation
\mathcal{F}
is said to be a
generalized curve
if there are no saddle-nodes in its reduction process of singularities. This concept was introduced by Camacho, Lins Neto and Sad in
[
9
]
and the non-dicritical case, delimits a family of foliations whose topology is closely related to that of their separatrices (i.e. local invariant curves). Generalized curve foliations are part of the broader family of

, introduced by Mattei and Salem in
[
30
]
. Foliations in this family may admit saddle-nodes in the reduction process of singularities, provided that they are not
tangent saddle-nodes
(see
[
18

). In order to study second type foliations, the
\chi
-number of
\mathcal{F}
was introduced in
[

, Section 3]
. This number, denoted by

, can be interpreted as the difference
\mu_{p}(\mathcal{F})-\mu_{p}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{B})
, where
\mu_{p}(\mathcal{F})
is the Milnor number of
\mathcal{F}

\mathcal{F}

Now, we can establish our main result as follows:

Theorem A
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a germ of a singular holomorphic foliation at
(\mathbb{C}^{2},p)
, and
\mathcal{B}
be an effective primitive balanced divisor of separatrices for
\mathcal{F}
at

.
Then

\frac{\mu_{p}(\mathcal{F})}{\tau_{p}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{B})+\chi_{p}(\mathcal{F})}<\frac{4}{3}.

Moreover, if

is of second type then

\frac{\mu_{p}(\mathcal{F})}{\tau_{p}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{B})}<\frac{4}{3}.
In particular, if
\mathcal{F}
is a non-dicritical foliation of second type, and
C
is the total union of separatrices, then
\frac{\mu_{p}(\mathcal{F})}{\tau_{p}(\mathcal{F},C)}<\frac{4}{3}.

The proof of Theorem

will be given in Section

Finally, we apply our local results in three ways. First, we provide a new proof of a result by Cerveau-Lins Neto
[

]
and a new proof of a Soares’s inequality
[

]

2.
Basic tools

To establish the terminology and the notation, we recall some basic concepts from local Foliation Theory. Unless stated otherwise, throughout this text,
\mathcal{F}
denotes a germ of a singular holomorphic foliation at
(\mathbb{C}^{2},p)
. In local coordinates
(x,y)
centered at
p
, the foliation
\mathcal{F}
is given by a holomorphic 1-form

\omega=P(x,y)dx+Q(x,y)dy,

or by its dual vector field

v=-Q(x,y)\frac{\partial}{\partial{x}}+P(x,y)\frac{\partial}{\partial{y}},

where
P(x,y),Q(x,y)\in\mathbb{C}\{x,y\}
are relatively prime and
{\mathbb{C}}\{x,y\}
is the ring of complex convergent power series in two variables.
The
algebraic multiplicity

\nu_{p}(\mathcal{F})
is the minimum of the orders
\nu_{p}(P)
,
\nu_{p}(Q)
at
p
of the coefficients of a local generator of
\mathcal{F}
.

Let
f(x,y)\in\mathbb{C}[[x,y]]
, where
{\mathbb{C}}[[x,y]]
is the ring of complex formal power series in two variables.
We say that
C:f(x,y)=0
is
invariant
by
\mathcal{F}
or
\mathcal{F}
-
invariant
if

\omega\wedge df=(f.h)dx\wedge dy,

for some
h\in\mathbb{C}[[x,y]]
. If
C
is an irreducible
\mathcal{F}
-invariant curve then we will say that
C
is a
separatrix
of
\mathcal{F}
. The separatrix
C
is analytical if
f
is convergent. We denote by
\mathrm{Sep}_{p}(\mathcal{F})
the set of all separatrices of
\mathcal{F}
. When
\mathrm{Sep}_{p}(\mathcal{F})
is a finite set we will say that the foliation
\mathcal{F}
is
non-dicritical
and we call
total union of separatrices
of
\mathcal{F}
to the union of all elements of
\mathrm{Sep}_{p}(\mathcal{F})
. Otherwise we will say that
\mathcal{F}
is a
dicritical
foliation.

2.1.
GSV-index

The GSV-index was introduced by Gómez-Mont-Seade-Verjovsky in
[
25
]
. This index is a topological invariant for vector fields on singular complex varieties and is closely related to the Euler-Poincaré characteristic of the Milnor fiber (see, for instance
[
8
, Theorem 3.1]
). It also relates to the Schwartz index and local Euler obstruction through a proportionality theorem
[
5
, Theorem 3.1]
. In this subsection, we follow the presentation of
[
6
]
.

Let
\mathcal{F}:\omega=0
be a singular foliation at
(\mathbb{C}^{2},p)
. Let
C:f(x,y)=0
be an
\mathcal{F}
-invariant curve, where
f(x,y)\in\mathbb{C}[[x,y]]
is reduced. Then, as in the convergent case, there are
g,h\in\mathbb{C}[[x,y]]
(depending on
f
and
\omega
), with
f
and
g
and
f
and
h
relatively prime and a
1
-form
\eta
(see
[
36
, Lemma 1.1 and its proof]
) such that

(1)

g\omega=hdf+f\eta.

The
Gómez-Mont-Seade-Verjovsky index
of the foliation
\mathcal{F}
at
(\mathbb{C}^{2},p)
(GSV-index) with respect to an analytic
\mathcal{F}
-invariant curve
C
is

(2)

GSV_{p}(\mathcal{F},C)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\partial C}\frac{g}{h}d\left(\frac{h}{g}\right),

where
g,h\in\mathbb{C}\{x,y\}
are from (
1
).

2.2.
The multiplicity of a foliation along a separatrix.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a germ of a singular foliation at
(\mathbb{C}^{2},p)
induced by the vector field
v
and
B
be a separatrix of
\mathcal{F}
. Let
\gamma:(\mathbb{C},0)\to(\mathbb{C}^{2},p)
be a primitive parametrization of
B
. Camacho-Lins Neto-Sad
[
9
, Section 4]
defined
the
multiplicity of
\mathcal{F}
along
B
at
p
as

\mu_{p}(\mathcal{F},B):=\text{ord}_{t}\theta(t)
,
where
\theta(t)
is the unique vector field at
(\mathbb{C},0)
such that

\gamma_{*}\theta(t)=v\circ\gamma(t)
.
If
\omega=P(x,y)dx+Q(x,y)dy
is a 1-form inducing
\mathcal{F}
and
\gamma(t)=(x(t),y(t))
, we have

\theta(t)=\begin{cases}-\frac{Q(\gamma(t))}{x^{\prime}(t)}&\text{if $x(t)\neq 0$}\\
\frac{P(\gamma(t))}{y^{\prime}(t)}&\text{if $y(t)\neq 0$}.\end{cases}

2.3.
The Tjurina number of a foliation along an invariant reduced curve

Let
\mathcal{F}
be the germ of holomorphic foliation defined by
\omega=P(x,y)dx+Q(x,y)dy
at
(\mathbb{C}^{2},p)
, and let
C:f(x,y)=0
be an
invariant reduced curve
. The
Tjurina number
of
\mathcal{F}
with respect to
C
is by definition

\tau_{p}(\mathcal{F},C):=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\mathbb{C}[[x,y]]/(P,Q,f).

This number appears for first time in
[
24
]
, and the terminology of Tjurina number of
\mathcal{F}
with respect to
C
was given in
[
10
, p. 159]
.

3.
Adjunction formulas for the Tjurina number of a foliation

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a germ of a singular holomorphic foliation at
(\mathbb{C}^{2},p)
, and let
C=\cup_{j=1}^{r}C_{j}
be an
\mathcal{F}
-invariant reduced curve such that each
C_{j}
is irreducible. Then, from
[
38
, Theorem 6.5.1]
,
[
27
, Proposition 3.2]
,
[
29
, Equation (2.2), p. 329]
, and
[
7
, p. 29]
, we obtain the following formulas, respectively:

(3)

\displaystyle\mu_{p}(C)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{r}\mu_{p}(C_{j})+2\left(\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq r}I_{p}(C_{i},C_{j})\right)-r+1,

(4)

\displaystyle\tau_{p}(C)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{r}\tau_{p}(C_{j})+\left(\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq r}I_{p}(C_{i},C_{j})\right)+\Theta,

(5)

\displaystyle\mu_{p}(\mathcal{F},C)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{r}\mu_{p}(\mathcal{F},C_{j})-r+1,

(6)

\displaystyle GSV_{p}(\mathcal{F},C)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{j=1}^{r}GSV_{p}(\mathcal{F},C_{j})\right)-2\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq r}I_{p}(C_{i},C_{j}),

where
I_{p}(C_{i},C_{j})
denotes the intersection multiplicity of the branches
C_{i}
and
C_{j}
at
p
, and
\Theta
is a number that depends on the module of Kähler differentials of
C
. Its explicity formula is given in
[
27
, Theorem 5.1]
.

Analogous to the adjunction formula for the Tjurina number of curves, we provide an adjunction formula for the Tjurina number of a foliation
\mathcal{F}
with respect to a reduced
\mathcal{F}
-invariant curve
C
, in terms of the Tjurina numbers of
\mathcal{F}
with respect to the irreducible components of
C
.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a germ of a singular holomorphic foliation at
(\mathbb{C}^{2},p)
and let
C=\cup_{j=1}^{r}C_{j}
be a
\mathcal{F}
-invariant reduced curve. Then

\displaystyle\tau_{p}(\mathcal{F},C)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum^{r}_{j=1}\tau(\mathcal{F},C_{j})-\left(\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq r}I_{p}(C_{i},C_{j})\right)+\Theta.

Proof.

After
[
16
, Proposition 6.2]
we get
\tau_{p}(\mathcal{F},C)=GSV_{p}(\mathcal{F},C)+\tau_{p}(C)
. Hence by equalities (
6
) and (
4
), we obtain

\displaystyle\tau_{p}(\mathcal{F},C)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle GSV_{p}(\mathcal{F},C)+\tau_{p}(C)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum^{r}_{j=1}GSV_{p}(\mathcal{F},C_{j})-2\sum_{i<j}I_{p}(C_{i},C_{j})+\sum^{r}_{j=1}\tau_{p}(C_{j})+\left(\sum_{i<j}I_{p}(C_{i},C_{j})\right)+\Theta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum^{r}_{j=1}\tau(\mathcal{F},C_{j})-\left(\sum_{i<j}I_{p}(C_{i},C_{j})\right)+\Theta.

∎

We illustrate Proposition
3.1
with the following example.

Example 3.2
.

We borrow from
[
16
, Example 6.5]
the family of foliations

,

, given by the

which is a family of dicritical foliations which are not of second type. An effective balanced divisor of separatrices of

is
\mathcal{B}=\mathcal{B}_{0}:xy=0
. Put
B_{1}:x=0
and
B_{2}:y=0
. Hence
\tau_{0}(\mathcal{F}_{k},\mathcal{B})=3k-2
,
\tau_{0}(\mathcal{F}_{k},B_{1})=k
, and
\tau_{0}(\mathcal{F}_{k},B_{2})=2k-1
, since

by (
4

\begin{array}[]{lll}\tau_{0}(\mathcal{F}_{k},\mathcal{B})&=&\tau_{0}(\mathcal{F}_{k},B_{1})+\tau_{0}(\mathcal{F}_{K},B_{2})-I_{0}(B_{1},B_{2})+\Theta\\
&=&k+2k-1-1+0=3k-2.\end{array}

4.
The GSV-index, the multiplicity and the Tjurina number of a foliation

5.
Multiplicity of a foliation along a divisor of separatrices

Following

(12)

\mu_{p}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{B})=\left(\displaystyle\sum_{B}a_{B}\cdot\mu_{p}(\mathcal{F},B)\right)-\deg(\mathcal{B})+1.

The notion of balanced divisor of separatrices of a foliation was introduced by Genzmer in
[
21
]
, see also
[
23
]
.
By convention we set
\mu_{p}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{B})=1
for any empty divisor
\mathcal{B}
.
In particular, when
\mathcal{B}=B_{1}+\cdots+B_{r}
is an
effective primitive divisor of separatrices
of the singular foliation
\mathcal{F}
at
p
, we recover
[
29
, Equation (2.2), p. 329]
(for the reduced plane curve
C:=\cup_{i=1}^{r}B_{i}
).
Hence, if we write
\mathcal{B}=\mathcal{B}_{0}-\mathcal{B}_{\infty}
where
\mathcal{B}_{0}
and
\mathcal{B}_{\infty}
are effective divisors we obtain

(13)

\mu_{p}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{B})=\mu_{p}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{B}_{0})-\mu_{p}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{B}_{\infty})+1.

Since
\mu(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{B}_{\infty})\geq 1
, it follows that

\mu_{p}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{B}_{0})\geq\mu_{p}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{B}).
The
Milnor number

\mu_{p}(\mathcal{F})
of the foliation
\mathcal{F}
at
p
given by the
1
-form
\omega=P(x,y)dx+Q(x,y)dy
is defined by

\mu_{p}(\mathcal{F})=i_{p}(P,Q).

Remember that we consider
P
and
Q
coprime, so
\mu_{p}(\mathcal{F})
is a non negative integer. In
[
9
, Theorem A]
it was proved that the Milnor number of a foliation is a topological invariant.

In
[
16
, Section 3]
the
\chi
-number of the foliation
\mathcal{F}
at
p
was introduced as
\chi_{p}(\mathcal{F})=\left(\sum_{q\in\mathcal{I}_{p}(\mathcal{F})}\nu_{q}(\mathcal{F})\xi_{q}(\mathcal{F})\right)-\xi_{p}(\mathcal{F})
, where
\mathcal{I}_{p}(\mathcal{F})
denotes the set of infinitely near points of
\mathcal{F}
at
p
(see
[
16
, p. 7]
),
\nu_{q}(\mathcal{F})
is the algebraic multiplicity of the strict transform of
\mathcal{F}
passing by
q
and
\xi_{p}(\mathcal{F})
is the tangency excess of the foliation
\mathcal{F}
(see
[
16
, Definition 2.3]
).
By
[
16
, Proposition 4.7]
we get

(14)

\mu_{p}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{B})=\mu_{p}(\mathcal{F})-\chi_{p}(\mathcal{F}).

Since
\chi_{p}(\mathcal{F})
is a nonnegative number (see
[
16
, Proposition 3.1]
) we have

(15)

\mu_{p}(\mathcal{F})\geq\mu_{p}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{B}).

Observe that, if
\mathcal{B}
is a primitive balanced divisor of separatrices for
\mathcal{F}
at
p
and
\mu_{p}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{B})=\mu_{p}(\mathcal{F})
then, by
[
16
, Proposition 3.1]
),

\mathcal{F}
is of second type or has algebraic multiplicity equals one. Moreover, if
\mathcal{F}
is of second type and
\mathcal{B}
is a primitive balanced divisor of separatrices of
\mathcal{F}
then
\mu_{p}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{B})=\mu_{p}(\mathcal{F})
.

The following proposition generalizes
[
16

.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a germ of a singular foliation at
(\mathbb{C}^{2},p)
. Let
\mathcal{B}=\mathcal{B}_{0}-\mathcal{B}_{\infty}
be a primitive balanced divisor of separatrices for
\mathcal{F}
at

\mu_{p}(\mathcal{F})-\tau_{p}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{B}_{0})=\mu_{p}(\mathcal{B}_{0})-\tau_{p}(\mathcal{B}_{0})-\mu_{p}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{B}_{\infty})+\chi_{p}(\mathcal{F})+1.

Moreover,
\mu_{p}(\mathcal{F})=\tau_{p}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{B}_{0})
if and only if

\mu_{p}(\mathcal{B}_{0})-\tau_{p}(\mathcal{B}_{0})=\mu_{p}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{B}_{\infty})-\chi_{p}(\mathcal{F})-1.

In particular, if
\mathcal{B}
is an effective divisor then
\mu_{p}(\mathcal{F})=\tau_{p}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{B}_{0})
if and only if
\mu_{p}(\mathcal{B}_{0})=\tau_{p}(\mathcal{B}_{0})

\chi_{p}(\mathcal{F})=0

Proof.

It follows from (
14
) and (
13
) that
\mu_{p}(\mathcal{F})=\mu_{p}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{B}_{0})-\mu_{p}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{B}_{\infty})+\chi_{p}(\mathcal{F})+1
. The proof follows by applying Proposition
4.1
to
\mathcal{B}_{0}
, and substituing into the last equation.
Moreover,
\mu_{p}(\mathcal{F})=\tau_{p}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{B}_{0})
if and only if

\mu_{p}(\mathcal{B}_{0})-\tau_{p}(\mathcal{B}_{0})=\mu_{p}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{B}_{\infty})-\chi_{p}(\mathcal{F})-1.

If
\mathcal{B}
is effective, then the last part of the proposition follows since
\mu_{p}(\mathcal{B}_{0})-\tau_{p}(\mathcal{B}_{0})
and
\chi_{p}(\mathcal{F})
are nonnegative integers.
∎

7.
Applications to global foliations

In this section, we now work with global foliations on the complex projective plane
\mathbb{P}^{2}_{\mathbb{C}}
. Let
\mathcal{F}
be a holomorphic foliation on
\mathbb{P}^{2}_{\mathbb{C}}
. The number of points of tangency counted with multiplicities, between
\mathcal{F}
and a non-invariant
L\subset\mathbb{P}^{2}_{\mathbb{C}}
is the
degree
of the foliation, denoted by
d
.

A holomorphic foliation
\mathcal{F}
on
\mathbb{P}^{2}_{\mathbb{C}}
of degree

, is a homogeneous
1-
form
\omega=A(x,y,z)dx+B(x,y,z)dy+C(x,y,z)dz
with
A,B
and
C
homogeneous polynomials of degree

7.1.
New proof of a global result of Cerveau-Lins Neto

In
[
11
, p. 885]
Cerveau and Lins Neto stablished the following propostion. The main ingredients in their proof are the reduction of singularities of foliations and the Poincaré-Hopf formula. Here, we provide an alternative proof using our previous results and some known local formulas.

Proposition 7.1
.

(Cerveau-Lins Neto
[
11
]
)
Let
C
be an irreducible curve on
\mathbb{P}^{2}_{\mathbb{C}}
of degree
d_{0}
and
\mathcal{F}
be a holomorphic foliation of degree
d
having
C
as a separatrix. Then

2-2g(C)=\sum_{p}\sum_{i}\mu_{p}(\mathcal{F},C_{i})-d_{0}(d-1),

where
g(C)
is the geometric genus of
C
and the sum is taken over all local branches
C_{i}
of
C
passing through the singularities
p
of
\mathcal{F}
in
C
.

Proof.

For each singularity
p
of
\mathcal{F}
such that
p\in C
, we have by (
7
),

\mu_{p}(\mathcal{F},C)=GSV_{p}(\mathcal{F},C)+\mu_{p}(C).

Therefore, by (
5
)

(17)

\sum_{p}\sum_{i}\mu_{p}(\mathcal{F},C_{i})-\sum_{p}(r_{p}-1)=\sum_{p}GSV_{p}(\mathcal{F},C)+\sum_{p}\mu_{p}(C),

where
r_{p}
is the number of branches at
p
.
Since
C
is irreducible, the geometric genus formula gives

(18)

g(C)=(d_{0}-1)(d_{0}-2)/2-\sum_{p}\delta_{p},

where
\delta_{p}=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\widetilde{\mathcal{O}_{p}}/\mathcal{O}_{p}
is the colength of the local ring
\mathcal{O}_{p}
of
(C,p)
in its normalization. Now, it follows from the
Milnor formula

[
31
, Theorem 10.5]
that

(19)

\mu_{p}(C)=2\delta_{p}-r_{p}+1.

The proof follows by combining (
18
), (
19
), and the GSV-index formula
[
7
, Proposition 2.3]

\sum_{p}GSV_{p}(\mathcal{F},C)=(d+2)d_{0}-d_{0}^{2}

and then substituting these expressions into (
17
).
∎

7.2.
New proof of a global result of Soares

In
[
35
]
, Soares studied the problem of bounding the degree of an algebraic curve
C
invariant for a foliation
\mathcal{F}
on
\mathbb{P}^{2}_{\mathbb{C}}
in terms of the degree of
\mathcal{F}
, in order to give an answer to the

Poincaré Problem
. For a recent account of this problem, we refer to
[
13
]
and the references therein. For a local version of the Poincaré problem, see
[
20
]
.

Before stating the main result of this subsection, we need the following remark.

Remark 7.2
.

We note that
\tau_{p}(\mathcal{F},C)\geq 1
for all singularity
p
of
\mathcal{F}
. Indeed, there would exist a point
q\in C
which is a singularity of
\mathcal{F}
with
\tau_{q}(\mathcal{F},C)=0
, then we can take local coordinates centered at
q
such that
q=(0,0)
,
\mathcal{F}
is given by a 1-form
\omega_{q}=P(x,y)dx+Q(x,y)dy
and
C=\{f(x,y)=0\}
. Since
\tau_{q}(\mathcal{F},C)=0
, it follows that
1\in(P,Q,f)
so there exist
g,h,k\in\mathbb{C}\{x,y\}
such that

(20)

1=g\cdot P+h\cdot Q+k\cdot f.

However, since
q\in C
is a singularity of
\mathcal{F}
, we have
P(0,0)=Q(0,0)=f(0,0)=0
. Substituing these values into the equation (
20
) leads to a contradiction.

M. Soares proved the following theorem using polar varieties and characteristic classes. Here, we present a simpler proof based on the indices studied above. Recently, in
[
14
, Theorem 4]
, the author presents a more general version of Soares’s theorem in higher dimensions.

Theorem 7.3
.

(Soares
[
35
, Theorem II]
)
Let
C
be an irreducible curve of degree
d_{0}>1
that is invariant by a foliation
\mathcal{F}
on
\mathbb{P}^{2}_{\mathbb{C}}
of degree
d\geq 2
with isolated singularities. Then

d_{0}(d_{0}-1)-\sum_{p}(\mu_{p}(C)-1)\leq(d+1)d_{0},

where the sum runs over all singularities
p
of
C
.

Proof.

For each singularity
p
of
\mathcal{F}
such that
p\in C
, we have, by (
7
),

(21)

\mu_{p}(C)=\mu_{p}(\mathcal{F},C)-GSV_{p}(\mathcal{F},C).

It follows from (
8
) that
\mu_{p}(\mathcal{F},C)\geq\tau_{p}(\mathcal{F},C)
, since
\mu_{p}(C)\geq\tau_{p}(C)
, and therefore
\mu_{p}(\mathcal{F},C)\geq 1
by Remark
7.2
. In particular, from (
21
), we obtain

(22)

\displaystyle\mu_{p}(C)-1\geq-GSV_{p}(\mathcal{F},C).

The proof follows by summing over all singularities of
\mathcal{F}
in
C
and using the GSV-index formula
[
7
, Proposition 2.3]

\sum_{p}GSV_{p}(\mathcal{F},C)=(d+2)d_{0}-d_{0}^{2}
,
and then substituting this equality into (
22
).
∎

7.3.
Bounds of the global Tjurina number of a foliation

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a holomorphic foliation on
\mathbb{P}^{2}_{\mathbb{C}}
of degree
d
, having an invariant reduced algebraic curve
C
of degree
d_{0}
. We define the global Tjurina number of
\mathcal{F}
with respect to
C
as
\tau(\mathcal{F},C):=\sum_{p}\tau_{p}(\mathcal{F},C)
, which is the sum of the Tjurina number of
\mathcal{F}
at all singularities
p
of
\mathcal{F}
on
C
. Similarly, we denote
\mu(C):=\sum_{p}\mu_{p}(C)
, the
global Milnor number
of
C
, and
\tau(C):=\sum_{p}\tau_{p}(C)
, the
global Tjurina number
of
C
.

Remark 7.4
.

Teissier’s Proposition
[
37
, Chapter II, Proposition 1.2]
implies that

(23)

i_{p}(\mathcal{P}^{df},C)=\mu_{p}(C)+\nu_{p}(C)-1,

and by
[
26
, Theorem 4.18]
we get

(24)

i_{p}(\mathcal{P}^{df},C)\geq\nu_{p}(df)\cdot\nu_{p}(C)=(\nu_{p}(C)-1)\cdot\nu_{p}(C).

Using (
23
) and (
24
) we obtain
\mu_{p}(C)\geq(\nu_{p}(C)-1)^{2}.

Here
i_{p}(\mathcal{P}^{df},C)
denotes the polar intersection number of
df
and
C
at
p
(see for instance
[
16
, Section 4]
).

Now, we present bounds for the global Tjurina number of a foliation on
\mathbb{P}^{2}_{\mathbb{C}}
.

Theorem 7.5
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a holomorphic foliation on
\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
of degree
d\geq 2
having an invariant irreducible algebraic curve
C
of degree
d_{0}\geq 2
. Then

\tau(\mathcal{F},C)=d_{0}(d-1)-2g(C)-\sum_{p}(r_{p}-1)-\mu(C)+\tau(C)+2,

where
g(C)
is the geometric genus of
C
and
r_{p}
is the number of branches of
C
at
p
.
Moreover,

\tau(\mathcal{F},C)\leq d_{0}(d_{0}-3)+d(d+1)-2g(C)-\sum_{p}(r_{p}-1)-\sum_{p}(\nu_{p}(C)-1)^{2}+3,

and if
C
is not concurrent lines, then

2-2g(C)+\left[\frac{d_{0}}{2}\right]+d-d_{0}-\sum_{p}(r_{p}-1)\leq\tau(\mathcal{F},C).

Proof.

For each singularity
p
of
\mathcal{F}
such that
p\in C
, we have

\tau_{p}(\mathcal{F},C)=\mu_{p}(\mathcal{F},C)-\mu_{p}(C)+\tau_{p}(C)

by Proposition
4.1
. Then, using the definition of
\tau(\mathcal{F},C)
, the equation (
5
) and Proposition
7.1
, we get

(25)

\displaystyle\tau(\mathcal{F},C)=\sum_{p}\tau_{p}(\mathcal{F},C)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{p}\mu_{p}(\mathcal{F},C)-\mu(C)+\tau(C)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{p}\left(\sum_{i}\mu_{p}(\mathcal{F},C_{i})-r_{p}+1\right)-\mu(C)+\tau(C)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{p}\sum_{i}\mu_{p}(\mathcal{F},C_{i})-\sum_{p}(r_{p}-1)-\mu(C)+\tau(C)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle d_{0}(d-1)+2-2g(C)-\sum_{p}(r_{p}-1)-\mu(C)+\tau(C).

Moreover, by
[
34
, Corollary 3.4]
, if
C
is not concurrent lines, we have

(26)

\mu(C)\leq(d_{0}-1)^{2}-\left[\frac{d_{0}}{2}\right].

Furthermore, it follows from
[
15
, Theorem 3.2]
that

(27)

(d_{0}-d-1)(d_{0}-1)\leq\tau(C)\leq(d_{0}-1)^{2}-d(d_{0}-d-1).

Finally, the theorem follows by substituing (
26
), (
27
) and Remark
7.4
into (
25
).
∎

7.4.
Alcántara–Mozo-Fernández’s conjecture

Now, we address the following conjecture posed by Alcántara–Mozo-Fernández
[
1
, p. 16]
:

Does there exist a logarithmic, non-dicritical, and quasihomogeneous foliation
\mathcal{F}
on
\mathbb{P}^{2}_{\mathbb{C}}
, with a unique singular point
p
, having an invariant reduced algebraic curve
C
passing through
p
?

The authors indicate that they have some evidence suggesting that this type of foliation does not exist; however, they state that they do not yet have a solution to the problem.
We recall that a non-dicritical foliation
\mathcal{F}
is
quasi-homogeneous
at
p
, if the set of all separatrices of
\mathcal{F}
passing by
p
is a germ of curve given in some coordinates by a quasi-homogeneous polynomial, see
[
22
, p. 1657]
.

We propose the following answer in the case where
C
is irreducible. Note that in this situation it is not necessarily assume that the foliation is quasi-homogeneous.

Theorem 7.6
.

There does not exist a logarithmic, non-dicritical foliation
\mathcal{F}
on
\mathbb{P}^{2}_{\mathbb{C}}
of degree
d
, with a unique singular point
p
, having an invariant irreducible algebraic curve
C
of degree
d_{0}\geq 2
passing through
p
.

Proof.

Suppose, for contradiction, that there exists a logarithmic, non-dicritical foliation
\mathcal{F}
on
\mathbb{P}^{2}_{\mathbb{C}}
of degree
d
, with a unique singular point
p
, having an invariant irreducible algebraic curve
C
of degree
d_{0}\geq 2
passing through
p
.
Since
\mathcal{F}
is logarithmic, it follows that
\mathcal{F}
is a generalized curve foliation at
p
and
d_{0}=d+2
(see
[
18
, Section 6]
). Moreover, since
\mathcal{F}
is non-dicritical at
p
, we have

(28)

\mu_{p}(C)=\mu_{p}(\mathcal{F})=d^{2}+d+1

by
[
9
]
and
[
28
]
. Now, since
C
is irreducible, we can apply Płoski’s inequality
[
32
, Lemma 2.2]
to
C
at
p
, obtaining

(29)

\mu_{p}(C)\leq(d_{0}-1)(d_{0}-2).

Combining (
28
), (
29
), and using
d=d_{0}-2
, we get

d^{2}+d+1=d_{0}^{2}-3d_{0}+3\leq d_{0}^{2}-3d_{0}+2,

implying
1\leq 0
, which is a contradiction. This completes the proof.
∎

To continue, we present an example that answer the conjecture posed by Alcántara–Mozo-Fernández.

Example 7.7
.

Let
\zeta,\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\in\mathbb{C}^{*}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
with
n\geq 2
such that
\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}(n+1)=0
. Consider the foliation
\mathcal{F}
in homogeneous coordinates
[x:y:z]\in\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
given by

\Omega_{\zeta,\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}}=-\zeta(n+1)\lambda_{2}yx^{n}dx+((\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}n)zy^{n}-\zeta\lambda_{1}x^{n+1})dy+\lambda_{2}y^{n+1}dz.

Then we have the following properties:

(1)

\mathcal{F}
is a non-dicritical logarithmic foliation on
\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
has degree
n
with a unique singularity
[0:0:1]
.

(2)

The curve
C=\{y(y^{n}z-\zeta\cdot x^{n+1})=0\}\subset\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
is quasi-homogeneous,
\mathcal{F}
-invariant and
[0:0:1]\in C
.

In fact, let
A(x,y,z)=-\zeta(n+1)\lambda_{2}yx^{n}
,
B(x,y,z)=(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}n)zy^{n}-\zeta\lambda_{1}x^{n+1}
, and
C(x,y,z)=\lambda_{2}y^{n+1}
. Therefore,

\Omega_{\zeta,\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}}=Adx+Bdy+Cdz\quad\text{and}\quad Ax+By+Cz=0.

Since the degree of
A
,
B
and
C
is
n+1
, we get
\Omega_{\zeta,\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}}
defines a foliation
\mathcal{F}
of
\mathbb{P}^{2}_{\mathbb{C}}
of degree
n
.
On the other hand, a direct calculation shows

(30)

\Omega_{\zeta,\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}}=\lambda_{1}\frac{dy}{y}+\lambda_{2}\frac{d(y^{n}z-\zeta\cdot x^{n+1})}{y^{n}z-\zeta\cdot x^{n+1}}.

Since
\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}(n+1)=0
, we deduce that
\mathcal{F}
is a logarithmic foliation. Analyzing the chart
z=1
of
\mathbb{P}^{2}_{\mathbb{C}}
, we have
\mathcal{F}
is given by

\omega=-\zeta(n+1)\lambda_{2}yx^{n}dx+((\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}n)y^{n}-\zeta\lambda_{1}x^{n+1})dy.

We get
p=[0:0:1]
is a singularity of
\mathcal{F}
with
\mu_{p}(\mathcal{F})=n^{2}+n+1
, implying that
p
is the unique singularity of
\mathcal{F}
. By (
30
), we obtain that
C
is
\mathcal{F}
-invariant, moreover the germ of
C
at
p
is a quasi-homogeneous curve with
\mu_{p}(C)=n^{2}+n+1=\mu_{p}(\mathcal{F})
.
Finally, to prove that
p
is a non-dicritical singularity of
\mathcal{F}
is suffices to find the a local expression to the strict transform of
\mathcal{F}
by a blow-up at
p
. Of course, taking local coordinates
\pi(x,t)=(x,tx)=(x,y)
of the blow-up of
\mathbb{C}^{2}
, we get

\tilde{\omega}=\frac{\pi^{*}(\omega)}{x^{n}}=(-\zeta(n+1)\lambda_{2}xt+t((\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}n)t^{n}-\zeta\lambda_{1}x))dx+x((\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}n)t^{n}-\zeta\lambda_{1}x)dt

The strict transform
\tilde{\mathcal{F}}
of
\mathcal{F}
by
\pi
is defined by
\tilde{\omega}
and since the exceptional divisor
\{x=0\}
is
\tilde{\mathcal{F}}
-invariant, we conclude that
p
is a non-dicritical singularity of
\mathcal{F}
. These verify the above items.

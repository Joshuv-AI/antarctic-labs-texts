Phase-Induced Particle Creation in the Kappa-Gamma Vacuum
Arash Azizi

Abstract
We introduce a two-parameter ( κ , γ \kappa,\gamma ) family of field modes in flat spacetime that generalizes the recently developed κ \kappa -plane wave modes, which correspond to real squeezing. By incorporating a phase parameter, γ \gamma , our new κ ​ γ \kappa\gamma plane wave modes describe a vacuum state, | 0 κ , γ ⟩ \ket{0_{\kappa,\gamma}} , characterized by continuous-mode phase squeezing, where κ \kappa governs the squeezing magnitude and γ \gamma sets its angle. This generalization reveals a novel physical effect: particle creation induced purely by a relative phase shift between observers. The particle spectrum is shown to be a function of this phase difference and the deformation parameter κ \kappa . This work provides a comprehensive framework for exploring a new class of non-trivial quantum vacua, where both thermal and squeezing properties can be continuously adjusted.

2
Kappa-Gamma Plane Wave Modes

We consider a massless scalar field in (1+1)-dimensional Minkowski spacetime using light-cone coordinates
u=t-x
. We begin by constructing a general field mode as a linear combination of plane waves, initially allowing the frequency
\Omega
to span the real line
\mathbb{R}
:

\Phi(u,\Omega)=\alpha(\Omega)e^{-{{\rm i\,}}\Omega u}+\beta(\Omega)e^{{{\rm i\,}}\Omega u}\,.

(2)

The coefficients
\alpha(\Omega)
and
\beta(\Omega)
are constrained by the orthonormality conditions imposed by the Klein-Gordon inner product.

2.1
Inner Product Constraints and Frequency Restriction

The standard inner product for a scalar field,
\langle\phi_{1},\phi_{2}\rangle=i\int(\phi_{1}^{*}\partial_{t}\phi_{2}-\phi_{2}\partial_{t}\phi_{1}^{*})dx
, gives the following relation for plane waves:

\left\langle e^{-{{\rm i\,}}\Omega u},e^{-{{\rm i\,}}\Omega^{\prime}u}\right\rangle=4\pi\Omega\,\delta(\Omega-\Omega^{\prime})\,.

(3)

We demand that our modes form an orthonormal set, satisfying:

\langle\Phi(u,\Omega),\Phi(u,\Omega^{\prime})\rangle=\delta(\Omega-\Omega^{\prime})\quad\text{and}\quad\langle\Phi(u,\Omega),\Phi^{*}(u,\Omega^{\prime})\rangle=0.

(4)

Applying the first condition to our mode in Eq. (
2
) yields:

\displaystyle\langle\Phi(u,\Omega),\Phi(u,\Omega^{\prime})\rangle

\displaystyle=4\pi\Omega\delta(\Omega-\Omega^{\prime})\left(|\alpha(\Omega)|^{2}-|\beta(\Omega)|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+4\pi\Omega\delta(\Omega+\Omega^{\prime})\Big(\alpha^{*}(\Omega)\beta(-\Omega)-\beta^{*}(\Omega)\alpha(-\Omega)\Big).

(5)

For this to equal
\delta(\Omega-\Omega^{\prime})
, we must satisfy two separate constraints:

\displaystyle|\alpha(\Omega)|^{2}-|\beta(\Omega)|^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4\pi\Omega}\,,

(6)

\displaystyle\alpha^{*}(\Omega)\beta(-\Omega)-\beta^{*}(\Omega)\alpha(-\Omega)

\displaystyle=0\,.

(7)

The second condition,
\langle\Phi(u,\Omega),\Phi^{*}(u,\Omega^{\prime})\rangle=0
, gives a third constraint:

-\alpha(\Omega)\alpha(-\Omega)+\beta(\Omega)\beta(-\Omega)=0\,.

(8)

However, constraints (
7
) and (
8
) lead to a contradiction. As shown in the analysis of
\kappa
-plane waves, satisfying them simultaneously requires
|\alpha(\Omega)|^{2}-|\beta(\Omega)|^{2}=0
, which violates the fundamental normalization condition (
6
).

This inconsistency is resolved by restricting the mode frequency to be strictly positive, which we denote by
\Lambda>0
. In this regime, the term
\delta(\Lambda+\Lambda^{\prime})
is always zero, automatically satisfying the conditions derived from it. We are left with only the essential normalization constraint for
\Lambda>0
:

|\alpha(\Lambda)|^{2}-|\beta(\Lambda)|^{2}=\frac{1}{4\pi\Lambda}\,.

(9)

2.2
The Kappa-Gamma Ansatz and Field Expansion

To satisfy Eq. (
9
) while introducing a physically meaningful structure, we propose the following ansatz for the complex coefficients
\alpha(\Lambda)
and
\beta(\Lambda)
, parameterized by a deformation constant
\kappa
and a phase
\gamma
:

\alpha(\Lambda,\kappa,\gamma)=\frac{e^{\frac{\pi\Lambda}{2\kappa}}e^{i\gamma}}{\sqrt{{\cal N}_{\Lambda,\kappa}}}\,,\qquad\qquad\beta(\Lambda,\kappa,\gamma)=\frac{e^{-\frac{\pi\Lambda}{2\kappa}}e^{-i\gamma}}{\sqrt{{\cal N}_{\Lambda,\kappa}}}\,,

(10)

where the normalization factor is
{\cal N}_{\Lambda,\kappa}=8\pi\Lambda\,\sinh(\frac{\pi\Lambda}{\kappa})
. This specific form is chosen because it ensures
\kappa
is a frequency-independent constant and, crucially, establishes the resulting vacuum as a continuous-mode squeezed state.

With this ansatz, the mode takes the form of a
\kappa\gamma
plane wave mode
:

\boxed{\quad\Phi(u,\Lambda,\kappa,\gamma)=\frac{1}{{\sqrt{{\cal N}_{\Lambda,\kappa}}}}\Bigg\{e^{\frac{\pi\Lambda}{2\kappa}}e^{i\gamma}e^{-{{\rm i\,}}\Lambda u}+e^{-\frac{\pi\Lambda}{2\kappa}}e^{-i\gamma}e^{{{\rm i\,}}\Lambda u}\Bigg\}\,.\quad}

(11)

The quantum field
\Phi(u)
can then be expanded in this basis:

\Phi(u)=\int_{0}^{\infty}d\Lambda\Big(\Phi(u,\Lambda,\kappa,\gamma){\cal A}_{\Lambda,\kappa,\gamma}+\Phi^{*}(u,\Lambda,\kappa,\gamma){\cal A}^{\dagger}_{\Lambda,\kappa,\gamma}\Big)\,,

(12)

where
{\cal A}_{\Lambda,\kappa,\gamma}
is the annihilation operator for a
\kappa\gamma
mode, and the vacuum is defined by
{\cal A}_{\Lambda,\kappa,\gamma}\ket{0_{\kappa,\gamma}}=0
for all
\Lambda>0
.

In the limit
\kappa\to 0
, the mode simplifies to:

\Phi(u,\Lambda,\kappa\rightarrow 0,\gamma)=\frac{e^{i\gamma}}{\sqrt{4\pi\Lambda}}e^{-{{\rm i\,}}\Lambda u}\,.

(13)

This is a standard Minkowski plane wave up to a global phase factor. The corresponding annihilation operator is
{\cal A}_{\Lambda,0,\gamma}=e^{-i\gamma}a_{\Lambda}
, where
a_{\Lambda}
is the standard Minkowski operator. As we will see, this phase becomes physically significant when comparing frames with different values of
\gamma
.

3
Phase-Induced Particle Creation

A remarkable consequence of introducing the
\gamma
parameter is that a mere shift in phase induces a non-trivial Bogoliubov transformation, leading to particle creation. To see this, we consider two sets of operators,
({\cal A}_{\Lambda,\kappa,\gamma},{\cal A}^{\dagger}_{\Lambda,\kappa,\gamma})
and
({\cal A}_{\Lambda,\kappa,\gamma^{\prime}},{\cal A}^{\dagger}_{\Lambda,\kappa,\gamma^{\prime}})
, corresponding to the same deformation parameter
\kappa
but different phases
\gamma
and
\gamma^{\prime}
.

The relationship between these two bases is a special case of the general Bogoliubov transformations derived in Section
4
. By setting
\kappa^{\prime}=\kappa
in the general formulas, we find the transformation:

\boxed{\quad{\cal A}_{\Lambda,\kappa,\gamma^{\prime}}=\Big[\cos(\gamma' - \gamma)-i\coth\Big(\frac{\pi\Lambda}{\kappa}\Big)\sin(\gamma' - \gamma)\Big]{\cal A}_{\Lambda,\kappa,\gamma}-i\frac{\sin(\gamma' - \gamma)}{\sinh(\frac{\pi\Lambda}{\kappa})}{\cal A}^{\dagger}_{\Lambda,\kappa,\gamma}\quad}

(14)

This transformation mixes annihilation and creation operators, which proves that the vacua
\ket{0_{\kappa,\gamma}}
and
\ket{0_{\kappa,\gamma'}}
are physically inequivalent.

An observer associated with the
(\kappa,\gamma)
vacuum will perceive the
(\kappa,\gamma^{\prime})
vacuum as populated by particles. The particle number density is given by the modulus-squared of the Bogoliubov
\beta
coefficient (the coefficient of
{\cal A}^{\dagger}_{\Lambda,\kappa,\gamma}
):

\boxed{N_{\Lambda}(\gamma^{\prime},\gamma)=|\beta_{\Lambda\gamma\gamma^{\prime}}|^{2}=\frac{\sin^{2}(\gamma^{\prime}-\gamma)}{\sinh^{2}(\frac{\pi\Lambda}{\kappa})}}

(15)

This striking result demonstrates that particle creation can be induced purely by a relative phase shift between two observers’ reference frames. The effect is most pronounced for small
\kappa
and low frequencies
\Lambda
, and it vanishes when the phases align (
\gamma^{\prime}=\gamma
), confirming that the phase
\gamma
is a physically significant parameter.

5
Discussion: Continuous Phase Squeezing and Mode Structure

The Bogoliubov transformation (
21
) provides deep insight into the nature of the
\ket{0_{\kappa,\gamma}}
vacuum. While the
\kappa
-plane wave vacuum (the
\gamma=0
case) was shown to be a standard squeezed state, the inclusion of the phase
\gamma
generalizes this to a
phase-squeezed state
, adding a new tunable dimension to the vacuum structure.

The vacuum is defined by the condition
{\cal A}_{\Lambda,\kappa,\gamma}\ket{0_{\kappa,\gamma}}=0
for all
\Lambda>0
. Using Eq. (
21
), this translates into a condition on the Minkowski operators:

\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\sinh(\frac{\pi\Lambda}{\kappa})}}\left(e^{\frac{\pi\Lambda}{2\kappa}}e^{-i\gamma}a_{\Lambda}-e^{-\frac{\pi\Lambda}{2\kappa}}e^{i\gamma}a^{\dagger}_{\Lambda}\right)\ket{0_{\kappa,\gamma}}=0\,.

(24)

Rearranging this gives the defining relation for the
\kappa\gamma
vacuum for each frequency mode
\nu
:

\boxed{\left(a_{\nu}-e^{-\frac{\pi\nu}{\kappa}}e^{2i\gamma}a^{\dagger}_{\nu}\right)\ket{0_{\kappa,\gamma}}=0}

(25)

This is the defining equation for a squeezed vacuum state where the squeezing parameter is now
complex
. Such states are known in quantum optics as phase-squeezed states. The complex parameter
\alpha(\nu,\gamma)=e^{-\frac{\pi\nu}{\kappa}}e^{2i\gamma}
controls both the magnitude and phase of the squeezing.

Squeezing Parameters

The state
\ket{0_{\kappa,\gamma}}
can be constructed by applying a unitary squeezing operator
S(\xi)
to the Minkowski vacuum
\ket{0_M}
. A general squeezed state is described by a complex parameter
\xi=re^{i\phi}
, where the state satisfies
a\ket{\psi}=\tanh(r)e^{i\phi}a^{\dagger}\ket{\psi}
. By comparing this with our condition (
25
), we can directly identify the physical roles of
\kappa
and
\gamma
. The squeezing magnitude is determined by
\kappa
via
\tanh r(\nu)=|e^{-\frac{\pi\nu}{\kappa}}e^{2i\gamma}|=e^{-\frac{\pi\nu}{\kappa}}
, while the phase
\gamma
directly controls the squeezing angle in phase space via
\phi(\gamma)=\arg(e^{-\frac{\pi\nu}{\kappa}}e^{2i\gamma})=2\gamma
. The complete squeezing parameter for the unitary operator
S(\xi)=\exp\{\frac{1}{2}(\xi(a^{\dagger})^{2}-\xi^{*}a^{2})\}
is therefore
\xi(\nu,\gamma)=-r(\nu)e^{i\phi(\gamma)}
.

The Squeezed Vacuum State

The
\kappa\gamma
vacuum can therefore be expressed as the action of a continuous-mode, phase-squeezing operator on the Minkowski vacuum. Using the relation
r(\nu)=\tanh^{-1}(e^{-\frac{\pi\nu}{\kappa}})=\frac{1}{2}\ln(\coth(\frac{\pi\nu}{2\kappa}))
, we can write the state as:

\displaystyle\ket{0_{\kappa,\gamma}}

\displaystyle=\exp\{\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}d\nu\,\left(\xi(\nu,\gamma)(a^{\dagger}_{\nu})^{2}-\xi^{*}(\nu,\gamma)(a_{\nu})^{2}\right)\Bigg\}\ket{0_M}

(26)

\displaystyle\text{where}\quad\xi(\nu,\gamma)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}e^{2i\gamma}\ln\left(\coth\left(\frac{\pi\nu}{2\kappa}\right)\right)\,.

This result firmly establishes the
\ket{0_{\kappa,\gamma}}
vacuum as a continuous-mode, phase-squeezed Gaussian state. It represents a significant generalization of the
\kappa
-vacuum: while
\kappa
controls the
amount
of squeezing for each frequency, the new parameter
\gamma
controls the
axis
of that squeezing in phase space.

,

Appendix B
Gamma Integrals

We use the standard Gamma function identity

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}u^{s-1}e^{-bu}\,\mathrm{d}u=b^{-s}\Gamma(s),

(27)

which holds for
b,s\in\mathbb{C}
with
\real(b)>0
. Therefore, the regulated integrals evaluate to

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\mathrm{d}u\,\theta(u)u^{i\Omega-1}e^{+i\Lambda u}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}u^{i\Omega-1}e^{+i\Lambda u}\,\mathrm{d}u=\Lambda^{-i\Omega}e^{-\frac{\pi\Omega}{2}}\Gamma(i\Omega),

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\mathrm{d}u\,\theta(u)u^{i\Omega-1}e^{-i\Lambda u}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}u^{i\Omega-1}e^{-i\Lambda u}\,\mathrm{d}u=\Lambda^{-i\Omega}e^{+\frac{\pi\Omega}{2}}\Gamma(i\Omega),

(28)

where we assume
\Lambda>0
. Identical results hold for the integrals over
\theta(-u)
.

Appendix C
Derivation:
\kappa\gamma
-Plane Wave in terms of
\kappa^{\prime}
-Rindler Operators

This appendix provides a detailed derivation of the Bogoliubov transformation that expresses the
\kappa\gamma
plane wave operators (
{\cal A}_{\Lambda,\kappa,\gamma}
) in terms of the
\kappa^{\prime}
-Rindler operators (
{\cal B}_{\Omega,\kappa^{\prime}}
).

Our strategy is as follows:

1.

Start with the identity
\Phi(u)=\Phi(u)
, where the field is expanded in the
\kappa\gamma
-plane wave basis on one side and the
\kappa^{\prime}
-Rindler basis on the other.

2.

Apply the Fourier transforms
\int du\,e^{\pm i\Lambda_{0}u}
to this identity to create a system of two algebraic equations.

3.

Solve this system for the operator
{\cal A}_{\Lambda_{0},\kappa,\gamma}
.

C.1
Fourier Projection of the Field Expansions

First, we project the
\kappa\gamma
plane wave expansion. Applying the Fourier transform to the field
\Phi(u)
for a fixed frequency
\Lambda_{0}>0
yields a direct relationship for its operators:

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}du\,e^{i\Lambda_{0}u}\Phi(u)

\displaystyle=\frac{2\pi}{\sqrt{{\cal N}_{\Lambda_{0},\kappa}}}\Bigg\{e^{\frac{\pi\Lambda_{0}}{2\kappa}+i\gamma}{\cal A}_{\Lambda_{0},\kappa,\gamma}+e^{-\frac{\pi\Lambda_{0}}{2\kappa}+i\gamma}{\cal A}^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{\Lambda_{0}},\kappa,\gamma}\Bigg\},

(29)

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}du\,e^{-i\Lambda_{0}u}\Phi(u)

\displaystyle=\frac{2\pi}{\sqrt{{\cal N}_{\Lambda_{0},\kappa}}}\Bigg\{e^{-\frac{\pi\Lambda_{0}}{2\kappa}-i\gamma}{\cal A}_{\Lambda_{0},\kappa,\gamma}+e^{\frac{\pi\Lambda_{0}}{2\kappa}-i\gamma}{\cal A}^{\dagger}_{\Lambda_{0},\kappa,\gamma}\Bigg\}.

(30)

Note: In Eq. (
29
), we have corrected a likely typo from the original draft, changing
{\cal A}^{\dagger}_{-\Lambda_{0},\kappa,\gamma}
to
{\cal A}^{\dagger}_{\Lambda_{0},\kappa,\gamma}
to match the positive frequency expansion.

Next, we apply the same Fourier transforms to the
\kappa^{\prime}
-Rindler expansion of
\Phi(u)
. This is more involved, requiring the Gamma function integrals detailed in Appendix
B
. Let’s define the result of this projection as
\mathcal{M}_{\Lambda_{0}}
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{\Lambda_{0}}\equiv\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}du\,e^{i\Lambda_{0}u}\,\Phi(u)\bigg|_{\text{Rindler basis}}

(31)

Explicitly,

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{\Lambda_{0}}=

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\frac{d\Omega\;i}{\sqrt{{\cal N}_{\Omega,\kappa^{\prime}}}}\Bigg\{\left(-\,e^{\frac{\pi\Omega}{2}}e^{\frac{\pi\Omega}{2\kappa^{\prime}}}+e^{-\frac{\pi\Omega}{2}}\,e^{-\frac{\pi\Omega}{2\kappa^{\prime}}}\right)\Lambda_{0}^{-(1+i\Omega)}\Gamma(1+i\Omega)\,\mathcal{B}_{\Omega,\kappa^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\phantom{\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\frac{d\Omega\;i}{\sqrt{{\cal N}_{\Omega,\kappa^{\prime}}}}\Bigg\{}+\left(-e^{-\frac{\pi\Omega}{2}}\,e^{\frac{\pi\Omega}{2\kappa^{\prime}}}-e^{\frac{\pi\Omega}{2}}\,e^{-\frac{\pi\Omega}{2\kappa^{\prime}}}\right)\Lambda_{0}^{-(1-i\Omega)}\Gamma(1-i\Omega)\,\mathcal{B}^{\dagger}_{\Omega,\kappa^{\prime}}\Bigg\}.

(32)

The projection with
e^{-i\Lambda_{0}u}
simply yields the conjugate,
\mathcal{M}^{\dagger}_{\Lambda_{0}}
.

C.2
System of Equations and Solution

By equating the results from projecting both bases, we obtain a system of two linear equations relating the operators of the two frames:

\displaystyle\frac{2\pi}{\sqrt{{\cal N}_{\Lambda_{0},\kappa}}}\Bigg\{e^{\frac{\pi\Lambda_{0}}{2\kappa}+i\gamma}{\cal A}_{\Lambda_{0},\kappa,\gamma}+e^{-\frac{\pi\Lambda_{0}}{2\kappa}+i\gamma}{\cal A}^{\dagger}_{\Lambda_{0},\kappa,\gamma}\Bigg\}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{M}_{\Lambda_{0}},

(33)

\displaystyle\frac{2\pi}{\sqrt{{\cal N}_{\Lambda_{0},\kappa}}}\Bigg\{e^{-\frac{\pi\Lambda_{0}}{2\kappa}-i\gamma}{\cal A}_{\Lambda_{0},\kappa,\gamma}+e^{\frac{\pi\Lambda_{0}}{2\kappa}-i\gamma}{\cal A}^{\dagger}_{\Lambda_{0},\kappa,\gamma}\Bigg\}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{M}^{\dagger}_{\Lambda_{0}}.

(34)

This can be conveniently written in matrix form:

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}e^{\frac{\pi\Lambda_{0}}{2\kappa}}e^{i\gamma}&e^{-\frac{\pi\Lambda_{0}}{2\kappa}}e^{i\gamma}\\
e^{-\frac{\pi\Lambda_{0}}{2\kappa}}e^{-i\gamma}&e^{\frac{\pi\Lambda_{0}}{2\kappa}}e^{-i\gamma}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}{\cal A}_{\Lambda_{0},\kappa,\gamma}\\
{\cal A}^{\dagger}_{\Lambda_{0},\kappa,\gamma}\end{pmatrix}=\frac{\sqrt{{\cal N}_{\Lambda_{0},\kappa}}}{2\pi}\begin{pmatrix}\mathcal{M}_{\Lambda_{0}}\\
\mathcal{M}^{\dagger}_{\Lambda_{0}}\end{pmatrix}

(35)

The determinant of the matrix on the left is
2\sinh(\frac{\pi\Lambda_0}{\kappa})
. We can now solve for
{\cal A}_{\Lambda_{0},\kappa,\gamma}
by inverting the matrix:

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}{\cal A}_{\Lambda_{0},\kappa,\gamma}\\
{\cal A}^{\dagger}_{\Lambda_{0},\kappa,\gamma}\end{pmatrix}=\frac{1}{2\sinh\left(\frac{\pi\Lambda_{0}}{\kappa}\right)}\begin{pmatrix}e^{\frac{\pi\Lambda_{0}}{2\kappa}}e^{-i\gamma}&-e^{-\frac{\pi\Lambda_{0}}{2\kappa}}e^{i\gamma}\\
-e^{-\frac{\pi\Lambda_{0}}{2\kappa}}e^{-i\gamma}&e^{\frac{\pi\Lambda_{0}}{2\kappa}}e^{i\gamma}\end{pmatrix}\frac{\sqrt{{\cal N}_{\Lambda_{0},\kappa}}}{2\pi}\begin{pmatrix}\mathcal{M}_{\Lambda_{0}}\\
\mathcal{M}^{\dagger}_{\Lambda_{0}}\end{pmatrix}.

(36)

Focusing on the top row gives the expression for the annihilation operator:

\displaystyle{\cal A}_{\Lambda_{0},\kappa,\gamma}=\frac{\sqrt{{\cal N}_{\Lambda_{0},\kappa}}}{4\pi\sinh(\frac{\pi\Lambda_0}{\kappa})}\left(e^{\frac{\pi\Lambda_{0}}{2\kappa}}e^{-i\gamma}\mathcal{M}_{\Lambda_{0}}-e^{-\frac{\pi\Lambda_{0}}{2\kappa}}e^{i\gamma}\mathcal{M}^{\dagger}_{\Lambda_{0}}\right).

(37)

C.3
Final Result

After substituting the full integral expression for
\mathcal{M}_{\Lambda_{0}}
and performing the algebraic simplification of the hyperbolic and exponential terms, we arrive at the final transformation. The result expresses the
\kappa\gamma
plane wave annihilation operator as an integral over the
\kappa^{\prime}
-Rindler operators:

\displaystyle{\cal A}_{\Lambda,\kappa,\gamma}=

\displaystyle-\frac{{{\rm i\,}}}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\Omega\,\frac{1}{\sqrt{\Lambda\Omega\sinh(\frac{\pi\Lambda}{\kappa})\sinh(\frac{\pi\Omega}{\kappa'})}}

(38)

\displaystyle\times\Bigg\{\Lambda^{-{{\rm i\,}}\Omega}\,\Gamma(1+{{\rm i\,}}\Omega)\Bigg[e^{\frac{\pi\Lambda}{2\kappa}-i\gamma}\sinh\left({\textstyle{\frac{\scriptstyle\pi\Omega}{\scriptstyle 2}}}\big(\frac{1}{\kappa^{\prime}}+1\big)\right)+e^{-\frac{\pi\Lambda}{2\kappa}+i\gamma}\sinh\left({\textstyle{\frac{\scriptstyle\pi\Omega}{\scriptstyle 2}}}\big(\frac{1}{\kappa^{\prime}}-1\big)\right)\Bigg]{\cal B}_{\Omega,\kappa^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\Lambda^{{{\rm i\,}}\Omega}\,\Gamma(1-{{\rm i\,}}\Omega)\Bigg[e^{\frac{\pi\Lambda}{2\kappa}-i\gamma}\sinh\left({\textstyle{\frac{\scriptstyle\pi\Omega}{\scriptstyle 2}}}\big(\frac{1}{\kappa^{\prime}}-1\big)\right)+e^{-\frac{\pi\Lambda}{2\kappa}+i\gamma}\sinh\left({\textstyle{\frac{\scriptstyle\pi\Omega}{\scriptstyle 2}}}\big(\frac{1}{\kappa^{\prime}}+1\big)\right)\Bigg]{\cal B}^{\dagger}_{\Omega,\kappa^{\prime}}\Bigg\}\,.

This is the first of the two “Mother of All Bogoliubov Transformations,” generalized to include the phase parameter
\gamma
.

Appendix D
Derivation:
\kappa^{\prime}
-Rindler in terms of
\kappa\gamma
-Plane Wave Operators

This appendix details the derivation of the Bogoliubov transformation that expresses the
\kappa
-Rindler operators
{\cal B}_{\Omega,\kappa^{\prime}}
in terms of the
\kappa\gamma
plane wave operators
{\cal A}_{\Lambda,\kappa,\gamma}
. The method involves projecting the quantum field
\Phi(u)
onto the appropriate basis functions for each wedge and solving the resulting system of equations.

D.1
Field Expansions

We begin by writing the massless scalar field
\Phi(u)
in both the
\kappa\gamma
plane wave and
\kappa^{\prime}
-Rindler bases.

The
\kappa\gamma
plane wave expansion is:

\displaystyle\Phi(u)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{d\Lambda}{\sqrt{8\pi\Lambda\sinh(\frac{\pi\Lambda}{\kappa})}}\bigg\{

\displaystyle\Big(e^{\frac{\pi\Lambda}{2\kappa}}e^{i\gamma}e^{-i\Lambda u}+e^{-\frac{\pi\Lambda}{2\kappa}}e^{-i\gamma}e^{i\Lambda u}\Big){\cal A}_{\Lambda,\kappa,\gamma}

\displaystyle+\Big(e^{\frac{\pi\Lambda}{2\kappa}}e^{-i\gamma}e^{i\Lambda u}+e^{-\frac{\pi\Lambda}{2\kappa}}e^{i\gamma}e^{-i\Lambda u}\Big){\cal A}^{\dagger}_{\Lambda,\kappa,\gamma}\bigg\}\,.

(39)

The
\kappa^{\prime}
-Rindler expansion is defined piecewise across the right (
u<0
) and left (
u>0
) Rindler wedges:

\displaystyle\Phi(u)=

\displaystyle\theta(-u)\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{d\Omega^{\prime}}{\sqrt{8\pi\Omega^{\prime}\sinh(\frac{\pi\Omega'}{\kappa'})}}\Big\{(-u)^{i\Omega^{\prime}}e^{\frac{\pi\Omega^{\prime}}{2\kappa^{\prime}}}{\cal B}_{\Omega^{\prime},\kappa^{\prime}}+\text{h.c.}\Big\}

\displaystyle+\theta(u)\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{d\Omega^{\prime}}{\sqrt{8\pi\Omega^{\prime}\sinh(\frac{\pi\Omega'}{\kappa'})}}\Big\{u^{i\Omega^{\prime}}e^{-\frac{\pi\Omega^{\prime}}{2\kappa^{\prime}}}{\cal B}_{\Omega^{\prime},\kappa^{\prime}}+\text{h.c.}\Big\}\,.

(40)

Note that h.c. stands for the Hermitian conjugate of the preceding term.

D.2
Projection onto Rindler Modes

To isolate the
\kappa^{\prime}
-Rindler operators, we project
\Phi(u)
using weighted integrals that act as inverse Mellin transforms for each wedge. This provides a system of two equations for the two unknowns,
{\cal B}_{\Omega,\kappa^{\prime}}
and
{\cal B}^{\dagger}_{\Omega,\kappa^{\prime}}
. We then find two distinct equations:

1.

Projection onto the Right Wedge (
u<0
)

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{0}du\,(-u)^{i\Omega-1}\Phi(u)=\frac{2\pi}{\sqrt{8\pi\Omega\sinh(\frac{\pi\Omega}{\kappa'})}}\left(e^{-\frac{\pi\Omega}{2\kappa^{\prime}}}{\cal B}_{-\Omega,\kappa^{\prime}}+e^{\frac{\pi\Omega}{2\kappa^{\prime}}}{\cal B}^{\dagger}_{\Omega,\kappa^{\prime}}\right)\,.

(41)

2.

Projection onto the Left Wedge (
u>0
)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}du\,u^{i\Omega-1}\Phi(u)=\frac{2\pi}{\sqrt{8\pi\Omega\sinh(\frac{\pi\Omega}{\kappa'})}}\left(e^{\frac{\pi\Omega}{2\kappa^{\prime}}}{\cal B}_{-\Omega,\kappa^{\prime}}+e^{-\frac{\pi\Omega}{2\kappa^{\prime}}}{\cal B}^{\dagger}_{-\Omega,\kappa^{\prime}}\right)\,.

(42)

D.3
Solving the System of Equations

We now have a system of two linear equations. We can write the system in matrix form. Let the right-hand side of the projections onto the
\kappa\gamma
basis be defined as
\int d\Lambda(M_{\Omega\Lambda}{\cal A}_{\Lambda,\kappa,\gamma}+N_{\Omega\Lambda}{\cal A}^{\dagger}_{\Lambda,\kappa,\gamma})
and
\int d\Lambda(K_{\Omega\Lambda}{\cal A}_{\Lambda,\kappa,\gamma}+P_{\Omega\Lambda}{\cal A}^{\dagger}_{\Lambda,\kappa,\gamma})
respectively.

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}e^{\frac{\pi\Omega}{2\kappa^{\prime}}}&e^{-\frac{\pi\Omega}{2\kappa^{\prime}}}\\
e^{-\frac{\pi\Omega}{2\kappa^{\prime}}}&e^{\frac{\pi\Omega}{2\kappa^{\prime}}}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}{\cal B}_{-\Omega,\kappa^{\prime}}\\
{\cal B}^{\dagger}_{\Omega,\kappa^{\prime}}\end{pmatrix}=\int_{0}^{\infty}d\Lambda\begin{pmatrix}M_{\Omega\Lambda}&N_{\Omega\Lambda}\\
K_{-\Omega\Lambda}&P_{-\Omega\Lambda}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}{\cal A}_{\Lambda,\kappa,\gamma}\\
{\cal A}^{\dagger}_{\Lambda,\kappa,\gamma}\end{pmatrix}\,.

(43)

The determinant of the matrix on the left is
2\sinh(\frac{\pi\Omega}{\kappa'})
. Inverting this matrix to solve for
{\cal B}_{\Omega,\kappa^{\prime}}
gives:

\displaystyle{\cal B}_{\Omega,\kappa^{\prime}}=\frac{1}{2\sinh(\frac{\pi\Omega}{\kappa'})}\int d\Lambda\bigg[

\displaystyle\Big(e^{\frac{\pi\Omega}{2\kappa^{\prime}}}M_{\Omega\Lambda}-e^{-\frac{\pi\Omega}{2\kappa^{\prime}}}K_{-\Omega\Lambda}\Big){\cal A}_{\Lambda,\kappa,\gamma}

\displaystyle+\Big(e^{\frac{\pi\Omega}{2\kappa^{\prime}}}N_{\Omega\Lambda}-e^{-\frac{\pi\Omega}{2\kappa^{\prime}}}P_{-\Omega\Lambda}\Big){\cal A}^{\dagger}_{\Lambda,\kappa,\gamma}\bigg]\,.

(44)

D.4
Evaluating the Integral Kernels and Simplifying

The next step is to evaluate the integral kernels
M,N,K,P
by performing the projections on the
\kappa\gamma
plane wave expansion, using the Gamma function integral identity. After a lengthy but straightforward calculation, the expressions for the kernels are inserted into Eq. (
44
). The resulting combination of terms simplifies significantly.

After combining all terms, we arrive at the final Bogoliubov transformation:

\displaystyle{\cal B}_{\Omega,\kappa^{\prime}}=

\displaystyle\frac{\operatorname{sgn}(\Omega)}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}d\Lambda\sqrt{\frac{\Omega}{{\sinh(\frac{\pi\Omega}{\kappa'})\Lambda\sinh(\frac{\pi\Lambda}{\kappa})}}}\Lambda^{{{\rm i\,}}\Omega}\Gamma(-{{\rm i\,}}\Omega)

\displaystyle\times\Bigg\{\,\left[e^{\frac{\pi\Lambda}{2\kappa}+i\gamma}\sinh\left(\frac{\pi\Omega}{2}\left(\frac{1}{\kappa^{\prime}}+1\right)\right)+e^{-\frac{\pi\Lambda}{2\kappa}-i\gamma}\sinh\left(\frac{\pi\Omega}{2}\left(\frac{1}{\kappa^{\prime}}-1\right)\right)\right]{\cal A}_{\Lambda,\kappa,\gamma}

\displaystyle\phantom{\times\Bigg\{}+\left[e^{\frac{\pi\Lambda}{2\kappa}-i\gamma}\sinh\left(\frac{\pi\Omega}{2}\left(\frac{1}{\kappa^{\prime}}-1\right)\right)+e^{-\frac{\pi\Lambda}{2\kappa}+i\gamma}\sinh\left(\frac{\pi\Omega}{2}\left(\frac{1}{\kappa^{\prime}}+1\right)\right)\right]{\cal A}^{\dagger}_{\Lambda,\kappa,\gamma}\Bigg\},

(45)

where we have used properties of the Gamma function and made a substitution to express the result over the domain
\Omega>0
.

Pushforward of Siegel flag varieties in the Chow ring
Simon Cooper

Abstract
. Given a reductive group G G over an algebraically closed field and subsets I , J ⊂ Δ I,J\subset\Delta of the simple roots Δ \Delta determined by a choice of maximal torus and Borel subgroup, we obtain a closed embedding of flag varieties L J / L J ∩ P I ↪ G / P I L_{J}/L_{J}\cap P_{I}\hookrightarrow G/P_{I} . In this paper we compute the class of the sub flag variety [ L J / L J ∩ P I ] ∈ A ∙ ​ ( G / P I ) [L_{J}/L_{J}\cap P_{I}]\in A^{\bullet}(G/P_{I}) in the Chow ring for the ‘Siegel’ case where G G is a general symplectic group of rank g g and P I P_{I} is the parabolic stabilising a maximal isotropic subspace. This corresponds, under the isomorphism with the tautological ring of the moduli space of principally polarised abelian varieties T ∙ ​ ( 𝒜 g tor ) ≅ A ∙ ​ ( G / P I ) T^{\bullet}(\mathcal{A}_{g}^{\rm tor})\cong A^{\bullet}(G/P_{I}) , to the generator of the classes in the tautological ring which are supported on the toroidal boundary. We conjecture that this is not a coincidence.



1.
Introduction

Let
G
be a reductive group over a field
k
. Given subsets
I,J\subset\Delta
of the simple roots (for some choice of maximal torus and Borel subgroup), there is an embedding
L_{J}/L_{J}\cap P_{I}\hookrightarrow G/P_{I}
. This gives a class in the Chow ring
[L_{J}/L_{J}\cap P_{I}]\in A^{\bullet}(G/P)
. Can we describe this class? In the case where
G=GSp_{2g}
is the general symplectic group and
I\coloneqq\Delta\setminus\{2e_{g}\}
and
J\coloneqq\Delta\setminus\{e_{1}-e_{2}\}
are obtained from
\Delta
by removing extremal vertices of the Dynkin diagram, we have the following answer in terms of the top Chern class of a homogeneous vector bundle:

Theorem 1

(
3.4.2
)
.

Let
G=GSp_{2g}
,
I\coloneqq\Delta\setminus\{2e_{g}\}
and
J\coloneqq\Delta\setminus\{e_{1}-e_{2}\}
. Then

[L_{J}/L_{J}\cap P_{I}]=a_{J,g}.c_{g}(\mathcal{V}(\rho_{\rm Hdg}))\in A^{\bullet}(G/P_{I})

for some constant
a_{J,g}\in\mathbb{Q}\setminus\{0\}
, where
\mathcal{V}(\rho_{\rm Hdg})
is the vector bundle corresponding to the irreducible subrepresentation of the dual of the standard representation
\rho_{\rm Hdg}\subset\textnormal{std}^{\vee}|_{P_{I}}
. Moreover,
(-1)^{g}a_{J,g}>0
.

Theorem
1
is motivated by, though essentially independent of, a connection between tautological rings of Shimura varieties and Chow rings of flag varieties (always with rational coefficients). The tautological ring of a Shimura variety
S
is defined to be the subring of the Chow ring
T^{\bullet}(S)\subset A^{\bullet}(S)
generated by Chern classes of automorphic vector bundles. The connection originates with a diagram of the following form (due to Milne
[
8
]
).

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{Y}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X^{\vee}\cong G/P}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S^{\rm tor}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}

where
\pi
is a
G
-torsor and
f
is a smooth
G
-equivariant morphism. In positive characteristic it is known (due to Wedhorn-Ziegler
[
11
]
and conjectured to be true in characteristic zero too) that

T^{\bullet}(S^{\rm tor})\cong A^{\bullet}(G/P)

where
S^{\rm tor}
is the special fibre of a smooth toroidal compactification of a Shimura variety of Hodge type and
G/P
is the special fibre of an integral model of the compact dual of the symmetric domain defining the Shimura variety.
Building on this, in
[
4
]
a conjectural description is given of the tautological ring of the Shimura variety itself in terms of Chow rings of flag varieties:

Conjecture 1.0.1
.

Let
S
be the special fibre of an integral canonical model of a Shimura variety of Hodge type.

T^{\bullet}(S)\cong A^{\bullet}(G/P)/([L_{J}/L_{J}\cap P]\mid J\in\mathcal{J}\text{ maximal w.r.t. }\leq)

where
\mathcal{J}\coloneqq\{\Delta\setminus\{\alpha\}\mid\alpha\in\Delta\text{ and }P_{J}\subset G\text{ defined over }\mathbb{Q}\}
admits a partial order
\leq
according to the stratification of the boundary of a toroidal compactification
S^{\rm tor}
of
S
.

This connection has primarily been used to transport information from the flag variety side to the tautological ring side. For example, by providing a presentation of the tautological ring using Borel’s classic presentation of the cohomology ring of a flag variety (
[
1
]
, see
2.2
). Additionaly, in the case of the moduli space of principally polarised abelian varieties
S=\mathcal{A}_{g}
there is a complete description of the tautological ring, due to van der Geer
[
10
]
:

T^{\bullet}(\mathcal{A}_{g})\cong A^{\bullet}(G/P)/(\lambda_{g})

where
\lambda_{g}
is the top Chern class of the Hodge vector bundle
\Omega/\mathcal{A}_{g}
. In this case there is one maximal
J\in\mathcal{J}
and the conjectural description
1.0.1
of the tautological ring predicts Theorem
1
, which is proved in this paper by independent means. Thus, Theorem
1
provides basic evidence for the conjecture.
The statement of Theorem
1
does not reference tautological rings or Shimura varieties and can be considered purely as a question about Chow rings of flag varieties. Moreover, the proof that
[L_{J}/L_{J}\cap P]
is proportional to
\lambda_{g}
relies only on facts about Chow rings of flag varieties. The main idea is to use the self-intersection formula for the embedding
L_{J}/L_{J}\cap P\hookrightarrow G/P
to prove that the class
[L_{J}/L_{J}\cap P]\in A^{\bullet}(G/P)
vanishes when pulled back to
A^{\bullet}(L_{J}/L_{J}\cap P)
. This follows from the fact that the top Chern class of the normal bundle vanishes in this context (
3.4.1
). A description of the kernel of the pullback map
A^{\bullet}(G/P)\rightarrow A^{\bullet}(L_{J}/L_{J}\cap P)
(
3.3.1
) completes the computation, since the kernel is generated by
\lambda_{g}
.

2.
Generalities

2.1.
Group theoretic input data

Let
G
be a reductive group over an algebraically closed field
k
. A choice of maximal torus
T
determines a root datum
(X^{*}(T),\Phi,X_{*}(T),\Phi^{\vee})
. Given a choice of Borel subgroup
B
containing
T
, we denote a set of positive roots
\Phi^{+}\coloneqq\{\alpha\in\Phi^{+}\mid U_{-\alpha}\subset B\}
. This choice of positive roots means that ample line bundles on
G/B
correspond to dominant and regular characters. Denote
\Delta
the basis of simple roots. A subset
K\subset\Delta
determines a standard parabolic subgroup
P_{K}\subset G
. Every parabolic subgroup is conjugate to a standard parabolic subgroup
B\subset P_{K}\subset G
for some
K\subset\Delta
. For sake of exposition, we shall work mostly with standard parabolic subgroups. Denote
W=W(G,T)\coloneqq N_{G}(T)/T
the Weyl group of
G
with respect to
T
. Given a subset of the simple roots
I\subset\Delta
, denote
W_{I}\subset W
the group generated by the simple reflections
\{s_{\alpha}\in W\mid\alpha\in I\}
. Denoting
L_{I}\coloneqq P_{I}/\mathcal{R}_{u}(P_{I})
the Levi quotient of
P_{I}
, we have that
W(L_{I},T)\cong W_{I}
.

Intersection theory

Given a smooth scheme
X
, we denote
A^{\bullet}(X)
the Chow ring with rational coefficients. If there is an action of
G
on
X
then we denote
A^{\bullet}_{G}(X)
the equivariant Chow ring.

Given a diagram of the form

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi}

-torsor and

. The ‘inverse’ functor sends

to
P_{I}\rightarrow GL(E_{x})
where

. On Chow rings we have

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S^{W_{I}}\cong A^{\bullet}_{G}(G/P_{I})}

]

Proposition 2.2.1

(Self-intersection formula

]
)
.

Let
\iota\colon X\hookrightarrow Y
be a closed embedding of smooth varieties over an algebraically closed field with normal bundle

\iota^{*}\iota_{*}(\gamma)=c_{\rm top}(\mathcal{N}).\gamma

2.3.
Standard sub flag varieties

Let
I,J\subset\Delta
and fix an embedding
L_{J}\hookrightarrow G
. Now,
L_{J}\cap P_{I}
is a parabolic subgroup of
L_{J}
and the closed embedding
L_{J}\hookrightarrow G
descends to a closed embedding
\iota\colon L_{J}/L_{J}\cap P_{I}\hookrightarrow G/P_{I}
(see for example
[
6

). The Levi subgroup
L_{J}
has maximal torus
T

.

2.4.
Pullback & Pushforward

Suppose we have
\iota\colon L_{J}/L_{J}\cap P_{I}\hookrightarrow G/P_{I}
as above. There is an inclusion of Weyl groups
W(L_{J},T)\cong W_{J}\subset W
. Pullback gives a map of Chow rings

\iota^{*}\colon S^{W_{I}}/(S_{+}^{W})\cong A^{\bullet}(G/P_{I})\longrightarrow A^{\bullet}(L_{J}/L_{J}\cap P_{I})\cong S^{W_{I\cap J}}/(S_{+}^{W_{J}})

Moreover, if we consider the diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces L_{J}/L_{J}\cap B}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces G/B}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces L_{J}/L_{J}\cap P_{I}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces G/P_{I}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\nu}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\theta}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\iota}

with the full flag varieties then pullback gives us

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S/(S_{+}^{W_{J}})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S/(S_{+}^{W})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S^{W_{I\cap J}}/(S_{+}^{W_{J}})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S^{W_{I}}/(S_{+}^{W})}

Here the top map is simply projection as
W_{J}\subset W
implies that
S_{+}^{W}\subset S_{+}^{W_{J}}
. The vertical maps
\theta^{*}
and
\pi^{*}
are injective so we can often reduce to the case of full flag varieties, as in
2.5
.

2.5.
Normal bundle

In order to utilise the self-intersection formula we require some knowledge of the Chern classes of the normal bundle.

Proposition 2.5.1
.

Let

. Then its Chern polynomial is

c_{t}(\theta^{*}\mathcal{N})=\prod_{\alpha\in\Phi^{+}\setminus(\Phi_{I}^{+}\cup\Phi_{J}^{+})}(1+\left[\alpha\right]t)

Proof.

Firstly,

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{T}_{L/L\cap P_{I}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\iota^{*}\mathcal{T}_{G/P_{I}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{N}}

so
c_{t}(\theta^{*}\iota^{*}\mathcal{T}_{G/P_{I}})=c_{t}(\theta^{*}\mathcal{N})c_{t}(\theta^{*}\mathcal{T}_{L_{J}/L_{J}\cap P_{I}})
.

Secondly, the tangent bundle is

where

P_{I}\hookrightarrow G\xrightarrow{\rm ad}GL(\mathfrak{g})\rightarrow GL(\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{p})

with the third map being induced by projection. This is because there is an equivalence of categories

given by

where

. The tangent bundle is

we get a surjective

with kernel

. Hence,
\mathcal{T}_{G/P,x}\simeq\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{p}
. The action of
G

\mathfrak{g}
is given by the adjoint representation.

Now, the roots of
\rho
are
\Phi^{+}\setminus\Phi^{+}_{I}

c_{t}(\pi^{*}\mathcal{T}_{G/P})=\prod_{\alpha\in\Phi^{+}\setminus\Phi_{I}^{+}}(1+\left[\alpha\right]t)

Similarly,
c_{t}(\theta^{*}\mathcal{T}_{L/L\cap P})=\prod_{\alpha\in\Phi_{J}^{+}\setminus\Phi_{I\cap J}^{+}}(1+\left[\alpha\right]t)
.
∎

3.1.

Let

be a
2g

\begin{pmatrix}0&-J\\

This gives

3.2.
Weyl groups

The Weyl group can be described as

W=\{\sigma\in S_{2g}\mid\sigma(i)+\sigma(2g+1-i)=2g+1\text{ for }i=1,\ldots,g\}\cong S_{g}\rtimes\{\pm 1\}^{g}

The simple reflections are

W_{I}=\langle s_{e_{1}-e_{2}},\ldots,s_{e_{g-1}-e_{g}}\rangle\cong S_{g}\subset W

3.3.
Chow rings & pullback map

The picture for the Chow rings is the following:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S/(S_{+}^{W_{J}})\cong A^{\bullet}(L_{J}/B\cap L_{J})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A^{\bullet}(G/B)\cong S/(S_{+}^{W})}

We describe these rings and the pullback maps between them explicitly in terms of generators and relations. Denote
\lambda_{i}\coloneqq\sigma_{i}(e_{1},\ldots,e_{g})
(resp.

Lemma 3.3.1
.

We have the following.

S^{W_{I}}/(S^{W}_{+})\cong\mathbb{Q}[\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{g}]/((1+\lambda_{1}+\ldots+\lambda_{g})(1-\lambda_{1}+\ldots+(-1)^{g}\lambda_{g})-1)

and the pullback map
\iota^{*}\colon A^{\bullet}(G/P_{I})\rightarrow A^{\bullet}(L_{J}/L_{J}\cap P_{I})
is surjective with kernel

Proof.

W_{I}=\langle s_{e_{1}-e_{2}},\ldots,s_{e_{g-1}-e_{g}}\rangle\cong S_{g}
implies that
S^{W_{I}}\cong\mathbb{Q}[\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{g}]
. If

and thus

\mathbb{Q}[e_{1}.\ldots,e_{g}]^{W}\cong\mathbb{Q}[e_{1}^{2},\ldots,e_{g}^{2}]^{S_{g}}\cong\langle\sigma_{1}(e_{1}^{2},\ldots,e_{g}^{2}),\ldots,\sigma_{g}(e_{1}^{2},\ldots,e_{g}^{2})\rangle

by the fundamental theorem of symmetric polynomials.

The required description of

follows from Lemma

which says that the homogeneous part of
(1+\lambda_{1}+\ldots+\lambda_{g})(1-\lambda_{1}+\ldots+(-1)^{g}\lambda_{g})-1

. The same argument works for
S^{W_{I\cap J}}/(S^{W_{J}}_{+})
.

Now, we have that

, since
s_{e_{i}-e_{i+1}}(e_{1})=e_{1}
for

and similarly for

. Firstly, this implies that
\iota^{*}
is surjective, since
\iota^{*}\lambda_{i}=\widetilde{\lambda_{i}}

We record here for the sake of completeness the following result about symmetric polynomials.

Lemma 3.3.2
.

Let

. For all

(-1)^{l}\sigma_{l}(x_{1}^{2},\ldots,x_{g}^{2})=\sum_{i+j=2l}(-1)^{j}\sigma_{i}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{g})\sigma_{j}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{g})

Induction on

. Base case. Suppose for induction that it holds for all

. We make use of the fact that for

\sigma_{i}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{g})=\sigma_{i}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{g-1})+x_{g}\sigma_{i-1}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{g-1})

Indeed, fixing

, this yields

\displaystyle\sum_{i+j=2l}(-1)^{j}\sigma_{i}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{g})\sigma_{j}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{g})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i+j=2l}(-1)^{j}\sigma_{i}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{g-1})\sigma_{j}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{g-1})

\displaystyle-x_{g}^{2}\sum_{i+j=2l-2}(-1)^{j}\sigma_{i}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{g-1})\sigma_{j}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{g-1})

Using the inductive hypothesis for

we have that

\displaystyle\sum_{i+j=2l}(-1)^{j}\sigma_{i}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{g-1})\sigma_{j}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{g-1})

\displaystyle-x_{g}^{2}\sum_{i+j=2l-2}(-1)^{j}\sigma_{i}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{g-1})\sigma_{j}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{g-1})

\displaystyle=(-1)^{l}\sigma_{l}(x_{1}^{2},\ldots,x_{g-1}^{2})+(-1)^{l}x_{g}^{2}\sigma_{l-1}(x_{1}^{2},\ldots,x_{g-1}^{2})

\displaystyle=(-1)^{l}\sigma_{l}(x_{1}^{2},\ldots,x_{g}^{2})

Theorem 3.4.2

(Theorem
1
)
.

[L_{J}/L_{J}\cap P_{I}]=a\lambda_{g}\in A^{\bullet}(G/P_{I})
for some
a\neq 0
.

Proof.

We have a closed immersion
\iota\colon L_{J}/L_{J}\cap P_{I}\rightarrow G/P_{I}
and
\iota_{*}(1)
is proportional to
[L_{J}/L_{J}\cap P_{I}]\in A^{\bullet}(G/P_{I})
. To determine this class up to scalar we apply the pullback map to get
\iota^{*}\iota_{*}(1)=c_{g}\mathcal{N}=0
by Corollary
3.4.1
. However, the pullback map is surjective with kernel
\lambda_{g}A^{\bullet}(G/P_{I})
and so
\iota_{*}(1)=a.\lambda_{g}
for some
a\in\mathbb{Q}
. Given that
L_{J}/L_{J}\cap P_{I}
is a closed subvariety in the projective variety
G/P_{I}
, its class in Chow cannot be zero, so
a\neq 0
.
∎

What can be said about the constant
a
? For example, what is its sign? A precise statement is the following:

Lemma 3.4.3
.

Let
\rho_{\rm Hdg}\in\Rep(P_{I})
be the representation giving the Hodge vector bundle
\Omega=\mathcal{W}(\rho_{\rm Hdg})
on

. Then
[L_{J}/L_{J}\cap P_{I}]=a_{J,g}c_{g}(\mathcal{V}(\rho_{\rm Hdg}))
in

where
(-1)^{g}a_{J,g}>0
.

To prove this we compare with the Shimura variety and use positivity statements on both sides together with Hirzebruch-Mumford proportionality. Let
S
be a Shimura variety of Hodge type. Write
d\coloneqq\dim(S)
. Applying
2.2
to the standard principal bundle on

gives

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Rep(P_{I})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Vect(S^{\rm tor})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Vect(G/P_{I})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{W}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{V}}

Hirzebruch-Mumford Proportionality

There is some

]

3.4.5.
Proof of Lemma
3.4.3

Under the Borel isomorphism

A^{\bullet}(G/P_{I})\cong\mathbb{Q}[\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{g}]/((1+\lambda_{1}+\ldots+\lambda_{g})(1-\lambda_{1}+\ldots+(-1)^{g}\lambda_{g})-1)

c_{g}(\mathcal{V}(\rho_{\rm Hdg}))
is proportional to
\lambda_{g}
so Theorem
1
gives that
[L_{J}/L_{J}\cap P_{I}]=a_{J,g}c_{g}(\mathcal{V}(\rho_{\rm Hdg}))
for some
a_{J,g}\neq 0
. It remains to show that
(-1)^{g}a_{J,g}>0
.

Firstly, in the Siegel case
S^{\rm tor}=\mathcal{A}_{g}^{\rm tor}
the Hirzebruch-Mumford proportionality constant satisfies:
(-1)^{d}R>0
. The line bundle
\mathcal{L}\coloneqq\mathcal{V}(\det(\rho_{\rm Hdg}))
on
G/P_{I}
is anti-ample by
[
5
, Lemma 3.2.5]
,
[
6
, II (4.4)]
and the Hodge line bundle
\omega\coloneqq\det(\Omega)=\mathcal{W}(\det(\rho_{\rm Hdg}))
on
S^{\rm tor}
is nef.

\displaystyle\int_{G/P_{I}}c_{1}(\mathcal{L}^{\vee})^{d}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{d}\int_{G/P_{I}}c_{1}(\mathcal{L})^{d}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{d}R\int_{S^{\rm tor}}c_{1}(\omega)^{d}

Thus,
(-1)^{d}R>0
.

Secondly, applying Hirzebruch-Mumford proportionality with
\rho_{\rm Hdg}
and homogeneous polynomial
f=c_{g}c_{1}^{d-g}
gives:

\displaystyle\int_{G/P_{I}}[L_{J}/L_{J}\cap P_{I}]c_{1}(\mathcal{L}^{\vee})^{d-g}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{d-g}a_{J,g}\int_{G/P_{I}}c_{g}(\mathcal{V}(\rho_{\rm Hdg}))c_{1}(\mathcal{L})^{d-g}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{d-g}a_{J,g}R\int_{S^{\rm tor}}c_{g}(\Omega)c_{1}(\Omega)^{d-g}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{d-g}a_{J,g}R\int_{S^{\rm tor}}\frac{1}{N_{g,p}}[V_{0}]c_{1}(\omega)^{d-g}

where
N_{g,p}
is a positive constant depending on
g
and
p
. The last line follows from
[
10
, Prop 9.2]
which says that the cycle class of the
p
-rank
0
locus
V_{0}\subset\mathcal{A}_{g}^{\rm tor}
is
[V_{0}]=N_{g,p}c_{g}(\Omega)
. Hence,
\int_{S^{\rm tor}}\frac{1}{N_{g,p}}[V_{0}].c_{1}(\mathcal{W}(\rho))^{d-g}\geq 0
. The line bundle
\mathcal{L}
on
G/P_{I}
is anti-ample so
\int_{G/P}[L_{J}/L_{J}\cap P_{I}].c_{1}(\mathcal{L}^{\vee})^{d-g}>0
. This implies that

(-1)^{d-g}a_{J,g}R\geq 0

Thus,
(-1)^{g}a_{J,g}>0
. ∎

4.

There are other Shimura varieties where we obtain fairly trivial confirmations of Conjecture
1.0.1
. In the case of the Hilbert modular variety at an unramified prime the parabolic subgroup is the Borel subgroup and so the sub flag variety has dimension
0
. This forces it to be proportional to the only class in degree
0
in
A^{\bullet}(G/B)\cong T^{\bullet}(S^{\rm tor})
. In
[
3
]
it is shown that
T^{\bullet}(S)\cong T^{\bullet}(S^{\rm tor})/(z)
where
z
is a generator of the
1
-dimensional space
T_{0}(S^{\rm tor})
. So in this case,
1.0.1
is (trivially) confirmed.

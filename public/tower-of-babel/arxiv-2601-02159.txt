Projectively equivalent para-Kähler and para-Kähler-Einstein metrics with non-parallel Benenti tensors and their normal forms in dimension four
Gianni Manno, Filippo Salis

Abstract
The study of projectively equivalent metrics, i.e., metrics sharing the same unparametrized geodesics, is a classical and well-established area of investigation. In the Kähler context, such branch of research goes by the name of c-projective geometry : it mainly studies c-projectively equivalent metrics , i.e., Kähler metrics sharing the same curves that are the complex analogue of the geodesics, called J J -planar , where J J is the complex structure. In this paper, we develop the theory of the projective equivalence in the para-Kähler context by studying para-Kähler metrics sharing the same 𝒯 \mathcal{T} -planar curves , where 𝒯 \mathcal{T} is the para-complex structure: we call such metrics pc-projectively equivalent . After establishing some general results in arbitrary dimension, we focus on the 4 4 -dimensional case. One of the main achievement is a local description of 4 4 -dimensional pc-projectively (but not affinely) equivalent metrics and, as an application of this result, we characterize which of them are of Einstein type.

1.3.1
Preliminary results: Benenti tensors in the para-Kähler context

Even though affinely equivalent metrics remain of independent interest, in line with
[
10
]
,
this paper focuses on the case that is most natural from the perspective of projective geometry: metrics that are pc-projectively equivalent but not affinely equivalent. To study such metrics, of fundamental importance is the following tensor field
A
of type
(1,1)
: let
g,\hat{g}
be two pc-projectively equivalent metrics, then we define

A:=\left(\frac{\det\hat{g}}{\det g}\right)^{\frac{1}{2(n+1)}}\hat{g}^{-1}g.

(5)

(We note that the previous quantity is well defined as
\frac{\det\hat{g}}{\det g}>0
since para-Kähler metrics possess neutral signature. Consequently, we also have

\det A>0
).
An important property of
A
is that it is parallel w.r.t. the Levi-Civita connection
\nabla^{\hat{g}}
of
\hat{g}
(equivalently, w.r.t.
\nabla^{g}
) if and only if
g
and
\hat{g}
are affinely equivalent. Indeed,
from (
5
), we get
g=(\det A)^{1/2}\,\hat{g}A
.
Differentiating with respect to
\nabla^{\hat{g}}
yields

\nabla^{\hat{g}}g=(\det A)^{1/2}\,\hat{g}\left(\tfrac{1}{2}\operatorname{tr}(A^{-1}\nabla^{\hat{g}}A)\,A+\nabla^{\hat{g}}A\right).

Hence,

\nabla^{\hat{g}}g=0\;\;\Longleftrightarrow\;\;\tfrac{1}{2}\,\operatorname{tr}(A^{-1}\nabla^{\hat{g}}A)\,\mathrm{Id}+A^{-1}\nabla^{\hat{g}}A=0.

Taking the trace of the latter relation shows that
\operatorname{tr}(A^{-1}\nabla^{\hat{g}}A)=0
, and substituting back gives

\nabla^{\hat{g}}A=0
.
In particular, the vanishing of
\nabla^{\hat{g}}g
is equivalent to
\nabla^{\hat{g}}A=0
, which precisely means that the Levi-Civita connections of
g
and
\hat{g}
coincide.

To address the problem of finding normal forms of pc-projectively equivalent para-Kähler metrics, as shown in Section
2
, it is crucial to study the space

\mathcal{A}(g,\mathcal{T})=\{A\in\Gamma(\mathrm{End}(TM))\;|\;A\text{ is $g$-symmetric, }[A,\mathcal{T}]=0,\ A\text{ satisfies \eqref{eq.A} below $\forall$ vector fields }X\},

(6)

\nabla^{g}_{X}A=X^{\flat}\otimes\Lambda+\Lambda^{\flat}\otimes X-(\mathcal{T}X)^{\flat}\otimes\mathcal{T}\Lambda-(\mathcal{T}\Lambda)^{\flat}\otimes\mathcal{T}X,

(7)

where

\Lambda=\tfrac{1}{4}\operatorname{grad}\operatorname{tr}A\,,

(8)

with
{}^{\flat}:TM\to T^{*}M
the musical isomorphism associated to
g
. We note that
(
7
) is the para-complex analogue of the equation governing c-projectively equivalent Kähler metrics (cf.
[
10

In fact,
an important property of tensors (
5
), shown in the paper, is that they belong to
\mathcal{A}(g,\mathcal{T})
. Moreover, any non-degenerate
A\in\mathcal{A}(g,\mathcal{T})
is of type (
5
) (see Corollary

\hat{g}=(\det A)^{-\frac{1}{2}}gA^{-1}

\textrm{\emph{Benenti tensors of} }(g,\mathcal{T}):=\{\,A\in\mathcal{A}(g,\mathcal{T})\,\,|\,\,\det(A)\neq 0\,\}\,.

Indeed, as in the real case, in view of what we said above, the knowledge of the space of the Benenti tensors of
(g,\mathcal{T})

On the other hand, from the symplectic viewpoint, if we denote

1.3.3
Differences with the Kähler case and new strategies

The variety of normal forms arising in the para-Kähler setting is richer than in the Kähler one. Indeed, while the non-degenerate situation
\dim D=4
and the first family in the case
\dim D=2
admit direct counterparts in the Kähler classification (cf.
[
10
, Theorem 3.1]
), none of the other degenerate configurations occurring in Theorem
2
has a Kähler analogue.
This discrepancy reflects intrinsic algebraic differences between the para-complex and complex structures. In particular, phenomena such as isotropic gradients of the eigenvalues of Benenti tensors and the appearance of odd-dimensional ranks for the distribution
D
, have no counterpart in the Kähler context. These structural reasons, discussed more in details in Remark
26
, explain why the para-Kähler theory naturally exhibits a broader spectrum of local models. Consequently, one cannot rely on what is known in the Kähler setting to guide the analysis in the para-Kähler case. For instance, the construction of local frames adapted to
D
in the degenerate cases, offers a particularly illustrative example of a situation where techniques from the Kähler case cannot be adapted.
Even though certain aspects of the analysis may still be guided by the conceptual analogy, without however becoming straightforward as a consequence, these analogies nevertheless remain insufficient to fully capture the phenomena arising in the para-Kähler setting. The latter ultimately requires additional arguments specifically tailored to its own structural features.

We recall, by definition of weighted tensors, that if we perform a change of coordinates

\tilde{\sigma}^{km}=|\det J\,|^{-\frac{1}{n+1}}\sigma^{ij}J^{k}_{i}J^{m}_{j}.

\nabla_{i}\sigma^{jk}=\sigma^{jk}_{,i}+\Gamma^{j}_{im}\sigma^{mk}+\Gamma^{k}_{im}\sigma^{mj}-\tfrac{1}{n+1}\Gamma^{p}_{ip}\sigma^{jk}.

Canonical Killing vector fields and Ricci tensors of pc-projectively equivalent para-Kähler metrics

In this section, we study vector fields naturally associated with scalar invariants of

Their geometric significance will become particularly clear in Section

Proposition 12
.

Let
(M,g,\mathcal{T})
be a para-Kähler manifold and let
A\in\mathcal{A}(g,\mathcal{T})
.
Then the vector field

\mathcal{T}\operatorname{grad}\sqrt{\det A}

is a Killing vector field of
g
.

Proof.

To prove the proposition, it suffices to check locally that the vector field
\mathcal{T}\operatorname{grad}\sqrt{\det A}
is Killing, that is,

\nabla_{i}^{g}(\mathcal{T}\operatorname{grad}\sqrt{\det A})_{j}+\nabla_{j}^{g}(\mathcal{T}\operatorname{grad}\sqrt{\det A})_{i}=0\quad\text{for all }i,j.

(25)

Let us recall that the symplectic form
\omega
of the para-Kähler manifold is given by (
1
). Since, by definition,
\mathcal{T}\operatorname{grad}\sqrt{\det A}=\omega^{-1}\,d(\sqrt{\det A})
, we have, by Remark
11
and in particular by formulas (
23
) and (

), that

\mathcal{T}\operatorname{grad}\sqrt{\det A}=-2\,e^{-2\psi}\,\omega^{-1}(\Psi).

Recalling that
\nabla^{g}
preserves
\mathcal{T}
, i.e.
\nabla^{g}\mathcal{T}=0
, a direct computation then yields

\nabla_{i}^{g}(\mathcal{T}\operatorname{grad}\sqrt{\det A})_{j}+\nabla_{j}^{g}(\mathcal{T}\operatorname{grad}\sqrt{\det A})_{i}=-2\bigl(-2\Psi_{i}\Psi_{k}+\nabla_{i}^{g}\Psi_{k}\bigr)\,\mathcal{T}^{k}_{j}-2\bigl(-2\Psi_{j}\Psi_{k}+\nabla_{j}^{g}\Psi_{k}\bigr)\,\mathcal{T}^{k}_{i}.

Hence, the Killing condition (
25
) is equivalent to requiring that the
(0,2)
-tensor

S:=2\,\Psi\otimes\Psi-\nabla^{g}\Psi

is para-Hermitian, i.e.,

S(\mathcal{T}\cdot,\mathcal{T}\cdot)=-S(\cdot,\cdot)\,.

Below we shall prove it.

To this aim, we now consider a para-Kähler metric
\hat{g}
on
M
pc-projectively equivalent to
g
.
In particular, we take into account the relation between the Ricci tensors of
g
and
\hat{g}
by exploiting formula (
12
) and (
13
).
Recalling that the components of the curvature tensor
R
of
g
are given by

R^{k}_{lij}=\partial_{i}\Gamma^{k}_{lj}-\partial_{j}\Gamma^{k}_{li}+\Gamma^{k}_{ir}\Gamma^{r}_{lj}-\Gamma^{k}_{jr}\Gamma^{r}_{li}

(and analogously the components of the curvature tensor
\widehat{R}
of
\hat{g}
), it follows that

\widehat{R}^{k}_{lij}-R^{k}_{lij}=\nabla_{i}^{g}\bigl(\widehat{\Gamma}^{k}_{lj}-\Gamma^{k}_{lj}\bigr)-\nabla_{j}^{g}\bigl(\widehat{\Gamma}^{k}_{li}-\Gamma^{k}_{li}\bigr)+\bigl(\widehat{\Gamma}^{k}_{ir}-\Gamma^{k}_{ir}\bigr)\bigl(\widehat{\Gamma}^{r}_{lj}-\Gamma^{r}_{lj}\bigr)-\bigl(\widehat{\Gamma}^{k}_{jr}-\Gamma^{k}_{jr}\bigr)\bigl(\widehat{\Gamma}^{r}_{li}-\Gamma^{r}_{li}\bigr).

Taking the trace with respect to
k
and

, using the fact that
\nabla^{g}\mathcal{T}=0
and considering (

), a tedious computation yields the following relation between the Ricci tensors of

and
\hat{g}
:

\widehat{\operatorname{Ric}}_{lj}-\operatorname{Ric}_{lj}=\nabla_{l}^{g}\Psi_{j}-(2n+1)\nabla_{j}^{g}\Psi_{l}+\mathcal{T}^{r}_{l}\mathcal{T}^{k}_{j}\nabla_{k}^{g}\Psi_{r}+\mathcal{T}^{r}_{j}\mathcal{T}^{k}_{l}\nabla_{k}^{g}\Psi_{r}+2(n-1)\Psi_{l}\Psi_{j}+2(n-1)\Psi_{r}\mathcal{T}^{r}_{l}\,\Psi_{s}\mathcal{T}^{s}_{j}.

Since
\Psi
is an exact one-form, its covariant derivative
\nabla\Psi
is symmetric. Therefore, the previous relation can be conveniently rewritten as

\widehat{\operatorname{Ric}}-\operatorname{Ric}=-2n\bigl(\nabla^{g}\Psi-\Psi\otimes\Psi-(\Psi\circ\mathcal{T})\otimes(\Psi\circ\mathcal{T})\bigr)+2\bigl(\nabla^{g}\Psi(\mathcal{T}\cdot,\mathcal{T}\cdot)-\Psi\otimes\Psi-(\Psi\circ\mathcal{T})\otimes(\Psi\circ\mathcal{T})\bigr).

(26)

It is well known that the Ricci tensor of any para-Kähler metric is para-Hermitian (see, for instance,
[
2

); consequently, the right-hand side of (

) must also be para-Hermitian. Applying the defining property of para-Hermitian
(0,2)
tensors, we obtain

-2n\bigl(\nabla^{g}\Psi-\Psi\otimes\Psi-(\Psi\circ\mathcal{T})\otimes(\Psi\circ\mathcal{T})\bigr)+2\bigl(\nabla^{g}\Psi(\mathcal{T}\cdot,\mathcal{T}\cdot)-\Psi\otimes\Psi-(\Psi\circ\mathcal{T})\otimes(\Psi\circ\mathcal{T})\bigr)\\
=2\bigl(\nabla^{g}\Psi-\Psi\otimes\Psi-(\Psi\circ\mathcal{T})\otimes(\Psi\circ\mathcal{T})\bigr)-2n\bigl(\nabla^{g}\Psi(\mathcal{T}\cdot,\mathcal{T}\cdot)-\Psi\otimes\Psi-(\Psi\circ\mathcal{T})\otimes(\Psi\circ\mathcal{T})\bigr),

which implies that

\nabla^{g}\Psi-\Psi\otimes\Psi-(\Psi\circ\mathcal{T})\otimes(\Psi\circ\mathcal{T})=\nabla^{g}\Psi(\mathcal{T}\cdot,\mathcal{T}\cdot)-\Psi\otimes\Psi-(\Psi\circ\mathcal{T})\otimes(\Psi\circ\mathcal{T}).

Hence, the difference of the Ricci tensors (
26
) can be expressed in the more compact form:

\widehat{\operatorname{Ric}}-\operatorname{Ric}=-2(n-1)\bigl(\nabla^{g}\Psi-\Psi\otimes\Psi-(\Psi\circ\mathcal{T})\otimes(\Psi\circ\mathcal{T})\bigr).

(27)

Finally, observing that the left-hand side of

\widehat{\operatorname{Ric}}-\operatorname{Ric}+2(n-1)\bigl(\Psi\otimes\Psi-(\Psi\circ\mathcal{T})\otimes(\Psi\circ\mathcal{T})\bigr)=2(n-1)\bigl(2\,\Psi\otimes\Psi-\nabla^{g}\Psi\bigr),

is para-Hermitian, we conclude that

2\,\Psi\otimes\Psi-\nabla^{g}\Psi

is para-Hermitian as well.
This completes the proof.
∎

The previous proposition identifies a Killing vector field associated with any tensor
A\in\mathcal{A}(g,\mathcal{T})
.
We now extend this construction to associate with
A
a set of Killing vector fields.

As a direct consequence of the linearity of equation (
7
), for any
A\in\mathcal{A}(g,\mathcal{T})
and any
t\in\mathds{R}
, the tensor
A-t\,\mathrm{Id}_{2n}
also belongs to
\mathcal{A}(g,\mathcal{T})
. Moreover, the properties of the determinant of
A
discussed in Remark
11
imply that
\det(A-t\,\mathrm{Id}_{2n})
is a perfect square, so the function
\sqrt{\det(A-t\,\mathrm{Id}_{2n})}
is a polynomial in
t
. Expanding it as

\sqrt{\det(A-t\,\mathrm{Id}_{2n})}=\sum_{i=0}^{n}(-1)^{n-i}\,\mu_{i}\,t^{n-i},

(28)

we obtain a distinguished family of smooth functions
\mu_{i}\in C^{\infty}(M)
naturally associated with
A
. Moreover, we define the following gradient vector fields

V_{i}:=\operatorname{grad}\mu_{i},\qquad\text{for }i=1,\mathellipsis,n.

(29)

Corollary 13
.

Let
(M,g,\mathcal{T})
be a para-Kähler manifold and let
A\in\mathcal{A}(g,\mathcal{T})
. Then, for each
i
, the vector field
\mathcal{T}V_{i}
is Killing with respect to
g
.

Proof.

Since
A-t\,\mathrm{Id}_{2n}\in\mathcal{A}(g,\mathcal{T})
for all
t\in\mathds{R}
, Proposition
12
implies that

X(t):=\mathcal{T}\operatorname{grad}\sqrt{\det(A-t\,\mathrm{Id}_{2n})}

is a Killing vector field of
g
for every
t
. Hence
\mathcal{L}_{X(t)}g=0
.

From (
28
), each coefficient
\mu_{i}
can be written as

\mu_{i}=\frac{1}{(n-i)!}\,\frac{d^{\,n-i}}{dt^{\,n-i}}\Big|_{t=0}\sqrt{\det(A-t\,\mathrm{Id}_{2n})}.

Since the map
F:\mathds{R}\times M\rightarrow\mathds{R}
, defined as
F(t,p)=\sqrt{\det(A(p)-t\,\mathrm{Id}_{2n})}
,
is smooth, differentiation with respect to
t
commutes with all differential operators acting on
p
, such as the gradient and the Lie derivative. Therefore, being
\mathcal{T}
independent of t, we have

\mathcal{T}V_{i}=\mathcal{T}\operatorname{grad}\mu_{i}=\frac{1}{(n-i)!}\,\frac{d^{\,n-i}}{dt^{\,n-i}}\Big|_{t=0}X(t),

and consequently

\mathcal{L}_{\frac{d^{\,n-i}}{dt^{\,n-i}}\big|_{t=0}X(t)}g=\tfrac{d^{\,n-i}}{dt^{\,n-i}}\big|_{t=0}\,\mathcal{L}_{X(t)}g=0.

It follows that
\mathcal{T}V_{i}
is a Killing vector field.
∎

In particular, to each Benenti tensor of
(g,\mathcal{T})
one can canonically associate a family of Killing vector fields of
g
. As shown in the following proposition, these vector fields admit a symplectic interpretation and turn out to be para-holomorphic. This latter property will be repeatedly exploited in constructing the coordinate vector fields for the degenerate cases of Theorem
2
, see Sections

-
6.4
.

Proposition 14
.

The vector fields
\mathcal{T}V_{i}
and
V_{i}
, where
V_{i}
given by (
29
), are para-holomorphic for any
i
.

Proof.

The vector fields
\mathcal{T}V_{i}
considered in Corollary
13
are in fact the Hamiltonian vector fields, with respect to the para-Kähler form

\omega
given by (
1
), associated with the functions
\mu_{i}
,
that is,

\omega(\mathcal{T}V_{i},\cdot)=d\mu_{i}.

Hamiltonian vector fields preserve
\omega
, namely

\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{T}V_{i}}\omega=0,

so they are
symplectic
.
By Corollary
13
, the same vector fields are also Killing with respect to
g
, that is,
\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{T}V_{i}}g=0
.
Since
g
and
\omega
determine the endomorphism
\mathcal{T}
by the relation (
1
), it follows that

g\bigl((\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{T}V_{i}}\mathcal{T})(\cdot),\cdot\bigr)=(\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{T}V_{i}}\omega)(\cdot,\cdot)=0,

hence
\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{T}V_{i}}\mathcal{T}=0
.
In other words, the vector fields
\mathcal{T}V_{i}
are
para-holomorphic
. Moreover, the vanishing of the Nijenhuis tensor of
\mathcal{T}
implies

\mathcal{L}_{V_{i}}\mathcal{T}=\mathcal{T}\,\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{T}V_{i}}\mathcal{T},

that is each
V_{i}
is also para-holomorphic.
∎

5.1
Eigenvalue partition of para-Kähler surfaces: construction of an open dense subset

Let
(M,g,\mathcal{T})
be a para-Kähler surface and let
A\in\mathcal{A}(g,\mathcal{T})
. In view of Remark
11
, the
(1,1)
tensor field
A
has at most two distinct eigenvalues.

Notation
:
From now on,
we denote the eigenvalues of
A
by
\rho
and
\sigma
, unless otherwise specified .

The eigenvalue structure of
A
induces a natural partition of
M
according to the spectral type of
A
at each point
p\in M
. More precisely, we define the following three subsets of
M
:

\displaystyle\bullet\quad p

\displaystyle\in M^{\mathrm{r}}

\displaystyle\text{if the eigenvalues $\rho(p)$ and $\sigma(p)$ of $A(p)$ are real and distinct, each of algebraic multiplicity $2$,}

\displaystyle\bullet\quad p

\displaystyle\in M^{\mathrm{c}}

\displaystyle\text{if the eigenvalues $\rho(p)$ and $\sigma(p)$ of $A(p)$ are complex-conjugate, each of algebraic multiplicity $2$,}

\displaystyle\bullet\quad p

\displaystyle\in M^{\mathrm{sing}}

\displaystyle\text{if $A(p)$ has a single real eigenvalue of algebraic multiplicity $4$.}

It follows that
M^{\mathrm{r}}
and
M^{\mathrm{c}}
are open subsets, whereas
M^{\mathrm{sing}}
is closed.
Indeed,

M^{\mathrm{r}}=\Delta^{-1}\bigl((0,\infty)\bigr),\qquad M^{\mathrm{c}}=\Delta^{-1}\bigl((-\infty,0)\bigr),\qquad M^{\mathrm{sing}}=\Delta^{-1}(0),

where

\Delta:=\mu_{1}^{2}-4\mu_{2}

is the discriminant of the characteristic polynomial of
A
, and the functions
\mu_{i}
are defined by (
28
).
Thus, the eigenvalues
\rho
and
\sigma
are given by the functions

\frac{-\mu_{1}\pm\sqrt{\Delta}}{2},

that turn out to be continuous on
M
, but smooth only on
M^{\mathrm{r}}\cup M^{\mathrm{c}}
.
Then, if neither
\rho
nor
\sigma
is constant on the whole of
M
, we introduce the open subset

M^{\circ}:=\left\{p\in M\;\middle|\;\rho(p)\neq\sigma(p),\ d\rho(p)\neq 0,\ d\sigma(p)\neq 0\right\}.

In the special case in which
\sigma
is constant on
M
, we instead set

M^{\circ}:=\left\{p\in M\;\middle|\;\rho(p)\neq\sigma,\ d\rho(p)\neq 0\right\}.

In Section
5.2
we shall further restrict this set to a (possibly disconnected) open dense subset

\widehat{M}^{\circ}\subseteq M^{\circ}
,
where the distribution
D
defined by (
11
) has constant rank on each connected component.
Our aim in this section is to show that
M^{\circ}
is dense in
M
.

Lemma 18
.

Let
(M,g,\mathcal{T})
be a connected para-Kähler surface and let
A\in\mathcal{A}(g,\mathcal{T})
be
non-parallel. Then the open set
M^{\mathrm{r}}\cup M^{\mathrm{c}}
is dense in
M
.

Proof.

Suppose, by contradiction, that
M^{\mathrm{sing}}
contains a nonempty open subset
U\subseteq M
.
Then, on
U
, the tensor
A
admits a single real eigenvalue
\rho
of algebraic multiplicity 4.
By Proposition
14
and Corollary
13
, both
\rho
and
\rho^{2}
are Hamiltonian functions for Killing vector fields on
U
.
In particular, a direct computation shows that
d\rho\circ\mathcal{T}
and

are constant along any geodesics. This implies that
\rho
itself is constant on
U
.
Consequently, using the block form of
A
introduced in Remark
11
, the Killing vector field
\mathcal{T}\operatorname{grad}\operatorname{tr}A^{\prime}
(which coincides with
\tfrac{1}{2}\mathcal{T}\operatorname{grad}\operatorname{tr}A
) vanishes on

, then it vanishes on the whole of

, being

an open set, by the unique continuation property of Killing fields.
Equation (

) then shows that

is parallel, contradicting the hypothesis.
Therefore,
M^{\mathrm{sing}}
has empty interior, and
M^{\mathrm{r}}\cup M^{\mathrm{c}}
is dense in
M
.
∎

If
A
is non-parallel, there cannot exist a non-empty open subset on which both eigenvalues of
A
are constant.
Indeed, on such an open set, the Killing vector field
\mathcal{T}\operatorname{grad}\operatorname{tr}A
would vanish identically and, as we said above, it would then vanish on whole of

, so that equation (

) would imply that

is parallel: a contradiction. Therefore, in the remaining part of the paper, we will adopt the following

Assumption
:
w.l.o.g. we assume that

is an eigenvalue of

non-constant on the whole of

Proposition 19
.

Let
(M,g,\mathcal{T})

Let

Remark 20

Lemma 21
.

Let
(M,g,\mathcal{T})
be a para-Kähler surface and let
A\in\mathcal{A}(g,\mathcal{T})
be non-parallel.
Then the gradients of the eigenvalue functions
\rho
and
\sigma

Furthermore, the eigenspaces of
A
relative to
\rho
and
\sigma
are
g
-orthogonal.

Proof.

The computation can be carried out on the dense open subset
M^{\mathrm{r}}\cup M^{\mathrm{c}}
, where the eigenvalues are smooth and distinct (see Lemma
18
).

Let
Y\in\Gamma(T^{\mathds{C}}M)
be an eigenvector of
A
corresponding to the eigenvalue
\sigma
, i.e.,

AY=\sigma Y\,.

(43)

Then, for any
X\in\Gamma(T^{\mathds{C}}M)
, by considering the covariant derivative of (
43
) along

, equation (
7
) yields

(A-\sigma\,\mathrm{Id})\,\nabla_{X}^{g}Y=X(\sigma)\,Y-g(Y,X)\,\Lambda-g(Y,\Lambda)\,X+g(Y,\mathcal{T}X)\,\mathcal{T}\Lambda+g(Y,\mathcal{T}\Lambda)\,\mathcal{T}X.

(44)

Now, take
X
to be an eigenvector of
A
with eigenvalue
\rho
.
Since

is
g
-symmetric and commutes with

g(X,Y)=0,\qquad g(\mathcal{T}X,Y)=0\quad\text{on }M^{\mathrm{r}}\cup M^{\mathrm{c}}.

\sigma

Furthermore, in view of (

(A-\sigma\,\mathrm{Id})\,\nabla_{X}^{g}Y=X(\sigma)\,Y-g(Y,\Lambda)\,X+g(Y,\mathcal{T}\Lambda)\,\mathcal{T}X.

Taking the inner product with

If

Equivalently,

By the density of

Remark 26

\begin{array}[]{l}a_{2}\|X_{1}\|^{2}_{\phi_{t}(p)}=g_{\phi_{t}(p)}(\phi_{t*}\big(Y_{1}(p)\big),X_{1}(\phi_{t}(p)))=g_{p}(Y_{1}(p),X_{1}(p))=0\,,\\[5.69054pt]

), we have that

X_{1}\,,\quad U_{2}^{\mathrm{ext}}=\phi_{t*}(hX_{2})=hX_{2}\,,\quad U_{3}^{\mathrm{ext}}=\phi_{t*}(Y_{1}+fX_{1})=a_{1}Y_{1}+fX_{1},\quad U_{4}^{\mathrm{ext}}=\phi_{t*}(Y_{2})=a_{3}Y_{2}

(83)

mutually commute. In the remaining part of the present section, we shall denote by

\frac{1}{h}&0&\frac{f}{h}&0\\

0&0&0&-1\end{pmatrix}\,,\quad A=\begin{pmatrix}\rho&0&f(\rho-c)&0\\

6.3.2

In this case, recalling (

. We shall complete
\{V_{1},V_{2}\}
to a frame

V_{1}
and
V_{2}

Let
\Sigma

Let

and

and

and

Also, since

(84)

and
V_{2}

is proportional to
X
and that
Y^{\mathrm{ext}}
is proportional to
Y
.
Indeed, since

and

.

To conclude, we have constructed four vector fields

(85)

that mutually commute.

In the remaining part of the present section, we shall denote by

(x^{1},x^{2},x^{3},x^{4})

a local system of coordinates where the vector fields (
85

g
in the aforementioned system of coordinates. Since

, we have that

Therefore,

\rho_{x^{1}}+\sigma_{x^{1}}=0\,,\quad\sigma\rho_{x^{1}}+\rho\sigma_{x^{1}}=0\,,

and

\sigma
is independent of

g=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}0&0&\rho_{x^{3}}&\sigma_{x^{4}}\\
0&0&\sigma\rho_{x^{3}}&\rho\sigma_{x^{4}}\\
\rho_{x^{3}}&\sigma\rho_{x^{3}}&0&0\\
\sigma_{x^{4}}&\rho\sigma_{x^{4}}&0&0\\

and

) with

-1&0&0&0\\

6.3.4
The case where
\operatorname{grad}\rho\in\Gamma(T^{+})\setminus\{0\}
,

In this case, recalling again (
48

that, in view of Proposition
23
, is integrable. Similarly to what was done in the previous sections, we shall complete

and the other to
\Gamma(T^{+})\setminus\{0\}

\mathcal{T}V_{1}
and
\mathcal{T}V_{2}
and have to form with these latter a system of independent vector fields.
The construction of the aforementioned additional two vector fields goes as follows.

Let
X\in\Gamma(T^{-}\cap E_{\rho})
and
Y\in\Gamma(T^{+}\cap E_{\sigma})
. Since

TM=\mathcal{D}\oplus\mathrm{span}\{X,\,Y\}

and
g(X,Y)=g(\operatorname{grad}\rho,\operatorname{grad}\sigma)=0
(the eigenspaces of
A
are
g
-orthogonal, see Lemma
21
), then
X(\rho)=g(X,\operatorname{grad}\rho)\neq 0
and
Y(\sigma)=g(Y,\operatorname{grad}\sigma)\neq 0
(otherwise the metric would be degenerate).
Taking into account that both
X
and
Y
are isotropic, i.e.,
g(X,X)=g(Y,Y)=0
, then

\left(\mathrm{span}\{X,\,Y\}\right)^{\bot}=\mathrm{span}\{X,\,Y\}\,.

Now we prove that
\mathrm{span}\{X,\,Y\}
is integrable.

Let
Z
be an arbitrary section of
\mathrm{span}\{X,\,Y\}
. Then,

g\left(\nabla^{g}_{Z}X,X\right)=Z\,g\left(X,X\right)-g\left(\nabla^{g}_{Z}X,X\right)\quad\Rightarrow\quad g\left(\nabla^{g}_{Z}X,X\right)=0\,.

Similarly,
g\left(\nabla^{g}_{Z}Y,Y\right)=0
.
Furthermore, we have that

\sigma\;g\left(\nabla^{g}_{Z}Y,X\right)=\sigma\Big(Z\big(g(Y,X)\big)-g\left(Y,\nabla^{g}_{Z}X\right)\Big)=-g\left(\sigma Y,\nabla^{g}_{Z}X\right)=\\
-g\left(AY,\nabla^{g}_{Z}X\right)=-g\left(Y,A(\nabla^{g}_{Z}X)\right)=g\Big(Y,\left(\nabla^{g}_{Z}A\right)(X)-\nabla^{g}_{Z}(AX)\Big)=\\
g\Big(Y,\left(Z^{\flat}\otimes\Lambda+\Lambda^{\flat}\otimes Z-(\mathcal{T}Z)^{\flat}\otimes\mathcal{T}\Lambda-(\mathcal{T}\Lambda)^{\flat}\otimes\mathcal{T}Z\right)(X)-\nabla^{g}_{Z}(\rho X)\Big)\overset{(*)}{=}\\
-\rho\;g\left(Y,\nabla^{g}_{Z}X\right)=\rho\;g\left(\nabla^{g}_{Z}Y,X\right)

(the step
(*)
holds true as
g(X,Z)=g(Y,Z)=0
).

Thus, since
\sigma\neq\rho
, we have that

g\left(\nabla^{g}_{Z}Y,X\right)=0
, that implies

g\left(\nabla^{g}_{Z}X,Y\right)=0
. Hence, we can conclude that
\mathrm{span}\{X,\,Y\}
is totally geodesic, then integrable.

Up to rescaling
X
and
Y
by suitable functions, we can suppose
[X,Y]=0
. Now, let
\Sigma
be a leaf of
\mathrm{span}\{X,\,Y\}
. Let us denote by
X^{\mathrm{ext}},Y^{\mathrm{ext}}
, respectively, the extensions of

and
Y|_{\Sigma}
along the para-holomorphic Killing vector fields

\mathcal{T}V_{1}
,
\mathcal{T}V_{2}
(cfr. Corollary
13
and Proposition
14
). This way, we obtain four linearly independent vector fields

\mathcal{T}V_{1},\mathcal{T}V_{2},X^{\mathrm{ext}},Y^{\mathrm{ext}}

(86)

that mutually commute.

Since
\mathcal{T}V_{1}
and
\mathcal{T}V_{2}
are para-holomorphic vector fields, in view also of the fact that

and

and
Y^{\mathrm{ext}}\in\Gamma(T^{+})

(87)

Since
\mathcal{T}V_{1}
and
\mathcal{T}V_{2}

0=g(X,Y)|_{\Sigma}=g(X^{\mathrm{ext}},Y^{\mathrm{ext}})=f_{1}h_{2}X(\rho)+h_{1}f_{2}Y(\sigma).

(88)

In the remaining part of the present section, we shall denote by

(x^{1},x^{2},x^{3},x^{4})

a local system of coordinates where the vector fields (

\partial_{1}=\operatorname{grad}\rho-\operatorname{grad}\sigma,\quad\partial_{2}=\sigma\operatorname{grad}\rho-\rho\operatorname{grad}\sigma,\quad\partial_{3}=X^{\mathrm{ext}},\qquad\partial_{4}=Y^{\mathrm{ext}}\,.

Now we compute the components of the metric
g
in the aforementioned system of coordinates. Note that

g(\operatorname{grad}\rho,\partial_{1})=g(\operatorname{grad}\rho,\partial_{2})=g(\operatorname{grad}\rho,\partial_{4})=g(\operatorname{grad}\sigma,\partial_{1})=g(\operatorname{grad}\sigma,\partial_{2})=g(\operatorname{grad}\sigma,\partial_{3})=0\,,

implying

from which, in view of (

g=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}0&0&\rho_{x^{3}}&-\sigma_{x^{4}}\\
0&0&\sigma\,\rho_{x^{3}}&-\rho\,\sigma_{x^{4}}\\
\rho_{x^{3}}&\sigma\,\rho_{x^{3}}&0&0\\
-\sigma_{x^{4}}&-\rho\,\sigma_{x^{4}}&0&0\\

and, in view of (

Proposition 35

is an arbitrary function such that
F_{x^{4}}\neq 0
, we obtain

\frac{F_{\varphi\varphi}F_{x^{2}\varphi}-F_{\varphi}F_{x^{2}\varphi\varphi}}{F_{\varphi}^{2}}=-\left(\frac{F_{\varphi\varphi}}{F_{\varphi}}\right)_{x^{2}}=0

that gives

F(x^{2},\varphi(x^{3},x^{4}))=\alpha(\varphi(x^{3},x^{4}))\beta(x^{2})+\gamma(x^{2})

with
\alpha,\beta,\gamma
arbitrary functions. A direct computation shows that, if
\lambda=0
and
F
is given by the above formula, the curvature tensor of
g
is zero.

With similar computations one can show that, under the hypothesis that
g
is Einstein, i.e., in this particular case, flat, also the metrics (
4
), with
\hat{g}
given by (
9
), are flat.
∎

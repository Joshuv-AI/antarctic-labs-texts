Development of program requirements for obesity medicine fellowship training in the U.S.
Authors: Stacy L. Schmidt, Amin Sabet, Denise H. Wong, Adam H. Gilden, Edmond P. Wickham, Sarah Raatz, Claudia K. Fox, Kimberly A. Gudzune
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundSubspecialty fellowship training programs typically adhere to program requirements for graduate medical education for their field, which are established by accreditation organizations. However, US-based obesity medicine fellowships currently lack this guidance. Therefore, our objective was to develop and validate subspecialty fellowship program requirements for both adult and pediatric obesity medicine in the US.MethodsA work group from the Obesity Medicine Fellowship Council (OMFC) was convened to define program requirements for obesity medicine (OM) fellowships, including standards for curricular and clinical experiences during training. Draft program requirements for adult OM and pediatric OM fellowships were developed through an iterative consensus group process with repeated cycles of drafting and revising by an expert work group who then gained input from the American Board of Obesity Medicine and leadership from all OMFC-recognized fellowship programs. The draft documents were subsequently revised and then validated through a national survey to all US-based OM fellowship programs, where adult OM fellowships evaluated the adult OM program requirements and pediatric OM fellowships evaluated the pediatric OM program requirements. Respondents were asked to ‘approve’/’not approve’ each proposed section with space for comments. An agreement of at least 75% among respondents was set as the threshold a priori for retaining a program requirement.ResultsRepresentatives from all adult and pediatric OM fellowship programs participated in the survey (n = 31). All items met the minimum threshold of ≥75% approving the requirement for both adult and pediatric OM fellowship programs. Comments for revisions on specific elements were primarily related to changes in language and formatting rather than conceptual underpinnings.ConclusionA group consensus method strengthened by inclusion of a national survey of OM fellowship program directors resulted in carefully-developed and broadly-vetted program requirements. These OM fellowship program requirements offer educators and trainees a roadmap to develop comprehensive OM training.

Introduction

Despite the high US prevalence of obesity [1], obesity education is lacking throughout physicians’ education and training experiences [2]. The clinical presentation of obesity varies widely, where some individuals may have limited sequelae of excess adiposity whereas others have severe disease with multiple complications [3]. Given this clinical spectrum and an expanding range of therapeutic options, individuals with severe obesity may benefit from treatment by subspecialty physicians in obesity medicine – a paradigm similar to treatment of other chronic diseases. For example, complicated type 2 diabetes (T2D) is often referred to endocrinology, whereas uncomplicated T2D may typically be managed in primary care. Subspecialty physicians traditionally complete fellowships as part of their training to advance their clinical expertise and engage in scholarly activities. In general, research has found that fellowship-trained physicians deliver higher quality care, adhere to guidelines, and achieve better results than physicians without this training [[4], [5], [6]].

Since 2018, the Obesity Medicine Fellowship Council (OMFC) has been supporting obesity medicine fellowship programs and trainees [7]. The OMFC currently supports over 30 obesity medicine (OM) fellowship programs, and OM fellowship training is one pathway to certification by the American Board of Obesity Medicine (ABOM) [8]. OMFC includes both adult- and pediatric-focused OM fellowship programs. To date, one adult OM fellowship has published their multi-phase curriculum development process [9]. A 2023 survey of all OM fellowships found that most programs prioritized outpatient clinical experiences, promoted academic productivity, delivered formal education, and provided mentorship [10]. However, fellowship programs differed regarding the degree of supervision, type and amount of independent continuity clinic, institutional support, and faculty involvement. These results suggest that OM fellowships may benefit from establishing standards regarding their program requirements.

This article describes the development of program requirements for both adult OM and pediatric OM fellowships with the goal of setting standards for training across the US. This work is an essential first step in developing OM subspecialty training and will provide the foundation for future work including the development of OM fellowship milestones and entrustable professional activities.

Methods

Development of the OM fellowship program requirements consisted of 4 phases: 1) developing draft documents among a work group of current and former OM fellowship directors; 2) engaging key stakeholder groups to solicit feedback on the draft documents; 3) surveying US-based OM fellowship directors to validate the program requirements for adult OM and pediatric OM fellowship programs; and 4) using the survey results to refine the final program requirements. Development of draft program requirements by expert work group

The need to develop program requirements for OM fellowship was identified in November 2024 at the annual OMFC business meeting at the ObesityWeek national conference. To develop the program requirements, a work group of content experts used a consensus building process through iterative group discussions beginning in February 2025. The work group initially included 4 physician educators in adult OM (AS, DHW, AHG, KAG), which was then expanded to include 3 additional physician educators in pediatric OM (EPW, SR, CKF). All members had served or were currently serving as an OM fellowship director, participated in OM educational initiatives at the local or national levels, and were OMFC members. The work group was supported by the OMFC coordinating director (SLS).

Through monthly virtual meetings, the work group established the processes for developing the program requirements. The approach was modeled after processes described for other subspecialty training programs [11,12]. The group identified and reviewed program requirements from the Accreditation Council for Graduate Medical Education (ACGME) for related medical subspecialties (e.g., endocrinology, cardiology, gastroenterology) with the goal of identifying typical elements outlined in these documents for US-based fellowships. The work group also identified and agreed upon the following foundational OM documents: 2019 Obesity Medicine Educational Collaborative (OMEC) Competencies [13,14], 2023 ACGME-International Advanced Specialty Program Requirements for Obesity Medicine/Bariatric Medicine [15], 2023 survey of OM fellowship programs [10], 2024 OMFC Obesity Medicine Core Curriculum Guidelines for Subspecialty Fellowship Programs [15], and 2025 ABOM Content Outline [16,17]. In addition, a member of the work group (AS) met with former OM fellows to gather information and insights about their training experiences to better understand differences among programs from trainees’ perspectives (n = 4). To facilitate the development process, the work group agreed that a dyad (SLS and KAG) would compare, combine, and distill information from all these source materials to create an initial draft document outlining adult OM fellowship program requirements. Over a series of months, the work group applied an iterative consensus-building process that included reviewing all elements of this document, discussing concerns and agreeing upon revisions, as appropriate, to create draft program requirements for adult OM fellowships. The draft document included elements that must be included (required elements) as well as other elements that ideally should be included (recommended elements), which is similar to the approach adopted by ACGME and ACGME-International. The work group shared the draft adult OM fellowship program requirements with all OMFC members as well as the ABOM board of directors. Each organization provided feedback through online comments as well as during in-person meetings in October–November 2025. The work group reconvened to review feedback from these organizations, which resulted in revisions to create the adult OM fellowship program requirements.

With the expertise of the pediatric OM fellowship program directors, the work group modified the adult OM fellowship program requirements to meet the needs of pediatric OM fellowship programs. These pediatric OM fellowship requirements were revised and electronically distributed to the pediatric OM fellowship directors for additional feedback. The work group revised the document in response to their comments to create the pediatric OM fellowship program requirements.

Prior to use in the national validation survey, the work group edited both documents for consistency of style and formatting.

Validation survey

Sample and recruitment

In February 2026, an electronic survey was distributed to the leadership of all current US-based OM fellowships recognized by the OMFC to solicit feedback and determine approval status of key program requirements. OMFC-recognized programs were selected because of their experience providing advanced education and training in obesity medicine. These fellowship leaders would also be invested in the validation of these program requirements given their future applicability to their own fellowship programs. Adult OM fellowships evaluated the adult OM program requirements, and pediatric OM fellowships evaluated the pediatric OM program requirements.

Using the OMFC database, the OMFC coordinator sent out an e-mail to the leadership of each US-based fellowship program – 27 adult OM and 4 pediatric OM fellowship programs – which directed the leaders to submit only one survey response on behalf of the program. The survey was administered through a secure online platform (SurveyMonkey). A reminder e-mail with the survey link was sent weekly for four weeks at which time the survey closed. Of note, one Canada-based OM fellowship program is recognized by the OMFC that focuses on obstetric OM, and therefore, their responses were excluded from this US-based study of adult and pediatric OM fellowship programs.

Measures

In the online survey, each program requirements document was divided into sections for review. For general content related to fellowship training (e.g., Definition of Graduate Medical Education, Well-Being, or Clinical Responsibilities, Teamwork, and Transitions of Care) that aligned with ACGME standards, fellowship programs were asked to acknowledge this component rather than vote to approve the content. For content specific to OM fellowship training, respondents were asked to indicate whether they approved or did not approve each section. If the respondent did not approve a section, they were provided with a comment box to explain their concerns or rationale for not approving the requirement.

The survey did not capture demographics about the fellowship programs, as the OMFC collects this information from all programs as part of their ongoing database of programs. This database includes programs’ current OM scope of practice (e.g., adult OM, pediatric OM) and other program characteristics (e.g., location, number of fellowship positions).

Statistical analysis

Descriptive analyses were performed for all variables. Response frequency was calculated for all sections for both adult OM and pediatric OM fellowship program requirements to assess agreement rates. An agreement of at least 75% among respondents was set a priori, as the threshold for approving a section on the final program requirements documents.

Both adult OM and pediatric OM fellowships approved the following definition of the subspecialty: Obesity medicine physicians and pediatric obesity medicine physicians provide respectful, evidence-based, effective care to patients with or at-risk of obesity and its complications. Obesity medicine physicians and pediatric obesity medicine physicians often serve as clinical leaders of an interdisciplinary team involving nutrition, physical activity, and psychological support, in addition to pharmacological management and periprocedural care. Obesity medicine physicians and pediatric obesity medicine physicians manage obesity-related complications as well as engage in obesity prevention activities. Both adult OM and pediatric OM programs approved the requirement that fellowship training in this subspecialty must be at least 12 months in length.

Both adult OM and pediatric OM fellowships approved competencies in the domains of 1) Professionalism, 2) Patient Care and Procedural Skills, 3) Medical Knowledge, 4) Practice-Based Learning and Improvement, 5) Interpersonal and Communication Skills, and 6) Systems-Based Practice. For example, demonstrating ethical behavior and integrity when counseling patients with overweight/obesity as well as their families was a Professionalism competency, while eliciting a comprehensive obesity-focused medical history that includes weight, nutrition, eating behavior, physical activity, sleep, medications, and social drivers of health was a Patient Care and Procedural Skills competency. Supplemental Tables 1 and 2 contain the complete list of subspecialty-specific educational competencies for adult and pediatric OM, respectively.

Both adult OM and pediatric OM fellowships approved that there must be an obesity medicine specialty clinic available for dedicated clinical experiences in obesity medicine. The clinical experiences must include longitudinal obesity medicine specialty clinic, where the fellow is primarily managing patients. Clinical experiences must also include nutrition, psychology and mental health, physical activity, metabolic-bariatric surgery, and electives in disciplines related to obesity medicine (Table 3). Interprofessional and multidisciplinary experiences must also be included. Adult OM fellowship programs should offer clinical experiences in group visits, metabolic-bariatric endoscopy, and pediatric OM, while pediatric OM fellowship programs should offer clinical experiences in group visits and adult OM. Additional details regarding these clinical experiences, such as training hours, content, and supervision, are available in Supplemental Tables 1 and 2

Both adult OM and pediatric OM fellowships approved requirements regarding the fellowship program staffing and resources, regularly-scheduled educational activities, and scholarly activity (Table 3). Requirements regarding the evaluation of fellows, including the use of a clinical competency committee, evaluation of faculty, as well as evaluation of the program, including the use of a program evaluation committee, were also approved. Additional details on these aspects are available in Supplemental Tables 1 and 2

While all requirements met the threshold for approval, some fellowship programs provided comments for revisions on specific elements. These comments were primarily related to changes in language and formatting rather than conceptual underpinnings. All comments were reviewed by the OMFC work group. Minor changes were made based on this feedback, as appropriate, that resulted in final program requirements documents for both adult OM and pediatric OM fellowships. These final documents are available online from the OMFC (https://omfellowship.org/program-guidelines/).

Discussion

In this article, we describe the process used to develop program requirements for both adult and pediatric OM fellowship training in the US. OMFC used an iterative consensus group process with repeated cycles of engagement of an expert work group as well as input from ABOM and all OMFC members. All program requirements met the threshold (≥75% of programs) to receive approval during the validation survey where all 27 adult and all 4 pediatric OM fellowship programs participated. Notably, core curricular requirements, including subspecialty-specific educational competencies and clinical experiences, exceeded 90% approval for nearly all items included, which suggests strong alignment across programs. These results represent the first-ever program requirements for US-based OM fellowships.

As a next step, the OMFC will establish a timeline for adoption of these standards among all OM fellowship programs it recognizes and will create resources to help facilitate adoption of these standards. For example, OMFC has already held an educational session open to all members regarding the new evaluation requirements, specifically on best practices related to clinical competency committees and program evaluation committees. OMFC is also working to develop a self-assessment checklist for fellowship directors to use to examine whether their program meets requirements and identify areas that need improvement.

Establishing OM fellowship program requirements is an essential first step in advancing OM subspecialty training. Now that these standards are approved, OMFC intends to use a similar consensus group process to develop adult and pediatric OM fellowship milestones as well as entrustable professional activities (EPAs). ACGME has established milestones as assessment tools in a competency-based medical education framework [18]. Milestones describe the performance levels that fellows are expected to demonstrate for skills, knowledge, and behaviors – they represent observable behaviors and other attributes along a developmental continuum allowing faculty to assess trainees’ performance. Prior research has found that ACGME milestones are reliable for tracking individualized progress over time across all competencies among family medicine residents [19], and general surgery residents and faculty have viewed the adoption of milestones positively [20]. EPAs are observable, routine activities that a physician should be able to perform safely and effectively to meet the needs of their patients [21]. EPAs are not an alternative to milestones but aid the translation of milestones into clinical practice and have been used by program directors to determine the level of supervision required by trainees [22].

Limitations

This work has limitations. Our sample was limited to US-based OM fellowship programs, and most programs focused on adult OM. As additional pediatric OM fellowship programs are established, the pediatric OM requirements may need to be revisited to ensure that they meet the needs of the field. As additional OM fellowships are established in Canada and additional obstetric OM fellowships are established, program requirements will also need to be developed for these groups. While we used a multi-step process to establish the OM fellowship program requirements, future research will be needed to determine the feasibility of implementing these requirements within US-based OM fellowships as well as monitor outcomes among fellows once these requirements have been implemented (e.g., ABOM exam scores). Additional collaboration between OMFC and ABOM may be required to determine how these program requirements will impact the fellowship candidates’ eligibility for the ABOM exam through the fellowship pathway.

Conclusions

With this work, the subspecialities of adult and pediatric OM now have program requirements for US-based fellowships. This builds upon the previously defined standards for OM fellowship internationally established by the ACGME-International in 2023 [23].

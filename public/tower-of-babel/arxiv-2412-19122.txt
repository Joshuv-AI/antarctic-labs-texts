On skein invariants
Igor Nikonov

Abstract
A knot invariant is called skein if it is determined by a finite number of skein relations. In the paper we discuss some basic properties of skein invariants and mention some known examples of skein invariants.

Keywords
: knot, skein relation

1
Introduction

In the late 1960s John Conway
[
5
]
noted that the renown Alexander polynomial
\nabla
can be defined using a simple relation on values of the polynomial on diagrams which differ locally from each other:

\displaystyle\nabla(\raisebox{-0.25pt}{\includegraphics[width=14.22636pt]{skcrro.eps}})-\nabla(\raisebox{-0.25pt}{\includegraphics[width=14.22636pt]{skcrlo.eps}})=z\nabla(\raisebox{-0.25pt}{\includegraphics[width=14.22636pt]{skcrvo.eps}}).

Conway used the term
skein
for this type of relations.

In fifteen years, other polynomial invariants (like Jones
[
13
]
, HOMFLY-PT
[
8
,
28
]
) appeared which can also be defined using skein relations. At about the same time, L.H. Kauffman
[
16
]
gave a description of Arf invariant using pass move. It became clear that a new type of invariants had appeared in knot theory. The value of such an invariant of a knot is the class of the knot in some quotient of the set of knots generated by skein relations. The next step was made by J.H. Przytycki
[
27
]
and V.G. Turaev
[
34
]
who defined the notion of skein module. A review of known results on skein modules can be found in
[
4
]
.

The aim of the paper is to recall some known examples of skein invariants and discuss their basic properties.

The paper is organized as follows. We start with definitions of knot diagrams and moves and skein invariants. In Section
3
we give the formal definition of skein invariants and consider some structures on them. Section
3
contains some examples of skein invariants. We conclude the paper with a list of open problems concerning skein invariants.

2
Knot diagrams

Definition 1
.

Let
F
be an oriented compact connected surface. A
tangle diagram

D
is an embedded finite graph with vertices of valences
1
and
4
such that the set
\partial D
of vertices of valence
1
coincides with
D\cap\partial F
and the vertices of valence
4
(called
crossings
) carry additional structure: structure of a classical or a virtual crossing (Fig.
1
). A diagram without virtual crossings is called
classical
.

Diagrams are considered up to isotopy fixed on the boundary.

Figure 1:
A classical and a virtual crossings

Edges incident to a
4
-valent vertex of a tangle diagram split naturally in two pairs of
opposite
edges. Correspondence between opposite edges induces an equivalence relation on the set of edges of the diagram. Equivalence classes of this relation are called
(unicursal) components
of the diagram. A component is
long
if it contains vertices of valency
1
(in this case the edges of the component form a path in the diagram), otherwise the component is called
closed
(in this case the edges of the component form a cycle), see Fig.
2
.

We say that a diagram is
oriented
if all its components are oriented.

Figure 2:
A diagram with one closed and one long component

A diagram without long components is a
link
diagram, a link diagram with one component is a
knot
diagram. A diagram with one component which is long, is called a
long knot
diagram.

Definition 2
.

An
n
-tangle
is a diagram
D
in the standard disk
\mathbb{D}^{2}
such that
\partial D=X
where
X\subset\mathbb{D}^{2}
is a fixed counterclockwise enumerated set with
2n
elements. The set of
n
-tangles is denoted
\mathcal{T}_{n}
, and
\mathcal{T}_{n}^{+}
is the set of oriented
n
-tangles. An
n
-tangle may have crossings of any type (classical or virtual, Fig.
1
).

Figure 3:
A 2-tangle

Definition 3
.

A
local move
is a pair
M=(T_{1},T_{2})
of
n
-tangles such that
\partial T_{1}=\partial T_{2}
.

Given a move
M=(T_{1},T_{2})
, a diagram
D
and a disk
B\subset F
such that
T=D\cap B
is homeomorphic to
T_{1}
, one gets a new diagram
R_{M}(D,T)
by replacing the subtangle
T
with the subtangle homeomorphic to
T_{2}
.

Figure 4:
Application of a local move
(T_{1},T_{2})
to a diagram

Remark 1
.

We will also consider local moves
(T_{1},T_{2})
with
T_{1},T_{2}\in A[\mathcal{T}_{n}]
where
A
is a coefficient ring. In this case the result of the local move applied to a diagram is a linear combination of diagrams with coefficients in
A
. The moves
(T_{1},T_{2})
with
T_{1},T_{2}\in\mathcal{T}_{n}

We can use the notion of moves to give a combinatorial definition of knots.

Definition 4
.

A
(classical) knot
(link, tangle) in a surface

is an equivalence class of classical knot (link, tangle) diagrams in

modulo the classical Reidemeister moves
\Omega_{1},\Omega_{2},\Omega_{3}

A

(link, tangle) is an equivalence class of virtual diagrams in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
modulo the classical and virtual Reidemeister moves
\Omega_{1}
,
\Omega_{2}
,
\Omega_{3}
,
V\Omega_{1}
,
V\Omega_{2}
,
V\Omega_{3}
,

A

(link, tangle) is an equivalence class of virtual diagrams in
\mathbb{R}^{2}

Skein invariants

Definition 5
.

Let
\mathscr{K}
be the set of classical (virtual, welded) knots (links, tangles),
A
a ring and
\mathcal{M}=\{(T_{i},T^{\prime}_{i})\}_{i\in\mathcal{I}}
, where
T_{i},T^{\prime}_{i}\in A[\mathcal{T}_{n_{i}}]
,
n_{i}\in\mathbb{N}
,
i\in\mathcal{I}
, a set of moves. Given two
A
-linear combinations of diagrams
D
and
D^{\prime}
, we say that
D
and
D^{\prime}
are
equivalent modulo the set of moves
\mathcal{M}
if there exists a sequence of
A
-linear combinations of diagrams
D=D_{1},D^{\prime}_{1},\dots,D_{l},D^{\prime}_{l}=D^{\prime}
such that
D_{k}
and
D^{\prime}_{k}
,
k=1,\dots,l
, differ by Reidemeister moves and diagram isotopies (i.e. define the same
A
-linear combination of knots in
A[\mathscr{K}]
), and
D_{k+1}
is the result of application of some local move
M\in\mathcal{M}
to
D^{\prime}_{k}
,
k=1,\dots,l-1
. For equivalent
D
and
D^{\prime}
we use the notation
D\sim_{\mathcal{M}}D^{\prime}
.

The set
A[\mathscr{K}]/\mathcal{M}
of equivalence classes of
A
-linear combinations of diagrams modulo
\mathcal{M}
is called the
skein module
induced by the set of moves
\mathcal{M}
.

Definition 6
.

Let
\mathscr{K}
be the set of knots (links, tangles) and
A
a ring. A knot invariant
I\colon\mathscr{K}\to X
with values in a set
X
is called a
skein invariant
if there exists a finite set of moves
\mathcal{M}
and an injection
f\colon A[\mathscr{K}]/\mathcal{M}\to X
such that
I=f\circ p
, where
p\colon\mathscr{K}\to A[\mathscr{K}]/\mathcal{M}
is the natural projection.

Remark 2
.

If the moves in
\mathcal{M}
are monomial then in Definition
6
we can consider the map
f\colon\mathscr{K}/\mathcal{M}\to X
.

The first examples of skein invariants were polynomial invariants of oriented classical links in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.

.

For an oriented link

The Jones polynomial of an oriented link

Below we will focus on monomial moves on knots.

3.1
Existence of non-skein knot invariants

First of all, note that any invariant is ‘‘infinitely skein’’.

Proposition 1
.

For any knot invariant
I\colon\mathscr{K}\to X
there exists a set of moves
\mathcal{M}
and an injection
f\colon\mathscr{K}/\mathcal{M}\to X
such that
I=f\circ p
, where
p\colon\mathscr{K}\to\mathscr{K}/\mathcal{M}
is the natural projection.

Proof.

Indeed, we can take
\mathcal{M}=\{(K_{1},K_{2})\in\mathscr{K}\times\mathscr{K}\mid I(K_{1})=I(K_{2})\}
.
∎

Do there exist non-skein invariants? Yes. The argument is as follows. The set of moves as well as the set of skein invariants is countable. But the set of knot invariants has the cardinality of the continuum.

Let us give a more concrete construction.

Example 2

(Non-skein invariant)
.

Let
\{(T_{i},T^{\prime}_{i})\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
be the set of all local moves such that
\partial T_{i}=\partial T^{\prime}_{i}\neq\emptyset
. Let us construct two sequences of knots
\{\tilde{K}_{i}\}
and
\{\tilde{K}^{\prime}_{i}\}
.

Consider a move
(T_{i},T^{\prime}_{i})
. If all the closures
T_{i}\#S
and
T^{\prime}_{i}\#S
of the tangles are equal in
\mathscr{K}
then skip the move. Otherwise, take nonequivalent closures
K_{i}=T_{i}\#S
and
K^{\prime}_{i}=T^{\prime}_{i}\#S
. There exists a prime knot
K^{\prime\prime}_{i}
such that the knots
\tilde{K}_{i}=K_{i}\#K_{i}^{\prime\prime}
and
\tilde{K}^{\prime}_{i}=K^{\prime}_{i}\#K_{i}^{\prime\prime}
differ from the knots
\tilde{K}_{j}
,
\tilde{K}^{\prime}_{j}
,
j<i
.

Then any invariant
I\colon\mathscr{K}\to\mathbb{Z}_{2}
such that
f(\tilde{K}_{i})=0
and
f(\tilde{K}^{\prime}_{i})=1
, is not skein.

3.2
Degree of moves

Definition 7
.

A set of moves
\mathcal{M}
has
degree
n
if for any move
M=(T,T^{\prime})\in\mathcal{M}
the tangles
T
and
T^{\prime}
are
n
-tangles.

A skein invariant
I
has
degree
n
if it can be determined by a set of moves of degree
n
. Denote the set of skein invariant
I
of degree
n
by
\mathscr{I}_{n}
.

Let us describe skein invariants of small degrees.

3.2.1
Skein invariants of degree
0

Proposition 2
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a finite set of moves and
\bar{\mathcal{M}}\subset\mathcal{M}
the subset of moves of degree
>0
. Let
I_{\mathcal{M}}\colon\mathscr{K}\to\mathscr{K}/\mathcal{M}=X_{\mathcal{M}}
and
I_{\bar{\mathcal{M}}}\colon\mathscr{K}\to\mathscr{K}/\bar{\mathcal{M}}=X_{\bar{\mathcal{M}}}
be the corresponding skein invariants. Then there exist finite distinct subsets
S_{1},\dots,S_{k}\subset X_{\bar{\mathcal{M}}}
such that for any knots
K
,
K^{\prime}
one has
I_{\mathcal{M}}(K)=I_{\mathcal{M}}(K^{\prime})
if and only if
I_{\bar{\mathcal{M}}}(K)=I_{\bar{\mathcal{M}}}(K^{\prime})
or
I_{\bar{\mathcal{M}}}(K),I_{\bar{\mathcal{M}}}(K^{\prime})\in S_{i}
for some
1\leq i\leq k
.

Proof.

Since
\bar{\mathcal{M}}\subset\mathcal{M}
, there is a natural surjection
p\colon X_{\bar{\mathcal{M}}}\to X_{\mathcal{M}}
. The map
p
is determined by the set
\mathcal{M}\setminus\bar{\mathcal{M}}=\{(K_{i},K^{\prime}_{i})\}
of moves of degree
0
, where
K_{i}
and
K^{\prime}_{i}
are some knots. These moves glue the values
I_{\bar{\mathcal{M}}}(K_{i})
and
I_{\bar{\mathcal{M}}}(K^{\prime}_{i})\in X_{\bar{\mathcal{M}}}
. Then we set the subsets
S_{i}
to be the preimages
p^{-1}(x)
,
x\in X_{\mathcal{M}}
, which contain two or more elements.
∎

Corollary 1
.

Let
I\in\mathscr{I}_{0}
. Then there exist finite distinct subsets
S_{1},\dots,S_{k}\subset\mathscr{K}
such that for any knots
K
,
K^{\prime}
one has
I(K)=I(K^{\prime})
if and only if
K=K^{\prime}
or
K,K^{\prime}\in S_{i}
for some
1\leq i\leq k
.

Proof.

Indeed, in this case
\bar{\mathcal{M}}=\emptyset
and
X_{\bar{\mathcal{M}}}=\mathscr{K}
.
∎

Definition 8
.

A set of moves
\mathcal{M}
is
reduced
if
\mathcal{M}=\bar{\mathcal{M}}
.

3.2.2
Additivity of skein invariants

Definition 9
.

A knot invariant
I\colon\mathscr{K}\to X
is called
additive
if
X
has a commutative monoid structure and
I(K\#K^{\prime})=I(K)+I(K^{\prime})
for any
K,K^{\prime}\in\mathscr{K}
.

Theorem 1
.

Let
I\colon\mathscr{K}\to X
be a reduced skein knot invariant and
X=I(\mathscr{K})
. Then
I
is additive.

Lemma 1
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a reduced set of moves. Then
K_{1}\sim_{\mathcal{M}}K_{2}
implies
K_{1}\#K^{\prime}\sim_{\mathcal{M}}K_{2}\#K^{\prime}
for any
K^{\prime}\in\mathscr{K}
.

Proof.

The condition
K_{1}\sim_{\mathcal{M}}K_{2}
means there is a sequence of diagrams
K_{1}=\tilde{K}_{1},\tilde{K}^{\prime}_{1},\dots,\tilde{K}_{l},\tilde{K}^{\prime}_{l}=K_{2}
such that the diagrams
\tilde{K}_{i}
and
\tilde{K}^{\prime}_{i}
are connected by a sequence of Reidmeister moves, and
\tilde{K}^{\prime}_{i}
and
\tilde{K}_{i+1}
are connected by a move
M_{i}\in\mathcal{M}
. Since the move
M_{i}
has positive degree, there is an arc in
\tilde{K}^{\prime}_{i}
which lies outside the move. We place the knot
K^{\prime}
on this arc and get diagrams
\hat{K}^{\prime}_{i}
and
\hat{K}_{i+1}
connected by the move
M_{i}
. Since the diagrams
\tilde{K}_{i}
and
\tilde{K}^{\prime}_{i}
present the same knot, the diagrams
\hat{K}_{i}=\tilde{K}_{i}\#K^{\prime}
and
\hat{K}^{\prime}_{i}=\tilde{K}_{i}\#K^{\prime}
are connected by a sequence of Reidemester moves. Thus, the sequence
K_{1}\#K^{\prime}=\hat{K}_{1},\hat{K}^{\prime}_{1},\dots\hat{K}_{l},\hat{K}^{\prime}_{l}=K_{2}\#K^{\prime}
establishes the equivalence
K_{1}\#K^{\prime}\sim_{\mathcal{M}}K_{2}\#K^{\prime}
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a reduced set of moves which determines the invariant
M
. Then the coefficient set
X
is identified with the set
\mathscr{K}/\mathcal{M}
of equivalence classes of knots modulo the moves of
\mathcal{M}
. Define a binary operation on
X
by the formula
[K_{1}]+[K_{2}]=[K_{1}\#K_{2}]
,
K_{1},K_{2}\in\mathscr{K}
. The operation is well defined, because for any
K_{1}\sim_{\mathcal{M}}K_{2}
and
K^{\prime}_{1}\sim_{\mathcal{M}}K^{\prime}_{2}
we have
K_{1}\#K_{2}\sim_{\mathcal{M}}K_{1}\#K^{\prime}_{2}\sim_{\mathcal{M}}K^{\prime}_{1}\#K^{\prime}_{2}
by Lemma
1
. The operation defines on
X
a monoid structure because the operation
\#
of connected sum does. The equality
I(K\#K^{\prime})=I(K)+I(K^{\prime})
follows from the definition of the binary operation.
∎

Remark 3
.

The set of additive knot invariants has the cardinality of the continuum. Hence, there are non-skein additive invariants.

3.2.3
Skein invariants of degree
1

Example 3
.

Let
P
be a finite subset of the set
\mathscr{P}
of prime knots and
\phi\colon P\to M
be a map to a commutative monoid
M
. Let us define a knot invariant
I_{P,\phi}\colon\mathscr{K}\to M\times\mathbb{Z}[\mathbb{N}]
as follows. Enumerate the prime knots
\mathscr{P}=\{K_{1},K_{2},\dots\}
so that
P=\{K_{1},\dots,K_{n}\}
. For a knot
K=\sum_{i}a_{i}K_{i}\in\mathscr{K}
set

I_{P,\phi}=\left(\sum_{i=1}a_{i}\phi(K_{i}),\sum_{i=n+1}^{\infty}a_{i}\cdot[i-n]\right).

Then
I_{P,\phi}\in\mathscr{I}_{1}
.
Indeed, consider the knots in
P
as long knots. The map
\phi
extends to a monoid homomorphism
\phi\colon\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}[P]\to M
. The image
\mathrm{im}\phi
is finitely generated. By Redey’s theorem
[
30
]

\mathrm{im}\phi
is finitely presented, hence,
\mathrm{im}\phi\simeq\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}[P]/\sim
where the equivalence
\sim
is generated by a finite set of relations
T_{i}\sim T^{\prime}_{i}
. The linear combinations
T_{i},T^{\prime}_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}[P]
can be identified with connected sums of prime knots from
P
. Then
I_{P,\phi}
is generated by the set of moves

\mathcal{M}=\{(T_{i},T^{\prime}_{i})\}\subset\mathscr{I}_{1}.

Proposition 3
.

Let
I\in\mathscr{I}_{1}
. Then
I
is equivalent to the invariant
I_{P,\phi}
for some finite
P\subset\mathscr{P}
and
\phi\colon P\to M
in the following sense: there is a bijection
f\colon\mathrm{im}I_{P,\phi}\to\mathrm{im}I
such that
I=f\circ I_{P,\phi}
.

Proof.

The invariant
I
is determined by a set
\mathcal{M}=\{(T_{i},T^{\prime}_{i})\}_{i=1}^{l}
of moves of degree
1
. Then the tangles
T_{i},T^{\prime}_{i}
are long knots. Let
P=\{K_{1},\dots,K_{n}\}
be the set of prime knots which divide one of these tangles and
A=\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}[P]
. Let
\sim
be the congruence in
A
generated by the relations
T_{i}\sim T^{\prime}_{i}
and
M=A/\sim
the quotient monoid. Denote the natural projection
P\to M
by
\phi
. Then
I
is equivalent to the skein invariant
I_{P,\phi}
.
∎

3.2.4
Stabilization of degree

Proposition 4
.

For any
n\geq 1

\mathscr{I}_{n}\subset\mathscr{I}_{n+1}
.

Proof.

Given a move
M=(T,T^{\prime})
of degree
n>0
, we can assign to it a move
M^{+}
of degree
n+1
called the
stabilization of the move
M
(Fig.
6
). It is easy to see that the move
M
implies the move
M^{+}
.

Figure 6:
Stabilization of a move

On the other hand, the move
M
can be expressed using the move
M^{+}
(Fig.
7
).

Figure 7:
Destabilization of a move

Let
\mathcal{M}=\{M_{i}\}\subset\mathscr{I}_{n}
. Consider the set of moves
\mathcal{M}^{+}=\{M^{+}_{i}\}\subset\mathscr{I}_{n+1}
. Then the moves of
\mathcal{M}
can be expressed by moves of
\mathcal{M}^{+}
and vice versa. Hence, the skein invariants induced by the sets
\mathcal{M}
and
\mathcal{M}^{+}
are equivalent. Thus,
\mathscr{I}_{n}\subset\mathscr{I}_{n+1}
.
∎

Thus, we have an increasing filtration of skein invariants:

\mathscr{I}_{1}\subset\mathscr{I}_{2}\subset\mathscr{I}_{3}\subset\cdots.

A natural question is whether this filtration stabilizes.

3.3
Partial order on skein invariants

There is a natural partial order on the set of skein invariants.

Definition 10
.

A knot invariant
I\colon\mathscr{K}\to X
is
weaker
than a knot invariant
I^{\prime}\colon\mathscr{K}\to X^{\prime}
if there is a map
f\colon X^{\prime}\to X
such that
I=f\circ I^{\prime}
. In this case we write
I\preceq I^{\prime}
.

A set of moves
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}
is
finer
than a set of moves
\mathcal{M}
if any move
M^{\prime}\in\mathcal{M}^{\prime}
can be expressed as a sequence of moves from
\mathcal{M}
and Reidemeister moves. In this case we write
\mathcal{M}\preceq\mathcal{M}^{\prime}
.

Proposition 5
.

1. If
\mathcal{M}\preceq\mathcal{M}^{\prime}
then
I_{\mathcal{M}}\preceq I_{\mathcal{M}^{\prime}}
.

2. If
I\preceq I^{\prime}
are skein invariants then there exist sets of moves
\mathcal{M}\preceq\mathcal{M}^{\prime}
such that
I=I_{\mathcal{M}}
and
I^{\prime}=I_{\mathcal{M}^{\prime}}
.

Proof.

The first statement follows from definitions.

Let
I\preceq I^{\prime}
and
I=I_{\mathcal{M}_{0}}
and
I^{\prime}=I_{\mathcal{M}^{\prime}}
. Denote
\mathcal{M}=\mathcal{M}_{0}\cup\mathcal{M}^{\prime}
. Then
I_{\mathcal{M}_{0}}
is equivalent to
I_{\mathcal{M}}
, and
\mathcal{M}\preceq\mathcal{M}^{\prime}
.
∎

Remark 4
.

1.
I_{\mathcal{M}}\preceq I_{\mathcal{M}^{\prime}}
does not imply
\mathcal{M}\preceq\mathcal{M}^{\prime}
. For example, consider the unknotting moves
\mathcal{M}=\{\Delta\}
,
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}=\{CC\}
(Fig.
8
).

2.
\mathscr{I}
is a lower semilattice (with the meet operation given by the union of sets of moves).

3.3.2
Binary skein invariants

Definition 12
.

A nontrivial skein invariant
I
is a
weakest nontrivial invariant
if any skein invariant
I^{\prime}\prec I
is trivial (i.e. a constant map).

A skein invariant
I
is
binary
if
|\mathrm{im}I|=2
.

Proposition 6
.

A skein invariant
I
is weakest nontrivial if and only if it is binary.

Proof.

Let
I
be binary. If
I^{\prime}\prec I
then
|\mathrm{im}I^{\prime}|<|\mathrm{im}I|=2
. Hence,
|\mathrm{im}I^{\prime}|=1
, and the invariant
I^{\prime}
is trivial.

Let a skein invariant
I=I_{\mathcal{M}}
be not binary. Then
I
is not trivial and there exist knots
K
,
K^{\prime}
such that
I(K)\neq I(K^{\prime})
. Denote
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}=\mathcal{M}\cup\{(K,K^{\prime})\}
. Then
I_{\mathcal{M}^{\prime}}\prec I
and
|\mathrm{im}I_{\mathcal{M}^{\prime}}|=|\mathrm{im}I|-1>1
. Thus,
I_{\mathcal{M}^{\prime}}
is not trivial, and
I
is not a weakest nontrivial invariant.
∎

.

Any skein invariant

with a finite value set

3.4
Cardinality of skein invariant

Definition 13
.

The
cardinality

|\mathcal{M}|
of a set of moves
\mathcal{M}
is the number of moves in
\mathcal{M}
.

The
cardinality
|I|
of a skein invariant

I\colon\mathscr{K}\to X
is the minimal number of moves generating
I
:

|I|=\min\{|\mathcal{M}|\mid I\sim I_{\mathcal{M}}\}.

Remark 6
.

1.

A skein invariant
I
has cardinality
0
if and only if it is a full invariant.

2.

Skein invariants of cardinality
1
can be various:

•

The invariant generated by a
0
-degree move
(K_{1},K_{2})
, where
K_{1},K_{2}
are some knots, is almost full: it distinguishes all knots except the pair
K_{1},K_{2}
.

•

The classical knot invariant generated by the crossing change move
CC
is trivial.

•

The invariant generated by the Conway relation

\raisebox{-0.25pt}{\includegraphics[width=14.22636pt]{skcrro.eps}}-\raisebox{-0.25pt}{\includegraphics[width=14.22636pt]{skcrlo.eps}}\rightleftharpoons z\cdot\raisebox{-0.25pt}{\includegraphics[width=14.22636pt]{skcrvo.eps}}

is equivalent to the Alexander–Conway polynomial.

3.

The cardinality induces an increasing filtration of skein invariant

\mathscr{I}^{\prime}_{0}\subset\mathscr{I}^{\prime}_{1}\subset\mathscr{I}^{\prime}_{2}\subset\cdots

where
\mathscr{I}^{\prime}_{n}=\{I\in\mathscr{I}\mid|I|\leq n\}
. This filtration is infinite. Indeed, the
0
-degree invariant
I_{\mathcal{M}}
determined by a set of moves
\mathcal{M}=\{(K_{i},K^{\prime}_{i})\}_{i=1}^{n}
such that the knots
K_{i}
,
K^{\prime}_{i}
,
i=1,\dots,n
, are all distinct, has cardinality
n
.

5
Open questions

In the paper we have took first steps towards a theory of skein invariants, and there are a lot of unanswered questions. Let us list some of them.

•

Are known knot invariants such as unknotting number
u(K)
, the crossing number
c(K)
, the genus
g(K)
skein? Skeinness of the unknotting and the crossing number would imply their additivity.

•

Does the sequence of skein invariants
\mathscr{I}_{1}\subset\mathscr{I}_{2}\subset\mathscr{I}_{3}\subset\cdots
stabilize?

•

Find conditions for a set of moves to be unknotting.

•

Describe binary skein invariants.

•

Describe skein invariants of cardinality
1
.

•

Besides order or cardinality, one can filter skein invariants by complexity (number of crossings) of moves. The task is to describe skein invariants of small complexity.

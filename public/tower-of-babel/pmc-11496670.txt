Work extraction from quantum coherence in non-equilibrium environment
Authors: Maryam Hadipour, Soroush Haseli
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Ergotropy, which represents the maximum amount of work that can be extracted from a quantum system, has become a focal point of interest in the fields of quantum thermodynamics and information processing. In practical scenarios, the interaction of quantum systems with their surrounding environment is unavoidable. Recent studies have increasingly focused on analyzing open quantum systems affected by non-stationary environmental fluctuations due to their significant impact on various physical scenarios. While much research has concentrated on work extraction from these systems, it often assumes that the environmental degrees of freedom are substantial and that the environment is effectively in equilibrium. This has led us to explore work extraction from quantum systems under non-stationary environmental conditions. In this work, the dynamics of ergotropy will be investigated in a non-equilibrium environment for both Markovian and non-Markovian regime. In this study, both the coherent and incoherent parts of the ergotropy will be considered. It will be shown that for a non-equilibrium environment, the extraction of work is more efficient compared to when the environment is in equilibrium.

Introduction

Quantum thermodynamics investigates the interplay between the macroscopic principles of thermodynamics and the microscopic world governed by quantum mechanics. It plays a crucial role in understanding the fundamental energetic constraints of quantum technologies. Since many quantum tasks involve energy transfer processes, quantum thermodynamics provides a framework to quantify the maximum extractable work, termed ergotropy1, from a quantum system via unitary operations. Restricted to unitary operations, ergotropy embodies the process of extracting energy in the form of work, facilitated by the presence of an external field. On the other hand, the interaction of the system with the environment will lead to a change in the level of ergotropy. In the framework of open quantum systems2, heat and work can interchange via thermodynamic processes. Such interchange facilitates the development and experimentation of diverse quantum devices, encompassing quantum heat engines3–5, quantum refrigerators6, quantum batteries7–54. Researchers are investigating the potential of ergotropy as a resource for executing various quantum tasks, including quantum computing, quantum cryptography, and quantum simulation. The use of ergotropy as a resource for quantum applications must adhere to the energy balance dictated by the first law of thermodynamics. Alicki has introduced the quantum version of the first law of thermodynamics55. In this description, the internal energy is considered to be the average value of the Hamiltonian of the quantum system, which governs the transformations. The work, is associated with a change in the gap structure of the energy spectrum induced by a time-dependent Hamiltonian.

Identifying uniquely quantum signatures in thermodynamic settings has been a central theme in the field of quantum thermodynamics over the past decade. This includes the identification of quantum signatures in various aspect such as thermal machines56–71, work extraction protocols71–85, in fluctuations of work86–92, and in work deposition processes93–99. In the context of quantum thermodynamics, a quantum signature refers to distinctive features or phenomena that arise due to the inherently quantum nature of the system under consideration. One could confidently assert that quantum coherence is the most fundamental of all non-classical features. However, precise mathematical techniques for its quantification have only recently been developed within the field of quantum information theory100,101. So far, many studies have focused on highlighting the role of quantum coherence from the perspective of quantum thermodynamics102–112. In quantum information theory, coherence is a basis-dependent quantity that can be geometrically expressed as the distance between the state of the system and its dephased counterpart113. The notion of the quantum coherence establishes a connection with the finite-time thermodynamics of quantum systems, where relative entropy is widely used to evaluate the irreversible entropy production in closed and open quantum systems114–124. Recently, this connection has been utilized to distinguish the coherent component of entropy production in quantum dynamics125–128.

In recent decades, there has been a growing theoretical interest in studying the dynamics of open quantum systems2. According to the descriptive framework established in the classical approach by Anderson and Kubo, environmental interactions lead to stochastic fluctuations in certain observables within the quantum system129,130. The statistical attributes of these the stochastic fluctuations serve to delineate environmental state as well as the the interaction between the system and its environment. This interaction is conceptualized through the framework of noise representation. In specific contexts where the interaction between the system and its environment is strong and the environmental degrees of freedom are few, random telegraph noise (RTN), characterized by non-Gaussian statistical properties, exerts a significant influence on the dynamical evolution of quantum systems131–139. In recent studies, there has been increasing attention to the analysis of open quantum systems influenced by non-stationary environmental fluctuations, due to the significant role these phenomena play in various important physical scenarios140–143. So far, extracting work from open quantum systems interacting with their environment has been the focus of numerous studies47,144–146. However, most of these investigations assume that the environmental degrees of freedom are not negligible, and that the environment is effectively in equilibrium. In contrast, real-world scenarios may involve environments with a limited number of degrees of freedom and strong coupling between the system and its surroundings. Under such realistic conditions, examining the process of work extraction becomes critically important. This reality has motivated us to investigate work extraction from quantum systems in the presence of non-stationary environments.

Recently, the non-equilibrium feature of environments has been experimentally observed in many vital dynamical processes. In such processes, the initial state of the environment, which is disturbed from equilibrium due to the interaction with the system, cannot reach equilibrium over time. Consequently, the environment surrounding the quantum system remains out of equilibrium147–153. Furthermore, it has been shown in recent years that the non-equilibrium feature of the environment with unstable statistical properties leads to a frequency shift, which will have a significant effect on reducing decoherence effects154–157. In this work, by considering both the coherent and incoherent contributions of the ergotropy, we investigate the dynamics of ergotropy in non-equilibrium environments. In the studies conducted in this work, both Markovian and non-Markovian regimes will be considered.

Before delving into the discussion regarding the dynamics of ergotropy under this non equilibrium stochastic process, it’s preferable to address the nature of the process in terms of being Markovian or non-Markovian. In Markovian evolution, information flows continuously from the system to the environment, while in non-Markovian evolution, back-flow of information occurs from environment to system. In Markovian evolution, the future state of the system is depend only on its current state, meaning the future is independent of past states, making the evolution memory-less. Conversely, in non-Markovian evolution, the system’s future state depends on its previous states, thus making the evolution with-memory2. So far, various criteria have been introduced for measuring the degree of non-Markovianity of a quantum process164–171. Summary and results

Ergotropy represents the maximum amount of work that can be extracted from a quantum system using appropriate quantum operations. It serves as a measure of the system’s ability to convert internal energy into usable work and is crucial for optimizing quantum processes. By optimizing quantum operations, one can achieve the maximum extractable work, making ergotropy a key concept in the study of quantum thermodynamics and energy management within quantum systems. In contrast to classical thermodynamics, where work extraction is determined solely by energy differences between states, ergotropy offers a more comprehensive perspective. It not only considers energy differences but also accounts for the potential for work extraction arising from quantum coherence. Quantum coherence refers to the system’s ability to maintain superposition states, which can be leveraged to extract work. So, ergotropy is a more comprehensive measure for evaluating the capacity of quantum systems to extract work. This metric not only considers the energy differences between states but also incorporates the effects of quantum coherence. Given that isolating a quantum system from its surrounding environment is nearly impossible, the study of work extraction from open quantum systems is of significant importance. In most studies focused on work extraction from open quantum systems, the environment with which the system interacts is typically assumed to be in equilibrium with a significant degrees of freedom. In this study, we examined the process of work extraction from a quantum system interacting with a non-equilibrium environment. Our analysis specifically addresses how deviations from equilibrium in the surrounding environment influence the efficiency and feasibility of extracting work from the quantum system. By investigating these dynamics, we aim to provide a deeper understanding of work extraction mechanisms under non-equilibrium conditions, thereby contributing to the broader knowledge of quantum thermodynamics in environments that are not in equilibrium. The considered non-equilibrium noise primarily affects the coherence of the quantum system and not the population of energy levels. Therefore, the internal energy, which is determined by the populations of the energy levels (diagonal elements of the density matrix), remains largely unaffected by noise. So, due to the effect of this non-equilibrium environment on the system, the coherent part of the ergotropy vanishes over time and just its incoherent part of ergotropy unaffected. Furthermore, it was observed that increasing the non-equilibrium parameter leads to an increase in the amount of the ergotropy. In other words, it can be concluded that the extraction of work from the quantum system in a non-equilibrium environment occurs more optimally compared to an equilibrium environment. We also examined how non-Markovian effects of the environment influence the work extraction process. Our analysis revealed that work extraction is more efficiently achieved in a non-Markovian regime. This observation underscores the potential for enhanced work extraction when non-Markovian dynamics are present, suggesting that non-Markovian interactions can significantly optimize the performance of work extraction processes in quantum systems.

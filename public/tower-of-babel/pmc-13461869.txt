A new species of Mariauxiella de Chambrier & Rego, 1995 (Cestoda: Proteocephalidae) infecting the driftwood catfish Ageneiosus militaris Valenciennes (Siluriformes: Auchenipteridae) in Brazil
Authors: Philippe Vieira Alves, Hugo Lago Rocha, Reinaldo José da Silva
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe tapeworm genus Mariauxiella (Onchoproteocephalidea, Proteocephalidae) was recently revised and its species composition reappraised; however, its actual species diversity remains incompletely understood. In the present study, an integrative taxonomic approach combining morphological, ultrastructural, and molecular phylogenetic analyses was applied to proteocephalid tapeworms collected from the driftwood catfish Ageneiosus militaris (Siluriformes: Auchenipteridae) in the Upper Paraná River basin, Brazil. The evidence obtained supports the proposal of Mariauxiella saltogrande n. sp., the third species assigned to the genus. The new species can be differentiated from the most similar species, Mariauxiella brevifilis, by its body size, proglottis width, scolex shape, and the arrangement of the osmoregulatory canals in the proglottides. Phylogenetic analyses based on the partial large subunit nuclear ribosomal RNA (RNA28S) and the complete mitochondrial cytochrome c oxidase subunit I (MT-CO1) sequence data strongly supported the monophyly of Mariauxiella and recovered Mariauxiella saltogrande n. sp. as sister to M. brevifilis. The discovery of this new species expands the known diversity of Mariauxiella and provides further evidence for the usefulness of integrative taxonomy in resolving species boundaries and evolutionary relationships among Neotropical proteocephalids.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1007/s11230-026-10298-4.

Introduction

Neotropical proteocephalids (Onchoproteocephalidea, Proteocephalidae) represent the largest radiation of adult tapeworms infecting bony fishes worldwide (Scholz & Kuchta, 2022). However, their true diversity is likely much higher than currently recognized, with around 90% of the total number of species yet to be discovered in South American freshwater fishes (Scholz & Kuchta, 2022). This estimate is plausible because most representatives of the main group of teleosts harboring adult cestodes, i.e., Neotropical catfishes (Siluriformes), have never been scrutinized for tapeworm infections (Alves et al., 2017a; Scholz & Kuchta, 2022).

Beyond the still poorly known proteocephalid diversity in South American fishes, another limitation is that most genera and higher-level taxa were established solely based on morphological traits (Rego, 1994; de Chambrier et al., 2017). These characteristics have failed to reflect the evolutionary relationships within the group, as revealed by molecular phylogenetic analyses (de Chambrier et al., 2015a, 2017), and a natural classification is still pending for many genera of Proteocephalidae (Alves et al., 2025, 2026). Relying on combined morphological and molecular evidence, Alves et al. (2021b) addressed this issue and proposed a taxonomic rearrangement of three Neotropical genera infecting freshwater fishes: Ageneiella de Chambrier & Vaucher, 1999, Manaosia Woodland, 1935, and Mariauxiella de Chambrier & Rego, 1995. As a result, Manaosia now comprises Man. bracodemoca Woodland, 1935 (type-species) and Man. piscatorum (de Chambrier & Vaucher, 1999) (= Mar. piscatorum), while Ageneiella was synonymized with Mariauxiella, which currently includes two species, i.e., Mar. pimelodi de Chambrier & Rego, 1995 (type-species) and Mar. brevifilis (de Chambrier & Vaucher, 1999) (= Ageneiella brevifilis).

As part of ongoing efforts to improve our understanding of the diversity, phylogenetic interrelationships and host-parasite associations of proteocephalids, tapeworms collected from Ageneiosus militaris Valenciennes (Siluriformes: Auchenipteridae) from the Upper Paraná River basin, State of São Paulo, Brazil, were subjected to morphological, ultrastructural, and molecular phylogenetic analyses. The evidence gathered indicates that these tapeworms represent a new species of Mariauxiella, which is described herein.

Materials and methods

Sample collection

Twenty-six specimens of A. militaris were collected in the lowermost Pardo River (22°54′41.6″S 49°57′39.9″W), near its discharge into the Paranapanema River, in the municipality of Salto Grande, State of São Paulo, Brazil. Hosts were euthanized by pithing and had their intestines screened for cestodes.

The tapeworms were removed from the host’s intestine, placed in saline, and gently cleaned of intestinal contents. The posteriormost proglottides were excised and fixed in 96% molecular-grade ethanol for molecular analyses. The remaining strobila with the scolex (hologenophores sensu Pleijel et al., 2008) were placed in a small amount of saline and heat-killed with almost boiling 4% formaldehyde solution. After two weeks, formalin-fixed specimens were transferred to 70% ethanol before morphological and ultrastructural analyses.

Morphological and ultrastructural data

Tapeworms were processed following the guidelines of Chervy (2024). The specimens were stained with Mayer’s hydrochloric carmine solution, dehydrated in an ethanol series, cleared with eugenol (clove oil) and mounted in Canada balsam. For histological sections, a few proglottides were embedded in paraffin wax, cross-sectioned, and stained with haematoxylin-eosin. For scanning electron microscopy (SEM) analyses, the scolex and two proglottides of the holotype were dehydrated through a graded ethanol series, dried in hexamethyldisilazane, coated with gold and examined in a FEI Quanta 200 scanning-electron microscope at the Centro de Microscopia Eletrônica, Instituto de Biociências, Universidade Estadual Paulista ‘Júlio de Mesquita Filho’ (UNESP), Botucatu.

Photomicrographs and measurements were taken using the Leica Application Suite software LAS V3 connected to a Leica DMLB 5000 (Leica Microsystems, Wetzlar, Germany). Detailed line drawings were made using a Leica DM 2500 LED with an attached drawing tube and digitized using the tablet-based drawing software Procreate v.5.4.10.

Microthrix terminology follows Chervy (2009) and the fish names follow Froese & Pauly (2026). All measurements in descriptions are given in micrometres unless otherwise indicated. Abbreviations used are: x – mean; n – number of measurements; SG – host field code (= Salto Grande, Brazil); CHIOC – Helminthological Collection of the Instituto Oswaldo Cruz, Rio de Janeiro, Brazil.

Molecular data

Posteriormost proglottides of two specimens were used for the genomic DNA extraction. PCR amplification of the large nuclear ribosomal RNA subunit (RNA28S; D1–D3 domains), and the complete mitochondrial cytochrome c oxidase subunit I (MT-CO1) were performed following Alves et al. (2021a). PCR products were purified using magnetic beads Agencourt AMPure XP (Beckman Coulter, Inc.), and Sanger sequenced on an Applied Biosystems ABI 3500 DNA genetic analyzer. Contiguous sequences were assembled and inspected for errors using Geneious Prime v.2024.0.5, and their consensus were submitted to GenBank.

The newly generated sequences were assembled into two alignments (RNA28S and MT-CO1 markers) together with selected members of the Clade VIII of Alves et al. (2025), mostly composed of fish proteocephalids from the Neotropical region (92 publicly available sequences; Table 1). The alignments were performed using ‘--auto’ command of MAFFT v.7.490 (Katoh et al., 2002; Katoh & Standley, 2013) implemented in Geneious; unreliable positions were identified and removed using the Gblock web server (https://ngphylogeny.fr/; Dereeper et al., 2008) with less stringent settings. Final alignments of the RNA28S (1,423 bp long; Online Resource 1) and MT-CO1 (1,608 bp long; Online Resource 2) genes were concatenated in a larger matrix 3,031 bp long (Online Resource 3). Outgroup was chosen based on a wider phylogenetic analysis of Alves et al. (2025).

GenBank accession numbers of DNA sequences of proteocephalids (Proteocephalidae) used in the phylogenetic analyses. New sequences are in bold.

Phylogenetic reconstructions were performed with the maximum likelihood (ML) criterion for all datasets (RNA28S, MT-CO1, and combined RNA28S + MT-CO1), whereas Bayesian inference (BI) was applied only to the concatenated one. The best evolutionary models were estimated using ModelFinder (Kalyaanamoorthy et al., 2017) within IQ-TREE v.2.3.6 (Minh et al., 2020), based on the small sample size corrected Akaike information criterion (AICc). The model GTR+F+I+G4 was chosen for the individual and concatenated datasets with two partitions.

The ML trees were generated using IQ-TREE and branch supports were estimated with 1,000 replicates of the ultrafast bootstrap (UFBoot) (Hoang et al., 2018) and 1,000 SH-aLRT tests (Guindon et al., 2010). To avoid overestimation of UFBoot, we used a hill-climbing nearest-neighbor interchange (NNI) (Hoang et al., 2018). BI analyses were performed in MrBayes v.3.2.7a (Ronquist et al., 2012), running two independent MC3 runs of four chains for 10 million generations, sampling tree topologies every 1000th generation, with the first 25% of samples discarded as burn-in. We used Tracer v.1.7.2 (Rambaut et al., 2018) to check the convergence and mixing of parameters and to confirm that individual effective sample size (ESS) was adequate to provide reasonable estimates of the variance in model parameters. Nucleotide divergences among taxa were calculated as uncorrected p-distances in Geneious.

Mariauxiella de Chambrier & Rego, 1995

Type species: Mariauxiella pimelodi de Chambrier & Rego, 1995, by original designation

Mariauxiella saltogrande n. sp.

Type-host: Ageneiosus militaris Valenciennes (Siluriformes: Auchenipteridae).

Type-locality: Lowermost Pardo River (22°54′41.6″S 49°57′39.9″W; tributary of the Paranapanema River, Upper Paraná River basin), municipality of Salto Grande, State of São Paulo, Brazil.

Type material: Holotype – specimen on one slide (scolex and two proglottides observed using SEM) and cross sections on two slides [SG-34A; CHIOC 41117a–c (hologenophore)]; paratypes – two complete specimens [SG24A, SG 34B; CHIOC 41119 (hologenophore), and CHIOC 41118, respectively]. All specimens were collected by P. V. Alves and R. J. da Silva on 11 and 12 October 2022.

Other material: Two immature, incomplete specimens (SG23A, B; not deposited).

Site of infection: Anterior intestine.

Prevalence: 12% (3/26). Two host specimens harbored two tapeworms each, whereas a single cestode was found in the remaining infected host.

Representative DNA sequences: Two identical partial RNA 28S sequences (length of 1,399 bp; PZ704327, PZ704327) and two identical complete MT-CO1 sequences (length of 1,617 bp, encoding 538 amino acids; PZ704266, PZ704267).

ZooBank registration: The Life Science Identifier (LSID) for Mariauxiella saltogrande n. sp. is urn:lsid:zoobank.org:act:9C96E959-80D4-4AF6-AA74-35658E12B3AD. The publication is also registered in ZooBank: urn:lsid:zoobank.org:pub:DDB2C589-3315-4B01-BD0E-1FDBB074B749

Etymology: The specific name refers to the municipality of Salto Grande, State of São Paulo, Brazil, through which the Pardo River flows and where the fish hosts were collected. The name is treated as a noun in apposition.

Scolex massive, quadrilobed, 640–835 long and 930–1,180 wide, much wider than proliferative zone 725–920 wide, bearing four biloculate suckers directed antero-laterally (Figs. 1a, b, 2a, 3a); apex slightly conical, apical organ and metascolex absent (Figs. 1a, 2a, 3a). Suckers conspicuous, with free posterior margin; anterior loculus 245–300 × 275–320 larger than posterior loculus 160–205 × 185–260 (Figs. 1a, b, 2a, 3a), with well-developed circular musculature at upper and lower marginal regions (Fig. 2b). Apex of scolex, upper and lower rim, septum, and luminal surface of suckers covered with acicular filitriches, interspersed with gladiate spinitriches becoming more numerous towards lower rim of suckers (Fig. 1e–j). Proliferative zone and strobila covered with acicular filitriches of similar appearance and density (Fig. 1k, l).

Inner longitudinal musculature well developed, formed by numerous large bundles of muscle fibres, clearly visible in whole-mounts (Figs. 2f, g, 3b, e). Two pairs of osmoregulatory canals slightly sinuous, situated at same level, overlapping cirrus-sac and rows of vitelline follicles, lateral to lateralmost testes (Figs. 1d, 2f, 3c, e).

Testes cortical, spherical, 40–60 in diameter, arranged in up to three layers, forming one dorsal field between anterior margin of proglottides and anterior ovarian lobes, 188–256 in number (x = 221, n = 5), absent or less numerous at level of lower and middle portions of uterine stem in mature and pregravid proglottides; testes partially overlap vitelline follicles, rarely reaching osmoregulatory canals (Figs. 2d, e, g, 3b, c, e). Vas deferens strongly coiled, never crossing median line of proglottides (Figs. 2d, e, 3b, c, e). Remarks

The new species was assigned to Mariauxiella based on the following combination of characters: a massive scolex with biloculate suckers bearing strong circular musculature, well-developed internal longitudinal musculature, vagina always anterior to the cirrus-sac with a symmetrical, enlarged terminal part abruptly narrowing at the level of the vaginal sphincter, vitelline follicles arranged in crescent-shaped lateral rows on each side, and lateral uterine diverticula projecting multiple outgrowths that cross the entire medulla to the dorsal cortex (uterine development type 3) (Alves et al., 2021b).

The most distinctive feature distinguishing the two previously known species of Mariauxiella is the number of loculi in the suckers. Whereas Mar. pimelodi possesses uniloculate suckers with double crescent-shaped circular musculature, Mar. brevifilis exhibits biloculate suckers with circular musculature restricted to the upper and lower marginal portions (Table 2; de Chambrier & Rego, 1995; de Chambrier & Vaucher, 1999). Mariauxiella saltogrande n. sp. resembles Mar. brevifilis by also having biloculate suckers and a similar arrangement of the circular musculature. However, the species differ in several features, including body size (length 80–92 mm vs 22–49 mm, and maximum width 1.78 mm vs 5.1 mm), scolex shape (quadrilobed vs rectangular in Mar. brevifilis; compare fig. 98 of de Chambrier & Vaucher, 1999 and Fig. 1a), and arrangement of the osmoregulatory canals (ventral and dorsal canals overlapping each other vs ventral canal median to dorsal canal; compare fig. 29 of de Chambrier & Vaucher, 1999 and Figs. 1d, 3c) (see Table 2). New molecular data also support Mariauxiella saltogrande n. sp. as an independent lineage of the monophyletic Mariauxiella (see below).

Molecular analyses

Phylogenetic analyses based on individual [Online Resources 4 (RNA28S) and 5 (MT-CO1)] and combined RNA28S + MT-CO1 datasets (Fig. 4; Online Resource 6) revealed a sister relationship between Mariauxiella saltogrande n. sp. and Mar. brevifilis. In turn, both species formed a clade together with the type species and the only other species in the genus, Mar. pimelodi. The close relationship between the new species and Mar. brevifilis, as well as the monophyly of Mariauxiella, were strongly supported in all analyses, whereas their interrelationships with other Neotropical proteocephalids remained uncertain (Fig. 4). Pairwise comparison of the RNA28S and MT-CO1 sequence data revealed interspecific divergence among Mariauxiella species of 0.18–1.07% (3–15 nt) and 7.8–15.1% (124–243 nt), respectively, whereas only Mar. brevifilis showed some degree of intraspecific variation, ranging from 0.11–0.18 (1–3 nt) for RNA28S and 0.06–0.81 (1–13 nt) for MT-CO1.

Discussion

Using an integrative taxonomic framework, the present study recognizes Mariauxiella saltogrande n. sp. as a new species based on newly collected material from the auchenipterid catfish Ageneiosus militaris in the Upper Paraná River basin in Brazil. Although only a few specimens were available, they provided compelling morphological, ultrastructural, and molecular evidence supporting the establishment of this new taxon, expanding our current knowledge of species diversity within Mariauxiella. With the addition of Mariauxiella saltogrande n. sp., the genus now comprises three species parasitizing pimelodid and auchenipterid catfishes in South America.

The genus Mariauxiella has recently been revised to better reflect the evolutionary history of its species (Alves et al., 2021b). This reappraisal demonstrated that the similarities between Mariauxiella pimelodi and Manaosia piscatorum (previously Mariauxiella piscatorum) evolved independently, whereas those shared by Mar. pimelodi and Mar. brevifilis were inherited from a common ancestor (see Alves et al., 2021b for details). Likewise, the morphological similarities shared between these two species and Mariauxiella saltogrande n. sp. likely represent homologous traits, as supported by their close phylogenetic relationship (see Fig. 4). The differentiation between Mar. pimelodi and the two other congeners is straightforward, as the type species has a scolex bearing uniloculate suckers, while Mar. brevifilis and the new species have biloculate suckers. Congeneric species having different numbers of sucker loculi are rare among proteocephalids and, in fact, were only recognized after molecular evidence became available (Alves et al., 2021a, b). Furthermore, Mar. pimelodi is the only species known from pimelodid catfishes (Pimelodus ornatus Kner), whereas Mar. brevifilis and Mariauxiella saltogrande n. sp. parasitize Ageneiosus inermis Linnaeus and A. militaris, respectively, both auchenipterid catfishes. Mariauxiella saltogrande n. sp. differs from Mar. brevifilis, and is mainly characterized, by having a longer but narrower body and a scolex with four well-delimited suckers that give it a distinctly quadrilobate appearance, rather than the deeply embedded suckers lacking a free posterior margin that result in a rectangular appearance observed in Mar. brevifilis. Differences in the arrangement of the osmoregulatory canals between the two species (see Remarks) also affect the distribution of the testes, which rarely reach the osmoregulatory canals in the new species but extend beyond them in Mar. brevifilis.

The most striking feature shared by the three species of Mariauxiella is their uterine development, characterized by a ventral cortical uterine stem that gives rise to lateral diverticula bearing several dorsal outgrowths that cross the entire medulla and reach the dorsal cortex (de Chambrier & Rego, 1995; de Chambrier & Vaucher, 1999; present study). This pattern is similar to type 2 uterine development sensu de Chambrier et al. (2004), but differs by the presence of dorsal outgrowths extending from the ventral cortex to the dorsal cortex. It was described in detail by de Chambrier & Rego (1995) and designated as type 3 uterine development by Alves et al. (2021b). Apart from Mariauxiella spp., type 3 uterine development has been observed only in Amazotaenia yvettae de Chambrier, 2001, Luciaella ivanovae Gil de Pertierra, 2009, and Man. piscatorum (see de Chambrier & Vaucher, 1999; de Chambrier, 2001; Gil de Pertierra, 2009). These species, considering that Man. piscatorum was known as Mar. piscatorum until recently, were all assigned to the former subfamily Peltidocotylinae Woodland, 1934, together with Jauella glandicephala Rego & Pavanelli, 1985, and Peltidocotyle spp., all Neotropical catfish cestodes (Gil de Pertierra, 2009). Even though former peltidocotyline genera shared the cortical position of vitelline follicles and testes, and a medullary ovary with projections into the dorsal cortex (unfortunately not seen in our cross-sections), phylogenetic reconstructions have shown that most of these similarities represent examples of convergent evolution. This is a phenomenon commonly found across several proteocephalid lineages (de Chambrier et al., 2015a; Arredondo et al. 2017; Alves et al. 2021a, b).

The presence of a circular musculature in the suckers acting as sphincters is another relatively rare and independently inherited trait among proteocephalids (de Chambrier et al., 2009). Its distribution and appearance vary among taxa and can be used as a good discriminating character at species-level, yet with little systematic value (de Chambrier et al., 2009). Even though Alves et al. (2021b) diagnosed Mariauxiella species as having a circular musculature in the suckers forming a double crescent, i.e., two curved structures with their concave sides directed toward one another, only Mar. pimelodi exhibited this feature (see fig. 1 of de Chambrier & Rego, 1995). In contrast, Mar. brevifilis and Mariauxiella saltogrande n. sp. have a well-developed circular musculature at upper and lower marginal regions only.

Unlike uterine development and the presence of circular musculature in suckers, which are shared with other proteocephalids, the morphology of the distal vaginal canal in species of Mariauxiella appears to be unique to this genus. This structure is characterized by a symmetrically enlarged, chamber-like terminal portion that abruptly narrows at the level of the vaginal sphincter (de Chambrier & Rego, 1995; de Chambrier & Vaucher, 1999; present study). To the best of our knowledge, Frezella vaucheri Alves, de Chambrier, Scholz & Luque, 2015 is the only other proteocephalid that possesses a chamber-like enlargement of the terminal portion of the vaginal canal in most proglottides (Alves et al., 2015). However, in contrast to the symmetrical enlargement observed in Mariauxiella spp., the terminal vaginal canal of F. vaucheri is asymmetrically expanded.

The increasing availability of molecular data for proteocephalid tapeworms has made it possible to generate robust phylogenetic reconstructions and improve our understanding of their evolutionary interrelationships. However, deep branches within the so-called “Neotropical fish superclade” or “Clade VIII” remain largely unresolved (de Chambrier et al., 2015a; Alves et al., 2025, 2026). Likewise, although our results strongly support the monophyly of Mariauxiella and the sister relationship between the new species and Mar. brevifilis, which together form the sister lineage to Mar. pimelodi, their phylogenetic affinities with other Neotropical proteocephalids remain unclear due to low statistical support for most internal branches. MT-CO1 nucleotide divergence among species of Mariauxiella (7.8–15.1%) falls within the range of interspecific variation reported for Neotropical fish proteocephalids, as demonstrated by the well-characterized genus Pseudoendorchis, which exhibits interspecific divergences of 3.4–16.2% (Alves et al., 2021a).

Most proteocephalids have a strict (oioxenous) host specificity, i.e., are known only from a single fish host species (Scholz & Kuchta, 2017). This pattern is also observed in Mariauxiella, as Mar. pimelodi, Mar. brevifilis, and Mariauxiella saltogrande n. sp. are only found in Pimelodus ornatus (the unidentified Pimelodus sp. in the original description most likely corresponds to P. ornatus), A. inermis, and A. militaris, respectively. Gil de Pertierra (2009) reported Mar. brevifilis (as Ageneiella brevifilis) from A. militaris in Argentina, but this record may represent a misidentification of Mariauxiella saltogrande n. sp. given the morphological similarities between these two species. Additional sampling of auchenipterid catfishes, combined with robust taxonomic assessments, may reveal a higher diversity of Mariauxiella and closely related taxa; only three out of the 13 valid species of Ageneiosus have been scrutinized for tapeworms, with A. inermis alone harbouring as many as four species (Alves et al., 2017a). One of these species, Luciaella ivanovae, may be closely related to Mariauxiella due to their morphological similarities (Gil de Pertierra, 2009; Alves et al., 2021b) but it has yet to be molecularly characterized and subjected to phylogenetic analyses.

The three known species of Mariauxiella have been described from the Paraná River basin. However, Mar. pimelodi ex P. ornatus and an unidentified species of Mariauxiella ex A. inermis (reported as Ageneiella sp.) have also been recorded in the Amazon River basin (de Chambrier et al., 2015b; Alves et al., 2021a). Unlike A. militaris, which is restricted to the Paraná River basin, most species of Ageneiosus are found exclusively in the Amazon River basin (Ribeiro et al., 2017). Given the strict specificity exhibited by species of Mariauxiella and their apparent tight association with Ageneiosus spp., we hypothesize that the diversity of Mariauxiella in the Amazonia is likely higher than currently recognized and may exceed that observed in the Paraná River basin, thereby reflecting the greater host diversity in the former drainage.

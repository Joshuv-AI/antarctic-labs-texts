Existence results for Tzitzéica equation via topological degree method on graphs
Kaizhe Chen, Heng Zhang

Abstract
We derive some existence results for the solutions of the Tzitzéica equation − Δ ​ u + h 1 ​ ( x ) ​ e A ​ u + h 2 ​ ( x ) ​ e − B ​ u = 0 -\Delta u+h_{1}(x)e^{Au}+h_{2}(x)e^{-Bu}=0 and the generalized Tzitzéica equation − Δ ​ u + h 1 ​ ( x ) ​ e A ​ u ​ ( e A ​ u − 1 ) + h 2 ​ ( x ) ​ e − B ​ u ​ ( e − B ​ u − 1 ) = 0 -\Delta u+h_{1}(x)e^{Au}(e^{Au}-1)+h_{2}(x)e^{-Bu}(e^{-Bu}-1)=0 on any connected finite graph G = ( V , E ) G=(V,E) . Here, h 1 ​ ( x ) > 0 h_{1}(x)>0 , h 2 ​ ( x ) > 0 h_{2}(x)>0 are two given functions on V V , and A , B > 0 A,B>0 are two constants. Our approach involves computing the topological degree and using the connection between the degree and the critical group of an associated functional.

Introduction

Our research is motivated by the recent work of Jevnikar and Yang
[
25
]
. In their previous work, they considered the following equation:

\Delta u+h_{1}e^{u}-h_{2}e^{-2u}=0\qquad\mathrm{in}~B_{1}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2},

(1)

where
h_{1}
and
h_{2}
are smooth positive functions, and
B_{1}
denotes the unit ball in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Jevnikar and Yang provided the quantization of local blow-up masses associated with a sequence of solutions exhibiting blow-up behavior and established the general existence results for equation (
1
).

Equation (
1
) is closely related to the Tzitzéica equation, which arises in differential geometry in the context of surfaces with constant affine curvature (see, for example,
[
31
,
32
,
33
]
). Additionally, it is also related to Euler’s equation for one-dimensional ideal gas dynamics, the Hirota–Satsuma equation, and others (see, for example,
[
6
,
7
,
16
,
17
]
).

Unlike Euclidean spaces, the analysis on graphs has a wide range of applications, including neural networks, digital image processing, numerical computation, and more. Recently, partial differential equations (PDEs) arising in geometry and physics have been extensively studied. Grigor’yan, Lin, and Yang
[
12
,
13
,
14
]
first investigated the existence of solutions for a series of nonlinear elliptic equations on graphs using variational methods and the mountain pass theorem. For other related existence results of PDEs on graphs obtained via variational methods, we refer to
[

,

,

,
8
,

,

,
20
,

,

,

,

]
.

Similar to variational methods, topological degree theory is a powerful tool for studying the existence of solutions to partial differential equations in Euclidean space or on Riemann surfaces. For example, see Li
[
27
]
. Topological degree theory was first employed by Sun and Wang
[
30
]
to prove the existence and non-existence of solutions for the Kazdan-Warner equation on finite graphs. For additional related results on the existence of PDEs on graphs using topological degree methods, we refer to
[

,

,

.

To state the Tzitzéica equations in the graph setting, we review some notations.
Let
G=(V,E)
be a connected finite graph, where
V
is the set of vertices and
E
is the set of edges. Let

\mu:V\rightarrow(0,+\infty)
and
\{w_{xy}:xy\in E\}
be its measure and weights, respectively.
We assume that the weights
w_{xy}
on edges
xy\in E
are always positive and symmetric. The Laplacian of a function
u:V\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
reads as

\Delta u(x)=\frac{1}{\mu(x)}\sum_{y\sim x}w_{xy}(u(y)-u(x)),

where
y\sim x
means
xy\in E
. For two functions
u
and
v
, the associated gradient form reads

\Gamma(u,v)(x)=\frac{1}{2\mu(x)}\sum_{y\sim x}w_{xy}(u(y)-u(x))(v(y)-v(x)).

Write
\Gamma(u)=\Gamma(u,u)
. We denote the length of its gradient by

|\nabla u|(x)=\sqrt{\Gamma(u)(x)}=\left(\frac{1}{2\mu(x)}\sum_{y\sim x}w_{xy}(u(y)-u(x))^{2}\right)^{1/2}.

The integral of any function
u:V\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is given by

\int_{V}ud\mu=\sum_{x\in V}\mu(x)u(x),

and the integral average of
u
is defined as

\overline{u}=\frac{1}{\text{Vol}(G)}\int_{V}ud\mu=\frac{1}{\text{Vol}(G)}\sum_{x\in V}\mu(x)u(x),

where
\text{Vol}(G)=\sum_{x\in V}\mu(x)
is the volume of
G
. Denote by
L^{\infty}(V)
the space of all functions
f:V\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
with finite norm
\|f\|_{\infty}
which is
defined as
\|f\|_{\infty}=\max_{x\in V}|f(x)|
.

We are interested in the existence results of the Tzitzéica equation on finite graphs, namely

-\Delta u+h_{1}(x)e^{Au}+h_{2}(x)e^{-Bu}=0,

(2)

where
h_{1}(x)
and
h_{2}(x)
are given functions on
V
, with
h_{1}(x)>0
, and
A,B>0
are two constants.
We consider the map
\mathcal{F}_{h_{1},h_{2}}:L^{\infty}(V)\rightarrow L^{\infty}(V)
defined by

\mathcal{F}_{h_{1},h_{2}}(u)=-\Delta u+h_{1}(x)e^{Au}+h_{2}(x)e^{-Bu},

(3)

Denote by
B_{R}=\{u\in L^{\infty}(V):||u||_{\infty}<R\}
. We conclude by Lemma
2
that the Brouwer degree
\text{deg}(\mathcal{F}_{h_{1},h_{2}},B_{R},0)
is well-defined for
R>K
large. Then by the homotopic invariance,
\text{deg}(\mathcal{F}_{h_{1},h_{2}},B_{R},0)
is independent of
R
. We define the topological degree by

\mathbf{d}_{h_{1},h_{2}}=\lim_{R\rightarrow+\infty}\text{deg}(\mathcal{F}_{h_{1},h_{2}},B_{R},0).

For a more comprehensive understanding of the Brouwer degree, including its key properties such as homotopic invariance and Kronecker existence, we refer the readers to Chang
[
3
, Chapter 3]
.

For the topological degree
\mathbf{d}_{h_{1},h_{2}}
, we have the following results.

Theorem 1
.

Let
h_{1}(x)
be a given positive function on
V
, and
A
,
B>0
be two constants. Then

\mathbf{d}_{h_{1},h_{2}}=\begin{cases}1,&\max_{V}h_{2}(x)<0,\\
0,&\min_{V}h_{2}(x)>0.\\
\end{cases}

Hence, Theorem
1
and the Kronecker existence show that the Tzitzéica equation has at least one solution if
\max_{V}h_{2}(x)<0
. From the process of proving Lemma
4
, we have that if
\max_{V}h_{2}(x)>0
, then the Tzitzéica equation has no solutions.

Further, we consider the following generalized Tzitzéica equation:

-\Delta u+h_{1}(x)e^{Au}(e^{Au}-1)+h_{2}(x)e^{-Bu}(e^{-Bu}-1)=0.

(4)

It also can be seen as a generalization of the Chern-Simons Higgs model, which has been studied in
[
26
]
. We consider the map
\mathcal{G}_{h_{1},h_{2}}:L^{\infty}(V)\rightarrow L^{\infty}(V)
defined by

\mathcal{G}_{h_{1},h_{2}}(u)=-\Delta u+h_{1}(x)e^{Au}(e^{Au}-1)+h_{2}(x)e^{-Bu}(e^{-Bu}-1)

(5)

Similar as before, we conclude by Lemma
6
that the Brouwer degree
\text{deg}(\mathcal{G}_{h_{1},h_{2}},B_{R},0)
is well defined for
R>L
large. Then by the homotopic invariance,
\text{deg}(\mathcal{G}_{h_{1},h_{2}},B_{R},0)
is independent of
R
. We define the topological degree by

\mathbf{\tilde{d}}_{h_{1},h_{2}}=\lim_{R\rightarrow+\infty}\text{deg}(\mathcal{G}_{h_{1},h_{2}},B_{R},0).

As for the topological degree
\mathbf{\tilde{d}}_{h_{1},h_{2}}
, we have the following results.

Theorem 2
.

Let
h_{1}(x)>0
,
h_{2}(x)>0
be two given functions on
V
, and
A
,
B>0
be two constants. Then

\mathbf{\tilde{d}}_{h_{1},h_{2}}=0.

By taking the variational method and critical groups into account, we find the multiplicities of solutions of (
4
) under certain conditions.

Theorem 3
.

Let
h_{1}(x)>0
,
h_{2}(x)>0
be two given functions on
V
, and
A
,
B>0
be two constants. If
A\max_{V}h_{1}(x)<B\min_{V}h_{2}(x)
or
A\min_{V}h_{1}(x)>B\max_{V}h_{2}(x)
, then the equation (
4
) has at least two solutions.

Proof of Theorem
1

Firstly, we give the following elliptic estimate.

Lemma 1
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be a finite connected simple graph, then there exists a positive constant
C
such that for all
u\in L^{\infty}(V)
,

\max_{V}u-\min_{V}u\leq C\|\Delta u\|_{L^{\infty}(V)}.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we assume that
u(x_{1})=\min_{V}u
,
u(x_{\ell})=\max_{V}u
and
x_{1}x_{2}
,
x_{2}x_{3}
,
\cdots
,
x_{\ell-1}x_{\ell}
is the shortest path connecting
x_{1}
and
x_{\ell}
. It follows from Cauchy inequality that

\displaystyle 0\leq u(x_{\ell})-u(x_{1})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{\ell-1}|u(x_{j+1})-u(x_{j})|

(6)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{\ell-1}}{\sqrt{w_{0}}}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{\ell-1}w_{x_{j+1}x_{j}}(u(x_{j+1})-u(x_{j}))^{2}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{\ell-1}}{\sqrt{w_{0}}}\left(\int_{V}|\nabla u|^{2}d\mu\right)^{1/2},

where
w_{0}=\min_{x\in V,\,y\sim x}w_{xy}>0
. Denoting
\overline{u}=\frac{1}{|V|}\int_{V}ud\mu
, we obtain by integration by parts

\displaystyle\int_{V}|\nabla u|^{2}d\mu

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{V}u\Delta ud\mu

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{V}(u-\overline{u})\Delta ud\mu

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\int_{V}(u-\overline{u})^{2}d\mu\right)^{1/2}\left(\int_{V}(\Delta u)^{2}d\mu\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{\lambda_{1}}\int_{V}|\nabla u|^{2}d\mu\right)^{1/2}\left(\int_{V}(\Delta u)^{2}d\mu\right)^{1/2},

which gives

\displaystyle\int_{V}|\nabla u|^{2}d\mu\leq\frac{1}{\lambda_{1}}\int_{V}(\Delta u)^{2}d\mu\leq\frac{1}{\lambda_{1}}\|\Delta u\|_{L^{\infty}(V)}^{2}\text{Vol}(G),

(7)

where
\lambda_{1}=\inf_{\overline{v}=0,\int_{V}v^{2}d\mu=1}\int_{V}|\nabla v|^{2}d\mu>0
. Combining (
6
) and (
7
), we conclude

\displaystyle\max_{V}u-\min_{V}u\leq\sqrt{\frac{(\ell-1)\text{Vol}(G)}{w_{0}\lambda_{1}}}\|\Delta u\|_{L^{\infty}(V)}:=C\|\Delta u\|_{L^{\infty}(V)},

(8)

as desired.
∎

Secondly, we give the following priori estimate for equation (
2
).

Lemma 2
.

Let
u
be a solution of equation (
2
), where
A
and
B
are positive constants,
h_{1}(x)>0
and
h_{2}(x)<0
are two functions on
V
. Then there exists a constant
K
, depending only on
A
,
B
,
h_{1}
,
h_{2}
, and the graph
G
, such that
|u(x)|\leq K
for all
x\in V
.

Proof.

Let
x_{0}\in V
be a vertex such that
u(x_{0})=\min_{V}u(x)
. Then

\displaystyle-\Delta u(x_{0})=\frac{1}{\mu_{x_{0}}}\sum_{y\in V}w_{x_{0}y}(u(x_{0})-u(y))\leq 0.

Hence, we have

h_{1}(x_{0})e^{Au(x_{0})}+h_{2}(x_{0})e^{-Bu(x_{0})}\geq 0.

That is,

u(x_{0})\geq\frac{1}{A+B}\log\frac{-h_{2}(x_{0})}{h_{1}(x_{0})}\geq\frac{1}{A+B}\log\frac{-\max_{V}h_{2}(x)}{\max_{V}h_{1}(x)}:=C_{1}.

Similarly, for a vertex
x_{1}\in V
such that
u(x_{1})=\max_{V}u(x)
, we have

u(x_{1})\leq C_{2}:=\frac{1}{A+B}\log\frac{-\min_{V}h_{2}(x)}{\min_{V}h_{1}(x)}.

We obtain the desired estimate.
∎

Lemma 3
.

In the case of
h_{1}(x)>0
and
h_{2}(x)<0
, we have
\mathbf{d}_{h_{1},h_{2}}=1
.

Proof.

We define a smooth map
\tilde{\mathcal{F}}:L^{\infty}(V)\times[0,1]\rightarrow L^{\infty}(V)
by

\tilde{\mathcal{F}}(u,t)=-\Delta u+[t\epsilon+(1-t)h_{1}(x)]e^{Au}+[-t\epsilon+(1-t)h_{2}(x)]e^{-Bu}.

where
\epsilon>0
is sufficiently small such that
t\epsilon+(1-t)h_{1}(x)>0
and
-t\epsilon+(1-t)h_{2}(x)<0
. Note that
\tilde{\mathcal{F}}(u,0)=\mathcal{F}_{h_{1},h_{2}}.
Applying Lemma
2
, we deduce that the solutions of
\tilde{\mathcal{F}}(u,t)=0
are uniformly bounded with respect to
t
. We consider the equation

\tilde{\mathcal{F}}(u,1)=-\Delta u+\epsilon e^{Au}-\epsilon e^{-Bu}=0.

(9)

By homotopic invariance, the equation (
2
) and the equation (
9
) have the same Brouwer degree. Thus, it is sufficient to calculate the Brouwer degree of the equation (
9
).

We claim that
u_{1}\equiv 0
is the unique solution of equation (
9
). Suppose
v_{1}\not\equiv 0
is another solution of equation (
9
), then

-\Delta(u_{1}-v_{1})+\epsilon(e^{Au_{1}}-e^{Av_{1}})-\epsilon(e^{-Bu_{1}}-e^{-Bv_{1}})=0,

which implies that

\displaystyle\Delta(u_{1}-v_{1})|\leq

\displaystyle\epsilon|e^{Au_{1}}-e^{Av_{1}}|+\epsilon|e^{-Bu_{1}}-e^{-Bv_{1}}|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\epsilon(A+B)|u_{1}-v_{1}|\cdot\max\{e^{Au_{1}},e^{Av_{1}},e^{-Bu_{1}},e^{-Bv_{1}}\}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle C_{3}\epsilon|u_{1}-v_{1}|,

here we have used Lemma
2
.
Together with Lemma
1
, we derive

\displaystyle\max_{V}(u_{1}-v_{1})-\min_{V}(u_{1}-v_{1})

\displaystyle\leq CC_{3}\epsilon\max_{V}|u_{1}-v_{1}|

\displaystyle:=C_{4}\epsilon\max_{V}|u_{1}-v_{1}|

\displaystyle\leq C_{4}\epsilon\left(\max_{V}(u_{1}-v_{1})-\min_{V}(u_{1}-v_{1})+|\min_{V}(u_{1}-v_{1})|\right).

Thus for sufficient small
\epsilon>0
, we have

\max_{V}(u_{1}-v_{1})\leq\min_{V}(u_{1}-v_{1})+\frac{1}{2}|\min_{V}(u_{1}-v_{1})|.

Since

\int_{V}(e^{Au_{1}}-e^{-Bu_{1}})-(e^{Av_{1}}-e^{-Bv_{1}})d\mu=0,

it must hold that

\min_{V}(u_{1}-v_{1})<0<\max_{V}(u_{1}-v_{1}).

Hence,

0<\max_{V}(u_{1}-v_{1})\leq\min_{V}(u_{1}-v_{1})-\frac{1}{2}\min_{V}(u_{1}-v_{1})=\frac{1}{2}\min_{V}(u_{1}-v_{1})<0,

which is a contradiction. Therefore, the equation (
9
) has the unique solution
u\equiv 0
.

A straightforward calculation shows that

\mathbf{d}_{h_{1},h_{2}}=\mathbf{d}_{\epsilon,-\epsilon}=\text{sgn\;det}(D\mathcal{F}_{\epsilon,-\epsilon}(0))=\text{sgn\;det}(-\Delta+A\epsilon I+B\epsilon I)=1,

for sufficient small
\epsilon>0
, where we have used the fact that the eigenvalues of
-\Delta
are non-negative. This completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 4
.

In the case of
h_{1}(x)>0
and
h_{2}(x)>0
, we have
\mathbf{d}_{h_{1},h_{2}}=0
.

Proof.

Integrating the both side of (
2
), we deduce that the left side is positive whereas the right side is
0
. Hence the equation (
2
) has no solution. Therefore, we conclude

\mathbf{d}_{h_{1},h_{2}}=0,

as desired.
∎

Combining Lemmas
3
and
4
, we complete the proof of Theorem
1
.

Proof of Theorem
2

In order to ensure that the Brouwer degree is well-defined and to utilize the homotopy invariance, we prove the following estimate.

Lemma 5
.

Let
u_{t}
be a solution of equation

-\Delta u_{t}+h_{1}(x)e^{Au_{t}}(e^{Au_{t}}-t)+h_{2}(x)e^{-Bu_{t}}(e^{-Bu_{t}}-t)=0,

(10)

where
t\in[0,1]
. Then there exists a constant
L
, depending only on
A
,
B
,
h_{1}
,
h_{2}
, and the graph
G
, such that
|u_{t}(x)|\leq L
for all
x\in V
and all
t\in[0,1]
.

Proof.

Firstly, we prove that
u_{t}(x)
has a uniform upper bound. Fix
t\in[0,1]
. Let
x_{0}\in V
be a vertex such that
u_{t}(x_{0})=\max_{V}u_{t}(x)
. Then

\displaystyle-\Delta u_{t}(x_{0})=\frac{1}{\mu_{x_{0}}}\sum_{y\in V}w_{x_{0}y}(u_{t}(x_{0})-u_{t}(y))\geq 0.

Therefore, we have

h_{1}(x_{0})e^{Au_{t}(x_{0})}(e^{Au_{t}(x_{0})}-t)+h_{2}(x_{0})e^{-Bu_{t}(x_{0})}(e^{-Bu_{t}(x_{0})}-t)\leq 0.

Thus,

e^{Au_{t}(x_{0})}(e^{Au_{t}(x_{0})}-t)\leq-\frac{h_{2}(x_{0})}{h_{1}(x_{0})}e^{-Bu_{t}(x_{0})}(e^{-Bu_{t}(x_{0})}-t)\leq\frac{t^{2}\max_{V}h_{2}(x)}{4\min_{V}h_{1}(x)},

which implies that

\max_{V}u(x)\leq\frac{1}{A}\log\left(\frac{t}{2}+\sqrt{\frac{t^{2}\max_{V}h_{2}(x)}{4\min_{V}h_{1}(x)}+\frac{t^{2}}{4}}\right)\leq\frac{1}{A}\log\left(\frac{1}{2}+\sqrt{\frac{\max_{V}h_{2}(x)}{4\min_{V}h_{1}(x)}+\frac{1}{4}}\right):=C_{1}.

Secondly, we show that
u_{t}(x)
has also a uniform lower bound. Without loss of generality, we may assume
\min_{V}u_{t}(x)<0
. For otherwise,
u_{t}(x)
has already lower bound
0
. We derive from integrating both sides of equation (
10
) that

\int_{V}h_{1}(x)e^{Au_{t}(x)}(e^{Au_{t}(x)}-t)+h_{2}(x)e^{-Bu_{t}(x)}(e^{-Bu_{t}(x)}-t)d\mu=0,

which leads to

\displaystyle\left|\int_{u_{t}<0}h_{2}(x)e^{-Bu_{t}(x)}\left(e^{-Bu_{t}(x)}-t\right)d\mu\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\int_{u_{t}\geq 0}h_{2}(x)e^{-Bu_{t}(x)}\left(e^{-Bu_{t}(x)}-t\right)d\mu+\int_{V}h_{1}(x)e^{Au_{t}(x)}\left(e^{Au_{t}(x)}-t\right)d\mu\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\max_{V}h_{2}(x)\int_{u_{t}\geq 0}\left|e^{-Bu_{t}(x)}\left(e^{-Bu_{t}(x)}-t\right)\right|d\mu+\max_{V}h_{1}(x)\int_{V}\left|e^{Au_{t}(x)}\left(e^{Au_{t}(x)}-t\right)\right|d\mu.

Observe that

\int_{u_{t}\geq 0}\left|e^{-Bu_{t}(x)}\left(e^{-Bu_{t}(x)}-t\right)\right|d\mu\leq\max\left\{\frac{t^{2}}{4},1-t\right\}\text{Vol}(G)\leq\text{Vol}(G),

and

\int_{V}\left|e^{Au_{t}(x)}\left(e^{Au_{t}(x)}-t\right)\right|d\mu\leq\max\left\{\frac{t^{2}}{4},e^{AC_{1}}\left(e^{AC_{1}}-t\right)\right\}\text{Vol}(G).

This together with the fact that

\left|\int_{u_{t}<0}h_{2}(x)e^{-Bu_{t}(x)}\left(e^{-Bu_{t}(x)}-t\right)d\mu\right|\geq\min_{V}h_{2}(x)\cdot e^{-B\min_{V}u_{t}(x)}\left(e^{-B\min_{V}u_{t}(x)}-t\right)\mu_{0}

leads to

\displaystyle\min_{V}h_{2}(x)\cdot e^{-B\min_{V}u_{t}(x)}\left(e^{-B\min_{V}u_{t}(x)}-t\right)\mu_{0}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\max_{V}h_{2}(x)\text{Vol}(G)+\max_{V}h_{1}(x)\cdot\max\left\{\frac{t^{2}}{4},e^{AC_{1}}\left(e^{AC_{1}}-t\right)\right\}\text{Vol}(G)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\max_{V}h_{2}(x)\text{Vol}(G)+\max_{V}h_{1}(x)\cdot\max\left\{\frac{1}{4},e^{2AC_{1}}\right\}\text{Vol}(G):=C_{2},

where
\mu_{0}=\min_{x\in V}\mu(x)>0
. Hence, there holds

\min_{V}u_{t}(x)\geq-\frac{1}{B}\log\left(\frac{t}{2}+\sqrt{\frac{C_{2}}{\min_{V}h_{2}(x)\mu_{0}}+\frac{t^{2}}{4}}\right)\geq-\frac{1}{B}\log\left(\frac{1}{2}+\sqrt{\frac{C_{2}}{\min_{V}h_{2}(x)\mu_{0}}+\frac{1}{4}}\right):=C_{3}.

This completes the proof.
∎

In particular, we have the follow result.

Let
u
be a solution of equation (

), where
A
and
B
are positive constants,
h_{1}(x)>0
, and
h_{2}(x)>0
. Then there exists a constant
L
, depending only on
A
,
B
,
h_{1}
,
h_{2}
, and the graph
G
, such that
|u(x)|\leq L
for all
x\in V
.

Now, we are prepared to calculate the Brouwer degree.

Lemma 7
.

Let
A
and
B
be two positive constants,
h_{1}(x)>0
, and
h_{2}(x)>0
. Then

\mathbf{\tilde{d}}_{h_{1},h_{2}}=0.

Proof.

We define a smooth map
\tilde{\mathcal{G}}:L^{\infty}(V)\times[0,1]\rightarrow L^{\infty}(V)
by

\tilde{\mathcal{G}}(u,t)=-\Delta u+h_{1}(x)e^{Au}(e^{Au}-t)+h_{2}(x)e^{-Bu}(e^{-Bu}-t).

By Lemma

\mathbf{\tilde{d}}_{h_{1},h_{2}}=\text{deg}(\tilde{\mathcal{G}}(u,1),B_{R},0)=\text{deg}(\tilde{\mathcal{G}}(u,0),B_{R},0)=\mathbf{d}_{h_{1},h_{2}}=0,

Proof of Theorem
3

In this section, we adopt the variational method to find a solution to equation (
4
) with the assumption in Theorem
3
.

Lemma 8
.

Let
h_{1}(x)>0
,
h_{2}(x)>0
be two given functions on
V
, and
A
,
B>0
be two constants. If
A\max_{V}h_{1}(x)<B\min_{V}h_{2}(x)
or
A\min_{V}h_{1}(x)>B\max_{V}h_{2}(x)
, then the equation (
4
) has at least one solution.

Proof.

If
A\max_{V}h_{1}(x)<B\min_{V}h_{2}(x)
, then there exists a small
\delta>0
such that

\frac{h_{1}(x)}{h_{2}(x)}e^{(A+B)\delta}<-\frac{e^{-B\delta}-1}{e^{A\delta}-1}

for all
x\in V
. That is,

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{h_{1},h_{2}}(\delta)=h_{1}(x)e^{A\delta}(e^{A\delta}-1)+h_{2}(x)e^{-B\delta}(e^{-B\delta}-1)<0,\ \ \text{for\ all}\ \ x\in V.

(11)

Obviously, there exists a sufficiently large constant
\beta>0
such that

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{h_{1},h_{2}}(\beta)=h_{1}(x)e^{A\beta}(e^{A\beta}-1)+h_{2}(x)e^{-B\beta}(e^{-B\beta}-1)>0,\ \ \text{for\ all}\ \ x\in V.

(12)

We define the energy functional
J:L^{\infty}(V)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
as

J(u)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{V}|\nabla u|^{2}+\frac{1}{A}h_{1}(x)(e^{Au}-1)^{2}-\frac{1}{B}h_{2}(x)(e^{-Bu}-1)^{2}d\mu.

(13)

It is direct to check
J\in C^{2}(L^{\infty}(V),\mathbb{R})
, since
L^{\infty}(V)
is a finite-dimensional Banach space. As the set
\left\{u\in L^{\infty}(V):\delta\leq u(x)\leq\beta\ \text{for\ all}\ \ x\in V\right\}
is bounded and closed in
L^{\infty}(V)
, we find some
\tilde{u}\in L^{\infty}(V)
satisfying
\delta\leq\tilde{u}\leq\beta
for all
x\in V
and

\displaystyle J(\tilde{u})=\min_{\delta\leq u\leq\beta}J(u).

(14)

We
claim
that

\delta<\tilde{u}(x)<\beta\text{\ for \,all\ \,}x\in V.

(15)

In fact, if
\tilde{u}(x_{0})=\delta
, then we take a small
\epsilon>0
such that

\delta\leq\tilde{u}(x)+t\delta_{x_{0}}(x)\leq\beta,\quad\forall x\in V,\,\forall t\in(0,\epsilon),

where
\delta_{x_{0}}(x)
denotes the dirac function at
x_{0}
. Combining with (
11
) and (
14
), we have

\displaystyle 0\leq

\displaystyle\left.\frac{d}{dt}J(\tilde{u}+t\delta_{x_{0}})\right|_{t=0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{V}\left(-\Delta\tilde{u}+h_{1}(x)e^{A\tilde{u}}(e^{A\tilde{u}}-1)+h_{2}(x)e^{-B\tilde{u}}(e^{-B\tilde{u}}-1)\right)\delta_{x_{0}}d\mu

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\Delta\tilde{u}(x_{0})+h_{1}(x)e^{A\tilde{u}(x_{0})}(e^{A\tilde{u}(x_{0})}-1)+h_{2}(x)e^{-B\tilde{u}(x_{0})}(e^{-B\tilde{u}(x_{0})}-1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\Delta\tilde{u}(x_{0})+\mathcal{G}_{h_{1},h_{2}}(\delta)

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle-\Delta\tilde{u}(x_{0}).

(16)

On the other hand, since
\tilde{u}(x)\geq\tilde{u}(x_{0})
for all
x\in V
, we conclude
\Delta\tilde{u}(x_{0})\geq 0
,
which contradicts (
16
). Hence,
\tilde{u}(x)>\delta
for all
x\in V
. Similarly, we can also rule out the possibility that
\tilde{u}(x_{1})=\beta
for some
x_{1}\in V
. This confirms our claim.

Combining (
14
) and (
15
), we conclude
that
\tilde{u}
is a local minimum critical point of
J
. In particular,
\tilde{u}
is a solution of the Tzitzéica equation (
4
). The similar argument holds true if
A\min_{V}h_{1}(x)>B\max_{V}h_{2}(x)
. This completes the proof.
∎

Proof of Theorem
3
.

Let
\tilde{u}
be a local minimum critical point of
J
that is defined as (
13
). With no loss of generality, we may assume
\tilde{u}
is the unique
critical point of
J
. For otherwise,
J
has already at least two critical points, and the proof ends. Set
a:=J(\tilde{u})
.
Recall the
q
-th critical group of
{J}
at
\tilde{u}
is defined by (
[
2
]
, Chapter 1, Definition 4.1)

\displaystyle\mathsf{C}_{q}({J},\tilde{u})=\mathsf{H}_{q}({J}^{a}\cap{U},{J}^{a}\setminus\{\tilde{u}\}\cap{U},\mathbf{G}),

(17)

where
{J}^{a}=\{u\in L^{\infty}(V):{J}(u)\leq a\}
,
U
is a neighborhood of

\tilde{u}\in L^{\infty}(V)
,
\mathsf{H}_{q}

is the singular homology group with the coefficients groups
\mathbf{G}
. By the excision property of

\mathsf{H}_{q}
, we derive that the definition of
\mathsf{C}_{q}({J},\tilde{u})
is independent on the choice of
U
.
Since
\tilde{u}
is the unique local minimum critical point, we get
{J}^{a}=\{\tilde{u}\}
. Therefore, we deduce

\displaystyle C_{q}({J},\tilde{u})

\displaystyle=\mathsf{H}_{q}(J^{a}\cap U,\{J^{a}\setminus\{\tilde{u}\}\cap U;\mathbf{G})

\displaystyle=\mathsf{H}_{q}(\{\tilde{u}\},\varnothing;\mathbf{G})

\displaystyle=\mathsf{H}_{q}(\{\tilde{u}\};\mathbf{G})

\displaystyle=\delta_{q0}\mathbf{G},

(18)

where

\displaystyle\delta_{q0}\mathbf{G}=\begin{cases}\mathbf{G},&q=0,\\
0,&q>0.\end{cases}

We claim that
J
satisfies the Palais-Smale condition at any value
c\in\mathbb{R}
. Indeed, let
\{u_{k}\}
be a sequence such that
J(u_{k})\rightarrow c\in\mathbb{R}
and
J^{\prime}(u_{k})(\phi)\rightarrow 0
for any
\phi\in L^{\infty}(V)

as
k\rightarrow\infty
. Using the method of proving Lemma
5
, we obtain that
\{u_{k}\}
is uniformly bounded. Since
L^{\infty}(V)
is pre-compact,
then up to a subsequence,
\{u_{k}\}
converges uniformly to some
u^{\ast}\in L^{\infty}(V)
which is a critical point of
J
, as claimed.
Notice that

DJ(u)=-\Delta u+h_{1}(x)e^{Au}(e^{Au}-1)+h_{2}(x)e^{-Bu}(e^{-Bu}-1)=\mathcal{G}_{h_{1},h_{2}}(u).

According to (
[
2
]
, Chapter 2, Theorem 3.2), based on (
18
), we have, for sufficiently large
R
,

\deg(\mathcal{G}_{h_{1},h_{2}},B_{R},0)=\deg(DJ,B_{R},0)=\sum_{q=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{q}{\rm rank}\,\mathsf{C}_{q}(J,\tilde{u})={\rm rank}\mathbf{G}\neq 0.

This contradicts
\deg(\mathcal{G}_{h_{1},h_{2}},B_{R},0)=0
derived from Theorem
2
. Therefore the equation (
4
) has at least two different solutions,
and the proof of Theorem
3
is finished.

Limiting behavior of inertial manifolds for stochastic differential equations driven by non-Gaussian Lévy noise
Longyu Wu, Ji Shu

Abstract




Abstract:
In this paper, we study the limiting behavior for stochastic differential equations driven by non-Gaussian
\alpha
-stable Lévy noise as
\alpha
approaches 2. We first prove the convergence of solutions for system driven by
\alpha
-stable Lévy noise to those of the system driven by Brownian motion. Then we construct the
C^{1}
inertial manifolds for both systems and show that these inertial manifolds converge in probability as
\alpha\rightarrow 2
.

Key words:
Random dynamical systems; non-Gaussian system;
\alpha
-stable Lévy noise; Inertial manifolds

1
Introduction

We consider a class of stochastic differential equations driven by
\alpha
-stable Lévy noise in a separable Hilbert space
H
:

dv^{\alpha}+Av^{\alpha}=F(v^{\alpha})dt+v^{\alpha}\diamond L^{\alpha}_{t},

(1.1)

where
-A
is a generator of an analytic semigroup
e^{-At}
on
H
,
F
is a nonlinear operator from a fractional power space
H^{\sigma}
into
H
for
0\leq\sigma<1
,
L^{\alpha}
is an
\alpha
-stable Lévy process and the symbol
\diamond
denotes the Marcus integral.

Let
\{W_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
be a one-dimensional standard Brownian motion. Let
\{S_{t}^{\alpha}\}_{t\geq 0}
be an independent
\frac{\alpha}{2}
-stable subordinator and
J^{\alpha}
be a Poisson random measure with intensity measure
dt\lambda^{\alpha}(dx)
, where
\lambda^{\alpha}
is a Lévy measure on
\mathbb{R}^{+}
and

\lambda^{\alpha}(dx):=\mathbb{E}J^{\alpha}(1,dx)=C(\alpha)\frac{dx}{|x|^{1+\frac{\alpha}{2}}},

with

C(\alpha):=\frac{1}{2^{1-\frac{\alpha}{2}}}\frac{\alpha}{\Gamma\left(1-\frac{\alpha}{2}\right)}.

Since
\lim\limits_{s\to 0}s\Gamma(s)=1
, we get

\lim\limits_{\alpha\to 2}\frac{2C(\alpha)}{2-\alpha}=1.

The
\frac{\alpha}{2}
-stable subordinator
S^{\alpha}
is given by

S_{t}^{\alpha}=\int_{0}^{\infty}xJ^{\alpha}(t,dx).

The subordinated Brownian motion in
\mathbb{R}
is defined by
\{W_{S_{t}^{\alpha}}\}_{t\geq 0}
. It is well-known that the subordinated Brownian motion
\{W_{S_{t}^{\alpha}}\}_{t\geq 0}
is an
\alpha
-stable process (see
[
2
,
28
]
). We sometimes denote

W_{S_{t}^{\alpha}}
by
L^{\alpha}_{t}
.

In the current paper, we consider the following stochastic differential equation driven by subordinated Brownian motion in separable Hilbert spaces
H
,

\frac{dv^{\alpha}}{dt}+Av^{\alpha}=F(v^{\alpha})+v^{\alpha}\diamond\dot{W}_{S_{t}^{\alpha}},\quad v^{\alpha}(0)=x\in H.

(1.2)

When
\alpha=2
, we have the standard Brownian motion, which the Marcus integral reduces to the Stratonovich stochastic integral. Then we get equation

\frac{dv}{dt}+Av=F(v)+v\circ\dot{W}_{t},\quad v(0)=x\in H,

(1.3)

where the symbol
\circ
denotes the Stratonovich integral and
W_{t}
is a scalar standard Brownian motion.

Inertial manifolds are useful tools in the study of the long-time behaviour of infinite dimensional dynamical systems. The concept of inertial manifolds was introduced by Foias, Sell and Temam
[
14
]
. These manifolds are finite-dimensional invariant surface which attracts exponentially
all solutions, and thus the long-time dynamics restricted to the inertial manifold reduces to a finite system,
which is called an inertial form. The theory of inertial manifolds has
been extensively studied for deterministic and stochastic systems, as exemplified by reference such as
[
4
,
5
,
6
,
7
,
8
,
10
,
12
,
16
,
18
,
29
,
33
,
34
]
. These studies are focused on either deterministic differential equations or stochastic differential equations driven by Gaussian noise.

However, non-Gaussian noise plays an important role in many complex systems, which are often observed in biological, physical and
neural systems, etc. In particular, non-Gaussian Lévy noise has been widely applied
in recent researches, see for example
[
2
,
22
,
23
,
26
,
28
,
30
]
and the references therein. As a special non-Gaussian stochastic process, the
\alpha
-stable Lévy process are gaining increasing attention in applications. Gu and Ai
[
15
]
proved the existence of a random attractor for stochastic lattice
dynamical systems with
\alpha
-stable Lévy noises. Qiao and Duan
[
25
]
studied the asymptotic methods for stochastic dynamical systems with small non-Gaussian Lévy noise. Yuan et al.
[
31
]
constructed the slow manifolds for dynamical systems with non-Gaussian
\alpha
-stable Lévy noise. Chao et al.
[
11
]
explored the role of slow manifolds in parameter estimation for a multiscale stochastic system with
\alpha
-stable Lévy noise. Liu
[
19
]
discussed the random stable invariant manifolds of stochastic evolution equations driven by Gaussian and non-Gaussian noises. Liu
[
20
]
studied the convergence behavior of the solutions to stochastic differential equations with Lipschitz and Hölder drifts, and driven by
\alpha
-stable Lévy processes. To the best of our knowledge, there are few results available in the literature concerning invariant manifolds of stochastic differential equations driven by non-Gaussian Lévy noise. In this work, we investigate the
C^{1}
-convergence of inertial manifolds for the system subject to an
\alpha
-stable Lévy process.

The goal of this paper is to construct the
C^{1}
inertial manifolds for stochastic differential equations driven by
\alpha
-stable Lévy noise, as well as to study the convergence both in the solutions and
C^{1}
inertial manifolds of systems driven by Gaussian and non-Gaussian noises. More precisely, we first prove the convergence relation of solutions between equation (
1.2
) and equation (
1.3
). Then, we establish the existence of
C^{1}
inertial manifolds for equation (
1.2
) and equation (
1.3
). Furthermore, we show the
C^{1}
inertial manifolds of equation (
1.2
) converges in probability to those of equation (
1.3
) as
\alpha\rightarrow 2
.

We organize this paper as follows. In Section 2, we first introduce some basic concepts and fundamental assumptions. In Section 3, we prove the convergence of solutions for equation (
1.2
) and equation (
1.3
). In Section 4, we construct the
C^{1}
inertial manifolds and show the convergence of these inertial manifolds for equation (
1.2
) and equation (
1.3
).

2
Preliminaries

Let
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
be a probability space and
H
be a separable Hilbert space. We denote by

\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R})
,
\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{+})
and
\mathcal{B}(H)
the collections of Borel sets on
\mathbb{R}
,
\mathbb{R}^{+}
and
H
, respectively. We first recall some basic concepts about random dynamical systems which from Arnold
[
1
]
.

2.1
Random dynamical system

Definition 2.1
.

(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P},\{\theta_{t}\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}})
is called a metric dynamical system if

(\mathbf{i})

\theta:\mathbb{R}\times\Omega\rightarrow\Omega
is
(\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R})\otimes\mathcal{F},\mathcal{F})
-measurable;

(\mathbf{ii})

\theta_{0}=id_{\Omega}
, the identity on
\Omega,\theta_{t+s}=\theta_{t}\circ\theta_{s}
for all
t,s\in\mathbb{R}
;

(\mathbf{iii})

\theta_{t}\mathbb{P}=\mathbb{P}
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
.

Definition 2.2
.

A mapping

\phi:\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\Omega\times H\rightarrow H,\ (t,\omega,x)\mapsto\phi(t,\omega,x)

is called a random dynamical system over a metric dynamical system
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P},\{\theta_{t}\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}})
if

(\mathbf{i})

\phi
is
(\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{+})\otimes\mathcal{F}\otimes\mathcal{B}(H),\mathcal{B}(H))
-measurable;

(\mathbf{ii})
the mapping
\phi(t,\omega):=\phi(t,\omega,\cdot):H\rightarrow H
forms a cocycle over
\theta_{t}
:

\phi(0,\omega)=id_{H}
,
\forall\omega\in\Omega
,

\phi(t+s,\omega)=\phi(t,\theta_{s}\omega)\circ\phi(s,\omega)
,
\forall s,t\in\mathbb{R}^{+},\ \omega\in\Omega
.

\phi
is called a
C^{1}
smooth random dynamical system if
\phi
is a random dynamical system and for each
(t,\omega)\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\Omega
the mapping

\phi(t,\omega):H\rightarrow H,\ x\mapsto\phi(t,\omega)x

is
C^{1}
.

2.2
Random inertial manifolds

Definition 2.3
.

A multifunction
M=(M(\omega))_{\omega\in\Omega}
of nonempty closed sets
M(\omega)\subset H
,
\omega\in\Omega
, is called a random set if

\displaystyle\omega\mapsto\inf_{y\in M(\omega)}\|x-y\|

is a random variable for all
x\in H
.

Definition 2.4
.

A random set
M(\omega)
is called an invariant set for a random dynamical system
\phi(t,\omega,x)
if

\displaystyle\phi(t,\omega,M(\omega))\subset M(\theta_{t}\omega),\ \forall t\geq 0.

Definition 2.5
.

A invariant set
M=(M(\omega))_{\omega\in\Omega}\subset H
is called Lipschitz (resp.
C^{1}
) inertial manifold of random dynamical system
\phi(t,\omega,x)
if the following properties are satisfied:

(\mathbf{i})

M(\omega)
can be represented by a graph of a Lipschitz (resp.
C^{1}
) map
m(\omega,\cdot):H_{1}\to H_{2}
, i.e.,

\displaystyle M(\omega)=\{\xi+m(\omega,\xi)\mid\xi\in H_{1}\},

where
H_{1}
is a finite dimensional subspace of
H
such that
H=H_{1}\oplus H_{2}
.

(\mathbf{ii})

M
is exponentially attracting: there exists a positive constant
b
such that for every
x\in H
, there is a random variable
K=K(\omega,x)
such that

\displaystyle\text{dist}(\varphi(t,\omega,x),M(\theta_{t}\omega))\leq Ke^{-bt},\ \forall t\geq 0.

We say that
M
has the
asymptotic completeness
property: For any
u_{0}\in H
, there exists
\tilde{u}_{0}=\tilde{u}_{0}(\omega)\in M(\omega)
and
C
,
b>0
such that

\displaystyle\|\varphi(t,\omega,u_{0})-\varphi(t,\omega,\tilde{u}_{0})\|\leq Ce^{-bt}\|u_{0}-\tilde{u}_{0}\|,\ \forall t\geq 0.

Clearly,
M
has the asymptotic completeness property implies that
M
is exponentially attracting.

2.3
Setting

Let
H
be a separable Hilbert spaces with norm
\parallel\cdot\parallel
, we will specify the assumptions on the linear operator
A
and nonlinear term
F
.

Hypothesis 2.1
.

A
is a positive operator with discrete spectrum on
H
, i.e., there exists an orthonormal basis
\{e_{k}\}
of
H
such that

\displaystyle Ae_{k}=\lambda_{k}e_{k},\ with\ 0<\lambda_{1}\leq\lambda_{2}\leq\cdots,\ \lim_{k\to+\infty}\lambda_{k}=+\infty.

For arbitrary
\sigma\geq 0
, we denote by
D(A^{\sigma})
the domain of
A^{\sigma}
, which is a Banach space under the norm
\|\cdot\|_{\sigma}=\|A^{\sigma}\cdot\|
. Let
0\leq\sigma<1
, we make the following hypothesis on
F
.

Hypothesis 2.2
.

F:D(A^{\sigma})\to H
satisfies the Lipschitz condition, i.e., there exists a constant
L>0
such that

\displaystyle\|F(u_{1})-F(u_{2})\|\leq L\|u_{1}-u_{2}\|_{\sigma},\ \forall u_{1},u_{2}\in D(A^{\sigma}),

(2.1)

and
F(0)=0
.

Fixing an integer
N\geq 1
such that
\lambda_{N}<\lambda_{N+1}
and denote by
P=P_{N}
the orthogonal projector onto the space spanned by the first
N
eigenvectors of
A
. Let
Q=I-P
, then one has the following estimates (see
[
8
]
):

\displaystyle\|A^{\sigma}e^{-At}P\|

\displaystyle\leq\lambda_{N}^{\sigma}e^{\lambda_{N}|t|},\ \forall t\in\mathbb{R},

(2.2)

\displaystyle\|e^{-At}Q\|

\displaystyle\leq e^{-\lambda_{N+1}t},\ \forall t\geq 0,

\displaystyle\|A^{\sigma}e^{-At}Q\|

\displaystyle\leq\left[\left(\frac{\sigma}{t}\right)^{\sigma}+\lambda_{N+1}^{\sigma}\right]e^{-\lambda_{N+1}t},\ \forall t>0,\ \forall\sigma>0.

Let
D([0,T],\mathbb{R})
be the space of càdlàg
\mathbb{R}
-valued functions on
[0,T]
. The following
J_{1}
-metric was defined by Skorokhod
[
27
]
:

d_{J_{1}}(\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2})=\inf_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\left\{\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}|\varphi_{1}(t)-\varphi_{2}(\lambda(t))|+\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}s,t\in[0,T]\\
s\neq t\end{subarray}}\left|\log\frac{\lambda(s)-\lambda(t)}{s-t}\right|\right\},\ \varphi_{1},\varphi_{1}\in D([0,T],\mathbb{R}),

where
\Lambda
is the set of all the strictly increasing continuous functions that map
[0,T]
onto itself.
Let
D_{J_{1}}([0,T],\mathbb{R})
be
D([0,T],\mathbb{R})
equipped with the
J_{1}
-metric, and
D_{U}([0,T],\mathbb{R})
denote
D([0,T],\mathbb{R})
equipped with the usual uniform metric
d_{U}
, see
[
3
,
17
,
21
]
.

In this paper, we consider the two-sided subordinated Brownian motion
W_{S_{t}^{\alpha}}
. Let
\mathbb{W}
be the space of all continuous functions from
\mathbb{R}
to
\mathbb{R}
taking zero value at
t=0
, which is endowed the compact open (locally uniform
convergence) topology and Wiener measure
\mu_{\mathbb{W}}
so that the coordinate process
W_{t}(\omega)=\omega_{t}
is a standard one-dimensional Brownian motion. Let
\mathbb{S}
be the space of all increasing and càdlàg functions from
\mathbb{R}
to
\mathbb{R}
with
\lim\limits_{s\to 0}\ell_{s}=0
, which is endowed with the
J_{1}
-Skorokhod metric and the probability
\mu_{\mathbb{S}}
so that the coordinate process

\displaystyle S_{t}^{\alpha}(\ell^{\alpha}):=\ell_{t}^{\alpha}

is an
\frac{\alpha}{2}
-stable subordinator
S_{t}^{\alpha}
(see
[
28
,
32
]
).
Consider the following product probability space:

\displaystyle(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P}):=(\mathbb{W}\times\mathbb{S},\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{W})\times\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{S}),\mu_{\mathbb{W}}\times\mu_{\mathbb{S}})

and define

\displaystyle W_{S_{t}^{\alpha}}=L_{t}^{\alpha}(\omega,\ell^{\alpha}):=\omega_{\ell_{t}^{\alpha}}.

Then
\{L_{t}^{\alpha}\}_{t\geq 0}
is an
\alpha
-stable process on
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
. Since
(\mathbb{W},\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{W}),\mu_{\mathbb{W}})
and
(\mathbb{S},\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{S}),\mu_{\mathbb{S}})
are two Polish spaces, then
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
is also a Polish space. The driving system
\theta
on
\Omega
is defined by the shift operator

\displaystyle\theta_{t}(\omega_{\ell_{s}^{\alpha}})=\omega_{\ell_{t+s}^{\alpha}}-\omega_{\ell_{t}^{\alpha}},

which satisfies the condition in Definition
2.1
.
Then the map
(t,\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}})\mapsto\theta_{t}\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}}
is continuous, thus measurable, and the Lévy probability measure
\mathbb{P}
is
\theta
-invariant, see
[
19
]
.

3
Convergence of solutions

In this section, we show the solutions of equations (
1.2
) converge to the solutions of equation (
1.3
) as
\alpha\rightarrow 2
. Firstly, we give the conjugate random equations of equation (
1.2
) and equation (
1.3
), respectively.
Let
W_{t}
be a two-sided standard Brownian motion, then
W_{S_{t}^{\alpha}}=L_{t}^{\alpha}(\omega,\ell^{\alpha})
is a two-sided symmetric
\alpha
-stable Lévy motion on
\mathbb{R}
. We consider the Langevin equation

\displaystyle dz=-zdt+dW_{S_{t}^{\alpha}}.

(3.1)

A solution of this equation is called an Ornstein–Uhlenbeck process. Moreover, for every
\alpha\in(1,2)
, we have the following lemmas from
[
19
]
.

Lemma 3.1
.

(\mathbf{i})
There exists a
\{\theta_{t}:t\in\mathbb{R}\}
-invariant set (still denoted as)
\Omega
of full measure such that the sample paths
\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}_{t}}
of
W_{S_{t}^{\alpha}}
satisfy

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\pm\infty}\frac{\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}_{t}}}{t}=0,\quad\omega\in\Omega.

(\mathbf{ii})
The random variable

\displaystyle z(\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}})=-\int_{-\infty}^{0}e^{s}\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}_{s}}ds,\quad\omega\in\Omega

is well-defined and the unique stationary solution of (
3.1
) is given by

\displaystyle z(\theta_{t}\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}})

\displaystyle=-\int_{-\infty}^{0}e^{s}\theta_{t}\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}_{s}}ds=\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}_{t}}-\int_{-\infty}^{0}e^{s}\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}_{t+s}}ds.

Moreover, the mapping
t\mapsto z(\theta_{t}\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}})
is càdlàg.

(\mathbf{iii})
In addition,

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\pm\infty}\frac{|z(\theta_{t}\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}})|}{|t|}=0,\ \mathrm{and}\ \lim_{t\to\pm\infty}\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}z(\theta_{s}\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}})ds=0.

We now replace
\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{W})\times\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{S})
by

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}=\{\Omega\cap B:B\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{W})\times\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{S})\}

for
\Omega
given in Lemma
3.1
. The probability measure is the restriction of
\mu_{\mathbb{W}}\times\mu_{\mathbb{S}}
to this new
\sigma
-algebra, also denoted by
\mathbb{P}
. In the following, we will consider the metric dynamical system
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P},\mathbb{R},\theta)
. Note that for standard Brownian motion
W_{t}
, we have the similar results, see
[
13
]
.

Lemma 3.2
.

For every
p
in
(0,2)
and
T>0
,

\displaystyle\lim_{\alpha\to 2}\mathbb{E}\sup_{-T\leq t\leq T}\left|z(\theta_{t}\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}})-z(\theta_{t}\omega)\right|^{p}=0.

This lemma characterizes the convergence behavior of the stationary Ornstein–Uhlenbeck process
z(\theta_{t}\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}})
as
\alpha\rightarrow 2
.

Let
u^{\alpha}=e^{-z(\theta_{t}\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}})}v^{\alpha}
. Then,
u^{\alpha}
satisfies the following random differential equation

\frac{du^{\alpha}}{dt}+Au^{\alpha}=z(\theta_{t}\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}})u^{\alpha}+G(\theta_{t}\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}},u^{\alpha}),

(3.2)

where
G(\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}},u^{\alpha}):=e^{-z(\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}})}F(e^{z(\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}})}u^{\alpha})
. We denote
u^{\alpha}(t,\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}},x)
by the solution of (
3.2
) with initial condition
u^{\alpha}(0,\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}},x)=x
. It is easy to verify that
G
has the same Lipschitz constant
L
as
F
. Obviously, the solution mapping
(t,\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}},x)\mapsto u^{\alpha}(t,\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}},x)
generates a random dynamical system.

Similarly, let
u=e^{-z(\theta_{t}\omega)}v
, we get random differential equation

\frac{du}{dt}+Au=z(\theta_{t}\omega)u+G(\theta_{t}\omega,u),

(3.3)

where
G(\omega,u):=e^{-z(\omega)}F(e^{z(\omega)}u)
. The same conclusions as for equation (
3.2
) also hold for equation (
3.3
).

For
x\in H
and
\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}}\in\Omega
, we introduce the transformation

\displaystyle T(\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}},x)=e^{-z(\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}})}x,

and its inverse transformation

\displaystyle T^{-1}(\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}},x)=e^{z(\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}})}x.

Observe that

\displaystyle v^{\alpha}(t,\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}},x)=T^{-1}(\theta_{t}\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}},\cdot)\circ u^{\alpha}(t,\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}},T(\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}},x)),

from which we find that the solution mapping
(t,\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}},x)\mapsto v^{\alpha}(t,\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}},x)
also generate a random dynamical system.

Recall that
-A
is a generator of analytic semigroup
e^{-At}
. There exists constants
\kappa>0
and
M\geq 1
such that

\displaystyle\|A^{\sigma}e^{-At}\|\leq Mt^{-\sigma}e^{-\kappa t},\ \forall t>0,

(3.4)

see
[
24
]
.

Next we give the estimate of
u(t,\omega,x)
.

Lemma 3.3
.

For every
\omega\in\mathbb{W}\subset\Omega
and
x\in H^{\sigma}
, there exists a positive constant
C
depending on
T
,
\sigma
and
x
such that

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|u(t,\omega,x)\|_{\sigma}\leq C.

(3.5)

Proof.

Following (
3.3
), we have

\displaystyle u(t,\omega,x)=e^{-At+\int_{0}^{t}z(\theta_{r}\omega)dr}x+\int_{0}^{t}e^{-A(t-s)+\int_{s}^{t}z(\theta_{r}\omega)dr}G(\theta_{s}\omega,u(s,\omega,x))ds.

By Hypothesis
2.2
and (
3.4
), for all
t\in[0,T]
, we get

\displaystyle\|u(t,\omega,x)\|_{\sigma}\leq Me^{-\kappa t}N_{\omega,T}\|x\|_{\sigma}+MLN_{\omega,T}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\sigma}e^{-\kappa(t-s)}\|u(s)\|_{\sigma}ds,

where
N_{\omega,T}=\int_{0}^{T}|z(\theta_{r}\omega)|dr
. Let

\displaystyle E_{\sigma}(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{+\infty}x^{n(1-\sigma)}/\Gamma(n(1-\sigma)+1),\ \text{for }x\geq 0,

\displaystyle\theta

\displaystyle=\left[MLN_{\omega,T}\Gamma(1-\sigma)\right]^{1/(1-\sigma)}.

Here
\Gamma(\cdot)
is the Gamma function. It then follows from
[
16
, Lemma 7.1.1]
that

\displaystyle e^{\kappa t}\|u(t,\omega,x)\|_{\sigma}\leq e^{\kappa T}MN_{\omega,T}\|x\|_{\sigma}E_{\sigma}(\theta t),\ \forall t\in[0,T].

Thus (
3.5
) holds. This completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 3.4
.

For every
\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}}\in\Omega
,
x\in H^{\sigma}
and
T>0
, we have

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|u^{\alpha}(t,\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}},x)-u(t,\omega,x)\|_{\sigma}\rightarrow 0

(3.6)

in probability as
\alpha\rightarrow 2
.

Proof.

Let
\tilde{u}^{\alpha}(t)=u^{\alpha}(t)-u(t)
, by (
3.2
) and (
3.3
), we get

\displaystyle\tilde{u}^{\alpha}(t)=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}e^{-A(t-s)+\int_{s}^{t}z(\theta_{r}\omega)dr}\Big[G(\theta_{s}\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}},u^{\alpha}(s))-G(\theta_{s}\omega,u(s))

\displaystyle\ \ \ +\big(z(\theta_{s}\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}})-z(\theta_{s}\omega)\big)u(s)\Big]ds.

It follows from (
2.1
) that

\displaystyle\|G(\theta_{s}\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}},u^{\alpha}(s))-G(\theta_{s}\omega,u(s))\|\leq L\|\tilde{u}^{\alpha}(s)\|_{\sigma}+2L|e^{z(\theta_{s}\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}})-z(\theta_{s}\omega)}-1|\|u(s)\|_{\sigma}.

Along with (
3.4
) and Lemma
3.3
, we have

\displaystyle\|\tilde{u}^{\alpha}(t)\|_{\sigma}\leq

\displaystyle ML\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\sigma}e^{-\kappa(t-s)}e^{\int_{s}^{t}z(\theta_{r}\omega)dr}\|\tilde{u}^{\alpha}(s)\|_{\sigma}ds

\displaystyle+CM\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\sigma}e^{-\kappa(t-s)}e^{\int_{s}^{t}z(\theta_{r}\omega)dr}\big[2L|e^{z(\theta_{s}\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}})-z(\theta_{s}\omega)}-1|+|z(\theta_{s}\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}})-z(\theta_{s}\omega)|\big]ds.

Applying
[
16
, Lemma 7.1.1]
, we obtain that

\displaystyle\|\tilde{u}^{\alpha}(t)\|_{\sigma}\leq

\displaystyle CMN_{\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}},T}E_{\sigma}(\tilde{\theta}t)

\displaystyle\times\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\sigma}e^{-\kappa(t-s)}\big[2L|e^{z(\theta_{s}\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}})-z(\theta_{s}\omega)}-1|+|z(\theta_{s}\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}})-z(\theta_{s}\omega)|\big]ds,

where
N_{\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}},T}=\int_{0}^{T}|z(\theta_{r}\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}})|dr
and

\tilde{\theta}=\left[MLN_{\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}},T}\Gamma(1-\sigma)\right]^{1/(1-\sigma)}
.

By Lemma
3.2
, for each subsequence
\{\alpha_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
, there exists a sub-subsequence
\{\alpha_{n(k)}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
such that
z(\theta_{s}\omega_{\ell^{\alpha_{n(k)}}})
converges to
z(\theta_{s}\omega)
in
D_{u}([0,T],\mathbb{R})
, almost surely, i.e., for every
T>0
,

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\sup_{s\in[0,T]}|z(\theta_{s}\omega_{\ell^{\alpha_{n(k)}}})-z(\theta_{s}\omega)|=0,

which implies that for every
\varepsilon>0
, there exist
K>0
such that for every
k\geq K
,

\displaystyle\sup_{s\in[0,T]}|z(\theta_{s}\omega_{\ell^{\alpha_{n(k)}}})-z(\theta_{s}\omega)|\leq\varepsilon.

This yields that

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|\tilde{u}^{\alpha_{n(k)}}(t)\|_{\sigma}\leq CMN_{\omega_{\ell^{\alpha_{n(k)}}},T}E_{\sigma}(\tilde{\theta}t)\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\sigma}e^{-\kappa(t-s)}\big(2L|e^{\varepsilon}-1|+\varepsilon\big)ds.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|u^{\alpha_{n(k)}}(t)-u(t)\|_{\sigma}\rightarrow 0,\ \text{almost surely}.

This implies that

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|u^{\alpha}(t)-u(t)\|_{\sigma}\rightarrow 0

in probability as
\alpha\rightarrow 2
. The proof is complete.
∎

Theorem 3.1
.

Assume Hypothesis
2.1
and
2.2
hold. For every
\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}}\in\Omega
,
x\in H^{\sigma}
and
T>0
, we have

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|v^{\alpha}(t,\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}},x)-v(t,\omega,x)\|_{\sigma}\rightarrow 0

(3.7)

in probability as

and in
D_{U}([0,T],H^{\sigma})
as well as
D_{J_{1}}([0,T],H^{\sigma})
.

Proof.

As in Lemma
3.4
, for each subsequence
\{\alpha_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
, there exists a sub-subsequence
\{\alpha_{n(k)}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
such that

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|z(\theta_{t}\omega_{\ell^{\alpha_{n(k)}}})-z(\theta_{t}\omega)|\rightarrow 0

and

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|u^{\alpha_{n(k)}}(t,\omega_{\ell^{\alpha_{n(k)}}},x)-u(t,\omega,x)\|_{\sigma}\rightarrow 0,\ \text{almost surely}.

Also we can get

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|e^{z(\theta_{t}\omega_{\ell^{\alpha_{n(k)}}})}-e^{z(\theta_{t}\omega)}|\rightarrow 0,\ \text{almost surely}.

Notice that

\displaystyle\|v^{\alpha_{n(k)}}(t,\omega_{\ell^{\alpha_{n(k)}}},x)-v(t,\omega,x)\|_{\sigma}

\displaystyle\leq|e^{z(\theta_{t}\omega_{\ell^{\alpha_{n(k)}}})}|\Big[\|u^{\alpha_{n(k)}}(t,\omega_{\ell^{\alpha_{n(k)}}},e^{-z(\omega_{\ell^{\alpha_{n(k)}}})}x)-u(t,\omega,e^{-z(\omega_{\ell^{\alpha_{n(k)}}})}x)\|_{\sigma}

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ +|\|u(t,\omega,e^{-z(\omega_{\ell^{\alpha_{n(k)}}})}x)-u(t,\omega,e^{-z(\omega)}x)\|_{\sigma}\Big]

\displaystyle\ \ \ +|e^{z(\theta_{t}\omega_{\ell^{\alpha_{n(k)}}})}-e^{z(\theta_{t}\omega)}|\|u(t,\omega,e^{-z(\omega)}x)\|_{\sigma},

which along with Lemma
3.3
and

, we have

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|v^{\alpha_{n(k)}}(t,\omega_{\ell^{\alpha_{n(k)}}},x)-v(t,\omega,x)\|_{\sigma}\rightarrow 0,\ \text{almost surely}

in

. Therefore,

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|v^{\alpha}(t,\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}},x)-v(t,\omega,x)\|_{\sigma}\rightarrow 0

in probability as

and in

.
∎

4
Convergence of inertial manifolds

In this section, we first prove the existence and
C^{1}
-smoothness of inertial manifolds for equation (
3.2
) and equation (
3.3
). Then we show the convergence of
C^{1}
inertial manifolds for equation (
3.2
) and equation (
3.3
) as
\alpha\rightarrow 2
.

Let
\mathcal{C}_{\beta}^{\alpha,-}
be a Banach space defined by

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{\beta}^{\alpha,-}=\Big\{\varphi\in C((-\infty,0],D(A^{\sigma}))\ \big|\sup_{t\leq 0}\big\{e^{\beta t-\int_{0}^{t}z(\theta_{r}\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}})dr}\|\varphi(t)\|_{\sigma}\big\}<+\infty\Big\}

with the norm

\displaystyle\|\varphi\|_{\mathcal{C}_{\beta}^{\alpha,-}}=\sup_{t\leq 0}\big\{e^{\beta t-\int_{0}^{t}z(\theta_{r}\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}})dr}\|\varphi(t)\|_{\sigma}\big\}.

Here
\beta:=\lambda_{N}+\frac{2}{\mu}L\lambda_{N}^{\sigma}
,
\mu\in(0,1)
, which is in
(\lambda_{N},\lambda_{N+1})
. We denote the set

\displaystyle\mathscr{M}^{\alpha}(\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}})=\big\{x\in D(A^{\sigma})\ \big|\ u^{\alpha}(t,\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}},x)\in\mathcal{C}_{\beta}^{\alpha,-}\big\}.

In order to prove that
\mathscr{M}^{\alpha}(\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}})
is a inertial manifold, we first need the following lemma.

Lemma 4.1
.

x\in\mathscr{M}^{\alpha}(\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}})
if and only if there exists a function
u^{\alpha}(\cdot)\in\mathcal{C}_{\beta}^{\alpha,-}
with
u^{\alpha}(0)=x
and satisfies

\displaystyle u^{\alpha}(t)

\displaystyle=e^{-At+\int_{0}^{t}z(\theta_{r}\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}})dr}\xi+\int_{0}^{t}e^{-A(t-s)+\int_{s}^{t}z(\theta_{r}\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}})dr}PG(\theta_{s}\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}},u^{\alpha}(s))ds

\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{-\infty}^{t}e^{-A(t-s)+\int_{s}^{t}z(\theta_{r}\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}})dr}QG(\theta_{s}\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}},u^{\alpha}(s))ds,

(4.1)

where
\xi=Px
.

Proof.

Let
x\in\mathscr{M}^{\alpha}(\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}})
, by the variation of constants formula, for
\tau>t
, we have that

\displaystyle Pu^{\alpha}(t,\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}},x)=

\displaystyle e^{-At+\int_{0}^{t}z(\theta_{r}\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}})dr}Px+\int_{0}^{t}e^{-A(t-s)+\int_{s}^{t}z(\theta_{r}\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}})dr}PG(\theta_{s}\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}},u^{\alpha}(s))ds,

and

\displaystyle Qu^{\alpha}(t,\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}},x)=

\displaystyle e^{-A(t-\tau)+\int_{s}^{t}z(\theta_{r}\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}})dr}Qu^{\alpha}(\tau,\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}},x)

\displaystyle+\int_{\tau}^{t}e^{-A(t-s)+\int_{s}^{t}z(\theta_{r}\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}})dr}QG(\theta_{s}\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}},u^{\alpha}(s))ds.

From (
2.2
), we get

\displaystyle\|e^{-A(t-\tau)+\int_{\tau}^{t}z(\theta_{r}\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}})dr}Qu^{\alpha}(\tau,\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}},x)\|_{\sigma}

\displaystyle\leq e^{-\lambda_{N+1}(t-\tau)}e^{-\beta\tau+\int_{0}^{t}z(\theta_{r}\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}})dr}\|u^{\alpha}\|_{{\mathcal{C}_{\beta}^{\alpha,-}}}\rightarrow 0,\ as\ \tau\rightarrow-\infty.

Then we have

\displaystyle Qu^{\alpha}(t,\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}},x)=\int_{-\infty}^{t}e^{-A(t-s)+\int_{s}^{t}z(\theta_{r}\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}})dr}QG(\theta_{s}\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}},u^{\alpha}(s))ds.

Thus (
4.1
) can be obtained. The converse follows by direct computation.
∎

Theorem 4.1
.

Assume that the spectral gap condition

\displaystyle\lambda_{N+1}-\lambda_{N}\geq\frac{2L}{\mu}\left(\lambda_{N}^{\sigma}+\sigma^{\sigma}\Gamma(1-\sigma)(\lambda_{N+1}-\lambda_{N})^{\sigma}+\lambda_{N+1}^{\sigma}\right)

(4.2)

holds for some
\mu\in(0,1)
. Then there exists a Lipschitz inertial manifold for equation (
3.2
), which is given by

\displaystyle\mathscr{M}^{\alpha}(\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}})=\big\{\xi+\psi^{\alpha}(\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}},\xi)|\xi\in PD(A^{\sigma})\big\},

where
\psi^{\alpha}(\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}},\cdot):PD(A^{\sigma})\rightarrow QD(A^{\sigma})
is Lipschitz continuous, and
\psi^{\alpha}
is measurable in
(\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}},\xi)
. Furthermore,
\widetilde{\mathscr{M}}^{\alpha}(\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}})=T^{-1}(\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}},\mathscr{M}^{\alpha}(\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}}))
, i.e.,

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathscr{M}}^{\alpha}(\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}})=\left\{\xi+e^{z(\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}})}\psi^{\alpha}(\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}},e^{-z(\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}})}\xi)|\xi\in PD(A^{\sigma})\right\}

is the Lipschitz inertial manifold of equation (
1.2
).

In addition, if
F\in C^{1}
, then
\mathscr{M}^{\alpha}(\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}})
is a
C^{1}
inertial manifold for equation (
3.2
), i.e.,
\psi^{\alpha}(\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}},\xi)
is
C^{1}
in
\xi
. Consequently,
\widetilde{\mathscr{M}}^{\alpha}(\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}})
is a
C^{1}
inertial manifold of equation (
1.2
).

Proof.

We prove the theorem in five steps.

\mathbf{Step\ 1}
. We first prove that equation (
4.1
) has a unique solution
u^{\alpha}=u^{\alpha}(\cdot,\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}},\xi)
in
\mathcal{C}_{\beta}^{\alpha,-}
. Denote the right-hand side of (
4.1
) by
\mathcal{Y}(u^{\alpha},\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}},\xi)
. Multiplying both side of (
4.1
) by
e^{\beta t-\int_{0}^{t}z(\theta_{r}\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}})dr}
, it follows from (
2.1
) and (
2.2
) that

\displaystyle e^{\beta t-\int_{0}^{t}z(\theta_{r}\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}})dr}\Big\|\mathcal{Y}(u^{\alpha},\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}},\xi)\Big\|_{\sigma}

\displaystyle\leq e^{(\beta-\lambda_{N})t}\|\xi\|_{\sigma}+L\lambda_{N}^{\sigma}\int_{t}^{0}e^{(\beta-\lambda_{N})(t-s)}\|u^{\alpha}\|_{\mathcal{C}_{\beta}^{\alpha,-}}ds

\displaystyle\ \ \ +L\int_{-\infty}^{t}\Big[\Big(\frac{\sigma}{t-s}\Big)^{\sigma}+\lambda_{N+1}^{\sigma}\Big]e^{(\beta-\lambda_{N+1})(t-s)}\|u^{\alpha}\|_{\mathcal{C}_{\beta}^{\alpha,-}}ds

\displaystyle\leq\|\xi\|_{\sigma}+L\left(\frac{\lambda_{N}^{\sigma}}{\beta-\lambda_{N}}+\frac{\sigma^{\sigma}\Gamma(1-\sigma)}{(\lambda_{N+1}-\beta)^{1-\sigma}}+\frac{\lambda_{N+1}^{\sigma}}{\lambda_{N+1}-\beta}\right)\|u^{\alpha}\|_{\mathcal{C}_{\beta}^{\alpha,-}},

which implies that
\mathcal{Y}(u^{\alpha},\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}},\xi)
maps
\mathcal{C}_{\beta}^{\alpha,-}
into itself.

For any
u_{1}^{\alpha},u_{2}^{\alpha}\in\mathcal{C}_{\beta}^{\alpha,-}
, we have

\displaystyle\Big\|\mathcal{Y}(u_{1}^{\alpha},\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}},\xi)-\mathcal{Y}(u_{2}^{\alpha},\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}},\xi)\Big\|_{\mathcal{C}_{\beta}^{\alpha,-}}

\displaystyle\leq L\lambda_{N}^{\sigma}\int_{t}^{0}e^{(\beta-\lambda_{N})(t-s)}ds\|u_{1}^{\alpha}-u_{2}^{\alpha}\|_{\mathcal{C}_{\beta}^{\alpha,-}}

\displaystyle\ \ \ +L\int_{-\infty}^{t}\Big[\Big(\frac{\sigma}{t-s}\Big)^{\sigma}+\lambda_{N+1}^{\sigma}\Big]e^{(\beta-\lambda_{N+1})(t-s)}ds\|u_{1}^{\alpha}-u_{2}^{\alpha}\|_{\mathcal{C}_{\beta}^{\alpha,-}}

\displaystyle\leq L\left(\frac{\lambda_{N}^{\sigma}}{\beta-\lambda_{N}}+\frac{\sigma^{\sigma}\Gamma(1-\sigma)}{(\lambda_{N+1}-\beta)^{1-\sigma}}+\frac{\lambda_{N+1}^{\sigma}}{\lambda_{N+1}-\beta}\right)\|u_{1}^{\alpha}-u_{2}^{\alpha}\|_{\mathcal{C}_{\beta}^{\alpha,-}}.

Since
\lambda_{N+1}-\beta\leq\lambda_{N+1}-\lambda_{N}
, we find that

\displaystyle L\left(\frac{\lambda_{N}^{\sigma}}{\beta-\lambda_{N}}+\frac{\sigma^{\sigma}\Gamma(1-\sigma)}{(\lambda_{N+1}-\beta)^{1-\sigma}}+\frac{\lambda_{N+1}^{\sigma}}{\lambda_{N+1}-\beta}\right)

\displaystyle\leq L\left(\frac{\lambda_{N}^{\sigma}}{\beta-\lambda_{N}}+\frac{\sigma^{\sigma}\Gamma(1-\sigma)(\lambda_{N+1}-\lambda_{N})^{\sigma}+\lambda_{N+1}^{\sigma}}{\lambda_{N+1}-\beta}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\mu<1.

Thus, (
4.2
) implies that
\mathcal{Y}(\cdot,\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}},\xi)
is a contraction map. Applying the contraction mapping principle, the mapping
\mathcal{Y}(\cdot,\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}},\xi)
has a unique fixed point
\bar{u}^{\alpha}(\cdot,\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}},\xi)\in\mathcal{C}_{\beta}^{\alpha,-}
. Then, for any
\xi_{1},\xi_{2}\in PD(A^{\sigma})
, we get

\displaystyle\|\bar{u}^{\alpha}(t,\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}},\xi_{1})-\bar{u}^{\alpha}(t,\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}},\xi_{2})\|_{\mathcal{C}_{\beta}^{\alpha,-}}

\displaystyle\leq\|\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}\|+\mu\|u^{\alpha}(\cdot,\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}},\xi_{1})-u^{\alpha}(\cdot,\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}},\xi_{2})\|_{\mathcal{C}_{\beta}^{\alpha,-}}.

Hence, we obtain

\displaystyle\|\bar{u}^{\alpha}(t,\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}},\xi_{1})-\bar{u}^{\alpha}(t,\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}},\xi_{2})\|_{\mathcal{C}_{\beta}^{\alpha,-}}\leq(1-\mu)^{-1}\|\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}\|_{\sigma}.

(4.3)

This implies that
\bar{u}^{\alpha}
is Lipschitz continuous in
\xi
.

\mathbf{Step\ 2}
. We prove the existence of Lipschitz invariant manifolds for equation (
3.2
). Let
\psi^{\alpha}(\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}},\xi)=Q\bar{u}^{\alpha}(0,\omega,\xi)
, it follows from (
4.1
) that

\displaystyle\psi^{\alpha}(\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}},\xi)=\int_{-\infty}^{0}e^{As+\int_{s}^{0}z(\theta_{r}\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}})dr}QG(\theta_{s}\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}},\bar{u}^{\alpha}(s,\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}},\xi))ds.

By Lemma
4.1
, we get

\displaystyle\mathscr{M}^{\alpha}(\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}})=\big\{\xi+\psi^{\alpha}(\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}},\xi)|\xi\in PD(A^{\sigma})\big\}.

For any
\xi_{1},\xi_{2}\in PD(A^{\sigma})
, by using (
4.3
), we have

\displaystyle\|\psi^{\alpha}(\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}},\xi_{1})-\psi^{\alpha}(\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}},\xi_{2})\|_{\sigma}

\displaystyle\leq L\int_{-\infty}^{0}\Big[\Big(\frac{\sigma}{-s}\Big)^{\sigma}+\lambda_{N+1}^{\sigma}\Big]e^{(\lambda_{N+1}-\beta)s}ds\|\bar{u}^{\alpha}(t,\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}},\xi_{1})-\bar{u}^{\alpha}(t,\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}},\xi_{2})\|_{\mathcal{C}_{\beta}^{\alpha,-}}

\displaystyle\leq L(1-\mu)^{-1}\left(\frac{\sigma^{\sigma}\Gamma(1-\sigma)}{(\lambda_{N+1}-\beta)^{1-\sigma}}+\frac{\lambda_{N+1}^{\sigma}}{\lambda_{N+1}-\beta}\right)\|\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}\|_{\sigma}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\mu}{2(1-\mu)}\|\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}\|_{\sigma},

(4.4)

where the last inequality follows from (
4.2
). Hence,
\psi^{\alpha}(\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}},\xi)
is Lipschitz continuous in
\xi\in PD(A^{\sigma})
.

Then, we show that
\mathscr{M}^{\alpha}(\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}})
is a random set. Since
D(A^{\sigma})
is a dense subspace of separable Banach space
H
,
D(A^{\sigma})
is also separable.
Let
D(A^{\sigma})_{c}
be a countable dense set of
D(A^{\sigma})
. For each
x\in D(A^{\sigma})
, we observe that

\displaystyle\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}}\mapsto\inf_{y\in D(A^{\sigma})}\big\{\|x-(Py+\psi^{\alpha}(\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}},Py))\|_{\sigma}\big\}=\inf_{y\in D(A^{\sigma})_{c}}\big\{\|x-(Py+\psi^{\alpha}(\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}},Py))\|_{\sigma}\big\}.

By
[
9
, Theorem III.14]
, we obtain that
\mathscr{M}^{\alpha}(\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}})
is
\mathcal{F}
-measurable. Finally, we prove that
\mathscr{M}^{\alpha}(\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}})
is invariant, i.e., for any
s\geq 0
,

\displaystyle u^{\alpha}(s,\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}},\mathscr{M}^{\alpha}(\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}}))\subset\mathscr{M}^{\alpha}(\theta_{s}\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}}).

For any
x\in\mathscr{M}^{\alpha}(\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}})
, we have
u^{\alpha}(t,\theta_{s}\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}},u^{\alpha}(s,\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}},x))=u^{\alpha}(t+s,\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}},x)
. Hence

u^{\alpha}(s,\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}},x)\in\mathscr{M}^{\alpha}(\theta_{s}\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}})
, which implies that
u^{\alpha}(s,\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}},\mathscr{M}^{\alpha}(\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}}))\subset\mathscr{M}^{\alpha}(\theta_{s}\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}})
. Thus
\mathscr{M}^{\alpha}(\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}})
is a Lipschitz invariant manifold.

\mathbf{Step\ 3}
. We prove that
\mathscr{M}^{\alpha}(\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}})
has the asymptotic completeness property.
Let
\mathcal{C}_{\beta}^{\alpha,+}
is a Banach space given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{\beta}^{\alpha,+}=\Big\{\varphi\in C([0,+\infty),D(A^{\sigma})\ \big|\sup_{t\geq 0}\big\{e^{\beta t-\int_{0}^{t}z(\theta_{r}\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}})dr}\|\varphi(t)\|_{\sigma}\big\}<+\infty\Big\}

with the norm

\displaystyle\|\varphi\|_{\mathcal{C}_{\beta}^{\alpha,+}}=\sup_{t\geq 0}\big\{e^{\beta t-\int_{0}^{t}z(\theta_{r}\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}})dr}\|\varphi(t)\|_{\sigma}\big\}.

For any
y^{\alpha}\in\mathcal{C}_{\beta}^{\alpha,+}
,
x\in D(A^{\sigma})
, using the same procedure as Lemma
4.1
, we observe that
y^{\alpha}+u^{\alpha}(s,\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}},x)

is a solution of equation (
3.2
) if and only if there exists some
p\in QD(A^{\sigma})
such that
y^{\alpha}
satisfies

\displaystyle y^{\alpha}(t)=

\displaystyle e^{-At+\int_{0}^{t}z(\theta_{r}\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}})dr}p+\int_{0}^{t}e^{-A(t-s)+\int_{s}^{t}z(\theta_{r}\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}})dr}Q\Delta G(\theta_{s}\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}},y^{\alpha}(s),u^{\alpha}(s,\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}},x))ds

\displaystyle+\int_{+\infty}^{t}e^{-A(t-s)+\int_{s}^{t}z(\theta_{r}\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}})dr}P\Delta G(\theta_{s}\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}},y^{\alpha}(s),u^{\alpha}(s,\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}},x))ds,

(4.5)

where

\displaystyle\Delta G(\theta_{s}\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}},y^{\alpha}(s),u^{\alpha}(s,\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}},x))=G(\theta_{s}\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}},y^{\alpha}(s)+u^{\alpha}(s,\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}},x))-G(\theta_{s}\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}},u^{\alpha}(s,\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}},x)).

Denote the right hand side of (
4
) by
\mathcal{P}(y^{\alpha},\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}},p)
, by (
2.1
) and (
2.2
) we get for
t\geq 0
,

\displaystyle e^{\beta t-\int_{0}^{t}z(\theta_{r}\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}})dr}\Big\|\mathcal{P}(y^{\alpha},\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}},p)\Big\|_{\sigma}

\displaystyle\leq e^{(\beta-\lambda_{N+1})t}\|p\|_{\sigma}+L\int_{0}^{t}\Big[\Big(\frac{\sigma}{t-s}\Big)^{\sigma}+\lambda_{N+1}^{\sigma}\Big]e^{(\beta-\lambda_{N+1})(t-s)}\|y^{\alpha}\|_{\mathcal{C}_{\beta}^{\alpha,+}}ds

\displaystyle\ \ \ +L\lambda_{N}^{\sigma}\int_{t}^{+\infty}e^{(\beta-\lambda_{N})(t-s)}\|y^{\alpha}\|_{\mathcal{C}_{\beta}^{\alpha,+}}ds

\displaystyle\leq\|p\|_{\sigma}+L\left(\frac{\lambda_{N}^{\sigma}}{\beta-\lambda_{N}}+\frac{\sigma^{\sigma}\Gamma(1-\sigma)}{(\lambda_{N+1}-\beta)^{1-\sigma}}+\frac{\lambda_{N+1}^{\sigma}}{\lambda_{N+1}-\beta}\right)\|y^{\alpha}\|_{\mathcal{C}_{\beta}^{\alpha,+}}

\displaystyle\leq\|p\|_{\sigma}+\mu\|y^{\alpha}\|_{\mathcal{C}_{\beta}^{\alpha,+}}.

This shows that
\mathcal{P}(y^{\alpha},\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}},p)
maps
\mathcal{C}_{\beta}^{\alpha,+}
into itself. Then, for any
y_{1}^{\alpha},y_{2}^{\alpha}\in\mathcal{C}_{\beta}^{\alpha,+}
, we have

\displaystyle\Big\|\mathcal{P}(y_{1}^{\alpha},\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}},p)-\mathcal{P}(y_{2}^{\alpha},\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}},p)\Big\|_{\mathcal{C}_{\beta}^{\alpha,+}}\leq\mu\|y_{1}^{\alpha}-y_{2}^{\alpha}\|_{\mathcal{C}_{\beta}^{\alpha,+}},

which implies that
\mathcal{P}(y^{\alpha},\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}},p)
is a contraction map. By the contraction mapping principle,
\mathcal{P}(y^{\alpha},\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}},p)
has a unique fixed point
\bar{y}^{\alpha}(\cdot,\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}},p)
and satisfies

\displaystyle\|\bar{y}^{\alpha}(\cdot,\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}},p)\|_{\mathcal{C}_{\beta}^{\alpha,+}}\leq(1-\mu)^{-1}\|p\|_{\sigma}.

(4.6)

Consequently, we find that
\bar{y}^{\alpha}(\cdot,\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}},p)+u^{\alpha}(\cdot,\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}},x)
is a solution of equation (
3.2
). This implies that there exists a
\tilde{x}\in D(A^{\sigma})
such that

\displaystyle u^{\alpha}(t,\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}},\tilde{x})=\bar{y}^{\alpha}(t,\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}},p)+u^{\alpha}(t,\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}},x),

(4.7)

where
\tilde{x}=\bar{y}^{\alpha}(0,\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}},p)+x
.

Following from Step 2, we can see that
\tilde{x}\in\mathscr{M}^{\alpha}(\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}})
if and only if
Q\tilde{x}=\psi^{\alpha}(\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}},P\tilde{x})
. Hence, letting

\displaystyle p=\psi^{\alpha}(\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}},\int_{+\infty}^{0}e^{As+\int_{s}^{0}z(\theta_{r}\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}})dr}P\Delta G(\theta_{s}\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}},y^{\alpha}(s),u^{\alpha}(s,\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}},x))ds+Px)-Qx,

we have that
\tilde{x}\in\mathscr{M}^{\alpha}(\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}})
. Combining (
4.6
) and (
4.7
), we obtain that

\displaystyle\|u^{\alpha}(t,\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}},x)-u^{\alpha}(t,\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}},\tilde{x})\|_{\sigma}\leq e^{-\beta t+\int_{0}^{t}z(\theta_{r}\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}})dr}(1-\mu)^{-1}\|x-\tilde{x}\|_{\sigma}.

According to Lemma
3.1
, we have that
e^{\int_{0}^{t}z(\theta_{r}\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}})dr}
satisfies the sublinear growth, there exists a random variable
C(\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}})
such that

\displaystyle e^{\int_{0}^{t}z(\theta_{r}\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}})dr}\leq C(\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}})e^{\frac{1}{2}\beta t}.

Thus we arrive at the asymptotic completeness property of
\mathscr{M}^{\alpha}(\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}})
as

\displaystyle\|u^{\alpha}(t,\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}},x)-u^{\alpha}(t,\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}},\tilde{x})\|_{\sigma}\leq C(\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}})(1-\mu)^{-1}e^{-\frac{1}{2}\beta t}\|x-\tilde{x}\|_{\sigma}.

(4.8)

Therefore,
\mathscr{M}^{\alpha}(\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}})
is a Lipschitz inertial manifolds for equation (
3.2
).

\mathbf{Step\ 4}
. We show the existence of Lipschitz inertial manifolds for equation (
1.2
). Notice that
\widetilde{\mathscr{M}}^{\alpha}(\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}})=T^{-1}(\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}},\mathscr{M}^{\alpha}(\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}}))
is a Lipschitz invariant manifold of equation (
1.2
), and

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathscr{M}}^{\alpha}(\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}})

\displaystyle=\Big\{T^{-1}(\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}},\xi+\psi^{\alpha}(\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}},\xi))|\xi\in PD(A^{\sigma})\Big\}

\displaystyle=\left\{e^{z(\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}})}(\xi+\psi^{\alpha}(\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}},\xi))|\xi\in PD(A^{\sigma})\right\}

\displaystyle=\left\{\xi+e^{z(\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}})}\psi^{\alpha}(\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}},e^{-z(\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}})}\xi)|\xi\in PD(A^{\sigma})\right\}.

In fact, we find that

\displaystyle v^{\alpha}(t,\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}},\widetilde{\mathscr{M}}^{\alpha}(\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}}))

\displaystyle=T^{-1}(\theta_{t}\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}},u^{\alpha}(t,\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}},T(\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}},\widetilde{\mathscr{M}}^{\alpha}(\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}}))))

\displaystyle=T^{-1}(\theta_{t}\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}},u^{\alpha}(t,\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}},\mathscr{M}^{\alpha}(\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}})))

\displaystyle\subset T^{-1}(\theta_{t}\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}},\mathscr{M}^{\alpha}(\theta_{t}\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}}))=\widetilde{\mathscr{M}}^{\alpha}(\theta_{t}\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}}).

Finally, we prove the asymptotic completeness property of
\widetilde{\mathscr{M}}^{\alpha}(\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}})
. Given
x\in D(A^{\sigma})
, we observe that

\displaystyle v^{\alpha}(t,\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}},x)

\displaystyle=T^{-1}(\theta_{t}\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}},u^{\alpha}(t,\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}},T(\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}},x)))

\displaystyle\ \ \ +|e^{z(\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}})}|\|\psi^{\alpha}(\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}},e^{-z(\omega)}\xi)-\psi(\omega,e^{-z(\omega)}\xi)\|_{\sigma}

\displaystyle\ \ \ +|e^{z(\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}})}-e^{z(\omega)}|\|\psi(\omega,e^{-z(\omega)}\xi)\|_{\sigma}.

From the same sub-subsequence argument, we obtain

\displaystyle\|e^{z(\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}})}\psi^{\alpha}(\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}},e^{-z(\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}})}\xi)-e^{z(\omega)}\psi(\omega,e^{-z(\omega)}\xi)\|_{\sigma}\rightarrow 0

in probability as
\alpha\rightarrow 2
.
Using the similar procedure, we have

\displaystyle\|D_{\xi}\big(e^{z(\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}})}\psi^{\alpha}(\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}},e^{-z(\omega_{\ell^{\alpha}})}\xi)\big)-D_{\xi}\big(e^{z(\omega)}\psi(\omega,e^{-z(\omega)}\xi)\big)\|\rightarrow 0

in probability as
\alpha\rightarrow 2
. Thus the proof is complete.
∎

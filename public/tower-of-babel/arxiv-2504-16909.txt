The CKN inequality for spinors: symmetry and symmetry breaking
Jean Dolbeault, Maria J. Esteban, Rupert L. Frank, Michael Loss

Abstract
. This paper is devoted to Sobolev interpolation inequalities for spinors, with weights of Caffarelli-Kohn-Nirenberg (CKN) type. In view of the corresponding results for scalar functions, a natural question is to determine whether optimal spinors have symmetry properties, or whether spinors with symmetry properties are linearly unstable, in which case we shall say that symmetry breaking occurs. What symmetry means has to be carefully defined and the overall picture turns out to be richer than in the scalar case. So far, no symmetrization technique is available in the spinorial case. We can however determine a range of the parameters for which symmetry holds using a detailed analysis based mostly on spectral methods.

1.
Introduction and main result (I)

A perennial question in the calculus of variation concerns the symmetry properties of optimizers.
A functional
\mathcal{F}
is invariant under a group
G
if
\mathcal{F}(g^{*}\psi)=\mathcal{F}(\psi)
for all
\psi

and for all
g\in G
, where
g^{*}\psi
denotes the action of
g
on
\psi
. If
\psi_{\!m}
is an optimizer for the functional
\mathcal{F}
,
is it true that
\psi_{\!m}
is invariant under the action of the group
G
?
This is often not the case; the symmetry is then broken.
Exceptions to this rule are, in general, difficult to find and can pose considerable mathematical challenges.
An example where these features are nicely displayed are the Caffarelli-Kohn-Nirenberg inequalities
[
7
]
(also see
[
25
]
for an earlier related result),

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\frac{|\nabla v(x)|^{2}}{|x|^{2a}}\,dx\geq C_{a,b,d}\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\frac{|v(x)|^{p}}{|x|^{bp}}\,dx\right)^{2/p}\,.

(CKN)

Here
d\geq 3
is an integer,
a
,
b
are real numbers with
a\leq b\leq a+1
and, to ensure scale invariance,
p=\frac{2d}{d-2-2a+2b}
. This inequality is often considered only for real functions, but extends trivially to complex-valued functions (see Remark
13
).
The problem is to find the optimal constant
C_{a,b,d}
, which is positive if
a\neq(d-2)/2
,
to discuss whether
C_{a,b,d}
is achieved or not, and in case optimizers exist, to analyse their main qualitative properties, like positivity and symmetry.
If one replaces the function
v(x)
by
v\big(R^{-1}x\big)
, where
R
is any rotation, then the value of the functional does not change.
Thus, the expectation is that the optimal function, when it exists, is radial.
It was shown by Catrina and Wang
[
8
]
, with later improvements by Felli and Schneider
[
21
]
, that
below a certain curve
C_{\rm FS}^{(d)}(a,b)=0
in the parameter space
\{a,b\}
, the rotational symmetry of the optimizer is broken.
In a later work
[
13
]
, the rotational symmetry in the remaining region was established.

A natural continuation of this line of research is the analogous question for
spinor valued functions
. While this question could be studied in any dimension
d
, below we restrict ourselves to the case where
d=3
. The scalar variable
v
in (
CKN
) is replaced by the
{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
-valued function, or ‘2-spinor’,

\psi(x)=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\psi_{1}(x)\\
\psi_{2}(x)\end{array}\right),

and the gradient is replaced by

\sigma\cdot\nabla\psi=\sum_{j=1}^{3}\sigma_{\!j}\,\partial_{\!j}\psi\,.

Here,
\sigma=(\sigma_{\!j})_{j=1,2,3}
is the family of the Pauli matrices

\sigma_{1}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&1\\
1&0\end{array}\right)\,,\hskip 10.00002pt\sigma_{2}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&-i\\
i&0\end{array}\right)\,,\hskip 10.00002pt\sigma_{3}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&0\\
0&-1\end{array}\right)\,.

The role of the functional inequality (
CKN
) is played by the inequality

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\frac{|\sigma\cdot\nabla\psi(x)|^{2}}{|x|^{2\,\alpha}}\,dx\geq\mathcal{C}_{\alpha,\beta}\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\frac{|\psi(x)|^{p}}{|x|^{\beta\,p}}\,dx\right)^{2/p}\,.

(SCKN)

Throughout we assume that

\alpha\leq\beta\leq\alpha+1\,,

and, by definition,
\mathcal{C}_{\alpha,\beta}\geq 0
is the best constant in the space

\mathcal{D}_{\alpha,\beta}:=\Big\{\psi:\,|x|^{-\beta}\psi\in\mathrm{L}^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{3},{\mathbb{C}}^{2})\;;\;\;|x|^{-\alpha}\,\sigma\cdot\nabla\psi\in\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3},{\mathbb{C}}^{2})\Big\}\,.

For the same scaling reasons as above, the exponent
p
is given by

p=\frac{6}{1-2\,\alpha+2\,\beta}\,.

(1)

In contrast to the scalar inequality (
CKN
), an infinite set of special values of
\alpha
appears for which the inequality fails. We set

\Lambda:=\left\{k-\tfrac{1}{2}\,:\,k\in{\mathbb{Z}}\setminus\{0\}\right\}\,.

(2)

Proposition 1
.

If
\alpha\in\Lambda
, then
\mathcal{C}_{\alpha,\beta}=0
for all
\alpha\leq\beta\leq\alpha+1
. If
\alpha\not\in\Lambda
, then
\mathcal{C}_{\alpha,\beta}>0
for all
\alpha\leq\beta\leq\alpha+1
.

Our main interest in this paper is in the sharp constant
\mathcal{C}_{\alpha,\beta}
and the corresponding set of optimizers.
The chief reason for investigating such a problem is that spinors are ubiquitous in many of the equations of physics: for instance, in the Dirac equation, the Pauli equation and in the influence of the L-S coupling on the spectrum of atoms.
In a particular case, Inequality (
SCKN
) can be viewed as a generalization of the Hardy inequality for spinors when
\alpha=-\,1/2
,
\beta=1/2
and
p=2
,

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}|x|\,|\sigma\cdot\nabla\psi(x)|^{2}dx\geq\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\frac{|\psi(x)|^{2}}{|x|}\,dx\,,

(3)

where the constant
\mathcal{C}_{-1/2,1/2}=1
is sharp. This inequality has proven to be very useful for analyzing Dirac-Coulomb Hamiltonians
[
16
,
15
,
18
,
19
,
20
,
35
]
.

The scalar (
CKN
) inequality is invariant under the group O(3) in the sense that both sides of the inequality remain the same if
v
is replaced by
v_{R}
for
R\in
O(3), where
v_{R}(x):=v\big(R^{-1}x\big)
. Similarly, we now show that the spinorial inequality (
SCKN
) is invariant under the group SU(2). If
A
is any SU(2) matrix, then, recalling that
\sigma=(\sigma_{k})_{k=1,2,3}
is a basis for the traceless Hermitean
(2\times 2)
-matrices, one has

A^{*}\sigma_{\!i}A=\sum_{k=1}^{3}R_{ik}(A)\,\sigma_{k}\,.

(4)

The matrix
R(A)=(R_{ik}(A))
turns out to be a rotation. The map
A\mapsto R(A)
is a representation of the group SU(2), i.e.,
R(A\,B)=R(A)\,R(B)
.
If one transforms

\psi(x)\mapsto A\,\psi\big(R^{-1}(A)\,x\big)=:\psi_{\!A}(x)\,,

one finds that

\sigma\cdot\nabla\psi_{\!A}(x)=(\sigma\cdot\nabla\psi)_{A}(x)\,.

Thus,

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\frac{|\sigma\cdot\nabla\psi_{\!A}(x)|^{2}}{|x|^{2\,\alpha}}\,dx=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\frac{|\sigma\cdot\nabla\psi(x)|^{2}}{|x|^{2\,\alpha}}\,dx\hskip 10.00002pt{\rm and}\hskip 10.00002pt\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\frac{|\psi_{\!A}(x)|^{p}}{|x|^{\beta\,p}}\,dx=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\frac{|\psi(x)|^{p}}{|x|^{\beta\,p}}\,dx\,.

This shows that inequality (
SCKN
) is SU(2)-invariant.

In the symmetry problem for the scalar (
CKN
) inequality one asks whether some (or any) optimizer is invariant under the O(3) action. In the spinorial case, however, the symmetry question has to be phrased differently, and
this is one of the main differences from the scalar case
. The reason is that there is no nontrivial spinor that is invariant under the SU(2) action. Indeed, if
\psi\in\mathrm{L}^{1}_{\rm loc}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})
satisfies
\psi_{\!A}=\psi
for all
A\in
SU(2), then
\psi\equiv 0
. The proof of this assertion is simple. Indeed, the invariance of the left side of (
4
) under
A\mapsto-A
implies that
R(A)=R(-A)
. Thus, if
\psi_{\!A}=\psi=\psi_{-A}
, then
A\,\psi(x)=\psi\big(R(A)\,x\big)=\psi\big(R(-A)\,x\big)=-\,A\,\psi(x)
, so
\psi(x)=0
.

We now explain how to formulate the notion of symmetry in the spinorial case. We consider the uniform probability measure
d\omega
on
{\mathbb{S}^{2}}
, that is, in spherical coordinates,
d\omega=\tfrac{1}{4\,\pi}\sin\theta\,d\theta\,d\varphi
and the corresponding Hilbert space
\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}^{2}},{\mathbb{C}}^{2};d\omega)
of (angular) spinors on the sphere
{\mathbb{S}^{2}}
. For
\chi\in\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}^{2}},{\mathbb{C}}^{2};d\omega)
, let
\chi_{\!A}(\omega):=A\,\chi\big(R^{-1}(A)\,\omega\big)
. Then
\chi\mapsto\chi_{\!A}
is a representation of SU(2) in
\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}^{2}},{\mathbb{C}}^{2};d\omega)
. Decomposing into irreducible representations we obtain the orthogonal decomposition

\mathrm{L}^{2}\left({\mathbb{S}^{2}},{\mathbb{C}}^{2};d\omega\right)=\bigoplus_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}\setminus\{-1\}}\mathcal{H}_{k}\,.

Each subspace
\mathcal{H}_{k}
transforms irreducibly under the transformation
\chi\mapsto\chi_{\!A}
. The integer
k
in this decomposition is an eigenvalue of the operator
\sigma\cdot L
, where
L:=\omega\wedge(-i\,\nabla)
. We recall the details of this decomposition in Appendix
B
.

An elementary calculation shows that

(\sigma\cdot\omega)(\sigma\cdot L+1)=-\,(\sigma\cdot L+1)\,(\sigma\cdot\omega)

(5)

for all
\omega\in{\mathbb{S}^{2}}
. Since
\sigma\cdot\omega
provides a unitary equivalence between
\mathcal{H}_{k}
and
\mathcal{H}_{-2-k}
, we see that the representations for
k\geq 0
and
k\leq-\,2
are equivalent.

The irreducible subspaces of lowest dimension are
\mathcal{H}_{0}
and
\mathcal{H}_{-2}
and the dimension of each of these two spaces is
2
. Thus, we propose to reformulate the notion of spherical symmetry of an optimizer of (
SCKN
) as
belonging to an irreducible representation of lowest dimension
. This should be compared with the notion of symmetric functions in the setting of the scalar inequality (
CKN
), where the irreducible subspace of the lowest dimension of the representation of O(3) has dimension one and corresponds to spherically symmetric functions.

Using the fact that
\mathcal{H}_{0}
consists of constant spinors, and consequently
\mathcal{H}_{-2}
consists of
\sigma\cdot\omega
times constant spinors, we arrive at the following definition.

Definition 2
.

A spinor
\psi
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
is
symmetric
if there is a scalar, complex-valued function
f
on
{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
and a constant spinor
\chi_{0}\in{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
such that either

\psi(x)=f(|x|)\,\chi_{0}\hskip 10.00002pt\text{for a.e.}\ x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}\,,

or

\psi(x)=f(|x|)\,\sigma\cdot\tfrac{x}{|x|}\,\chi_{0}\hskip 10.00002pt\text{for a.e.}\ x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}\,.

We call these symmetric spinors of type
\mathcal{H}_{0}
and
\mathcal{H}_{-2}
, respectively.

Let us define
\mathcal{C}^{\star}_{\alpha,\beta}
as the best constant in the Inequality (
SCKN
) when restricted to
symmetric spinors
. This constant can be easily computed and the optimizers can be classified explicitly, as will be shown in Proposition
29
. In particular, symmetric optimizers are of type
\mathcal{H}_{0}
if
\alpha>-1/2
and of type
\mathcal{H}_{-2}
if
\alpha<-1/2
.

These preliminary considerations raise an obvious question:

For which
\alpha
and
\beta
are the global optimizers for Inequality (
SCKN
) symmetric ?

Before stating our main result concerning this question, we mention one more special feature of the family of inequalities (
SCKN
). Namely, given
\psi\in\mathcal{D}_{\alpha,\beta}
, let us set

\widetilde{\psi}(x):=|x|^{-2\,\alpha-1}\,\sigma\cdot\tfrac{x}{|x|}\,\psi(x)\,.

Then one has
\widetilde{\psi}\in\mathcal{D}_{-\alpha-1,\beta-2\,\alpha-1}
and

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\frac{|\sigma\cdot\nabla\widetilde{\psi}(x)|^{2}}{|x|^{-2\,(\alpha+1)}}\,dx=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\frac{|\sigma\cdot\nabla\psi(x)|^{2}}{|x|^{2\,\alpha}}\,dx\hskip 10.00002pt\text{and}\hskip 10.00002pt\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\frac{|\widetilde{\psi}(x)|^{p}}{|x|^{(\beta-2\,\alpha-1)p}}\,dx=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\frac{|\psi(x)|^{p}}{|x|^{\beta\,p}}\,dx\,.

These identities follow by a straightforward computation, but they will become obvious later in this introduction. As a consequence, we deduce that

\mathcal{C}_{\alpha,\beta}=\mathcal{C}_{-\alpha-1,\beta-2\,\alpha-1}\hskip 10.00002pt\text{and}\hskip 10.00002pt\mathcal{C}^{\star}_{\alpha,\beta}=\mathcal{C}^{\star}_{-\alpha-1,\beta-2\,\alpha-1}\,.

Moreover, minimizers for the inequality with parameters
(\alpha,\beta)
are in one-to-one correspondence with minimizers for the inequality with parameters
(-\alpha-1,\beta-2\,\alpha-1)
. Note that the exponent
p
in (
1
) is the same for both parameter pairs. Moreover, parameter pairs with
\alpha=-\,\frac{1}{2}
are invariant under the transformation. As a consequence of this transformation we restrict ourselves in the following theorem to parameter values
\alpha\geq-1/2
.

The following is a first version of our main result.

Theorem 3
.

There are optimizers for (
SCKN
) for all
\alpha\in{\mathbb{R}}\setminus\Lambda
and
\alpha<\beta<\alpha+1
. Moreover, there are two functions
\overline{\alpha}_{\star}
and
\overline{\alpha}^{\star}
on
(0,1)
, taking values respectively in
(-1/2,0]
and
[1/2,1)
, such that the following holds for all
\alpha\in[-1/2,\infty)\setminus\Lambda
and
\alpha<\beta<\alpha+1
:

(i)

if either
\overline{\alpha}_{\star}(\beta-\alpha)<\alpha<1/2
or
1/2<\alpha<\overline{\alpha}^{\star}(\beta-\alpha)
, then
\mathcal{C}_{\alpha,\beta}=\mathcal{C}^{\star}_{\alpha,\beta}
and all optimizers are then symmetric,

(ii)

if either
-\,1/2\leq\alpha<\overline{\alpha}_{\star}(\beta-\alpha)
or
\alpha>\overline{\alpha}^{\star}(\beta-\alpha)
, then
\mathcal{C}_{\alpha,\beta}<\mathcal{C}^{\star}_{\alpha,\beta}

The corresponding results are valid for
\alpha\in(-\infty,-1/2]
in view of the symmetry of the problem under the map
(\alpha,\beta)\mapsto(-\alpha-1,\beta-2\,\alpha-1)
.

Theorem
3
is only a qualitative version of what we will prove. Namely, we will give quantitative upper and lower bounds on the functions
\overline{\alpha}_{\star}
and
\overline{\alpha}^{\star}
; see Remark
7
.
A graphical representation of what we prove can be seen in Figure
1
.

Already on a qualitative level our results concerning the spinorial inequality (
SCKN
) are quite different from the results concerning the scalar inequality (
CKN
). In the spinorial case, each line
\beta-\alpha\equiv\mathrm{const}\in(0,1)
intersects the symmetry region in two bounded intervals, while such an intersection by a line
b-a\equiv\mathrm{const}\in(0,1)
occurs only in a single interval in the scalar case (up to the case
a=1/2
which is trivial because
C_{1/2,b,3}=0
). Another difference is that in the spinorial case symmetry can occur only for
-\,2\leq\alpha\leq 1
, while in the scalar case it can occur for arbitrarily large
a\in{\mathbb{R}}
. We also note that in the spinorial case the figure is symmetric with respect to
\alpha=-\,1/2
, while it is symmetric with respect to
a=+1/2
in the scalar case. Our results are summarized in Figure
1
.

Symmetry regions are shown in green and symmetry breaking regions appear in red. The upper left corner represents the parameter domain for (
CKN
) inequalities (scalar functions), while the main figure is the parameter domain for (
SCKN
) inequalities (spinors). In the latter case, the threshold between symmetry and symmetry breaking is determined by the functions
\overline{\alpha}_{\star}
and
\overline{\alpha}^{\star}
which take values in the white areas of the strip
\alpha\leq\beta\leq\alpha+1
.

2.
Passing to logarithmic variables and main result (II)

Our goal in this section is to bring inequality (
SCKN
) into an equivalent form and state a more precise version of Theorem
3
in this equivalent reformulation.

A crucial feature of Inequality (
SCKN
), just like of (
CKN
), is its scale invariance. In logarithmic coordinates, this invariance becomes translation invariance, and these coordinates have been useful in the study of Inequality (
CKN
). Correspondingly, we will now state an equivalent form of Inequality (
SCKN
) in logarithmic coordinates.

Lemma 4
.

If we set
s=\log r
,
r=|x|\neq 0
,
\omega=x/r
and define a function
\phi
on
{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{S}^{2}}
by
\psi(x)=r^{\alpha-1/2}\,\phi(s,\omega)
, then Inequality (
SCKN
) becomes

\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{S}^{2}}}{\left(|\partial_{s}\phi|^{2}+\big|\big(\sigma\cdot L-\alpha+\tfrac{1}{2}\big)\,\phi\big|^{2}\right)}\,ds\,d\omega\geq C_{\alpha,p}\left(\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{S}^{2}}}{|\phi|^{p}}\,ds\,d\omega\right)^{2/p}.

(SCKN
log
)

The optimal constant is
C_{\alpha,p}=(4\,\pi)^{(2-p)/p}\,\mathcal{C}_{\alpha,\beta}
, where
\alpha
,
\beta
and
p
are related by (
1
).

We parametrize the constant in (
SCKNlog
) by
\alpha
and
p
, because
\beta
does not appear explicitly in this inequality. The parameters under consideration are now
\alpha\in{\mathbb{R}}
and
p\in[2,6]
. According to Proposition
1
we have
C_{\alpha,p}>0
if and only if
\alpha\in{\mathbb{R}}\setminus\Lambda
.

In logarithmic coordinates the before-mentioned symmetry between
(\alpha,\beta)
and
(-\alpha-1,\beta-2\,\alpha-1)
can be seen as follows. For an arbitrary function
\phi
, using (
5
) and setting
\eta=(\sigma\cdot\omega)\,\phi
,
(
SCKNlog
) takes the form

\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{S}^{2}}}{\left(|\partial_{s}\eta|^{2}+\big|\big(\sigma\cdot L+\alpha+\tfrac{3}{2}\big)\,\eta\big|^{2}\right)}\,ds\,d\omega\geq C_{\alpha,p}\left(\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{S}^{2}}}{|\eta|^{p}}\,ds\,d\omega\right)^{2/p}.

(6)

This puts into evidence that

C_{-\,(\alpha+1),p}=C_{\alpha,p}\,.

An optimizer for (
SCKNlog
) is transformed by
\phi\mapsto(\sigma\cdot\omega)\,\phi
into an optimizer for (
6
) and reciprocally because
(\sigma\cdot\omega)^{2}=1
.

The concept of symmetry translates as follows to logarithmic coordinates.

Definition 5
.

A spinor
\phi
on
{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{S}^{2}}
is
symmetric
if there is a scalar, complex-valued function
u
on
{\mathbb{R}}
and a constant spinor
\chi_{0}\in{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
such that either

\phi(s,\omega)=u(s)\,\chi_{0}\hskip 10.00002pt\text{for a.e.}\ (s,\omega)\in{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\,,

or

\phi(s,\omega)=u(s)\,\sigma\cdot\omega\,\chi_{0}\hskip 10.00002pt\text{for a.e.}\ (s,\omega)\in{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\,.

We call these symmetric spinors of type
\mathcal{H}_{0}
and
\mathcal{H}_{-2}
, respectively.

Let
C^{\star}_{\alpha,p}=(4\,\pi)^{(2-p)/p}\,\mathcal{C}^{\star}_{\alpha,\beta}
be the best constant in (
SCKNlog
) restricted to the space of symmetric functions
\phi
as in Definition
5
, for
\alpha\in{\mathbb{R}}\setminus\Lambda
. Computing
C^{\star}_{\alpha,p}
and identifying the equality cases can be reduced to the case of a Gagliardo-Nirenberg interpolation inequality on
{\mathbb{R}}
: see Appendix
A
. Up to translation and multiplication by a constant, any optimizer among symmetric spinors satisfies the form of Definition
5
with

u(s)=\big(\cosh(s/s_{0})\big)^{-2/(p-2)}\hskip 10.00002pt\forall\,s\in{\mathbb{R}}\,,

(7)

for some
s_{0}>0
, which depends only on
\alpha
and
p
. Moreover, symmetric spinors are of type
\mathcal{H}_{0}
if
\alpha>-1/2
and of type
\mathcal{H}_{-2}
if
\alpha<-1/2
. Note that this is consistent with the symmetry between
(\alpha,p)
and
(-\alpha-1,p)
, since the map
\phi\mapsto(\sigma\cdot\omega)\,\phi
maps
\mathcal{H}_{0}
to
\mathcal{H}_{-2}
.

The question raised above becomes:

For which
\alpha
and
p
are the global optimizers for Inequality (
SCKNlog
) symmetric ?

Symmetry and symmetry breaking regions are considered as
open sets
in
{\mathbb{R}}\times(2,6)\ni(\alpha,p)
: symmetry breaking means
C_{\alpha,p}<C^{\star}_{\alpha,p}
while symmetry means
C_{\alpha,p}=C^{\star}_{\alpha,p}
and all optimizers are then symmetric. In strong contrast with the scalar case (see Fig.
1
), our main result goes as follows.

Theorem 6
.

There are optimizers for (
SCKNlog
) for all
\alpha\in{\mathbb{R}}\setminus\Lambda
and
p\in(2,6)
. Moreover, there are two functions
\alpha_{\star}
and
\alpha^{\star}
on
(2,6)
, taking values respectively in
(-1/2,0]
and
[1/2,1)
, such that the following holds for all
\alpha\in[-1/2,\infty)
and
2<p<6
:

(i)

symmetry holds if either
\alpha_{\star}(p)<\alpha<1/2
or
1/2<\alpha<\alpha^{\star}(p)
,

(ii)

symmetry breaking holds if either
-\,1/2\leq\alpha<\alpha_{\star}(p)
, or
\alpha>\alpha^{\star}(p)
.

Symmetry and symmetry breaking regions are invariant under the reflection with respect to
\alpha=-\,1/2
, i.e., the transformation
(\alpha,p)\mapsto\big(-(\alpha+1),p\big)
, so that the range
\alpha<-1/2
is also covered. The functions
\alpha_{\star}
and
\alpha^{\star}
are such that

-\,\frac{1}{2}\leq\max\left\{\alpha_{1}(p),\alpha_{2}(p)\right\}\leq\alpha_{\star}(p)\leq\min\left\{0,\alpha_{0}(p)\right\}\\
\mbox{and}\quad\min\left\{\alpha_{3}(p),\alpha_{4}(p)\right\}\leq\alpha^{\star}(p)\leq\alpha_{5}(p)

where

\displaystyle\alpha_{0}(p):=\tfrac{4-\sqrt{-3\,p^{2}+20\,p-12}}{2\,(p-2)}\,,\hskip 10.00002pt

\displaystyle\alpha_{1}(p):=\tfrac{2\,p^{2}-7\,p-6-\sqrt{3\,(19\,p^{2}-20\,p+12)}}{4\,p\,(p-2)}\,,

\displaystyle\alpha_{2}(p):=\tfrac{p^{2}-2\,p+24-2\,\sqrt{3\,(11\,p^{2}-4\,p+12)}}{2\,(p-2)\,(p+6)}\,,\hskip 10.00002pt

\displaystyle\alpha_{3}(p):=\tfrac{6-p}{2\,(p-2)}\,,

\displaystyle\alpha_{4}(p):=\tfrac{3\,p-10+\sqrt{-3\,p^{2}+20\,p-12}}{4\,(p-2)}\,,\hskip 10.00002pt

\displaystyle\alpha_{5}(p):=\tfrac{2\,p^{2}+5\,p-30+\sqrt{3\,(19\,p^{2}-20\,p+12)}}{4\,(p-2)\,(p+3)}\,.

If
\alpha\in(\alpha_{\star}(p),1/2)\cup(1/2,\alpha^{\star}(p))
, then
C_{\alpha,p}=C_{\alpha,p}^{\star}
and all optimizers are symmetric.

For
\alpha=\alpha_{\star}(p)
or
\alpha=\alpha^{\star}(p)
, then we have
C_{\alpha,p}=C_{\alpha,p}^{\star}
and there is a symmetric optimizer. Whether in this case
all
optimizers are symmetric or not is an open question.

2
below, we synthetize the results of Theorem
6
.

SCKNlog
) for spinors and, for sake of comparison, its counterpart for scalars. The threshold between symmetry and symmetry breaking is determined by the functions

Remark 7
.

The equivalence between (
SCKN
) and (
SCKNlog
) shows that Theorem
3
is equivalent to the first part of Theorem
6
via

\overline{\alpha}_{\star}(\beta-\alpha)=\alpha_{\star}\left(\frac{6}{1-2\,\alpha+2\,\beta}\right)\,,\hskip 10.00002pt\overline{\alpha}^{\star}(\beta-\alpha)=\alpha^{\star}\left(\frac{6}{1-2\,\alpha+2\,\beta}\right)\,.

This allows us to translate the bounds on
\alpha_{\star}
and
\alpha^{\star}
into bounds on
\overline{\alpha}_{\star}
and
\overline{\alpha}^{\star}
.

The reader familiar with the large literature on the sharp constant in (
CKN
) will probably recognize the various strategies that we employ to prove symmetry or symmetry breaking. Looking closely at the details of how these strategies are implemented here, however, one finds substantial differences and, as a general rule, the present spinorial case is considerably more involved than the scalar case. A posteriori, this is not surprising, of course, given the more complicated shape of (
SCKN
) compared to (
CKN
).

More specifically, in Section
5
we employ a monotonicity argument that is similar in spirit to an argument that appears in
[
32
,
14
]
. In contrast to the scalar case, when applying this argument for
\alpha\geq-1/2
one has to distinguish the cases where
\alpha<1/2
and
\alpha>1/2
. In Section
6
we employ an argument that is based on Gagliardo-Nirenberg interpolation inequalities on the sphere, inspired by
[
12
]
. The corresponding Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality is new and might be interesting on its own. When adapting the strategy from
[
12
]
we overcome the difficulties from the spinorial nature through a judicious application of Minkowski’s inequality. Finally, in Section
7
we derive a symmetry breaking region by analyzing the stability of symmetric solutions in the spirit of
[
21
]
. Here again a subtlety appears, since the spinorial inequality (
SCKN
) is intrinsically complex and the second variation operator is real-linear but not complex-linear. We manage to find a nontrivial symmetry breaking region, but in contrast to the scalar case, we are not able to determine the complete instability region.

One reason for these differences between the spinorial and the scalar case is the following. In logarithmic variables we find the angular operator
(\sigma\cdot L-\alpha+1/2)^{2}
; see Lemma
4
. Normalizing this operator so that it vanishes on constant spinors, we arrive at the effective angular operator
\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}=(\sigma\cdot L)^{2}+(1-2\,\alpha)\,\sigma\cdot L
. Repeating the same in the scalar case we arrive first at the operator
L^{2}+(\alpha-1/2)^{2}
and then, after normalization, at the effective angular operator
\mathcal{K}^{\rm scalar}=L^{2}
, which
is independent of
\alpha
. Not only does
\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}
depend on
\alpha
, but this dependence is also non-monotone, since
\sigma\cdot L
is unbounded both from above and from below. Dealing with the
\alpha
-dependence of the effective angular operator is the cause of many of the difficulties that we face in this work and is one of the main new obstacles that we overcome in this paper.

Despite these difference, however, it is perhaps an interesting observation that the strategies that have previously been employed for the scalar (
CKN
) problem are not entirely linked to the specifics of the problem. They are more robust than one might have imagined and may have a wider applicability.

Needless to say that the results obtained in this paper are not sharp in the sense that they do not cover the full parameter regime. Giving a complete description of the symmetry and symmetry breaking regions is an
open problem
.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
3
we discuss the reformulation in logarithmic coordinates and consider, in particular, the limit case
p=2
, where Inequality (
SCKN
) becomes a Hardy-like inequality for which the best constant can be computed explicitly. Existence of optimizers is proved if
\alpha<\beta<\alpha+1
in Section
4
. Sections
5
and
6
provide us with symmetry results for (
SCKNlog
), while in Section
7
we find regions where the symmetric solutions are linearly unstable. The proof of Theorem
6
is established in Section

3.
Changes of coordinates and preliminary estimates

We begin with a computation that is crucial for much that follows.

Proposition 8
.

For
\psi\in\mathcal{D}_{\alpha,\beta}
, setting
\psi(x)=|x|^{\alpha-1/2}\,\Phi(x)
, we have

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}{\frac{|\sigma\cdot\nabla\psi|^{2}}{|x|^{2\,\alpha}}}\,dx=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}{\frac{|\partial_{r}\Phi|^{2}}{|x|}}\,dx+\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}{\frac{\left|\left(\sigma\cdot L-\alpha+\tfrac{1}{2}\right)\Phi\,\right|^{2}}{|x|^{2}}}\,dx\,.

(8)

Here we use the notation
r=|x|
and
\partial_{r}\psi=\frac{x}{r}\cdot\nabla\psi
. On
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
, the angular momentum is defined by

L:=x\wedge(-i\,\nabla)\,.

This definition extends to
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
the operator
L
acting on spinors on
{\mathbb{S}^{2}}
, as in Section
1
.

Proof.

For any
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}\setminus\{0\}
, we compute

\frac{1}{|x|^{2\,\alpha}}\,(\sigma\cdot\nabla\psi)^{*}\,(\sigma\cdot\nabla\psi)=\frac{1}{|x|}\,(\sigma\cdot\nabla\Phi)^{*}\,(\sigma\cdot\nabla\Phi)+\frac{1}{|x|^{3}}\left(\alpha-\tfrac{1}{2}\right)^{2}\,|\Phi|^{2}\\
+\frac{2}{|x|^{3}}\left(\alpha-\tfrac{1}{2}\right)\,\mathrm{Re}\left((\sigma\cdot\nabla\Phi)^{*}\,(\sigma\cdot x)\,\Phi\right)\,.

Since
\nabla\cdot(x\,|x|^{-3})=0
and
(\sigma\cdot\mathsf{a})\,(\sigma\cdot\mathsf{b})=\mathsf{a}\cdot\mathsf{b}+i\,\sigma\cdot(\mathsf{a}\wedge\mathsf{b})
, we learn that

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}(\sigma\cdot\nabla\Phi)^{*}\,(\sigma\cdot x)\,\Phi\,\frac{dx}{|x|^{3}}=-\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}{\Phi^{*}\,(\sigma\cdot\nabla)\left(\sigma\cdot\frac{x}{|x|^{3}}\right)\,\Phi}\,dx=-\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\Phi^{*}\,(\sigma\cdot L)\,\Phi\,\frac{dx}{|x|^{3}}\,.

Hence

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}{\frac{|\sigma\cdot\nabla\psi|^{2}}{|x|^{2\,\alpha}}}\,dx=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}{\frac{|\sigma\cdot\nabla\Phi|^{2}}{|x|}}\,dx+\left(\alpha-\tfrac{1}{2}\right)^{2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}{\frac{|\Phi|^{2}}{|x|^{3}}}\,dx\\
-2\left(\alpha-\tfrac{1}{2}\right)\mathrm{Re}\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}{\frac{\Phi^{*}\,(\sigma\cdot L)\,\Phi}{|x|^{3}}}\,dx\right)\,.

We learn from
[
36
, p. 125]
that

\sigma\cdot\nabla=\left(\sigma\cdot\tfrac{x}{r}\right)\partial_{r}-\tfrac{1}{r}\left(\sigma\cdot\tfrac{x}{r}\right)\,(\sigma\cdot L)

and use it to compute

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}{\frac{|\sigma\cdot\nabla\Phi|^{2}}{|x|}}\,dx=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}{\frac{|\partial_{r}\Phi|^{2}}{|x|}}\,dx+\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}{\frac{\left|\sigma\cdot L\,\Phi\right|^{2}}{|x|^{3}}}\,dx

using the fact that

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}(\partial_{r}\Phi)^{*}\,(\sigma\cdot L)\,\Phi\,\frac{dx}{|x|^{2}}=0\,.

Collecting estimates, we obtain

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}{\frac{|\sigma\cdot\nabla\psi|^{2}}{|x|^{2\,\alpha}}}\,dx=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}{\frac{|\partial_{r}\Phi|^{2}}{|x|}}\,dx+\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}{\frac{\left|\sigma\cdot L\,\Phi\right|^{2}}{|x|^{3}}}\,dx\\
+\left(\alpha-\tfrac{1}{2}\right)^{2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}{\frac{|\Phi|^{2}}{|x|^{3}}}\,dx-2\left(\alpha-\tfrac{1}{2}\right)\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}{\frac{\Phi^{*}\,(\sigma\cdot L)\,\Phi}{|x|^{3}}}\,dx\,.

This proves the claimed equality.
∎

4.
Existence of optimizers

Here we prove the following existence result.

Theorem 11
.

For all
p\in(2,6)
and all
\alpha\not\in\Lambda

\mathrm{H}^{1}({\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{S}^{2}},{\mathbb{C}}^{2})\setminus\{0\}

Proof.

Here we follow very closely the arguments in the proof of
[
8

and
[
10
]
. Let us consider a sequence
(\phi_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
in

\lim_{n\to\infty}\left(\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{S}^{2}}}{|\partial_{s}\phi_{n}|^{2}}\,ds\,d\omega+\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{S}^{2}}}{\big|\left(\sigma\cdot L-\alpha+\tfrac{1}{2}\right)^{2}\phi_{n}\big|^{2}}\,ds\,d\omega\right)=C_{\alpha,p}\,.

(13)

Because of Lemma
10
, we know that
(\phi_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
is bounded in
\mathrm{H}^{1}({\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{S}^{2}},{\mathbb{C}}^{2})

Take any fixed

and consider

for
\sigma\in{\mathbb{R}}

[
8

, which is adapted from
[

, that any bounded sequence
(f_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
in

satisfies

. The same result holds for spinors. Since

\lim_{n\to\infty}\|{\phi_{n}}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}({\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{S}^{2}})}=1\neq 0\,,

we deduce that there exists a constant
C\in(0,1]
and

\int_{A_{r}(\sigma_{n})}|\phi_{n}|^{p}\,dy\geq C\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{for $n$ large enough}\,.

(14)

converges weakly in

\lim_{n\to\infty}\|{\widetilde{\phi}_{n}}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}({\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{S}^{2}})}^{p}=\|{\phi}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}({\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{S}^{2}})}^{p}+\lim_{n\to\infty}\|{\widetilde{\phi}_{n}-\phi}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}({\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{S}^{2}})}^{p}

\lim_{n\to\infty}\|{\widetilde{\phi}_{n}-\phi}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}({\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{S}^{2}})}^{2}=(1-\mathsf{x})^{2/p}\,.

Meanwhile, by the weak convergence in
\mathrm{H}^{1}({\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{S}^{2}},{\mathbb{C}}^{2})

\begin{array}[]{l}\lim_{n\to\infty}\left(\|{\partial_{s}\widetilde{\phi}_{n}}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{S}^{2}})}^{2}+\|{\left(\sigma\cdot L-\alpha+\tfrac{1}{2}\right)\widetilde{\phi}_{n}}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{S}^{2}})}^{2}\right)\\[6.0pt]
\hskip 56.9055pt=\|{\partial_{s}\phi}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{S}^{2}})}^{2}+\|{\left(\sigma\cdot L-\alpha+\tfrac{1}{2}\right)\phi}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{S}^{2}})}^{2}\\[6.0pt]

From this we deduce that

) for

The main result of this section in Theorem

6.1.
A Gagliardo-Nirenberg interpolation inequality for spinors on the sphere

Let us consider the operator

\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}:=(\sigma\cdot L)^{2}+(1-2\,\alpha)\,\sigma\cdot L

acting on angular spinors on
{\mathbb{S}^{2}}
and define

\mathsf{m}(\alpha):=\min\big\{1-\alpha,1+2\,\alpha\big\}

such that
\mathsf{m}(\alpha)=1+2\,\alpha
if
\alpha\in(-1/2,0]
and
\mathsf{m}(\alpha)=1-\alpha
if
\alpha\in[0,1)
, and

\mathsf{q}(\alpha):=4-\frac{6}{\alpha}\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{if}\hskip 10.00002pt\alpha\in(-1/2,0)\,,\hskip 10.00002pt\mathsf{q}(0):=+\infty\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{and}\hskip 10.00002pt\mathsf{q}(\alpha):=2+\frac{2}{\alpha}\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{if}\hskip 10.00002pt\alpha\in(0,1)\,.

(18)

Theorem 16
.

Let
\alpha\in(-1/2,1)
and
q\in(2,\infty)
. Then there is a constant
\mathcal{B}_{\alpha,q}>0
such that for all
\Phi\in\mathrm{H}^{1}({\mathbb{S}^{2}},{\mathbb{C}}^{2})
, we have

\int_{{\mathbb{S}^{2}}}{\left\langle{\Phi},{\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}\,\Phi}\right\rangle}\,d\omega\geq\frac{\mathcal{B}_{\alpha,q}}{q-2}\left(\left\|{\Phi}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{q}({\mathbb{S}^{2}},d\omega)}^{2}-\left\|{\Phi}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}^{2}},d\omega)}^{2}\right)\,.

(19)

Moreover, if
q\leq\mathsf{q}(\alpha)
, then the inequality holds with
\mathcal{B}_{\alpha,q}=2\,\mathsf{m}(\alpha)
and equality holds if and only if
\,\Phi\in\mathcal{H}_{0}
, that is, if and only if
\,\Phi
is a constant spinor.

We have no reason to think that
\mathcal{B}_{\alpha,q}=2\,\mathsf{m}(\alpha)
is optimal, even if
q\leq\mathsf{q}(\alpha)
. We recall that
d\omega
is the uniform probability measure on
{\mathbb{S}^{2}}
. In particular, if
q>2
, by Hölder’s inequality we have
\left\|{\Phi}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{q}({\mathbb{S}^{2}},d\omega)}^{2}-\left\|{\Phi}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}^{2}},d\omega)}^{2}\geq 0
and then equality holds if and only if
|\Phi|
is constant.

Also notice that, by taking the limit as
q\to 2_{+}
in the right-hand side of (
19
) and using
\mathcal{B}_{\alpha,q}=2\,\mathsf{m}(\alpha)
for
q
sufficiently close to
2
, for any
\alpha\in(-1/2,1)
, we obtain the logarithmic Sobolev inequality

\int_{{\mathbb{S}^{2}}}{\left\langle{\Phi},{\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}\,\Phi}\right\rangle}\,d\omega\geq\frac{1}{2}\,\mathsf{m}(\alpha)\int_{{\mathbb{S}^{2}}}{|\Phi|^{2}\,\log\left(\frac{|\Phi|^{2}}{\left\|{\Phi}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}^{2}},d\omega)}^{2}}\right)}\,d\omega\hskip 10.00002pt\forall\,\Phi\in\mathrm{H}^{1}({\mathbb{S}^{2}},{\mathbb{C}}^{2})\,.

We begin the proof of Theorem
16
by extending a well-known interpolation inequality obtained by Bidaut-Véron and Véron in
[
4
]
and Beckner in
[
2
]
for scalar functions on
{\mathbb{S}^{2}}
, to spinors, before adapting it to the operator
\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}
.

\bullet
An interpolation inequality for spinors.

Let us review Beckner’s rewriting
[
2
]
of Lieb’s sharp Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality
[
29
]
for scalar functions. Decomposing
F\in\mathrm{H}^{1}({\mathbb{S}^{2}},{\mathbb{C}})
into spherical harmonics, that is, writing

F=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}F_{\ell}\hskip 10.00002pt\text{with}\hskip 10.00002ptL^{2}\,F_{\ell}=\ell\,(\ell+1)\,F_{\ell}\,,

we can formulate the sharp Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality in the form

\frac{1}{q-2}\left(\left\|{F}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{q}({\mathbb{S}^{2}},d\omega)}^{2}-\left\|{F}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}^{2}},d\omega)}^{2}\right)\leq\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\zeta_{\ell}(q)\int_{{\mathbb{S}^{2}}}{|F_{\ell}|^{2}}\,d\omega

(20)

with

\zeta_{\ell}(q):=\frac{\gamma_{\ell}\big(\tfrac{2}{q}\big)-1}{q-2}\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{and}\hskip 10.00002pt\gamma_{\ell}(x):=\frac{\Gamma(x)\,\Gamma(\ell+2-x)}{\Gamma(2-x)\,\Gamma(x+\ell)}\,.

We refer to
[
2
, Ineq. (19)]
and also
[
17
, Ineq. (1.6)]
for more details. Let us recall how (
20
) is proved in
[
2
]
. Consider the sharp Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality
written on the sphere

0\leq\iint_{{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\times{\mathbb{S}^{2}}}\frac{\overline{F(\omega)}\,F(\omega^{\prime})}{|\omega-\omega^{\prime}|^{\lambda}}\,d\omega\,d\omega^{\prime}\leq C_{p}\,\|F\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}({\mathbb{S}^{2}})}^{2}\,,

(21)

where
\lambda=4/q
,
1\leq p<2
,
1/p+1/q=1
and
C_{p}
is the explicitly known sharp constant from
[
29
]
. Computing the eigenvalues of the integral operator with kernel
|\omega-\omega^{\prime}|^{-\lambda}
by means of the Funk-Hecke formula, we see that

\iint_{{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\times{\mathbb{S}^{2}}}\frac{\overline{F(\omega)}\,F(\omega^{\prime})}{|\omega-\omega^{\prime}|^{\lambda}}\,d\omega\,d\omega^{\prime}=C_{p}\,\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\gamma_{\ell}\big(\tfrac{2}{p}\big)\,\|F_{\ell}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}^{2}})}^{2}

(22)

with the constants
\gamma_{\ell}(2/p)
defined above. (The details of this computation can be found, for instance, in
[
23
]
.) Thus, Inequality (
21
) is equivalent to

\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\gamma_{\ell}\big(\tfrac{2}{p}\big)\,\|F_{\ell}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}^{2}})}^{2}\leq\|F\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}({\mathbb{S}^{2}})}^{2}\,.

By duality, noting that
\gamma_{\ell}(2/p)^{-1}=\gamma_{\ell}(2/q)
if
p
and
q
are Hölder conjugates, we see that this is equivalent to the inequality

\|F\|_{\mathrm{L}^{q}({\mathbb{S}^{2}})}^{2}\leq\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\gamma_{\ell}\big(\tfrac{2}{q}\big)\,\|F_{\ell}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}^{2}})}^{2}\,.

The latter is a rewriting of (
20
), thus concluding the proof as in
[
2
]
.

We can now generalize this method to the spinorial case. We decompose (angular) spinors
\Phi
as

\Phi=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\Phi_{\ell}\hskip 10.00002pt\text{with}\hskip 10.00002ptL^{2}\,\Phi_{\ell}=\ell\,(\ell+1)\,\Phi_{\ell}\,.

Lemma 17
.

Let
q\in(2,\infty)
. For any spinor
\Phi\in\mathrm{H}^{1}({\mathbb{S}^{2}},{\mathbb{C}}^{2})
, we have

\frac{1}{q-2}\left(\left\|{\Phi}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{q}({\mathbb{S}^{2}},d\omega)}^{2}-\left\|{\Phi}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}^{2}},d\omega)}^{2}\right)\leq\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\zeta_{\ell}(q)\int_{{\mathbb{S}^{2}}}{|\Phi_{\ell}|^{2}}\,d\omega

(23)

with equality if and only if

\Phi(\omega)=(1-\zeta\cdot\omega)^{-2/q}\chi_{0}

for some
\zeta\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
with
|\zeta|<1
and some
\chi_{0}\in{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
.

Proof.

For
\Phi\in\mathrm{L}^{p}({\mathbb{S}^{2}},{\mathbb{C}}^{2})
we have

\iint_{{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\times{\mathbb{S}^{2}}}\frac{\left\langle{\Phi(\omega)},{\Phi(\omega^{\prime})}\right\rangle}{|\omega-\omega^{\prime}|^{\lambda}}\,d\omega\,d\omega^{\prime}\leq\iint_{{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\times{\mathbb{S}^{2}}}\frac{|\Phi(\omega)|\,|\Phi(\omega^{\prime})|}{|\omega-\omega^{\prime}|^{\lambda}}\,d\omega\,d\omega^{\prime}\,.

(24)

By the sharp Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality (
21
) applied with
F=|\Phi|
, we obtain

\iint_{{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\times{\mathbb{S}^{2}}}\frac{\left\langle{\Phi(\omega)},{\Phi(\omega^{\prime})}\right\rangle}{|\omega-\omega^{\prime}|^{\lambda}}\,d\omega\,d\omega^{\prime}\leq C_{p}\,\|\Phi\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}({\mathbb{S}^{2}})}^{2}\,.

(25)

Meanwhile, we have

\iint_{{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\times{\mathbb{S}^{2}}}\frac{\left\langle{\Phi(\omega)},{\Phi(\omega^{\prime})}\right\rangle}{|\omega-\omega^{\prime}|^{\lambda}}\,d\omega\,d\omega^{\prime}=C_{p}\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\gamma_{\ell}\big(\tfrac{2}{p}\big)\,\|\Phi_{\ell}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}^{2}},{\mathbb{C}}^{2})}^{2}\,.

(26)

Indeed, to see this, we can decompose
\Phi_{\ell}=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\Phi^{1}_{\ell}\\
\Phi^{2}_{\ell}\end{smallmatrix}\right)
and the components
\Phi^{1}_{\ell}
and
\Phi^{2}_{\ell}
are complex-valued spherical harmonics of degree
\ell
. Since

\iint_{{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\times{\mathbb{S}^{2}}}\kern-3.0pt\frac{\left\langle{\Phi(\omega)},{\Phi(\omega^{\prime})}\right\rangle}{|\omega-\omega^{\prime}|^{\lambda}}\,d\omega\,d\omega^{\prime}=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\,\iint_{{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\times{\mathbb{S}^{2}}}\kern-3.0pt\frac{\overline{\Phi_{\ell}^{1}(\omega)}\,\Phi_{\ell}^{1}(\omega^{\prime})}{|\omega-\omega^{\prime}|^{\lambda}}\,d\omega+\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\,\iint_{{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\times{\mathbb{S}^{2}}}\kern-3.0pt\frac{\overline{\Phi_{\ell}^{2}(\omega)}\,\Phi_{\ell}^{2}(\omega^{\prime})}{|\omega-\omega^{\prime}|^{\lambda}}\,d\omega\,,

we can apply the scalar result (
22
) and obtain the claimed equality (
26
).

By combining (
25
) and (
26
) we find

\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\gamma_{\ell}\big(\tfrac{2}{p}\big)\,\|\Phi_{\ell}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}^{2}},{\mathbb{C}}^{2})}^{2}\leq\|\Phi\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}({\mathbb{S}^{2}},{\mathbb{C}}^{2})}^{2}\,,

(27)

and, by duality, with
q=p/(p-1)
, we obtain

\|\Phi\|_{\mathrm{L}^{q}({\mathbb{S}^{2}},{\mathbb{C}}^{2})}^{2}\leq\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\gamma_{\ell}\big(\tfrac{2}{q}\big)\,\|\Phi_{\ell}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}^{2}},{\mathbb{C}}^{2})}^{2}\,.

The latter is a rewriting of (
23
).

We now consider the cases of equality in (
27

) holds if and only if
\left\langle{\Phi(\omega)},{\Phi(\omega^{\prime})}\right\rangle=|\Phi(\omega)|\,|\Phi(\omega^{\prime})|

\Phi(\omega)=|\Phi(\omega)|\,U(\omega)\,\Phi_{0}\,,

where
\Phi_{0}=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1\\

and

is a

for a.e.

. This implies that there is a constant spinor
\tilde{\Phi}
such that

for a.e.

. (Note that this is the case even if

has several connected components.) Thus,
\Phi/|\Phi|
is a constant spinor on

. Equality in (

is an optimizer in the Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality (

[

]

\Phi(\omega)=(1-\zeta\cdot\omega)^{-(4-\lambda)/2}\,\chi_{0}
for some
\chi_{0}\in{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
and some
\zeta
as before.

By duality (more precisely, by the characterization of cases of equality in Hölder’s inequality) we deduce the claimed form of functions for which Inequality (
23
) is saturated.
∎

\bullet
An estimate on the decomposition into spherical harmonics.

Inequality (
23
) is phrased in terms of the decomposition of
\Phi
in terms of spherical harmonics that are eigenfunctions of the operator
L^{2}
. In contrast, Inequality (
19
) in Theorem
16
involves the operator
\sigma\cdot L
and therefore we now decompose
\Phi
with respect to the spectrum of the latter operator, that is, with respect to spinor spherical harmonics. We write

\Phi=\sum_{k=-\infty}^{-2}\chi_{k}+\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\chi_{k}\,.

(28)

This decomposition is related to the decomposition in Lemma
17
by
\Phi_{\ell}=\chi_{\ell}+\chi_{-(\ell+1)}
for all
\ell\geq 0
. For a proof of this fact we refer to Appendix
B
; see, in particular, Corollary
34
. Consequently, we can rewrite (
23
) as

\frac{\left\|{\Phi}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{q}({\mathbb{S}^{2}},d\omega)}^{2}-\left\|{\Phi}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}^{2}},d\omega)}^{2}}{q-2}\leq\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\zeta_{k}(q)\int_{{\mathbb{S}^{2}}}{|\chi_{k}|^{2}}\,d\omega+\sum_{k=-\infty}^{-2}\zeta_{-(k+1)}(q)\int_{{\mathbb{S}^{2}}}{|\chi_{k}|^{2}}\,d\omega\,.

Noting that

\int_{{\mathbb{S}^{2}}}{\left\langle{\Phi},{\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}\,\Phi}\right\rangle}\,d\omega=\sum_{k\neq-1}k\,(k+1-2\,\alpha)\int_{{\mathbb{S}^{2}}}{|\chi_{k}|^{2}}\,d\omega\,,

we obtain Inequality (
19
) with

\mathcal{B}_{\alpha,q}:=\min\left\{\inf_{k\geq 1}\frac{k\,(k+1-2\,\alpha)}{\zeta_{k}(q)}\,,\,\inf_{k\leq-2}\frac{k\,(k+1-2\,\alpha)}{\zeta_{-(k+1)}(q)}\right\}\,.

(29)

Thus, the proof of the first assertion of Theorem
16
is reduced to the proof that
\mathcal{B}_{\alpha,q}>0
for any
\alpha\in(-1/2,1)
and
q\in(2,\infty)
. Likewise, the proof of the second assertion is reduced to the proof that, for
q\leq\mathsf{q}(\alpha)
, we have
\mathcal{B}_{\alpha,q}=2\,\mathsf{m}(\alpha)
and the infimum in the definition of
\mathcal{B}_{\alpha,q}
is attained only at either
k=1
or
k=-\,2
. We begin with the proof of the latter assertion.

Lemma 18
.

Let
\alpha\in(-1/2,1)
. Then the following inequalities hold:

(a)

Assuming
2<q<\infty
for
\alpha\in[0,1)
and
2<q\leq 4-\frac{6}{\alpha}
for
\alpha\in(-1/2,0)
, we have

\zeta_{\ell}(q)\leq\frac{\ell\,(\ell+1-2\,\alpha)}{2\,(1-\alpha)}\hskip 10.00002pt\text{for all}\ \ell\in{\mathbb{N}}\setminus\{0\}\,.

(30)

(b)

Assuming
2<q<\infty
when
\alpha\in(-1/2,0]
and
2<q\leq 2+\frac{2}{\alpha}
when
\alpha\in(0,1)
, we have

\zeta_{\ell}(q)\leq\frac{(\ell+1)\,(\ell+2\,\alpha)}{2\,(1+2\,\alpha)}\hskip 10.00002pt\text{for all}\ \ell\in{\mathbb{N}}\setminus\{0\}\,.

(31)

There is equality in (
30
) and (
31
) for
\ell=1
while both are strict for
\ell\geq 3
. For
\ell=2
there is equality if and only if
q=4-\frac{6}{\alpha}
,
\alpha\in(-1/2,0)
in the first case and
q=2+\frac{2}{\alpha}
,
\alpha\in(0,1)
in the second case.

To prove Lemma
18
we extend a method of
[
6
, proof of Theorem 7]
for scalar functions in the special case
\alpha=0
to the case
\alpha\neq 0
.

Proof.

Throughout the proof the parameter
\alpha\in(-1/2,1)
is fixed. Let us define

z_{\ell}^{+}(\alpha,q):=\frac{2\,(1-\alpha)}{\ell\,(\ell+1-2\,\alpha)}\,\zeta_{\ell}(q)\hskip 10.00002pt\text{and}\hskip 10.00002ptz_{\ell}^{-}(\alpha,q):=\frac{2\,(1+2\,\alpha)}{(\ell+1)\,(\ell+2\,\alpha)}\,\zeta_{\ell}(q)\,.

An elementary computation shows that

z_{\ell+1}^{\pm}(\alpha,q)=\mathsf{a}_{\ell}^{\pm}(\alpha,q)+\mathsf{b}_{\ell}^{\pm}(\alpha,q)\,z_{\ell}^{\pm}(\alpha,q)

(32)

with

\mathsf{a}_{\ell}^{+}(\alpha,q):=\frac{4\,(1-\alpha)}{(\ell+1)\,(\ell+2-2\,\alpha)\,(\ell\,q+2)}\,,\hskip 10.00002pt\mathsf{b}_{\ell}^{+}(\alpha,q):=\frac{\ell\,(\ell+1-2\,\alpha)\,\big((\ell+2)\,q-2\big)}{(\ell+1)\,(\ell+2-2\,\alpha)\,(\ell\,q+2)}

and

\mathsf{a}_{\ell}^{-}(\alpha,q):=\frac{4\,(1+2\,\alpha)}{(\ell+2)\,(\ell+1+2\,\alpha)\,(\ell\,q+2)}\,,\hskip 10.00002pt\mathsf{b}_{\ell}^{-}(\alpha,q):=\frac{(\ell+1)\,(\ell+2\,\alpha)\,\big((\ell+2)\,q-2\big)}{(\ell+2)\,(\ell+1+2\,\alpha)\,(\ell\,q+2)}\,.

We now make two claims concerning
\mathsf{a}_{\ell}^{\pm}(\alpha,q)+\mathsf{b}_{\ell}^{\pm}(\alpha,q)
. The first claim is that if
2<q<\infty
for
\alpha\in[0,1)
and
2<q<4-\frac{6}{\alpha}
for
\alpha\in(-1/2,0)
, then

\mathsf{a}_{\ell}^{+}(\alpha,q)+\mathsf{b}_{\ell}^{+}(\alpha,q)<1\hskip 10.00002pt\forall\,\ell\geq 1\,.

(33)

Moreover, if
q=4-\frac{6}{\alpha}
for
\alpha\in(-1/2,0)
, then the inequality in (
33
) holds for all
\ell\geq 2
, while it is an equality for
\ell=1
. The second claim is that if
2<q<\infty
for
\alpha\in(-1/2,0]
and
2<q<2+\frac{2}{\alpha}
for
\alpha\in(0,1)
, then

\mathsf{a}_{\ell}^{-}(\alpha,q)+\mathsf{b}_{\ell}^{-}(\alpha,q)<1\hskip 10.00002pt\forall\,\ell\geq 1\,.

(34)

Moreover, if
q=2+\frac{2}{\alpha}
for
\alpha\in(0,1)
, then the inequality in (
34
) holds for all
\ell\geq 2
, while it is an equality for
\ell=1
.

Before proving these two claims, let us use them to complete the proof of the lemma. We observe that
z_{1}^{\pm}(\alpha,q)=1
. By induction, this together with (
32
), (
33
) and (
34
) implies that
z_{\ell}^{\pm}(\alpha,q)<1
for any
\ell\geq 2
. This completes the proof of (
30
) and (
31
), even with strict inequality for
\ell\geq 2
, except for the cases
q=4-\frac{6}{\alpha}
,
\alpha\in(-1/2,0)
for (
30
) and
q=2+\frac{2}{\alpha}
,
\alpha\in(-1/2,0)
for (
31
). In these exceptional cases, the equality in (
33
) and (
34
) for
\ell=1
implies that
z_{2}^{\pm}(\alpha,q)=1
. The strict inequalities for
\ell\geq 2
then allows us to repeat the induction argument and arrive again at (
30
) and (
31
), now with strict inequality for
\ell\geq 3
.

Thus, we have reduced the proof of the lemma to the proof of (
33
) and (
34
). We set

F_{\ell}^{\pm}(\alpha,q):=\mathsf{a}_{\ell}^{\pm}(\alpha,q)+\mathsf{b}_{\ell}^{\pm}(\alpha,q)\,.

It is convenient to consider these as functions of
q
in the interval
(-2/\ell,\infty)
. We note that
F_{\ell}^{\pm}(\alpha,\cdot)
is a quotient of affine functions. A computation, plus some straightforward estimates show that
F_{\ell}^{\pm}(\alpha,\cdot)
is an increasing function in the interval
(-2/\ell,\infty)
. Moreover, as
q\to\infty
, it converges to its horizontal asymptote
y_{\ell}^{\pm}(\alpha)
, given by

y_{\ell}^{+}(\alpha):=\frac{(\ell+1-2\,\alpha)\,(\ell+2)}{(\ell+1)\,(\ell+2-2\,\alpha)}\hskip 10.00002pt\text{and}\hskip 10.00002pty_{\ell}^{-}(\alpha):=\frac{(\ell+1)\,(\ell+2\,\alpha)}{(\ell+1+2\,\alpha)\,\ell}\,.

To complete the proof of (
33
) and (
34
), we now consider these two cases separately.

Proof of Inequality (
33
).

A computation shows that
y^{+}_{\ell}(\alpha)\leq 1
if
\alpha\in[0,1]
. Thus, in this case we have
F_{\ell}^{+}(\alpha,q)<y^{+}_{\ell}(\alpha)\leq 1
for all
q>-\frac{2}{\ell}
, which immediately implies the assertion.

For
\alpha<0
we observe that
F_{\ell}^{+}(\alpha,q_{\ell}^{+}(\alpha))=1
for
q_{\ell}^{+}(\alpha):=4-2\,\frac{\ell+2}{\alpha}
. Thus, by monotonicity, we have
F_{\ell}^{+}(\alpha,q)<1
for all
-\,\frac{2}{\ell}<q<q_{\ell}^{+}(\alpha)
. In particular, we have
F_{\ell}^{+}(\alpha,q)<1
for all
\ell\geq 1
when
0\leq q<4-\frac{6}{\alpha}=\min_{\ell\geq 1}q_{\ell}^{+}(\alpha)
. We also see that for
q=4-\frac{6}{\alpha}
, this inequality holds for all
\ell\geq 2
with equality for
\ell=1
. This proves (
33
).

Proof of Inequality (
34
).

A computation show that
y^{-}_{\ell}(\alpha)\leq 1
if
\alpha\in(-1/2,0]
. Thus, in this case we have
F_{\ell}^{-}(\alpha,q)<y^{-}_{\ell}(\alpha)\leq 1
for all
q>-\frac{2}{\ell}
, which immediately implies the assertion.

For
\alpha>0
we observe that
F_{\ell}^{-}(\alpha,q_{\ell}^{-}(\alpha))=1
for
q_{\ell}^{-}(\alpha):=2\,\big(\frac{\ell}{\alpha}+\frac{1+2\ell}{2+\ell}\big)
. Thus, by monotonicity, we have
F_{\ell}^{-}(\alpha,q)<1
for all
-\,\frac{2}{\ell}<q<q_{\ell}^{-}(\alpha)
. In particular, we have
F_{\ell}^{-}(\alpha,q)<1
for all
\ell\geq 1
when
0\leq q<2+\frac{2}{\alpha}=\min_{\ell\geq 1}q_{\ell}^{-}(\alpha)
. We also see that for
q=2+\frac{2}{\alpha}
, this inequality holds for all
\ell\geq 2
with equality for
\ell=1
. This proves (
34
).
∎

Remark 19
.

For later purposes we record that clearly
\lim_{\ell\to+\infty}\big(\mathsf{a}_{\ell}^{\pm}(\alpha,q),\mathsf{b}_{\ell}^{\pm}(\alpha,q)\big)=(0,1)
. A more precise analysis show that
\mathsf{a}_{\ell}^{\pm}(\alpha,q)+\mathsf{b}_{\ell}^{\pm}(\alpha,q)<1
for all
\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}
large enough (depending on
\alpha
and
q
). Proceeding as in the above prove, we deduce that
z_{\ell}^{\pm}(\alpha,q)
achieves a maximum for some finite
\ell\geq 2
. In particular, we have that

\sup_{\ell\geq 1}\frac{2\,(1-\alpha)}{\ell\,(\ell+1-2\,\alpha)}\,\zeta_{\ell}(q)+\sup_{\ell\geq 1}\frac{2\,(1+2\,\alpha)}{(\ell+1)\,(\ell+2\,\alpha)}\,\zeta_{\ell}(q)<\infty\,.

\bullet
Proof of Theorem
16
.

We recall the
\mathcal{B}_{\alpha,q}
was defined in (
29
). We can rewrite this as

\mathcal{B}_{\alpha,q}=\min\left\{\inf_{\ell\geq 1}\frac{\ell(\ell+1-2\,\alpha)}{\zeta_{\ell}(q)}\,,\,\inf_{\ell\geq 1}\frac{(\ell+1)\,(\ell+2\,\alpha)}{\zeta_{\ell}(q)}\right\}\,.

It follows from Remark
19
that
\mathcal{B}_{\alpha,q}>0
, which, according to the discussion before Lemma
18
, implies the first assertion of Theorem
16
.

Moreover, recalling the definition of
\mathsf{q}(\alpha)
in (
18
), we deduce from Lemma
18
that

\mathcal{B}_{\alpha,q}=\min\Big\{2\,(1-\alpha)\,,\,2\,(1+2\,\alpha)\Big\}=2\,\mathsf{m}(\alpha)\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{if}\hskip 10.00002ptq\leq\mathsf{q}(\alpha)\,.

This proves the second assertion of Theorem
16
.

Still assuming
q\leq q(\alpha)
, let us discuss the cases of equality in (
19
). Clearly, both sides of the inequality vanish for constant spinors. Conversely, assume that equality holds in the inequality for some spinor
\Phi
decomposed according to (
28
). By Lemma
18
, we deduce that
\chi_{k}=0
for all
k\neq-\,3
,
-\,2
,
0
,
1
,
2
. (The cases
k=-\,3
,
2
are only relevant when
q=\mathsf{q}(\alpha)
.)

We note that both components of angular spinors in
\mathcal{H}_{-3}\oplus\mathcal{H}_{-2}\oplus\mathcal{H}_{0}\oplus\mathcal{H}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{H}_{2}
are polynomials in
\omega_{1}
,
\omega_{2}
,
\omega_{3}
of degree
\leq 2
. Meanwhile, from Lemma
17
we know that
\Phi(\omega)=(1-\zeta\cdot\omega)^{-2/q}\chi_{0}
for some
\zeta\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
with
|\zeta|<1
and
\chi_{0}\in{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
. We deduce that
\zeta=0
(unless
\chi_{0}=0
). Thus,
\Phi
is a constant spinor, as claimed. This completes the proof of Theorem
16
.
∎

6.2.
A symmetry range

In this subsection, our goal is to characterize a set of parameters
(\alpha,p)\in(-1/2,1]\times(2,6)
such that
C^{\star}_{\alpha,p}=C_{\alpha,p}
. We adapt the method of
[
12
]
, but significant changes have to be implemented in order to deal with spinors.

Theorem 20
.

If one of the following conditions is satisfied,

\displaystyle\alpha\in\big(-\tfrac{1}{2},0\big]\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{and}\hskip 10.00002pt2<p\leq 2\,\frac{3+4\,(\alpha+1)^{2}}{3+4\,\alpha^{2}}\,,

(35a)

\displaystyle\alpha\in\big(0,\tfrac{1}{2}\big)\cup\big(\tfrac{1}{2},1\big)\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{and}\hskip 10.00002pt2<p\leq\min\left\{2\,\frac{3+2\,\alpha}{1+2\,\alpha},\;2\,\frac{7-10\,\alpha+4\,\alpha^{2}}{3-6\,\alpha+4\,\alpha^{2}}\right\}\,,

(35b)

then
C_{\alpha,p}=C^{\star}_{\alpha,p}
and any optimizer for Inequality (
SCKNlog
) is symmetric of type
\mathcal{H}_{0}
.

See Fig.
5
for a plot of Conditions (
35a
) and (
35b
).

Proof.
We split the proof in several steps.

\rhd
First step

The following result was suggested by Keller in
[
26
]
and proved independently by Lieb and Thirring in
[
28
]
. The underlying one-dimensional interpolation inequality was established by Nagy
[
37
]
. As it is stated below, it is taken from
[
12
]
. Let us define

c_{\rm LT}(\gamma):=\frac{\pi^{-1/2}}{\gamma-1/2}\,\frac{\Gamma(\gamma+1)}{\Gamma(\gamma+1/2)}\left(\frac{\gamma-1/2}{\gamma+1/2}\right)^{\gamma+1/2}\,.

(36)

Lemma 21
.

Let
V=V(s)
be a non-negative real valued potential in
\mathrm{L}^{\gamma+1/2}({\mathbb{R}})
for some
\gamma>1/2
. If
-\,\lambda_{1}(V)
is the lowest eigenvalue of the Schrödinger operator
-\,\frac{d^{2}}{ds^{2}}-V
, then

\lambda_{1}(V)^{\gamma}\leq c_{\rm LT}(\gamma)\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}{V^{\gamma+1/2}(s)}\,ds\,,

(37)

with equality if and only if

V(s)=B^{2}\,V_{0}\big(B\,(s-C)\big)\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{and}\hskip 10.00002ptV_{0}(s):=\frac{\gamma^{2}-1/4}{(\cosh s)^{2}}\hskip 10.00002pt\forall\,s\in{\mathbb{R}}\,,

where
B>0
,
C\in{\mathbb{R}}
are constants. In that case,
\lambda_{1}(V_{0})=\left(\gamma-1/2\right)^{2}
and the corresponding eigenspace is generated by

u_{0}(s)=\left(\frac{\Gamma(\gamma)}{\sqrt{\pi}\,\Gamma(\gamma-1/2)}\right)^{1/2}\,\big(\cosh s\big)^{1/2-\gamma}\,.

See Appendix
A
for a proof. Here
u_{0}
is normalized by the condition
\left\|{u_{0}}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}=1
.

Let

\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}[\phi]:=\|{\partial_{s}\phi}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{S}^{2}})}^{2}+\big\|\big(\sigma\cdot L+\tfrac{1}{2}-\alpha\big)\,\phi\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{S}^{2}})}^{2}-C_{\alpha,p}\,\|{\phi}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}({\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{S}^{2}})}^{2}\,.

We assume that
\phi
is an optimizer for (
SCKNlog
). By homogeneity we may assume without loss of generality that

\|{\phi}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}({\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{S}^{2}})}^{p-2}=C_{\alpha,p}\leq C^{\star}_{\alpha,p}\,.

(38)

It follows from this normalization that
\phi
satisfies the Euler–Lagrange equation in the form

-\,\partial_{s}^{2}\,\phi+\big(\sigma\cdot L-\alpha+\tfrac{1}{2}\big)^{2}\,\phi=|\phi|^{p-2}\,\phi\hskip 10.00002pt\text{in}\hskip 10.00002pt{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\,.

(39)

Multiplying this equation by
\phi
and integrating, we deduce that

0=\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{S}^{2}}}{\left(|\partial_{s}\phi|^{2}+\big|\big(\sigma\cdot L-\alpha+\tfrac{1}{2}\big)\,\phi\big|^{2}-|\phi|^{p}\right)}\,ds\,d\omega=\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}[\phi]\,.

(40)

Similar estimates apply in the symmetric case. Indeed, for
\alpha>-1/2
, symmetric optimizers for (
SCKNlog
) can be rewritten, after a translation, as

\phi_{\star}(s,\omega)=u_{\star}(s)\,\chi\,,

(41)

where
\chi\in{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
and
u_{\star}(s)=A\,u_{0}(B\,s)
with
B=\frac{1}{2}\,(p-2)\,\sqrt{\lambda}
and
\lambda=\big(\alpha-\tfrac{1}{2}\big)^{2}
, that is,

u_{\star}(s)=\left(\tfrac{p\,\lambda}{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{p-2}}\left(\cosh\left(\tfrac{1}{2}\,(p-2)\,\sqrt{\lambda}\,s\right)\right)^{-\frac{2}{p-2}}\hskip 10.00002pt\forall\,s\in{\mathbb{R}}\,.

(42)

Without loss of generality, we shall assume that
|\chi|=1
.
See Appendix
A
for details. As above, let us choose
A
such that

\left\|{u_{\star}}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}({\mathbb{R}})}^{p-2}=C^{\star}_{\alpha,p}\,.

(43)

As a consequence of this and (
38
), we obtain

\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{S}^{2}}}{|\phi|^{p}}\,ds\,d\omega\leq\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{S}^{2}}}{|\phi_{\star}|^{p}}\,ds\,d\omega\,.

(44)

For the same reasons as before,
\phi_{\star}
solves (
39
) and

0=\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{S}^{2}}}{\left(|\partial_{s}\phi_{\star}|^{2}+\big|\big(\sigma\cdot L-\alpha+\tfrac{1}{2}\big)\,\phi_{\star}\big|^{2}-|\phi_{\star}|^{p}\right)}\,ds\,d\omega\,.

(45)

After these preliminaries, we establish a lower bound for
\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}[\phi]
. Let

\gamma=\frac{1}{2}\,\frac{p+2}{p-2}>\frac{1}{2}\,.

(46)

For a.e.
\omega\in{\mathbb{S}^{2}}
, we apply Lemma
21
with
V(s)=|\phi(s,\omega)|^{p-2}
and infer that the lowest eigenvalue
-\,\lambda_{1}(V)
of the operator
\,-\frac{d^{2}}{ds^{2}}-V\,
acting in
\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{S}^{2}})
is bounded from below according to

-\,\lambda_{1}(V)\geq-\,c_{\rm LT}(\gamma)^{1/\gamma}\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}{|\phi(s,\omega)|^{p}}\,ds\right)^{1/\gamma}\,.

The same estimate applies to the lowest eigenvalue of the corresponding operator acting in
\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{S}^{2}},{\mathbb{C}}^{2})
, since the operator acts trivially on the spin. By the variational characterization of the first eigenvalue, we deduce that for a.e.
\omega\in{\mathbb{S}^{2}}
we have

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}{\left(|\partial_{s}\phi(s,\omega)|^{2}-|\phi(s,\omega)|^{p}\right)}\,ds\geq-\,c_{\rm LT}(\gamma)^{1/\gamma}\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}{|\phi(s,\omega)|^{p}}\,ds\right)^{1/\gamma}|u(\omega)|^{2}\,,

where we set

u(\omega):=\sqrt{\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}{|\phi(s,\omega)|^{2}}\,ds}\,.

Integrating this inequality with respect to
\omega\in{\mathbb{S}^{2}}
we obtain

\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}[\phi]\geq-\,c_{\rm LT}(\gamma)^{1/\gamma}\int_{{\mathbb{S}^{2}}}{\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}{|\phi(s,\omega)|^{p}}\,ds\right)^{1/\gamma}|u(\omega)|^{2}}\,d\omega+\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{S}^{2}}}{\left|\left(\sigma\cdot L-\alpha+\tfrac{1}{2}\right)\phi\right|^{2}}\,ds\,d\omega\,.

\rhd
Second step

Applying Theorem
16
to
\phi(s,\cdot)
for almost every
s\in{\mathbb{R}}
and integrating with respect to
s
we find that, for each
q\in\big(2,\infty\big)
,

\displaystyle\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{S}^{2}}}{\left|\left(\sigma\cdot L-\alpha+\tfrac{1}{2}\right)\phi\right|^{2}}\,ds\,d\omega=\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{S}^{2}}}{\left\langle{\phi},{\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}\,\phi}\right\rangle}\,ds\,d\omega+\left(\alpha-\tfrac{1}{2}\right)^{2}\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{S}^{2}}}{|\phi|^{2}}\,ds\,d\omega

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\mathcal{B}_{\alpha,q}}{q-2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(\left(\int_{{\mathbb{S}^{2}}}{|\phi(s,\omega)|^{q}}\,d\omega\right)^{2/q}-\int_{{\mathbb{S}^{2}}}{|\phi(s,\omega)|^{2}}\,d\omega\right)\,ds+\left(\alpha-\tfrac{1}{2}\right)^{2}\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{S}^{2}}}{|\phi|^{2}}\,ds\,d\omega\,.

By Minkowski’s inequality (see for instance
[
30
, Section 2.4, p. 47]
),

\left(\int_{{\mathbb{S}^{2}}}{u(\omega)^{q}}\,d\omega\right)^{2/q}=\left(\int_{{\mathbb{S}^{2}}}{\left({\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}{|\phi(s,\omega)|^{2}}\,ds}\right)^{q/2}}\,d\omega\right)^{2/q}\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(\int_{{\mathbb{S}^{2}}}{|\phi(s,\omega)|^{q}}\,d\omega\right)^{2/q}\,ds\,.

By combining this with the inequality from Step 1, we obtain for all
q\in\big(2,\mathsf{q}(\alpha)\big)
,

\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}[\phi]\geq-\,c_{\rm LT}(\gamma)^{1/\gamma}\int_{{\mathbb{S}^{2}}}{\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}{|\phi(s,\omega)|^{p}}\,ds\right)^{1/\gamma}|u(\omega)|^{2}}\,d\omega\\
+\frac{\mathcal{B}_{\alpha,q}}{q-2}\left(\left(\int_{{\mathbb{S}^{2}}}{|u(\omega)|^{q}}\,d\omega\right)^{2/q}-\int_{{\mathbb{S}^{2}}}{|u(\omega)|^{2}}\,d\omega\right)+\left(\alpha-\tfrac{1}{2}\right)^{2}\int_{{\mathbb{S}^{2}}}{|u(\omega)|^{2}}\,d\omega\,.

\rhd
Third step

Assuming
\gamma>1
, we apply Hölder’s inequality,

\int_{{\mathbb{S}^{2}}}{\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}{|\phi(s,\omega)|^{p}}\,ds\right)^{\frac{1}{\gamma}}|u(\omega)|^{2}}\,d\omega\leq\left(\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{S}^{2}}}{|\phi(s,\omega)|^{p}}\,ds\,d\omega\right)^{\frac{1}{\gamma}}\left(\int_{{\mathbb{S}^{2}}}{|u(\omega)|^{\frac{2\,\gamma}{\gamma-1}}}\,d\omega\right)^{\frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma}}\,.

Thus, setting

D:=c_{\rm LT}(\gamma)^{1/\gamma}\left(\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{S}^{2}}}{|\phi(s,\omega)|^{p}}\,ds\,d\omega\right)^{\frac{1}{\gamma}}\,,

we obtain

\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}[\phi]\geq-D\left(\int_{{\mathbb{S}^{2}}}{|u(\omega)|^{\frac{2\,\gamma}{\gamma-1}}}\,d\omega\right)^{\frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma}}+\left(\alpha-\tfrac{1}{2}\right)^{2}\int_{{\mathbb{S}^{2}}}{|u(\omega)|^{2}}\,d\omega\\
+\frac{\mathcal{B}_{\alpha,q}}{q-2}\left(\left(\int_{{\mathbb{S}^{2}}}{|u(\omega)|^{q}}\,d\omega\right)^{2/q}-\int_{{\mathbb{S}^{2}}}{|u(\omega)|^{2}}\,d\omega\right)\,.

At this point we choose the parameter
q
such that

q=\frac{2\,\gamma}{\gamma-1}=2\,\frac{p+2}{6-p}\,.

(47)

This choice is consistent with the requirement
\gamma\in(1,+\infty)
if and only if
p\in(2,6)
. We find that

\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}[\phi]\geq\left(\frac{\mathcal{B}_{\alpha,q}}{q-2}-D\right)\left(\int_{{\mathbb{S}^{2}}}{|u|^{q}}\,d\omega\right)^{\frac{2}{q}}+\left(\left(\alpha-\tfrac{1}{2}\right)^{2}-\frac{\mathcal{B}_{\alpha,q}}{q-2}\right)\int_{{\mathbb{S}^{2}}}{|u|^{2}}\,d\omega=:\mathcal{E}[u]\,.

\rhd
Fourth step

By (

Appendix B
Spinor spherical harmonics decomposition

The purpose of this appendix is twofold. On the one hand, we want to give a self-contained proof of the decomposition of

into eigenspaces of the operator

We consider the operator

The following result is well-known in physics and in differential geometry. We provide an elementary proof along the lines of

Theorem 32
.

The spectrum of
D
is discrete and consists of the eigenvalues
\pm(1+\kappa)
,
\kappa\in{\mathbb{N}}

Proof.

\rhd

Step 1.
Let
\mathcal{M}_{+}:={\mathbb{C}}^{2}
, identified with constant spinors in
\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}^{2}},{\mathbb{C}}^{2})

\mathcal{M}_{-}:=\big\{\sigma\cdot\omega\,\Psi\,:\,\Psi\in{\mathbb{C}}^{2}\big\}\,.

We claim that

\ker(D\mp 1)=\mathcal{M}_{\pm}\,.

(52)

Indeed, clearly we have
D\,\psi=\psi
for
\psi\in\mathcal{M}_{+}
and conversely, if
\psi\in\mathrm{H}^{1}({\mathbb{S}^{2}},{\mathbb{C}}^{2})

(since

52

52

D\,\sigma\cdot\omega=-\,\sigma\cdot\omega\,D\,,

(53)

5

\rhd

Step 2.
We claim that for either choice of the sign, the spectrum of

(\ell+1/2)^{2}
,
\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}

2\,(2\,\ell+1)

{\mathbb{C}}

\left(D\,\mp\tfrac{1}{2}\right)^{2}\,(f\,\psi)=\left(-\Delta f+\tfrac{1}{4}f\right)\psi\hskip 10.00002pt\text{if}\hskip 10.00002pt\psi\in\mathcal{M}_{\pm}\,.

Here

denotes the Laplace-Beltrami operator on

with the sign convention

It is well known that the spectrum of the operator

\rhd

\dim\mathcal{H}_{k}=2\,|k+1|
by Corollary
34
, these functions form a basis of
\mathcal{H}_{k}
. Finally, the normalization of
\chi_{k}^{m}
follows immediately from that of the
Y_{k}^{m\pm 1/2}
.

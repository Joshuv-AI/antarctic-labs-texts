Content validity, practicality and testing of the reliability of various tools for the detection and screening of delirium in residents with dementia in German nursing homes: a study protocol
Authors: Johanna Christina Seiters, Vincent Molitor, Alessandro Morandi, Tania Zieschang, Martin N Dichter, Burkhard Haastert, Maria Erdmann, Falk Hoffmann, Rebecca Palm
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionAlthough individuals with dementia who reside in nursing homes are particularly susceptible to developing delirium, this condition is rarely recognised in these settings. Detection of delirium requires validated and reliable tools for this setting that can be applied by nursing staff. The primary objective of the study is to assess the comprehensibility, practicality and inter-rater reliability of two delirium detection tools (4 A’s test (4AT) and 4-item delirium superimposed on dementia (4-DSD)) and one tool for assessing both the diagnosis and severity of delirium (Delirium Rating Scale-Revised-98 (DRS-R-98)) in people with dementia who reside in nursing homes when used by nurses.Methods and analysisThe comprehensibility and practicality of the German versions of the 4AT, 4-DSD and DRS-R-98 will be evaluated using cognitive interviews in accordance with the consensus-based standards for the selection of health measurement instruments framework. On the basis of the results of the cognitive interviews, a manual for each of the three tools will be developed and finalised by an expert panel. Finally, the inter-rater reliability and measurement error of the three tools will be determined using the manuals. For this purpose, a total of 70 residents from six nursing homes will be assessed by at least two nurses. The data analysis will include descriptive statistics and inter-rater reliability assessment for individual items (kappa value) and total scores (Intraclass correlation coefficients).Ethics and disseminationThis study will assess the comprehensibility, practicality and inter-rater reliability of the 4AT, 4-DSD and DRS-R-98 for use in residents with dementia in nursing homes. The project was approved by the medical ethics committee of the University of Oldenburg (reference number: 2025-093).Trial registration numberThe inter-rater reliability study was registered in the German Clinical Trials Register. Registration number: DRKS00037458 (https://www.drks.de/search/de/trial/DRKS00037458/details).Keywords: Delirium, Dementia, Psychometrics, Nursing Homes

Introduction

Background and rationale

Delirium is a complex, multifactorial neuropsychiatric syndrome characterised by an acute disturbance in attention and awareness, which arises over a brief period and often shows fluctuations in severity during the course of the day.1 It is also associated with impairment in cognitive functions, which cannot be accounted for by the presence of another established or developing neurocognitive disorder.1 2 The underlying cause of delirium is generally direct physiological effects related to a medical illness, substance intoxication or withdrawal, or exposure to toxic agents.1 Furthermore, it is a medical emergency that requires immediate action and is associated with severe complications, including increased mortality and cognitive and functional decline.3,6

Nursing home residents and people with dementia in particular are very susceptible to developing delirium.7,9 Despite this, it is rarely recognised and diagnosed.8 For people with dementia in particular, identifying delirium is highly challenging for nurses and physicians.10,13 It is assumed that 87% of delirium cases among people with dementia go undetected by nursing staff.14 Therefore, identifying delirium in the nursing home population, where the majority of residents are affected by dementia,15 is highly important. To achieve this goal, it is essential to use standardised screening and detection tools.13 14 16 17 However, in nursing homes, nurses are at the forefront of care because physicians rarely attend to patients. Therefore, nurses play a pivotal role in detecting delirium.10 18 However, the psychometric properties of delirium detection tools among high-risk nursing home residents with dementia have rarely been investigated when these tools are used by nurses.8 A recent scoping review further emphasised the lack of standardised delirium detection methods in nursing homes.16 Among the tools examined in this review, the 4 A’s test (4AT)19 has demonstrated high test accuracy.16 The Delirium Rating Scale-Revised-98 (DRS-R-98) was highlighted because it includes all three domains of delirium.16

As part of this study, the 4AT,19 4-item delirium superimposed on dementia (4-DSD)20 and the DRS-R-9821 tools are used. These instruments were selected because of their suitability for detecting delirium in people with dementia, as well as their practical applicability in care settings.

The 4AT requires neither specialised training nor a baseline assessment, making it particularly well suited for routine use in nursing homes (https://www.the4at.com/userguide). Furthermore, it is internationally recommended by the National Institute for Health and Care Excellence in Guideline Delirium: Diagnosis, Prevention and Management.22 The 4AT has been extensively tested in settings other than nursing homes and has been used in numerous studies.1923,30 Although the 4AT is generally considered appropriate for use in people with dementia, studies have shown limitations in its specificity within this population.19 31 To date, the inter-rater reliability of the 4AT has been examined only in clinical settings,27 meaning that data specific to nursing home populations are currently lacking. Moreover, owing to its reduced specificity in nursing home residents with dementia, the 4AT carries a risk of false-positive results.19 31 The 4AT is already available in German. However, there is no information concerning the translation process, and whether the tests performed by nursing staff in nursing homes are sufficiently reliable and valid has not been tested. The tool is freely available at https://static1.squarespace.com/static/543cac47e4b0388ca43554df/t/58219f9fd1758e7a255932a0/1478598561271/4ATgerman.pdf.

The 4-DSD was specifically developed to detect delirium in people with moderate to severe dementia, with the goal of improving diagnostic accuracy when other tools may be less effective.20 Although the 4-DSD has been tested in only one validation study, it demonstrated satisfactory diagnostic accuracy in individuals with moderate dementia.20 The validation does not include information on comprehensibility, practicality or inter-rater reliability. Owing to its recent development, a validated German version of the 4-DSD is not yet available, and no psychometric evaluation has been conducted for this language. Since the 4-DSD is currently available only in English and Italian, translation into German was carried out using the forwards-backwards method of Beaton et al.32 Similarly, the process followed the consensus-based standards for the selection of health measurement instruments (COSMIN) Study Design checklist for patient-reported outcome measurement tools33 adapted for clinician-reported outcome measures. Although the original tool was developed in Italian, English served as the source language for this translation. Permission to translate the 4-DSD into German was obtained from the authors. Details of the translation process are provided in online supplemental appendix 1. The final stages of the adaptation process (Stage 5—Pretest and Stage 6—Submission of documentation to the developers for evaluation) are planned but have not yet been completed.

The DRS-R-98 is a widely used assessment tool for diagnosing delirium and evaluating its severity in various clinical settings.34 It was selected because, in addition to supporting delirium identification, it provides valuable information about the degree of symptom intensity,21 which is highly relevant for clinical decision-making and evaluating the effectiveness of interventions.35 Additionally, it has been evaluated in people with dementia in different settings.2136,38 However, it requires specific knowledge and clinical experience to apply.21 Thus far, research on the inter-rater reliability of the DRS-R-98 has focused mainly on acute clinical settings, with data from nursing home residents limited to a small and non-representative subsample.21 37 The German translated version of the DRS-R-98 is freely available at https://www.unispital-basel.ch/dam/jcr:a0a90e1f-53d6-49f6-83d7-3cc06965b800/medizinische-direktion_praxisentwicklung-DRS-R-98.pdf. The psychometric quality of the German translation of the tool in the nursing home setting when used by nurses has not been tested.

Overall, the three tools have shown utility in older patients, including people with dementia.1921 24 28 29 31 34 36,45 However, the current body of research reveals limitations regarding the robustness of these tools, particularly among residents with moderate to severe dementia in nursing homes and when they are used by nurses.

Study objective

This study aims to evaluate the comprehensibility, as an aspect of content validity, practicality and inter-rater reliability of the 4AT, 4-DSD and DRS-R-98 tools for use by nurses in nursing homes among residents with dementia. This will be performed in accordance with the standards of the COSMIN Framework.33 46 The secondary aim of the study is to develop a manual for each of the tools to improve the quality of screening and assessment by nurses.

This study serves as a preparatory study for a prospective cohort study on the occurrence and course of delirium in people with dementia in nursing homes (DeliDem-NH, funded by the Federal Ministry of Research, Technology and Space (Bundesministerium für Forschung, Technologie und Raumfahrt, BMFTR); funding reference: 01GY2502).

Methods and analysis

Study design

The study consists of three phases, which are depicted in figure 1. The study is planned to run from July 2025 to April 2026. In the first phase, the comprehensibility and practicality of the tools will be evaluated using cognitive interviews. Based on the interview findings, user manuals for each of the three instruments will be developed in Phase two and finalised through expert consensus. In the third phase, inter-rater reliability and measurement error will be assessed. This will be based on the assessment of 70 residents in six nursing homes using the three tools.

Study design and timeline.

Tools 4AT, 4-DSD and DRS-R-98

The 4AT and 4-DSD are referred to as episodic tests, which are intended to be used only when there is a justified suspicion of delirium (https://www.the4at.com/userguide).

4AT

The 4AT test is a brief (<2 min) and easy-to-use tool for delirium detection that does not require special training to use and can be applied to individuals with visual or auditory impairments.19 It was developed by Bellelli et al for use in the clinical setting, but it has since been validated for a variety of contexts, including emergency or acute care, care homes, home settings, hospice settings, preoperative settings and care transitions.1923 27,29 31 40 41 45 47 The tool is intended for use by healthcare professionals, including nurses and physicians. It is available in numerous languages (https://www.the4at.com/userguide).

The tool consists of the following four items: (1) Alertness, (2) Orientation (abbreviated mental test-4), (3) Attention (month-backwards test) and (4) Acute or fluctuating symptoms. The total score is the sum of the item values (Item 1: 0–4; Item 2: 0–2; Item 3: 0–2; Item 4: 0–4). If one item value is missing, the value of the total score is defined as missing. The maximum score of the 4AT is 12 points. A score of four or more indicates possible delirium.

4-DSD

The 4-DSD was developed by Morandi et al for use in acute and rehabilitation hospital settings.20 It is based on the 4AT and aims to provide a brief (<3 min), reliable assessment of the presence of delirium in people with moderate and severe dementia.20 This target group, in particular, often has limited communication skills, which makes it difficult to use the 4AT. The 4-DSD is, therefore, based on methods that do not rely on verbal communication.20 The tool is intended for use by healthcare professionals. The 4-DSD and instructions for its use are publicly available in English and Italian at https://www.the4at.com/4-dsd.

The tool consists of four items: (1) Alertness, (2) Altered brain function, (3) Attention and (4) Acute change or fluctuation in mental status.20 Item 1, Alertness, is assessed using the Modified Richmond Agitation and Sedation Scale (mRASS).51 The mRASS is available in German and is freely available at (https://www.unispital-basel.ch/dam/jcr:c9c125a2-b378-4112-ab57-83f2eb55ca85/konzeptDelirmanagement_Vollversion.pdf). The maximum score of the 4-DSD is 12 points, with four or more points indicating delirium.20 The total score is the sum of the individual values (Item 1: 0–1; Item 2: 0–3; Item 3: 0–6; Item 4: 0–2). If one item value is missing, the value of the total score is defined as missing (no imputations).

DRS-R-98

The DRS-R-98 was developed by Trzepacz et al for use in medical-surgical, psychiatric, rehabilitation and nursing home settings.21 The tool can be completed by psychiatrists, other physicians, nurses and psychologists with adequate clinical training in evaluating psychiatric phenomenology.21 The estimated time to rate the items is 20–30 min for scoring and up to 1 hour for gathering the information needed to rate the other items (https://deliriumnetwork.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/12/DRS-R-98.pdf).

The tool consists of 16 items. While three items are related to primary characteristics for the differential diagnosis of delirium (temporal onset of symptoms, fluctuations in symptom severity and physical disorders), the remaining 13 items aim to assess the severity of delirium (sleep–wake cycle disturbance, perceptual disturbances and hallucinations, delusions, lability of affect, language, thought process abnormalities, motor agitation, motor retardation, orientation, attention, short-term memory, long-term memory and visuospatial ability).21 The maximum total score is 46 points, which is derived by summing the individual item scores (no imputation of missing items). Within this range, the delirium severity score accounts for a maximum of 39 points, depending on the scoring range per item (0–3 or 0–2/3 for diagnostic items).21 A delirium diagnosis is indicated when the total score is 14.5 or higher.38

Evaluation of comprehensibility and practicality

The objective is to investigate the comprehensibility and practicality of the tools when used by nurses and nursing assistants. Additionally, whether the nurses and nursing assistants have sufficient information to support the assessments is evaluated.

Study design

As recommended by the COSMIN guidelines,33 at least seven cognitive interviews with nurses or nursing assistants will be conducted. Additional interviews will follow until inductive thematic saturation is reached, which is defined as the point at which no new relevant information emerges from targeted probing.52

In this study, a nurse is a licensed professional with 3 years of vocational training or a degree from a 3 to 4 year bachelor’s degree programme,53 while a nursing assistant describes staff with 1–2 years of training (depending on regional regulations) who support basic care under supervision by a nurse.54

Eligibility criteria for participants

Participants will be selected to ensure variability (eg, non-native German speakers). The inclusion criteria are participants who care for residents with dementia and have at least 1 year of nursing training.

Data collection procedure

The interviews will be conducted online via Big Blue Button and will be audio recorded and transcribed. They will be carried out by the first author (JCS) using a standardised guide, which is based on Tourangeau’s 4-stage model (1984), the CIRF framework55 and the GESIS Survey Guideline Cognitive Pretesting.56 The think-aloud technique and probing will be used to explore participants’ understanding, response processes and decision-making for each item.57 58 Sociodemographic data of the participants will be collected through LimeSurvey, with work experience categorised into 5-year intervals and age grouped into 10-year intervals.

Interview guide

For the interview guide, probing questions were developed for each item and addressed various cognitive aspects. To assess the comprehensibility and determine whether the interviewees could be helpful in assessing the other items, they will be asked to describe situations in which they would assess the items. This allowed for testing whether their understanding aligns with the intended procedure. The understanding of the individual items will also be evaluated through targeted questioning, for example, whether certain technical terms are known. Additionally, participants will be asked whether the requested observations are documented and to what extent the cognitive tests could practically be applied to residents. The probing questions are listed in the appendix (online supplemental appendix 2). Minor adaptations of the interview guide during data collection will be incorporated.

Data management

Sociodemographic data from the participants will be exported in a pseudonymised form. Audio recordings will be transcribed, identifying details will be removed during transcription, and audio files will be deleted once the transcripts are finalised. The resulting transcripts will be fully anonymised and stored in secure project folders accessible only to the project team.

Data analysis

The biographical data will be analysed descriptively using Microsoft Excel to describe the sample. JCS and VM will independently analyse the transcripts using the Question Appraisal System 2004,59 identifying and categorising issues related to comprehension, practicality and item assessment.

Development and consensus of manuals for the assessment of delirium

For each instrument, a manual will be developed based on the results of the cognitive interviews and the existing manuals of the respective tools. These manuals will support the application of the tools and improve their psychometric quality. They will include illustrative situations linked to specific items and standardised explanations for technical terms, and descriptions of score calculations. The aim is to improve the understanding among the participating nursing staff. A consensus process with experts from acute and long-term settings will guide its development. The expert panel will consist of at least four participants. They will be recruited based on availability through the contacts of the Division of Nursing Science at the University of Oldenburg. Inclusion criteria for the experts are professional experience in the medical or nursing care of people with dementia. In addition, at least half of the panel members will be required to have a background in long-term care. The consensus process will be conducted via email. Each expert will be provided with the material and asked to give their feedback independently. Feedback will then be collated and summarised by the research team, and a second round of review will be conducted if necessary to resolve any discrepancies and reach agreement among the panel members.

Measurement of inter-rater reliability and measurement error

In a further step, inter-rater reliability and, subsequently, measurement error will be assessed. The testing procedures differ between the 4AT/4-DSD and DRS-R-98. The 4AT and the 4-DSD tools will be tested by three raters, and the DRS-R-98 will be tested by two of the three raters. Therefore, the testing procedures are reported for the 4AT/4-DSD and the DRS-R-98 separately (figures2 3).

Study design and data collection procedure

For the 4AT and 4-DSD, each resident will be assessed independently by three raters: two external raters (JCS and VM), who are both researchers and master’s level nurses with experience in dementia and delirium care, and one nurse employed by the nursing home (the following internal nurse rater). Additionally, a nursing assistant or nurse who is familiar with the resident will assist during the assessment by supporting the administration of cognitive tests and acting as an informant, providing relevant background information. The external raters are nurses with master’s degrees who have clinical experience in dementia and/or delirium care.

All assessments will be conducted independently, in parallel and under blinded conditions, with no interaction among the raters. Raters are strictly prohibited from communicating with one another regarding their assessments of the resident during and after the evaluation process. Assessments will be documented on paper using pseudonyms, sealed and transported to the study centre by the external raters. Data entry will be performed by a blinded student assistant.

The 4AT and the 4-DSD include cognitive tests requiring active resident participation. In addition to cognitive testing, both tools contain elements requiring clinical judgement on the basis of longitudinal observation (eg, Question 4 of the 4AT: fluctuating symptoms; Question 2 of the 4-DSD: altered brain function; and Question 4 of the 4-DSD: acute change or fluctuation in mental status). These items are assessed via informational interviews supplemented as needed by nursing documentation. To avoid role conflict, the internal rater informants are different people, typically nursing or auxiliary staff. The order of tool administration (4AT or 4-DSD) is randomised. A schematic overview of the data collection procedure is presented in figure 2.

After the completion of the 4AT and 4-DSD, all the residents will additionally be assessed with the DRS-R-98. Of the 16 DRS-R-98 items, six normally require direct interaction with the residents. However, for two of these items, the previous 4AT and 4-DSD assessments will be used. Consequently, only four items will require additional direct cognitive testing of the residents. This approach minimises resident burden while preserving the completeness of the assessment.

To obtain all the remaining item information, the external raters will conduct an interview with the informant and review the nursing documentation of the previous 2 days. The interview will be led by one of the external raters, assigned randomly via a randomisation app (zufallsgenerator.app). Both external raters will score independently and be blinded to each other.

The DRS-R-98 will be applied solely by the external raters, as it requires clinical training that is not guaranteed by the nursing home staff.

Clinical training encompasses simulation training and participation in teaching visits and supervised testing. The simulation training will be conducted at the simulation centre of the University of Oldenburg, Faculty of Medicine and Health Sciences. Teaching visits and supervised testing will be conducted at the university hospital in Oldenburg on the geriatric ward. Testing will be performed for geriatric patients with dementia.

The simultaneous assessment of the external and internal raters is based on the fluctuating nature of delirium. Symptoms of delirium can change rapidly within a few hours. To ensure that the test conditions are kept constant, it was decided to proceed with this approach. A maximum of 45 min is allocated for the assessment with the three tools. If needed and if the resident provides continued consent, the session may be extended by 15 min. If the assessment is not completed within an hour, it will be terminated because of the burden on the resident (missing value of the rating).

The internal nurse rater will be trained on the 4AT and 4-DSD instruments and the scenario of a possible suspicion of delirium in a resident. If the internal nurse rater’s assessment of the 4AT or 4-DSD is positive and a suspicion of delirium exists, the nurse will initiate measures after the assessment, while remaining blinded to the results of the external raters.

Setting and eligibility criteria for participants

The assessments will take place in six nursing homes in North Rhine-Westphalia, Germany. Five of them belong to a single long-term care provider. The nursing homes were recruited as a convenience sample.

The internal nurse raters will be required to have completed 3 years of nursing training (European Qualifications Framework level 4) and the training on the instruments. The selection will be made by the respective unit managers, based on staff availability. Informants will need to have known the resident for at least 3 months.

The inclusion criteria for the residents to be assessed are to have a documented dementia diagnosis, a positive result on the Dementia Screening Scale (score >2), and a legal representative.60 The exclusion criteria for the study are aphasia, deafness, inability to communicate in German or a pre-terminal condition. A pre-terminal condition is defined using the surprise question, which indicates whether the nursing staff would be surprised if the resident died within the next 4 weeks.61 Residents with dementia will be recruited using a convenience sample. To do so, the nurses in the nursing homes will initiate the first contact with the residents and their legal representatives.

At baseline, additional resident characteristics, including age in 5-year intervals, sex and, if available in the medical records, the type of dementia will be recorded.

Sample size calculation

To assess inter-rater reliability, a total of 70 individuals with dementia will be recruited at the two study sites. This number is chosen in accordance with the recommendations of Bujang et al and will be implemented as follows.62

Sample size calculation for kappa values for the reliability of individual items of the tools

To assess the reliability of individual items, exact agreement will be examined using (weighted) kappa statistics, along with the corresponding tests and CIs. Assuming a significance level of 5% and a power of at least 80% to detect a difference from kappa=0.0 to kappa=0.4, a sample size of 47 individuals is needed when the item under investigation has at least two categories and data from at least two raters are analysed. Assuming a dropout rate of 20%, at least 60 residents would be required; thus, conservatively, 70 residents are planned.

Sample size calculation for the ICCs for the reliability of the total scores of the tools

The three tools result in a continuous sum score. At least two raters will be planned for the investigation of each tool, with three raters for the 4AT and 4-DSD tools. The reliability of the sum scores will be investigated using intraclass correlation coefficients (ICCs) with corresponding tests and CIs. A sample size of at least 22 individuals is required for a significance level of 5% and a power of at least 80% to detect a difference between an ICC (to 0.0) of at least 0.50 with two raters.62 The planned sample size of 70 individuals will result in greater power and a smaller 95% CI (width of 0.2 if ICC=0.7 for n=67 and 3 raters according to63), which will allow for a sufficiently precise estimation of the ICCs. The 95% CIs for the ICCs will be conservatively calculated using nonparametric bootstrapping.

Recruitment

Eligible residents will be identified by the responsible nurses. Recruitment of participants will be non-randomised and based on availability. Participating nursing homes will receive a financial incentive of 15€ per assessment per informant and 30€ per assessment per internal nurse rater to support recruitment and data collection efforts.

Data management

Resident sociodemographic data and delirium assessment results will be collected in pseudonymised form. Delirium assessment results will be documented on paper, sealed in envelopes by the informant and transported by the external raters to the University of Oldenburg. A designated student assistant will open the envelopes, digitise the data into Research Electronic Data Capture, a secure web-based application for data collection and management, and perform plausibility checks. Pseudonymised data will be stored in secure, access-controlled folders.

Data analysis

All data analyses will be performed using statistical software packages (R Version 4.5.2 or SAS Version 9.4 (TS1M9)). The characteristics of the participants and the raters will be described according to their distribution using frequency tables, mean values±SDs, or quantiles. The values of the individual items of the tools will be evaluated descriptively using frequency tables, while the sum scores will be described using mean values±SDs and quartiles.

For the individual items, the proportion of exact agreement will be calculated for pairwise comparisons between testers and retesters with 95% CIs using kappa values. The 95% CIs for kappa will be determined using bootstrapping. For ordinal values, weighted kappa values will be used. If values from three raters are available, all pairwise comparisons will be used, and the mean of the corresponding three numbers of agreements will be formed and divided by the number of ratings. Afterwards, the weighted kappa (Conger’s kappa) will be calculated,64 65 and CIs will be determined using bootstrapping66 instead of parametric CIs because of independence from distribution assumptions which might be more restrictive in low sample sizes.

For the continuous sum scores, ICCs will be calculated using 2-way random effects models with participants and raters as random effects. The corresponding 95% CIs will be determined using non-parametric bootstrapping. As a graphical representation of the pairwise comparison of the continuous sum score values between each pair of raters, Bland‒Altman plots (a scatter plot of the differences against the mean values of the pairs) will be created and discussed.

It is assumed that only a few missing values will occur in the individual items and the sum scores of the tools. Therefore, no imputation of missing values will be planned.

Patient and public involvement

Public involvement in this study is embedded in multiple stages. It begins with cognitive interviews conducted with nurses and nursing assistants to evaluate the comprehensibility and practicality of the tools. Research questions and outcomes were developed jointly with the participating nursing homes, and the practical expertise of the staff was incorporated.

Building on this collaboration, the nursing homes will also play a key role in recruiting residents and staff for interviews and assessments. As the third rater and informant roles are filled by nursing staff from the nursing homes, they are actively involved in the implementation phase of the study. Residents themselves will participate directly through cognitive testing. Nursing home representatives will also contribute to the study design by providing feedback on practicality. They will be regularly updated on study progress. After data analysis, the results will be presented in an accessible format, and internal working groups will be initiated to further address the topic. One representative (ME) will also be included as a co-author.

Data monitoring

As this is an observational study without interventions, a formal data monitoring committee is not needed. No interim analyses or stopping guidelines are planned.

Ethics and dissemination

Research ethics approval

The project, which is described in the study protocol, was approved by the medical ethics committee of the University of Oldenburg (reference number: 2025-093). The study will be conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and relevant national data protection laws. The authors gave permission to use the 4-DSD for translation and validation.

Protocol amendments

Any substantial amendments to the study protocol will be submitted to the responsible ethics committee for approval and communicated to all study sites prior to implementation.

Consent

For the interviews, informed consent will be obtained from the participating nurses or nursing assistants by the author.

For data collection from residents with dementia, signed consent from legal representatives is needed. The scientific staff will contact legal representatives following initial contact by the nurses of the nursing homes. Consent procedures follow the S2k guidelines for the consent of individuals with dementia in medical measures.67 Dementia does not preclude consent per se but must be individually assessed.68 Given that consent capacity requires special medical expertise and cannot be easily assessed, only residents with a legal representative or informal representative will be included. Consent will be obtained in two stages: legal representatives are informed first, and residents are then informed using picture cards adapted to their cognitive abilities.69

Residents’ presumed willingness will be respected throughout the study in line with the principle of ongoing consent.70 71 The scientific team will continuously monitor for any signs of discomfort during data collection. If signs of discomfort or resistance are observed, data collection will be discontinued immediately. Additionally, legal representatives may withdraw consent at any time without providing reasons.

Confidentiality

Personally identifiable data (sociodemographic data of the interviewees, consent forms, resident assessments and sociodemographic data of the residents) will be stored in separate, access-restricted folders accessible only to project staff at the Carl von Ossietzky University Oldenburg. For the interviews, sociodemographic data and signed consent forms from participants will be pseudonymised via LimeSurvey. Transcripts will be anonymised by removing identifying details and deleting audio recordings. For the inter-rater reliability assessment, residents’ personal data and delirium assessments will be pseudonymised and stored in secure folders. Consent forms from legal representatives will be kept separate from the pseudonymised data and assessment results. After data verification, and no later than 3 months after data collection, the ID code list will be deleted to ensure complete anonymisation.

Ancillary and post-trial

No ancillary or post-trial care is planned for the residents, as this is an observational study.

Dissemination

The results of this study will be published as part of a peer-reviewed publication. Additionally, the results of the study will be presented at scientific congresses and practical conferences. A summary of the results will be sent to the cooperating institutions.

Discussion

Expected results

This study will generate evidence on the comprehensibility, practicality and inter-rater reliability of three delirium tools used by nurses in nursing homes for people with dementia. Interview results on comprehensibility and practicality are expected to be more favourable for the 4AT and 4-DSD tools than for the DRS-R-98, as its terminology may be viewed as more complex and less accessible. Compared with the DRS-R-98, which has more items and response options and thus increased variability, the 4AT and 4-DSD are expected to show higher inter-rater agreement. However, the inter-rater reliability of the DRS-R-98 may benefit from its reliance on clinical judgement questions answered by a single informant, providing a shared basis for all the raters.

Limitations

Recruiting residents for inter-rater reliability assessment is considered a major challenge. Nursing home staff must work in cooperation with the research team to identify residents who meet the inclusion criteria. The described procedure may affect the generalisability of the findings, as residents with dementia severe enough to result in aphasia, as well as those without reachable representatives, will not be included.

It is difficult to predict how many cases will have consent withdrawn and assessment discontinued. Reaching the targeted sample size will be difficult if a considerable number of assessments are discontinued.

Another limitation arises from the fact that residents with dementia will be approached using a non-random sampling strategy. Initial contact with residents and their legal representatives will be initiated by the nursing staff in the facilities, which may inadvertently favour those residents who are more accessible or familiar to staff, thereby introducing an additional layer of selection bias.

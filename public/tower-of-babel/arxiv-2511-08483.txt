Let

Weakly regular functions

Let

A regular function

\lim\limits_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle x\to A}}{{x\in X\!\smallsetminus\!A}}}h(x)f(x)=0.

Assume that

.

Since

are both non-singular and

is of codimension 1. Let

be the ideal of

and take for

the generator of
\mathscr{I}_{a}
,

. Then

generates
\mathscr{I}_{x}
for

near

. Let

satisfying

:

is a rational function in

,
f=P/Q
with

,
Q
regular near

. If

,

extends through

(if

Remark 2.2
.

Using the normalization, it is easy to show that weakly regular functions are integral over the ring of the regular functions. The converse is also true thanks to the existence of the universal denominator.

2.3.
Extension of morphisms

2.3.1.
Gaps and holes

Let
X,Y
be algebraic varieties,
A\not\equiv X
a closed subvariety and

f:X\!\smallsetminus\!A\to Y
a morphism. Let

\Gamma(f)=\big\{(x,f(x))\in X\times Y:x\in X\!\smallsetminus\!A\big\}

be the graph of
f
and
\overline{\Gamma(f)}
its closure.

For every subset
S
of
X

f(S):={\rm pr}_{Y}\big((S\times Y)\cap\overline{\Gamma(f)}\big)

is, by definition, the
image
of
S
under
f
. We set
f(x):=f(\{x\})
.

A point
x\in X
is said a
hole
of
f
if
x\notin{\rm pr}_{X}(\overline{\Gamma(f)})
and it is said a
gap
if either
f(x)=\varnothing
or
f(x)\neq\varnothing
but
f(x)
is not complete.

A morphism
f:X\to Y
is said
gapless
if
f(x)
is a non-empty, complete subvariety
of
Y
for every
x\in X
. As it is easily seen,
f
is gapless if
{{\rm pr}_{X}}_{|\overline{\Gamma(f)}}
is a proper morphism. Trivially, gapless implies no hole.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
X,Y
be algebraic varietes,
A\not\equiv X
a closed subvariety and
f:X\!\smallsetminus\!A\to Y
a morphism. If
x\in A
and

there exists a curve

near
x
such that
C\cap A=\{x\}
and
y\in f(C)\cap f(A)
.

Proof.

We may assume that
Y
is complete.
Consider a desingularization
p:\Gamma^{\ast}\to\overline{\Gamma(f)}
of
\overline{\Gamma(f)}
and a point
w^{\ast}\in\Gamma^{\ast}
such that
p(w^{\ast})=(x,y)
. Let
U
be an open neighborhood of
w*
and
C^{\ast}\subset U
be a closed curve through
w^{\ast}
such that

C^{\ast}\cap p^{-1}(\overline{\Gamma(f)}\!\smallsetminus\!\Gamma(f))=w^{\ast}.

Since
{\rm pr}_{X}\circ p
is proper there exists a neighborhood

of
x
in
X
such that
U^{\prime}:=({\rm pr}_{X}\circ p)^{-1}(V)\subset U
and
{\rm pr}_{X}\circ p
is proper from

to

. Then the projection

has the desired properties.
∎

In the sequel we will use the Zariski’s Main Theorem (ZMT) in the following form:

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
X
be a normal variety,
A
a closed subvariety with
{\rm codim}\,A_{x}\geq 1
. Let
f
be a morphism
X\!\smallsetminus\!A\to Y
. Then for a point
x\in A
one of the following instances occurs:

i )

f(x)=\varnothing
;

ii )

f(x)
is a point and then
f
is regular at
x
;

iii )

f(x)
is connected of positive dimension at every point.

As a consequence, the set
{\rm Sing}(f)
of the non-regular points of
f:X\!\smallsetminus\!A\to Z
is the algebraic subset

\big\{x\in X:{{\dim}_{\Bbbk}}\,f(x)>0\big\}.

Proposition 2.5
.

Under the hypothesis of the above theorem if
f
has no hole then

{{\dim}_{\Bbbk}}\,{\rm Sing}(f)_{x}\leq{{\dim}_{\Bbbk}}\,X-2

for every
x\in X
.

Proof.

We may assume that
Y
is complete. Let
n={{\dim}_{\Bbbk}}\,X
and

r(x,y)=n-{{\dim}_{\Bbbk}}\,f(x)_{y}

be the rank of the regular map
{\rm pr}_{X}:\Gamma(f)\to X
.
Since
f
has no hole

\displaystyle{\rm Sing}(f)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big\{x\in X:{{\dim}_{\Bbbk}}\,f(x)>0\big\}=

\displaystyle{\rm pr}_{X}\big\{(x,y)\in X\times Y:r(x,y)<n\big\}

S:=\big\{(x,y)\in X\times Y:r(x,y)<n\big\}

is an algebraic set and
{{\rm pr}^{-1}_{X}(x)}\hskip-5.69054pt\subset\hskip-5.69054ptS
for every
x\in{\rm Sing}(f)
. Moreover, since
{{\dim}_{\Bbbk}}\,f(x)_{y}\geq 1
, for every
(x,y)\in S
the rank of
{\rm pr}_{X}|S
at a point
(x,y)
is

r_{S}(x,y)={{\dim}_{\Bbbk}}\,S_{(x,y)}-{{\dim}_{\Bbbk}}\,f(x)_{y}\leq n-2.

This implies

{{\dim}_{\Bbbk}}\,{\rm Sing}(f)_{x}\leq{{\dim}_{\Bbbk}}\,X-2.

∎

2.3.2.
Extension

An algebraic variety
X
is said to be
\rm K
-
complete
if for a given point
y\in X
there are finitelely many regular functions
f_{1},\ldots,f_{m}\in\mathcal{O}(X)
such that
y
is an isolated point
of

\{x\in X:f_{j}(x)=f_{j}(y),1\leq j\leq m\}.

An affine variety is
\rm K
-complete.

Theorem 2.6
.

Let
X,Y
be algebraic varieties with
X
normal. Let
A\subset X
be a subvariety with
{\rm codim}\,A_{x}\geq 2
, for every
x\in A
and
f:X\!\smallsetminus\!A\to Y
a morphism. If
Y
is affine or
\rm K
-complete and
f
is without holes, then
f
extends to a morphism
X\to Y
.

Proof.

If
Y
is affine, then
f=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{n})
,
f_{j}\in\mathcal{O}(X)
,
1\leq j\leq n
. Since
X
is normal the thesis is a consequence of the extension theorem for regular functions.
Assume that
Y
is
\rm K
-complete and that
f
has no hole. Let
x_{0}\in A
. Then
\Sigma_{f}(x_{0})\not\equiv\varnothing
. Let
y_{0}\in\Sigma_{f}(x_{0})
and
h
be a regular function on
Y
; then, since
X
is normal,
h\circ f
extends on all of
X
by a regular function
g
. By Lemma
2.3
there is a net
\{x_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
with the properties
x_{\lambda}\to x_{0}
and
f(x_{\lambda})\to y_{0}
. Then
g(x_{0})=h(y_{0})
and consequently

\Sigma_{f}(x_{0})\subseteq\bigcap\limits_{h\in\mathcal{O}(Y)}\big\{y\in Y:h(y)=g(x_{0})=h(y_{0})\big\}=\{y_{0}\}

since
Y
is
\rm K
-complete. Thus for every
x_{0}\in A
,
\Sigma_{f}(x_{0})
is a point. Then, ZMT implies that the map
x\mapsto\Sigma_{f}(x)
is the desired extension of
f
.
∎

Let
X
be a Stein space,
U\subset X
an open subset. A long standing problem in complex analysis, the so called
Levi problem
, asks the following: if
U
is locally Stein is then
U
a Stein space?

The Levi problem has been completely solved for manifolds of arbitrary dimension and for singular spaces of dimension 2 (
[
AnNa
]
).

The algebraic analogue of the Levi problem, merely obtained replacing “Steineness” by “affineness” has an immediate positive answer because then the embedding
U\hookrightarrow X
is an affine morphism and consequently
U
is affine (cfr.
[
Ha2
, p. 128]
).
The
algebraic Levi problem
could then be formulated as follows: is a locally A-convex (respectively strongly
\mathcal{O}
-convex, weakly-
\mathcal{O}
-convex) open subset
U
of an A-convex (respectively strongly
\mathcal{O}
-convex, weakly-
\mathcal{O}
-convex,) algebraic variety
X
an A-convex(respectively strongly
\mathcal{O}
-convex, weakly-
\mathcal{O}
-convex) algebraic variety?

4.2.
Concavity

The notion of concavity concerns the algebraic varieties
X

\geq
2.

Such a variety

X\hookrightarrow\overline{X}
such that

An affine variety is neither strongly
\mathcal{O}

\geq 2

\mathcal{O}
-concave.

Theorem 4.11
.

Let

be a normal strongly
\mathcal{O}
-concave variety of pure dimension

. Under the condition of the last proposition

\Bbbk
-vector space.

Proof.

Let

be a normal completion of
X
and
U

{\rm b}_{\overline{X}}X
.

\mathcal{F}
extends to
\overline{X}
by a torsion free coherent sheaf
\overline{\mathcal{F}}
. Indeed, by

4.2.1.
d
-concavity.

A more geometric definition of concavity is based on the notion of “envelope”. Let
{\mathcal{C}}^{d}_{X}
be the set of all connected,
complete and
d
-dimensional algebraic subvarieties of an algebraic variety
X
. Given a point
x\in X
, denote
{\mathcal{C}}^{d}_{X,x}
the
subset of those elements of
{\mathcal{C}}^{d}_{X}
passing through
x
. Let
K
be a subset of

X
. We define the
{\mathcal{C}}^{d}_{X}\!-\!envelope
of
K
in
X
by

{\rm Env}l_{d}(K)=\{x\in X:Z\cap K\not\equiv\varnothing\>\forall Z\in{\mathcal{C}}^{d}_{X,x}\}.

An algebraic variety
X
is said to be
d
-
concave
if there exists a
complete
(n-d)
-dimensional subvariety
Y
such that
{\rm Env}_{d}(Y)
has a nonempty
interior part.

A complete variety is
d
-concave for every
d\geq 0
. An affine variety of positive dimension is never
d
-concave.

Here are some natural questions

1 )

Is the complement
{\mathbb{C}}{{\mathbb{P}}}^{\,n}\!\smallsetminus\!Y_{d}
, of a subvariety
Y_{d}
of pure
dimension
d
,
(d+1)
-concave ?

2 )

Let
X
be a complete algebraic variety of dimension
n
and
Y_{d}
a subvariety
of pure dimension
d
. Assume that
X\!\smallsetminus\!Y_{d}
is
q
-concave with
q<d+1
. Is then

X
contractible along
Y_{d}
in the category of the algebraic spaces?

3 )

Let
X
be a
1
-concave algebraic variety and
\overline{X}
one completion of
X
.
Does a neighborhood of
\overline{X}\!\smallsetminus\!X
have some convexity?

5.
Convex and concave line bundles

5.1.
Convex line bundles

Let
{\sf L}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi}}{{\rightarrow}}X
be a line bundle on the algebraic variety
X
,
\mathcal{O}(\sf L)
the sheaf of the germs of regular sections of
\sf L
and
{\sf L}^{\ast}
the dual of
\sf L
. We denote
\overline{\sf L}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\overline{\pi}}}{{\rightarrow}}X
the projective closure
{\mathbb{P}}({\sf L}\oplus\Bbbk)
of
{\sf L}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi}}{{\rightarrow}}X
.
We say that
{\sf L}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi}}{{\rightarrow}}X
is

strongly
\mathcal{O}
-convex along the fibres
if for every point
z\in\overline{\sf L}\!\smallsetminus\!{\sf L}
and a net
\sigma=(z_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in I}
on
{\sf L}
converging to
z
there exists a regular function
f:{\sf L}\to\Bbbk
which is divergent on
\sigma
. Observe that, if
X
is complete and
{\sf L}
is strongly
\mathcal{O}
-convex along the fibres then (the total space of the line bundle)
{\sf L}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi}}{{\rightarrow}}X
is strongly
\mathcal{O}
-convex variety.

More generally

Theorem 5.1
.

Let

be strongly
\mathcal{O}
-convex variety and

a line bundle which is strongly
\mathcal{O}
-convex along the fibres. Then,
{\sf L}
is a strongly
\mathcal{O}
-convex variety.

Now assume that

is complete and

of

If

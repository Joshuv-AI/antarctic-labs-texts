Local solubility of ternary cubic forms
Golo Wolff

Abstract
We consider cubic forms ϕ a , b ​ ( x , y , z ) = a ​ x 3 + b ​ y 3 − z 3 \phi_{a,b}(x,y,z)=ax^{3}+by^{3}-z^{3} with coefficients a , b ∈ Z a,b\in\mathbb{Z} . We give an asymptotic formula for how many of these forms are locally soluble everywhere, i.e. we give an asymptotic formula for the number of pairs of integers ( a , b ) (a,b) that satisfy 1 ≤ a ≤ A 1\leq a\leq A , 1 ≤ b ≤ B 1\leq b\leq B and some mild conditions, such that ϕ a , b \phi_{a,b} has a non-zero solution in Q p \mathbb{Q}_{p} for all primes p p .



1
Introduction

The idea for this paper is based on a paper
[
2
]
by John Friedlander and Henryk Iwaniec. In their paper the authors give an asymptotic formula for the number of ternary quadratic forms
ax^{2}+by^{2}-z^{2}
with integer coefficients
1\leq a\leq A
,
1\leq b\leq B
that are locally soluble everywhere and thus have a non-zero rational solution due to the Minkowski local-global principle for indefinite quadratic forms. They restrict to counting pairs
(a,b)
which are square-free, coprime and odd. We intend to modify their argument and prove a similar result in the cubic case.

Our setup is the following. Let
\phi_{a,b}(x,y,z)=ax^{3}+by^{3}-z^{3}
have coefficients
a,b\in\mathbb{Z}
and let
N(A,B)
denote the number of integer pairs
(a,b)
that satisfy the following conditions:

•

1\leq a\leq A
,
1\leq b\leq B
,

•

\phi_{a,b}
has a non-zero solution locally everywhere,

•

a,b
are square-free, coprime and not divisible by three.

Then we get the following asymptotic.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
0<\delta<1
. For
A,B\geq\exp((\log AB)^{\delta})
we have

N(A,B)=c_{2}\cdot\frac{AB}{(\log A)^{\frac{1}{3}}(\log B)^{\frac{1}{3}}}\left(1+\mathcal{O}\bigg(\frac{1}{\log A}+\frac{1}{\log B}\bigg)\right),

where
c_{2}
is the constant

c_{2}=\Big(\Gamma\Big(\frac{2}{3}\Big)\Big)^{-2}\prod_{p}\left(1+\frac{2}{3^{\omega_{1}(p)}p}\right)\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)^{\frac{4}{3}}.

For the cubic case of this paper we have no analogue to the local-global principle used by Friedlander and Iwaniec in the quadratic case. With the tools of our paper it is not possible to infer that
\phi_{a,b}
has a rational solution, even if it is locally soluble everywhere. As an immediate consequence of Theorem
1.1
, we only achieve an upper bound on the number of forms that have a global solution.

Corollary 1.2
.

Let
0<\delta<1
and
A,B\geq\exp((\log AB)^{\delta})
. Then the number of cubic forms
\phi_{a,b}
that have a global solution is bounded from above by

c_{3}\cdot\frac{AB}{(\log A)^{\frac{1}{3}}(\log B)^{\frac{1}{3}}}\left(1+\frac{1}{\log A}+\frac{1}{\log B}\right),

if we assume the same restrictions on the coefficients
a,b
as in the definition of
N(A,B)
. The leading constant
c_{3}
may depend on
\delta
.

Furthermore

\limsup_{A,B\rightarrow\infty}\;N(A,B)\bigg(\frac{AB}{(\log A)^{\frac{1}{3}}(\log B)^{\frac{1}{3}}}\bigg)^{-1}\leq c_{2}.

Note that the condition
A,B\geq\exp((\log AB)^{\delta})
intuitively means that
A,B
are not extremely different in size. It implies, for example, that
(\log B)^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{\delta}}}\geq\log A\geq(\log B)^{\delta}
and it is mainly required for the way that we have bounded the error terms in the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

3
Proof of the theorem

The proof of the theorem will need four lemmas. Two of the lemmas detect solutions of
\phi_{a,b}
in
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
, the other two lemmas are character oscillation results.

3.1
Lemmas to detect solutions

The following two lemmas answer the question how to detect a solution of
\phi_{a,b}
in
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
. The first lemma implies that for all primes
p\nmid ab
,
\phi_{a,b}
has a solution in
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
, except for the special case
p=3
. The second lemma states that for primes
p\mid ab
we can express the solubility of
\phi_{a,b}
in
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
through cubic characters.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
(a,b)
be any pair of integers with
3\nmid ab
. Then
\phi_{a,b}
has a non-zero solution

i)

in
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
if
p\nmid ab
and
p\neq 3
,

ii)

in
\mathbb{Q}_{3}
if and only if
(a,b)
is congruent modulo 27 to an “admissible pair” from the “list of admissible pairs” compiled in the appendix
A
.

Proof of Lemma
3.1
.

We first prove statement
i)
for primes
p\geq 11
and afterwards for the smaller primes. The strategy is to show that
\phi_{a,b}
has a solution in
\mathbb{F}_{p}
that can be lifted to a solution in
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
with Hensel’s lemma
[
1
, Theorem 2.1]
.

For
p\nmid ab
and
p\geq 11
,
\phi_{a,b}
is an non-singular cubic curve of genus 1, so we may use the Hasse-Weil bound
[
7
]
to infer that there are at least 4 projective solutions over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
. For the cubic
\phi_{a,b}
this implies that there is at least one solution
(x_{0},y_{0},z_{0})\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{3}
with
z_{0}\neq 0
. Pick a lift of this triple
(\tilde{x},\tilde{y},\tilde{z})\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}
and define
f(z)\coloneqq\phi_{a,b}(\tilde{x},\tilde{y},z)=a\tilde{x}^{3}+b\tilde{y}^{3}-z^{3}\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}[z]
. Note that by definition
f(z)
reduces to
\phi_{a,b}(x_{0},y_{0},z)
in
\mathbb{F}_{p}
, so we have
z_{0}\not\equiv 0
mod
p
,
f(z_{0})\equiv 0
mod
p
and
f^{\prime}(z_{0})=-3z_{0}^{2}\not\equiv 0
mod
p
. Thus we may apply Hensel’s lemma
[
1
, Thm 2.1]
to lift
z_{0}
to a non-zero solution of
f(z)=\phi_{a,b}(\tilde{x},\tilde{y},z)
in
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
.

If
p\nmid ab
and
p=2,5,7
, proving that
\phi_{a,b}
has a solution in
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
is similar. Instead of the Hasse-Weil bound, we use a small computer program (code in Appendix
A
) to check all possible values of
x,y,z\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
until we find a solution of
\phi_{a,b}
in
\mathbb{F}_{p}
. Indeed for all pairs
(a,b)\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{2}
we find a solution of
\phi_{a,b}
with at least one non-zero coordinate and it can then be lifted to a solution in
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
with Hensel’s lemma similarly to the previous case.

We now turn to statement
ii)
. For a solution in
\mathbb{Q}_{3}
we cannot apply the same version of Hensel’s lemma as in the previous cases, as the derivative of
\phi_{a,b}
is zero modulo 3. We use a stronger version of Hensel’s lemma for this case and will prove the following:
\phi_{a,b}
has a solution in
\mathbb{Z}_{3}
if and only if
\phi_{a,b}
has a solution mod 27 with at least one coordinate not congruent to 0 modulo 3. We will call the pairs
(a,b)
such that
\phi_{a,b}
has a solution mod 27 “admissible pairs”.

Suppose
\phi_{a,b}
has a solution
(x_{0},y_{0},z_{0})
mod 27 with at least one coordinate that is not congruent to 0 mod 3. Assume that
z_{0}\not\equiv 0
mod 3, in the other cases the argument works analogously. Lift the triple
(x_{0},y_{0},z_{0})
to
(\tilde{x},\tilde{y},\tilde{z})\in\mathbb{Z}_{3}
and define
f(z)\coloneqq\phi_{a,b}(\tilde{x},\tilde{y},z)=a\tilde{x}^{3}+b\tilde{y}^{3}-z^{3}\in\mathbb{Z}_{3}[z]
. Then
z_{0}
is a solution mod 27 of
f(z)\equiv ax_{0}^{3}+by_{0}^{3}-z^{3}\bmod 27
(and also mod 3). Using these properties of
z_{0}
, we obtain
|f(z_{0})|_{3}\leq\frac{1}{27}<\frac{1}{9}=|3z_{0}|_{3}^{2}=|f^{\prime}(z_{0})|_{3}^{2}
. Thus we may apply a stronger version of Hensel’s lemma
[
1
, Thm 4.1]
to lift the solution
z_{0}
from
\mathbb{F}_{3}
to a solution of
f(z)
in
\mathbb{Z}_{3}
.

Conversely, if
\phi_{a,b}
has a solution in
\mathbb{Z}_{3}
we may scale it by a power of 3 such that all coordinates have non-negative valuation but at least one them has valuation 0. The scaled triple remains a solution of
\phi_{a,b}
because
\phi_{a,b}
is homogeneous. The triple then reduces to a solution of
\phi_{a,b}
mod 27 and the coordinate with valuation 0 is not congruent to 0 mod 3.
∎

In the appendix
A
we have compiled a “list of admissible pairs”.
\phi_{a,b}
has a solution mod 27 with one coordinate not congruent to 0 mod 3 if and only if
(a,b)
is congruent modulo 27 to one of the pairs given in that list. The list was compiled with a computer program that simply iterates through all possible values of
x,y,z
mod 27 until it finds a solution to
\phi_{a,b}(x,y,z)=ax^{3}+by^{3}-z^{3}
that meets the requirement.

Lemma 3.2
.

i)

Let
(a,b)
be a pair of coprime, square-free integers with
3\nmid ab
.

For all primes
p\equiv 2
mod 3 with
p\mid ab
,
\phi_{a,b}
has a solution in
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
.

ii)

For all primes
p\equiv 1
mod 3 we can construct a cubic character
\chi_{p}
of modulus

that satisfies the following:

Let
(a,b)
be a pair of coprime, square-free integers with
3\nmid ab
. If
p\mid a
, we have

\frac{1}{3}(1+\chi_{p}(b)+\chi_{p}^{2}(b))=\begin{cases}1&\text{if }\phi_{a,b}\text{ has a non-zero solution in }\mathbb{Q}_{p}\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Analogously if
p\mid b
,

\frac{1}{3}(1+\chi_{p}(a)+\chi_{p}^{2}(a))=\begin{cases}1&\text{if }\phi_{a,b}\text{ has a non-zero solution in }\mathbb{Q}_{p}\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Proof of Lemma
3.2
.

Let
(a,b)
be a pair of coprime, square-free integers with
3\nmid ab
. Assume for this proof that
p\mid a
and thus by co-primality that
p\nmid b
. The analogue argument works for
p\mid b
,
p\nmid a
. We will first show that
\phi_{a,b}
has a solution
(x_{1},y_{1},z_{1})
in
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
if and only if
\phi_{a,b}\equiv by^{3}-z^{3}
mod
p
has a solution
(x_{0},y_{0},z_{0})
in
\mathbb{F}_{p}
that is not one of the trivial solutions
(x_{0},0,0)
. Afterwards we will give criteria for the existence of a non-trivial solution mod
p
and construct the cubic character
\chi_{p}
.

If
\phi_{a,b}
has a solution
(x_{1},y_{1},z_{1})
in
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
, we can assume that - after scaling by a power of
p
- the valuations of
x_{1},y_{1},z_{1}
are non-negative but at least one valuation is zero. Furthermore comparing the valuations of the terms on both sides of the equation
ax_{1}^{3}+by_{1}^{3}-z_{1}^{3}=0
shows that at least one of

and
z_{1}
need to have valuation zero. Thus
(x_{1},y_{1},z_{1})
reduces to a non-trivial solution of
\phi_{a,b}\equiv by^{3}-z^{3}
mod
p
.

Conversely if
\phi_{a,b}\equiv by^{3}-z^{3}
mod
p
has a solution
(x_{0},y_{0},z_{0})
in
\mathbb{F}_{p}
with
y_{0}\neq 0
or
z_{0}\neq 0

\mathbb{Z}_{p}
with Hensel’s Lemma
[
1

, as in the proof of
i)
in the previous lemma.

In
\mathbb{F}_{p}
,
\phi_{a,b}(x,y,z)\equiv by^{3}-z^{3}
has a non-trivial solution (a solution that is not of the form
(x_{0},0,0)
)
\iff

z^{3}=by^{3}
has a solution in
\mathbb{F}_{p}
with
z\neq 0

\iff

is a cube in
\mathbb{F}_{p}
. For primes
p\equiv 2
mod 3 this is always the case because for these primes cubing is an isomorphism of
\mathbb{F}_{p}
. This proves
i)
. For primes
p\equiv 1
mod 3 we will construct a cubic character
\chi_{p}
which satisfies

\begin{cases}\chi_{p}(b)=1,&\text{if $b$ is a cube mod $p$}\\
\chi_{p}(b)\in\{\zeta_{3},\zeta_{3}^{2}\},&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

(3.1)

Then
\frac{1}{3}(1+\chi_{p}(b)+\chi_{p}^{2}(b))
is the indicator function of “
b
is a cube modulo
p
”, i.e. of “
\phi_{a,b}
has a solution in
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
”.

For primes
p\equiv 1
mod 3 there are exactly two characters that satisfy the conditions in (
3.1
) and they can be constructed as follows. The multiplicative group
G\coloneqq\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\ast}
is a cyclic group of order
3k
and thus has a subgroup
H
of index 3 (if
g
generates
G
, then
g^{3}
is a generator of
H
). The coset
1\cdot H
in
\nicefrac{{G}}{{H}}
contains all cubes modulo
p
and thus we may define a character that maps elements of
H
to
1\in\mathbb{C}
and the elements of the other two cosets to
\zeta_{3}
and
\zeta_{3}^{2}
respectively. The two characters obtained this way are of order 3, conductor
p
and complex conjugates of each other.
∎

3.2
Lemmas on sums of characters

As just shown in the proof above, there are precisely two characters for each prime
p\equiv 1
mod 3 that satisfy property
ii)
of Lemma
3.2
. We now make a choice among these two for the rest of this paper.

Any prime
p\equiv 1\bmod 3
splits completely in
\mathbb{Q}[\zeta_{3}]=\mathbb{Q}[\frac{-1+\sqrt{-3}}{2}]=\mathbb{Q}[\sqrt{-3}]
, i.e.
p=\pi_{p}\overline{\pi_{p}}
with
\pi_{p}\in\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_{3}]
prime and
\text{im}(\pi_{p})>0
. Note that the 3rd power residue symbols

\left(\frac{\cdot}{\pi_{p}}\right)_{\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_{3}],3}\text{ and }\left(\frac{\cdot}{\overline{\pi_{p}}}\right)_{\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_{3}],3}

are cubic characters when interpreted as a function in the top argument and restricted to the rational integers. Furthermore these cubic characters have modulus
p
, map integers that are cubes modulo
p
to 1 and are distinct. We conclude that on the rational integers, these two power residue symbols correspond to the two possible choices for
\chi_{p}
above.

Definition 3.3
.

For rational primes
p\equiv 1\bmod 3
define
\chi_{p}
to be the cubic character

\chi_{p}(\cdot)\coloneqq\left(\frac{\cdot}{\pi_{p}}\right)_{\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_{3}],3}:\mathbb{Z}\mapsto\mathbb{C}\;.

Here
\left(\frac{\,\cdot\,}{\,\cdot\,}\right)_{\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_{3}],3}
is the 3rd power residue symbol and
\pi_{p}\in\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_{3}]
is the prime over
p=\pi_{p}\overline{\pi_{p}}
with
\text{im}(\pi_{p})>0
. Note that
\chi_{p}
satisfies
ii)
of Lemma
3.2
.

Multiplicatively extend the above definition to square-free integers whose prime factors are all equivalent to 1 mod 3, i.e. for square-free
d\in\mathbb{Z}
with
p\,|\,d\implies p\equiv 1
mod 3, define

\pi_{d}\coloneqq\prod_{p|d}\pi_{p}\quad\text{ and }\quad\chi_{d}\coloneqq\prod_{p|d}\chi_{p}

in the same way that the power residue symbol can be extended

\chi_{d}(\cdot)=\left(\frac{\cdot}{\pi_{d}}\right)_{\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_{3}],3}\coloneqq\prod_{p|d}\left(\frac{\cdot}{\pi_{p}}\right)_{\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_{3}],3}.

Lastly we ease notation a bit and simply write
\left(\frac{\,\cdot\,}{\,\cdot\,}\right)_{3}
for the cubic power residue symbol.

For the proof of the main theorem we need character oscillation results. The first such result is a “double oscillation result”, derived from Proposition
2.1
.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
s,t\in\{0,1,2\}
but
(s,t)\neq(0,0)
. Then there exist constants
0<\kappa_{1}<1
and
\kappa_{2}>0
such that

\bigg|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1<d\leq A\\
p|d\implies\\
p\equiv 1\bmod 3\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1<e\leq B\\
p|e\implies\\
p\equiv 1\bmod 3\end{subarray}}\alpha_{d}\beta_{e}\chi_{d}(e)^{s}\chi_{e}(d)^{t}\bigg|\ll(A^{-\kappa_{1}}+B^{-\kappa_{1}})AB(\log AB)^{\kappa_{2}}

where
\alpha_{d}
,
\beta_{e}
are non-zero complex numbers with absolute value bounded by one, that may depend on
d
and
e
respectively.

Remark 3.5
.

Intuitively the values of non trivial characters seem to oscillate randomly as one varies the argument, thus summing a wide range of characters over a wide range of arguments (that are not chosen in an overly picky manner) should lead to some cancellation. The above lemma is a result quantifying this intuition for the special case that we need.

Proof of Lemma
3.4
.

As remarked before this result derives from Proposition
2.1
. We replace the Dirichlet characters by the respective power residue symbols.

\chi_{d}(e)^{s}\chi_{e}(d)^{t}=\left(\frac{e}{\pi_{d}}\right)_{3}^{s}\left(\frac{d}{\pi_{e}}\right)_{3}^{t}=\left(\frac{N(\pi_{e})}{\pi_{d}}\right)_{3}^{s}\left(\frac{N(\pi_{d})}{\pi_{e}}\right)_{3}^{t}

Here
N(\cdot)
denotes the norm in
\mathbb{Q}[\zeta_{3}]
. We now choose the correct setup to apply Proposition
2.1
. Let
K=\mathbb{Q}[\zeta_{3}]
and
\mathcal{O}_{K}=\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_{3}]
the ring of integers. Recall that
\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_{3}]
is a PID, in which rational primes equivalent to 1 modulo 3 split, rational primes equivalent to 2 modulo 3 are inert and
3=(1-\zeta_{3})(1-\zeta_{3}^{2})
. As a consequence
N(z)\equiv 1
mod 3 for all primes except
1-\zeta_{3}
, i.e. the 3rd power residue symbol is defined on all elements coprime to 3. Furthermore the ring of integers is a lattice with 6 units,
\{\pm 1,\pm\zeta_{3},\pm\zeta_{3}^{2}\}

,

\displaystyle\gamma(w,z)=\left(\frac{N(z)}{w}\right)_{3}^{s}\left(\frac{N(w)}{z}\right)_{3}^{t}.

We check that

P1) Multiplicativity:
\gamma(w_{1}w_{2},z)=\gamma(w_{1},z)\gamma(w_{2},z)

P2) Periodicity: For

mod

and
z_{1}\equiv z_{2}
mod

mod

\displaystyle\left(\frac{N(z_{1})}{w}\right)_{3}\left(\frac{N(w)}{z_{1}}\right)_{3}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{N(z_{1})}{w}\right)_{3}\left(\frac{uz_{1}}{N(w)}\right)_{3}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{N(z_{2})}{w}\right)_{3}\left(\frac{uz_{2}}{N(w)}\right)_{3}=\left(\frac{N(z_{2})}{w}\right)_{3}\left(\frac{N(w)}{z_{2}}\right)_{3}

and thus
\gamma(w,z_{1})=\gamma(w,z_{2})

is primary. Additionally, the power residue symbol

Furthermore, if

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\xi\bmod MN(w)\\

\displaystyle\xi\mapsto\left(\frac{N(\xi)}{w}\right)_{3}^{s}\left(\frac{N(w)}{\xi}\right)_{3}^{t}=\left(\frac{\xi\bar{\xi}}{w}\right)_{3}^{s}\left(\frac{w\bar{w}}{\xi}\right)_{3}^{t}

(3.2)

is a nontrivial character on the finite abelian group

2.2
. We now choose the correct parameters to apply the theorem to our character sum. Let

some large integer to be specified later and

some real number to be specified later. Define the function

\displaystyle=

\flat\end{subarray}}\frac{\mu^{2}(d_{1}d_{3}a_{2}e_{2}e_{3}b_{2})}{3^{\omega(d_{1}d_{3}e_{2}e_{3})}}\bigg|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}V<d_{2}\leq\frac{A}{d_{1}d_{3}a_{2}}\\
V<e_{1}\leq\frac{B}{e_{2}e_{3}b_{2}}\\
\flat\end{subarray}}\alpha_{d_{2}}\beta_{e_{1}}\chi_{d_{2}}(e_{1})\bigg|

\displaystyle\ll\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d_{1}d_{3}a_{2}\leq A\\
e_{2}e_{3}b_{2}\leq B\\
\flat\end{subarray}}\frac{\mu^{2}(d_{1}d_{3}a_{2}e_{2}e_{3}b_{2})}{3^{\omega(d_{1}d_{3}e_{2}e_{3})}}\frac{A}{d_{1}d_{3}a_{2}}\frac{B}{e_{1}e_{3}b_{2}}\left(\bigg(\frac{A}{d_{1}d_{3}a_{2}}\bigg)^{-\kappa_{1}}+\bigg(\frac{B}{e_{1}e_{3}b_{2}}\bigg)^{-\kappa_{1}}\right)\log(AB)^{\kappa_{2}}

\displaystyle\ll\frac{AB\log(AB)^{\kappa_{2}}}{V^{\kappa_{1}}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d_{1}d_{3}a_{2}\leq A\\
e_{2}e_{3}b_{2}\leq B\\
\flat\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{3^{\omega(d_{1}d_{3}e_{2}e_{3})}}\frac{\mu^{2}(d_{1}d_{3}a_{2}e_{2}e_{3}b_{2})}{d_{1}d_{3}a_{2}e_{1}e_{3}b_{2}},

where
\alpha_{d_{2}}
and
\beta_{e_{1}}
are complex numbers with absolute values
\leq 1
, absorbing the summation condition “admissible pair”, the factor
\frac{1}{3^{\omega(d_{2}e_{1})}}\chi_{d_{2}}(e_{2}e_{3}b_{2})
and the fact that
e_{1},d_{2}
are square-free.

To evaluate the last sum, remember that the remaining summation conditions are
p\mid d_{1}d_{3}e_{2}e_{3}\implies p\equiv 1
mod 3 and
p\mid a_{2}b_{2}\implies p\equiv 2
mod 3. We make two variable substitutions,
m\coloneqq d_{1}d_{3}e_{2}e_{3}
and
n\coloneqq a_{2}b_{2}
,

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d_{1}d_{3}a_{2}\leq A\\
e_{2}e_{3}b_{2}\leq B\\
\flat\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{3^{\omega(d_{1}d_{3}e_{2}e_{3})}}\frac{\mu^{2}(d_{1}d_{3}a_{2}e_{2}e_{3}b_{2})}{d_{1}d_{3}a_{2}e_{1}e_{3}b_{2}}\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\leq AB\\
n\leq AB\\
\flat\end{subarray}}\frac{4^{\omega(m)}2^{\omega(n)}}{3^{\omega(m)}}\frac{\mu^{2}(mn)}{mn}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\leq AB\\
p\mid m\implies p\equiv 1\bmod 3\end{subarray}}\left(\frac{4}{3}\right)^{\omega(m)}\frac{\mu^{2}(m)}{m}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq AB\\
p\mid n\implies p\equiv 2\bmod 3\end{subarray}}\frac{\mu^{2}(n)}{n}\ll(\log AB)^{\frac{4}{3}}(\log AB),

where in the last step we used that
f(m)\coloneqq(\frac{4}{3})^{\omega(m)}\mu^{2}(m)
is a multiplicative function, thus
\sum_{m\leq AB}\frac{f(m)}{m}\leq\prod_{p\leq AB}(1+\frac{f(p)}{p}+\frac{f(p^{2})}{p^{2}}+...)=\prod_{p\leq AB}(1+\frac{4}{3p})\ll\prod_{p\leq AB}(1+\frac{1}{p})^{\frac{4}{3}}\ll(\log AB)^{\frac{4}{3}}
. Overall for the sub-case
d_{2}>V
,
e_{1}>V
and all analogous sub-cases we get a contribution to
N(A,B)
of

\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{AB(\log AB)^{\kappa_{2}+\frac{7}{3}}}{V^{\kappa_{1}}}\right).

(3.5)

For the second case, assume that one of the indices in (
3.4
) is larger than
V
but none of the variables in the argument of that character are larger than
V
. We will show the sub-case that
d_{2}>V
and
e_{1},e_{2},e_{3},b_{2}\leq V
, but our argument works the same for the other sub-cases. We use the triangle inequality and bound trivially

\displaystyle\big|N(A,B)_{\begin{subarray}{c}d_{2}>V\\
e_{1},e_{2},e_{3},b_{2}\leq V\end{subarray}}\big|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}e_{1}e_{2}e_{3}b_{2}\leq B\\
e_{1},e_{2},e_{3},b_{2}\leq V\\
\flat\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d_{1}d_{2}d_{3}a_{2}\leq A\\
\flat\end{subarray}}\frac{\mu^{2}(3d_{1}d_{2}d_{3}a_{2}e_{1}e_{2}e_{3}b_{2})}{3^{\omega(d_{1}d_{2}d_{3}e_{1}e_{2}e_{3})}}

\displaystyle\leq V^{4}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d_{1}d_{2}d_{3}a_{2}\leq A\\
p\mid d_{1}d_{2}d_{3}\implies p\equiv 1\bmod 3\\
p\mid a_{2}\implies p\equiv 2\bmod 3\end{subarray}}\frac{\mu^{2}(d_{1}d_{2}d_{3}a_{2})}{3^{\omega(d_{1}d_{2}d_{3})}}

\displaystyle=V^{4}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq A\end{subarray}}\mu^{2}(n)

\displaystyle\leq V^{4}A.

For all four analogous sub-cases where one of the indices is large, but none of the respective arguments, we get a contribution to
N(A,B)
of

\mathcal{O}\left(V^{4}A+V^{4}B\right).

(3.6)

With the previous two cases we have fully dealt with the summation ranges where at least one of the indices in the big character sum (
3.4
) is large. We now turn to the summation range where all indices are small, i.e.
d_{2},d_{3},e_{2},e_{3}\leq V
.

For the third case assume that
d_{2},d_{3},e_{2},e_{3}\leq V
, but not
d_{2}=d_{3}=e_{2}=e_{3}=1
, i.e. that at least one index is larger than one. We choose
1<d_{2}\leq V
, but there are four equivalent sub-cases. We want to apply a single oscillation result
3.6
to the character
\chi_{d_{2}}(e_{1}e_{2}e_{3}b_{2})
. More specifically we would like to apply it to a sum of the form
\sum_{e_{1}\leq\frac{B}{e_{2}e_{3}b_{2}}}\frac{\chi_{d_{2}}(e_{1})}{3^{\omega(e_{1})}}
to obtain a
\log(\frac{B}{e_{2}e_{3}b_{2}})^{-C}
saving compared to the trivial bound. If no further restrictions are placed on
e_{1}
, the
\log(\frac{B}{e_{2}e_{3}b_{2}})^{-C}
saving becomes poor when
e_{2}e_{3}b_{2}
is close to
B
. Thus we need to divide the third case into two further ranges: one where
e_{1},b_{2}\leq B^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{k}}}
(call this case 3a) and one where
e_{1}>B^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{k}}}
or
b_{2}>B^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{k}}}
(call this case 3b). Here
k
is some integer greater than 2 that can be chosen later for convenience (
k=4
works for example).

So we turn to case 3a, i.e. we assume
d_{2},d_{3},e_{2},e_{3}\leq V
,
d_{2}>1
,
b_{2},e_{1}\leq B^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{k}}}
. Then we may bound trivially, similarly to case 2:

\displaystyle\big|N(A,B)_{\begin{subarray}{c}d_{2},d_{3},e_{2},e_{3}\leq V\\
d_{2}>1\\
e_{1},b_{2}\leq B^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{k}}}\end{subarray}}\big|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}e_{1}e_{2}e_{3}b_{2}\leq B\\
e_{1},b_{2}\leq B^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{k}}}\\
e_{2},e_{3}\leq V\\
\flat\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d_{1}d_{2}d_{3}a_{2}\leq A\\
\flat\end{subarray}}\frac{\mu^{2}(3d_{1}d_{2}d_{3}a_{2}e_{1}e_{2}e_{3}b_{2})}{3^{\omega(d_{1}d_{2}d_{3}e_{1}e_{2}e_{3})}}

\displaystyle\leq B^{\nicefrac{{2}}{{k}}}V^{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d_{1}d_{2}d_{3}a_{2}\leq A\\
p\mid d_{1}d_{2}d_{3}\implies p\equiv 1\bmod 3\\
p\mid a_{2}\implies p\equiv 2\bmod 3\end{subarray}}\frac{\mu^{2}(d_{1}d_{2}d_{3}a_{2})}{3^{\omega(d_{1}d_{2}d_{3})}}

\displaystyle\leq B^{\nicefrac{{2}}{{k}}}V^{2}\sum_{n\leq A}\mu^{2}(n)

\displaystyle\leq B^{\nicefrac{{2}}{{k}}}V^{2}A.

For all cases analogous to 3a, we get a contribution to
N(A,B)
of

\mathcal{O}\left(ABV^{2}\big(A^{\frac{2-k}{k}}+B^{\frac{2-k}{k}}\big)\right).

(3.7)

We turn to case 3b, i.e. we assume
d_{2},d_{3},e_{2},e_{3}\leq V
,
d_{2}>1
and that at least one of
b_{2},e_{1}
is larger than
B^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{k}}}
. We choose to show the argument for
e_{1}>B^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{k}}}
, but Lemma
3.6
is flexible enough to deal with the slightly different sub-case
b_{2}>B^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{k}}}
in just the same way. Let
C>0
be a parameter to be chosen later. Then we have,

\displaystyle\big|N(A,B)_{\begin{subarray}{c}d_{2},d_{3},e_{2},e_{3}\leq V\\
d_{2}>1\\
e_{1}>B^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{k}}}\end{subarray}}\big|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d_{1}d_{2}d_{3}a_{2}\leq A\\
e_{2}e_{3}b_{2}\leq B\\
d_{3},e_{2},e_{3}\leq V\\
1<d_{2}\leq V\\
\flat\end{subarray}}\frac{\mu^{2}(3d_{1}d_{2}d_{3}a_{2}e_{2}e_{3}b_{2})}{3^{\omega(d_{1}d_{2}d_{3}e_{2}e_{3})}}\;\bigg|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}B^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{k}}}<e_{1}\leq\frac{B}{e_{2}e_{3}b_{2}}\\
p\mid e_{1}\implies p\equiv 1\bmod 3\end{subarray}}\mu^{2}(e_{1})\frac{\chi_{d_{2}}(e_{1})\chi_{d_{3}}^{2}(e_{1})}{3^{\omega(e_{1})}}\bigg|

\displaystyle\ll_{C}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d_{1}d_{2}d_{3}a_{2}\leq A\\
\flat\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}e_{2}e_{3}b_{2}\leq B\\
\flat\end{subarray}}\frac{\mu^{2}(3d_{1}d_{2}d_{3}a_{2}e_{2}e_{3}b_{2})}{3^{\omega(d_{1}d_{2}d_{3})}3^{\omega(e_{2}e_{3})}}\frac{B}{e_{2}e_{3}b_{2}}(\log(B^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{k}}}))^{-C}

\displaystyle\ll_{C}\frac{B}{(\log B)^{C}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d_{1}d_{2}d_{3}a_{2}\leq A\\
\flat\end{subarray}}\frac{\mu^{2}(3d_{1}d_{2}d_{3}a_{2})}{3^{\omega(d_{1}d_{2}d_{3})}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}e_{2}e_{3}b_{2}\leq B\\
\flat\end{subarray}}\frac{\mu^{2}(3e_{2}e_{3}b_{2})}{3^{\omega(e_{2}e_{3})}}\frac{1}{e_{2}e_{3}b_{2}}

\displaystyle\ll_{C}\frac{B}{(\log B)^{C}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\leq A\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq B\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{n}

\displaystyle\ll_{C}\frac{AB}{(\log B)^{C-1}}.

Here we applied Lemma
3.6
to evaluate the character sum in the second line and used that
B^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{k}}}<\frac{B}{e_{2}e_{3}b_{2}}
, hence
\log(B^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{k}}})^{-C}>\log(\frac{B}{e_{2}e_{3}b_{2}})^{-C}
. Note that in the second line,
d_{2},d_{3}
are bounded by a large power of
\log B
as required in Lemma
3.6
because of the assumption that
A,B\geq\exp((\log AB)^{\delta})
and the fact that
V
is a power of
\log AB
(yet to be chosen). For all sub-cases analogous to 3b we get a contribution to
N(A,B)
of

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}\left(ABV^{2}\bigg(\frac{1}{(\log A)^{C-1}}+\frac{1}{(\log B)^{C-1}}\bigg)\right).

(3.8)

This leaves us with the fourth and last range of summation, the range where
d_{2}=d_{3}=e_{2}=e_{3}=1
, i.e. all characters in the big character sum (
3.4
) are trivial. This range constitutes the main contribution to
N(A,B)
.

For the fourth case, assume
d_{2}=d_{3}=e_{2}=e_{3}=1
. Then the big character sum simplifies to

\displaystyle N(A,B)_{\begin{subarray}{c}d_{2}=d_{3}=1\\
e_{2}=e_{3}=1\end{subarray}}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d_{1}a_{2}\leq A\\
e_{1}b_{2}\leq B\\
\flat\end{subarray}}\frac{\mu^{2}(3d_{1}a_{2}e_{1}b_{2})}{3^{\omega(d_{1}e_{1})}}=

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\leq A\end{subarray}}\frac{\mu^{2}(3m)}{3^{\omega_{1}(m)}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq B\\
(m,n)\,\text{admissible}\end{subarray}}\frac{\mu^{2}(3mn)}{3^{\omega_{1}(n)}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\leq A\end{subarray}}g(m)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq B\\
(m,n)\,\text{admissible}\end{subarray}}g_{m}(n).

(3.9)

Here we defined
\omega_{1}(n)
to be the function that counts the number of prime divisors of
n
which are congruent to 1 mod 3,
\omega_{1}(n)\coloneqq\#\{p\text{ prime}:p\equiv 1\text{ mod 3 and }p\mid n\}
. Furthermore we define the functions

g_{m}(n)\coloneqq\frac{\mu^{2}(3mn)}{3^{\omega_{1}(n)}}\quad\text{and}\quad g(m)\coloneqq\frac{\mu^{2}(3m)}{3^{\omega_{1}(m)}}

and note that they are multiplicative. In order to evaluate the sum (
3.9
), we want to apply a theorem on sums of multiplicative functions, Theorem
2.2
. But in order to apply the theorem we first have to deal with the summation condition “
(m,n)
admissible”, which is not multiplicative.

Denote by
G=(\nicefrac{{\mathbb{Z}}}{{27\mathbb{Z}}})^{\ast}
the multiplicative group modulo 27 and by
\widehat{G}=\widehat{(\nicefrac{{\mathbb{Z}}}{{27\mathbb{Z}}})^{\ast}}
the group of characters modulo 27. Using orthogonality of characters, we may express the indicator function of a pair
(m,n)
being admissible as follows,

\displaystyle\mathbb{1}_{\{(m,n)\text{ admissible}\}}=\frac{1}{\phi(27)^{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\delta,\epsilon\bmod 27\\
(\delta,\epsilon)\text{ admissible}\end{subarray}}\sum_{\chi,\chi^{\prime}\in G}\chi(\delta^{-1}m)\chi^{\prime}(\epsilon^{-1}n).

We use this expression to rewrite (
3.9
)

\displaystyle N(A,B)_{\begin{subarray}{c}d_{2}=d_{3}=1\\
e_{2}=e_{3}=1\end{subarray}}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\leq A\end{subarray}}g(m)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq B\\
(m,n)\,\text{admissible}\end{subarray}}g_{m}(n)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\phi(27)^{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\delta,\epsilon\bmod 27\\
(\delta,\epsilon)\text{ admissible}\end{subarray}}\sum_{\chi,\chi^{\prime}\in G}\chi(\delta^{-1})\chi^{\prime}(\epsilon^{-1})\sum_{m\leq A}\chi(m)g(m)\sum_{n\leq B}\chi^{\prime}(n)g_{m}(n).

(3.10)

The sum
\sum_{n\leq B}\chi^{\prime}(n)g_{m}(n)
is now of the right shape for an application of Theorem
2.2
, so we check that the multiplicative function
\chi^{\prime}(n)g_{m}(n)
satisfies the requirement (
2.1
) for any choice of
\chi^{\prime}
and
m
. There are three possibilities we treat separately;

i)

\chi^{\prime}
is the trivial character,

ii)

\chi^{\prime}
is non-trivial on
G
but trivial on the index two subgroup
H=\{z\in G:z\equiv 1\bmod 3\}
,

iii)

\chi^{\prime}
is non-trivial on
H
.

i) If
\chi^{\prime}
is the trivial character, we show that
\chi^{\prime}(n)g_{m}(n)=g_{m}(n)
satisfies condition (
2.1
) with parameters
\alpha=\nicefrac{{2}}{{3}}
,
k=1
,
J=2
,
Q=\exp((\log B)^{\frac{1}{100}})
,
\epsilon=1
. For all
x\geq Q
and all
A^{\prime}>0
,

\displaystyle\sum_{p\leq x}\chi^{\prime}(p)g_{m}(p)\log p

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq x\\
p\equiv 1\bmod 3\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{3}\log p+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq x\\
p\equiv 2\bmod 3\end{subarray}}\log p-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\mid m\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{3^{\omega_{1}(p)}}\log p

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{3}\cdot\frac{1}{\phi(3)}x+\frac{1}{\phi(3)}x+\mathcal{O}(x\exp(-C_{W}(\log x)^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}))-\mathcal{O}(\omega(m)\log m)

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{3}x+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{x}{(\log x)^{A^{\prime}}}\right).

In the second line we have applied the Siegel–Walfisz theorem and
C_{W}>0
is some constant coming from this theorem. Since we will apply Siegel–Walfisz multiple times in the remainder of the proof and the associated constants will always just be absorbed into
\mathcal{O}(\frac{x}{(\log x)^{A^{\prime}}})
, we will abuse notation and call them
C_{W}
every time. Note that we used that
\log m\leq\log A
and the assumption that
A,B\geq\exp((\log AB)^{\delta})
to bound the second error term.

In case that
\chi^{\prime}
is trivial, applying Theorem
2.2
(with the parameters chosen above) to the sum
\sum\chi^{\prime}(n)g_{m}(n)
yields

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq B\end{subarray}}\chi^{\prime}(n)g_{m}(n)=\frac{B}{(\log B)^{\frac{1}{3}}\Gamma(\frac{2}{3})}c_{0}(m)+\mathcal{O}\bigg(\frac{B}{(\log B)^{\frac{4}{3}}}\bigg),

(3.11)

where

c_{0}(m)=\prod_{p}\left(1+\frac{g(p)}{p}\right)\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)^{\frac{2}{3}}\prod_{p\mid m}\left(1+\frac{g(p)}{p}\right)^{-1}.

ii) If
\chi^{\prime}
is non-trivial on
G
but trivial on
H
, there is a small difference to case i) when showing that
\chi^{\prime}(n)g_{m}(n)
satisfies condition (
2.1
). Note that if
\chi^{\prime}
is trivial on the index 2 subgroup
H
but non-trivial on
2H
, then
\chi^{\prime}
takes values in
\{+1,-1\}
. Upon closer inspection there is only one character that satisfies these properties;
\chi^{\prime}
has to be equal to 1 on
H
and equal to
-1
on
2H
. Thus the sum over primes
p\equiv 2\bmod 3
evaluates to

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq x\\
p\equiv 2\bmod 3\end{subarray}}\chi^{\prime}(p)\log p

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\delta\bmod 27\\
\delta\equiv 2\bmod 3\end{subarray}}\chi^{\prime}(\delta)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq x\\
p\equiv\delta\bmod 27\end{subarray}}\log p

\displaystyle=-9\left(\frac{1}{\phi(27)}x+\mathcal{O}(x\exp(-C_{W}(\log x)^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}))\right)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}x+\mathcal{O}(x\exp(-C_{W}(\log x)^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}})).

Here we applied the Siegel–Walfisz theorem, and
C_{W}
is some constant coming from this application. The sum over primes
p\equiv 1\bmod 3
and the sum over primes that divide
m
can be treated the same as in i). Thus (for
Q
as in i)) we obtain that for all
x\geq Q
and
A^{\prime}>0
,

\displaystyle\sum_{p\leq x}\chi^{\prime}(p)g_{m}(p)\log p

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{6}x-\frac{1}{2}x+\mathcal{O}(x\exp(-C_{W}(\log x)^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}))-\mathcal{O}(\omega(m)\log m)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{3}x+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{x}{(\log x)^{A^{\prime}}}\right).

In case that
\chi^{\prime}
is non-trivial on
G
but trivial on
H
, applying Theorem
2.2
(with
\alpha=-\nicefrac{{1}}{{3}}
and otherwise the same parameters as in i)) to the sum
\sum\chi^{\prime}(n)g_{m}(n)
yields

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq B\end{subarray}}\chi^{\prime}(n)g_{m}(n)=\mathcal{O}\bigg(\frac{B}{(\log B)^{\frac{4}{3}}}\bigg).

(3.12)

iii) If
\chi^{\prime}
is non-trivial on
H
,
\chi^{\prime}(n)g_{m}(n)
satisfies condition (
2.1
) with
\alpha=0
,
k=1
,
Q=\exp((\log B)^{\frac{1}{100}})
,
\epsilon=1
and
J=1
. We split the sum as we did in i),

\displaystyle\sum_{p\leq x}\chi^{\prime}(p)g_{m}(p)\log p

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq x\\
p\equiv 1\bmod 3\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{3}\chi^{\prime}(p)\log p+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq x\\
p\equiv 2\bmod 3\end{subarray}}\chi^{\prime}(p)\log p-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\mid m\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{3^{\omega_{1}(p)}}\chi^{\prime}(p)\log p.

However,
\chi^{\prime}
is now non-trivial and we get a cancellation of the main term due to orthogonality of characters. For the sum over
p\equiv 2\bmod 3
we get that for all
x\geq Q
and
A^{\prime}>0
,

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq x\\
p\equiv 2\bmod 3\end{subarray}}\chi^{\prime}(p)\log p

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\delta\bmod 27\\
\delta\equiv 2\bmod 3\end{subarray}}\chi^{\prime}(\delta)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq x\\
p\equiv\delta\bmod 27\end{subarray}}\log p

\displaystyle=\chi^{\prime}(2)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\xi\in H\end{subarray}}\chi^{\prime}(\xi)\left(\frac{1}{\phi(27)}x+\mathcal{O}(x\exp(-C_{W}(\log x)^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}))\right)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{O}(x\exp(-C_{W}(\log x)^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}))

\displaystyle=\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{x}{(\log x)^{A^{\prime}}}\right).

The sum over
p\equiv 1\bmod 3
can be bounded analogously and the sum over
p\mid m
fits into the same error term, as shown in i). Thus
\chi^{\prime}(n)g_{m}(n)
satisfies condition (
2.1
) with
\alpha=0
. Applying Theorem
2.2
(with parameters
\alpha=0
,
k=1
,
Q=\exp((\log B)^{\frac{1}{100}})
,
\epsilon=1
and
J=1
) yields

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq B\end{subarray}}\chi^{\prime}(n)g_{m}(n)=\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{B(\log Q)^{J+1}}{(\log B)^{J+1}}\right)=\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{B}{(\log B)^{\frac{4}{3}}}\right).

(3.13)

Importantly we have followed the convention in Remark
2.3
.

Combining (
3.11
), (
3.12
) and (
3.13
), we see that (
3.10
) becomes

\displaystyle N(A,B)_{\begin{subarray}{c}d_{2}=d_{3}=1\\
e_{2}=e_{3}=1\end{subarray}}=\frac{1}{\phi(27)^{2}}

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\delta,\epsilon\bmod 27\\
(\delta,\epsilon)\text{ admissible}\end{subarray}}\sum_{\chi,\chi^{\prime}\in G}\chi(\delta^{-1})\chi^{\prime}(\epsilon^{-1})

\displaystyle\sum_{m\leq A}\chi(m)g(m)\left(\mathbb{1}_{\{\chi^{\prime}=\chi_{0}\}}\frac{B}{(\log B)^{\frac{1}{3}}\Gamma(\frac{2}{3})}c_{0}(m)+\mathcal{O}\bigg(\frac{B}{(\log B)^{\frac{4}{3}}}\bigg)\right),

(3.14)

where
\mathbb{1}_{\{\chi^{\prime}=\chi_{0}\}}
is the indicator function of
\chi^{\prime}
being trivial and

c_{0}(m)=\prod_{p}\left(1+\frac{g(p)}{p}\right)\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)^{\frac{2}{3}}\prod_{p\mid m}\left(1+\frac{g(p)}{p}\right)^{-1}.

To evaluate the sum over
m
in (

), one can perform a calculation that is analogous to the above. Again one has to distinguish three cases, depending on the (non-)triviality of the character
\chi
, and use a combination of Siegel–Walfisz and Theorem
2.2
. The main term comes from the summand where
\chi
and
\chi^{\prime}
are both trivial. All other combinations of characters lead to smaller contributions that fit into the error term (as in cases ii) and iii) above), so the sum over
m
becomes

\displaystyle\sum_{m\leq A}\chi(m)g(m)\left(\mathbb{1}_{\{\chi^{\prime}=\chi_{0}\}}\frac{B}{(\log B)^{\frac{1}{3}}\Gamma(\frac{2}{3})}c_{0}(m)+\mathcal{O}\bigg(\frac{B}{(\log B)^{\frac{4}{3}}}\bigg)\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{1}_{\{\chi=\chi^{\prime}=\chi_{0}\}}c_{1}\frac{AB}{(\log A)^{\frac{1}{3}}(\log B)^{\frac{1}{3}}(\Gamma(\frac{2}{3}))^{2}}+\mathcal{O}\bigg(\frac{AB}{(\log A)^{\frac{1}{3}}(\log B)^{\frac{1}{3}}}\bigg(\frac{1}{\log A}+\frac{1}{\log B}\bigg)\bigg).

(3.15)

Here
c_{1}
is the constant

Substituting (

\displaystyle N(A,B)_{\begin{subarray}{c}d_{2}=d_{3}=1\\

where

We end the proof by choosing the parameters

,

and
